## Question

A 33-year-old female presents to your clinic with left forefoot pain. She has had difficulty wearing shoes over the last 5 years and now has developed pain over the medial aspect of the first metatarsal phalangeal (MTP) joint. On examination, she has tenderness over the medial 1st metatarsal head but no pain or hypermobility to the 1st tarsometatarsal (TMT) joint. Her foot AP radiograph is shown in Figure A. She is interested in surgical correction. Which of the following would be most appropriate for this patient?

![[q211341-a-ap-foot.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Akin osteotomy with modified Mcbride procedure
2. Isolated distal chevron osteotomy
3. Crescentic osteotomy with an akin osteotomy
4. Lapidus procedure with Keller resection arthroplasty
5. Broomstick osteotomy with a 1st tarsometatarsal arthrodesis

## Correct option

**3. Crescentic osteotomy with an akin osteotomy**

## Answer explanation

The patient in the vignette has a severe left hallux valgus (HV) with a hallux valgus angle (HVA) of 45 and an intermetatarsal angle (IMA) of 17. The most optimal treatment of those listed is a proximal (crescentic) 1st metatarsal osteotomy and an akin (proximal phalanx medial closing wedge) osteotomy for the correction of her deformity.

A broad array of surgical treatment options for HV are available, with multiple options for almost any specific case depending on the severity of several parameters to include the HVA and IMA of the patient. Of the listed procedures, only a proximal metatarsal osteotomy combined with an akin osteotomy addresses severe values of both of these angles. There are several different proximal metatarsal osteotomies that are described: crescentic, broomstick, Ludloff and Scarf which vary depending on the orientation and size of the cut. For patients with pain or subluxation of the first tarsometatarsal joint (TMT) joint, a fusion of the first TMT is indicated.

*Easley et al. performed a review on the pathogenesis and treatment of HV.* **They noted** that a distal chevron osteotomy appears to be a great treatment for mild and some moderate HV deformities. **The authors also note** that multiple proximal first metatarsal procedures, when combined with a distal soft-tissue procedure, appear to provide satisfactory treatment for moderate-to-severe hallux valgus deformity. These include proximal crescentic, proximal chevron, proximal oblique (Ludloff), proximal closing wedge, and scarf osteotomies and the Lapidus procedure.

*Robinson et al. performed an overview of the treatment of HV.* **They noted** that if the correct surgery is performed for the correct deformity there is a high rate of success in appropriately selected patients. However, a small number of patients have poor outcomes following surgery. **They conclude** that randomized, controlled trials are needed to elucidate the factors which determine a good outcome with a good validated outcome score following HV correction.

*Smith et al. reviewed the importance of the DMAA for the treatment of HV.* **They noted** that the goal of correction is to achieve a realigned first ray and preserve the congruent first MTP articulation. **They conclude** that in patients with an increased DMAA and congruent joint, the use of double and triple first ray osteotomies must be used to achieve satisfactory correction.

Figure A is an AP foot radiograph that reveals a severe left HV with an HVA of 45 and an IMA of 17.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Due to the severity of the HVA, and IMA, an Akin osteotomy with a modified Mcbride would not be sufficient correction.
Answer 2: An isolated chevron osteotomy would be the treatment for mild-moderate HV with an IMA < 13.
Answer 4: While a Lapidus procedure can be used to correct large IMA in HV especially in the setting of an arthritic or hypermobile 1st TMT, this is often not combined with an Akin procedure and not a Keller resection arthroplasty. The Keller arthroplasty is rarely indicated given the significantly high recurrence rates and decreased postoperative function of the 1st MTP joint.
Answer 5: A Broomstick osteotomy is a proximal metatarsal osteotomy which is often combined with a distal metatarsal osteotomy or an akin osteotomy (not a 1st tarsometatarsal arthrodesis)
