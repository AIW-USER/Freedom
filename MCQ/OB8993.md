## Question

You are called to evaluate a patient in the PACU who underwent arthroscopic rotator cuff repair who is now having difficulty with his vision. The surgery was performed arthroscopically in the beach chair position with four bio-absorbable suture anchors in a double row configuration under a regional anesthetic block with sedation. No intra-operative complications were noted. A clinical photo of the patient is shown in Figure A after being asked to gaze left. Which of the following interventions might have resulted in a lower probability of developing this complication?

![[q8993-a-eyes-cn6-palsy3.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Performance of an open rather than arthroscopic rotator cuff repair
2. Performance of the procedure in the lateral decubitus position
3. Use of metal rather than bio-absorbable anchors
4. Use of general rather than regional anesthesia
5. Maintenance of systolic blood pressure below 70 mm Hg throughout the procedure

## Correct option

**2. Performance of the procedure in the lateral decubitus position**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a left cranial nerve VI (abducens) palsy, resulting in inability to gaze left with the left eye, due to paralysis of the left lateral rectus muscle. Lateral decubitus positioning has been theorized to mitigate the risk of cerebral hypoperfusion in patients undergoing shoulder arthroscopy.

There is increased concern for cerebral hypoperfusion in the beach chair position as compared to lateral decubitus however the risk remains small and stroke has been reported after lateral decubitus surgery as well. Current anesthesia literature recommends maintenance of systolic blood pressure greater than 90 mm Hg and use of regional anesthesia for procedures performed in the beach chair position.

*Gelber et al. performed a cadaveric study to determine the risk of neurologic injury when establishing an anterior-inferior 5 o'clock portal in lateral decubitus versus beach chair position.* **They found** that the musculocutaneous and axillary nerves were closer to a Kirschner wire drilled orthogonally into the 5 o'clock position on the glenoid when doing so in the lateral decubitus position. **They concluded** that the beach chair position was safer but recommended against using a trans-subscapularis portal in either position.

*Li et al. review the advantages and complications of lateral decubitus and beach chair positioning.* **They note** that beach chair positioning is associated with significantly more cerebral desaturation events (CDEs) as measured on intra-operative near infrared spectroscopy. However, the degree and duration of such hypoperfusion required to produce neurologic injury is unknown.

Figure A shows a left lateral rectus palsy with inability to gaze left with the left eye. Illustration A shows a table from Li et al. detailing the pros and cons of each position.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1. Performance of an open technique is unlikely to affect cerebral perfusion in the absence of excessive blood loss.
Answer 3. Anchor type would not be thought to affect cerebral perfusion.
Answer 4. General anesthesia would INCREASE the risk of CDE.
Answer 5. Lowering systolic blood pressure would INCREASE the risk of CDE. Although there is evidence that it is safe to perform hypotensive anesthesia with systolic blood pressure below the current recommended beach chair minimum of 90 mm Hg, maintenance below 70 mm Hg is not recommended and would not decrease the risk of cerebral hypoperfusion.
