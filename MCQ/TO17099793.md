## Question

Risk factors for development of pseudotumor after metal-on-metal arthroplasty are all except

## Options

1. female sex
2. high acetabular inclination angle
3. age<40 yrs.
4. BMI> 40

## Correct option

**4. BMI> 40**

## Answer explanation

Pseudotumor of the hip is defined as a (semi)-solid or cystic peri-prosthetic soft-tissue mass with a diameter ≥ 2 cm that cannot be attributed to an infection, malignancy, bursa or scar tissue. The diagnosis is based on CT evaluation and confirmed by MRI and/or ultrasound.

Risk factors for pseudotumor after MOM THR:

1. An association between pseudotumor and elevated levels of serum cobalt and chromium has been established

(cobalt levels > 5 µg/l).

2. Female gender

3. Implant malposition: - Implants malposition, bearing surfaces, diametral clearance and sector angle are associated with high ions level. Position of the implants is vital for the outcome of MoM HRA. Mal-positioning of the implants and especially the acetabular component, increase the risk of impingement and edge loading. Increased angle of anteversion and inclination of acetabular components (High acetabulum inclination angle > 55 degree) are associated with increased incidence of pseudotumor. Diametral clearance is the gap between the two mating metal surfaces forming a gland’s internal cavity. The low clearance of the implant is associated with increased wear due to reduced fluid film formation.

4. Pain

5. Another risk factor is the corrosion on the taper and trunnion contributing to the formation of metal debris also in well-positioned implants.

6. The younger the patient, the greater the risk for revision surgery. Studies show a greater risk for patients under the age of 40. Maybe this can be explained by the higher activity level.

7. In some studies large size metal head has also been associated with pseudotumor. Metal allergy and High BMI are not associated with increased incidence of pseudotumor.

Answer: 4
