## Question

A 24-year-old male patient fell off of his roof while hanging Christmas lights. He presents to the emergency department due to pain and inability to use his left elbow. Xrays obtained in the ER are shown in Figure A. You perform a successful closed reduction, and the patient is found to be stable through a passive range of motion. When should the patient remove the splint and start motion?

![[q216843-a-a-lateral-and-b-anteroposterior-radiographs-of-a-s.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Immediately after he is discharged
2. In 1 week from today
3. In 3 weeks from today
4. In 6 weeks from today
5. Two weeks after you perform LCL repair on him

## Correct option

**2. In 1 week from today**

## Answer explanation

This patient sustained a simple elbow dislocation with a stable arc of motion after reduction, these are best treated with early range of motion in 5-7 days.

Elbow dislocations are the second most common dislocation after shoulder dislocations and the most common dislocation in children. The majority are posterolateral and occur after falling onto an outstretched supinated hand. Simple dislocations makeup 50-60% of all elbow dislocations and do not have an associated fracture. Conservative treatment of simple elbow dislocations is with brief immobilization of 5-10 days, followed by early therapy for range of motion. If instability is present after brief immobilization, that may be an indication for ligamentous repair versus reconstruction. However, recurrent instability after proper treatment is rare at <1-2%.

*Anakwe et al. reviewed 110 simple elbow dislocations an average of 88 months after injury.* **They found** that postoperative stiffness occurred in 56% and residual pain in 62% of patients. Worse DASH and Oxford elbow scores were associated with loss of elbow flexion. Decreased overall flexion-extension was associated with worse Oxford function, pain, and satisfaction scores. **They concluded** that while benign simple elbow dislocations can have long-lasting effects on patients with high rates of stiffness and residual pain.

*Calderazzi et al. reviewed 26 patients with 40 months average follow-up treated with closed reduction and conservative treatment.* **They found** 21 of 26 patients had full return of range of motion, with five patients lacking 20 degrees of full extension at final follow-up. All five patients with decreased range of motion were immobilized for 3 weeks post-reduction, while 20 of 21 who achieved full range of motion were immobilized for only 7 days.

Figure A demonstrates an AP and lateral x-ray of a left elbow with a posterolateral dislocation without associated fractures.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: While the patient did have a stable range of motion, short-term immobilization can help decrease swelling and improve pain control. Additionally, the patient is at risk for re-dislocation in this acute period.
Answer 3&4: Prolonged immobilization is not indicated in this patient and will only result in increased stiffness.
Answer 5: This patient had a stable arc of motion after reduction and does not require ligamentous repair or reconstruction.
