## Question

A patient sustains a laceration to the radial aspect of the index finger. On examination, he has a complete lack of sensation of the radial aspect of the index finger. Surgical exploration reveals a lacerated digital nerve with a 14mm gap. The nerve is subsequently repaired with a synthetic nerve conduit. The collagen used in this synthetic nerve conduit is the same as the primary collagen found in which of the following?

## Options

1. Articular cartilage
2. Nucleus pulposis
3. Basement membrane
4. Blood vessels
5. Ligaments

## Correct option

**5. Ligaments**

## Answer explanation

This patient sustained a peripheral nerve injury that was treated with a synthetic nerve conduit. Type I collagen is the protein commonly used in synthetic nerve conduits.

Peripheral nerve injuries encompass a range of reversible and irreversible impairments determined by the injury level, axonal disruption, and time to treatment. Diagnosis can be made based on clinical examination and confirmed with EMG/NCS. Treatment may involve observation, repair, tendon transfers, or nerve grafting depending on the acuity, degree of injury, and mechanism of injury. The indications for use of a nerve conduit include a defect up to 20 mm. These nerve conduits allow for coaptation without tension, typically in small sensory nerves. Collagen conduits allow for nutrient exchange and accessibility to neurotrophic factors to the axonal growth zone during regeneration.

*Rbia et al. reviewed the outcomes of digital nerve gap reconstruction with either a type 1 collagen nerve conduit or a nerve graft.* **They reported** that the mean nerve gap length was 14mm for the collagen conduits versus 18mm for nerve allografts. After 12 months, outcomes were graded as good or excellent sensory recovery in 74% of the collagen conduit repairs and 94% of the nerve allografts. **They concluded** that nerve conduits and processed nerve allografts offer effective means of reconstructing a digital nerve gap <2.5 cm at a minimum of 12 months of follow-up.

*Braga et al. reported on peripheral nerve injuries treated with nerve conduits as they compare with nerve grafting and neurorrhaphy.* **They report** that the outcomes showed no significant difference between groups, but synthetic conduits had more complications. **They concluded** that the use of nerve conduits is preferable over suture repair and nerve grafting, as the functional recovery rates are above 80%, but the use of synthetic conduits is discouraged due to their higher complication rates.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1 and 2: Articular cartilage and the nucleus pulposis are primarily made of type II collagen
Answer 3: The basement membrane is composed primarily of type IV collagen
Answer 4: Blood vessels are composed primarily of type III collagen
