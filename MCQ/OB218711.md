## Answer explanation

The operative management of calcaneus fractures carries a risk of postoperative wound complications. Surgeon experience is negatively correlated with wound complications.

Calcaneus fractures are associated with high-energy axial load injuries such as a fall from height. There is a high degree of morbidity associated with this injury. The goals of operative fixation are to improve calcaneus alignment and height, reduce the articular fracture component, and limit the risk of wound complications. Wound complications vary in the literature (10-35%). There are patient and surgeon factors that are associated with an increased risk of postoperative wound complications. Patients who smoke, have diabetes, or present with open fractures are at a higher risk of wound complications. The surgical approach utilized and surgeon experience have also been correlated with higher wound complication rates. The total number of cases performed by a particular team and the institutional fracture load have been associated with decreasing the overall rate of wound complications. Some experts have proposed specialized institutional trauma care to improve outcomes associated with the operative treatment of calcaneal fractures.

*Schepers et al performed a retrospective analysis of a consecutive series of 53 calcaneus fractures in 50 patients.* *49 patients were treated with an extensile lateral approach with a subcuticular single-layer closure technique.* The overall deep infection rate was 4.1% (n=2) and the overall superficial infection rate was 4.1% (n=2). An association was reported between the total number of surgeries a surgical team performed and the number of postoperative wound complications(p=0.026). **The authors conclude** that a subcuticular one-layer suture technique is a suitable closure method. They also review the literature on the surgeon-volume effect on postoperative complications and recommend centralized treatment of this complex injury.

*Poeze et al performed a metanalysis of 21 studies which included a total number of 1656 calcaneus fractures.* Multiple regression analysis showed that institutional fracture load is a significant independent predictor of the development of serious infection after surgery. The infection rate increased exponentially with a decreasing fracture load (r(2) = -0.5; p = 0.03). **The authors also recommend** a specialized institutional trauma care program to improve outcomes.
