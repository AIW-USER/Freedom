## Question

An 82-year-old Caucasian female presents to your clinic with complaints of low back pain. She was referred to you by her primary care physician, and brings results from a recent DEXA scan. She is accompanied by her daughter, with whom she lives. Both the patient and her daughter cannot recall any recent trauma or falls. On physical examination, the patient is tender to palpation to the lumbar spine. Upon review of her radiographs (Figure A) and DEXA results (T-score -2.7), you prescribe her a medication that acts by inhibiting the osteoclast farnesyl pyrophosphate synthase enzyme. What is the effect of this medication on her risk of sustaining a future hip fracture?

![[q217004-a-450px-l4-compressionfracture2008.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. No effect
2. Decreased by approximately 25%
3. Decreased by approximately 50%
4. Decreased by approximately 75%
5. Decreased by approximately 100%

## Correct option

**3. Decreased by approximately 50%**

## Answer explanation

This female octogenarian has sustained an osteoporotic vertebral compression fracture. Nitrogren-containing bisphosphonates (i.e. Alendronate) confer a 45-55% risk reduction in future hip fracture (Answer 3).

Osteoporosis, which leads to decreased bone quantity due to impaired bony mineralization, affects approximately 200 million individuals worldwide. Roughly 1.5 million fractures occur each year secondary to osteoporosis. Osteoporotic insufficiency fractures occur most frequently in the lumbar spine, followed by the peritrochanteric region and finally the distal radius. Fractures of the hip are particularly concerning, as these injuries are associated with a markedly increased rate of all-cause mortality. Furthermore, only 33% of patients return to their previous level of function following a hip fracture. Treatment of osteoporosis is multimodal, with a backbone of lifestyle modification(s), supplementation with calcium and vitamin D, and pharmacologic treatment with an antiresorptive and/or anabolic agent.

*Black et al. presented findings from the Fracture Intervention Trial, one of the seminal randomized controlled trials demonstrating the efficacy of bisphosphonate therapy for the prevention of insufficiency fractures.* *They randomized 2027 women aged 55-81 to one of two groups: placebo (1005 patients) and alendronate (1022 patients).* *98% of surviving patients were available for follow-up.* Hip fracture rate in the alendronate group was 49% of that of the placebo group. Adverse experiences, including upper GI disorders, did not vary between the two groups.

*Papapoulous et al. provide a comprehensive systematic review and meta-analysis regarding the efficacy of alendronate on fragility hip fracture prevention.* *Their pooled analysis included 9,023 patients with osteopenia and/or a vertebral insufficiency fracture and 6,804 patients with osteoporosis and/or a vertebral insufficiency fracture.* **They found** that in patients with a T-score of less than or equal to -2.0, or with a vertebral fracture, alendronate therapy was associated with a 45% decrease in the rate of subsequent hip fragility fracture. This decrease increased to 55% in patients with overt osteoporosis (T-score < -2.5). **They concluded** that alendronate therapy is an effective pharmacologic agent by which to decrease the risk of future fragility fracture in patients with decreased bone mineral density.

*Black et al. continued their research on bisphosphonates.* Despite the enormous benefits of these agents, a risk of atypical femur fractures (AFFs) and osteonecrosis of the jaw lead to investigation regarding alternative pharmacologic agents and continued research regarding bisphosphonates. In this study, the authors reported on 196,129 women aged 50 and older who received bisphosphonate therapy while enrolled in the Kaiser Permanent Southern California health care system. They were followed from January 1, 2007 through November 30, 2017. Among 196,129 women, 277 AFFs occurred. Multivariable analysis identified longer duration of bisphosphonate therapy, race, height, weight and glucocorticoid use as risk factors for AFFs. In Caucasian patients, 149 hip fractures were prevented while 2 bisphosphonate-related AfFs occurred. **The authors postulate** that despite the association of AFFs with bisphosphonate therapy, the benefits of treatment far outweigh the risk.

Figure A demonstrates a lateral radiograph of the lumbar spine. There is evidence of osteopenia, with loss of vertebral body height at L4 consistent with a compression fracture.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1,2,4,5: bisphosphonate therapy reduces the risk of future hip fracture by approximately 45-55%.
