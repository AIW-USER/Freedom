## Question

A 38 year-old female is referred to you with knee pain for the past year. The pain is an intermittent dull ache that is not relieved with NSAIDs. She has a normal knee exam with no focal tenderness. Figure A is a radiograph of her knee. Figure B is from a recent biopsy ordered by her PCP. Which of the following is the best initial treatment option?

## Options

1. Bisphosphonate therapy
2. Percutaneous radiofrequency ablation
3. Methylprednisolone acetate injection
4. MR-guided high-intensity focused ultrasound
5. Curettage with autograft and internal fixation

## Correct option

**1. Bisphosphonate therapy**

## Answer explanation

The patient's history, radiographs, and biopsy results are consistent with symptomatic fibrous dysplasia of the distal femur. Bisphosphonate therapy can be used to treat bone pain associated with fibrous dysplasia.

Fibrous dysplasia is caused by a GS alpha protein mutation that results in failure to produce normal lamellar bone. Patients are generally asymptomatic with lesions found incidentally on imaging. Typically lesions have a ground glass appearance or a "punched-out" lesion with well-defined margins. Microscopic analysis will show fibroblast proliferation surrounding islands of woven bone. Treatment is usually nonoperative with observation for asymptomatic lesions or bisphosphonates for symptomatic ones. Surgical treatment is indicated for impending fracture, severe deformities, and failure of nonoperative treatment.

*Motamedi et al review various benign bone tumors grouped by matrix produced including osteoid, chondroid, fibrous, fat, vascular, and other.* *They reflect on the typical skeletal locations, location within bone, and the characteristic radiographic features.*

DiCaprio and Enneking provide an overview of the pathophysiology, evaluation, and treatment options. They note that most lesions are found incidentally and can be observed. Bisphosphonates can help decrease pain and risk of fracture in some patients. Surgery is indicated for deformity, impending fracture, and symptomatic lesions.

Figure A is an AP of a knee showing a well-defined, central lesion of the distal femur with sclerotic margins and a hazy matrix without significant cortical thinning and no signs of periosteal reaction. Figure B is a histological slide showing irregular trabeculae of woven bone in a fibrous background, consistent with fibrous dysplasia.

Incorrect Answer:
Answer 2: RFA is used for osteoid osteomas
Answer 3: Methylprednisolone acetate injections are more effective for active unicameral bone cyst
Answer 4: MR-guided high-intensity focused ultrasound has been shown to be useful inthe management of osteoid osteomas
Answer 5: Curettage with allograft (not autograft) and internal fixation is reserved for fibrous dysplasia lesions associated with impending fractures, severe deformity, and those who have failed nonoperative therapy. Autogenous bone will convert to fibrous dysplastic bone.
