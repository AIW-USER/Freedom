Chapter: Arthroscopy MCQs — Meniscus Injuries

## Question

True about wrisberg type discoid meniscus is

## Options

1. Normal in shape
2. Most common type
3. More commonly seen in adults
4. Medial is more common than lateral

## Correct option

**1. Normal in shape**

## Answer explanation

Explanation: Discoid meniscus is an uncommon meniscal anomaly that occurs more frequently laterally than medially.

Discoid lateral menisci generally are categorized, according to the system of Watanabe et al., as complete, incomplete, and Wrisberg type, on the basis of the degree of coverage of the lateral tibial plateau and the presence or absence of the normal posterior meniscotibial attachment.

- Complete: complete discoid and have a posterior meniscal attachment.
- Incomplete: Incompletely discoid and have a posterior meniscal attachment.
- Wrisberg-type discoid menisci: least common type, usually nearly normal in size and shape and have no posterior attachment except the ligament of Wrisberg.

Complete and incomplete discoid menisci are more commonly seen in and usually asymptomatic, with no abnormal motion of the meniscus during knee flexion or extension. If an incomplete or complete discoid meniscus is torn, symptoms are similar to those of any other meniscal tear: lateral joint line tenderness, clicking, and effusion.

Wrisberg-type discoid menisci often occur at a younger age than complete or incomplete types and are unassociated with trauma. Abnormal motion of this type of discoid meniscus results in a popping sound during knee flexion and extension (“snapping knee syndrome”).

Answer: 1
