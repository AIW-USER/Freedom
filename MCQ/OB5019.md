## Answer explanation

The risk of avascular necrosis after pediatric hip fracture is highest in transphyseal fractures with dislocation of the epiphysis (Figure B, Delbet 1B, AVN risk up to 100%), followed by transphyseal fractures without dislocation of the epiphysis (Figure A, Delbet 1A), transcervical fractures (Figure C, Delbet II, AVN risk 0-61%), cervicotrochanteric fractures (Figure D, Delbet III, AVN risk 27%) and is least common in intertrochanteric fractures (Figure E, Delbet IV, AVN risk 14%).

Avascular necrosis (AVN) of the femoral head is the most common complication of pediatric hip fractures. It is likely the result of vascular injury that occurs at the time of fracture, either through kinking of vessels, direct laceration, or tamponade of vessels secondary to increased intracapsular pressure. Urgent reduction and stable fixation with capsular decompression may reduce the likelihood of AVN. Annual radiographic surveillance is recommended to monitor for development of AVN, until skeletally maturity in younger children with displaced fracture or for a minimum of 2 years in older adolescents approaching skeletal maturity.

*Boardman et al states that fracture type and age at the time of injury are the best predictors of osteonecrosis.* Delbet type I, II, and III fractures were 15, 6, and 4 times more likely, respectively, to result in osteonecrosis than were type IV fractures.

*Beaty et al reviewed fractures of the hip in children.* **They concluded** that stable fixation of pediatric hip fractures is necessary and should be prioritized over preservation of the proximal femoral physis. However, the development of osteonecrosis is most likely related to the severity of the initial injury and is largely unaffected by treatment of the fracture.

Figures A-E and Illustration A demonstrate the different types of pediatric hip fractures, as detailed above, described by the Delbet classification. Illustration B depicts the blood supply to the femoral head in pediatric patients. Prior to age 4, the femoral head is vascularized by the circumflex arteries (medial and lateral) as well as the artery of the ligamentum teres. After age 4, the artery of the ligamentum teres and the lateral circumflex arterial system regress and the femoral head is predominantly vascularized by the medial circumflex artery.

Answers 1, 3, 4, 5 - These are not the correct order of images with regard to risk of subsequent osteonecrosis.
