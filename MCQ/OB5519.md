## Question

A 78-year-old woman with metastatic thyroid carcinoma presents with left leg pain for a year. She has difficulty bearing weight. Recent imaging is shown in Figures A through C. Estimated survival time is 1 year. What is the best next step?

## Options

1. Functional bracing
2. Long leg cast
3. Radiation therapy
4. Chemotherapy
5. Surgery

## Correct option

**5. Surgery**

## Answer explanation

This patient has metastatic carcinoma to the tibia with pathological fracture. As metastatic thyroid carcinoma is often associated with lengthy survival, surgical stabilization, such as with an intramedullary nail, is recommended.

Pathological fractures of the tibia have to be treated differently from conventional trauma. While a minimally displaced tibia fracture may heal with nonoperative treatment (bracing, casting), pathological fractures progress instead of heal. Surgery should involve placement of a construct that will allow immediate unrestricted weightbearing, while protecting the entire bone from fracture.

*Biermann et al. reviewed metastatic bone disease.* **They emphasize** that these are perpetually mechanically unstable bones with large, non-regenerating defects and that fixation constructs should be immediately stable for full weightbearing. Causes of construct failure include incomplete debulking, inadequate fixation, failure to fix the entire bone, use of degradable substances instead of cement, or use of familiar implants and techniques instead of ones appropriate for fixation of pathologic fractures.

Coleman discussed the skeletal complications of malignancy. He states that median survival is 20 months for skeletal metastases from breast carcinoma, 53 months for prostate carcinoma, and 2-3 years for multiple myeloma. He also states the that major complications of skeletal metastases include hypercalcemia (19%), pathological fracture (19%), spinal cord compression (10%), and bone marrow failure (9%).

Figure A shows a pathologic fracture at the diaphysis of the left tibia from an osteolytic lesion in the medullary cavity. Figure B is a T1-weighted magnetic resonance image showing several tibial lesions associated with clinically significant cortical erosion. Figure C is a T1-weighted magnetic resonance image demonstrating a hypointense lesion with endosteal scalloping of the tibia and cortical erosion.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1 and 2: This patient has a pathological fracture which will not go on to heal without surgical stabilization.
Answers 3 and 4: While radiation therapy is an important adjunct following surgical stabilization (adjuvant), radiation therapy alone or chemotherapy alone will not provide immediate stability to the fracture to allow weightbearing. Preoperative radiation therapy (neoadjuvant) increases the risk of wound complications.
