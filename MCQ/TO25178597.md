Chapter: Pediatric Orthopedics MCQs — Cerebral Palsy (CP)

## Question

In a GMFCS Level II patient with crouch gait and a fixed knee flexion deformity (FKFD) of , which surgical intervention is most appropriate to restore the knee extension moment arm?

## Options

1. Guided growth using anterior tension band plating
2. Fractional lengthening of the hamstrings only
3. DFEO combined with patellar tendon advancement/placation
4. Isolated distal femoral extension osteotomy (DFEO)

## Correct option

**3. DFEO combined with patellar tendon advancement/placation**

## Answer explanation

DFEO corrects the FKFD, while patellar
advancement addresses the concomitant patella alta to optimize the quadriceps'
mechanical advantage.

Principles of knee
deformity correction in CP
Management of Flexion Contracture
(Crouch Gait)
Crouch gait is characterized by excessive
knee flexion during the stance phase, often driven by weak plantaflexors (the
plantarflexion-knee extension couple failure) or tight hamstrings.

 - Hamstring Lengthening: Indicated
 when the popliteal angle is >45–50°. However, isolated medial hamstring
 lengthening (semitendinosus/gracilis) is preferred over lateral
 lengthening to avoid increasing external tibial torsion and lumbar
 lordosis.

 - Distal Femoral Extension Osteotomy (DFEO): For fixed knee flexion deformities (FKFD) >15°. A
 supracondylar wedge is removed to bring the joint into neutral extension.

 - Patellar Advancement: Crucial in
 older children. Chronic crouch leads to patellar tendon elongation
 (patella alta). After DFEO or hamstring lengthening, the patella often
 sits too high to effectively transmit force from the quadriceps.
 Shortening the patellar tendon (advancement) restores the mechanical
 advantage of the extensor mechanism.

Addressing Stiff-Knee Gait (Swing Phase
Pathology)
Stiff-knee gait is defined by decreased
peak knee flexion during the swing phase, leading to clearance issues
(tripping/circumduction).

 - Rectus Femoris Transfer (RFT): This
 is the gold standard for "swing phase stiffness" caused by
 rectus femoris spasticity. The distal tendon of the rectus femoris is
 detached from the patella and transferred to the gracilis or
 semitendinosus. This converts the muscle from a knee extensor to a knee
 flexor (or at least removes the extensor restraint), facilitating better
 swing-phase ground clearance.

The "Lever-Arm" Principle
In CP, multi-level bony deformities often
compromise muscle efficiency. Correction must include:

 - Derotation Osteotomies: Correcting
 femoral anteversion or external tibial torsion to ensure the muscles act
 in the sagittal plane.

 - Foot Stabilization: Ensuring a
 stable, plantigrade foot to allow the gastroc-soleus complex to provide
 the necessary extension moment at the knee during mid-stance.

Guided Growth (Hemi-epiphysiodesis)
In younger patients with significant
remaining growth (typically <11–12 years), anterior distal femoral stapling
or "eight-plates" can be used to gradually correct FKFD. This is less
invasive than DFEO but requires careful monitoring to prevent overcorrection or
permanent physeal arrest.
Post-Operative Priorities

 - Early Weight Bearing: Vital to
 prevent further muscle atrophy and osteopenia.

 - Extension Bracing: Night-time knee
 immobilizers are mandatory for years post-correction to prevent the
 recurrence of contractures during growth spurts.

Answer: 3
