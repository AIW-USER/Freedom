## Question

A 12-year-old female is referred to your clinic by her pediatrician for scoliosis. Standing scoliosis radiographs demonstrate a 35 degree right thoracic curve and a Risser stage of 0. She reports that she is premenarchal. After discussing treatment options with her and her parents, the decision is made to proceed with bracing. What is the minimum number of hours of bracing during the day that will maximize her chances of success?

## Options

1. 4 hours
2. 6 hours
3. 13 hours
4. 18 hours
5. 24 hours

## Correct option

**3. 13 hours**

## Answer explanation

To achieve a success rate of 90%, braces should be worn at least 12.9 hours daily. This patient is skeletally immature (based on her Risser stage and menstrual history) and her curve falls between 25 and 45 degrees, making her a candidate for bracing.

Bracing success in adolescent idiopathic scoliosis (AIS) is defined as preventing curve progression to a surgical threshold (50 degrees). Bracing can be effective in children who are not skeletally mature and have flexible curves. Typically, females reach skeletal maturity 18 months following menarche. Bracing is not intended to correct scoliosis but to prevent curve progression. Poor in-brace correction, hypokyphosis, male gender, obesity, and noncompliance are all associated with a poor prognosis.

*Weinstein et al. conducted a prospective cohort study (Bracing in Adolescent Idiopathic Scoliosis, also known as the BrAIST trial) looking at the effectiveness of bracing for appropriate candidates with AIS.* *Patients were asked to wear their brace for at least 18 hours a day with treatment failure defined as curve progression to 50 degrees or more and treatment success defined as curve progression to less than 50 degrees at skeletal maturity.* *The study identified a dose-dependent relationship, and brace wear for an average of at least 12.9 hours per day was associated with success rates of >90%.* Overall, bracing demonstrated a 72% success rate versus 48% with observation alone.

*Rahman et al. designed a prospective trial to demonstrate the efficacy of using a temperature sensing monitor (Cricket) to determine brace wear compliance.* *Patients were asked to record the amount of time they spent daily in their braces in a diary, and this was compared to the data obtained by the Cricket monitor.* The Cricket was found be a reliable, accurate, and sensitive method to determine brace compliance.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1 and 2: Braces should be worn for a minimum of 12.9 hours a day to achieve a 90% success rate based on the BrAIST trial.
Answers 4 and 5: Diminishing returns are associated with brace wear beyond 12.9 hours, as bracing success is 93% when worn longer than 17.7 hours a day.
