## Question

A 32-year-old male is brought to the trauma bay by EMS after a motorcycle accident with multiple long bone fractures, soft tissue abrasions, and lacerations. A cervical collar which was placed by EMS in the field and is well-positioned. On initial examination, he is saturating well on room air with a blood pressure at 145/87 mm Hg. He is currently GCS 9 and unable to participate in a complete neurologic examination. There are no step-offs on palpation of the cervical spinous processes. What would be the most appropriate next step in the evaluation of the cervical spine?

## Options

1. Remove the cervical collar
2. No imaging required but maintain the C-collar until an examination can be performed while the patient is alert and oriented
3. AP, lateral, and open-mouth odontoid to include the superior endplate of C7
4. Flexion and extension cervical spine radiographs
5. CT scan of the cervical spine to include the inferior endplate of T1

## Correct option

**5. CT scan of the cervical spine to include the inferior endplate of T1**

## Answer explanation

Obtunded, multi-trauma patients with concern for a cervical spine injury should be evaluated radiographically by either a CT scan to include the level of the inferior endplate of T1 or radiographs to include a lateral view that includes the superior endplate of T1, an AP, and an open mouth odontoid view.

Cervical spine trauma in patients with altered mental status cannot be taken lightly as missed injuries can lead to permanent paralysis and disability. In order to avoid missing these injuries, one should assume all trauma patients have a cervical spine injury until proven otherwise. Several protocols have been proposed for cervical spine clearance, which includes physical exam findings and radiographic evaluation. Initial radiographic evaluation can include cervical spine radiographs or CT scans. However, several studies have reported CT to have a higher sensitivity of detecting injuries and may be quicker to obtain compared to traditional AP and lateral radiographs.

*Chiu et al. performed a retrospective study of incidence and evaluation of cervical spine ligamentous injury in blunt trauma patients.* *They reported on 143 obtunded patients with cervical spine injuries, of which 14 were found to have purely ligamentous injuries that were initially diagnosed with standard AP and lateral cervical spine radiographs.* **They concluded** that isolated ligamentous cervical spine injuries are rare and could be effectively managed using the Eastern Association for the Surgery of Trauma (EAST) guidelines.

*Sanchez et al. performed a retrospective study of a cervical spine clearance protocol consisting of a clinical examination, CT, and MRI in obtunded patients presenting with blunt trauma.* *They reported on 99 patients with cervical spine injuries that were evaluated with the protocol and described sensitivity of 99%, a specificity of 100%, and a 0.04% risk of missing a cervical spine injury.* **They concluded** that their protocol had high sensitivity and specificity while enabling early cervical spine clearance.

Nunez provided a commentary on a study that evaluated the use of CT for the detection of cervical spine injuries. The author supported the use of CT clearance due to increased specificity compared to radiographs and cost-effectiveness.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: In an obtunded patient, removing the cervical collar prior to proper C-spine clearance protocols and imaging places the patient at an increased risk for neurologic injury from an unrecognized unstable spinal injury.
Answer 2: Despite the patient being immobilized and a cervical collar, advanced imaging studies, such as a CT, should be performed to detect subtle injuries.
Answer 3: Cervical spine radiographs are helpful in detecting spinal injuries, but an adequate radiographic study would include the T1 vertebral body. Additionally, there is a lower sensitivity rate of detecting spinal injuries with radiographs compared to CT.
Answer 4: Flexion and extension radiographs should not be performed as an initial screening study. Rather, this can be performed to ensure there is no occult instability after cervical spine clearance.
