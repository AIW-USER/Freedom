## Question

A patient presents to your office with complaints of long-standing and worsening wrist pain. The patient has tried an extensive course of immobilization but remains symptomatic. Imaging reveals avascular necrosis of the capitate. When discussing treatment options for this condition, which of the following is an appropriate salvage procedure option for in this patient?

## Options

1. Midcarpal fusion
2. Proximal row carpectomy
3. Radial wedge osteotomy
4. Temporary scaphotrapeziotrapezoidal pinning
5. Total wrist arthroplasty

## Correct option

**1. Midcarpal fusion**

## Answer explanation

This patient has avascular necrosis of the capitate and has failed nonoperative management with immobilization. A midcarpal fusion is a salvage procedure for this condition that addresses the pain associated with the midcarpal arthritic changes.

Avascular necrosis (AVN) of the capitate is a relatively uncommon condition. The etiology of AVN of the capitate is believed to be secondary to trauma, abnormal interosseous vascular supply, and/or hypermobility. Initial nonoperative management consists of immobilization. In patients who have failed nonoperative management, surgical treatment is considered. A midcarpal fusion is a salvage procedure and is the most appropriate option given to address the pain associated with the midcarpal arthritic changes.

*Peters et al. reviewed for avascular necrosis of the capitate.* *They reported on patients treated with scaphocapitolunate arthrodesis or four corner arthrodesis.* **They concluded** that better results were seen when the arthrodesis fused.

*Hattori et al. reviewed avascular necrosis of the capitate.* **They reported** that this rare disorder has an unknown etiology that causes wrist pain and limitation of function. **They concluded** that a vascularized pedicled bone graft from the dorsal aspect of the distal radius may be an appropriate treatment in select patients.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Proximal row carpectomy (PRC) is indicated in stage IIIB disease or stage IV disease for Kienbock's disease (AVN of the lunate)
Answer 3: Radial wedge osteotomy is indicated in stage I, II, IIIA Kienbock's disease with ulnar positive or neutral variance
Answer 4: Temporary scaphotrapeziotrapezoidal pinning is indicated for an adolescent with radiographic evidence of Kienbock's and progressive wrist pain
Answer 5: Total wrist arthroplasty is not indicated in patients with isolated capitate avascular necrosis
