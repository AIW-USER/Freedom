Chapter: Arthroplasty MCQs — Wear And Osteolysis (Knee)

## Question

Which factor does not reduce back side wear in total knee arthroplasty?

## Options

1. Polished surface of tibial tray
2. Rough surface of tibial tray to improve locking
3. Robust locking mechanism of tibial insert
4. Monoblock design

## Correct option

**2. Rough surface of tibial tray to improve locking**

## Answer explanation

The primary wear process in knee arthroplasty takes place at the articulating sliding surfaces of the joint. In addition to primary wear, secondary wear processes can also due to micro-movement between a tibial tray and a tibial insert causing wear on the backside of the PE insert. In total knee replacement, osteolysis is often observed below the tibial tray and in the area of tibial screw holes. It is assumed that a clinically relevant degree of PE backside wear is generated, which is responsible for the osteolysis that occurs in these areas.

A number of factors affect the propensity for backside wear. These include

- The design of the articular surfaces-It affects the stress distribution through the insert
- The surface finish of the metallic tray in contact with the insert- It affects abrasive and adhesive polyethylene wear and the implantation time of the insert. Studies have also shown that polished surfaces of tibial trays show reduced backside PE damage compared to non-polished surfaces.
- The mechanism used to lock the insert in the tray- Because wear requires micromotion, the locking mechanism may be the most important factor.

Studies have shown that a robust locking mechanism decreases backside wear. The partial peripheral capture with anterior constraint/central antirotational iland and the tongue-in-groove locking mechanism show lower polyethylene linear wear rates compared with baseplate design with only a peripheral-rim capture.

- Patient weight- obese patients have more wear rate.
- Modularity - Adding a polyethylene-metal interface by making it modular leads to more backside wear. Modular meal tibial base plates offer he advantage of customization of insert size but at the cost of increased rates of osteolysis and backside polyethylene wear due to micromotion between tibial baseplate and undersurface of polyethylene that occurs during loading. Compared to modular metal back tibial base plate, monoblock tibial component designs have less backside wear but with challenge of choosing size prior to cementing.

Answer : b
