## Question

A mother brings her 5-year-old daughter to clinic for evaluation of an abnormal gait and increased falls. On exam, she ambulates with an internal foot progression angle of approximately 25° with her knees in line with her feet. Which of the following exam findings is likely present?

## Options

1. Abnormal Galeazzi test
2. Abnormal thigh-foot angle
3. Abnormal heel bisector line
4. Increased hip internal rotation
5. Increased hip external rotation

## Correct option

**4. Increased hip internal rotation**

## Answer explanation

An in-toeing gait secondary to increased femoral anteversion is characterized by excessive internal rotation (>70°) and diminished external rotation. Abnormalities in the thigh-foot angle (-5° to +20°) and heel bisector line (normal passing through the 2nd and 3rd toe webspace) are due to tibial torsion and metatarsus adductus, respectively.

In-toeing gait in children is one of the most common conditions seen in pediatric orthopaedics. It can be caused by excessive femoral anteversion, internal tibial torsion or metatarsus adductus. Increased femoral anteversion resulting in an intoeing gait presents in early childhood, ages 3-6 years, and often resolves spontaneously by 10-13 years of age. Observation is most appropriate, as numerous modalities including bracing, orthotics, physical therapy, and sitting restrictions have been shown to have no effect on the natural history of the condition.

*Blackmur et al. performed a retrospective review of 202 patients referred to a pediatric orthopaedic department for evaluation of in-toeing.* **The authors concluded** that 86% of children were discharged after their first visit and no significant pathology was identified in any of the patients. Management and outcome for these children were not affected by referral to the orthopaedic clinic.

*Lincoln et al. authored a review article detailing the common rotational variations in children.* In-toeing is generally attributable to metatarsus adductus in the infant, internal tibial torsion in the toddler, and femoral anteversion in children younger than 10 years. Increased femoral anteversion tends to occur in females and is symmetric. Frequently, no treatment is necessary; however, surgical intervention may be indicated in a child older than 8 years with a marked cosmetic or functional deformity, anteversion >50°, and internal hip rotation >80°.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The patient's abnormal gait is not secondary to a leg length discrepancy; therefore, a normal Galeazzi test is expected.
Answers 2: Thigh-foot angle < -10° is diagnostic of internal tibial torsion, which can also result in an in-toeing gait. This patient's abnormal gait is due to excessive femoral anteversion without associated internal tibial torsion, as her knees are in line with her feet. Therefore we expect the thigh-foot angle to be normal (-5° to +20°).
Answer 3: A heel bisector line passing through the 3rd and 4th toe webspace is suggestive of metatarsus adductus, another cause of in-toeing gait. This patient's in-toeing gait is due to excessive femoral anteversion and therefore we expect a normal heel bisector line that passes through the 2nd and 3rd toe webspace.
Answer 5: Femoral anteversion is characterized by decreased hip external rotation.
