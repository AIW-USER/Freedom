## Question

A 68-year-old male is brought to the operating room for revision of a total knee arthroplasty (TKA). The patient is 7 years status-post his index procedure. Preoperative evaluation and intraoperative findings are consistent with aseptic loosening. The patient is 130 kg and has a negative preoperative nasal MRSA screen. He reports being told as a child that he is allergic to cephalosporin antibiotics, but cannot state what reaction he exhibits. Due to his cardiac history, you plan on admitting the patient for at least 24 hours following surgery. Which of the following statements is most accurate regarding the optimal antibiotic prophylaxis for this patient?

## Options

1. A single weight-based perioperative dose of cefazolin would be the appropriate antibiotic prophylaxis for this patient
2. Given the patients allergy history, his prophylaxis should consist of non-cephalosporin antibiotics
3. In the aseptic revision setting, there is a morbidity and mortality benefit to administering weight-dosed antibiotics for 48 hours following surgery
4. The patient should receive a single weight-based perioperative dose of cefazolin, followed by three additional weight-based doses for 24 hours
5. There is no proven benefit to weight-based dosing of perioperative antibiotics, and this patient should receive 2 grams of cefazolin perioperatively, followed by three additional 2 gram doses for 24 hours

## Correct option

**4. The patient should receive a single weight-based perioperative dose of cefazolin, followed by three additional weight-based doses for 24 hours**

## Answer explanation

This patient is undergoing revision for aseptic loosening. The appropriate antibiotic prophylaxis for this patient is a single weight-based dose perioperatively, followed by three additional weight-based doses every 8 hours for 24 hours (Answer 4). Given the patient's weight of 130kg, he should be given 3-gram doses.

Prosthetic joint infection (PJI) is a catastrophic complication following joint replacement and represents a common cause of revision arthroplasty. The incidence of PJI following primary joint replacement is 1-2% for total knee arthroplasty (TKA) and 0.3-1.3% for total hip arthroplasty (THA). In the revision setting, these incidences increase to 5-6% and 3-4%, respectively. A variety of risk factors of PJI have been identified, and much research has been performed on the optimal mitigation strategies.

Regarding optimal antibiotic prophylaxis, a few critical concepts have emerged. First, weight-based dosing should be utilized, as research has demonstrated that patients with a body weight > 120kg have historically been underdosed. Second, while evidence suggests that in the primary setting, a single dose of weight-based antibiotics may suffice, 24 hours of weight-based antibiotics should be utilized in the aseptic revision setting. In addition, in the aseptic revision setting, there is no benefit to extended antibiotic prophylaxis (24 to 48 hours). Finally, allergy testing should be utilized before administrating non-cephalosporin antibiotics in patients with a reported cephalosporin allergy, as there is a demonstrated morbidity and mortality benefit to cephalosporin antibiotics over alternative therapies.

*Kuo et al. investigated the possible benefit of extended postoperative antibiotic prophylaxis with first-generation cephalosporins following aseptic revision TKA.* *Their study included 236 patients, 76 who received ≤24 hours of antibiotics and 176 who received > 24 hours of antibiotics.* While patients who received extended prophylaxis demonstrated a lower PJI rate (1.1% vs. 3.9%), this comparison did not reach statistical significance. At 10-year follow-up, the two groups had similar infection-free implant survival. Overall, **the authors concluded** that there was no benefit to extended antibiotic prophylaxis.

*Kuo et al. also investigated the possible benefit of extended postoperative antibiotic prophylaxis with first-generation cephalosporins following aseptic revision THA.* *The authors matched 209 patients undergoing revision THA who received > 24 hours of antibiotics with 209 patients who received ≤24 hours of antibiotics.* These cohorts were matched based on age, sex, body mass index, Charlson comorbidity index, and operative time. The incidence of subsequent PJI was 4.8% in patients receiving extended prophylaxis versus 2.4% in patients receiving standard prophylaxis, and both cohorts demonstrated comparable infection-free survival rates. Overall, **the authors concluded** that there was no benefit to extended antibiotic prophylaxis.

*Rondon et al. investigated the effect of weight-based dosing on PJI.* *Their study included 17,393 primary joint arthroplasty patients who received cefazolin for perioperative prophylaxis.* *The patients were stratified into 2 groups (adequately dosed or underdosed) based on the patient weight and antibiotic dosage.* **The authors found** that the majority (95.9%) of patients weighing greater than 120 kg were underdosed and that these patients had a higher rate of PJI at one year compared with adequately dosed patients (1.51% vs. 0.86%, P = 0.002). Overall, **the authors concluded** that cefazolin underdosing is common, especially for patients weighing more than 120kg, and underdosed patients were more likely to develop PJI. As a result, patients less than 60 kg should receive 1 gram of cefazolin per dose, patients between 60 and 120 kg should receive 2 grams per dose, and patients > 120 kg should receive three grams per dose.

*Wyles et al. in their 2019 Charnley award-winning paper investigated the rate of PJI when cefazolin alternative antibiotic prophylaxis was used.* **Their study identified** 29,295 primary arthroplasties in 22,705 patients, of whom 2,576 underwent preoperative allergy testing. Of the 2,576 allergy-tested patients, who reported a history of allergy to either penicillin or cephalosporin antibiotics, 97% were cleared to receive cephalosporin antibiotics. Overall, the risk of PJI was 32% lower in patients treated with cefazolin, with the number needed to treat to prevent one PJI being 164 patients at one year and 84 patients at ten years. **The authors conclude** that in a hypothetical cohort of 1,000,000 primary THA/TKA patients, 6098 PJIS could be prevented at one year and 11,905 by ten years through the appropriate use of preoperative allergy testing and appropriate administration of cefazolin.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: In the aseptic revision setting, the appropriate antibiotic prophylaxis is a single weight-based dose of cefazolin within 30 minutes of incision, with three additional weight-based doses every 8 hours for 24 hours.
Answer 2: Patients with a reported allergy to cephalosporin or penicillin antibiotics should undergo preoperative allergy testing.
Answer 3: In the aseptic revision setting, there is no benefit to extended antibiotic prophylaxis.
Answer 5: Patients with weights > 120 kg are frequently underdosed and placed at a higher risk for PJI.
