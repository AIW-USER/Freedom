## Question

Figure A is the MRI of a 74-year-old female who complains of shoulder pain and decreased motion for the past 3 years. She has completed a course of physical therapy with only mild improvement in symptoms. On examination she is found to have limited motion with active forward elevation and external rotation of 45° and 25°, respectively. She has a negative belly press sign. Which of the following would favor the diagnosis of pseudoparesis over pseudoparalysis in this patient?

![[q213973-a-mri-supra-tear.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Advanced glenohumeral arthritis with glenoid wear
2. Active forward elevation of 100° following an injection
3. No improvement in motion following an injection
4. Presence of a concomitant subscapularis tear on MRI
5. Retraction of the supraspinatus tendon medial to the glenoid

## Correct option

**2. Active forward elevation of 100° following an injection**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a massive rotator cuff tear with limitations in motion. Following an injection, she has significant improvement in forward elevation favoring the diagnosis of pseudoparesis over pseudoparalysis.

Patients with massive rotator cuff tears often present with severe limitations in motion. Distinguishing the causality of these limitations is imperative. Patients with pseudoparalysis have the inability to actively elevate the arm above 90° but retain full passive motion; these patients will not have a significant improvement in motion following a lidocaine injection. Conversely, pseudoparesis is typically a pain-related inability to raise the arm. Following an injection, the patient experiences relief in pain and will demonstrate improvement in motion past 90 degrees.

*Collin et al. evaluated the effect of tear size and location on active motion in patients with chronic and massive rotator cuff tears with severe muscle degeneration.* **They found** that pseudoparalysis was evident in 80% of the cases with supraspinatus and complete subscapularis tears and in 45% of the cases with tears involving the supraspinatus, infraspinatus, and superior subscapularis. **They concluded** that dysfunction of the entire subscapularis and supraspinatus or 3 rotator cuff muscles is a risk factor for pseudoparalysis.

*Tokish et al. reviews the literature regarding pseudoparalysis.* **They found** that the most consistent definition was a massive rotator cuff tear with active elevation less than 90°. **They concluded** that pseudoparalysis is poorly defined in the literature and they propose an algorithm to serve as a treatment guideline to aid in surgical decision making for this condition.

Figure A is the coronal MRI image demonstrating a massive rotator cuff tear with retraction of the supraspinatus tendon. Illustration A is a proposed treatment algorithm for pseudoparalysis.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1&5: These are common findings in both patients with pseudoparalysis and pseudoparesis
Answer 3: No improvement following a cortisone injection would favor the diagnosis of pseudoparalysis over pseudoparesis
Answer 4: The presence of a complete subscapularis tear along with a supraspinatus tear was found to have an 80% incidence of pseuoparalysis
