## Question

She states that she rolled her ankle on a hike and has not been able to walk since that time without the assistance of crutches. ## Options

1. Early functional rehabilitation; primary focus on proprioception
2. Brostrom procedure; primary repair and imbrication of torn ligaments
3. Brostrom-Gould procedure; primary repair augmented with the extensor retinaculum
4. Watson-Jones procedure; tenodesis reconstruction using weaved peroneus brevis tendon
5. Evans procedure; tenodesis reconstruction using non-weaved peroneus brevis tendon

## Correct option

**1. Early functional rehabilitation; primary focus on proprioception**

## Answer explanation

This patient presents with an acute injury to the anterior talofibular ligament confirmed on physical examination and MRI. Both acute injuries and chronic lateral ankle instability should be treated with a trial of nonoperative management to include early functional rehabilitation focusing on proprioception.

The lateral ankle has three primary ligaments that impart stability (Illustration A). The anterior talofibular ligament (ATFL) is the weakest ligament followed by the calcaneofibular (CFL) and the posterior talofibular ligament (PTFL), respectively. Inversion ankle injuries are common and may result in ligamentous injuries. The anterior drawer test may be performed in neutral/plantar flexion to assess the integrity of the ATFL, whereas it may be performed in dorsiflexion to assess the integrity of the CFL. Excessive ankle inversion (>15 degrees) compared to the uninjured contralateral side is suggestive of both ATFL and CFL disruption. The PTFL is seldom disrupted. Nonoperative management serves as the primary treatment modality in both acute and chronic injuries, regardless of the degree of injury. Early mobilization facilitates faster recovery compared to periods of immobilization. Functional rehabilitation should focus on range of motion and proprioceptive exercises with gradual incorporation of strength training once acute pain/swelling subsides. If patients fail this conservative management, they meet indications to undergo surgical stabilization. At this time, no operative technique has demonstrated clear superiority in outcomes.

*Maffulli and colleagues presented a comprehensive review article on the management of acute and chronic ankle instability.* **They discuss** that obtaining MRI (Illustration B) is usually only indicated in the setting of chronic ankle instability (minimum 6-8 weeks after the initial inversion injury and/or after failed nonoperative management) to assess for any associated injuries during the preoperative planning process (i.e. peroneal injuries or talus osteochondral lesion). They further review different operative repair/reconstruction techniques and conclude by focusing on functional rehabilitation as the primary treatment modality.

*Al-Mohrej and Al-Kenani presented an updated comprehensive review article on the management of acute and chronic ankle sprains.* *The authors discuss the epidemiology, anatomic considerations, and classifications of these injuries followed by recommended consensus management.* **They conclude** by emphasizing early involvement in a functional rehabilitation program after injury to control pain and swelling, improve and maintain range of motion, and minimize the risk of stiffness and muscle wasting.

Figure A displays axial T1 and T2 MRI ankle images with the absence of the ATFL where it would normally be expected (i.e. distal fibula and talus, NOT at the level of the syndesmosis between tibia and fibula). Figure B is a lateral ankle radiograph demonstrating a subtle cavus foot deformity, which serves as a risk factor for recurrent inversion injuries. Illustration A is a diagram of the lateral ankle ligaments imparting stability. Illustration B demonstrates T1 axial MRI imaging with an intact ATFL.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2-5: Even in the setting of chronic ankle instability, primary repair of torn ATFL/CFL ligaments is generally possible as the ligaments remain attached to the underlying capsule. The primary repair can be augmented by incorporating the extensor retinaculum (Gould modification) and a fibular periosteal flap. Autograft/allograft augmentation or internal brace reconstruction procedures are generally reserved for failed primary repair cases. Tenodesis techniques are generally discouraged as they alter normal ankle biomechanics. Ultimately, no technique has demonstrated superiority despite multiple randomized control trials and systematic reviews with meta-analysis.
