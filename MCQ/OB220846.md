## Question

A 10-year-old male with a known history of Spondyloepiphyseal Dysplasia Congenita (SEDC) presents with a progressive change in his gait. While he has always had a waddling gait due to bilateral coxa vara, his parents report that he is now more unsteady and has begun stumbling. Physical examination reveals hyperreflexia in his lower extremities, a positive Hoffman's sign, and decreased dexterity in his hands. Based on his known diagnosis, which of the following is the MOST likely underlying cause of this patient's new neurological findings?

## Options

1. Cervical myelopathy due to atlantoaxial instability.
2. Progression of his thoracolumbar scoliosis.
3. Tethered spinal cord syndrome.
4. Vertebral compression fractures from a fall.
5. Waddling gait due to bilateral coxa vara.

## Correct option

**1. Cervical myelopathy due to atlantoaxial instability.**

## Answer explanation

The classic signs of cervical myelopathy (hyperreflexia, Hoffman's sign, gait change) in a patient with SEDC are most likely caused by craniocervical instability from odontoid hypoplasia (answer 1).

Spondyloepiphyseal dysplasia congenita (SEDC) is a skeletal dysplasia with a classic constellation of findings, including dwarfism, flattened facies, and a waddling gait. A critical and life-threatening complication of this condition is cervical instability, which arises from odontoid hypoplasia or an os odontoideum. This instability can lead to progressive spinal cord compression, known as cervical myelopathy, which is manifested by the neurological signs described in the case. These findings necessitate urgent evaluation and often require surgical stabilization to prevent permanent neurological deficit.

*Miyoshi et al. evaluated the surgical treatment of atlantoaxial subluxation with myelopathy in patients with spondyloepiphyseal dysplasia congenita.* **They noted** that odontoid hypoplasia was a primary cause of instability, and that posterior atlantoaxial fixation and fusion was an effective surgical treatment for this patient population. **They concluded** that surgical stabilization is a safe and effective method to prevent progression of myelopathy in these patients.

*Falls et al. reviewed the literature and presented two cases on the management of craniocervical instability in spondyloepiphyseal dysplasia congenita.* **They noted** that the odontoid hypoplasia and subsequent instability can lead to devastating neurological outcomes if not addressed. **They concluded** that regular screening and surgical stabilization of the craniovertebral junction are critical for preventing neurological deterioration in this high-risk population.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: While scoliosis is a common finding in SEDC, it typically causes trunk asymmetry and is a less likely cause of upper and lower extremity myelopathic signs.
Answer 3: Tethered cord can cause neurological symptoms, but it is not a hallmark finding of SEDC, and atlantoaxial instability is a more specific and likely cause in this patient.
Answer 4: This patient's symptoms are progressive and not related to a recent traumatic event, making an acute fracture an unlikely cause.
Answer 5: The patient's waddling gait is a known, chronic symptom of his coxa vara, but it does not account for the new and progressive signs of upper motor neuron dysfunction (hyperreflexia, Hoffman's sign, decreased dexterity).
