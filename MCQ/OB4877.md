## Question

A 32-year-old man undergoes a proximal femoral replacement as part of his care for a sarcoma of the proximal femur. Three-months postoperatively, he returns for a routine follow-up office visit. Which physical exam finding would most likely be positive?

![[q4877-a-fig-2.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Trendelenburg sign
2. Flexion, adduction and internal rotation test
3. Ober test
4. Patrick test
5. Thomas test

## Correct option

**1. Trendelenburg sign**

## Answer explanation

This patient had a lesion of the proximal femur that was treated with resection and reconstruction with a megaprosthesis. Megaprosthesis placement requires detachment of abductor musculature and reattachment to the prosthesis. This leaves residual abductor weakness which would lead to a positive Trendelenburg sign.

Gluteus medius inserts in the greater trochanter and is the major abductor of the hip. A deficiency on the stance side leads to the pelvis dropping on the contralateral side on a single-leg stance, and shifting of the center of gravity over the stance leg. With proximal femoral replacement, the inability of the host abductor tendon to heal to metal leaves the patient with a Trendelenberg gait and positive Trendelenburg sign and a high risk of hip instability.

*Parvizi et al. reviewed the use of a megaprosthesis in non-neoplastic conditions.* **They recommend** megaprosthesis for massive proximal bone loss, as long as there's sufficient distal femoral length to secure the stem. **They found** that common complications include instability because of deficient abductors from repeat surgeries, poor healing of abductors to metal, wrong leg length, and inadequate soft tissue tension.

*Giurea et al. examined the function of reinserted abductors after femoral replacement.* **They advocate** direct fixation of the gluteus medius to the prosthesis (with a trochanteric bone block) for improved outcomes, compared with fixation to the iliotibial band.

Figure A shows reconstruction with a megaprosthesis. Illustration A shows reattachment of the gluteus medius tendon with a greater trochanter bone block. Illustration V is a video showing the Trendelenburg sign.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: The flexion, adduction and internal rotation test detects hip impingement.
Answer 3: The Ober test examines for iliotibial band tightness. In the lateral position, the knee is flexed with the hip abducted and extended. Inability to adduct beyond the midline indicates a tight iliotibial band.
Answer 4: The Patrick test examines for hip or SI joint pathology. While supine, the hip is flexed, abducted, externally rotated, and extended. If pain is elicited in the groin, it suggests hip joint pathology. If pain is elicited in the SI joint, it suggests SI joint dysfunction.
Answer 5: The Thomas test examines for hip flexion contracture.
