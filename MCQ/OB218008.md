## Question

You see a 76-year-old female with Alzheizmers disease in the office as an ER follow-up after she sustained her 3rd posterior hip dislocation by bending over to tie her shoe. Her initial total hip arthroplasty (THA) was done by an outside surgeon in a different state. On radiographic evaluation of her components, you note that her cup is positioned in 42° of abduction and 5° of anteversion. Both components appear well fixed without evidence of loosening. There are no signs of infection. You discuss revision THA with the patient and her family in order to improve stability. For which of the following reasons would you want to avoid using a constrained liner intraoperatively?

## Options

1. Multiple dislocations
2. Component malposition
3. Prior posterior approach
4. Abductor insufficiency
5. History of Alzheimers

## Correct option

**2. Component malposition**

## Answer explanation

Constrained liners should be reserved for patients who have recurrent dislocations with well positioned and fixed components.

Many factors may lead to prosthetic hip dislocation, most common of which is malpositioning of components. In a patient with recurrent dislocations, one must confirm that both the acetabular and femoral components are positioned appropriately. While each patient is different, the acetabular component should be positioned with about 40 degrees of abduction and 20 degrees of anteversion. Too much anteversion of the acetabular component leads to anterior dislocation, whereas too much retroversion leads to posterior instability. If the components are appropriately positioned, the surgeon should then try to gain stability with changing other modular parts of the construct (ie. larger femoral head, more offset, dual mobility etc). If these fail to provide a stable hip and the components are well positioned, then a constrained liner is appropriate.

*Jones et al review the use of constrained liners in revision THA.* *They report on 38 studies evaluating a total of 2852 constrained liners with a mean follow-up 4.3 years.* *They had a mean dislocation and/or constrained failure rate of 11.4%.* **They conclude** that the implantation of a constrained liner should be considered when all other factors related to the total hip arthroplasty have been optimized, especially component malposition.

*Hailer et al review the use of dual mobility cups for revision total hip arthroplasty.* *They report on 228 cup revisions in which a dual mobility liner was inserted in patients with recurrent hip instability.* Overall there was a 2% re-revision rate for instability in this cohort. **They conclude** that dual mobility constructs when used during cup revisions may lessen the risk of dislocation following revision surgery.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: An increasing number of dislocations would be a reason to consider using a constrained liner.
Answer 3: The use of a constrained liner is acceptable after a posterior approach has been performed, in the setting of well positioned components.
Answer 4: Abductor insufficiency in the setting of well fixed and positioned components is a reason to consider use of a constrained liner.
Answer 5: A history of Alzheimers or neuromuscular conditions would be a reason to consider the use of a constrained liner in the setting of well positioned components.
