## Question

A 55-year-old female undergoes an anatomic total shoulder arthroplasty. For the first 9 months, the patient's postoperative course was uneventful, and she was able to return to playing pickleball at her local country club. At her 1-year follow-up appointment, the patient reports worsening pain with range of motion and subtle lucency around the humeral implant. Her inflammatory labs are normal, but a joint aspiration eventually grows C. acnes. What is the antibiotic of choice for treatment of this pathogen?

## Options

1. Trimethoprim/Sulfamethoxazole
2. Clindamycin
3. Meropenem
4. Penicillin
5. Vancomycin

## Correct option

**4. Penicillin**

## Answer explanation

Penicillin-class antibiotics (Answer 4) are the first-line antibiotic choice for C. acnes periprosthetic shoulder infections.

Cutibacterium acnes is a gram-positive, anaerobic, non-spore-forming, rod-shaped bacterium that is part of the normal skin microbiota, particularly in sebaceous gland-rich areas. It is slow-growing and requires prolonged culture incubation (up to 3 weeks) for reliable detection, making diagnosis of infections challenging. It produces a biofilm, a protective extracellular matrix that enhances its resistance to host immune responses and antibiotics, contributing to its role in periprosthetic joint infections. C. acnes causes chronic, low-grade infections that may present with subtle symptoms, requiring surgical intervention and prolonged antibiotic therapy for effective management. Penicillin-class antibiotics, particularly penicillin G and amoxicillin, are generally effective against C. acnes. In cases where penicillin allergy is a concern, alternatives such as ceftriaxone or clindamycin may be considered. Given the slow-growing nature of C. acnes, extended antibiotic duration is often necessary to prevent recurrence and ensure successful eradication of the infection.

*Paxton and colleagues* **highlighted** the challenges of diagnosing periprosthetic shoulder infections, emphasizing the importance of a multimodal approach, including clinical assessment, serologic markers, synovial fluid analysis, and microbiologic cultures. Their findings underscore the prevalence of Cutibacterium acnes as a common pathogen, often requiring prolonged culture incubation for accurate detection. **The authors conclude** that successful management relies on surgical strategies, including staged revision and targeted antibiotic therapy.

*Garrigues and colleagues summarized the key findings from the 2018 International Consensus Meeting on Orthopedic Infections,* **emphasizing** the need for standardized diagnostic criteria for periprosthetic shoulder infections. Their conclusions highlight the limitations of traditional serologic markers, the importance of prolonged culture incubation for detecting Cutibacterium acnes, and the role of advanced molecular diagnostics. The study underscores that a combination of clinical, laboratory, and microbiological assessments is essential for accurate diagnosis, guiding appropriate surgical and antimicrobial management strategies to improve patient outcomes.

Incorrect answers:
Answers 1, 3, and 5: These antibiotics are not in the first-line antibiotic class of choice for C. acne infections.
Answer 2: Clindamycin can be considered for C. acne infections if the patient is allergic to penicillin.
