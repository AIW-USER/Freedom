## Question

A 75-year-old female undergoes total knee arthroplasty (TKA) using a cruciate retaining implant with a highly cross-linked polyethylene insert. During the case, the femoral component is positioned against the lateral edge of the femoral cut and the center of the tibial tray sits medial to the medial 1/3 of the tibial tubercle. Total thickness of the patella with button is 22mm. Extension and flexion gaps are felt to be balanced intraoperatively. What is the most likely complication that will be seen in this patient?

## Options

1. Flexion instability
2. Osteolysis
3. Patellar maltracking
4. Patellar fracture
5. Patellar clunk

## Correct option

**3. Patellar maltracking**

## Answer explanation

This patient is at risk for patellar maltracking given the inappropriate internal rotation of the tibial tray, which effectively increases the Q-angle.

Patellar maltracking is one of the most common complications after total knee arthroplasty (TKA) and the most common reason for a secondary surgery after TKA. Patellar tracking is effectively altered with any alterations in coronal plane alignment (medial/lateral placement of femur/tibia implants) or rotational alignment (femoral implant or tibial tray rotation). An increase in the Q-angle causes increased lateral pull on the patellar implant and causes it to subluxate or dislocate. In order to ensure alignment is appropriate, the tibial and femoral components should be placed laterally and internal rotation of both components should be avoided. The tibial tray should be aligned with the medial 1/3 of the tibial tubercle in order to prevent excessive internal rotation of the component. The patellar button is the only implant which should be placed more medially, as this effectively lateralizes the patella and decreases the Q-angle.

*Miller et al. reviewed the effect of component positioning on knee kinetics after TKA.* *They specifically reviewed the effects of patellar button medialization and femoral external rotation and noted significantly decreased coronal plane forces with patellar button medialization but not with femoral external rotation.* **They ultimately concluded** that patellar button medialization was more important in improving kinematics than femoral external rotation.

*Rhoads et al. published on the effects of femoral component positioning on TKA kinematics.* **They noted** that internal rotation or medial displacement of the femoral component exaggerated medial patellar displacement, while external rotation of the femur did provide some benefit to patellar tracking, except in deeper flexion >100°. **They concluded** that lateral femoral component positioning was important in providing the most normal patellar tracking mechanics in TKA.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Flexion instability would be unlikely with a CR knee that has well balanced gaps intraoperatively.
Answer 2: With the use of a highly cross-linked polyethylene insert and an appropriately balanced knee, osteolysis and polyethylene wear is unlikely to be the main cause of failure.
Answer 4: With a total thickness of 22mm including the native patella and the button, the patient is not at significant risk for patellar fracture. The average patellar thickness preoperatively is said to be around 21mm and may differ slightly between males and females.
Answer 5: Patellar clunk most often occurs in posterior stabilized (PS) knees, not CR knees.
