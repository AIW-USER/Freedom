## Question

Figures 1a and 1b show the radiographs of a 12-year-old boy who injured his knee while playing basketball. An attempt at closed reduction was unsuccessful. Which of the following is the best treatment option?

## Options

1. Open reduction and internal fixation of the tibial tubercle
2. Arthroscopic-assisted anterior cruciate ligament reconstruction with bone-tendon-bone patella allograft
3. Arthroscopic-assisted anterior cruciate ligament reconstruction with hamstring allograft
4. Arthroscopic-assisted physeal-sparing anterior cruciate ligament reconstruction using iliotibial band autograft
5. Arthroscopic-assisted reduction and internal fixation of the tibial eminence

## Correct option

**5. Arthroscopic-assisted reduction and internal fixation of the tibial eminence**

## Answer explanation

The radiographs show an angulated and displaced tibial eminence fracture in a skeletally immature boy. Avulsion of the tibial spine occurs with the same mechanism as anterior cruciate ligament tears. The fractures are often associated with meniscal entrapment. These fractures are best treated with arthroscopic reduction and fixation. Open reduction of tibial eminence fractures is also an accepted approach. Formal anterior cruciate ligament reconstruction is not recommended as a primary treatment for tibial eminence fractures.
