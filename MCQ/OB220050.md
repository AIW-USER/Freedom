## Question

A 9-month-old male presents with basal metacarpal aplasia and a deficient right 1st carpometacarpal (CMC) joint. Parents note that his deformity has been present and worsening since birth. On exam, motion is absent at the metacarpophalangeal (MCP) and interphalangeal (IP) joints in the right thumb, with underdevelopment of the thenar musculature. Among the answers below, which is the most appropriate definitive treatment option for this patient?

## Options

1. Observation
2. Opposition tendon transfer
3. 1st web space release, opposition transfer, and fusion of 1st MCP joint
4. Stabilization of the 1st MCP joint via reconstruction of UCL with FDS tendon
5. Pollicization

## Correct option

**5. Pollicization**

## Answer explanation

The most appropriate definitive treatment option for this patient with thumb hypoplasia, a deficient right 1st carpometacarpal (CMC) joint, and a mobile index finger is pollicization (Answer 5).

Thumb hypoplasia is a condition characterized as the underdevelopment of the thumb and is frequently associated with partial or complete absence of the radius. This condition is diagnosed clinically with hypoplasia of the thumb and thenar muscles. Treatment consists of observation, opposition transfer, 1st web space deepening, stabilization of MCP joint, and pollicization of the index finger. Treatment depends on the level of deficit and the stability of the 1st CMC joint, with thumb amputation and pollicization required in those with deficient 1st CMC joints.

*Kozin published a review article analyzing the concept, technique, and outcomes of pollicization which essentially substitutes a finger for a deficient thumb.* The most important indication for pollicization is a hypoplastic thumb with a deficient or unstable 1st CMC joint. *Kozin* **noted** this procedure is most reliable in isolated thumb hypoplasia and a mobile index finger with surrounding well-developed musculature.

*Taghinia et al. reviewed index finger pollicization.* Absolute indications for pollicization include hypoplastic thumb Blauth types 3B, 4, and 5, all of which have a deficient 1st CMC joint. **They emphasize** that a short and stiff index finger will create a stiff new thumb following pollicization and can thus be a relative contraindication to this procedure along with older age.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Observation is reserved for minor thumb hypoplasia with all musculature and neurovascular structures present but hypoplastic.
Answer 2: Opposition tendon transfer is utilized in patients with minor hypoplasia, an intact CMC joint, and insufficient thumb abduction.
Answers 3-4: These surgical treatment options are reserved for patients with a hypoplastic thumb, instability at the 1st MCP joint, and an intact 1st CMC joint.
