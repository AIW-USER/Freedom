## Question

Figures A and B are the MRI and CT images of a 28-year-old male 3 weeks following an ACL reconstruction. On examination he has a positive Lachman. Based on these findings, what is the next most appropriate action?

![[q214414-b-ct-bone-plug.jpg]]
*Figure B*

## Options

1. Excision of femoral bone plug
2. No modification to post-operative ACL rehabilitation protocol
3. Modified ACL rehabilitation to limit weight bearing
4. Modified ACL rehabilitation to limit flexion
5. Revision ACL reconstruction

## Correct option

**5. Revision ACL reconstruction**

## Answer explanation

Advanced imaging demonstrates an intra-articular position of the femoral bone plug. Failure to seat the bone plug within the femoral tunnel requires revision reconstruction of the ACL

Graft displacement is a known complication following ACL reconstruction. While rare, displacement of the femoral plug severely compromises the ACL reconstruction. Advanced imaging is often utilized to evaluate the integrity of the graft, tibial and femoral bone stock, bone tunnel placement, and alignment prior to revision ACL reconstruction. If recognized early, this graft may be suitable to use for the revision, but an alternate should be available.

*Safran and Greene reviewed the complications related to ACL reconstructions.* Such complications include those associated with tunnel placement, notchplasty, graft fixation and advancement, suture laceration, graft laceration, guidewire insertion and removal, intra-articular hardware, posterior cruciate ligament laceration, compartment syndrome, and vascular injury. **They conclude** that appropriate technique will minimize postoperative complications.

*Cain et al. reviewed the use of proper surgical technique and preoperative planning to decrease complications.* **They report** that when an autogenous bone-patellar-tendon-bone graft is used for ACL reconstruction, pitfalls may occur at each step during the surgical procedure including preoperative assessment, graft harvest, notch preparation, tunnel preparation, graft passage and fixation, and rehabilitation. **They conclude** that an array of options should be available for fixation of a patellar tendon graft, as well as alternative graft sources and graft preparation techniques.

*Groves et al. review the use of CT prior to revision ACL reconstruction.* *They report on the utility of CT to be able to assess for the complications of primary surgery, such as tunnel malpositioning, tunnel widening, failure of bone graft incorporation, and fixation device failure.* **They conclude** that CT imaging is a valuable tool prior to revision ACL reconstruction.

Figure A in the sagittal MRI images demonstrating a displaced femoral bone plug. Figure B is the sagittal CT image demonstrating a displaced femoral bone plug. Illustration A illustrates that common causes of ACL failures and revision ACL considerations.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1-4: Excision of the femoral bone plug, continued or modified ACL rehabilitation would not adequately address the ACL reconstruction failure with intra-articular displacement of the femoral bone plug. Revision ACL reconstruction is recommended
