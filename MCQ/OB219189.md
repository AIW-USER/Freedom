## Question

A 34-year-old female presents to the emergency department after a motor vehicle collision. She is complaining primarily of ankle pain. Radiographs demonstrate a Hawkins II talar neck fracture. Appropriate initial management is undertaken in the trauma bay and she is consented for surgical fixation. Which of the following factors is correlated with an increased risk for osteonecrosis in this setting?

## Options

1. Delay in fixation > 24 hours
2. Degree of initial fracture displacement
3. Utilization of dual medial and lateral approach
4. Increased duration of surgery
5. Presence of open fracture

## Correct option

**2. Degree of initial fracture displacement**

## Answer explanation

The degree of initial fracture displacement has been shown to be the most predictive factor of increased rates of osteonecrosis in the setting of talar neck fractures.

Talar neck fractures are usually caused by high-energy mechanisms that result in a forced dorsiflexion injury with an axial load. The blood supply to the talus is somewhat tenuous and supplied by three main sources: the posterior tibial artery, the anterior tibial artery, and the peroneal artery. With talar neck fractures, these vessels can be injured, and with increasing degrees of displacement and dislocation, they are increasingly affected. Osteonecrosis can ultimately lead to the collapse of the subchondral bone and pain at the adjacent joints. The primary treatment for osteonecrosis is fusion of the adjacent joints.

*Large et al. looked at post-traumatic AVN after different fractures including those of the proximal femur, proximal humerus, talar neck, and scaphoid.* **They found** that AVN only correlated with nonunion of the fracture in the scaphoid but fracture collapse in all settings could become symptomatic. They specifically mention the likelihood of talar AVN increasing with the increasing severity of the injury and noted that the development of AVN portended a worse prognosis and increased the likelihood of patients requiring additional procedures.

*Vallier et al. evaluated the Hawkins classification for talar neck fractures and attempted to slightly modify it to help better predict the rates of osteonecrosis.* *They divided Hawkins II fractures into IIA (subtalar subluxation) and IIB (subtalar dislocation) and noted that osteonecrosis never occurred in fractures without subtalar dislocation.* **They noted** that timing of reduction, nor the presence of an open fracture were related to the development of osteonecrosis.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Reduction and fixation within 24 hours have not been shown to affect rates of AVN.
Answer 3: Dual approaches to the talus are often necessary to achieve reduction and appropriate fixation of the talar fracture and are not linked to increased rates of AVN.
Answer 4: Duration of surgery has not been shown to affect rates of AVN.
Answer 5: The presence of an open fracture does not show significantly higher rates of AVN compared to closed talar neck fractures.
