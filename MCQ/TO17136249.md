## Question

Which among the following is false about caffeine as an ergogenic response?

## Options

1. increased free fatty acid (FFA) and glycerol levels
2. increased catecholamine
3. increased muscle fat oxidation
4. increased carbohydrate oxidation

## Correct option

**4. increased carbohydrate oxidation**

## Answer explanation

Caffeine is member of the METHYLXANTHINES, a family of naturally occurring stimulants found in the leaves, nuts and seeds of a number of plants.

Major dietary sources of caffeine—tea, coffee, chocolate, cola and energy drinks—typically provide 30–200 mg of caffeine per serve, whereas some non-prescriptive medications contain 100–200 mg of caffeine per tablet.

Caffeine has been removed from the WADA list of prohibited substances in January 2004. Prior to this time, athletes were allowed to consume caffeine, but only up to urine levels of 12 microgram/ml following competitions. This level coincides with the consumption of ∼10 mg caffeine/kg body mass (BM), which is a high dose (∼700 mg of caffeine for a 70 kg individual).

Ergogenic response of caffeine is produced by

- Increased free fatty acid (FFA) and glycerol levels
- increased catecholamine-by increasing epinephrine & nor epinephrine levels and generating flight, fight & fright response.
- increased muscle fat oxidation thus sparing muscle glycogen

 Skeletal muscle **excitation– contraction coupling by caffeine

(1) increases the release of Ca2+ from the sarcoplasmic reticulum

(2) enhancing troponin/myosin Ca2+ sensitivity

(3) decreasing the reuptake of Ca2+ by the sarcoplasmic reticulum.

stimulate Na + K + ATPase activity in inactive skeletal muscle leading to increased rates of K+ uptake and Na + efflux. Rise in plasma K+ with exercise help maintain the membrane potential in contracting muscle and contribute to caffeine’s ergogenic effect during exercise.

- Consuming ∼250 to 300 mg of caffeine has a mild diuretic effect at rest
- ∼500 mg (∼7 mg/kg bm) leads to dehydration at rest or, importantly, negatively affects exercise hydration status, performance, or the ability to thermoregulate in a cool or hot environment.

Answer: 4
