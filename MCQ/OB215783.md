## Question

An engineer is designing a new orthopedic implant. While evaluating the mechanical properties of various metals, he is interested in determining the amount of energy per volume a material can absorb before failure (fracture). Which of the following points on the load-displacement plot accurately describes this property?

![[q215783-a-fig-1a.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. A
2. B
3. C
4. D
5. E

## Correct option

**5. E**

## Answer explanation

Toughness is defined as the amount of energy per volume a material can absorb before failure (fracture). It can be calculated as the area under the stress-strain curve (point E).

Toughness refers to the amount of energy that a material can absorb before failing or fracturing. On a load-displacement plot, toughness can be calculated as the area under the stress-strain curve, labeled as E in this figure. Other important material definitions include elastic deformation (reversible changes in shape to a material due to a load), plastic deformation (irreversible changes in shape to a material due to a load), creep (increased load-deformation with time under constant load), and load relaxation (decrease in applied stress under conditions of constant strain).

*Navarro et al. reviewed biomaterials used in orthopedics.* **They report** that biomaterials have evolved through three different generations, namely first generation (bioinert materials), second-generation (bioactive and biodegradable materials) and third-generation (materials designed to stimulate specific responses at the molecular level). **They concluded** that material characteristics offer several advantages and/or disadvantages depending on their utilization in orthopedics.

*Hou et al. reviewed the effects of the design and microstructure on the mechanical strength of tibial locking devices.* **They report** that both yielding strength and fatigue life were closely related to the section modulus of the inner diameter of screws. **They concluded** that the main cause of hardware failure was mechanical overloading.

Figure A is the unlabeled stress-strain curve (answers are listed below). Illustration A depicts toughness as the area under the stress-strain curve.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1 (A): Modulus of Elasticity is the slope on the linear portion of the curve
Answer 2 (B): Yield Strength is the maximum limit of stress a material can endure so that the material with still stretch elastically
Answer 3 (C): Ultimate Tensile Strength is the point where a given material cannot handle any more increases in tensile stress
Answer 4 (D): Fracture is the maximum strain at which a specimen fractures
