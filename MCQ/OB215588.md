## Question

A 75-year-old male presents to the ED following a MVC. Radiographs reveal a 4-part proximal humerus fracture of his dominant shoulder. When considering treatment with either a reverse shoulder arthroplasty (RSA) or open reduction and internal fixation (ORIF) with a locking plate, which of the following statements is true?

## Options

1. The most common complication following ORIF is avascular necrosis
2. ORIF has a <10% failure rate
3. Fracture type is correlated with ORIF failure
4. There is an equal complication rate between ORIF and RSA
5. Use of a fibular strut significantly reduces the failure rate with fracture fixation, compared with RSA

## Correct option

**3. Fracture type is correlated with ORIF failure**

## Answer explanation

This elderly patient has an acute 4-part proximal humerus fracture. Fracture type has been shown to correlate with failure following ORIF using a locking plate.

Proximal humerus fractures are common fractures often seen in elderly patients with osteoporotic bone. Most proximal humerus fractures may be successfully managed nonoperatively. Indications for ORIF include a greater tuberosity displaced >5mm, displaced 2-part fractures, 3- and 4-part fractures in younger patients, and head-splitting fractures in younger patients. Outcomes following ORIF are dependent on medial support in the presence of posteromedial comminution, and calcar screw placement to decrease varus collapse of the head. ORIF in elderly patients carries a higher failure and complication rate. RSA may be better suited for low-demand elderly individuals with non-reconstructible tuberosities and poor bone stock or with a fracture-dislocation.

*Barlow et al. reviewed patients aged >60 years that were treated with ORIF using locking plates.* **They reported** an overall complication rate of 44%, and an overall failure rate of 34%. Moreover, the failure rate was correlated with fracture type: 26% failure rate in 2-part fractures, 39% in 3-part fractures, and 45% in 4-part fractures. Interestingly, there was no difference between the failure rate with and without fibular allograft. **They concluded** that ORIF of proximal humerus fractures with locking plates in patients aged >60 years carries a high complication and failure rate, particularly in older patients and more complex fractures.

*Ferrel et al. reported on the clinical and radiographic outcomes of patients treated with reverse shoulder arthroplasty (RSA) or hemiarthroplasty for acute complex proximal humeral fractures.* **They reported** that patients treated with RSA had improved forward flexion but decreased external rotation, no significant clinical differences in outcome scores, and an increased rate of clinical complications and a lower revision rate when compared with hemiarthroplasty. **They concluded** that RSA offers an acceptable surgical option for patients after complex acute proximal humeral fractures.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The most common complication following periarticular locking plating fixation is screw cutout (up to 14%)
Answer 2: ORIF in patients >60-years-old has been shown to have a high failure rate (>30%)
Answer 4: There is a higher complication rate (>40%) with ORIF in elderly patients
Answer 5: Studies have shown no difference between the failure rate with and without the use of a fibular allograft compared with RSA
