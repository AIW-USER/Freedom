## Question

Patients A and B are two 8-year-old females who present to the pediatric emergency department after sustaining identical transverse fractures of the left femoral diaphysis. They both undergo flexible intramedullary nailing. If Patient A is 115 pounds and Patient B is 85 pounds, which of the following best describes their expected outcomes if all other patient and injury characteristics are similar?

## Options

1. Equal rates of radiographic nonunion and malunion, but higher rates of complications and reoperation in Patient A
2. Equal rates of radiographic nonunion, radiographic malunion, complications, and reoperation
3. Higher rates of radiographic nonunion, radiographic malunion, complications, and reoperation in Patient B
4. Higher rates of radiographic nonunion, radiographic malunion, complications, and reoperation in Patient A
5. Equal rates of radiographic nonunion and malunion, but higher rates of complications and reoperation in Patient B.

## Correct option

**4. Higher rates of radiographic nonunion, radiographic malunion, complications, and reoperation in Patient A**

## Answer explanation

Flexible intramedullary nailing of femoral shaft fractures in pediatric patients over 108 pounds is associated with higher rates of radiographic nonunion, radiographic malunion, complications, and reoperation (Answer 4).

Pediatric femoral shaft fractures are common injuries. Optimal treatment varies based on patient age, fracture pattern, and weight. Patients between the ages of 5 and 11 may be potentially treated with either flexible titanium rods or submuscular bridge plating, depending on fracture pattern and weight. Known risk factors for postoperative complications after flexible nailing include unstable fracture patterns and weight above 108 pounds, which have been specifically shown to increase the risk of radiographic nonunion, radiographic malunion, complication rate, and reoperation rate.

*Makarewich et al. published a systematic review and meta-analysis assessing the impact of patient weight on outcomes of flexible intramedullary nails in pediatric femur fractures.* **They found** that patients with higher weights had a 20% greater risk of having any complication and a 32% greater risk of having a major complication. They additionally assessed postoperative imaging findings and found a higher rate of radiographic nonunion or malunion in patients with higher weights. **They concluded** that heavier pediatric patients who were treated with flexible intramedullary nails for a femoral shaft fracture have worse outcomes compared to those of lesser weight.

*Murphy et al. published an overview of flexible intramedullary nailing of pediatric diaphysial femur fractures.* *They cover the details of the procedure, including positioning, nail implantation, and rationale for selecting flexible nailing over other forms of fixation.* **They conclude** that outcomes of this procedure are generally excellent, but that length unstable fractures, those at the proximal or distal third of the diaphysis, or those in heavier children carry a higher risk of postoperative complications.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1, 2, 3, and 5: The use of flexible intramedullary nails for pediatric femoral shaft fractures in patients above 100 pounds is associated with a higher rate of radiographic nonunion and malunion, overall complications, and reoperation.
