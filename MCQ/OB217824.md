## Question

You are an adult reconstruction surgeon reviewing prosthetic hip joint infections (PJI) for a lecture you are putting together for a group of medical students. Based on the current 2018 criteria, which of the following preoperative laboratory values meets diagnostic criteria for a prosthetic hip joint infection with the highest sensitivity/specificity?

## Options

1. Labs: CRP = 15 mg/L, ESR = 55 mm/h; Synovial Fluid Analysis: 500 WBC, 70% PMN
2. Labs: CRP = 12 mg/L, ESR = 45 mm/h; Synovial Fluid Analysis: 3,200 WBC, 78% PMN
3. Labs: CRP = 5 mg/L, ESR = 40 mm/h; Synovial Fluid Analysis: 1000 WBC, 82% PMN
4. Labs: CRP: 3 mg/L, ESR = 15 mm/h; Synovial Fluid Analysis: 750 WBC, 70% PMN
5. Labs: CRP 20 mg/L, ESR = 18 mm/h; Synovial Fluid Analysis: 2000 WBC, 65% PMN

## Correct option

**2. Labs: CRP = 12 mg/L, ESR = 45 mm/h; Synovial Fluid Analysis: 3,200 WBC, 78% PMN**

## Answer explanation

Based on the 2018 criteria for prosthetic joint infections (PJI), the preoperative labs and synovial fluid analysis (SFA) would add up to a minor criteria of 6, indicating a high sensitivity and specificity for infection.

*Prosthetic joint infection can be diagnosed using major and minor criteria, as described by Parvizi et al.* Major criteria include a sinus tract communicating with the prosthesis or 2 separate positive joint cultures with the same isolated pathogen. Only 1 positive major criteria is necessary to make the diagnosis of a PJI. Minor criteria are scored based on their importance and a combined score >=6 indicates a >98% sensitivity and specificity for infection. Minor pre-operative criteria include elevated serum CRP (2 pts), ESR (1 pt), and D-dimer (2 pts), as well as SFA analysis that includes elevated synovial WBC (3 pts), leukocyte esterase (3 pts), alpha defensin (3 pts), synovial PMN (2 pts), and synovial CRP (1 pt). While a score >=6 indicates high likelihood of infection, a score from 2-5 is inconclusive and requires more investigation, and a score of 0-1 indicating low likelihood of infection.

*Delle Valle et al. provided AAOS clinical practice guidelines (CPG) on the diagnosis of PJI of the hip and knee.* With regards to PJI in hips, they made several strong recommendations including support for the use of serum ESR and CRP testing, joint aspiration in the setting of elevated ESR/CRP, and the use of frozen section in patients undergoing reoperation who have not had a formal PJI diagnosis. **They recommend** against the use of intraoperative gram stain, but also strongly advise avoiding preoperative antibiotics in suspected cases as well obtaining multiple cultures at the time of surgery.

*Kuzyk et al. reviewed two-stage revision arthroplasty in chronic PJI of the hip and knee.* **They note** that controversy remains regarding how to diagnose a persistent PJI, as well as the type of spacer to use (static vs. mobile) and the duration of appropriate antibiotic therapy. They further outline techniques and outcomes of performing two-stage revision in these complex situations.
