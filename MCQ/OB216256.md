## Answer explanation

Bicondylar tibial plateau fractures with posteromedial fragments are best treated with dual plating to provide buttress fixation of both the posteromedial fragment and lateral condyles.

Tibial plateau fractures are typically the result of high-energy injury mechanisms, such as motor vehicle collisions and falls from height. With greater energy, fractures of both condyles of the tibial plateau can occur. This poses a treatment consideration as both the medial and lateral sides of the tibial plateau are exposed to shear forces. The most biomechanically sound fixation is both medial and lateral buttress plating to provide shear resistance.

*Ricci et al. performed a prospective trial of comminuted proximal tibia fractures treated with the Less Invasive Skeletal Stabilization (LISS) system in 38 patients.* **They reported** 37 patients went onto union without the need for further procedures and 37 patients had satisfactory alignment after union. **They concluded** the LISS internal fixator is appropriate to treat complex proximal tibia fractures without medial stabilization.

*Cole et al. performed a retrospective study of 77 proximal tibia fractures treated with a LISS internal fixation system.* **They reported** a 97% union rate with 2 early fixation failures, 2 nonunions, 2 deep infections, 1 peroneal nerve palsy, and 7 patients developing mild post-operative varus malalignment. **They concluded** the LISS system achieves a high rate of union with low complication rates in patients with complex proximal tibia fractures.

Figure A is an AP radiograph of the right knee with a Schatzker IV medial tibial plateau fracture. Figure B is an AP radiograph of the right knee with a Szhatzker I tibial plateau fracture. Figure C is an AP and lateral radiograph of the left knee with a Schatzker V bicondylar tibial plateau fracture with a posteromedial fragment. Figure D is a coronal CT of the right knee with a Schatzker II tibial plateau fracture. Figure E is a coronal CT of the right knee with a Schatzker III tibial plateau fracture.

Incorrect answers
Answer 1: Figure A is a Schatzker IV tibial plateau fracture that requires a medial buttress plate.
Answer 2: Figure B is a Schatzker I tibial plateau fracture that can be effectively treated with a single incision lateral plate.
Answer 4: Figure D is a Schatzker II tibial plateau fracture that required joint elevation with bone grafting of the void and a lateral buttress plate and rafting screws.
Answer 5: Figure E is a Schatzker III tibial plateau fracture that is best treated with the creation of a lateral cortical window, bone grafting, and lateral plating through a single incision.
