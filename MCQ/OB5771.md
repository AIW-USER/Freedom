## Question

*Figure A*

![[q5771-b-screen-shot-2016-04-03-at-6-18-28-pm.jpg]]
*Figure B*

## Options

1. Foot ankle orthosis
2. Observation for resolution of deformity
3. Observation for resolution of deformity and possible limb length discrepancy
4. Genetic counselling for neurofibromin gene
5. Osteotomy and bone grafting

## Correct option

**3. Observation for resolution of deformity and possible limb length discrepancy**

## Answer explanation

Figure A and B show AP and lateral of a newborn with unilateral posteromedial tibial bowing. The next best step would be reassurance that deformity will resolve but follow up for potential leg length discrepancy.

Posteromedial tibial bowing is likely the result of intrauterine positioning. It most commonly occurs in the middle or distal third of the tibia and may be associated with a calcaneovalgus foot. Initial management of this condition involves a period of observation, as the bowing usually resolves by 5 to 7 years of age. However, these patients should be followed for residual leg length discrepancy. Other types of tibia bowing includes Anteromedial bowing which is associated with fibular hemimelia and anterolateral bowing which is associated with neurofibromatosis Type 1.

*Shah et al. studied case records and radiographs of 20 children with congenital posteromedial bowing of the tibia.* Resolution of the deformity relies on physeal and diaphyseal remodelling potential. Persistent deformities may result in significant bowing, ankle deformity, muscle weakness, and limb length inequality.

*Pappas et al. studied the patterns of growth and development in 33 patients with congenital posteromedial bowing.* In general, the greater the initial bowing, the greater the expected length discrepancy. **They showed** that the differences in length remained proportionate after the age of 12 months.

Figure A and B show a unilateral tibial deformity. The apex of the deformity defines the direction of deformity. In this patient, the apex of the deformity is posterior on the lateral radiograph and medial on the AP radiograph.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1,2: Reassurance that deformity will resolve would be appropriate, but follow up for potential leg length discrepancy is required. Foot ankle orthosis is not required as it is for tibial pseudoarthrosis from anterolateral bowing as there is no risk of fracture.
Answer 4: Genetic counselling for neurofibromin gene may be considered for the diagnosis of neurofibromatosis. This genetic condition may present more commonly with anterolateral tibial bowing.
Answer 5: Urgent surgical planning is not needed. There is commonly an associated imb length discrepancy at skeletal maturity in these patients, so longitudinal follow-up in required.
