## Question

Most common site of osteochondritis dissecans is (arthroscopy- knee arthroscopy- cartilage injury)

## Options

1. Lateral femoral condyle
2. Medial femoral condyle
3. Anteromedial corner of Talus
4. Superomedial part of femoral head

## Correct option

**2. Medial femoral condyle**

## Answer explanation

Osteochondritis Dissecans

Osteochondritis refers to inflammation of bone and cartilage part of the joint. Osteochondritis dissecans is a special type of osteochondritis in which an osteochondral fragment separates to form a loose body in the joint. Disease is more common in males and mostly affects adolescents. It is the most common cause of loose body in the knee joint in adolescents and young adults.

Common sites of osteochondritis dissecans are:

 - Knee (lateral surface of medial femoral condyle) - commonest site

 - Femoral head (superomedial part)

 - Elbow (capitulum)

 - First metatarsal head

 - Talus (anteromedial corner)

Patients come with vague complaints of knee discomfort and may also give history of something catching into the knee joint due to loose bodies. Wilson test is suggestive of diagnosis. In this test the patient is made to sit with legs hanging at the edge of the couch. Pain occurs on internal rotation of the leg which disappears on external rotation.

X-rays classically reveal a loose body or may show a loosely attached osteochondral fragment. MRI is the investigation of choice, provides earliest diagnosis and also aids in management.

In smaller lesions multiple drilling may cause lesions to heal due to infusion of growth factors from bleeding marrow through drill holes. Larger lesions require a mosaicplasty or osteochondral autologous transplantation surgery (OATS), in which graft is harvested from a non-weight bearing area and implanted into the defect.

Answer: 2
