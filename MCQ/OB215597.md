## Question

A 50-year-old male presents with diffuse back pain of 3 weeks duration. He admits to using intravenous (IV) drugs daily. He also has a history of hepatitis C and smokes 1 pack per day. You are consulted to evaluate him for vertebral osteomyelitis and diskitis. From most to least commonly, which area of the spine is affected by these conditions?

## Options

1. Cervical > Lumbar > Thoracic > Sacrum
2. Thoracic > Lumbar > Cervical > Sacrum
3. Lumbar > Cervical > Thoracic > Sacrum
4. Lumbar > Thoracic > Cervical > Sacrum
5. Lumbar = Thoracic > Cervical > Sacrum

## Correct option

**4. Lumbar > Thoracic > Cervical > Sacrum**

## Answer explanation

Osteomyelitis and diskitis are most commonly seen in the lumbar spine (50-60%), followed by the thoracic (30%), and cervical (20%) spine. The sacrum is rarely affected.

Osteomyelitis and diskitis are a spectrum of diseases which are most commonly caused by hematogenous spread through the vertebral endplate or intervertebral discs. Patients typically complain of diffuse back pain in the affected areas, with neurologic deficits present in 10-20% of cases. The most commonly affected spinal segment is the lumbar spine, followed by the thoracic spine, cervical spine, and the sacrum. Treatment is guided by organism identification with blood cultures or CT guided biopsy and usually starts with IV antibiotics. In some cases, operative intervention with decompression, debridement, and stabilization is necessary.

*Connor et al. evaluated outcomes in epidural abscesses treated operatively compared to nonoperatively.* **They noted** axial back pain was the most common presenting symptom, with weakness and radiculopathy also common. **They recommended** that patients with focal neurological deficits should be treated with immediate surgical decompression, while other patient subsets may benefit from a trial of nonoperative management.

*Grados et al. reviewed management of pyogenic discitis in adults.* **They note** that antibiotic therapy should be begun after an organism can be identified from blood cultures or disk biopsy and two bactericidal antibiotics should be the treatment of choice initially. Radiographs at 1 and 3 months into treatment and 3 months after treatment should be obtained to follow improvement. Blood work and MRI are useful adjuncts in certain circumstances.

Incorrect Answers:
1,2,3,5: From most to least likely to be affected by osteomyelitis/discitis are the lumbar, thoracic, and cervical spines followed rarely by the sacrum.
