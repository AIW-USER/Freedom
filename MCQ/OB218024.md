## Question

A 65-year-old female with right knee osteoarthritis undergoes a planned total knee arthroplasty. Her preoperative radiograph is depicted in figure A. During the procedure, the knee is coronally balanced in extension, but the lateral compartment is tight in flexion. Which of the following should be done to appropriately balance the knee?

![[q218024-a-valgus-oa.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Pie-crust the iliotibial band
2. Recess the PCL
3. Release anterior portion of the popliteus tendon
4. Release the LCL
5. Release the semimembranosus

## Correct option

**3. Release anterior portion of the popliteus tendon**

## Answer explanation

Release of the popliteus tendon can address a tight lateral compartment in knee flexion.

Coronal plane gap balancing is critical when performing primary total knee arthroplasty with cruciate-retaining and posterior-stabilized implants. Piecrusting the iliotibial band will improve extension gap balancing whereas popliteus tendon release (anterior portion) will improve the flexion gap. The complete release of the LCL is not recommended unless the conversion to a constrained implant is planned.

*Burkhart et al. performed a cadaveric biomechanical analysis of medial joint space gapping with soft tissue tensioning in a total knee arthroplasty model.* **The authors reported** a significant increase in gapping of the medial compartment with sectioning of the anterior portion of the medial collateral ligament. **They concluded** there was no significant increase in joint line gapping with medial parapatellar arthrotomy, ACL, or PCL resection.

*Rajgopal et al. studied the long-term outcomes of primary total knee arthroplasty for valgus knee arthrosis.* **They reported** all knees had a good patellar station, significant improvement in HSS knee scores, and significant improvement in the tibiofemoral angle at the final follow-up. **They concluded** primary total knee arthroplasty can be successful in valgus knee arthrosis, but emphasized the importance of lateral soft tissue releases.

Figure A is an AP radiograph of the right knee with significant lateral compartment arthritis and valgus coronal alignment.

Incorrect answers
Answer 1: Pie-crusting the iliotibial band can address extension gap balancing of the lateral compartment.
Answer 2: Recessing of the PCL would primarily affect the medial compartment flexion gap.
Answer 4: Complete release of the LCL will lead to significant instability and would require the conversion to a constrained prosthesis.
Answer 5: A semimembranosus release would affect the medial compartment extension gap.
