## Question

A 45-year-old man presents the emergency department after being stabbed in the upper back. His physical examination is consistent with an incomplete spinal cord injury caused by a complete hemitransection of the spinal cord. Which of the following is consistent with this patient's physical examination?

## Options

1. Ipsilateral to injury loss of motor function and propriception, contralateral loss of pain/temperature sensation
2. Burning parathesias in forearms, loss of upper extremity motor function more than lower extremity
3. Loss of pain/temperature sensation, loss of motor function, intact proprioception below level of injury
4. Loss of proprioception, intact motor function, intact pain/temperature sensation below level of injury
5. Ipsilateral to injury loss of pain/temperature sensation, contralateral loss of motor function and proprioception

## Correct option

**1. Ipsilateral to injury loss of motor function and propriception, contralateral loss of pain/temperature sensation**

## Answer explanation

The clinical presentation is consistent with a Brown-Sequard syndrome (BSS) incomplete spinal cord injury. His examination would be consistent with ipsilateral to injury loss of motor function and proprioception and contralateral loss of pain/temperatures sensation.

Incomplete SCI is defined by the preservation of some motor or sensory function below the level of injury. The motor and sensory tracts followed well-defined paths in the spinal cord, and so certain injury patterns will present with a predictable constellation of symptoms. BSS, also known as cord hemitransection, is typically secondary to a stabbing mechanism affecting approximately half of the spinal cord. Motor and proprioception tracts are affected on the side of the injury. Pain/temperature tracts cross in the spinal cord (as opposed to the brain) usually two levels below the site of injury. Therefore, these patients typically lose pain/temperature sensation on the contralateral side of the injury. With other factors being equal, BSS has the best prognosis of incomplete SCIs with reported rates upwards of 98% ambulatory patients at final follow-up.

*McKinley et al. investigated the presentation and outcomes of different SCI syndromes at a tertiary level I trauma center.* **They found** that central cord syndrome (CCS) was the most common incomplete SCI with the lowest functional level at admission. BSS patients typically had more functional gain than CCS patients, but results were confounded by BSS patients being of younger age and better baseline functional status.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: These findings are consistent with central cord syndrome
Answer 3: These findings are consistent with anterior cord syndrome
Answer 4: These findings are consistent with posterior cord syndrome
Answer 5: This is not a typical constellation of symptoms and in fact is the reverse of what would be seen in a BSS patient.
