## Question

She has a past surgical history of a subcapital femoral neck fracture requiring arthroplasty following a mechanical fall 3 years ago. Radiographs demonstrate a wedge compression fracture. ## Options

1. She is unlikely to have comorbid hypertension
2. She is less likely to smoke
3. She has the same age-adjusted mortality risk
4. She is at greater risk of death from pulmonary conditions
5. She is unlikely to use calcium and vitamin D supplements

## Correct option

**4. She is at greater risk of death from pulmonary conditions**

## Answer explanation

The patient presents with an old osteoporotic vertebral compression fracture as T12, likely sustained during her fall 6 months ago. Compared to peers without this injury, the patient is at increased risk of dying from pulmonary causes such as pneumonia.

Osteoporotic vertebral compression fractures are common injuries and affect up to 40% of women older than age 80 years. These injuries are associated with significant physical and psychosocial disabilities and represent a growing public health concern in aging societies. As delineated in the studies cited, these injuries are associated with increased age-adjusted mortality, smoking, hypertension, and the use of calcium and vitamin D supplements. The available literature suggests that women do not die directly from fracture sequelae. Rather, the fractures occur in the setting of increasing generalized frailty, which increases the risk of both fracture and mortality.

*Schlaich et al. performed pulmonary function tests on patients who had sustained thoracic spinal compression fractures.* *Patients with osteoporotic fractures had significantly decreased forced expiratory volume and vital capacity.* This decrease in pulmonary function correlated with clinical and radiologic indices of post-traumatic spinal deformity.

*Kado et al. prospectively observed a case series of women >65 years old with osteoporotic vertebral compression fractures diagnosed either acutely or as incidental findings on plain radiographs.* *Among the 9575 patients, women with vertebral fractures were significantly older, less likely to exercise, and more likely to have hypertension, report poor health status, smoke, and take vitamin D and calcium supplements compared to women without fractures.* Women with osteoporotic compression fractures also had higher age-adjusted mortality than women without these fractures, even if the fractures were subclinical in nature.

*Gold et al. reviewed the clinical impact of vertebral fractures in osteoporotic women.* Not only were these injuries associated with acute and chronic pain, but they also had a significant impact on psychosocial function, anxiety, depression, and diminished self-esteem. **The authors emphasize** the importance of post-fracture physical therapy to optimize physical and functional outcomes.

*Kado et al. prospectively evaluated the association between incidental vertebral fractures and mortality in women >80 years of age.* **The authors found** that incidental vertebral compression fractures were not associated with increased mortality when adjustments were made for 3 markers of patient frailty: weight loss, inability to rise from a chair, and difficulty standing on feet for >2 hours. **The authors concluded** that although there is an overall increased risk of mortality following an osteoporotic vertebral compression fracture, the mortality risk is likely part of the complex pathogenesis involving decreased physiologic reserve and frailty.

Figure A shows a compression fracture at T12 with the involvement of both endplates. The STIR MRI sequence shows no signal in T12, which is suggestive of its chronicity.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1, 2, 3, 5: Patients with osteoporotic vertebral compression fractures are likely to have comorbid hypertension, be smokers, and use calcium/vitamin D supplements. The age-adjusted mortality risk is increased after these injuries.
