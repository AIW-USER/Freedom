## Question

A 29-year-old female presents with worsening activity-related groin pain and occasional mechanical symptoms. Physical exam and radiographic evaluation demonstrate femoroacetabular impingement with an associated labral tear. She has exhausted all conservative measures and elects to undergo arthroscopic surgery. Figure A demonstrates a left hip with the proposed arthroscopic portal sites marked. In the postoperative period, the patient reports diminished sensation over the anterolateral thigh. The neurologic injury responsible for this finding most likely occurred as a result of which aspect of the surgery?

![[q213011-a-hip-portals.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Axial traction
2. Extra-articular fluid extravasation
3. Poorly fitted post-operative hip abduction orthosis
4. Utilization of the "AL" portal intra-operatively
5. Utilization of the "AP" portal intra-operatively

## Correct option

**5. Utilization of the "AP" portal intra-operatively**

## Answer explanation

The patient in this vignette presents with paresthesias in the distribution of the lateral femoral cutaneous nerve (LFCN). Of the choices given, the anterior portal (AP) will place the LFCN at the greatest risk of injury.

With advances in instrumentation and surgical technique, hip arthroscopy has become increasingly utilized to address intra- and peri-articular hip pathology. The procedure however remains technically demanding due to the anatomical location, specialized instrumentation, and distraction required to access the joint, and as a result carries inherent risks associated with the procedure. The perioperative complication profile has become increasingly well-characterized. While the most common complications are neuropraxias related to traction, direct injury to periarticular neurovascular structures such as the LFCN are commonly reported as well due to the vicitnty to common arthroscopic portal sites. The lateral femoral cutaneous nerve (LFCN) runs from the inguinal ligament distally along the sartorius and has a variable pattern of arborization. As a result, overly-medial placement of the anterior portal is thought to increase risk of iatrogenic injury.

*Thorey et al. performed a cadaveric study evaluating the relationship between different arthroscopic portals, joint accessibility, and neurovascular structures.* **They found** that the standard AP allows exceptional visualization of the central compartment (from 430 to 830 o'clock). Portal medialization did not improve visualization and increased risk of injury to the femoral vessels and LFCN, leading the authors to recommend against medial portal placement.

*Robertson et al. performed a cadaveric study evaluating the relationship of arthroscopic portals to relevant hip anatomy.* **They found** the AP to be an average of 15.4mm from the LFCN and 10.1mm from the transverse branch of the lateral femoral circumflex artery. The study contributed to an understanding of the relationship of the most common arthroscopic portals with underlying at-risk neurovascular structures.

Figure A shows standard and accessory portals used in hip arthroscopy. Notice that all portals are lateral to the line drawn distally from the ASIS. Illustration A is a cadaveric dissection showing the close relationship of the AP to LFCN. Illustrations B-D depict the relationship of the various portals to the hip joint and surrounding musculature.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Traction is most commonly associated with pudendal nerve palsy.
Answer 2: Fluid extravasation has been linked in rare cares to the development of abdominal compartment syndrome.
Answer 3: Poorly fitting orthoses has not been linked to post-operative LFCN paresthesia.
Answer 4: The superior gluteal nerve is at risk with placement of the AL portal.
