## Question

A Morton’s extension orthotic would be the first line of treatment in a patient with which of the following diagnoses?

## Options

1. Hallux valgus
2. Hallux rigidus
3. Hallux varus
4. Hammer toe
5. Mallet toe

## Correct option

**2. Hallux rigidus**

## Answer explanation

A Morton’s extension orthotic is used for the nonoperative treatment of hallux rigidus.

Hallux rigidus is characterized by loss of motion of the first metatarsophalangeal joint (MTPJ) secondary to arthritis, and represents the most common location of osteoarthritis in the foot. Patient often present with first ray and/or MTPJ pain that is worse during the push off phase of gait. On exam, patients will have pain during terminal dorsiflexion, which progresses to pain throughout the entire arc of motion as the disease worsens. Initial treatment is non-operative and consists of NSAIDs, activity modification and a Morton’s extension orthotic. This orthotic (Illustration A) extends past the first MTPJ providing a stiff construct during gait, which limits dorsiflexion at the MTPJ.

*Coughlin et al review the demographics, etiology and radiographic assessment of patients with hallux rigidus.* *They report on 114 patients with a confirmed diagnosis of hallux rigidus that ultimately went on to require surgery; 80 cheilectomies and 30 arthrodesis patients.* **They conclude** that hallux rigidus was associated with hallux valgus interphalngeus, bilateral involvement in those with a familial history, unilateral involvement in those with a history of trauma and female gender.

*Perez-Aznar et al review the outcomes following dorsal wedge phalangeal osteotomies in the treatment of hallux rigidus.* **They report** good and excellent outcomes in 34/42 feet with a mean AOFAS score improvement of 37 points and an improvement in the VAS of 5.7. **They conclude** that phalangeal osteotomy was a reliable and reproducible treatment for moderate hallux rigidus in active patients.

*Raikin et al review long-term clinical and radiographic outcomes of metallic hemiarthroplasty and arthrodesis for the treatment of severe hallux rigidus.* **They report** that 24% of hemiarthroplasties failed with 80% of these ultimately requiring fusion. **They conclude** that arthrodesis provides a more predictable outcome in the treatment of severe hallux rigidus.

*Yee et al review the current concepts in the treatment of hallux rigidus.* **They report** that the use of foot orthoses is the first line treatment as they limit motion and irritation cause by the dorsal osteophyte in hallux rigidus. **They conclude** that is an effective treatment with nearly 50% of patients responding to this alone.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1 & 3: Morton’s extensions are the mainstay orthotic used to treat hallux rigidus, not hallux valgus or hallux varus.
Answer 4: Hammer toe is a deformity in which the PIP joint is flexed, thus shoes with a high toe box are used for initial treatment.
Answer 5: Mallet toe is a deformity in which the DIP joint is flexed, thus shoes with a high toe box are used for initial treatment.
