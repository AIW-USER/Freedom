## Options

1. Osteochondral defect measuring > 2 cm2
2. Uncorrectable limb malalignment
3. Ligamentous instability of the knee
4. Meniscal insufficiency
5. Multi-compartmental arthrosis

## Correct option

**1. Osteochondral defect measuring > 2 cm2**

## Answer explanation

Osteochondral allograft transplantation (OCA) in the knee is typically indicated for patients with large focal full-thickness chondral or osteochondral defects (> 2 cm2), for which other techniques such as microfracture, osteochondral autograft transplantation, and autologous chondrocyte implantation (ACI) are inadequate due to the size, location or depth of the lesion.

The patient has a symptomatic osteochondral defect of his medial femoral condyle, which has not responded to nonsurgical and prior arthroscopic management. He has subchondral bone involvement as evident by increased T2 signal underlying the cartilage defect on MRI. Osteochondral allograft addresses both the cartilage defect and the compromised subchondral bone compared to ACI which requires healthy subchondral bone stock. Contraindications to OCA include ligamentous instability of the knee, meniscal insufficiency, uncorrectable limb malalignment, severe tricompartmental arthritis, and inflammatory arthropathies.

*Bedi et al. review the use of osteochondral allograft transplantation as a cartilage resurfacing procedure that involves transplantation of a cadaver graft which consists of intact, viable articular cartilage and its underlying subchondral bone into the defect.* **They report** that size, depth, and location of the defect are all critical factors in the tailoring of the donor graft. **They conclude** that the advantages to the use of osteochondral allografts include the ability to achieve precise surface architecture, immediate transplantation of viable hyaline cartilage as a single-stage procedure, the potential to replace large defects or even hemicondyles, and no donor site morbidity.

*Krych et al. reviews the return to sport and functional outcomes following various cartilage restoration techniques.* **They report** a return to sport following OCA to be 88%. The time to return to sport following OCA was 9.6 ± 3.0 months. **They concluded** that OCA is a viable treatment option with a satisfactory ability to return to sports.

Figure A is an intraoperative image during a knee arthroscopy demonstrating a debridement of a loose femoral cartilage defect. Figure B is the standing AP radiograph of the lower extremity demonstrating normal limb alignment. Figure C is the sagittal MRI of the knee demonstrating subchondral bone involvement with increased T2 signal underlying the cartilage defect.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Uncorrectable limb malalignment is a contraindication to the placement of osteochondral allograft.
Answer 3: Ligament instability of the knee is a contraindication to the placement of osteochondral allograft.
Answer 4: Meniscal insufficiency is a contraindication to the placement of osteochondral allograft.
Answer 5: Advanced or diffuse degenerative changes as seen on weight-bearing radiographs is a contraindication to the placement of osteochondral allograft.
