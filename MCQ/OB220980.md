## Question

A 64-year-old man with poorly controlled type 2 diabetes mellitus presents with a chronic plantar foot ulcer that has been present for ten weeks. The ulcer measures 4 × 3 centimeters with moderate depth and exposed subcutaneous tissue but no exposed bone or tendon. He has peripheral neuropathy, palpable pedal pulses, and no clinical or laboratory evidence of infection or osteomyelitis. The patient has been compliant with appropriate offloading and local wound care, but the ulcer has failed to significantly decrease in size. Which of the following treatments has been shown to decrease ulcer size and time to healing?

## Options

1. Custom diabetic shoes with accommodative inserts
2. Electrophysical therapy
3. Hyperbaric oxygen therapy
4. Negative pressure wound therapy
5. Platelet-derived growth factor topical therapy

## Correct option

**4. Negative pressure wound therapy**

## Answer explanation

Negative pressure wound therapy (NPWT) has been shown to reduce wound size and accelerate healing in chronic diabetic foot ulcers, particularly those with adequate perfusion, no active infection, and failure to progress with standard wound care (Answer 4).

Randomized controlled trials and systematic reviews have demonstrated improved healing rates and shorter time to closure with NPWT compared with moist wound dressings alone in appropriately selected diabetic foot ulcers. NPWT is especially beneficial for deeper ulcers with significant tissue loss after debridement and in patients who are already appropriately offloaded, making it a logical next-line therapy when conventional measures fail. By applying controlled subatmospheric pressure, NPWT promotes granulation tissue formation, reduces wound edema, removes exudate, and improves local blood flow, all of which contribute to faster wound contraction and healing.

*Zhang et al. conducted a meta-analysis to evaluate the effectiveness and safety of negative-pressure wound therapy (NPWT) compared with conventional wound care for diabetic foot ulcers.* **The authors found** that NPWT significantly increased the rate of complete ulcer healing, reduced time to healing, and decreased ulcer size without increasing adverse events. **They concluded** that NPWT is a safe and effective treatment modality that improves healing outcomes in diabetic foot ulcers compared with standard wound dressings.

*Kunze et al. published a review of the indications, mechanisms, and clinical outcomes of negative-pressure wound therapy in foot and ankle surgery.* **The authors found** that NPWT was associated with reduced wound complications, improved healing, and lower rates of dehiscence and infection in high-risk patients, including those with diabetes. **They concluded** that NPWT is a valuable adjunct in foot and ankle surgery, particularly for complex wounds and patients at elevated risk for wound-healing complications.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: Custom diabetic shoes with accommodative inserts are helpful for long-term pressure reduction but have not been shown to reliably accelerate healing of established ulcers.
Answer 2: Electrophysical therapy has not consistently been shown to reduce ulcer size or time to healing.
Answer 3: Hyperbaric oxygen therapy has had mixed and limited results in select ischemic or refractory cases, with no consistent reduction in healing time.
Answer 5: Platelet-derived growth factor topical therapy has had inconsistent and inferior results compared to therapies that mechanically stimulate granulation and wound contraction.
