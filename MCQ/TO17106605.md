## Question

Which factor does not increase scapular notching in reverse shoulder arthroplasty

## Options

1. Superior placement of glenosphere
2. Superior tilt of glenosphere
3. Superior glenoid erosion
4. lateralized offset of glenosphere

## Correct option

**4. lateralized offset of glenosphere**

## Answer explanation

Explanation: Scapular notching is a radiographic finding, which may occur following reverse total shoulder arthroplasty (RTSA). The term is used to describe an erosive lesion of the axillary border of the inferior scapular neck that occurs when the medial rim of the humeral implant contacts the scapula during shoulder adduction. Despite numerous reports on the phenomenon, its clinical and radiological consequences remain unclear.

While early reverse designs were fraught with problems, including catastrophic failure of the glenoid secondary to excessive torque and shear forces, instability, and poor active shoulder motion, in 1985, Grammont introduced the first successful reverse shoulder prosthesis. One of the distinguishing biomechanical characteristics of Grammont's design included medialization and distalization of the center of rotation of the glenoid implant. This decreased the shear force and lever arm across the glenoid bone-implant interface. Distalization of the glenoid implant served to tension the deltoid. The combination of these factors resulted in a lower rate of glenoid implant failure seen in previous designs. However, the design frequently resulted in contact between the medial rim of the humeral implant polyethylene and the scapular neck with arm adduction.

- Factors that increases incidence of scapular notchingSurgical approach: More with anterosuperior approach compared with deltopectoral approach.
- Anatomical factors:

decreased scapular neck length (SNL) leads to increased rates of notching.

- Superior placement and superior tilt of glenosphere- Both superior placement of the glenosphere and superior tilt of the glenosphere increase chances of scapular notching. Inferior glenoid tilt affords a greater arc of impingement free motion with a more even distribution of force at the bone-glenoid implant interface. Placing the glenosphere with inferior overhang (inferior placement of glenosphere) appears to confer the greatest reduction in scapular notching with the least adverse consequence.
- Lateralized offset of the glenosphere appears to be protective against scapular notching Lateral offset can be achieved in a variety of ways, including bone or metal augmentation of the glenoid.
- Varus neck shaft angle of humeral prosthesis- As prosthesis neck shaft angle decreases, the humeral cup becomes more vertical, and the humeral cup is positioned in more abduction relative to the humeral shaft. In a cadaver model, this has been shown to improve the impingement-free adduction angle while providing more lateral offset
- Superior glenoid erosion ((Type E2 glenoid wear) - When the glenoid is eroded superiorly the inclination is in valgus (superior tilt), and the tendency is to position the base plate with a similar orientation, allowing the humeral cup to override the inferior glenoid hemisphere and contact the scapular pillar.
- Rotator cuff tear arthropathy with a narrowed acromiohumeral distance.
- Preoperative MRI evidence of Grade 3 or 4 fatty infiltration of the infraspinatus muscle.

Answer: 4
