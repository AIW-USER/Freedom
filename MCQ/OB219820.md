## Question

A 23-year-old female soccer player presents to the orthopedic clinic with a history of multiple patellar dislocations. She has trialed physical therapy and patellar bracing; however, she recently experienced a third dislocation in a single calendar year. Plain films reveal mild genu valgum, and a Caton-Deschamps ratio of 1.2. A CT scan shows a tibial tubercle to trochlear groove (TT-TG) of 14.5 mm with mild trochlear dysplasia. The patient would like to return to playing soccer as soon as possible postoperatively. What is the most appropriate management for this patient?

## Options

1. Medial patellofemoral ligament repair
2. Medial patellofemoral ligament reconstruction
3. Tibial tubercle osteotomy
4. Medial patellofemoral reconstruction with tibial tubercle osteotomy
5. Arthroscopic lateral release

## Correct option

**2. Medial patellofemoral ligament reconstruction**

## Answer explanation

This patient presents with recurrent patellar instability that has failed nonoperative management. Recent evidence has shown that patellar instability can be managed effectively with isolated medial patellofemoral ligament (MPFL) reconstruction in patients without an elevated TT–TG distance or patella alta (Answer 2).

Patellar instability is a common condition that generally affects patients between the ages of 10 and 25. The current standard of care for a first-time dislocation without a significant osteochondral injury or loose body is nonoperative management with patellar stabilization rehabilitation. Patients with recurrent instability recalcitrant to nonoperative management should be offered surgical intervention. Patellar instability has been treated with MPFL repair, MPFL reconstruction, tibial tubercle osteotomy, trochleoplasty, lateral release, or a combination of these surgeries. Osteotomies have been used to improve patellar alignment; however, they extend the postoperative recovery time and have the additional risk of fracture or nonunion at the osteotomy site. In patients without significantly elevated TT–TG distance or patella alta, isolated MPFL reconstruction provides equivalent clinical outcomes.

*Erickson et al. report on the 2-year outcomes of a cohort of 90 patients who underwent MPFL reconstruction.* The mean TT–TG distance was 14.7 ± 5.4 mm and the mean Canton–Deschamps Index (CDI) was 1.2. At 2-year follow-up, 100% of patients reported no recurrent patellofemoral instability. **The authors conclude** that isolated MPFL reconstruction is an effective treatment for patellar instability regardless of bony pathologies, including increased TT–TG distance, patella alta, and trochlear dysplasia.

*Liu et al. published a retrospective review of 121 consecutive patients who underwent MPFL reconstruction (mean follow-up: 44 months).* The mean TT–TG distance was 13.5 mm and the mean CDI was 1.2. Results showed that 94.5% of patients were able to return to sport at 1 year, while 3 patients reported a postoperative instability event. **The authors conclude** that in patients without significantly elevated TT–TG distance or significant patella alta, isolated MPFL reconstruction is an effective treatment.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: MPFL repair is reserved for acute first-time dislocations with MPFL avulsions and maintained mid-substance ligament integrity or those with large bony avulsions. Chronic dislocation acquires reconstruction with either autograft or allograft.
Answer 3: This patient has mildly elevated TT–TG, a tibial tubercle osteotomy is not indicated and would not address her MPFL deficit.
Answer 4: The patient would like to return to sport as soon as possible. A tibial tubercle osteotomy will increase her recovery time without any proven benefit in patient-reported outcomes, recurrent instability, or return to sport.
Answer 5: Isolated lateral release is not indicated for instability and may lead to iatrogenic medial instability.
