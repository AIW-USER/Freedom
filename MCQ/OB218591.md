## Question

A 14-year-old female presents with continued left foot pain associated with planovalgus deformity. A radiograph is obtained in the office and is depicted in Figure A. Her pain is localized to the region of the arrow on the radiograph. What is the function of the muscle that attaches to the involved structure?

![[q218591-a-accessory-navicular.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Ankle dorsiflexion
2. Ankle plantarflexion
3. Inverts hindfoot and assists with plantarflexion
4. Hindfoot eversion
5. Plantarflexion and hindfoot eversion

## Correct option

**3. Inverts hindfoot and assists with plantarflexion**

## Answer explanation

The radiographs depict an accessory navicular within the posterior tibial tendon, which functions as a hindfoot inversion and assists with ankle plantarflexion.

An accessory navicular is a common variant seen in up to 12% of the population with most patients being asymptomatic. Symptomatic patients will present with medial pain over the prominence. Initial management with activity modification, shoe modifications, and over-the-counter analgesics is often effective. Patients with refractory symptoms can be treated with excision of the accessory navicular via a tendon splitting approach.

*Cha et al. performed a prospective comparative study of simple excision versus the Kidner procedure with re-attachment of the posterior tibial tendon for the treatment of type 2 accessory navicular in pediatric patients.* **They reported** no significant differences between both techniques regarding hindfoot alignment, midfoot alignment, AOFAS scores, and VAS scores. **They concluded** both procedures were effective for treating type 2 accessory navicular and flatfoot.

*Kopp and Marcus retrospectively studied the surgical outcomes of symptomatic accessory navicular.* **They reported** 13/14 feet were without pain at the final follow-up with 2/14 requiring shoe inserts. **They concluded** surgical treatment of symptomatic accessory navicular is highly successful in patients that are refractory to conservative measures.

Figure A is an AP radiograph of the left foot with an accessory navicular present.

Incorrect answers
Answer 1: The tibialis anterior functions as an ankle dorsiflexor.
Answer 2: The gastrocsoleous muscles function as ankle plantar flexors.
Answer 4: The peroneus brevis acts as a hindfoot evertor.
Answer 5: The posterior tibialis does not revert the hindfoot.
