## Question

A 63-year-old female diabetic patient develops severe foot and ankle pain over the preceding five years. She has tried treating her condition with activity modifications, physical therapy, ankle gauntlet bracing, and medications without improvement in her pain. The decision is made to proceed with surgery consisting of an arthrodesis. When discussing the surgical plan, the treating physician mentions that fusing a single joint would lead to a >90% decrease in hindfoot motion. Which joint is this surgeon referring to?

## Options

1. Naviculocuneiform joint
2. Talonavicular joint
3. Calcaneocuboid joint
4. Talocalcaneal joint
5. Tibiotalar joint

## Correct option

**2. Talonavicular joint**

## Answer explanation

Fusing the talonavicular joint decreases the hindfoot range of motion by >90%.

Arthrosis of the hindfoot is a debilitating condition that involves the subtalar joint (talocalcaneal), calcaneocuboid, and talonavicular joints. Nonoperative treatment is the first line for this condition consisting of activity modifications, bracing, physical therapy, and pain medications. However, surgical treatment is recommended for refractory cases. Arthrodesis of the talonavicular joint leads to the greatest reduction of the hindfoot range of motion compared to other hindfoot joints.

*Jarrell et al. performed a cadaveric study of eight specimens to test the biomechanical strength of talonavicular fusion constructs.* **They reported** there was a decrease in peak pressure of the medial forefoot while increasing lateral forefoot pressure, but no differences were found between screws alone and plate with screws regarding bending stiffness or failure. **They concluded** talonavicular fusion leads to lateralization of plantar forefoot pressure.

*Easley et al. performed a retrospective study of 184 consecutive patients undergoing isolated subtalar arthrodesis.* **They reported** an overall union rate of 84% and 42% of patients were found to have >2 mm of avascular bone visible at the time of surgery which was correlated with non-union. **They concluded** subtalar arthrodesis success rate was severely diminished by smoking, the presence of >2 mm avascular bone, and previous arthrodesis failure.

*Astion et al. simulated hindfoot motion after arthrodesis single, double, and triple hindfoot arthrodesis.* **They reported** that single talonavicular arthrodesis limited remaining joint motion to less than 2 degrees and reduced posterior tibial tendon excursion by 25%. **They concluded** simulated arthrodesis of the talonavicular joint had the greatest impact on the hindfoot range of motion with essentially all motion eliminated.

Incorrect answers
Answer 1: Naviculocuneiform arthrodesis has little effect on hindfoot range of motion.
Answer 3: Calcaneocuboid arthrodesis has little effect on the subtalar range of motion, but reduces talonavicular motion by 67%.
Answer 4: Subtalar arthrodesis reduces calcaneocuboid motion by 44% and talonavicular motion by 74%.
Answer 5: Tibiotalar arthrodesis has little effect on hindfoot range of motion.
