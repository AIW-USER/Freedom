## Question

A 35-year-old nurse presents to the orthopedic clinic endorsing right elbow pain and weakness for the past 5 months. She initially suffered a fall while biking which resulted in a simple elbow dislocation that was reduced at an emergency room. Her persistent lateral elbow pain was diagnosed as tennis elbow and she completed therapy, which did not improve her symptoms. She reports her elbow feels unstable and limits her function at work. On exam, she has pain with wrist extension and a positive chair rise test. Plain films show no osseous abnormality. Which additional maneuver will assist in understanding the degree of her instability?

## Options

1. Medial elbow pain while a valgus stress is applied with the elbow at 30 degrees of flexion
2. Apprehension of the patient when her elbow is flexed to 90 degrees, the forearm is supinated and the thumb is pulled in a posterior direction.
3. Radiocapitellar subluxation when the elbow is ranged from 70-120 degrees with the forearm supinated and a posterior directed pull on the thumb.
4. Radiocapitellar reduction when the supine patient’s arm is held overhead in a supinated position and the elbow is taken from full extension to 90 degrees of flexion while a valgus force is applied
5. Radiocapitellar subluxation when the supine patient’s arm is held overhead in a supinated position and the elbow is taken from full extension to 90 degrees of flexion while a valgus force is applied

## Correct option

**4. Radiocapitellar reduction when the supine patient’s arm is held overhead in a supinated position and the elbow is taken from full extension to 90 degrees of flexion while a valgus force is applied**

## Answer explanation

This 35-year-old female presents with chronic posterolateral rotary instability (PLRI) secondary to a lateral ulnar collateral ligament injury (LUCL). The lateral pivot shift is a dynamic test that evaluates the structural integrity of the LUCL.

Injury of the LUCL is commonly associated with a combined force of forearm supination, axial load, and valgus stress. This results in tearing of the anterior capsule with progression around the posterolateral elbow, resulting in subluxation or dislocation of the radiocapitellar joint. If left untreated, this can result in chronic instability, especially when an axial load and varus stress (such as rising chair while pushing off the handrails) is applied. The lateral pivot shift test is helpful in understanding the degree of elbow instability but is most accurate when performed on an anesthetized patient. With the patient supine, the affected arm is held overhead in a supinated position, and a valgus stress is applied. As elbow flexion increases the triceps tension increases, which ultimately reduces the radial head. This is analogous to the IT band reducing the lateral tibial plateau as the knee is flexed in an ACL-deficient knee. Chronic LUCL injuries with instability are best managed with autograft or allograft ligament reconstruction.

*Conti Mica et al. published a review article that discusses the presentation, pathophysiology, and treatment of chronic PLRI.* **The authors proposed** a treatment algorithm that recommends LUCL repair in the acute setting. In the chronic setting, the decision to perform LUCL imbrication versus reconstruction is dependent on the viability of local tissue and the result of the pivot shift test under general anesthesia.

*Anakwenze et al. published a systematic review of PLRI.* *They included 130 patients with a mean follow-up of 44.5 months.* The report on several surgical techniques; however, **they note** that the overall complication rate is 11% with around 8% of patients suffering recurrent instability.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: This describes the valgus stress test, which is used for medial ulnar collateral ligament evaluation.
Answer 2: This describes the milking maneuver, which is used for medial ulnar collateral ligament evaluation.
Answer 3: This describes the moving valgus stress test, which is used for medial ulnar collateral ligament evaluation.
Answer 5: The radiocapitellar joint is reduced from a subluxated or dislocated position as the tension of the triceps increases.
