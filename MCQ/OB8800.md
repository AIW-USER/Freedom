## Question

Which of the following medications works by inhibiting presynaptic acetylcholine vesicle docking and release at peripheral neuromuscular junctions?

## Options

1. Trihexyphenidyl
2. Botulinum toxin A
3. Baclofen
4. Gabapentin
5. Neostigmine

## Correct option

**2. Botulinum toxin A**

## Answer explanation

Botulinum toxin A irreversibly binds at the neuromuscular junction to prevent presynaptic release of acetylcholine, thereby preventing muscular contraction.

Botulinum toxin A is commonly used for the treatment of dynamic spasticity in patients with cerebral palsy. The generally accepted mechanism of botulinum toxin is inhibition of acetylcholine release at the neuromuscular junction after uptake into cholinergic neurons. Once inside the cell, one of the serotypes cleave SNARE proteins which are required for vesicle docking and release, thereby inhibiting neurotransmitter release. Each serotype (A, B, C...) acts at a different site and has a slightly different effect and duration. Intramuscular injections of botulinum toxin A can result in 3-4 months of muscle relaxation. It is important to note that it is only effective on the dynamic spasticity of cerebral palsy and is ineffective on static contractures.

*Molenaers et al. evaluated the efficacy of botulinum toxin A in patients with cerebral palsy.* **They found** injections lead to greater functional improvements in patients with mild cerebral palsy and when multilevel injections were given. **They also found** that outcomes were more successful with physical therapy, post-injection casting, and frequency of orthotic use. **The authors concluded** that multilevel and repeat injection of botulinum toxin A can provide functional improvements.

*Aoki et al. review the pharmacology and immunological effects of botulinum neurotoxins.* **They state** that in addition to reducing muscle contractility, botulinum neurotoxins can have indirect effects on sensory afferents and antinociceptive effects. Additionally, diffusion from the site of injection can cause unwanted paralysis (i.e. sternocleidomastoid injection diffusing towards the esophagus causing dysphagia) and immunologic responses can lead to sensitization. **They emphasize** an awareness of the difference in preparation of botulinum toxins that can have different pharmacologic profiles.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: Trihexyphenidyl is a synthetic anticholinergic drug which functions as a competitive antagonist at muscarinic receptors to decrease acetylcholine.
Answer 3: Baclofen is an analogue of gamma-aminobutyric acid (GABA) that binds to GABA receptors in the spinal cord, decreasing the release of excitatory neurotransmitters.
Answer 4: Gabapentin can decrease pain transduction by reducing hyper-excitability of voltage-gated calcium channels in neurons.
Answer 5: Neostigmine is an acetylcholinesterase inhibitor, which would cause increased levels of acetylcholine, and therefore, stimulation of nicotinic and muscarinic receptors.
