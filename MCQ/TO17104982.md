## Question

True about femoral cam jump is all except

## Options

1. Complication specific to posterior stabilized knee
2. Associated with tight flexion gap
3. Revision total knee replacement is needed finally
4. Popliteus over release may cause it

## Correct option

**2. Associated with tight flexion gap**

## Answer explanation

Explanation: Femoral cam jump is a complication specific to posterior stabilized knee replacements. It occurs when the flexion gap is left too loose. Varus or valgus stress, when the knee is flexed, causes flexion gap to open up, and femoral cam then rotates in front of the post and then comes to rest in front of the tibial post.

Closed reduction maneuver: Under anesthesia the knee is positioned at 90 degrees of flexion and anterior drawer maneuver is performed. Knee is reduced with a clunk. Ultimate solution requires knee revision to address loose flexion gap.

Answer: 2
