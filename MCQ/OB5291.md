## Question

All of the following have been shown as significant preoperative risk factors for wound complications after surgery for neuromuscular scoliosis in patients with cerebral palsy EXCEPT:

## Options

1. Serum albumin <3.5 g/dL
2. Presence of a ventriculoperitoneal shunt
3. Hemiplegia
4. WBC <1,500 cells/ucL
5. Severity of neuromuscular disease including seizure history

## Correct option

**3. Hemiplegia**

## Answer explanation

All of the above have been shown to increase risk of wound complications after surgery in patients with neuromuscular scoliosis except hemiplegia.

Though highly variable, some studies have shown significant increased risk of infection in those with poor nutritional status (serum albumin <3.5 g/dL), immunocompromised status (WBC <1,500 cells/ucL), presence of a ventriculoperitoneal (VP) shunt, as well as those with severe spastic quadriplegia nonambulatory status with seizures.

*Master et al. performed a retrospective regression analysis of 151 patients with CP undergoing spinal correction.* *In this study, the only significant risk factor for post-operative infection was presence of a VP shunt.*

*Szoke et al. reported on 172 CP patients undergoing surgical correction and reported a 8.7% (15/172) infection rate, which included both superficial and deep.* Notably, the authors specified that all of the patients with any type of infection were severe spastic quadriplegics with seizure history.

*Jevsevar et al. performed a case-cohort comparison study in 44 total patients.* *Group 1 consisted of 24 patients with a serum albumin >3.5 g/dL and WBC count >1,500 cells/ucL) while group 2 consisted of 20 patients with less than the aforementioned parameters.* Head-to-head comparison revealed significantly lower infection rate, hospital stay, and shorter intubation period for Group 1 when compared to Group 2.

*More recently Ramo et. al published a series on over 400 patients over a 30 year period.* *This study reported a 10% infection rate in which implant removal was required in 58% of infections.* *In their series the significant patient risk factors for infection were spina bifida and BMI >25mg/kg.* However, **they note** that most patients had been nutritionally optimized prior to surgery so the number with albumin<3.5 was very small. In their series, appropriate antibiotic dosing and drain usage were also significant in assessment of infection risk.

Incorrect answers:
Answers 1,2,4,5: Studies have supported and shown poor nutritional status, immunocompromised status, VP shunt presence and severe disease as significant risk factors for wound complications.
