## Question

Single-leg stance AP pelvis radiographs can be useful to assess which of the following?

## Options

1. Stability of the hip joint in a patient with a posterior wall acetabulum fracture
2. Vertical translation of the pubis in evaluation of a chronic pelvic ring injury
3. Sacroiliac joint widening in a patient with an acute, complete sacral fracture and bilateral pubic rami fractures
4. Pubic symphysis diastasis in the evaluation of a chronic pelvic ring injury
5. Stability of the hip joint in a patient with an anterior wall acetabulum fracture

## Correct option

**2. Vertical translation of the pubis in evaluation of a chronic pelvic ring injury**

## Answer explanation

Chronic instability of the pelvic ring can be evaluated by anteroposterior pelvic radiographs with the patient alternating between right and left single-leg stance, otherwise known as flamingo views.

The pelvic ring is formed by two innominate bones joined posteriorly through the sacrum and anteriorly by the symphysis pubis. The pelvic ring has no inherent bony stability. The pubic symphysis provides anterior stability, and posterior stability comes from the anterior and posterior sacroiliac ligaments (posterior are stronger than anterior). The ligaments of the pelvic floor include the sacrospinous ligament, which resists external rotation, and the sacrotuberous ligament, which resists shear as well as external rotation. Acute, traumatic disruption of pelvic ring stability is usually diagnosed through static imaging alone (standard pelvic radiographs and CT scan) on the basis of substantial displacement. Static imaging alone may not demonstrate the subtle nature of chronic pelvic ring instability. Single-leg stance causes a vertical shear and tensile force across the sacroiliac joint and a vertical shear and compressive force across the pubic symphysis. Thus, these “flamingo views” can help provide a dynamic evaluation of motion in the workup of suspected pelvic ring injuries.

*Garras et al. evaluated the amount of physiologic motion present at the pubic symphysis in normal adult men and nulliparous and multiparous women with alternating single-leg-stance radiographs.* **They found** that the mean total vertical translation (and standard deviation) at the pubic symphysis was 1.4 mm, 1.6 mm, and 3.1 mm for the men, nulliparous women, and multiparous women, respectively, and suggest that up to 5 mm of physiologic motion can occur at the pubic symphysis in asymptomatic individuals.

*Sanzone et al. provided a case report of a 50-year-old man who had severe, functionally limiting anterior pelvic pain and 12 mm of vertical translation on a right-sided single-leg stance AP pelvis radiographs.* It was noted that a physical exam is a key component during the evaluation of a suspected chronic pelvic ring injury. Specifically, the physical exam should include: 1) Palpation of the pubic symphysis at rest 2) Assessment of anterior/posterior pelvic pain with manual compression of both innominate bones 3) Evaluation of anterior/posterior pelvic pain with compression force applied to the exposed iliac crest with the patient lying on their side 4) Evaluation of anterior/posterior pelvic pain with resisted leg abduction. **They also suggest** that time from initial injury is important to consider during evaluation, as anterior pelvic instability can propagate to disrupt posterior stabilizing structures.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The best method to assess hip stability in the setting of a posterior wall acetabulum fracture is an examination under anesthesia. The hip is positioned in flexion and adduction, and an axial load is applied and opening of the medial clear space (in an obturator oblique radiograph) suggests instability of the posterior wall fracture.
Answer 3: Such a patient has a fracture pattern that has disrupted anterior and posterior pelvic ring stability, and acute surgical fixation is warranted (to include sacral iliac screw fixation).
Answer 4: Single-leg stance help evaluate for VERTICAL shear and compressive force across the pubic symphysis (not diastasis)
Answer 5: Stability of the hip joint in an anterior wall fracture would be determined examination under anesthesia and looking at iliac oblique Judet views and assessing congruity of the hip joint with a CT scan.
