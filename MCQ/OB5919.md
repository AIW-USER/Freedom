## Question

A 62-year-old male sustains a fall and presents with right shoulder pain. Figures A and B are his radiographs. He undergoes closed reduction and is discharged home in a sling. Two weeks later he is seen in your clinic and is unable to actively abduct at the right shoulder despite the deltoid muscle firing. Passive range of motion is full. Sensation is intact throughout the right upper extremity. Figure C is an illustration of the brachial plexus. The structure that is most likely injured is innervated by which nerve in Figure C?

## Options

## Correct option

**2. B**

## Answer explanation

This patient has most likely sustained a rotator cuff tear involving the supraspinatus +/- infraspinatus in the setting of an anterior glenohumeral dislocation. The supraspinatus and infraspinatus are innervated by the suprascapular nerve (B).

Traumatic anterior GH dislocations can occur with a variety of associated injuries. In young patients, anterior GH dislocations are associated with a high rate of labral injuries, most commonly anteroinferior labral tears (Bankart lesions), and osseous injuries, such as Hill Sachs defects and Bony Bankart lesions. In older patients (> 40 years of age), anterior GH dislocations are commonly associated with rupture of the rotator cuff.

*Neviaser et al performed a case study of 31 older patients (mean age = 57.5 years) with inability to abduct the arm following closed reduction of an anterior shoulder dislocation.* On arthrography, 100% of patients had rupture of the rotator cuff, most commonly the supraspinatus (SS) and infraspinatus (IS). All patients who experienced recurrent dislocation had subscapularis (SB) tears. Only 7.8% of all patients had injury to the axillary nerve.

*Jouve et al authored a retrospective case series looking at 27 patients (mean age = 47 years) with recurrent anterior shoulder instability.* One hundred percent of patients had full thickness rotator cuff tears. The SS tendon alone was most commonly involved (43%), followed by SS + IS (35%), SS + SB (4%) and all three tendons (18%). Patients who underwent anterior stabilization (Latarjet procedure) + rotator cuff repair had no episodes of recurrence and better functional outcome scores.

Figures A and B are AP and scapular Y radiographs of the right shoulder demonstrating an anterior glenohumeral joint dislocation. Figure C and Illustration A are unlabeled and labeled diagrams, respectively, of the brachial plexus.

Incorrect Responses:
Answer 1: A = Dorsal scapular nerve – innervates the rhomboid major/minor and the levator scapulae.
Answer 3: C = Lateral pectoral nerve – innervates the pectoralis major.
Answer 4: D = Musculocutaneous nerve – innervates the biceps brachii, brachialis and coracobrachialis.
Answer 5: E = Axillary nerve – innervates the deltoid and teres minor. The axillary nerve can be injured itself in a small percentage of anterior shoulder dislocations, resulting in a transient neuropraxia, but is far less common than a rotator cuff tear in an older patient. Furthermore, the patient has intact sensation.
