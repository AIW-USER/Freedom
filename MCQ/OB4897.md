## Question

A 5-year-old boy with cerebral palsy returns to your office for routine follow-up. He meets Gross Motor Function Classification System level V criteria. Since his last visit, his mother complains it is getting more difficult to get him well seated and change his diapers. She notes pain during sitting and during any movements of either hip. Physical examination reveals limited abduction with tight adductors along with pain with any hip of range of motion. His mother notes that he has already had several months of therapy and has not improved. His radiograph is shown in Figure A. What is the most appropriate treatment?

## Options

1. Observation
2. Bilateral adductor releases alone
3. Bilateral adductor releases with bilateral femoral shortening osteotomies
4. Bilateral adductor releases with left varus derotational osteotomy
5. Bilateral adductor releases, bilateral varus derotational osteotomies, and possible pelvic osteotomies

## Correct option

**5. Bilateral adductor releases, bilateral varus derotational osteotomies, and possible pelvic osteotomies**

## Answer explanation

Patient has cerebral palsy (CP) with criteria meeting Gross Motor Function Classification System (GMFCS) level V. His mother is having difficulty taking care of him and he has pain and difficulty with sitting in association with coxa valga and a left hip dislocation, making him an operative candidate. Adductor release will improve hygeine, and the varus procedure will correct the coxa valga and reduce the left hip dislocation. A bilateral procedure is required to maintain symmetry for sitting. Acetabular osteotomies are controversial and could be done in one stage surgery or later if significant dysplasia develops.

*Bischof et al. reported a series of non-ambulating CP children with either uni- or bilateral hip dislocations.* Predictors for significant pain secondary to dislocation were responses during diaper changes, transfers, or improper positioning during sitting.

*Gamble et al. in a retrospective study of 24 CP patients meeting GMFCS V criteria noted that best results were obtained with combined soft tissue releases and osteotomies when indicated.* Soft tissue release alone led to poor results and reoperations.

Figures A and B exhibit the AP and frog pelvis radiograph with bilateral dislocated, coxa valga hips with minimal acetabular dysplasia. Note, the femoral heads are relatively well-seated and the tri-radiates are still open leaving potential for future remodeling.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1. Intervention is indicated as the patient is symptomatic
Answer 2. Soft tissue procedures alone will not suffice. A migration index > 60% is associated with failure of soft tissue surgery
Answer 3. Femoral shortening alone will not correct the noted deformity, a varus producing osteotomy is required
Answer 4. Both sides should be addressed to maintain symmetry of the neck shaft angles.
