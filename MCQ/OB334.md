## Question

A 14-year-old girl complains of left midfoot pain with activity. She has recently sustained multiple inversion sprains of this ankle. Radiographs of the left foot are shown in figures A and B. Shoe modification and bracing and therapy have failed to provide relief. Surgical management should consist of:

## Options

1. Resection of periarticular osteophytes at the talonavicular joint
2. Excision of the os trigonum
3. Mobilization of the flexor hallucis longus and excision of Steida's tubercle
4. Resection of the calcaneonavicular coalition and interposition of the extensor digitorum brevis
5. Resection of the talonavicular coalition and interposition of the flexor hallucis longus

## Correct option

**4. Resection of the calcaneonavicular coalition and interposition of the extensor digitorum brevis**

## Answer explanation

The clinical presentation is consistent with symptomatic calcaneonavicular coalitions that has failed extensive nonoperative treatment. The most appropriate treatment includes resection of the coalition and interposition of the extensor digitorum brevis.

Calcaneonavicular coalition is the most commonly encountered tarsal coalition, presenting clinically in adolescent or teenage patients with painful rigid pes planus and multiple "ankle sprains." Surgical intervention is indicated in patients failing nonoperative measures including rest, orthotics with medial arch support, immobilization, and symptomatic treatment. Calcaneonavicular coalition resection with local interposition of the extensor digitorum brevis is the treatment of choice. Additionally, Achilles lengthening and calcaneus osteotomy may be necessary for contracture and deformity correction, respectively.

*Cooperman et al. performed a three-dimensional anatomic study evaluating the relationship and morphology of the subtalar and transverse tarsal articulations.* *They evaluated 5964 feet from 2982 intact human skeletons and identified 37 calcaneonavicular coalitions in 26 skeletons (incidence 1.2%).* **They noted** significant variation in the degree of subtalar and transverse tarsal joint involvement between individual coalitions, which in some cases would lead to theoretical instability if resected. **They concluded** that this may contribute to unsatisfactory results in patients undergoing coalition resection and extensor digitorum brevis interposition for calcaneonavicular coalition.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The patient does not present with significant talonavicular arthritis, and osteophyte resection would not address the underlying calcaneonavicular coalition causing the patient's pain.
Answer 2: Excision of a symptomatic os trigonum is a surgical treatment option for posterior ankle impingement. Such a patient would present with posterior ankle pain exacerbated by forced plantarflexion. The current patient's presentation is not consistent with this diagnosis.
Answer 3: Mobilization of the flexor hallucis longus and excision of an elongated Stieda's tubercle (posterolateral process of the talus) is a surgical treatment option for posterior ankle impingement. The current patient's presentation is not consistent with this diagnosis.
Answer 5: The current patient does not have a talonavicular coalition.
