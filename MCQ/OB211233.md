## Question

A 13-year-old girl with fevers, chills, and rigor for the last 2 weeks presents with the MRI findings shown in Figure A. She undergoes CT-guided biopsy and culture. Final cultures reveal methicillin-resistant staphylococcus aureus (MRSA). She becomes septic and is taken to the operating room for urgent surgical debridement. Which of the following is often associated with this diagnosis and requires close surveillance in the acute setting?

![[q211233-a-casereports-2018-september-2018-f1-large.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Septic arthritis
2. Gas gangrene
3. Squamous cell carcinoma
4. Marjolin's ulcer
5. Deep vein thrombosis (DVT)

## Correct option

**5. Deep vein thrombosis (DVT)**

## Answer explanation

In a pediatric patient with MRSA tibial osteomyelitis who has undergone surgical debridement, close surveillance for DVTs should be undertaken. While DVTs are an uncommon complication in pediatric patients, there has been an observed increase in the incidence of DVTs with rising rates of MRSA.

The risk factors for the development of a DVT after pediatric osteomyelitis include surgical treatment, CRP > 6 mg/L, MRSA isolate, and age > 8 years. Several studies have suggested that MRSA exotoxins, such as the Panton-Valentine leukocidin (PVL) results in leukocyte lysis and additional injury to endothelial surfaces, which ultimately lead to microthrombosis and DVT. Thus, imaging studies such as doppler venous ultrasound should be considered during the evaluation of this complication.

*Montgomery et al. reviewed pediatric osteomyelitis and its complications.* **They reported** that DVT is a known complication of pediatric osteomyelitis (particularly involving the tibia), is more common in boys than girls, and the most commonly occurs with MRSA. They theorized that leukocidin, a product of PVL, may be responsible for DVTs in these patients.

*Martínez-Aguilar et al. investigated the clinical characteristics and virulence factors related to osteomyelitis caused by community-acquired MRSA and methicillin-susceptible staphylococcus aureus in the pediatric population.* **They reported** an increased rate of DVTs in patients who were positive for the PVL, which was more common in the MRSA cohort. **They concluded** that PVL was predictive of complications including DVT and chronic osteomyelitis.

Figure A depicts osteomyelitis localized in the metaphyseal region of the proximal tibia in a skeletally-immature patient. Illustration A demonstrates the mechanism by which PVL secreted by MRSA leads to endothelial cell damage during the formation of DVT.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Septic arthritis of the knee is not as common in patients with tibial metaphyseal osteomyelitis since the metaphysis is not intra-articular (unlike the shoulder, hip, elbow, and ankle).
Answer 2: Gas gangrene does not have a high association with MRSA osteomyelitis.
Answer 3 and 4: While Marjolin's ulcer and the subsequent development of a squamous cell carcinoma may occur in cases of chronic osteomyelitis, these are not as common in the pediatric population and would also not occur acutely.
