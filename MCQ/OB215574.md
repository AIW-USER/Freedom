## Question

A 17-year-old patient is scheduled to undergo distraction osteogenesis of his femur due to a limb length discrepancy. During the first week following the osteotomy, how much distraction should occur?

## Options

1. No lengthening should occur during this time
2. 3.5mm of lengthening (0.5mm per day)
3. 7mm of lengthening (1mm per day)
4. 14mm of lengthening (2mm per day)
5. 21mm of lengthening (3mm per day)

## Correct option

**1. No lengthening should occur during this time**

## Answer explanation

When lengthening, distraction is not initiated until 7 days after surgery. After this latency period distraction typically occurs at a rate of 1 mm per day.

Limb lengthening of a short limb is indicated when there is a >5 cm projected limb length discrepancy (LLD). Often, lengthening is combined with a shortening procedure (epiphysiodesis, ostectomy) on long side when there is remaining growth. Distraction osteogenesis allows for controlled lengthening of the shorter limb. A latency period, which is the time before distraction is initiated following surgery, occurs during the first week. Following this latency period, a distraction of 1mm per day (usually as 0.25mm 4x per day) is often undertaken until the desired lengthening is achieved. Rapid distraction (>1mm per day) may result in painful neuropathy and soft-tissue complications.

*White et al. compared immediate with delayed distraction in rabbit tibias.* **They reported** a 2.6 times greater mineralized callus volume and improved development of extraosseous blood supply with the 7-day latency. **They concluded** that delay is an important factor in promoting osteogenesis, believe to be partly mediated by an improvement in the extra-osseous blood supply.

*Yasui et al. reviewed the factors affecting the process of callus distraction in limb lengthening include the type of osteotomy, timing and rate of distraction, and stability of fixation.* Following a transverse osteotomy with delayed distraction (10 days), they reported on callus formation following variable rates of distraction (0.35 mm/12 hours, 0.7 mm/12 hours, 1.4 mm/12 hours), and noted intact blood vessels across the regenerate with the slower rates but complete disruption of the bridging vessels with the 1.4-mm/12-hours rate. **They concluded** that a waiting period after osteotomy is more practical than achieving immediate distraction after uncertain corticotomy.

*Compton et al. reviewed distraction osteogenesis utilizing periods of latency, distraction, and consolidation.* **They reported** that mechanical tension, controlled by the rate and rhythm of distraction, influences cell proliferation, angiogenesis, and genetic expression in the distraction gap. **They concluded** that high-speed distraction may result in painful neuropathy and soft-tissue complications.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 2-5: The acceptable parameters for distraction osteogenesis in the femur include a latency period (no distraction) for the first 7 days following surgery, followed by distraction at 1 mm per day
