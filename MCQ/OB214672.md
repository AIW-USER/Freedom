## Question

Figures A and B are the radiographs of a 43-year-old male who presents to the trauma bay with two closed injuries following a motorcycle accident. He was helmeted, but loss of consciousness was reported on scene. He is hemodynamically stable and now alert and communicating in the trauma bay. He reports that his pain is localized to the left hip and left wrist. Which of the following is the best next step?

![[q214672-a-screen-shot-2021-04-05-at-5-50-55-am.jpg]]
*Figure A*

![[q214672-b-screen-shot-2021-04-05-at-5-54-10-am.jpg]]
*Figure B*

## Options

1. CT pelvis
2. CT left hip
3. MRI left hip
4. Closed reduction left hip
5. Closed reduction left wrist

## Correct option

**4. Closed reduction left hip**

## Answer explanation

Upon recognition of a hip dislocation, the hip should be promptly reduced. A time to reduction of greater than 12 hours has been associated with adverse outcomes.

Native hip dislocations most commonly occur in the setting of high-energy trauma. The position of the hip at the time of impact determines the associated acetabular injury, with posterior wall fractures and posterior hip dislocations being the most common combination. Emergent closed reduction should be performed once a native hip dislocation is identified. An increase in adverse events occurs if the reduction is delayed greater than 12 hours. Advanced imaging should not be performed prior to reduction of the hip. Once the hip is reduced, skeletal traction may be necessary to assist in maintaining reduction.

*Moed et al. review the results of operative treatment of fractures of the posterior wall of the acetabulum.* **They report** variables related to unsatisfactory clinical results following these injuries is a delay of greater than 12 hours to reduction of the hip, age >55-years-old at the time of injury, intraarticular comminution, and osteonecrosis. **They conclude** that prompt reduction of a dislocated hip should be performed to improve outcomes.

*Foulk et al. review the evaluation and management of hip dislocations.* **They report** that these injuries most commonly occur following high-energy motor vehicle accidents, and are associated with post-traumatic arthritis. The incidence of arthritis is much lower with simple dislocations than with complex dislocations associated with fractures of the acetabulum. **They conclude** that hips should be reduced emergently in an atraumatic fashion in both simple and complex hip dislocations.

Figure A is an AP pelvis radiograph demonstrating a complex left posterior hip dislocation. Figure B demonstrates AP and lateral radiographs of an intra-articular distal radius fracture with volar displacement.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: A CT scan of the pelvis should be obtained AFTER the hip is reduced to ensure there are no intra articular loose bodies requiring removal. It is not uncommon to see a portion of the posterior acetabular wall or the femoral head retained in the joint, necessitating removal to prevent third body chondral wear.
Answer 2: A CT scan of the hip should be obtained following reduction, not prior.
Answer 3: MRI of the left hip is not indicated in the setting of a traumatic dislocation.
Answer 5: Reduction of the hip should be performed prior to reduction of the wrist.
