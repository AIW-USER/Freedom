## Question

A 22-year-old collegiate baseball pitcher presents to your office with complaints of medial-sided elbow pain in his throwing elbow for the past 5 months. On examination, he has tenderness over the medial epicondyle. He also has apprehension with the maneuver depicted in Figure A. Which phase of the throwing cycle (Figure B) most likely will reproduce his symptoms?

## Options

2. B

## Correct option

**2. B**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a medial ulnar collateral ligament (MUCL) injury. Valgus overload is likely to reproduce his symptoms and is most pronounced during the late cocking phase (B) of the throwing cycle.

MUCL injuries are characterized by attenuation or rupture of the ulnar collateral ligament of the elbow leading to valgus instability in overhead throwing athletes. The late cocking phase of throwing leads to a high torque phase with maximal shoulder external rotation and results in valgus loading of the elbow. The FDS and FCU muscles provide local dynamic stability of the elbow during late cocking and are protective against injury to MUCL. Diagnosis is usually made by a combination of physical exam and MRI studies. Treatment should start with a shutdown period with physical therapy focusing on peri-scapular and forearm strengthening, and core stability. If this fails, surgery would be a reasonable option.

*In articles by Safran as well as Safran et al., the author(s) reviewed the anatomy, biomechanics, and pathophysiology of injury to the MUCL and injuries to the other structures that result from MUCL injury.* **They reported** that sports requiring an overhead motion, such as throwing a ball, hitting a ball overhead, or serving a tennis ball, impart a valgus stress on the elbow that is resisted by the UCL. **They note** that repeated or excessive valgus stress places a force on the MUCL that may result in injury to the ligament. **They concluded** that injury to the MUCL may result in problems in other areas of the elbow, including the ulnar nerve, the flexor-pronator musculotendinous unit, the radiocapitellar joint and the posterior compartment of the elbow, in addition to being a cause of loose bodies within the elbow.

Figure A depicts the milking maneuver, which is performed by creating a valgus stress by pulling on the patient's thumb with the forearm supinated and elbow flexed at 90 degrees. This may be performed with the patient either supine or seated/standing. A positive test is a subjective apprehension, instability, or pain at the MCL origin. Figure B is an illustration of the throwing cycle. Illustration A is the labeled phases of throwing.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Early cocking has peak muscle activation of the deltoid.
Answer 3: Early acceleration has peak muscle activation of the triceps, while late acceleration has peak muscle activation of the pectoralis major, latissimus dorsi, and serratus anterior
Answer 4: Deceleration results in eccentric contraction of all muscles is required to slow down arm motion, has the highest torque phase, and is recognized as the most harmful phase of throwing. Associated pathology during this phase of throwing include superior labrum (SLAP lesion), biceps tendon injury, brachialis injury, and teres minor injury.
Answer 5: Follow through is a phase where body rebalances and stops forward motion. Muscle activity returns to resting levels.
