## Question

A patient presents to your office with chronic knee pain who has failed all conservative measures. They are inquiring about a total knee replacement and the different implant designs. Which of the patients below would benefit most from a posterior cruciate retaining total knee arthroplasty?

## Options

1. 52-year-old female with severe rheumatoid arthritis of the knee
2. 73-year-old male with post-traumatic arthritis of the knee and prior patellectomy
3. 67-year-old male with degenerative arthritis and 10 degree valgus deformity of the knee
4. 55-year-old male with post-traumatic arthritis of the knee 20 years after bicruciate ligament ruptures
5. 63-year-old female with a chronic history of steroid treatment of systemic lupus erythematosus and an arthritic knee

## Correct option

**3. 67-year-old male with degenerative arthritis and 10 degree valgus deformity of the knee**

## Answer explanation

Among the patient scenarios presented in this question, a 67-year-old male with degenerative osteoarthritis (OA) and a 10-degree valgus knee deformity would benefit most from a posterior cruciate-retaining total knee arthroplasty (CR-TKA)

CR-TKA knees require a functional posterior cruciate ligament (PCL) to produce femoral rollback during knee flexion. Posterior-stabilized (PS-TKA) knees rely on the component to produce femoral rollback. Contraindications to using a CR knee include any condition that may render the PCL incompetent at the time of surgery or in the future, including prior PCL rupture, inflammatory arthritis, patellectomy, and over-release of the PCL during surgery. Mild varus or valgus deformity is not a contraindication.

*Gonzalez et al reviews the appropriate evaluation and management of the patient with unsatisfactory results following TKA.* The most common etiologies include pain, stiffness, and instability.

Illustrations A and B demonstrate the differences between posterior cruciate retaining knees and posterior-stabilized knees in regards to femoral rollback.

Incorrect answer choices:
Answers 1,2,4, and 5: Posterior cruciate-retaining TKA implants are contraindicated in conditions that may render the PCL incompetent, including a history of rheumatoid arthritis, history of patellectomy, bicruciate ligament ruptures, and a history of systemic lupus erythematosus.
