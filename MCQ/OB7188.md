## Question

What is the etiology of the bone lesion shown in the radiograph in Figure 100a and the corresponding histopathology shown in Figure 100b?

## Options

1. Gs alpha gene mutation causing constitutive action of cAMP
2. Ring chromosome 12
3. Metastatic spread of primary neoplasm
4. Parathyroid tumor
5. 11:22 translocation gene mutation

## Correct option

**1. Gs alpha gene mutation causing constitutive action of cAMP**

## Answer explanation

The radiograph and histopathology reveal fibrous dysplasia. Fibrous dysplasia is the result of a somatic mutation of the Gs alpha subunit resulting in deficient inhibition of cAMP, improper formation of calcified osteoid, and high bone turnover. The histology depicts the disconnected haphazard woven bone with interspersed fibrous tissue. The imaging and histology do not demonstrate a malignancy, nor is this the appearance of "brown tumors" as seen with hyperparathyroidism. Ring chromosome 12 is associated with low-grade osteogenic sarcoma. The 11:22 EWS:FLI1 translocation is commonly seen in Ewing's sarcoma, which is not seen in this individual.
