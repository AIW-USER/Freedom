## Options

1. Decreased incidence of strength loss compared to baseline
2. Decreased incidence of unplanned surgeries
3. Decreased risk of nonunion
4. Faster return to full weightbearing activity
5. Significant improvement in overhead range of motion

## Correct option

**3. Decreased risk of nonunion**

## Answer explanation

Mid-shaft clavicle fractures, when treated operatively, have been shown to have reduced rates of nonunion and symptomatic malunion, but have not shown statistically significant differences in terms of rates of unplanned surgeries or clinically significant differences in functional outcomes, despite the early published findings from the frequently cited Canadian Orthopaedic Trauma Society trial from 2007.

Clavicle fractures are common traumatic injuries caused by direct impact to the shoulder girdle, are most commonly seen in young, active adults, and most commonly occur in the middle-third of the shaft (75-80% of cases). Treatment can either be operative or non-operative, with relative indications for operative fixation being displacement causing >2cm of shortening, presence of bilateral clavicle fractures, and polytraumatized patients. Non-operative treatment has been shown to result in non-union in up to 15% of patients, while operative treatment results in nonunion in 1-5% of patients, with hardware prominence occurring in up to 8-30% of operatively treated patients.

The Canadian Orthopaedic Trauma Society (COTS) published a multicenter, randomized clinical trial of 132 patients with displaced mid-shaft fractures of the clavicle. They found that Constant and DASH scores were improved in the operative fixation group at all points in time, with union time being 28 weeks in the nonoperative group and 16 weeks in the operative group. Malunion was higher in the nonoperative group as well. They concluded that operative fixation of a displaced fracture of the clavicular shaft results in improved functional outcome and a lower rate of malunion and nonunion compared with nonoperative treatment at one year of follow-up, with hardware removal remaining the most common reason for repeat intervention in the operative group.

*Axelrod et al. performed a systematic review and network meta-analysis of 22 randomized controlled trials involving operative versus non-operative treatment of clavicle fractures.* **The authors found** that, despite the findings published by COTS in 2007, subsequent literature has not supported the rest of their evaluated parameters (other than union rates) when compared to non-surgical treatment, noting in particular that surgery did not result in clinically important improvements in validated Constant-Murley and DASH scores. **They concluded** that, based on more recent literature and pooled effects studies, patients should not assume that they will achieve better function after surgery.

*Amer et al. performed a systematic review and meta-analysis of the literature surrounding operative versus nonoperative outcomes of middle-third clavicle fractures.* *The authors reviewed 11 studies including 497 patients treated surgically and 457 managed conservatively.* **They found** that plate fixation significantly reduced the risk of nonunion, but concluded that there was no difference in Constant–Murley or DASH scores between the two treatment groups or in the rate of secondary operative procedures.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1, 4, 5: Despite the findings published in the often-cited COTS trial of 2007, more recent studies have not shown statistically significant differences in functional outcomes in terms of strength, range of motion, or time to return to weightbearing activity between operatively and non-operatively managed mid-shaft clavicle fractures.
Answer 2: The literature has not shown a statistically significant difference in the incidence of unplanned surgeries with plating of clavicle fractures; however, plate prominence leads to hardware removal in up to 30% of patients.
