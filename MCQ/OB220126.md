## Question

A 22-year-old male presents to the emergency department with a laceration over his dorsal hand. He sustained the injury five days ago, but chose to come in today because he has been having fevers and erythema around his wound. He is apprehensive about giving a complete history, but ultimately admits that he punched someone in the mouth during an altercation at a theme park. Which bacteria occurs more frequently in human bites than in cat and dog bites?

## Options

1. Capnocytophaga canimorsus
2. Eikenella corodens
3. Pasteurella canis
4. Pseudomonas aeruginosa
5. Staphylococcus aureus

## Correct option

**2. Eikenella corodens**

## Answer explanation

Eikenella corodens (Answer choice 2) is the most characteristic pathogen for human bite injuries (isolated 17-30% of the time). It is rarely isolated from dog or cat bites.

Dog, cat, and human bite injuries differ in terms of infection risk, microbial composition, and severity. Dog bites are the most common and typically result in crushing or tearing injuries due to the strong jaw force, but they have a lower infection rate (approximately 5–15%) compared to cat and human bites. Cat bites, though less frequent, pose a higher infection risk (30–50%) due to their deep puncture wounds. Human bites, especially fight bites, also have a high infection risk. Animal and human injuries are often polymicrobial and can result in deep-space infections. Eikenella is uniquely isolated more commonly in human fight bites, whereas Pasteurella isolation is more characteristic of dog and cat bites. Bite injuries should be treated with irrigation and debridement and antibiotics.

Eikenella corrodens is a facultative anaerobic, gram-negative rod that is a pathogen characteristically isolated from human bite injuries. It is isolated in 17-30% of fight bites. Notably, most of these injuries are also polymicrobial with S. aureus being the overall most common pathogen isolated. Eikenella is often resistant to certain empiric antibiotics, such as aminoglycosides, first-generation cephalosporins, and clindamycin. The preferred antibiotic regimen includes beta-lactam/beta-lactamase inhibitor combinations (e.g., amoxicillin-clavulanate) or a combination of penicillin with metronidazole to cover anaerobes. In severe cases, intravenous antibiotics may be required.

*Kennedy et al. discussed human and mammalian bite injuries of the hand,* **highlighting** the high risk of polymicrobial infections, including Eikenella corrodens, Streptococcus, and anaerobes. **The authors emphasize** the importance of early wound evaluation, proper irrigation, and empiric antibiotic therapy, typically including amoxicillin-clavulanate, to prevent serious morbidity. Their conclusions stress that timely intervention, along with surgical debridement when necessary, is essential to optimizing outcomes and minimizing functional impairment in bite-related hand injuries.

*Shoji et al. discussed the management of human bite injuries to the hand,* **emphasizing** the high risk of infection due to oral flora, including Eikenella corrodens. **They highlight** the importance of early intervention, recommending prompt surgical exploration, irrigation, and appropriate antibiotic therapy to prevent severe complications. **The authors conclude** that timely and aggressive treatment is essential to preserve hand function in patients with such injuries.

*Osterman et al. reviewed the increasing challenge of treating hand infections due to the rise of antibiotic-resistant bacteria and the limited development of new antibiotics over the past five decades.* **They emphasize** that prompt diagnosis and treatment are crucial to preventing hand stiffness, contractures, or amputation. **The authors conclude** that trauma is the most common cause of these infections, with subcutaneous tissue being the most frequently affected site.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: Capnocytophaga canimorsus is more common in animal bites than human bites.
Answer 3: Pasteurella canis is more commonly isolated from dog and cat bites compared to human bites.
Answer 4: Pseudomonas is a less common pathogen to be isolated from animal or human bite injuries. It most commonly affects immunocompromised patients.
Answer 5: Staphylococcus aureus is the most common pathogen identified in human bite wounds, but is also commonly found in dog and cat bite wounds.
