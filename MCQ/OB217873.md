## Question

A 78-year-old female with a past medical history of diabetes, coronary artery disease and hypertension presents to your office after being seen at a local urgent care for a distal radius fracture. Upon further evaluation, the fracture is extra-articular and nondisplaced with maintained radial height. You elect to manage the patient nonoperatively in a fracture brace. Which of the following complications is the patient most at risk for in this clinical scenario?

## Options

1. Acute carpal tunnel syndrome
2. Flexor pollicis longus (FPL) rupture
3. Ulnocarpal abutment syndrome
4. Extensor pollicis longus (EPL) rupture
5. Radiocarpal joint arthritis

## Correct option

**4. Extensor pollicis longus (EPL) rupture**

## Answer explanation

Extensor pollicis longus (EPL) rupture, while uncommon overall, is the complication most at risk after nonoperative treatment of a nondisplaced distal radius fracture.

Distal radius fractures account for >17% percent of all adult fractures and occur more commonly in older females with osteoporosis after a fall on an outstretched hand. Radiographic criteria exist to classify the severity of the fracture and identify the need for surgical intervention. These criteria include radial height (nl=13mm), radial inclination (nl=23º), articular step-off (nl=0mm), and volar tilt (nl=11º). In cases where nonoperative intervention is considered, EPL rupture is the most commonly identified complication. This is thought to be spontaneous due to attritional wear and local ischemia of the tendon after adjacent fracture. Usually, an extensor indicis proprius (EIP) to EPL tendon transfer is necessary as poor EPL tendon quality does not lend itself to easy repair.

*Gelb published a review on tendon transfers used for EPL ruptures.* **They note** that the most likely cause of rupture is interruption of vascularity secondary to pressure from a hematoma, causing the tendon to later suffer from ischemic necrosis. **They recommend** EIP tendon transfer as the most predictable means of restoring function of the EPL, with low rates of complication.

*Shah et al. investigated the effects of EPL transposition and EIP transfer on thumb biomechanics.* **They noted** that EPL transposition out of the 3rd dorsal compartment decreased the adduction moment arm significantly, as did subcutaneous EIP to EPL transfer. Conversely, **they noted** that EIP to EPL transfer through an intact extensor retinacular pulley produced adduction moment arms that resembled an intact EPL.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: While acute carpal tunnel syndrome is possible even in distal radius fractures treated nonoperatively, it is less likely to occur compared to attritional EPL rupture.
Answer 2: FPL rupture is more commonly seen after ORIF with volar plating of distal radius fractures distal to the watershed line.
Answer 3: Ulnocarpal abutment syndrome can occur after a distal radius fracture which heals in a shortened position, but is unlikely in a nondisplaced fracture where radial height is maintained.
Answer 5: Radiocarpal joint arthritis would be unlikely to occur after a nondisplaced, extra-articular fracture of the distal radius as the joint is not involved.
