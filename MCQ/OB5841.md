## Question

A 15-year-old-female presents with mid-back pain and weakness in the right lower extremity. A T1 and T2 weighted MRI, as well as gadolinium enhanced MRI are shown in figure A. A needle biopsy is performed, with cytopathology demonstrated in figure B. Staging studies including CT chest, full body scintigraphy, and bone marrow biopsy are negative. Due to the morbidity of resection, local control with radiation therapy is being considered. What is the most likely diagnosis?

## Options

1. Liposarcoma
2. Chordoma
3. Ewing sarcoma
4. Osteogenic sarcoma
5. Chondrosarcoma

## Correct option

**3. Ewing sarcoma**

## Answer explanation

The patient presents with a "small round blue cell tumor of bone" and local control by radiation therapy is being considered. This is consistent with a diagnosis of Ewing sarcoma.

Ewing sarcoma is thought to be in the family of primitive neuroectodermal tumors, though its true origin cell remains unknown. It typically presents in children ages 8-15 years, who complain of a painful enlarging mass, which has been identified by incidental minor trauma. At the time of presentation, the presence of subclinical metastases is to be presumed, which makes local tumor control as well as systemic metastatic control imperative for successful treatment. Local control of Ewing's can be accomplished by wide resection, or, when the morbidity of resection is extreme, by radiation.

*Grubb et. al. report the Mayo Clinic experience with primary spinal Ewing's Sarcoma.* *From 1951 to 1988, 36 patients were treated, and the mean 5-year survival rate was 33%.* Treatment included radiation in all cases, chemo in rare cases. Twenty-one patients (58%) presented with a neurologic deficit, and 17 of them (81%) underwent posterior decompression. Only 12 patients (57%) had neurologic improvement after decompression.

Figure A is a T1 (a), T2 (b), and gadolinium-enhanced axial (c) and sagittal (d) MRI of the T9 spinal level which reveals an enhancing bony mass in the left hemivertebra involving the pedicle and extending into the canal, with mass effect upon the cord. Figure B is a high powered field demonstrating a "small round blue cell tumor" which is consistent with the diagnosis of Ewing Sarcoma.

Illustration A is a histology slide demonstrating liposarcoma with scattered lipoblasts with high-grade peripheral nucleoli.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Liposarcoma is the second most common adult soft tissue sarcoma and is characterized by high-grade lipoblasts on histology. Radiation is not effective for local control.
Answer 2: Chordoma is a spinal tumor typically of the clivus or sacrum which develops from the remnant notochord. Its histology is characterized by physaliferous cells. Radiation is not effective for local control.
Answer 4: Osteogenic sarcoma is a common primary tumor of bone characterized by high-grade cellularity and bone formation. Radiation is not adequate for local control.
Answer 5: Chondrosarcoma is a primary tumor of cartilage, and radiation is not adequate for local control.
