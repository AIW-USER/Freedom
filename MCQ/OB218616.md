## Options

1. Observation with annual follow up
2. Intralesional curettage and bone grafting
3. Wide resection and intercalary allograft reconstruction
4. Neoadjuvant chemotherapy, wide excision with limb salvage, adjuvant chemotherapy
5. Amputation of the extremity

## Correct option

**1. Observation with annual follow up**

## Answer explanation

This child presents with an anterior tibial lesion and biopsy findings consistent with osteofibrous dysplasia. These should be initially treated with observance unless there are concerns for a pending pathologic fracture.

Osteofibrous dysplasia (OFD) is a benign fibro-osseous tumor of long bones, almost exclusively arising in the anterior cortex of the tibia and/or fibula during childhood (<10 years old). Due to similar nomenclature, it is often confused for fibrous dysplasia (FD); however, both are distinct clinical entities. Fibrous dysplasia rarely occurs as an isolated tibial lesion and more commonly presents in the femur, ribs, or cranial bones. More importantly, OFD must be differentiated from an adamantinoma, as both appear nearly identical. Patient age is often highly suggestive of the underlying diagnosis; younger children (<10 years old) more often have OFD and lack aggressive radiographic features (i.e., multilayered periosteal reaction or moth-eaten destruction of an adamantinoma). If they are a young adult (15-35 years) or if there are red flags in presentation (changes in tumor size/morphology), then a biopsy is warranted to rule out adamantinoma. On histologic examination, OFD can be easily identified by its bland spindle cell (i.e., fibrous) stromal background with osteoblasts lining areas of woven bone (i.e., “osteoblastic rimming”, Illustration A). Fibrous dysplasia lacks this osteoblastic rimming around islands of woven bone. If there are areas (i.e., “clusters” or “nests”) of epithelial cells, this is pathognomonic for an adamantinoma requiring surgical treatment with wide resection.

*Most and colleagues reviewed osteofibrous dysplasia and adamantinoma.* *They reviewed the overall clinical presentation, treatment options, and outcomes.* Importantly, they discussed how to differentiate each lesion from one another. While **they report** a possibility of OFD and Adamantinoma being related on a disease spectrum, increasing evidence would suggest otherwise.

*Scholfield and colleagues queried their institution’s tumor registry for long-term follow-up on patients with OFD.* *In one of the largest retrospective cohort studies to date (n=73), they did not have any OFD patients undergo malignant transformation to an adamantinoma.* **They conclude** that their findings support OFD and adamantinoma as separate clinical diagnoses that do not fall on a spectrum of diseases.

*Westacott and colleagues reviewed the long-term outcomes of their patients with OFD.* **They reported** that no cases resulted in transformation to adamantinoma; however, bilateral OFD involvement was more often symptomatic, requiring surgical intervention. **They conclude** that surgical intervention should only be utilized if required and not as a prophylactic treatment.

Figure A shows radiographic imaging of a skeletally immature patient with an eccentric cortical expanding lesion along the anterior diaphysis of the tibia, consistent with osteofibrous dysplasia. Illustration A displays the key histologic findings contrasting fibrous dysplasia, osteofibrous dysplasia, and adamantinoma.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Intralesional curettage and bone grafting would be considered appropriate for OFD when the patient is experiencing pain secondary to pending pathologic fracture. However, this patient’s OFD was found incidentally and did not complain of preexisting symptoms justifying surgical intervention.
Answer 3: Wide resection and intercalary allograft reconstruction would be an appropriate option if the patient was found to have an adamantinoma. However, his biopsy results show the characteristic osteoblastic rimming seen in OFD and did not display keratin positive clusters of epithelial cells that are pathognomonic for adamantinoma.
Answer 4: Neoadjuvant chemotherapy, wide excision with limb salvage, and adjuvant chemotherapy would be considered an appropriate option if the patient had osteosarcoma or Ewing sarcoma.
Answer 5: Amputation, especially in the pediatric population, is reserved for patients with tumors that preclude the ability to perform limb salvage reconstructive surgery.
