## Question

Which of the following is true about the progression of rotator cuff tears?

## Options

1. Full-thickness cuff tears typically do not progress in size
2. An increase in symptoms does not correlate with increase in cuff tear size
3. Progression of pain is independent of rotator cuff muscle quality
4. Larger initial cuff tears are likely to progress faster
5. All asymptomatic cuff tears will eventually become symptomatic

## Correct option

**4. Larger initial cuff tears are likely to progress faster**

## Answer explanation

Approximately 50% of rotator cuff tears progress within 2-3 years of diagnosis, and those which are initially larger progress more quickly with regards to size and symptoms.

Rotator cuff tears represent a common subset of shoulder pathology and increase in incidence with increasing age. Approximately 1/3 of adults over the age of 60 have a full thickness rotator cuff tear, with this number increasing to greater than 60% at age 70. Many of these are asymptomatic, but about half of these tears will progress and become symptomatic within 2-3 years. An increase in symptoms is related to an increase in cuff tear size and a decrease in the quality of the rotator cuff musculature.

*Moosmayer et al. evaluated the natural history of asymptomatic rotator cuff tears three years after initial diagnosis.* **They noted** 36% became symptomatic over the course of the study and within this group there was a larger increase in tear size and muscle atrophy. **They concluded** by suggesting that close follow-up should be instituted in order to evaluate for tear progression.

*Mall et al. prospectively reviewed a large cohort of patients with asymptomatic cuff tears and* **found** 44% became symptomatic over a two-year period. Those who developed pain had a larger increase in tear size and lower ASES outcome scores. **They concluded** that the larger initial tears were more likely to progress and contribute to pain development.

*Yamaguchi et al. compared 588 symptomatic and asymptomatic patients with rotator cuff tears.* **They noted** a relationship between increasing age and rotator cuff tears as well as a significant correlation between the size of the tear and the presence of symptoms. **They also note** that there is a high degree of bilateral rotator cuff disease in patients with symptoms on one side.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: About 50% of full-thickness rotator cuff tears, regardless of symptom presence, will progress in size within 2-3 years of diagnosis.
Answer 2: As the size of the rotator cuff tear increases, symptoms will typically worsen accordingly.
Answer 3: An increase in muscle atrophy and an increase in fatty degeneration has been correlated with the progression of pain in rotator cuff tears.
Answer 5: While many asymptomatic rotator cuff tears will eventually become painful, there is a small subset of patients who do not have progression of pain.
