## Question

A 52-year-old woman has a 3-cm, slowly enlarging, well-circumscribed mass in the proximal forearm that is tender to deep palpation and elicits paresthesias to the volar index and middle fingers. MRI shows a T1-isointense, heterogenous T2-hyperintense ovoid lesion that is eccentrically related to the neurovascular bundle. Surgical excision is performed, and the lesion stains diffusely positive for S100 proteins. Which additional MRI feature would most strongly favor a benign rather than malignant tumor of nerve sheath origin?

## Options

1. Greater than 2cm of peritumoral edema
2. Upper extremity versus lower extremity location
3. A well-defined capsule surrounding the lesion
4. Lack of lesion enhancement on post-contrast T1-weighted imaging
5. Lobulated contour with two or more deep lobulations

## Correct option

**3. A well-defined capsule surrounding the lesion**

## Answer explanation

The history, imaging, and pathology findings are consistent with a diagnosis of a schwannoma. A well-defined capsule is one of several characteristics on MRI favoring a benign schwannoma over a malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumor (MPNST) (answer choice C).

Schwannomas (neurilemmomas) are benign, encapsulated peripheral nerve sheath tumors composed of Schwann cells, often arising on the surface of peripheral nerves. Typical MRI features include low T1 and high T2 signal with diffuse gadolinium enhancement; histology typically shows a zonal pattern composed of cellular areas (Antoni A) with nuclear palisading (Verocay bodies) as well as a hypocellular component (Antoni B) with strong and diffuse S100 immunostaining. Asymptomatic lesions without malignant features are typically observed, whereas symptomatic lesions may be treated with marginal excision with care to preserve fascicles. Recurrence is rare, and postoperative neurologic deficits are overall uncommon.

*Crist et al. presented a pictorial MRI review of schwannomas, describing typical low-to-isointense T1 and hyperintense fluid-sensitive signal with frequent diffuse enhancement and heterogeneous change from cystic/myxoid degeneration.* **They noted** that features favoring malignancy include ill-defined margins and larger size (>5 cm), whereas features supporting schwannoma include a defined capsule, split-fat and bright-rim signs, and limited peritumoral edema (<18 mm). **They concluded** that although these characteristics are helpful to recognize, diagnosis by imaging alone can be challenging.

*Gosk et al. reviewed a series of 34 patients (44 extremity schwannomas) treated surgically.* **They reported** high rates of pain and paresthesia resolution and a low risk of new permanent neurological deficits, with no recurrences observed. **They concluded** that gradual tumor growth causes compression neuropathy that may warrant surgery and that outcomes after excision are generally favorable.

*Hirai et al. conducted a multicenter retrospective analysis of extremity schwannomas focusing on postoperative neurological outcomes.* **They reported** overall complication rates and explored risk factors related to the nerve of origin and other variables. **They concluded** that while most postoperative issues are sensory and transient, nerve origin influences risk, and careful surgical planning is important.

Incorrect answer choices:
Answer choice A: Extensive peritumoral edema favors malignancy rather than a benign schwannoma.
Answer choice B: Upper versus lower extremity location has not been found to influence the risk of malignancy in these lesions.
Answer choice D: Benign schwannomas typically demonstrate intense enhancement on post-contrast T1-weighted MRI.
Answer choice E: A lobulated contour with two or more deep lobulations is not supportive of schwannoma and should raise concern for possible alternative diagnoses.
