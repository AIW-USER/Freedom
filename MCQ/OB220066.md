## Question

A 65-year-old male undergoes right total knee arthroplasty (TKA) after being extensively counseled on the risks, benefits, and alternatives to surgical management. Eight weeks after the patient's operation, he presents to the emergency department with worsening pain and expressible purulence from the distal extent of his surgical incision. What is the most likely reason the surgeon is required to provide a written response and all relevant medical records to the medical board in this clinical setting?

## Options

1. There was an automatic suspension of the physician's license due to the surgical complication
2. The surgeon was instructed to directly communicate with the patient's attorney to negotiate a settlement
3. A formal complaint was filed against the surgeon by the patient
4. The surgeon is mandated to retain an attorney which can be supplied through the medical board
5. The surgeon's operating privileges were immediately revoked at the treating hospital

## Correct option

**3. A formal complaint was filed against the surgeon by the patient**

## Answer explanation

In circumstances where a formal complaint has been filed by the patient, the physician needs to provide a written response to the medical board and needs to include all relevant medical records (Answer 3).

Dissatisfied patients can bring forth formal complaints by filing a complaint with the state medical board, complaining to the patient relations office or credentialing committee, directing a complaint to the hospital's chief of medical staff, or submitting a grievance to the medical society. Regardless of the modality of formal complaint, all formal complaints mandate a physician response letter with all relevant medical records attached. This process maintains accountability among surgeons and promotes self-advocacy among patients.

*DuBois et al. examined 280 cases of serious ethical violations including sexual abuse, criminal prescribing, and unnecessary surgeries.* **Their analysis revealed** a pattern of intentional wrongdoing, often by males in nonacademic settings. The most common reason for committing ethical violations was cited to be personal gain, oversight failures, and personality or substance use disorders in over 50% of the cases. **The authors conclude** that preventing these violations requires significant policy changes prioritizing patient safety and a multidisciplinary approach to improve oversight, discipline, and education reform.

*Levinson et al. reviewed the available tools to help physicians resolve disagreements within the patient-physician relationship.* The group emphasized that physicians must first ensure they are on ethically firm ground before addressing conflicts with patients, advocating for high-quality care, and, if necessary, resolving systemic issues with administrators. **The authors conclude** that effective communication strategies such as empathetically understanding patient concerns in conjunction with collaborative decision-making can foster mutual respect and successfully resolve conflict.

*Austin et al. reviewed the factors and the key indicators that identify doctors at risk of complaints, malpractice claims, or impaired performance.* *This review of 70 publications identified 23 key risk factors for malpractice claims and physician-impaired performance, categorized as demographic or workplace-related.* Factors such as gender, age, years in practice, patient load, substance abuse, and burnout were linked to increased risk. **The findings suggest** risk factors are interdependent and context-specific, highlighting the need for tailored support systems to reduce adverse events and enhance patient safety.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: Automatic suspension of a physician's license is not standard practice when a formal complaint is filed. However, a physician may experience automatic suspension if they are determined to be an imminent threat to the public.
Answer 2: Physicians are not encouraged to communicate directly with the patient's attorney to negotiate a settlement.
Answer 4: This is incorrect, surgeons are not mandated to retain an attorney.
Answer 5: In this clinical scenario, the patient experienced a known risk of TKA. Automatic removal of the surgeon's privileges at the treating facility is inappropriate.
