## Question

Which of the following landmarks and measurements most accurately describe the insertion of the Anterior Cruciate Ligament (ACL) on the tibia?

## Options

1. 5 mm posterior to the posterior border of the anterior horn of the lateral meniscus
2. 17 mm posterior to the inter-meniscal ligament
3. 11 mm posterior to the anterior border of the PCL
4. 11 mm anterior to the anterior border of the PCL
5. 11 mm anterior to the median eminence

## Correct option

**4. 11 mm anterior to the anterior border of the PCL**

## Answer explanation

The point approximately 11 mm anterior to the anterior border of the posterior cruciate ligament (PCL) can be used to assess accurate tibial tunnel placement when performing an anterior cruciate ligament reconstruction (ACLR).

Anatomic tunnel placement in ACLR creates knee kinematics closer to that of an intact knee. Positioning the tibial tunnel too anterior leads to tightness in flexion and impingement of the graft against the roof of the intercondylar notch in extension. Conversely, a tibial tunnel that is placed too posterior can result in impingement of the graft against the PCL.

*Hutchinson et al. evaluated the reproducibility of anatomic tibial landmarks for ACLR in 42 cadaveric knees.* **They found** the center of the ACL insertion was consistently 10.7 mm anterior to the anterior border of the PCL. The posterior edge of the ACL insertion was located 6.7 mm anterior to the anterior border of the PCL. **The authors recommend** using the old ACL stump and the rigid over-the-back landmark as primary guides for tunnel position and the PCL to verify correct graft placement.

*McGuire et al. performed a retrospective study of 20 patients who underwent ACLR with bone-patellar tendon-bone autograft, followed by a post-operative CT scan.* The distance from the posterior aspect of the tibial tunnel to the anterior edge of the PCL insertion, defined as “backset”, was 6.2mm. The backset value was found to vary with knee size, as measured by the anteroposterior (AP) diameter of the tibial plateau. **The authors recommend** a backset of 5 mm for small knees (AP diameter < 50mm), 6 mm for medium knees (AP diameter 50-60mm), and 7mm for large knees (AP diameter > 60mm).

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The insertion of the ACL is medial and anterior to the posterior border of the AHLM.
Answer 2: The insertion of the ACL has been described as approximately 9 mm posterior to the inter-meniscal ligament.
Answer 3: This would place the ACL insertion posterior to the PCL.
Answer 5: The insertion of the ACL has been described as approximately 6 mm anterior to the medial tibial eminence.
