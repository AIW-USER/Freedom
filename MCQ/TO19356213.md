## Question

In Multi directional shoulder instability, cause is [FNB 2023]

## Options

1. Inferior capsular redundancy
2. Bankart lesion
3. Hill Sachs lesion
4. Cuff tear

## Correct option

**1. Inferior capsular redundancy**

## Answer explanation

Ans: A
EXPLANATION: -
Instability is basically laxity involving a joint that is symptomatic. At shoulder instability is common and recurrent owing to peculiar anatomical features. Recurrent instability at shoulder can be
Trauma induced- Torn loose
Microtrauma induced- Worn loose (as in athletes)
From lax ligaments/ capsule- Born loose (as in collagen disorders)
Now,
Multidirectional shoulder instability (MDI) is a condition characterized by instability of the shoulder in at least 2 planes of motion (one of which has to be Inferior). The underlying mechanisms could include generalized ligamentous laxity (as in cases of Marfan or Ehler Danlos syndrome) or microtrauma to shoulder stabilizers from overuse injuries in overhead throwers; so these are born loose or worn loose category of patients.
The hallmark pathological finding includes a patulous inferior capsule on MRI (IGHL anterior and posterior bands) and rotator interval deficiency. Labral lesions (Bankart or Kim lesion- posterior inferior labral avulsion) or glenoid erosions can still be found as add ons from traumatic events.
Symptoms can at times be very deceptive and many patients who are usually in second/ third decade of life may simply come with complaints of weakness, pain or paraesthesia rather than frank instability. Impingement signs if present in patient under 20 years old warrants a search for MDI. Signs of generalized ligamentous laxity (Beighton criteria positive) can be looked for to catch a clue to strengthen the diagnosis.
In physical examination one has to look for Sulcus sign (to document patulous inferior capsule and rotator internal). It is checked by pulling the arm distally in 0 degree and 45 degrees of abduction. The shoulder is observed for a sulcus or dimple between the acromion and the humeral head.

![[TO19356213-1.png]]

Appearance of dimple points to redundant inferior capsule and is graded as below.
Grading :
• 1+ : acromiohumeral interval < 1cm
• 2+ : acromiohumeral interval 1-2cm
• 3+ : acromiohumeral interval >2cm

Laxity of rotator interval presents as increased external rotation with the arm fully adducted and at 90 degrees abduction. So, positive sulcus that is 2+ or 3+ with external rotation at side points to a pathologic rotator interval as well in addition to patulous inferior capsule.
The classical arthroscopic catch point is presence of “drive-through sign” on performing shoulder arthroscopy. A positive drive-through sign is considered the ability to pass an arthroscope easily between the humeral head and the glenoid at the level of the anterior band of the IGHL.
Initial treatment is predominantly non operative in form of rigrous physical therapy for a period of 3-6 months. Failure of non operative treatment with persistence of symptoms is the sole indication for operative intervention. Surgery if indicated involves: -
• Inferior capsular shift (to move capsule superiorly to address patulous inferior capsule)
• Capsular plication (plicating the loose capsule to labrum to decrease capsular volume both anteriorly and posteriorly in balanced fashion)
• Rotator interval closure
Since patients of MDI are basically cases of recurrent shoulder instability only; the only differentiating fact being they are non traumatic; Mathysen gave a useful physiological classification of recurrent instability that guides the treatment for both traumatic and non traumatic scenarios.
