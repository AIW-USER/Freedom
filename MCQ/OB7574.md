## Question

Figures 86a and 86b show the radiograph and biopsy specimen of a 16-year-old boy who reports increasing right foot pain. What is the most likely diagnosis?

*Figure A*

![[q7574-b-86b.jpg]]

## Options

1. Enchondroma
2. Giant cell tumor
3. Chondrosarcoma
4. Chondromyxoid fibroma
5. Nonossifying fibroma

## Correct option

**4. Chondromyxoid fibroma**

## Answer explanation

The radiograph shows a benign-appearing bubbly radiolucent lesion with a narrow zone of transition and a thin rim of surrounding reactive bone. The biopsy specimen demonstrates spindle cells with a myxoid cartilaginous matrix. The diagnosis of the lesion is a chondromyxoid fibroma. This rare, benign tumor is usually painful and can be locally aggressive. Enchondroma is typically associated with calcifications and a bland hyaline cartilage on histology. Giant cell tumors are typically radiolucent without the bubbly appearance seen here and composed of multinucleated giant cells with stromal cells having similar nuclear morphology. Chondrosarcoma is uncommon in the hands and feet. Nonossifying fibromas can have a similar radiographic appearance but are histologically distinguished by a storiform pattern of bland fibroblasts with scattered giant cells.
