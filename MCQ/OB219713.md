## Question

A 17-year-old female with a history of cerebral palsy presents to your clinic. She underwent surgical treatment for hallux valgus two years ago with a distal first metatarsal osteotomy and modified McBride procedure, but her symptoms and deformity have recurred. She brings in her preoperative X-rays demonstrating a hallux valgus angle (HVA) of 25 degrees and an intermetatarsal angle (IMA) of 13 degrees. Which factor in her history likely contributed to her recurrence and what is the most appropriate treatment at this time?

## Options

1. Increased HVA; proximal 1st metatarsal osteotomy
2. Cerebral palsy; proximal 1st metatarsal osteotomy
3. Increased HVA; 1st metatarsophalangeal joint arthrodesis
4. Cerebral palsy; 1st metatarsophalangeal joint arthrodesis
5. Cerebral palsy; revision distal 1st metatarsal osteotomy

## Correct option

**4. Cerebral palsy; 1st metatarsophalangeal joint arthrodesis**

## Answer explanation

This patient presents with a recurrence of her hallux valgus deformity after surgical treatment. Patients with cerebral palsy are at risk for recurrence of hallux valgus due to muscle imbalances and abnormal gait patterns. The most appropriate treatment at this time would be a 1st metatarsophalangeal joint (MTPJ) arthrodesis.

Patients with cerebral palsy often have pes planovalgus deformities with accompanying hallux valgus. Surgical correction is offered to cerebral palsy patients with pain who fail nonoperative management. Surgical management with 1st MTPJ arthrodesis limits the risk of recurrence in these patients. The bunion presented in the clinical vignette is mild (HVA <30 degrees, IMA <14 degrees), and in a patient without cerebral palsy a distal first metatarsal osteotomy could be considered an appropriate treatment. However, in this particular patient with a history of cerebral palsy, a 1st MTPJ would be more appropriate.

*Bishay et al. investigated the outcome of 1st MTPJ arthrodesis in treating hallux valgus in adolescents with cerebral palsy.* *They included a series of 24 cases and found that at a median follow-up of 3.5 years, there were no recurrences and 75% of patients reported excellent results.* **The authors conclude** that 1st MTPJ arthrodesis is an appropriate treatment for hallux valgus in cerebral palsy.

*Robinson et al. published a review article on the treatment of hallux valgus.* **They state** that more than 130 different operations have been described adding to the challenges of operative planning. They describe neuromuscular disease as an indication for 1st MTPJ arthrodesis. **The authors conclude** that while most patients have good results after surgery, this can be optimized if the appropriate surgical procedure is selected.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1,3: The HVA in the vignette would not be too severe to treat with a distal osteotomy in a patient without neuromuscular disease.
Answer 2: Given the patient's history of cerebral palsy, the most appropriate treatment at this time would be a 1st MTPJ arthrodesis
Answer 5: A revision distal 1st metatarsal osteotomy would not be an appropriate treatment for hallux valgus in a patient with cerebral palsy.
