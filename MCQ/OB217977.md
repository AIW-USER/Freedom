## Question

A 56-year-old male construction worker presents with insidious onset of right wrist pain that progressed over the past year. He denies any specific wrist injuries and has not sought previous care. He states it doesn’t limit his activities of daily living, however, does restrict his ability to deadlift and lift heavy at the gym. His presenting images are shown in Figure A. Which of the following most likely led to this patient’s pathology?

![[q217977-a-figure-for-upload.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Repetitive micro-traumatic events
2. Altered baseline wrist biomechanics
3. Increased intraosseous pressure
4. Ulnar negative variance
5. All of the above

## Correct option

**5. All of the above**

## Answer explanation

This patient presents with avascular necrosis of his lunate, referred to as Kienbock Disease. Its etiology is most often due to multifactorial causes that culminate in insufficient blood supply to the lunate bone.

Kienbock disease presents as insidious onset of wrist pain with progressive collapse of the carpus. While there are cases of definitive preceding traumatic events (i.e. lunate dislocation), most cases are considered to be multifactorial in nature. Prolonged wrist immobilization has been shown to halt the progression for early-stage disease. In progressed stages without definitive carpus biomechanical or arthritic changes, lunate unloading procedures (radius core decompression, radial shortening osteotomy) may be attempted. Once carpal malalignment or dorsal intercalary instability develops, salvage options should be sought for lasting pain relief (i.e. proximal row carpectomy versus fusion).

*Wells and Dunn reviewed the underlying causes of avascular necrosis of carpal bones.* **They discuss** that most cases occur from multifactorial causes, including vascular disruption, intravascular obliteration, or extravascular compression. **They conclude** that diagnosing and treating earlier stages of carpal bone avascular necrosis may halt the progression of the disease, however, the low incidence rates limit declarative treatment options.

*Lichtman and colleagues reviewed the updated classification of Kienbock disease and different treatment options.* *They reviewed both direct and indirect surgical revascularization procedures with the goal of encouraging spontaneous revascularization to the lunate.* **They conclude** by discussing the importance of continued research into treatment options due to the limited validation studies currently available.

Figure A shows the AP radiographic and magnetic resonance imaging of a patient with Lichtman stage 3 Kienbock disease.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1-4: All of these factors play a role in the development of Kienbock disease, hence answer 5 is the most appropriate answer choice.
