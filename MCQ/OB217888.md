## Question

You are seeing a 68-year-old male with end-stage hip osteoarthritis that has failed nonoperative management. You recommend proceeding to surgery for total hip arthroplasty (THA). He inquires about the different approaches by which a THA can be performed. In discussing the potential complications of these approaches, you mention the risk of intra-operative femur fracture. Which approach is thought to be associated with the highest risk of femoral-sided complications?

## Options

1. Posterolateral
2. Direct lateral
3. Anterolateral
4. Direct anterior
5. No difference in risk of femoral-sided complication among approaches

## Correct option

**4. Direct anterior**

## Answer explanation

In patients undergoing total hip arthroplasty (THA) through a direct anterior approach, the risk of femoral-sided complication (femoral fracture, subsidence, etc) is higher.

Multiple approaches exist and are utilized during THA, each with distinct advantages and disadvantages. The direct anterior approach (DAA) has been recently popularized because of it's purported quicker recovery, decreased pain and lower rates of postoperative instability. However, a steep learning curve exists, and rates of femoral sided complications have been shown to be higher. This is in particular reference to the rates of intra-operative femur fracture during the DAA, likely related to difficulty with femoral exposure at times if appropriate releases are not performed.

*Aggarwal et al. compared THA complication rates across different surgical exposures.* **They noted** that the DAA had the highest overall rate of complications (8.5%), with the posterior approach being lowest at 5.85%. Infection, periprosthetic fracture and dislocation were among the most common complications in their cohort.

*Fleischman et al. reviewed mechanical complications with THA based on surgical approach.* **They noted** that the direct lateral approach had the lowest rate of mechanical complication and the lowest rate of instability. The risk for periprosthetic fracture and loosening was higher with the DAA.

*Meneghini et al. studied the DAA and its correlation with early femoral failure in THA.* *They reviewed 478 revision THAs and noted that revisions due to early femoral failure were more common in patients who had undergone a DAA.* After performing a multi-variate regression analysis to control for other variables, they still concluded that the DAA was a significant predictor of early femoral failure.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1-3,5: The DAA is thought to have higher rates of femoral-sided complication in THA than the posterolateral, direct lateral, and anterolateral exposures.
