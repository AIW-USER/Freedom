## Question

A 68-year-old male presents with symptoms of pain in the low back and bilateral buttocks. He denies significant pain radiating distal to his knee and denies any weakness or numbness in his legs. A video in Figure V shows axial and sagittal T2-weighted MRI images. Which of the following components of the history and physical would be most expected in this patient?

## Options

1. Decreased pain going down stairs
2. Increased pain with combined passive straight elevation of his right leg and dorsiflexion of his ankle
3. Progressive and step-wise worsening of symptoms
4. Improvement in pain while riding a stationary bicycle
5. Positive clonus and positive hoffman's sign.

## Correct option

**4. Improvement in pain while riding a stationary bicycle**

## Answer explanation

The clinical presentation and MRI are consistent with multi-level degenerative lumbar spinal stenosis. Buttock pain with prolonged lumbar extension and improvement with forward flexion (while riding a stationary bicycle for instance) is most consistent with lumbar spinal stenosis.

Degenerative lumbar spinal stenosis is a narrowing of the spinal canal often secondary to a multitude of factors including facet osteophytes, herniated discs, spondylolisthesis, ligamentum flavum hypertrophy, and synovial facet cysts. The presentation is variable and depends on the extent and location of stenosis (central, lateral recess, foraminal, and extraforaminal). Symptoms generally include lower back pain, referred buttock pain, leg pain and in severe cases weakness, with bowel and bladder disturbances (caudal equina syndrome). The pain is generally worse with extension-related activities (walking, standing upright, going down stairs) and improved with flexion (sitting, going up stairs). Physical exam is most often unremarkable for any focal neurologic signs such as positive straight leg raise. As a result, the diagnosis relies on the concordance of a careful history, physical exam, imaging, and diagnostic injections to accurately identify the primary cause of symptoms and to differentiate from vascular claudication.

*Singh et al. presented a review of lumbar spinal stenosis.* **The authors noted** that spinal stenosis is a manifestation of several distinct etiologies that result in narrowing of the spinal canal and degenerative changes involving the facet joints, intervertebral disks, and ligaments all may contribute to stenosis or exacerbate symptoms in the patient with congenitally narrowed canals. **They discussed** that for most patients, management involves non-operative measures such as activity modification, exercise, NSAIDs and epidural injection. Operative measures for those patients with intractable pain, neurologic compromise, or mechanical instability includes microscopic laminectomy, lumbar laminoplasty, and lumbar laminotomy.

*Hilibrand et al. discusses the pathoanatomy of lumbar stenosis and details both non-surgical and surgical options with reports of surgical success rates as high as 85%.* **The authors state** that surgical outcomes can be compromised by inadequate decompression, inadequate stabilization, or medical co-morbidities. **The authors also note** that short-term follow-up data indicate that operative management provides more effective relief than nonoperative treatment, but prospective studies comparing the effects of nonoperative and operative interventions on the long-term natural history of lumbar spinal stenosis are needed.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Symptoms associated with lumbar spinal stenosis are generally exacerbated by lumbar extension and improved with flexion. As such, patients often complain of worsening pain going down stairs (due to relative lumbar extension) and improved pain going up stairs (due to relative flexion).
Answer 2: A positive straight leg test is most indicative of lumbar radiculopathy. Physical exam is more often negative for focal neurologic signs in patients with lumbar spinal stenosis.
Answer 3: A progressive and step-wise deterioration is characteristic of cervical myelopathy but has not been found to be associated with lumbar spinal stenosis.
Answer 5: Upper motor neuron findings such as clonus or a positive Hoffman's sign signify spinal cord compression, but lumbar spinal stenosis affects the nerve roots. Though tandem stenosis can occur, patients with isolated lumbar spinal stenosis would not demonstrate upper motor neuron signs.
