## Question

A 20-year-old female elite ballet dancer presents to your office with chronic left posterolateral ankle pain. Her pain has progressively worsened and is prominent while she is in the "en pointe" position. She denies any associated paresthesias, and conservative measures have failed to provide pain relief. On exam, she has a full and painless range of motion in all toes. Moderate pain is noted in the posterolateral aspect of the ankle, especially with forced plantarflexion. A lateral ankle radiograph is shown in Figure A. Which treatment option provides a high rate of return-to-play with a low complication rate?

## Options

1. Arthroscopic debridement of the flexor hallucis longus tendon
2. Arthroscopic debridement of the posterior tibial tendon
3. Open surgical excision of the os trigonum
4. Peroneal tendon debridement with reinforcement of the superior peroneal retinaculum
5. Retrial adequate rest from ballet and physical therapy to strengthen the lateral ankle structures

## Correct option

**3. Open surgical excision of the os trigonum**

## Answer explanation

Patient has a symptomatic os trigonum that has failed conservative treatment. Therefore, surgical intervention is indicated. Open surgical excision of the os trigonum has been shown to provide a high rate of return to play with a low complication rate (answer 3).

Os trigonum is an accessory ossicle with a prevalence of 10-25%. This is a secondary ossification center that remains separated from the posterolateral tubercle of the talus. This condition has been associated with posterior ankle impingement and flexor hallucis longus (FHL) entrapment. This is commonly symptomatic in ballet dancers while in maximal plantarflexion (en pointe position). Os trigonum syndrome leads to posterolateral ankle pain as the os lies lateral to the FHL, tibial nerve, posterior tibial tendon, and posterior tibial artery. Diagnosis is made through a physical examination, plain radiographs, and an MRI, which demonstrates associated inflammation and edema surrounding the os trigonum and FHL tendon in the posterior ankle. Treatment begins with conservative management, and if this fails, it proceeds to open versus arthroscopic excision of the fragment.

*Micheli et al. reported the outcomes in a case series of patients who underwent posterior ankle decompression and os trigonum or Stieda process resection.* *In this study, they utilized an open posterolateral approach in 54 professional, pre-professional, and dedicated recreational dancers.* Excellent and good results were subjectively found in 89% (48/54) of the dancers, moderate results in 11% (6/54), and poor results in 0%. Compared to 17 other studies comparing the posteromedial approach and arthroscopic approaches, **they reported** similar outcomes. **They conclude** that an open posterolateral approach to fragment excision resulted in a high rate of return to dance with minimal complications.

*Vosseller et al. published a JAAOS article reviewing ankle injuries in dancers.* **They report** that foot and ankle injuries are among the most common injuries experienced in dancers. Os trigonum syndrome and FHL tenosynovitis are more common in ballet dancers due to the extreme plantarflexion involved in dancing. **They conclude** that in most cases, a multidisciplinary approach to treatment is necessary, and good results can be achieved with surgical treatment and a specialized rehabilitation program.

Figure A is a lateral ankle radiograph demonstrating an os trigonum.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: Debridement of the FHL tendon will not correct the culprit of the posterior ankle impingement, which is the os trigonum.
Answer 2: Debridement of the posterior tibial tendon will not correct the culprit of the posterior ankle impingement, and this patient does not complain of posteromedial ankle pain. Therefore, posterior tibial tendon impingement or insufficiency is less likely to be a cause of her pain.
Answer 4: She does not complain of peroneal tendon snapping or instability, and therefore, peroneal tendon debridement and superior peroneal retinaculum reinforcement will not correct the primary cause of her ankle pain.
Answer 5: Since the patient has already failed conservative treatment measures, a retrial of this is less likely to result in success.
