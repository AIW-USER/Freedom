## Question

Given below is the X ray AP view of pelvis of a young male. Anterior wall (AW) and posterior wall (PW) are shown. True statement about it is

![[TO17099603-1.png]]

## Options

1. Retroverted acetabulum
2. Excessively anteverted acetabulum
3. Normal acetabulum
4. Excessively retroverted acetabulum

## Correct option

**1. Retroverted acetabulum**

## Answer explanation

X ray of normal acetabulum:

![[TO17099603-2.png]]

Normally acetabulum is slight anteverted in the AP view and the anterior wall (yellow dashed line) projects medially to the posterior wall (blue dashed line). Posterior wall lies lateral to anterior wall and descends approximately through the center point of the femoral head.

In a normal hip, the visible outline of the posterior wall descends approximately through the center point of the femoral head. If the posterior line lies laterally to the femoral center, a more prominent posterior wall is present. This is seen in excessive anteversion.

![[TO17099603-3.png]]

Acetabular retroversion: Acetabular retroversion is defined as anterior wall (AW) being more lateral than posterior wall (PW), whereas in normal hip anterior wall lies more medially. Acetabular retroversion is also defined by a positive crossover, posterior wall sign, and ischial spine sign.

A crossover sign: the anterior rim is projected more laterally in the cranial portion of the acetabulum and crosses the posterior wall more caudally

![[TO17099603-4.png]]

Posterior wall sign: the posterior wall (red line) runs medial to the femoral head center (white dot), normally it descends through the femoral head center.

![[TO17099603-5.png]]

Ischial spine sign: the ischial spine protrudes into the true pelvis (white arrow)

Other signs of acetabular retroversion which have been defined in the literature include a narrow ischium and a laterally protruding iliac wing. The retroversion index is defined as the quotient between the length of the retroverted zone by the entire length of the acetabulum

Answer: 1
