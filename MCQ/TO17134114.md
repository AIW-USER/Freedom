Chapter: Spine MCQs — Cauda Equine Conus Medullaris

## Question

Hemi Cauda Equina syndrome is different from cauda equina syndrome in all of the following aspects except

## Options

1. There is unilateral leg symptomatology
2. Motor deficit is usually absent
3. The cause mostly is a herniated disc
4. People do not experience urinary sphincter paralysis

## Correct option

**3. The cause mostly is a herniated disc**

## Answer explanation

Ans: C

Explanation:

Hemi–cauda equina syndrome is an incomplete or partial form of cauda equina involvement, usually presenting with unilateral leg pain and sensory symptoms, often sparing bladder and bowel function. Motor deficits may be mild or absent compared to complete cauda equina syndrome.

However, both hemi cauda equina syndrome and complete cauda equina syndrome most commonly result from a large lumbar disc herniation. Therefore, the etiology being a herniated disc does not differentiate the two conditions, making option C the correct “EXCEPT” answer.

Cauda Equina V/S Hemi-Cauda Equina Syndrome

Cauda Equina Syndrome (CES) refers to compression of the spinal nerves hanging in spinal canal below L1 vertebrae.

There are two features which make cauda equina nerve roots susceptible to the effects of compressive and tensile forces. First, the nerve roots have no Schwann cell covering; second, cauda equina nerve roots lack a regionalized segmental blood supply with relative hypo vascularity in the central portion of the nerve root, making it vulnerable to ischaemia from compression.

Though the etiology could be multifactorial, the commonest cause tends to be lumbar disc prolapse (1–10% of cases).

In patients with low back pain or sciatica, the presence of one of the following features – saddle anesthesia, urinary retention or incontinence, bowel incontinence or bilateral variable lower limb sensory and motor disturbances – suggests the diagnosis of CES.

Contrary to the above statement, several case series have been reported with varied clinical manifestations like unilateral leg symptomatology, unilateral or bilateral saddle anesthesia with or without leg symptoms etc. One such variation is Hemi Cauda Equina Syndrome.

Hemi-cauda equina syndrome results mostly from ruptured disc and is defined as a combination of unilateral leg pain, unilateral sensibility loss in dermatomes S1 to S5 and sphincter paralysis (proven urinary retention or incontinence). Motor deficit is not necessarily present. Patients presenting with micturition complaints other than urinary retention or incontinence do not suffer from a hemi-cauda equina syndrome. Emergency surgery is warranted here also as in cases of CES.
