## Options

1. It contains both a medial and lateral branch and also supplies the lateral meniscus.
2. It contains both a medial and lateral branch and also supplies the medial meniscus.
3. In the majority of patients, it branches from the superior medial genicular artery.
4. It branches from the popliteal artery and pierces the oblique popliteal ligament.
5. In the majority of patients, it branches from the superior lateral genicular artery.

## Correct option

**4. It branches from the popliteal artery and pierces the oblique popliteal ligament.**

## Answer explanation

The middle geniculate artery is the main blood supply to the anterior cruciate ligament (ACL).

The ACL is most commonly injured during non-contact pivoting injuries. The primary blood supply to the ACL is the middle geniculate artery and it is innervated by the posterior articular nerve. The posterior cruciate ligament (PCL) also receives its blood primary blood supply from the middle geniculate artery and fat pad. This is why acute ACL tears are often accompanied by an immediate large hemarthrosis.

*Toy et al. prepared two fresh cadaver knees and inspected them by means of dissection and CT.* **They concluded** that the vasculature of the ACL arises from the middle geniculate artery, vessels of the infra-patellar fat pad, and adjacent synovium.

Scapinelli provided a detailed description of the blood supply to the knee through cadaveric dissection, vascular injections, and histologic studies. He concluded that the middle geniculate artery is the primary blood supply to the ACL, with lesser contributions from the synovial network supplying the fat pad and the anterior horns of the menisci. The femoral and tibial attachments of the ACL receive no significant contributions from the underlying bone.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The lateral meniscus is supplied by the inferior lateral genicular artery.
Answer 2: The medial meniscus is supplied by the inferior medial genicular artery.
Answers 3 and 5: The middle geniculate artery arises directly from the popliteal artery and pierces the oblique popliteal ligament.
