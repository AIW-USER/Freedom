## Question

A 28-year-old male laborer suffered a left ankle injury 4 months ago at work while unloading a dolly. He notes difficulty walking and pain with ambulating. Physical exam shows an inability to toe walk on the left, as well as the finding demonstrated in Figure 1. What other test will be positive and which procedure will likely best result in the restoration of power and function?

![[q210279-a-matles-test-e-prempeh-j-young-m-costa-flouroquinlo.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Thompson test, primary repair with synthetic graft augmentation
2. Thomas test, VY advancement
3. Thompson test, VY advancement with tendon transfer
4. Thessaly test, VY advancement with tendon transfer
5. Thompson test, debridement of osseous spur and primary repair

## Correct option

**3. Thompson test, VY advancement with tendon transfer**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a chronic Achilles tendon rupture. He would have a positive Thompson (Simmonds) test and would most benefit from a V-Y advancement with FHL tendon transfer given the chronicity of the injury.

Chronic Achilles tendon ruptures are challenging conditions for both the patient and surgeon. Functional rehab has shown to be effective for acute ruptures but there seems to be a little role for this in chronic cases unless medical co-morbidities preclude surgery. Primary repairs of chronic injuries are difficult to achieve due to scarring and tendon stump retraction. Instead, local tissue augmentation such as VY advancements allows functional lengthening of the tendon to span this gap. Additionally, tendon transfers allow restoration of plantarflexion strength, with the FHL tendon being the most common transfer.

*Padanilam reviewed the diagnosis and management of chronic Achilles ruptures.* **They note** that diagnosis is often made on physical exam with either the Thompson or Matles test, but that MRI is valuable for gauging the size of the gap needed to be restored or for patients with equivocal exams. Freeing all tendon stump adhesions and manual traction may help gain improved excursion. Fascial turndown flaps, VY advancements and/or FHL tendon transfers may all be done additionally to achieve a repair without excess tension.

*Maffulli et al. reviewed techniques for addressing chronic ruptures.* **They report** that many different techniques have been described over the years with generally good results. However, it is difficult to compare any of these techniques directly due to the sample size, variable outcomes measures, and the retrospective nature of studies.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Synthetic graft augmentation has not shown a significant benefit for Achilles ruptures.
Answer 2: The Thomas test evaluates for hip flexion contractures.
Answer 4: The Thessaly test evaluates for meniscal pathology.
Answer 5: This would be reasonable in cases of chronic tendonitis with a Haglund deformity.
