## Question

A 68-year-old woman presents to the emergency department complaining of lower back pain for the last 4 days after a ground-level fall. Her pain has been so significant that she has been unable to perform her normal activities. She also describes severe pain radiating into the left anterior thigh and knee and difficulty straightening her left leg due to weakness. She has no bladder or bowel changes. On physical examination, she is tender over the lumbar spine and has 3/5 strength in the left quadriceps. Imaging studies show an L3 burst fracture with 40% canal compromise and indeterminate posterior ligamentous complex (PLC) integrity. What is her Thoracolumbar Injury Classification and Severity (TLICS) score and what would be the most appropriate treatment?

## Options

1. TLICS Score = 0, Nonoperative Treatment (TLSO)
2. TLICS Score = 4, Nonoperative Treatment (TLSO)
3. TLICS Score = 4, Operative Treatment
4. TLICS Score = 6, Operative Treatment
5. TLICS Score = 8, Operative Treatment

## Correct option

**4. TLICS Score = 6, Operative Treatment**

## Answer explanation

The patient is presenting with a burst fracture with indeterminate PLC integrity and clinical evidence of nerve root injury The TLICS score would be 6 (2 points for burst fracture, 2 points for indeterminate PLC integrity, and 2 points for nerve root injury) and operative treatment would be the most appropriate treatment.

Thoracolumbar burst fractures often result from high energy traumas. However, in the elderly population, due to poor bone quality, a much smaller insult, such as a fall from standing can cause this injury pattern. The Thoracolumbar Injury Classification and Severity (TLICS) score is a tool that surgeons can use to decide on operative versus nonoperative treatment. Based on this system, most fractures in this patient population that do not have neurological symptoms can typically be managed nonoperatively.

*Patel et al. reviewed thoracolumbar spine injuries and the classification of these injuries to help develop the TLICS score.* The scoring system, based on fracture morphology, neurological status, and posterior ligamentous complex integrity, recommends nonoperative management of patients with scores <4, surgery for patients >4, and either management for patients with a score of 4.

*Esses et al. describe the AAOS clinical practice guidelines for patients with osteoporotic spinal compression fractures.* There was strong evidence to recommend against vertebroplasty and moderate evidence to recommend for prescribing calcitonin in patients with symptomatic osteoporotic spinal compression fractures. All other recommendations had weak evidence or were inconclusive.
