## Question

A 5-year-old child is brought to the emergency department after falling from a swing set. The child is awake but crying and complains of neck pain, and initial trauma evaluation confirms no neurological deficit. Cervical spine radiographs, including flexion and extension views, show a fracture line through the odontoid synchondrosis with 3 millimeters of anterior displacement and motion at the fracture site on dynamic views. A CT scan confirms the diagnosis of an unstable fracture through the C2 synchondrosis. Given the patient's age and the nature of the unstable odontoid synchondrosis fracture, what is the most appropriate initial management strategy?

## Options

1. Closed reduction followed by external immobilization using a Minerva cast or halo vest
2. Immediate surgical fixation with an anterior odontoid screw
3. Immobilization with a soft cervical collar and close observation
4. Open reduction and posterior C1-C2 fusion
5. Urgent referral for a neurosurgical consultation for laminectomy

## Correct option

**1. Closed reduction followed by external immobilization using a Minerva cast or halo vest**

## Answer explanation

Odontoid synchondrosis fractures in children are typically treated with closed reduction and rigid external immobilization, as the synchondrosis has a high healing potential (answer choice 1).

Fractures through the C2 odontoid synchondrosis are the most common cervical spine fractures in children under 7 years of age due to the relative weakness of the unossified growth plate. The primary goal of treatment is to achieve and maintain an adequate reduction to allow the synchondrosis to heal. Since these fractures generally have an excellent prognosis for union, non-operative treatment is the gold standard. This involves closed reduction, often performed under image guidance, followed by rigid external immobilization, typically using a Minerva cast or a halo vest, which provides greater stability than a standard cervical collar. Operative fixation is reserved for non-union or highly unstable fractures that fail external immobilization.

Štulík et al. conducted an anatomical and radiological study of the pediatric dens (odontoid process) to understand its development and implications for fracture treatment. They noted that the odontoid synchondrosis remains a vulnerable site for injury during early childhood until it ossifies, usually by ages 5 to 7. They concluded that understanding the dens's unique anatomy and fusion pattern is critical for selecting the appropriate, typically closed, treatment method for pediatric fractures.

*Fassett et al. reviewed the literature and surgical management principles for odontoid synchondrosis fractures in children.* **They noted** that most fractures can be successfully treated with closed reduction and rigid immobilization (Minerva or halo), reserving open interventions for cases of non-union or instability that cannot be corrected externally. **They concluded** that non-operative management provides a high rate of successful union and should be the initial approach.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: This is a salvage procedure typically reserved for non-union or failure of external immobilization, not the initial treatment in a child.
Answer 3: A soft collar provides insufficient rigidity to stabilize this type of fracture, which is unstable with 3 mm of displacement
Answer 4: Fusion is an aggressive procedure that sacrifices motion and is only indicated for failed conservative treatment or chronic instability.
Answer 5: Laminectomy is a decompression procedure and is not indicated for primary fracture stabilization unless there is concurrent severe posterior cord compression.
