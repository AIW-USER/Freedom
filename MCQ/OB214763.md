## Question

A 36-year-old female comes in 6 months status post intramedullary nailing of a right tibial shaft fracture with continued pain in her right leg while ambulating. On examination, there is no evidence of infection. You obtain radiographs in your clinic, shown in Figure A. What previous surgery may have contributed to this patient's nonunion?

![[q214763-a-tibial-shaft-nonunion.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Gastric sleeve procedure
2. Roux-en-Y gastric bypass
3. Partial colectomy
4. Unilateral Nephrectomy
5. Gastric band procedure

## Correct option

**2. Roux-en-Y gastric bypass**

## Answer explanation

Vitamin D and calcium deficiency can result secondary to gastric bypass surgery due to bypassing of the duodenum.

Fracture nonunion is a complex problem for orthopedic surgeons to address. Numerous factors may contribute including fracture blood supply, mechanical factors related to the fixation construct, as well as patient characteristics. Specific patient factors include nicotine use, comorbid conditions such as diabetes mellitus, HIV infection, and nutritional deficiencies. Vitamin D deficiency is found in greater than 66% of nonunions. Gastric bypass patients are unique, as their digestive tract no longer includes the duodenum and therefore absorption of calcium and Vitamin D is limited. These patients develop secondary hyperparathyroidism, which increases the resorption of calcium from bone and can result in nonunion.

*Brinker et al. published a case series of 37 patients with nonunions who underwent endocrinologic evaluation.* **They found** the most common abnormality was Vitamin D deficiency, present in 68% of their studied patients. Other common metabolic abnormalities included calcium imbalance, central hypogonadism, thyroid disorders, and parathyroid hormone disorders.

*Nauth et al. reviewed principles of nonunion management.* In their article they discuss numerous etiologies of nonunion, including metabolic causes, which impact healing in 5-10% of patients. Metabolic abnormalities to test for include vitamin D deficiency, diabetes, hypogonadism, calcium imbalances, growth hormone deficiency, and hyperparathyroidism. **They recommend** evaluating for a metabolic etiology if a nonunion persists despite adequate treatment without obvious technical error.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1,3,5: These do not affect calcium and Vitamin D absorption in the body as they do not affect the duodenum where the majority of absorption occurs.
Answer 4: Studies have shown that after unilateral nephrectomy, the retained kidney is able to compensate and convert 25-Vit D to 1,25-Vit D at appropriate levels.
