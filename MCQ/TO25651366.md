Chapter: Metabolic Bone Disorder MCQs — Osteoporosis and Fragility Fractures

## Question

Which medication is documented to produce the most rapid and numerically highest percentage increase in lumbar spine BMD within a 12-month period?

## Options

1. Romosozumab
2. Teriparatide
3. Denosumab
4. Abaloparatide

## Correct option

**1. Romosozumab**

## Answer explanation

By inhibiting sclerostin, Romosozumab exerts a dual effect—increasing bone formation and decreasing bone resorption—leading to a increase in spine BMD within 12 months.

Romosozumab (Evenity) — Anti-Sclerostin Antibody (Dual-Action Agent)

Mechanism: A humanised monoclonal antibody that binds and neutralises sclerostin — an endogenous protein produced by osteocytes that inhibits the Wnt/β-catenin signalling pathway in osteoblasts. The Wnt pathway is a master regulator of osteoblast differentiation, proliferation, and survival — sclerostin acts as a brake on this pathway, suppressing bone formation. By neutralising sclerostin, romosozumab releases the Wnt pathway from inhibition, producing a potent increase in bone formation (the osteoblasts are now "unbraked") while simultaneously reducing bone resorption (through Wnt-mediated suppression of RANKL and stimulation of OPG) — a dual action that is unique among osteoporosis drugs and produces the most rapid and greatest BMD gains of any available agent.

Dosing: 210 mg subcutaneous monthly (administered as two 105 mg injections at the same visit) for 12 months — the anabolic effect of romosozumab is time-limited (the "modelling window" — the bone formation rate peaks at 1–3 months and returns toward baseline by 12 months as the system adapts), and treatment beyond 12 months provides diminishing additional benefit.

Efficacy:
The FRAME trial (Cosman et al., NEJM 2016) demonstrated a 73% reduction in vertebral fractures over 12 months compared to placebo (the fastest fracture reduction of any osteoporosis drug — meaningful fracture reduction within the first year). Lumbar spine BMD increased by 13.3%
and total hip BMD by 6.9% over 12 months — the largest and most rapid BMD gains achieved by any osteoporosis agent. The ARCH trial (Saag et al., NEJM 2017) — a head-to-head comparison with alendronate — demonstrated that romosozumab for 12 months followed by alendronate produced 48% fewer vertebral fractures and 38% fewer hip fractures compared to alendronate alone over 24 months.

Sequential Therapy: After the 12-month romosozumab course, transition to an antiresorptive agent (denosumab or bisphosphonate) is mandatory to consolidate and maintain the dramatic BMD gains. The romosozumab → denosumab sequence produces the greatest cumulative BMD gain of any currently available treatment strategy.

Safety Concern — Cardiovascular Risk: The ARCH trial identified a numerical imbalance in serious cardiovascular events (myocardial infarction, stroke, cardiovascular death) in the romosozumab group compared to alendronate (2.5% versus 1.9%) — leading to an FDA black box warning contraindicating romosozumab in patients who have had a myocardial infarction or stroke within the preceding year, and recommending that the cardiovascular risk-benefit be considered in all patients. Despite this concern, romosozumab remains the most potent osteoporosis drug available when cardiovascular risk is acceptable.

Answer: 1
