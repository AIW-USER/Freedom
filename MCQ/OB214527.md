## Question

A 43-year-old female presents to the trauma bay after a motor vehicle collision where she was the restrained driver. She complains of isolated severe right foot pain. Radiographs are shown in Figures A and B. She undergoes temporizing closed reduction and is placed in a splint. Which of the following is the next best step in management?

## Options

1. Casting and non-weight-bearing for 6 weeks
2. Percutaneous screw fixation of the medial column
3. Open reduction and internal fixation of the medial column
4. Arthrodesis of the medial and lateral column
5. Arthrodesis of the medial column and K-wire fixation of the lateral column

## Correct option

**5. Arthrodesis of the medial column and K-wire fixation of the lateral column**

## Answer explanation

The patient has sustained a homolateral Lisfranc fracture-dislocation involving the medial and lateral columns of the foot. The next best step in management would be arthrodesis of the medial column and K-wire fixation of the lateral column.

Lisfranc fracture-dislocations are high energy injuries that cause destabilization of the midfoot. Whereas most fracture-dislocations of the Lisfranc joint may be treated with formal open reduction and internal fixation, primary fusion may be indicated in those fractures with severe intra-articular comminution, complete ligamentous dislocations, or delayed treatment. A fusion of the lateral column is shown to have inferior results to an isolated medial column arthrodesis and is thus not advised. As such, when there is a severe displacement of the lateral column, temporary K-wire percutaneous fixation for additional stability is the recommended treatment.

*Ly and Coetzee performed a prospective randomized trial comparing clinical and radiographic outcomes of primary arthrodesis versus ORIF for the treatment of primarily ligamentous Lisfranc joint injuries in 41 patients.* *They randomized 20 patients to the ORIF group and 21 patients to the primary arthrodesis group.* **They noted** at 2 years post-op, that there was a significant increase in mean AOFAS Midfoot score in the arthrodesis group compared to the ORIF group. **The authors concluded** that a primary stable arthrodesis of the medial two or three rays appears to have a better short and medium-term outcome than ORIF of ligamentous Lisfranc joint injuries.

*Sangeorzan et al. evaluated 16 patients with Lisfranc fracture-dislocations who had failed initial treatment and were subsequently salvaged by arthrodesis.* **They noted** good to excellent results in 11 patients (69%) with five patients having fair or poor results. **They noted** that accurate reduction and early treatment had a significant positive relationship with outcome. **The authors also noted** that injuries that occurred in the workplace and those that incurred a long delay until treatment showed a significant negative correlation to outcome.

Figures A and B show a homolateral Lisfranc injury with disruption of the medial and lateral columns of the foot. Illustration C and D are postoperative AP and oblique radiographs showing medial column arthrodesis supplemented by lateral column percutaneous fixation.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Non-operative management of severely displaced Lisfranc fracture-dislocations can lead to persistent midfoot instability.f
Answers 2 and 3: Given the significant displacement of the lateral column, temporary fixation with screws or K-wires is recommended.
Answer 5: Lateral column tarsometatarsal fusion in Lisfranc injuries has been associated with decreased patient functional outcome scores.
