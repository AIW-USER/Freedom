## Question

The most significant plica causing pain in knee [FNB 2023]

## Options

1. Medial
2. Lateral
3. Infrapatellar
4. Suprapatellar

## Correct option

**1. Medial**

## Answer explanation

Answer: 1

EXPLANATION: -
A plica is a band of thick, fibrotic tissue that extends from the synovial capsule of a joint. As a result of overuse or injury, plica can become inflamed or irritated due to friction across the patella or the medial femoral condyle. When the plica becomes inflamed or irritated, it can cause plica syndrome, which is anterior knee pain due to the plica.
There are four different normal plicae in knee. These are suprapatellar, infrapatellar, medial, and lateral. It is not uncommon for a patient to have more than one plica. The suprapatellar plica is found between the knee joint and the suprapatellar bursa. The infrapatellar plica is found between the intercondylar notch and the synovium around the infrapatellar fat pad. The medial plica is found between the infrapatellar fat pad and the medial aspect of the knee joint.

Symptomatic plicae can sometimes be differentiated from normal plicae on MRI because symptomatic plicae could appear thick and may have synovitis. This is opposed to normal plicae which may appear thin.
If left untreated, medial plica syndrome can cause grade IV outerbridge chondral lesions, which is when the subchondral bonehead is exposed.
Treatment options for plica syndrome include stretching and strengthening as first line followed by intraplical corticosteroid injections if required, while non responders may be taken up for arthroscopic excision.
