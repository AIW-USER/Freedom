## Question

A 5-year-old child with GMFCS Level IV spastic diplegia presents for a routine follow-up. A year ago, her Migration Percentage (MP) was 18% bilaterally. Her current AP pelvis radiograph reveals an MP of 28% on the right and 31% on the left. Clinical exam shows passive hip abduction of 25° on the left and 35° on the right. She has no pain.
What is the most appropriate management according to hip surveillance guidelines?

## Options

1. Maintain current annual surveillance since she is pain-free and the right hip is still < 30%.
2. Transition to 6-monthly radiographs and refer to a pediatric orthopedic surgeon for potential preventative soft-tissue surgery.
3. Schedule a Varus Derotation Osteotomy (VDRO) for the left hip to prevent dislocation.
4. Discharge from the surveillance program as she has reached 5 years of age without a dislocation.

## Correct option

**2. Transition to 6-monthly radiographs and refer to a pediatric orthopedic surgeon for potential preventative soft-tissue surgery.**

## Answer explanation

The left hip has crossed the 30% MP threshold, which officially classifies it as "displaced." Even though she is pain-free, surveillance is designed to act before pain or dislocation occurs.

 The MP increased from 18% to 31% (a 13% increase) in just one year. Any increase > 10% per year is a "red flag" for rapid progression.

Clinical Correlation: Her left hip abduction is 25°, which is below the critical 30° threshold. This suggests adductor spasticity/contracture is actively pulling the femoral head out of the acetabulum.

At this stage (MP 30-40%), preventative soft-tissue surgery (e.g., adductor and psoas releases) is often successful in stabilizing the hip. If you wait until the MP is > 40-50%, the child will likely require more invasive bony surgery (Option C: VDRO), which is not the first-line "preventative" step.

Hip Surveillance in Cerebral Palsy (CP)

Hip surveillance is the systematic process of monitoring the hip joints in children with CP to identify displacement early—long before it becomes painful or causes visible deformity. Unlike developmental dysplasia of the hip (DDH), hip displacement in CP is a progressive, acquired condition.

1. The Core Objective: Migration Percentage (MP)

The primary metric used in surveillance is the Migration Percentage (MP) (Reimers' Index). This is measured on a standardized AP radiograph of the pelvis with the hips in neutral rotation.

 - Calculation: The percentage of the femoral head that lies lateral to Perkin's Line (a vertical line dropped from the lateral edge of the acetabulum).

 - Thresholds:

 - MP < 30%: Generally considered "stable" but requires monitoring.

 - MP > 30%: Defined as "hip displacement." This is the critical threshold for increased monitoring or surgical consultation.

 - MP > 60%: Associated with a high risk of progression to total dislocation.

The frequency of X-rays is determined by the child’s GMFCS level, as the risk of dislocation is directly proportional to their gross motor impairment.

Clinical Red Flags

Even if an X-ray is not yet due, a child should be referred for immediate imaging if they exhibit:

 - Reduced hip abduction: If passive abduction is < 30° (tested with knees extended).

 - Pain: New onset of pain during dressing, hygiene, or sitting.

 - Leg Length Discrepancy: New or worsening asymmetry.

Answer: 2
