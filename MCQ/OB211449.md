## Question

A 68-year-old male presents with right knee pain of 5 weeks duration. He delayed seeking treatment because he was social distancing due to COVID-19. He underwent a right total knee arthroplasty (TKA) 7 years prior that was previously asymptomatic. He is a diabetic and has had a right forefoot ulcer for the past year. On physical exam, he is acutely tender over the anterior knee with minimal pain on small arcs. Motion is 0-100 and he is able to bear weight with difficulty. ## Options

1. Broad spectrum oral antibiotics and wound care
2. Admission for intravenous antibiotics and wound care
3. Irrigation and debridement (I&D) with hardware retention
4. I&D with polyethylene exchange and intra-articular Vancomycin powder
5. Two-stage revision (explant and antibiotic spacer with staged reimplantation)

## Correct option

**5. Two-stage revision (explant and antibiotic spacer with staged reimplantation)**

## Answer explanation

The patient has a chronic PJI that is best treated with a 2-stage revision.

According to the 2018 Musculoskeletal Infection Society (MSIS) criteria, this patient has a PJI due to the presence of a sinus fistula communicating with the joint. Two-stage revisions have a better chance of eradicating infection than I&D in the setting of chronic PJIs. The source of this patient's infection is likely from his ipsilateral forefoot ulcer. The source of the infection should also be addressed to prevent further hematogenous seeding. Lastly, methicillin-resistant organisms carry a worse prognosis for eradication of infection and functional outcomes compared to methicillin-sensitive organisms even following a 2-stage revision.

*Parvizi et al. examined the economic impact of methicillin-resistant infections.* *They performed a retrospective analysis of patients at 2 academic centers with PJIs over a 10 year period, identifying 391 total patients.* They found that the average cost of surgical treatment for a PJI was $68,053 for a MSSA organism compared to a staggering $107,264 for MRSA.

*Leung et al. asked how often a 2-stage revision in the setting of a PJI effectively eradicates MRSA.* *They retrospectively reviewed 50 patients that underwent a 2-stage hip revision with an MRSA or methicillin-resistant S. epidermidis infection.* **They found** a treatment failure of 21% at a minimum 2-year follow-up, higher than that of including both MSSA and MRSA infections (4-11% failure rate in the literature).

*Marculescu et al. looked at the outcomes of prosthetic joint infections treated with debridement and retention of components.* *They performed a retrospective cohort analysis of all patients with a prosthetic joint infection who were treated with debridement and retention over a 4 year period and identified 91 patients.* Treatment failure occurred in 53 episodes and was associated with the presence of a sinus tract and duration of symptoms prior to debridement of >/ 8 days.

*Azzam et al. reexamined the indications for irrigation and debridement in the management of PJI.* *They retrospectively reviewed 104 patients diagnosed with a hip or knee PJI that underwent irrigation and debridement with prosthesis retention at their institution.* **They found** a failure rate of 56% and suggest that debridement and retention be reserved for select healthy patients with low virulence organisms.

Figure A is a clinical photo of a draining sinus tract in a patient with a TKA. Figure B shows the AP and lateral radiographs of the same patient, demonstrating a small effusion with a well-fixed prosthesis. Illustration A is the 2018 MSIS criteria for the diagnosis of a PJI.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1-4: In the setting of an acute infection with a methicillin-sensitive or low virulence organism, I&D with polyethylene exchange may be indicated. In a chronic PJI or an infection with a methicillin-resistant infection, a 2-stage revision carries the best chance for eradication of infection.
