## Question

Avascular necrosis of the patella is a potentially devastating complication following total knee arthroplasty, and the occurrence of this pathology is directly related to the blood supply of the patella. Which of the following is most accurate regarding the blood supply of the patella?

## Options

1. A lateral retinacular release does not place the lateral patellar vessels at risk of disruption
2. Excision of the infrapatellar fat pad disrupts both the medial and lateral patellar vessels
3. Performance of a lateral meniscectomy disrupts the lateral and inferior anastomotic vessels
4. Performance of a medial parapatellar arthrotomy and lateral reticular release can disrupt 50-100% of the patellar blood supply
5. The lateral sided patellar vessels contribute more significantly than the medial sided patellar vessels

## Correct option

**4. Performance of a medial parapatellar arthrotomy and lateral reticular release can disrupt 50-100% of the patellar blood supply**

## Answer explanation

The blood supply of the patella is an anastomotic ring, and commonly used surgical approaches and intraoperative steps can place this blood supply at risk. The intraoperative performance of a medial parapatellar arthrotomy and lateral retinacular release can disrupt up to 75% of the blood supply to the patella (Answer 4).

Patellar osteonecrosis has been identified as a possible complication following total knee arthroplasty (TKA), with some studies reporting an incidence as high as 33% at 1-year and 10% at 10-year follow-up. While the exact etiology of this complication is unknown, multiple studies assert that commonly performed steps during TKA can place the patella's blood supply at risk. However, a direct association between this vascular disruption and the development of osteonecrosis remains elusive.

Multiple studies have examined the effect of commonly performed steps on patellar vascularity. Previous studies have found that the medial arthrotomy can disrupt 0 to 35% of patellar vascularity, that the excision of the infrapatellar fat pad can disrupt 0 to 16% of vascularity, and that a lateral release can disrupt 53 to 75% of vascularity. As previously stated, the patellar blood supply is an anastomotic ring, with significantly greater contribution from medial-sided vessels, further highlighting the deleterious effect these surgical steps may have on patellar perfusion. As a result, it is recommended that a cuff of at least 15mm is left on the medial patella, which can help to preserve the anastomotic network, and that if a lateral release is deemed necessary, the release should be kept distal to the superolateral patellar border, proximal to the lateral joint line, and performed at least 15mm lateral to the patellar border, if possible. Special care should be taken when converting a failed UKA to TKA, as an inappropriately performed medial arthrotomy in the setting of a prior lateral arthrotomy (as is done for a lateral UKA), as failure to preserve the anastomotic ring can greatly disrupt patellar perfusion.

*Lazaro et al. performed a cadaveric study investigating the blood supply of the patella.* *The authors utilized 21 cadaveric knees.* Overall, **the authors found** that the medial-sided vessels contributed more significantly than the lateral-sided vessels and that careful soft tissue management could preserve the anastomotic ring.

*Bonutti et al. performed a cadaveric study to investigate the effect of a medial parapatellar arthrotomy and lateral release on patellar perfusion.* *Overall, their study utilized 10 cadaveric specimens.* **The authors found** that the performance of a medial parapatellar arthrotomy and lateral release caused a 75% decrease in overall patellar perfusion. Overall, **the authors noted** that following these steps, surgeons should take care to preserve the anterior soft tissue and central cancellous bone, to maintain patellar perfusion.

Incorrect Answers
Answer 1: A lateral release places the lateral superior and inferior genicular arteries at risk if the release is performed too extensively.
Answer 2: The excision of the infrapatellar fat pad does not disrupt the medial and lateral vessels but does disrupt the anterior tibial recurrent artery and inferior anastomotic ring.
Answer 3: The performance of a lateral meniscectomy does not disrupt the anastomotic ring.
Answer 5: The medial-sided vessels contribute more significantly than the lateral-sided vessels.
