## Question

A 72-year-old male with a past medical history of tobacco and alcohol use undergoes total shoulder arthroplasty due to osteoarthritis. Prior to surgical intervention, the patient had been managed with physical therapy and pain control, including a narcotic prescription. At his 6-week follow-up appointment, the patient returns his unused 5 mg oxycodone pills. Of the 30 tablets prescribed, the patient used 10. Which of the following factors from the patient’s history are associated with decreased post-operative narcotic consumption?

## Options

1. Male gender
2. Age
3. History of narcotic use
4. History of alcohol use
5. History of tobacco use

## Correct option

**2. Age**

## Answer explanation

Patients who undergo shoulder arthroplasty are often prescribed narcotics for immediate post-operative pain control. Age has been shown in multiple shoulder arthroplasty studies to be protective for narcotic consumption (Answer 2).

Over the past two decades, the incidence of total shoulder arthroplasty has increased. During the same period, the rate of opioid use disorder and overdose deaths has risen similarly, with the CDC reporting 107,941 overdose deaths in the United States in 2022. Orthopedic surgeons are the highest narcotic-prescribing surgical specialty. While several risk factors exist for increased post-operative narcotic use after shoulder arthroplasty, including pre-operative narcotic use and unemployment, increasing age is protective for narcotic consumption following shoulder arthroplasty.

*Martusiewicz published a prospective study of 50 patients undergoing primary total shoulder arthroplasty.* The median narcotic consumption was 193 morphine equivalent units, approximately 25 5 mg oxycodone tablets. Preoperative narcotic was associated with increased narcotic consumption (P=0.004). When evaluated as a continuous variable, age was found to be protective (P=0.04).

*Kopechek et al. published a retrospective cohort study of 100 patients who underwent total shoulder arthroplasty.* **They found** that age over 65 was a protective factor for opioid consumption. The median 5 mg oxycodone tablets in the under-65 group was 83, while 65 and older was 65 tablets (P=0.009). Unemployment and preoperative opiate use were associated with additional post-operative narcotic refills.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: Gender has not been shown to correlate with post-operative narcotic use after shoulder arthroplasty.
Answer 3: A history of narcotic use is a risk factor for increased post-operative narcotic consumption.
Answers 4 & 5: A history of smoking and alcohol use has not been shown to correlate with post-operative narcotic use after shoulder arthroplasty.
