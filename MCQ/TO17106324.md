Chapter: Arthroplasty MCQs — Wear And Osteolysis (Knee)

## Question

Which knee design will have minimum backside wear with same insert

## Options

1. Highly constrained knee design
2. Posterior cruciate-sacrificing knee designs
3. Cruciate retaining knee design
4. All have similar back side wear

## Correct option

**3. Cruciate retaining knee design**

## Answer explanation

Insert type and the degree of articular conformity are variables that potentially can influence the forces at the main and undersurface articulations during normal physiologic loading.

Cruciate-retaining inserts should have less shear forces transmitted to the insert undersurface articulation relative to their posterior-stabilized and constrained condylar counterparts because of the lack of a cam and post and decreased articular conformity. The translations seen on radiographs and fluoroscopy will tend to be dissipated, rather than resisted, at the articulation with the surrounding ligaments being the checkreign to stability. This decreases the potential for articular interactions to be transmitted to the undersurface articulation. In CR inserts tractive forces at the articulation or tangential forces associated with sliding or rolling of the femoral component on the insert surface probably will not be sufficient to cause undersurface motion unless locking mechanism failure occurs.

Therefore, although backside wear still is likely to occur from the affects of axial loading, its severity in cruciate-retaining, flat-on-flat type designs, should be less than posterior-stabilized counterparts if polyethylene of adequate thickness is used.

Posterior cruciate-sacrificing knee designs are similarly susceptible to backside wear from axial loads that can cause micromotion. Additionally, these knees typically have a cam and post, or anterior and posterior sagittal constraint, to resist posterior displacement of the tibia on the femur. These articular interactions may additionally increase the possibility for shear forces and micromotion at the undersurface articulation.

Some cruciate-sacrificing posterior-stabilized designs use a highly conforming sagittal geometry without a post to increase joint stability and produce low stresses in the polyethylene tibial insert. But although maximal contact area may minimize wear at the main articulation, the potential for backside wear actually may be increased. This can occur when the insert geometry is used to inhibit anteroposterior (AP) or mediolateral motion of the femur on the tibial insert, creating significant shear plane forces at the main articulation. These forces subsequently are transferred in part to the other interfaces including the insert backside.

Highly constrained knee designs should have the greatest likelihood of having undersurface motion because of their high conforming insert geometries and rigid cam-post mechanism that can resist AP, varus and valgus, and rotational stresses.

Answer :c
