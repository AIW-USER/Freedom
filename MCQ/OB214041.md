## Question

Which of the following spinal cord injuries conveys the lowest chance for functional recovery and the greatest associated lifetime healthcare costs?

## Options

1. American Spinal Injury Association (ASIA) A
2. American Spinal Injury Association (ASIA) B
3. American Spinal Injury Association (ASIA) C
4. Brown-Sequard syndrome
5. Central cord syndrome

## Correct option

**1. American Spinal Injury Association (ASIA) A**

## Answer explanation

American Spinal Injury Association (ASIA) A spinal cord injuries (SCIs) have the least chance for marked recovery (defined as an improvement of at least two grades from initial injury severity) and the greatest associated lifetime healthcare costs.

ASIA A SCIs are complete injuries with no spared motor or sensory function below the affected level. Injury severity must be determined after the resolution of spinal shock. Eighty percent of patients with these injuries can expect an improvement of one nerve root level, while 20% of patients with these injuries can expect an improvement of two nerve root levels. Patients with complete SCIs have less chance for recovery than those with incomplete injuries (ASIA B-D). Patients with complete SCIs also have greater associated lifetime healthcare costs.

*Coleman et al. performed a retrospective study in which they analyzed the effect of spinal cord injury severity and anatomical region on prognosis.* **They found** that American Spinal Injury Association (ASIA) A injuries in the thoracic spine had a marked recovery rate of 12.8%, while ASIA A injuries to the cervical spine had a marked recovery rate of 15.5%. **They also found** that ASIA B injuries had a marked recovery rate of 46.6% and ASIA C and D injuries had a marked recovery rate of 84.1%. **They concluded** that injury severity is the strongest predictor of prognosis after a spinal cord injury.

*Dukes et al. utilized the National Spinal Cord Injury Statistical Center (NSCISC) database to estimate the lifetime costs of thoracic spinal cord injuries.* They found that the 35-year post-injury cost of hospitalization associated with an ASIA A injury was $321,534 with a stepwise decrease down to ASIA D injuries at $68,120. **They concluded** that persons with less severe thoracic spinal cord injuries spend fewer days in hospital over their lifetimes and have lower costs associated with their injury than do patients with more severe spinal cord injuries.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: ASIA B is an incomplete SCI and has a 46.6% chance of marked recovery.
Answer 3: ASIA C is an incomplete SCI and has an 84.1% chance of marked recovery.
Answer 4: Brown-Sequard syndrome is an incomplete SCI that demonstrates ipsilateral loss of motor and proprioception, and contralateral loss of pain/temperature sensation. These injuries have a good prognosis with 99% able to ambulate at the final follow up.
Answer 5: Central cord syndrome is an incomplete SCI that demonstrates motor deficits worse in the upper extremities than the lower extremities. These patients have a good prognosis with a strong likelihood of returning to walking but a low likelihood of restoration of fine dexterity to the hands.
