## Question

Figure A is an arthroscopic view of the glenohumeral joint from the posterior portal in the beach chair position. Release of which of the following labelled structures will increase both inferior translation and external rotation of the humeral head with the shoulder adducted?

![[q8863-a-scope.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. A
2. B
3. C
4. D
5. E

## Correct option

**1. A**

## Answer explanation

Arthroscopic release of the rotator interval (RI), labelled A, results in increased inferior translation and external rotation of the humeral head with the shoulder adducted.

Arthroscopic capsular release may be performed in cases of recalcitrant shoulder stiffness including post-traumatic, post-operative, post-infectious and from incompletely resolved adhesive capsulitis. Complete release of the RI and its contents - the superior glenohumeral ligament (SGHL), coracohumeral ligament (CHL) and joint capsule - is necessary to restore shoulder external rotation at the side in patients. The RI spans from the anterior edge of the supraspinatus (SSP) to the superior edge of the subscapularis (SSC) and is confined medially by the coracoid base.

*Jost et al. studied the anatomy of the RI in cadaveric specimens and* **identified** distinct medial and lateral regions with separate functional roles. The medial RI was found to primarily control inferior translation of the adducted arm. The lateral RI mainly influenced external rotation of the adducted arm. The CHL was well-developed in all specimens and played a key role in both inferior translation and external rotation.

*Tetro et al. performed a cadaveric study of the RI and described intra-articular anatomic parameters to guide complete release.* With the arm in neutral rotation, the anterior edge of the biceps tendon at the glenoid rim is a useful landmark for the anterior edge of the SP. Arthroscopic release from the biceps tendon to the SSC should continue until the coracoacromial (CA) ligament is visualized through the RI defect. Use of these landmarks resulted in successful division of the SGHL and CHL in all specimens.

Figure A is an arthroscopic view of the glenohumeral joint from the posterior portal showing the rotator interval [A], middle glenohumeral ligament (MGHL) [B], subscapularis tendon [C], anterior labrum [D] and the anterior inferior glenohumeral ligament (IGHL) [E].

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 2 + 4: Care should be taken not to injure the subscapularis tendon [C] or the labrum [D] when performing an arthroscopic release.
Answer 3: Release of the MGHL [B] would result in increased anterior translation of the humeral head with the shoulder at 45° of abduction and external rotation.
Answer 5: Release of the anterior IGHL [E] would result in increased anterior and inferior translation of the humeral head with the shoulder at 90° of abduction and external rotation.
