## Question

## Options

1. Symptomatic peroneus brevis tendon tear, tenodesis if tear less than 50%
2. Os trigonum syndrome, excision
3. Symptomatic peroneus brevis tendon tear, tubularization if tear less than 50%
4. Talar dome osteochondral defect, microfracture if lesion less than 2x2cm
5. Talar dome osteochondral defect, osteochondral allograft is lesion greater than 2x2cm

## Correct option

**3. Symptomatic peroneus brevis tendon tear, tubularization if tear less than 50%**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a symptomatic peroneus brevis tendon tear following an ankle instability event. Tears less than 50% of the tendon substance should be excised and tubularized.

Peroneal tendon tears are common sequelae following ankle inversion episodes, often presenting as persistent lateral ankle pain with tenderness. Surgical management of these injuries depends on tendon integrity and the number of tendons involved. Simple and longitudinal tears involving less than 50% of one or both tendons (Type I tears) are amenable to debridement and tubularization. Larger complex tears involving over 50% of the tendon substance (Type II tear) should be debrided with ends tenodesed to the neighboring tendon.

*Redfern et al. described a series of 28 patients with various degrees of peroneal tendon tearing and proposed a treatment algorithm for concomitant peroneal tears.* **They noted** that surgical management of these injuries can yield favorable outcomes as long as underlying structural causes of instability (cavovarus alignment, lateral ligamentous laxity, etc.) are addressed. They therefore strongly recommend a thorough pre-operative evaluation for these injuries.

*Sobel et al. reported a case of peroneus brevis tearing due to ankle laxity and instability.* *They detail that lateral ligamentous incompetence in combination with superior peroneal retinacular laxity allows the brevis excessive mobility, causing undue friction of the tendon against the fibula leading to a tear.* *They again highlight that surgical treatment of peroneal tears must also address the underlying cause(s).*

Figures A and B are fluid-sensitive coronal MR sequences showing a split tear of the peroneus brevis tendon with surrounding edema. The intact peroneus longus tendon has moved more anteriorly in the groove.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Tubularization of the tendon should be done for small symptomatic tears less than 50%.
Answer 2: There is no os trigonum on the imaging.
Answers 4 and 5: There is no talar osteochondral lesion on imaging.
