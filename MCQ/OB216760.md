## Options

1. At 1-year post-injury, the right shoulder range of motion will most likely be equal to the contralateral extremity.
2. At 1-year post-injury, the right shoulder range of motion will most likely be diminished without functional deficit.
3. Early range of motion exercises risk fracture displacement and should be avoided until at least 4 weeks post-injury.
4. Most patients do not return to work following this injury.
5. Surgery is recommended to reduce the risk of nonunion .

## Correct option

**2. At 1-year post-injury, the right shoulder range of motion will most likely be diminished without functional deficit.**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a minimally displaced proximal humerus fracture (PHF) involving the anatomic neck (i.e. 1-part fracture). This injury pattern is most commonly managed nonoperatively, with the majority of patients returning to their baseline functional status with mildly decreased range of motion and no functional deficit.

PHF's can be classified by the number of parts involved (Neer classification), with a part defined as a fracture fragment displaced > 1cm or angulated > 45°. Surgical treatment of greater tuberosity fractures is recommended in patients with >5 mm displacement to restore rotator cuff function. Minimally displaced fractures comprise ~80% of all PHF's and are treated nonoperatively with a sling and early range of motion (ROM).

*Tejwani et al. performed a prospective study of 67 patients with a 1-part PHF.* *At 1-year follow-up, the ASES score and functional status were similar to pre-injury status.* However, the ROM of the affected shoulder was diminished in both external and internal rotation while forward flexion was preserved.

*Hanson et al. prospectively analyzed 160 patients with PHF's of all types (i.e. 1-4 part fractures and head-split fractures) managed nonoperatively.* *At 1-year follow-up, 93% showed solid union.* Constant and DASH scores improved steadily over time, but were still lower compared to the contralateral extremity. Employed patients returned to work 97.6% of the time with a median time off of 10 weeks with no difference between manual and nonmanual workers.

Figures A and B are the AP and scapular-Y radiographs of the right shoulder, respectively, demonstrating a 1-part PHF involving the anatomic neck of the proximal humerus.

Incorrect Responses:
Answer 1: At 1-year follow-up, most patients have restored forward flexion but lack both internal and external rotation compared to the contralateral extremity.
Answer 3: Early ROM exercises starting at 2 weeks post-injury have not been shown to result in fracture displacement. Patients managed with early rehabilitation exhibit high union rates and largely return to baseline functional status.
Answer 4: The vast majority of patients with PHF's of all types return to work post-injury.
Answer 5: One-part PHF have an excellent union rate (>90%) and rarely require surgery.
