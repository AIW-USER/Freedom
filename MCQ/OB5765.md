## Question

A 45-year-old woman presents with a 6-year history of right medial hallux pain that has gotten progressively worse. The pain is no longer relieved by wearing wide toe-box shoes. She notes her mother had similar problems with her feet and required surgical management. ## Options

1. First metatarsophalangeal (MTP) joint arthrodesis
2. First tarsometatarsal (TMT) joint arthrodesis
3. First and second TMT arthrodesis
4. Distal metatarsal osteotomy
5. Proximal metatarsal osteotomy

## Correct option

**2. First tarsometatarsal (TMT) joint arthrodesis**

## Answer explanation

The patient has hallux valgus (HV) with a large intermetatarsal angle (IMA) and hypermobility in the 1st TMT joint. As such, she is indicated for 1st TMT joint arthrodesis (Lapidus fusion) as part of her hallux valgus correction.

Lapidus fusion of the 1st TMT joint is indicated for the correction of severe hallux valgus, metatarsus primus varus, post-traumatic/end-stage arthritis, adolescent bunions, 1st TMT joint hypermobility or 1st TMT joint arthritis. One disadvantage of this procedure is the high nonunion rate (up to 12%). Resection of articular cartilage but the preservation of the subchondral plate will help prevent excessive shortening and enhance construct stability.

*Thompson et al. retrospectively evaluated the fusion rate of 1st TMT arthrodesis in the modified Lapidus procedure as part of a flatfoot reconstruction in 182 patients.* **The authors noted** a union rate of 96% in the patient cohort. **The authors advocate** the Lapidus procedure for correction of hallux valgus with patients with a hypermobile first ray and for certain patients undergoing flatfoot reconstruction.

*Scranton et al. compared 2 methods of Lapidus fusion across the 1st TMT joint (crossed 4.0mm compression screw vs Lapidus plate) in 10 pairs of cadaveric feet.* **The authors noted** that the load to failure and bending moment/rigidity was higher with the Lapidus plate. **The authors concluded** that Lapidus plates may minimize nonunion or malunion in the Lapidus procedure.

Figures A and B show a severe right HV with a hallux valgus angle (HVA) of 37 and IMA of 18. Figure C shows a 1st TMT stress test. Illustration A shows bony resection during a Lapidus fusion.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: First MTP fusion alone will not address the large IMA and hypermobile 1st TMT joint.
Answer 3: The 2nd TMT is not fused during the correction of hallux valgus.
Answers 4 and 5: Distal and proximal metatarsal osteotomies do not address 1st TMT hypermobility.
