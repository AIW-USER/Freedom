## Question

A 62-year-old male is found to have an extensor mechanism failure after total knee arthroplasty. Several treatment options including allograft reconstruction, synthetic graft reconstruction, and reconstruction with host tissue are discussed with the patient. Which of the following is true?

## Options

1. Failure rates are equivalent between allograft and synthetic graft reconstructions
2. Revision rates are higher in allograft compared with synthetic graft reconstructions
3. Infection rates are higher in allograft compared with synthetic graft reconstructions
4. Functional outcomes are greater in synthetic graft compared with allograft reconstructions
5. Allograft reconstructions have a lower revision rate but higher infection rate compared with synthetic graft reconstructions

## Correct option

**1. Failure rates are equivalent between allograft and synthetic graft reconstructions**

## Answer explanation

No significant differences exist in failure, revision, infection, or outcome scores when comparing allograft and synthetic material reconstruction (Answer 1).

TKA extensor mechanism ruptures are traumatic periprosthetic injuries that consist of patellar tendon injuries or quad tendon injuries. Diagnosis can be made clinically in a patient with history of a TKA who is unable to perform a straight leg raise. Radiographs may show patella alta or patella baja. Treatment is generally surgical repair versus reconstruction, depending on available patella bone stock and chronicity of injury. There are many reconstructive options including allograft reconstruction, synthetic graft reconstruction, and reconstruction with host tissue including a rotational gastrocnemius flap. No one technique has been shown to be superior to another. Primary repair is not a reliable option in this setting. Unfortunately, most reconstructive options lead to some degree of extensor lag.

*Ricciardi et al. reviewed the outcomes of extensor mechanism allograft (EMA) reconstruction in a large single-center case series.* **They reported** that 69% of knees had retained their initial allograft reconstruction at their latest follow-up despite reoperation rates of 58%. **They noted** that younger age was significantly associated with failure of the initial allograft reconstruction. **They concluded** that EMA reconstruction shows adequate overall intermediate-term survival; however, reoperation rates were high and associated with worse functional outcomes.

*Deren et al. reviewed extensor mechanism disruption following TKA.* *They compared both allograft and synthetic reconstructive techniques.* **They reported** that the overall success rate of allograft was 73% and not significantly different from synthetic material. **They noted** that there was no significant difference in revision rates, infection, or outcome scores between allograft and synthetic material. **They concluded** that both techniques are overall equivalent in treating this condition.

*Busfield et al. reviewed patients with chronic extensor mechanism disruption that were treated with an extended medial gastrocnemius rotational flap reconstruction of the extensor mechanism.* **They reported** that all patients were able to regain sufficient extensor mechanism strength to return to independent ambulation, and all infections resolved after treatment. **They concluded** that medial gastrocnemius flap reconstruction can provide successful salvage of a failed extensor mechanism allograft or an alternative to allograft reconstruction in patients with poor soft tissue coverage, previous infection, or a compromised immune system.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Revision rates are equivalent between allograft and synthetic graft reconstructions.
Answer 3: Infection rates are equivalent between allograft and synthetic graft reconstructions.
Answer 4: Functional outcomes are equivalent between allograft and synthetic graft reconstructions.
Answer 5: Revision and infection rates are equivalent between allograft and synthetic graft reconstructions.
