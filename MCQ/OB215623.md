## Answer explanation

Mutations involving Core-Binding Factor A1(CBFA1) or RUNX2 are associated with Cleidocranial Dysplasia, which is best depicted by Figure B.

Cleidocranial Dysplasia is a rare congenital disorder affecting 1 per 1,000,000 individuals and is caused by an autosomal dominant mutation in the RUNX2/CBFA1 gene leading to abnormal intramembranous ossification. Patients present with proportionate dwarfism with the characteristic feature of hypoplastic or absent clavicles. Other clinical manifestations include short stature, genu valgum, scoliosis, a prominent forehead, and the ability to touch the shoulders together(Figure B).

*Balioglu et al. detail this rare skeletal dysplasia in a review of the literature on Cleidocranial Dysplasia.* In addition to the clavicle, cranium, and pelvis abnormalities **they emphasize** the spinal deformities associated with this dysplasia in the form of progressive scoliosis and kyphosis. **They state** the importance of early surveillance and regular radiographic monitoring as these spinal abnormalities may require surgical intervention.

*Farrow et al. present a review describing the clinical, radiological, and genetic features of Cleidocranial Dysplasia.* **They note** that the most common defect in Cleidocranial Dysplasia is loss of the lateral end of the clavicle. Other manifestations include large heads with elfin faces and slightly wide-set eyes. Additionally, RUNX2 is the only gene in which mutation is known to cause Cleidocranial Dysplasia, but mutations are detected in only 65% of all patients with a clinical diagnosis.

Figure A represents a marfanoid body habitus with a tall and slim frame and disproportionately long limbs, fingers, and toes. Figure B represents the classic manifestation of Cleidocranial Dysplasia where completely or partially missing clavicles allow these patients to touch their shoulders together. Figure C represents Achondroplasia with frontal bossing, short stature, and disproportionately short arms and legs when compared to the body length. Figure D depicts calf pseudohypertrophy seen in Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy. Figure E represents a high arched cavovarus foot seen in both Charcot-Marie-Tooth Disease and Friedreich's Ataxia.

Answer 1: This picture depicts the classic marfanoid body habitus seen in Marfan’s Syndrome which is the result of a mutation in fibrillin-1.
Answer 3: This picture depicts rhizomelic dwarfism with frontal bossing seen in Achondroplasia which is caused by an autosomal dominant mutation in the fibroblast growth factor receptor-3 gene (FGFR-3).
Answer 4: This picture depicts calf pseudohypertrophy seen in Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy which is caused by an X-linked recessive mutation in the dystrophin gene.
Answer 5: This picture depicts a cavovarus foot seen in both Charcot-Marie-Tooth Disease and Friedreich's Ataxia which are caused by mutations in peripheral myelin protein 22 (PMP 22) and Frataxin (FXN), respectively.
