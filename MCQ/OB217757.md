## Question

A 17-year-old female soccer player presents to your office with a complete rupture of her ACL after a non-contact pivoting injury during a recent game. You recommend ACL reconstruction and she and her parents are interested in the post-operative rehabilitation guidelines. With regards to activities in the first several weeks post-operatively, what should be avoided?

## Options

1. Resisted hamstring curls
2. Seated leg press
3. Wall squats
4. Resisted knee extensions
5. Stationary bike

## Correct option

**4. Resisted knee extensions**

## Answer explanation

Knee extensions are open chain activities which employ primarily the quadriceps muscle, simulating an anterior drawer and placing increased stress through the ACL graft.

ACL reconstruction is one of the most common procedures performed by orthopaedic surgeons, and therefore, patient's subsequent recovery and rehabilitation is important to understand. Goals immediately after reconstruction include achieving full extension and managing swelling. As rehab progresses, the focus shifts to strengthening the quadriceps and surrounding muscles without placing excess stress on the ACL graft. It is well accepted that open-chain knee extension, particularly with the knee closer to extension (0-40º), increases the load seen in the ACL graft 3-4x. Part of the reason for increased graft stress is because during this movement there is minimal to no hamstring co-contractility which leaves the anterior tibial translation unopposed.

*Wilk et al. performed a comparison of joint forces through the knee during open and closed chain exercises.* **They noted** that from full extension to 40º of flexion, anterior knee shear forces were at their highest during open chain activities. **They noted** higher degrees of quadriceps/hamstring co-activation during closed chain activities like squat and leg press.

*Beynnon et al. reviewed the science of ACL rehabilitation,* **noting** the importance of understanding the knee's biomechanics in order to protect the ACL graft from undo strain. They encouraged early range of motion and weight bearing in order to decrease contracture and patellofemoral pain. **They also concluded** that closed chain kinetic exercises resulted in decreased laxity and earlier return to activity compared with open chain exercises.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Hamstring curls should be avoided after posterior cruciate ligament (PCL) reconstruction as they produce similarly unopposed posterior tibial translation. However, they do not place the ACL graft at increased risk.
Answer 2: Seated leg press represents a closed chain exercise with co-contractility of the quadriceps and hamstrings groups, placing the ACL graft at less risk.
Answer 3: Walls squats are closed chain exercises which also represent co-contraction of the quadriceps and hamstrings muscle groups.
Answer 5: Riding a stationary bike is a closed chain activity which does not introduce unopposed anterior tibial translation.
