## Question

A 32-year-old male presents with acute pain and swelling of the right ankle following a high-energy motor vehicle accident. Radiographs confirm a displaced intra-articular ankle fracture. Synovial fluid aspirated from the ankle reveals a markedly increased neutrophil count. Histopathology of the synovium shows prominent neutrophil and macrophage infiltration with acute synovitis. Which cytokine is most likely responsible for recruiting these neutrophils and driving neutrophil-mediated cartilage degradation?

## Options

1. Interleukin-1ß (IL-1ß)
2. Interleukin-8 (IL-8)
3. Tumor necrosis factor-a (TNF-a)
4. Interleukin-10 (IL-10)
5. RANK-L

## Correct option

**2. Interleukin-8 (IL-8)**

## Answer explanation

The patient presents with post-traumatic osteoarthritis (PTOA). This is likely secondary to cartilage degeneration due to neutrophil recruitment mediated primarily by interleukin-6 (IL-6) and interleukin-8 (IL-8) (Answer 2).

Following joint injury, such as an intra-articular fracture, there is a rapid and robust increase in synovial fluid concentrations of IL-6 and IL-8, which contribute to the inflammatory cascade and subsequent cartilage degradation. IL-6 is implicated in both acute and chronic phases of PTOA. It drives catabolic processes in cartilage by inducing matrix metalloproteinases and promoting chondrocyte hypertrophy and mineralization, which accelerates cartilage breakdown. IL-6 signaling through STAT3 and ERK pathways mediates cartilage catabolism and pain, with IL-6 levels in synovial fluid and serum correlating with disease severity and progression. IL-8 acts primarily as a neutrophil chemoattractant and activator, amplifying local inflammation. Chondrocytes themselves can produce IL-8 in response to injury and stimulation by other cytokines.

*Elford and Cooper performed a study demonstrating that recombinant human IL-8 significantly increased the release of radiolabeled cartilage components when neutrophils were present, implicating IL-8 as a mediator of cartilage damage through neutrophil activation.* These findings suggest that IL-8 may play a pathogenic role in inflammatory joint diseases by promoting neutrophil-driven cartilage destruction.

*Furman et al.* **demonstrated** that acute articular ankle fractures lead to significantly increased synovitis, greater synovial macrophage infiltration, and elevated concentrations of inflammatory cytokines, including IL-6 and IL-8, in synovial fluid compared to both normal and osteoarthritic joints. Only IL-6 was found to be significantly increased in the serum, indicating that the inflammatory response is predominantly localized within the joint. **The authors concluded** that this acute local inflammatory environment following intra-articular fracture may contribute to the risk of developing post-traumatic arthritis.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: IL-1β primarily stimulates chondrocytes and synoviocytes to produce matrix metalloproteinases and prostaglandins, but it is not the major chemotactic factor for neutrophil recruitment.
Answer 3: TNF-α is a potent proinflammatory cytokine that activates macrophages and promotes cytokine release, but does not directly drive neutrophil chemotaxis.
Answer 4: IL-10 is an anti-inflammatory cytokine that down-regulates proinflammatory cytokines and neutrophil activation.
Answer 5: RANK-L stimulates osteoclast differentiation and bone resorption, and is involved in subchondral bone loss but not acute neutrophil recruitment or synovial inflammation.
