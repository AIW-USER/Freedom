## Answer explanation

Figure B represents a posterior meniscal root avulsion, which necessitates repair.

The anterior and posterior meniscal roots are anchor points for the menisci to the tibial plateau. These anchors allow the meniscus to appropriately distribute hoop stresses evenly. When either root is compromised, there is great alteration in the tibiofemoral contact pressures that have been shown to be functionally similar to that of a total meniscectomy. If not repaired, patients will continue to have pain and dysfunction that ultimately leads to rapidly progressive degenerative disease. Most commonly repair consists of a tranosseous approach, where the meniscus is passed through the tibia and fixed to the anterior tibial cortex.

*Steadman et al review outcomes after meniscal suture repair in patients older than or less than 40-years-old.* *They report on 339 meniscal repairs, all performed by a single surgeon utilizing the inside-out technique.* **They conclude** that there was no significant difference in outcome scores after meniscal suture repair based on age cohort or meniscus side, presence of an ACL tear, or concomitant microfracture procedure. Patients who underwent meniscus repair had higher function and patient satisfaction at an average of 16 years postop.

*LaPrade et al review the outcomes following posterior meniscal root repairs utilizing the anatomic transtibial pull-out technique.* **They report** a revision rate of 6.7% with all failures being on the medial meniscus. There was no difference in failure rate based on age or laterality. **They conclude** that double tunnel-transtibial pull-out meniscal root repair provides improvement in function, pain, and activity level.

*Furumatsu et al review the extent and timing of medial meniscus extrusion following posterior root tears.* **They report** that in the early period after diagnosis medial meniscus extrusion was 32.7%, however, this increased to 49.2 and 60.3% in the subacute and chronic periods, respectively. **They conclude** that the early diagnosis of root tears are an important aspect of ultimately decreasing the amount of meniscal extrusion.

Figure A demonstrates a degenerative tear of the meniscus. Figure B is the arthroscopic image of a posterior root avulsion. Figure C is representative of a normal knee. Figure D shows a complex meniscal tear. Figure E demonstrates a parrot beak type meniscal tear.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Degenerative meniscal tears are best treated with partial meniscectomy.
Answer 3: This is the arthroscopic image of a normal knee.
Answer 4: Complex tears, specifically in the white-white zone are not treated with repair.
Answer 5: This parrot beak tear is would also be best treated with partial meniscectomy.
