## Question

A 14-year-old male with sickle cell disease presents to the clinic for a follow-up with complaints of multiple joint aches and pains. On exam, there is pain with manipulation of multiple joints, including bilateral hip joints. A complete blood count is ordered to assess the ratio of hemoglobin to hematocrit ratio. What is the purpose of this study?

## Options

1. Determine the presence of vaso-occlusive crisis
2. Screen for salmonella osteomyelitis
3. Prognosticate the need for narcotic pain management
4. Screen for elevated risk of femoral head osteonecrosis
5. Assess response to hydroxyurea treatment

## Correct option

**4. Screen for elevated risk of femoral head osteonecrosis**

## Answer explanation

The hemoglobin to hematocrit ratio is one of the strongest predictors of femoral head osteonecrosis in sickle cell disease patients.

Femoral head osteonecrosis (ON) is a common complication of sickle cell disease (SCD) in children and can progress to femoral head collapse requiring total hip arthroplasty. Early identification of ON can facilitate surgical treatment aimed at preserving the native hip joint that can prevent or delay the need for arthroplasty. Recent studies reported that the best predictor of ON was a clinical proxy for mean corpuscular hemoglobin concentration (MCHC) (hemoglobin to hematocrit ratio). High-risk children with SCD should receive hip magnetic resonance imaging to diagnose early ON and facilitate interventions focused on hip preservation.

*Vanderhave et al. reviewed the orthopedic manifestations of SCD.* *They described several orthopedic manifestations of SCD, such as osteonecrosis, and osteomyelitis which are among the most severe and debilitating complications of the disease.* **They emphasized** that effective management of these bone and joint complications requires accurate diagnosis, understanding of the pathophysiology, and knowledge of available medical and surgical treatment options.

*Worrall et al. retrospectively identified risk factors associated with femoral head ON secondary to SCD in children.* **They found** that systolic and diastolic blood pressure, weight, and hemoglobin levels were elevated in children with ON. The mean MCHC was found to be the strongest predictor of ON in children with SCD. The study recommends that children who are at high risk for ON should receive hip MRI scans to diagnose early ON and facilitate interventions that focus on hip preservation and prevent the need for total hip arthroplasty.

Incorrect answers
Answers 1, 2, 3, and 5: The hemoglobin to hematocrit ratio, which is a clinical proxy to the MCHC, is the strongest predictor of femoral head ON.
