## Question

A pediatric male patient presents to the emergency department with left hip and knee pain and the inability to bear weight. Upon radiographic evaluation of the left hip, Klein's line fails to intersect with the femoral epiphysis, and the patient is diagnosed with a left slipped capital femoral epiphysis (SCFE). Following the diagnosis, pelvis and contralateral hip radiographs, metabolic labs, vitals, and a thorough history are obtained. Based on this information, you recommend prophylactic pinning of the contralateral hip to the family. What patient factors would lead to this recommendation?

## Options

1. Weight in the 40th percentile
2. Age under 10 years
3. Diabetes insipidus
4. Closed triadiate cartilage on AP pelvis radiographs
5. A modified Oxford Score of 24

## Correct option

**2. Age under 10 years**

## Answer explanation

Indications for prophylactic fixation of the contralateral hip in patients with SCFE include age younger than 10 for females, age younger than 12 for males, open triradiate cartilage, certain endocrinopathies, or a modified Oxford score of <18 (answer choice 2).

Slipped capital femoral epiphysis (SCFE) is the most common hip disorder in adolescents. Clinicians should have a high suspicion for SCFE in patients with hip or knee (referred) pain with or without ambulation. Prompt treatment with surgical stabilization is the gold standard to decrease rates of osteonecrosis. The development of a contralateral SCFE is not a rare phenomenon, with some authors reporting contralateral SCFE occurring in up to 60% of patients. Several risk factors have been reported to be associated with an increased risk of a contralateral SCFE, including younger age, skeletal immaturity, metabolic disorders, trisomy 21, Oxford bone scores, and radiographic parameters. Identifying risk factors and having an informed discussion with the patient and family are paramount to avoiding undue comorbidity. Although controversial, most authors agree that younger age (<10 years old), open triradiate cartilage, obesity, and endocrine disorders are indications for contralateral hip prophylactic fixation.

*Souder et al. performed a retrospective comparative study of 88 hips treated surgically for SCFE.* Seventy-one patients underwent in-situ fixation, and 17 underwent reduction with the Modified Dunn procedure. Reoperation, complications, and avascular necrosis (AVN) rates were studied between the two groups. **The authors found** AVN rates in 20% of cases where a Modified Dunn procedure was used for stable slips. **The authors concluded** that in situ pinning is a safe and predictable method for the treatment of stable SCFE.

*Davis et al. performed a retrospective review of 44 patients who underwent the modified Dunn procedure for SCFE.* *In total, 31 patients were treated for unstable slips, and 17 for stable slips.* **The authors found** that 2 patients in the unstable group developed AVN, while 5 patients in the stable group developed AVN. **The authors concluded** that the modified Dunn procedure results in higher complication rates (AVN) in patients with stable slips.

*Swarup et al. performed a retrospective study of patients with unilateral SCFE in an attempt to identify risk factors for subsequent contralateral slips.* *In total, 183 patients were included in the study, with 33 (18%) eventually developing contralateral SCFE.* **The authors found** that the risk of subsequent contralateral SCFE was 73% in patients with age ≤ 11 years, a modified Oxford score ≤ 20, and a difference in epiphyseal-diaphyseal angle ≤ 21°. **The authors concluded** that patients with these three risk factors, in particular, have a significant risk of subsequent contralateral SCFE.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer choice 1: Weights greater than the 90th percentile or weights lower than the 10th percentile have both been found to increase the risk of contralateral SCFE. Higher weight places more stress on the physis, while lower weight is thought to be associated with a weakened physis. Weight within the 40th percentile is not an isolated indication for contralateral prophylactic fixation.
Answer choice 3: While endocrinopathies (hypoparathyroidism, growth hormone deficiencies, diabetes mellitus) are associated with an increased risk for contralateral SCFE, diabetes insipidus alone is not known to be an independent risk factor.
Answer choice 4: Closed triradiate cartilage, a sign of increased skeletal maturity, is not an indication for prophylactic contralateral hip pinning. Open triradiate cartilage, on the other hand, is an indication for contralateral fixation, due to an increased risk of contralateral slippage.
Answer choice 5: The modified Oxford score is used to determine bone maturity radiographically. This score is frequently calculated when evaluating for risk of contralateral SCFE. Scores are based on radiographic ossification centers around the hip, with scores ranging from 16-26. Studies have found around a 98% risk for contralateral SCFE with scores 16-18, while a score of 24 (indicating more skeletally mature) has a 0% likelihood of contralateral SCFE. Therefore, this is not an indication for prophylactic contralateral pinning.
