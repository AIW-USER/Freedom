## Question

A 16-year-old presents with lateral based ankle pain of the sinus tarsi. Examination reveals a rigid flatfoot with diminished subtalar motion. The ankle is stable to anterior drawer testing. The patient states that he has had recurrent ankle sprains while playing sports. A lateral radiograph of the foot is shown in Figure A. This reveals an abnormal connection between which two bones, and what is the next step in treatment?

![[q210273-a-tarsal-coalition-1.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Calcaneus and talus; short period of immobilization
2. Navicular and calcaneus; coalition resection
3. Cuboid and navicular; coalition resection
4. Calcaneus and navicular; short period of immobilization
5. Calcaneus and talus; shoe-wear modification

## Correct option

**4. Calcaneus and navicular; short period of immobilization**

## Answer explanation

The radiograph reveals the "anteater" sign, which is representative of a calcaneonavicular tarsal coalition.

Tarsal coalition is an anatomic anomaly that results in an abnormal link between two bones in the foot. The presentation is usually that of a rigid flat foot, commonly during late childhood or early adolescence, and may result in recurrent ankle sprains. The calcaneonavicular coalition is most common and usually presents symptomatically between 8-12 years of age with lateral based ankle pain. An examination will reveal diminished subtalar motion. Plain radiographs are usually diagnostic.

*Mubarak et al. reports on the treatment of symptomatic calcaneonavicular coalitions with excision and interpositional local fat graft placement.* **They conclude** that resection of a calcaneonavicular coalition with interposition of fat graft, when meticulously performed, is an effective way to relieve symptoms, restore subtalar motion, and return patients to activities while preventing reossification.

Swensen at al. author a review on tarsal coalitions. The congenital condition is characterized by an aberrant osseous, cartilaginous, or fibrinous union of the calcaneal and navicular bones. Calcaneonavicular coalitions are the most common form of tarsal coalitions identified within epidemiologic studies. The authors highlight that fact that the first step in treatment is a short period of immobilization, while surgery is indicated for continued symptomatic cases that fail non-operative modalities.

Figure A is a lateral view of the left foot showing the "anteater sign", representing an elongated anterior process of the calcaneus, and is commonly seen in calcaneonavilcular coalitions. Image Case courtesy of Radswiki, Radiopaedia.org, rID: 11992. Illustration A demonstrates the "C" sign, which is classic of a talocalcaneal coalition.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1, 3, and 5 incorrectly identify the coalition seen in the radiograph. A coalition between the talus and calcaneus would demonstrate a "C" sign as depicted in illustration A. The first step in treatment is a short period of immobilization and symptomatic management with NSAIDs. A thorough understanding of this clinically significant entity is important for restoring joint motion and preventing long-term disability.
Answer 2: The first step in treatment is a short period of immobilization and symptomatic management with NSAIDs.
