## Question

An otherwise healthy 7-year-old male has had intermittent left ankle pain over the last 9 months. Weight-bearing radiographs are shown in Figure A. What is the next best step in treatment?

![[q8985-a-ap-standing-preop.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Distal tibia and fibula epiphysiodesis
2. Medial hemiepiphysiodesis
3. Short leg cast
4. Curettage and bone grafting
5. Complete mass resection with negative margins

## Correct option

**2. Medial hemiepiphysiodesis**

## Answer explanation

The radiographs show an osteochondroma causing a valgus ankle deformity. This is seen in multiple hereditary exostosis and given the skeletal immaturity seen in the radiographs, correcting this deformity would be best with a medial hemiepiphysiodesis.

Osteochondromas are common benign bone lesions among children and adolescents, typically presenting between ages 10-20 years of age. These lesions can be sporadic, solitary findings or arise as a result of genetic mutation of the EXT gene passed down in autosomal dominant fashion. Radiographs will show characteristic appearance with confluence of the exostosis with cortical and cancellous bone of the metaphysis and a hyaline cartilage cap. These can be asymptomatic and discovered incidentally or may cause local mass effect on nearby structures, an unsightly appearance, and/or limb deformities. For angular limb deformities, corrective surgery is required to restore the mechanical axis, with technique dependent on skeletal maturity. As in cases of ankle valgus in the skeletally immature, medial hemiepiphysiodesis will restore alignment.

*Stieber et al. reviewed management of various exostosis-induced deformities of the long bones.* Leg length discrepancies and valgus-angular deformity of the distal ankle are not uncommon. Hemiepiphysiodesis for skeletally immature, or opening-wedge varus osteotomy in skeletally mature, have proven beneficial. Not all cases require surgery, as frequently opposing deformity of the femur corrects the mechanical alignment of the extremity.

*Rupprecht et al. evaluated outcomes after temporary medial tibial screw hemiepiphysiodesis for valgus-producing ankle osteochondromas.* *They recorded a correction from 14 degrees of valgus tibiotalar tilt to neutral after an average of 37 months.* This demonstrates a simple and effective treatment for correction of valgus ankle deformities among individuals with multiple hereditary exostoses.

Figure A shows bilateral AP ankle radiographs demonstrating ankle valgus alignment of 14 degrees from neutral. The fibula is shortened due the osteochondroma with the characteristic horned appearance at the distal metaphysis.
Illustration A shows correction to within 7 degrees of neutral after 34 months. Illustration B shows a gross section of a osteochondroma arising from the metaphyseal region of a long bone.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1- Epiphysiodesis would not correct the angular deformity and cause an additional leg length discrepancy
Answer 3- This would be an option if this were an impending fracture from a unicameral bone cyst.
Answer 4- Curettage and grafting would be an option for an aneurysmal bone cyst but the radiograph is showing a classic osteochondroma.
Answer 5- This would be appropriate for locally symptomatic lesions or malignancy but this wouldn't address the deformity.
