## Question

Figure A shows a radiograph of a 10-year-old male who sustained a left shoulder injury playing hockey 1-week ago. He was initially seen by the team trainer and given a sling for comfort. What would be the most appropriate next step?

![[q5683-a-20e2ec8ee8e14045a39000d5db6198-2.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. CT scan left shoulder and chest
2. MRI arthrogram
3. Percutaneous biopsy
4. Closed reduction and internal fixation
5. Continue sling immobilization

## Correct option

**5. Continue sling immobilization**

## Answer explanation

This patient has sustained a pathological fracture of the left proximal humerus due to an underlying unicameral bone cyst.

Unicameral bone cysts are non-neoplastic, serous fluid-filled bone lesions that are thought to result from temporary failure of medullary bone formation near the physis. They most commonly present in the proximal humerus. Initial treatment of fractures through cystic lesions of the humerus focus on immobilization with a sling for 3-6 weeks. Pathologic fracture through the cyst upon presentation versus those who present without fracture have an increased rate of healing with observation alone. Alternative options when more aggressive treatment is indicated include injection with steroid or bone marrow, curettage and bone grafting, and/or internal fixation.

*Ortiz et al. reviewed their experience with pathologic fractures involving benign bony lesions.* **They recommend** initial management of UBC with conservative measures as fracture healing is predictable without surgical treatment. Adjuvant therapies (eg. steroids) may be indicated when there is a persistence of the cyst after healing.

*Urakawa et al retrospectively reviewed 155 cases of UBC.* **They found** healing rates were 30% and 83% with observation and surgery, respectively in their study. Of note, 50% of cysts that presented with fractures went on to heal with observation alone, as defined in their study as healing of over 50% of the cyst area on X-ray at the last follow up.

Figure A shows a proximal humerus pathological fracture. There is no periosteal reaction or cortex destruction. There is no soft-tissue reaction. The lesion has benign findings that are consistent with a UBC.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1,2,3,4: The diagnosis of a UBC is usually made on the basis of the lesion's typical radiographic appearance. Typically, no further evaluation is required.
