## Options

1. Prompt administration of an antibiotic that functions by inhibition of protein synthesis by binding to 50S ribosomal subunit and an antibiotic that disrupts the synthesis of bacterial peptidoglycan layers.
2. Prompt administration of bactericidal antibiotics, one that functions by inhibiting protein synthesis by binding to the 30S ribosomal subunit and one that disrupts the synthesis of bacterial peptidoglycan layers.
3. Prompt administration of bacteriostatic antibiotics, one that functions by inhibiting protein synthesis by binding to the 30S ribosomal subunit and one that disrupts the synthesis of bacterial peptidoglycan layers.
4. Prompt administration of an antibiotic that disrupts DNA replication by inhibiting DNA gyrase.
5. Prompt administration of a bactericidal antibiotic that interrupts the synthesis of bacterial peptidoglycan layers by blocking peptidoglycan polymerization.

## Correct option

**2. Prompt administration of bactericidal antibiotics, one that functions by inhibiting protein synthesis by binding to the 30S ribosomal subunit and one that disrupts the synthesis of bacterial peptidoglycan layers.**

## Answer explanation

This patient presents with a type III open tibia fracture. The most important intervention to prevent complications is urgent administration of systemic antibiotics. Currently, the recommendations for type III open fractures are coverage with a first-generation cephalosporin and an aminoglycoside (bactericidal agents; Answer 2).

Open fractures are a common issue faced by orthopedists. The most important first steps in the management of open fractures include urgent administration of systemic antibiotics, followed by urgent irrigation and debridement with fracture stabilization. The Gustilo-Anderson classification of open fractures is the most commonly used to guide antibiotic prophylaxis for varying types of open fractures. Multiple different antibiotic regimens have been trialed and evaluated. Currently, first-generation cephalosporins and aminoglycosides are the standard of care for type III open fractures. Cephalosporins are beta-lactam antibiotics that act in a bactericidal fashion by binding to penicillin-binding proteins and preventing cell wall synthesis. Aminoglycosides are also bactericidal antibiotics that cause cell death by inhibiting the 30S subunit of the bacterial ribosome. The aminoglycoside family includes gentamycin, neomycin, tobramycin, and others that are effective against gram-negative aerobic bacteria.

*Garner et al. provide a review of antibiotic use in the management of open fractures.* *The authors reviewed classes of antibiotics and their mechanism of action and the evolving use of various medications in the changing landscape of antibiotic stewardship.* *This review highlights the importance of gram-positive coverage (typically with first-generation cephalosporin) for all open fractures and the addition of gram-negative coverage (typically with aminoglycoside) for type III open fractures.* **The authors emphasize** prompt administration of antibiotics as the most important factor in open fracture management.

*Carver et al. also published a review of antibiotic use and delivery methods in open fractures.* **The authors recommend** a first-generation cephalosporin and an aminoglycoside for type III open fractures. This paper concluded that the single most important factor for reducing infection rate is the early administration of antibiotics with efficacy against gram-negative and gram-positive bacteria.

*Lack et al. performed a retrospective study of antibiotic timing of type III open tibia fractures at a level 1 trauma center.* Time to antibiotic administration and eventual wound coverage were evaluated in their 137-patient cohort. **The authors found** that antibiotic administration greater than 66 minutes and greater than 5 days to wound coverage were univariate predictors of infection. **The authors concluded** that antibiotic prophylaxis and wound coverage should be achieved as early as possible in the setting of type III open tibia fractures.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer choice 1: Antibiotics that function by inhibiting protein synthesis by acting on the 50s ribosomal subunit include Macrolides, Chloramphenicol, Linezolid, Clindamycin, and others. These are not indicated for type III open fractures. The second antibiotic mentioned includes Cephalosporins, Penicillin, and others. Cephalosporins are indicated for open fracture management.
Answer choice 3: Bacteriostatic antibiotics that bind to the 30S subunit refer to tetracyclines. These are not routinely indicated for open fracture management. Furthermore, cephalosporins are cell wall synthesis inhibitors but are bactericidal, not bacteriostatic.
Answer choice 4: This mechanism describes fluoroquinolones (ciprofloxacin, levofloxacin). Fluoroquinolones can be added to the typical antibiotic regimen in the setting of open fractures contaminated with fresh or saltwater wounds.
Answer choice 5: Vancomycin is a bactericidal antibiotic that binds to the D-Ala-D-Ala peptide, disrupting the basic building blocks of bacterial cell walls.
