Chapter: Trauma MCQs — Polytrauma Patients

## Question

In a polytrauma trauma patient most important deciding factor for definite fixation of a femoral shaft fracture is (provided no severe head, cardiac injury)

## Options

1. Blood pressure
2. lactate <4.0 mmol/L
3. No of long bone fractures in excess of 3
4. base excess 5.5 mmol/L

## Correct option

**2. lactate <4.0 mmol/L**

## Answer explanation

The majority of current data support early fracture stabilization of not only the femoral shaft, but also the proximal femur, pelvic ring, acetabulum, and thoracolumbar spine. The type and timing of surgical intervention will depend on the magnitude and duration of acidosis, associated coagulopathy, and the response to resuscitation. Injury complexity and underlying factors, including patient age and cardiac function are also important to consider. Underlying severe head injury could also be an indication for damage control, in order to maintain cerebral perfusion pressure (CPP) greater than 70 mm Hg.

Severe musculoskeletal trauma and associated hemorrhage result in tissue hypoperfusion, which leads to acidosis. The extent of acidosis can be assessed by pH, base excess, or lactate. Normalization of these values within 24 hours of injury is predictive of survival. The pH and base excess are less specific than lactate and are more easily regulated by compensatory mechanisms. The pH and base excess are also more subject to confounders, including alcohol ingestion or renal disease; thus, lactate is the most reliable indicator of resuscitation. Improvement in lactate over time is one of the most accurate and specific indicators of a patient's volume repletion and tissue oxygenation.

Clinical reports suggest that a patient with improving acidosis, as measured by pH 7.25, base excess 5.5 mmol/L, or lactate <4.0 mmol/L, will benefit from definitive fracture care, as long as severe head injury or other medical pathology does not preclude it.

These parameters have been termed “Early Appropriate Care” (EAC) and recommend surgical stabilization of spine, pelvis, acetabulum, and femur fractures within 36 hours of injury in adequately resuscitated patients. However, damage control would be indicated for protracted metabolic acidosis, severe head injury, or cardiac ischemia.

Answer: 2
