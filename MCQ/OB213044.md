## Question

A 23-year-old female patient, with a history of IV drug use (IVDU), presents with worsening lower back and right hip pain that is worse with weight-bearing for the past several days. She has a history of multiple soft tissue infections and abscesses that have resolved with bedside I&Ds and oral antibiotics regimens. Current vitals are significant for a blood pressure of 87/62, HR 105, RR 28, T 102° F, and SpO2 of 97%. The physical exam is remarkable for pain with lateral compression of the iliac crest, but a painless range of motion of bilateral hips. Peripheral blood cultures were significant for gram-positive cocci in clusters and chains. Prior to the onset of symptoms, the patient denies any history of lower back or leg pain. ## Options

1. Initiate 1st-generation cephalosporin pharmacotherapy
2. Proceed with surgical incision and drainage of the L4-5 disc space followed by instrumented fusion
3. Urgent incision and drainage of the right SI joint
4. Initiate vancomycin and clindamycin pharmacotherapy
5. Urgent incision and drainage of the right hip

## Correct option

**4. Initiate vancomycin and clindamycin pharmacotherapy**

## Answer explanation

The patient is an IV drug-user (IVDU) presenting septic shock from acute sacroiliitis without an apparent abscess on magnetic resonance imaging. The appropriate next step would be to start vancomycin and clindamycin given the patient's history concerning for resistant organisms and gram stain suggesting concomitant Streptococcus and Staphylococcus involvement.

Sacroiliitis can be the result of inflammatory arthropathies, such as ankylosing spondylitis, or from an infection. Septic arthritis of the SI joint is relatively uncommon but can occur in patients with a history of IVDU. If patients are presenting with signs of septic shock, the most important treatment step is to initiate broad-spectrum antibiotics that cover the most commonly involved organisms. In most cases, infectious sacroiliitis can be treated solely with antibiotics. If the patient fails a regimen of appropriate antibiotics, surgical debridement may be necessary.

*Allison et al. performed a retrospective review of 215 IV drug-abusing patients presenting with bone and/or joint infections over a seven-year period.* **The authors reported** an oxacillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus infection rate increase from 21% to 73% at the end of the study period with osteomyelitis having a significantly increased involvement with gram-negative rods, anaerobes, and polymicrobial infections. **The authors recommend** initiating broad-spectrum antibiotics in patients with a history of IV drug abuse presenting with bone or joint infections.

*Buckman et al. reviewed antibiotic administration and stewardship in the setting of sepsis.* **The authors stated** that the prompt administration, within one hour of recognition, of broad-spectrum antibiotics is the most important factor for patient survival when septic shock develops. **The authors recommended** including clindamycin pharmacotherapy in patients presenting with septic shock and possible streptococcal involvement. **They concluded** that early antibiotic administration is the most important factor in the treatment of patients with septic shock.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The patient is at high risk for infection with resistant organisms from the IVDU, which necessitates vancomycin as an initial agent. Once the infecting organism is confirmed, adjusts to the antibiotic regimen can be made accordingly. Additionally, the gram stain demonstrated gram-positive cocci in chains, suggesting possible streptococcal involvement, which should be addressed with clindamycin.
Answer 2: The patient does have evidence of grade 1 isthmic spondylolisthesis at L4-5, but the history and MRI imaging do not suggest this as the cause of the patient's current symptoms.
Answer 3: The MRI imaging of the pelvis does indicate right-sided sacroiliitis consistent with an infectious process. However, surgical treatment for sacroiliitis is recommended if the patient fails an initial trial of antibiotics or there is a discrete abscess.
Answer 5: The physical exam was unremarkable for septic arthritis of the hip given that painless range of motion was present.
