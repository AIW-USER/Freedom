## Question

A 57-year-old female presents with a mass on the volar aspect of her right forearm. The mass has been growing in size over the last 4 months and is palpable on exam. She does not report any pain, fever or paresthesias. She denies any recent trauma to the extremity. Figures A and B show axial T1 and T2 weighted MRI of the right wrist. What is the next best step in the management of this patient?

## Options

1. Observation
2. Wide excision
3. Radiation
4. Chemotherapy
5. Biopsy

## Correct option

**5. Biopsy**

## Answer explanation

This patient has an expanding, painless soft tissue mass that has an indeterminate appearance on MRI. A biopsy should be performed to clarify the diagnosis.

Indications for biopsy include aggressive bone or soft tissue lesions, lesions larger than 5 cm, lesions deep to the fascia, lesions overlying neurovascular structures, lesions that remain undiagnosed after imaging, solitary bony lesions in patients with a history of malignancy and for confirmatory purposes.

*Rougraff et al. reviewed the literature in order to create evidence based recommendations for when to perform a biopsy for soft-tissue masses.* **They found** a paucity of evidence on the subject, and as a result, the authors offered their expert opinion and recommended biopsy be performed whenever a mass has biologic activity, is larger than 3 cm, is actively growing, is symptomatic, in cases of soft tissue “hematoma” that is not associated with trauma, has no history of ecchymosis, and is increasing in size or symptoms.

Figure A and B are T1 and T2 weighted MRIs of the right wrist revealing a soft tissue mass associated with the flexor carpi radialis tendon sheath (arrows). Illustration A is a histologic image from the biopsy of the patient in the stem demonstrating a biphasic synovial cell sarcoma showing the typical pattern of epithelial cells and fibrosarcoma-like spindle cells.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Observation would not be appropriate in a patient with an expanding soft tissue mass.
Answer 2-4: Wide excision, radiation, and chemotherapy (i.e., treatment) without confirming the diagnosis would not be the most appropriate next step.
