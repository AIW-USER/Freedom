## Question

A 34-year-old male presents to the trauma bay after sustaining a gunshot wound to their cervical spine. On examination, you note that the patient has diminished right-sided sensation to pain and temperature in the C7 dermatome and distal levels, as well as loss of left-sided motor function and vibratory sensation in the C5 myotome/dermatome and distal levels. Which of the following etiologies best describes the pathology of this patient’s injury?

## Options

1. Hemisection injury of the right spinal cord at C5
2. Hemisection injury of the left spinal cord at C5
3. Hemisection injury of the right spinal cord at C7
4. Hemisection injury of the left spinal cord at C7
5. Injury to the anterior spinal artery at C5

## Correct option

**2. Hemisection injury of the left spinal cord at C5**

## Answer explanation

The patient presents with Brown Sequard Syndrome caused by an incomplete left-sided spinal cord injury manifesting in ipsilateral loss of motor function and vibratory sensation, as well as contralateral loss of sensation to pain and temperature two to three levels below the level of the injury (Answer 2).

Brown Sequard Syndrome is caused by spinal cord hemisection and is typically seen with penetrating trauma to the spinal cord. Patients with this condition typically present with motor weakness and loss of vibratory sensation and proprioceptive abilities on the ipsilateral side of the injury, resulting from damage to the dorsal columns and corticospinal tracts. They also present with contralateral loss of temperature and pain sensation starting 2-3 levels below the level of the injury due to damage to the spinothalamic tracts. This condition has the best prognosis out of all incomplete spinal cord injuries, with over 75% of patients independently ambulating at final follow-up.

*Klakeel et al. presented two case reports of 13-year-old adolescent boys with anterior spinal cord syndrome.* *They describe the two cases in detail, reviewing the blood supply of the spinal cord and discussing the pathophysiology of anterior spinal cord syndrome as well as the rehabilitation that must occur after injury.*

*Moskowitz and Schroeppel described a case of lateral hemisection syndrome (Brown Sequard Syndrome).* *They discuss the common physical findings of this condition, which include ipsilateral hemiplegia and contralateral hemianalgesia, as well as the conservative treatment favored for this diagnosis.* **They conclude** that Brown Squared has the most favorable prognosis of all the spinal cord injuries, with over 75% of patients having restored ambulatory ability after rehabilitation.

*Hashmi et al. described a case report of central cord syndrome and reviewed the current knowledge regarding the pathophysiology and treatment of this condition.* *They discuss the manifestations of this condition, which typically include symmetric and bilateral upper extremity weakness greater than lower extremity weakness, bladder dysfunction, and variable sensory changes below the level of the injury.* Treatment includes maintaining adequate perfusion to prevent spinal cord ischemia and possible surgical intervention depending on the stability of the spine/presence of cord compression. **They concluded** that central cord syndrome requires a meticulous neurological exam and appropriate imaging when suspected, along with thoughtful consideration of non-operative and operative interventions depending on the patient’s clinical status, comorbidities, and underlying injury.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1. Hemisection injury of the right spinal cord at C5 would lead to diminished right-sided motor function and vibratory sensation, as well as diminished left-sided sensation to pain and temperature.
Answer 3. Hemisection of the left spinal cord at C7 would lead to diminished left-sided vibratory sensation and motor function, as well as diminished right-sided sensation to pain and temperature. However, these findings would only be found in the C7 dermatome/myotome and distal levels.
Answer 4. Hemisection of the right spinal cord at C7 would lead to diminished right-sided vibratory sensation and motor function, as well as diminished left-sided sensation to pain and temperature. However, these findings would only be found in the C7 dermatome/myotome and distal levels.
Answer 5. Injury to the anterior spinal artery leads to anterior spinal cord injury, which presents with bilateral lower extremity weakness and loss of sensation to pain and temperature but not vibration due to the preservation of the dorsal columns.
