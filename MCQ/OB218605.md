## Question

A 30-year-old flag football player sustained a hyperextension injury to his second toe when he attempted to spin around a defender. Since the injury, he is unable to run without pain during the push-off phase of his stance. On examination, the toe is quite painful with hyperextension and subtle instability is present with vertical translation at the MTP joint. An MRI is obtained which shows grade II ligamentous disruption off of the second proximal phalanx. What is the next best step in management?

## Options

1. Rest, NSAIDS, and use of stiff-soled shoe
2. Direct repair of injured structure
3. Direct repair of injured structure + metatarsal osteotomy
4. Reconstruction of injured structure
5. Arthrodesis

## Correct option

**1. Rest, NSAIDS, and use of stiff-soled shoe**

## Answer explanation

This patient sprained his 2nd MTP plantar plate, which is typically treated nonoperatively with ice, rest, NSAIDS, stiff-soled shoes, and metatarsal pads.

Plantar plate rupture, occurs when there is a hyperextension injury to the toe. The plantar plate usually ruptures off the proximal phalanx and can retract proximally. Tears are graded I-III based on the severity, with I being a sprain, II a partial tear, and III a complete tear of the plantar plate. Plantar plate ruptures can occur at the hallux or the lesser toes. Diagnosis can be confirmed on physical exam and radiographs. Most injuries can be treated nonoperatively initially with rest, NSAIDS, ice and stiff-soled shoes, and metatarsal pads. In more serious cases, sometimes a period of casting is necessary to allow for healing. If conservative care is unsuccessful, repair of the plantar plate can be performed.

*Flint et al. reviewed subjective, functional, and radiographic outcomes for lesser MTP joint plantar plate repairs.* *They performed the plantar plate repairs from a dorsal approach where they also performed a Weil osteotomy.* Good to excellent satisfaction scores were seen in 80% of patients at 12 months and pain/outcome scores also improved significantly after surgery. They also mentioned that mean MTP range of motion decreased by about 12° on average from pre- to post-operatively.

*Doty and Coughlin reviewed lesser toe MTP joint instability and plantar plate deficiency in JAAOS.* *They discuss the understanding of the pathoanatomy and advocate for using primary repair as part of the algorithm to correct deformities related to this injury.* *They further expand on plantar plate repair and discuss the different approaches and techniques that have been successfully utilized.*

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Repair of the plantar plate would be appropriate in cases of failed conservative management, retraction of sesamoids for the hallux or loose fragments in the MTP joint.
Answer 3: Repair of the plantar plate and Weil metatarsal shortening osteotomy is indicated in lesser toe plantar plate ruptures that require surgery.
Answer 4: Reconstruction of the plantar plate is not typically necessary, and the plantar plate is usually repaired.
Answer 5: Arthrodesis may be a late consideration if MTP arthritis were to develop, but this is not described here.
