## Question

A 16-year-old male patient sustains a right shoulder injury. Growth charts and radiographic assessment estimate less than 1 year of growth remaining. Which of the following shoulder injuries would be best treated with closed reduction and percutanous pinning?

## Options

1. Minimally displaced greater tuberosity fracture (<5mm)
2. Medially angulated proximal humerus metaphyseal fracture (10-degrees)
3. Neer-Horowitz classification Type I proximal humerus physeal fracture
4. Neer-Horowitz classification Type III proximal humerus physeal fracture
5. Displaced distal clavicle fracture (<5mm)

## Correct option

**4. Neer-Horowitz classification Type III proximal humerus physeal fracture**

## Answer explanation

Severely displaced proximal humeral physeal fractures (Neer-Horowitz classification Type III and IV) are best treated with closed reduction and percutanous pinning in older adolescents (15 years or older) nearing skeletal maturity.

Proximal humerus fractures in adolescents older than 12 years of age are most commonly Salter–Harris type II physeal fractures. Management of these fractures is largely based on the degree of displacement, fracture pattern/stability and bone remodelling potential (or age of patient). The ability of bone to remodel has been shown to significantly decrease as patients reach skeletal maturity. Therefore, the most appropriate management for severely displaced fractures would be fixation with closed reduction and percutanous pinning.

*Dobbs et al. examined 20 of 28 patients with Neer III/IV proximal humeral physeal fractures treated with closed reduction and percutaneous pinning.* *At the 4 year follow-up, all patients had near-normal glenohumeral motion and stated to have regained full preinjury functional use of the involved extremity.* All fractures healed with acceptable alignment.

*Hutchinson et al. retrospectively reviewed outcomes of percuatneous pinning vs. IM nailing of unstable proximal humerus fractures in 50 adolescent patients.* **They found** that pinning resulted in shorter surgeries and decreased blood loss, and lesser need for operative implant removal. Both modalities were effective at stabilizing severely displaced proximal humerus fractures in the older paediatric population.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Minimally displaced greater tuberosity fracture (<5mm) can be treated conservatively with weekly radiographs to ensure acceptable fracture alignment.
Answer 2: Medially angulated proximal humerus metaphyseal fracture (10-degrees) can be treated conservatively with weekly radiographs to ensure acceptable fracture alignment.
Answer 3: Neer-Horowitz classification Type I proximal humerus fracture can be treated conservatively with weekly radiographs to ensure acceptable fracture alignment.
Answer 5: Displaced distal clavicle fractures in children (<5mm) are typically treated conservatively, unlike the adult counterparts which may be treated conservatively or operatively depending on fracture pattern, comminution and integrity of the C-C ligament complex. In either population, operative intervention does not usually involve pinning due to the potential risk of neurovascular and lung injury with pin migration/displacement.
