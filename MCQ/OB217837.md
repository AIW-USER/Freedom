## Correct option

**5. Squamous Cell Carcinoma**

## Answer explanation

This patient's findings are consistent with squamous cell carcinoma (SCC) of the finger. Amputation is the recommended treatment when there is bone involvement

Squamous cell carcinoma is a malignant soft tissue tumor most commonly found in elderly patients. Common anatomic locations for this tumor are the dorsum of the hand and forearm. It is the most common subungual malignancy. The diagnosis is made with a biopsy. The treatment choice is dependent upon the extent of the lesion at the time of presentation. It can vary from Mohs microsurgery to digital amputation. Amputation is recommended when there is bone involvement.

*Askari et al. reviewed the risk factors for local and regional recurrence of SCC of the hand.* **They reported** that specific characteristics (web space location, bilateral tumors, multiple tumors, and prior history of SCC) of the tumor may increase the chance of recurrence. **They concluded** that SCC tumors of the hand have a high tendency for local recurrence but a low rate of metastasis.

*Plate et al. reviewed malignant tumors in the hand and wrist.* **They reported** that skin tumors predominate; the most common are squamous cell carcinomas, followed in frequency by basal cell carcinomas and malignant melanomas. **They noted** that these tumors often appear as painless masses that sometimes have been present for months or even years and deceptively appear to be benign. **They concluded** that bone malignancies require special considerations.
