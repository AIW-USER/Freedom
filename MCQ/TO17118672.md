Chapter: Arthroscopy MCQs — ACL

## Question

Not true about ACL anatomy

## Options

1. Narrowest in the mid substance
2. There is no histological separation of two bundles
3. None of ACL bundle behave isometrically during flexion and extension
4. Posterolateral bundle is tight in flexion and loosen in extension

## Correct option

**4. Posterolateral bundle is tight in flexion and loosen in extension**

## Answer explanation

The ACL is enveloped into the synovial membrane of the human knee, which by definition places the ligament intraarticular but extra synovial. The ligament originates at the medial side of the lateral femoral condyle and runs an oblique course through the intercondylar fossa distal anteromedial to the insertion at the medial tibial eminence.

ACL consists of two bundles, the anteromedial and posterolateral bundle, so named for the orientation of their tibial insertions. The narrowest diameter of the ACL occurs in the mid-substance, relative to the ligament mid substance, the tibial and femoral insertion of the ACL are over 3.5 times larger.

There is no histological separation of different bundles whether there are two, three, or multiple functional bundles−there is general consensus two functional bands can be distinguished as the tension varies among the fibers in the ligament with range of motion.

Femoral attachment:

The anteromedial bundle is located high and deep in the intercondylar fossa, whereas the postero-lateral bundle is located lower and shallower. Because arthroscopic surgery is normally performed with the knee in 90° of flexion, in the clinical literature the notch roof is considered high (anatomic description, anterior), the anterior rim is considered shallow, the posterior part is considered deep, and the posterior part is considered low.

Tibial attachment: The terminology of the bundles is determined according to their tibial insertion, with the fibers of the anteromedial bundle originating in the most proximal part of the femoral insertion and inserting at the anteromedial tibial insertion. Fibers of the posterolateral bundle originate distally at the femur and insert on the posterolateral part of the tibial insertion.

Intercondylar notch: The intercondylar fossa has its maximum diameter in the posterior part and converges towards the anterior direction. The width of the notch is apparently smaller in women when compared to men. This could probably be an additional factor explaining the higher incidence of ACL injuries in female athletes besides gender-specific neuromuscular differences. The notch width index is used for the ratio epicondylar width to notch width. Patients with smaller notch –width index have high incidence of ACL tear.

On conventional lateral knee radiographs the notch roof projects as a dense profile known as Blumensaat's line. The average angle between the longitudinal axis of the femur and the notch roof (notch roof angle) is given as 37°, but varies between 23° and 60.

Two bundles: Length and orientation of the fibers of the ACL change throughout the passive flexion and extension and tibial internal and external rotation. When the knee is extended, the posterolateral bundle is tight and the anteromedial bundle is moderately lax (or moderately tight). As the knee is flexed, the femoral attachment of the ACL moves to a more horizontal orientation, causing the anteromedial bundle to tighten and the posterolateral bundle to loosen up. However, none of the ACL fibers behave isometrically. The AM bundle is longer and bulkier than the PL bundle. Primarily ACL prevents anterior tibial translation on the femur. The ACL is a secondary restraint to internal rotation of the tibia and resist varus displacement at full extension.

Answer: 4
