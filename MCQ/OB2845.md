## Question

What is the preferred orthotic device for a symptomatic adult foot deformity that is shown in Figure A, has no arthritis on radiographs, and responds to Coleman block testing as shown in Figure B?

![[q2845-a-cavovarus-foot.jpg]]
*Figure A*

![[q2845-b-cavovarus-foot-colamn-block-test-corrected.jpg]]
*Figure B*

## Options

1. Short walker boot
2. Accommodative custom orthotics
3. Lace up soft ankle brace
4. Medial hindfoot posting with arch support
5. Lateral hindfoot posting with recessed first ray

## Correct option

**5. Lateral hindfoot posting with recessed first ray**

## Answer explanation

The best orthotic for this correctible deformity is a semi-rigid orthotic with a recess for the head of the plantarflexed first ray and lateral hindfoot posting to correct the heel varus. This choice involves recognizing that the plantarflexed first ray drives the hindfoot into varus creating the cavovarus foot deformity. This cavus arch and varus hindfoot place excessive pressure on the lateral border of the foot. The Coleman block test demonstrates if the hindfoot deformity is correctable with depression of the first ray as seen in Figure B.

*Manoli et al described the best orthotic for this correctible deformity to be a semi-rigid orthotic with a recess for the head of the plantarflexed first ray and lateral hindfoot posting to correct the heel varus.* Approximately 75% of patients have improved stability and pain relief with this type of orthosis. Rigid orthoses molded to the cavus usually exacerbate symptoms.

*Janisse et al reviewed the four classes of orthotics: (1) accommodative - cushions and protects the foot; (2) the semi-rigid - cushions and protects as well as provides support, control, and weight redistribution; (3) rigid – offers arch support; and (4) partial foot prosthesis - addresses partial amputations and helps protect the foot.*
