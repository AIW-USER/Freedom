## Question

A 24-year-old competitive LARPer falls from a siege engine onto an outstretched wrist during a simulated 12th-century battle reenactment. Three years later, his pain progresses, and he is seen by his local hand surgeon who diagnoses the faux-knight with early scaphoid nonunion advanced collapse (SNAC). The hand surgeon recommends a distal pole of the scaphoid excision. Which of the following findings, if present, would be a contraindication to the procedure?

## Options

1. Arthrosis localized to the radial side of the scaphoid and radial styloid
2. Scaphocapitate arthrosis
3. Periscaphoid arthrosis
4. Arthrosis of the scapho-trapezium-trapezoid (STT) joint
5. Dorsal intercalated segment instability (DISI)

## Correct option

**5. Dorsal intercalated segment instability (DISI)**

## Answer explanation

Excision of the distal pole of the scaphoid may precipitate DISI and authors of numerous case series have reported pre-existing DISI as the primary contra-indication to distal pole excision.

Excision of the distal pole of the scaphoid, in the setting of SNAC, is a less invasive alternative to a scaphoid excision with four-corner fusion or a proximal row carpectomy. However, excision of the distal pole has led to an increase in the scapholunate (SL) angle and it is believed that excision can destabilize the proximal row. There is some evidence that this also causes an increase in carpal collapse and arthrosis. Therefore, pre-existing DISI is a contra-indication to distal pole excision in a SNAC wrist.

*Malerich et al reviewed 19 patients at 15 years who underwent distal scaphoid excision for degenerative arthritis from SNAC.* *Patients noted an increase in grip strength and total arch of motion.* However, half developed worsening arthritis and there was also a notable increase in the SL angle. Two patients went onto receive a proximal row carpectomy and one went onto a wrist fusion. **The authors report** that distal pole resection promises favorable long-term clinical outcomes, although it may not be indicated in patients with SL instability.

*Ruch and Papadonikolakis reviewed 13 patients at 5 years who underwent distal pole scaphoid excision for non-union following surgical treatment of the scaphoid.* *After surgery, only 2 patients had pain with strenuous activity.* The arc of motion improved by 52 degrees. **The authors noted** an increase in the SL angle postoperatively, indicating DISI in 6 patients. **The authors recommend** this procedure in patients without pre-existing DISI deformity.

*Soejima et al reviewed 9 patients at 2 years who underwent a distal pole excision for SNAC wrist.* After surgery, 4/9 had no pain, and 5 had mild pain with strenuous activity. Arc of motion increased by 70 degrees, grip strength improved by 66%. Degenerative arthritis occurred in the lone patient with a type II lunate.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: SNAC I changes do not represent a contra-indication to a distal pole excision.
Answer 2: SNAC II changes do not represent a contra-indication to a distal pole excision.
Answer 3: SNAC III changes do not represent a contra-indication to a distal pole excision.
Answer 4: STT arthritis represents a completely different pathology and is not an indication for a distal pole excision if the conditions existed concurrently.
