## Question

A 28-year-old female recreational runner presents with chronic right lateral ankle pain. She reports several ankle sprains over the past several years. She localizes her pain to her posterior fibula with increasing pain while walking on uneven surfaces. Physical exam reveals tenderness to palpation along the posterior aspect of the lateral malleolus, and pain is reproduced with resisted ankle eversion. No subluxation of the peroneal tendons is appreciated and she has a negative anterior drawer; her hindfoot is supple. Her current radiograph is depicted in Figure A. What most likely contributes to the development of this patient's symptoms?

![[q215797-a-lateral-foot-radiograph.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Hindfoot alignment
2. 5th metatarsal base fracture
3. Posterior tibial tendon insufficiency
4. Talocalcaneal coalition
5. Osteochondral lesion of the talus

## Correct option

**1. Hindfoot alignment**

## Answer explanation

The patient is presenting with a chronic peroneal tendon tear, which is associated with cavovarus hindfoot alignment.

Peroneal tendon tears are typically longitudinal tears of the tendon. Injuries often occur in the setting of lateral ankle instability and cavovarus hindfoot alignment. Nonoperative treatment consists of immobilization and physical therapy. Surgical treatment consists of tendon repair/tubularization for tears <50% of the tendon and tenodesis/tendon transfer for tears >50% of the tendon. Concomitant pathology, such as hindfoot alignment and instability, should be addressed at the time of the repair.

*Squires et al. reviewed the surgical repair of peroneal tendon tears.* *They discussed mechanical ankle instability (chronic ATFL tears), cavovarus hindfoot alignment, and hypertrophied peroneal tubercle.* **The authors recommended** addressing associated pathology that contributes to the development of peroneal tendon tears at the time of peroneal tendon reconstruction or repair.

*Heckman et al. reviewed the treatment of peroneal tendon tears.* **They reported** tenosynovitis is successfully treated with immobilization and activity modification in most cases. **They stated** surgical treatment is indicated for peroneal tendon subluxation and symptomatic peroneal tendon tears.

Figure A is the lateral radiograph of a right foot with cavovarus alignment.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: A 5th metatarsal base fracture can occur at a higher frequency in the setting of a cavovarus hindfoot. However, this does not predispose patients to peroneal tendon tears.
Answer 3: Posterior tibial tendon insufficiency can lead to flat foot deformity, which has not been associated with peroneal tendon tears.
Answer 4: A talocalcaneal coalition is typically signified with the "C-sign" on lateral foot radiographs, which is not seen in Figure A. Additionally, there is a supple hindfoot on the physical exam, which is atypical of tarsal coalitions.
Answer 5: An osteochondral lesion of the talus in itself does not predispose patients to peroneal tendon tears.
