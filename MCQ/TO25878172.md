Chapter: Arthroplasty MCQs — Unicondylar and Patellofemoral Arthroplasty

## Question

When comparing mobile-bearing (Oxford) UKA to fixed-bearing UKA, which of the following is an inherent disadvantage specific to the mobile-bearing design?

## Options

1. Higher risk of bearing dislocation.
2. Strict requirement for PCL sacrifice.
3. Limited femoral component sizing.
4. Increased rate of polyethylene wear.

## Correct option

**1. Higher risk of bearing dislocation.**

## Answer explanation

Mobile-bearing systems allow the polyethylene to move freely on the tibial tray to maximize congruency. However, this introduces the risk of bearing spin-out or dislocation, particularly if the gap tension is unbalanced or the components are malaligned.

Fixed vs. Mobile Bearing UKR

The biomechanical philosophies between fixed and mobile bearings dictate their specific failure modes and surgical techniques.

1. Mobile Bearing (e.g., Oxford)

 - Biomechanics: Uses a fully congruent, unconstrained polyethylene bearing that glides freely between the femoral and tibial components.

 - Advantage: The full congruency distributes load evenly, drastically reducing contact stress and polyethylene wear. It decouples flexion/extension from rotation.

 - Surgical Demand: The implant relies entirely on the patient's soft tissue envelope (specifically the MCL) for stability. Gap balancing is paramount. The flexion and extension gaps must be exactly equal to prevent the bearing from dislocating (spinning out).

2. Fixed Bearing (e.g., Miller-Galante, Zuk)

 - Biomechanics: The polyethylene is locked into the tibial tray. Because the knee rotates during flexion (screw-home mechanism), the tibial surface must be relatively flat to allow the femoral component to move without excessive constraint.

 - Advantage: It is more forgiving of slight ligamentous laxity and minor gap mismatches. Bearing dislocation is impossible.

 - Disadvantage: The flat-on-round articulation creates high point-contact stresses, theoretically increasing the risk of polyethylene wear over time compared to mobile bearings.

Complications Specific to UKR

While general risks like infection and DVT apply to all arthroplasties, UKR has unique failure modes that students must identify.

Mobile-Bearing Specific Complications

 - Bearing Dislocation (Spin-out): The most classic complication. It almost always occurs posteriorly during deep flexion. The primary cause is a loose flexion gap or unrecognized MCL insufficiency. If the gap is too large, the bearing simply slides out.

 - Impingement: If osteophytes are not adequately cleared (especially anteriorly in the notch), they can impinge on the bearing and push it out of the joint.

Shared Complications (Fixed and Mobile)

 - Disease Progression: The most common cause of late failure (> 5 years) is the development of osteoarthritis in the lateral compartment or severe progression in the patellofemoral joint.

 - Aseptic Loosening: More common on the tibial side. In fixed bearings, this can be secondary to polyethylene wear debris. In mobile bearings, it is often due to poor bone preparation or thermal necrosis.

 - Medial Tibial Plateau Fracture:
 Usually occurs early post-op. It is primarily a technical error caused by making the vertical sagittal saw cut too deep (past the horizontal cut) into the posterior cortex, creating a massive stress riser.

Answer: 1
