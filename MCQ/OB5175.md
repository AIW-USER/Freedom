## Question

A 4-year-old boy presents to your clinic for evaluation of 'clumsiness.' His parents state that he began walking at 30 months of age. They note that he is not able to keep up with his peers in athletic activities. He is unable to climb steps without the aid of a handrail. On exam, he stands with increased lumbar lordosis and a wide based stance. When asked to rise from a seated position on the floor, the child first rolls into a crawling position on all four limbs, stands up by extending his knees and uses his upper extremities bracing against his thighs to help elevate his trunk. You confirm the diagnosis with serum enzyme assay followed by DNA analysis. The parents ask you what the chances are of their child passing on this genetic disease to his male offspring.

## Options

1. 0%
2. 25%
3. 50%
4. 75%
5. 100%

## Correct option

**1. 0%**

## Answer explanation

This patient has Duchenne's Muscular Dystrophy (DMD). This is an X-linked recessive disorder. As such, only females carriers can pass on the disease to their offspring. Males can only pass on the trait.

DMD is characterized by progressive muscle weakness starting with proximal muscles. Patients often present to orthopaedic surgeons for clumsiness, toe-walking, late walking or poor physical abilities. Diagnosis can be confirmed with serum creatine kinase (CK) levels, which will be elevated. Two thirds of cases can be absolutely confirmed with DNA testing. Muscle biopsy may be necessary to confirm the remainder of cases.

Sussman reviews DMD and its orthopaedic implications by stage (1-5). He states that because the diagnosis is so devastating, absolute certainty in confirmation is necessary. He reviews the etiology, diagnosis, medical management and orthopaedic management of patients with DMD. He states that spinal deformity is the most critical orthopaedic issue for patients with DMD. He recommends starting the discussion of fusion with parents early, when the child is 5 to 10 years old, in order to prepare them for the necessity of surgery later, which will greatly improve their quality of life and survival.

Incorrect answers:
Answers 2-5: Men cannot pass on DMD to their sons because they contribute a Y chromosome, and DMD is X-linked.
