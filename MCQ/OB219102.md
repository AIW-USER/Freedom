## Question

A 55-year-old male underwent a lumbar discectomy four weeks ago. His surgery was remarkable for a dural tear that was repaired. He presents to the clinic for a one-month postoperative evaluation endorsing the recurrence of his leg pain and back pain. Physical exam shows moderate erythema surrounding the incision with expressible serosanguinous drainage and a one-centimeter area of wound dehiscence. An MRI with and without gadolinium is performed and shown in Figures A and B. What is the most appropriate next step in management?

## Options

1. Continue routine postoperative care
2. Placement of a lumbar drain with a period of bedrest
3. Hospital admission, IV antibiotics, and serial ESR and CRP
4. CT guided aspiration
5. Surgical irrigation and debridement with intra-operative cultures followed by antibiotics

## Correct option

**5. Surgical irrigation and debridement with intra-operative cultures followed by antibiotics**

## Answer explanation

The clinical presentation and imaging studies are consistent with a postoperative surgical infection. Surgical irrigation and debridement is the most appropriate treatment followed by a course of antibiotics (Answer 5).

Postoperative infections are one of the most common complications of lumbar spine surgery. Reports show a 0.7% infection rate with lumbar discectomy despite prophylactic antibiotics. It has been documented that the use of a microscope doubles this infection rate to 1.4%. The risk of infection is even higher with spinal fusion because of the presence of spinal instrumentation, with some studies showing an infection rate of up to 10%.

*Sasso et al. review the diagnosis and management of postoperative spinal wound infections.* **They report** that Staphylococcus aureus is the most common organism causing postoperative spinal infections, which account for greater than 50% of cases. Other organisms found in these infections include Staphylococcus epidermidis, Peptococcus, Enterobacter cloacae, and Bacteroides. **They report** preoperative antibiotic prophylaxis decreases the infection rate for all scenarios.

*Cousins et al. review MRI imaging of the spine.* **They report** that gadolinium-containing contrast enhancement is used to distinguish scar tissue, which enhances on MRI scans, from fragments of disk tissue in the epidural space that, lacking capillaries, do not enhance. In patients with a spinal infection, contrast medium administration enhances affected vertebral bodies and abscesses.

Figures A and B show T1 sagittal and T2 axial cuts of the lumbar spine with an L5/S1 epidural abscess, superficial fluid collection, and enhancement of the L5/S1 vertebral bodies.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The clinical presentation is consistent with a deep infection, which requires surgical debridement.
Answer 2: The placement of a lumbar drain is indicated by a persistent CSF leak that has failed multiple repair attempts.
Answer 3: IV antibiotics alone are not the appropriate treatment of a lumbar surgical infection with evidence of epidural abscess, nor would this address the wound dehiscence.
Answer 4: A CT-guided aspiration is not indicated as there are clear signs of postoperative infection with epidural abscess on MRI imaging, and this would not address the superficial aspect of the infection and wound dehiscence
