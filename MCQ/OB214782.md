## Question

A 69-year-old woman sustains the injury seen in Figure A after slipping in the shower and falling on her buttocks yesterday. She presented due to continued pain in her back. On examination, she states her pain is severe but better after receiving medication in the ER. She is an avid tennis player and denies any fractures in the past. What is the mechanism of action for the indicated medication for this patient?

*Figure A*

## Options

1. Inhibition of calcitonin receptors
2. Activation of RANK receptors
3. Inhibition of bone estrogen receptors
4. Continuous activation of parathyroid hormone receptors
5. Inhibition of farnesyl pyrophosphate synthase

## Correct option

**5. Inhibition of farnesyl pyrophosphate synthase**

## Answer explanation

Farnesyl pyrophosphate synthase inhibition is the mechanism of action of nitrogen-containing bisphosphonate medications, which are indicated in patients with hip or vertebral fragility fractures.

Osteoporosis and osteopenia are disorders on a continuum that result from decreased bone mass and are quantitative disorders. Vertebral compression fractures fall under the category of fragility fractures, which also include hip fractures and distal radius fractures, that can occur in patients with these disorders. Objective diagnostic criteria can be performed with a DEXA scan, which measures a baseline score for bone density called the T-score. A T-score of -1 to -2.5 is classified as osteopenia, while a T-score < -2.5 is classified as osteoporosis. A prior fragility fracture has been found to be a strong risk factor for a second fracture due to factors other than bone density such as worsening vision or balance, confusion, or other predispositions to falls. The National Osteoporosis Foundation published guidelines that recommend that, regardless of whether a DEXA scan has been performed, anti-osteoporotic medication should be initiated following a fragility vertebral or hip fracture. Examples of farnesyl pyrophosphate synthase inhibitors are alendronate, pamidronate, and zoledronic acid.

*Cosman et al. published the Clinician’s Guide to Prevention and Treatment of Osteoporosis.* **They recommend** initiating pharmacologic therapy in 3 groups: 1) patients with hip or vertebral fractures, regardless of T-score; 2) patients with T-scores ≤ −2.5 at the femoral neck, total hip, or lumbar spine; and 3) postmenopausal women and men aged > 50 years with T-score between −1.0 and −2.5 at the femoral neck, total hip, or lumbar spine AND a 10-year hip fracture probability ≥ 3% or a 10-year major osteoporosis-related fracture probability ≥ 20% based on FRAX.

*Kim et al. performed a retrospective study to determine whether the timing of the post-operative administration of bisphosphonate affects fracture healing and the rate of complication following an intertrochanteric fracture.* *Patients were randomized into 3 groups, with Group A beginning bisphosphate therapy 1 week after surgery, Group B beginning bisphosphate therapy 1 month after surgery, and Group C beginning bisphosphate therapy 3 months after surgery.* **They found** that functional outcomes one year after surgery and the incidence of complications were similar in the three groups. **They concluded** that the timing of the post-operative administration of bisphosphonates does not appear to affect the incidence of complications or rate of healing of an intertrochanteric fracture.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1: Calcitonin can be used to increase bone mass density, therefore inhibition of the receptor would result in bone resorption.
Answer 2: Activation of RANK results in osteoclast activation and bone resorption.
Answer 3: Estrogen replacement therapy can be used to treat osteoporosis in select patients, not estrogen receptor inhibition.
Answer 4: Continuous activation of parathyroid hormone receptors results in bone resorption, while intermittent activation results in increased bone mass density.
