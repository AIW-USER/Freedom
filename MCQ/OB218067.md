## Question

A 17-year-old high school baseball pitcher presents to your clinic with elbow pain in his dominant elbow. Radiographs are obtained and physical examination is performed. After a long discussion with the athlete and his family, you diagnose him with valgus extension overload syndrome and recommend a rest period then strengthening program before returning to pitching. What examination findings are you most likely to see in this patient?

## Options

1. Positive lateral pivot shift test with pain rising from chair
2. Posteromedial elbow pain, crepitus, and flexion contracture
3. Anterior elbow pain with a milking maneuver
4. Tenderness distal to medial epicondyle, worsened with resisted pronation
5. Lateral elbow tenderness with catching/locking sensation

## Correct option

**2. Posteromedial elbow pain, crepitus, and flexion contracture**

## Answer explanation

Patients with valgus extension overload (VEO) usually complain of tenderness over the posteromedial olecranon, crepitus with motion, and a flexion contracture that can be painful if forcefully extended.

VEO, sometimes called "Pitcher's elbow," occurs mostly in the dominant arm of overhead throwers and causes posteromedial elbow pain. Repetitive valgus stresses in the elbow cause impaction of the posteromedial olecranon into the olecranon fossa, which can lead to osteochondral lesions, osteophyte formation, and loose bodies. As a result of this pathologic process, athletes can experience posteromedial elbow pain, crepitus, and loss of terminal extension. First-line management is rest and activity modification, while elbow arthroscopy with debridement may ultimately be required.

*Ahmad et al. looked primarily at elbow arthroscopy as the treatment of VEO.* *They reviewed the collection of injuries that are seen from VEO including posteromedial osteophyte formation, chondral injury, olecranon stress fracture, and loose body formation.* **They note** patients have symptoms during the deceleration phase of throwing which can be reproduced by forced extension on exam. They finish by going into more detail about the techniques for arthroscopic elbow debridement in order to safely remove osteophytes without destabilizing the medial elbow.

*Dugas reviewed the diagnosis and treatment of VEO,* **noting** that it is characterized by posteromedial elbow pain that is caused by repeatedly forcing the elbow into full extension with concomitant valgus stress. **He notes** that the initial treatment is rest and a gradual return to a throwing program with arthroscopic osteophyte resection being used if nonoperative management fails.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: A positive lateral pivot shift test and pain pushing up from a chair would be indicative of posterolateral rotatory instability and incompetence of the lateral ulnar collateral ligament (LUCL).
Answer 3: Pain with milking maneuver would indicate injury to the ulnar collateral ligament (UCL) but should cause pain medially, not anteriorly.
Answer 4: Tenderness 5-10 mm distal to the medial epicondyle, worsened with resisted pronation would indicate medial epicondylitis.
Answer 5: Lateral elbow pain and swelling with sensations of catching and locking could indicate a capitellar osteochondral lesion.
