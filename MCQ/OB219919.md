## Question

A 60-year-old presents with anterior shoulder pain exacerbated by calisthenic workouts. He has tried 6 months of physical therapy and NSAIDs without improvement in his symptoms. An MRI demonstrates isolated longitudinal tearing of the long head of the biceps tendon with surrounding edema. When discussing biceps tenodesis versus tenotomy, what is the primary difference in outcomes?

## Options

1. American Shoulder and Elbow Surgeon (ASES) scores
2. Cosmetic deformity
3. Range of motion
4. Strength
5. Visual analogue scale (VAS) scores

## Correct option

**2. Cosmetic deformity**

## Answer explanation

Biceps tenotomy has a higher risk of residual "Popeye" deformity compared to tenodesis (Answer 2). Biceps tenodesis and tenotomy have similar functional outcomes and pain scores.

Biceps tenodesis and tenotomy are common procedures for treating proximal biceps tendonitis after failing nonoperative treatment options. Tenodesis has the benefit of reattaching the tendon to the humerus, which gives a more natural contour to the muscle postoperatively. Performing a tenodesis comes with additional risks, including nerve injury from bicortical drilling through the humerus or retraction of the musculocutaneous nerve. The benefit of tenotomy is that it is a faster, more straightforward procedure, requires less rehabilitation, and has a faster return to activity. However, patients who undergo tenotomy have a significantly higher risk of a "Popeye" deformity. The two procedures otherwise have similar outcomes, such as functional scores, pain, strength, and range of motion.

*Belk and colleagues performed a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials comparing biceps tenodesis and tenotomy.* *At a mean follow-up of 23 months, there was no difference in Constant-Murley scores, visual analog scale, or American Shoulder and Elbow Surgeons scores.* Only 6.8% of patients who underwent tenodesis had a cosmetic deformity compared to 23.3% in the tenotomy group. **They concluded** that patients who undergo both procedures have similar functional outcomes, but cosmetic deformity is more common with tenotomy.

*Friedman and colleagues performed a cohort study comparing the outcomes of biceps tenotomy versus tenodesis in active patients younger than 55 years old.* *Patients who underwent a tenotomy had a 35% risk of a "Popeye" deformity compared to 18% in the tenodesis group.* Strength and subjective functional outcome scores were similar between groups. **The authors concluded** surgeon and patient preference can continue dictating the decision between biceps tenodesis versus tenotomy.

Incorrect answers:
1, 3, 4, and 5: In multiple studies, biceps tenodesis and tenotomy have demonstrated similar strength, range of motion, pain scores, and subjective functional outcome scores
