## Question

A 42-year-old carpenter falls off a ladder at work and sustains a large coronoid fracture involving 40% of the coronoid height. Which of the following nerves provides innervation to the muscle involved in the approach and fixation of an isolated coronoid fracture?

## Options

1. Median nerve
2. Anterior interosseous nerve
3. Radial nerve
4. Posterior interosseous nerve
5. Ulnar nerve

## Correct option

**5. Ulnar nerve**

## Answer explanation

Fixation of an isolated large coronoid fracture warrants a direct approach and buttress plate fixation, which is best accomplished through a medial flexor carpi ulnaris (FCU) splitting approach. The FCU is innervated by the ulnar nerve.

Surgical approaches to coronoid fractures depend on the condition of the radial head and the complexity of injury (i.e. terrible triad elbow fracture-dislocation). When an associated radial head fracture is present, a lateral or posterior approach to the coronoid fracture is often performed as the radial head is addressed in the same setting. An isolated coronoid fracture is typically approached from the medial side using the FCU splitting approach.

*Steinmann et al. reviewed coronoid process fractures.* **They reported** that these fractures can be either an isolated finding following elbow dislocation or part of a more complex fracture-dislocation and that surgical approaches to coronoid fractures depend on the condition of the radial head. **They highlighted** that intraoperative stress testing may be helpful in assessing the need for surgery and choosing the surgical approach.

*Doornberg et al. investigated fractures of the anteromedial facet of the coronoid process.* **They reported** that these fractures are associated with either subluxation or complete dislocation of the elbow in most patients. **They concluded** that secure fixation of the coronoid fracture usually restores good elbow function.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: It is the ulnar (not median) nerve that innervates the FCU.
Answer 2 and 4: The AIN and PIN do not innervate the muscles/interval used during the approach to an isolated coronoid fracture.
Answer 3: If a displaced radial head fracture was present, a posterior (triceps splitting versus reflecting) approach may be appropriate to address both medial and lateral injuries.
