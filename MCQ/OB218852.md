## Question

A 24-year-old professional football player experiences acute onset knee pain and swelling while blocking. He is unable to actively perform a straight leg raise. Figure A is a lateral radiograph of the affected knee. What is the best treatment for this patient with respect to gap formation?

![[q218852-a-patellar-tendon-rupture.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Primary end-to-end repair
2. Rehabilitation until strength and range-of-motion are recovered followed by reconstruction with autograft
3. Achilles allograft reconstruction
4. Suture anchor repair
5. Transosseous suture repair

## Correct option

**4. Suture anchor repair**

## Answer explanation

Biomechanical studies have repeatedly demonstrated greater ultimate load to failure and lower gap formation with suture anchor techniques than transosseous tunnel repair.

Patellar tendon ruptures are often the result of an eccentric contraction of the quadriceps in the setting of a diseased tendon. Underlying risk factors include anabolic steroid use, inflammatory arthritis, chronic steroid use, diabetes mellitus, and long-standing tendinosis. Complete tears that compromise the extensor mechanism require surgical repair to enable the function of the knee and normal gait. The most common repair technique involves refixing the tendon with sutures in transosseous tunnels, sometimes combined with augmentation. However, data about the most appropriate suture technique and suture material are lacking. In recent years, suture anchors have emerged as a potential alternative to transosseous sutures. Suture anchors consist of an eyelet that is drilled into the bone and a screw that is inserted into the eyelet to secure the suture. A controlled laboratory study comparing suture anchors to transosseous suture repair found that suture anchors provided significantly better biomechanical results.

*Lanzi et al. performed a controlled laboratory study comparing the transosseous and screw-in anchor repair techniques for patellar tendon injuries in terms of gap formation and load-to-failure strength.* **They found** that the anchor construct was superior to the transosseous technique for minimizing gap formation and improving load-to-failure strength. **The authors suggested** that the 4.75-mm PEEK screw-in anchor construct may be an effective method for patellar tendon repair, which could potentially support early load-bearing rehabilitation.

*Ettinger et al. performed a controlled laboratory study comparing the efficacy of suture anchors versus transosseous suture repair for patellar tendon ruptures.* **They reported** that suture anchors provided significantly better results than transosseous sutures in terms of gap formation during cyclic loading and ultimate failure load. **They concluded** that suture anchors may be a more effective method for patellar tendon repair and could be relevant for future clinical treatment of these injuries.

Incorrect answers
Answer 1: Primary end-to-end repair is typically not feasible in patellar tendon rupture due to that rupture occurring at the inferior pole of the patella. There is often not sufficient proximal tendon to perform a primary end-to-end repair
Answer 2: Rehabilitation to regain strength and range-of-motion prior to surgical reconstruction is not advantageous for patellar tendon ruptures. Early tendon repair, followed by rehab after 4-6 weeks is optimal.
Answer 3: Achilles tendon allograft reconstruction is performed for revision repairs or chronic patellar tendon ruptures.
Answer 5: Transosseous suture repair is commonly performed for acute patellar tendon ruptures, but biomechanical data has demonstrated inferiority compared to suture anchor fixation with respect to ultimate load to failure and gap formation.
