## Question

In the treatment of carpal tunnel syndrome, which of the following has the American Academy of Orthopaedic Surgeons provided a Strong recommendation?

## Options

1. Endoscopic carpal tunnel release confers short-term benefits as compared to open carpal tunnel release
2. Night splints do not improve patient-reported outcomes
3. Non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs do not improve patient symptoms
4. Oral steroid therapy improve patient-reported outcomes
5. Surgical treatment has a greater treatment benefit as compared to nonoperative management

## Correct option

**5. Surgical treatment has a greater treatment benefit as compared to nonoperative management**

## Answer explanation

The only selection amongst the options listed that the American Academy of Orthopaedic Surgeons (AAOS) has given a Strong recommendation is that surgical treatment has a greater treatment benefit as compared to nonoperative management.

Carpal tunnel syndrome (CTS) is the most common compressive neuropathy. The treatment of CTS includes nonoperative management: NSAIDs, night splints, activity modification, and corticosteroid injections. The surgical treatment of CTS is carpal tunnel release (either open or endoscopic).

*Graham et al. provided a number of AAOS clinical practice guidelines (CPGs) for the management of carpal tunnel syndrome, including the following Strong recommendations:*
1. Immobilization (brace/splint/orthosis) improves patient-reported outcomes
2. Steroid injections improve patient-reported outcomes
3. Magnet therapy should not be included in the treatment of CTS.
4. Releasing the transverse carpal ligament improves symptoms and function.
5. Surgical treatment of CTS confers a greater treatment benefit at 6 and 12 months as compared to nonoperative management.
6. There is no benefit to postoperative immobilization after carpal tunnel release.

*Jarvik et al. performed a randomized controlled trial (RCT) comparing surgery to nonoperative management (NSAIDs, occupational therapy, night splints, and therapeutic ultrasound) for CTS.* **They found** that at 12 months out from intervention, both surgery and nonoperative management improved, but that surgery had a greater improvement in function and symptoms compared to nonoperative management. **They concluded** that for patients with non-severe CTS, surgery is superior to nonoperative management.

*Gerritsen et al. performed a RCT comparing splinting to surgery in the treatment of CTS.* Eighteen months after intervention the surgical group had a 90% success rate and the splint group had a 75% success rate. **They concluded** that surgical treatment for non-severe CTS is superior to night splints.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: This guideline was given a Limited recommendation.
Answer 2: This answer is incorrect because it states that night splints do not improve outcomes. Night splints have a strong recommendation as they have been shown to improve patient-reported outcomes.
Answer 3: This guideline was given a Moderate recommendation.
Answer 4: This guideline was given a Moderate recommendation.
