## Question

A healthy, 10-month-old girl has left sided development dysplasia of the hip (DDH). She underwent closed reduction under general anesthesia. Intraoperative arthrography is shown in Figure A. The surgeon notes there is less than 5mm medial dye pooling and feels the hip is stable with the leg positioned in 90 degrees flexion, 10 degrees internal rotation and 50 degrees abduction. What is the next best step?

![[q5798-a-isabellearthrogram.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Adductor tenotomy
2. Hip spica cast application and repeat radiographs prior to discharge
3. Hip spica cast application and post-reduction CT/MRI scan
4. Repeat closed reduction maneuver
5. Open reduction of hip

## Correct option

**3. Hip spica cast application and post-reduction CT/MRI scan**

## Answer explanation

The reduction of the left hip in the cast must be confirmed with either CT or MRI. Advanced imaging is the best way to confirm hip positioning after reduction.

In children aged 6 to 18 months, the preferred treatment of a chronically dislocated/subluxed hip is closed reduction under general anesthesia. The aim of treatment is to (1) obtain concentric reduction, (2) determine the stability of reduction. Blocks to concentric hip reduction include interposed labrum, capsule, ligamentous teres, pulvinar, psoas tendon, neolimbus, and/or transverse ligament. Stability is measured by the adduction-abduction angle in which the hip remains reduced and stable - known as 'the safe-zone'. This is considered to be at least 25 degrees arc of motion where the hip remains reduced, and the hip is positioned in less than 60 degrees of abduction.

*Cooper et al. describe a novel method for assessing postoperative femoral head reduction in developmental dysplasia of the hip.* Using a post reduction CT scan, they used axial imaging to describe the acetabular morphology, as seen in Illustration A. On the left there is the ‘C’ shaped morphology, indicating the normal concavity in a hip without dysplasia. On the right is the ‘S’ shaped morphology, indicating a transition from the concavity of the acetabulum to the posterior wall convexity reproducibly seen in an abnormal dysplastic hip.

*Madhu et al. re-evaluated the common concept that increasing the abduction angle in a hip spica will affect the vascularity of the femoral head in children with DDH.* The age at reduction and casting was 6 months. **They showed** radiographic evidence of AVN was 19% in children immobilized in 60 degrees of abduction vs. 16% in children immobilized in 45 degrees of abduction. **Their results showed** a nonsignificant reduction in the incidence of AVN when the hip abduction angle was reduced.

Figure A shows an intraoperative hip arthography radiograph. Illustration A shows an axial CT scan of a child with DDH as described above.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: If adductor tightness is contributing to hip instability and/or an arc of stability with greater than 60 degrees of abduction is required to keep the hips reduced, adductor tenotomy would be indicated.
Answer 2: The reduction of the left hip in the cast must be confirmed with either CT or MRI.
Answer 4: The hips are reduced within the acetabulum in this patient. Therefore another repeat closed reduction maneuver would not be required.
Answer 5: Indication for open reduction of the hip includes failed closed reduction, persistent subluxation, reducible but unstable hip, or an older children >18-24 months old with a chronic hip dislocation.
