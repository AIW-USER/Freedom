## Question

## Options

1. Increased risk of implant failure when compared to open reduction internal fixation (ORIF)
2. Earlier return to full weight bearing when compared to ORIF
3. Decreased risk of arthrofibrosis when compared to primary total knee arthroplasty (TKA) for osteoarthritis
4. Decreased risk of complications when compared to primary TKA for osteoarthritis
5. Higher complication rate compared to TKA performed following ORIF of tibial plateau fractures

## Correct option

**2. Earlier return to full weight bearing when compared to ORIF**

## Answer explanation

Primary TKA for the treatment of periarticular fractures about the knee in patients greater than 70 years old has the main advantage of earlier time to weight bearing, which is related to decreased patient morbidity.

Periarticular fractures about the knee in elderly patients can be difficult to manage. ORIF often requires long periods of immobilization and non-weightbearing, which can be difficult for elderly patients and associated with increased morbidity. Secondly, patients may ultimately require salvage TKA given the high rate of implant failure following ORIF in this age group. Primary TKA provides patients with a stable implant and the ability to bear weight immediately following surgery. Additionally, outcomes following primary TKA for periarticular fractures are better compared to TKA performed as a salvage operation after failed ORIF.

*Malviya et al. review acute primary TKA for periarticular fractures in patients greater than 65 years old.* **They report** a mean Knee Society Score (KSS) of 90.2 and a mean Short Form 36 (SF-36) score of 37.3, both indicating good functional outcomes. **They conclude** that, similar to arthroplasty for hip fractures, TKA should be considered for fractures about the knee in elderly patients.

*Ali et al. review failure of fixation of tibial plateau fractures.* **They report** failure of fixation in 79% of patients older than 60 years old, compared to only 7% in the younger cohort. **They conclude** that treatment of tibial plateau fractures in elderly patients should be limited to restoring stability and alignment.

Figure A demonstrates an insufficiency type fracture of the medial tibial plateau. Figure B are the postoperative radiographs of a patient who underwent TKA using revision components.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: High rates of implant failure are seen following ORIF of the tibial plateau in elderly patients.
Answer 3 and 4: Increased complications, including arthrofibrosis, are seen when performing TKA for fracture rather than osteoarthritis.
Answer 5: Complication rates of TKA are increased following failed ORIF compared to primary TKA.
