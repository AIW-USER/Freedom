## Question

What secondary malignancy is most likely to arise in the lesion shown in Figures 3a and 3b?

*Figure A*

![[q6372-b-03b.jpg]]
*Figure B*

## Options

1. Osteogenic sarcoma
2. Chondrosarcoma
3. Angiosarcoma
4. Squamous cell carcinoma
5. Adamantinoma

## Correct option

**2. Chondrosarcoma**

## Answer explanation

The radiographic and CT scan findings reveal a benign osteochondroma. The CT scan depicts medullary continuity between the ilium and the tumor, pathognomonic for this condition. MRI is useful to confirm the size of the cartilaginous cap, which when less than 1.5 cm, is also consistent with a benign osteochondroma. A very small percentage (about 1%) of solitary osteochondromas can undergo malignant transformation to chondrosarcoma. Tumors in an axial location (such as this pelvic osteochondroma) and those associated with polyostotic disease (multiple hereditary exostoses) are more likely to undergo malignant change.
