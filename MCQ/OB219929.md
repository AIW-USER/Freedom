## Question

A patient presents for follow-up three months after primary total knee arthroplasty. They have been compliant with their post-operative instructions, attended physical therapy, and did not have any other complications. On physical exam today, their range of motion is from 10 to 85 degrees. Which of the following histories would most likely lead to this outcome?

## Options

1. Prior distal femur fracture treated with open reduction, internal fixation
2. Preoperative range of motion from 0-120 degrees
3. Utilization of a posterior stabilized knee prosthesis
4. Presence of an ipsilateral total hip arthroplasty
5. Not using tourniquet intraoperatively

## Correct option

**1. Prior distal femur fracture treated with open reduction, internal fixation**

## Answer explanation

The patient has a limited range of motion (arthrofibrosis) following total knee arthroplasty (TKA). A history of prior femoral fracture increases the risk of excessive scar tissue and limited ROM after TKA (Answer 1).

The etiology of arthrofibrosis is multifactorial, and numerous preoperative, intraoperative, and postoperative risk factors have been identified. Decreased preoperative knee range of motion, higher complexity surgery (i.e., amount of trauma, length of surgery), and history of surgery increase the risk of excessive scar tissue after TKA. Previous studies have shown the rate of arthrofibrosis doubles or even triples when patients have undergone previous surgeries for multiple ligamentous injuries or surgeries that require immobilization. Treatment options available after physical therapy are manipulation under anesthesia (if within 12 weeks of index surgery and exhibiting less than 90 degrees of motion), debridement, and revision TKA.

*Papadopoulos et al. reviewed their series of patients who underwent TKA after previous distal femoral fractures.* **The authors report** that 21 patients had significant deformity at the time of primary TKA and six ultimately required distal femoral osteotomy. Other procedures were commonly needed during arthroplasty and included: lateral retinacular release (22 knees), extensor mechanism realignment (eight knees), and collateral ligament reconstruction. Postoperatively four patients required manipulation under anesthesia. **The authors recommend** preserving the vascularity of the skin and subcutaneous tissues and restoring limb alignment to mitigate these risks.

*Rajgopal et al. reviewed 84 TKAs performed in patients with spontaneously ankylosed knees who presented with limited preoperative arc of motion.* **They report** significant intraoperative difficulties that required extensile exposure techniques and releases. Despite the limited final range of motion (average 75 degrees), patients achieved an average improvement of 60 degrees after surgery.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 2: A patient with limited ROM before TKA is at greater risk of arthrofibrosis. A preoperative ROM of 0-120 would be considered good to excellent.
Answer 3: Posterior stabilized designs have not been associated with this complication.
Answer 4: A history of ipsilateral THA is not associated with an increased risk of arthrofibrosis.
Answer 5: Tourniquet use in TKA is controversial with some studies showing decreased ROM and outcomes at one year. Lack of tourniquet, however, has not been associated with this outcome.
