## Question

A 7-month-old female with a left developmental hip dislocation underwent closed reduction and spica casting. Which of the following is the safest and most accurate imaging modalities to evaluate the reduction after casting?

## Options

1. Ultrasound
2. AP and lateral radiographs of the hip
3. Arthrogram
4. Computed tomography
5. Magnetic resonance imaging

## Correct option

**5. Magnetic resonance imaging**

## Answer explanation

Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) is the safest and most accurate study to evaluate reduction after closed reduction and spica casting. There is no radiation exposure and a high detection rate of persistent dislocation.

Developmental dysplasia of the hip (DDH) is a spectrum of disease involvement includes dysplasia, subluxation, and dislocation. Closed reduction and spica casting are indicated in patients who fail treatment with a Pavlik harness or greater than 6 months of age. An arthrogram is used to confirm reduction, and a hip spica cast is applied. Post-reduction computed tomography (CT) has historically been the study of choice to confirm reduction following spica casting, but recent evidence suggests that MRI has the equivalent diagnostic capability without radiation exposure. The hip spica cast may obscure radiographic findings, and limits the ability for an ultrasound examination.

*Dibello et al. performed a retrospective study of 25 patients that underwent a closed reduction for Graf type IV DDH.* **The authors reported** that all patients underwent a post-reduction MRI within 24 hours with 24/25 patients having a concentric reduction. **The authors concluded** that MRI is a safe and effective imaging modality to ensure adequate femoral head position following closed reduction.

*Chin et al compared the use of CT versus magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) for evaluation of hip reduction in DDH in a retrospective review.* *They reviewed 32 CT scans and 33 MRI scans of 39 patients and found that CT demonstrated a sensitivity of 100% and a specificity of 96%, while MRI exhibited a sensitivity and specificity of 100%.* This affirms the use of MRI as an alternative for CT scan for evaluation of hip reduction in patients with DDH.

*Macdonald et al reviewed CT scans performed after infants with DDH underwent closed reduction and immobilization with spica casting.* *In 5 of 22 patients, postreduction CT demonstrated subluxation not seen in the earlier radiographs, and 10 of 22 patients returned to the operating room for a cast change.* **They concluded** that in infants, CT scans demonstrate information regarding the hip position that cannot always be adequately demonstrated on radiographs, which may alter the perioperative course of the patient.

Incorrect answers:
1. Ultrasound - the hip spica cast limits the ability to perform an ultrasound.
2. AP and lateral radiographs of the hip - the spica cast limits adequate visualization of the location of the femoral head in the acetabulum
3. Arthrogram - is used intra-operatively to confirm reduction after closed reduction and identify possible blocks to reduction, but faces same issues described above for radiographs once hip spica cast is applied.
4. Computed tomography - historically has a high detection rate of persistent hip dislocation, but there is added radiation exposure in pediatric patients, which is unfavorable and does not visualize the unossified epiphysis as well as MRI.
