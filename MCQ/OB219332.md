## Question

A 12-year-old female presents to your office with her parents due to concerns about "abnormal appearing" wrists. She is diagnosed with Madelung's deformity. Disruption of which of the following is most associated with this patient's underlying condition?

## Options

1. Radial dorsal physis of the distal radius
2. Ulnar dorsal physis of the distal radius
3. Radial volar physis of the distal radius
4. Radial volar physis of the distal ulna
5. Ulnar volar physis of the distal radius

## Correct option

**5. Ulnar volar physis of the distal radius**

## Answer explanation

Madelung's deformity is believed to be caused by disruption of the ulnar volar physis of the distal radius.

Madelung's Deformity is a congenital dyschondrosis of the distal radial physis that leads to partial deficiency of growth of the distal radial physis. Diagnosis is made radiographically with disruption of the ulnar volar physis of the distal radius, excessive radial inclination, excessive volar tilt, and ulnar carpal impaction. Treatment is observation in asymptomatic patients. Operative management is indicated for patients with wrist pain, decreased range of motion, and/or functional limitations.

*Kozin et al. reviewed Madelung's deformity of the wrist.* **They reported** that patients with Madelung's deformity are associated with a Vickers ligament that creates a tether across the volar-ulnar radial physis that restricts growth across this segment. **They concluded** that this results in the distal radius deformity in the coronal (increasing radial inclination) and the sagittal (increasing volar tilt) planes.

*Ghatan et al. reviewed Madelung's deformity.* **They reported** that this rare congenital anomaly of the wrist is caused by asymmetric growth at the distal radial physis secondary to a partial ulnar-sided arrest. **They concluded** that the deformity can occur in isolation or as part of a genetic syndrome.

*Otte et al. reviewed the surgical outcomes in a series of Madelung's wrists treated with a Vickers ligament release at a young age.* **They reported** that there was no loss of range of motion and demonstrated radiographic improvements in the radial physeal angle in 83% of patients. **They concluded** that in patients with early Madelung deformity, Vickers ligament release with radial physiolysis is a safe treatment option that theoretically has the potential to minimize the progression of the radiographic deformity.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1-4: Madelung's deformity is believed to be caused by disruption of the ulnar volar physis of the distal radius.
