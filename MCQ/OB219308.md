## Question

A 42-year-old male is involved in a motor vehicle collision. Imaging reveals a cervical superior facet fracture with instability. The patient demonstrates some numbness of the radial forearm and thumb. Injury of which of the following would most accurately explain this patient's symptoms?

## Options

1. C4 superior facet fracture with instability of the C3-4 joint
2. C5 superior facet fracture with instability of the C4-5 joint
3. C6 superior facet fracture with instability of the C5-6 joint
4. C7 superior facet fracture with instability of the C6-7 joint
5. T1 superior facet fracture with instability of the C7-T1 joint

## Correct option

**3. C6 superior facet fracture with instability of the C5-6 joint**

## Answer explanation

A superior facet fracture of the C6 vertebra with instability of the C5-6 joint would be expected to lead to numbness of the radial forearm and thumb given motion and irritation at the level of the exiting C6 nerve root.

Subaxial cervical vertebral fractures are a subset of cervical spine injuries that consist of compression fractures, burst fractures, flexion teardrop fractures, facet fractures, and extension teardrop avulsion fractures. In the cervical spine, nerve roots exit above their corresponding vertebral level. Thus, a C6 superior facet fracture causes instability of the C5-6 joint, so the C6 nerve root is irritated. Diagnosis is made with radiographs of the cervical spine. CT scan can be helpful for fracture characterization and surgical planning. Associated cervical radiculopathy is characterized by unilateral arm pain, numbness and tingling in a dermatomal distribution in the hand, and weakness in specific muscle groups. Treatment can be nonoperative or surgical stabilization depending on fracture pattern, mechanical stability, and the presence of neurological deficits.

*Caridi et al. reviewed cervical radiculopathy.* **They reported** that the clinical consequence of radiculopathy is arm pain or paresthesias in the dermatomal distribution of the affected nerve and may or may not be associated with neck pain and motor weakness. **They noted** that surgical indications include those with clinically significant motor deficits, debilitating pain that is resistant to conservative modalities and/or time, or instability in the setting of disabling radiculopathy. **They concluded** that understanding the pathophysiology, diagnosis, treatment indications, and treatment techniques is essential for rapid diagnosis and care of patients with cervical radiculopathy.

*McAnany et al. retrospectively reviewed cervical radiculopathy patterns in a surgical population to determine how often patients present with the standard textbook pattern of radiculopathy (i.e., Netter diagram) versus non-standard patterns.* **They reported** that observed patterns of cervical radiculopathy only followed the standard pattern in 54% of patients and did not differ by the cervical level involved. **They concluded** that cervical radiculopathy often presents with a non-standard pattern.
