## Question

An 8-year-old male patient is admitted to the orthopaedic service with a 2-day history of right hip pain. He has been unable to bear weight on his right leg and has a temperature of 38.7 degrees Celsius. Lab workup reveals a white blood cell count of 12,500/mm3, CRP of 22 mg/L, and ESR of 46 mm/hr. Ultrasound imaging of the bilateral hips reveals an effusion in the right hip, and image-guided aspiration yields cloudy fluid. The fluid is sent for Gram stain and culture, and culture is positive for the most common causative organism. Which of the following conditions is most often associated with this organism?

## Options

1. Tetanus
2. Impetigo
3. Urinary tract infection
4. Bacterial meningitis
5. Whooping cough

## Correct option

**2. Impetigo**

## Answer explanation

This patient presents with septic arthritis of the hip, which is most commonly caused by Staphylococcus aureus. S. aureus is also implicated in impetigo, a highly contagious skin infection (Answer 2).

Septic arthritis is a surgical emergency - patients may present with several characteristic signs, symptoms, and lab values that can be used to determine the likelihood of a septic hip. The original Kocher criteria include a fever >38.5 degrees Celsius, inability to bear weight, ESR >40 mm/hr, and WBC >12,000/mm3. The 2006 Caird modification added a CRP of >20 mg/L, which was demonstrated to be a strong independent risk factor for septic arthritis of the hip. Treatment almost always involves surgical irrigation and drainage to mitigate the chondrolytic effects of pus. S. aureus is the most common causative organism; less common ones include Kingella Kingae, Streptococcus pneumonia, Klebsiella species, Salmonella, Brucella melitensis, and Haemophilus influenzae.

*Erkilinc et al. published a 2021 JAAOS review on pediatric septic arthritis, where the authors reviewed the etiology, workup, diagnosis, and treatment.* Causative pathogens can vary depending on the child’s age, comorbidities or immunodeficiency, socioeconomic factors, and vaccination status. Overall, S. aureus is the primary causative organism; however, recent studies suggest that Kingella kingae may be the most common cause in children younger than 5. In adolescents, Neisseria gonorrhoeae must be considered. Additionally, although Haemophilus influenzae type B was historically the most common cause, immunization has eradicated it as an etiology of septic arthritis.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Tetanus is caused by Clostridium tetani, which has not been associated with pediatric septic arthritis.
Answer 3: Urinary tract infections are most commonly caused by E. coli.
Answer 4: Bacterial meningitis is most commonly caused by Group B Streptococcus in infants ages <2 months, Streptococcus pneumoniae in children ages 2 months to 10 years, and Neisseria meningitidis in children ages 11-17 years.
Answer 5: Whooping cough (pertussis) is caused by Bordetella pertussis.
