## Question

A 39-year-old male laborer has long-standing lateral elbow pain that is worse with gripping activities and has noted some decreased grip strength. On exam, the pain is exacerbated with resisted wrist extension and maximal wrist flexion. He fails conservative treatment and undergoes arthroscopic surgery. Postoperatively he is recovering well and his pain resolved but when he resumes exercising he complains of lateral elbow pain with clicking and catching and is unable to do pushups. Figure A is a view from the proximal anteromedial portal. Excessive debridement beyond which point may explain his symptoms?

## Options

## Correct option

**3. C**

## Answer explanation

This patient is experiencing posterolateral rotatory instability (PLRI) from iatrogenic LUCL injury due to excessive debridement posterior to the equator of the radial head.

Lateral epicondylitis is an overuse injury with tendinosis primarily involving the extensor carpi radialis brevis ECRB due to repetitive wrist extension and forearm pronation. Patients present with pain over the lateral epicondyle exacerbated by resisted wrist extension and gripping activities. Angiofibroblastic hyperplasia with disorganized collagen is the classic appearance on microscopic evaluation. Nonoperative treatment is successful in most cases and consists of activity modification, PT, counter-force bracing, and steroid injections. Operative treatment is indicated when patients fail a prolonged course of nonoperative treatment. Surgery consists of debridement of the ECRB origin which can be done open or arthroscopically. The dreaded complication of iatrogenic LUCL injury can be due to excessive debridement extending posterior to the equator of the radial head and may lead to PLRI.

*Kwak et al. evaluated 122 elbows (117 patients) with lateral epicondylitis who underwent surgery, with no instability on preoperative exam.* *Patients were then examined under anesthesia at the time of surgery to assess for subtle instability and then grouped as stable or unstable.* Predictors of instability were identified as patients with a history of multiple corticosteroid injections (≥3), severe pain, and MRI with signs of instability.

*Conti et al. provided a comprehensive review of chronic posterolateral rotatory instability (PLRI).* PLRI is the most common form of chronic elbow instability. PLRI presents a history of clicking and lateral-sided pain, and instability with a positive pivot-shift test, push-up, chair and tabletop test. MRI can also assist with diagnosis and surgical planning. Surgery can performed open or arthroscopic repair or reconstruction. **The authors advocate** for reconstruction with allograft but note that repairs can do well, especially with more acute injuries.

*Anakwenze et al. also provide a similar review of PLRI.* In addition, **they noted** a 25% rate of recurrent instability following reconstruction or repair.

Figure A is an arthroscopic image from the proximal anteromedial portal of showing the radiocapitellar joint and the common extensor tendon following debridement. A and E are anterior to the equator, B is superficial and would be the ECRL, C is posterior to the equator, and D is distal.

Illustration A is a drawing depicting an arthroscopic view of the lateral elbow showing the common extensor tendon.

Incorrect Answers
Answer 1,2,4,5: Iatrogenic LUCL injury during ECRB debridement can occur with excessive debridement extending posterior to the equator of the radial head and may lead to PLRI. Resection anteriorly (A and E) and superficial (B) would not lead to instability. Resection too distal (D) may place the PIN at risk.
