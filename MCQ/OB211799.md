## Question

A patient with a chronic posterior cruciate ligament and posterolateral corner injury is planned to undergo an opening medial wedge high tibial osteotomy. What is the goal of this procedure with respects to tibial slope and the relationship of the tibia relative to the femur, respectively?

## Options

1. Decreased and anterior
2. Decreased and posterior
3. Increased and anterior
4. Increased and posterior
5. Increased and no change

## Correct option

**3. Increased and anterior**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a chronic posterior cruciate ligament (PCL) and posterolateral corner (PLC) injury. A valgus-producing high tibial osteotomy (HTO) addresses these injuries by increasing tibial slope and shifting the tibia anterior relative to the femur.

Chronic PCL instability increases wear in the patellofemoral joint and the medial compartment of the knee. Patients with a chronic PCL and PLC injury often demonstrate a varus thrust and abnormal Dial test on examination. A valgus-producing HTO is often recommended in these patients. Use of a valgus-producing medial opening HTO addresses both varus malalignment and PCL deficiency. Compared with a lateral closing wedge osteotomy, use of a medial opening wedge osteotomy in a PCL deficient knee increases the tibial slope which reduces posterior tibial sag. This shifts the tibia more anterior relative to the femur preventing posterior tibial translation.

*Arthur et al. prospectively assessed the functional outcomes of patients with combined PLC instability and genu varus alignment initially treated with a proximal tibial opening wedge osteotomy.* **They found** that 38% of patients had sufficient improvement in knee function and that a subsequent PLC reconstruction was not necessary. **They concluded** that proximal tibial opening wedge osteotomy can be an effective first method of treatment for patients with chronic combined PLC knee injuries and genu varus alignment.

*Zawodny et al. reported on the unique complications related to PCL reconstruction.* **They state** that these complications are secondary to the relative infrequency of PCL injuries, the lack of knowledge and experience in treating them, the proximity of neurovascular structures to the PCL, and the technically demanding nature of reconstructive procedures. **They concluded** that proper technique will minimize postoperative complications.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1,2,4,5: A medial wedge opening HTO for chronic PCL and PLC injury would increase (not decrease) and shift the tibia anterior (not posterior) relative to the femur
