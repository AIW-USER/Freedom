## Correct option

## Answer explanation

The clinical presentation is consistent with cauda equina syndrome. The most common presenting symptom of CES is radicular leg pain. Bowell and bladder symptoms of CES symptoms include urinary retention, which if untreated, eventually leads to overflow urinary incontinence.

Cauda equina syndrome is a condition typically associated with a large, space-occupying lesion within the canal of the lumbosacral spine. The syndrome is characterized by varying patterns of low back pain, sciatica, lower extremity sensorimotor loss, and bowel and bladder dysfunction. Early diagnosis is often challenging because the initial signs and symptoms are subtle. Classically, the full-blown syndrome includes urinary retention, saddle anesthesia of the perineum, bilateral lower extremity pain, numbness, and weakness. Decreased rectal tone may be a relatively late finding.

*McCarthy et al. performed a retrospective review in patients with cauda equina syndrome and report that radiculopathy was the most common presenting symptom.* Presenting symptoms were, in decreasing frequency: radiculopathy (90%), back pain (83%), urinary retention (60%), urinary incontinence (55%). Objective findings were, in decreasing frequency: reduced perianal sensation (76%), sensory deficit in the leg (62%), absent ankle jerk (62%), leg weakness (55%), and reduced rectal tone (50%).

*Spector et al. in a review, report that patients begin to complain of prodromal symptoms of low back pain and/or unilateral leg pain which is usually progressive.* Bladder dysfunction, however, is a required element of the diagnosis. Early bladder dysfunction can be subtle but then evolves into retention and eventually overflow incontinence as the bladder continues to fill.
