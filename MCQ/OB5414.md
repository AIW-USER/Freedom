## Question

A 47-year-old woman with no past medical history undergoes a work-up for a 5cm thigh mass deep to the fascia. Needle-biopsy resulted in undifferentiated pleomorphic sarcoma. What is the most important factor in preventing recurrent local disease after resection?

## Options

1. Use of preoperative chemotherapy with wide resection
2. Use of preoperative and postoperative chemotherapy with wide resection
3. Radiation alone
4. Adequacy of surgical margin
5. Use of chemotherapy only

## Correct option

**4. Adequacy of surgical margin**

## Answer explanation

The risk of local recurrence in soft tissue sarcoma is most directly related to the adequacy of surgical margin. While radiation can be used to supplement surgery, it cannot take the place of adequate surgical margins.

*Noria et al. retrospectively reviewed 65 patients referred to their oncologic center for sarcomas with residual disease following resection.* *The authors did not find any correlation between the amount of residual disease and initial size, grade or treatment regimen.* **They recommended** that the most important factor to prevent recurrence was wide resection with clean margins.

*Venkatesan et al. reviewed 42 patients that presented with residual disease following unplanned excision of high-grade sarcomas without planned biopsy.* These patients had devastating results leading the authors to definitively conclude, "excision of deep or large soft tissue masses without tissue diagnosis is unacceptable."

Incorrect answers:
Answers 1,2,3,5: The gold standard remains wide resection with clean margins for undifferentiated (high-grade) pleomorphic sarcomas.
