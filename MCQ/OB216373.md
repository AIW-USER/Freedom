## Question

While performing open reduction and internal fixation of the fracture shown in Figure A, you decided to use fresh-frozen allograft bone to help maintain the articular surface. Pre-operatively you discussed with the patient the risk of possible pathogenic transmission with the use of this bone graft. Which of the following pathogens did you inform the patient has a theoretical one-in-a-1.67 million chance of transmission?

![[q216373-a-tibial-plateau-fracture.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Creutzfeldt-Jakob Disease (CJD)
2. Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV)
3. Hepatitis B (HBV)
4. Human T-cell Lymphotropic Virus (HTLV)
5. Hepatitis C (HCV)

## Correct option

**2. Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV)**

## Answer explanation

The transmission rate of HIV in fresh-frozen allograft bone transplantation is approximately 1:1,670,000, although this number may be lower due to modern nucleic-acid amplification testing.

Allogenic human tissue carries the risk of transmission of bacterial, viral, and prion diseases. Methods of procurement, processing, storage, and screening of patients all affect the probability of this occurring. Even with approximately 1 million bone allografts being implanted every year, the last known transmission of HIV via allograft bone was in 1985. The theoretical risk in freeze-dried allograft bone is 1:2.6 billion.

Vincent Ng provided a thorough discussion on disease transmission in allograft bone. He cites bacterial transmission may be as high as 12% in massive bone allografts and 0.7% in morselized bone. He demonstrates the probability of viral transmission is exceptionally low, as it would require multiple false-negative tests to occur prior to implantation.

*Zamborsky et al. reviewed infections in bone allograft transplantations.* *They go through the stepwise process from screening to implantation.* As part of their discussion they discuss viral and infection transmission with a comprehensive review of previously reported cases. **They conclude** that likely the majority of the previous cases are due to lacking appropriate screening and processing.

*Delloye et al. comprehensively reviewed bone allograft.* Within their article, they discuss the various types of allograft bone products, the procurement and processing of grafts, and methods for optimizing their use. They briefly review known cases of pathogen transmission with a discussion of current standards for screening, procurement, and processing.

Figure A demonstrates serial coronal CT cuts of a Schatzker II tibial plateau fracture with articular depression.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Transmission of CJD has never been identified in allogenic bone transplantation. It has been identified in corneal and dura mater transplantation.
Answer 3 & 5: Transmission rates of hepatitis B and C are expected to be higher than HIV due to the overall prevalence of these viral infections in the general population. The last known transmission of HCV was in 2000.
Answer 4: There is only one ever confirmed case of HTLV transmission, and was due to improper screening of a femoral head allograft. With appropriate screening, no cases have ever been identified.
