## Question

Transferring fascicles from the ulnar and median nerves to the biceps brachii and brachialis muscles can be used to treat deficits resulting from an injury to which of the following regions of the brachial plexus?

## Options

1. Posterior divisions
2. Posterior cord
3. Medial cord
4. Lateral cord
5. Inferior trunk

## Correct option

**4. Lateral cord**

## Answer explanation

The musculocutaneous nerve arises from the lateral cord. Transferring fascicles from the median and ulnar nerves to the musculocutaneous nerve can be used to restore elbow flexion after a nerve injury.

Nerve transfers are a valuable reconstructive technique that can partially or completely restore motor and sensory deficits. Muscle reinnervation depends on regenerating muscle axons reaching target muscles before irreversible loss off motor endplates, which typically occurs within 1 year after injury. Transfer of expendable fascicles of the ulnar and median nerves to branches of a nonfunctional musculocutaneous nerve can be used to restore elbow flexion.

*Tung et al. reviewed the indications, techniques, and outcomes of nerve transfers.* **The authors note** that donor nerves should be expendable and provide a synergistic function to the target muscle to facilitate postoperative rehabilitation and motor re-education. Expendable branches include fascicles of the ulnar nerve innervating the flexor carpi ulnaris (FCU) and fascicles of the median nerve innervating the flexor digitorum superficialis (FDS), palmaris longus (PL), and flexor carpi radialis (FCR). **The authors report** several possible donors for the restoration of elbow flexion, but that a double fascicular transfer to the biceps and brachialis muscles from the median and ulnar nerves are most preferred.

*Ray et al. reported on clinical outcomes after double fascicular nerve transfers to the biceps brachii and brachialis muscles in patients with brachial plexus injuries.* **The authors report** that elbow flexion was regained in 28 of the 29 patients (97%) without functional deficits from the donor nerve distribution. **They conclude** that the procedure results in reliable restoration of elbow flexion in patients with brachial plexus injuries.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1 and 2: The three posterior divisions combine to form the posterior cord. Nerve branches of the posterior cord are the axillary, radial, thoracodorsal, upper subscapular, and lower subscapular.
Answer 3: Nerves arising from the medial cord are the medial pectoral, medial brachial cutaneous, medial antebrachial cutaneous, ulnar, and a contribution to the median.
Answer 5: The inferior trunk contributes to the posterior and medial cords.
