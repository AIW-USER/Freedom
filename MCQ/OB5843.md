## Question

A 28-year-old female comes for evaluation of her right ankle, which remains painful six weeks after she sustained an inversion injury while playing volleyball. She was treated in a functional brace for five weeks and completed a course of physical therapy. On examination, she has diffuse tenderness at the anterior ankle without any retromalleolar tenderness. Anterior drawer testing is symmetric to the contralateral ankle. There is no pain with static weight bearing but she has pain with running. Inversion, eversion, dorsiflexion and plantarflexion strength are all graded as 5/5. In addition to a lateral ankle ligament injury, an MRI in this patient in the acute setting will most likely show which of the following?

## Options

1. Peroneus brevis injury
2. Peroneus longus injury
3. Talar bone bruise
4. Talar and medial malleolar bone bruises
5. Avulsion fracture of the distal aspect of the lateral malleolus

## Correct option

**3. Talar bone bruise**

## Answer explanation

This patient has an acute ankle sprain. Of the options listed, the most common associated MRI finding in the setting of acute lateral ankle ligament injury is talar bone bruising.

Patients with acute lateral ankle ligament injury should be treated with some form of functional bracing. These orthoses have outperformed taping and CAM boots in several studies. Despite restoration of ankle stability, some patients may have persistent symptoms. Associated injuries and alternative diagnoses must be considered.

*Prado et al. conducted a prospective, randomized trial comparing two non-operative protocols following first-time lateral ankle injuries.* **The authors found** that functional bracing followed by PT resulted in better short-term AOFAS scores and quicker return to activity compared to boot immobilization followed by PT.

*Kemler et al. performed a systematic review of non-operative treatments for ankle sprains.* **The authors report** positive outcomes with ankle bracing compared to other functional treatments. **They found** talar bone bruising in 28.5%, talar and medial malleolous bone bruising in 8.1%, peroneus brevis injury in 7.5%, and avulsion fracture of distal part of the lateral malleolous in 2.7% of patients.

Incorrect answers:
Answers 1 and 2: Peroneus brevis injury may occur in 7.5% of patients
Answer 4: Talar and medial malleolar bone bruising may occur in 8.1% of patients.
Answer 5: Avulsion of the distal aspect of the lateral malleolus may occur in 2.7% of patients.
