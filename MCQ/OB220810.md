## Question

A 22-month-old male is being evaluated for scoliosis. Radiographs demonstrate a unilateral bar with contralateral hemivertebrae and a Cobb angle of 41 degrees. A spine MRI was performed and no neural axis abnormalities were found. Which of the following is the recommended treatment option?

## Options

1. Bracing
2. Hemivertebrectomy with short segment posterior instrumentation and fusion
3. Instrumentation with growing rods
4. Observation
5. Risser casting

## Correct option

**2. Hemivertebrectomy with short segment posterior instrumentation and fusion**

## Answer explanation

A unilateral bar with contralateral hemivertebrae is best managed with resection and short-segment fusion due to their high rate of significant curve progression (answer 2).

Congenital scoliosis occurs due to failure of normal vertebral development between the 4th and 6th weeks of gestation. It is classified as a failure of formation (hemivertebrae, wedge vertebrae) or failure of segmentation (block vertebrae). Due to their association with neural axis abnormalities and systemic anomalies, MRI of the spine, as well as cardiac and renal studies, are recommended. Hemivertebrae with contralateral unilateral bars are associated with the most rapid progression.

*Karol et al. published a review article on early-onset scoliosis.* When discussing congenital scoliosis, **they highlight** the poor results of in situ fusion in cases of unbalanced malformations. **They conclude** that fusion in cases of patients with progressive deformity can encourage proper pulmonary development.

*Hedequist et al. published a review on congenital scoliosis.* *They reviewed the natural history, imaging, associated conditions, and treatment options.* When discussing patients with contralateral hemivertebrae with unilateral bars, **they recommended** excision in patients with hemivertebrae causing curves greater than 40 degrees.

Incorrect Answer Choices:
Answer choice 1: Bracing is not typically effective in congenital scoliosis as the curves are not flexible.
Answer choice 3: Given that the patient already presents with significant deformity, hemivertebrectomy would allow for greater correction.
Answer choice 4: This pattern of congenital scoliosis has a high rate of progression, and observation is not an appropriate treatment.
Answer choice 5: This pattern of congenital scoliosis has a high rate of progression and would not be effectively treated with Risser casting.
