## Question

A healthy 62-year-old male is evaluated in the joint reconstruction clinic and found to have severe bilateral osteoarthritis of the knees that has failed nonoperative management. The patient’s only past medical history is hypertension and he would like to limit his total recovery time. One month later the patient undergoes bilateral total knee arthroplasty. Intraoperatively, the orthopedic surgeon uses tourniquets and a robot-assisted system. The patient receives bilateral cruciate retaining cementless total knee arthroplasty with patellar resurfacing. The surgeon places bilateral intra-articular drains. Which part of this patient’s treatment course holds an AAOS moderate recommendation against its use?

## Options

1. Tourniquet use
2. Robotic-assisted total knee arthroplasty
3. Patellar resurfacing
4. Intra-articular closed drain suction
5. Bilateral total knee arthroplasty

## Correct option

**4. Intra-articular closed drain suction**

## Answer explanation

This patient elects to undergo bilateral total knee arthroplasty. The 2022 clinical practice guidelines on the surgical management of osteoarthritis of the knee have a moderate recommendation against the use of intraarticular drains.

Total knee arthroplasty is an exceedingly common treatment for knee osteoarthritis that has failed nonoperative management. There is a vast sum of literature regarding the preoperative, intraoperative, and postoperative management of patients undergoing total knee arthroplasty. The AAOS, in conjunction with AANA and AAHKS, published clinical guidelines on the surgical management of osteoarthritis of the knee in 2022. The authors made a moderate-strength recommendation that drains should not be used with total knee arthroplasty. The use of wound drains after total knee arthroplasty is associated with increased cost and hospital length of stay with no difference in postoperative range of motion, infections, or knee swelling.

*Quinn et al. published a review article of six randomized control trials evaluating the difference between drain placement and no drain placement after total knee arthroplasty.* **The authors found** no reported difference in the postoperative range of motion or knee swelling. There was also reported no difference in the postoperative hemoglobin drop between the two groups. **The authors conclude** that drains are likely not required after total knee arthroplasty.

*Watanabe et al. published a prospective trial of 63 patients who underwent bilateral total knee arthroplasty.* *20 patients received bilateral drains, 22 received a drain on one side and no drain on the other side, and 21 received no drain.* The hemoglobin drop on postoperative day 1 was higher in the bilateral drain and unilateral drain group. There were no significant differences in knee range of motion, circumference, or measured clinical outcomes between the groups. **The authors conclude** that closed suction drainage is not necessary for total knee arthroplasty.

*Bjerke-Kroll et al. published a cost analysis of drain usage in 536 primary total hip and 598 primary total knee arthroplasty patients at a single center.* They found that the use of a drain was associated with $538 of additional cost per total hip arthroplasty and $455 per total knee arthroplasty. **The authors conclude** that drain usage is not a cost-effective strategy.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: The AAOS guidelines gave a strong recommendation that there is no difference in outcomes with or without tourniquet use.
Answer 2: The AAOS guidelines gave a limited recommendation that there is no difference in outcomes between robotic and conventional total knee arthroplasty.
Answer 3: The AAOS guidelines strongly recommend that there is no difference between patellar resurfacing and non-patellar resurfacing.
Answer 5: The AAOS gives a consensus recommendation that simultaneous bilateral total knee arthroplasty could be performed but should be avoided in patients who are at high risk for cardiopulmonary complications.
