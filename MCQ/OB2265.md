## Options

1. Avascular necrosis
2. Coxa vara
3. Symptomatic malunion
4. Nonunion
5. Subtrochanteric fracture

## Correct option

**1. Avascular necrosis**

## Answer explanation

This patient sustained a Delbet type II displaced transcervical femoral neck fracture. The most common complication following this femoral neck fracture is avascular necrosis (AVN).

Pediatric femoral neck fractures occur most commonly following high-energy trauma. The complications of femoral neck fractures in children include AVN, coxa vara or valga, malunion, nonunion, and premature physeal closure. Unlike the other complications, the risk of osteonecrosis is directly related to the amount of displacement at the time of injury. The other complications are influenced by the type of treatment. ­­The Delbet classification describes the location of the fracture, with more proximal fractures having an increased incidence of AVN.

*Canale et al. review pediatric femoral neck fractures.* **They report** that these injuries require closed reduction or open reduction and internal fixation to avoid complications of coxa vara deformity and nonunion. **They conclude** that AVN appears to be related to the severity of the initial injury and is unaffected by treatment.

*Hughes et al. review several complications following pediatric femoral neck fractures.* **They reported** an incidence of 20-30% for coxa vara following these injuries. **They conclude** that the incidence is increased in the fractures treated nonoperatively.

Figures A and B are the AP and lateral radiographs demonstrating a Delbet type II displaced femoral neck fracture. Figure C is the post-operative radiograph demonstrating screw fixation. Illustration A outlines the Delbet classification with associated AVN risk. Illustration B is the schematic representation of the Delbet classification.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Coxa vara is defined as a neck-shaft angle <120 degrees and is the second most common complication following pediatric femoral neck fractures.
Answer 3: Symptomatic malunion is a complication that more commonly occurs following conservative management or with inadequate reduction. AVN is a more common complication.
Answer 4: Nonunion is more common following conservative management of type II or III fractures but is less common than AVN.
Answer 5: This injury does not increase the risk of a subtrochanteric femur fracture. AVN would be a more common complication following this injury.
