## Question

Which of the following is true as it relates to the patella in TKA?

## Options

1. Resurfacing leads to a decrease in anterior knee pain and improved Oxford outcome scores
2. Component should be placed laterally to improve patellar tracking
3. Resurfacing in patients with inflammatory arthritis has shown consistent benefit
4. Metal backed components have a lower rate of aseptic loosening
5. Higher infection rates are seen in patients who have had their patella resurfaced

## Correct option

**3. Resurfacing in patients with inflammatory arthritis has shown consistent benefit**

## Answer explanation

Patellar resurfacing, while controversial in most patients undergoing TKA, has been shown to have consistent benefit in patients with inflammatory arthritis.

“Selective resurfacing” has become more popular with modern TKA in an attempt to decrease complications including postoperative anterior pain in the knee, subluxation, dislocation, patellar fracture, rupture of the quadriceps tendon or the patellar ligament, and patellar clunk. While literature on selective resurfacing is supportive of either decision, those patients with rheumatoid arthritis and other inflammatory conditions have consistently shown improved results following patellar resurfacing. Other patient populations in which patellar resurfacing should be performed are those with severe patellar deformity and those undergoing TKA primarily for patellofemoral arthritis.

*Barrrack et al reviewed patellar resurfacing and retention following TKA.* **They report** that anterior knee pain following TKA had not association with whether or not the patella was resurfaced. Further, **they conclude** that outcomes measures were not changed by the preoperative presence of anterior knee pain either.

*Pilling et al review patellar resurfacing in primary TKA.* **They report** postoperative anterior knee pain in 13% of patients who underwent patellar resurfacing compared to 24% in those with patellar retention, however, this did not reach statistical significance. **They conclude** that outcomes were similar between the two groups, but that further studies need to be performed with specific patella-specific scores.

*Maney et al review surgeon preference as it relates to patella resurfacing.* **They report** that 57% of surgeons “selectively” resurface, 37% “rarely” resurface and 7% “usually” resurface. Improved Oxford scores were identified at 6 months and 5 years in surgeons reporting to “usually” resurface the patella. No difference in revision rates per 100 component years was identified between groups. **They conclude** that “usually” resurfacing the patella was related with improved patient outcomes, but no change in revision rates.

*Kajino et al compared patellar resurfacing to retention during bilateral procedures in patients with rheumatoid arthritis.* **They found** no difference in pain scores, functional range of motion, muscle strength or instability between groups. They did however, identify a decrease in standing pain and pain when traversing stairs in the knees that had patellar resurfacing. It is for these reasons, that **the authors recommend** resurfacing the patella in patients with rheumatoid arthritis.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Anterior knee pain and functional outcome scores (Oxford, Knee Injury and Osteoarthritis Outcome, Visual Analogue) have been shown to be no different
Answer 2: Components should be placed medially on the patella to improve with tracking.
Answer 4: Metal backed patellar components have a higher rate of loosening.
Answer 5: Infection rates are not different between patients undergoing resurfacing and retention.
