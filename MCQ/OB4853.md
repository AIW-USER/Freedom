## Question

The video in Figure V shows the gait pattern of a child with spastic diplegic cerebral palsy. Sagittal kinematic studies show ankle equinus, knee flexion contracture, hip flexion contracture, and anterior pelvic tilt. Which of the following best describes this gait pattern.

## Options

1. True equinus
2. Jump gait
3. Apparent equinus
4. Crouch gait
5. Asymmetrical gait

## Correct option

**2. Jump gait**

## Answer explanation

The child demonstrates a jump gait which is characterized by hip and knee flexion with ankle equinus.

Characteristic sagittal gait patterns in spastic diplegia include the following: true equinus, jump gait, apparent equinus, crouch gait and asymmetrical gait. The ground reaction force is anterior to the knee in true equinus and posterior to the knee in crouch gait. Sagittal plane kinematics for each gait pattern is seen in Illustration A below.

*Rodda et al. performed a cross-sectional study on 174 children with spastic diplegia and abnormal gait.* **They found** that 27% had true equinus, 22% had jump gait, 18% had apparent equinus, 16% had crouch gait, and 17% had asymmetrical gait. 68 limbs were followed over time (34 patients), there was a shift towards true equinus in 17 limbs, a shift towards crouch gait in 17 limbs, and no change in 34 limbs.

The video shows a child with a jump gait pattern. Illustration A shows the different sagittal gait patterns in spastic diplegia and treatment modalities used to address them. Illustration B is a diagram showing the differences between jump and crouch gait.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: True equinus may be present, but this alone does not best describe this complex gait with multiple other abnormalities.
Answer 3: Apparent equinus is characterized by an appearance of toe walking secondary to excessive knee and hip flexion. However, the ankle is able to reach a plantigrade position and maintains a more normal range of motion than seen in a true equinus deformity.
Answer 4: Crouch gait is characterized by ankle calcaneus, and flexed hip and knee (with normal/anterior pelvic tilt).
Answer 5: In asymmetrical gait, the subjects 2 lower limbs belong to different groups e.g. right has apparent equinus, and left has jump gait.
