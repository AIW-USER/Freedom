## Question

A 10-month-old patient is undergoing open reduction for developmental dysplasia of the hip via the medial approach. During the procedure, the surgeon performs a psoas tenotomy to facilitate reduction. Which of the following is true of the artery at risk during this step?

## Options

1. The artery passes above the piriformis muscle and leaves the pelvis through the greater sciatic notch.
2. The artery can also be found between the sartorius and tensor fascia lata on a direct anterior approach to the hip.
3. The artery most commonly originates from the profunda femoris artery, but has been found to originate from the femoral artery.
4. The artery continues down the medial thigh towards the adductor canal.
5. The artery is a branch of the internal iliac artery.

## Correct option

**3. The artery most commonly originates from the profunda femoris artery, but has been found to originate from the femoral artery.**

## Answer explanation

The medial femoral circumflex artery (MFCA) is a known structure at risk during a psoas tenotomy through a medial approach. The MFCA most commonly originates from the profunda femoris artery, but may also originate from the femoral artery (Answer 3).

The medial approach to the hip can be used for open reduction for developmental dysplasia of the hip in pediatric patients. This approach involves a superficial dissection between the gracilis and adductor longus and a deep dissection between the adductor brevis and adductor magnus. Surgeons may use this approach to provide direct access to obstructing tissues and facilitate hip reduction. When performing a psoas tendon release, the MFCA is a known structure at risk. The MFCA is most commonly a branch off the profunda femoris artery (or occasionally directly from the femoral artery) that passes posteriorly between the psoas tendon and pectineus to the posterior aspect of the femoral neck. Due to its course along the psoas tendon, the vessel is at particular risk during psoas tenotomy. Injury to the MFCA can have profound implications, leading to avascular necrosis of the femoral head. Therefore, meticulous dissection and direct visualization of the psoas tendon are critical during a medial approach to the hip.

*Novais et al. published a systematic review and meta-analysis of patients with DDH to evaluate the risk of osteonecrosis comparing age (< 12 months or > 12 months) at surgery and the approach (medial or anterior) used.* *A total of 364 hips were reduced using a medial approach, and 220 hips were reduced using an anterior approach.* *This study found no difference in the development of osteonecrosis between medial or anterior approaches (18.7% vs 19.6%).* **The authors concluded** that without an association of osteonecrosis based on approach, the approach should be determined by surgeon preference.

*Gardner et al. published a systematic review of 14 studies covering 734 hips treated for open reduction of DDH via a medial approach.* *The average follow-up in this study was 10.9 years, with clinically significant avascular necrosis (AVN) occurring in 24% of cases.* *The authors specifically mention the interest in studying AVN rates in the medial approach due to the high risk of damage to the MFCA during capsulotomy or psoas tendon release.* **The authors conclude** by emphasizing the unfavorable outcomes associated with AVN and report that clinically significant AVN can occur in over 20% of patients with medial open reduction.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer choice 1: This course describes the superior gluteal artery, which originates from the posterior division of the internal iliac artery and supplies the gluteal musculature. Due to its posterior nature on the ilium, it is not at risk with the medial approach.
Answer choice 2: The ascending branch of the lateral femoral circumflex artery is found between the sartorius and the tensor fascia lata.
Answer choice 4: The superficial femoral artery (SFA) continues down the medial side of the thigh towards the adductor canal to become the popliteal artery. The SFA is not at particular risk during the psoas tenotomy step of the procedure.
Answer choice 5: The internal iliac artery gives several branches, including the obturator artery, the superior gluteal artery, the inferior gluteal artery, and the internal pudendal artery. None of these arteries are at particular risk during a psoas tenotomy through a medial approach.
