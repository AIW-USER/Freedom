## Question

A patient presents to your office shortly after watching the Buffalo Bills football game where a player became unresponsive after making a tackle. She is inquiring about more information regarding sudden cardiac death and particular risk factors for this condition. Which patient below likely has the highest risk of sudden cardiac death after blunt trauma during sports?

## Options

1. 24-year-old White male who is a professional rower
2. 65-year-old Asian male playing baseball with his grandson
3. 33-year-old Hispanic male professional football player
4. 22-year-old African American male professional football player
5. 45-year-old African American female playing basketball with her daughter

## Correct option

**4. 22-year-old African American male professional football player**

## Answer explanation

Sudden cardiac death and commotio cordis have been studied in more recent years. Risk factors include male sex, African-American race, history of cardiomyopathy, and contact sports athletes. Therefore, a 22-year-old African American male professional football player is at the highest risk of suffering from sudden cardiac death (answer choice 4).

Commotio cordis is rare, with less than 30 cases diagnosed annually. It is a condition caused by blunt chest wall trauma that occurs during ventricular repolarization. It results in ventricular fibrillation and requires prompt defibrillation for treatment. Overall, the most common cause of sudden death in young athletes is secondary to a cardiac condition. This includes hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (most common), coronary artery disease, or commotio cordis. Thorough documentation of medical and family history is essential to rule out any pre-existing cardiac condition or risk factors for such. Based upon recent literature, male sex, African-American race, cardiomyopathic condition, and participation in contact sports are all risk factors for developing sudden cardiac death. A thorough physical exam and further workup, including an EKG or echocardiogram if warranted, is important to diagnose cardiac conditions before participation in contact sports. If a cardiac condition is found, vigorous exercise and sports should be avoided.

*Emery et al. conducted a general review of sudden cardiac death in athletes.* **They found** male athletes have a 3-5x higher incidence compared to females, black or African-American athletes have an increased risk compared to white athletes, contact sports (basketball had the highest risk), and pre-existing cardiomyopathic conditions are all risk factors for sudden cardiac death. **They conclude** that it's important to inform the cardiac care team regarding strategies to identify those at risk and to respond to events.

*Okorare et al. conducted an update on sudden cardiac death regarding commotio cordis.* **They note** blunt trauma to the precordium, with multiple course-altering factors like the type of stimulus, the force of impact, the qualities of the projectile, the site of impact, and the timing of impact in relation to the cardiac cycle all play a role in this rare condition. Management includes emergent resuscitative measures. Prevention of future episodes includes chest wall protection against blunt trauma such as chest protectors and safety balls.

Incorrect answers:
Answers 1-3 and 5: Risk factors for sudden cardiac death include male sex, African-American race, history of cardiomyopathy, and contact sports. Of the provided answer choices, answer choice 4 provides the greatest combination of risk factors, thus likely placing this patient at highest risk.
