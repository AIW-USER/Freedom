## Question

A 64-year-old male presents with right hip pain and incisional drainage 2 years following primary right total hip arthroplasty (THA). He undergoes component explantation and placement of an articulating antibiotic spacer. When performing his second-stage revision THA, which of the following is most likely to effectively decrease the risk of recurrent instability following the procedure?

## Options

1. Utilization of a jumbo cup
2. Decreasing impingement through use of a small femoral head diameter
3. Debridement of posterior capsular tissue
4. Placement of a dual mobility construct
5. Decreasing abductor tension through trochanteric advancement

## Correct option

**4. Placement of a dual mobility construct**

## Answer explanation

Utilization of a dual mobility construct has been associated with decreased rates of instability following revision total hip arthroplasty (THA)

Compared to primary THA, the risk of postoperative instability is substantially higher in the revision setting. Therefore, it is important to consider using surgical techniques and strategies that improve stability, such as decreasing potential impingement through the use of a larger femoral head component (improved head-neck offset), performing a meticulous repair of the posterior capsule and external rotators, and utilization of a dual mobility construct. In cases where abductor tension is a concern, a trochanteric advancement has been performed with successful results. The placement of jumbo cups has been associated with instability in the revision setting and is hypothesized to be due to soft tissue overgrowth and resulting impingement.

*Levin et al performed a systematic review and meta-analysis of the use of dual mobility constructs in revision THA.* **They found** that the overall dislocation risk was approximately 2.2% and the risk of intraprosthetic dislocation was 0.3%. Compared to fixed bearings, dual mobility constructs had an 0.24 odds ratio of dislocation.

*Hartzler et al performed a retrospective review of 355 revision THA cases, in which 146 received a dual mobility construct and 209 received a large femoral head (40 mm).* There were significantly less dislocations in the dual mobility group (3% vs 10%). Although complications were otherwise similar between groups, reoperations were overall less in the dual mobility group as well (6% vs 15%).

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Jumbo cups have been associated with increased rates of instability in the revision setting.
Answer 2: A smaller femoral head component would increase impingement of the neck on the acetabular component.
Answer 3: Posterior capsular repair (NOT debridement) has been shown to decrease instability.
Answer 5: The goal of trochanteric advancement would be to increase abductor tension.
