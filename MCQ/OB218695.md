## Question

A 32-year-old female is playing recreational basketball with her friends. As she lands from a jump shot, she feels a pop in the posterior aspect of her ankle and is unable to continue playing. She presents to your clinic and you note an inability to plantarflex with Thompson's test and a palpable defect in the posterior ankle. You recommend open surgical repair of the ruptured tendon. What is the most common complication you should make the patient aware of?

## Options

1. Re-rupture
2. Wound complication
3. Nerve injury
4. Stiffness
5. Failure to return to pre-injury activity level

## Correct option

**2. Wound complication**

## Answer explanation

The most common and feared complication of an open achilles tendon repair is wound complication (Answer 2).

Achilles tendon ruptures are relatively common injuries that usually occur with sudden, forced dorsiflexion of a plantarflexed foot. Diagnosis can be made with a palpable defect, inability to plantarflex, or with a positive Thompson's test. MRI can also be of use if the diagnostic exam is equivocal. Treatment may include nonoperative management with an early functional rehabilitation protocol or operative repair. Repair options include open end-to-end repair and percutaneous, minimally invasive techniques. There remains a debate as to which technique is better but most studies have shown relatively equivalent long-term outcomes. The risks/complication profile of each approach is slightly different, however. With open repairs, the primary concern is wound healing complication, which occurs at a rate of 5-10%. One reason this occurs is because the blood supply to the posterior ankle is poor. Breakdown or infection of the posterior ankle can be a devastating complication. While the minimally invasive approaches have lower risk for wound complications, they portend an increased risk for iatrogenic sural nerve injury.

*Grassi et al. compared outcomes and complications between open and minimally invasive achilles tendon repairs.* **They showed** a decreased risk for overall complication (RR 0.21) and wound complications (RR 0.15) with the minimally invasive approach and noted that the minimally invasive group was more likely to report good or excellent outcomes (RR 1.18). **They concluded** that a decreased risk of postoperative complications made minimally invasive approaches worth considering.

*Alcelik et al. compared a minimally invasive achilles tendon repair (Achillon) to open achilles tendon repair.* **They also noted** reduced complication rates in the Achillon group with an odds ratio = 0.14. Otherwise, there were no significant differences in re-rupture rate, return to sports, or AOFAS outcome scores between the groups.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: While re-rupture is a known complication it occurs in only 1-2% of cases versus wound complications which occur in approximately 5-10% of open repairs.
Answer 3: The risk of nerve injury, particularly to the sural nerve, has traditionally been highest in the minimally invasive group and is not often seen in the open repair group.
Answer 4: Stiffness is an uncommon complication after achilles tendon repair.
Answer 5: Most patients are able to return to their pre-injury activity level after an achilles tendon repair and the risk of not doing so is out-shadowed by the risk for wound complications.
