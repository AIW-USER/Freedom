## Question

A 57-year-old active patient develops increasing ankle pain over the last 2 years due to post-traumatic arthritis. Radiographs are shown in figures A and B. Despite bracing, the patient continues to have debilitating pain and decides to undergo an ankle arthrodesis. What is the optimal position for an ankle arthrodesis?

## Options

1. 5° plantarflexion, 10° external rotation, 0° valgus, talus centered on tibial plafond
2. Plantigrade, 10° external rotation, 5° valgus, posterior positioning of the talus on tibial plafond
3. 5° dorsiflexion, 10° external rotation, 5° varus, anterior positioning of the talus on tibial plafond
4. Plantigrade, 15° external rotation, 5° valgus, talus centered on the tibial plafond
5. Plantigrade, neutral rotation, 0° valgus, talus centered on tibial plafond

## Correct option

**2. Plantigrade, 10° external rotation, 5° valgus, posterior positioning of the talus on tibial plafond**

## Answer explanation

The optimal positioning for an ankle arthrodesis is plantigrade, 10° external rotation, 5° of valgus, and 5mm of posterior positioning of the talus on the tibial plafond.

Ankle arthrodesis is a salvage procedure for the treatment of post-traumatic arthritis. Patients undergoing arthrodesis usually have substantial pain relief, but altered gait mechanics. Sole tibiotalar fusion leads to accelerated subtalar joint arthrosis. Ideal positioning of the ankle during arthrodesis enables optimal knee mechanics and ambulation on uneven surfaces, positioning the ankle in neutral flexion, 5° of hindfoot valgus, 10° of external rotation, and with the talus positioned posteriorly beneath the tibial plafond. Neutral flexion reduces the extension that occurs at the knee during gait and medial collateral ligament laxity. Slight valgus positioning of the hindfoot prevents lateral column overloading. Positioning the talus 5mm posterior from the center on the tibial plafond decreases the lever arm of the foot during gait.

*Thomas et al. performed a retrospective gait analysis of patients that underwent tibiotalar arthrodesis for primary ankle arthritis.* **The authors found** that tibiotalar fusion did provide acceptable pain relief and sense of health, but there were substantial differences in gait compared to controls as well as the progression of hindfoot arthritis. **The authors concluded** that patients should be counseled that fusion is a salvage procedure that permanently alters gait mechanics.

*Buck et al. performed a gait analysis study of patients that underwent an ankle arthrodesis.* **The authors found** that a plantarflexed position of the ankle leads to the development of genu recurvatum and medial collateral ligament laxity. **The authors concluded** that the ideal position for ankle arthrodesis is neutral flexion, 5° of valgus, 5-10° of external rotation, and slight posterior displacement of the talus under the tibia.

*Singer et al. performed a gait analysis study of patients that underwent either ankle arthroplasty or arthrodesis for primary ankle arthritis.* **They found** that patients with ankle arthroplasty had a greater range of motion throughout gait compared to the arthrodesis group, but neither group was similar to controls. **The authors concluded** that both ankle arthroplasty and arthrodesis had similar functional outcomes in the treatment of primary ankle arthritis.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1, 3, 4, 5: The optimal positioning for an ankle arthrodesis is neutral dorsiflexion, 5° valgus, 10° external rotation, and posterior positioning of the talus on the tibial plafond. All of the other answer choices are incorrect.
