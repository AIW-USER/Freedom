## Question

A 17-year-old male lacrosse player gets hit in the right flank by an opposing player. He returns to the sideline and is unable to continue playing. On examination, you note pain and ecchymosis surrounding the inferior aspect of the right ribcage. He denies any shortness of breath. He is taken to the hospital where his vital sigs are stable and labs are performed. Hemoglobin is 14.3 g/dl. Urinalysis is pink in color and reveals negative leukocyte esterase and >5 RBC/hpf. Pelvic radiographs are negative for fracture. CT scan of the chest, abdomen and pelvis is ordered. Given his history and physical exam findings, what will this scan likely reveal?

## Options

1. Splenic laceration
2. Renal laceration
3. Hemothorax
4. Liver laceration
5. Posterior urethral injury

## Correct option

**2. Renal laceration**

## Answer explanation

This athlete has most likely sustained a right renal laceration given the mechanism of injury and subsequent hematuria.

Sport-related renal lacerations most commonly occurs in males and with a single blow to the flank. Those injured will complain of flank/abdominal pain and often have associated ecchymosis as well as hematuria. They should be evaluated in an emergency department with a CT of the abdomen and pelvis as soon as possible, as there is a risk for developing hemodynamic instability with continued bleeding. If the patient is hemodynamically stable, most renal lacerations can be managed non-operatively, however, a small subset will require surgical exploration and in extreme cases, partial/complete nephrectomy.

*Patel et al. evaluated grade III/IV renal injuries in blunt sports-related flank trauma and compared this data to renal injuries in a non-sports population.* **They found** that contact sports and skiing/snowboarding were the most common sports-related causes of renal injury and sports-related injuries were more likely to be isolated injuries. They finish by noting that sports-related injuries had lower rates of hemodynamic instability and mortality, lending to a better overall outlook.

*Dangle et al. reviewed the trends in management of traumatic pediatric renal trauma over 20 years.* **They found** low grade injuries in males to be the most common cohort, with ATV, bike, and motor vehicle collisions becoming more common causes throughout the collection period. **They note** that only a small percentage required surgical intervention.

*McAleer et al. retrospectively reviewed a trauma registry over the course of 16 years to evaluate the incidence and severity of testicular/renal injuries in team sports.* **They found** that <5% of testicular/renal injuries in the cohort occurred during team sports participation and all patients recovered uneventfully without the loss of organs or function. **They conclude** that the rate of testicular/renal injury is low in team sports, and those with solitary testis or kidney may not need to have participation restricted.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Splenic laceration can occur with blunt abdominal trauma, but trauma normally occurs on the left side. Additionally, intra-abdominal bleeding would be seen, not hematuria.
Answer 3: Hemothorax can be seen with blunt trauma, but would produce chest pain and shortness of breath, rather than abdominal pain and hematuria.
Answer 4: Liver laceration can occur in blunt abdominal trauma and may cause intra-abdominal bleeding, but would not cause hematuria.
Answer 5: Posterior urethral injury can cause hematuria, but is almost always associated with displaced pelvic fractures, not a direct blow to the flank.
