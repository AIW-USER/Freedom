## Question

A 64-year-old man presents with forearm swelling. A gadolinium-enhanced MRI of his forearm is shown in Figure A and a CT scan of his chest is shown in Figure B. The lesion is resected and its gross and cellular histological appearance is shown in Figure C and D respectively. Fluorescence-in-situ-hybridization is negative for t(X:18) and t(12:16). Immunohistochemistry is negative for desmin, myogenin and CD34. What is the most likely pathologic diagnosis?

## Options

1. Alveolar rhabdomyosarcoma
2. Myxoid liposarcoma
3. Dermatofibrosarcoma protuberans
4. Synovial sarcoma
5. High-grade pleomorphic undifferentiated sarcoma

## Correct option

**5. High-grade pleomorphic undifferentiated sarcoma**

## Answer explanation

The tumor resected is high-grade pleomorphic undifferentiated sarcoma, previously known as pleomorphic malignant fibrous histiocytoma (MFH).

MFH is the most common soft-tissue sarcoma that occurs in adults. However, the origin of the cell type(s) remains unclear. Subtypes include pleomorphic sarcoma with giant cells (giant cell MFH) and undifferentiated pleomorphic sarcoma with prominent inflammation (inflammatory MFH).

*Randall et al. reviewed soft tissue sarcomas and* **state** that pleomorphic undifferentiated sarcoma is the most common subtype (60-70% of cases), affects adults >40years, affects males >females, is found deep in the extremities, may rapidly enlarge and may be painful. In addition, 5% of patients have lung metastasis (Figure B).

*Vasileios et al. reviewed high-grade undifferentiated sarcoma of the extremities.* **They found** that local recurrence developed in 19.3% and 31.5% developed lung metastases. 5-year-survival was 66.7%. Tumors >5cm were significant variables for metastases and decreased survival. **They concluded** that overall prognosis was poor.

Figure A shows an MRI of a heterogenous mass on the volar-radial side of the forearm with peripheral enhancement. Figure B shows pulmonary metastases on CT scan. Figure C shows the gross appearance of pleomorphic MFH. Figure D shows the histological appearance of pleomorphic undifferentiated sarcoma, with high cellularity, comprising high-grade, heterogenous, pleomorphic cells with high mitotic index, and occasional bizarre multinucleated giant cells. Illustration A shows alveolar rhabdomyosarcoma. Illustration B shows pleomorphic liposarcoma. Illustration C shows dermatofibrosarcoma protuberans. Illustration D shows hemangiopericytoma.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Alveolar rhabdomyosarcomas display alveolar round cells and primitive rhabdomyoblasts. Myogenin and desmin would be positive.
Answer 2: Myxoid liposarcomas would be positive for t(12:16).
Answer 3: Dermatofibrosarcoma protuberans affects young to middle aged adults. They present as a painless subcutaneous nodule. CD34 would be positive.
Answer 4: Synovial sarcoma would be positive for t(X:18)
