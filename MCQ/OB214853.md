## Question

A 53-year-old woman with sickle cell anemia is undergoing a cementless total hip arthroplasty for avascular necrosis. During implant impaction an audible crack is heard. A non-displaced metaphyseal fracture is found and intra-operative portable radiographs shows no involvement of the lesser trochanter or diaphysis. What is the most appropriate treatment for this?

## Options

1. Keep the current implant, and make the patient partial weightbearing for 4 weeks post-operatively
2. Keep the current implant, open reduction with internal fixation and fibular strut allograft
3. Remove the current implant, place a cerclage wire, and place an under-sized implant
4. Remove the current implant, place a cerclage wire, and replace the same implant
5. Remove the current implant, open reduction with internal fixation and place a modular implant

## Correct option

**4. Remove the current implant, place a cerclage wire, and replace the same implant**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a non-displaced fracture involving the metaphyseal calcar (Intra-operative Vancouver A2). This is common risk among sickle cell patients as the bone can be sclerotic, requiring care to prevent. Once this has occurred, these can be treated by removing the implant, placing a cerclage wire circumferentially, and replacing the appropriately sized implant.

Periprosthetic fractures are not uncommon and associated with multiple causal factors. Unfortunately, many of these fractures occur intra-operatively and go unrecognized until later in the postoperative period. If any concern for fracture during the broaching process or implant insertion, exploration of the fracture and/or intra-operative radiographs can be obtained to characterize the fracture pattern and extent of involvement. A minimally or non-displaced fracture that does not extend distally into the diaphysis can be treated safely with removing the broach/implant and placing a cerclage wire followed by resuming broaching or implant placement is acceptable. The cerclage wire restores the hoop stresses of the cylindrical femur and can allows stable fixation. If the fracture extends past the lesser trochanter and/or is displaced (Intra-operative Vancouver A3), placing a fully porous-coated stem may allow more stable long term fixation.

Kelley provided recommendations and perils for avoiding intra-operative fractures. He emphasized diligent scrutinization of pre-operative radiographs for osteoporosis and canal cortical thickness. A low threshold should be maintained for using cemented components if either of these are worrisome.

Berry also discussed management of periprosthetic fracture during total hip arthroplasty. He points out that intraoperative treatment should focus on stabilizing the implant and fracture. For postoperative fractures, stable implants only require open reduction and internal fixation, whereas unstable ones will require a complex revision and fracture stabilization.

*Berend et al. reviewed a series of 58 patients who underwent cerclage wiring for intra-operative non-displaced calcar fractures.* **They showed** 100% survival and no subsidence of the femoral stem at 16 year followup, with excellent improvement in patient-reported outcomes. This study serves to validate cerclage wiring an acceptable treatment method with minimal detriment on patient outcomes.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1- This would be appropriate for Vancouver B2 fractures.
Answer 2- This would have a role for Vancouver C fracture which are more distal.
Answer 3- By placing a cerclage wire, the hoop stresses are restored, so an appropriately sized implant is needed, otherwise you risk implant subsidence.
Answer 5- This would be an option for Vancouver A3 fractures, so long as the modular stem is fully-porous.
