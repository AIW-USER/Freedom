## Question

An orthopedic surgery resident is helping design a randomized, controlled trial evaluating the effect of vancomycin powder on surgical site infections in total joint arthroplasty. The resident proposes blinding the patient, the treating physician, and the outcome assessor. What is the likely effect of blinding the outcome assessor on the study results?

## Options

1. Decreases selection bias
2. Increases procedural bias
3. Decrease the reported treatment effect
4. Increases bias introduced in the study
5. Defines the criteria for surgical site infection

## Correct option

**3. Decrease the reported treatment effect**

## Answer explanation

Blinding the outcome assessor will likely decrease the reported treatment effect in the study.

Orthopedic research provides useful evidence to guide treatment decisions and improve the quality of care. In order to fully appreciate the findings reported, a systematic and evidence-based approach is necessary. Blinding can remove potential bias that is introduced by the patient and treating physician, thus revealing the true treatment effect. However, also blinding the outcome assessor can further reduce the treatment effect reported. Bias can be further reduced by establishing strict a priori requirements for treatment outcomes.

*Spindler et al. reviewed how to interpret the orthopedic literature in a systematic and evidence-based approach.* *They posited that approaching the literature in a systematic way allows the reader to find true merit to study results as it pertains to patient care.* **They concluded** evidence-based information will aid in decision-making related to patient care.

*Poolman et al. reviewed 32 randomized, controlled trials to assess the effect of blinded outcomes assessors.* **They found** that in studies with dichotomous outcomes reported the unblinded assessments were associated with a significantly greater treatment effect than the blinded assessments (p < 0.001). **The authors concluded** that future orthopedic trials should blind the outcome assessors to limit bias.

*Fischgrund presented a consensus statement of 20 journal editors on conflict of interest statements in the orthopedic literature.* **The author stated** the American Academy of Orthopedic Surgeons (AAOS) intends to adopt the International Committee of Medical Journal Editors (ICMJE) form for conflict of interest statements. **The author concluded** this should clarify the reporting responsibilities, facilitate the reporting process, and lead to a clearer understanding of potential conflicts of interest.

Incorrect answers
Answer 1: Selection bias is the result of selecting study subjects for treatment groups in a nonrandomized fashion, which may lead to a preferential patient type in the experimental arm of the study. Blinding the outcome assessor would have minimal effect on this.
Answer 2: Procedural bias is the effect of added treatment protocols, beyond the experimental treatment, in a study group that is not standardized to all groups. This can lead to an exaggerated effect of the experimental treatment. Blinding the outcome assessor would not have an effect on this.
Answer 4: Blinding the outcome assessor will decrease the bias introduced by the assessor.
Answer 5: Establishing a priori criteria for the definition of surgical site infection will decrease the bias incorporated by variable definitions.
