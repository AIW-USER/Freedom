## Question

An 8-year-old girl injured her knee during basketball after another player fell into her knee. She is unable to bear weight due to pain. On examination, she is noted to have a moderate knee effusion, pain with range of motion, significant apprehension with valgus stress, and tenderness most prominent over the medial epicondyle. Figure A demonstrates her current radiographs. What takes place in the zone of cartilage typically injured by this fracture?

![[q216816-a-sh1-df-physeal-fx.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Chondrocytes produce most extracellular matrix
2. Contains precursor cells
3. Woven bone is formed
4. Woven bone is converted to lamellar bone
5. Chondrocytes undergo apoptosis

## Correct option

**5. Chondrocytes undergo apoptosis**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a displaced Salter-Harris type 1 (SH1) distal femoral physeal fracture. Physeal injuries typically occur through the area of provisional calcification in the hypertrophic zone, which is the site of chondrocyte apoptosis.

Distal femoral physeal fractures often occur due to direct trauma to the knee. They are often misdiagnosed as ligamentous injuries, however, the physis is biomechanically weaker than surrounding ligaments and tendons and are predisposed to injury. Physeal injuries generally occur through the provisional calcification zone within the hypertrophic zone. In this region, chondrocytes undergo apoptosis and release calcium resulting in calcification of the matrix. This is the weakest zone within the physis and is most susceptible to shear forces.

Zionts LE reviewed common pediatric injuries around the knee including distal femoral and proximal tibial epiphyses, tibial tubercle apophysis, tibial spine, patella, articular cartilage, and proximal tibial metaphysis. He reviewed the classification, clinical and radiographic evaluation, treatment, and potential complications of each type of injury.

*Young et al. reviewed distal femoral physeal fractures and* **noted** that while uncommon (2%) they can result in devastating injuries, especially neurovascular injury in displaced fractures. Growth disturbance can occur in more than 50% of cases and as such patients should be followed until skeletal maturity. Nonoperative treatment is generally reserved for nondisplaced SH I fractures. Operative treatment is indicated for SH II, III, and IV fractures.

Figure A is a radiograph of a skeletally immature knee showing widening of the medial distal femoral physis, consistent with a displaced SH1 distal femoral physeal fracture.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The proliferative zone is where chondrocytes replicate and produce the highest rate of extracellular matrix.
Answer 2: The reserve zone contains quiescent precursor cells.
Answer 3: Woven bone is formed in the primary spongiosa.
Answer 4: Conversion of woven bone to lamellar bone occurs in the secondary spongiosa.
