## Question

A 6-year-old male with bilateral radial club hand deformities presents to establish care after recently arriving in the United States from a developing nation. During physical examination, the patient is noted to have thumb aplasia and syndactyly of the radial two digits of the involved extremities, and radiographs identify the presence of a shortened radius with associated radioulnar synostosis. In addition to the appropriate orthopedic care, what additional testing should be considered?

## Options

1. Entire spine radiographs
2. Complete blood count (CBC)
3. Echocardiogram
4. Bronchoscopy
5. Chromosomal breakage test

## Correct option

**3. Echocardiogram**

## Answer explanation

This patient has Holt-Oram syndrome, which is evident by bilateral involvement, radioulnar synostosis, thumb absence, and syndactyly of the radial two digits in the setting of radial longitudinal deficiency (RLD). These clinical findings should prompt the surgeon to obtain an echocardiogram to identify possible atrial or ventricular septal cardiac defects (Answer 3).

RLD is a congenital condition characterized by hypoplasia or absence of the radius, leading to radial deviation of the hand, wrist, and forearm shortening. Its presentation occurs on a spectrum and is often associated with syndromic conditions, including Holt-Oram syndrome. Holt-Oram syndrome is an autosomal dominant disorder caused by mutations in the TBX5 gene and is distinguished from isolated RLD by its hallmark feature of congenital heart defects, most commonly atrial septal defects (ASDs) and ventricular septal defects (VSDs).

*Kozin et al. review common pediatric congenital deformities of the hand and provide oversight into the spectrum of RLD as it relates to thumb hypoplasia.* **The authors state** that thumb hypoplasia can range from a slight decrease in thumb size to a complete absence. Initial evaluation of these patients must include screening for genetic conditions such as Holt-Oram syndrome in addition to VACTERL (vertebral anomalies, anal atresia, tracheoesophageal fistula, renal abnormalities, and limb differences), Fanconi anemia, and CHARGE syndrome (coloboma of the eye, heart defects, atresia of the nasal choanae, retardation of growth and/or development, genital and/or urinary abnormalities, and ear abnormalities and deafness). **The authors conclude** that physical examination and radiographic features of the deformity should guide appropriate diagnostic and genetic testing which can be life-saving for the child.

*Little et al. review the principles of management of pediatric congenital anomalies of the hand.* **The authors state** that patients with radial and thumb deficiencies commonly have other musculoskeletal and systemic abnormalities. In the case of Holt-Oram syndrome, **the authors recommend** that the surgeon obtain an echocardiogram to evaluate for cardiac malformations such as atrial or ventral septal defects. Ultimately, a referral to a pediatric geneticist is mandatory, but it is incumbent on the surgeon to begin systemic and further musculoskeletal evaluation in an appropriate manner based on the suspected diagnosis.

*Wall et al. review the defining features of the upper extremity in children with Holt-Oram syndrome.* **They note** that the upper extremity in Holt-Oram syndrome differs from the typical presentation of RLD. Defining characteristics include bilateral involvement, more frequent forearm involvement with associated radial shortening, radioulnar synostosis, thumb aplasia, and syndactyly of the radial two digits. **The authors conclude** that this constellation of findings should prompt the surgeon to obtain a cardiac evaluation with associated genetic testing for an autosomal dominant mutation in the TBX5 gene which is specific for Holt-Oram Syndrome.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Entire spine radiographs are a necessary diagnostic step in the workup of a patient suspected to have a diagnosis of VACTERL. However, vertebral defects are not typically associated with Holt-Oram syndrome.
Answer 2: A complete blood count (CBC) is useful in diagnosing thrombocytopenia absent radius (TAR) syndrome. However, these patients typically have thumbs present with differing degrees of hypoplasia.
Answer 4: Rigid or flexible bronchoscopy can be used to identify possible tracheoesophageal fistulas which are commonly seen in VACTERL.
Answer 5: A chromosomal breakage test is an assessment of genomic instability and can be used in the diagnosis of Fanconi anemia.
