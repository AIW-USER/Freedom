## Question

What changes are seen in an ageing disc?

## Options

1. Water content decreases with decrease in proteoglycans
2. Water content increases with decrease in proteoglycans
3. Water content decreases with increase in proteoglycans
4. Water content increases with increase in proteoglycans

## Correct option

**1. Water content decreases with decrease in proteoglycans**

## Answer explanation

An intervertebral disc consists of an annulus fibrosus (AF) ring, a nucleus pulposus (NP) core, and two cartilaginous superior and inferior endplates. The outer annulus is made up of highly ordered collagen lamellae in which type I collagen fibers are aligned with elongated fibroblasts . Relative to the outer annulus, the inner annulus is more like cartilage, containing spherical chondrocyte-like cells, and greater amounts of type II collagen and proteoglycans.

The composition of the different regions of the disc differ, with the NP consisting mainly of a high concentration of proteoglycans and water, embedded in a loose collagen network, while the AF has a high concentration of fibrillar collagens, organised into concentric lamellae.

Morphological changes seen in aging intervertebral disc:

- disappearance of the vascular channels originating from the endplate
- densification of the fibers that constitute the annulus fibrosus
- progressive loss of annulus-nucleus distinction
- substitution of the original nuclear tissue by fibrocartilage,
- the appearance of chondrocyte agglomerates in the deep part of the disc,
- cartilaginous hyalinization of the endplate,
- vascularization of the posterior portion of the annulus fibrosus,
- the appearance of fissures in the disc, and the extrusion (in a small quantity) of nuclear material through the endplate (Schmorl's node)

Changes in the extracellular matrix (ECM) of the intervertebral disc with aging:

- Proteoglycan loss is one of the early features of disc degeneration
- Concentrations of sulfated proteoglycans and hyaluronan decrease with aging
- The cell density decreases with age
- Sulphation of GAGs provides the osmotic pressure, necessary for maintaining disc hydration, fall in its degree of sulfation would decrease disc hydration (water content)
- Keratin sulphate(KS) and chondroitin sulphate(CS) are the most abundant GAGs in both AF and NP. Concentration of both decreases with aging.
- The GAG: collagen ratio significantly decreases. Ratio of KS:CS increases with aging.

Answer : A
