## Question

True about histopathology of tennis elbow ( regional orthopedics-elbow- tennis elbow)

## Options

1. Chronic inflammatory pathology with abundant inflammatory cells
2. Inflammatory cells are not seen
3. Angiogenesis with neutrophils surrounding the micro vessels
4. Cellular atypia

## Correct option

**2. Inflammatory cells are not seen**

## Answer explanation

In the majority of cases of tennis elbow extensor carpi radialis brevis (ECRB) is the most commonly affected muscle, but supinator and other wrist extensors such as extensor carpi radialis longus, extensor digitorum, extensor digiti minimi and extensor carpi ulnaris can be involved. Any activity involving excessive and repetitive use of these muscles (repetitive wrist extension) may cause tendinosis. Smoking and obesity have been identified as significant risk factors.

The histological picture here is dominated by dense populations of fibroblasts, vascular hyperplasia and disorganized collagen. This has been termed angiofi-broblastic hyperplasia and is believed to occur in tendons that have been damaged by repetitive microtrauma. Given the absence of inflammatory changes in tennis elbow (absence of inflammatory cells) the terms tendonitis and epicondylitis seem to be misnomers. Thus, it is probably a failed reparative process following repetitive microtrauma rather than active inflammation.

Answer: 2
