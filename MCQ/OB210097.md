## Question

Figures A and B show images from her whole-body PET-CT. She reports that she is concerned about the multiple nodules she has on her skin and subcutaneous tissues. ## Options

1. Undifferentiated pleomorphic sarcoma vs. malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumors
2. Infectious lesions vs. tumors
3. Malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumors vs. benign neurofibromas
4. Radiation-sensitive tumors vs. radiation-resistant tumors
5. Mucinous adenocarcinoma vs. renal cell carcinoma

## Correct option

**3. Malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumors vs. benign neurofibromas**

## Answer explanation

PET-CT can be used to identify malignant transformation of neurofibromas.

Neurofibromatosis is an autosomal dominantly inherited disorder that predisposes the patient to an almost 4x higher risk of cancer. Positron emission tomography (typically F18-FDG PET) can evaluate tumor glucose metabolism. The degree of glucose uptake (based on tissue radioactivity) is quantified in the standard uptake value (SUV). PET is useful in patients with neurofibromatosis who have multiple neurofibromas throughout their bodies as it can detect malignant transformation of benign neurofibromas to malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumors by quantifying an increased glucose metabolism signifying increased proliferation, undifferentiation, and metastasis.

*Derlin et al. compared the effectiveness of whole-body MRI with 18F-FDG PET/CT in detecting malignant transformation in 31 patients with neurofibromatosis.* PET/CT was found to have a sensitivity of 100%, while MRI was 67% sensitive. Tumor size, SUVmax, SUVmean, and heterogeneity were all associated with malignant transformation.

*Van Der Gucht et al. reported on 40 patients with neurofibromatosis type 1 who had lesions evaluated by PET/CT.* **They found** that certain parameters measurable on PET/CT were predictive of malignant transformation. Maximum standard uptake value (SUV>4.5), total lesion glycolysis (TLG>377), and total metabolic tumor volume (TMTV>88 cm3) were predictive of malignant transformation, with the most predictive being total lesion glycolysis. This study demonstrates that TLG and TMTV can be used to identify malignant transformation and predict survival.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Both of these lesions would demonstrate increased uptake on PET/CT, but PET/CT would not distinguish between the two.
Answer 2: Both infection and tumors can have increased PET/CT uptake.
Answer 4: Both radiation-sensitive and radiation-resistant tumors can have FDG avidity.
Answer 5: Both of these tumors are notoriously indolent and may not manifest on PET/CT
