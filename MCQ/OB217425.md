## Question

A 23-year-old male rolled his ankle when he fell playing pickle ball with his grandmother. There is moderate swelling about the ankle with associated ecchymosis. On exam, he has moderate tenderness over the lateral ligamentous complex. During provocative testing he has laxity with anterior drawer when the ankle is dorsiflexed. Stability is achieved, however, if the ankle is plantar flexed. The injured ligament attaches to which bony landmarks?

## Options

1. Anterior margin of the fibula to the lateral neck of the talus
2. Tip of the fibula to the lateral calcaneal tubercle
3. Distal fibula to the lateral tubercle of the posterior talus
4. Anterolateral tibia to anterior distal fibula
5. Posterior edge of the tibial plafond to the proximal edge of the fibular malleolar fossa

## Correct option

**2. Tip of the fibula to the lateral calcaneal tubercle**

## Answer explanation

A positive anterior drawer with dorsiflexion of the ankle is consistent with injury to the calcaneofibular ligament, which traverses from the tip of the fibula to the lateral calcanea tubercle.

Ankle sprains most commonly involve inversion injuries and involve the lateral ligamentous complex. The anterior talofibular ligament (ATFL) is the most commonly injured ligament and can be recognized on exam by a positive anterior drawer test with the ankle in plantar flexion. Conversely, if a positive anterior drawer is appreciated with the ankle in dorsiflexion, the patient most likely suffered a calcaneofibluar ligament (CFL) tear. The CFL extends from the tip of the fibula to the lateral calcaneal tubercle and is the second most common ligament involved in low ankle sprains.

*Matsui et al review the anatomy of anterior talofibular ligament and calcaneofibular ligaments.* **They report** that the fibular origin of the CFL was located on the anterior border of distal fibula at a distance of 5.3-8.5 mm proximal to the tip of the fibula. The calcaneal insertion of the CFL was located at a point 12-13 mm along a line drawn perpendicular to the subtalar joint as indicated by the star in Illustration B. **They conclude** that this is the most detailed anatomic description of the CFL present in the literature.

*Hunt et al review the role of the CFL in ankle instability.* **They report** significantly more inversion of the talus and calcaneus as well as calcaneal medial displacement was seen with weight-bearing inversion after sectioning of the CFL. **They conclude** that consideration should be given to the repair of the CFL given the stability changes appreciated after sectioning.

Illustration A is a schematic demonstrating the lateral ligamentous complex of the ankle. Illustration B is a schematic in which the star represents the insertion of the CFL and f is the subtalar joint.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: This describes the anterior talofibular ligament (ATFL), which would have a positive anterior drawer with the ankle plantarflexed if injured.
Answer 3: This describes the posterior talofibular ligament (PTFL), which is the least common of the lateral ligament complex to be involved.
Answer 4 and 5: These describe the AITFL and transverse tibiofibular ligament, respectively, both of which are parts of the syndesmosis.
