## Question

An 11-year-old female with spinal muscular atrophy presents for preoperative counseling regarding her planned posterior spinal fusion. She is wheelchair-dependent and has a progressive 82º right thoracolumbar curve. Her father is inquiring about what functional changes to expect post-operatively. You tell him that she may experience:

## Options

1. Improved strength in proximal muscle groups
2. Difficulty with sitting in a wheelchair
3. Progressive return of ambulation
4. Improvement of her lower extremity contractures
5. Diminished ability to self-propel her wheelchair

## Correct option

**5. Diminished ability to self-propel her wheelchair**

## Answer explanation

Patients with spinal muscular atrophy undergoing posterior spinal fusion may experience a post-operative reduction in upper extremity function resulting in the need for assistance with gross or fine motor tasks.

Spinal muscular atrophy is a disease of progressive motor dysfunction characterized by proximal muscle weakness and neuromuscular scoliosis. Surgery is indicated for progressive curves that are refractory to bracing. The primary goal of surgical correction in nonambulatory patients with SMA is to improve sitting balance, halt curve progression, and minimize restrictive lung disease. Patients should be counseled that they may experience decreased function of their upper extremities post-operatively. This is likely because acutely lengthening the spinal column puts the already weak shoulder musculature at further mechanical disadvantage, resulting in increased weakness.

*Furumasu and colleagues describe a series of 57 patients with SMA treated with spinal fusion.* Post-operatively, lack of spinal flexibility resulted in a decline in gross motor function and increased use of upper extremity aids. The percentage of patients who could feed independently and perform self-hygiene decreased by 6% and 23%, respectively, and the requirement for mobile arm supports increased by 58%.

*Granata et al. also examined a series of patients with SMA undergoing spinal fusion.* The average scoliotic curve corrected from 106º to 53º. **They found** 20% of patients reported the loss of some movement of the upper limbs, though all but one patient reported satisfaction with their outcome and stated they would choose to have the operation again.

*Bentley and associates reported on a series of 101 patients with neuromuscular scoliosis treated with spinal fusion, 33 of whom had SMA.* Excellent satisfaction was reported by 89.6% of patients, and there was a marked improvement in seating tolerance and length of time seated following surgery.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Spinal fusion has not been shown to improve muscle strength in patients with SMA. It may, in fact, worsen strength in the extremities by lengthening the trunk and altering the lever arm of the extremities.
Answer 2: The primary goal of surgery is to improve seating balance and the ability to sit in a wheelchair.
Answer 3: Spinal fusion in a nonambulatory patient with SMA will not result in regaining the ability to ambulate.
Answer 4: Spinal fusion may resolve issues of pelvic obliquity, however soft tissue contractures of the lower extremities are not affected.
