## Question

An orthopedic surgeon performs a prospective, randomized control trial comparing a new anti-inflammatory medication to standard ibuprofen for treating arthritic pain. The patients are randomly assigned using random sequenced generation with allocation concealment from the treating surgeon. A patient inadvertently discovers they were given the new anti-inflammatory medications prior to their final evaluation. Which type of bias may occur due to loss of patient treatment blinding?

## Options

1. Selection Bias
2. Observer-Expectancy Bias
3. Procedure Bias
4. Recall Bias
5. Publishing Bias

## Correct option

**4. Recall Bias**

## Answer explanation

When patients became aware of which medication they received, they may be more likely to remember specific positive or negative factors that influence outcome scores. This is a form of recall bias.

Bias is generally defined as the non-random systematic error in the design or conduct of a study. While it is most often unintentional, it can be pervasive and detrimental. The ability of a study design to optimize validity while minimizing bias, confounding, and chance is best modeled in the hierarchical levels of evidence (Illustration A). The higher quality of evidence is inversely proportional to the study’s risk of bias, confounding, and random chance. Recall bias occurs when study subjects have different recollection of past experiences, and can be influenced by becoming aware of their treatment or disease. This is unfortunately common in retrospective studies or survey-based studies which are performed after patients already received some form of treatment. Recall bias can be minimized, or effectively eliminated, by performing prospective studies. However, in this example, recall bias was introduced into this patient’s outcomes when they became aware of which treatment they received prior to the final evaluation and patient-reported outcomes.

*Pannucci and colleagues provided a narrative review on the topic of bias, particularly in the Journal of Plastic and Reconstructive Surgery.* **They discuss** that bias can occur in the planning, data collection, analysis, and publication phases of research. **They conclude** that underlying bias is the foundation for the practice of evidence-based medicine and interpreting scientific results.

Kocher and Zurakowski provided a comprehensive review article regarding clinical epidemiology and biostatistics for orthopedic surgeons. They discuss the various study designs, hypothesis testing, outcome assessments, and biostatistics used with real-world examples. They also provide examples of bias and how to minimize their effects in the study design. The authors conclude that an understanding of these concepts is essential both for investigators conducting clinical research and for practitioners interpreting clinical research reports.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Selection bias is when dissimilar groups are compared to each other. In the setting of a randomized control trial, both patient randomization AND allocation concealment helps prevent selection bias. Allocation concealment is when the treating physicians do NOT know which group each patient will be assigned to, eliminating their potential preferences to place patients in one group based on when they enroll.
Answer 2: Observer expectancy bias is a form of confirmation bias where the investigators are looking for results that confirms what they already believe to be true. In this example, if the investigating surgeon is aware of which medications were provided to each patient, observer expectancy bias would be concerning in the interpretation of final results. “Masking” is a technique where either the investigator or study subject (or both) is blinded to the treatment rendered, ultimately preventing observer-expectancy bias AND recall bias.
Answer 3: Procedure bias is when subjects in different groups are not treated the same, which can influence the study’s outcome. For example, if the patients randomized to the new anti-inflammatory medication group also received physical therapy when the standard ibuprofen did not, that would be considered a procedure bias.
Answer 5: Publishing bias refers to the tendency to avoid publishing unfavorable results. This is a considerable concern in publications that were directly funded by an implant company. These companies serve to profit off favorable results and may choose to provide limited information on negative results, or just maximize emphasis on only their positive results.
