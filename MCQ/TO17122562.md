## Question

Which of the following statements is false about treatment in mucopolysaccharidoses?
(FNB Pattern Paper 2014)

## Options

1. Hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (HSCT) has little impact in bone disease
2. In patients <2 years of age with severe MPS I (Hurler), hematopoietic cell transplantation (HCT) is the preferred treatment
3. Enzyme replacement therapy has little role in alleviating cognitive decline and spinal cord compression
4. There is a relatively low incidence of graft failure and (graft versus host disease) GVHD after HSCT

## Correct option

**4. There is a relatively low incidence of graft failure and (graft versus host disease) GVHD after HSCT**

## Answer explanation

Mucopolysaccharidosis type I (MPS-I) is an inherited disorder of metabolism, characterized biochemically by a deficiency of the lysosomal enzyme α-L-iduronidase, with the consequent accumulation of the glycosaminoglycans (GAGs) dermatan sulfate and heparan sulfate. Clinically, there is progressive multisystem involvement including degenerative joint disease, and in the more severely affected patients, mental retardation and cardiomyopathy, leading to progressive disability and death. Bone marrow transplantation (BMT) has been shown to slow down or reverse some of the features of this disease, but not others. Patients with MPS I have a reduced life expectancy. Without treatment, patients with the most severe form of the disease have a median survival of 6.8 years.

Orthopedic manifestations: Skeletal alterations typical of MPS I are multiple dysostosis, which include thickened calvarium, J-shaped Sella turcica, odontoid process hypoplasia, thoraco-lumbar kyphosis, Atlanto-axial instability, thoracic asymmetry, hypoplasia of the antero-superior portion of the vertebral body, platyspondyly, small and widened clavicles, elevated broadened scapula’s, incompletely formed ischium and pubis, acetabular dysplasia, and coxa valga that can evolve to hip subluxation . Thoracic-lumbar kyphosis (gibbus deformity) is often the first clinically evident manifestation of the disease, which occurs at approximately 6 months of age. Carpal tunnel syndrome, stiffening of the interphalangeal joint, hook finger, and gripped hand are frequent, and impair hand function.

Bone marrow replacement (BMT) therapy: The term BMT has been largely replaced by the broader term hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (HSCT). HSCT has little impact in bone disease, probably because of poor penetration of the enzyme in chondrocytes and failure to correct or replace osteocytes. Improvements in the CNS are slower because of turnover of microglia and their slow replacement by donor-derived cells. Neuropsychological evolution is closely correlated with age at HSCT. Children who undergo transplantation at <24 months of age demonstrated significantly better development than older patients.

Most of the positive effects occur within the first few years after HSCT and improve quality of life. However, with time there is a relatively high incidence of graft failure (approximately 30%), with reconstitution of autologous marrow that may require further transplant. Failure of the graft and graft versus host disease (GVHD) are important obstacles to procedure success.

Enzyme replacement therapy (ERT):

ERT has been effective in decreasing GAG storage in the viscera and the heart, reducing or normalizing GAG urinary excretion, and improving joint mobility, endurance, and respiratory function. On the other hand, it has become clear that ERT has a limited impact on other tissues and organs, such as cardiac valves, bone, and cornea. Of even greater importance for the outcome of patients is the inability of the recombinant enzymes used for ERT to cross the blood–brain barrier and to cure or alleviate cognitive decline and spinal cord compression, especially in MPS I, II, III, VI, and VII in which neurological involvement is often a critical and debilitating manifestation. Enzyme replacement therapy (ERT) is approved in the United States, European Union, and several other countries for patients with MPS I, MPS II, MPS IVA, MPS VI, and MPS VII. Indications vary across the MPS, but generally ERT is used in patients with moderate-to-severe disease or clinical complications. The exception is patients <2 years of age with severe MPS I (Hurler), for whom hematopoietic cell transplantation (HCT) is preferred if there is a suitable donor.

Answer: 4
