## Question

A 36 year-old male presents with a moderate sized, painless, soft-tissue mass in his mid-thigh. Examination of locoregional lymph nodes is negative. Radiographs of the femur are normal. A biopsy specimen is shown in Figure A. Reverse transcription-polymerase chain reaction-based assay detected a SYT-SSX fusion transcript. What additional investigations would be most appropriate?

![[q5775-a-ss2.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Observation only
2. Ultrasound mass; CT scan of pelvis, abdomen, chest and spine
3. MRI of femur; CT scan of chest
4. Whole-body bone scan; CT scan of femur, chest and spine
5. Whole-body bone scan; MRI of femur, pelvis, chest and brain

## Correct option

**3. MRI of femur; CT scan of chest**

## Answer explanation

The appropriate workup of a suspected soft-tissue sarcoma should include examination of locoregional lymph nodes, biopsy, and imaging of the affected bone (radiographs and MRI femur) + common site of metastasis (chest radiograph, chest CT scan). In addition, cytogenetic testing of the biopsied lesion should be performed to confirm the suspected diagnosis (e.g., (X;18) translocation and SYT-SSX fusion gene for synovial sarcoma).

Synovial sarcoma is an aggressive soft tissue sarcoma and can appear heterogeneous on MRI. In 90% of cases, it results from the chromosomal translocation t(X:18). This yields the fusion protein SYT-SSX. The most common location for synovial sarcoma is the lower extremity, and the most common site for metastasis is the lung. Therefore, the diagnostic work up of this lesion requires examine for regional lymphadenopathy, as well as advanced imaging of the lung.

*Gilbert et al. reviewed soft-tissue sarcomas including their presentation and treatment.* **The authors state** that an MRI scan should be performed before the removal of ANY deep soft-tissue mass, or large (>5cm) superficial masses. If regional lymphadenopathy is detected clinically, the MRI scan should also include the pelvis. Findings of inguinal and pelvic nodes on MRI means a follow-up CT scan of the abdomen would be required to look for any para-aortic lymphadenopathy.

*Sandberg et al. reviewed the the diagnosis of synovial sarcoma.* **They showed** that the t(X:18) is a reliable cytogenetic test, as this test has been shown to best correlate with synovial sarcomas, even amongst rare variants. Reverse transcription-polymerase chain reaction-based assay may also help with detecting SYT-SSX fusion genes.

Figure A shows a biopsy specimen of a monophasic synovial sarcoma with the typical spindle cell component, which can look deceptively bland. There is subtle pleomorphism and nuclei appear slightly large. The background shows atrophic muscle. Illustrations A and B show a coronal T2-weighted image and axial TI MR image of this lesion. These images are consistent with synovial sarcoma.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The biopsy specimen and SYT-SSX fusion gene are characteristic of synovial sarcoma, which should not be treated with observation only
Answer 2: An ultrasound examination can confirm the size of the lesion and its relationship to the fascia. However, ultrasound images will not be able to give any reliable information on the underlying pathology and clinicians should not be falsely reassured by ultrasound findings
Answer 4, 5: Bone scans have no role in the preoperative investigation of a soft tissue sarcoma.
