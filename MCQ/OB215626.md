## Question

Figure A is the radiograph of a patient with a metabolic bone disease. His past medical history is significant for several long bone fractures, cranial nerve palsies, and persistent low back pain. Which of the following is the underlying cause of this patient's condition?

![[q215626-a-osteopetrosis.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Osteoclast inability to create an acidic medium from Howship lacunae
2. Inability to excrete phosphate from the kidneys
3. Increase in bone forming extracellular matrix proteins and paracrine factors
4. Mutation resulting in abnormal collagen cross-linking and decrease in type 1 collagen
5. Mutation in fibrillin-1 (FBN1) gene

## Correct option

**1. Osteoclast inability to create an acidic medium from Howship lacunae**

## Answer explanation

This patient has osteopetrosis. In osteopetrosis, there is an inability of the osteoclast to acidify Howship's lacuna due to carbonic anhydrase II or chloride channel dysfunction.

Osteoclasts secrete hydrochloric acid and enzymes through a ruffled border facing bone, which creates a local hypodense pocket called a Howship lacuna. In osteopetrosis, the ability of the osteoclast to create an acidic medium from Howship lacunae is compromised due to carbonic anhydrase II or chloride channel dysfunction. Ultimately, this congenital metabolic bone disease results in defective osteoclastic resorption of immature bone leading to dense bone and obliterated medullary canals. Patients often present with an increased frequency of long bone fractures, cranial nerve palsies, and low back pain.

*Landa et al. reviewed osteopetrosis.* **They report** that the various clinical manifestations ultimately are caused by genetic mutations affecting acidification of Howship's lacuna. **They noted** that common radiographic features include a generalized sclerosis and a rugger jersey spine, while medical problems include cranial nerve palsies and pancytopenia. **They concluded** that patients typically present with such orthopedic problems as frequent fractures, coxa vara, osteoarthritis, and osteomyelitis.

*Hasan et al. retrospectively reviewed osteopetrosis as a rare inheritable genetic disorder.* **They report** that abnormal osteoclast activity results in defective bone resorption with patients having varied clinical presentations. Furthermore, **they noted** that genetic studies showed multiple genes to be involved and varied patterns of inheritance in different types of osteopetrosis. **They concluded** that these genetic factors and inheritance pattern should be discussed with patients.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Hyperparathyroidism and secondary hyperphosphatemia are caused by hypocalcemia and lack of phosphate excretion by damaged kidneys
Answer 3: Osteoblasts produce extracellular matrix proteins and paracrine factors that together support formation of bone tissue
Answer 4: Osteogenesis Imperfecta is a common congenital disorder caused by a mutation in COL1A1 or COL1A2 genes resulting in abnormal collagen cross-linking and overall decrease in type 1 collagen
Answer 5: Marfan Syndrome is a congenital connective tissue disorder caused by a mutation the fibrillin-1 gene
