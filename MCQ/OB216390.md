## Question

Which of the following best describes paradoxical translation as demonstrated in some TKA designs?

## Options

1. Posterior translation of the tibiofemoral contact point as the knee is taken from extension to flexion
2. Anterior translation of the tibiofemoral contact point as the knee is taken from extension to flexion
3. Translation of the patellar component laterally so it loses contact with the medial femoral component during flexion
4. Medial translation of the tibiofemoral contact point as the knee is taken from extension to flexion secondary to the screw home mechanism
5. Lateral translation of the tibiofemoral contact point as the knee is taken from extension to flexion secondary to the screw home mechanism

## Correct option

**2. Anterior translation of the tibiofemoral contact point as the knee is taken from extension to flexion**

## Answer explanation

Posterior cruciate retaining (CR) TKA designs may demonstrate “paradoxical translation” which involves anterior translation of the flexion axis as the knee is flexed.

Native knee kinematics differ from those observed with various TKA designs. As the native knee is taken from extension to flexion, the center of rotation of the femur translates posteriorly, which allows for greater knee flexion. This motion is termed “femoral rollback”, and may not always occur in CR TKA. With most TKA designs the resting contact point in full extension is more posterior than that of the native knee, mainly because of the lack of an ACL. As the knee begins to flex, the contact point sometimes shifts anteriorly, which is opposite of that seen in the native knee. This finding is termed “paradoxical translation” and is seen most commonly with CR TKA designs.

*Dennis et al review anteroposterior femorotibial translation of total knee arthroplasty.* **They report** a more posterior femorotibial contact point with the knee in full extension with anterior translation occurring in midlfexion and terminal flexion for CR TKA. **They conclude** that the abnormal anterior translation observed in CR TKA designs may be a factor in premature poly wear observed in many retrieval studies.

*Angerame et al review total knee arthroplasty kinematics.* **They report** that CR TKA designs exhibited paradoxical anterior translation from extension to 40 degrees of flexion after which posterior translation was noted. **They conclude** that the differences in these findings with that of a native knee are likely due to the ACL deficiency.

*Koga performed a 3-dimensional motion analysis to better understand knee kinematics.* **They identified** two types of motions - straight femoral rollback and paradoxical rollback during flexion. **They conclude** that the observed paradoxical translation was related to decreased tension on the PCL.

*Yoshiya et al reviewed in vivo kinematics of CR and PS knees.* *They report on twenty patients who underwent paired bilateral TKA by a single manufacturer and a single surgeon.* **They conclude** that in CR knee designs a paradoxical anterior translation occurs during weight bearing between 30 and 60 degrees of knee flexion.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Posterior translation or, "femoral rollback," is a part of normal knee kinematics.
Answer 3: Lateral translation of the patellar component results from increasing the Q-angle and is not related to paradoxical translation.
Answer 4 & 5: No significant medial/lateral translation of the tibiofemoral contact point occurs with flexion/extension. The screw home mechanism describes tibial external rotation in the last 15 degrees of knee extension and is not associated with paradoxical translation.
