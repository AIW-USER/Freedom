## Question

A 61-year-old female presents to the clinic for her 12-week follow-up appointment following left index finger distal interphalangeal joint arthrodesis secondary to degenerative osteoarthritis. She reports continued sensitivity to the tip of her index finger. Using an implant with which of the following features is most likely responsible for her continued symptoms?

## Options

1. Headless screw type
2. Cannulated screw type
3. Non-cannulated screw type
4. Excessive screw diameter
5. Excessive screw length

## Correct option

**5. Excessive screw length**

## Answer explanation

This 61-year-old female underwent left index finger DIP joint arthrodesis. Excessive screw length, which can lead to prominent hardware at the tip of the distal phalanx, can result in fingertip hypersensitivity (Answer 5).

Distal interphalangeal (DIP) and proximal interphalangeal (PIP) joint osteoarthritis of the fingers are ubiquitous in orthopedic practice. However, the majority remain untreated secondary to their asymptomatic nature and limited functional impact. Nevertheless, surgery is indicated for chronically painful or unstable joints and can come in many forms, such as k-wire fixation, bioabsorbable implants, plates, and headless compression screws. The most commonly utilized implants are the latter option, as they have the highest fusion rates of the available surgical options. Headless compression screws are not without risks, however, as too large of a screw diameter can risk fracture or nail bed injury. In contrast, fingertip hypersensitivity can present in those with prominent hardware at the tip of the distal phalanx.

*Matsumoto et al. performed a retrospective review of 89 interphalangeal joint arthrodeses (60 patients) using a specific type of headless compression screw purposed for DIP and thumb IP joints ("reverse fix nail" [RFN]).* **The authors noted** a 97% union rate, with an average time to union being ten weeks. One case exhibited hardware prominence after being incompletely buried into the distal phalanx. **The authors believe** the RFN performs similarly to more traditional headless compression screws and may portend a lower risk for hardware prominence.

*Cox et al. performed a retrospective review of 48 digits in 28 patients undergoing arthrodesis for the DIP or thumb IP joint utilizing commercial headless variable pitch screws of smaller diameter compared to non-cannulated implants.* **The authors noted** no cases of nail deformity, clinical malalignment, or fingertip hypersensitivity. Radiographic union was noted in 94% of patients by the time of final follow-up (12 months), with a 9% complication rate, which included the three nonunions and one intraoperative distal phalanx fracture. **The authors conclude** that this type of screw achieves satisfactory fusion rates with a modest complication rate and may be advantageous given their smaller size.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Headless screw type reduces the risk for prominent hardware and thus fingertip hypersensitivity.
Answers 2 & 3: Cannulated vs. non-cannulated screw type has not been shown to make an impact on fingertip hypersensitivity.
Answer 4: Excessive screw diameter increases the risk of fracture and nailbed deformity.
