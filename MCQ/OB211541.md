## Question

A team of researchers is attempting to conduct a study to evaluate genetic variants among patients with osteoporosis in order to determine who would benefit from a specific pharmacologic treatment. In order to evaluate these genetic variants, they discuss genomic sequencing techniques that could be used. Which of the following is true?

## Options

1. Whole-exome sequencing identifies genetic variants found in the non-coding region of genes which affect protein function
2. Whole-exome sequencing measures the expression levels of genes to determine relative abundance of nucleic acid sequences in the target
3. Genome-wide association sequencing determines the likelihood that genetic material came from a particular individual or group
4. Genome-wide association sequencing uses single-nucleotide polymorphisms to evaluate mutational variability among patients of interest
5. Whole-genome association sequencing involves sequencing of only the protein-coding regions of genes in a genome in order to identify specific genetic variants that alter protein sequences

## Correct option

**4. Genome-wide association sequencing uses single-nucleotide polymorphisms to evaluate mutational variability among patients of interest**

## Answer explanation

Genome-wide association sequencing (GWAS) uses single-nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) to evaluate mutational variability among patients with a phenotype (or disease) of interest.

There are multiple technologies available that identify genetic variants. The use of genotyping is increasingly being investigated in order to determine not only causal mutation(s) for specific phenotypes but also to predict how an individual patient may respond to a specific treatment or be at risk for complications following an intervention. Whole-genome sequencing (WGS) is the process of determining the entirety, or near entirety, of the DNA sequence of an organism's genome, including coding and non-coding DNA. The purpose of WGS is to identify a disease-causing mutation directly. Similar in terms of principle, whole-exome sequencing (WES) allows sequencing of only the protein-coding regions of genes in a genome (known as the exome). The human exome consists of 1% of the human genome but harbors 85% of disease-related variants. The cost of exome sequencing is typically only one-sixth that of whole genome sequencing. The goal of this approach is to identify genetic variants that alter protein sequences, and to do this at a much lower cost than whole-genome sequencing. These variants can be responsible for both Mendelian (i.e., single allele mutations) and polygenic diseases, such as osteoarthritis. As opposed to identifying a specific genetic variant in an individual with a disease of interest using WES or WGS, GWAS has been utilized to evaluate differences between multiple individuals with the same phenotype. The principal goal of GWAS is to classify a patient’s genome in relation to their disease in order to tailor treatment and predict outcomes and complications that would be impossible from clinical risk factors alone.

*Lacey et al.* *2014 performed a comparison study between WGS and WES for family-based association analysis.* **They reported** that the increased cost of multiple testing needed for WGS counteracted the benefits and resulted in a higher false discovery rate. **They concluded** that WES is a cost-effective way to identify disease-related variants, while WGS has the potential to identify essential variants in regulatory regions typically inaccessible for WES.

Matzko and Smith 2012 provide a review of orthogenomics and the use of different techniques for studying genome-phenotype associations. Specifically, they review the use of single nucleotide polymorphisms, haplotype and quantitative trait loci analysis. They reported on using orthogenomics in the study of multiple conditions, including osteoporosis, osteoarthritis, and spinal deformity. They conclude that more work is required in order to facilitate improvement in clinical orthopedic practice.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: WES allows sequencing of ONLY the protein-coding regions (i.e., exons) of genes in a genome in order to identify genetic variants that alter protein sequences. These variants can be responsible for both Mendelian (i.e., single allele mutations) and polygenic diseases, such as osteoarthritis). Introns are NON-coding portions of DNA.
Answer 2: This describes DNA microarrays (not WES), which can be used to quantify DNA or RNA that may or may not be translated into proteins. The process of measuring gene expression via cDNA is called expression analysis or expression profiling.
Answer 3: This describes DNA profiling (not GWAS), which is the process of determining an individual’s DNA characteristics, often used in forensics for criminal investigations. It does not contain additional information on genetic relationships, origin or susceptibility to specific disease.
Answer 5: This describes WES.
