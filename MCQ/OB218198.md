## Question

A 22-year-old college soccer player sustains a twisting injury to the knee during a match and presents to the clinic with an MRI showing a tear of the medial meniscus. Which of the following arthroscopic tear patterns is biomechanically equivalent to a posterior meniscal root avulsion injury?

## Options

1. Bucket handle tear
2. Complete radial tear
3. Flap tear
4. Longitudinal tear
5. Parrot beak tear

## Correct option

**2. Complete radial tear**

## Answer explanation

A complete radial tear disrupts the circumferential fibers of the meniscus, creating the functional equivalent of a total meniscectomy. Posterior meniscal root avulsions cause an analogous functional loss by compromising the meniscus's ability to convert axial load to hoop stress.

Meniscal tears occur in several well-defined tear patterns (Illustration A). Of increasing interest has been the growing body of evidence suggesting that a posterior root tear of the medial meniscus can lead to meniscal extrusion, which serves as a precursor to the development of (sometimes rapid) degenerative knee arthritis. A complete posterior root tear of the medial meniscus theoretically results in a functional meniscectomy, owing to a complete disruption of the circumferential fibers of the meniscus. This prohibits the generation of hoop stresses, which are crucial to meniscal function. Without a functional meniscus, the articular cartilage of the knee is exposed to pathologic loads and subsequently degenerates in an accelerated fashion. A complete radial tear, in similar fashion, provides a complete disruption of the circumferential meniscal fibers and resultant hoop stresses, allowing for a biomechanically similar degradation pathway.

Allaire, et al. provide a review of the biomechanical consequences of a tear of the posterior root of the medial meniscus in a cadaveric model. Nine cadaver knees were used to show that a posterior root tear of the medial meniscus caused a 25% increase in peak contact pressures compared with that found in the intact condition, equivalent peak pressures to that of a total meniscectomy. Posterior root repair restored normal peak contact pressures. The authors concluded that root repair was successful in restoring joint biomechanics to within normal limits.

Padalecki, et al. studied the biomechanical consequences of a complete radial tear adjacent to the medial meniscus posterior root attachment site. The authors used 6 cadaveric knees to show that posterior horn radial tears adjacent to the medial meniscus root that extend to the meniscocapsular junction can lead to derangement of the loading profiles of the medial compartment that are similar to a root avulsion. They concluded that repair of these radial tears with an in situ pull-out technique restored joint mechanics to the intact state.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: A bucket handle tear is a sub-type of vertical tear that has a tendency to displace into the notch.
Answer 3: A flap tear is an oblique tear that only partially disrupts the circumferential fibers of the meniscus.
Answer 4: A longitudinal, or vertical tear, by definition occurs in line with the fibers of the meniscus and does not cause complete disruption of the circumferential hoop stresses.
Answer 5: A parrot beak tear is another variant of oblique/flap tear that can cause mechanical symptoms, but again only partially disrupts the circumferential fibers of the meniscus.
