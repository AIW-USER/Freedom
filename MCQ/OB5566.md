![[q5566-b-fig-b-histo.jpg]]

## Options

1. bone scan, MR of the knee, and chest radiograph
2. bone scan, MR of the knee and tibia, and CT of the chest
3. whole body FDG PET / PET-CT scan
4. bone scan, MR of the knee and tibia, CT of the chest, and reactive zone biopsy
5. whole body FDG PET/PET CT scan, and MR of the femur/knee/tibia

## Correct option

**2. bone scan, MR of the knee and tibia, and CT of the chest**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a conventional, high-grade intramedullary osteosarcoma. Staging should include local staging (MR of the knee and entire involved bone, i.e. tibia) and global staging (bone scan and CT chest).

Osteosarcoma workup should include baseline laboratory values to assess organ function prior to chemotherapy (CBC, BMP, liver function, urinalysis), echocardiography (baseline cardiac function prior to doxorubicin therapy), and ALP and LDH levels (for osteoblastic and osteoclastic activity, both of which portend a poor prognosis). Staging tests include CT chest, bone scan, and MRI. The utility of PET remains to be determined.

*Kaste reviewed imaging in pediatric bone sarcomas.* *She opined that even chest CT underestimates the number of pulmonary lesions detected surgically by 35%.* Intramedullary tumor extent is best seen on longitudinal T1-weighted noncontrast images. Osteosarcoma decreases minimally in size following chemotherapy, while Ewing sarcoma can respond to chemotherapy with a decrease in tumor size of >25%, indicating favorable response.

*Messerschmitt et al reviewed osteosarcoma.* Multi-agent chemotherapy aims to avoid chemoresistance and increase tumor necrosis. Survival can be up to 75% with resection of primary tumor and solitary lung metastases. Long term survival is 90% for parosteal osteosarcoma, 80% for periosteal osteosarcoma, and 60-75% for localized osteosarcoma (all variants combined).

Figure A shows a permeative, lytic lesion in the proximal tibia. Figure B is a high-power photomicrograph showing "lacey osteoid" (characteristic of woven bone being laid down by the tumor), stromal cells with cellular atypia and high nuclear to cytoplasmic ratio.

Incorrect Answers
Answer 1: Chest radiography is not as sensitive as chest CT for detection of small pulmonary metastases. MR imaging must encompass the primary tumor site AND the entire bone in which the tumor resides (the tibia) in order to identify skip lesions. Skip lesions occur in 2-6% of high grade osteosarcomas.
Answers 3 and 5: FDG PET is superior to bone scan for detection of metastases in Ewing sarcoma. But in osteosarcoma, bone scan is superior. PET/PET-CT is unable to detect pulmonary nodules <5-9mm in size. Even if PET/PET-CT were used, a spiral chest CT is still necessary. However, PET/PET-CT is superior to CT and MRI in detecting skeletal and soft tissue metastases.
Answer 4: A diagnostic biopsy has already been obtained. A biopsy of the reactive zone would show a reactive cellular response and is both non-diagnostic and unnecessary.
