## Question

You recently completed a hip and knee replacement fellowship. You start practice in South Florida in a predominately black non-Hispanic (BNH) and Hispanic area. When discussing total hip and knee arthroplasties, which of the following is true amongst ethnic and cultural minorities compared with White non-Hispanic (WNH)?

## Options

1. BNH have shorter length of stay compared with WNH but a greater length of stay compared with Hispanics
2. BNH have decreased 30-day readmission compared with both WNH and Hispanics
3. Hispanic have a greater 30-day readmission compared with BNH but a lower readmission compared with WNH
4. Hispanics have a lower rate of discharge to a nursing facility compared with both BNH and WNH
5. WNH have a shorter length of stay compared with both BNH and Hispanics

## Correct option

**5. WNH have a shorter length of stay compared with both BNH and Hispanics**

## Answer explanation

BNH and Hispanic patients have shown significant increases in length of stay, 30-day readmission, and discharge to a nursing facility when their place of origin was home.

Disparities in outcomes after total joint arthroplasty (TJA) have been identified in ethnic and cultural minorities. BNH and Hispanic patients have shown significant increases in length of stay, 30-day readmission, and discharge to a nursing facility when their place of origin was home. Studies have demonstrated increased length of stay and non-home discharges in BNH patients when compared with WNH patients despite BNH patients being significantly younger.

*Dharmasukrit et al. reviewed race/ethnicity as a potential moderator of the associations of frailty and functional status with arthroplasty outcomes.* **They reported** that compared to WNH, BNH patients had higher odds for all outcomes after TKA and for adverse discharge/longer LOS after THA. **They noted** similar associations for Hispanics. **They concluded** that disparities for BNH and Hispanic patients persist for readmission, adverse discharge, and LOS.

*Stone et al. reviewed the influence of race on length of stay, discharge disposition, and complications requiring reoperation following TJA.* **They reported** that BNH patients undergoing TJA were younger with longer length of stay and a higher rate of nonhome discharge. **They concluded** that BNH was identified as an independent risk factor.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1-4: BNH and Hispanic patients have shown significant increases in length of stay, 30-day readmission, and discharge to a nursing facility when their place of origin was home.
