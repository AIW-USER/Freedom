## Options

## Correct option

**1. The measurements are with the normal limits for the age and pathology.**

## Answer explanation

The patient has underlying physiologic genu varum and will likely go on to improve and resolve with further growth. The Drennan angle (MD angle) is within normal limits.

 Genu varum, also known as bowlegs, is a common presenting complaint in a pediatric orthopaedic and can be characterized as physiologic or pathologic.

Cover up test:

The cover-up test is a useful screening to assess the alignment of the upper part of the lower leg in children presenting with bow legs between the ages of 1 and 3 years. The lower extremity is positioned with the patella forward and the lower leg and foot are covered by the examiner’s hand. A positive test (bowing in the upper tibia) or neutral test (a straight thigh–upper leg axis) is an indication for radiographic evaluation. A negative test (slight valgus at the upper tibia) indicates physiologic bowing. A positive cover-up test has a high sensitivity; a negative cover-up test has a high specificity and negative predictive value

![[TO17138299-2.png]]

Physiologic genu varum presents in patients under 2 years of age and is a consequence of angular relationship between the femur and the tibia. Physiologic genu varum will progress to genu valgum followed by normal adult physiologic valgus by age 7. This is depicted well in the below Salenius curve.

![[TO17138299-3.png]]

 Pathologic genu varum can present because of tibia vara or a varus deformity of the proximal tibial known as Blount's disease. This is often seen after the age of two and is evident on radiographs by medial metaphyseal fragmentation, depression, and beaking.

Metaphyseal-diaphyseal angle greater than 16 is considered abnormal and has a high chance of progression. Metaphyseal-diaphyseal angle less than 10 degrees has a 95% chance of natural resolution of bowing.

Figure shows an AP image of a skeletally immature child with a normal metaphyseal-diaphyseal angle and no beaking of the distal femur and tibia.

Answer: 1
