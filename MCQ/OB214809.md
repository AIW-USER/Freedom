## Question

A 4-year-old boy has had an isolated painful limp for the past month. He is diagnosed with Legg-Calve-Perthes disease (LCPD). His radiographs confirm group B lateral pillar classification staging. Which of the following best describes his prognosis at skeletal maturity?

## Options

1. A 40% chance of a good outcome
2. An incongruous hip joint at maturity
3. An aspherical, painless hip at maturity
4. An 80% chance of a good outcome
5. Rapid recovery with minimal sequelae

## Correct option

**4. An 80% chance of a good outcome**

## Answer explanation

Children with LCP who are age < 6 with Lateral pillar classification A or B have an 80% chance of a good outcome at skeletal maturity based on the Stulberg classification.

Legg-Calve-Perthes (LCP) is an idiopathic avascular necrosis of the proximal femoral epiphysis, most commonly affecting males between the ages of 4 to 8 years of age. LCP is most often unilateral but 12% of patients do have bilateral hip involvement (with each hip at a different stage of involvement). The most commonly used classification for LCP is the lateral pillar (Herring) classification based on the height of the lateral pillar of the femoral epiphysis with group A maintaining full epiphysis height, group B maintaining > 50% of the lateral pillar height, group B/C consistent of narrowing of the pillar by 2-3 mm with height > 50%, and group C having less than 50% of height maintained. The most important variables for favorable prognosis are age < 6 at the time of diagnosis, Group A or B lateral pillar classification, and spherical femoral head at the time of skeletal maturity.

*Schoenecker et al. performed a radiographic review of 109 patients with unilateral LCP, all of whom had experienced the onset of symptoms prior to their 6th birthday.* *The average age at final follow-up was 12 years.* *A change in Mose rating between assessments at healing and final follow-up occurred in 16% of patients, with most of them showing improvement, supporting the concept that femoral heads in young children have the capacity to remodel.*

*Rosenfeld et al. performed a clinical and radiographic review of 160 patients affected by LCP.* According to the lateral pillar classification, 7 were group A, 101 group B, 27 group B/C, and 29 group C. **The authors noted** that using the Stulberg classification, there were 131 Class I or II (good) results (80%), 14 Class-III (fair) results (9%), and 19 Class-IV (poor) results (11%). **The authors noted** that the lateral pillar classification was highly correlated with the outcome with only lateral pillar group B/C and group C hips having a poor prognosis. **The authors also noted** that treatment was not found to influence the outcome. **The authors concluded** that the prognosis for patients with lateral pillar group A and group B with the onset of LCP before the age of 6 is favorable, with 80% having a good result at skeletal maturity.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Patients with lateral pillar classification group A or B diagnosed at age < 6 have an 80% chance of a good outcome
Answers 2 and 3: Patients with lateral pillar classification A or B diagnosed at age < 6 have an 80% chance of a good outcome defined as a "spherical, congruent" hip at skeletal maturity.
Answer 5: While LCP is a self-limiting process, it can take 2-5 years to remodel and develop resolution of symptoms.
