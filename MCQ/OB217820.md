## Question

As the new arthroplasty surgeon at the local hospital, you meet with the OR coordinator and scrub techs to review your preferences. When it comes to cemented total knee arthroplasty, they ask which type of cement you wish to use, high viscosity or low viscosity. Which of the following is true of high viscosity bone cement compared to low viscosity?

## Options

1. Longer mixing time
2. Longer working time
3. Higher incidence of pulmonary complications
4. Lower rates of aseptic loosening
5. Improved bone penetration

## Correct option

**2. Longer working time**

## Answer explanation

High viscosity cement has a longer working time when compared to low viscosity bone cement and is more "doughy" after being mixed.

There has been a trend in the last decade towards the use of high viscosity bone cement for total knee arthroplasty. Proponents of its use highlight the longer working time as well as shorter mixing and waiting times. The working time is important, as it's the portion of the setting up process in which the cement is manageable for cementing components into place. Those with resistance to high viscosity products state their concern about bone penetration and the potential complication of aseptic loosening given the less liquid nature of the cement. There have been conflicting reports regarding bone penetration, aseptic loosening, and all-cause revision rates with the use of high viscosity cement, however, its use continues to rise amongst surgeons in the United States.

*Kelly et al review the trends in the use of high viscosity cement in patients undergoing TKA in the United States.* **They report** a 15% increase in the use of high viscosity cement during the 7-year study period with a precipitous decrease in the use of low viscosity products. **They conclude** that with increasing adoption rates, further studies are needed to evaluate the rates of aseptic loosening.

*Dinh et al review intrusion characteristics of high viscosity bone cement on the tibia.* **They report** no differences in cement penetration between high viscosity cement when negative pressure application was performed, however, they did note differences with finger packing between several different manufacturers. **They conclude** that the combination of different bone cement formulations and the cementing technique has a significant effect on cement penetration into the cut bone surface.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: High viscosity cement has a shorter mixing time.
Answer 3: No difference in pulmonary complications has been observed between high and low viscosity cement use in TKA.
Answer 4: There is conflicting data on whether or not high viscosity cement is associated with higher, not lower, rates of aseptic loosening compared to low viscosity cement.
Answer 5: High viscosity cement has equivalent or less bone penetration than low viscosity products, not improved penetration.
