## Question

Which of the following players should be allowed to return to play on the same day they sustain an injury?

## Options

1. A 15-year-old male soccer player who is struck in the head by an opponent during the course of play and experiences some initial confusion, which resolves completely shortly thereafter. He has no headache, no memory loss and is alert & oriented to person, place and time.
2. An elite 19-year-old female basketball player who is elbowed in the head, has a headache, no loss of consciousness, no memory loss and is alert and oriented.
3. A 21-year-old male professional football player who has a helmet-on-helmet collision with an opposing player and is complaining of weakness and paresthesias in both of his hands.
4. A 12-year-old female baseball player who experiences feels "foggy" after slamming into the outfield while attempting to catch a fly ball and cannot recall the event.
5. A 16-year-old male rugby player who experiences numbness, tingling and weakness of his left arm after being tackled to the ground with a normal physical exam and complete resolution of symptoms by the time he gets to the sideline.

## Correct option

**5. A 16-year-old male rugby player who experiences numbness, tingling and weakness of his left arm after being tackled to the ground with a normal physical exam and complete resolution of symptoms by the time he gets to the sideline.**

## Answer explanation

The 16-year-old male rugby player who developed transient unilateral paresthesias and weakness of his left upper extremity likely sustained a transient brachial plexus neuropraxia, more commonly referred to as a "stinger". He may return to play the same day as he has no signs or symptoms of a concussion and has a normal physical exam and no residual symptoms.

A concussion is a disturbance in brain function caused by a direct or indirect force to the head. It results in a variety of non-specific signs and/or symptoms including headache, unsteadiness, confusion, loss of consciousness, change in personality and amnesia. The acute presentation of concussion as well as the diagnosis of concussion is often straightforward and obvious, but it can also be subtle and difficult to discern. A number of standardized assessment tools have been developed to assist clinicians with sideline diagnosis (i.e., Maddocks Score) and on-going evaluation (i.e., Sport Concussion Assessment Tool 3 [SCAT3]) of patients with a concussion.

*McCrory et al. published a consensus statement on concussion in sport outlining the current understanding and evidence based management of concussions.* Although **they found** that there was a dearth of high-level evidence regarding optimal management of concussions they unanimously agreed that no return to play on the day of concussive injury should occur.

*Putukian reviewed the diagnosis and on-field management of acute sport-related concussions.* **She concluded** that although most (80%–90%) sport-related concussions resolve within 7 to 10 days, for some of these injuries the time for complete recovery takes much longer. As such, **she suggested** it may be useful to consider a team approach to concussion management, with a variety of tools for evaluation and diagnosis, and consultants for treatment and management.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1, 2 and 4: All of these players have signs or symptoms of concussion. As a result, none of these players would be cleared to return to play the day of their injury.
Answer 3: This athlete will require a thorough neurological work-up to rule out a spinal cord injury and will not be able to return to play the day of their injury.
