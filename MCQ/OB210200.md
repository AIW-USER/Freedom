## Answer explanation

The clinical presentation is vertebral osteomyelitis involving two vertebral bodies with neurologic deficits. Treatment will be a two-level anterior corpectomy that must be supplemented with posterior instrumentation and fusion.

A cervical spinal epidural abscess is a rare but serious condition often necessitating early surgical management due to the poor outcomes of medical management alone. This is partially due to the reduced space available for the cord when compared to epidural abscesses in the lumbar spine, which are often successfully managed with IV pathogen-directed antibiotics alone. Surgical management may consist of spinal cord decompression, debridement, restoration of sagittal alignment, and stabilization if needed. A ventral approach is often utilized in the case of a ventrally compressive abscess, and anterior-posterior stabilization/fusion is often utilized in cases of multi-level involvement when structural integrity or sagittal alignment is compromised (instrumentation may be placed posteriorly to avoid bacterial seeding).

*Ghobrial et al. performed a retrospective database review of 40 consecutive patients with cervical spinal epidural abscesses managed surgically.* The majority of patients (65%) underwent front-back decompression and stabilization. **They concluded** that staged treatment (performing anterior decompression and strut grafting followed by posterior instrumentation as separate procedures) did not lead to increased perioperative complication rates.

*Alton et al. performed a retrospective review of 62 patients with cervical spinal epidural abscesses to determine if a difference in neurologic outcomes exists between medical versus early surgical management.* *Six patients were successfully managed medically (18 failed medical management and were treated with delayed surgery), and 38 patients were treated with early surgery.* **They concluded** that early surgery improved post-treatment motor scores (American Spinal Injury Association 0-100) and that there was a high failure rate associated with medical management (75%).

Figure A is a cervical spine MRI that demonstrates multilevel vertebral osteomyelitis, discitis, epidural abscess, an anterior vertebral abscess, loss of sagittal alignment, and cord compression. Figure B demonstrates multilevel cord compression with associated myelomalacia in a cervical MRI. Figure C demonstrates left nerve root compression in an axial cervical MRI. Figure D is a cervical MRI demonstrating an odontoid fracture. Figure E is a sagittal T2 image showing faint, patchy, intramedullary T2 hyperintensities consistent with multiple sclerosis.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: This patient has multi-level cervical myelopathy that may be treated with ACDF, posterior laminectomy and fusion, or laminoplasty.
Answer 3: This patient would best be treated with an ACDF to restore sagittal alignment and decompress the affected nerve root.
Answer 4: The clinical presentation is consistent with an odontoid nonunion in and active older patient; posterior C1-2 fusion would be most appropriate.
Answer 5: The clinical presentation is consistent with multiple sclerosis; treatment is nonsurgical.
