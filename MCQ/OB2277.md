## Correct option

## Answer explanation

Figures 1 and 2 demonstrate a Gartland Type III supracondylar humerus fracture. Isolated AIN palsy alone is not an indication for overnight surgical treatment.

Supracondylar humerus fractures are among the most commonly seen fractures in pediatric patients. Injuries are classified using the Gartland Classification (Illustration 1). Treatment for Type II and III injuries is typically closed reduction percutaneous pinning, with the neurovascular exam dictating the timing of surgery. Indications for urgent fixation are pulselessness in a well-perfused hand, sensory nerve deficits, excessive swelling, brachialis sign (dimpling in the antecubital fossa), open injuries, and floating elbow. Emergent reduction, performed within hours, is reserved for those patients with a pulseless and poorly perfused hand. An isolated AIN palsy is not an indication for overnight treatment.

*Iyengar et al review early vs delayed reduction and pinning of type III displaced supracondylar humerus fractures.* **They report** no difference in the need for open reduction or in the outcome measures of patients who underwent closed reduction and pinning within the 8 hours following injury. **They conclude** that type III supracondylar humerus fracture can be safely treated in a delayed manner the following day.

*Leet et al review delayed treatment of type III supracondylar humerus fractures in children.* *They report on 158 type III supracondylar fractures, 5 of which had nervous deficits at initial evaluation.* **They conclude** that there is no correlation between increased time to surgical intervention and increased operative time, need for open reduction, longer hospital stay or increased unsatisfactory results.

Figures 1 and 2 are the AP and lateral radiographs demonstrating a Gartland Type III supracondylar humerus fracture. Illustration 1 reviews the Gartland Classification.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Open supracondylar humerus fractures require urgent irrigation and debridement and reduction.
Answer 2: Floating elbows have a higher rate of compartment syndrome and should be treated urgently.
Answer 4: A poorly perfused hand is an indication for emergent treatment.
Answer 5: The "brachialis sign" indicates that the proximal fracture fragment has pierced the brachialis, which demonstrates a more severe injury and should be treated urgently.
