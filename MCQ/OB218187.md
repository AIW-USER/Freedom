## Question

A 79-year-old female with painful rotator cuff tear arthropathy is preparing to undergo a primary reverse total shoulder arthroplasty. Which of the following has been validated as an effective preventative measure to take to reduce the risk of periprosthetic joint infection?

## Options

1. Application of 5% povidone-iodine solution to each nostril pre-operatively if nares are colonized with Methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus (MRSA)
2. Axillary hair shaving
3. Donning of sterile surgical helmet systems
4. Routine use of laminar flow ventilation
5. Use of clindamycin in patients with penicillin allergy

## Correct option

**1. Application of 5% povidone-iodine solution to each nostril pre-operatively if nares are colonized with Methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus (MRSA)**

## Answer explanation

Patients with a positive nasal swab for MRSA can be treated preoperatively with 2% mupirocin to the nares twice daily for 5 days, or 5% povidone-iodine solution to each nostril for 10 seconds per nostril, 1 hour before surgery.

Periprosthetic joint infections in the shoulder represent a serious complication and a major cause for revision in up to 4-5% of shoulder replacements. Though there is little formal consensus, numerous studies have been done to validate prophylactic measures to prevent infection. The most common causative organism is Cutibacterium acnes, an indolent bacterium that often has an insidious presentation without gross purulence or sinus tract formation, and typically does not cause significant elevations in inflammatory markers as are seen with infections caused by S. aureus (particularly MRSA variants). Validated prophylactic measures include the use of routine MRSA screening with pre-operative treatment as indicated above, pre-operative IV antibiotics preferably with Cefazolin or Vancomycin (not Clindamycin) in patients with a Penicillin allergy, surgical skin preparation with the use of ChloraPrep or a chlorhexidine-based solution, frequent surgical field irrigation at regular intervals during surgery with sterile normal saline, and the use of dilute povidone-iodine solution before surgical wound closure.

*Weiser et al. review the current state of screening and decolonization for the prevention of staphylococcus aureus surgical site infection after total hip and knee arthroplasty.* **The authors note** that patients colonized with MSSA or MRSA have an increased risk for a staphylococcal infection at the site of a total hip or knee arthroplasty, which has also been shown to be the case in total shoulder arthroplasty. **They conclude** that screening and nasal decolonization of S. aureus can potentially reduce the rates of staphylococcal surgical site infection.

*Clark et al. review strategies and the data supporting them for preventing infection in shoulder surgery.* **The authors note** that Staphylococcus aureus, Staphylococcus epidermidis, and Cutibacterium acnes are among the most frequently isolated pathogens in shoulder periprosthetic joint infection. **They conclude** that pre-operative measures should include (1) Chlorhexidine cloth wipes the night before and morning of surgery, (2) standard soap-and-water shower the morning of surgery, (3) MRSA screening and treatment, (4) no need to clip axillary hair unless hair obscures visualization, and (5) Chlorhexidine with alcohol-based skin preparation
solution.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Axillary hair shaving has not been shown to produce any meaningful reduction in the rates of infection after shoulder replacement surgery.
Answer 3: The use of sterile hoods/helmets has not been shown to reduce infection rates, with some studies suggesting that they actually increase infection rates.
Answer 4: The routine use of laminar flow ventilation has not been validated as an effective infection-reduction tool in the operating room.
Answer 5: Vancomycin, not clindamycin, has been suggested as a valid alternative for antibiotic prophylaxis in penicillin-allergic patients.
