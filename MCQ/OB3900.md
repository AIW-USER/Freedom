## Question

Which of the following foot deformities may be treated with medial hindfoot posting?

## Options

1. Rigid pes planovalgus
2. Flexible pes planovalgus
3. Cavovarus
4. Hallux rigidus
5. Equinovarus

## Correct option

**2. Flexible pes planovalgus**

## Answer explanation

Flexible pes planovalgus may be treated with medial hindfoot posting.

In flexible pes planovalgus, lateral hindfoot pain is caused by impingement as the foot pronates and abducts. With medial hindfoot posting, the medial aspect of the heel will be elevated, which will decrease the pressure on the lateral hindfoot. These orthotics may be placed in any shoe.

*Pomeroy et al. reviewed flatfoot deformity in adults.* **They report** that the use of a full-length custom orthotic device helps to support the longitudinal arch and reduce strain on the posterior tibial tendon. The orthosis should provide support to the medial longitudinal arch, correct the valgus angulation of the hindfoot, and correct abduction of the forefoot with use of a medial heel-wedge and a medial column post.

*Elftman reviewed the use of orthotics in the acquired adult flatfoot deformity.* **He reports** that in stage I or II, medial heel lifts and longitudinal supports may be used. In stage III deformity, patients may be managed with an articulated ankle-foot orthosis (AFO). Stage IV deformities typically require a non-articulated AFO due to deltoid ligament incompetence and talar tilt.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: A non-articulated AFO may be used in a rigid planovalgus deformity.
Answer 3: A full-length semi-rigid insole orthotic with a depression for the first ray and a lateral wedge may be used in a cavovarus deformity.
Answer 4: A Morton's extension with a stiff foot plate is the mainstay of treatment of hallux rigidus.
Answer 5: A mild equinovarus foot defofrmity may be treated with an AFO.
