## Question

A 32-year-old male presents with left shoulder pain and weakness. He reports sustaining a forced external rotation injury to his left upper extremity during a recreational football game 3 months ago. The decision is made to proceed with arthroscopic treatment of his injury. Video V shows the view from the posterosuperior portal. The structure highlighted by the black comma is innervated by a nerve(s) that branch off which anatomic structure of the brachial plexus?

## Options

1. Posterior cord
2. Lateral cord
3. Superior Trunk
4. Inferior Trunk
5. Middle Trunk

## Correct option

**1. Posterior cord**

## Answer explanation

The patient has injured his subscapularis, labelled by the black comma in Video V, which is innervated by the upper and lower subscapular nerves, both of which branch off posterior cord of the brachial plexus.

The subscapularis muscle originates at the subscapular fossa of scapula and inserts at the lesser tuberosity of humerus. Its primary action is medial/internal rotation and adduction of the shoulder. It also helps hold humeral head in glenoid cavity of scapula. Blood supply is from the subscapular artery. Identifying an injury to the subscapularis is best done by examining internal rotation strength with the belly-press test and lift-off test.

*Greis et al. reviewed the EMG findings of periscapular musculature during shoulder examination and* **found** that the lift-off test achieves a higher proportion of subscapularis contraction as compared to the other muscles of the rotator cuff. No differences were seen in contraction of the upper or lower segments of the subscapularis muscle.

*Tokish et al. investigated the belly-press and lift-off tests and* **found** that both activated the upper and lower portions of the subscapularis muscle to a greater extent than all other muscles of the rotator cuff, indicating that both tests are valid and specific for evaluation of the subscapularis muscle. The belly-press test activated the upper subscapularis muscle significantly more than the lift-off test, whereas the lift-off test posed a significantly greater challenge to the lower subscapularis muscle than the belly-press test.

Incorrect Answers
Answer 2: The lateral cord gives off the lateral pectoral nerve.
Answer 3: The superior trunk gives off the suprascapular nerve and nerve to the subclavius.
Answer 4: The inferior trunk does not have any branches.
Answer 5: The middle trunk does not have any branches.
