## Question

A 77-year-old female had a witnessed fall at home and sustained the injury shown in Figures A and B. After she is medically optimized for surgery, you go to discuss her surgical options. Which of the following statements is true regarding total elbow arthroplasty (TEA) as compared to open reduction internal fixation (ORIF)?

## Options

1. ORIF has been shown to have a lower reoperation rate since it is a joint-preserving procedure
2. ORIF is expected to have better range of motion post-operatively
3. ORIF is favored for patients with pre-existing rheumatoid arthritis
4. TEA has generally shown better early patient-reported outcomes measures
5. TEA is favored for patients with higher-demand occupations

## Correct option

**4. TEA has generally shown better early patient-reported outcomes measures**

## Answer explanation

This is an elderly patient with a comminuted distal humerus fracture. Studies have shown superior patient-reported outcome measures following TEA as compared to ORIF.

Distal humerus fractures are troublesome injuries which carry significant rates of complications. Traditionally these were treated with an open reduction internal fixation but significant complication rates accompanied this. This spurred a trend towards total elbow arthroplasty given the low-demands of this patient population in addition to advanced age, making TEA durability less worrisome.

*McKee et al. performed a randomized controlled trial comparing TEA versus ORIF for comminuted distal humeral fractures in an elderly population.* TEA showed superior Mayo Elbow Performance score at 6 months, 1, and 2 years post-operatively. TEA also showed better early DASH scores and faster operative time by 32 minutes. This supports the use of TEA as a primary surgical procedure for distal humerus fractures, especially given the 25% re-operation rate observed after ORIF.

*Frankle et al. retrospectively reviewed a cohort of 24 women over the age of 65 who either underwent TEA or ORIF for distal humerus fracture.* At 2 year followup **they showed** 92% of excellent outcomes for TEA compared with 33% for ORIF. Notably, none of the TEA in this series required re-operation, which again supports this procedure for elderly patients with comminuted distal humerus fracture.

*Athwal et al. reviewed the use of TEA in the trauma setting.* **The authors recommend** using a triceps-sparing approach, implanting a linked prosthesis when treating fractures and utilizing antibiotic-laden bone cement to secure the implant. **They note** that if the condyles are fractured, supplemental fixation of these may help gauge rotation of the implant and add stability to the construct.

Figures A and B are AP and lateral radiographs showing a highly comminuted distal humerus fracture with very osteopenic bone. Illustrations A and B are 6 week post-operative radiographs of the same patient who underwent an ORIF, which shows early failure of the construct. Illustration C shows the construct following revision to a TEA.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1- ORIF has a higher re-operation rate.
Answer 2- ORIF is much more frequently associated with stiffness compared to TEA
Answer 3- Pre-existing rheumatoid arthritis is indication for TEA.
Answer 5- TEA results in much higher failure rates in high demand individuals.
