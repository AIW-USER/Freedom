## Question

A 45-year-old male presents for evaluation of a left foot injury. His radiograph is shown in Figure A. Which of the following is true with respect to arthrodesis compared to open reduction and internal fixation alone for the patient's injury?

![[q214783-a-lis-franc.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Improved functional outcomes at 1 year
2. Decreased rate of hardware removal at 1 year
3. Decreased rate of symptomatic hardware
4. Improved forefoot alignment
5. Increased rate of infection

## Correct option

**2. Decreased rate of hardware removal at 1 year**

## Answer explanation

Patients with Lisfranc injuries undergoing primary arthrodesis have decreased rates of hardware removal compared to patients undergoing ORIF.

The radiographs show a displaced bony Lisfranc injury, which is a tarsometatarsal fracture-dislocation between the articulation of the medial cuneiform and the base of the second metatarsal. Lisfranc injuries are relatively rare, approximately 0.2% of all fractures, with an incidence of one per 55,000 persons yearly. These injuries are frequently misdiagnosed or there is a delay in the diagnosis. The current recommendations of treatment emphasize anatomic reduction and rigid internal fixation to prevent post-traumatic arthritis and midfoot deformity. As such, surgical treatment consists of ORIF or primary arthrodesis. Aside, from hardware removal, recent studies have not shown any difference in outcomes or complications between primary arthrodesis or ORIF. Patients who undergo ORIF often undergo a planned secondary hardware removal procedure, which is required to allow the TMT joints to return to motion.

*Desmond et al. performed a review of the management of Lisfranc injures.* **They note** that the severity of subtle Lisfranc injuries often is underestimated. **They also note** that displaced Lisfranc injuries with compromised vascularity should be treated with prompt reduction of the joint whereas, in patients with good vascularity, the status of the soft tissues should dictate the timing of definitive surgery. **They state** that anatomic reduction of the Lisfranc joint is one of the most important factors in determining patient outcomes. **They conclude** that post-traumatic arthritis is related to articular damage and inadequate reduction.

*Smith et al. performed a systematic review of level 1 comparative trials assessing operative management of Lisfranc injuries to determine if ORIF or primary arthrodesis led to fewer reoperations, less frequent revision surgery, and higher patient outcome scores.* **The authors noted** that the risk ratio for hardware removal was 0.23 indicating more hardware removal for ORIF than fusion. Neither arthrodesis nor ORIF was favored with respect to patient-reported outcomes or the rate of revision surgery. **The authors concluded** that the surgeon should consider the increased risk of hardware removal along with its associated morbidity and discuss this with the patient preoperatively when considering ORIF of Lisfranc injuries.

Figure A is an AP radiograph of the left foot showing a widened 1st tarsometatarsal joint and fractured 2nd metatarsal base, consistent with a bony Lisfranc injury.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1-4: There have been no differences between ORIF and primary arthrodesis with respect to functional outcomes, symptomatic hardware, forefoot alignment, and rates of infection in patients with bony Lisfranc injuries.
