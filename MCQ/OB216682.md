## Question

A 49-year-old male presents with a 2-year history of chronic lower back pain without radicular symptoms. He has undergone a course of physical therapy and taken oral anti-inflammatory medications with modest improvement in his symptoms. Despite this, he wishes to proceed with surgical treatment for his condition. His current MRI is demonstrated in Figure A. Which of the following is true regarding this condition?

![[q216682-a-lumbar-degen.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Successful treatment of discogenic back pain has no effect on depressive symptoms
2. Lumbar disc replacement results in clinically significant improvement in back pain scores
3. Cognitive behavioral therapy as an adjunct to nonsurgical treatment can improve functional status
4. Lumbar fusion has a >90% patient satisfaction rate for the treatment of this condition
5. Lumbar discography does not increase the risk of disc herniation of the tested level

## Correct option

**3. Cognitive behavioral therapy as an adjunct to nonsurgical treatment can improve functional status**

## Answer explanation

Structured nonsurgical care with cognitive behavioral therapy is equally effective for decreasing back pain and disability status as surgical treatment.

Discogenic back pain is the result of axial back pain from disc degeneration. Typically this occurs in the absence of radicular symptoms as there is seldom associated spinal stenosis. Chronic discogenic back pain lasting >2 years is associated with a poor prognosis for return to work and improvement in disability. *The Swedish Lumbar Spine Study (Fritzell et al.) demonstrated a significant improvement in pain and functional improvement with surgical treatment for chronic lower back pain, but nonsurgical treatment was unstandardized and based on an outdated standard of care.* *A more recent Cochrane review (Gibson et al.) that included four randomized controlled trials demonstrated no significant superiority of surgical treatment over nonsurgical treatment for chronic discogenic back pain.* *Treatment with cognitive-behavioral therapy (Brox et al.) has been associated with a significant improvement in pain and disability symptoms.*

*Gibson et al. performed a Cochrane review of surgical treatment for degenerative lumbar spondylosis.* *They included 31 randomized controlled trials which reported heterogeneous conclusions on the superiority of surgery over nonoperative treatment, including intradiscal electrotherapy and disk arthroplasty.* **They concluded** there is insufficient evidence to determine whether fusion provides significant clinical improvement over nonoperative treatment for degenerative lumbar spondylosis.

*Fritzell et al. performed a randomized controlled trial of 294 patients treated surgically and nonsurgically (per 1990's standard of care) for discogenic lower back pain.* **They reported** a significant improvement in lower back Visual Analog Scale pain score, Oswestry disability scores, and Zung depression scores in patients treated with lumbar fusion compared to traditional nonsurgical treatment, however, this difference decreased at 2 years follow-up. **They concluded** lumbar fusion can be effective in well-selected and informed patients with chronic discogenic back pain.

*Brox et al. performed a randomized controlled trial of 64 patients with chronic lower discogenic back pain at L4-5 and L5-S1 treated with surgical and conservative treatment.* **They reported** no significant difference in mean Oswestry scores, pain scores, return to work rates, and narcotic use following surgical and nonsurgical treatment. **The authors concluded** there were equivalent outcomes for lumbar discogenic back pain treated surgically and nonsurgically.

Figure A is a sagittal T2 MRI of the lumbar spine with disc degeneration at L4-5 and L5-S1.

Incorrect answers
Answer 1: Successful treatment of chronic discogenic back pain with both surgical and nonsurgical treatment has been shown to significantly improve depressive symptoms.
Answer 2: Lumbar disc replacement has not been associated with a clinically significant improvement in pain and disability symptoms compared to nonoperative treatment.
Answer 4: Lumbar fusion for discogenic back pain is associated with a ~60% patient satisfaction rate.
Answer 5: Lumbar discography has been associated with an increased rate of disc herniation at the tested level.
