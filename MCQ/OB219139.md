## Question

You are on call at a level I trauma center over the Fourth of July weekend. A local motorcycle club's annual ride is cut short when a drunk driver veers out of his lane and causes a massive crash at highway speeds. Two of the motorcyclists are transferred to your hospital, with more en route. Clinical photographs of Patient A (34-year-old active-duty military) and Patient B (33-year-old civilian) are available for review in Figures A and B, respectively. Aside from their occupational status, the patients are remarkably similar with respect to comorbidities, age, and clinical presentation. You have a discussion with both patients regarding limb salvage versus amputation. Which of the following best describes the outcomes for these two patients regarding these two treatment strategies?

## Options

1. Both Patient A and Patient B are more likely to have better functional outcomes with limb salvage.
2. Both Patient A and Patient B are unlikely to have long-term disability following amputation
3. Patient A is more likely to have worse outcomes following amputation than Patient B
4. Patient A is more likely to have better functional outcomes following amputation versus limb salvage
5. Patient B is more likely to have better functional outcomes following limb salvage versus amputation

## Correct option

**4. Patient A is more likely to have better functional outcomes following amputation versus limb salvage**

## Answer explanation

Both patients have sustained catastrophic lower extremity injuries with extensive soft-tissue damage and open fractures secondary to a high-energy mechanism. In military patients, as a result of better access to skilled rehabilitation, the presence of support groups, and access to state-of-the-art prostheses, amputation is preferred to limb salvage, with patients undergoing amputation demonstrating superior functional outcomes to those who undergo limb salvage (Answer 4).

Tibial shaft fractures represent the most common long bone fracture and account for 17% of all lower extremity fractures. In the younger population, these injuries are typically the result of high-energy mechanisms. Concerningly, high-energy mechanisms are more likely to be associated with higher degrees of soft-tissue injury, with the severity of muscle injury having the highest impact on the eventual need for amputation. With the increased accessibility of "orthoplastics" at trauma centers, the optimal treatment of open tibial shaft fractures with severe soft-tissue injury remains under ongoing investigation. Currently, no widely accepted scoring system to predict the need for amputation exists; however, significant soft-tissue trauma, a warm ischemia time > 6 hours, and/or severe ipsilateral foot trauma represent relative indications for amputation versus limb salvage.

Much of the information available regarding the mangled lower extremity comes from the LEAP (lower extremity assessment project) study. In the manuscripts which resulted from this study, the following trends emerged:
1) The most important predictor of eventual amputation is the severity of ipsilateral extremity soft-tissue injury.
2) The most important step to prevent infection other than early antibiotic administration is definitive transfer to a trauma center.
3) Overall, there is no significant difference in functional outcomes between amputation and salvage.
4) Loss of plantar sensation is not an absolute indication for amputation.
Overall, these injuries are catastrophic, and most patients report long-term functional disability, regardless of treatment strategy. A multidisciplinary approach is crucial in the treatment of these patients, and favorable outcomes are not guaranteed.

*MacKenzie et al. investigated the persistence of long-term disability following severe lower extremity trauma managed with either limb salvage or amputation.* *Their study included 397 patients with an average follow-up of 84 months.* Overall, **the authors found** that functional outcomes for both limb salvage and amputation were similar; however, patients in both cohorts reported persistent disability and poor functional outcomes. **The authors conclude** that priority should be given to strategies to improve post-acute care services that address secondary conditions that may compromise optimal recovery.

The Major Extremity Trauma Research Consortium (METRC), in the OUTLET study, investigated outcomes following severe distal tibia, ankle, or mid/hindfoot trauma treated with either limb salvage or transtibial amputation. Their study population included 488 patients treated with limb salvage and 151 patients treated with amputation, with a minimum follow-up of 18 months. The authors found that patients who underwent limb salvage would have had small differences in most outcomes had they undergone amputation. Overall, the authors assert that amputation should be considered a treatment option, rather than a salvage procedure, in most complex terminal lower-limb injuries.

*Doukas et al., in the Military Extremity Trauma Amputation/Limb Salvage (METALS) study, investigated the outcomes of amputation versus limb salvage following major lower-extremity trauma in military patients.* *This retrospective cohort study included 324 service members deployed to Afghanistan or Iraq who sustained a lower-limb injury requiring either amputation or limb salvage.* After adjustment for covariates, patients who underwent amputation had better scores in all short musculoskeletal function assessment (SMFA) domains compared to patients who underwent limb salvage. They also had a lower prevalence of PTSD and a higher likelihood of participation in vigorous sporting activity. Overall, **the authors conclude** that service members who undergo amputation appear to have better functional outcomes than those who undergo limb salvage.

Figures A and B are clinical photographs of two patients with a mangled lower extremity. Note the high degree of exposed bone and muscle and soft-tissue injury.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Overall, the best available evidence suggests that there are either no differences in outcomes between limb salvage and amputation, or a benefit to amputation, for civilian patients. For military patients, amputation appears to provide better functional outcomes.
Answer 2: Persistent functional disability is common following either amputation or limb salvage.
Answer 3: Military patients are more likely to report better functional outcomes following amputation when compared to limb salvage.
Answer 5: Overall, the best available evidence suggests that there are either no differences in outcomes between limb salvage and amputation, or a benefit to amputation, for civilian patients.
