## Answer explanation

Over time, growing rod constructs require greater amounts of force and achieve diminishing amounts of distraction (law of diminishing returns).

For early onset scoliosis that requires surgical treatment, a growing rod construct allows for correction of deformity while maintaining the ability of the spine and trunk to grow. Approximately every six months the patient is brought back to the operating room to lengthen the construct. Definitive spinal fusion is then done at skeletal maturity. It has been found that with each lengthening procedure a greater degree of force is needed in order to effectively lengthen the construct. Despite this greater force, each lengthening tends to produce less distraction of the construct.

*Noordeen et al. measured distraction forces and length gained during 60 lengthening procedures in 26 patients.* **They found** that distraction force required doubled by the fifth lengthening, and that with each procedure the length gained decreased.

*Farooq et al. present a retrospective review of 88 patients treated with growing rods for early onset scoliosis.* Mean cobb angle before surgery was 73°, correcting to mean 44°. Approximately 1 cm of growth was obtained per year.

Figures A - E are graphic representations of trends in force required and length gained compared to number of lengthenings. Illustration A is PA radiograph demonstrating a single-rod growing rod construct. Dual rod constructs are more common.

Incorrect answers:
Answers 1, 3, 4, 5: Each of these incorrectly illustrates the expected trends. Force required to lengthen a construct increases with each lengthening. The amount of length gained decreases with each lengthening.
