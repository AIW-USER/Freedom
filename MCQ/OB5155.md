## Question

A 13-year-old girl presents with her mother for evaluation of left knee pain. Her mother also has short stature and is followed in the orthopaedic spine clinic. Both patients have frontal bossing. What is the genetic defect responsible for this condition?

## Options

1. Cartilage oligomeric matrix protein (COMP) on chromosome 17
2. Cartilage oligomeric matrix protein (COMP) on chromosome 19
3. Fibroblast growth factor receptor 3 (FGFR-3)
4. Runt-related transcription factor 2 (RUNX2)
5. Core-binding factor subunit alpha-1 (CBF-alpha-1)

## Correct option

**3. Fibroblast growth factor receptor 3 (FGFR-3)**

## Answer explanation

This patient has achondroplasia, an autosomal dominant condition caused by a defect in fibroblast growth factor receptor 3, on chromosome 4P.

Achondroplasia is the most common skeletal dysplasia. Eighty percent of mutations are sporadic, but the condition is autosomal dominant. The mutation affects the gene encoding for the receptor of fibroblast growth factor, rather than the growth factor itself. The clinical result is hypoplasia of long bones formed by enchondral ossification, with proximal limb segments more affected than distal.

*Shirley et al. review the orthopaedic implications of achondroplasia.* **They note** that foramen magnum stenosis, spinal stenosis, thoracolumbar kyphosis, short stature and genu varum are orthopaedic manifestations of this dysplasia.

Figure A shows a full-length, standing, bilateral lower extremity radiograph of a patient with achrondoplasia. Note the pronounced shortening of the femora and genu varum. Figure B demonstrates squared iliac wings, champagne glass pelvis (wider than it is deep) and flattened femoral heads.

Incorrect answers:
Answers 1 and 2: Pseudoachondroplasia is caused by a defect in the cartilage oligomeric matrix protein (COMP) on chromosome 19. Patients with pseudoachondroplasia have normal facies and no increased incidence of spinal stenosis.
Answers 4 and 5: Cleidocranial dysplasia is caused by a defect in the gene encoding for runt-related transcription factor 2 (RUNX2), also known as core-binding factor subunit alpha-1 (CBF-alpha-1), a transcription factor essential for osteoblast differentiation.
