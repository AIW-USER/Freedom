## Question

A 9-year-old girl presents to the emergency department after a MVC. She was found to have an initial GCS of 3 at the scene of the accident but improved to a GCS of 12 on arrival to the trauma bay. She is found to have a closed head injury and a right transverse femoral shaft fracture, shown in Figure A. Shortly after her arrival to the hospital, she is cleared for surgery and brought to the operating room for intramedullary nailing of her femur fracture using flexi nails (Figure B). Compared to delayed treatment, early treatment of the patient's femur fracture is most likely to lead to which of the following outcomes?

![[q2195-b-jkoa-47-353-g001-l.jpg]]

## Options

1. Increased risk of ARDS
2. Decreased long-term mental function
3. Increased risk of pneumonia
4. No increased risk of pulmonary complications
5. No difference in length of hospital stay

## Correct option

**4. No increased risk of pulmonary complications**

## Answer explanation

Early fixation of this patient's femur fracture would not lead to an increase in pulmonary complications.

Young patients who sustain head injuries have better outcomes than adults with similar head injuries and typically achieve full neurologic recovery. For femur fractures within this age group, flexible intramedullary nailing routinely produces good outcomes with few complications, even in those patients who have sustained a head injury. Delaying surgical intervention in this patient would lead to an increased length of hospital stay but would not affect the risk of pulmonary or neurologic complications.

*Mendelson et al. reviewed 25 pediatric patients with a moderate or severe head injury and a femoral shaft fracture who underwent operative fixation.* **They found** that patients who were treated early (< 48 hours) spent 8 fewer days in the hospital and had similar orthopaedic and neurologic complications compared to the group whose femur fractures were treated late.

*Hedequist et al. reviewed 387 pediatric patients with femur fractures to assess the effect of timing of femur fracture stabilization on pulmonary complication rates in pediatric trauma patients.* **They found** that the early (< 24 hours) treatment of femur fractures did not lead to an increased risk of pulmonary complications. In both the early and late fixation groups, the risk of pulmonary complications was increased in patients with severe head injuries.

Figure A is an AP radiograph of the right femur with a transverse femoral shaft fracture. Figure B is a post-op radiograph of the fracture shown in Figure A that was treated with flexible intramedullary nailing.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1 & 3: As noted above, early treatment has not been found to be associated with increased pulmonary complications.
Answer 2: No association between early treatment and decreased long-term mental function has been found.
Answer 5: Early fixation of pediatric femur fractures has been found to lead to a decreased length of hospital stay.
