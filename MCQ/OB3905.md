## Question

A 25-year-old military service member presents four weeks after sustaining an inversion ankle injury with continued severe pain despite conservative treatment. Anterior drawer testing and MRI obtained at initial presentation demonstrated a tear of the anterior talofibular ligament (ATFL). Exam reveals improved swelling but significant pain with light touch about the dorsal foot and anterolateral distal leg. Management should include prescription of a medication that acts by which of the following mechanisms?

## Options

1. Interferes with nerve conduction through binding of intracellular sodium channels
2. Reduces hyper-excitability of voltage dependent presynaptic calcium channels
3. Binds and activates the mu receptor
4. Directly activates the gamma-amino-butyric acid (GABA) receptor
5. Selectively binds and inhibits cyclooxygenase type 1 (COX-1) enzymes

## Correct option

**2. Reduces hyper-excitability of voltage dependent presynaptic calcium channels**

## Answer explanation

Gabapentin is an anticonvulsant medication used to manage neuropathic pain, in this case, stretch neurapraxia of the superficial peroneal nerve (SPN).

Inversion ankle injuries can result in stretch neurapraxias of SPN. Neuropathic symptoms such as burning, tingling, and hyperalgesia (i.e., exquisite tenderness to light touch) should raise concern for a neuropathic etiology. Pregabalin (Lyrica) and Gabapentin (Neurontin) decrease the transduction of pain through reducing hyperexcitability of voltage-dependent pre-synaptic calcium channels in activated neurons. Tricyclic antidepressants, such as amitriptyline, may also be considered for the treatment of neuropathic pain.

*Dr. Rosenquist reviewed gabapentin and its role in the treatment of neuropathic pain.* Although the medication was initially approved for the treatment of seizures, its safety and efficacy led to common use for neuropathic pain conditions. Gabapentin is a structural analog of gamma-amino-butyric acid (GABA), an inhibitory amino acid. However, gabapentin does not bind to GABA receptors. Instead, the medication acts by diminishing neurotransmitter release by acting on pre-synaptic calcium channels. **The author concludes** gabapentin’s safety record, limited side-effect profile, wide dose-ranging capabilities, and effectiveness make it a valuable treatment option for neuropathic pain.

Incorrect Answers
Answer 1: Local anesthetics, such as lidocaine and bupivacaine, inhibit nerve conduction through the binding of intracellular sodium channels. These medications are not indicated as first-line treatment of neuropathic pain.
Answer 3: Opioid analgesics are mu receptor agonists, which are not indicated for neuropathic pain.
Answer 4: Muscle relaxants, such as diazepam and baclofen, decrease muscle tone via direct GABA stimulation. These medications are not indicated for neuropathic pain.
Answer 5: Inhibitors specific for COX-2, not COX-1, result in anti-inflammatory action without disrupting beneficial effects of COX-1 (maintains gastric mucosa, regulates renal blood flow, influences platelet aggregation).
