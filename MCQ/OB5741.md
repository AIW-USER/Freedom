## Options

1. There may be a mutation in the gene for a sulfate transporter protein.
2. An anteroposterior (AP) radiograph may demonstrate decreasing interpedicular distance from L1 to S1.
3. This infection shows preferential involvement of the vertebral body and sparing of the intervertebral disc.
4. Pathological cells demonstrate coffee-bean nuclei with a prominent nuclear groove.
5. Examination will also reveal cloudy corneas and mental retardation.

## Correct option

**3. This infection shows preferential involvement of the vertebral body and sparing of the intervertebral disc.**

## Answer explanation

This child has a sharp gibbus (kyphus) from Pott's disease of the spine (tuberculosis, TB). TB shows a predilection for the anterior vertebral body, and spares the disc space until late in the disease, unlike pyogenic infections which tend to start at the disc space.

TB tends to affect the vertebral bodies (sparing the disc). In adults, the deformity is static after the disease is cured. In children, kyphosis continues to increase in 40% of patients even after healing. In children, risk for progression is high when anterior resection and fusion alone is performed, and lowest when both anterior and posterior fusion is performed.

*Jain et al. reviewed TB of the spine.* **They state** that nonoperative cases show an increase in deformity of 15°. Indications for surgery include panvertebral lesions, therapeutically refractory disease, severe kyphosis, developing neurological deficit and lack of improvement or deterioration. Sharp kyphosis >60° or one which is likely to progress requires anterior decompression, posterior shortening, posterior instrumentation and anterior and posterior bone grafting in the active stage of the disease.

*Chunguang et al. reviewed anterior release, decompression, deformity correction and instrumented fusion, followed by posterior osteotomy, deformity correction, and pedicle screw instrumented fusion of healed kyphosis in 16 children.* *They achieved good cosmesis in all patients and neurologic improvement in 4 patients.* Kyphosis correction was from 55.8° to 21.7° postoperatively, and 23.2° at followup. Scoliosis correction was from 6.9° to 0.8° postoperatively and 0.9° at followup.

Figures A through D show healed TB of the thoracolumbar spine with a 118°kyphotic deformity. Illustrations A and B show closing–opening wedge osteotomy with titanium mesh cage filled / bone graft to reconstruct the anterior column.

Incorrect Answers
Answer 1: Diastrophic dysplasia is a mutation in the sulfate transporter protein. Patients demonstrate short stature and cauliflower ears, which is absent here.
Answer 2: Lumbar spinal stenosis is characteristic of achondroplasia, which also presents with rhizomelic dwarfism (shortening of the proximal limb segment, the humerus), which is absent here.
Answer 4: Langerhans cell histiocytosis results in progressive vertebra plana which contributes to a gibbus. This is not present in this case.
Answer 5: Mucopolysaccharidoses may present with progressive mental retardation and cloudy corneas (e.g. Hurler syndrome) and proportionate dwarfism. Dwarfism is absent in this case.
