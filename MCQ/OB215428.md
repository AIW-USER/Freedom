## Question

A 36-year-old male presents to the emergency department after a car accident. Injury radiographs are seen in Figures A and B. Closed reduction is attempted in the emergency department but fails. Therefore, the patient is brought to the operating room for closed reduction under anesthesia, which is also unsuccessful. What structure is likely prohibiting reduction?

![[q215428-a-subtalar-medial-dislocation-ap.jpg]]
*Figure A*

![[q215428-b-subtalar-medial-dislocation-lateral.jpg]]
*Figure B*

## Options

1. Extensor digitorum brevis
2. Extensor hallucis longus
3. Flexor hallucis longus
4. Posterior tibial tendon
5. Tibialis anterior

## Correct option

**1. Extensor digitorum brevis**

## Answer explanation

In medial subtalar dislocations, the talar head can buttonhole through the extensor digitorum brevis (EDB) muscle belly and become irreducible by closed means.

A subtalar dislocation is defined as a dislocation of both the subtalar and talonavicular joints. Medial subtalar dislocations represent about 75% of these and are seen with forceful inversion on a plantar-flexed foot as the talar neck pivots on the sustentaculum tali. Around 10% of medial subtalar dislocations are irreducible by closed means, even with appropriate anesthesia. Blocks to reduction for medial subtalar dislocations commonly include the EDB, the peroneal tendons, and the talonavicular joint capsule.

*Rammelt and Goronzy reviewed subtalar dislocations detailing important anatomy and epidemiology.* *They discuss initial management, including CT scan, which shows associated fractures in around 60% of cases.* *They review different blocks to reduction for medial and lateral dislocations,* **noting** lateral subtalar dislocations are more commonly irreducible. **The authors conclude** that purely ligamentous dislocations have an excellent prognosis, with associated fractures leading to worse outcomes.

*Rammelt et al. described traumatic injuries to the subtalar joint and* **highlighted** important specifics regarding subtalar dislocations. **They note** that fractures of the talar process, talar head, and sustentaculum tali are most commonly seen with subtalar dislocations. They reiterate that early reduction and stabilization of any fractures are vital in reducing the risk of avascular necrosis and that rates of post-traumatic subtalar arthritis are linked to associated fractures and cartilage damage.

Figures A and B depict AP and lateral radiographs demonstrating a medial subtalar dislocation. On the lateral view, the talar head appears superior to the navicular.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Extensor hallucis longus is located more dorsally and, therefore, is less likely to become entrapped in the subtalar joint and prevent reduction.
Answers 3 and 4: Flexor hallucis longus and posterior tibial tendons more commonly block reduction of lateral subtalar dislocations. Given their medial location, they are often drawn into the subtalar joint during dislocation and prevent reduction.
Answer 5: Tibialis anterior, like the extensor hallucis longus, is located more dorsomedially and less likely to be entrapped or involved during the subtalar dislocation.
