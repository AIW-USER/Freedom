Chapter: Orthopedics Oncology MCQs — Giant Cell Tumor

## Question

A major disadvantage and clinical concern regarding the long-term, multi-year administration of Denosumab for unresectable or metastatic GCT is the risk of malignant transformation. Which of the following best characterizes this risk?

## Options

1. Malignant transformation is entirely prevented if Denosumab is combined with low-dose radiation therapy.
2. Malignant transformation is strictly a radiographic phenomenon (pseudoprogression) and does not represent true histological sarcoma conversion.
3. The transformed sarcoma consistently retains high sensitivity to further RANKL inhibition, ensuring a stable prognosis.
4. There is a reported 1% to 6% risk of secondary malignant transformation into high- grade sarcoma, with a higher prevalence noted in patients receiving prolonged therapy.

## Correct option

**4. There is a reported 1% to 6% risk of secondary malignant transformation into high- grade sarcoma, with a higher prevalence noted in patients receiving prolonged therapy.**

## Answer explanation

Sarcomatous (malignant) transformation is a rare but highly critical and devastating problem associated with the long-term use of Denosumab for Giant Cell Tumor (GCT) of bone.

While Giant Cell Tumor is traditionally classified as a benign but locally aggressive lesion, it has an inherent, baseline risk of spontaneous malignant transformation (about 1% to 2% in untreated or conventionally curetted cases). However, multiple international large-scale surveillance studies and clinical trials have confirmed that long-term, multi-year exposure to Denosumab statistically increases this risk.

Answer: 4
