## Answer explanation

Cleft hand

Cleft hand, also called as ectrodactyly or colloquially as "split hand," is defined as a central longitudinal deficiency expressed as suppression of bone and soft tissues in the central elements of the hand, including the index, middle, and ring fingers

These central ray deformities typically divide into

- "typical" cleft hands - generally of a genetic origin and bilateral with the classic "v-shaped" defect. Furthermore, typical cleft hands are often associated with "cleft foot" deformities as well
- "atypical" cleft hands - are a form of symbrachydactyly involving the index, long, and ring fingers, often involving an absence of the digits, syndactyly of the digits, or hypoplasia of the digits. Atypical cleft hands generally appear as more of a "u-shaped" deformity compared to the classic "v-shape". Rarely associated syndromes or deformities, and the disorder is not inherited.

Etiology

"typical" cleft hands” - inherited x-linked dominant trait with incomplete penetrance

"atypical" cleft hands – Sporadic mutation

Theory is wedge shaped degeneration of central part of apical ectodermal ridge (AER) because of loss of function of certain genes expressed in that part of the AER.

Epidemiology

Male: Female – 5:1

Usually bilateral

Presentation

Symptoms –

- Aesthetic limitation
- Functional limitation

Examination

- Absent or shortened central (third) ray
- May have absent radial digits
- May involve feet – Cleft feet

Classification:

Currently, the most accepted clinical classification is the one given by Manske and Halikis.

Treatment

Type 1

No surgery is indicated for the web of these hands, since it is normal and there is no functional impairment.

The surgical procedures recommended for treatment of the central deficiency included (1) cleft closure, (2) reduction of the widened intermetacarpal space bordering the deficiency, and (3) excision of the polydactylous bony elements when present

 Type 2A

The recommended web surgery is a 4-flap Z-plasty. The surgeries for the central deficiency consisted of soft tissue closure with dorsally and palmarly based flaps from the cleft and reduction of the intermetacarpal spaces as outlined for type I.

Type 2B

Surgical procedures related to restoring a functional thumb-index span required greater reconstruction than in the preceding groups. Dorsally and/or palmarly based flaps are transposed from the cleft into the thumb web. The central deficiency is closed in conjunction with the web procedure, using the remaining skin and soft tissue of the cleft. The intermetacarpal space is reduced by ligament reconstruction

Type 3

Priority is given to reconstructing the thumb web. Web reconstruction is accomplished by rotation of dorsally and/or palmarly based flaps from the cleft, along with thumb-index syndactyly release and skin grafts. Cleft closure is accomplished using local pedicle flaps, and intermetacarpal space reduction was accomplished by ligament reconstruction.

Type 4

Surgical procedures directed at the merged thumb web space and cleft are usually not done as functional impairment is less

Metacarpophalangeal joint is stabilized by arthrodesis, and the web space is reduced by removing excess skin.

Type 5

No surgical procedure is recommended for this group of patients. Thumb reconstruction using free tissue toe-to-hand transfer can be considered to provide a type IV merged web.

The image given in the question shows the middle finger ray and a normal thumb index web space, hence it is a Manske and Hallis Type 1 Cleft hand

Ideal treatment option is no surgery for web of these hands and cleft closure and reduction of the intermetacarpal spaces for the central deficiency.

Answer : a
