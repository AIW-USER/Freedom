## Question

A 15-year-old female is treated in your clinic for adolescent idiopathic scoliosis. Despite adequate bracing her curve magnitude progresses to 65°. The decision is made to proceed with posterior spinal instrumented fusion with correction. During this procedure, which of the following neuromonitoring changes is most concerning for a spinal cord event?

## Options

1. 30% decrease in somatosensory evoked potential amplitude
2. 5% increase in somatosensory evoked potential latency
3. 65% drop in motor evoked potentials in lower extremities
4. Thoracic pedicle screw stimulation of 14 mA
5. 30% global decrease in motor evoked and somatosensory evoked potential amplitude

## Correct option

**3. 65% drop in motor evoked potentials in lower extremities**

## Answer explanation

A >50% decrease in motor evoked potentials is a significant neuromonitoring change and suggestive of a spinal cord event.

Neuromonitoring provides real-time assessment of spinal cord function during surgical procedures. It can provide early warning of an impending iatrogenic spinal cord injury, which greatly increases the safety of complex spine operations. The accuracy of neuromonitoring depends on several factors, including mean arterial pressure, halogenated anesthesia, patient body temperature, and hemoglobin. In the presence of a neuromonitoring event (significant changes in signals) the factors must be corrected if they are not satisfactory. Some events suggest a spinal cord injury, such as a >50% decrease in motor evoked potential amplitude. Transcranial motor evoked potentials are more sensitive and specific for spinal cord injuries than somatosensory evoked potentials.

*Vitale et al. developed a consensus statement from 20 prominent pediatric spine surgeons regarding surgical site infection avoidance in high-risk pediatric spine surgery patients.* **The authors recommended**: (1) preoperative chlorhexidine skin wash; (2) preoperative urine cultures; (3) preoperative Patient Education Sheet; (4) preoperative nutritional assessment; (5) clipping to remove hair preoperatively; (6) perioperative IV ancef; (7) perioperative gram-negative bacilli prophylaxis; (8) adherence to antimicrobial regimens; (9) limited OR traffic; (10) UV lights are not required; (11) intraoperative wound irrigation; (12) vancomycin powder should be applied in the bone graft or surgical wound; (13) impervious dressings postoperatively; (14) minimized post-operative dressing changes prior to discharge.

*Vitale et al. developed a consensus statement from 16 spine surgeons regarding the avoidance of wrong-level surgery for spinal deformity.* *The authors reached a consensus on 17 interventions to avoid wrong-level surgery including a final checklist that standardizes vertebral-level counting and imaging criteria.* **The authors concluded** these interventions can reduce the incidence of wrong-level surgery by standardizing preoperative and intraoperative practices.

*Vitale et al. performed a consensus statement regarding the best practice use of intraoperative neuromonitoring in spine deformity surgery.* *The authors provided a checklist to navigate in the event of neuromonitoring changes for increased safety, promote a team-based approach, and decrease variability in addressing monitoring alerts.* **They concluded** the checklist provides important items to consider in the setting of neuromonitoring alerts.

Incorrect answers
Answers 1 ad 2: A >50% decrease in amplitude and >10% increase in latency of somatosensory evoked potential amplitude are considered significant spinal cord events.
Answer 4: Thoracic pedicle screw stimulation <8 mA is suggestive of a medial breach. However, this does not necessarily indicate the spinal cord has been injured.
Answer 5: A 30% global decrease in motor evoked and somatosensory evoked potential amplitude suggests a technical issue, such as low MAP or halogenated anesthesia. However, these issues must be resolved prior to proceeding with the remainder of the case.
