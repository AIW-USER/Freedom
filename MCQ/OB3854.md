## Question

A 37-year-old female presents complaining of persistent burning and tingling on the plantar surface of her foot. She was previously treated with night splints and a stretching exercise regimen. A sagittal cut of her MRI is shown in Figure A. What is the most likely diagnosis?

![[q3854-a-figure-a.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Vascular insufficiency
2. Os trigonum
3. Recalcitrant plantar fascitis
4. Tarsal tunnel syndrome
5. Synovial sarcoma

## Correct option

**4. Tarsal tunnel syndrome**

## Answer explanation

The correct diagnosis is tarsal tunnel syndrome.

The MRI cut shown in Figure A exhibits a ganglion cyst located in the tarsal tunnel, which causes compression on the tibial nerve, causing burning and tingling symptoms.

*Recht et al. review the importance and indications for MRI usage in the diagnosis of foot and ankle pathology.* Particularly in regard to tarsal tunnel syndrome, high-resolution images can offer distinct causes for compression and help identify causes for nerve entrapment in the foot and ankle.

*Pomeroy et al. review the differential diagnoses that cause nerve entrapment in the foot and ankle.* Specifically regarding the medial side of the foot, a common misdiagnosis for tarsal tunnel syndrome is plantar fasciitis. Characteristic symptoms distinguish plantar fasciitis from tarsal syndrome, specifically the most severe pain occurring first thing in the morning. Compression of the tarsal tunnel during physical examination is also highly sensitive in making the correct diagnosis.

Figure A exhibits a sagittal MRI image with a ganglion cyst in the tarsal tunnel.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: Vascular insufficiency, while it may cause parasthesias, is unlikely in this clinical scenario, especially given the imaging.
Answer 2: Os trigonum, which may cause symptomatic impingement, is not typically associated with symptoms of burning and tingling.
Answer 3: Plantar fasciitis, commonly misdiagnosed, is not correct in this scenario, not only due to conservative treatment failure, but also due to the MRI finding.
Answer 5: Synovial sarcoma, which may occur in the foot and ankle, is a malignant oncologic process that typically presents with more aggressive type imaging, which is not seen in this MRI image.
