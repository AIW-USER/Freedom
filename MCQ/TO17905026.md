## Question

On sudden accent to high altitude, one develops shortness of breath, pink frothy sputum. First step of treatment would be

## Options

1. Oxygen
2. Rapid descent by 1000 ft
3. Place in hypoxia chamber
4. Oral Nifedipine

## Correct option

**1. Oxygen**

## Answer explanation

answer-a

This is a case of High altitude pulmonary edema (HAPE): Ascending to or being at a new high altitude may cause high-altitude illness (HAI). HAI includes acute mountain sickness (AMS), high-altitude cerebral edema (HACE) and high-altitude pulmonary edema (HAPE).

High altitude pulmonary edema (HAPE) is a noncardiogenic pulmonary edema which typically occurs in lowlanders who ascend rapidly to altitudes greater than 2500-3000 meters. It is a potentially fatal condition in which lung capillaries leak and fluid accumulates in the lungs. "Rapid Ascent" 8000 feet (2500 meters) is considered the usual threshold altitude for HAI.

Cause: Exaggerated hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction and Elevated pulmonary artery pressure. It occurs due to leaks in the capillary wall (‘stress failure’).

Symptoms: HAPE presents within 2-5 days of arrival at high altitude. It is rarely observed below altitudes of 2500-3000 m and after 1 week of acclimatsation at a particular altitude.

Initial symptoms include nonproductive cough, Dyspnoea on exertion, Reduced exercise performance. Later dyspnoea occurs at rest. Orthopnea may occur. Gurgling in the chest and pink frothy sputum indicate advanced cases. Patients may have cyanosis, tachycardia, tachypnoea and elevated body temperature generally not exceeding 38.5°C. Rales are discrete initially and located over the middle”lung fields.

Imaging of the thorax reveals patchy opacities with inconsistent predominance of location, but often infiltrates are seen in the region of the right middle lobe

Prevention- preventive medicines include nifedipine (commonly used to treat high blood pressure), tadalafil, dexamethasone, or acetazolamide. ACETAZOLAMIDE 500-1000 mg/day 8-12 hourly or 125 mg BD, It should be started 24 hours prior to ascent.

Treatment-Immediate Descent ( Definitive Treatment) and Supplemental oxygen – First Line of treatment to be given. Portable hyperbaric chamber is also used to treat HAPE. Nifedipine ORAL 30MG SR every 12 hourly. For the mountaineer in a remote area without medical care, descent has first priority.

Answer : a
