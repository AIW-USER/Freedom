## Question

A 55-year-old female sustains the injury depicted in figure A from a mechanical fall from standing height. The decision is made to treat the patient surgically. Which of the following is true regarding treatment for this injury?

![[q215468-a-screen-shot-2021-03-21-at-8-52-00-pm.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Increased implant cutout has been observed with sliding hip screws and tip-to-apex distance <25 mm
2. Lower reoperation rates are observed with internal fixation compared to arthroplasty
3. Sliding hip screws result in increased avascular necrosis rates compared to cannulated screws
4. Timing to surgical fixation has a greater impact on AVN rates than quality of reduction
5. Cannulated screws are associated with a higher overall reoperation rate compared to sliding hip screws

## Correct option

**3. Sliding hip screws result in increased avascular necrosis rates compared to cannulated screws**

## Answer explanation

The use of sliding hip screws has been associated with increased femoral head avascular necrosis as reported by the Fixation using Alternative Implants for the Treatment of Hip fractures (FAITH) study.

Femoral neck fractures are common injuries in the geriatric population and are associated with 1-year mortality rates of up to 45%, depending on the severity of medical comorbidities. The goals of treatment consist of stabilization that enables early mobilization and weight-bearing to minimize post-op morbidity. Few differences have been reported between fixation types, however, the FAITH study did reveal a higher risk of avascular necrosis with the use of sliding hip screws.

*Lu et al. reviewed the anatomy, classification, biomechanics of internal fixation, and implant-related complications for the treatment of femoral neck and intertrochanteric fractures.* **They reported** greater biomechanical strength of cephalomedullary nails compared to sliding hip screw constructs, but an increased risk of implant cut-out with helical blade compared to lag screw fixation. **They concluded** that appropriate implant selection for corresponding fracture patterns allows for early mobilization and weight-bearing.

The FAITH investigators performed an international, multicenter randomized controlled trial studying reoperation and complication rates in patients treated with sliding hip screws and cannulated screws for femoral neck fractures. They reported no difference in reoperation rates between sliding hip screws and cannulated screws in the pooled analysis and a higher rate of avascular necrosis with sliding hip screws. They concluded sliding hip screws may be advantageous for smokers, displaced fractures, and basicervical fractures.

Figure A is an AP pelvis radiograph depicting a displaced and varus angulated femoral neck fracture.

Incorrect answers
Answer 1: Sliding hip screw constructs with a tip-to-apex distance >25 mm is associated with increased implant cut-out.
Answer 2: Internal fixation has been associated with increased reoperation rates compared to arthroplasty for the treatment of femoral neck fractures.
Answer 4: Avascular necrosis occurs at increased rates with poor reduction of the fracture.
Answer 5: Reoperation rates of cannulated screw fixation and sliding hip screws are similar as reported by the FAITH study.
