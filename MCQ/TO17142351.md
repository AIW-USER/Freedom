Chapter: Regional Orthopedics MCQs — Tendoachilles Injury

## Question

A person finds pain and edema on back of heel on walking upstairs. Thompson test shows weak plantar flexion. IOC would be

## Options

1. Venography
2. USG
3. X ray
4. CT scan

## Correct option

**2. USG**

## Answer explanation

Patient in this question has TA tear as suggested by Thompson test.

Clinical diagnosis:

Thompson squeeze test: It is performed by squeezing the calf muscle just distal to its maximal girth while the patient is prone to bring about plantar flexion of the ankle joint. The test is positive for complete rupture when there is no plantar flexion of the ankle.

Matles test: The patient is placed prone with the lower legs extending off the bed and is asked to actively flex the knees to 90 degrees. Active flexion of the knee should cause the gastrocnemius to shorten, causing plantar flexion of the foot. If the foot falls into neutral or slight dorsiflexion, the Achilles tendon is likely ruptured.

Needle test described by O’Brien : a 25-gauge needle is placed percutaneously in the midline of the proximal tendon. Motion of the proximal tendon indicating continuity is detected by observing the needle when the foot is put through passive range of motion. No motion of the needle indicates complete tear.

Radiological diagnosis:

Ultrasound is an important complementary diagnostic tool in the diagnosis of lesions of Achilles tendon, it is as good as MRI in the diagnosis of tendinopathy and full thickness tear, however MRI is more superior in the diagnosis of partial thickness tear, and in the differentiation of the different types of tendinopathy. So USG is the initial investigation of choice but MRI has the highest accuracy in the diagnosis of TA tear. For complete TA tear both are equally good.

Suggested ultrasound-based criteria for choosing nonoperative treatment include (1) gap of less than 5 mm with maximal plantar flexion, (2) gap of less than 10 mm with the foot in neutral position, and (3) more than 75% tendon apposition with the foot in 20 degrees of plantar flexion.

Answer: 2
