## Correct option

## Answer explanation

This 9-month-old boy presents with bilateral rockerbottom feet in the setting of plantarflexion radiographs that depict persistent dorsal dislocation of the talonavicular joint, all consistent with congenital vertical talus (CVT). Before the ossification of the navicular bone around age 3 years, the first metatarsal is used as a proxy for the navicular on radiography.

CVT is rigid rocker-bottom flatfoot deformity due to the significantly plantarflexed position of the talus, dorsally dislocated navicular, and the equinus position of the calcaneus. In the treatment of CVT, goals include restoration of the normal anatomical relationships between the talus, navicular, and calcaneus, and provision of a plantigrade foot. Closed treatment alone in the treatment of CVT often results in treatment failure, necessitating surgical reduction and pinning of the talonavicular joint.

*Kodros et al. investigated the long-term results of a single-stage surgical correction of CVT.* **They reported** no wound complications or avascular necrosis of the talus in their series, and that there were significant improvements in the lateral talocalcaneal and talo-first metatarsal angles. **They concluded** that good clinical and radiographic results can be achieved with a low incidence of complications using this single-stage surgical correction.

*Stricker et al. investigated early one-stage reconstruction of CVT.* **They reported** 17 good, 3 fair, and no poor results, with very few perioperative complications. Furthermore, **they observed** overall good correction with plantigrade, painless feet when this procedure was performed in children younger than 27 months.

Figure A demonstrates bilateral rockerbottom feet. Figure B demonstrates a plantarflexion lateral radiograph with persistent dorsal dislocation of the talonavicular joint. Illustration A demonstrates plantarflexion radiographs of congenital oblique talus and CVT. In congenital oblique talus, the talonavicular joint lines up in plantarflexion, while in true CVT, talonavicular joint remains dorsal to the talus in plantar flexion.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: CVT is associated with neuromuscular conditions in about 50% of cases.
Answer 2: While the talonavicular dislocation often reduces with forced plantarflexion of the foot in congenital oblique talus, it does not reduce in CVT.
Answer 3: The foot deformity in CVT is most commonly a rigid flatfoot (eqinovalgus), not equinovarus.
Answer 5: Most cases of CVT resolve after serial manipulation, casting, and eventual surgical reduction and pinning of the talonavicular joint.
