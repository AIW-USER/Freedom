## Question

Which of the following is true about the structure marked with the asterisk in Figure 1 as viewed from an anterolateral portal of a knee arthroscopy?

![[q211645-a-screen-shot-2020-03-11-at-9-15-52-pm.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Tight in flexion, loose in extension, and more isometric through range of motion
2. Loose in mid flexion, loose in extension, and primary supply is the middle geniculate artery
3. Tight in flexion, isometric, and responsible for rotatory stability
4. Loose in flexion, loose in mid-flexion, and responsible for rotatory stability
5. Isometric, tight in extension, and primary blood supply from middle geniculate artery

## Correct option

**4. Loose in flexion, loose in mid-flexion, and responsible for rotatory stability**

## Answer explanation

The structure marked with the asterisk in Figure 1 is the posterolateral (PL) bundle of the ACL. The PL bundle is loose in flexion and mid-flexion and responsible for rotatory stability (Answer 4).

The anterior cruciate ligament (ACL) consists of the anteromedial (AM) and posterolateral (PL) bundles. The AM bundle is primarily responsible for resistance to anterior drawer, is isometric, but tightest in flexion and is tested with the Lachman's or Anterior drawer test. The PL bundle is tightest in extension, has the greatest change in length, primarily resists rotatory instability, and is best tested through the Pivot Shift test. The bifurcate ridge separates the two bundles at the femoral attachment and both bundles receive their blood supply from the middle geniculate artery.

Petersen and Zantop describe the anatomy of the ACL. They describe how the AM bundle is tight with flexion and resists anterior translation and how the PL bundle is tight with extension and resists rotary instability. They state the ACL becomes more horizontal with flexion and the AM bundle is high and deep in the femoral notch, while the PL bundle is shallow and low on the femoral notch. The AM bundle originates at the 10:30 position and the PL bundle at the 9:30 position on the femur. On the tibia, the AM bundle is anterior to the PL bundle.

*Zantop et al. reviewed the anatomy in addition to the structural and mechanical properties of the ACL.* *They describe the AM bundle as tight in flexion and the PL tight in extension.* *They describe that the tibial and femoral insertions are 3.5 times larger than the mid substance.* In addition, they discuss the structural properties of a 10mm wide bone-patellar-tendon-bone graft with a quadrupled hamstring graft.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Tight in flexion, loose in extension and more isometric through the range of motion describes the anteromedial bundle
Answer 2: The anteromedial bundle is loose in extension. The posteromedial bundle is loose in mid flexion. Both receive their blood supply from the middle geniculate artery
Answer 3: The anteromedial bundle is tight in flexion and more isometric, but the posterolateral bundle is responsible for rotatory stability
Answer 5: The anteromedial bundle is more isometric (tightest in flexion). The posterolateral bundle is tight in extension. Both receive their blood supply from the middle geniculate artery.
