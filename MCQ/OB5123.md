## Question

A 10-month-old girl is brought to the emergency room by her father, reporting that she has been fussy and not moving her right leg for the past 4 to 5 days. He does not recall any recent injuries or illnesses. She has swelling and ecchymosis of her thigh. A radiograph is shown in Figure A. Which of the following aspects of her presentation is NOT a risk factor for non-accidental trauma?

![[q5123-a-ap-femur-fx.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Age less than 12 months
2. Unknown mechanism of injury
3. Female gender
4. Skin findings
5. Femoral fracture

## Correct option

**3. Female gender**

## Answer explanation

Gender is not known to be a risk factor for non-accidental trauma (NAT).

Child abuse is a serious cause of morbidity and mortality in young children. It is especially important to properly diagnose these injuries, as there is a 30-50% chance of repeat abuse and 5-10% chance of death. Associated findings often include a history of prior injuries, unknown mechanism of injury, ecchymosis, burns, and social or family difficulties at home. Radiographs showing corner fractures, bucket handle fractures (corner fracture seen en face), and multiple fractures in various stages of healing, are all concerning findings. Any patients suspected of having a non-accidental trauma should be worked up with broader imaging and social work evaluation. Specifically, femur fractures in non-ambulatory children should be considered non-accidental trauma until proven otherwise.

*Baldwin et al performed a retrospective case-control study in patients younger than 4 years old with femur fractures comparing children with non-accidental vs accidental mechanism of injury.* **They found** that a history suspicious of abuse, evidence of prior injury, and age less than 18 months were all risk factors. They created a predictive score in which patients with all 3 had a 92% chance of NAT mechanism.

*Hui et al performed a retrospective single-institution cohort study of all patients younger than 3 years old with femur fractures.* **They found** an overall 11% rate of NAT in this population with 17% in patients younger than 12 months old. Age less than 12 months, non-ambulatory status, delayed presentation, unwitnessed or unclear mechanism of injury, and other associated injuries were also all significant risk factors in their cohort.

Figure A shows an AP radiograph of both femurs demonstrating a spiral, oblique, midshaft fracture of the right femur.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1, 2, 4, 5 - Age less than 12 months, unclear/unknown mechanism of injury, ecchymosis, burns and other skin findings, and femur fracture have all been found to be risk factors of non-accidental trauma in the pediatric population.
