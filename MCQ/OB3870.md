## Question

Figure A shows a clinical image of a 45-year-old female who complains of swelling and pain over the lateral aspect of the fifth metatarsophalangeal joint. She has tried all nonoperative recommendations with no improvement. Figure B shows an AP radiograph of her foot. What surgical treatment is indicated in this scenario?

## Options

1. Lateral Condylar Resection
2. Metatarsal Head Resection
3. Distal Osteotomy
4. Proximal Osteotomy
5. Diaphyseal Osteotomy

## Correct option

**1. Lateral Condylar Resection**

## Answer explanation

The patient has an enlarged fifth metatarsal head with a painful lateral prominence that has failed nonoperative measures. The best surgical option in this scenario is lateral condylar resection.

Bunionette deformities present as a painful prominence over the 5th metatarsal head. They can be classified according to Coughlin as Types I-III. Type I deformities have enlarged metatarsal head or exostosis with normal fifth metatarsal width reported as less than 13mm. Lateral condylar resections for Type I deformities are to include approximately the lateral third of the metatarsal head or up to the lateral articular margin. Complications following condylar resection include metatarsophalangeal joint subluxation, persistent pain, and recurrence of lateral eminence.

*Cohen et al. reviewed the bunionette deformity presentation and treatment.* **The authors state** that lateral condylar resection is effective in patients with a lateral callosity and Type I deformity.

Figure A shows a clinical image of a patient with a lateral prominence. Figure B shows a radiograph of a patient with a Type I bunionette with normal 4-5 IMA and large metatarsal head. Illustration A shows a postoperative image of a patient after lateral condylar resection

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Metatarsal head resection is indicated as a salvage procedure only with a high complication rate.
Answer 3: Distal metatarsal osteotomies would be indicated in patients with mildly increased 4-5 IMA of 8-12 degrees.
Answer 4: Proximal osteotomies are only recommended in patients who remain symptomatic following distal osteotomies.
Answer 5: Diaphyseal osteotomies are recommended in patients with large 4-5 IMA greater than 12 degrees.
