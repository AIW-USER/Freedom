## Question

What is the most appropriate surgical treatment for a stage III symptomatic scapholunate advanced collapsed (SLAC) wrist?

## Options

1. Radioscapholunate arthrodesis
2. Scaphotrapeziotrapezoid arthrodesis
3. Scaphocapitate arthrodesis
4. Proximal row carpectomy
5. Scaphoid excision and capitate-lunate-triquetrum-hamate arthrodesis

## Correct option

**5. Scaphoid excision and capitate-lunate-triquetrum-hamate arthrodesis**

## Answer explanation

SLAC is the end result of chronic scapholunate instability. The arthritis follows a predictable pattern. Stage I disease involves cartilage loss between the waist of the scaphoid and the radial styloid. In stage II, the arthritis progresses to include the proximal pole of the scaphoid and the scaphoid fossa of the radius. Finally, stage III goes on to include arthritis of the capitolunate joint. The only treatment option that addresses all of the sites of arthritis is the scaphoid excision and four corner fusion.
