## Question

An 18-year-old female presents with left medial foot pain. A radiograph of the left foot is pictured in Figure A. She has failed conservative management, including shoe modification, anti-inflammatory medications and cast immobilization. You recommend surgical intervention. The muscle attached to the tendon insertion that must be elevated and subsequently reattached is innervated by which of the following nerves?

![[q5327-a-xr.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Deep peroneal nerve
2. Lateral plantar nerve
3. Medial plantar nerve
4. Superficial peroneal nerve
5. Tibial nerve

## Correct option

**5. Tibial nerve**

## Answer explanation

Excision of an accessory navicular requires elevation and subsequent reattachment of the tibialis posterior tendon insertion, which is innervated by the tibial nerve.

Accessory navicular is an anatomic variant present in 2-21% of the population and is usually asymptomatic. Symptoms include medial arch pain and swelling that worsens with activity. Initial treatment is activity modification, NSAIDs, shoe wear modification and a period of cast immobilization. Patients who fail conservative management may undergo excision of the accessory ossicle. This involves elevating the insertion of the tibialis posterior to access the accessory bone, followed by reattachment of the tendon.

*Macnicol et al reported on 47 patients with an accessory navicular who underwent one of two procedures: (1) Simple excision, (2) Kidner operation (excision + rerouting of tibialis posterior).* Both procedures were successful in relieving symptoms. The Kidner procedure did not confer any advantage over simple excision. Failures resulted from errors in selection of patients and surgical technique.

*Kopp et al retrospectively reviewed 13 patients who underwent surgery for a symptomatic accessory navicular.* **The authors concluded** that when conservative measures fail to relieve symptoms, simple excision of the accessory navicular and anatomic repair of the tibialis posterior provides reliable pain relief and patient satisfaction.

Figure A is an AP radiograph of the left foot showing a type 2 accessory navicular. Illustration A is a picture depicting the insertion of the tibialis posterior tendon to the accessory navicular, which must be elevated to allow excision.

Incorrect Responses:
Answers 1-4: The tibialis posterior is not innervated by any of these nerves.
