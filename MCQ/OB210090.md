## Question

A 12-year-old female presents to the emergency room after she fell and sustained the injury seen in Figures A and B. On examination, her skin is closed and she is neurovascularly intact. The on-call orthopedic surgeon is unavailable for the next 8 hours due to multiple emergent operative cases. The emergency room performs a closed reduction followed by application of a long arm splint at 90 degrees. Post reduction films are seen in Figure C and D. She is treated nonoperatively with her splint removed at 4 weeks in the orthopedic department. At four months the patient is noted to have loss of elbow extension. ## Options

1. Females are at higher risk of loss of extension compared to males
2. Children >9 are at higher risk of loss of extention
3. Improper closed reduction by emergency room staff.
4. Immobilization in a splint for 4 weeks.
5. Failure to perform open reduction internal fixation.

## Correct option

**4. Immobilization in a splint for 4 weeks.**

## Answer explanation

The patient sustained an elbow dislocation following the fall. The elbow should be reduced promptly even if an orthopedic provider is not available and immobilization >2 weeks puts patients at risk for loss of extension.

Pediatric elbow fracture-dislocations should be treated promptly with a closed reduction to avoid chondral injury and discomfort. The reduction should be performed in the emergency department by the most available provider under sedation to allow for adequate relaxation of muscles. Once reduced, the joint should be ranged to assess for stability and placed into a splint or sling for 1-2 weeks to minimize stiffness. Post reduction imaging should be obtained to evaluate for incarcerated fragments. Medial epicondyle fixation is controversial with the only definitive indication being an incarcerated fragment. If patients remain neurovascularly intact they are able to be discharged home with outpatient orthopedic follow-up.

*Murphy et al. reviewed 145 elbow dislocations in pediatric patients.* **They found** that risk factors for less than excellent outcomes were multiple associated fractures, operative intervention, and prolonged immobilization. **They found** that immobilization longer than two weeks led to loss of terminal extension. **They conclude** that the majority of pediatric elbow dislocations experience good to excellent functional outcomes.

*Protzman retrospectively reviewed 49 patients with closed elbow dislocations all treated with closed reductions.* **They state** all reductions were reduced within 3 hours and most within 1 hour. They did not comment if different outcomes were seen between those treated within 1 hour versus 3 hours. **They found** patients treated with longer immobilization may develop more pronounced flexion contractures. **They conclude** that immediate reduction and immobilization for one to five days produces excellent outcomes.

Figures A and B show an AP and lateral x-ray of a pediatric elbow dislocation with an associated medial epicondyle fracture. Figure C shows a fluoroscopic image of the same patient following reduction of the elbow joint. Figure D shows a post reduction x-ray showing a medical epicondyle fracture without incarceration.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1: There is no evidence that female children are at higher risk for elbow stiffness following elbow fracture dislocations.
Answers 2: There is no evidence that a patient >9 is at higher risk for stiffness following pediatric elbow fracture dislocations.
Answers 3: There is no evidence that reduction performed by an emergency room physician rather than an orthopedic surgeon leads to worse outcomes. If the orthopedic team is unavailable the reduction should be performed promptly by the emergency room.
Answers 5: Imaging shows no incarcerated fragments. Fixation of medial epicondyle fractures is controversial with the risk of missed treatment being instability not stiffness.
