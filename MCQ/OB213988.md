## Options

2. Radioscaphoid, radial styloid, capitolunate

4. Radial styloid, radioscaphoid, capitolunate
5. Capitolunate, radial styloid, radioscaphoid

## Correct option

**4. Radial styloid, radioscaphoid, capitolunate**

## Answer explanation

The radiographs demonstrate scapholunate dissociation, which will progress to scaphoid lunate advanced collapse (SLAC) if left untreated. Arthritic changes in patients with SLAC wrist progress in a predictable pattern from the radial styloid to the radioscaphoid and capitolunate joints, typically sparing the radiolunate joint.

Chronic scapholunate ligament injury destabilizes the proximal row of the carpus, allowing the scaphoid to flex volarly and the lunate to extend. This causes an abnormal distribution of forces across the carpus, resulting in a predictable and well-documented progression of degenerative changes. Arthritis first develops at the radial styloid, where there is a point of contact between with the abnormally rotated scaphoid, and eventually progresses to involve the entire radioscaphoid articulation. The capitolunate joint is affected next, as the capitate migrates proximally into the widened scapholunate interval and abuts the lunate. The radiolunate joint is relatively congruent in all positions of lunate rotation and therefore is typically spared from these arthritic changes, though some authors have described involvement late in the disease process. Surgical treatment options include radial styloidectomy, proximal row carpectomy (PRC), and four corner fusion, depending on which articulations are affected.

*Kitay and colleagues review the anatomy and biomechanics of scapholunate instability and scapholunate advanced collapse.* **They highlight** the critical role that the scapholunate interosseous ligament plays in stabilizing not only the scapholunate joint, but the entire carpus, and describe the spectrum of injury that results from its disruption. **They propose** a framework for approaching these injuries based on the presence of static versus dynamic instability, whether the ligament is repairable versus irreparable, and the degree of arthritic involvement.

*Watson et al. also reviewed the spectrum from scaphoid instability to degenerative SLAC wrist.* *These authors similarly underscore the importance of understanding the kinematics of the periscaphoid anatomy and how specific injuries to this region contribute to this pathologic process in order to properly diagnose and treat these conditions.*
