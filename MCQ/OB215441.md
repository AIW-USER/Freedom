## Question

A 37-year-old male Genoese shipbuilder presents to the clinic complaining of progressively worsening left-hand paresthesias over the past 2 months. Numbness and tingling are present in the ring and little fingers. In his free time, is an avid cyclist and bikes over 100 miles per week. On physical exam, his grip strength is weak compared to his right hand, and his little finger rests in an abducted position, as seen in Figure A. These physical exam findings are caused by weakness of what muscle?

![[q215441-a-screen-shot-2021-02-13-at-1-10-30-pm.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Extensor digiti quinti
2. Abductor digiti minimi
3. Second dorsal interosseous
4. Third dorsal interosseous
5. Third palmar interosseous

## Correct option

**5. Third palmar interosseous**

## Answer explanation

Weakness of the third palmar interosseous muscle is responsible for the physical exam finding seen in this patient. A positive Wartenberg sign is seen when the little finger rests in an abducted position and occurs when there is unopposed action of the extensor digiti quinti (posterior interosseous nerve) due to third palmar interosseous (ulnar nerve) weakness.

Ulnar tunnel syndrome is one type of ulnar nerve compressive neuropathy that leads to weakness of the intrinsic hand muscles, ring and little finger digital flexion, and thumb adduction. Ulnar tunnel syndrome is caused by direct compression of the ulnar nerve in Guyon's canal. It is commonly known as "handlebar palsy", and may be seen in cyclist. Patients may present with paresthesias in the little and ring finger, weakness with grip/grasp strength, and positive Froment or Wartenberg signs.

Staples et. el. reviewed cubital tunnel syndrome pathoanatomy and current treatment concepts. They found that, much like ulnar tunnel syndrome, patients with ulnar nerve compressive neuropathies present with typical findings of paresthesias in the little and ring fingers, weakness with grip strength, and difficulty with fine motor tasks such as buttoning shirts. Patients with advanced disease may present with a positive Wartenberg sign, Froment sign, or Jeanne sign. They concluded that early identification and appropriate treatment of ulnar neuropathy is essential in preserving ulnar nerve function.

*Bertelli et. al. evaluated little finger abduction and adduction testing in 34 patients with known ulnar nerve injuries.* **They found** that every patient with an ulnar nerve injury had some level of dysfunction with little finger adduction. 19 of 20 (95%) of patients with ulnar nerve compressive neuropathy had partial paralysis of little finger adduction. **They concluded** that little finger adduction testing is a useful diagnostic modality in identifying patients with ulnar nerve compressive neuropathy.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Extensor digiti quinti is innervated by the posterior interosseous nerve. Its' action is unopposed in ulnar tunnel syndrome leading to a positive Wartenberg sign
Answer 2: Abductor digiti minimi is innervated by the ulnar nerve. Its action is to abduct the little finger. Weakness would result in an the little finger being adducted, not abducted.
Answers 3-4: The dorsal interossei are innervated by the ulnar nerve. They function to abduct the fingers. Weakness would result in an adducted little finger, with inability to abduct the digits.
