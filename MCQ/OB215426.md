## Question

Figure A is the radiograph of a 55-year-old female who is a poorly-controlled diabetic with neuropathy and peripheral vascular disease (PVD) that underwent ankle open reduction internal fixation (ORIF) two years ago at an outside facility. She was noncompliant with her immediate postoperative weight-bearing instructions and went on to fixation failure. She subsequently underwent revision ORIF with bone grafting soon after as seen in Figure B. One year ago, she underwent hardware removal secondary to irritation. Current radiographs are seen in Figure C. She complains of continued pain in the ankle which is not manageable with non-surgical treatments and would like to proceed with surgical treatment. She asks if limb salvage is an option for her. Which surgery is most indicated at this time?

## Options

1. Ankle arthrodesis
2. Below knee amputation
3. Re-revision ORIF with bone grafting
4. Supramalleolar osteotomy and total ankle arthroplasty
5. Triple arthrodesis

## Correct option

**1. Ankle arthrodesis**

## Answer explanation

Post-traumatic tibiotalar arthritis in a diabetic patient with peripheral neuropathy and PVD is most appropriately treated with ankle arthrodesis.

Diabetic ankle fractures are at increased risk of prolonged healing, hardware failure, and infection. In these patients, enhanced fixation should be employed at the index procedure. This may include multiple quadricortical syndesmotic screws (even in the absence of syndesmotic injury), tibiotalar Steinmann pins or hindfoot nailing, ankle spanning external fixation, augmentation with intramedullary fibular K-wires, and use of stiffer, more rigid fibular plates (instead of 1/3 tubular plates). Additionally, there should be delayed weight-bearing for approximately 8-12 weeks, instead of 4-8 weeks in non-diabetic patients. Appropriate treatment following failed ORIF in a diabetic patient with peripheral neuropathy who wishes to proceed with limb salvage includes either arthrodesis or revision ORIF with increased fixation. The development of post-traumatic arthritis may best be treated with ankle arthrodesis in this patient with PVD.

*Vaudreuil et al. retrospectively reviewed ankle fractures in patients with diabetes mellitus (DM) who fail initial operative management with attempts at limb salvage.* **They found** an overall rate of limb salvage of 82%. All patients who achieved successful limb salvage ended up with a clinically fused ankle joint. **They concluded** that in this complicated patient population, it is difficult to achieve limb salvage with an end result of a functional ankle joint as all patients who underwent revision ORIF also ended up with clinically fused ankle joints. Primary fusion procedures (tibio-talar arthrodesis, tibio-talar-calcaneal nail) were associated with a high rate of limb salvage and a decreased number of operations.

*Wallace et al. reviewed unstable ankle fractures in diabetics with peripheral neuropathy treated with primary ankle arthrodesis.* **They report** that 62% of patients achieved a successful clinical outcome at a mean follow-up of 48 months following the initial surgery. 23% and 8% had a successful outcome after a second and third surgery, respectively. **They concluded** that in spite of the high risk for complications and initial failure, primary ankle fusion is a reasonable option for diabetic neuropathic patients who develop acute neuropathic arthropathy following ankle fracture.

Figures A is the AP radiograph of an unstable ankle fracture that has failed ORIF. Figure B is the AP radiograph of a revision ankle ORIF. Figure C is the lateral radiograph demonstrating advanced tibiotalar post-traumatic arthritis

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Below knee amputation may be a reasonable treatment option in a patient with diabetes and peripheral neuropathy, but if limb salvage is desired, an ankle arthrodesis may be more appropriate
Answer 3: Revision ORIF with bone grafting may be employed in a diabetic patient with peripheral neuropathy, but would not be indicated with this amount of post-traumatic arthritis and healed fractures
Answer 4: The presence of PVD is a contraindication to performing a TAA
Answer 5: A triple arthrodesis would not address failure at the tibiotalar joint
