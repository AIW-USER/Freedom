## Question

A 56-year-old male presents to the clinic for his 8 week visit following his L5-S1 lumbar fusion. He states that he has been experiencing trouble with his sex life following surgery, citing the inability to ejaculate. The patient is suspected to be suffering from retrograde ejaculation. Which of the following approaches was likely performed to put the patient at most risk for this complication?

## Options

1. anterior lumbar interbody fusion, retroperitoneal approach
2. anterior lumbar interbody fusion, transperitoneal approach
3. lateral lumbar interbody fusion
4. extreme lateral interbody fusion
5. oblique lumbar interbody fusion

## Correct option

**2. anterior lumbar interbody fusion, transperitoneal approach**

## Answer explanation

This 56-year-old male presents to the clinic for follow-up of his L5-S1 lumbar body fusion with evidence of retrograde ejaculation, which most commonly occurs following anterior lumbar interbody fusion (ALIF), transperitoneal approach (Answer 2).

A number of approaches can be performed for lumbar interbody fusions in addition to the ALIF, which includes the lateral, extreme lateral, oblique lumbar, transforaminal, and posterior approaches. The ALIF approach, in particular, can be further broken down into transperitoneal and retroperitoneal approaches, where the former involves incising the peritoneum and navigating through the abdominal contents, while the latter consists of remaining extraperitoneal, retracting the peritoneal contents. With the transperitoneal approach, the risk for retrograde ejaculation is substantially higher, estimated at 4 to 10 times the risk. The believed pathophysiology behind the phenomenon revolves around iatrogenic injury to the hypogastric plexus traversing the L5/S1 interspace, which causes sympathetic dysfunction of the internal bladder sphincter during ejaculation, facilitating retrograde flow.

*Body et al. performed a systematic review and pooled analysis detailing the incidence and risk factors of retrograde ejaculation in 2,503 males undergoing ALIF procedures.* **The authors noted** a 2.3% overall rate of retrograde ejaculation, with a significantly higher rate seen in the transperitoneal approach (8.6%) compared with the retroperitoneal approach (3.2%). Usage of recombinant human bone morphogenetic protein–2 (rhBMP-2) was also found to increase risk for retrograde ejaculation (5.0 vs. 1.8% with bone graft alone). **The authors conclude** by noting many retrograde ejaculation cases end up resolving (45.8%) between 3 and 48 months post-operatively.

*Bateman et al. performed a systematic review and meta-analysis of 76 articles (11,410 patients) examining the complication profile of the ALIF approach.* **The authors noted** an overall complication rate of 14.1%, with 3% requiring a reoperation. The most common complication was venous injury (3.2%), followed by retrograde ejaculation (2.7%), other neurologic injuries (2.0%), and postoperative ileus (1.4%). The transperitoneal approach was found to be a significant risk factor for the complication of retrograde ejaculation. **The authors concluded** the ALIF to be a safe approach with a low complication rate but highlighted that potential complications are often related to surgical technique and cite the importance of a systematic, stepwise approach to the lumbar spine to mitigate risk.

*Burkus et al. performed a prospective, randomized, multicenter trial (n=508) to assess the incidence and risk factors for postoperative development of retrograde ejaculation following L4-5 or L5-S1 fusion via the ALIF approach.* **The authors noted** a 4.1% incidence of retrograde ejaculation, noting the transperitoneal (vs retroperitoneal) approach being the only significant risk factor for the development of retrograde ejaculation (8.6 vs. 1.6%, p=0.007). While rhBMP-2 was also associated with increased rates of retrograde ejaculation (3.4 vs. 1.7%), this was not significant (p=0.242). **The authors concluded** that the surgical approach is the single-largest risk factor for the complication of retrograde ejaculation.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: the anterior lumbar interbody fusion, retroperitoneal approach puts the ureters at most risk
Answer 3-5: lateral, "extreme lateral," and oblique interbody fusion approaches are most commonly associated with lumbar plexus injuries
