## Options

1. Reversibly binds to the 50S subunit inhibiting bacterial protein synthesis
2. Binds to the transpeptidase enzyme and inhibits cell wall synthesis
3. Irreversibly binds to the 30S ribosomal subunit inhibiting bacterial protein synthesis
4. Prevents formation of the initiation complex between the the 30S and 50S subunits of the ribosome
5. Binds to the cell wall and disrupts cross-linking of peptidoglycans during cell wall synthesis.

## Correct option

**2. Binds to the transpeptidase enzyme and inhibits cell wall synthesis**

## Answer explanation

This patient has undergone a reverse total shoulder arthroplasty (rTSA) and has a history that is concerning for periprosthetic joint infection involving Cutibacterium acnes (C. acnes, formerly known as Propionibacterium acnes). Penicillin remains the best antibiotic, due to its low minimal inhibitory concentration (MIC), for the treatment of C. acnes.

Periprosthetic shoulder infection is the major cause of revisions within the first 2 years after shoulder arthroplasty with a ~4% incidence after both primary anatomic total arthroplasty (TSA) and rTSA. Risk factors for periprosthetic infection include uncontrolled medical comorbidities, smoking, and intra-articular steroid injection within 3 months of surgery. The most commonly colonized organism is C. acnes (formerly P. acnes) which is concentrated in the axilla and is a gram-positive, facultative, anaerobic rod that ferments lactose. It has a mean duration of culture incubation between 7-21 days; as such intra-operative cultures in patients with suspected C. acnes infections should be held for 21 days. Penicillin remains the standard antibiotic of choice for the treatment of C. acnes.

*Achermann et al. discuss C. acnes and its role in shoulder prosthetic joint infections.* **They note** that treatment requires a combination of surgery and a prolonged antibiotic treatment regimen to successfully eliminate the remaining bacteria. **They further note** that most authors suggest a course of 3 to 6 months of antibiotic treatment, including 2 to 6 weeks of intravenous treatment with a beta-lactam.

*Bjerke-Kroll et al. review the incidence of various bacteria in periprosthetic joint infection (PJI) which* **they note** occurs in 0.3%-1.7% of total hip arthroplasties (THAs) and 0.8%-1.9% of total knee arthroplasties (TKAs). They analyzed organisms from PJIs at one institution for temporal trends over 14 years. **They noted** an increase in the proportion of MRSA, Streptococcus viridans, and C. acnes. **The authors conclude** that these increases in trends may warrant further discussion on pre-surgical MRSA screening, increased incubation time to detect P. acnes, and dental prophylaxis against S. viridans.
