## Question

A 27-year-old triathlete comes to the office with lateral foot pain and bruising after slipping on the pool deck while training for an upcoming event. Radiographs are obtained, and she is diagnosed with a zone III fifth metatarsal base fracture. She would like to compete in 12 weeks and states that her primary goal is to be running again in six weeks. Which of the following is the most appropriate treatment option to discuss with this patient?

## Options

1. Brief period of immobilization followed by early physical therapy
2. Immediate weightbearing in a hard-soled shoe until pain abates
3. Intramedullary screw fixation
4. Non-operative treatment with protected weightbearing in a CAM boot
5. Non-weightbearing in a short leg cast

## Correct option

**3. Intramedullary screw fixation**

## Answer explanation

In athletic patients with zone III fifth metatarsal base fractures, intramedullary screw fixation allows for an earlier return to athletic activity and decreases the risk of nonunion (Answer 3).

Fifth metatarsal base fractures are often treated differently based on the described zone of injury. The Torg anatomic classification system (Illustration A) classifies fifth metatarsal base fractures into three zones, with zone I injuries representing proximal tubercle avulsions termed "Pseudo-Jones" fractures, zone II injuries representing injuries about the metaphyseal-diaphyseal watershed region termed "Jones" fractures, and zone III injuries representing proximal diaphyseal fractures. In zone I fractures, non-union is uncommon, and patients can often be treated with protected weight bearing in a hard-soled shoe or CAM boot. Zone II injuries, given that they occur at a vascular watershed area, have a propensity for non-union (up to 33% in the literature) and are often treated surgically; however, in the non-athlete or recreational athlete, immobilization in a short leg cast can be performed for 6-8 weeks if surgery is not desired. Zone III fractures occur frequently in athletes, particularly in the setting of stress fractures, and should be fixed if the patients wish to return to athletic activities in a more expeditious manner. Intramedullary fixation with a single 4.5, 5.5, or 6.5mm partially-threaded screw is the most common form of fixation used, with patients undergoing a short period of non-weightbearing from 1-3 weeks, followed by a return to running and impact activities at 6 weeks if the surgical site is pain-free and there are signs of radiographic callous.

*Yates et al. published a systematic review and meta-analysis of the treatment of fifth metatarsal base fractures.* *The authors included six studies reviewing a total of 237 patients (51% treated non-operatively versus 49% treated surgically) and found that those in the non-operative group had a significantly higher odds ratio (OR) of fracture non-union with prolonged healing time and a longer time to return to sports.* **The authors concluded** that surgical intervention is recommended for patients presenting with a Jones fracture, as it is found to result in a lesser non-union rate and an improved time to union.

*Smith et al. performed a meta-analysis of the interventions available for treating proximal fifth metatarsal base fractures in adults.* *Six studies assessing 330 patients were included, and* **the authors found** that when comparing surgical and non-surgical management, intramedullary screw fixation results in a shorter time to fracture union, reduced complication rates, and earlier return to pre-injury activities compared to non-surgical cast immobilization. **The authors concluded** that further well-conducted randomized controlled trials are required to determine the optimal management strategy for the different types of proximal fifth metatarsal fractures.

*Polzer et al. developed a classification system and corresponding treatment recommendations for acute fractures of the proximal fifth metatarsal bone based on the current evidence.* **The authors suggest** that contrary to established belief, acute fractures to the proximal fifth metatarsal bone should be classified into two entities only: (1) metaphyseal fractures not extending beyond the distal end of the fourth–fifth intermetatarsal articulation (those that should be treated functionally) and (2) meta-diaphyseal fractures located at the distal end of the fourth–fifth intermetatarsal articulation or just distally (those that require early intramedullary screw fixation).

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1, 2, 4: These would be appropriate management options for patients with zone I, or "Pseudo-Jones" fractures.
Answer 5: Non-weight bearing in a short leg cast for 6-8 weeks is an appropriate option in recreational athletes, or non-athletic patients with zone II or III fractures, so long as the risk of non-union is communicated with the patient in the informed consent process.
