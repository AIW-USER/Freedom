## Question

Which of the following accurately lists the mortality rate of fragility fractures in DECREASING order?

## Options

1. Vertebral compression fracture > hip fracture > distal radius fracture
2. Hip fracture > distal radius fracture > vertebral compression fracture
3. Distal radius fracture > vertebral compression fracture > hip fracture
4. Hip fracture > vertebral compression fracture > distal radius fracture
5. Vertebral compression fracture > distal radius fracture > hip fracture

## Correct option

**4. Hip fracture > vertebral compression fracture > distal radius fracture**

## Answer explanation

Hip fractures have the highest mortality rate of the fragility fractures, while vertebral compression fractures have the lowest and distal radius fractures have not been associated with increased mortality.

One year mortality following a hip fracture is approximately 25%, depending on comorbidities. 1 year mortality following vertebral compression fracture is approximately 7-15%. Distal radius fractures may be associated with an increase the risk of other fragility fractures, but have not demonstrated any increased risk of mortality. Osteoporotic compression fractures can lead to a decrease in volume of the chest and abdominal cavity, causing early satiety and decreased pulmonary function.

*Lyles et al. performed a case control study of 20 patients (10 with compression fractures and 10 without of similar demographics) to assess changes in functional and psychosocial metrics following their fractures.* *Patients with fractures had decreased mobility skills and increased pain with activity.* Psychosocial performance metrics such as self-perception of health were also worse in patients with fractures. **The authors conclude** vertebral compression fractures can lead to serious physical, functional, and psychosocial impairments.

*Leidig-Bruckner et al. analyzed radiologic and qualitative metrics of quality of life on 140 patients (63 with osteoporosis and 77 with low back pain alone).* *The patients with osteoporosis had significantly increased disability scores.* Increasing spinal deformity correlated significantly with increased functional impairments, **suggesting** that vertebral osteoporosis and spinal deformity can have severe effects on quality of life.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1-3 + 5: Of the listed options, hip fractures are associated with the highest rate of one-year mortality, followed by vertebral compression fractures.
