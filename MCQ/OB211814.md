## Question

A 25-year-old male sustains a displaced talar neck fracture and undergoes open reduction with internal fixation. Radiographs at the 8-week post-operative follow-up appointment are depicted in Figures A and B. Which of the following statements is true regarding the radiographic findings?

![[q211814-b-xr-lat.jpg]]

## Options

1. The presence of subchondral lucency conveys an increased risk for talar dome collapse
2. The current radiographic findings are associated with the choice of fixation construct
3. Increased fracture displacement has been associated with the current radiographic findings
4. There is evidence of bony resorption consistent with talar perfusion
5. The talus has developed avascular necrosis with little chance of later revascularization

## Correct option

**4. There is evidence of bony resorption consistent with talar perfusion**

## Answer explanation

The current radiographs depict a positive Hawkins sign, which suggests the talus has revascularized and excludes the presence of avascular necrosis.

Talar neck fractures are the most common fractures of the talus and most often occur secondary to forced ankle dorsiflexion under an axial load. The blood supply to the talus is predominately retrograde. As a result, displaced talar neck fractures have a high rate of avascular necrosis, proportionate to the grade of Hawkins injury. The presence of subchondral lucency following internal fixation indicates revascularization of the talar dome and thus the absence of avascular necrosis.

*Bellamy et al. performed a retrospective study of treatment outcomes of combat-related talus fractures.* **They reported** a positive Hawkins sign at an average of 7 weeks in 59% of the patients and found no correlation of avascular necrosis or subtalar arthritis with time to fixation. **The authors concluded** that delayed internal fixation does not increase the rate of avascular necrosis.

*Vallier et al. performed a retrospective study of 102 talar neck fractures treated with internal fixation.* **They reported** radiographic osteonecrosis in 49% of patients which was associated with fracture comminution (p <0.03) and open fractures (p <0.05), of which 37% of patients with avascular necrosis later developed revascularization without collapse. **The authors concluded** that delayed fixation of talar neck fractures did not affect avascular necrosis rate and higher energy injuries (fracture comminution and open fractures) were associated with increased avascular necrosis rates, post-traumatic arthritis, and worse functional outcomes.

*Elgafy et al. retrospectively reviewed 60 talus fractures managed both operatively and nonoperatively.* **They reported** subtalar arthritis in 53.3% and avascular necrosis in 16.6% of patients, the ladder of which occurred most commonly with talar body and neck fractures. **The authors reported** that clinical outcomes declined from the talar process to the neck and ultimately to body fractures, which were associated with the worst outcomes.

Figure A and B are the AP and lateral radiographs of the right ankle demonstrating internal fixation of a talar neck fracture with the presence of Hawkins sign and absence of tibiotalar and subtalar arthritis.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The presence of subchondral lucency suggests revascularization of the talus and is referred to as a positive Hawkins sign. This decreases the risk of subchondral collapse that often occurs with avascular necrosis.
Answer 2: The type of internal fixation construct does not affect the rate of avascular necrosis. The quality of the reduction has been shown to affect the risk of avascular necrosis.
Answer 3: Increased fracture displacement is associated with vascular disruption which has been associated with higher avascular necrosis rates. In this case, the presence of a Hawkins sign excludes avascular necrosis of the talus.
Answer 5: The patient does not have evidence of avascular necrosis by the presence of the Hawkins sign. However, patients that initially don't have a Hawkins sign may later develop revascularization without collapse in up to 44% of cases.
