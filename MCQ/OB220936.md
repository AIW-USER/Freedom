## Question

A 9-month-old male presents to the children’s hospital with the inability to bear weight after his parents report that he fell while running. Plain radiographs of his right femur demonstrate a spiral diaphyseal fracture. Which of the following additional physical exam or radiographic findings would strengthen the decision to involve social work and child protective services?

## Options

1. A lytic lesion present at the site of the fracture
2. Blue sclera
3. Posterior rib fractures
4. Low serum vitamin D level
5. Metaphyseal fraying

## Correct option

**3. Posterior rib fractures**

## Answer explanation

Injury inconsistent with history, as present in this patient, especially long bone fractures in non-ambulatory children (9-month-old patients typically do not have the ability to run), is concerning for pediatric abuse. The presence of posterior rib fractures is additionally concerning for pediatric abuse, as these can occur during anterior/posterior compression of the thoracic cavity (answer 3).

Pediatric abuse is one of the most common causes of death in children, and providers must be on alert for its presence at all times. Patients may present with an injury inconsistent with the reported history. Specific injuries characteristic of pediatric abuse include metaphysical fractures, long-bone fractures in non-ambulatory patients, posterior rib fractures, scapula fractures, sternal fractures, and spinous process fractures. A full body exam should be performed to assess for the presence of multiple bruises or burns, which may be suspicious for child abuse. If suspicion for pediatric abuse is present, the patient should be further evaluated with a skeletal survey, and social work/child protective services should be notified immediately prior to the patient leaving the hospital.

*Greenhill and Herman reviewed the management of pediatric femoral shaft fractures.* *They cover age-specific and fracture-specific interventions and discuss the complications that may occur with these injuries.* **They conclude** that non-accidental trauma should be ruled out prior to utilizing age and injury-specific treatment of pediatric patients with femur fractures.

*Jevsevar et al. published updated clinical practice guidelines for the management of pediatric femoral shaft fractures on behalf of the American Academy of Orthopedic Surgeons.* Their recommendations cover non-operative and operative interventions for different age groups and indications for specific forms of surgical stabilization. **They concluded** that the literature published in the interim since the prior guidelines had been published did not provide any evidence that would substantially alter their prior recommendations.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: This finding is concerning for a pathological fracture and would not heighten suspicion for child abuse by itself.
Answer 2: Blue sclera is a finding consistent with osteogenesis imperfecta, and the additional presence of this finding would not heighten suspicion for child abuse by itself.
Answers 4 and 5: These findings are more characteristic of rickets and warrant nutritional consultation rather than social work/child services.
