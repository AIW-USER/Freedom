## Question

A 48-year-old middle school teacher presents to your clinic to evaluate hand pain and paresthesia. She reports that symptoms primarily occur at night, and affect the volar aspect of her radial three digits. Provocative testing with repeated tapping and manual compression in proximity to the intersection of the distal wrist crease and palmaris longus tendon recreate her symptoms. Which of the following best describes the anatomic insertion point of the muscle most likely to be affected in advanced stages of this compressive neuropathy?

## Options

1. Radial side of the base of the first proximal phalanx
2. Ulnar side of the base of the first proximal phalanx
3. Dorsal base of the distal phalanx of the thumb
4. Base of the distal phalanx of the index finger
5. Radial side of the shaft of the first metacarpal

## Correct option

**1. Radial side of the base of the first proximal phalanx**

## Answer explanation

The abductor pollicis brevis (APB) is a thenar muscle solely innervated by the median nerve distal to the carpal tunnel, through the recurrent motor branch of the median nerve. Given this anatomic relationship, it is often utilized to evaluate motor function and muscle strength in cases of carpal tunnel syndrome (CTS).

Carpal tunnel syndrome (CTS) results from median nerve compression within the carpal tunnel, a confined passage formed by the carpal bones and transverse carpal ligament. This tunnel houses the median nerve and nine flexor tendons. Compression of the median nerve can manifest as sensory disturbances (numbness, tingling, and pain in the radial three digits and radial half of the fourth digit), and motor weakness in the hand, particularly affecting thumb abduction due to APB weakness. Chronically untreated or advanced CTS may present with visible thenar eminence atrophy, in part due to atrophy of the ABP muscle.

*Neral et al. investigated the diagnostic utility of motor examination in CTS,* **concluding** that manual muscle testing (MMT) of thenar muscles is unreliable. They compared median-innervated thenar muscle strength (i.e., APB) with ulnar-innervated intrinsic hand muscle strength (i.e., first dorsal interosseous [FDI]) in an attempt to account for patient-determined factors that may skew motor strength evaluation. **They concluded** that comparisons to ulnar nerve innervated muscle strength did not improve the sensitivity or specificity of the MMT examination, but that the use of the dynamometer did lead to successful detection of CTS in truly positive cases as determined by evaluating APB strength versus either ABP strength in the contralateral hand, or FDI strength in the ipsilateral hand.

*Mathews et al. evaluated the reliability of a hand-held dynamometer for measuring palmar thumb abduction strength (TAS) in individuals with and without CTS.* **Their findings demonstrated** excellent intrarater and interrater reliability, advocating for the use of dynamometry to quantify and monitor TAS deficits.

*Padua et al.'s comprehensive review of CTS detailed the clinical presentation, diagnostic process, and management options for the syndrome.* **They emphasized** the progressive nature of CTS, the high variability in clinical practices surrounding diagnosis, and the importance of early intervention to mitigate long-term morbidity. Given the high prevalence of carpal tunnel syndrome, combined with the impact on patient quality of life and overall health system cost burden, **they advocate** for thoughtful research initiatives that seek to unveil meaningful conclusions to bridge the remaining gaps in diagnosis and treatment of this condition.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer choice 2: The ulnar side of the base of the first proximal phalanx is the insertion point of the adductor pollicis, which is ulnar-innervated and therefore not affected in CTS.
Answer choice 3: The dorsal base of the distal phalanx of the thumb describes the insertion of the extensor pollicis longus, a muscle innervated by the posterior interosseous nerve.
Answer choice 4: The base of the distal phalanx of the index finger is where the flexor digitorum profundus to the index finger inserts. As this muscle receives its innervation from the anterior interosseous nerve proximal to the carpal tunnel, it would be expected to be spared in CTS.
Answer choice 5: The radial side of the shaft of the first metacarpal is the insertion site of the abductor pollicis longus, a muscle innervated by the posterior interosseous nerve.
