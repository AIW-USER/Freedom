## Options

1. Neurofibromatosis
2. Holt-Oram Syndrome
3. McCune-Albright Syndrome
4. Fibular hemimelia
5. Ellis-van-Creveld Syndrome

## Correct option

**3. McCune-Albright Syndrome**

## Answer explanation

Given the café-au-lait spots with the jagged borders and the fibrous dysplasia seen in the hips, the most likely diagnosis is McCune-Albright Syndrome (MAS).

MAS is a condition in which patients present with dermatologic abnormalities, endocrine abnormalities (precocious puberty frequently) and polyostotic fibrous dysplasia. Consistent with this condition are café-au-lait spots that have jagged borders, akin to the “Coast of Maine”.

*Volkl et al. review the clinical manifestations of MAS.* **They note** that multiple organ systems may be involved (including cardiovascular and hepatobiliary). The classic triad of MAS consists of endocrinopathies, polyostotic fibrous dysplasia and café-au-lait spots.

*Parekh et al. review the etiology, histopathology and clinical presentation of fibrous dysplasia.* Fibrous dysplasia is caused by mutations in the Gs-alpha protein. Fibrous tissue distorts the normal architecture of metaphyseal bone to produce a “ground glass” appearance. Although surgical intervention can be used for symptomatic lesions, it has limited success in McCune-Albright Syndrome.

Figure A shows a clinical photo of a patient with café-au-lait spots associated with MAS. Note the jagged borders (in contrast to the smooth borders of café-au-lait spots seen in neurofibromatosis). Figure B shows a pelvic radiograph with polyostotic fibrous dysplasia. Note the ground glass appearance seen within the femurs.

Incorrect Answers
Answer 1: Neurofibromatosis has smooth bordered café-au-lait spots; it is not associated with fibrous dysplasia.
Answer 2: Holt-Oram Syndrome is associated with radial club hand and cardiac defects
Answer 4: Fibular hemimelia is not seen with this condition
Answer 5: Ellis-van-Crevald syndrome is an autosomal recessive condition affecting the EVC gene. Mutations in the EVC gene give rise to post-axial polydactyly, short-limbed dwarfism, congenital heart defects and malformed carpal bones.
