## Options

2. Metastatic disease is common at the time of presentation

## Correct option

**3. MRI studies are in most cases sufficient for diagnosis without the need for formal biopsy**

## Answer explanation

The MRI studies and histology are consistent with a sacral chordoma. While there can be increased suspicion for a chordoma based on an MRI showing a large sacral mass, a biopsy is still required to confirm the diagnosis given that lymphoma and chondrosarcoma can also present with a large destructive sacral masses.

Chordomas are malignant and aggressive tumors that are primitively notochordal in origin. Imaging most often demonstrates a large destructive sacral mass and, unlike some tumors which may often be diagnosed by imaging alone (lipoma, hemangioma, etc.), a biopsy is required for confirmation of diagnosis. The majority of chordomas (~50%) are found in the sacrum, distal to the S1 body, and are slow-growing. As such, most patients with chordomas do not present with any motor deficits other than bowel/bladder incontinence. However, on diagnosis, as many as 30-50% of these tumors have already metastasized leading to a 60% 5-year survival and a 25% long-term survival. The treatment of a sacral chordoma is en-block wide resection with or without radiation. There is no role for chemotherapy for these tumors as they are generally chemoresistant.

*Sun et al. presented an update on the pathogenesis of the chordoma.* *The authors cited the increasing evidence of the notochordal origin of such tumors while illustrating signaling pathways and identifying crucial targets in chordomagenesis.* *They go on to describe several molecular mechanisms that may lead to the formation of the chordoma.*

*Weber et al. reviewed the entity of malignant bone tumors with respect to anatomic location and age of presentation.* *They described that the chordoma has an anatomic predilection for the sacrum and typically present in older patients.* *They further discuss the importance of accurate biopsy of these lesions to assist with diagnosis as well as to help determine specific tumor characteristics and treatment options.* **The authors concluded** that a multi-disciplinary approach to the treatment of patients with primary malignant bone tumors is essential.

*Demircay et al. retrospectively reviewed the presentation and management of 70 patients with primary lymphoma of bone (PLB).* **They noted** the importance of biopsy for accurate diagnosis given that the appearance and location are similar to other primary bone lesions including chordoma and chondrosarcoma, which require aggressive bony resection instead of primarily medical management. **They noted** surgery was usually needed for PLB in the setting of pathologic fractures, avascular necrosis, or spinal cord compression.

Figure A is a T1 sagittal MRI of a large destructive sacral mass. Figure B shows a histological slide of the characteristic foamy and vacuolated notochordal cells (physaliferous cells) residing in an abundant myxoid stroma.
