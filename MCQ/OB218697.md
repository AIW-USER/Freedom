## Question

A 43-year-old male returns to your clinic for follow-up. The patient has ankle arthritis which has been recalcitrant to conservative management, and the patient desires a definitive solution for his symptoms. The patient reminds you that he is a manual laborer, and he has been having difficulty getting enough hours at work. Which of the following statements is most accurate regarding the surgical management of ankle osteoarthritis?

## Options

1. Ankle arthroplasty is preferred in this patient, since it will afford durable restoration of normal gait
2. Supramalleolar osteotomy has a five-year survival rate of 65% in patients with >10° of preoperative talar tilt
3. Patients with severe osteoporosis should undergo arthroplasty as opposed to arthrodesis
4. Subtalar osteoarthritis is rare following a well-performed ankle arthrodesis
5. Supramalleolar osteotomy is contraindicated in patients under the age of 50

## Correct option

**2. Supramalleolar osteotomy has a five-year survival rate of 65% in patients with >10° of preoperative talar tilt**

## Answer explanation

The management of ankle arthritis in young patients represents a challenging clinical scenario. Often performed for patients with neutral or varus hindfoot alignment, supramalleolar osteotomy (SMO) has a five-year survival rate of 85% for patients with 4-10° of talar tilt and 65% for patients with greater than 10° of talar tilt (Answer 2).

Ankle arthritis is a degenerative disease of the tibiotalar joint which can be divided into three main types: primary osteoarthritis, post-traumatic arthritis, and inflammatory arthritis. While occurring less frequently than hip or knee osteoarthritis, ankle arthritis can be debilitating, and can affect both old and young patients. Ankle arthritis can be classified according to the Takakura classification system (Illustration A).

The treatment of ankle arthritis begins with a trial of nonoperative interventions including activity modification, bracing, and anti-inflammatory medications. Surgical intervention is indicated in patients who have symptoms recalcitrant to conservative management with radiographic evidence of ankle arthritis. Some of the surgical options available include debridement with exostectomy, distraction arthroplasty, supramalleolar osteotomy (SMO), arthrodesis, and arthroplasty. Concerning SMO, this technique is indicated in patients with near-normal ROM, minimal talar-tilt or varus heel alignment, and predominantly medially focused ankle arthritis (stage II or IIIa).

*Krahenbuhl et al. reported on the results of SMO in patients with varus ankle osteoarthritis.* *Their study included 44 patients who underwent SMO for post-traumatic ankle arthritis.* **The authors found** that patients with preoperative Takakura stage 2 and 3a arthritis demonstrated higher survival rates compared to patients with stage 3b. Furthermore, **the authors found** that patients with 4-10° of preoperative talar tilt had a five-year survival rate of 85%, while those with > 10° of preoperative talar tilt had a five-year survival rate of 65%.

*Hongmou et al. reported on the outcomes of SMO with and without fibular osteotomy for varus ankle arthritis.* *Their study included 41 patients with stage 2 or 3 varus ankle arthritis, with an average age of 50.7 years.* *22 cases required a fibular osteotomy and 19 did not.* Overall, outcomes did not differ for patients who underwent an osteotomy compared to those who did not. **The authors concluded** that patients with higher preoperative talar tilt and/or small tibiocrural angles are more likely to require a fibular osteotomy.

Incorrect Answers
Answer 1: Ankle arthroplasty should be avoided in young laborers, as these patients are at a high risk of implant failure and revision surgery.
Answer 3: Severe osteoporosis is a contraindication to ankle arthroplasty.
Answer 4: Over 50% of patients who undergo ankle arthrodesis experience subtalar degeneration within ten years of surgery.
Answer 5: Age < 50 is not a contraindication to SMO.
