## Question

A 13-year-old male undergoes operative fixation of a triplane ankle fracture, and postoperative CT imaging reveals a residual intra-articular gap of 3 mm. His parents are concerned about long-term function and potential for arthritis. What is the most appropriate counseling regarding expected clinical outcomes?

## Options

1. Continued observation is appropriate, as functional outcomes are equivalent to operative treatment for gaps up to 5 mm
2. Postoperative bracing and prolonged non-weightbearing are more predictive of functional outcome than articular reduction quality
3. Residual intra-articular gap >2.5 mm is associated with worse functional outcomes
4. The risk of posttraumatic arthritis is negligible in transitional fractures, regardless of residual gap size
5. Functional outcomes are primarily influenced by premature physeal closure rather than residual joint incongruity

## Correct option

**3. Residual intra-articular gap >2.5 mm is associated with worse functional outcomes**

## Answer explanation

The patient undergoes operative fixation for a triplane ankle fracture, with postoperative imaging revealing a residual 3 mm intra-articular step-off. This residual incongruity should prompt concern, as patients with residual intra-articular gaps >2.5 mm are at increased odds for worse functional outcomes (answer 3).

Tillaux and triplane fractures are transitional fractures of the distal tibial epiphysis, occurring in adolescents nearing skeletal maturity due to the asymmetric closure of the distal tibial physis. Triplane fractures involve coronal, sagittal, and axial planes and often require advanced imaging (CT) to delineate the fracture morphology fully. Nonoperative management is generally appropriate for nondisplaced or minimally displaced fractures with ≤2 mm of intra-articular displacement. However, operative fixation is indicated when displacement exceeds 2.5 mm or when there is significant articular incongruity or instability, to restore joint congruity and minimize long-term sequelae such as post-traumatic arthritis.

*Lurie et al. conducted a retrospective study evaluating functional outcomes in pediatric patients with Tillaux and triplane ankle fractures who had 2 to 5 mm of intra-articular gapping after closed reduction.* *The study found that a greater residual gap, nonoperative management, and the presence of grade-III complications were all associated with worse long-term functional outcomes.* Importantly, **the authors concluded** that surgical management was associated with the greatest functional benefit when the residual intra-articular gap exceeded 2.5 mm, supporting the practice of operative intervention in these cases to optimize joint congruity and long-term function.

Schnetzler and Hoernschemeyer provide a comprehensive review of pediatric triplane fractures, emphasizing their unique anatomy and the importance of accurate classification and imaging. They stress that triplane fractures, though physeal injuries, behave more like adult-type intra-articular fractures with treatment dictated by joint congruity. Consequently, they emphasize the importance of CT imaging for preoperative planning, with prioritization placed on restoration of the joint line. The authors recommend that non- or minimally displaced fractures (<2 mm) be treated nonoperatively in a cast, while displaced triplane fractures involving >2 mm of articular incongruity should undergo anatomic reduction, often with percutaneous or open internal fixation.

Incorrect Answer Choices:
Answer 1: Nonoperative management is associated with worse functional outcomes when the residual intra-articular gap exceeds 2.5 mm. Surgical management is likely to provide the greatest benefit in patients with gaps >2.5 mm, as demonstrated by worse SANE Sports scores in nonoperatively managed patients with larger gaps.
Answer 2: Bracing or specific variances in postoperative weight-bearing protocols are not stronger predictors of long-term outcome when compared to the quality of articular reduction.
Answer 4: Residual articular incongruity, even in transitional fractures, increases the risk of posttraumatic arthritis.
Answer 5: While premature physeal closure is a possible complication, functional outcomes are primarily determined by restoration of articular surface congruity within acceptable limits (<5 mm).
