## Options

1. Observation
2. Curettage and bone grafting
3. Thumb amputation
4. Systemic chemotherapy
5. Radiation and intralesional excision

## Correct option

**2. Curettage and bone grafting**

## Answer explanation

The radiographs and histology are consistent with a diagnosis of benign enchondroma of the distal phalanx of the thumb. While enchondromas can occur in just about any bone, the histological picture of enchondromas of the hand is often more aggressive than those seen in the long bones. The treatment however is the same - a painful, growing enchondroma would be best treated with curettage and bone grafting.

O'Connor and Bancroft review benign and malignant cartilage tumors of the hand and discuss treatment options. For most benign cartilage tumors of the hand like enchondromas, maximization of patient function is of the utmost importance and their recommended treatment reflects this. On the other hand, malignant cartilage lesions like chondrosarcoma require wide excision which includes ray resection or partial hand amputation.

Gaulke and Suppelna review their results with 21 patients who underwent operative treatment for solitary enchondromas of the hand. After local treatment, tumor recurrence may be difficult to detect and as such, these authors recommend periodical radiological re-examination in order to identify patients who are at risk for pathological fracture.

Incorrect answers:
1) Observation isn't appropriate given the painful/growing nature of this mass
3,4,5) Aggressive treatment modalities like amputation, chemotherapy, or radiation aren't indicated for a benign lesion.
