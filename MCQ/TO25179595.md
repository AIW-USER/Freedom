Chapter: Orthopedics Oncology MCQs — Giant Cell Tumor

## Question

When utilizing Denosumab as a neoadjuvant agent for a salvagable, Campanacci Grade III distal radius GCT prior to joint-preserving curettage, which surgical disadvantage or complication is most frequently encountered?

## Options

1. The formation of an ultra-thin, hyper-vascular pseudo-capsule that increases intraoperative blood loss during curettage.
2. Sclerosis of the tumor margin and consolidation of neoplastic stroma into normal bone, which obscuring surgical margins and significantly increasing local recurrence rates after curettage.
3. Induced thinning of the subchondral bone plate, making joint surface preservation impossible.
4. Spontaneous intraoperative fracture of the adjacent metaphysis due to drug-induced osteomalacia.

## Correct option

**2. Sclerosis of the tumor margin and consolidation of neoplastic stroma into normal bone, which obscuring surgical margins and significantly increasing local recurrence rates after curettage.**

## Answer explanation

Asignificant concern with neoadjuvant denosumab is that it alters the histological interface between tumour and normal bone — instead of a soft, friable tumour that is easily distinguishable from normal bone during curettage, denosumab-treated tumour becomes densely ossified and merges with the surrounding reactive bone, making it difficult to identify the tumour boundary intraoperatively. This may paradoxically increase the risk of incomplete curettage (leaving viable stromal cells behind the ossified front) and has been associated with higher recurrence rates in some studies (20–30% after denosumab-facilitated curettage versus 10–15% without neoadjuvant denosumab in certain series). The stromal cells (the neoplastic population) are not killed by denosumab — they are merely quiescent, and they retain the H3.3 G34W mutation and the capacity to resume RANKL production and tumour growth once denosumab is discontinued.

Answer: 2
