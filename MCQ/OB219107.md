## Question

A 74-year-old woman with end-stage rheumatoid arthritis of the elbow undergoes an uneventful semi-constrained total elbow arthroplasty through a triceps-sparing approach. Which of the following best describes the recommended post-operative immobilization protocol that should be used for this patient?

## Options

1. Immediate initiation of active flexion and gravity-assisted passive extension, with no immobilization
2. Splinting at 60 to 90 degrees of flexion for 2-5 days, followed by initiation of active flexion and gravity-assisted passive extension
3. Splinting at 60 to 90 degrees of flexion for 5-10 days, followed by initiation of active flexion and gravity-assisted passive extension
4. Splinting at 60 to 90 degrees of flexion for at least 10-14 days, followed by initiation of active flexion and gravity-assisted passive extension
5. All of the above are acceptable techniques, and can be utilized based on surgeon and patient preference

## Correct option

**5. All of the above are acceptable techniques, and can be utilized based on surgeon and patient preference**

## Answer explanation

Following total elbow arthroplasty, a variety of post-operative immobilization protocols can be used, with no single strategy demonstrating clinical superiority, especially when a triceps-sparing approach is utilized (Answer 5).

Total elbow arthroplasty (TEA) is a motion-preserving modality used for the treatment of many debilitating elbow pathologies. While historically used to treat rheumatoid arthritis, the use of TEA to treat comminuted intra-articular distal humerus fractures in elderly patients has experienced rapid growth. Three major types of TEA implants have been developed and studied: unconstrained or unlinked (Illustration A), semi-constrained (Illustration B), or constrained (Illustration C), with semi-constrained implants demonstrating the best results of all the designs. In addition to three main implant designs, a variety of surgical approaches can be utilized including, but not limited to, a triceps-reflecting approach, a triceps tongue approach, a triceps-splitting approach, and a triceps-sparing approach. Given the variety of implant designs and approaches available and utilized, a variety of post-operative immobilization strategies have been proposed.

Historically, citing concerns with the surrounding soft-tissue envelope, patients were immobilized for four weeks following surgery. More recent evidence, however, suggests that immediate motion is safe, and that extended immobilization may adversely affect patient outcomes. The optimal immobilization strategy is dependent on a variety of implant- and patient-specific factors, and is not a "one size fits all" approach.

*Dam et al. performed a survey of European orthopedic surgeons, with 54 dedicated elbow specialists responding.* Approximately half of the surgeons who utilized the triceps-sparing approach recommended immobilization, with a variety of immobilization periods utilized. Overall, few surgeons immobilized for more than 14 days, with many only using a short course of immobilization to allow for soft-tissue rest. In summary, given the variety of indications, implant design, and patient-specific factors, there does not appear to be a single best immobilization strategy.

*Polmear et al. performed a systematic review of the literature investigating post-operative immobilization following TEA for rheumatoid arthritis.* *Thirty-six articles reporting on 2,346 elbows were included in their analysis.* Post-operative immobilization was broken down into no immobilization, short-term (2-5 days) immobilization, and extended (7-14 days) immobilization. **The authors found** that total complication rates were lowest in elbows that were not immobilized post-operatively, but implant survival rates were greatest in elbows that underwent 7-14 days of immobilization.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1-4: all of these are accepted immobilization strategies following TEA.
