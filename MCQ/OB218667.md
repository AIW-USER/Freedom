## Question

A 52-year-old female presents to your clinic with the chief complaint of right foot pain. She states the pain began insidiously and has worsened over the last 3 months. She feels like she has a rock in her shoe at times and states her pain is improved with well-padded tennis shoes compared to walking barefoot. On exam, the pain is localized to the level of the 2nd metatarsophalangeal (MTP) joint, and no gross deformity is present on inspection. Passive flexion and extension of the 2nd MTP are full, with pain as the toe is hyperextended. There is no palpable click with compression of the forefoot but dorsal subluxation of the 2nd proximal phalanx base is appreciated with modified Lachman testing. A radiograph is seen in Figure A. What is the diagnosis most likely causing her symptoms?

## Options

1. Midfoot arthritis
2. Freiberg's infarction
3. Plantar plate rupture
4. Morton's neuroma
5. Claw toe deformity

## Correct option

**3. Plantar plate rupture**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a 2nd metatarsophalangeal (MTP) joint plantar plate injury, as identified with a positive modified Lachman test and pain with hyperextension.

The plantar plate has increasingly been recognized for its importance in maintaining the function and stability of the MTP joints of the foot. This includes the lesser MTP joints, with the 2nd MTP joint most commonly being injured. Patients often complain of a sensation of walking on a pebble or marble underneath the 2nd MTP due to the loss of the plantar plate. As the plantar plate fails, a hammer toe can develop with hyperextension at the MTP and flexion at the PIP joint. The modified Lachman test is helpful in identifying excess laxity and dorsal subluxation at the MTP joint. If there is a concomitant injury to one/both of the collateral ligaments at the MTP, a crossover toe deformity may also develop. Nonoperative treatment with metatarsal pads, taping, splinting, shoe wear modification, and NSAIDS are first-line treatments with operative management including plantar plate repair and possible distal metatarsal shortening osteotomy to protect the repair.

*Bergeron et al. evaluated the use of the "modified Lachman's test," in identifying clinically significant plantar plate disruption.* *They used a cadaver model to section the plantar plate and then performed the modified Lachman to test for the degree of subluxation,* **noting** that a tear as small as 2 mm produced gross instability in the 2nd MTP joint. **They concluded** that the modified Lachman test has high sensitivity (90%) in identifying plantar plate insufficiency.

*Hsu et al. reviewed the management of plantar plate reconstruction for the lesser MTP joints.* **They noted** that clinical examination, plain radiographs, and MRI are helpful in identifying tears. They go on to discuss different approaches to plantar plate repair, **highlighting** promising results from a dorsal approach with regard to good pain relief and patient satisfaction.

Figures A & B are the AP and lateral radiographs of a normal-appearing right foot.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: There is no report of tarsometatarsal (TMT) joint pain through the midfoot and radiographs do not demonstrate any evidence of arthritis or other TMT joint abnormalities.
Answer 2: There is no radiographic evidence of a Freiberg's infraction, which is an uncommon process seen secondary to avascular necrosis and flattening/collapse of the 2nd MTP joint.
Answer 4: A Morton's neuroma is most commonly seen in the 3rd webspace, is associated with numbness/tingling, and causes pain with a palpable click during compression of the forefoot (Mulder's sign).
Answer 5: Claw toe deformity is most commonly secondary to intrinsic weakness in the foot and would be demonstrated on clinical exams and typically seen on radiographs.
