## Question

Figure A is the radiograph of a 23 year-old gymnast who presents with complaints of worsening left hip pain. He states that the pain is exacerbated with activity. On examination, he has no clicking or popping, normal strength, and pain with hip flexion, adduction, and internal rotation. Which of the following characterizes the underlying pathology in this patient?

![[q211997-a-fai.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Anterosuperior acetabular rim overcoverage
2. Femoral neck anteversion
3. Decreased femoral head-neck offset
4. Increased head-to-neck ratio
5. Spherical femoral head

## Correct option

**3. Decreased femoral head-neck offset**

## Answer explanation

This patient has clinical and radiographic findings suggesting a cam-type femoroacetabular impingement (FAI). A cam-type FAI is characterized by decreased head-to-neck ratio, aspherical femoral head, decreased femoral offset, and femoral neck retroversion.

FAI is an abnormal contact between the femur and acetabulum which leads to labral damage and various degrees of chondral injury. It may become symptomatic in patients who participate in activities requiring extreme range of motion such as ballet, gymnastics, and martial arts. Cam impingement
refers to femoral based disorder usually in young athletic males and is characterized by decreased head-to-neck ratio, aspherical femoral head, decreased femoral offset, and femoral neck retroversion. This sphericity mismatch causes shearing at the chondro-labral junction leading to cartilage delamination and labral separation. Conversely, pincer impingement refers to acetabular based disorder usually in active middle-aged women and is characterized by anterosuperior acetabular rim overcoverage, acetabular retroversion, acetabular protrusion, and coxa profundal. Combined Cam/Pincer impingement remains the most common type of FAI.

*Schoenecker et al. reviewed the surgical management of the problematic hip in adolescent and young adult patients.* **They report** that in FAI, pain occurs secondary to pathologic impingement of the deformed proximal femur against the anterolateral acetabulum. **They conclude** that restoration of more normal proximal femoral morphology results in marked improvement in functional outcome.

*Parvizi et al. reviewed the morphologic abnormalities of FAI and their contributing factor to osteoarthritis in the young patient.* **They report** that the morphologic abnormalities result in abnormal contact between the femoral neck/head and the acetabular margin, causing tearing of the labrum and avulsion of the underlying cartilage region, continued deterioration, and eventual onset of arthritis. **They conclude** that nonsurgical treatment typically fails to control symptoms and surgical management should include femoroacetabular osteoplasty.

*Matsuda et al. analyzed the current approaches to the surgical management of symptomatic FAI including open surgical dislocation, mini-open, and arthroscopic procedures.* *They report on the surgical efficacy, failure rates, and complications.* **They conclude** that open dislocation, mini-open, and arthroscopic methods for treating symptomatic FAI are effective in improving pain and function and that the historic gold standard of open dislocation surgery had a comparatively high major complication rate primarily because of trochanteric osteotomy-related issues.

Figure A shows bilateral cam-type FAI. Illustration A demonstrates the pistol-grip deformity (Arrows) with epiphyseal scar (Yellow dotted line) which extends lateral to a best fit circle of the femoral head also known as “horizontal growth plate sign" seen in cam-type FAI.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Anterosuperior acetabular rim overcoverage is a feature of pincer-type FAI
Answer 2: Increased femoral neck retroversion is a feature of cam-type FAI
Answer 4: Decreased head-to-neck ratio is a feature of cam-type FAI
Answer 5: Aspherical femoral head is a feature of cam-type FAI
