## Question

A 32-year-old female presents to your office for evaluation of left ankle pain for the past 8 weeks. She reports that she previously sustained an injury that involved ankle inversion and dorsiflexion during an axial load while running. Advanced imaging is obtained and reveals a contained osteochondal lesion measuring 15mm with an intact cartilage cap. Which of the following would be the most appropriate initial surgical interventions for this patient?

## Options

1. Arthroscopy with removal of the loose fragment, debridement and marrow stimulation
2. Retrograde drilling and/or bone grafting
3. Osteochondral autograft transplantation
4. Autologous chondrocyte implantation
5. Bulk allograft

## Correct option

**2. Retrograde drilling and/or bone grafting**

## Answer explanation

Retrograde drilling and/or bone grafting is indicated for lesions measuring >1 cm with intact cartilage cap.

Osteochondral lesions of the talus are focal injuries to the talar dome with variable involvement of the subchondral bone and cartilage which may be caused by a traumatic event or repetitive microtrauma. Diagnosis can be made with plain ankle radiographs, however, MRI studies are helpful in determining the size of the lesion, the extent of bony edema, and identifying unstable lesions. Treatment can be nonoperative or operative depending on patient's age, patient activity demands, lesion size, and stability of the lesion. The ideal size for bone marrow stimulation are lesions <10mm in diameter (<100mm2 in area and <5mm in depth). Retrograde drilling is reserved for lesions where the cartilage is intact. Autologous chondrocyte implantation can be considered in both primary and revision scenarios for lesions greater than 1 cm2, with or without cystic component, and shoulder lesions. Fresh osteochondral allograft transplantation is indicated in patients who have failed prior arthroscopic procedures with attempts at cartilage restoration, lesions with a large cystic component, contained lesions greater than 1.5 cm2, uncontained shoulder lesions, knee osteoarthritis, history of knee infection, and patients expressing concern with donor site morbidity of the knee.

*Hannon et al. reviewed the consensus statements on "Debridement, Curettage and Bone Marrow Stimulation" developed at the 2017 International Consensus Meeting on Cartilage Repair of the Ankle.* **They reported** that of the 14 statements on debridement, curettage, and bone marrow stimulation that reached consensus during the 2017 International Consensus Meeting on Cartilage Repair of the Ankle, one achieved unanimous support, 12 reached strong consensus (>75% agreement), and 1 achieved consensus. **They concluded** that all statements reached at least 72% agreement.

*Rothrauff et al. reviewed the consensus statements on "Scaffold-Based Therapies" developed at the 2017 International Consensus Meeting on Cartilage Repair of the Ankle.* **They reported** that of the 9 statements on scaffold-based therapies that reached consensus during the 2017 International Consensus Meeting on Cartilage Repair of the Ankle, one achieved unanimous support, 8 reached strong consensus (>75% agreement), and 1 was removed because of redundancy in the information provided. **They concluded** that all statements reached at least 80% agreement.

*Smyth et al. reviewed the consensus statements on "Osteochondral Allograft" developed at the 2017 International Consensus Meeting on Cartilage Repair of the Ankle.* **They report** that of the 15 statements on osteochondral allograft that reached consensus during the 2017 International Consensus Meeting on Cartilage Repair of the Ankle, one achieved unanimous support and 14 reached strong consensus (>75% agreement). **They concluded** that all statements reached at least 85% agreement.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Arthroscopy with removal of the loose fragment, debridement and marrow stimulation is indicated for chronic lesions, those with a size <1 cm, and/or displaced smaller fragment with minimal bone on the osteochondral fragment (poor healing potential).
Answers 3-5: Cartilage restoration techniques (ie. osteochondral autograft transplantation, autologous chondrocyte implantation, bulk allograft) are indicated for lesions >1 cm and displaced lesions, shoulder lesions, or as a salvage for failed marrow stimulation or drilling. They are contraindicated in the setting of diffuse ankle arthritis, bipolar kissing lesions, and/or advanced osteonecrosis of the talar dome.
