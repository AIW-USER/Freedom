## Question

A 51-year-old male with type 2 insulin-dependent diabetes who has a 2-week history of redness and swelling along the forefoot. Despite being currently treated with intravenous (IV) antibiotics, the redness has rapidly spread proximally into the lower leg in the past 12 hours. His laboratory values include an ESR of 80 mm/h (range, 0 to 20 mm/h), CRP of 6.2 mg/L (range, 0.08 to 3.1 mg/L), WBC of 15 (range, 4.500 to 11), and albumin level of 1.6. Which microorganism(s) is most commonly present in this condition?

## Options

1. Group A ß-hemolytic Streptococci
2. Marine Vibrio vulnificus
3. Methicillin-resistant staphylococcus aureus
4. Non-group A ß-hemolytic Streptococci
5. Polymicrobial

## Correct option

**5. Polymicrobial**

## Answer explanation

This patient's presentation is concerning for necrotizing fasciitis. A polymicrobial infection is most common (80-90% of cases).

Necrotizing fasciitis is a life threatening infection that spreads along soft tissue planes and may lead to rapid progression of the infection in the setting of a diabetic foot wound. It has a reported mortality rate of approximately 32%, which correlates with time to surgical intervention. Polymicrobial infection accounts for approximately 80-90% of cases; typically 4-5 aerobic and anaerobic species are cultured, including non-Group A Strep, anaerobes (ie. Clostridia), facultative anaerobes, enterobacteria, and there is synergistic virulence between organisms. Diabetes is a known risk factor for a polymicrobial infection as it causes immune suppression. The LRINEC Scoring system may be used to help in the diagnosis. Treatment should include emergency radical debridement with broad-spectrum IV antibiotics.

*Armstrong et al. reviewed foot ulcers in diabetic patients.* **They found** no differences in healing with supplementation in non-ischaemic patients or those with normal albumin, but the addition of arginine, glutamine and β-hydroxy-β-methylbutyrate as an adjunct may improve healing of diabetic foot ulcers in patients with risk of poor limb perfusion and/or low albumin levels.

*Himes et al. reviewed the importance of nutritional intervention and rate of wound healing.* **They report** that nutritional intervention must be provided early enough to prevent a catabolic-induced decline in lean muscle mass, further impairing wound healing. **They concluded** that chronic, nonhealing wounds are difficult to treat and contribute to significant morbidity, mortality, and hospitalizations.

*Lipsky et al. prospectively reviewed diabetic foot infections to assess the association between clinical treatment failure and baseline history, physical and laboratory findings.* **They report** that baseline factors which were significantly associated with treatment failure include 'severe' University of Texas (UT) wound grade; elevated WBC, CRP or ESR; high wound severity score; inpatient treatment; low serum albumin; male sex; and skin temperature of affected foot >10 degrees C above that of unaffected foot. **They concluded** that increased WBC and severe UT wound grade at baseline, but not other features, were significant independent and additive risk factors for clinical failure in patients treated for a diabetic foot infection.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Group A ß-hemolytic Streptococci is seen and a monomicrobial infection in about 5% of cases
Answer 2: Marine Vibrio vulnificus is rare but occurs following marine exposure
Answer 3: Monomicrobial MRSA infection is less common that a polymicrobial infection
Answer 4: Monomicrobial infection with non-group A ß-hemolytic Streptococci is less common than a polymicrobial infection
