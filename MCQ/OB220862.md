## Question

A 58-year-old male is scheduled for an anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) at C5-C6. As you are reviewing his preoperative imaging, you note a significant abnormality. On the axial CT scan, you observe that the transverse foramen on the right side is more medial than usual and appears enlarged. The left side's transverse foramen is in its typical lateral position. Given the patient's preoperative imaging findings, which of the following is the most significant surgical risk associated with this anatomical variation during the planned ACDF?

## Options

1. Injury to the internal jugular vein
2. Injury to the internal carotid artery
3. Injury to the vagus nerve
4. Injury to the vertebral artery
5. Retropulsion of bone graft into the spinal canal

## Correct option

**4. Injury to the vertebral artery**

## Answer explanation

An anomalous medial course of the transverse foramen places the vertebral artery at an increased risk of iatrogenic injury during a standard anterior cervical approach (Answer 4).

The vertebral artery normally courses through the transverse foramen of the cervical vertebrae, which is typically located laterally. An anatomical variation where the foramen, and thus the vertebral artery, is more medially located or enlarged puts the artery in an atypical and vulnerable position during an anterior cervical approach. Standard surgical dissection and screw placement, which are performed lateral to the vertebral body, could inadvertently injure this vital structure, leading to catastrophic bleeding or a stroke. Preoperative recognition of this anomaly on imaging is crucial for surgical planning and risk mitigation.

*Hong et al. evaluated the anatomical variations of the vertebral artery in the lower cervical spine using three-dimensional computed tomography angiography.* **They noted** that variations in the vertebral artery's course and the transverse foramen's position are common and pose a risk during surgery. **They concluded** that preoperative imaging is essential to identify these variations and minimize the risk of arterial injury.

Schroeder and Hsu provided a review of vertebral artery injuries in cervical spine surgery. They noted that these injuries are rare but can have devastating consequences, including stroke and death. They concluded that careful preoperative planning and meticulous surgical technique are paramount to preventing these injuries.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The internal jugular vein is a component of the carotid sheath, which is located more medially and anteriorly, and is not directly related to the position of the transverse foramen.
Answer 2: The internal carotid artery is also part of the carotid sheath and is not in the path of the vertebral artery anomaly.
Answer 3: The vagus nerve is a cranial nerve located within the carotid sheath, far from the transverse foramen's course.
Answer 5: While a risk during an ACDF, retropulsion is related to a poor-fitting or displaced graft and is not directly associated with a vertebral artery anomaly.
