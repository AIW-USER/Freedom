## Question

## Options

1. Metastatic carcinoma
2. Giant-cell tumour
3. Enchondroma
4. Multiple myeloma
5. Non-ossifying Fibroma

## Correct option

**2. Giant-cell tumour**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a giant cell tumour (GCT) in the proximal tibia.

Giant cell tumor of bone (GCT) is a benign aggressive tumor that originates in the metaphysics of long bones but often extends into the epiphysis. Up to 50% of tumors occur around the knee (i.e., distal femur or proximal tibia). Other common sites include the distal radius, sacrum and proximal humerus. Treatment typically involves intralesional curettage with adjuvant treatment (e.g., phenol, hydrogen peroxide, argon beam, etc).

*Raskin et al. reviewed giant cell tumor of bone and* **state** the most common site for GCT metastasis is the lungs. Risk factors for lung metastasis include local recurrence, the location of the primary giant cell tumor (i.e., distal radius, proximal femur, and sacrum), immunocompromised state and Musculoskeletal Tumor Society Stage 3.

*Klenke et al. retrospectively reviewed the medical records of 46 patients with recurrent GCTs of long bones treated with wide resection or intralesional curettage.* The showed that the rate of rerecurrence after wide resection was 6%, compared to 32% wtih Intralesional curettage. Interestingly, the use of polymethylmethacrylate (PMMA) instead of bone grafting was associated with a lower risk of subsequent recurrence with intralesional procedures (14% versus 50%).

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Metastatic carcinoma is rare in young patients. The primary cancers most commonly associated with bone metastases are: lung, breast, prostate, thyroid and kidney. Lung cancer is the most common cause of distal, below-elbow and below-knee metastases.
Answer 3: Enchondromas have sharply defined scalloped margins, with calcifications throughout the lesion (may range from punctate to rings).
Answer 4: A negative, or cold, bone scan is classically seen in patients with multiple myeloma due to the lack of osteoblastic activity. The predominant sites of involvement include the vertebral column, ribs, skull, pelvis, and femur.
Answer 5: Non-ossifying fibroma are more common in children aged 5-15 years old. Lesions will usually become sclerotic as the patient approaches skeletal maturity.
