## Options

## Correct option

**4. 67-year-old diabetic female, mild bilateral leg weakness, WBC 12k, CRP 9.0mg/d, MRSA-positive blood culture**

## Answer explanation

Based on the existing literature, the patient in answer #4 is MOST likely to fail nonoperative management. Neurologic deficit, especially if progressive, has been shown to be the most predictive of surgical management. Additional risk factors include age >65 years, diabetes, MRSA infection, positive blood cultures, WBC >12.5k, and CRP >11.5mg/dL.

The ideal candidate for medical management of a spinal epidural abscess remains a point of contention. This is because a significant portion (>40%) of patients who are initially managed with IV antibiotics fail conservative management and require surgical intervention, most often due to progressive pain and/or neurologic deficits. Neurologic recovery can furthermore be impaired as a result of a delay in surgical intervention. Multiple studies have attempted to create management algorithms based on known risk factors for medical failure.

*Patel et al. evaluated prognostic indicators of neurologic outcomes following management of spinal epidural abscesses.* While nearly half of the cases were initially managed nonoperatively, 41% of these failed medical treatment. Diabetes, CRP >11.5mg/dL, WBC >12.5k, and positive blood cultures were predictive of medical failure. Patients with 0, 1, 2, and 3 or more risk factors had an 8.3%, 35.4%, 40.2%, and 76.9% risk of failure, respectively. Patients who underwent early surgical decompression furthermore had improved neurologic recovery versus those who fail medical management and underwent delayed surgical intervention.

*Kim et al. assessed predictors of failure of medical management of spinal epidural abscesses.* **The authors found** that 43% of patients ultimately failed IV antibiotic therapy and required urgent surgical decompression. Multivariate analysis determined that age >65 years, presence of diabetes, MRSA infection, and neurologic compromise were all independent predictors of medical failure. Neurologic deficits were the strongest predictor of necessity for surgical intervention, and patients with all 4 risk factors had a 99% chance of failure.
