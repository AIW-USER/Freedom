## Question

A 55-year-old male underwent a total shoulder arthroplasty (TSA) secondary to significant glenohumeral arthritis. When he was a teenager, he had repeated dislocations leading to instability. He ultimately had a Magnuson-Stack procedure to address his instability. What tendon's anatomic relationship is changed and how would a failed repair or reconstruction affect the functional outcomes following a TSA?

## Options

1. Subscapularis, increased external rotation, posterior instability, and positive belly press test
2. Teres Minor, increased internal rotation, anterior instability, and positive horn blowers signs
3. Supraspinatus, increased external rotation, inferior instability, and positive empty can testing
4. Pectoralis Major, decreased external rotation, anterior instability, and positive belly press test
5. Subscapularis, increased external rotation, anterior instability, and positive belly press test

## Correct option

**5. Subscapularis, increased external rotation, anterior instability, and positive belly press test**

## Answer explanation

This patient underwent a total shoulder arthroplasty following a Magnuson-Stack procedure, which involves transferring the subscapularis lateral to the bicipital groove. This may result in inadvertent laceration of the subscapularis tendon on the deltopectoral approach leading to increased external rotation, anterior instability, and a positive belly press test following a total shoulder arthroplasty (Answer 5).

Total shoulder arthroplasty is a commonly performed procedure for glenohumeral arthritis with an intact rotator cuff. Recurrent episodes of instability can cause microtrauma and lead to the advancement of glenohumeral arthritis. The Magnuson-Stack procedure is a historical non-anatomic instability procedure that transferred the middle part of the subscapularis with a part of the lesser tuberosity lateral to the bicipital groove to restrict external rotation. During the release of the biceps tendon, the subscapularis is lacerated following this procedure as it no lies over the bicipital groove. The loss of the subscapularis and/or failure of its repair or reconstruction on closure can lead to anterior instability, internal rotation weakness, and increased external rotation.

*Matsoukis et al. followed 55 TSAs for a mean of 45 months in people with previous anterior instability.* Twenty-seven of those shoulders had a prior anterior stabilization procedure. **They found** that 50 patients rate the results as good or excellent and improvement in active forward flexion and external rotation. **They concluded** that good results are obtainable with a TSA in a patient with a history of anterior instability.

*Green and Norris retrospectively reviewed 19 shoulders with prior anterior instability procedures.* *Fifteen of those patients had a TSA and 2 patients had a hemiarthroplasty.* **They found** that there was decreased pain in 16 of the 17 cases. There was increased active forward flexion, external rotation, and passive internal rotation. Three patients required revision procedures. **They concluded** that TSA following prior anterior instability procedure can be technically challenging, but reduces pain and improves function.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1-4: Failure of a subscapularis repair or reconstruction following a total shoulder arthroplasty leads to increased external rotation, anterior instability, and a positive belly press test.
