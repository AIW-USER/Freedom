## Question

A 45-year-old former minor league baseball pitcher - turned cryptocurrency day trader, reports having symptoms of locking in his left elbow. Advanced imaging demonstrates evidence of intra-articular loose bodies. The surgical plan is to arthroscopically remove these loose bodies. The surgeon prepares for the case the night before surgery, particularly studying the various portals and their respective inherent anatomical risk. The surgeon comes across an image (Figure A) demonstrating a particularly useful portal. However, which of the following correctly matches the portal in the image with the respective neurovascular risk?

![[q215451-a-40c.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Proximal anteromedial portal: Posterior Interosseous Nerve
2. Proximal anteromedial portal: Anterior Interosseous Nerve
3. Anteromedial (direct medial) portal: Medial antebrachial cutaneous nerve and median nerve
4. Anteromedial (direct medial) portal: Posterior Interosseous Nerve
5. Anteromedial (direct medial) portal: Anterior Interosseous Nerve

## Correct option

**3. Anteromedial (direct medial) portal: Medial antebrachial cutaneous nerve and median nerve**

## Answer explanation

The portal in question is the Anteromedial (direct medial) portal which carries the risk to the Medial antebrachial cutaneous nerve (1 mm) and median nerve (7 mm).

The anteromedial portal is 2cm anterior and 2cm distal to the medial epicondyle. Placed under direct visualization, it is most often used to augment the proximal anteromedial portal to access the medial recess. The proximal anteromedial portal is made 2cm proximal to the medial epicondyle and just anterior to the intermuscular septum.

The portal is useful in viewing the entire anterior compartment, radial head, capitellum, coronoid, and the trochlea. The "safe" portal, the direct lateral portal, is made in the "soft spot" which is in the triangle formed by olecranon, radial head, and lateral epicondyle. This is the initial site for joint distension before the scope is inserted and can also be used in the visualization of the posterior compartment. While this portal is often made first and is very safe, injury to the lateral antebrachial cutaneous nerve poses a minor risk.

*Adams et al reviewed the indications, techniques, outcomes, and complications of elbow arthroscopy.* The indications include loose body removal, extensor carpi radialis brevis debridement, contracture release, synovectomy, and in the treatment of osteochondral defects. Elbow arthroscopy has a profound learning curve. A detailed understanding of anatomical principles and correct patient positioning is necessary.

*Miller et al reviewed the neuroanatomy in elbow arthroscopy in a cadaveric analysis of 6 elbows.* Insufflation likely improves the safety of the procedure as it increased the nerve-to-bone distance with the elbow in flexion, but not in extension.
