## Question

A surgeon was doing TKR and Anterior referencing was being used in the procedure. 8 mm of posterior femoral cut was taken but still surgeon was finding tight flexion gap. Options you would suggest in this scenario [FNB 2023]

## Options

1. Cut more distal femur
2. Downsize implant
3. Decrease tibial slop
4. Decrease poly size

## Correct option

**2. Downsize implant**

## Answer explanation

Ans: B
EXPLANATION
Decreasing tibial slope would further tighten the flexion gap is not an option. Cutting distal femur would widen extension gap not flexion. Decreasing poly size would indirectly increase flexion gap but would have same impact on extension gap creating extension instability. So, downsizing the implant by removing more of posterior femoral condyle bone would be ideal option particularly with anterior referencing being employed by the surgeon.

REFERENCING SYSTEMS
These systems help to let a surgeon know the size of the femoral component. The width choices are not available as they are linked to AP dimensions of cup. So surgeon has to choose AP dimension of the implant that can best fit the bone after osteotomy. Referencing systems help in this step.

![[TO19356211-1.jpg]]

A.) POSTERIOR REFERENCING
First distal femur is cut and a sizing block placed on cut femur. The aim is to get AP dimension of cup to be chosen. Now if the sizing jig is following Posterior Refencing mechanics, then here the amount of posterior femoral bone that will be cut will be fixed (around 8-9mm corroborating with thickness of implant) so flexion gap size is fixed. The anterior cutting level is decided by using a stylus that helps to know there will be no notching (cutting anterior cortex is notching that may lead to fracture). The size that comes on the jig upon properly placing stylus is chosen. Improper placement of stylus and choosing a smaller implant than actual size will lead to notching as anterior cut will get deeper; posterior is fixed. Even when sizer is in between sizes, select upper size as smaller sizes can end up in notching.
Sequence of cuts in Posterior referencing: Distal femur cut  Size the implant and choose cutting jig of that size  Take anterior and posterior cuts  Chamfer and notch cuts

![[TO19356211-2.jpg]]

Sizer placed such that lower flanges are resting on posterior femoral condyles. So amount of bone being cut posteriorly is fixed by jig. The stylus on anterior cortex is marking the depth to which anterior resection can go without causing notching. Choosing smaller size will lead to more anterior bone loss and notching when in between sizes.

B.) ANTERIOR REFERENCING
Here after matching with the rotation deciding landmarks (Whiteside’s line), first anterior cut is performed (even before cutting distal femur). Only that much bone is removed so that notching does not occur. So, amount to be removed anteriorly is fixed (end point is anterior cortex not to be breached). Now a sizer is placed on anterior cut femur (see image) and amount of bone that can be removed from posterior femoral condyles depends on size surgeon choses. A smaller implant will only remove more bone from posterior condyles (widening flexion gap) but notching will be not be a risk. Here if in between sizes on sizer, choose smaller size to keep flexion gap wide and have good post op flexion. However, too much resection can lead to mid flexion instability although rare.

![[TO19356211-3.jpg]]

This is Anterior Referencing. Here the Anterior cut was first taken with depth fixed (the depth chosen was where anterior cortex is not cut). Now sizer is placed on cut anterior femur and size of implant is being chosen as the posterior flanges rest on posterior condyles. Now if size shown by jig is chosen, 9 mm of bone will be removed (measured resection) but going up or down the size will alter bone to be removed from posterior femoral condyles only.
