## Question

A 23-year-old female soccer player presents with chronic lateral ankle pain. She reports a history of many ankle sprains and now feels as if her ankle is unstable. She has treated this with bracing and physical therapy focusing on proprioceptive training. Despite this, she continues to be symptomatic. On exam, there is a positive anterior drawer test and no pain during resisted foot eversion. Current radiographs are depicted in Figures A and B. She is scheduled to treat her condition with a modified Broström-Gould reconstruction. What other procedure should be performed to address a commonly associated condition?

## Options

1. Subtalar arthrodesis
2. Ankle arthroscopy
3. Peroneal tendon debridement
4. 1st ray dorsiflexion osteotomy
5. Dwyer osteotomy

## Correct option

**2. Ankle arthroscopy**

## Answer explanation

There is a high incidence of intra-articular pathology with chronic lateral ankle instability which can be addressed with ankle arthroscopy.

Chronic lateral ankle instability is a condition that occurs when the ligaments on the lateral aspect of the ankle joint are stretched or torn, resulting in recurrent instability of the ankle. Patients may experience symptoms such as pain, swelling, and a feeling of "giving way" of the ankle. This can lead to associated intraarticular pathology such as osteochondral defects of the talus, anterior ankle impingement, or anterior ankle synovitis. Surgical treatment typically involves ligament reconstruction of the lateral ligaments (Broström-Gould reconstruction) with possible ankle arthroscopy to diagnose and debride associated pathology.

*Odak et al. retrospectively studied the arthroscopic findings of patients with chronic lateral ankle instability treated with a Broström-Gould reconstruction.* **They reported** a high percentage (63%) of patients with chronic lateral ankle instability had intra-articular synovitis, mostly in the anterior and/or anterolateral compartment, which required arthroscopic debridement. However, the incidence of osteochondral lesions (OCLs) (17%) and anterior bony impingement lesions (12%) was relatively low in this patient population. **The authors concluded** that there is a high incidence of anterior/anterolateral synovitis with a low incidence of bony impingement or OCLs in patients with chronic lateral ankle instability.

*Ferkel et al. retrospectively studied the arthroscopic findings of 21 patients with chronic lateral ankle instability that were treated with Broström-Gould reconstruction.* **They reported** a 95% association with intraarticular pathology with 100% of patients achieving good or excellent outcomes. **The authors concluded** that chronic lateral ankle instability is frequently associated with intra-articular pathology.

Figures A and B are the lateral and Harris view radiographs of the foot with no significant abnormalities or malalignment.

Incorrect answers
Answer 1: The patient does not have subtalar arthritis.
Answer 3: Peroneal tendon tears can occur with lateral ankle instability, but the patient does not seem to have this issue based on the lack of pain with resisted foot eversion.
Answers 4 and 5: Realignment osteotomies are helpful in patients with cavovarus foot alignment, which predisposes them to ankle sprains. However, this patient has normal foot alignment.
