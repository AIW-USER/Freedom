## Question

A 27-year-old right-hand dominant baseball pitcher presents to your office with persistent shoulder pain for the past several months in his throwing arm. His physical examination is remarkable only for an internal rotation deficit of 25 degrees compared to his contralateral side. Tightness of which of the following structures is likely to contribute most to his underlying pathology?

![[q212172-a-glenohumeral-ligs-1.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. A
2. B
3. C
4. D
5. E

## Correct option

**1. A**

## Answer explanation

This patient has internal impingement of his throwing shoulder. The etiology of this condition is due to tightness of the posterior structures, notably the posterior band of the inferior glenohumeral ligament (IGHL).

Internal impingement is a condition in which the greater tuberosity and articular surface of the posterior supraspinatus contact the posterosuperior glenoid during maximal shoulder abduction and external rotation. It is believed that the etiology is secondary to tightness in the posterior soft tissues including the capsule and posterior band of the IGHL. Nonoperative management is the mainstay of treatment and includes rest, sleeper and cross-body stretches, scapular strengthening, and proprioception.

*Heyworth et al. reviewed the pathomechanics, clinical complaints, physical examination findings, and imaging findings that are associated with internal impingement of the shoulder.* **The authors discuss** that the pathology is attributable to excessive or repetitive contact of the greater tuberosity with the posterosuperior aspect of the glenoid when the arm is abducted and externally rotated, leading to articular-sided rotator cuff tears and posterosuperior labral lesions. **They concluded** that that this condition was likely secondary to a combination of glenohumeral instability, posterior capsular contracture, and scapular dyskinesia.

*Neri et al. reviewed the outcome of SLAP repairs in overhead athletes.* *The authors discuss the well-established relationship between SLAP lesions and articular-sided rotator cuff tears initiated by a shift in glenohumeral range of motion, capsular adaptations, and humeral translation, ultimately culminating in posterior-superior glenoid rim–rotator cuff contact, or internal impingement.* **They concluded** that the Kerlan-Jobe Orthopaedic Clinic score serves as an accurate assessment tool in this population of elite overhead athletes with SLAP tears.

*Myers et al. reviewed throwers with pathologic internal impingement.* **They found** that throwing athletes with internal impingement demonstrated significantly greater glenohumeral internal rotation deficit and posterior shoulder tightness compared with the control subjects. **The authors concluded** that a contracture of the posterior elements of the shoulder (capsule, rotator cuff) may contribute to impingement and should be managed with stretching to restore flexibility to the posterior shoulder.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 2-5: The anterior axillary pouch of the IGHL (B), anterior band of the IGHL (C), MGHL (D), SGHL (E) are not implicated in the pathophysiology of internal impingement.
