## Question

A 12-year-old basketball player reports frequent and moderately painful popping in his left knee during his games. His symptoms have remained persistent and both he and his parents are concerned as this limits his performance. He is evaluated and the decision is made to proceed with arthroscopic saucerization. Which of the following is true of his pathology?

## Options

1. Incomplete is more common than complete type
2. Instability of the meniscus is uncommon
3. Long-term degenerative changes are similar between arthroscopic saucerization, partial, and complete meniscectomy
4. Saucerization with repair results in inferior clinical outcomes compared to saucerization alone
5. A 6-8mm peripheral rim is recommended following saucerization

## Correct option

**5. A 6-8mm peripheral rim is recommended following saucerization**

## Answer explanation

The clinical presentation is consistent with a symptomatic discoid meniscus. Saucerization to a 6-8mm peripheral rim is recommended, given that this maintains the function of the meniscus while avoiding re-tear and persistent symptoms associated with larger remnants.

Discoid menisci involve congenital variations in the morphology and peripheral attachments most often of the lateral meniscus. Due to the increased thickness, instability, disorganized microstructure and decreased vascularity compared to a normal meniscus, discoid menisci are more prone to tearing and may result in pain and associated mechanical symptoms. Saucerization with a possible meniscal repair is indicated in the setting of symptomatic discoid menisci. Though results are improved over the traditional complete meniscectomy, degenerative changes have still been reported at intermediate-term follow-up.

*Kocher et al. review the diagnosis, management, and outcomes following surgical treatment for pediatric discoid lateral menisci.* **They discuss** that the complete variant is most common and that instability is frequent and typically involves the anterior horn. Furthermore, unstable discoid menisci are more likely to present at a younger age. **The authors note** that surgical intervention is indicated for symptomatic discoid menisci and that saucerization with a repair is superior to complete meniscectomy. **They caution** that all-inside repair may be difficult in young children due to small knee size and risk of deep posterior perforation.

*Good et al. discusses the arthroscopic management of symptomatic discoid menisci in children.* **The authors noted** that arthroscopic saucerization was successful in 93% of knees, while the remaining knees necessitated complete meniscectomy for complex tears. **They found** that complete discoid menisci were the most common variant, and that tears and instability were present in the majority of cases (77% for each pathology). At the time of final follow-up, **the authors demonstrated** that all patients had full knee flexion (>135 degrees) and that only 14% had residual pain and 19% had residual mechanical symptoms.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The complete discoid variant has been found to be the most common, followed by the incomplete, and lastly the Wrisberg variant.
Answer 2: Instability of the anterior horn is the most common, followed by the posterior horn, and lastly the body. Complete detachment at all peripheral sites have been reported as well but is least common.
Answer 3: At long-term follow-up, degenerative changes have been reported in roughly 20% of patients managed with arthroscopic saucerization, 40% of patients managed with partial meniscectomy, and 90% of patients managed with complete meniscectomy.
Answer 4: The outcomes following arthroscopic saucerization alone versus saucerization with repair when indicated are comparable in terms of the range of motion, pain, and complication rates.
