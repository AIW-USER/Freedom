## Answer explanation

The patient has evidence of compartment syndrome associated with a tibial tubercle fracture, which most commonly results from an injury to the recurrent branch of the anterior tibial artery, indicated by C in Figure C.

Tibial tubercle fractures are a common fracture that occurs in adolescent boys near the end of skeletal growth during athletic activity. Comparment syndrome can occur in up to 4% and is most commonly due to injury to the recurrent branch of the anterior tibial artery. The recurrent branch of the anterior tibial artery arises from the anterior tibial artery as it passes through the interosseous space. It then ascends in the tibialis anterior muscle and along the anterolateral aspect of the knee until it anastomoses with the genicular branches of the popliteal artery.

*Mosier et al. performed a retrospective study on acute tibial tubercle fractures.* **They found** that 77% of injuries resulted from participation in athletics, particularly basketball. **They also found** that final outcomes were good in all patients regardless of fracture type or treatment.

*McKoy et al. performed a review of acute tibial tubercle fractures.* **They found** that these injuries typically occur in mature-appearing adolescent boys involved in jumping sports, particularly basketball. **They report** that the developmental anatomy of the tibial tuberosity and the changes surrounding normal physiologic epiphysiodesis render this structure susceptible to acute avulsion fractures.

*Pape et al. published a review on compartment syndrome complicating tibial tubercle avulsion fractures.* **They report** that tibial tubercle fractures are uncommon physeal fractures that are predisposed to anterior compartment syndrome due to the proximity of the anterior tibial recurrent artery. **They concluded** that soft tissue disruption associated with tibial tubercle injuries are often underappreciated and that compartment syndrome should therefore be considered, especially in adolescent boys.

Figures A and B are AP and lateral radiographs of the right knee demonstrating a tibial tubercle fracture. Figure C is an unlabeled diagram of the arterial supply of the knee, with A representing the lateral superior geniculate artery, B representing the lateral inferior geniculate artery, C representing the recurrent branch of the anterior tibial artery, D representing the popliteal artery, and E representing the medial inferior geniculate artery. Illustration A is the labeled version of Figure C.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The lateral superior geniculate artery is not the most common cause of compartment syndrome in patients with tibial tubercle fractures.
Answer 2: The lateral inferior geniculate artery is not the most common cause of compartment syndrome in patients with tibial tubercle fractures.
Answer 4: The popliteal artery is more commonly injured in knee dislocations. It is not the most common cause of compartment syndrome in patients with tibial tubercle fractures.
Answer 5: The medial inferior geniculate artery is not the most common cause of compartment syndrome in patients with tibial tubercle fractures.
