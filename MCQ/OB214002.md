## Question

A 47-year-old man presents with the closed injury depicted in Figure A after a motor vehicle collision. Physical examination reveals a complete palsy of the nerve most commonly involved with this injury. The treating surgeon proceeds with closed management of the fracture and observation of the nerve palsy. What is the insertion of the first muscle to recover during the resolution of this nerve injury?

![[q214002-a-screen-shot-2020-02-29-at-12-05-42-pm.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Base of 2nd metacarpal
2. Base of 3rd metcarpal
3. Thumb distal phalanx
4. Radial styloid
5. Index finger extensor hood

## Correct option

**4. Radial styloid**

## Answer explanation

The radial nerve is most commonly involved in the setting of a closed distal one-third spiral humeral shaft (Holstein-Lewis) fracture. With this palsy, the first muscle to recover motor function is the brachioradialis, which inserts onto the radial styloid.

The radial nerve is most susceptible to injury in fractures involving the junction of the middle and distal thirds of the humeral shaft because this is the location at which the nerve pierces the intermuscular septum and enters the anterior compartment and as a result at which it is often the least mobile. While treatment of associated radial neuropraxia is typically non-operative, surgical treatment is indicated when there is no improvement after a period of conservative management and in cases when risk of nerve transection is increased, such as in the setting of open fractures. When radial nerve function returns, the brachioradialis has been shown to be the first muscle to recover, followed most often by the extensor carpi radialis longus (ECRL) and the extensor carpi radialis brevis (ECRB), though variably. The extensor indicis proprius (EIP) is the last muscle to recover given its most distal innervation.

*Holstein et al. investigated patients with humeral fractures complicated by radial nerve paralysis in a small case series.* **The authors observed** that concomitant injury occurred when the fracture involved the distal third of the humerus and postulated that this resulted from the apex of the distal fragment shifting proximally and radially at the time of injury, in effect lacerating or trapping the radial nerve between the fracture fragments.

*Bumbasirevic et al. reviewed radial nerve palsies.* **They reported** that the close proximity of the radial nerve to the humeral bone increases its vulnerability when humeral fractures occur. **The authors recommended** that the treatment algorithm be based on the cause and expected recovery of the neuropathy.

Figure A demonstrates a distal third humeral shaft fracture.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The ECRL inserts onto the base of the 2nd metacarpal and typically is the 2nd muscle to recover after the brachioradialis.
Answer 2: The ECRB inserts onto the base of the 3rd metacarpal and typically is the 3rd muscle to recover following the brachioradialis and ECRL.
Answer 3: The EPL inserts onto the thumb distal phalanx and is one of the last muscles to recover.
Answer 5: The EIP inserts onto the extensor hood of the index finger. This is the last muscle to recover following a radial nerve palsy.
