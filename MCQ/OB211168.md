## Options

1. It will increase the rate of nonunion compared to nonoperative treatment.
2. It will increase the rate of dysphagia compared to treatment in a halo immobilization.
3. It will decrease mortality compared to nonoperative treatment.
4. It has a decreased fusion rate compared to an anterior surgical approach
5. It will lead to a lower SF-36 score.

## Correct option

**3. It will decrease mortality compared to nonoperative treatment.**

## Answer explanation

The clinical presentation is consistent with a geriatric Type II odontoid fracture that was treated with posterior C1-2 fusion. This treatment option leads to decrease mortality compared to nonoperative treatment in this age population.

Type 2 odontoid fractures are the most common fracture of the cervical spine in patients >65 years usually due to low energy falls at standing height. In patients > 65 years of age, there is a high risk of nonunion since these fractures occur in a vascular watershed area between the carotid and vertebral arteries. Studies have shown surgical treatment with a posterior C1-2 fusion increases the fusion rate and decreases the mortality as compared to nonoperative treatment with cervical immobilization in a hard or soft collar. Halo immobilization in this patient group has known complications, including dysphagia, medical comorbidities, and increase mortality, so is typically avoided in elderly patients.

*A multicenter cohort study by Fehlings et al.* *2013 examined the outcomes of 159 patients over 65 years old with type 2 odontoid fractures.* Nonunion rates were significantly higher in non-operatively treated patients, but non-union was not associated with poorer outcomes. Initial nonsurgical treatment, of the male sex, and of older age were associated with greater risk of treatment failure.

*Schroeder et al.* *2015 systematically reviewed 21 articles including 1233 patients with geriatric odontoid fractures.* Non-operatively treated patients were found to have higher short- and long-term mortality rates than surgically treated patients. Furthermore, there was no difference in outcomes regarding anterior versus posterior surgery with the exception of increased fusion rates with posterior arthrodesis.

Figure A is a sagittal CT scan of the cervical spine demonstrating a posteriorly displaced odontoid fracture. Figures B and C are the sagittal MRI T2 and STIR sequences demonstrating the same odontoid fracture. Note the cord compression as a result of the posterior displacement.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: Posterior C1-2 surgery has been shown to increase the fusion rate (decrease the non-union rate) compared to nonoperative treatment.
Answer 2: Dysphagia occurs in both surgically treated and nonsurgically treated patients. However, halo immobilization results in greater rates of dysphagia than surgical management.
Answer 4: Anterior screw osteosynthesis is indicated in select patients with the correct fracture morphology, and is typically used in younger patients. It has a decreased fusion rate compared to a posterior C1-2 fusion.
Answer 5: SF-36 scores are significantly improved (higher score) in surgically managed patients compared to nonoperative treatment. However, this outcome is not as important as mortality.
