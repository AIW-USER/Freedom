## Question

You are consulted for a patient in the ED who has a lower extremity infection. You determine the patient requires operative intervention, and during the subsequent procedure take intraoperative cultures. For which of the patients below would a polymicrobial infection most likely be identified on culture?

## Options

1. 65-year-old female with chronic periprosthetic knee infection
2. 15-year-old male with calcaneal osteomyelitis after steping on a nail while wearing work boots
3. 2-month-old male with hip joint septic arthritis
4. 27-year-old diabetic female with a necrotizing soft tissue infection
5. 58-year-old male with drainage 3 weeks after a total knee arthroplasty

## Correct option

**4. 27-year-old diabetic female with a necrotizing soft tissue infection**

## Answer explanation

The most common culture isolate from necrotizing fasciitis is polymicrobial.

Necrotizing fasciitis is an aggressive, rapidly spreading infection that spreads along fascial planes. While the initial presentation may suggest cellulitis and early skin appearance may be deceptively benign-appearing, rapid progression along fascial planes may result in skin necrosis and subsequent myofascial necrosis. The time from admission to debridement has been shown to be a significant predictor of outcomes. Immediate empiric antibiotics should be used covering polymicrobial species including aerobic, anaerobic, gram-positive, and gram-negative bacteria.

*Hakkarainen et al. reviewed current concepts in treatment and outcomes of necrotizing soft tissue infections.* **They reported** that these infections are typically caused by toxin-producing bacteria and are characterized clinically by very rapid progression of the disease with significant local tissue destruction. **They highlighted** that the infections are classically polymicrobial with various species of gram-positive cocci, gram-negative rods, and anaerobes—including clostridial species typically being isolated.

*Wong et al. reviewed the clinical presentation, microbiology, and determinants of mortality for necrotizing fasciitis.* **They reported** that polymicrobial synergistic infection was the most common cause (Streptococci and Enterobacteriaceae being the most common isolates), and Group-A streptococcus was the most common cause of monomicrobial infection, and the most common associated comorbidity was diabetes mellitus. **They concluded** that early operative debridement reduces mortality among patients with this condition.

*Lancerotto et al. reviewed the classification, diagnosis, and management of necrotizing fasciitis.* **They reported** that it is a life-threatening rare infection of the soft tissues and is a medical and surgical emergency, necessitating immediate and extensive radical debridement of necrotic tissues once suspected.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The most common organism isolated from a chronic PJI is coagulase-negative Staphylococci.
Answer 2: This patient's presentation is associated with Pseudomonas aeruginosa osteomyelitis.
Answer 3: The most common organism in a neonate septic joint infection is Group B Streptococcus
Answer 5: Acute PJI infections are most commonly caused by Staphylococcus aureus.
