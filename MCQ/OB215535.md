## Question

As a bone graft material, demineralized bone matrix (DBM) differs from freeze-dried allograft in which of the following properties?

## Options

1. The ability to provide a supporting environment capable of hosting indigenous mesenchymal stem cells, osteoblasts, and osteoclasts.
2. Contains factors that bind to specific transmembrane receptors on mesenchymal stem cells, osteoblasts, and mature chondrocytes, resulting in further recruitment of mesenchymal stem cells and subsequent proliferation and differentiation.
3. Contains viable mesenchymal stem cells, osteoblasts and osteocytes.
4. Both answers 2 & 3.
5. DBM has equivocal properties as freeze-dried allograft.

## Correct option

**2. Contains factors that bind to specific transmembrane receptors on mesenchymal stem cells, osteoblasts, and mature chondrocytes, resulting in further recruitment of mesenchymal stem cells and subsequent proliferation and differentiation.**

## Answer explanation

Answers 1, 2, and 3 describe osteoconductive, osteoinductive, and osteogenic properties of bone graft, respectively. While both demineralized bone matrix (DBM) and freeze-dried allograft are osteoconductive, DBM also has osteoinductive properties.

Bone grafts are generally classified into three categories: autogenous bone, allograft, and bone graft substitutes, and they differ in terms of their osteoconductive, osteoinductive, and osteogenic properties. Based on the preparation procedure, allograft can be divided into 3 groups: fresh-frozen, freeze-dried, and DBM. DBM is developed from allograft bone treated with acid extraction, which removes calcium and phosphate but leaves the extracellular matrix. The resultant matrix contains a type-I collagen framework in addition to a number of growth factors such as bone morphogenic proteins, transforming growth factor-β (TGF-β), insulin-like growth factor, and fibroblast growth factor. These components make DBM both osteoinductive and osteoconductive. This is in comparison to freeze-dried allografts, which only retains osteoconductivity. The preparation of freeze-dried allograft relies on the sublimation of water, which occurs when a frozen piece of allograft (below 0°C) is exposed to an atmospheric pressure below that of the vapor pressure of ice. Drying takes place as water is converted straight to vapor without becoming a liquid. While this process decreases the immunogenicity of the bone graft, it also alters the protein configuration in growth factors, which is the reason why freeze-dried allograft has very limited if any osteoinductivity.

*Drosos et al. provide a review of the existing literature concerning the use of DBM products in various procedures in the extremities.* **They state** that the osteoinductive and osteoconductive capabilities of DBM vary among different products, depending on donor characteristics and differences in the processing of the bone. **They conclude** that further clinical studies of higher quality are required in order to improve the Recommendation Grades for or against the use of DBM products in bone grafting procedures.

*Fillingham et al. present a review of the bone repair process and properties of bone grafts.* *They discuss the autogenous, allograft, bone graft substitutes (e.g. calcium sulphate, calcium phosphate, etc.) and their limitations as well as indications.*

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: This describes osteoconduction. Both DBM and freeze-dried allograft are osteoconductive.
Answer 3: This describes osteogenicity. Neither DBM nor freeze-dried allograft are osteogenic.
Answer 4: Neither DBM nor freeze-dried allograft are osteogenic
Answer 5: DBM is osteoinductive and osteoconductive, whereas freeze-dried allograft is only osteoconductive.
