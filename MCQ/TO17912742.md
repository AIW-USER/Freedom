## Question

A patient had developed advanced osteoarthritis in his ankle and underwent Total ankle arthroplasty for same. However, he failed to gain a good ROM so an x ray was done. Heterotopic Ossification was seen on the film. Bone spurs were visible but posterior joint space reduction was less than 50%. The classification grade for the case would be

## Options

1. Grade 1
2. Grade 2
3. Grade 3
4. Grade 4

## Correct option

**2. Grade 2**

## Answer explanation

Ans: B

Heterotopic ossification (HO) after Total Ankle Arthroplasty (TAA), occurring as soon as 3 months post-surgery, is a known sequela (reported incidence of osseous overgrowth after TAA: 3.8-82%) and often leads to reduced range of motion and poor functional outcomes. Osteophytes so formed can lead to malleolar impingement generating pain on traversing uneven terrains and might necessitate a repeat surgical intervention.

Risk factors are largely unknown but gender is thought to be an important factor. Males are involved twice more commonly than females. Large amount of soft tissue dissection, failure to remove bone debris from the surgical field, post operative haematoma, prolonged surgical time, inappropriately sized prosthetic components or changes made to biomechanics of ankle from mispositioned components are some of the speculated causes.

HO after TAA is classified as follows (based upon the Brooker et al. classification devised for HO after THR): -

Class 0: No Heterotopic ossification

Class I: Islands of bone within the soft tissue about the ankle

Class II: Bone spurs form the tibia or talus, reducing the posterior joint space by < 50%
Class III: Bone spurs from tibia or talus reducing posterior joint space > 50%, but non bridging
Class IV: Bridging bone continuous between the tibia and the talus

Management principles: Early treatment with active and active assisted range of motion exercises should be implemented once the presence of HO is confirmed Absolute treatment consists of surgical resection of mature bone once the HO has fully matured, in symptomatic individuals. However, once has to ensure there is no component malalignment else the condition will reoccur, which requires TAA revision. Prophylactic NSAID (Indomethacin) use has been thought to prevent HO after THR; the supportive evidence for TAA is doubtful.
