## Question

A 10-year-old male presents to your office for evaluation global ligamentous laxity. His past orthopedic history is significant for multiple fractures, including a spontaneous olecranon apophyseal avulsion. Recent genetic testing reveals a glycine substitution in the procollagen molecule. Which of the following contributed to the pathophysiology of this patient's underlying medical condition?

## Options

1. Insufficient osteoid production by periosteal osteoblasts
2. Increased collagen secretion
3. Nonsense mutation resulting in amino acid substitution
4. Mutation in EXT gene affecting prehypertrophic chondrocytes
5. Uninterrupted bone remodeling

## Correct option

**1. Insufficient osteoid production by periosteal osteoblasts**

## Answer explanation

This patient has osteogenesis imperfecta (OI). OI results in periosteal osteoblasts being unable to form sufficient osteoid and therefore unable to remodel normally.

Osteogenesis Imperfecta is a common congenital disorder caused by a mutation in COL1A1 or COL1A2 genes (90% of cases) resulting in abnormal collagen cross-linking and overall decrease in type 1 collagen. This abnormal collagen cross-linking occurs via a glycine substitution in the procollagen molecule. The presence of glycine, the smallest amino acid, causes a twist in the 3-dimensional structure of the amino acid chain, enabling it to orient into a triple helix structure. In almost all patients with osteogenesis imperfecta, a missense mutation results in a substitution of glycine by another amino acid, causing a disordering of the amino acid chain and subsequently, of the overall triple helix structure.

*Kocher et al. reviewed OI.* **They report** that OI encompasses a phenotypically and genotypically heterogeneous group of inherited disorders that result from mutations in the genes that code for type I collagen. **They concluded** that molecular genetic studies have identified more than 150 mutations of the COL1A1 and COL1A2 genes, which encode for type I procollagen.

*Shoulders et al. reviewed the structural properties of collagen.* **They report** that collagen is a fibrous, structural protein that comprises a right-handed bundle of three parallel, left-handed polyproline II-type helices. **They concluded** that the structural integrity of collagen's triple helices is important for normal function.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: OI can result from DECREASED collagen secretion and abnormal production of collagen
Answer 3: In almost all patients with osteogenesis imperfecta, a MISSENSE mutation results in a substitution of glycine by another amino acid, causing a disordering of the amino acid chain and subsequently of the overall triple helix structure
Answer 4: Multiple Hereditary Exostosis results from a mutation in EXT gene affecting prehypertrophic chondrocytes of growth plate. Conversely, OI results from a mutation in COL1A1 or COL1A2 genes cause ABNORMAL collagen cross-linking via a glycine substitution in the procollagen molecule
Answer 5: In OI, periosteal osteoblasts CANNOT form sufficient osteoid and therefore CANNOT remodel normally
