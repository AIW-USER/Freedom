## Correct option

## Answer explanation

Figure D shows a right extraforaminal (far lateral) disc herniation which would be best resected using a paraspinal, muscle-splitting approach.

Far lateral lumbar disc herniations comprise roughly 10% of all lumbar herniations. Compared to paracentral disc herniations, far lateral herniations are best approached using a paramedian (far lateral) approach which allows resection from an outside-in approach to the foramen. This allows preservation of the facet, which is beneficial for spinal stability, and better visualization and decompression of the nerve root within the foramen.

*Epstein et al. looked at 170 patients treated with surgical decompression for far lateral disc disease and followed for an average of five years postoperatively.* A variety of accompanying pathologies such as stenosis, spondylolisthesis and scoliosis made this group heterogeneous. Good to excellent outcomes were obtained in 79% of patients with an intertransverse approach, 70% of patients with a full facetectomy and 68% with a medial facetectomy.

*McCulloch et al. present an Instructional Course Lecture detailing the anatomy, pathology (extraforaminal or foraminal lumbar disc herniation, uncinate spurring, facet hypertrophy), imaging and surgery of the intertransverse window region.* *They stress that a key anatomic principle of the approach is to find a pedicle, knowing that medial to the pedicle will be a nerve root.* Microscopy is especially helpful in this approach because of the depth of the wound and limited room for instrument maneuvering.

Figure A shows a the incision for an extraforaminal approach. Figure C shows a paracentral herniation. Figure D shows a far lateral herniation on the right. Figure E shows a myxopapillary ependymoma. Illustration A shows a diagram highlighting the difference between midline and far lateral approaches to foraminal pathology.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Figure B shows a right sided synovial cyst. These are resected with a standard midline approach.
Answer 2: Figures C shows a paracentral herniations, accessible through a midline approach.
Answer 4: Figure E shows an myxopapillary ependymoma, which is an intraspinal neoplasm that may occupy the lumbosacral canal in its entirety.
Answer 5: Figure F shows a arachnoiditis, which is a clustering of the nerve roots in the thecal sac, which is not treated surgically.
