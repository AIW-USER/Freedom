## Question

## Options

1. Reconstruction using the original Jobe technique
2. Primary UCL repair with suture augmentation
3. Reconstruction using the modified Jobe technique
4. Arthroscopic debridement with UCL repair
5. Arthroscopic debridement with UCL repair and post-operative immobilization for 4 weeks

## Correct option

**3. Reconstruction using the modified Jobe technique**

## Answer explanation

This is a high-level throwing athlete with ulnar collateral ligament (UCL) insufficiency that has not improved with rest and therapy. The modified Jobe technique is the best surgical option.

UCL ruptures are synonymous with overhead throwers and baseball pitchers. These injuries are due to the high valgus torque placed on the ligament in the late-cocking/early acceleration phase of throwing. Incomplete tears are amenable to a course of non-operative treatment like described in the question stem. Surgical treatment for complete tears depends on the quality of tissue and tear pattern. High-level throwers who are advanced in their career often show attritional damage to the ligament and calcifications, and are most likely to require reconstruction. As seen in the images, there is a large calcification within the UCL, which, when removed, leaves a tissue void within the UCL. In these cases, a repair is not a viable option and the patient requires a reconstruction.

*Erickson and Romeo reviewed management of UCL injuries.* *They describe the frequency of concurrent diagnoses like valgus extension overload, capitellar OCD lesions, and flexor-pronator tendon tears.* Though there are various contemporary techniques for UCL reconstruction, most series report return to play rates over 80%, **highlighting** the benefit of this procedure. Recent evidence has not seen significant differences in return to sport rates between the modified Jobe and docking techniques.

*Dugas et al. evaluated the biomechanical strength of a UCL repair with suture augmentation compared to the modified Jobe reconstruction.* After cyclic loading, the augmented UCL repair showed similar gap formation to reconstruction. This lays the groundwork for UCL repair as a viable alternative to reconstruction, when indicated. However, in this case, the tissue void that will be left after the calcification is removed is a contraindication to a UCL repair.

Figures A and B are radiographs showing calcification within the UCL. Figure C is a coronal MRI showing a UCL tear with a degenerative ligament.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The original Jobe technique required the flexor-pronator mass to be completely taken down for graft placement. This has been abandoned as other less morbid techniques have emerged.
Answers 2,4,5: UCL repair would be reasonable in avulsion-type tears and/or those without intra-ligament calcifications.
