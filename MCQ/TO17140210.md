Chapter: Regional Orthopedics MCQs — Clinical Examination And Gait

## Question

Staheli’s test is done for (FNB Pattern Paper 2020)

## Options

1. Psoas Contracture
2. Hamstring contracture
3. Gastrocnemius contracture
4. Rectus femoris contracture

## Correct option

**1. Psoas Contracture**

## Answer explanation

- Staheli test: this test is also used to know hip flexion contracture. Staheli test is performed with the participant in the prone position on the edge of the examination table. The examiner places one hand on the pelvis and gradually extends the thigh with the other. The point at which the pelvis begins to rise indicates the end of hip motion. At this point, the horizontal-thigh angle is measured. The psoas muscle (arrow) is primarily responsible for hip flexion contracture.

Other tests for clinical examination of the hip joint

- Thomas test : The Thomas test (TT), named after Dr. Hugh Owen Thomas, was created to rule out hip flexion contracture. The patient lies supine upon an examination table with both legs straight out in front of them on the table top. While supine, the patient flexes the hip of one leg and holds the knee of the same leg maximally flexed at the chest. The pelvis is maintained in neutral throughout. The contralateral leg is allowed to remain relaxed and flat against the tabletop. A positive TT, which is taken as indicative of hip flexion contracture, is where there is noticeable hip flexion of the contralateral leg, as indicated by a gap between this leg and the table top.

![[TO17140210-1.png]]

- Modified Thomas test: The modified TT (MTT) is performed in a similar fashion to the original Thomas test, but is carried out at the edge of the tabletop. Thus, the contralateral leg is allowed to hang down over the edge of the table, which permits the measurement of a peak hip extension angle in all individuals and not just those with a hip extension deficit.

![[TO17140210-2.png]]

- Ober's test : patient is placed in lateral position with affected side up. With the hip in slight extension, abduct the leg then allow it to drop into adduction. Positive test (tight ITB) is indicated by inability to adduct leg.
- Stinchfield resisted hip flexion test : patient is made supine with extended knees. Now the patient is asked to flex the hip joint. Examiner resists active hip flexion past 30-45 deg. a positive test elicits pain which is likely to be associated with an intra articular hip pathology.

- Ely test: in prone position patient is asked to flex the affected knee toward the buttocks; make sure leg does not abduct; If pelvis on affected side flexes as the knee is being flexed, indicates short rectus femoris.

Answer: 1
