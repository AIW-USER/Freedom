## Question

A 10-year-old male is brought to the orthopedic clinic for evaluation of various orthopedic manifestations associated with mucopolysaccharidosis (MPS) type I. Upon examination, the patient exhibits notable skeletal abnormalities, coarse facial features, and hepatosplenomegaly. Which of the following musculoskeletal abnormalities is most commonly observed in patients with MPS?

## Options

1. Progressive joint contracture
2. Sacralization of L5 vertebrae
3. Congenital absence of anterior cruciate ligament
4. Block vertebrae secondary to failure of segmentation
5. Congenital radial head dislocation

## Correct option

**1. Progressive joint contracture**

## Answer explanation

Progressive joint contractures are a frequent orthopedic manifestation observed in patients diagnosed with mucopolysaccharidoses (MPS).

MPS comprises a group of disorders characterized by the accumulation of glycosaminoglycans due to deficiencies in lysosomal enzyme activity, leading to inefficient metabolism of normal cellular byproducts. Skeletal abnormalities manifest early and prominently in individuals with MPS, often prompting orthopedic surgeons as one of the first healthcare professionals to consider this diagnosis. Progressive joint contractures represent one of MPS patients' most common early musculoskeletal manifestations. Commonly affected joints include the wrist, joints of the digits, hips, and knee, causing considerable limitations in motion. Other musculoskeletal manifestations include hip dysplasia, carpal tunnel syndrome, trigger finger, and progressive thoracolumbar kyphosis.

*A review article by White et al. discusses the role of orthopedics in patients with mucopolysaccharide disorders.* *The authors comprehensively examine musculoskeletal ailments associated with MPS and emphasize the crucial significance of early diagnosis and intervention in minimizing deformity and disability.* **The authors report** trigger fingers as typical in MPS, often leading to phalangeal joint contracture if left untreated.

*In a literature review by Williams and colleagues, the role of surgical intervention in patients with MPS is explored.* After surgical interventions, **the authors report** potential improvements in quality of life and function for patients with genu valgum, carpal tunnel syndrome, and trigger fingers. However, **they note** a scarcity of data regarding quality of life and functional outcomes for MPS patients undergoing surgical intervention for hip and spine deformities. **The authors report** that more studies are needed on quality of life and functional outcomes in patients with MPS undergoing surgical intervention for hip and spine abnormalities. **The authors caution** that trigger fingers should be distinguished from joint contractures to optimize patient outcomes.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 2: Sacralization of the L5 vertebra can be seen in sacral dysmorphism and is not commonly associated with MPS.
Answer 3: Congenital absence of the anterior cruciate ligament can be seen in patients with neurofibromatosis type I or fibular hemimelia and is not commonly associated with MPS.
Answer 4: Block vertebrae, resulting from a failure of segmentation, can be associated with VACTERL syndrome and are not commonly associated with MPS.
Answer 5: Congenital radial head dislocation is not associated with MPS.
