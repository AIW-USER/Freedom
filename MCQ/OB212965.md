## Answer explanation

Figure A is an illustration of a piriformis-entry nail that should not be used in children and adolescents due to the increased risk of AVN compared to other implants.

Femoral shaft fractures treated with intramedullary devices in children may have complications. Avascular necrosis (AVN) is a rare, but serious complication of this treatment. The entry site of the nail has been speculated to have an effect on the risk of AVN with the tip of the greater trochanter, and the lateral aspect of the greater trochanter used in an attempt to avoid the proximal femoral blood supply. Lateral entry nails, which are inserted in the lateral aspect of the greater trochanter, have been shown to be the safest implant choice in order to decrease the risk of AVN.

*Keeler et al. performed a retrospective review of 78 children with femur fractures treated with lateral entry nails.* **They found** that all patients went on to union. They had no cases of nonunion, delayed union, or malunion. No patient had evidence of osteonecrosis of the femoral head. **They conclude** that IMN fixation through the lateral aspect of the greater trochanter in children and adolescents is effective.

*MacNeil et al. performed a systematic review to elucidate the effects of nail entry site on the risk of AVN of the femoral head in the skeletally immature patient.* **They found** that the piriformis entry nail had an AVN rate of 2%. The AVN rate for entry at the tip of the greater trochanter was 1.4%. There were no reported cases of AVN using the lateral aspect of the greater trochanter. **They conclude** that the lateral trochanter has the lowest risk of AVN when treating pediatric femur fractures.

Figure A is an illustration of a reconstruction nail inserted through the piriformis fossa. Figures B and C are both illustrations of lateral entry nails. Figure D is an illustration of flexible nails. Figure E is an illustration of a greater trochanter-entry nail. Illustration A is an illustration demonstrating entry sites for pediatric nails (A: lateral-entry, B: greater trochanter-entry, C: piriformis-entry).

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 2, 3, 4, & 5: All of these have a lower risk of causing AVN and may therefore be used to treat pediatric femoral shaft fractures.
