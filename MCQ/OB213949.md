## Question

A 60-year-old female presents to the office with medial sided knee pain refractory to extensive conservative treatment. Imaging studies are shown in Figures A and B. She undergoes a successful unicompartmental knee arthroplasty (UKA). What is the most common reason for revision surgery following UKA for this pathology?

## Options

1. Aseptic Loosening
2. Bearing Dislocation
3. Implant Failure
4. Periprosthetic Fracture
5. Progression of Arthritis

## Correct option

**5. Progression of Arthritis**

## Answer explanation

Progression of osteoarthritis is the most common reason for revision arthroplasty in patients with spontaneous osteonecrosis of the knee (SONK) treated with unicompartmental knee arthroplasty (UKA).

Treatment for SONK initially involved non-operative symptomatic management. Surgical options for the younger or more active patients generally include microfracture, osteochondral allograft transfer, and high tibial osteotomy. For the older or less active patient, treatment involves UKA for disease isolated to a single compartment or total knee arthroplasty for diffuse degenerative changes involving multiple compartments of the knee. While UKA has been shown to offer reliable symptomatic relief, revision is most often related to progression of arthritis in the remaining compartments.

*Chalmers et al. present their findings of 46 patients undergoing UKA for knee osteonecrosis.* **The authors found** that UKA for secondary osteonecrosis (i.e. risk factors such as lupus, alcohol use, sickle cell, etc.) had less durability when compared to those with UKA for SONK. **The authors conclude** that UKA for SONK is an effective means of treatment, and that failure of treatment was due to the progression of knee arthritis.

*Kaneko et al. present their findings of 61 patients with SONK treated with medial UKA.* **The authors found** that ten-year implant survival for UKA in this population was 90%. **They conclude** that based on patient-reported outcome measures, UKA for SONK is an acceptable and effective treatment modality.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: While aseptic loosening is a mode of failure in UKA, it is not more common than the progression of arthritis.
Answer 2: The risk of bearing dislocation is not increased in patients with SONK.
Answer 3: Implant failure is a less common etiology for revision arthroplasty than the progression of arthritis in patients with SONK.
Answer 4: While periprosthetic fracture may occur in patients with SONK, it is a less common indication for revision than the progression of arthritis.
