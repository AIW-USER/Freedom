Chapter: Orthopedics Oncology MCQs — Osteosarcoma

## Question

All statements are false with regards to Osteosarcoma except

## Options

1. Radiation Induced Osteosarcoma has worse prognosis than Conventional High-Grade Osteosarcoma
2. High dose methotrexate based 3 drug Chemotherapy regimen (MAP Protocol) has higher toxicity profile than 2 drug (Adriamycin/ Cisplatin) regimen and hence not preferred
3. Radiation therapy can be given as local treatment for unresectable sacral osteosarcoma with a curative intent
4. Parosteal Osteosarcoma most commonly occurs in distal femur in adolescent girls

## Correct option

**1. Radiation Induced Osteosarcoma has worse prognosis than Conventional High-Grade Osteosarcoma**

## Answer explanation

Radiation induced osteosarcoma occurs after the patient has received radiation therapy for unrelated malignancy in the past.

For any sarcoma to be labelled as radiation induced, it must meet the following criteria described by Arlen

- Microscopic/ radiologic evidence of non-malignancy / malignancy of different histology before radiation
- Sarcoma developing in the path of radiation beam
- Long asymptomatic period between radiation and diagnosis of secondary sarcoma
- Histological proof of diagnosis of secondary sarcoma

Radiation induced osteosarcoma is not very responsive to chemotherapy and many a times occur in elderly age group who may not tolerate full dose of chemotherapy unlike children and hence it has a worse prognosis than conventional high-grade osteosarcoma

Current standard of chemotherapy for osteosarcoma is as per MAP Protocol, where M signifies High Dose Methotrexate, A signifies Adriamycin and P signifies Cisplatin.

The chemotherapy regimen is as follows

Day 1-3 Adriamycin 25mg/m2 per day over 3 days (total of 75 mg/m2)

Day 1-2 Cisplatin 100mg/m2 over 2 days

Day 21 High Dose Methotrexate (12 gm/m2)

Day 28 High dose Methotrexate (12 gm/m2)

Day 35 second cycle starts

3 drug regimens though have higher side effect profile than chemotherapy regimens with two drugs but they offer survival advantage of around 10 % than two drug regimens and hence MAP protocol is the current preferred chemotherapy regimen for osteosarcoma in children.

Radiation therapy has no role in the management of osteosarcoma. In unresectable sacral osteosarcomas, radiation therapy can be given for pain relief as a palliative measure and not with a curative intent. Another use of radiation in osteosarcoma is its use via ECRT to sterilize the bone and put it back where 50Gy is administered in a single fraction which kills all the living cells.

Parosteal osteosarcoma is low grade osteosarcoma commonly seen in middle aged females not adolescent girls. Common site is posterior condyle of distal femur. Being low grade, parosteal osteosarcoma is treated only with surgery and does not require chemotherapy. Patients may give a history of swelling over years and sudden increase in size which has been progressing slowly over the years should raise the suspicion of it getting dedifferentiated into higher grade.

Answer A
