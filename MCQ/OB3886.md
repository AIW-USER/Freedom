## Question

Excision of the medial sesamoid of the great toe is indicated for which of the following presentations or procedures?

## Options

1. Coupled with Lapidus procedure for hallux valgus
2. Recalcitrant medial sesamoid stress fracture with fragmentation
3. Acute medial sesamoid fracture
4. Coupled with first metatarsophalangeal joint arthrodesis for hallux rigidus
5. Morton's neuroma

## Correct option

**2. Recalcitrant medial sesamoid stress fracture with fragmentation**

## Answer explanation

Recalcitrant medial sesamoid fractures that have failed non-operative management are treated with a partial or complete sesamoidectomy.

Medial sesamoid stress fractures with fragmentation that have not responded to nonsurgical management have done well after sesamoid excision. Stress fractures occur more often at the medial sesamoid, and females are mainly afflicted. When a stress fracture is suspected, bone scan and CT scan are suggested as more reliable in confirming the diagnosis than other imaging methods. Initial treatment almost always involves an accommodative orthosis, but surgical intervention may be necessary in recalcitrant cases. When conservative treatment has failed, surgical excision of the proximal fragment is recommended in some instances, whereas other instances may require removal of the entire sesamoid. Excision of both sesamoids is not recommended.

*Biedert et al. reported on stress fractures of the sesamoids of the great toe and* **reported** that the use of CT or MRI may be needed for diagnosis. **They report** that conservative treatment should be utilized first, but if symptoms persist, excision of the medial sesamoid was successful in all patients at a mean of over 4 years postoperatively.

*Dedmond et al. present a review of the anatomy of the hallucal sesamoid complex.* **They report** that the hallucal sesamoids are vitally important to normal weight-bearing and foot mechanics, as the sesamoid complex of the hallux normally transmits up to 50% of body weight and during push-off can transmit loads >300%.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: A Lapidus procedure does not require a sesamoidectomy in combination.
Answer 3: Acute medial sesamoid fractures can be treated with non-operative modalities at first. If treatment fails after 3-12 months a partial or complete sesamoidectomy may be performed.
Answer 4: Arthrodesis of the hallux metatarsophalangeal joint does not necessitate a sesamoidectomy.
Answer 5: Morton's neuroma is treated with a neurectomy with the burial of the nerve in the foot intrinsic muscles.
