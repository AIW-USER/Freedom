## Question

## Options

1. Pinning that crosses the physis, but not the DIPJ
2. Pinning that crosses the physis and DIPJ
3. Repair of the nailbed injury with 6-0 dissolvable suture
4. Long term intravenous (IV) antibiotic suppression
5. Removing the interposed soft tissue from the fracture site.

## Correct option

**5. Removing the interposed soft tissue from the fracture site.**

## Answer explanation

Surgical treatment of Seymour fractures must include removal of soft tissue from the fracture site.

A Seymour fracture is a distal phalanx physeal fracture with an associated nail bed injury. The middle is the most commonly affected finger. The nailbed is typically lacerated, the nail plate is displaced, and the germinal matrix can be interposed at the fracture site. Seymour fractures can be managed closed if there is no interposed soft tissue at the fracture site; however, surgery is indicated primarily to remove interposed soft tissue, reduce the fracture, and repair the nailbed. Pinning is a possible adjunct to achieve improved skeletal stability but is not required.

*Lin et al reviewed 65 Seymour fractures.* *89% were managed in the emergency department (ED) and 11% were taken to the operating room (OR) (which included irrigation and debridement, open reduction, and k-wire fixation).* The primary reason for OR treatment was unsuccessful closed-reduction. Four patients managed in the ED needed eventual treatment in the OR because of fracture displacement. Superficial infection was the most common complication. **The authors postulate** that most Seymour fractures can be managed in the ED.

*Reyes and Ho reviewed 35 Seymour fractures; 31% of which received acute appropriate treatment, 37% of which received acute, partial treatment, and 31% of which received delayed treatment.* There were 7 infections that varied by the three groups (0%, 15%, and 45%). Early appropriate treatment of Seymour fractures decreases the likelihood of an infection.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1-2: Pinning is not required in the surgical treatment of a Seymour fracture. It may be done to help stabilize a displaced and unstable distal phalanx, but it is not as critical as removing interposed soft tissue from the fracture site.
Answer 3: The nail bed injury could be treated with small suture repair or dermabond.
Answer 4: Undiagnosed Seymour fractures may develop infections, but this case does not indicate that this patient must be treated with long term IV antibiotics.
