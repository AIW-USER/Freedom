## Question

A 56-year-old Type 2 diabetic man presents to your clinic with a complaint of chronic neck pain and stiffness. Recently had trouble with food getting "stuck" in his throat. He denies any recent fevers or weight loss. On physical exam, he has less than 5 degrees of cervical flexion, extension, and rotation. Figure A is a recently obtained x-ray after undergoing total hip arthroplasty. What is the next most appropriate step in management?

## Options

1. Genetic testing for HLA B-27
2. Magnetic resonanc imaging of the cervical, thoracic and lumbar spine
3. Referral to pain management for epidural steroid injections
4. Serum labs including ESR, CRP, and WBC
5. Xray of the cervical, thoracic and lumbar spine

## Correct option

**5. Xray of the cervical, thoracic and lumbar spine**

## Answer explanation

The patient has risk factors, symptoms, and physical exam findings concerning for Diffuse Idiopathic Skeletal Hyperostosis (DISH). Radiographs of the cervical, thoracic and lumbar spine are the most appropriate next step in management to evaluate his spine, and determine the diagnosis.

DISH is an enthesopathy of the spine and extremities with unknown etiology. It is diagnosed on x-ray demonstrating non-marginal syndesmophytes involving three successive levels of the spine (4 contiguous vertebrae). Additional features include preservation of disc height, and absence of facet joint and SI joint involvement. The thoracic spine is most commonly involved, followed by the cervical and lumbar spine. Often this is asymptomatic and found incidentally. If symptoms exist they include chronic back pain and stiffness for thoracic and lumbar spine involvement. Cervical spine involvement can present with pain, stiffness, dysphagia, and hoarseness. Comorbid risk factors include diabetes, hyperlipidemia, and gout.

*Mader et al. reviewed the clinical features of DISH.* **They report** DISH is characterized by entheseal ossification/calcification of the spine as well as entheses of the peripheral joints. **They also highlight** that DISH is associated with many metabolic derangements including hyperlipidemia and diabetes.

*Kuperus et al. reported on the lack of consensus for classification criteria for DISH.* **They point out** most articles report spinal hyperostosis as a requirement for the diagnosis of DISH, but there are discrepancies regarding the number of levels and developmental phases. **They report** peripheral entheses involvement was a co-requirement for a diagnosis in only 5 of 24 reviewed articles.

Figure A shows a total hip arthroplasty with an intact SI joint. Ankylosing spondylitis would have destruction of the SI joints while in DISH they would be relatively spared. Illustration A is a lateral cervical radiograph showing the flowing non-marginal syndesmophytes, characteristic of DISH.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: HLA B-27 is not associated with DISH
Answer 2: Radiographs are indicated prior to any advanced imaging to confirm the diagnosis of DISH
Answer 3: Prior to possible pain management evaluation, radiographs should be obtained.
Answer 4: ESR, CRP and WBC would be indicated for infection workup, however the patient has no signs or symptoms indicative of possible infection.
