## Options

1. Time to surgery
2. Smoking history
3. Body mass index
4. Displacement of the fracture
5. Time to corticosteroid administration

## Correct option

**4. Displacement of the fracture**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a displaced sacral U-type fracture; the amount of initial displacement in a sacral fracture is most predictive of neurologic dysfunction and prognosis.

Spinopelvic dissociation refers to an injury pattern where the upper part of the sacrum remains attached to the lumbar spine while the lower part remains attached to the pelvis. They typically result from high energy mechanisms or low energy falls in patients with osteoporotic bone. They are associated with high morbidity/mortality and additional fractures, as well as neurological, soft-tissue, and vascular injuries. There is a high prevalence of lumbosacral plexus injury and cauda equina syndrome in these patients; studies have demonstrated a poor neurological prognosis regardless of reduction/fixation quality.

*Lindahl et al. performed a retrospective chart review of patients with spinopelvic dissociation undergoing surgical fixation to determine factors associated with prognosis.* *Despite acceptable reduction and fixation, 15/36 patients had a poor outcome when utilizing the Pelvis Outcome Scale.* **They found** that translation/displacement of the sacral fracture was most predictive of outcomes and neurologic dysfunction.

*Adelved et al. performed a prospective cohort study to determine patient-reported outcomes 8 years after operatively treated displaced sacral fractures.* The primary outcome measure was patient-reported health as determined by the SF-36. **They found** that, at a mean of 11 years post-injury, patient outcomes were poor, without meaningful additional improvement after the first year.

Figure A is an AP pelvis radiograph of a patient with a U-type sacral fracture (appears to be an inlet view secondary to kyphotic deformity); these fractures are notoriously difficult to appreciate on x-ray and can be easily missed. Figures B and C, respectively, are axial and sagittal CT cuts of the same patient. Illustration A is a radiograph demonstrating the technique of triangular osteosynthesis, the most biomechanically stable construct in treating a spinopelvic dissociation injury. Illustration B is the Denis classification system for sacral fractures; Zone 3 injuries are associated with the highest risk of neurologic injury.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Time to surgery has not been shown to affect the outcome in spinopelvic dissociation injuries.
Answers 2, and 3: While smoking history and BMI may put the patient at risk of infection and poor wound healing, they are not associated with a higher risk of neurologic injury.
Answer 4: There is no definitive evidence supporting the use of corticosteroids in sacral fractures or spinal cord injuries in general, and their use is controversial.
