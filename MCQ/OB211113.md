## Question

When discussing with a patient the surgical treatment options for the condition seen in Figure A you are asked about the addition of an interbody spacer compared to posterior spinal fusion (PSF) alone. You state that the scientific evidence shows the use of an interbody fusion for this condition leads to which of the following?

![[q211113-a-l4-5-degenerative-spondylolisthesis.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Longer hospital stay
2. Improved 36-Item Short-Form Health Survey (SF-36) scores
3. Improved Oswestry Disability Index (ODI) scores
4. Higher fusion rates
5. Increased hospital costs

## Correct option

**5. Increased hospital costs**

## Answer explanation

The addition of an interbody fusion for the treatment for degenerative spondylolisthesis has been shown to increase hospital costs.

Treatment for degenerative spondylolisthesis ranges from therapy and injections to wide decompression and fusion. There are multiple methods for fusion following wide decompression at the unstable level or levels. Interbody fusion is theorized to improve chances of fusion given the additional biologic interface for bone healing. Although the technique has become increasingly popular, the value in the technique has not been shown to offset the increased healthcare cost.

*Gottschalk et al. reviewed 179 patients who underwent posterior spinal fusion (PSF) alone or PSF with interbody fusion for degenerative spondylolisthesis.* **They found** no difference in Oswestry Disability Index (ODI) scores, SF-36 scores, fusion rates, or cost/value. **They concluded** that interbody fusion increased hospital costs without an improvement in fusion rates or outcomes scores when compared to PSF alone.

*Carreon et al. modeled the cost-effectiveness of posterior spinal fusion versus an interbody fusion technique.* They found interbody fusion would be cost prohibitive at various dollar amounts ranging from $1570 to $4830 depending on the outcomes scale used and the threshold for quality-adjusted life years (QALYs). **They conclude** that performing a cost-effectiveness study on this topic depends on the measure of health utility selected, the durability of the intervention, readmission rates, and accuracy of cost assumptions.

Figure A shows an MRI of the lumbar spine demonstrating multilevel degenerative disease and stenosis with an L4-5 degenerative spondylolisthesis.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Length of stay has not been shown to increase with the use of an interbody fusion technique.
Answer 2 & 3: SF-36 and ODI scores have not been shown to be significantly different following an interbody fusion technique when compared to a posterior fusion technique.
Answer 4: Fusion rates have not been shown to increase with an interbody fusion technique when compared to posterior spinal fusion.
