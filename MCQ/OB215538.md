## Question

In which of the following patients would prophylaxis for deep vein thrombosis (DVT) not be absolutely indicated?

## Options

1. 74-year-old female status post total hip arthroplasty for femoral neck fracture
2. 81-year-old male with history of lung cancer who underwent hip intramedullary nailing (IMN) for intertrochanteric hip fracture
3. 22-year-old obese male who underwent femur intramedullary nailing (IMN)
4. 35-year-old female smoker on birth control, who is status post open reduction and internal fixation (ORIF) of a tibial plateau fracture
5. 37-year-old healthy male who undergoes open reduction & internal fixation (ORIF) for an isolated rotational ankle fracture

## Correct option

**5. 37-year-old healthy male who undergoes open reduction & internal fixation (ORIF) for an isolated rotational ankle fracture**

## Answer explanation

In patients who undergo surgery for orthopedic injuries below the knee and who do not have known risk factors, prophylaxis for deep vein thrombosis (DVT) may not be absolutely indicated.

DVT prophylaxis is an important consideration in orthopedic surgery as reported post-operative DVT rates are as high as 3-5% in some cohorts. Risk factors for DVT which would necessitate prophylaxis include: older age, obesity, history of DVT, history of malignancy, birth control, smoking, long-term nonambulatory status, spinal cord injury or axial skeleton injury. In lower extremity injuries below the knee, rates of DVT have not been shown to be significantly higher with the use of placebo compared to chemical prophylaxis post-operatively. Despite this evidence, many surgeons continue to prescribe DVT prophylaxis in these cases out of fear for litigation should a DVT or pulmonary embolism occur.

*Sagi et al attempted to review orthopedic surgeon's DVT prophylaxis use and provide guidelines for best practice in this area.* **They found** that there is a wide variation of DVT prophylaxis with regard to the types of cases it is used for and the type of chemical prophylaxis chosen. They review each of the potential chemical prophylaxis options but do not provide specific recommendations on which should be used in particular clinical scenarios.

*Selby et al reviewed the use of DVT prophylaxis in isolated below knee fractures by comparing subcutaneous dalteparin to placebo in 265 patients.* **They note** no significant decline in DVT in the dalteparin group to suggest clinical benefit. **They conclude** by stating that in isolated below knee fractures of the tibia, fibula and ankle, risk for DVT is low without DVT prophylaxis and the risk of chemical prophylaxis may outweigh theoretical benefits.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: This patient sustained a hip fracture and subsequently underwent total hip arthroplasty, both of which are indications for chemical DVT prophylaxis.
Answer 2: This patient's lung cancer history places him at a higher risk of DVT given his hyper-coagulable state. DVT prophylaxis is indicated.
Answer 3: This patient's obesity and above-knee fracture place him at higher risk for DVT.
Answer 4: Smoking and use of oral contraceptives both increase risk for DVT after lower extremity injury and given the patient's non-weight bearing requirements after surgery, chemical DVT prophylaxis would be recommended.
