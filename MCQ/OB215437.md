## Question

A 25-year-old male was referred to the rheumatologist after being diagnosed with bilateral sacroiliitis 3 weeks ago. Laboratory workup at that time revealed a negative rheumatoid factor (RF). Which of the following additional findings is most likely present in this patient?

## Options

1. Increased forced expiratory volume (FEV)
2. Inflammation of the eye
3. Non-marginal syndesmophytes
4. Champagne glass pelvis
5. Diabetes

## Correct option

**2. Inflammation of the eye**

## Answer explanation

Uveitis, inflammation of the eye, is one of the most common presenting symptoms for patients with undiagnosed ankylosing spondylitis and warrants referral to an ophthalmologist.

Ankylosing spondylitis (AS) is a systemic, autoimmune spondyloarthropathy that commonly presents with bilateral sacroiliitis in a young male during their 20’s. Most patients diagnosed with AS are found to be HLA-B27 positive (90%) and RF negative, and this combination with bilateral sacroiliitis are the main criteria for diagnosis. Many systemic manifestations also occur including anterior uveitis, pulmonary fibrosis, and cardiac conduction abnormalities. Radiographic evaluation of the spine should also be performed during the workup of these patients and will reveal marginal syndesmophytes, which are pathognomonic for AS.

*Weiss et. al. review juvenile spondyloarthritis.* **They report** that this subset of diseases is characterized by its male predominance, adolescent onset and association with HLA-B27. **They conclude** that treatment should include NSAIDs and disease modifying agents for peripheral disease and tumor necrosis factor inhibitory agents for axial disease.

*Thomas et. al. review the genetics and genomics of ankylosing spondylitis.* **They report** that uveitis does not precede axial or peripheral inflammation but is often the first clinical sign presented to physicians. **They conclude** that 40% of AS patients are also diagnosed with uveitis.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Patients with AS commonly have pulmonary fibrosis, which leads to a decrease in forced expiratory volume (FEV).
Answer 3: AS is associated with marginal syndesmophytes, whereas, non-marginal syndesmophytes are commonly seen with DISH.
Answer 4: A champagne glass pelvis is commonly seen in patients with Achondroplasia, not AS.
Answer 5: Diabetes is not associated with AS, however, may be seen in patients with DISH.
