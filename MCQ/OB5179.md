## Question

All of the following decrease the risk of burn from a cast saw EXCEPT:

## Options

1. Using a vacuum
2. Using fiberglass cast material
3. Casting with a decreased cast thickness
4. Using an "up and down" saw technique
5. Checking the blade frequently

## Correct option

**2. Using fiberglass cast material**

## Answer explanation

Fiberglass generates more heat than plaster when cut with a cast saw and therefore increases the risk of causing a burn.

A common mistake in cast removal technique is cutting in a line without removing the blade from the cast material. This continuous contact generates more heat than using an 'up and down' technique. Additionally, an 'up and down' technique allows continuous assessment of the depth of saw penetration.

*Shuler et al. performed a cadaver study in which they recorded temperatures at the skin padding interface, cast-padding interface and the blade.* **They found** that poor cast removal technique, fiberglass, and using less cast padding increased temperatures at the skin-padding interface.

*Killian et al. tested three cast saw models to determine blade temperature during cast removal.* **They found** blade temperatures ranging from 105 to 215 degrees for all three models (Stryker was the lowest). The Stryker model tested had a vacuum attachment, and use of this was required to keep operating temperatures consistently below 140 degrees. However, after just 10 uses, all three models had significantly increased temperatures (in the 150 to 210 degree range).

Incorrect answers:
Answers 1, 3, 4, 5: All of these decrease temperature generated by the cast saw.
