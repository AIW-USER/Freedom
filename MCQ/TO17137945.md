## Question

A man sustained a low velocity handgun wound to his arm following a quarrel. X ray showed no fracture of humerus. On examination both entry and exit wounds are present. Appropriate management includes

## Options

1. local wound care and oral antibiotics
2. surgical debridement and single shot iv antibiotic
3. surgical debridement and continued iv antibiotics until repeat debridement shows healthy granulation tissue
4. surgical debridement and single shot iv antibiotic and discharge on oral antibiotics

## Correct option

**1. local wound care and oral antibiotics**

- In most low-velocity GSW injuries, formal debridement is not required. Simple local wound care of the entry and exit site, combined with a short course of an oral broad-spectrum antibiotic (e.g., cephalosporin) will suffice. If there is a stable fracture that is amenable to conservative management, then cast immobilization with a window to allow wound care is appropriate. Where surgical fixation of the fracture is warranted, the injury can be treated as a closed fracture. However, caution should be exercised when dealing with fractures involving the ulna and tibia, which are relatively exposed due to their subcutaneous nature resulting in increased rates of subsequent infection.28 The risk of exposed bone fragments into the wound is high and such fractures should be treated as any open fracture with 799 thorough wound excision, debridement, appropriate fracture stabilization, and soft tissue coverage if required.
- In high-velocity wounds, the principle of management is similar to that of blast injuries. Priority is given to life- and limb-saving procedures. The risk of infection is much greater in high-energy injuries due to the amount of soft tissue injury. As a result, unlike low-velocity gunshot injuries, these injuries are always treated similarly to open fractures.

a first-generation cephalosporin such as cefazolin or co-amoxiclav can be recommended for a period of 48 to 72 hours, without the addition of enhanced gram-negative coverage, even for type III open fractures.72 As these wounds may require repeat debridement and irrigations, the decision to continue antibiotics for a longer duration can be made during the subsequent debridement. In wounds that require repeat debridement, the use of antibiotic-impregnated polymethylmethacrylate (PMMA) beads or spacers can be considered.

Through surgical wound debridement should be done in all high velocity wounds to remove all devitalized tissues. Depending on the contamination repeat debridement may be required.

Retained bullet fragments are generally benign and can commonly be left in place as long as they are not located in a location that will cause pain, that is, palm of hand, sole of foot or periarticular, or are intra-articular due to concern for lead poisoning.

However, in close-range shotgun injuries, as these will require surgical debridement due to the subsequent tissue damage, the wadding should be removed to minimize the risk of infection as it is commonly composed of organic material.

Answer: 1
