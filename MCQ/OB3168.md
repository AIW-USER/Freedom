## Options

1. Neoadjuvant chemotherapy followed by surgical excision with subsequent adjuvant chemotherapy
2. 600-800 cgy irradiation
3. Observation with serial radiographs every 6 months
4. Nail removal and surgical excision of the lesion
5. Hallux amputation

## Correct option

**4. Nail removal and surgical excision of the lesion**

## Answer explanation

Subungual exostosis is a benign osteocartilaginous tumor. It occurs mostly in children and young adults and often affects the hallux. It has been thought to be a reactive metaplasia resulting from microtrauma, but its exact pathogenesis is unclear. Surgical excision is indicated in symptomatic cases that have failed conservative treatment.

The reference by Campanelli and Borradori is a case report of a subungal exostosis. *Letts et al conducted a Level 4 review of 21 children treated for subungal exostosis.* *They performed complete excision and report that removal of the nail over the exostosis facilitates the entire removal of the lesion.* The lesion recurred in three children. Illustration A is a histology slide of subungal exostosis demonstrating mature trabecular bone covered by hyaline fibrocartilage tissue.
