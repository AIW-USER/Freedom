## Question

An 18-year-old patient with paralytic polio comes to you with rigid equinus deformity of foot. He has grade 2 power in Tibialis Anterior (TA) and Tibialis Posterior (TP) muscles. What would be the best treatment for him?

## Options

1. Ankle arthrodesis
2. Triple arthrodesis
3. Pan Talar arthrodesis
4. Tendon transfer procedure

## Correct option

**3. Pan Talar arthrodesis**

## Answer explanation

Answer: 3

Poliomyelitis is rare in developed countries, cases with late sequela of poliomyelitis are common in developing countries. Foot and ankle deformities in poliomyelitis can be attributed to abnormal muscle balance, abnormal posture, gravitation forces, and continued walking over the deformed foot.Late presenting or neglected poliomyelitis deformities of the foot and ankle can lead to gait abnormalities, abnormal pressure areas, increased energy expenditure, and joint arthritis.Callosities are also common and can be troublesome at times and hence, a rigid equinus foot deformity, as mentioned in the question, must be corrected.

The goal of the treatment is to get a painless, stable, plantigrade foot.

Conservative modalities like orthotics, physiotherapy, stretching and bracing should always be tried first. Corrective orthotics may especially work for a flexible deformity in childhood but compliance may be an issue. In non-compliant cases, long-standing, progressive and rigid (fixed) deformity cases addressing surgically is better alternative but surgical management is undertaken only after a failure of conservative modalities. Corrective osteotomies, soft tissue releases, tendon transfers, and arthrodesis are all part and parcel of surgical management.

If flexible deformities (that failed conservative management) require surgery, soft tissue procedures in the form of tendon releases, lengthening and tendon transfers, are chosen. The majority of these flexible deformities in polio on a long-standing course become fixed ones. The fixed deformities require more bony procedures in the form of corrective osteotomies and arthrodesis. Arthrodesis is reserved until the age of maturity.

Tibialis anterior is one of the most frequently completely paralysed muscles. This results in a foot drop that eventually becomes a fixed equinus deformity. However, in Polio, depending on the pattern of paralysis (muscles involved), a range of deformities may occur that could include equinus, calcaneus, hindfoot varus or valgus, pes cavus or pes planus, or any combination of these.

An established deformity may be corrected by releasing or lengthening contracted tendons in the younger child but bony surgery is mostly needed in older children (like the given case). Resection of wedges of bone from the tarsus often facilitates the correction of rigid deformities. The location and orientation of the wedge will vary with different deformities; the wedge resection should be extra-articular whenever possible. Wedges that include the articular surfaces of joints may be excised when an arthrodesis of the joint is planned as part of the strategy of deformity correction.

Now,

In given question, where deformity is fixed and power in TA and TP is Grade 2 only, tendon transfers won’t be a feasible plan to choose as they require supple joints. Careful estimation of the muscle power by manual muscle testing is extremely important in planning a tendon transfer and the power of each muscle must be documented before contemplating a tendon transfer as the muscle selected for transfer must have normal or near normal power (Grade IV or V) for the transfer to be effective. This is again not the case in given question.

Since, patient is an adult and ankle-foot is almost flail (no power in TA and TP) that entails an arthrodesis procedure rather, like the triple arthrodesis or Pan talar arthrodesis.

Suppose Triple arthrodesis is chosen, which entails a fusion of the talo-calcaneal (sub-talar), talo-navicular and calcaneo-cuboid joints, it will be extremely important to restore muscle balance at the time of performing a triple fusion; failure to do so will result in late secondary deformities at the ankle as the unbalanced forces will act on the mobile joint proximal to the level of fusion. A pan talar arthrodesis (Triple arthrodesis combined with Tibio-talar arthrodesis) will provide better stability and safety in this context. So best to choose will be

Pan Talar > Triple arthrodesis
