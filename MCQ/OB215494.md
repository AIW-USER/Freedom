## Question

Figure A is an intraoperative arthroscopy image of a 62-year-old male with long-standing, diffuse knee pain without mechanical symptoms. He has failed conservative measures including physical therapy, activity modifications, NSAIDs, and corticosteroid injections. Which of the following would provide the most symptomatic improvement for this patient?

![[q215494-a-screen-shot-2021-04-23-at-8-19-14-pm.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Allopurinol
2. Injection of platelet rich plasma (PRP)
3. Meniscectomy
4. Chondroplasty
5. Arthroplasty

## Correct option

**5. Arthroplasty**

## Answer explanation

This is a 62-year-old patient with end-stage osteoarthritis without mechanical symptoms, thus arthroplasty is most likely to improve his symptoms.

Total knee arthroplasty (TKA) or unicompartmental arthroplasty (UKA) are recommended for patients with symptomatic arthritis in at least one (UKA) or multiple (TKA) compartments of the knee after other treatment options have been exhausted. Nonoperative measures include activity modifications, NSAIDs, physical therapy, and corticosteroid injections. The AAOS strongly recommends AGAINST knee arthroscopy for patients with a primary diagnosis of knee arthritis without mechanical symptoms. Other treatment options such as platelet-rich plasma or stem cell injections have shown promise, but remain unproven and are not currently recommended in this setting. Arthroplasty would provide this patient with the most predictable relief of symptoms.

*Duivenvoorden et al. performed a Cochrane review on braces and orthoses as they relate to the treatment of osteoarthritis of the knee.* **They report** that in the setting of isolated varus medial compartment arthritis, that there is low-quality, inconclusive evidence showing a benefit to bracing to improve pain, stiffness, or function. **They conclude** that the optimal orthosis for knee arthritis is unclear and that long-term good quality research is lacking.

*Moseley et al. performed a single-blinded randomized controlled trial directly comparing arthroscopic debridement, arthroscopic lavage, and placebo sham surgery of the knee for patients with a primary diagnosis of osteoarthritis of the knee.* **The authors found** that neither the debridement nor lavage group experienced any difference in pain or function as compared to the placebo group at any time point within the first two years of surgery. **They concluded** that there was no discernable benefit.

*Campbell et al. performed a meta-analysis on the outcome of intraarticular platelet-rich plasma (PRP) injections in patients with primary knee osteoarthritis.* *They report on three studies (Level II to Level IV evidence) comparing PRP to corticosteroids or hyaluronic acid injections.* **They found** improvement starting at 2 months in the PRP groups and lasting up to 12 months, however, this was most significant in patients with low-grade arthritis. **They conclude** that PRP should be considered in patients with early degenerative changes to the knee.

Figure A is an arthroscopic image of the medial compartment of the right knee demonstrating advanced degenerative changes. Illustration A demonstrates the arthroscopic appearance of a normal knee.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Allopurinol is a xanthine oxidase inhibitor and is used in the treatment of gout.
Answer 2: While some studies show potential improvement with PRP for early-stage osteoarthritis, this remains unproven and would not be recommended in this patient given the presence of advanced arthritis.
Answer 3: Meniscectomy in a patient with advanced osteoarthritis without mechanical symptoms is unlikely to improve his symptoms.
Answer 4: This patient has advanced degenerative changes, thus chondroplasty is unlikely to improve his symptoms.
