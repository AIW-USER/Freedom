## Question

A 30-year-old male presents to your office for continued pain in his right shoulder. He injured his shoulder 2-weeks prior while riding motocross. Figure A is the radiograph obtained at the ED visit 2-weeks ago. Further imaging obtained in office today is seen in Figure B. ## Options

1. Axillary nerve injury
2. Acromial stress fracture
3. Avascular necrosis
4. Degenerative joint destruction
5. Instability

## Correct option

**5. Instability**

## Answer explanation

This patient sustained a posterior shoulder dislocation. Following stiffness, the next most common complication following surgical stabilization of this injury is recurrent instability.

Posterior shoulder instability is a rare form of instability that often presents with pain rather than feelings of instability. It often occurs in young athletes during activities that put the shoulder in an “at-risk position” (flexion, adduction, internal rotation). Repetitive microtrauma can lead to posterior shoulder instability (ie. football linemen). Arthroscopic posterior labral repair may be performed with a posterior labral tear and posterior instability. The most common complication seen after arthroscopic posterior labral repair is stiffness, followed by recurrent instability and then degenerative joint disease.

*Millett et al. reviewed recurrent posterior shoulder instability.* **They reported** that it is often unrecognized, leading to incorrect diagnoses, delays in diagnosis, and even missed diagnoses, but surgical intervention is indicated when conservative treatment fails. **They concluded** that arthroscopic treatment of posterior shoulder instability has increased application, and a variety of techniques has been described to manage posterior glenohumeral instability related to posterior capsulolabral injury.

*Lee et al. evaluated the 3 heads of the deltoid as dynamic stabilizers of the glenohumeral joint.* **They reported** that the deltoid provided increased stability with the arm in the scapular plane and only decreased the stability of the shoulder with the arm in the coronal plane. **They concluded** that the middle and posterior heads of the deltoid should be strengthened in anterior shoulder instability in both conservative and operative treatment because they provide more stability, generate higher compressive force, and lower shear forces than the anterior head.

Figure A is the AP radiograph suggestive of a posterior shoulder dislocation. Figure B is a slice from an axillary CT scan demonstrating a posterior shoulder dislocation with the humeral head impaled on the glenoid. Illustration A depicts the "lightbulb sign"

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1-4: These are less common complications than recurrent instability following posterior shoulder stabilization. Stiffness is the most common complication after labral repair.
