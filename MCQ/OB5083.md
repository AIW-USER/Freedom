## Question

Which of the following can be expected in an ambulatory child who walks with a true equinus gait and has been affected by a static encephalopathy resulting in spasticity that mostly affects the lower extremities?:

## Options

1. Increased ankle dorsiflexion in stance and swing phases
2. Toe-walking with lack of heel strike at initial foot contact
3. A dorsiflexion moment throughout stance phase
4. Improved ankle dorsiflexion with the knee in extension compared with ankle dorsiflexion with the knee in flexion
5. Underestimation of ankle dorsiflexion in the child with pes planovalgus if the subtalar joint is not controlled during examination

## Correct option

**2. Toe-walking with lack of heel strike at initial foot contact**

## Answer explanation

A child with cerebral palsy (CP) and spastic diplegia may present with toe walking due to equinus contracture of the ankle or flexion contracture of the hip and knee with normal ankle positioning.

Normally, initial foot contact with the walking surface is with heel strike with the ankle in slight equinus, followed by progressive plantar flexion controlled by eccentric contraction of the tibialis anterior. Spasticity of the gastroc-soleus or weakness of the tibialis anterior causes initial toe contact in the child with CP.

*Renshaw et al. performed a systematic review of surgically treated equinus deformity in children with CP.* **They identified** 35 articles meeting inclusion criteria, 19 of which used instrumented gait analysis, with an average follow up of 2.8 years. **They identified** age at surgery and type of CP as factors affecting outcome. Younger age was associated with increased risk of recurrent equinus. The rate of postoperative calcaneus deformity was higher in spastic diplegia than in hemiplegia.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1. Spasticity of the gastroc-soleus complex in CP interferes with the normal dorsiflexion that occurs in stance phase as the tibia moves forward over the foot. Equinus contracture results in plantar flexion in swing and prevents initial heel contact.
Answer 3. Stretch on the gastroc-soleus triggers a spastic response resulting in a plantarflexion moment.
Answer 4. Ankle dorsiflexion will be worse with knee extension in the setting of a spastic gastroc-soleus, as the gastrocnemius crosses both the knee and ankle joints.
Answer 5. This would result in OVERestimation of ankle dorsiflexion.
