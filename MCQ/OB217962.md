## Question

A 27-year-old male presents to your office with complaints of anterior hip pain. He is as recreational hockey player and notices the pain more when he skates. He has an irritable hip examination suggesting intra-articular pathology. He has previously seen a different orthopedic surgeon who obtained an MRI and noted a labral tear. The patient was told he would likely need to undergo an arthroscopic labral repair. After hearing this, you review his MRI and order a complete set of hip radiographs. Which of the following would be a contraindication to performing only the aforementioned procedure on this patient?

## Options

1. Age
2. Alpha angle = 40°
3. Presence of acetabular chondrosis
4. Tonnis angle = 8°
5. Lateral center edge angle (LCEA) = 15°

## Correct option

**5. Lateral center edge angle (LCEA) = 15°**

## Answer explanation

A lateral center edge angle (LCEA) of 15° represents a radiographic indicator of hip dysplasia, which would not be best addressed by an arthroscopic hip procedure in isolation.

Hip arthroscopy is useful for addressing various intra- and extra-articular hip pathologies. Among these, the most commonly treated include cam deformities, labral tears, loose bodies, chondral defects, synovial diseases, gluteal tears, and hamstring pathology. This being said, a complete hip workup should be performed with regards to the patient's history, physical examination, and radiographic findings. One of the primary contraindications to hip arthroscopy is evidence of concomitant hip dysplasia. This can be identified on radiographs with an LCEA <20°, anterior center edge angle (ACEA) <20°, or a Tonnis angle >10°. If dysplasia is present, an arthroscopic labral repair will not adequately address the primary pathology and the patient will be at risk for for continued pain/repair failure. In these cases, a periacetabular osteotomy (PAO) in isolation or in combination with an arthroscopic labral repair would be best.

*Peters et al. evaluated the treatment of femoro-acetabular impingement (FAI) with surgical hip dislocation and debridement in young adults as an additional modality for managing labral/chondral pathology.* *In the 31 hips evaluated,* **they noted** a consistent increase in Harris hip scores from pre- to post-operatively (70 pts -> 87 pts). They did not record any cases of osteonecrosis, and noted that only patients with worse pre-operative chondral wear went on to develop arthritic changes.

*Tannast et al. retrospectively compared the radiographs of dysplastic and "over-covered (pincer type FAI)" hips to better define the reference values of each.* **They noted** that from dysplastic to over-covered hips, the LCEA, acetabular arc, and anteroposterior/craniocaudal coverage increased significantly. With their study, they were able to better define the radiographic differences between excessive and deficient coverage in order to use these values as predictors of surgical and clinical outcomes in the future.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: This patient's age (27) would not be a contraindication to performing an arthroscopic labral repair. Age younger than 40 is a positive prognostic indicator.
Answer 2: An alpha angle of 40° is normal and would indicate no evidence of femoral cam deformity. If cam deformity was present, an isolated labral repair without addressing the cam lesion would not be recommended.
Answer 3: Cartilage lesions of the acetabulum are commonly associated with labral tearing in the setting of impingement and can be addressed at the time of hip arthroscopy. Therefore, they would not represent a contraindication in this case.
Answer 4: The Tonnis angle measures the inclination of the weight bearing acetabulum and angles > 10° are indicative of hip dysplasia, which would represent a contraindication to arthroscopic labral repair in isolation.
