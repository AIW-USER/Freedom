## Question

A team of orthopedic residents is formulating new research ideas. They are organizing their proposed studies into those that would require Institutional Review Board (IRB) review and those that would not. Of the following studies, which would NOT require IRB review?

Study 1: A retrospective case-control study evaluating radiographic outcomes for patients with distal radius fractures treated operatively using a database with medical record numbers as the only patient identifier.

Study 2: An internal prospective study evaluating the effect on their hospital’s efficiency when one surgeon is given two operating theaters for a single operative day versus the same surgeon using only one operating theater on two consecutive operative days.

Study 3: A prospective study investigating the cost-benefit ratio with the use of a novel walking aid versus a traditional aid in pediatric patients with cerebral palsy.

Study 4: An internal retrospective study using their institutional database with name, date of birth, and medical record number of patients to evaluate causes of preoperative delays in order to improve efficiency.

## Options

1. Study 1 only
2. Studies 1 and 4
3. Study 2 only
4. Studies 2 and 4
5. Studies 3 and 4

## Correct option

**4. Studies 2 and 4**

## Answer explanation

Of the studies above, both Studies 2 and 4 are quality improvement studies that are using patient data with the goal of evaluating and/or improving processes and systems internally, within the organization, and only have internal implications. These studies would therefore NOT require IRB review.

The primary purpose of the IRB is to ensure the privacy and safety of human research subjects. In research protocols that involve human subjects, the IRB can employ one of three levels of review, which include: exempt, expedited, or full board review. The difference in the level of review is determined based on the risk level of the study. The IRB ultimately makes the final determination of the proper review category. Quality improvement assessments that involve data collection and analysis with the goal of improving specific processes and systems internally, within organizations, are NOT considered research and therefore do NOT require IRB review/approval. Note that in contrast to these quality improvement studies, studies that are considered exempt from review still require review by the IRB chair or designee to certify that they qualify for an exemption. In both Studies 2 and 4, the residents simply wish to perform quality improvement studies using a patient database with INTERNAL implications only. However, note that IRB review would be required if the residents sought publication of the data in a national journal or presentation at a national meeting and/or the results of the study would have application beyond the confines of the investigators’ local institution or practice.

*Slover et al.* *2013 reviewed the basic principles for conducting human research in orthopedic surgery.* *The authors acknowledged that most orthopedic surgeons do not have in-depth training in regulatory requirements and scientific research methods.* **They emphasized** that researchers need to understand the regulations for compliance and proper data management, including the requirements of the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act, informed consent, use of the IRB, and data protection guidelines.

*Parvizi et al.* *2007 reviewed the importance of the IRB process.* **They reported** that IRBs exist to protect human subjects involved in research from any potential harm. **The authors highlighted** that the Belmont Report provided the ethical principles (respect for persons, beneficence, and justice) that should be observed while conducting research on human subjects.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1 & 2: A patient’s medical record number is protected health information, and as such, Study 1 would require IRB review.
Answer 3: In addition to Study 2, Study 4 would also not require IRB review.
Answer 5: While there is likely negligible risk associated with the new walking aid, this study involves children with cerebral palsy, which would be considered a vulnerable population and therefore requires IRB review. Vulnerable populations include prisoners, pregnant women, fetuses, neonates, cognitively impaired persons, children, students, and employees.
