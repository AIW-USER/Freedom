## Question

A 40-year-old female presents to your office with complaints of left wrist pain that began approximately one month ago after she slipped and fell down the stairs. She is found to have a disruption of the deep fibers of the triangular fibrocartilage complex (TFCC). Injury to which of the following structures in Figure A would lead to dorsal subluxation of the ulna?

![[q219312-a-tfcc-no-label.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. A
2. B
3. C
4. D

## Correct option

**1. A**

## Answer explanation

Dorsal subluxation of the ulna may result following volar radioulnar ligament (A) injury of the TFCC.

The triangular fibrocartilage complex (TFCC) is a structure that supports the stability of the ulnar carpus and the distal radioulnar joint; it transmits axial forces from the hand to the forearm and also supports the stability of the radius with respect to the ulna as the wrist pronates and supinates. The TFCC anatomy consists of the dorsal and volar radioulnar ligaments, central articular disk, meniscus homolog, ulnar collateral ligament, extensor carpi ulnaris tendon subsheath, and the origin of ulnolunate and ulnotriquetral ligaments. TFCC injuries are a common cause of ulnar-sided wrist pain and may result from trauma or degenerative changes. A disruption of the deep fibers of the TFCC (the ligamentum subcruentum) results in distal radioulnar joint instability and subluxation of the ulna in respect to the radius. Commonly this results in dorsal subluxation of the ulna with respect to the radius secondary to volar radioulnar ligament injury. Diagnosis is made clinically with ulnar-sided wrist pain that is worse with ulnar deviation and a positive "fovea" sign. An MRI can help confirm the diagnosis. Treatment is generally conservative with NSAIDs and immobilization. Surgical debridement, TFCC repair, or ulnar shortening procedures may be indicated depending on the severity of symptoms and underlying cause.

*Wijffels et al. reviewed untreated distal radioulnar joint (DRUJ) injuries.* **They reported** that MRI has proven to be more sensitive and specific for TFCC tears, potentially causing DRUJ instability. **They concluded** that operative treatment of DRUJ injuries should consist of restoration of osseous and ligamentous anatomy.

*Adams et al. reviewed the anatomic origin and insertion of the palmar and dorsal radioulnar ligaments.* With reconstruction, **they reported** that recovery of strength and motion was at least 85%. **They concluded** that ligament reconstruction is effective for DRUJ instability but requires a competent sigmoid notch; however, it may not fully correct ulnocarpal instability.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: This is the triangular fibrocartilage proper. Injury to this structure leads to pain.
Answer 3: This is the ulnocarpal collateral ligament. Injury to this structure leads to pain.
Answer 4: This is the meniscal homologue. Injury to this structure leads to pain.
Answer 5: This is the dorsal radioulnar ligament. Injury to this structure would lead to volar subluxation of the ulna.
