## Question

Which of the following statements is a correct description of the normal alignment of the spine [SPINE-ANATOMY]

## Options

1. Apex of thoracic kyphosis is at T4
2. Majority of lumbar lordosis exists below L3
3. curve of less than 20 deg in the frontal plane is considered normal.
4. The normal range of thoracic kyphosis is 40 deg to 50 deg.

## Correct option

**2. Majority of lumbar lordosis exists below L3**

## Answer explanation

Ans: B

- A normal spine is straight in the frontal plane. More than 10 deg of curvature is considered as scoliosis.
- The amount of thoracic kyphosis and lumbar lordosis is variable with normal ranges of 20-50 degree and 20-60 degree respectively. These curves compensate for one another, so the sagittal vertical axis from center of c7 body normally falls within 2 cm of posterior superior corner of sacrum.
- The thoracic apex is usually located between T5 &T8.
- Most lumbar lordosis exists within the last two segments.

The spinal cord is shorter than the vertebral column and terminates as the conus medullaris (sacral segments of cord) behind the lower border of second lumbar vertebra in adults and the third lumbar vertebra in neonates. From the conus, a fibrous cord called the filum terminale extends to the dorsum of the first coccygeal segment.

Spinal nerves exit the canal at each level. Spinal nerves C2-7 exit above the pedicle for which they are named (the C6 nerve root exits the foramen between the C5 and C6 pedicles). The C8 nerve root exits the foramen between the C7 and T1 pedicles. All spinal nerves caudal to C8 exit the foramen below the pedicle for which they are named (the L4 nerve root exits the foramen between the L4 and L5 pedicles). Because the spinal cord is shorter than the vertebral column, the spinal nerves course more vertically as one moves caudally. The spinal nerves handing in the spinal canal distal to L1 vertebra, where spinal cord is over, resemble the tail of a horse and are hence called Cauda Equina.
