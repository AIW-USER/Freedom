## Question

A 26-year-old male presents to your office after injuring his finger while playing volleyball. Radiographs are obtained and demonstrate a fracture of the proximal phalanx of the index finger. He was initially seen in the ED where a reduction was performed, and the patient was placed into a splint. Which of the following would be an indication for surgical intervention compared with nonoperative management if present at this time?

## Options

1. Nondisplaced intraarticular fracture
2. Extra-articular fracture with 5 degrees of dorsal angulation
3. Extra-articular fracture with 5 degrees of volar angulation
4. Extra-articular fracture with 1mm of shortening
5. Extra-articular fracture with 5 degrees of rotational deformity

## Correct option

**5. Extra-articular fracture with 5 degrees of rotational deformity**

## Answer explanation

This patient sustained a fracture of the proximal phalanx. Any rotational deformity of the proximal phalanx should be considered for surgical intervention by either CRPP or ORIF

Phalanx fractures are common hand injuries that involve the proximal, middle or distal phalanx. Diagnosis can be confirmed with orthogonal radiographs of the involved digit. Treatment involves immobilization or surgical fixation depending on location, severity and alignment of injury. Nonoperative management with buddy taping or splinting is indicated in extraarticular fractures with < 10° angulation or < 2mm shortening and no rotational deformity, and in non-displaced intraarticular fractures. Operative management with CRPP or ORIF is indicated in extraarticular fractures with > 10° angulation or > 2mm shortening or rotational deformity, displaced intraarticular fractures, unstable or irreducible fracture pattern (e.g., spiral, oblique, fracture with severe comminution).

*Kawamura et al. reviewed surgical management for phalangeal and metacarpal fractures.* **They reported** that lag screw fixation may be the best choice for open fixation of long oblique phalangeal and metacarpal fractures. **They noted** that for short oblique fractures, plating or tension band wiring is recommended. **They concluded** that overall, successful outcomes of treating phalangeal and metacarpal fractures require a clear appreciation of fracture anatomy and pattern.

*Pun et al. performed a prospective study of 52 fresh traumatic unstable fractures of the proximal or middle phalanges of the hand.* **They reported** that all the fractures were fixed with A.O. miniature screws and plates. **They concluded** that when compared with a comparable group of fractures fixed with Kirschner wires, there was no significant improvement in the results.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Displaced intraarticular fractures should be considered for surgical intervention
Answers 2 & 3: Extraarticular fractures with >10° angulation should be considered for surgical intervention
Answer 4: Extraarticular fractures with > 2mm of shortening should be considered for surgical intervention
