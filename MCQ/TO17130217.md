## Question

22-year-old woman has a relatively low-energy distal radius fracture. She takes no medications and is in good health, but her vitamin D levels are very low. Vitamin D metabolite and sclerostin levels are assessed before and after 3 months of vitamin D replacement therapy to determine if therapy will improve her long-term bone health. There is expected elevation in

## Options

1. 25-hydroxycholecalciferol and sclerostin levels but not 1,25-dihydroxycholecalciferol levels.
2. 1,25-dihydroxycholecalciferol level with a lower level of sclerostin but no effect on 25-hydroxycholecalciferol level.
3. 1,25-dihydroxycholecalciferol and 25-hydroxycholecalciferol levels with a lower level of sclerostin.
4. 1,25-dihydroxycholecalciferol and 25-hydroxycholecalciferol levels with a higher level of sclerostin.

## Correct option

**3. 1,25-dihydroxycholecalciferol and 25-hydroxycholecalciferol levels with a lower level of sclerostin.**

## Answer explanation

Sclerostin is a protein that is elaborated by osteocytes. It downregulates osteoblast function. This is an important feedback mechanism for normal bone growth and development; however, higher levels of sclerostin will suppress osteoblast activity. Because osteocytes have receptors for 1,25-dihydrixycholecalciferol, they will respond by decreasing sclerostin synthesis, resulting in increased bone formation. In a healthy individual without renal disease, oral vitamin D supplementation will bring about increased levels of both 1,25-dihydroxycholicalciferol and 25-hydroxycholicalciferol.

Answer: 3
