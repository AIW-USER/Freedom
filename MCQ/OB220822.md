## Answer explanation

Patients with spondylolysis can present with lower back pain, hamstring tightness, pain with lumbar extension while standing on one leg, and negative plain radiographs. MRIs have comparable sensitivity and specificity to SPECT and will demonstrate a fracture through the pars interarticularis in patients with this condition (Answer A).

Pediatric spondylolysis is a condition commonly seen in adolescent athletes, particularly those participating in contact sports or sports requiring repetitive hyperextension. It is characterized by a fracture of the pars interarticularis, which can progress to varying grades of spondylolisthesis. Patients typically present with activity-associated lower back pain, buttock pain, and hamstring tightness. Lumbar extension in a single-leg stance may provoke symptoms on physical exam. Diagnosis can be made via plain radiographs, but abnormalities may not always be appreciated. MRI may subsequently be used for evaluation and is a preferred imaging modality to SPECT due to its limited use of radiation and comparable sensitivity and specificity. Non-operative management (physical therapy, activity restriction) is first-line treatment for symptomatic patients, including those with low-grade spondylolisthesis. Surgical management is considered in patients who have progressive symptoms, neurologic deficits, high-grade spondylolisthesis, or low-grade spondylolisthesis that has failed non-operative management.

*Choi et al. retrospectively reviewed 201 adolescent patients with lumbar spondylolysis to describe the outcomes of a non-operative treatment regimen consisting of activity cessation, TLSO bracing, and bone stimulator utilization for 3 months.* **They observed** a 98% rate of return to prior levels of activity with this regimen and a 50% rate of bony healing on follow-up CT scans, with those adherent to bone stimulators showing greater rates of bone healing. **They concluded** that conservative treatment in adolescent patients with this condition is associated with excellent outcomes.

*Wu et al. published a comprehensive review of pediatric overuse injuries, which included a discussion of lumbar spine spondylolysis.* *They describe a preadolescent/adolescent male preponderance of this injury, particularly in those involved with sports requiring hyperextension/truncal rotation movements.* **They emphasized** the favorable utility of non-operative management and the utilization of surgical intervention in settings where non-operative management has failed. **They concluded** that overuse injuries are becoming more frequent in the setting of increased year-round sports participation, and thus warrant the development of injury prevention programs.

*Kobayashi et al. assessed the utility of MRI in early diagnosis of spondylolysis by reviewing 200 young athletes who presented with lower back pain.* **They found** that 45.5% of patients with negative plain radiograph findings had MRI findings consistent with spondylolysis. **The authors concluded** that MRI is an effective imaging modality to assess for suspected or early spondylolysis when plain radiographs are negative, especially as many physical exam tests are not beneficial for early diagnosis.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Discitis is typically treated with prolonged antibiotic therapy, not solely with physical therapy and activity restriction
Answer 3: Intramedullary spinal tumors typically present with weakness and sensory changes in addition to pain. These findings are not present in this patient. Patients with this condition would also not be treated solely with physical therapy and activity restriction.
Answer 4: Herniated discs frequently present with radiculopathic signs or symptoms, findings not present in this patient. Additionally, pain with lumbar extension while in a single-leg stance is more specific for spondylolysis and the degree of resultant spinal canal stenosis present in Figure D would likely warrant intervention beyond rest, activity restriction, and physical therapy.
Answer 5: Spondyloptosis is treated with surgical intervention, not physical therapy and activity restriction
