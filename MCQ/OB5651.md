## Question

A 45-year-old woman presents with a 2 month history of worsening right knee pain and difficulty rising from a chair. She has no history of malignancy. Physical examination, including breast examination, is normal. Complete blood cell count, erythrocyte sedimentation rate, liver enzymes are within normal limits. ## Options

1. Consultation with medical oncology and radiation oncology
2. Resection and reconstruction with an endoprosthesis
3. Resection and reconstruction with a cemented intramedullary nail
4. Resection and reconstruction with a cemented intramedullary nail and supplemental locking plate
5. CT scans of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis followed by biopsy

## Correct option

**5. CT scans of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis followed by biopsy**

## Answer explanation

This patient has bony metastases of unknown origin. The etiology has to be determined with CT scans and a biopsy prior to definitive treatment.

The workup of bony metastases should start with history/physical examination (focused on thyroid, breast and prostate), laboratory studies (serum/urine electrophoresis, PSA, liver enzymes) as clinically indicated, and imaging (bone scan, chest radiograph, CT scans). If the tests are undiagnostic, a biopsy of the most accessible lesion is indicated.

*Rougraff reviewed metastases to bone.* *He advised that investigations should NOT start with a biopsy before other tests because: (1) An ill-planned biopsy of a sarcoma may compromise limb salvage, (2) another lesion may be identified that may be easier/safer to sample, (3) renal cell metastases are vascular and should be embolized prior to biopsy, (4) biopsy is unnecessary in multiple myeloma, (5) histology alone will identify the primary site in only a small number of patients.*

Figures A through C are AP and lateral plain radiographs and CT of the distal femur showing a large metastasis with destruction of the anterior cortex and thinning of the posterior cortex. Illustrations A through C show resection of the distal femur and reconstruction with an endoprosthesis.

Incorrect Answers
Answer 1: Medical/radiation oncology can only begin treatment following a definitive diagnosis.
Answers 2, 3, 4: Resection and reconstruction based on presumptive metastases without a definitive biopsy-proven diagnosis is inappropriate. The lesion is uncontained (anterior cortical breach, posterior thinning) - a cemented intramedullary device would be inappropriate.
