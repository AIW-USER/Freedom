## Question

A 40-year-old male presents to the emergency department after being involved in an motor vehicle collision. He is found to have sustained an open fracture of the left distal femur with severe bone loss and a resultant nine centimeter defect. Which of the following best describes the initial stage of an induced-membrane technique?

## Options

1. Debridement and temporizing fixation
2. Debridementand cement spacer
3. Debridement, cement spacer and temporizing fixation
4. Debridement, bone grafting and temporizing fixation
5. Debridement, bone grafting and definitive fixation

## Correct option

**3. Debridement, cement spacer and temporizing fixation**

## Answer explanation

Masquelet technique is a 2-stage technique in which the 1st stage consists of a debridement, placement of a cement spacer, and temporizing fixation.

Patients with critical bone loss pose a unique challenge to the treating orthopedic surgeon. Patients with critical bone defects may be candidates for a Masquelet technique ("induced-membrane" technique). This is a 2-stage technique. The 1st stage consists of an I&D, cement spacer placement, and temporizing fixation. The 2nd stage consists of placement of bone graft into an "induced membrane" and definitive fixation. Studies have shown that the optimal time frame for bone grafting is 4-6 weeks after placement of cement spacer.

*Nauth et al. reviewed bone defects and their management is challenging.* **They reported** that these injuries have a profound clinical and economic impact, and outcomes are limited by high rates of complication and reoperation and poor functional outcomes. **They concluded** that a focus on defining critical-size bone defects, using the induced membrane technique, biologics' role, and managing infected bone defects are important to the outcome.

*Giannoudis et al. prospectively reviewed the treatment of bone defects managed with the induced membrane (IM) technique.* **They reported** that the induced membrane technique appears to be an alternative option for the management of large bone defects secondary to acute bone loss or infected non-unions. **They concluded** that the technique should be considered in the surgeon's armamentarium as it is effective and is associated with a low rate of complications.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1&2: The 1st stage consists of an I&D, cement spacer placement, and temporizing fixation.
Answers 4&5: The 2nd stage consists of the placement of bone graft into an "induced membrane" and definitive fixation.
