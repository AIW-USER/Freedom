## Question

A 16-year-old sexually active male presents with severe right hip pain of several days duration. He denies any recent trauma. He complains of difficulty weight-bearing on the right hip at this time. Upon further questioning, he denies alcohol, smoking, or drug use. On physical examination, his leg is flexed and externally rotated. A maculopapular rash is noted on his trunk. Radiographs are normal. Hip aspiration is performed. Which of the following is true of the most likely diagnosis?

## Options

1. It is caused by a Gram-negative coccobacillus
2. Always requires urgent irrigation and debridement
3. Synovial fluid cultures are overwhelmingly negative
4. Should be treated with a dose of IV ketorolac
5. Is usually associated with arthralgias of multiple joints

## Correct option

**5. Is usually associated with arthralgias of multiple joints**

## Answer explanation

This patient has septic arthritis of his hip secondary to Neisseria gonorrhoeae, which is usually associated with a maculopapular rash and migratory polyarthralgias.

Gonococcal septic arthritis is a common cause of joint infection in young, sexually active adults. Teenagers are less likely to be forthcoming about their sexual history so providers must be suspicious. It affects females more than males at a ratio of 3:1. The difference may be because women usually have a more asymptomatic clinical picture and present more often with untreated genitourinary tract infections. There is a characteristic triad of clinical components: migratory polyarthralgia, dermatologic lesions usually presenting as a maculopapular rash, and tenosynovitis often affecting multiple joints simultaneously (particularly wrists, fingers, ankles, and toes). While most joint infections due to other microorganisms require urgent irrigation and debridement, there are reports of Neisseria gonorrhoeae-derived septic arthritis being treated with large doses of penicillin alone and no surgical debridement.

*Rice et al. performed a review of gonococcal arthritis.* **They report** that septic arthritis caused by N. gonorrhoeae may be monoarticular or pauciarticular and is more commonly associated with positive synovial fluid cultures and negative blood cultures. **They recommend** initial treatment with antibiotics including ceftriaxone, penicillin, or other cephalosporin until signs of symptoms have improved.

*Garcia-De La Torre et al. performed a review of gonococcal and nongonococcal arthritis.* **They report** that exposure to sexual encounters should raise suspicion of this type of arthritis and even though a positive Gram stain of synovial fluid is not always present, simultaneous cultures of the cervix, urethra, and rectum can be obtained to assist with diagnosis.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Neisseria gonorrhoeae is an intracellular Gram-negative diplococci. A Gram-negative coccobacillus describes Haemophilus influenzae.
Answer 2: Neisseria gonorrhoeae infections can sometimes be treated with large doses of antibiotics, rather than formal surgical debridement.
Answer 3: Cultures can be negative in cases of Neisseria gonorrhoeae, but this only occurs in about 50% of cases.
Answer 5: Cases of transient synovitis of the hip can be treated with IV ketorolac before initiating antibiotics.
