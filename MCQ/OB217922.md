## Question

A 21-year-old collegiate male lacrosse player presents to your office complaining of bilateral leg pain that is worse with activity. He notes that the pain is located vaguely over the anterolateral aspect of the leg and he describes it as achy. He notes that it is correlated with exercise, as it tends to worsen with longer periods of activity and resolves with rest. Radiographs of his leg are normal. His examination in the office is also normal, without bony tenderness or any neurovascular compromise, although he does complain of vague distal paresthesias with activity. He has been attempting to modify his activities for several months without improvement in his symptoms. Therefore, you recommend surgical management of his suspected condition with a single anterolateral incision. What structure is most at risk during surgical management?

## Options

1. Sural nerve
2. Superficial peroneal nerve
3. Saphenous nerve
4. Peroneal artery
5. Posterior tibial artery

## Correct option

**2. Superficial peroneal nerve**

## Answer explanation

This patient has exertional compartment syndrome (ECS) and the superficial peroneal nerve (SPN) would be most at risk during release of the anterior and lateral compartments.

ECS is an exercise-induced process where reversible muscular ischemia occurs in one or multiple leg compartments during and immediately post-exercise. The anterior and lateral compartments are most commonly affected. Patients usually complain of aching and burning sensations in the leg with exercise and they often have distal paresthesias depending on the compartment affected. Compartment pressure monitoring should be performed to establish a formal diagnosis. Diagnostic criteria include:
-Resting pressure >15 mmHg
-Immediate post-exercise pressure >30 mmHg
-Post-exercise pressure >20 mmHg at 5 minutes
-Post-exercise pressure >15 mmHg at 15 minutes

One or two incision fasciotomy is indicated for patients who don't improve after nonoperative management. Fasciotomy can also be performed endoscopically. The SPN is the neurovascular structure most at risk as it crosses the surgical field superficially near the fascial border between the anterior and lateral compartments.

*Beck et al. reviewed the surgical management of chronic ECS in 155 patients <18 years old.* **They noted** that 85% of patients had bilateral symptoms, and 48% of patients had only the anterior and/or lateral compartments released. ~80% returned to sports, while ECS recurred in ~19% of these patients, with a 3.4x greater risk of recurrence in those who had only selective compartments released.

*Wittstein et al. evaluated the use of endoscopic compartment release for CES in 9 patients.* **They noted** that 8/9 patients returned to their same level of activities and there were no neurovascular injuries noted. There were no recurrences in this population and authors concluded that endoscopic compartment release was a safe and effective way of treating CES.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The sural nerve is located more posterolaterally, and has a lower risk for injury compared to the SPN.
Answer 3: The saphenous nerve has a low risk of injury with anterior and lateral compartment release given its medial location.
Answer 4: Peroneal artery injury would be uncommon during anterior and lateral compartment releases given its deep course within the leg.
Answer 5: Posterior tibial artery injury would be unlikely as it runs posteromedially about the leg/ankle.
