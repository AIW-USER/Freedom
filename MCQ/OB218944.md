## Question

## Answer explanation

Blood flow restriction therapy (BFR) has been shown to increase strength and muscle volume following ACL reconstruction.

BFR utilizes a tourniquet to disrupt venous return of blood. This then lowers the oxygen delivered to muscle tissues and mimics an anaerobic environment. This allows the patient to increase their strength while exercising with lower resistance. Studies have shown that it may increase quadriceps cross-sectional area, but it has not yet been clearly shown to improve outcomes or shorten recovery time after ACL reconstruction. Therapists must be trained how to perform BFR safely and effectively.

*DePhillipo et al. reviewed the indications and safety considerations of BFR.* **They note** that it decreases oxygen delivery to muscle tissues, which mimics an anaerobic environment. This allows patients to exercise with lower resistance but produce similar muscle hypertrophy to those seen with higher resistance exercises.

*Lu et al. performed a systematic review investigating the role of BFR following ACL reconstruction.* **They identified** 6 relevant studies. **They concluded** that there are potential strength and muscle hypertrophy advantages to BFR. But also caution that the literature is sparse and has not yet been associated with improved clinical outcomes.
