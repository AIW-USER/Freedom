## Question

A 6-month old female presents to the office for follow up evaluation. Your attending mentions that the patient suffers from the most common obstetric brachial plexus palsy and has not had any improvement in function. How would you expect the patient's arm to be positioned?

## Options

1. Shoulder external rotation and elbow extension
2. Shoulder abduction with elbow extension
3. Shoulder internal rotation and forearm pronation
4. Elbow flexion wrist extension
5. Elbow extension forearm supination

## Correct option

**3. Shoulder internal rotation and forearm pronation**

## Answer explanation

Erb's palsy is the most common vaginal brachial plexus palsy. This results in shoulder adduction and internal rotation with elbow extension, forearm pronation and wrist flexion.

Erb's palsy affects 1:1000 live births making it the most common vaginal brachial plexus palsy. Fortunately, even though it's the most common, it also has the best prognosis. Known as an "upper" lesion, it primarily affects the C5-6 nerve roots. The resultant deformity consists of adduction and internal rotation of the shoulder, a pronated forearm, and the inability to flex the elbow. This culmination of findings results from C5 deficiency affecting the axillary nerve (deltoid, teres minor), suprascapular nerve (supraspinatus, infraspinatus), musculocutaneous nerve (biceps and brachialis) and C6 deficiency affecting the radial nerve (brachioradialis and supinator).

*Abzug et al review brachial plexus birth palsies.* **They report** the best way to diagnose these injuries is with serial physical exams. **They conclude** that specifically shoulder rotation should be noted as this is needed for the normal development of the glenohumeral joint.

*Hale et al review current concepts in the management of brachial plexus birth palsy.* **They report** that physiotherapy, microsurgical nerve reconstruction, secondary joint corrections, and muscle transpositions can all be employed to help children maximize function in the affected upper extremity. **They conclude** that there is currently a place for both microsurgery and shoulder reconstruction.
