## Question

Which of the following is true regarding Hoffa fractures in patients who sustain a distal femur fracture?

## Options

1. Most likely to occur after a ground-level fall
2. Most likely to involve the medial condyle
3. Occurs in approximately 20% of distal femur fractures
4. Most commonly seen on anteroposterior (AP) radiograph of the knee
5. More common in open fractures than closed fractures

## Correct option

**5. More common in open fractures than closed fractures**

## Answer explanation

The Hoffa fracture is a coronal fracture of the distal femur involving one or both of the femoral condyles. Patients with an open distal femur fracture are nearly three times more likely to sustain a concomitant Hoffa fracture than those with closed injuries.

Isolated Hoffa fractures are rare, occurring in <1% of femur fractures. However, coronal plane fractures through one or both of the femoral condyles occurs in nearly 40% of supracondylar and intercondylar distal femur fractures. The lateral femoral condyle is involved in approximately 80% of Hoffa fractures. Computed tomography (CT) scan of the injured knee can aid in the diagnosis of Hoffa fracture as nearly 30% of these injuries are missed on pre-operative radiographs. Once anatomic reduction of these fragments is achieved, fixation is with an anterior-to-posterior lag screw for interfragmentary compression.

*Patel and Tejwani performed a literature review of the Hoffa fracture.* **They found** Hoffa fractures occurred most commonly in high-energy injuries and were best evaluated with CT scan. **They conclude** operative management is the treatment of choice in most patients and describe multiple surgical approaches for fixation of these injuries.

*Arastu et al. reviewed the current concepts and management of Hoffa fractures.* **They propose** the mechanism of injury is most likely due to a vertical shear force on the posterior condyle throughout various degrees of knee flexion. **They conclude** nonoperative management is associated with poor outcomes and anatomic reduction with rigid fixation is preferred.

*Nork et al. evaluated the association of Hoffa fractures in patients with supracondylar and intercondylar distal femur fractures.* **They found** that coronal plane fractures occurred in 38% of distal femur fractures, were 2.8 times more likely in open fractures, and occured in the lateral condyle most commonly. **They conclude** CT scan should be performed in patients with distal femur fractures, especially if an open wound is present.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Hoffa fractures are more common in high-energy mechanisms of injury, not ground-level falls
Answer 2: Hoffa fractures involve the lateral condyle in approximately 80% of cases
Answer 3: Hoffa fractures occur in approximately 40% of distal femur fractures
Answer 4: Hoffa fractures are most commonly seen on lateral knee radiographs or CT scan of the knee
