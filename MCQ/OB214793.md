## Question

A 21-year-old male sustains a right traumatic hip dislocation that is urgently reduced in the trauma bay by the on-call orthopedic resident. Post reduction computed tomography (CT) of the pelvis is demonstrated in Figure A. What is the most appropriate treatment option?

## Options

1. Rapid sequence magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) of the right hip to rule out an occult femoral neck fracture
2. Placement of a knee immobilizer and delayed open reduction and internal fixation
3. Placement of a distal femoral traction pin and open reduction internal fixation (ORIF) as soon as possible
4. Delayed ORIF
5. Nonsurgical management and restricted weight bearing

## Correct option

**3. Placement of a distal femoral traction pin and open reduction internal fixation (ORIF) as soon as possible**

## Answer explanation

The patient has sustained a posterior wall fracture that is now incarcerated in the hip joint following a closed reduction of traumatic hip dislocation. The patient should be placed in distal femoral skeletal traction and taken for open reduction and internal fixation as soon as reasonably possible.

Traumatic hip dislocations can result in posterior wall fractures and femoral head fractures. Fracture fragment incarceration can occur upon a closed reduction maneuver which necessitates either urgent surgical intervention or placement of skeletal traction with open reduction and internal fixation as soon as reasonably possible. Traction decreases pressure injury to the articular surface and decreases the risk for late post-traumatic arthritis.

*Epstein et al. reviewed the treatment of posterior hip dislocations with associated femoral head fractures.* **The authors recommended** early hip reduction with post-reduction radiographs including both hips to ascertain whether there is any incongruity of the reduced femoral head. Further, the authors urged the use of seat belts as many of these injuries could have been prevented in motor vehicle collisions.

*Anglen et al. reported on a retrospective series of 12 patients that were treated with a trochanteric osteotomy for incarcerated posterior wall fractures following traumatic hip dislocations.* **Their study revealed** the patients treated with trochanteric osteotomy had longer operations, more blood loss, and trended towards a higher incidence of posttraumatic arthritis compared to similar patients not requiring an osteotomy. Overall, there were no identified adverse effects associated with the osteotomies.

Figures A and B are axial and sagittal CT images of the pelvis with a large incarcerated fragment in the right hip joint.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: A rapid-sequence MRI is a newly described modality for detecting occult femoral neck fractures in the setting of femoral shaft fractures. It has not been described in the setting of hip dislocations or acetabular fractures.
Answers 2 and 4: Delayed ORIF of the posterior wall fracture is an option, but the patient must be placed in skeletal traction to offload the articular cartilage and prevent post-traumatic arthritis.
Answer 5: There is no role for nonoperative treatment of an incarcerated fragment in a posterior hip dislocation.
