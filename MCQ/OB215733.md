## Answer explanation

A Segond fracture is an avulsion fracture of the anterolateral ligament (ALL) off the lateral aspect of the proximal tibia and is pathognomonic for an anterior cruciate ligament (ACL) tear. ACL tears can be associated with a characteristic bone bruising pattern of the middle third of the lateral femoral condyle and the posterior third of the lateral tibial plateau, as seen in Figure F. Not all ACL tears will show this bone bruise pattern, but when it is present, it is pathognomonic for an ACL tear.

ACL tears are one of the most common ligamentous knee injuries and are often associated with a non-contact pivoting injury or a lateral blow to the knee. Patients often report a history of a popping sensation and immediate swelling due to a large hemarthrosis. Initial radiographs are often normal, but the presence of a Segond fracture should alert the provider to the high probability of an ACL tear. Segond fractures are associated with ACL tears in 75-100% of cases. MRI is the diagnostic study of choice for ACL tears and discontinuity or absence of fibers on sagittal and coronal views ("empty notch" sign) are diagnostic. Another predictable MRI finding is the bone bruising pattern seen in Figure F. At the time of injury the tibia translates anteriorly relative to the femur resulting in an impaction injury of the middle third of the lateral femoral condyle and the posterolateral tibia. The ensuing bone contusion is best seen on a fat-suppressed T2-weighted MRI.

*Goldman et al. reviewed the knee arthrogram of 9 patients with Segond fractures.* **They found** the ACL was torn or absent on the arthrogram in all cases. Surgical correlation was then performed for 6 patients and **they noted** that the ACL was torn in 5 patients and it was incompletely visualized in one. **The authors concluded** that Segond fractures were uniformly associated with ACL tears.

*Sanders et al. describe a systematic approach to review an MRI of the knee.* *They review the normal appearance of each anatomical structure, as well as the optimal plane and image sequence to view each structure.* *The authors then review the associated MRI findings for various injury patterns.*

Figure A is an AP radiograph of a knee demonstrating a Segond fracture. Figure B is a T1 sagittal MRI showing a PCL tear. Figure C is a T1 sagittal MRI demonstrating a patella tendon tear. Figure D is a fat-suppressed coronal MRI demonstrating a mid-substance LCL tear. Figure E is a T2 coronal MRI showing an MCL tear. Figure F is a T2 sagittal MRI demonstrating bone contusion of the middle third of the lateral femoral condyle and posterolateral tibia.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: PCL injuries are not associated with Segond fractures
Answer 2: Patella tendon ruptures are not associated with Segond fractures
Answer 3: While LCL tears can be associated with ACL injuries, Segond fractures are more specifically related to ACL tears
Answer 4: Segond fractures are not directly associated with MCL tears
