## Options

1. Increased loss of reduction
2. Increased failure rates
3. Decreased symptomatic anterior knee pain
4. Improved biomechanical stability
5. Decreased infection rates

## Correct option

**4. Improved biomechanical stability**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a transverse patella fracture that was treated with open reduction and internal fixation utilizing a plate and screw construct. This construct has demonstrated improved biomechanical stability compared with other methods of patella fracture fixation.

Patella fractures occur most commonly following direct trauma to the anterior aspect of the knee or secondary to an eccentric contraction injury from the quadriceps tendon. Transverse fractures are a common fracture pattern. These injuries may be treated in a variety of ways including lag screw fixation alone, cerclage wiring, plate and screw fixation, and Kirschner wire fixation plus tension band wiring. Recent biomechanical testing has demonstrated superior biomechanical stability with ORIF utilizing a plate and screw construct for patella fractures.

*Wagner et al. performed a biomechanical comparison of plate fixation and screw fixation with cerclages under dynamic loading conditions.* **They found** less fracture displacement and loosening following locking plate fixation after 100 cycles. **They concluded** that anterior locking plate osteosynthesis of comminuted patellar fractures biomechanically provides better primary stability compared to cannulated screws with anterior tension band wiring.

*Thelen et al. utilized simulated cyclic loading to evaluate whether fixed-angle plating is superior to modified anterior tension wiring or cannulated lag screws with anterior tension wiring.* **They found** that anterior tension wiring as well as lag screws with tension wiring showed significant fracture displacement, whereas fixed-angle plates showed no significant fracture gap widening after completion of 100 cycles. **They concluded** that in contrast to modified anterior tension wiring and cannulated lag screws with anterior tension wiring, the fixed-angle plate was the only fixation device to stabilize transverse patella fractures securely and sustainably.

Figures A and B are AP and lateral radiographs of a knee demonstrating a transverse patella fracture. Figures C and D are the AP and lateral radiographs of a patella fracture treated with plate and screws. Illustration A is a schematic of several different patella fracture fixation methods.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Plate and screw fixation has not been shown to increase the risk of loss of reduction
Answer 2: Plate and screw fixation has not been shown to increase failure rates
Answer 3: Plate and screw fixation has not been shown to decrease symptomatic anterior knee pain
Answer 5: Plate and screw fixation has not been shown to have lower infection rates compared with other methods of fixation
