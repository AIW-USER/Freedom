Chapter: Sports Medicine MCQs — Drugs And Dopping In Sports

## Question

A 50 year old male comes to your clinic for advise on participation in half marathon race that will be held 8 months later. He says that he has been active and used to run marathons and play masters badminton until 3 years ago he had angina after which he was diagnosed with hypertension which is being treated with beta blocker. He has been doing mild exercises since the start of his medication. What would be the next step in the management.

## Options

1. Full cardiac assessment and treadmill stress test
2. Tell him that since he had an angina he will not be cleared to run a half marathon
3. Change his antihypertensive medication and then do full cardiac assessment
4. Apply for a TUE for the use of beta blocker

## Correct option

**3. Change his antihypertensive medication and then do full cardiac assessment**

## Answer explanation

Ans: C

EXPLANATION: -

Beta Blockers :

They are competitive antagonists that block receptor sites for adrenaline and noradrenalin on beta adrenergic receptors.

• B1 adrenergic receptors – present in the heart and kidney
• B2 adrenergic receptors – present in the lungs, GI tract, vascular smooth muscle and skeletal muscle
• B3 adrenergic receptors – fat cells

Beta blockers decrease the activity of the heart and reduce the oxygen demand. It decreases heart rate and cardiac output with no effect on the stroke volume.
Intake of beta blockers is associated with increase in perceived exertion levels, impairment of cardiac output and maximum oxygen uptake, earlier fatigue and lactate threshold, possible exacerbation of exercise-induced bronchospasm or asthma. Therefore, they are avoided in patients with asthma and endurance athletes.

A full cardiac assessment would be required as a part of pre participation evaluation considering his age and history of angina in the past. However doing a treadmill stress test would not be the best thing to do for an elderly athlete on beta blocker since it can significantly impair exercise performance by lowering the heart rate, potentially leading to fatigue and reduced endurance.

Changing his medication to drugs like ACE inhibitors, ARBs or Calcium channel blockers followed by blood pressure monitoring and full cardiac assessment can be done since these drugs do not affect training.

A therapeutic use exemption (TUE) need not be filed for Beta Blockers, since they are banned only in particular sports and since the athlete is not competing in tournaments / competitions that he would be tested by an Anti-doping agency.

Doping regulations for Beta Blockers:
• Beta blockers are banned in particular sports completely (in and out of competition): Archery, Shooting and Underwater sports.
• Beta blockers are banned in particular sports (in competition only): Biliards, Golf, Darts, Automobile sports
