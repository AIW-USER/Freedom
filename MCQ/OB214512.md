## Question

A 5-year-old boy presents to the ER with 2 weeks of progressive back pain. His mother states that the boy has been febrile over the past 3 days. His labs reveal a WBC of 8K, ESR of 30, CRP of 4 and blood cultures with 2/2 bottles growing gram-positive cocci. Radiographs are shown in Figure A. A lumbar MRI with contrast does not reveal any fluid collection at that level. Which of the following is the next best step in management?

*Figure A*

## Options

1. Discharge home with oral antibiotics
2. Admission to hospital for bed rest and IV antibiotics
3. Admission to hospital for IV antibiotics and surgery
4. Admission to hospital for bed rest and cast immobilization
5. Admission to hospital for IV antibiotics, surgery and cast immobilization

## Correct option

**2. Admission to hospital for bed rest and IV antibiotics**

## Answer explanation

The patient is bacteremic with L3-L4 spondylodiscitis. He should be admitted to the hospital and treated with IV antibiotics and rest. Given the absence of a fluid collection, surgery should be held off until the patient has been treated with a trial of IV antibiotics.

Disk space infections in the pediatric population are most common amongst males and children ages 5 or younger. The lumbar spine is the most commonly affected location (50-60%) and staph aureus is responsible for over 80% of cases. In the absence of a paraspinal abscess, non-operative management has excellent results. Administration of IV antibiotics speeds resolution and minimizes recurrence. Bed rest and cast immobilization alone have been successfully used to treat this disorder but can be associated with prolonged recovery and frequent recurrence, even when oral antibiotics are administered. The ideal combination for the initial treatment of pediatric spondylodiscitis is IV antibiotics, bed rest and immobilization.

*Ring et al. reviewed the outcomes of 47 patients, ranging in age from 7 months to 15 years, with presumed pyogenic infectious spondylodiscitis with regard to duration and recurrence of symptoms, and the need for readmission, retreatment, or surgical intervention.* **They noted** among patients treated with or without spinal immobilization, IV antibiotics led to the quickest resolution of symptoms. **The authors conclude** that IV antibiotics are more likely to lead to rapid relief of symptoms and signs without recurrence than spine immobilization alone.

*Crawford et al. reviewed the course of 36 patients with spondylodiscitis with 29 months of follow-up.* **The authors noted** that 82% of patients showed intervertebral disk space narrowing. Treatment consisted of bed rest for all patients, plaster casts for 50%, antibiotics for 40%, and traction for 23%. **They noted** that at the completion of the study, all patients were asymptomatic including three children who had recurrences. **The authors concluded** that the administration of antibiotics appears to be appropriate and that disk space aspiration or biopsy should be reserved for those cases that are refractory to immobilization and antibiotics.
