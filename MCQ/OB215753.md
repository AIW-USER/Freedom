## Question

A 57-year-old male presents for evaluation of a left thigh mass that was noticed four months ago after pulling his groin. He undergoes appropriate workup for an unknown soft tissue mass, which includes MRI imaging and biopsy. He is later diagnosed with undifferentiated pleomorphic sarcoma (UPS). What characteristic findings on MRI would be consistent with UPS?

## Options

1. Homogenous mass that is isointense with fat on both T1- and T2-weighted sequences
2. Mass is isointense with surrounding muscle on T1-weighted sequence, heterogenous high signal intensity on T2-weighted sequence
3. Well-circumscribed mass with low signal intensity on T1-weighted sequence and homogenous high signal intensity on T2-weighted sequence
4. Intramuscular mass with high signal intensity and peripheral edema most pronounced at the poles on T2-weighted sequence, contrast-enhanced T1-weighted fat-saturated sequence demonstrating thin septal and peripheral enhancement
5. Poorly-circumscribed mass with heterogenous high signal intensity on T1-weighted sequence and homogenous isointensity on T2-weighted sequences

## Correct option

**2. Mass is isointense with surrounding muscle on T1-weighted sequence, heterogenous high signal intensity on T2-weighted sequence**

## Answer explanation

As with other soft tissue sarcomas, UPS demonstrates low signal intensity (i.e., isointensity with surrounding muscle) on T1-weighted and high signal intensity on T2-weighted MRI imaging. Histologic evaluation demonstrates high cellularity, heterogenous, pleomorphic cells with a high mitotic index, and occasional bizarre multinucleated giant cells.

Undifferentiated pleomorphic sarcoma, previously known as malignant fibrous histiocytoma (MFH), is considered the most common type of soft tissue sarcoma. It has aggressive biological behavior and a poor prognosis. Malignant fibrous histiocytoma (MFH) was removed from the 2013 World Health Organization Classification of Tumors of Soft Tissue and Bone. Typically, UPS occurs in adults (range 32-80; mean 59 years) with a slight male predilection (M: F ratio of 1.2:1). The presentation is usually a painless, enlarging, palpable mass. While risk factors for developing UPS are not well defined, it is the most frequent soft tissue sarcoma due to radiotherapy. It may also be seen in patients with a history of Paget’s disease. While UPS most commonly arises from soft tissue, it may occasionally arise in or from bone (1-5%). Although UPS can occur almost anywhere in the body, they have a predilection for lower and upper extremities corresponding to ~50% and ~20% of the cases, respectively. While the origin of UPS remains unknown, histology demonstrates histiocyte-like cells with significant cellular pleomorphism, storiform architecture (i.e., disorganized), and occasional bizarre multinucleated giant cells.

*Chen et al. evaluated clinicopathological variables and treatment strategies of 100 UPS patients who underwent surgical resections to identify predictors of poor outcomes.* **They found** that tumor presentation, size, and important structures involved were independent prognostic factors associated with overall survival. **They concluded** that the primary treatment of UPS should be conducted by experts in large sarcoma centers and that a wide surgical margin provides sufficient control of the disease recurrence.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: An isointense mass with fat on all imaging sequences is consistent with either a lipoma or low-grade liposarcoma.
Answer 3: This is consistent with a myxoid-type lesion. UPS has heterogeneous high signal intensity on T2-weighted imaging.
Answer 4: This is consistent with a myxoma, a benign mesenchymal neoplasm without malignant potential, and is most frequently found in 40 to 70-year-old patients, with a slight female predilection. It is composed of bland, undifferentiated stellate cells with thin collagen fibers within a myxoid stroma. MRI will often show a circumferential rind of fatty tissue adjacent to at least one of the poles of the mass that will demonstrate peripheral edema most pronounced at the poles on T2.
Answer 5: UPS, as with other soft tissue sarcomas, demonstrates isointensity (i.e., is dark) with surrounding muscle on T1-weighted sequences and has high signal intensity (i.e., is bright) on T2-weighted sequences.
