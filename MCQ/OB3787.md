## Question

What is the most commonly affected level and gender predominance for the pathology depicted in Figure A?

![[q3787-a-ct-pars-defect-right-9.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. L4-L5, female
2. L4-L5, male
3. L5-S1, female
4. L5-S1, male
5. L5-S1, no gender predominance

## Correct option

**4. L5-S1, male**

## Answer explanation

The image shows a defect in the pars interarticularis. This finding is more common in males and the vast majority of the time involves the L5-S1 level.

The pars is an anatomic region of the posterior spinal elements at the junction of the superior and inferior articular processes with the lamina and pedicle. A defect in this area constitutes a spondylolysis. This is present in 4-6% of the population in North America.

*Jones et al. present a review on adult isthmic spondylolisthesis, including epidemiology, clinical presentation, imaging, and treatment.* **They note** that in the classic study of Frederickson et al., 90% of the lytic defects were at the L5-S1 level and two-thirds of the defects were in males, whether children or adults.

*Beutler et al. followed 500 elementary school students prospectively with radiographs, identifying lumbar spondylolysis in 4.6% of children at age 6 years.* By adulthood, the prevalence of this finding had increased to 6%. These patients were then followed for 45 years. No slippage was found in patients with unilateral defects and there was no association with slip progression and back pain.

Figure A shows a sagittal CT image of a lumbar pars defect.

Incorrect answers:
Answers 1 and 2: The most common level involved in pars defect is L5-S1.
Answers 3 and 5: The finding is more common in males.
