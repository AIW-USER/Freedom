## Question

A 7-year-old female presents with her mother due to concerns of rapidly progressive "toe-walking". On physical exam, there is atrophy of the left lower extremity and her left foot is 15 degrees less than neutral when passively dorsiflexed in both knee extension and 90 degrees of knee flexion. She is also found to have several skin lesions demonstrated in Figures A and B. Of the following, what is the most likely corresponding diagnosis?

![[q2214-b-leg-lesions.jpg]]

## Options

1. Eczema
2. Arthrogryposis
3. Juvenile idiopathic arthritis (JIA)
4. Scleroderma
5. Child abuse

## Correct option

**4. Scleroderma**

## Answer explanation

This child's skin findings, limb atrophy, and Achilles contracture is consistent with scleroderma, which can be rapidly progressive in pediatric patients.

Scleroderma is an autoimmune connective tissue disease that can be focal or systemic. It is a rare disease and orthopaedists may have the first opportunity to diagnose these patients who may initially present with joint contractures. Orthopaedic manifestations may include limb atrophy, joint contractures, limb-length discrepancy, osteolysis (especially associated with the calcaneus), and angular malalignment. Early surgical intervention, combined with immunosuppressive therapy, is critical to preserving function in these patients.

*Bottoni et al. present a case series of 17 pediatric patients with scleroderma.* Their goal was to determine the natural course of the disease, orthopaedic manifestations, and role of surgical intervention. **They note** the characteristic asymmetric cutaneous lesions and the role of early, individualized treatment; they emphasize the importance of physical therapy and surgical release of joint contractures.

Figure A shows a linear lesion of scleroderma involving the forehead. Figure B shows multiple hypopigmented lesions of the left lower extremity and global atrophy.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Eczema is not typically associated with joint contractures or limb atrophy.
Answer 2: Arthrogryposis is not progressive and not associated with skin lesions.
Answer 3: The skin lesions and exam are not characteristic of JIA.
Answer 5: While these skin lesions may appear to be caused by abuse (linear lesions may resemble a belt or weapon and sclerotic pigmented skin can resemble burns), joint contractures and other features point to a different diagnosis.
