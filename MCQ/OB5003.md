## Question

All of the following statements about preoperative evaluation of patients with scoliosis and cerebral palsy are true EXCEPT:

## Options

1. Patients should be weighed or otherwise evaluated for nutritional status
2. Pulmonary status should be assessed either with formal pulmonary function testing, sleep study, or history, clinical exam and radiography, (a separate chest radiograph may not be needed if there is a full scoliosis film)
3. Preoperative MRI of the spinal axis is routinely performed
4. Some anti-seizure medications may interfere with coagulation
5. Evaluation of swallowing ability and gastroesophageal reflux status should be considered preoperatively to assess the risk for aspiration pneumonia (however if the history indicates that this is clearly not a problem, formal evaluation is not needed)

## Correct option

**3. Preoperative MRI of the spinal axis is routinely performed**

## Answer explanation

MRI of the spine is not routinely performed preoperatively in patients with cerebral palsy (CP) and scoliosis.

Pulmonary, nutrition, gastrointestinal, and neurologic evaluations are key components of a multi-disciplinary approach to the preoperative evaluation of cerebral palsy patients undergoing scoliosis surgery. The true incidence of intra-spinal lesions in CP patients is unknown but believed to be uncommon. MRI may still be indicated based on other factors such as change in neurologic exam or rapid curve progression, but it is not routinely performed.

*McCarthy et al. review the etiology, natural history, assessment and treatment of patients with scoliosis and cerebral palsy.* *They stress the importance of a multidisciplinary approach to these patients as they are at higher risk of complications.* These include (aspiration) pneumonia, gastrointestinal dysmotility, implant failure, neurologic complications (uncommon), and wound infection. It is noted that as patients whose weight is less than the 5th percentile may be at increased risk for such complications.

Incorrect answers:
Answers 1, 2 and 5: These are all elements of a multidisciplinary approach to the preoperative evaluation of a CP patient indicated for scoliosis surgery.
Answer 4: Valproic acid may interfere with coagulation (proposed mechanism: decrease in von Willebrand factor). Platelet and coagulation tests are performed at initiation of treatment, periodically during treatment, and before any planned surgery. However there are no clinical data to suggest increased intra-operative blood loss in scoliosis surgery attributable to valproic acid.
