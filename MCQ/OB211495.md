## Question

His radiographs are shown in Figures A and B. He undergoes surgical correction for the deformity noted. On his first post-operative visit, he complains of paresthesias and numbness. ## Options

1. Intermediate dorsal cutaneous nerve
2. Medial dorsal cutaneous nerve
3. Sural nerve
4. Saphenous nerve
5. Deep peroneal nerve

## Correct option

**2. Medial dorsal cutaneous nerve**

## Answer explanation

The patient has a severe hallux valgus (HV) deformity with an incongruent metatarsophalangeal joint (MTPJ). Surgical correction of the deformity is most likely to injure the medial dorsal cutaneous nerve (a branch of the superficial peroneal nerve) during the surgical approach.

HV deformities can be corrected with a wide variety of procedures. Complications following HV surgery can be common and include recurrence, avascular necrosis, overcorrection (hallux varus), cock-up toe deformity, transfer metatarsalgia, and neuropraxia. The most commonly injured nerve during HV surgery is the medial dorsal branch of the superficial peroneal nerve which runs over the dorsal medial forefoot and supplies sensation to the dorsum of the foot and medial dorsal hallux. The progressive valgus and pronation deformity of the hallux places the medial dorsal cutaneous nerve directly over the medial eminence and will be directly encountered during the surgical approach.

*Perera et al. discuss the pathogenesis of HV.* **They note** that there is a genetic predisposition for a nonlinear osseous alignment or a laxity of the static stabilizers of the big toe that disrupts the muscle balance of the toe. **They note** that in any patient, a number of factors have to come together to cause HV. **The authors conclude** that if an adequate understanding of the pathogenesis of HV is understood, a more scientific approach to the management of HV is possible and this allows for appropriate surgical treatment in order to minimize complications.

*Makwana et al. performed a cadaver study to investigate the anatomical relationships of the medial dorsal cutaneous nerve during the surgical approach to the big toe.* **The authors noted** an easily identifiable vein that runs transversely superficial and proximal to the nerve. Subsequently, in a prospective clinical study of 171 operations on the great toe, **they confirmed** this anatomical relationship in 142 procedures (83%) and as a result, they noted no complaints of numbness or nerve pain at follow-up. **The authors concluded** that the careful identification of the 'sentinel' vein and the subjacent sensory nerve can lead to a decreased risk of neuropraxia following hallux valgus surgery.

Figures A and B are radiographs revealing a severe hallux valgus deformity with an incongruent MTPJ. Illustration A depicts the relevant neuromuscular anatomy for hallux valgus approaches.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1, 3, 4 and 5: The medial dorsal cutaneous nerve is the most commonly injured nerve during HV surgery given its course and proximity to several approaches to the big toe.
