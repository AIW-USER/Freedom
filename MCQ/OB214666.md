## Options

1. A broken screw results in better outcomes than a loosened screw
2. A loosened screw results in better outcomes than a broken screw
3. Removing a broken screw results in better outcomes than leaving a broken screw
4. An intact screw may result in better outcomes than a broken screw
5. An intact screw may result in a worse outcome than a loosened screw

## Correct option

**5. An intact screw may result in a worse outcome than a loosened screw**

## Answer explanation

In a patient who sustained a syndesmotic injury, an intact screw may be associated with worse functional outcomes when compared to a broken, loose, or removed screw.

Stabilization of the syndesmosis with screws allows for the healing of the ligamentous structures torn during an injury. Maintenance of intact syndesmotic screws can restrict motion by preventing physiologic external rotation, as well as proximal and posterior translation of the fibula relative to the tibia. Intact screw removal or the screws breaking once the ligamentous structures have healed can theoretically allow for the restoration of normal syndesmosis motion. Indications for intact screw removal include painful screw prominence with decreased dorsiflexion (<10 degrees) well outside the perioperative period (on average 9 months).

*Manjoo et al. conducted a retrospective review at a level 1 trauma center analyzing the outcomes of 106 patients that underwent syndesmotic fixation.* **They found** functional outcomes to be superior in patients that had fractured, loosened, or removed syndesmotic screws compared to intact screws. **They concluded** that patients with an intact syndesmosis screw may benefit from screw removal while those with loose or broken screws are unlikely to benefit from screw removal.

*Lash et al. retrospectively reviewed functional outcomes in 141 patients that sustained ankle fractures and* **concluded** that the majority of patients still have some functional disability at 2 years. Weber A fracture patterns had the best functional outcomes, while Weber C patterns had the worst.

*Tucker et al. performed a retrospective review of 63 patients that underwent syndesmotic screw fixation during ankle fracture ORIF.* *The 20 out of 63 patients that retained their screws had statistically significant higher functional outcome scores and decreased pain compared to patients that underwent routine screw removal.* **The authors suggest** that routine syndesmotic screw removal in asymptomatic patients may be unnecessary and even detrimental.

Figure A shows a syndesmotic injury. Figure B shows two syndesmotic screws in place following syndesmotic reduction.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Broken and loosened syndesmotic screws have similar outcomes.
Answer 2: Broken and loosened syndesmotic screws have similar outcomes.
Answer 3: Removing broken screws does not appear to improve functional outcomes and may put the patient at undue risk.
Answer 4: An intact syndesmotic screw may lead to worse outcomes and if symptomatic may benefit from removal.
