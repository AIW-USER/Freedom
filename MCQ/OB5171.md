## Question

Which of the following is true regarding the Ponseti method of treating idiopathic talipes equinovarus?:

## Options

1. Equinus is the first component of the deformity to be corrected
2. Correction of the severe varus deformity of the hindfoot is accomplished with pronation of the whole foot into valgus
3. Short leg cast immobilization is utilized
4. The plane of the metatarsal heads should be turned into supination with the first cast
5. The need for a percutaneous Achilles tenotomy can be expected in 20% of cases

## Correct option

**4. The plane of the metatarsal heads should be turned into supination with the first cast**

## Answer explanation

Forcible pronation of the clubfoot increases cavus deformity and causes a break in the mid-foot, failing to control the hindfoot. It therefore leaves the hindfoot varus uncorrected. Supination is the initial forefoot manipulation, aligning the metatarsals in the same plane.

The Ponseti casting method for the treatment of congenital clubfoot corrects deformity by addressing cavus, forefoot adductus, hindfoot varus and lastly equinus ("C.A.V.E."). Cavus is corrected by apparent supination, which actually brings the forefoot into alignment with the hindfoot. Once the metatarsals, cuneiforms and navicular are in the same plane, they can be used as a lever to laterally displace the navicular on the talus.

Ponseti reviews the principles of treatment of clubfoot. In his experience, achilles tenotomy is required in 70% of patients, and is performed when 15 degrees of dorsiflexion is not attained with casting. Combined with Achilles tenotomy, Ponseti reports 85-90% success with manipulative casting of clubfeet.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: Equinus is the last deformity to be corrected. The heel must be in valgus prior to this correction.
Answer 2: Attempts to correct varus by forefoot pronation will exacerbate cavus and lose control of the hindfoot.
Answer 3: toe-to-groin casts are the mainstay of Ponseti casting
Answer 5: Achilles tenotomy is utilized in 70% of cases.
