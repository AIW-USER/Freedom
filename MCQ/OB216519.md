## Question

A 22-month-old female presented to your office for spinal deformity noted by her pediatrician. Imaging is unremarkable for any evidence of failure of formation or segmentation, syrinx, tumor, or a tethered cord. She is diagnosed with left thoracic infantile idiopathic scoliosis. Which of the following would be an indication for definitive treatment with serial Mehta casting (derotational) or thoracolumbosacral orthosis (TLSO)?

## Options

1. Cobb angle of 18 degrees
2. Cobb angle of 28 degrees
3. Rib Vertebra Angle Difference (RVAD) of 26 degrees
4. Rib Vertebra Angle Difference (RVAD) of 16 degrees
5. Rib Vertebra Angle Difference (RVAD) of 5 degrees

## Correct option

**3. Rib Vertebra Angle Difference (RVAD) of 26 degrees**

## Answer explanation

In this patient with progressive infantile idiopathic scoliosis with no tether, cyst, or syrinx on MRI and RVAD >20, Mehta casting (derotational) or thoracolumbosacral orthosis (TLSO) use is the next best step in treatment.

Infantile Idiopathic Scoliosis is a coronal plane spinal deformity which most commonly presents in children ages 3 years or less. Diagnosis is made with full-length PA and lateral spine radiographs. MRI studies are indicated to rule out syrinx, tumor, or tethered cord. Treatment can be observation, bracing, or surgical management depending on the skeletal maturity of the patient, magnitude of deformity, and curve progression. Patients with phase 2 ribs, RVAD >20, Cobb angle >20-30 degrees, and flexible curves should be treated with Mehta casting (derotational) or thoracolumbosacral orthosis (TLSO) use. Those with Cobb angles >50-60 degrees and who have failed derotational casting should be considered for growing rod constructs as close to skeletal maturity as possible.

*Sanders et al. reviewed 55 patients with progressive infantile idiopathic scoliosis for 1 year following casting.* **They reported** that 49 of the 55 patients responded to casting with improved alignment and space available for the lung. **They concluded** that serial casting for infantile scoliosis results in full correction if done in patients with curves less than 60 degrees and under 20 months of age.

*Tis et al. reviewed the available treatment options for early onset scoliosis (EOS).* **They reported** that those patients in whom no surgical correction has been attempted with curve progression of 10-20 degrees or an RVAD >20 and phase 2 ribs did well with derotational serial casting. **They concluded** that larger magnitude curves and older aged patients were at risk for failure of casting and subsequent surgical intervention.

*Waldron et al. reviewed 20 patients with early-onset scoliosis treated with serial Risser casting.* **They reported** that a mean major curve decrease in those patients treated with Risser casting. **They concluded** that serial Risser casting is safe and effective and can stabilize children with large curves until they are at a suitable age for other treatment options such as growing rods.

Incorrect answers:
Answers 1&2: A Cobb angle <30 may often be observed as most resolve spontaneously. A Cobb angle >50-60 degrees meets surgical indications for use of a growing rod construct (dual rod or VEPTR). This should be delayed until as close to skeletal maturity as possible as fusion before age 10 years results in pulmonary compromise
Answers 4&5: A Rib Vertebra Angle Difference (RVAD) of <20 degrees may often be observed as most resolve spontaneously
