## Question

An 8-year-old boy sustained a fall while skateboarding. He only complains of right wrist pain. Focused examination reveals pain with range of motion and tenderness in the anatomic snuffbox. Wrist radiographs reveal a scaphoid fracture. Pediatric scaphoid fractures most commonly present with what morphology?

## Options

1. Distal one third fractures
2. Middle one third fractures
3. Proximal one third fractures
4. Unicortical fractures
5. Buckle fractures

## Correct option

**2. Middle one third fractures**

## Answer explanation

Pediatric patients present most commonly with middle one-third scaphoid fractures.

Scaphoid fractures commonly occur when a patient falls onto an outstretched hand. They present with tenderness at the anatomic snuffbox and volarly over the scaphoid tubercle. Historically in pediatric patients, 59-94% will fracture in the distal one-third of the scaphoid due to the ossification of the distal pole before the proximal pole. However more recent literature has found that scaphoid waist fractures are the most common. This change is thought to be due to the increased weight of children today. Treatment for most pediatric scaphoid fractures is with 4 to 6 weeks of immobilization for nondisplaced and open reduction and internal fixation for displaced fractures.

*Gholson et al. retrospectively reviewed 351 scaphoid fractures in patients 18 years or less.* *The average age was 14.6 years old.* **They found** 71% of patients fractured at the scaphoid waist, 23% at the distal pole, and 6% at the proximal pole. Union was 90% with cast treatment alone. Male sex, high-energy mechanism, closed physes, and high body mass index was associated with waist fractures. Union rate with surgery was 96.5% combining both acute and chronic fractures.

Stanciu and Dumont present a case series of 21 patients aged 13.9 years on average who sustained scaphoid fractures. Follow up was a minimum of 1 year. They found scaphoid waist fractures to occur most often with 12 of 21 patients (57%)occurring in this location. All patients were treated in a cast and healed without surgery. All patients at the final follow up had painless full range of motion without radiographic evidence of arthritis.

Incorrect:
Answers 1, 3-5: Pediatric scaphoid fractures often occur in the middle one third. Unicortical fractures can occur in up to 21% of pediatric scaphoid fractures, this is not the most common morphology.
