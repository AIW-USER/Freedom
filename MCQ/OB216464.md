## Question

A 62-year-old man with a BMI of 36 is scheduled for a total knee arthroplasty. In his pre-operative office visit, he asks questions about different tibial components. Which of the following is true of the tibial component shown in Figure B as compared to the component in Figure A?

![[q216464-a-fig-a-new.jpg]]
*Figure A*

![[q216464-b-fig-b.jpg]]
*Figure B*

## Options

1. Less expensive
2. Higher likelihood of getting infected
3. Greater instability because of its monobloc nature
4. Has worse functional outcomes at 10-years
5. Should only be considered in patients with a BMI <32

## Correct option

**1. Less expensive**

## Answer explanation

Figure B shows an all-polyethylene tibia (APT) component, which on average is $470-$1650 less expensive than metal-backed tibia (MBT) designs.

All-polyethylene tibial (APT) components have been shown to have many benefits when compared to metal back designs. The most stark, and least debated, is their cost, which is significantly less expense than comparable metal backed designs. Traditional teaching has been that the two components have similar long term outcomes. However, recent studies have demonstrated improved survivorship in addition to significantly lower rates of infection, instability, tibial component loosening, and periprosthetic fracture.

*Voigt et al. performed a systematic review comparing APT and MBT.* **They found** that the former was cheaper. There was no difference in adverse events, durability (need for revision or radiographic failure) at 2, 10, and 15 years, and functional status at 2, 8, and 10 years.

*Toman et al. compared APT and MBT retrospectively.* **They found** that APT implants perform as well as MBT implants in patients with BMI <37.5. There were 4 tibial implant failures in the MBT group in patients BMI >40.

*Dalury et al. examined APT performance in obese patients (125 knees) after a minimum of 7 years.* There were no implant failures. There were 5 nonprogressive tibial radiolucencies and 1 case of nonprogressive osteolysis.

*Houdek et al reviewed long term outcomes in infection rates in patients with APT components.* **They report** significantly improved implant survival, reduced rates of postoperative infection, fracture, and tibial component loosening. **They conclude** that APT designs should be considered in all patients regardless of age or BMI.

Figure A shows a cemented metal-backed tibial component. Figure B shows a cemented all-polyethylene tibial component.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: APT have been shown to have a lower rate of infection compared to MBT.
Answer 3: Instability (>0.2 mm migration) was reduced by 48% with an APT (compared with MBT) but this was not significant (p = 0.05, Voight study).
Answer 4: Functional outcomes have been shown to at least be equivalent at 2, 8 and 10 years.
Answer 5: APT have demonstrated at least equivalent outcomes in patients with BMI <37.5.
