## Options

1. Greater than 10% of patients will develop a symptomatic neuroma after digital amputation
2. Patients who undergo neurorrhaphy for digital nerve injuries have significantly more symptomatic neuromas than patients with digital amputations
3. Less than 10% of patients will develop a symptomatic neuroma after digital amputation that will likely improve spontaneously
4. Less than 10% of patients will develop a symptomatic neuroma after digital amputation and are likely to undergo revision surgery after 1 year
5. Less than 10% of patients will develop a symptomatic neuroma with digital amputation and those that do will likely need further operative intervention before 1 year

## Correct option

**5. Less than 10% of patients will develop a symptomatic neuroma with digital amputation and those that do will likely need further operative intervention before 1 year**

## Answer explanation

After digital amputation, less than 10% of patients will develop symptomatic neuromas. Over half of these patients will require revision surgery for neuroma within a year of their digital amputation (Answer 5).

In traumatic digital nerve injuries, the proximal nerve stump attempts to regenerate toward its distal target. If this process becomes disorganized or incomplete, this will result in the formation of a neuroma, defined as a potentially painful bulbous growth of nerve tissue. In isolated digital nerve injuries or digital replants, neurorrhaphy can be performed. Neurorrhaphy allows for the surgical reapproximation of the proximal and distal ends of the digital nerve, directing and organizing the axonal sprouting. In the setting of digital amputations where neurotmesis has occurred and neurorrhaphy cannot be performed, the established rates of development of a symptomatic neuroma lie between 6.6 and 7.8%. Symptoms typically include sharp or burning pain, cold intolerance, and hypersensitivity to direct pressure. These symptoms ultimately lead to more than half of these digital amputation patients requiring revision surgery for neuroma within one year of their index procedure.

*Van Der Avoort et al. completed a study including 583 individuals with peripheral nerve injuries; 177 had digital amputations.* The incidence of digital nerve injury without amputation and with neurorrhaphy was 1%. However, symptomatic neuromas developed in 7.8% of cases involving digital amputation. **They concluded** that patients with digital amputations experienced more symptomatic neuromas compared to those undergoing neurorrhaphy.

*Vlot et al. conducted a review of 1,083 patients who experienced traumatic digital amputations.* **They found** that 71 patients (6.6%) developed symptomatic neuromas, with an average diagnosis time of 6.4 months. Among these patients, 66% underwent surgery for their painful neuromas, typically around 11 months after the initial injury. The study identified index finger and avulsion injuries as significantly associated with a higher risk of developing symptomatic neuromas.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Less than 10% of patients develop a symptomatic neuroma after traumatic digital amputation.
Answer 2: The rate of symptomatic neuromas is higher in patients with digital amputations than in patients who undergo neurorrhaphy for digital nerve injuries without amputation.
Answers 3 and 4: In patients who develop a symptomatic neuroma after digital amputation, more than half of these patients will undergo revision surgery within one year.
