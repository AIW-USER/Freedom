## Question

You are consulted to evaluate a newborn on the floor with limited range of motion in his right arm (shown in Figure A). The mother denies any past medical history and had an uncomplicated pregnancy without gestational diabetes. She gave birth to the 4.475kg (9.9lb) child through a vaginal delivery requiring the use of McRoberts maneuver. The child appears well otherwise and had appropriate APGAR scores. In general, which of the following serves as the greatest risk for the development of this condition?

![[q218558-a-jojo-palsy.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Caucasian race
2. Fetal macrosomia
3. Gestational diabetes
4. Non-breech birth
5. Shoulder dystocia

## Correct option

**5. Shoulder dystocia**

## Answer explanation

This newborn has the classic “waiter’s tip” deformity seen in upper brachial plexus injuries. Of all the risk factors for brachial plexus birth injury (BPBI), shoulder dystocia remains the greatest risk factor across numerous institutional and population-based studies.

BPBI is a relatively rare injury seen in newborns, which most commonly results secondary to traction neuropraxia from shoulder dystocia. Many of these patients have somewhat complicated deliveries requiring assistance through forceps/vacuum assistance or the use of McRoberts maneuver. Upper trunk injuries are more common than lower trunk or pan-plexus injuries and, thankfully, the vast majority of patients will recover spontaneously without the need for surgical intervention. If symptoms persist beyond 3 months of life without appreciably improving functional outcomes, diagnostic and surgical interventions are often pursued.

*Lalka and colleagues performed a retrospective case-control analysis comparing babies born in Colorado with BPBI to those born without injury.* **They found** that shoulder dystocia was the highest risk factor followed by instrumented forceps birth, breech delivery, and gestational diabetes. **They conclude** that the overall rate of BPBI is gradually decreasing but there is a continued need to seek preventative measures.

*Vakhshori and colleagues utilized the Kid’s Inpatient Database to compare babies born with BPBI to those born without injury.* **They found** similar results with shoulder dystocia, fetal macrosomia and gestational diabetes to be the highest risk factors for the development of BPBI. Additional risk factors included breech delivery and forceps or vacuum-assisted delivery.

*Wells and colleagues performed a systematic review detailing the changes in surgical intervention for patients with persistent BPBI over the past three decades.* *The authors reviewed the differing operative modalities and showed a significant increase in the number of nerve transfer procedures performed over time.* **They concluded** that although neuroma excision with sural nerve autografting has been the historic gold-standard treatment for BPBI, peripheral nerve transfers are becoming increasingly utilized for surgical management.

Figure A shows a newborn infant with right-sided upper trunk BPBI known as Erb palsy. This is characterized by the functionally weak or nonfunctional shoulder and biceps musculature. This patient’s symptoms resolved with the restoration of biceps function by 1 month after birth.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1, 2, 3: Caucasian race, fetal macrosomia, and gestational diabetes are all considered risk factors for BPBI. However, they are not as great of risk factors as shoulder dystocia.
Answer 4: Breech birth is considered a risk factor for BPBI, however similarly is not as great a risk factor as shoulder dystocia.
