## Question

A 28-year-old male presents to the ER with the injury shown in Figure A after a firework blew up in his hand. The patient ultimately elects to undergo a wrist disarticulation. When counseling the patient on the outcomes of limb salvage versus amputation for upper extremity injuries, which of the following statements is most true?

![[q219132-a-img-5321.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Amputation results in significantly improved psychological outcomes
2. Blast injury is a broadly accepted contraindication to attempted limb salvage
3. Functional outcomes are significantly improved with limb salvage
4. The Mangled Extremity Severity Score (MESS) accurately predicts outcomes after upper extremity amputation
5. Wrist disarticulation provides a longer residual lever arm with improved pronosupination compared to transradial amputation

## Correct option

**5. Wrist disarticulation provides a longer residual lever arm with improved pronosupination compared to transradial amputation**

## Answer explanation

The only true statement in the answer choices is that for upper extremity amputations, wrist disarticulation provides a longer residual lever arm with improved pronosupination. In the available literature, no statistically significant differences have been observed for functional or psychological outcomes in civilian patients between those who underwent limb salvage and those who underwent amputation for upper extremity injuries, and there are no hard-and-fast or broadly accepted indications for either.

Indications for upper-extremity amputation are varied, complex, and multifactorial across studies. Key factors to consider can be categorized into three subgroups: global patient factors, limb-specific factors, and mechanism of injury factors. The main global patient factor is the presence of uncontrollable hemodynamic instability. Limb-specific factors include extensive and concurrent soft tissue, bone, vascular and/or nerve injuries, and prolonged limb ischemia. Mechanisms of injury factors include blunt arterial trauma and crush injuries. Amputation is generally undertaken where there are elements from at least two of these subgroups present, however, this is again at the discretion of the treating physician in concordance with the patient's wishes.

Mitchell, et al. published The Military Extremity Trauma Amputation/Limb Salvage (METALS) Study, which studied the outcomes of amputation compared with limb salvage following major upper-extremity trauma. The authors found no significant differences in outcomes were observed between patients who underwent limb salvage and those who underwent amputation.

Kumar, et al. published on the reliability of the MESS scoring system to justify amputation versus limb salvage for upper extremity trauma. The authors found that the MESI score was more reliable than the MESS score, and that upper limb and lower limb mangled injuries cannot be considered the same because of their anatomy. They concluded that salvage should be the prime realistic aim given that the upper limb has more rich vascularity and efficient collaterals than the lower limb, as well as smaller muscle mass, making ischemia time relatively more.

Fochtmann, et al. published on the outcomes and relevance of the MESS score in third-degree open fractures and traumatic sub-/total amputations of the upper extremity. The authors found that definite limb salvage could be achieved in the mangled upper extremity regardless of MESS, making the MESS score an unreliable predictor.

Figure A demonstrates a mangled hand after a blast injury that was deemed unsalvageable.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1 and 3: No statistically significant differences have been observed for functional or psychological outcomes in civilian patients between those who underwent limb salvage and those who underwent amputation for upper extremity injuries.
Answer 2: There are no broadly accepted indications for either treatment and limb salvage can be attempted with subsequent replantation/reconstructive endeavors at the discretion of the treating physician in accordance with the patient's wishes.
Answer 4: The Mangled Extremity Severity Score (MESS) has been shown to be a poor predictor of outcomes after upper extremity amputation.
