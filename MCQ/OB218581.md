## Question

A 55-year-old female presents to your office with pain and deformity of her great toe. On examination, hallux valgus deformity is present with HVA = 25º, and IMA = 11º measured on radiographs. She also has notable osteophyte formation at her 1st MTP joint. You elect to perform a 1st MTP arthrodesis to correct her hallux valgus deformity and address the joint's arthritic changes. Upon postoperative follow-up, you note the arthrodesis is positioned in neutral rotation, 5º of plantarflexion relative to the floor, and 5º of valgus. Given this postoperative finding, which of the following is the most likely complication?

## Options

1. Progression of hallux valgus deformity
2. Pseudoarthrosis
3. Transfer metatarsalgia
4. First TMT instability
5. Cock up toe deformity

## Correct option

**3. Transfer metatarsalgia**

## Answer explanation

Arthrodesis of the 1st MTP joint should be positioned in 5-10º of valgus, 5-15º of dorsiflexion relative to the floor and neutral rotation. If fused in a suboptimal position, transfer metatarsalgia can occur.

Hallux valgus deformity is quite common, especially in middle-aged females. Treatment options vary based on the degree and location of the deformity. First MTP arthrodesis is indicated in hallux valgus cases with moderate to severe deformity, severe concomitant osteoarthritis involving the 1st MTP, or in patients with inflammatory or connective tissue disorders. Proper positioning of the 1st MTP arthrodesis is important in maintaining a plantigrade, painless foot postoperatively. In cases where the arthrodesis is malpositioned, abnormal compensatory loading of the adjacent metatarsals may lead to transfer metatarsalgia.

*Kelikian reviewed technical considerations in hallux MTP arthrodesis,* **noting** that the procedure remains the gold standard for painful arthritis of the first MTP. The author reviews the different approaches and techniques that can be used to achieve successful hallux MTP fusion. The author also discusses different complications that can occur based on the clinical scenario in which the MTP arthrodesis is performed.

*Rajczy et al. reviewed first MTP joint arthrodesis,* **noting** its versatility for hallux MTP arthritis as well as other pathologies centered on the MTP joint. They review techniques for fusion as well as appropriate positioning of fusion to provide reasonable gait. Overall, **they recommend** first MTP arthrodesis as a useful procedure for restoring the function of the hallux.

*Ho et al. review 1st MTP arthritis/hallux rigidus,* **noting** that about 2.5% of patients >50 years old have degenerative changes of the 1st MTP. They review nonoperative management including shoe modifications and orthotics and go on to discuss surgical management with cheilectomy or arthrodesis. Additionally, they discuss the patient outcomes with these different methods of treatment.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Provided the arthrodesis heals appropriately, the risk for progressive hallux valgus deformity centered on the 1st MTP joint should be minimal, especially given the mild deformity noted radiographically.
Answer 2: Nonunion after 1st MTP arthrodesis is uncommon and would not be more likely due to malpositioning of the arthrodesis.
Answer 4: First TMT instability would not be likely to occur secondary to an incorrectly positioned 1st MTP arthrodesis.
Answer 5: Cock up toe deformity is more common in cases of FHL injury or after Keller MTP resection of the lesser toes and would be unlikely in this case, particularly with the 1st MTP joint incorrectly fused in plantarflexion.
