Chapter: Trauma MCQs — Chronic Regional Pain Syndrome (CRPS)

## Question

A patient with focal CRPS in the knee following an ACL reconstruction has failed conservative management and sympathetic blocks. Which neuromodulation technique is currently considered superior for focal limb CRPS in the lower extremity?

## Options

1. Traditional Tonic Spinal Cord Stimulation (SCS)
2. Intrathecal Morphine Pump
3. Dorsal Root Ganglion (DRG) Stimulation
4. Deep Brain Stimulation (DBS)

## Correct option

**3. Dorsal Root Ganglion (DRG) Stimulation**

## Answer explanation

When conservative management fails, we move to Neuromodulation. While Spinal Cord Stimulation (SCS) has been the "traditional" choice, Dorsal Root Ganglion (DRG) Stimulation
has revolutionized treatment for orthopedic-related focal pain.

 The Anatomical Advantage

 - Traditional SCS: The leads are placed in the epidural space over the dorsal columns. The electrical current must travel through the Cerebrospinal Fluid (CSF). This often results in "paresthesia" (tingling) that migrates when the patient changes position. It is difficult to target a specific spot, like just the knee or just the hallux.

 - DRG Stimulation: The leads are placed directly over the Dorsal Root Ganglion at a specific spinal level (e.g., L3 or L4 for the knee).

 - Focal Targeting: Because the DRG contains the cell bodies of the primary sensory neurons for a specific dermatome, you can achieve nearly 100% "coverage" of the painful area (e.g., the surgical site of a TKA) without tingling in the rest of the leg.

 - The ACCURATE Trial: This landmark study proved that DRG stimulation provided superior pain relief (up to 80% reduction) and had less "postural change" issues compared to traditional SCS for CRPS Type I and II in the lower extremities.

 - The "Gatekeeper" Effect:
 The DRG acts as a filter for pain signals. By stimulating it, we effectively "close the gate" before the pain signal even enters the spinal cord.

Practical Tip for Exams:

If the MCQ mentions focal pain
(knee, ankle, foot) or CRPS following a specific orthopedic surgery, DRG Stimulation is almost always the "most correct" advanced interventional answer. If the pain is diffuse or affects the whole limb, traditional or high-frequency SCS may be more appropriate.

Answer: 3
