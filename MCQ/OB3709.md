## Question

A 45-year-old male roofer presents after falling from a 30-foot roof and landing directly on his feet. He reports severe lower back and bilateral foot pain. On physical exam, there is 3/5 motor strength in the iliopsoas, quadriceps, tibialis anterior, and gastrocnemius-soleus muscles. There are diffuse paresthesias in the lower extremities, but perianal sensation remains intact. The bulbocavernosus reflex is present. Current imaging is shown in figures A-E. What is the most appropriate treatment option for his spine injury?

## Options

1. IV methylprednisolone and nonoperative treatment with early weight bearing
2. IV methylprednisolone and hyperextension casting
3. Decompression and instrumented fusion T11-L3 with postural reduction
4. In situ posterior instrumented fusion T11-L3
5. T12-L1 posterior lumbar interbody fusion with pedicle screw instrumentation T12-L1

## Correct option

**3. Decompression and instrumented fusion T11-L3 with postural reduction**

## Answer explanation

The clinical presentation is consistent with an L1 burst fracture with neurologic deficits. His TLICS score is > 4 so surgical decompression and instrumented stabilization is indicated. A postural reduction, surgical decompression of the neurologic elements, and instrumented stabilization two levels above and below the fracture (T11-L3) is the most appropriate treatment option listed.

Patient TLICS Calculation: 7 or 8
Fracture morphology: L1 burst fracture (2 points)
Neurologic status: Nerve root injury (2 points) or incomplete spinal cord injury (3 points); would need an MRI to determine level of the conus to conclude if this is an incomplete spinal cord injury or nerve root injury.
Posterior ligamentous complex integrity: disrupted (+3 points) as evidence by widening of the interspinous distance on the sagittal CT scans; in this case an MRI would be useful to confirm signal intensity between the spinous processes to confirm injury to the PLC.

Thoracolumbar burst fractures are the result of a compressive force to the anterior and middle columns of the spine leading to comminution and retropulsion of bone into the spinal canal. Most of these fractures are stable and can be treated nonoperatively with bracing and early ambulation. Operative fixation is recommended in cases where there is instability or neurologic deficits. The TLICS scoring system is an objective tool to determine if thoracolumbar fractures require operative fixation and take into account fracture morphology, neurologic status, and the integrity of the PLC. Posterior decompression with pedicle screw stabilized instrumented fusion is the most common surgical treatment for these injuries. The challenge with a posterior approach is obtaining an adequate decompression of the neurologic elements which is done with a complete laminectomy and reduction of the retropulsed bone fragments out of the spinal canal by pushing the fragments ventrally back into the body of the fractured vertebrae. Some argue an anterior or direct lateral approach is indicated to removed all retropulsed bone and reconstruct the anterior column, providing better sagittal stability than with posterior instrumented fusion alone.

*Patel et al. reviewed the classification of thoracolumbar spine trauma.* *They mentioned several classification systems that were fraught with low interobserver reliability and many that did not guide treatment or prognosticate outcomes.* *They reviewed the newer TLICS score that was designed to improve on these deficiencies and provide a universal language and framework for clinicians treating these injuries.*

*Wood et al. reviewed the management of thoracolumbar spine fractures.* Referenced literature found high infection rates (surgical site and respiratory) associated with IV methylprednisolone without significant neurologic recovery. **They stated** that posterior pedicle screw fixation and instrumented fusion is an efficient and effective treatment for burst fractures, but anterior corpectomy can be utilized for comminuted fractures of the lower lumbar spine (L2-5) to reconstitute the anterior column and restore sagittal alignment.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1 and 2: IV methylprednisolone has been associated with increased infection rates without improved neurologic recovery. It is currently not recommended to administer IV steroids in the setting of a spinal cord injury. This patient has a TLICS score of 8 requiring operative treatment. Nonoperative treatment would be preferred in patients with a TLICS score of less than 4.
Answer 4: This patient has neurologic deficits, which requires a decompression with a laminectomy in addition to stabilization with instrumented fusion.
Answer 5: Interbody fusion is not indicated in this patient as there is comminution of the L1 vertebral body, which would lead to the interbody device subsiding into the cancellous bone and subsequent loss of sagittal alignment. An anteriorly based corpectomy with struct grafting would be a better option for reconstituting the anterior column but is fraught with surgical approach morbidity.
