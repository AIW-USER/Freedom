## Question

A 17-year-old female with Down Syndrome presents to the office with her mother for a chief complaint of “knocked-knees”. She ambulates unassisted and does not endorse knee pain. Left knee radiographs are shown in Figures A and B. Right knee radiographs appear identical. The family reports that their friend's daughter, who also has Down Syndrome, required surgery for patella stabilization. What is the best initial course of treatment for this patient?

## Options

1. Observation
2. Patellar stabilizing brace
3. Lateral release
4. Tibial tubercle osteotomy
5. Trochlear groove deepening procedure

## Correct option

**1. Observation**

## Answer explanation

In a skeletally mature patient with Down Syndrome that ambulates without pain, the initial treatment of choice is observation. Bracing and surgical interventions are reserved for symptomatic patients.

Down Syndrome is a known risk factor for patellofemoral instability. Other associated conditions for patellofemoral instability include Larson syndrome, arthrogryposis, diastrophic dysplasia, nail-patella syndrome, and Ellis-van Creveld syndrome. In cases of chronic dislocations, the trochlea becomes hypoplastic which interferes with patellar stability. In a skeletally mature patient without pain, observation is indicated. Patellar stabilizing braces are used in symptomatic patients. Lastly, surgical procedures are reserved for refractory cases, and often require an osteotomy in the skeletally mature patient. Moreover, persistent instability and knee pain may continue to be present after surgical stabilization procedures in these patients.

*Dugdale et al. reviewed more than 360 patients with Down syndrome to determine the prevalence of patellofemoral instability and to assess the value of orthotic and surgical treatment.* **He reported** that instability of the patellofemoral joint may occur in patients with Down syndrome but is rarely disabling. **They concluded** that almost all patients with patellofemoral instability adapted to the problem and were able to walk.

*Mendez et al. retrospectively reviewed 16 patients with Down's syndrome with patellofemoral instability.* **They reported** that nonoperative treatment either maintained or improved the ambulatory status in 67% of these knees with either fair or good ambulation. However, 80% of the knees with poor ambulation did not improve. **They concluded** that operative treatment results in good ambulatory ability in knees with fair or poor preoperative ambulatory status.

Figures A and B demonstrate AP and Merchant radiographs, respectively, of a left knee lateral patellar dislocation.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Patellofemoral bracing is reserved for the symptomatic patient.
Answer 3: Lateral release is rarely indicated alone for patellofemoral instability.
Answer 4: Tibial tubercle osteotomy is often the treatment of choice in a symptomatic, mature patient.
Answer 5: Trochlear groove deepening procedures may be indicated for very shallow trochleas which are unlikely to maintain patella tracking despite other surgical interventions.
