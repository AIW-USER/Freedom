## Question

Based upon the most recent literature, which of the following is true regarding plating of humeral shaft fractures as compared to intramedullary nailing?

## Options

1. Significantly higher rate of nonunion
2. Significantly less reoperation rate
3. Significantly higher incidence of radial nerve injury
4. Significantly lower incidence of shoulder pain postoperatively
5. Significantly lower rates of infection

## Correct option

**4. Significantly lower incidence of shoulder pain postoperatively**

## Answer explanation

Controversy exists regarding intramedullary nailing (IMN) versus plating of humeral shaft fractures; however, the most recent evidence indicates a significantly lower incidence of shoulder pain postoperatively when these injuries are plated (answer 4).

Humeral shaft fractures account for 3-5% of all fractures, with 60% of these injuries occurring in patients older than 50 years of age. Risk factors for these humeral diaphysis fractures include a previous history of fracture, smoking, elderly age, and a history of osteoporosis. These injuries are associated with radial nerve injuries because of the natural course of this nerve running posteriorly in the spiral groove of the humerus. Diagnosis is made with clinical history, physical exam, and plain radiographs. Advanced imaging can be used for atypical patterns or for surgical planning in some instances. Treatment includes nonoperative and operative treatment depending on location, displacement, patient characteristics, and fracture pattern. Operative treatment includes IMN (antegrade or retrograde) and plating.

*Hurley et al. performed a meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials to compare outcomes of IMN vs plating of humeral shaft fractures.* *This level 1 study included 512 patients, and* **they found** no significant difference in nonunion rates (8.4% vs 6.4%, p=0.57), reoperation (11.6% vs. 7.6%, p=.26), or radial nerve palsy (2.8% vs. 4.2%, p=0.58) between the IMN and plating groups, respectively. **They found** significant differences in a faster time to union (10 weeks vs. 11.9 weeks, p<0.05), a lower rate of infection (1.2% vs. 5.3%, p<0.05), and lower operative time (61 minutes vs. 88 minutes, p<0.05) with the IMN vs the plating group, respectively. **They conclude** that the best treatment strategy for humeral shaft fractures depends on surgeon preference and fracture pattern.

*Derbas et al. performed a retrospective cohort study comparing outcomes between IMN and dynamic compression plating of humeral shaft fractures.* *They included 65 patients (25 IMN and 40 plating).* No significant differences were reported for nonunion, reoperation rates, or postoperative neuropathy. Significant improvement in shoulder range of motion (98% unaffected in plating vs 76% in IMN group, p=0.007) and a significantly lower incidence of shoulder pain (28% in plating group vs. 98% in IMN group, p < 0.001) were reported. **They conclude** that both plating and nailing of humeral shaft fractures achieve fracture union, and the variation of technique should be decided based upon the surgeon's preference and fracture pattern.

Incorrect Answer Choices:
Answer 1: Plating and IMN have similar rates of nonunion based upon the most recent literature.
Answer 2: Plating and IMN have similar rates of reoperation based upon the most recent literature.
Answer 3: The most recent literature has not reported a significant difference in the incidence of radial nerve injury between IMN and plating humeral shaft fractures.
Answer 5: The most recent literature has not reported a significant difference in the rate of postoperative infection between IMN and plating humeral shaft fractures.
