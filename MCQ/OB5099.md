## Question

Figure A is a radiograph of a 16-year-old-boy who sustained an injury to his right shoulder during a hockey game. What is the most likely injury pattern associated with this fracture?

![[q5099-a-distal-clavicle-physeal-fracture-x-ray.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Distal clavicle physeal separation, ruptured inferior periosteal sleeve, intact coracoclavicular ligaments, separated acromioclavicular joint
2. Intact distal clavicle physis, ruptured inferior periosteal sleeve, intact coracoclavicular ligaments, separated acromioclavicular joint
3. Intact distal clavicle physis, ruptured inferior periosteal sleeve, ruptured coracoclavicular ligaments, intact acromioclavicular joint
4. Distal clavicle physeal separation, intact inferior periosteal sleeve, intact coracoclavicular ligaments, intact acromioclavicular joint
5. Distal clavicle physeal separation, ruptured inferior periosteal sleeve, intact coracoclavicular ligaments, intact acromioclavicular joint

## Correct option

**4. Distal clavicle physeal separation, intact inferior periosteal sleeve, intact coracoclavicular ligaments, intact acromioclavicular joint**

## Answer explanation

A distal clavicle physeal separation often results in a preserved inferior periosteal sleeve and rarely involves injury to the coracoclavicular ligaments or separation of the acromioclavicular joint.

Distal clavicle physeal separation is a rare injury accounting for only 5% of clavicle fractures in children. A direct fall on the shoulder often results in superior displacement of the proximal metaphyseal fragment, leaving the inferior periosteum and its attachment to the coracoclavicular ligaments and the acromioclavicular joint intact. The presence of the intact inferior periosteal sleeve allows for significant remodeling to occur and permits conservative management.

*Black et al. reported a retrospective cohort of 59 children aged 5 to 16 years with lateral clavicle injuries, who presented over a 15-year period.* *The most commonly diagnosed injury was a distal clavicle fracture, which occurred in 45 children (76.2%).* **They reported** excellent results with conservative management in these patients.

*Nenopoulos et al. conducted a retrospective study of 75 children aged 3 to 16 years who sustained lateral clavicle injuries over a 25-year period.* *Only 2 patients (2.7%) were diagnosed with acromioclavicular separations and 12 patients (16%) required surgery.* *All patients were reported to have excellent long-term functional outcomes.*

The blue arrow points to the metaphysis, which is superiorly displaced relative to the epiphysis (white arrow). Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1,2,3 and 5: These are incorrect as they contain rare injury patterns such as ruptured periosteal sleeves, CC ligament injuries and AC separations, which are not commonly associated with distal clavicle fractures in children.
