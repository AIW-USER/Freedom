## Question

A 19-year-old male presents to your office after sustaining his 4th anterior shoulder dislocation during a lacrosse game. He previously underwent arthroscopic bankart repair in high school after his 3rd dislocation and had been doing well until this injury. You obtain an MRI which confirms re-tear of his anterior labrum with a Hill-Sachs lesion. There is also concern on the MRI for anterior glenoid bone loss. Therefore, CT scan is obtained and demonstrates 20% bone loss anteriorly. Which of the following is true regarding the this patient's management?

## Options

1. Revision arthroscopic bankart repair with capsular plication should be attempted before open management
2. Open bankart repair is best surgical option given prior failed arthroscopic management
3. Latarjet and distal tibial allograft would both provide stability by same mechanism
4. Traditional latarjet technique is recommended as it increases the glenoid track compared to congruent arc technique
5. Risk of recurrence is <10% after latarjet

## Correct option

**5. Risk of recurrence is <10% after latarjet**

## Answer explanation

In patients who undergo a latarjet procedure (coracoid transfer), the recurrence rate is thought to be between 0-8%.

Anterior shoulder instability is a common problem, particularly in young males participating in contact sports. Recurrence rates can be high in this population and tend to be higher in cases where there is significant bony involvement of the glenoid. The amount of bone loss constituting "critical bone loss," has been debated recently with some stating as low as 13% bone loss may be enough to create instability and risk for recurrence. Regardless of a specific cutoff value, it is well understood that worsening anterior glenoid bone loss results in associated worsening instability. Therefore, in cases with glenoid bone loss, a bony solution is recommended to decrease rates of recurrent instability compared to arthroscopic or open bankart repair. Popular options include the laterjet coracoid transfer and distal tibial allograft, although they function slightly differently to improve stability.

*Bhatia et al. reviewed 10 studies examining outcomes and surgical techniques of the latarjet procedure.* **They noted** a rate of recurrent instability ranging from 0-8%, with excellent patient satisfaction >90% after this procedure. **They conclude** that given the high levels of satisfaction and the low rates of recurrence, the clinical impact of the latarjet procedure is promising.

*Streubel et al. published a review of anterior glenohumeral instability focused on an algorithmic approach towards treatment based on pathology.* **They note** that history and physical examination, as well as bone lesions of the glenoid and humerus are all important to recognize prior to definitive management. **They recommend** that in the setting of >20% bone loss, iliac crest bone graft or coracoid transfer be performed based on its favorable outcomes.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Revision arthroscopic bankart repair is not recommended after initial bankart failure in the presence of significant glenoid bone loss, as repeat failure rates are increased.
Answer 2: Open bankart repair is an option and results are comparable to that of arthroscopic repair, but in cases with large (>13%) degree of bone loss it is not recommended.
Answer 3: While the latarjet procedure and distal tibial allograft both offer a bony solution, they function differently. The latarjet procedure functions by increasing the glenoid track, providing a sling effect on top of the subscapularis, and also by reconstructing the capsule, while the distal tibial allograft primarily functions to increase the glenoid track.
Answer 4: Both traditional and congruent arc techniques can be used but the glenoid track is increased more with the congruent arc technique because the undersurface of the coracoid is used to abut the anterior glenoid.
