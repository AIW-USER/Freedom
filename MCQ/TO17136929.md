Chapter: Trauma MCQs — Calcaneus Fractures

## Question

Which of the following patient-related risk factors is associated with an increased risk for infection after open reduction and internal fixation of calcaneal fractures through standard lateral incision?

## Options

1. Rheumatoid arthritis
2. Use of systemic steroids
3. History of alcohol abuse
4. History of smoking

## Correct option

**4. History of smoking**

## Answer explanation

Risk factors for wound complications include diabetes, smoking, open fracture, single layer closure, Sander’s type, high number of residents and/or fellows during the case, long duration of surgery, large estimated blood loss, high number of persons present in the operating room during the entire case, extended time between injury and surgery, and high body mass index.

Wound related complications can be minimized by following measures

- Tourniquet use, is associated with a decreased risk for the development of a wound complication
- Patients should be advised not to smoke until the wound has healed and should be informed of the consequences of doing so before surgery is planned.
- Carefully retracting the soft tissues and maintaining a full-thickness flap with the extensile approach are crucial.
- Drains should be used under the lateral flap to prevent hematoma formation postoperatively.
- A two-layer closure should be performed, closing the wound from both ends to the middle.
- Sutures should be left in place for 2 to 3 weeks, and motion exercises should be avoided during that time to lessen shear forces under the flap.
