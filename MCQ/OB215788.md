## Question

A 10-year-old male with spastic hemiplegia type cerebral palsy presents to your office for evaluation of his feet. On examination, he is noted to have an equinovarus foot deformity. The decision is made to proceed with serial casting and a treatment that blocks presynaptic acetylcholine release into the synaptic cleft. Which of the following medications works by this mechanism?

## Options

1. Acetylcholinesterase
2. Baclofen
3. Botulinum toxin A
4. Methacholine
5. Pyridostigmine

## Correct option

**3. Botulinum toxin A**

## Answer explanation

This patient has cerebral palsy and is being treated with Botulinum toxin A. Botulinum toxin A is a competitive inhibitor of presynaptic cholinergic receptors that blocks presynaptic acetylcholine release into the synaptic cleft.

Cerebral Palsy is a common congenital condition caused by injury to the immature brain that leads to upper motor neuron disease and presents with cognitive and musculoskeletal manifestations of varying severity. The diagnosis is made clinically with evaluation of developmental milestones, cognitive function, and musculoskeletal abnormalities including spasticity, loss of motor control, and impaired balance. Treatment involves a multidisciplinary approach to address spasticity, orthopedic manifestations, and cognitive function. Botox (botulinum - A toxin) is often used as it is a competitive inhibitor of presynaptic cholinergic receptors with a finite lifetime (usually lasts 2-3 months). It is used to maintain joint motion during rapid growth when a child is too young for surgery. It is a useful adjunct in the treatment of dynamic contractures; it provides little benefit with static contractures.

*Moore et al. performed a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, parallel-group study reviewed the use of botulinum toxin A in the treatment for spasticity in cerebral palsy.* **They reported** no cumulative or persisting benefit from repeated botulinum toxin A (BtA) at the injection cycle troughs at 1 year or 2 years. **They concluded** that this finding does not deny the value, individually, of single injection cycles or prove that repeating them is unhelpful.

*Seyler et al. reviewed botulinum neurotoxins, which are produced by Clostridium botulinum, an anaerobic, gram-positive, spore-forming rod-shaped bacterium.* **They report** that there are seven known serotypes of botulinum neurotoxins that cleave soluble N-ethyl-maleimide-sensitive factor attachment receptor (SNARE) proteins, preventing effective release of neurotransmitters across specialized synaptic junctions, resulting in flaccid paralysis, anhidrosis, and increased nutritional blood flow in vascular beds. **They concluded** that controlled injections of botulinum neurotoxins could be used to treat neuromuscular disorders.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Acetylcholinesterase is an enzyme that degrades acetylcholine, but does not blocks presynaptic acetylcholine release
Answer 2: Baclofen acts as a centrally acting Gamma-aminobutyric acid (GABA) agonist that inhibit the transmission of both monosynaptic and polysynaptic reflexes
Answer 4: Methacholine is an example of a direct-acting cholinergic agent
Answer 5: Pyridostigmine blocks the function of acetylcholinesterase, thus increasing acetylcholine levels
