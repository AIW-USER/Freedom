## Question

Following a motor vehicle collision, a 26-year-old male presents with the radiograph in Figure A. An appropriate pelvic binder is placed; he is hemodynamically stable and has no other injuries. The decision is made to proceed to the operating room for posterior and anterior pelvic fixation. Regarding anterior pelvic ring fixation, which of the following is true?

![[q219221-a-apc-ii.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Locking symphyseal plates offer superior biomechanical stability compared to nonlocking symphyseal plates.
2. Multi-hole symphyseal plates offer lower failure rates compared to two-hole symphseal plates.
3. Anterior external fixator pins in the iliac crest provide better stability than supraacetabular pins.
4. Reoperation rates are higher for multi-hole plates than two-hole plates.
5. Based on the fracture morphology, anterior fixation will not be necessary

## Correct option

**2. Multi-hole symphyseal plates offer lower failure rates compared to two-hole symphseal plates.**

## Answer explanation

This pelvic ring injury requires definitive anterior fixation. Multi-hole plates provide lower failure and malunion rates for anterior symphyseal diastasis than two-hole plates (Answer 2).

Pelvic ring fractures with anterior disruption present significant challenges due to the potential for instability and associated morbidity. Along with posterior stabilization, displacement of the pubic symphysis greater than 2.5 cm is an indication for anterior fixation. Furthermore, the anterior structures of the pelvis contribute approximately 40% of pelvic stability. Fixation aims to restore the pelvic ring integrity to prevent hemodynamic instability and facilitate early mobilization. Techniques vary based on patient stability, comorbidities, and ultimate surgical plan. In this scenario, definitive fixation of the anterior pelvis with a multihole plate is indicated. Typically, a four or six-hole 3.5-mm plate is sufficient to span the pubic symphysis. Due to the symphysis' mobility during gait, it is not uncommon for symphyseal fixation constructs to ultimately loosen or fail to some degree.

*Sagi et al. performed a retrospective review of the outcomes of different symphyseal plating techniques for anterior pelvic ring injuries.* *This study compared the effectiveness of two-hole plates (THP) versus multi-hole plates (MHP) in 229 skeletally mature patients at a level 1 trauma center.* Radiographic and clinical outcome measures were recorded. **The authors found** a higher rate of fixation failure in the THP group (33%) than in the MHP group (12%). Malunion was also higher in the THP group (57%) compared to the MHP group (15%). The reoperation rate was slightly higher in the THP group but was not statistically significant. **The authors concluded** that THP symphyseal fixation has a higher failure rate and malunion rate than MHP and, therefore, recommend using MHP in unstable pubic symphyseal disruptions.

*Grimshaw et al. evaluated the biomechanical stability of locking and nonlocking symphyseal plates for open-book pelvic injuries in a cadaveric study.* *The authors studied twelve osteopenic pelvic specimens with partially stable open book injuries (OTA 61-B3.1) treated with six-hole 3.5-mm plates, either locked or nonlocked.* The pelvic specimens underwent one million cycles before maintenance of reduction was assessed. Diastasis occurred in both groups, but neither group experienced abrupt failures. **The authors concluded** that locked plating of the pubic symphysis does not offer any biomechanical advantage over standard nonlocked plating in open-book pelvic injuries.

Figure A is a pelvic inlet radiograph demonstrating an AP compression type II injury with greater than 2.5 cm of anterior ring diastasis and widening of the anterior SI joints.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Based on biomechanical studies, locking plate constructs do not significantly increase stability compared to nonlocking plates.
Answer 3: The biomechanical stability of supraacetabular pins is superior to external fixator pins placed in the iliac crest.
Answer 4: Based on the Sagi study, no statistically significant difference has been found between reoperation rates for the two different plates.
Answer 5: The radiograph in Figure A demonstrates significant pubic symphyseal disruption, necessitating anterior fixation for ring stability.
