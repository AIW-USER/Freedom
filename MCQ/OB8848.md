## Question

A young child falls during gymnastics practice and sustains the isolated injury shown in Figure A. She is admitted to hospital for surgery. The prevalence of which complication is increased with this injury pattern?

![[q8848-a-fig1-png.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Compartment syndrome
2. Infection rates
3. Open fracture
4. Triceps avulsion
5. Vascular injury

## Correct option

**1. Compartment syndrome**

## Answer explanation

This patient has sustained an ipsilateral both-bone forearm fracture and a contiguous extension-type Gartland III supracondylar humerus fracture. Studies have found an increase in ipsilateral nerve injuries and compartment syndrome in patients with this injury pattern.

Fracture energy is commonly associated with fracture displacement, comminution, and soft-tissue injury. High energy injuries are also associated with ipsilateral limb fractures, such as an ipsilateral forearm fracture associated with a Gartland III supracondylar humerus fracture. This "floating elbow" type of injury places in the pediatric patient has been linked to an increased risk of ipsilateral nerve injuries and the development of compartment syndrome. Therefore, patients these concomitant injuries should be acutely evaluated, admitted to the hospital, and monitored closed within the perioperative period for these complications.

*Muchow et al. compared patients with operatively managed ipsilateral supracondylar humerus and forearm fractures with patients presenting with isolated, operatively treated supracondylar humerus fractures, to quantify the prevalence of neurovascular injury.* The prevalence of nerve palsies was 14.7% in the floating elbow patients compared with 7.8% of isolated injury patients. However, there was no significant difference in pulseless extremities when comparing the ipsilateral (6/150 4%) and isolated (50/1228 4.1%) injury patients.

*Blakemore et al. reviewed a series of pediatric patients that developed compartment syndrome after sustaining ipsilateral humerus and forearm fractures.* **They showed** that displaced fractures, involving the forearm AND distal humerus, were more likely to develop compartment syndrome. The prevalence in this group was 33%, compared to the overall prevalence of 7%.

Figure A shows an AP and Lateral radiograph showing a greenstick ipsilateral both bone forearm fracture associated with an extension-type Gartland III supracondylar humerus fracture.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Infection rates have not been found to be increased in patients with ipsilateral forearm fractures with supracondylar humerus fractures. These can often successfully be managed with closed reduction percutaneous pinning and cast immobilization.
Answer 3: Despite the higher energy involved in combined injuries of the elbow and forearm, comparative studies have not found an increased incidence of these injuries being open.
Answer 4: Tendon avulsions, including the triceps and biceps brachii, are uncommon in children and are not associated with supracondylar humerus fractures or both bone forearm fractures.
Answer 5: There is not an increased risk of pulseless extremity or vascular injury among patient with combined injuries as compared to patients with isolated supracondylar or both-bone forearm fractures.
