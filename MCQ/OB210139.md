## Question

A 37-year-old woman is referred to you by her primary care physician one week after feeling a sharp pain in the medial ankle while after tripping over her new puppy. She has weakness with heel lift and pain in the swelling in the medial ankle as seen in Figure A. The patient presents an MRI disc, ordered by her primary care physician. Figures B and C show the corresponding MRI images. She enjoys tennis and yoga and hopes to return to both activities as soon as possible. What is the most appropriate first step in treatment?

## Options

1. Physical therapy
2. Anti-inflammatories
3. Chemotherapy
4. Resection
5. Biopsy

## Correct option

**5. Biopsy**

## Answer explanation

The patient has a large medial sided ankle lesion on MRI suggestive of a soft tissue sarcoma which should be biopsied prior to any other treatment.

Soft tissue sarcomas often present as an enlarging, painless mass and in some instances may only be noticed following a history of injury. A detailed history and physical examination is always important but particularly in maintaining vigilance for potentially oncologic causes of musculoskeletal complaints. Common diagnoses such as muscle strain or joint sprain may distract providers from an underlying pathologic process on physical exam or imaging. When imaging is indeterminate or suggestive of a soft tissue sarcoma, either a core needle biopsy or open biopsy should be performed.

*Rougraff et al. performed a literature review to answer questions such as who, when, and how biopsies of soft tissues should be performed.* **They found** open biopsy had the highest diagnostic accuracy and that diagnostic difficulty is associated with myxoid and round cell neoplasms, infections, and tumors in the paraspinal region. No evidence addressed who should perform soft tissue mass biopsies. **They concluded** the limited number of references demonstrates the need for more research in the area of biopsy of soft tissue masses.

*Tantisricharoenkul et al. reviewed two cases of proximal biceps soft tissue lesions mistaken for proximal biceps ruptures.* *Both cases had delayed diagnosis, however, they were ultimately properly diagnosed with biopsy and treated with wide excision and radiation.* **They concluded** that obtaining a good history and detailed shoulder physical examination is critical to avoid mistaking an apparent proximal biceps rupture for a soft tissue sarcoma.

Figure A shows a clinical image of a patient with medial ankle and foot swelling and ecchymosis. Figure B shows an axial T1 of the ankle showing a dark medial sided lesion. Figure C shows an sagittal T2 MRI of the ankle with a bright medial sided lesion.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1, 2, 3, & 4: Physical therapy, anti-inflammatories, chemotherapy, and resection would not be appropriate in the setting of an MRI lesion concerning for a sarcoma.
