## Question

A 23-year-old male presents to the trauma bay after being stabbed on the right side of his posterior neck by his girlfriend. On exam he is found to have left sided loss of pain and right sided loss of proprioception. He is diagnosed with Brown-Séquard syndrome. Which of the following best describes his suspected motor and temperature exam consistent with this diagnosis?

## Options

1. Right sided loss of motor, Right sided loss of temperature
2. Right sided loss of motor, Left sided loss of temperature
3. Left sided loss of motor, Left sided loss of temperature
4. Left sided loss of motor, Right sided loss of temperature
5. Normal bilateral motor exam, Right sided loss of temperature

## Correct option

**2. Right sided loss of motor, Left sided loss of temperature**

## Answer explanation

Brown-Séquard syndrome is an incomplete spinal cord injury characterized by ipsilateral loss of motor and proprioception function with contralateral loss of pain and temperature.

Brown-Séquard occurs most commonly following penetrating trauma and is a result of hemitransection of the spinal cord. Ipsilateral loss of motor function is present secondary to disruption of the lateral corticospinal tracts which decussate in the brainstem. Conversely, contralateral loss of pain and temperature is observed secondary to the lateral spinothalamic tracts which decussate one or two levels prior to the nerve exiting. Surprisingly, this condition holds the best prognosis for motor recovery as it relates to incomplete spinal cord injuries with 99% of patients being ambulatory at final follow up.

*Leven et al review a case of Brown-Séquard syndrome occurring secondary to a gunshot wound.* **They report** that on presentation the patient had ipsilateral motor scores of 0/5 in both the affected upper and lower extremities with normal motor scores contralaterally. The patient subsequently underwent corpectomy, bullectomy and anterior cervical decompression with fusion. One month after surgery she had 5/5 strength in the ipsilateral lower extremity and 3/5 strength in the ipsilateral upper extremity. **They conclude** that this was the first reported case of high cervical Brown-Séquard syndrome following a GSW that was successfully treated with surgery.

*Wirz et al review outcomes after incomplete spinal cord injuries, specifically Brown-Séquard and central cord syndrome.* **They report** no difference in the outcome of motor score, walking function, daily life activities, somatosensory evoked potentials and length of stay between age matched groups with both conditions. **They conclude** that these findings are contradictory to the assumption that patients with Brown-Séquard have improved outcomes compared to those of central cord syndrome and that more research needs to be conducted to evaluate the mechanisms of recovery in these groups.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The patient should have contralateral (left) sided loss of temperature.
Answer 3: The patient should have ipsilateral (right) sided loss of motor function.
Answer 4 & 5: Motor function is lost on the ipsilateral (right) side with contralateral (left) loss of temperature function in this condition.
