## Question

A 9-year-old patient presents to your clinic with right forearm, right arm, and left collar bone pain that is worse at night. No history of trauma or recent illnesses, and she denies having fevers. Ibuprofen and acetaminophen moderately help the pain. Her only past medical history is Crohn's disease. Radiographs of the right upper extremity were obtained and shown in figures A and B. Laboratory studies were obtained with WBC of 9.5 (normal 4.5-10), CRP of 1.3 mg/L (normal <1.0 mg/L), and ESR of 35 mm/hr (normal 0-25 mm/hr). A biopsy was taken and shown in Figure C. ## Options

1. Osteosarcoma
2. Ewing sarcoma
3. Osteomyelitis
4. Osteoid Osteoma
5. Chronic recurrent multifocal osteomyelitis

## Correct option

**5. Chronic recurrent multifocal osteomyelitis**

## Answer explanation

This patient has chronic recurrent multifocal osteomyelitis (CRMO), a non-infectious, rheumatologic-based process.

CRMO, also known as chronic non-bacterial osteomyelitis, is characterized by multiple lesions of sterile bone inflammation. Patients are typically young and will often complain of worse pain at night. Laboratory studies can demonstrate either normal or minimally elevated WBC, CRP, and ESR. Xrays will show osteolytic lesions with surrounding sclerosis abutting the growth plate of long bone metaphyses.

Ferguson and Sandu review the pathogenesis and features of CRMO. Common characteristics include sterile bone inflammation that is multifocal and occurs multiple times over several years. These patients do not respond to antibiotics and will have negative cultures if taken. There is an association with other rheumatologic diseases, particularly psoriasis, inflammatory bowel disease, and palmar-plantar pustulosis. There are two syndromic forms, Majeed Syndrome and deficiency of the interleukin-1 receptor antagonist (DIRA).

*Roderick et al. give a broad overview of the characteristics of CRMO.* *Most patients will present with pain and swelling to affected bones, with the clavicle and long bones being most commonly symptomatic.* Vertebral involvement is common, however, it is often asymptomatic. There is a 2:1 female: male preponderance and a strong association with other autoimmune diseases. Genetically the IL1RN and PLIN2 genes are believed to play a role. First-line treatment is typically NSAIDs, which can be effective. Other treatment options include DMARDs and bisphosphonates. Possible long-term complications include chronic pain and bone deformities.

Figures A-B demonstrate an osteolytic lesion in the distal radius and humerus with surrounding sclerosis. Figure C shows inflammatory cells with normal trabecular bone.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: The Xrays demonstrate an osteolytic lesion and biopsy shows normal-appearing cells, without evidence of cellular atypia. Additionally, she has multiple lesions at once which atypical for osteosarcoma, unless metastases are present.
Answer 2: Biopsy does not demonstrate any small round blue cells that are characteristic of Ewing sarcoma. Additionally, her history and Xray imaging are inconsistent with Ewing sarcoma.
Answer 3: There is no evidence of infection in this patient. CRMO is often misdiagnosed as infectious osteomyelitis and patients can undergo extended periods of antibiotic therapy without improvement in their symptoms.
Answer 4: Although both lesions improve with NSAIDs, the radiographs do not demonstrate the classic nidus seen in osteoid osteoma. Additionally, osteoid osteomas are solitary lesions.
