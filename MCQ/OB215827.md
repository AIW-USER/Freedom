## Question

A 28-year-old basketball player sustained an inversion injury to the right ankle when he landed on an opposing player's foot after making a 3-point shot. He was able to bear weight and did not have tenderness over the distal fibula. He did, however, have tenderness over the lateral ankle ligaments. The patient was treated with a short leg cast and made non-weight bearing. How do outcomes of this treatment modality compare to functional rehabilitation with an ankle brace and weight-bearing as tolerated?

## Options

1. More likely to return to sport at long term follow up
2. Higher patient satisfaction
3. Longer time to return to sport
4. Decreased instability as viewed on stress radiographs
5. Improved swelling at short term follow up

## Correct option

**3. Longer time to return to sport**

## Answer explanation

Functional rehabilitation following lateral ligament ankle injuries has improved outcomes including a shorter time to return to sport when compared to those treated with immobilization.

Lateral ankle ligament injuries are very common in active adults participating regularly in sports. Typically, the diagnosis is made on physical exam alone and no imaging is required. Radiographs should be obtained in patients who are unable to bear weight or those with tenderness over the distal fibula. Functional rehabilitation has shown to be superior to immobilization as it relates to patient outcomes, return to sport, decreased instability on stress radiographs, and improved swelling.

Ardèvol et al performed a randomized controlled trial comparing cast immobilization and functional treatment in patients with complete rupture of their lateral ankle ligaments. They report that the functional group had significantly earlier and better return to physical activity, fewer symptoms at 3 and 6 months, and decreased joint laxity. They conclude that functional treatment is safe, associated with a more rapid recovery, and particularly suitable in athletic populations.

*Kerkhoffs et al compare immobilization and functional rehabilitation for acute lateral ankle ligament injuries in adults.* **They report** statistically significant differences favoring functional treatment such that patients returned to sport and work more reliably and more quickly, had less swelling, and less objective instability on stress radiographs. **They conclude** that functional treatment appeared to be the most favorable strategy for treating acute ankle sprains when compared with immobilization.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Patients treated with functional rehabilitation are more likely to return to sport at long-term follow-up.
Answer 2: Patients were more satisfied with the treatment that did not involve immobilization in a cast.
Answer 4: Those treated with immobilization in a cast demonstrated more instability on stress radiographs.
Answer 5: Functional treatment results in less swelling at short-term follow-up.
