## Question

A surgeon is preparing for an upcoming total hip arthroplasty and reviewing femoral head bearing options. After careful consideration, the surgeon chooses a bearing material that is relatively nonductile with superior wettability, a superior modulus of elasticity, and a relatively lower fracture toughness. What implant material was chosen?

## Options

1. Ceramic
2. Cobalt-chromium alloy
3. Stainless steel
4. Tantalum
5. Titanium

## Correct option

**1. Ceramic**

## Answer explanation

Compared to metal femoral heads, ceramic heads have a greater wettability, higher modulus of elasticity, are more brittle, and have a unique complication of bearing fracture (Answer 1).

When paired with cross-linked polyethylene liners, ceramic heads have become the standard of care in bearing implant choice for total hip arthroplasty (THA). Ceramic heads exhibit reduced wear rates secondary to their high wettability and high resistance to scratching. Ceramic materials are chemically inert and biocompatible, and thus induce substantially fewer biological reactions compared to other bearing surfaces. Modern ceramics are typically made of a combination of alumina and zirconia to better address the risk of component fracture. However, the risk of component fracture is still present and unique to this material. Ceramics have the highest modulus of elasticity, followed by cobalt-chromium alloys, stainless steel, and titanium.

*Kim et al. performed a review of THA in patients under 30 years old using ceramic-on-polyethylene bearings.* *The average age was 28.3 years at the time of surgery, and a minimum of 10-year follow-up was completed.* *The study found no signs of osteolysis or aseptic loosening in any patients.* **The authors concluded** that ceramic on highly cross-linked polyethylene bearings results in well-functioning implants without signs of symptomatic or radiographic wear in younger patients.

*Traina et al. performed a systematic review covering ceramic-bearing fractures in THA.* After final exclusions, 53 publications were included. The authors aimed to assess risk factors for ceramic component fracture and possible predictive diagnostic measures. After review, the only associated risk factor predictive of ceramic head fracture is the use of a 28 mm head on a short femoral neck. The review found a reported occurrence of ceramic liner fracture between 0.013% and 1.1% in patients undergoing ceramic-on-ceramic (CoC) THA. **The authors concluded** that ceramic bearing fractures are a catastrophic, but rare complication of CoC THAs and short neck 28 mm heads may be predictive of this complication.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Cobalt-chromium alloys (CoCr) are commonly used as THA bearings for many reasons. However, when compared to ceramic implants, CoCr demonstrates less wear resistance, a lower modulus of elasticity, and less biocompatibility. In contrast to ceramic bearings, CoCr implants are more ductile and therefore demonstrate a lower bearing fracture risk.
Answer 3: Stainless steel is predominantly an iron-nickel-chromium-carbon alloy that exhibits a high susceptibility to corrosion and wear. Stainless steel is often reserved for fracture plates and screws and not used in arthroplasty bearings.
Answer 4: Tantalum is a material commonly used in THAs for acetabular shells or metal augments due to its porous nature, osteoconductivity, and structural properties. Tantalum's high friction and susceptibility to wear make it an unsuitable material for THA bearings.
Answer 5: Titanium is not commonly used for bearing surfaces in THA, as it has a high failure rate secondary to poor resistance to wear and notch sensitivity. Furthermore, ceramics, CoCr, and stainless steel have a higher modulus of elasticity.
