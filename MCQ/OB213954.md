## Question

Which of the following statements does not have a strong recommendation based on the 2016 AAOS Surgical Management of Osteoarthritis of the Knee: Evidence-based Guideline?

## Options

1. Obese patients have less improvement following total knee arthroplasty
2. There is no difference in pain and function following patellar resurfacing
3. There is no difference in cruciate retaining and posterior stabilized implants
4. Hepatitis C and cirrhosis increases complication risks
5. Periarticular and peripheral nerve anesthesia reduces post operative pain and opiate use

## Correct option

**4. Hepatitis C and cirrhosis increases complication risks**

## Answer explanation

According to the 2016 AAOS Surgical Management of Osteoarthritis of the Knee: Evidence-based Guideline, hepatitis C/cirrhosis increases complication risks is given a limited strength recommendation.

The guideline provides strong, moderate, and limited strength recommendations for various peri-operative practices in total knee arthroplasty (TKA). Strong evidence supports obese patients having less improvement following TKA, periarticular and peripheral nerve anesthesia reducing post-op pain and opiate use, no difference between cruciate-retaining and posterior stabilized implants, and no difference in pain or function following patella resurfacing. Moderate strength of recommendation highlights includes diabetes as a risk factor for higher risk of complications and neuraxial anesthesia as a method to improve perioperative outcomes and complication rates. There is limited strength of evidence to suggest that hepatitis C/cirrhosis increases complications.

*McGrory et. al. provide a comprehensive review of the evidence for routine TKA practices.* **They report** that there is strong evidence to suggest delaying TKA for obese patients up to 8 months to allow weight reduction and that there is no evidence to suggest a strong advantage to either cruciate-retaining or posteriorly stabilized implants. **They conclude** that these recommendations are based on available evidence as of January 2015 and are subject to change with further research.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: There is strong evidence to support obese patients have less improvement after TKA.
Answer 2: There is strong evidence that there is no difference in pain and function with patellar resurfacing.
Answer 3: There is strong evidence to suggest no difference in outcomes with cruciate retaining versus posterior stabilized knee replacements.
Answer 5: There is strong evidence to support less pain and opiate use with periarticular and peripheral nerve anesthesia.
