## Question

An 79-year-old male presents with longstanding left shoulder pain and difficulty with raising his arm over his head. His exam shows wasting of the deltoid and obvious scapular dysrhythmia on the left side. He lacks the ability to do any forward flexion or external rotation in his left shoulder. He can internally rotate without difficulty. His radiograph and MRI images are seen in figures A and B respectively. He wants to know if he is a candidate for a reverse shoulder arthroplasty (RSA). Which of the following answer choices is the MOST appropriate response?

![[q3654-b-massiverotatorcufftear.jpg]]

## Options

1. He is a candidate for RSA due to rotator cuff tear arthropathy
2. He is a candidate for RSA due to ability to internally rotate
3. He is not a candidate for RSA due to deltoid dysfunction
4. He is not a candidate for RSA due to massive rotator cuff tear
5. He is not a candidate for RSA due to his age

## Correct option

**3. He is not a candidate for RSA due to deltoid dysfunction**

## Answer explanation

Figure A shows a radiograph with a high riding humeral head consistent with a rotator cuff tear. Figure B is a coronal T2 weighted MRI with a high riding humeral head, and a rotator cuff tear. This patient would have been a good surgical candidate for RSA except for the fact he has deltoid insufficiency. Intact deltoid muscle function is a strict necessity when considering the indications for a reverse shoulder arthroplasty. After reverse total arthroplasty, the deltoid muscle gains a mechanical advantage through increased working distance and generates more force to move the shoulder. Indications for reverse shoulder arthroplasty include: elderly patients over 70, glenohumeral arthritis, massive irrepairable rotator cuff tears, intact deltoid muscle, and adequate glenoid bone stock.

*In their retrospective review, Boileau et al* **showed** that both arthroscopic biceps tenotomy and tenodesis helped to treat severe pain or dysfunction caused in patients with irreparable rotator cuff tears. Lesions of the long head of the biceps tendon associated with massive rotator cuff tears were found to be contributory shoulder pain and dysfunction. However, pseudoparalysis of the shoulder and severe rotator cuff arthropathy are contraindications to this isolated procedure.
