## Answer explanation

After excluding osteonecrosis, the most commonly reported cause for early hip replacement (<50 years of age) is arthritis secondary to developmental dysplasia of the hip (DDH, answer choice D).

There are a variety of disorders that can lead to end-stage arthritis within the hip joint. Morphologic changes to the acetabulum or femoral neck result in altered biomechanics of the hip joint that, over time, can result in early osteoarthritis requiring hip replacement. There has been increasing interest in the longitudinal impact of femoroacetabular impingement (FAI) and its implication on early onset osteoarthritis. However, developmental dysplasia of the hip (DDH) serves as the more common culprit for early-onset osteoarthritis in cross-sectional and retrospective studies. When treating a patient for FAI, it is important to also screen for underlying DDH, as unrecognized DDH results in poor outcomes when treated with hip arthroscopy osteoplasty procedures.

*Clohisy and colleagues retrospectively reviewed patients from three medical centers who underwent primary total hip arthroplasty before 50 years of age.* **They report** osteoarthritis as the most common cause of early native hip failure (47.5%). When reviewing the specific etiologies of osteoarthritis, approximately half (48.4%) of the cases were due to DDH. Further, **the authors found** a large portion of patients with radiographic findings consistent with femoroacetabular impingement (FAI). **They concluded** FAI is a potentially underrecognized cause of premature osteoarthritis.

*Wyles and colleagues retrospectively reviewed patients from their quaternary treatment center who underwent primary total hip arthroplasty before 55 years of age.* *They specifically looked at the contralateral, unaffected hip and tracked its progression over a minimum 10-year follow-up.* **They found** that out of the initial 172 patients, 35 patients went onto contralateral THA for the following causes: DDH 33%, FAI 18%, or normal morphology 15%. **They concluded** that DDH has a higher probability of progressing to end-stage osteoarthritis or THA compared to FAI and normal morphology.

Figure D shows an AP pelvis radiographic image with bilateral under-coverage of the femoral heads, shallow acetabular sockets, aspherical femoral heads, and coxa valga. Collectively, these findings are consistent with DDH.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Figure A shows bilateral femoroacetabular impingement. While this condition is being increasingly recognized as a potential cause for early-onset hip arthritis, DDH remains the most common etiology.
Answer 2: Figure B shows a right-sided slipped capital femoral epiphysis (SCFE) treated with percutaneous single cannulated screw fixation. This is an infrequent cause for conversion to THA which serves as a testament to fixing these patients in the operative theatre.
Answer 3: Figure C shows bilateral Legg-Calve-Perthes disease or potential multiple epiphyseal dysplasia (a skeletal survey would be required to determine the ultimate diagnosis). This patient would undoubtedly go on to require bilateral THA, however, the overall prevalence of both diseases is relatively low and thus not commonly encountered etiology overall.
Answer 5: Figure E shows a left-sided femoral neck fracture treated with open reduction and internal fixation. This patient has an increased risk for both osteonecrosis of the femoral head and post-traumatic arthritis, however, this is overall an infrequent cause of early hip failure.
