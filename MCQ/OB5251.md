## Question

Traumatic physeal growth arrest in which of the following locations would create the greatest limb-length discrepancy in a 12-year-old male?

## Options

1. Distal humerus
2. Distal radius
3. Distal femur
4. Proximal tibia
5. Proximal femur

## Correct option

**3. Distal femur**

## Answer explanation

The distal femoral physis has the greatest annual contribution to limb length, with an average of 9-10 mm of growth per year.

Injury to the reserve or proliferative zones of the physis are more likely to result in permanent damage to the physis. Growth at the physis continues until skeletal maturity, which may be assumed at chronological age 14 years in girls and 16 years in boys. Growth arrest at 12 years old in a male assumes 4 years of remaining growth. Therefore expected limb-length discrepancy can then be calculated as 4 x 10mm = 40mm.

*Abzug et al. reviewed physeal arrest of the distal radius.* **They state** that after physeal injury, repeat radiographs are recommended every 3 months to ensure normal growth resumes. This is often demonstrated by parallel Park-Harris lines through the metaphysis of the distal radius.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Distal humerus averages 2-3 mm of growth per year (4 x 3mm = 12mm).
Answer 2: Distal radius averages 5-6mm of growth per year (4 x 6mm = 24mm).
Answer 4: Proximal tibia averages 6mm of growth per year (4 x 6mm = 24mm).
Answer 4: Proximal femur averages 3mm of growth per year (4 x 3mm = 12mm).
