## Question

Figures 40a and 40b are the radiographs of a 53-year-old woman. On examination she has a limited arc of motion, however has pain only with terminal dorsiflexion at the 1st metatarsophalangeal joint. There is no pain at mid range of motion. She has attempted shoe wear modifications, activity modifications, non-steroidal anti-inflammatories and rigid orthotics, however still has persistent pain and dysfunction. Which of the following is the most appropriate surgical management?

## Options

1. Plantar release
2. Dorsal cheilectomy
3. Interpositional soft-tissue arthroplasty
4. Arthrodesis
5. Prosthetic joint replacement

## Correct option

**2. Dorsal cheilectomy**

## Answer explanation

The patient presents with dorsal pain and grade 2 hallux rigidus that is refractory to nonoperative treatment. Dorsal cheilectomy is the most appropriate treatment based on the physical examination and imaging.

Surgical indications in the management of hallux rigidus include pain, stiffness and limitations in motion that are refractory to nonsurgical measures. Grade 1 and grade 2 hallux rigidus can be reliably managed with dorsal cheilectomy. Grade 3 and 4 hallux rigidus are typically treated with arthrodesis, however appropriately selected patients with grade 3 hallux rigidus may be treated with dorsal cheilectomy.

Coughlin and Shurnas review their long-term results with the management of hallux rigidus based on the classification as proposed by Couglin. The authors found excellent long-term results in terms of range of motion, subjective and objective (AOFAS forefoot) evaluation in patients treated with cheilectomy, including select patients with Grade 3 hallux rigidus

Mann and Clanton review their results with treatment of hallux rigidus with cheilectomy. In appropriately selected patients they found substantial improvements when compared with historical techniques including arthrodesis and arthroplasty.

Figures A and B demonstrate AP and lateral weight bearing foot radiographs in a patient with grade 2 hallux rigidus. The patient has less than 50% joint narrowing, no subchondral cysts, a large dorsal osteophyte, and less than 25% of joint involvement on the lateral view.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: Plantar release is indicated only in those patients with loss of motion but no radiographic changes (Grade 0 disease)
Answer 3: Interpositional arthroplasty can be used in those patients with advanced hallux rigidus who are strongly opposed to arthrodesis
Answer 4: Arthrodesis is not indicated based on both the clinical exam and imaging findings. Arthrodesis is appropriate for patients with grade 4 disease and certain patients with grade 3 hallux rigidus
Answer 5: The indications for prosthetic joint replacement are unclear. While it is performed for patients with advanced hallux rigidus, the failure rates are high and patients are then faced with complex revision arthrodesis with bone loss and shortening of the MTP joint
