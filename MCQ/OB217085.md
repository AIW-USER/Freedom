## Question

A 5-year-old male falls from the monkey bars while playing at school and sustains a type 3 supracondylar humerus fracture. The patient subsequently undergoes closed reduction and percutaneous pinning. Unfortunately, they miss their next two appointments secondary to vacation. The patient ultimately returns to the office and is found to have healed in varus. Which of the following is most likely to occur in this patient if no other treatment is done?

## Options

1. Decreased elbow range of motion
2. Limited external rotation of the forearm
3. Posteromedial rotatory subluxation of the elbow
4. Poor appearance and normal function of the elbow
5. Tardy anterior interosseous nerve palsy

## Correct option

**4. Poor appearance and normal function of the elbow**

## Answer explanation

Cubitus varus may occur following malunion of supracondylar humerus fractures and is primarily a cosmetic deformity without functional limitations.

Cubitus varus is one of the most common complications of supracondylar humerus fractures managed with either casting or pinning. Cubitus varus most often occurs when the distal fragment is medially displaced, internally rotated, and left in extension. The resulting malunion is thought to be primarily a cosmetic concern, however, delayed posterolateral elbow rotatory instability, as well as internal rotation deficits of the forearm, have been reported.

*O’Driscoll et al review tardy posterolateral rotatory instability of the elbow in patients with cubits varus.* **They report** that the mechanical axis, the olecranon, and the triceps all have a more medial pull secondary to the cubitus varus. **They conclude** that this deformity may result in stretching of the lateral collateral ligament and subsequent posterolateral rotatory instability.

*Abzug et al review the management of supracondylar humerus fractures in children.* **They report** that cubitus varus mainly produces a cosmetic deformity, but that risk exists for delayed posterolateral rotatory instability. Given these two findings, **the authors conclude** that corrective osteotomy should be discussed with patients who heal in this position.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1 & 2: Cubitus varus is not typically functionally limiting, however, patients may experience difficulty with internal rotation of the forearm secondary to overgrowth of the lateral elbow. Flexion and external rotation are not typically affected.
Answer 3: Posterolateral, not posteromedial rotatory instability may result following stretching of the lateral ligamentous complex.
Answer 5: Tardy ulnar nerve palsy has been reported in patients with cubitus varus as well as those with lateral condyle fractures. AIN palsy, however, more commonly occurs at the time of injury with extension-type supracondylar humerus fractures.
