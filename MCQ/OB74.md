## Question

A 10-month-old male is diagnosed with a femoral shaft fracture as seen in Figure A. The parents can not clearly explain the cause of the injury and the child has several other bruises on the right arm. In addition to femur x-rays, what radiologic study is most appropriate?

![[q74-a-infant-femur-fx.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. whole body SPECT scan
2. MRI of the brain and spine
3. CT of the chest, abdomen and pelvis
4. skeletal survey
5. right forearm x-ray

## Correct option

**4. skeletal survey**

## Answer explanation

The study of choice to evaluate suspected child abuse is a skeletal survey.

The skeletal survey is the gold standard in detecting additional injuries that may be associated with child abuse. The skeletal survey generally includes frontal views of the appendicular skeleton (upper and lower extremities) and frontal and lateral views of the axial skeleton (skull, spine, ribs). Any additional findings on skeletal survey can be followed by more detailed exams as needed.

*Kocher et al. provide a review of pediatric abuse.* *They cite recommendations from the American Academy of Pediatrics that a skeletal survey is the study of choice for cases of suspected abuse.* **They recommend** obtaining a skeletal survey for all patients younger than age 3 years, and selected patients between ages between 3 and 5. **They state** after age 5 the skeletal survey becomes less important because physical exam can often be used to locate sites of injury and focused radiographs can be obtained.

Figure A is a radiograph demonstrating a mid-shaft femur fracture in an infant. A femur fracture in a child prior to walking age, as in this case presentation, is considered pathognomonic for abuse. Illustration A is list of radiographs that should be obtained for a thorough skeletal survey. Exact protocols may vary per institution.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: SPECT scan can help show other injuries, has been described for evaluating suspected cases of child abuse, and is considered more sensitive than skeletal survey. However, disadvantages of bone scanning include cost, radionuclide exposure, lack of specificity, and limited availability and expertise. Skeletal survey is the preferred study.
Answer 2: MRI of the brain and spine is more expensive, may require sedation, and is not the best study to evaluate for bony injuries. Also, this study would not allow evaluation of the pelvis or extremities.
Answer 3: CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis would require a higher dosage of radiation, and would not allow evaluation of the extremities.
Answer 5: Although bruising is noted on the right forearm, an isolated study would not adequately evaluate for other bony injuries related to abuse.
