## Question

A patient presents to your office with lateral elbow pain and instability that worsens with varus and external rotation stress exam maneuvers. Which primary static stabilizers of the elbow may be incompetent in this patient?

## Options

1. ulnohumeral joint, lateral ulnar collateral ligament, annular ligament, and radial collateral ligament
2. radiocapitellar joint, lateral ulnar collateral ligament, radial collateral ligament and annular ligament
3. ulnohumeral joint, anterior bundle of the ulnar collateral ligament, lateral ulnar collateral ligament and radial collateral ligament
4. lateral ulnar collateral ligament, accessory collateral ligament, annular ligament and radial collateral ligament
5. radiocapitellar joint, ulnohumeral joint, lateral ulnar collateral ligament and transverse ligament

## Correct option

**4. lateral ulnar collateral ligament, accessory collateral ligament, annular ligament and radial collateral ligament**

## Answer explanation

There are 3 primary static stabilizers of the elbow: the ulnohumeral articulation, the anterior bundle of the ulnar collateral ligament (UCL), and the lateral collateral ligament complex. Of these, the lateral collateral ligament complex is the primary constraint against varus and external rotation force vectors. The components of the lateral collateral ligament complex include the radial collateral ligament (RCL), lateral ulnar collateral ligament (LUCL), accessory collateral ligament, and annular ligament (Answer 4).

The entire lateral collateral ligament complex is derived from a capsular thickening which aids in elbow stability. Injury to this complex can be sustained during a simple elbow dislocation or fracture-dislocations such as terrible triad injuries. Physical examination in the setting of a lateral collateral ligament complex injury will likely reveal pain and laxity with external rotation and varus stress testing. Other provocative exam maneuvers include the lateral pivot-shift test, apprehension test, chair-rise test, and table-top relocation test.

*McKee et al. sought to determine the pathoanatomy of lateral collateral ligament complex disruptions in simple dislocations and fracture-dislocations about the elbow.* *The study cohort was comprised of 42 men and 19 women (mean age: 43 years), who underwent surgery on average 15 days post-injury.* Among the elbows with associated fractures, including coronoid, radial head, proximal ulna, and distal humerus fractures, examination of the lateral collateral ligament complex revealed uniform disruption in all elbows, exhibiting various injury patterns. These findings underscore the consistent presence of LCL disruption, with avulsion from the distal humerus being the most prevalent pattern.

*Mehta et al.* **demonstrated** that injury to the LUCL permits abnormal supination of the ulna on the humerus, causing the radial head to subluxate posteriorly leading to posterolateral rotatory instability (PLRI) of the elbow. PLRI can be concisely described as a post-traumatic condition characterized by a three-dimensional displacement pattern involving abnormal external rotatory subluxation of the ulna and valgus displacement on the humeral trochlea.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The ulnohumeral articulation is a primary static stabilizer of the elbow but is not part of the lateral collateral ligament complex.
Answer 2: The radiocapitellar articulation is a secondary static constraint and is not part of the lateral collateral ligament complex.
Answer 3: The anterior bundle of the ulnar collateral ligament is a primary static stabilizer of the elbow but is not part of the lateral collateral ligament complex and does not resist varus and external rotation force vectors.
Answer 5: The transverse ligament is part of the ulnar collateral ligament complex, not the lateral collateral ligament complex.
