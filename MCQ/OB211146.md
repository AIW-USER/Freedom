## Question

A 72-year-old patient fell from standing height approximately 10 days ago. The patient's neck pain continued to worsen over the last 10 days but has not experienced any associated arm pain or weakness, nor any loss of dexterity. Figure A is her CT scan of the cervical spine during the office visit. Surgical treatment is recommended, but the patient prefers to avoid surgery. The patient is at an increased risk of which of the following complications with nonoperative treatment as opposed to operative treatment?

![[q211146-a-google-ct-cervical-spine.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Vertebral artery occlusion
2. Fracture nonunion
3. Dysphagia
4. Progressive Myelopathy
5. Cervical Radiculopathy

## Correct option

**2. Fracture nonunion**

## Answer explanation

This patient sustained a type 2 odontoid fracture and is at greatest risk for nonunion. Risk factors present for nonunion in this case are the delay to treatment, elderly age, greater than 1 mm of fracture gapping, and the presence of posterior displacement.

Odontoid fractures can be classified using the Anderson and D'Alonzo classification system. Type 2 fractures involving the waist of the odontoid, which is a vascular watershed region between the carotid and vertebral arteries; making these injuries high risk for nonunion. Risk factors for nonunion include initial displacement >6 mm, age >50 years, fracture comminution, fracture gap >1 mm, angulation >10 degrees, delay in treatment >4 days, posterior re-displacement >2 mm, and being a smoker.

*Fehlings et al.* *2013 examined the outcomes of 159 patients over 65 years old with type 2 odontoid fractures.* Non-union rates were 21% and 5% in the non-surgical and surgical groups respectively, but nonunion was not associated with pooer outcomes. Treatment failure occurred at a much higher rate in their study in both the surgical and non-surgical groups, 30% and 70% respectively. Initial nonoperative management, male sex, and older age were associated with a greater risk of treatment failure.

*Schroeder et al.* *2015 analyzed 21 articles including 1233 patients on geriatric odontoid fractures.* The forest plots generated in their paper indicated higher short- and long-term mortality in nonoperatively treated patients. Furthermore, the only difference between surgical approaches was increased fusion rates with posterior arthrodesis.

Figure A is a sagittal CT scan of the cervical spine demonstrating a type 2 odontoid fracture. There is greater than 1 mm of displacement of the fracture and angulation >10 degrees. Illustration A demonstrates the location of type 2 odontoid fracture.

Incorrect answers:
1- Vertebral artery occlusion has not been reported as a complication of nonoperative treatment for type 2 odontoid fractures.
3- Dysphagia has been found to occur more frequently with operative treatment than nonoperative treatment, however, this finding has not been significant.
4- Progressive myelopathy has not been associated with nonoperative treatment specifically.
5- Cervical radiculopathy has not been associated with nonoperative treatment specifically.
