## Question

Double thoracic curve means

## Options

1. Structural Main Thoracic curve and Non-structural Proximal thoracic and thoracolumbar curves
2. Structural (major) Main Thoracic curve and Proximal thoracic and Non-structural Thoracolumbar curves
3. Structural Main Thoracic curve, Non-structural Proximal thoracic and Structural Thoracolumbar curves (major)
4. Non-Structural Main Thoracic curve, Non-structural Proximal thoracic and Structural Thoracolumbar curves (major)

## Correct option

**2. Structural (major) Main Thoracic curve and Proximal thoracic and Non-structural Thoracolumbar curves**

## Answer explanation

Ans: B

Scoliosis is characterized by an abnormal lateral curvature of the spine > 10 degrees (Cobb’s angle).

The most pronounced curve is usually the one at which the main structural abnormality is present and is termed the primary curve, major curve or the structural curve.

Secondary curves, minor curves and non-structural curves, all usually refer to the less pronounced compensatory curves that develop above and/or below the primary curve to maintain balance.

Now, Howard King presented first presented his classification system (1983) for adolescent idiopathic scoliosis (AIS) known as King Moe classification. Curves were divided into five types to frame guidelines for management but mainly the coronal deformity was considered. Sagittal plane balance was not addressed. Now, scoliosis is a three-dimensional deformity having developmental anomaly in all planes (sagittal, coronal and frontal) so the classification went out of favor and new system (Lenke classification) gained popularity.

Lenke classification provided a much more reliable tool and better assessment (though it still does not address the rotational component).

In order to define a curve type by the Lenke classification, one must identify the curve type, the lumbar modifier and, for the first time in this classification, the sagittal profile was also included (thoracic sagittal modifier), see attached figure.

So, the classification has three variables:

- Curve Types
- Lumbar spine modifier
- Thoracic sagittal modifier

CURVE TYPES

(1) Major curve: the curve of greatest magnitude and is always structural.

(2) Minor curve: a smaller curve which may be structural or nonstructural.

(3) Nonstructural curve: a curve which bends to less than 25° on side bending radiographs.

According to these definitions there are six different curve types:

- Type 1: main thoracic (MT) is the only structural curve while the others (proximal thoracic and lumbar or thoracolumbar) are nonstructural.
- Type 2: double thoracic in which the MT is the major curve, the proximal thoracic (PT) is the minor curve but is structural and the thoracolumbar (TL) or lumbar (L) curves are minor and nonstructural.
- Type 3: double major curve pattern in which the MT is the major curve and the lumbar is the minor one but is structural whereas the PT is nonstructural.
- Type 4: triple major curve pattern when the MT is the major curve but all three curves are structural.
- Type 5: the TL or L curve is the major and only structural curve, with the PT and\or MT curves being minor and nonstructural.
- Type 6: the TL or L curve is the major curve measuring at least 5° more than the MT which is the minor but structural curve.
