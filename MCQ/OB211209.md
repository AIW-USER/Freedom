## Question

Figure A is the radiograph of a 36-year-old female that presents with a high-steppage gait and claw toes. On genetic testing, she has duplication of the PMP gene on chromosome 17. Which of the following answers correctly identifies relative muscle strengths in this patient population?

![[q211209-a-screen-shot-2020-01-23-at-10-07-52-am.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Weak anterior tibialis and weak peroneus longus
2. Weak peroneus longus and weak brevis
3. Normal anterior tibialis and weak peroneus longus
4. Weak peroneus brevis and normal posterior tibialis
5. Normal peroneus longus and weak posterior tibialis

## Correct option

**4. Weak peroneus brevis and normal posterior tibialis**

## Answer explanation

In the patients with a cavovarus foot deformity due to Charcot Marie Tooth (CMT) disease, the peroneus brevis is weak, and the posterior tibialis has normal strength.

Patients with CMT disease develop cavovarus foot deformity due to muscle balance. The first ray is driven into plantarflexion due to normal peroneus longus strength relative to a weak anterior tibialis. Hindfoot varus is driven by normal posterior tibialis function relative to a weak peroneus brevis. Additionally, these patients frequently have forefoot claw toe deformity due to weakness of the intrinsic muscles (lumbricals and interosseous) and relatively normal strength of the flexor and extensor muscles. The result of the cavovarus foot deformity is locking of the transverse tarsal joint during the stance phase (normally flexible in stance) and overloading of the lateral column.

*Younger et al. review the adult cavovarus foot.* **They report** that the cavovarus foot due to CMT may develop overload of both the lateral column of the foot and the first metatarsal head due to the strong pull of the peroneus longus. **They conclude** that a dorsiflexion osteotomy is frequently indicated in the CMT cavovarus foot.

*Alexander et al. also review the management of the cavovarus CMT foot.* **They report** that unilateral foot involvement in CMT is rare and should prompt the clinician to look for other etiologies (e.g., poliomyelitis, trauma, etc.). **They conclude** that addressing the forefoot etiology of the CMT cavovarus foot is critical in the treatment of these patients.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: peroneus longus has normal strength in CMT
Answer 2: peroneus longus has normal strength and peroneus brevis is weak
Answer 3: anterior tibialis is weak in CMT, and peroneus longus is normal
Answer 5: posterior tibialis is normal in CMT
