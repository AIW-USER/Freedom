## Question

A 55-year-old male presents to the office for evaluation of right foot pain. He reports feeling that he is always "walking on a marble". Pain is reproduced with palpation in the webspace and a Mulder's click is present on exam. Radiographs obtained in the office are unremarkable. Which of the following is true of the treatment of this condition?

## Options

1. Corticosteroid injections do not provide long-term relief
2. Percutaneous nerve ablation is more effective than open neurectomy at reducing pain
3. Orthotics with increased arch height are the first line of treatment
4. Transecting the transverse metatarsal ligament at the time of surgery reduces success rates
5. Traction neuritis is the most common complication of surgery

## Correct option

**1. Corticosteroid injections do not provide long-term relief**

## Answer explanation

To date, corticosteroid injections and alcohol injection have not been found to reliably provide long-term relief in the treatment of interdigital neuromas.

Interdigital neuromas, also known as Morton's neuromas, are compressive neuropathies of the interdigital nerve that often lead to plantar forefoot pain. Diagnosis is made clinically by the presence of tenderness with palpation of the plantar aspect of the webspace. First line treatment consists of a widened shoe box with metatarsal pad. Corticosteroid injections have been shown to provide short term symptomatic relief, but evidence of long-term improvement from any type of injection is lacking. Surgical neurectomy may be performed through either a dorsal or plantar approach for those who do not improve with conservative measures.

Lizano-Díez et al review corticosteroid injections as a treatment for interdigital neuromas. They report that nearly 50% of patients requested neuroma excision following treatment with injections. They conclude that the injection of a corticosteroid plus a local anesthetic was not superior to a local anesthetic alone in terms of pain and function improvement in patients with Morton's neuroma.

*Gurdezi et al review alcohol injections for the treatment of interdigital neuromas.* **They report** that only a minority of patients (29%) received long-term symptomatic improvement. **They conclude** that alcohol injection does not offer a permanent resolution of symptoms for most patients and can be associated with considerable morbidity.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Percutaneous nerve ablation is not used in the treatment of interdigital neuromas.
Answer 3: Orthotics with a wide shoe-box and metatarsal pads are used as first-line treatment.
Answer 4: The transverse metatarsal ligament should be resected to improve visualization at the time of surgery. It should be repaired during closure to prevent instability postoperatively.
Answer 5: Inadequate resection is the most common complication following surgical neurectomy.
