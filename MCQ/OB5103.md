## Options

1. Klippel-Feil syndrome
2. Nondystrophic scoliosis in neurofibromatosis Type 1
3. Nondystrophic scoliosis in neurofibromatosis Type 2
4. Dystrophic scoliosis in neurofibromatosis Type 2
5. Dystrophic scoliosis in neurofibromatosis Type 1

## Correct option

**5. Dystrophic scoliosis in neurofibromatosis Type 1**

## Answer explanation

This patient presents with dystrophic scoliosis in neurofibromatosis (NF) Type 1.

Neurofibromatosis type 1 (NF-1) is an autosomal dominant disease. Spinal deformity is considered the most common skeletal manifestation of NF-1 with an incidence ranging from 10 to 60%. Scoliosis is not usually found in NF-2. Deformities occurs in two forms: i. Nondystrophic scoliosis are regular shaped, gentle curves similar to idiopathic scoliosis, and ii. Dystrophic scoliosis are short [~4–6 vertebrae], progressive, and sharp angular curves that are commonly located in the thoracolumbar spine. Axillary freckling is common in both NF1 or NF2.

*Crawford et al. reviewed neurofibromatosis in children.* **They report** that nondystrophic scoliosis is more common than dystrophic scoliosis. The more severe the dystrophic changes, the more rapid are the deterioration and curve progression.

*Koptan et al. reviewed 32 surgical cases of severe, rigid dystrophic neurofibromatosis curves.* *All patients had a two-staged procedure; an anterior release/fusion followed by posterior correction/instrumentation augmented by sub-laminar wires.* There were 5/32 complications (15.6%) with no hardware failures or implant-related complications with this technique.

Figure A shows a clinical picture showing a severe spinal deformity. Figure B shows an AP X-ray revealing a dystrophic, thoracolumbar curve with an apex to the left. Dystrophic changes may include: vertebral scalloping, rib pencilling, vertebral wedging, paraspinal soft-tissue mass, sharp curves, and dysplastic pedicles.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Klippel-Feil syndrome does not present with axillary freckling. The scoliosis is usually congenital with pathologic curves. Examination of the chest wall may show possible rib anomalies, such as multiple rib fusions and Sprengel's Deformity. MRI spine may show cord compression and syringomyelia.
Answers 2,3: Nondystrophic scoliosis tends to behave like adolescent idiopathic scoliosis. These are usually regular shaped, gentle curves similar to idiopathic scoliosis.
Answers 4: Scoliosis is not usually found in NF-2.
