## Question

A 19-year-old female cross-country runner presents to the clinic with progressing right lateral foot pain. In addition to imaging, you elect to obtain several labs. Which of the following statements best describes the relationship between the female athlete triad and thyroid dysfunction?

## Options

1. Thyroid dysfunction is a core component of the Female Athlete Triad, directly linked to menstrual dysfunction and low energy availability.
2. Thyroid dysfunction is not directly part of the Female Athlete Triad but may occur as a secondary consequence of the metabolic and hormonal disruptions associated with it.
3. Thyroid dysfunction is unrelated to the Female Athlete Triad and does not impact the energy availability or menstrual irregularities seen in affected athletes.
4. Thyroid dysfunction is the primary cause of the Female Athlete Triad and directly leads to decreased bone mineral density.
5. Thyroid dysfunction is always present in athletes with the Female Athlete Triad and is necessary for its diagnosis.

## Correct option

**2. Thyroid dysfunction is not directly part of the Female Athlete Triad but may occur as a secondary consequence of the metabolic and hormonal disruptions associated with it.**

## Answer explanation

Female Athlete Triad consists of three specific interrelated conditions: low energy availability (with or without disordered eating), menstrual dysfunction, and decreased bone mineral density. Thyroid dysfunction is not a core component but may arise secondarily due to the metabolic imbalances associated with the Triad (answer 2).

The Female Athlete Triad is a medical condition observed in physically active women and is characterized by the interrelationship of three components: low energy availability (with or without disordered eating), menstrual dysfunction, and decreased bone mineral density. These issues arise from prolonged energy imbalance, where caloric intake does not meet the demands of exercise and daily functioning. The Triad can lead to serious health consequences, including amenorrhea, osteopenia or osteoporosis, and impaired athletic performance. Early recognition and intervention, focusing on restoring energy balance and addressing underlying issues, are critical for preventing long-term complications and promoting overall well-being in affected athletes.

*Wright et al. performed a systematic review investigating risk factors for stress fracture.* **The authors identified** previous history of stress fractures and female sex as the most strongly supported risk factors. These results highlight the importance of recognizing these factors for prevention and management strategies in at-risk patients.

*Nazem et al. performed a systematic review of articles containing the female athlete triad, reviewing diagnosis via screening during physical examinations as well as laboratory and imaging evaluation for menstrual dysfunction, low energy availability, and low bone mineral density.* **They state** that potential complications include possible infertility, decreased immune function, cardiovascular disease, and irreversible loss of bone mineral density. **They concluded** that prevention, early recognition, and a multidisciplinary treatment team with a focus on proper nutrition and the natural return of menses are vital.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: While thyroid function can influence menstrual cycles and metabolism, it is not inherently part of the Triad’s definition.
Answer 3: Thyroid dysfunction is related to the Triad, albeit indirectly. While not part of its diagnostic criteria, thyroid imbalances may emerge as a secondary issue due to the hormonal and metabolic effects of chronic low energy availability. Moreover, thyroid dysfunction can exacerbate menstrual irregularities and metabolic disturbances, so it is incorrect to claim it is unrelated.
Answer 4: Thyroid dysfunction is not the primary cause of the Female Athlete Triad. The primary driver of the Triad is low energy availability, which disrupts the hypothalamic-pituitary-ovarian axis, leading to menstrual dysfunction and decreased bone mineral density. While thyroid dysfunction can contribute to bone health issues, it is not the underlying cause of the Triad.
Answer 5: Thyroid dysfunction is not always present in athletes with the Female Athlete Triad and is not a criterion for its diagnosis
