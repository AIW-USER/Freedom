## Question

A midfoot arthrodesis would be best indicated for which of the following clinical scenarios?

## Options

1. 47-year-old with acute nutcracker cuboid injury
2. 38-year-old diabetic with worsening equinus contracture
3. 82-year-old with talonavicular arthrodesis nonunion
4. 24-year-old with chronic ulcers under the 2nd and 3rd metatarsal heads
5. 55-year-old with chronic ligamentous Lisfranc injury

## Correct option

**5. 55-year-old with chronic ligamentous Lisfranc injury**

## Answer explanation

Midfoot arthrodesis is indicated for chronic Lisfranc injuries as well as with purely ligamentous injuries to this region of the foot.

Considering that his injury is listed as chronic, the ligament will not heal in a functional way even if joint position is restored. The most reasonable treatment option is a midfoot arthrodesis. Cast immobilization can be useful for patients with partial rupture of the Lisfranc ligament with no joint displacement. Closed reduction and percutaneous fixation is an option for acute, reducible injuries. Open reduction, with either spanning-bridge fixation or transarticular screw fixation, is appropriate for acute, displaced injuries.

*Watson et al. review Lisfranc injuries and stress the importance of prompt diagnosis and treatment to avoid risks of arthritis.* **They report** percutaneous fixation is only appropriate when the joints are anatomically reduced.

*Desmond et al. provide an overall review of the Lisfranc complex, injury patterns, and treatment methods.* **They report** that the best outcomes are associated with open reduction and internal fixation, and postoperative weightbearing protocol should consist of non-weightbearing for 6-8 weeks, followed by full weightbearing in a cast or boot for an additional 6 weeks.

Illustration A is a non-weightbearing radiograph of a chronic, painful Lisfranc ligamentous injury. There is disruption of the intercuneiform (med/middle) and 1st-2nd MT base. Illustration B is a radiograph of a well-healed Lisfranc arthrodesis.

Incorrect Answers:
1: Nutcracker cuboid injuries require restoration of lateral column length with either internal or external fixation.
2: Equinus contracture is most commonly treated with stretching, percutaneous Achilles fractional lengthening, or gastrocnemius recession.
3: A failed arthrodesis generally requires revision of that joint, not adjacent joints.
4: Chronic ulceration under the 2nd/3rd metatarsal heads is typically a result of overload due to relative plantarflexion of these metatarsals or from relative dorsiflexion of the 1st ray.
