## Question

A 36-year-old carpenter undergoes "elbow ligament reconstruction" for chronic elbow instability. During early rehabilitation postoperatively, he is instructed to avoid varus arm positioning. Which of the following ligaments in Figure A was likely reconstructed?

![[q214120-a-screen-shot-2020-05-23-at-9-37-46-pm.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. A
2. B
3. C
4. D
5. E

## Correct option

**5. E**

## Answer explanation

Varus arm positioning would place increased stress on a lateral ulnar collateral ligament (LUCL) reconstruction and should be avoided in the early rehabilitation phases. The LCL is depicted by "E" in Figure A.

Injury to the LUCL results in posterolateral rotatory instability, which may occur after a fall on the outstretched hand, generating an axial load, valgus force, and an external rotational moment (supination) about the forearm with respect to the humerus. The LUCL helps prevent external rotatory subluxation of the ulna relative to the humerus and stabilizes the elbow against varus loads. Thus, positioning the arm in the gravity-loaded varus position is typically avoided in the first 6 to 12 weeks after LUCL injuries and after LUCL reconstruction to avoid putting tensile stresses on lateral elbow structures.

*Manocha et al. investigated the effects of several variables used in LUCL injury rehabilitation on elbow stability.* **They reported** that elbow stability was enhanced by overhead positioning, active motion, and pronation relative to the other arm positions. **They recommended** that beginning earlier range of motion in these positions decreases elbow stiffness and allows optimal ligament healing. **They highlighted** that varus positioning be avoided.

*Alolabi et al. investigated a safe rehabilitation protocol for elbow dislocations with MCL and LUCL injuries.* **They reported** significant elbow instability with varus and valgus orientations using both active and passive motion. **They concluded** that active motion with the injured MCL and LCL in the horizontal and vertical orientations resulted in kinematics similar to the intact elbow.

Figure A is a schematic of the elbow collateral ligaments. Illustration A is the labeled version of Figure A.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Label A depicts the annular ligament, which is part of the lateral collateral ligament complex. However, this is not part of the LUCL reconstruction.
Answer 2-4: Avoidance of forearm varus would not be indicated in anterior, posterior, and transverse MCL reconstruction.
