## Question

A 55-year-old accountant who plays in a masters league for tennis returns continuing to complain of nonradiating medial sided elbow pain. You have been treating him for 2 months and despite taking a one month break from tennis and doing physical therapy, wearing a counterforce brace, and taking scheduled ibuprofen, he is denying any improvement and is quite frustrated. What is the next most reasonable treatment option?

## Options

1. Elbow arthroscopy and selective debridement
2. Corticosteroid injection into the common flexor tendon peritendinous area
3. Corticosteroid injection into the cubital tunnel
4. Accupuncture
5. EMG

## Correct option

**2. Corticosteroid injection into the common flexor tendon peritendinous area**

## Answer explanation

Medial sided elbow pain consistent with medial epicondylitis and refractory to therapy, NSAIDS, bracing and activity modification, it is reasonable to offer a corticosteroid injection in the common flexor tendon.

Medial epicondylitis is a common condition among individuals aged 40-60 years whose activities involve repeated forearm pronation and wrist flexion. Repeated eccentric loading causing microtrauma at the tendon origin sets in motion the degenerative process initially marked by peritendinous inflammation followed by angiofibrous hyperplasia and eventually fibrosis. Initial noninvasive treatment options involve NSAIDs and cessation of provoking activities, followed by physical therapy and counterforce bracing. Extracorpeal shockwave therapy has been used with equivocal results. Local corticosteroid injections have been used effectively with the most benefit seen on a short-term basis. Surgery is reserved until a patient has failed 4-6 months of the above conservative treatment, unless they are an elite level athlete and have a definitive tear of the tendon.

*Cain et al. reviewed common elbow pathology in the overhead athlete.* *They discuss the need to differentiate between injuries of the flexor-pronator tendon mass and ulnar collateral ligament, the later presenting with pain posterior and distal to the medial epicondyle.* Strains and inflammation of the flexor mass for the overhead athlete usually respond to period of rest, anti-inflammatories and therapy addressing any imbalances in the throwing kinetic chain. If persistent pain remains despite these modalities, this should raise suspicion for other underlying pathology. Corticosteroid injections can be used in chronic cases and surgery is rarely necessitated.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1. Surgery is indicated for cases of medial epicondylitis refractory to conservative treatment after at least 4-6 months of treatment in the compliant patient. These procedures are done in an open fashion not arthroscopically
Answers 3 and 5. Though cubital tunnel syndrome can occur concomitantly, there is no indication that the patient is having ulnar nerve symptoms.
Answer 4. Acupuncture has not been shown to be of benefit
