## Question

Not true about ceramic-on-ceramic bearing

## Options

1. Stripe wear
2. Squeaking
3. Impingement
4. High wear rate

## Correct option

**4. High wear rate**

## Answer explanation

Characteristics of ceramic-on-ceramic bearings

- Impingement between the femoral neck and rim of the ceramic acetabular component creates problems unique to this type of articulation.
- Lowest wearing rate: The linear wear rate of alumina-on-alumina has been shown to be 4000 times less than cobalt-chrome alloy-on-polyethylene.
- Stripe wear: It is specific to ceramic-on-ceramic bearings. This term describes a long, narrow area of damage resulting from contact between the head and the edge of the ceramic liner. Squeaking/noisy movement: Enthusiasm for ceramic-on-ceramic implants has recently been somewhat tempered by reports of reproducible noise, particularly squeaking. The onset of squeaking usually occurs more than 1 year after implantation, and the development of strip wear has been implicated in noise generation. Incidence up to 10 % has been reported. The etiology of squeaking has not been fully elucidated and is likely multifactorial.

Answer: 4
