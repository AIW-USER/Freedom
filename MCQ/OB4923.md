## Question

A 7-year-old with a history of an elbow injury treated conservatively presents for evaluation of ongoing elbow pain. The coronal alignment of her elbows in extension is symmetric. On physical examination, she has full, but painful range of motion of her elbow. She has tenderness at the lateral elbow. She is able to cross her fingers without difficulty. A radiograph is shown in Figure A. What is the best definitive treatment plan for this patient?

![[q4923-a-lateralcondyle-nonunion.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Observation alone
2. Observation with splinting
3. Osseous fragment excision
4. Internal fixation of the nonunion
5. Ulnar nerve decompression

## Correct option

**4. Internal fixation of the nonunion**

## Answer explanation

Based on the clinical examination and radiographs shown, the patient has developed a painful lateral condylar nonunion. To address her pain, an ORIF of the lateral condylar nonunion should be completed.

Delay in diagnosis and treatment of lateral condylar fractures may result in a nonunion. Cubitus valgus may develop secondary to the fracture nonunion. As the deformity progresses, a tardy ulnar nerve palsy may develop. However, if no symptoms are present and non-union is the isolated issue, observation is the recommended treatment. If the non-union presents with instability, pain, or cubitus valgus with ulnar nerve palsy, then fixation of the fracture fragments is recommended.

*Toh et al. evaluated lateral condylar nonunion to ascertain long-term clinical outcome.* *Patients were separated into groups based on prior history of either Milch Type 1 or Type 2 injuries.* *Patients with Milch Type 1 were more likely to develop limitations in range of motion and limitations in functional outcome.*

*Shimada et al. reviewed the results of fixation of lateral condylar nonunions in 16 children.* Their indications for treatment were pain, instability, cubitus valgus and ulnar nerve dysfunction. Thirteen of the sixteen patients had osseous union after initial operation; most patients had good to excellent functional outcomes.

Figure A shows the radiograph of a lateral condylar nonunion.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1, 2: Observation is not indicated given the findings of a painful lateral condylar nonunion.
Answer 3: Fragment excision will lead to the development of a cubitus valgus deformity.
Answer 5: An ulnar nerve decompression is not indicated in this patient with intact ulnar nerve function.
