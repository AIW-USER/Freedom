## Question

Which of the following correctly identifies the internervous plane utilized for the Modified Judet approach to the scapula?

![[q215447-a-bp.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. A and B
2. B and I
3. L and J
4. A and E
5. C and F

## Correct option

**2. B and I**

## Answer explanation

The Judet approach to the posterior scapula utilizes the interval between infraspinatus (suprascapular nerve) and the teres minor (axillary nerve), as represented by B and I, respectively in Figure A.

Fractures of the scapular neck, scapular body, and posterior glenoid that require open reduction internal fixation are most commonly treated through the Judet approach. This approach utilizes a true intermuscular and internervous plane between the infraspinatus (suprascapular nerve) and the teres minor (axillary nerve). Care should be taken as the teres minor is retracted inferiorly, as injury may occur to the posterior branch of the axillary nerve. Likewise, as the infraspinatus is retracted superiorly, the suprascapular nerve and artery are at risk.

*Obremskey et al review the modified Judet approach to the scapula.* **They report** that the goals of the modified Judet approach are exposure of all bony elements of the scapula which have adequate bone stock for internal fixation, minimal trauma to the rotator cuff musculature, and protection of the major neurologic structures (suprascapular nerve superiorly and axillary nerve laterally). **They conclude** that the main advantage of this approach is the limited muscular dissection, which has the potential to improve functional outcomes.

*Cole et al reviewed the approaches to the posterior scapula.* **They report** that the Modified Judet approach allows adequate exposure, but avoids the significant soft tissue trauma that occurs with other more extensile posterior approaches. **They conclude** that reduction goals, wound closure, and postoperative care remain the same as for all posterior approaches. However, a certain subset of patients may benefit from tissue-sparing procedures.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1,3-5: The Judet approach utilizes the interval between the suprascapular nerve (Label B) and the axillary nerve (Label I).
