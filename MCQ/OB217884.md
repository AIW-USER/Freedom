## Question

A 67-year-old male undergoes a right-sided primary total knee arthroplasty without any complications. He was doing well for three years when he sustained a forward fall landing on a flexed right knee. He was seen two weeks later by his arthroplasty surgeon with an antalgic gait on presentation. On physical examination, there is ecchymosis overlying the anterior aspect of the knee, unhindered range of motion between 0-115 degrees, appears to be ligamentous intact and the patient could perform a straight leg raise without assistance. Radiographs showed a nondisplaced, transverse patella fracture. Which of the following is the best next step in management?

## Options

1. Immobilization in extension for 4-6 weeks
2. Open reduction internal fixation with cannulated screws and fiber wire tension band construct
3. Open reduction internal fixation with circumferential cerclage wiring alone
4. Revision total knee arthroplasty
5. Total patellectomy with extensor mechanism reconstruction

## Correct option

**1. Immobilization in extension for 4-6 weeks**

## Answer explanation

Nondisplaced patella periprosthetic fractures with an intact extensor mechanism and stable implants should be treated with non-operative management.

A periprosthetic fracture can be a devastating complication in the setting of total knee arthroplasty (TKA). While intraoperative fractures can be acutely fixed prior to leaving the operative theatre, later presenting fractures may necessitate operative intervention. Although rare, patella periprosthetic fractures can result in a loose patellar component and compromise the extensor mechanism. Nondisplaced TKA patellar periprosthetic fractures with an intact extensor mechanism and stable implants should undergo a trial of conservative management with restricted knee motion.

*Ritter et al. performed a retrospective review on over one thousand TKA procedures by their institution in which a lateral release was performed for intraoperative patellar maltracking at the time of initial TKA implantation.* **They found** that the lateral release often interrupts the superolateral genicular artery resulting in a significant increase in post-operative patellar fractures. **They concluded** that surgeons performing a lateral release at the time of index TKA implantation should avoid excessive patella stress for the first year post-operatively.

*Ng et al. performed a review of extensor mechanism failure in the setting of TKA and the various treatment options.* *They discuss common risk factors contributing to extensor mechanism failure to include component malrotation, excessive joint line elevation, and compromised blood supply to the patella.* **They recommend** non-operative management for the treatment of partial extensor mechanism tendon ruptures, extensor lags of less than 20°, and nondisplaced patellar fractures. Conversely, **the authors recommended** that acute tendinous ruptures and patella fractures with significant extensor lags should be repaired in the operating room to avoid poor long-term functional outcomes.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 2 and 3: Open reduction internal fixation would be appropriate if the patellar component seemed loose or if the patient had a clinically significant extensor lag.
Answer 4. Revision total knee arthroplasty would be appropriate if the patella button or other components seemed loose. Given this patient’s ambulating for 2 weeks with a fracture with a maintained range of motion, one can assume the prosthesis and patella button are stable.
Answer 5: Total patellectomy would be considered a salvage procedure. While partial patellectomy and tendon advancement may be required in certain settings, removing the entire patella is generally discouraged due to poor functional outcomes with >50% loss of extension strength.
