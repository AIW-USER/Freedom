## Question

A 63-year-old female presents to the ED after tripping on the stairs and falling on her shoulder. Radiographs demonstrate a 3-part proximal humerus fracture. You discuss management of these fractures with her and warn her that there is a risk of humeral head ischemia. What is the most prognostic radiographic risk factor for humeral head ischemia?

## Options

1. Displacement of 7 mm
2. 3-part valgus fracture pattern
3. Intact calcar length of 6 mm
4. Angulation of 30 degrees
5. Concomitant fracture involvement of the metadiaphyseal humerus

## Correct option

**3. Intact calcar length of 6 mm**

## Answer explanation

Based on Hertel's criteria, vascularity to the humeral head is less likely to be preserved if there is <8 mm of intact calcar attached to the humeral head after a fracture.

Proximal humerus fractures are common, especially in elder patients with osteoporotic bone. Humeral head ischemia, and in some cases, subsequent avascular necrosis can occur due to compromise of the blood supply after fracturing through the calcar. It is thought that a longer length of intact calcar attached to the head fragment after fracture leads to a lower risk of ischemia. Hertel established specific criteria to predict the risk for humeral head ischemia. Including the length of intact calcar, other risk factors include a disrupted medial hinge, increasing fracture complexity, displacement >10mm, and angulation >45 degrees. In cases where humeral head ischemia and post-traumatic arthritis develop, patients may require eventual shoulder arthroplasty.

*Hertel et al. evaluated 100 proximal humerus fractures in an attempt to predict risk factors for humeral head ischemia based on the fracture pattern.* **They noted** calcar length <8mm, disrupted medial hinge, and complex fracture pattern were good predictors of ischemia. When these 3 factors were combined PPV was up to 97% for humeral head ischemia occurring.

*Campochiaro et al. evaluated the ability of Hertel's criteria to predict humeral head necrosis.* The overall incidence of AVN was 3.7% and 30% of the patients in the AVN group met Hertel's criteria. While Hertel's criteria were important in understanding the risk for humeral head ischemia, **the authors concluded** that the criteria alone were not enough and the calcar had to be evaluated in all three planes.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Displacement >10mm is a risk factor for humeral head ischemia
Answer 2: While increasing fracture severity is a risk factor for humeral head ischemia, 3-part valgus fractures are not necessarily a specific risk factor for ischemia.
Answer 4: Angulation > 45 degrees is a risk factor for humeral head ischemia.
Answer 5: Concomitant involvement of the metadiaphyseal proximal humerus is not a risk factor for humeral head ischemia.
