## Question

A Patient of head injury presents 2 weeks later with a pulsatile proptosis, blurring of vision, conjunctival congestion (as shown). What is the most likely diagnosis [FNB 2023]

![[TO19356200-1.jpg]]

## Options

1. Sympathetic ophthalmitis
2. Carotico-cavernous fistula
3. Vitreous prolapsed
4. None

## Correct option

**2. Carotico-cavernous fistula**

## Answer explanation

Ans : B

EXPLANATION: -

Pulsatile proptosis, also known as pulsatile exophthalmos, AND can have many causes, including: -

• Carotid-cavernous fistula (CCF)

• Neurofibromatosis type 1 (NF1): A hereditary neurocutaneous disorder that can cause sphenoid dysplasia, which can lead to encephaloceles.

• Orbital roof fractures: Blunt trauma can cause orbital roof fractures, which can lead to traumatic intraorbital encephaloceles.

• Vascular orbital tumors: These can cause pulsatile proptosis.

• Metastatic tumor of orbit: This is a rare cause of pulsatile proptosis.

• Intraorbital meningocele: This can cause pulsatile proptosis.

• Other causes of proptosis include: encephalocele, arteriovenous malformation, and arachnoid cyst.

CAROTICO-CAVERNOUS FISTULA (CCF)

An abnormal connection between the carotid artery and the cavernous sinus.

Divided into direct (Type A) and indirect types (Type 2 B-D)

1. Type A: direct (high-flow) shunts between the internal carotid artery and cavernous sinus

 a) Traumatic (including iatrogenic): occur in 0.2% of patients with cranio cerebral trauma.

Iatrogenic: may follow percutaneous trigeminal rhizotomy, endovascular procedures.

b) Spontaneous: usually due to ruptured cavernous sinus ICA aneurysm. May also occur in patients with connective tissue disorders

2. Indirect (dural): most are shunts: from dural arteries that are branches of the external carotid (not from ICA) (exception: Type B) – low flow

- Type B: from meningeal branches of the internal carotid artery (ICA)

- Type C: from meningeal branches of the external carotid artery (ECA)

- Type D: from meningeal branches of both the ICA and ECA

• Clinical Presentation of CCF :

1. orbital and/or retro-orbital pain

2. chemosis (arteriolization of conjunctiva)

3. pulsatile proptosis

4. ocular and/or cranial bruit

5. deterioration of visual acuity: may be due to hypoxic retinopathy as a result of reduced arterial pressure and increased venous pressure and increased intraocular pressure

6. diplopia: abducens (VI Nerve) palsy is the most common

7. pupillary dilatation

8. ophthalmoplegia (usually unilateral, but m ay present initially as bilateral or may progress to bilateral)

9. increased intraocular pressure

10. neo-vascularization of the iris or retina

11. Rarely: SAH

• Evaluation :

- CT or MRI: usually demonstrates proptosis. Serpiginous and engorged intraocular vessels including the superior ophthalmic vein (best seen on T2WI coronals – helps to differentiate from rectus muscles) and convexity of lateral wall of cavernous sinus.

- Angiography: shunting of blood from ICA into cavernous sinus. Rapid opacification of petrosal sinus and/or ophthalmic vein may be seen.

• Treatment :

- 20–50% of low flow CCF spontaneously thrombose, therefore one may observe these as long as visual acuity is stable and intra-ocular pressure is < ≈ 25.

- Symptomatic (e.g. progressive visual deterioration) high-flow CCFs rarely resolve spontaneously, and urgent treatment is usually indicated.

- Treatment is usually in the form of embolization or trapping between surgically placed clips. Even if normal ocular motility cannot be achieved in affected eye, preservation of vision is desirable.

- Indications for treatment :

1.Proptosis

2. visual loss

3. cranial nerve VI palsy

4. intractable bruit

5. severely elevated intraocular pressure

6. increased filling of cortical veins on angiography

- Endovascular treatment Options include:

1. Electrolytically detachable coils

2. Amplatzer vascular plug

- With indirect fistulas, it is mandatory to place coils on the venous side (otherwise new feeders will be recruited). Coils or clips may be used to occlude direct fistulas.
