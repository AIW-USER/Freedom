## Question

A patient has a Proximal Tibia fracture for which dual plating was done six years ago. Patient has medial and lateral healed scars. But now has come for TKR. Which incision would you prefer to use?

## Options

1. Medial one
2. Lateral one
3. Use conventional TKR single midline incision ignoring previous
4. Use a transverse incision now

## Correct option

**2. Lateral one**

## Answer explanation

Ans: B
EXPLANATION: -

For displaced tibial plateau fractures, the standard of care is open reduction and internal fixation However, future total knee arthroplasty (TKA) is a consideration in these patients as post-traumatic osteoarthritis is a common long-term complication. In fact, the risk of TKA in patients with previous fracture of the tibial plateau is 5-times higher than matched controls from the general population, at 7.3% in the 10-year period following injury. TKA as primary treatment of tibial plateau fracture is also opted but mostly in elderly patients with poor bone stock who are shown to have high rates of post-traumatic arthritis and fixation failure.
When planning TKA for patients with prior fixation of tibial plateau fracture, surgeons must consider several factors. First of all, patients should undergo a standard and thorough preoperative evaluation. This includes a medical evaluation, full length alignment radiographs along with an infectious and nutrition workup. Next the surgeon should take into consideration the previous hardware present in the knee. not all hardware requires removal. Some authors suggest removing only the hardware that is going to directly interfere with the prosthesis. Exposure of the knee can be difficult, as post traumatic arthritis patients often have stiff knees. Certain maneuvers can be employed in different scenarios to provide excellent exposure as well as to manage injury to extensor mechanism and collateral ligaments. These include quadriceps, snip, VY turndown, tibial tubercle osteotomy and lateral retinacular release. Managing bony loss in these patients involves techniques similar to those for revision TKA including cement filling, morselized bone grafting and metal wedge augmentation, among others. There is also well-known benefit to using computer assistance (CAS) in total knee arthroplasty in such cases.
A special attention needs to be given to choice of skin incisions. Prior incisions should be approached carefully by using the most lateral incision and taking care not to create acute angles across prior transverse incisions. Careful attention should be taken to preserve the medial skin flap, as this is where the “perforators” arise. Basically, the blood supply of skin around knee comprises of extrinsic arteries (lying in subcutaneous layer) that include the saphenous artery plus muscular branches coming from medial side; and the intrinsic arteries (lying in subfascial layer) which are primarily the genicular arteries that form a ring around the patella. The extrinsic vessels in the superficial layer anastomose with the deep-seated intrinsic arteries through the “perforating arteries”, rather than anastomosing with each other. Now the extrinsic arteries mainly impact wound healing. So, when the skin incision is made more medially, more vascular disruption occurs on the lateral side. Too much dissection of the deep fascia compromises the perforating arteries to cause a wound healing problem (see Fig). Therefore, making the skin incision too far medially and too much subcutaneous dissection on the lateral side of the incision should be avoided.
