## Question

A 22-year-old college football player sustains a hit to the lower leg during a game and is found to have a Gustillo grade II open tibia fracture. He is splinted appropriately and brought to the emergency department by an ambulance. He had his initial tetanus vaccination series in his youth but doesn't think he has had a booster since he started high school. Which of the following treatment regimens would be most appropriate to decrease the risk of infection in this patient?

## Options

1. Bedside irrigation & debridement, no antibiotics, no tetanus
2. Bedside irrigation & debridement, ancef within 3 hours of injury, no tetanus
3. Bedside irrigation & debridement, ancef within 3 hours of injury, tetanus prophylaxis (Td)
4. Bedside irrigation & debridement, ancef within 5 hours of injury, tetanus prophylaxis (Td)
5. Bedside irrigation & debridement, ancef + gentamicin within 3 hours of injury, tetanus prophylaxis (Td)

## Correct option

**3. Bedside irrigation & debridement, ancef within 3 hours of injury, tetanus prophylaxis (Td)**

## Answer explanation

This athlete has a grade II open tibia fracture and should be managed with bedside irrigation and debridement, cephalosporin administration within 3 hours, and tetanus prophylaxis given the fact he has a grade II injury with no prophylaxis within the last 5 years.

Open fracture management is common when dealing with traumatic orthopedic injuries and must be well understood by orthopedic surgeons in order to obtain the best outcomes for patients with these injuries. The degree of soft tissue and bony injury are helpful in classifying the injury with regard to severity (Gustilo-Anderson classification). These fractures are at increased risk for infection due to their communication with the environment, therefore urgent irrigation and debridement to remove any gross contaminants is crucial. The most important predictor of decreasing infection risk, however, is early administration of IV antibiotics. In grade I and II injuries, a cephalosporin is recommended, while in grade III injuries the addition of an aminoglycoside to cover gram-negative bacteria is also recommended. Specific antibiotics used vary by location but the most important part is early administration. Though antibiotics should be administered within 1 hour of arrival, studies show that delaying administration more than 3 hours increases infection risk to 7.4% from 4.7%. With regard to tetanus prophylaxis, the degree of contamination and past history of vaccination are important to consider. Illustration A highlights these considerations in more detail.

*Melvin et al. reviewed the management of open tibial shaft fractures including evaluation and initial wound management.* **They note** that administration of antibiotics as soon as possible is crucial to decreasing the risk for infection; specifically, rates of infection dropped significantly if antibiotics were able to be given within 3 hours of injury. Thorough soft tissue and bony debridement as well as rigid bony stabilization are also recommended as soon as possible.

*Lack et al. looked specifically at more severe open tibia fractures, primarily those that were grade III.* *In their study, delayed wound coverage >5 days and delayed antibiotic administration were the two primary risk factors for infection.* **They note** that in some cases, antibiotics given prior to arrival to the hospital would be helpful in managing infection risk.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1,2,4,5: Bedside irrigation & debridement should always be performed as well as antibiotics within 3 hours and appropriate tetanus prophylaxis.
