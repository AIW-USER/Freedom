## Question

A 40-year-old male presents to your office for evaluation of his right hand. He has a known history of intravenous drug use which led to fasciotomies of the right forearm and hand six months prior for compartment syndrome after an overdose. While the limb was salvaged, the patient is being treated for an intrinsic plus hand. Which of the following is descriptive of this hand posturing?

MCPJ: Metacarpophalangeal joint
PIPJ: Proximal interphalangeal joint
DIPJ: Distal interphalangeal joint

## Options

1. MCPJ flexion with PIPJ & DIPJ flexion
2. MCPJ flexion with PIPJ & DIPJ extension
3. MCPJ extension with PIPJ & DIPJ extension
4. MCPJ extension with PIPJ & DIPJ flexion
5. MCPJ extension with PIPJ flexion & DIPJ extension

## Correct option

**2. MCPJ flexion with PIPJ & DIPJ extension**

## Answer explanation

This patient has intrinsic plus hand secondary to a history of compartment syndrome. This is characterized by MCPJ flexion with PIPJ & DIPJ extension.

An intrinsic plus hand is a hand posture characterized by MCPJ flexion with PIPJ and DIPJ extension. It is caused by an imbalance between spastic intrinsics and weak extrinsic muscles of the hand. Diagnosis is made by clinical examination. Treatment may be nonoperative or operative depending on the severity of the contracture and impact on quality of life.

*Paksima et al. reviewed contractures of the intrinsic muscles of the fingers.* **They reported** that the loss of muscle function primarily affects the interphalangeal joints but also may affect metacarpophalangeal joints. **They concluded** that the resulting clinical picture is often termed, intrinsic contracture or intrinsic-plus hand.

*Tosti et al. reviewed the intrinsic contracture of the hand.* **They reported** that this may result from trauma, spasticity, ischemia, rheumatologic disorders, or iatrogenic causes. **They noted** that in severe cases, the hand assumes a posture with hyperflexed metacarpophalangeal joints and hyperextended proximal interphalangeal joints as the contracted interossei and lumbrical muscles deform the natural cascade of the fingers. **They concluded** that nonsurgical management may be appropriate in mild cases and consists of occupational therapy, orthoses, and botulinum toxin injections, while surgical management is reserved for more severe contractures.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1,3,5: Intrinsic plus hand is characterized by MCPJ flexion with PIPJ & DIPJ extension
Answer 4: Intrinsic minus hand is a hand deformity characterized by MCPJ hyperextension with PIPJ & DIPJ flexion caused by an imbalance between strong extrinsics and deficient intrinsics
