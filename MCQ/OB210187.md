## Question

Figures A and B show the radiographs of a 55-year-old female with a history of atraumatic right hip pain. Her past medical history is significant for lung cancer successfully treated with immunotherapy. Follow up studies as recent as one year ago showed no progression of disease or metastatic lesions. Which of the following is the next best step in treatment?

![[q210187-a-right-hip-ap-impending.jpg]]
*Figure A*

![[q210187-b-right-hip-lateral-impending.jpg]]
*Figure B*

## Options

1. Chemotherapy
2. Radiation
3. Prophylactic intramedullary nail
4. Cemented hemiarthroplasty
5. Biopsy

## Correct option

**5. Biopsy**

## Answer explanation

The next best step in treatment is a biopsy of the subtrochanteric bone to provide a tissue for accurate diagnosis.

Bony lesions in the setting of prior malignancy should be biopsied to confirm the diagnosis. A new lesion in the setting of prior malignancy is an indication to repeat staging studies including CT chest/abdomen/pelvis in addition to a full body bone scan. Other indications for biopsy include aggressive-appearing bone or soft tissue lesions, lesions larger than 5 cm, lesions deep to the fascia, lesions overlying neurovascular structures, lesions that remain nondiagnostic after imaging, solitary bony lesions in patients with a history of malignancy and for confirmatory purposes. The biopsy should be performed by the surgeon who plans to treat the patient definitively.

*Piccioli et al. reviewed the literature on the epidemiology and management of bone metastasis with unknown origin.* **They state** that bone metastases from unknown origin with complete workup may be nondiagnostic even at autopsy. **They conclude** that epidemiologic data from the literature suggests the lungs should be the considered the probable site of primary carcinoma in the setting of bone metastases of unknown origin.

*Mankin et al. reported on the results of 597 patients at multiple institutions that underwent biopsy at a referring institution instead of the treating center and* **found** a diagnostic error of 17.8%. The study also found that there was a 19.3% rate of the treating surgeon needing to do a different, more complex procedure, or requiring additional chemotherapy or radiation due to an incorrect biopsy at a referring institution. In conclusion, errors, complications, and changes in the course and outcome for patients were 2-12x greater when biopsied at a referring center compared to the treatment center.

*Traina et al. performed a systematic literature review of 21 articles discussing the topic of biopsy of bone and soft-tissue primary tumors.* **They found** that core needle biopsy had greater diagnostic accuracy than fine needle aspiration. If a percutaneous biopsy is nondiagnostic, a small incisional biopsy should be performed.

Figure A and B show an AP and lateral of a right hip with a lytic lesion in the subtrochanteric region.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1 & 2: Chemotherapy and radiation would not be appropriate until an accurate tissue diagnosis has been made.
Answer 3 & 4: Orthopedic fixation should be delayed until a tissue diagnosis has been made and risks contamination of the limb with tumor cells.
