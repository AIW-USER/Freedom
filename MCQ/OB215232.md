## Question

Which of the following statements is true regarding prosthetic joint infection?

## Options

1. Lowest risk of PJI in revision THA
2. Highest risk of PJI in revision TKA
3. Lowest risk of PJI in revision hip and knee arthroplasty
4. Highest risk of PJI in primary THA
5. Lowest risk of PJI in primary TKA

## Correct option

**2. Highest risk of PJI in revision TKA**

## Answer explanation

Revision total knee arthroplasty has the highest risk for prosthetic joint infection (PJI) with a rate of 5-6% reported in the literature.

PJIs are highly morbid complications of adult reconstructive surgery and are associated with tremendous increased health care expenditures. Increased infection risk has been reported with revision arthroplasty compared to primary arthroplasty. Further, total knee arthroplasty has a higher reported rate of PJI than total hip arthroplasty. The highest rate of PJI has been reported in revision TKA (5-6%), followed by revision THA (3-4%), primary TKA (1-2%), and primary THA (0.3-1.3%). Acute infections can be successfully treated with irrigation and debridement with polyethylene exchange and antibiotics. Chronic infections usually necessitate two-stage revision due to biofilm formation on the prosthesis.

*Ottesen et al. performed a retrospective study of 67 patients with prosthetic joint infection following primary total knee arthroplasty treated with debridement, antibiotics, and implant retention.* **They reported** an 84% success rate at 2-years with treatment success not affected by the microorganism. **They concluded** that debridement, antibiotics, and implant retention to be an effective treatment option for prosthetic joint infection following primary total knee arthroplasty when performed early after the index procedure.

*Wyles et al. performed a retrospective study of 22705 patients undergoing total joint arthroplasty with 2576 patients that were cefazolin allergy-tested prior to surgery.* **They reported** 97% of the patients being cleared for cefazolin that were tested and a significantly decreased infection rate in patients given cefazolin for surgical prophylaxis (32% lower). **The authors concluded** that perioperative testing for true penicillin allergy can be a modifiable factor for prosthetic joint infections.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1, 3, 4, and 5: The risk of prosthetic joint infections from highest to lowest risk is as follows: revision TKA > revision THA > primary TKA > primary THA.
