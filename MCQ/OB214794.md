## Question

A 63-year-old female with no significant medical history undergoes an uncomplicated right total knee arthroplasty without the use of a tourniquet. She weighs 85 kg and has a BMI of 39 kg/m^2. What would be the optimal venous prophylaxis option from the following choices?

## Options

1. Aspirin 81 mg twice daily for 6 weeks
2. Aspirin 325 mg daily for 2 weeks
3. Low molecular weight heparin, weight based dosing, daily for 2 weeks
4. Warfarin with goal INR 2.5 - 3.5 for 6 weeks
5. Mobile SCDs only for 4 weeks

## Correct option

**1. Aspirin 81 mg twice daily for 6 weeks**

## Answer explanation

Of the following options, treatment with mechanical prophylaxis and aspirin 81 mg twice daily for the 6 weeks is the optimal choice.

Venous thromboembolism is a potentially lethal complication following hip and knee arthroplasty. In patients not treated with pharmacologic prophylaxis, the literature has reported DVT rates >50%. Strong treatment recommendations are lacking due to the absence of rigorous comparative studies. The American Academy of Orthopedic Surgeons (AAOS) and American College of Chest Physicians (ACCP) has provided guidelines for the prevention of venous thromboembolism (VTE). Both groups conceded that prophylaxis should consist of both mechanical and pharmacologic means. The ACCP recommended prophylaxis for a minimum of 10-14 days, but ideally up to 35 days in the outpatient setting. The preferred prophylaxis reported by the ACCP was LMWH, but aspirin is an acceptable alternative. Studies have shown the low-dose aspirin, 81 mg twice daily, has the same efficacy as high-dose aspirin, 325 mg twice daily, but with less GI side effects.

*Mont et al. reported the clinical practice guidelines for the prevention of venous thromboembolic disease in elective hip and knee arthroplasty.* The consensus recommendation from the study group was pharmacologic and mechanical prophylaxis should be initiated in patients who are not at elevated risk for VTE or bleeding, but did not specify specific agents. Patients that are at increased risk for bleeding are recommended to undergo bleeding diatheses evaluation.

*Lieberman and Heckmann reviewed venous thromboembolism prophylaxis in total knee and hip arthroplasty.* *They cited recommendations from the ACCP guidelines to use LMWH, fondaparinux, apixaban, dabigatran, rivaroxaban, aspirin, or vitamin K antagonists for a minimum of 10-14 days which should be extended to 35 days in the outpatient setting.* **The authors recommend** an individualized regimen that balances the risks and benefits given the paucity of data in the current literature.

*Azboy et al. performed a systematic review of low-dose aspirin for VTE prophylaxis in total joint arthroplasty.* **They reported** that patients receiving <162 mg of aspirin a day had no differences in symptomatic pulmonary embolism, symptomatic DVT, 90-day mortality, and major bleeding episodes, but reported higher rates of 90-day mortality and major bleeding episodes with treatment <4 weeks. **The authors concluded** low-dose aspirin is not inferior to high-dose aspirin following total joint arthroplasty.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 2: Aspirin 325 mg daily for 2 weeks is not a sufficient duration for chemoprophylaxis following adult reconstructive surgery. Studies have shown increased mortality and major bleeding episodes with treatment less than 4 weeks.
Answer 3: Weight-based doing of low-molecular-weight heparin is considered therapeutic anticoagulation, rather than prophylactic. There is a higher risk of bleeding and wound drainage complications with therapeutic dosing and should be avoided in the absence of confirmed DVT or VTE.
Answer 4: Warfarin is an option for chemoprophylaxis following adult reconstruction, but has the disadvantages of daily international normalized ratio (INR) testing and dose titration. An INR of 2-3 is recommended for prophylactic dosing rather than 2.5-3.5.
Answer 5: Mechanical prophylaxis alone is not recommended in total knee arthroplasty due to the excessive rates of DVTs reported without chemoprophylaxis (>50%).
