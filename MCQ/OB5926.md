## Question

Giant-cell tumor of bone (GCTB) is most commonly found in the following locations, as listed from MOST-to-LEAST common?

## Options

1. distal femur > proximal tibia > distal radius > sacral ala
2. proximal tibia > distal radius > distal femur > phalanges
3. distal femur > distal radius > sacral ala > proximal tibia
4. distal radius > distal femur > proximal tibia > phalanges
5. proximal tibia > proximal humerus > distal femur > distal radius

## Correct option

**1. distal femur > proximal tibia > distal radius > sacral ala**

## Answer explanation

The most common sites of giant cell tumors in bone are distal femur > proximal tibia > distal radius > sacral ala.

Giant cell tumor of bone (GCTB) is a benign aggressive tumor typically found in the epiphysis of long bones. 50% of tumors occur around the knee (distal femur or proximal tibia). GCTB is characterized by proliferation of mononuclear stromal cells and the presence of multi-nucleated giant cells with homogenous distribution. Treatment typically involves intralesional curettage with adjuvant treatment (e.g., phenol, hydrogen peroxide, argon beam, etc).

*Becker et al. looked at the use of adjuvants after curettage in the treatment of giant cell tumor of bone.* **They found** that polymethylmethacrylate as an adjuvant significantly reduced the recurrence rate following intralesional treatment of benign giant cell tumours compared to no adjunct treatment (22% vs 49% respectively).

*Williams et al undertook a prospective study to evaulate the recurrence rate of giant cell tumors of the hand and* **they found** that soft tissue extension of the tumor significantly increased the rate of local recurrence. This finding is significant because multiple previous studies have demonstrated similar higher recurrence rates for giant cell tumors with soft tissue extension in other areas like around the knee and the distal radius.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 2-5: All of the following structures listed may be involved with GCTB. The two most commonly affected areas are distal femur and proximal tibia, with an incidence of ~50% of cases. Other common locations in the extremities are proximal humerus, distal radius, and phalanges. The sacrum is the most common site in the axial skeleton.
