## Question

A 40-year-old woman has bilateral hip fractures and sustained a low-energy fracture 1 month ago. She has a history of end-stage renal disease and is on dialysis. Laboratory studies show elevated BUN, creatinine, alkaline phosphatase, and PTH levels. Radiographs and a CT scan are shown in Figures 39a and 39b, and a biopsy specimen is shown in Figure 39c. What is the most likely underlying diagnosis to explain the hip fractures?

## Options

1. Metastatic carcinoma
2. Renal osteodystrophy
3. Multiple myeloma
4. Liver cancer
5. Osteosarcoma

## Correct option

**2. Renal osteodystrophy**

## Answer explanation

The patient's laboratory profile and imaging studies reveal a classic case of renal osteodystrophy, and the histopathology demonstrates extreme bone turnover with thinning of the trabecular bone. Renal failure causes an inability to form the active form of vitamin D and inability to secrete phosphate. Failure to activate vitamin D results in osteomalacia. Phosphate retention causes calcium precipitation and stimulation of PTH secretion and secondary hyperparathyroidism. Metabolic bone disease secondary to renal osteodystrophy may mimic bone tumors on radiographs.
