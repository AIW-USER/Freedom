## Question

A 65-year-old male presents with 2 weeks of acute severe neck pain, bilateral upper extremity weakness, and radiating pain. He has a history of hypothyroidism, hypertension, alcohol abuse, and is currently a one pack per day cigarette smoker. An MRI of the cervical spine is demonstrated in figures A and B. He is indicated for a single level cervical decompression and fusion (ACDF). Which of the following factors will have the greatest impact on his likelihood of achieving a solid fusion?

## Options

1. Application of an anterior plate (verses no anterior instrumentation)
2. Use of allograft spacer (verses synthetic interbody cage)
3. Successful completion of smoking cessation program four weeks prior to surgery with no return to smoking postoperatively
4. Wearing a hard cervical collar 6 weeks following ACDF with anterior plating (verses no collar in postoperative period)
5. Use of tricortical iliac crest autograft (verses allograft spacer)

## Correct option

**3. Successful completion of smoking cessation program four weeks prior to surgery with no return to smoking postoperatively**

## Answer explanation

The patient is indicated for a single-level ACDF for an acute radiculopathy with neurologic changes, and has co-morbid medical issues including tobacco use. In order to maximize the likelihood of successful fusion, he should stop his use of tobacco.

Pseudoarthrosis is a well-known and challenging complication of spinal fusion surgery and can lead to hardware failure, chronic pain, and the need for revision surgery. Its rate in single-level ACDF is thought to be 2.6-4.8%. Many factors are thought to contribute to the development of pseudoarthrosis in the cervical spine. Smoking and diabetes mellitus specifically have been implicated as modifiable risk factors for pseudoarthrosis. In single-level fusions, graft type and the use of anterior plate fixation do not appear to affect the rates of fusion.

*Samartzis et. al. compared the fusion rates of autograft and allograft in 66 patients who underwent single-level ACDF with anterior plate fixation.* *95.5% of patients studied had solid fusion with a 100% fusion rate in allograft patients and 90.3% in autograft patients.* This difference was nonsignificant. **The authors concluded** that autograft yielded similar and high fusion rates in single level ACDF without the added morbidity of autograft harvest

*Samartzis et. al. evaluated the influence of rigid instrumentation on fusion rates in single-level ACDF in 69 patients who underwent one-level ACDF with or without anterior plate fixation.* Fusion was assessed radiographically, and outcomes assessed with Cervical Spine Outcomes Questionnaire. **They found** no significant difference between groups with respect to fusion rate or outcome scores, concluding that both plating and non-plating were effective techniques.

*Shriver et. al. performed a meta-analysis of all prospective studies reporting pseudoarthrosis rates following one-, two-, and three-level ACDF.* **They found** an overall pseudoarthrosis rate of 2.6%, with single-level ACDF having a rate of 3.7%. Importantly, randomized controlled trials demonstrated a higher pseudoarthrosis rate than non-randomized prospective studies (4.8% vs 0.2%), **suggesting** the quality and extent of follow-up can significantly impact reported rates.

*Jackson et. al. performed a systematic review of the effect of cigarette use on spinal fusion rates.* **They report** significant increase in the rates of pseudoarthrosis following cervical and lumbar spine surgery. For the lumbar spine, smoking appears to significantly increase pseudoarthrosis rates, perhaps in a dose-dependent manner. For the cervical spine, smoking may increase the risk of pseudoarthrosis, but the results in the literature are mixed. Pre-operative smoking cessation for 4 weeks, as well as post-operative abstinence for 6 months are associated with improved fusion rates, outcome scores, return to work rates, satisfaction scores, though not as good as in non-smokers.

*Campbell et. al. performed a randomized clinical trial evaluating the utility of routine cervical collar use following single level ACDF with plating.* **They found** that patients treated without cervical collar had significantly higher Neck Disability Index (NDI) scores at 6 weeks post-op, and at final follow-up (24 months) there was no significant difference in fusion rate between those treated with a collar compared with those wearing no collar (fusion rate 89.5% versus 94.5%, p=0.379).

Figure A is an axial T2-weighted MRI image through the C6-7 disc space demonstrated an acute, soft-disc herniation with central and bilateral paracentral impingement. Figure B is a mid-sagittal T2-weighted MRI demonstrating a soft-disc herniation at the C6-7 disc space.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Application of an anterior plate has been demonstrated to have no effect on fusion rates in single-level ACDF
Answers 2 & 5: Use of allograft versus autograft has been demonstrated to have no effect on fusion rates in single-level ACDF.
Answer 4: There is no evidence of improved fusion rates with the use of post-operative cervical collar following single level ACDF with plating.
