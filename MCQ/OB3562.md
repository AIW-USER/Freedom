## Question

An enzymatic mutation leading to abnormal carbonic anhydrase function in osteoclasts would lead to a condition best illustrated by which of the following radiographs in Figures A thru E.

![[q3562-a-osteopetrosis-erylenmeyer-flask-femur.jpg]]
*Figure A*

![[q3562-b-pagets-pelvis-radiograph.jpg]]
*Figure B*

![[q3562-c-melorheostosis-forearm-radiograph.jpg]]
*Figure C*

![[q3562-d-osteosarcoma-distal-femur-ap-radiograph.jpg]]
*Figure D*

![[q3562-e-bone-infarct-radiograph-ap-knee.jpg]]
*Figure E*

## Options

1. Figure A
2. Figure B
3. Figure C
4. Figure D
5. Figure E

## Correct option

**1. Figure A**

## Answer explanation

Figures A show the classic "erlenmeyer flask" femurs seen in osteopetrosis. While the exact cause of osteopetrosis is not precisely known, most authors agree it is related to abnormal carbonic anhydrase function within the osteoclast. Illustration A shows the "rugger jersey spine" lateral radiograph, also diagnostic of osteopetrosis.

Incorrect Answers:
Figure B: AP view of the pelvis shows classic signs of Paget's disease with thickening of the femoral neck and diffuse sclerosis of bone.
Figure C: Melorheostosis diagnosed by the "flowing wax" appearance of the radiograph
Figure D: Osteosarcoma distal femur - the sunburst new bone formation and aggressive periosteal reaction are diagnostic
Figure E: Bone infarct - radiographically, bone infarcts can be difficult to distinguish from enchondroma. One differentiator is that bone infarcts look like "smoke in a chimney stack"
