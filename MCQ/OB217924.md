## Question

A 76-year-old woman recently underwent a cementless right total hip arthroplasty. Her 6-week postoperative radiographs show a 5mm subsidence of the femoral stem. Which of the following factors may have contributed of this postoperative finding?

## Options

1. BMI of 44
2. Oversizing of the femoral component
3. Dorr A femur
4. Use of a tapered femoral stem
5. Use of a modular neck prosthesis

## Correct option

**1. BMI of 44**

## Answer explanation

This patient has early femoral stem subsidence. Subsidence with the first 6 weeks would suggest a lack of initial stability and is likely related to under-sizing of the implant at the time of surgery and/or a high BMI.

Early migration and aseptic loosening of cementless femoral components may predict late failure. Early subsidence ultimately indicates a lack of initial stability. A high BMI and/or under-sizing of the femoral stem implant at the time of surgery are risk factors for postoperative femoral stem subsidence.

*Streit et al. reviewed femoral stem axial migration among cementless stems that develop aseptic loosening.* **They reported** that femoral components that had aseptic loosening develop showed greater mean distal migration at 24 months postoperatively than did components that remained well fixed. **They concluded** that early migration has good diagnostic capabilities for detection of uncemented femoral components at risk for aseptic loosening during the first and early second decades after surgery.

*Goodnough et al. reviewed patients with a failed primary THA and the influence of obesity with aseptic loosening and need for early revision surgery.* **They reported** that the rate of primary THA failure before 5 years was 49% in obese and 37% in nonobese patients. **They concluded** that obesity is independently associated with early primary THA failure for aseptic loosening.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Under-sizing of the femoral stem implant at the time of surgery is a risk factor for early stem subscidence
Answers 3-5: These have not been identified as risk factors for early postoperative femoral stem subsidence
