## Question

A 17-year-old male track runner presents with worsening right hip pain over the last 4 months. He states his pain is worse at night. He has undergone a course of physical therapy without significant benefit. His pain is improved with NSAIDs initially, but no longer provides relief. He denies any increase in weekly mileage and denies any fevers or chills. Current MRI imaging and CT is depicted in figures A-E. What is the most appropriate treatment for this patient at this time?

## Options

1. Protected weight bearing right lower extremity
2. Non-weight bearing right lower extremity
3. Percutaneous screw fixation right hip
4. Radiofrequency ablation
5. CT guided biopsy and cultures followed by IV antibiotics

## Correct option

**4. Radiofrequency ablation**

## Answer explanation

The patient is presenting with history and imaging findings consistent with osteoid osteoma. Given his symptoms are refractory to NSAIDs, radiofrequency ablation is the appropriate treatment option at this time.

Osteoid osteoma is a benign bone lesion consisting of a central nidus of woven bone surrounded by reactive fibrovascular tissue. Lesions commonly occur in the proximal femur, but may also be present in the posterior elements of the spine. This can lead to painful scoliosis in adolescent patients. Patients report worsened pain at night and improvement with NSAID use. Percutaneous radiofrequency ablation is a minimally invasive treatment option for symptomatic lesions that have failed nonoperative treatment.

*Boscainos et al. reviewed the diagnosis and treatment of osteoid osteoma.* **They reported** natural history of symptom resolution over 6-15 years without NSAIDs and 2-3 with NSAIDs. **They recommended** surgical or radiofrequency ablation in patients with symptoms refractory to NSAID treatment.

*Klontzas et al. performed an MRI study of osteoid osteoma of the femoral neck and described the "half-moon" sign for the diagnosis.* The "half-moon" sign is described as the half-disc appearance of the surrounding bony edema caused by an active osteoid osteoma. **They reported** the half-moon sign was present in all 11 patients with osteoid osteoma and only 1/19 patients without osteoid osteoma. **They concluded** the half-moon sign is highly specific and sensitive (94.7% and 100%, respectively) for the diagnosis of osteoid osteoma of the femoral neck.

Incorrect answers
Answer 1: Protected weight bearing would be the preferred treatment for transient osteoporosis of the hip.
Answer 2: Nonweightbearing would be the appropriate treatment option for a compression-sided femoral neck stress fracture with less than 50% extension.
Answer 3: Percutaneous screw fixation is the preferred treatment option for a tension-sided femoral neck stress fracture or if there is greater than 50% extension into the neck.
Answer 5: CT-guided cultures and IV antibiotics would be the treatment of choice for osteomyelitis.
