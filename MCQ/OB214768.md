## Answer explanation

The patient sustained a talar neck fracture and concomitant subtalar dislocation (Hawkins 2) which has an estimated rate of avascular necrosis (AVN) of roughly 15-40%.

Talar neck fractures are high energy injuries with complications of varus malunion, nonunion, post-traumatic osteoarthritis, and AVN of the talus. The rates of AVN have been found to correlate with the injury pattern based on the Hawkins classification. The chance that a patient goes on to AVN is likely determined at the time of injury based on the severity of injury and blood supply disruption.

*Vallier et al. retrospectively reviewed 100 patients with 102 talar neck fractures all treated with open reduction and internal fixation.* **They found** an overall AVN rate of 31% and a rate of 39% in those fractures with Hawkins Type II fractures. **They concluded** that AVN was associated with talar neck comminution and open fracture and no correlation with timing to OR.

*Metzger et al. reviewed the literature regarding talar neck fractures and AVN rates.* **They found** the rates of type II talar neck fractures and AVN were roughly 34% based on the compiling of 12 different papers. **They found** type II injuries had AVN rates ranging from 16% to 71%.

*Halvorson et al. performed a systematic review of all the available data evaluating talar neck fractures.* **They found** the overall rate of AVN was 33% with no association with time to surgery. **They found** an AVN rate of 18.4% in Hawkins type II fractures. **They concluded** that of those patients with talar neck fractures, one-third will develop AVN, two-thirds will develop posttraumatic arthritis and 20% will go on to have a salvage procedure.

Figure A shows a displaced talar neck fracture with dislocation of the subtalar, tibiotalar, and talonavicular joints. Figures B and C show displaced talar neck fractures with subtalar and tibiotalar joint dislocations. Figure D shows a displaced talar neck fracture with a subtalar dislocation. Figure E shows a minimally displaced talar neck fracture. Illustration A depicts the full Hawkins classification of talar neck fractures.

Answers 2 and 3: Figures B and C show Hawkins 3 talar neck fractures.
Answer 5: Figure E shows a Hawkins 1 talar neck fracture.
