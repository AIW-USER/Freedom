## Question

All the following are true about tranexamic acid (TXA) EXCEPT?

## Options

1. Stabilizes clot formation
2. Amino acid derivative that blocks plasmin binding sites
3. Causes an increase in fibrinolysis
4. IV and topical dosing are equally efficacious
5. Leads to decreased transfusion rates in arthroplasty patients

## Correct option

**3. Causes an increase in fibrinolysis**

## Answer explanation

The use of tranexamic acid (TXA) is associated with all of the following except increased fibrinolysis (Answer 3).

Tranexamic acid (TXA) is a lysine derivative that blocks the plasmin binding sites on fibrin, which results in a decrease in fibrinolysis and stabilizes clot formation. There are multiple dosing regimens and routes of administration including IV, oral, and topical, which all have been shown to be equally efficacious. TXA has also demonstrated transfusion rates of less than 3% for TKA and less than 10% for THA.

*Whiting et al. retrospectively reviewed 1131 primary total joint arthroplasties with an American Society of Anesthesiologist (ASA) score of III or IV.* *402 had at least one of the following: DVT, PE, MI, CVA, CAD with stent placement, CABG, or a prothrombotic condition.* *Out of the 402 patients, 240 were given TXA.* **They demonstrated** no increased symptomatic thromboembolic events (even in high-risk patients) and fewer patients required transfusions with TXA (11% vs. 41%).

Sculco and Pagnano discuss the four impediments to early rehabilitation and discharge which are volume depletion, blood loss, pain, and nausea. They discuss the different interventions directed at correcting and/or preventing these impediments to discharge, which includes the use of TXA to limit blood loss and reduce the need for other blood management systems.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1, 2, 4, 5: All of these statements about TXA are true. TXA is a lysine derivative that blocks plasmin binding sites, which results in more stable clots and a DECREASE in fibrinolysis. All dosing forms have been shown to be equally efficacious and have led to decreased transfusion rates in arthroplasty patients.
