## Question

During the work up chronic periprosthetic joint infection, which of the following diagnostic tests have the best combined sensitivity and specificity?

## Options

1. Serum diagnostic tests; CRP and IL-6
2. Synovial diagnostic tests; lateral flow alpha-defensin and PMN%
3. Tissue diagnostic tests; histological analysis
4. Synovial diagnostic tests; IL-6 and leukocyte esterase (LER) strips
5. Synovial diagnostic tests; laboratory-based alpha-defensin and leukocyte esterase (LER) strips

## Correct option

**5. Synovial diagnostic tests; laboratory-based alpha-defensin and leukocyte esterase (LER) strips**

## Answer explanation

Synovial-based tests are more predictive of periprosthetic joint infection (PJI) than serum and tissue-based tests. Of the synovial-based tests, laboratory-based alpha-defensin and leukocyte esterase strips have the best combined sensitivity and specificity.

Chronic periprosthetic joint infection is a devastating complication that is associated with significantly lower quality-of-life scores and increased short-term mortality compared to patients without infection. Confirming the diagnosis of PJI remains challenging as there is no clear-cut “gold standard” diagnostic workup. Diagnostic tests are generally subcategorized as serum, synovial or tissue-based. Most common serum-based tests include CRP, ESR, IL-6, pro-calcitonin (PCT), and WBC count. Most common synovial-based tests include alpha-defensin, CRP, LER strips, WBC count, PMN%, joint aspiration culture, and IL-6. Most common tissue-based tests include histological analysis, perioperative tissue culture, PCR, gram stain, and swab culture. Synovial-based tests have been shown to be more predictive of PJI than serum and tissue-based tests. Of the synovial-based tests, alpha-defensin (specifically laboratory-based) and leukocyte esterase strips (specifically 2+) have the best combined sensitivity and specificity for PJI.

*Alberto et al. performed a systematic review comparing the diagnostic accuracy of serum, synovial, and tissue-based tests for chronic PJI.* **They found** that LER strips (2+) and laboratory-based alpha-defensin were the most predictive of PJI.

*Stone et al. reviewed synovial alpha-defensin and synovial fluid C-reactive protein (CRP) tests that were performed in 183 patients undergoing evaluation for PJI.* **They report** that alpha-defensin in combination with synovial fluid CRP demonstrates very high sensitivity for diagnosing PJI but may yield false-positive results in the presence of metallosis or false-negative results in the presence of low-virulence organisms. **They concluded** that when both alpha defensin and synovial fluid CRP tests are positive, there is a very high specificity for diagnosing periprosthetic joint infection.

*Frangiamore et al. evaluated the accuracy of synovial alpha-defensin in diagnosing PJI.* **They found** that for first-stage and single-stage revisions, alpha defensin had a sensitivity of 100% and a specificity of 98% with a positive predictive value of 96% and negative predictive value of 100% for diagnosing PJI.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Synovial-based tests have greater sensitivity than serum-based tests. Mean sensitivity (SN) and specificity (SP), respectively, of serum CRP: 0.81, 0.85 and IL-6: 0.87, 0.88.
Answer 2: While synovial alpha-defensin is highly predictive of PGI, LER strips are better than PMN%. Furthermore, laboratory-based alpha defensin has higher sensitivity (0.97 vs. 0.96) and specificity (0.97 vs 0.82) than lateral flow alpha-defensin.
Answer 3: Perioperative tissue culture is expensive, carries the risks of an invasive procedure, and does not have better sensitivity or specificity than aspiration.
Answer 4: Synovial alpha-defensin is more predictive of PJI than synovial IL-6.
