## Question

A 62-year-old female presents with worsening lower back pain, buttock pain, and right lower extremity pain that has been present for several months. She has attempted physical therapy, activity modifications, and oral steroids without significant improvement in her symptoms. On exam, there is 4/5 motor strength in her right extensor hallicus longus and tibialis anterior. Her current radiographs and MRI imaging are shown in figures A and B. Which of the following treatment options is likely to give the best long-term results?

## Options

1. CT-guided cyst rupture with epidural steroid injection
2. L4-5 posterior instrumented fusion, laminectomy, and cyst excision
3. L4-5 posterior uninstrumented fusion, laminectomy, and complete facetectomy
4. L4-5 laminectomy with cyst excision
5. L4-5 laminectomy, cyst excision, and complete facetectomy

## Correct option

**2. L4-5 posterior instrumented fusion, laminectomy, and cyst excision**

## Answer explanation

The patient has grade 1 degenerative spondylolisthesis with a synovial cyst at the right L4-5 facet which would be most definitively treated with an L4-5 posterior instrumented fusion, laminectomy, and cyst excision.

Synovial facet cysts can frequently cause lumbar radiculopathy and spinal stenosis. Often, synovial facet cysts occur in the presence of spinal instability and have been associated with degenerative spondylolisthesis. Initial treatment consists of nonoperative measures, which include physical therapy, NSAIDs, and activity modifications for a period of 6-8 months. Patients that have failed nonoperative treatment or have motor weakness are candidates for surgical treatment. The most definitive treatment option, especially in the setting of spinal instability, is posterior instrumented fusion, decompression, and cyst excision.

*Lyons et al. performed a retrospective review of 194 patients treated for symptomatic lumbar facet cysts.* **They reported** 85% of patients presenting with complaints of radiculopathy, 44% with neurogenic claudication, 43% with sensory loss, and 27% with motor weakness all of whom were treated with laminectomy and facet resection with or without fusion. **The authors concluded** that synovial facet cysts can be a common cause of radicular pain and is often the chief patient complaint that can be effectively treated with decompression and cyst excision.

*Khan et al. performed a retrospective study of 39 patients treated for lumbar synovial facet cysts.* **The authors reported** the most common complaints being lower extremity pain (100%) and back pain (95%) most commonly occurred at L4-5 (63%) and was highly associated with degenerative spondylolisthesis (82%). **They concluded** the etiology of synovial facet cysts remains unclear but speculated underlying instability to be a causative factor and can be effectively treated with decompression with fusion if deemed unstable.

*Epstein reported on the surgical outcomes of 45 patients that were treated for lumbar facet cysts with associated spinal stenosis or degenerative spondylolisthesis.* **The author reported** that 11/35 patients with grade 1 spondylolisthesis treated with laminectomy and cyst excision progressed to grade 2 spondylolisthesis and a 58% to 63% rate of good to excellent results with decompression alone. **She concluded** consideration should be given for primary fusion for the treatment of lumbar facet cysts with associated degenerative spondylolisthesis.

Figure A is the lateral radiograph of the lumbar spine with grade 1 degenerative spondylolisthesis at L4-5. Figure B is an axial T2 MRI of the lumbar spine demonstrating a synovial cyst and the right L4-5 facet.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: A CT-guided cyst rupture with epidural steroid injection is likely to result in short-term pain relief for the patient. However, the cause of the cyst has not been addressed with this treatment approach and has a high recurrence risk with medium- and long-term follow-up.
Answer 3: An L4-5 posterior uninstrumented fusion, laminectomy, and complete facetectomy have a higher risk of pseudoarthrosis as demonstrated by earlier studies for the treatment of degenerative spondylolisthesis.
Answer 4: An L4-5 laminectomy with cyst excision would likely relieve the patient of her radiculopathy, but would be at increased risk of slip progression or cyst recurrence.
Answer 5: An L4-5 laminectomy, cyst excision, and complete facetectomy would destabilize the L4-5 motion segment and lead to higher grade spondylolisthesis.
