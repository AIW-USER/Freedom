## Question

You see a 70-year-old male in the office with complaints of diffuse foot pain. He has continued to have pain despite taking oral NSAIDS and acetaminophen. He has also failed to see improvement with physical therapy. Based on radiographs, you recommend the orthotic seen in Figure A. Which of the following is the most likely diagnosis?

![[q218573-a-rocker-bottom-shoe1.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Hallux rigidus
2. Freiberg's disease
3. First and second tarsometatarsal arthritis
4. Planovalgus deformity
5. Cavovarus deformity

## Correct option

**3. First and second tarsometatarsal arthritis**

## Answer explanation

A rocker bottom shoe is best suited for the treatment of midfoot arthritis, as it efficiently transfers load from the hindfoot to the forefoot with minimal stress through the midfoot.

Midfoot arthritis can cause debilitating pain and can occur in the setting of primary osteoarthritis or as the result of trauma, such as a remote Lisfranc injury. Patients complain primarily of pain during the push-off phase of gait. Once other conservative measures have failed, and before proceeding to surgery, orthotic management is appropriate. A rocker bottom shoe can be used to transfer load more efficiently from the hindfoot to the forefoot during the push-off phase of gait.

*Patel et al. reviewed midfoot arthritis in a JAAOS article,* **noting** it is a common cause of foot pain and disability. They discuss the lack of motion that occurs at the 1st-3rd TMT joints, but also highlight their importance in providing stability and translating energy from the hindfoot and ankle to the forefoot. They discuss nonsurgical management which includes the use of different shoe modifications and orthotic inserts, as well as surgical intervention, which usually involves arthrodesis of the midfoot.

*Rao et al. evaluated plantar loading and function after placement of shoe inserts in patients with midfoot arthritis.* *They compared the use of a full-length arch-restoring carbon orthosis with a three-quarter length orthosis.* **They noted** that the full-length arch restoring orthosis led to improvement in functional outcomes as well as a reduction in midfoot pressure loading.

Figure A demonstrates a rocker bottom shoe.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: A Morton's carbon fiber extension would be the best orthosis for treating hallux rigidus.
Answer 2: A rocker bottom shoe would not be ideal to manage Freiberg's disease or AVN of the 2nd metatarsal head. Metatarsal pads can be used to offload this area.
Answer 4: Medial wedge insoles and UCBL orthoses can be used to correct flexible planovalgus deformities.
Answer 5: A lateral wedge insole with a cutout for the 1st ray would be used to help correct a flexible cavovarus deformity.
