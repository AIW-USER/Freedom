Chapter: Arthroplasty MCQs — TKR in valgus knee

## Question

True about lateral parapatellar approach for valgus knee deformity in total knee replacement is all except

## Options

1. More chances of patellar necrosis compared to medial arthrotomy
2. Less patellar maltracking
3. Difficult exposure
4. Fixed varus deformity is a contraindication.

## Correct option

**1. More chances of patellar necrosis compared to medial arthrotomy**

## Answer explanation

In severe valgus deformities, release of lateral patellar retinaculum is necessary in most cases in order to prevent patellar instability. Lateral release in combination with medial capsulotomy, results in significant impairment of the extensor mechanism blood supply . If the knee joint is approached via a lateral parapatellar arthrotomy, release of the lateral retinaculum is integrated in the approach. A lateral capsulotomy offers excellent exposure of the contracted lateral structures, thus facilitating their adjustment.

Lateral approach has been recommended as the “approach of choice” for fixed valgus deformity in TKA. The advantages of this approach in comparison with the medial are that firstly the lateral release is direct and is performed as part of the procedure, without affecting patella's medial blood supply; and secondly the medial displacement of the quadriceps–patellar tendon mechanism internally rotates the tibia, which brings the pathologic posterolateral corner forward into the operating field, facilitating the release of contracted elements. Lastly, the patellofemoral tracking and alignment stability are optimized, and if needed the tubercle can be transferred medially, eliminating the postoperative hazard for patellar maltracking. Fixed varus deformity is a contraindication for lateral parapatellar approach. On the other hand, potential disadvantages of lateral parapatellar approach include difficulty in patellar eversion, sometimes requiring TTO, and less familiarity of many surgeons with this technique

Answer : a
