## Question

A 75-year-old man presents after tripping over his cat and falling with severe left hip pain. His radiographs are presented in Figure A. Which of the following is the correct associated biological activity within the first 6 weeks of healing if treated with closed reduction and submuscular bridge plate application?

![[q220477-a-vancouverb1.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Endochondral ossification
2. Intramembranous ossification
3. Bone-implant ongrowth
4. Immediate bone-cement interface stability
5. Bone-implant ingrowth and osseointegration

## Correct option

**1. Endochondral ossification**

## Answer explanation

This patient has sustained a periprosthetic femur fracture about a well-fixed total hip arthroplasty stem (Vancouver B1). He is indicated for open reduction and internal fixation with a long bridge plate, leading to secondary bone healing (endochondral) if applied in bridging fashion. (Answer 1)

Periprosthetic femur fractures about hip arthroplasty are an increasingly common problem and the most common type of periprosthetic fracture. The most commonly used classification system is the Vancouver system. Vancouver B-type fractures are fractures at the level of the femoral stem and are subdivided into B1 (stable stem), B2 (unstable stem, good bone stock), and B3 (unstable stem, insufficient bone stock). Vancouver B1 fractures and select B2 fractures are indicated for open reduction and internal fixation with a long bridge plate construct to promote fracture healing. This can be performed through percutaneous submuscular techniques to minimize soft tissue disruption. Ideally, the plate is applied in bridge function with long working length and relative stability to promote secondary bone healing.

*Springer et al. perform a retrospective analysis of revision total hip arthroplasty in the treatment of periprosthetic femur fractures with loose stems.* Kaplan-Meier analysis showed a 90% implant survival at five years and 79.2% at ten years. The main complications were prosthetic loosening and fracture nonunion, with better outcomes observed using uncemented, extensively porous-coated stems. The study concluded that while revision arthroplasty generally restored function, the challenges of prosthetic loosening and nonunion remained significant. **They recommend** the use of open reduction and internal fixation in the treatment of fractures around stable stems.

*Ricci et al. present the surgical technique in the treatment of Vancouver B1 periprosthetic fractures.* The technique employs indirect reduction and internal fixation with a lateral plate without the need for bone grafting, adhering to modern principles of biologic fracture fixation to preserve soft tissue and promote healing. In the study, 50 patients with Vancouver Type-B1 fractures were treated using this method, with 41 patients achieving satisfactory fracture healing at an average of 12 weeks post-surgery. **The results support** the effectiveness of this technique, with 30 patients returning to baseline ambulatory status and minimal complications, including only one deep infection and a few hardware issues without implant loosening or malalignment. **The authors emphasize** the importance of using an appropriately long plate to avoid stress risers and recommend locked plates for improved fixation in osteoporotic bone.

Figure A is the AP hip radiographs demonstrating a periprosthetic femur fracture about a total hip arthroplasty, which remains well fixed (no evidence of subsidence or loss of metaphyseal fixation).

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Intramembranous ossification (primary bone healing) requires interfragmentary compression and rigid fixation, which was not performed in this scenario
Answers 3, 4, 5: These modes of healing are relevant to revision total hip arthroplasty, which is not indicated in this scenario
