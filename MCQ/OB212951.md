## Options

1. Radial head subluxation
2. Excessive glenoid retroversion
3. Absence of the anterior cruciate ligament
4. Hemihypertrophy
5. Developmental dysplasia of the hip

## Correct option

**4. Hemihypertrophy**

## Answer explanation

The patient is presenting with congenital pseudoarthrosis of the tibia and dystrophic scoliosis which are associated with Neurofibromatosis type 1. Of the given answer choices, hemihypertrophy is most likely to be associated with this condition.

Neurofibromatosis type 1, also known as von Recklinghausen disease, is the result of a mutation in the neurofibromin gene on chromosome 17. About half of the cases are the result of a new mutation. Diagnosis is based on the presence of two or more common orthopedic manifestations. These include congenital anterolateral bowing of the tibia, dystrophic scoliosis, hemihypertrophy, atlantoaxial instability, cervical and thoracic kyphosis, ulnar and radial pseudoarthrosis, and malignant nerve sheath tumors. Dystrophic scoliosis is characterized by rib penciling, vertebral rotation, sharp deformity involving 6 segments or less, posterior vertebral scalloping, and enlarged neuroforamina.

*Koptan et al.* *2010 performed a retrospective cohort study in patients with NF-1 scoliosis with and without sagittal plane deformity undergoing combined anterior and posterior fusion.* **The authors found** kyphotic patients had a significant increase in correction loss, greater intraoperative blood loss, and longer operative times. **The authors suggest** that sagittal plane deformity poses a risk for correction loss, but combined anterior and posterior procedures do provide an effective means to address these deformities.

*Crawford et al. in 2006 reviewed and updated the orthopedic manifestation of NF-1 and associated treatments.* Posterior spinal fusion is recommended for dystrophic scoliosis with curves greater than 20-40 degrees and curves greater than 50 degrees require combined anterior and posterior fusion. Rigid orthosis for anterolateral bowing and intramedullary nailing with graft for associated fractures or pseudoarthrosis and amputation is a viable option after 3 failed fusion attempts. Hemihypertrophy manifests as unilateral wavy elongation of bones, with early epiphyseal arrest provides greater success over debulking procedures.

*Stevenson et al. in 2009 performed a retrospective cohort analysis of neurofibromatosis type 1 patient with anterolateral bowing of the tibia.* Compared to healthy controls, congenital tibial dysplasia was associated with thicker cortices with medullary narrowing, especially at the apex of the deformity. Apices tended to occur at the juncture of the middle and distal third of the tibia.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Radial head subluxation is not specifically associated with NF-1. It is seen with Multiple Hereditary Exostoses.
Answer 2: Excessive glenoid retroversion is not associated with NF-1 but with persistent congenital brachial plexus palsy
Answer 3: Absence of the anterior cruciate ligament is associated with fibular hemimelia, not NF-1.
Answer 5: Developmental dysplasia of the hip has not been associated with NF-1.
