## Question

Figure A is the AP radiograph of a child with inability to bear weight on the left lower extremity. All the following are relative indications for contralateral hip prophylactic pinning EXCEPT:

![[q2247-a-scfe-left-hip.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Age <10 years
2. African American ethnicity
3. Growth hormone deficiency
4. Hypothyroidism
5. Open triradiate cartilage

## Correct option

**2. African American ethnicity**

## Answer explanation

This patient has an unstable left slipped capital femoral epiphysis (SCFE) which will require surgical intervention. Contralateral hip prophylactic fixation is indicated for patients who have a high risk (40-80%) of a contralateral slip, and these relative indications include age <10 years, open triradiate cartilage, and endocrine disorders.

SCFE is the most common disorder affecting adolescent hips with a reported incidence of 10 per 100,000. Certain demographic factors have an increased association with the occurrence of a SCFE; including obesity, male gender, and specific ethnicities (African American, Latinos, and Pacific islanders). A SCFE is most likely to occur during a period of rapid growth with an average age for boys and girls of 13.4 and 12.2 years, respectively. The occurrence of a SCFE in a child <10 years of age or with a weight <50th percentile merits an endocrine workup. Certain endocrine disorders have been associated with SCFE; these include hypothyroidism, renal osteodystrophy, growth hormone deficiency, and panhypopituitarism.

*Loder et al. review 85 patients with endocrine disorders and SCFE.* **They reported** that only those with hypothyroidism or growth hormone deficiency were < 10 years of age. **They concluded** that because the prevalence of bilaterality was 61%, prophylactic treatment of the opposite hip should be considered.

*Wells et al. review 40 bilateral SCFE in patients with known endocrinopathies including hypothyroidism, panhypopituitarism, and hypogonadism.* **They reported** an increased frequency of bilateral SCFE in patients with endocrine disorders, primarily hypothyroidism (6.9%). **They noted** that these patients either presented with or developed bilateral slips in an average of 11.17 months. **They concluded** that any child with a unilateral slip and one of these endocrine deficiencies has a high risk of subsequent bilateral involvement and that prophylactic pinning of the uninvolved hip is recommended as 100% of patients eventually had bilateral slips.

Figure A is the AP radiograph of the pelvis demonstrating a left slipped capital femoral epiphysis (SCFE).

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1,3-5: These are relative indications for prophylactic pinning of the contralateral hip. Young age (<10 year and open triradiate cartilage) and endocrine disorders (hypothyroidism and growth hormone deficiency) put the patient at a high risk for a contralateral slip. Although African American ethnicity has a higher association with SCFE, it is not an indication for prophylactic hip pinning.
