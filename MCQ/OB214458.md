## Question

A 51-year-old male reports difficulty with urination and numbness in his bilateral buttocks. His symptoms began 12 hours ago. Which of the following statements about his condition is true?

## Options

1. The imaging study of choice is a CT myelogram.
2. The presence of saddle anesthesia and bladder dysfunction predicts a poor prognosis for this patient.
3. Epidural steroid injection is the next best step in treatment.
4. The patient's age predicts poor outcomes for this patient.
5. High dose methylprednisone should be given to this patient.

## Correct option

**2. The presence of saddle anesthesia and bladder dysfunction predicts a poor prognosis for this patient.**

## Answer explanation

This patient has classic symptoms of cauda equina syndrome (CES). Patients with CES who present with saddle anesthesia and bladder dysfunction have been found to have inferior outcomes.

Cauda equina syndrome has been described as a complex of low back pain, bilateral or unilateral sciatica, saddle anesthesia, and motor weakness that can progress to paraplegia and permanent bowel/bladder dysfunction. Cauda equina syndrome is an absolute indication for surgical treatment of lumbar disc disease. Patients with saddle anesthesia and bladder dysfunction have been found to have poorer outcomes.

*Buchner et al. performed a retrospective study to evaluate outcomes of patients who underwent diskectomy following a diagnosis of CES due to prolapsed intervertebral lumbar disks.* **They found** that there was not any statistically significant difference concerning the time between onset of symptoms and surgical decompression and subsequent outcomes.

*Kostuik et al. performed a retrospective study to evaluate CES due to lumbar disc herniation.* **They found** that bladder function was the most seriously affected function preoperatively and remained so postoperatively. The prognosis for return of motor function, however, was good. **They also found** that patients treated with surgery at 1.1 days compared to those treated at 3.3 days did not have any difference in return of function. **They therefore concluded** that, although early surgery should be performed, decompression does not have to be performed in less than six hours.

*Kennedy et al. performed a retrospective study on the predictors of outcome in CES.* **They found** that saddle hypoaesthesia and bladder dysfunction was present in all patients, and that there was a clear correlation between the presence of complete perineal anesthesia and significant sphincter dysfunction as predictors of a poor overall outcome. No correlation was found between initial motor function loss, bilateral sciatica, level or cause of injury as predictors of a poor outcome.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: MRI is the study of choice in patients who have CES.
Answer 3: Surgical decompression, not epidural steroid injection, is the next best step in management of this patient.
Answer 4: Age has not been proven to be a risk factor for poor outcomes in patients with CES.
Answer 5: The administration of high dose methylprednisone is controversial and no longer indicated in spinal cord injuries.
