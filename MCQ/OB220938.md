## Question

A 57-year-old man presents with bothersome nodules on the plantar surface of his bilateral feet. You diagnose him with plantar fibromatosis and initiate conservative treatment with activity modification, orthotics, and nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory medications. He experiences minimal symptomatic relief and asks for the option that is supported by the highest-grade clinical evidence of efficacy. Which statement correctly characterizes that evidence?

## Options

1. Collagenase injection has multiple randomized trials showing superiority over placebo
2. Surgery is proven superior to nonoperative care in double-blind, randomized controlled trials
3. No prospective controlled data exist for any treatment option
4. Radiotherapy is supported by a randomized double-blind sham-controlled trial with positive outcomes with minimum one year follow-up
5. Meta-analysis of prospective randomized and cohort trials supports orthotics over injections and surgery at one year follow-up

## Correct option

**4. Radiotherapy is supported by a randomized double-blind sham-controlled trial with positive outcomes with minimum one year follow-up**

## Answer explanation

A recent (2023) multicenter double-blind sham-controlled trial investigating radiotherapy as a treatment modality for plantar fibromatosis (Ledderhose disease) suggests durable clinical efficacy and acceptable toxicity profile at minimum one year follow-up (answer choice 4).

Plantar fibromatosis, eponymously known as Ledderhose disease, is a benign myofibroblastic proliferation of the plantar fascia that may cause painful nodules, most commonly along the medial band. Initial treatment is observation and supportive therapy (orthotics, activity modification, shoe wear changes), whereas surgery is reserved for large, painful, or activity-limiting lesions. When surgery is performed, recurrence is common after local excision; total or subtotal fasciectomy reduces recurrence but at the cost of greater morbidity, and adjuvant radiation is sometimes considered to minimize recurrence. Overall, evidence quality for most modalities has historically been limited, with variable recurrence after surgery.

*De Haan and colleagues reported the LedRad study, a prospective, multicenter, randomized, double-blind, phase III trial comparing radiotherapy with sham radiotherapy in symptomatic Ledderhose disease.* Radiotherapy produced lower mean pain scores at 12 and 18 months, higher quality-of-life scores, and better barefoot walking speed/step rate versus sham; reported side effects (erythema, dryness, burning) were generally mild and resolved mainly by 18 months. **The authors concluded** that radiotherapy is effective for pain reduction with favorable safety.

*De Haan et al. also reported long-term outcomes of radiotherapy for Ledderhose disease,* **showing** significant and durable pain reduction (mean pain score 5.7 pre-RT to 1.7 at assessment), with complete or partial pain response in most treated feet and high patient satisfaction. Quality-of-life scores approximated age-matched population norms, and late toxicity was limited, with dryness being the most common residual side effect. These results support long-term symptom relief and tolerability.

*Espert et al. reviewed plantar fibromatosis treatments and* **emphasized** that orthotics and shoe modifications are recommended initially but are supported by Level V evidence (Grade C). Collagenase remains unproven (Grade I), and surgery is reserved for recalcitrant cases (Grade B) with substantial recurrence after local excision and lower recurrence after more radical fasciectomy. Historically, radiotherapy was supported by Level IV data (Grade C) before the aforementioned randomized trial.

Incorrect answer choices:
Choice 1: Collagenase lacks randomized trials in plantar fibromatosis and is graded as insufficient evidence (Grade I) with only limited case reports.
Choice 2: Surgery does not have Level I comparative trials demonstrating superiority and carries a high recurrence with local excision; more radical resection lowers but does not eliminate recurrence.
Choice 3: Controlled data does exist as discussed above.
Choice 5: Orthotics are supported primarily by expert opinion (Level V, Grade C) rather than Level I or II trials.
