## Question

You encounter a complication while performing a total hip arthroplasty (THA) on a 71-year-old female through a posterior approach. As you were placing your acetabular retractors, you encountered brisk arterial bleeding believed to be from the femoral artery. In which of the following positions seen in Figure A was your retractor likely positioned?

![[q217988-a-pelvis-safe-zones2-copy.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Position 1
2. Position 2
3. Position 3
4. Position 4
5. Position 5

## Correct option

**2. Position 2**

## Answer explanation

Injury to the femoral artery can occur during placement of an anterior acetabular retractor in the anterosuperior quadrant while attempting to expose the acetabulum.

Exposure of the acetabulum and retraction of the femur anteriorly during total hip arthroplasty (THA) is important to obtain appropriate cup positioning. Retractors are typically placed anteriorly underneath capsule as well as posteroinferior. It is important, however, to recognize that complications can occur with errant retractor placement. The femoral artery and associated neurovascular bundle crosses the hip joint just anterior to the capsule around the level of the AIIS, and therefore is at risk for damage by retractors in this position. In the anteroinferior quadrant, the obturator nerves and vessels are at risk. In the posteroinferior quadrant, the inferior gluteal nerves/vessels, internal pudendal nerves/vessels and the sciatic nerve are all at risk. The posterosuperior quadrant is a relative safe zone for retractor and screw placement. This said, retractors are necessary for visualization, but must be meticulously placed to avoid complication.

*Kirkpatrick et al. reviewed the relationship of intrapelvic vasculature to the acetabulum as it related to acetabular screw fixation.* *They reviewed both anatomic cross-sectional anatomy as well as CT scans to determine which vessels might be at risk in certain regions of the acetabulum.* **They noted** the iliac vessels are at risk in the anterosuperior region and the obturator vessels are at risk in the anteroinferior region.

*Wasielewski et al. designed a study to assess for the safest zones of the acetabulum, primarily as it relates to screw fixation of acetabular components.* *They designed the four-quadrant safe zone model still used today and noted that the postersuperior and posterioinferior had the best bone stock and were both safer than anterior placement of hardware.* *They reiterate that using this model can help prevent surgeons from unnecessary vascular injury during THA.*

Figure A is a diagram demonstrating potential options for retractor placement with Illustration A showing the quadrants labeled: (1) posterosuperior quadrant, (2) anterosuperior quadrant, (3) anteroinferior quadrant, (4) posteroinferior quadrant, (5) posteroinferior quadrant. Quadrants are drawn first by a line from the ASIS through the center of the acetabulum followed by a line directly perpendicular.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: This represents the posterosuperior quadrant, which is relatively safe for retractor/screw placement.
Answer 3: This represents the anteroinferior quadrant, which is home to the obturator vessels/nerves.
Answers 4&5: These both fall within the posteroinferior quadrant, which houses the sciatic nerve, inferior gluteal nerves/vessels, and the internal pudendal nerves/vessels.
