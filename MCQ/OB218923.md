## Question

A 16-month-old male with an underlying medical condition that leads to abnormal phosphorylation of selected tyrosine residues by upregulated tyrosine kinase activity presents for an initial orthopedic evaluation to establish long-term care. An MRI of the cervical spine reveals foramen magnum stenosis. Which of the following most accurately describes this patient's underlying medical condition?

## Options

1. Gain-of-function mutation in fibroblast growth factor receptor-3
2. Gain-of-function mutation in cartilage oligomeric matrix protein on chromosome 19
3. Gain-of-function in function mutation in COL2A1
4. Loss-of-function mutation in parathryoid hormone-related peptide (PTHrP)
5. Loss-of-function mutation in type X collagen

## Correct option

**1. Gain-of-function mutation in fibroblast growth factor receptor-3**

## Answer explanation

This patient has achondroplasia. Patients with achondroplasia have a mutation in the fibroblast growth factor receptor-3 (FGFR3) gene resulting in abnormal phosphorylation of selected tyrosine residues by upregulated tyrosine kinase activity.

Achondroplasia is a common congenital skeletal dysplasia caused by a sporadic or autosomal dominant gain-of-function mutation in FGFR3 gene. Patients present with rhizomelic dwarfism, lumbar and foramen magnum stenosis, frontal bossing, and normal intelligence. Diagnosis is usually made based on typical clinical and radiographic features on skeletal survey. Treatment involves observation and physical therapy for majority of anomalies. Surgery is indicated in patients with foramen magnum stenosis with sleep apnea or cord compression and progressive spinal stenosis that fails nonoperative treatment.

*Shirley et al. reviewed achondroplasia.* **They reported** that orthopedic manifestations are exhibited in the spine and the extremities. **They concluded** that in the infant with achondroplasia, foramen magnum stenosis may result in brainstem compression with apnea and sudden death, and anatomic anomalies of the vertebral column place the patient at risk for spinal stenosis.

*Margalit et al. reviewed thoracolumbar kyphosis (TLK) in patients with achondroplasia.* **They reported** that in patients with achondroplasia, TLK resolved at walking age in 15% of patients and after a year of walking in 58% of patients. **They concluded** that earlier bracing may slow TLK progression in patients with achondroplasia and developmental motor delay; patients with kyphotic curves between 20-40 degrees should be examined intermittently for progressive deformity or worsening symptoms of spinal cord compression.

*Bodensteiner et al. reviewed neurological complications seen in patients with achondroplasia.* **They reported** that these patients are subject to distortion and the result is that bone impinges on nervous tissue, most commonly at the foramen magnum, spinal canal, and nerve root outlet foramen. **They concluded** that awareness of the range of these complications will, hopefully, allow early and more effective intervention so as to ameliorate the nature and severity of the long-term effects of the neurological complications in patients with achondroplasia.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Multiple Epiphyseal Dysplasia (MED) is a congenital disorder caused most commonly by an autosomal mutation in cartilage oligomeric matrix protein on chromosome 19. MED generally has no characteristic spine manifestations.
Answer 3: Spondyloepiphyseal Dysplasia is a rare congenital disorder most commonly caused by a COL2A1 mutation leading to abnormal Type II collagen synthesis. SED may present with upper cervical instability secondary to odontoid hypoplasia.
Answer 4: Jansen syndrome (a form of metaphyseal chondrodysplasia) is an autosomal dominant genetic defect in parathryoid hormone-related peptide (PTHrP). Generally, there are no characteristic spine manifestations.
Answer 5: Schmidt (a form of metaphyseal chondrodysplasia) is an autosomal dominant genetic defect in type X collagen. It is associated with excessive lumbar lordosis, but generally does not have any characteristic cervical manifestations
