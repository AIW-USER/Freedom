## Question

Figures 27a and 27b show the MRI scans of a patient who has a history of metastatic lung cancer. He has greater right than left motor weakness in the lower extremities and has been unable to independently ambulate for the past 48 hours. He has a life expectancy of 6 to 12 months per his oncologist. Which of the following has the best chance of restoring and maintaining this patient's potential for ambulation?

*Figure A*

![[q6446-b-27b.jpg]]

## Options

1. High-dose radiation therapy
2. Circumferential spinal cord decompression and surgical stabilization with postoperative radiation therapy
3. Enbloc resection
4. Laminectomy alone and IV steroids
5. Multi-drug chemotherapy based on biopsy-proven tissue diagnosis

## Correct option

**2. Circumferential spinal cord decompression and surgical stabilization with postoperative radiation therapy**

## Answer explanation

Surgical treatment with circumferential decompression and postoperative radiation therapy in patients with metastatic spinal cord compression with or without motor weakness has been shown to result in the best prognosis for regaining and maintaining ambulatory status. Laminectomy with or without steroids has proven to be of limited benefit. Chemotherapy alone will not reverse spinal cord compression resulting from most metastatic lesions. Enbloc resection is usually reserved for primary bone tumors, and would not likely improve life expectancy in a patient with metastatic lung cancer.
