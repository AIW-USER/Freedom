## Question

Figure A is the radiograph of a patient who is undergoing a total knee arthroplasty. Intraoperatively, the popliteus tendon is released. What is the main impact of this release on coronal plane balancing?

![[q211350-a-valgus-knee.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Increases medial extension space
2. Increases lateral extension space
3. Increases medial flexion space
4. Increases lateral flexion space
5. No impact on coronal plane balancing

## Correct option

**4. Increases lateral flexion space**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a valgus knee and is undergoing a total knee arthroplasty (TKA). An isolated release of the popliteus may be utilized to preferentially increase the lateral flexion space.

The valgus knee poses unique challenges to the treating surgeon. The goals of a TKA in the valgus knee are to create precise bone cuts, release the tight lateral ligaments, tighten the lax medial ligaments, and balance the flexion and extension gaps. When the knee is found to be tight in flexion, release of the popliteus tendon may be performed to increase the lateral flexion gap. Due to the fact that the tibia maximally internally rotates with knee flexion, the popliteus is tightest in this position despite the relaxing effect in the sagittal plane.

*Favorito et al reviewed the order and sequencing for anatomic release of the lateral structures of the knee.* **They report** that soft-tissue deformities complicate restoration of proper alignment, positioning of components, and attainment of joint stability. **They conclude** that primary or acquired contracture of the lateral capsular and ligamentous structures need to be adequately addressed with the valgus knee to optimize component position and restore soft-tissue balance.

*Krackow et al. reviewed 99 valgus knees with varying severity which required soft-tissue releases.* **They report** that less severe valgus knees were treated with lateral soft-tissue release, while more severe valgus knees were also treated with medial capsular ligament tightening. **They report** similar outcome scores and alignment between groups. **They conclude** that these soft-tissue releases are appropriate based on degree of severity.

Figure A is the AP radiograph of the right knee demonstrating a valgus knee with advanced osteoarthritis.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1-3,5: The popliteus may be released for a preferential impact on the flexion space laterally, with minimal influence on the extension space.
