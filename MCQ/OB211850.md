## Question

A 56-year-old male presents to the ED with 1 week of increasing back pain and leg weakness. He has a history of intravenous drug abuse (IVDA) and smokes 1.5 packs of cigarettes per day. Laboratory analysis reveals WBC: 11K, CRP: 98 mg/L, ESR: 81 mm/h. On exam, he is febrile and found to have 4 out of 5 strength in his bilateral lower extremities. An MRI is obtained and demonstrates a spinal epidural abscess. Which factor is the strongest predictor that THIS patient will likely fail medical treatment?

## Options

1. Age
2. Smoking history
3. Lower extremity weakness
4. Intravenous drug abuse
5. CRP

## Correct option

**3. Lower extremity weakness**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a spinal epidural abscess, likely relating to his IVDA. Neurologic deficits are the strongest predictor of medical treatment failure.

Spinal epidural abscesses are a collection of pus between the dura mater and surrounding fat. The most commonly encountered risk factors are IVDA, immunodeficiency, malignancy, HIV, immunosuppression, and recent spinal procedures. Historically, epidural abscesses have been considered a surgical emergency, however, there has been a recent trend towards nonoperative management. Failure of medical treatment has found to be associated with neurologic deficits (strongest predictor), diabetes, CRP >115 mg/L, WBC >12K, positive blood cultures, age >65 years, and MRSA. In patients who fail nonoperative management, decompressive laminectomy should be performed.

*Patel et al. retrospectively reviewed the risk factors and medical vs. surgical management of 128 patients with spinal epidural abscesses.* **They report** that site-specific pain was the most common presenting symptom, present in 100% of patients. IVDA was the highest associated risk factor (39%) followed by diabetes (22%). **They conclude** that early surgical management improves neurologic outcomes compared with delayed surgery following a trial of medical management.

*Kim et al. review the independent predictors of failure for nonoperative management of spinal epidural abscesses.* *They report on 355 patients who initially underwent nonoperative management, with only 54 patients ultimately requiring surgery.* **They conclude** that incomplete or complete spinal cord deficits were the most significant risk factor for the failure of medical management.

*Al-Hourani et al. review the diagnosis and management of upper cervical epidural abscesses.* **They report** that this is a rare surgical emergency and most commonly found in patients with a history of IVDA, diabetes or who are immunocompromised. **They conclude** that an MRI should be obtained and that treatment has shifted towards surgical management with favorable outcomes.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Age >65 years has been associated with an increased risk of failing medical management.
Answer 2: Nicotine use has not been associated with increased failure of medical treatment for spinal epidural abscesses.
Answer 4: While IVDA is a risk factor for developing spinal epidural abscesses, it has not been found to be a strong predictor of failing medical management.
Answer 5: This patient's CRP is less than the cut-off of 115 for predicting failure of medical management. Additionally, neurologic deficits are the strongest predictor of failed treatment.
