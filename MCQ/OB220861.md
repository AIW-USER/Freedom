## Question

A 6-month-old infant is diagnosed with arthrogryposis. On physical examination, the child is found to have a rigid, unilateral hip dislocation. Radiographs confirm the diagnosis. The child's parents are concerned about future mobility and ask about the best treatment option. Based on the known pathophysiology and established treatment protocols for hip dislocation in arthrogryposis, what is the MOST appropriate initial management strategy?

## Options

1. Attempt closed reduction with a Pavlik harness
2. Close observation and deferral of treatment, as many patients can ambulate with a dislocated hip
3. Open reduction through an anteromedial or anterolateral approach
4. Postpone any surgical intervention until the child is older than 1 year to allow for growth
5. Treatment with spica casting

## Correct option

**3. Open reduction through an anteromedial or anterolateral approach**

## Answer explanation

Unilateral hip dislocations in arthrogryposis are typically rigid and do not respond to non-operative measures, making early open reduction the preferred treatment to prevent pelvic obliquity and aid in ambulation (Answer choice 3).

Hip dislocations in children with arthrogryposis occur early in utero, resulting in stiff, rigid dislocations that are not amenable to non-operative treatment such as bracing or closed reduction. Unilateral dislocation, in particular, should be addressed surgically to prevent pelvic obliquity, which can lead to difficulties with sitting and standing, and progressive scoliosis. Current literature supports performing an open reduction, ideally before 1 year of age, to achieve the best success rate and reduce the need for further surgery. Anteromedial or anterolateral approaches have both been shown to provide good results.

*Miao et al. evaluated the outcomes of early open reduction using a modified Smith-Petersen (anteromedial) approach for hip dislocations in arthrogryposis multiplex congenita.* **They noted** that this approach had a high success rate with low rates of avascular necrosis and recurrence. **They concluded** that early open reduction is an effective and safe treatment option for these patients.

*Van Bosse et al. reviewed the treatment of lower extremity contractures and deformities in arthrogryposis, providing an overview of various surgical and non-surgical approaches.* **They noted** that hip dislocation is a common finding and that the treatment strategy is often guided by the unilateral or bilateral nature of the dislocation. **They concluded** that treatment should be individualized and often involves surgical intervention to address the underlying contractures and instability.

*Bradish reviewed the challenges and principles of managing hip dislocations in arthrogryposis, summarizing the key literature on the topic.* **They noted** that early treatment of unilateral dislocations is important to prevent secondary issues, such as scoliosis and pelvic obliquity. **They concluded** that surgical open reduction is the treatment of choice for most hip dislocations in arthrogryposis.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1 & 5: Non-operative methods are rarely successful due to the rigid nature of the dislocations, which occur early in utero and are typically stiffer. Treatment with bracing or attempted closed reduction is rarely successful and is therefore not recommended.
Answer 2: While some patients with bilateral dislocations can ambulate, unilateral dislocations should be treated to prevent secondary complications.
Answer 4: Surgery prior to 1 year of age is associated with a higher success rate.
