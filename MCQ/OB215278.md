## Question

A 13-year-old male patient played in a pond hockey tournament and ever since he has had significant knee pain. You obtain X-rays, shown in Figure A. On examination, the patient denies fevers, chills, or weight loss. What else could you do as part of the workup for the patient?

![[q215278-a-pre-op-ap-lg.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Infectious disease consultation
2. CT of his abdomen and pelvis
3. Whole body technetium bone scan
4. Angiography of the thigh and knee
5. Skeletal survey to evaluate for fractures

## Correct option

**3. Whole body technetium bone scan**

## Answer explanation

The radiograph shows an expansile, radio-opaque lesion of the distal femur concerning osteogenic malignancy. Diagnostic workup of this lesion prior to treatment should include a whole-body bone scan.

As this is concerning for malignancy (osteosarcoma in this case), staging studies should be performed followed by a biopsy. Radiographic staging studies include CT of the chest to look for pulmonary metastasis, whole-body bone scan to look for other sites of malignancy and/or metastasis, and MRI of the entire femur to look for skip lesions. Other staging studies include laboratory studies CRP, LDH, ESR, CBC, BMP, and coagulation studies. If there is a concern for metastatic disease, which is more likely in adults, PSA, UPEP/SPEP, and CEA can be ordered to help evaluate for common bone-metastasizing malignancies. They however would likely not be needed in this pediatric patient.

*Simon and Finn reviewed diagnostic workup for bone and soft-tissue tumors.* *They discuss common presenting symptoms of bone and soft-tissue tumors, radiographic interpretation, and diagnostic steps for benign, malignant, and metastatic lesions.* For all progressive benign tumors, malignant tumors, and metastatic lesions **they recommend** a bone scan as part of the diagnostic workup. For every study, **they recommend** there is a discussion on what to look for and its clinical importance.

*Steffner and Jang reviewed the staging of bone and soft tissue sarcomas.* Within the article, they review the importance of plain radiographs, MRI of the entire bone to evaluate for skip lesions, CT chest to look for pulmonary metastasis, and bone scan for boney metastasis. If a whole-body bone scan is not feasible **they recommend** whole-body MRI or PET/CT. Within the article, they also review AJCC and Enneking staging systems for malignancy.

Figure A shows a radio-opaque lesion of the distal femur that is destructive in nature and expansile outside the femoral cortex. There is a Codman's triangle medially and onion-skinning laterally proximal on the x-ray indicating a periosteal reaction to the lesion.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: While malignancy and infection can often present similarly, this patient's presentation and imaging are more consistent malignancy. Often what is biopsied is sent for culture to prevent a possibly missed diagnosis of osteomyelitis.
Answer 2: CT of abdomen and pelvis (with chest CT) is part of metastatic disease workup, which is unlikely in a pediatric patient. Only a CT chest should be performed to look for pulmonary metastases.
Answer 4: Angiography is not typically utilized in the staging of osseous malignancies.
Answer 5: Skeletal surveys can be used in the workup for multiple myeloma patients and in non-accidental pediatric trauma patients. It is not typically performed for patients with presentation and exam concerning for osteosarcoma.
