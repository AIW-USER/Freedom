## Answer explanation

The clinical presentation is consistent with an isolated right L5 radiculopathy (isolated weakness in right foot hallux extension and numbness in the dorsum of the right foot). This would likely be the result of compression of the right L5 nerve root, which is depicted in Figure A as compression of the right L5 exiting nerve root.

In the lumbar spine, the location of disc herniation determines its symptoms. Paracentral disc herniations may cause back pain or cauda equina syndrome, usually affecting the lower/transverse nerve root. Foraminal disc herniations affect the higher/exiting nerve root. For instance, L5/S1 foraminal disc herniations cause L5 symptoms while paracentral disc herniations cause S1 symptoms.

*Singh et al. reviewed lumbar spinal stenosis.* **They reported** that lumbar spinal stenosis involves a constellation of symptoms including leg pain, difficulty with ambulation, and/or neurologic deficits, with stenosis occurring either centrally along the lateral recess or in the neuroforamina. **They concluded** that for most patients, lumbar spinal stenosis is successfully managed with activity modification, exercise, NSAIDS, and epidural injection, with surgical intervention reserved for cases with intractable pain, neurologic compromise, or mechanical instability.

*Rhee et al. published an instructional course lecture on radiculopathy and the herniated lumbar disc.* **They reported** that lumbar disc herniation has a favorable natural history, with 80% of the patients having major improvements after 6 weeks, 90% after twelve weeks, and 93% after 24 weeks. **They suggested** that surgical intervention should be independent of disc herniation size and rather based on symptoms and lack of response to nonoperative care.

Figure A demonstrates a right L5-S1 foraminal disc herniation. Figure B demonstrates a left L5-S1 foraminal disc herniation. Figure C demonstrates significant L4-L5 spinal canal stenosis. Figure D demonstrates a right L3-L4 paracentral herniation. Figure E demonstrates the L5-S1 disc with a patent canal and minimal left foraminal stenosis. Illustration A demonstrates how an L5/S1 paracentral disc herniation affects the traversing S1 root, but a far lateral disc herniation affects the L5 root.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: A left L5-S1 foraminal disc herniation would manifest as left L5 radiculopathy. The patient in the stem has right-sided symptoms.
Answer 3: Lumbar spinal stenosis at L3-4 would manifest with symptoms of neurogenic claudication and would likely not present with symptoms of isolated right EHL weakness.
Answer 4: A right L3-4 paracentral herniation would likely manifest as a right L4 radiculopathy, which does not fit the exam in the stem.
Answer 5: A patient with a patent spinal canal and minimal left L5-S1 foraminal stenosis would likely not manifest with the symptoms in this question stem.
