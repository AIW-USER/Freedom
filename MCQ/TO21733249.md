## Question

A patient with grade 3 L5-S1 spondylolisthesis with significant back pain and radiating pain for 6 months is not responding to conservative treatment. X ray shows retroverted pelvis but clinically patient’s spine is having sagittal balance. What would be the most appropriate treatment in this case scenario

## Options

1. Complete Reduction and fusion
2. Partial reduction and fusion
3. Fusion in situ
4. Conservative treatment

## Correct option

**2. Partial reduction and fusion**

## Answer explanation

Ans: B
EXPLANATION: -

![[TO21733249-1.jpg]]

In high grade listhesis with
1. balanced pelvis- in situ fusion is enough.
2. Retroverted pelvis but clinically the spine is balanced sagittally without any forward stoop: Partial reduction and fusion is enough.
3. Retroverted pelvis with clinical sagittal imbalance (patient stooping forward): reduction is mandatory.
The Spinal Deformity Study Group (SDSG) classification is a system for categorizing spondylolisthesis based on radiographic measurements. It was developed in 2011. The SDSG classification is used to evaluate and treat L5-S1 spondylolisthesis. It's not applicable for degenerative spondylolisthesis or L4-L5 pathology.
Steps in the SDSG classification: -
1. Meyerding grading: Quantify the spondylolisthesis as low grade (I and II) or high grade (III, IV, and V)
2. Measure sagittal balance: Determine if the spine is balanced or unbalanced
3. Measure sacro-pelvic alignment: Determine the sacral slope, pelvic tilt, and PI

INTERPRETATION: -
1. Prediction of disease progression: Type 1 and 2 category cases are less likely to progress. Contrarily, type 3 is prone to slip progression, especially in skeletally immature cases.

2. Reduction vs in situ fusion for High grade spondylolisthesis (HGS): type 4 and type 5a patients have a balanced spine, in situ fixation can be tolerated as the patient will continue to have a stable spinal sagittal balance. type 5b and type 6 patients have an unstable pelvic orientation and sagittal spinal imbalance. In these cases, proper reduction is essential to achieve spinal balance requiring more intensive surgical intervention, including 360-degree fusion.

3. Deciding the goal of surgery: though slip grade continues to be the foundation of classification of lumbar spondylolisthesis, measures of spinopelvic balance affect the postoperative outcome significantly. It becomes more pronounced in HGS cases where aggressive slip reduction has been reported to cause postoperative radiculopathy and deficits. So, the first aim of HGS is the correction of the focal LSK and the correction of pelvic-spinal balance. Slip grade reduction is only a second aim, that too a partial correction is considered adequate as a complete reduction of HGS is a potential cause of neurological impairment.
