## Question

A 72-year-old female with a history of chronic kidney disease presents to your office with complaints of worsening left shoulder pain. She previously underwent left anatomic total shoulder arthroplasty 7 years prior. She is concerned that her shoulder is now infected. Which of the following has a significant independent correlation with positive Propionibacterium acnes colonization after total shoulder arthroplasty?

## Options

1. Age >70
2. Chronic kidney disease
3. Female gender
4. Presence of radiolucent lines around the glenoid
5. Presence of native hair at the surgical site

## Correct option

**5. Presence of native hair at the surgical site**

## Answer explanation

This patient has concerns for a shoulder prosthetic joint infection. Of the variables listed, only presence of hair (ie. surgical incision over a site with native hair), has been cited to have a significant correlation with positive P acnes colonization.

Shoulder prosthetic joint infections are serious complications of shoulder arthroplasty and a major cause for revision within the first 2 years. Diagnosis is multifaceted and involves a high index of suspicion given the indolent nature of the most common organism Cutibacterium acnes. Elevated inflammatory markers, radiographic changes around the prosthesis and aspiration results can all assist with diagnosis. Male gender, presence of hair, humeral loosening on radiographs show a significant correlation with positive P acnes colonization. Treatment generally involves prolonged IV antibiotics and two-stage revision arthroplasty.

*Hsu et al. reviewed P acnes infection following shoulder arthroplasty.* **They reported** that these infections do not present with obvious signs of infection, such as swelling, erythema, drainage, or tenderness, but rather are of the so-called stealth type, presenting with unexplained pain, stiffness, or component loosening months to years after the index arthroplasty. **They concluded** that recovery of P acnes from the surgical wounds requires that multiple specimens for culture be taken from different areas of the shoulder to reduce sampling error, and cultures should be held for two weeks on multiple culture media.

*Koh et al. reviewed the rate of P. acnes colonization in patients undergoing primary shoulder arthroplasty.* **They reported** that male gender and presence of hair had significantly higher rates of P. acnes superficial cultures. they concluded that male gender and presence of hair were significant risk factors for P. acnes colonization.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Age has not been cited to have a significant correlation with positive P acnes colonization
Answer 2: CKD has not been cited to have a significant correlation with positive P acnes colonization
Answer 3: MALE gender (not female) been cited to have a significant correlation with positive P acnes colonization
Answer 4: Glenoid component loosening is the most common cause of TSA failure and is not specific to infection. Often, the presence of radiographic lines does not correlate with symptoms and does not always correlate with clinical failure. Humeral component loosening is much more concerning for a shoulder prosthetic joint infection
