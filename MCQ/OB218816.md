## Question

A 44-year-old male presents to the clinic for continued management of left leg pain radiating from the buttock down to the anterior knee and medial malleolus, which has been present for 12 weeks. On examination, weakness was noted with knee extension. Previous lumbar flexion and extension radiographs did not demonstrate any instability. MRI obtained demonstrated disc herniation with nerve compression. He has previously attempted physical therapy and spinal injections, with minimal improvement. Which of the following surgeries would be most appropriate to address the patient's symptoms?

## Options

1. L3-4 far lateral microdiscectomy
2. L3-4 posterior lumbar interbody fusion
3. L4-5 far lateral microdiscectomy
4. L4-5 hemilaminectomy with paracentral microdiscectomy
5. L4-5 anterior lumbar interbody fusion

## Correct option

**3. L4-5 far lateral microdiscectomy**

## Answer explanation

This patient exhibits symptoms consistent with an L4 radiculopathy recalcitrant to nonoperative intervention. The most suitable procedure for this patient would be L4-5 far lateral microdiscectomy (Answer 3).

Lumbar disc herniations are ubiquitous in spine practices, causing pain and occasionally weakness in specific distributions. In the lumber spine, the nerve root travels underneath the same pedicle (e.g., the L4 nerve root runs under the L4 pedicle) when exiting the spinal canal. Moreover, the location of herniation within the lumbar spine affects different nerve roots. For example, paracentral herniations affect the traversing nerve root (L4 at the L3-4 level), whereas a far lateral herniation affects the exiting nerve root (L3 nerve root at the L3-4 level). After failed nonoperative treatment, the Spine Patient Outcomes Research Trial (SPORT) demonstrated improved patient outcomes following lumbar discectomy compared to continued nonoperative treatment, highlighting the appropriateness of the intervention in the setting of continued symptoms.

*Weinstein et al. performed the SPORT trial, which was a prospective, randomized multicenter trial examining the impact of surgery on lumbar disc herniation, comprising 743 patients.* Utilizing an intent-to-treat analysis, discectomy was favorable, with faster symptom improvement for patients with surgery. While no statistical difference was observed between those who had diskectomy versus those who did not at two years, a very high crossover rate disrupted this data. Therefore, most consider the as-treated analysis as a more accurate representation of the true clinical effect of treatment.

*Weinstein et al. reported on their four-year SPORT results and* **noted** that those treated surgically for intervertebral disc herniation showed significantly greater improvement in pain, function, satisfaction, and self-rated progress over four years compared to their nonoperative counterparts. **They found** that, at four years, there was no significant difference in work status between the surgical and nonsurgical groups. **The authors conclude** surgery for lumbar disc herniation improves outcomes compared to nonoperative treatment but fails to impact work status.

*Dr. Weber performed a prospective, controlled trial of 280 patients randomized into surgical and non-surgical treatment for lumbar disc herniation.* **The author found** statistically significant better results in the surgically treated group at the one-year follow-up. After four years, the operated patients continued to demonstrate better results, but the difference was no longer statistically significant. These findings suggest surgical intervention allows patients to return to activity at a better rate than their nonoperative counterparts.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: L3-4 far lateral microdiscectomy would target the exiting nerve root (L3).
Answer 2: L3-4 posterior lumbar interbody fusion is not indicated in this patient, given her lack of instability.
Answer 4: L4-5 hemilaminectomy with paracentral microdiscectomy would improve symptoms in the L5 nerve distribution.
Answer 5: L4-5 anterior lumbar interbody fusion would not be indicated in this patient given his lack of instability.
