## Question

A 5-year-old male presents with bowed legs. You suspect rickets due to nutritional Vitamin D deficiency. You refer the patient for endocrinology evaluation and order several laboratory tests. Which of the following laboratory findings are consistent with your suspected diagnosis?

## Options

1. Decreased calcium and increased parathyroid hormone levels
2. Decreased calcium and parathyroid hormone levels
3. Increased calcium and decreased parathryoid hormone levels
4. Normal serum calcium and parathyroid hormone levels
5. Normal 25(OH) Vitamin D and decreased 1,25(OH) Vitamin D levels

## Correct option

**1. Decreased calcium and increased parathyroid hormone levels**

## Answer explanation

This patient presents with Vitamin D deficiency rickets. Patients with Vitamin D deficiency rickets most often have decreased calcium and phosphorus levels with increased alkaline phosphatase and parathyroid hormone levels (Answer choice 1).

Rickets is a metabolic bone disease with multiple underlying etiologies. Its overall pathophysiology is a defect in mineralization caused by a disruption of calcium and phosphate homeostasis. It is largely classified as Vitamin-D resistant, Vitamin-D deficient (nutritional), or Vitamin-D dependent. Treatment is based on the underlying cause. Patients with Vitamin D-deficient rickets have low gastrointestinal absorption of calcium, leading to increased parathyroid hormone levels. This, in turn, leads to increased bony resorption and alkaline phosphatase levels.

*Nield et al. published a review article on rickets.* When discussing nutritional rickets, **they highlight** that patients who are solely breastfed, dark-skinned, or with limited sunlight exposure have a higher risk of developing this condition. **They discuss** that nutritional rickets typically presents within the first two years of life with short stature, gait abnormality, and sometimes developmental delay. Infants younger than six months can present with hypocalcemic tetany or seizures.

*Horn et al. published a retrospective review on guided growth to treat lower limb deformities in patients with hypophosphatemic rickets.* *They treated a total of 23 limbs, with eight limbs in valgus and 15 in varus.* *All patients with a valgus deformity were treated successfully at the end of treatment, while 8 of the 15 (53%) varus limbs had successful treatment.* **The authors concluded** that guided growth is a successful way of addressing coronal plane deformities in patients with X-linked hypophosphatemic rickets.

Incorrect Answer Choices:
Answer choices 2-4: These combinations of findings are not consistent with the increased parathyroid hormone and decreased calcium levels seen in patients with Vitamin D-deficient rickets.
Answer choice 5: This is most consistent with Type 1 Vitamin D-dependent rickets.
