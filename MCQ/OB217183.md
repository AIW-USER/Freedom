## Question

A 64-year-old male is evaluated for a painful, swollen right knee 5 weeks after undergoing primary total knee arthroplasty (TKA). As part of his workup, a right knee aspiration is performed and the synovial fluid white blood cell count returns at 12,500 cells/microliter. How should this value be interpreted in the context of evaluating for a prosthetic joint infection (PJI)?

## Options

1. It is above the cutoff of 3,000 cells/microliter in the perioperative period; a PJI is likely present
2. It is below the cutoff of 25,000 cells/microliter in the perioperative period; a PJI is unlikely
3. It is above the cutoff of 10,000 cells/microliter in the perioperative period; a PJI is likely present
4. It is below the cutoff of 50,000 cells/microliter in the perioperative period; a PJI is unlikely
5. It is not possible to interpret the synovial WBC count within 6 weeks of a primary TKA

## Correct option

**3. It is above the cutoff of 10,000 cells/microliter in the perioperative period; a PJI is likely present**

## Answer explanation

Among the listed cutoff values, 10,000 cells/microliter is the most correct answer to indicate an acute prosthetic joint infection (PJI).

PJI is a rare and devastating complication in total joint arthroplasty. Despite technological advances, diagnosis of PJI remains difficult. This is especially true in the acute perioperative period when post-surgical hemarthrosis and inflammation can confound the interpretation of the synovial fluid WBC count. Cutoff values for acute PJI have evolved. *In 2011, Bedair et al. reported a cutoff of 27.8k, whereas, more recently in 2018 Parvizi et al completed a multicenter study and* **concluded** a cutoff of 10k cells is more accurate in the acute period.

*Parvizi et al. updated their definition of PJI in 2018.* *They kept the major criteria for a chronic periprosthetic joint infection the same (an infection is indicated when 2 positive cultures of the same organism are isolated, or a sinus tract is present), but made several changes to the minor criteria.* The synovial WBC cutoff for acute (<90d) infections is set at 10k, and the cutoff for chronic (>90d) infections is set at 3k. The update to the MSIS criteria provides a sensitivity of 97.7% and a specificity of 99.5%.

*Bedair et al.* (2011) evaluated the diagnostic performance of the synovial fluid WBC count within 6 weeks of a primary TKA. **They found** that a cutoff value of 27,800 cells/microliter had a positive predictive value of 94% and a negative predictive value of 98% for the diagnosis of a PJI in the first 6 weeks following a primary TKA. **The authors concluded** that the standard cutoff value of 3,000 cells/microliter used at their institution would have led to unnecessary operations.

The major criteria are the same as the 2011 MSIS consensus, but the minor criteria have changed and there is a difference in acute versus chronic, as well as scores for intraoperative findings in patients with inconclusive minor criteria.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1 and 2: The cutoff for synovial fluid WBC count for acute infections is 10k.
Answer 4: While a PJI is unlikely, 50k is the cutoff typically used for native, non-prosthetic joint infections (septic arthritis).
Answer 5: The synovial fluid WBC count can be interpreted within 6 weeks of a primary TKA.
