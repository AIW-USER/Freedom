## Options

1. C1-C2 arthrodesis
2. Open reduction
3. Halter traction, muscle relaxants and analgesics
4. Halo skeletal traction
5. Soft collar

## Correct option

**5. Soft collar**

## Answer explanation

This patient has Atlantoaxial Rotatory Displacement (AARD). Conservative management with use of a soft collar when subluxation is present for <1 week (traumatic or Grisel's disease) is recommended.

Atlantoaxial rotatory displacement (AARD) is a pediatric cervical spine rotatory instability caused by C1-C2 subluxation or facet dislocation. The most common presentation is a young child who presents with torticollis. Grisel's syndrome is the condition of AARD following a respiratory infection, retropharyngeal abscess, or recent head/neck surgery. Diagnosis is made by dynamic CT scan of the cervical spine. Treatment can be a soft collar, halter traction, halo traction, or surgical fusion depending on the chronicity of the condition.

*Pilge et al. reported on a case of Grisel's syndrome in a patient that presented 2 months after surgery (cochlea implantation) with a persistent torticollis.* **They noted** that radiographs revealed a subluxated atlantoaxial joint. They treated the patient with manual repositioning, immobilization with a cervical collar for 2 weeks, and antibiotics. **They concluded** that this treatment led to shorter recovery and avoidance of halo fixation.

*Fielding et al. reviewed seventeen cases of irreducible atlanto-axial rotatory subluxation.* **They reported** that all patients had torticollis and restricted, often painful neck motion. **They noted** that the diagnosis was suggested by the plain radiographs and CT imaging.

Illustration depicts the Fielding and Hawkins classification for atlantoaxial rotatory subluxation. Type I: Simple rotatory displacement without anterior shift. The transverse ligament is intact and the dens acts as a pivot point. Type II: Rotatory and anterior displacement between 3-5 mm. The transverse ligament is injured and the opposite facet acts as the pivot point. Type III: Rotatory and anterior displacement greater than 5 mm with both lateral atlantoaxial joints anteriorly subluxed. The transverse ligament and facet capsules are injured. Type IV: Posterior subluxation of both lateral atlantoaxial joints. Rare and described in adult patients with rheumatoid arthritis and destruction of the dens.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1-4: Initial treatment for this is soft collar immobilization and rest for 1 week. If subluxation persists, the patient should be transitioned to halter traction, and be given muscle relaxants and pain medications for up to 3 weeks. After a month of intractable symptoms, the patient can be placed in halo traction. If this is ineffective, a C1-C2 arthrodesis should be considered
