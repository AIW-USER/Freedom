## Question

A 17-year-old football player limps off the sideline after a collision during the game. You evaluate him on the sideline and make the correct diagnosis. You explain to him and his parents that immobilizing his knee in 120 degrees of flexion will expedite his recovery. His parents are skeptical and take him to get an MRI the following day. What is the most likely finding on subsequent MRI?

## Options

1. Bucket handle tear of the medial meniscus
2. Grade 3 injury to the MCL
3. Hematoma formation in the anterior compartment of the thigh
4. Mid-substance tear of the ACL
5. Partial tear of the proximal hamstring tendon

## Correct option

**3. Hematoma formation in the anterior compartment of the thigh**

## Answer explanation

Immobilization in flexion is the treatment for a quadriceps contusion. An MRI would reveal hematoma formation in the anterior compartment of the thigh.

Quadriceps contusions are common injuries in collision athletes. It is caused by a direct blow to the anterior thigh. Patients will have tenderness and ecchymosis over the anterior thigh. Mild cases have knee range of motion over 90 degrees, whereas more severe injuries have range of motion <45 degrees. The mainstay of treatment is immobilization of the leg in 120 degrees of knee flexion for 24 hours, followed by quadriceps stretching and strengthening. In rare cases, myositis ossificans can develop in the anterior compartment.

*Aronen et al. reported their results on immediate immobilization in 120 degrees of flexion after quadriceps contusion.* *They prospectively collected outcomes of 47 military patients and immobilized them for 24 hours, followed by quadriceps stretching and strengthening.* The average return to full activities was 3.5 days.

*Ryan et al. published their study of 117 quadriceps contusion in West Point cadets.* **They found** that the average disability time varied depending on severity, with mild cases taking 13 days and severe cases taking 21 days to fully recover. **They also found** a 9% rate of myositis ossificans. Risk factors for myositis ossificans were knee motion less than 120 degrees, injury occurring during football, previous quadriceps injury, delay in treatment greater than 3 days, and ipsilateral knee effusion.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1, 2, 4, and 5: None of these injuries are treated with immediate immobilization in flexion.
