## Question

A 44-year-old male presents to the emergency room after falling off a ladder. Radiographs are obtained and demonstrate a comminuted calcaneus fracture. Which of the following is true of potential complications associated with these injuries?

## Options

1. Open injuries have an equivalent rate of wound complications after open reduction to closed injuries
2. Malunion with a talar declination angle <20 may lead to limited dorsiflexion
3. Flexor hallucis longus injury most commonly occurs with medial to lateral screw placement at level of sustentaculum tali
4. Risk of subtalar arthritis is equivalent between those treated with nonoperative management and ORIF
5. The superficial peroneal nerve is most at risk with use of an extensile lateral approach

## Correct option

**2. Malunion with a talar declination angle <20 may lead to limited dorsiflexion**

## Answer explanation

Malunion with a talar declination angle <20 may lead to limited dorsiflexion of the ankle.

Calcaneus fractures are the most common fractured tarsal bone and are associated with a high degree of morbidity and disability. Diagnosis is made radiographically with foot radiographs with CT scan often being required for surgical planning. Treatment is nonoperative versus operative based on fracture displacement and alignment, associated soft tissue injury, and patient risk factors. The talar declination angle is drawn on the weight-bearing lateral foot radiograph between the mid-talar axis and the supporting surface. If the first metatarsal axis is steep, the mid-talar axis approaches the horizontal and is projected above the first metatarsal. This indicates that the longitudinal arch is relatively high and there is pes cavus. Conversely, where there is pes planus and a low arch, the first metatarsal axis is flattened and the mid-talar angle increases such that the mid-talar axis passes inferior to the first metatarsal. This may lead to limited dorsiflexion of the ankle.

*Hsu et al. reviewed intra-articular calcaneus fractures.* **They reported** that common complications include hematoma formation, skin edge necrosis, wound breakdown, and superficial or deep infection. **They concluded** that as a result, less invasive techniques have been developed in recent years, including limited-incision sinus tarsi open reduction and internal fixation, percutaneous fixation, and arthroscopic-assisted fixation in an attempt to lower complication rates.

*Folk et al. retrospectively reviewed associations between preoperative variables and the occurrence of wound complications in the surgical treatment of calcaneus fractures.* **They reported** that smoking, diabetes, and open fractures all increase the risk of wound complication after surgical stabilization of calcaneus fractures. **They concluded** that cumulative risk factors increase the likelihood of wound complications.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Wound complications (10-25%) are increased risk in smokers, diabetics, and open injuries.
Answer 3: Damage to the FHL is most at risk with placement of lateral to medial (NOT medial to lateral) screws, especially at level of sustentaculum tali (constant fragment).
Answer 4: There is an increased risk of subtalar arthritis with nonoperative management.
Answer 5: Nerve injury is a possibility (but not of the superficial peroneal nerve), but rather of the sural nerve.
