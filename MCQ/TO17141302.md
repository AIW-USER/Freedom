Chapter: Regional Orthopedics MCQs — Compound Palmar Ganglion

## Question

A 40 year old truck driver presented to your clinic with complaint of dumb-bell shaped swelling on his left hand and wrist across flexion crease of the wrist joint. The swelling shows a positive cross fluctuation test. What is the likely underlying comorbidity predisposing to the underlying condition?

## Options

1. Tuberculosis
2. Rheumatoid arthritis
3. Hyperparathyroidism
4. Hypoparathyroidism

## Correct option

**1. Tuberculosis**

## Answer explanation

Compound palmar ganglion is also known as tuberculous tenosynovitis of flexor tendons of wrist and hand and is a rare entity. Compound palmar ganglion is an uncommon condition characterized by a swelling in the distal part of the volar aspect of the wrist and communicating with another swelling over the palm across the flexor retinaculum.

On clinical examination, swelling both above and below the proximal wrist crease is found, with positive cross-fluctuation. Compound palmar ganglion affects the radial and ulnar bursae. In 80% of cases, the ulnar bursa communicates with the radial bursa, which accounts for horseshoe-shaped tenosynovitis that simultaneously affects both the bursae.

Though the incidence is very less, it is not uncommon in developing countries. The clinical picture is very typical and is always confirmed by histopathology. The disease can progress and result in a gross destruction of structures around the wrist and hence requires excision without delay.

Other causes of compound palmar ganglion are:

SLE, rheumatoid arthritis, gouty arthritis, pigmented villonodular synovitis of tendon sheath and fungal infection, etc.

Answer: 1
