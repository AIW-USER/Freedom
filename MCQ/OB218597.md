## Question

A 70-year-old female presents to your office with complaints of a painful bunion deformity and pain under the plantar forefoot. On examination, she has a callus on the dorsal aspect of her second toe, and the appearance is strongly suggestive of a hammer toe. Attenuation of the plantar plate would result in which deformity in this patient?

## Options

1. Distal interphalangeal joint flexion
2. Distal interphalangeal joint malrotation
3. Proximal interphalangeal joint extension
4. Metatarsophalangeal joint malrotation
5. Metatarsophalangeal joint extension

## Correct option

**5. Metatarsophalangeal joint extension**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a hammer toe, and attenuation of the plantar plate would result in MTP joint extension.

Hammer toe is a lesser toe deformity characterized by PIP flexion, DIP extension, and slight MTP extension. The diagnosis is made clinically with a rigid or flexible lesser toe deformity. Treatment is initially a trial of nonoperative management with shoe modification. Surgical management is indicated for progressive deformity, fixed contractures, and dorsal toe ulcerations.

*Akoh et al. reviewed lesser toe plantar plate injuries at the metatarsophalangeal (MTP) joint as a common source of metatarsalgia.* **They reported** that the second MTP joint is the most commonly affected digit and that the fibrocartilaginous plantar plate is the most important static stabilizer of the MTP joint. **They concluded** that untreated plantar plate instability can lead to hammer toe and mallet toe deformities.

*Flint et al. reviewed lesser metatarsophalangeal (MTP) joint instability as a common cause of forefoot pain.* **They reported** that the plantar plate could be repaired through a dorsal approach with reliable outcomes. **They concluded** that plantar plate repair was a viable option to anatomically restore the ligamentous support in the unstable lesser MTP joint.
