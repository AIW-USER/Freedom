The fact that curve in picture is Energy dissipation curve of a tendon being cyclically loaded, coupled with classical clinical points viz. young female typist with tenderness on dorsolateral radius point to diagnosis of Dequervain’s disease (tenosynovitis involving first extensor compartment of wrist viz. abductor pollicis longus and extensor pollicis brevis due to repetitive activity involving thumb use like typing).

Stress strain relationship for ligaments/ tendons

As with all biological tissues, the hierarchical structure of ligaments and tendons has a significant influence on their mechanical behavior.

Unlike bone, not as much quantitative structure function relationships, have been ascertained for ligaments and tendons. This is for two reasons:

- the hierarchical structure of ligaments and tendons is much more difficult to quantify than bone
- ligaments and tendons exhibit both nonlinear and viscoelastic behavior even under physiologic loading, which is more difficult to analyze than the linear behavior of bone.

If one neglects viscoelastic behavior, a typical stress strain curve for ligaments and tendons can be drawn as:

![[TO17141322-2.jpg]]

There are three major regions in this stress strain curve:

- the toe or toe-in region
- the linear region and
- the yield and failure region.

- In physiologic activity, most ligaments and tendons exist in the toe and somewhat in the linear region.
- This constitutes a nonlinear stress strain curve, since the slope of the toe-in region is different from that of the linear region.

In terms of structure function relationships, the toe-in region represents "un-crimping" of the crimp in the collagen fibrils. Since it is easier to stretch out the crimp of the collagen fibrils, this part of the stress strain curve shows a relatively low stiffness (change in tendon length in relation to load applied).

As the collagen fibrils become uncrimped, then we see that the collagen fibril backbone itself is being stretched, which gives rise to a stiffer material (liner region). As individual fibrils within the ligament or tendon begin to fail damage accumulates, stiffness is reduced and the ligament/tendons begins to fail (yield region).

Thus, a key concept is that the overall behavior of ligaments and tendons depends on the individual crimp structure and failure of the collagen fibrils.

Viscoelasticity: An important aspect of ligament/tendon behavior is their viscoelasticity. Viscoelasticity indicates time dependent mechanical behavior. The relationship between stress and strain is not constant but depends on the time of displacement or load.

There are two major behavior characteristics of a viscoelasticity material like tendon or ligament: -

- Creep: This is increasing deformation under a constant load only.

- This contrasts with an elastic material which does not exhibit increase deformation no matter how long the load is applied, provided load is same.

- Stress relaxation: This means that the stress will be reduced or will relax under a constant deformation.

Hysteresis or Energy dissipation curve

- This means that if a viscoelastic material is loaded and unloaded, the unloading curve will not follow the loading curve. The difference between the two curves represents the amount of energy that is dissipated or lost during loading.

![[TO17141322-5.jpg]]

Fig: Energy dissipation curve when a tendon/ ligament is loaded and unloaded; the unloading curve will not follow the loading curve. The energy (shaded area) is lost as heat.

- Now, with cyclical loading the stress (load)- strain (elongation) curve gradually shifts to right.

![[TO17141322-6.jpg]]

- It shows that the amount of hysteresis (or energy dissipation) under cyclic loading is reduced and eventually the stress-strain curve becomes reproducible.
- This gives rise to the use of word “pseudo-elasticity” for tendons/ ligaments to represent the nonlinearity of their stress strain behavior.
