## Question

Figures 52a and 52b show the radiographs of a 30-year-old-man who had Ewing's sarcoma at the age of 10 treated with radiation therapy for local control. What is the most likely diagnosis?

![[q7537-a-52a.jpg]]
*Figure A*

![[q7537-b-52b.jpg]]
*Figure B*

## Options

1. Recurrent Ewing's sarcoma
2. Osteomyelitis
3. Radiation-induced sarcoma
4. Metastatic adenocarcinoma
5. Bone infarct

## Correct option

**3. Radiation-induced sarcoma**

## Answer explanation

The most likely diagnosis is radiation-induced sarcoma, which typically occur years after the radiation is received. The radiographs show a lytic lesion in the area of previously treated Ewing's sarcoma. Occasionally Ewing's sarcoma is treated with radiation therapy, typically in locations where limb salvage is not feasible. Recurrent Ewing's sarcoma would be unusual 20 years after the primary tumor, bone infarct is not purely radiolucent, adenocarcinoma is rare in patients younger than age 40, and osteomyelitis would be an unusual diagnosis in this clinical setting.
