## Question

An 8-year-old patient with spina bifida presents with bilateral hip dislocations. Physical exam is remarkable for signficant periarticular contracture. Which of the following is accurate regarding management of hip dislocation in a patient with mid-lumbar myelomeningocele?

## Options

1. Muscle transfer and open reduction with proximal femoral and/or pelvic osteotomy reliably improves ambulatory ability and quality of life
2. Contracture release alone is not indicated as this does not reliably improve hip range of motion
3. Long-term clinical outcomes are most dependent on maintenance of hip reduction
4. Long-term functional outcomes are not associated with hip status
5. Radiographic appearance of the hip following muscle transfer correlates with functionally important improvements in ambulatory ability

## Correct option

**4. Long-term functional outcomes are not associated with hip status**

## Answer explanation

The long-term functional outcomes of patients with myelomeningocele are unrelated to the hip status but instead correlate more closely with the neurological level. As a result, open reduction is not recommended given the high rate of redislocation and surgical complications.

The operative management and optimal surgical technique for hip dislocation in mid-lumber myelomeningocele remains a subject of controversy. Contracture release, muscle transfer, and bony containment procedures have demonstrated inconsistent outcomes, and while these may results in reduction of the hip, there is no clear improvement in function or quality of life. Despite improvements in periarticular strength following external oblique abdominal or iliopsoas muscle transfer, patients do not experience any significant improvement in gait patterns compared to those without. Muscle transfer furthermore does not decrease the need for assistive orthoses.

*Thompson et al. evaluated the functional status of adult patients with myelomeningocele over a 12-month period.* **The authors found** no significant correlation between standardized mental or physical functional scores and hip status after controlling for neurological level, though improved range of motion did portend a better outcome. As a result, **they concluded** that there was no functional benefit to maintaining hip reduction in this patient population.

*Yildirim et al. compared the radiographic and clinical outcomes between patients with myelomeningocele who underwent open reduction with and without muscle transfer.* Despite mild but clinically insignificant improvement in abductor strength, **the authors showed** no difference between the groups in regard to maintenance of reduction, ambulatory ability, or need for assistive orthoses. **They concluded** that routine muscle transfer did not improve the outcome following management of hip instability.

*Wright et al. performed a systematic review of the literature to determine the value of hip and spine surgery in the patient with spina bifida.* Hip reduction did not demonstrate any impact on ambulatory ability, which was instead primarily determined by neurological level. The rate of redislocation ranged from 30-45% following surgery. **The author noted** that the controversy in the literature arises largely from the abundance of uncontrolled case series and non-comparative studies.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Muscle transfers and bony containment procedures have not demonstrated consistent improvements in ambulatory ability or quality of life.
Answer 2: Contracture release has been shown to provide a functional benefit through improvements in hip range of motion. This may in turn contribute to improved ambulatory ability regardless of hip status.
Answer 3: Long-term clinical and functional outcomes have been shown in multiple studies to be independent of hip status.
Answer 5: Functional outcomes do not correlate with maintenance of radiographic hip reduction. In fact, patients with maintained reduction have been shown to have increased pain scores over patients with persistently or recurrently dislocated hips.
