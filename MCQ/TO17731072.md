## Question

Oncogenic Osteomalacia, a paraneoplastic syndrome can be associated commonly with all of the following tumors Except [Metabolic Bone Disorder-Osteomalacia]

## Options

1. Hemangiopericytoma
2. Giant cell tumor
3. Chondromyxoid fibroma
4. Multiple Myeloma

## Correct option

**4. Multiple Myeloma**

## Answer explanation

Ans: D

Oncogenic Osteomalacia is a paraneoplastic syndrome (tumor induced Osteomalacia- TIO) mostly involving adolescents and young adults characterized by mesenchymal tumors that secrete the fibroblast growth factor* (FGF-23) aka ‘Phosphatonin’ that produces Osteomalacia and hypophosphatemic rickets due to phosphaturia caused by phosphatonin.

These tumors include fibrous dysplasia, hemangiopericytoma (most common cause), giant cell tumor, osteosarcoma, chondroblastoma, chondromyxoid fibroma, malignant fibrous histiocytoma, fibrosarcoma, metaphyseal fibrous defect and hemangioma. Most tumors producing this condition are benign and slow growing in nature.

Orthopedic manifestations include: fatigue, generalized bone pain, proximal muscle weakness and at times even pathological fractures.

Biochemical abnormalities consisting of hypophosphatemia (severe), normocalcemic, and increased levels of ALP.

As opposed to Osteomalacia (dietary) serum phosphate levels are unusually low in TIO and the serum PTH levels are not as elevated, as in former.

X rays may show diffuse osteopenia and the classical Looser’s zones. An Octreotide scan (also known as somatostatin receptor scintigraphy, used to check the body for the presence of neuroendocrine tumor cells) can be very useful to reach the diagnosis.

![[TO17731072-1.png]]

Fig showing Looser’s zones. These are indentations caused on a soft Osteomalacia bone by vascular crossings.

Tumor excision is the prime line of treatment. Resolution of hypophosphatemia and Osteomalacia occur within hours of resection. Still a supplementation regime for phosphate and vitamin D should be given till documentation of normal levels is made.

*Fibroblast growth factor 23 (Phosphatonin) is a peptide produced by Phosphatonin gene located on chromosome 12 (x arm). Overexpression of same by a tumor leads to TIO.
