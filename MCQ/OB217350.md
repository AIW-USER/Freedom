## Question

A 17-year old male pitcher sustained an injury to the medial elbow while practicing. MRI reveals a partial tear to the origin of the medial ulnar collateral ligament. The decision is made to proceed with surgical repair of the ligament. Which of the following is true regarding the axis of rotation of this structure?

## Options

1. Located at the anterior-inferior aspect of the medial epicondyle
2. Located at the anterior-superior aspect of the medial epicondyle
3. Located at the posterior-inferior aspect of the medial epicondyle
4. Located at the posterior-superior aspect of the medial epicondyle
5. Located at the sublime tubercle

## Correct option

**1. Located at the anterior-inferior aspect of the medial epicondyle**

## Answer explanation

This patient is undergoing a medial ulnar collateral ligament (UCL) repair. The origin of the UCL is located at the anterior-inferior aspect of the medial epicondyle.

Medial ulnar collateral ligament injuries are characterized by attenuation or rupture of the UCL of the elbow leading to valgus instability in overhead throwing athletes. Diagnosis is usually made by a combination of physical exam and MRI studies. Treatment for most individuals is rest and physical therapy. Surgery is reserved for high level overhead athletes such as pitchers. UCL repair is mostly performed in young athletes with avulsion-type tear patterns. UCL anterior band ligament reconstruction (Tommy John Surgery) is indicated in high-level throwers that want to continue competitive sports or those who have failed nonoperative management with partial tears and are willing to undergo extensive rehabilitation.

*Paletta et al. reviewed UCL injuries.* **They reported** that utilization of some type of autograft tissue has been the standard of care for UCL-injured athletes but there has been a recent revival in the interest in primary repair of the UCL as an option for the treatment of select athletes. **They concluded** that early clinical results indicate short-term successful outcomes of the procedure in properly selected athletes.

*Savoie et al. reviewed young athletes with injuries to the UCL that underwent repair of the ligament without graft reconstruction.* **They reported** that primary repair of proximal and distal injuries of the UCL is a viable alternative in the non-professional athlete. **They concluded** that graft reconstructions may not be necessary to obtain favorable outcomes and rapid return to sports in non-professional athletes who require surgical intervention for medial elbow instability.

*Moore et al. reviewed UCL injuries.* **They reported** that injuries of the UCL can range from partial thickness tears, end avulsions, to chronic attritional ruptures with poor tissue quality. **They concluded** that if an athlete fails conservative measures, surgery is recommended for those desiring to return to overhead sports, and that UCL reconstruction has been the gold standard for all varieties of UCL injuries.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 2-4: The axis of rotation is located at the origin of the UCL which can be found at the anterior-inferior aspect of the medial epicondyle of the humerus
Answer 5: The sublime tubercle is the insertion of the UCL, but the axis of rotation (point of isometry) occurs at the origin of the UCL.
