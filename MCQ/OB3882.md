## Question

The condition seen in Figure A may be associated with all of the following EXCEPT:

![[q3882-a-hallux-rigidus.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Inverted gait
2. Restricted dorsiflexion
3. Decreased push-off strength
4. Transfer metatarsalgia
5. Flexor hallucis longus rupture

## Correct option

**5. Flexor hallucis longus rupture**

## Answer explanation

The radiograph shows hallux rigidus which does not lead to flexor hallucis longus rupture.

Hallux rigidus is a term to describe the condition characterized by pain and limited motion, particularly dorsiflexion, at the first metatarsophalangeal joint. Pain and limitations in dorsiflexion lead to forefoot accommodation through internal rotation with subsequent decrease in push-off strength and transfer metatarsalgia. Surgical treatment is indicated when nonoperative measures have failed to provide relief and are tailored to each patient based on degree of arthritis at the metatarsophalangeal joint.

*Coughlin et al. retrospectively reviewed characteristics of 114 patients who underwent cheilectomy or arthrodesis for hallux rigidus.* **They found** no association between hallux rigidus and primus elevatus, first ray hypermobility, long first metatarsal, achilles or gastrocnemius tightness, abnormal foot posture, symptomatic hallux valgus, adolescent onset, shoeware, or occupation. They did find an association between hallux rigidus and hallux valgus interphalangeus, bilateral involvement in patients with a family history, unilateral involvement following a history of trauma, and female gender.

*Giannini et al. presented the results of 111 feet with Grade 0 through Grade 3 hallux rigidus treated surgically.* *They describe their recommended treatments for each grade individually.* **They concluded** surgical treatment for hallux rigidus should be individualized for each patient based on the degree of arthritis and other clinical considerations.

Figure A shows a radiograph with hallux rigidus. Illustration A shows a figure from Giannini et al (2004) depicting forefoot accommodation leading to transfer metatarsalgia. Illustration B shows an example of a second metatarsal stress fracture as a result of transfer metatarsalgia.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1, 2, and 3: Hallux rigidus leads to restricted dorsiflexion, decreased push-off strength, and a subsequently inverted gait.
Answer 4: Hallux rigidus has been associated with transfer metatarsalgia.
