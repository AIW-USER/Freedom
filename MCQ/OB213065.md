## Question

A 26-year-old patient presents with knee pain associated with catching and locking several weeks after playing rugby. Figures A and B demonstrate the MRI of the patient's knee. The patient opts to undergo a surgical repair of the meniscus. An inside-out technique is performed. What is the approach interval and the neurovascular structure at greatest risk?

## Options

1. Between the semimembranosus and medial head of the gastrocnemius; inferior medial genicular artery
2. Between the joint capsule and the medial head of the gastrocnemius; saphenous nerve
3. Between the sartorius and the gracilis; saphenous nerve
4. Between the lateral head of the gastrocnemius and joint capsule; peroneal nerve
5. Between the lateral head of the gastrocnemius and the biceps femoris; inferior lateral genicular artery

## Correct option

**2. Between the joint capsule and the medial head of the gastrocnemius; saphenous nerve**

## Answer explanation

The patient is presenting with a bucket-handle medial meniscus tear as demonstrated by the MRI images. When performing an inside-out technique for a medial tear, the deep interval is between the medial head of the gastrocnemius and the joint capsule. This places the saphenous nerve at greatest risk.

Meniscal injuries are the most common reason for knee surgeries and typically present with joint line pain, clicking, and catching. Bucket handle tears are more severe tears of the meniscus that can present with locking of the knee. In patients that are young and active, surgical repair of the meniscus is preferred over meniscectomy if tear pattern allows as it can lead to better long-term outcomes. Inside-out repair requires both arthroscopic visualization of the meniscus tear as well as open exposure of the outside of the joint capsule. For medial meniscal tears, the incision is made posterior to the MCL and then the interval between the medial head of the gastrocnemius and the joint capsule is used, placing the saphenous nerve at risk. For lateral meniscal tears, the interval between the lateral head of the gastrocnemius and the joint capsule is used, placing the peroneal nerve at risk.

*Nelson et al. authored a technique guide for inside-out repairs of the medial and lateral meniscus.* *They detailed that inside-out repairs are the standard of care for meniscus injuries, as a large number of sutures can be placed during the repair and as it allows better stabilization of far peripheral tears compared to all-inside techniques.* *The authors described a posterolateral approach for lateral meniscus tears that begins with an incision posterior to the LCL and distal to the joint line.* The interval is then developed between the biceps femoris tendon and iliotibial band, which is then continued between the lateral head of the gastrocnemius and the joint capsule.

*Chahla et al. described the surgical technique for inside-out medial and lateral meniscus repairs.* **They found** that the all-inside technique had higher implant costs and more implant-related complications compared to the inside-out technique, which has a higher risk of nerve injuries. They described the posteromedial approach for medial meniscus pathology, which begins just posterior to the MCL with two-thirds of the incision distal to the joint line. The pes tendons are then identified and retracted posteriorly to allow the interval between the medial head of the gastrocnemius and the joint capsule to be developed.

Figures A and B demonstrate a sagittal and coronal T1-weighted MRI of the knee with a double PCL sign visible on Figure A, which is consistent with a bucket handle medial meniscus tear. Illustrations A and B demonstrate the approach and interval when performing an inside-out medial meniscus repair.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The interval between the semimembranosus and the medial head of the gastrocnemius is too posterior for this technique.
Answer 3: The interval between the sartorius and gracilis would create a very narrow operative window that splits the pes tendons.
Answer 4: The interval between the lateral head of the gastrocnemius and the joint capsule is the correct interval for lateral meniscus tears, but this patient has medial pathology.
Answer 5: The interval between the biceps femoris and the lateral head of the gastrocnemius is too posterior for this technique and places the peroneal nerve directly in the operative field.
