## Question

An 86-year-old man complains of knee pain and swelling 10 years after total knee arthroplasty. On examination, he has no fever, and his knee is neither red nor warm. There is pain with active and passive knee flexion, but no extensor lag is appreciated. The clinical appearance of his knee is shown in Figures A and B. ## Options

1. Infection
2. Extensor mechanism rupture
3. Fracture
4. Implant loosening
5. Patellofemoral overstuffing

## Correct option

**4. Implant loosening**

## Answer explanation

This patient has patellar component loosening with the migration of the patellar component to a subcutaneous position via a capsular defect.

Patellar resurfacing during TKA remains a subject of debate. The rate of patella component loosening as been reported in 0.6-4.8% of cases. The proposed mechanisms of loosening includes: maltracking (from malalignment) or overstuffing the patellofemoral joint (PFJ) leading to high shear, or subclinical infection. Other risks include metal-backed patella, obesity, lateral release, joint-line elevation, postoperative flexion >100°, poor bone stock, asymmetric patella resection, inadequate implant fixation (small pegs), secondary osteonecrosis, and osteolysis.

*Schroer et al. assessed the etiology of TKA revision in 6 arthroplasty centers retrospectively.* **They determined** that aseptic loosening (31%) was the most common cause of failure (instability 19%, infection 16%, polyethylene wear 10%, arthrofibrosis 7%, and malalignment 7%). The mean time to failure was 6 years. Polyethylene wear is less common because of improved implant design and sterilization techniques.

*Pilling et al. performed a meta-analysis on patella resurfacing during 3465 knee replacements in 16 randomized controlled trials.* There were no differences in infection rates and operative time. **They concluded** that postoperative satisfaction and anterior knee pain rates in both resurfaced and non-resurfaced groups were not different, but the non-resurfaced groups had more additional surgical procedures.

*Jacobs et al. report on a case of recurrent patellar component loosening (twice) with no apparent cause.* There was no maltracking, bone necrosis, or tilting preoperatively. They eventually removed the patellar button.

Figure A shows a subcutaneous prominence, suggestive of patellar component loosening and migration with associated fluid collection or bursa. Figure B shows the component being pinched between the examiner's fingers with the edge readily visible. Figure C shows an intact patellar component on the lateral view, suggested by the radiolucent space in the PFJ. Figure D shows the patellar component superficial to the patella, and the collapse of the radiolucent space in the PFJ with patella bone-femoral component contact. Illustration A depicts an intraoperative photograph of the loose patellar component.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The clinical presentation and arthrocentesis results do not suggest infection.
Answer 2: The patient does not have an extensor mechanism rupture given the lack of extensor lag on physical examination.
Answer 3: There are no signs of periprosthetic fracture. Occult fractures would likely yield a hemarthrosis on aspiration.
Answer 5: While overstuffing is a possible cause for loosening and dislocation (increased patellofemoral joint reaction force, leading to high shear at high flexion angles), the main diagnosis is loosening of the patella component.
