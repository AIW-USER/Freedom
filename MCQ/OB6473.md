## Question

An 18-year-old man has thigh pain that wakes him from sleep. AP and lateral radiographs reveal the abnormality shown in Figures 38a and 38b. A CT scan of the lesion is shown in Figure 38c, and a biopsy specimen is shown in Figure 38d. What is the most likely diagnosis?

## Options

1. Low-grade intramedullary osteosarcoma
2. Brodie's abscess
3. Osteoid osteoma
4. Osteoblastoma
5. Enchondroma

## Correct option

**3. Osteoid osteoma**

## Answer explanation

While night pain is suspicious for malignancy, patients with infection and benign bone tumors (particularly osteoid osteoma) can have significant pain at night. The radiograph and CT scan are very suggestive of a benign process. The CT scan demonstrates an intra-cortical nidus surrounded by thickened cortex. The differential diagnosis for this radiographic picture includes infection (Brodie's abscess) or osteoid osteoma. The histology reveals small spicules of cellular woven bone separated by fibrous stroma, diagnostic of osteoid osteoma. Infection would reveal numerous neutrophils. Osteoblastoma has a similar histologic appearance but radiographically is larger (usually > 2 cm), intramedullary, and possesses more aggressive features. Low-grade intramedullary osteogenic sarcoma is quite rare (accounting for approximately 1% of all osteosarcomas) and is characterized by intramedullary sclerosis, not an intracortical abnormality. Enchondroma is an intramedullary, benign, and usually painless cartilaginous lesion. The clinical scenario, radiographic findings, and histology are entirely incompatible with enchondroma.
