## Question

Figures A and B are the MRI images of a 51-year-old male who presents with complaints of severe low back pain with radiation in the left posterior thigh for the past 5 days after working out. He denies any numbness, weakness, or bowel/bladder dysfunction. Which treatment modality is indicated at this time for the pathology evident on MRI?

*Figure A*

![[q214131-b-disc-sagittal.jpg]]
*Figure B*

## Options

1. Bed rest for 3 weeks
2. Bracing for 3 weeks
3. Left laminectomy
4. Extension exercises
5. Microdisckectomy

## Correct option

**4. Extension exercises**

## Answer explanation

This patient has an MRI-proven acute herniated disc. The first line of treatment for most patients with an acute disc herniation is conservative management.

Approximately 90% of acute herniated discs will improve with conservative management. This patient demonstrates no evidence of significant neurological dysfunction. Moreover, the radiation remains proximal to the knee and he has not yet had any treatment. Initial conservative management should consist of bed rest followed by progressive activity as tolerated, limited medications such as NSAIDs, muscle relaxants, and an oral steroid taper. Physical therapy with extension exercises has been shown to be extremely beneficial.

*Lurie et al. assessed the 8-year outcomes of surgery versus nonoperative care for lumbar intervertebral disc herniations.* **They reported** advantages for surgery in intent-to-treat analyses for the randomized cohort for all primary and secondary outcomes other than work status. **They concluded** that carefully selected patients who underwent surgery for a lumbar disc herniation achieved greater improvement than nonoperatively treated patients and there was little to no degradation of outcomes in either group (operative and nonoperative) from 4 to 8 years.

*Kreiner et al. provided an evidence-based educational tool to assist spine specialists in the diagnosis and treatment of lumbar disc herniation with radiculopathy.* *They reported on 29 clinical questions that were formulated and addressed while providing respective recommendations graded by the strength of the supporting literature and stratified by levels of evidence.* **They concluded** that the clinical guideline created uses the techniques of evidence-based medicine and best available evidence to aid practitioners in the care of patients with symptomatic lumbar disc herniation with radiculopathy.

Figures A&B are the axial and sagittal MRI images of a predominantly foraminal left-sided L4-5 disc herniation which extends cranially and is about 8 mm. There is also evidence of the herniation indenting the exiting nerve root.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1&2: Prolonged bed rest and bracing leads to deconditioning of the patient and is not recommended as first-line treatment.
Answers 3&5: Surgical intervention is not indicated at this time as this patient has not received any treatment. A trial of conservative management is likely to lead to a resolution in symptoms in approximately 90% of acute herniated discs.
