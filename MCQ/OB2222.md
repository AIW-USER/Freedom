## Options

1. Osteogenesis imperfecta
2. Rickets
3. Scurvy
4. Accidental trauma
5. Non-accidental trauma

## Correct option

**5. Non-accidental trauma**

## Answer explanation

Corner fractures, rib fractures, and humeral physeal separation are all indicative that this patient is a victim of non-accidental trauma

Non-accidental trauma is an important diagnosis to always consider as missed child abuse can lead to continued abuse and is a leading cause of death. Multiple fractures in various stages of healing should raise concern for non-accidental trauma. Isolated fractures that follow certain patterns (corner fractures, long bone fractures in children <2, epiphyseal separation, etc) should also raise concern for non-accidental trauma. Metabolic or systemic diseases can cause multiple fractures and mimic non-accidental trauma and should be considered if objective findings support the diagnosis.

*Kempe et al. summarize and review the battered-child syndrome.* **They state** the syndrome should be considered in any children with fractures and/or additional findings. **They state** psychiatric factors are probably contribute heavily to the pathogenesis. **They conclude** all providers should have a high index of suspicion and report any scenarios that raise concern to prevent further abuse and harm.

*Akbarnia et al. describe the orthopaedist role in child abuse and neglect.* **They state** a high index of suspicion is key. **They conclude** that orthopedic surgeons should be involved in prevention, detection, and management to prevent the cycle of continued abuse.

Figure A shows a corner fracture at the distal medial aspect of the right tibia. Figure B shows multiple healing rib fractures. Figure C shows a healing left distal tibia fracture. Figure D shows a transphyseal distal humerus separation. Illustration A shows some of the clinical findings seen in child abuse.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1,2,&3: The imaging does not show any patterns characteristic of osteogenesis imperfecta, rickets, or scurvy.
Answer 4: Accidental trauma can be considered however with multiple fractures in different stages of healing in this limited mobility infant, non-accidental trauma should be favored.
