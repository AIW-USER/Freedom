## Question

A 31-year-old male presents with worsening left-sided lumbar radiculopathy. MRI imaging demonstrates a left-sided L5-S1 paracentral disc herniation. He has undergone physical therapy for 8 weeks without improvement and uses over-the-counter anti-inflammatories. The patient makes the decision to proceed with surgical treatment. Which of the following statements are true regarding surgery for the patient's condition?

## Options

1. Percutaneous laser discectomy is associated with significantly greater pain relief and faster return to work than open microdiscectomy
2. Single patients undergoing surgical treatment have a greater clinical improvement than married patients
3. Progressively worsening symptoms are associated with a better response to surgical treatment
4. Predominant leg pain symptoms are associated with worse outcomes following surgical treatment
5. Nonoperative treatment has been associated with greater pain relief and faster return to baseline activities

## Correct option

**3. Progressively worsening symptoms are associated with a better response to surgical treatment**

## Answer explanation

Patients undergoing surgical treatment for a lumbar herniated intervertebral disc had a greater improvement in pain and function if there were progressively worsening symptoms prior to surgery.

Lumbar disc herniations are a common cause of lower back pain and lumbar radiculopathy with the L4-5 and L5-S1 disc involvement being most common. The majority of patients (~90%) improve with nonsurgical treatment consisting of physical therapy and anti-inflammatory medications. Patients that fail to improve with a 6-week course of nonoperative treatment may benefit from surgical decompression of the affected nerve root. Patients that are married, have predominant leg symptoms, and progressively worsening symptoms at baseline have greater treatment effects with surgical treatment.

*Radcliff et al. reviewed laser technology in spine surgery.* *The authors described the application of lasers for tissue dissection in traditional spine procedures and percutaneous discectomy.* **They concluded** lasers predispose patients to increased complications without added clinical benefit despite an increase in the public interest.

*Pearson et al. studied treatment effect modifiers for patients with lumbar herniated intervertebral discs treated surgically and nonsurgically in the SPORT trial.* **They reported** a greater treatment effect with surgical intervention in patients who were married, did not have other joints problems, and progressively worsening symptoms. **They concluded** strict indications for surgical treatment of lumbar intervertebral disc herniation are critical for treatment success.

Incorrect answers
Answer 1: Laser spine surgery has not been associated with superior clinical outcomes compared to traditional surgical techniques.
Answer 2: Married patients have been associated with greater improvement with surgical treatment compared to single patients.
Answer 4: Predominant leg pain symptoms are associated with a greater response to surgical treatment.
Answer 5: Surgical treatment has been associated with greater improvement in pain scores and decreased disability compared to nonoperative treatment.
