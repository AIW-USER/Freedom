## Options

1. Flexion-adduction-internal rotation, periacetabular osteotomy
2. Flexion-adduction-internal rotation, arthroscopic Iliopsoas tenotomy
3. Flexion-adduction-internal rotation, femoroacetabular osteoplasty
4. Flexion-abduction-external rotation, arthroscopic loose body removal and Iliopsoas tenotomy
5. Flexion-abduction-external rotation, femoroacetabular osteoplasty

## Correct option

**3. Flexion-adduction-internal rotation, femoroacetabular osteoplasty**

## Answer explanation

This patient has symptomatic femoroacetabular impingement (FAI) that has failed physical therapy and injections. The flexion-adduction-internal rotation (FADIR) position will cause abutment of the femur and acetabulum, and femoroacetabular osteoplasty is the best treatment at this time.

FAI is due to contact between an abnormally shaped femoral neck and/or acetabular rim. The location of bone formation dictates the physical exam findings seen. Femoral-based cam lesions occur at the anterosuperior head-neck junction, creating an aspherical head with a loss of normal head-neck offset. Flexion of the hip with adduction and internal rotation (FADIR) will cause mechanical impingement often with an obligate decrease in range of motion, in addition to pain reproduction.

*Ganz et al. wrote the seminal article proposing FAI as a cause of osteoarthritis.* *They detailed that abnormal bony deformity of the femur and/or acetabulum will lead to abnormal contact stress across the joint.* Early surgery that removes the excessive bone may prolong or prevent the development of arthritis.

*Ganz et al. later described their preferred method to surgically address FAI.* *They performed a trochanteric flip osteotomy to access the joint and dislocate the femoral head anteriorly, allowing complete visualization of the acetabulum and femur.* *Of the series of 213 hips undergoing this procedure, none developed avascular arthritis.*

*Beck et al. described outcomes of a series of patients with FAI after undergoing open surgical dislocation with femoroacetabular osteoplasty.* *19 individuals with an average age of 36 years were followed for average 4.7 years, with 13 having good-to-excellent results.* This demonstrates osteoplasty as an effective treatment for FAI in the pre-arthritic hip.

Figure A is an AP radiograph of the hip showing a cam deformity at the femoral head/neck junction. Figures B-D are various fluid-sensitive MR sequences demonstrating the cam deformity with characteristic separation of the labrum from the chondral surface.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Periacetabular osteotomy is for the dysplastic acetabulum.
Answer 2: Iliopsoas tenotomy may treat internal impingement or a snapping hip.
Answer 4: The FADIR position, not the FABER position, will reproduce impingement. In addition, there is no evidence of loose bodies on imaging.
Answer 5: The FADIR position, not the FABER position, will reproduce impingement.
