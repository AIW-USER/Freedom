## Question

Figure A is the MRI image of a 34-year-old female who presents with complaints of right ankle pain, limited motion and recurrent swelling for the past 8 months. She denies any recent trauma to the ankle. She undergoes arthroscopic synovectomy of the right ankle. Histologic evaluation from the intraoperative biopsy demonstrates multiple multinucleated giant cells with hemosiderin and very few mitotic figures. What is the most common complication following this procedure?

![[q3910-a-pvns-mri.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Arthrofibrosis
2. Chondrolysis
3. Metastasis
4. Recurrence
5. Skin necrosis

## Correct option

**4. Recurrence**

## Answer explanation

This patient's clinical and histological presentation is consistent with pigmented villonodular synovitis (PVNS) of the ankle. Following synovectomy, recurrence is the most common complication.

PVNS is an idiopathic monoarticular neoplastic synovial disease that most commonly affects adults aged 30-40 years. Patients will often complain of mechanical pain, swelling, erythema, limited motion, and recurrent effusions. When symptomatic, synovectomy is often indicated. The arthroscopic evaluation will demonstrate brownish or reddish inflamed synovial tissue. The histological evaluation demonstrates hemosiderin stained multinucleated giant cells. Despite successful synovectomy, recurrence is the most common complication of localized or diffuse PVNS, 8% and 30%, respectively.

*Tyler et al. review monoarticular PVNS.* **They report** that the diffuse form has a reported recurrence rate of up to 46%. **They conclude** that arthroscopic synovectomy has several advantages but is associated with higher recurrence rates in diffuse PVNS.

*Lee et al. prospectively reviewed seven patients with histologically confirmed diffuse PVNS who underwent incomplete synovectomy, followed by radiotherapy.* **They report** that at an average follow up of 24 months, six patients reported better function and reduced levels of pain. **They concluded** that a combined approach to a primary PVNS of the foot and ankle may reduce the risk of recurrence without functional impairment.

*Rhee et al. retrospectively reviewed the long-term results of localized PVNS (LPVNS) treated with arthroscopic excision and partial synovectomy.* **They noted** 2 cases in which posteromedial lesions recurred. **They concluded** that arthroscopic excision of LPVNS can improve symptoms with a return to preoperative activity levels, but patients may develop secondary osteoarthritis after treatment.

Figure A is a sagittal MRI image showing a well-circumscribed inhomogeneous mass at the anterior and posterior ankle joint consistent with a diagnosis of pigmented villonodular synovitis (PVNS).

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Arthrofibrosis is an unlikely complication following proper surgical technique for arthroscopic synovectomy
Answer 2: PVNS is due to inflamed synovium and the cartilage is uninvolved. Chondrolysis is an uncommon complication of PVNS
Answer 3: PVNS rarely metastasizes
Answer 5: Skin necrosis may be a complication of radiation therapy, but is not a common complication following arthroscopic synovectomy
