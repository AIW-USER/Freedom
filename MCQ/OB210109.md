## Question

An 80-year-old man presents to clinic complaining of low back pain, bilateral buttock pain, and heaviness in both of his posterior thighs. He reports his symptoms are exacerbated by walking and standing. He describes clear symptom relief when sitting and using a shopping cart. He denies saddle anesthesia, changes in bladder and bowel function, or pain distal to his knee. A lateral radiograph, sagittal MRI, and axial MRI at the L3-4 level are shown in Figures A, B, and C respectively. What is the most likely finding on physical exam?

## Options

1. Positive Babinski
2. Weakness in ankle dorsiflexion
3. Positive straight leg raise
4. No neurologic deficits
5. Foot numbness in the first web space

## Correct option

**4. No neurologic deficits**

## Answer explanation

This patient is suffering from lumbar spinal stenosis with neurogenic claudication. Patients often have a normal neurologic exam without focal deficits.

Lumbar spinal stenosis results from reduced volume in the spinal canal often caused by bony and soft tissue structures. Stenosis can be acquired or congenital and can take the form of central, lateral recess, foraminal, or extra-foraminal stenosis. Patients often complain of back pain, buttock pain, leg pain, claudication worse with extension, weakness, and bladder disturbances. Cauda equina syndrome is rare. Symptoms must be differentiated from vascular claudication.

*Lurie et al. presented a review on the management of lumbar spinal stenosis.* **They note** that clinical care and research is complicated by the lack of standard criteria for diagnosis, heterogeneity or presentation, and high rates of radiographic stenosis in the asymptomatic population. **They conclude** that outcomes appear to be better for patients managed surgically, but the evidence is mixed and often lacking in quality.

*Katz et al. presented an overview of lumbar spinal stenosis.* **They state** that neurogenic claudication is the most common symptom. They reinforce that patients are generally symptomatic only with prolonged walking or standing, but often asymptomatic when sitting. They add that patients may have a positive Romberg, pain with active lumbar extension, and mild motor and sensory deficits.

Figure A is a standing lateral lumbar radiograph that demonstrates spondylosis, L3/4 retrolisthesis, and L4/5 spondylolisthesis. Figures B and C are sagittal T2 MRIs at the same level, demonstrating central lumbar stenosis. Illustration A details the anatomy of central, lateral recess, and foraminal stenosis of the lumbar spine.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Lumbar spinal stenosis affects the lower motor neurons. The Babinski test assesses upper motor neuron disease.
Answer 2: Insult to the L4 nerve root can cause ankle dorsiflexion weakness. The patient’s clinical complaints do not correspond to radiculopathy and his MRI reflects central lumbar stenosis. Radiculopathy often results from lateral recess or foraminal stenosis.
Answer 3: This patient’s MRI and clinical complaints do not indicate that he is suffering from a radiculopathy, which the straight leg raise test evaluates.
Answer 5: First web space numbness in the foot is associated with an insult to the deep peroneal nerve.
