## Question

A 58-year-old male is referred to your clinic for a sacral mass found on a lumbar spine MRI. He brought a pathology report from a biopsy that was performed at an outside hospital that states “primary malignant bone tumor of notochordal remnant cells.” Staging work-up showed no distant disease. Which of the following describes the best treatment strategy for this patient?

## Options

1. Neo-adjuvant chemotherapy, surgical excision, maintenance chemotherapy
2. Wide surgical excision
3. Tumor debulking, chemotherapy
4. Radiation therapy
5. Observation with serial imaging

## Correct option

**2. Wide surgical excision**

## Answer explanation

This patient's pathology and location of the lesion are consistent with a chordoma. The mainstay of treatment for a chordoma is wide surgical excision.

Chordomas most often occur in middle-aged Caucasian males. Patients with chordomas usually present with complaints of a slow insidious onset of pain and often have bowel or bladder changes due to mass effect of the tumor. The mainstay of treatment consists of wide surgical excision, with radiation reserved for margin positive resections. Wide tumor-free excision has been shown to directly correlate with length of tumor-free survival. Chemotherapy is rarely indicated for chordomas as they remain largely chemoresistant. When excising a tumor in the sacrum, special care must be taken for the preservation of sacral nerve roots to maintain bowel and bladder function. Lastly, vertebral body chordomas can be treated with total vertebrectomy, but require large anterior/posterior reconstruction to maintain spinal stability.

*Stacchiotti et. al presented their outcomes of 130 patients who underwent chordoma excision.* **The authors found** that tumor size was an independent predictor of overall survival, local relapse-free survival, and distant relapse-free survival. **They concluded** that tumor size and surgical margins affected outcome only on initial presentation.

*Fuchs et al. reported on 52 patients treated surgically for sacrococcygeal chordoma with 21 achieving a wide surgical margin.* **They found** the overall survival rates were 74%, 52%, and 47% at five years, ten years, and fifteen years, respectively with the most important predictor of survival was a wide margin. **They concluded** a wide surgical margin was the most important predictor of survival and local recurrence in patients with a sacrococcygeal chordroma.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Chordomas are largely chemoresistant, so any chemotherapy would unnecessarily harm the patient.
Answer 3: Tumor debulking suggest an intralesional excision, which would not give the patient a chance for curative treatment.
Answer 4: Radiation alone would not be adequate for treatment of a chordoma given that wide excision is the standard of care.
Answer 5: Observation would put the patient at unnecessary risk for tumor growth and progression.
