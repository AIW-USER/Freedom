## Question

A 23-year-old collegiate wrestler returns to your office complaining of persistent anterior ankle pain despite a course of physical therapy, NSAIDs and bracing which provided only minimal relief. On exam, ankle range of motion shows 10 degrees of dorsiflexion which reproduces his pain. Figures A and B show his MRI taken 1 month prior. What is the best next step in treatment?

## Options

1. Arthroscopic os trigonum excision
2. Arthroscopic debridement
3. Percutaneous achilles repair
4. Functional rehab emphasizing proprioceptive training
5. Arthroscopic loose body removal

## Correct option

**2. Arthroscopic debridement**

## Answer explanation

This vignette is classic for anterior ankle impingement after exhausting non-operative modalities the treatment of choice for anterior ankle impingement is arthroscopic debridement.

Anterior ankle impingement is a common condition among athletes who engage in sports requiring maximum dorsiflexion or is played on a field surface. Though imaging will often show spurs of the talar neck and anterior tibia, cadaveric studies show these rarely come into contact, rather anterior synovial and capsular tissue impingement is the source of pain. Anterior tenderness with painful and/or limited dorsiflexion is commonly seen on exam. Additionally, lateral ankle instability and osteochondral lesions are often seen in conjunction with impingement and should be treated accordingly.

*Parma et al. evaluated a case series of 8 patients with bony impingement, treated with arthroscopic debridement.* **They noted** that concomitant osteochondral defects, age, and previous ankle fractures faired worse on long-term follow-up, as compared isolated tibial spurs did better. Based on this **they proposed** a new classification scheme for anterior impingement.

*Talusan et al. thoroughly reviewed the management of anterior ankle impingement.* **They recommend** a high suspicion for concomitant injuries, noting the high incidence of ankle instability, osteochondral lesions, and gastrocnemius contractures with impingement, and that surgery is reserved for those who have exhausted non-operative modalities.

Figures A and B are the fluid-sensitive MR sequence showing anterior capsular thickening and tibial osteophyte.
Illustration A and B demonstrate the classification scheme proposed by Parma et al.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Os trigonum is a cause of posterior impingement and pain
Answer 3: The patient has no evidence of achilles pathology.
Answer 4: Physical therapy has already been attempted without improvement
Answer 5: There is no evidence of a loose body on imaging
