## Question

A 22-year-old soccer player complains of foot pain after sustaining a twisting injury. A clinical photo is shown in Figure A. On the physical examination, he is focally tender in the midfoot region. Weight bearing radiographs are shown in Figure B. What is the optimal definitive treatment for this patient?

*Figure A*

![[q4802-b-weightbearingap.jpg]]
*Figure B*

## Options

1. Observation alone
2. Weight bearing in a walking boot
3. Immobilization in a cast for 8 weeks
4. Open reduction and internal fixation of the injury
5. Open reduction and internal fixation of the injury along with lateral column stabilization

## Correct option

**4. Open reduction and internal fixation of the injury**

## Answer explanation

This patient has sustained a Lisfranc injury without evidence of lateral column instability. Optimal definitive treatment should include open reduction and internal fixation of the medial two columns alone.

Lisfranc injuries may be treated nonoperatively with cast immobilization when there is limited bony diastasis on stress radiographs. A weight bearing AP radiograph (stress radiograph) should be obtained if there is clinical concern for a Lisfranc injury and the static radiographs are non-diagnostic. Presence of instability as seen with > 2 mm of displacement, is an indication for open reduction and internal fixation.

*Nunley et al. reviewed midfoot sprains sustained in athletes.* Injuries were diagnosed with weight bearing radiographs and bone scans. Conservative management was used for patients with non-displaced injuries. Diastasis between metatarsals or medial arch compromise were indications for operative intervention. Excellent outcomes were achieved in > 90% of patients using this algorithm.

*Watson et al. review the diagnosis and management of Lisfranc injuries.* **They recommend** use of ORIF for Lisfranc injuries. This enables the surgeon to adequately assess for soft tissue barriers to reduction and optimize medial column alignment.

Figure A shows a clinical photo of a patient with plantar ecchymosis. This is characteristic of a Lisfranc injury. Figure B shows weight bearing AP radiographs of the right and left foot. Diastasis seen between the first and second metatarsals of the left foot is consistent with a Lisfranc injury.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1 and 2: These options are not indicated in this clinical scenario.
Answer 3: If there was minimal diastasis or if the patient was not an appropriate surgical candidate, then this could be considered.
Answer 5: There is no evidence of instability of the lateral columns on the weight bearing AP radiograph.
