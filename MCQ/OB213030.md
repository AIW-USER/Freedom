## Question

A 40-year-old patient presents with a mass in the left great toe that has increased in size over the last several months. The patient denies any fevers or chills. Figure A demonstrates an AP radiograph of the left foot. Figure B is the biopsy of the lesion. Genetic analysis reveals an SYT-SSX1 fusion protein. Which of the following is true of this tumor compared to SYT-SSX2 translocation?

## Options

1. Smaller with less metastases
2. Monophasic
3. Similar prognosis
4. Biphasic
5. Less common

## Correct option

**4. Biphasic**

## Answer explanation

The patient is presenting with synovial sarcoma with an SYT-SSX1 translocation, which is associated with biphasic tumor histology.

Synovial sarcoma is the most common sarcoma of the foot and is caused by chromosomal translocation t(X,18) creating the SYT-SSX fusion protein. Characteristically tumors are biphasic on histology with epithelial differentiation, glandular epithelial organizing, and spindle cell stroma. In rare cases, there is an SYT-SSX2 fusion protein that results in a monophasic tumor, but it has been shown that the biphasic subtype (SYT-SSX1) has improved survival at five and ten years compared to the monophonic subtype.

*Fisher reviewed the genetic and pathogenesis of synovial sarcoma.* *The author posited that there are monophasic and biphasic forms of synovial sarcoma, which the latter displays glandular or epithelial histologic structures.* **The authors stated** that these tumors are immunoreactive for cytokeratin, epithelial membrane antigen, bcl2 protein, and negative for CD34.

*Saito reviewed the biochemistry of the SYT-SSX fusion protein and the histologic differentiation of synovial sarcoma.* *The authors posited that the biphasic histology of synovial sarcoma and epithelial differentiation is influenced by the level of E-cadherin expression.* **The authors concluded** that the increased E-cadherin expression in SYT-SSX1 is responsible for the biphasic histological appearance and epithelial differentiation.

Figure A demonstrates an AP radiograph of the left foot with a large tumor involving the great toe and associated tissue calcification. Figure B demonstrates the biphasic histology of an SYT-SSX1 synovial sarcoma.

Incorrect answers:
Answers 1, 2, 3, and 5: Synovial sarcoma with an SYT-SSX2 translocation is associated with smaller tumors with fewer metastases, monophasic histology, a better prognosis, and lower incidence.
