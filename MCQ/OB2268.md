## Question

A 13-year-old patient elects to undergo distraction osteogenesis to address a 5cm limb length inequality. An osteotomy was performed, and a fixator was placed 6 days ago. She now presents to the office to begin distraction. At what rate is distraction typically performed?

## Options

1. 1 mm/day
2. 3 mm/day
3. 5 mm/day
4. 8 mm/day
5. 1 cm/day

## Correct option

**1. 1 mm/day**

## Answer explanation

Following an osteotomy and fixator placement for limb lengthening, there is typically a 5-7-day period before distraction begins. Distraction often occurs at a rate of 1mm/day.

Growth of the lower extremities is generally assumed to continue in boys and girls until the ages of 16 and 14 years, respectively. Historical treatment of limb length discrepancies include no treatment for discrepancies less than 1.5 cm, epiphysiodesis for those between 1.5 to 5 cm, and lengthening of the short side with a >5cm projected limb length deformity (LLD). A strategy involving the combination lengthening the long limb with a shortening procedure of the contralateral limb can be utilized. Initiation of distraction osteogenesis begins with an osteotomy and fixator placement, followed by a 5-7-day rest period before distraction begins. Then, distraction typically occurs at a rate of 1mm/day. Radiographs are utilized during the lengthening process to ensure that appropriate regenerate bone is being formed. If, during this process, radiographs reveal poor regenerate bone formation, the first step in management is to slow the distraction rate. If slowing the distraction rate is unsuccessful, then temporary reversal of the distraction, or “accordionization,” may be used to induce a greater healing response. Finally, following completion of the distraction, the fixator is left in place for as many days as lengthening occurred.

*Stanitski et al. review the treatment options for LLD.* **They report** that the indications for limb lengthening is a planned LLD exceeding 5cm. **They concluded** that limb lengthening of the short limb with concomitant shortening of the contralateral limb is indicated in such instances, and. epiphysiodesis or femoral shortening is reserved for smaller discrepancies or for residual differences following a contralateral lengthening.

*Dimeglio et al. review growth in pediatric orthopedics.* **They report** that the lower limbs double in length at age 2 years, and there remains 50% of growth in the lower limbs at the age of 4 years, but the velocity of the standing height always has the same pattern. **They found** that it is extremely rapid during intrauterine life, rapid during the first 5 years after birth, slows after 5 years of age, and followed by acceleration at puberty. **They concluded** that the best way to understand growth is to follow its patterns and embrace its rhythms and cadences in order to control it.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 2-5: Distraction is typically recommended to occur at a rate of 1mm/day. More rapid lengthening may exceed the ability for regenerate bone to form.
