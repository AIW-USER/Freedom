## Question

A 48-year-old male office worker presents to the orthopedic clinic to discuss treatment options for severe pain in his left arm and neck. Five months ago, he underwent a cervical spine MRI, which revealed disc degeneration and herniation at C5-C6, along with left foraminal stenosis and nerve root compression. He experienced temporary relief from a corticosteroid injection targeting the C6 nerve root; however, the debilitating pain returned. The patient is now considering cervical disc arthroplasty (CDA) or anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF). Which of the following statements are accurate when referring to the 2- to 5-year outcomes comparing CDA to ACDF?

## Options

1. CDA has lower rates of adjacent segment disease requiring reoperation
2. ACDF has lower rates of dysphagia
3. CDA and ACDF have equivalent patient-reported outcomes
4. ACDF has lower patient-reported pain values
5. CDA has higher rates of reoperation at the index level

## Correct option

**3. CDA and ACDF have equivalent patient-reported outcomes**

## Answer explanation

This patient presents with single-level cervical radiculopathy that has failed nonoperative management. Despite motion preservation at the index level, CDA and ACDF have similar levels of adjacent segment disease and patient-reported outcomes at 2-5 years postoperatively.

Cervical radiculopathy is caused by compression of exiting nerve roots. For single-level disease, ACDF has been the historical gold standard. Fusion increases the stress load on the adjacent levels, which can cause long-term adjacent segment disease. Cervical disc arthroplasty was developed to allow for spinal nerve root decompression via discectomy while preserving motion at that level. Theoretically, motion preservation should reduce adjacent segment disease. However, several studies have shown equivalent functional outcomes between ACDF and CDA, with no significant difference in adjacent segment disease.

*Vleggeert-Lankamp et al published the two-year results from a randomized controlled trial that compared ACDF, CDA, and anterior cervical discectomy (ACD) alone.* *109 patients with one-level herniated discs and radicular symptoms were randomized to ACDF, ADA, or CDA.* Neck disability index (cervical spine patient-reported outcome), pain scores, radiographic cervical curvature, reoperation rate (index level or adjacent), and adjacent segment degeneration parameters were collected for all included patients. The above-mentioned criteria were similar in all groups, and **the authors concluded** that ACDA does not lead to superior clinical outcomes at 2-year follow-up.

*Goedmakers et al published the five-year results from the above-mentioned randomized clinical trial.* At five years, the rates of reoperation at adjacent levels and index level were similar at the index and adjacent levels. Pain scores and patient-reported outcomes were also similar. **The authors concluded** that there is a persistent absence of clinical superiority of CDA over ACDF.

*Evan et al published a retrospective single-center, single-surgeon cohort study.* *They included 58 patients who underwent either a single-level ACDF or CDA over a 10-year period, which included 58 patients.* **They reported** good clinical results in both groups. No difference was found in dysphonia or dysphagia. However, the authors did find a higher adjacent level ossification development in the ACDF group. **They concluded** that further long-term studies are needed to understand the consequences of adjacent-level ossification.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: ACDF and CDA have similar rates of surgical intervention for adjacent segment disease.
Answer 2: ACDF and CDA have similar levels of postoperative dysphagia and dysphonia.
Answer 4: While some studies have found lower early self-reported pain scores in CDA. Long-term studies show equivalent pain scores between CDA and ACDF.
Answer 5: CDA and ACDF have similar low levels of reoperation at the index level.
