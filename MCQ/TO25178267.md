Chapter: Arthroscopy MCQs — Shoulder Instability[Unidirectional and Multidirectional]

## Question

A 22-year-old competitive rugby player presents with recurrent anterior shoulder instability. 3D-CT imaging reveals 12% anterior glenoid bone loss and a 'narrow' Hill-Sachs lesion. On-track/off-track calculation confirms the lesion is 'Off-Track.' What is the most appropriate surgical strategy?

## Options

1. Isolated Arthroscopic Bankart Repair
2. Eden-Hybinette Procedure
3. Arthroscopic Bankart Repair with Remplissage
4. Latarjet Procedure

## Correct option

**3. Arthroscopic Bankart Repair with Remplissage**

## Answer explanation

Management of bipolar lesions (hill sachs and glenoid bone loss) in shoulder instability

To decide the surgery, we categorize bone loss into three main zones:

A. Isolated Bankart Repair (Soft Tissue Alone)

 - Indication: * Glenoid bone loss < 15–20% (some surgeons use a stricter 13.5% "best fit circle" threshold for high-demand athletes).

 - The Hill-Sachs lesion is On-Track (HSI < GT).

A standard arthroscopic labral repair is usually sufficient because the "articular arc" is preserved enough to prevent engagement.

B. Bankart + Remplissage

 - Indication: * Glenoid bone loss is minimal (< 15%).

 - The Hill-Sachs lesion is Off-Track (HSI > GT).

 "Remplissage" means "to fill." By suturing the infraspinatus and posterior capsule into the Hill-Sachs defect,it is made extra-articular. This effectively "shortens" the Hill-Sachs Interval, making it On-Track
again.

C. Bone Grafting: Latarjet or Bone Block

 - Indication: * Glenoid bone loss > 20–25% (The "Inverted Pear" glenoid).

 - Failed previous Bankart repair.

 - Bipolar Bone Loss: When even an On-Track Hill-Sachs becomes Off-Track due to significant glenoid narrowing.

 The Latarjet
(coracoid transfer) works via the "Triple Effect":

 - Bone Effect: Increases the glenoid surface area/width.

 - Sling Effect: The conjoint tendon stabilizes the inferior capsule in abduction/external rotation.

 - Capsular Effect: Using the remaining CA ligament to repair the capsule.

The "Treatment Algorithm" for Bipolar Lesions

When both sides have bone loss, the decision is based on whether the Hill-Sachs lesion is "Engaging" (Off-Track).

Other Bone Grafting Procedures

When the glenoid loss is massive or the Latarjet is insufficient/already failed:

 - Distal Tibial Allograft (DTA):
 Using a fresh-frozen distal tibial graft to reconstruct the glenoid.

 - Advantage: Matches the radius of curvature of the humeral head better than the coracoid and provides a large articular surface.

 - Iliac Crest Bone Graft (Eden-Hybinette): Autograft used primarily for large defects where coracoid volume is inadequate.

 - Humeral Head Allograft (OATS): For massive, deep Hill-Sachs lesions (>40% of humeral head), where Remplissage would severely limit external rotation.

The most difficult clinical scenario is the "Sub-critical" bone loss (15–20%).

 - If the patient is a contact athlete (rugby, kabaddi, wrestling), current evidence leans heavily toward Latarjet even for 15% bone loss because the failure rate of Bankart is unacceptably high (~30% in some series).

 - For a non-contact patient, a Bankart + Remplissage might be considered.
