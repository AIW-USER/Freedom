## Answer explanation

Figure E is an x-ray representative of a patient with a severe form of osteogenesis imperfecta. The majority of cases of OI (85-90 %) are caused by a dominant mutation in a gene coding for type I collagen.

Osteogenesis imperfecta (OI) is a genetic disorder most commonly due to a mutation of the COL1A1 and COL1A2 genes, which causes a defect in the metabolism of type I collagen. Manifestations can include short stature, wormian bones, scoliosis, blue sclera and hearing loss (depending on type and severity of the disorder). Figure E is a radiograph with bilateral lower extremity deformities consistent with osteogenesis imperfecta. Originally 4 types of OI were described, all of which involved mutations of Type I collagen. However, more recently 3 more types (Types V, VI, and VII) have been described and have not been localized to a mutation of Type I collagen.

*Burnei et al. provide is a relatively recent review of osteogenesis imperfecta that utilizes the classification most often quoted in the orthopaedic literature.* **They note** there are multiple types of OI, most of which (Types I - IV) involve mutations to the COL1A1 or COL1A2 genes with a glycine substitution altering collagen I synthesis or structure. **They note** that bone marrow transplantation may help increase numbers of mesenchymal cells, and thus increase osteoblast density. This has been shown in a small series to benefit patients with OI, although further research is needed.

*Cole et al. present a review of heritable connective tissue diseases such as OI.* **They note** that type I collagen is the most important structural component of bone. Mutations to type I collagen genes (COL1A1 and COL1A2) lead to OI with four types most commonly described. **They note** that mutations leading to quantitative disorders are milder than those causing qualitative disorders.

*Van Dijk et al. wrote an update that reviews and includes the ever evolving classification system for osteogenesis imperfecta.* **They emphasize** the importance of phenotyping in order to diagnose, classify, and assess severity of OI, and suggest classifying the condition based on phenotype will provide insight into the probable course of the disorder and help evaluate the effect of therapy.
