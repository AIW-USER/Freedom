## Question

Patients who are smokers and are undergoing spinal surgery have been found to be at increased risk for infection, pseudarthrosis, and reoperation. Smoking has also been found to be associated with which of the following?

## Options

1. Longer hospital stay
2. Airway compromise following anterior cervical corpectomy
3. Lower patient satisfaction
4. Lower functional outcome scores
5. Wound hematoma

## Correct option

**4. Lower functional outcome scores**

## Answer explanation

Smoking is associated with lower functional outcome scores and increased risks for pseudarthrosis, postoperative wound infection, and reoperation rates. In a prospective study evaluating the risks of airway compromise following anterior cervical surgery, smoking was not a significant factor. Increased hospital days following spinal surgery has not been independently associated with smoking. The increase risk for the development of a wound hematoma has not been associated with smoking.
