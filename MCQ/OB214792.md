## Question

A 17-year-old male presents with severe right hip pain of two days duration. He reports the pain started insidiously without any inciting event. The pain has worsened to the point where he now has difficulty ambulating. He reports subjective fevers of 102 degrees F that began at the time he noticed a macular rash. His leg is held in a resting position of flexion and external rotation. Subsequently an ultrasound guided hip aspiration is performed for suspected septic arthritis of the hip. Which of the following is descriptive of the most likely organism to be identified?

## Options

1. Gram positive cocci in clusters
2. Gram positive cocci in chains
3. Gram negative intracellular rods
4. Gram negative diplococci
5. Gram positive diplococci

## Correct option

**4. Gram negative diplococci**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a diagnosis of septic arthritis. The most likely etiology of septic arthritis in this age group is Neiserria gonorrhoeae, which would be identified as gram negative intracellular diplococci.

Gonococcal septic arthritis is by far the most common cause of monoarthritis in young, sexually active adults. Teenagers likely will not be forthcoming about their sexual history so one must be suspicious. It affects females to males at a ratio of 3:1. The difference may be due to women typically presenting with a more asymptomatic clinical picture and more often with untreated genitourinary tract infections. Patients present with systemic inflammatory symptoms in addition to a characteristic triad of clinical symptoms: migratory polyarthralgia, dermatologic lesions usually presenting as macules and papules, and tenosynovitis often affecting multiple joints simultaneously (particularly wrists, fingers, ankles, and toes).

*Rice et al. performed a review of gonococcal arthritis.* **They report** that septic arthritis caused by N. gonorrhoeae may be monarticular or pauciarticuar, and is more commonly associated with positive synovial fluid cultures and negative blood cultures. **They recommend** initial treatment with ceftriaxone or another advanced cephalosporin until signs and symptoms have improved.

*Garcia-De La Torre et al. performed a review of gonococcal and nongonococcal arthritis.* **They report** that exposure to sexual encounters should raise suspicion of this type of arthritis in young patients. Even though a positive Gram stain is present in less than 50% of these patients, simultaneous cultures of cervix, urethra, and rectum should be obtained to augment the positive result, especially searching for the presence of N. gonorrhoea.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: This is typical of a staph species, which is not the most common cause of septic arthritis in this patient population.
Answer 2: This is typical of a strep species, which is not the most common cause of septic arthritis in this patient population.
Answer 3: Gram negative rods are not indicative of N. gonorrhoeae.
Answer 5: This finding would be indicative of S. pneumoniae, not N. gonorrhoeae.
