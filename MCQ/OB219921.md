## Question

A 73-year-old female presents to the clinic complaining of progressive pain and discomfort in her right hip. She underwent metal-on-metal arthroplasty 15 years ago and has had no complications to this point. Upon further questioning, the patient revealed she had previous skin reactions to jewelry. The patient is most likely experiencing symptoms secondary to what pathologic process?

## Options

1. Lymphocyte-mediated hypersensitivity reaction
2. Macrophage-mediated hypersensitivity reaction
3. Lymphocyte-mediated large particle wear debris
4. Macrophage-mediated large particle wear debris
5. Lymphocyte-mediated small particle wear debris

## Correct option

**1. Lymphocyte-mediated hypersensitivity reaction**

## Answer explanation

Patients with metal-on-metal arthroplasty are at risk of developing pseudotumor, also known as an aseptic lymphocyte-dominant vasculitis-associated lesion (ALVAL). In patients with metal hypersensitivity, this process is thought to be secondary to a lymphocyte-mediated hypersensitivity reaction (Answer 1).

The use of metal-on-metal (MoM) bearings in total hip arthroplasty (THA) was popularized due to its enhanced wear profile and the ability to use large femoral heads to reduce post-operative instability. However, enthusiasm for the bearing precipitously declined following serious complications encountered at the primary articulation. MoM THAs are associated with pseudotumors, which are mass-forming tissue reactions caused by metal-on-metal wear. Two different mechanisms have been proposed for the formation of pseudotumor, including hypersensitivity to metal ions and local high-wear debris. While the presentations are very similar, there is variation in the distribution of metal debris, degree of necrosis, and number, type, and arrangement of inflammation in each of the proposed mechanisms.

*Campbell et al. examined the synovial lining and metal wear particles of pseudotumor-like tissues from 32 metal-on-metal (M-M) hips revised for suspected wear-related and suspected metal hypersensitivity causes.* **The author found** that patients with significant wear had a lower incidence of aseptic lymphocytic vasculitis-associated lesions (ALVAL), fewer lymphocytes, but more macrophages and metal particles than those tissues from hips revised for pain and suspected metal hypersensitivity. The highest ALVAL scores occurred in patients who were revised for pain and suspected metal hypersensitivity. Given these findings **the authors conclude** that pseudotumor-like reactions can be caused by high wear, but may also occur around implants with low wear, likely because of a metal hypersensitivity reaction and histologic features may help differentiate these causes.

*Kwon et al. reviewed their series of 92 patients following metal-on-metal hip resurfacing arthroplasty (MoMHRA).* *The authors collected venous blood samples and analyzed serum Nickel (Ni), Cobalt (Co), and chromium (Cr) ion level measurements and lymphocyte transformation tests.* **They found** a higher incidence and level of enhanced lymphocyte reactivity to Ni in patients with MoMHRA compared to the patients without MoM implants. Based on these results, **the authors postulate** that metal sensitivity in patients with MoMHRA appears to be mediated by lymphocyte reactivity to Ni, rather than Co and Cr.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: In patients with a history of metal hypersensitivity (inability to wear jewelry), a pseudotumor reaction is most likely to be secondary to lymphocytes, not macrophages.
Answers 3-5: While there are two proposed mechanisms for the formation of pseudotumor (metal hypersensitivity and wear debris), a patient with a history of metal sensitivity would be more likely to have a hypersensitivity reaction.
