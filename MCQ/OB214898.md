## Question

With respect to the management of proximal biceps tendonitis, which of the following is a true statement comparing tenodesis to tenotomy?

## Options

1. Decreased biceps strength with tenodesis compared to tenotomy at 1 year followup
2. Increased pain scores with tenotomy compared to tenodesis at 6 months followup
3. Improved elbow supination with tenotomy compared to tenodesis at 1 year followup
4. Decreased rate of post-operative "popeye" deformity with tenodesis compared to tenotomy
5. Increased shoulder range of motion with tenotomy compared to tenodesis at 1 year followup

## Correct option

**4. Decreased rate of post-operative "popeye" deformity with tenodesis compared to tenotomy**

## Answer explanation

To date, the only literature-supported difference between tenodesis and tenotomy of the proximal biceps is improved cosmetic appearance of the biceps contour after tenodesis. Some studies have suggested a lower rate of cramping with biceps tenodesis compared to tenotomy, but this difference has not been seen in high level studies.

Surgical management of isolated proximal biceps brachii tendonitis generally consists of either tenotomy versus tenodesis of the tendon. Tenodesis can be performed arthroscopically or open (subpectoral). Open subpectoral biceps tenodesis requires an incision at the insertion of the pectoralis major tendon on the humerus. The theoretical advantage of the open subpectoral biceps tenodesis is that the biceps tendon is removed from the bicipital groove, which may eliminate a source of pain in the biceps tendon. Recent level 1 evidence has shown that the only difference between tenotomy and tenodesis is an increased rate of post-operative "popeye" deformity with tenotomy. There is no difference between shoulder or elbow range of motion, patient-reported outcomes, or pain between tenodesis or tenotomy.

*Mazzocca et al. described a surgical technique for subpectoral tenodesis of the proximal biceps tendon.* **They note** that the skin incision for the subpectoral open biceps tenodesis is made in the axilla exposing the inferior border of the pectoralis major muscle. After the tendon is withdrawn from the joint and out of the incision, a Krackow whipstitch is placed in the 10 to 15 mm of tendon proximal to the musculotendinous junction and is attached just distal to the bicipital groove with a tenodesis screw. **The authors noted** good preliminary results with a case series of 22 patients with none of the patients reporting persistent pain or loss of function at 10 months followup.

*MacDonald et al. performed a prospective randomized controlled trial to compare clinical outcomes between tenotomy and tenodesis for the treatment of lesions of the long head of the biceps.* *A total of 114 participants were randomized to undergo either biceps tenodesis or tenotomy with postoperative functional and clinical results being reported up to 24 months.* **The authors noted** a statistically significant improvement in ASES, WORC, and pain scores with both treatments but no difference between the 2 groups. The risk of cosmetic deformity in the tenotomy group relative to the tenodesis group at 24 months was 3.5 with 4 (10%) occurrences in the tenodesis group and 15 (33%) in the tenotomy group. There were no differences between elbow flexion or supination strength. **The authors concluded** that tenotomy and tenodesis as treatment for lesions of the long head of biceps tendon both result in good subjective outcomes but there is a higher rate of Popeye deformity in the tenotomy group.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1, 2, 3, and 5: There is no difference in post-operative biceps strength, pain scores, elbow supination, or shoulder range of motion between those undergoing tenodesis or tenotomy
