## Question

## Options

1. Closed reduction of elbow and immobilization in cast for 6 weeks
2. ORIF with repair of the LCL to the lateral condyle
3. ORIF with repair of the LCL to the supinator crest
4. Open reduction with percutaneous fixation or internal fixation
5. Closed reduction and percutaneous pinning of the lateral condyle

## Correct option

**4. Open reduction with percutaneous fixation or internal fixation**

## Answer explanation

The patient has sustained a lateral condyle fracture with an associated posteromedial elbow dislocation. The lateral collateral ligament remains intact and attached to the avulsed lateral condyle fragment. Treatment would require an open reduction and internal fixation given the amount of displacement of the fracture.

Lateral condyle fractures are the second most common pediatric elbow fracture next to supracondylar fractures. Concomitant posteromedial elbow dislocations are exceedingly rare. Mechanically, these injuries are the result of a fall on an outstretched hand with the elbow in slight flexion and an applied adduction force, with the lateral collateral ligament avulsing the lateral condyle fragment from the distal humerus. Fractures are classified using the Weiss, Jakob, or Milch classification. An anatomic reduction is necessary in order to mitigate the high nonunion rates with these fractures.

*Tejwani et al. reviewed the treatment of lateral condyle fractures.* **They found** that treatment is dependent on the degree of displacement with nondisplaced fractures amenable to cast immobilization. **They recommend** fractures with greater than 2 mm and less than 4 mm of displacement can be treated with CRPP and displacement greater than 4 mm usually requires ORIF.

*Silva et al. performed a retrospective analysis on 191 patients that presented with lateral condyle fractures, 12 with associated elbow dislocations and 179 with simple fractures requiring operative treatment.* **They found** that fractures with associated posteromedial dislocations were associated with a prolonged range of motion recovery with the final range of motion similar to simple lateral condyle fractures. **The authors concluded** that good outcomes can be expected with these injuries if an anatomic reduction is achieved.

*Pribaz et al. performed a retrospective review of 212 lateral condyle fractures in pediatric patients.* **The authors found** the initial amount of fracture displacement was both positively correlated with the development and severity of a lateral spur. **They report** the presence of a lateral spur, regardless of size, did not affect elbow function at final follow-up.

Figures A-C demonstrate radiographs of a lateral condyle fracture with an associated posteromedial elbow dislocation. Illustration A depicts the Milch classification system which is based on the involvement of the lateral trochlea. Illustration B depicts the Jakob classification which is based on the amount of fracture displacement.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: Immobilization in a cast for six weeks would not be ideal given the dislocation, the amount of displacement, and the inherent instability of this injury.
Answer 2 and 3: The LCL tends to remain intact with these injuries, thus repair of the LCL is unnecessary.
Answer 5: Given the amount of fracture displacement, closed reduction and percutaneous pinning would not allow inspection of the articular surface to ensure an anatomic reduction.
