## Question

A 54-year-old female presents to the emergency department with a closed right ankle injury seen in Figure A. Past medical history is significant for Type II diabetes with HbA1c of 10.2, Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD) stage III, peripheral neuropathy, and morbid obesity (BMI 41.5). The patient elects to undergo operative stabilization. Which of the following patient risk factors is the most important predictor of complications associated with this injury?

![[q212067-a-screen-shot-2021-02-26-at-9-43-43-am.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. BMI
2. CKD stage III
3. Age
4. Peripheral neuropathy
5. Sex

## Correct option

**4. Peripheral neuropathy**

## Answer explanation

Peripheral neuropathy is an important independent risk factor for the development of complications in diabetic patients who sustain ankle fractures.

Diabetic ankle fractures are challenging injuries due to the increased risk of complications compared to the non-diabetic patient. Increased complication risks associated with diabetic ankle fractures include: prolonged healing, poor quality of healing bone, hardware failure, infection risk, and future amputation. Current surgical treatment strategies to manage these complex injuries involve the utilization of stiffer plates for fibula fixation (e.g. anatomic locking plates), quadricortical syndesmotic screws, fibula augmentation with intramedullary fixation, tibiotalar Steinmann pins, hindfoot nailing, and delayed weight-bearing. Limited soft tissue dissection is encouraged when appropriate to mitigate the risk of complications, especially wound infections.

*Wukich et al. reviewed the management of diabetic ankle fractures.* **They found** that patients with diabetes and peripheral neuropathy were more likely to have complications, such as malunion, nonunion, or infection, than patients without diabetes or peripheral neuropathy. **They concluded** that peripheral neuropathy is an important risk factor for postoperative complications, and rigid fixation with delayed weight-bearing are important treatment strategies for the management of diabetic ankle fractures.

*Costigan et al. reviewed a series of 84 patients with diabetic ankle fractures who underwent operative management.* **They found** that 12 patients (14%) developed postoperative complications, with infection being most common. Additionally, peripheral neuropathy and vasculopathy (absent pre-operative pedal pulse) were statistically significant risk factors for developing postoperative complications. **They concluded** that patients with peripheral neuropathy and vasculopathy have an increased risk of complications with operative management.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: High BMI is a risk factor for delayed wound healing. However, peripheral neuropathy is the most important predictor of complications.
Answer 2: CKD stage IV or V is a risk factor for delayed wound healing in diabetic ankle fractures. However, peripheral neuropathy is the most important predictor of complications.
Answer 3: Age has not been shown to be a risk factor for complications in diabetic patients with ankle fractures.
Answer 5: Sex has not been shown to be a risk factor for complications in diabetic patients with ankle fractures.
