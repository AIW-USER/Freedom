## Question

A 28-year-old male complains of pain and deformity of the middle finger one day after a fall. A radiograph is provided in figure A. Which of the following is the primary objective when treating this fracture pattern?

![[q214831-a-pip-fx.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Anatomic reconstruction of the articular surface
2. Palmar plate reattachment
3. Maintaining joint reduction
4. Immobilization until fracture healing
5. Central slip reconstruction

## Correct option

**3. Maintaining joint reduction**

## Answer explanation

The primary objective for treating a proximal interphalangeal (PIP) joint fracture-dislocation is to achieve and maintain joint reduction, which allows for a normal gliding flexion arc.

Volar lip fractures are the most common fracture pattern seen with dorsal PIP dislocations. The mechanism of injury can be an avulsion type or impaction shear from axial loading. The primary goal of both nonoperative and operative management is to achieve a smooth gliding arc by maintaining the reduction of the middle phalanx on the condyles of the proximal phalanx.

*Kiefhaber and Stern reviewed the presentation, evaluation, and treatment of fracture-dislocations of the PIP joint.* **The authors note** that restoration of the middle phalangeal glide around the proximal phalangeal head during the flexion arc is paramount. This is achieved by eliminating joint subluxation and re-establishing joint stability after reduction. Hinging at the fracture site should be avoided.

*Elfar et al. reviewed fracture-dislocations of the PIP joint and* **note** three fracture-dislocation patterns: dorsal, volar, and pilon. **The authors state** that acceptable outcomes are dependent on achieving and maintaining a well-aligned and well-reduced joint, re-establishing normal joint kinematics, and restoring motion. **They conclude** that optimal outcomes are dependent on expedient diagnosis and recognition of injury severity.

Figure A demonstrates a long finger dorsal fracture-dislocation of the PIP joint.

Incorrect Answers
Answer 1: Anatomic reconstruction of the articular surface is desirable but not necessary for successful clinical outcomes.
Answer 2: Palmar plate arthroplasty can be considered to maintain reduction, but is not always necessary for the treatment of acute PIP joint fracture-dislocations.
Answer 4: Early motion should be initiated whenever possible to minimize adhesions and contractures.
Answer 5: Central slip tears are typically associated with volar PIP joint dislocations.
