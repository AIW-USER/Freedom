## Question

A variety of joint-preserving procedures are available for patients with specific patterns of knee osteoarthritis. Which of the following is true regarding a varus-producing distal femoral osteotomy (DFO)?

## Options

1. A varus-producing DFO can be completed either through a lateral closing wedge or a medial opening wedge osteotomy
2. Patients with lower preoperative knee function experience a longer-lasting benefit following a varus-producing DFO
3. The fifteen year survival rate in appropriately selected patients is approximately 50%
4. This procedure is best indicated for patients over the age of 50 with isolated medial compartment arthritis
5. Total knee arthroplasty following a varus-producing DFO requires the used of stemmed or highly-constrained implants in most patients

## Correct option

**3. The fifteen year survival rate in appropriately selected patients is approximately 50%**

## Answer explanation

While patients often experience short-term improvements in functional outcome scores following a distal femoral osteotomy (DFO), this procedure is associated with high rates of mid- and long-term functional deterioration and conversion to total knee arthroplasty (TKA), with a 15-year failure rate of 48.5% (Answer 3).

Adult limb deformity is a lower extremity condition that can result from a variety of underlying etiologies including, but not limited to, childhood growth impairment, metabolic bone disorders, severe osteoarthritis, trauma, or fracture malunion/nonunion. This phenomenon does not have a gender predilection and typically occurs in the lower extremities. Lower limb deformity can occur at both the tibia and/or the femur and the location of the deformity is helpful in determining where the correction should be made.

Patients will typically present with pain, deformity, and complaints of abnormal gait or loss of function. Patients with a varus deformity will typically complain of medial knee pain, and patients with a valgus deformity will note lateral knee pain. Treatment is centered around the restoration of angulation, translation length, and rotation, in that order. Most adult cases of genu varum are secondary to tibial deformity, while most cases of genu valgum are secondary to a femoral deformity. With respect to genu valgum, patients under the age of 50 with > 12° of valgus deformity and no clinical and/or radiographic evidence of medial compartment disease may be appropriate candidates for a varus-producing distal femoral osteotomy.

*Kosashvili et al. provide a case series of 33 consecutive distal femoral varus osteotomies in 31 patients with a minimum follow-up of 10 years (range 10-25).* *Overall, 16 patients required conversion to TKA at a mean of 15.6 years, a failure rate of 48.5%.* *The authors did note, however, a short-term improvement in functional outcome scores, with greater improvement seen in patients with higher postoperative knee function.* **The authors conclude** that following well-indicated varus-producing DFOs approximately 50% of patients will require conversion to TKA at a mean of 15.6 years.

*Backstein et al. also provide a long-term follow-up case series following varus-producing DFO.* *Their study included 40 patients with a mean follow-up of 123 months.* *At final follow-up, 24 knees (60%) had good or excellent results, but eight knees (20%) had already undergone conversion to TKA.* Overall, **the authors observed** a ten-year survival rate of 82% and a 15-year survival rate of 45%.

*Kosashvili et al. investigated the outcomes of patients treated with TKA for the progression of symptoms following DFO.* *Their study included a consecutive series of 22 patients with a mean follow-up of 5 years.* *Stemmed components were used in 5 knees (secondary to concerns with bone quality) and unstemmed components were used in the remaining 17 knees.* Overall, **the authors conclude** that standard components provided satisfactory stability, and that stemmed or highly constrained components are not ubiquitously necessary when performing a TKA following DFO.

Incorrect Answers
Answer 1: A varus-producing DFO is performed through either a lateral opening wedge or medial closing wedge osteotomy.
Answer 2: Patients with higher preoperative knee function typically experience a longer-lasting benefit following DFO
Answer 4: This procedure is best indicated in younger (< 50) patients with isolated lateral compartment arthritis
Answer 5: As long as the patient has adequate bone stock, conventional components can be utilized.
