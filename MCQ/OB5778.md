## Question

## Options

1. Resection
2. Tendon repair
3. Tenotomy
4. Capsuloligamentous repair
5. Internal fixation of bone

## Correct option

**4. Capsuloligamentous repair**

## Answer explanation

This patient has Grade III left turf toe with sesamoid retraction that has failed nonoperative measures. Capsuloligamentous repair is indicated.

Turf toe occurs after axial loading on the heel with the ankle plantarflexed and the hallux MTP hyperextended. The incidence is up to 45% in NFL players, with most occurring on artificial turf. Grade I injuries involve attenuation of plantar structures, localized swelling, and minimal ecchymosis. Grade II injuries involve partial tear of plantar structures, moderate swelling, and restricted motion as the result of pain. Grade III injuries involve complete disruption of plantar structures, significant swelling/ecchymosis, hallux flexion weakness, or frank instability of the MTP joint.

*George et al. reviewed turf toe injuries in intercollegiate football over 5 seasons.* **They found** that (1) athletes were more likely to sustain injury during competition than practice, (2) less than 2% required surgery, (3) injury rates were higher on artificial turf, (4) most injuries occurred after contact with the playing surfaces or another player, and (5) running back and quarterbacks were most likely to sustain this injury.

*Anderson et al. reviewed sports foot and ankle problems.* For Grade I injuries, **they recommend** taping and early rehabilitation, and early return to play (RTP). For Grade II injuries, **they recommend** turf-toe plate/carbon fiber orthosis, taping, rehabilitation, and RTP at about 2 weeks. For Grade III injuries, although nonoperative treatment is possible, **they report** good outcomes with direct primary repair of the ruptured capsuloligamentous complex. RTP may take 6-12months.

Figure A shows left foot sesamoid retraction (Grade III) compared to the normal sesamoid position on the right foot. Figure B shows sesamoid retraction with hallux dorsiflexion. Figure C is a sagittal T2-weighted MRI demonstrating complete disruption of the plantar plate distal to the sesamoid. Illustration A shows the mechanisms for sustaining turf toe - (A) axial load on a dorsiflexed great toe (35%) and (B) landing on another object, such as another player’s foot, causing hyperdorsiflexion of the great toe (33%).

Incorrect Answers
Answer 1: Sesamoid resection may be indicated for sesamoid fractures or fragmentation.
Answers 2 and 3: Plantar plate rupture and sesamoid retraction does not involve tendon disruption or contracture.
Answer 5: There are no fractures that require internal fixation.
