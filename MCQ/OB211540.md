## Question

A 51-year-old male presents to the trauma bay as a level 1 trauma following a workplace injury. You are consulted due to the patient's mangled left upper extremity. A mangled extremity severity score (MESS) of 9 is given to the extremity. This score predicts amputation with what specificity and sensitivity?

![[q211540-a-thumb-img-4102-1024.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Low specificity and low sensitivity
2. Low specificity and high sensitivity
3. High specificity and low sensitivity
4. High specificity and high sensitivity
5. Specificity and sensitivity unknown

## Correct option

**3. High specificity and low sensitivity**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a mangled extremity. The mangled extremity severity score (MESS) is used to predict the necessity of amputation after lower extremity trauma.

High-energy trauma to the lower extremity often results in failed efforts at limb salvage and is associated with increased mortality and high hospital costs. Lower extremity injury severity scoring systems were developed to assist the surgical team with the initial decision to amputate or salvage a limb. The MESS considers several variables including skeletal and soft tissue injury (graded 1-4), limb ischemia (graded 1-3), shock (graded 0-2), and age (graded 0-2). A calculated score is determined by adding scores of components in four categories. In short, a score of >7 is predictive of amputation; however, several studies have shown that this score should not be the sole criterion by which amputation decisions are made and should be used cautiously. Scores double if there is ischemia >6 hours. This scoring system has high specificity but low sensitivity for predicting amputation.

*Bosse et al. prospectively evaluated the clinical utility of five lower-extremity injury severity scoring systems; the Mangled Extremity Severity Score (MESS); the Limb Salvage Index (LSI); the Predictive Salvage Index (PSI); the Nerve Injury, Ischemia, Soft-Tissue Injury, Skeletal Injury, shock, and Age of Patient Score (NISSSA); and the Hannover Fracture Scale-97 (HFS-97) for ischemic and non-ischemic limbs.* **They found** that low scores could be used to predict limb-salvage potential. **They concluded** that lower-extremity injury-severity scores at or above the amputation threshold should be cautiously used by a surgeon who must decide the fate of a lower extremity with a high-energy injury.

Schirò et al. performed a literature review to evaluate the reliability of several scoring systems. They found that the MESS seems to be more accurate than the LSI in the prediction of limb salvage. They concluded that scoring systems cannot be used as the sole criterion by which amputation decisions are made and, in case of successful limb salvage, they are not predictive of the functional recovery.
