## Question

A 66-year old insulin-dependent diabetic male has had 4 months of atraumatic right shoulder pain and loss of motion. On examination he has loss of active and passive external rotation. He has previously undergone physical therapy and a corticosteroid injection. He is interested in undergoing an arthroscopic capsular release with a manipulation under anesthesia. In addressing expectations, which of the below is most accurate?

## Options

1. Axillary nerve injury is the most common complication
2. Postoperative improvements are maintained at longterm follow up
3. Release of the posterior capsule alleviates the most common area of stiffness
4. Residual stiffness is a rare complication
5. Surgery is typically the first line of treatment

## Correct option

**2. Postoperative improvements are maintained at longterm follow up**

## Answer explanation

If patients with adhesive capsulitis require arthroscopic capsular release, improvements in pain, function, and range of motion are durable at long-term follow up.

Adhesive capsulitis (also known as frozen shoulder) is a condition of the shoulder characterized by functional loss of both passive and active shoulder motion commonly associated with diabetes, and thyroid disease. Treatment is a prolonged course of physical therapy and medical management of underlying disease if present (e.g. diabetes, thyroid disorder). Manipulation under anesthesia or arthroscopic capsular release is indicated in patients with progressive loss of motion having failed a prolonged course of physical therapy.

*Le Lievre and Murrell performed a retrospective review of 43 patients who required arthroscopic capsular release for idiopathic adhesive capsulitis.* The average duration of follow up was seven years. **They found** patients to have sustained relief of pain, improved function and improved motion. **They concluded** that arthroscopic treatment of adhesive capsulitis confers longterm improvements in pain, function, and motion.

*Yian et al. performed a retrospective review on 1150 diabetic patients with the diagnosis of frozen shoulders.* **They found** insulin-dependent diabetics and a longer duration of having diabetes to increase the risk for having a frozen shoulder; however, they did not find a relationship between A1C and the risk for having a frozen shoulder. **They concluded** that A1C does not have a relationship with the risk of having a frozen shoulder, but that other factors of diabetes (insulin-dependence and duration) increase the risk of diabetic patients having a frozen shoulder.

*Hsu et al. performed a review of adhesive capsulitis.* *They described the spectrum of care for adhesive capsulitis including physical therapy, closed manipulation, arthroscopic release, and open release of adhesions.* **They concluded** that there had been a historical misunderstanding over the definition and diagnosis of adhesive capsulitis.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Axillary nerve injury may occur with release of the inferior capsule; however, it is a more rare complication.
Answer 3: Loss of external rotation is most common. Regaining external rotation is achieved by releasing the rotator interval. Releasing the posterior capsule would aid in restoring internal rotation.
Answer 4: Stiffness is the most common complication of surgical management of adhesive capsulitis.
Answer 5: The first line of treatment is physical therapy.
