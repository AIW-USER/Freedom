## Question

A 63-year-old male presents to your clinic with several years of progressive left knee pain. The patient is an avid golfer and has had difficulty playing over the last year. The patient was referred by your non-operative sports colleague after a course of physical therapy and multiple corticosteroid injections, which have had a decreasing effect. The patient’s radiographs demonstrate isolated medial compartment arthritis with evidence of subchondral sclerosis and cyst formation. Which of the following represents a potential advantage of unicompartmental knee arthroplasty (UKA) over total knee arthroplasty (TKA)?

## Options

1. UKA patients typically experience faster rehabilitation and recovery than TKA
2. Lower revision rates than TKA
3. Lower risk of aseptic loosening compared to TKA
4. Higher implant survivorship over TKA
5. Technically less demanding surgery

## Correct option

**1. UKA patients typically experience faster rehabilitation and recovery than TKA**

## Answer explanation

TKA patients typically experience a longer recovery when compared to patients undergoing UKA (Answer 1) but enjoy lower revision rates and higher survivorship.

The decision to perform UKA, TKA, or high tibial osteotomy (HTO) in the setting of unicompartmental arthritis relies on careful evaluation of patient-specific factors. While UKA may be indicated in some patients, there are absolute and relative contraindications that should be considered. This includes inflammatory arthritis, ACL deficiency (controversial), fixed deformities (varus/valgus/ flexion contracture), and other periarticular diseases like arthritis or previous meniscectomy. Historically, UKA has higher revision rates than TKA, but lower rates than HTO. Complications more commonly associated with UKA include aseptic loosening, bearing dislocation, and intraoperative fractures.

*Zuiderbaan et al. reviewed global trends, indications, outcome scores, and survivorship results of UKAs and HTOs.* **The authors report** that UKA includes wider selection criteria and optimization is often required before HTO to improve survivorship. Although current data support better ten-year implant survivorship amongst UKA (90%) vs HTO (75%) there is limited literature directly comparing outcomes. **The authors conclude** that rates of UKA are increasing on a global scale, while HTO is decreasing amongst patients with isolated single-compartment osteoarthritis.

*Lee et al. retrospectively reviewed 61,145 patients from the Korean National Health Insurance database to evaluate the effect of age on survival rate and complications after HTO for medial unicompartmental osteoarthritis.* **The authors found** a lower conversion rate and lower complication rate amongst patients less than 60 years old, but no differences between those 60-65 or older. **The authors conclude** that patient age is one of the factors that predicts a high risk of failure after HTO.

Incorrect answers
Answer 2: UKA has higher revision rates compared to TKA
Answer 3: UKA experiences a higher rate of aseptic loosening compared to TKA
Answer 4: UKA has lower rates of survivorship compared to TKA
Answer 5: UKA is considered a more technically demanding surgery
