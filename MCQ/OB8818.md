## Answer explanation

The patient's history of a painless fracture and thin, osteopenic bone from prolonged non-weightbearing is most consistent with a myelomeningocele, as seen in figure D.

Myelomeningocele is a congenital malformation of the spinal column and/or spinal cord that results from failure of neural tube closure. In a myelomeningocele, as opposed to a meningocele, the neural elements are contained in the posteriorly extruded pouch. As a result, the neural elements are only covered by dura and skin and patients typically have significant motor and sensory deficits. Nonambulators often have osteopenia and lower extremity fractures can be caused by minimal trauma which can be clinically confused with a local infection if radiographs are not performed.

*Kumar et al. retrospectively reviewed 16 patients with a history of myelomeningocele who sustained a total of 37 fractures.* **They found** that metaphyseal and diaphyseal fractures typically presented with warmth, swelling, and erythema. Most fractures healed with immobilization within 4 weeks.

*Lock et al. reviewed 37 patients with myelomeningocele who sustained a total of 76 fractures.* *86% of the fractures occurred in patients younger than 9 and 97% of the fractures were in the lower extremities, distal to their myelomeningocele.* All of the fractures in the metaphysis and diaphysis healed uneventfully with immobilization, however, 43% of the the physeal fractures developed nonunion and 29% had a growth arrest, **suggesting** that physeal fractures are more concerning in this population.

Figures A and B show a proximal tibia fracture with valgus angulation in a patient with osteopenia. Figure C demonstrates a posterior thoracic epidural abscess. Figure D demonstrates a myelomeningocele with posterior cystic extrusion. Figure E illustrates dural ectasia. Figure F shows thoracolumbar scoliosis.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: An epidural abscess could cause bacteremia and subsequent knee infection, however, the patient does not have fevers or chills and it would not explain the delayed presentation, lack of pain, or thin, osteopenic bones on the radiographs.
Answer 3: Dural ectasia can be seen in Marfan's syndrome and neurofibromatosis and would not explain the clues indicating a long-standing neurological problem.
Answer 4: Scoliosis would not lead to a painless proximal tibia fracture.
