## Question

A 45-year-old male presents to your clinic with complaints of a painful left hip. He states the hip has been bothering him for several years now and is worse with increased activity. Radiographs of the hip demonstrate severe arthritic changes with osteophyte formation and subchondral sclerosis. Which of the following changes occurs most commonly in arthritic cartilage?

## Options

1. Decreased proteoglycan degradation
2. Increased water content
3. Increased collagen organization and crosslinking
4. Increased cartilage stiffness
5. Increased keratin sulfate:chondroitin sulfate ratio

## Correct option

**2. Increased water content**

## Answer explanation

Articular cartilage undergoes changes in the setting of osteoarthritis (OA), including increased water permeability, leading to it becoming more elastic.

As cartilage becomes arthritic, several changes are known to take place. First, water content increases and makes the cartilage more elastic. There is a change in the concentrations of glycosaminoglycans, with the ratio of chondroitin 4 sulfate increasing relative to keratin sulfate. Collagen in OA cartilage becomes more disorganized. All of these changes lead to break down of the articular surface and corresponding joint changes of osteophyte formation, subchondral sclerosis and cystic changes. Aging cartilage has many opposite findings, including decreased water content which makes it more brittle.

*Silver et al. reviewed biomechanical, biochemical, and cellular changes in OA.* **They note** that OA is characterized by extracellular matrix breakdown, cartilage degeneration, subchondral bone exposure and eventually, limited joint motion and pain. **They discuss** that these changes are caused by decreases in cartilage tensile strength and loss of the collagen fibril organization in the superficial zone.

*Silver et al. again reviewed the mechanical changes of cartilage in the setting of OA.* **They note** that as cartilage fissuring worsens, the ability of the cartilage to store energy and dissipate stress decreases. **They found** that as compressive loads caused damage to the superficial cartilage zones, all zones became more at risk for degradation.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: OA demonstrates increased proteoglycan degradation, rather than decreased.
Answer 3: Aging cartilage demonstrates increased collagen cross-linking, while OA demonstrates collagen disorganization.
Answer 4: Aging cartilage becomes brittle and stiff, while cartilage in OA becomes more elastic.
Answer 5: In OA, the ratio of chondroitin sulfate:keratin sulfate increases; the opposite is true of aging cartilage.
