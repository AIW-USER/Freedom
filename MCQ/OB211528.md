## Question

A 58-year-old active man with worsening heel pain in the setting of chronic ankle pain for the last 2 years after missing a step off a curb. An MRI was obtained and is depicted in Figure A. He elects to undergo surgical intervention. Intraoperatively, a 4 cm gap is observed. Which of the following would lead to the best functional outcome?

![[q211528-a-achilles-rupture.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Open primary repair with suture anchors
2. Debridement and augmentation with a tendon that inserts on plantar aspect of great toe metatarsal head
3. Debridement and augmentation with a tendon that inserts on plantar aspect of the metatarsal heads of the lesser toes
4. Debridement and augmentation with a tendon that inserts on plantar aspect of great toe distal phalanx
5. Debridement and augmentation with a tendon that inserts on dorsal aspect of distal phalanges of the lesser toes

## Correct option

**4. Debridement and augmentation with a tendon that inserts on plantar aspect of great toe distal phalanx**

## Answer explanation

This patient is presenting with a subacute achilles tendon rupture in the setting of chronic achilles tendonosis. Achilles debridement and augmentation with local tendons including the flexor hallucis longus (FHL) tendon is a viable option shown to improve functional outcomes. The FHL tendon inserts on the plantar aspect of the great toe distal phalanx.

The first step in treating chronic achilles tendonosis consists of conservative measures such as oral analgesic medication and eccentric strengthening exercises of the calf muscles. Operative treatment may be indicated in cases of chronic symptoms lasting >6 months without improvement or in cases of subacute ruptures. Various surgical techniques have been described for chronic achilles tendonosis, with open debridement and primary repair for small tears, while reconstructive options are reserved for larger gaps. In cases with larger gaps, augmentation procedures are preferred, including V-Y myotendinous flaps, free tendon grafts, synthetic grafts, or transfer from local tendons including peroneus brevis, flexor digitorum longus (FDL), and FHL.

Den Hartog reported on his experience with FHL transfers for chronic achilles tendonosis. He reported that the ankle-hindfoot scale ratings improved significantly, all but three patients graded their result as good or excellent in regards to improved function and pain, and no patient had a significant functional deficit or deformity of the hallux after transfer of the FHL tendon. He concluded that the FHL transfer to the achilles for chronic advanced tendonosis provides good or excellent return of function and a significant reduction in pain in the majority of patients.

*Rahm et al. investigated the clinical and radiographic outcomes of FHL transfer to the achilles tendon and compared the transtendinous technique to the transosseous technique.* While they hypothesized that the type of technique would have a notable impact on outcome, no statistically significant differences were found between the groups. **They concluded** that either technique for FHL transfer to the achilles tendon provided good to excellent clinical and functional outcome in the treatment of irreparable achilles tendonosis.

Figure A depicts a T2 sagittal MRI image of an achilles tendon rupture 3.5 cm proximal to the calcaneal attachment with the proximal and distal segments of the tendon severely thickened, suggesting underlying chronic achilles tendonosis.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Open primary repair with suture anchors would most likely fail and require re-operation in a patient with chronic achilles tendonosis with degenerative tissue and a significant gap.
Answer 2: The FHL tendon inserts on the plantar aspect of the great toe distal phalanx, not the metatarsal head.
Answer 3: The FDL tendon inserts on the plantar aspect of the distal phalanges of the lesser toes, not the metatarsal heads.
Answer 5: The FDL tendon inserts on the plantar aspect of the distal phalanges of the lesser toes, not the dorsal aspect.
