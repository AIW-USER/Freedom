## Question

A 65-year-old male presents with a painful right total knee arthroplasty, which was performed ten years ago. CRP is 15 mg/L. Knee aspiration reveals a purulent fluid with 3,100 WBC's with 83% PMN's. Culture results are pending. Which of the following is the best management option?

## Options

1. Physical therapy, ice, and follow-up evaluation in 2 weeks
2. Repeat aspiration if cultures are positive
3. Oral antibiotics
4. Intravenous antibiotics
5. Surgical explant of components

## Correct option

**5. Surgical explant of components**

## Answer explanation

This patient is infected and requires explantation to cure the infection.

*Ghanem et al reviewed 161 infected TKA's vs 268 aseptic failures and discovered synovial fluid aspiration with WBC of >1100 cells/mm3 and PMN > 64% are suggestive of infection.* When both tests yielded results below their cutoff values, the negative predictive value was 98.2% (95% confidence interval, 95.5% to 99.5%), whereas when both tests yielded results greater, infection was confirmed in 98.6% (95% confidence interval, 94.9% to 99.8%) of the cases. Thus, according to the most recent literature, WBC >1100 and PMN > 64% should be considered suggestive of infection in a TKA.

*In an earlier, smaller study, Mason et al reported on 31 septic TKA failures and* **concluded** that aspirates with a WBC count greater than 2,500 and 60% PMNs are highly suggestive of infection.

*In another small study focused on THA, Schinsky et al reviewed 55 infected THA's and* **found** that when combined with an elevated erythrocyte sedimentation rate and C-reactive protein level, the optimal cut-point for the synovial fluid cell count was >3000 white blood cells/mL, which yielded the highest combined sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value, negative predictive value, and accuracy of the tests studied.
