## Question

Which of the following is true regarding patients undergoing hand ray resections?

## Options

1. Patients are unlikely to return to their previous occupation following the procedure
2. There is a greater loss of grip strength with resection of the small finger
3. There is a greater loss of pinch strength with resection of the long finger
4. There is a decrease in palmar width following the procedure
5. Ray resection results in poorly tolerated cosmetic outcomes

## Correct option

**4. There is a decrease in palmar width following the procedure**

## Answer explanation

Patients undergoing a ray resection will experience a decrease in palmar width.

Ray resection can be performed as salvage for a non-functional digit that usually impairs the use of the affected hand. The treatment goal is to improve overall hand function and improved hand cosmesis compared to PIP or MCP level amputations, however, there is decreased grip strength due to loss of palmar width with these techniques. Resection of the long finger has been associated with the greatest decrease in grip strength. Generally, resection is well cosmetically well-tolerated with most patients able to return to their previous occupation.

*Bhat et al. performed a retrospective study of 45 patients that underwent a single-digit ray amputation of the hand.* **They reported** an average decrease in grip and pinch strength of 43.3% and 33.6%, respectively, and primary ray resection being associated with decreased disability and lower treatment costs than secondary reconstructive procedures. **They concluded** that most patients can return to the same level of work following hand-directed therapy.

*Blazar and Garon reviewed the indications, techniques, and outcomes of ray resections.* **They reported** that ray resection can be performed for vascular insufficiency, tumors, infection, and recurrent debilitating Dupuytren's contracture. The literature supports acceptable functional and cosmetic outcomes.

Incorrect answers
Answer 1: Patients typically can return to their previous occupation with appropriate therapy.
Answer 2: The greatest loss in grip strength occurs with resection of the long finger.
Answer 3: The greatest loss in pinch strength occurs with resection of the index finger.
Answer 5: Ray resection is typically associated with good cosmetic outcomes.
