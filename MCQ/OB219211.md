## Question

A 24-year-old male is getting ready for discharge from your level 1 trauma center. Your orthopaedic team has been following the patient since his admission over four weeks ago. The patient was originally admitted following a high-speed motor vehicle accident, and the patient has undergone multiple orthopaedic and general surgical procedures. Which of the following statements regarding psychiatric comorbidities in the setting of polytrauma is most accurate?

## Options

1. Patients admitted to an orthopaedic service are more likely to receive instructions for psychiatric follow-up after discharge
2. Patients with preexisting depression are less likely to experience readmission following discharge
3. Preexisting depression has been identified as an independent predictor of increased complications
4. Preexisting psychiatric disorders in polytrauma patients are rare, occuring in less than 5% of patients
5. Dementia is the most commonly preexisting psychiatric comorbidity encountered in polytraumatized patients

## Correct option

**3. Preexisting depression has been identified as an independent predictor of increased complications**

## Answer explanation

Preexisting depression is the most common preexisting psychiatric condition encountered in polytraumatized patients, and it has been identified as an independent risk factor for increased complications (Answer 3).

Polytraumatic injuries are a major public health concern. They are associated with high rates of disability and death and often confer profound societal costs. As a result, multiple trauma scoring systems have been introduced and are designed to aid in the appropriate triage of trauma patients, predict outcomes for patient and family counseling, aid in quality assurance, and allow for standardized research. As a result of these scoring systems, the burden of preexisting psychiatric illness in trauma patients has come to light. There has been rapid expansion regarding research into the psychosocial component of trauma, as these comorbidities can confer multiple risks onto trauma patients. Overall, depression and substance use disorder have been identified as the most common psychiatric comorbidities in polytraumatized patients.

*Weinberg et al. reported on 362 skeletally mature patients admitted with polytrauma (ISS >= 16) at a level 1 trauma center.* Overall, 39.2% (130) of patients had preexisting psychiatric disorders. Depression was the most common (22.3%) followed by substance abuse (16.9%). **The authors identified** preexisting depression as an independent predictor of increased complications, with an odds ratio of 2.956 (95% C.I 1.502 to 5.816). Important for orthopaedic surgeons, **the authors found** that patients managed by an orthopaedic service were less likely to receive their home psychiatric medications and less likely to receive instructions for psychiatric follow-up at discharge.

*Vincent et al. performed a secondary analysis of data collected during a randomized controlled study.* *Their patient population included 101 patients admitted for trauma.* **The authors found** that patients with preexisting depression or anxiety exhibited blunted physical and emotional recovery following trauma, and that patients with depression were more likely to have a greater length of stay and a higher readmission rate.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Patients admitted to an orthopaedic service are less likely to receive instructions for psychiatric follow-up after discharge.
Answer 2: Patients with preexisting depression are more likely to experience readmission.
Answer 4: Preexisting psychiatric disorders are common, occurring in up to 40% of patients admitted with polytrauma.
Answer 5: Depression and substance abuse disorder are the most common preexisting psychiatric disorders.
