## Question

A 19-year-old healthy female presents to your office with complaints of right leg pain while running. Radiographs are unremarkable. There is some concern for exertional compartment syndrome. Which of the following findings demonstrates elevated 1- and 5-minute post-exercise pressures but a normal pre-exercise pressure?

## Options

1. Rest 20 mmHg; 1 minute 25 mmHg; 5 minute 30 mmHg
2. Rest 10 mmHg; 1 minute 25 mmHg; 5 minute 30 mmHg
3. Rest 20 mmHg; 1 minute 25 mmHg; 5 minute 35 mmHg
4. Rest 10 mmHg; 1 minute 35 mmHg; 5 minute 10 mmHg
5. Rest 10 mmHg; 1 minute 35 mmHg; 5 minute 25 mmHg

## Correct option

**5. Rest 10 mmHg; 1 minute 35 mmHg; 5 minute 25 mmHg**

## Answer explanation

This patient has concerns for exertional compartment syndrome. Findings that would confirm this diagnosis would be a resting pressure >15 mmHg, a 1- and 5-minute post-exercise pressures of >30 and >20 mmHg, respectively.

Exertional compartment syndrome is an exercise-induced condition of the leg characterized by reversible ischemia to muscles within a muscular compartment. Diagnosis is made by obtaining compartment pressures at rest, during exercise and post-exercise. Treatment generally involves surgical fasciotomies of the compartments involved.

*Aweid et al. reviewed systematically the relevant published evidence on diagnostic criteria commonly in use for exertional compartment syndrome.* **They reported** that clinicians may consider measurements taken at 1 minute after exercise. **They concluded** that levels above the highest reported value for controls (27.5 mmHg) along with a good history, should be regarded as highly suggestive of exertional compartment syndrome.

*Roscoe et al. compared dynamic intramuscular compartment pressure (IMCP) and anthropometric factors between patients with exertional compartment syndrome and asymptomatic controls.* **They reported** that subjects had higher IMCP immediately upon standing at rest compared with controls and this relationship persisted throughout the exercise protocol. **They concluded** that anterior compartment IMCP is elevated immediately upon standing at rest in subjects with exertional compartment syndrome.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1-4: Diagnostic criteria for exertional compartment syndrome is a resting (pre-exercise) pressure >15 mmHg, an immediate (1 minute) post-exercise is >30 mmHg and a post-exercise pressure >20 mmHg at 5 minutes
