## Question

A 76-year-old woman has a painful, snapping mass at the inferior angle of the scapula. She has no history of trauma to the area. MRI scans are shown in Figures 71a through 71c. A biopsy specimen is shown in Figure 71d. What is the most appropriate treatment at this time?

## Options

1. Observation
2. Excision
3. Radiation therapy alone
4. Chemotherapy followed by wide excision
5. Local steroid injection

## Correct option

**2. Excision**

## Answer explanation

Elastofibroma is a benign, likely inflammatory mass that typically occurs deep to the inferior angle of the scapula. It may be bilateral and may be symptomatic with either pain or mechanical symptoms related to its location. The recommended treatment for symptomatic lesions is excision. Small or asymptomatic lesions may also be observed. Biopsy to confirm the diagnosis and to exclude sarcoma seems prudent. Neither radiation therapy or chemotherapy are indicated for this benign tumor. Local steroid injection plays no role in the treatment of benign elastofibroma.
