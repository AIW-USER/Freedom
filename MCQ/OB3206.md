## Question

A 9-year-old boy injures his ankle while jumping on a trampoline and cannot bear weight on the extremity. A radiograph taken in the emergency room is displayed in Figure A. A biopsy of this lesion would most likely be consistent with which of the following histology slides?

![[q3206-a-jpg-non-ossifying-fibroma-0401-emedicine.jpg]]
*Figure A*

![[q3206-b-ewings-sarcoma-histo.jpg]]
*Figure B*

![[q3206-c-liposarcoma.jpg]]
*Figure C*

![[q3206-d-lymphoma-histo.jpg]]
*Figure D*

![[q3206-e-osteoblastoma.jpg]]
*Figure E*

![[q3206-f-nof-histo.jpg]]
*Figure F*

## Options

1. Figure B
2. Figure C
3. Figure D
4. Figure E
5. Figure F

## Correct option

**5. Figure F**

## Answer explanation

The clinical presentation and radiographs are consistent for an incidental finding of a non-ossifying fibroma (NOF) in a patient who likely suffered an ankle sprain or related injury. The radiographs show a characteristic eccentric, "bubbly" lytic lesion surrounded by a sclerotic rim. NOF is a benign fibrogenic lesion and is the most common benign bone tumor in childhood. It is commonly found in the metaphysis of long bones. Typically, the radiographic findings of NOF are characteristic enough to allow for observation only, and biopsy is not required.

Figure F shows characteristic histology of NOF including numerous giant cells amongst a background of fibroblastic spindle cells and fibroblastic connective tissue. Figure B is representative of a Ewing's sarcoma, C is a liposarcoma, D is a lymphoma, and E is an osteoblastoma.
