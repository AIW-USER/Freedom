Chapter: General Orthopedics MCQs — DVT Prophylaxis

## Question

A 68-year-old male undergoes an uncomplicated primary total hip arthroplasty (THA). The surgical team aims to utilize a contemporary "step-down" chemoprophylaxis protocol to maximize venous thromboembolism (VTE) protection during the acute inflammatory phase while minimizing the risk of delayed surgical site hematoma. Based on validated landmark trials (e.g., EPCAT II) and established pharmacokinetic switchover principles, which of the following regimens represents the most accurate and safe prophylactic strategy?

## Options

1. Initiate Enoxaparin 40 mg subcutaneously daily starting 12 hours post-operatively. On postoperative day 5, to prevent a sub-therapeutic trough during transition, overlap the LMWH with Apixaban 2.5 mg twice daily for 48 hours, then continue Apixaban monotherapy until day 35.
2. Initiate Rivaroxaban 10 mg orally once daily starting 12 to 24 hours post-operatively. On postoperative day 6, discontinue Rivaroxaban and immediately step down to Ecosprin (Aspirin) 81 mg orally once daily for an additional 30 days.
3. Initiate Ecosprin 325 mg orally twice daily starting 6 hours post-operatively. On postoperative day 14, due to decreasing mobility, transition directly to Rivaroxaban 20 mg daily by administering the first dose concurrently with the final dose of Ecosprin.
4. Initiate Enoxaparin 30 mg subcutaneously twice daily. On the day of hospital discharge, switch directly to a DOAC by administering the first oral dose exactly 2 hours after the final subcutaneous LMWH injection to ensure seamless Factor Xa inhibition.

## Correct option

**2. Initiate Rivaroxaban 10 mg orally once daily starting 12 to 24 hours post-operatively. On postoperative day 6, discontinue Rivaroxaban and immediately step down to Ecosprin (Aspirin) 81 mg orally once daily for an additional 30 days.**

## Answer explanation

Option B is correct because it perfectly describes the highly validated EPCAT II trial protocol for THA. Rivaroxaban is administered at the standard prophylactic orthopedic dose (10 mg daily) starting 12–24 hours post-op (once hemostasis is secure). After 5 days of potent Factor Xa inhibition during the peak hypercoagulable window, stepping down to low-dose Ecosprin (81 mg daily) for the remaining 30 days provides non-inferior VTE protection compared to 35 days of continuous Rivaroxaban, but with a significantly lower risk of major bleeding.

Option A is incorrect because overlapping a low-molecular-weight heparin (LMWH) with a Direct Oral Anticoagulant (DOAC) is strictly contraindicated. DOACs have a rapid onset of action (peak plasma concentration in 1–4 hours). Bridging them with LMWH causes immediate, profound over-anticoagulation and severe bleeding risk.

Option C is incorrect for multiple reasons: 20 mg is the treatment dose for a DVT/PE, not the prophylactic dose (which is 10 mg). Furthermore, starting Ecosprin at 6 hours post-op is generally too early and threatens acute hemostasis, and "stepping up" from aspirin to a DOAC at day 14 defies the logic of peak post-operative risk.

Option D is incorrect due to improper switchover timing. When transitioning from LMWH to a DOAC, the first dose of the DOAC must be given exactly when the next scheduled dose of LMWH would have been due (e.g., 12 hours after the last 30 mg BID dose, or 24 hours after a 40 mg daily dose), not 2 hours after. Administering it at 2 hours would stack the peak effects of both drugs.

Therefore, "step-down" or hybrid transition pathways are increasingly utilized for patients without specific high-risk hypercoagulable conditions.

 Rationale:
 Validated by the EPCAT II trial, administering Rivaroxaban for the first 5 days, then stepping down to Aspirin 81 mg daily for 9 additional days (TKA) or 30 additional days (THA) is as effective as taking Rivaroxaban for the entire duration, and is highly cost-effective with a low bleeding profile.

 LMWH to DOAC (Direct Switch)

 Administer the first dose of the DOAC exactly when the next scheduled dose of LMWH would be due (e.g., 24 hours after the last 40 mg LMWH dose).

 Rationale:
 Direct Oral Anticoagulants have a rapid onset of action (reaching peak plasma concentrations in 1–4 hours). Overlapping a DOAC with LMWH causes immediate and profound over-anticoagulation, severely threatening surgical site hemostasis.

Essential Clinical Caveats:

 - Renal Function: LMWH and DOAC dosages must be adjusted or strictly avoided in patients with severe renal impairment (Creatinine Clearance < 30 mL/min).

 - Neuraxial Anesthesia: If an epidural catheter remains in place, DOACs and prophylactic LMWH doses require strict timing intervals regarding catheter removal to prevent spinal hematoma (e.g., LMWH must be held for 12 hours prior to catheter removal, and the next dose delayed for at least 4 hours post-removal).

 - Mechanical Prophylaxis: Regardless of the chemical agent utilized, intermittent pneumatic compression (IPC) devices and early mobilization must be implemented from post-op Day 0, provided there are no contraindications.
