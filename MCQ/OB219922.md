## Options

1. The patient is at increased risk for pathologic fracture
2. The patient is at increased risk of postoperative infection
3. The patient will require postoperative radiation therapy
4. The patient should be prescribed a colony-stimulating factor 1 receptor (CSF1R) inhibitor
5. The patient is at increased risk of local disease recurrence

## Correct option

**5. The patient is at increased risk of local disease recurrence**

## Answer explanation

The mass is consistent with a tenosynovial giant cell tumor of the tendon sheath (TGCT), which has an increased recurrence rate in the setting of poor encapsulation and osseous involvement (Answer 5).

TGCT is the second most common soft tissue tumor of the hand, with ganglion cysts being the most common. TGCT is considered idiopathic with no report of malignant transformation. The mass is typically well-circumscribed and isointense on T1-weighted images and may be hyperintense on T2-weighted images. Treatment is marginal excision. The risk of recurrence is increased with diffuse poorly encapsulated lesions, presence of satellite lesions, distal location in the digit, flexor or extensor tendon involvement, joint involvement, intraosseous involvement, and associated degenerative joint disease.

*Lanzinger et al. explored the risk factors implicated in the recurrence rate of TGCT.* *They comment that the reported recurrence rate after surgical excision varies significantly between 4% to 44%, which is influenced by certain morphologic features, namely poorly encapsulated lesions, intraosseous involvement, associated degenerative joint disease, involvement of the extensor or flexor tendon sheath, distal location in the digit as well as the presence of satellite lesions.* **They conclude** multicentric, diffuse disease with satellite lesions has a higher recurrence rate than the locally confined encapsulated tumor. However, the morphologic differences between these two forms of TGCT need further delineation. There is a consensus that recurrence is associated with incomplete surgical excision.

*Hamdi et al. analyzed 27 cases of TGCT involving the flexor tendon sheath of the hand.* *They describe TGCT as a slowly progressing benign mass that arises from the synovial cells of tendon sheaths.* *Within their study population,* **they noted** recurrence in two surgically managed cases with a mean follow-up length of 4.5 years. **They conclude** that surgical excision should be meticulous and complete to avoid recurrence.

*Reilly et al. reviewed 70 patients who underwent surgical excision of TGCT, with 19 (27%) patients developing recurrence.* Recurrence occurred at an average of 27 months following the initial procedure. Statistically significant risk factors for recurrence included adjacent degenerative joint disease, location near the distal interphalangeal joint of the finger or interphalangeal joint of the thumb, and radiographic presence of an osseous erosion. Age, gender, size, and volar or dorsal location were not associated with recurrence. **The authors conclude** that awareness of the risk factors for TGCT recurrence is paramount for patient counseling and expectations before surgical excision.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Poorly encapsulated TGCT is not associated with pathologic fracture.
Answer 2: The described morphologic features of this lesion are associated with recurrence, not postoperative infection.
Answer 3: The role of adjuvant radiation is unclear in this clinical setting and is associated with wound complications and fibrosis. The patient should be counseled on the risk of recurrence.
Answer 4: Colony-stimulating factor 1 receptor (CSF1R) inhibitors may be used for TGCT, a non-surgical treatment option in clinical settings where surgery is considered excessively morbid or clinically unfeasible. This is typically used in diffuse-type TGCT.
