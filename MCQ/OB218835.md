## Question

A 34-year-old female presents to your office for evaluation of right wrist pain. She previously underwent radial head excision for the treatment of a comminuted radial head fracture four years ago. Radiographs of the wrist today reveal ulnocarpal impingement. In addition to the radial head fracture, which of the following injuries also most likely occurred at the initial injury?

## Options

1. Distal radius fracture alone
2. Distal radioulnar joint injury alone
3. Distal radius fracture and distal radioulnar joint injury
4. Distal radioulnar joint and interosseous membrane injuries
5. Distal radius fracture, distal radioulnar joint injury, and interosseous membrane injury

## Correct option

**4. Distal radioulnar joint and interosseous membrane injuries**

## Answer explanation

This patient has an Essex-Lopresti injury. An Essex-Lopresti injury involves a radial head fracture, distal radioulnar joint (DRUJ) injury, and an interosseus membrane injury.

Essex-Lopresti injuries involves a radial head fracture, distal radioulnar joint (DRUJ) injury, and an interosseus membrane injury resulting in a loss of longitudinal stability. Essex-Lopresti lesions that are treated with a radial head excision will exacerbate elbow/wrist instability and may result in proximal radial migration and ulnocarpal impingement. Therefore, treatment must include that the radial head must be fixed or replaced to restore stability and prevent proximal migration of the radius and ulnocarpal impaction.

*Matson et al. reviewed Essex-Lopresti injuries.* **They reported** that these injuries are characterized by fractures of the radial head, disruption of the forearm interosseous membrane, and dislocation of the distal radioulnar joint. **They noted** that this injury pattern results in axial and longitudinal instability of the forearm. **They concluded** that early recognition and treatment are preferred as failure to fully treat the problem may result in chronic wrist pain from ulnar abutment or chronic elbow pain from radiocapitellar arthrosis.

*Adams et al. reviewed Essex-Lopresti injuries.* *They reported on the evaluation and treatment considerations for Essex-Lopresti injuries.* **They concluded** that radial head excision in these injuries could lead to proximal radial migration and ulnocarpal impingement.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1,3,5: An Essex-Lopresti injury does not involve a distal radius fracture
Answer 2: An Essex-Lopresti injury involves injury to the DRUJ, but also includes injuries to the interosseous membrane and a fracture of the radial head
