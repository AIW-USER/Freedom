## Question

A 56-year-old male sustains a displaced, 2-part proximal humerus fracture after a motor vehicle collision. You recommend open reduction and internal fixation to reduce and stabilize the fracture. Which of the following complications is most likely to occur directly as a result of suboptimal inferomedial calcar support?

## Options

1. Varus collapse
2. Avascular necrosis
3. Nonunion
4. Post traumatic arthritis
5. Screw cutout

## Correct option

**1. Varus collapse**

## Answer explanation

With inadequate inferomedial calcar support, risk for varus fracture collapse post-operatively increases.

Proximal humerus fractures are common, particularly in older patients with osteoporotic bone. Displaced 2-, 3- and 4-part fractures as well as head split patterns are indicated for open reduction and internal fixation (ORIF). Inferomedial calcar support is important in preventing varus collapse postoperatively and can be achieved with an anatomic reduction, placement of long calcar screws or use of a medial cortical reconstruction with a fibular strut. In cases where significant varus collapse occurs, functional outcomes are affected and this may necessitate revision surgery.

*Gardner et al. reviewed the importance of medial calcar support in 35 patients who underwent locked plating of the proximal humerus.* **They found** the presence of medial calcar support significantly decreased the amount of reduction loss that occurred postoperatively. **They concluded** that even with locked plating, a stable reduction with inferomedial calcar support was important in maintaining reduction and preventing varus collapse.

*Ponce et al. evaluated the role of medial calcar support in preventing varus collapse with locked proximal humerus plating in cadavers.* Inferomedial comminution decreased load to failure by 48% and the use of calcar screws increased load to failure by 31%. With these results, they reiterate the importance of innate or supplemental stability of the posteromedial calcar region to improve stability and prevent varus collapse.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: While decreased calcar length attached to the articular segment increases the risk for humeral head ischemia, this has not been shown to correlate with symptomatic avascular necrosis.
Answer 3: Calcar support affects rates of malunion and has less of an effect on nonunion rates.
Answer 4: Post-traumatic arthritis rates are not affected by the amount of inferomedial calcar support as this does not directly alter the cartilage interface.
Answer 5: Screw cutout can occur after varus collapse, but would not be a direct result of insufficient inferomedial calcar support.
