## Question

In addition to a true lateral radiograph of the sacrum to assess for the iliac cortical densities, which radiographic view in a dysmorphic sacrum reveals the anterior cortical indentation, inadvertent iliosacral screw penetration of which may result in an L5 nerve injury?

## Options

1. Inlet view
2. Outlet view
3. Iliac oblique view
4. Obturator outlet view
5. Obturator inlet view

## Correct option

**1. Inlet view**

## Answer explanation

Anterior iliosacral screw penetration, as evident on an intra-operative inlet radiograph, places the L5 nerve at risk.

Correct iliosacral screw placement in patients with sacral dysmorphism is achieved with proper intraoperative imaging. The true lateral sacrum radiograph reveals the iliac cortical densities as is the best image to assess for iliosacral screw placement within the sacral ala. Additionally, the inlet radiograph shows the anterior limit of the screw pathway. However, with the inlet radiograph it is possible for the screw to be placed in an "in-out-in" fashion in a dysmorphic sacrum. If the drill or screw is placed anterior to the anterior sacral indentation, injury to the L5 nerve root may occur. Conversely, the outlet view shows the appropriate trajectory in relation to the S1 neuroforamen and the cortical density of the neuroforaminal tunnel as it courses from cranial to caudal.

*Miller et al. reviewed the variations in sacral morphology and implications for iliosacral screw fixation.* *They discuss the several key characteristics of sacral dysmorphism.* **They concluded** that the surgeon must be knowledgeable about individual patient anatomy to ensure safe iliosacral screw placement.

*Conflitti et al. retrospectively quantified upper sacral dysmorphic osseous anatomy and assessed its impact on second sacral segment iliosacral screw insertion in patients with unstable posterior pelvic ring disruptions.* **They report** that the maximum potential screw length for the dysmorphic S1 averaged 100.8 mm and for S2 measured 151.9 mm. **They concluded** that dysmorphic S1 segments are anatomically competent for routine screw fixation and the S2 segment provides a larger osseous site for screw insertion than S1 in the dysmorphic sacrum, allowing for significantly longer screws in S2 compared with the dysmorphic S1 segment.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: The outlet view shows the appropriate trajectory in relation to the S1 neuroforamen and the cortical density of the neuroforaminal tunnel as it courses from cranial to caudal
Answer 3: The iliac oblique radiograph is useful in assessing anterior wall and posterior column of the acetabulum
Answer 4: The obturator outlet best view to rule out joint penetration
Answer 5: The obturator inlet view best to determine position of a supraacetabular screw within tables of the ilium
