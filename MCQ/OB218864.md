## Question

A 12-year-old patient with a Gross Motor Function Classification System (GMFCS) level V undergoes a posterior spinal fusion for neuromuscular scoliosis. The intraoperative course is uneventful. The patient then spikes a fever of 102.1° F on postoperative day 2. Chest radiographs and sputum samples are obtained, and are consistent with pneumonia. Which of the following is a risk factor for the postoperative respiratory complication seen in this patient vignette?

## Options

1. Total lymphocyte count <1,000/mm3
2. Gastrostomy tube in-situ
3. Serum albumin <3.5 g/dl
4. History of reactive airway disease
5. Preoperative hemoglobin <8g/dl

## Correct option

**2. Gastrostomy tube in-situ**

## Answer explanation

The patient in the vignette developed post-operative pneumonia after undergoing posterior spinal fusion for neuromuscular scoliosis. The presence of a gastrostomy tube is the only risk factor for developing a postoperative respiratory complication of the answer choices listed.

Medically complex patients undergoing posterior spinal fusion for neuromuscular scoliosis are at a higher risk for postoperative complications. Compared with other types of scoliosis, neuromuscular scoliosis (NMS) possesses the highest rate of complications, followed by congenital (10.6%) and idiopathic (6.3%) scoliosis. Respiratory complications following posterior spinal fusion are common for patients with neuromuscular scoliosis, with studies demonstrating an incidence of 15 to 28% of all operative cases. Such complications can lead to significant morbidity and an overall reduction in the quality of care. As such, it is essential to identify patient-specific risk factors for developing postoperative respiratory complications among this patient cohort to implement strategies to reduce respiratory-related morbidity.

*Luhmann et al. conducted a single surgeon institutional database retrospective study to identify risk factors associated with developing postoperative complications in patients undergoing posterior spinal fusion for neuromuscular scoliosis.* *The authors defined postoperative respiratory complications as the development of pneumonia, pleural effusion, pneumothorax, need for intubation, respiratory status requiring a return to the pediatric intensive care unit, or prolonged hospital need for mechanical ventilation exceeding four days.* *Their study entailing 111 patients, demonstrated that a history of pneumonia and the presence of a gastrostomy tube were the only two variables associated with developing postoperative respiratory complications after adjusting for other variables.*

*Furdock et al. conducted a retrospective study to identify risk factors associated with developing surgical site infection in neuromuscular scoliosis patients undergoing posterior spinal fusion.* **The authors revealed** that low preoperative nutritional labs, hemoglobin, and total lymphocyte count values were not associated with increased surgical site infection, thereby questioning the utility of obtaining preoperative laboratory values to identify at-risk populations for surgical site infection in patients with neuromuscular scoliosis undergoing posterior spinal fusion.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1,3-5: Low total lymphocyte count, serum albumin <3.5g/dl, history of reactive airway disease, and preoperative hemoglobin <8g/dl have not been associated with the development of postoperative respiratory complications in this patient cohort.
