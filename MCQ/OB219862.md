## Question

A 24-year-old female with a history of scleroderma presents to the hand clinic for evaluation of her Raynaud phenomenon. The patient was treated several years ago with Botox injections with success and would like to initiate treatment again. What test or procedure should be performed before beginning treatment?

## Options

1. Urine pregnancy test
2. Hemoglobin A1C
3. Sympathectomy
4. Lipid Profile
5. Chest Xray

## Correct option

**1. Urine pregnancy test**

## Answer explanation

Pregnancy and breastfeeding are absolute contraindications to botulinum toxin, and the patient should be screened before initiating treatment (Answer 1).

Raynaud phenomenon (RP) is characterized by episodic vasospasm of the small arteries and arterioles. This vasospastic reaction reduces blood flow, resulting in color changes, numbness, pain, ulceration, and potential digit loss. Raynaud's can be primary (idiopathic) or secondary to conditions like scleroderma or lupus. Treatment aims to prevent episodes and manage symptoms, primarily through lifestyle modifications, such as avoiding cold exposure, smoking, and stress. Pharmacologic therapies to promote vasodilation include calcium channel blockers, prostaglandins, phosphodiesterase inhibitors, angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors, and nitrates.

Botulinum-toxin-A injections offer an alternative noninvasive treatment option for RP. While the exact therapeutic mechanism in RP is unknown, the therapy is believed to work by blocking alpha-2 receptor recruitment on vascular smooth muscle or inhibiting pain-mediating chemicals like glutamate and substance P. Absolute contraindications include anaphylactic allergy, pregnancy, breastfeeding, active infections, and coadministration with medications affecting nerve transmission (e.g., aminoglycosides, anticholinergics). Particular caution must be taken for patients with respiratory issues or neuromuscular disorders like myasthenia gravis. Sympathectomy is reserved for the most severe cases that fail conservative treatment. Periarterial sympathectomy is the most common method and involves removing the adventitia and sympathetic nerve fibers in the affected digits.

*Ennis et al. review the effectiveness and efficacy of botulinum toxin injection in primary and secondary Raynaud’s phenomenon.* *The authors discuss botulinum toxin formulation dosage, administration sites, and adverse effects.* Btx-A is the most studied formulation, and the palm is the most common site for injection. Numerous scoring algorithms have been used to evaluate the response of botulinum toxin in Raynaud’s with a mean duration of benefit of 6 months. **The authors conclude** that botulinum toxin injection is a promising treatment but should be provided under the supervision of knowledgeable specialists.

*Uppal et al. presented their series of botulinum toxin treatments for Raynaud’s syndrome in patients with scleroderma.* *The authors assessed changes in pain, appearance, cold intolerance, pinch and power grip, ranges of movement, and Disabilities of the Arm, Shoulder, and Hand (DASH) score.* *80% of patients reported an overall improvement in their symptoms, pain reduction, and DASH score.* Additionally, 90% improved pinch grip and 65% in power grip. Based on these findings, **the authors conclude** that botulinum toxin effectively treats Raynaud's syndrome secondary to scleroderma.

*Gallegos et al. review botulinum toxin as an effective therapy for treating Raynaud phenomenon (RP) refractory to medical management.* First, the authors review the pathophysiology of RP, which is secondary to an exaggerated reaction to the sympathetic nervous system. Next, the authors summarize alternative treatments for RP, including calcium channel blockers, SSRIs, ACE inhibitors, topic nitrates Alpha -1 blockers, and several others. Finally, the authors discuss the mechanism, indications, contraindications, and complications of botulinum toxin injection.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2. Diabetes or elevated A1c are not contraindications to botulinum toxin and do not need to be evaluated before beginning treatment.
Answer 3. Sympathectomy is generally reserved for patients who have failed all non-invasive conservative management. This patient responded well to prior injections and should attempt repeat injections before undergoing surgical intervention.
Answer 4. Botulinum toxin injections have not been shown to affect serum lipid concentrations
Answer 5. While concurrent infections are a contraindication to botulinum injections, the patient is not endorsing any other conditions as reflected in the normal review of systems.
