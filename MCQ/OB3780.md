## Question

Figure 1 is the lateral cervical spine radiograph of a 28-year-old male after a motor vehicle collision. His physical examination reveals a Glasgow coma scale score of 15 and ASIA C impairment. He consents to closed reduction with high weight traction. Five minutes after adding weight he has ASIA B neurologic function. What is the next best step?

![[q3780-a-screen-shot-2020-01-21-at-8-19-21-am.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Add more weight and reassess
2. Emergent MRI
3. Urgent anterior decompression
4. Removal of traction
5. Vasopressors to maximize mean arterial pressure

## Correct option

**4. Removal of traction**

## Answer explanation

The patient has bilateral facet dislocation with incomplete spinal cord injury (SCI). He has a progressive decline in neurologic function with reduction; therefore, the next best step is the removal of traction and weight.

Closed reduction with traction for facet dislocations is an effective means of spinal cord decompression. It is indicated in awake, alert, and cooperative patients. Contraindications include skull fracture and inability to continually assess the patient's neurologic exam. Weights are added in increments of 10 lbs every 10 minutes followed by repeat neurologic examination and radiographs. Any decline in neurologic status should be followed by the removal of weight first and urgent MRI if not already completed.

*Fehlings et. al. reviewed the evidence regarding the timing of surgical intervention in spinal cord injury.* **They report** that nonoperative treatment of incomplete spinal cord injury (SCI) is associated with a 10% decline in neurologic status, and support the urgent closed reduction of bilateral locked facets with incomplete SCI. **They conclude** that urgent decompression (<24 hours) is optimal in acute cervical SCI.

*Rabinowitz et. al. evaluated the effect of steroids alone, steroids and decompression or decompression alone in acute spinal cord injury.* In their animal model, **they found** that steroid only treatment resulted in worse 2-week outcomes. **They concluded** that regardless of steroid administration, surgical decompression results in the best outcomes following acute SCI.

Figure 1 is a lateral radiograph of the cervical spine that demonstrates a facet dislocation at C5-6.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: The patient has sustained neurologic deterioration after the addition of weight; therefore, adding more weight would be incorrect
Answer 2: An emergent MRI would be performed after removing the traction
Answer 3: Urgent decompression is likely in this patient, however, removal of weight and MRI are the next best steps in order
Answer 5: While appropriate spinal cord perfusion is necessary for the SCI patient, the next best step would be to remove the traction
