## Question

A 24-year-old male professional football player is tackled from behind and forcibly contacts the ground with his knee in flexion and ankle in plantarflexion. On subsequent examination of the knee, there is a significant effusion, with stable Lachman testing and no tenderness at the proximal tibia. There is 11mm of tibial translation on posterior drawer testing with the knee in neutral rotation, and 7mm of translation with the knee internally rotated. No varus or valgus instability is noted. After advanced imaging is obtained, open-reduction and internal fixation is planned for definitive management. Which of the following images is most consistent with the physical examination and planned surgical treatment?

## Answer explanation

The mechanism of injury and physical examination are consistent with an isolated posterior cruciate ligament (PCL) injury. Open-reduction and internal fixation is indicated for displaced bony avulsion fractures at the tibial insertion of the PCL.

Bony avulsion fractures are a rare variant of PCL injuries. Several treatment options have been described in the literature, including both operative and nonoperative modalities. Injuries occurring in young, active patients typically warrant surgical intervention, particularly when there is displacement of the avulsed fracture fragment. Unlike intra-substance tears, surgical treatment involves reduction and fixation of the avulsed fragment rather than ligamentous reconstruction. Successful outcomes have been reported with both open and arthroscopic techniques.

*Katsman et al. reviewed the diagnosis and treatment of PCL avulsion fractures,* **highlighting** the indications for operative and nonoperative management and associated outcomes. **They reported** that operative reduction and fixation is indicated in the setting of displaced fractures, and excellent outcomes can be obtained with both open and arthroscopic techniques with low complication rates. **The authors concluded** that displaced fractures warrant surgical intervention, with successful outcomes expected.

*Hooper et al. performed a systematic review assessing treatment options for tibial-sided PCL avulsion injuries, including 28 articles and 637 patients.* Arthroscopic treatment resulted in a higher percentage of International Knee Documentation Committee (IKDC) grade A scores (indicating a normal knee), a higher percentage of return to pre-injury level of activity, and a slightly higher rate of arthrofibrosis compared to open treatment. **The authors concluded** that both open and arthroscopic approaches yield similar outcomes and complications, with arthroscopic treatment carrying the added benefit of intra-articular evaluation and management of concomitant injuries such as a meniscal tear or loose osteochondral fragment.

Figure A demonstrates an intra-substance PCL injury.
Figure B demonstrates a displaced tibial-sided PCL avulsion injury.
Figures C and D demonstrate tibial-sided ACL avulsion injuries.
Figure E demonstrates a tibial tuberosity avulsion injury.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Intra-substance PCL injuries can be managed non-operatively or with PCL reconstruction.
Answers 3 and 4: The physical examination is not consistent with an ACL injury.
Answer 5: The physical examination is not consistent with a tibial tuberosity avulsion injury.
