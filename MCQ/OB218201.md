## Question

A 66-year-old woman has known osteoarthritis of the middle finger metacarpophalangeal (MCP) joint. She is now scheduled for operative intervention. The treating surgeon is considering either a pyrocarbon or silicone arthroplasty. Which of the following is true regarding contraindications for MCP joint arthroplasty?

## Options

1. Deficient sagittal band is a contraindication to use of silicone implants
2. Deficient radial lateral band is a contraindication to use of pyrocarbon implants
3. Deficient intermetacarpal ligaments are a contraindication to use of silicone implants
4. Deficient volar plate is a contraindication to use of silicone implants
5. Deficient collateral ligaments are a contraindication to use of pyrocarbon implants

## Correct option

**5. Deficient collateral ligaments are a contraindication to use of pyrocarbon implants**

## Answer explanation

Pyrocarbon implants are unlinked surface replacement arthroplasties and therefore rely on the soft tissues, namely the collateral ligaments and the volar plate, for joint stability.

Arthritis of the MCP joint is a common form of osteoarthritis seen in the hand and can be associated with pain and deformity. The diagnosis is made radiographically with joint space narrowing seen in the MCP joint of the fingers. The surgical treatment options include joint denervation, arthrodesis, and joint arthroplasty. With joint arthroplasty, two of the more common implants used are pyrocarbon and silicone. Pyrocarbon implants are unlinked and therefore rely on the soft tissues, namely the collateral ligaments and the volar plate, for joint stability. Conversely, a silicone implant is a one-piece linked implant that provides joint stability inherent in its design. Deficiencies in the radial sagittal band, the radial lateral band, the subchondral bone of the metacarpal head, and the intermetacarpal ligaments are not contraindications to the use of pyrocarbon or silicone arthroplasties in the MCP joint.

*Dickson et al. reviewed the outcomes, complications, and survivorship of pyrocarbon metacarpophalangeal joint arthroplasty in noninflammatory arthropathy at a minimum 5-year follow-up.* **They reported** good pain relief, a functional range of motion, and high satisfaction in the majority of patients. **They concluded** that all implant revisions were performed within 18 months of the index procedure which may represent technical issues rather than problems with the implant.

*Srnec et al. reviewed arthritis of the hand.* **They reported** that arthroplasty serves as a surgical option in the surgical management of arthritis and aims to create a pain-free joint with preservation of motion. **They noted** that the hinged silicone prosthesis was one of the early implants and, in many cases, remains the gold standard. However, problems with deformity correction, implant fracture, and synovitis remain. **They concluded** that implants made of alternative materials such as metal-plastic and pyrocarbon have evolved; however, survivorship and re-operation rates remain a concern.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1-4: Deficiencies in the radial sagittal band, the radial lateral band, the subchondral bone of the metacarpal head, and the intermetacarpal ligaments are not contraindications to the use of pyrocarbon or silicone arthroplasties in the MCP joint.
