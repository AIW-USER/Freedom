## Options

2. HVA = 40, IMA = 20, DMAA = 8, advanced MTP arthritic changes

## Correct option

**3. HVA = 20, IMA = 10, DMAA = 20, congruent joint**

## Answer explanation

In a patient with a markedly abnormal distal metatarsal articular angle (DMAA), a normal or near-normal intermetatarsal angle (IMA), and no tarsometatarsal (TMT) joint hypermobility, a biplanar distal first metatarsal Chevron osteotomy would be effective in treating his hallux valgus.

There are numerous procedures described for the correction of hallux valgus, with proximal osteotomies typically indicated for greater deformities to provide more powerful corrections. Additionally, the surgeon must take into account factors such as joint congruity, TMT hypermobility, arthritic changes, neurological conditions, and patient age. The Chevron osteotomy has been widely used to correct mild-to-moderate hallux valgus deformities. Utilizing a biplanar Chevron osteotomy is useful when the DMAA exceeds 10 degrees. Utilizing a soft tissue procedure, in addition to an osteotomy, is useful when the MTP joint is incongruent (lateral subluxation of the proximal phalanx on the metatarsal head).

*Coughlin reviewed the etiology and treatment of juvenile hallux valgus (JHV).* *He conducted an 11-year retrospective study of 45 patients with JHV.* **He found** that 92% of patients had good and excellent results with a multi-procedural approach, 88% of patients were female, and 40% of deformities were found in patients aged 10 or younger.

*Trnka et al. evaluated the outcomes of the chevron osteotomy used for hallux valgus in 43 patients at 2 and 5 years follow-up.* *They evaluated the range of motion, patient satisfaction, maintenance of correction, and the development of arthritis.* **They found** the procedure to be reliable at both time points and not affected by patient age.
