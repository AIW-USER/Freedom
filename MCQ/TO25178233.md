Chapter: Regional Orthopedics MCQs — Avascular Necrosis Of Hip (Avn)

## Question

A 28-year-old male athlete presents with insidious onset of right groin pain. MRI reveals a necrotic lesion in the anterosuperior femoral head with a "Double Line Sign." There is no evidence of subchondral fracture or flattening on high-resolution CT. The Modified Kerboul Angle is calculated at 210°, and the "Modified Combined Necrotic Index" is 45.

Question:
Which of the following statements best describes the surgical prognosis and most appropriate management strategy for this patient according to recent literature?

## Options

1. The patient has a high probability of successful joint preservation with isolated Multiple Small-Diameter Core Decompression due to the absence of the "Crescent Sign."
2. The Modified Kerboul Angle and Combined Necrotic Index place this patient in a "High-Risk" category for collapse, making core decompression alone insufficient; vascularized fibular grafting or cellular augmentation (BMAC) should be considered.
3. The presence of the "Double Line Sign" on T2-weighted MRI indicates that the repair process is complete and the lesion is stable, requiring only protected weight-bearing and Bisphosphonates.
4. Because the necrotic index is > 33, the patient has already passed the "window of opportunity" for joint preservation, and a primary cementless Total Hip Arthroplasty (THA) with a ceramic-on-ceramic bearing is the only indicated treatment.

## Correct option

**2. The Modified Kerboul Angle and Combined Necrotic Index place this patient in a "High-Risk" category for collapse, making core decompression alone insufficient; vascularized fibular grafting or cellular augmentation (BMAC) should be considered.**

## Answer explanation

Correct Answer: B

Detailed Explanation and Rationale:

 - Understanding the Thresholds: In AVN management, the volume of the necrotic lesion is the single best predictor of collapse.

 - The Combined Necrotic Index
 (calculated on MRI) > 33 is associated with a > 80 % rate of femoral head collapse if left untreated.

 - The Modified Kerboul Angle > 200° is considered a high-risk lesion.

 - Since this patient has a Kerboul angle of 210° and an index of 45, he is at extreme risk for mechanical failure despite currently being in a "Pre-collapse" stage (Ficat II).

 - Why Choice A is incorrect: While the "Crescent Sign" is absent (meaning he is still pre-collapse), the size of the lesion is too large for simple core decompression. Simple CD relies on the body's native repair mechanism, which is often overwhelmed in lesions of this magnitude.

 - Why Choice B is correct: Recent meta-analyses and studies (notably by Hernigou et al.) demonstrate that for high-risk pre-collapse lesions, "biological priming" with BMAC (Bone Marrow Aspirate Concentrate) or providing structural support via a Vascularized Fibular Graft significantly reduces the conversion rate to THA compared to CD alone.

 - Why Choice C is incorrect: The "Double Line Sign" actually represents the active
 transition zone between dead bone and granulation tissue. It is a sign of an ongoing, often failing, repair process, not stability. Bisphosphonates have not been shown to prevent collapse in lesions of this size (> 30 % volume).

 - Why Choice D is incorrect: While the patient is high-risk, he is 28 years old and pre-collapse. In the modern "Joint Preservation" era, every attempt should be made to save the native head in a patient this young before committing to THA, especially since cellular therapies have shown promise in this specific "high-risk but not yet collapsed" window.

The Kerboul Sign (and Modified Kerboul Sign)

 - The original Kerboul sign was designed to calculate the "Combined Angle of Necrosis" using plain radiographs (AP and Lateral views). However, the Modified Kerboul Sign (based on MRI) is significantly more accurate and is widely cited in recent joint-preservation literature.

Calculation:

Mid-coronal MRI slice: Measure the arc of the necrotic lesion relative to the center of the femoral head (Angle A).

Mid-sagittal MRI slice: Measure the arc of the necrotic lesion {Angle B}

{Modified Kerboul Grade} = {Angle A} + {Angle B}.

Prognostic Value:

 - Grade 1 (Total Angle < 160°): Low risk of collapse; high success rate for core decompression.

 - Grade 2 (160° – 190°): Moderate risk.

 - Grade 3 (190° – 240°): High risk of collapse.

 - Grade 4 (> 240°): Very high risk; joint preservation is often contraindicated as collapse is imminent.

Answer: 2
