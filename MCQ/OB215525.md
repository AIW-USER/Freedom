## Question

A 42-year-old male sustains a tibial shaft fracture which is treated as seen in Figure A. Which of the following is true regarding the type of healing that occurs with this construct?

![[q215525-a-screen-shot-2021-04-27-at-7-33-34-pm.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Undifferentiated mesenchymal cells aggregate and differentiate into osteoblasts which deposit an organic matrix
2. Initial soft callus is replaced by woven bone
3. Heals in an environment with <2% mechanical strain
4. Heals with absolute stability
5. Uses cutting cones to bridge the fracture gap

## Correct option

**2. Initial soft callus is replaced by woven bone**

## Answer explanation

Bony healing after intramedullary nailing (IMN) takes place through endochondral ossification, a form of secondary bone healing that occurs with relative fracture stability.

Fracture healing is dictated by the amount of mechanical stability of the final bony construct. Intramedullary nailing is a form of relative stability which allows for bony apposition with mechanical strains between 2-10%. This creates an environment that leads to endochondral ossification where a cartilage scaffold (soft callus) is converted to a hard callus made of woven bone. The woven bone eventually undergoes remodeling and is replaced by lamellar bone. This is in contrast to absolute stability, which consists of a low mechanical strain environment (<2%) with direct bony contact, leading to intramembranous ossification and no cartilage-based callus intermediary.

*Elliott et al. detail their clinical theories on the "bone-healing unit," which represents a specific environment that leads to the optimal healing of bone.* **They note** that bone healing is based on multiple factors including mechanical forces, time, and biologic environment. **The authors conclude** that when nonunions occur, mechanical stability, not lack of biology, is to blame and should be addressed.

*Claes et al. review the effect of bone healing with regards to different methods of fixation.* *They review new forms of "flexible" fixation which heal by callus formation.* **They note** that knowledge of the different forms of bone healing is important for the orthopedic surgeon to optimize the construct's healing potential.

*Norris et al. detail the importance of understanding the mechanical and biologic environments related to fracture healing.* *They purport that this allows the surgeon to choose the most appropriate construct to encourage the best opportunity for fracture healing and patient recovery.*

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1-3,5: These answers are all representative of primary bone healing with absolute stability. An example of primary, intramembranous bony healing with absolute stability would be the use of a compression plate to stabilize a transverse radial shaft fracture.
