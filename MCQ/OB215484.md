## Question

A 13-year-old male presents to your office with a painful, palpable prominence over the anterior leg that has been present for over a year. He has tried rest and has completed a 6 week course of physical therapy, without much benefit. NSAIDs do help alleviate the pain but his pediatrician fears gastrointestinal side effects with prolonged use. The pain is currently limiting his participation in sporting activities. ## Options

1. Radical resection
2. Physical therapy
3. Chemotherapy
4. Magnetic resonance guided high-intensity focused ultrasound (MR-HIFU)
5. Marginal resection + radiation

## Correct option

**4. Magnetic resonance guided high-intensity focused ultrasound (MR-HIFU)**

## Answer explanation

Osteoid osteoma is the most likely diagnosis in this patient given his radiographic findings and responsiveness to NSAIDs and definitive management with magnetic resonance guided high-intensity focused ultrasound (MR-HIFU) is most appropriate in this case.

Osteoid osteoma is a benign bone tumor that occurs most commonly in young males and is usually found in the lower extremity (femur > tibia), but can also be seen in the pelvis and spine. Pain is thought to be mediated by prostaglandin E2, which is the reason NSAIDS usually improve symptoms. However, prolonged NSAID use can cause gastrointestinal (GI) upset and renal abnormalities. CT-guided radiofrequency ablation has traditionally been the gold standard of management. Occasionally, resection with open curettage is necessary. However, more recently MR-HIFU has become more popular given its safe, non-invasive nature and success in decreasing or altogether eliminating symptoms.

*Sharma et al. reviewed effectiveness and safety of MR-HIFU in treating 9 pediatric patients with osteoid osteomas.* **They noted** significant improvement in pain scores with complete resolution of pain by 4 weeks in all patients. **They concluded** that MR-HIFU was a reasonable and safe treatment for osteoid osteoma in pediatric patients who had failed medical therapy.

*Napoli et al. studied MR-HIFU use in a prospective trial of 40 children with osteoid osteoma.* **Their analysis showed** improvement in VAS pain from a median of 8/10 pre-procedure to 0/10 at all subsequent follow-up intervals. **They noted** that the patients were all able to resume their desired activities and MR-HIFU allowed for complete pain relief in all patients, with no treatment related complications. For these reasons, **they suggest** that MR-HIFU should be considered a part of the standard of care for pediatric patients with osteoid osteoma.

Figures A and B demonstrate the AP and lateral radiographs of a pediatric patient with open physes and a nidus representing osteoid osteoma in the proximal medial cortex. Figure C shows a coronal sequence that redemonstrates the osteoid osteoma nidus adjacent to the medial tibial cortex.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Radical resection is would not be indicated in initial management of osteoid osteoma and could cause unnecessary morbidity.
Answer 2: Physical therapy would not be appropriate given the fact the patient has failed conservative management already.
Answer 3: Chemotherapy is not used in the routine management of osteoid osteoma.
Answer 5: Marginal resection and radiation is not a treatment combination used to manage osteoid osteoma. In certain instances resection and curettage has been described, but this is not the standard of care or initial strategy.
