## Question

A 19-year-old college baseball pitcher presents to your office with complaints of decreased velocity and control. He has a history of vague posteromedial elbow pain over the past few weeks which has now become more persistent. Radiographs are obtained which do not show any osseous abnormalities. MRI arthrogram demonstrates a "capsular T sign." Which of the following is true about the patient's most likely injury?

## Options

1. MRI arthrogram lacks specificity in diagnosis
2. Injured structure should be evaluated with pulling on the patient's thumb with the forearm supinated and elbow flexed
3. Isometry is maintained in all components of injured structure throughout range of motion
4. Injured structure is primary valgus restraint of elbow from 0-20º of flexion
5. Injured structure likely to rupture during deceleration phase of throwing

## Correct option

**2. Injured structure should be evaluated with pulling on the patient's thumb with the forearm supinated and elbow flexed**

## Answer explanation

To evaluate for ulnar collateral ligament (UCL) tears on examination the milking maneuver can be performed, which involves creating a valgus stress through the elbow by pulling on the patient's thumb with the forearm supinated and flexed to around 90 degrees (Illustration A).

UCL tears are most common in throwing athletes who consistently place high degrees of valgus strain on the medial elbow. Most UCL injuries occur during the late cocking/early acceleration phase of throwing when the greatest degree of strain is seen through the ligament. The UCL is divided into anterior, posterior and transverse bundles with the anterior bundle further subdivided into anterior and posterior bands. The anterior band of the anterior bundle is the only portion of the ligament that remains essentially isometric. The UCL is the primary valgus restraint to valgus stress between 30-120º of motion. Provocative physical examination testing includes valgus stress testing, milking maneuver and the moving valgus stress test. With regards specifically to the milking maneuver, a positive test is interpreted with subjective apprehension, instability or pain at the MCL origin. MRI findings are helpful in confirming a UCL tear when suspected on physical exam. The "capsular T-sign" can be seen on MRI arthrogram in these patients, which is when contrast extravasates through the tear.

*Erickson and Romeo published a review of UCL injuries with regards to evaluation and treatment.* *They discuss different physical examination maneuvers for detecting UCL injury, including moving valgus stress and milking maneuvers.* Finally, they review treatment options, most commonly being UCL reconstruction (Tommy John).

*Farrow et al. looked at the UCL footprint in relation to the sublime tubercle.* **They noted** that the ulnar footprint of the UCL is quite long, at almost 3 cm. **They also pointed out** that there is a reliable ulnar insertion point at an osseous ridge just medial to the brachialis attachment on the proximal ulnar which was reproducibly noticed in all specimens studied.

Illustration A demonstrates the milking maneuver, performed with a valgus stress on the elbow with the forearm supinated and the elbow flexed around 90 degrees. Illustration B depicts the anterior, posterior, and transverse bundles of the UCL.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Specificity of MRI arthrogram is almost 100% in "ruling in" the diagnosis of UCL tear and one of the most common MRI findings in these cases is the capsular "T-sign," where contrast extravasates.
Answer 3: Only the anterior band of the anterior bundle is thought to be isometric; otherwise the strain through the UCL bundles changes at different degrees of flexion.
Answer 4: The UCL is the primary valgus elbow restraint from 30-120º. From 0-20º of flexion, the radiocapitellar articulation provides bony stability to resist valgus forces.
Answer 5: The UCL is injured during the late cocking/early acceleration phase of the throwing sequence.
