## Question

You are seeing a middle-aged female with predominantly medial compartment arthritis who has failed conservative measures to control her pain and improve her function. She states she is miserable and is willing to undergo surgery. You discuss treatment options with her including medial unicompartmental knee arthroplasty (UKA). Which of the following is true of UKA?

## Options

1. Mobile-bearing UKA preferred in ACL deficient patient
2. UKA preferred in patients age < 45
3. Better outcomes with slight overcorrection of mechanical axis into valgus by 2-3º
4. Lower risk of periprosthetic joint infection compared to total knee arthroplasty
5. Tibial stress fracture represents most common mode of failure

## Correct option

**4. Lower risk of periprosthetic joint infection compared to total knee arthroplasty**

## Answer explanation

Compared to total knee arthroplasty (TKA), unicompartmental knee arthroplasty (UKA) has been shown to have a lower rate of wound complication and periprosthetic joint infection (PJI).

UKA is a type of knee replacement used in patients who only have arthritis in a single compartment of their knee. It is thought to maintain some of the normal biomechanics of the knee joint by relying on the classic ligamentous stabilizers of the knee and thus decreasing wear patterns often seen in other modes of replacement such as TKA. Other benefits of UKA compared to TKA include lower rates of other medical complications such as myocardial infarction and pulmonary embolism. However, UKA is not without complication. Aseptic loosening represents the highest risk for early failure, with published rates >25%. Stress fractures are also possible, which almost always involve the tibia and present spontaneously after a pain-free interval.

*Niinimaki et al. compared survivorship of UKA with TKA by evaluating a Finnish joints registry.* **They noted** that mean survivorship of UKA was significantly lower than that of TKA, with 70% of UKA still in place at 15 years compared to 89% of TKA. This relationship remained after age and sex were adjusted for in this model, **suggesting** that patients should be informed of this risk before choosing UKA over TKA.

*Hansen et al. performed a similar study of United States registries, comparing UKA results to TKA.* **They noted** that complication rates for TKA patients were significantly higher, including rates of wound complication, PJI, myocardial infarction, pulmonary embolism, stiffness, re-admission, and death. However, similar to the prior study, **they recognized** that survivorship was lower at all time points in the UKA group, especially in younger patients.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Mobile-bearing UKA implants are contraindicated in patients with ACL deficiency because of the altered joint mechanics and increased risk for polyethylene wear and bearing dislocation. The use of fixed bearing UKA is controversial with regards to ACL deficient patients.
Answer 2: UKA has traditionally been preferred in older patients >60, who are lower demand, although these indications may be expanding some.
Answer 3: It is not recommended to overcorrect the mechanical axis during UKA.
Answer 5: Aseptic loosening, not tibial stress fracture, represents the most common mode of failure in UKA.
