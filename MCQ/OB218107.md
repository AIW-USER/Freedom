## Question

A 62-year-old male undergoes rotator cuff repair surgery. The resident asks about postoperative immobilization protocols. Which of the following is true regarding accelerated versus delayed motion postoperative protocols?

## Options

1. Early ROM protocol may improve the rate of tendon healing
2. Early ROM protocol has lower patient reported outcomes at 1 year
3. Early ROM shows no difference in terminal clinical ROM compared with delayed ROM
4. Delayed ROM protocol has great range of motion at 1 year
5. Delayed ROM protocol results in higher retear rate

## Correct option

**3. Early ROM shows no difference in terminal clinical ROM compared with delayed ROM**

## Answer explanation

Studies have demonstrated similar results in terms of range of motion, retear rate, and functional outcome scores among patients who undergo early versus delayed rehabilitation programs.

Rotator cuff tears are a very common source of shoulder pain and decreased motion that can occur due to both traumatic injuries in young patients as well as degenerative disease in the elderly patient. Diagnosis can be suspected clinically with provocative tests of the supraspinatous, infraspinatous, teres minor and subscapularis, but confirmation requires an MRI of the shoulder. Treatment can be nonoperative or operative depending on the chronicity of symptoms, severity of the tear, degree of muscle fatty atrophy, patient age and patient activity demands. Postoperative rehabilitation protocols often involve either early (<6 weeks) or delayed (6 weeks) range of motion. Studies have shown similar results in terms of range of motion, retear rate, and functional outcome scores among patients who undergo early versus delayed rehabilitation programs.

*Chan et al. conducted a meta-analysis of randomized trials to compare delayed vs early motion therapy on function after arthroscopic rotator cuff repair.* **They reported** that their meta-analysis did not identify any significant differences in functional outcomes and relative risks of recurrent tears between delayed and early motion in patients undergoing arthroscopic rotator cuff repairs. **They concluded** that a statistically significant difference in forward elevation range of motion was identified; however, this difference is likely clinically unimportant.

*Cuff et al. reviewed patient outcomes and rotator cuff healing after arthroscopic rotator cuff repair using a postoperative physical therapy protocol with early passive motion compared with a delayed protocol that limited early passive motion.* **They reported** that patients in the early group and delayed group both demonstrated very similar outcomes and range of motion at 1 year. **They concluded** that there was a slightly higher rotator cuff healing rate in the delayed passive range of motion group compared with the early passive range of motion group (91% vs 85%).

*Parsons et al. retrospectively reviewed patients with full-thickness rotator cuff tears who underwent a standardized, conservative protocol of full-time sling immobilization without formal therapy for 6 weeks after arthroscopic repair.* **They reported** that at 6 to 8 weeks of follow-up, they categorized patients as "stiff" if they demonstrated forward elevation of less than 100° and external rotation of less than 30° passively; all others were designated "non-stiff." They noted that early restriction of motion did not lead to long-term stiffness after arthroscopic rotator cuff repair, even in patients who were clinically stiff in the early postoperative period. **They reported** that sling immobilization for 6 weeks after arthroscopic rotator cuff repair does not result in increased long-term stiffness and may improve the rate of tendon healing.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Delayed ROM protocol may improve the rate of tendon healing.
Answers 2&4: Patients in the early group and delayed group demonstrate very similar outcomes and range of motion at 1 year.
Answer 5: There is similar results in retear rate among patients who undergo early versus delayed rehabilitation programs.
