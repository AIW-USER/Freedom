## Question

A 64-year-old man with diabetes presents with chronic bilateral plantar foot pain. His most recent hemoglobin A1c was 9.5% and has decreased sensation with monofilament testing up to his ankles. You recommend a plastazote shoe insert to prevent ulceration. What property of this material makes it ideal for ulcer prevention?

## Options

1. Increased stiffness
2. Lack of polyethylene cross-linking
3. Increased peak pressures
4. Surface area distribution
5. Ability to be heated and molded without losing material integrity

## Correct option

**4. Surface area distribution**

## Answer explanation

Plastazote is a common material used in diabetic foot inserts. It is used because of the ability to distribute forces over a wide surface area, decreasing peak pressures.

Diabetic foot ulcers are common, occurring in 12% of diabetics and responsible for 85% of lower extremity amputations. Diabetic neuropathy diminishes the protective sensation of the foot and leads to ulceration formation. Risk factors include Hb A1c >8, inability to offload the affected area, poor circulation, infection, and malnutrition. Shoe modification is indicated in Brodsky 0 ulcers, when signs of potential ulcers are present. The goal of shoe modification is to redistribute the peak pressures throughout the foot.

*Brodsky et al* **state** that the most common method of customizing inserts is by heating and then molding the materials. They tested common materials before and after heating including plastazote, medium plastazote, puff, and nickelplast. **They found** that heating these materials changed the stress-strain curve so that the materials became stiffer and less effective in distributing forces.

*Brodsky et al utilized a bony prominence model to determine which material composition in diabetic insoles distribute peak loading pressures before and after 100,000 testing cycles.* *They tested 5 different combinations of materials.* **They found** that medium plastazote + nickelplast and nickelplast + poron combinations showed no difference in peak pressure measurements before and after 100,000 cycles. The other tested materials increased peak pressures after loading.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Increased stiffness would worsen the ability to distribute loads.
Answer 2: Plastazote contains cross-linked polyethylene.
Answer 3: The goal of inserts is to decrease the peak pressures in the foot.
Answer 5: It has been shown that heating and molding reduce the mechanical properties of plastazote.
