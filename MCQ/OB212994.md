## Options

1. Perform a moberg osteotomy
2. Perform a superior capsulotomy
3. Add a medial-to-lateral compression screw
4. Revise to a plate with less dorsiflexion
5. Irrigate and close

## Correct option

**5. Irrigate and close**

## Answer explanation

A 5mm clearance of the tip of the hallux from the footplate is acceptable and ideal. Therefore no further intra-operative adjustment is required, and the surgical site may be irrigated and closed.

The ideal dorsiflexion is between 10 and 15 degrees. Excessive dorsiflexion is not well tolerated because of dorsal irritation of the interphalangeal joint with shoewear. The most effective method of intraoperative assessment of the dorsiflexion of the hallux is the use of a flat sterile instrument tray with the ankle held in neutral dorsiflexion to simulate weight-bearing. The great toe should be in 10 to 15 degrees of dorsiflexion relative to the tray. This can be visually represented by the tip of the toe clearing the box top by 4 to 8 mm, then the position of the hallux is optimal to allow normal toe-off with limited risk of hallux overload.

*Womack et al. author a review article on 1st MTP arthrodesis.* **He notes** that the ideal dorsiflexion is between 10 and 15 degrees. The most reliable way to determine this is using a flat surface to simulate weight-bearing and use the tip of the phalanx relative to the floor as a reference point.

*Kelikian authored a technique review regarding 1st MTP fusion.* **He notes** that one of the pitfalls in arthrodesis positioning is reliance on the first metatarsal declination angle for the reference point of the hallux. Because of variations in the foot, such as metatarsus elevatus, cavus, or pes planus, **he argues** that the floor or flat weight-bearing surface is the best reference point on which to base the alignment of the hallux in the sagittal plane.

Figure A demonstrates end-stage arthritis of the 1st MTP joint. Figure B is an intraoperative photo showing the sterile footplate used to assess the sagittal alignment of the 1st MTP fusion.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: A Moberg osteotomy may be used after a dorsal cheilectomy to improve the amount of the toe dorsiflexion at the MTP joint, here we are performing an arthrodesis.
Answer 2: A superior capsulotomy is not routinely performed and would not be beneficial given the acceptable degree of toe tip clearance.
Answer 3: A medial to lateral compression screw is often utilized in a 1st MTP fusion, but it not dictated by the amount of dorsiflexion as depicted in the clinical photograph.
Answer 4: The clinical photograph demonstrates an acceptable degree of dorsiflexion. Therefore while plates do exist with varying degrees of built-in dorsiflexion, revision is unnecessary and may compromise fixation.
