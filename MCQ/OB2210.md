## Options

1. Figure A
2. Figure B
3. Figure C
4. Figure D
5. Figure E

## Correct option

**3. Figure C**

## Answer explanation

A skeletal survey is used to confirm the diagnosis of Multiple Epiphyseal Dysplasia (MED) which is demonstrated in Figure C by symmetric and bilateral (simultaneous) flattening of the proximal femoral epiphyses.

MED represents a spectrum of disorders that are characterized by irregular and delayed ossification of multiple epiphyses. These patients typically present with hip pathology and a waddling gait. LCP, however, can have a very similar presentation and radiographic appearance. The key difference is the stages of the proximal femoral disease. In patients with MED, the hips appear in the same stage, whereas, patients with bilateral LCP disease present with hips in different stages of the disease.

*Crossan et al. present the radiographic comparisons of patients with bilateral LCP to those with four other skeletal dysplasias.* **The authors found** distinguishing features of each disease that can be identified on plain radiographs. They affirm the importance of differentiating transient disorders such as LCP and more progressive diseases as early as possible.

*Anthony et al. performed a review of MED.* *They discuss the importance of differentiating MED from LCP because there is no remodeling potential in patients with MED, and this changes treatment.* **They conclude** that patients with MED present with symmetric proximal femoral disease and that a skeletal survey is mandatory in these patients to confirm the diagnosis.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Figure A is the AP pelvis radiograph of a patient with cerebral palsy who has windswept hips, that is, abduction of one hip with adduction of the other. Cerebral palsy is an upper motor neuron condition that is not diagnosed via skeletal survey.
Answer 2: Figure B is the AP pelvis radiograph of a patient with a left-sided Slipped Capital Femoral Epiphysis (SCFE). SCFE is diagnosed from AP pelvis and frog-leg lateral radiographs and is evident by the slippage of the metaphysis relative to the epiphysis
Answer 4: Figure D is the AP pelvis radiograph of a patient with a left-sided hamstring bony avulsion. Avulsion of the hamstring apophysis is typically an isolated injury that occurs following forceful contraction.
Answer 5: Figure E is the AP pelvis radiograph which demonstrates unilateral Legg-Calve-Perthes with no involvement of the right hip, therefore, a skeletal survey is not needed to differentiate it from MED.
