## Question

A latissimus dorsi tendon transfer is a well-established procedure for treatment of massive irreparable posterosuperior rotator cuff tears. Which of the following factors has not been shown to result in worse clinical outcomes after a transfer?

## Options

1. Nonsynergistic action of the transferred muscle
2. Fatty atrophy of the supraspinatus and infraspinatus
3. Deficiency of the subscapularis
4. Absence of the coracoacromial ligament
5. Deltoid weakness

## Correct option

**4. Absence of the coracoacromial ligament**

## Answer explanation

A latissimus dorsi tendon transfer can be utilized in patients with a massive, irreperable rotator cuff tear involving the supraspinatus and infraspinatus. It has been reported to relieve pain and improve function in a carefully selected patient population.

Absence of the CA ligament may allow anterosuperior escape in RC deficient shoulders but has not been shown to lead to worse outcomes after a tendon transfer. The paper by Warner, et. al demonstrated that poor tendon quality, stage 3/4 muscle fatty degeneration, and detachment of the deltoid insertion each had a statistically significant effect on the Constant score noting that salvage reconstruction of a previous cuff repair had more limited gains as compared to primary. The reference by Ianotti, et. al showed that synchronous in-phase contraction of the transferred latissimus dorsi is associated with a better clinical result while improved preoperative shoulder function and general strength also positively influence the clinical result.

Incorrect answers: Those patients with deficiency of the deltoid or subscapularis, nonsynergistic muscle action after transfer, or fatty infiltration of the posterosuperior cuff have worse clinical outcomes.
