## Question

A 51-year-old female is involved in a MVC. In the trauma bay she is found to have an acetabular fracture. During surgery the following morning, fracture reduction is extremely difficult. Postoperative CT scan shows continued displacement of 4mm at the articular surface. Which of the following fracture patterns is most closely linked to the need for conversion to total hip arthroplasty in patients older than 50 years old?

## Options

1. Anterior column
2. Posterior column
3. Anterior wall
4. Posterior wall
5. Associated both column

## Correct option

**4. Posterior wall**

## Answer explanation

Posterior wall fractures, especially in patients >50 years old are associated with a high rate of conversion to total hip arthroplasty if a non-anatomic reduction in achieved.

Posterior wall fractures are the most common type of acetabular fractures. Only 30% of posterior-wall acetabular fractures involve a single large fragment, with the majority being multifragmentary or having areas of impaction. Non-anatomic reduction and age >50-years-old have both been associated with a high incidence of conversion to THA.

Kreder review the determinants of functional outcome after simple and complex acetabular fractures involving the posterior wall. They report that fracture pattern, marginal impaction and residual displacement of > 2 mm were associated with the development of arthritis, which related to poor function and the need for hip replacement. They conclude that it may be appropriate to consider immediate total hip replacement for patients aged > 50 years with marginal impaction and comminution of the wall, since 7 of 13 (54%) of these required early hip replacement.

*Firoozabadi et al review the risk factors associated with conversion to THA following acetabular fractures.* **They report** that among cases with less than 1 mm of step-off on postoperative computed tomography scan, there were no THA conversions, 10% conversion for 1-4 mm, and 54% if 4 mm or more of malreduction. **They conclude** that acetabular fractures with posterior wall involvement are associated with a significantly higher rate of conversion to THA if reduction is not near-anatomic.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1, 2, 3, & 5: Posterior wall fractures in patients older than 50 and those with non-anatomic reduction are much more likely to undergo conversion to THA.
