Chapter: Arthroplasty MCQs — Complications Of TKR

## Question

Patellar clunk syndrome results in a posterior stabilized TKR due to synovial interposition at

## Options

1. Superior pole of patella that impinges on femoral component during flexion
2. Superior pole of patella that impinges on femoral component during extension
3. Inferior pole of patella that impinges on femoral component during flexion
4. Inferior pole of patella that impinges on femoral component during extension

## Correct option

**2. Superior pole of patella that impinges on femoral component during extension**

## Answer explanation

Ans: B

Explanation:

In 1989, Hozack et al. first coined the term "Patellar Clunk Syndrome" (PCS) after reporting on three cases of patellofemoral pain, with sensation of patellar "catch" or "clunk" following Posterior Stabilized (PS) TKR in three patients.

The condition is attributed to a prominent fibrous nodule formation at the junction of the proximal patellar pole and the articular aspect of the quadriceps tendon, essentially secondary to the impingement of the undersurface of the tendon into the sharp anterosuperior edge of the intercondylar notch of the femoral component. The nodule gets entrapped into the intercondylar box of the femoral component during flexion, and suddenly dislodged when the knee moves in extension to 30-45 degrees of full extension, resulting in a painful audible clunk.

This patellofemoral dysfunction that consists of painful palpable and audible clunk, catch, grinding, or jumping of the patella that happens as the knee moves from flexion to extension may occur in up to 18% (12-21 %) of patients with posterior stabilized total knee prosthesis (a mean prevalence of around 3.5 % has been reported).

PCS usually occurs between 3-12 months - occasionally up to 3 years after PS-TKR with patellar resurfacing and little less commonly, after patellar non-resurfacing procedure.

Arthroscopic appearance of nodule

PCS must not be confused with other entities that can produce similar symptoms:-

- Patellofemoral crepitus: This is a separate entity, that shares a common pathophysiology. The symptoms range from a painless subtle crepitation to a painful, palpable and audible clunk but in a case of patellofemoral crepitus, the fibrosynovial hyperplasia does not develop into a discrete nodule and no audible clunk is present. Conversely, these patients complain of anterior knee pain and a continued grinding sensation with loading of the knee in a 30o-60o range. Symptoms typically develop within 2 months to 2 years following TKR.

- Patellar crunch syndrome: This occurs when scar tissue accumulates around the patellar component, causing a crunching sensation when bringing the knee from extension to flexion.

- A more recently described entity is “Intraarticular fibrous band syndrome” that may also potentially cause a similar painful audible clunk. Here mostly a transverse fibrous band prevents the patella from seating well in the sulcus of the femoral component.

Takahashi et al. (2002) suggested a classification for PCS dividing it into three types going by the soft-tissue impingement under the patella:

(I) Patellar clunk syndrome, the isolated fibrous nodule located in the suprapatellar lesion without the other fibrous tissues causing the impingement

(II) Impinging hypertrophic synovitis, generalized hypertrophic synovitis with no fibrous nodule; and

(III) Combination of types I and II, the suprapatellar fibrous nodule with generalized hypertrophic synovitis
