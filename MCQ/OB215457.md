## Question

An elderly Irish fisherman from rural western Cork cut his hand while bringing in his nets 20 days ago. He developed pain and swelling on the dorsal aspect of his right hand. At the time of operative debridement and tenosynovectomy, a culture was obtained. The organism grew best on Löwenstein-Jensen medium at 30°C after two weeks of incubation. Which of the following pathogens is most likely causing the seafarer's symptoms?

## Options

1. Mycobacterium marinum
2. Mycobacterium terrae
3. Sporothrix schenckii
4. Bartonella henselae
5. Eikenella corrodens

## Correct option

**1. Mycobacterium marinum**

## Answer explanation

Mycobacterium marinum is most commonly found in immunocompromised marine workers, often in the hand and wrist, and grows best on Löwenstein-Jensen medium at 30°C.

Mycobacterium marinum represents a nontuberculous mycobaterial infection, along with M. kansasii, M. terrae, and M. avium intracellulare. Patients infected with Mycobacterium marinum may develop papules, nodules, and ulcers in the hand or wrist. Late presentation may include findings of tenosynovitis, septic arthritis, or osteomyleitis. The mortality rate for Mycobacterium marinum is 32% if left untreated. If the infection is caught early, it may be treated with oral antibiotics including trimethoprim-sulfamethoxasole or ethambutol. Debridement is needed in cases that present late. Other aquatic pathogens include Vibrio species and Aeromonas hydrophila.

Al-Qattan and Helmi review chronic hand infections including both tuberculosis of the hand as well as atypical mycobacterial infections. While M. marinum infections are often treated first by dermatologists, hand surgeons should consider this pathogen in maritime workers. Most patients infected will have some degree of immunosuppression and often present in a delayed fashion after a missed diagnosis. A prior history of a steroid injection may be associated with poorer outcomes.

*Probst et al. presented a single case of an immunocompromised 32-year-old male with a 6 month history of pain and swelling to the dorsum of his wrist.* After an exhaustive work up, the diagnosis was finally made after a tenosynovectomy during which tuberculosis-induced extensor tenosynovitis was noted. The diagnosis was confirmed with specific PCR sequencing. **The authors note** that tuberculous can be overlooked but should be included in the differential diagnosis in the immunocompromised patient.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Mycobacterium terrae can cause significant soft tissue injury, but it is often found in the soil and grown on agar at 37°C.
Answer 3: Sporothrix schenckii is a fungus that precipitates infection often after spores are introduced by way of a thorn.
Answer 4: Bartonella henselae, or cat scratch disease, causes tender lymphadenopathy (oftentimes just proximal to the medial epicondyle) and may cause bacterial endocarditis.
Answer 5: Eikenella corrodens is a gram negative anaerobic bacillus that is often associated with human bites.
