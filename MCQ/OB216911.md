## Options

1. Patient age
2. Dominant arm
3. 9 mm calcar attached to articular surface

5. Number of fracture parts per Neer classification

## Correct option

**4. Head-split pattern**

## Answer explanation

The patient has sustained a comminuted right proximal humerus fracture with a head-split morphology. They would best be treated with hemiarthroplasty (HA).

Proximal humerus fractures are a common injury to the shoulder, and treatment can be highly variable. Typically in younger patients, open reduction and internal fixation (ORIF) is preferred, even in isolated head-split patterns. Arthroplasty is reserved for patients with severe comminution, older patients with head-split patterns, pre-existing arthritis, and those with low physical demand. HA is used most commonly with middle-aged patients (40 - 65 years old) with comminuted fractures and head-split patterns due to the high likelihood of fixation failure. Reverse shoulder arthroplasty is used with low-demand individuals and poorly reconstructable tuberosities.

*Gravaskar and Tummala studied the difference in outcomes of young (<55 years of age) patients with simple head-split and complex head-split fracture patterns when treated with ORIF.* **They found** no incidence of nonunion or avascular necrosis (AVN) in the simple head-split cohort when treated with ORIF, but 6 out of 11 patients with complex head-split patterns did develop AVN or nonunion. **They concluded** that ORIF in complex head-split patterns results in unacceptably high rates of failure secondary to either AVN or nonunion.

*Cadet and Ahmad evaluated the indications for HA in proximal humeral fractures.* **They concluded** that head-split fractures, fracture-dislocations, and highly comminuted fracture patterns with over 20 degrees of varus would benefit from HA compared to ORIF. **They caution** that the main technical challenges, including obtaining proper humeral head height, retroversion, and optimal positioning and fixation of the tuberosities, have a substantial effect on patient outcomes. The current recommendations for this are tuberosity height 8 mm below superior portion of HA, superior portion of HA is 5.6 cm superior to pectoralis major tendon, and avoiding humeral retroversion > 40 degrees.

*Griewe et al completed a retrospective review on patients with comminuted 3 and 4-part proximal humerus fractures with and without head-split patterns treated with HA over a 52-month period.* The head-split group had superior forward elevation ability and equivalent American Shoulder and Elbow Surgeons and Simple Shoulder Test scores. **They concluded** that HA is a reliable treatment modality for head-split fracture patterns when compared to traditional 3 and 4-part fracture patterns.

Figure A is an AP left shoulder radiograph demonstrating a comminuted proximal humerus fracture. Figure B is an axial CT scan of the left shoulder showing a comminuted proximal humerus fracture with a head-split pattern.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The patient's age is acceptable for either ORIF or HA, however, studies looking at complex head split fracture patterns caution that ORIF may be associated with increased failure rates secondary to nonunion and AVN.
Answer 2: Arm dominance does not play a role in deciding the type of operation performed for this patient.
Answer 3: Calcar length of >8 mm is favorable against the development of humeral head AVN and is a useful measurement for deciding on ORIF versus arthroplasty in non-head-split fracture patterns.
Answer 5: The number of fracture parts can play a role in deciding on ORIF vs arthroplasty, but the presence of a head split raises the likelihood of AVN and subsequent ORIF failure to an unacceptable degree.
