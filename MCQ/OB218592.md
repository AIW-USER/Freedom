## Question

A 13-year-old boy with a history of pituitary adenoma presents with several days of new onset hip pain. His BMI is in the 80th percentile. He is unable to bear weight on the leg. Radiographs of bilateral hips demonstrate a slipped capital femoral epiphysis (SCFE) of the right hip with a posterior slip angle of 10°. Fixation of the unstable SCFE is planned. Which of the following factors would support prophylactic fixation of the contralateral asymptomatic hip?

## Options

1. Age
2. BMI
3. Posterior slip angle
4. Unstable nature of symptomatic slip
5. Endocrine history

## Correct option

**5. Endocrine history**

## Answer explanation

Prophylactic fixation of his contralateral hip should be considered given his history of a pituitary adenoma, which often leads to hypopituitarism.

Slipped capital femoral epiphysis (SCFE) is a common condition in adolescent children that leads to slippage of the femoral metaphysis superior and anterior relative to the epiphysis. One frequent concern is the risk for contralateral SCFE, which is not uncommon. Risk factors for contralateral SCFE include young age (<10), open triradiate cartilage, obesity (BMI > 95th percentile), endocrine disorders (hypothyroidism, renal osteodystrophy, pituitary adenoma, etc), and increased posterior slip angle on radiographs. Prophylactic contralateral hip fixation is controversial overall, but is still mostly indicated in the aforementioned group of patients.

*Swarup et al. reviewed the rates of contralateral SCFE in the U.S. using the PHIS database.* *They looked at 9,755 patients who underwent pinning for unilateral SCFE, and noted an 11% (1,077) rate of eventual contralateral SCFE development.* **They noted** that the main risk factor for development of contralateral SCFE in their study was younger age, with each increasing year of age decreasing the odds of contralateral slip by 20%.

*St. George et al. discussed implementation of a prophylactic fixation protocol designed around evaluation of the posterior slope angle on radiographs.* *They used a posterior slope angle of 14.5° as a cutoff for recommending prophylactic contralateral fixation.* **They found** that this protocol prevented 77% of possible contralateral slips with a number needed to treat of only 2.4 and no associated complications.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Recommendations vary but age <10 or up to 13 years are are typically the indications for prophylactic contralateral fixation. Open triradiate cartilage is also on indication for prophylactic fixation.
Answer 2: BMI in the 95th percentile or higher is an indication for prophylactic fixation.
Answer 3: Posterior slip angle of the SCFE, as measured on radiographs, >14.5° is thought to be a risk factor for contralateral SCFE development and represents an indication for prophylactic contralateral fixation.
Answer 4: The fact that his presenting SCFE is unstable does not, in isolation, represent an indication for prophylactic fixation of the contralateral hip.
