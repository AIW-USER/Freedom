## Question

A 25-year-old marathon runner in Florida presents to the clinic for worsening foot odor and shallow crater-like lesions at the weight-bearing surface of his foot. The skin over the sole of his foot is thickened and slightly painful when he runs. A Wood's lamp exam demonstrates red fluorescence. What is the most likely diagnosis?

## Options

1. Erythrasma
2. Keratosis pilaris
3. Pitted keratolysis
4. Palmoplantar psoriasis
5. Tinea pedis

## Correct option

**3. Pitted keratolysis**

## Answer explanation

Pitted keratolysis presents with shallow craters at the weight-bearing surfaces of the foot with a red fluorescence on Wood's lamp examination.

Pitted keratolysis is a bacterial skin infection that primarily affects the soles of the feet and, less commonly, the palms. It is characterized by small, crater-like pits in the thickened skin, often accompanied by a foul odor and excessive sweating. The condition is caused by bacteria such as Corynebacterium species, which thrive in moist environments. Diagnosis is typically based on the appearance of the lesions and patient history, though a Wood's lamp examination demonstrating red fluorescence can confirm the presence of Corynebacterium. Treatment involves keeping the feet dry, using topical antibiotics (e.g., clindamycin, erythromycin, or mupirocin), and applying antiperspirants to reduce moisture. In severe cases, oral antibiotics may be prescribed. Proper foot hygiene, breathable footwear, and moisture-wicking socks help prevent recurrence.

The study by Vlahovic, Dunn, and Kemp evaluated the efficacy of a clindamycin 1%-benzoyl peroxide 5% topical gel as a novel treatment for pitted keratolysis. Findings showed significant improvement in lesions, odor reduction, and overall symptom relief after consistent application, suggesting that the combination therapy effectively eradicates bacterial overgrowth while reducing moisture. The authors concluded that this topical gel presents a promising, convenient, and well-tolerated alternative to traditional antibiotic treatments for pitted keratolysis.

*The study by van der Snoek et al. investigated the treatment approaches and perceptions of physicians managing pitted keratolysis in Dutch army personnel.* Findings revealed that while topical antibiotics, such as clindamycin and erythromycin, were commonly prescribed, there was variability in treatment choices and perceived effectiveness. **The authors concluded** that standardized treatment guidelines and increased physician awareness could improve management outcomes for military personnel affected by pitted keratolysis.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: Erythrasma is also caused by Corynebacterium. However, it is characterized by well-defined reddish-brown patches instead of small pits.
Answer 2: Keratosis pilaris is caused by keratin buildup in hair follicles. It does not present with malodor because it is not a bacterial infection.
Answer 4: Palmoplantar psoriasis is an autoimmune disease characterized by thickened, scaly plaques on the palms and soles. It does not fluoresce under Wood's lamp.
Answer 5: Tinea pedis is a fungal infection that presents with scaling and itching but not crater-like pits.
