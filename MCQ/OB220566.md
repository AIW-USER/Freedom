## Question

As the program director for an orthopaedic surgery residency program, you have read the new guidelines from the National Council on Radiation Protection and Measurements (NCRP) regarding eye protection and prevention of cataracts and have decided to purchase a pair of the radiation glasses shown in Figure A for each of your residents. Which of the following represents the highest annual cumulative dose threshold of radiation to the eyes recommended before a significant increase in risk for cataract formation occurs?

![[q220566-a-screenshot-2025-06-04-at-11-40-47-am.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. 5 milligray (mGy)
2. 15 millisievert (mSv)
3. 50 mGy
4. 100 mSv
5. 150 mGy

## Correct option

**3. 50 mGy**

## Answer explanation

The eyes are exquisitely sensitive to radiation exposure, with cumulative annual exposures to ionizing radiation > 50 mGy significantly increasing the risk for cataract formation (Answer 3).

Radiation-induced damage to the lens of the eye can include the loss of clarity resulting in opacification or clouding several years after exposure, with the impact being highly dependent on the type of radiation, how the exposure of the lens was delivered, the genetic susceptibilities of the individual exposed, and the location of the opacity relative to the visual axis of the individual.

*Dauer et al. provide an overview of the recommendations in NCRP Commentary No. 26 regarding guidance on radiation dose limits for the lens of the eye.* **The authors concluded** that there is a preponderance of epidemiological evidence suggesting that lens damage could occur at lower doses than previously considered, and the NCRP has determined that it is prudent to reduce the recommended annual lens of the eye occupational dose limit from an equivalent dose of 150 mSv to an absorbed dose of 50 mGy.

*Hamada et al. published an update on the effects of ionizing radiation exposure on the eye.* **The authors note** that prior thresholds for progression were assumed based on similar risks of cataracts and cataract surgery in Japanese atomic bomb survivors, with the absence of a dose rate effect derived from the observed similar thresholds for protracted exposures in Chernobyl cleanup workers and in atomic bomb survivors. However, since 2011, there has been an increasing body of epidemiological evidence relating to cataracts and other ocular diseases (i.e., glaucoma and macular degeneration), particularly at low doses and low dose rates, **suggesting** new dose limits for the lens to prevent the occurrence of vision-impairing cataracts (VICs).

*Keenen et al. review the risks and methods for decreasing exposure from intraoperative fluoroscopy radiation using an OEC 9900 Elite C-arm.* Using adult-sized mannequins wearing standard lead protective aprons placed at seven positions around large and small BMI cadavers, the authors recorded in real time at thyroid level with Bluetooth-enabled dosimeters for a variety of fluoroscope settings and imaging views after shooting a total of 320 images, resulting in 2,240 dosimeter readings from the seven mannequins. **They found** a strong correlation between the CAK and the recorded scattered radiation doses, concluding that the highest radiation doses were recorded across all settings for the mannequin positioned immediately adjacent to the C-arm x-ray tube, with the larger BMI cadaver generating greater scattered radiation than the smaller BMI cadaver for all views and settings.

Figure A is a representative photograph of a pair of radiation goggles that can be used to help prevent ionizing radiation exposure to the lens of the eye and reduce the risk of cataract formation for orthopaedic surgeons later in life.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1, 2, 4, and 5: The NCRP has determined that the recommended annual lens of the eye occupational dose limit from ionizing radiation should be set at a cumulative dose of 50 mGy. Of note, for radiation in diagnostic medicine, the equivalent dose (mSv) is numerically equal to the absorbed dose (mGy). Therefore, 1 mSv equals 1 mGy.
