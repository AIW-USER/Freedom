## Question

## Options

1. Mirel score 5; Intramedullary nailing
2. Mirel score 9; Radiation
3. Mirel score 4; Observation
4. Mirel score 10; Radiation
5. Mirel score 12: Intramedullary nailing

## Correct option

**5. Mirel score 12: Intramedullary nailing**

## Answer explanation

This patient presents with a peritrochanteric, osteolytic lesion, with functionally limiting pain, and greater than 2/3 cortical thickness involvement. This is consistent with a Mirel's score of 12, which indicates the patient is at high risk for pathologic fracture and should undergo prophylactic intramedullary nailing.

Metastatic adenocarcinoma is the most common cause of a destructive bone lesion in patients over 40 years, followed by multiple myeloma and lymphoma of bone. Orthopedic surgeons must remain vigilant and rule out a primary malignancy prior to surgical treatment, typically done with biopsy. When metastatic adenocarcinoma is confirmed, prophylactic fixation with a cephalomedullary interlocking nail is indicated to protect the entire bone and allow for immediate weight-bearing. Mirel's rating system may be used for predicting pathologic fracture probability, and deciding if a patient may benefit from osseous stabilization for metastatic disease.

*Arvinius et al. evaluated differences in prophylactic and therapeutic intramedullary nailing in femoral metastasic implants.* **They reported** a significantly reduced rate of postoperative death, transfusion, and length of stay when intramedullary nailing was undertaken prophylactically versus therapeutically. **They concluded** that the prophylactic treatment of metastatic lesions prevents fractures and improves the quality of life.

*Ormsby et al. reviewed prophylactic femoral intramedullary nailing for metastatic cancer.* **They reported** that due to the significant forces loads (up to four times the body weight) on long bones during weight-bearing, there is a high risk of fracture through areas of metastasis. **They concluded** that these loads result in a poor union rate for these fractures, resulting in significant morbidity, and thus recommended prophylactic augmentation of the diseased femurs using intramedullary nails.

Figure A demonstrates an AP of the patient's left hip with a peritrochanteric, osteolytic lesion comprising more than 2/3rd of the cortical diameter of the bone. Figure B is a histopathology slide of cells arranged in glandular appearing tubes, consistent with papillary thyroid carcinoma. Illustration A depicts Mirel's rating score for bony metastases.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1-4 do not correctly calculate the Mirel score of 12 for this patient. He would receive three points each for location (peritrochanteric), pain (functionally limiting), characteristic (lytic), and size (>2/3 cortical thickness). Patients with scores greater than 9 should undergo prophylactic stabilization procedures.
