## Question

Which biochemical profile is consistent with renal tubular rickets

## Options

1. Low urinary phosphate, low urinary calcium, low 1,25 (OH)2 vitamin D, high serum phosphate, low PTH
2. High urinary phosphate, low urinary calcium, normal 1,25 (OH)2 vitamin D, low serum phosphate and normal PTH
3. High urinary phosphate, low urinary calcium, normal 1,25 (OH)2 vitamin D, high serum phosphate, high PTH
4. Low urinary phosphate, low urinary calcium, normal 1,25 (OH)2 vitamin D, low serum phosphate and normal PTH

## Correct option

**2. High urinary phosphate, low urinary calcium, normal 1,25 (OH)2 vitamin D, low serum phosphate and normal PTH**

## Answer explanation

Explanation: Biochemical profiles of metabolic bone diseases:

In Renal tubular rickets, the basic abnormality is renal tubules' inability to retain phosphate. Large amounts of phosphorus are excreted in the urine, leading to hypophosphatemia; the calcium levels are however normal. Defective mineralization results as both calcium and phosphorus are needed for mineralization. Since calcium levels are normal, PTH is not stimulated and its blood levels remain unchanged, so would be the case with vitamin D metabolites as the metabolic pathway is not affected. ALK phosphatase is elevated as in all above forms. The diagnosis is established by documenting a low urinary pH and decreased urinary calcium and increased levels of urinary phosphates. Rachitic features in this type appear early in infancy and children lag behind in growth, have severely deformed bones, but no myopathy and no hypocalcemia.

The above scenario may be seen in two cases:

- Patient having renal tubular defects like in Fanconi anemia and renal tubular acidosis where there is a problem with the kidney to reabsorb phosphorus. In acidosis, the body has to excrete fixed base, ie,. bicarbonate and calcium phosphate are excreted along.
- As a familial form as in X-linked hypophosphatemic rickets. This tends to be a genetic disorder with dominant inheritance characterized by mutations in the phosphate-regulating gene (PHEX gene-having homology to endopeptidases) present on chromosome X. This leads to excessive urinary excretion of phosphate by restricting the ability of proximal renal tubular brush border to reabsorb phosphorus and calcium.

In Renal rickets (like in cases of Renal tubular acidosis), due to defect in renal tubules there is defective phosphate reabsorption by kidney tubules. This leads to increased phosphate excretion (and some calcium goes along bound to phosphate, free urine calcium is thus low). This changes the Ca/P ratio in serum required for mineralization leading to defective mineralization in Physis and changes simulating rickets.

Vit D is normal (as this form is unrelated to Vit D) and since calcium is not major culprit PTH remains nearly normal/ mild high.

Answer: 2
