## Question

Doxorubicin, a common chemotherapeutic agent used in the treatment of osteosarcoma, has which of the following major dose-limiting toxicities?

## Options

1. Cardiomyopathy
2. Pulmonary fibrosis
3. Myelosuppression
4. Encephalopathy
5. Urotoxicity

## Correct option

**1. Cardiomyopathy**

## Answer explanation

Doxorubicin is a key chemotherapeutic agent used in the treatment of osteosarcoma. While there are many side effects associated with doxorubicin and chemotherapy in general, the major dose-limiting toxicity of doxorubicin is cardiomyopathy. Please note that doxorubicin is also commonly referred to by its brand name of Adriamycin.

*Chatterjee et al review the issues surrounding doxorubicin cardiomyopathy.* Specifically, when congestive heart failure develops secondary to doxorubicin therapy, the overall mortality reaches 50%. Currently, there is no effective treatment for doxorubin induced cardiomyopathy and so the focus of treatment has been disease prevention by limitation of doxorubicin treatment and dosage.

As treatment for childhood osteosarcoma is not itself benign, Nicholson and Mulvihill provide an excellent review of the potentially life-threatening consequences of osteosarcoma therapy. These late effects of therapy include an increased risk of secondary cancers and dose related toxicity to chemotherapeutics like doxorubicin. As treatment for these processes centers on prevention, new chemotherapy agents or improved dosing regimens are a key area of active research.

Ifosfamide is known to have major neurological toxicity like encephalopathy.
Cyclophosphamide is associated with myelosuppression and urotoxicity.
Bleomycin is associated with pulmonary fibrosis.
