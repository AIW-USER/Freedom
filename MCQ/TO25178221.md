Chapter: Trauma MCQs — Sacral Fracture

## Question

During the placement of an S1 iliosacral screw in a patient with a dysmorphic sacrum, which fluoroscopic view is most critical to ensure the screw does not breach the cephalad cortex of the sacral ala and injure the L5 nerve root?

## Options

1. AP Inlet View
2. AP Outlet View
3. Lateral Sacral View
4. Obturator-Oblique View

## Correct option

**2. AP Outlet View**

## Answer explanation

The Outlet View is the only view that provides a true tangential look at the cephalad border of the sacral ala. In dysmorphic sacra, the "safe corridor" is narrow and angled. On the Outlet view, you can visualize the "alar void" and ensure your screw trajectory remains within the bony confines of the S1 body, preventing a superior breach where the L5 root resides

Understanding Dysmorphic Sacrum Anatomy

A dysmorphic sacrum has congenital variations that alter the "safe zones" for screw placement. Look for these five classic signs on CT and Fluoroscopy:

 - Non-Collinear SI Joints: The top of the sacrum is not level with the iliac crests; it sits higher (often above the L5-S1 intervertebral disc space).

 - Acute Alar Slope: The top of the sacral ala slopes downward and forward acutely. A horizontal screw will exit the bone and hit the L5 nerve root.

 - Mammillary Processes: Large bony protuberances on the dorsal sacrum.

 - Tongue-in-Groove SI Joints: The sacroiliac joint surface is irregular rather than smooth.

Crescent Sign: On a lateral view, the upper part of the sacrum has a crescent-shaped void where the S1 and S2 corridors are constricted.

Answer: 2
