## Question

Total hip arthroplasty is the most appropriate procedure for which of the following patients?

## Options

1. 83-year-old female with an intertrochanteric hip fracture
2. 56-year-old female with a nondisplaced femoral neck fracture
3. 48-year-old male with a compression sided femoral neck stress fracture
4. 32-year-old male with a displaced femoral neck fracture following a high energy MVC
5. 27-year-old male with osteonecrosis and subchondral collapse of the femoral head

## Correct option

**5. 27-year-old male with osteonecrosis and subchondral collapse of the femoral head**

## Answer explanation

When subchondral collapse has occurred in the setting of osteonecrosis, arthroplasty is the most reliable long-term solution.

Osteonecrosis of the femoral head may occur idiopathically or after trauma that compromises the medial femoral circumflex artery. The Ficat staging system is used to classify avascular necrosis of the hip and is also used to guide treatment. Patients, regardless of age, with subchondral collapse, as indicated by the crescent sign (Stage III) or more advanced degenerative changes, are most reliably treated with total hip arthroplasty (THA). Patients with lesions identified earlier in the disease process (Ficat stages 0-II) may be candidates for preservation procedures, including core decompression, rotational osteotomies, bone grafting, and vascularized fibula transfers.

*Swarup et al (2018). reviewed implant survival and patient-reported outcomes after THA in patients <35-years-old.* **They report** the most common diagnosis requiring THA in this cohort was AVN, and that patients were most commonly treated with standard, cementless, metal-on-plastic implants. **They conclude** that THA has good short-term and midterm survival, but is significantly affected by the primary diagnosis, age, and sex.

*Swarup et al (2017). reviewed outcomes after THA in young patients with osteonecrosis.* **They report** 10 and 20-year survivorship to be 86 and 66%, respectively, in this unique subset of patients. Implant survivorship was longer in male patients and those who were >25-years-old at the time of surgery. **They conclude** that young patients with osteonecrosis have good implant survival and long-term outcomes after THA.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Intertrochanteric hip fractures are best treated with cephalomedullary nails or sliding hip screw constructs.
Answer 2: Nondisplaced femoral neck fractures are typically treated with internal fixation.
Answer 3: Compression sided femoral neck fractures should be treated with non-weight bearing and activity modifications if <50% of the width of the femoral neck and ORIF with percutaneous screws if >50% of the neck width.
Answer 4: Displaced femoral neck fractures in young patients are generally treated with ORIF to at least allow a chance for bone healing and native hip preservation.
