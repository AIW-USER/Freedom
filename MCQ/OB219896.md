## Options

## Correct option

**2. Increased pull-out strength**

## Answer explanation

The patient has sustained a patellar tendon rupture, as evidenced by the patella alta on her presenting radiograph and obvious tendon defect on her MRI. Suture anchor-based constructs have been shown to have superior biomechanical properties when compared to bone tunnel-based approaches as shown in Figures C-E.

Patellar tendon ruptures occur due to a tensile overload of the extensor mechanism, which typically occurs with the knee in a flexed position. There are three patterns of injury, with the most common being avulsion with or without attached bone from the proximal insertion at the inferior pole of the patella, and less commonly at the mid-substance or distal avulsion from the tibial tubercle given that the strain at the proximal tendon-bone interface is 3-4x the strain at the mid-substance region. Treatment of partial tears with an intact extensor mechanism may be treated non-operatively; however, complete tears require timely operative intervention to restore the competency of the extensor mechanism. Though many different repair techniques exist, numerous studies have shown that suture anchor-based repairs result in less gap formation at the bone-tendon interface, resulting in biomechanically stronger constructs that have higher pull-out strengths and ultimate loads to failure. Despite these findings, studies have shown no difference in overall re-operation rates, infection rates, or release from medical care and return to sport when comparing trans-osseous suture fixation and suture anchor-based constructs.

*O'Dowd et al. reviewed 361 patients with patellar tendon ruptures who underwent either trans-osseous or suture anchor-based repairs.* **The authors found** that there was a significant difference in re-rupture rate between transosseous and anchor repairs (7.5% vs. 0%, respectively), but no difference in infection rate or time to release from medical care. **They concluded** that anchor repair demonstrated a significant decrease in re-rupture rate when compared with transosseous repair, despite no difference in overall re-operation rate.

*Onggo et al. performed a systematic review of biomechanical studies comparing trans-patellar suture and suture anchor-based constructs that included 212 knees and* **found** that gap formation for the suture anchor cohort ranged from 0.9 mm to 4.1 mm, while that of the trans-patellar group ranged from 2.9 mm to 10.3 mm. **The authors concluded** that suture anchor fixation displays a better biomechanical profile than traditional trans-patellar techniques in terms of smaller gap formations in the repair of both patella and quadriceps tendon injuries.

*Ettinger et al. performed a cadaveric study comparing the biomechanical properties of suture anchor repair with those of transosseous sutures in patellar tendon ruptures.* *The authors included 30 cadaveric knees and found that tendon repairs with suture anchors yielded significantly less gap formation during cyclic loading and resisted significantly higher ultimate failure loads, leading them to conclude that patellar tendon repair with suture anchors yields significantly better biomechanical results than repair with the commonly*
applied transosseous sutures.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Though suture anchor-based constructs have shown biomechanical superiority to transosseous suture tunnel repairs, studies have not shown statistically significant differences in overall re-operation rates.
Answers 3-5: There is no data to support reduced infection rates, improved PROMs, or quicker return to sports for anchor-based repairs.
