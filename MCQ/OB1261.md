## Question

A 62-year-old female that was myelopathic due to severe ossification of the posterior longitudinal ligament and cervical kyphosis underwent a three-level corpectomy and anterior cervical fusion using an anterior cage and plate several weeks ago. She is presenting now worsening neck pain and difficulty swallowing. On examination, there are no motor or sensory changes. Recent radiographs are shown in figure A. What could have been done to prevent this complication?

![[q1261-a-xr-lat-catastrophic-failure.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Performing diskectomy at every level instead of corpectomy
2. Using a mesh cage instead of fibular graft
3. Combined anterior and posterior fixation
4. Leaving the patient in a rigid collar longer
5. Using a combination of fixed and variable screws

## Correct option

**3. Combined anterior and posterior fixation**

## Answer explanation

The patient is presenting with failure of the anterior plate construct after a 3-level corpectomy and fusion. This could have been avoided by utilizing a posterior lateral mass screw construct in addition to the anterior cervical plate.

Ossification of the posterior longitudinal ligament with extensive retrovertebral disease can be technically difficult to treat in the setting of cervical kyphosis. In these cases, multilevel cervical corpectomy may be necessary in order to obtain sufficient decompression. Stabilization of a strut graft with an anterior plate alone can be sufficient fixation in 2-level corpectomies. However, 3-level corpectomies and above require the addition of a posterior lateral mass screw construct to prevent catastrophic screw pull-out with graft dislodgement.

*Sasso et al. performed a retrospective study of 40 patients treated with anterior corpectomy and fusion (ACF) for various spinal pathologies to identify factors associated with graft and plate complications.* **They reported** the 33 patients treated with a 2-level ACF had a 94% fusion rate with two non-catastrophic failures. The seven patients that underwent a 3-level ACF had a 71.4% failure rate, all of which were catastrophic, resulting from graft fracture prior to fusion. **The authors concluded** that 3-level ACF procedures should be adjunctively stabilized with a posterior lateral mass screw construct to minimize the high failure rates associated with anterior-only fixation.

*Riew et al. performed a retrospective study of 18 patients who previously underwent a posterior laminectomy that underwent a 4-level ACF without instrumentation but immobilized in a halo vest postoperatively and* **observed** for complication rates. **They reported** complications in 11 of the patients including five grafts extruding or collapsing which required four reoperations. Additionally, halo immobilization did not appear to prevent graft complications. **The authors concluded** that ACF without instrumentation in patients with a previous cervical laminectomy is associated with high complication rates and should be performed with caution.

Figure A is the lateral radiograph of the cervical spine with the loosening of the caudal screws from the plate construct and dislodgement of the strut graft anteriorly.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: Performing a diskectomy at every level instead of performing a corpectomy would leave the spine intrinsically more stable, but this would not sufficiently decompress the patient given the ossification of the posterior longitudinal ligament and retrovertebral involvement.
Answer 2: Regardless of the use of a mesh cage or a fibular strut graft, additionally fixation with a posterior construct is necessary to afford enough stability to prevent graft dislodgement.
Answer 4: Studies have shown that even with halo immobilization following anterior corpectomy did not prevent graft dislodgement.
Answer 5: No studies demonstrated that a combination of fixed and variable angle screws in the anterior plate construct prevents graft dislodgement.
