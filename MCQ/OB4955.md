## Question

A 4-year-old presents to your clinic with a longitudinal metatarsal epiphyseal bracket on his right foot. In addition to examining the patient for other deformities, the best next step in treatment is:

## Options

1. Percutaneous epiphyseolysis
2. Symes amputation
3. Lengthening using of multi-planar external fixator
4. Bracket excision and fat graft interposition
5. Fusion when the metatarsal reaches the length of the father's

## Correct option

**4. Bracket excision and fat graft interposition**

## Answer explanation

Longitudinal epiphyseal brackets of the metatarsals or phalanges are best treated with open excisions of the deformed physeal bracket. Subsequent procedures may be needed to correct hallux varus deformity, but early excision may obviate the need for future deformity correction.

Metatarsal longitudinal epiphyseal brackets are a rare deformity that can lead to severe shortening and angulation of the affected ray. The pathology consists of an anomaly of the primary ossification center that restricts growth on the medial side of the metatarsal, creating a delta-shaped metatarsal with progressive hallux varus deformity. Early open excision is recommended as near normal growth can occur when the bracket is excised at an early age. Multiple techniques have been described, including fat graft interposition or acute osteotomy and realignment, but regardless of the technique used, radiographic follow up is needed to monitor for deformity progression. Secondary interventions include realignment surgeries or lengthening with external fixation.

*Mubarak et al. report a case series of 5 patients, all treated with central physiolysis and acute correction of varus deformity.* *All patients resumed longitudinal growth and maintained alignment following the correction.* The condition was associated with both tibial hemimelia and duplication of the great toe.

*Shea et al. showed through a case series that early intervention - before the development of the secondary ossification center - may be beneficial if treated with excision alone.*

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: This requires an open excision to assure disruption of the central portion of the physis.
Answer 2: The patients can have highly functioning feet following appropriate treatment.
Answer 3: An external fixator may be beneficial for secondary corrective procedures, but it is not indicated as an index procedure at this young age.
Answer 5: Fusion would be indicated for macrodactyly.
