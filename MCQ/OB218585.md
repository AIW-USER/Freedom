## Question

A 32-year-old male sustains a right trimalleolar ankle fracture after he slipped and fell down a flight of stairs. The fracture is reduced and the patient follows up with the treating orthopedic surgeon. During the discussion of surgical treatment, the surgeon mentions the need for a posterolateral incision in addition to a medial incision. What is the purpose of adding a posterolateral incision for the treatment of trimalleolar ankle fracture?

## Options

1. Minimize risk of superficial peroneal nerve injury
2. Decrease post-operative soft tissue complications
3. Improve the reduction and stabilization of the syndesmotic attachments
4. Direct syndesmosis repair
5. Assess articular congruity

## Correct option

**3. Improve the reduction and stabilization of the syndesmotic attachments**

## Answer explanation

Performing a posterolateral approach to the ankle for a trimalleolar ankle fracture allows for fixation of the posterior malleolus which improves syndesmotic reduction and stability.

Trimalleolar ankle fractures are rotational injuries that often have a disruption of the syndesmosis. Malreduction or decreased stability of the syndesmosis can impair long-term functional outcomes and cause chronic pain. Screw or suture-button techniques afford reduction of the lateral malleolus to the distal tibia, but may affect the station if there is a compromised incisura. Direct fixation of the posterior malleolus can reconstitute the congruity of the incisura and provide direct fixation of the soft tissue syndesmosis attachments to the posterior malleolar fragment. Typically, a posterolateral approach is performed to address the posterior malleolus fracture.

*Miller et al. retrospectively assessed syndesmotic stability following the fixation of ankle fractures involving the posterior malleolus.* **They reported** a 13-fold higher rate of syndesmotic instability in patients that were treated with initial supine fixation. **They concluded** syndesmotic instability of trimalleolar ankle fractures can be minimized by initial direct fixation of the posterior malleolus through a prone position approach.

*Fitzpatrick et al. performed a cadaveric study of syndesmosis reduction in the setting of posterior malleolus fractures.* **They reported** lateral displacement of the distal fibula with a large unfixed fragment of the posterior malleolus. **They concluded** the mediolateral reduction of the syndesmosis is affected by the condition of the posterior malleolus.

*Irwin et al. reviewed the treatment of posterior malleolus fractures.* *The authors claimed fixation of the posterior malleolus can provide increased stability of rotational ankle fractures by restoring articular congruity and ligamentous attachments leading to decreased posterior talar translation.* **They concluded** fixation recommendations remain unclear and significant surgeon variability exists.

Incorrect answers
Answer 1: A posterolateral approach is not chosen to prevent a superficial peroneal nerve injury.
Answer 2: A posterolateral approach is not chosen to minimize soft tissue complications, but to address certain fracture patterns and provide optimal fixation options.
Answer 4: Direct repair of the syndesmosis is not typically performed in the setting of a trimalleolar ankle fracture.
Answer 5: Articular surface visualization is difficult through a posterolateral approach.
