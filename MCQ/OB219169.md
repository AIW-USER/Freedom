## Question

A 75-year-old female falls down the steps of her back porch and is unable to bear weight afterwards. She calls for help and is taken to the emergency department where radiographs demonstrate a comminuted intertrochanteric fracture with reverse obliquity. Surgery is recommended with a long, statically locked cephalomedullary nail. What is the most likely consequence of this procedure if the radius of curvature of the nail is significantly greater than the patient's femur?

## Options

1. Nonunion
2. Neurovascular injury
3. Anterior cortical perforation of distal femur
4. Fracture malreduction
5. Cutout of blade/screw

## Correct option

**3. Anterior cortical perforation of distal femur**

## Answer explanation

An intramedullary nail with a greater radius of curvature than the patient's femur increases the risk of anterior cortical perforation of the distal femur.

Cephalomedullary nailing of intertrochanteric and subtrochanteric femur fractures is generally accepted as safe, with high rates of fracture healing noted. This said, there are potential complications with their use. One particular complication is anterior perforation of the distal femoral cortex. While uncommon, this can be a devastating complication that is usually seen with the insertion of long cephalomedullary nails. It typically occurs in patients who have greater degrees of anatomic femoral bow. This is secondary to most femoral nail implants having a relatively higher radius of curvature (straighter) compared to the native femoral anatomy. This can be further exaggerated by a posterior starting point on the greater trochanter, which causes the nail to hinge off the posterior diaphyseal cortex and redirects the nail anteriorly as it passes distal within the femoral canal. Therefore, posterior starting points should be avoided if possible with the use of long cephalomedullary nails.

*Bazylewicz et al. looked at anterior distal femur encroachment after cephalomedullary nailing of the proximal femur based on the nail radius of curvature.* Using a nail with a 180mm radius of curvature to stabilize 214 proximal femur fractures, **they noted** only 1 case of distal femoral anterior perforation but did note that 40% of the nails ended up "far" anterior, 48% were anterior, 10% posterior, and 2% "far" posterior. **They concluded** that these results highlight the importance of paying close attention to the anterior cortex distally while performing this procedure.

*Parker and Handoll compared cephalomedullary nails to extramedullary implants for the fixation of extracapsular hip fractures.* **They noted** the nails were associated with an increased risk of intra-operative and post-operative fracture as well as an increased reoperation rate, while mortality and functional outcomes were similar. **They concluded** that for stable peri-trochanteric fractures, they recommended a sliding hip screw construct, while for unstable fractures, intramedullary nails had better results.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Rates of nonunion are low in the setting of intertrochanteric fractures and the radius of curvature would not affect the ability for fracture site healing.
Answer 2: Provided that the guidewire/nail remains within the medullary canal, there should be no increased risk for neurovascular injury in this setting.
Answer 4: Varus malreduction can occur with a lateral starting point but would not be affected by the radius of nail curvature.
Answer 5: Cutout of the blade/screw is primarily affected by fracture reduction and tip-to-apex distance of implant positioning within the femoral head.
