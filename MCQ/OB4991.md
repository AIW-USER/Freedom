## Question

A 16-year-old male presents to your clinic complaining of imbalance while sitting in his wheelchair. On exam, he has a right thoracic and left lumbar paraspinal prominence, pelvic obliquity, and equinovarus foot deformities. He has been wheelchair-bound since age 12, and used ambulatory assistive devices from ages 5-11. His maternal uncle had the same progressive condition. The patient and family desire an improved sitting posture. An AP scoliosis x-ray is shown in Figure A. You recommend a T2-Pelvis posterior spinal fusion. Which of the following medical workups is appropriate for this patient prior to surgery?

## Options

1. Rheumatology panel and a muscle biopsy
2. MRI to rule out dural ectasia or syrinx
3. Echocardiogram and pulmonary function studies
4. Genetic screening of all siblings and first cousins
5. Trial of Baclofen pump and physical therapy prior to fusion

## Correct option

**3. Echocardiogram and pulmonary function studies**

## Answer explanation

This patient has Duchenne's muscular dystrophy. Cardiac and pulmonary function should be evaluated prior to spinal fusion surgery due to the increased cardiopulmonary demands required by this procedure.

Duchenne's muscular dystrophy is a progressive neuromuscular disease caused by an absent dystrophin protein, which, over time leads to a degeneration of skeletal and cardiac muscle with fibrous fatty tissue replacing the muscle tissue. It is an X-linked recessive condition, with mortality from cardiac and/or pulmonary compromise by their early 20s. Orthopedic manifestations include neuromuscular scoliosis, equinovarus foot deformities, and other joint contractures. Spinal fusion is indicated when scoliosis reaches 20-30 degrees to avoid severe deformity correction when the patient has cardiac or pulmonary compromise.

Sussman's review of DMD and orthopedic implications comments on the value of a thorough cardiac and pulmonary pre-operative evaluation. Both an echocardiogram and spirometry testing should be performed for proper risk stratification, and results can impact decision-making regarding the timing of extubation.

*McCarthy et al. review scoliosis in cerebral palsy (CP) patients, for which surgical management often is targeted toward sitting posture.* CP does not cause the same predictable decline in cardiopulmonary function as DMD, so the pre-operative evaluation may not require significant cardiac and pulmonary function studies.

Figure A shows the preoperative X-rays with scoliosis and poor sitting balance. Illustration A shows the post-operative x-rays following T2-Pelvis posterior spinal fusion.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: This would be more important to obtain a diagnosis if there were concerns for a systemic inflammatory process.
Answer 2: Dural ectasias are associated with Marfan's Syndrome rather than DMD. MRI is not typically required for patients with DMD
Answer 4: Genetic screening may be something the patient's family wishes to undergo, but has no impact on their decision to pursue spinal fusion for sitting posture.
Answer 5: Baclofen pump trials are appropriate for spastic neuromuscular disorders, not Duchenne's Muscular Dystrophy.
