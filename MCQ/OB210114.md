## Question

A 70-year-old male presents to your clinic complaining of left hip pain for the past 3 weeks that is made worse with weight bearing. He has a positive cancer history and is status post right nephrectomy. He has been doing well for the past 5 years and recent staging studies show no other evidence of disease. Radiographs of his left hip and histology of the lesion are provided in figures A and B. Prior to this, he was an unassisted community ambulator. What is the best treatment option at this time?

## Options

1. Chemotherapy and radiation alone
2. Cephalomedullary nail following curettage and cement augmentation
3. Proximal femur replacement
4. Hip disarticulation
5. Observation for disease progression and referral to palliative care

## Correct option

**3. Proximal femur replacement**

## Answer explanation

This patient has metastatic renal cell carcinoma (RCC) to the bone and would benefit most from a proximal femur replacement in this scenario.

Common cancers that metastasize to the bone include prostate, breast, kidney, thyroid, and lung (Pb-KTL). Renal cell carcinoma does not respond well to radiation. Furthermore, RCC to the bone (and thyroid) is typically well vascularized and can be associated with substantial intra-operative blood loss; pre-operative embolization should be considered depending on the treatment strategy. Due to advances in chemotherapy, patients with RCC are living longer, and incomplete resection of bony metastasis can lead to progression of the disease so that more limited treatment strategies (ex. curettage and ORIF vs. IMN with cement augmentation) may fail in their lifetime. Complete resection is correlated with improvements in overall survival.

*Laitinen et al. looked at survival and complications of skeletal reconstructions after surgical treatment of bony RCC in a retrospective cohort of 268 patients.* **They found** that the overall complication rate was 17% and that the best survivorship was achieved when an endoprosthetic replacement was performed as the index procedure. They add that factors associated with poorer outcomes, such as amputation, include prior surgery and radiotherapy.

*Hwang et al. evaluated factors influencing patient survival in a prospective study of 135 patients treated with a massive endoprosthetic replacement for RCC bony metastasis.* **They found** an increased mortality risk in patients with multiple bony lesions, 1 or more visceral metastases, and local recurrence; 10 patients in their study required surgical revision. **They concluded** that patients with solitary bony metastasis and without visceral metastasis are the best candidates for resection and endoprosthetic reconstruction.

*Miller et al. looked at why IMN for long bone metastasis failed in a retrospective review of 112 patients.* **They identified** 10 failures requiring construct revision and 3 associated with progressive RCC. **They concluded** that IMN can be successful in patients with appropriate lesions when proper implants are utilized, curettage or resection of RCC is performed prior to nailing, radiation-associated fractures are avoided, and life expectancy is correctly estimated.

Figure A is an AP left hip radiograph demonstrating a destructive proximal femur lesion with involvement of the subtrochanteric region. Figure B is histology showing clear cell RCC. Illustration A is an AP left hip radiograph following a proximal femur replacement.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Renal cell carcinoma is radioresistant and this patient will likely fracture through this pathologic lesion if not addressed surgically.
Answer 2: Complete resection is correlated with improvements in overall survival; incomplete resection strategies may result in continued tumor progression and hardware failure in the patient's lifetime.
Answer 4: Amputation is not indicated, especially in the setting of metastatic disease when limb salvage options are available.
Answer 5: Complete resection is correlated with improvements in overall survival; neglecting this lesion will likely result in a pathologic fracture.
