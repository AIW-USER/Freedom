## Question

While on your orthopedic sports medicine rotation, you perform an isolated medial ulnar collateral ligament reconstruction on Patient A and a lateral ulnar collateral ligament repair with radial head replacement on Patient B. The physical therapist calls regarding postoperative motion for each patient. Which of the following elbow extension exercises should be recommended?

## Options

1. Passive flexion with the forearm in pronation for both patients
2. Passive flexion with the forearm in supination for both patients
3. Passive flexion with the forearm in supination for Patient A and in pronation for Patient B
4. Passive flexion with the forearm in pronation for Patient A and in supination for Patient B
5. Passive flexion with the forearm in pronation for Patient A and in neutral for Patient B

## Correct option

**3. Passive flexion with the forearm in supination for Patient A and in pronation for Patient B**

## Answer explanation

Elbow extension exercises should be performed with the forearm in pronation following repair of the LUCL and in supination following MUCL reconstruction.

Medial ulnar collateral ligament injuries are characterized by attenuation or rupture of the ulnar collateral ligament of the elbow leading to valgus instability in overhead throwing athletes. Treatment for most individuals is rest and physical therapy. Surgery is reserved for high level overhead athletes such as pitchers. Lateral ulnar collateral ligament injury is a ligamentous elbow injury usually associated with a traumatic elbow dislocation, and characterized by posterolateral subluxation or dislocation of the radiocapitellar and ulnohumeral joints. Treatment may be nonoperative or operative depending on presence of concomitant elbow fractures, as well as elbow stability following reduction. Postoperatively, the surgeon must communicate to the therapist that elbow extension exercises should be performed with the forearm in pronation as a result of the compromised LUCL. Elbow extension exercises in supination and neutral are recommended for compromised MUCL or combined medial and lateral ligament injury, respectively.

*Armstrong et al. reviewed the relative contribution of muscle activity and the effect of forearm position on the stability of the medial collateral ligament (MCL)-deficient elbow through simulated active and passive elbow flexion with the forearm in both supination and pronation.* **They reported** that following MCL transection, the elbow was more stable with the forearm in supination than pronation during passive flexion. **They concluded** that splinting and passive mobilization of the MCL-deficient elbow with the forearm in supination should minimize instability and valgus elbow stresses should be avoided throughout the rehabilitation period.

*Dunning et al. reviewed the influence of muscle activity and forearm position on the stability of the lateral collateral ligament deficient elbow.* **They reported** that in the vertical orientation during passive elbow flexion, stability of the lateral collateral ligament deficient elbow was similar to the intact elbow with the forearm held in pronation, but not similar to the intact elbow when maintained in supination. **They concluded** that passive mobilization should be done with the forearm maintained in pronation.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1,2,4,5: Elbow extension exercises should be performed with the forearm in pronation following LUCL repair. Elbow extension exercises in supination and neutral are recommended for compromised MUCL or combined medial and lateral ligament injury, respectively.
