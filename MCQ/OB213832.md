## Question

A 62-year-old female undergoes the procedure shown in Figure A. According to the most recent evidence, what is the overall probability that the patient will need additional lumbar surgery for adjacent segment degeneration (ASD) within 10 years after this type of surgery (without consideration of number of levels involved at index procedure)?

![[q213832-a-lateral-x-ray-of-lumbar-spine-for-typical-patient-.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. 1-9%
2. 10-19%
3. 20-29%
4. 30-39%
5. 40-49%

## Correct option

**3. 20-29%**

## Answer explanation

The patient underwent a lumbar decompression and fusion with instrumentation. At 10 years, roughly 20-29% of patients will require additional surgery (either decompression or decompression and fusion) at an adjacent level.

Lumbar fusion is the mainstay of treatment for spinal stenosis with spondylolisthesis. Fusion in the lumbar spine can accelerate degeneration at adjacent cranial and caudal segments. A combination of increased mechanical stresses, altered regional alignment, and genetic factors contributes to this pathologic process. In many cases, the presence of radiographic degeneration may not be associated with symptoms. For those who are persistently symptomatic and do require surgery, laminectomy alone or combined with fusion may be necessary depending on the presentation. More novel techniques, such as lateral interbody fusion, can often be used to address adjacent level pathology

*Sears et al. retrospectively reviewed 912 consecutive patients who underwent lumbar fusion and* **found** an annual incidence of 2.5% for surgery for ASD. At 10 years, **they report** approximately a 22% rate of surgery for ASD.

*Lee et al. retrospectively studied over 1000 patients undergoing lumbar fusion to determine risk factors for adjacent segment degeneration (ASD).* **The authors found** that pre-existing facet degeneration was a strong risk factor and that ASD occurred more frequently at the cranial segment than caudal segment.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1-4: While data from the SPORT trial suggests that 18% of patients will require additional surgery for ASD in the lumbar spine at 8 years, the most recent data shows that this is increased to 20-29% at 10 years.
