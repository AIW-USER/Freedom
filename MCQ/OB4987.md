## Question

A patient presents with scoliosis associated with neurofibromatosis. In addition to intracanal neurofibromas, which of the following is commonly associated with this condition, and should be assessed by MRI of the spinal axis preoperatively?

## Options

1. Syrinx
2. Hemivertebra
3. Dural ectasia
4. Diastematomyelia
5. Spina bifida occulta

## Correct option

**3. Dural ectasia**

## Answer explanation

All patients with neurofibromatosis (NF) and scoliosis must be evaluated with an MRI of the spinal axis to rule out both an intracanal neurofibroma, and dural ectasia prior to surgical intervention.

Scoliosis and kyphosis are commonly seen in patients with neurofibromatosis. Non-dystrophic forms of scoliosis can be treated similarly to adolescent idiopathic scoliosis (AIS) not otherwise associated with NF. However, dystrophic scoliosis is resistant to bracing and often requires early surgical intervention. There is a known association of NF with dural ectasia, or a widening of the dural sac. A preoperative MRI of the spinal axis must be done preoperatively to rule out this abnormality and avoid potential complications. Preoperative MRI can also help to evaluate for neurofibromas of the cord or nerve roots.

McCarthy at al. present a review article that describes the common presentation, work-up and treatments for early onset scoliosis. They note the need for MRI in certain circumstances, including for patients with NF.

*Crawford et al. provide a review of the orthopaedic manifestations of neurofibromatosis.* *They comment that dural ectasia is a circumferential dilation of the dural sac without enlargement of the neural elements.* It is caused by neurofribomata about the spinal axis and abnormal pressure phenomena. **They recommend** contrast-enhanced MRI prior to surgical intervention.

Incorrect Asnwers:

Answer 1: Syrinx typically presents with neurologic abnormalities similar to central cord syndrome. Presence of NF alone is not sufficient to require MRI to rule this out.
Answer 2: A hemivertebra can be a cause of scoliosis, but there is no known association with NF.
Answer 4: Diastematomyelia is a division of the spinal cord due to a fibrous, cartilagenous, or osseous bar believed to be a congenital anomaly. While it can be an underlying cause of early onset scoliosis, it is not known to be associated with NF.
Answer 5: Spina bifida occulta is not known to be associated with NF or scoliosis.
