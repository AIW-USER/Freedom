## Question

What is the correct sequence of events for on-field management of a prone football player who is unable to get up following a tackle and verbalizing an inability to move his arms and legs?

## Options

1. Log-rolling the patient supine while stabilizing the cervical spine, removing the facemask, adding hard collar while helmet still on, and taping towel bolsters on either side of the helmet
2. Maintaining prone positioning, controlled backboard placement, taping towel bolsters on either side of the helmet
3. Log-rolling the patient supine while stabilizing the cervical spine, removing the helmet, controlled backboard placement, taping towel bolsters on either side of the head
4. Maintaining prone positioning, removing the helmet, controlled backboard placement, taping towel bolsters on either side of the head
5. Log-rolling the patient supine while stabilizing the cervical spine, removing the facemask, removing the helmet and shoulder pads, placement of a hard cervical collar, controlled backboard placement

## Correct option

**5. Log-rolling the patient supine while stabilizing the cervical spine, removing the facemask, removing the helmet and shoulder pads, placement of a hard cervical collar, controlled backboard placement**

## Answer explanation

This athlete has a possible cervical spine (C-spine) injury and, in order to protect the airway and minimize the risk of further injury to the cervical spine, the following sequence should be followed: Log-rolling the patient supine while stabilizing the cervical spine, removing the facemask, removing the helmet and shoulder pads when possible (if it can be done safely) and controlled backboard placement (log-roll).

The cervical spine must be protected and immobilized whenever a concern for a spinal injury exists. Historically, cervical spine injuries were exacerbated or even caused by inappropriate on-field management or patient transport. In summary, the patient should be carefully log-rolled with the C-spine stabilized to a supine position with the face mask removed to allow for airway access/management. Many newer helmets are equipped with facemasks that can be easily removed for this purpose. Recent NATA guidelines now recommend helmet and shoulder pad removal on the field if it can be done safely. If the helmet and shoulder pads are removed, then a hard cervical collar should be placed. If not, towel rolls can be placed on either side of the helmet and secured to the backboard with tape to further immobilize the C-spine.

*Rihn et al. reviewed cervical spine injuries in American football.* *They cite that, although significant improvement in rules, tackling/blocking techniques, and medical care over the past few decades, C-spine injuries remain a serious issue.* In this article, they review stingers, cervical cord neuropraxia, congenital/acquired cervical stenosis, and catastrophic neurological injury; evaluation, management, and contraindications to play are also discussed.

*Mall et al. similarly review spine and axial skeleton injuries in National Football League players.* *They performed a descriptive epidemiological study covering all spine/axial injuries during 11 seasons.* **They concluded** that these are frequent injuries resulting in a significant amount of time missed from practices and games; tackling and blocking resulted in the greatest number of these injuries.

*Boden et al. reviewed catastrophic C-spine injuries in high school and college football players.* *They retrospectively reviewed 196 incidents during 13 academic years.* **They concluded** that the total number of quadriplegic events for both groups is about 6 per year, with more at the collegiate level, and that reports of noncontiguous injuries exist.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1: A hard collar should not be placed while the helmet is still on
Answers 2 and 4: The first step in the management of a spine injury in the football player should be flipping the player SUPINE while maintaining spine precautions.
Answer 3: The facemask should be removed (airway) prior to removing the helmet.
