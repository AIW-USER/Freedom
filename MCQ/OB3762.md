## Question

## Options

1. Inferior and medial to the vertebral artery
2. Inferior and lateral to the vertebral artery
3. Superior and medial to the vertebral artery
4. Superior and lateral to the vertebral artery
5. Superior and aligned in the coronal plane

## Correct option

**1. Inferior and medial to the vertebral artery**

## Answer explanation

During placement of a C1 lateral mass screw, the screw is directed 10 degrees medial and 22 degrees cephalad from the optimal starting point in order to be placed inferior and medial to the course of the vertebral artery.

There are a variety of screw fixation techniques for screw constructs that incorporate the atlas (C1): lateral mass screws, trans-articular screws, intralaminar screws, and wiring. Lateral mass screws have the benefit of staying within the confines of the C1 lateral mass which theoretically minimizes risk to the vertebral artery. Trans-articular screws that traverse the atlantoaxial articulation risk injury to the vertebral artery in cases of the aberrant vertebral artery intraosseous anomalies. For lateral mass screws, the optimal entry point has been described as the directly beneath the medial edge of the posterior arch/lamina where it joins the lateral mass. In the medial-lateral plane, direct anterior placement could be used, but the lateral mass will tolerate 20 degrees of medial angulation from this starting point, so 10 degrees is often described as the optimal angulation.

*Seal et al. used cadaveric measurements to determine the appropriate placement of C1 lateral mass screws.* *The authors studied 15 specimens and measured appropriate screw placement using calipers and CT scans.* **The authors noted** that the C1 lateral mass safely accommodated screw fixation with a trajectory of 10 degrees medial and 22 degrees cephalad. **The authors concluded** that C1 lateral mass fixation is a safe alternative for upper cervical fixation with several potential advantages versus other techniques.

*Lee et al. performed a cadaveric study on 709 atlas specimens to determine the thickness of the posterior lateral arch at the level of the lateral mass to determine the feasibility of potential screw placement.* **The authors noted** that the average minimal thickness of the posterior-lateral atlas was 3.95 mm. **They noted** that although only a small percentage of patients can accept a screw that is directly inserted via the posterior-lateral arch into the lateral mass, the notching technique is possible in the vast majority of patients. **They conclude** that while using the posterior-lateral arch as the starting point for these screws is feasible, alternative starting points for these screws are possible in a large percentage of the cases.

Figure A through C show sagittal and coronal CT cuts that reveal a comminuted type II odontoid fracture with a lordotic deformity. Illustration A and B are post-operative CT cuts showing the appropriate placement of a C1 lateral mass screw with 10 degrees of medial and about 22 degrees of cephalad angulation. Illustration C is a graphic showing the lateral mass starting point at C1 with an appropriate screw position being medial and inferior to the vertebral artery.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2, 3, 4 and 5: The appropriate trajectory of C1 lateral mass screws will place the screw medial and inferior to the course of the vertebral artery.
