## Question

Which is false about perthes disease?( Pediatrics- perthes disease)

## Options

1. Early surgery (containment procedure) before the age of 6 years prevents arthritis
2. Loss of abduction and internal rotation
3. Age at first presentation is most important prognostic factor
4. In a child of 7 years with maintained height of lateral pillar, treatment is activity modification

## Correct option

**1. Early surgery (containment procedure) before the age of 6 years prevents arthritis**

## Answer explanation

Perthes disease

Most common presenting complaint is limp. Classically, a painless limp is present but there may be pain during the periods of exacerbation. Second most common complaint is pain in the hip (may be referred to knee). There is limitation of abduction and internal rotation in severe cases. When the hip is flexed, it may go into obligatory external rotation (Catterall sign). On examination, Trendelenburg test is positive (as the head is flattened there is upward migration of greater trochanter which slackens the gluteus medius). One may also note shortening of the affected limb.

Radiographic Features:

In the early course the X-ray may be normal with only widening of the joint space appreciated. As the head undergoes ischemia and bone death, the area becomes sclerotic (appears radio dense) and starts fragmenting . Eventually the head collapses, becomes flattened (mushroom-shaped) and enlarged (coxa magna. Some other X-ray signs that may aid the diagnosis include Gage sign (a radiolucent V-shaped area can be appreciated in the lateral part of femoral head epiphysis).

Associated conditions: hypercoagulability, delayed bone age, attention deficit hyperkinetic disorder.

Prognostic Factors

Presence of following factors marks a bad prognosis in Perthes disease:

• Higher age at onset (most important factor): While children below 6 years have mild disease, those above 9 years have worst outcome)

• Patients with bilateral disease

• Presence of Catterall head at risk signs

• Extensive subchondral fracture lines seen

• Aspherical incongruent head (Stulberg classification)

• Lateral pillar C (Herring classification).

.

At risk signs (indicate a more severe disease course)

Gage sign, calcification lateral to the epiphysis, lateral subluxation of the femoral head, horizontal proximal femoral physis and metaphyseal cyst.

Lateral pillar (Herring) classification: most commonly used classification

Group A
Maintained height of lateral pillar of femoral head
Good prognosis

Group B
More than 50% height is maintained
Poor prognosis in children>6 years age

Group C
Less than 50% height is maintained
Poor prognosis

Management :

Nonoperative: Observation alone, activity restriction (non-weight bearing), and physical therapy (ROM exercises)- This is indicated in young children <age 8 years (bone age <6 years), lateral pillar A involvement

Operative: Femoral and/or pelvic osteotomy: This is indicated in children > 8 years of age, especially lateral pillar B and B/C. Poor outcome is seen in lateral pillar C disease regardless of treatment.

Proximal femoral varus osteotomy, Pelvic osteotomy (Salter or triple innominate osteotomy): These are required to provide containment.

Shelf arthroplasty may be performed to prevent lateral subluxation and resultant lateral epiphyseal overgrowth.

Valgus and/or shelf osteotomies (Abduction-extension osteotomy): This is required in hinge abduction (lateral extrusion of the capital femoral epiphysis producing a painful hinge effect on the lateral acetabulum during abduction) to reposition the hinge segment away from the acetabular margin.

Answer: 1
