## Question

A 24-year-old male presents with persistent right hip pain for 3 months duration. He is an active runner and had been training for a full marathon until his pain limited his activities. He describes vague groin pain that is dull and occurs during the initiation of running. He has stopped running over the last 2-3 weeks and notes the pain is present even while he is sitting. On exam, he has worsening of his pain when his hip is brought into flexion, adduction and internal rotation. There is no pain with axial loading of the leg in a resting position and he has full strength of all muscle groups. Which of the following imaging findings would most likely explain the physical exam findings in this patient?

## Answer explanation

The patient has physical exam findings of anterosuperior chondrolabral pathology. This can be present with the imaging findings of Figure D which show a magnetic resonance arthrogram (MRA) with fluid deep to the anterior chondrolabral junction consistent with an anterior labral tear.

Femoroacetabular impingement (FAI, CAM or pincer lesions), hip labral pathology or a combination of both can lead to mechanical symptoms and anterior hip pain. The most common cause of hip labral pathology is FAI and both the labral and FAI bony pathology have to be addressed for a patient to have symptomatic improvement. The most common location of a hip labral tear is at the anterosuperior chondrolabral junction. Physical exam findings of increased pain with flexion, adduction and internal rotation can be indicative of both CAM impingement as well as anterosuperior chondrolabral pathology. Furthermore, patients with anterior hip pathology generally have pain while sitting as well. The gold standard for diagnosis of a hip labral tear is an MRA which studies have shown is >90% sensitive for detecting such pathology.

*Konan et al. developed a classification system to describe acetabular chondral lesions with respect to location, cartilage wear and size of the lesion.* The cartilage was graded on a scale of 0 to 4 from normal articular cartilage lesions (grade 0) to full-thickness defects with exposed bone (grade 4). The site of the lesion was classed as A, B or C based on whether the lesion is less than one-third of the distance from the acetabular rim to the cotyloid fossa, one-third to two-thirds of the same distance and greater than two-thirds of the distance, respectively. The authors were able to validate their findings with 6 surgeons perform hip arthroscopy and noted that their classification system had high intra-observer reliability of 0.81 and high interobserver reliability of 0.88.

*Safran performed a review of the anatomy of acetabular labral tears and indications for hip arthroscopy.* *He described that labral tears increased friction within the joint, cartilage consolidation, and strain within the articular cartilage, thereby possibly resulting in accelerated degeneration of the joint.* **He noted** that while both partial labrectomy and labral repair are current surgical options, current data supports labral repairs leading to better healing and functional restoration. **He concludes** that validated outcomes measure to adequately assess active patients with a painful non-arthritic hip are needed to determine the efficacy of such repair.

*Matsuda et al. performed a comparative systematic review of open surgical dislocation, mini-open and arthroscopic approach for surgical treatment of FAI.* **The authors found** 6 open surgical dislocation, 4 mini-open, and 8 arthroscopic studies, all with Levels III or IV evidence. **They noted** that all three methods are effective in improving pain and function in short-term to midterm studies. **They noted** a higher overall complication rate with open dislocation surgery and a significant incidence of iatrogenic injury to the lateral femoral cutaneous nerve in the mini-open surgery. **The authors concluded** that the all-arthroscopic method had surgical outcomes equal to or better than the other methods with a lower rate of major complications when performed by experienced surgeons.

*Leunig et al. performed a study to determine if an MRA can show differences in disorders of the labrum in symptomatic patients with developmental hip dysplasia (DDH) or FAI.* Fourteen patients in each group were evaluated clinically, along with conventional radiographs and MRA studies. In both DDH and FAI, disorders of the labrum localized identically with a predilection to the anterosuperior quadrant of the acetabulum. Labral tears were observed in nine hips of each group. The labrum was enlarged in 12 hips with dysplasia but in none of the hips with impingement. **The authors concluded** that the anterosuperior acetabulum represents the initial fatiguing site of the hip under both conditions.

Figure A shows a frog-leg lateral radiograph with an alpha angle measurement of 40. Figure B shows a T2-weighted MRI coronal cut showing a compression-sided femoral neck stress fracture. Figure C shows a T2-weighted MRI axial cut with a complete right proximal hamstring avulsion injury (labeled in illustration A). Figure D shows a T2-weighted post-contrast MRA with an anterior hip labral tear (labeled in illustration B). Figure E shows a true lateral hip radiograph with a femoral head-neck offset ratio of 0.20.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: While CAM-type FAI could present with the exam-findings of the patient, Figure A shows a frog-leg lateral with an alpha angle of 40. This is considered a normal alpha angle with values > 42 indicative of CAM-type FAI.
Answer 2: Figure B shows a compression sided femoral neck fracture which is not consistent with the symptoms and exam findings. Specifically, there is no pain with axial load of the hip.
Answer 3: Figure C shows a complete right hamstring avulsion which is not consistent with the symptoms and exam findings.
Answer 5: While CAM-type FAI could present with the exam-findings of the patient, Figure E shows a lateral hip radiograph with a head-neck offset ratio of 0.2. This is considered a normal head-neck offset ratio with values < 0.17 indicative of CAM-type FAI.
