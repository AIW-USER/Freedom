Chapter: Spine MCQs — Subaxial Cervical Vertebral Body Fractures

## Question

Patient presents within 30 minutes of injury with C4- C5 fracture dislocation and jumped facets on the lateral view x-ray cervical spine. Patient is awake and alert. Airway, Breathing and Circulation assessment is normal. What is the appropriate next step/ Appropriate management? (FNB Patter Paper 2020)

## Options

1. Anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF)
2. Gartner well’s tong application with serial traction
3. Hard collar application and MRI of cervical spine
4. Immediate posterior surgical reduction and stabilization.

## Correct option

**3. Hard collar application and MRI of cervical spine**

## Answer explanation

A jumped facet joint occurs when the inferior articular process of the superior vertebra is locked anteriorly to the superior articular process of the inferior vertebra. Surgical open reduction may increase the neurologic deficit if a disc herniation exists. Evidence from animal studies suggests that rapid decompression of the spinal cord may improve recovery. Serially increasing traction weight to reduce the dislocation has been shown to be safe when used in patients who are awake.

Using MRI to assist in the “clearance” of the cervical spine remains controversial at this time.

Indications for MRI: If a patient has abnormal findings on CT suggesting soft-tissue injury, such as soft-tissue density anterior to the midbody of C3 greater than 5 mm, a widened disc space (>1 to 2 mm) at one level relative to adjacent disc levels particularly if there is an anterior osteophyte avulsion at that level, or excessive widening of the interspinous distance posteriorly, MRI should be obtained.

Timing of the MRI: If the MRI is obtained within the first 48 hours after injury, the sensitivity for hemorrhage and edema is optimal. The ability of MRI to identify injury after 48 hours is dependent on the direct demonstration of tissue disruption or subluxation of the spine.

Evaluation of the available literature reveals level III evidence to support the “clearance” of the cervical spine in a symptomatic patient if CT and MRI done within 48 hours of injury are found to be normal.

Answer: 3
