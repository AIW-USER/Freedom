## Question

A 48-year-old male undergoes prophylactic fixation for a humeral shaft lesion. Following surgery the patient's neurovascular exam is changed from the one documented preoperatively. It's suspected that the injury occurred during the insertion of the distal anterior to posterior interlocking screw. Which of the following nerves would most likely be affected if this is when the injury occurred?

## Options

1. Ulnar nerve
2. Radial nerve
3. Median nerve
4. Medial antebrachial cutaneous nerve
5. Lateral antebrachial cutaneous nerve

## Correct option

**5. Lateral antebrachial cutaneous nerve**

## Answer explanation

The musculocutaneous nerve is at risk with placement of the distal anterior to posterior screw in a humeral nail. The terminal branch of the musculocutaneous nerve is the lateral antebrachial nerve and is responsible for the resultant symptoms.

Humeral intramedullary nails can be used for operative stabilization of humeral shaft fractures and impending fractures resulting from pathologic lesions. When placing the distal most interlocking screws in the nail, blunt dissection should be used to visualize the bone to prevent injury to surrounding nervous structures. The radial nerve can be injured with lateral to medial screws, whereas the musculocutaneous nerve can be injured with anterior to posteriorly placed screws. The terminal branch of the musculocutaneous nerve is the lateral antebrachial cutaneous nerve (LABCN). If injury occurs with placement of the anterior to posterior screws patients will have decreased sensation to the radial volar forearm secondary to a loss of function of the LABCN.

*Steinmetz et al review the relationship of multiplanar distal and uniplanar proximal interlocking screws to neurovascular structures with the Depuy Synthes humeral nail.* **They report** that the median distance of the screw path/screw head inserted (A-P) to the LABCN was 0 mm/0 mm in the left arm and 3.5 mm/1.6 mm in the right arm. **They conclude** that A-P screws in the distal portion of this nail place the LABCN at risk.

*Rupp et al review the risk of neurovascular injury with distal locking screws of humeral intramedullary nails.* **They report** that the radial nerve was at direct risk with lateral to medial screw placement and the ulnar nerve and median nerve/brachial artery bundle was at risk if significant overpenetration of the medial cortex occurred. The musculocutaneous nerve was at direct risk with placement of a distal screw from anterior to posterior. **They conclude** that the surgeon must be aware of these risks when placing distal locking screws.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The ulnar nerve is at risk with over penetration of the lateral to medial interlocker.
Answer 2: The radial nerve is at risk with insertion of the lateral to medial screw.
Answer 3: The median nerve is medial to the insertion site of the distal interlocking screw and is not at risk.
Answer 4: The medial antebrachial cutaneous nerve is a branch of the medial cord of the brachial plexus and often runs with the ulnar nerve.
