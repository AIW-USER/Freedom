## Options

1. Infection
2. Pseudoarthrosis
3. Proximal junctional failure
4. Dural tear
5. Neurologic deficit

## Correct option

**2. Pseudoarthrosis**

## Answer explanation

Psuedoarthrosis is the most common complication requiring revision surgery within 3 years of long spinal fusions for adult spinal deformity, which has been reported as high as 25% of cases.

Adult spinal deformity is a complex disease process that is characterized by coronal and sagittal plane imbalance in the setting of degenerative disc disease. Degenerative scoliosis typically occurs in the lumbar spine compared to idiopathic scoliosis, which often involves the thoracic spine. Surgical treatment is fraught with high complication rates with pseudoarthrosis being the most common (up to 25%) followed by proximal junctional failure. The most common locations for pseudoarthrosis are at L5-S1 and the thoracolumbar junction. Pseudoarthrosis has been reported as the most common reason for late revision surgery following adult spinal deformity correction.

*Crawford et al. performed a retrospective review of a consecutive series of 153 patients undergoing surgical treatment for degenerative lumbar scoliosis.* **They reported** a 24% unplanned reoperation rate with rod fracture/pseudoarthrosis being the most common reason for reoperation, accounting for 62% of unplanned procedures. **They concluded** that efforts to minimize this complication could have a great impact on outcomes in adult spinal deformity surgery.

*Koerner et al. reviewed the diagnosis and management of degenerative lumbar scoliosis.* **The authors stated** that surgical treatment is directed at addressing neurological deficits and disability from the deformity. **They concluded** that outcomes in adult deformity surgery can be favorable in well-selected patients despite the high rate of perioperative complications.

Figures A and B are the AP and lateral radiographs of the lumbar spine demonstrating extensive degenerative disc disease with degenerative levoscoliosis of the lumbar spine and loss of lumbar lordosis. Figures C and D are the AP and lateral radiographs of the spine with T10-pelvis posterior instrumentation and interbody fusion from L1-S1 and near physiologic restoration of lumbar lordosis and coronal alignment.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: Infection is not the most common cause of failure in adult spinal deformity with rates reported as high as 1.5% of cases.
Answer 3: Proximal junctional failure is a common complication, but does not occur at the same rate as pseudoarthrosis in the reported studies. Most cases of proximal junctional failure occur within 6-9 months of surgery.
Answer 4: Dural tear occurs at a rate of 2.9% in adult spinal deformity surgery
Answer 5: Neurologic deficits are relatively uncommon depending on the type of procedure. Pedicle subtraction osteotomies and vertebral column resections are associated with high neurologic complication rates. Despite, the patient initially having deficient lumbar lordosis, there were no pedicle subtraction osteotomies or vertebral body resections performed to address the deformity.
