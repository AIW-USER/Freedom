## Question

Which radiographic index has been shown to be the best predictor of failure of closed reduction and casting of pediatric distal radius fractures?

## Options

1. Padding index
2. Gap index
3. Three-point index
4. Cast index
5. Second metacarpal-radius index

## Correct option

**4. Cast index**

## Answer explanation

A cast index higher than 0.8 is associated with loss of fracture reduction in the cast following closed reduction of pediatric distal radius fractures.

The cast index is a ratio of the measure of the inner diameter of the cast at the fracture site on the lateral radiograph divided by the inner diameter on the anteroposterior radiograph. Application of a well-moulded cast with three-point bending or appropriate interosseous mould is critical in maintaining reduction and minimizing the risk of subsequent late displacement.

*Kamat et al. reviewed radiographs of 1001 children who underwent a manipulation under anesthesia for a displaced fracture of the distal forearm and* **found** that in patients with a cast index of <=0.8, the displacement rate was only 5.58%. However, in patients with a cast index of >=0.81, the displacement rate was 26%. A high cast index was the sole factor that was significantly higher in the redisplacement group. No statistically significant difference was noted for age, sex, or surgeon seniority.

*McQuinn et al. retrospectively reviewed 155 children with distal radius and forearm fractures to assess the significance of clinical and radiographic factors in redisplacement after treatment with closed reduction.* **They found** that initial displacement of >50% and inability to achieve anatomic reduction were major risk factors for redisplacement. **They also noted** that the cast index was the most useful measure of cast molding and radiographic predictor of failure.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The padding index measures the dorsal gap on the lateral (at the fracture site) over the maximum interosseous space on the AP (a/b). This is not the best radiographic index for predicting failure of closed reduction.
Answer 2: The gap index is the radial and ulnar fracture site gap added together divided by the inner diameter on the AP plus the dorsal and volar fracture site gap added together divided by the inner diameter on the lateral [(a+b)/c]+[(d+e)/f]. This is not predictive of failure of closed reduction.
Answer 3: The three-point index is a calculation of fracture gap, angle and contact between the fracture fragment [(a+b+c)/d]+[(e+f+g)/h]. This is not the best predictor for failure of closed reduction.
Answer 5: The second metacarpal-radius index is the angle created by the bisection of the long axis of the second metacarpal and long axis of the radius on the AP. This is a poor predictor of failure of closed reduction.
