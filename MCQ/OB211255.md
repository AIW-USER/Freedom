## Options

2. Patients with the EXT2 mutation have a more severe presentation than those with EXT1 mutation

5. Distal lesions are more likely to undergo malignant transformation than proximal lesions

## Correct option

**4. Analysis of her physis would reveal a decrease amount of heparan sulfate**

## Answer explanation

The radiographs demonstrate multiple osteochondromas or exostoses consistent with the diagnosis of multiple hereditary exostoses (MHE). The underlying genetic abnormality of a mutation in the EXT gene leads to a decreased production of heparan sulfate (HS) made by chondrocytes at the physis.

MHE is a predominately autosomal dominant disorder leading to the production of multiple osteochondromas throughout the body. Patients with MHE have a genetic mutation in the EXT1, EXT2, and EXT3 genes that encode for the production of heparan sulfate at the physis. Heparan sulfate plays an important role in physeal signaling, and its absence leads to chondrocyte disorganization leading to exostosis development. Most common sites include the proximal tibia, distal femur, proximal humerus, scapula, pelvis, and wrist. More recent studies reveal a 3-10% risk of malignant secondary transformation of osteochondromas to chondrosarcomas in patients with MHE. Malignant transformation can occur in an exostosis in any location; however, pelvic exostoses have been shown to have a higher rate of malignant transformation.

*Czajka et al. performed an anonymous web-based survey to evaluate the incidence and progression of malignant transformation in patients with MHE.* *They had 742 patients with MHE who responded to the queries.* **They noted** a median age of 28 years for the patients. The proportion of respondents that reported a malignant transformation was 2.7% with the most common sites of malignant transformation being the pelvis and scapula.

*Pedrini et al. evaluated 529 patients with MHE from 2 different European referral centers and looked at the phenotype variation amongst the patient.* The presence or absence of deformities and functional limitations in the patients led to a breakdown of the severity of the phenotype: mild (the absence of both aspects), intermediate, or severe (the concurrent presence of both aspects). In their cohort of patients, **they noted** that female sex, fewer than 5 areas of exostosis, the EXT2 mutation were associated with a mild phenotype; in contrast, a severe phenotype was associated with male sex, EXT1 mutations, and more than twenty affected skeletal sites.

*Huegel et al. performed a review of HS's role in the pathogenesis of MHE.* *They go on to describe recent advancements in the pathogenesis of exostosis formation in patients with MHE with a focus on the distribution, activities, and roles of signaling proteins in wild-type and HS-deficient cells and tissues.* **They note** that the aberrant distribution of signaling factors combined with the aberrant responsiveness of target cells to those factors appears to be a major culprit in exostosis formation. **They note** that knowledge of this could potentially find a pathway for the treatment of MHE in the future.
