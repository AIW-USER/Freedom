## Question

A 59-year-old female was involved in a motor vehicle collision (MVC)in which she sustained a pelvic ring injury that will require surgical fixation. On computed tomography (CT) imaging the patient is found to have a steep sacral alar slope and large irregular neural foramina. Which of the following is true of iliosacral fixation in this patient?

## Options

1. Safe zone at S1 is more horizontally oriented than a normal sacrum
2. Safe zone at S2 is more horizontally oriented than a normal sacrum
3. Safe zone at S1 is larger compared to a normal sacrum
4. Safe zone at S2 is larger compared to a normal sacrum
5. Percutaneous iliosacral fixation is contraindicated in sacral dysmorphism

## Correct option

**4. Safe zone at S2 is larger compared to a normal sacrum**

## Answer explanation

This patient has CT findings consistent with sacral dysmorphism. The safe zone in S2 is significantly larger in patients with sacral dysmorphism compared to normal sacra.

Sacral dysmorphism is a constellation of radiographic features that differ from a normal sacrum and include anterior up-sloping upper sacral ala, irregular sacral nerve root tunnels, residual S1 disk and a tongue-and-groove sacral iliac joint. These findings are present in 20-40% of the population and are of significant importance for safely placing iliosacral screws. The dysmorphic sacral anatomy has consistent, yet varying safe zones for screw placement. The safe zone at S1 is smaller and more obliquely oriented than in normal sacra. At S2 the angle of the safe zone is no different, however, the cross-sectional area of the safe zone is significantly larger in the dysmorphic sacrum. This larger S2 corridor may represent the primary fixation opportunity in patients with sacral dysmorphism.

*Miller et al. review variations in sacral morphology and its impact on iliosacral screw fixation.* **They report** that the dysmorphic sacrum has several key features including the presence of mammillary bodies, anterior upper sacral foramina that are not circular, residual upper sacral disks and an acute alar slope. Furthermore, **they highlight** that on the outlet view the upper portion of the sacrum is more colinear with the iliac crest compared to a normal sacrum. **They conclude** these characteristics must be identified and well understood for safe placement of iliosacral screws.

*Conflitti et al. review the radiographic features of sacral dysmorphism as it related to iliosacral screw insertion.* **They report** that the S2 segment provides a larger osseous site for screw insertion than S1 in the dysmorphic sacrum. **They conclude** that S2 iliosacral screws can be safely and accurately accomplished using a standard technique in patients with sacral dysmorphism.

*Gardner et al. review the safe zones for iliosacral screw fixation in normal and dysmorphic sacra.* **They report** that the S1 safe zone is significantly smaller and more obliquely oriented in the dysmorphic sacrum. Conversely, the S2 safe zone in a dysmorphic sacrum maintains the same horizontal angle, but is significantly larger than in a normal sacrum. **They conclude** that these findings are atypical, but consistent and of great importance when placing iliosacral screws.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The safe zone for S1 is more oblique oriented in dysmorphic sacra.
Answer 2: There is no difference in the horizontal orientation of S2 between normal and dysmorphic sacra.
Answer 3: A dysmorphic sacrum has a smaller S1 safe zone.
Answer 5: Percutaneous iliosacral fixation is not contraindicated in patients with sacral dysmorphism. However, the anatomical differences must be identified for safe placement.
