Chapter: Spine MCQs — Spondylolisthesis

## Question

Progress in Spondylolisthesis corelates with

## Options

1. Slip angle
2. Sacral slope
3. Pelvic Incidence
4. Lumbosacral angle

## Correct option

**3. Pelvic Incidence**

## Answer explanation

Ans: C

Progression in Spondylolisthesis corelates with all but Pelvic Incidence can be taken as the most important of these.

A perfect spinopelvic alignment is important to maintain an energy efficient posture. Spinopelvic alignment evaluation requires evaluation of both GLOBAL (overall) and regional parameters (Pelvic parameters and local kyphosis at pathology site).

The most common measure of GLOBAL sagittal alignment evaluation is the Sagittal Vertical Axis (SVA) that is measured as the horizontal distance between a plumb line drawn from center of C7 and a line drawn from center of C7 to posterior superior corner of S1. Radovanovic et al. found that a sagittal vertical axis (SVA) of over 50 mm was associated with worse outcomes following decompression and fusion for degenerative spondylolisthesis.

![[TO17135844-1.jpg]]

Pelvic parameters (PI, SS and PT) are equally important to be considered as a perfect spinopelvic alignment requires a balanced pelvis also.

Now, a balanced pelvis would have a high sacral slope and low pelvic tilt whereas an unbalanced pelvis has a low sacral slope and high pelvic tilt. And the corelation of these dynamic indices is best synchronized as the Pelvic Incidence (the answer in the question).

![[TO17135844-2.png]]

Slip angle and Lumbosacral angle also have been studied in the context but they are measures of regional kyphosis in spondylolisthesis.

The slip angle is a measure of the local kyphosis at the L5-S1 level. It is defined as the angle formed between a line perpendicular to the posterior aspect of the upper sacrum and a line parallel to the L5 inferior endplate. The slip angle has been found to have some predictive value for slip progression when it is larger than 30 degrees.

![[TO17135844-3.jpg]]

The lumbosacral angle is defined as the angle formed by the intersection of a line perpendicular to the upper sacrum and a line parallel to the upper endplate of the L5 vertebra. It has been found to have some predictive value for progression when it is larger than 10 degrees.
