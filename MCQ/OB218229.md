## Question

As a medical student, you are rotating with the upper extremity surgeon in the office. You see a patient that is being scheduled to undergo a cubital tunnel decompression. You are asked about the anatomy of the cubital tunnel. Which of the following structures makes up the floor of the cubital tunnel?

## Options

1. Osborne's ligament
2. Anterior band of the anterior bundle of the UCL
3. Posterior band of the anterior bundle of the UCL
4. Posterior bundle of the UCL
5. Transverse bundle of the UCL

## Correct option

**4. Posterior bundle of the UCL**

## Answer explanation

The floor of the cubital tunnel is the posterior bundle (posterior oblique ligament) of the medial ulnar collateral ligament.

The medial ulnar collateral ligament (MUCL) is divided into three components: the anterior bundle, the posterior bundle, and the transverse bundle. The anterior bundle is the strongest and most significant stabilizer of valgus stress. It courses from the anteroinferior ridge on the medial epicondyle to 2.8mm distal to the ulna articular margin on the sublime tubercle. It has a mean length of 54mm and is subdivided into anterior and posterior bands. The anterior band is the primary restraint to valgus stress, exhibiting nearly isometric strain during elbow ROM, while the posterior band exhibits increasing strain during higher degrees of elbow flexion. The posterior bundle demonstrates the greatest change in tension from flexion to extension, being tighter in flexion. It makes up the floor of the cubital tunnel. The transverse ligament has no contribution to stability as it does not cross the ulnohumeral joint.

*Morrey et al. reviewed the dissections of 10 fresh cadaver specimens.* **They reported** that the humeral origin of the medial ulnar collateral ligament attachments were found to lie posterior to the axis of elbow flexion; in this position, a cam effect is created so that ligament tension varies with elbow flexion. **They concluded** that the distance of the posterior portion increased by about 9 mm from 60 degrees to 120 degrees of flexion.

*Callaway et al. reviewed the anatomical dissection and biomechanical testing of 28 cadaveric elbows in order to determine the role of the medial ulnar collateral ligament.* **They reported** that the anterior bundle was, in turn, composed of anterior and posterior bands that tightened in a reciprocal fashion as the elbow was flexed and extended. **They also noted** that the posterior bundle was a secondary restraint at 30 degrees only. **They concluded** that the posterior bundle was not vulnerable to valgus overload unless the anterior bundle was completely disrupted.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The cubital tunnel retinaculum (aka Osborne's ligament) is the roof of the cubital tunnel
Answers 2,3,&5: The floor of the cubital tunnel is the posterior bundle (aka posterior oblique ligament) of the MUCL
