## Question

An orthopaedic surgeon is approached by a new implant company regarding performing a randomized control trial using their newly designed total knee arthroplasty (TKA) implant. The surgeon agrees and is paid fair market value to compare the new TKA implant to a standard alternative. The surgeon discloses the company payment and obtains informed consent from all participants. The study is registered with the National Institute of Health. The study protocol is maintained in a regulatory binder in the surgeon’s office that is kept up to date. The company provides one of its statisticians for data analysis and the generation of results tables. The findings are published in a large, indexed journal with the surgeon’s disclosures provided. Which of the following was considered inappropriate for research conduct?

## Options

1. Registering the trial with the National Institute of Health (NIH)
2. Having a physical regulatory binder held in the physician’s office
3. The surgeon being paid by a company to perform research on their implant
4. The company’s statistician performing data analysis and generating final tables
5. Publishing the findings in a large, indexed journal

## Correct option

**4. The company’s statistician performing data analysis and generating final tables**

## Answer explanation

It would be considered inappropriate for a private, company-funded clinical trial to have a company statistician perform data analysis and generate final tables. This would introduce an unacceptable level of bias, as the statistician has the implicit motive to manipulate the data to provide skewed, positive results.

Conflicts of interest in medical research can be difficult to manage, particularly in orthopaedic surgery where technological innovations occur at such a rapid pace. Public disclosures play an important role in preventing and displaying potential bias in published research. Many surgeons are paid by companies for reasons including, but not limited to: (1) speaking on a company’s behalf promoting the benefits of their implants (2) providing input on designing/improving a company’s implants, or (3) performing clinical trials using a company’s implants. In accordance with the Sunshine Act, all payments made to physicians from companies are required, by law, to be publicly disclosed to the Center for Medicare and Medicaid Services (CMS). While these regulations help make the public aware of potential biases in the interpretation of research findings, there are other areas of academics without such restrictions. Investigators should do all they can to prevent the introduction of bias in funded studies (or all studies for that matter), even when it is preventing bias from themselves. In this case, the company’s statistician has an implicit bias toward providing skewed positive results even if they are an upstanding citizen. For this reason, a blinded independent statistician should be utilized to provide unbiased results.

*Slover and colleagues provided an overview of the basic principles for conducting human research in orthopaedic surgery.* *They discuss some of the various state/federal laws and ethical principles which guard against compromising patient information and keeping them safe in clinical trials.* *The authors further provide an overview of the requirements of the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) and how to appropriately navigate this complex clinical and regulatory environment.*

Okike and Kocher provided case examples highlighting legal and ethical issues surrounding financial conflict of interest in orthopaedic research. They argue that the prioritization of financial interest over patient welfare or scientific objectivity is objective in nature and should not be taken lightly. They conclude that the Association of American Medical Colleges provides guidelines for the oversight of individual financial interests in human subjects research that should be incorporated into the rules/regulations of all academic institutions.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: In accordance with the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) federal law, all clinical trials that utilize an implanted device are required to be registered online (ClinicalTrials.Gov).
Answer 2: While not mandated by law, most institutions require a physical regulatory binder to be maintained for each individual study protocol at that institution. It is usually part of the research compliance program. The regulatory binder should contain all investigators’ education, IRB training certificates, and monitoring information in order to ensure all personnel on the study (including the principal investigator) are qualified and that all duties being conducted are compliant with the law and local institutional requirements.
Answer 3: While being paid has the potential to introduce bias, it is considered fair and appropriate for an investigator to be paid for their time to perform research for a private company. However, the amount should be appropriate at a fair market value. By law, all research-related payments, and other transfers of value, to physicians and teaching hospitals must be reported to the Center for Medicare and Medicaid Services (CMS).
Answer 5: Publishing the findings of ANY study is highly encouraged, regardless if there is a positive or negative outcome. This is a large gray area in research, as privately funded studies are not required to publish results to the public regardless of findings (i.e. publishing bias). The only time these privately funded studies must report information to the FDA is when there is a serious adverse outcome. Essentially these privately funded studies can choose to publish only positive results. Government-funded studies (i.e. the NIH or Department of Defense), on the other hand, are required to publish results regardless of outcomes.
