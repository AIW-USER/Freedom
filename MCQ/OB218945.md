## Question

A 42-year-old recreational beach volleyball player is referred to your clinic by his primary care physician. The patient states that he has right-sided posterior ankle pain which is keeping him from being able to complete his conditioning runs. The patient reports that he had similar symptoms a few years ago while training for a marathon. On exam, the patient has tenderness to palpation along the Achilles tendon, and his posterior ankle is swollen when compared to the unaffected extremity. You prescribe the patient rest and provide him with a CAM boot as needed for symptomatic relief. Six weeks later, the patient reports that his pain has improved, and he is eager to begin physical therapy. Which of the following movements is important for the patient to perform as part of an appropriate rehabilitation program?

## Options

1. Performing bilateral box-jumps from the ground up to a 16-inch box
2. While sitting on the ground, utilizing both legs to plantar flex the feet against a wall with maximal effort for 15 seconds at a time
3. While standing on a one-inch board, utilizing both legs to achieve a maximally plantar flexed ankle position, then lowering down slowly with the right leg only
4. While standing on a one-inch board, utilizing the right leg only to slowly achieve a maximally plantar flexed ankle position, then lowering down quickly with both legs
5. It is inappropriate to progress rehabilitation at this time

## Correct option

**3. While standing on a one-inch board, utilizing both legs to achieve a maximally plantar flexed ankle position, then lowering down slowly with the right leg only**

## Answer explanation

This patient has signs and symptoms of Achilles tendonitis. After a brief period of rest, rehabilitation with a focus on eccentric strengthening (Answer 3) represents the backbone of the appropriate rehabilitative strategy for Achilles tendonitis.

Achilles tendinitis is initially treated with nonoperative means including activity modification, shoe wear modification, brief immobilization, and physical therapy once the patient's symptoms have partially abated. The backbone of rehabilitation for this condition is a combination of eccentric training and gastrocnemius-soleus stretching. Corticosteroid injections should be avoided due to the risk of tendon rupture. Only after failure of an appropriate course of nonoperative management should surgery be considered. Thankfully, a majority of patients achieve complete resolution with appropriate nonoperative management.

*O'Neill et al. provide a review of eccentric exercise in the setting of Achilles tendonitis.* **The authors note** that despite the ample evidence which supports the use of eccentric exercises for the management of Achilles tendonitis, the mechanism by which the exercise modality exerts its effects remains unknown. **The authors note** that the most likely mechanism for eccentric exercise efficacy is a change in neuromuscular output, which causes an increase in the strength and stiffness of the muscle-tendon unit and a shift in the length-tension curve which better prepares the tendon to adapt to load.

*Alfredson et al. provided a seminal paper on the management of Achilles tendonitis through eccentric exercise.* *Their study reports on 15 recreational athletes with a mean age of 44.3 years.* *The authors utilized a 12-week rehabilitation program.* At week 0, all patients had Achilles pain which prevented them from performing their athletic pursuit of choice. After the 12-week training period, all 15 patients had returned to their pre-injury level of athletic activity. These 15 athletes were compared to a control group of 15 similar individuals who performed standard physical therapy without an emphasis on eccentric strengthening. None of these 15 patients were able to return to their pre-injury level of activity, and all were eventually treated surgically. **The authors conclude** that an eccentric-heavy rehabilitation program is highly-effective in managing and treating Achilles tendonitis in middle-aged athletes.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: This describes plyometric exercise, which is not the appropriate exercise modality for Achilles tendonitis at this time.
Answer 2: This describes isometric exercise, which is not the appropriate exercise modality for Achilles tendonitis at this time.
Answer 4: This describes concentric exercise, which is not the appropriate exercise modality for Achilles tendonitis at this time.
Answer 5: The patient has undergone rest and activity modification, and is ready to progress onto eccentric strengthening.
