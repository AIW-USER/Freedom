## Question

A surgeon is performing an arthroscopic osteocapsular arthroplasty on a 54-year-old golfer with severe elbow pain due to chronic degenerative changes. When performing the anterior capsulectomy, presuming that the surgeon is in the appropriate plane, which of the following structures serves to protect the radial nerve proximal to the level of the radial neck?

## Options

1. Anconeus
2. Brachioradialis
3. Brachialis
4. Distal biceps tendon
5. Pronator teres

## Correct option

**3. Brachialis**

## Answer explanation

The brachialis provides approximately 4mm of protection to the overlying radial nerve during an arthroscopic elbow anterior capsulectomy proximal to the level of the radial neck.

Elbow arthroscopy is technically demanding and can be challenging if not performed regularly. In young patients with significant degenerative elbow changes in whom a total elbow arthroplasty would not be appropriate or in those with significant elbow contractures, arthroscopic releases may be indicated. In order to perform them safely in an arthroscopic fashion, a thorough understanding of the neurovascular anatomy around the elbow is paramount. When performing an anterior capsulectomy, for instance, it is important to understand the course of the radial nerve as it crosses the joint line, given that it courses longitudinally, medial to the capitellum with the brachialis muscle providing ~4 mm or greater of protection between nerve and anterior capsule up until the radial neck. Thus, it has been demonstrated that the most dangerous area for capsular resection is distally over the radial head/neck, leading to the recommendation that the anterior capsule should be transected at least 2 to 3 cm proximal to the level of the radial head to take advantage of the protective brachialis muscle and to avoid potential direct damage to the radial nerve.

*Keener et al. provide a review article on the arthroscopic management of the stiff elbow.* **The authors note** that recent technical advances in elbow arthroscopy have led to the development of minimally invasive procedures for the management of select cases of recalcitrant elbow stiffness, but as with most arthroscopic procedures, a notable learning curve is associated with the safe, effective execution of these surgical techniques. **They conclude** that arthroscopic capsular release of the elbow is effective for restoring a functional arc of motion in the short term in most patients with extrinsic contractures.

*Omid et al. published on the relation of the radial nerve to the anterior capsule of the elbow during elbow arthroscopy.* *The authors performed elbow arthroscopy on 24 cadavers and found that in all specimens the radial nerve coursed longitudinally medial to the capitellum, with the brachialis muscle found to lie between the radial nerve and the joint capsule at the joint line and all proximal levels with a thickness of 4 mm or greater at the joint line and at all proximal measurement points.* **They concluded** that at the level of the radiocapitellar joint line, the radial nerve runs medial to the capitellum and that the brachialis muscle lies between the radial nerve and the joint capsule at the level of the joint line and proximally.

*Batko et al. reviewed complications in elbow arthroscopy and proposed solutions for management and prevention.* **The authors note** that although less common than the ulnar nerve, injuries to the radial nerve can occur at reported rates between 0% and 6.6%, given that the radial nerve passes within 15mm of the arthroscope at the level of the anterolateral portal. **They propose** that joint insufflation helps reduce radial nerve injury and, in addition, starting with an anteromedial or proximal medial portal and then creating a lateral portal with a spinal needle under direct visualization with the camera can help avoid injury to the radial nerve.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1, 2, 4, and 5: Only the brachialis muscle provides direct protection to the radial nerve during an anterior capsulectomy when an appropriate plane is used proximal to the level of the radial head/neck.
