## Question

All of these arteries can make up the Corona mortis except: [Trauma-Pelvis and Acetabular Fracture-Pelvic Ring Fracture]

## Options

1. Internal pudendal artery
2. External iliac artery
3. Obturator artery
4. Inferior epigastric artery

## Correct option

**1. Internal pudendal artery**

## Answer explanation

The Corona Mortis is a common vascular variant between the external and internal iliac (through their branches, which are inferior epigastric and the obturator). Internal pudendal artery is a branch of Internal iliac artery but it does not take part in the anastomosis.

CORONA MORTIS

The Corona Mortis (CMOR), also known as the “crown of death”, serves to represent the high risk of hemorrhage that may occur if this anatomical variant is injured and the difficulty of achieving hemostasis. Corona mortis is a vascular connection between the external and internal iliac arteries. This collateral circulation occurs due to either the inferior epigastric or accessory obturator artery arising from the external iliac artery joining the obturator artery arising from the internal iliac artery. Usually, the obturator artery arises from the anterior trunk of the internal iliac artery. Occasionally, it may arise from the external iliac or femoral artery instead of the internal iliac artery.

Its location on the superior pubic ramus is variable. It is about 3-7cms from the symphysis pubis. It is located behind the superior pubic ramus and hence during surgery care has to be taken during lateral dissection along superior pubic ramus. The Corona mortis is susceptible to injury in pelvic trauma and in pelvic surgery. This structure presents a challenge to the surgeon and a hazard to the patient during Anterior approaches to the pelvis such as with the Ilioinguinal approach and Modified Stoppa approach.
