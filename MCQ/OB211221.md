## Question

A 44-year-old male presents with a comminuted 4-part right radial head fracture. The decision is made to proceed with a radial head arthroplasty. Which of the following described surgical intervals would likely provide the least likelihood of injury to the lateral ulnar collateral ligament?

## Options

1. Anconeus and ECU
2. Anconeus and ECRB
3. EDC and ECRB
4. Anconeus and EDC
5. ECU and EDC

## Correct option

**3. EDC and ECRB**

## Answer explanation

A Kaplan approach to the elbow, which utilizes a more anterior interval (the EDC and ECRB) will provide the most protection against injury to the LUCL.

The Kaplan approach to the elbow is a commonly used lateral approach to the elbow for the treatment of radial head and distal humerus injuries. The intermuscular approach of this interval is the ECRB (radial nerve or PIN) and EDC (PIN). The benefit of this approach is that it is more anterior and has a lesser likelihood of injury to the LUCL. However, this approach also poses more risk to the PIN and care should be taken to keep the forearm pronated during the approach to help minimize injury to the nerve.

*Mathew et al. reviewed terrible triad injuries of the elbow.* **They note** that historically, the combination of an elbow dislocation, a radial head fracture, and a coronoid process fracture almost always rendered the elbow unstable, making surgical fixation necessary. **They note** that the primary goal of surgical fixation is to stabilize the elbow to permit early motion. **They conclude** that recent literature has improved our understanding of elbow anatomy and biomechanics along with the pathoanatomy of this injury, thereby allowing the development of a systematic approach for treatment and rehabilitation.

*Ring et al. performed a retrospective review of 56 patients who underwent ORIF of radial head fractures with 48 months followup.* Thirty patients had a Mason Type-2 (partial articular) fracture, and 26 had a Mason Type-3 (complete articular) fracture. **They noted** that 15/30 of the Mason-2 fractures were comminuted and 14/26 of the type-3 fractures consisted of more than three fragments. **They noted** that 4/15 of the comminuted Mason type 2 fractures had unsatisfactory results with less < 100 degrees ROM. Furthermore, 13/14 patients with a Mason Type-3 fractures with more than three articular fragments had an unsatisfactory result. In contrast, **they noted** that all 15 patients with an isolated, noncomminuted Type-2 fracture had a satisfactory result with ORIF. **The authors conclude** that ORIF is best reserved for minimally comminuted fractures with three or fewer articular fragments.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The anconeus and ECU interval (Kocher approach) is a more posterior approach to the lateral elbow and places the LUCL at increased risk of injury compared to the Kaplan approach
Answer 2, 4 and 5: These are not described surgical intervals
