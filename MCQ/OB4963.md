## Question

A 6-year-old, skeletally immature girl presents to the ED for evaluation of low back pain for four weeks. She is an avid gymnast and trains daily. She has no history of trauma. She is neurologically intact on examination but has pain with hyperextension and flexion of her back, and is tender to palpation over her lumbar spine. Her labs are notable for WBC of 11.8 (reference range 4.2-11.0) and elevated ESR and CRP. Figure A shows an MRI of her lumbar spine. Which of the following is true regarding this patient's condition?

![[q4963-a-pediatricdiscitis.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Urgent debridement is necessary to prevent neurological deterioriation
2. Bracing and activity restriction is the first line of treatment
3. Surgery is reserved for patients that do not improve with bracing and activity restriction
4. Management with chemotherapeutic agents typically leads to resolution
5. Management with intravenous antibiotics typically leads to resolution

## Correct option

**5. Management with intravenous antibiotics typically leads to resolution**

## Answer explanation

The patient's clinical presentation and MRI is most consistent with discitis/osteomyelitis. Management with intravenous antibiotics typically leads to resolution of symptoms.

Clinical presentation of pediatric discitis depends on the age of the child with toddlers typically limping or unwilling to walk and older children presenting with back pain and point tenderness. Staph aureus is the causative agent in over 80% of cases. Initial treatment is with bedrest and IV antibiotics targeting staph aureus. If there is no clinical response in 7-10 days, biopsy is warranted. Surgical debridement is reserved for patients with neurological deficit and those not responding to conservative measures.

*Ring et al. reviewed outcomes of immobilization and antibiotic treatment in childhood discitis.* **They found** that regardless of use of immobilization, treatment with intravenous antibiotics leads to better outcomes than oral or no antibiotics.

*Early et al. reviewed the clinical presentation and management of childhood discitis.* **They suggest** the majority of children improve rapidly within 4-6 weeks of intravenous antibiotics. Immobilization can help without symptomatic relief and decrease deformity when osseous destruction is present.

Figure A is a T2 sagittal MRI showing a L2-L3 discitis and adjacent osteomyelitis in the vertebral body.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Debridement is reserved for patients who develop epidural abscess with neurological deficit or do not improve with antibiotic therapy
Answer 2: Bracing and activity restriction is first line treatment for spondylolysis
Answer 3: This is true for spondylolysis, not for discitis
Answer 4: Chemotherapy would be indicated for lymphoma
