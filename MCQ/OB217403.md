## Question

A 46-year-old male is seen in the office and indicated for revision spine surgery. He underwent initial correction of an adult spinal deformity 18 months ago that extends from T6-S1. If his complication is related to the most common cause of reoperation, what would you expect to see on workup of this patient?

## Options

1. Heterogenous fluid collection identified on MRI
2. Implant fracture
3. Degenerative disease of the adjacent segment superiorly
4. Elevated inflammatory markers
5. Inability to dorsiflex the ankle

## Correct option

**2. Implant fracture**

## Answer explanation

Implant failure is the most common cause of reoperation following adult spinal deformity correction.

Degenerative scoliosis results from the asymmetric degeneration of disc space and/or facet joints in the spine. Patients who continue to have debilitating pain or progressive neurologic compromise may require correction in the form of spinal fusion. Complications following these large cases vary between 7-25% in the literature. The most common complication is hardware failure, which may be related to pseudoarthrosis. Other complications that occur less commonly are infection and proximal junctional failure.

*Crawford et al review prevalence and indications for unplanned reoperations following adult spinal deformity surgery.* **They report** that 24% of patients required a repoperation with implant failure being the most common indication (62%). **They conclude** that efforts should be made to decrease this important complications as rates of failure will likely increase with longer term follow up.

*Pitter et al review the revision risk following adult spinal deformity correction.* **They report** that 19.9% were revised within the first two years and that 7.2% of those patients were revised more than once. **They conclude** that implant failure was the most common cause of revision surgery.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Fluid collection 18 months following surgery may be indicative of an infection, but would not necessarily indicate hardware failure.
Answer 3: Proximal junctional failure is the second most common cause of revision surgery.
Answer 4: Implant failure is more common than infection for the cause of revision following adult spinal deformity correction.
Answer 5: Neurologic compromise is not a common cause of reoperation.
