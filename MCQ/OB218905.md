## Question

A 27-year-old patient presents to your office with complaints of right wrist pain, which is worst with forearm rotation and ulnar deviation. Your office staff inquires about what radiographs you would like them to obtain. Which of the following is true when comparing a PA and AP view of the wrist?

## Options

1. AP view more sensitive for detecting scaphoid fracture
2. Ulnar variance is more positive on PA view
3. Less radial inclination seen on PA view
4. PA view better demonstrates lunate facet of distal radius
5. DRUJ is better visualized on AP view

## Correct option

**2. Ulnar variance is more positive on PA view**

## Answer explanation

Ulnar variance comes more positive on the posteroanterior (PA) wrist radiograph compared to the anteroposterior (AP) view.

Ulnar variance is represented by the relationship between the distal ulna and the radius at the wrist. The degree of ulnar variance changes based on the position where the wrist radiograph is obtained. With the wrist in relative pronation to obtain a PA view, ulnar variance becomes more positive, whereas with the wrist in supination to obtain an AP view, the ulnar variance becomes more negative. With positive ulnar variance, ulnar abutment with the lunate may occur which can also lead to associated TFCC tearing or development of arthrosis. In order to obtain the most neutral ulnar variance possible radiographically, the shoulder can be abducted to 90 degrees and the elbow flexed to 90 degrees.

*Acott and Greenberg reviewed ulnar abutment syndrome in athletes,* **noting** that the condition occurs with excessive loading of the ulnar wrist through the TFCC and ulnocarpal joints. Both static and dynamic versions of ulnar abutment can occur and these can be represented during different radiographic views. The goal of surgical management in these patients is to unload the ulnocarpal joint which can be done by performing an ulnar-shortening osteotomy.

*DaSilva et al. reviewed ulnar-sided wrist pain for the JAAOS.* **They noted** that determining the cause of ulnar-sided wrist pain can be challenging, as there are multiple bony and ligamentous structures at play. They discuss physical exam maneuvers and diagnostic imaging that can assist in diagnosis and also mention the importance of wrist arthroscopy from a diagnostic and therapeutic standpoint.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Scaphoid fractures may be detected on AP or PA views of the wrist although a PA view is typically recommended.
Answer 3: Radial inclination does not significantly differ between the AP and PA views, although it is typically measured on the AP view.
Answer 4: The distal radius lunate facet can be identified on PA and AP views.
Answer 5: The DRUJ can be visualized on the PA or AP views.
