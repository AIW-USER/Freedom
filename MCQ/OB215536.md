## Answer explanation

Figure B represents a supination-adduction type injury that resulted in a vertical shear fracture of the medial malleolus. The plate indicated by the arrow alone is serving as a buttress for the shear component of the fracture. With the addition of the independent lag screw outside of the plate, which is also secured distally through the fracture site, the buttress plate is thereby serving the dual function of buttressing and neutralization.

While there are many different designs and dimensions of plates, plate function can be largely determined based on surgical technique. There are five key functions or “modes” a plate can have, which are compression, buttressing, tension band, bridging, and neutralization/protection. Neutralization plating is used to neutralize torsional and bending forces at a fracture following lag screw fixation. If a simple fracture pattern is reduced with a lag screw and left as is, the fracture would be highly susceptible to rotational strain and failure. Therefore, the lag screw(s) are protected with the addition of a plate in order to protect or neutralize against any shearing or rotational forces. Buttress plating is used for fracture fragments that are displaced secondarily to shear forces, which creates a vertically oriented fracture pattern. Applying a buttress effect relies on the application of an under-contoured plate. The so-called axillary screw is placed in the stable segment of bone just adjacent to the apex of the fracture where the plate is not contacting bone. Tightening this screw pushes the under-contoured plate down to the bone generating compression while also preventing longitudinal translation of the fractured fragment.

Lauge-Hansen provides a review of ankle joint biomechanics and the mechanisms of ankle fracture patterns by using fresh cadavers and manual stress. He describes common patterns of ankle fractures according to a dual designation, which denotes the position of the foot followed by the vector of stress applied at the moment of injury.

Illustration A is the Lauge-Hansen classification of ankle fractures.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Figure A demonstrates an oblique distal fibula fracture fixed with an antiglide posterolateral plate. This construct is not acting through neutralization because it is not protecting any independent lag screws. While antiglide and buttressing are often used interchangeably during fixation of shearing type fracture patterns, there are subtle differences in connotation. Both act to oppose the direction of displacement of a fracture but differ in how this is accomplished. As described above, buttress plating relies on the application of an under contoured plate to primarily provide compression as a means of preventing displacement. When applying a plate to function as an antiglide plate, the plate is not contoured. Rather, the plate is initially fixed proximally (in the case of a lateral malleolus fracture) with cortical screws on the side that corresponds with the apex of the fracture, or the direction of displacement (e.g. posterolateral in the case of a SER pattern). This will often reduce the displaced fragment in of itself. It is the position of the plate that acts to prevent shortening (or "gliding") of the fractured segment, rather than compression from an under-contoured plate.
Answer 3: Figure C represents a bridge plate. Rather than obtaining anatomic reduction and compression of individual fracture fragments, a bridge plate provides support above and below the fractured fragments. This results in relative stability by allowing some motion and callus formation.
Answer 4: Figure D represents a plate functioning as a tension band. A tension band converts tensile forces into compression forces. The following criteria must be fulfilled for a plate to act as a tension band: The fractured bone must be eccentrically loaded; The plate must be placed on the tension side; The plate must be able to withstand the tensile forces; The bone must be able to withstand the compressive force which results from the conversion of distraction forces by the plate.
Answer 5: Figure E represents a short oblique fibula fracture. As demonstrated by the order of screw placement on the left side of the illustration, this plate functions as a compression plate. The first screw was placed proximal to the fracture site where the fracture angle is >90 degrees from the plate. By doing so, this creates an “axilla” into which the other fragment will be compressed. The second screw (which was inserted into the fragment with a fracture angle <90 degrees) was placed eccentrically. Tightening the second screw will cause compression at the fracture site. Of note, if the order was reversed, it would risk shortening at the fracture site.
