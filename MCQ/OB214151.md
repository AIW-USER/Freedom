## Question

Which of the following joints is at the lowest risk for concurrent septic arthritis in the setting of metaphyseal osteomyelitis in a pediatric patient?

## Options

1. Shoulder
2. Elbow
3. Hip
4. Knee
5. Ankle

## Correct option

**4. Knee**

## Answer explanation

Intra-capsular metaphyses are located at the shoulder (proximal humerus), elbow (proximal radius), hip (proximal femur), and ankle (distal fibula). Septic arthritis of these joints can arise from metaphyseal osteomyelitis.

Pediatric metaphyses are predisposed to bacterial deposition due to the turbulent blood flow from sharp turns of capillaries prior to entering venous sinusoids. The risk of bacterial spread to a nearby joint is increased when the metaphysis is intracapsular. Early diagnosis and treatment are vital to prevent joint destruction and morbidity.

*Arkader et al. reviewed pediatric musculoskeletal infections including septic arthritis.* **The authors note** that primary septic arthritis is the most common type of septic arthritis and results from hematogenous spread or direct inoculation. Secondary septic arthritis results from the spread of osteomyelitis in the joint and occurs when the metaphysis is intracapsular. Children are often younger than 2 years old and the hip is reported as the most common location. **The authors conclude** that targeted antibiotic therapy and joint decompression, either open or arthroscopic, with debridement, are the mainstay of treatment.

*Schallert et al. retrospectively evaluated pediatric metaphyseal osteomyelitis to determine the role of various MRI findings in predicting coexisting septic arthritis.* **The authors found** joint effusions in 55 cases (53%) of metaphyseal osteomyelitis, 41 (40%) of which had surgically confirmed septic arthritis. **They conclude** patients with a joint effusion identified by MRI in the setting of metaphyseal osteomyelitis should be presumed to have septic arthritis until proven otherwise, particularly at sites of intra-articular metaphyses.

Incorrect Answers
Answers 1, 2, 4 & 5: The shoulder, elbow, hip, and ankle are all at increased risk of septic arthritis from concomitant spread from their respective intra-articular metaphyses.
