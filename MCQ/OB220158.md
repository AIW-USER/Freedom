## Question

Figure A is the lateral radiograph of a 65-year-old female who underwent right TKA 5 years ago. Over the weekend, she sustained a ground-level fall when she slipped and landed on the first stair entering her apartment. Your colleague suggests extensor mechanism reconstruction utilizing a polypropylene synthetic graft. Which of the following is true of this type of reconstruction?

![[q220158-a-extensor2.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Lower rates of deep prosthetic joint infection compared to allograft reconstruction
2. Lower rates of wound complications compared to allograft reconstruction
3. Larger extensor lag compared to allograft reconstruction
4. Higher all cause revision rate when compared to allograft reconstruction
5. Less cost compared to allograft reconstruction

## Correct option

**5. Less cost compared to allograft reconstruction**

## Answer explanation

The literature provides mixed results regarding whether polypropylene synthetic grafts have superior results compared to allograft extensor mechanism reconstruction; however, polypropylene synthetic grafts (such as Marlex mesh) are a more affordable and available option than using allograft tissues for extensor mechanism reconstruction following TKA.

Extensor mechanism disruption is a devastating complication following TKA and may result from trauma, diabetes, chronic steroid use, multiple
knee operations, and iatrogenic injury. Numerous allograft reconstruction techniques have been described, however, problems with graft stretching, graft mismatch, availability and cost have also been observed. Synthetic reconstruction utilizing polypropylene grafts reduces cost. Some studies suggest polypropylene grafts have improved outcomes and lower revision rates, while others have less promising results.

*Deren et al. reviewed extensor mechanism disruption following TKA and compared allograft and synthetic reconstructive techniques.* **They reported** that the overall success rate of allograft was 73% and not significantly different from that of synthetic mesh. **They noted** that there was no significant difference in revision rates, infection, or outcome scores between allograft and synthetic material. **They concluded** that both techniques are equivalent in treating this condition.

*Abdel et al. review extensor mechanism reconstruction utilizing Marlex mesh.* **They report** that at the latest follow-up, 84% of the mesh reconstructions were in place, and the functional outcomes were excellent. These patients had a mean improvement in extensor lag of 26°. **They conclude** that synthetic reconstruction is viable for this catastrophic complication following TKA.

*Wood et al. review synthetic reconstruction of the extensor mechanism compared to allograft reconstruction in patients with TKA.* **They reported** zero graft failures after synthetic reconstruction, with an overall revision surgery rate of 15%. Conversely, allograft graft failure was 21%, and the revision surgery rate was 43%. Synthetic grafts were also cheaper. **They conclude** that synthetic grafts afford improved outcomes at a lower cost.

Figure A shows the AP and lateral radiographs of a patient with a TKA who sustained an extensor mechanism rupture, as evident by significant patellar alta seen on the lateral view.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Deep infection rates of TKA are equivalent between allograft and synthetic reconstructions. Allografts can potentially result in disease transmission, but this is exceedingly rare.
Answer 2: A lower rate of wound complications with polypropylene synthetic grafts has not been demonstrated in the literature.
Answer 3: Larger extensor lags are seen following allograft reconstruction compared to synthetic materials.
Answer 4: Literature is mixed on revision rates, with some studies showing superior results with synthetic reconstruction, while others have shown equivalent failure rates.
