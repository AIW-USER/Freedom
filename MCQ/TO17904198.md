## Question

Which of the following ligaments connect Atlas to the Occiput?

## Options

1. Tectorial membrane
2. Ascending Cruciate ligament
3. Alar ligament
4. Transverse Atlantal ligament

## Correct option

**2. Ascending Cruciate ligament**

## Answer explanation

The cruciate ligament ascending limb connects the posterior surface of dens to the foramen magnum. It is also connected to the atlas vertebra via the transverse limbs and hence plays a role in the AO stability.

The tectorial membrane is a continuation of Posterior longitudinal ligament and connects the bodies of C2, C3 to the foramen magnum above. It does not connect to the atlas.

The alar ligaments connect the tip of the dens to the foramen magnum and do not connect to the atlas. The transverse atlantal ligaments are the horizontal bands (part of cruciate ligament) which adheres dens to the atlas vertebrae and donot play a role in the AO stability.

1. ligaments that connect the atlas to the occiput:

a) anterior atlantooccipital membrane: cephalad extension of the anterior longitudinal ligament. Extends from anterior margin of foramen magnum (FM) to anterior arch of C1

b)posterior atlantooccipital membrane: connects the posterior margin of the FM to posterior arch of C1

c) the ascending band of the cruciate ligament

2. ligaments that connect the axis (viz. the odontoid) to the occiput:

a) tectorial membrane: some authors distinguish 2 components

● superficial component: cephalad continuation of the posterior longitudinal ligament. A strong band connecting the dorsal surface of the dens to the ventral surface of the FM above, and dorsal surface of C2 & C3 bodies below

● accessory (deep) portion: located laterally, connects C2 to occipital condyles b) alar (“check”)ligaments

● occipito-alar portion: connects side of the dens to occipital condyle

● atlanto-alar portion: connects side of the dens to the lateral mass of C1

C) apical odontoid ligament: connects tip of dens to the FM. Little mechanical strength

3. ligaments that connect the axis to the atlas:

A) transverse atlantal ligament (TAL) or (usually) just transverse ligament: the horizontal component of the cruciate ligament. Attaches at the medial tubercles of C1. Traps the dens against the anterior atlas via a strap-like mechanism Provides the majority of the strength (“the strongest ligament of the spine”)

B) atlanto-alar portion of the alar ligaments

C) descending band of the cruciate ligament

Answer : b
