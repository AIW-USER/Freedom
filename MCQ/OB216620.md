## Question

Figures A and B are the radiographs of a 66-year-old female who underwent an uncomplicated right THA through a direct lateral approach 3 months ago. In comparing the patients pre and post-operative films, you recognize that the femoral offset was decreased by 2 mm, but her leg length was increased by 3 mm compared to preoperative films. Based on the current radiographs, which of the following is this patient most likely to exhibit?

![[q216620-a-screen-shot-2021-07-30-at-9-18-02-pm.jpg]]
*Figure A*

![[q216620-b-screen-shot-2021-07-30-at-9-18-07-pm.jpg]]
*Figure B*

## Options

1. Trochanteric bursitis
2. Instability
3. Iliopsoas tendonitis
4. Steppage gait
5. Meralgia paresthetica

## Correct option

**3. Iliopsoas tendonitis**

## Answer explanation

A malpositioned acetabular component that is prominent over the anterior edge of the acetabulum may cause iliopsoas tendinitis.

The iliopsoas arrises from the lumbar transverse processes and passes anterior to the hip joint prior to inserting on to the lesser trochanter. A malpositioned acetabular component with anterior overhang may irritate the ilipsoas tendon and be a cause of groin pain postoperatively. Patients often report pain with activities of hip flexion such as walking up stairs or getting in/out of the car. Treatment often involves a diagnostic and therapeutic injection in to the iliopsoas tendon sheath. If symptoms resolve, treatment consists of either acetabular revision or transection of the tendon.

*Lachiewicz et al review iliopsoas impingement following THA.* **They report** this to be an under recognized cause of postoperative groin pain. **They conclude** that identification of this problem is best done with advanced imaging following by diagnostic injection.

*O'Sullivan et al review iliopsoas impingement as a complication of THA.* **They report** that transection of the tendon resulted in excellent results in 15/16 patients. **They conclude** that this etiology should always be considered in the differential of a painful THA.

Figure A is an AP radiograph demonstrating a right THA in overall good alignment. Figure B is the cross table lateral of the same patient demonstrating anterior overhang of the acetabular component.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Trochanteric bursitis may occur following an increase in offset. This patient has very minimal offset as demonstrated on the AP radiograph.
Answer 2: The components appear to be positioned well in such a way that instability, especially following a lateral based approach would be unlikely.
Answer 4: Steppage gait occurs in patients with a foot drop, which there is no indication of in this patient. This would also be unlikely following an uncomplicated lateral approach.
Answer 5: Meralgia peristhetica occurs with damage to the lateral femoral cutaneous nerve, which may be injured during anterior based approaches.
