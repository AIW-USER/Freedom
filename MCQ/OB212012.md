## Question

Figure A is the radiograph of a 13-year-old boy who presents to your office for his first evaluation of recurrent ankle sprains. Which of the following treatment modalities is indicated at this time?

![[q212012-a-cn-coalition.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Immobilization with a short leg cast or boot for six-weeks
2. Coalition resection with extensor digitorum brevis interposition, +/- correction of associated foot deformity
3. Coalition resection with split flexor hallucis longus tendon interposition, +/- correction of associated foot deformity
4. Coalition resection with fat graft interposition, +/- correction of associated foot deformity
5. Subtalar arthrodesis

## Correct option

**1. Immobilization with a short leg cast or boot for six-weeks**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a calcaneonavicular coalition, as evident on the oblique radiograph. Initial management should be conservative with immobilization for six weeks.

Tarsal coalitions are a structural anomaly between two or three tarsal bones causing a rigid flatfoot. With a calcaneonavicular coalition, there is an elongated anterior process of calcaneus, showing an "anteater sign" on an oblique radiograph of the foot. Conversely, a talocalcaneal coalition will show a c-shaped arc formed by the medial outline of the talar dome and posteroinferior aspect of the sustentaculum tali on a lateral radiograph. With pediatric tarsal coalitions, initial management should be conservative and include a below-knee walking cast or boot for six-weeks, as some will become pain-free with a short period of immobilization.

*Mubarak et al. reviewed symptomatic calcaneonavicular coalitions treated with resection and interposition of fat graft.* **They report** that one year after resection, 87% of the patients returned to sport or their past activities, whereas 5% had symptomatic regrowth requiring repeat resection. Moreover, 74% had improvement of subtalar motion and 82% had improvement of plantarflexion. **They concluded** that reossification and reoperation rates with fat graft interposition are lower than in most published reports of EDB interposition.

*Gantsoudes et al. retrospectively reviewed outcomes of patients treated for symptomatic talocalcaneal coalition with resection and interposition of fat graft.* **They report** that at an average of 43 months, 90% of cases had excellent outcomes scores, with only a 3% recurrence rate. **They concluded** that a symptomatic talocalcaneal coalition can be treated with excision and fat graft interposition.

Figure A demonstrates a calcaneonavicular coalition, as evident by the "anteater sign." Illustration A is a "C-sign" seen with a talocalcaneal coalition which is a c-shaped arc formed by the medial outline of the talar dome and posteroinferior aspect of the sustentaculum tali

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 2&4: Coalition resection with extensor digitorum brevis or fat graft interposition in a reasonable treatment option for a symptomatic calcaneonavicular coalition that has failed nonoperative management
Answer 3: Coalition resection with split flexor hallucis longus tendon interposition is a reasonable option to treated a talocalcaneal coalition that has failed nonoperative management
Answer 5: A subtalar arthrodesis shoulder be considered if the coalition involves >50% of the joint surface of a talocalcaneal coalition
