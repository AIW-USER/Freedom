## Question

A 62-year-old male presents with acute onset left wrist pain. He denies any injury to the wrist. His past medical history is unremarkable and he denies any intravenous drug use. He is afebrile and pain is located only in the left wrist. Radiographs are unremarkable. The wrist is red, hot to the touch, and painful with motion. He undergoes an aspiration of the joint and is ultimately treated with anti-inflammatory medication for gout. Which of the following findings is consistent with gout on crystal analysis?

## Options

1. Negatively birefringent needle-shaped crystals
2. Negatively birefringent rhomboid-shaped crystals
3. Neutrally birefringent rhomboid-shaped crystals
4. Positively birefringent needle-shaped crystals
5. Positively birefringent rhomboid-shaped crystals

## Correct option

**1. Negatively birefringent needle-shaped crystals**

## Answer explanation

This patient is being treated for gout following an aspiration of the wrist joint. Crystal analysis consistent with gout will demonstrate negatively birefringent needle-shaped crystals.

Gout is a form of inflammatory arthritis caused by intra-articular monosodium urate crystal deposition that typically presents with recurrent acute exacerbation of joint swelling and severe pain. Diagnosis is made by joint aspiration and crystal analysis showing strongly negatively birefringent needle-shaped intracellular crystals. Treatment for acute gout includes NSAIDs with chronic gout being treated with allopurinol and medical management to reduce uric acid.

*Beck et al. reviewed the incidence of scapholunate (SL) dissociation in patients with aspiration-confirmed gout of the wrist and aspiration-confirmed gout in any joint excluding the wrist.* **They reported** that the incidence of SL dissociation in patients with gouty arthropathy of the wrist as 60% and 25%, respectively. **They concluded** that as a result of the systemic nature of gout, a patient with aspiration-confirmed gout at a joint remote from the wrist might have subclinical effects in the wrist, leading to SL dissociation over time.

*Eggebeen et al. reviewed gouty arthritis.* **They reported** that gout is caused by monosodium urate crystal deposition in tissues leading to arthritis, soft tissue masses (ie. tophi), nephrolithiasis, and urate nephropathy. **They noted** that the diagnosis is confirmed if monosodium urate crystals are present in synovial fluid and first-line therapy for acute gout is nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs or corticosteroids, depending on comorbidities; colchicine is second-line therapy. **They concluded** that Allopurinol is the most common therapy for chronic gout.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 2-5: Gout will demonstrate negatively birefringent needle-shaped crystals, while pseudogout will demonstrate positively birefringent rhomboid-shaped crystals
