## Question

A 45-year-old male retired professional football running back develops disordered cognition, memory loss, executive dysfunction, depression, apathy, disinhibition, and irritability. There is marked bradykinesia, tremors, and muscle rigidity on physical examination. What is the most likely diagnosis?

## Options

1. Early onset Alzheimer's disease
2. Parkinson's disease
3. Amyotropic lateral sclerosis
4. Chronic traumatic encephalopathy
5. Frontotemporal dementia

## Correct option

**4. Chronic traumatic encephalopathy**

## Answer explanation

The patient most likely has chronic traumatic encephalopathy (CTE), which is typically diagnosed post-mortem with histopathologic examination of brain tissue. Given the patient's history and presenting symptoms, CTE is the most likely diagnosis.

The exact pathologic mechanism of CTE is still poorly understood but current theories are based on a series of repetitive diffuse axonal injuries leading to the build-up of tau protein, beta-amyloid, and TDP-43 proteins. Compared to Alzheimer's disease (AD), CTE typically affects the superficial cortical laminae (layers II and III) as opposed to layer III and below. Despite there being a unique clinical presentation of CTE patients there are no established neuropathologic criteria for diagnosis with definitive diagnosis based solely on post-mortem histopathologic analysis of brain tissue. Patients typically present in midlife with disordered cognition, memory loss, executive dysfunction, depression, apathy, disinhibition, irritability, and Parkinsonian symptoms.

*Gavett et al. reviewed the clinical presentation, histopathology, preventive factors, and diagnosis of CTE.* **The authors stated** that tau protein accumulation is a unique histologic characteristic in CTE compared to other neurodegenerative diseases, as it is the most abundant aberrant protein identified. **The authors concluded** that intense research to identify biomarkers is necessary to detect disease, monitor progression and develop therapies to halt or slow disease course.

*McKee et al. reviewed 47 cases of histopathologically verified CTE.* *They described CTE consisting of memory disturbances, behavioral and personality changes, Parkinsonism, and speech and gait abnormalities.* **They concluded** that CTE is a distinctive, slowly progressive tauopathy with a clear environmental etiology.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Early-onset Alzheimer's is unlikely in this patient given the age and history. The onset of symptoms before the age of 65 is considered early-onset and rarely occurs in patients in their 40s.
Answer 2: Parkinson's disease typically occurs in patients over 60 years of age. It is unlikely to occur in this patient given the age and history, but there are similar clinical characteristics.
Answer 3: Amyotrophic lateral sclerosis is the result of selective motor neuron cell death resulting in progressive weakness without neurocognitive abnormalities. This patient does not have symptoms consistent with this diagnosis.
Answer 5: Frontotemporal dementia is a possibility given the patient's age and presenting symptoms, but the history of professional football suggests CTE. Frontotemporal dementia commonly presents with behavioral abnormalities, impulsiveness, and loss of executive function.
