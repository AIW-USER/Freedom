## Question

A gastrocnemius recession should be performed in which of the following patients after correction of a Stage IIA adult acquired flat foot deformity?

## Options

1. Ankle cannot be passively dorsiflexed above neutral with the knee flexed but can be with the knee extended
2. Ankle cannot be passively dorsiflexed above neutral with the knee extended but can be dorsiflexed past neutral with the knee flexed
3. Plantarflexion of the ankle is noted with squeezing of the calf
4. No plantarflexion of the ankle is noted with squeezing of the calf
5. Resting position of the ankle following reconstruction is in only 5 degrees of plantarflexion

## Correct option

**2. Ankle cannot be passively dorsiflexed above neutral with the knee extended but can be dorsiflexed past neutral with the knee flexed**

## Answer explanation

A gastrocnemius recession should be performed following reconstruction if there is evidence of gastrocnemius tightness on the Silfverskiold test.

Surgical correction of a Stage IIA adult acquired flat foot deformity typically consists of a medial calcaneus displacement osteotomy and FDL transfer to the navicular. Following reconstruction, if the gastroc is found to be tight, as evidence by the Silfverskiold test a gastroc recession is performed. A positive Silfverskiold test for gastrocnemius tightness occurs when the patient has greater ankle dorsiflexion with the knee flexed at 90 degrees than they do with the leg extended. In a patient with a tight gastrocnemius muscle, flexing the knee to 90 degrees takes this out of the equation because it crosses the knee joint, and allows the ankle to dorsiflex to a greater degree.

*Deland et al review adult acquired flatfoot deformity.* **They report** that spring ligament failure occurs in conjunction with the failing posterior tibial tendon. **They conclude** that with progression of the pes planovalgus deformity, patients will experience deformity and attenuation along the entire medial longitudinal arch.

*Haddad et al reviews the operative management of patients with adult acquired flatfoot deformity.* **They report** that most patients ultimately require a gastrocnemius recession following medial calcaneus displacement osteotomy and FDL transfer. **They conclude** that it is crucial to fully understand Stage II flatfoot deformities so that the correct procedures and to what degree can be selected for each patient.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: This is the opposite finding of a positive Silfverskiold test for gastrocnemius tightness.
Answer 3 and 4: Describe findings of the Thompson test, which is used in the evaluation of achilles rupture, and should not be used for determining the need for gastrocnemius recession.
Answer 5: Resting position of the foot is not used to determine the need for gastrocnemius recession.
