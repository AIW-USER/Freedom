## Question

Figure A is the radiograph of a 53-year-old male that previously underwent anatomic total shoulder arthroplasty 8-months ago but recently slipped on ice and felt a "pop" in his shoulder. Which of the following physical examination findings would be expected in this patient?

![[q216845-a-subscap.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. ER lag sign
2. Increased passive external rotation
3. Positive drop arm test
4. Positive Hornblower's sign
5. Positive Yergason's sign

## Correct option

**2. Increased passive external rotation**

## Answer explanation

This patient's postoperative radiograph is suggestive of a displaced lesser tuberosity indicating a failed lesser tuberosity osteotomy (LTO). Examination with subscapularis insufficiency would reveal increased external rotation.

Anatomic total shoulder arthroplasty (TSA) is dependent on the healing of the subscapularis repair postoperatively to maximize function. Several subscapularis takedown techniques may be used including LTO, subscapularis peel, or tenotomy. The LTO has theoretical advantages including improve subscapularis function postoperatively. Fortunately, there is a high union rate following this technique but loss of fixation and subscapularis failure may still occur. Such examination findings that would suggest subscapularis insufficiency include a positive internal rotation lag, lift-off, belly-press, bear-hug, and increased passive external rotation.

*Levy et al. reviewed patients with a displaced lesser tuberosity osteotomy (LTO) following TSA.* **They reported** that advanced imaging is helpful in identifying displaced lesser tuberosity osteotomy in cases in which displacement is not seen on postoperative axillary radiographs. **They concluded** that displacement of lesser tuberosity osteotomy following shoulder arthroplasty is best observed using a true AP radiograph.

*Shi et al. reviewed patients with a failed LTO repair after primary TSA.* **They reported** that failure of the LTO repair after TSA may possibly be an under-reported complication that is associated with poor clinical outcomes and limited options for revision surgery. **They concluded** that in patients with a high risk of LTO failure, considerations should be made to augment the LTO repair during the index TSA procedure.

*Schmidt et al. reviewed the effect of LTO thickness and suture configuration on repair integrity.* **They reported** that thin osteotomies displaced less than thick osteotomies and that adding a tension band improved construct stability and eliminated some failure modes. **They concluded** that the ideal repair was a thin wafer with both tension and compression sutures.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: ER lag sign is used to assess the function/integrity of the infraspinatus
Answer 3: A drop arm test is used to assess the function/integrity of the supraspinatus
Answer 4: A Hornblower's test is used to assess the function/integrity of the teres minor
