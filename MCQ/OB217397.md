## Question

A 64-year-old female presents to your office with persistent low back pain. She previously underwent a three-level lumbar fusion and is believed to have developed SI joint dysfunction. Which of the following is true regarding the diagnosis and treatment of this patient's condition?

## Options

1. A 50% reduction in pain following injection is diagnostic
2. Injection is recommended as the first line of treatment
3. Injection is likely to have less success due to patient's previous lumbar fusion
4. Tenderness at the posterior superior iliac spine is most common location of pain
5. Two positive provocative tests is highly suggestive of diagnosis

## Correct option

**3. Injection is likely to have less success due to patient's previous lumbar fusion**

## Answer explanation

This patient has sacroiliac joint dysfunction following a 3-level lumbar fusion. There is a lower success rate following injection in patients with previous lumbar fusion.

Sacroiliac joint dysfunction is a degenerative condition of the sacroiliac joint resulting in lower back pain. Diagnosis is made clinically with pain just inferior to the posterior superior iliac spine that is made worse with hip flexion, abduction, and external rotation. One known risk factor includes previous lumbar spine fusion, especially when there is >3 levels involved. Treatment is usually conservative with pain management, physical therapy, and injections. Surgical management is indicated in patients with progressive symptoms who fail nonoperative management.

*Sembrano et al. reviewed the relative frequencies of the spine, the sacroiliac joint, and the hip joint being the primary pain generator among patients presenting at a spine surgery clinic for low back pain (LBP).* **They reported** that up to 25% of patients may have significant pain contribution from the hip or SI joints. **They concluded** that 10% of patients will still have an undefined pain source even after diagnostic workup.

*Thawrani et al. reviewed the sacroiliac joint.* **They reported** that this joint is implicated as a pain generator in approximately 10% to 25% of patients with mechanical low back or leg symptoms. **They noted** that although provocative physical examination tests have not received reliable consensus, if three or more provocative tests are positive, pursuing a diagnostic SIJ injection is considered reasonable. **They concluded** that notable pain relief with intra-articular anesthetic injection under radiographic guidance has been shown to provide reliable evidence in the diagnosis of SIJ pain.

*Unoki et al. retrospectively reviewed the risk factors for sacroiliac joint pain (SIJP) after lumbar or lumbosacral fusion.* **They reported** that the incidence of SIJP was higher with fixed fusion (13.1%) than with floating fusion (10.0%). With regard to the number of fused segments, the incidence of SIJP was 5.8% for one fused segment, 10.0% for two segments, 20.0% for three segments, 22.5% for at least four segments. **They concluded** that fusion of multiple segments (at least three) can increase the incidence of SIJP after lumbar or lumbosacral fusion.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: >75% reduction in SI joint pain following a single injection is confirmatory of the diagnosis. >50% reduction in SI joint pain following two injections is diagnostic
Answer 2: Oral medication, physical therapy, pelvic belt, and prolotherapy are the first line of treatment. Injection of the SI joint is the second line of treatment
Answer 4: The posterior superior iliac spine is the second most tender location. The sacral sulcus is the most tender location
Answer 5: >3 positive tests is highly suggestive of the diagnosis
