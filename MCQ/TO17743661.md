## Question

Romanus lesion is seen in ( arthritis- ankylosing spondylitis)

## Options

1. LS spine X ray in AS
2. MRI of LS spine in AS
3. CT chest in RA
4. X ray chest in RA

## Correct option

**2. MRI of LS spine in AS**

## Answer explanation

Some important MRI signs in ankylosing spondylitis-

Earliest diagnosis of AS is made by MRI [short TI inversion recovery (STIR) sequence or with gadolinium-enhanced MRI]. For active sacroiliitis, a dynamic MRI with fat suppression is considered to be best.

• Romanus lesion (shiny corner sign): This is an early MRI finding and represents small erosions at the anterosuperior and the anteroinferior margin (corners on lateral radiograph) of the vertebral bodies, with surrounding reactive sclerosis.

• Anderson lesion (spinal pseudarthrosis): Refers to the inflammatory involvement of the intervertebral disks (spondylodiscitis) in the AS.

X ray signs of AS:

Radiographs of the lumbosacral spine show loss of lumbar lordosis, diffuse osteoporosis, calcification of the anterior and posterior longitudinal ligaments, squaring of the vertebral bodies and bridging osteophytes (k/a syndesmophytes). Eventually, the spine in advanced stages gives the classical bamboo spine appearance . Dagger sign refers to a single central radiodense line in the anteroposterior (AP) radiographs of spine related to ossification of supraspinous and interspinous ligaments
