## Question

Deviation of which radiographic parameter has been the greatest association with worsening patient-reported outcome scores?

## Options

1. Truncal shift
2. Cobb angle
3. C7 plumb line deviation
4. Thoracic kyphosis
5. Pelvic incidence

## Correct option

**3. C7 plumb line deviation**

## Answer explanation

Increased sagittal vertical axis malalignment or C7 plumb line deviation is most strongly associated with worse patient-reported outcome measures (PROM).

Adult spinal deformity can be the result of untreated adolescent, juvenile, or infantile scoliosis or de novo scoliosis, also known as degenerative scoliosis. In addition to a coronal plane deformity that is characteristic of scoliosis, many patients will present with a sagittal plane imbalance. This can have both cosmetic manifestations as well as pain and disability as they get older. Assessment of radiographic parameters should include lumbar lordosis, pelvic tilt, and sacral slope. In cases of sagittal plane imbalance, patients frequently retrovert their pelvis to bring the head centered over the pelvis. This results in a compensatory increased pelvic tilt and decreased sacral slope. Sagittal plane malalignment has been strongly associated with worsening PROMs.

*Glassman et al. performed a retrospective review of 352 patients with adult spinal deformities that were evaluated for a relationship between spinopelvic parameters and adverse health outcomes.* **The authors reported** that there was a significant correlation between increasing C7 plumb line deviation and SF-12 physical composite score, SRS-29 pain domain, SRS-29 activity domain, SRS-29 total, and Oswestry Disability Index (ODI) scores. Further, **they found** that ODI scores worsened when the region of maximal kyphosis progressed from thoracic to lumbar. **The authors concluded** that patient-reported outcome scores were inversely related to the degree of C7 plumb line deviation.

*Schwab et al. performed a review of the correlation between several important spinopelvic parameters (including the SVA, pelvic tilt, and lumbar lordosis), and optimal clinical outcomes.* **They note** that good clinical outcome requires achieving proper spinopelvic alignment in the treatment of adult spinal deformity. **They noted** that although variations in pelvic morphology exist, a framework has been established to determine ideal values for the regional and global parameters in an individualized patient approach. **They conclude** that when planning realignment surgery for adult spinal deformity, restoring low SVA and pelvic tilt values are the critical goals, and should be combined with proportional lumbar lordosis to pelvic incidence.

*Smith et al. reviewed the clinical and radiographic evaluation of adult spinal deformity patients.* *The authors cited literature supporting that spinal deformity in younger patients usually causes cosmetic issues whereas older patients present with pain and disability.* **They further stated** that positive sagittal balance is substantially affected by the relationship between the spine and the pelvis. **They recommend** obtaining full-length standing PA and lateral radiographs of the spine to evaluate for sagittal and coronal plane alignment as well as spinopelvic parameters.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: Truncal shift is the measure of the deviation between the central sacral axis the cranial-cervical spine axis. It measures how coronally shifted the patient trunk has shifted as a result of a coronal plane deformity. This has not been associated with worsening PROMs.
Answer 2: Cobb angles are a measure of the extent of coronal plane deformity, however, this has not been as strongly associated with worsening PROM as sagittal axis malalignment.
Answer 4: Thoracic kyphosis is the degree of kyphosis of the thoracic spine with normal measurements <45°. However, this has not been strongly associated with poor PROMs as sagittal axis malalignment.
Answer 5: Pelvic incidence is the angle between the center of the acetabulum to the center of the sacral endplate and a line parallel to the sacral endplate. This is a fixed spinopelvic parameter and has not been found to affect PROMs.
