## Question

An 85-year-old male trips while getting up to use the restroom overnight. He's brought directly to the emergency department by squad. Radiographs upon presentation are shown in Figure A. Operative treatment within 24 hours of admission has been shown to affect which of the following?

![[q215781-a-screen-shot-2021-08-22-at-5-12-40-pm.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Quality of reduction
2. Mortality rate
3. Postoperative time to ambulation
4. Infection rate
5. Ability to return to previous ambulatory status

## Correct option

**2. Mortality rate**

## Answer explanation

Operative stabilization of hip fractures within 24 hours of admission in elderly patients decreases 30-day, 90-day, and 1-year mortality rates.

Hip fractures in the elderly are very common orthopedic injuries that should be treated within 24-hours of admission. Treatment of displaced femoral neck fractures in this population is somewhat controversial, but consists of either total hip arthroplasty or hemiarthroplasty. Most surgeons base their decision on several patient factors including age, comorbidities, and activity level. Regardless of treatment method, operative stabilization should be performed within 24 hours to decrease mortality rates and should not be delayed for unnecessary clearance or logistical reasons.

*Nyholm et al. review the time to surgery as it relates to mortality following proximal femur fractures.* **They report** a surgical delay of more than twelve hours significantly increased the adjusted risk of 30-day mortality and a surgical delay of more than twenty-four hours significantly increased the adjusted risk of 90-day mortality. **They conclude** that operative stabilization should be performed as soon as practicable following these injuries.

*Maheshwari et al. review the benefits of early surgery for patient with hip fractures.* **They report** a linear relationship between surgical timing and 1-year mortality with each 10-hour delay from admission to surgery confirming a 5% higher rate of 1-year mortality. **They conclude** that hip fractures should be treated on an urgent basis.

*Rosso et al. review the prognostic factors for mortality following hip fractures.* **They report** a postoperative mortality rate was 4% at 30 days, 14.1% at six months, and 18.8% at one year after surgery in their cohort. **They conclude** that early surgery influenced late mortality, with a decreased risk in patients operated on within 48 hours.

Figure A is an AP radiograph of the hip demonstrating a displaced femoral neck fracture.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Displaced femoral neck fractures should be treated with arthroplasty in elderly patients.
Answer 3: Postoperative time to ambulation is not influenced by timing of surgery.
Answer 4: Infection rate is not linked to time to surgery.
Answer 5: Combined medical disease, not timing of surgery, has been shown to influence ambulatory status following hip fractures.
