## Question

A 4-year-old male presents to the clinic with multiple recurrent fractures. On examination, he has blue sclera but otherwise normal findings. He is diagnosed with osteogenesis imperfecta. Which of the following injuries is a common presenting injury in these patients?

## Options

1. Calcaneus fractures
2. Olecranon fractures
3. Metaphyseal corner injuries
4. Rib fractures
5. Tibial plateau fractures

## Correct option

**2. Olecranon fractures**

## Answer explanation

Olecranon fractures are common presenting injuries in patients with osteogenesis imperfecta (OI).

Osteogenesis imperfecta is the abnormal collagen production in the body leading to a lack of tensile strength of bone. This clinically manifests as recurrent fractures and osteoporosis. In severe cases, this can lead to neonatal death. Often, patients may have a delayed diagnosis and will sustain multiple fractures before prompting a formal workup. In these cases, olecranon fractures are commonly the first presenting sign of the diagnosis.

*Zionts and Moon reviewed 17 fractures of the olecranon apophysis in children with OI.* **They reported** that 70% of patients sustained injuries to bilateral olecranons within 5 years, with 15/17 fractures requiring surgical treatment. **They concluded** that OI patients presenting with an olecranon fracture should be counseled on the risk of a fracture to the contralateral side.

*Stott and Zionts retrospectively reviewed seven olecranon fractures in OI patients treated with open reduction and internal fixation.* **They reported** all fractures healed uneventfully without growth disturbances. **They concluded** surgical treatment of olecranon fractures in OI patients can lead to good outcomes without functional limitations.

Incorrect answers
Answers 1, 3, 4, and 5: Patients with OI can present with many of these fractures, but patients with olecranon fractures, especially recurrent, are often the first presenting sign of the diagnosis.
