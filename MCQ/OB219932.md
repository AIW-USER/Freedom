## Question

An 18-year-old black freshman on the men’s basketball team presents for his preparticipation evaluation before starting summer training camp. The athlete denies any prior medical conditions or family history. Which of the following is the most appropriate evaluation given his race, sex, and sport?

## Options

1. Routine medical history and physical exam
2. Electrocardiogram (ECG)
3. Cardiac stress test
4. Hemoglobin electrophoresis
5. Lung diffusion capacity test

## Correct option

**1. Routine medical history and physical exam**

## Answer explanation

Although the patient is at increased risk for sudden cardiac death (SCD) due to their sex, race, and sport, routine medical history and physical exam are considered the most effective screening tests. Further cardiac examination is not routinely performed (Answer 1).

Sudden cardiac death (SCD) in athletes, though rare, is a devastating event often precipitated by intense physical exertion that typically occurs due to undiagnosed structural or electrical heart abnormalities. Conditions such as hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, arrhythmogenic right ventricular cardiomyopathy, coronary artery anomalies, and myocarditis are common causes in young athletes. In comparison, older athletes more often experience SCD due to coronary artery disease. The risk of SCD highlights the importance of pre-participation screening, which may include history-taking and physical examination. In some cases, electrocardiography (ECG) may be indicated if risk factors are identified during a screening history and physical, such as patients with a family or personal history of cardiac disease. However, an ECG should not be universally performed. Early recognition of warning signs, like unexplained fainting or chest pain during exercise, and rapid intervention in the event of cardiac emergencies with cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR) and automated external defibrillator (AED) use are critical. Ongoing research is focused on improving screening methods and understanding genetic predispositions to prevent SCD among athletes better.

*Miller et al. review current concepts in the Preparticipation Evaluation of the Young Athlete.* **The authors emphasize** the importance of the medical interview as the most crucial tool for identifying athletes at increased risk for injury. Based on physical examination and history, secondary specialty tests may be ordered. While universal cardiac screening is not recommended, it, along with neuropsychological testing for management of concussion and laboratory testing for sickle cell trait, may be indicated in select groups of athletes. Despite routine screening, there is significant room for improvement and research.

*Harmon et al. created a database of all National Collegiate Athletic Association deaths (2003-2013).* **The authors report** 514 student-athlete deaths during the study period, with accidents being the most common cause (50%). The most common medical cause of death was sudden cardiac death. Risk factors identified included male gender, black race, and basketball as a sport. The most common finding at autopsy is autopsy-negative sudden unexplained death with definitive evidence for hypertrophic cardiomyopathy seen in 10% of cases.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 2-5: Routine history and physical exam are the recommended screening tests for detecting potential medical conditions. Secondary testing should be reserved for patients with a family or personal history of cardiac disease.
