## Question

A 17-year-old field hockey player sustains an injury to her knee during a match. An MRI is obtained, shown in Figure A, leading her treating surgeon to recommend reconstruction of the affected ligament with patellar tendon autograft, as well as augmentation with a lateral extra-articular tenodesis (LET) procedure. Which of the following anatomic sites shown in Figure B best approximates the insertion site of the normal anatomic structure that the LET procedure attempts to recreate?

## Options

1. Site A
2. Site B
3. Site C
4. Site D
5. Site E

## Correct option

**2. Site B**

## Answer explanation

The normal anatomic insertion of the anterolateral ligament (ALL), the function of which is approximated by a lateral extra-articular tenodesis (LET) procedure, is on the anterolateral tibial plateau between Gerdy's tubercle and the fibular head, as shown by Site B (Answer 2).

Tears of additional ligamentous structures often occur alongside tears of the anterior cruciate ligament (ACL). The radiographic finding of a Segond fracture at the time of injury, which represents an avulsion fracture of the insertion site of the ALL at the anterolateral tibial plateau, has become pathognomonic for the diagnosis of an ACL tear. This ligament originates from the femur just proximal and posterior to the lateral epicondyle and represents a distinct ligamentous structure responsible for anterolateral rotatory stability of the knee. Formal ALL reconstructions, though still performed, seem to have fallen way to the use of the LET, which involves re-routing a strip of the iliotibial (IT) band attached at Gerdy's, under the lateral collateral ligament (LCL), and anchoring it to the ALL origin posterior and proximal to the lateral epicondyle on the femur. Though this does not exactly reconstruct the anatomic footprint of the ligament, the approximation has shown to improve rotatory stability and failure rates when used in concert with soft tissue grafts, though long-term data is still needed.

*Ariel et al. provide a step-by-step dissection of the anterolateral ligament of the knee.* *The authors used 20 fresh-frozen cadavers and established a standardized dissection technique involving a transverse incision performed about the iliotibial band (ITB), around 10 cm proximal to the lateral epicondyle of the femur, followed by blunt dissection of the ITB until its insertion at Gerdy’s tubercle, with dissection anterior to the biceps femoris insertion until the lateral collateral ligament (LCL) is found.* Then, using the LCL, internal rotation and 30 to 60° flexion as references, the ALL can be located in the anterolateral topography of the knee, with its origin near the lateral epicondyle (proximal and posterior) and insertion between Gerdy’s tubercle and the fibula (4.0 mm to 7.0 mm below the tibial plateau), expanding to the lateral meniscus (between the body and anterior horn), exhibiting a mean length of 4.0 ± 0.4 cm and mean width of 5.5 ± 0.8 mm. **They concluded** that these steps provide an effective and reproducible ALL dissection technique that made it was possible to identify the ligament in 100% of the cases.

*Van Dyck et al. published a study looking at anterolateral complex (ALC) injuries in patients with acute anterior cruciate ligament (ACL) rupture on magnetic resonance imaging (MRI).* *The authors reviewed 69 MRI scans and found that 51% had associated injuries to the ITB, 33% had associated injuries to the Kaplan fibers , and 57% had associated injuries to the ALL.* **They concluded** that, on the basis of MRI analysis, ALL injuries were found with varying degrees of severity and intensity with noted injuries to associated surrounding fibers in patients with acute ACL rupture.

Figure A is a sagittal T2-weighted MRI sequence showing complete rupture of the ACL. Figure B is a labelled AP radiograph of a normal knee, showing the following labelled structures: (A) Tibial Tubercle; (B) Anterolateral tibial plateau; (C) Anterior fibular head; (D) Posterior fibular head; (E) Lateral epicondyle.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The tibial tubercle is the insertion point for the patellar tendon, not the ALL.
Answer 3: The lateral collateral ligament (LCL) inserts on the anterior portion of the fibular head, not the ALL.
Answer 4: The biceps femoris inserts on the posterior fibular head, not the ALL.
Answer 5: The lateral epicondyle of the femur represents the origin, not the insertion, of the ALL.
