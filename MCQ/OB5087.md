## Question

A pre-menarchal 12-year-old girl presents for evaluation of scoliosis. She has no complaints and on physical exam has a positive Adam's forward bend. Her PA spine radiograph shown in Figure A demonstrates a 27 degree thoracic curve and a 31 degree lumbar curve. Your recommend a customized Boston-type
thoracolumbosacral orthosis. In order to obtain success rates of 90-93%, meaning that the use of the brace prevents the need for surgical intervention, how many hours per day minimum must the brace be worn.

![[q5087-a-figure-a.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Minimum 6-8 hours actual wear time
2. Minimum 9-10 hours actual wear time
3. Minimum 12-13 hours actual wear time
4. Minimum 15-16 hours actual wear time
5. Minimum 19-20 hours actual wear time

## Correct option

**3. Minimum 12-13 hours actual wear time**

## Answer explanation

Brace wear for an average of at least 12.9 hours per day was associated with success rates of 90 to 93% in recent level 1 clinical studies.

In patients with AIS, a TLSO is indicated in patients with a cobb angle from 25° to 45° if the deformity is flexible and the patient is still skeletally immature (Risser 0, 1, 2). The goal of brace treatment is to stop progression, not to correct the deformity.

*Weinstein et al. conducted a multi-center trial studying both a randomized arm and a preference arm in regards to bracing versus observation.* *The trial was stopped early due to efficacy of bracing with the authors noting a significantly higher rate of curve control than the observation arm.* **They found** 90-93% success when the brace was worn for at least 12.9 hours per day.

*Katz et al. investigated curve progression based on actual brace wear using a temperature sensor to accurately assess brace wear.* The total hours of brace wear correlated with lack of curve progression with a dose-response effect noted. Curves did not progress in 82% of patients who wore the brace more than 12 hours per day. For those who wore the brace for fewer than 7 hours per day, curves progressed in 69%. Prescribed bracing regimens (eg, 16 hours/day or 23 hours/day) had no effect on actual brace wear or curve progression.

*Morton et al. prospectively followed 126 patients who were prescribed between 16 and 23 hours of Boston Brace wear.* A pre-prescription questionnaire was administered, which if a low score was reported, correlated with low adherence to wear. Approximately 40% of patients reported adequate compliance with prescribed brace wear.

Figure A exhibits a PA radiograph with adolescent idiopathic scoliosis, maturity Risser 0. Illustration A exhibits examples of the Boston Brace, depending on the level of the curve being treated. Illustration B represents the Risser Classification: stages; I: apophysis over 25% of the iliac crest; II: apophysis over 25% to 50% of the iliac crest; III: apophysis over 50% to 75% of the iliac crest; IV: apophysis over >75% of the iliac crest; V: complete ossification and fusion of the iliac crest apophysis. Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1,2,4 and 5: Recent studies utilizing heat sensors to measure compliance, have shown that actual wear time and compliance does not directly correlate to prescribed time. A minimum of 12 hours actual wear time is required to prevent curve progression, with improved efficacy associated with greater hours of wear time.
