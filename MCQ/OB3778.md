## Question

A 57-year-old male with a history of neck stiffness presents after a fall from standing height. He reports severe neck pain but denies subjective weakness in his extremities. Neurological examination reveals normal motor strength in the upper and lower extremities without sensory changes. Figure A is the sagittal CT scan of the cervical spine on presentation. Which of the following is true regarding his clinical presentation and treatment?

## Options

1. The is a 90% probability that he is HLA-B27 positive
2. A nondisplaced fracture can be treated with a hard cervical collar and immediate discharge so long as there is close follow up.
3. The underlying condition is associated with increased morbidity and mortality compared to individuals without the condition
4. The patient is unlikely to have diabetes or coronary artery disease
5. The condition usually presents in patients young than 50 years of age

## Correct option

**3. The underlying condition is associated with increased morbidity and mortality compared to individuals without the condition**

## Answer explanation

The patient is presenting with a cervical spine fracture in the setting of underlying diffuse idiopathic skeletal hyperostosis (DISH). Cervical spine trauma patients with DISH have increased morbidity and mortality compared to the general population.

DISH is the result of pathologic ossification of the anterior longitudinal ligament. The cause remains unknown and has not been linked to a genotype like ankylosing spondylitis (AS). It is characterized by a flowing, non-marginal ossification spanning at least four sequential vertebral segments and frequently affects patients between 50 and 60 years of age. Diabetes and coronary artery disease have been linked to the disease. Patients are at risk for developing serious spinal injuries from low energy, standing height falls due to the longer lever arm of the spine, lack of vertebral segment flexibility, and frequent coexisting osteoporosis. Studies have shown a high rate of neurological decline, complications, and greater mortality rates compared to non-ankylosed cervical spine trauma.

*Whang et al. performed a retrospective cohort study of patients with either AS or DISH who present with spinal injuries.* **The authors found** that the most common injury mechanism was a ground-level fall and that there were inherent mechanistic differences between associated injuries. AS patients were more likely to sustain a compression or burst fracture, while DISH patients were more likely to sustain an extension-distraction injury. Neurologic injuries were high in both groups, but AS patients were more likely to present with neurologic deficits and undergo operative treatment. **The authors recommend** restoring "normal" spinal anatomy for the patient during surgical treatment, rather than performing corrective osteotomies.

*Westerveld et al. performed a systematic review of case series regarding the treatment, neurologic status, and complications of patients with DISH or AS presenting with spine fractures.* **The authors reported** that DISH patients had higher thoracic spine injuries (34.5% compared to 10.7%) with fractures that propagated through the vertebral body, whereas AS patients almost always had cervical spine involvement with fractures occurring through the disc space and initially present with neurologic deficits. **They found** patients with DISH had higher mortality rates with both surgical and conservative treatment measures, but AS patients had higher complications rates following both surgical and nonsurgical treatment. **The authors concluded** that patients with an ankylosed spine are prone to worse clinical outcomes compared to those following spinal injuries in the general population.

Figure A depicts the sagittal CT scan of the cervical spine of a patient with DISH and a fracture through the C4-5 disc space.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: HLA-B27 is a histocompatibility genotype that has been associated with AS, not DISH.
Answer 2: Patients presenting with cervical spine fractures, even if nondisplaced, require admission and surgical stabilization. Hard collar immobilization and close outpatient follow-up are not appropriate for a patient with DISH and a cervical spine fracture.
Answer 4: The etiology of DISH still remains unclear, but there has been a strong correlation with diabetes mellitus and coronary artery disease.
Answer 5: DISH typically affects patients older than 50 years of age.
