## Question

A 69-year-old female former smoker (5’5, 179lbs BMI 29.8) with a history of hypertension, COPD, and diabetes (HgbA1C 7.8) returns to the clinic for her first postoperative visit after an L3-L5 posterior lumbar decompression and fusion. Her surgery was complicated by notable blood loss without the need for intraoperative transfusion. On examination, she has purulent drainage with erythema and induration from her lumbar incision site. Which of the following factors likely contributed to this patient's risk of wound complication?

## Options

1. Age
2. Operative time of 2-hours
3. Preoperative Hgb A1C 7.8
4. BMI
5. History of former smoker

## Correct option

**3. Preoperative Hgb A1C 7.8**

## Answer explanation

Risk factors associated with the increased risk of surgical site infection after spinal surgery include prolonged operative time (>3 hours), uncontrolled diabetes (A1C > 7.5; Answer 3), surgeries involving a posterior approach, and elevated body mass index (>35 kg/m^2).

Surgical site infections (SSIs) are the most common complication encountered after spinal surgery, reportedly as high as 18% of cases. Depending on whether the fascial layer is violated, they are generally classified as superficial or deep. Additionally, the infection is defined as acute if occurring within three weeks of the index surgery and chronic if longer than three weeks from surgery. Risk factors for SSI include active smoking, increased subcutaneous fat thickness, preoperative albumin <20mg/dl, diabetes and hgba1c >7.5%, age >70 years, prolonged operative time greater than 3 hours, posterior approaches, and elevated BMI >35.

*Luo et al. performed a systematic review and meta-analysis in 2022 to evaluate impact of intraoperative vancomycin powder after posterior spinal surgery on reducing the risk of infection.* *The review included 22 studies, totaling more than 11,555 patients undergoing posterior spine surgery with varying doses of intraoperative vancomycin powder (0.5-2grams), and found significant reductions in surgical site infection.* Therefore, **the authors concluded** that the use of local vancomycin powder can significantly reduce the incidence of surgical site infection in particular with gram-positive organisms and MRSA.

*Yao et al. performed a meta-analysis of more than 94 studies evaluating surgical site infection risk factors in adult spine surgery.* **They found** that BMI >35, diabetes, multiple medical comorbidities, current smoking, malnutrition, preoperative radiation, intraoperative transfusion, and combined anterior/posterior approach were associated with increased risk for surgical site infection. Therefore, **they concluded** that numerous modifiable and non-modifiable risk factors contribute to the risk of surgical site infection and should be optimized prior to surgery.

*Fei et al. performed a systematic review of twelve studies that investigated risk factors for superficial surgical site infection following spinal surgery, which included 13,476 patients.* **They found** that the most important predictors of surgical site infection were diabetes, prolonged operative time >3 hours, BMI >35, posterior approach, and surgery on more than seven intervertebral levels. *Their conclusion was similar to Yao et al.'s findings,* **demonstrating** modifiable and non-modifiable risk factors before surgery.

*Xiong et al. reviewed 668 patients undergoing primary posterior cervical instrumented spinal surgery, with 212 patients (31.7%) screened for MRSA preoperatively, of which 6 (2.8%) were colonized for MRSA.* *They had twelve patients (1.8%) who ultimately required irrigation and debridement but found that preoperative MRSA testing was not associated with postoperative irrigation and debridement risk.* Therefore, **they concluded** that there was no association between preoperative nasal MRSA screening and surgical site infections in patients undergoing primary cervical instrumentation.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Patients over 70 years old are at increased risk of surgical site infections.
Answer 2: Prolonged operative times longer than 3 hours are associated with a significantly increased risk of surgical site infections following posterior lumbar fusion surgery.
Answer 4: The patient’s BMI is elevated at 29.8, but categorized as overweight and not obese. The risk of surgical site infection is significantly increased in obese patients with a BMI >30.
Answer 5: While active smoking is a significant risk factor for surgical site infection, the patient is a former smoker and this does not increase their risk of a surgical site infection.
