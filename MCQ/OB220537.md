## Question

On exam, there is left knee hemarthrosis and he is unable to perform a straight leg raise. Injury radiographs are shown in Figures A and B. ## Options

1. Primary repair with 3 transosseous bone tunnels
2. Primary repair with 2 transosseous bone tunnels
3. Primary repair with suture anchors
4. End-to-end repair technique with nonabsorbable sutures
5. Autograft reconstruction with ipsilateral gracilis tendon

## Correct option

**3. Primary repair with suture anchors**

## Answer explanation

This weekend warrior sustained a left patellar tendon rupture during a basketball game. Suture anchors have demonstrated a high load to failure with less gap formation compared to transosseous techniques when surgically repairing these injuries (answer choice 3).

Patellar tendon ruptures are rare injuries (<1 per 100,000 people annually) that most commonly occur in males in their 30s or 40s. Risk factors for these injuries are related to a weakening of the collagen structure and include systemic diseases (e.g., diabetes, lupus, rheumatoid arthritis, and chronic renal disease), patellar degeneration (most common), previous tendon injury or tendinopathy, and corticosteroid injections. These injuries are caused by a tension overload of the extensor mechanism during activity in a patient at risk, which typically occurs during sudden quadriceps contraction with the knee in a flexed position. When the knee is flexed >60 degrees, this exerts the greatest force on the tendon. Three patterns of injury occur: avulsion of the tendon with or without bone from the inferior pole of the patella (most common), mid-substance tear of the tendon, and a distal avulsion from the tibial tubercle. Physical exam demonstrates the inability to perform a straight leg raise with a palpable gap distal to the inferior pole of the patella, and radiographs demonstrate a more proximal position of the patella relative to the tibiofemoral joint (patella alta). Treatment options for acute injuries include immobilization, end-to-end repair, and primary tendon repair with transosseous bone tunnels or suture anchors.

*Danaher et al. reviewed repair techniques in patellar and quadriceps tendon tears.* **They report** that for complete or high-grade partial tendon tears primary repair via transosseous bone tunnels or suture anchors is the most common treatment. **They found** that based on biomechanical testing, load to failure is not statistically different between transosseous bone tunnels and suture anchors; however, suture anchors have demonstrated less gap formation at the repair site. Graft augmentation may be beneficial with chronic injuries, mid-substance tears, or with poor tissue quality. Postoperatively, patients can mobilize right away in a knee orthosis, and by 6 weeks full range of motion and a concentric training program may begin.

*McGowan et al. reported their surgical technique and outcomes for suture anchor repair in quadriceps and patellar tendon injuries.* **They reported** suture anchors to provide stronger biomechanical fixation, reduced soft tissue disruption, a smaller incision, reduced postoperative pain, shorter operative time, lower knot burden, and lack of prepatellar bursa scarring. **They conclude** this is a safe and reproducible surgical technique for repairing quadriceps and patellar tendon injuries.

Figures A and B are AP and lateral x-rays of a left knee demonstrating patella alta and soft tissue swelling around the patellar tendon.

Incorrect answer choices:
Answers 1 and 2: Repairing with suture anchors has been shown to provide less gap formation when compared to transosseous bone tunnels.
Answer 4: End-to-end repair of the tendon is performed at the rupture site if there is a cuff of tissue on both sides of the rupture. This has not been shown to provide less gap formation at the repair site when compared to the other techniques described.
Answer 5: Tendon reconstruction can be performed in chronic injuries, mid-substance tears, or with poor tissue quality, but has not been shown to provide less gap formation at the repair site when compared to the other techniques described.
