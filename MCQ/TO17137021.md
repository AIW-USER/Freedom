Chapter: Trauma MCQs — Talus Fractures

## Question

Which of the following is true of talar neck fractures?

## Options

1. If a displaced talar neck fracture is reduced and stably fixed, the chance of subtalar arthritis is 25%.
2. A Hawkins sign seen on a radiograph 6 weeks post-injury is a poor prognostic sign.
3. Type I and II fractures may be treated non-operatively.
4. A varus malunion causes decreased eversion.

## Correct option

**4. A varus malunion causes decreased eversion.**

## Answer explanation

Talar neck fractures are the commonest fracture of the talus, and follow forced

dorsiflexion with axial load. They present a difficult problem, mainly due to the high

complication rate, particularly osteonecrosis and post-traumatic osteoarthritis.

The Hawkins classification is used for these fractures:

I – undisplaced

II – associated subtalar dislocation

III – associated subtalar and tibiotalar dislocation

IV (added by Canale) – associated subtalar, tibiotalar and talonavicular dislocation

Type I injuries can be treated nonoperatively, but the others need reduction and fixation.

Hawkin’s sign refers to a subchondral lucency see on an anteroposterior radiograph at

6 to 8 weeks, and represents resorption, implying good vascularity, and hence is a good

prognostic sign. A varus malunion occurs in one-third of cases and results in decreased

eversion.

Answer: 4
