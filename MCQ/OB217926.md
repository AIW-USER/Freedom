## Question

A 64-year-old female presents with persistent weakness after a left shoulder dislocation that occurred 6 weeks ago. She has attempted physical therapy, but her weakness has not improved. She reports pain that radiates into the left upper arm and occasionally the neck when she sleeps at night. What would be the next step for this patient?

## Options

1. EMG
2. Cervical spine MRI
3. Left shoulder MRI
4. Glenohumeral corticosteroid injection
5. Physical therapy for periscapular and rotator cuff strengthening

## Correct option

**3. Left shoulder MRI**

## Answer explanation

Elderly patients that sustain shoulder dislocations are at high risk for concomitant rotator cuff tears. In the setting of persistent weakness after a dislocation, an MRI should be obtained to identify an associated rotator cuff tear.

Traumatic rotator cuff tears are common after shoulder dislocation in elderly patients with rates as high as 80% in patients over 60 years reported. Often these tears are full thickness and lack the ability to heal with nonoperative treatment. Patients with persistent weakness after a shoulder dislocation shoulder be evaluated for a rotator cuff tear with an MRI.

*Robinson et al. prospectively studied a trauma database of 3633 for injuries associated with anterior shoulder dislocations.* **They reported** 13.5% of patients presented with a neurologic deficit and 33.4% presented with rotator cuff tears, commonly seen in female patients over 60 years of age. **They concluded** rotator cuff tears and axillary nerve palsy following glenohumeral dislocation are more common than previously expected.

*Murthi and Ramirez reviewed shoulder dislocation in elderly patients.* **They stated** glenohumeral dislocations in elderly patients are more likely to result in rotator cuff tears, axillary nerve palsies, and brachial plexus palsies. **They recommended** careful evaluation for rotator cuff pathology in elderly patients with persistent shoulder weakness following an anterior shoulder dislocation.

Incorrect answers
Answer 1: An EMG is useful for assessing persistent weakness when there is a concern for a brachial plexus palsy.
Answer 2: A cervical spine MRI is useful when there is a concern for cervical radiculopathy.
Answer 4: A corticosteroid injection can be helpful in managing rotator cuff tears that are incomplete. However, complete tears after a shoulder dislocation usually require surgery, which can be negatively impacted if there is an injection beforehand.
Answer 5: A traumatic rotator cuff tear is highly likely in a patient with persistent shoulder weakness after a shoulder dislocation. Further evaluation with an MRI is preferred prior to physical therapy.
