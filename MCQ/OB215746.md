## Question

You are preparing a lecture for medical students on acetabular fractures and associated injuries. You review the different elementary and associated fracture patterns and note that there are several different approaches for fixation of these fractures. In which of the following cases would the use of a trochanteric flip osteotomy be most indicated?

## Options

1. Posterior wall acetabular fracture with femoral neck fracture
2. Posterior wall acetabular fracture with femoral head fracture
3. Transverse + posterior wall acetabular fracture
4. Both column acetabular fracture
5. T-type acetabular fracture

## Correct option

**2. Posterior wall acetabular fracture with femoral head fracture**

## Answer explanation

A trochanteric flip osteotomy and surgical hip dislocation is indicated in cases of Pipkin IV femoral head fractures where it would be difficult to fix the associated femoral head fracture with a standard posterior approach.

Pipkin IV femoral head fractures are defined by a femoral head fracture in addition to an acetabular fracture. These injuries are often accompanied by a posterior hip dislocation which leads to a posterior acetabular fracture and anterior femoral head fracture. For isolated posterior wall acetabular fractures, a Kocher-Langenbeck approach is normally utilized. However, in cases with associated femoral head fractures that require fixation, a surgical hip dislocation with a trochanteric flip osteotomy is indicated to better visualize the femoral head.

*Solberg et al. investigated the use of trochanteric flip osteotomy in Pipkin IV fractures.* **They note** that the approach allows simultaneous exposure and at follow-up, clinical outcomes were good or excellent in 10/12, fair in 1/12, and poor in 1/12. **They conclude** that this is a useful approach in cases where fixation of both the femoral head and acetabulum are necessary.

*Giannoudis et al. performed a systematic review regarding general management of femoral head fractures, including 29 studies and 453 femoral head fractures.* **They noted** that Pipkin I fractures had better results with excision and recommended anatomic reduction and fixation for Pipkin II fractures. They also reviewed the various approaches to the acetabulum and associated complications, **noting** that the trochanteric-flip approach did not increase the risk of damage to the femoral head blood supply compared to the posterior approach.

*Henle et al. discussed treatment strategies of femoral head injuries.* **They noted** that anatomic reduction and stable fixation is the major factor in improving outcomes. They went on to discuss their experience with the trochanteric-flip approach and note that they have had good results and favorable outcomes in the treatment of femoral head fractures.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: While rare, a posterior acetabular fracture and femoral neck fracture could be treated simultaneously through a posterior Kocher-Langenbeck approach.
Answer 3: Transverse + posterior wall acetabular fractures can be treated through a posterior Kocher-Langenbeck approach and would not require a trochanteric flip osteotomy.
Answer 4,5: Both column and T-type acetabular fractures may require a combination of approaches to ensure stable fixation but would not require a trochanteric flip osteotomy.
