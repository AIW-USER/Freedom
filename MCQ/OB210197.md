## Question

Which of the following complications is a concern when using the start point shown in figure A for the management of this skeletally immature patient’s femoral shaft fracture with a rigid intramedullary nail?

![[q210197-a-piriformis-start.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Sciatic nerve injury
2. Varus and flexion deformity
3. Refracture
4. Femoral head avascular necrosis
5. Rotational deformity

## Correct option

**4. Femoral head avascular necrosis**

## Answer explanation

Disruption of the femoral head blood supply and subsequent avascular necrosis (AVN) is a described complication of a piriformis fossa start point for intramedullary nail (IMN) fixation in skeletally immature patients.

Antegrade rigid intramedullary nails are typically used for patients approaching skeletal maturity with unstable femoral shaft fracture. After age 4 years, the medial femoral circumflex artery (MFCA) and its branches provide the main arterial supply to the femoral head. The piriformis start point is in close proximity to branches of the MCFA and therefore may compromise femoral head perfusion. Many modern pediatric rigid nails utilize a lateral trochanteric start point to avoid this complication.

*Mileski et al. reported on a 14-year-old female who developed femoral head AVN after intramedullary nail fixation for a femoral shaft fracture using a piriformis start point.* The 11 mm IMN was removed 14 months postoperatively after the patient noted increasing hip pain with evidence of AVN on imaging. **The authors conclude** IMN fixation can cause femoral head AVN, a complication that may be avoided with a plate, external fixation, or placement of a nail without reaming.

*MacNeil et al. performed a systematic review of rigid IMN insertion sites and femoral head AVN in skeletally immature patients.* The risk of AVN for piriformis, tip of greater trochanter, and lateral trochanter start points were 2%, 1.4%, and 0%, respectively. **The authors concluded** the lateral trochanter start point for rigid IMN fixation has the lowest risk of AVN when treating pediatric femur fractures. However, the authors acknowledged the relatively low number of patients included in their analysis and that these results were not statistically significant.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Sciatic nerve injuries are not associated with the use of a piriformis start point
Answer 2: Varus and flexion are the typical deformities in femoral shaft fractures but are not associated with a piriformis start point
Answer 3: Refracture is most common after external fixator removal in patients with a varus deformity
Answer 5: Rotational malalignment is a known complication of femoral shaft fractures, but is not unique to IMN placement using a piriformis start point
