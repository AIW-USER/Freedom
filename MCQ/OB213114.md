## Question

Which of the following statements regarding the peroneal division of the sciatic nerve is true?

## Options

1. It crosses anterior to the lateral head of the gastrocnemius muscle through the posterior intermuscular septum
2. An injury at the level of the thigh will denervate the branches to the short head of the biceps and the tibialis anterior
3. It is most commonly injured during an anterior knee dislocation
4. The deep division initially runs medial to the tibial artery and later crosses over to become lateral
5. It originates from the L3-S1 nerve roots

## Correct option

**2. An injury at the level of the thigh will denervate the branches to the short head of the biceps and the tibialis anterior**

## Answer explanation

An injury to the peroneal division of the sciatic nerve at the level of the thigh will denervate the branches to the short head of the biceps and the tibialis anterior.

The peroneal division of the sciatic nerve travels through the posterior compartment of the thigh and supplies the short head of the biceps femoris muscle before separating from the tibial nerve in the upper popliteal fossa. The same fibers that run laterally in the sciatic nerve to innervate the short head of the biceps femoris will form the common peroneal nerve at the knee. Thus, to differentiate between an injury to the peroneal nerve branch of the sciatic nerve in the thigh or at the knee, it is necessary to assess the innervation to the short head of the biceps femoris. An injury at the level of the thigh will denervate the branches to the short head of the biceps and to the tibialis anterior, whereas an injury at the level of the knee will spare the biceps femoris. If a nerve is found to be transected, the optimal time to definitive repair is within 1-3 weeks from injury in order to allow time for the zone of injury to declare itself.

*Baima et al. published a review on the evaluation and treatment of peroneal neuropathy.* **They reported** that the sciatic nerve receives contributions from the L4–S3 ventral rami and exits the sacral plexus by passing through the greater sciatic foramen and traveling inferior to the piriformis muscle. The posterior divisions of the ventral rami ultimately form the common peroneal nerve and innervate the short head of the biceps femoris muscle, while the anterior divisions of the ventral rami innervate the other three hamstring muscles and ultimately form the tibial nerve.

*Garg et al. published a review on the evaluation and management of peroneal nerve palsies.* **They reported** that the peroneal division of the sciatic nerve travels through the posterior compartment of the thigh and supplies the short head of the biceps femoris muscle before separating from the tibial nerve in the upper popliteal fossa. **They stated** that studies may be performed on the short head of the biceps and a tibial-innervated muscle distal to the knee to identify more proximal lesions and/or injury to the sciatic nerve.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The peroneal nerve crosses posterior (not anterior) to the lateral head of the gastrocnemius muscle through the posterior intermuscular septum
Answer 3: The peroneal nerve is most commonly injure during a lateral, not anterior, knee dislocation.
Answer 4: The deep division of the peroneal nerve initially runs lateral to the tibial artery and later crosses over to become medial.
Answer 5: The peroneal nerve originates from the L4-S3 nerve roots.
