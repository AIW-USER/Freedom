## Question

Which of the following procedures results in a healed lesion consisting of Type II > Type I collagen on the articular surface?

## Options

1. Chondroplasty
2. Microfracture
3. Debridement with focal bone marrow stimulation procedure
4. Matrix-associated autologous chondrocyte implantation
5. Single core osteochondral autograft

## Correct option

**4. Matrix-associated autologous chondrocyte implantation**

## Answer explanation

Matrix-associated autologous chondrocyte implantation (MACI) is the only FDA approved cell therapy for cartilage in the USA and when successful, results in a healed lesion consisting of both Type I and Type II collagen, with type II collagen predominating.

Type I collagen is the most common collagen in the body and is found in bones, ligaments, tendons etc, whereas, type II collagen is found in articular (hyaline) cartilage. MACI is a two-stage procedure which uses autologous chondrocytes cultured on a porcine collagen membrane for the treatment of large chondral lesions in the knee. This is the third generation of chondrocyte implantation technology and is the first to utilize a scaffolding to grow chondrocytes. During the second stage of the procedure, the matrix can be secured with fibrin glue or sutures. Upon healing of the lesion, both type I and type II collagen can be identified.

*Schmiesing et al review the technique for open implantation of autologous chondrocytes for the treatment of full thickness patellar cartilage lesions.* *They review the procedure step-by-step, which includes starting with a diagnostic arthroscopy with chondrocyte harvesting and ultimately leads to implantation with stability provided by fibrin glue.* **They conclude** that MACI is a simpler technique than previous generations and also results in more reliable chondrocyte seeding.

Brittberg reviews cell carriers as the next generation of cell therapy for cartilage repair. He reports that patients treated with MACI have overall improved clinical outcomes including decreased visual analog scores and improvement in several clinical outcome measures. He concludes that MACI is a promising third-generation cell therapy for the repair of symptomatic, full thickness articular cartilage defects.

*Harris et al review autologous chondrocyte implantation.* **They report** that defect size >4cm^2 was the only factor predictive of better outcomes when MACI was compared with non-autologous chondrocyte implantation techniques. **They conclude** that successful outcomes can be obtained with microfracture, MACI and osteochondral autografts and that patient specific and defect specific factors influence overall clinical outcomes.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1: Chondroplasty is the debridement of loose cartilage flaps and does not result in restoration of Type II collagen.
Answer 2 & 3: Marrow stimulation techniques, which includes microfracture, results in fibrocartilage (Type I collagen) rather than hyaline cartilage, which is Type II collagen.
Answer 5: A single osteochondral autograft is a procedure in which autograft cartilage with attached bone is removed from a non weight bearing surface and placed in to the defect after drilling. This results in Type II cartilage only as the autograft plug fills the entire void.
