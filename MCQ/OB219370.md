## Question

A 32-year-old male suffered a fall while trail running 5 days prior and landed on outstretched arms with his hands out in front of him. He has persistent ulnar-sided wrist pain that is worse with palpation in the soft spot between the ulnar styloid and flexor carpi ulnaris. His distal radius ulnar joint is stable when compared to his uninjured side. Plain films and selected sequences of his wrist MRI are seen in Figures 1-3. What is the most appropriate treatment course?

## Options

1. Immobilization with NSAIDS
2. Diagnostic wrist arthroscopy with potential debridement
3. Triangular fibrocartilage complex repair
4. Ulnar diaphyseal shortening
5. Distal ulna Wafer procedure

## Correct option

**1. Immobilization with NSAIDS**

## Answer explanation

This patient presents with a traumatic triangular fibrocartilage complex tear (TFCC) injury after a fall on an outstretched hand. MRI confirms an ulnar avulsion of the TFCC, making this a Palmer 1B TFCC injury. The initial recommended treatment for an acute injury is immobilization and NSAIDS.

The TFCC encompasses the ligamentous/soft tissue complex providing stability to the distal radial-ulnar joint.TFCC injuries present with ulnar-sided wrist pain and are categorized as traumatic or degenerative injuries. Traumatic injuries are further classified as 1A-1D under the Palmer classification, as shown in Illustration A. The initial treatment of choice for all traumatic injuries is a trial of non-operative management including wrist immobilization, oral nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory medication, corticosteroid injections into the ulnocarpal joint, and/or occupational therapy. Surgical management is generally reserved for acute injuries with symptoms recalcitrant to conservative treatment, high-demand patients, chronic degenerative tears with ulnar impaction, and/or persistent DRUJ instability. This patient has an ulnar-sided avulsion without an ulnar styloid fracture and would best be treated initially with conservative management.

*Henry published a 2008 JAAOS review on the diagnostic and treatment modalities for acute TFCC injuries, detailing the Palmer classification (Illustration A) and the associated surgical options if non-operative management fails.* The recommendation is that all traumatic injuries with a stable DRUJ are initially treated with a course of immobilization, NSAIDS, corticosteroid injections, and physical therapy.

*Shin et al. analyzed 13 distal ulnae using microcomputed tomography with 3D reconstruction.* In addition, the distal radioulnar ligaments were examined under polarized light microscopy to determine the histological characteristics of collagen within the ligaments. The study showed that the deep ligament of the TFCC attaches to the ulna fovea while the superficial ligament inserts on the ulnar styloid (Illustration B).

Figures A and B are plain films of the wrist with no acute osseous abnormality and negative ulnar variance.

Figure C is a coronal T1 fat saturation MRI showing an avulsion of the TFCC from the ulna without ulnar styloid fracture, making this a Palmer 1B TFCC injury.

Illustration A depicts the Palmer classification for traumatic TFCC tears.

Illustration B shows the attachment of distal radioulnar ligaments as described in Shin et al.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: While arthroscopy is the diagnostic gold standard, this patient has not yet trialed non-operative care.
Answer 3: TFCC repair is indicated in 1B, 1C, and 1D tears that fail non-operative management.
Answer 4 & 5: Both of these answers are treatment options for degenerative or Type II tears.
