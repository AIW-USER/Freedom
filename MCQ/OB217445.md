## Question

A 43-year-old female sustains an intra-articular distal humerus fracture of her non-dominant arm. She underwent open reduction and internal fixation utilizing dual plating. Which subsequent surgery may be required to address the most common postoperative complication following this surgery?

## Options

1. Open release of the capsule and posterior bundle of the MUCL
2. Revision ORIF for malunion
3. Revision ORIF for nonunion
4. Removal of hardware for painful implants
5. Removal of heterotopic ossification

## Correct option

**1. Open release of the capsule and posterior bundle of the MUCL**

## Answer explanation

This patient sustained a distal humerus fracture that was treated with ORIF. Postoperative stiffness is the most common complication following ORIF of these injuries.

Distal humerus fractures are traumatic injuries to the elbow that comprise of supracondylar fractures, single column fractures, column fractures or coronal shear fractures. Diagnosis is made with plain radiographs of the humerus and elbow. CT scan is helpful for intra-articular assessment and operative planning. Treatment is usually ORIF, however total elbow arthroplasty may be utilized in comminuted intra-articular fractures in elderly patients. The most common complication following ORIF of these injuries is stiffness.

*Nowak et al. reviewed humerus fractures.* **They reported** that the majority of intra-articular distal humerus fractures should be treated with plate fixation; dual plating is generally accepted as the gold standard treatment, while the optimal surgical approach and plate configuration requires more research. **They reported** that stiffness is the most common postoperative complication following distal humerus fracture fixation.

*Patel et al. reviewed the complications after operative treatment of distal humerus fractures with anatomic, pre-contoured, locking distal humeral plates.* **They reported** a mean complication rate of 61%; elbow stiffness (19%) was the most common complication followed by nerve palsy (16%). Other complications included fracture nonunions (9%), deep infections (9%), painful implants (9%), post-traumatic arthritis (9%), and heterotopic ossification (9%). **They concluded** that distal humerus fractures treated with ORIF utilizing anatomic, pre-contoured, locking distal humeral plates have a high complication rate, with many requiring reoperation.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 2-5: These complications may occur following ORIF of distal humerus fractures, but are much less common than stiffness.
