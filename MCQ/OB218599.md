## Question

A 25-year-old male presents to your clinic for evaluation of his second toe deformity. He says that over a year ago he had a traumatic injury where he hyper-extended his foot. The pain gradually improved but now he is having pain from the deformity of his second toe. A radiograph of his deformity can be seen in Figure A. Which of the following is most likely present on physical examination?

![[q218599-a-screen-shot-2023-01-16-at-6-05-14-pm.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Improvement in pain with hyper-extension
2. Pain exacerbated with axial loading of the MTP joint
3. Pain exacerbated with distraction of the MTP joint
4. Increased laxity of the MTP joint with dorsal/plantar directed force
5. Valgus instability of the MTP joint

## Correct option

**4. Increased laxity of the MTP joint with dorsal/plantar directed force**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a chronic plantar plate injury resulting in a crossover toe deformity. A vertical Lachman test, which assesses the stability of the second metatarsophalangeal (MTP) joint, would be positive. This indicates an injury to the plantar plate, as it is the main stabilizer of the MTP joint.

Plantar plate injuries come as a result of hyperextension and axial loading injury to the forefoot. These are also known as “turf toe” injuries, as they commonly occur in athletes on turf fields. While the great toe is the most commonly injured toe, plantar plate tears can also occur in the lesser toes. The diagnosis can be made with plantar forefoot pain made worse with extension and a positive vertical Lachman. Diagnosis is confirmed radiographically with an MRI scan. Disruption of the plantar plate can also lead to a crossover toe deformity. Activity and shoe wear modification are the first-line treatment options. Surgical options include direct repair of the plantar plate or advancement into bone tunnels.

*Doty et al. wrote a review on MTP instability and plantar plate deficiency.* **They note** that primary repair of the plantar plate results in reliably good results. This can be done both plantarly and dorsally. Other surgical options include advancement of the plantar plate through bone tunnels.

*Coughlin et al. dissected 16 cadaveric specimens with clinical second-toe crossover.* **They found** that all specimens had transverse tears of the plantar plate. Severe deformities had mid-substance tears, collateral ligament tears, and complete disruption of the plantar plate. Based on these findings they developed an anatomical grading system to describe these progressive changes seen in the plantar plate.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Patients with plantar plate injuries would have worsened pain with hyperextension.
Answer 2: Pain that is exacerbated with compaction is typical of late Freiberg’s disease.
Answer 3: Pain that is exacerbated with distraction is typical of early Freiberg’s disease.
Answer 5: Valgus instability would be seen in MCL deficiency, whereas the pathophysiology of second toe crossover comes from plantar plate disruption and possible varus instability.
