## Question

A 65-year-old female is in your clinic for her preoperative visit for an upcoming total elbow arthroplasty (TEA). As part of the informed consent process, you explain to her the risks, expected benefits, and alternative treatments for her upcoming surgery. Which of the following statements regarding TEA is most accurate?

## Options

1. A triceps reflecting approach is not associated with any long-term loss of elbow extension power
2. Complication rates following TEA for for rheumatoid arthritis are higher in patients who are not immobilized postoperatively
3. Out of the three major implant designs, semiconstrained components demonstrate the most favorable results
4. When used for acute fracture in a geriatric patient, TEA allows for more predictable functional outcomes, at the expense of a slower recovery, when compared to internal fixation
5. When used for the sequelae of rheumatoid arthritis, TEA confers 10-year survivorship rates of >90% with complication rates <5%

## Correct option

**3. Out of the three major implant designs, semiconstrained components demonstrate the most favorable results**

## Answer explanation

Out of the three major total elbow arthroplasty (TEA) implant designs, semi-constrained components demonstrate more favorable results when compared to both unconstrained/unlinked and constrained components (Answer 3).

TEA is a motion-preserving modality used for the treatment of many debilitating elbow pathologies. Originally utilized for the sequelae of end-stage rheumatoid arthritis (RA), indications for this procedure have expanded to post-traumatic arthritis, primary osteoarthritis, and intra-articular distal humeral fractures in the elderly and/or low-demand patients with poor bone stock. Survivorship, outcomes, and complications vary based on surgical indication, and patients undergoing TEA for RA can be expected to demonstrate 10-year survival rates of >90%. Unfortunately, complication rates following TEA are relatively high, and patients undergoing TEA for RA have demonstrated complication rates as high as 15%.

A variety of implant designs and surgical approaches are available for utilization by surgeons. Concerning approach, the optimal management of the triceps tendon remains unknown, and a variety of surgical strategies (triceps reflecting, triceps tongue, triceps-splitting, etc.) have been proposed. Three major implant designs are currently available (Illustrations A-C), with semi-constrained implants demonstrating the most favorable outcomes. As mentioned above, regardless of approach and implant design, complications are common and include aseptic loosening (radiographic 17%, clinical 6%), infection (8%), instability (7-19%), bushing wear, wound healing complications, ulnar neuropathy, and triceps insufficiency.

*Voloshin et al. performed a systematic review investigating the complications following TEA.* *Their study pooled data from 64 studies reporting on 2,938 patients.* The overall complication rate was 24.3 ± 5.8%. The most common complications reported included clinical loosening, instability, deep infection, and intraoperative fracture. Triceps weakness was observed in 1.2 ± 3.3% of patients following a triceps reflecting approach. The authors did note, however, that documentation of triceps status in the literature is poor, and that the incidence reported in their paper likely understates the true extent of this complication.

*Kim et al. also provide a review of the literature regarding complications following TEA.* **The authors note** that the overall rate of triceps insufficiency following TEA ranges from 2% to 8%, but can be as high as 30% following a triceps-reflecting approach. **They also note** that constrained components demonstrate the highest rates of loosening. The authors do note that while TEA for RA is associated with a high 10-year survivorship rate, complications are common.

*Polmear et al. performed a systematic review investigating the optimal duration of post-operative immobilization following TEA for RA.* *Their study pooled data from 36 articles reporting 2,346 elbows in 2,015 patients.* Overall, **the authors found** that total complication rates were lowest in elbows without post-operative immobilization, but long-term survival rates were greatest in elbows with extended post-operative immobilization.

Illustration A demonstrates an unconstrained/unlinked component. Given the lack of connection, instability is a common complication with this implant design. Illustration B demonstrates a semi-constrained (linked) component, characterized by a sloppy hinge which allows for some degree of varus/valgus and rotational laxity, thereby reducing stress on the bone-cement interface. Illustration C is a constrained (rigid hinged) design, which is (theoretically) the most stable design but, due to the lack of motion afforded through the rigid hinge, demonstrates high rates of loosening.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: A triceps reflecting approach is associated with loss of extension power in up to 30% of patients.
Answer 2: Complication rates following TEA for RA are lowest in patients who are not immobilized postoperatively.
Answer 4: TEA for acute fracture allows for a quicker and more predictable return to recovery when compared to internal fixation.
Answer 5: TEA for RA is associated with high survivorship rates (>90%) with overall complication rates of approximately 15%.
