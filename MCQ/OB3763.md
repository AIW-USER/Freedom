## Question

Which of the following metastatic tumours should be treated with embolisation prior to surgical management?

## Options

1. Renal, thyroid
2. Breast, renal
3. Liver, breast
4. Lung, colon
5. Prostate, thyroid

## Correct option

**1. Renal, thyroid**

## Answer explanation

Renal and thyroid tumors are considered hypervascular lesions and should be considered for treatment with preoperative embolization.

The main indication for embolisation of hypervascular tumors is to reduce intraoperative and postoperative blood loss. The aim of embolization is the exclusion of the tumor capillary bed (not the major arterial feeder), as occluding the major vessel supplying the tumor will lead to revascularization of the tumor due to numerous collaterals in hypervascular bone tumours. Liquid embolisation agents include glue, absolute alcohol, sodium tetradecyl sulphate, and particulate agents include Embosphere™, polyvinyl alcohol (PVA)-particles (Contour™) and Gelfoam™.

*Itoh et al. looked at the role of vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) in thyroid tumour angiogenesis.* VEGF was not detected in normal thyroid follicular cells, and weakly detectable in benign follicular adenoma and adenomatous goiters. However, in malignant thyroid tumors, it was strongly expressed. **They conclude** that VEGF is expressed in the hyperplastic thyroid tissues and is a marker for transformation and differentiation state of malignant tumours.

*Cernoch et al. used 3 different pre-operative embolisation techniques in 46 patients with metastatic spinal tumours and compared intraoperative blood loss.* Overall, 33 percent of tumours undergoing embolization were renal cell carcinoma. **They found** no statistically significant difference regarding blood loss between coils, particles or a combination of both.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 2-5: Renal and thyroid tumours are the most vascular tumours.
