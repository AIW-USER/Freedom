## Question

A 47-year-old female presents to clinic with a gradual onset of joint pain over the last 6 months. She states that she has had pain in both wrists, the majority of her proximal interphalangeal (PIP) joints, and her ankles that is associated with morning stiffness. On exam, there are subcutaneous nodules on her fingers (Figure A). Which of the following autoantibodies is most likely present?

![[q220961-a-screenshot-2025-12-21-at-7-55-13-pm.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Autoantibodies against double-stranded DNA (Anti-dsDNA)
2. Autoantibodies against immunoglobulin G
3. Autoantibodies against the acetylcholine receptor
4. Autoantibodies against topoisomerase I
5. Autoantibodies against glutamic acid decarboxylase

## Correct option

**2. Autoantibodies against immunoglobulin G**

## Answer explanation

Symmetric polyarthropathy with morning stiffness and findings of rheumatoid nodules is consistent with a diagnosis of rheumatoid arthritis. This diagnosis is associated with the presence of a positive rheumatoid factor titer, which is an autoantibody against immunoglobulin G (Answer 2).

Rheumatoid arthritis is an autoimmune condition characterized by an abnormal immune response leading to pannus formation (inflamed synovial tissue) and subsequent joint damage and destruction. Patients present with symmetric polyarthropathy frequently involving the hands and feet but sparing the distal interphalangeal joints. Other musculoskeletal manifestations include subcutaneous nodules, ulnar deviation of the digits, hallux valgus, and swan neck deformities. Anti-cyclic citrullinated peptide (anti-CCP) is the most specific serum marker for rheumatoid arthritis, but other markers, such as rheumatoid factor and anti-mutated citrullinated vimentin, may also be positive. The current diagnostic criteria for rheumatoid arthritis assign distinct point values across 4 categories, which are based on the number of joints involved, the presence of positive rheumatoid factor or anti-CCP antibodies, the presence of abnormal inflammatory markers (ESR and CRP), and symptom duration.

*Shapiro and Wormser published a review on Lyme disease, covering new literature and treatment strategies for this condition.* *They cover Borrelia Miyamotoi infections, which can mimic Lyme diseases, the elimination of Western blots in the 2-tiered diagnostic algorithm, the use of short-term use doxycycline in children under 8 years of age, and recommended antibiotic regimens.* **They conclude** that Lyme disease is a very relevant medical problem that contributes to morbidity across the United States and needs continued research to further treatment strategies.

*McInnes and Schett performed a review of rheumatoid arthritis and the underlying pathologic mechanisms underlying this condition.* *They discuss a multitude of disease components, including the genetic and environmental risk factors predisposing to the development of this disease, the faulty regulatory immune pathways present (including those which result in synovitis, bone erosion, and cartilage destruction secondary to the loss of protective synovium), and the systemic manifestations of the disease.* **They conclude** that the extensive research surrounding rheumatoid arthritis has led to the development of various biological agents that can successfully induce disease remission; however, there are still various gaps of knowledge surrounding joint health and different systemic manifestations that need to be addressed with additional investigation.

Figure A demonstrates subcutaneous nodules that may be present in patients with rheumatoid arthritis (also known as rheumatoid nodules).

Incorrect Answers
Answer 1: Anti-dsDNA antibodies are associated with Lupus.
Answer 3: Anti-acetylcholine receptor antibodies are associated with Myasthenia Gravis.
Answer 4: Anti-topoisomerase I antibodies are associated with Scleroderma.
Answer 5: Anti-glutamic acid decarboxylase antibodies are associated with Type 1 Diabetes.
