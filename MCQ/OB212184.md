## Question

Which of the following increases the risk of osseous overgrowth following microfracture?

## Options

1. Increased awl perforations per unit surface area
2. Use of smaller awls (<1.2 mm diameter)
3. Entrance angle of 90 degrees to the articular surface
4. Removal of the subchondral plate
5. Removal of the calcified cartilage layer

## Correct option

**4. Removal of the subchondral plate**

## Answer explanation

Removal of the subchondral plate during lesion preparation is a significant risk factor for osseous overgrowth following microfracture.

Microfracture is a marrow stimulation procedure for repair of cartilaginous defects. Osseous overgrowth has emerged as an increasingly recognized complication following this procedure. Increasing size of the osseous overgrowth decreases the volume of repair cartilage within the defect, and can affect the biomechanics and overall outcome of this procedure. Violation of the subchondral plate, which most commonly occurs during over aggressive debridement of the cartilage cap, has been identified as one of the main risk factors for the development of osseous overgrowth.

*Kaul et al review the resultant repair tissue following failed marrow stimulation treatments for articular defects in patients with osteoarthritis.* **They report** that repaired cartilage defects were mostly filled with fissured fibrocartilaginous tissue that stained for Types I, II and X collagen. **The authors concluded** that articular cartilage repair did not produce quality on par with the surrounding normal hyaline cartilage.

*Mithoefer et al review the incidence, degree, and clinical effect of subchondral bone overgrowth following microfracture of the knee.* **The authors report** that debridement and incidental removal of the subchondral plate is a significant risk factor for osseous overgrowth. **They conclude** that outcomes following microfracture are diminished in patients who develop this complication.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Over-penetration of the awl per unit surface area was not found to be related to failure rates following microfracture.
Answer 2: Some studies have found improved outcomes with the use of smaller awls (1.0mm) compared to larger awls (1.2mm)
Answer 3: Entrance angles should ideally be perpendicular to the joint surface to avoid skipping and to prevent collapse of adjacent microfracture holes.
Answer 5: Removal of the cartilage cap should be performed during microfracture.
