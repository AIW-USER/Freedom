## Question

A 13-year-old male presents with lower back pain and hamstring tightness of 1-month duration. The patient underwent a routine radiograph which is demonstrated in figure A. Which of the following factors are associated with the progression of this condition?

![[q214059-a-edit-highgrade-spondy.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Acute onset of symptoms
2. Presentation after adolescent growth spurt
3. Male gender
4. > Myerding 2 grade spondylolisthesis on presentation
5. Decreased lumbosacral kyphosis

## Correct option

**4. > Myerding 2 grade spondylolisthesis on presentation**

## Answer explanation

Higher grade spondylolisthesis on initial diagnosis is associated with slip progression in pediatric patients.

Pediatric spondylolysis is present in 4-6% of the population. Spondylolysis can progress to spondylolisthesis in this population and can occur in 15% of patients with spondylolysis. Spondylolisthesis can be the result of a pars interarticularis defect, also termed isthmic spondylolisthesis, or due to doming of the S1 superior endplate, dysplastic facet joints, elongated pars, or spina bifida at L5 or S1 (dysplastic spondylolisthesis). Risk factors for slip progression include presentation before adolescent growth spurt, female gender, >50% slip on initial diagnosis, dysplastic spondylolisthesis, and increased slip angle.

*Tsirikos et al. reviewed the etiology, pathoanatomy, diagnosis, and treatment of pediatric spondylolysis and spondylolisthesis.* **They stated** most symptomatic patients with spondylolysis and low-grade spondylolisthesis present with mechanical low-back pain and hamstring tightness that is worse with prolonged standing or activity. The concluded that nonoperative treatment is effective in over 80% of patients and surgical intervention should be reserved for those with progressive slips, persistent symptoms despite conservative treatment for 6 months, and neurologic deficits.

*Cavalier et al. reviewed the diagnosis, natural history, and nonsurgical management of pediatric spondylosis and spondylolisthesis.* **The authors stated** the vast majority of patients with spondylolysis and low-grade spondylolisthesis are asymptomatic and rarely does the slippage progress. **They recommended** careful examination for discogenic, abdominal, and pelvic sources of lower back pain in patients presenting with spondylolysis or low-grade spondylolisthesis as this pathology is rarely symptomatic.

Figure A is a lateral radiograph of the lumbar spine with grade 3 isthmic spondylolisthesis.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: The acuity of the patient's symptoms has not been correlated with an increased risk of slip progression.
Answer 2: Presentation prior to adolescent growth spurt is associated with slip progression.
Answer 3: Females have a higher risk of slip progression than males.
Answer 5: Increased lumbosacral kyphosis, which is expressed as the slip angle, is associated with an increased likelihood of slip progression.
