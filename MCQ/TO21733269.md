Chapter: Pediatric Orthopedics MCQs — Tibial Bowing

## Question

Congenital fibular deficiency is associated with all except

## Options

1. Anteromedial bowing
2. Anterolateral bowing
3. Fibular anlage
4. Equinovalgus foot

## Correct option

**2. Anterolateral bowing**

## Answer explanation

Answer : B
congenital fibular deficiency (CFD), also called fibular hypoplasia and fibular hemi-melia, is
the most common congenital long bone deficiency, with an approximate incidence of 7.4
to20 per 1 million live births.
Etiology: The etiology of CFD remains unclear. Most cases are sporadic; however, chromosomal anomalies and autosomal dominant, autosomal recessive, and X-linked transmission have been reported.
Clinical features: clinical features vary from mild limb inequalities and asymmetry to severe
shortening, with a wide range of associated anomalies. Bilateral involvement is not
uncommon. Most patients with a complete CFD present with anterior or anteromedial
bowing of the tibia; half of these patients typically have a skin dimple at the apex of the
deformity. Sixty percent of patients with complete CFD have a palpable posterior band(ie,
fibular anlage), representing a fibrocartilaginous fibular remnant. This anlage produces
tethering effects that lead to tibial bowing.
Associated abnormalities: Upper extremity involvement ranges from syndactyly to extensive
deficiencies. Congenital shortening of the femur and proximal focal femoral deficiency, genu
valgum, ACL/PCL deficiency, patella alta, and hypoplastic patella have been.
In the feet, equinovalgus deformities (equinovarus is less frequent), missing feet rays, and
ankle instability and tarsal coalition are commonly seen.

Management: Optimal candidates for an orthosis typically are patients with mild forms of
CFD, a functional plantigrade foot, and mild (>6%) LLD. These patients can be managed
successfully with shoe lifts and foot orthoses.

The decision to manage patients with CFD by amputation or limb lengthening is a matter of
debate. In general, patients with severe foot deformity (ie,three or more absent rays) and a
predicted LLD of 30% at the age of skeletal maturity, or >5 cm of discrepancy at birth, are
candidates for amputation and prosthetic rehabilitation.
In moderate LLD (10-30%), both procedures (Contralateral epiphysisodesis and limb
lengthening) can be done together.
