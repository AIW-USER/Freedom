## Question

What is the most common causative organism in an acute pediatric sacro-iliac septic arthritis?

## Options

1. Group B streptococcal
2. Hemophilus influenza
3. Mycobacterium tuberculosis
4. Salmonella
5. Staphylococcus aureus

## Correct option

**5. Staphylococcus aureus**

## Answer explanation

Staphylococcus aureus is the causative organism found in most sacroiliac joint (SIJ) infections. First line treatment is conservative with use of antibiotics.

SIJ septic arthritis is rare and accounts for only 1-2% of septic arthritis cases. These patients often present with complaints of low back and buttock pain with inability to bear weight on the affected side. MRI is the most sensitive diagnostic study for SIJ infections. Blood cultures may be positive and should be obtained prior to starting antibiotics. Unless there is an unusual factor in the history such as IV drug use, immune system compromise, or unusual travel, SIJ aspiration is unnecessary as these infections are often managed successfully with IV antibiotics targeted from organisms identified on blood cultures. Surgical debridement is rarely needed but may be indicated in acute cases with the presence of an abscess or those who have failed conservative management. SIJ arthrodesis is reserved for chronic cases with significant joint destruction or in those with recurrent infection.

*Beaupre et al. reviewed pediatric iliac osteomyelitis.* **They found** that the most commonly identified organism was coagulase-positive Staphylococcus aureus. **They concluded** that when diagnosed early, the infection can be cured with intensive intravenous antibiotic therapy alone.

*Reilly et al. retrospectively review 17 pediatric patients who were treated for a disorder of a SIJ.* **They report** that pain in the hip, thigh, and buttock was the most common symptom. Of the 13 acutely ill patients, 11 had septic arthritis of a SIJ. Of the 4 patients who had chronic symptoms, two had septic arthritis of a SIJ. **They noted** that organisms were cultured successfully from blood, material aspirated from the SIJ, or from stool of all 13 patients who had sepsis. **They concluded** that the 13 infections responded well to appropriate antibiotics, which were administered intravenously to 7 patients and first intravenously and then orally to 6 patients.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Group B streptococcal infection is the most common organism of osteomyelitis in neonates
Answer 2: Hemophilus influenza is an uncommon organism in osteomyelitis in pediatric patients since the advent of the H. influenza vaccine
Answer 3: Skeletal tuberculosis accounts for 3-5% of all tuberculosis, of which 10% occurs at the SI joint but is not the most common cause of SIJ infections.
Answer 4: Salmonella is much less common than S. aureus but may occur in sickle cell disease
