## Question

A 78-year-old man presents to the emergency department with persistent neck pain after a ground-level fall 10 days ago. He has a remote history of cervical spine surgery but does not recall the exact procedure. His medical history is significant for emphysema, and he reports smoking one pack of cigarettes per day with a 65 pack-year history. On examination, he is neurovascularly intact and has a barrel-shaped chest. Selected coronal and sagittal CT images of the cervical spine are shown in Figures A and B. What is this patient’s risk of nonunion, and what is the most appropriate treatment?

## Options

1. Moderate risk of nonunion; halo vest immobilization is the treatment of choice
2. Low risk for nonunion; observation with soft collar is sufficient
3. Moderate risk of nonunion; rigid cervical collar immobilization is acceptable
4. High risk of nonunion; anterior odontoid screw fixation is preferred
5. High risk for nonunion; C1-C2 posterior spinal fusion

## Correct option

**5. High risk for nonunion; C1-C2 posterior spinal fusion**

## Answer explanation

An odontoid fracture through the waist (Anderson D’Alonso type II) in an elderly patient with a delayed presentation in an active smoker has a high risk for nonunion. Given the patient’s age and history of emphysema with a barrel chest, C1-C2 posterior spinal fusion is preferred (answer choice 5).

The management of type II odontoid fractures is controversial. Treatment algorithms focus on the risk of nonunion. Fractures through the waist of the odontoid are in a watershed zone and are at increased risk for nonunion. Apart from fracture location, multiple patient and fracture characteristics are associated with an increased risk of nonunion. Such factors include increasing age, delayed surgical intervention, smoking history, osteoporosis, greater displacement, and angulation ≥11 degrees. In patients with an elevated risk of nonunion, operative management is associated with increased union rates and decreased mortality, particularly in elderly patients (>65 years old). Posterior C1-C2 spinal fusion in elderly patients is associated with higher union rates than anterior odontoid screw fixation.

*Ricciardi et al. evaluated clinical, radiographic, and surgical risk factors affecting outcomes after anterior odontoid screw fixation for type II odontoid fractures.* **They noted** a union rate of 77%, with multiple factors associated with higher rates of nonunion; delay of >7 days from injury to surgery was associated with a 48-fold higher risk of nonunion.

*Tyagi et al. evaluated the surgical nuances, complications, and factors affecting fracture union after anterior odontoid screw fixation.* **They noted** a union rate of 84%. **They concluded** that anterior odontoid screw fixation preserves cervical mobility when compared to posterior spinal fusion and that, with careful patient selection, good outcomes are achievable.

*Yuan et al. compared anterior odontoid screw fixation with posterior C1-C2 instrumentation without fusion in patients with type II odontoid fractures.* **They noted** union rates of 91% with anterior odontoid screw fixation and 96% with posterior instrumentation without fusion. **They conclude** that similar union rates can be achieved with either technique, but that anterior odontoid screws preserve more motion; however, if anterior screw fixation is contraindicated, posterior instrumentation is a good alternative.

Figures A and B are sagittal and coronal CT images of the cervical spine demonstrating a fracture through the waist of the odontoid. Additionally, findings from prior cervical spine surgery are seen.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: This underestimates the nonunion risk and recommends an inappropriate treatment, as halo vests are associated with high complication rates and poor tolerance in patients over 75 years old. Halo immobilization is generally contraindicated in elderly patients with odontoid fractures.
Answer 2: This significantly underestimates the nonunion risk in elderly patients with type II odontoid fractures, where rates approach 60-71% with conservative management. A soft collar provides inadequate immobilization for an unstable type II fracture.
Answer 3: This underestimates the patient's risk for nonunion. However, a rigid cervical orthosis may be appropriate for elderly patients in whom a stable fibrous nonunion is an acceptable endpoint.
Answer 4: While the risk assessment is accurate, anterior screw fixation shows no significant advantage over conservative treatment in elderly patients and has higher pseudarthrosis rates in this population. The patient's barrel chest from emphysema also makes the anterior approach technically difficult.
