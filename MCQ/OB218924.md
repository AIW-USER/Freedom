## Question

The pediatrician orders XRs (shown in Figure B), and refers the patient to a pediatric orthopaedic surgeon. What is the most likely etiology of the condition associated with the findings in Figures A and B?

## Options

1. Autosomal recessive (AR) inheritance
2. Post-natal fracture non-union
3. Homeobox D10 (HOXD10) gene mutation
4. Intrauterine positioning
5. Sonic hedgehog (Shh) gene mutation

## Correct option

**4. Intrauterine positioning**

## Answer explanation

The patient presents with both posteromedial tibial bowing and calcaneovalgus foot deformities. These conditions are often associated with one another and are the result of intrauterine positioning.

Posteromedial tibial bowing is a congenital condition that presents at birth and usually involves the middle and distal third of the tibia. It is not associated with any known genetic associations or familial patterns of inheritance. Though often associated with calcaneovalgus foot deformity when the apex of the bowing is at the ankle and causes a pathologic dorsiflexion contracture, the two conditions can occur independently of one another. Diagnosis is made radiographically with XRs of the tibia (Figure B), and treatment typically involves observation until the deformity spontaneously resolves between ages 5 and 7. The most likely consequence is limb length discrepancy, which may require limb equalization procedures after resolution of the deformity.

Wright. et al, provide a review of 38 patients with posteromedial tibial bowing. A total of 20 patients (53%) required limb lengthening procedures given significant residual deformity. The authors note that significantly higher rates of limb length discrepancy were seen in those undergoing lengthening under the age of ten.

Di Gennaro., et al report on 44 cases of posteromedial tibial bowing, and found an average limb length discrepancy of 4.3 cm. Twenty-six patients underwent surgical treatment, including 23 limb lengthening procedures, 1 corrective tibial osteotomy, 1 contralateral epiphysiodesis, and 1 ipsilateral hemiepiphysiodesis of the distal tibia to correct the residual ankle valgus. Further research is needed to delineate the best treatment strategy for deformity correction during childhood.

Figure A is a clinical photograph demonstrating calcaneovalgus of the right foot, with a normal contralateral foot. Figure B is an AP and lateral XR demonstrating posteromedial bowing of the right tibia.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Posteromedial tibial bowing is not associated with any form of familial inheritance pattern.
Answer 2: Post-natal fracture and pseudarthrosis is associated with anterolateral tibial bowing, which has a strong association with Neurofibromatosis Type I.
Answer 3: HOXD10 is associated with congenital vertical talus, not calcaneovalgus foot or posteromedial tibial bowing.
Answer 5: Shh gene mutation is associated with anteromedial tibial bowing and fibular hemimelia.
