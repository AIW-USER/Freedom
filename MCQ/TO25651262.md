## Question

Regarding the molecular mechanism of PTH receptor (PTHR1) signaling, which statement best describes the difference between Abaloparatide and Teriparatide?

## Options

1. Abaloparatide acts as a selective PTHR2 agonist.
2. Abaloparatide binds more selectively to the R0 state.
3. Abaloparatide lacks any affinity for the RG state.
4. Abaloparatide binds more selectively to the RG state.

## Correct option

**4. Abaloparatide binds more selectively to the RG state.**

## Answer explanation

Abaloparatide has a higher affinity for the (active/G-protein coupled) state of the receptor than the state, leading to a more transient cAMP response and potentially less induction of bone resorption compared to Teriparatide.

While both teriparatide and abaloparatide are anabolic agents that bind to the same parathyroid hormone type 1 receptor (PTH1R), their mechanism of action differs fundamentally in receptor binding kinetics and conformational selectivity.

Molecular Origins

 - Teriparatide: A recombinant fragment of endogenous human parathyroid hormone, consisting of the first 34 amino acids, PTH (1-34).

 - Abaloparatide: A synthetic analog of human parathyroid hormone-related protein, PTHrP (1-34), possessing 41% homology to teriparatide.

Receptor Binding Dynamics

The PTH1R receptor exists in two distinct functional conformations at the cell surface:

 - R0 Conformation:
 Associated with prolonged intracellular signaling, which strongly stimulates osteoclastic activity and drives bone resorption.

 - RG Conformation:
 Associated with transient signaling, which preferentially stimulates osteoblastic activity and drives bone formation.

The Divergence in Affinity

 - Teriparatide: Binds with high affinity to both the R0 and RG conformations. This prolonged cyclic AMP (cAMP) signaling leads to a robust stimulation of bone formation, but simultaneously triggers significant bone resorption.

 - Abaloparatide: Binds with high affinity to the RG conformation but has an 8.5-fold lower affinity for the R0 conformation compared to teriparatide. This transient binding heavily favors the anabolic pathway, effectively uncoupling bone formation from bone resorption.

Clinical Consequences of Differential Binding

Because abaloparatide stimulates osteoblasts with minimal subsequent osteoclast activation, it yields specific clinical differences:

 - Cortical Bone Preservation:
 Teriparatide's strong resorptive effect often leads to early cortical porosity and a transient decrease in bone mineral density (BMD) at the distal radius. Abaloparatide minimizes this cortical remodeling, resulting in superior BMD preservation and gains at the radius and femoral neck.

 - Overall BMD Velocity: The "anabolic window" (the period where formation outpaces resorption) is maximized with abaloparatide. This results in faster and greater total BMD gains at the lumbar spine and total hip compared to teriparatide.

 - Hypercalcemia Risk: Due to the diminished stimulation of bone resorption, the incidence of hypercalcemia is significantly lower with abaloparatide.

Answer: 4
