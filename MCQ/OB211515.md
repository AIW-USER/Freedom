## Question

## Answer explanation

Staph aureus, a gram-positive cocci found in clusters, is most commonly associated with acute THA periprosthetic joint infections (PJI).

PJI can be classified based on the timing of the onset of symptoms, with acute infections occurring in the first 6 weeks postoperatively. Acute infections are most commonly caused by staphylococcus aureus, a gram-positive cocci found in clusters. In acute infections treated with debridement, antibiotics, and implant retention (DAIR), the bacterial species and the antibiotic chosen following surgery are two of the major determinants of a successful outcome. Conversely, S. epidermidis, S. viridans, and P. acnes are more commonly isolated in chronic infections.

*Ottesen et al. review their experience with debridement, antibiotics and implant retention (DAIR) following PJI of the knee.* **They report** S. aureus to be the inciting bacteria in half of all PJIs with streptococcus being the second most common (27%). Furthermore, **they report** a success rate of 89% in the treatment of acute PJI caused by S. aureus, and an overall success rate of 90% regardless of the bacteria isolated in patients presenting within 90 days of their index procedure. **They conclude** that DAIR is a viable option for the treatment of acute PJI.

*Fink et al. review a standardized regimen for the treatment of acute postoperative infections and acute hematogenous infections associated with THA and TKA.* **They report** a success rate of 82.1% following acute infections treated with DAIR. They too found S. aureus to be the most commonly encountered organism. Variables that influenced the recurrence of infection were the timespan between revision and the first appearance of symptoms (<2 days), the number of previous operations, ASA score and nicotine use. **They conclude** that a reproducibly high rate of success can be achieved following this technique for acute PJI.

Figure A demonstrates gram-positive cocci in chains, consistent with streptococcus species. Figure B shows gram-positive cocci in clusters as is found with S. aureus. Figure C depicts gram-negative bacteria, and in this case, shows Pseudomonas aeruginosa. Figure D shows gram-negative cocci consistent with Neisseria. Figure E is the photomicrograph of Borrelia burgdorferi, the pathogen responsible for Lyme disease.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: S. aureus is found in clusters, not in chains like streptococcus species.
Answers 3 and 4: S. aureus is a gram-positive bacterium, not gram-negative.
Answer 5: Spirochetes such as Borrelia burgdorferi are not a common cause of periprosthetic infection.
