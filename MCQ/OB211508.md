## Question

A second year resident is coordinating a research study to evaluate skin healing with the use of a new bandage following total knee arthroplasty. Patient randomization assignments are computer generated and sealed in opaque envelopes which are opened following skin closure prior to application of the dressing. Which of the following is minimized with the use of this protocol?

## Options

1. Number needed to harm
2. Selection bias
3. Crossover
4. Power
5. Attritional bias

## Correct option

**2. Selection bias**

## Answer explanation

Selection bias is limited with the use of randomization.

Selection bias is a type of bias that may occur with clinical research in which recruiters selectively enroll patients in a trial based on what they expect the next treatment allocation to be. Randomization and blinding are ways in which selection bias can be minimized. In this scenario, randomization is carried out by computer generation and the results are sealed in opaque envelopes, which limits selection bias as there is no way to predict which patients will be involved with the treatment arm.

*Spindler et al review techniques for reading and reviewing orthopedic literature.* **They report** that randomization attempts to ensure that the treatment groups under evaluation are initially similar with respect to known (measurable) and unknown (immeasurable) sources of bias. **They conclude** that selection bias can even be present in randomized trials if the researcher is able to predict which arm the next enrolled patient will be assigned.

*Poolman et al review whether or not blinding of assessors is needed in randomized controlled trials.* **They report** that among the studies with continuous outcome measure, unblinded outcomes assessment was associated with significantly larger treatment effects than blinded outcomes assessment. **They conclude** that whenever possible, outcome assessors should be blinded to limit bias.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Number needed to harm is a measure of how many people need to be treated (or exposed to a risk factor) in order for one person to have a particular adverse effect. This is unrelated to randomization and blinding.
Answer 3: Crossover is a type of study in which there is administration of two or more therapies, one after the other, in a random order. This design is subject to bias if a sufficient washout period is not achieved between treatments.
Answer 4: Power is the probability that a test of significance will detect a deviation from the null hypothesis, should such a deviation exist. This is not related to how patients are entered in to each treatment arm of a study.
Answer 5: Attritional bias occurs when patients do not complete the entire study period.
