## Question

A 4-year-old patient presents for follow-up of left hip dysplasia previously treated with closed reduction and spica cast application The patient does not have any symptoms at this time. Figure A is an AP radiograph of the pelvis during the visit. What is the most appropriate treatment option?

![[q213089-a-ddh-edit.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Continued observation with routine follow-up
2. Left varus derotational osteotomy with shortening, continued observation of right hip
3. Open reduction with spica casting
4. Left pelvic osteotomy
5. Repeat closed reduction with spica casting

## Correct option

**4. Left pelvic osteotomy**

## Answer explanation

The patient is presenting with residual left-sided developmental hip dysplasia despite a previous closed reduction with spica casting. There is residual dysplasia with increased acetabular index which would be best addressed with a pelvic osteotomy to increase acetabular coverage.

Developmental dysplasia of the hip (DDH) is the abnormal development of the hip that includes a spectrum from dysplasia to a complete dislocation. Firstborn, female, and breech neonates are risk factors. with the left side being affected in 60% of cases. Despite successful reduction in a Pavlik harness or spica cast, the acetabulum may demonstrate residual dysplasia as shown by an increased acetabular index. Pelvic osteotomies are considered in patients with an acetabular index greater than 28 degrees after 4 years of age, though exact indications continue to evolve.

*Vitale et al. reviewed the literature regarding the treatment of DDH between 6 months and 4 years of age.* For patients 18 months to 4 years of age with persistently dislocated hips, **the authors recommend** open reduction and capsulorrhaphy with high dislocated hip requiring varus derotational osteotomy with or without a pelvic osteotomy depending on the stability.

*Smith et al. performed a retrospective radiographic study looking at the predictive power of the acetabular teardrop on outcomes in DDH.* **The authors reported** the appearance of the acetabular teardrop in DDH patients only after a stable reduction with radiographic appearance occurring within 6 months of treatment leading to good outcomes. **The authors concluded** that the appearance of the acetabular teardrop is the earliest radiographic measure that a stable reduction has been achieved.

*Albinana et al. performed a study looking at early predictors or residual hip dysplasia after treatment with closed or open reduction.* *At forty years of follow-up, they that Severin grade was predictive total hip arthroplasty with early measurements of the acetabular index being predictive of Severin grade.* **The authors concluded** that performing pelvic osteotomies on hips with an acetabular index of 28˚ or more is associated with both the lowest false positive and overall misclassification rates.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The patient has left-sided hip dysplasia with a high acetabular index. Continued observation would not address potential early-onset hip osteoarthritis.
Answers 2: Varus derotational osteotomy would not address the increased acetabular index, which may predispose the hip to future subluxation and dislocation.
Answer 4: Open reduction followed by spica casting is a viable treatment option at younger ages. However, acetabular remodeling potential in this patient is limited and there is clear dislocation of the hip seen with disruption of Shenton's line on the AP pelvis.
Answer 5: Closed reduction with spica casting is not indicated in patients older than 18 months. Additionally, this patient has failed this previously which negates further treatment with this approach.
