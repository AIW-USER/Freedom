## Options

1. A Salter osteotomy extends from the AIIS proceeding posteriorly and ends at the level of the ilioischial limb of the triradiate cartilage
2. A Pemberton osteotomy extends from the acetabular roof to the triradiate cartilage, with incomplete cuts through the pericapsular portion of the innominate bone
3. A Bernese osteotomy consists of multiple osteotomies in the pubis, ilium, and ischium near the acetabulum
4. A Dega osteotomy extends from above the acetabulum through the ilium to the sciatic notch
5. A Chiari osteotomy involves placing an extra-articular buttress of bone over the subluxed femoral head

## Correct option

**3. A Bernese osteotomy consists of multiple osteotomies in the pubis, ilium, and ischium near the acetabulum**

## Answer explanation

This patient is presenting with radiographic and physical exam findings consistent with adult hip dysplasia. A Bernese (or Ganz) periacetabular osteotomy (PAO) is indicated in skeletally mature individuals and involves multiple osteotomies in the superior pubic ramus, supra-acetabular ilium, and ischium to improve three-dimensional correction of the acetabulum.

The PAO was first described in 1988 by Reinhold Ganz at the University of Berne in Switzerland and is also commonly referred to as the Ganz or Bernese osteotomy. There are numerous bony procedures described for acetabular correction in skeletally immature individuals with hip dysplasia; however persistent or missed symptomatic dysplasia in skeletally mature individuals usually warrants a periacetabular osteotomy (PAO) in order to achieve increased acetabular coverage. Patients commonly present with an insidious onset of hip or groin pain, and may have concomitant labral pathology that can present as pain with flexion, adduction, and internal rotation (FADIR). Achieving increased acetabular coverage through a Bernese osteotomy reliably improves radiographic parameters and symptoms, and is associated with 92% survivorship at 15 years in avoiding total hip arthroplasty (THA).

*Wyles et al. performed a multicenter cohort study to describe the long-term radiographic natural history of the dysplastic hip following PAO.* *The authors evaluated all patients undergoing PAO for DDH from 1996 to 2012 at 3 academic institutions, with a minimum 5-year radiographic follow-up.* *At the time of final follow-up, 50% of patients had progressed at least one Tönnis grade, with 14.6% of the study population having undergone THA.* *Patients with higher Tönnis grades following PAO had a significantly increased rate of progression to THA (p<0.001).* For Tönnis grade 1, the probability of progression to THA at 5 and 10 years was 2% and 11%, respectively, compared with 23% and 53%, respectively, for Tönnis grade 2. **The authors concluded** that PAO effectively alters the natural history of DDH, and precise radiographic progression based on the Tönnis grade can be used to prognosticate native hips undergoing PAO.

*Schmitz et al. published a review on developmental dysplasia in adolescents and young adults.* *The authors describe insidious groin pain as the most common presenting symptom and review the spectrum of management options including activity modification and anti-inflammatory medication, hip arthroscopy, and PAO.* **The authors state** that the Bernese PAO has become the primary acetabular realignment osteotomy used for the management of dysplasia in skeletally mature patients because of its ability to address multiplanar deformities and permit less restrictive postoperative weight-bearing precautions.

*Domb et al. published a case series of prospectively collected and retrospectively reviewed surgical and outcome data of 17 patients who underwent concomitant hip arthroscopy and PAO for symptomatic dysplasia.* *All patients were found to have concomitant chondrolabral pathology and arthroscopically underwent either labral repair or partial labral debridement.* At a mean of 2.4 years, all patients showed satisfactory mean clinical improvement, **suggesting** that concomitant PAO and hip arthroscopy is safe and effective. Hip arthroscopy with PAO did not appear to introduce complications beyond the PAO alone.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: This describes a Pemberton osteotomy. A Salter osteotomy extends from above the acetabulum through the ilium to the sciatic notch.
Answer 2: This describes a Dega osteotomy. A Pemberton osteotomy extends from the AIIS proceeding posteriorly and ends at the level of the ilioischial limb of the triradiate cartilage.
Answer 4: This describes a Salter osteotomy. A Dega osteotomy extends from the acetabular roof to the triradiate cartilage, with incomplete cuts through the pericapsular portion of the innominate bone.
Answer 5: This describes a Staheli shelf osteotomy. A Chiari osteotomy is a salvage procedure where the osteotomy starts above the acetabulum and extends to the sciatic notch, shifting the ilium laterally beyond the edge of the acetabulum.
