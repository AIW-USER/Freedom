## Question

Nerve involved in heel pain syndrome (peripheral nerve injuries- compression neuropathies- lateral plantar nerve compression)

## Options

1. Medial plantar nerve
2. Medial calcaneal nerve
3. 1st branch of lateral plantar nerve
4. 2nd branch of sural nerve

## Correct option

**3. 1st branch of lateral plantar nerve**

## Answer explanation

Neural causes of heel pain:

Plantar heel pain may also have a neural origin, with a lesion or dysfunction of the tibial, plantar or calcaneal nerves. Nerve entrapment may play a role in both acute and chronic conditions of plantar heel pain.

Neural anatomy of heel pain: The tibial nerve is the larger of the two major divisions of the sciatic nerve, and distally it divides into the following branches: medial calcaneal nerve (MCN), medial plantar nerve (MPN), lateral plantar nerve (LPN) and the first branch of the LPN.

Entrapment of the LPN and its first branch: The LPN supplies most of the foot muscles and the skin of the lateral one-third of the plantar aspect of the foot and the fourth and fifth toes. Entrapment of the LPN can result from compression between the abductor hallucis and quadratus plantae muscles. The first branch of the LPN, also called the nerve to abductor digiti minimi. Although it gives off sensory branches to the calcaneal periosteum, this nerve does not supply the skin.

Entrapment of the first branch of the LPN is the most common cause of plantar heel pain of neural origin. Entrapment of this nerve can occur at different sites: (1) where the nerve passes at the sharp edge of the deep fascia of the abductor hallucis and (2) just distal to the medial edge of the calcaneus.

Medial calcaneal nerve: MCN is the second most commonly reported nerve that has been related to plantar heel pain of neural origin.

Entrapment of the Medial plantar nerve: The MPN innervates the abductor hallucis, flexor hallucis brevis, flexor digitorum brevis and first lumbrical, and the skin of the medial two-thirds of the plantar aspect of the foot. Entrapment of the MPN is not as common as entrapment of the other nerves.

Answer: 3
