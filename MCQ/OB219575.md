## Question

Lower extremity megaprosthesis reconstruction is utilized in the treatment of a variety of malignant pathologies. Unfortunately, the complication rate following these procedures is high. Which of the following statements is most accurate regarding postoperative complications following this procedure?

## Options

1. Five days of post-operative antibiotics is associated with a lower risk of postoperative infection
2. Shorter operative time is associated with an increased short-term (<1 year) reoperation rate
3. Patients undergoing this procedure for a primary malignant bone tumor have a higher short-term (<1 year) reoperation rate compared to patients with a benign aggressive bone tumor
4. The utilization of a medial gastrocnemius flap is associated with an increased risk of postoperative infection
5. The use of negative pressure wound therapy (NPWT) is associated with a higher risk of postoperative infection

## Correct option

**3. Patients undergoing this procedure for a primary malignant bone tumor have a higher short-term (<1 year) reoperation rate compared to patients with a benign aggressive bone tumor**

## Answer explanation

Lower extremity megaprosthesis reconstruction is utilized in the treatment of a variety of ortho-oncologic pathologies. Patients undergoing these procedures for primary malignant bone tumors have a higher short-term reoperation rate compared to patients undergoing these procedures for benign, aggressive bony tumors (Answer 3).

Surgical treatment of malignant tumors of the femur or tibia often necessitates resection of the affected bone and soft-tissue envelope, typically necessitating subsequent reconstruction. The most common prosthesis type utilized in these reconstructions is the modular metallic and polyethylene megaprosthesis. As a result of the surgical case complexity and length, the risk of surgical site infection is high, a complication associated with a high rate of subsequent amputation (50%).

As a result of the disastrous nature of this complication, much research has been performed regarding strategies to prevent postoperative infection. The Prophylactic Antibiotic Regimens in Tumor Surgery (PARITY) trial was conducted to investigate the role of extended antibiotic prophylaxis. Multiple other studies have been conducted to identify factors that may predict the development of postoperative infection or the need for early reoperation. While there are multiple ongoing additional studies and secondary analyses, a few key findings have emerged:

-Extended antibiotic prophylaxis (5-day vs. 1-day) does not appear to reduce infection rates and increases risk of antibiotic-related complications
-Longer operative time is associated with a higher rate of complications
-Patients with a diagnosis of a primary malignant bone tumor have a higher short-term reoperation rate than patients with a benign, aggressive bony tumor
-The utilization of soft-tissue reconstructive procedures (i.e. flaps) appears to reduce the rate of postoperative infection
-The use of NPWT is not associated with increased infection rates but is associated with increased rates of revision surgery

*Ghert et al. provide the results of the PARITY trial, investigating the role of extended antibiotic prophylaxis in preventing surgical site infection (SSI).* *293 patients received a 5-day regimen and 311 received a 1-day regimen.* Overall, there was no difference in the incidence of SSIs at one year between the two groups (5-day: 15%. 1-day 16.7%, p-value 0.73), and patients who received the extended regimen had a higher rate of antibiotic-related complications (5.1% vs. 1.6%).

*Slawaska-Eng et al. provide a secondary analysis of the results of the PARITY trial, investigating the patient and surgical risk factors for developing SSI.* Overall, 96/604 (15.9%) of patients experienced an SSI, and four variables were identified as significant univariate predictors: preoperative diagnosis, operative time, volume of muscle excised, and hospital length of stay. Overall, only hospital length of stay was identified as a risk factor following multivariate analysis, with a hazard ratio of 1.03 per day of stay.

*LiBrizzi et al. provide an additional secondary analysis of the PARITY trial, investigating the use of NPWT and postoperative drains in developing SSI.* Overall, **the authors found** that an operative time of ≥ 8 hours and a postoperative drain duration of ≥ 14 days were independent predictors of SSI. The use of NPWT was not associated with the development of an SSI.

*Myers et al. report on the long-term results of endoprosthetic reconstructions for proximal tibial bone tumors.* *Their study included 194 patients, 95 of which had a fixed-hinge design and 99 a rotating-hinge design.* *The mean follow-up was 14.7 years.* The risk of revision at ten years for the fixed-hinge group was 61%, and for the rotating-hinge group, it was 25%. Overall, **the authors concluded** that based on survival data, a cemented, rotating hinge design may confer the best chance of long-term implant survival.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: extended antibiotic prophylaxis is not associated with a reduction in the risk of developing an SSI.
Answer 2: extended operative time is associated with an increased short-term reoperation rate.
Answer 4: the use of a flap is protective against the development of an SSI.
Answer 5: there is no correlation between the use of NPWT and the development of an SSI. The use of NPWT is associated with an increased risk for short-term reoperation, however.
