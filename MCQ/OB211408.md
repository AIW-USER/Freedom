## Question

A 36-year-old retired wrestler sustains a comminuted and 100% displaced right midshaft clavicle fracture. Which of the following is likely true should he pursue nonoperative management?

## Options

1. Decreased shoulder forward flexion
2. Improved endurance
3. Decreased shoulder external rotation
4. Increased rate of nonunion
5. Increased rate of secondary operation

## Correct option

**4. Increased rate of nonunion**

## Answer explanation

Of the above statements, the only accurate statement regarding nonoperative management of displaced midshaft clavicle fractures is its increased rate of nonunion.

Non-operative management of midshaft clavicle fractures is associated with decreased shoulder strength, increased nonunion, and symptomatic malunion rates. Range of motion has not been shown to be sufficiently different between operative and nonoperative management. Risk factors for nonunion in the nonoperative management of midshaft clavicle fractures include advanced age, female gender, displacement, and comminution (Z-deformity).

*Walton et al. performed a cost analysis of internal fixation versus nonoperative treatment in adult midshaft clavicle fractures using multiple randomized controlled trials.* They reported that the costs for operative and nonoperative treatment were $14,763 and $3112, respectively. They concluded that nonoperative management results in cost savings of $11,650.

*McKee et al. performed a meta-analysis of multiple RCTs investigating the operative versus nonoperative care of displaced midshaft clavicular fractures.* **They reported** that the nonunion and symptomatic malunion rate was higher in the nonoperatively treated patients. **They concluded** that operative treatment provides a significantly lower rate of nonunion and symptomatic malunion and an earlier functional return compared with nonoperative treatment.

*Woltz et al. performed a prospective, RCT in 167 patients with a displaced midshaft clavicular (at least 1 shaft width) fracture to compare nonoperative treatment versus ORIF.* **The authors noted** that the rate of nonunion was significantly higher in the nonoperatively group than in the operatively treated group (23.1% compared with 2.4%; p < 0.0001). No differences were found between the groups with respect to the Constant and DASH scores at all time points. **The authors concluded** that for patients with a displaced clavicle fracture, ORIF improves the odds of union but does not improve shoulder function or general symptoms compared with nonoperative treatment in a sling.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1 and 3: When comparing operative versus nonoperative treatment, there is no difference in shoulder range of motion.
Answer 2: Some studies (i.e. the Canadian Orthopaedic Trauma Society trial) suggest decreased (not increased) shoulder endurance with nonoperative management.
Answer 5: Nonoperative management is associated with a decreased (not increased) rate of secondary reoperation.
