## Question

A 15-year-old female presents with progressive deformity about her left knee. A standing AP radiograph shows fused triradiate . Physical examination reveals significant varus and a leg-length discrepancy of 2.0cm right greater than left. Which of the following is the most appropriate method of management currently?

![[TO17138301-1.jpg]]
![[TO17138301-2.jpg]]

## Options

1. Growth modulation (Hemiepiphysiodesis)
2. Bracing
3. Lateral closing wedge osteotomy
4. Medial opening wedge osteotomy

## Correct option

**4. Medial opening wedge osteotomy**

## Answer explanation

Adolescent Blount's disease is defined as pathologic genu varum with onset at greater than 10 years of age. Surgical management is the rule, as the deformity has a tendency to progress and cause medial joint pain. Surgical management consists of either hemiepiphysiodesis or osteotomy. If insufficient growth is remaining to allow for correction via hemiepiphysiodesis, an osteotomy is the best option for correction of the deformity.

In the above case the child is a female, 15 years age and with the triradiate fused. So, despite the open tibial physis there is limited growth potential there. So growth modulation is not an option.

When associated with LLD, If the deformity and leg length discrepancy (LLD) can be corrected with an opening wedge osteotomy alone, plate fixation is sufficient. If the leg length discrepancy cannot be correct with an opening wedge alone, the placement of an external fixator after osteotomy allows for additional correction of LLD.

![[TO17138301-3.png]]

Compared to this case, if the child did not have any LLD, a focal dome osteotomy would be ideal. The focal dome osteotomy (smiling) has the CORA and ACA mutually located so that after the osteotomy the angulation and the obligatory translation that occurs results in a realignment of the bone axis.
