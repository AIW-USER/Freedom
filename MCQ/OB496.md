## Question

A 22-year-old male sustained a distal radius fracture following a fall down the stairs. The emergency room physician noticed abnormal bone appearance on the radiographs and referred him for skeletal survey and further orthopaedic evaluation. Pelvis and spine radiographs are provided in Figures A and B. Which of the following best describe the etiology of the bony disease?

![[q496-a-osteopetrosis-pelvis.jpg]]
*Figure A*

![[q496-b-osteopetrosis-spine.jpg]]
*Figure B*

## Options

1. Defect in renal 25-(OH)-vitamin D1 alpha-hydroxylase
2. Defective osteoclast function
3. Autosomal dominant inheritance of altered fibroblast growth factor receptor 3 (FGFR3)
4. Alteration in osteoblast differentiation
5. Inability of proximal tubules to reabsorb phosphate

## Correct option

**2. Defective osteoclast function**

## Answer explanation

Pelvis radiograph demonstrates classic findings of osteopetrosis including dense bone, loss of trabeculations, and narrow femoral canals. The spine radiograph demonstrates the classic "rugger jersey" spine with very dense vertebral bodies. Osteopetrosis results from a defect in the osteoclast altering resorption of bone. This results in pathologic dense bone that is susceptible to fracture.

*Asagiri et al provide a comprehensive review of the molecular biology and and signaling pathways involved in the differentiation of osteoclasts.*

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1 describes Type I hereditary Rickets.
Answer 3 describes Achondroplasia.
Answer 4 is incorrect because osteoblasts are not defective in the bone remodelling process of osteopetrosis. Answer 5 describes familial hypophosphatemic rickets.
