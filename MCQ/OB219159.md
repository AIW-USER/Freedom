## Question

A 44-year-old motorcycle enthusiast is riding down the Pacific Coast Highway and is blind-sided by a delivery truck making a U-turn. He is brought to the emergency room with a closed injury of the tibial plateau and imaging showing a hyperextension varus bicondylar tibial plateau fracture. Which of the following is most characteristic of this pattern of tibial plateau injury?

## Options

1. Decreased risk of injury to the common peroneal nerve
2. Increased risk of posteromedial corner injury
3. Increased risk of lateral bucket-handle meniscus injury
4. Posterior tibial artery injury
5. Reversal or loss of normal tibial slope

## Correct option

**5. Reversal or loss of normal tibial slope**

## Answer explanation

A hyperextension varus bicondylar tibial plateau fracture (HEVBTP) typically results in the loss or complete reversal of tibial slope due to the characteristic pattern of bony and soft tissue failure.

Tibial plateau fractures are most commonly classified according to the Schatzker classification; however, this often fails to accurately classify additional injury patterns such as the HEVBTP that have been shown in the literature to have a unique subset of additional risks to the surrounding structures. A HEVBTP fracture classically exhibits the following radiographic hallmarks: (1) sagittal plane malalignment with loss or reversal of the normal posterior tibial slope (2) tension failure of the posterior cortex (3) compression of the anterior cortex (4) varus deformity in the coronal plane. Given this pattern of injury, increases in the incidence of popliteal artery injury requiring repair, partial or complete peroneal nerve injury, and development of compartment syndrome have been reported in the literature. Thus, it is essential to know and understand the radiographic hallmarks so that appropriate and timely diagnosis of any concomitant injuries can be made.

Firoozabadi, et al. provide a review of the diagnosis and treatment strategies for hyperextension varus bicondylar tibial plateau fractures. They note that significant associated injuries occurred 32% of the time with HEVBTP fractures compared to 16% in control groups, with a notable 12% risk of popliteal artery injury requiring repair compared to a 1% risk in controls, a 16% risk of partial or complete peroneal nerve injury compared to 8% in controls, and a 12% incidence of compartment syndrome compared to 10% of controls. The authors recommend anteromedial placement of the medial buttress plate along with grafting/back-filling of the anterior defect in order to prevent recurrent loss of slope and fixation failure.

Gonzalez, et al. review the hyperextension tibial plateau fracture pattern and the reasons that it is a predictor of poor outcomes. The authors reviewed 84 patients, 82% with non-HEVBTP fractures and 18% with HEVBTP fractures. They noted that there was no difference in knee range of motion at 1-year follow-up between the two groups, but that patients with hyperextension mechanisms did exhibit a trend toward higher pain scores,
indicating worsened functional outcomes and were more likely than their non-hyperextension mechanism counterparts to have associated soft-tissue damage and to develop post-traumatic osteoarthritis. The authors concluded that non-HEVBTP and HEVBTP fracture patients have similar outcomes in terms of range of motion at approximately 1 year of follow-up, however, differ significantly in terms of functional recovery and the types of complications associated with their injuries.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: This injury pattern has been shown to increase, not decrease, the risk of peroneal nerve injury, with incidence as high as 16%.
Answer 2: Given that soft tissues fail in tension, posterolateral corner injuries have been associated more commonly with HEVBTP fractures, not posteromedial corner injuries.
Answer 3: An increased risk of lateral bucket-handle meniscus tears is more common with Schatzker Type II lateral split depression tibial plateau fractures. Again, soft tissues fail in tension, with HEVBTP fractures demonstrating anteromedial compression injuries.
Answer 4: Popliteal artery injuries occur more commonly (up to 12% incidence), not posterior tibial artery injuries, in HEVBTP fractures.
