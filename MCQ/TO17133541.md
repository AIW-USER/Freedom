Chapter: Spine MCQs — Fractures of ATLAS and AXIS

## Question

All of the following are true for hangman’s fracture except?

## Options

1. It is a traumatic spondylolisthesis of C2 on C3 as a result of bilateral fracture of C2 pars or pedicles.
2. The mechanism of injury is a primary hyperflexion of the neck.
3. Type IIA fracture has a significant angulation without displacement.
4. Type I fracture has an intact C2/C3 disc.

## Correct option

**2. The mechanism of injury is a primary hyperflexion of the neck.**

## Answer explanation

A hangman’s fracture is a traumatic spondylolisthesis of C2 on C3 as a result of bilateral

fracture of C2 pars or pedicles. The mechanism of injury is an extension injury (causes pars fracture) with a secondary flexion (disrupts the posterior longitudinal ligament (PLL) and the disc) resulting in the anterolisthesis. In 30% there is a concomitant C-spine fracture.

There is usually no neurological deficit except in type III. Levine and Edwards have

classified this fracture as follows:

Type I – minimal displacement and angulation with an intact disc. Treatment:

Philadelphia-like collar.

Type II – displaced fracture of the pars with >3 mm displacement and significant

angulation (disc and PLL disrupted). Reduce with traction then apply halo for 2 months.

Type IIA – no horizontal displacement but significant angulation. Reduction with

hyperextension WITHOUT TRACTION then apply halo for 2 months.

Type III – is associated with bilateral facet dislocation and requires an open reduction of

the dislocation with C2/C3 fusion.

Answer: 2
