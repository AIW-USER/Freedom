## Question

An 18-year-old football player is tackled and sustains an injury to his right lower extremity. There is obvious deformity at the knee and he is immobilized on the field and taken to a nearby hospital. Lateral radiograph is seen in Figure A. Successful reduction is performed, followed by a CT with angiography, which demonstrates intimal tearing in the popliteal artery. Dorsalis pedis and posterior tibial pulses are not dopplerable in the right lower extremity. You elect to place a knee-spanning external fixator before vascular intervention. Initial ligamentous examination in the operating room demonstrates increased anterior and posterior laxity as well as increased laxity with varus stress. Which additional physical exam finding would be indicative of a commonly associated injury given this patient's presentation?

![[q214676-a-anterior-knee-dislocation.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Ankle brachial index = 0.9
2. Dimpling of skin over medial knee
3. Difficulty with dorsiflexion of ankle
4. Increased procurvatum with knee held in extension
5. Abnormal sensation over the anterior, distal thigh

## Correct option

**3. Difficulty with dorsiflexion of ankle**

## Answer explanation

This patient sustained an anterior knee dislocation with vascular injury and has a high risk for concomitant common peroneal nerve injury.

Common peroneal nerve injury is the most common nerve injury seen with knee dislocations, occurring in approximately 25% of cases and leads to decreased sensation on the dorsum of the foot as well as difficulty with dorsiflexion and eversion of the ankle/foot. Subclinical tibial nerve injury is also seen on imaging in about half of the cases where peroneal nerve injury is noted, but almost never occurs in isolation. Common peroneal nerve injury is most often seen with anterior knee dislocations, with KDIIIL patterns causing the highest risk, likely relating to traumatic stretching of the nerve at the time of injury. The risk of common peroneal nerve injury also increases substantially in cases where there is associated vascular injury. In anterior knee dislocations, intimal tearing of the popliteal artery is the most common vascular injury seen and is present in approximately 20% of cases. Recovery is variable in peroneal nerve palsies with reports of complete neurologic recovery ranging from 30-50% of cases.

*Peskun et al. reviewed 91 knee dislocations with 26 having concomitant peroneal nerve palsies over a 5-year period.* **They found** that male gender, higher BMI, vascular injury, fibular head fracture, and KDIIIL injury patterns were associated with risk for nerve injury. **They concluded** that younger age was the only factor which improved the chances of nerve recovery.

*Niall et. al. published a study on common peroneal nerve palsy after traumatic dislocation of the knee.* **They found** that the most common presenting direction of the dislocation was anterior. **They concluded** that no useful motor or sensory function returned in 50% of patients.

*Goitz et al. published a review on the management of peroneal nerve injuries associated with knee dislocations.* **They reported** that peroneal nerve palsy after knee dislocation is a serious problem, and neurolysis at the time of knee reconstruction does not always result in the return of peroneal nerve function.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Ankle brachial index would be <0.9 in the case of vascular injury as described above.
Answer 2: Medial skin dimpling represents an irreducible posterolateral knee dislocation. In the question above, the knee was able to be reduced.
Answer 4: Increased recurvatum would be seen in the case of a multiligament knee injury with PCL rupture, not procurvatum.
Answer 5: Anterior, distal thigh sensation is provided by the femoral nerve and would not be likely injured in the setting of knee dislocation.
