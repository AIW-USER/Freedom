## Question

A 36-year-old male sustained a posterior column acetabular fracture and traumatic brain injury following a motor vehicle accident. He underwent open reduction and internal fixation of the acetabular fracture with debridement of the gluteus minimus. Figure A is the 2-year postoperative radiograph. Which surgical approach has the highest association with the development of heterotopic ossification?

![[q213908-a-acetabular-fx-ho.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Direct anterior
2. Ilioinguinal
3. Stoppa
4. Kocher-Langenbeck
5. Pararectus

## Correct option

**4. Kocher-Langenbeck**

## Answer explanation

Of the answers listed, the Kocher-Langenbeck approach has the highest association with the formation of heterotopic ossification (HO).

HO is characterized by the formation of bone in atypical, extraskeletal tissues, and is a common complication following the surgical management of acetabular fractures. The hip is moreover a common site for HO formation following traumatic brain injury (TBI). Posterior acetabular fractures treated through the Kocher-Langenbeck approach have been associated with an intermediate risk of developing HO. Debridement of the damaged gluteus minimus and medius musculature and single-dose radiation have dually been found to decrease the incidence of HO postoperatively. The ilioinguinal and Stoppa approaches are conversely associated with a LOW incidence of HO. The extended-iliofemoral approach has traditionally been associated with the highest incidence of HO, but this approach is rarely utilized today.

*Rath et al. review the impact of necrotic gluteus minimus debridement on the formation of HO after operative treatment of posterior wall and associated transverse-posterior wall acetabular fracture fixations utilizing a Kocher-Langenbeck approach.* **The authors reported** that clinically significant HO was noted in 10% of patients and that there were no complications associated with debridement of necrotic gluteus minimus musculature. **They concluded** that necrotic gluteus minimus resection from the zone of injury is an efficient and safe method of preventing significant HO in patients after operative fixation of posterior acetabular fractures utilizing a Kocher-Langenbeck approach.

*Davis et al. review whether the use of the Kocher-Langenbeck approach with gluteus minimus debridement and a single-dose radiotherapy was associated with a decreased risk of HO.* **They reported** that single-dose radiotherapy conveyed a reduced risk of clinically important HO after a Kocher-Langenbeck approach with gluteus minimus debridement. **The authors concluded** that single-dose radiation in combination with gluteus minimus débridement was sufficient to substantially decrease the risk of clinically signficant HO compared with gluteus minimus débridement alone after utilization of a Kocher-Langenbeck approach for acetabular fracture.

Figure A is the postoperative AP radiograph of the pelvis demonstrating substantial HO formation following ORIF of a left posterior acetabular fracture.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: A direct anterior approach would not be used in isolation for ORIF of a posterior acetabular fracture and furthermore has not been associated with a greater an increased risk of HO formation following ORIF of femoral head fractures.
Answers 2-3: The anterior-based ilioinguinal and Stoppa approaches have similarly been associated with a low incidence of HO formation.
Answer 5: A pararectus approach would not permit sufficient access to a posterior acetabular fracture and is moreover not associated with a low risk of HO formation compared to a Kocher-Langenbeck approach.
