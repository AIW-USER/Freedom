## Question

Figure A is the radiograph of an 8-year-old male who presents to the ED after an ATV accident. Following secondary examination, this is believed to be his only injury. Operative stabilization is planned for the following morning. Overnight, the patient complains of feeling lightheaded and has a heart rate of 150 bpm and a blood pressure of 90/50 mmHg. An urgent CBC is drawn which is remarkable for a hemoglobin of 8.5 g/dL (normal range 11.5-13.5 g/dL). A bolus of IV fluids is started. What is the next best step?

![[q215559-a-ped-femur-fx-1.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Begin vasopressor medication
2. Blood transfusion
3. Emergent fasciotomies of the thigh
4. Emergent stabilization of long bone fracture
5. Re-evaluate for other potential injuries

## Correct option

**5. Re-evaluate for other potential injuries**

## Answer explanation

This patient sustained an isolated pediatric femoral shaft fracture and is now presenting with hypovolemic shock. Hemodynamic instability is unlikely following an isolated pediatric femoral shaft fracture and additional injuries need to be evaluated.

Studies have shown it is rare for an isolated femur fracture in pediatric patients to lead to hemodynamic instability. Therefore, further work-up is needed to look for other potential injuries that could lead to this much blood loss and hemodynamic instability. The first step in management should be to obtain a hemoglobin and hematocrit. With a hemoglobin value >7 g/dL, IV fluids are often an appropriate initial intervention. Blood transfusion is generally reserved for a hemoglobin <7 g/dL, or in the setting of rapidly declining hemodynamic instability.

*Adams et al. retrospectively reviewed femoral shaft fractures in pediatric patients.* *Of the 32 patients with preoperative coagulation laboratories, 11 were abnormal; however, none resulted in hematology consultations or procedure delays.* **They concluded** that perioperative laboratory orders may be unnecessary in most isolated pediatric femoral shaft fractures, subjecting patients to extraneous costs and associated pain. However, laboratories may be justified based on clinical circumstances or for older patients treated with reamed nails or plates/screws.

*Unal et al. attempted to define the magnitude of blood loss resulting from pediatric femoral fractures and the factors influencing the loss.* **They reported** that patients with additional trauma showed significant decreases in both hemoglobin concentrations and hematocrit levels in comparison with the patients who had only isolated femoral fractures. **They concluded** that if there is an obvious decrease in hematocrit and/or hemoglobin concentration in a child with a femoral fracture, the possibility of additional injuries should be investigated.

Figure A is a radiograph demonstrating a left femoral shaft fracture. Illustration A is that same fracture treated with flexible intramedullary nails. Illustration B is that same fracture following healing and hardware removal.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Vasopressor medication may be needed in cases of hemodynamic instability refractory to IV fluids and blood transfusion
Answer 2: Blood transfusion is generally reserved for a hemoglobin <7 g/dL; initial management would be IV fluids
Answer 3: Emergent fasciotomies may be indicated in the setting of compartment syndrome, but is not utilized as a means of managing hemodynamic instability
Answer 4: Emergent stabilization of the long bone fracture may help with pain relief and minimize bleeding into the thigh, but this injury alone is unlikely to be the lone source for this patient's hemodynamic instability and further evaluation for an additional source of bleeding should be performed.
