## Question

A patient presents with a complex pelvic ring injury and concomitant both-column acetabulum fracture after a high-speed skiing accident. Hemodynamic instability prompts initial temporizing stabilization and angiography prior to planned definitive fixation. Which of the following scenarios is most likely to increase the risk for postoperative infection after subsequent fracture fixation?

## Options

1. Angiography without embolization
2. Bilateral internal iliac artery embolization
3. Presence of venous plexus injury
4. Use of external fixation as a means of initial temporizing stabilization
5. Definitive fixation within 48 hours of initial presentation

## Correct option

**2. Bilateral internal iliac artery embolization**

## Answer explanation

Pelvic-injured patients who undergo initial angiography with arterial embolization may be at an increased risk for postoperative infection after definitive fracture fixation. This risk is most closely associated with nonselective, bilateral iliac artery embolization.

Complex pelvic ring injuries can be associated with vascular injury causing hemodynamic instability. Venous plexus injury is the most common source of hemorrhage and can be addressed with external compression and stabilization or pelvic packing. When arterial injury is suspected, angiography is warranted. Though the potential increased risk for postoperative deep infection after definitive fracture fixation in patients who first undergo angiography with or without embolization is somewhat controversial in the literature, the risk is most clearly defined in those who are treated with nonselective, bilateral iliac artery embolization.

*Manson et al. performed a retrospective review investigating the risk for deep infection after angiography with embolization and subsequent ORIF of acetabular fractures with or without concomitant pelvic ring injuries.* The rate of infection in patients requiring embolization was 58% compared to 14% in those who underwent angiography without embolization. There was a disproportionate number of infections occurring in patients who had embolization of the entire iliac artery. **The authors concluded** that embolization of the entire iliac artery should be avoided and more distal embolization performed when possible to decrease the risk of infection.

*Matityahu et al. separately reported complication rates among a retrospective series of pelvic fracture patients undergoing initial angiography with embolization.* *In their series, there was a deep infection rate of 4%, none of which occurred in patients who underwent angiography without embolization.* All complications occurred in patients who underwent bilateral embolization and subsequent surgical fracture fixation. **They concluded** that nonselective, bilateral arterial embolization is associated with a higher risk of overall complications, and selective, unilateral embolization should be performed when indicated.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Angiography without embolization has not been closely associated with an increased risk of deep infection after definitive fracture fixation.
Answer 3: Venous plexus injury is the most common source of hemorrhage associated with pelvic ring fractures and is not associated with an increased risk of infection after definitive fixation.
Answers 4&5: Temporizing stabilization with external fixation and early definitive fixation have not been associated with an increased risk of deep postoperative infection.
