## Correct option

## Answer explanation

Figure A demonstrates an extension type II supracondylar humerus fracture which is most appropriately treated with closed reduction and percutaneous pinning.

Gartland type II supracondylar humerus fractures are those which are displaced with an intact posterior humeral cortex. Closed reduction and passage of two percutaneous lateral pins is the most appropriate treatment method in order to maintain reduction and prevent deformity.

*Otsuka et. al. reviewed the treatment of supracondylar humerus fractures.* **They suggest** that for type II fractures, percutaneous pinning is the preferred treatment due to the risk of compartment syndrome with reduction and casting in hyperflexion as well as the risk of resultant elbow deformity. **They suggest** evaluating reduction using Baumann's angle, the anterior humeral line, and the reconstruction of the olecranon fossa.

Figure A demonstrates a Gartland type II SCH fracture. Figure B demonstrates a a both bone forearm fracture with angulation. Figure C demonstrates a displaced proximal humerus fracture. Figure D demonstrates a Salter-Harris type II distal radius fracture with minimal displacement. Figure E demonstrates a non-displaced humeral shaft fracture.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Both bone forearm fracture with angulation are best treated with closed reduction and casting.
Answer 3: Displaced proximal humerus fractures in patients less than 12 years of age have significant remodeling potential and are most appropriately treated non-operatively.
Answer 4: SH2 fractures of the distal radius with minimal displacement can be treated non-operatively in a cast.
Answer 5: Nondisplaced humeral shaft fractures are best treated in a fracture brace or coaptation splint.
