## Question

You see a 45-year-old male construction worker with moderate, symptomatic osteoarthritis of his elbow. He has failed conservative management with physical therapy, injections, and is fearful he will lose his job if his elbow pain persists. Recent MRI demonstrates multiple loose bodies in the elbow. You elect to perform an elbow arthroscopy with debridement and loose body removal. Intra-operatively, you establish your anterolateral portal first, followed by anteromedial and posteromedial portals. Based on your portal placement, which post-operative neurologic deficit would you be most likely to see?

## Options

1. Inability to extend metacarpal phalangeal (MCP) joints
2. Inability to flex the wrist
3. Paresthesias over medial volar forearm
4. Paresthesias over dorsal aspect of 5th digit
5. Weakness of wrist extension with concomitant ulnar deviation

## Correct option

**4. Paresthesias over dorsal aspect of 5th digit**

## Answer explanation

The use of a posteromedial portal in elbow arthroscopy should be avoided as it places the ulnar nerve at high risk. This may lead to intrinsic weakness, loss of finger ab/adduction and paresthesias over the volar and dorsal aspects of the 4th and 5th digits.

Elbow arthroscopy is technically demanding, although its use is becoming more widespread. The primary risk associated with elbow arthroscopy is damage to neurovascular structures. Injury risk typically increases with more distal portal placement, therefore elbow arthroscopy is normally initiated with an anterolateral portal and then an anteromedial portal is placed under direct visualization. Injuries are best avoided by distending the capsule with fluid before portal placement and using the nick and spread technique. Additionally, the use of a posteromedial portal should be avoided, as risk to the ulnar nerve in this area is unacceptably high. Other common, avoidable nerve injuries include radial nerve (proximal/distal anterolateral portals), posterior interroseus nerve (proximal/distal anterolateral portals) medial antebrachial cutaneous nerve (anteromedial portal), median nerve (anteromedial portal).

*Adams et al. reviewed the indications and techniques for elbow arthroscopy as well as associated complications.* Indications included debridement in cases of septic arthritis, inflammatory arthritis, osteoarthritis, loose body removal, contracture release, and OCD management. They follow by reviewing the anatomy and associated portal placement that can lead to neurovascular compromise.

*Jinnah et al. published on risk factors for peripheral nerve injury in elbow arthroscopy.* **They noted** 12 nerve injuries in a total of 227 cases over 10 years, 10 of which were "minor." They did not find any specific associated risk factors for nerve injury but did conclude that elbow arthroscopy was a safe and effective procedure.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Injury to the posterior interosseous nerve (PIN) would lead to the inability to extend the MCP joints.
Answer 2: Injury to the median nerve would lead to the inability to flex the wrist.
Answer 3: Injury to the medial antebrachial cutaneous nerve (MABCN) would lead to paresthesias over the volar/medial forearm.
Answer 5: Injury to the radial nerve proper would cause weakness with wrist extension and concomitant ulnar deviation due to the loss of radial deviation from the extensor carpi radialis longus (ECRL).
