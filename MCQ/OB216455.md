## Question

A 45-year-old male presents to the trauma bay following a motor vehicle collision. Imaging reveals an isolated non-displaced femoral neck fracture. The decision is made to proceed with operative stabilization of the fracture that evening. Which of the following vascular structures provides the most significant contribution to the blood supply of the femoral head?

## Options

1. Internal pudendal
2. Inferior gluteal artery
3. Superior gluteal artery
4. Medial femoral circumflex artery
5. Lateral femoral circumflex artery

## Correct option

**4. Medial femoral circumflex artery**

## Answer explanation

The medial femoral circumflex (MFC) artery is the major blood supply to femoral head. It is a branch of the profunda femoris.

Blood supply to the femoral head changes with age. From birth to 4 years of age, the medial circumflex, lateral circumflex, and ligamentum teres are all major sources of blood supply to the femoral head. Once an adult, the MFC artery becomes the dominant blood supply to the femoral head. For this reason, in non-arthroplasty surgery, one should avoid transection of the quadratus during the posterior approach to the hip, as it may injure the MFC artery.

*Kalhor et al. described the vascular anatomy of the hip with particular reference to the contributions of the capsular and pericapsular vessels.* **They reported** that the medial femoral circumflex artery provided the dominant blood supply to the femoral head in most specimens. **They concluded** that the dominant blood supply to the femoral head comes from vessels that approach the joint posteriorly and penetrate the joint near the femoral attachment of the capsule.

*Ganz et al. reviewed the vascular anatomy of the proximal femur and an approach to safely dislocate the femoral head.* **They report** that using the extended retinacular soft-tissue flap, four surgical techniques (relative neck lengthening, subcapital realignment in slipped capital femoral epiphysis, true femoral neck osteotomy, and femoral head reduction osteotomy) evolved or became safer with respect to perfusion of the femoral head. **They concluded** that the extended retinacular soft-tissue flap offers the technical and biologic possibility for a new class of intra-articular procedures.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The internal pudendal is a branch of the internal iliac artery that exits the greater sciatic notch and re-enters the pelvis via the lesser sciatic notch
Answer 2: The inferior gluteal artery is a branch of the internal iliac artery and supplies short external rotators and gluteus maximus; it runs inferior to the piriformis after it exits the greater sciatic notch
Answer 3: The superior gluteal artery is a branch of the internal iliac artery and supplies the hip abductors and tensor fascia lata
Answer 5: The lateral femoral circumflex is also a branch of the profunda femoris artery and itself has three branches; the ascending branch is at risk during the direct anterior approach to the hip
