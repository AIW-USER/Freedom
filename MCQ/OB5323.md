## Question

A 10-year-old boy is referred to your office for knee swelling. The swelling began after he returned from summer camp in the Catskills, NY and has come and gone over the past several weeks. He has full range of motion that is painless. Joint aspiration is drawn and sent for analysis. Which figure likely corresponds to the causative agent?

## Answer explanation

Intermittent swelling with painless range of motion and a history of camping in the Northeastern United States suggests Lyme disease, caused by Borrelia burgdorferi (depicted in Figure D).

Borrelia burgorferi is a spirochete transmitted by the deer tick (Ixodes), native to the Northeast United States, but also has been reported in Wisconsin, Minnesota, and Northern California. Clinically, a 'bullseye rash (erythema migrans)' can occur, along with fatigue, fever, headache; the knee typically presents with effusion and painless range of motion. Diagnosis is confirmed via ELISA test.

*Smith et al. outline the epidemiology, diagnosis and treatment of Lyme disease.* Highlights include the common physical exam findings, including erythema migrans ("bulls-eye rash"), painless effusions, and a two-step diagnostic test that includes ELISA and Western blot.

Figure A exhibits gram positive cocci in clusters indicative of Staph aureus. Figure B exhibits gram negative rods indicative of Pseudomonas. Figure C exhibits gram positive rods indicative of Propionibacterim acnes. Figure D exhibits fluorescent stain of the spirochete, Borrelia burgdorferi. Figure E exhibits an acid-fast fast identifying Mycobacterium tuberculosis.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: Staph aureus (as seen in Figure A) does not cause Lyme disease.
Answer 2: Pseudomonas (as seen in Figure B) does not cause Lyme disease; typically is associated with ventilator associated pneumonia.
Answer 3: Propionibacterium acnes (as seen in Figure C) is typically an indolent infection found following shoulder procedures; does not cause Lyme disease.
Answer 5: Acid fast bacilli (as seen in Figure E) causes tuberculosis, not Lyme disease.
