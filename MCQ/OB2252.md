## Question

A 16-month-old child with an obstetrical palsy has a significant internal rotation contracture of the upper extremity. If left untreated, which progressive glenohumeral deformity would be LEAST likely to occur?

## Options

1. Anterior-superior glenohumeral escape
2. Glenoid hypoplasia
3. Glenoid retroversion
4. Humeral head flattening
5. Posterior humeral head subluxation

## Correct option

**1. Anterior-superior glenohumeral escape**

## Answer explanation

Obstetrical palsy with a significant internal rotation contracture has a 70% probability of developing glenoid dysplasia. Humeral dysplasia is also likely and can be managed surgically.

Greater than 90% of obstetric brachial plexopathy cases resolve without surgical intervention. However, of those that do not resolve without surgical intervention, an internal rotation contracture of the shoulder is the most common presentation. The most common type, and specific to the case presented, is Erb-Duchenne Palsy (C5 and C6 root deficit). This results in an imbalance between the unaffected internal rotators and the paralytic external rotators. If left untreated, glenohumeral dysplasia often ensues which includes glenoid and humeral hypoplasia, glenoid retroversion with posterior humeral head subluxation, and humeral head flattening. Other associated orthopedic conditions with an obstetrical palsy include elbow flexion contractures, clavicle and humerus fractures, and torticollis.

*Pearl et al. review 25 children who had an operative release of internal rotation contracture secondary to brachial plexus birth palsy; 11 had a latissimus dorsi transfer to augment external rotation power.* With the use of intraoperative arthrograms, they report on the pathological changes that occur in the glenohumeral joint during growth. **They found** that 72% of patients had a deformity of the posterior aspect of the glenoid. **They concluded** that internal rotation contracture secondary to brachial plexus birth palsy may lead to a glenoid deformity that is severely advanced by the time that the child is two years old.

*Waters et al. prospectively review 94 patients with brachial plexus birth palsy to evaluate the association between persistent palsy, age-related musculoskeletal deformity, and functional limitations.* **They reported** that the mean degree of retroversion of the glenoid on the affected side was -25.7 degrees compared with -5.5 degrees on the unaffected side. **They also noted** that 62% of patients had evidence of posterior subluxation of the humeral head. **They concluded** that progressive deformity occurs with increasing age.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 2-5: All of these answers are common glenohumeral dysplasia changes that occur in untreated obstetrical brachial plexus palsy.
