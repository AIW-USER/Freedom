## Question

After attempting a closed reduction on a 2-year-old with developmental dysplasia of the hip you obtain an arthrogram represented by Figure A. What do the arrows represent?

![[q214764-a-screen-shot-2021-04-26-at-11-17-05-pm.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Inverted limbus
2. Inverted labrum
3. Contracted transverse acetabular ligament
4. Hypertrophy of ligamentum teres
5. Capsule constriction by the iliopsoas tendon

## Correct option

**5. Capsule constriction by the iliopsoas tendon**

## Answer explanation

This arthrogram image is demonstrating the classic ‘hourglass’ shape caused by capsular constriction of the iliopsoas tendon over the acetabular introitus (arrows).

Developmental dysplasia of the hip (DDH) encompasses a spectrum of disorders which ranges from mild acetabular dysplasia to hip subluxation to dislocation. Risk factors for this condition include females, first-born, family history, and breech position. While most infants <6months old can be successfully treated in a Pavlik Harness older patients or those who fail Pavlik bracing may be a candidate for closed reduction with hip spica casting. After closed reduction, an intra-operative arthrogram is used to verify concentric reduction. Reasons for an unsuccessful closed reduction hinges on soft tissue barriers in and around the hip to include an inverted labrum, inverted limbus, hypertrophied ligamentum teres, capsule constriction by the iliopsoas tendon, hypertrophy and shortening of the transverse acetabular ligament, and hypertrophied intra-articular fibro-fatty tissue known as the pulvinar. CT or MRI are also utilized to confirm reduction after cast placement.

*Murray et al. analyzed the success rates of closed reduction and spica casting for the treatment of DDH.* **They found** that only 2 out of 33 hips (6%) were unable to be concentrically reduced with sufficient stability at the time of closed reduction. Despite having successfully reduced the other hips, they still had a 30% failure of normal acetabulum development requiring a second surgery. Also, 12% of the 33 hips developed radiographic evidence of avascular necrosis.

*Zhang et al. retrospectively reviewed 138 dysplastic hips that underwent closed reduction.* Overall, **they found** failure to be associated with older age at the time of closed reduction (≥ 18.35 month), severe dislocation of the affected hip, and a medial interval (distance from the medial edge of the ischium to the middle point of the proximal metaphyseal border of the femur) of ≥ 35.35 mm.

Figure A is a left hip arthrogram demonstrating a non-concentrically reduced hip with dye pooling in a characteristic ‘hourglass’ configuration signifying capsular constriction by the iliopsoas tendon (red arrows). Illustration A depicts the possible soft tissue blocks to a successful closed reduction.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1, 2, 3 and 4: These are all potential blocks to a successful closed reduction, but not visualized in the arthrogram above.
