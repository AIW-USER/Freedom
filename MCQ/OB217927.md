## Question

A 22-year-old skinny male ultra-marathon runner presents after a fall on his right shoulder during a steeple chase. He is healthy and denies tobacco use. His presenting radiographs are shown in Figure A. He undergoes open reduction internal fixation using a mini fragment plate followed by an additional 2.7mm reconstruction plate. Which of the following places him at the greatest risk of needing subsequent surgery?

![[q217927-a-clavicle-fx.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Age
2. Body Mass Index
3. Male Gender
4. Mechanism of Injury
5. Number of plates used in surgery

## Correct option

**2. Body Mass Index**

## Answer explanation

Midshaft clavicle fractures treated with open reduction internal fixation have a notable risk of requiring subsequent hardware removal. Female patients and patients with a lower body mass index are at risk for hardware irritation likely due to the cosmetic appearance or relatively lower amounts of subcutaneous tissue, respectively.

*Wiesel et al. performed a review on the management of adult midshaft clavicle fractures.* *They discuss the common complications of nonoperative management including nonunion or malunion.* *They contrasted these to the complications of operative management including implant irritation requiring removal and supraclavicular numbness.* **They conclude** by recommending surgical intervention for patients with midshaft clavicle fractures with 2cm of shortening, greater than 100% displacement, significant comminution, or a z-type pattern.

*Guerra et al. performed a systematic review of randomized control trials comparing nonoperative to operative management of midshaft clavicle fractures.* **They reported** that surgical management resulted in shorter time to union, lower risk for nonunion, and improved long-term shoulder functional scores. However, the patients treated operatively had a higher overall complication rate and a higher rate of subsequent surgery (i.e. plate removal). **The authors conclude** that midshaft clavicle fractures have improved long-term functional outcomes when treated operatively at the risk of having a higher complication rate.

Figure A shows a right-sided, middle-third clavicle shaft fracture with 100% displacement.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1. Younger adult patients have not been shown to be at increased risk for subsequent hardware removal over older populations.
Answer 3. Females, not males, have been shown to be at risk for hardware irritation and need for removal.
Answer 4. A ground-level fall on the affected shoulder is the most common cause of midshaft clavicle fractures. Higher energy mechanisms that result in significant comminution, open fractures, or segmental fractures have an increased risk of needing subsequent surgery.
Answer 5. Dual plating with 2.7mm, lower profile plates has been shown to have a lower risk for irritation requiring subsequent hardware removal over single 3.5mm LCDC plates.
