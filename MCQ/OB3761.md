## Options

1. L5 arch congenital abnormality
2. Fatigue fracture of the pars interarticularis
3. Degenerative instability with intact pars interarticularis
4. Traumatic fracture with intact pars interarticularis
5. Pathologic local bone disease

## Correct option

**2. Fatigue fracture of the pars interarticularis**

## Answer explanation

The figures demonstrate a fracture through the pars interarticularis consistent with isthmic spondylolisthesis or Type II according to the Wiltse classification scheme.

Spondylolisthesis or intersegmental instability can occur in multiple fashions. The Wilste-Newman Classification categorizes spondylolisthesis into five types. Type I is a dysplastic spondylolisthesis due to sacral or L5 arch congenital abnormality. Type II is an isthmic spondylolisthesis involving a lesion of the pars interarticularis. Type II can be further broken down as Type IIA or a fatigue fracture of the pars, Type IIB an elongated pars, or Type IIC an acute pars fracture. Type III is a degenerative spondylolisthesis from degenerative instability without a pars fracture. Type IV is a traumatic spondylolisthesis characterized by acute fracture anywhere other than the pars interarticularis. Type V is a pathologic spondylolisthesis from local or generalized bone disease. Identification of pars interarticularis defects can often be seen in lateral or oblique plain films. Additional imaging such as CT scan or MRI can be performed if suspicion is high.

*Jones et al. reviewed adult isthmic spondylolisthesis incidence, presentation, imaging, and treatment.* **They state** isthmic spondylolisthesis is present in 6% of the population. In patients with axial or radicular pain and imaging showing a pars defect, careful evaluation is required to ensure their symptoms are coming from the pars defect. **They conclude** nonoperative treatment is often successful and surgery should be reserved for recalcitrant cases.

*Labelle et al. evaluated multiple radiographic parameters involved in spino-pelvic balance in patients with developmental spondylolisthesis.* **They found** an increase in all parameters in patients with developmental spondylolisthesis. **They conclude** spino-pelvic balance tends to be abnormal in patients with developmental spondylolisthesis which has implications in evaluation and treatment.

Figure A shows a lateral radiograph of the lumbar spine with evidence of a defect in the pars interarticularis of L5. Figure B shows a sagittal CT scan of the same patient with an L5 pars interarticularis defect. Illustration A shows a graphic representation of the various types of spondylolisthesis.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: A Type I spondylolisthesis involves congenital abnormalities of the sacrum or L5 arch.
Answer 3: A Type III spondylolisthesis is degenerative in nature and would not show a pars interarticularis fracture.
Answer 4: A Type IV spondylolisthesis is traumatic and is characterized by an intact pars interarticularis.
Answer 5: A Type V spondylolisthesis is pathologic in nature either locally or globally.
