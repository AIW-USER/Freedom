## Options

1. Observation
2. CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis
3. Chest radiograph
4. Marginal excision
5. Radical excision

## Correct option

**4. Marginal excision**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a spindle cell lipoma (SCL), and marginal excision is the tratment of choice.

A SCL is a benign atypical lipoma that consists of mature fat and bland spindle cells. It is usually found in the neck, back, and shoulders. They are usually found in males between the fifth and seventh decade of life. Microscopically, SCLs consist of mature adipocytes, numerous bland spindle cells, collagen fibers along with mast cells and many areas of myxoid degeneration. Mitosis or cellular pleomorphism are rare or absent. SCL is CD34 positive and negative for S-100. They are treated with marginal excision and typically do not recur.

*Pardhe et al. performed a case review of a SCL.* A 14-year-old male had a SCL of the right periorbital region. They obtained an incisional biopsy which showed bundles of interlacing, elongated, spindle-shaped cells. The cells were set in a fibromyxoid background. They initially thought the diagnosis was neurofibroma and the mass was excised. After they ran histopathology and immunohistochemistry on the sample, **they concluded** that it was a SCL.

*Enzinger and Harvey performed a review of SCL.* **They report** that SCL is a specific type of lipoma that may be easily mistaken for a liposarcoma. They analyzed 114 cases of SCL and found that microscopically it consists of an intricate mixture of lipocytes and uniform spindle cells within a matrix of mucinous material traversed by a varying number of birefringent collagen fibers. **They conclude** that local excision is the treatment of choice.

Figures A and B are T1 MR sequences showing a mass within the deltoid with a homogenous fatty appearance.
Figure C is a photomicrograph of a SCL showing immunostaining positivity for CD34. Note the presence of mature fat and a spindle component. Figure D is an H&E stain on lower power, with adipose cells and little cellular atypia.
