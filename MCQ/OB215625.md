## Correct option

## Answer explanation

Of the radiographs shown, Figure A may be suggestive of X-linked hypophosphatemia (aka Vitamin D-resistant rickets).

Vitamin D-resistant (familial hypophosphatemic) rickets is the most common form of heritable rickets. It is caused by an inability of the renal tubules to absorb phosphate. Multiple inheritance patterns are known with this condition including X-linked dominant, autosomal dominant, and autosomal recessive. Of these, the X-linked dominant form is most common, and results from a mutation in PHEX gene that leads to increased levels of FGF23, which decreases renal phosphate absorption and suppresses renal 25-(OH)-1α-hydroxylase activity.

*Sharkey et al. reviewed X-linked hypophosphatemia.* **They report** that medical treatment is paramount to the success of orthopedic surgical procedures and that successful correction of complex, multi-apical bone deformities is possible with careful surgical planning and exacting surgical technique. **They conclude** that multiple methods of deformity correction are used, including acute and gradual correction and that some bony deformities may be addressed with guided growth techniques.

*Petersen et al. retrospectively reviewed children with X-linked hypophosphatemia to assess which factors are associated with a favorable growth response with the treatment with calcitriol [1,25-(OH)2D3] and inorganic phosphate (Pi) therapy.* **They report** no difference between the good growers and poor growers before or after the 3 year period of therapy in mean age, dietary calcium, calcitriol dose or compliance, or Pi dose or compliance. **They conclude** that calcitriol may exert a direct effect on the renal tubule to improve Pi reclamation in X-linked hypophosphatemia.

Figure A is a radiograph of the lower extremities demonstrating tibial bowing with widened proximal tibial physes. Figure B is a chest radiograph of the chest demonstrating absent clavicles, suggestive of cleidocranial dysplasia. Figure C is an AP and lateral radiograph of the lower extremity demonstrating an absent fibular, suggestive of fibular deficiency. Figure D is an AP and lateral radiograph of the lower extremity demonstrating anterolateral tibial bowing, which may be suggestive of neurofibromatosis type 1. Figure E is a lateral radiograph demonstrating an olecranon fracture, which in the absence of a trauma is suggestive of osteogenesis imperfecta

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: This chest radiograph depicts absent clavicles, suggesting cleidocranial dysplasia. which is a rare congenital disorder caused by an autosomal dominant mutation in the RUNX2/CBFA1 gene.
Answer 3: Fibular Deficiency is a congenital condition caused by shortening or absence of the fibula which typically presents with anteromedial bowing of the tibia and a leg length discrepancy. It has no known inheritance pattern.
Answer 4: Anterolateral bowing and congenital pseudoarthrosis of the tibia are congenital conditions, most commonly associated with Neurofibromatosis Type 1, that present with a bowing deformity of the tibial. Neurofibromatosis Type 1 has an autosomal dominant inheritance pattern.
Answer 5: An olecranon fracture in the absence of trauma is suggestive of osteogenesis imperfecta, which is a common congenital disorder caused by a mutation in COL1A1 or COL1A2 genes resulting in abnormal collagen cross-linking and an overall decrease in type 1 collagen. OI has autosomal dominant and autosomal recessive forms.
