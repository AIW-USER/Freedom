## Question

A 36 year-old male presents after feeling a pop in his right heel after a recreational basketball game. On exam, he has a positive Thompson test, a palpable defect over the Achilles tendon, and is unable to actively plantarflex the foot. After a lengthy discussion, the patient opts to proceed with operative treatment. Compared to non-operative management, which of the following best describes the outcomes of operative management for this patient's injury?

## Options

1. Higher rates of nerve injury, slower return to sport, slower return to work
2. Higher rates of nerve injury, quicker return to sport, quicker return to work
3. Lower rates of nerve injury, slower return to sport, slower return to work
4. Higher rates of nerve injury, equivalent time to return to sport, equivalent time to return to work
5. Higher rates of nerve injury, slower return to sport, equivalent time to return to work

## Correct option

**2. Higher rates of nerve injury, quicker return to sport, quicker return to work**

## Answer explanation

This patient has sustained an Achilles tendon rupture. Operative intervention for these injuries is associated with a higher rate of nerve injury but a quicker return to sport and work (Answer 2).

Achilles tendon injuries are one of the most common injuries of the lower extremities. Physical exam findings suggestive of this condition include a palpable gap at the Achilles tendon, a positive Thompson test, and weakness with plantarflexion. Treatment options include non-operative and operative options. Non-operative management consists of bracing in a position of equinus with progressive functional rehabilitation. Techniques for operative intervention include open and percutaneous repair, reconstruction using a V-Y advancement, or flexor hallucis longus transfer. Operative strategies are associated with higher rates of nerve injury but quicker return to sport and work. Literature regarding the risk of re-rupture is more mixed, as both equivalent re-rupture rates and lower re-rupture rates in the operative population have been observed in various publications.

*Myhrvold et al. performed a multicenter, randomized controlled trial to compare non-operative and operative (open and minimally invasive) interventions with Achilles tendon ruptures.* **They found** in their cohort of 554 patients that patient outcomes (measured by the Achilles’ tendon Total Rupture Score), physical performance, and physical function were comparable between groups. The re-rupture rate was higher in the non-operative group. **They concluded** that surgical intervention is not associated with superior outcomes compared with non-operative management.

*Glazebrook and Rubinger reviewed rehabilitation protocols used for non-operative treatment of Achilles tendon ruptures.* **They highlighted** the importance of initiating non-operative rehabilitation protocols within 48 hours of injury, patient compliance, and program supervision with a physical therapist. **They concluded** that though non-operative and operative management strategies are comparable with respect to patient outcomes, non-operative management requires strict adherence to rehabilitation protocols to achieve full benefit.

*Ochen et al. published a systematic review and meta-analysis of 29 studies assessing the outcomes of operative and non-operative treatment of Achilles tendon ruptures.* When comparing these two treatment modalities, **they found** that operative management resulted in a lower re-rupture rate (2.3% vs 3.9%). This difference was not seen specifically in patients who underwent a rehabilitation program with an emphasis on early range of motion. **They additionally noted** a higher rate of complications with surgical treatment (4.9% vs 1.6%). **They concluded** that the benefits of operative intervention should be weighed against its higher rate of complications. Ultimately, **they emphasized** that shared decision-making is needed to identify the best modality for each patient.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1, 3, 4, and 5 do not accurately reflect the literature demonstrating that operative intervention is associated with higher rates of nerve injury but quicker return to sport and work.
