## Question

A 22-year-old motorcyclist sustains open fractures to the left radial shaft and second and third metacarpals with exposed extensor tendon and bone. The fractures are approached via the dorsal open wounds of the forearm and hand with no additional incisions made. The radiograph and clinical photograph of the remaining defect in the hand are shown in Figures 55a and 55b. The remaining wound can be most appropriately covered with which of the following?

## Options

1. Split-thickness skin grafting
2. Posterior interosseous rotational flap
3. Radial forearm rotational flap
4. Groin flap
5. Free lateral arm flap

## Correct option

**3. Radial forearm rotational flap**

## Answer explanation

After adequate debridement, there is exposed bone, tendon, and hardware. Split-thickness skin grafting over exposed tendon will not have a viable bed to support the graft. The tendons would not have healthy surrounding tissue, resulting in poor tendon gliding. The dorsal wound has disrupted the posterior interosseous artery that runs in the septum between the extensor digiti minimi and the extensor carpi ulnaris. Following the reconstructive ladder, the radial forearm rotational flap accomplishes wound coverage with a local flap rather than a groin flap (a distant flap) or a lateral arm flap (microvascular free tissue transfer).
