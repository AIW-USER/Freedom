## Question

A 22-year-old collegiate tennis player presents to the clinic with an acute onset of pain over the medial aspect of the proximal leg after a tennis match two days ago. An ultrasound is obtained and demonstrates a rupture of the medial head of the gastrocnemius. What is the expected outcome for the recommended initial first-line treatment?

## Options

1. Poor outcomes with surgical management with a minority of patients ever achieving full return to activity
2. Excellent outcomes with surgical management with most returning to activity in 3 months
3. Excellent outcomes with conservative management, with most achieving return to activity within 3 months
4. Fair outcomes with conservative management, with a quarter of patients eventually requiring surgical intervention
5. Good outcomes with surgical management, though with half of patients requiring prolonged physical therapy for knee stiffness.

## Correct option

**3. Excellent outcomes with conservative management, with most achieving return to activity within 3 months**

## Answer explanation

This collegiate athlete has torn the medial head of his gastrocnemius. This initial treatment for this is with conservative management. The vast majority of patients, especially athletes, have excellent outcomes with return to activity within 2 months (Answer choice 3).

Tears of the medial head of the gastrocnemius are injuries that more commonly occur in the athlete population as a result of simultaneous knee extension and ankle dorsiflexion. Patients present with a sudden onset of pain and calf ecchymosis. Ultrasound and MRI may be used to confirm this clinical diagnosis but are often unnecessary. Initial treatment for this injury is conservative, and the vast majority of patients report excellent outcomes with full return to activity within three months.

*Domeracki et al. published two cases of patients with partial tears of the gastrocnemius tendon, an injury colloquially named “tennis leg”.* *They reviewed that though these injuries are more commonly seen in athletes, they may also occur in non-athletic settings.* **They concluded** that initial management of this injury, regardless of the mechanism of injury, is conservative, though non-athlete patients may have more prolonged recoveries.

*Ahmad et al. published a retrospective review of 30 patients with MRI-confirmed myotendinous Achilles ruptures to describe the clinical outcomes of non-operative management for these patients.* Treatment consisted of a gradual progression of weightbearing in an Achilles boot with physical therapy initiated at 3 weeks. **They reported** complete healing in all patients with significant improvements in foot and ankle specific outcome and pain scores. **They concluded** that non-operative management is a successful treatment strategy for this injury that achieves high patient satisfaction.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1, 2, and 5: Surgical management is not the first-line treatment for this injury
Answer 4: Conservative management typically yields excellent outcomes. Non-operative treatment failure is a rare occurrence.
