## Question

You are evaluating a baby with the condition shown in Figure A. This problem is unilateral. Which of the following statements regarding this condition is true?

![[q5055-a-clubfoot.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. The difference between bone age and chronological age is significantly different for patients with this condition
2. Hemihypertrophy of the uninvolved limb is associated with this condition
3. The entire involved limb is hypoplastic relative to the uninvolved limb, which may result in a limb length discrepancy
4. The anterior tibial artery is the dominant blood supply
5. This condition is not amenable to casting

## Correct option

**3. The entire involved limb is hypoplastic relative to the uninvolved limb, which may result in a limb length discrepancy**

## Answer explanation

This patient has congenital clubfoot (idiopathic talipes equinovarus). Hypoplasia of the entire lower extremity is characteristic of clubfoot. Ultimately, this may result in limb length discrepancy, with the primary length difference attributable to the tibia.

The pathology of clubfoot is incompletely understood and multiple proposed etiologies for the limb hypoplasia have been investigated. Although no definitive etiology has been established, it is apparent that there is a global abnormality affecting multiple tissue types in the clubfoot limb. Studies have shown hypoplasia of the anterior tibial and dorsalis pedis arteries but no difference in bone age.

*Hootnick et al. present a case report of a patient who underwent clubfoot surgery and subsequently developed necrosis of the hallux and first ray.* Arteriography revealed hypoplasia of the anterior and posterior tibial arteries as well as failure of the dorsalis pedis artery to complete the deep plantar arch.

*Shimode et al. measured limb lengths in 38 patients with unilateral clubfoot.* All measurements were shorter on the affected side, but tibial length contributed most to shortening.

*Spiegel et al. measured limb length and bone age in 47 patients with unilateral clubfoot.* **They found** a limb length discrepancy greater than 0.5 cm in 32 patients but no difference between bone age and chronological age.

Figure A is a clinical photo of a clubfoot.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1. Bone age and chronological age have not been demonstrated to be significantly different in the unilateral clubfoot population
Answer 2. Hypoplasia of the involved limb, rather than hemihypertrophy of the uninvolved limb, is characteristic of unilateral clubfoot.
Answer 4. The posterior tibial artery is the dominant blood supply in the clubfoot. The anterior tibial artery is more often hypoplastic.
Answer 5. Casting is the first line of treatment for congenital idiopathic clubfoot.
