## Question

A 56-year-old male presents to your clinic for follow-up of lower back pain. His MRI demonstrates L4/L5 disc height loss, signal intensity loss of the disc, and mild bulging posteriorly. Which of the following combination of disk component changes is most likely present with this condition?

## Options

1. Decreased metalloproteinase (MMP)-1 activity, decreased water content, decreased presence of proteoglycans
2. Decreased MMP-1 activity, increased water content, increased presence of proteoglycans
3. Increased MMP-1 activity, increased water content, increased presence of proteoglycans
4. Increased MMP-1 activity, decreased water content, decreased presence of proteoglycans
5. Increased MMP-1 activity, increased water content, decreased presence of proteoglycans

## Correct option

**4. Increased MMP-1 activity, decreased water content, decreased presence of proteoglycans**

## Answer explanation

This patient has findings consistent with degenerative disc disease. Progression of this condition is associated with increased MMP-1 activity, decreased presence of proteoglycans, and decreased water content (Answer 4).

Degenerative disc disease is a common cause of back pain that afflicts a significant proportion of the general population. Its pathophysiology is characterized by changes in the molecular composition of the cells that make up the intervertebral disc. These changes include an increase in the activity of degradative enzymes such as MMPs, which leads to the breakdown of major disc components such as collagen and proteoglycans (e.g., aggrecan). A lower level of proteoglycans, in turn, leads to a decrease in osmotic pressure and a subsequent reduction in water content. These processes ultimately result in disc height loss and diminished disc function.

*Kepler et al. reviewed the biology of disc degeneration and current strategies for addressing this condition.* *They review the anatomy, biomechanics, and local protein expression of the intervertebral disc before discussing proposed treatment strategies, including disk tissue preimplantation, stem cell implantation, platelet-rich plasma, disc innervation, intradiscal electrothermic therapy, protein-based therapy, and gene therapy.* **They conclude** that a good understanding of intervertebral disc degeneration cell biology is critical for the development of potential therapeutic modalities.

*Matsui et al. assessed the histology of herniated lumbar disc specimens to determine the association between MMP activity and the presence of inflammatory cell infiltrate.* **They found** that increased MMP activity was more frequently seen in herniated discs and was associated with the degree of granulation tissue present. **They concluded** that their findings suggest that MMPs have an inflammatory role in degenerative disc disease.

Answers: 1, 2, 3, and 5 do not accurately reflect the molecular changes seen in degenerative disc disease.
