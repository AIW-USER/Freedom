## Question

A 68-year-old engineer presents to your office for evaluation of progressively worsening flat feet. He reports buying increasingly expensive arch supports without relief. In the discussion of his condition, you mention attenuation of the spring ligament. Which of the following is true of this ligamentous complex?

## Options

1. Inferior ligament is the most important stabilizer of the longitudinal arch
2. Superomedial ligament is the most important stabilizer of the longitudinal arch
3. Inferior ligament is the largest section of the spring ligament
4. Originates on the anterior calcaneus and inserts on the medial border of the medial cuneiform
5. Originates on the anterior talus and inserts on the medial border of the navicular

## Correct option

**2. Superomedial ligament is the most important stabilizer of the longitudinal arch**

## Answer explanation

The superomedial calcaneonavicular ligament of the spring ligament complex provides the greatest stability to the longitudinal arch of the foot.

The spring ligamentous complex is intimately related, both anatomically and functionally, to the deltoid ligament of the ankle. It consists of both the superomedial and inferior calcaneonavicular ligaments. Of these, the superomedial calcaneonavicular, is the largest and provides the most support to the medial longitudinal arch of the foot. It originates at the anteromedial aspect of the sustentaculum tali on the calcaneus and inserts broadly along the medial aspect of the navicular. Attenuation of the spring ligament commonly occurs following posterior tibial tendon dysfunction and is associated with pes planus.

*Davis et al review the gross and biomechanical anatomy related to the spring ligament.* **They report** two distinct portions of the complex including both the superomedial and inferior calcaneonavicular ligaments. Of these, the superomedial calcaneonavicular ligament was larger and showed microscopic evidence of significant load-bearing function. In conclusion, **they report** that this complex likely provides a sling effect for the talar head.

*Taniguchi et al review the anatomy of the spring ligament.* **They report** that in addition to the superomedial and inferior calcaneonavicular ligament, a third structure they coined the "third ligament" also exists. **They conclude** that this structure consists of fibers running from the notch between the calcaneal facets and the navicular tuberosity.

*Mengiardi et al review the spring ligamentous complex and its relationship with the posterior tibial tendon.* **They report** that isolated injury to the spring ligamentous complex is rare and that most commonly attenuation occurs in the setting of posterior tibial tendon dysfunction. **They conclude** that when injuries occur to the medial stabilizers of the foot including the spring ligamentous complex, posterior tibial tendon, or deltoid ligament adult-acquired flatfoot deformity results.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1&3: The superomedial portion of the spring ligament is the largest and most important stabilizer of the medial longitudinal arch.
Answer 4&5: The superomedial portion of the spring ligament originates at the anteromedial aspect of the sustentaculum tali on the calcaneus and inserts broadly along the medial aspect of the navicular.
