## Question

The radiograph in Figure A shows the injury an 8-year-old male sustained after a fall on the playground. If left untreated, what deformity or complication would you expect from this injury?

![[q210143-a-ap-lat-condyle-ob.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Cubitus Valgus
2. Heterotopic ossification
3. PIN neurapraxia
4. Recurvatum
5. Radioulnar synostosis

## Correct option

**1. Cubitus Valgus**

## Answer explanation

The image demonstrates a displaced lateral condyle fracture. If neglected, this injury may progress to nonunion with subsequent cubitus valgus deformity. Other complications include lateral spurring, seen approximately 50% of the time, which may lead to a clinical pseudo-varus appearance but not actual cubitus varus.

Lateral condyle fractures are common injuries and are usually Salter-Harris IV injuries extending into the trochlea but sparing the ossific nucleus. It is thought that due to the lateral extensor pull at the fracture and/or synovial fluid interposing the fragments, these injuries are more prone to nonunion as compared with other pediatric elbow fractures. Treatment for nonunions is variable, ranging from curettage with in situ fixation with or without bone grafting to re-directional osteotomies.

*Park et al. reported a series of 16 lateral condyle nonunions that underwent in situ compression screw placement without bone grafting.* *All patients achieved union and had improvements in range of motion.* It should be noted that 3 of these did go on to have angular growth deformity over 10 degrees.

*Tien et al. developed a novel technique to correct cubitus valgus secondary to nonunions after lateral condyle fracture.* Their technique involves fixing the fracture in situ with compression screws then performing a supracondylar dome-shaped osteotomy correcting the angular joint line deformity. This technique poses a solution to the common osteonecrosis encountered after ORIF and bone grafting for these deformities.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Heterotopic ossification is seen after elbow trauma but rarely after lateral condyle fractures.
Gunstock deformity refers to cubitus varus which is more commonly seen with a supracondylar fracture.
Answer 3: PIN neurapraxias may be seen after Monteggia fractures
Answer 4: Recurvatum is more commonly seen in extension type supracondylar fractures
Answer 5: Radioulnar synostosis is seen after radial head/neck ORIF but typically not with lateral condyle fractures
