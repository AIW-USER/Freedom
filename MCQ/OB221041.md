## Question

A 13-year-old obese male presents to your clinic with a 6-month history of intermittent left hip pain and limping. His parents report that he has been avoiding sports activities. On examination, you note limited internal rotation of the left hip and obligatory external rotation with hip flexion. Radiographs are shown in Figure A. The parents are hesitant about surgery and ask about the long-term outcomes if their son undergoes in situ pinning versus more aggressive realignment procedures. Based on expected natural history, which of the following statements is most accurate regarding long-term outcomes for this condition?

## Options

1. Approximately 25% of patients with SCFE will require total hip arthroplasty within 20 years of diagnosis
2. Closed reduction techniques have demonstrated superior long-term outcomes compared to in situ pinning for chronic stable slips
3. Most slips are well tolerated long-term with approximately 5% requiring total hip arthroplasty at 20-year follow-up
4. Realignment procedures have been definitively shown to prevent degenerative arthritis and reduce the rate of total hip arthroplasty
5. Avascular necrosis occurs in less than 5% of patients following acute unstable slips when treated with in situ pinning

## Correct option

**3. Most slips are well tolerated long-term with approximately 5% requiring total hip arthroplasty at 20-year follow-up**

## Answer explanation

The patient presents with a stable SCFE. Most slips appear well tolerated long-term, with approximately 5% resulting in total hip arthroplasty (THA) at 20-year follow-up (Answer 3).

The fundamental goal of SCFE treatment is to prevent slip progression and minimize complications, particularly avascular necrosis (AVN). Current evidence supports in situ pinning as the gold standard for stable SCFE, offering the best balance of effectiveness and safety with low complication rates. For unstable SCFE, urgent surgical fixation (ideally within 24 hours) with in situ pinning and capsular decompression is recommended to minimize the risk of AVN. Patients should be made immediately non-weight-bearing upon diagnosis to prevent slip progression. While newer realignment procedures (such as the modified Dunn procedure) have gained popularity to address residual deformity and femoroacetabular impingement, long-term data demonstrating superiority over traditional in situ fixation remain lacking. Treatment must also address underlying medical comorbidities, including obesity and potential endocrine disorders.

*Mathew et al. present the natural history of SCFE and* **found** that most slips are well tolerated long-term, with approximately 5% resulting in total hip arthroplasty (THA) at 20-year follow-up. The most significant complication is avascular necrosis (AVN), occurring in 15-50% of patients with acute unstable slips, which represents the most common cause of THA in SCFE patients. **The authors conclude** that despite innovative realignment techniques gaining popularity, long-term outcome data still support in situ pinning for stable slips and in situ pinning with capsular decompression for unstable slips to minimize AVN risk. Importantly, SCFE patients should receive interventions for weight loss, blood pressure management, and lifestyle modifications given their increased risk of disability and death from underlying medical comorbidities.

*Sucato et al. reviewed the North American approach to SCFE and summarized how treatment has evolved over the past 15-20 years due to improved understanding of femoral head blood supply, real-time intraoperative monitoring of blood flow/pressure, and development of surgical approaches providing better access to the deformity.* **They conclude** that these advances have primarily impacted the management of unstable SCFE to reduce avascular necrosis risk, but are also applied to stable SCFE with severe residual deformity.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: This overestimates the rate of THA. The literature indicates approximately 5%, not 25%, require THA at 20-year follow-up. While THA rates are higher than the general population, most patients do not require joint replacement in the first two decades after diagnosis.
Answer 2: Classic data actually reveal poor outcomes following closed reduction for treatment of SCFE. Closed reduction, particularly forceful manipulation, is associated with increased risk of avascular necrosis and worse long-term outcomes compared to in situ pinning.
Answer 4: While these procedures have increased in popularity to address anatomic abnormalities, it remains unclear whether they prevent degenerative arthritis and reduce the rate of THA. Long-term outcome data still support in situ pinning as the standard of care.
Answer 5: This significantly underestimates AVN risk in unstable slips. Avascular necrosis occurs in 15% to 50% of patients following acute, unstable slips, not less than 5%. This high complication rate makes unstable SCFE a true orthopedic emergency requiring urgent surgical intervention.
