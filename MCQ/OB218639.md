## Question

2. Figure B with a 4-5 intermetatarsal angle (IMA) of 9°

5. This procedure is not routinely indicated for the patient's pathology

## Correct option

**3. Figure C with a 4-5 intermetatarsal angle (IMA) of 14°**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a symptomatic bunionette deformity. An oblique mid-diaphyseal rotational osteotomy is indicated for type II or III bunionettes with a 4-5 intermetatarsal angle (IMA) greater than 12° (Answer 3).

Bunionette deformities, eponymously known as tailor's bunions, are prominences on the lateral aspect of the fifth metatarsal head which typically occur as a result of compression of the forefoot. They are commonly seen in adolescents and adults and occur more frequently in female patients. Patients will often complain of bony prominence and bursitis over the lateral aspect of the fifth metatarsal head, and this pathology is often associated with varus alignment of the metatarsal phalangeal joint and pes planus.

Bunionettes are classified based on the type of deformity present. Type I bunionettes represents an enlarged fifth metatarsal head or lateral exostosis, type II represents congenital bowing of the fifth metatarsal with a normal IMA (6.5° - 8.0°) and type III represents bunionettes secondary to an increased 4-5 IMA angle. The initial management of a bunionette is nonoperative with NSAIDs, shoe wear modification, orthotics, padding, and callous shaving, with surgery reserved for deformities with symptoms recalcitrant to nonoperative management.

*Shi et al. provide an overview of the management of bunionette deformities.* **The authors note** that the best initial management for these deformities is nonoperative and that patients with symptoms unresponsive to nonoperative management often do well with surgery. **The authors further note** that for patients with an IMA > 12°, an oblique mid-diaphyseal rotational osteotomy is often necessary in order to normalize the pathologically increased 4-5 IMA.

*Ceccarini et al. provide a comprehensive systematic review of the literature surrounding the treatment of bunionette deformities.* **The authors highlight** that a majority of the published literature is limited to level IV evidence and, as a result, the ability to draw conclusions on what constitutes the gold standard management remains limited. Promisingly, however, is that both operative and nonoperative management provide predictable improvement in pain and patient-reported outcomes for patients.

Incorrect Answers
Answer 1: this patient would be best managed with a lateral condylectomy.
Answers 2 and 4: this patient would be best managed with a distal metatarsal osteotomy.
Answer 5: an oblique mid-diaphyseal rotational osteotomy is the appropriate treatment for patients with type II or III bunionettes with an IMA > 12°.
