## Question

A 40-year-old patient with COMP-related pseudoachondroplasia presents with difficulty ambulating. On physical examination, they have difficulty maintaining balance, a wide-based gait, and 3+ bilateral patellar and Achilles reflexes. What radiographic study and paired finding likely explain this patient's symptoms?

## Options

1. Flexion-extension radiographs of the cervical spine with a difference in the atlantodens interval (ADI) of 6 mm
2. Open mouth odontoid radiograph with a lateral mass displacement of 9 mm
3. Hip radiographs with joint space narrowing and osteophyte formation
4. Standing thoracic/lumbar PA and lateral radiographs showing a Cobb angle of 70 degrees
5. MRI of the lumbar spine showing diffuse stenosis

## Correct option

**1. Flexion-extension radiographs of the cervical spine with a difference in the atlantodens interval (ADI) of 6 mm**

## Answer explanation

Difficulty maintaining balance, a wide-based gait, and hyperreflexia are myelopathic examination findings that may result from spinal cord compression from atlanto-axial instability which is associated with pseudoachondroplasia. The first step in the evaluation is obtaining flexion-extension radiographs of the cervical spine to assess atlanto-axial instability (answer choice 1).

A mutation in the COMP gene causes pseudoachondroplasia. In patients with pseudoachondroplasia, myelopathic findings must raise the clinician’s suspicion for atlanto-axial instability. The initial work-up should include flexion-extension radiographs of the cervical spine, with assessment of the ADI difference between views. In adults, a difference of greater than 3.5 mm in the ADI between flexion and extension radiographs is diagnostic for atlanto-axial instability. In pediatric patients, a difference of greater than 5 mm is diagnostic.

*Adam et al. reviewed the clinical characteristics, diagnosis/testing, management, and genetic counseling for patients with pseudoachondroplasia.* **They noted** that patients with neurologic manifestations require careful monitoring. **They concluded** that symptomatic patients may require C1-C2 fixation for atlanto-axial instability.

*Weiner et al. review the orthopedic manifestations of pseudoachondroplasia.* **They noted** several characteristic radiographic changes that occur in both the axial and appendicular skeleton. **They concluded** that patients with pseudoachondroplasia are at increased risk of early arthritis of the hips and knees.

*McKay et al. review the cervical spine anomalies in common genetic syndromes.* *They review that patients with pseudoachondroplasia typically present with atlanto-axial instability secondary to odontoid dysplasia (hypoplasia, os odontoideum, etc.).* **They concluded** that orthopedic surgeons must be vigilant in monitoring these patients, as symptomatic instability may require treatment.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Lateral mass displacement >8.1 mm suggests an unstable atlas fracture and is not the most likely explanation for this patient’s myelopathic findings.
Answer 3: Hip osteoarthritis is common in pseudoachondroplasia. However, this would not explain myelopathic findings.
Answer 4: Patients with pseudoachondroplasia can develop scoliosis. However, atlanto-axial instability is more likely the cause of this patient’s myelopathic examination.
Answer 5: Patients with achondroplasia more commonly present with lumbar stenosis, whereas pseudoachondroplasia more commonly presents with atlanto-axial instability. Additionally, lumbar stenosis would not explain myelopathic findings.
