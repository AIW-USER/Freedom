## Question

Which of the following represents inappropriate age-based treatment for a healthy female with developmental dysplasia of the hip (DDH) who presents with a painless dislocation?

## Options

1. 2 months of age, application of a Pavlik harness
2. 17 months of age, closed reduction, arthrogram with an 8mm medial dye pool, and spica casting
3. 22 months of age, open reduction and spica casting
4. 3.5 years of age, open reduction, varus proximal femoral osteotomy, and spica casting
5. 5 months of age, application of a rigid abduction brace

## Correct option

**2. 17 months of age, closed reduction, arthrogram with an 8mm medial dye pool, and spica casting**

## Answer explanation

At 17 months of age a closed reduction under anesthesia is acceptable treatment, however a dye-pool >6mm has been associated with high rates of treatment failure and osteonecrosis.

The appropriate treatment of DDH is dependent upon patient age and degree of dysplasia. Children presenting with dysplasia prior to the age of 6 months are treated with continuous application of Pavlik harness, or alternatively, application of a rigid abduction brace in the event of Pavlik failure. After 6 months of age, most authors recommend variations of closed and open reduction under anesthesia, followed by spica casting for maintenance of reduction. Intraoperative closed reduction is confirmed by arthrogram under dynamic fluoroscopy with measurement of medial dye pool. A dye pool measurement of 5-6mm is suggestive of successful reduction, however dye pools >6mm are predictive of failure.

*Vitale et. al. reviewed the treatment of DDH from 6 months to 4 years of age.* **They report** that a concentric reduction can frequently be achieved with closed reduction reduction under anesthesia, adductor tenotomy, and spica casting. They endorse arthrography under dynamic fluoroscopy as a method to confirm reduction, and that a medial dye pool of <5-7mm has been demonstrated to improve outcomes. A dye pool >7mm is associated with an unacceptable outcome, including a 57% rate of osteonecrosis.

*Race et. al. evaluated 59 children with congenital hip dislocations treated with closed reduction under anesthesia.* Among patients with medial dye pools of >7mm (an inadequate reduction), acceptable outcome occurred in only 22% of hips, with an osteonecrosis rate of 57%. Those patients with reductions with <7mm dye pool had an osteonecrosis rate of 20%, and nearly all had restoration of the acetabular and lateral center edge angles.

*Murphy et. al. performed an updated review on surgical treatment of hip dysplasia.* **They report** that medial dye pool <6mm is consistent with a successful reduction.

A dye pool <7mm is suggestive of reduction and associated with improved outcomes. Illustration B is the same hip following reduction, with a clear decrease in the medial dye pool.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1, 3-5: All of these are potential age-appropriate treatment strategies for developmental dysplasia of the hip.
