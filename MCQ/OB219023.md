## Question

A 24-year-old with sickle-cell disease undergoes a left total hip arthroplasty for AVN and is unhappy 6 months after the surgery due to a very perceptible leg length discrepancy. Which of the following is associated with decreased medical litigation amongst Orthopaedic surgeons?

## Options

1. Shorter clinic visit times
2. Effective communication
3. Templated documentation
4. Avoidance of high complication risk patients
5. Surgical technique

## Correct option

**2. Effective communication**

## Answer explanation

Errors in communication represent an associated cause of medical litigation in the field of Orthopaedic surgery.

While it is important to perform diligent surgery in properly selected patients, complications can still be expected to occur despite the best of preparations and intentions. Methods for avoiding malpractice claims include:

(1) adhering to the standard of care and taking measures to avoid blatant errors
(2) providing comprehensive informed consent with effective communication
(3) clear and accurate documentation of all pre-operative discussions that include discussion of surgical risks and complications

*Pappas et al. provide a review of medical malpractice in hand surgery and* **showed** that nearly 40% of the US members of the American Society for Surgery of the Hand has been sued at some point for the treatment of a hand condition. The authors outline "rules for staying out of trouble," which include avoiding blatant mistakes such as wrong site surgery, being honest with your patients, and keeping them informed. **They state** that patients are inclined to sue for malpractice if they have an outcome that was not properly explained to them when obtaining surgical consent.

*Nebel et al. review the critical components that enhance a good doctor and patient relationship.* **The authors note** that the occurrence of a malpractice lawsuit is more often the result of the failure to practice the art of medicine by the orthopaedist rather than a failure to apply the science of orthopaedics.

Incorrect Answers:
1. Long waits and shorter clinic visit times are common complaints among patients. While this can be somewhat expected in high-volume practices, it results in less effective communication with patients due to time constraints. Hiring mid-level providers to help minimize wait times and facilitate high-volume clinics may help negate the risk of future malpractice claims.
3. Templated documentation helps increase productivity. Though documentation errors can be used unfavorably in malpractice suits, they are not in and of themselves considered a leading cause of litigation amongst Orthopaedic surgeons.
4. Proper patient selection and adherence to surgical indications are involved in providing standard-of-care treatments. Providing informed consent with a thorough risk and benefits discussion negates the potential for malpractice claims.
5. Though errors in surgical technique can lead to complications, they are not considered to be a leading cause of litigation. Errors do occur despite the best preparations, and informed consent should be provided to the patient prior to any surgical intervention to educate them of potential complications.
