## Question

A 42-year-old obese male with a BMI of 41 sustains a closed left transverse midshaft tibia fracture. He subsequently undergoes reamed intramedullary fixation 36 hours following the injury. After surgery, the patient is prescribed 800 mg ibuprofen twice daily as well as oxycodone for pain control. The patient also endorses smoking cigarettes, 1 pack per day, for 20 years. Which of the following factors places this patient at highest risk for developing a non-union?

## Options

1. Reamed nailing of a closed tibia fracture
2. NSAID use postoperatively
3. Delay in surgical treatment >24 hours
4. BMI >40
5. Smoking

## Correct option

**5. Smoking**

## Answer explanation

Smoking has been repeatedly shown to have a high association with the development of non-union.

Numerous factors have been associated with the development of non-union, however, smoking has consistently been indicated as a risk factor for development of this complication. The basis of this finding is thought to be caused by a reduction in peripheral blood flow secondary to nicotine (a powerful vasoconstrictor) and a reduction in the oxygen-carrying capacity of hemoglobin due to bonding of hemoglobin with carbon monoxide. The effects are also long lasting, as patients who report being prior smokers are also at an increased risk for non-union, albeit, not as high as those who currently smoke.

*Scolaro et al. review cigarette smoking and the risk of complications following orthopedic surgery.* **They report** an odds ratio of 2.32 when comparing risk of non-union in patients who are smokers vs. nonsmokers. **They conclude** that smoking significantly increases the risk of nonunion of all fractures, particularly tibial fractures and open fractures.

*Jeffcoach et al. review the impact of NSAIDS on fracture healing and non-union.* **They report** that patients who received NSAIDs in the postoperative period were 2 times as likely and smokers more than 3 times as likely to suffer complications such as nonunion/malunion or infection. **They conclude** that surgeons should counsel their patients on these potentially complications.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Reamed intramedullary fixation of tibial shaft fractures has been shown to decrease, not increase, non-union rates.
*Answer 2: Smoking results in a 3x increase risk of non-union compared to the 2x risk in patients receiving NSAIDs in the study by Jeffcoach et al.*
Answer 3: Delayed surgical fixation for closed tibial shaft fractures has not been linked to fracture non-union.
Answer 4: While BMI is a risk factor associated with increased risk of complications, it has not been linked as strongly as smoking to the development of non-union.
