## Question

A 59-year-old women with known metastatic lung cancer presents with acute left hip pain with ambulation, as shown in Figure A. She previously underwent a right lung lobectomy 2 years ago, which has been complicated by chronic chest pain. Her oncologist predicts an estimated life expectancy of 12-18 months. She currently lives independently and walks for 2 hours per day. What would be the most appropriate treatment and rehabilitation plan?

![[q5794-a-image123456.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Observation; restrict activities with cane assistance as needed
2. Radiation therapy; partial weight bearing with crutch assistance
3. Prophylactic intramedullary nailing; non-weight bearing with crutch assistance
4. Prophylactic intramedullary nailing; full weight bearing with cane assistance
5. Long stemmed total hip arthroplasty; full weight bearing with walker assistance and hip precautions

## Correct option

**4. Prophylactic intramedullary nailing; full weight bearing with cane assistance**

## Answer explanation

This patient presents with a peritrochanteric, lytic lesion, which is > 2/3 the diameter of the bone, and causing functional pain (Mirels score = 12/12). The best treatment would be prophylactic intramedullary nailing, followed by full weight bearing with a ambulatory assistance.

The femur is the most common long bone associated with metastatic disease and therefore the most common site for pathologic fracture. Both Mirel's criteria and Harrington's criteria are used to predict the risk of pathologic fracture in the presence of a metastatic lesion and indications for prophylactic fixation.

*Mirels et al. proposed a scoring system to predict the risk of pathological fracture over a 6 month period based on a score out of 12 (e.g., type of lesion, site, size and pain).* Using this scoring system, the risk of fracture (%) can be estimated as:

Score 4-6: 0%
Score 7: 4%
Score 8: 15%
Score 9: 33%
Score 10: 72%
Score 11: 96%
Score 12: 100%

*Faruqui et al. reviewed the role of ambulatory assistive devices in orthopaedics.* **They state** that a single cane can support approximately one quarter of a person's body weight, whereas a forearm crutch can bear almost half of the weight. Crutches permit up to 80% of weight bearing of the body for a single crutch and up to 100% of weight bearing of the body for two crutches in swing-through gaits.

Figure A is an AP radiograph of the left hip showing a subtrochanteric lytic lesion without evidence of fracture.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: A Mirel score 12: 100% chance of fracture within 6 months. Observation would be incorrect.
Answer 2: Radiation therapy only may be used as a source of pain control if the estimated life expectancy was less than 6 months.
Answer 3: Prophylactic intramedullary nailing would be acceptable treatment. Usually these patients are weight-bearing as tolerated after the procedure. Axillary crutches would not be appropriate in a patient with lymphadenopathy.
Answer 5: A long stemmed total hip arthroplasty would not be necessary, as this patient has acute hip pain with only mild radiographic arthritis. Arthroplasty would not be the first line treatment for this lesion.
