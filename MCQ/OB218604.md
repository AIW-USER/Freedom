## Question

A 10-year-old male presents to the Emergency Department with complaints of right shoulder pain. Radiographs obtained reveal a midshaft clavicle fracture with 100% displacement and 12mm of shortening. On examination, there is no skin tenting, open wounds, or altered neurovascular findings. Regarding treatment options for this patient, which of the following is true?

## Options

1. 50% of patients with malunion will be symptomatic after nonoperative management
2. Nonoperative management is indicated in this patient because of his age
3. Plate fixation is indicated given the amount of displacement
4. The amount of shortening places him at high risk for nonunion
5. The amount of displacement places him at high risk for nonunion

## Correct option

**2. Nonoperative management is indicated in this patient because of his age**

## Answer explanation

Nonoperative management by way of sling or shoulder immobilizer is indicated in patients <12 years of age due to high remodeling potential. Almost all fractures in this age group are treated nonoperatively.

Clavicle shaft fractures are common pediatric fractures that most commonly occur due to a fall on an outstretched arm or direct trauma to the lateral aspect of the shoulder. Diagnosis can be made with plain radiographs. Treatment is generally nonoperative management with a sling. Surgical management is indicated for open fractures or those associated with impending soft tissue compromise.

*Yang et al. reviewed midshaft clavicle fractures in adolescents.* **They reported** that recent literature in adult fractures favors open reduction and plate fixation for significantly displaced and/or shortened midshaft clavicle fractures. **They concluded** however, this does not necessarily apply to the management of displaced adolescent clavicle fractures.

*McIntosh et al. reviewed adolescent clavicle fractures.* **They reported** that adult literature is not applicable as adolescent mid-diaphyseal clavicle fractures rarely develop nonunions, and only a small percentage (10% to 20%) are symptomatic from malunions. **They concluded** that current indications for operative fixation are: (1) completely displaced midshaft fracture with shortening of >2 cm; (2) superior displacement with skin tenting and/or an impending open fracture; (3) associated neurovascular injury; (4) open clavicular fracture; and (5) floating shoulder with a completely displaced clavicular fracture.

*Scott et al. reviewed displaced middle-third clavicular fractures in pediatric and adolescent patients.* **They reported** that existing literature does not clearly delineate appropriate indications for nonoperative care or relative indications for operative intervention. **They concluded** that further studies are needed to guide treatment and promote better-informed consent.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: 10-20% of the subjects with clavicle fracture malunion remain symptomatic.
Answer 3: Almost all fractures in this age group are treated nonoperatively.
Answers 4&5: Adolescent mid-diaphyseal clavicle fractures rarely develop nonunions.
