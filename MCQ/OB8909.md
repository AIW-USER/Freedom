## Question

A 51-year-old male presents for evaluation of his left foot. He states that he previously underwent multiple surgeries to the left big toe, with the most recent surgery being 10 years ago. He did well initially but over the past 3 years, he has had persistent pain of the big toe which worsens with 1st metatarsophalangeal joint (MTPJ) dorsiflexion and plantarflexion. His radiographs are shown in Figures A through C. His wound is well healed without any drainage. He is interested in surgical management. What would be the most appropriate next step in management?

## Options

1. Antibiotic spacer and dorsal plate fixation
2. Distraction bone block arthrodesis
3. Shortening arthrodesis
4. External fixation lengthening
5. Revision MTP arthroplasty

## Correct option

**2. Distraction bone block arthrodesis**

## Answer explanation

The patient has a painful hallux MTPJ and severe 1st metatarsal shortening with a history of prior MTPJ arthroplasty. The most appropriate next step in management would be a distraction bone block arthrodesis to restore length and reduce pain.

A distraction bone block arthrodesis of the 1st MTP joint is a salvage procedure used to restore length and help improve symptomatic hallux MTPJ pain in the setting of metatarsal shortening and hallux rigidis or prior MTPJ arthroplasty. Other indications for structural bone graft for 1st MTPJ arthrodesis include shortening that cannot be adequately rebalanced with a lesser metatarsal osteotomy (usually shortening > 5 mm), significant proximal phalanx bone loss with inadequate remaining bone for fixation without compromising IP joint, and 1st MT shortening with loss of medial support of the 2nd toe predisposing to varus at the 2nd MTP joint. The bone block used can be both structural autograft (typically iliac crest) or allograft.

*Malhotra et al. performed a retrospective review of a series of 24 patients who had an interposition bone block arthrodesis procedure performed using a tricortical iliac crest autograft with a mean follow-up period of 62 months.* **The authors observed** a mean lengthening of 4.4 mm with 21/24 patients achieving a successful union. **The authors conclude** that a 1st MTPJ bone block arthrodesis is successful in salvaging failed first ray surgery and prevented further shortening.

*Myerson et al. performed a review of 24 patients who underwent bone block arthrodesis of the 1st MTPJ for the restoration of first ray length.* **The authors noted** successful union occurred in 19/24 patients at a mean of 13.3 weeks, and the first ray was lengthened by a mean of 13 mm. **The authors noted** that all patients were satisfied with the final outcome of treatment and stated that they would undergo the surgical procedure again. **The authors concluded** that arthrodesis of the hallux MTPJ with bone graft to restore bone loss and length of the first ray may be a worthwhile procedure despite the technical difficulty and the high rate of nonunion.

Figures A through C are the AP, oblique and lateral foot radiographs that reveal a 1st MTPJ arthroplasty with a significant metatarsal shortening deformity. Illustration A reveals an AP radiograph of a patient who underwent bone block 1st MTPJ arthrodesis with tricortical allograft.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: There is no evidence of any wound complications or other signs of infection in this patient so an antibiotic spacer is not indicated.
Answer 3: The 1st metatarsal is already short compared to the 2nd and 3rd metatarsals so there is no indication for a shortening osteotomy.
Answer 4: External fixation lengthening would be an indication to restore length in brachymetatarsia.
Answer 5: Given the significant bone loss and painful MTPJ, the patient would not benefit from another arthroplasty.
