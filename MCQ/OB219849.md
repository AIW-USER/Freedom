## Question

A 74-year-old female presents to your clinic for evaluation of left hip arthritis. She has failed conservative management and would like to discuss replacement. When considering the possibility of post-operative, peripheral nerve injury which of the following represents a risk factor for peripheral nerve injury?

## Options

1. Operative time
2. Use of DMARDs
3. Chronic pain medication use
4. Body mass index
5. Decreased pre-albumin

## Correct option

**1. Operative time**

## Answer explanation

Patients with longer operating times are at an increased risk of peripheral nerve injury compared to their peers (Answer 1).

Postsurgical acute nerve injury is a rare but potentially devastating complication following total hip arthroplasty (THA). Several large database studies have identified risk factors associated with this complication, with a history of spine pathology or surgery and younger age being repeatedly identified. Other risk factors include current or prior tobacco use, uncontrolled diabetes, and a history of thromboembolism. Additional risk factors include operative time, timing of surgery (first case vs later in the day), and surgery performed by a more experienced surgeon.

*Shetty et al. reviewed their series of THA and* **identified** 93 nerve injuries in 43,761 THAs (0.21%). **They report** several risk factors for nerve injury including age < 45, current or previous tobacco use, spinal disease or history of surgery, and increased operative time. Conversely, patients who had surgery as the first case experienced a protective effect against injury. **They conclude** that although the overall risk is very low, these factors are possibly modifiable.

*Christ et al. used the New York Statewide Planning and Research Cooperative System (SPARCS) to identify risk factors for peripheral nerve injury after THA.* **The authors found** 487 nerve injuries amongst a cohort of 207, 981 cases. **They report** preexisting spinal conditions, dislocation, uncontrolled diabetes, younger age, in-hospital complications, and thromboembolic events are all predictive of nerve injury. **The authors conclude** that their data allows healthcare providers to identify patients at higher risk of this serious complication.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 2: While the use of DMARDs is generally associated with an increased risk of infection, they have not been shown to increase the risk of peripheral nerve injury.
Answer 3: Chronic pain medication use is not associated with peripheral nerve injury in THA
Answer 4: Body mass index was not found to be associated with increased risk
Answer 5: Decreased pre-albumin has not been investigated as a risk factor for peripheral nerve injury
