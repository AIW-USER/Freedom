Chapter: Arthroplasty MCQs — Reverse Shoulder Arthroplasty

## Question

True about humeral prosthesis in RSA are all except

## Options

1. increased risk of tuberosity fracture with inlay stem
2. Improved external rotation with onlay stem
3. Onlay design have a more valgus cut and cause more damage to remaining rotator cuff
4. Onlay designs are convertible to or from hemiarthroplasty and anatomic TSA.

## Correct option

**3. Onlay design have a more valgus cut and cause more damage to remaining rotator cuff**

## Answer explanation

Answer: 3
Inlay versus onlay prosthesis RSA:
Humerus preparation has also been modified since Grammont’s inlay prosthesis. Grammont’s initial stem was straight with a horizontal inlay-type humeral tray. A theoretical advantage of the inlay stem is increased bony contact between the proximal component and bone. However, the inlay design involves reaming more metaphyseal bone and preparation may risk greater tuberosity fracture.
Curved-stem designs with an onlay proximal interface have been utilized as well. The onlay design preserves proximal bone, and is generally has a more varus cut, preserving greater tuberosity bone and minimizing damage to the remaining rotator cuff. Furthermore, these prostheses may be convertible to or from hemiarthroplasty and anatomic TSA.

 Intrinsic to the more vertical inclination is increased humeral offset. Modular onlay humeral stems allow for the humeral tray to be rotated on the stem, with intraoperative adjustment to low/high offset configurations. Clinical studies have shown improved ROM in adduction, extension, and external rotation with onlay stems compared to inlay stem.
