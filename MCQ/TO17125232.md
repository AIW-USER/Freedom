#TO #basics #materialproperties
Chapter: General Orthopedics MCQs — Material Properties And Orthopedic Implants

## Question

True about working length are all except

## Options

1. It is maximum distance between proximal most and distal most screws in a plate
2. A greater working length will reduce stress risers at the fixation points
3. The working length of a unicortical screw is less than a bicortical screw
4. For external fixators, the working length is determined by the spacing of fixation pins or pin clusters, or the spacing of circular frames within the same bone segment.

## Correct option

**1. It is maximum distance between proximal most and distal most screws in a plate**

## Answer explanation

![[TO17125232-1.png]]
Working length refers to the distance between the closest fixation points (screws or pins) adjacent to the fracture on either side. It should not be confused with bridge span, which is the length of the plate segment spanning the fracture zone without screws.

A greater working length increases the flexibility of the fixation construct and reduces stress risers at the end screws, thereby decreasing the risk of implant failure. A short working length concentrates bending forces at the nearest screw to the fracture, creating high stress risers and increasing the risk of fatigue failure.

In plate fixation, increasing the working length is achieved by placing screws farther away from the fracture site. A longer plate with screws spaced apart provides better load distribution and reduces stress concentration at fixation points. The number of screws is less important than their position; beyond three diaphyseal screws, additional screws add minimal axial stability and only slightly improve torsional stability.

Bridge span and working length are related but distinct. Increasing the bridge span by omitting screws near the fracture increases the working length, which promotes controlled interfragmentary motion and callus formation in bridge plating.

In intramedullary nailing, a centrally located diaphyseal fracture results in comparable working lengths proximally and distally. In contrast, distal fractures create a short distal working length, leading to higher stress at distal interlocking screws and the nail–bone interface.

In external fixators, working length is determined by the distance between fixation pins or pin clusters within the same bone segment. A shorter working length increases construct stiffness, while a longer working length allows more motion at the fracture site.

Regarding screws, the effective working length of a unicortical screw is limited to the thickness of a single cortex, making it more susceptible to toggle and failure under torsional forces. Bicortical screws, engaging both cortices, have a greater working length and provide superior resistance to stress at the bone–screw interface.

Why Option A is Incorrect

“It is the maximum distance between proximal most and distal most screws in a plate” describes the bridge span, not the working length.

Correct answer: A
