## Question

Figure A is the radiograph of a 55-year-old male who presents to your office for 6 week's follow-up for his right great toe. Last you saw him, he was diagnosed with degenerative disease of the first MTP joint. At that time you prescribed a Morton extension insole that extended just under the great toe. The patient continues to report pain in the first MTP joint that is worse with walking. He works as a construction manager and is constantly walking job sites at work. On exam, his pain remains on the dorsum of the hallux with pain during extreme dorsiflexion. He has no mid-range pain. Which of the following is the best next step in treatment?

![[q218565-a-gr1-lrg.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Physical therapy
2. Extension of the Morton's extension to include the second metatarsal
3. Proximal phalanx corrective osteotomy
4. Dorsal cheilectomy
5. First MTP arthrodesis

## Correct option

**4. Dorsal cheilectomy**

## Answer explanation

Dorsal cheilectomies are reserved for patients who have failed conservative measures but continue to have hallux pain at the extremes of motion.

Hallux rigidus is a common foot condition characterized by pain and loss of motion of the 1st MTP joint in adults due to degenerative arthritis. Treatment of early disease consists of a trial of nonoperative management with a Morton's extension orthotic that extends under the 1st MTP to prevent it from flexing and extending during gait, which is the source of pain. Operative management may consist of a dorsal cheilectomy, which is commonly reserved for patients who have pain at the extremes of motion, or arthrodesis for patients with pain at the mid-range of motion.

*Hamid et al review the clinical presentation and management of hallux rigidus.* **They report** that nonsurgical management with anti-inflammatory medications, corticosteroid injections, or shoewear and activity modifications can be successful in appropriately selected patients. However, **they conclude** that surgical intervention may be necessary for patients with more severe disease or those who have failed conservative treatment, consisting of cheilectomy or joint altering procedures such as arthrodesis.

*Coughlin et al review their experience with the treatment of hallux rigidus.* **They report** that 92% (n=86) of the cheilectomies were successful in terms of pain relief and function. **They conclude** that the cheilectomy can be used with predictable success to treat Grade-1 and 2 and selected Grade-3 cases of hallux rigidus.

Figure A is the lateral radiograph of a foot showing dorsal osteophytes of the first MTP joint with a relatively well-maintained joint space.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Physical therapy would likely aggravate rather than improve this patient's hallux rigidus.
Answer 2: Extending Morton's extension to the second metatarsal would not provide any additional benefit to the first MTP joint.
Answer 3: Proximal phalanx osteotomies are reserved for patients who fail to have an adequate range of motion following cheilectomy.
Answer 5: First MTP arthrodesis is aggressive for this patient with low-grade hallux rigidus and pain at the extremes of motion.
