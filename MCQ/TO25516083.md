Chapter: Regional Orthopedics MCQs — Pes Planus - Flat Foot

## Question

During a biomechanical evaluation of a rigid flatfoot secondary to a middle facet talocalcaneal coalition, which secondary radiographic sign on a standard lateral weight-bearing radiograph of the foot is most indicative of the altered kinematics, specifically representing the remodeling of the dorsal talonavicular joint lip?

## Options

1. Loss of the normal calcaneal pitch angle
2. The 'C-sign'
3. The 'Anteater nose' sign
4. Talonavicular 'beaking'

## Correct option

**4. Talonavicular 'beaking'**

## Answer explanation

Talonavicular beaking occurs due to abnormal micro-motion and altered mechanical stress transference at the talonavicular joint, resulting in periosteal lifting and hypertrophic bone formation at the dorsal lip of the talus.

Radiological signs of tarsal coalitions:

1. Signs of Calcaneonavicular (CN) Coalition

These signs are best visualized on a standard lateral weight-bearing view and a $45^\circ$ internal oblique view of the foot.

The "Anteater Nose" Sign

 - View: Lateral radiograph or internal oblique radiograph.

 - Pathology: This is the most classic sign of a CN coalition. It represents a dramatic anterior elongation and tapering of the anterior process of the calcaneus as it reaches up toward the navicular bone, resembling the snout of an anteater.

 - Clinical Note: Even if a true bony synostosis is not present (i.e., it is a fibrous syndesmosis), this osseous elongation is highly indicative of the pathology.

The "Comma" Sign

 - View: 45 degree internal oblique view.

 - Pathology: In a normal foot, the space between the anterior calcaneus and the navicular is distinct and clear. In a CN coalition, the bridging tissue or closely apposed bone creates a blurred, elongated, comma-shaped shadow overlapping the midtarsal joint line.

2. Signs of Talocalcaneal (TC) Coalition

Because the middle facet of the subtalar joint sits at an angle, direct visualization of a talocalcaneal coalition on plain films is difficult. Therefore, surgeons look for secondary mechanical signs on the weight-bearing lateral view.

The "C-Sign"

 - View: Lateral radiograph.

 - Pathology: This is a highly reliable indicator of a middle facet subtalar coalition. It is a continuous, C-shaped radiopaque line formed by the silhouette of the dome of the talus tracing inferiorly and posteriorly across the blocked sustentaculum tali and the inferior border of the calcaneus.

 - Pitfall: A false-positive C-sign can occasionally be seen if the lateral X-ray is taken with poor positioning (severe internal or external rotation).

Talonavicular "Beaking"

 - View: Lateral radiograph.

 - Pathology: This appears as a sharp, prominent osteophytic projection on the superior/dorsal aspect of the talonavicular joint (the dorsal lip of the talus).

 - Biomechanical Cause: Because the rigid subtalar joint restricts normal hindfoot inversion and eversion, excessive compensatory sagittal micro-motion and shearing forces are transferred directly to the talonavicular joint. This causes localized periosteal lifting and hypertrophic bone remodeling.

 - Distinction: This is a mechanical adaptation to altered kinematics, not a sign of generalized osteoarthritis.

The "Dysmorphic Sustentaculum Tali" (Oestreich Sign)

 - View: Lateral radiograph.

 - Pathology: The normal sustentaculum tali appears as a crisp, horizontal oblong density on a lateral film. In a middle-facet TC coalition, the sustentaculum becomes hypertrophied, hypoplastic, or down-sloping. It loses its clear horizontal profile, often appearing as an ill-defined, rounded bone mass.

3. Advanced Axial Imaging Signs (CT and MRI)

When plain radiographs are inconclusive but clinical suspicion remains high (e.g., rigid flatfoot with peroneal spasticity), axial and coronal CT or MRI scans provide definitive confirmation.

Coronal Multi-Planar Signs

 - Joint Line Obliquity: On a normal coronal CT slice of the hindfoot, the posterior and middle facets sit relatively parallel to the horizon. In a TC coalition, the middle facet is frequently tilted downward (medial obliquity).

 - Complete Joint Obliteration: In a true bony synostosis, there is a total absence of a joint space between the talus and calcaneus, with continuous trabecular bone bridging the two structures.

 - The "Drifting" or Narrowed Space: In fibrous (syndesmosis) or cartilaginous (synchondrosis) coalitions, the joint line appears narrowed, highly irregular, and is frequently bordered by subchondral sclerosis or micro-cysts on CT. On MRI, a bridge of low signal (fibrous) or intermediate fluid/cartilage signal (cartilaginous) can be directly traced crossing the joint interval.

Answer: 4
