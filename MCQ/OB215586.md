## Question

A 72-year-old male with a history of peripheral vascular disease presents to the ED following a motor vehicle accident. His physical examination suggest injury to the posterior spinal cord. Loss of which of the following on physical examination findings would be expected in posterior cord syndrome?

## Options

1. Fine motor
2. Gross motor
3. Pain
4. Proprioception
5. Temperature

## Correct option

**4. Proprioception**

## Answer explanation

With posterior cord syndrome, there is preservation of temperature, motor, and pain. However, loss of proprioception would be observed.

Posterior cord syndrome, also known as posterior spinal artery syndrome, is characterized by a lesion in the posterior part of the spinal cord. Both anterior cord syndrome and posterior cord syndrome arise from non-traumatic insults to the cord such as an arterial occlusion. However, this may also occur secondary to trauma or cord compression from a tumor. Posterior cord syndrome is a very rare injury. Unlike other incomplete spinal cord injuries, only loss of proprioception would be expected in most cases.

*Maynard et al. reviews the International Standards for Neurological and Functional Classification of Spinal Cord Injury.* *They report on neurological clinical syndromes including central cord, Brown-Séquard, anterior cord, conus medullaris, and cauda equina syndromes.* **They concluded** that a thorough clinical examination is important to aid in the diagnosis of these syndromes.

*McKinley et al. retrospectively compared functional outcomes for individuals with spinal cord injury (SCI) clinical syndromes, including central cord (CCS), Brown-Sequard (BSS), anterior cord (ACS), posterior cord (PCS), cauda equina (CES), and conus medullaris (CMS).* **They report** that CCS was the most common (44.0%), followed by CES (25.1%) and BSS (17.1%). **They concluded** that
patients with ACS and PCS show functional gains with inpatient rehabilitation, with patients with ACS displaying the longest LOS of the SCI syndromes.
