## Question

A 5-year-old boy presents to your office with dorsal foot pain that started about one week ago. His parents are concerned because they have noticed a limp and he has not been as active in school. On exam, he has swelling, redness, and warmth over the dorsum of the midfoot. Figure A is a radiograph of his foot. What is the next best step?

![[q215792-a-kohler-disease-ap.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Antibiotics
2. Surgical excision
3. Non-weight bearing in short leg cast
4. Weight bearing in short leg cast
5. MRI of foot

## Correct option

**4. Weight bearing in short leg cast**

## Answer explanation

This patient's clinical presentation and radiographs are consistent with Kohler's disease, which has limited his daily activities and should be managed in a short-leg walking cast.

Kohler's disease is caused by avascular necrosis of the navicular bone and is seen in young children, most commonly boys. They can either be asymptomatic or present with dorsomedial foot pain associated with swelling, redness, or warmth. Radiographs may demonstrate flattening, sclerosis, or fragmentation of the navicular bone. Management includes anti-inflammatories and the use of shoe modifications with arch supports. Patients with more severe symptoms may benefit from a short-leg walking cast for 4 to 6 weeks. Surgery is not indicated for Kohler's disease.

*DiGiovanni et al. reviewed osteonecrosis in the foot which is most commonly seen in the talus, the first and second metatarsals, and the navicular.* Osteonecrosis is associated with various pathologies in the foot including fracture of the talar neck, navicular, and base of 5th metatarsal as well as Kohler's and Freiberg's diseases. Because osteonecrosis can mimic several other conditions, providers should consider a wide differential and be familiar with their typical presentations and the various treatment options.

*Shastri et al. presented a case report of a young girl who presented to the Emergency Department with foot pain due to Kohler's disease.* *They reviewed the epidemiology, pathophysiology, typical presentation, treatment, and prognosis of Kohler's disease.*

Figure A is an AP of a right foot demonstrating flattening of the navicular bone.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: This patient does not have an infection and therefore antibiotics are not necessary.
Answer 2: Surgical excision is not indicated for Kohler's disease.
Answer 3: Weight-bearing is safe for patients with Kohler's disease
Answer 5: MRI of the foot is not indicated in the initial management of Kohler's disease. If the patient has persistent pain, an MRI can be considered to evaluate for a concomitant tarsal coalition.
