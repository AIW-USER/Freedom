## Question

A 15-year-old male cross-country runner presents for evaluation of his ankle pain. He endorses occasional mechanical symptoms. His exam demonstrates ligamentous stability with a joint effusion. His radiographs are unremarkable. You discuss with the parents and the patient that you suspect an osteochondral defect and would like to order an MRI to further investigate. Which of the following findings on MRI has been associated with the stability of the lesion, and therefore the potential for non-operative management?

## Options

1. Absence of an effusion
2. Open physes
3. Subchondral sclerosis
4. Perilesional marrow edema
5. Homogeneity on T1-weighted images

## Correct option

**2. Open physes**

## Answer explanation

Skeletal immaturity is the one factor that has been correlated with lesion stability. Stable lesions in skeletally immature patients can be treated with a trial of non-operative management.

Osteochondral lesions of the talus (OLTs) are focal injuries to the talar dome with variable involvement of the subchondral bone and cartilage. Posteromedial lesions are typically caused by chronic overuse, while lateral injuries are typically from acute injuries. Treatment varies from nonoperative to operative depending on patient age, activity demands, lesion size, and stability of the lesion.

*Patel et al. investigated the performance of MRI findings to predict the stability of osteochondral lesions of the talus.* Skeletal maturity was the only factor found to correlate with lesion stability. The presence of effusion, intra-articular body, cartilage changes, subchondral disruption, signal intensity rim, cysts, marginal sclerosis, and perilesional marrow edema were not found to correlate with stability.

*Bauer et al. reviewed osteochondritis dissecans lesions of the knee, elbow, and ankle.* **They state** that younger patients are most affected and typically present with vague, intermittent symptoms. Skeletal maturity and stability of the OCD are the main determinants for treatment.

*Rungprai et al. discussed the management of osteochondral lesions of the talar dome.* **They state** that surgery is indicated in chronic cases and acute cases with displaced articular fragments. **They discuss** that larger lesions may require juvenile particulate cartilage, autologous chondrocyte implantation, or osteochondral transplantation.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1, 3, 4, and 5: These MRI findings have not been shown to correlate with lesion stability.
