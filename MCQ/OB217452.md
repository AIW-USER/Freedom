## Question

While investigating the association of fingertip injuries and various occupations, you perform a cross-sectional survey of persons in your city. In your survey, you collect occupation and the number of fingertip injuries they have sustained while at work. The results of your study best establish what?

## Options

1. Incidence
2. Prevalence
3. Hazard ratio
4. Effect size
5. Power

## Correct option

**2. Prevalence**

## Answer explanation

Cross-sectional surveys are best used to establish prevalence.

Understanding statistical definitions is critical for interpreting orthopedic research and practicing evidence-based medicine. Two common statistic definitions are incidence and prevalence. Incidence is the number of newly reported cases in a specific time period per unit of measured population and does not take into account the number of pre-existing cases at the start of the time period used and is best measured with population-based, long-term observational studies (e.g. Framingham Heart Study). Prevalence is similar but provides different information. Prevalence is the total number of active cases within a population at a specific time point and can be measured with a single cross-sectional survey of a population. An example of a cross-sectional survey looking at prevalence would be the United States Census which occurs every 10 years. If you compared one census to another, you could determine the incidence of a particular data point of interest.

*Kocher and Zurakowski published a review that provides a basic understanding of clinical epidemiology and biostatistics.* Within are separate discussions on study design, hypothesis testing, measuring performance of tests, and means of measuring effect within a study. Additionally, they discuss different statistical tests based on if your data is continuous, ordinal, nominal, or survival.

*Spronk et al. published a review on how to appropriately calculate incidence and prevalence.* *They discuss different options for the denominator and numerator of each calculation in order to convey the information you desire accurately.* *They discuss the importance of choosing your numerator and denominator appropriately as it will change the results of your calculations.*

Incorrect:
Answer 1: Incidence is determined by a study that examines a population over multiple time points.
Answer 3: Hazard ratio is a comparison of rates over a study period. An example would be comparing survivorship curves in two groups over time.
Answer 4: Effect size is a measurement of the difference in a result between the control and experimental group.
Answer 5: Power is the estimate a study will be able to detect a true effect of an intervention. This should be done prior to initiating the study of interest.
