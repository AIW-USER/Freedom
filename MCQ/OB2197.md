## Question

A 3-month-old patient with clubfoot who you've been treating with the Ponseti Method has returned to your office. On examination, the foot appears as it does in Figure A. What treatment error has been made?

![[q2197-a-rockerbottom-foot.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Under correction of foot pronation
2. Over corrected metarsus adductus
3. Tried correcting equinus before heel varus
4. Used below knee casts instead of above knee casts
5. Transitioned to the wrong size braces after casting

## Correct option

**3. Tried correcting equinus before heel varus**

## Answer explanation

Rocker bottom foot deformity occurs when equinus is corrected before heel varus and forefoot pronation.

Congenital clubfoot, or talipes equinovarus, consists of four main parts: midfoot cavus, metatarsus adductus, varus hindfoot, and equinus contracture. The goal of manipulation is to rotate the foot around a fixed talus. This must be done in the order described by Ponseti. The mnemonic C-A-V-E is an easy method to remember the order of treatment (Cavus, metatarus Adductus, Varus, Equinus). If equinus is addressed before the cavus and varus hindfoot, dorsiflexion occurs through the midfoot instead of treating the hindfoot equinus, thus creating a rockerbottom foot.

*Ponseti et al. in 1963 published the Ponseti technique of serial manipulations with casting for the treatment of clubfeet.* His original paper had 94 clubfeet with between 5 and 13 years of follow up. In this paper, he describes in detail the deformity and the manipulations required to correct the deformity in a step-wise fashion. The patients averaged 9.5 weeks of casting and 21.5 months of brace wear. Additionally, the treatment methods for re-occurrences were discussed. The original report had 56% initial reoccurences which only required repeat manipulation and casting in most cases.

*Ponseti et al. in 1997 described the most common errors in treatment of clubfoot and how to avoid them.* The three most common errors were pronation of the foot, external rotation of the foot with the calcaneus in varus, and abducting the forefoot through the midtarsal joints. He describes a total of eight errors and how to avoid them.

Figure A demonstrates a rocker bottom deformity of the foot.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: Under-correction of foot pronation can lead to residual cavus deformity
Answer 2: Over correction of metatarsus adductus would lead to an abduction deformity of the forefoot.
Answer 4: Below knee casts are not considered sufficient immobilization to fully take advantage of the manipulations you perform
Answer 5: Improper brace size can lead to increased skin irritation, loss of correction and decreased brace compliance.
