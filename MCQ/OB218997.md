## Question

A 47-year-old female presents with a posterior thigh mass deemed to be a high-grade liposarcoma on biopsy. The tumor is found to be localized without metastatic spread and her treatment plan involves curative-intent wide resection with post-operative adjuvant radiation. In pre-operative planning, the surgeon identifies tumor abutment against the superficial femoral artery (red arrow) and sciatic nerve (yellow arrow; Figure A). After thorough discussions with the patient, the tumor is removed with planned possible positive margins along these two critical structures. The pathologic exam denotes tumor cells within 1mm of the margins adjacent to these critical structures but ultimately no tumor cells invade the stained specimen’s periphery. Which of the following is true?

## Options

1. Marginal status does not affect the risk of local recurrence or survival
2. This type of marginal status would be considered equivalent to a wide resection involving the removal of both critical structures
3. If local tumor recurrence were to occur, it would most likely be over 5 years after surgery
4. Adjuvant radiation therapy would not impart any effect on this patient’s risk for local recurrence
5. The only appropriate resection would have included these two critical structures en-bloc

## Correct option

**2. This type of marginal status would be considered equivalent to a wide resection involving the removal of both critical structures**

## Answer explanation

In the setting of soft tissue sarcomas abutting critical structures, planned close or even microscopically positive margins ONLY along critical structures are appropriate. This resection method can significantly preserve limb function and has been shown to have equivalent local recurrence rates to that of tumors resected with removing the entire critical structures as well (answer 2).

When a soft tissue sarcoma is resected as a large block of tissue, a pathologist will coat the entire removed specimen in an ink stain. They then look at various slices of the specimen under microscopic evaluation to determine if the entire tumor was removed with a peripheral rim of normal tissue (i.e. wide resection with negative margins) or if areas of the tumor were left behind along the stained rim (i.e. wide resection with positive margins). Contemporary classifications generally denote findings simply as negative margins, microscopically positive, or grossly positive margins. Positive margins significantly increase the risk of tumor recurrence in the area and, in many studies, lower survival rates. When a tumor abuts a critical structure necessary for limb function, planned close or microscopically positive margins along the critical structure result in significantly retained limb function and have similar local recurrence and survival outcomes as if the entire critical structure was also removed when radiation is included in their treatment plan. It should be noted this technique involves meticulous operative removal of the perineurium, adventitia, or periosteum of the critical structure with the tumor and requires adjuvant or neo-adjuvant radiation therapy for optimizing oncologic outcomes.

*O’Donnell and colleagues performed a retrospective review comparing their institutional outcomes in various scenarios in which positive margins can occur.* **They found** that the 5-year survival of patients with positive margins along retained critical structures was worse than for patients with close but ultimately negative margins along critical structures (59.4% vs 80.3%, respectively). However, patients who instead underwent resection of the critical structures (due to being encased in the tumor) to ensure a negative margin demonstrated only a nonsignificant, slightly improved 5-year cause-specific survival rate to 63.6% with a similarly nonsignificant improvement in local relapse rates when compared to patients with positive margins along critical structures. **The authors note** that these findings suggest that (1) soft tissue sarcomas CAN directly invade bone periosteum, vessel adventitia, or nerve epineurium and (2) it is the tumor’s biologic aggressiveness to invade these structures that results in a worse overall prognosis rather than the positive margin status along a critical structure. **They conclude** that sparing adjacent critical structures abutting soft tissue sarcomas is safe, appropriate, and contributes to improved functional outcomes.

*Gundle and colleagues performed a retrospective review of their institutional data to determine the most appropriate marginal classification system for soft tissue sarcomas.* **The authors reported** that not all positive margins carried the same risk for local recurrence, however, clinical context can provide additional risk stratification. Specifically, **they found** that a planned close but ultimately positive margin at a critical structure did not significantly increase the risk of local recurrence when compared with negative microscopic margins (p=0.18). **The authors concluded** that the R classification (Illustration A) was the most consistent and best determined the risk of local recurrence over the other commonly utilized classification systems. However, **they note** that it is not a perfect system as it does not account for the clinical context of positive margins.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Marginal status affects the risk of local recurrence of soft tissue sarcomas. In many, but not all studies, positive margins have also been associated with lower survival rates. While different marginal classifications have been described, all have consistently shown that grossly positive margins (i.e. having left some tumor behind) significantly increase the local recurrence rates.
Answer 3: The vast majority of cancer recurrence cases are reported within 5 years after the curative-intent surgical removal. This is why so many studies report 5-year survival rates; if the patient has no local recurrence or metastatic disease found 5 years after treatment, they are most likely cured of the initial cancer and no longer require frequent follow-ups (although annual visits would be prudent).
Answer 4: Adjuvant radiation helps eliminate microscopic tumor cells in the reactive zone along the outer margins of the tumor that may be inadvertently left behind after resection. For this reason, adjuvant radiation should be employed in nearly all cases of soft tissue sarcoma resection, as it has been shown to lower recurrence rates regardless of marginal status.
Answer 5: Soft tissue sarcomas that surround/encase critical structures require wide resection en-bloc (i.e. “all in one piece”) with removal of the critical structure. While this may result in significant functional deficits, a planned close margin cannot be reasonably obtained in tumors that encase critical structures. However, in this setting where the soft tissue sarcoma abuts a critical structure (as shown in this case), a planned close and even possibly positive margin only along the critical structure is technically possible with similar oncologic outcomes as explained above.
