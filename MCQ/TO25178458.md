## Question

A 12-year-old with spastic diplegia (GMFCS II) demonstrates a 'Crouch Gait' with excessive knee flexion and ankle dorsiflexion in stance. Preoperative 3D gait analysis reveals a significantly reduced Plantarflexion-Knee Extension (PFKE) couple. Which anatomical 'lever-arm' dysfunction is the most likely primary cause of this failure to stabilize the tibia?

## Options

1. Isolated spasticity of the rectus femoris during the swing phase.
2. Fixed contracture of the psoas and iliacus muscles causing anterior pelvic tilt.
3. Shortening of the hamstrings relative to the femoral length.
4. Iatrogenic over-lengthening of the Achilles tendon or triceps surae weakness.

## Correct option

**4. Iatrogenic over-lengthening of the Achilles tendon or triceps surae weakness.**

## Answer explanation

Answer : D

An incompetent gastroc-soleus complex
cannot prevent the tibia from falling forward (excessive dorsiflexion), which
forces the knee into flexion to maintain balance.
