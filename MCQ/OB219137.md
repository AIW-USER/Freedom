## Question

A 45-year-old male suffered an acetabular fracture and is in the emergency department. After CT and radiographs, the emergency physician calls you with the radiology read which reports a complete dissociation of the acetabulum from the rest of the pelvis and also notes a "spur sign" on Judet films but reports the sacroiliac joint and posterior wall are intact. Based on this information, what surgical approach would be most indicated to fix this fracture in an acute setting?

## Options

1. Extended Iliofemoral
2. Iliofemoral
3. Ilioinguinal
4. Kocher-Langenback
5. Modifed Gibson

## Correct option

**3. Ilioinguinal**

## Answer explanation

The fracture being described is an associated both column (ABC) fracture of the acetabulum. The standard approach for this fracture is the ilioinguinal approach (Answer 3).

Acetabular fractures are typically classified by the Letournel classification system (Illustration A). Associated both column (ABC) fractures feature an acetabulum that is completely disconnected from the axial skeleton with a split between the posterior and anterior columns. Articular fragments can medialize and remain congruent with the femoral head due to the muscular pull and create a "secondary congruence" appearance (Illustration B). On the obturator oblique view, this medialization creates a unique projection of the most caudal end of the intact ilium, termed the "spur sign" (Illustration C) which is pathognomonic for both column fractures. The ilioinguinal approach is the standard approach for ABC fractures, allowing access to the entire iliac fossa from the pubic symphysis to the sacroiliac joint and the entire anterior column.

*Mauffrey et al. provided a review article on the radiographic evaluation of acetabular fractures and an updated algorithm for diagnosis.* **The authors recommend** a diagnostic algorithm using three plain radiographs (AP and Judet films) to classify acetabular fractures according to Letournel's original classification system. **The authors believe** that this diagnostic algorithm will facilitate clinicians and increase diagnostic accuracy.

*Chen et al. published a retrospective study on the management of posterior wall (PW) fractures in associated both column fractures.* *The authors evaluated differences in radiographic and clinical outcomes when the posterior wall was either reduced and stabilized or left alone.* All acetabular fractures were approached using the ilioinguinal approach or a combined lateral window and anterior intrapelvic approach. **The authors concluded** that reduction and fragment-specific stabilization of the PW fracture did not improve outcomes in ABC fractures.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The extended iliofemoral approach is an extensile approach that is typically not employed as a first choice for acetabular fractures, with the exception of transtectal T-shaped fractures and ABCs with significant posterior wall comminution, SI joint involvement, or delay to surgery of 3 weeks. As the patient in our vignette has no SI or posterior wall involvement and is being treated acutely, an extensile approach such as the extended iliofemoral is not indicated.
Answer 2: The iliofemoral approach is a standard acetabular approach with limited applications. With a plane similar to the Smith-Peterson approach to the hip, the iliofemoral approach extends proximally for access to high anterior column fractures. The iliofemoral approach is not useful for ABC fractures.
Answer 4: The Kocher-Langenback approach addresses the posterior column and posterior wall acetabular fractures by splitting the gluteus maximus fibers. It is not used for both column fractures as it would not address the anterior column fracture sufficiently.
Answer 5: The modified Gibson approach is similar to the Kocher-Langenback with a more vertical proximal incision. This approach utilizes the gluteus maximus and tensor fascia lata interval and is often paired with a trochanteric flip osteotomy. Indications for the modified Gibson include posterior wall or column, selected T-shaped, and selected transverse fractures.
