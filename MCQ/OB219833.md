## Question

A 75-year-old man undergoes total hip arthroplasty for severe right hip osteoarthritis. When planning the acetabular resection, the surgeon takes care to ream down until the fovea is no longer present. The surgeon selects a high offset femoral stem component during trialing. How does increased femoral offset affect the hip joint reactive forces experienced by the implant postoperatively?

## Options

1. Increased joint reactive forces; increased offset shortens the abductor moment arm
2. Increased joint reactive forces; increased offset lengthens the abductor moment arm
3. Decreased joint reactive forces; increased offset shortens the abductor moment arm
4. Decreased joint reactive forces; increased offset lengthens the abductor moment arm
5. No change in joint reactive forces; joint reactive forces are based on hip center position alone

## Correct option

**4. Decreased joint reactive forces; increased offset lengthens the abductor moment arm**

## Answer explanation

Increased femoral offset in total hip arthroplasty lengthens the abductor moment arm and decreases joint reactive forces.

In total hip arthroplasty, component positioning is critical to balance the joint reactive forces of the hip. Joint reactive force is the resultant normal force from the summation of the force imparted by the body weight and abductor moment arms. Both hip center of rotation and femoral offset, respectively, change the length of each moment arm from which these forces act on the hip joint. Body weight is a constant force. As femoral offset increases, the lever arm of the abductor muscles is lengthened, and thus, the exertional force required by the abductors to counter bodyweight torque decreases. As the hip center is medialized, the lever arm of body weight shortens, and thus, the necessary torque from the abductors to counter body weight decreases. In short, joint reactive forces are decreased by medializing the hip center or increasing femoral offset.

*Lim et al. review the biomechanics of total hip arthroplasty, including available literature regarding in vivo and in vitro testing of joint reactive forces.* *They review the forces encountered in the hip through various activities, including walking, jogging, and load bearing, as well as the effects of hip geometry.* **They note** that medialization of the acetabular cup usually reduces joint reactive forces by maximizing hip abductor moment-generating capacity through effective lateralization of the greater trochanter.

*Bowman et al. review native hip biomechanics.* **They note** that there is a residual compressive force acting across the joint at all times, with an average magnitude approximately equal to the body weight, as a result of the need to balance the moment arms of the body weight with the pull of the hip abductors at the greater trochanter to maintain a level pelvis. During rapid hip motion, the vector of force on the hip joint fluctuates, with forces as high as four times body weight during level walking and stair ascent.

Illustration A is a free-body diagram that demonstrates the resultant vectors of the hip joint reactive force. In this case, A increases as the surgeon implants a high offset stem, which in turn decreases the required M force to balance the hip. This decreases the overall joint reactive force (R).

Incorrect answers:
Answers 1, 2: Increased femoral offset decreases joint reactive forces.
Answer 3: Increased femoral offset lengthens the abductor moment arm.
Answer 5: Joint reactive forces are affected by both hip center position and femoral offset.
