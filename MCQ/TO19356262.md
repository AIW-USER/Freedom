## Question

Which test is best described by the diagram given below? [FNB 2023]

![[TO19356262-1.png]]

## Options

1. Neer’s impingement test
2. Empty can sign
3. Hawkins-Kennedy test
4. External rotation lag sign

## Correct option

**3. Hawkins-Kennedy test**

## Answer explanation

Answer: 3

 Tests (pictorial description) for shoulder joint examination :
Test for shoulder impingement:
Neer’s impingement sign: Examiner passively raises the arm in forward flexion while stabilizing the scapula (to prevent scapulothoracic movement). This causes pain in the shoulder anteriorly.

![[TO19356262-2.png]]

Hawkins-Kennedy test: Forward flexion of shoulder to 90° with forcible internal rotation reproduces the impingement pain. This test is sensitive, but not very specific.

![[TO19356262-3.png]]

Scapular assistance test: Examiner pushes the inferior medial scapular angle during shoulder elevation to assist scapular upward rotation and posterior tilt. A positive result is indicated by relief of painful symptoms related to the subacromial impingement and increased arc of motion.

![[TO19356262-4.png]]

Tests for Glenohumeral Instability:
For Anterior Instability
 Jobe’s Apprehension test: In supine position the patient's arm is abducted to 90 degrees and externally rotated to behind the coronal plane (elbow flexed to 90 degrees). The patient becomes apprehensive and resists the examiner.

![[TO19356262-5.png]]

Anterior drawer test: The patient is placed supine and the arm is abducted 60°. Axial force is applied to the humerus with the arm in neutral rotation. Now the examiner translates the humeral head anteriorly. Translation of the head to the glenoid rim is grade I, translation over the rim that spontaneously reduces is grade II, and dislocation without spontaneous reduction is grade III.

![[TO19356262-6.png]]

For Posterior Instability
Jerk test/Jahnke test: This can be performed with the patient standing or sitting. The examiner holds patient’s elbow with one hand and stabilizes the scapula with the other hand. The shoulder is flexed to 90°, internally rotated and adducted, and an axial load is applied to the elbow. The shoulder may subluxate or dislocate posteriorly with a sudden jerk. Now the arm is gradually abducted, the humeral head may reduce back onto the glenoid.

![[TO19356262-7.png]]

For Inferior Instability
Sulcus sign: The arm is pulled distally in 0 degree and 45 degrees of abduction. The shoulder is observed for a sulcus or dimple between the acromion and the humeral head.

![[TO19356262-8.png]]

Test for rotator cuff muscles:
For Subscapularis
Belly-press test (Gerber)/Napoleon sign: Patient is asked to place the hand on the belly (with elbow in front of the trunk) and press the abdomen or apply pressure against the examiner’s hand. If subscapularis is normal, the patient presses the abdomen by hand by internal rotation of the shoulder and elbow remains in front of the trunk. If the subscapularis is weak, then internal rotation cannot be maintained and the patient presses the abdomen by extending the shoulder and dropping the elbow behind the trunk (Belly press test) or by flexing the wrist (Napoleon sign, named after the peculiar posture in which Napoleon is depicted in many photographs).

![[TO19356262-9.png]]

Lift-off test (Gerber): Patient’s arm is internally rotated with the dorsum of the hand touching the back. If the patient is able to lift his hand off the back, subscapularis function can be presumed to be normal.

![[TO19356262-10.png]]

Bear hug test (Burkhart and De Beer): Both the earlier tests fail to recognize subscapularis tears when less than 50% of the muscle may be torn (especially when only the upper third fibers are torn). In such situations more sensitive is the Bear Hug test. Patient places the hand on the contralateral shoulder. Examiner supports the elbow in front of the body. Now the examiner tries to raise the hand off the shoulder and the patient resists it. A positive test is indicated by failure of the patient to resist the examiner.

![[TO19356262-11.png]]

For Supraspinatous Jobe’s empty can sign: Elevate the shoulder to 90° abduction in scapular plane (30° forward flexion from coronal plane of body) and internally rotate so that the thumb points toward the floor (as if emptying a can). Now ask the patient to elevate his arm against a resistance. Weakness suggests a tear of supraspinatus.

![[TO19356262-12.png]]

Drop arm test (Codman): Patient’s arm is elevated overhead and then he is asked to move it down slowly in reverse motion. Test is considered positive if there is sudden onset acute pain or if the patient is unable to smoothly control the lowering motion.

![[TO19356262-13.png]]

 For Infraspinatus and Teres Minor
External rotation resistance stress test: The test is performed to check the integrity of external rotators (infraspinatus and teres minor). Patient keeps his arms by his side in 0° abduction-flexion with elbows bent to 90°. Now the shoulders are externally rotated to 60°. Next, the examiner tries to internally rotate the shoulders by applying pressure on the dorsum of the patient's hands which the patient is asked to resist. Pain or weakness suggests insufficiency of external rotators.

![[TO19356262-14.png]]

External rotation lag sign (ERLS,): This tests the integrity of infraspinatus mainly. To elicit the ERLS, the patient is seated on a chair with elbows bent to 90°. The examiner elevates the patient’s shoulder by 20° in the scapular plane and rotates them externally to full extent. The patient is asked to maintain this position. The test is positive when angular drop occurs.

![[TO19356262-15.png]]

Hornblower sign (Patte,): This sign predicts more accurately the integrity of teres minor. With elbows bent to 90°, the patient's arm is elevated to 90° in the scapular plane. The patient is then asked to laterally rotate the arm. Weakness or pain on trying to do so suggests insufficiency of teres minor. Patient assumes a position of hornblower’s sign.

![[TO19356262-16.png]]

![[TO19356262-17.png]]

Tests for SLAP tear
O’brian test:
The arm to be tested at 90° of flexion and about 10° of adduction and in full internal rotation, pointing the thumb to the ground. The patient is now asked to resist downward force by examining the distal forearm. The test is then repeated but with the arm in external rotation. Pain in the shoulder in internal rotation, but no pain in external rotation indicates a SLAP tear

![[TO19356262-18.png]]

Modified dynamic labral shift test: In a standing patient the shoulder is abducted to 120°, arm kept in the scapular plane, elbow flexed to 90°, and their shoulder externally rotated. The examiner then lowers the arm while maintaining the external rotation. A positive test is indicated by a painful clicking at the posterior joint line when the arm is lowered between 120° and 90°.

![[TO19356262-19.png]]

Upper cut test: With the shoulder in neutral rotation, elbow flexed to 90° and the forearm supinated and hand in a fist, the patient rapidly brings the fist toward the chin (as in a upper cut punch) against examiner’s resistance. Pain in the shoulder indicates a SLAP tear
