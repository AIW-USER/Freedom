## Question

A 65-year-old male presents to the office for evaluation of a painful right total knee arthroplasty. He reports having the index surgery approximately 3 years ago in a different state. He reports knee pain that is worse with activity, but also present at rest. On exam, he does have a palpable effusion. You elect to aspirate the right knee which returns 50cc of cloudy synovial fluid. You send the fluid for alpha-defensin immunoassay. Which of the following is true of this study?

## Options

1. Specific but not sensitive for infection
2. Sensitive but not specific for infection
3. Alpha-defensin is released primarily by macrophages
4. Alpha-defensin is released primarily by neutrophils
5. Alpha-defensin is released primarily by monocytes

## Correct option

**4. Alpha-defensin is released primarily by neutrophils**

## Answer explanation

Alpha defensin is an antimicrobial peptide that is secreted by apoptotic human neutrophils in the setting of a microbial infection.

The diagnosis of prosthetic joint infection (PJI) can be challenging and the clinical picture may sometimes be unclear. Recently, interest in synovial fluid biomarkers have been investigated to help improve our diagnostic accuracy as it relates to PJI. The most promising synovial fluid biomarker in terms of sensitivity and specificity for PJI has been alpha-defensin, which has been found to be as high as 100% sensitive and 96% specific in some studies. This marker is released specifically by human neutrophils in response to the presence of microbial pathogens. Alpha-defensin is active against Gram-positive and Gram-negative bacteria, fungi and enveloped viruses. While the mechanism of action is not fully understood, it is thought to integrates into the pathogen’s cell membrane leading to disruption and eventual lysis.

*Wyatt et al. review the alpha-defensin immunoassay and leukocyte esterase colorimetric strip testing as it relates to PJI.* **They report** 100% sensitivity and 96% specificity of the alpha defense test for the diagnosis of PJI. The leukocyte esterase colorimetric strip test showed a specificity of 97%. **They conclude** that the diagnostic accuracy of both tests is high, but the cost differential between the two tests warrants further research.

*Deirmengian et al. evaluated the alpha-defensin test for the diagnosis of PJI.* **They report** that the synovial fluid alpha-defensin immunoassay correctly predicted the MSIS classification of all patients in the study, demonstrating a sensitivity and specificity of 100% for the diagnosis of PJI. **They conclude** that the data provided from this test make it a very useful tool for the diagnosis of PJI.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1 and 2: The alpha defensin test is both sensitive and specific for the diagnosis of PJI.
Answer 3 and 5: Alpha defensin is a peptide released directly from human neutrophils.
