## Question

A 23-year-old male presents after a motor vehicle collision rollover. A shard of glass punctured his medial elbow, causing him to sustain a complete ulnar nerve transection. The hand surgeon on-call primarily repairs the nerve and performs an anterior interosseous nerve (AIN) to ulnar motor fascicle supercharged end-to-side (SETS) procedure. Which of the following is the purpose of the SETS procedure?

## Options

1. Regain sensation along the small finger and ulnar aspect of the ring finger
2. Regain sensation along the volar thumb and index finger
3. Maintain ability to perform finger abduction and adduction
4. Prevent the loss of active wrist extension
5. Maintain patient’s ability to perform thumb opposition

## Correct option

**3. Maintain ability to perform finger abduction and adduction**

## Answer explanation

The AIN to motor fascicle of the ulnar nerve SETS procedure is utilized to maintain/improve the ulnar-innervated intrinsic musculature of the hand in this setting. Finger abduction/adduction is performed by the dorsal and palmar interosseous muscles, which are innervated by the ulnar nerve distal to the site of injury.

Ulnar nerve injuries are broadly divided into “high” or “low” nerve injuries, distinguished as being proximal or distal, respectfully, to the motor branches of the flexor carpi ulnaris and flexor digitorum profundus to the ring and little fingers (i.e. above and below the elbow). Functional recovery after injury is predicated based on the time required for regenerating motor neurons to reach the motor endplates in the hand before the terminally innervated muscles atrophy beyond a recoverable state. In acute nerve injuries where neurorrhaphy (repair) is performed, the anterior interosseous nerve (AIN) can be transferred to the motor branch of the ulnar nerve in an end-to-side manner. This preserves the terminal endplates until the repaired ulnar nerve has time to recover, thus preventing irreparable intrinsic hand atrophy.

*Woo and colleagues reviewed the overall management of ulnar nerve injuries.* *They provide an exhaustive anatomical review of the ulnar nerve course and expected functional deficits dependent on the site of injury.* **They conclude** that shorter time from injury, younger age, and shorter recovery distance all favor better outcomes with improvements shown as late as 5 years after nerve repair.

*Barbour and colleagues described their technique for performing the SETS procedure in order to maintain or “babysit” intrinsic musculature innervation in the setting of ulnar motor nerve injuries.* *They provide a step-by-step guide focusing on how to perform internal neurolysis to separate and identify the motor branch of the ulnar nerve as the recipient for AIN end-to-side coaptation.* **They conclude** with pearls and pitfalls including the utilization of intraoperative electric stimulation, utilizing a wide perineurial window, and avoiding tension at the coaptation site.

Susan Mackinnon, one of the major early pioneers for peripheral nerve transfers, provided an all-encompassing review of these procedures and technical considerations. She discusses the importance of nerve topography, donor nerve synergism to recipient sites, and surgical technicalities. She concludes that these all play a role in patient outcomes and that surgical treatment should be tailored to each individual patient’s needs.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1 and 2: The SETS procedure involves coaptation from the donor terminal AIN to the recipient motor fascicle of the ulnar nerve. These are both purely motor branches that lack sensory fibers. While some sensations may recover from the primary nerve repair, there is no expected sensory recovery from the SETS procedure.
Answer 4: The radial nerve innervates the extensor muscles of the wrist. This patient sustained an ulnar nerve injury, which does not play a role in performing active wrist extension.
Answer 5: Thumb opposition is primarily performed via the opponens pollicis muscle which is innervated by the recurrent motor branch of the median nerve.
