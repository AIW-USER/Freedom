## Question

A 43-year-old female presents with foot pain and the flexible deformity depicted in Figure A. Which of the following features of her deformity does the procedure depicted in Figure B most effectively correct?

![[q211583-b-screen-shot-2019-12-28-at-3-48-13-pm.jpg]]

## Options

1. Hindfoot valgus
2. Hindfoot varus
3. Forefoot adduction
4. Forefoot abduction
5. Cavus

## Correct option

**4. Forefoot abduction**

## Answer explanation

This patient appears to have stage 2B posterior tibial tendon insufficiency (PTTI), exhibited by "too many toes" sign when the foot is viewed posteriorly. The procedure depicted in Figure B is the lateral column lengthening procedure (Evans procedure), which aims to correct the forefoot abduction feature of her deformity.

PTTI results in adult-acquired flatfoot deformity and is the result of combined forefoot abduction and hindfoot valgus. In stage 2 PTTI, patients are unable to perform single heel raises and the deformity correction is achieved through a combination of soft tissue and bony procedures. A common combination of procedures for stage II disease includes a transfer of the flexor digitorum longus (FDL), tendoachilles lengthening (TAL), medial displacement calcaneal osteotomy, and lateral column lengthening (through the calcaneocuboid joint or the anterior calcaneus). Lateral column lengthening is typically added if there is >40% talar head under coverage as FDL transfer and medial calcaneal osteotomy alone will not correct severe forefoot abduction.

*Chan et al. evaluated the contribution of lateral column lengthening to the correction of forefoot Abduction in stage IIb PTTI.* **They reported** that the variables that significantly affected the change in lateral incongruency angle were patient weight and the amount of the lengthening performed, with multivariate regression analysis demonstrating the amount of lengthening as the only significant predictor. **They concluded** that the lateral incongruency angle is a useful preoperative measurement tool to help surgeons predict the proper amount of correction to be performed intraoperatively.

*Benthien et al. investigated the use of lateral column calcaneal lengthening, flexor digitorum longus transfer, and opening wedge medial cuneiform osteotomy for flexible flatfoot in a cadaver study.* **They reported** that lateral column lengthening with simulated flexor digitorum longus transfer on a severe flatfoot model resulted in a significant decrease in lateral talus-first metatarsal angle, talonavicular angle, and medial cuneiform height, and an increase in lateral forefoot pressure. **They concluded** that the lateral column lengthening procedure results in radiographic improvement and an increase of lateral column pressures in severe flexible flatfeet.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The medial displacement calcaneal osteotomy would correct hindfoot valgus.
Answer 2: Hindfoot varus is not a feature of PTTI.
Answer 3: Forefoot adduction is not a feature of PTTI.
Answer 5: Cavus is not a feature of PTTI.
