## Question

A 67-year-old female follows up in clinic to discuss surgical management of her long-standing right ankle pain. She has a history of rheumatoid arthritis (RA), hypertension, and hyperlipidemia. BMI is 24. Medications include meloxicam and lisinopril. She is employed as a secretary and walks 1-2 miles daily for exercise. On exam, she has pain throughout ankle range of motion. There is no pain with hindfoot inversion or eversion. Figures A and B are current weight-bearing radiographs. At this time, she wishes to discuss total ankle arthroplasty (TAA) for her ankle pain. Which of the following is true regarding TAA compared to ankle arthrodesis (AA) ?

## Options

1. TAA is contraindicated in patients with RA
2. TAA patients have superior gait mechanics compared to AA post-operatively
3. TAA is indicated in young patients who are laborers
4. TAA is associated with higher rates of subtalar arthritis post-operatively
5. TAA is indicated in patients with Charcot arthropathy

## Correct option

**2. TAA patients have superior gait mechanics compared to AA post-operatively**

## Answer explanation

This patient has end-stage tibiotalar arthritis, a history of RA, and a low-physical demand on the ankle. Total ankle arthroplasty (TAA) may provide improved ankle function post-op as TAA has demonstrated superior gait mechanics compared to AA.

Without any history of trauma, rheumatoid arthritis (RA) is likely the primary cause of this patient's worsening ankle arthritis. Surgical options for end-stage ankle arthritis secondary to RA include TAA and ankle arthrodesis. TAA is preferred for patients in which maximal physiologic motion of the ankle joint is desired. Additionally, TAA has demonstrated superior gait mechanics, including increased gait speed and stride length, compared to AA. AA could negatively impact overall function in this patient.

*Krouse et al. examined the decision making that must occur in deciding whether AA or TAA is indicated for the treatment of end-stage ankle arthritis.* *They described age, cause of arthritis, deformity or ligamentous laxity, ankle ROM, and the presence of adjacent joint arthritis as major criteria to consider when deciding which surgery is appropriate.* **They conclude** that patients with end-stage ankle arthritis due to RA have been shown to benefit from TAA.

*Raikin et al. compared the demographic, geographic, and financial trends in treatment of ankle arthritis between TAA and AA.* **They found** female patients and older patients were more likely to undergo TAA whereas AA was more common in patients who were diabetic or obese. **They concluded** that while utilization of TAA was rising, AA was performed 6 times as often as TAA.

Figures A and B are AP and lateral weight-bearing ankle x-rays demonstrating end-stage ankle arthritis with loss of tibiotalar joint space and osteophyte formation.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: TAA is indicated for treatment of inflammatory tibiotalar arthropathy, such as RA.
Answers 3 & 5: TAA is contraindicated in patients who are young laborers or with Charcot arthropathy.
Answer 4: AA is associated with higher rates of subtalar arthritis post-op. One study demonstrated a subtalar arthritis rate of 50% at 10 years post-op.
