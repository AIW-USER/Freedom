## Question

The newborn nursery requests consultation for a baby with a possible left hip dislocation. On physical exam, the shoulders are adducted, arms internally rotated, and elbows extended. There are no flexion creases. The lower extremity exam is consistent with unilateral hip dislocation and a stiff rocker bottom foot. A Pavlik harness is applied for four weeks. What is the most likely outcome at the baby’s 1-month office visit?

## Options

1. Reduced, alpha angle of 65 degrees and Beta angle of 50 degrees on ultrasound
2. Hypertrophy of the posterior wall of the acetabulum
3. Complete occlusion of the anterior retinacular branch of the medial femoral circumflex artery
4. Abnormal alpha angle of 45 degrees and normal beta angle of 50 degrees on ultrasound
5. Abnormal alpha angle of 40 degrees and beta angle unmeasurable on ultrasound

## Correct option

**5. Abnormal alpha angle of 40 degrees and beta angle unmeasurable on ultrasound**

## Answer explanation

This patient has an arthrogrypotic phenotype—the use of a Pavlik harness in this tetralogic hip dislocation is not successful in achieving hip reduction and thus the follow up ultrasound would show a persistent dislocation.

Arthrogryposis is a phenotypic descriptor applied to patients with underlying conditions causing multiple joint contractures. Patients with an arthrogrypotic phenotype may present with flexed, abducted, and externally rotated hips, clubfeet, and congenital vertical talus, long scoliosis curve and normal intelligence. For patients with hip dislocation, treatment choice can be controversial (i.e. need for reduction in bilateral dislocations, use of the anteromedial versus anterolateral approach). However, both closed reduction and Pavlik harness/semi-rigid abduction orthosis are not successful and thus not indicated in this setting.

*Szoke et al., report on 40 hip dislocations in 26 children with amyoplasia-type arthrogryposis multiplex congenita.* **They found** that in 25 patients treated with medial open reduction, there was an 80% rate of good clinical outcomes. **They conclude** that medial-approach open reduction may be a successful option for infants with amyoplasia-type arthrogryposis multiplex congenita.

*Yau et al. report on the outcomes of 38 hips in patients with an arthrogrypotic phenotype.* **They report** that closed treatment failed in all patients and that openly reduced patients had long term hip stiffness. Despite hip stiffness in the openly treated group, the long-term hip function was not impaired.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: In a one month old infant, an alpha angle of 65 degrees and a beta angle of 50 degrees would be normal
Answer 2: use of a Pavlik harness in a dislocated hip prevents the development of the posterior acetabular wall
Answer 3: occlusion of the posterosuperior retinacular branch of the medial femoral circumflex artery is seen in patients with excess abduction (>60 degrees) applied to their Pavlik harness
Answer 4: While the alpha angle is abnormal, a dislocated patient at one month would have a beta angle that is unmeasurable. A subluxated hip would have a beta angle that is > 77 degrees.
