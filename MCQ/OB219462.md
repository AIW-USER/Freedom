## Question

A 79-year old femur sustains repeat falls two weeks after total knee arthroplasty was performed. Radiographs (Image A and B) demonstrate a distal femur periprosthetic fracture. In counseling her regarding her treatment options, what is one important outcome difference when comparing intramedullary nail fixation, open reduction internal fixation, and distal femoral replacement?

## Options

1. Open reduction internal fixation has a higher rate of complications
2. Distal femoral replacement has better outcome scores
3. Intramedullary nail fixation has a higher rate of malunion
4. Distal femoral replacement has a higher rate of reoperation
5. Distal femoral replacement has greater postoperative knee range of motion

## Correct option

**3. Intramedullary nail fixation has a higher rate of malunion**

## Answer explanation

Intramedullary nail (IMN) fixation, open reduction internal fixation (ORIF), and distal femoral replacement (DFR) are each valid surgical techniques in the treatment of distal femur periprosthetic fractures and largely have equivalent outcomes; however, IMN fixation is associated with a higher rate of malunion.

Periprosthetic distal femur fractures are becoming more common as we continue to perform more TKAs on an aging population. These injuries are most commonly classified utilizing the Lewis and Rorabeck or Su classifications. Treatment largely depends on fracture location, implant stability, and host bone stock. Fractures occurring proximal to a stable prosthesis can be treated with either retrograde IMN or ORIF depending on whether or not the femoral component will accommodate an intramedullary nail. Fractures occurring distal to the anterior flange can be treated with ORIF or DFR depending on the stability of the implant and the amount of host bone available for fixation.

*Wadhwa et al. performed a systematic review (SR) and meta-analysis (MA) comparing DFR and ORIF for the treatment of distal femur periprosthetic fractures.* Included studies were mostly Level IV and Level III. **They found** similar Knee Society Scores, return to preop ambulatory status, and reoperation, but range of motion (ROM) was greater in the ORIF group. **They concluded** that outcomes were similar between groups, but that additional high-level studies were required to better study the treatment of distal femur periprosthetic fractures.

*Quinzi et al. also performed an SA and MA of distal femur periprosthetic fractures.* *They differed from the above Wadhwa study in that they included IMN in their comparison with ORIF and DFR.* Major findings included a higher rate of deep infection in the DFR group, and malunion was more prevalent with IMN. **They concluded** that most outcomes were similar with each treatment for distal femur periprosthetic fractures.

Figures A and B are AP and Lateral plain radiographs that demonstrate a periprosthetic distal femur fracture with prior press-fit TKA in place.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The rate of complications is similar between IMN, ORIF, and DFR.
Answer 2: Outcome scores are similar between IMN, ORIF, and DFR.
Answer 4: Reoperation rates are similar between IMN, ORIF, and DFR.
Answer 5: When comparing ORIF to DFR, ORIF has actually been found to have greater knee ROM.
