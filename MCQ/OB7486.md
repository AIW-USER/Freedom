## Options

1. Impingement syndrome
2. Adhesive capsulitis
3. Partial-thickness rotator cuff tear
4. Full-thickness rotator cuff tear
5. Calcific tendinitis

## Correct option

**5. Calcific tendinitis**

## Answer explanation

Calcific tendinitis of the rotator cuff is a common disorder of unknown etiology. It typically affects women more often than men, and usually involves the supraspinatus and/or the infraspinatus. The radiograph shows the typical calcific deposition. MRI sequences show the typical globular area of low signal intensity abnormality located in the supraspinatus tendon. There is no significant acromial spurring to indicate impingement syndrome, and no evidence of partial- or full-thickness rotator cuff tearing. Adhesive capsulitis is a clinical diagnosis that has no consistent radiographic findings.
