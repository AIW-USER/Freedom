## Question

You are consulted to evaluate a patient who sustained a left acetabulum fracture after a fall. Upon patient evaluation, review of the available imaging, and subsequent consideration of treatment options, in which of the following scenarios would combined open reduction internal fixation with total hip arthroplasty be the most appropriate treatment option?

## Options

1. 45-year-old female, displaced both-column fracture
2. 45-year-old male, displaced fracture involving 40% of the posterior wall
3. 70-year-old female, marginal impaction of the acetabulum
4. 70-year-old male, both-column fracture with secondary congruence
5. 85-year-old female, nondisplaced fracture involving 15% of the posterior wall

## Correct option

**3. 70-year-old female, marginal impaction of the acetabulum**

## Answer explanation

There are multiple treatment options for acetabulum fractures in geriatric patients. Combined open reduction internal fixation (ORIF) with total hip arthroplasty (THA) is an effective strategy in the presence of marginal impaction, posterior wall comminution, associated femoral head fracture, superomedial dome impaction, pre-existing arthritis, and hip dislocation.

The treatment of acetabulum fractures in geriatric patients is a complex clinical scenario that must take into account both patient and injury factors. Options include nonoperative management, ORIF, percutaneous reduction and fixation, and combined fixation with acute or delayed THA. In the presence of the above-listed fracture characteristics, combined ORIF with THA allows for early weightbearing, a more rapid recovery, and has demonstrated excellent results in the literature. Both-column fracture patterns with secondary congruence (all fracture fragments dissociated from the intact ilium allowing for congruent reduction around the femoral head) are indicated for nonoperative management in select patients.

*Butterwick et al. reviewed the evaluation and management of acetabular fractures in elderly patients and* **identified** poor prognostic factors for isolated ORIF compared to combined ORIF with THA. These included the presence of marginal impaction of the acetabulum, posterior wall comminution, associated femoral head impaction or fracture, the presence of a gull sign on the AP pelvis x-ray indicating superomedial dome impaction and quadrilateral plate displacement, pre-existing arthritis, and hip dislocation. The authors also discussed the potential efficacy of nonoperative management in the presence of a both-column fracture pattern with secondary congruence. **They concluded** that patients with poor prognostic factors can be effectively treated with combined ORIF and THA.

*Rickman et al. published a retrospective series of 24 patients aged 65 years or older who sustained acetabulum fractures with significant marginal impaction or femoral head damage and were treated with combined ORIF with THA.* *All patients tolerated full weightbearing within seven days postoperatively, and all fractures demonstrated radiographic healing by six months.* **The authors concluded** that combined ORIF with THA in select patients is an effective strategy that can allow for immediate weightbearing.

*Antell et al. reviewed the management of acetabular fractures in elderly patients and discussed the efficacy of various treatment strategies.* **They noted** that patients with secondary congruency on initial imaging can be managed nonoperatively, often having minimal pain and high functional outcome scores with low complication rates at the time of union. Percutaneous skeletal fixation can be utilized in the setting of nondisplaced or minimally displaced fracture patterns, with the benefit of avoiding the increased blood loss and physiologic stresses associated with more extensive open procedures. In comparing isolated ORIF to combined ORIF with THA, they discussed the need for a critical evaluation of the bone quality and fracture pattern to determine whether successful reduction can be maintained with isolated ORIF. **They concluded** that combined ORIF with THA is an ideal option in geriatric acetabulum fractures with underlying arthritis, or when there is a high risk for early failure or posttraumatic arthritis.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1 and 2: Combined ORIF with THA is not a commonly utilized strategy in younger patients with fracture patterns requiring surgical intervention.
Answer 4: Both-column fractures with secondary congruency in geriatric patients are often amenable to nonoperative management.
Answer 5: Combined ORIF with THA is typically not necessary in the setting of nondisplaced fractures involving less than 20% of the posterior wall in geriatric patients.
