## Question

A 53-year-old female sustains a humeral diaphyseal fracture while skiing. She is treated non-operatively in a Sarmiento brace. Mobility at the fracture site without radiographic callus formation 6 weeks after injury has what specificity for the development of a nonunion?

## Options

1. 15%
2. 45%
3. 65%
4. 95%
5. 99%

## Correct option

**5. 99%**

## Answer explanation

Fracture site mobility 6 weeks after a humeral shaft fracture has a 99% specificity for the development of a humeral shaft nonunion.

Humeral shaft nonunion is characterized by the arrest of the fracture repair process of a humeral shaft fracture which may occur following nonoperative or operative management. Diagnosis can be made with plain radiographs. CT studies are helpful to assess the extent of bridging callous and for preoperative planning. Treatment is generally open reduction with compression plating with or without bone grafting.

*Cadet et al. reviewed humeral fracture nonunion.* **They reported** that surgical management should be considered for fractures that demonstrate no evidence of progressive healing on consecutive radiographs taken at least 6 to 8 weeks apart during the course of closed treatment. **They concluded** that for humeral shaft nonunions, ORIF with compression plating and bone graft remains the standard of care, with a >90% rate of union and good functional outcomes.

*Driesman et al. reviewed 84 patients that were being treated nonoperatively for a diaphyseal humeral shaft fracture.* **They reported** that fracture site mobility at 6 weeks follow-up identified fracture healing with 82% sensitivity and a 99% specificity. **They also noted** a positive predictive value (PPV) and negative predictive value (NPV) of 90% and 97%, respectively. When patients had both callus formation and no motion at 6 weeks, all the patients proceeded to union (sensitivity 83.6%, specificity 100%, and positive predictive value 100%); those that had both an absence of callus formation and motion at 6 weeks, all proceeded to nonunion (sensitivity 63.6%, specificity 100%, and positive predictive value 100%). **They concluded** that clinical examination of fracture motion at 6 weeks carries an increased risk of nonunion progression.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1-4: Fracture site mobility at 6 weeks after injury has an 82% sensitivity, a 99% specificity, a 90% PPV, and 97% NPV for the development of a humeral shaft nonunion
