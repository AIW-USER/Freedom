## Question

A healthy 2-week-old male presents to your office with bowing of his lower extremity, as shown in Figure A. The patient was born at term without a complicated delivery. Mother notes this deformity has not changed since birth, despite attempting to straighten his leg. What other physical exam finding is most likely present in this patient?

![[q220984-a-screenshot-2026-01-26-at-4-32-57-pm.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Lengthening of the ipsilateral tibia and fibula
2. Cafe-au-lait spots
3. Calcaneovalgus foot deformity
4. Entire absence of fibula on the contralateral side
5. Congenital talipes equinovarus foot deformity

## Correct option

**3. Calcaneovalgus foot deformity**

## Answer explanation

This patient has posteromedial tibial bowing as depicted in Figure A. This is most likely associated with a calcaneovalgus foot deformity (answer 3).

Posteromedial tibial bowing is a congenital condition thought to be the result of intrauterine positioning. This typically results in an ipsilateral leg length discrepancy due to the bowing and a calcaneovalgus foot deformity. This tibial bowing commonly occurs in the middle and distal thirds of the tibia without a known genetic predisposition. Diagnosis is made clinically and radiographically. Treatment consists of observation, which typically spontaneously resolves over 5-7 years. If the leg length discrepancy continues to persist and becomes severe, operative intervention may be indicated.

*Shah et al. performed a retrospective study of 20 children with congenital posteromedial bowing of the tibia (CPMBT).* **They noted** children presented with deformities up to 70 degrees, with natural correction occurring secondary to physeal realignment and diaphyseal remodeling. The fastest time to remodel was during the first year of life, while some bowing persisted beyond 4 years. **They note** that deformities often improve spontaneously in early childhood, but residual bowing, ankle deformities, muscle weakness, and limb length discrepancies may persist. **They conclude** by recommending regular follow-up through skeletal maturity to monitor for these persistent problems and even pursue surgical intervention if necessary.

*Gordon et al. performed a retrospective case series of 16 children with CPMBT who underwent limb lengthening to surgically address significant limb length discrepancies.* *The average LLD was 7.7 cm in this study, with treatment consisting of a single-stage circular external fixator in 13 children and a two-stage early lengthening plus deformity correction with later definitive lengthening in 3 children.* *At final follow-up, all patients were pain-free and walked without a limp, and the mean final LLD was 0.3 cm with angular correction.* *However, 50% of patients developed residual symptomatic angle valgus requiring a hemiepiphyseodesis in 7 patients and an osteotomy in 1 patient.* **They conclude** that limb lengthening is effective for children with an LLD of 5 cm or more, and that it is critical to monitor residual ankle valgus in these patients, as this may require additional surgical correction.

*Sagade et al. performed a level IV therapeutic retrospective case series of 23 children with CPMBT undergoing 28 tibial lengthening procedures (14 per group) by a single surgeon.* *They grouped patients into 2 equal groups (A: 5 years and younger vs B: >5 years old) with an average follow-up of 7.9 years, and all procedures used circular external fixators.* **They found** that projected LLD at maturity using growth multipliers was higher than previously reported (mean 7.2 cm in Group A and 5 cm in Group B). Both external fixator duration and index were significantly lower in younger children, and younger children had fewer complications. **They conclude** that early lengthening in children with CPMBT aged 5 years and younger is safe, with shorter external fixator time and fewer complications compared to older children.

Figure A is an AP and lateral radiograph of the tibia and fibula demonstrating posterior and medial tibial bowing.

Incorrect answer choices:
Answer 1: Lengthening of the ipsilateral tibia and fibula is the opposite of what would be expected in posteromedial tibial bowing; typically, there is ipsilateral limb shortening.
Answer 2: Cafe-au-lait spots would be found in anterolateral tibial bowing, a more concerning condition.
Answer 4: Anteromedial tibial bowing is more associated with fibular hemimelia, not posteromedial tibial bowing.
Answer 5: Clubfoot is not associated with posteromedial tibial bowing.
