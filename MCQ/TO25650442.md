## Question

Which of the following statements accurately compares the properties of Polyglycolic acid (PGA) and Poly-L-lactic acid (PLLA) when used as biodegradable orthopedic implants?

## Options

1. Both PGA and PLLA maintain their maximum mechanical strength for exactly 12 months before initiating rapid degradation.
2. PGA exhibits higher initial mechanical strength but loses its structural integrity and degrades significantly faster than PLLA.
3. PLLA is primarily used in load-bearing areas because it degrades within 4 to 6 weeks, allowing rapid bone in-growth.
4. PLLA degrades faster than PGA due to its highly crystalline structure and lower molecular weight.

## Correct option

**2. PGA exhibits higher initial mechanical strength but loses its structural integrity and degrades significantly faster than PLLA.**

## Answer explanation

PGA is highly crystalline with excellent initial tensile strength, but its hydrophilic nature causes it to undergo rapid hydrolysis, losing mechanical strength within weeks, whereas PLLA degrades over months to years.

 - PGA vs. PLLA Properties: PGA exhibits excellent initial mechanical strength but is highly hydrophilic, causing it to undergo rapid hydrolysis and lose its structural integrity in a matter of weeks. Conversely, PLLA contains a hydrophobic methyl group that resists water penetration, allowing it to degrade much more slowly (over months to years).

 - PLGA Copolymer Ratios: Combining PLA and PGA monomers creates the PLGA copolymer. Adjusting the ratio of these monomers dictates the degradation time.

 - The 50:50 PLGA Rule: A 50:50 ratio of PLA to PGA disrupts the structural regularity of the polymer chains, creating a highly amorphous material. This allows water to penetrate very easily, resulting in the fastest degradation time compared to either pure homopolymer (PLA or PGA) or other copolymer ratios.

Answer: 2
