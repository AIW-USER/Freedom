## Question

A 90-year-old woman sustains a ground-level fall at her nursing home and is unable to bear weight. She presents to the emergency department, where she is found to have a displaced femoral neck fracture. Which of the following associates the best treatment option with its associated outcome?

## Options

1. Total hip arthroplasty; lower dislocation rate compared to hemiarthroplasty
2. Open reduction internal fixation; decreased rate of failure compared to arthroplasty
3. Uncemented hemiarthroplasty; lower periprosthetic fracture rate compared to cemented fixation
4. Cemented hemiarthroplasty; higher quality of life at 4 months postoperatively compared to uncemented fixation
5. Uncemented hemiarthroplasty; higher quality of life at 4 months postoperatively compared to cemented fixation

## Correct option

**4. Cemented hemiarthroplasty; higher quality of life at 4 months postoperatively compared to uncemented fixation**

## Answer explanation

Cemented hemiarthroplasty is the most appropriate treatment for the patient in the clinical vignette. Cemented hemiarthroplasty has been demonstrated to have higher health-related quality of life measurements at 4 months postoperatively and lower periprosthetic fracture rates than uncemented hemiarthroplasty.

Geriatric femoral neck fractures often occur due to low-energy falls. Treatment of these injuries depends on fracture displacement, and nondisplaced fractures can often be treated with closed reduction and cannulated screw fixation. In cases of displaced femoral neck fractures, arthroplasty procedures are recommended. Surgical fixation of displaced geriatric femoral neck fractures has a high rate of failure. Total hip arthroplasty is recommended in older, active patients, however, there is a higher rate of dislocation compared to hemiarthroplasty. Hemiarthroplasty is recommended for elderly patients with restricted mobility.

*Veldman et al. conducted a systematic review of randomized control trials to compare the outcomes of cemented vs cementless hemiarthroplasty in the treatment of femoral neck fractures.* **They found** that while cementless stems had a significantly lower operative time, there was a higher rate of complications. **The authors conclude** that cemented stems lead to lower implant-related complications and similar mortality compared to cementless stems.

*Duijnisveld et al. conducted a retrospective review of hemiarthroplasty procedures through the Dutch Arthroplasty registry.* **The authors conclude** that while long-term mortality did not differ between the two methods, revision rates were lower for cemented compared to cementless hemiarthroplasty.

*Fernandez et al. conducted a landmark multicenter randomized control trial comparing patients older than 60 with intracapsular femoral neck fractures to either cemented or cementless hemiarthroplasty.* Their primary outcome was health-related quality of life at 4 months after surgery. **They found** that cemented hemiarthroplasty led to higher quality of life scores at 4 months and a lower risk of periprosthetic fracture compared to cementless hemiarthroplasty.

Incorrect Answers;
Answer choice 1: Compared to hemiarthroplasty, total hip arthroplasty has a greater risk of dislocation when treating older patients with femoral neck fractures.
Answer choice 2: Arthroplasty has a lower failure rate than fixation when treating geriatric femoral neck fractures.
Answer choice 3: Compared to cemented hemiarthroplasty, uncemented hemiarthroplasty has a higher risk of periprosthetic fracture.
Answer choice 5: Uncemented hemiarthroplasty is associated with significantly lower health-related quality of life measurements at 4 months postoperatively.
