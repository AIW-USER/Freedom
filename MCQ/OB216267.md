## Question

A 17-year-old lacrosse player presents to your office for evaluation of her left ankle. She reports sustaining an injury to the ankle while playing in a game last week. She denies any previous ankle injuries. She was evaluated in the ED the same day where radiographs were unremarkable and she was placed into a posterior splint and given crutches. She was seen by her PCP and had an MRI of the ankle which was remarkable for complete rupture of the anterior talofibular and calcaneofibular ligaments without syndesmotic injury or bony avulsion. Which of the following treatments is most indicated at this time?

## Options

1. Cast immobilization for 6 weeks
2. RICE, elastic wrap to minimize swelling, followed by therapy
3. Distal tibiofibular stabilization with use of a suture button
4. Gould modification of Brostrom anatomic reconstruction
5. Reconstruction using a tendon transfer with tenodesis

## Correct option

**2. RICE, elastic wrap to minimize swelling, followed by therapy**

## Answer explanation

This patient sustained a Grade III ankle sprain. Initial management should include RICE, elastic wrap to minimize swelling, followed by therapy.

Ankle Sprains are very common twisting injuries to the ankle that are the most common reason for missed athletic participation. Diagnosis can be made clinically with swelling and ecchymosis of the ankle and pain with range of motion. Radiographs are only indicated when clinical examination meets criteria (Ottawa ankle rules). Grade III lateral ankle ligament injuries involve a complete rupture of the lateral ligaments (ATFL and CFL). Early weight bearing and use of a protective brace during functional activities facilitates recovery better than non–weight bearing or immobilization. Rarely, surgery may be considered when an adequate trial of nonsurgical treatment fails to control symptoms.

*Kannus et al reviewed treatment of acute grade-III sprains of the lateral ligaments of the ankle.* **They reported** that in 75-100% of patients, the one-year prognosis was good or excellent irrespective of therapy. However, early controlled mobilization led to improved recovery time. **They concluded** that early controlled mobilization provides the quickest recovery to full ROM, return to work and physical activity, while not compromising the late mechanical stability of the ankle.

*DiGiovanni et al reviewed acute ankle injury and chronic instability in the athlete.* **They report** that in acute lateral ligament injuries, a functional ankle rehabilitation program is the mainstay of treatment. **They concluded** that good results have been obtained with nonoperative treatment for Grade III injuries.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Initial immobilization may require short period (approx. 1 week) of weight-bearing immobilization in a walking boot, aircast or walking cast, but early mobilization facilitates a better recovery
Answer 3: Syndesmotic stabilization with either screws or suture button may be indicated in a syndesmotic injury
Answers 4&5: Operative intervention may be indicated in Grade I-III injuries that continue to have pain and instability despite extensive nonoperative management or in Grade I-III with a bony avulsion
