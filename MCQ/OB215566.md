## Question

A 72-year-old male has a known metastatic lesion at T12 with mild cord compression. He undergoes a corpectomy and cage reconstruction utilizing a wide, left-sided anterolateral exposure. Following surgery, he is found to have impaired motor function in his legs with preserved sensation. His postoperative studies reveal a patent spinal canal. What is the most likely cause of his new motor deficits?

## Options

1. Anterior thecal sac buckling
2. Continued cord compression
3. Intraoperative hypertension
4. Occlusion of arterial supply to spine
5. Venous emboli to the spinal cord

## Correct option

**4. Occlusion of arterial supply to spine**

## Answer explanation

This patient is presenting with anterior cord syndrome. The most likely cause of anterior cord syndrome in this patient is injury to the artery of Adamkiewicz.

The artery of Adamkiewicz (aka arteria radicularis magna) is the largest anterior segmental medullary artery that commonly arises on the left side between T8 and L1. Injury to this structure may lead to anterior cord syndrome and this patient's examination is consistent with this, as evident by motor deficits with preserved sensation.

*Taterra et al. reviewed postoperative neurological deficit due to spinal cord ischemia following injury to the artery of Adamkiewicz.* **They report** that the artery of Adamkiewicz was present in 85% of the population, with most patients having a single vessel (87%) on the left side (77%) originating between T8 and L1 (89%). **They concluded** that the artery of Adamkiewicz is present in the majority of the population, and caution should be taken during vascular and endovascular surgical procedures to avoid injury or ensure proper reconstruction.

*Charles et al. reviewed the preoperative topography of the artery of Adamkiewicz and the blood supply of the lumbosacral spinal cord in patients who underwent spinal surgery utilizing anterior approaches.* **They report** that the Adamkiewicz artery was always located between T8 and L3, at T9 or T10 in 50%, and arising from the left side in 75% of the cases. **They concluded** that spinal angiography safely determines the topography of the Adamkiewicz artery, and a contralateral approach or selective surgical techniques without vessel ligation may avoid damage to the Adamkiewicz artery if the pathology does not dictate the side and the extent of the surgical approach.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The spinal canal is noted to be patent, so buckling of the thecal sac would not explain his examination
Answer 2: Postoperative studies show that the spinal canal is patent with no continued cord compression
Answer 3: Hypotension could cause ischemic changes to the cord, but hypertension typically does not
Answer 5: A venous emboli would be unlikely and this patient's anterior cord syndrome is likely secondary to injury during the anterolateral exposure of the spine
