## Question

A healthy 36-year-old male feels a pop in his right knee while pivoting to return a serve in a pickleball match. He is evaluated in the clinic setting and found to have a 3B Lachman exam with an MRI confirming an anterior cruciate ligament (ACL) tear. The patient is counseled on ACL reconstruction and graft options. The patient would like to avoid donor site morbidity; however, you warn him that an allograft tendon may lengthen and fail over time. You discuss with the patient the different biomechanical properties of allograft tendon, including creep deformation. Which of the following best describes the principle of creep?

## Options

1. A characteristic of viscoelastic materials where the loading curve does not follow the unloading curve
2. An increase in strain as the load is held constant over time
3. A decrease in load while strain is held constant
4. An irreversible change in the shape of a material due to a constant loading force
5. The amount of energy per volume a material can absorb before failure

## Correct option

**2. An increase in strain as the load is held constant over time**

## Answer explanation

Creep is defined as an increased strain on a material as an applied load is held constant. In this example, there is concern the allograft tendon will deform over time while under constant tension in the knee due to the principle of creep.

Several definitions are key to understanding the mechanical properties of tissues and implants. Creep is a phenomenon of progressive deformation while a constant force is held over time. In other words, the strain (change in length/original length) increases over time while under constant stress. As seen in Illustration A, the additional deformation that occurs to a material during this time of constant loading is known as creep deformation. This biomechanical property of materials is of particular importance when selecting a graft for a reconstructive procedure, such as in the setting of ACL reconstruction.

*Gokler et al. compared different tendon types (T-tibialis anterior, P-peroneus longus), sterilization methods (G-gamma irradiation of 21 kGy, E-electron beam irradiation of 21 kGy), and storage times (5 and 6 months) on their respective creep behaviors when loads were applied.* **They concluded** that deep-frozen allografts stored for less than 5 months had significantly less creep when compared to allografts stored for greater than 6 months.

*Shelton et al. compared non-irradiated, whole bone-tendon-bone allografts from donors less than 30 years old and greater than 50 years old.* **They concluded** that grafts from older donors had a decrease in the ultimate tensile strength at the bone tendon junction. However, there was no difference in displacement, strain, or creep displacement.

Illustration A shows strain on the Y-axis and time on the X-axis while stress is held constant. The increase in strain from X1 to X2 represents secondary to creep.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: This is the definition of hysteresis.
Answer 3: This is the definition of stress relaxation, which is also a property of viscoelastic materials.
Answer 4: This is the definition of plastic deformation.
Answer 5: This is the definition of toughness.
