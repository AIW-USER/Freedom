## Options

1. Hindfoot varus is corrected to hindfoot valgus with a wooden block placed under the medial foot and heel
2. Hindfoot varus remains in hindfoot varus with a wooden placed under the medial foot and heel
3. Hindfoot varus is corrected to hindfoot valgus with a wooden block placed under the lateral foot and heel
4. Hindfoot varus remains in hindfoot varus with a wooden block placed under the lateral foot and heel
5. Inability to perform single-limb heel rise

## Correct option

**4. Hindfoot varus remains in hindfoot varus with a wooden block placed under the lateral foot and heel**

## Answer explanation

A lateralizing calcaneal osteotomy suggests a rigid cavus foot was present preoperatively. A rigid varus deformity would remain in varus with a wooden block placed under the lateral foot and heel.

Cavovarus deformity can occur in pediatric or adult populations and may present with pain on the lateral or medial borders of the foot. Initial treatment with orthotics may be appropriate for mild disease. Surgical planning relies on the determination of the cavus deformity to be rigid or flexible. The Coleman block test is performed by placing a wooden block under the lateral foot and heel and assessing for correction of hindfoot varus. Correction to neutral or valgus suggests the deformity is flexible and driven by first metatarsal plantarflexion. If the hindfoot remains in varus the deformity is rigid and needs to be corrected with a lateralizing calcaneal osteotomy often in conjunction with a dorsiflexion osteotomy of the first metatarsal.

*Younger et al. review the adult cavovarus foot including etiology, diagnosis, and treatment options.* **They state** that when the hindfoot does not correct to neutral a lateralizing or valgus producing calcaneal osteotomy is indicated in order to create a plantigrade foot and lateralize the moment arm of the Achilles during toe-off.

*Schwend et al. review the cavus foot deformity in children including etiology, diagnosis, and treatment options.* *They discuss the Coleman block test and state when the hindfoot is flexible treatment includes bony forefoot procedures or tendon transfers of the ankle, hindfoot, and/or midfoot.* However, if the hindfoot deformity is rigid, surgical correction involves both the forefoot and hindfoot, with the addition of a lateralizing calcaneal osteotomy.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1, 2: The Coleman block test for cavovarus deformities is performed by placing a wooden block under the lateral border of the foot and heel.
Answer 3: A varus hindfoot that corrects to neutral or valgus with a wooden block under the lateral foot and heel suggests a flexible deformity.
Answer 5: Inability to perform a single heel raise may be seen in posterior tibial tendon insufficiency.
