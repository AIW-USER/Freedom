## Question

A 65-year-old female electively undergoes the procedure shown in Figure A. She has a remote history of breast cancer treated with lumpectomy and has been receiving conjugated estrogen therapy from her nurse practitioner since the initiation of menopause. She has chronic, painless varicose veins on both legs and states that she had a "clot in her leg" once after a flight to Australia nearly 30 years ago. Which of the following is most true regarding the use of postoperative duplex ultrasonography in this patient?

*Figure A*

## Options

1. A bilateral duplex screening ultrasound is a cost effective measure after routine total hip arthroplasty but not after total knee arthroplasty
2. History of prior unprovoked deep vein thrombosis (DVT) is an absolute indication for post-operative screening duplex ultrasonography
3. Routine use of post-operative duplex ultrasonography is strongly recommended against after elective joint replacement surgery
4. The data on the use of post-operative screening ultrasound for deep vein thrombosis (DVT) is inconclusive
5. Women on estrogen therapy should be screened with duplex ultrasonography after elective joint replacement surgery

## Correct option

**3. Routine use of post-operative duplex ultrasonography is strongly recommended against after elective joint replacement surgery**

## Answer explanation

The patient, despite her risk factors for thrombosis, has undergone an elective total hip arthroplasty with no indication of symptomatic postoperative deep vein thrombosis (DVT). The American Academy of Orthopaedic Surgeons' (AAOS) Guidelines for Preventing Venous Thromboembolic Disease in Patients Undergoing Elective Hip and Knee Arthroplasty strongly recommends against the routine use of postoperative duplex ultrasonography screening.

The AAOS published a clinical practice guideline on the management of venous thromboembolism in elective joint arthroplasty in concordance with the American Society of Hematology 2019 guidelines, making conditional recommendations for mechanical prophylaxis over no prophylaxis, for pneumatic compression prophylaxis over graduated compression stockings, and against inferior vena cava filters. In patients undergoing total hip or total knee arthroplasty, conditional recommendations included using either aspirin or anticoagulants, as well as a direct oral anticoagulant over low-molecular-weight heparin (LMWH) were also made. The regular use of duplex ultrasonography screening for DVT was not found to be clinically useful leading the AAOS to publish a strong recommendation against the practice.

Schwarcz, et al. published a review of surveillance venous duplex after total joint arthroplasty. The authors reviewed 398 patients and concluded that postoperative inpatient surveillance duplex scans for DVT provide very minimal benefit and that a routine screening program is not clinically useful for patients managed with effective DVT prophylaxis.

Brothers, et al. reviewed whether or not duplex venous surveillance is worthwhile after arthroplasty. The authors reviewed 738 arthroplasty cases and found that in asymptomatic patients only two (0.5%) surveillance duplexes were positive for DVT. They found that the incremental cost was $35,000 to detect 1 additional unsuspected DVT and $110,000 per additional quality-adjusted life-year gained, leading them to conclude that the current effectiveness of prophylaxis does not justify an aggressive screening program.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Literature has shown that in asymptomatic patients, routine post-operative screening with duplex ultrasonography is not a cost-effective measure. There is no difference in cost-effectiveness after total hip versus total knee arthroplasty.
Answer 2: Despite the presence of increased pre-operative risk factors, in the presence of adequate chemo and mechanical prophylaxis under current guidelines, there is no indication to perform routine post-operative surveillance duplex scanning of the lower extremities in elective total joint arthroplasty cases, even with a history of prior unprovoked DVT.
Answer 4: The data and best clinical practice guidelines are, in fact, not inconclusive, and strongly recommend against performing routine post-operative surveillance duplex scanning of the lower extremities in elective total joint arthroplasty cases.
Answer 5: Women on estrogen therapy may have a higher baseline risk of coagulopathy, but there is no indication for them to receive screening duplex ultrasonography after elective joint replacement surgery if they are asymptomatic.
