## Question

A 14-year-old female presents for evaluation of painful hallux valgus deformity of her left great toe. She has attempted modifying her footwear and wearing orthotic inserts, however her pain has progressed and she is now having difficulty with ambulation. Current radiographs demonstrate a hallux valgus angle of 36º, an intermetatarsal angle of 16º, and a distal metatarsal articular angle of 21º with closed first metatarsal physis. Which of the following is the best next step in treatment?

## Options

1. Double first metatarsal osteotomy
2. Proximal Ludloff osteotomy with distal soft tissue procedure
3. First cuneiform osteotomy
4. Metatarsophalangeal arthrodesis
5. Distal biplanar chevron osteotomy

## Correct option

**1. Double first metatarsal osteotomy**

## Answer explanation

This adolescent has a severe hallux valgus deformity with increased DMAA that has failed conservative therapy including orthotic inserts and footwear modifications. The most appropriate treatment to correct the deformity and reorient the articular surface in this patient is a first metatarsal double osteotomy.

Juvenile hallux valgus is a hereditary condition of the first ray involving a congruent deformity of the metatarsal head articular surface in combination with proximal metatarsal varus or deformity through the tarsometatarsal joint. Initial management should consist of conservative therapy with shoe modifications. Surgical correction can be offered to the symptomatic patient with moderate to severe deformity who fails nonoperative treatment, though ideally should be delayed until skeletal maturity to prevent deformity recurrence. Surgical options are similar to those of the adult patient if the metatarsal physis is closed, however a second osteotomy is typically required to address the congruent joint deformity. Double first metatarsal osteotomy has proven to provide effective and reliable correction in the adolescent patient with moderate to severe hallux valgus and increased DMAA.

*Coughlin et al. describe a series of 60 patients with juvenile hallux valgus treated with multiprocedural approaches.* The double metatarsal osteotomy achieved average HVA correction of 22.9º and IMA correction of 8.3º – correction ratios of four to five times that of the Chevron osteotomies. There were no recurrences of deformity in this group.

*Johnson and colleagues similarly report reliable angular correction of double first metatarsal osteotomy in adolescent hallux valgus patients of 21.54º, 9.25º, and 6.21º for HVA, IMA, and DMAA, respectively.* *Ninety percent of patients in this series reported good to excellent functional results at a mean follow-up of 27 months post-operatively.*

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: A proximally-based osteotomy would not address the greatly increased DMAA in this patient.
Answer 3: First cuneiform osteotomy is indicated in patients with severe deformity and open first metatarsal physis. This patient is skeletally mature.
Answer 4: MTP arthrodesis is indicated for hallux valgus in the setting of cerebral palsy, Down’s syndrome, rheumatoid arthritis, severe degenerative joint disease, and Ehler-Danlos syndrome.
Answer 5: A distally-based osteotomy alone would not address the increased IMA in this patient.
