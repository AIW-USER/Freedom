## Question

## Options

1. It helps in deciding treatment plan of compound fracture
2. More is the score, greater are the chances of amputation

## Correct option

**3. It tells about chances of survival of the polytrauma patient**

## Answer explanation

Ganga hospital score for open fractures:

Open fractures are conventionally classified by Gustilo-Anderson classification. Gustilo classification does not tell about treatment plan and prognosis with increasing severity of type III B compound fractures. In such severe injuries Ganga score was devised by Rajasekaran and Raja Sabapathy (Ganga hospital, Tamilnadu) to help decision making amongst surgeons on deciding soft tissue reconstruction or amputation in such injuries.

The score considers three areas- skin and fascia (i.e. the covering soft tissues), the musculo-tendinous units (i.e. functional units) and bone (i.e. skeletal support), with the severity scale in each category ranging from 1-5. Further points are added if the patient has certain comorbidities. Final score is calculated after debridement. Patients of Gustilo type III B injuries are grouped in 4 groups

Group I: score 5 or less

Group II: score between 6 to 10

Group III: score between 11 to 15

Group IV: score >15

Parameters:

(1) injury to skin and fascia (covering structures)

(2) injury to bone and joints (skeletal structures)

(3) injury to musculotendinous and nerve units (functional tissue)

(4) comorbid conditions

• A score of 1 or 2 points indicates an injury that can be managed without any special secondary procedure. A score of 3 indicates that a special secondary procedure (i.e. plastic surgery) is needed with expectation of good functional outcome. A score of 4 or 5 indicates a serious, complex injury which may have a poor functional outcome despite optimum care. Chances of amputation increases with increasing scores and score more than 5 in more than two components and all group IV patients eventually require amputation.

Comorbid conditions - 2 points for each of the following:

(1) > 12 hours interval between injury and debridement

(2) age > 65 years

(3) contamination of the wound with sewage, organic material, farmyard, etc

(4) systolic blood pressure < 90 mm Hg at presentation (hypotension)

(5) compartment syndrome or another major injury to the same limb

(6) increased risk anesthesia due to drug-dependent diabetes mellitus or cardiorespiratory diseases

(7) polytrauma involving chest and abdomen with ISS > 25 and/or fat embolism

Ganga Hospital Score = SUM (points for all 4 parameters)

Interpretation:

• minimum score: 0

• maximum score: 29

• The higher the score the more severe the trauma.

• A score >= 14 was used as the threshold score for amputation.

Reference: Rajasekaran S, Raja Sabapathy S. A philosophy of care of open injuries based on the Ganga hospital score. Injury (Int J Care Injured). 2007; 38: 137-146
