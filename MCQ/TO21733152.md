Chapter: Arthroplasty MCQs — Periprosthetic Fractures

## Question

Post TKR a patient has sustained Type 2b periprosthetic tibia fracture. Management option

## Options

1. Locking plate
2. Revision with long stem
3. LCP
4. LISS

## Correct option

**2. Revision with long stem**

## Answer explanation

Answer: 2
EXPLANATION: -
The most commonly used system for classifying periprosthetic tibial fractures was described in 1997 by Felix et al. The classification scheme has four types based on anatomic location and three subcategories based on component fixation and timing of the fracture (intra- operative, postoperative).
Type I fractures involve the implant and plateau interface
Type II fractures are located adjacent to the stem in the metaphyseal-diaphyseal junction.
Type III fractures are tibial shaft fractures distal to the implant
Type IV fractures are tibial tubercle fractures.

![[TO21733152-1.png]]

Subtype A fractures involve stable components,
Subtype B fractures involve an unstable tibial component.
Subtype C fractures occur intraoperatively.

The majority of postoperative Types I and II fractures are Types IB and IIB, and these are treated with revision surgery. Types IIA, IIIA, and IVA fractures should be managed successfully by the usual principles of tibial fracture treatment.
Type IC fractures usually are managed by intraoperative fixation, Type IIC by bone grafting or external immobilization and weightbearing restrictions, and Type IIIC by conventional fracture management
More recently, the Unified Classification System (UCS) was proposed for periprosthetic fractures through- out the body, a schema that can be applied to the tibia. According to the UCS, type A fractures are apophyseal, type B fractures involve the bed of the implant, and type C fractures are clear of the bed of the implant. Type D fractures divide one bone that separates two arthroplasties, type E fractures are fractures through each of two bones supporting one arthroplasty, and type F fractures articulate or face an implant although the bone is not resurfaced.
