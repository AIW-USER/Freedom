## Question

An 83-year-old female sustains the injury seen in Figure A. Which of the following significantly reduces mortality and postoperative complications when caring for this injury?

![[q214843-a-right-geriatric-femoral-neck-fracture.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Surgical intervention for all patients between 24-48 hours regardless of optimization
2. Discharge disposition to a skilled nursing facility
3. Immediate initiation of preoperative distal femur traction
4. A patient with a preoperative American Society of Anesthesiologists’ (ASA) Class IV designation
5. Utilization of a multidisciplinary treatment team

## Correct option

**5. Utilization of a multidisciplinary treatment team**

## Answer explanation

The utilization of a multidisciplinary treatment team has been shown to reduce the mortality and complications associated with a geriatric hip fracture.

Geriatric hip fractures pose a challenge to the healthcare treatment team as the patients require special medical and social attention. The goal of surgical fixation of patients with geriatric hip fractures is an early return of preinjury function through surgical intervention while avoiding the many complications associated with caring for this fragile population. The timing of surgery for these patients should be as soon as medically optimized, ideally within 48 hours from injury. Variables such as pain management and DVT prophylaxis are all without definite consensus and should be therefore tailored to each individual patient.

*Egol et al. reviewed the topic of managing geriatric hip fractures in an evidence-based approach.* **They found** that timing of surgery depends on comorbidities and medical optimization, preoperative traction was not recommended, DVT prophylaxis regimens were without consensus, and while pain management lacks consensus, frequent pain assessments may allow for tailoring of analgesia and dosage. **They also found** a multidisciplinary treatment team led to lower mortality, complications, and hospital stay. **They concluded** that an evidence-based algorithm when managing geriatric hip fracture patients provides the best opportunity for a successful outcome.

*Parvizi et al. compiled data from a single institution registry regarding mortality following hip arthroplasty for fracture.* **They found** a mortality rate of 2.4% in the thirty day period after arthroplasty for fracture with the mortality rates the highest for patients with cemented implants, female gender, elderly patients, those with cardiorespiratory disease, and those with intertrochanteric fractures. **They concluded** that hip arthroplasty for acute fracture is associated with a tenfold increase in mortality compared to elective arthroplasty.

Figure A shows a radiograph with a right displaced femoral neck fracture. Illustration A shows the same patient following hemiarthroplasty.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Early surgical intervention has been shown to improve outcomes and decrease mortality but only in patients who have been optimized within that time frame.
Answer 2: Discharge disposition to a skilled nursing facility has not been shown to decrease mortality.
Answer 3: Traction in patients with geriatric hip fractures has not been shown to decrease mortality or complications.
Answer 4: ASA Class IV patients are considered to have a significant systemic disease and are at an increased risk of perioperative mortality.
