## Answer explanation

Bunionette deformities that have failed nonoperative measures with increased 4-5 intermetatarsal angle (4-5 IMA) greater than 12 degrees should be corrected with oblique diaphyseal osteotomies.

Bunionette deformities present as a painful prominence over the 5th metatarsal head. They can be classified according to Coughlin as Types I-III. Type I deformities have enlarged metatarsal head or exostosis. Type II deformities have a lateral bowing of the fifth metatarsal. Type III deformities have a widened 4-5 IMA. Type II and Type III bunionettes with greater 4-5 IMA greater than 12 or painful keratotic lesions should be corrected. The oblique diaphyseal rotational osteotomy can correct deformity and an additional lateral condylectomy can be performed to decrease prominence. Complications following the procedure include metatarsal shortening and metatarsalgia.

*Cohen et al. reviewed bunionette deformity presentation and treatment.* **They state** oblique diaphyseal rotational osteotomy should be performed in patients with increased 4-5 IMA angles or painful Type II and III lesions with associated keratotic deformity.

*Vienne et al. followed 33 feet who underwent corrective osteotomy for bunionette deformity with or without bunionectomy of the fifth metatarsal head.* **They found** no difference in patient satisfaction with or without bunionectomy. **They conclude** the modified Coughlin procedure leads to high patient satisfaction with low complication rates.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1 & 4: Surgical correction for Type I deformities consists of lateral condylectomy or on certain occasions distal chevron osteotomy.
Answer 2 & 3: Type II in addition to Type III deformities are treated with oblique diaphyseal rotational osteotomies when 4-5 IMA >12 degrees.
