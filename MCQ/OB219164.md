## Question

A 71-year-old female undergoes cephalomedullary nailing for the injury shown in Figure A. Given the underlying etiology of her injury, her orthopaedic surgeon recommends an anabolic medication to promote healing of her injury. Which of the following is a mechanism of action of this medication?

![[q219164-a-atypicalsubtroch.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. increases serum calcium through increased bone resorption
2. increases calcium uptake through the small intestine
3. inhibits osteoclastic activity through the formation of toxic ATP analogues
4. inhibits osteoclastic activity through inhibition of the mevalonate pathway
5. monoclonal antibody inhibition of RANK-L

## Correct option

**1. increases serum calcium through increased bone resorption**

## Answer explanation

Figure A is a radiograph of an atypical subtrochanteric femur fracture, likely associated with long-term bisphosphonate therapy. Teriparatide is an analog of parathyroid hormone (PTH), which increases serum calcium through increased bone resorption and may be an adjunct medication to promote the healing of these fractures (Answer 1).

Subtrochanteric femur fractures are a feared complication of long-term bisphosphonate therapy. Several key criteria define atypical fractures: low-energy trauma or atraumatic mechanism, transverse fracture pattern, noncomminuted fracture pattern, presence of a medial spike, and periosteal reaction or beaking at the lateral cortex. Upon presentation, the contralateral femur should be imaged as well to evaluate for similar pathology, regardless of whether symptoms are present, and complete and symptomatic impending fractures are conventionally treated with intramedullary nail fixation. Due to bisphosphonates' mechanism and long half-life, atypical subtrochanteric femur fractures are typically associated with delayed healing. Teriparatide has anabolic effects on bone when given intermittently, evidenced by increased bone turnover markers and bone mineral density in the lumbar spine. While the evidence supporting its use specifically in the healing of atypical subtrochanteric femur fractures is inconsistent throughout the literature, teriparatide may be indicated in those who do not demonstrate radiographic union despite conservative treatment with vitamin D, calcium, and cessation of bisphosphonate therapy. Additionally, its anabolic effects may mitigate the risk of sustaining additional fragility fractures elsewhere.

*Shane et al. published two reports on atypical femur fractures, one in 2010 and 2014.* The latter report provided an update on the epidemiology, pathogenesis, and medical treatment of atypical femur fractures. The authors’ conclusions on the usage of teriparatide in atypical femur fractures are based on the available evidence, primarily anecdotal accounts published in low-quality case studies. While teriparatide was shown to have a positive effect on bone mineral density and increased bone turnover markers, the absence of a randomized, placebo-controlled trial makes it impossible to definitively conclude the efficacy of teriparatide in the treatment of atypical femur fractures. However, patients who have failed other methods of conservative treatment may be suitable candidates for teriparatide therapy.

*Van de Laarschot et al. performed a 2020 systematic review of the medical management of atypical femur fractures with 67 total papers.* *Of these 31 were case reports, 9 retrospective reviews, and 3 prospective studies.* In 98 total AFF patients on teriparatide, fracture union occurred within 6 months in 13 of 30 (43%) conservatively managed incomplete AFFs, 9 of 10 (90%) incomplete AFFs with surgical intervention, and 44 of 58 (75%) complete AFFs. Four of nine AFFs that previously failed conservative treatment went on to union after initiating teriparatide therapy. The authors do caution that most of these cases were from observational studies and not higher-quality studies.
