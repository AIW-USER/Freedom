## Options

1. Curettage and bone graft for giant cell tumor
2. Aspiration and steroid injection for unicameral bone cyst
3. Curettage and bone graft for aneurysmal bone cyst
4. Wide resection for telangiectic osteosarcoma
5. Chemotherapy and wide resection for telangiectic osteosarcoma

## Correct option

**3. Curettage and bone graft for aneurysmal bone cyst**

## Answer explanation

This patient has an aneurysmal bone cyst (ABC) which is best treated with curettage and bone grafting.

ABCs are benign non-neoplastic reactive bone lesions characterized by multiple blood-filled cavities. Radiographs show expansile, eccentric metaphyseal lesions with bony septae ("bubbly appearance"). The histological appearance is that of cavernous blood-filled spaces without endothelial lining, numerous benign giant cells and spindle cells, and thin strands of woven bone. Treatment is with curettage and bone graft.

*Steffner et al. retrospectively evaluated the efficacy of argon beam coagulation and factors associated with recurrence.* **They found** that recurrence was 7.5% after curettage plus argon beam coagulation, and 20% with curettage alone. Recurrence was associated with pathological fracture, age <12 years, and female sex. **They concluded** that argon beam coagulation is effective but may lead to an increased risk of postoperative fracture. This technique is currently not standard of care.

Figure A comprises of AP, mortise and lateral radiographs of the ankle showing an eccentric, lytic, expansile metaphyseal lesion of the distal tibia with no matrix mineralization. Figure B is a low-power image of an ABC showing cystic spaces separated by septa containing giant cells and spindle cells. The spaces are filled with blood and lack an endothelial lining. Figure C is an intermediate-power image showing clusters of giant cells in a background of spindle cells within the septa.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: This is not a giant cell tumor. GCTs are not normally found in patients with open growth plates, and are usually metaphyseal-epiphyseal (rather than purely metaphyseal). GCT with secondary ABCs will demonstrate numerous giant cells whose nuclei are identical to the adjacent stromal cells. They are lytic/destructive on radiograph and are not wider than the adjacent physis (unlike ABCs).
Answer 2: This is an ABC and not a UBC. UBCs are characterized histologically by a thin fibrous lining containing fibrous tissue, giant cells, and hemosiderin pigment. Radiologically, they do not expand the bone wider than the adjacent physis.
Answers 4 and 5: Histologically, telangiectic osteosarcomas are characterized by lakes of blood mixed with atypical, pleomorphic malignant cells with mitotic bodies. Treatment is with neoadjuvant chemotherapy, wide resection and adjuvant chemotherapy.
