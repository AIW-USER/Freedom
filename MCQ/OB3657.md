## Question

A 56-year-old male undergoes resection of a mass that was suspected to be a simple lipoma. The mass was contained within his sartorius muscle and shown in Figure A. The final pathology came back as a high grade soft tissue sarcoma. The treating surgeon operated completely within the sartorius compartment and did not violate any other compartments, however the final pathology showed positive margins. Staging studies show isolated disease in his right thigh. What is the most appropriate treatment for this patient?

![[q3657-a-f31-large.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Amputation
2. Systemic chemotherapy
3. Radiation therapy
4. Wide excision of the tumor bed
5. Wide excision of the tumor bed followed by radiation therapy

## Correct option

**5. Wide excision of the tumor bed followed by radiation therapy**

## Answer explanation

Incompletely excised soft tissue sarcomas present special treatment dilemmas for the orthopaedic oncologist. As such, any DEEP lesion requires advanced imaging prior to surgical excision. Because of the complex muscle planes and anatomy, deep lesions which are incompletely excised can make limb-salvage very difficult. Superficial lesions may be removed based on the treating surgeon's judgement, however they need to be removed without violation of the superficial fascia.

*Potter et al review the rates of local recurrence after unplanned incomplete excision of high-grade soft tissue sarcomas.* With appropriate radiation therapy and negative margin tumor bed re-excision, these patients have nearly the same rates of local recurrence as all other negative margin patients. They do however require more morbid surgical excision to achieve these negative margins.

Incorrect answers:
1) Amputation - without contamination of the femoral vessels, the fascia deep to sartorius will serve as a good boundary for the secondary tumor bed excision
2) Systemic chemotherapy isn't indicated for local control in soft tissue sarcoma
3&4) Given the positive margins of the initial excision and the close proximity to the femoral vessels, radiation therapy can be used, but only in combination with tumor bed re-excision.
