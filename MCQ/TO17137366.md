#TO #trauma #radialheadfractures
Chapter: Trauma MCQs — Radial Head and Neck Fracture

## Question

Safe position for radial head fracture plating is

## Options

1. Just posterior to mid axial line with the forearm in neutral rotation
2. Just anterior to mid axial line with the forearm in neutral rotation
3. Just posterior to mid axial line with the forearm in pronation
4. Just anterior to mid axial line with the forearm in supination

## Correct option

**3. Just posterior to mid axial line with the forearm in pronation**

## Answer explanation

Answer: 3

Explanation: -

Only 75% of radial head articulates with ulna. The remaining 25% which does not articulate is considered the SAFE ZONE and is considered for placement of internal fixation implants (screws/ plates).

The safe position for radial head fracture plating is located posterior to the mid-axial line (an imaginary horizontal line parallel to ground), on the posterolateral aspect of the radial head, if forearm is in full supination. The same zone becomes lateral when forearm is in neutral rotation and antero lateral when the forearm is in pronation (see pics).

![[TO17137366-1.png]]

Image 1 depicts imaginary mid axial line. Image 2 shows safe zone (shaded area) with forearm in pronation (antero lateral), supination (postero lateral) and neutral rotation (postero lateral).

Technically, Safe zone is roughly defined as the area between a longitudinal line along the radial styloid and a longitudinal line along Lister's tubercle. This "safe zone" typically covers a 90°–110° arc that does not articulate with the ulna, ensuring the hardware does not interfere with the proximal radioulnar joint (PRUJ). The safest placement is cantered, which is approximately 166° (±10°) opposite the bicipital tuberosity.

The safe zone is most easily accessed by placing the forearm in neutral rotation or slight supination to avoid the posterior interosseous nerve (PIN), which is closer to the field when in full supination. The plate should be oriented laterally or posterolateral, avoiding the anterior aspect to prevent impairment of pronation/supination.
