## Question

Which of the following is the first branch of facial nerve?

## Options

1. Greater superior petrosal nerve
2. Chords tympani nerve
3. Nerve to stapedius
4. Posterior auricular nerve

## Correct option

**1. Greater superior petrosal nerve**

## Answer explanation

Ans: A

The course of facial nerve is divided in many segments:

- Cisternal segment: This is the part of the nerve after its origin from the brain stem, up to its entry in the internal acoustic meatus. There are no branches in this part.
- Canalicular segment: This part lies in the Facial canal. There are few salient features of this part.
- The geniculate ganglion - gives its first branch - the GSPN.
- The external genu of the facial nerve lies here.
- The middle ear segment: this segment has three parts of facial nerve, the horizontal part, the curve segment and the vertical part. The branch is nerve to stapedius.
- The mastoid segment: which lies in the bone between the middle ear and the mastoid antrum. The branch in this segment is chorda tympani nerve.
- The cervical segment: this is the part after it leaves the cranium via stylomastoid foramen. The branches in this segment are - posterior auricular nerve, nerve to Digastric, nerve to stylohyoid and the terminal branches (temporal, zygomatic, buccal, marginal mandibular and cervical)

Also remember: the geniculate ganglion is a SENSORY ganglion. The GSPN is a parasympathetic nerve, so the fibres of GSPN do not relay in the geniculate ganglion.

The lesser petrosal nerve is a branch of glossopharyngeal nerve.

The deep petrosal nerve is a sympathetic nerve.

The facial nerve enters the parotid gland and forms the pes ancinerus.

The facial nerve plane divides the parotid gland into superficial part and the deep part. In superficial parotidectomy, the part of the gland, above the facial nerve is removed.
