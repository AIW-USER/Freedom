## Question

Figure A is the radiograph of a 7 year-old boy who initially presented with right shoulder pain after serving a tennis ball. Prior to referral, another surgeon performed a biopsy, shown in Figure B. What is the most likely diagnosis?

![[q5435-a-figure-a.jpg]]
*Figure A*

![[q5435-b-histology.jpg]]
*Figure B*

## Options

1. Chondroblastoma
2. Aneurysmal bone cyst
3. Osteochondroma
4. Chondromyxoid fibroma
5. Unicameral bone cyst

## Correct option

**5. Unicameral bone cyst**

## Answer explanation

This patient has an unicameral bone cyst (UBC). Plain radiographs are usually sufficient to make the diagnosis. Magnetic resonance imaging, computed tomography and biopsy are usually unnecessary.

Unicameral bone cysts are meta-diaphyseal cystic lesions that may extend into the physis of patients in the first or second decade. Males are more commonly affected than females, and 90% occur in the proximal humerus or femur. Most are asymptomatic or present as pathologic fractures. The lesion may be multi-loculated. Differential diagnosis includes aneurysmal bone cyst.

*Kilgore et al. review tumors of the calcaneus, where 4% of all UBCs occur.* UBCs are the most common tumor of the calcaneus. They rarely cause pathologic fractures in this area. Observation is appropriate unless they are highly symptomatic, in which case curettage and bone grafting may be performed.

Figure A shows a unicameral bone cyst of the proximal humerus. Figure B shows representative histology marked by foci of reactive bone, hemosiderin pigment and scattered giant cells.

Incorrect answers:

Answer 1. Chondroblastoma is typically epiphyseal and no chondrocytes are visible in the histology
Answer 2. Aneurysmal bone cysts typically will exhibit many red blood cells on histology.
Answer 3. Osteochondroma can be metaphyseal but will typically be pedunculated or sessile and histology will show benign-appearing cartilage
Answer 4. Chondromyxoid fibroma may also be metaphyseal but histology will show lobulated growth, fibroblasts, chondromyxoid tissue with an extra-cellular matrix of glycosaminoglycans and proteoglycans
