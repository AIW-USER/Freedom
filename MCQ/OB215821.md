## Question

An 85-year-old female with a history of osteoporosis has a ground level fall at her nursing home and lands on her right hip. She is found to have an intertrochanteric hip fracture and undergoes intramedullary nailing. Which of the following is true regarding early bisphosphonate administration after fragility fracture of the hip?

## Options

1. Increased risk of nonunion
2. Increased risk of delayed union
3. IV bisphosphonates improve bone mineral density significantly more than PO
4. The most significant improvement in bone mineral density is seen within the first year of administration
5. Increased incidence of peri-implant fracture postoperatively

## Correct option

**4. The most significant improvement in bone mineral density is seen within the first year of administration**

## Answer explanation

Administration within the first year after fragility fracture has been shown to have the most significant improvement in bone mineral density (BMD), without having any adverse effects on time to union or rate of union following hip fragility fractures.

Intertrochanteric hip fractures are quite common, particularly in elderly females due to high rates of osteoporosis in this population. After fracture fixation, it is important to initiate appropriate medical management to improve bone quality and prevent subsequent fragility fractures. Bisphosphonates function to decrease osteoclast activity and therefore, bone turnover. It has been demonstrated that bisphosphonates are effective at increasing BMD within 12 months of administration and do not affect postoperative fracture healing or decrease time to union.

*Li et al. performed a meta-analysis evaluating early administration (<3 months) of bisphosphonates after orthopedic surgery.* There was no increase in nonunion or time to union in the bisphosphonate group. Conversely, BMD increased significantly at 12 months in the group taking bisphosphonates, which led to the authors concluding that bisphosphonate therapy is useful in preventing other fractures in this population.

*Cho et al. sought to evaluate the timing of bisphosphonate administration on bony union in intertrochanteric femur fractures.* **They noted** no cases of nonunion and no difference in time to union in the groups taking bisphosphonates. **They concluded** that there is no difference in outcomes when bisphosphonates are initiated between 1 week and 3 months postoperatively.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: No increase in nonunion rate has been shown in those taking bisphosphonates.
Answer 2: No increased delay in time to fracture union has been noted in those taking bisphosphonates.
Answer 3: Both oral and IV formulations of bisphosphonates have been shown to improve BMD.
Answer 5: There is no evidence of a higher risk of postoperative peri-implant fracture after bisphosphonate administration.
