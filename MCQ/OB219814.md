## Options

1. Intralesional excision with post-operative radiation given location of mass
2. Marginal excision of the atypical lipomatous mass
3. Neo-adjuvant chemotherapy, wide excision, and post-operative radiation
4. Referral to dermatology for Mohs micrographic surgery
5. Wide excision

## Correct option

**5. Wide excision**

## Answer explanation

The patient has dermatofibrosarcoma protuberans with fibrosarcomatous transformation, as evidenced by the histology in Figure E. The treatment for dermatofibrosarcoma protuberans is wide excision with or without adjuvant radiation therapy, given the high risk for local recurrence (Answer 5).

Dermatofibrosarcoma protuberans represents a rare, low-grade fibrogenic cutaneous sarcoma that occurs in early to mid-adult life, more frequently in African American males aged 20-50. It most frequently occurs on the trunk and proximal extremities is usually slow-growing and painless and may have a dark red or blue discoloration. On histology, the lesion classically appears with uniform fibroblasts arranged in a storiform pattern around inconspicuous vasculature (Figure D); however, the fibrosarcomatous variant includes more malignant-looking areas of fibroblasts in a herringbone pattern (Figure E). Treatment involves wide surgical excision, with adjuvant radiation used in patients with positive margins, large tumors, or in those in whom a wide excision would result in major cosmetic or functional defects. Recently, Imatinib (an inhibitor of PDGF-receptor tyrosine kinase) has been used in adult patients with unresectable, recurrent, and/or metastatic disease that possess the t(17,22) mutation with an overall 65% response rate.

*Diwakar et al. review a rare case of recurrent dermatofibrosarcoma protuberans in a 35-year-old male after a latency of 8 years in the region of a previous surgical scar.* **The authors note** that it was managed by a wide local excision followed by reconstruction using a latissimus dorsi flap. **They concluded** that it is important to carefully manage recurrent cases because the post-operative margin status is an important determinant of recurrence and, therefore, requires vigilant resection of the tumor without causing extensive morbidity to the patient.

*Hayakawa et al. reviewed the risk factors for distant metastasis of dermatofibrosarcoma protuberans.* **The authors note** that dermatofibrosarcoma protuberans (DFSP) may recur locally but rarely metastasizes; thus, they reviewed 67 cases of DFSP and found that out of five cases with metastasis, three had not recurred and two had recurred twice with no clear correlation identified. However, **they found** that the tumor diameters in metastatic cases were significantly larger, leading them to conclude that there was a stronger correlation between DFSP metastasis and tumor size, highlighting that early diagnosis before primary tumor growth and wide resection is considered important.
