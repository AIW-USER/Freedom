## Options

1. Inability to flex the interphalangeal joint of the thumb
2. Inability to cross fingers
3. Inability to extend the index finger
4. Inability to flex the wrist
5. Inability to flex the elbow

## Correct option

**2. Inability to cross fingers**

## Answer explanation

A medial entry percutaneous pin used for fixation of a supracondylar humerus fracture most commonly causes an injury to the ulnar nerve, which would manifest as an inability to cross or scissor the fingers.

Closed reduction and percutaneous pinning is the treatment of choice for most pediatric supracondylar humerus fractures. The pattern of pin placement can vary including 2 or 3 lateral pins, or 2 or 3 crossing pins with one inserted from the medial side. Care must be taken when placing a medial sided pin because it poses a risk to the ulnar nerve either by direct trauma or, more commonly, tethering around the pin. Due to this risk, some authors have recommended that all-lateral constructs be used as long as adequate stability can be obtained.

Ozçelik et al. reviewed 90 supracondylar humerus fractures treated with a crossed pin construct and measured the coronal and sagittal angles at which the medial pin was inserted compared to the occurrence of an ulnar nerve injury. They found a significant difference in the average sagittal angle of the pin between the two groups with the ulnar nerve injury group being higher (12.1° vs 1.6°). They concluded that inserting the pin in an anterior to posterior direction on the lateral projection increased the risk of iatrogenic injury to the ulnar nerve.

*Shim et al. reported on 63 supracondylar humerus fractures treated by first placing two lateral pins to obtain some stability.* *They then extended the elbow, palpated the ulnar nerve, and placed a medial pin to complete the construct.* No iatrogenic ulnar nerve injuries were noted in this group, and they authors stated that with proper technique, a crossed-pin construct can be done safely.

Figures A and B are AP and lateral radiographs of a type III supracondylar humerus fracture. Figures C and D are AP and lateral radiographs of the same fracture now reduced and stabilized with 2 lateral and 1 medial pin (labeled A).

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Inability to flex the interphalangeal joint of the thumb is consistent with an injury to the anterior interosseous nerve. This is a common nerve palsy secondary to the injury itself, but not due to placement of a medial pin.
Answer 3: Inability to extend the index finger would be consistent with a posterior interosseous nerve palsy.
Answer 4: Inability to flex the wrist would be consistent with a median nerve palsy.
Answer 5: Inability to flex the elbow would be consistent with an injury to the musculocutaneous nerve which would require injury well proximal to where the medial sided pin is placed.
