## Question

An athlete had been having chronic shoulder pain for which he had taken intra articular steroid injection a few months back. Pain settled for a while but now he has developed that same pain again, but now he has come to you. What will be best for him (FNB 2022)

## Options

1. Give another dose and retrograde file a TUE
2. Take permission first and only then give repeat injection
3. Decline to give the injection
4. IA steroids have recently been banned by WADA for any reason

## Correct option

**1. Give another dose and retrograde file a TUE**

## Answer explanation

Ans: A

Although no option looks perfect, the best answer is A.

WADA guidelines on Glucocorticoids (GC) use and Therapeutic Use Exemptions (TUE)

- As of the 2022 Prohibited List, GCs are prohibited In-competition when administered by all injectable, oral, or rectal routes.
- Examples of injectable routes of administration include intravenous, intramuscular, periarticular, intraarticular, peritendinous, intratendinous, epidural, intrathecal, intrabursal, intralesional (e.g., intrakeloid), intradermal, and subcutaneous.
- Examples of oral routes of administration include oromucosal, buccal, gingival, and sublingual routes.
- All other routes of administration including inhalation, intranasal spray, ophthalmological drops, perianal, dermal, dental intracanal application and topical applications are permitted at all times and do not require a TUE.

- An athlete risks being sanctioned when a GC, its metabolites or markers are found to exceed the laboratory reporting levels in a urine sample collected “in-competition”.
- “Out-of-competition” use of GCs, by any route, is not prohibited.
- An In-competition urine sample may contain evidence of GC use even though this took place out-of-competition, and an adverse analytical finding (AAF) may be reported.
- If the athlete and attending physician provide appropriate clinical justification for GC use, a retroactive TUE may be granted.
- However, if no TUE is granted, the AAF may lead to a sanction.

The given scenario: -

Now in the given case it is acknowledged that GC treatment is in response to an unpredictable exacerbation of chronic disease or an acute recurrent musculoskeletal condition. Since, GC has been used therapeutically, exemption through the TUE pathway is appropriate. In these cases, the TUE application will, of necessity, be retroactive. But keep in mind, GC use may be occurring outside the competition period, yet may still result in an in-competition AAF.
