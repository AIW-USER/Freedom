Chapter: Regional Orthopedics MCQs — Foot and Ankle Arthrodesis

## Question

True about ankle arthrodesis is ALL except

## Options

1. Ideal position for ankle arthrodesis is neutral flexion/extension, external rotation of 5 degrees or so, 5 degrees of valgus, and slight anterior translation of the talus under the tibia.
2. Entry point for retrograde intramedullary fixation of tibiocalcaneal arthrodesis is crossing point of bimalleolar bisector and a line in sagittal plane drawn from tip of second toe to center of heel
3. Ideal position for ankle arthrodesis is neutral flexion/extension, external rotation of 5 degrees or so, 5 degrees of valgus, and slight posterior translation of the talus under the tibia.
4. Subtalar arthritis is a common complication.

## Correct option

**1. Ideal position for ankle arthrodesis is neutral flexion/extension, external rotation of 5 degrees or so, 5 degrees of valgus, and slight anterior translation of the talus under the tibia.**

## Answer explanation

Ideal position for ankle arthrodesis is Common to all techniques is the desire to position the ankle in the proper orientation: neutral flexion/extension, external rotation of 5 degrees or so, 5 degrees of valgus, and slight posterior translation of the talus under the tibia. Although slight flexion may be tolerated, extension is not and may result in excessive pressure and intractable pain under the heel. Other positions to avoid are any varus and anterior translation of the talus because this may lead to a “vaulting” type of gait pattern and knee pain.

Method for estimating insertion site for retrograde intramedullary fixation of tibiocalcaneal arthrodesis. A, Line in sagittal plane drawn from tip of second toe to center of heel. B, Line can be drawn in the coronal plane bisecting medial malleolus. Insertion of lines indicates the correct entry portal for the nail.

Subtalar arthritis is a common complication after ankle arthrodesis.

Answer: 1
