## Question

A 68-year-old male with advanced osteoarthritis of the left hip is scheduled to undergo left total hip arthroplasty (THA). When counseling him on the options for the surgical approach, utilizing which superficial muscular interval has been associated with higher rates of femoral-sided revision due to loosening?

## Options

1. Gluteus medius and vastus lateralis split
2. Sartorius and rectus femoris
3. Tensor fascia lata and sartorius
4. Gluteus maximus split
5. Gluteus medius and tensor fascia lata

## Correct option

**3. Tensor fascia lata and sartorius**

## Answer explanation

The anterior approach for THA is associated with higher rates of femoral-sided revision and utilizes the superficial interval between the tensor fascia lata and sartorius muscles (Answer 3).

The anterior, anterolateral, lateral, and posterior approaches are well-established techniques in primary total hip arthroplasty, each offering unique benefits and challenges. While all four approaches have demonstrated effectiveness, it’s critical to recognize their differing complication profiles. The anterior approach is associated with a potential for higher rates of femoral-sided revisions related to loosening and periprosthetic fractures. The posterior approach may present an increased risk of posterior hip dislocation, whereas the lateral or anterolateral approaches are associated with a persistent postoperative limp or Trendeleburg gait. Understanding these nuances can help surgeons make informed decisions tailored to each patient's needs, ultimately improving outcomes.

*Hoskins et al. analyzed 122,345 THA from the Australian Orthopaedic Association Registry to compare revision rates based on surgical approach.* While the overall cumulative percent revision (CPR) was similar across posterior, lateral, and anterior approaches, the anterior approach showed higher rates of major revisions and femoral complications, specifically, periprosthetic fractures and loosening. **The authors conclude** that the anterior approach resulted in lower revision rates for dislocation and infection when compared to the posterior approach. Additionally, the anterior approach had fewer infections than the lateral approach within the first three months postoperatively. In contrast, the posterior approach exhibited the highest revision rate due to dislocations across all follow-up time points.

*Post et al. reviewed the indications, techniques, and results for the direct anterior approach for THA.* This group cites that the direct anterior approach (DAA) for THA, was originally described in the 19th century, and has regained popularity due to the demand for minimally invasive techniques. Advocates for this approach suggest that utilization of an intermuscular plane allows for a muscle-sparing technique that may promote faster recovery, though evidence supporting this claim is limited. While long-term outcomes are comparable to other approaches, the DAA has a steep learning curve with a risk of fractures and/or nerve damage that decreases with surgeon experience. **The authors conclude** that ongoing interest remains in whether the DAA offers better early outcomes and long-term results similar to other THA approaches.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: This represents the direct lateral approach, which is more commonly associated with a Trendelenburg gait due to violation of the abductors, as well as an increased risk for heterotopic ossification, rather than an increased risk of femoral-sided loosening.
Answer 2: The deep interval, rather than the superficial interval, of the direct anterior approach, involves the gluteus medius and the rectus femoris.
Answer 4: Splitting the gluteus maximus is involved in the posterior approach to the hip, which has been associated with an increased risk of dislocation, not femoral-sided loosening.
Answer 5: This interval represents the anterolateral approach, which is more frequently associated with post-operative limp than femoral-sided loosening, though its use has been associated with a lower dislocation rate than that of the posterior approach.
