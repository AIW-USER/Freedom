## Options

1. Bone marrow biopsy to be followed by bone marrow transplant for cure
2. Combination cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, vincristine, and prednisone therapy
3. CT scan of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis to look for primary tumor source
4. Hemipelvectomy
5. Radiation therapy and serial imaging to monitor the size of the lesions

## Correct option

**2. Combination cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, vincristine, and prednisone therapy**

## Answer explanation

Based on the imaging and biopsy findings, the patient has diffuse large B-cell lymphoma of bone, which is best treated utilizing multi-agent chemotherapy with or without radiation (Answer 2).

Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma is the most common lymphoma of bone, typically occurring in patients between the ages of 35 and 55 with regional pain associated with fever, night sweats, and weight loss. The diagnosis can be challenging, as plain radiographs are often unremarkable and needle biopsy is often non-diagnostic. When the biopsy is diagnostic, it shows mixed small round blue cell infiltrates that are CD20 and CD45 positive on immunohistochemical staining. The mainstay of treatment is multi-agent chemotherapy alone, which is effective for most lesions with a 70% 5-year survival in disseminated disease. However, radiation may be added to obtain local control in persistent disease.

*Rezazadeh et al. published a multicenter, retrospective study on the outcomes of limited-stage primary bone diffuse large B-cell lymphoma in the rituximab era.* *The authors reviewed 112 patients, including 78 who received chemotherapy and radiation (RT group) and 34 who received chemotherapy alone.* **They found** that the overall survival at 10 years was 77.9% in the RT group and 89.0% in the no RT group, and that the relapse-free survival at 10 years was 73.5% in the RT group and 80.3% in the no RT group. **They concluded** that neither improved overall survival nor relapse-free survival was associated with the addition of consolidative radiation therapy.

*Mikhaeel et al. provided a clinical review of primary bone lymphoma.* **The authors note** that primary bone lymphoma is a distinct clinical entity that accounts for 5% of extra-nodal lymphoma, with most patients having diffuse large B-cell lymphoma and presenting with bone pain, a mass, or both. **They also note** that though there are no prospective clinical studies on this disease, patients have been treated with radiotherapy, chemotherapy or a combination, with a trend towards improved outcome with combined modality treatment and further improvement with the addition of rituximab. **They conclude** that when treated as such, the prognosis of primary bone lymphoma is generally good.

Figures A-C are low AP pelvis, AP hip, and frog-leg lateral hip radiographs, respectively, showing involvement of the iliac wings, ischia, and proximal femur with numerous poorly defined regions of mixed lytic and blastic changes bilaterally. Figure F is a representative histology slide (H&E, 400x) showing effacement of the normal tissue architecture by a diffuse infiltrate of atypical, small round blue lymphoid cells.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Though a bone marrow aspiration and biopsy is indicated for staging, bone marrow transplant is not the primary treatment or cure for diffuse large B-cell lymphoma of bone.
Answer 3: Based on the biopsy results and immunohistochemical staining, the identity of the tumor is known. This is not metastatic disease of bone.
Answer 4: Hemipelvectomy is a salvage procedure for malignant lesions without other recourse, while B-cell lymphoma of bone is best treated with chemotherapy.
Answer 5: Radiation alone is insufficient for a malignant hematopoietic tumor such as this, but can be used as an adjunct to multi-agent chemotherapy to obtain local control in persistent disease.
