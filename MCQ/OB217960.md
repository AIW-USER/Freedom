## Question

A 68-year-old woman previously presented with chronic right knee pain. She had undergone physical therapy and received a cortisone injection to the right knee without pain relief. She subsequently underwent a total knee replacement. She now presents to clinic six months after her total knee replacement. The patient states that her pain was not changed by her operation. Examination of the right knee shows a well-healed surgical incision without erythema or drainage. She ambulates with a trendelenburg gait. Figures A and B are her pre-operative and post-operative bilateral knee films. What is the best next step?

## Options

1. Order inflammatory labs ( ESR, CRP, CBC)
2. Continued physical therapy
3. Order X-rays of the right hip
4. Revision of right total knee arthroplasty
5. Referral to a pain specialist

## Correct option

**3. Order X-rays of the right hip**

## Answer explanation

The patient in the vignette presents with knee pain potentially secondary to hip pathology. Evaluation of the ipsilateral hip is the necessary next step.

Atypical presentation of hip pain can pose a diagnostic challenge for clinicians. Concomitant hip pathology accounts for 2 to 29% of patients with knee pain. As such, hip pathology must be evaluated in patients presenting with knee pain with significant disability disproportionate to the severity of knee pathology, failure of symptomatic relief with conservative modalities (such as cortisone injections), and failure of symptomatic improvement with surgical intervention.

*Sakamoto et al. retrospectively reviewed 113 patients with hip joint arthritis who subsequently underwent hip arthroplasty and investigated the nerve distributions of the hip and knee joints in two cadavers.* Anterior knee pain was observed preoperatively in 13.3% of patients at rest and 33.3% during motion. **The authors reported** a decrease in the incidence of knee pain after hip arthroplasty. Anatomical analysis revealed an anteromedial innervation of the knee by the articular branches of the obturator and femoral nerve.

*Khan et al. investigated lower limb distribution of pain using body image maps in 60 patients awaiting primary hip replacement and in 60 patients awaiting spinal decompression for confirmed spinal stenosis.* *Patients with hip osteoarthritis had pain below the knee in 47% of cases.*

*Dibra et al. retrospectively reviewed 21 patients with knee pain that were ultimately diagnosed with hip pathology.* *Of the 21 patients, 16 patients required major assistive devices (including the use of a wheelchair), and 12 patients had undergone knee surgery with minimal to no relief of their pain.* *Of the 21 patients reviewed, 17 underwent a total hip arthroplasty, of which 14 had complete resolution of knee pain postoperatively.*

Figure A is a bilateral AP knee film demonstrating moderate bilateral knee arthritis. Figure 2 is an AP right X-ray demonstrating a well-fixed unconstrained knee prosthesis without evidence of loosening or gross malalignment or poor sizing.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1. Ordering inflammatory lab markers can be included as part of the diagnostic workup to rule out infection. However, an assessment of the ipsilateral hip should be conducted first, given her clinical examination and history of present illness.
Answer 2. This option is not appropriate as the patient has already tried a course of physical therapy without improvement
Answers 4 and 5. Evaluation of the ipsilateral hip must first be conducted to rule out hip pathology as the source of her knee pain.
