## Question

A 70-year-old male has had end-stage knee arthritis for the past several years. He is considering undergoing a total knee arthroplasty but has heard from his neighbor that the recovery is painful. The surgeon discusses his typical postoperative pain regimen. Which of the following is not an effective addition to modern evidence-based multimodal pain protocols?

## Options

1. Acetaminophen
2. Intra-articular pain catheter
3. Opioids
4. Gabapentin
5. Nonsteroidal anti-inflammatories

## Correct option

**2. Intra-articular pain catheter**

## Answer explanation

Intra-articular pain catheters have not been shown to be an effective component of a multimodal pain regimen. Typical components include acetaminophen, nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs), low-dose opioids, preoperative and/or intraoperative regional anesthesia, and gabapentinoids.

A multimodal pain regimen following total knee arthroplasty (TKA) involves a comprehensive approach to managing pain while minimizing opioid use and enhancing recovery. Preoperatively, patients may receive nerve blocks, such as adductor canal or femoral nerve blocks, combined with long-acting local anesthetics like ropivacaine. Intraoperatively, the surgeon may administer periarticular injections containing a mixture of local anesthetics, anti-inflammatory agents, and opioids. Postoperatively, a combination of non-opioid medications such as acetaminophen, NSAIDs, and neuromodulators (e.g., pregabalin or gabapentin) are often prescribed.

*Fitz and colleagues conducted a randomized controlled trial to evaluate the necessity of intra-articular pain catheters for postoperative pain management following TKA.* *The study found that adding an intra-articular pain catheter to a multimodal analgesic regimen, which included intraoperative periarticular injections, did not significantly improve 48-hour pain scores or reduce opioid consumption compared to periarticular injections alone.* **They concluded** that intra-articular pain catheters may be unnecessary when effective multimodal pain management strategies are employed during TKA.

*Li and colleagues performed a group-randomized trial to assess the impact of a postdischarge multimodal pain management (MMPM) regimen on patients following TKA.* *The study found that patients receiving the MMPM cocktail reported lower pain scores and reduced opioid consumption during the 30-day postoperative period compared to those on an opioid-only regimen.* These findings suggest that implementing a multimodal pain management approach after TKA can effectively decrease opioid use and enhance pain control in the early recovery phase.

Incorrect answers:
Answers 1, 3, 4, and 5: Acetaminophen, opioids, gabapentin, and NSAIDs are well-supported components of a multimodal pain control regimen.
