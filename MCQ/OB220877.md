## Question

A 12-year-old male presents to the emergency room with a Type 2 Salter-Harris distal tibia fracture. Which of the following choices best describes the anatomic region/structure that is most commonly damaged with this type of injury?

## Options

1. The region where lipids, glycogen, and proteoglycans are stored for future growth
2. The region closest to the epiphysis
3. A fibrous structure that stabilizes the physis and connects the epiphysis to metaphysis
4. The region where extracellular matrix calcification occurs
5. The region where the most extracellular matrix production occurs

## Correct option

**4. The region where extracellular matrix calcification occurs**

## Answer explanation

Extracellular matrix calcification occurs in the zone of provisional calcification, which is located in the hypertrophic zone. This is the physeal region most susceptible to shear forces, and thus, the area most commonly affected by a Type 2 Salter-Harris fracture (answer choice 4).

Pediatric patients are uniquely susceptible to injuries to the physis. Fractures of the distal tibia are common and are typically Type 2 Salter-Harris injuries (fractures through the physis that exit in the metaphysis). They are characterized by a Thurston-Holland fragment at the site where the fracture exits the metaphysis. Type 2 Salter-Harris fractures most commonly occur through the zone of provisional calcification, a layer of the hypertrophic zone that is characterized by extracellular matrix calcification.

*Podeszwa and Mubarak reviewed physeal fractures of the distal tibia and fibula.* *They review the thorough physical exam that must occur in these patients, appropriate imaging, treatment strategies for non-displaced and displaced Salter-Harris fractures, and the incidence of physeal closure as well as its relationship to injury severity.* **They concluded** that significant displacement after closed reduction warrants surgical intervention to minimize the risk of physeal arrest in these patients and that extended follow-up is needed to provide adequate surveillance for this complication.

*Kay and Mathys published a review on the evaluation and treatment of pediatric ankle fractures.* *They cover the anatomy of the ankle and distal tibial physis, pertinent physical exam, and treatment algorithms for specific Salter-Harris injuries/ankle injuries.* *They additionally review complications following these ankle injuries, which include growth arrest, post-traumatic arthritis, ankle stiffness, and reflex sympathetic dystrophy.* **They concluded** that a good understanding of an individual’s fracture pattern is paramount to tailoring treatment/closed reduction strategies and that inadequate closed reduction places patients at a high risk for complications, especially physeal arrest, and warrants surgical intervention. Nevertheless, the patient should still be monitored for growth disturbances postoperatively.

Incorrect answers:
Answers 1 and 2: These describe the resting zone of the physis, which is not the physeal region most commonly affected in a Type 2 Salter-Harris fracture.
Answer 3: This best describes the Ring of La Croix, which is not typically injured in Type 2 Salter-Harris fractures but can be affected in Type 6 Salter-Harris fractures.
Answer 5: This describes the proliferative zone, which is not the physeal region most commonly affected in a Type 2 Salter-Harris fracture.
