## Options

1. Absent dystrophin protein
2. Duplication on chromosome 17
3. PITX1 gene mutation
4. Posterior tibial tendon insufficiency
5. Tarsal coalition

## Correct option

**2. Duplication on chromosome 17**

## Answer explanation

Figure A demonstrates a fifth metatarsal stress fracture and Figure B demonstrates a mild cavus deformity. These findings may occur with Charcot-Marie-Tooth Disease (CMT), which is caused by a duplication of a gene on chromosome 17 that codes for peripheral myelin protein 22 (PMP22).

Patients with hindfoot varus overload the lateral column of their foot, making them predisposed to fifth metatarsal stress fractures. This may also be seen in patients with a cavus deformity as it also causes hindfoot varus. The fracture is usually located distal to the 4/5 metatarsal articulation. Patients with CMT disease develop a cavovarus deformity of their foot which would, therefore, predispose them to fifth metatarsal stress fractures.

*Raikin et al. performed a retrospective review of 20 patients treated with a Jones fracture.* **They found** that clinical varus was found in 16 patients while 18 patients had radiographic measurements indicating hindfoot varus. **They conclude** that a majority of patients sustaining a Jones fracture have evidence of a varus hindfoot, which may be a predisposing factor to develop the fracture.

*Leumann et al. performed a retrospective review of patients who had intramedullary screw fixation in proximal fifth metatarsal fractures.* **They report** that stress fractures are related to lateral column overload that may be caused by a cavus or hindfoot varus deformity. **They conclude** that intramedullary screw fixation of this injury leads to a bony union and complete return to sport.

Figure A is a radiograph demonstrating a stress fracture of the fifth metatarsal. Figure B is a clinical photograph demonstrating a mild cavus deformity. Illustration A is a lateral radiograph demonstrating a cavovarus foot.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: An absent dystrophin protein causes muscular dystrophy which is associated with an equinovarus foot.
Answer 3: A PITX1 gene mutation has been associated with clubfoot.
Answer 4: Posterior tendon insufficiency is the most common cause of adult acquired flatfoot deformity.
Answer 5: A tarsal coalition has not been found to be associated with stress fractures of the fifth metatarsal.
