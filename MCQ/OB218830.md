## Question

A 75-year-old female with an extensive cardiac history and osteoporosis presents to the emergency department with complaints of left hip pain after a fall at her assisted living facility. Radiographs demonstrate an intertrochanteric hip fracture with approximately 2 cm of subtrochanteric extension. What surgical option would provide the best combination of fracture stability, ability for early weight bearing, and shortest operative duration?

## Options

1. Sliding hip screw
2. Proximal femoral locking plate
3. Cemented arthroplasty with revision femoral stem
4. Short cephalomedullary nailing
5. Long cephalomedullary nailing

## Correct option

**4. Short cephalomedullary nailing**

## Answer explanation

A short cephalomedullary nail is the best treatment choice in this scenario and can be used for intertrochanteric (IT) hip fractures with subtrochanteric extension up to 3-4cm.

Intertrochanteric hip fractures are very common in the elderly population. They can be stable or unstable depending on the degree of posteromedial comminution, lateral wall integrity, fracture obliquity, and amount of subtrochanteric extension. Cephalomedullary nailing is the most commonly indicated treatment for these fractures. Recent debate has ensued regarding the indications for short intramedullary nailing vs. long intramedullary nailing. Studies have demonstrated the ability of short intramedullary nailing to maintain fracture stability without significant toggling of the proximal fragment in cases of up to 3-4 cm of subtrochanteric extension. With more distal subtrochanteric extension, long cephalomedullary nailing, which is statically locked distally, should be utilized to provide a longer lever arm to resist fracture displacement.

*Lee et al. looked at IT fractures with distal extension and the efficacy of using a short intramedullary nail.* **They noted** that construct strength decreased rapidly when the fracture extended>40 mm into the subtrochanteric region. For this reason, **they recommended** long intramedullary nailing with fractures extending distal to this region and noted that a short intramedullary nail might be biomechanically acceptable proximal to this level.

*Shannon et al. performed a randomized control trial evaluating functional and clinical outcomes of short and long cephalomedullary nails.* *At a mean 14-month follow-up in 168 patients, the short and long nail cohorts were comparable concerning outcome measures.* The short-nail cohort did offer shorter operative times. Still, it did not have an increased rate of implant cut-out, infection, or peri-implant fracture when used with fractures extending within 3 cm of the subtrochanteric region.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: A sliding hip screw is inappropriate for unstable IT hip fractures or those with subtrochanteric extension.
Answer 2: A proximal femoral locking plate offers a fixed angle construct but would not allow early weight bearing.
Answer 3: Cemented arthroplasty would not be the best initial option in a patient with an unstable IT fracture. If an intramedullary nail were to fail, arthroplasty would be a possible solution.
Answer 5: Long intramedullary nailing would be a reasonable option in this fracture pattern, but operative duration would be longer than that of a short intramedullary nail.
