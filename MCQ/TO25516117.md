## Question

A 12-year-old boy with genetically confirmed XLH has been managed with conventional therapy (oral phosphate salts and calcitriol) since infancy. Routine renal ultrasonography reveals bilateral medullary nephrocalcinosis. What is the most appropriate management strategy to address this iatrogenic complication while continuing to treat the underlying bone disease?

## Options

1. Discontinue calcitriol entirely and maximize oral cholecalciferol.
2. Add a loop diuretic to increase calcium excretion.
3. Transition the patient to Burosumab therapy.
4. Increase the dose of oral phosphate salts to bind excess renal calcium.

## Correct option

**3. Transition the patient to Burosumab therapy.**

## Answer explanation

Burosumab treats the root cause (FGF23 excess) without requiring high-dose oral phosphate and active vitamin D, thereby halting or potentially reversing the progression of nephrocalcinosis caused by conventional therapy.

Answer: 3
