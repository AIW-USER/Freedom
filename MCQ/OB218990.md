## Question

You are on your orthopedic trauma rotation at a busy Level 1 trauma center. You are consulted multiple times over the weekend on patients that have sustained gunshot wounds (GSW). Of the following, which treatment is appropriate for the respective scenario?

## Options

1. Administration of intravenous antibiotics without arthrotomy for a bullet lodged in the iliac wing after passing through the left hip with no intra-abdominal injury
2. Administration of intravenous antibiotics and local wound care for a retained intra-articular bullet of the hip
3. Administration of intravenous antibiotics without arthrotomy for a trans-abdominal GSW with the bullet lodged in the femoral neck
4. Arthrotomy for a bullet lodged in the iliac wing after passing through the left hip with no intra-abdominal injury
5. Local wound care with primary closure of a GSW through the forearm musculature with no fractures or neurovascular injury

## Correct option

**1. Administration of intravenous antibiotics without arthrotomy for a bullet lodged in the iliac wing after passing through the left hip with no intra-abdominal injury**

## Answer explanation

This patient sustained an intra-articular gunshot wound (GSW) without other intra-articular pathology. In the absence of intra-articular pathology, intra-articular gunshot injuries do not appear to necessitate surgical debridement to decrease the risk of infection and may be treated with local wound care, tetanus, and IV antibiotics.

Gun shot wounds (GSWs) are high energy injuries that contribute to extensive soft tissue damage and comminuted bony fractures. Nonoperative management with local wound care, tetanus prophylaxis +/- short course of oral antibiotics is indicated in low-velocity injury with no bone involvement or non-operative fractures. Generally, primary closure of the wound is contraindicated. Antibiotic use is somewhat controversial, however, low-velocity intra-articular (IA) GSWs may be effectively managed with antibiotics. The incidence of infection after IA gunshot injuries is low with the routine use of antibiotic prophylaxis. Moreover, in the absence of IA pathology, IA gunshot injuries do not appear to necessitate surgical debridement to decrease the risk of infection.

*Nguyen et al. retrospectively reviewed the demographics, interventions, infection rates, and other complications after intra-articular (IA) gunshot wounds.* **They reported** that the incidence of infection after IA gunshot injuries is low with the routine use of antibiotic prophylaxis. **They concluded** that in the absence of IA pathology, IA gunshot injuries do not appear to necessitate surgical debridement to decrease the risk of infection.

*Nguyen et al. retrospectively reviewed the overall treatment costs associated with isolated low-energy GSWs to the extremity and the estimate cost savings associated with a single-dose IV antibiotic strategy administered in the emergency room for patients with simple GSWs.* **They reported** that the overall cost associated with isolated low-energy GSWs to the extremity is high. **They concluded** that limiting antibiotics to a single IV dose in the emergency room can reduce treatment expenses substantially for patients with simple GSWs.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: An arthrotomy is indicated for intra-articular bullet as it may lead to local inflammation, arthritis and lead intoxication (plumbism).
Answer 3: Trans-abdominal intra-articular GSWs are considered contaminated. An arthrotomy is indicated in these cases. For GSWs with trans-abdominal trajectories, the laparotomy takes precedence over arthrotomy.
Answer 4: Local wound care, tetanus prophylaxis, and administration of IV antibiotics have been showed to be appropriate for low-velocity intra-articular GSWs. In the absence of intra-articular pathology, intra-articular gunshot injuries do not appear to necessitate surgical debridement to decrease the risk of infection.
Answer 5: Primary closure of the GSW is contraindicated.
