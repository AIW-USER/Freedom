## Question

A patient presents to your office with complaints of hand numbness. He is diagnosed with a peripheral neuropathy. When discussing potential sites of nerve compression, abnormal sensation in which of the following areas depicted in Figure A would suggest compression caused by a ganglion cyst located between the transverse carpal ligament and the volar carpal ligaments?

![[q217953-a-derm-2.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. A only
2. A and C
3. C only
4. D only
5. C and D

## Correct option

**3. C only**

## Answer explanation

A ganglion cyst between the transverse carpal ligament and the volar carpal ligaments would be located with Guyon's canal and cause ulnar tunnel syndrome. Contrary to cubital tunnel syndrome, compression at Guyon's canal will spare the dorsal ulnar hand.

Ulnar tunnel syndrome is a compressive neuropathy of the ulnar nerve at the level of the wrist (Guyon's canal), most commonly due to a ganglion cyst. Diagnosis can be made clinically with paresthesia of the small and ring finger with intrinsic weakness with a Tinel's sign over Guyon's canal. Patients with cubital tunnel syndrome have numbness on the dorsal ulnar aspect of the hand due to involvement of the dorsal ulnar sensory nerve branch (DUSN), whereas patients with Guyon's neuropathy do not have symptoms of numbness in the dorsal ulnar distribution, because the DUSN branch arises more proximally in the forearm and is not compressed in the ulnar tunnel at the wrist. Treatment involves a course of conservative management with splinting, and surgical decompression in the presence of a compressive lesion (i.e ganglion) or continued symptoms.

*Chen et al. reviewed ulnar neuropathy at or distal to the wrist, the so-called ulnar tunnel syndrome.* **They reported** that paresthesias may be nonspecific or related to coexisting pathologies, such as carpal tunnel syndrome, cubital tunnel syndrome, thoracic outlet syndrome, C8-T1 radiculopathy, or peripheral neuropathy, which makes accurate diagnosis challenging. **They concluded** that the advances in electrodiagnosis, ultrasonography, computed tomography, and magnetic resonance imaging have improved diagnostic accuracy.

*Kenney et al. reviewed a detailed examination of the hand.* **They reported** that a detailed examination includes vascular, sensory, and motor assessments. **They concluded** that evaluations could correctly identify specific hand diseases and injuries.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1&2: Ulnar tunnel syndrome should not cause an abnormal sensation in the median nerve distribution.
Answers 4&5: Ulnar tunnel syndrome typically spares the dorsal ulnar hand. Cubital tunnel syndrome would be expected to also cause an abnormal sensation in this area.
