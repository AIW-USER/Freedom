## Question

A 72-year-old male presents with atraumatic hip pain. He previously enjoyed playing golf and hiking but has recently been limited in these activities. He is considering undergoing total hip arthroplasty (THA) but would like to know more about the type of implants you use and any associated risks. You discuss fretting corrosion as a risk of THA. Which of the following is true of fretting corrosion?

## Options

1. Caused by fatigue failure due to differences in oxygen tension
2. More likely to occur with the use of a ceramic head
3. Mixed metal neck-stem combinations are the leading cause
4. Severity is not time dependent
5. Caused by small motion at modular junctions

## Correct option

**5. Caused by small motion at modular junctions**

## Answer explanation

Fretting corrosion is caused by micromotion between metals at the implant's modular junctions.

Fretting corrosion is one of several types of corrosion which are particularly relevant to joint arthroplasty, especially in reference to modular implants in total hip arthroplasty (THA). In this case, the micromotion at any modular junction (head/neck, neck/stem) leads to metal corrosion products that can cause local osteolysis and synovitis. This production of metal ions can lead to increased serum concentrations of cobalt and chromium as well as local tissue reactions. Fretting corrosion tends to worsen over time and can eventually lead to component failure at modular junctions.

*Shenoy et al. looked at metal-on-metal acetabular shell/taper liners and their susceptibility to corrosion.* *They applied cyclic loads into the acetabular shell/taper at a physiologic angle and measured the amount of fretting corrosion that occurred.* Interestingly, **they noted** relatively low levels of corrosion for all metal types examined, and noted no differences between the metals. **They conclude** that metal acetabular designs may not be a significant contributor to fretting corrosion.

*Siljander et al. reviewed fretting corrosion and its contributors at the femoral head/neck taper interface.* *They evaluated 92 cases and noted that males had a higher degree of corrosion, as did those who were younger at revision and had an increased body mass.* **They also noted** a weak correlation with smaller femoral head sizes and increased offset being associated with more fretting corrosion and trunnionosis.

*Civinini et al. reviewed the risk of fretting corrosion in patients who received dual mobility implants during revision THA.* **They postulated** that fretting corrosion occurs at the interface of the acetabular component and Co-Cr liner and noted a significant correlation between increased serum metal ions and dual mobility implants, indicative of corrosion. They finish by saying this increased risk of fretting corrosion must be considered when deciding when to use dual mobility implants, especially in younger, more active individuals.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Crevice corrosion is caused by differences in oxygen tension between metals and can lead to fatigue cracking and failure.
Answer 2: Ceramic heads can actually decrease the amount of fretting corrosion as this eliminates a source of metal/metal contact at the head/neck junction.
Answer 3: Mixed metal-metal neck/stem combinations most commonly cause galvanic corrosion as there is an electrochemical energy transfer between these dissimilar metals.
Answer 4: The severity of fretting corrosion will worsen over time as the amount of micromotion continues to increase at the stem's modular junctions.
