## Question

A 27-year-old male presents to clinic for evaluation of his right hand. The patient suffered a traumatic laceration of his ulnar nerve at the level of the wrist several months ago. The patient works as a mechanic and is having difficulty with work secondary to functional deficits of the hand. On exam, there is clawing present. The patient is unable to actively extend the affected digits at the proximal interphalangeal joint (PIPJ). While asking the patient to extend the digits, you perform the maneuver demonstrated in Figure A and the patient is still unable to actively extend at the PIPJs. However, you are able to fully passively extend the PIPJs while holding the patient’s hand/wrist in the same position. Which of the following would most likely benefit this patient?

*Figure A*

## Options

1. MCPJ volar plate advancement capsulodesis
2. Long digital flexor tendon lengthening
3. Serial casting of the PIPJ
4. Partial release of the A1 and A2 pulleys
5. FDS transfer to lateral bands

## Correct option

**5. FDS transfer to lateral bands**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a complex claw hand secondary to an extensor mechanism that is overly stretched out, as demonstrated by a negative Bouvier test. Therefore, a dynamic tendon transfer not only prevents the clawing, but also assists with PIPJ extension and would be the most beneficial to this patient of the answers listed above.

There are a plethora of surgical techniques described for claw correction, and Bouvier maneuver is used to help determine the most appropriate surgical options. In the case of paralysis of the intrinsic muscles of the hand with claw deformation, the Bouvier maneuver is performed via the examiner preventing MCPJ hyperextension to evaluate if active IP extension is possible through the long extensor(s). The patient is first asked to try to fully extend all the fingers with the wrist in the neutral position. If the examiner stabilizes the dorsal aspect of the proximal phalanx to prevent hyperextension at the MCP joint (via passively flexing MCPJ about 10 degrees), and the patient is now able to fully extend the fingers, this is classified as a positive Bouvier. The patient would be referred to as having a simple claw hand. The maneuver is deemed negative when the active extension of the PIPJ/DIPJ is not possible despite the prevention of hyperextension of MCPJ, and this is referred to as a complex claw hand.

*Muzykewicz et al. performed a cadaver-based biomechanical study comparing grasp kinematics between 2 intrinsic balancing procedures, the Zancolli-lasso and the House procedure.* *The intrinsic muscles of 12 cadaver hands were reconstructed by either the Zancolli-lasso or the House procedure.* **They reported** the Zancolli-lasso reconstructed hands flexed first in the IP joints, and then in MCP joints, resembling an unreconstructed intrinsic-minus hand whereas the House reconstructed hands flexed first in the MCP joints and then in the IP joints, resembling an intrinsic-activated hand. **They concluded** both intrinsic balancing techniques improved grasp but advocate the more physiological House procedure for restoration of intrinsic function in tetraplegic patients.

Sammer and Chung provide part II of their review article series on tendon transfers for ulnar nerve palsy and median nerve palsy. This article discusses the use of tendon transfer procedures for the treatment of median and ulnar nerve palsy and combined nerve palsies. They also report postoperative management and potential complications.

Figure A is demonstrating a lack of PIPJ extension with Bouvier’s maneuver signifying a complex claw hand.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: If this patient could extend at the PIPJ with the Bouvier’s maneuver (i.e., simple claw), this would indicate a positive test and that the extensor mechanism is intact and not over-stretched. In these scenarios, a static operation that only prevents hyperextension at the MCP joint would correct the claw deformity, such as a MCPJ volar plate advancement capsulodesis.
Answers 2 and 3: Given that the patient has full passive extension of the PIPJ with the wrist at neutral, it is unlikely that either lengthening of the long digital flexor tendons or serial casting of the PIPJ would provide any benefit. Full extension of the PIPJ with the wrist in flexion would signify shortened flexors. Lack of extension at the PIPJ with the wrist in flexion would signify a tight PIPJ.
Answer 4: Partial release of the A1 and A2 pulleys would allow bowstringing of the flexor tendons, which results in increasing the moment arm of the flexor tendons at the MCPJ, thereby preventing MCPJ hyperextension. This could be used to improve a simple claw, similar to answer choice 1.
