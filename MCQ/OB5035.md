## Question

A 13-month-old patient is being evaluated for late recognition of septic arthritis. A known risk factor for the development of pediatric septic arthritis is the presence of intra-articular metaphysis. All of the following joints have intra-articular metaphysis EXCEPT?

## Options

1. Ankle
2. Elbow
3. Hip
4. Knee
5. Shoulder

## Correct option

**4. Knee**

## Answer explanation

Bones with an intra-articular metaphyses include the proximal humerus, proximal radius, proximal femur, and distal fibula/tibia. The presence of an intra-articular metaphysis is a known risk factor for septic arthropathy in pediatric patients.

Septic arthritis is a true orthopedic emergency and the damage to the joint can be extensive. As in the case of pediatric septic arthritis of the shoulder, drainage of the shoulder is indicated. However, the methodology of drainage in shoulder sepsis remains controversial; aspiration, open drainage, and arthroscopic lavage have all been recommended. Irrespective of treatment modality, drainage is indicated to prevent further cartilage damage. The damage to the cartilage may be irreversible and can occur within 8 hours.

*Lossos et al. review shoulder septic arthritis.* **They found** that associated infections were more prevalent in the pediatric population and that elevated body temperature (> 38 degrees C) appeared in over 90% of the pediatric patients. Moreover, blood cultures were positive in 90% of pediatric patients and gram-negative bacilli were more prevalent in the pediatric population, especially the neonates. **They concluded** that pyogenic shoulder arthritis should first be treated with intravenous antibiotics, and in the pediatric population, a closed needle aspiration, if needed at all, is the optimal treatment.

*Forward et al. review the operative treatment of septic arthritis of the shoulder in infants.* **They report** that after initial aspiration of the joint, an arthroscope was inserted using the posterior approach and washout was performed under direct vision and complete clearance of pus allowed assessment of the inflammation and the damage to articular cartilage. **They concluded** that the procedure was minimally invasive and gave excellent cosmesis without compromising care.

*Smith et al. prospectively reviewed 61 children in Malawi with septic arthritis of the shoulder who underwent either aspiration or arthrotomy.* **They report** that of the 33 radiographs available for review at follow-up at six months, 70% showed evidence of glenohumeral damage with no statistical difference in radiological outcomes for the two groups. **They concluded** that there was no statistical difference in the clinical outcome for the two treatment groups at any stage during the period of follow-up.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1-3, 5: All these joints have intra-articular metaphysis. The presence of intra-articular metaphysis can be remembered with the acronym “SHEA” (Shoulder, Hip, Elbow, Ankle).
