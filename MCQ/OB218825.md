## Question

An 83-year-old female sustains a comminuted distal humerus fracture, depicted in Figures A and B, that is treated with open reduction and internal fixation. She presents five weeks postoperatively and radiographs reveal pull-out of screws in the distal humerus with intra-articular migration of the screws. Which of the following is the best treatment option that permits early rehabilitation and similar outcomes of a primary total elbow arthroplasty for this injury?

*Figure A*

![[q218825-b-figure-b-edit.jpg]]
*Figure B*

## Options

1. Revision fixation
2. Removal of hardware and "bag of bones" technique
3. Elbow arthroplasty
4. Elbow arthrodesis
5. Removal of hardware after fracture union

## Correct option

**3. Elbow arthroplasty**

## Answer explanation

Salvage total elbow arthroplasty is associated with similar functional outcomes as primary total arthroplasty for comminuted distal humerus fractures.

Distal humerus fractures in elderly patients are challenging fractures to treat. Osteoporosis and fracture comminution make fixation a challenge with open reduction and internal fixation techniques. This becomes especially problematic in post-op rehabilitation when regaining range of motion. Total elbow arthroplasty may provide a more stable elbow that permits range of motion rehabilitation post-operatively. Although, elbow prostheses come with 5 lb weight-bearing restrictions for life which limits indications to elderly, low-demand patients. TEA as a salvage operation to failed internal fixation is associated with similar functional outcomes and range of motion as primary TEA.

*Githens et al. performed a systematic review and meta-analysis comparing total elbow arthroplasty (TEA) and open reduction internal fixation (ORIF) for the treatment of geriatric distal humerus fractures.* **They reported** there were no significant differences in functional outcomes and range of motion in patients treated with either construct but a slightly higher reoperation with ORIF. **They concluded** TEA and ORIF of distal humerus fractures in geriatric patients result in similar outcomes.

*Logli et al. retrospectively studied the outcomes of distal humerus fractures treated with primary TEA or TEA after failed internal fixation.* **They reported** no difference in Mayo Elbow Performance Scores, motion, and reoperation rates in either group. **They concluded** that salvage TEA can provide similar outcomes as primary TEA, but with higher aseptic loosening and deep infection rates.

Figures A and B are the AP and lateral radiographs of the left elbow with a comminuted intra-articular distal humerus fracture.

Incorrect answers
Answer 1: Revision ORIF can be performed for this fracture, but is unlikely to be successful given the patient's poor bone quality and failed fixation previously.
Answer 2: Nonoperative treatment of comminuted distal humerus fractures in elderly patients, often termed the "bag of bones" technique, can produce good outcomes with a similar range of motion. After failed internal fixation, providing a stable elbow that allows for range of motion and rehabilitation should be prioritized.
Answer 4: Elbow arthrodesis is a salvage option for failed ORIF, but does not allow for range of motion, which may impair the patient's function.
Answer 5: Waiting for fracture union in this patient is an option, but may not provide a stable elbow for further rehabilitation. There is also a risk of soft tissue injury with prominent hardware with fixation failure.
