## Question

A 14-year-old skeletally immature male presents with persistent knee pain despite 6 months of strict non-weight-bearing and bracing for a classic medial femoral condyle OCD lesion. Repeat MRI reveals a 400 mm² lesion with a high-intensity T2 fluid line completely circumscribing the osteochondral fragment, but no loose body is identified in the joint space. Diagnostic arthroscopy confirms the cartilage overlying the lesion is ballotable but minimally displaced. According to the latest treatment algorithms, what is the most appropriate surgical management?

## Options

1. Transarticular multiple drilling alone to promote angiogenesis.
2. In situ fixation of the fragment using bioabsorbable screws, darts, or compression screws.
3. Excision of the fragment followed by microfracture of the defect base.
4. Osteochondral autograft transfer (OATS) from a non-weight-bearing surface.

## Correct option

**2. In situ fixation of the fragment using bioabsorbable screws, darts, or compression screws.**

## Answer explanation

Drilling alone is indicated for stable lesions (no fluid behind the fragment on MRI, firm on probing) that have failed conservative management. Because this lesion has a circumscribing fluid line and is ballotable, it is unstable and requires stabilization, not just drilling. So option A is not correct.

This question describes an unstable (fluid circumscribing the lesion, ballotable cartilage) but salvageable/hinged fragment in a skeletally immature patient. The standard of care is to salvage the native articular cartilage via primary internal fixation (with or without bone grafting) to achieve union.

Answer: 2
