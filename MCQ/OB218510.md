## Question

A 32-year-old male construction worker presents with severe back pain and right lower extremity pain after lifting a 100-lb bag of concrete. He has tried physical therapy for 6 weeks and an epidural injection with brief but effective pain relief. The patient inquires about surgical treatment for his condition. Which of the following statements is true regarding surgical and nonsurgical treatment for lumbar disc herniations?

## Options

1. Patients treated with surgery have significantly less back pain than those not treated surgically
2. Studies reporting intent-to-treat analysis favor nonsurgical treatment with respect to ODI scores
3. Nonsurgical treatment has little effect on long term sciatica symptoms
4. Studies evaluating as-treated analyses demonstrate significant benefit of surgery regarding pain, function, and satisfaction
5. Surgical treatment has lower return to work rates than nonsurgical treatment

## Correct option

**4. Studies evaluating as-treated analyses demonstrate significant benefit of surgery regarding pain, function, and satisfaction**

## Answer explanation

As-treated analyses have demonstrated a significant benefit of surgery with respect to pain, function, and patient satisfaction.

Lumbar disc herniations are a common cause of lower back pain and radiculopathy in relatively young patients. Pain often starts with lifting a heavy object with a flexed lumbar spine which leads to tearing of the annulus fibrosis and herniation of the nucleus pulposus into the spinal canal or foramen. Nonsurgical treatment can be effective in most cases as the body resorbs the herniated disc material spontaneously. However, patients with intractable pain, significant weakness, or persistent symptoms beyond 6 weeks may benefit from surgical treatment. Studies have revealed a significant benefit of surgical treatment that lasts out to 4-years with respect to pain relief, patient function, and patient satisfaction.

*Atlas et al. performed a prospective cohort study comparing 5-year outcomes of surgical and nonsurgical treatment of lumbar disc herniations.* **They reported** that 70% of patients treated initially with surgery had improvement in their predominant symptoms compared to 56% of patients treated nonsurgically. **They concluded** surgical treatment for lumbar disc herniation is associated with greater improvement in symptoms than nonsurgical treatment at 5 years.

*Weinstein et al. performed a concurrent prospective and randomized observational study comparing the outcomes of surgical and nonsurgical treatment of lumbar disc herniation at 4-year follow-up.* The as-treated analysis revealed a significantly greater improvement in primary outcome measures, but the intent-to-treat analysis favored surgery at all time points without significance, despite being fraught with a high patient cross-over rate. **The authors concluded** the as-treated analysis demonstrates significant improvement in pain, function, and satisfaction at 4-years follow-up.

Incorrect answers
Answer 1: Surgical treatment has less back pain improvement at 6 weeks post-op due to recovery from the surgical site pain.
Answer 2: Intent-to-treat analysis of the SPORT study favored surgery with respect to SF-36 and ODI scores, but was not statistically significant at all time points. The high study cross-over rate is believed to be the reason for a lack of significance.
Answer 3: Patients treated nonoperatively for lumbar disc herniations can experience significant improvement in radicular pain at long-term follow-up.
Answer 5: Return to work rates are similar between surgical and nonsurgical treatment.
