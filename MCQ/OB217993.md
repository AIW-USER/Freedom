## Question

A 32-year-old construction worker presents with severe hand pain after a crush injury from a large stone. The affected hand was pinned beneath the object for 45 minutes. Radiographs of the hand do not reveal any fractures. The orthopedic service is consulted for concern of hand compartment syndrome. Which of the following would most support a diagnosis of acute hand compartment syndrome?

## Options

1. Loss of vibratory sensation
2. Finger compartment pressure of 15 mm Hg
3. Swelling and pain out of proportion
4. Intrinsic plus positioning
5. Cool and pale appearing hand

## Correct option

**3. Swelling and pain out of proportion**

## Answer explanation

Extensive swelling and pain out of proportion with the exam are findings consistent with acute compartment syndrome of the hand.

Hand compartment syndrome is the result of significant swelling of the soft tissues leading to impaired venous return. If unrecognized there can be devastating clinical sequelae. Diagnosis is confirmed by clinical examination with suggestive findings of excessive swelling, pain out of proportion with the exam, pain reproduced with passive MCP stretching, and intrinsic minus positioning. Once diagnosed, a fasciotomy should be performed to decompress the compartments of the hand.

*Lipschitz and Lifchez reviewed the measurement of compartment pressures in the hand and forearm.* **They stated** patients who are unable to participate in a physical exam should undergo invasive compartment pressure measurement testing. **They conclude** that the clinical examination can be altered in patients with median or ulnar nerve injuries and suggest performing compartment measurements at the level of the heart.

*Codding et al. reviewed acute compartment syndrome of the hand.* The stated diagnosis can be made clinically with intrinsic minus positioning, extensive swelling, pain reproduced with passive MCP motion, or intracompartmental pressure of 30 mm Hg. **They concluded** a low threshold should be maintained for performing compartment releases to preserve tissue metabolism in the affected hand.

Incorrect answers
Answer 1: Loss of vibratory sensation in the hand is not a specific finding that is concerning for hand compartment syndrome.
Answer 2: Compartment pressure measurements are not typically performed for the fingers.
Answer 4: Patients with hand compartment syndrome often position in intrinsic minus.
Answer 5: A cool and pale-appearing hand is a late finding and is more indicative of an arterial injury.
