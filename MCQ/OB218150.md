## Question

A 12-year-old gymnast sustains an injury during practice and presents to the emergency department with the injury shown in Figure A. She undergoes nonoperative treatment. What is her expected outcome, compared to a younger patient with a similar injury?

![[q218150-a-n-h4.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Higher risk of nonunion
2. More likely to return to preinjury activity level
3. Higher risk of progressive displacement
4. Increased risk of injury-related limitations in daily activities
5. No difference in risk of injury-related limitations in daily activities

## Correct option

**4. Increased risk of injury-related limitations in daily activities**

## Answer explanation

Figure A is a radiograph of a pediatric Neer-Horowitz Type IV proximal humerus fracture. Older patients near skeletal maturity have a higher risk of a less than desirable outcome with nonoperative treatment compared to younger patients sustaining similar injuries, which includes injury-related limitations in daily activities.

The pediatric proximal humerus has tremendous remodeling potential, as the proximal physis contributes 80% of longitudinal growth, averaging 7mm per year. *Neer and Horowitz developed a classification system for proximal humerus fractures based on the degree of displacement: Type I (minimal, <5mm displacement), Type II (<1/3 shaft width displacement), Type III (1/3 to 2/3 shaft width displacement), and Type IV (>2/3 shaft width displacement).* Most proximal humerus fractures in the pediatric population are amenable to nonoperative treatment, and outcomes are usually excellent without surgical intervention, owing both to the remodeling potential and range of motion of the shoulder joint itself. However, older patients with type III and IV fractures have an increased risk of reporting less than desirable outcomes following nonoperative treatment.

*Pahlavan et al. performed a 2010 systematic review of the literature over a 50-year period on proximal humerus fracture in children ages 0-18 years.* The vast majority of patients in the studies examined returned to unimpeded function without major complications; however, there were reports of post-injury stiffness, pain, weakness, nerve symptoms, physeal injury, and functional limitations. Interestingly, earlier papers almost uniformly recommended nonoperative treatment, whereas articles published in the 2000s recommended surgery for fractures with residual displacement, particularly for older children.

*Chaus et al. performed a 2015 retrospective study comparing treatment outcomes among skeletally immature patients who underwent operative versus nonoperative treatment for Neer-Horowitz Type III or IV fractures.* A less than desirable outcome was defined as subjects who met ≥1 of the following adverse criteria: (1) developed a clinically relevant complication; (2) reported that, during the past week, their work or regular daily activities were limited by their shoulder injury; and (3) reported the presence of shoulder pain during the past week. **The authors found** no statistical difference in clinical outcome or functional status between the operative and nonoperative groups. In a secondary analysis of the nonoperative fractures, there was a statistically significant association between age and the development of a poor outcome. More importantly, the probability of a less than desirable clinical outcome increased substantially in patients older than 12 years of age who were treated nonoperatively.

Lähdeoja et al. published a 2023 retrospective cohort study investigating the course of primary treatment for Neer-Horowitz Type III and IV proximal humerus fractures sustained in adolescence and the long-term outcomes in adulthood, comparing the outcomes with those from age-matched controls. After a mean follow-up of 13.1 years, DASH, Simple Shoulder Test, and 15D quality-of-life scores showed no impairment compared with age-matched normal values. Additionally, there were no differences in outcomes between operative and nonoperative treatment in the propensity-matching analysis.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Older age has not been associated with an increased risk of nonunion.
Answer 2: Older age has not been associated with an increased likelihood of returning to preinjury level of activity following nonoperative treatment.
Answer 3: Older age is not associated with an increased risk of displacement.
Answer 5: Older age is associated with a less than desirable outcome, particularly in children older than 12 years of age undergoing nonoperative treatment.
