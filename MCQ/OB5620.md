## Options

1. admit for in-situ pinning of both hips
2. admit for in-situ pinning of one hip and femoral neck osteochondroplasty of the other hip
3. admit for in-situ pinning of one hip and follow-up radiographs of the other hip in one week
4. admit for in-situ pinning of both hips and follow-up radiographs of the knees in one week
5. admit for surgical treatment of one hip and followup radiographs of the other hip in one week

## Correct option

**1. admit for in-situ pinning of both hips**

## Answer explanation

This child has stable bilateral slipped capital femoral epiphyses (SCFE). These must be treated promptly with in-situ pinning to prevent further displacement of the epiphysis.

The Loder classification indicates that stable slips present with the patient able to weightbear, whereas in unstable SCFE the child is unable to bear weight. Stable SCFE comprises 85-95% of cases, and unstable SCFE comprises 5-15% of cases. Avascular necrosis occurs in up to 60% of unstable SCFE.

*McPartland et al. reviewed the characteristics of 82 patients with unstable SCFE.* **They found** that 88% had hip, thigh or knee pain for an average of 42 days prior to presenting with an unstable SCFE. They advise early recognition and treatment to prevent morbidity from unstable SCFE.

*Novais et al. reviewed the prevalence, pathogenesis and natural history of SCFE.* **They found** that there is an increase in both childhood obesity of incidence of SCFE, that residual abnormal morphology of the proximal femur is the mechanical cause of FAI, and hypothesized that public health policies to reduce childhood obesity will reduce SCFE prevalence. Obese children may have increased mechanical load, metabolic disorder, decreased femoral anteversion, and more vertically oriented physis.

Figure A is an AP radiograph of the pelvis showing an obvious SCFE on the right, and more subtle slip on the left. Both sides demonstrate the metaphyseal blanch sign (increased metaphyseal opacity) and Trethowan's sign (Kleins line along the superior neck fails to intersect the femoral epiphysis). Figure B is a frog-leg lateral radiograph showing bilateral slips. Figures C and D are normal AP and lateral knee radiographs.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: In the immediate setting, in-situ pinning of both hips would be indicated in cases of stable, mild slips. If the patient presents later with femoroacetabular impingement, arthroscopic femoral neck osteochondroplasty through a limited anterior hip approach or surgical hip dislocation, and flexion intertrochanteric
osteotomy are options. Acutely, surgical hip dislocation and reduction is reserved for severe, unstable slips.
Answer 3: Urgent in-situ pinning is necessary to prevent evolution into unstable SCFE.
Answer 4: There is no knee pathology
Answer 5: Both hips have SCFE and bilateral in-situ pinning is required.
