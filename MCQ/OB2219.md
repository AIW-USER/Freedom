## Options

1. Immediate bracing is indicated to prevent disease progression
2. Oral steroid treatment will eliminate the need for surgery
3. A combined anterior and posterior approach is necessary
4. Forced vital capacity (FVC) <35% predicts better post-operative pulmonary outcomes
5. Surgery is indicated for a Cobb angle of >30 degrees

## Correct option

**5. Surgery is indicated for a Cobb angle of >30 degrees**

## Answer explanation

In patients with Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy (DMD), spine stabilization is indicated in nonambulatory patients with Cobb angle of >20 to 30 degrees.

DMD is an X-linked recessive disorder that leads to progressive replacement of muscle tissue with fibrous and fatty tissue beginning around 2-6 years old. Clinical findings in this population include equinovarus foot deformity, scoliosis, calf pseudohypertrophy, cardiomyopathy, proximal girdle muscle weakness, and increased lumbar lordosis. More than 90% of patients with DMD will develop scoliosis. Surgery is indicated for scoliosis with a Cobb angle of >20-30 degrees and before pulmonary function reaches a critical threshold (FVC <35%).

*Sussman et al. reviewed the clinical manifestations and treatment of patients with DMD.* **The authors report** that spinal deformity in this population is not responsive to nonsurgical modalities such as bracing and that patients with FVC <35% may require prolonged intubation or tracheostomy after surgery. **The author concludes** that patients undergoing spinal stabilization have both enhanced quality and length of life.

*Lebel et al. performed a non-randomized study evaluating the effect of glucocorticoid administration in ambulatory patients with DMD.* **They report** that after 15 years, the rate of avoiding spine surgery was 78% in the glucocorticoid group and 8.3% in the no-glucocorticoid group. **They conclude** that steroid administration in ambulatory DMD patients substantially decreases the need for future spine surgery.

Figures A and B are the AP and lateral radiographs of a DMD patient with scoliosis.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Bracing is not effective in patients with DMD.
Answer 2: Beginning steroids in ambulatory DMD patients reduces the 15-year risk of spine fusion but does not completely eliminate the need or possibility of surgery.
Answer 3: Combined anterior and posterior approaches are not often needed in DMD patients.
Answer 4: FVC >50% predicts good post-operative pulmonary outcomes.
