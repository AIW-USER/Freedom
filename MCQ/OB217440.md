## Question

During a total shoulder arthroplasty procedure, the surgeon is performing a standard deltopectoral approach. The subscapularis tendon insertion is identified on the lesser tuberosity. What is the superior technique to address the subscapularis with respect to long-term outcomes?

## Options

1. Lesser tuberosity osteotomy
2. Subscapularis peel
3. Subscapularis tenotomy
4. Subscapularis partial tenotomy
5. No technique is superior to the others

## Correct option

**5. No technique is superior to the others**

## Answer explanation

There have been no established superior techniques to address the subscapularis during total shoulder arthroplasty (TSA).

TSA is an effective treatment for glenohumeral arthritis but is prone to several complications. During the approach, the subscapularis needs to be reflected or retracted to gain access to the glenohumeral joint. Several techniques are described including lesser tuberosity osteotomy, subscapularis peel, tenotomy, and partial tenotomy. At long-term follow-up, no differences have been reported with respect to subscapularis strength or range of motion.

*Shields et al. reviewed the management of the subscapularis tendon during TSA.* *They described multiple techniques, including full tenotomy, partial tenotomy, peel, lesser tuberosity osteotomy, and subscapularis sparing approaches.* Due to the fact the literature has not established the superiority of one technique over the other, the authors cannot make specific recommendations.

*Lapner et al. performed a randomized controlled trial of 87 patients undergoing TSA treated with either lesser tuberosity osteotomy or subscapularis peel.* **They reported** no differences in subscapularis muscle strength, Western Ontario Osteoarthritis of the Shoulder Index scores, and American Shoulder and Elbow Surgeons scores between the two techniques. **The authors concluded** lesser tuberosity osteotomy and subscapularis peel are equivalent.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1, 2, 3, and 4: No technique has been determined to be superior over the other.
