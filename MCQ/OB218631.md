## Question

A 68-year-old male was recently traveling in Italy and sustained an injury to his foot after crashing a moped while driving through the countryside with his wife. He refused operative treatment abroad, electing to be placed into a cast until he could be evaluated upon returning home. He presents to the clinic eight weeks later with a Lisfranc fracture-dislocation involving the medial and middle columns. The patient's treating surgeon counsels him on the risks and benefits of open reduction internal fixation (ORIF) versus those of primary arthrodesis. Which of the following statements is most true regarding these two treatment options?

## Options

1. Arthrodesis has been shown to have higher complication rates than open reduction internal fixation
2. Combined medial and lateral column arthrodesis is superior to medial column arthrodesis alone
3. Delayed treatment is best served with open reduction internal fixation
4. Functional outcomes are likely equivalent for arthrodesis when compared to open reduction internal fixation
5. Open reduction internal fixation has been associated wtih greater patient satisfaction

## Correct option

**4. Functional outcomes are likely equivalent for arthrodesis when compared to open reduction internal fixation**

## Answer explanation

The patient has a subacute/chronic Lisfranc fracture-dislocation that was initially treated improperly. The current literature suggests that arthrodesis performed for Lisfranc injuries has equivalent functional outcomes when compared to open reduction internal fixation.

Lisfranc joint injuries encompass a broad range of injuries from subtle athletic sprains to severe crush injuries, and are missed in up to 20% of initial encounters. When left untreated, they often result in painful post-traumatic arthritis and arch collapse. Initial management often involves a closed reduction in the setting of fracture-dislocations with or without CRPP and splinting to allow for soft tissue rest. There is ongoing significant debate regarding operative treatment with ORIF versus primary arthrodesis. In general, acute bony fractures involving the Lisfranc joint have traditionally been treated with ORIF; however, recent studies have shown relatively high rates of post-traumatic arthritis (exceeding 25%) even with appropriate, anatomic reduction. Thus, for similar reasoning, chronic injuries and those undergoing delayed treatment are likely to benefit more from arthrodesis given the high rates of post-traumatic arthritis.

Weatherford, et al. provide a comprehensive review of the management of tarsometatarsal joint injuries. The authors note that in treating injuries to the Lisfranc joint, the appropriate choice of initial treatment is often controversial with regard to ORIF versus primary arthrodesis, and depends on injury pattern and concomitant injuries. Further studies are needed to clarify which injury patterns will benefit from primary arthrodesis.

Buda, et al. published a retrospective comparative study on re-operation rate differences between open reduction internal fixation and primary arthrodesis of Lisfranc injuries. The authors reviewed 217 patients with a minimum of one-year follow-up (163 ORIF, 54 primary arthrodeses), excluding those who had a planned re-operation solely for hardware removal, and found no significant difference in re-operation rates between the two groups.

Peters, et al. provide an analysis of conflicting meta-analysis data regarding primary arthrodesis (PA) versus open reduction and internal fixation (ORIF) outcomes for Lisfranc injuries. The authors reviewed five meta-analyses on the topic, finding that all articles agreed that while PA had lower rates of hardware removal, there was no difference between revision surgery, anatomic reduction, postoperative infection, total complications, and patient satisfaction between the two groups as a whole. They ultimately concluded that in terms of functional return to work, there was no difference between PA and ORIF treatment options; but in terms of PROMs and VAS scores, further studies are needed to appropriately analyze functional differences in terms of AOFAS midfoot or ankle-hindfoot scores.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Arthrodesis has not been shown to have higher complication rates than open reduction internal fixation; contrarily, higher rates of hardware removal have been seen with ORIF.
Answer 2: Combined medial and lateral column arthrodesis has been shown to be inferior to medial column arthrodesis alone, given that preserving lateral column mobility improves patient outcomes.
Answer 3: Delayed treatment may be better served with arthrodesis, as described in the discussion above, though no such claim has been made about ORIF.
Answer 5: Patient satisfaction has been shown to be equivalent between arthrodesis and ORIF when performed for Lisfranc injuries.
