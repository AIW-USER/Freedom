## Question

A 66-year-old male undergoes a right revision total hip arthroplasty and is 4 days post-op. He complains of abdominal pain and distension and states he hasn't had a bowel movement in over 7 days. What is the most severe complication associated with this condition?

## Options

1. Superior mesenteric syndrome
2. Renal failure
3. Ascending cholangitis
4. Bowel ischemia
5. Appendicitis

## Correct option

**4. Bowel ischemia**

## Answer explanation

Colonic pseudo-obstruction if left untreated can lead to bowel ischemia requiring a colectomy.

Colonic pseudo-obstruction, also known as Ogilvie Syndrome, is acute colonic distension without mechanical obstruction. Continued distension of the colon can lead to compromised vascular flow to the viscus leading to ischemic bowel, which can be fatal if left untreated. In the presence of bowel ischemia, a laparotomy is necessary to remove the necrotic segments. Initial treatment of early phases consists of discontinuation of opioid medications, nasogastric tube, IV fluids, electrolyte repletion, and neostigmine. If these measures are unsuccessful, colonic decompression with a colonoscopy can resolve distension.

*Clarke et al. reviewed 30 cases of colonic obstruction following total hip arthroplasty.* **They reported** the rate of colonic obstruction was 0.29% with the most common presenting symptom being abdominal distension at a mean of 3.5 days post-op. **The authors concluded** prompt recognition by the treating orthopedic surgeon enables early treatment and hastens recovery.

*Ballaro et al. reported on six cases of colonic obstruction following total hip arthroplasty requiring laparotomy.* **They reported** surgery was performed in three cases due to peritonism and three cases out of concern for ischemia. **The authors concluded** prompt recognition and treatment with involvement of the colorectal service is essential for post-op recovery.

*Petrisor et al. retrospective reviewed 31 patients that developed colonic peudo-obstruction after total joint arthroplasty.* **They reported** a 1.2% overall incidence with a high rate observed in revision total hip arthroplasty and associated with male gender and slow postoperative mobilization. The stated two cases required operative intervention with all cases having a prolonged hospitalization (13.2 days mean).

Incorrect answers
Answers 1, 2, 3, and 5: Abdominal distension and pain following total joint arthroplasty is suggestive of colonic pseudo-obstruction, which if left untreated can lead to bowel ischemia.
