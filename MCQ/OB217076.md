## Question

A 9-year-old female has a non-healing pathologic proximal humerus fracture. She has a diagnosis of unicameral bone cyst (UBC) and was initially treated with sling immobilization. Which of the following adjuncts has been used to prevent UBC pathologic fracture recurrence?

## Options

1. Denosumab
2. Bone Morphogenic Protein 2
3. Intralesional corticosteroid, demineralized bone matrix, and autologous bone marrow injection
4. Pexidartinib
5. Bisphosphonates

## Correct option

**3. Intralesional corticosteroid, demineralized bone matrix, and autologous bone marrow injection**

## Answer explanation

This vignette is characteristic for a unicameral bone cyst (UBC) pathologic fracture. Injection of intralesional corticosteroid, demineralized bone matrix, and autologous bone marrow has been demonstrated to prevent recurrence.

A simple bone cyst or unicameral bone cyst (UBC) is a non-neoplastic, serous fluid-filled bone lesion that is thought to result from temporary failure of medullary bone formation near the physis. It is usually found in patients less than 20 years of age. Common locations are the proximal humerus, proximal femur, distal tibia, ilium, calcaneus, distal radial, and occasionally the metacarpals and phalanges. Patients are usually asymptomatic and may present with pathologic fractures in up to 50% of cases. These are typically treated with immobilization but UBCs that contact the physis, termed active lesions, may require a series of injections.

*Wilkins reviewed the management of UBCs.* **The author notes** that radiographs of fractures usually reveal a non-displaced or minimally displaced fracture through an area of thin cortical bone. Occasionally a fragment of the cyst wall will fracture and fall into the fluid-filled cavity, known as the "fallen leaf sign." While corticosteroid injections were previously commonly performed, high recurrence rates are seen and has caused a push towards more aggressive curettage with bone grafting, or injection bone substitutes.

*Sung et al. performed a review of 187 patients under 20 years treated surgically for humeral or femoral unicameral bone cysts with either injection of corticosteroids, curettage plus bone grafting, or a combination of steroids, demineralized bone matrix, and bone marrow aspirate (SDB).* **They conclude** that SDB is a reasonable first treatment for unicameral bone cysts in the humerus and femur in patients younger than 20 years, being less invasive yet comparable to curettage in preventing recurrence.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1: Denosumab is a monoclonal antibody against RANK-ligand and has been shown to decrease giant cell tumor (GCT) size.
Answer 2: Bone Morphogenic Protein 2 (BMP-2) has been studied in the setting of open tibia shaft fractures.
Answer 4: Pexidartinib (CSF-1 receptor antagonist) has been approved for severe pigmented villonodular synovitis (PVNS) in patients who are not likely to benefit from surgery
Answer 5: Bisphosphonates have a number of clinical indications but have not been described in the setting of UBC pathologic fractures.
