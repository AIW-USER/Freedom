## Question

When dealing with distal tibia physeal fractures in the pediatric population, which of the following is not a risk factor for premature physeal closure?

## Options

1. Degree of initial displacement
2. Periosteum entrapped in the fracture site
3. Patient age >8 years
4. A residual physeal gap of >3mm
5. Salter type III or IV injury pattern

## Correct option

**3. Patient age >8 years**

## Answer explanation

For pediatric distal tibia physeal fractures, age has not been identified as a specific risk factor for premature physeal closure (PPC).

PPC is a known complication of physeal injuries, with occurence after distal tibial physeal fractures reported between 8-50%. Expectant management of PPC requires careful follow up in order to identify the closure and intervene upon the subsequent limb length discrepancies or angular deformities which may arise. All Salter-Harris fractures types are at risk, with a slightly increased risk among types III and IV. The degree of initial displacement and post-reduction fracture gap are both associated with increasing risk of PPC. Age has not specifically been described as a risk factor for PPC.

*Wuerz et. al. reviewed the management pediatric physeal ankle fractures.* **They report** these injuries should be followed for two years in order to prevent complications related to PPC. The clinician should look for Harris growth arrest lines to determine the resumption of normal growth, as asymmetry within the line is suggestive of asymmetric physeal closure, which can result in angular deformity. Management involves guided growth, angular deformity correction, and potentially physeal bar resection.

*Barmada et. al. evaluated 92 distal tibial physeal injuries for PPC and* **identified** risk factors for this phenomenon. The overall rate of PPC was 27% in their cohort. **They found** that anatomic reduction was protective against PPC, whereas a residual fracture gap >3mm (suggesting interposition of periosteum within the fracture site) increased the incidence of PPC to 60%.

*Leary et. al. reviewed 124 distal tibial physeal fractures.* **The authors found** a rate of PPC of 12.1%. **They identified** an increasing risk of PPC depends upon the degree of initial displacement, with an increasing risk of 15% for each millimeter of displacement.

Illustrations A and B are taken directly from the Barmada et al. reference. Illustration A is an example of the Salter-Harris classification of distal tibia fractures. Illustration B is a case demonstrating a Salter-Harris type 2 fracture of the distal tibia which was incompletely reduced. The injury went on to develop premature physeal closure of the distal tibia.

Incorrect answers:
Answers 1, 2, 4, 5: All of these represent defined risk factors for PPC following distal tibial physeal injury.
