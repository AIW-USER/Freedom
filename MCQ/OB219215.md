## Question

You are giving a lecture on orthopedic trauma management to the general surgery trauma team. As a part of this lecture, you discuss cases which require urgent/emergent evaluation. You mention that certain fractures have a higher rate of neurovascular injury that can increase morbidity if initially missed. Which of the following fracture patterns is most likely to have vascular compromise requiring an ankle-brachial index (ABI) in the emergency department?

## Options

1. Schatzker II tibial plateau fracture
2. Schatzker IV tibial plateau fracture
3. Comminuted midshaft tibia fracture
4. Grade II open pilon fracture
5. Displaced subtrochanteric femur fracture

## Correct option

**2. Schatzker IV tibial plateau fracture**

## Answer explanation

Patients with a Schatzker IV tibial plateau fracture are most likely to have a vascular injury and if suspected based on physical exam, should undergo evaluation with ankle-brachial index (ABI) testing in the emergency department (Answer 2).

Tibial plateau fractures are common, with Schatzker IV fractures affecting only the medial plateau. These normally occur with a varus load and are commonly considered a knee dislocation variant as they are length unstable and can be associated with subluxation or dislocation of the tibiofemoral joint. In a Schatzker IV fracture/dislocation, the popliteal artery is at risk of being excessively stretched or directly injured, similar to a true multiligamentous knee dislocation. Injuries to the nerves around the knee are also common in this setting. Obtaining a thorough neurovascular exam is vitally important in these patients and an ABI is a quick, cheap, and easily reproducible test that can help to ensure adequate blood flow if there is concern. If the ABI <0.9, further imaging should be considered and a vascular surgery consultation may ultimately be required.

*Halvorson et al. looked at vascular injury associated with extremity trauma and the best management practices.* **They note** that the rate of vascular injury in long bone fractures is <1%, while it is up to 16% in fracture patterns and injuries associated with a knee dislocation. They reiterate that ABIs should be the initial screening tool, with further imaging such as CT angiography utilized if the ABI is <0.90.

*Stannard et al. reviewed vascular injuries in knee dislocations and the role of physical exam in determining the need for arteriography.* *They looked at 126 consecutive patients with multiligamentous knee injuries and found the prevalence of popliteal artery injury to be 7% in their cohort.* Nine of ten patients with abnormal physical exam findings were found to have popliteal artery injuries and none were missed based on findings. **They concluded** that physical exam findings strongly correlated with the findings of arteriography and these should drive the need for further management.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1, 3-5: The risk of vascular injury is much lower with these fracture patterns, therefore, ABIs are not required for initial evaluation provided the neurovascular exam is otherwise normal.
