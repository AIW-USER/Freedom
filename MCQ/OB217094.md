## Question

A 21-year-old male collegiate athlete sustains a hyperflexion injury to the cervical spine when he is tackled during a game. He experiences immediate pain. A cervical spine injury is suspected. When treating an athlete with a suspected cervical spine injury what is the most important treatment consideration in the prehospital setting?

## Options

1. Patient assessment off of the playing field
2. Removal of shoulder pads and retention of helmet
3. Removal of helmet and retention of shoulder pads
4. Maintaining neutral cervical spine alignment
5. Immediate high-dose steroid administration

## Correct option

**4. Maintaining neutral cervical spine alignment**

## Answer explanation

When treating suspected cervical spine injuries in athletes, maintaining neutral cervical spine alignment is most important to prevent further damage to the spinal cord.

Cervical spine injuries in athletes can be potentially devastating injuries with the highest rate of spinal cord injury occurring in bilateral facet dislocations. In scenarios where an athlete has a suspected cervical spine injury, immediate evaluation including neurologic exam, airway management are critical. Historically, there were recommendations against the removal of helmets and shoulder pads until presentation to a spinal cord injury center. However, an updated recommendation by the National Athletic Trainers Association (NATA) permits on-field removal of helmets and shoulder pads by trained personnel provided neutral spinal alignment is maintained.

*Schroeder and Vaccaro reviewed cervical spine injuries in athletes.* **They stated** athletes with suspected cervical spine injuries should be evaluated with a neurologic examination and initiation of spinal precautions (including removal of a facemask with retention of helmet). **The authors concluded** athletes can often return to play following cervical spine injuries.

Gastel, et al. performed a cadaveric study of a C5-6 flexion-distraction injury and spinal alignment following helmet and shoulder pad removal. They reported increased C5-6 kyphosis with helmet retention alone, increased lordosis with shoulder pad retention alone, and neutral alignment with helmet and shoulder pad retention. The authors concluded that shoulder pads and helmets should be retained in the setting of a suspected cervical spine injury to avoid further neurologic damage.

Mills, et al. provided a consensus statement on the management of catastrophic cervical spine injuries in athletes. They recommended the removal of helmets and shoulder pads in the field to be performed at the discretion of the trained personnel at the scene. They emphasized maintaining neutral spinal alignment regardless of whether athletic wear is removed or maintained.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: The patient should be evaluated and stabilized immediately, regardless of whether the patient is on the playing field.
Answers 2 and 3: The decision to remove or retain athletic wear (including helmets and shoulder pads) is at the discretion of trained personnel at the scene. The most important factor is to maintain neutral cervical spine alignment in order to prevent further spinal cord injury.
Answer 5: There is weak evidence to support high-dose steroids in the presence of a spinal cord injury. Some spine surgeons will administer this at their discretion if they believe the patient to have a low risk for complications.
