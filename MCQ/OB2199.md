## Question

## Options

1. Motor deficits correlate with the vertebral level affected, but sensory deficits do not
2. Neurologic deficits are static, but musculoskeletal manifestations are progressive
3. Protective sensation is often absent, increasing the risk for decubitus ulcers
4. The goal of spinopelvic fusion is to promote ambulation
5. There is an association with maternal diabetes and folate deficiency

## Correct option

**1. Motor deficits correlate with the vertebral level affected, but sensory deficits do not**

## Answer explanation

The patient in this clinical vignette has sacral agenesis. Unlike patients with myelodysplasia, the associated motor deficits have been shown to correlate with the affected level, while protective sensation does not and is frequently intact.

Sacral agenesis, or caudal regression syndrome, represents a spectrum of diseases ranging from hypoplasia to the complete absence of the sacrum and lower lumbar spine. The syndrome has been linked to maternal hypoglycemia and poor glucose control prior to and during the early weeks of pregnancy. Typical features of sacral agenesis include cardiovascular, gastrointestinal and genitourinary aplasia or hypoplasia, as well as various orthopaedic manifestations such as severe neurologic deficits, deformities, and joint contractures of the lower extremities. Treatment goals include maintaining ambulatory ability in less severe types with physical therapy. In severe forms of sacral agenesis, spinopelvic fusion may be necessary to maintain sitting balance and to permit the use of the upper extremities for more meaningful tasks.

*Renshaw presented a case series of 23 patients with sacral agenesis.* *The author offered the first morphologic classification that can be used to guide diagnosis and management, and detailed the numerous orthoapedic manifestations of the syndrome.* Notably, the associated motor loss reliably correlated with the affected vertebral level, while sensory loss did not. Patients often had an intact protective sensory function.

*Pang et al. discussed the etiology and clinical features of sacral agenesis.* *The authors compared patients with sacral agenesis and static neurologic deficits to those with progressive neurologic deficits.* **They noted** that patients with progressive neurologic deficits often had myelographically demonstrable lesions that were amenable to surgical treatment, and advised prudence in diagnosis and treatment to avoid irreversible functional loss.

Figure A is an AP pelvic radiograph of a pediatric patient with complete sacral agenesis. The caudal endplate of the last formed vertebrae rests above an iliac amphiarthrosis. Figure B is a lateral view of the pelvis and lumbar spine demonstrating complete spinopelvic discontinuity.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Unlike cerebral palsy, the neurologic deficits and associated musculoskeletal manifestations may be progressive.
Answer 3: Sacral agenesis is most often associated with predominantly motor deficits and intact protective sensation, which decreases the risk for developing decubitus ulcers.
Answer 4: Spinopelvic fusion is indicated in the patient who requires upper extremity support due to spinopelvic instability but is chiefly to maintain sitting balance and not to promote ambulation.
Answer 5: While there is an association between sacral agenesis and maternal diabetes, this has not been shown for folate deficiency. Myelodysplasia has been associated with both of these risk factors.
