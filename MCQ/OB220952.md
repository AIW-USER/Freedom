## Question

During a biomechanics lecture for orthopaedic residents, the professor displays a stress-strain curve for human ligamentous tissue and compares it to the stress-strain curve of a titanium alloy bone plate. Which of the following biomechanical features is uniquely characteristic of the ligamentous tissue's stress-strain curve but is absent in the curve of the metal implant?

## Options

1. Modulus of elasticity
2. Plastic deformation region
3. Strain energy density
4. Toe region
5. Yield point

## Correct option

**4. Toe region**

## Answer explanation

The toe region is a unique feature of soft tissues, such as ligaments and tendons, representing the initial phase where crimped collagen fibers straighten under low tension before linear stiffness begins (answer choice 4).

In biomechanics, soft tissues like ligaments exhibit a non-linear relationship between stress and strain during the initial loading phase, known as the toe region. This occurs because the collagen fibers within the ligament are naturally "crimped" or wavy at rest. As a tensile load is applied, these fibers begin to unfold and straighten without significantly resisting the load, resulting in high strain for relatively low stress. Once the fibers are fully straightened, the tissue enters the linear region, where the actual stretching of the collagen occurs, defining the tissue's modulus of elasticity. In contrast, rigid materials like metal implants do not have crimped fibers and therefore enter the linear elastic phase immediately upon loading.

*Woo et al. evaluated the structural and mechanical properties of knee ligaments during the healing process following injury.* **They noted** that the mechanical behavior of ligaments is highly dependent on the orientation and recruitment of collagen fibers, which creates the characteristic non-linear stress-strain curve. **They concluded** that restoring the complex biomechanical profile of the native ligament is the primary challenge in surgical reconstruction and rehabilitation.

*Lewis et al. evaluated the use of computational modeling to simulate the mechanical performance of various fracture fixation constructs.* **They noted** that while biological tissues exhibit complex viscoelasticity, metal implants behave linearly in the elastic region until they reach a definitive yield point. **They concluded** that finite element analysis must accurately account for the distinct material property differences between bone, soft tissue, and implants to improve clinical outcomes.
