## Options

1. Thyroid-Stimulating Hormone
2. Metalloproteases
3. HbA1c
4. White Blood Cells
5. Alkaline Phosphatase

## Correct option

**2. Metalloproteases**

## Answer explanation

The patient's history and clinical exam demonstrate subacromial bursitis, or outlet impingement. Metalloproteases have been shown to be at increased levels with in these patients.

Originally popularized by Neer, inflammation of the subacromial bursa is the first of three stages on the continuum of shoulder pathology leading to tendonitis and fibrosis, and eventually partial or complete rotator cuff tears and arthropathy. It is characterized by pain with overhead activities in addition to pain while sleeping. This is often associated with concomitant structural or functional pathology that impairs normal glenohumeral mechanics; including scapular dyskinesia, os acromiale and type 3 hooked acromions. The resulting inflammation of the subacromial bursa and space has guided treatment algorithms to include use of anti-inflammatory agents.

*Blaine et al evaluated subacromial bursa specimens from 11 patients with subacromial bursitis undergoing mini-open rotator cuff repair.* *These were compared with bursa from 4 patients undergoing surgery for shoulder instability.* **They found** elevated levels metalloproteases and other inflammatory markers on histological analysis compared to the control group. COX, TNF-alpha, IL-1, IL-6 and IL-8 were all found to be elevated as well. **They concluded** that subacromial bursectomy is a key part to treating rotator cuff disease surgically.

Figure A is a T1-weighted coronal MR showing irregularity within the rotator cuff tendon, while Figures B and C are fluid-sensitive coronal MR sequances showing signal within the rotator cuff tendon, subacromial and subdeltoid bursas.
Illustration A shows Hematoxylin and eosin staining for Metalloprotease 9. The stain on the left is from a patient with subacromial bursitis, compared with a negative stain for MMP9 from a patient without bursitis symptoms.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1 is seen in patients with hypothyroidism, which is often seen in cases of adhesive capsulitis. This patient is demonstrating pain but has full range of motion making adhesive capsulitis very unlikely.
Answer 3 is seen in diabetics and is a common finding for individuals with adhesive capsulitis, though this patient's exam is not consistent with adhesive capsulitis
Answer 4 would be indicative of a septic bursitis, which her history, exam and imaging do not support
Answer 5 would be elevated in osteoblastic-type tumors, Paget's disease, or hyperparathyroidism, not with subacromial bursitis
