## Question

A 10-year-old female with adolescent idiopathic scoliosis presents to a pediatric orthopaedic surgeon as a referral from her pediatrician given concern for high risk of progression of her deformity. In counseling the patient and her mother, the orthopaedist informs the patient that she must wear the brace for at least 18 hours per day to achieve the maximal effect of the treatment. Which of the following factors is most correlated with curve progression if the brace is worn for less than 18 hours per day?

## Options

1. Curves < 25°
2. Curves between 25° and 30°
3. Open triradiate cartilage
4. Risser stage 1
5. Sanders stage 6

## Correct option

**3. Open triradiate cartilage**

## Answer explanation

Patients with open triradiate cartilage who fail to wear their brace for at least 18 hours per day have been shown to go on to curve progression that ultimately requires surgical correction.

Adolescent idiopathic scoliosis (AIS) is most commonly seen in females aged 10 to 18. Right thoracic curves are most common, and there is a reported incidence of 3% for small curves (between 10-20°) and an incidence of 0.3% for curves >/= 30°. The three broad categories of predictors of curve progression are curve magnitude, remaining skeletal growth, and curve type. In terms of curve magnitude, generally speaking, curves >25° prior to skeletal maturity are predicted to continue progressing, while thoracic curves >50° and lumbar curves >40° at skeletal maturity are expected to progress 1-2°/year. With respect to remaining skeletal growth, pre-pubertal patients with open triradiate cartilage and large curves, by definition, have the greatest amount of growth potential remaining. Patients in Risser stage 0, which covers the first two-thirds of the pubertal growth spurt, are in the period of peak growth velocity (the period just before menarche and Risser stage 1), which has also been shown to predict curve progression. Finally, in terms of curve type, thoracic curves are more likely to progress than lumbar curves, and double curves are more likely to progress than single curves.

In general, treatment recommendations for AIS are based on the aforementioned factors of skeletal maturity at time of diagnosis, magnitude of deformity, and expected curve progression. Observation alone is indicated for curves < 25°, while the initiation of bracing is indicated for patients with curves of 25°-45° who are in Risser stage 2 or less (indicating skeletal immaturity) with the goal of stopping progression, not correcting the deformity. Finally, posterior spinal fusion has remained the gold standard in patients with thoracic and double curves > 45°, as these are likely to progress without surgical correction.

Karol, et al. prospectively followed 168 patients with AIS and curves between 25° and 45° who were Risser stage 0,1 or 2 and measure compliance with brace wear using thermal monitors. The authors found a progression to curves requiring surgery (>/= 50°) of 44.2% for patients who were Risser 0, 6.9% for patients who were Risser 1, and 0% for patients who were Risser 2. Notably, 10 patients at Risser stage 0 with closed triradiate cartilage wore braces > 18 hours per day, and none underwent surgery, while 7 of 10 patients at Risser stage 0 with open triradiate cartilage and similar daily brace wear underwent surgery. In conclusion, the authors found that patients with open triradiate cartilage were at highest risk, especially those with curves of >30, and ultimately recommend that Risser stage 0 patients should be prescribed a minimum of 18 hours of brace wear.

Weinstein, et al. published in the New England Journal of Medicine on the effects of bracing in adolescents with idiopathic scoliosis. The authors conducted a multi-center study of 242 patients, including both randomized and preference cohorts, with patients in the bracing sub-cohorts instructed to wear braces for at least 18 hours per day. Primary outcomes were measured as curve progression to > 50° (treatment failure) and attainment of skeletal maturity without the same degree of progression (treatment success), with the ultimate outcome being the early termination of the study due to the overwhelming efficacy of bracing. The authors ultimately concluded that bracing significantly decreased the progression of high-risk curves to the threshold for surgery in patients with adolescent idiopathic scoliosis, with the benefit increasing incrementally with longer hours of brace wear.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Patients with curves < 25° are typically observed for progression prior to starting bracing therapy.
Answer 2: In general, curves >/= 30°, especially in the setting of open triradiate cartilage, have been shown to progress if patients fail to wear braces for > 18 hours per day. Meanwhile, according to the tested article by Karol, et al. above, patients with curves < 30° who wear braces for at least 12.9 hours/day are less likely to require a surgical correction.
Answer 4: Patients in Risser stage 1 have surpassed the peak growth velocity period associated with Risser stage 0 and are less likely than patients with open triaradiate cartilage and large curves to progress to surgical correction with < 18 hours of brace wear.
Answer 5: The Sanders staging system, which has fallen out of favor, correlates physeal closure within the bones of the hand with level of skeletal maturity. Sanders stage 6 represents the late adolescent steady state period after peak growth velocity, and correlates with a Risser stage >1.
