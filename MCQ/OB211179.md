## Question

A 56-year-old man undergoes the procedure depicted in Figure A for severe cervical myelopathy. Postoperatively, he develops 3 out of 5 weakness in one motor group in his right upper extremity. Which of the following is the nerve root and functional deficit that most likely describes this new deficit?

![[q211179-a-acffig2.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. C4; deltoid weakness
2. C4; biceps weakness
3. C5; biceps weakness
4. C5; triceps weakness
5. C6; triceps weakness

## Correct option

**3. C5; biceps weakness**

## Answer explanation

This patient underwent a C4-C6 anterior cervical diskectomy and fusion (ACDF) and is most likely experiencing a postoperative C5 palsy, which would manifest as biceps and deltoid muscle weakness.

One of the most serious complications of cervical myelopathy surgery (both anterior and posterior) is intraoperative and postoperative neurologic deterioration. In the postoperative setting, neurologic deterioration may occur immediately or may take a few weeks to present. A C5 nerve root palsy is not uncommon after ACDF, laminoplasty, or laminectomy and fusion. A few theories for this palsy have been proposed, including direct nerve root injury and/or segmental spinal cord disorder. Generally, patients with a C5 palsy have a good prognosis, with most achieving full functional recovery by 6 months on average.

*Lebl et al. published a review article on the diagnosis and management of cervical spondylotic myelopathy.* **They reported** that potential complications after cervical spine surgery for cervical spondylotic myelopathy include dysphagia, dysphonia, Horner syndrome, cerebrospinal fluid leak, persistent axial neck pain, pseudoarthrosis, nerve palsy, and infection. Interestingly, **they concluded** that the incidence of C5 palsy (traditionally associated with posterior laminectomy/ laminoplasty procedures) was not specific to either the anterior or posterior based approaches.

Currier at al. published a review article on neurologic complications of cervical spine surgery and C5 palsy. They reported that the incidence of isolated C5 palsy was between 0-30% and that the average time to full spontaneous recovery was 4-5 months. They concluded that the key to managing neurological complications in cervical spine surgery is through careful preoperative planning, neuromonitoring, and meticulous surgical technique.

Figure A depicts the radiograph of a multi-level C4-C6 ACDF.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1 & 2: The most common cause of biceps and deltoid weakness after cervical spine surgery is a C5 palsy, not C4.
Answers 4 & 5: Triceps weakness would be seen with a C7 palsy.
