## Question

A 37-year-old male presents to your office for a second opinion with complaints of worsening hip pain for the last 3 months. Past medical history is significant for hypertension, hyperlipidemia, and Chrohn’s disease. He has been taking medication daily for the last 5 years to help to control his Chrohn’s. Figures A and B demonstrate the patient’s hip radiograph and MRI, respectively. Based on the likely diagnosis and degree of severity, you prescribe a medication that in some studies may decrease rate of femoral head collapse. What is the mechanism by which this medication works?

## Options

1. Decreases osteoclast resorption by flattening ruffled border
2. Increases intestinal absorption of calcium and phosphate
3. Monoclonal antibody that binds to RANK-L
4. Partial parathyroid hormone (PTH) agonist that increases osteoblast formation
5. Inhibition of cyclooxygenase

## Correct option

**1. Decreases osteoclast resorption by flattening ruffled border**

## Answer explanation

This patient with pre-collapse osteonecrosis of the right hip was prescribed a bisphosphonate, which functions to decrease osteoclast resorption and increase osteoclast apoptosis by flattening the ruffled border.

There are a number of causes of hip osteonecrosis and staging is important in guiding management. Ficat and Steinberg classifications are the most widely used classifications for staging purposes, both using radiographs, MRI and clinical findings. In cases without evidence of femoral head collapse on imaging (Steinberg 0-2 & Ficat 0-2a), some studies have suggested bisphosphonates may improve patient outcomes. It is thought that decreasing bony resorption around the area of osteonecrosis may slow, or prevent, collapse in this population, therefore leading to decreased pain and improved function. However, more recent studies with similar designs have disputed these claims. Additionally, bisphosphonates are not without risk, as they can cause atypical femur fractures, osteonecrosis of the jaw, gastric ulcers, and esophagitis.

*Nishii et al. reviewed the use of alendronate in patients with osteonecrosis of the hip over the course of a year, theorizing that preventing progression of osteonecrosis could be achieved by slowing bony resorption in the femoral head.* *Patients receiving alendronate therapy showed decreased bone resorption markers as well as less radiographic collapse and less pain.* *They finished by suggesting that based on these results, alendronate may be useful in preventing the progression of femoral head collapse and improving outcomes.*

*Lee et al. performed a randomized control trial using zolendronate in patients with Steinberg I or II hip osteonecrosis for two years.* During this time period, more hips in the zolendronate cohort demonstrated collapse. Additionally, zolendronate did not reduce the need for total hip arthoplasty (THA). This led them to conclude that zolendronate was not a useful treatment in this population.

*Chen et al. performed a randomized control trial looking at the effect of alendronate on radiographic and clinical outcomes in Steinberg II and III hips.* *They noticed no difference in radiographic/MRI results or the number of patients who proceeded to THA.* **They concluded** that alendronate has no role in reducing progression of collapse or preventing need for THA in this population.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Vitamin D functions by increasing intestinal absorption of calcium and phosphate as well as modulating PTH function. While important for bone health, it does not affect osteoclast function and doesn't slow the progression of osteonecrosis.
Answer 3: Denosumab is a monoclonal antibody that binds to RANK-L, which decreases activation of osteoclasts. However, it has not shown to be helpful in decreasing the progression of osteonecrosis.
Answer 4: Teriparatide is a partial PTH agonist and the only osteoporosis medication that is anabolic with regards to stimulating osteoblast function and bone growth. It does not slow the progression of osteonecrosis.
Answer 5: NSAIDs function by inhibiting the function of cyclooxygenase (COX). While they may help treat inflammation and pain, they do not slow the progression of osteonecrosis.
