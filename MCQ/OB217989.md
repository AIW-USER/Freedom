## Question

A 20-year-old man presents as a motorcycle accident polytrauma patient with an isolated orthopedic injury including a type 3A open tibial shaft fracture. The wound requires multiple debridements resulting in a 7cm diaphyseal defect. His soft tissues are now amenable to definitive treatment, and he is neurovascularly intact distally. After prolonged discussion, he is not amenable to distraction osteosynthesis. Which of the following osteogenic treatment options would be most appropriate for this patient?

## Options

1. Intramedullary nailing with demineralized bone matrix grafting
2. Intramedullary nailing with calcium triphosphate grafting
3. Open reduction internal fixation with fresh frozen cortical allograft
4. Open reduction internal fixation with vascularized fibular autograft
5. Below knee amputation

## Correct option

**4. Open reduction internal fixation with vascularized fibular autograft**

## Answer explanation

Segmental bone defects of the tibia are amenable to multiple treatment options with vascularized free fibula autograft being the most appropriate osteogenic treatment option provided. As a vascularized autograft, it contains osteoconductive, osteoinductive, and osteogenic properties.

Bone grafting is commonly required in bone defects caused by trauma, tumor excision, osteomyelitis, congenital pseudoarthrosis, or radiation necrosis. Segmental bone defects of the extremities can be managed by Ilizarov external fixator bone transport, excisional arthrodesis, Masquelet technique, and/or bone grafting. Nonvascularized bone grafts incorporate via creeping substitution through a dead bone matrix whereas vascularized bone grafts allow for direct healing with preservation of biomechanical properties and mineral content. A free fibula autograft has osteoconductive, osteoinductive, and osteogenic combined properties and would serve as a suitable option in this patient’s tibial segmental defect. It is a reliable and reproducible graft for both upper and lower extremity segmental defects, particularly in diaphyseal locations.

Archual and colleages reviewed some commonly used vascularized bone grafts in orthopaedic surgery. They discuss various pedicled vascularized bone grafts and free vascularized bone grafts, stressing the importance of the reconstructive ladder and appropriate indications for use. They concluded that these grafts are powerful tools for managing bone defects and can be considered for primary use, rather than a last-resort option, in the appropriate setting.

*Noaman described their case series of sixteen patients with upper limb bone defects treated with free vascularized fibular autograft transfer.* **They report** 15/16 patients went onto union at an average time of 3.5 months with normal hand function in all cases. **The author concludes** that the vascularized fibular bone graft provides a stable bone implant allowing for early range of motion, appropriate anatomic reconstruction, and restoration of function in the upper extremity.

*McClure and colleagues reviewed the reconstructive options for tibial bone defects.* *They discuss the Masquelet technique, bone transport, fibular autografts, and other options based on anatomic location and defect size.* **They conclude** that few large comparative studies exist and surgeons must rely on their own training experience and technique familiarity when treating these patients.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Intramedullary nailing with demineralized bone matrix grafting would not be considered appropriate for this patient with such a large segmental defect. Further, the demineralized bone matrix has osteoconductive and osteoinductive properties, however, does not have osteogenic properties.
Answer 2: Intramedullary nailing with calcium triphosphate grafting can be considered in length stable, small defects of the tibia however would not be considered appropriate in this patient. Further, calcium triphosphate serves as an osteoconductive graft, however, does not have osteogenic properties.
Answer 3. Open reduction internal fixation with fresh frozen cortical allograft could be considered appropriate, however, does not have osteogenic properties.
Answer 5. A below-knee amputation should be reserved as a last resort option in which the soft tissue injury is so severe it precludes reconstructive options. This patient has retained neurovascular viability and limb preservation surgery would be prudent.
