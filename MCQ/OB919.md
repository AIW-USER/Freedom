## Question

## Options

1. First tarsometatarsal joint arthrodesis (Lapidus), lateral column lengthening, and spring ligament repair
2. Medial heel lift, longitudinal arch support, and medial forefoot posting
3. Short period of immobilization in walker boot with lateral heel wedge
4. Triple arthrodesis
5. Flexor digitorum longus tendon transfer and medial calcaneal displacement osteotomy

## Correct option

**2. Medial heel lift, longitudinal arch support, and medial forefoot posting**

## Answer explanation

The patient's clinical presentation is consistent with a Stage 2 Posterior Tibial Tendon Insufficiency (PTTI). Stage II PTTI disease is treated with a medial heel lift, longitudinal arch support, and medial forefoot posting orthotic if the forefoot displays a fixed forefoot supination.

The treatment of PTTI is dictated by stage of disease, duration of symptoms, and/or failure of nonoperative measures. Stage I disease, characterized by pain over the tendon is managed nonoperatively with either a walking boot/cast or custom-molded orthosis. Refractory cases require tenosynovectomy. Stage II disease can be treated with a custom orthosis with a medial lift, longitudinal arch support and medial forefoot posting in cases of fixed supination. Operative intervention typically involves flexor digitorum longus transfer, calcaneal osteotomy, and Achilles lengthening. Adjunctive procedures can be added as needed to correct remaining forefoot deformities. In severe Stage III and IV disease or disease with significant arthritis, triple or tibiotalocalceaneal arthrodesis are also options.

*Myerson described the staging system for PTTI and* **state**: 1) Stage I PTTI includes a flexible hindfoot and ability to a single leg heel rise. 2) Stage II dysfunction is characterized by a supple, reducible hindfoot that is unable to perform a single leg heel rise as shown. 3) Stage IIA dysfunction represents <30% talonavicular uncoverage on AP radiographs whereas Stage IIB has >30% uncoverage.

*Elftman reviewed the appropriate use of orthotics in PTTI and* **state**: 1) Medial heel lifts/longitudinal supports are appropriate in Stage I/II. 2) Stage III PTTI can sometimes be managed with an articulated ankle-foot orthosis (AFO). 3) Stage IV typically requires a non-articulated AFO due to deltoid ligament incompetence and talar tilt.

Figure A is clinical picture showing flatfoot deformity on the left side with inability to single-heel raise. Figure B shows the accompanying forefoot abduction with "too many toes." Figure C shows the fixed forefoot supination that can sometimes be seen in PTTI.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Lapidus procedure would be appropriate for Stage II disease with hypermobile, unstable, or arthritic first tarsometatarsal joint. First-line treatment would be custom orthosis
Answer 3: Immobilization is typically used for Stage I disease
Answer 4: Triple arthrodesis is used for Stage II disease with severe subtalar arthritis or Stage III disease. Conservative management would be the first step.
Answer 5: This treatment would be appropriate if the patient fails conservative management.
