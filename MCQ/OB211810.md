## Question

A 21-year-old football player sustains the isolated injury depicted in Figure A, resulting in pain, swelling, and feeling of instability. The decision is made to pursue reconstruction of the injured structure. During the reconstruction, transtibial drilling is performed with the knee in full extension. The risk of which of the following complications is likely increased as a result of this technique?

![[q211810-a-screen-shot-2020-03-22-at-12-19-17-pm.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Saphenous nerve injury
2. Medial inferior geniculate artery injury
3. Medial and patellofemoral degenerative changes
4. Popliteal artery injury
5. Rotational instability

## Correct option

**4. Popliteal artery injury**

## Answer explanation

This patient has sustained an isolated posterior cruciate ligament (PCL) injury and is undergoing PCL reconstruction. Transtibial drilling with the knee in full extension increases the risk of injury to the popliteal artery.

PCL injuries are rare in isolation and more commonly occur in the multi-ligament injured knee. Vascular injuries are uncommon, but potentially devastating complications associated with PCL reconstruction. As such, several strategies have been described to reduce the risk of vascular complications during PCL reconstruction. During transtibial drilling, increasing knee flexion increases the distance between the posterior tibia and the popliteal artery/vein, which decreases the risk of injury. Other techniques that have been described in the literature include the use of an accessory posteromedial incision to allow finger retraction of the popliteal artery and vein, the use of oscillating drills to prevent excessive soft-tissue entanglement, and the use of tapered drill bits to minimize cut-out of sharp edges as drilling reaches the posterior tibial cortex.

*Zawodny et al. reviewed PCL surgery complications.* **They reported** that the most common reasons for complications include the relative rarity of PCL injuries, the lack of knowledge and experience of providers treating them, the proximity of neurovascular structures to the PCL, and the technically demanding nature of reconstructive procedures. **They emphasized** the increased danger to the popliteal artery during drilling of the tibial tunnel as it lies just posterior to the PCL insertion on the tibia.

*Matava et al. reviewed techniques in the surgical treatment of PCL injuries.* **They reported** that placement of the tunnel more posteriorly or inferiorly will fail to reproduce normal PCL anatomy and risks injury to the popliteal neurovascular bundle. **They emphasized** that the risk of popliteal neurovascular bundle injury is also increased with knee extension during tibial drilling.

*Voos et al. review the anatomy, biomechanics and outcomes following repair of the PCL.* **They highlighted** the biomechanics of the anterolateral bundle (tight in flexion) and the posteromedial bundle (tight in extension). **They concluded** clinical studies have not revealed a difference in single versus double-bundle reconstruction, although laboratory and cadaveric studies demonstrated better restoration of normal knee kinematics with double-bundle reconstruction.

Figure A shows an isolated PCL injury on MRI.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The saphenous nerve may be injured during placement of the accessory posteromedial portal, but not during transtibial drilling in full extension.
Answer 2: Transtibial drilling with the knee in full extension increases the risk of injury to the popliteal artery, not the medial inferior geniculate artery.
Answer 4: Untreated PCL injuries may result in degenerative changes in the medial and patellofemoral knee compartments. However, this is not the result of transtibial drilling.
Answer 5: Failure to reconstruct both bundles of the PCL may result in rotational instability, but is not predicated on transtibial drilling in full extension.
