## Question

A 7-year-old female presents to your office for in-toeing. On examination, you observe intoeing and an internally rotated patella on gait evaluation. Examination while supine reveals internal rotation of the hip of >70° and external rotation of the hip of <20°. Based on this patient's most likely underlying condition, which of the following treatments would be most indicated at this time?

## Options

1. Observation
2. Derotational femoral osteotomy
3. Derotational tibial osteotomy
4. Derotational femoral and tibial osteotomies
5. Derotational femoral and reconstructive pelvic osteotomies

## Correct option

**1. Observation**

## Answer explanation

This patient has excessive femoral anteversion. Treatment is observation with parental reassurance as most cases resolve by age 10.

Femoral anteversion is a common congenital condition caused by intrauterine positioning which leads to increased anteversion of the femoral neck relative to the femur with compensatory internal rotation of the femur. It is characterized by increased anteversion of the femoral neck relative to the femur, compensatory internal rotation of the femur lower extremity, and intoeing. The diagnosis is made clinically with the presence of intoeing combined with an increase in internal rotation of the hip of >70° (normal is 20 to 60°) with an accompanying decrease in external rotation of the hip of <20° (normal is 30 to 60°). Treatment is observation with parental reassurance as most cases resolve by age 10. Rarely, surgical management is indicated in the presence of <10° of hip external rotation in children >10 years of age.

*Stambough et al. prospectively reviewed excessive femoral anteversion treated with a femoral derotational osteotomy and antegrade intramedullary nail (IMN) stabilization.* **They reported** that after femoral derotation osteotomy over an IMN, femoral anteversion, as measured by the trochanteric prominence angle, improved an average of 29 degrees. **They concluded** that for adolescents with symptomatic excessive femoral anteversion, derotational osteotomy over an IMN offers a reliable surgical option that provides predictable deformity correction and significant improvements in both function and pain scales.

*Bruce et al. retrospectively reviewed the results of surgical correction of miserable malalignment syndrome associated with significant patellofemoral pain, which included excessive femoral anteversion.* *They reported on the patients that were treated by ipsilateral outward femoral osteotomy and inward tibial osteotomy after failing nonoperative treatment.* **They noted** that all of the patients reported full satisfaction with their surgery and outcomes. **They concluded** that when evaluating patients with patellofemoral pain, it is imperative to assess the rotational profiles of the femur and tibia, as rotational osteotomies may be performed in patients with patellofemoral pain associated with excessive femoral and tibial torsion.

*Leonardi et al. reported on torsional malalignment syndrome (TMS), which is a combination of femoral anteversion and tibial lateral torsion.* *They reported on patients that underwent double femoral and tibial osteotomy for TMS bilateral correction.* **They concluded** that a clinical, radiographic and CT scan evaluation should be performed in adolescent patients with torsional deformity.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 2-4: A derotational femoral osteotomy is indicated with <10° of external rotation on exam in an older children (>10 years old), but is rarely needed
Answer 5: Reconstructive pelvic osteotomies are most commonly utilized in patients with developmental dysplasia of the hip (DDH)
