## Question

Figures A & B are the radiographs of a 76-year-old woman with a past medical history of rheumatoid arthritis who presents to the Emergency Department complaining of left elbow pain after falling off her porch. She has a long history of oral corticosteroid use. What is the best surgical option for this patient?

![[q217947-b-dh-2.jpg]]

## Options

1. Closed reduction and percutaneous pinning
2. ORIF with parallel plating
3. ORIF with orthogonal plating
4. Unconstrained total elbow arthroplasty
5. Semiconstrained total elbow arthroplasty

## Correct option

**5. Semiconstrained total elbow arthroplasty**

## Answer explanation

This rheumatoid patient has a comminuted distal humerus fracture. At her age, and with her underlying inflammatory condition, a semi-constrained total elbow arthroplasty (TEA) would be indicated.

TEA is the best surgical option for elderly patients with comminuted distal humerus fractures, particularly in those with underlying inflammatory diseases and long-term oral corticosteroid use. Complications specific to this treatment include activity restrictions, implant loosening, polyethylene wear, and periprosthetic fracture.

*Frankle et al. performed a comparative study of TEA versus ORIF in elderly women.* **They reported** that TEA outcomes were superior to ORIF at a minimum of 2 years of follow-up. Moreover, **they noted** that TEA was useful in patients with comorbidities that compromise bone stock, including osteoporosis and oral corticosteroid use. **They concluded** that TEA is a viable treatment option for distal intraarticular humerus fractures in women older than age 65.

*McKee et al. performed a multicenter randomized controlled trial comparing ORIF with TEA in elderly patients.* **They reported** that TEA resulted in better 2-year clinical functional scores and more predictable outcomes compared with ORIF. **They concluded** that TEA also has a lower reoperation rate; one-quarter of patients with fractures randomized to ORIF could not achieve stable fixation.

Figures A & B are the AP and lateral radiographs of the elbow demonstrating a comminuted distal humerus fracture.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Closed reduction and percutaneous pinning studies have not been published for elderly patients with comminuted distal humerus fractures. Further, poor bone quality precludes the limited fixation of pins outside of using a multi-planar external fixator.
Answers 2&3: Semi-constrained TEA is recommended in elderly comminuted distal bicolumnar intra-articular distal humerus fractures, particularly in those with underlying inflammatory conditions such as RA. This is due to the high rate of nonunion and fixation failure.
Answer 4: Semi-constrained TEA is recommended in patients with inflammatory conditions such as RA as healing of the collateral ligaments is not necessary for stability as it is with unconstrained TEA designs.
