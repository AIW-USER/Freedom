## Question

A 48-year-old woman is to undergo spinal deformity surgery for the deformity depicted in Figures A and B. To maximize the rate of fusion, which of the following is most important?

![[q3371-b-screen-shot-2019-09-29-at-4-42-09-pm.jpg]]

## Options

1. Inclusion of ilium alone in the distal fixation
2. Restoration of coronal balance
3. Inclusion of the ilium and sacrum in the distal fixation
4. Inclusion of the sacrum alone in the distal fixation
5. Inclusion of the ischium alone in the distal fixation

## Correct option

**3. Inclusion of the ilium and sacrum in the distal fixation**

## Answer explanation

Studies have shown that inclusion of both the ilium and sacrum in the distal fixation of long fusion constructs for spinal deformity increases fusion rates.

Surgical treatment of adult spinal deformities using long fusion constructs can be difficult, with relatively high rates of pseudoarthrosis and instrumentation failure. Many patients with these fusions ultimately experience degenerative conditions or deformities distal to the fusion and eventually undergo revision surgery to extend the fusion to the pelvis. Current literature suggests that instrumentation that includes the ilium and sacrum exhibits higher rates of fusion and improved sagittal balance.

*Islam et al. performed a study to determine whether extension of fusions to the pelvis in idiopathic scoliosis resulted in increased fusion rates with fewer complications.* **They reported** a statistically significant decrease in the rate of pseudoarthrosis when the distal posterior fixation included both the ilium and sacrum (21%) compared to both the sacrum alone (53%) and the ilium alone (42%). **They concluded** that when fixed to both the ilium and sacrum, these constructs appear to be capable of maintaining sagittal alignment with lower rates of pseudoarthrosis but have high complication rates.

*Emami et al. performed a study to evaluate the clinical outcome and complications following 3 methods of lumbosacral fixation for adult spine deformities: Luque-Galveston (smooth L-rod and segmental sublaminar wire instrumentation to pelvis), combined iliac and sacral screws, and bicortical sacral screws.* **They reported** that the highest rate of pseudoarthrosis occurred in patients undergoing the Luque-Galveston technique, compared to those undergoing combined iliac and sacral screws, and those undergoing bicortical sacral screw fixation. **They concluded** that the Luque-Galveston fixation technique has an unacceptably high rate of pseudoarthrosis and is therefore not recommended for adult deformities.

Figures A and B depict a severe adult scoliotic deformity. Illustrations A and B depict an example of a long posterior spinal instrumented fusion with the incorporation of the ilium and sacrum (S2 alar-iliac) for fixation.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Maximal fusion rate is increased when the distal fixation includes both the ilium and sacrum, versus the ilium in isolation.
Answer 2: While coronal imbalance is often addressed in large spinal deformity surgeries, it has not been demonstrated to directly maximize fusion rate.
Answer 4: While the inclusion of the construct to the sacrum in isolation increases its stability, there is an increased risk of sacral fractures and pseudoarthrosis.
Answer 5: Inclusion of the ischium in the distal fixation of fusion constructs has not been shown to maximize fusion rates.
