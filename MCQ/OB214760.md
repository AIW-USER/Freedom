## Question

Which of the following is not considered a potential side-effect of prolonged non-steroidal anti-inflammatory (NSAID) medication use?

## Options

1. Gastric ulcers
2. Increasing creatinine
3. Increased bleeding time
4. Tinnitus
5. Increased circulating levels of IL-6

## Correct option

**5. Increased circulating levels of IL-6**

## Answer explanation

Prolonged usage of non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) is not associated with increased circulating levels of IL-6. Some studies have shown that NSAIDs actually decrease the circulating levels of IL-6 which is a pro-inflammatory interleukin.

NSAIDs are shown to have anti-inflammatory, antipyretic, anti-platelet, and analgesic effects. The mechanism of action of NSAIDs is via the inhibition of the COX (cyclooxygenase) enzymes ultimately inhibiting the synthesis and release of prostaglandins. NSAIDs have been a mainstay in the treatment of pain associated with osteoarthritis. However, prolonged use of NSAIDs has shown to have several side-effects including gastrointestinal bleeding, gastroesophageal reflux, prolonged bleeding time, renal dysfunction, and inner ear disturbances. There are 2 types of COX enzymes, COX-1 and COX-2. While the majority of NSAIDs block both COX-1 and COX-2, selective COX-2 inhibitors exist with the benefit of anti-inflammatory action without disrupting the beneficial effects of COX-1 (maintaining gastric mucosa, regulating renal blood flow, influencing platelet aggregation).

*Argoff performed a systematic review of the evidence supporting the efficacy and safety profiles of new NSAIDs introduced from 2000 to 2010 for the treatment of patients with osteoarthritis (OA).* The medications reviewed were meloxicam, naproxen plus lansoprazole, oxycodone/ibuprofen, and diclofenac patch, gel, and solution. **The authors found** evidence to support efficacy in treating patients with OA for all agents except oxycodone/ibuprofen. Although low-dose meloxicam and naproxen plus lansoprazole offer a reduced risk of adverse gastrointestinal (GI) events, cardiovascular and renal risks remain similar to traditional oral NSAID therapy. **The authors conclude** that newly-approved NSAID agents may improve the ability of clinicians to tailor analgesic therapy for their diverse patient populations and to achieve realistic functional improvements.

*Vista et al. performed a review of "health" supplements currently being marketed as beneficial in the prevention and regression of osteoarthritis.* *The authors cite evidence stating that routine use of dietary supplements is not recommended for the treatment of OA.* **They state** that uniformity among research design trials is warranted to investigate the potential of all commercially available OA supplements for the management of OA. **They conclude** that stringent approaches and consensus among international regulatory bodies are required to control misconceptions by the general public regarding the use of OA supplements.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Chronic NSAID use has been shown to be associated with the development of both gastric ulcers
Answer 2: Chronic NSAID use has been associated with renal dysfunction leading to elevated creatinine levels.
Answer 3: NSAIDs have anti-platelet properties and have been shown to increase bleeding times.
Answer 4: Chronic NSAID use has been associated with inner ear disturbances including tinnitus and mild to moderate sensorineural hearing loss. These otological side effects are often transient and reversible after the cessation of NSAID consumption.
