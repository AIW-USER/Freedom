## Question

A 10-day-old infant is seen for evaluation of a right brachial plexus injury sustained during birth. The arm is flail. In discussing the prognosis with the family, which of the following is a favorable prognostic sign?

## Options

1. Return of biceps function by 3 months of age
2. Presence of unilateral anhidrosis and miosis
3. Presence of a shoulder internal rotation contracture
4. Caesarian delivery
5. Birth weight under 3 kilograms

## Correct option

**1. Return of biceps function by 3 months of age**

## Answer explanation

Treatment of obstetrical brachial plexus usually consists of physical therapy until about 3 months of age. If biceps function returns by that age, surgery generally is not needed. Surgery is typically indicated for patients with either no return of biceps function by 3 months of age or with a total plexus injury with a flail arm and Horner's syndrome 1 month after injury. The presence of Horner's syndrome (unilateral facial ptosis, enophthalmos, anhidrosis, and miosis) suggests an avulsion injury of the lower trunk, and is a poor prognostic sign.
