## Question

A 56-year-old male is putting up Christmas lights and falls from the ladder, sustaining the injury shown in Figures A and B. He undergoes operative fixation without any complications as shown in Figure C. Three years later, he continues to endorse knee pain with post-traumatic arthritic changes on radiographic evaluation. He is indicated to undergo hardware removal and total knee arthroplasty. He is counseled on the risks, benefits, and alternative treatments. It is discussed that most patients undergo total knee arthroplasty for primary osteoarthritis, however, his indication is for post-traumatic arthritis with prior tibial plateau fixation. What complication is this patient at increased risk for developing?

## Options

1. Poor functional outcomes
2. Limited knee flexion
3. Aseptic loosening
4. Heterotopic Ossification
5. Life Threatening Pulmonary Embolism

## Correct option

**2. Limited knee flexion**

## Answer explanation

When compared to standard total knee arthroplasty performed for non-traumatic osteoarthritis, patients undergoing total knee arthroplasty for post-traumatic osteoarthritis (PTOA) secondary to tibial plateau fractures are at an increased risk for stiffness requiring manipulation under anesthesia (option B).

Tibial plateau fractures are typically high-energy mechanism injuries in younger patients, as compared to low-energy injuries in elderly cohorts. Displaced, intra-articular tibial plateau fractures are considered a risk factor for subsequent post-traumatic arthritis. It is paramount to ensure that the limb mechanical axis is restored after operative fixation with the restoration of normal condylar width. These parameters are more predictive of postoperative outcomes than articular reduction. While the reported rates vary, patients who undergo tibial plateau operative fixation are approximately five times more likely to undergo a total knee replacement (TKA) to the affected knee compared to the general population. Further, if they undergo knee replacement they are at an increased risk for wound complications and post-operative stiffness.

*Weiss and colleagues performed a descriptive retrospective review regarding patient outcomes after being treated with TKA for post-traumatic arthritis from a previous fracture of the tibial plateau.* **They reported** high patient satisfaction with Knee Society Scores improving from 44 points for pain and 52 points for function preoperative to 83 and 84 points postoperative, respectively. However, 21% of patients required additional operative intervention for manipulation under anesthesia, wound revision, or component revision. **The authors concluded** that TKA in these patients can lead to significant pain and functional improvement, however with an increased reoperation rate.

*Scott and colleagues performed a matched cohort analysis of patients at their institution who underwent TKA after fracture of the tibial plateau compared to matched TKAs carried out for primary osteoarthritis.* **They reported** that patients with instability or nonunion needed TKA earlier than those with intra-articular malunion. **They also reported** higher complication rates in the post-traumatic group compared to primary osteoarthritis which included wound complications (13% vs 1%, respectively) and persistent stiffness (10% vs 0%). **The authors concluded** that TKA after fracture of the tibial plateau has a higher rate of complication, however, outcomes and satisfaction are comparable to TKAs performed for primary osteoarthritis.

Figure A and B show radiographic and CT imaging, respectively, of a split-depression fracture of the lateral tibial plateau (Schatzker II). Figure C shows intraoperative fluoroscopic imaging of this patient after open reduction internal fixation was performed. Most importantly, the mechanical axis and condylar width were restored.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: There are many case series showing that there is a relatively greater improvement in pain and function when TKA is performed for post-traumatic arthritis as compared to primary osteoarthritis. This is because the preoperative pain and functional status are reportedly worse in post-traumatic arthritis than in primary osteoarthritis.
Answer 3: The rate of aseptic loosening in TKA for post-traumatic arthritis secondary to tibial plateau fractures is comparable to that of patients with primary osteoarthritis.
Answer 4: Heterotopic Ossification is rarely reported as a complication after any indicated total knee arthroplasty.
Answer 5: Though some small series have found the rate of VTE as being higher in TKA for PTOA as compared to those with primary OA, the rate of life-threatening pulmonary embolism has not been demonstrated as being different between the two groups.
