## Options

1. Wide-resection of the arm, radiation and chemotherapy
2. Marginal resection and radiation
3. Limb salvage resection and chemotherapy
4. Marginal resection only
5. Percutaneous radiofrequency ablation and NSAIDS

## Correct option

**4. Marginal resection only**

## Answer explanation

This patient presents with symptomatic Schwannoma (neurilemmoma) of the forearm. Treatment is usually observational, but symptomatic masses may be treated with marginal resection alone.

Schwannomas are benign soft tissue tumors that are derived from schwann cells. They usually arise from the periphery of nerves, known as the epineurium. They most commonly originate in large peripheral nerves of the head, neck, and flexor surfaces of the upper extremities. Treatment of symptomatic lesions is usually marginal excision alone, as local recurrence rates are low (<1%) and malignant degeneration is extremely rare. An important feature of these tumors is the "percussion" sign - i.e., when tapped the patient experiences a sharp radiating pain in the distribution of the nerve. (They rarely cause pain at rest, this is in fact a worrisome sign of malignant nerve sheath tumours.)

*Lai et al. discussed the management of extremity neurilemmomas.* **They showed** that delicate enucleation of a neurilemmoma preserves most of the nerve fascicles and causes minimal nerve functional impairment.

*Koga et al. discussed the "Target sign" as a MRI finding suggestive of Schwannomas.* On T2 MR-images, the heterogenous mass shows low signal in the central area and high signal in the peripheral. This appearance is called the “Target Sign”.

Figures A is an ultrasound image of the mass showing a hypoechoic encapsulated lesion along the median nerve. Figure B shows saggital and coronal MR imaging of the mass. Figure C shows axial MR imaging. MR imaging shows isointense signaling relative to skeletal muscle on T1 weighted images and increased, slightly heterogenous signal intensity on T2 weighted images (known as the "Target signs"). Figure D shows biphasic orientations of cells with compact hypercellular Antoni A areas and myxoid hypocellular Antoni B areas typical of schwanomma.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1,2,3,4: Marginal treatment alone is acceptable for this pathology as the risk of recurrence is so low and malignant transformation is extremely rare.
