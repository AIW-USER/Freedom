## Question

A 17-year-old male high school football wide receiver is struck directly in the chest by an opposing player's helmet and stumbles to the ground. The training staff attends to him immediately but he is unresponsive on the field. Basic life-saving (BLS) protocols are followed and an AED is applied which detects ventricular fibrillation. Which of the following best describes the pathophysiology of this event?

## Options

1. Dynamic obstruction of blood outflow caused by enlarged left ventricle
2. Abnormal plaque build-up causing blockage of the coronary arteries
3. Direct impact during ventricular repolarization
4. Lengthening of the QT interval
5. Abnormality in AV node function

## Correct option

**3. Direct impact during ventricular repolarization**

## Answer explanation

Commotio cordis is a condition where blunt trauma affects cardiac repolarization and leads to arrhythmias, the most common being ventricular fibrillation.

Commotio cordis is rare but typically affects younger athletes who are participating in contact sports or sports involving a hard ball (baseball, lacrosse, etc). The cardiac event occurs when the athlete is struck directly over the myocardium during the phase of the cardiac rhythm where the ventricle is repolarizing. This leads to rapid alterations in ion channels which causes premature depolarization and can send the heart's rhythm into ventricular fibrillation. Basic life support (BLS) and advanced life cardiac support (ACLS) protocols should be followed which include immediate, high-quality CPR and rapid defibrillation. This is one of several reasons why AEDs should always be kept nearby at sporting events and training staff and medical providers should be able to quickly locate and efficiently use the device.

*Maron and Estes published a review article in the NEJM discussing commotio cordis.* **They note** that it is triggered by a blunt, nonpenetrating blow to the chest which sends the patient into a rhythm consistent with ventricular fibrillation. They go on to discuss the pathophysiology of the event as well as treatment, which includes rapid defibrillation and a complete cardiac workup.

*Link reviewed the pathophysiology, prevention, and treatment of commotio cordis.* **He notes** that those affected are commonly young males who are struck in the left side of the chest during a narrow window of ventricular repolarization which causes abnormalities of ion channels and leads to rapid depolarization and secondarily, ventricular fibrillation. He goes on to discuss prevention strategies as well as noting that treatment should be rapid life-saving measures such as defibrillation.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Dynamic obstruction of blood flow by an enlarged left ventricle describes hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, which is the most common cause of sudden cardiac death in young patients but is typically not related to direct contact injuries.
Answer 2: Abnormal plaque buildup and narrowing of the coronary arteries could cause angina or a myocardial infarction which would be unlikely in a young, healthy athlete.
Answer 4: Lengthening of the QT interval may occur naturally or due to certain medications but can lead to ventricular tachycardia and a torsades rhythm. It can ultimately lead to sudden cardiac death, but again, is not related to contact events.
Answer 5: Abnormalities in AV node function affect the transfer of electrical signals from the atrium to the ventricle and could lead to heart block.
