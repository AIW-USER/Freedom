## Question

A mother brings in her 5-year-old son for an evaluation. She notes that he is having difficulty with ambulation. When he stands on one leg, his pelvis drops to the opposite side. The appearance of his legs is shown in Figure A. What is the most likely genetic etiology of this condition?

![[q1528-a-calf-pseudohypertrophy.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Deletion in the Xp21 gene
2. Mutation in fibrillin-1 (FBN1) gene
3. Deletion of repeated elements of D4Z4 on chromosome 4q35
4. Mutation in the DMPK gene
5. Mutation in the CNBP gene

## Correct option

**1. Deletion in the Xp21 gene**

## Answer explanation

This patient exhibits calf pseudohypertrophy and the Trendelenburg sign which are suggestive of Duchenne's muscular dystrophy (DMD). DMD is caused by an absence of the dystrophin protein which is found on the Xp21 gene.

DMD is an X-linked recessive disease of muscle caused by the absence of the protein dystrophin. Affected males begin manifesting signs of disease early in life and develop progressive weakness beginning with proximal muscles first. As they develop gluteal muscle weakness, they exhibit the Trendelenburg sign. They have pseudohypertrophy of the calves as normal muscle is infiltrated by connective tissue.

*Sussman performed a review of DMD.* **He reports** that patients have a complete absence of the dystrophin protein in the muscle tissue. This abnormality is most frequently caused by a deletion of a segment of the gene. The result is a cessation of synthesis and rapid intracellular degradation of the nonsense protein.

*Shieh performed a review of muscular dystrophies.* **He reports** that DMD affects up to 1 in 3600 boys, making it the most common. **He reports** that 25% of mutations are sporadic in nature. If the mutation preserves the reading frame, it results in the milder Becker phenotype while mutations that disrupt the reading frame result in the Duchenne phenotype.

Figure A demonstrates calf pseudohypertrophy. Illustration A is a pedigree demonstrating X-linked recessive inheritance.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: A mutation in the fibrillin-1 gene causes Marfan syndrome.
Answer 3: A deletion of repeated elements of D4Z4 on chromosome 4q35 causes facioscapulohumeral muscular dystrophy.
Answers 4 & 5: Mutations in the DMPK gene or the CNBP gene cause myotonic dystrophy.
