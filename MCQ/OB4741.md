## Question

A 45-year-old woman undergoes surgical treatment for hallux valgus. One year later she presents with the painless foot deformity shown in Figure A. All of the following variables can lead to the development of this deformity EXCEPT:

![[q4741-a-fig-a-hallux-varus.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Resection of the fibular sesamoid
2. Resection of the tibial sesamoid
3. Avascular necrosis (AVN) of the first metatarsal head secondary to a distal osteotomy
4. Excessive postoperative bandaging
5. Excessive medial capsular reefing

## Correct option

**2. Resection of the tibial sesamoid**

## Answer explanation

Hallux varus is most commonly caused by forefoot surgery for correction of hallux valgus. The tibial sesamoid is not resected during hallux valgus surgery. Further, resection of the tibial sesamoid will not lead to hallux varus.

Factors implicated in the development of hallux varus after hallux valgus (HV) surgery or bunionectomy include: fibular sesamoid resection or detachment of adductor hallucis, overcorrection of the intermetatarsal angle, excessive removal of the medial eminence, medial tibial sesamoid subluxation, excessive plantar lateral release, excessive postoperative bandaging, osteoarthritis of the 1st metatarsophalangeal (MTP) joint, and malunion of a distal 1st metatarsal osteotomy.

*Grimes et al. reviewed 1st MTP joint fusion for treatment for failed HV surgery.* This procedure was successful in treating HV recurrence, hallux varus, cock-up deformity, degenerative arthritis of the 1st MTP joint, and transfer lesions beneath the lesser metatarsals.

*Skalley et al. presented an algorithm for treatment for acquired hallux varus.* Depending on etiology and presentaiton, treatment options include tendon transfer (EHL or EHB), arthrodesis, resection arthroplasty, soft tissue release, and osteotomy.

Figure A shows postoperative hallux varus deformity. Illustration A shows the attachments on the underside of the foot. Abductor hallucis brevis attaches to the tibial sesamoid, adductor hallucis attaches to the fibular sesamoid, flexor hallucis brevis (FHB) attaches to both sesamoids, and flexor hallucis longus (FHL) runs between the sesamoids in the FHB hiatus.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: This is the most common cause of hallux varus following hallux valgus surgery.
Answer 3: Avascular necrosis (AVN) of the first metatarsal head with collapse into varus is a known cause of hallux varus. AVN can occur after distal osteotomies.
Answer 4: Excessive postoperative bandaging, emphasizing a varus moment around the MTP joint, is associated with hallux varus.
Answer 5: Excessive medial capsular reefing, with detachment of the adductor hallucis and lateral head of the flexor hallucis brevis (but without fibular sesamoid resection), is the second most common cause of hallux varus following hallux valgus surgery.
