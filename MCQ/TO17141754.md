## Question

The “Iceberg concept” of TFCC was given by

1. Palmer and Werner
2. Nakamura
3. Atzei & Luchetti
4. Darrach

## Correct option

**3. Atzei & Luchetti**

## Answer explanation

Answer – C) Atzei & Luchetti

Triangular Fibrocartilage Complex

The Triangular fibrocartilage complex (TFCC) is an incredibly complex structure that provides intrinsic stability to the distal radioulnar joint and the ulnocarpal aspect of the wrist. It is named so because of its triangular anatomic as well as its structural shape.

The TFCC comprises of:

1. Superficial and deep limbs of the dorsal and volar radio-ulnar ligaments.

2. Fibro-cartilaginous disc and ulno-meniscal homologue.

3. Extensor Carpi Ulnaris (ECU) tendon sub-sheath.

4. Ulno-carpal ligaments on the volar aspect i.e. ulno-lunate and ulno-triquetral ligaments. These ligaments originate from corresponding carpus and insert on the palmar aspect of the radio-ulnar ligament and the fovea.

5. Ulnar collateral ligament (UCL)

Various authors have described the structure and function of the TFCC based on their observations

The two-dimensional structure of the TFCC by Palmer and Werner (1981)

TFCC formed by disc proper, meniscus homologue, volar and dorsal radio-ulnar ligaments, ulnar collateral ligament, the floor of the ECU, ulno-lunate and ulna triquetral ligaments. Volar and dorsal radio-ulnar ligaments surrounded the disc proper which in turn inserted on the ulna styloid. They were described as structures that supported the TFCC, rather than true ligaments.

The function of superficial and deep components of the volar and dorsal ligaments and the role of disc proper in DRUJ mechanics was not well understood then.

Three-dimensional structure of the triangular fibrocartilage complex of Nakamura (“Hammock” concept/ suspension theory)

TFCC is composed of fibrocartilage and attaches loosely to hyaline cartilage contributing to the wrist mobility and flexibility. Also, it firmly attaches to bone periosteum by Sharpey’s fibres.

The TFCC is divided into 3 zones:

1. Proximal portion: Its insertions into the triangular foveal ligament. It contributes to DRUJ stability.

2. Distal portion: Represents the “hammock” supporting the carpus.

3. Ulnar collateral ligament.

The three components suspend each other and their suspension arrangement and loose internal structure allows for both mobility and stability of the ulnar side of the wrist.

During forearm rotation, the central articular disc remains immobile, whereas the triangular ligament twists at its foveal insertion. Dynamic changes occur at the ulnar aspect of the TFCC and UCL.

The proximal portion of the TFCC reinforced the hammock structure which in turn supported the carpus. The morphology of the proximal portion of the TFCC was categorized as fan shape, V-shape or funnel shape in appearance. Its volar and dorsal margins were found to be thick and serve as stabilizing ligaments. The ulnar collateral ligament was described as the floor of the ECU and surrounding loose ligamentous structures. Also, UCL underwent deformation during pronation and supination. Ulnar joint capsule originated from the tip and middle portion of the ulna styloid and then coalesced with the meniscus homologue to constitute the ulnar wall of the TFCC. In extreme movements, the DRUJ capsule prevents the joint dislocation

Iceberg concept by Atzei & Luchetti

Further studies in the anatomy and biomechanics coupled with the advances in the surgical treatment of the TFCC injuries especially the wrist arthroscopy brought about changes in the perspective of the TFCC.

TFCC is further divided into proximal component (pc-TFCC) and distal component (dc-TFCC). This concept was proposed focusing on the nature of TFCC 1B avulsion injury.

a): TFCC is divided into (1) proximal component (pc-TFCC)- foveal attachment of the proximal triangular ligament. (2) Distal component (dc-TFCC)- including the UCL and hammock structure.

(b): The portion of TFCC that is visible during wrist arthroscopy functions as a shock absorber, however, the portion that is not visualized directly serves to provide DRUJ and proximal carpal row stability.

Pc-TFCC injuries cause DRUJ instability leading to reduced forearm rotation, ulnar sided wrist pain, and reduced grip strength. Also, persistent DRUJ instability causes dissatisfaction and leads to high reoperation rates. During the radio-carpal scope, the dc-TFCC represents the emerging tip of the iceberg and serves as a shock absorber. The most important portion of the TFCC is not visible directly through radiocarpal portals. Indirect evidence of it is obtained by hook sign. The pc-TFCC signifies the submerged portion of the iceberg, which rather holds greater functional importance. They can be visualized by DRUJ portals.
