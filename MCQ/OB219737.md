## Question

An 87-year-old female sustains a ground-level fall while attempting to get out of bed in her assisted living facility and is immediately unable to bear weight. The patient is brought to the emergency department by EMS, and radiographs are obtained, which demonstrate a displaced left femoral neck fracture. Which of the following is true regarding the possible treatment options for this patient?

## Options

1. There is no difference in aseptic revision rates when comparing cemented and uncemented stem fixation, but cemented stems are associated with a lower incidence of short-term postoperative periprosthetic femur fracture (PPFFx)
2. Cemented stem fixation is associated with both lower aseptic revision rates and a reduced incidence of short-term PPFFx as compared to uncemented fixation
3. In-hospital mortality is higher following uncemented hemiarthroplasty versus cemented hemiarthroplasty
4. When comparing uncemented collared stem designs, fit and fill stems have a lower one-year aseptic revision rate compared to single wedge stems
5. When comparing uncemented stem designs, collared stems are associated with higher aseptic revision rates compared to collarless stems

## Correct option

**2. Cemented stem fixation is associated with both lower aseptic revision rates and a reduced incidence of short-term PPFFx as compared to uncemented fixation**

## Answer explanation

Geriatric hip fractures are a common injury, and those involving the femoral neck are often treated with arthroplasty. Cemented stem fixation is associated with both lower aseptic revision rates and a reduced incidence of short-term periprosthetic postoperative femur fracture (PPFFx) when compared to uncemented/press-fit stems (Answer 2).

Femoral neck fractures are common injuries to the proximal femur and are becoming increasingly common due to an aging population. These injuries occur more commonly in Caucasian women, and the United States has the highest incidence of these injuries worldwide. In young patients, these injuries occur secondary to high-energy mechanisms. On the other hand, they typically result from low-energy falls in older patients.

These injuries are almost always treated operatively. While non-displaced or displaced fractures in young patients may be treated with screw fixation or open reduction with internal fixation, respectively, the mainstay of treatment in the elderly population is arthroplasty. While the debate regarding hemiarthroplasty vs. total hip arthroplasty remains heated, recent evidence continues to support the utilization of cemented femoral-sided fixation, especially when hemiarthroplasty is utilized, as this fixation method is associated with both lower rates of aseptic revision and a reduced incidence of short-term postoperative femur fracture.

*Okike et al. investigated the association between femoral stem design and the risk of aseptic revision after hemiarthroplasty.* *Their study included 12,071 patients with a mean age of 82.0 years.* Overall, uncemented stems were associated with a markedly higher risk of aseptic revision. No difference in in-hospital mortality between cemented and uncemented fixation was observed.

*Springer et al. investigated the incidence of short-term postoperative periprosthetic femur fracture following both total and hip hemiarthroplasty.* *The authors used the American Joint Replacement Registry data from 2012-2017, and their study included 10,277 revisions.* Overall, patients with uncemented stems were 2.6 times more likely to undergo early revision for short-term postoperative periprosthetic femur fracture than those with cemented fixation.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: cemented stem fixation is associated with both lower aseptic revision rates and a reduced incidence of PPFFx.
Answer 3: there is no difference in in-hospital mortality when comparing the two stem fixation techniques.
Answers 4 and 5: there is no difference in aseptic revision rate when comparing single-wedge and fit-and-fill collared stems or when comparing collared and collarless stems.
