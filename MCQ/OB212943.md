## Question

Which of the following has most effectively reduced radiation exposure in pediatric trauma patients?

## Options

1. Increased use of fluoroscopy in injury evaluation
2. Implementing clinical practice guidelines resulting in less CT for cervical spine clearance
3. Routine use of CT with low-dose radiation protocols
4. Avoiding radiographs to vital organ regions whenever possible
5. MRI for all patient cervical spine clearance

## Correct option

**2. Implementing clinical practice guidelines resulting in less CT for cervical spine clearance**

## Answer explanation

Updated practice guidelines with the use of NEXUS criteria aim to reduce radiation exposure in cervical spine clearance in pediatric trauma patients, which decreases the utilization of computed tomography for cervical spine clearance.

Cervical spine trauma in pediatric patients poses a challenge for evaluating physicians. Children under three years of age lack the mental development to cooperate with clinical cervical spine clearances. Furthermore, the cervical spine in children has more ligamentous laxity as well as open physes which are sites for injury. Regular CT evaluation in these patients may overlook a soft tissue based injury, while also exposing them to increased radiation. Preferably clearance should be done clinically if possible and cervical spine radiographs to screen for bone injuries. If radiographs are abnormal then CT evaluation is warranted.

*Rosati et al. performed a retrospective review at a level 1 trauma center, which included 233 pediatric trauma patients.* *The authors evaluated the use of CT in C-spine clearance before and after an updated clearance protocol.* Postimplementation there was a 23% decrease in CT use in NEXUS clearable spines.

*Connelly et al. performed a retrospective review including 2299 pediatric trauma patients before and after implementation of an updated cervical spine evaluation protocol.* After implementation, there was a decrease in CT clearance with increased utilization of radiography. Further, there was a 54% decrease in thyroid cancer risk from decreased radiation exposure.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: Radiation exposure with fluoroscopy is relatively less compared to conventional radiographs. However, there is less diagnostic yield of these images which make them less useful in the trauma setting.
Answer 3: Low dose radiation CT protocols have been documented to reduce radiation exposure, this has not been established in the pediatric trauma setting.
Answer 4: Decreasing the use of traditional radiographs would decrease radiation exposure compared to the standard views. However, this would increase the chances of missing injuries in trauma evaluation. Further, CT scans count for the majority of radiation exposure suggesting that limiting radiographs would have a negligible effect on radiation exposure overall.
Answer 5: Use of MRI for injury evaluation would decrease radiation exposure, but the time required to perform this study and it's effectiveness in the trauma setting has not been established. Additionally, radiographs are better screening studies and when abnormal necessitate the use of CT or MRI for further analysis based on cost, time to complete study, and radiations exposure.
