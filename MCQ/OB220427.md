## Question

A 13-year-old female presents to the orthopedic clinic one week after suffering a left knee patellar dislocation while playing competitive soccer due to an opposing player impacting the medial aspect of her left knee. The sideline athletic trainer reduced her patella, and the patient was taken to an urgent care facility. Plain films taken at that time showed a suprapatellar effusion with no acute osseous abnormality. MRI confirmed no osteochondral injury. The athlete would like to return to play and finish the season. Which of the following risk factors is associated with an increased risk of another patellar dislocation?

## Options

1. Age
2. Caton Deschamps ratio of 1.1
3. Tibial tubercule-trochlear groove distance of 11mm
4. Medial patellofemoral ligament rupture
5. Sex

## Correct option

**1. Age**

## Answer explanation

This patient presents after a first-time patella dislocation. Age, open physes, patella alta, trochlear dysplasia, and an elevated tibial tuberosity-trochlear groove (TT-TG) distance are all risk factors associated with an increased risk of recurrent dislocation. Therefore, this patient's age places her at an increased risk of a subsequent patellar dislocation (answer choice 1).

Patellofemoral instability (PFI) is common among young athletes. Recurrent dislocations after a first-time patellar dislocation occur in approximately 1/3 of patients. Initial evaluation after closed reduction is focused on identifying the cause of dislocation and concomitant injuries. A thorough history and physical examination, including evaluation of generalized ligamentous hyperlaxity, is a key portion of the evaluation. Plain radiographs are utilized for initial diagnosis of the injury. They can also be used to evaluate patella baja or patella alta as measured by different methods including Insall-Salvati (normal 0.8-1.2), Blackburne-Peel (normal 0.5-1.0), Caton Deschamps (normal 0.6-1.3), or the Plateau-patella angle (normal 20-30 degrees). Advanced imaging is also often used to assess injury patterns and anatomy. MRI can be used to characterize ligamentous and chondral abnormalities, measure TT-TG, and assess trochlear dysplasia. The treatment of PFI should be individualized to each patient based on injury pattern, activity level, and risk factors for recurrent instability.

*Pauyo et al published a review article regarding the evaluation and nonsurgical treatment of PFI.* *The authors reviewed the anatomy and mechanisms of instability and recommend a structured therapy protocol with early mobilization and dynamic bracing.* **The authors report** that risk factors for recurrent instability include trochlear dysplasia, patella alta, increased TT-TG, torsional malalignment, and age at first dislocation.

*Huntington et al published the results of a systematic review and meta-analysis regarding factors associated with an increased risk of recurrence after a first-time dislocation.* **The authors reported** that younger age (less than 16 or 18), open physes, trochlear dysplasia, elevated TT-TG, and patella alta are all associated with an increased risk of recurrent instability. Sex and the presence or type of MPFL injury were not found to be predictive of increased recurrence. **The authors conclude** that the presence of 3 or more factors increases the risk of recurrence to over 70%.

Incorrect answer choices:
Answer 2: A CD ratio of 1.1 is within normal limits and not indicative of patella alta.
Answer 3: A TT-TG under 12 is not a risk factor for recurrent instability.
Answer 4: MPFL injury is not associated with an increased risk of recurrent instability.
Answer 5: Female sex is not associated with an increased risk of recurrent instability.
