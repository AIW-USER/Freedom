## Question

A 71-year-old female presents for a follow-up of severe lower back pain and leg pain due to degenerative scoliosis. She has tried physical therapy, injections, activity modifications, and pain medications without significant improvement. She currently takes apixaban for atrial fibrillation and is on insulin for type 2 diabetes. Her last Hb A1c was 6.3%. She currently smokes 5 cigarettes per day but is cutting back. She is taking oxycodone 10 mg every 12 hours as prescribed by her pain management physician. Her last DEXA scan was significant for a T score of -1.7. What is the best next step for this patient?

## Options

1. Schedule for surgery with no increased risk of post-operative complications
2. Schedule for surgery but advise patient to switch to subcutaneous heparin prior to surgery
3. Refer to rheumatologist for teriparatide treatment and follow-up in 6 months
4. Refer to smoking cessation clinic and follow-up for nicotine tests in 6 weeks
5. Post-pone surgery until patient is no longer taking narcotics

## Correct option

**4. Refer to smoking cessation clinic and follow-up for nicotine tests in 6 weeks**

## Answer explanation

Smoking places patients at increased risk of pseudoarthrosis following spine surgery. Patients undergoing elective procedures should be postponed until they have ceased cigarette smoking.

Tobacco use places patients undergoing spine surgery to a multitude of complications. The impaired vascular function and effects on bone biology negatively impact the ability to form a solid fusion mass. The biochemical rationale for this remains unclear, but a multifactorial contribution from cigarette smoke is believed to be responsible. Patients undergoing elective procedures should quit tobacco use prior to surgery. This can prevent the development of pseudoarthrosis, wound healing complications, or deep infections.

*Daffner et al. performed an in vitro study of bone formation affected by nicotine levels.* **They reported** that osteoblast activity was increased in a dose-dependent manner as determined by alkaline phosphatase levels and mineralization. **They concluded** that nicotine may not be the culprit for increased pseudoarthrosis rates in smokers.

*Porter and Hanley reviewed the musculoskeletal effects of smoking.* **They stated** cigarette smoking is associated with decreased bone mineral density, increased degenerative disc disease, increased hip fracture rates, and wound healing complications. **They concluded** that current published data support a direct relationship between these complications and cigarette smoking.

*Raizman et al. reviewed pseudoarthrosis of the spine.* **They state** multiple factors are known contributors to pseudoarthrosis including patient factors, instrumentation selection, grafting material, and surgical techniques. **They concluded** symptomatic patients should be treated with a revision arthrodesis.

Incorrect answers
Answer 1: The patient is at increased risk of pseudoarthrosis and wound complications due to her active tobacco use. This modifiable risk factor should be optimized prior to proceeding with surgery.
Answer 2: The patient should be converted to a heparin-based anticoagulation regimen prior to surgery to allow for the apixaban to clear her system. However, she should not be scheduled with active tobacco use.
Answer 3: The patient has osteopenia per her last DEXA scan which does not necessitate referral for anabolic medications.
Answer 5: Preoperative narcotic usage places the patient at increased risk of complication and makes post-operative pain management increasingly challenging. However, proceeding with surgery is recommended in patients with clear pathology that necessitates narcotic pain control. Perioperative management with pain specialists is recommended in these scenarios.
