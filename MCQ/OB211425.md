## Question

A 21-year-old recreational athlete lands awkwardly after a basketball rebound. He presents with lateral ankle pain and swelling, and reports that he feels as if his ankle is unstable. Plain radiographs were obtained initially as he was unable to bear weight and are depicted in Figures A and B. An MRI is obtained by his primary care physician and depicted in Figure C. ## Options

1. Sinus tarsi corticosteroid injection with ankle taping during basketball
2. Lateral ankle ligament repair with suture tape augmentation
3. Lateral ankle reconstruction with a peroneal tendon autograft
4. Early weightbearing, functional bracing and peroneal muscle strengthening
5. Immobilization in walking boot for 4-6 weeks with delayed proprioception exercises

## Correct option

**4. Early weightbearing, functional bracing and peroneal muscle strengthening**

## Answer explanation

This patient is presenting with no osseous abnormalities on plain radiography in the setting of lateral ankle swelling, and MRI findings of an anterior talofibular ligament (ATFL) injury. The best next step in management is early weightbearing, use of a protective brace during functional activities, and peroneal muscle and proprioception exercises.

The ATFL is the most common ligament injured with low ankle sprains and often occurs with inversion of the foot in slight plantar flexion. Its integrity is assessed with the ankle anterior drawer test, which is performed with the ankle in plantarflexion. Neither plain radiographs nor advanced imaging (i.e. MRI) are indicated when following the Ottawa ankle rules in evaluation of acute ankle sprains. The mainstay of treatment for acute ATFL injuries is symptomatic treatment with rest, ice, compression, elevation, with early functioning bracing, early active range of motion exercises, proprioceptive training with a tilt-board, and peroneal strengthening. Surgery is less commonly performed in the acute setting and reserved for chronic pain and instability recalcitrant to nonoperative management.

*Tropp et al. reviewed ankle sprain prevention strategies in soccer players, comparing coordination training with orthotics.* **They reported** that orthotics are an alternative to taping, and can be used during the rehabilitation period after injury. **They also reported** that to prevent functional instability, coordination training must be included during the rehabilitation process. **They concluded** that the above methods may be considered prophylactically in players with previous ankle problems to decrease the incidence of recurrent sprains.

*Hertel et al. investigated the effects of four different types of immobilization devices (Tubigrip compression bandage, Bledsoe boot, Aircast brace, and below-knee cast) on the outcomes of patients recovering from severe ankle sprains.* **They observed** that below-knee casting for 10 days resulted in quicker symptom and pain resolution and the greatest recovery of self-reported ankle function at 3 months compared with the other three treatments. **They concluded** that their findings are controversial since prior consensus recommendations have been towards early functional treatment of ankle sprains.

*Osborne et al. reviewed the prevention and treatment of ankle sprains in athletes.* **They reported** that ankle srpain prevention programs and ankle rehabilitation techniques including proprioceptive exercises protect the joint from sprain recurrence. **They concluded** that surgical management is reserved for patients who fail a comprehensive non-operative treatment program and those with chronic functional instability.

Figures A and B demonstrate normal ankle radiographs. Figure C is an MRI demonstrating an ATFL injury. Illustration A is the same MRI cut from Figure C with the ATFL identified by the blue arrow. Illustration B demonstrates the ankle anterior drawer test, performed with the ankle in plantarflexion.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Sinus tarsi corticosteroid injection with ankle taping is not a well-accepted technique in treating ATFL injuries and would not prevent recurrence.
Answer 2 and 3: While both of these may be reasonable surgical treatments in chronic cases recalcitrant to conservative measures, the best next step in the acute setting is early functional rehabilitation.
Answer 5: Immobilization for 4-6 weeks is not indicated and would result in ankle deconditioning and recurrence of ankle sprains when discontinued.
