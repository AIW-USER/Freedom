## Question

A 25-year-old ballet dancer presents with vague pain over the dorsomedial aspect of the foot with occasional radiation into the first web space. She sustained a foot injury 6 months ago that was never investigated. Physical examination shows no sensory loss and mild atrophy of extensor digitorum brevis muscle. Radiographs of the foot are seen in Figure A. What is the most likely diagnosis?

![[q5515-a-tarsal-arthritis-xray.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Missed compartment syndrome of the foot
2. Anterior tarsal tunnel syndrome
3. L5/S1 radiculopathy
4. Morton's neuroma
5. Complex regional pain syndrome

## Correct option

**2. Anterior tarsal tunnel syndrome**

## Answer explanation

This patient presents with the classic presentation of anterior tarsal tunnel syndrome.

Anterior tarsal tunnel syndrome usually presents with a dull ache, numbness and paresthesias on the dorsum of the foot, radiating to the first interdigital space. These symptoms may be exacerbated by certain positions of the foot (e.g., plantar flexion of the foot), inactivity, or when wearing tight shoes. Pain is related to compression of the the deep peroneal nerve (DPN) or its branches, as it cross through a fibro-osseous canal (known as the anterior tarsal tunnel) that is deep to the extensor hallucis longus and extensor digitorum longus in the foot.

*Liu et al. reviewed 10 patients treated for anterior tarsal tunnel syndrome produced by compression of the deep peroneal nerve or its branches.* The presentation most commonly occurred after contusion of the dorsum of the foot, tight shoe laces, talonavicular osteophytosis, ganglion, and pes cavus. Diagnosis was often clinical, but electromyography was used for confirmation. Operative decompression gave successful results at 1.5 to 4 years follow-up in all patients.

*Eibel et al. reviewed anterior tarsal tunnel syndrome.* The most common etiology for anterior tibial tunnel syndrome included, deep peroneal compression by scar tissue, osteophytes, synovial cysts, tendon ganglions, hypertrophic extensor foot muscles, trauma, or dorsalis pedis artery aneurysm. Anterior tarsal tunnel syndrome may cause atrophy of the extensor digitorum brevis muscle.

Figure A shows a lateral radiograph of the foot. There is a mildly displaced, dorsal avulsion fracture fragment involving the navicular bone which is likely compressing the deep peroneal nerve in the foot. Illustration A demonstrates dorsal foot anatomy (a) and a common site of deep peroneal nerve compression (b).

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Missed compartment syndrome of the foot usually results in chronic, debilitating pain. These patients usually present with an intrinsic-minus deformity arising from interosseous and lumbrical muscle atrophy and subsequent fibrosis. Diffuse or patchy paresis and anesthesia present more often than a well defined dermatome.
Answer 3: L5/S1 radiculopathy may present with radiating pain and dermatomal numbness. However, this patient describes a specific history of foot injury and persistent dorsal pain.
Answer 4: Morton's neuroma occurs with compression/tension of the interdigital nerve around the transverse intermetatarsal ligament. This most commonly involves the 2nd and 3rd interdigital nerve between the metatarsal heads.
Answer 5:The most common symptom of complex regional pain syndrome is diffuse pain and sensitivity in the affected region, which including burning, stabbing, grinding, and throbbing pain. Moving or touching the limb is often intolerable.
