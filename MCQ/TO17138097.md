Chapter: Nerve Injury MCQs — Ulnar Nerve Palsy

## Question

Wartenberg sign is not caused by

## Options

1. Ulnar nerve palsy
2. Third volar interosseous muscle avulsion
3. Cervical cord injury
4. Median nerve palsy

## Correct option

**4. Median nerve palsy**

## Answer explanation

Wartenberg’s sign: Wartenberg’s sign, as described by Wartenberg, “consists of a position of the abduction ASSUMED by the little finger”. As Wartenberg noted, adduction of the little finger is performed by interosseous muscles, and abduction is performed by hypothenar muscles, both innervated by the ulnar nerve. However, abduction is also performed by the extensor digiti minimi and the extensor digitorum communis to the little finger, both innervated by the radial nerve. If the ulnar nerve is injured, intact radial nerve function can result in unopposed abduction of the little finger. Wartenberg noted that this finding is much more pronounced with fingers in extension than flexion. He also noted that occasionally the ring finger could also assume a position of abduction.

It should be noted that not all cases of the abducted little finger are due to ulnar nerve palsy. Other rare causes of abducted little finger include:

- mild hemiparesis “digiti quinti sign”

- hemiplegic migraine

- cervical cord compression

- avulsion of the insertion of the third volar interosseous muscle.

These seem to be rarer causes of the abduction of the little finger, and ulnar nerve palsy should be high on the list when evaluating patients with a Wartenberg’s sign.

Answer: 4
