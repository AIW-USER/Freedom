## Question

A 59-year-old man reports a 1-month history of nagging thigh pain. A radiograph is shown in Figure 50. A physical examination and a review of systems are noncontributory. What is the next most appropriate step in management?

![[q6510-a-50.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis
2. Biopsy
3. Retrograde intramedullary nail
4. Distal femoral replacement
5. Radiation therapy

## Correct option

**1. CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis**

## Answer explanation

The patient has a bone lesion in the setting of an unknown primary. Given his age, the most likely diagnosis is metastatic carcinoma. The work-up of an unknown primary consists of a complete physical examination, a thorough review of systems, blood work, CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis, and a whole body bone scan. Treatment, including any surgery or adjuvants (radiation therapy or chemotherapy), is inappropriate without a diagnosis. Biopsy ideally should be performed once complete diagnostic and staging studies have been completed.
