## Question

A patient presents to the trauma bay with a closed intra-articular calcaneus fracture after falling from a roof. After initial radiographs and splint placement, you are evaluating the CT scan; in the coronal plane at the widest portion of the posterior facet, there are two distinct displaced fracture lines. With this new information, how should you counsel the patient?

## Options

1. This injury does not need surgical fixation and can be treated in a splint
2. Operative fixation with anatomic articular reduction is the most important factor in decreasing the chance of post-traumatic arthritis
3. The initial energy and fracture severity best correlates with the development of post-traumatic arthritis
4. This injury requires primary subtalar arthrodesis
5. This injury is associated with an increased Bohlers angle and a decreased anlge of Gissane portending negative outcomes

## Correct option

**3. The initial energy and fracture severity best correlates with the development of post-traumatic arthritis**

## Answer explanation

The patient sustained a Sanders type III fracture of the calcaneus, indicating a significant level of fracture severity, and endured initial energy. Increasing fracture severity, as denoted by the Sanders Classification is correlated with an increased risk of post-traumatic arthritis development.

The Sanders classification is determined in the coronal plane on CT of the calcaneus at the widest portion of the posterior facet. The number of distinct fracture lines and the resulting fracture fragments determines the Sanders Type, which is demonstrated in Illustration A. Specifically, Sanders type III fractures (two fracture lines in the posterior facet or three fragments) often disrupt the anatomical alignment and congruity of the subtalar joint. The loss of joint congruity predisposes the patient to uneven weight distribution and abnormal subtalar joint mechanics, increasing the likelihood of degenerative changes and post-traumatic arthritis.

*Sanders et al. conducted a study aimed to assess the prognostic value of the Sanders CT scan classification in predicting outcomes for patients with displaced intra-articular calcaneal fractures (DIACFs) treated with open reduction and internal fixation (ORIF), specifically comparing type II versus type III fractures.* Findings revealed that after a minimum of 10 years, type III fractures were 4 times more likely to require subtalar arthrodesis compared to type II fractures, indicating the prognostic value of the Sanders classification.

*Rao et al. retrospectively reviewed fracture severity in DIACFs and assessed its correlation with Sanders classification, post-traumatic osteoarthritis, and patient outcomes.* Thirty-six patients with 48 DIACFs were analyzed using preoperative CT scans graded by the Sanders classification and quantified for fracture severity using CT-based measures of fracture energy. Results showed that fracture energy correlated positively with Sanders classification, indicating that higher fracture energy corresponds to more severe fractures.

*Schindler et al. retrospectively reviewed the outcomes of surgically treated intra-articular calcaneus fractures.* A high rate of posttraumatic subtalar arthritis (77%) and subsequent subtalar fusion (18%) without independent risk factors for fusion were observed. The revision rate after primary fusion was notably high at 60%. In summation, the study provides valuable insights for treatment decision-making in calcaneal fractures.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Sanders type III fractures are treated operatively unless there are medical comorbidities or other factors precluding surgery.
Answer 2: Anatomic reduction of the articular surface is desirable and may improve outcomes but is not as influential as initial fracture energy/severity in the development of post-traumatic arthritis.
Answer 4: Primary subtalar arthrodesis is associated with a high complication rate and is typically reserved for Sanders IV calcaneus fractures.
Answer 5: Intra-articular calcaneus fractures are associated with a decreased Bohlers angle and increased angle of Gissane which represents depression of the posterior facet and intra-articular incongruity.
