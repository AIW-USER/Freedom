## Question

A 37-year-old man undergoes radial head excision for the treatment of a chronic malunion of a Monteggia fracture-dislocation with persistent radiocapitellar dislocation. Which of the following structures must remain competent to prevent valgus elbow instability after radial head excision?

## Options

1. Anterior bundle of the medial collateral ligament
2. Posterior bundle of the medial collateral ligament
3. Brachialis insertion
4. Radial collateral ligament
5. Lateral ulnar collateral ligament

## Correct option

**1. Anterior bundle of the medial collateral ligament**

## Answer explanation

The anterior bundle of the medial collateral ligament (MCL) is the primary stabilizer of the elbow to valgus-directed force. In the setting of radial head excision, a competent medial collateral ligament is essential to prevent global instability of the elbow.

Patients who undergo nonanatomic reduction and internal fixation for Monteggia fracture-dislocations can result in malunion and persistent radiocapitellar dislocation. After the fracture has healed, osteotomy of the ulna could be considered in an attempt to restore proper alignment, but treatment usually focuses on the chronic attenuation of the collateral ligaments and insufficiency of the coronoid process. Because of malalignment of the proximal radioulnar joint, it often is necessary to resect the radial head to provide forearm rotation. Excision of the radial head does not drastically affect elbow stability as long as the MCL is intact.

*Ring and Jupiter conducted a narrative review of the reconstruction of posttraumatic elbow instability.* *They present the primary constituents of medial, lateral, anterior, and posterior elbow stability: the anterior bundle of the MCL, the lateral ulnar collateral ligament (LUCL), the coronoid process, and the olecranon process, respectively.* *They discuss several injury patterns and treatment options.* In the setting of a malaligned proximal ulnar fracture that has not healed yet, **they recommend** revision internal fixation. In a healed trans-olecranon fracture dislocation with persistent dislocation, **they recommend** radial head excision to restore forearm rotation, in addition to the reconstruction of the medial and lateral collateral ligaments.

Ring, Jupiter, and Waters review the anatomy, classification, and management of Monteggia fractures in both children and adults. They emphasize the importance of anatomic reduction of the proximal ulna to facilitate persistent reduction of the radiocapitellar joint. They recommend intraoperative fluoroscopic confirmation through all points of motion to confirm an anatomic radiocapitellar reduction.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: The posterior and transverse bundles of the MCL are secondary stabilizers of the elbow to valgus stress
Answer 3: The brachialis muscle is a secondary dynamic stabilizer of the ulnohumeral joint to anterior dislocation
Answer 4, 5: The radial collateral ligament and LUCL are stabilizers of the elbow to varus stress
