## Question

A 23-year-old male Olympic rower presents with left sided chest pain exacerbated by cough. Bone scan results are shown in Figure A. Pain started 6 weeks after switching to a new oar with a bigger blade and a change in training regimen to include long-endurance rows at low stroke rates. Which of the following is correct?

![[q4718-a-fig-a.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Inspiration and expiration chest radiographs are necessary to exclude pneumothorax
2. Operative stabilization is indicated
3. The bone scan appearance of this lesion differs when caused by chronic cough rather than rowing
4. Treatment typically includes rest, analgesia, and slow return to rowing
5. A modified rowing pattern involving more scapula protraction at the beginning of the stroke, and more retraction at the end of the stroke is recommended to prevent further lesions

## Correct option

**4. Treatment typically includes rest, analgesia, and slow return to rowing**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a rib stress fracture. The treatment involves rest, analgesia, and a slow return to rowing.

Rib stress fractures (RSF) occur more commonly in elite rowers than non-elite rowers. Most rowing RSF cases occur in ribs 4 -8 (or 5-9), mostly along the anterolateral/lateral rib cage. The incidence is equal among sweep rowers (1 oar) and scullers (2 oars). Increased risk is associated with amenorrhoea, low bone density or poor technique, in combination with increases in training volume. Injury management involves 1–2 weeks cessation of rowing, analgesia, and a slow return to rowing with low-impact intensity and modified pain-free training.

*Karlson et al. presented a case series on RSFs in rowers.* *They hypothesized that RSFs occur because of the deforming forces of the serratus anterior and external oblique.* Another factor is introduction of a bigger blade, yielding faster speeds at increased load per stroke, and increases in training volume, including long endurance rows. **They proposed** a modified stroke with less reach and pull through to decrease the forces transmitted to the rib by these muscles.

*McDonnell et al. reviewed rib stress fractures.* Increased risk may be due to low calcium, low vitamin D, eating disorders, low testosterone or use of depot medroxyprogesterone injections. Treatment involves 1–2 weeks cessation of rowing, analgesia, and a slow return to rowing with low-impact intensity.

Figure A shows the typical appearance of a rib stress fracture. Illustration A shows the attachments of the serratus anterior and external oblique muscles on ribs 5-9.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: RSFs may occasionally show up on chest radiographs as a fracture line or callus. Inspiration and expiration radiographs are used to identify pneumothorax, which is not associated with rib stress fractures.
Answer 2: These injuries are treated as previously mentioned, and are only stabilized operatively when recalcitrant to closed means.
Answer 3: Cough-related RSFs occur at the anterior to posterior axillary line of ribs 5-9. On bone scan, rowing-related RSFs appear identical to those caused by chronic cough.
Answer 5: A modified rowing pattern involving LESS scapula protraction at the beginning, and LESS retraction at the end of stroke has been proposed but this has not been evaluated clinically.
