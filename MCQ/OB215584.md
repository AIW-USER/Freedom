## Question

A 29-year-old male sustained an open distal third tibial shaft fracture after a motocross accident. Thorough irrigation and debridement followed by external fixation were performed on the day of admission. It is determined during the initial operation that flap coverage will ultimately be required. Which of the following represents the best choice for coverage in this patient?

## Options

1. Gastrocnemius flap within 7 days
2. Soleus flap within 7 days
3. Free flap within 7 days
4. Gastrocnemius flap within 11 days
5. Free flap within 11 days

## Correct option

**3. Free flap within 7 days**

## Answer explanation

Free flaps are the most reliable option for coverage of the distal third of the tibia and should be performed within 7 days.

Open high-energy distal third tibial shaft fractures are commonly treated in a staged manner with irrigation, debridement, and external fixation performed at the index procedure. Subsequent procedures are performed for definitive fixation and soft tissue management. The lack of local donor muscle in the distal tibial region makes free flap coverage the most reliable option. Furthermore, coverage should be performed within 7 days to decrease the risk of developing complications including deep infection, osteomyelitis, and/or amputation.

*Pincus et al. review outcomes as it relates to timing of coverage for open tibia fractures.* **They report** an overall increase in complications in patients in which final coverage was performed >7 days after the injury. Each additional week delay in coverage was associated with a 40% increase in risk of complications. **They conclude** that coverage should be performed within 7 days whenever possible.

*Jenkinson et al. review delayed wound closure in patients with low-grade open fractures.* **They report** a 4% deep infection rate in patients treated with immediate closure compared to 18% in those with delayed primary closure. **They conclude** that immediate closure of carefully selected wounds by experienced surgeons is safe and associated with improved infection rates compared to delayed primary closure.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Gastrocnemius flaps are best for proximal third tibial wounds and do not have the excursion to reach the distal leg.
Answer 2: Soleus flaps are best for middle third wounds and are not used for coverage in the distal leg.
Answer 4 & 5: Coverage occurring >7 days after injury is associated with increased complications.
