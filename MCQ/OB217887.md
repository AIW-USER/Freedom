## Question

A 52-year-old female with post-traumatic arthritis of the elbow is being seen in your office to discuss total elbow arthroplasty (TEA). You schedule her for a semi-constrained TEA. When discussing postoperative complications with her, what is the most common mode of early failure?

## Options

1. Aseptic component loosening
2. Bushing wear
3. Component fracture
4. Infection
5. Ulnar neuropathy

## Correct option

**4. Infection**

## Answer explanation

Infection is the most common mode of early failure after TEA. It typically occurs within the first 5 years and has an overall rate of approximately 5-8%

Total elbow arthroplasty (TEA) is an increasingly used motion-preserving modality for the treatment of many debilitating elbow pathologies. Primary indications include rheumatoid arthritis, post-traumatic arthritis, and intra-articular distal humeral fractures in the elderly with poor bony quality. Semiconstrained implants have the best longevity and most optimal functional outcomes. Several postoperative complications may occur with short-term, intermediate-term, and long-term follow-up. Acute infection (< 30 days) is often treated with aggressive serial irrigation and debridement and antibiotic bead placement. The success of this treatment depends on the organism; staphylococcus epidermidis is associated with persistent infection because it is an encapsulating organism, and it is best treated with implant removal and IV antibiotic.

*Throckmorton et al. reviewed TEA for the treatment of posttraumatic arthritis.* **They reported** that semiconstrained TEA in patients with posttraumatic arthritis places high demands on the implant and is associated with a relatively high failure rate. **They concluded** that 75% of failures occur in patients <60 years of age, and infection continues to represent a frequent mode of early failure, while bushing wear and component loosening or fracture are seen more commonly in the intermediate and late term; whereas loosening remains relatively uncommon.

*Morrey et al. reviewed TEA use for acute injuries and posttraumatic conditions of the elbow.* **They reported** that the ideal prosthetic implant appears to be linked and semiconstrained, with an anterior flange to resist posterior and rotatory forces. **They noted** that the ability to fix the stem without condyle preservation is important in treating a posttraumatic condition. **They concluded** that the results of TEA can deteriorate over time because of periprosthetic fracture, implant fracture, bushing wear, or other mechanical failure; the rate of aseptic loosening is <10% after 10 years. However, approximately 25% of patients have a complication.

*Schneeberger et al. reviewed 41 consecutive patients that were managed for post-traumatic osteoarthrosis or dysfunction of the elbow with use of a non-customized semiconstrained Coonrad-Morrey TEA.* **They reported** that 27% of patients had a major complication including ulnar component fractured and polyethylene bushings wear. **They noted** that these complications were attributed principally to the performance of strenuous physical labor, such as lifting more than ten kilograms on a regular basis, against the advice of the surgeon; excessive preoperative deformity of the joint; or an unstable traumatic injury. **They concluded** that semiconstrained joint replacement of the elbow can be a reliable form of treatment, and frequently is the only viable option, for the difficult problems encountered with post-traumatic destruction of a joint; however, the mechanical failures underscore the fact that this procedure is relatively contraindicated in patients who anticipate strenuous physical activity or who are not expected to comply with the postoperative protocol.

Incorrect Answer:
Answer 1: Aseptic component loosening to be uncommon (<10% of patients) and usually occurring >10 years after surgery
Answer 2: Bushing wear has been reported as the most common cause of mechanical TEA failure in this population at intermediate-term follow-up
Answer 3: Prosthetic fracture, usually of the ulnar component, is also a late-term finding
Answer 5: Ulnar neuropathy may occur following TEA, but is not a mode of implant failure
