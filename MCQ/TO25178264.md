Chapter: Regional Orthopedics MCQs — Rheumatoid Arthritis Involvement of The Hand

## Question

When performing a Nalebuff Type I reconstruction for a rheumatoid thumb (Boutonnière), what is the goal of the surgical intervention?

## Options

1. Fusion of the CMC joint
2. Tendon transfer of the FPL
3. Release of the adductor pollicis
4. Restoration of MCP extension and stabilization of the IP joint

## Correct option

**4. Restoration of MCP extension and stabilization of the IP joint**

## Answer explanation

The Type I deformity involves MCP flexion and IP hyperextension; reconstruction focuses on the extensor mechanism at the MCP level.

The Rheumatoid Thumb is often more functionally disabling than the other digits because it accounts for nearly 40-50% of hand function. The stability of the "key pinch" and "power grip" depends entirely on the integrity of the thumb's three joints (CMC, MCP, and IP).

Pathoanatomy: The "Zigzag" Collapse

In the thumb, the collapse follows the same biomechanical principles as the fingers: failure at one joint leads to a reciprocal deformity at the next.

 - Synovitis destroys the joint capsule and ligaments.

 - Muscle Imbalance: The intrinsic muscles (Adductor Pollicis) and extrinsic muscles (EPL/FPL) become "bowstrings," pulling the joints into deformed positions to compensate for instability.

Nalebuff Type I (Boutonnière)

Pathoanatomy

 - Trigger: Synovitis of the MCP joint causes attenuation of the EPB (Extensor Pollicis Brevis)
 insertion and the dorsal capsule.

 - Cascade: The EPL (Extensor Pollicis Longus) tendon and the adductor/abductor expansions subluxate volarly (below the axis of the MCP joint).

 - Deformity: The MCP joint flexes because the extensors now act as flexors. The entire extensor effort is diverted to the distal attachment, pulling the IP joint into hyperextension.

Surgical Management of Type I

 - Early/Flexible: MCP Synovectomy and EPL rerouting. The EPL is harvested, moved dorsally, and sutured to the base of the proximal phalanx to restore MCP extension.

 - Late/Fixed: MCP Arthrodesis
 in 10-15° of flexion. This provides a stable "post" for the thumb and is the most common and reliable surgical solution for rheumatoid patients.

 - IP Joint: If the IP hyperextension is fixed, an IP arthrodesis may also be required.

Nalebuff Type III (Swan-Neck) in Detail

Pathoanatomy

 - Trigger: Synovitis starts at the CMC joint, leading to dorsal/radial subluxation of the metacarpal base.

 - Cascade: The first metacarpal becomes fixed in adduction (pulled toward the palm). To reach around objects, the patient over-extends the MCP joint.

 - Deformity: CMC Adduction + MCP Hyperextension + IP Flexion.

Surgical Management of Type III

 - Priority: You must address the CMC joint first.

 - CMC Surgery: Arthroplasty (e.g., LRTI- Ligament Reconstruction Tendon Interposition) or Trapeziectomy to
 release the adduction contracture.

 - MCP Surgery: If the MCP hyperextension is >30°, it must be stabilized via Volar Plate Capsulodesis or Arthrodesis.

 Exam Pearl :

When differentiating Type I from Type III, look at the CMC joint.

 - If the CMC is normal, it's a Type I (problem is at the MCP).

 - If the CMC is subluxated and the metacarpal is adducted, it's a Type III.
