## Question

In children with Erb’s palsy most commonly done procedure to improve shoulder function with congruent glenohumeral joint is

## Options

1. transfer of pectoralis and latissimus dorsi to rotator cuff
2. transfer latissimus dorsi and teres major muscle to rotator cuff
3. lengthening of latissimus dorsi and transfer of pectoralis to rotator cuff
4. lengthening of latissimus dorsi and transfer of pectoralis to rotator cuff

## Correct option

**2. transfer latissimus dorsi and teres major muscle to rotator cuff**

## Answer explanation

Treatment of residual deformities in children and adults in Erb’s palsy:

Initial treatment in Erb’s palsy includes physiotherapy with passive exercises to prevent joint contractures and strengthen the recovering muscles. In the most common upper trunk lesion, children who demonstrate significant recovery within two months usually recover normal function. Incomplete recoveries in C5–C6 and C5–C7 palsies lead to shoulder muscle imbalance characterized by strong internal rotators and weak external rotators and abductors and subsequently lead to contracture of the internal rotators (pectoralis major, subscapularis, latissimus dorsi and teres major muscles).

Clinical sequels are restrictions of external rotation and abduction of the extremity, with limitations to reaching the mouth and neck with the hand. Scapular winging, changes in coracoid process, humeral head flattening and glenoid deformity with possible glenohumeral joint posterior dislocation are the anatomical sequelae.

Active external rotation is crucial for upper-extremity function. Therefore, restoring external rotation in combination with shoulder abduction is generally recommended. In congruent glenohumeral joints, muscle release and transfer are indicated. Latissimus dorsi and teres major muscle are transferred to rotator cuff muscles (to the lateral side of the proximal humerus) to restore active external rotation, together with lengthening the pectoralis major and subscapularis muscles.

Answer: 2
