## Question

A 3-year-old male presents with progressively worsening right knee pain over the last 2 weeks. He is febrile and refuses to bear weight. His WBC is 14.2k/mm3, CRP is 23 mg/L, and ESR is 67 mm/hr. His right lower extremity is swollen and erythematous. There is associated posterior calf pain and pain is reproduced with forced foot dorsiflexion. An MRI demonstrates osteomyelitis of the right distal femur with a periosteal abscess. The patient is taken to surgery for an I&D and cultures reveal MRSA. Which of the following is true regarding this patient?

## Options

1. The patient's younger age poses little risk for long-term sequelae
2. Repeat surgical debridements are not commonly needed
3. Direct inoculation is the most likely pathogenesis
4. Work-up should include a venous duplex ultrasound
5. Preoperative MRI imaging does not provide treatment guidance

## Correct option

**4. Work-up should include a venous duplex ultrasound**

## Answer explanation

Methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus (MRSA) osteomyelitis predisposes pediatric patients to deep vein thrombosis.

Pediatric osteomyelitis can present insidiously and often involves the metaphysis due to the slower blood flow near the physis. The first line of treatment is typically the use of culture-specific antibiotics. However, initial empiric treatment of suspected pathogens is acceptable in pediatric patients due to the morbidity of sampling. Surgical debridement is recommended in cases of persistent infection despite adequate antibiotic coverage, the presence of an abscess, or chronic infections. Infections involving MRSA are associated with the need for multiple I&Ds and can predispose patients to deep vein thrombosis.

*Davis and Gilbert performed a large database study comparing the demographics, clinical course, and outcomes for patients with MRSA versus methicillin-susceptible Staphylococcus aureus (MSSA) osteomyelitis.* *The study also found that MRSA patients were more likely to undergo multiple surgeries compared with MSSA patients and were more likely to have complications including severe sepsis, thrombophlebitis, and pulmonary embolism.* *Patients with MRSA had longer lengths of stay than those with MSSA and higher total charges after controlling for length of stay.*

*Rosenfeld et al. instructed a course lecture on musculoskeletal infections.* **They stated** management of musculoskeletal infections relies on accurate organism identification and thorough recognition of the sites of infection and the tissues that are involved. **They recommended** MRI evaluation to aid in the localization of musculoskeletal infection and identification of sites that require surgical débridement.

*Griswold et al. evaluated the implementation of practice guidelines for the management of osteoarticular infections in children by performing preoperative MRI.* **They found** that by implementing the guidelines, the need for repeat surgery was reduced by 50% to 27%. **They also found** that adjacent infections were identified before surgery in 72% of the patients in group B, which was the group treated after the guidelines were implemented, compared to 32% of patients in group A. They concluded that preoperative MRI is resource-intensive, but offers significant benefits in preoperative planning.

*Vorhies et al. studied the risk for long-term adverse outcomes in children with osteomyelitis.* **They found** that children with severe illness were less likely to have normal radiographs and more likely to have osteonecrosis, chondrolysis, or deformity. However, functional outcome measures did not significantly differ between severity categories. The study concluded that long-term severe adverse outcomes among children with osteomyelitis occurred in 7.9% of children and were predicted by initial severity of illness, MRSA etiology, and young age.

Incorrect answers
Answer 1: Patients that are of younger age, have higher disease severity, and MRSA etiology are at increased risk of long-term adverse sequelae.
Answer 2: MRSA osteomyelitis infections frequently require repeat I&D.
Answer 3: Hematogenous seeding of metaphyseal bone is the most common pathogenesis of pediatric osteomyelitis.
Answer 5: Preoperative MRI imaging may be expensive and time-consuming, but can provide valuable information that can reduce the number of repeat operations through adjacent infection identification.
