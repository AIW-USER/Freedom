## Question

A 42-year-old female presents to your clinic in a left arm sling for follow-up one week after sustaining a fall from an electric bicycle. You recommend surgical management and proceed to the operating room the following week. Postoperative radiographs are displayed in Figures A and B. Which of the following surgical factors will most likely contribute to a lower rate of fixation failure in this patient?

## Options

1. Use of a locked plate rather than an intramedullary nail
2. Fixation at least two weeks following injury to allow for swelling resolution
3. Achievement of anatomic medial cortical apposition
4. Use of ample non-locking screws into the humeral head to obtain compression
5. Avoidance of a medial calcar screw crossing the fracture

## Correct option

**3. Achievement of anatomic medial cortical apposition**

## Answer explanation

This patient sustained a proximal humerus fracture, which was fixated with a proximal humerus locking plate. Anatomic reduction, with particular focus on restoring bony medial calcar support, has been shown to protect against construct failure and improve patient outcomes (answer choice 3).

Proximal humerus fractures are common injuries, predominantly affecting older osteoporotic patients from falls, but also younger individuals due to high-energy trauma. Diagnosis is confirmed with orthogonal radiographs, and a detailed neurovascular examination is crucial due to the proximity of nearby neurovascular structures. Management ranges from sling immobilization for mildly displaced fractures and lower demand patients, to surgical intervention such as open reduction and internal fixation (ORIF) or shoulder arthroplasty for more complex patterns. Closed reduction and percutaneous pinning is rarely utilized. Multiple injury and surgical factors have been identified as positive or negative predictors of fixation failure and patient outcomes. One such factor is medial calcar integrity, where anatomical reduction and restoration of medial cortical support (including utilization of structural allograft when necessary) have consistently been shown to optimize construct stability and prevent complications.

*Krappinger et al. reviewed risk factors influencing failure after surgical fixation of unstable proximal humerus fractures, employing a multivariate analysis to assess various parameters.* **They found** that patient age, local cancellous bone mineral density (BMD), meticulous anatomic reduction, and restoration of medial cortical support significantly influenced failure rates, with the risk of fixation failure increasing proportionally to the number of identified risk factors. **They concluded** that successful surgical fixation hinges on thorough preoperative assessment of local BMD and patient biological age, along with diligent intraoperative anatomic reduction and medial cortical support. **They suggest** augmentation or primary arthroplasty when ideal reduction is unattainable, particularly in complex fractures in osteoporotic patients.

*Schnetzke et al. reviewed the influence of fracture reduction quality, fracture pattern, and patient-related factors on clinical outcomes following locked-plate fixation of displaced proximal humeral type-C fractures.* **They found** that achieving anatomical or acceptable fracture reduction significantly reduced complication rates (20.0% vs. 41.4%) and improved clinical outcomes without increasing the risk of humeral head osteonecrosis. Conversely, increased head-shaft displacement, cranialization of the greater tuberosity, and valgus head-shaft alignment were associated with higher relative risks (two- to threefold) for inferior outcomes. **They concluded** that precise anatomical fracture reduction with a locked plate significantly improves clinical outcomes for unstable and displaced proximal humeral fractures involving the anatomical neck.

Figures A and B are the AP and oblique radiographs of a proximal humerus fracture fixated with a proximal humerus locking plate, with anatomic alignment.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer choice 1: Studies have failed to demonstrate consistent differences in postoperative outcomes and fixation failure rates favoring either fixation method.
Answer choice 2: Delaying fixation to two weeks would not be expected to improve fixation success. Conversely, there is evidence to suggest improvements in outcomes with earlier fixation.
Answer choice 4: Non-locking screws would not be expected to improve fixation failure rates. Typically, locking screws are utilized for fixation of the humeral head to counteract the osteopenia and significant torsional forces in the fragment.
Answer choice 5: Avoidance of a medial calcar screw crossing the fracture would not be expected to improve outcomes. In fact, literature supports the use of a screw placed appropriately close to the medial calcar as a method for reducing failure.
