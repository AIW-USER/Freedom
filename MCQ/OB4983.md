## Question

A 14-year-old male twists his ankle during a soccer game. His ankle radiograph is shown in Figure A. All of the following is associated with the lesion EXCEPT

![[q4983-a-figure-a.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Can be associated with a higher incidence of stress fractures
2. Can be described as the most common benign bone lesion of childhood
3. Can be found in a syndrome, which is characterized by cafe-au-lait spots, mental retardation, and abnormalities of the heart, eyes, and gonads
4. Associated with a fallen leaf sign
5. Hemosiderin can be visualized on histologic analysis

## Correct option

**4. Associated with a fallen leaf sign**

## Answer explanation

The "fallen leaf sign" is associated with unicameral bone cysts (UBC), not non-ossifying fibroma (NOF), which is depicted in the radiograph.

NOF is the most common benign bone lesion of childhood and is often found incidentally, typically when ruling out another injury. NOFs are typically found in the long bones, eccentrically located in the metaphysis. NOFs are described as 'bubbly' with a scalloping appearance and can expand the surrounding cortex. With increasing involvement of the cortex, stress fractures can occur.

Wyers, in this review, outlines the step-wise evaluation of pediatric bone lesions. The author recommends starting with the most common lesions to start your differential, citing the most common benign bone lesions of childhood: NOF, osteochondroma, UBC, aneurysmal bone cysts (ABC), and Langerhans cell histiocytosis. Plain radiographs remain the standard for initial imaging work-up, and benign characteristics include well-defined, sclerotic borders, and a sharp zone of transition, all of which can be used to describe NOF.

*Shimal et al. retrospectively analyzed their oncologic database for stress-fractures and a concomitant diagnosis of NOF for patients younger than 16-years-old.* *Over this 16 year period, 82 patients sustained stress-fractures, 42 of which had a diagnosis of NOF.* With such a high incidence, **the authors recommended** the work-up for occult fracture in children presenting with pain, swelling and concomitant NOF.

Figure A is an AP radiograph of the ankle, exhibiting an NOF in the distal tibial metaphysis. Note the eccentric location within the metaphysis, the bubbly appearance, and the sclerotic scalloping towards the lesion's edges. Subtle cortical expansion can also be seen.

Incorrect answers:
Answer(s) 1, 2, 3, and 5: As noted above, NOF is the most common benign bone lesion of childhood and can be associated with a higher incidence of stress fractures. If found in conjunction with cafe-au-lait spots, mental retardation, and cardiac, optholmalgic or gonadal abnormalities, concern for Jaffe-Campanacci syndrome should be high. On histology, hemosiderin is also readily found.
