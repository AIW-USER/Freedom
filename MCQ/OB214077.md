## Question

Which structure is at risk for injury when establishing the portal labeled "2" in Figure A?

![[q214077-a-ankle-arthroscopy-question-2.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Saphenous nerve
2. Sural nerve
3. Superficial peroneal nerve
4. Dorsalis pedis artery
5. Posterior tibial artery

## Correct option

**1. Saphenous nerve**

## Answer explanation

The saphenous nerve and great saphenous vein are at risk when establishing the anteromedial portal during ankle arthroscopy.

Nerve injury is the most common complication following ankle arthroscopy with rates reported anywhere between 6-20%. The most common nerve injured is the superficial peroneal nerve while establishing an anterolateral portal. However, the saphenous nerve is also susceptible to injury on the medial side while establishing the first viewing portal, the anteromedial portal.

Golanó et al. describe ankle arthroscopy and review the anteromedial portal. They state the saphenous nerve and great saphenous vein are at a mean safe distance of 7.4mm and 9mm from the anteromedial portal, respectively. They also provide numerous photographs and illustrations demonstrating the various portals for ankle arthroscopy.

*Stetson et al. discuss techniques and complications with ankle arthroscopy specifically with the various portals.* **They reported** a rate of 49% for neurologic complications and within that a rate of 18% for saphenous nerve injury.

Figure A shows an anterolateral viewing portal labeled "1" and an anteromedial viewing portal labeled "2".

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: The sural nerve can be injured when establishing a posterolateral portal.
Answer 3: The dorsal intermediate cutaneous branch of the superficial peroneal nerve can be injured when establishing an anterolateral portal.
Answer 4: The dorsalis pedis artery can be injured when establishing an anterocentral portal.
Answer 5: The posterior tibial artery can be injured when establishing a posteromedial portal.
