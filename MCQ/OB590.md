## Question

A 67-year-old retired male custodian complains of progressively worsening low back pain over the past 6 months. He has not seen a doctor in the past 15 years. He admits to a 40 pack year smoking history, fatigue, and an unintentional 15 pound weight loss over the past year. A radiograph of the pelvis is provided in Figure A. Which of the following would most likely confirm the diagnosis?

![[q590-a-prostate-mets.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Urine immunoelectrophoresis (UPEP)
2. Thyroid biopsy
3. CBC and blood smear
4. Prostate biopsy
5. Renal ultrasound

## Correct option

**4. Prostate biopsy**

## Answer explanation

The radiograph demonstrates osteoblastic lesions throughout the pelvis and lumbar spine. Bony metastatic lesions may be osteoblastic, osteolytic, or mixed. Lung, breast, prostate, renal, and thyroid are the five most common sites of origin. Prostate metastasis is strongly associated with osteoblastic lesions. Renal, lung and thyroid metastases are osteolytic, and breast metastases are mixed. Multiple myeloma (diagnosed by UPEP) is a purely lytic lesion.

Rougraff discusses a diagnostic algorithm to approach bony metastasis of unknown origin including history, focused physical exam, laboratory exam, imaging, and tissue biopsy. He also states that skeletal metastasis of unknown origin (including lytic, blastic, and mixed lesions) most frequently originate from the lung or kidney.
