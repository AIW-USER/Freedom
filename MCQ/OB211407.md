## Question

A 70 year old male has debilitating ankle osteoarthritis. He has tried activity modification, bracing, and NSAIDs to no avail. He is wondering what his surgical options are. If he undergoes the procedure in figure A, how will his functional outcomes compare to the outcomes of the procedure shown in figure B?

*Figure A*

![[q211407-b-ankle-arthrodesis.jpg]]
*Figure B*

## Options

1. Decreased range of motion
2. Decreased stride length
3. Improved ability to go up and down stairs
4. Improved daily step count
5. No significant difference

## Correct option

**3. Improved ability to go up and down stairs**

## Answer explanation

Total ankle arthroplasty (TAA) and ankle arthrodesis have important differences. TAA restores more natural ankle mechanics, which confers a superior ability to perform tasks like going up and downstairs.

Ankle OA is surgically treated with either arthrodesis or arthroplasty. Arthrodesis reduces ankle mobility but improves patient function, including pain relief and improved walking ability. Arthrodesis can be complicated by nonunion, malalignment, infection, and premature OA to surrounding joints. Total ankle arthroplasty preserves ankle motion, improves function, and relieves pain. Complications of TAA include infection, intraoperative fracture, loosening, subsidence, and osteolysis.

*Jastifer et al. performed a prospective cohort to determine the performance of total ankle arthroplasty and arthrodesis on uneven surfaces.* **They found** that while both groups were satisfied and had improved functional scores compared to their preoperative state, patients who had undergone arthroplasty had better outcome scores, ROM, and ability to walk up and downstairs. **They concluded** that TAA and ankle arthrodesis both improve patient performance on uneven surfaces, but TAA has overall better outcome scores.

*Hahn et al. performed a prospective cohort comparing outcomes between TAA and ankle arthrodesis.* **They found** that both groups had improved pain and gait function (velocity + stride length), while neither group improved daily step count. **They concluded** that both TAA and ankle arthrodesis improve pain and gait function, and TAA may restore more natural ankle joint function.

*Coester et al.* **determined** that at longterm follow up (22 years) patients who had undergone ankle arthrodesis were at increased risk of developing ipsilateral OA to the subtalar, talonavicular, calcaneocuboid, naviculocuneiform, tarsometatarsal, and 1st MTP joints, but not to the knee. The affected limb had activity limitation, pain, and disability compared to their contralateral limb. **The authors concluded** ankle arthrodesis provides early pain relief but is associated with premature OA to the above foot joints.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: Those who undergo TAA have superior ankle ROM in comparison to those who undergo ankle arthrodesis.
Answer 2: TAA improves gait mechanics (including increased stride length) compared to arthrodesis.
Answer 4: Neither TAA nor ankle arthrodesis significantly improve daily step count.
Answer 5: TAA and ankle arthrodesis have important differences in functional outcomes.
