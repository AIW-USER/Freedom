## Question

A 20-year-old male has an autosomal dominant condition associated with mutations in the EXT gene. This mutation is characteristic for causing multiple exostoses. What malignant condition is most commonly associated with this diagnosis?

## Options

1. Chondrosarcoma
2. Osteoblastoma
3. Chondroblastoma
4. Osteosarcoma
5. Leiomyosarcoma

## Correct option

**1. Chondrosarcoma**

## Answer explanation

Multiple Hereditary Exostosis (MHE) is an autosomal dominant condition caused by mutations in EXT1, EXT2, and EXT3 genes (tumor suppressor genes). The most common malignant transformation for osteochondromas is into chondrosarcomas.

MHE is a disorder characterized by multiple osteochondromas. The condition is associated with tumor-suppressor exostosin genes, EXT I, EXT II, and EXT III, in chromosomes 8, 11, and 19, respectively. Individuals with the EXT mutations have unregulated prehypertrophic chondrocytes growth resulting in the characteristic exostoses. Patients with the EXT1 gene have a greater number of exostoses compared to those with EXT2 gene mutations. Similarly, EXT1 have higher rates of sarcomatous changes.

*Muthusamy et al. reviewed five polyostotic conditions including Multiple Hereditary Exostosis (MHE).* **They state** that osteochonroma lesions occur in metaphyseal bone and morphologically can exhibit a stalk (pedunculated) or a broad base (sessile). Pedunculated lesions are more often encountered in patients with solitary exostoses, whereas the sessile morphology is more common in patients with MHE.

*Steffner et al. reviewed benign bone lesions.* **They state** that patients with MHE should be evaluated regularly because they are at risk for malignant transformation to chondrosarcoma. Secondary chondrosarcomas are typically low grade, and can be treated by surgery alone with a good prognosis.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2-5: Mutations in the EXT gene are not associated with these pathologies.
