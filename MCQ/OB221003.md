## Question

A 48-year-old recreational golfer presents to your clinic with an 11-month history of a slowly growing mass in his left thigh. A palpable 5 cm mass is present on exam, but the patient is otherwise neurovascularly intact. A biopsy is obtained, and based on the histology, a recommendation is made for active surveillance with serial MRIs. Which of the following diagnoses is most likely present given the patient’s presentation and definitive treatment strategy?

## Options

1. Lipoma
2. Extra-abdominal desmoid tumor
3. Giant cell tumor
4. Osteoid osteoma
5. Osteosarcoma

## Correct option

**2. Extra-abdominal desmoid tumor**

## Answer explanation

Minimally symptomatic desmoid tumors may be treated with active surveillance and serial MRIs (Answer 2).

Desmoid tumors are locally aggressive, benign soft tissue tumors. They occur most frequently in young females and can be found in association with familial adenomatous polyposis or Gardner syndrome diagnoses. They typically present as enlarging masses that may compress surrounding nerves, leading to symptoms such as paresthesias, pain, and motor dysfunction. The majority of patients are treated with active surveillance (“watch and wait”) and serial MRIs, with some regressing spontaneously over time. Medical treatment (chemotherapy/tamoxifen) or surgical excision is reserved for those who have failed active surveillance. Recurrence, however, is still high after surgical resection and can occur in up to 28% of patients even with negative margins.

*Penel et al. performed a prospective review of 771 patients with desmoid-type fibromatosis to compare outcomes of en bloc resection and the “wait-and-see” approach in the management of patients with this condition.* *They characterized specific tumor locations as favorable (abdominal wall, intra-abdominal, breast, digestive viscera, lower extremity) and unfavorable (chest wall, head/neck, upper extremity).* **They found** that active surveillance was associated with greater event-free survival in patients with tumors in unfavorable locations and comparable event-free survival in patients with tumors located in favorable locations. **They concluded** that prognostication can be based on tumor location and advocated for the use of the “wait and see” strategy when clinically reasonable.

The Desmoid Tumor Working Group published a consensus treatment on the management of pediatric and adult patients with desmoid tumors. In their statement, they cover the pathology and treatment strategies for these tumors, which include active surveillance, medical management, and surgical resection. They establish that active surveillance should be used as the primary treatment strategy and that medical management should be reserved for patients who fail observation, with the exception of abdominal wall desmoid tumors, which should be treated with surgical resection if active surveillance fails. They concluded that ultimately, the optimal treatment strategy should be tailored to each individual’s tumor and treatment goals.

*Borghi and Gronchi published a review of the diagnosis and treatment strategies for desmoid tumors.* *They discussed the historical transition of first-line care from wide resection to a “wait and see” approach and the clinical trials that led to this transformation.* *They additionally covered newer targeted medical treatments such as tyrosine-kinase inhibitors, VEGF inhibitors, gamma-secretase inhibitors, ablation, and cryotherapy, which may have a growing role in the treatment of this condition.* **They concluded** that active surveillance is the preferred first-line treatment strategy, but when additional interventions are indicated, priority should be placed on balancing the risks/benefits of each modality.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Lipomas may be treated with observation, but do not require serial MRIs.
Answer 3: Giant cell tumors may be treated with medical management (denosumab/bisphosphonates) or with surgery. Non-operative management in patients with this condition does not include serial MRIs.
Answer 4: Osteoid osteomas may be treated with observation and NSAIDs, but their non-operative protocols do not include serial MRIs.
Answer 5: Osteosarcomas are best treated with neoadjuvant chemotherapy, wide resection, and adjuvant chemotherapy
