## Question

A 63-year-old female presents to the clinic 6 months postoperatively from a TKA with knee swelling and stiffness. On examination, the knee is warm with moderate swelling. Aspiration results showed 3,600 WBC with 81% PMN and blood work is pending. Figure A shows her immediate postoperative x-ray. Which of the following would have reduced the risk of the most likely complication?

![[q217362-a-normal-tka.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Antibiotic cement
2. Earlier rehabilitation
3. Accurate intraoperative assessment of flexion and extension gaps
4. Skin closure with synthetic, absorbable monofilament suture over staples
5. Cefazolin administered within 1 hour of incision

## Correct option

**5. Cefazolin administered within 1 hour of incision**

## Answer explanation

The patient clinical picture is most consistent with a prosthetic joint infection as they are presenting with knee swelling, stiffness, and aspirations results with WBC >3,000 and PMN > 80%. The use of preoperative cefazolin has been shown to decrease the risk of infection.

Prosthetic joint infection (PJI) is a devastating complication and occurs in about 1-2% of primary TKA. Several preoperative, surgical, and postoperative factors can influence the risk of PJI. Various measures are used to prevent PJI, including optimizing medical comorbidities and modifiable patient factors (BMI, smoking, A1C, nutrition), managing the operating room environment (laminar flow, limit operating room traffic), skin preparation, and use of antibiotics. The use of preoperative antibiotics, particularly cefazolin administered within 1 hour of incision, reduces the risk of infection.

*Wyles et al reviewed nearly 30,000 TJA and evaluated perioperative antibiotic administration and infection-free survivorship at final follow-up.* *Of these patients, 95% received cefazolin and 5% received non-cefazolin antibiotics (clindamycin or vancomycin).* Infection-free survivorship was higher for those receiving cefazolin compared with non-cefazolin. **The authors recommended** perioperative allergy testing for patients with a penicillin allergy.

*Myers et al reviewed the current recommendation for the use of antibiotic prophylaxis, including antibiotic selection and microorganism coverage, timing and dosing administration, S. Aureus carrier screening and decolonization, antibiotic cement, and local antibiotics delivery.* *Large randomized studies to evaluate the optimal use of perioperative antibiotics are lacking but the authors list several active RCT that may soon provide more information.*

Figure A is a radiograph of an uncomplicated, unconstrained cemented primary total knee arthroplasty.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The routine use of antibiotic cement in primary TKA has not been shown to reduce the risk of PJI
Answer 2: This patient's knee stiffness is due to a PJI and not delayed rehabilitation
Answer 3: Tight flexion-extension gaps can result in knee stiffness but this patient's aspiration results are consistent with a PJI
Answer 4: Synthetic absorbable monofilament suture for subcuticular closure has not been shown to reduce the risk of PJI over staples
