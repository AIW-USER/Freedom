## Question

A group of researchers is planning to perform a single-center randomized controlled trial of two rehabilitation protocols designed to mitigate the risk of knee stiffness after a total knee replacement. Their primary endpoint is active knee range of motion as determined by the operating surgeon at the 6-month, 1-year, and 2-year postoperative time points. An alpha of 0.05 is set as the level of statistical significance. Which of the following changes would most likely reduce the risk of getting a type 2 error?

## Options

1. Decreasing alpha from 0.05 to 0.01.
2. Blinding the operating surgeon to the rehabilitation protocol each patient received.
3. Mandating the use of a goniometer for range of motion assessments
4. Increasing the sample size
5. Performing a Bonferroni correction

## Correct option

**4. Increasing the sample size**

## Answer explanation

Increasing the sample size will increase the study’s power and lower the likelihood of a type 2 error (Answer 4).

Type 2 errors occur when a study fails to reject the null hypothesis when a statistically significant difference does exist (false negative). These errors most frequently occur in underpowered studies and can be prevented by increasing the study’s power by enlarging the sample size. Typically, a study with 80% power is considered sufficient and carries with it, a 20% chance of a type 2 error (type 2 error = 1 - power). An a priori power analysis is critical in establishing the requisite sample size needed to detect a specific effect size.

*Abdullah et al. characterized the prevalence of underpowered studies by retrospectively reviewing randomized controlled trials in the orthopaedic surgery field from January 2012 to December 2013.* **They noted** that in the 456 randomized controlled trials reviewed, 215 studies reported negative results with 60 of these studies being underpowered. Studies in journals with higher impact factors had greater odds of being adequately powered. **They concluded** that there is a significant proportion of orthopaedic studies that are not sufficiently powered to allow for conclusions to be made regarding their respective data.

*Gazendam et al. performed a narrative review of orthopedic literature to describe contemporary changes in randomized controlled trial design.* **They discussed** that although there has been advances in study design such as the utilization of expertise-based trial design, patient-reported outcomes, and meaningful clinical outcomes, substantial progress is still needed to encourage the execution of more blinded and adequately powered studies. **They concluded** that continued progress is needed to be made in order to enhance the quality of orthopaedic research being produced.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1. Decreasing the alpha will lower the likelihood of committing a type 1 error but will increase the probability of making a type 2 error.
Answer 2. Blinding reviewers mitigates the risk of detection bias but does not influence the probability of making a type 2 error.
Answer 3. Utilizing a goniometer would enhance the precision of measurements and mitigate the risk of measurement error but would not affect the likelihood of a type 2 error.
Answer 5. Performing a Bonferroni correction would be necessary in settings where multiple hypotheses are being tested and is not indicated in this scenario. Additionally, while performing a Bonferroni correction decreases the likelihood of making a type 1 error, it consequently increases the risk of a type 2 error.
