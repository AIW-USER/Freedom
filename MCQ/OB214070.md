## Question

An 8-year-old male presents to the emergency department following a fall from 15-feet. His right arm was forcefully jerked as he grabbed a ladder rung to lessen the fall. On examination, he has constriction of the right pupil, drooping of the eyelid, pain, and ecchymosis of the right shoulder, no sensation of the index finger and thumb, 5/5 motor function of the deltoid, biceps, and brachioradialis with decreased motor function noted with wrist flexion and extension, and limited hand function. What is the most likely location of his injury?

## Correct option

**1. A**

## Answer explanation

This patient's clinical examination is concerning for a preganglionic brachial plexus injury. His injury most likely occurred at the level of the nerve roots.

Traumatic brachial plexus injury (BPI) can involve any degree of injury at any level of the plexus. The more severe injuries involve rupture of plexal segments or root avulsions associated with higher energy trauma. This patient sustained a preganglionic injury, defined as avulsion proximal to dorsal root ganglion. Injuries occurring at this level have a poor prognosis as regenerative potential of the central nervous system at this level is poor and little recovery of motor function is expected. Additionally, this patient is demonstrating signs of the Horner's syndrome, including ptosis and miosis, which supports the expected level of injury.

*Dorsi et al. reviewed the prevalence of BPI in pediatric multi-trauma patients.* **The authors report** that BPI were identified in 113 (0.1%) of the 103,434 injured children and the most common mechanism of injury was motor vehicle accidents (32%). **They concluded** that BPI is commonly associated with closed head injuries, upper-extremity vascular injuries, and fractures of the spine, humerus, ribs, scapula, and clavicle.

*Chim et al. reviewed the transfer of the triceps motor branch for isolated axillary nerve injury in the pediatric population with traumatic injuries.* **The authors report** an average postoperative deltoid muscle strength was 3.6. **They noted** that unlike the adult population, age, body mass index of the patient, and delay from injury to surgery were not significant factors affecting the outcome of the procedure. **They concluded** that in the pediatric population with traumatic injuries, isolated axillary nerve injury treated with triceps motor branch transfer can result in good outcomes.
