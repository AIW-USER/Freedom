## Question

A 44-year-old female presents to you with lateral foot pain for the past few years. She has pain over the head of the fifth metatarsal, and there is an associated callous. On her radiographs, you note an intermetatarsal angle between her fourth and fifth metatarsals of 9 degrees, with prominence of the fifth metatarsal head. You are the first provider she has seen, and she has largely dealt with the pain on her own over the past few years. What is the most appropriate treatment for this patient?

## Options

1. Shoewear modifications, custom orthotics, and anti-inflammatory medications as needed
2. Lateral eminence resection
3. First tarsometatarsal joint arthrodesis with possible Akin osteotomy
4. Fifth metatarsal osteotomy
5. Fifth metatarsal head resection

## Correct option

**1. Shoewear modifications, custom orthotics, and anti-inflammatory medications as needed**

## Answer explanation

This patient is presenting with a bunionette, as evidenced by her pain and callousing about the fifth metatarsal head with an increased intermetatarsal angle (IMA) between the fourth and fifth digits. The first-line treatment is conservative, with shoewear modifications, custom shoe inserts or pads, and pain medications (Answer 1).

A bunionette deformity involves a painful lateral prominence over the fifth metatarsal head. Anatomically, a bunionette can result from a prominent metatarsal head, lateral bowing of the fifth metatarsal shaft, or an increase in the 4-to-5 IMA. A type 1 bunionette involves an enlarged fifth metatarsal head, a type 2 results from lateral bowing of the metatarsal shaft, type 3 is associated with an increased IMA, and a type 4 is a combination of any of the preceding features. Patients typically present with pain over the prominence and have difficulty wearing constrictive shoes. Management is largely conservative, focusing on shoewear modifications, padding, custom orthotics, and possible shaving of the callous. When this fails, several surgical options such as lateral eminence resection (resecting the enlarged lateral condyle of the metatarsal head), various metatarsal osteotomies to improve bony alignment, or a complete metatarsal head resection. However, a majority of cases can be managed with conservative therapy alone.

*Shi et al. authored a review article describing the diagnosis, work-up, and management of bunionette deformity.* *They also describe their preferred treatment algorithm for management and stratify treatment based on the type of bunionette.*

*Del Vecchio et al. conducted a retrospective cohort study of 74 feet in 57 patients who underwent sliding distal metatarsal osteotomy for a painful bunionette.* **They found** that the 4-to-5 IMA significantly improved postoperatively after correction, and that 89.1% of patients reported “excellent” results after the procedure. **The authors concluded** that the distal metatarsal osteotomy provided significant improvement in terms of pain and function, with a relatively high satisfaction rate.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2, 4, 5: While all of these procedures can be considered for treatment of a bunionette, the first line of treatment is conservative. If conservative measures fail, then an osteotomy can be considered
Answer 3: This procedure would be indicated for a patient with a bunion deformity, not a bunionette.
