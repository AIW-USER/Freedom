## Question

A 22-year-old male presents to the emergency department after falling out of a tree. Radiographs are obtained and reveal a bimalleolar ankle fracture, and the decision is made to proceed with open reduction and internal fixation. Postoperative imaging is seen in Figure A. Which of the following most accurately describes the type of bone healing that is achieved with the medial malleolus fixation?

![[q219158-a-4.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Endochondral healing
2. Haversian remodeling
3. Healing strain of approximately 5%
4. Relative stability
5. Secondary stability

## Correct option

**2. Haversian remodeling**

## Answer explanation

Primary bone healing occurs via intramembranous healing with Haversian remodeling.

There are two main types of bone healing. Primary bone healing (strain is < 2%) occurs via intramembranous healing with Haversian remodeling. This type of bone healing occurs after absolute stability constructs (ie. compression plating). Conversely, secondary bone healing (strain is between 2%-10%) involves responses in the periosteum and external soft tissues. This occurs via endochondral healing. This type of bone healing occurs with non-rigid fixation (ie. fracture braces, external fixation, bridge plating, intramedullary nailing, etc...).

*Goodnough et al. reviewed the relationship between fracture biomechanics and fracture biology.* **They reported** that the benefits of performing an open approach to a fracture, obtaining an anatomic reduction, and achieving absolute stability must be weighed against the biologic cost to the tissues and the potential to compromise healing. **They concluded** that the decision to perform a closed fracture reduction; apply a splint, bridge plate, or intramedullary nail; and achieve relative stability requires that the surgeon understand the implications of increased strain at a given fracture site.

*Marsell et al. reviewed the biology of fracture healing.* **They reported** that following the initial trauma, bone heals by either direct intramembranous or indirect fracture healing, which consists of both intramembranous and endochondral bone formation. **They noted** that the most common pathway is indirect healing, since direct bone healing requires an anatomical reduction and rigidly stable conditions, commonly only obtained by open reduction and internal fixation. **They concluded** that when such conditions are achieved, the direct healing cascade allows the bone structure to immediately regenerate anatomical lamellar bone and the Haversian systems without any remodeling steps necessary.

Figure A is a radiograph of an ankle treated with ORIF. Illustration A depicts Haversian remodeling with primary bone healing.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Primary bone healing occurs via intramembranous healing.
Answer 3: Primary bone healing has a strain is < 2%.
Answer 4: Primary bone healing occurs with absolute stability.
Answer 5: Primary bone healing occurs with primary stability.
