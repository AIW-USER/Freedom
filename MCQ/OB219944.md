## Question

A 50-year-old laborer presents to the orthopedic clinic for his first postoperative appointment after undergoing a right elbow arthroscopic debridement and capsular release for post-traumatic arthritis and stiffness. Ten years prior, the patient underwent radial head operative fixation and lateral collateral ligament (LCL) repair through a posterior skin incision. In the clinic today, he reports weakness in the right hand since the elbow arthroscopy. Which exam finding below is most suggestive of an iatrogenic injury that could have occurred during placement of the anterolateral portal?

## Options

1. Inability to extend the thumb at the interphalangeal joint
2. Inability to extend and radially deviate the wrist
3. Inability to abduct the index finger
4. Inability to abduct or oppose the thumb
5. Both answer 1 and answer 2

## Correct option

**1. Inability to extend the thumb at the interphalangeal joint**

## Answer explanation

This 50-year-old male had a prior surgical approach to the lateral elbow and underwent elbow arthroscopy. The anterolateral portal places the posterior interosseous nerve (PIN) at risk, which would present as an inability to extend the interphalangeal joint (IPJ) of the thumb (answer 1).

Elbow arthroscopy is a useful tool for the treatment of a variety of pathologies of the elbow. This includes drainage of septic arthritis, debridement of osteophytes, capsular release, loose body removal, and the treatment of osteochondral defects. However, the complex anatomy around the elbow joint creates an inherent risk to surrounding neurovascular structures. Illustration 1 shows a clinical picture with several standard elbow arthroscopy portals. The anterolateral portal is placed just anterior to the radiocapitellar joint, which puts this portal in the direct vicinity of the PIN. A PIN palsy will typically present with weakness and no paresthesia. The extensor pollicis longus (EPL) extends the thumb IPJ and is innervated by the PIN.

*Adams et al. published a review article in 2014 regarding the indications, outcomes, and complications of elbow arthroscopy.* **The authors note** that prior surgical management of the elbow in some patients is a relative contradiction to arthroscopy. **The authors conclude** that the anterolateral portal has the smallest margin of safe distance and that it is reasonable to establish this portal first before fluid extravasation.

*Claessen et al. reported the results of a cadaver study in which 10 inexperienced trainees created anterolateral, direct lateral, and anteromedial portals in 10 specimens.* *The specimens were then dissected and the distance between the guide pin and adjacent structures were measured.* **The authors concluded** that the average distance between the anterolateral portal and the PIN was 12mm.

Illustration 1 shows a clinical image of the lateral and medial sides of the elbow with the following portals labeled: direct posterior (DP), posterolateral (PL), anterolateral (AL), proximal anterolateral (pAL), and anteromedial (AM).

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Extensor carpi radialis brevis (ECRB) and extensor carpi radialis longus (ECRL) typically branch before the division to PIN. Wrist extension will be weaker and coupled with radial deviation in the setting of a PIN palsy.
Answer 3: The ulnar nerve innervates the first dorsal interossei and is responsible for index finger abduction.
Answer 4: Although abductor pollicis longus (APL) is innervated by the PIN, abductor pollicis brevis (APB) is typically innervated by the median nerve.
Answer 5: This combination would be expected in an injury to the radial nerve proper, which is associated with the placement of the proximal anterolateral portal.
