## Question

A 40-year-old female presents with chronic left plantar forefoot pain, exacerbated by narrow-toed shoes. She has a history of polyarthropathy. Physical exam demonstrates no palpable lesion, but pain is reproduced with forefoot squeeze. She is RF negative but ANA positive. Surgical excision of the painful lesion is performed, and frozen section pathology demonstrates perineural fibrosis without fibrinoid necrosis. These findings are consistent with which of the following diagnoses:

## Options

1. Morton’s neuroma
2. Neurofibroma
3. Benign peripheral nerve sheath tumor
4. Plantar fibromatosis
5. Rheumatoid nodule

## Correct option

**1. Morton’s neuroma**

## Answer explanation

The clinical and histologic findings are consistent with Morton’s neuroma. A physical exam will demonstrate pain with squeezing of the metatarsals, sometimes with a painful click (Mulder's click), and pathology will characteristically show perineural fibrosis with entrapment of the interdigital nerve.

Morton’s interdigital neuroma is an entrapment neuropathy that affects the common digital nerves of the lesser toes. While the pathogenesis is incompletely understood, cumulative microtrauma is thought to result from a combination of repetitive compression of the interdigital nerve between neighboring metatarsals as well as the intermetatarsal ligament in toe-off and traction from tethering between the more fixed medial and more mobile lateral metatarsals. As a result, symptoms are exacerbated by high heels and a narrow toe box. Treatment initially involves metatarsal pads and a wider shoebox, though most patients go on to require surgical management consisting most commonly of transverse intermetatarsal ligament release and neurectomy.

*Stamatis et al. review the pathogenesis and management of interdigital neuromas of the foot.* *The authors discuss the substantial failure rate of nonoperative management and advocate for surgical neurectomy.* However, **they conclude** that while surgical management is perhaps the most reliable, it too results in persistent pain or recurrence in nearly one-third of cases.

*Akermark et al. compared dorsal to plantar digital neurectomy for primary intermetatarsal Morton’s neuroma.* *At a minimum 2-year follow-up,* **the authors found** that while the clinical outcomes were similar between the two groups, the plantar incision was associated with less sensory loss and complications. Additionally, **they cautioned** that the dorsal approach was associated with an increased risk of incomplete excision of the neuroma.

*Park et al. evaluated the addition of metatarsal shortening osteotomy to the standard algorithm for decompression of Morton’s neuroma.* *The authors directly compared deep transverse intermetatarsal ligament release alone to that combined with a metatarsal shortening osteotomy.* **They found** that the combined ligament release and metatarsal shortening osteotomy was associated with significantly better clinical outcomes at final follow-up.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Neurofibromas may similarly present in association with a peripheral nerve, but pathology will show distorted neural histology with a hypocellular, predominantly fibroblastic network of wire-like collagen and scattered myxoid regions.
Answer 3: Benign peripheral nerve sheath tumors (or Schwannomas) may share a similar clinical presentation. However, histologically they demonstrate a characteristic encapsulated, well-circumscribed lesion with alternating areas of densely-packed palisading spindle cells (Antoni A) and hypocellular mucinous stroma with fibrillar collagen (Antoni B).
Answer 4: Plantar fibromatosis arises from the plantar fascia, and would not be exacerbated by the physical exam maneuvers described. The lesions are usually asymptomatic but may become painful. Pathology is significant for dense fibrous tissue with mature collagen and interdigitated fibrocytes.
Answer 5: Rheumatoid nodules are generally subcutaneous, and pathology demonstrates a center of fibrinoid necrosis surrounded by a fibrous cellular palisade.
