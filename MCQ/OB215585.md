## Question

A demented 84-year-old man presents to the ED after striking his head following a ground-level fall. On examination, he has a laceration to his forehead, pain in his neck, and is neurologically intact. CT imaging of the cervical spine reveals a nondisplaced type II odontoid fracture. Which of the following is true regarding type II odontoid fractures?

## Options

1. Halo vest fixation is recommend in demented elderly patients
2. Fibrous union is likely following treatment with a hard cervical orthosis
3. Treatment with an anterior odontoid screw is recommended in transverse fracture patterns
4. They consist of fractures that extend into the cancellous body of C2
5. They are due to an avulsion of alar ligament

## Correct option

**2. Fibrous union is likely following treatment with a hard cervical orthosis**

## Answer explanation

This elderly patient has a nondisplaced type II odontoid fracture. If treated in a hard cervical orthosis, an osseous union is unlikely, however a fibrous union should provide sufficient stability except in the case of major trauma

Odontoid fractures are relatively common fractures of the C2 (axis) dens that can be seen in low energy falls in elderly patients and high energy traumatic injuries in younger patients. Treatment of these injuries may be nonoperative or operative depending on fracture type and risk factors for nonunion. Patients older than 80 years of age have a high morbidity and mortality regardless of nonoperative or operative treatment. Treatment with a hard cervical orthosis is a reasonable treatment modality for Type II fractures in the elderly who are not surgical candidates. In such cases, osseous union is unlikely, however a fibrous union should provide sufficient stability except in the case of major trauma.

*Chapman et al. retrospectively reviewed the differences in short- and long-term mortality between operative and nonoperative treatment for elderly patients with type II odontoid fractures.* **They reported** that surgically treated patients had a longer hospital and intensive care unit stay, and were more likely to receive a feeding tube. However, nonoperative treatment was associated with higher 30-day mortality. **They concluded** that there is a significant 30-day survival advantage and a trend toward improved longer-term survival for operatively treated over nonoperatively treated patients.

*Schroeder et al. compared the short-term (<3 months) mortality, long-term (≥12 months) mortality, and complication rates of patients >60 years of age with a type II odontoid fracture managed either operatively or nonoperatively.* **They reported** that the short-term and long-term mortalities were lower in patients who underwent surgical treatment than in those who had nonsurgical treatment, and there were no significant differences in the rate of complications. **They concluded** that the current literature suggests that well-selected patients >60 years of age undergoing surgical treatment for a type II odontoid fracture have a decreased risk of short-term and long-term mortality without an increase in the risk of complications.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Halo vest fixation can lead to high healing rates but is generally contraindicated in elderly patients, especially with dementia
Answer 3: Anterior odontoid screw is indicated in Type II fractures with risk factors for nonunion AND acceptable alignment and minimal displacement (reduction obtained), An anterior oblique fracture pattern is needed for this treatment to ensure that the fracture line is perpendicular to the screw trajectory. The patient's body habitus must allow proper screw trajectory
Answer 4: Type III odontoid fractures extend into the cancellous body of C2 and involve a variable portion of the C1-C2 joint
Answer 5: Type I odontoid fractures consist of an oblique avulsion fracture of tip of odontoid that is due to avulsion of alar ligament. Although rare, atlantooccipital instability should be ruled out with flexion and extension films.
