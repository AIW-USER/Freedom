## Question

A 16-year-old female sustained a hyperextension injury to her right wrist while cheerleading two years ago. She was initially diagnosed with a sprain, however, her pain never subsided. Her most recent radiographs and MRI are shown in Figures A and B. On exam today, her wrist flexion and extension are 40 degrees in either direction. She additionally has tenderness across her central midcarpus. She has previously undergone lengthy immobilization without symptomatic improvement. Which surgical option best restores bone vascularity and morphology?

## Options

1. Intercarpal arthrodesis
2. Resection with interpositional arthroplasty
3. Implant arthroplasty
4. Curettage and non-vascularized bone graft
5. Vascularized bone graft

## Correct option

**5. Vascularized bone graft**

## Answer explanation

This patient has avascular necrosis (AVN) of the capitate. A vascularized bone graft, such as a free medial femoral condyle (MFC) bone flap with microvascular anastomosis, gives her the best chance at restoring the vascularity and structure of her capitate.

The capitate is at risk for AVN, much like the proximal pole of the scaphoid, because of its irregular and retrograde blood supply. Other risk factors include steroid use, vibration exposure, repetitive wrist extension, and ligamentous laxity. There is a paucity of literature about capitate AVN and its treatment. Options include casting, proximal pole resection with interpositional arthroplasty, intracarpal fusion, local bone grafting, or vascularized bone grafting.

*Kazmers et al. report a single case of capitate AVN treated with a medial femoral condyle (MFC) bone flap in a 16-year-old girl.* Post-operatively the patient was pain-free and resumed all recreational activities by 6 months. **The authors advocate** for the MFC free flap for capitate AVN.

*Peters et al. reviewed six cases of capitate AVN over a twenty-year period, four of which were treated with scapho-capito-lunate fusions and 2 of which with 4-corner fusion.* **The authors found** the Mayo wrist score was satisfactory in 5 cases and poor in 1. Better results were seen when the arthrodesis fused. They underscored the rarity of the diagnosis, with only 42 total cases reported as of 2015.

Figure A is an AP radiograph of the wrist which demonstrates mild capitate sclerosis without arthritic change.
Figure B is a T2 MRI coronal image of a wrist demonstrating cystic changes primarily at the proximal pole of the capitate without subchondral collapse.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: Intercarpal arthrodesis does not preserve anatomy and is not a good initial surgical option in a 16-year-old.
Answer 2: Resection and interpositional arthroplasty would not preserve anatomy and has been reported with a poor outcome.
Answer 3: Implant arthroplasty would not preserve anatomy and is not a good initial surgical option in a 16-year-old female due to concerns over prosthesis durability and longevity.
Answer 4: Curettage would not preserve vascularity.
