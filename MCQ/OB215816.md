## Question

A 23-year-old female is brought to the emergency department after being involved in a motor vehicle collision. She is found to have an isolated, closed injury to her right lower extremity. Radiographs are shown in Figures A and B. What is the second most common complication after surgical treatment of her injury?

![[q215816-a-injury-ankle-ap.jpg]]
*Figure A*

![[q215816-b-injury-lateral.jpg]]
*Figure B*

## Options

1. Complications are not common
2. Malunion
3. Nonunion
4. Osteonecrosis
5. Subtalar arthritis

## Correct option

**4. Osteonecrosis**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a Hawkins III talar neck fracture-dislocation as evidenced by dislocation of both the subtalar and tibiotalar joints. The second most common complication of these injuries is osteonecrosis (with the most common being subtalar arthritis).

Talar neck fractures are classified using the Hawkins classification: I – nondisplaced, II – subtalar dislocation, III – subtalar and tibiotalar dislocations, and IV – pantalar dislocations (subtalar, tibiotalar, and talonavicular dislocations). The standard treatment for these injuries is open reduction internal fixation (ORIF). The second most common complication after surgical treatment for displaced talar neck fractures is osteonecrosis.

*Lindvall et al. performed a retrospective review of 26 displaced talar neck and body fractures treated with ORIF.* With an average of 74 months of follow up, **they found** that subtalar arthritis was present in 100% of fractures, while osteonecrosis was present in 50%, and nonunion complicated the course of 12% of fractures. **They concluded** that subtalar arthritis is the most common complication after operative treatment of talar neck and body fractures.

*Vallier et al. performed a retrospective review of 102 talar neck fractures treated with ORIF.* With an average follow up of 36 months **they found** that osteonecrosis was present in 49% of fractures and that collapse took place in 31% of these patients. Additionally, **they found** that delay of surgery an average of 5 days did not correlate with osteonecrosis. **They concluded** that timing of fixation did not correlate with development of osteonecrosis.

Figure A and B are AP and Lateral radiographs, respectively, demonstrating a talar neck fracture with dislocation of the tibiotalar and subtalar joints with a concomitant comminuted medial malleolus fracture.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1 – Surgical treatment for talar neck fractures unfortunately has a high complication rate.
Answer 2 – Malunion is not the most common complication.
Answer 3 - Nonunion is not the most common complication.
Answer 5 – Subtalar arthritis is the most common complication, while osteonecrosis is the 2nd most common.
