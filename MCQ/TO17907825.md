## Answer explanation

Ans: B

Shown are images of hip resurfacing showing solid/ fluid filled masses around replacement implants which could very possibly be pseudotumors, a complication native to MoM articulation.

HIP RESURFACING

Hip resurfacing came up as an alternative to Total hip replacement particularly for young patients a couple of decades ago, precisely because of its bone preserving nature. The procedure consisted of placing a metal cap covering the femoral head that would articulate with a matching metal cup (as in THR) placed in the acetabulum.

![[TO17907825-3.jpg]]

The earlier versions supported a large metal head on a polyethylene (PE) cup and hence miserably failed as the large head resulted in high volumetric wear of the PE and high osteolysis rates leading to early failures.

However, with the advent of metal on metal articulations there is regenerated interest in the articulation as its been reapproved by FDA in 2006 (Birmingham Hip Resurfacing system, designed by British Orthopaedic surgeon Derek McMinn) whereby metal on metal (MoM) articulations have been introduced that support a large metal head.

Hip resurfacing has the greatest potential advantage of preserving proximal femoral bone and providing physiologic stress transfer (better restoration of hip biomechanics). Resurfacing also has a lower incidence of dislocation (0-1%) compared to conventional hip arthroplasty as it allows use of large heads (that’s a feature of any MoM articulation). An indirect advantage is that since penetration of the femoral canal is not required, procedure is particularly suitable in patients with proximal femoral deformity where total hip arthroplasty may be difficult.

However, potential disadvantages include the inability to adjust offset and limb-length as readily compared to a conventional total hip arthroplasty. Presence of a good proximal femoral bone stock is a pre requisite. Fracture of the femoral neck, likely because of osteonecrotic bone, is the most common and troublesome complication (incidence 0-4%, more common in first 3 years) of hip resurfacing, and hence procedure is relatively contraindicated in cases of coxa vara, owing to heighted stresses leading to fractures. Aseptic loosening of the components (specifically cemented femoral resurfacing component) can also occur (although not as common as with the introductory system) and would be an indication for conversion to total hip arthroplasty. And as with all MoM articulations the procedure is contraindicated in females of child bearing age and renal failure cases (due to harmful impact of metal ions that could enter circulation).

A very peculiar disadvantage includes metallosis, mostly due to edge loading of the MoM bearing surface. Pseudotumors, defined as a solid or fluid mass, have been seen to developed in the peri-prosthetic soft tissue, and are considered a severe complication of these MoM implants, which may cause pain, swelling, deep vein thrombosis and extensive soft tissue damage, resulting in unexpected failures, especially in young females. Currently the only treatment option in case of pseudotumors is revision surgery, during which the MoM articulation is replaced by a non-MoM articulation.

The current state of hip-resurfacing: There is an ongoing debate over material choice, implant design and surgical technique.
