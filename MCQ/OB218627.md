## Question

A 35-year-old laborer sustains a fall from a height off of a ladder. Radiographs of his injury are available for review in Figure A and he is indicated to undergo sub-acute open reduction and internal fixation following the resolution of soft-tissue swelling. During surgery, failure to restore the height of the starred anatomic structure in Figure B is most likely to result in which of the following sequelae?

![[q218627-a-calc.jpg]]
*Figure A*

![[q218627-b-marked-lateral.jpg]]
*Figure B*

## Options

1. Calcaneofibular impingement
2. Difficulty with shoe fit
3. Tibiotalar impingement
4. Valgus hindfoot alignment
5. Varus hindfoot alignment

## Correct option

**3. Tibiotalar impingement**

## Answer explanation

This patient has sustained a highly-comminuted, intra-articular calcaneal fracture. Failure to restore the height of the posterior facet (marked structure in Figure B) can result in anterior tibiotalar impingement (Answer 3).

The calcaneus is the most commonly fractured tarsal bone, and is associated with a high degree of morbidity and disability. Close to 75% of these injuries are intra-articular, and 15-20% of these injuries are open at the time of presentation. Calcaneal fractures occur secondary to a traumatic axial load i.e. a fall from height or a motor vehicle accident and co-exist with a variety of orthopaedic injuries. Surgical treatment of these injuries requires careful planning and meticulous reduction and fixation, as failure to restore subtalar congruity, calcaneal height, calcaneal width, and hindfoot alignment can lead to a variety of malunion sequelae.

*Reddy et al. provide a comprehensive overview of the management of calcaneus fracture malunion and nonunion.* **The authors note** that failure to restore the height of the posterior facet can lead to subsequent anterior ankle impingement. Furthermore, failure to restore calcaneal width and alignment can lead to difficulty with shoe wear, sub-fibular impingement, and hindfoot varus. **The authors note** that surgery for calcaneal malunion is fraught with poor outcomes and that all attempts should be made to ensure optimal alignment at the time of the index treatment.

*Sharr et al. provide an overview of the current treatment recommendations for displaced intra-articular fractures of the calcaneus.* **The authors note** that multiple factors must be considered during surgery and that surgical treatment of these injuries should only be performed by surgeons with expert knowledge of the surgical approaches, injury patterns, and fixation strategies. **The authors also highlight** the intra-operative mistakes which could lead to malunion, and review the signs and symptoms of these malunions.

Figure A demonstrates a highly-comminuted, intra-articular calcaneus fracture with loss of posterior facet height. Figure B is a lateral radiograph of an uninjured foot with an asterisk on the posterior facet.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: failure to restore hindfoot alignment and/or lateral wall blowout can lead to sub-fibular impingement.
Answer 2: failure to restore calcaneal width can lead to difficulty with shoe fit
Answers 4 and 5: failure to correct hindfoot alignment typically will lead to varus malunion
