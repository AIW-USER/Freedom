## Question

A 40-year-old, active male underwent a opening-wedge, high tibial osteotomy (HTO) for early medial compartment arthritic changes. He presents 15 years later with tricompartmental arthritis. Which of the following findings is most consistent with his history?

## Options

1. Caton-Deschamps ratio of 1.4
2. Blackburne-Peel ratio of 0.6
3. Insall-Salvati ratio of 0.7
4. Tibial tubercle-trochlea groove (TT-TG) difference of 22 mm
5. Patellar translation of 24 mm

## Correct option

**3. Insall-Salvati ratio of 0.7**

## Answer explanation

Patella baja is most commonly associated with a prior HTO and can be problematic in patients undergoing total knee arthroplasty.

For younger patients with early arthritic changes isolated to a single compartment wishing to avoid arthroplasty, a tibial or femoral osteotomy can be a durable option. A varus-producing femoral osteotomy is often warranted for genu valgum, whereas a valgus-producing HTO is often preferred in the setting of genu varum. It is important to obtain weight-bearing, mechanical axis films to evaluate the extent of the deformity; this is also critical when considering if a patient is indicated for a meniscal transplant. Lastly, patients with prior HTOs often develop patellar baja, which is partially attributed to the development of scar tissue or shortening of the patellar tendon during the osteotomy.

*Scuderi et al. retrospectively reviewed the radiographs of 66 knees that underwent a prior HTO in order to determine changes in patellar height.* **They found** that 76-89% of knees had patella baja, as measured by the Blackburne-Peel and IS index, respectively. **They found** no correlation between the degree of patella baja and need for future conversion to total knee arthroplasty (TKA).

*Aglietti et al. retrospectively reviewed the outcomes of HTOs in 120 knees at 10 to 21 year follow-up.* *After an average of 11 years, 30 knees were converted to TKAs.* Nineteen percent of the knees showed patella baja in their study, which **they found** correlated with postoperative immobilization in a cylinder cast.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: A Caton-Deschamps ratio of 1.4 would indicate patella alta.
Answer 2: A Blackburne-Peel ratio of 0.6 would indicate a normal patella height.
Answer 4: A TT-TG difference of 22 mm may be expected in a patient with patellar instability.
Answer 5: You would expect limited patellar translation secondary to scarring and arthritic changes in this patient; >20 mm is abnormal.
