## Question

A 25-year-old male is planning on starting a family with his wife. He has a history of hypophosphatemic rickets. Which of the following is true regarding this condition?

## Options

1. A mutation of the FGFR3 gene is implicated in the pathogenesis of this condition
2. The patient's sons are likely to be carriers of this disease
3. Medical management consists of recombinant parathyroid hormone and elemental calcium supplementation
4. 100% of the patient's daughters will either be carriers or have the disease
5. Hemiepiphysiodesis is ineffective in treating varus/valgus deformities of the lower extremities in these patients

## Correct option

**4. 100% of the patient's daughters will either be carriers or have the disease**

## Answer explanation

Hypophosphatemic rickets is X-linked dominant which would make result in all of the patient's daughters either being carriers or developing the disease.

Hypophosphatemic rickets is an X-linked dominant disease that results in a mutation of the PHEX gene. This mutation leads to overexpression of FGF-23 which increases renal phosphate wasting. Patients that present with this condition have low serum phosphate and progressive skeletal deformities consistent with rickets. The disease can be more severe in males than females. Treatment consists of medical management with calcitriol and elemental phosphorus supplementation. Skeletal deformities can be corrected with hemiepiphysiodesis or osteotomies depending on the extent of deformity and the amount of growth remaining.

*Sharkey et al. reviewed the medical and surgical treatment of X-linked Hypophosphatemic rickets.* *They discussed medical management is the cornerstone of treatment consisting of calcitriol and elemental phosphorus supplementation.* *They further elaborated that complex bone deformities can be addressed with surgical correction following appropriate medical management.*

*Novais and Stevens performed a case series of 10 patients and a literature review on the role of hemiepiphysiodesis in the treatment of hypophosphatemic rickets.* **They reported** progressive varus and valgus deformities of the knee can occur prior to medical management which is often irreversible once appropriate medications are administered. **They concluded** hemiepiphysiodesis is a minimally invasive procedure that can address the deformities in the growing child.

Incorrect answers
Answer 1: X-linked hypophosphatemic rickets is the result of a mutation in the PHEX gene that leads to increased expression of the fibroblast growth factor 23 (FGF-23) protein and results in increased renal phosphate wasting. The FGFR3 gene is implicated in achondroplasia.
Answer 2: The patient's sons will not receive the X chromosome from the father.
Answer 3: Appropriate medical management consists of calcitriol and elemental phosphorus supplementation.
Answer 5: Guided growth can be effective in corrective limb malalignment in growing patients once they are treated with appropriate medications.
