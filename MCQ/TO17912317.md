## Question

Trigonocephaly results from

## Options

1. Unilateral fusion of coronal suture
2. Bilateral fusion of coronal suture
3. Sagittal suture premature fusion
4. Metopic suture fusion

## Correct option

**4. Metopic suture fusion**

## Answer explanation

Ans: D

Trigonocephaly (also called as Metopic craniosynostosis) - is premature closure of metopic suture whereby forehead gets shaped like a triangle (keel of a boat). The metopic suture separates the two frontal bones at birth and is the first skull suture to close physiologically, starting as early as at 3 months and generally being completely fused at the age of 8 months.

CRANIOSYNOSTOSIS

- Craniosynostosis refers to the premature closure of the cranial sutures. The skull then undergoes characteristic shape changes depending on which suture(s) close early.
- There is a 3:1 male predominance with an overall incidence of 1 in 2000-2500. 8% of cases are syndromic or familial.
- Associations - Most occur as isolated anomalies but syndromic associations can be seen in a small proportion of cases (~10%) like: -
- Apert syndrome
- Carpenter syndrome
- Crouzon syndrome

- Sutures of the skull

![[TO17912317-2.jpg]]

- Ages of normal sutural/fontanelle closure: -

- anterior fontanelle: 18-24 months
- sphenosquamosal: 6-10 years
- sphenofrontal: approximately 15 years
- occipitomastoid: approximately 16 years
- sagittal: approximately 22 years
- coronal: approximately 24 years
- lambdoid: approximately 26 years
- squamosal: approximately 60 years

- In Craniosynostosis, there is premature sutural closure. The sagittal suture is most commonly involved (≈50%), where the lateral growth of the skull is arrested while anteroposterior growth continues, producing a narrowly elongated skull known as scaphocephaly (meaning boat-shaped) or dolichocephaly (from the ancient Greek for long ; dolichos).

The next most common sutures in terms of involvement are:

- coronal (~20%)
- lambdoid (~5%)
- metopic (~5%)

TERMINOLOGY: -

- Brachycephaly: bicoronal and/or bilambdoid sutures
- Scaphocephaly/dolichocephaly: sagittal suture
- Plagiocephaly: unilateral coronal and lambdoid sutures

 frontal plagiocephaly: unilateral coronal suture

 occipital plagiocephaly: unilateral lambdoid suture

- Trigonocephaly: metopic suture
- Pachycephaly: lambdoid suture
- Oxycephaly/turricephaly: sagittal, coronal and lambdoid sutures (tower like skull)
- Cloverleaf skull: intrauterine sagittal, coronal, lambdoid sutures (most severe)
- Harlequin eye: ipsilateral coronal suture
- Progressive postnatal pansynostosis: a rare form of craniosynostosis which involves late (postnatal) fusion of all cranial sutures

- Treatment is often with CRANIOPLASTY, a surgical procedure to repair defects/ deformities of skull.
