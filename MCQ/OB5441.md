## Question

Figure A shows an MRI image of an anatomical structure labelled in red. What would be the most appropriate treatment modality if this structure was acutely injured, in isolation, resulting in a partial tear?

![[q5441-a-atfl.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Cast immobilization and non-weight bearing for 2-4 weeks
2. Cast immobilization and non-weight bearing for 6-8 weeks
3. Early open surgical repair
4. Physical therapy
5. Early arthroscope exploration and repair

## Correct option

**4. Physical therapy**

## Answer explanation

Figure A shows a coronal and axial MRI image of the ankle. The red structure is the anterior talofibular ligament (ATFL). The most appropriate treatment of an acute partial or complete ATFL injury would be physical therapy for peroneal strengthening and proprioception.

The anterior talofibular ligament (ATFL) is the most commonly involved ligament in low ankle sprains. Partial tears of the ATFL are usually categorized as Grade I injuries; which means there is no frank ligamentous disruption and the ankle is relatively stable on examination. Treatment consists of RICE (rest, ice, compression, elevation), early protected weight-bearing, and when comfortable, physical therapy for peroneal strengthening and proprioception.

*Maffulli et al. reviewed the management of acute and chronic ankle sprains.* **They showed** that anatomic reconstruction or tendon transfers may be considered if patients continue to have pain and instability despite extensive nonoperative management.

*Janssen et al. conducted a randomized controlled trial in athletes that compared bracing versus neuromuscular training in reducing the incidence of recurrent low ankle sprains.* **They found** that bracing during sport alone for 8 weeks was superior to neuromuscular training for 12 weeks in reducing the rate of recurrent ankle sprains.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1,2: Cast immobilization and non-weight bearing are not recommended. Bracing has been shown to be effective during sport, but weight bearing is recommended.
Answer 3: Early open surgical repair may be warranted if there is an associated fracture or intra-articular lesion requiring repair or debridement. Partial tears do not require acute repair.
Answer 4: Early arthroscopic exploration and repair would not be warranted in an acute, isolated, ATFL injury. Arthroscopy may be indicated in acute or recurrent ankle sprains caused by anteriorinferior tibiofibular ligament impingement or posteromedial impingement lesions of the ankle.
