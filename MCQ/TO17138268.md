Chapter: Pediatric Orthopedics MCQs — Congenital Muscular Torticollis

## Question

True about sternocleidomastoid contracture (FNB Pattern Paper 2017)

## Options

1. Disappear spontaneously after infancy
2. Best age for surgery is between one and two years
3. Unipolar release of sternal end may suffice
4. Surgical release before three years may cause deep and painful scarring.

## Correct option

**4. Surgical release before three years may cause deep and painful scarring.**

## Answer explanation

Sternocleidomastoid contractures: Fibromatosis of the sternocleidomastoid (SCM) muscle leads to formation of a palpable mass (palpable at birth or within 4 weeks of birth) in the muscle. Mostly deformity involves the right side. Mass spontaneously regresses in a few months leaving behind a fibrosed muscle which becomes palpable as fibrous cord. Exact etiology is not clear but an increased incidence has been reported in breech delivery and first born cases. It is also associated with high incidence of DDH and metatarsus adductus.

Clinical features: Head is tilted toward the involved muscle and chin is rotated toward opposite side (Cock Robin appearance). A palpable mass may be felt in the middle to lower third of the sternal portion of the SCM muscle. Asymmetrical face (plagiocephaly) due to sleeping on one side only develops later. Infants may also have bat ears.

Treatment: Most cases resolve with regular stretching in infancy. For persistent deformity surgery should be done after the age of 3-4 years. At this age asymmetry of face and skull reverse completely. Surgery before this age may result in deep scarring at the site of surgical release. Unipolar release (at clavicular attachment only) or bipolar release of SCM (Z plasty at clavicular side and release at mastoid attachment, preferred method) followed by physiotherapy is done.

 Also know:

Torticollis due to Atlanto-axial rotatory displacement: It is due to rotational malalignment between the atlas and the axis and may range from mild subluxation to complete dislocation. CT scan axial images can establish the diagnosis.

Paroxysmal torticollis of infancy: This rare disorder is characterized by episodic neck tilting due to vestibular dysfunction. It is a self-limiting condition and treatment of the underlying condition is only required.

Answer: 4
