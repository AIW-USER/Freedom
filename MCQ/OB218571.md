## Answer explanation

This patient has midfoot arthritis and collapse and a rocker bottom shoe would be most appropriate to offload his midfoot and prevent pain and further dysfunction.

Midfoot arthritis can be primary, post-traumatic, or inflammatory and can become severely debilitating for patients given the large amount of load seen through the midfoot during gait. Physical exam findings include longitudinal arch collapse, forefoot abduction, and hindfoot valgus and patients often have pain during the push-off phase. First-line therapies include NSAIDS, activity modification, and bracing. A stiff-soled, rocker bottom shoe is the best orthosis for midfoot arthritis as it helps transfer body weight from the hindfoot to the forefoot without excess stress on the midfoot.

*Nemec et al. reviewed outcomes for midfoot arthrodesis in cases of midfoot arthritis.* **They noted** significant improvement in AOFS midfoot scores as well as patient satisfaction with follow-up averaging 4 1/2 years. **They also noted** high rates of union (92%) and very few complications.

*Thordarson et al. discuss management of midfoot arthritis and* **note** that despite nonoperative care, many end up in debilitating pain. **They state** that arthrodesis is the mainstay of treatment in these patients, but the anatomy and biomechanics of the foot must be respected. **They recommend** selective fusion to only joints that are problematic in order to maximize remaining motion and function.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: An Arizona brace is useful for flexible hindfoot planovalgus deformities and provide stability to the ankle and hindfoot.
Answer 2: A lateral heel wedge is used in cases of pes cavovarus with fixed forefoot pronation, as it corrects the hindfoot deformity passively and allows for a plantigrade forefoot.
Answer 3: A Morton's carbon fiber extension is used for cases of 1st MTP arthritis as it prevents excess motion at the 1st MTP joint.
Answer 5: A posterior leaf spring ankle foot orthosis (AFO) used used to correct ankle deformity in cases of muscular imbalance, and the leaf spring orthosis particularly, is useful in cases of foot drop.
