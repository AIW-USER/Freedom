## Question

A 28-year-old professional golfer chunked his 9-iron on an approach shot about three weeks ago and has continued hand pain despite rest and immobilization. You obtain the radiograph in Figure A and recommend fragment excision for the patient. When counseling him on expected outcomes, what is the most likely time frame for return to play after excision?

![[q218177-a-hook-of-hamate-fracture-3.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. 4 weeks
2. 6 weeks
3. 4 months
4. 6 months
5. Professional athletes rarely return to competitive play after this injury

## Correct option

**2. 6 weeks**

## Answer explanation

This patient suffered a hook of hamate fracture. In most high-level athletes, fragment excision can reliably establish a return to play in 6 weeks (Answer 2).

A hook of hamate fracture is a rare, often missed, injury. It is commonly seen in athletes participating in repetitive gripping or impact, such as baseball players or golfers. The fracture can occur from a direct blow to the palm (such as grounding a golf club or checking a baseball swing) or excessive force while gripping. Patients often complain of pain, weakness, and limited range of motion. The carpal tunnel view radiograph (Figure A) is the most sensitive radiograph for hook of hamate fractures. Treatment ranges from immobilization and rest to fragment fixation or fragment excision. Nonoperative treatment has a high rate of asymptomatic nonunion, up to 40-50%. Fragment excision has demonstrated reliable quicker symptom relief with a consistent return to play in high-level athletes.

*Scheufler et al. performed a retrospective review of 14 patients with hook of hamate fractures.* Eight patients were treated surgically, five with excision, and three with open reduction internal fixation. All patients treated surgically had complete resolution of symptoms at 3 months (the first documented follow-up) with no statistical differences in grip strength. Patients treated conservatively did not fare as well compared to the operative group during the early postoperative period. **The authors concluded** that surgical intervention is superior and recommended in hook of hamate fractures.

*Devers et al. performed a retrospective analysis of 11 high-level athletes with a hook of hamate fractures treated with excision.* **The authors found** that all patients returned to full sport at 6 weeks postoperatively with no difference in pre-injury and post-injury outcome scores. **The authors concluded** that fragment excision is a successful procedure for high-level athletes with hook of hamate fractures.

*Aldridge & Mallon retrospectively reviewed the excision of hook of hamate fractures in 7 competitive golfers.* **The authors found** that most golfers resumed play at 6 weeks, while 3 resumed complete play without restriction at 3 months. All patients were able to return to play with complete resolution of symptoms with no appreciable decrease in grip strength. **The authors concluded** that hook of hamate fragment excision should be performed on elite golfers with the intent of expeditious return to full activity.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer choice 1: Based on the above studies, 4 weeks is too soon for players to be released to full activity or to expect a full return to play.
Answer choices 3 & 4: Most athletes who undergo fragment excision have near complete resolution of symptoms and return to play by 6 weeks, with some returning to full play at 3 months. Players should expect to return to play prior to the 4-month or 6-month mark.
Answer choice 5: Professional athletes can reliably expect to return to competitive play after this injury is treated with excision.
