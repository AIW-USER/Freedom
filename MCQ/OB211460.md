## Question

In the treatment of a chronic prosthetic joint infection (PJI), what is the difference between outcomes comparing methicillin-resistant and methicillin-sensitive Staphylococcus aureus (MRSA and MSSA, respectively)?

## Options

1. A chronic MSSA PJI can be effectively treated with debridement and poylethlene exchange
2. A chronic MRSA PJI can be effectively treated with debridement and poylethlene exchange
3. Patients with an MRSA chronic PJI have better functional outcomes following a 2-stage revision compared with MSSA
4. Following a 2-stage revision for chronic PJI, the prognosis is the same regardless of MSSA or MRSA
5. Patients with an MRSA infection have a poorer prognosis following 2-stage revision compared with MSSA

## Correct option

**5. Patients with an MRSA infection have a poorer prognosis following 2-stage revision compared with MSSA**

## Answer explanation

Methicillin-resistant organisms are associated with a poorer prognosis for the eradication of infection in prosthetic joint infections (PJI), even in the setting of a 2-stage revision (debridement with explant and antibiotic spacer followed by a period of intravenous antibiotics for an average of 6-8 weeks with reimplantation once no evidence of remaining infection exists).

Methicillin-resistant S. aureus is a particularly virulent bacteria associated with abscess formation, biofilms, and deep venous thrombosis; it is particularly hard to eradicate and newer evidence suggests it can remain in a dormant state in bone for years. The gene that confers methicillin resistance in MRSA is mecA. Healthcare workers, athletes, nursing/long-term care residents, and prisoners are more susceptible to these infections. More virulent organisms, such as S. aureus (and MRSA), are associated with acute infections, whereas chronic PJIs are often associated with S. epidermidis. Even in the setting of an acute PJI, infections with MRSA (or fungal organisms) are typically treated with a 2-stage revision. There have been some promising results in the European literature for 1-stage revisions with less virulent organisms.

*Parvizi et al. examined the economic impact of methicillin-resistant infections.* *They performed a retrospective analysis of patients at 2 academic centers with PJIs over a 10 year period, identifying 391 total patients.* They found that the average cost of surgical treatment for a PJI was $68,053 for an MSSA organism compared to a staggering $107,264 for MRSA.

*Leung et al. asked how often a 2-stage revision in the setting of a PJI effectively eradicates MRSA.* *They retrospectively reviewed 50 patients that underwent a 2-stage hip revision with a MRSA or methicillin-resistant S. epidermidis infection.* **They found** a treatment failure of 21% at a minimum 2-year follow-up, higher than that of MSSA infections.

*Marculescu et al. looked at the outcomes of PJIs treated with debridement and retention of components.* *They performed a retrospective cohort analysis of all patients with a PJI who were treated with debridement and retention over a 4 year period and identified 91 patients.* Treatment failure occurred in 53 episodes and was associated with the presence of a sinus tract and pre-debridement duration of symptoms of >/ 8 days.

*Azzam et al. reexamined the indications for irrigation and debridement in the management of PJI.* *They retrospectively reviewed 104 patients diagnosed with a hip or knee PJI that underwent irrigation and debridement with prosthesis retention at their institution.* **They found** a failure rate of 56% and suggest that debridement and retention be reserved for select healthy patients with low virulence organisms.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1 and 2: Chronic PJIs should be treated with a 2-stage revision in most circumstances.
Answer 3: Functional outcomes are lower following a 2-stage revision for an MRSA PJI compared with an MSSA PJI.
Answer 4: MRSA PJIs carry a worse prognosis following a 2-stage revision compared to MSSA.
