Chapter: Arthroplasty MCQs — Total Shoulder Arthroplasty

## Question

A patient has been planned by you for Total Shoulder Arthroplasty for glenohumeral arthritis. In which of the following situations would you choose to eccentrically ream the glenoid during the procedure

## Options

1. When glenoid has a retroversion of 15 degrees
2. When humerus has an anteversion of 15 degrees
3. When glenoid has a retroversion of 25 degrees
4. When shoulder arthroplasty is being done for rotator cuff arthropathy

## Correct option

**1. When glenoid has a retroversion of 15 degrees**

## Answer explanation

Ans: A

Explanation:

Glenohumeral arthritis has been classified by Walch et al. and the classification is a good guide to treatment.

The most common type out of patterns described for shoulder OA is one of Posterior subluxation of head that leads predominantly to a posterior glenoid wear. This leads to glenoid actually getting more retroverted than usual.

Now, as one proceeds with Shoulder Arthroplasty in such cases, following basic principles are followed:

- If Retroversion is up to 15 degrees: Glenoid can be reamed eccentrically (means you ream more of anterior part than posterior) so as to generate a normal version post operatively.

- If Retroversion is between 15-25 degrees: Here eccentric reaming will lead to perforation of the anterior glenoid vault so it’s better to build up with glenoid component augments.

- If Retroversion is > 25 degrees: Here the deficiency is very severe as a large bone stock is deficient and hence use of a bone graft is advisable to build up bone stock.
