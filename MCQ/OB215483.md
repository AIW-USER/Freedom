## Question

A 17-year-old female athlete presents to your office complaining of right foot pain and you obtain the radiograph seen in Figure A. You go on to obtain a detailed history and physical examination. She reports a similar fracture injury in her contralateral foot one year ago. Which of the following additional findings would most likely be present in this patient?

![[q215483-a-right-foot-stress-fx.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Takes ethinylestriadiol/levonorgestrel contraceptive daily
2. Participates in multiple sports
3. Bone mineral density (BMD) T-score = -0.5
4. Body mass index (BMI) of 26
5. Amenorrhea for 12 months

## Correct option

**5. Amenorrhea for 12 months**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a right 4th metatarsal stress fracture and out of the answers listed, would most likely be amenorrheic, which is part of the female athlete triad.

The female athlete triad includes low energy availability, amenorrhea, and reduced bone mineral density (BMD). It occurs most commonly in female athletes who participate in sports that emphasize aesthetics or endurance like gymnastics or cross country. A multidisciplinary approach is important to effectively treat these patients and includes counseling, dietary management, supplemental calcium & vitamin D, reduced training and potentially oral contraceptives to regulate periods.

*Frank et al published a review of injuries to female athletes with the major finding that female athletes more suffered overuse, rather than traumatic injuries.* *They specifically focused on the fact that rates of stress fractures are much higher in similar cohorts of males including runners and military personnel.* **They conclude** by reviewing the female athlete triad and its importance as it relates to the overall management of bony injuries in female athletes.

*Tenforde et al attempted to risk stratify female athletes with regards to their risk for stress fractures based on factors related to their sport and the factors included in the female athlete triad.* **They noted** that approximately 30% of the female athletes included were considered moderate (26%) or high risk (4%) and moderate/high-risk athletes were 2.6 and 4x more likely to sustain a stress fracture, respectively. **They concluded** by noting that the most important predictors of stress fracture are amenorrhea and prior stress fracture.

Figure A is an AP radiograph depicting a 4th metatarsal stress fracture with periosteal reaction.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Oral contraceptives are generally thought to help regulate menses, and may be useful in treating the amenorrhea component of the female athlete triad.
Answer 2: Participation in multiple sports has not been found to be a risk factor for stress fracture in female athletes. However, certain sports do have a higher risk including gymnastics, cross country and lacrosse.
Answer 3: BMD of -0.5 is within acceptable ranges. BMD <-1.0 represents osteopenia and BMD <-2.5 represents osteoporosis.
Answer 4: Low caloric intake and energy availability is a risk factor for stress fracture in the female athlete triad, however, a BMI of 26 is within the normal range. BMI < 21 has been found by some to be predictive of stress fracture in these female patients.
