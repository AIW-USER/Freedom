## Answer explanation

A displaced bucket handle tear of the medial meniscus is seen on MRI as a "double PCL" sign as seen in Figure F.

Bucket handle meniscal tears can be diagnosed on MRI as a double PCL sign on sagittal imaging. These should be repaired in order to preserve meniscal biomechanics and protect from future chondral injury. Following reduction of the meniscal tear, one of a variety of fixation techniques should be used to secure the large flap of the meniscus. The various methods include inside-out (gold standard), outside-in, and all inside techniques. Recent studies have shown the three techniques to lead to equivalent outcomes.

*Blackmore et al. review the management of meniscal injuries.* **They state** preserving any remaining meniscus or meniscal transplantation is favorable given the degenerative changes seen following loss of the meniscus or a non-functioning meniscus. **They conclude** research should continue with regard to meniscal repair and preservation as well as biologic augmentation.

*Paxton et al. performed a systematic review of outcomes following meniscal repair (inside-out, outside-in, and all inside) versus partial meniscectomy.* **They found** that long-term clinical outcomes following meniscal repairs showed lower rates of radiographic degeneration and higher functional outcome scores. **They concluded** that although partial meniscectomies have lower reoperation rates in the short term, meniscal repairs fair better in the long term.

Figure A shows and an arthroscopic photograph of a displaced bucket handle tear of the medial meniscus. Figure B shows an MRI demonstrating an acute tear of the ACL. Figure C shows a T1 MRI with an osteochondral lesion on the femoral condyle. Figure D shows a sagittal MRI demonstrating a degenerative posterior horn meniscal tear. Figure E shows a T1 sagittal MRI showing a "double anterior horn sign" signifying a flipped lateral meniscal tear. Figure F shows a sagittal MRI demonstrating a double PCL sign indicative of a bucket handle medial meniscus tear. Illustration A shows an arthroscopic image following meniscal repair.
