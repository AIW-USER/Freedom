## Question

A 5-year-old girl presented to your office for evaluation of genu varum. She has a family history of bowed legs and short stature. Her laboratory studies show a decreased phosphorus, increased serum alkaline phosphatase, normal calcium, and normal vitamin D 25-OH. Mutation of which of the following genes would be most consistent with her underlying diagnosis?

## Options

1. COMP
2. COL5A1
3. PHEX
4. MECP2
5. TNSALP

## Correct option

**3. PHEX**

## Answer explanation

This patient has the diagnosis of X-linked hypophosphatemic rickets, which is associated with a mutation in the PHEX gene.

Rickets is a metabolic bone disease caused by a defect in the mineralization of osteoid matrix caused by inadequate calcium and phosphate that occurs prior to closure of the physes. Patients present with characteristic features such as bowing of long bones, ligamentous laxity, brittle bones and enlargement of costal cartilage. Diagnosis is made based on a thorough evaluation of serum labs, clinical features, and radiographic findings. X-linked hypophosphatemic rickets, which is associated with a mutation in the PHEX gene, will demonstrate labs that show a decreased phosphorus, increased serum alkaline phosphatase, normal calcium and vitamin D 25-OH.

*Sharkey et al. reviewed X-linked hypophosphatemia.* **They reported** that this is an inheritable disorder of renal phosphate wasting that clinically manifests with rachitic bone pathology. **They concluded** that medical treatment is paramount to the success of orthopaedic surgical procedures in both children and adults with X-linked hypophosphatemia.

*Treitz et al. reviewed the several types of rickets.* **They reported** that although vitamin D deficiency is a common cause of rickets, other types of rickets, including hereditary forms, must be considered in the differential diagnosis. **They concluded** that X-linked hypophosphatemic rickets is a disorder caused by renal wasting of phosphate that occurs as a result of a mutation in the PHEX gene.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: A mutation in COMP (cartilage oligomeric matrix protein) gene on chromosome 19 is the most common and most severe form of Multiple Epiphyseal Dysplasia (MED)
Answer 2: COL5A1 mutation is seen in patients with Ehlers-Danlos syndrome
Answer 4: MECP2 gene mutation is encodes the methyl-CpG-binding protein-2, which methylates DNA, and is seen in Rett syndrome
Answer 5: A mutation in the tissue-nonspecific isoenzyme of alkaline phosphatase (TNSALP) is seen in patients with hypophosphatasia
