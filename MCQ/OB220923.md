## Question

Your hospital is launching an initiative to reduce major lower-extremity amputations among adults with diabetes seen in its safety-net clinics. The patient panel includes many individuals with peripheral neuropathy, prior healed ulcers, and high tobacco use. Funding allows only one new program to start this year. Which initiative is most likely to reduce long-term amputation risk in this population?

## Options

1. Annual plantar-pressure screening with custom footwear referral
2. Mandatory total contact casting for all plantar ulcers at presentation
3. Smoking cessation program with counseling and pharmacotherapy
4. Routine arterial ultrasound with referral for angiography as indicated
5. GLP-1 agonist clinic focused on weight loss and cardiovascular risk reduction

## Correct option

**3. Smoking cessation program with counseling and pharmacotherapy**

## Answer explanation

While plantar pressure screening and mechanically-focused treatment modalities (e.g., shoe wear modifications, casting) are important aspects of diabetic ulcer management, smoking cessation, along with glycemic control, is the most impactful modifiable risk factor for lowering future complications and amputation risk (answer choice 3).

Diabetic foot ulceration is a leading predictor of eventual amputation, and prevention requires risk-factor modification plus meticulous mechanical care when ulcers occur. Offloading with total contact casting is the gold standard for active plantar ulcers, and footwear/casting strategies reduce plantar pressure; however, these primarily address mechanical risk during treatment episodes rather than person-level drivers of future amputation. Once ulcers occur, they may be described via grading based on depth, presence of abscess or osteomyelitis, and gangrene. For high-grade ulcerations, operative intervention is often required to unload bony prominences (e.g., via tendon releases or ostectomies), prior to contact casting. The most impactful modifiable risk factors for lowering amputation risk in those with diabetic foot ulcers are smoking cessation and glycemic control.

*Rossboth et al. systematically reviewed 31 studies (28 populations) on risk factors for diabetic foot complications in type 2 diabetes.* **They found** consistently positive associations with various outcomes of interest related to diabetic foot, including gender, peripheral neuropathy, retinopathy, nephropathy, poor glycemic control, insulin use, duration of diabetes, smoking, and height. **They concluded** that glycemic control and smoking are the only modifiable risk factors with a consistently positive association for diabetic foot complications such as ulcerations and amputations.

*Van Netten et al. conducted a systematic review of interventions targeting modifiable foot-specific risk factors (e.g., education, pre-ulcer care, orthoses/footwear, exercises) in at-risk patients without active ulcers.* **They noted** that therapeutic footwear/insoles effectively reduce foot-related mechanical pressure at high-pressure areas, while callus removal immediately reduces in-shoe peak plantar pressure. In contrast, foot- and mobility-related exercises do not reduce peak plantar pressure but may improve neuropathy symptoms and joint range of motion. **They concluded** that pressure-offloading orthotic interventions (custom shoes/insoles) are the most reliable means to reduce peak plantar pressures, whereas exercise-based approaches may offer functional benefits without proven pressure reduction.

Incorrect answer choices:
Answer choice 1: Plantar-pressure screening and custom footwear reduce peak pressures and callus risk but primarily affect surrogate mechanical metrics; evidence for reducing population-level amputation rates is weaker than for smoking cessation.
Answer choice 2: Total contact casting is the gold-standard treatment for active plantar ulcers; however, this has not been shown to consistently lower amputation rates on its own.
Answer choice 4: Arterial imaging with selective revascularization benefits patients with documented ischemia, but routine screening of all comers is low-yield, resource-intensive, and not the single most impactful program for amputation reduction.
Answer choice 5: A GLP-1–focused weight-loss clinic improves cardiometabolic health, but weight loss alone has an inconsistent link to amputation risk; it does not target a consistently supported amenable amputation risk like smoking.
