## Question

Antegrade rigid intramedullary nailing with a trochanteric entry nail is appropriate for which of the following?

## Options

1. A 2-month-old female with displaced, spiral, mid-diaphyseal femur fracture
2. A 26-month-old boy with a displaced spiral mid-diaphyseal femur fracture with <2cm shortening
3. A 7-year old boy with a transverse, non-comminuted mid-diaphyseal femur fracture
4. A 7-year-old boy with a highly comminuted mid-diaphyseal femur fracture
5. A 15-year-old girl with a displaced butterfly fragmented mid-diaphyseal femur fracture

## Correct option

**5. A 15-year-old girl with a displaced butterfly fragmented mid-diaphyseal femur fracture**

## Answer explanation

Antegrade rigid intramedullary nailing with a trochanteric or lateral entry nail is appropriate for skeletally mature or nearly mature adolescents with displaced diaphyseal femur fractures.

Rigid intramedullary nails share load and allow early mobilization of even heavy children. Near skeletal maturity is preferred to avoid damage to an open proximal femoral physis. Avoidance of the piriformis fosaa is also key as this endangers the blood supply to the femoral head, potentially resulting in osteonecrosis of the femoral head (in at least 4%).

*Kocher et al. provide the AAOS Clinical Practice Guideline for the treatment of pediatric diaphyseal femur fractures.* For children aged 11 years to maturity, **they note** that flexible intramedullary nails, rigid trochanteric entry nails, external fixation and submuscular plating are all options for diaphyseal femoral fractures. Piriformis entry nails are not an option, as they place femoral head blood supply at risk.

*Flynn et al. review the management of pediatric femoral shaft fractures.* **They note** that the treatment of choice for displaced femoral shaft fractures in skeletally mature adolescents is rigid intramedullary nailing, as it provides the maximum in stability and load-sharing.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: Children less than 6 months may be treated with spica casting or Pavlik harness. Pavlik is generally favored due to its ease of application and use and lower risk of skin complications
Answer 2: This patient is most appropriately treated with spica casting.
Answers 3 and 4: These patients are too young for intramedullary nailing, which is typically reserved for children aged 11 years to skeletal maturity. Length stable fractures can be treated with flexible intramedullary nails in this age group. Length unstable fractures can be treated with plating or external fixation.
