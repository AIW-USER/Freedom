Chapter: Trauma MCQs — Proximal Humerus Fractures

## Question

Hertel’s criteria helps in predicting the chances of Avascular necrosis in Proximal humerus fractures. The criteria include all of the following except:

## Options

1. Metaphyseal extension (Calcar) <8 mm
2. Loss of integrity of medial hinge (<2mm)
3. Comminution of medial metaphysis
4. Surgical neck fractures

## Correct option

**4. Surgical neck fractures**

## Answer explanation

Proximal humerus fracture

Proximal humerus fractures represent 4–5 % of all fractures.

Mechanism of injury

In 60 years or older, 90 % occur from fall from standing height and in younger individual’s high energy trauma like motor vehicle accidents is the cause.

Patho anatomy and applied anatomy

The proximal humerus vascularity is mainly perfusion from Axillary artery when it passes between pectoralis minor and Teres major. Gives two branches

Anterior humeral circumflex artery – Runs horizontally behind conjoined tendon and anastomose laterally with PHCA. Gives two branches – Ramus Ascendens and Arcuate artery.

Posterior humeral circumflex artery – Supplies 64% of the proximal humerus (Superior, lateral, inferior) [Hettrich et al]. PHCA branch crosses quadrilateral space winding around surgical neck anastomose with AHCA.

Risk of AVN

In proximal humerus fractures, the risk of AVN is higher in

- Varus displacement of head
- Metaphyseal fracture extension of humeral head
- Medial displacement of humeral shaft in relation to humeral head

 Hertel’s criteria (Hertel et al 2004) - Hertel’s criteria helps in predicting the chances of Avascular necrosis in Proximal humerus fractures. (97% positive predictive value)

- Metaphyseal extension (calcar) <8mm
- Loss of integrity of medial hinge (<2mm)
- Fracture pattern – Anatomic neck
- Comminution of medial metaphysis

Classification

The Neer classification for proximal humerus fractures is based on 4 fracture parts: the greater tuberosity, the lesser tuberosity, the humeral head, and the humeral shaft. A fragment is considered displaced if it is separated more than 1 cm or angulated more than 45°.

Treatment

Non-operative treatment

Conservative approach is followed in patients with –

- Undisplaced fracture
- Displaced fracture – High comorbidity, low demand, low compliance, poor bone quality

Operative treatment

Open reduction and internal fixation widely done by Deltopectoral approach. Proximal Humerus Interlocking system plate allows angular stability fixation.

Closed reduction and percutaneous pinning – done mainly in 3-part GT fracture, should not be done when calcar is insufficient, lesser tuberosity fractures and with metaphyseal comminution.

Hemiarthroplasty is done in comminuted head splitting fractures, head depression fractures (>40% articular surface), predictors of head ischemia (Hertel criteria). Following hemiarthroplasty of the humeral head due to a comminuted proximal humerus fracture, poor outcome is commonly associated with migration of the greater tuberosity, the glenoid is generally not resurfaced. Excessive prosthetic retroversion is more closely associated with a poor result than excessive anteversion.

Answer: 4
