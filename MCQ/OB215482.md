## Question

## Options

1. End-stage pantalar arthritis
2. Osteonecrosis of the talus with collapse
3. Osteonecrosis of the talus without collapse
4. Osteonecrosis of the navicular with collapse
5. Osteonecrosis of the calcaneus without collapse

## Correct option

**3. Osteonecrosis of the talus without collapse**

## Answer explanation

This patient sustained a total talar dislocation (extruded talus). The expected radiographic finding following injury is osteonecrosis of the talus without collapse.

Total talus dislocation (extruded talus) is rare. With these injuries, the talus is completely dislocated from the ankle, subtalar, and talonavicular joints. This results from a continuation of forces required for medial or lateral dislocation with disruption of talocrural ligaments and extrusion of talus from ankle joint. These injuries are usually open given the thin soft-tissue envelope at the ankle level.

*Boden et al. reviewed the complications and outcomes following pantalar dislocation without a talar fracture.* **They found** that 88% of the patients with a minimum of 11 months of radiographic follow-up developed osteonecrosis, with only 2 patients demonstrating collapse of the talar dome. **They concluded** that osteonecrosis occurred in the majority of patients, but collapse was uncommon.

*Weston et al. reviewed the treatment and resulting outcomes for total talar dislocation.* *They reported on the complication of avascular necrosis (AVN) following these injuries.* **They concluded** that AVN is still a relatively common complication even in the absence of fracture or postoperative infection.

Figures A-C are various radiographs of an extruded talus rotated nearly 90 degrees and through the skin (the posterior aspect of the talus is directed laterally).

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Post-traumatic arthritis is a very common complication following this injury, with the subtalar joint most commonly being involved. The radiograph finding of end-stage pantalar arthritis 12 months following the injury is unlikely
Answer 2: Osteonecrosis of the talus is very common following a total talus dislocation, however, radiographic evidence of talar collapse is less common and more likely present later.
Answer 4 and 5: The calcaneal and navicular blood supplies are rarely disrupted in this injury pattern, so osteonecrosis here is uncommon.
