## Question

A 16-year-old male football player hits his head and losses consciousness briefly during a game. The player is now mentally lucid and without motor deficits. He denies any neck pain. There is some bloody discharge present in the external auditory meatus. What is the best step for this patient?

## Options

1. Return to play
2. Graduated return to play in 1 week
3. Discontinue play, but can resume practice tomorrow
4. Transfer to the ER for advanced imaging
5. Perform a SCAT5 assessment

## Correct option

**4. Transfer to the ER for advanced imaging**

## Answer explanation

Concern for a skull fracture should be maintained in the presence of blood in the external auditory meatus. This necessitates further workup with advanced imaging.

Sports-related concussions are a common occurrence in many contact sports and can lead to a range of symptoms including headaches, dizziness, memory loss, and more. While the diagnosis of a concussion is typically made through a combination of clinical assessment and symptom monitoring, the presence of red-flag signs necessitates immediate transfer to the emergency department and advanced imaging. These include deteriorating mental status, increasing restlessness, worsening headache, focal neurologic findings, neck pain/tenderness, bloody otorrhea, mastoid ecchymosis, and blepharohematoma.

*Shirley et al. reviewed sports-related concussions.* **The authors highlighted** the risk factors for concussion, including age, sex, and history of a previous concussion. The authors stressed the importance of prompt evaluation and appropriate clinical follow-up, physical and cognitive rest, and a gradual return-to-play protocol.

*Doperak et al. reviewed the evolution of knowledge and understanding of concussions over the past 20 years.* **The authors stated** the previous grading guidelines for concussions were based on loss of consciousness and amnesia, but modern medicine has seen a renaissance of discovery in concussion evaluation and management. The authors discuss the most recent knowledge and recommendations for concussion management based on published evidence.

Incorrect answers
Answer 1: The patient has signs of significant head trauma that necessitates transfer to an ED for further evaluation.
Answers 2 and 3: The patient will likely require a graduated return to play protocol, but further evaluation of head trauma is needed.
Answer 5: A neurologic assessment is necessary to determine the patient's mental status, but the presence of bloody otorrhea suggests significant head trauma requiring advanced imaging.
