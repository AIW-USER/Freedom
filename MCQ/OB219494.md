## Question

You are planning to perform total knee arthroplasty (TKA) on a 59-year-old female. Her body mass index (BMI) is 25, she smokes cigarettes, and she has valgus alignment. You plan to perform robotic TKA. Among the variables in her case, which is associated with increased postoperative opioid use?

## Options

1. Age
2. Body mass index (BMI)
3. Cigarette smoking
4. Robot-assisted total knee arthroplasty (TKA)
5. Valgus alignment

## Correct option

**3. Cigarette smoking**

## Answer explanation

Among the choices provided, only cigarette smoking has been associated with increased postoperative opioid use.

Total knee arthroplasty (TKA) is a painful procedure. Opioids are useful medications in treating postoperative pain, but they also come with side effects and the risk for addiction. Surgeons have a responsibility to control perioperative factors that may contribute to or improve postoperative opioid requirements. There are a number of host factors that have been shown to be associated with elevated opioid requirements: preoperative opioid or benzodiazepine use, cigarette smoking, elevated body mass index (BMI), comorbid psychiatric conditions, among others. Identifying individuals at particularly elevated risk of persistent postoperative opioid use can help direct targeted intervention programs.

*Cryar et al. performed a retrospective study evaluating the effect of preoperative factors on postoperative opioid requirements after total joint arthroplasty (TJA).* **They found** that preoperative opioids, benzodiazepines, smoking, and obesity were associated with greater postoperative morphine milligram equivalents (MME). **They concluded** that predisposition to substance abuse may be a characteristic that leads to increased postoperative narcotic use.

*Kim et al. performed a cohort study among chronic opioid users undergoing TKA, comparing those who discontinued opioids use by 6 months after surgery and those who persistently used opioids.* **They found** that 43.4% of those who chronically used opioids before surgery continued opioid use 6 months following surgery. Factors predictive of persistent opioid use included male gender, prior injury or surgery to the operative knee, cigarette smoking, and comorbid psychiatric condition. **They concluded** that a better understanding of the factors associated with persistent postoperative opioid use could aid in targeted opioid reduction programs.

*Politzer et al. performed a database study reviewing opioid prescription patterns for individuals undergoing TKA.* **They found** that of those taking opioids preoperatively 34.8% became chronic users compared to only 5.0% of the opioid-naïve. **They concluded** that risk factors for chronic postoperative opioid use included preoperative opioid use, female gender, and younger age.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Younger age (e.g. younger than 55) has been associated with increased postoperative opioid use.
Answer 2: Elevated BMI is associated with greater opioid need.
Answer 4: Robot-assisted total knee arthroplasty (TKA) has been found in cohort studies to potentially decrease opioid need.
Answer 5: Valgus alignment has not been associated with increased opioid need.
