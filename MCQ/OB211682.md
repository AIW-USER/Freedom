## Question

Figure A is the radiograph of a 45-year-old male who presents to the ED for evaluation of a right foot injury he sustained on an obstacle course. On exam, he is appropriately tender about the fracture site and has only minimal swelling. A well-padded splint is applied in the ED as a provisional measure pending prompt definitive treatment. Which of the following is the most appropriate position to splint this patient?

![[q211682-a-screen-shot-2021-01-04-at-8-01-27-am.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. 20 degrees ankle dorsiflexion
2. 10 degrees ankle dorsiflexion
3. Neutral
4. 10 degrees ankle plantarflexion
5. 30 degrees ankle plantarflexion

## Correct option

**5. 30 degrees ankle plantarflexion**

## Answer explanation

Skin compromise is a common complication of tongue-type calcaneus fractures, as such, they should be splinted in maximum plantarflexion prior to operative stabilization to decrease tension on the posterior skin.

Avulsion and tongue-type calcaneus fractures are uncommon and represent <15% of calcaneus fractures. This fracture pattern can lead to compromise of the posterior skin with displacement of the proximal fragment and should be treated urgently. Partial or full thickness skin necrosis creates a difficult problem for the treating surgeon. Often times, blanching of the posterior skin is seen at the time of presentation secondary to tension from the underlying bone (Illustration A). Splinting these injuries in plantarflexion helps reduce the displaced fragment and lessens tension on the posterior skin prior to operative stabilization.

*Watson et al. review soft tissue complications following calcaneus fractures.* **They report** skin necrosis, foot compartment syndrome, and neurovascular injuries to be the most commonly encountered complications following these fractures. **They conclude** that initial evaluation of the patient, including the quality of the posterior skin is paramount, as this allows for early treatment and reduces complications.

*Gardner et al. review secondary soft tissue complications specific to tongue-type calcaneus fractures.* **They report** a high incidence (21%) of posterior skin compromise with this specific fracture pattern. **They conclude** that patients should be treated with emergent reduction, plantarflexion splinting, and close monitoring.

Figure A is a lateral radiograph demonstrating a tongue-type calcaneus fracture. Illustration A is a clinical picture of the posterior heel in a patient with a tongue type calcaneus fracture who has early signs of skin compromise.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1-4: Patients with tongue type calcaneus fractures should be splinted in maximum plantarflexion to decrease tension on the posterior skin.
