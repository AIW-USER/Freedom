## Options

1. Observation
2. Medial, proximal tibial epiphysiodesis
3. Lateral, proximal tibial epiphysiodesis
4. Osteotomy and correction with a spatial frame
5. Osteotomy and correction with internal fixation

## Correct option

**1. Observation**

## Answer explanation

This patient sustained a proximal tibia fracture. This injury classically develops a valgus deformity, also known as Cozen's fracture, and commonly resolves with observation.

Proximal tibia metaphyseal fractures have been known to develop a late valgus deformity. This is known as Cozen's fracture or phenomenon. It most commonly occurs in children 3-6 years of age. It is theorized to be from medial physeal overgrowth. The angular deformity resolves spontaneously the majority of time, however, a 9 mm average leg-length discrepancy may persist. The parents of a child sustaining this injury should be counseled in advance of this potential issue. If the deformity fails to resolve, a medial hemi-epiphysiodesis in a skeletally immature patient may be indicated.

*Zionts et al. reviewed 7 cases of children with post-traumatic tibia valga.* **They found** that that the valgus deformity appeared to progress during the period of fracture healing as well as after union of the fracture, as determined clinically and radiographically. The angulation progressed most rapidly during the first year following the injury and then continued at a slower rate for up to seventeen months. **They found** that the deformity corrected spontaneously over time and therefore recommend a conservative approach to treating both the fracture and the possible deformity.

*In another paper, Zionts et al. presented a child who developed a valgus deformity following fracture of the proximal tibia and fibula in which quantitative bone scintigraphy obtained 5 months after injury demonstrated increased uptake at the proximal tibial growth plate with proportionally greater uptake on the medial side.* **They report** that the valgus deformity in this patient was due to a relative increase in vascularity and subsequent overgrowth of the medial portion of the proximal tibial physis.

Figures A & B are radiographs demonstrating proximal tibia fractures in a child. Illustration A is a radiograph of the eventual valgus or Cozen's phenomenon.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 2, 3, 4, & 5: The preferred treatment for Cozen's phenomenon is observation as these deformities typically resolve spontaneously.
