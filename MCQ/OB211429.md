## Question

Figures A is the MR image of a 32-year-old male who twisted his ankle while hiking. He has failed conservative management and continues to complain of pain and instability with ambulation on uneven terrain. Failure to correct which of the following prior to definitive treatment would jeopardize the indicated procedure?

![[q211429-a-30b.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Bunion
2. Tarsal coalition
3. Hindfoot varus
4. Accessory navicular
5. Talar osteochondritis dissecans lesion

## Correct option

**3. Hindfoot varus**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a superior peroneal retinaculum (SPR) rupture with subluxation of the peroneal tendons. Persistent hindfoot varus serves as a risk factor for failure following procedures to repair dislocating peroneal tendons.

Indications for the acute repair of the SPR include dislocating tendons in patients who are athletes or active and desire a faster return to activity. In chronic situations, tissue augmentation of the SPR, retrofibular groove deepening, or fibular bone block procedures may be merited. The presence of hindfoot varus may warrant surgical correction to prevent failure following surgical management for the dislocating tendons. In such cases, a calcaneal osteotomy or subtalar arthrodesis should be considered in patients with hindfoot varus and concomitant peroneal pathology.

*Cho et al. reviewed the operative outcome between retinaculum repair with and without fibular groove deepening for the treatment of recurrent traumatic peroneal tendon dislocation in young, active patients.* **They report** that improvements in AOFAS and VAS scores were not significantly different between the 2 groups. The mean time to return to sports activity was approximately 3 months in both groups. **They concluded** that isolated retinaculum repair compared to retinaculum repair with fibular groove deepening was a faster and simpler technique, but both techniques had good outcomes for the treatment of recurrent traumatic peroneal tendon dislocation.

*Heckman et al. reviewed the operative treatment for peroneal tendon disorders.* **They report** that operative treatment of peroneal tendon tears is based on the amount of remaining viable tendon. **They conclude** that primary repair and tubularization is indicated for tears involving <50% of the tendon, and tenodesis is indicated for tears involving >50% of the tendon.

Figure A is an MR image showing the peroneus longus is anterolateral to the fibula and a shallow retrofibular groove.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer A: A bunion has not been shown to be a risk failure for failure following peroneal tendon stabilization procedures
Answer B: Tarsal coalitions may lead to frequent ankle sprains, but have not been shown to be a risk failure for failure following peroneal tendon stabilization procedures
Answer D: Accessory navicular may become symptomatic, but has not been shown to be a risk failure for failure following peroneal tendon stabilization procedures
Answer E: Talar osteochondritis dissecans lesions have not been shown to be a risk failure for failure following peroneal tendon stabilization procedures
