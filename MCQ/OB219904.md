## Question

An 18-year-old volleyball player suffers her third anterior shoulder dislocation during a game. The surgeon recommends obtaining a CT with 3D reconstructions to better assess the patient’s glenoid bone loss. However, the patient is concerned about the effect of radiation from the study and asks why it is needed when she already has an MRI. Which statement about the use of imaging for evaluating glenoid bone loss is true?

## Options

1. Bone loss can be accurately measured on the Bernageau profile view
2. CT with 3D reconstruction has the lowest predictive error
3. CT with 3D reconstruction has the highest interrater reliability
4. MRI arthrogram can be used as a substitute for a CT scan
5. MRI typically underestimates glenoid bone loss

## Correct option

**3. CT with 3D reconstruction has the highest interrater reliability**

## Answer explanation

CT scans with 3D reconstruction have the highest interrater reliability for assessing glenoid bone loss, while predictive error is similar to MRI. For this reason, 3D CT scans are considered the gold standard for evaluating glenoid bone loss.

Glenoid bone loss can be assessed using multiple imaging modalities, including X-ray, CT, MRI, and 3D CT scans. The 3D CT scan is considered the gold standard and has the highest inter-rater reliability. The difficulty of assessing glenoid bone loss using 2D CT scans and MRI is that the cuts need to be en face with the glenoid to measure bone loss accurately. MRI shows less contrast between soft tissue and bone, which makes measuring glenoid bone loss more difficult. Specialized X-ray views, such as the Bernageau profile view, can also identify bone loss; however, they are significantly less accurate than advanced imaging.

*Ahmed and colleagues performed a retrospective study of patients who underwent arthroscopic Bankart repairs for anterior shoulder instability.* **They found** that the overall risk of recurrence was 13%. Risk factors for recurrence included younger patient age, greater glenoid bone loss, and an engaging Hill-Sachs lesion. **They concluded** that these risk factors need to be considered when determining a surgical procedure for anterior shoulder instability patients.

*Rerko and colleagues performed a cadaveric study to assess the accuracy of X-rays, MRI, 2D CT, and 3D CT scans in characterizing glenoid bone loss.* **They found** that 3D CT had the greatest inter-rater reliability, followed by 2D CT, MRI, and X-rays. The predictive error of the advanced imaging options were similar. **The authors concluded** that 3D CT is the most accurate modality for measuring glenoid bone loss.

Incorrect answers:
1: The Bernageau profile view is not as accurate at measuring glenoid bone loss as any type of 3-dimensional imaging.
2: In the cited article, CT with 3D reconstruction and MRI had similar predictive errors.
4: Depending on the paper, MRI, if anything, has a tendency to overestimate glenoid bone loss.
5: MRI with arthrogram can help assess labral injuries, but does not have the same definition of bony anatomy compared to CT.
