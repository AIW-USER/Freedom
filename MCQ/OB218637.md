## Question

A 50-year-old vintner presents to the clinic with three years of foot and ankle pain preventing him from being able to walk his vineyards to inspect his grapes without constant pain. He states he fell from one of his storage tanks directly onto his heels nearly five years ago and sustained a fracture of his "heel bone" that he did not want to be repaired. You inform him that he has subtalar arthritis with a calcaneal height that is shorter than the contralateral by > 10mm and would benefit from a subtalar arthrodesis with distraction. Which of the following factors would most influence the surgeon's decision to perform this procedure?

## Options

1. Anterior impingement with loss of ankle dorsiflexion
2. Isolated subtalar arthritis with pain over the sinus tarsi
3. Patient age and occupation
4. Presence of tibiotalar arthritis
5. Symptomatic subfibular impingement

## Correct option

**1. Anterior impingement with loss of ankle dorsiflexion**

## Answer explanation

The patient presents with a symptomatic malunion of the calcaneus after non-operative treatment. Loss of calcaneal height resulting in symptomatic anterior ankle impingement and loss of active ankle dorsiflexion, in the setting of subtalar arthritis, is the best indication for distraction subtalar arthrodesis in this patient (Answer 1).

Calcaneal malunions after non-operatively treated fractures of the calcaneus present a difficult problem for both the patient and the provider. Though patients most often go on to develop arthritis of the subtalar joint, which typically manifests symptomatically with pain over the sinus tarsi, numerous other coexisting symptoms and corresponding pathology can be present that ultimately help to guide treatment. Aside from pain originating from the subtalar joint, lateral ankle pain, for instance, can also be caused by calcaneofibular abutment due to lateral wall displacement which may impinge on the peroneal tendons, coexisting calcaneocuboid arthritis, and/or sural nerve irritation. In these patients, surgical options include lateral wall decompression, in situ subtalar arthrodesis, and/or corrective calcaneal osteotomy with arthrodesis. It is particularly important, however, to discern whether or not the patient's chief complaint includes symptoms of anterior impingement, as this can indicate the patient for distraction subtalar arthrodesis, given that in situ arthrodesis alone is unable to correct for the loss of calcaneal height and will not relieve the patient's symptomatic anterior ankle pain and loss of dorsiflexion.

Radnay, et al. published a consecutive series of 69 patients with 75 displaced, intra-articular calcaneus fractures that underwent subtalar arthrodesis for the treatment of painful post-traumatic subtalar arthritis. Thirty-four were initially fixed surgically, while 35 were initially treated non-operatively. The authors found that better functional outcomes and fewer wound complications were associated
with subtalar fusion for the treatment of symptomatic post-traumatic subtalar arthritis after initial open reduction and internal fixation of a displaced intra-articular calcaneal fracture as compared with subtalar arthrodesis for the treatment of symptomatic post-traumatic subtalar arthritis secondary to calcaneal malunion following initial nonoperative treatment. They concluded that ORIF should be employed for the initial treatment of displaced intra-articular calcaneal fractures when appropriately indicated.

*Fletcher et al. published a systematic review of subtalar distraction arthrodesis (SDA) for the treatment of subtalar arthritis.* *Across 25 studies and 467 patients,* **the authors found** that SDA provides good clinical results at short-term and midterm follow-up, with improvement in ankle function as well as acceptable complication and failure rates. **They conclude** that higher quality studies are necessary to better assess outcomes between different operative techniques.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Isolated subtalar arthritis exhibited by pain over the sinus tarsi without anterior ankle involvement can be treated with in situ subtalar arthrodesis.
Answer 3: Age and occupation, in and of themselves, are not the best indications to perform a distraction subtalar arthrodesis in this patient.
Answer 4: The presence of tibiotalar arthritis, in addition to subtalar arthritis, would indicate the patient for triple arthrodesis, not a distraction subtalar arthrodesis.
Answer 5: Symptomatic subfibular impingement from lateral wall impingement is not the best indication for distraction arthrodesis and would likely be better served with lateral wall decompression and subtalar fusion and/or corrective osteotomy.
