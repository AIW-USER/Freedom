## Question

Figure A shows the plain radiograph of a 12-year-old boy who sustained a knee injury playing football. On exam, motion is painful and he has a large, tense effusion. What is the most appropriate next step?

![[q5135-a-figure-a.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) of the knee
2. Biopsy
3. Application of a knee immobilizer and non-weight bearing for 6 weeks
4. Closed reduction. Cast if successful, surgery if non-anatomic
5. Laboratory testing for vitamin D level

## Correct option

**4. Closed reduction. Cast if successful, surgery if non-anatomic**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a Type II tibial spine fracture. These are treated with attempted closed reduction, with operative fixation if unsuccessful. Non-operative treatment is reserved for non-and minimally- displaced fractures.

Type I fractures (non-displaced) are treated with casting. Types II and III fractures are treated with attempted closed reduction and surgery if this is unsuccessful. Obstacles to closed reduction include interposition at the fracture site of the anterior horn of the medial meniscus (most common), intermeniscal ligament, or anterior horn of the lateral meniscus.

*Kocher et al. reviewed a consecutive series of 80 patients with Type III tibial spine fractures or Type II fractures that did not reduce in extension, who underwent surgical fixation.* *At surgery* **they found** entrapment of the anterior horn of the medial meniscus in 36 patients, entrapment of the intermeniscal ligament in six, and entrapment of the anterior horn of the lateral meniscus in one patient.

*Chandler et al. present a case report in which reduction of a tibial spine fracture under general anesthesia produced an apparently anatomic reduction.* However, they proceeded to arthroscopy which revealed entrapment of the medial meniscus in the fracture site. This led them to recommend arthroscopy for all tibial spine fractures with any displacement.

Figure A shows a Type II tibial spine fracture from Rockwood and Wilkins' text. Illustration A shows the classification of tibial spine fractures in that same text.

Incorrect answers.
Answer 1: MRI is not typically needed when plain radiographs have confirmed diagnosis of tibial spine fracture.
Answer 2: Biopsy is not indicated
Answer 3: Displaced fractures require reduction prior to immobilization
Answer 5: There is no indication for vitamin D testing.
