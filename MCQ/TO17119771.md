## Question

A patient had undergone rotator cuff repair 2 months back. He is undergoing rehabilitation. He has been asked to perform forward elevation in scapular plane as you see in picture. The picture is depicting exercises for (FNB 2022)

![[TO17119771-1.png]]

## Options

1. Lower trapezius
2. Serratus anterior
3. Latissimus dorsi
4. Upper Trapezius

## Correct option

**1. Lower trapezius**

## Answer explanation

Ans: A

Scapular exercises done with patient in prone position (prone arm lift exercises) are useful for toning the muscles of upper back, arms and shoulders, specifically, Trapezius, Serratus anterior (SA) and Latissimus dorsi (LD). Hence, they are included in the rehabilitation program for overhead athletes to fine tune the scapulohumeral rhythm.

SCAPULAR PRONE “RETRACTION” EXERCISES whereby arms are elevated in the scapular plane with scapular retraction as the one shown in the picture are optimal to increase activity in Lower trapezius (LT) > Middle trapezius (MT) as well as SA.

- The SA activity increases proportionally to the increase in the humeral elevation angle, but LT activity increases steeply in the end range.
- Therefore, humeral elevation in the end range may be effective in simultaneously increasing the activities of both muscles.

Post Operative patients with or without shoulder pain do not present with “weakness” of the trapezius and serratus anterior. Instead, they present with a lack of endurance, control, or timing of specific muscle activation. Hence, the therapeutic goal in these exercises is not be to induce muscle hypertrophy, but to optimize muscle activation by incorporating repetitive low-to-moderate level resistive exercises.

Exercise that specifically target the Trapezius Muscle

- UPPER TRAPEZIUS:

- “Shrug” exercises and retraction: Exercise starts with the individual with the arms at the side of the trunk and shoulders in retraction (A). Perform the shrug movement (B) and return to the starting position.

![[TO17119771-3.jpg]]

- Overhead “shrug”. Exercise starts with the individual standing, placing the arms in overhead position against the wall and performing a shrug movement and returning to the starting position.

- MIDDLE TRAPEZIUS

- “Retraction” overhead: Exercise starts with the individual standing, placing the arms in overhead position against the wall (A) and lifting both arms (black arrows) while performing retraction of the shoulders (B) and then return to the starting position.

![[TO17119771-4.jpg]]

- LOWER TRAPEZIUS

- Prone scapular setting: Exercise starts with the individual lying in prone with the arm in overhead position resting on the treatment table (A). Position the scapula in relative retraction and depression (black arrow). While maintaining this scapular position, lift the arm slightly off the treatment table (B). Avoid shrugging the shoulder and activity of the upper trapezius.

![[TO17119771-5.jpg]]

- LOWER & MIDDLE TRAPEZIUS:

- Prone scapular retraction: Exercise starts with the individual lying in prone position with arm hanging off the edge of the table. Position the scapula in retraction (black arrow) and depression. Maintain this position while extending the arm posteriorly in line with the trunk, elevating the arm to about 120°. The black arrows indicate the importance of maintaining relative scapular retraction and depression during the exercise to avoid activity of upper trapezius (that’s activated by shrug).
