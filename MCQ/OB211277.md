## Question

A short statured man with a height of 51" presents to your office to establish care regarding his spine. The patient has a normal appearing trunk, however, his humerus and femurs appear shorter than his forearms and tibias, respectively. You appreciate obvious frontal bossing and trident hands. He works as a computer programmer. The patient reports having several family members with the same condition. Based on this description, what is the most common inheritance pattern?

## Options

1. Autosomal dominant
2. Autosomal recessive
3. X-linked dominant
4. X-linked recessive
5. Mitochondrial

## Correct option

**1. Autosomal dominant**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a classic rhizomelic appearance with normal intelligence consistent with achondroplasia. The most common inheritance pattern is autosomal dominant.

Achondroplasia is a common skeletal dysplasia caused by an autosomal dominant gain of function mutation in the fibroblast growth factor receptor 3 (FGFR3) gene. Patients present with rhizomelic dwarfism, in which the humerus is shorter than the forearm and the femur is shorter than the tibia. Patients also typically have frontal bossing, trident hands, genu varum and radial head subluxation. While patients may have delayed motor milestones, they typically have normal intelligence.

*Shirley et al review achondroplasia and its manifestations and treatments.* **They report** that the inheritance is transmitted as a fully penetrant autosomal dominant trait. **They conclude**, however, that many cases are secondary to sporadic inheritance and patients may be born to normal statured parents.

*He et al review the genetic mutation surrounding achondroplasia.* **They report** the G380R mutation in the transmembrane domain of the FGFR3 is the ultimate cause of achondroplasia. **They conclude** that this is the most common form of human dwarfism.

Incorrect Answers:
2-5: Inherited forms of achondroplasia are most commonly due to an autosomal dominant pattern.
