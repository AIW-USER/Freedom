## Question

A 70-year-old female has unrelenting lower back pain and severe left anterior thigh pain. She has hip flexion weakness on the left that is limiting her ambulation. A representative image from her abdominal CT (Figure A) as well as a sagittal MRI of her spine (Figure B) is shown below. Nonoperative management has failed. ## Options

1. Percutaneous kyphoplasty
2. Preoperative embolization of spinal lesion the morning of corpectomy and stabilization
3. External beam radiation
4. Postoperative embolization of spinal lesion the morning after anterior corpectomy and stabilization
5. Immediate anterior corpectomy and stabilization of the spine

## Correct option

**2. Preoperative embolization of spinal lesion the morning of corpectomy and stabilization**

## Answer explanation

The CT of the abdomen shows a renal mass and the biopsy confirms a renal cell carcinoma (RCC), which is known to metastasize to the axial and appendicular skeleton. The goal is to reduce blood loss in patients undergoing surgery for these hypervascular tumors, thus the best next step would be preoperative embolization of spinal lesion the morning of corpectomy and stabilization.

Metastatic cancer is the most common reason for a destructive bone lesion in adults over the age of 40 years. After the lung and liver, bone is the third most common site for metastatic disease, with the most common bony location being the spine. RCC is among the carcinomas that spread to the bone (others include breast, lung, thyroid, and prostate). RCC is extremely hypervascular and has a propensity to bleed profusely. Thus, many authorities recommend arteriography and embolization of the lesion prior to performing any surgery on a suspected renal metastatic lesion.

*Sundaresan et al performed a case-control study of patients with RCC metastases to the spine treated with radiation alone or surgery +/- radiation.* The median survival of the surgically treated patients was 13 months, compared with 3 months for those treated by radiation alone. In addition, a greater proportion of the surgically treated patients were benefitted neurologically (70%) compared with those treated by radiation (45%).

*Prabhu et al. investigated the role of MRI in predicting tumor vascularity and the effectiveness of preoperative embolization in devascularizing spinal tumors.* **They reported** that tumor histology and MRI were predictive of hypervascularity, with hypervascular tumors predominantly RCC, thyroid carcinoma, angiosarcoma, and leiomyosarcoma. **They concluded** that preoperative embolization reduces tumor vascularity, and allows more complete excision and a lower morbidity rate in patients with hypervascular tumors.

Figure A is an abdominal CT axial cut demonstrating a left renal mass. Figure B is a sagittal T2-weighted MRI cut demonstrating a destructive osseous lesion at L2, with the compromise of the spinal canal. Figure C demonstrates the histology of RCC.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Percutaneous kyphoplasty is not indicated in a destructive spinal lesion secondary to metastatic RCC.
Answer 3: Radiation therapy would not alleviate her radicular symptoms and is not indicated in a destructive spinal lesion secondary to metastatic RCC, with spinal canal compromise.
Answer 4: Embolization aims to reduce blood loss prior to surgery for these hypervascular tumors, thus postoperative embolization is not indicated.
Answer 5: Preoperative embolization prior to surgical stabilization is recommended for hypervascular malignancies including RCC.
