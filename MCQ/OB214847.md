## Question

Which of the following is the best initial management for an acute periprosthetic patella fracture in a patient with an intact extensor mechanism and the patellar component demonstrated in Figure A?

![[q214847-a-screen-shot-2020-07-31-at-6-58-19-pm.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Immediate range of motion
2. Knee brace locked in extension or cylinder cast
3. Patellar component revision alone
4. Patellar resection arthroplasty and extensor mechanism repair
5. Open reduction internal fixation with patellar component revision

## Correct option

**2. Knee brace locked in extension or cylinder cast**

## Answer explanation

Nonoperative treatment in a cast or brace is the best initial management for periprosthetic patella fractures with a stable patellar component and intact extensor mechanism. This management avoids the high rate of complications and reoperation associated with operative treatment of these injuries.

Postoperative periprosthetic patella fractures can be classified based on the integrity of the extensor mechanism and the status of the patellar component. Type I describes an intact extensor mechanism and a stable implant. Type II describes the disruption of the extensor mechanism with or without a stable implant. Type III describes an intact extensor mechanism and a loosened component. Type I fractures can be successfully treated nonoperatively with a cast or bracing in extension. Operative management is usually required for fractures associated with extensor disruption (Type II) or those with a symptomatic loose implant (Type III).

*Ortiguera et al. retrospectively reviewed fractures of the patella after total knee arthroplasty (TKA).* **They identified** eighty-five fractures following 12,464 consecutive TKAs (0.68%). **The authors found** thirty-seven fractured patellae had a stable implant with an intact extensor mechanism that were treated nonoperatively. Of these, only one late failure occurred necessitating operative intervention. **The authors conclude** fractures associated with a stable implant and intact extensor mechanism were usually treated successfully with nonoperative means and minimal complications.

*Parvizi et al. reviewed periprosthetic knee fractures and* **note** multiple factors that contribute to variations in reported prevalence of patella fractures. This includes differences in surgical technique, degree of bone resection, and the numerous designs of patellar components. Metal-backed uncemented components or those with large central pegs have been associated with an increased incidence of patellar fractures. Disruption of the extensor mechanism and/or a loose implant almost always necessitates surgical intervention. **The authors conclude** nonoperative treatment of fractures with a stable component and intact extensor function (Type I) is usually successful.

Incorrect Answers
Answer 1: Immediate range of motion may cause fracture displacement.
Answers 3 & 4: The patellar component is intact without signs of loosening or significant bone loss.
Answer 5: Open reduction internal fixation with patella revision is not indicated in a patient with an intact extensor mechanism and stable component.
