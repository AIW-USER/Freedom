CERVICAL DISC REPLACEMENT
• A motion preserving alternative to ACDF, which has been gaining popularity in recent years, is Cervical Disc Arthroplasty (CDA) which involves removing the intervertebral disc and replacing it with an artificial disc that mimics the natural spine biomechanics. The first report on modern cervical disc replacement appeared in 2002.
• ADVANTAGES: -
Developed to preserve subaxial cervical spine biomechanics and natural segmental motion
To mimic the instant center of rotation without fusion
To avoid the complications of nonunion/ pseudoarthrosis and accelerated adjacent segment pathology associated with ACDF to allow for quicker return to activities
All in all, CDA is considered equivalent to fusion in neurological improvements and patient reported outcomes and definitely superior to fusion in reoperation rates
• IDEAL PATIENT
Skeletally mature patient with degenerative cervical radiculopathy with or without myelopathy at one or two levels between C3 and C7 with magnetic resonance pathology revealing cervical herniation of the nucleus pulposus or cervical degenerative disc disease with foraminal stenosis and compression of a nerve root correlating to patients’ symptoms of weakness and numbness that correlates with that specific nerve root patient. Ideally this candidate should be a young patient (not to be done above 70 years), having radicular pain, a single-level “soft” disc herniation, minimal spondylosis, and preserved facet joints.

• The choice of material and implant design plays a critical role to biomechanical and biological performances of a lumbar spine interbody fusion cage. Titanium alloy and polyetheretherketone (PEEK) are the most frequently used materials for an interbody fusion cage. Titanium alloy is believed to provide a better osteoconductive surface than PEEK, but possesses undesirable features (radiopacity, high elastic modulus). 3D-printed porous titanium cages have emerged as a potentially optimal choice for fusion and subsidence performance. Compared to traditional solid PEEK and titanium cages, a porous titanium cage with stress-optimized body lattice and microporous endplates may reduce subsidence risk and promote fusion.

• INDICATIONS AND CONTRAINDICATIONS

I. The index disc height should be 3 mm or more to ensure the insertion of the device does not result in a large distraction that imposes excessive stress on the posterior structures.
II. Patients with major spondylosis or solitary axial neck pain or those with advanced facet joint arthrosis are not ideal candidates, as it is unlikely that the surgery will address these symptoms.
III. Procedure to be avoided in case patient requires surgery at more than or equal to 3 vertebral levels.
IV. Furthermore, preoperative instability due to previous surgery or degenerative disease (translation > 3 mm/ rotational difference to adjacent level > 11o), poor bone quality, metabolic bone disease, morbid obesity and kyphotic deformity - all contribute to failure of the device and therefore careful surgical planning is necessary when these pathologies are encountered.

• COMPLICATIONS

Hardware failure is the major concerning issue that may have catastrophic consequence in case there is retropulsion into spinal canal.
