## Question

A 63-year-old male develops a severe contracture of the left index finger after undergoing irrigation and debridement for flexor tenosynovitis. Which of the technical errors was performed at the index surgery that predisposed to this patient's condition?

## Options

1. Use of a midlateral incision on the ulnar side of the finger
2. Not releasing the A1 pulley at the time of the I&D
3. Volar midline incision perpendicular to the skin creases
4. Bruner incision to debride to the entire flexor tendon sheath
5. Closed catheter irrigation of the tendon sheath

## Correct option

**3. Volar midline incision perpendicular to the skin creases**

## Answer explanation

The use of a volar midline incision perpendicular to the skin creases predisposes the digit to develop flexion contractures due to extensive scar formation.

Flexor tenosynovitis is an acute bacterial infection of the flexor tendon sheath and is considered a surgical emergency. Infections can rapidly progress and spread to more proximal portions of the hand and wrist leading to complicated surgical treatment and recovery. Treatment almost always involves I&D, except in early-stage infections. Several techniques exist for I&D, with the common intention of avoiding incisions perpendicular to the skin creases. Perpendicular incisions over the palmar surface of the hand promote scar tissue formation that may form a flexion contracture. In these scenarios, Z-plasty revision of the scar can be effective in regaining the range of motion of the affected digit.

*Kwan and Tredget reviewed the biological principles of scar formation and contractures.* **They stated** that hypertrophic scar and contracture in burn patients is a complex process influenced by various cell subpopulations, including fibroblasts, myofibroblasts, fibrocytes, and T-helper cells. Collectively, the inflammatory response causes scarring rather than regeneration. **The authors concluded** these cells influence each other via cellular profibrotic and antifibrotic signals, which help to determine the outcome of the wound.

*Neviaser retrospectively studied twenty patients with pyogenic flexor tenosynovitis treated with through-and-through saline irrigation using an indwelling catheter and small Penrose drain for 48 hours.* **The author reported** complete active and passive motion recovery in 18 patients within 1 week after the operation. **The author concluded** this technique was found to effectively treat pyogenic flexor tenosynovitis while providing a rapid and complete return to function to the patient.

Incorrect answers
Answer 1: A midlateral incision does not predispose the digit to develop a flexion contracture.
Answer 2: Release of the A1 pulley at the time I&D does not reduce the risk of flexion contracture development.
Answer 4: A Bruner incision is a zig-zag incision that avoids crossing volar skin creases at perpendicular angles to minimize scar contractures.
Answer 5: Closed catheter irrigation of the flexor tendon sheath involves an A1 and A5 incision with the insertion of a catheter into the tendon sheath at the level of the A1 pulley. Sterile saline is flushed through the tendon sheath and drains through the A5 incision. This technique minimizes scar tissue formation.
