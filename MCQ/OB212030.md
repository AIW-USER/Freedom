## Question

A 25-year-old lacrosse player presents for evaluation of persistent right hip pain. The pain has been ongoing for greater than 1 year and has not improved with 6 months of physical therapy. An intra-articular hip injection temporarily relieved his pain. On examination, he notes hip pain when his hip is moved from a fully extended, externally rotated, and abducted position to a position of flexion, internal rotation, and adduction. His right hip radiograph is shown in Figure A. Right hip MRI reveals an anterosuperior labral tear at the chondro-labral junction. Which of the following is the next step in management?

![[q212030-a-ap-right-hip.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Osteoplasty of Pincer lesion and labral repair
2. Osteoplasty of CAM lesion and labral repair
3. Osteoplasty of Pincer lesion and labral reconstruction
4. Osteoplasty of CAM lesion and labral debridement
5. Continued physical therapy for 3 months followed by hip labral repair only

## Correct option

**1. Osteoplasty of Pincer lesion and labral repair**

## Answer explanation

The vignette describes a young and active patient with a right hip labral tear from pincer-type femoroacetabular impingement (FAI). His radiographs suggest focal acetabular retroversion without a significant cam-type deformity. The most appropriate next step is to perform an arthroscopic osteoplasty of the pincer acetabular overhang and to repair the torn labral tear.

FAI results from abnormal contact between the femur and acetabulum which leads to labral and chondral damage. FAI may be due to either Cam impingement, Pincer impingement, or a combination of both. Cam impingement is the femoral-based disorder that generally results from a broad femoral neck while Pincer impingement is the acetabulum-based disorder that occurs when the acetabular bone/labrum overhang is too broad, mostly at the anterosuperior quadrant. Symptomatic patients who have failed conservative management should have surgical treatment of the underlying bony disease (Cam or Pincer lesion) in addition to the treatment of a torn labrum and cartilage, if present. Hip labrum tears at the chondrolabral junction are candidates for repair given the increased vascular supply from the acetabulum margin while intra-substance tears are generally debrided.

*Safran et al. review the etiology and treatment of acetabular labral tears.* **He notes** that labral tears may cause hip pain, microinstability of the hip joint, and increased friction within the joint which may result in accelerated degeneration of the joint. **He notes** that both partial labrectomy and labral repair are current surgical options, and basic science data suggest that labral repairs can heal and subsequently restore function. **He concludes** that since vascular supply to the labrum comes from the acetabular margin, tears at the chondrolabral junction would be the most likely to heal with a repair.

*Matsuda et al. performed a systematic comparative review of open surgical dislocation, mini-open, and all-arthroscopic management of FAI.* **The authors noted** that all 3 treatments are effective in improving pain and function in short-term to midterm studies. The open surgical dislocation had a high major complication rate primarily due to trochanteric osteotomy-related issues. The mini-open method showed comparable efficacy but a significant incidence of iatrogenic injury to the lateral femoral cutaneous nerve in some studies. **The authors concluded** that the arthroscopic method had surgical outcomes equal to or better than the other methods with a lower rate of major complications when performed by experienced surgeons.

*Byrd et al. performed a prospective study to assess the results and outcomes of the primary repair of the torn acetabular labrum.* *They evaluated 38 hips who underwent labrum repair with a minimum of 2-year follow-up.* *At the final follow-up, the mean improvement in the modified Harris Hip Score was 18.9 points, with 35 hips (92%) showing good and excellent results.* Four patients underwent repeat arthroscopy at a mean of 10 months. The labral repair site was fully healed in each of these 4 cases. There were no complications. **The authors concluded** primary repair of hip labrums was associated with favorable outcomes and evidence of good healing, even among the 11% of patients who required repeat arthroscopy.

Figure A is a right hip AP radiograph with the "cross-over" sign indicative of acetabular retroversion consistent with Pincer FAI. Illustration A is the same image with the cross-over sign marked.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 2 and 4: The patient has no evidence of a CAM lesion on the AP radiograph but a clear cross-over sign indicative of pincer impingement
Answer 3: A hip labral tear at the chondro-labral junction is most amenable to repair compared to a reconstruction.
Answer 5: The patient has already failed 6 months of physical therapy and is a candidate for surgical intervention. Furthermore, an isolated labrum repair is not appropriate and the underlying cause of the tear (pincer lesion) should be addressed.
