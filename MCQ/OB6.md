## Question

Both quadrupled hamstring tendon and 10-mm bone-patellar tendon-bone autografts are used for ACL recconstruction. Which of the following statements is true of the load to failure of the hamstring graft relative to the patellar tendon graft on biomechanical testing?

## Options

1. Equal load to failure.
2. One half the failure load.
3. One quarter the failure load.
4. Roughly one and a half times the failure load.
5. Roughly four times the failure load.

## Correct option

**4. Roughly one and a half times the failure load.**

## Answer explanation

The load to failure of the quadrupled hamstring graft relative to the patellar tendon graft on biomechanical testing is roughly one and a half times.

Graft selection is based on issues of strength, fixation, and potential complications such as donor site morbidity, weakness, arthrofibrosis, or anterior knee pain. The graft choices utilized most frequently are the bone patellar tendon bone autograft, quadrupled hamstring autograft, and allograft. BTB is considered the "gold standard" as it involves bone to bone healing, and can be fixed rigidly with screws. It has the downside of having the highest incidence of anterior knee pain. The hamstring graft may involves a smaller incision and has a lower incidence of anterior knee pain. Allograft is useful in revision situations, however has been shown to have a longer incorporation time and increased risk of re-rupture in young athletes.

*Wilson et al tested cadaveric specimens (mean age 40) and* **found** a significant difference in average load to failure comparing BTB to quadrupled hamstring grafts. The average load to failure for the BTB grafts was 1784 +/- 580 N compared to 2422 +/- 538 N for the quadrupled hamstring tendon grafts.

*Hamner et al.* **demonstrated** that four combined strands (2 gracilis and 2 semitendinosus) that were equally tensioned with weights and clamped were stronger and stiffer than all 10 mm patellar tendon grafts. All strands of a hamstring graft must be equally tensioned.

Incorrect Answer Choices:
Answers 1, 2, 3, and 5 do not have the correct stiffness of the quadrupled hamstring graft compared to the BTB graft.
