## Question

You are a junior resident on call at a level-one trauma center. You are called to the emergency department to evaluate an ankle fracture in a multiply injured patient after a motor vehicle collision. When evaluating radiographs and classifying the patient's ankle fracture, which of the following fracture patterns would be most concerning for having a concomitant injury to the talus?

## Options

1. Supination external rotation
2. Supination adduction
3. Pronation external rotation
4. Pronation abduction
5. Maissonneuve

## Correct option

**2. Supination adduction**

## Answer explanation

Supination adduction (SAD) ankle fractures are more likely to have concomitant injury to the talus and hindfoot, compared to rotational ankle fractures.

The Lauge-Hansen classification for ankle fractures is based on the position of the foot/ankle at the time of injury. SAD injuries account for 5-20% of all ankle fractures. SAD injuries occur in two stages, with a low, transverse fibula fracture typically occurring first, followed by a vertical shear fracture of the medial malleolus which causes impaction of the medial talar dome into the medial tibial plafond. This leads to a higher rate of talar osteochondral defects (OCD) as well as medial tibial plafond marginal impaction, both of which have a negative prognostic effect on outcomes. The combination of articular cartilage injury means that a higher percentage of these patients go on to develop post-traumatic arthritis, with rates up to 80% in some studies.

*Alluri et al. discussed the radiographic evaluation of marginal impaction in SAD ankle fractures.* **They noted** marginal impaction of the plafond in 61% of ankle fractures and stated that radiographs correctly identified this in 84% of cases when compared to CT imaging. **They concluded** that the majority of SAD ankle fractures do involve impaction of the plafond and plain radiographs were accurate in diagnosis and operative planning compared to CT.

*Benedick et al. reviewed SAD ankle fractures and their association with arthritis, outcomes and other injuries.* *Patients who sustained SAD injuries were more likely to be younger, involved in an MVC, and more frequently were polytrauma patients with regards to orthopedic and nonorthopedic injuries.* These patients were also more likely to have concomitant hindfoot (23%) and talus (20%) injuries compared to rotational type ankle fractures (hindfoot: 6%, talus: 2.5%).

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1, 3-5: These injury patterns are less likely to have concomitant orthopedic injuries, including injuries to the talus (fracture, articular impaction, etc) and hindfoot.
