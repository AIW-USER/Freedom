Chapter: Arthroscopy MCQs — Shoulder Instability[Unidirectional and Multidirectional]

## Question

A 22-year-old elite Kabaddi player presents with recurrent right shoulder instability (12 episodes). Clinical examination reveals a positive apprehension-relocation test and a Beighton score of 2.

3D-CT imaging (Best-Fit Circle Method) reveals:

 - Native Glenoid Diameter (D): 24 mm.

 - Current Anterior-Inferior Glenoid Width: 20 mm.

 - Humeral Head Measurement: A Hill-Sachs lesion is present. The distance from the medial margin of the Hill-Sachs defect to the lateral-most insertion of the infraspinatus (the Hill-Sachs Interval, HSI) is measured at 18 mm.

Based on the Glenoid Track concept, which of the following describes the status of the lesion and the most appropriate surgical intervention?

## Options

1. On-track lesion; isolated arthroscopic Bankart repair is sufficient.
2. Off-track lesion; requires arthroscopic Bankart repair combined with a Remplissage procedure.
3. On-track lesion; however, due to sub-critical bone loss (>15%), a Latarjet procedure is indicated.
4. Off-track lesion; requires a Latarjet procedure to address both the glenoid bone loss and the engagement.

## Correct option

**4. Off-track lesion; requires a Latarjet procedure to address both the glenoid bone loss and the engagement.**

## Answer explanation

Glenoid Bone Loss calculation

 - Native Diameter (D) = 24 mm.

 - Current Width = 20 mm.

 - Bone loss (d) = 24 - 20 = 4 mm

 - Percentage loss = (4 / 24) \times 100 = 16.6%.

 - This is considered "sub-critical" bone loss
 (typically 13.5%–20%), where a standard Bankart repair has a high failure rate in contact athletes.

Glenoid Track (GT) Width calculation:

The formula for the Glenoid Track is:

GT = (0.83 times D) - d

 - GT = (0.83 times 24) - 4

 - GT = 19.92 - 4 = 15.92

On track/off track calculation

 - Hill-Sachs Interval (HSI): 18 mm.

 - Glenoid Track (GT): 15.92 mm.

 - Comparison: Since HSI (18 mm) > GT (15.92 mm), the lesion is OFF-TRACK.

Why not B (Bankart + Remplissage)? While Remplissage can technically turn an off-track lesion into an on-track one, the patient has 16.6% glenoid bone loss and is an elite contact athlete (Kabaddi). In this population, the Latarjet is superior because it addresses the glenoid bone loss directly by increasing the "track" width.

Answer: 4
