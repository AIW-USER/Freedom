## Question

A fibular epiphysiodesis would be indicated in which of the following scenarios?

## Options

1. A 10-year-old boy with a SH IV distal tibia fracture with > 3mm of residual displacement
2. A 10-year-old girl with a < 50% physeal bar in the distal tibia 4 months after an ankle fracture
3. A 13-year-old girl with a < 50% central physeal bar in the distal tibia 6 months after an ankle fracture
4. A 15-year-old girl with a 25° varus angular deformity of the distal tibia 2 years after an ankle fracture
5. A 13-year-old boy with a > 50% physeal bar in the distal tibia 5 months after an ankle fracture

## Correct option

**5. A 13-year-old boy with a > 50% physeal bar in the distal tibia 5 months after an ankle fracture**

## Answer explanation

A fibular epiphysiodesis would be indicated in a 13-year-old boy with a > 50% physeal bar in the distal tibia 5 months after an ankle fracture. Consideration for a distal tibial epiphysiodesis is warranted as well if the bar is peripheral and could result in an angular deformity; if central, this may not be needed.

Physeal bridges commonly occur after Salter-Harris (SH) physeal fractures in early adolescence when the physis is thickest and the cartilage is weakest. Physeal bridges that are small (< 25% of physeal area) and central have a better prognosis. Presence of a physeal bridge < 50% of physeal area in a patient with at least 2 years of growth remaining is an indication for physeal bridge resection. The presence of a physeal bar > 50% of the physeal area in the distal tibia in a patient with at least 2 years of growth remaining and an open fibular growth plate can result in significant deformity and lateral ankle impingement, and thus an ipsilateral fibular epiphysiodesis should be considered to prevent lateral impingement.

*Podeszwa et al. review distal tibia and fibula fractures in the pediatric patient.* **They emphasize** that displaced fractures should undergo a gentle reduction with appropriate anesthesia and that multiple reduction attempts should be avoided. Gapping of the physis > 3mm after reduction should raise the suspicion of entrapped periosteum, which increases the risk of premature physeal closure.

*Nenopoulos et al. review the outcomes of physeal injuries of the distal tibia.* *They report on 83 patients with distal tibia fractures with 60/83 being treated nonoperatively.* Varus deformity, ranging from 10 to 15° in relation to the normal opposite leg, occurred in four cases.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: This patient would benefit from repeat open or closed reduction to improve his displacement and decrease the likelihood of physeal bar formation.
Answer 2: The presence of a physeal bridge < 50% of physeal area in a patient with at least 2 years of growth remaining is an indication for physeal bridge resection.
Answer 3: This patient has a central physeal bar with less than 2 years of growth remaining so neither a bar excision or a lateral fibular epiphysiodesis would be warranted given that there is minimal growth remaining.
Answer 4: At 15 years old, this patient has likely reached skeletal maturity and would therefore require an osteotomy to correct her angular deformity.
