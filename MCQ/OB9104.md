## Question

A 20-year-old male sustained an ankle sprain 4 weeks ago while skiing. He now complains of persistent painful snapping and popping posterior to the lateral malleolus. On exam, snapping is felt over the lateral fibula when the patient moves against resistance in dorsiflexion and eversion. What was the most likely mechanism of his injury?

## Options

1. Forced dorsiflexion and inversion
2. Forced dorsiflexion and eversion
3. Forced plantarflexion and eversion
4. Forced plantarflexion and inversion
5. Direct trauma to the fibula

## Correct option

**1. Forced dorsiflexion and inversion**

## Answer explanation

The patient presentation is consistent with peroneal tendon subluxation/dislocation secondary to a forced dorsiflexion and inversion injury.

The peroneal tendons are located posterior to the lateral malleolus and are a common source of pathlogy in refractory ankle sprains. This patient is complaining of subluxating peroneal tendon(s) as a result of a torn superior peroneal retinaculum (SPR). Patients with varus alignment are at higher risk for peroneal tendon pathology. On exam, the compression test elicits pain or crepitus with passive dorsiflexion and eversion. Patients may note apprehension or subluxation with resisted dorsiflexion and eversion. Patient history often includes a report of forced dorsiflexion and inversion leading to rapid reflexive contraction of the peroneal tendons. This places excessive tension on the tendons leading to tearing of the SPR.

*Maffulli et al followed 14 males who underwent surgical treatment for traumatic peroneal tendon subluxation.* No patients experienced reuinjury and all returned to their normal activities. **They concluded** that an anatomic repair of the retinaculum leads to good results in higher demand patients.

*Philbin et al reviewed peroneal tendon pathology including peroneal subluxation and dislocation.* **The author states** that treatment in acute cases may consist of nonoperative management with short leg cast immobilization of the ankle in plantarflexion and inversion for 6 weeks or surgical intervention in the form of SPR repair.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer choices 1, 3, 4, and 5 do not have the correct combination of forced dorsiflexion and eversion leading to peroneal tendon subluxation.
