## Options

1. Hypercalcemia; Isocitrate dehydrogenase 1
2. Hyperphosphatemia; Fibroblast growth factor 23
3. Hypovitaminosis D; Alpha-1 hydroxylase
4. Elevated creatinine kinase; dystrophin
5. Hyperphosphatasia; Activin A receptor type 1C

## Correct option

**2. Hyperphosphatemia; Fibroblast growth factor 23**

## Answer explanation

The patient is presenting with radiographic and histologic findings consistent with tumoral calcinosis which is caused by hyperphosphatemia. Defects in the fibroblast growth factor 23 (FGF-23) gene have been associated with elevated phosphate levels and tumoral calcinosis.

Tumoral calcinosis is the pathologic deposition of calcium adjacent to joints and occurs in the presence of phosphate dysregulation. Commonly associated syndromes include familial hyperphosphatemic tumoral calcinosis and calcinosis of renal failure. The genetic defect of the FGF-23 gene leads to hyperphosphatemic tumoral calcinosis. Laboratory findings are often significant for hyperphosphatemia but may be associated with elevated PTH, uric acid, and alkaline phosphatase. Calcinosis usually involves the hip and the shoulder. Treatment involves normalizing serum phosphate and surgical removal of symptomatic lesions.

*Bergwitz et al. reviewed FGF-23 mutations and disorders of phosphate regulation.* **The authors stated** that FGF-23 functions in response to vitamin D and phosphate levels to regulate serum phosphate levels. **They concluded** that mutations in this gene can lead to hyperphosphatemic familial tumoral calcinosis.

*Garringer et al. performed a study of FGF-23 mutations.* **The authors found** that gene mutation resulted in the production of a truncated FGF-23 protein that had substantially lower activity than the full sequence protein. **The authors concluded** that all mutations of the FGF-23 gene that result in tumoral calcinosis are due to the destabilization of the protein.

Figures A and B are AP and lateral radiographs of the right elbow with tumoral calcinosis of the olecranon bursa. Figure C demonstrates the histology of tumoral calcinosis.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: Hypercalcemia does not result in tumoral calcinosis. Defects in isocitrate dehydrogenase 1 can cause Maffucci's syndrome which leads to multiple enchondromas and angiomas with a deforming phenotype to affected hands.
Answer 3: Hypovitaminosis D does not result in tumoral calcinosis, but can result in rickets in pediatric patients with open physes. Deficiency in alpha-1 hydroxylase can lead to hypovitaminosis D.
Answer 4: Genetic defect of dystrophin leads to Duchenne's muscular dystrophy, which leads to early muscle dysfunction. Serum creatinine kinase will be elevated in these patients due to diffuse muscle breakdown from the absent protein.
Answer 5: Activin A receptor type 1 has been linked to fibrodysplasia ossificans progressiva, which results in abnormal ossification of connective tissue at a young age. Elevated alkaline phosphate levels can be found with this disease.
