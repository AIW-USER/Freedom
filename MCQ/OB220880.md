## Answer explanation

Continuity between the anterior and posterior horns of the meniscus on three consecutive sagittal MRI slices (bow-tie sign) indicates the presence of a discoid meniscus. Patients with discoid menisci may exhibit findings on plain radiographs such as joint space widening, lateral femoral condyle squaring, and lateral tibial plateau cupping, which may indicate the presence of this condition (Answer 3).

Discoid menisci are abnormal anatomic variants of the knee, frequently thicker in size and more discoid in shape. They most commonly affect the lateral meniscus. Though many patients remain asymptomatic, the abnormal anatomy predisposes patients to an increased risk of meniscal tears, which can lead to knee pain and mechanical symptoms such as clicking or locking. An exam of the contralateral extremity is often needed to rule out the presence of bilateral pathology. Plain radiographs of the knee sometimes, but not always, demonstrate findings that indicate the presence of a discoid meniscus. These include widening of the affected knee compartment, squaring of the lateral femoral condyle, the presence of a hypoplastic lateral tibial spine, and cupping of the lateral tibial plateau. MRI is often more diagnostic and will demonstrate meniscal continuity on at least three 5 mm cut sagittal slices (bow-tie sign).

*Tyler et al. reviewed the imaging findings characteristic of discoid menisci.* *They cover the anatomic abnormalities characteristic of this condition and the associated radiographic findings, which include indirect signs seen on plain radiograph and MRI findings such as a bow-tie sign or high lateral meniscus to tibia width ratio.* **They conclude** that a comprehensive understanding of the anatomy and tear patterns characteristic of this particular condition is important for pre-operative planning.

*Kocher et al. performed a systematic review of discoid meniscuses.* *They review the abnormal, thicker meniscus anatomy, Watanabe classification, presentation, radiographic findings, and outcomes.* **They conclude** that the current literature lacks follow-up studies to address questions regarding the long-term risks of osteoarthritis after meniscal saucerization.

Figures:
Figure A demonstrates evidence of a Segond fracture, suggestive of an anterior cruciate ligament injury.
Figure B demonstrates evidence of patella alta, which is not typically seen in conjunction with a discoid meniscus.
Figure C demonstrates evidence of lateral joint space widening and squaring of the lateral femoral condyle, which is suggestive of the presence of a discoid meniscus.
Figure D demonstrates evidence of chondrocalcinosis and extensive degenerative changes in the setting of calcium pyrophosphate dehydrate deposition disease.

Incorrect Answers:

Answers 1, 2, 4, and 5: These radiographs are not consistent with the findings of joint space widening, lateral femoral condyle squaring, or lateral tibial plateau cupping suggestive of the presence of a discoid meniscus.
