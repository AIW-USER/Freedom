## Options

1. Open reduction and internal fixation (ORIF) with a plate over nail construct
2. ORIF with medial and lateral locking plates
3. Acute total knee arthroplasty (TKA) with a stemmed component
4. Distal femoral replacement (DFR)
5. ORIF with a lateral locking plate

## Correct option

**4. Distal femoral replacement (DFR)**

## Answer explanation

Geriatric patients with intraarticular distal femur fractures and preexisting arthritis and presumptive osteopenia/osteoporosis are best managed with distal femur replacement (DFR) (Answer 4).

Distal femur fractures are traumatic injuries that extend from the distal metaphyseal-diaphyseal junction to the articular surface of the femoral condyles. They represent 3-6% of femur fractures and <1% of all fractures. These injuries have a bimodal distribution and often occur in young, healthy males secondary to high-energy trauma or in elderly osteopenic females secondary to falls.

Treatment is almost always operative, and the optimal surgical strategy depends on various factors, including fracture pattern and displacement, patient bone stock, and patient comorbidities. Recently, DFR has gained traction as an attractive treatment option for intraarticular distal femur fractures in elderly patients with preexisting osteoarthritis. While this technique has similar complication and reoperation rates compared to ORIF, patients undergoing DFR are much more likely to demonstrate ambulatory independence at short-term follow-up. It is important to note, however, that these results are drawn from a retrospective study and that prospective studies are currently underway.

*Hart et al. provide a comparative study investigating DFR and ORIF in treating distal femur fractures in patients over 70.* *Their study included 38 patients: 10 who underwent DFR and 28 who underwent ORIF.* The mean patient age in both cohorts was 82 years. Overall, nearly 1 in 5 patients developed nonunion after ORIF. Furthermore, 1 in 4 patients in the ORIF were wheelchair-bound at one year, versus no patients in the DFR group. It is important to note that this was a retrospective, nonrandomized observational study.

*Salazar et al. provide a systematic review investigating DFR versus surgical fixation (SF) in treating geriatric distal femur fractures.* *Their study included 30 studies reporting on outcomes in 766 patients.* Overall, DFR and SF demonstrated similar surgical complication rates. **The authors note**, however, that they did not investigate functional outcomes (due to heterogeneity of data), which limits their ability to recommend the superiority of one surgical strategy over another.

Figure A is a left knee radiograph demonstrating joint space narrowing and osteophyte formation, consistent with osteoarthritis. Figure B is a left knee radiograph demonstrating a comminuted intra-articular distal femur fracture in the setting of existing osteoarthritis.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1,2,5: While plate over nail (Answer 1), dual plate (Answer 2) or lateral locked plate (Answer 5) constructs are all applicable in the treatment of distal femur fractures, lower demand patients with high degrees of comminution and preexisting osteoarthritis are best treated with a distal femoral replacement (DFR).
Answer 3: A stemmed total knee arthroplasty construct is not typically used to treat comminuted distal femur fractures.
