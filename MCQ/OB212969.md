## Question

Which of the following is the likely mechanism of injury shown in Figure A?

![[q212969-a-tibial-tubercle.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Knee flexion with quadriceps contraction
2. Knee extension with hamstring contraction
3. Direct blow to the anterior knee
4. Knee extension with gastrocnemius contraction
5. Knee flexion with hamstring contraction

## Correct option

**1. Knee flexion with quadriceps contraction**

## Answer explanation

Figure A is a radiograph of a tibial tubercle fracture which results from knee flexion with quadriceps contraction.

Tibial tubercle fractures are uncommon in children. However, injuries can occur in young adolescent males during sporting activities. The proximal tibial physis closes distally towards the tubercle apophysis, which creates a mechanically vulnerable period in adolescence that predisposes the tubercle to avulsion during aggressive knee flexion and quadriceps contraction. There is a spectrum of injury, ranging from a minimal displacement of the apophysis, intra-articular extension, and potentially damage to the meniscus. Patients who sustain this injury require operative treatment to restore their extensor mechanism.

*Pandya et al. performed a review of tibial tubercle fractures.* **They report** that these injuries are usually due to high-energy mechanisms and can present with complications such as compartment syndrome and vascular compromise. Intra-articular extension is often missed with radiographs and **they conclude** that CT should be used if there is any concern for intra-articular extension.

*Pretell-Mazzini et al. performed a systematic review of tibial tubercle fracture in pediatric patients.* **They report** that operative treatment usually produces good results regardless of fracture type. **They also recommended** advanced imaging if there is a concern for intra-articular extension. **They concluded** that, if intra-articular fractures are not identified, more complications may occur.

Figure A is a lateral radiograph of the knee demonstrating a tibial tubercle fracture. Illustration A (Pandya et al.) is an illustration demonstrating the pattern of closure of the proximal tibial physis.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 2, 3, 4 & 5: Tibial tubercle fractures occur with rapid knee flexion and quadriceps contraction.
