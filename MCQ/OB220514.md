## Answer explanation

Based on the histologic specimen, the surgical treatment, and the age of the patient, this patient most likely has a chondroblastoma which is depicted in Figure C (Answer 3).

Chondroblastoma most commonly presents as a centrally located epiphyseal lesion in skeletally immature patients. The common differential diagnoses for epiphyseal lesions include giant cell tumors of bone, clear cell chondrosarcoma, and chondroblastoma. Chondroblastomas are characterized as rare, benign but locally destructive tumors of chondroblasts. The defining features of this disease process on histology are polygonal mononuclear chondroblasts with a pericellular "chicken wire" dystrophic calcification. Their benign but locally aggressive nature makes them amenable to surgical curettage and grafting with the utilization of adjuvant therapies such as phenol, argon beam coagulation, hydrogen peroxide, or cryotherapy.

*Grimm et al. reviewed tumors of the epiphysis.* **The authors state** that the most common epiphyseal tumors in order of decreasing frequency include giant cell tumors (GCTs), chondroblastomas, and clear cell chondrosarcomas. The defining features of chondroblastomas are that they affect younger males (5–20 years old), often in the femoral or humeral head, with a lytic lesion abutting the physis and “chicken wire” calcification histology. GCTs occur in adults (20–40 years old), are eccentric and lytic without a sclerotic rim, and feature multinucleated giant cells on histology. Clear cell chondrosarcomas appear in middle-aged males (30–50 years old), are larger lytic lesions with sclerotic rims, and show chondrocytes with perinuclear clearing. **The authors conclude** that accurate diagnosis is critical, as treatment varies significantly between the different entities.

*El-Ali et al. reviewed the radiologic-pathological correlation and epidemiology for solitary long-bone epiphyseal lesions in pediatric patients.* *In this retrospective study of 49 pediatric cases with biopsy-proven diagnoses, chondroblastoma was the most common lesion (45%), followed by nonspecific benign findings and osteomyelitis.* Chondroblastomas were more common in older children and demonstrated distinct imaging features, including peripheral location and a T1 hypointense rim, which differentiated them from osteomyelitis. **The authors conclude** that the distinct radiologic and pathologic features of chondroblastoma enable accurate diagnosis, aiding in appropriate management and reducing unnecessary interventions in pediatric epiphyseal lesions.

Figure A shows an AP radiograph of the knee demonstrating an eccentric lytic lesion involving the metaphysis and epiphysis in a skeletally mature patient likely indicative of a giant cell tumor. Figure B is a lateral radiograph of the knee demonstrating an osteogenic malignancy located on the posterior surface of the distal femur which is indicative of parosteal osteosarcoma. Figure C is an AP radiograph of the knee demonstrating a lytic and central disease process involving the epiphyseal region of the distal femur in a skeletally immature patient which is indicative of chondroblastoma. Figure D is an AP radiograph of the femur demonstrating a sunburst pattern emanating from the meta-diaphyseal region of the distal femur indicative of osteosarcoma. Figure E is a lateral radiograph of the distal femur which demonstrates a permeative process in the metaphysis with associated onion skinning which is indicative of Ewings Sarcoma. Figure F is a low-power histologic specimen demonstrating oval to polygonal chondroblasts adjacent to a mature cartilage matrix with associated "chicken wire" calcifications, indicative of chondroblastoma.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: This radiograph demonstrates an eccentric lesion involving the epiphysis of the distal femur in a skeletally mature patient which is indicative of a GCT rather than a chondroblastoma.
Answer 2: The surgical treatment for low-grade parosteal osteosarcoma is wide surgical resection, not curettage and grafting.
Answer 4: Osteosarcoma would be treated with neoadjuvant chemotherapy, wide surgical resection and adjuvant chemotherapy. Also, histology would demonstrate cellular atypia, mitoses and, lacey osteoid production.
Answer 5: This lesion has characteristics indicative of Ewing Sarcoma which would be treated with neoadjuvant chemotherapy, wide surgical resection and adjuvant chemotherapy. Also, histology would demonstrate small round blue cells.
