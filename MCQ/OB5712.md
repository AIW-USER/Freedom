## Question

A 65 year-old man known to have ankylosing spondylitis slipped on ice and sustained a fall from standing height. Upon arrival to the hospital, the patient complains of neck pain. On physical examination, no neurological deficits are identified. Several hours later the patient develops progressive lower extremity weakness. Figure A is an MRI of the patient’s cervical spine. Management of this patient’s condition should consist of the following:

![[q5712-a-5712.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Immobilization with hard-collar and observation
2. Dexamethasone followed by repeat MRI
3. Halo-vest immobilization
4. Anterior instrumented fusion
5. Posterior decompression and instrumented fusion

## Correct option

**5. Posterior decompression and instrumented fusion**

## Answer explanation

This patient with ankylosing spondylolitis (AS) has a C7/T1 fracture and associated posterior epidural hematoma. Urgent decompression and spinal stabilization are required and can be performed from a posterior approach.

Those with AS have an increased risk for epidural hematomas with spinal fractures. Although the pathophysiology that leads to increased rates of epidural hemtomas in this population is not fully understood, it has been postulated that the unstable fracture patterns observed, coupled with ligamentous ossification, promotes tearing of the epidural vessels. Epidural hematomas with associated neurological deficits necessitate urgent surgical decompression. Long stabilization constructs are required and are often achieved with posterior instrumented spinal fusions.

*Mathews and Bolesta reported on the neurological outcomes of 11 patients with AS and spinal fractures.* Six patients were treated with posterior spinal fusion. Three of these patients deteriorated neurologically (2 deteriorated prior to surgery). Five patients were treated non-operatively, with one patient reported to deteriorate neurologically. **The authors concluded** that individualized treatment is required in this population.

*Whang et al. described a cohort of patients with AS or diffuse idiopathic skeletal hyperostosis (DISH) and spinal fractures.* *For the 12 patients with AS, 41.2% of patients were classified as ASIA A, and 83.3% of all patients with AS underwent surgery.* Overall, there were four deaths, all occurring in patients treated with halo-vest immobilization.

Figure A is a T2-weighted cervical spine MRI of a patient with AS that demonstrates a C7/T1 fracture (white arrow) and associated posterior epidural hematoma (black arrow).

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1 & 2: These options do not provide appropriate management of the fracture or the evolving neurological deficit due to the epidural hematoma.
Answer 3: Halo-vest stabilization in this population is associated with increased mortality and this option does not address the epidural hematoma.
Answer 4: Again this option does not address the epidural hematoma. Although fracture stabilization can be achieved by an anterior approach, posterior approaches are more common in this population for the reasons stated above.
