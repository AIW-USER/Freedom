## Question

A 21-year-old man who reports prior left knee pain recently felt a pop in his knee and now is not able to ambulate. Examination reveals a well-developed, well-nourished man with some stiffness around the knee. He has some fullness in the lateral femoral condylar area and tenderness to palpation on the lateral side. There is no adenopathy. Radiographs are seen in Figures 91a and 91b. At the time of surgery, open biopsy specimens are seen in Figures 91c and 91d. What is the most appropriate management?

## Options

1. Irradiation
2. Methylmethacrylate injection
3. Chemotherapy followed by wide resection
4. Amputation above the level of the lesion
5. Lateral condylar resection and allograft reconstruction

## Correct option

**5. Lateral condylar resection and allograft reconstruction**

## Answer explanation

The patient has a giant cell tumor of the lateral condyle with a pathologic fracture. The best option would be resection of the lateral condyle and osteoarticular allograft reconstruction. There is collapse of the subchondral bone in the giant cell tumor, making curretting and simple cementation difficult. Methylmethacrylate injection for giant cell tumor is never indicated. Although giant cell tumors can be treated with irradiation, surgery when possible is a better option. Amputation is almost never indicated for giant cell tumor of bone. Chemotherapy is not indicated for giant cell tumor of bone.
