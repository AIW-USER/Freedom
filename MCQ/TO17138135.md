## Question

These are the radiographs of the lower extremity of a newborn girl. The child otherwise has a normal neurologic examination, no sacral dimple/sinus. The hips have full abductor spread and negative hip telescopy. What is the most appropriate treatment currently?

![[TO17138135-1.png]]
![[TO17138135-2.png]]

## Options

1. Casting
2. Observation
3. Counseling and early osteotomy
4. Bracing

## Correct option

**2. Observation**

## Answer explanation

The radiographs show a child with posteromedial bowing of the tibia. This is a congenital anomaly that is produces the appearance of a calcaneovalgus foot deformity.

It is important to differentiate this condition of a positional calcaneovalgus deformity which has its apex of deformity at the ankle joint and is secondary to intra-uterine positioning.

Posteromedial tibial bowing demonstrates an apex of deformity at the distal leg and it readily apparent on radiographs. It is a relatively benign condition.

The residual limb-length discrepancy presents the greatest challenge for orthopedic management. This, however, can usually be handled with limb-lengthening techniques. Casting can be used for severe cases with unresolving significant contracture; however, gradual spontaneous correction is usually the norm.

This condition is quite different from anterior lateral bowing that can be associated with neurofibromatosis and pathologic fracture or pseudoarthrosis of the tibia which should be prevented by early bracing. Fractures are uncommon in posteromedial bowing.

Answer: 2
