## Question

A 22-year-old male presents with recurrent right shoulder instability following a traumatic shoulder injury one year ago during a collegiate football game. You decide to perform an open labral repair and an inferior capsular shift. After the subscapularis is released from the inferior capsule while performing the inferior capsular shift, your assistant readjusts the medial retractor to provide countertraction on the subscapularis, but you realize afterward that the retractor was placing pressure on the inferomedial aspect of the subscapularis muscle belly. The procedure goes as planned, and postoperatively, the patient can perform elbow flexion and forearm supination; however, he is unable to abduct his shoulder. Which nerve and nerve roots are properly paired and responsible for this postoperative finding?

## Options

1. Musculocutaneous nerve; C5-7
2. Axillary nerve; C6-8
3. Axillary nerve; C5-6
4. Radial nerve; C5-T1
5. Lower subscapular nerve; C5-7

## Correct option

**3. Axillary nerve; C5-6**

## Answer explanation

This patient had an iatrogenic injury to his axillary nerve (C5-6) during an open labral repair and inferior capsular shift procedure (Answer 3).

Traumatic anterior shoulder instability typically results from an anterior force directed at the shoulder while in an abducted and externally rotated position. Diagnosis is made clinically with anterior shoulder instability during provocative tests and with MRI typically revealing an anterior labral tear, anterior glenoid defect, and/or a humeral head Hill-Sachs lesion. Surgical treatment for this condition depends on patient factors, severity of instability, and risk of recurrence. An open Bankart repair with an inferior capsular release is one technique described for patients with <20-25% glenoid bone loss and hyperlaxity on exam. This technique is typically performed through the deltopectoral approach. The axillary nerve runs superficial to the subscapularis muscle belly medially before running inferior and posterior to wrap around the humerus. Therefore, during the dissection of the subscapularis from the inferior capsule, the axillary nerve is at risk of iatrogenic injury.

*Strother et al. published a technique guide on anterior labral repair with an inferior capsular shift.* **They reported** that this procedure is best performed in select patients without significant glenoid bone loss undergoing revision stabilization. They performed this technique through the deltopectoral interval and highlighted the importance of careful dissection of the subscapularis from the anterior capsule due to the risk of axillary nerve injury. **They reported** that the advantages of this technique include adequate control of anchor placement with the additional stability of a lateral and superiorly directed capsular shift. **They conclude** that this technique is reliable, efficient, and reproducible in cases of anterior shoulder instability without significant glenoid bone loss.

*Sahin et al. reviewed multidirectional shoulder instability.* **They note** this condition is characterized by a redundant glenohumeral capsule with a resultant increase in joint volume. Successful results with rehabilitation and conservative management are well accepted. In patients who have persistent instability, surgical intervention is typically pursued. **They concluded** that historically, an open inferior capsular shift has demonstrated good results; however, more recently, arthroscopic capsular plication techniques have reported similar results to open procedures.

Incorrect answer choices:
Answer 1: The Musculocutaneous nerve (C5-7) can be injured during medial retraction of the conjoint tendon during a deltopectoral approach; however, the patient was able to perform elbow flexion and forearm supination, which would not be expected if this type of injury occurred.
Answer 2: Axillary nerve injury is suspected in this case; however, the nerve roots that form the upper trunk and ultimately the axillary nerve are C5 and C6.
Answer 4: Although the radial nerve is nearby, injuries are rare during a deltopectoral approach, given that this nerve travels more distally.
Answer 5: The lower subscapular nerve innervates the subscapularis and can be injured during the release of the subscapularis muscle if careful consideration is not taken and the muscle belly is severely damaged. With careful dissection of the subscapularis from the capsule, this is less likely to occur.
