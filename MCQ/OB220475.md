## Question

A 27-year-old male patient arrives in the trauma bay with a closed subtrochanteric femur fracture following a motorcycle crash. Radiographs are taken, showing a displaced fracture with significant angulation. What is the typical deformity of the proximal fragment?

## Options

1. Flexion, adduction, external rotation
2. Flexion, abduction, external rotation
3. Flexion, adduction, internal rotation
4. Flexion, abduction, internal rotation
5. Extension, abduction, internal rotation

## Correct option

**2. Flexion, abduction, external rotation**

## Answer explanation

The typical deformity of the proximal fragment in a subtrochanteric femur fracture is flexion, abduction, and external rotation (Answer 2).

Subtrochanteric femur fractures, defined as occurring within 5cm distal to the lesser trochanter, are generally categorized into two types: typical fractures, which are usually caused by high-energy mechanisms in younger patients, and atypical fractures, which are usually associated with bisphosphonate use in older patients. Regardless of the type of fracture, knowing the deforming forces about the proximal and distal fragments is crucial for obtaining the reduction. For the proximal fragment, the iliopsoas flexes; the gluteus minimus and medius abduct; and the short external rotators and gluteus maximus externally rotate. For the distal fragment, the adductor compartment adducts and shortens.

*Haidukewych and Berry published a 2004 CORR reviewing a large series of patients with nonunion or acute fixation failure of subtrochanteric femur fractures treated with a variety of techniques to obtain bony union, with the goal of learning more about the results, success rate, and complications of revision surgery.* Of note, pathologic and periprosthetic fractures were excluded. Techniques used for revision fixation included cephalomedullary nail, standard antegrade femoral nail, 95° angled blade plate, sliding hip screw, 95° dynamic condylar screw, and dual large fragment plates. Of the 21 nonunions evaluated, 20 (95%) healed. **The authors concluded** that revision internal fixation and selected bone grafting for failed treatment of subtrochanteric femur fractures led to a high rate of fracture union and clinical and functional improvement in most patients, with few associated medical and orthopaedic complications.

*Afsari et al. published a 2010 surgical technique in JBJS, detailing a clamp-assisted technique for fixation of subtrochanteric femur fractures.* *The authors acknowledge the deforming forces around the proximal fragment, which can make nail fixation difficult, particularly because it is often necessary to use nail-starting-point trajectories that are quite awkward.* The fragment is extended, adducted, and internally rotated with the clamp, facilitating an easier starting point for the nail. **The authors further state** that they apply this technique to the vast majority of subtrochanteric fractures in order to improve fracture reduction, particularly in the sagittal plane, and occasionally they place a cerclage cable before passing the nail, especially with long spiral fractures.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1, 3, 4, and 5: None of these are the typical deformities associated with a subtrochanteric femur fracture.
