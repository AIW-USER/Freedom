## Options

1. Figure B is associated with a lower rate of angular malunion in patients older than 11
2. Figure B is associated with a lower rate of hardware insertion site knee pain
3. Figure C is associated with a higher rate of shortening in length unstable fractures
4. Figure C is associated with a higher rate of femoral head avascular necrosis
5. Figure C is associated with a higher rate of complications in patients over 49kg

## Correct option

**4. Figure C is associated with a higher rate of femoral head avascular necrosis**

## Answer explanation

Rigid nailing in patients with open growth plates is associated with a higher risk of femoral head avascular necrosis when compared to flexible retrograde intramedullary nailing.

Antegrade rigid intramedullary nailing of femoral shaft fractures is indicated in patients older than age 11, over 49kg, and with length unstable fractures. Rigid antegrade nails have the benefit of providing a stiff construct to control length, rotation, and alignment while allowing for early mobilization. These benefits must be weighed against the risk of femoral head avsacular necrosis due to damage to the medial femoral circumflex artery near the piriformis fossa. Greater trochanter entry nails help lower the risk of avascular necrosis but do have a risk of greater trochanteric physeal arrest and subsequent proximal femoral valgus.

*Hosalkar et al. reviewed intramedullary nailing of pediatric femoral shaft fractures.* **They state** that to avoid osteonecrosis of the femoral head in skeletally immature patients rigid intramedullary nailing should start at the tip of the greater trochanter rather than the piriformis fossa.

*Flynn et al. reviewed management options for pediatric femoral shaft fractures across all ages.* **They reported** cases of femoral head osteonecrosis even when the proper lateral technique was used.

Figure A shows a midshaft femur fracture in a skeletally immature patient. Figure B shows retrograde flexible intramedullary nailing of a midshaft femur fracture. Figure C shows a greater trochanter entry intramedullary nail. Illustration A shows the blood supply to the femoral head, with the posterior based MFCA.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Flexible nailing is associated with higher rates of angular malunion in patients over 11 years of age.
Answer 2: Flexible nailing has a higher rate of insertion site knee pain.
Answer 3: Antegrade rigid intramedullary nailing allows for increased stability with interlocking screws to control length and rotation even in significantly unstable fractures.
Answer 5: Antegrade rigid intramedullary nailing has a lower risk of complications in patients >11 years of age and >49kg.
