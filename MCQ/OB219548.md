## Question

A 16-year-old female is being treated for Lenke type 2 adolescent idiopathic scoliosis. She is scheduled to undergo instrumented posterior spinal fusion. When considering the lowest instrumented vertebra in this patient, which of the following is true as it relates to the touched vertebra?

## Options

1. Fusion extending to or caudad to the touched vertebrae is associated with ideal lowest instrumented vertebrae (LIV) position
2. Fusion cephalad to touched vertebrae is associated with improved Cobb angle
3. Fusion caudad to the touched vertebrae is associated with truncal shift
4. Fusion extending cephalad to the touched vertebrae is associated with improved thoracic kyphosis
5. Fusion cephalad or caudad to the touched vertebrae results in similar radiographic and patient-reported outcomes

## Correct option

**1. Fusion extending to or caudad to the touched vertebrae is associated with ideal lowest instrumented vertebrae (LIV) position**

## Answer explanation

Patients who received a fusion extending to or caudad to the touched vertebra (TV) tend to experience better correction and outcomes, whereas those with fusions cephalad to the TV sustain more truncal shift and a higher likelihood of long-term unsatisfactory outcomes (Answer 1)

Adolescent idiopathic scoliosis (AIS) is a coronal plane spinal deformity that most commonly presents in teenage girls from ages 10 to 18. Diagnosis is made with full-length standing PA and lateral spine radiographs. Treatment can be observation, bracing, or surgical management, depending on the skeletal maturity of the patient, the magnitude of deformity, and the likelihood of curve progression. The last touched vertebra is defined as the last cephalad vertebra touched by the central sacral vertical line (Illustration A).

*Lenke et al. developed a new classification system for AIS with three components: curve type, a lumbar spine modifier, and a sagittal thoracic modifier.* **They reported** that this new two-dimensional classification of AIS, as evaluated by two groups of surgeons, was much more reliable than the King system. **They concluded**, however, that additional studies are necessary to determine the versatility, reliability, and accuracy of this new classification for defining the vertebrae to be included in an arthrodesis.

*Beauchamp et al. reviewed the lowest instrumented vertebra (LIV) in patients with AIS.* *They developed a simple and reproducible method for selection of the LIV in patients with Lenke type-1 (main thoracic) and 2 (double thoracic) curves.* *They investigated its effectiveness in producing optimal positioning of the LIV.* *Their study had a minimum follow-up of five years, and* **they noted** that in 86.6% of patients, the LIV was selected at or immediately adjacent to the TV. **They concluded** that choosing the TV as the LIV in patients with Lenke type-1 and 2 curves provides acceptable positioning of the LIV at long-term follow-up.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Fusions extending caudad to the touched vertebra are recommended, as studies have demonstrated that this strategy better corrects radiographic parameters.
Answers 3-5: Patients who received a fusion extending caudad to the TV did well, but those who were fused cephalad to the TV sustained more truncal shift and a higher likelihood of poor long-term outcomes.
