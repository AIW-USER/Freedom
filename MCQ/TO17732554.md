## Question

True about Allen test is (regional orthopedics- hand and wrist - miscellaneous)

## Options

1. A negative Allen test means that the patient does not have a patent dual blood supply to the hand
2. A positive Allen test is a negative indication for radial artery catheterization
3. In the Allen test both radial and ulnar arteries are compressed simultaneously and then released one by one.
4. In modified Allen test radial and ulnar arteries are compressed one by one to check the patency of hand collaterals.

## Correct option

**1. A negative Allen test means that the patient does not have a patent dual blood supply to the hand**

## Answer explanation

Ans: A

 EXPLANATION:

 Allen test: Allen test was devised by Dr. Allen in 1929 to assess the patency of collateral circulation of the hand. Radial artery catheterization or cannulation carries a risk of thrombosis, so before being cannulated, Allen test should be performed to know the adequacy of hand collaterals.

 A positive Allen test means that the patient has an adequate dual blood supply to the hand, which would be an indication for catheterization, removal of the radial artery, or any procedure which may result in occlusion of the vessel.

 Allen test: Original test (as proposed by Allen)

 - Both fists are simultaneously tightly clenched for 1 minute.

 - Both radial arteries are simultaneously compressed so as to occlude them.

 - The patient is then asked to open both hands rapidly. The color should change quickly from pale to pink.

 - The test may be repeated with ulnar arteries.

 Allen's test looks for abnormal circulation. If color returns quickly as described above, Allen's test is considered positive and demonstrates normal circulation. If the pallor persists for some time after the patient opens their fingers, this suggests a degree of occlusion of the uncompressed artery.

 Modified Allen’s Test (MAT)

 The MAT differs from the original Allen test, which primarily assesses the radial or ulnar arteries one hand at a time.

 In the modified Allen test, one hand is examined at a time:

 - The hand is raised and the fist is clenched for 30 seconds.

 - Both radial and ulnar arteries are compressed with thumb pressure.

 - The hand is kept elevated and the fist is opened. The palm and fingers will appear blanched.

 - Now, ulnar pressure is released and color should change from pale to pink within 6 seconds.

 If color returns to normal pink within 6 seconds, Allen test is considered normal or positive. If the hand remains blanched, it indicates that ulnar artery supply to the hand is not sufficient. This is a negative indication (negative test) for radial artery cannulation.

 So, In Allen test, a single artery in both hands is tested, while in modified Allen test, both arteries in one hand are tested.
