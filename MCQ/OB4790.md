## Question

A 14-year-old girl is kicked in the thigh during a soccer game. Six months later, she noticed that her left thigh appeared larger than the right. She had been actively playing soccer without limitation during this period and does not complain of pain. Examination reveals left thigh circumference to be 2cm greater than the right. There is neutral limb alignment, there is no limitation in knee motion, and she walks without a limp. CT scans are shown in Figures A and B. ## Options

1. Biopsy to exclude a malignant lesion
2. Standing long-leg radiographs to evaluate for mechanical axis deviation
3. Staging long-leg radiographs to evaluate for skip lesions
4. MRI to evaluate for the extent of intramedullary disease and extramedullary compartment involvement
5. Observation

## Correct option

**5. Observation**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a sessile exostosis on the anterior aspect of her left femur. This may either be a solitary exostosis, or may arise in the setting of hereditary multiple osteochondral exostosis (HMOCE). Also, the patient is close to skeletal maturity. The only treatment necessary at this time is observation.

HMOCE is an autosomal dominant disorder with multiple cartilage-capped exostosis. Mutations of the EXT1, 2 and 3 genes are responsible, leading to dysregulation of chondrocyte proliferation/maturation, resulting in abnormal bone growth. Asymptomatic lesions may be treated with observation alone.

*Stieber et al. review manifestations of HMOCE.* The commonest deformities include short stature, limb-length discrepancies, valgus knee and ankle, asymmetric pelvic and pectoral girdles, and radial bowing with ulnar deviation of the wrist.

*Douis et al. reviewed imaging of benign cartilage tumors.* **They recommend** further imaging (CT/MRI) for lesions arising from flat bones. Using the criterion cartilage cap thickness > 2 cm to differentiate osteochondromas from chondrosarcomas had 100% sensitivity, 98% specificity on MRI and 100% sensitivity, 95% specificity on CT.

Figures A and B are sagittal-reformatted and axial cut CT scan images showing a sessile exostosis off the anterior cortex of the left distal femoral shaft.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Biopsy is only indicated if there is persistent growth after skeletal maturity to exclude sarcomatous transformation. These patients usually present with an expanding, painful mass.
Answer 2: Femoral anteversion and coxa valga is associated with lesions around the lesser trochanter, while genu valgus deformities are associated with distal femur/proximal tibia lesions. This patient demonstrates neutral limb alignment and normal gait. Long leg films are not indicated at this time.
Answer 3: Staging is unnecessary as this is a benign lesion.
Answer 4: MRI is unnecessary as this is a benign process. If there is persistent growth after skeletal maturity, MRI may be performed to exclude sarcomatous transformation. Cartilage cap thickness >2cm is associated with increased chance of malignancy.
