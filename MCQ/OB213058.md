## Question

A 9-year-old girl presents with a rapidly growing mass on her left arm. She has no tenderness to palpation of the mass but is noted to have prominent axillary lymph nodes. ## Options

1. t(12;16)
2. GNAS
3. t(2;13)
4. t(11;22)
5. IDH1

## Correct option

**3. t(2;13)**

## Answer explanation

The patient has alveolar rhabdomyosarcoma, which is associated with a t(2;13) translocation.

Alveolar rhabdomyosarcoma is a malignant tumor of the primitive mesenchyme that occurs in adolescents and young adults. It is associated with a t(2;13) translocation. Patients typically present with a rapidly growing painless mass. Nodal metastasis are known to occur with rhabdomyosarcoma, so sentinel lymph node biopsy should be considered as part of treatment. On MRI, alveolar rhabdomyosarcoma appears like many soft tissue tumors, demonstrating hypointensity on T1-weighted images and hyperintensity on T2-weighted images. Histologically, it is characterized by cellular aggregates of poorly differentiated round cells and multinucleated giant cells that are surrounded by dense fibrous septae.

*Andreou et al. performed a study on the role of sentinel lymph node biopsy (SLNB) in soft tissue sarcoma subtypes with a high propensity for regional lymphatic spread (clear cell sarcoma [CCS], synovial sarcoma [SS], epithelioid sarcoma [ES], and rhabdomyosarcoma [RMS]).* **They found** that SLNB is an important diagnostic tool for patients with CCS, who appear to have a high rate of clinically occult regional lymph node metastases at diagnosis. For SS patients, SLNB appeared to be of very little relevance.

*Dall'Igna et al. performed a study on SLNB in pediatric patients.* **They found** that SLNB was a feasible and reliable technique that allowed a targeted sampling of regional lymph nodes. **They reported** that, although recently new imaging techniques (such as PET-CT scan) have obtained good results in identifying distant and lymphatic metastases, they have been found to be less reliable than SLNB, especially for small volume metastatic nodal disease in sarcoma.

*Marshall et al. published a review on the molecular drivers of alveolar rhabdomyosarcoma.* **They reported** that the majority of ARMS tumors carry one of several characteristic chromosomal translocations, such as t(2;13)(q35;q14), which results in the expression of a PAX3-FOXO1 fusion transcription factor.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: A t(12;16) translocation is associated with myxoid liposarcoma.
Answer 2: A GNAS mutation is associated with fibrous dysplasia.
Answer 4: A t(11;22) translocation is associated with Ewing sarcoma.
Answer 5: An IDH1 mutation is associated with Mafucci syndrome. Patients with Mafucci syndrome demonstrate multiple enchondromas in the bones and multiple soft-tissue hemangiomas with phleboliths in the soft tissues.
