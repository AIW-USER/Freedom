## Question

All are true regarding Synovial Sarcoma Except [ONCOLOGY-Malignant Tumor-Soft Tissue Sarcoma]

## Options

1. The tumor is most commonly located around the knee
2. It’s a biphasic tumor with synovial and epithelial differentiation
3. Its seen mostly in young adults and adoloscents
4. Trl (X;18) may be present in > 90% of the affected individuals

## Correct option

**2. It’s a biphasic tumor with synovial and epithelial differentiation**

## Answer explanation

Ans: B

Synovial cell sarcoma is a soft tissue tumor that does not have a synovial origin despite its name. it is a rare but aggressive (malignant) tumor that arises in the vicinity of a bursa, tendon sheaths or joint capsules where there are multipotent stem cell rests that differentiate into mesenchymal as well as epithelial structures, hence a “biphasic tumor”. It however, lacks a synovial differentiation.

TYPES

Three subtypes

- Biphasic: Contains epithelial and spindle cells in varying proportions

- Monophasic: Contains only spindle cells

- Poorly differentiated: Has some morphological features of above types but has varying proportions of poorly differentiated areas with high degree of nuclear pleomorphism and atypia.

ETIOLOGY

Almost all morphological subtypes are characterized by trl (X;18) which fuses two genes, SSX (1,2 or 4) located on chromosome X with SYT (now called SS18) located on Chromosome 18. The translocation leads to generation of SYT-SSX fusion oncoprotein whereby SYT acts as activator of transcription while SSX proteins remove transcriptional repressors thereby inducing signal transduction pathways that provide malignant potential to cells. Tumors with SYT-SSX1 fusion protein often exhibit Biphasic pattern classically.

INCIDENCE

Comprises 7% of all soft tissue sarcomas.

It is also the most common soft tissue sarcoma of the foot.

It is the most common non-rhabdomyosarcoma soft tissue sarcoma in paediatric population.

CLINICAL FEATURES

Occurs mostly in adolescents and young adults with a median age of 35 years. There is slight male preponderance.

Although any site can be involved, the tumor is most commonly located around the knee.

Presenting as a swelling in vicinity of joint the tumor often grows to a large mass (> 4cms) within no time (see Fig). The swelling is often painful and located deep to deep fascia.

RADIOLOGY

On Xray (Fig), tumor generally appears as a heterogenous well defined septate cystic mass. One third of the cases may exhibit calcification that is very well picked up by CT scan.

![[TO17744234-2.png]]

Fig: X ray of Synovial cell sarcoma

On MRI, it appears cystic, sharply marginated and multi-loculated with varying degrees of internal septations. Due to well defined nature it is many a times mis diagnosed as being benign in nature.

PET scan is used to detect metastasis and may also be employed to detect recurrence of disease. It may also be helpful in guiding metabolically active areas to take biopsy.

DIFFERENTIALS

Other soft tissue sarcomas viz. Leiomyosarcoma, Fibrosarcoma and Malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumors need to be ruled out. Genetic analysis and histology serve as reliant methods for the same.

GENETICS

Almost all (>90%) morphological subtypes are characterized by trl (X;18) that serves as a very good indicator for diagnosis.

Role of BCL-2 protein is under investigation for the use in diagnosis.

CD 56, CD 99 and High molecular weight keratins may be used as immunohistochemical markers for the poorly differentiated types.

CLASSIFICATION

All synovial cell sarcomas are considered high grade. However, there is lack of a specific staging system, so the FNCLCC grading system for the soft tisse sarcomas continues to be used as the alternative.

MANAGEMENT

For localized tumor, standard approach is wide surgical excision (>4cms beyond tumor margin) combined with radiotherapy and/or (neo)adjuvant chemotherapy as appropriate. Radiotherapy is given as adjuvant in all cases with tumor size > 5cms in either of the following ways- External beam therapy, Brachytherapy (radioactive source placed inside the body) or Intensity modulated radiation therapy (IMRT) that delivers different radiation amounts to different body areas.

Amputation is considered in fungating masses, pathological fractures difficult to treat or in cases of infection.

Surgery has limited role in metastatic disease. Compared to other soft tissue sarcomas as a group, Synovial cell Sarcoma is more sensitive to certain chemotherapeutic agents, so palliative chemotherapy forms mainstay in metastatic disease. First line includes combination chemotherapy with doxorubicin and ifosfamide that is reported to significantly improve the clinical outcome.

Pazopanib is the only ‘targeted drug’ approved for the treatment of soft tissue sarcomas and is employed in advanced disease. Pazopanib is an oral, tyrosine kinase inhibitor directed against the receptor tyrosine kinases (RTKs), vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGFR), platelet-derived growth factor (PDGFR), and KIT, thereby blocking tumor growth and inhibiting angiogenesis

Another drug employed in advanced disease includes Trabectedin. It is thought to inhibit the biological activity of the chimeric oncoprotein by blocking DNA binding of the oncogenic transcription factor thereby reversing the transcriptional program.

Epigenetic modifiers are also being studied. The EZH2 inhibitor Tazemetostat is currently the most studied epigenetic modulating drug. In integrase interactor 1 (INI1; aka SMARCB1) negative tumors, INI1 loss allows EZH2 to become an oncogenic driver in tumor cells that is blocked by the drug.

Immunotherapy, is the latest in line where the use of engineered T cells directed against the NY-ESO-1 cancer/testis antigen, which is expressed in 80% of synovial cell sarcoma patients, is being tested in clinical testing.

Isolated Limb Perfusion (ILP), the last in line is a medical technique that is used to deliver drugs locally/ directly to the site of interest. Hyperthermic isolated limb perfusion with tumor necrosis factor-α, Melphalan or Doxorubicin has been successfully used to treat limb soft tissue sarcomas (including Synovial cell sarcoma stage ¾ AJCC) with high response rates (limb preservation rate of 72–96%). The technique relied on the synergistic effects of cytostatic drugs and hyperthermia.

PROGNOSTIC FACTORS

Although the role of molecular genetics (SSX-SSY) remains to be characterized, various other factors have been implicated for a bad prognosis viz. age > 50 years, tumor size (> 5cms), high grade (poor histological differentiation) and positive surgical margins.

The most important factor remains to be Distant metastasis. In about 50% of cases, metastases occur. In most cases, metastases are localized in the lung (80%), although metastases can arise in lymph nodes (up to 20%), bone (9.9%), and liver (4.5%) as well

The prognosis of primary non metastasized sarcomas is related to the age of the patient, with a much better relative survival in children compared to older patients, may be due to more genomic instability with increasing age.

The 5-year overall survival (OS) for all synovial cell sarcomas is around 60%, being largely age-dependent as already mentioned.
