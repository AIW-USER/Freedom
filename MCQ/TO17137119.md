Chapter: Trauma MCQs — Fracture Of Distal End Radius

## Question

An otherwise healthy 35-year-old woman reports dorsal wrist pain and has trouble extending her thumb after sustaining a minimally displaced fracture of the distal radius 3 months ago. What is the most appropriate next step in management?

## Options

1. Neurophysiologic test to evaluate the posterior interosseous nerve
2. Transfer of the extensor indicis proprius to the extensor pollicis longus tendon
3. Fine-cut CT of the distal radius to evaluate Lister tubercle
4. Extension splinting of the thumb

## Correct option

**2. Transfer of the extensor indicis proprius to the extensor pollicis longus tendon**

## Answer explanation

Explanation: Extensor pollicis longus tendon rupture can occur after a fracture of the distal radius, even a minimally displaced one. Poor vascularity of the tendon within the third dorsal compartment is the suspected etiology, not the displaced fracture fragments. Tendon transfer will suitably restore active extension of the thumb interphalangeal joint.

Answer: 2
