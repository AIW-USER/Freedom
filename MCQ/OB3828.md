## Question

Which of the following conditions is most commonly associated with attenuation or tearing of the superomedial calcaneonavicular ligament?

## Options

1. Lisfranc injury
2. Adult-acquired flatfoot deformity
3. Peroneal tendon dislocation
4. Pes cavus deformity
5. Second toe crossover deformity

## Correct option

**2. Adult-acquired flatfoot deformity**

## Answer explanation

Adult-acquired flatfoot deformity is commonly associated with superomedial calcaneonavicular ligament (CNL) degeneration or injury.

The spring ligament complex is composed of the inferior calcaneonavicular and superomedial calcaneonavicular (CNL) ligaments. Together with the posterior tibialis tendon (PTT), the plantar fascia, and the plantar ligaments, they function as an important stabilizer for the longitudinal arch of the foot. Ligament complex failure can occur after failure of the posterior tibial tendon (PTT) in adult-acquired flatfoot deformities. Failure of these structures will result in plantar and medial migration of the talar head and flattening of the arch of the foot.

*Pomeroy et al. looked at acquired flatfoot in adults due to dysfunction of the posterior tibial tendon.* *He describes the progressive nature of acquired flatfoot that occurs following the onset of dysfunction of the posterior tibial tendon in an adults.* Treatment is based on the structures involved and whether the deformity is flexible or fixed.

*Gazdag et al. assessed the involvement of the spring ligament in adult-acquired flatfoot deformities.* **He showed** that eighteen of twenty-two patients who were having a tendon transfer to treat rupture of the posterior tibial tendon had evidence of injury to the spring ligament. The superomedial CNL was most commonly involved.

Illustration A shows a medial view of a cadaveric foot to show the relationship of posterior tibial tendon (PTT) and superomedial calcaneonavicular ligament (CNL). The superomedial CNL (black arrowheads) originates from the sustentaculum tali (straight arrows) and courses superiorly, with a broad attachment at the superomedial aspect of the navicular bone. On top of the superomedial CNL lies a gliding layer (*) of the distal PTT.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Lisfranc injury is associated with rupture of the Lisfranc ligament. The Lisfranc ligament originates from the plantar lateral aspect of the medial cuneiform and attaches to the plantar medial aspect of the second metatarsal base.
Answer 3: Peroneal tendon dislocation is associated superior peroneal retinaculum degeneration or tearing.
Answer 4: Pes cavus is largely a muscle imbalance disorder, including hindfoot inversion, talar dorsiflexion, medial midfoot plantar flexion and inversion, forefoot plantar flexion and adduction.
Answer 5: Second toe crossover deformity is associated with plantar plate and the collateral ligament dysfunction at the 2nd metatarsophalangeal joint.
