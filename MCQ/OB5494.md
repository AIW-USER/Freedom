## Question

A 36-year-old female presents with worsening left foot pain. Physical examination reveals a palpable, firm, non-mobile mass. MRI and biopsy results are shown in Figures A and B. What cytogenetic abnormality would confirm this suspected diagnosis?

![[q5494-a-figure-a.jpg]]
*Figure A*

![[q5494-b-figure-b.jpg]]
*Figure B*

## Options

1. 11:22
2. 12:16
3. X:18
4. 2:13
5. 12:22

## Correct option

**3. X:18**

## Answer explanation

The history, mri, and histology specimen are most consistent with synovial sarcoma. The translocation in synovial sarcoma is X:18.

Synovial sarcoma is an aggressive soft tissue sarcoma and can appear heterogeneous on MRI. On histology it can appear in either a monophasic or biphasic pattern, but with high-grade pleomorphism and mitotic figures all within a bed of fibrous stroma. Biphasic histology would exhibit a combination of longer spindle cells as well more plump, glandular epithelial cells.

*Sandberg et al. provides a current update on the diagnosis of synovial sarcoma, compiling the oncologic literature.* X:18 is still the final cytogenetic test found that best correlates with most synovial sarcomas, even amongst rare variants.

Figure A exhibits a T2 MRI of a heterogenous multiloculated, aggressive mass. Figure B exhibits a biphasic pattern in fibrous stroma with pleomorphic cells in a spindle pattern.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: 11:22 is associated with Ewing's sarcoma
Answer 2: 12:16 is associated with myxoid liposarcoma
Answer 4: 2:13 is associated with rhabdomyosarcoma
Answer 5: 12:22 is associated with clear cell sarcoma
