## Question

A 9-month-old boy is referred for evaluation of bilateral 'rocker bottom' feet. Figure A exhibits a lateral foot radiograph. This diagnosis has a high association with which of the following congenital anomalies?

![[q5243-a-figure-a.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Cleft lip/palate
2. Neuromuscular disease
3. Congenital heart disease
4. Deletion on chromosome 22q11
5. Duplication on chromosome 12

## Correct option

**2. Neuromuscular disease**

## Answer explanation

Congenital vertical talus (CVT) has a high association with neuromuscular diseases, such as myelodysplasia, arthrogryposis and cerebral palsy.

CVT is diagnosed not only with a rigid hindfoot, but on a radiograph that exhibits a vertical talus with a dorsal dislocation of the navicular.

*Merrill et al. reviewed 61 patients (101 feet) with CVT and reported 22/61 (36%) had associated neuromuscular conditions, which included myelodysplasia, cerebral palsy and arthrogryposis.* *Forty of the 61 patients had bilateral CVT, and of the remaining 21 patients, nearly 50% had a contralateral club foot.*

Figure A exhibits a lateral foot radiograph with CVT and characteristic dorsal dislocation of the navicular. Illustration A exhibits the maximum plantar flexion view, which exhibits a fixed dislocation and fixed CVT.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1,3,5: While cleft lip/palate, congenital heart disease and duplication on chromosome 12 have been reported as an association with CVT, they do not occur as frequently as neuromuscular disorders.
Answer 4: A deletion on chromosome 22q11 has not been associated with CVT.
