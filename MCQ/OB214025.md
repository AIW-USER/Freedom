## Question

The enthesis, or bone-tendon junction, contains which of the following?

## Options

1. Golgi organs
2. Free nerve endings
3. Pacini corpuscles
4. Meissner corpuscles
5. Ruffini endings

## Correct option

**2. Free nerve endings**

## Answer explanation

Free nerve endings, which are responsible for nociception, tend to be clustered at the enthesis of a tendon.

The insertion of a ligament or tendon on to bone is termed the enthesis. This area contains the free nerve endings primarily responsible for nociception. Other organs and mechanoreceptors are found at the myotendinous junction including the Golgi organ, Pacini corpuscles and Ruffini endings. The Golgi organ provides steady state information when undergoing large stimulation for a prolonged period of time. Similarly, Ruffini endings transmit signals for a prolonged period of time, but are more sensitive than golgi organs. Pacini corpuscles on the other hand are sensitive, fast-acting mechanoreceptors, but are also found at the myotendinous region.

*Chiodo et al. review the anatomy and biomechanics of tendons around the ankle.* *They report on specific enthesis anatomy including fibrous and fibrocartilaginous insertions and inclusion of free nerve endings in this area.* **They conclude** that understanding the biomechanics and local anatomy of tendons is an essential part of treating disorders of the foot and ankle.

*Lavagnino et al. review tendon mechanobiology and discuss the future research opportunities in this area of study.* **They report** that tendons are not isolated tissues, but rather have transitions into muscle at the myotendinous junction and to bone at the enthesis, which differ in mechanobiology. **They conclude** that connective tissue cells coordinate multiple interactive mechanisms of mechanical signal transduction which may lead to new therapeutic approaches in the future.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1,3,& 5: Golgi organs, Pacini corpuscles, and Ruffini endings are all found at the myotendinous junction.
Answer 4: Meissner corpuscles are located in the skin and responsible for sensitivity to light touch.
