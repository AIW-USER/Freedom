## Question

Patient A and Patient B are identical 48-year-old females who present with large masses in the left thigh. Fluorescence in situ hybridization analysis demonstrates the presence of an SS18-SSX fusion protein in both tumors. While both patients are scheduled to undergo wide resection, Patient A is treated with preoperative radiation, while Patient B is treated with postoperative radiation. Which of the following best describes the differences expected between these two patients’ outcomes?

## Options

1. Patient A is less likely to develop post-radiation fibrosis but more likely to develop lymphedema.
2. Patient A is more likely to have a wound-healing complication but is less likely to develop fibrosis.
3. Patient B is at a higher risk for post-radiation fracture but is at a lower risk for post-radiation fibrosis.
4. Patient B is more likely to develop lymphedema and a wound-healing complication.
5. Patient B is more likely to develop lymphedema but less likely to develop a wound-healing complication.

## Correct option

**2. Patient A is more likely to have a wound-healing complication but is less likely to develop fibrosis.**

## Answer explanation

Treatment of synovial sarcoma, a subtype of soft tissue sarcoma, is wide resection and radiotherapy. Preoperative radiation is associated with a lower risk of pathologic fracture, lymphedema, and fibrosis, but a higher risk of wound-healing complications when compared with postoperative radiation in patients with soft tissue sarcoma (Answer 2).

The treatment of soft tissue sarcomas consists of wide resection and radiotherapy for local control. Radiation may be administered before wide resection (neoadjuvant) or after wide resection (adjuvant). Broadly, preoperative radiation is typically over a smaller field and involves a smaller dose compared to postoperative radiation. The lower exposure to radiation is thought to contribute to the lower risk of lymphedema, fibrosis, pathologic fracture, and joint stiffness observed in patients who undergo neoadjuvant radiotherapy compared to adjuvant radiotherapy. Conversely, the utilization of preoperative radiotherapy predisposes patients to a higher risk of wound healing complications due to the direct damage to the surgical bed’s surrounding tissues.

*O’Sullivan et al. published a randomized controlled trial assessing wound complications in patients with soft tissue sarcomas who received either preoperative radiotherapy or postoperative radiotherapy.* *Patients who had preoperative radiotherapy had more wound complications compared to those who received postoperative radiotherapy.* Logistic regression analysis additionally revealed that anatomical site, preoperative radiotherapy, and specimen size were significantly associated with the development of wound complications. **The authors concluded** that the decision to perform preoperative or postoperative radiotherapy must take into consideration the differing sizes of radiation doses and the greater potential for wound healing complications with preoperative treatment.

*Davis et al. performed a multicenter randomized trial to compare the impact of preoperative and postoperative radiotherapy on morbidity and function in patients with soft tissue sarcoma.* *One hundred and ninety total patients were enrolled in this study.* **The authors found** that patients with postoperative radiotherapy had greater fibrosis and edema, but equivalent functional outcomes when compared to those who received only preoperative radiotherapy. **They concluded** that the smaller radiation field size utilized with preoperative radiotherapy may place patients at a lower risk for radiation-associated sequelae such as subcutaneous fibrosis and joint stiffness.

*Zagars et al. described the outcomes of preoperative and postoperative radiotherapy in 517 patients with soft tissue sarcoma.* **They found** that postoperative radiation, which was administered at a higher intensity, was associated with a higher incidence of radiation-related complications over a 10-year follow-up period. No difference in local control or disease-specific survival was observed. **They concluded** that though there were no differences in disease outcomes between the two groups, a higher rate of long-term complications can be expected with postoperative radiation, likely attributable to the higher dose used.

Incorrect Answers
Answers 1, 3, 4, and 5 do not reflect the increased risk of wound complications and decreased risk of lymphedema, fibrosis, and pathologic fracture associated with preoperative radiation.
