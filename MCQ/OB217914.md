## Question

A 15-year-old male who plays linebacker for his JV high school football team is preparing for his first game of the season. During the first half of game, he is involved in a collision that causes him to stumble to the sideline. He did not have a loss of consciousness but does complain of a headache. He has no focal neurologic deficits. Which of the following is true with regards to management of his injury and return to play?

## Options

1. Aerobic activity should be delayed until at least 1 week post injury
2. Each stage of recovery progression should last at least 48 hours without return of symptoms
3. History of attention deficit/hyperactivity disorder is a risk factor for prolonged recovery
4. Return to play is allowed in the second half if his headache resolves completely
5. Subacute headache and depression symptoms correlate with prolonged recovery

## Correct option

**5. Subacute headache and depression symptoms correlate with prolonged recovery**

## Answer explanation

This high school football player has sustained a concussion; research has shown that athletes with persistent subacute headaches and symptoms of depression take longer to return to pre-injury level of function.

Concussions represent a mild, traumatic brain injury (TBI) that is characterized by a complex combination of metabolic and pathophysiologic events, which can lead to focal or systemic symptoms. Athletic trainers and team physicians play a prominent role in the expedient diagnosis and treatment of concussions. Sideline testing is used in combination with other neurologic testing in order to obtain an initial examination. These tests can be further monitored at subsequent office visits. Further imaging should be obtained in cases associated with "red flag," symptoms. Return to play is somewhat controversial, but several protocols exist to encourage symptom-free return to play as safely as possible. Athletes should progress symptom free through each stage of the concussion protocol for at least 24-hours. Early, aerobic activity is encouraged as this is thought to speed recovery.

*Leddy et al. performed a randomized controlled trial evaluating the effect of early subthreshold aerobic exercise for concussion management.* **They noted** that early aerobic exercise (avg 4.9 days after concussion) decreased the incidence of delayed concussion recovery compared to a stretching only group. **They concluded** that inclusion of early aerobic activity has an important role in concussion return to play protocols.

*Harmon et al. published a position statement on concussion in sport which reviewed the pathophysiology, evaluation, diagnosis and management of concussions.* *They review different sideline tests,* **noting** that they each provide unique value and can be used to follow concussion patients in the office after injury. They discuss return to play protocols and risk factors for prolonged return to play, including persistent headache and symptoms of depression.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Aerobic activity as soon as several days after injury should be encouraged, as this may encourage recovery.
Answer 2: Athletes should spend at least 24 hours in each stage of the concussion protocol with symptom-free progression.
Answer 3: History of attention deficit/hyperactivity disorder is no longer thought to represent a significant risk factor for continued symptoms and prolonged recovery.
Answer 4: Return to play in the same game in which the concussion occur should not be allowed under any circumstance, as this increased the risk for second impact syndrome.
