## Answer explanation

Pelvic incidence is defined as the summation of pelvic tilt and sacral slope.

Pelvic incidence is a fixed measurement. It can be measured by drawing a line perpendicular to the center of the S1 superior endplate and a line from the center of the S1 superior endplate to the bicoxo-femoral axis. The angle between these lines represents pelvic incidence (see Figure C). Ideally, lumbar lordosis should equal pelvic incidence +/- 10 degrees.

*Lafage et al. developed two novel formulas to predict postoperative spinopelvic alignment is adult patients undergoing pedicle subtraction osteotomies.* **They found** that this model successfully predicted unsuccessful outcomes but was less reliable in predicting successful outcomes when applied retrospectively to a series of ninety-nine patients.

*Glassman et al. investigated the correlation of radiographic parameters and clinical symptoms in adult scoliosis patients.* **They found** that positive sagittal balance was most correlated with patient-reported outcomes. **They conclude** that the primary objective in adult scoliosis should focus on restoring sagittal balance over coronal alignment.

*Schwab et al. performed a prospective study evaluating radiographic parameters and disability in adult spinal deformity patients.* **They found** several sagittal radiographic parameters correlating with severe disability, establishing a threshold as follows: Pelvic tilt >/22 degrees, sagittal vertical axis >/47 mm, and (pelvic incidence) – (lumbar lordosis) >/11 degrees.

Figure A shows the sacral slope angle, Figure B shows the pelvic tilt angle, Figure C shows the pelvic incidence angle, Figure D shows the sagittal vertical axis, and Figure E shows the lumbar lordosis angle. Illustration A demonstrates several commonly used sagittal spinal parameters.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 2, 3, 4, 5: These measurements do not correlate with pelvic incidence when added.
