## Question

A 63-year-old female with right foot hallux rigidus that was treated with a 1st MTP hemiarthroplasty three years ago presents with worsening right hallux pain. Her pain is worse with weight-bearing and walking. Wearing stiff-soled shoes has not improved her symptoms. She denies any fevers or chills. On examination, there is a well-healed incision with no drainage or surrounding erythema. Pain is reproduced with flexion and extension of the 1st MTP joint. Current radiographs are depicted in figures A and B. What is the most reliable treatment for this patient?

## Options

1. In situ arthrodesis
2. Keller procedure
3. Bone-block arthrodesis
4. Moberg procedure
5. Conversion to a silicone arthroplasty

## Correct option

**3. Bone-block arthrodesis**

## Answer explanation

The patient has failure of a previous 1st metatarsophalangeal (MTP) hemiarthroplasty with significant bone loss. A bone-block arthrodesis would most reliably address the patient's symptoms.

Hallux rigidus is the degeneration of the 1st MTP joint that results in stiffness and pain. Early treatment involves Morton's extension orthotic and shoe modifications. Surgical treatment can include arthrodesis, cheilectomy, or arthroplasty. Hemiarthroplasty has been reported to have good outcomes, but prosthetic loosening with bone loss may occur. When there is significant bone loss associated with a failed arthroplasty a bone-block arthrodesis is preferred to reconstitute the ray length. On the contrary, if the 1st MTP joint was fused in situ it would result in a shortened 1st ray leading to an increased risk of transfer metatarsalgia. Silicone arthroplasty is associated with a significant inflammatory reaction that can cause osteolysis, wound necrosis, and infections.

*Coughlin and Shurnas performed a retrospective review of 114 patients treated for hallux rigidus.* **They reported** hallux valgus was associated with hallux valgus interphalangeus, bilateral involvement in those with familial history, unilateral involvement in those with a history of trauma, and female gender.

*Perez-Aznar et al. prospectively evaluated the outcomes of dorsal-wedge phalangeal osteotomy for the treatment of grade II and III hallux rigidus in 40 patients.* **The authors reported** the mean AOFAS score to improve from 51.7 to 88.8, mean VAS scores to improve from 7.6 to 1.9, and mean passive dorsiflexion improved from 20.3° to 55.7°. **They concluded** dorsal-wedge phalangeal osteotomy is an effective treatment for hallux rigidus.

*Coughlin and Shurnas performed a long-term follow-up study of 114 patients treated for hallux rigidus.* **They reported** an overall 97% good to excellent subjective results at a mean follow-up of 9.6 years for cheilectomies and 6.7 years for arthrodeses with 92% of patients undergoing cheilectomy have good pain relief and functional outcomes. **They concluded** cheilectomy was effective for treating grade 1-2 and select grade 3 disease, whereas grade 4 and grade 3 hallux rigidus with <50% of the metatarsal head cartilage intact are best treated with arthrodesis.

*Raikin and Ahmad compared the outcomes of arthrodesis or metallic hemiarthroplasty for the treatment of hallux rigidus in 46 patients.* **They reported** a 24% failure rate and a 38% plantar stem cut out in the hemiarthroplasty group whereas the arthrodesis group achieved a 100% fusion rate without any need for revisions. **The authors concluded** arthroplasty is more reliable for alleviating pain symptoms associated with hallux rigidus with a lower failure and revision rate.

Figures A and B are the AP and lateral radiographs of the right foot with a 1st MTP hemiarthroplasty and evidence of osteolysis of the 1st metatarsal and proximal phalanx. There is associated varus collapse.

Incorrect answers
Answer 1: An in situ arthrodesis would lead to a significant shortening of the 1st ray.
Answer 2: A Keller procedure, or a 1st MTP joint resection, would lead to gross instability of the 1st MTP joint. This is generally reserved for low demand patients.
Answer 4: A Moberg procedure, or a phalangeal dorsiflexion osteotomy, is performed in runners with decreased dorsiflexion from hallux rigidus, or those who have failed a cheilectomy.
Answer 5: Silicone arthroplasty is associated with significant osteolysis, wound complications, and infections. This would not be an ideal choice in the setting of bone loss from a previous failed arthroplasty.
