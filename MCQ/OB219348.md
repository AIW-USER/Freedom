## Question

## Options

1. It generally occurs in patients at a much younger age
2. It demonstrates an overall higher prevalence among the population
3. It is more likely to present as a low-grade tumor
4. Radiation therapy is a component of standard treatment
5. It has significantly higher overall 5-year survival rates

## Correct option

**4. Radiation therapy is a component of standard treatment**

## Answer explanation

Extraskeletal osteosarcoma (ESOS) presents and behaves similarly to that of soft tissue sarcomas rather than conventional osteosarcoma. As such, localized disease is treated with wide resection and radiation therapy (Answer 4).

ESOS represents less than 5% of all osteosarcomas and less than 1% of all soft tissue sarcomas. Compared to conventional osteosarcoma of bone, ESOS tends to occur in older populations and is associated with notably worse overall survival. Further, chemotherapeutic agents have not been reliably shown to improve long-term survival like in conventional osteosarcoma of bone. However, pre- or post-operative radiation has been shown to decrease local recurrence rates. For these reasons, ESOS is generally considered a soft tissue sarcoma and is treated similarly.

*Choi and colleagues performed a retrospective review of their institutional cohort of 53 patients with ESOS.* **They reported** that most (51/53) patients had high-grade tumors, high rates of local recurrence, and overall poor prognosis (~40% deceased at 3 years). **The authors conclude** that in their case series, improved survival was associated with superficial tumors and negative margins at the time of resection.

*Heng and colleagues performed the largest multi-institutional retrospective review of ESOS, involving 370 patients.* **They reported** a five-year survival rate of 56% with no significant difference in patients who received chemotherapy, radiation therapy, both, or neither. However, **they found** that radiation therapy resulted in a significant decrease in local recurrence rates (p=0.01). **The authors conclude** that radiation therapy demonstrates a benefit to patients with locally resectable ESOS, however, chemotherapy does not appear to have a role in treatment.

Figures A and B demonstrate T2-weighted MRI imaging with a hyperintense, heterogeneous mass located within the posteromedial thigh soft tissues (i.e. no bone involvement), consistent with a soft tissue sarcoma. Figure C demonstrates a biopsy with malignant pleomorphic tumor cells and predominant osteoid production, consistent with osteosarcoma.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Primary osteosarcoma of bone has a peak incidence in the second decade of life (late adolescent years) compared with the fifth or sixth decade of life for ESOS.
Answer 2: ESOS represents less than 5% of all osteosarcomas, with the remaining 95% being primary osteosarcomas of bone.
Answer 3: As opposed to low-grade variants of osteosarcoma of bone (i.e. parosteal osteosarcoma), ESOS is nearly always a high-grade lesion.
Answer 5: The 5-year survival rate for primary osteosarcoma of bone is ~75% overall whereas ESOS is ~50% overall.
