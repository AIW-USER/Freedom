## Question

With regard to Kinesiology taping, which of the following statement is true

## Options

1. Tonus decreasing application is done from muscle origin to insertion
2. Different colors of the tape indicate differences in stretch and hold capacity
3. Neutral application involves stretching the tissue and not the tape
4. It can stretch 40-60% of its original length

## Correct option

**3. Neutral application involves stretching the tissue and not the tape**

## Answer explanation

Ans: C

EXPLANATION: -

“ Stretchability of K tape is 40-60% on average but could vary up to 120-150% depending on manufacture. So choice C is best pick ’’

The practice of applying tape over joints (classic taping) for the purpose of immobilization or adding stability has been a long known concept. The main advantage offered by taping over traditional immobilization methods has been the fact that joints remain mobile after taping.

Kinesiology tape or K-Tape (named after word “kinesis” that’s movement) is a “stretchy” cotton/ synthetic tape designed to mimic the elasticity of the skin and the extension capacity of human muscles. It was originally invented by Japanese chiropractor Kenzo Kase in 1970s as an alternative to traditional available rigid tapes.

The stretching properties of the K tape improve lymph and blood circulation, improve proprioceptive feedback from joints and provide pain relief and supports to joints, muscles and tendons. The tape is stretched (has potential to stretch 40-60% of original length) and applied on skin over trigger points. As the tape contracts, it lifts skin subcutaneous tissues off the muscle fascia generating space in the region. This improves the circulation and especially the flow of lymphatics removing the inflammatory factors from muscle vascular/ lymphatic beds. This helps in reducing pain and swelling, improves muscle function as well as proprioception.

![[TO21733143-1.png]]

Once applied the tape can be left in place for 3-5 days. The durability is because of the special acrylic adhesive that is waterproof (rubbing during bathing should be avoided though). During its manufacture, the K tape is applied with a slight stretch (10%) to the backing paper. Despite this pre stretching the application is referred to as unstretched. This stretch (pre stretch) should be retained during application of the tape. Although a light covering of hair is not an obstacle, trimming of hairs is advisable. Taping should be avoided or done with caution if a patient is on anticoagulants as there remains a risk of developing small skin haemorrhages.

Methods of application:

1. Neutral technique – Stretch applied on the tissue but not the tape
2. Stretch technique – Stretch applied on the tape and not the tissue

Application shapes:

![[TO21733143-2.png]]

Techniques of application: There are four basic techniques of application viz. Muscle application, Ligament application, Corrective application and Lymphatic applications.

- Muscle Application: This can increase or decrease muscle tone to bring therapeutic effect. Tape is applied here with mostly 10% stretch.

1. Tonus decreasing application – from insertion to origin
2. Tonus increasing application – from origin to insertion.

- Ligament Application: This is used for chronic tendinopathies, ligament overload injuries and trigger points. Here the tape is fixed with maximum tape stretch to both osseous insertion points of the ligament/ tendon involved. It is thought to lead to receptor stimulation.

- Corrective applications: There are meant for functional corrections like patellar maltracking. In most cases Y tapes are used with maximum pre-stretching to bring about a position change in the osseous structures.

- Lymphatic applications: Here I-tapes with around 25% pre stretch are used to cause skin lifting thereby improving lymphatic circulation in disorders of lymphatic drainage.

Myths of taping: -

1. Taping increases performance: False belief. It is for treatment of diseases scenarios. Hence the method is not prohibited under anti-doping guidelines unless the tapes are coated with some banned medicinal substances. K taping therapy in fact does not require any pharmaceutically active ingredients which is again an advantage.
2. Different colors have different effects: The tapes are mostly available in four colors: cyan, magenta, beige and black. There is no difference in properties. The colors have been chosen to support treatment based on color theory, for instance, color red is regarded as stimulating while blue as calming.
3. Patients cannot heal without tape.
