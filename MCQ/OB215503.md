## Question

Which of the following is the most useful test to discriminate an atypical lipomatous tumor/well-differentiated liposarcoma from a lipoma?

## Options

1. MRI
2. Immunohistochemical staining for S-100
3. Immunohistochemical staining for CD138
4. Fluoresence in situ hybridization for MDM2 amplification
5. Immunohistochemical staining for CD20

## Correct option

**4. Fluoresence in situ hybridization for MDM2 amplification**

## Answer explanation

Fluorescence in situ hybridization is a molecular test that can be useful in detecting the MDM2 gene amplification that is often expressed in atypical lipomatous tumors/well-differentiated liposarcomas while absent in lipomas.

The MDM2 gene (Murine double minute 2) functions as a negative regulator for the p53 tumor suppressor gene. In atypical lipomatous tumors/well-differentiated liposarcomas, amplification of the MDM2 gene suggests the presence of this diagnosis over a lipoma. The use of MRI as a differentiating study is not as sensitive as molecular testing.

*Weaver et al. performed a cytogenic analysis with fluorescence in situ hybridization of MDM2 gene amplification in lipomatous tumors.* **They reported** 100% of well-differentiated and undifferentiated liposarcomas to express MDM2 gene amplification, 89% of spindle cell lipomas, 40% of pleomorphic liposarcomas, and 0% of the lipomas and angiolipomas. **The authors concluded** MDM2 amplification probe to be a valuable diagnostic tool in well-differentiated lipomatous neoplasms.

*Brisson et al. performed a retrospective histologic and MRI study of 87 patients with lipomatous tumors.* **They reported** a 90.9% sensitivity rate of detecting an atypical lipomatous tumor/liposarcoma using MRI with patients over 60 years, maximal length of lesion >10 cm, location in the lower limb, and presence of non-fatty areas being associated with atypical/sarcomatous lesions. **They concluded** the most reliable image characteristics are the lesion size and fatty content, but a definitive diagnosis should be made with molecular pathology.

Incorrect answers
Answer 1: MRI alone is not a sensitive test to differentiate a benign and malignant lesion.
Answer 2: Immunohistochemicalcal staining for S-100 is useful for identifying malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumors, melanoma, and, in some instances, synovial sarcoma.
Answer 3: Immunohistochemical staining for CD138 is useful for identifying multiple myeloma.
Answer 5: Immunohistochemical staining for CD20 is useful for identifying lymphoma.
