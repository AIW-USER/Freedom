## Question

An 8-year-old boy undergoes a transtibial amputation as a result of injuries sustained in a shark attack. He follows up 6 months later, complaining of pain in the distal residual limb. On examination, he has tenderness and skin irritation over the distal end of the tibia. Radiographs in the clinic show a tapering spike of bone extending distally from the residual limb. What is the most likely cause of this complication, and what is the best management?

## Options

1. Heterotopic ossification; initiate NSAIDs and consider excision once mature
2. Symptomatic neuroma formation; perform revision with targeted muscle reinnervation
3. Bony remodeling of the distal residual limb due to prosthetic pressure; observation and reassurance
4. Osteomyelitis sequelae; debridement and antibitiotics
5. Continued longitudinal growth from the tibial diaphysis; revise to more proximal amputation with capping of bone or Ertl

## Correct option

**5. Continued longitudinal growth from the tibial diaphysis; revise to more proximal amputation with capping of bone or Ertl**

## Answer explanation

Terminal bone overgrowth is a known complication of pediatric amputations. This is the most likely diagnosis in this scenario, given the tapering spike of bone extending distally. Treatment involves revision of the amputation with bone end capping or conversion to an Ertl-type amputation (Answer 5).

Pediatric amputations present with several unique challenges, the most common being osseous overgrowth. Terminal bony overgrowth can occur in up to 50% of transosseous amputations. This overgrowth can originate from periosteal, endosteal, or heterotopic sources, creating bony overgrowth that develops into a cone shape with a sharp spike. The result of this complication is increased morbidity secondary to skin ulceration, pain, challenges with prosthesis, and reoperations. Osteochondral capping is the most widely accepted management for terminal osseous overgrowth in children. Capping the distal diaphysis post-amputation with an osteocartilaginous cap prevents recurrent overgrowth and provides a predictable growth pattern that allows for eventual weight-bearing.

*McQuerry et al. published a retrospective review of 96 children undergoing various levels of lower extremity amputations to evaluate how amputation level affects quality of life, perceived function, and prosthetic satisfaction.* *The authors mention limiting diaphyseal-level (transfemoral & transtibial) amputations due to the historically high rate of osseous overgrowth, creating limitations in their study.* However, **the authors report** that transtibial and Syme amputees demonstrated better mobility and higher functional scores than those with transfemoral amputations.

*Le et al. published a review article on pediatric amputations and limb differences, covering congenital limb differences, amputations, and rehabilitation goals.* Regarding terminal overgrowth, **the authors note** that the overgrowth is a result of appositional bone growth on the end of the residual limb. This can cause pain, bursitis, and potentially skin breakdown in patients. **The authors conclude** by recommending a holistic approach to these challenging problems in patients, aiming to maximize function, participation, and quality of life for children with limb differences or amputations.

*Louer et al. published a review of key principles in pediatric amputation surgery.* *The authors discuss appropriate amputation levels to optimize prosthetic function while preserving limb length and growth potential.* *This review emphasized the use of autologous osteochondral grafts in attempts to mitigate terminal overgrowth, with a success rate of 90%, particularly in patients under 12 years of age.* **The authors conclude** that recommending ongoing surveillance, coordinated multidisciplinary care, and psychosocial support maximizes long-term functional outcomes and quality of life.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Heterotopic ossification (HO) can occur after an amputation. However, HO bone formation is not typically a tapered, continuous distal extension of the tibial diaphysis.
Answer 2: Neuromas can produce tenderness and a Tinel sign at the end of the nerve. However, this does not explain the radiographic bony overgrowth.
Answer 3: Prosthetic pressure can cause tenderness and skin irritation over the distal residual limb. However, a cortical spike at the distal extent of the bone is more consistent with terminal overgrowth.
Answer 4: Osteomyelitis typically does not cause bony overgrowth, as described in this patient's radiographs.
