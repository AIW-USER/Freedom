## Answer explanation

This patient has suprascapular neuropathy due to a compressive spinoglenoid cyst, which is frequently associated with SLAP tears in this patient population (Answer 4).

Suprascapular neuropathy is due to compression of the suprascapular nerve, which most commonly occurs at the suprascapular notch or spinoglenoid notch. While there are various etiologies, a compressive cyst is frequently associated with a SLAP tear, or less frequently a posterior labral tear. The labral tear creates a one-way valve resulting in the accumulation of synovial fluid outside the glenohumeral joint. Physical exam findings vary depending on where the compressive lesion is located. Lesions at the suprascapular notch result in weakness of the supraspinatus and infraspinatus, as opposed to lesions involving the spinoglenoid notch which result in only infraspinatus weakness. MRI is useful in identifying the location of the cyst as well as any associated labral tears. Nonoperative management is the first line of treatment for most patients, particularly when there is no identifiable lesion on MRI. Surgical management is reserved for patients with structural lesions (i.e. cyst) on MRI and those who have failed an extensive course of nonoperative treatment.

*Chen et al. reviewed three patients with suprascapular neuropathy due to spinoglenoid notch cyst associated with a SLAP tear who underwent arthroscopic excision of the cyst and labral repair.* **They demonstrated** the return of suprascapular nerve function using EMG studies in all cases.

*Martin et al. retrospectively reviewed 15 patients who underwent nonoperative treatment for suprascapular neuropathy with an average follow-up of almost 4 years.* Twelve patients had good or excellent outcomes. Meanwhile, three required surgery for continued symptoms, of which two had good-excellent outcomes and one had poor results.

*Piatt et al. reviewed 73 patients with a spinoglenoid notch cyst of which 65 (89%) had a posterior superior labral tear (i.e. SLAP tear).* *They were separated into four groups based on treatment including (1) nonoperative, (2) aspiration, (3) isolated labral repair, and (4) combined labral repair and excision of the cyst.* **They reported** that 53% were satisfied in Group I, 64% in Group II, 67% in Group III, and 97% in Group IV.
