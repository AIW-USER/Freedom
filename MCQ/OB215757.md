## Question

A 42-year-old female presents with a right foot deformity that has afflicted her since she was a teenager. After many years of conservative treatment, she opts to undergo surgical treatment due to worsening ankle pain and lateral foot overloading. Her current clinical photographs and radiographs are depicted in Figures A and B. There is painless motion of the 1st MTP and IP joints. When discussing surgical treatment for her for deformity, what surgical treatment would you recommend to address her hallux deformity?

## Options

1. 1st MTP fusion and FHL tenotomy
2. FHL tenotomy
3. 1st MTP and IP fusion
4. 1st IP fusion and EHL transfer
5. Keller resection arthroplasty

## Correct option

**4. 1st IP fusion and EHL transfer**

## Answer explanation

The patient is presenting with a cavovarus foot deformity with a clawed hallux. A 1st IP fusion with EHL transfer, or a modified Jones transfer, would appropriately address the deformity.

Clawed hallux deformity presents with a hyperextended 1st MTP joint and is associated with cavovarus foot deformities. A persistent deformity results in increased loading pressure of the plantar skin over the 1st MTP joint leading to ulcerations, metatarsalgia, and hallux catching. Surgical treatment consists of modified Jones transfer and FHL transfer. The Jones transfer consists of hallux IP fusion and transfer of the EHL to the 1st metatarsal neck.

*Elias et al. performed a cadaveric study of clawed hallux deformity treated with either a Jones procedure or FHL transfer.* **They reported** no significant differences in angular deformity correction and plantar pressure reduction. **They concluded** both procedures are effective in treating clawed hallux deformity, but FHL transfer is not dependent on fusion of the hallux IP joint.

*Breusch et al. performed a cross-sectional study of 51 patients (81 feet) with clawed hallux with a cavus foot treated with a modified Robert Jones tendon transfer.* **They reported** an 86% patient satisfaction rate and 80/81 deformities successfully corrected. **They concluded** the deforming force of the EHL was eliminated with the Jones transfer but at the cost of altered 1st MTP joint mechanics.

Figure A is a clinical photograph depicting a cavovarus foot deformity with a clawed hallux. Figure B is a lateral foot radiograph depicting a cavovarus foot deformity with a clawed hallux without significant arthritic changes.

Incorrect answers
Answer 1 and 2: An FHL tenotomy would not correct the deformity due to the persistent deforming force of the EHL.
Answer 3: A 1st MTP and IP fusion would lead to stiff 1st ray making ambulation difficult.
Answer 5: A Keller resection arthroplasty involves removal of the 1st metatarsal head and the base of the proximal phalanx and is used to treat hallux rigidus in elderly, low-demand patients.
