## Question

All are true regarding PRP except.

## Options

1. Growth factors released from PRP have effect that is NOT pH dependant
2. Prepared from autologous blood (description of process was there)
3. Alfa granules release growth factors
4. Leukocyte rich and poor forms are available

## Correct option

**1. Growth factors released from PRP have effect that is NOT pH dependant**

## Answer explanation

Ans: A

EXPLANATION: -

Platelets contain a milieu of growth factors and mediators in their alpha granules and Platelet Rich Plasma (PRP) offers the opportunity to take advantage of their regenerative potential.
A study done by Liu et al (https://doi.org/10.1046/j.1524-475x.2002.10510) showed that fibroblast proliferation due to exposure to a platelet concentrate in vitro is pH dependent with platelet derived growth factor (PDGF) in highest concentration at pH 5.1 whereas however transforming growth factor-beta was lower after incubation at pH 5.0 than at either pH 7.1 or 7.6.

PREPARATION OF PRP – Around 30 ml whole blood is usually collected and mixed with an anticoagulant factor, such as acid citrate dextrose, sodium citrate, or ethylene diamine tetra-acetic acid. Centrifugation (soft spin 1500 rpm for 5 min followed by hard spin 6000 rpm for 15min) is then required to separate red blood cells (RBCs) from platelet-poor plasma (PPP) and the “buffy coat,” which contains the concentrated platelets and ± leukocytes. The RBC and PPP layers may be discarded using various processing techniques to isolate the platelet-concentrated layer. The platelets can then be directly injected into the patient or be “activated” via the addition of either calcium chloride or thrombin, which then causes the platelets to degranulate and release the growth and differentiation factors. Approximately 70% of the stored growth factors are released within the first 10 minutes of activation, and nearly 100% of the growth factors are released within 1 hour of activation. Small amounts of growth factors may continue to be produced by the platelet during the remainder of its life span (8 to 10 days).
For most indications, up to three injections of PRP, 3-6 weeks apart are advocated and a volume of 5-10 ml may be injected depending on case scenario.
PRP preparations are categorized into leukocyte-rich PRP (LR-PRP) preparations, defined as having a neutrophil concentration of 5000 cells/ml, and leukocyte-poor PRP (LP-PRP) preparations, defined as having a leukocyte (neutrophil) concentration of 100 cells/ml.
PRP with elevated leukocyte content (i.e., LR-PRP) is associated with an acute inflammatory response. Elevated leukocyte (neutrophil) concentrations present in LR-PRP are also associated with elevated catabolic cytokines (IL-1β, TNF-α,metalloproteinases,7,8) which may antagonize the anabolic cytokines contained within platelets.
INDICATIONS FOR PRP: -

Knee osteoarthritis
Partial tears of rotator cuff, hamstring tendon, quadriceps tendon, common extensor or flexor origin, triceps
Tendinosis -achilles, & other chronic tendinopathies
Meniscus –grade 1 or 2 tears with/ without parameniscal cyst
Fractures – non-union/ delayed union cases
Partial ligament tears – in ACL and ATFL tears
