## Question

A 9-year-old male sustained the injury shown in Figure A. Which of the following is true with respect to the treatment of this injury?

![[q212967-a-distal-femur-physeal.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Anterograde pin fixation has a higher risk of septic arthritis.
2. The size of the Thurston-Holland fragment determines treatment with pins or screws.
3. Interposed periosteum on the compression side of fracture may prevent closed reduction
4. Fully threaded screws are superior to partially threaded screws
5. This physeal injury type has the highest rate of physeal growth arrest

## Correct option

**2. The size of the Thurston-Holland fragment determines treatment with pins or screws.**

## Answer explanation

This patient has sustained a Salter-Harris (SH) type II distal femur fracture with a relatively large Thurston-Holland (TH) fragment. Screw fixation across the metaphysis is the recommended treatment for this injury. Small TH fragments that are not able to accommodate screws, can be fixed with trans-physeal smooth pins.

The most common SH fracture of the distal femur is type II injuries as seen in this case. Treatment is usually closed reduction and percutaneous fixation. In some cases, periosteum may be entrapped on the tension side of the injury and a small incision must be made to remove it prior to reduction. Additionally, the physis fails on the tension side while the metaphysis fails on the compression side. The small triangular metaphyseal fracture fragment is known as a Thurston-Holland (TH) fragment. If the TH fragment is large enough, fixation is performed with lag screws across the metaphyseal fracture line, avoiding the physis. In the cases of very small TH fragments, smooth K-wires across the physis.

*Arkader et al. performed a retrospective review to predict the outcome of physeal fractures of the distal femur.* **They found** that the overall complication rate was 40% and growth arrest was the most frequent complication. **They found** that there was a higher incidence of growth arrest among displaced fractures but that the amount and direction of displacement did not correlate with the outcome. **They also found** that a higher incidence of complications occurred when the physis was violated by hardware. **They conclude** that the SH classification and displacement were predictors of the final outcome.

*Mayer et al. performed a review on pediatric knee dislocations and physeal fractures about the knee.* **They report** that given the high incidence of vascular and neurologic injury associated with these injury patterns, a thorough understanding of clinical and radiographic signs is crucial. **They report** that the incidence of these injuries is increasing due to high-energy activities. A thorough clinical examination should include evaluation of motor and sensory status, a detailed vascular exam, and measurement of ankle-brachial indices. **They conclude** that the final treatment depends on fracture pattern and stability.

Figure A is an AP radiograph of the knee demonstrating a SH II distal femur physeal fracture with a large TH fragment. Illustration A is an AP fluoroscopic image demonstrating metaphyseal screw fixation.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: If pin fixation is to be used, retrograde pin fixation across the physis has a higher theoretical risk of septic arthritis since the pins are entering through the knee joint capsule (although recent studies have shown no increased risk of septic arthritis with either method of pin entry).
Answer 3: Interposed periosteum on the TENSION side of fracture may prevent closed reduction.
Answer 4: There is no difference between fully threaded and partially threaded screws as long as the screw is applied using correct lag screw technique.
Answer 5: The highest rate of physeal growth arrest occurs in SH IV fractures of the distal femur.
