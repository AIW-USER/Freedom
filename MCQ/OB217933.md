## Question

A 45-year-old rugby player presents to your office with left elbow pain. He states that he was lifting weights the day prior and felt a "pop," in his elbow. He has bruising in his antecubital fossa and weakness with resisted supination. His hook test in the office is positive. Accordingly, you schedule him for a distal biceps repair. Which of the following is true regarding injury to the lateral antebrachial cutaneous (LABCN) after distal biceps repair?

## Options

1. Highest risk after single-incision approach
2. Higher rate after use of suture anchor fixation
3. Injury usually caused by direct laceration
4. Occurs less commonly than PIN neuropraxia
5. Sensory deficit is normally permanent

## Correct option

**1. Highest risk after single-incision approach**

## Answer explanation

The risk for lateral antebrachial cutaneous nerve (LABCN) injury is higher in a single-incision approach compared to a double incision approach.

Distal biceps ruptures present primarily in middle-aged men who are lifting something heavy. The biceps fails with excessive eccentric tension and will rupture off of the biceps tuberosity on the radius. Surgical management to reattach the biceps is usually recommended as nonoperative management leads to loss of supination and, to a lesser degree, flexion strength. Both single- and double-incision approaches with different methods of fixation are utilized, all with different risks. The single-incision technique risks nerve injury to the LABCN most commonly and is thought to cause the neuropraxia secondary to aggressive, prolonged retraction. The double incision technique has a lower risk of nerve injury, but traditionally has been described as having higher rates of heterotopic ossification. While both approaches are acceptable, specific risks must be taken into consideration and discussed with patients before proceeding with surgery.

*Grewal et al. performed an RCT evaluating single vs. double-incision techniques for the repair of distal biceps ruptures.* **They found** no differences in functional outcomes at follow-up and a slight improvement in flexion strength with the double-incision technique. The single-incision technique was associated with a greater number of LABC neuropraxias (19) compared to the double-incision group (3).

*Cain et al. reviewed general complications in 198 distal biceps repairs.* **They note** that 36% had some kind of complication, resulting in reoperation in 6 patients. Minor complications included LABC neuropraxia (26%) and radial nerve neuropraxia (6%), while major complications included PIN palsy (4%), heterotopic ossification (3%) and re-rupture (2%).

*Banerjee et al. published their observations of high complication rates when using cortical buttons for distal biceps fixation.* **They note** 33% of patients in the study had complications, which included PIN palsy, superficial radial nerve palsy and heterotopic ossification. Patients who had complications had worse outcomes and supination strength post-operatively.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: There is no indication that suture anchor fixation increases the risk of LABCN injury compared to other methods of fixation.
Answer 3: It is believed that the LABCN is injured indirectly by prolonged retraction, rather than direct laceration.
Answer 4: LABCN injury is the most common neuropraxia seen after distal biceps repair, more common than PIN neuropraxia.
Answer 5: While sensory deficit can be permanent after LABC neuropraxia, most resolve within 3-6 months.
