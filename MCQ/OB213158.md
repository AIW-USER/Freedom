## Question

A 65-year-old woman presents to your clinic for evaluation of low back pain and evidence of severe spinal stenosis secondary to degenerative spondylolisthesis. Because you are a "bone doctor" she also brings up her recent bone mineral density (BMD) test result of a T-score of -2.7 and asks about medications that can improve her bone strength. You indicate her for multilevel decompression with posterolateral fusion and instrumentation to treat her back pain and recommend which of the following medications to help improve BMD and increase fusion rates in lumbar spine surgery?

## Options

1. Denosumab
2. Clodronate
3. Teriparatide
4. Alendronate
5. Calcitonin

## Correct option

**3. Teriparatide**

## Answer explanation

Teriparatide, a recombinant parathyroid hormone, has been shown to improve spinal fusion rates in postmenopausal women with osteoporosis.

Post-menopausal women with osteoporosis are at an increased risk of nonunion and pseudoarthrosis after spinal fusion surgery. Osteoporosis, characterized by a T-score of <-2.5, commonly occurs in postmenopausal women due to the decrease in estrogen production, resulting in a net increase in osteoclast function compared to osteoblasts. Osteoporosis is a quantitative decrease in bone resulting in decreased bone quality, while osteomalacia is a qualitative decrease in bone due to decreased mineralization. Common treatments for osteopenia and osteoporosis range from lifestyle modifications like exercise and vitamin D/calcium supplementation to prescribed medications such as bisphosphonates, teriparatide, calcitonin, denosumab, and estrogen replacement therapy.

*Both studies by Ohtori et al directly evaluated the use of teriparatide in improving spinal fusion rates and decreasing pedicle screw cut out in postmenopausal women.* *In the first cited prospective study they compared two cohorts of patients, one treated with teriparatide and the other with bisphosphonates, on postoperative pain, fusion rates, and time to fusion.* Although pain scores were not different between the two groups, **they found** the teriparatide group had significantly higher rates of bony fusion (82% versus 68% in the bisphosphonate group) and a faster time to union (8 months versus 10 months in the bisphosphonate group).

*The second cited study by the Ohtari et al group separated subjects (again all postmenopausal women with osteoporosis) into 3 groups looking at the rates of pedicle screw loosening after lumbar fusion: one treated with teriparatide perioperatively, one treated with bisphosphonates perioperatively, and one control group.* **They found** a 2-3X increase in rates of screw loosening in the bisphosphonate group and control group compared to those patients treated with teriparatide.

*Lehman et al completed a high-quality RCT comparing the effect of teriparatide, calcitonin, and control after an intertransverse process spinal fusion in a rabbit model.* *They performed a postmortem histologic analysis on the union sites and found 87% fusion rates in the teriparatide group, while there was a fusion rate of 56% in the calcitonin group and 50% in the control group.* Concluding that calcitonin had a neutral effect on spinal fusion and teriparatide had favorable effects on spinal fusion.

*Khalid et al performed a retrospective review on patients who had spinal fusion surgery with normal BMD compared to those with decreased BMD.* **They found** that rates of pseudoarthrosis and need for revision surgery were 2-3X higher in patients with diagnosed osteopenia and osteoporosis compared to those with normal BMD. Those patients with decreased BMD already on medications to improve BMD (teriparatide, bisphosphonates, and denosumab) trended toward improved rates of spinal fusion and decreased need for revision surgery, but they were not statistically significant.

INCORRECT ANSWERS:
Answer 1: Denosumab, a monoclonal antibody targeting RANKL, has not been shown to improve union rates in spinal fusion procedures when used preoperatively.
Answer 2: Clodronate, a non-nitrogen-containing bisphosphonate that works as an ATP analog to decrease osteoclast function, has not been shown to improve fusion rates in spinal procedures when used preoperatively.
Answer 4: Alendronate, a nitrogen-containing bisphosphonate that works by inhibiting the enzyme farnesyl diphosphate synthase, has not been shown to improve spinal fusion rates and decrease pseudoarthrosis when used in the perioperative period.
Answer 5: Calcitonin, a direct osteoclast inhibitor, has been shown to have similar spinal fusion rates in spine surgery in a rabbit model compared to control rabbits.
