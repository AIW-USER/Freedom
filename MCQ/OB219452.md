## Question

A resident is performing a direct anterior total hip arthroplasty with the supervision of their attending. During placement of the acetabular retractors, one of the retractors slips and damages a nearby neurovascular structure. At the two-week follow-up, the patient demonstrates diminished sensation over the dorsum of the foot and absent dorsiflexion on the operative leg. Based on Figure A, which retractor placement likely damaged the neurovascular structure?

![[q219452-a-acetabular-retractor.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. 1
2. 2
3. 3
4. 4
5. It is unlikely that retractor placement caused nerve injury.

## Correct option

**4. 4**

## Answer explanation

This patient presents with signs of a sciatic nerve injury. Aberrant positioning of a posterior acetabular retractor is the most likely cause of sciatic nerve injury during any approach to the hip (Answer 4).

Intraoperative nerve injury during total hip arthroplasty (THA) is a potential iatrogenic complication with an incidence of up to 4%. Limb lengthening, patient positioning, heat injury, retractor placement, and laceration can all cause nerve injury. The most common nerve injury with a direct anterior approach (DAA; Smith-Peterson approach) THA is lateral femoral cutaneous nerve neuropraxia, followed by the femoral nerve, and lastly the sciatic nerve. The sciatic nerve (although more at risk in posterior approaches) is placed at risk primarily during posterior acetabular retractor placement. Deep insertion of a trochanteric retractor can also damage the sciatic nerve as it courses directly posterior to the greater trochanter.

*Wang et al. performed an anatomic study of 263 hips using magnetic resonance imaging.* **Their study found** that the average distance from the sciatic nerve to the posterior acetabular rim was 1.96 cm in normal hips. **The authors found** that the distance was decreased in shorter or female sex patients. **The authors concluded** that aberrant posterior acetabular retractor placement poses a significant risk for sciatic nerve injury.

*Vajapey et al. published a review on nerve injuries in the direct anterior approach to THA.* **The authors report** that sciatic nerve injury is more common with the posterior approach but can occur with the anterior approach, typically due to retractor placement. Inappropriate limb manipulation (excessive traction, extension, external rotation) has also been associated with indirect sciatic nerve injury. **The authors conclude** that anatomic knowledge and technique are critical to avoid damage to the sciatic nerve during retractor placement.

Figure A demonstrates the possible retractor positions for acetabular exposure.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: A retractor at position 1 risks damaging the superior gluteal nerve. Although this risk is low in DAA, nerve injury can be seen with excessive retraction of the tensor fascia latae muscle belly.
Answer 2: A retractor at position 2 risks damaging the femoral nerve, which lies on average 2 cm from the anterior lip of the acetabulum.
Answer 3: A retractor at position 3 risks damaging the obturator nerve, which is rare in DAA THA.
Answer 5: Although sciatic nerve injury is less common in DAA THAs, sciatic nerve injury secondary to retractor placement is an identified complication.
