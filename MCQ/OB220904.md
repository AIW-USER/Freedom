## Question

A 65-year-old male presents with a large, rapidly growing, painless mass in his posterior thigh that is deep to the fascia. MRI reveals a large, heterogeneous, subfacial soft-tissue mass with mixed signal intensity and enhancement. A needle biopsy confirms a high-grade malignant spindle cell neoplasm with marked cellular pleomorphism and frequent, atypical mitotic figures. Immunohistochemical staining is performed and is negative for smooth muscle actin (SMA), desmin, S100 protein, and MDM2 amplification. Based on the clinical presentation and the immunohistochemical profile (absence of specific lineage markers), which of the following is the most likely diagnosis?

## Options

1. Leiomyosarcoma
2. Liposarcoma, well-differentiated
3. Malignant Peripheral Nerve Sheath Tumor (MPNST)
4. Synovial Sarcoma
5. Undifferentiated Pleomorphic Sarcoma (UPS)

## Correct option

**5. Undifferentiated Pleomorphic Sarcoma (UPS)**

## Answer explanation

UPS is a diagnosis of exclusion characterized by pleomorphic, high-grade malignant cells lacking specific immunohistochemical markers for other sarcoma types (answer 5).

Undifferentiated Pleomorphic Sarcoma (UPS), formerly known as Malignant Fibrous Histiocytoma (MFH), is the most common soft-tissue sarcoma of the extremity in mature adults. It is fundamentally a diagnosis of exclusion. The histological features—a highly aggressive, pleomorphic spindle cell neoplasm with frequent mitosis—are non-specific but malignant. Crucially, the absence of specific immunohistochemical staining (e.g., negative for S100, desmin, and MDM2) rules out major differential diagnoses like MPNST, Leiomyosarcoma, and many Liposarcomas, leaving UPS as the most likely classification for a sarcoma that cannot be differentiated into a specific lineage.

*Roland et al. evaluated clinical and molecular factors that impact oncologic outcomes in a large cohort of 208 patients with Undifferentiated Pleomorphic Sarcoma (UPS).* **They noted** that clinical factors, including smaller tumor size, younger age, and negative surgical margins, were strongly associated with improved disease-specific survival. **They concluded** that clinical parameters and specific molecular signatures are vital prognostic markers for UPS, guiding risk stratification and therapeutic development.

*Chen et al. conducted a long-term follow-up study on 100 patients with Undifferentiated Pleomorphic Sarcoma (UPS) to identify predictors of poor oncologic outcomes.* **They noted** that tumor size, tumor presentation (primary vs. recurrent), and the involvement of important surrounding structures were significant independent prognostic factors for overall survival. **They concluded** that primary treatment of UPS should be conducted by experts in a large sarcoma center to achieve a wide surgical margin for optimal local control.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: This diagnosis would be expected to show positive immunostaining for muscle differentiation markers like desmin or smooth muscle actin (SMA).
Answer 2: This tumor is characterized by MDM2 gene amplification and the presence of atypical lipoblasts, and is typically a lower grade than the described pleomorphic neoplasm.
Answer 3: MPNST typically demonstrates neural differentiation, often showing positivity for the S100 protein on immunostaining.
Answer 4: While a high-grade sarcoma, Synovial Sarcoma is usually characterized by a specific genetic translocation, t(X;18), and often expresses EMA, which are not characteristic findings here.
