## Question

A 24-year-old male who is four years status post left arthroscopic ACL reconstruction presents with left knee pain. The patient endorses associated fevers and chills over the past several days, and he reports a similar episode of pain involving his right shoulder approximately three weeks prior. Examination of the left knee is significant for a palpable effusion and severe pain with passive flexion and extension. A diffuse rash is also noted along the patient’s trunk and chest. An aspiration of the left knee is performed, with a resultant white blood cell count of 65,000. Cultures of the aspirate grow gram-negative diplococci. What is the most appropriate initial treatment option?

## Options

1. Open irrigation and debridement alone
2. Open irrigation and debridement with antibiotics
3. Serial aspirations with antibiotics
4. Oral colchicine
5. Clinical observation

## Correct option

**3. Serial aspirations with antibiotics**

## Answer explanation

Arthritis-dermatitis is a clinical manifestation of disseminated gonococcal infection, which can be treated with serial aspiration and antibiotics (Answer 3).

The patient presents with a history of polyarthralgia and a diffuse skin rash. Aspirate results of the left knee demonstrate gram-negative diplococci. Together, these findings suggest a disseminated gonococcal infection. Initial treatment involves aspiration or arthroscopic drainage of the actively affected joints and parenteral ceftriaxone. Open irrigation and debridement is reserved for more severe cases or those not responsive to initial treatment.

Li and Hatcher outlined the presentation, evaluation, and treatment of gonococcal arthritis, outlining both the localized purulent arthritis and dermatitis-arthritis forms of the disease. Initial workup should involve screening for co-existing sexually transmitted infections including HIV, syphilis, and chlamydia. Treatment involves joint aspiration or arthroscopic drainage in combination with parenteral ceftriaxone. Response to treatment is monitored through the resolution of fever, joint pain, and leukocytosis. In cases involving septic arthritis that are not responsive to initial treatment, open irrigation, and debridement may be necessary.

*Blank et al. published a case report of arthritis-dermatitis syndrome in a healthy, 36-year-old female who presented with bilateral ankle pain, vaginal discharge, and a lower extremity skin rash.* Gonococcal infection was diagnosed through urinary nucleic acid amplification testing (NAAT). The patient was ultimately treated with a seven-day course of intramuscular ceftriaxone. Repeat urinary NAAT was negative for infection and the patient remained asymptomatic. The reported case was unique in that the involved rash was a painless, petechial, and purpuric rash rather than the more common, tender, papulopustular rash seen with arthritis-dermatitis syndrome.

Incorrect Responses
Answer 1: Antibiotics are a necessary component of any treatment algorithm
Answer 2: Open irrigation and debridement is typically reserved for cases that do not respond to initial aspiration and antibiotic therapy.
Answer 4: Colchicine would not be effective in eradicating infection.
Answer 5: The patient presents with an active infection necessitating prompt intervention.
