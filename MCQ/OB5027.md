## Options

1. Hinged knee brace and weight bearing as tolerated for 6 weeks
2. Direct repair of patella tendon to inferior patella pole
3. Activity as tolerated and observation only
4. Open reduction and internal fixation
5. Placement of a knee immobilizer and magnetic resonance imaging if patient remains symptomatic at 12 weeks

## Correct option

**4. Open reduction and internal fixation**

## Answer explanation

This patient has sustained a sleeve fracture of the superior patellar pole. Open reduction and internal fixation is recommended when the fracture gap is >2 mm.

Sleeve fractures of the superior patellar pole are thought to occur when the patella is pushed down, resulting in an intra-articular dislocation. If the gap between the sleeve fracture and the superior pole is less than 2 mm, and the extensor mechanism is intact, nonoperative treatment is usually considered. Open reduction and internal fixation is recommended when the fracture gaps are >2 mm or the extensor mechanism is not intact.

*Gettys et al. reviewed sleeve fracture of the superior pole of the patella.* **They state** that radiographs may reveal patella baja, anterior tilt, and suprapatellar calcification. Suspected injury may be further investigated with an ultrasound or magnetic resonance imaging (MRI).

*Maripuri et al. published a case report of a sleeve fracture of the superior pole of the patella.* With a fracture gap of 2 mm, they elected to manage the injury nonoperatively with immobilization of the knee in an extension splint. By ten weeks, the patient had regained full knee movement and radiographic union, without any quadriceps weakness.

Figure A and B are radiographs of the left knee, showing transverse orientation of the patella. Figure C and D show reduction of the patella in its correct anatomical position and a small hemarthrosis. Figure E is an ultrasound image of the left knee demonstrating the presence of a sleeve fracture of the superior pole of the patella, with a fracture gap of 3 mm.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1,3,5: Non-operative treatment is usually considered if the extensor mechanism is intact and fracture gap is <2 mm. An MRI may be ordered to better identify the pattern of injury, but this should be performed around the time of injury.
Answer 2: Injury involving the patella tendon would result in a sleeve fracture of the inferior patellar pole.
