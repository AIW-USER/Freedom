## Question

Most common acromion type for shoulder impingement is (FNB Pattern Paper 2017)

## Options

1. I
2. II
3. III
4. IV

## Correct option

**3. III**

## Answer explanation

The most common classification is the one by Bigliani et al. (1986) describing a flat (type-I), curved (type-II), or hooked (type-III) acromion on outlet-view radiographs. In some studies, a type-III acromion has been found to be associated with a higher prevalence of shoulder impingement and rotator cuff tears.

Type I: Flat

Type II: Curved (Most common type)

Type III: Hooked: Associated with increased incidence of shoulder impingement and rotator cuff tear

Type IV: Convex (upturned) (3%): This is most recent addition to acromion type. the undersurface of the acromion is convex near the distal end. It is not associated with increased incidence of impingement syndrome

Other parameters of acromion morphology that have been evaluated for their association with impingement are acromion tilt, acromion slope and lateral acromion angle.

The acromion index (AI): On true anteroposterior radiographs the distance from the glenoid plane to the acromion (GA) is divided by the distance from the glenoid plane to the lateral aspect of the humeral head (GH). The larger the extension of the acromion, the higher the AI.

![[TO17119654-1.png]]

The lateral acromial angle (LAA): On a true anteroposterior radiograph one line is drawn along the superior- and inferior-most lateral points of the glenoid and represents the glenoid surface. Another line is drawn parallel to the acromion undersurface. The angle between these 2 lines (a) represents the LAA

![[TO17119654-2.png]]

Low lateral acromion angle and high acromion index have been associated with high incidence of cuff impingement and tear.

Answer: 3
