## Question

A 10-year-old female with right hip pain presents with the radiographs in Figure A. She has a past medical history of hypothyroidism. She undergoes in-situ screw fixation of her right hip with no intra-operative complications. Which of the following problems is most likely to occur in this scenario?

![[q736-a-scfe2.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Chondrolysis
2. Ostenecrosis
3. Septic hip
4. Ipsilateral knee pain
5. SCFE on contralateral hip

## Correct option

**5. SCFE on contralateral hip**

## Answer explanation

Figure A shows a frog pelvis radiograph of a skeletally immature patient with a grade I slipped capital femoral epiphysis (SCFE) of the right hip. A contralateral hip SCFE is the most common postoperative complication in a skeletally immature patient with an endocrine disorder (e.g., hypothyroidism).

Controversy exists regarding the indications for prophylactic pinning of the contralateral, unaffected hip. Historically, prophylactic pinning was suggested in high risk patients, which include patients younger than 12 years of age at presentation and patients with endocrine abnormalities. However, complications of prophylactic pinning have been reported, such as avascular necrosis (AVN), bleeding, infection, chondrolysis, fracture, and implant pain. Complications of prophylactic pinning occur at a prevalence of 2-6%. This is significantly less than the risk of a SCFE on the contralateral side, which has shown to occur at a rate up to 36% in high risk groups. For this reason, the argument to prophylactically pin hips prevails in the high risk groups prevail. The indications for prophylactic pinning outside of the high risk groups remain less certain.

*Yildirim et al. looked at the impact of prophylactic pinning of contralateral hips in patients with a SCFE.* **They found** a high prevalence of a subsequent contralateral slips (36%) and potential complications (high slip severity) in high risk patients. **They noted** that the risk of osteonecrosis and chondrolysis on the prophylactic hip was low, between 2-4%.

*Sankar et al. examined the incidence and nature of complications after contemporary prophylactic fixation of the contralateral, unaffected hip in patients with a unilateral SCFE.* *In 99 patients, there were no subsequent slips on the side of the prophylactic pinning.* Complications include, two cases of focal AVN (2%), no cases of chondrolysis (0%), two patients sustained periimplant femur fractures (2%) and three patients had symptomatic hardware (3%).

Illustration A shows in situ screw fixation of the right hip and prophylactic pinning of the left hip. There were no post-operative complications.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Chondrolysis has been shown to occur in 0-2% of patients
Answer 2: Osteonecrosis has been shown to occur in 1-3% of patients
Answer 3: Septic hip has been shown to occur in 0-2% of patients
Answer 4: Ipsilateral knee pain as been shown to occur in 5-10% of patients
