## Question

A 52-year-old male presents with a painless soft-tissue mass involving the left thigh that has slowly gotten larger over the past 6 months. X-ray of the thigh demonstrates a soft-tissue mass only, and MRI of the thigh is demonstrated in figures A and B. Which of the following is true regarding the initial management of this lesion?

![[q215502-b-screen-shot-2021-04-30-at-5-52-26-am.jpg]]

## Options

1. Marginal resection and radiation therapy should be initiated
2. No further radiographic workup is indicated
3. CT-guided core needle biopsy is indicated
4. Open incisional biopsy should be performed utilizing a horizontal incision
5. Treatment should consist of radiation only

## Correct option

**3. CT-guided core needle biopsy is indicated**

## Answer explanation

This patient presents with history and MRI findings concerning for a soft-tissue sarcoma. Initial management should include a biopsy for histologic diagnosis of the lesion. CT-guided core needle biopsy is indicated in the initial management of this lesion.

Soft-tissue sarcomas are rare malignant tumors comprising a variety of subtypes distinguished by histologic findings. CT-guided core needle biopsy is gaining popularity in the diagnosis of soft tissue sarcomas. When performing an open biopsy, several important principles must be followed including: use of a longitudinal incision, meticulous hemostasis, avoidance of neurovascular structures, performed through involved compartment only, and drains should exit skin in line with the incision.

*De Marchi et al. performed a retrospective review evaluating the accuracy of ultrasound-guided core needle biopsy in diagnosis of soft tissue sarcomas in 115 patients.* **They found** 95% of samples obtained were adequate for diagnosis with sensitivities/specificities of 97%/93% for diagnosis and 100%/97% for grading, respectively. **They conclude** core needle biopsy is safe and effective for soft tissue sarcoma diagnosis.

*Hryhorczuk et al. performed a retrospective review of 63 CT-guided core needle biopsies performed on bone lesions in children.* **They found** 78% of the biopsy samples were adequate with a diagnostic accuracy of 84%. **They conclude** CT-guided core needle biopsy is safe and effective in diagnosing pediatric bone lesions.

Figures A and B are T1 and T2-weighted MRI images demonstrating a large, high-grade liposarcoma that is dark on T1 and bright on T2 as these lesions are made of ~10% fat.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1 and 5: Histologic diagnosis obtained by biopsy should be performed prior to marginal resection or radiation therapy.
Answer 2: CT scan of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis is warranted when patients present with soft tissue lesions concerning for malignancy.
Answer 4: Open incisional biopsy is indicated. However, a longitudinal incision should be utilized.
