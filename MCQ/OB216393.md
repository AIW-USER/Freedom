## Question

Which of the following patients at 2-year follow-up of a previous metal-on-metal hip arthroplasty may be managed with close clinical follow-up without further advanced imaging or revision arthroplasty?

## Options

1. 57-year-old symptomatic male with a serum cobalt (Co) of 6 and serum chromium (Cr) of 5; normal ESR, CRP; metal artifact reduction sequence (MARS) MRI without fluid/mass
2. 64-year-old asymptomatic female with Co of 8 and Cr of 10; normal ESR, CRP; normal radiographs
3. 62-year-old symptomatic male with Co of 5 and Cr of 4; MARS-MRI with moderate fluid collection, but no osteolysis
4. 73-year-old asymptomatic female with Co of 4 and Cr of 5; MARS-MRI with moderate fluid collection and mild increase in osteolysis over 1 year
5. 59-year-old symptomatic male with Co of 7 and Cr of 11; normal radiographs, MARS-MRI with moderate fluid collection

## Correct option

**1. 57-year-old symptomatic male with a serum cobalt (Co) of 6 and serum chromium (Cr) of 5; normal ESR, CRP; metal artifact reduction sequence (MARS) MRI without fluid/mass**

## Answer explanation

In the management of metal-on-metal total hip arthroplasty (THA), symptomatic patients with normal serum metal ions (<7 ppb for both serum cobalt or serum chromium) and normal MARS-MRI studies may be managed conservatively with close clinical follow-up (3-6 months) and regular serum metal ion labs.

Metal-on-metal THA has been associated with complications secondary to metal debris/wear. Of primary concern is the associated soft tissue reaction, termed aseptic lymphocyte-dominant vasculitis-associated lesion (ALVAL), which in the symptomatic patient warrants revision total hip arthroplasty. The management of patients following metal-on-metal THA is primarily done through routine radiographs and annual testing of serum metal ion levels. Elevated metal ions (>7 ppb for either serum cobalt or serum chromium), even in the asymptomatic patient, warrants further imaging with a MARS-MRI to evaluate for fluid/mass associated with ALVAL.

*Lombardi et al. summarize the recommended management of patients with previous metal-on-metal THA.* **They emphasize** the importance of regular serum metal ion testing, and further work-up with a MARS-MRI in those patients with elevated levels. Other factors considered when deciding whether to perform revision THA included cup version/abduction, implant track record, and radiographic osteolysis.

*Chang et al. evaluated the correlation between symptoms and MRI findings following metal-on-metal THA.* **They report** a 69% prevalence of pseudotumors on MRI after metal-on-metal hip arthroplasty but did not find a correlation between pseudotumor presence and pain. Bone marrow edema and tendon tearing on MRI were the only findings that correlated with patient symptoms.

*Hayter et al. focus on MRI findings in symptomatic (painful) patients with metal-on-metal THA in a review including 31 hip resurfacing and 29 THA.* In the THA group, **they report** 86% rate of synovitis, 10% extracapsular disease, and 24% osteolysis, with no statistically significant difference in rates between resurfacing and THA. Extracapsular disease and muscle edema were only seen in patients with ALVAL.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Elevated metal ion levels (>7 ppb) warrant obtaining a MARS-MRI, even in the asymptomatic patient
Answer 3: Patients who are symptomatic and have a MARS-MRI with fluid/mass should undergo revision THA
Answer 4: Asymptomatic patients who have elevated metal ions, a concerning MARS-MRI, and progression of osteolysis, shoulder undergo revision THA
Answer 5: Patients who are symptomatic, have elevated metal ions, and an abnormal MARS-MRI shoulder undergo revision THA
