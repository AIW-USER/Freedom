## Question

A 6-year-old male presents with worsening right knee pain and progressive difficulty with ambulation over the last 3 days. He reports falling down on his knee weeks ago but did not scrape his skin in his recollection. He denies any fevers or chills. On examination, the right knee is erythematous and swollen, but has minimal pain with passive range of motion. His laboratory studies are significant for WBC count of 9,300 cells/mm3 (reference <11,000 cells/mm3), ESR 35 mm/hr (reference <15 mm/hr), and CRP 12.2 mg/L (reference <10 mg/L). What is the most appropriate next step in management?

## Options

1. Initiate oral antibiotics and repeat lab studies in 1 week
2. Initiate IV antibiotics and monitor for clinical response to treatment
3. Obtain MRI of the right knee
4. Surgical debridement followed by culture specific antibiotics
5. Obtain bone biopsy

## Correct option

**3. Obtain MRI of the right knee**

## Answer explanation

The patient is presenting with suspected osteomyelitis of the right patella which should be further evaluated with an MRI of the right knee.

Pediatric osteomyelitis is typically the result of hematogenous seeding of the metaphyseal bone with bacteria rather than direct spread through open wounds. The most common offending organism is Staphylococcus aureus, but there is an increasing incidence of Kingella species being reported. Initial diagnostic workup consists of physical examination, laboratory studies, and plain radiographs. In cases of suspected osteomyelitis an MRI study to evaluate the extent of the infection as well as associated abscesses or fluid collections which may suggest surgical treatment is favorable. However, the vast majority of cases can be successfully treated with antibiotics alone.

*Dartnell et al. performed a systematic review of acute and subacute hematogenous pediatric osteomyelitis which included 132 studies.* **They reported** 40% of children are afebrile on presentation with S. aureus being the most common offending pathogen. **They concluded** that diagnosis relies on the combination of clinical examination, blood tests, and radiographic results.

Peltola and Pääkkönen reviewed acute osteomyelitis in children. They reported after confirmation of the diagnosis antibiotic therapy is often curative in the majority of cases in the absence of bone or subperiosteal abcess. The authors stated IV agents can be converted to oral agents after there is a documented clinical response.

Figures A and B are the AP and lateral radiographs of the right knee demonstrating lytic lesions in the patella consistent with a destructive process.

Incorrect answers
Answers 1 and 2: Antibiotics are the mainstay of treatment for osteomyelitis, however, further advanced imaging studies should be obtained to evaluate the extent of the disease process.
Answer 4: Surgical debridement of osteomyelitis is reserved for cases that are refractory to adequate medical treatment or in the presence of a discrete abscess.
Answer 5: A bone biopsy can be obtained to direct culture-specific antibiotics. However, this would likely be done after an MRI study and no isolated organisms present in blood or synovial fluid cultures.
