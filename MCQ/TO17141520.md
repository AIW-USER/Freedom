## Question

Incorrect statement about WALANT

## Options

1. Was mainly proposed to decrease waiting Time for surgery
2. Not suitable for complex procedures like tendon transfers due to short term anaesthesia effect
3. Less chances of adhesion formation post surgery
4. Allows better evaluation of problem intraoperatively

## Correct option

**2. Not suitable for complex procedures like tendon transfers due to short term anaesthesia effect**

## Answer explanation

ANSWER- B

Explanation

WALANT stands for WIDE AWAKE LOCAL ANAESTHESIA NO TOURNIQUE. It’s a technique used in hand and upper limb surgeries where LAs (Lidocaine/ Bupivacaine) and Hemostatic agents (Epinephrine) are used to provide a bloodless field for hand surgery without using any sedation and tournique.

The concept was pioneered by Canadian surgeon Dr Lanonde (2005) to decrease waiting time for surgery for the patients (injections were given in one room, then patient shifted and surgery in another)

Although things started with simple procedures, with advancements it’s become suitable for even complex procedures like tendon transfers due to short term anaesthesia effect

The biggest advantage of WALANT is that it cuts down chances of adhesion formation post surgery. Collagen formation and scarring starts after day 3 post op but here patient is mobilized immediately. Patient being awake further adds an advantage as it may allow better evaluation of problem intraoperatively, likely one can ask to move a finger to check tendon identification etc.

WALANT is specifically advantageous for patients with commorbidities and where there is some reason to avoid a tournique.
