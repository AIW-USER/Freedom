## Question

A 52-year-old man presents to clinic with several months of worsening bilateral lower extremity pain and heaviness, especially when walking. His MRI is shown in Figures A and B. Which of the following statements is true regarding the anatomic structure that is contributing most to his pathology?

## Options

1. It inserts on the ventral surface of the L5 lamina closer to the inferior edge than the superior edge
2. It originates from the ventral surface of the L4 lamina closer to the superior edge than the inferior edge
3. It is the primary ligamentous restraint to thoracolumbar flexion in the Posterior Ligamentous Complex (PLC)
4. It is made primarily of type 2 collagen
5. It is one of the anatomic structures of the middle column

## Correct option

**2. It originates from the ventral surface of the L4 lamina closer to the superior edge than the inferior edge**

## Answer explanation

The patient is presenting with symptoms of neurogenic claudication and lumbar spinal stenosis, mainly secondary to hypertrophy and infolding of the ligamentum flavum (LF). The LF originates approximately 60-70% of the distance from inferior to superior on the ventral surface of the lamina (i.e. closer to superior edge than inferior edge). It inserts on the superior edge of the caudal lamina.

Lumbar spinal stenosis is most commonly the result of a cascade of degenerative changes that leads to predictable pattern of pathology of spinal structures, among which is hypertrophy and infolding of the LF. The LF is composed of approximately 80% elastin and 20% collagen and is an important component of the posterior ligamentous complex that contributes to spinal stability. Removal of the LF, in addition to other pathological structures, is the focus of a lumbar laminectomy. The anatomic insertions of the LF are important to understand when doing a discectomy, as they need to be released to access the epidural space and disc.

*Shemesh et al. evaluated the histological appearance of the LF in patients with lumbar spinal stenosis.* **They found** loss of elastic fibers and fibrosis in all samples, and more loss of elastin in patients with diabetes mellitus.

*Yabe et al. evaluated the histo-chemical profile of the LF in patients with and without lumbar spinal stenosis.* **They found** that the gene expression related to fibrosis, chondrogenesis, and proteoglycan synthesis was significantly higher in the lumbar stenosis group

*Wu et al used finite element analysis to determine the biomechanical role of the thoracolumbar ligaments.* **They found** that the supraspinous ligament was the largest contributor to stability in flexion while the facet capsule was the largest stabilizer in extension.

Figure A and B are a T2 sagittal and axial MRI, respectively, showing severe lumbar spinal stenosis secondary mostly to LF hypertrophy. Illustration A is the same axial image with a blue arrow pointing to the LF.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The LF originates approximately 60-70% of the way up on the anterior surface of the cranial lamina and the superior edge of the caudal lamina.
Answer 3: The supraspinous ligament has been shown to be the primary stabilizer of the thoracolumbar spine in flexion.
Answer 4: The LF is made of approximately 80% elastin and 20% type I collagen
Answer 5: The LF is considered a part of the posterior column.
