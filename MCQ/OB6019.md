## Question

Figure 36 shows the radiograph of a patient who has hip pain and is unable to ambulate. What is the most appropriate management for this patient?

![[q6019-a-36.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Bisphosphonates
2. Protected weight bearing
3. Open reduction and internal fixation
4. Revision total hip arthroplasty
5. Resection arthroplasty

## Correct option

**4. Revision total hip arthroplasty**

## Answer explanation

The patient has a periprosthetic fracture of the greater trochanter - Vancouver A. The reason for the fracture of the greater trochanter is the extensive periarticular osteolysis that has occurred as a result of polyethylene wear. The latter is demonstrated by eccentric seating of the large femoral head in the acetabulum. The most appropriate management is to reverse the osteolysis process, which involves exchange of the acetabular liner with or without revision of the other components depending on their fixation and position. The greater trochanter can also be fixed during revision surgery.
