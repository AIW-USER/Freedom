## Question

A 74-year-old woman is diagnosed with rotator cuff tear arthropathy and undergoes a reverse total shoulder arthroplasty. A standard deltopectoral approach is used, but during the case excessive retraction on the tendons of the short head of the biceps and the coracobrachialis results in injury to a nervous structure. Which of the following corresponds to the most likely injured structure?

## Options

1. Axillary nerve from the posterior cord
2. Axillary nerve from the medial cord
3. Medial brachial cutaneous nerve from the medial cord
4. Musculocutaneous nerve from the medial cord
5. Musculocutaneous nerve from the lateral cord

## Correct option

**5. Musculocutaneous nerve from the lateral cord**

## Answer explanation

Excessive retraction on the conjoint tendon places the musculocutaneous nerve at risk. The musculocutaneous nerve is a branch of the lateral cord of the brachial plexus.

The deltopectoral approach is commonly used to expose the glenohumeral joint during total shoulder arthroplasty. Though the most common neurologic injury seen in total shoulder arthroplasty is an injury to the axillary nerve, the musculocutaneous nerve can be injured by aberrant retractor placement or excessive retraction on the conjoint tendon.

Apaydin, et al. published a cadaveric study on the effects on the positioning of the musculocutaneous nerve with adducted versus abducted positions of the arm during anterior approaches to the shoulder. The authors studied 40 shoulders in 20 cadavers and demonstrated that the musculocutaneous nerve courses 2.0 +/- 0.5 cm below the coracoid process and is closest with the arm abducted and internally rotated. The average distance from the coracoid process to the point of entrance of the nerve into the conjoint tendon was 5.8 +/- 0.8cm, while the nerve was typically as close as 0.5 +/- 0.05 cm to the glenoid labrum in 90 degrees of abduction.

Carofino, et al. performed a retrospective review that identified twenty-six patients with iatrogenic nerve injury secondary to shoulder surgery. The authors found that the injury occurred at the level of the brachial plexus in thirteen patients and at a terminal nerve branch in thirteen, with 15 patients (58%) failing to recover nerve function after extended observation. They concluded that nerve injuries occurring during shoulder surgery can produce severe morbidity and may require surgical management.

Boardman, et al. published on the neurologic complications of shoulder surgery. The authors note that the suprascapular nerve may lie no more than 1 cm from the glenoid rim, the axillary nerve may run no more than 3 mm from the inferior shoulder capsule and passes near the lower extent of the deltoid split used as an approach to the shoulder, while the musculocutaneous nerve passes as near as 3.1 cm below the coracoid. They conclude that because 1% to 4% of patients undergoing prosthetic arthroplasty of the shoulder experience nerve injuries, careful attention to patient positioning, retractor placement, and arm manipulation during surgery are essential.

Incorrect answers:
Answers 1-2: The axillary nerve is the most commonly injured nerve during total shoulder arthroplasty, but is not injured due to excessive retraction on the conjoint tendon. It is most at risk during humeral exposure at the inferior border of the subscapularis as the nerve courses from anterior to posterior.
Answer 3: The medial brachial cutaneous nerve is supplied by the T1 nerve root and branches from the medial cord prior to the terminal branching of the ulnar nerve, but it is not commonly cited as being injured in total shoulder arthroplasty.
Answer 4: The musculocutaneous nerve is at risk here, as above, but is a branch of the lateral cord of the brachial plexus, not the medial cord.
