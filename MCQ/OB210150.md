## Answer explanation

Bunionette deformities with a lateral bow that have failed nonoperative measures with 4-5 intermetatarsal angle (4-5 IMA) less than 12 degrees should be corrected with a distal chevron osteotomy.

Bunionette deformities present as a painful prominence over the 5th metatarsal head. They can be classified according to Coughlin as Types I-III. Type I deformities have enlarged metatarsal head or exostosis. Type II deformities have a lateral bowing of the fifth metatarsal. Type III deformities have a widened 4-5 IMA. Type II deformities can be corrected with distal chevron osteotomies if the 4-5 IMA is <12 degrees. The distal chevron osteotomy can correct deformity and may be combined with lateral condylectomy or lateral soft tissue tightening for additional correction. Type II deformities with 4-5 IMA >12 degrees can be corrected wtih an oblique diaphyseal rotational osteotomy.

*Cohen et al. reviewed bunionette deformity presentation and treatment.* **They state** painful type II deformities with a lateral bow should be treated with distal chevron osteotomies. **They recommend** securing the osteotomy with 2.0mm screw or 0.045-in K-wire and allowing patients to weight bear in a post-operative shoe for 2 to 4 weeks.

*Kitoaka et al. reviewed the distal chevron osteotomy for symptomatic bunionette deformities.* *They mention the benefits of a distal chevron osteotomy include high healing rate and minimal shortening.* **They reported** transfer metatarsalgia in only 1 out of 19 cases.

Figure A shows an example of a fracture of the base of the fifth metatarsal. Figure B shows a Type II deformity with 4-5 IMA <12. Figure C shows a Type III deformity with 4-5 IMA >12. Illustration A shows the Coughlin classification of bunionette deformities.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1 & 4: A fifth metatarsal base fracture would likely go on to heal without surgery in this patient; a distal metatarsal osteotomy would not be indicated for the treatment of this fracture.
Answers 3 & 5: Type III deformities with 4-5 IMA >12 degrees are corrected with oblique diaphyseal rotational osteotomies.
