## Options

1. Bony union rates approach 50-60% with screw fixation
2. Fixation with an intramedullary screw provides a less rigid construct than plate fixation
3. Given that this is not a Jones fracture, it should first be treated in a cast or CAM walker boot
4. Intramedullary screw fixation is associated with greater hardware prominence than plate fixation
5. Screw diameter should be as large as possible

## Correct option

**5. Screw diameter should be as large as possible**

## Answer explanation

When fixing fifth metatarsal fractures with intramedullary screws, whether they are true Jones fractures or proximal diaphyseal stress fractures, the largest screw diameter that can be accommodated by the canal should be chosen (Answer 5).

Fifth metatarsal base fractures can be classified according to three zones of injury (Illustration A). Type I fractures represent proximal avulsion injuries, termed "Pseudo-Jones" fractures, which can be treated successfully conservatively given that nonunions are uncommon. Type II fractures, termed "Jones" fractures, represent fractures involving the 4th-5th metatarsal articulation at an important vascular watershed area. Given an increased risk of nonunion (up to 15-30%), operative fixation is typically recommended. Type III fractures, on the other hand, occur in the proximal diaphysis distal to the 4/5 metatarsal articulation and often occur as stress fractures in athletes, as in the patient in question here. Like Type II fractures, an increased incidence of nonunion is seen, and early operative fixation should be considered in athletes in order to allow rapid return to sport. When fixing these fractures, intramedullary screw fixation is commonly performed, with screw diameters larger than 4mm recommended, ideally selecting the largest diameter screw that the canal can accommodate from 4.5mm to 6.5mm. Post-operatively, a short period of non-weight bearing is recommended (1-3 weeks), with subsequent progress rehabilitation and expected return to running and impact activities at 6 weeks if the surgical site is pain-free and there are adequate radiographic signs of callous.

*Thevendran et al. review the evidence for the management of fifth metatarsal fractures in the athlete.* **The authors note** that proximal metaphyseal/diaphyseal junction (zone 2) and proximal diaphyseal (zone 3) fractures should be surgically fixed to enable more rapid union and return to sports. **They conclude** that Intramedullary screw fixation is supported by level III and IV studies, although no implant has shown superiority and there is insufficient evidence to recommend bone grafting acutely.

*Huh et al. performed a biomechanical comparison of intramedullary screw versus low-profile plate fixation of Jones fractures.* *Simulated Jones fractures were created in 8 matched pairs of fresh-frozen cadaveric fifth metatarsals.* **The authors found** that intramedullary screw fixation demonstrated greater bending stiffness and less fracture site angulation than plate fixation during plantar-to-dorsal and lateral-to-medial bending. **They concluded** that although low-profile “hook” plates offer an alternative for fixation of fifth metatarsal Jones fractures, intramedullary screw fixation may provide better resistance to bending and initiation of fracture site rotation.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: Union rates after intramedullary screw fixation approach 100% in most published series.
Answer 2: Fixation with an intramedullary screw provides a more rigid construct than plate fixation.
Answer 3: Despite the fact that this is not a Jones fracture, it should be treated with screw fixation in an athlete that is looking for a quick return to sport.
Answer 4: Intramedullary screw fixation is associated with less hardware prominence than plate fixation.
