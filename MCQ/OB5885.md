## Question

A 52-year-old woman presents with a painful bunion deformity of her left foot shown in Figure A. A radiograph of her foot shows the following: (1) hallux valgus angle (HVA) = 35°, (2) intermetatarsal angle (IMA) = 12°, (3) hallux valgus angle distal metatarsal articulation angle (DMAA) = 9°, (4) hallux valgus interphalangeus angle is 12°, (5) the 1st metatarsal joint is congruent. Which combination of procedures will most effectively achieve correction?

![[q5885-a-fig-a.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. (1) Distal metatarsal osteotomy, displacing the metatarsal head laterally. (2) Distal soft-tissue release.
2. (1) Distal metatarsal osteotomy, displacing the metatarsal head medially. (2) Distal soft-tissue release.
3. (1) Distal metatarsal osteotomy, displacing the metatarsal head laterally. (2) Medial closing wedge proximal phalanx osteotomy.
4. (1) Distal metatarsal osteotomy, displacing the metatarsal head medially. (2) Medial closing wedge proximal phalanx osteotomy.
5. (1) Distal metatarsal osteotomy, displacing the metatarsal head laterally. (2) Lateral opening wedge proximal phalanx osteotomy.

## Correct option

**3. (1) Distal metatarsal osteotomy, displacing the metatarsal head laterally. (2) Medial closing wedge proximal phalanx osteotomy.**

## Answer explanation

Considering that the DMAA <10°, the HVA is moderate (<40°), the IMA is not large (<15°) the joint is congruent, and hallux valgus interphalangeus angle is >10°, a distal metatarsal osteotomy with lateral displacement of the MT head, and Akin medial closing wedge proximal phalanx osteotomy is indicated.

Indications for an Akin osteotomy include: (1) hallux valgus interphalangeus, (2) a congruent joint with DMAA < 10°, and use as a secondary procedure if a primary procedure (e.g., chevron or distal soft-tissue procedure) did not provide sufficient correction due to a large DMA angle or hallux valgus interphalangeus. It also repositions the FHL towards the midline, contributing both to biomechanical and cosmetic correction for residual hallux valgus deformity. It is contraindicated with an incongruent joint and where DMAA> 15°. For joint incongruence, perform distal soft tissue correction. For large DMAA, perform biplanar distal Chevron osteotomy.

*Chuckpaiwong compared distal (52 feet) and proximal osteotomies (79 feet) for moderate-to-severe HV (defined as HVA >40° or IMA >12°).* **They found** that proximal osteotomies had greater HVA and IMA correction, and both osteotomies had improvement in functional scores and pain. **They recommend** proximal osteotomy for moderate to severe HV in general, but state that distal osteotomies may be considered for moderate to severe HV in inactive or aging patients who have only desire pain relief and improved functionality.

Figures A shows hallux valgus. Illustrations A and B show correction with a proximal osteotomy (from Jung et al, not the answer), lateral soft tissue release, and Akin osteotomy.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1, 2, 4,: Distal soft tissue procedures are indicated where there is joint incongruence. In hallux valgus distal osteotomies, the metatarsal head is displaced laterally.
Answer 5: With the Akin osteotomy, a medial closing wedge is employed, rather than lateral opening wedge, because of faster bone healing (better bony contact), earlier removal of hardware, and no need for bone graft.
