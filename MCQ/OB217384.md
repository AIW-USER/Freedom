## Question

A patient presents to your office with wrist pain for several years. Radiographs are obtained and reveal Stage III scaphoid lunate advanced collapse. She is scheduled to undergo a four-corner fusion. Arthritis is likely to be absent between which of the following in this patient?

## Options

1. Scaphoid and radial styloid
2. Scaphoid and scaphoid facet of the radius
3. Scaphoid and capiate
4. Capitate and lunate
5. Lunate and radius

## Correct option

**5. Lunate and radius**

## Answer explanation

This patient has Stage III Scaphoid Lunate Advanced Collapse (SLAC) wrist. The radiolunate joint is spared in this condition.

Scaphoid lunate advanced collapse describes the specific pattern of degenerative arthritis seen in chronic dissociation between the scaphoid and lunate. Diagnosis is made clinically with progressive wrist pain and wrist instability with radiographs showing advanced arthritis of the radiocarpal and midcarpal joints (radiolunate joint spared). Treatment involves observation, NSAIDs, and splinting in the early stages of disease. A variety of operative procedures may be indicated depending on the severity of the disease and the patient's symptoms.

*Wall et al. reviewed proximal row carpectomy (PRC).* **They reported** that PRC provides satisfaction at a minimum of 20 years with a survival rate of 65%. **They concluded** by recommending a minimum age for PRC between 35 and 40 years.

*Strauch et al. reviewed scapholunate advanced collapse (SLAC).* **They reported** that SLAC wrist may also result from chronic pseudogout and can appear bilaterally without a clear history of injury. **They concluded** that surgical treatment for SLAC wrist includes 4-corner arthrodesis, capitolunate arthrodesis, complete wrist arthrodesis, proximal row carpectomy (PRC), denervation, and radial styloidectomy.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Stage I SLAC has arthritis between scaphoid and radial styloid
Answer 2: Stage II SLAC has arthritis between scaphoid and entire scaphoid facet of the radius
Answer 3: Stage III SLAC has arthritis between scaphoid and capitate
Answer 4: Stage III SLAC has arthritis between lunate and capitate
