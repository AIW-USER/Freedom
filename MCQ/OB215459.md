## Question

A 32-year-old, right hand dominant, Andorran laborer was using a high-pressure water gun when the hose slipped and he accidentally injected water into the volar aspect of his left index finger. On exam, the finger is perfused, but there is a loss of static two-point discrimination, significant swelling, erythema that tracks proximally into the palm, and an inability to move the finger. Radiographs show no fracture or dislocation. Which of the following treatments is most appropriate?

## Options

1. Emergent operative decompression, because of the high-pressure injection injury
2. Admission, observation, and intravenous antibiotics
3. Operative decompression scheduled on an outpatient basis, because water is a non-toxic and not an organic solvent
4. Operative decompression scheduled on an outpatient basis, because the finger is well-perfused
5. Oral antibiotics and clinical follow-up

## Correct option

**1. Emergent operative decompression, because of the high-pressure injection injury**

## Answer explanation

A high-pressure injection injury requires emergent operative decompression.

High-pressure injection injuries are characterized by a seemingly small entrance wound, but a wide zone of injury and extensive soft tissue damage. Young, male, laborers on the non-dominant index finger are most frequently affected. While treatment always involves emergent debridement, there is a 50% amputation rate for organic solvents (paint, paint thinner, diesel fuel, jet fuel, oil). Additionally, patients should be admitted post-operatively for observation and consideration of repeat debridement, as 42% have positive cultures.

*Bean et al. reviewed 19 patients who sustained high-pressure injection injuries to the hand.* *84% occurred in the non-dominant hand, and the index finger was the specific location in nearly half of the patients.* Only 2/10 of the patients who received early surgery, and 3/9 who had delayed surgery, required additional surgical intervention. Two patients in the delayed group required an amputation. 16% of patients developed an infection and 1 developed compartment syndrome. The authors underscored the severity of these injuries which may lead to amputation.

*Cannon reviewed high-pressure injection injuries of the hand, which are often overlooked and can have devastating sequelae owing to the significant inflammatory response.* Prognosis depends primarily on the material in the injection, location of the injection, and timing to surgical intervention. High-pressure injection injuries need emergent decompression and debridement within 6 hours. Most patients have residual pain, stiffness, loss of sensation, and difficulty returning to pre-injury level.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: The patient requires emergent surgical decompression. After surgery, he should be admitted and observed for possible secondary debridement or fasciotomies of the hand.
Answers 3,4,5: The patient should not be discharged as he will need emergent surgical debridement.
