## Question

A 52-year-old female presents with continued forefoot pain after excision of a Morton’s neuroma in the 3rd webspace through a dorsal incision. She now has localized tenderness and burning pain in the same webspace, similar to her previous symptoms. Compared to the plantar approach, which of the following is the primary advantage of a dorsal approach for revision excision?

## Options

1. Direct visualization of the nerve bifurcation
2. Lower risk of plantar scar tenderness and wound complications
3. Shorter length of resection possible
4. Earlier return to weightbearing with reduced recurrence risk
5. Improved exposure of the intermetatarsal ligament

## Correct option

**2. Lower risk of plantar scar tenderness and wound complications**

## Answer explanation

The dorsal approach for revision neuroma excision is associated with a lower risk of plantar scar tenderness and wound complications (Answer choice 2).

Revision neuroma excision can be performed through a dorsal or plantar approach. While the superiority of each approach for revision cases is debated in the literature, there are benefits to both. Most authors would recommend a plantar approach if the primary resection was done dorsally to avoid scarring and altered anatomy. Due to the neuroma's location plantar to the transverse metatarsal ligament, the plantar approach provides superior visualization and access to the neuroma and surrounding structures, particularly avoiding previous scar tissue from the dorsal approach. The plantar approach, however, has a risk of painful plantar foot scarring and typically requires a longer non-weight-bearing protocol. Functional outcomes are similar between the two approaches for revision surgery. For this reason, the American College of Foot and Ankle Surgeons does not have a recommendation favoring dorsal or plantar approaches for revision neuroma excision. Ultimately, approach selection depends on prior incisions, surgeon preference, and patient-specific factors.

*Akermark et al. published a prospective, randomized controlled trial of 76 patients with primary Morton's neuroma assigned to either plantar or dorsal excisions.* *Both groups showed comparable rates of "good" clinical outcomes with no significant differences in pain, functional limitations, or scar tenderness.* **The authors concluded** there was no clear superiority of one approach over the other.

*Pomeroy et al. published a review article highlighting different entrapment neuropathies around the foot and ankle.* Regarding Morton's neuroma specifically, the authors mention that most surgeons will prefer a dorsal approach for primary resection. Recurrent symptoms are the most common complication, however. In order to minimize this risk, **the authors recommend** the release of the transverse metacarpal ligament along with nerve resection.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer choice 1: Direct visualization of the nerve bifurcation is one of the main advantages of the plantar approach, not the dorsal approach.
Answer choice 3: Revision resections require a longer, more proximal resection to the healthy nerve to ensure adequate resection. Through the dorsal approach, it is more difficult to dissect proximally. Furthermore, a shorter length of resection is a disadvantage, not an advantage.
Answer choice 4: Studies have shown an earlier return to weight-bearing with a dorsal approach, compared to a plantar approach. However, the recurrence risk is not decreased with a dorsal approach, making this answer incorrect.
Answer choice 5: The intermetatarsal ligament is much more easily visualized from the plantar approach. Through the dorsal approach, the intermetatarsal ligament must be released to identify the nerve plantar to it. However, this requires a more extensive dissection from a dorsal approach.
