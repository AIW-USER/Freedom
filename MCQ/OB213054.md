## Question

A 15-year old male without previous medical history has been complaining of right shoulder pain for 1 month. He also reports that he has lost 5 pounds within that same timeframe. He states that he has difficulty sleeping at night due to feeling warm and sweaty. Current imaging is shown in Figures A and B. A bone marrow biopsy is performed and is found to be positive for a EWS-FL1 fusion protein. Which of the following combines the correct diagnosis with the correct translocation?

## Options

1. Synovial sarcoma; t(x;18)
2. Ewing's sarcoma; t(12;22)
3. Osteosarcoma; t(9;22)
4. Osteosarcoma; t(x;18)
5. Ewing's sarcoma: t(11;22)

## Correct option

**5. Ewing's sarcoma: t(11;22)**

## Answer explanation

Ewing's sarcoma has a translation of (11;22) which produces the EWS-FL1 fusion protein.

Ewing's sarcoma is an aggressive tumor found in adolescents and young adults. Nearly all cases of Ewing's sarcoma contain the (11;22)(q24;q12) chromosomal translocation that encodes the EWS/FLI oncoprotein. EWS/FLI contains the amino-terminus of EWS fused, in frame, to the carboxyl-terminus of FLI. EWS is a protein of uncertain function, whereas FLI is a member of the ETS family of transcription factors. In the context of this fusion, a strong transcriptional activation domain is contributed by EWS and an ETS-type DNA-binding domain is contributed by FLI. Both of these domains are required for the oncogenic function of EWS/FLI, supporting the notion that the fusion acts as an aberrant transcription factor.

*Toomey et al. performed a review on the recent advances in the molecular pathogenesis of Ewing's sarcoma.* **They report** that nearly all Ewing's sarcoma contain the (11;22)(q24;q12) chromosomal translocation that encodes the EWS/FLI oncoprotein. **They also report** that many cases involve other abnormalities, but (11;22) remains the most common.

de Alava performed a review on Ewing's Sarcoma, focusing on research on the molecular pathology and possible developments in targeted therapies in this malignancy. He reports that approximately 85% of patients with Ewing's sarcoma have EWS/FL1 fusions. He concludes that patients with extraskeletal Ewing's sarcoma have a better prognosis than patients with osseous tumors independent of age, ethnic group, or primary site.

*Biswas et al. performed a review of the management of the Ewing's Sarcoma family of tumors.* **They report** that the outcome has been improved dramatically from 10% with surgery and radiotherapy alone to 65%-70% now, in localized disease, with the introduction of chemotherapy. Chemotherapy regimen evolved from single agent to multiagent with effort of many cooperative clinical trials over decades. **They conclude** that novel therapy targeting the molecular pathways and pathogenesis is required to improve the survival of this tumor.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Synovial sarcoma results from a t(x;18) translocation, which produces a fusion protein of SYT-SSx1.
Answer 2: The Ewing's sarcoma translocation is t(11;22).
Answers 3 & 4: Osteosarcoma most commonly occurs as a germline mutation in p53.
