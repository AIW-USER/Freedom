## Question

A 46-year-old female sustains a midshaft clavicle fracture. The decision is made to proceed with open reduction and internal fixation of the fracture. Which of the following is true when comparing the use of a single 3.5-mm superior plate with dual fixation utilizing a 2.7-mm superior plate and a 2.4-mm anterior plate?

## Options

1. Single plate fixation has a higher rate of hardware removal
2. Single plate fixation has a longer time to union
3. Dual plate fixation carries a higher rate of re-operation
4. Dual plate fixation has a higher rate of maintenance of reduction
5. Dual plate fixation has a 10x increase in bending strength

## Correct option

**1. Single plate fixation has a higher rate of hardware removal**

## Answer explanation

Midshaft clavicle fractures fixed with a single 3.5-mm superior plate have a higher rate of hardware prominence leading to an increased rate of re-operation compared with dual fixation utilizing a 2.7-mm superior plate and a 2.4-mm anterior plate.

Midshaft clavicle fractures are common traumatic injuries caused by a direct impact to the shoulder girdle and are most commonly seen in young, active adults. Diagnosis can be made radiographically with AP and cephalic tilt clavicle x-rays. Treatment is nonoperative or operative based on patient activity and demands, along with the degree of displacement, shortening, and comminution. Both single and dual plating of the clavicle have been utilized. Though dual plating has not been shown to result in significantly higher union rates and often carries increased implant costs, lower rates of re-operation have been cited in the literature due to less hardware prominence associated with the smaller, lower-profile plates.

*Allis et al. reviewed whether reducing plate size with the use of dual mini-fragment plating compared with standard, 3.5-mm superior plating would minimize implant symptoms and the corresponding need for re-operation while still providing sufficient fixation to allow fracture healing and return to function.* **They reported** a 100% union rate in both groups. **They concluded** that dual fixation utilizing a 2.7-mm superior plate and a 2.4-mm anterior plate for the treatment of displaced midshaft clavicular fractures was associated with a significantly lower rate of re-operation when compared with single, 3.5-mm superior plate fixation.

*Czajka et al. retrospectively reviewed ORIF with a dual mini-fragment plating technique for clavicular shaft fractures.* **They reported** that the utilization of a dual mini-fragment plating technique in the treatment of clavicular shaft fractures results in a low rate of secondary surgery for symptomatic implant removal (3.7%) and similar QuickDASH scores when compared with historical controls treated with 3.5-mm plates placed on the superior clavicle. **They concluded** that potential disadvantages of using this technique include a higher surgical implant cost and longer length of surgery.

*DeBaun et al. retrospectively compared pre-contoured (Pc) small fragment plating to dual mini-fragment plating (DmF) for ORIF of diaphyseal clavicle fractures.* **They reported** that this retrospective comparative study found no difference in union or maintenance of reduction for diaphyseal clavicle fractures fixed with DmF compared with Pc plating. **They concluded** that patients treated with DmF plates may have lower rates of symptomatic implant removal.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Time to union has been shown to be similar between single and dual plate fixation.
Answer 3: Single plate fixation has a higher rate of re-operation.
Answer 4: No difference has been shown in the literature in regard to union or maintenance of reduction for diaphyseal clavicle fractures fixed with either construct.
Answer 5: Biomechanical studies have predominantly shown equivalence, rather than superiority to a single pre-contoured anatomic plate.
