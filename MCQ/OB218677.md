## Question

A 52-year-old female presents to your clinic with complaints of discomfort through the forefoot. She is a real estate agent and does note that she wears high-heels and other tight-fitting shoes frequently, which seem to exacerbate the issue. On examination, she localizes her pain to the third webspace and has a palpable click when the midfoot is squeezed. You notice an incision over the dorsal aspect of the webspace and when you inquire, she states she had surgery to "fix a nerve" approximately 1 year ago but her surgeon moved out of town. Given her most likely diagnosis, what is the next best step in her management to minimize further recurrence?

## Options

1. Plantar corticosteroid injection and physical therapy
2. Revision neurectomy through plantar or dorsal approach with nerve burial
3. Revision neurectomy through fishmouth incision in webspace with nerve burial
4. Revision neurectomy through plantar approach with transection of adjacent lumbrical and nerve burial
5. Revision neurectomy through plantar and dorsal approaches with nerve burial

## Correct option

**2. Revision neurectomy through plantar or dorsal approach with nerve burial**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a recurrence of her Morton's neuroma, which is best treated with revision neurectomy through a dorsal or plantar approach.

Morton's neuromas are compressive neuropathies of the forefoot that lead to fibrosis and entrapment of the interdigital nerves in the web spaces. Most commonly involved are the 3rd and 4th interdigital nerves. It typically occurs in middle-aged females and those who have wide feet or wear narrow shoes like high heels. Initial treatment is nonoperative including shoe wear modifications, orthotics, and corticosteroid injections. Ultimately if there is no improvement, operative intervention with neurectomy can be performed through a dorsal or plantar approach. The dorsal approach is more common and requires complete release of the transverse intermetatarsal ligament in order to access the neuroma. The plantar approach offers better visualization but more risk of wound healing complications and a scar on the plantar surface of the foot. In revision settings, tethering of the neuroma branches often occurs plantarly so a plantar approach can be beneficial, but a dorsal approach may still be utilized, provided the nerve is sufficiently released proximally.

*Title and Schon reviewed primary and secondary neurectomy in a JAAOS article.* *They review the symptoms and diagnostic algorithm as well as nonoperative management strategies.* **They recommend** a dorsal approach for primary neurectomy but in revision cases, they note that inadequate proximal nerve resection or incomplete resection is often due to tethering of the plantar nerve branches, with a plantar approach offering better exposure for complete neurectomy.

*Weinfeld and Myerson also reviewed the diagnosis and treatment of interdigital neuritis in a JAAOS article.* **They note** that either a dorsal or plantar approach may be utilized in primary cases, with the advantage of a dorsal approach being that it is technically easier but can be easier to insufficiently resect the nerve proximally enough. **They noted** that in cases of recurrence, the nerve should be transected as far proximally as possible, but they do not recommend either a plantar or dorsal approach as being superior to the other.

*Bhatia and Thomson published another review on Morton's neuromas,* **noting** the true pathology is more frequently related to nerve fibrosis. They review nonsurgical options, which include: orthotics, steroid injections, and other shoe wear/activity modifications. Surgical options include neurectomy, which **they note** is usually performed through a dorsal approach.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The patient has a recurrence of her Morton's neuroma and would benefit from surgical intervention to resect the nerve stump that is likely scarred to the surrounding structures. Physical therapy would not offer significant additional benefits in this setting. Corticosteroid shots are preferably given dorsally.
Answer 3: A fishmouth incision in the webspace should not be used as visualization may be poor and the risk for wound healing complications is higher.
Answer 4: While either a plantar or dorsal approach can be utilized in a revision setting, lumbrical transection should be avoided, as this can lead to extension deformity at the MTP joint.
Answer 5: Dual incisions are unnecessary, even in the setting of a recurrent Morton's neuroma, and may cause soft tissue compromise and/or excessive scar tissue burden.
