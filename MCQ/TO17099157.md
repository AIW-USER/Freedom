## Question

Incorrect statement regarding comparison of Smith Peterson Osteotomy (SPO) and Ponte osteotomy (PO) is

## Options

1. Pontes osteotomy (PO) is a modification of Smith Peterson osteotomy (SPO)
2. SPO was primarily described for thoracic spine deformity while PO for lumbar spine deformity
3. SPO involves narrow resection of facet joints while PO involves wider resection
4. Correction achieved on average is 10 degrees but slightly better with PO

## Correct option

**2. SPO was primarily described for thoracic spine deformity while PO for lumbar spine deformity**

## Answer explanation

SPO and PO have often been treated in literature interchangeably with great technical similarities. However, there are notable differences between the two osteotomies in the type and amount of resections, in the different anatomy of the regions they were originally developed for, that is lumbar for SPO versus thoracic for PO and also in the grading in Schwab's Osteotomy Classification—Grade 1 for Smith-Petersen osteotomy and Grade 2 for Ponte osteotomy.
Smith-Petersen's osteotomy was described in 1945 for the correction of a flat lumbar spine from ankylosing spondylitis. This osteotomy consists in a narrow resection of lumbar facet joints and in a detachment of ligamentum flavum from the inferior margin of the lamina and inferior articular process. There are no resections of the laminae. So basically, nibbling of facet joints + flavectomy + laminotomy (not laminectomy) is done. If SPO is used to obtain flexibility in thoracic kyphosis, correction is obtained by a wide opening of anterior disc spaces and a lengthening of the anterior column.

![[TO17099157-1.jpg]]

The Ponte osteotomy, developed in 1987 is modification of SPO. It consists in a wide resection of thoracic facet joints (complete removal of both superior and inferior facets) as well as of laminae and in a complete removal of the ligamentum flavum. Thoracic kyphosis, the original indication, is corrected by a substantial shortening of the posterior column, obtained by closing the gaps of osteotomies, preferably through segmentally applied and apically directed compression forces. The absence of anterior column lengthening better preserves the immediate and long-term load sharing capacity and the stability of correction.
