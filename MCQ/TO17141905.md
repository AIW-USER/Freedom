Chapter: Regional Orthopedics MCQs — Thumb Collateral Ligament Injuries (Skiers Thumb)

## Question

Skier’s thumb is characterized by all except(FNB Pattern Paper 2015)

## Options

1. Hyperabduction or extension injury of thumb
2. Rupture of ulnar collateral ligament (UCL) of the MCP joint of thumb
3. Valgus instability at thumb
4. Most common pattern is avulsion of UCL from head of 1st metacarpal

## Correct option

**4. Most common pattern is avulsion of UCL from head of 1st metacarpal**

## Answer explanation

SKIER’S/GAMEKEEPER’S THUMB

This is the most common injury pattern at the first metacarpophalangeal (MCP) joint and refers to rupture of the ulnar collateral ligament (UCL) of the MCP joint of the thumb. On most occasions, UCL is avulsed from the base of the proximal phalanx. It is named so because it was a common injury observed in Scottish gamekeepers (gamekeeper here refers to Scottish fowl hunters and game word refers to their prey which were rabbits) who used to kill small rabbits (game) by breaking the animal’s necks between their thumb and index finger. Hyperabduction or extension of the thumb is the usual event causing injury. The most common cause nowadays is a skier’s hand landing on a ski pole, causing a valgus force on the thumb and injury to UCL. However, while a skier's thumb is an acute injury, the gamekeeper's thumb is more of chronic nature (occurs with repetitive trauma).

Diagnosis: Patients present with valgus instability at thumb and with pain and swelling of the MCP joint. Tenderness can be localized to the medial aspect of the MCP joint of thumb. X-ray may show avulsion fracture at the ulnar corner of the base of the proximal phalanx and widening of MCP joint on stress views. MRI guides the diagnosis in doubtful cases.

Treatment: In partial ruptures the thumb is unstable in flexion only and these injuries are treated with immobilization in a thumb spica plaster for 4 weeks. For complete tears (thumb is unstable in both extension and flexion) operative repair is required.

Answer: 4
