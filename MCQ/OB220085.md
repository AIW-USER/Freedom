## Question

## Options

1. Patient A is more likely to have an extensor lag than patient B
2. Patient B is at a higher risk for patella fracture than patient A
3. Patient B is more likely to have significantly improved patient reported outcome measures than patient A
4. Patient B is at a high risk of nonunion
5. Patient A can be allowed to initiate range of motion sooner than patient B

## Correct option

**1. Patient A is more likely to have an extensor lag than patient B**

## Answer explanation

V-Y quadricepsplasty (also known as V-Y turndown) and tibial tubercle osteotomy (TTO) are both acceptable extensile approaches for revision total knee arthroplasty. However, V-Y turndown is associated with postoperative extensor lag (Answer 1).

Exposure in revision knee arthroplasty presents unique challenges compared to primary knee replacement due to a variety of factors such as scar tissue, altered anatomy, potential bone loss, and the presence of existing implants. Two techniques that can be employed to improve exposure during revision knee arthroplasty are the V-Y turndown and TTO. The V-Y turndown is a surgical technique used to improve exposure by lengthening the quadriceps tendon. An incision in the shape of a "V" is made at the distal end of the quadriceps tendon, and after the revision procedure is completed, the "V" is closed in a "Y" fashion. The TTO is a technique where the tibial tubercle is osteotomized and reflected anteriorly with the attached patellar tendon. This provides excellent exposure of the knee joint, especially when dealing with difficult tibial implant extraction. Both are associated with good clinical results. However, the V-Y turndown is associated with a postoperative extensor lag due to lengthening of the extensor mechanism.

*Divano et al. performed a systematic review to evaluate the outcomes and complications of TTO, drawing from 26 selected studies out of an initial pool of 322.* The literature shows that TTO can lead to significant clinical improvements and has a high rate of radiographic fragment healing, yet complications can occur in 3.8–20% of cases. TTO is a challenging but useful approach in knee arthroplasty; careful execution is key to minimizing risks and maximizing patient outcomes.

*Punwar et al. present a retrospective analysis of clinical and radiological outcomes following TTO in revision knee arthroplasty.* Results indicate TTO is a reliable method for improving surgical exposure, with all osteotomies uniting without fractures and most patients experiencing improved range of motion and Oxford Knee Scores. Minor complications were reported, such as proximal migration, but the technique proved particularly effective in two-stage revisions for infected knees without increasing the risk of nonunion or fracture. The study supports the safety and efficacy of TTO in complex knee revision surgeries.

*Della Valle et al. review approaches for revision total knee arthroplasty.* The V-Y turndown can improve exposure and allow for lengthening of the extensor mechanism, while the TTO provides excellent exposure and is biomechanically advantageous for subsequent operations, although it does not substantially lengthen the extensor mechanism. Both techniques require careful consideration due to the potential for complications such as extensor lag (V-Y turndown), difficulty with kneeling (TTO), and patient dissatisfaction when compared to non-extensile approaches.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: V-Y turndown has a risk of devascularizing the patella if the inferior geniculate is compromised, leading to patella necrosis or fracture.
Answer 3: There are no significant differences in PROMs between V-Y turndown and TTO.
Answer 4: TTO has a low rate of nonunion.
Answer 5: Patients who undergo V-Y turndown should be kept in extension until the extensor mechanism begins to heal.
