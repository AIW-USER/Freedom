## Question

A 63-year-old patient undergoes a right total hip arthroplasty for advanced degenerative joint disease. The immediate post-op course was uneventful. However, during postop day 3 the patient experiences sudden shortness of breath and chest pain. Chest CT demonstrated a saddle pulmonary embolus, which required therapeutic dosing of enoxaparin. Subsequently, the patient develops profound anemia requiring 3 units of packed red blood cells. The patient however reports new-onset right leg pain and foot drop. What is the most likely cause of the patient's neurologic changes?

## Options

1. Delayed onset mechanical nerve irritation
2. Lumbar spinal stenosis
3. Hematoma compressing the sciatic nerve
4. Nerve infarct from embolus from deep vein thrombosis
5. Nerve ischemia from profound anemia

## Correct option

**3. Hematoma compressing the sciatic nerve**

## Answer explanation

A hematoma compressing the sciatic nerve is the most likely cause of the nerve palsy in the acute postoperative period and administration of therapeutic anticoagulation.

Thromboembolic events are potentially lethal events following orthopedic procedures. Adult reconstruction has an especially high risk for the development of deep vein thrombosis due to the venous stasis at the time of the procedure and decreased limb motion following surgery, endothelial injury from surgical trauma, and relative hypercoagulable state following surgery. Treatment for deep vein thrombosis and pulmonary embolism involves therapeutic anticoagulation for three months or longer. Unfortunately, there is a risk of surgical site hematoma with therapeutic anticoagulation, which may cause a sciatic nerve palsy. In this setting, careful coordination with intensivist and hematology service regarding discontinuation of anticoagulation, placement of IVC filter, and early decompression are the ideal treatment options.

*Della Valle et al. reviewed the diagnosis and treatment of thromboembolism in total joint arthroplasty.* **They reported** that diagnosis consists of combined clinical findings with confirmatory duplex ultrasound, ventilation-perfusion scans, and CT angiogram. Diagnosed thromboembolic events should be treated with therapeutic anticoagulation with or without an inferior vena cava filter.

*Weil et al. reported a case of sciatic nerve palsy after administration of therapeutic heparin for a pulmonary embolism occurring after total hip arthroplasty.* **The authors recommended** prompt identification of the nerve palsy and early decompression to enable optimal nerve recovery.

Incorrect answers:
Answers 1, 2, 4, and 5: In the setting of therapeutic anticoagulation and drop in hemoglobin, a hematoma compressing the sciatic nerve is the most likely reason for the neurologic deficits.
