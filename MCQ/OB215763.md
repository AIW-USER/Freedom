## Answer explanation

The patient's young age, normal blood work, epiphyseal lesion, and s-100 positive staining are consistent with a chondroblastoma. Answer 3 is a histological specimen of a chondroblastoma.

Chondroblastomas are benign chondrogenic tumors typically seen in the epiphysis of young patients. An MRI will typically show an epiphyseal lesion with extensive surrounding edema. Histological findings include chondroblasts arranged in a "chickenwire" or "cobblestone" pattern with occasional multinucleated giant cells. Immunohistochemical staining for S-100 is often positive in chondroblastomas. Intralesional curettage and bone grafting is performed for symptomatic individuals.

*Sailhan et al performed a retrospective review of 87 cases of chondroblastoma to identify factors that increase the risk of recurrence.* *All patients were treated surgically.* *At two-year minimum follow-up, 32% had local recurrence.* Lesions isolated to the epiphysis were associated with a higher risk of recurrence. S-100 was present in all but one chondroblastoma that were tested for this marker.

*Bloem et al reviewed the clinical and radiographic features of 104 chondroblastomas.* *The average age of presentation was 16 years.* Around half were isolated to the epiphysis or apophysis. The most common location was around the knee. Common radiographic features included eccentric radiolucent epiphyseal lesions with sclerotic margins.

Figure A is a sagittal T2 MRI of a knee showing a hyperintense epiphyseal lesion. Figure B is a histological slide showing several neutrophils along with reactive bone formation consistent with osteomyelitis. Figure C demonstrates several giant cells surrounded by mononuclear stromal cells, typical of giant cell tumor (GCT). Figure D shows multiple chondroblasts within chondroid matrix and a single multinucleated giant cell compatible with a chondroblastoma. Figure E shows multiple blood-filled cystic spaces consistent with an aneurysmal bone cyst (ABC). Figure F demonstrates a sheet of monotonous small round blue cells and pseudorosettes as seen in Ewing's sarcoma.

Incorrect Answers
Answer 1: Given the patients normal WBC, ESR and lack of fevers osteomyelitis is unlikely to be the answer
Answer 2: GCT are more commonly seen in the 4-6th decade of life and do not stain positive for S-100
Answer 4: ABC classically show fluid-fluid levels on MRI and do not stain positive for S-100
Answer 5: Ewing's sarcoma commonly involve the diaphysis of long bones and present with elevated WBC, ESR, anemia, and fevers
