## Question

You are consulted to see a 67-year-old female with a new-onset inability to ambulate due to severe right hip pain. Her past medical history is significant for metastatic breast cancer. Radiographs demonstrate a peritrochanteric lytic lesion involving 40% of the cortical circumferential circumference. She has no radiographic evidence of osteoarthritis, acetabular involvement, or skip lesions. The oncology team feels her life expectancy is > 6 months. Which of the following is the recommended management correctly paired with her Mirel's score?

## Options

1. Hemiarthroplasty; Mirels' score of 11
2. Total hip arthroplasty; Mirels' score of 9
3. Radiation and physical therapy; Mirels' score of 7
4. Continued observation; Mirels' score of 11
5. Cephalomedullary nail; Mirels' score of 8

## Correct option

**1. Hemiarthroplasty; Mirels' score of 11**

## Answer explanation

The patient's Mirels' score is 11 and a hemiarthroplasty (HA) is an appropriate option given the lack of pre-existing osteoarthritis or acetabular involvement. However, an intramedullary nail (IMN) and total hip arthroplasty (THA) would also be appropriate treatment options in this scenario.

When evaluating a patient with a metastatic pathologic lesion, the Mirels' criteria can help predict an impending pathologic fracture. A score of ≥8 suggests the need for prophylactic fixation and takes into consideration the lesion site, pain, characteristic, and size. In determining an operative plan, the surgeon must create a stable construct with the goal of immediate weight-bearing and mobilization. Options include IMN, open reduction internal fixation (ORIF), or endoprosthetic reconstruction. If an intramedullary device or ORIF is utilized, cement augmentation should be considered to enhance fixation and fill any voids. Current literature supports the use of an endoprosthesis for the management of proximal femur lesions compared to IMN or ORIF, as they are associated with better long-term outcomes and lower revision rates.

*Wedin et al. performed a retrospective study of 142 patients treated surgically for 146 metastatic proximal femur lesions.* Their goal was to compare and contrast the use of an endoprosthesis versus IMN. **They concluded** that endoprosthetic reconstructions were favorable to IMN and were associated with lower reoperation rates.

*Macaulay et al. performed a prospective randomized clinical trial comparing hemiarthroplasty (HA) to THA in the treatment of displaced femoral neck fractures.* *They evaluated patient-reported outcomes scores at 6, 12, and 24 months in 17 patients treated with THA and 23 treated with HA.* **They found** significantly higher patient satisfaction rates and comparable complication rates in patients treated with THA.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: THA is a reasonable surgical option in this patient and is associated with greater patient-reported outcomes compared to HA. However, this patient's Mirels' score is 11.
Answers 3 and 4: This patient's Mirels' score is 11 and her life expectancy is > 6 mos; a Mirels' score of ≥8 is an indication for operative intervention to prevent an impending pathologic fracture.
Answer 5: A cephalomedullary nail may be a treatment consideration in this scenario, although this patient's Mirels' score is 11. However, as the life expectancy for cancer patients improves, the progression of pathologic lesions can lead to hardware failure over time.
