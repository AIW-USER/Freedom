## Question

You are reviewing a treatment algorithm for managing hallux rigidus and note there are a wide variety of options available. Of the following patient scenarios, which one would be most appropriately indicated for cheilectomy?

## Options

1. 55-year-old male with joint narrowing on radiographs, metatarsophalangeal joint stiffness, and pain at end-range of motion
2. 70-year-old female with severe joint space narrowing on radiographs, metatarsophalangeal joint stiffness, and pain throughout range of motion
3. 75-year old female with no joint space narrowing or osteophytes on radiographs and no metatarsophalangeal joint stiffness
4. 67-year old female with dorsal osteophyte on radiographs, no metatarsophalangeal joint stiffness, and pain at terminal dorsiflexion
5. 71-year-old male with large dorsal osteophyte on radiographs, metatarsophalangeal joint stiffness, and pain throughout mid-range of motion

## Correct option

**4. 67-year old female with dorsal osteophyte on radiographs, no metatarsophalangeal joint stiffness, and pain at terminal dorsiflexion**

## Answer explanation

Cheilectomy is most appropriately indicated in patients with grade 1 or 2 hallux rigidus, with the patient in this scenario having grade 1 findings.

Hallux rigidus is defined by pain and stiffness at the MTP joint of the great toe. There is a spectrum of severity with hallux rigidus, which has been classified by Coughlin and Shurnas based on the degree of pain at the mid-range or extremes of motion, combined with the degree of radiographic involvement (Illustration A). For less severe conditions or those that are asymptomatic, treatment begins nonoperatively using a Morton's extension orthotic as well as shoe and activity modifications. Dorsal cheilectomy is utilized in grade 1 and 2 disease where preoperative range of motion is relatively maintained. As radiographic joint space narrowing occurs, stiffness worsens, and pain is present in the mid-range of motion, arthrodesis or arthroplasty are the best options.

*Hamid and Parekh reviewed the clinical presentation and management of hallux rigidus,* **noting** it is the most common arthritic condition occurring in the foot. They discuss how both radiographic and clinical findings are important in classifying the degree of disease, which can help decide treatment. They go on to talk about different operative strategies and their indications including cheilectomy, osteotomies, arthroplasty and arthrodesis.

*McNeil et al. looked into the efficacy of different treatments for hallux rigidus.* **They noted** fair (grade B) evidence for supporting arthrodesis and poor (grade C) evidence for supporting cheilectomy, osteotomy, and arthroplasty for hallux rigidus. This said, **they point out** there are not many high-level studies comparing these options and suggest that this is the next step in understanding how to best manage these patients.

*Coughlin et al. graded hallux rigidus and associated outcomes based on this classification and management strategy.* **They noted** that cheilectomy had predictable success when performed in grades 1 and 2 and noted that 92% were successful in providing pain relief and improvement in function. In contrast, **they recommended** performing an arthrodesis in grades 3 and 4 where there was >50% joint space loss.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: This represents grade 3 hallux rigidus which should be treated with MTP arthroplasty vs. arthrodesis.
Answer 2: This represents grade 4 hallux rigidus which should be also treated with MTP arthroplasty vs. arthrodesis.
Answer 3: This represents grade 0 hallux rigidus and this patient is best treated nonoperatively before proceeding to operative intervention.
Answer 5: This represents grade 3 hallux rigidus and should be treated with MTP arthroplasty vs. arthrodesis.
