## Question

35 years old male with massive rotator cuff tear presents with pseudo paralysis. MRI findings of his shoulder includes acromiohumeral humeral distance <6mm with minimal glenohumeral arthritis, massive tear of posterosuperior rotator cuff muscles and gout Allier stage 3 fatty atrophy of supraspinatus muscle. Which is least preferred treatment option for him?

## Options

1. Reverse shoulder arthroplasty
2. Latissimus dorsi transfer
3. Lower trapezius transfer
4. Superior capsular reconstruction

## Correct option

**1. Reverse shoulder arthroplasty**

## Answer explanation

Massive and Irreparable rotator cuff tear: RCTs are defined as irreparable when the lesions cannot be repaired primarily to their insertion on the tuberosities despite conventional techniques of surgical release/mobilization, because of their size, retraction, and muscle impairment caused by atrophy and fatty infiltration. Characteristics of an irreparable cuff are:

- Cofield et al. described a massive tear as greater than 5 cm, while others consider a massive rotator cuff tear to be one involving two or more
- Posterosuperior tears involve the supraspinatus and infraspinatus and are more common than anterosuperior tears, which involve the supraspinatus and subscapularis
- Gout Allier stage III and IV fatty atrophy of cuff muscles
- Acromio-humeral distance less than 6 mm
- Chronic pseudo paralysis (see above question)
- Positive tangent sign (see question 30 for explanation)

In young patients with massive irreparable rotator cuff tear without significant arthritis surgical options are tendon transfer and superior capsular reconstruction. Reverse shoulder arthroplasty is done in old patients with rotator cuff arthropathy.

Tendon transfer procedures are options for younger and active patients with massive irreparable posterosuperior or anterosuperior rotator cuff tears without significant glenohumeral arthritis in the setting of an intact subscapularis tendon.

Latissimus dorsi transfers are indicated for patients with retracted posterosuperior tears with grade-3 or higher fatty degeneration of the infraspinatus and supraspinatus musculature and are contraindicated for those with insufficient subscapularis function and/or poor subscapularis muscle quality and obvious deltoid muscle atrophy.

A more anatomical transfer for irreparable posterosuperior RCTs is a lower trapezius transfer, and early results are promising.

Answer: 1
