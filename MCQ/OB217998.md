## Question

A 61-year-old female presents to your office with complaints of right shoulder pain which has worsened over the last year. She denies any distinct injury. On examination, she has full active and passive range of motion with 5/5 strength on empty can testing and a negative Hawkins test. O'Brien's and Speed's tests cause pain. An MRI is obtained and demonstrates intra-substance tearing of the biceps tendon in addition to a SLAP lesion. Subluxation of the biceps tendon is also seen. What is the innervation of the associated structure most likely to also be injured?

## Options

1. Axillary nerve
2. Musculocutaneous nerve
3. Suprascapular nerve
4. Subscapular nerve
5. Medial pectoral nerve

## Correct option

**4. Subscapular nerve**

## Answer explanation

Given the subluxation of the biceps tendon, the subscapularis is likely injured and is innervated by the upper and lower subscapular nerves.

Subscapularis tears are becoming increasingly recognized as a source of shoulder pathology. There is an intimate relationship between the subscapularis and the long head of the biceps tendon (LHBT) as it traverses through the shoulder joint and when there is a tear of the subscapularis, the LHBT can subluxate underneath or inside the subscapularis. In cases of complete subscapularis tearing with retraction, the LHBT may completely dislocate. In more subtle subscapularis tears, subluxation of the LHBT may be helpful for identification.

*Shi et al. evaluated LHBT subluxation on MRI as a predictor for subscapularis tears identified at the time of arthroscopy.* *They diagnosed 26 patients with LBHT subluxation preoperatively, and 9 of these had full-thickness tears of the subscapularis at the time of surgery.* LHBT subluxation on MRI had a sensitivity of 82%, specificity of 80%, PPV of 35%, and NPV of 97% and was closely correlated with the severity of the subscapularis tear.

*Yoon et al. also attempted to correlate medial subluxation/dislocation of the LHBT on MRI with subscapularis tears confirmed arthroscopically.* *Out of the 432 patients included,* **they identified** biceps subluxation/dislocation in 46, and 45 of these had a full-thickness subscapularis tear intra-operatively. In their study sensitivity was 45%, specificity was 99%, PPV was 98%, and NPV was 86%, which allowed them to conclude that if LHBT subluxation/dislocation was seen on MRI, there was a high likelihood of pathology being identified at the time of surgery.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The axillary nerve innervates the deltoid and teres minor muscles.
Answer 2: The musculocutaneous nerve innervates the biceps, brachialis, and coracobrachialis.
Answer 3: The suprascapular nerve innervate the supraspinatus and infraspinatus.
Answer 5: The medial pectoral nerve innervates the pectoralis major and minor.
