## Answer explanation

The supinated oblique view is the best imaging study to view the pisotriquetral articulation and characterize the degree of arthritis in this joint (Answer 1).

 Identifying the etiology of ulnar-sided wrist pain can be difficult due to the number of anatomic structures within a small geographic area. Utilizing a precise physical examination and tailored imaging studies improves surgeon accuracy in characterizing the inciting pathology. Regarding pisotriquetral arthritis, a supinated oblique view allows the surgeon to evaluate the volar ulnar aspect of the wrist and provides a profile view of the pisotriquetral articulation. The appropriate orientation for this imaging study is 30° supinated from neutral. Additionally, a modification to the supinated oblique view is the Garraud view, which images the wrist at 30° of supination and slight extension.

*Watanabe et al. reviewed the clinical relevance and current treatment strategies for ulnar-sided wrist pain.* **They determined** that the ulnar aspect of the wrist is a diagnostic challenge for hand surgeons and radiologists due to the small and complex anatomical structures involved. Imaging modalities that may be useful in diagnosing pathology about the ulnar side of the wrist include radiography, arthrography, ultrasound (US), computed tomography (CT), CT arthrography, magnetic resonance (MR) imaging, and MR arthrography. **They concluded** that the most common diseases that cause ulnar-sided wrist pain include extensor carpi ulnaris (ECU) tendonitis, flexor carpi ulnaris (FCU) tendonitis, pisotriquetral arthritis, triangular fibrocartilage complex (TFCC) lesions, ulnar impaction, lunotriquetral (LT) instability, and distal radioulnar joint (DRUJ) instability.

*O’Shaughnessy et al. reviewed the long-term outcomes associated with pisformectomy.* *Of the 61 wrists reviewed over 27 years, only two complications occurred (3%), including ulnar nerve palsy and a symptomatic retained suture.* *Most patients (93%) did not require additional surgeries,* **demonstrating** that pisiformectomy preserves motion and grip strength well, with low complication rates.
