## Question

A 15-year-old male from South America sustained a right distal femur fracture 6 years ago that was treated with casting. He reports this healed uneventfully and was able to return to activities without any issues. However, in the last several years he feels as if his right leg is now shorter than the left. Examination reveals a 5.5 cm leg length discrepancy (L>R). Radiographs show complete closure of the right distal femoral physis. What is the most appropriate treatment for this patient?

## Options

1. 5 cm shoe lift and observation
2. Left distal femur and proximal tibia epiphysiodesis in one year
3. Left closed femoral shortening
4. Right femoral lengthening
5. Right tibial lengthening

## Correct option

**4. Right femoral lengthening**

## Answer explanation

The patient has a 5.5 cm leg length discrepancy (LLD) with an expected discrepancy of 6.5 cm at the time of skeletal maturity. The ideal treatment would be a limb lengthening procedure of the right femur.

Post-traumatic LLD is the result of premature physeal closure from an injury that leads to progressively worsening leg length asymmetry with skeletal maturity. The magnitude of discrepancy is dependent on the age of the patient at the time of injury and the remaining growth left. Generally, males reach skeletal maturity at 16 years and females at 14 years. Treatment involves shoe lifts and observation for LLD <2 cm, epiphysiodesis or shortening of the longer limb for LLD between 2 and 5 cm, and lengthening of the short limb for LLD >5 cm.

Westh and Menelaus calculated the timing of the epiphyseal arrest and estimated leg length in 94 patients and compared them to final leg length measurements. They found 94.1% of the estimated leg lengths to be within 0.75 inches of the actual leg lengths and 85.1% within 0.5 inches.

*Sasso et al. performed a retrospective study of 18 patients undergoing closed femoral shortening with an intramedullary nail.* **They reported** radiographic union in all patients by 3 months post-op with an average of 4.4 cm of shortening obtained. **The authors recommended** to ream the intramedullary canal slowly and lock distally and proximally.

*Nordsletten et al. performed a retrospective study of thigh muscle strength following mid-diaphyseal and subtrochanteric femoral shortening osteotomies.* **They found** there was no limit to muscle strength loss and length shortened in subtrochanteric osteotomies, but a limit of 10% was evident in mid-diaphyseal femoral osteotomies. **The authors recommended** considering the level of the osteotomy when there is a need for greater than 10% of length shortening.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: A shoe lift is indicated in patients with an LLD <2 cm. In this case, a shoe lift would not provide sufficient functional improvement.
Answer 2: This patient has about 1 year left of growth remaining, which would negate any treatment benefit by delaying an epiphysiodesis of the left proximal tibia and distal femur.
Answer 3: Closed femoral shortening with an intramedullary nail of the left femur would require a 6.5 cm shortening which would negatively impact muscle strength of the extremity.
Answer 5: Lengthening of the right tibia would lead to the knee joint line being asymmetric from the contralateral side. Lengthening should be performed on the femur.
