## Answer explanation

The arrow in Figure D is pointed towards the inverted limbus, one of several structures that can block a reduction in developmental dysplasia of the hip (DDH) patients.

DDH is a spectrum of disease resulting from abnormal development of the hip, resulting in dysplasia, joint subluxation, and dislocation. Multiple treatment options exist depending on when the DDH is identified and the reducibility of the hip on examination. Multiple structures can prevent closed reduction of the hip including the inverted labrum, inverted limbus, transverse acetabular ligament, the hip capsule constricted by the iliopsoas tendon, the pulvinar, and hypertrophied ligamentum teres. The limbus is a pathologic fibrous response to abnormal pressures superiorly within the acetabulum. The pulvinar is fibro-fatty tissue that can form in the dysplastic acetabulum.

*Studer et al. retrospectively reviewed MRIs of patients who underwent closed reduction between 2009 and 2014 to correlate MRI findings with persistent subluxation after reduction.* *A total of 38 hips were included, with 8 showing persistent subluxation after closed reduction.* **They found** that the labrum, ligamentum teres, and the hypertrophic cartilage of the acetabular roof are the most important structures preventing a complete reduction in DDH.

*Cotillo et al. retrospectively reviewed 34 hips that underwent open reduction for DDH evaluating radiographic parameters and arthrogram findings.* **They found** DDH hips had significantly higher acetabular indices (average 38 deg) and thicker acetabular floors (average 5mm) on preoperative radiographs compared to the normal contralateral side. **They also found** that preoperative arthrograms were accurate in evaluating the limbus and ligamentum teres, but less accurate in evaluating the transverse acetabular ligament.

Figure A through E demonstrates a left dysplastic hip arthrogram identifying structures around the hip. The order from A to E are the femoral head, psoas tendon, pulvinar, inverted limbus, and transverse acetabular ligament.

Illustration A shows a drawing that correlates with the arthrogram in Figures A-E, identifying the various structures involved.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: This is pointed at the femoral head.
Answer 2: This is pointed at the psoas tendon.
Answer 3: This is pointed at the pulvinar.
Answer 5: This is pointed at the transverse acetabular ligament.
