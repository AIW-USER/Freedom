## Question

Which of the following is the exam maneuver used to test the structure indicated by the white arrow in Figure A (deep to the structure being suspended)?

![[q210206-a-screen-shot-2018-10-26-at-4-56-32-pm.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Ober's test
2. Dial test
3. Silverskiold test
4. Thessaly test
5. Thomas test

## Correct option

**3. Silverskiold test**

## Answer explanation

The structure labeled by the white arrow is the lateral head of the gastrocnemius. The Silverskiold test is used to differentiate an achilles contracture from an isolated gastrocnemius contracture.

The diagnosis of gastrocnemius tightness is primarily clinical and is determined using the Silverskiold test. The Silverskiold test is performed by assessing the change in ankle dorsiflexion when the knee is extended and flexed. A positive Silverskiold test occurs when an equinus deformity is present when the knee is extended but disappears when the knee is flexed, with a difference in dorsiflexion of greater than 10 degrees considered to be significant for a gastrocnemius contracture. Isolated gastrocnemius contractures are a well-recognized symptom producer of various foot and ankle pathologies and its assessment should be a routine part of any patient evaluation. The treatment for a gastrocnemius contracture is a gastrocnemius recession.

*Barouk et al. report on the clinical diagnosis of gastrocnemius tightness, which is predominantly based on the Silverskiold maneuver.* *They add that the manner in which the Silverskiold test is performed must be consistent in the applied strength of the maneuver, the correction of a flexible hindfoot valgus deformity while performing the test, and reproducibility.*

*Hsu et al. describe the surgical management of a gastrocnemius contracture.* **They note** that gastrocnemius recession can be effectively used either as the sole treatment for remedying primary contracture or in conjunction with other reconstructive procedures as a means of aiding deformity correction and offloading related foot pathology.

Figure A is a cadaveric lateral sided knee dissection. The asterisk (*) is labeling the IT band, the structure distal to this is the LCL, the structure attaching to the posterior aspect of the fibula is the biceps femoris, the structure being suspended is the peroneal nerve, and the structure deep to this (white arrow) is the lateral head of the gastrocnemius.

Illustration A from Hsu et al. demonstrates how the Silverskiold test can commonly be improperly performed. Inset "A" illustrates how hindfoot eversion and midfoot break can be misinterpreted as ankle pseudodorsiflexion when the foot is not held in neutral alignment through a reduced talonavicular joint. Inset "B" demonstrates how locking the midfoot and hindfoot in neutral shows significant gastrocnemius contracture. Inset "C" depicts fifteen degrees of dorsiflexion correction after gastrocnemius release in the same patient.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: This is an exam maneuver for the IT band.
Answer 2: This is an exam maneuver to assess the posterolateral corner of the knee.
Answer 4: This is an exam maneuver for the meniscus.
Answer 5: This is an exam maneuver for a hip flexion contracture.
