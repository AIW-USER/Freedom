## Correct option

**4. Neoadjuvant chemotherapy**

## Answer explanation

Figures A to E are characteristic of intramedullary osteosarcoma (IOS). The most appropriate initial treatment would be 6-12 weeks of neoadjuvant chemotherapy.

Treatment for intramedullary osteosarcoma includes neoadjuvant chemotherapy, surgery (usually limb-sparing), followed by additional postoperative chemotherapy. The four most commonly used chemotherapy medications include adriamycin/doxorubicin (blocks DNA/RNA synthesis - inhibits topoisomerase II), cisplatin (disrupts DNA by covalent binding), high dose methotrexate (inhibits dihydrofolate reductase - inhibits DNA synthesis) and ifosfamide (DNA alkylating agent).

*Messerschmitt et al. reviewed IOS.* **They state** that absolute and relative contraindications for limb-salvage surgery include the inability to obtain wide surgical margins, neurovascular involvement, and pathologic fracture. Poor prognostic signs include metastases on presentation, primary tumors in the axial skeleton, large tumors, increased ALP or LDH levels, poor response to preoperative chemotherapy, skip lesions, and lymph node involvement.

*Luetke et al. reviewed the treatment of IOS.* Most IOS seen was high grade (80-90%). Only 10% arose in the axial skeleton (pelvis, most commonly). Axial tumors tended to occur in older patients, and had worse outcome than appendicular lesions. **They found** that multimodality treatment achieved 5 year survival of 60-70% in extremity-localized, non-metastatic disease. Local recurrence was 2-3% after amputation and 5-7% after limb-salvage

Figures A and B shows very subtle periosteal reaction along the medial epicondyle on the AP and lateral radiograph. Figure C shows an axial T1-weighted MR image of the distal femur. There is a lesion along the medial metaphysis, showing cortical destruction and associated soft tissue mass. Figure D shows a coronal T1-weighted MR image of the distal femur that identifies a soft tissue mass beneath the vastus medialis muscle. Figure E is histology slide showing pleomorphism and malignant osteoid consistent with high grade osteosarcoma.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1-3,5: Intramedullary osteosarcoma occurs most commonly around the knees of children and young adults. Most lesions are Enneking Stage IIB lesions (high-grade, extracompartmental, no metastases). Treatment involves multi-agent chemotherapy and limb-salvage resection and reconstruction. Radiation plays no role in standard treatment of osteosarcoma, although it may be used for palliative control of inoperable cases.
