5. 14-year-old female with the deformity shown in Figure E and a Cobb angle of 80 degrees

## Correct option

**4. 5-year-old male with a 15 degree deformity shown in Figure D that developed 8 months after fracture**

## Answer explanation

Answer 4 describes Cozen Phenomenon, a post-traumatic valgus deformity of the lower extremity. Observation is the main treatment for Cozen Phenomenon.

Cozen phenomenon is a valgus deformity that can occur in 50-90% of proximal tibial metaphyseal fractures in pediatric patients. It presents 5-15 months after injury and resolves by 3 years post-injury. The maximal deformity is usually around 18 months and can be close to 20 degrees in some patients. Surgical intervention is reserved for patients with significant deformities that are near skeletal maturity, as they will not be able to correct the deformity before they stop growing. Surgical treatment includes varus producing proximal tibial osteotomies and medial hemiepiphysiodesis.

*Tuten et al. followed 7 patients for an average of 15 years after proximal tibial metaphyseal fractures.* Maximum angulation was present an average of 13 months post-injury and averaged a metaphyseal-diaphyseal angle of 18 degrees. At final follow up this corrected to an average of 6 degrees. Tibiofemoral angle was 14 degrees and 4 degrees at maximal and final measurements, respectively. At final follow up the average knee score was 92 on the affected side, and 96 on the contralateral side. Five patients participated in strenuous activity, while 2 were limited to recreation activity due to knee pain.

*McCarthy et al. compared operative versus nonoperatively treated posttraumatic genu valgum.* *They had 10 treated nonoperatively and 5 treated with osteotomy in 4 patients and hemiepiphysiodesis in the last.* **They found** no significant difference in maximal angulation or final angulation between groups when looking at both complementary physeal-shaft and tibial-femoral angles. **They concluded** that nonoperative treatment has as good of outcomes as surgery for posttraumatic genu valgum.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Blounts often requires either bracing or surgical intervention to correct the deformity or prevent further deformity.
Answer 2: Supracondylar elbow fractures require immobilization for all types. Type II fracture should undergo closed reduction and percutaneous pinning per AAOS guidelines.
Answer 3: These should be treated with internal fixation, with a possible reduction, to prevent the progression of the deformity.
Answer 5: Adolescent idiopathic scoliosis often benefits from surgical intervention if the Cobb angle is greater than 50 degrees.
