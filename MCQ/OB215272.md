## Question

A 62-year-old man with a 3-month history of an enlarging medial thigh mass. The patient has had a bump in his medial thigh for as long as he can remember. He endorses bony prominences in other locations, however, they have not changed. Xrays taken a year ago are shown in Figure A. You discuss performing a biopsy for further workup. What is the most likely gene involved and what is the most likely histology on biopsy?

## Options

1. EXT; secondary high-grade chondrosarcoma
2. MHE; secondary low-grade chondrosarcoma
3. EXT; secondary low-grade chondrosarcoma
4. MHE; secondary high-grade chondrosarcoma
5. EXT; secondary high-grade osteosarcoma

## Correct option

**3. EXT; secondary low-grade chondrosarcoma**

## Answer explanation

The clinical presentation and imaging studies are most consistent for a patient with multiple hereditary exostoses. This disorder is due to a mutation in an EXT gene and can lead to secondary low-grade chondrosarcoma.

Multiple hereditary exostoses (MHE) is an autosomal dominant inherited disorder characterized by multiple osteochondromas that develop throughout the body. Solitary osteochondromas carry a <1% rate of malignant transformation, however, patients with MHE with genetic mutations in EXT1, EXT2, or EXT3 have an up to 10% rate of malignant transformation of osteochondromas. A cartilage cap of 2cm is associated with malignant transformation. Typically malignant transformation results in low-grade chondrosarcoma. This occurs most commonly in the fourth decade of life. The treatment for this is the same as typical chondrosarcoma.

*Stieber et al review the genetics of MHE.* **They report** that MHE is an autosomal dominant disorder. Linkage analysis has implicated mutations in the EXT gene family, resulting in an error in the regulation of normal chondrocyte proliferation and maturation that leads to abnormal bone growth.

*Marco et al discuss the treatment of cartilage tumors.* **They report** that in intramedullary low-grade chondrosarcoma treatment ranges from intralesional excision with or without adjuvant therapy to wide excision. Intermediate- and high-grade chondrosarcomas are treated with wide excisions.

Figure A demonstrates characteristic radiographic features of MHE including multiple osteochondromas around both the proximal tibia and distal femur.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1-2,4-5: Multiple hereditary exostoses is caused by the EXT genes. Secondary malignancy is often a low-grade chondrosarcoma.
