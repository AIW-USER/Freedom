## Question

An 18-year-old female collegiate athlete sustains the injury seen in Figure A. The patient states that her father had a successful allograft reconstruction for a similar injury and would like to know if she could have the same procedure. Which of the following should be discussed with this patient regarding surgical reconstruction using an allograft?

![[q213012-a-acl-tear.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Increased rate of hamstring weakness
2. Lower immune reaction compared to autograft
3. Higher incidence of anterior knee pain
4. Shorter incorporation time
5. Increased risk of re-rupture

## Correct option

**5. Increased risk of re-rupture**

## Answer explanation

The use of an allograft reconstruction in younger athletes (10-19 years old) is associated with a higher risk of re-rupture.

Graft choice following ACL rupture should be decided based on individual patient factors and desires. Young patients may be at an unacceptably high risk of re-rupture with the use of allograft when compared to autograft. Graft re-rupture in young active patients commonly occurs within the first year following surgery.

*Ellis et al. looked at revision rates following bone-patellar-bone (BTB) autografts versus allografts in patients 18 years and younger.* **They found** the allograft group to be 15 times more likely to require a revision ACL reconstruction. **They conclude** there was no difference in functional, activity, or satisfaction scores when comparing groups however the allograft group had a higher failure rate all occurring inside of 1 year postoperatively.

*Pallis et al. reviewed the outcomes of 122 ACL reconstructions performed on military cadets.* **They found** a failure rate of 11%, 44%, and 13% in the BTB, allograft, and hamstring autograft groups, respectively. **They conclude** that autograft should be chosen when performing an ACL reconstruction in a young active patient.

*Engelman et al. reviewed 73 ACL reconstructions performed on adolescent patients at their institution.* **They found** a graft failure rate of 29% in the allograft group and 11% in the autograft group. **They conclude** autograft should be chosen when performing transphyseal ACL reconstruction in children and adolescents.

*Maletis et al. retrospectively reviewed a cohort from the Kaiser Permanente ACL registry to evaluate the risk of aseptic revision between BTB autografts and BPTB allografts.* **They found** a cumulative revision rate of 4.1% in the BTB allograft group compared to 1.7% in the BPTB autograft group. **They conclude** that surgeons and patients should be aware of the increased risk of revision when a BTB allograft is used for ACLR.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: A hamstring autograft may lead to residual hamstring weakness, although the clinical significance of this may be imperceptible.
Answer 2: Allografts are felt to cause a greater immune reaction compared to autograft.
Answer 3: Bone-patellar-bone autograft is associated with higher rates of anterior knee pain.
Answer 4: A BTB autograft is felt to have the shortest incorporation time due to the bone to bone interface.
