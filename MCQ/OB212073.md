## Question

A 62-year-old man presents to your clinic with persistent left thumb pain with daily activities. Radiographs demonstrate scaphotrapeziotrapezoidal arthritis. He fails a course of conservative management and opts to undergo surgical management. The patient follows up 2 years after surgery with wrist pain and instability. Radiographs reveal a scapholunate angle of 80 degrees. Which of the following procedures was most likely performed as the index operation?

## Options

1. Scaphotrapezialtrapezoidal arthrodesis
2. Trapezoid resection
3. Ligament reconstruction and tendon interposition
4. Distal scaphoid resection
5. 4-corner fusion

## Correct option

**4. Distal scaphoid resection**

## Answer explanation

A distal scaphoid resection for treatment of scaphotrapezial trapezoidal (STT) arthritis has been shown to be a risk factor for increased scapholunte (SL) angle and subsequent dorsal intercalated segment instability (DISI) deformity

Resection of the distal pole of the scaphoid eliminates the arthritic contact at the scaphotrapeziotrapezoid joint; however, it functionally shortens the scaphoid. Theoretically, the lunate is at equilibrium between the extension moment of the capitate and the triquetrum and the flexion moment of the scaphoid. Shortening the scaphoid allows the extension moment of the triquetrum to predominate, pulling the lunate into extension and creating a DISI deformity.

*Corbin and Warwick described an incidence of midcarpal instability in a 64 year old female who underwent distal scaphoid pole excision for STT arthritis.* **The authors noted** that at 9 months following the procedure, her right thumb base symptoms had completely resolved but she had developed severe mid-carpal pain which was exacerbated by forced hyperextension. The authors obtained repeat radiographs which demonstrated dorsal tilt and translation of the lunate and palmar tilt of the capitate, consistent with a DISI deformity. **The authors conclude** distal scaphoid pole excision can lead to severe midcarpal malalignment, requiring an intercarpal arthrodesis with an outcome that may be worse than for a STT fusion.

*Lluch et al. performed a review of the indications, operative technique and outcomes of resection arthroplasty for management of STT arthritis.* **They note** that the most severe complication of distal scaphoid pole excision is dorsal mid-carpal instability, characterized by severe rotation of the proximal row into DISI and dorsalcapitate subluxation. **They note** that it is important to rule out the existence of a dorsal mid-carpal instability, and if present, a partial fusion or a proximal row carpectomy should be performed in lieu of a distal scaphoid pole excision.

Illutration A is the PA wrist radiograph of a patient who underwent a distal pole of the scaphoid excision. Illustration B shows a lateral wrist radiograph of the same patient demonstrating a DISI deformity (illustration C shows the SL angle).

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1, 2, and 3: STT arthrodesis, trapezoid resection, and Ligament reconstruction and tendon interposition (LRTI) have not been shown to lead to DISI deformity since these procedures spare the SL ligament.
Answer 5: A 4-corner fusion involves the arthrodesis of the lunate, capitate, hamate, and triquetrum and would not lead to a DISI deformity.
