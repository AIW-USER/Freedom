## Question

A 90 years old male has come with long standing history of shoulder pain. The physical examination revealed a large lump on his right shoulder with restricted shoulder movements. MRI was done which depicted the classical Geyser sign. What is the diagnosis?

## Options

1. AC joint exostosis
2. AC joint cyst
3. SLAP tear type 4
4. Biceps tendon sheath GCT

## Correct option

**2. AC joint cyst**

## Answer explanation

Ans: B

AC JOINT CYSTS

Synovial cyst of the acromio-clavicular joint is an uncommon entity often associated with chronic rotator cuff disease. Precisely the cysts are divided into: -

Type 1 cysts – from AC joint chronic arthropathy

Type 2 cysts – secondary to chronic rotator cuff tears

Type 2 cysts are by and large the common variety. Elderly patients generally come with chronic shoulder pain and restricted mobility affecting their ADLs or at times because of the cosmetic concerns.

Mechanism: Chronic cuff tear leads to Superior migration of the humeral head. Constant contact between uprided head and inferior AC joint capsule leads to erosion of the latter. This is followed by fluid seepage into the AC joint and the surrounding subcutaneous tissue generating on MRI the popular Geyser sign (MRI/Arthrogram/USG).

MRI can reliably diagnose even the type of AC joint cyst.

Management depends on the cyst type: -

- Type 1 cysts should be surgically treated by excision and resection of the lateral clavicle.
- For type 2 cysts: -

For pain element is mild/ moderate and cuff seems reparable on MRI, Distal clavicular resection + cuff repair can be planned. But in case pain is severe and cuff seems irreparable on MRI, especially in elderly, its better to opt for Shoulder arthroplasty (RSA > Hemi).
