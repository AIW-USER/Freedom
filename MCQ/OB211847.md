## Question

Which of the following physical exam findings is indicative of a disease processes that contributes to the attritional failure of the spring ligament?

## Options

1. No firm end point with anterior translation of the foot with the ankle in 20 degrees of plantarflexion while stabilizing the distal tibia and cupping the heel
2. While supported on one leg, the patient is unable to raise the heel off the ground and stand on the toes
3. No correction of the hindfoot is noted while the patient stands with the lateral foot on a 3cm thick block
4. Subluxation and pain as the metatarsals are grasped and passively dorsiflexed and plantarflexed at the tarsometatarsal joint
5. No plantarflexion of the ankle is noted in a prone patient as the calf is squeezed

## Correct option

**2. While supported on one leg, the patient is unable to raise the heel off the ground and stand on the toes**

## Answer explanation

Adult acquired flatfoot deformity is commonly associated with attenuation of the spring ligament.

Inability to perform a single leg heel rise is one of the hallmark physical exam findings in a patient with posterior tibial tendon dysfunction (PTTD). With insufficiency of the posterior tibial tendon, patients develop attenuation of the medial longitudinal arch of the foot, including the calcaneonavicular ligament (spring ligament). Failure of these structures will result in plantar and medial migration of the talar head and flattening of the arch of the foot.

*Deland et al review adult acquired flatfoot deformity.* **They report** that spring ligament failure occurs in conjunction with the failing posterior tibial tendon. **They conclude** that with progression of the pes planovalgus deformity, patients will experience deformity and attenuation along the entire medial longitudinal arch.

*Haddad et al review the management and controversies of adult acquired flat foot deformity.* **They report** that Stage II deformity, in which the patient has swelling medially, an inability to do a single leg heel rise, and a passively correctable subtalar joint is the deformity most commonly requiring operative treatment. **They conclude** that treatment of this disease processes includes a medial calcaneal slide osteotomy, which realigns the hindfoot, improves the medial arch, and protects the FDL transfer.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: This describes an anterior drawer test, which is indicative of an ATFL injury.
Answer 3: The Coleman block test is used to assess cavovarus feet, which are not associated with attenuation of the spring ligament.
Answer 4: This describes the piano key sign, which may be used in the assessment of Lisfranc injuries.
Answer 5: The Thompson test is used to assist in the diagnosis of achilles rupture, which is not associated with the spring ligament.
