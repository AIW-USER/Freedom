## Question

Following free-handed instrumentation of pedicle screws in L3-5 during a posterior decompression and spinal fusion in a 49 year old male, clinical stimulation is utilized prior to obtaining radiographs. With the probe in the head of the right sided L3 pedicle screw a reading of 11 milliamps is recorded. What is the most likely explanation of this reading?

## Options

1. Hypoperfusion of the spinal cord
2. Incorrect placement of the probe
3. Incomplete decompression
4. Intact pedicle
5. Medial wall breach

## Correct option

**4. Intact pedicle**

## Answer explanation

Clinical stimulation is commonly used to evaluate the integrity of the pedicle following instrumentation. Thresholds >8 milliamps are consistent with an intact pedicle.

Triggered EMG (ie. clinical stimulation) is utilized to evaluate for medial wall breach following instrumentation of the pedicle. A monopolar probe is placed in the tulip head of the pedicle screw and progressive electrical stimulation is applied. A second needle is placed in the paraspinal musculature as the anode. The stimulation at which a sustained EMG response is recorded is considered the “threshold”. Lower levels suggest that the pedicle is breached resulting in earlier irritation of the nerve root. Levels >8 milliamps are obtained with an intact pedicle, whereas those <8 are encountered with breaching of the pedicle wall either medially or inferiorly (placing the exiting nerve root at risk).

*Laratta et al review neuromonitoring in spinal deformity surgery.* **They report** that tEMG may be used as an adjunct to evaluate pedicle breach following instrumentation with threshold levels of 6-15 milliamps being reported in the literature for intact pedicles. **They conclude**, that while this is useful tool, it should always be used in conjunction with careful palpation of the pedicle tract and radiograph evaluation.

Stecker reviews intraoperative monitoring for spinal surgery. He reports that pedicle screws with thresholds <10 milliamps should be inspected and that those measuring <5 milliamps are most likely malpositioned. He concludes that electrical stimulation as an adjunct facilitates proper screw placement in instrumented spinal fusions.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Spinal cord hypoperfusion would not result in a normal tEMG reading.
Answer 2: Probes should be placed on the screw tulip/head, as was done in this case.
Answer 3: tEMG is not used as a tool for evaluating adequacy of decompression.
Answer 5: Low thresholds, <8 milliamps are most consistent with pedicle breach, which may be medial or inferior.
