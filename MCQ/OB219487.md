## Question

A 12-year-old right-hand-dominant male presents to the clinic for evaluation of right shoulder pain. He reports extensive training to make his local Little League team, participating in competitive play year-round. Over the past month, he has noticed decreased pitch velocity and accuracy. On examination, there is tenderness over the proximal humerus. Imaging is obtained and demonstrated in Figure A. He subsequently refrains from pitching for one month and is cleared for gradual return to play, where he has been participating under the following requirements: utilization of a pitching coach, limiting pitch counts to 68 pitches per game, one game limit per week, and avoidance of breaking balls. Which of the following is considered a risk factor for recurrence in this patient?

![[q219487-a-ap-right-shoulder.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Pitch count restriction
2. Avoidance of breaking balls
3. Length of activity cessation
4. Number of games participated
5. Use of pitching coach

## Correct option

**3. Length of activity cessation**

## Answer explanation

This 12-year-old male pitcher is experiencing shoulder pain with physeal widening of the proximal humerus on plain radiographs, suggestive of proximal humerus epiphysiolysis (Little Leaguer's Shoulder). Pitchers should refrain from activity for typically two to three months before gradually returning to activity.

The mainstay of management for Little Leaguer's Shoulder is nonoperative, which includes cessation of activity for a minimum of three months followed by a gradual return to play coupled with physical therapy. Physical therapy focuses on both rotator cuff strengthening, as well as posterior capsular stretching to avoid recurrence. Patients can return to pitching as well, starting with low-velocity pitches spanning a short distance and progressing to longer distances and higher velocities. Additional ways to prevent recurrence include emphasizing appropriate pitching mechanics, avoidance of breaking balls, limiting pitch counts based on age, and participating in no more than two games per week.

*Fleisig et al. performed an observational study of 481 youth pitchers followed over 10 years with the endpoint being upper extremity injury necessitating surgery or retirement from baseball.* **The authors noted** a cumulative incidence of injury was 5.0%, while pitchers amassing more than 100 innings pitched within one year posed as the most significant risk factor for injury. **The authors suggest** paying close attention to the number of innings pitched within a season to prevent season- or career-ending injury.

*May and Bishop reviewed shoulder injuries commonly seen in the overhead sports athlete, which included the presentation, diagnosis, and management of various entities, including Little Leaguer Shoulder, shoulder instability, and superior labral anterior-posterior tears.* **The authors ultimately note** nonoperative means to be the mainstay of treatment, but surgical intervention is increasing secondary to young athletes competing at higher levels with increased intensity and duration.

Figure A is an AP radiograph demonstrating the widening of the proximal humerus physis.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: pitch count restriction in this patient is not considered a risk factor, as the recommendation is to remain under 68 pitches for his age group.
Answer 2: the avoidance of breaking balls is recommended in order to reduce risk for recurrence.
Answer 4: if the patient were to participate in more than two games per week, he would be considered at risk for recurrence.
Answer 5: the use of a pitching coach allows the pitcher to employ better pitching techniques, thereby reducing his risk for recurrence.
