## Question

A 43-year-old male sustained a left ankle injury 3 years ago. Radiographs at the time were negative and his pain improved over the next two months. However, for the last six months, he has developed persistent ankle pain with intermittent swelling. He has been treating his symptoms with physical therapy and anti-inflammatory medications with little effect. Physical examination elicits pain with ankle dorsiflexion and plantarflexion, although subtalar motion is normal. Figures A and B are radiographs of the left ankle. Figure C shows the corresponding MRI. What would be the next most appropriate step for treatment?

## Options

1. Total contact cast immobilization and nonweight-bearing for 6 weeks
2. Ankle arthrodesis
3. Open autologous chondrocyte implantation
4. Arthroscopic marrow stimulation
5. Ankle corticosteroid injection

## Correct option

**4. Arthroscopic marrow stimulation**

## Answer explanation

This patient presents with a chronic osteochondral lesion affecting the medial dome of the talus. The most appropriate treatment at this time would be arthroscopic marrow stimulation (eg. microfracture).

Osteochondral lesions of the talus are believed to occur after 2% to 6% of all ankle injuries. Patients with unexplained chronic ankle pain have been shown to have osteochondral lesions in 81% of cases. Treatment initially is non-operative with immobilization, anti-inflammatories and physical therapy. Nonoperative treatment has a reported success rate of 45% to 50%. Persistent symptoms are treated operatively with better results. Arthroscopic procedures with fragment excision and bone marrow stimulation has been shown to have a 87% success rate.

*Cuttica et al. retrospectively reviewed the clinical results of 130 patients treated with arthroscopic marrow stimulation for talus OCD lesions.* **They showed** that arthroscopic debridement and drilling of larger fragments had worse outcomes than smaller lesions.

*Choi et al. looked at a cohort study of 173 patients with osteochondral lesion of the talus.* **They showed** that age (<20, 20-29, 30-39, 40-49, 50-59, and =60 years) was not a predictor of clinical outcome.

Figure A and B show a OCD lesion affecting the medial dome of the talus. Figure C shows selected coronal T2 and sagittal T1 MRI images of the left ankle. There is a small defect (measuring <1 cm diameter) affecting the medial talar dome.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Total contact casting would be recommended in a patient with diabetes mellitus. Immobilization can be used in these patients acutely, however this patient has exhausted a period of non-operative treatment.
Answer 2: Ankle arthrodesis would not be recommended in this patient. There is no significant arthritis or deformity in the ankle joint.
Answer 3: Open autologous chondrocyte implantation would be considered in large OCD lesions. This is not considered a first-line treatment.
Answer 5: Ankle corticosteroid injections are not recommended in chronic OCD lesions. Improvement is inconsistent and typically short lasting.
