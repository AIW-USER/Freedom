## Question

A 78-year-old medically frail female underwent total elbow arthroplasty 8 years prior secondary to her rheumatoid arthritis. She now presents with increasing pain for the past 5 months. Her radiographs appear unchanged from 1 year ago. An aspiration is performed and reveals 25,000 WBCs. Microscopy of the cultured organism is depicted in Figure A. When discussing treatment options for this patient, which of the following is true?

![[q217372-a-staph-epi.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Lifelong suppression may be considered for this indolent infection
2. Incision and drainage with exchange of a polyethylene bearing should be considered due to the acute presentation
3. One-stage revision surgery will have an 80% chance of PJI eradication
4. Resection arthroplasty should be considered secondary to her medical comorbidities
5. Two-stage revision surgery should be completed 3 weeks from now

## Correct option

**4. Resection arthroplasty should be considered secondary to her medical comorbidities**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a periprosthetic joint infection (PJI) of her total elbow arthroplasty (TEA). Resection arthroplasty should be considered in those that are medically ill or those with inadequate bone stock.

TEA is an increasingly used motion-preserving modality for the treatment of many debilitating elbow pathologies. The primary indications include rheumatoid arthritis, post-traumatic arthritis, and intra-articular distal humeral fractures in the elderly with poor bony quality. Acute infection (<30 days after surgery) may be treated with aggressive serial irrigation and debridement and antibiotic bead placement; success depends on the organism. Staphylococcus epidermidis is associated with a persistent infection because it is an encapsulating organism, and it is best treated with implant removal and IV antibiotic. Chronic infections should be treated with two-staged reimplantation versus resection arthroplasty in medically ill patients or those with inadequate bone stock.

*Zmistowski et al. reviewed periprosthetic joint infection (PJI) with TEA.* **They reported** that two-stage revision results in a decreased rate of recurrent PJI. **They concluded** that certain patients (those with poor health or well-fixed components) may be more suitable for I&D and component retention, with a demonstrated 50% success rate over a period of 3 years.

*Spormann et al. reviewed the outcome of different surgical procedures (debridement and retention vs 1- or 2-stage exchange) after primary TEA.* **They reported** that in the group with early infections (n = 9), 8 cases were treated by irrigation and debridement and 1 case was treated by a 2-stage exchange without recurrence of infection. **They noted** that the delayed infection was treated by 1-stage exchange without recurrence of infection, and late infections (n = 10), 3 cases presented recurrence of infection after debridement and irrigation and eradication of infection could be achieved by staged revision and in 3 cases by debridement. **They concluded** that both debridement with retention and staged reimplantation are highly successful for appropriate indications, and staged revisions are successful even against biofilm-active microorganisms, but a prosthesis-free interval of at least 3 months is recommended.

*Yamaguchi et al. reviewed their experience with the treatment of twenty-five infections after TEA and examined indications for salvage of the prosthesis compared with those for resection arthroplasty.* **They reported** that salvage of the prosthesis with extensive irrigation and debridement in the presence of an infection about the elbow can be reasonably successful if the infecting organism is not Staphylococcus epidermidis and if the components are well fixed. **They concluded** that when removal of the components is warranted, staged reimplantation can also be highly successful when the infecting organism is not Staphylococcus epidermidis. However, resection arthroplasty remains the procedure of choice in medically frail patients or in patients for whom function of the elbow is less of a concern.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Lifelong antibiotic suppression is an option for indolent infections such as C. acnes but should not be considered with a more virulent organism, especially in the setting of loose TEA components
Answer 2: Incision and drainage with exchange of a polyethylene bearing can be considered in an acute postoperative infection, within 6 weeks of the primary surgery, if components are stable. This patient does not have an acute presentation
Answer 3: One-stage revision surgery has been found to only result in a 50% success rate in PJI eradication. There is not sufficient data to support one-stage TEA revision surgery
Answer 5: Two-stage revision surgery should be completed much later than 3 weeks after a Staph epidermidis confirmed PJI; likely performed 3-months later
