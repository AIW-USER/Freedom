## Question

A 33-year-old social media influencer presents to the Emergency Department after a fall from a cliff while attempting to take a selfie. Injury radiographs demonstrate multiple fractures of the foot and ankle warranting surgical intervention. Which of the following approaches to the foot and ankle place the sural nerve at greatest risk for iatrogenic injury?

## Options

1. Direct lateral
2. Anterolateral
3. Sinus tarsi
4. Posteromedial
5. Achilles tendon-splitting

## Correct option

**3. Sinus tarsi**

## Answer explanation

The surgical approach with the highest risk of iatrogenic sural nerve injury is the sinus tarsi approach used for calcaneus fractures.

The sinus tarsi approach is a commonly used minimally invasive technique to surgically address calcaneus fractures. This approach is described as an alternative to the more invasive extensile lateral approach which has a high complication profile that include high rates of wound healing complications. The sinus tarsi approach is a laterally based approach is just anterior and distal to the fibula avoiding the peroneal tendons, but placing the sural nerve at risk of injury. Care during the approach must be taken to avoid iatrogenic injury to the sural nerve, which should be identified and protected inferiorly. Damage to the sural nerve can lead to a painful neuroma and lateral forefoot numbness.

*Kikuchi et al discuss utilization of the sinus tarsi approach for calcaneus fractures.* **They report** an overall superficial infection rate of 13.6% with statistically significant improvements in bony alignment and restoration of Böhler’s angle. **They advocate** for its use as a safe and useful alternative to the extensile lateral approach with anecdotally less soft tissue swelling, postoperative pain, and stiffness.

*Schepers et al compare the sinus tarsi approach to the extensile lateral approach in the treatment of calcaneus fractures.* The sinus tarsi approach was found to be superior with a significant reduction in wound complications and operative time without compromising the quality of the reduction.

Incorrect Answers
Answer 1: The superficial peroneal nerve(SPN) is most at risk in the direct distal fibula approach as it course anterior to posterior approximately 10-15cm proximal to the tip of the lateral malleolus.
Answer 2: The superficial peroneal nerve(SPN) is most at risk in the anterolateral approach to the ankle as it crosses the surgical site immediately under the skin.
Answer 4: The posteromedial approach can endanger the posterior tibial artery/vein as well as the tibial nerve.
Answer 5: The posterior achilles tendon-splitting approach does not directly place the sural nerve at risk.
