## Question

A 35-year-old male is involved in a motor vehicle accident and presents to the trauma bay with bilateral upper and lower extremity weakness and paresthesias. He is found to have a cervical spine injury that the treating surgeon chooses an anterior corpectomy and fusion with anterior instrumentation to decompress and stabilize the cervical spine. ## Correct option

**3. C**

## Answer explanation

Figure C depicts a flexion teardrop fracture at C6 with spinal canal encroachment, which is best addressed with anterior corpectomy, fusion, and anterior instrumentation.

Subaxial cervical spine injuries can be the result of high-energy or low-energy mechanisms, which can lead to neurologic injuries resulting in permanent disability. Surgical treatment may be required depending on the fracture morphology, ligamentous injuries, and neurologic status of the patient. Flexion teardrop fractures are highly unstable injuries and are characterized by anterior column failure in flexion/compression and posterior column tension failure. The posterior portion of the vertebral body can posteriorly migrate into the canal leading to spinal cord compression. In these injuries, surgical treatment consists of anterior corpectomy, strut grafting, and instrumented fusion to allow for spinal cord decompression and stabilization.

*Vaccarro et al. presented the AOspine classification system for subaxial spine injuries and evaluated inter- and intraobserver reliability statistics.* The presented classification system was based on injury morphology, patient-specific modifiers, and neurologic status and reported an inter- and intraobserver reliability kappa statistic of 0.75 and 0.64, respectively. **They concluded** the classification system is highly reliable and provided a useful tool for communication between treatment providers.

*Gelb et al. performed a systematic review of subaxial spine injuries.* **They reported** decompression and stabilization with surgical fixation provided earlier recovery and faster mobility than nonsurgical care. **They concluded** both anterior and posterior stabilization are equally effective, but surgical approaches should be tailored to the patient's pathology and coexisting conditions.

Figure A is a sagittal T2 MRI of the cervical spine with atlantoaxial instability and spinal cord compression. Figure B is a lateral radiograph of the cervical spine with a bilateral facet dislocation at C5-6. Figure C is a Sagittal CT of the cervical spine with a flexion teardrop fracture at C6 and spinal canal encroachment from retropulsion of the vertebral body. Figure D is a sagittal T2 MRI of the cervical spine with spinal canal stenosis and myelomalacia at C4-5 and C5-6. Figure E is a sagittal CT of the cervical spine demonstrating a boney chance fracture through C7.

Incorrect answers
Answer 1: Figure A depicts atlantoaxial instability with spinal cord compression which is best addressed with posterior cervical fusion with decompression.
Answer 2: Figure B depicts a bilateral facet dislocation which should be addressed with an anterior reduction and fusion or posterior reduction and fusion with or without decompression.
Answer 4: Figure D depicts acute traumatic central cord syndrome with spinal cord edema and multilevel spondylosis. This can be treated either with an ACDF or posterior decompression and fusion.
Answer 5: Figure E is a chance fracture at C7 with predominantly bone involvement, which can be treated with halo vest immobilization or posterior cervical instrumented fusion.
