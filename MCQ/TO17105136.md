## Question

What does this lateral view of the knee show?

![[TO17105136-1.jpg]]

## Options

1. Bearing spin out in mobile bearing TKA
2. Femoral cam jump in posterior stabilized TKA
3. Knee dislocation in posterior stabilized TKA
4. Patellar component dislocation in mobile bearing TKA

## Correct option

**1. Bearing spin out in mobile bearing TKA**

## Answer explanation

Explanation: In the lateral view posterior stabilized knee will show outline of a Cam or post (as shown in the X ray below).

![[TO17105136-2.jpg]]

This is not seen in the lateral view of this patient. So it can’t be a posterior stabilized knee (PS Knee). This is a case of bearing spinout, a complication specific to mobile bearing knee. Femoral cam jump is a complication specific to PS knee (see Q. 36).

Bearing spinout: In mobile bearing knee when flexion gap is left too loose, on varus or valgus stress in flexed knee, flexion gap opens up, and tibia rotates behind femur. Femur then comes to rest in front of the tibial PE bearing and knee locks into spinout position.

![[TO17105136-3.jpg]]

Closed reduction maneuver: Under anesthesia the knee is positioned at 90 degrees of flexion and anterior drawer maneuver is performed. Knee is reduced with a clunk. Ultimate solution requires knee revision to address loose flexion gap.

Answer: 1
