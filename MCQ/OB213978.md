## Question

Figure A is the radiograph of a 22-year-old golfer who complains of palmar wrist pain after grounding his golf club during a tournament. On examination, he is also found to have paresthesia of the small and ring fingers. This injury may have a direct effect on the contents of Guyon's canal. All of the following form Guyon’s canal EXCEPT?

![[q213978-a-carpal-tunnel-view.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Capitate
2. Deep flexor retinaculum
3. Hypothenar muscles
4. Pisiform
5. Superficial palmar carpal ligament

## Correct option

**1. Capitate**

## Answer explanation

This patient’s presentation is consistent with a hook of the hamate fracture. The borders of Guyon’s canal include the superficial palmar carpal ligament, deep flexor retinaculum, hypothenar muscles, pisiform, pisohamate ligament, and the hook of hamate.

Hook of the hamate fractures are relatively rare, comprising approximately 2% of all carpal fractures. Club and racquet sports are a known risk factor for a hook of the hamate fracture. Fractures are most commonly seen in the nondominant hand during a swing, especially in baseball batters and ice hockey players. The deep branch of the ulnar nerve lies just under the hook of the hamate and the patient may complain of paresthesias in the ulnar nerve distribution. Figure A illustrates a carpal tunnel axial view with the wrist hyperextended, which allows for evaluation of the fracture. Advanced imaging such as CT or MRI are often required to confirm the diagnosis. Acute hook of the hamate fractures may be treated with immobilization for 6 weeks. Excision of the hamate fracture is recommended for chronic hook of the hamate fracture with non-union.

*Davis et al. review hook of the hamate anatomy.* *They report on imaging modalities used for clinical decision making for pathologic entities affecting the hook of the hamate.* **They conclude** that a thorough knowledge of the anatomy, imaging appearance, and clinical management of hook of the hamate abnormalities is important for radiologists in guiding the care of patients with ulnar-sided wrist symptoms.

*Kadar et al. retrospectively reviewed hook of hamate fractures in51 patients.* **They found** the clinical findings of hook of hamate tenderness had better sensitivity than carpal tunnel-view radiographs. **They reported** a nonunion rate of 0% and 24% in those patients treated operatively and non-operatively, respectively. **They concluded** that advanced imaging improved the time to diagnosis and treatment and nonunion is common in patients treated non-operatively.

Figure A is the carpal tunnel radiograph with the wrist maximally hyperextended revealing a fracture of the hook of the hamate

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 2-5: These are all components that make up the borders of Guyon’s canal. The roof is comprised of the superficial palmar carpal ligament. The floor is comprised of the deep flexor retinaculum and hypothenar muscles. The ulnar border is defined by the pisiform and pisohamate ligament. The radial border is defined by the hook of the hamate.
