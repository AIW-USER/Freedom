## Question

A 62-year-old male with a BMI of 29 is being evaluated for TKA. In discussion with the patient, he asks about the difference between modern cemented and modern cementless TKA. Which of the following is true of cementless TKA?

## Options

1. Longer operative times due to tibial preparation
2. Increased rates of femoral component loosening at 2 years
3. Decreased rate of perioperative DVT
4. Increased difference in preoperative and postoperative hemoglobin levels
5. Equivalent functional outcomes to cemented TKA

## Correct option

**5. Equivalent functional outcomes to cemented TKA**

## Answer explanation

When comparing modern cemented and modern cementless fixation methods for TKA no difference exists in functional outcomes between the two techniques.

Aseptic loosening is one of the leading causes for failure following conventional cemented TKA. Thus, new technologies and fixation strategies to improve biologic fixation continue to be investigated. The advent of porous metals with the ability for bony ingrowth have shown promising results as a fixation method in TKA. Advantages of cementless TKA in the right patient include biologic fixation that can strengthen, rather than loosen over time. The most recent randomized controlled trials show no difference in outcomes up to two years for cementless TKA as compared with their conventional cemented predecessors. While early results up to 2-years are very promising, longer term randomized controlled trials are needed to further evaluate clinical outcomes.

*Nam et al in a randomized controlled trial, reviewed cemented and cementless fixation of the same implant.* **They report** no difference in the rates of aseptic loosening or preoperative change in hemoglobin between groups. They do, however, report a shorter operative time with cementless techniques. **They conclude** that cementless fixation should continue to be evaluated as a fixation method for TKA.

*Fricka et al review the two year results of a level 1 study comparing cementless and cemented TKA.* **They report** that the KSS functional scores, Oxford scores, and self-reported questions for satisfaction were similar between the two groups. **They conclude** that these two fixation techniques showed equivalent survivorship at two years and should continue to be monitored in longer term studies.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Shorter operative times have been reported with the use of cementless components.
Answer 2: No difference in the rate of femoral component loosening has been appreciated between these two techniques.
Answer 3: DVT rates are equivalent between cemented and cementless fixation strategies.
Answer 4: Fixation technique has not been shown to relate to perioperative hemoglobin levels.
