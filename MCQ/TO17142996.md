## Question

A huge swelling of proximal and middle arm was evaluated clinically and radiologically using X-ray, CT scan and MRI. Clinically the axillary and radial nerve deficit was observed. The radiological picture denoted a malignant bone lesion in the proximal metaphysis and diaphysis of humerus. Biopsy revealed it to be osteosarcoma. The surgeon planned for the limb salvage surgery with endoprosthesis. But while going for excision of the tumor, the surgeon had to sacrifice the axillary and radial nerve. What should be the next step? (NEW QUE)

## Options

1. He may go for endoprosthesis as planned earlier
2. He should proceed for amputation
3. Amputation followed by fitting of an artificial limb
4. He should just excise the tumor and keep the limb flail

## Correct option

**1. He may go for endoprosthesis as planned earlier**

## Answer explanation

Ans: A

The best approach here will be to keep going ahead with the endoprosthesis as planned earlier.

- Regarding axillary nerve, it gets sacrificed in majority of cases so as to obtain desirable margins. In any sense, we are not expecting a great shoulder function as after proximal humerus resection cuff is gone, even sometimes a major part of deltoid goes with the margins.
- Regarding radial nerve, later stage tendon transfers can always be planned if patient survives; although one can keep option of repairing the nerve in the same sitting.
- For amputation, the indication will be spillage of tumor during surgery. It won’t be kept as a primary option here.
