## Question

A 37-year-old male presented to the Emergency Department after his autonomous vehicle failed to recognize a deer in the road resulting in a high-speed motor vehicle collision. He was found to have an isolated, open tibial shaft fracture as depicted in Figure A. Antibiotics were promptly given upon arrival and he was taken immediately to the operating room for a thorough debridement with internal stabilization. It is determined that the patient will require a soft tissue flap. When compared to definitive flap coverage <7 days after injury, undergoing definitive flap coverage >7 days after injury is associated with which of the following?

![[q215539-a-2-500x375.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Increased amputation rates
2. Increased deep vein thrombosis (DVT) rates
3. Decreased osteomyelitis rates
4. Decreased pneumonia rates
5. Equivalent complication rates

## Correct option

**1. Increased amputation rates**

## Answer explanation

The risk for amputation is significantly higher in those who have delayed flap coverage of an open tibial shaft fracture greater than 7 days after injury.

The tibia is the most common long bone fracture with up to a quarter of these fractures being open injuries. Due to its subcutaneous location, the management of these open fractures can be complex, with appropriate soft tissue coverage being one of the most important prognostic factors. Typically, a joint orthopedic and plastic surgery approach to these injuries is necessary for optimal outcomes.

*Pincus et al. retrospectively evaluated 672 adult patients who had an open tibial shaft fracture requiring a soft-tissue flap.* Delayed coverage >7 days was associated with a significant increase in complications to include deep infection, pneumonia, osteomyelitis, and amputation. **They found** no difference in deep vein thrombosis, pulmonary embolism, or deaths between groups. Furthermore, there was a 40% increased risk of complications for each additional week of delay.

*Gopal et al. retrospectively reviewed 84 patients who had sustained severe open tibial shaft fractures treated by an early soft tissue coverage protocol.* Most of the patients underwent soft tissue coverage with plastic surgery at the time of fracture fixation, known as the ‘fix and flap’ method. Ultimately, **they found** excellent union rates and low rates of infection with aggressive early management of these soft tissue injuries. The lowest rate of infection (3%) was achieved in the primary ‘fix and flap’ group. A delay in coverage of >72 hours was associated with most of the complications, including infection and amputation.

Figure A is the clinical picture of a Gustilo-Anderson 3B open tibial shaft fracture with extensive soft tissue damage and periosteal stripping requiring flap coverage.

Incorrect Answers
Answer 2: There is no difference in DVT rates between early (<7 days) and delayed (>7 days) flap coverage.
Answers 3-5: There is a significantly increased risk for osteomyelitis, pneumonia, and overall complications when flap coverage is delayed more than 7 days.
