## Question

Survival motor neuron protein deficiency leads to:

## Options

1. Progressive replacement of muscle tissue with fibrous and fatty tissue, causing calf pseudohypertrophy
2. Loss of anterior horn cells and progressive muscle weakness
3. Abnormal chondroid production by chondroblasts in the proliferative zone of the physis
4. Disturbances in cell metabolism with accumulation of sphingolipids in the liver, spleen and bone marrow
5. Fetal acetylcholine receptor inhibition and nonprogressive joint contractures

## Correct option

**2. Loss of anterior horn cells and progressive muscle weakness**

## Answer explanation

Spinal muscular atrophy (SMA) is caused by a deficiency in survival motor neuron proteins. Deficiency of these proteins leads to progressive loss of anterior horn cells and progressive muscle weakness in children.

Spinal muscular atrophy is an autosomal recessive disease characterized by a mutation in the survival motor neuron (SMN) gene. This mutation causes a lack of the survival motor neuron proteins. The severity of disease is directly related to the reduction of circulating levels of SMN proteins. For example, this can explain why SMA Type 1 produces lower levels of circulating SMN proteins, and also why it is considered a more severe disease compared to SMA types 2 and 3.

*Sucato et al. examined spine deformity in SMA.* The type of scoliosis in SMA is a long, c-shaped, right sided thoracolumbar curve which is more flexible and progresses more rapidly than an idiopathic curve. Surgery is recommended with curves >50 degrees.

*Mesfin et al. reviewed the orthopaedic manifestations of SMA.* Common orthopaedic issues include scoliosis (prevalence = 60%-95%), proximal muscle weakness, and hip subluxation/dislocation (prevalence = 29-62%). Scoliosis is often refractory to conservative treatment and may require growing rods in young children with progressive curves, or instrumented posterior spinal fusions in older patients. As a general rule curves should be surgically managed when > 40 degrees. In children < 9 year old use growing rods and children > 10 with curves >40 degrees should be managed with posterior spinal fusion.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: This describes Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy.
Answer 3: This describes Achondroplasia.
Answer 4: This describes Gaucher Disease.
Answer 5: This describes Arthrogryposis.
