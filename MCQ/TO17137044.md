Chapter: Trauma MCQs — Talus Fractures

## Question

Shepherd fracture refers to

## Options

1. Talar neck fracture
2. Lateral part of posterior process of talus
3. Medial part of posterior process of talus
4. Body of talus

## Correct option

**2. Lateral part of posterior process of talus**

## Answer explanation

The talus is a weight bearing bone, and in healthy subjects it is very resistant to traumas as it has very thick subchondral bone. Strong forces are required to produce talar fractures. The talus can be divided in three parts: a head, a neck, and a body. The body has three processes: posterior, lateral and medial process.

Fracture eponyms for talus fracture:

Aviator’s fracture: One of the first series of talar neck fractures were reported in pilots and parachutists of the Royal Air Force during the First World War, hence the term “aviator’s astragalus.

Shepherd and CeDell fracture: These are fractures of posterior process of body of talus. Shepherd fracture involves the lateral part of the posterior tubercle (so called “Shepherd fracture”); fractures of the medial part of the tubercle (CeDell fracture) are less frequent.

Answer: 2
