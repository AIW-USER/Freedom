## Question

A 66-year-old female with a past medical history of rheumatoid arthritis presents to the clinic for her 12-week follow-up after a total elbow arthroplasty. Continued drainage is noted from the mid-aspect of her incision with surrounding erythema. She undergoes arthrocentesis for suspected infection. Which organism with the following laboratory characteristics portends the worst prognosis for reimplantation?

## Options

1. Gram-positive cocci formed in clusters, catalase-positive, coagulase-positive
2. Gram-positive cocci formed in clusters, catalase-positive, coagulase-negative
3. Gram-positive cocci aligned in chains, alpha-hemolytic, optochin-sensitive
4. Gram-positive cocci aligned in chains, beta-hemolytic, bacitracin-sensitive
5. Gram-positive bacillus, anaerobic but aerotolerant, lactose-fermenting

## Correct option

**2. Gram-positive cocci formed in clusters, catalase-positive, coagulase-negative**

## Answer explanation

This 66-year-old's presentation is highly suggestive of an infected total elbow arthroplasty. Staphylococcus epidermidis, a gram-positive cocci formed in clusters that exhibit catalase-positive and coagulase-negative characteristics, portends the worst outcomes regarding reimplantation rates (Answer 2).

Total elbow arthroplasties (TEA) are rarely performed procedures, however, its overall incidence has been increasing secondary to expanding indications and improving functional outcomes, particularly following trauma (distal humerus fractures). Nevertheless, the potential for complications cannot be dismissed, as they have profound implications, particularly with infection, given the limited bone stock around the elbow. The complication occurs at a rate higher (3-5%) than the other more commonly performed total joint replacements, with the most commonly infecting agents being Staphylococcus aureus and Staphylococcus epidermidis. Increasing research is being performed on the outcomes in patients sustaining infections, with studies illustrating Staphylococcus epidermidis having the lowest reimplantation rates, believed to be secondary to the difficult eradication of the biofilm associated with the microorganism.

*Yamaguchi et al. performed a retrospective, single-institution review of 25 patients who sustained TEA infections and underwent one of three treatment regimens: 1) debridement with implant retention, 2) prosthesis removal with eventual reimplantation, and 3) resection arthroplasty.* Those infected with Staphylococcus epidermidis exhibit the worst success rates with debridements and implant retention, as well as explant removal and reimplantation. Moreover, no persistent infections were seen with resection arthroplasty. **The authors conclude** patients with Staphylococcus epidermidis infections are more likely to experience worse outcomes and believe resection arthroplasty remains a reasonable salvage procedure for those with chronic infections.

*Cheung et al. performed a single-institution retrospective review of 29 patients who sustained TEA infections and were subsequently treated with component explantation and eventual reimplantation.* *The mean reimplantation time was 1.4 years, with a mean follow-up of 7.4 years.* **The authors noted** significant improvements in functional outcomes following reimplantation compared to preoperatively. Staphylococcus epidermidis was found to be the most common infecting organism, which was closely followed by Staphylococcus aureus. Despite considerable increases in function, reoperation rates remain high at three years. **The authors also noted** the former to be most correlated with treatment failures, accounting for 50% of those unable to undergo reimplantation. **The authors conclude** that while two-stage surgery following infection can provide good functional outcomes, the process is fraught with complications and reoperations.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: This represents Staphylococcus aureus, which is the most common infecting agent in TEA
Answer 3: This represents Streptococcus pneumoniae, which is more commonly seen in pneumonia, osteomyelitis
Answer 4: This represents Streptococcus agalactiae ("group A strep"), which is classically associated with necrotizing soft tissue infections
Answer 5: This represents Cutibacterium acnes, the most common infecting organism in total shoulder arthroplasty, but not commonly seen in TEA
