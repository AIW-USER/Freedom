## Question

Which of the following would be the most appropriate treatment for a 1.6cm^2 displaced osteochondral lesion of the talar dome in a 33-year-old male?

## Options

1. Fragment excision
2. Marrow stimulation resulting in primarily type II collagen
3. Non-weight-bearing for 6 weeks
4. Osteochondral autograft transplantation of type II collagen
5. Retrograde drilling

## Correct option

**4. Osteochondral autograft transplantation of type II collagen**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a large osteochondral lesion (OCD). The most appropriate treatment listed above would be osteochondral autograft transplantation.

Focal injuries that occur to the talar dome with variable involvement of the subchondral bone and cartilage result in an osteochondral lesion of the talus. These may be caused by a traumatic event or be the result of repetitive microtrauma. They typically occur in either the medial or lateral talar domes. Those lesions within the medial talar dome usually have no history of trauma, are more posterior, and are often larger and deeper than lateral lesions. Conversely, lateral talar dome lesions usually have a traumatic history, are more superficial and smaller, are more central or anterior, have a lower incidence of spontaneous healing, and are more often displaced and symptomatic.

*Savage-Elliott et al. reviewed the treatments of OCDs of the talar dome.* **They reported** that conservative treatment yields best results in pediatric patients, while arthroscopic bone marrow stimulation is a common first-line treatment for smaller lesions in adults. Furthermore, **they noted** that autologous osteochondral transplantation is beneficial in larger, cystic lesions in adults.

*Flynn et al. reviewed the functional and MRI outcomes following the use of autologous osteochondral transplantation (AOT) to treat osteochondral lesions of the talus.* **They noted** that lesion size was negatively correlated with Magnetic Resonance Observation of Cartilage Repair Tissue (MOCART) score, and that functional outcomes were similar in patients irrespective of whether they had previous microfracture or concomitant procedures. **They concluded** that AOT was an effective treatment for large OCD lesions of the talus.

*Ahmad et al. prospectively evaluated and compared the long-term clinical and radiographic outcomes of using osteochondral autograft and allograft to manage either recurrent or large osteochondral lesions of the talar dome.* **They reported** that using fresh talar osteochondral allograft provided results that were comparable to the use of distal femoral osteochondral autograft for treating recurrent or large OCD lesions of the talus. **They concluded** that although the use of allograft avoided the risk of knee complications when harvesting autograft from the distal femur, fresh talar allograft may have lower healing rates than osteochondral autograft.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1&2: Arthroscopy with the removal of the loose fragment, debridement and marrow stimulation are indicated with chronic lesions, lesions <1 cm, or displaced smaller fragments with minimal bone on the osteochondral fragment (poor healing potential). Also, marrow stimulation results in fibrocartilage (type I collagen)
Answer 3: Immobilization and non-weight bearing is indicated in an acute injury with a nondisplaced fragment and incomplete fracture
Answer 5: Retrograde drilling is indicated with a lesion size >1 cm and an intact cartilage cap
