## Question

A 58-year-old diabetic caucasian male presents to the clinic for continued management of his left ring finger contracture. He had previously attempted physical therapy, with no improvement in his motion. On examination, there is a metacarpophalangeal joint flexion contracture of 35 degrees with a palpable nodule of tissue felt in the palm. He is adamant about avoiding surgery. One alternative treatment to surgery works through a mechanism of action of selective degredation of

## Options

1. Type I and III collagen
2. Type II and IV collagen
3. Type I collagen only
4. Type II collagen only
5. Type X collagen

## Correct option

**1. Type I and III collagen**

## Answer explanation

This 58-year-old male exhibits a metacarpophalangeal flexion contracture stemming from Dupuytren's disease, which is recalcitrant to physical therapy. Short of surgical intervention, the most appropriate treatment is collagenase injections, which target collagen types I and III (Answer 1).

Dupuytren's disease is characterized by the formation of pathologic cords secondary to myofibroblast proliferation in the early (proliferative) stage, which is followed by the deposition of type III collagen (involution stage) before transitioning to predominantly type I collagen (residual stage; Illustration A). The delineation of this process allowed for the identification and production of collagenase clostridium histolyticum injections. This therapy includes the harvestation of collagenases from clostridium histolyticum, which selectively degrade collagen types I and III. Additionally, the injection has low activity against collagen type IV, which is most prominent in the basement membrane of blood vessels and nerves, which is why neurovascular-related complications are uncommon. While this nonoperative therapy sufficiently improves contracture severity, other non-operative treatments, and in particular, needle aponeurotomy, have illustrated comparable results and remains a viable alternative.

*Lambi et al. reviewed the natural history, pathophysiology, and pharmacotherapies associated with Dupuytren's disease.* **They highlighted** transforming growth factor-beta as integral in developing fibrous tissue following an innocuous injury. The authors then discussed the various pharmacotherapies, but **they noted** that collagenase injections are the only pharmacotherapy that has demonstrated lasting effects when treating Dupuytren's disease. **They concluded** that while collagenase injections improve deformity, nearly 50% require adjuvant therapies to halt the pathologic process fully.

*Miyoshi et al. report on the biological response to Vibrio vulnificus metalloprotease by injecting the protease into guinea pigs and measuring the amount of hemorrhage and vascular permeability enhancement following injection.* **The authors found** vibrio metalloprotease to confer the largest hemorrhagic area and vascular permeability, while collagenase portended no hemorrhage or increased permeability. **The authors conclude** that vibro metalloprotease exerts its effect by degradation of the basement membrane/type IV collagen.

Illustration A is a diagram from the Lambi study demonstrating the pathologic cord formation classically seen in Dupuytren's disease.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 2-5: Collagenase injections target collagen types I and III, with minimal activity against types II and IV. Type X is collagen produced by osteoarthritis chondrocytes.
