## Question

A 33-year-old male fell while backpacking in the mountains and sustained a fracture of his right clavicle. The patient undergoes operative fixation with a single 3.5mm Low Contact Dynamic Compression Plate (LC-DCP) placed superiorly on the clavicle. Five months after surgery he has continued pain at the fracture site and he is diagnosed with a symptomatic hypertrophic nonunion of the clavicle. He undergoes an uneventful nonunion repair with dual minifragment plating and goes on to union. Which of the following statements is true regarding midshaft clavicle fractures?

## Options

1. Infection is the most common cause for reoperations
2. Nonoperative fixation leads to improved initial outcomes as compared to operative management
3. Dual mini frag plate fixation is biomechanically superior to single superior plate fixation
4. Nonoperative management with figure-of-eight bracing has better functional outcomes compared to sling use
5. Augmentation with iliac crest bone autograft has similar union rates with local bone autograft

## Correct option

**5. Augmentation with iliac crest bone autograft has similar union rates with local bone autograft**

## Answer explanation

Symptomatic nonunion of the clavicle is a known complication following nonoperative and operative management of the midshaft clavicle fractures. There are similar union rates after nonunion repair when augmented with local or distant bone autograft.

When indicated, operative fixation of the clavicle reduces the risk of nonunion to approximately 2.5%. Consistent risk factors for nonunion are advanced age, female gender, comminution, and shortening >15-20mm. When a nonunion following plate fixation is diagnosed there are two broad considerations to consider: biologic and mechanical. The biologic reasons for failure are related to those variables that compromise the vascularity of the fracture site. These are most commonly the surrounding soft-tissue injury, one or more surgical approaches, infection, or systemic diseases. The mechanical reasons for failure are related to malreduction, inadequate plate strength or length, or inappropriate construct stiffness. Traditionally, the iliac crest has been used for autogenous bone grafting because of the large volume of progenitor cells, and access to both cancellous and cortical bone. However, there is well-known morbidity related to it. The use of local bone graft has been shown to be a viable alternative without negatively affecting the union rate.

*Rosenberg et al. published an article on the disability caused by nonunion or malunion of midshaft clavicle fractures.* *They evaluated the functional outcome in 13 patients who were treated with nonunion repair with bone autograft augmentation.* *The mean follow-up was 41 months.* *Satisfactory osseous union was achieved in 13/13 patients yet only 46% of the patients returned to their previous professional and recreational activities.* Although solid union after nonunion repair of midshaft clavicle fractures is predictable, patients can remain functionally impaired.

*Endrizzi et al. completed a retrospective analysis of forty-seven consecutive patients that underwent superior plating for clavicular nonunion between 1995 and 2005 to assess the efficacy of plate fixation and the need for iliac crest bone grafting.* *Of the patients, 30 were treated with local bone graft only, 14 with demineralized bone matrix, and 3 with distant autogenous bone graft.* *Of the fractures, 93% united after a single operation.* **The authors concluded** that distant autogenous bone graft is not necessary to obtain union in most cases.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Primary implant removal is the most common reason for reoperation.
Answer 2: When an osseous union is achieved, the most conclusive evidence for improved outcomes for operative treatment is seen in the first 6 months after surgery. After 6 months the results are mixed with the majority of findings demonstrated improved radiographic and functional outcomes in favor of operative fixation.
Answer 3: Dual small plate fixation of a midshaft clavicle fracture is biomechanically equivalent to a single large plate superior fixation.
Answer 4: There are similar functional outcomes with nonoperative management using either a sling or figure-of-eight brace.
