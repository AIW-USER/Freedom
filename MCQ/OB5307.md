## Question

A concerned mother brings her 7-year-old boy to the Emergency Room stating that he had a fever at home that morning to 101.5 degrees Farenheit and is refusing to walk on his left leg. He undergoes a full workup in the hospital and is found to have a white blood cell count (WBC) of 11 and an erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR) of 18. What is the percent chance this patient has septic arthritis from the Kocher criteria?

## Options

1. 0-9%
2. 10-25%
3. 26-50%
4. 51-75%
5. > 76%

## Correct option

**3. 26-50%**

## Answer explanation

The patient has a positive history of fever and inability to bear weight, but a WBC < 12 and ESR <40. Therefore he has 2 out of 4 Kocher Criteria which is associated with a 40% prediction probability of having septic arthritis.

The Kocher Criteria emerged from a study to help distinguish septic arthritis from transient synovitis in children with hip symptoms. The four independent variables associated with an increased risk of septic arthritis were inability to bear weight, history of fever >101.3 F or 38.5 C, WBC > 12, and ESR > 40. Based on this original data set in the 1999 publication, the predicted probability of septic arthritis for patients with 0, 1, 2, 3, or all 4 of these findings was 0.2%, 3%, 40%, 93.1% and 99.6% respectively.

*Kocher et al. then conducted a validation study published in 2004 in which they validated the original Kocher criteria in a new patient sample, though at the same institution.* *After a study of 154 patients total,* **they concluded** that the prediction algorithm remained a useful, now validated tool, though they note an ROC statistic of 0.86 in the new sample, slightly less than the 0.96 in the original sample.

*Nguyen et al. in 2017 conducted an analysis of the Kocher criteria as applied to the diagnosis of osteomyelitis over 71 patients with at least 3 positive Kocher criteria findings at a different institution, now using MRI as a gold standard.* These patients had a 23% rate of septic arthritis, but a 48% rate of osteomyelitis, and recommend that even in the setting of a hip effusion and positive Kocher criteria, one consider MRI to evaluate for osteomyelitis before treating septic arthritis alone.

Incorrect Answers:
The original Kocher Criteria studies demonstrate a 40% predictive probability of septic arthritis if 2 of the criteria are positive. Answers 1, 2 ranges are too low. Answers 4, 5 ranges too high.
