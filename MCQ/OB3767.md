## Question

Which of the following clinical presentations is most consistent with an anterior cord syndrome?

## Options

1. An 85-year-old male with peripheral vascular disease presents with acute onset of unilateral loss of proprioceptive and vibratory sensation in his right lower extremity.
2. An 76-year old female falls from ground height and strikes her forehead on a bathtub. She presents with significant weakness in her bilateral upper extremities, specifically loss of hand grip strength. Her lower extremity motor function is 3+/5 in all distributions, and her sensory perception is unaltered.
3. A 27-year-old male sustains a stab injury to the left side of his thoracic spine and presents with motor loss to his left lower extremity and loss of temperature sensation to his right lower extremity.
4. A 64-year-old man undergoes repair of a thoracoabdominal aortic aneurysm. Post-operatively he has complete loss of motor function as well as loss of temperature and light touch sensation below his waist. His upper extremity function is preserved.
5. A 26-year old make presents with a fracture dislocation of the cervical spine. He has 0 of 5 strength in his lower extremities, complete sensory loss in the peri-anal area, his bulbocavernousus reflex is intact.

## Correct option

**4. A 64-year-old man undergoes repair of a thoracoabdominal aortic aneurysm. Post-operatively he has complete loss of motor function as well as loss of temperature and light touch sensation below his waist. His upper extremity function is preserved.**

## Answer explanation

Answer 4 demonstrates a patient who likely had an ischemic spinal cord injury during a vascular procedure. The result is infarction of the anterior two-thirds of the spinal cord, resulting in loss of caudal motor function and light touch/temperature sensation.

Anterior cord syndrome presents as clinical syndrome of bilateral motor and light touch/temperature losses caudal to the region of injury. It is the result of traumatic anterior cord compression as well as cord ischemia after an insult to the anterior spinal artery. The vascular supply to the anterior two-thirds of the spinal cord is delivered via the descending aorta through a plexus of segmental arteries (including the artery of Adamkiewicz) which anastomose dorsally as the anterior spinal artery. Insult to this vascular supply will yield injury to the caudal bilateral lateral corticospinal tracts and lateral spinothalamic tracts, causing dysfunction in motor function as well as pain/temperature sensation. The dorsal column function (proprioception) is preserved.

*Wan et. al. reviewed the prevention of ischemic cord injury during thoracoabdominal aortic surgery.* **They report** options including minimizing aortic cross-clamp time, use of intra-operative motor-evoked potential cord monitoring, draining CSF to reduce intra-thecal pressure, and hypothermia as possible modalities to prevent cord injury during these procedures.

*Gupta et. al. reviewed the management of spinal cord injury.* **They report** that successful acute management requires multi-disciplinary care, with attention to medical management of blood pressure to prevent secondary injury due to cord hypoperfusion. Additional interventions such as therapeutic hypothermia, pharmacologic therapies, cellular transplantation, peripheral nerve micro grafts, and stem cell therapies have all demonstrated variable clinical efficacy for cord injury.

*Harrop et. al. reviewed the management and outcomes of central cord syndrome.* This incomplete cord injury presents as motor impairment in the upper more than the lower extremities, bladder dysfunction, and variable sensory loss below the level of the injury. It is typically due to hyperextension injury in a spondylotic cervical spine. Early surgical decompression and stabilization may provide improved motor recovery in some patients.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Clinical presentation is consistent with Posterior Cord Syndrome.
Answer 2: Clinical presentation is consistent with Central Cord Syndrome.
Answer 3: Clinical presentation is consistent with Brown-Sequard Syndrome.
Answer 5: Clinical presentation is consistent with a complete spinal cord injury.
