## Question

Figure A is an MRI of a 42-year-old male who presents to your office with complaints of elbow pain. He was recently diagnosed with inflammatory arthritis. He has failed an extensive course of nonoperative management and is interested in proceeding with surgical intervention. On examination, he has relatively preserved motion of the elbow, with pain in the elbow, forearm and wrist. He demonstrates ipsilateral wrist and thumb extension weakness. No instability of the elbow is appreciated. Which of the following would be the most appropriate treatment for this patient?

![[q217971-a-elbow-mri2.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Ulnohumeral interpostion arthroplasty
2. Non-operative management with pharmaceutical treatment alone
3. Synovectomy with possible radial head excision
4. Semi-constrained total elbow arthroplasty
5. Unconstrained total elbow arthroplasty

## Correct option

**3. Synovectomy with possible radial head excision**

## Answer explanation

This patient has inflammatory arthritis with posterior interosseous nerve compression secondary to radial head synovitis. A synovectomy with possible radial head excision would likely be of benefit to this patient.

Elbow arthritis is a degenerative joint disease of the elbow that can be broken into three main types: osteoarthritis, post-traumatic arthritis and inflammatory arthritis. Diagnosis can be made with plain radiographs of the elbow. Treatment can be nonoperative or operative depending on patient activity demands, the severity of elbow pain, and degree of elbow dysfunction. Arthroscopic or open synovectomy is indicated when there is pain without instability and no significant loss of ROM. Synovectomy and radial head excision is indicated when the focus of degeneration is in the radiohumeral joint and/or posterior interosseous nerve compression is occurring secondary to radial head synovitis. Interposition arthroplasty is indicated in young active patients who are not candidates for TEA.

*Sanchez-Sotelo et al. reviewed elbow arthritis in patients with rheumatoid arthritis.* **They report** that the development of newer pharmacologic agents and the adoption of early therapeutic interventions have translated into milder forms of polyarticular destruction. **They reported** that arthroscopic synovectomy has become the procedure of choice for rheumatoid patients with uncontrolled symptoms, while patients with more advanced joint destruction can be considered for elbow arthroplasty. **They concluded** that interposition arthroplasty may be considered for patients interested in avoiding the potential complications of elbow arthroplasty.

*Kang et al. reviewed the clinical outcomes of arthroscopic synovectomy in patients with rheumatoid elbow.* **They reported** that arthroscopic synovectomy of the elbow by use of multiple portals is a technically feasible procedure. **They concluded** that it could effectively relieve pain, increase range of motion, improve Mayo Elbow Performance Score, and delay radiologic progression in rheumatoid elbows, resulting in a high satisfaction rate. However, recurrent synovitis occurs in some patients.

Figure A is an elbow MRI demonstrating significant synovitis of the elbow. Illustration A depicts intraoperative images of an arthroscopic debridement and synovectomy.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: An ulnohumeral only interposition arthroplasty would not be indicated in this patient with primarily radial-sided symptoms. An Achilles interposition arthroplasty could be considered in young active patients who are not candidates for TEA.
Answer 2: While medical management would likely improve his symptoms, he has failed extensive non-operative management with significant synovitis and preserved joint cartilage on MRI. Performing synovectomy may help subside progressive joint destruction. Again, arthroscopic or open synovectomy is indicated when there is pain without instability and no significant loss of ROM.
Answer 4: Semi-constrained TEA is ideally reserved for elderly patients with end-stage painful elbow arthritis with or without evidence of instability.
Answer 5: Unconstrained TEA is reserved for end-stage arthritis with no evidence of elbow instability. Use in inflammatory arthritis is generally considered a contraindication to use of an unconstrained TEA.
