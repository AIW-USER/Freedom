## Question

A 50-year-old male laborer with a past medical history of major depressive disorder, anxiety and diabetes falls from a ladder, sustaining a right open tibial shaft fracture and an associated small subdural hematoma. Physical examination reveals a ten centimeter open wound over the fracture site. He is appropriately treated for his open fracture in the Emergency Department and admitted to the intensive care unit for neuromonitoring. Eventually, his fracture is definitively fixed in the operating room with irrigation and debridement, followed by tibial intramedullary nailing. Which of the following factors contributes to the development of depressive symptoms in this orthopedic trauma patient?

## Options

1. Employment at time of injury
2. Intramedullary nailing versus open reduction internal fixation
3. Male gender
4. Previous psychiatric diagnosis
5. Private insurance

## Correct option

**4. Previous psychiatric diagnosis**

## Answer explanation

A history of psychiatric diagnosis is a significant risk factor for the development of depressive symptoms at injury or at 9 months for orthopaedic trauma patients.

Recovery and outcomes in orthopedic trauma patients are significantly influenced by psychological health, particularly depression and anxiety. Studies have shown that pre-existing or post-injury depression can negatively impact functional recovery, pain levels, and overall rehabilitation success. Psychological distress not only prolongs recovery but also affects patients' ability to engage in rehabilitation, highlighting the need for integrated mental health support. Addressing these psychological factors through early screening and intervention can improve both physical and mental recovery, leading to better long-term outcomes.

*Becher et al.* *(2014) examined the relationship between orthopedic trauma and depression in a prospective cohort study.* **They find** that a significant proportion of trauma patients experience depressive symptoms, which can negatively impact recovery and functional outcomes. The study highlights the importance of early psychological screening and intervention to improve post-injury rehabilitation.

*Haupt et al.* (2018) investigated the role of pre-injury depression and anxiety in orthopedic trauma patients and their treatment outcomes. **Their findings suggest** that patients with pre-existing mental health conditions have worse postoperative pain, functional recovery, and overall prognosis. The study emphasizes the need for integrated mental health care in orthopedic trauma management.

*Schemitsch & Nauth (2020) reviewed the impact of psychological factors on trauma recovery,* **emphasizing** the influence of stress, anxiety, and depression on healing and rehabilitation. They discuss the bidirectional relationship between mental health and physical recovery, advocating for a multidisciplinary approach that includes psychological support. The article underscores the importance of addressing mental health to optimize patient outcomes after trauma.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Unemployment contributes to the development of depressive symptoms, not employment at the time of injury.
Answer 2 & 3: There is no evidence regarding intramedullary nailing versus open reduction internal fixation or gender for open tibia fractures regarding the development of depression.
Answer 5: Private insurance does not predispose patients to depression.
