## Question

A 4-year-old child presents with a short forearm and a hand deformity. The child is otherwise healthy with no congenital defects. The deformity of the forearm is shown. The child has a thumb, but it is in an abnormal position. In which of the given scenario will Pollicization be the treatment of choice?

![[TO17905803-1.jpg]]

## Options

1. Blauth Type 3A, Stiff index finger
2. Blauth Type 3A, With flexible index finger
3. Blauth Type 3B, Stiff index finger
4. Blauth Type 3B, With flexible index finger

## Correct option

**4. Blauth Type 3B, With flexible index finger**

## Answer explanation

Radial club hand / Radial dysplasia

Malformation with longitudinal failure of formation of parts along the pre-axial or radial border of the upper extremity.

ETIOLOGY

- Genetics – likely related to Sonic hedgehog gene
- Thalidomide, valproic acid, Phenobarbital toxicity
- Genetic and Environmental factors
- Associated with other malformation syndromes –

Holt Oram syndrome – mutation on chromosome 12 at TXBS gene

Presentation –

- 75% have congenital heart problems- most common defects are ASD, VSD
- Absent radial bone in forearm
- Heart block
- Unequal upper arm bones
- Missing thumb
- Abnormality in collar bone or shoulder blade

TAR syndrome –

Characterized by

Thrombocytopenia – platelet deficiency

Absent radial bone in each forearm

Fanconi Anemia –

Rare genetic disease resulting in impaired response to DNA damage.

Characterized by bone marrow failure, AML, solid tumors and developmental abnormalities.

Classical features are

- Cafe au lait spots

- Microcephaly, triangular face

- Abnormal kidneys

- Absent radii

- Short stature

- Abnormal thumbs

- Decreased fertility

- Abnormal blood picture – Elevated MCV, Low platelet count

VATER syndrome

Features are

- Abnormalities of vertebrae
- Anal atresia
- Tracheo-oesophageal fistula
- Esophageal atresia
- Radial anomalies
- Renal anomalies

VACTERL syndrome

- Vertebral anomalies - hypoplastic vertebrae or hemi vertebrae
- Anorectal malformations
- Cardiovascular anomalies
- Tracheo esophageal fistula
- Esophageal atresia
- Renal anomalies
- Radial anomalies
- Limb defects

Clinical features –

Subtle findings – Mild hypoplastic thumb

- Missing or fused carpal bones

Severe manifestations

- total absence of radius
- deficiency of proximal part of upper limb
- Shortened humeral segment
- Hypoplastic glenoid

Classification:

- Type 4 Heikel is most common type of Radial club hand

![[TO17905803-2.png]]

BAYNE AND KLUG CLASSIFICATION

- TYPE 1 – Radius is shorter than ulna, Distal growth plate (+)

- TYPE 2 – Radius is smaller and thinner than ulna, no growth plate

- TYPE 3 – Only a small radial segment present
- TYPE 4 – Radius is completely absent, ulna may be curved

Modified Bayne and Klug classification for radial club hand

![[TO17905803-3.png]]

Blauth classification of thumb hypoplasia

![[TO17905803-4.png]]

Treatment:

General principles –

Stretching and splinting should be started early infancy – maintains soft tissue length

Address tight structures – notably fibrous anlage

Aggressive surgery in early childhood is avoided – causes distal ulnar growth arrest

Recommendations based on age –

0-6 months

- Splinting above elbow – preferred
- Serial casting

6- 18 months – splinting less tolerated

- Stretching program
- Night splinting

2-3 years – Operative treatment

Current treatment used

Bilobed flap + Soft tissue release (Resection of anlage) + Ulnar osteotomy + temporary longitudinal wiring of carpus to distal end of Ulna in corrected position

For thumb hypoplasia,

Blauth type 1-

- No surgery
- 1st web space lengthening

Blauth type 2, type 3 a –

- Opponensplasty
- Ulnar collateral ligament reconstruction

Blauth type 3 b, type 4, type 5

- Pollicization (after 2 years of age)

Pre-requisites –

- Flexible index finger
- Orientation of wrist on forearm
- Index finger should be functional

Assessed by side to side finger grip

In adolescents,

If wrist flexion is required for function – NO WRIST FUSION

If not, WRIST FUSION + PARTIAL CORRECTION

Hand centralization

Improves the position of carpus on the forearm and may increase overall length of limb

- Indications

-

- good elbow motion and biceps function intact
- done at 6-12 months of age
- followed by tendon transfers

- Contraindications

-

- older patient with good function
- patients with elbow extension contracture who rely on radial deviation
- proximate terminal condition

- Technique

-

- involves resection of varying amount of carpus, shortening of ECU, and, if needed, an angular osteotomy of the ulna (be sure to spare ulnar distal physis)
- may do as two stage procedure in combination with a distraction external fixator
- if thumb deformity then combines with thumb reconstruction at 18 months of age

Other procedures –

Radialization

Preoperative soft tissue distraction + carpus fixed on top of distal end of ulna with longitudinal wire

Radial side tendons transferred ulnarly

illizarov correction

Vascularized epiphyseal transfer

Answer : d
