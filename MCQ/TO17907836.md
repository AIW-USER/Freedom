## Question

85 years female, a known case of Parkinson’s disease (PD) has landed up with Intra capsular fracture neck femur. She is capable of only a limited house hold activity. Treatment of choice for her would be

## Options

1. Hemiarthroplasty
2. THR
3. Osteosynthesis with DHS and derotation screw
4. Bed rest and derotation boot

## Correct option

**1. Hemiarthroplasty**

## Answer explanation

Answer is a) hemiarthroplasty

Patients diagnosed with PD have a two-fold increased risk of all fractures, and an estimated 3-4 fold increase in risk of hip fractures. Multiple pathophysiological mechanisms associated with the disease process may be implicated. Greater incidence of falls has been attributed to motor symptoms and gait disturbance in combination with non-motor symptoms where an impaired cognition compounds this risk . Further evidence has indicated that patients diagnosed with PD have a reduced bone mineral density. Immobility and a coinciding decreased muscle strength, and low body weight may also be implicated as causative factors in this group.

While several options are available for surgical management of patients with a displaced femoral neck fracture, a growing body of evidence supports Hemiarthroplasty > Total hip arthroplasty as a definitive treatment in the elderly with concomitant PD. Total hip arthroplasty appears to be particularly advantageous for active, lucid patients with a relatively long life expectancy as functional outcomes are shown to be superior for total hip arthroplasty when compared to hemiarthroplasty.

Contemporary research shows internal fixation (IF) to be inferior in terms of functional outcome and pain relief when assessing treatment outcomes. It is likely that patients with PD may potentially fare worse from internal fixation with a more prolonged and painful recovery compared to patients without neurological impairment. When the adverse recovery period amongst PD patients is considered, demonstrating that arthroplasty represents a safer alternative to IF is of clinical importance.

So, in this case at the age of 85 with concomitant PD, with limited house hold activity, a bipolar hemiarthroplasty would benefit the most with reduced the rate of dislocation associated with a standard THR. In case THR is to be opted, then a dual mobility cup should be the most preferred implant. Replacement will certainly be superior to internal fixation and conservative management (bed rest with derotation boot).
