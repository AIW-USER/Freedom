## Question

A 45-year-old male construction worker develops severe left thigh pain after lifting a heavy bag of concrete 3 months ago. The patient reports worsening pain with prolonged sitting, coughing, and straining during bowel movements. He localized his pain to his anterior thigh. Current imaging is shown in figure A. He has undergone extensive physical therapy and pain medication treatment without benefit. Which of the following is true regarding his condition?

![[q214125-a-far-lateral-disc-herniation-1.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Treatment with cognitive behavioral therapy would be the most cost effective treatment plan for this patient
2. Compression of the dorsal root ganglia can manifest as increased pain severity compared to the common presentation/location
3. Decompression through a standard posterior approach with laminotomy can effectively address the pathology
4. A spinal cord stimulator would be most effective for managing this patients symptoms
5. Given the location of the pathology and anterior lumber interbody fusion would most effectively address the patients symptoms

## Correct option

**2. Compression of the dorsal root ganglia can manifest as increased pain severity compared to the common presentation/location**

## Answer explanation

Far lateral disc herniations can lead to compression of the dorsal root ganglion which can manifest as increased pain severity compared to traditional posterolateral disc herniations.

Lumbar disc herniations are the result of torsional strain that weakens the outer annulus fibrosus leading to herniation of nucleus pulposus outside of the disc space. The most common location for lumbar disc herniation is posterolateral, which is adjacent to the posterior longitudinal ligament and usually compresses the traversing nerve root. Far lateral disc herniations are less common and typically occur in the foramen or more lateral and affect the exiting nerve root. Given the adjacency of the dorsal root ganglion, far lateral disc herniations have been described as excruciatingly painful compared to posterolateral disc herniations. Patients requiring surgical decompression are best addressed with a paramedian approach that directly decompressed the nerve as it exits the foramen.

*Wiltse et al. described the paraspinal sacrospinalis-splitting approach to the lumbar spine.* **They reported** advantages of this approach to include more direct decompression of the nerve root, ease of access to the posterior superior iliac spine for bone graft harvesting, and preservation of the spinous process with the soft tissue attachments that decreases postoperative pain and instability. **They concluded** this approach to be exceptionally valuable in some circumstances.

*O'Hara and Marshall performed a cadaver study and a retrospective study of 20 patients with symptomatic far lateral disc herniations that underwent a Wiltse approach for discectomy and nerve root decompression.* **They reported** excellent-good results in 90% of patients and consistently identified the posterior ramus as a landmark for safe exposure of the affected nerve root. **The authors recommended** the Wiltse approach for the treatment of far lateral disc herniations.

Figure A is the axial T1 MRI of the lumbar spine with a left far lateral disc herniation at L3-4.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: Patients with symptomatic disc herniations that are refractory to nonoperative treatment are ideal candidates for surgical decompression. Treatment with cognitive behavioral therapy may provide coping skills, but may not enable the patient to return to work.
*Answer 3: Patients with far lateral disc herniations requiring a decompression are best addressed with a paramedian approach described by Wiltse et al.*
Answer 4: Spinal cord stimulator would not be an appropriate treatment option for this patient.
Answer 5: An anterior lumbar interbody fusion would effectively remove the disc and decompress the nerve, but the patient does not have an indication for a lumbar fusion without evidence of instability.
