## Question

A 23-year-old runner presents to your office with lower extremity pain. She has a history of recurrent ankle sprains. She reports that she was recently running and rolled her ankle. Which of the following, according to the Ottawa ankle rules, would merit obtaining radiographs?

## Options

1. Ability to ambulate only 10 feet with reported lateral ankle pain
2. Ecchymosis of the medial ankle
3. Ecchymosis of the lateral ankle
4. Tenderness to palpation over the deltoid ligament
5. Tenderness to palpation at the tip of the lateral malleolus

## Correct option

**5. Tenderness to palpation at the tip of the lateral malleolus**

## Answer explanation

The Ottawa ankle rules are indications for radiographs with an ankle injury. Of the choices listed, tenderness to palpation of the lateral malleolus would merit radiographs of the ankle.

Ankle sprains are very common twisting injuries to the ankle that are the most common reason for missed athletic participation. Diagnosis can be made clinically with swelling and ecchymosis of the ankle and pain with range of motion. Radiographs are only indicated when clinical examination meets criteria (Ottawa ankle rules). These include an inability to bear weight, medial or lateral malleolus point tenderness, 5th metatarsal base tenderness, and navicular tenderness. These rules are 96-99% sensitive in ruling out ankle fracture. Treatment of ankle sprains usually includes a brief period of immobilization followed by early functional physical therapy. Rarely, operative management is indicated in the setting of syndesmosis injury with tibiofibular diastasis or chronic ankle instability with recurrent sprains.

*Slater et al. reviewed acute injuries to the lateral ankle complex.* **They reported** that initial assessment should use the Ottawa ankle rules, and grading should take into account the degree of mechanical instability. **They concluded** that functional treatment after a short period of relative immobilization gives satisfactory results, and residual chronic ankle instability can be managed by repair or reconstruction.

*Czajka et al. reviewed ankle injuries.* **They reported** that inversion injuries about the ankle involve about 25% of all injuries of the musculoskeletal system and 50% of all sports-related injuries. **They noted** that medial-sided ankle sprains occur less frequently than those on the lateral side. **They concluded** that providers should apply the Ottawa ankle rules when radiography is indicated and refer fractures and more severe injuries to orthopedic surgery as needed.

*Beckenkamp et al. reviewed the diagnostic accuracy of the Ottawa Ankle and Midfoot Rules in a systematic review.* **They reported** that the ankle and midfoot rules presented similar accuracies, which were homogeneous and high for sensitivity and negative likelihood ratios and poor and heterogeneous for specificity and positive likelihood ratios (mean, 95% CI pooled sensitivity of Ankle Rules: 99.4%, 97.9% to 99.8%; specificity: 35.3%, 28.8% to 42.3%). **They concluded** that their study features and methodological quality influence estimates of the diagnostic accuracy of the Ottawa Ankle and Midfoot Rules.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: An inability to ambulate would merit radiographs
Answers 2&3: Ecchymosis of the ankle are not a criterion included in the Ottawa ankle rules.
Answer 4: Medial and lateral malleolus tenderness merit radiographs, but soft tissue tenderness is not included in the Ottawa ankle rules.
