## Question

A 57-year-old female presents to your clinic with persistent, worsening great toe pain. The hallux is resting in a valgus and pronated position. Radiographs confirm your diagnosis of hallux valgus with HVA of 35º and IMA of 11º. You recommend a distal metatarsal osteotomy, akin osteotomy and medial capsule imbrication and the patient wishes to move forward. Surgery is successful in achieving appropriate correction but throughout her follow-up, she complains of persistent numbness over the medial hallux. What structure was most likely injured at the time of surgery?

## Options

1. Deep peroneal nerve
2. Dorsomedial cutaneous nerve
3. Intermediate dorsal cutaneous nerve
4. Medial plantar nerve
5. Saphenous nerve

## Correct option

**2. Dorsomedial cutaneous nerve**

## Answer explanation

The dorsomedial cutaneous nerve is commonly encountered at the time of surgery for hallux valgus correction and can be injured, causing decreased sensation over the dorsal/medial hallux.

The dorsomedial cutaneous nerve is a branch of the superficial peroneal nerve and can be affected at the time of hallux valgus correction. As a result, patients may complain of decreased sensation over the dorsal/medial aspect of the great toe. This may be due to nerve laceration, irritation, or postoperative swelling. If the nerve remains intact, symptoms typically resolve over time without the need for additional intervention, however, this is something that patients should be counseled about prior to surgery.

*Miller et al. review dorsomedial cutaneous nerve syndrome and treatment,* **noting** it can cause significant neuritic pain if injured during hallux valgus surgery. They developed a technique that involves burying the damaged nerve in the proximal aspect of the 1st metatarsal or medial cuneiform with patients experiencing significant relief. **They conclude** that in cases where this nerve pain does not resolve, resection and nerve burial may be a useful adjunct to resolve neuritis.

*Solan et al. reviewed the surgical anatomy of the dorsomedial cutaneous nerve,* **noting** that most hallux valgus surgeries require a dorsomedial incision in this area. They make note of a previously unrecognized branch of the nerve that is in a vulnerable position during this approach. They finish by concluding that a slightly more plantar incision be utilized in order to avoid harming the dorsomedial cutaneous nerve or its branches.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The deep peroneal nerve innervates the extensors of the hallux and toes as well as the 1st/2nd webspace but causes numbness over the medial hallux and is not typically affected by hallux valgus surgery.
Answer 3: The intermediate dorsal cutaneous nerve provides sensory innervation to the mid-dorsum of the foot but does not reach far enough medially to be damaged during the approach to hallux valgus correction.
Answer 4: The medial plantar nerve is a branch of the tibial nerve and innervates the plantar surface of the medial foot. It would not lead to numbness over the medial hallux.
Answer 5: The saphenous nerve provides some sensation to the medial midfoot but would not be affected by hallux valgus surgery.
