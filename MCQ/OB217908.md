## Question

You are performing a revision total knee arthroplasty (TKA) on a 67-year-old male who has pain from confirmed aseptic loosening. During surgery, you are struggling with obtaining adequate exposure, therefore, you perform a quadriceps snip in addition to your standard medial parapatellar arthrotomy. Provided that adequate implant fixation is achieved, how does this affect this patient's postoperative activity instructions?

## Options

1. Weight bearing as tolerated, range of motion as tolerated
2. Weight bearing as tolerated, locked in extension for 6 weeks
3. Non weight bearing for 2 weeks, locked in extension for 2 weeks
4. Non weight bearing for 2 weeks, locked in extension for 4 weeks
5. Non weight bearing for 4 weeks, range of motion as tolerated

## Correct option

**1. Weight bearing as tolerated, range of motion as tolerated**

## Answer explanation

The quadriceps snip exposure is an extensile exposure that does not require any additional immobilization or weight bearing restrictions post-operatively.

During total knee arthroplasty (TKA), especially in the revision setting, extensile exposures may be desirable in order to increase visualization and ease of instrumentation. Extensile exposures include the quadriceps snip, V-Y turndown, and tibial tubercle osteotomy. With a quadriceps snip, an oblique, 45º angle cut is placed from the apex of the quadriceps incision towards the vastus lateralis. No change in postoperative protocol is required with this approach and it is not thought to produce any extensor lag. If other extensile approaches are used, the knee often needs to be immobilized for several weeks, with weight-bearing also limited at times.

*Abdel et al. reviewed the use of the quadriceps snip approach in 321 revision TKAs.* *They compared patients who underwent quadriceps snip to a control and noted no significant differences in extensor lag, extensor mechanism disruption or component loosening.* Range of motion was slightly less in flexion in the quadriceps snip group but overall, **the authors recommend** the technique as a valuable alternative, especially in a revision setting.

*Garvin et al. discussed the quadriceps snip approach in 16 patients (10 revision TKA, 6 primary TKA).* **They noted** good-excellent outcomes in all patients in the study with questionable extensor weakness in the quadriceps snip group compared to the contralateral native knee. This said, they did concluded that the technique was useful, safe and did not require postoperative protocols to be altered.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2-5: With extensile approaches to TKA aside from the quadriceps snip, range of motion and weight bearing are usually limited for a period of time after surgery.
