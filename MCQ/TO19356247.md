## Question

A patient of caries spine with paraplegia is on ATT since 3 weeks. Despite treatment his neurological status has worsened and deficit has increased and now he has bladder involvement as well. Next step for this case should be [FNB 2023]

## Options

1. Add Streptomycin, continue ATT for 3 weeks more and reassess
2. Decompression and evaluate for MDR TB
3. Give steroids and observe
4. A repeat MRI

## Correct option

**2. Decompression and evaluate for MDR TB**

## Answer explanation

Ans: B
EXPLANATIONS: -
Tuberculosis of spine is often referred to as Caries Spine. Involvement of dorsal and lumbar spine can often lead to paraplegia in caries and this scenario where caries spine has resulted in neurological deficit is referred to as Pott’s spine (Pott’s paraplegia).
Pott’s paraplegia is further categorized into early and late paraplegia based on the onset timing and symptom severity. Early paraplegia manifests during the active tuberculosis infection phase, predominantly observed in adults within two years of infection onset. In contrast, late paraplegia emerges in patients with healed tuberculosis, typically defined as neurological symptoms appearing after two years of primary infection.

Timing Occurs within weeks to months after spinal infection Occurs months to years after initial spinal infection

Modified Tuli Classification: Stages of Pott’s Paraplegia
Stage 1: Neurological deficits are subtle and detectable only through clinical examination, characterized by signs like ankle clonus, exaggerated deep tendon reflexes, and a positive Babinski sign.
Stage 2: The patient exhibits an upper motor neuron (UMN) type motor deficit with spasticity but remains ambulatory. Motor scores range from 60 to 100 in tetraparesis and 80 to 100 in paraparesis, with sensory deficits affecting the lateral column.
Stage 3: The patient is bedridden and exhibits spasticity. Motor scores are low, ranging from 0 to 30 in tetraparesis and 50 to 80 in paraparesis, with sensory deficits still localized to the lateral column.
Stage 4: The patient remains bedridden with severe sensory loss and possibly pressure sores. Motor scores are 0 in tetraplegia and around 50 in paraplegia, with sensory deficits extending to both posterior and lateral columns.
Stage 5: This stage mirrors stage 4 but may also include bladder and bowel involvement, flexor spasms, or flaccid tetraplegia/paraplegia.

MANAGEMENT OF POTTS PARAPLEGIA
ATT is administered to all cases (with or without neurological deficit). There is no separate protocol in DOTS for Spine/ Osteoarticular TB (comes under common category- extra pulmonary) hence, WHO guidelines (Index-TB guidelines) are followed mostly in India.
The regimen involves HRZE for 2 months and then HRE for minimum 10 more months (total 12-16 months therapy). The drugs are given on daily basis (not alternate day as in DOTS) owing to poor bone penetration. Since the disease is potentially disastrous in its complications, our advice is, stopping ATT with a predetermined period (as advised in Index guidelines) may not be advisable all the time; but should be guided by multiple factors, including clinical response as well as neuroimaging and no single factor should be used to determine the end-point of therapy.
The most common surgical indications cited in the literature include neurologic deficit not responding to minimum 3 weeks of medica therapy (ATT), severe kyphosis, pain due to spinal instability, , large paraspinal or epidural abscess, and more than four levels of vertebral involvement. Regarding steroid use, there is no definitive role of steroids in the treatment of spinal TB except in cases associated with arachnoiditis/ meningitis or if there is paraplegia to non-osseous spinal TB (rare scenario).
