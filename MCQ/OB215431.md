## Correct option

## Answer explanation

The imaging, biopsy, and Tinel's sign on physical exam are all indicative of a schwannoma, also called a neurilemmoma. These can be treated adequately with marginal resection.

Neurilemmomas are benign tumors of Schwann cells that form on the surface of peripheral nerves. Peak incidence is in the 3rd to 6th decade and often form on the flexor surfaces of the extremities, head, and neck. Typically they are asymptomatic, however, patients can present with paresthesias in the affected nerves distribution. Treatment for symptomatic patients is marginal excision without affecting nerve function and with rare recurrence.

*Hirai et al. investigated factors that predict complications in the surgical treatment of schwannomas.* *They retrospectively reviewed 141 total schwannoma cases.* *They have 49 cases with complications (34.8%), with 42 sensory and 8 motor neurologic deficits.* After multivariate analysis, only involvement of a major motor nerve was predictive of complication. Six of eight patients with motor complications postop did not return to baseline function at final follow-up.

*Kim et al.* **determined** predictive factors for complications in 30 patients who had histopathologic verification of schwannomas. They only included those patients with major lower extremity motor nerve involvement. They had a 76.7% immediate postoperative nerve deficit rate. At final follow up 37.7% had persistent neurologic deficits. **They found** larger tumor size as the only predictive measure for postoperative complications, although do not discuss what was considered "large".

Figure A shows mass which has low intensity on T1 and high intensity on T2 MRI with classic "string" sign. Figure B is an AP and Lateral thumb X-rays with an osteoblastic process involving the metacarpal with biopsy specimen demonstrating a sheet of blue cells, consistent with Ewing sarcoma. Figure C is an AP of the left humerus with a lytic lesion in the midshaft region. Figure D is an AP of the right shoulder with a destructive lesion with biopsy demonstrating lacy osteoid matrix and cellular atypia and high nuclear to cytoplasmic ratio. Figure E demonstrates a T2 weighted MRI with a large heterogeneous mass within the volar forearm and a pathology slide with a malignant appearance with significant cellular atypia and small areas of what appear to be lipocytes throughout the slide, consistent with a de-differentiated liposarcoma.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: This clinical story and imaging are consistent with Ewing sarcoma, which often requires chemotherapy and wide resection.
Answer 3: This patient has likely an impending pathologic fracture and would require prophylactic stabilization.
Answer 4: Patient imaging and biopsy results are consistent with osteosarcoma.
Answer 5: Liposarcoma would best be treated with wide excision with or without adjuvant or neoadjuvant radiation therapy.
