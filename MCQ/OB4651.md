## Question

A 45-year-old laborer sustained the injury shown in Figure A. Closed reduction is performed and post-reduction films are shown in Figure B. He elects to proceed with nonoperative treatment. Two years later he now presents with persistent ankle pain and difficulty walking long distances. On physical exam, he is found to have an antalgic gait with limited ankle motion secondary to pain. Crepitus is felt with passive range of motion of the ankle. The most recent radiographs are shown in Figure C. An MRI report indicates the presence of degenerative changes in the ankle. What would be the most appropriate option for definitive management?

## Options

1. Steroid injection into the ankle
2. Total ankle arthroplasty.
3. Tibiotalocalcaneal (TTC) arthrodesis with femoral head allograft
4. Subtalar Fusion
5. Triple arthrodesis

## Correct option

**3. Tibiotalocalcaneal (TTC) arthrodesis with femoral head allograft**

## Answer explanation

The clinical presentation is consistent with a talar neck fracture nonunion with subsequent severe arthritis of the tibial-talar joint. Considering the degree of avascular necrosis (AVN) and talar head collapse, the ideal procedure would be a tibiotalocalcaneal (TTC) arthrodesis with structural allograft.

Varus malunions complicate treatment of talar neck fracture in close to 30% of cases. Malunion of the talus can lead to abnormal loading of the ankle joint and subsequent arthritis. Arthrodesis of the ankle is indicated for painful posttraumatic ankle arthritis. When significant bone loss of the talus is identified, use of structural allograft along with a TTC arthrodesis can provide good outcomes.

*Easley et al. review the current results of total ankle arthroplasty.* **They note** that survivorship at 8-12 years is close to 80-95%. There is controversy about the age at which an ankle replacement can be completed, with reports suggesting that patients less than 50 years of age may be at higher risk for failure.

*Rammelt et al. reviewed the results of TTC nailing of the hind foot in 38 patients.* **They found** a union rate of 84%, with low pain levels and a high return to work rate. **They conclude** that TTC nailing provides a safe and reliable option for those with severe ankle/hindfoot disease.

Figure A shows the lateral radiograph of a displaced talar neck fracture; a post reduction radiograph is seen in Figure B. Figure C shows AP and lateral radiographs of a talar varus malunion with degeneration of the ankle joint.

Incorrect answers
Answer 1: A steroid injection is not indicated for definitive management.
Answer 2: An ankle replacement would not be indicated in this young patient who is involved in higher impact activities.
Answer 4: While the subtalar joint should be evaluated, fusing this joint alone would not address the ankle pathology
Answer 5: A triple arthrodesis is not indicated in this patient.
