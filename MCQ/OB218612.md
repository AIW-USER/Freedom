## Question

A 3-week-old male infant presents to your clinic with his foster parents who note that they hear a click in his left hip when getting him dressed. On examination, his Ortolani sign is positive and his left leg is slightly shorter with the hip flexed at 90°. Based on his initial ultrasound, you classify his hip as Graf IV. Which of the following patient factors places this infant most at risk for failing initial treatment with a Pavlik harness?

## Options

1. Age
2. Gender
3. Graf Classification
4. Unilateral involvement
5. Laterality

## Correct option

**3. Graf Classification**

## Answer explanation

Worsening severity of developmental hip dysplasia (DDH), as noted by the Graf classification, is associated with higher rates of failed treatment with a Pavlik harness.

Developmental hip dysplasia is related to abnormal development of the hip which results in a continuum of dysplasia, subluxation, and dislocation secondary to multiple mechanisms. It is more common in females and most commonly affects the left hip, with bilateral DDH occurring in ~20% of affected patients. Ultrasound is first line imaging for evaluation of the dysplastic hip. The most common treatment for infants <6 months old is bracing using a Pavlik harness. The ability to successfully use a Pavlik harness requires normal muscle function in order to dynamically maintain hip reduction. Risk factors for treatment failure using a Pavlik harness include a negative Ortolani sign, Graf IV hip, treatment initiation at >7 weeks old, and right-sided dislocations. The more of these factors present together concomitantly further increase the risk for treatment failure.

*Tibrewal et al. reviewed current concepts surrounding the use of Pavlik harness treatment.* **They noted** satisfactory clinical and radiographic outcomes with the use of Pavlik harnesses at long-term follow-up but noted failures can be associated with avascular necrosis. They describe alternative methods of splinting but there are no major comparative studies that exist at this time.

*Upasani et al. prospectively evaluated the success of brace treatment for infant hip dislocations, following all patients for a minimum of 18 months.* Bracing was successful in 79% of the cohort with six variables being found to be significant risk factors for brace failure: femoral nerve palsy during brace treatment, treatment with static bracing, irreducibility of hip, treatment initiation after 7 weeks of age, right hip dislocation, and a Graf IV hip. Hips with 4 or 5 of these risk factors present had a 100% probability of failure with bracing.

Incorrect Answer:
Answer 1: Patients > 7 weeks old at initiation of bracing have a higher risk of bracing failure; this patient is 3 weeks old.
Answer 2: No correlation has been seen when comparing gender as a risk factor for bracing failure.
Answer 4: The presence of a unilateral dislocation is not a risk factor for bracing failure.
Answer 5: Right hip, not left hip dislocation is a risk factor for bracing failure.
