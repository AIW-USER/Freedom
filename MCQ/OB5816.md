## Question

A 68-year-old man presents to the clinic complaining of right shoulder pain and weakness since a fall 1 month ago. An MRI is obtained and shown in Figure A. What specific examination finding would be expected with a complete tear to the structure labeled number "10"?

![[q5816-a-screen-shot-2016-05-31-at-11-53-44-pm.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. with 90 degrees of elbow flexion and internal rotation of shoulder, inability to push elbow forward
2. with 90 degrees of elbow flexion and arm abducted, inability to hold external rotation
3. with 90 degrees of elbow flexion, inability to actively maintain passive external rotation of shoulder
4. with arm abducted, positioned forward 30 degrees with thumb pointing down, inability to resist downward pressure
5. with 90 degrees of elbow flexion and forearm pronation, pain with active supination against resistance

## Correct option

**3. with 90 degrees of elbow flexion, inability to actively maintain passive external rotation of shoulder**

## Answer explanation

The structure of interest in the image is the infraspinatus muscle. An infraspinatus tear will often manifest on exam by the external rotation lag sign (ERLS), in which the examiner passively places the arm in an externally rotated position at the shoulder, and the patient is unable to actively maintain this position after release.

Rotator cuff tears are common due to both traumatic and chronic degenerative etiologies. It is important to be able to diagnose such tears on exam, with maneuvers specific to each of the 4 major muscles. Exam of the supraspinatus involves either active resistance against pressure with the arm abducted, internally rotated, and positioned forward in the plane of the scapula (Jobe's test) or in this same position asking the patient to slowly lower his or her arm and looking for pain or a sudden drop rather than slow downward motion (drop test). The subscapularis is examined when the patient places both hands on his or her belly with elbows out, and is asked to actively push his or her elbows forward, or to resist the examiner lifting his or her hand off of the belly (belly press test). The subscapularis can also be tested with the back of the patient's hand on his or her back and asking him or her to lift off (lift off test). Infraspinatus tendon tears can manifest with the external rotation lag sign. The teres minor is examined by passively placing the patient's arm in 90 degrees abduction, 90 degrees of elbow flexion and 90 degrees of external rotation, and looking for an inability by the patient to hold this position (Hornblower's sign).

*Castoldi et al assess the ERLS for its ability to detect full-thickness tears of the supraspinatus and* **find** a sensitivity of 56% and specificity of 98%. Though ERLS is typically used to detect infraspinatus tears, larger cuff tears often involve both supra- and infraspinatus. As anticipated, these numbers (sensitivity and specificity for supraspinatus tears) improve when the infraspinatus is involved, as well as the teres minor. They encourage use of the ERLS for overall evaluation of the shoulder including for supraspinatus tears.

*Walch et al correlate the Hornblower's sign and ERLS with CT-scan fatty degeneration and clinical findings.* In a 54 patient series **they report** a 100% sensitivity and specificity of the ERLS for stage 3 or 4 fatty degeneration of infraspinatus and 100% sensitivity with 93% specificity of Hornblower's sign for stage 3 or 4 degeneration of teres minor. **They caution** against repair in this setting given high rate of failure at stage 3 or 4 degeneration.

Figure A is a T1 sagittal image from an MR arthrogram of the shoulder in which the infraspinatus is labeled with the number 10. The other labels are 1, Biceps brachii muscle, short head. 2, Subscapularis muscle. 3, Deltoid muscle. 4, Coracoid process. 5, Supraspinatus muscle. 6, Clavicle. 7, Trapezius muscle. 8, Acromion. 9, Deltoid muscle (posterior). 11, Teres minor muscle. 12, Teres major muscle.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1 - this describes the belly press sign, indicating injury to the subscapularis
Answer 2 - this describes the Hornblower's sign, indicating injury to the teres minor
Answer 4 - this describes the Jobe's test, indicating injury to the supraspinatus
Answer 5 - this describes the Yergason's test, indicating injury/strain on the biceps tendon
