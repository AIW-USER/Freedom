## Question

A 45-year-old patient presents with pain and swelling after undergoing an arthroscopic rotator cuff repair 10 weeks ago. On physical exam the portal sites are healed and there is no drainage. Testing of the integrity of the rotator cuff is limited secondary to pain. He has a WBC of 11.0 (reference range, 3-11 cells/mL), ESR of 40 mm/hr (reference range, 0-22 mm/hr), and CRP of 1.5 mg/dL (reference range, 0-1 mg/dL). An aspiration is completed and no organisms are seen on the gram stain. Twelve days after the aspiration, positive cultures are reported. Which organism is most likely to have grown in culture medium?

## Options

1. Staphylococcus aureus
2. Cutibacterium acnes
3. Corynebacterium sp.
4. Staphylococcus epidermidis
5. Pseudomonas aeruginosa

## Correct option

**2. Cutibacterium acnes**

## Answer explanation

Based on the delayed clinical presentation and laboratory findings, the most likely bacterium to have grown in culture is Cutibacterium acnes (C. acnes), formerly known as Propionibacterium acnes (p.acnes)

Infections after arthroscopic rotator cuff repairs have a reported prevalence of 0.006%-3.4%. The typical skin flora includes staph and strep as well as C. acnes, which has a propensity for the shoulder. Because it is an anaerobic organism, cultures may only become positive after 7-21 days.

*Millett et al. reviewed 10 patients with post-operative shoulder infections and confirmed C. acnes infection.* Cultures became positive at an average of 7 days after the start of incubation. **They indicate** that C. acnes shoulder infections may be insidious in onset and may lead to persistent shoulder pain.

*Dodson et al. reviewed 11 patients who sustained C. acnes shoulder infections after undergoing shoulder arthroplasty.* **They found** that cultures became positive at an average of 9 days from the start of incubation. **They note** that C. acnes shoulder infections are an important cause of implant failure, especially because patents may lack traditional signs of infection (clinical/laboratory).

*Saltzman et al. review the microbiology, risk factors and management of infections after shoulder surgery.* **They indicate** that deeper infections are more difficult to identify and can present in an insidious fashion. Cultures should be held for up to 21 days in order to account for fastidious organisms such as C. acnes., for 4 weeks to account for fungal organisms, and for 8 weeks to account for mycobacterium.

*Matsen et al. studied the presence of C. acnes on skin and in surgical wounds of patients undergoing revision shoulder arthroplasty.* Males were found to have more positive cultures for C.acnes than females; this included superficial and deep cultures. **They recommend** observing cultures for at least 17 days and being vigilant to minimize potential surgical wound contamination.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1, 3, 4: While these bacteria are found on the skin are cause post-operative infections, they are less likely to present with an indolent course and prolonged incubation times.
Answer 5: Pseudomonas is a water-borne gram-negative rod that is not typically implicated in infections after shoulder surgery.
