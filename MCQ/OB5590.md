## Question

## Answer explanation

Pars defects and isthmic spondylolisthesis are most strongly associated with pelvic incidence. Figure B shows the correct measurement of pelvic incidence.

Pediatric spondylolisthesis involves a continuum of disease ranging from pars stress reaction to spondylolysis and spondylisthesis. This entity is usually the result of repetitive hyperextension, especially in gymnasts and weighlifters. The L5-S1 level is most commonly involved level. Pelvic incidence has been shown to directly correlate with severity of disease as increased pelvic incidence is believed to increase shear stresses at the pars interarticularis at the lumbosacral junction. This increased stress and subsequent loss of bony integrity, in addition to other genetic factors lead to spondylolisthesis.

*Labelle et al. evaluated the radiological parameters involved in spinopelvic balance using special software and* **determined** that pelvic incidence is significantly greater in patients with spondylolisthesis. This parameter is inherent to individual patients and does not change with surgical reduction.

*Hanson et al. looked at pelvic incidence in pediatric and adult patients with low and high grade spondylolisthesis and* **found** a significant association. There was also a significant association with Meyerding-Newmans grade.

Figures A-E show several radiologic parameters involved in spino-pelvic balance Figure A, B, C, and D are the alpha angle, pelvic incidence, pelvic tilt, and sacral slope, respectively. Figure E is not a known parameter.

Incorrect Answers:
1,3,4,5: While some of these parameters do correlate with spondylolisthesis, pelvic incidence has the strongest correlation.
