## Question

For which of the following vignettes of patients with chronic knee pain is reduction and fixation of an osteochondritis dissecans (OCD) most indicated based on MRI findings?

## Options

1. A 10-year-old girl with open physis and a 1.5-cm OCD lesion at the medial femoral condyle with an intact cartilage cap
2. A 14-year-old boy with open physis and a 2.0-cm OCD lesion at the lateral femoral condyle with an intact cartilage cap
3. A 16-year-old boy with closed physis and a 2.5-cm hinging OCD lesion from the medial femoral condyle
4. A 12-year-old girl with open physis and a 0.7-cm OCD lesion with an intact cartilage cap at the medial patellar facet
5. A 19-year-old boy with a 5.2-cm OCD lesion w/ moderate cartilage changes

## Correct option

**3. A 16-year-old boy with closed physis and a 2.5-cm hinging OCD lesion from the medial femoral condyle**

## Answer explanation

Of the above patient vignettes, reduction and fixation of an OCD lesion is most indicated in a 16-year-old boy with closed physis and a 2.5-cm hinging (unstable) lesion from the medial femoral condyle.

Treatment for OCD of the knee is directed based on the stability of the lesion assessed on MRI. For stable, mildly symptomatic lesions a course of limited weight bearing and activity modification is indicated. Fixation is indicated for unstable or hinged lesions, and stabilization of the fragment can be achieved using a variety of implants through an arthroscopic or open approach. The fragment should be salvaged and the normal articular surface restored whenever possible. Osteochondral allograft is reserved for larger lesions.

*Magnussen et al. investigated the healing outcomes and long-term maintenance of knee function after operative fixation of an OCD loose body.* **They reported** that operative fixation of unstable OCD lesions results in stable fixation at an average 9 years after surgery. **They highlighted** that patients did not have symptoms of osteoarthritis pain and had normal function in activities of daily life. **They concluded** that fixation is a superior alternative to lesion excision.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1, 2 and 4: Lesions this size with an intact cartilage cap are stable and do not require reduction and fixation. Subchondral drilling may be indicated if nonoperative modalities fail.
Answer 5: With an OCD lesion this large, this patient would most likely benefit from an osteochondral allograft procedure.
