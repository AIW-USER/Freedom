## Options

1. Curettage and targeted intravenous antibiotics for 6 weeks.
2. Curettage, cancellous bone grafting, non-weightbearing until visible callus is seen on follow-up radiographs.
3. Curettage, placement of antibiotic beads, intravenous antibiotics for 6 weeks, removal of beads and placement of bone graft substitute at 6 weeks.
4. Neoadjuvant chemotherapy, physeal-sparing resection, intercalary bone allograft, locked plate fixation, postoperative adjuvant chemotherapy.
5. Neoadjuvant chemotherapy, wide resection of the distal one-third of the femur, reconstruction with a custom implant, postoperative adjuvant chemotherapy.

## Correct option

**5. Neoadjuvant chemotherapy, wide resection of the distal one-third of the femur, reconstruction with a custom implant, postoperative adjuvant chemotherapy.**

## Answer explanation

The patient has intramedullary osteosarcoma of the distal femur. Limb-salvage surgery comprises wide resection and reconstruction. A custom expandable prosthesis is an option.

Intramedullary osteosarcoma occurs most commonly around the knees of children and young adults. Most lesions are Enneking Stage IIB lesions (high-grade, extracompartmental, no metastases). Treatment involves multi-agent chemotherapy and limb-salvage resection and reconstruction. Reconstruction options include adult tumor-prosthesis, expandable prosthesis, osteoarticular allograft, combination alloprosthetic composite and rotationplasty.

*Maheshwari et al. reviewed prosthesis failure in 3 patients with a custom expandable endoprosthesis capable of noninvasive lengthening.* These implants failed at the expansion mechanism and they hypothesized that unexpanded implants have greater risk of failure during weight-bearing than implants actively undergoing expansion.

*Campanacci et al. reviewed patients with osteoarticular allografts for osteosarcoma.* Twelve of 20 surviving allografts failed (4 distal femur, 8 proximal tibia) because of graft fracture. In 4 patients, there was subchondral collapse and conversion to an alloprosthetic composite. **They note** 10-year graft survival of 58% in the distal femur and 20% in the proximal tibia.

Figures A and B are AP and lateral radiographs of intramedullary osteosarcoma. The lesion is a permeative lytic lesion. Figure C is a T2-weighted fat saturated image of osteosarcoma extending from metaphysis across the physis to the epiphyseal region. Figure D is a bone scan showing increased uptake typical of osteosarcoma. Figure E is a high power slide of osteosarcoma showing islands of lacey osteoid, and stromal cells with atypia, high nuclear to cytoplasmic ratio, and abnormal mitotic figures. Illustrations A and B show a custom expandable prosthesis before and after implantation.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1, 2, 3: Curettage of a malignant lesion is contraindicated. The management must involve wide or radical resection, taking a margin of normal tissue.
Answer 4: Physeal-sparing surgery is not possible for osteosarcomas around the knee. The articular surface has to be sacrificed. This particular lesion shows invasion through the physis on MRI.
