## Options

1. Most commonly noted in the African American population
2. A CT cervical spine is necessary to rule out a cervical spine fracture
3. His condition is assocaited with hyperlipidemia
4. Has been associated with HLA-B8
5. Disc space is generally preserved

## Correct option

**1. Most commonly noted in the African American population**

## Answer explanation

The radiographic imaging is compatible with diffuse idiopathic skeletal hyperostosis (DISH) of the cervical spine. DISH is most commonly seen in the caucasian population and less often seen in the African American, Native American and Asian American populations.

DISH is defined by the presence of non-marginal syndesmophytes at three successive levels (involving 4 contiguous vertebrae). This results in significant rigidity in the affected spinal segment. As a result, the spine acts like a rigid long bone that is vulnerable to bending forces and fracture (e.g. hyper-extension injury). DISH patients (and those with ankylosing spondylitis (AS)) presenting with neck pain after trauma should undergo CT of the cervical spine to assess for occult fractures as these are usually very unstable injuries with high morbidity if they go unrecognized.

*Belanger et al. reviewed the diagnosis and treatment of both spinal and extraspinal manifestations of DISH.* **They report** the features that help distinguish DISH from AS to include: nonmarginal syndesmophytes, no involvement of the SI joints (bilateral sacroiliitis in AS), anterior cervical bone formation with preservation of disc space, and radiographic findings on the right side of the thoracic spine.

*Whang et al. reviewed the management of spinal injuries in patients with DISH and AS.* Most of these injuries involved the subaxial cervical spine between C5 and C7. **The authors noted** no statistically significant differences between the survivorship and outcomes of the AS and DISH group and 81.3% of all respondents were classified as having excellent or good outcomes. Although the rate of neurologic injury was high for both AS and DISH, AS patients were more likely to exhibit neurologic deficits and undergo operative management. **The authors concluded** that although the majority of these spinal injuries were treated surgically, stable fractures without any associated neurologic deficits were often successfully managed with immobilization.

Figure A and B shows the AP and lateral cervical spine radiographs that show findings of DISH including nonmarginal syndesmophytes and anterior cervical bone formation with preservation of disc space amongst 4 contiguous vertebrae.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: This is true as patients with DISH, neck pain and history of trauma must be evaluated with a CT or MRI for an occult fracture.
Answer 3: This is true as DISH has been associated with gout, hyperlipidemia, and diabetes.
Answer 4: Whereas AS is associated with HLA-B27, there have been recent reports of DISH association with HLA-B8 which is common in diabetics with DISH.
Answer 5: Unlike in AS where the disc space is ossified, the disc space in patients with DISH is generally preserved.
