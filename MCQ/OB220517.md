## Question

A 72-year-old woman presents with persistent mid-back pain and progressive stooping posture. Imaging reveals two vertebral compression fractures at T11 and L1, as well as thoracolumbar kyphosis. She has failed extensive nonoperative management, including bracing, activity modification, and pharmacologic treatment with calcium and vitamin D. She is being considered for surgical intervention due to progressive spinal deformity and impaired quality of life. Her bone density is markedly low, and the team is concerned about instrumentation failure or pseudoarthrosis. What is the most appropriate next step in the management of this patient?

## Options

1. Begin bisphosphonate therapy to improve bone quality before surgery
2. Obtain a DEXA scan to confirm osteoporosis
3. Refer for surgical correction of spinal deformity without medical optimization
4. Start anabolic therapy with teriparatide prior to surgical intervention
5. Treat with continued bracing and observation only

## Correct option

**4. Start anabolic therapy with teriparatide prior to surgical intervention**

## Answer explanation

Teriparatide has been shown to enhance bone healing, reduce instrumentation-related complications, and improve outcomes in patients with osteoporosis undergoing spinal fusion (Answer 4).

Patients with multiple vertebral compression fractures are, by definition, osteoporotic, regardless of DEXA results. Initiating anabolic therapy like teriparatide before surgery is critical to optimizing bone quality. Anabolic agents stimulate new bone formation and improve bone microarchitecture, reducing the risk of instrumentation failure and nonunion (pseudoarthrosis). Recent studies have demonstrated that such agents not only improve mechanical stability but also enhance patient-reported outcomes and reduce revision rates in spinal deformity surgery.

*Gupta et al.* (2021) evaluated revision surgery rates in osteoporotic patients undergoing long spinal fusions. **They noted** that osteoporosis was significantly associated with higher revision rates and mechanical complications. **They concluded** that identifying and optimizing bone quality preoperatively is essential for improving surgical outcomes.

*Zhang et al.* (2023) reviewed current pharmacologic strategies for osteoporosis optimization in adult spinal deformity patients. **They noted** that anabolic agents like teriparatide showed superior benefits compared to antiresorptives in enhancing fusion and reducing complications. **They concluded** that initiating appropriate pharmacotherapy preoperatively is critical in high-risk osteoporotic patients.

*Mohanty et al.* (2024) investigated the effect of teriparatide on complications and outcomes in patients undergoing long spinal fusions. **They found** that teriparatide use was associated with fewer hardware complications and better patient-reported outcomes. **They concluded** that teriparatide is an effective preoperative therapy for osteoporotic patients undergoing spinal surgery.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Bisphosphonates inhibit bone turnover and may impair spinal fusion, especially if initiated immediately before or after surgery.
Answer 2: The presence of two or more vertebral compression fractures already meets clinical criteria for osteoporosis; DEXA results would not change management.
Answer 3: Failing to optimize bone health increases the risk of complications such as instrumentation failure and nonunion, especially in osteoporotic bone.
Answer 5: The patient has failed nonoperative management and continues to experience deformity and functional impairment, making surgical evaluation and bone optimization necessary.
