## Question

Figure 46 shows the radiograph of a 65-year-old man who reports restricted range of motion and pain with sitting 18 months after undergoing right side revision total hip arthroplasty. What is the most appropriate management?

![[q7034-a-46.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Intensive physiotherapy
2. Alendronate
3. Indomethacin
4. Radiotherapy
5. Excision

## Correct option

**5. Excision**

## Answer explanation

The presence of Brooker grade 1 or 2 heterotopic ossification (HO) does not influence the outcome of total hip arthroplasty, whereas restricted range of motion and pain may occur in patients with more severe grade 3 or 4 HO. Treatment may be nonsurgical or surgical. Nonsurgical management includes intensive physiotherapy during the maturation phase of the disease in an attempt to limit the final stiffness. There appears to be no data regarding the effectiveness of this treatment. There is no role for NSAIDs or radiotherapy as a treatment for preexisting HO. Surgical treatment involves excision of the heterotopic bone and can be expected to improve the functional outcome. Bisphosphonates have been used in the past, but their use has been discontinued as they only postpone ossification until treatment is stopped.
