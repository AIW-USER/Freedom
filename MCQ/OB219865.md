## Question

An adult reconstructive surgeon's practice involves changing their closure technique on a case-by-case basis. The surgeon decides to pursue a quality improvement project, to evaluate the relationship between various closure techniques and the development of hip instability and infection rates. What component of the study protocol will best reduce the risk of confounding?

## Options

1. Randomization
2. Hawthorne Effect
3. Blinding
4. Perform power analysis
5. Perform survivorship analysis

## Correct option

**1. Randomization**

## Answer explanation

The proposed quality improvement study has numerous potential confounders, including intraoperative stability, cup position, body mass index, and other variables. Randomly assigning subjects to groups can help distribute these confounding variables evenly, reducing their overall impact (Answer 1).

Confounding variables in statistics are external factors that can distort the true relationship between a study's independent and dependent variables. These variables are not the focus of the research but can influence both the predictor and outcome, leading to misleading conclusions if not properly accounted for. Identifying and adjusting for confounding variables is essential in scientific studies to ensure the validity and reliability of the results. Failing to control for confounders can create a false association or mask a true one, potentially leading to incorrect interpretations of causal relationships. Techniques like stratification, multivariable regression analysis, and randomization help minimize the impact of confounding, improving the overall rigor and reliability of the research findings.

*Vavken et al. performed a cross-sectional study of 126 controlled trials to determine whether studies routinely adjust results to account for confounding.* **The authors found** that less than half of trials considered confounding, and even fewer (30%) attempted to control for confounding. **They also found** studies were more likely to control for confounding if a methodologically trained researcher participated. **The authors conclude** that the validity of published studies may need to be questioned when confounding was not considered.

*Petrie et al. review the terminology involved in simple data analysis and outline areas of medical statistics within the context of orthopedic research.* *The authors also analyze common errors that occur in published orthopedic journals.* Specifically, **they identified** mistakes in the design, analysis, presentation, and/or interpretation of results. **They propose** that the majority of errors could be decreased if study design and analysis were performed by statisticians with an adequate understanding of statistical theory.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: The Hawthorne effect is a type of human behavior reactivity that occurs when people change their behavior because they know they are being observed.
Answer 3: Blinding patients to their closure technique may improve the overall internal validity of their study findings. However, it would not directly address potential confounding.
Answer 4: Performing a power analysis would help determine the number of patients required to see a potential difference between techniques but would not directly influence confounding in any way. Regardless of the group sizes determined by the power analysis, either recognized or unrecognized bias toward one technique versus another may exist.
Answer 5: Survivorship analysis could help determine the superiority of one technique over another but would not help reduce confounding as bias toward one technique versus another based on patient factors may remain unaddressed.
