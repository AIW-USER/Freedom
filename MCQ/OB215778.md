## Question

A 28-year-old male IV drug user presents to the ED with a forearm abscess after injecting heroin. Incision and drainage is performed and cultures reveal Methicillin-sensitive Staphylococcus Aureus (MSSA). He is treated with intravenous Cefazolin and transitioned to oral Cephalexin as an outpatient. He returns to the ED 2 months later with a similar forearm abscess. Repeat incision and drainage is performed and cultures/sensitivities are again obtained. Staphylococcus aureus is identified, however, it is now resistant to Methicillin and several other penicillins and cephalosporins. What is the primary mechanism by which this antibiotic resistance develops?

## Options

1. Decrease in cell membrane permeability
2. Overexpression of efflux pumps
3. Alteration of peptidoglycan subunits
4. Genetic mutation of mecA gene
5. Formation of biofilm

## Correct option

**4. Genetic mutation of mecA gene**

## Answer explanation

The development of methicillin resistance in Staphylococcus aureus infections is thought to be due to a genetic mutation that leads to the expression of the mecA gene encoding for penicillin binding protein 2a (PBP2a), which has a low affinity for beta-lactam antibiotics.

Antibiotic resistance is a difficult problem in the field of medicine today and several mechanisms of resistance exist. Of particular relevance is the rise of Methicillin Resistant Staphylococcus aureus (MRSA) infections. This is attributed to a genetic mutation of the mecA gene that encodes for PBP2a, which has decreased binding affinity for beta-lactam antibiotics. For this reason, it is important to obtain cultures and sensitivities in infectious cases to most accurately identify the bacteria and the most appropriate antibiotic choice. In cases of MRSA infection, vancomycin, trimethoprim/sulfamethoxazole (TMP/SMX), clindamycin, and linezolid are possible antibiotic choices as they do not function by attaching to PBPs.

*Chuard et al. studied the effects of antimicrobials on Staphylococcus aureus organisms chronically infected hardware in rats.* **They found** that the effects of all antibiotics studied were decreased in their ability to kill the Staphylococcus organisms in question. **They concluded** that antibiotic resistance and decreased susceptibility may become an issue in chronic in vivo infections.

Munita and Arias provided a review of the many mechanisms of antibiotic resistance. They note that as antibiotics have evolved, so have mechanisms of resistance including active efflux pumps, changes in cell wall permeability, and genetic modifications/transfers. They purport that understanding the reasons for resistance is crucial for decreasing resistant organism spread and designing new drug and treatment strategies.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Altered cell wall permeability is not thought to be the primary reason for the development of MRSA infections, although altered permeability can cause resistance to tetracyclines and fluoroquinolones.
Answer 2: Overexpression of efflux pumps is not the primary reason for MRSA infection resistance, although this can contribute to resistance of erythromcyin and tetracyline antibiotics.
Answer 3: Alteration of peptidoglycan subunits can contribute to vancomycin resistance as the D-ala D-ala subunit is altered.
Answer 5: Biofilm formation creates an environment which keeps organisms safe from the host immune response but this is a more common mechanism in Staphylococcus epidermidis and Salmonella infections.
