## Question

## Options

1. Low-dose oxycodone
2. Surgical resection
3. Radiofrequency ablation
4. Open biopsy
5. Vertebrectomy

## Correct option

**2. Surgical resection**

## Answer explanation

This patient has chronic neck pain from osteoid osteoma in the cervical spine. Osteoid osteoma refractory to medical treatment in posterior elements of the spine is treated with surgical resection when the lesion is close to neural elements.

Osteoid osteoma is a self-limited pathology, with most patients experiencing relief with NSAID administration and observation. Imaging studies often reveal a central nidus with a surrounding reactive zone. Failure of medical management necessitates surgical intervention. Unfortunately, the small nidus of a lesion is often difficult to localize without added morbidity and has led to the increased popularity of radiofrequency ablation. However, when lesions are in close proximity to the neural elements, surgical resection of the nidus is recommended.

*Hadjipavlou et al. compared the effectiveness of radiofrequency coagulation and surgical resection using minimally invasive methods.* **They reported** that both patients enjoyed immediate pain relief, were able to return to activities and remained symptom-free at 3-year followup. **They concluded** that when the nidus of the osteoid osteoma is not adjacent to the neural elements, radiofrequency thermal ablation is an effective and safe treatment of osteoid osteoma in the spine.

*Volkmer et al. published a review article on the use of the radiofrequency ablation in the treatment of musculoskeletal tumors.* **They reported** that osteoid osteomas are often self-limiting and the first line of treatment is NSAIDs with observation, with surgical intervention reserved after failure of medical management. Surgical options include radiofrequency ablation, which is indicated for painful scoliosis resulting from spinal lesions not adjacent to neural elements, or periarticular lesions which have an increased risk of cartilage injury with open surgical resection.

Figures A depicts a nidus representative of osteoid osteoma in the posterior elements of the cervical spine.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Low-dose narcotics are not an appropriate treatment for osteoid osteoma in the spine refractory to medical managaement.
Answer 3: When lesions are in close proximity to the neural elements, surgical resection of the nidus is recommended and not radiofrequency ablation.
Answer 4: Open biopsy is not indicated as the lesion is classic for osteoid osteoma.
Answer 5: Vertebrectomy is not indicated for this benign lesion.
