## Question

An 8-year-old male presents with complaints of left hip pain for 3 days. He denies any recent trauma and has a temperature of 99.8F. On examination, he has mild discomfort with internal rotation and abduction of the hip but otherwise has full painless motion of the hip. Laboratory studies show a WBC of 6,000/mL with an unremarkable differential, an ESR of 35 mm/h and a CRP of 1.8 mg/l. He is admitted overnight and started on ketorolac. Twenty-four hours later his pain has improved and he is ambulating in the hallway without difficulty. Which of the following is the most likely diagnosis?

## Options

1. Fracture
2. Juvenile idiopathic arthritis
3. Leukemia
4. Transient synovitis
5. Septic arthritis

## Correct option

**4. Transient synovitis**

## Answer explanation

The most likely cause for the clinical presentation is transient synovitis of the hip. Transient synovitis of the hip is an inflammation of the synovium and is the most common cause of hip pain in the pediatric population.

Transient synovitis of the hip presents in approximately 3% of children between the ages of 3-10 years and is known to have a high recurrence rate (~20%). The exact cause of transient synovitis of the hip is mostly unknown but is believed to be related to upper respiratory or bacterial infections, trauma, or allergic reactions. A hallmark of this disease process is rapid improvement is symptoms, usually within the first 24-48 hours, with complete resolution in 1-2 weeks. The patient may present with the hip in a flexed, abducted and externally rotated position to reduce intracapsular pressure; internal rotation and adduction may aggravate the hip. With low suspicion of septic arthritis, treatment is conservative with NSAIDs and close observation.

*De Cunto et al. review patients with poststreptococcal reactive arthritis/arthralgia.* **They report** that during the initial episode all patients had arthritis or arthralgia and a documented streptococcal infection. **They found** that one patient developed classic acute rheumatic fever (ARF) with valvulitis 18 months after the initial episode. **They concluded** that poststreptococcal reactive arthritis/arthralgias is part of the disease spectrum of ARF.

*Haueisen et al. performed a 30-year retrospective review of 497 cases of transient synovitis of the hip.* **They reported** no significant changes of the femoral head at follow-up though 2.5% of patients were found to have Legg-Calvé-Perthes disease at follow-up. **They noted** a recurrent synovitis with a benign prognosis in 19 patients. **They concluded** that despite a generally good prognosis in most cases, radiographic assessment at 6 months after the initial episode is recommended.

Incorrect Answer:
Answer 1: Fracture is unlikely without a history of trauma and improving symptoms with the ability to now ambulate
Answer 2: Juvenile idiopathic arthritis is unlikely to present with hip involvement
Answer 3: A normal CBC with differential makes the diagnosis of leukemia unlikely
Answer 5: Despite an elevated ESR, septic arthritis is unlikely due to the patient’s rapidly improving symptoms with a normal CBC and CRP
