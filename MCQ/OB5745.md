## Options

1. CT of the brain, chest, abdomen and pelvis, and bone scan
2. Chest radiograph and bone scan
3. CT of the chest, abdomen and pelvis, bone scan, and radiation oncology
4. CT of the chest and bone scan
5. CT of the chest, bone scan, and radiation oncology

## Correct option

**4. CT of the chest and bone scan**

## Answer explanation

Staging for osteosarcoma involves CT of the chest and a bone scan. Referral to oncology is necessary for initiation of neoadjuvant chemotherapy, and follow-up after surgery with adjuvant chemotherapy.

The National Comprehensive Cancer Network recommends the following for staging: plain radiographs, MRI of the extremity, CT of the chest and bone scan (with or without PET scan) for patients with suspected osteosarcoma. MRI will demonstrate the extent of tumor invasion into surrounding tissues, the extent of bone marrow replacement and the presence of skip lesions. Chest CT is more sensitive than chest radiography for evaluation of pulmonary metastases and acts as a baseline study to follow response to chemotherapy or evaluate for recurrence. Bone scan with Tc-99 methylene diphosphonate scintigraphy will reveal increased metabolic activity at the site of primary as well as discontinuous tumor and distant metastases.

*Messerschmitt et al. reviewed osteosarcoma.* Multi-agent chemotherapy aims to avoid chemoresistance and increase tumor necrosis. Survival can be up to 75% with resection of primary tumor and solitary lung metastases. Long term survival is 90% for parosteal osteosarcoma, 80% for periosteal osteosarcoma, and 60-75% for localized osteosarcoma (all variants combined).

Figures A and B are the AP radiograph and coronal T2-weighted MRI, respectively, of his aggressive proximal lateral tibial osteosarcoma. Figures C and D show low and high power micrographs of osteosarcoma demonstrating deposition of lacey osteoid and interspersed highly pleomorphic cells.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1, 4, 5: CT of the abdomen and pelvis is unnecessary as the most common site of metastases is the lung (80% of all metastases). The 2nd most common site of metastases is bone (skip lesions, in 1-10% of patients with metastases). Radiation oncology is an unnecessary referral as the treatment of osteosarcoma involves a multidisciplinary approach with surgery and oncology.
Answer 2: Chest CT is more sensitive than chest radiography for evaluation of pulmonary metastases.
