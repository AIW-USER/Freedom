## Question

A 78-year-old man presents to the foot and ankle clinic with chronic right foot and ankle pain. His medical history is significant for symptomatic severe aortic stenosis and is scheduled for an aortic valve replacement next week. Plain radiographs demonstrate decreased calcaneal pitch, increased Meary’s angle, with subtalar arthritis and talar tilt. Physical examination demonstrates the inability to perform a single-leg heel raise, severe sinus tarsi pain, and a rigid hindfoot valgus deformity. What is the most appropriate treatment at this time?

## Options

1. Tibiotalocalcaneal arthrodesis
2. Triple arthrodesis
3. A custom-molded ankle-foot orthosis (AFO)
4. Triple arthrodesis with associated deltoid ligament reconstruction
5. A custom-molded in-shoe orthosis with a medial forefoot post

## Correct option

**3. A custom-molded ankle-foot orthosis (AFO)**

## Answer explanation

The radiographic findings of decreased calcaneal pitch, increased Meary’s angle, subtalar arthritis, and talar tilt, combined with the inability to perform a single-leg heel raise, severe sinus tarsi pain, and a rigid hindfoot valgus deformity on physical exam, are consistent with advanced adult acquired flatfoot deformity (AAFD). Given the patient’s history of symptomatic severe aortic stenosis, at this time, the patient is not an operative candidate, and non-operative management in a custom-molded AFO is the most appropriate treatment at this time (answer choice 3).

Adult-acquired flatfoot deformity is a progressive disease typically associated with posterior tibial tendon insufficiency. The disease begins with tenosynovitis of the posterior tibial tendon. As tenosynovitis progresses to insufficiency, the failure of this dynamic arch stabilizer leads to attritional failure of static stabilizers (e.g., spring ligament, plantar ligaments, etc.). Patients with significant medical comorbidities who are not good surgical candidates should be treated with non-operative management, including activity modification, physical therapy, NSAIDs, and orthotics. Many orthoses are available for the treatment of AAFD. In cases with advanced AAFD with rigid deformities, a custom-molded orthosis fitted in the in situ position is appropriate.

*Henry et al. review the pathogenesis, classification, and treatment of AAFD.* **They noted** that there is limited evidence to guide surgeons on the optimal procedure. **They conclude** that further research is needed to clarify and support surgical indications and techniques.

*Neville et al. review the effect of the shoe only, custom solid Arizona AFO, custom articulated Arizona AFO, and off-the-shelf AirLift AFO on foot kinematics in patients with stage II posterior tibial tendon dysfunction.* **They noted** that the custom articulated Arizona brace showed greater improvements in hindfoot inversion, all devices improved forefoot plantarflexion, and none corrected forefoot abduction. **They concluded** that in stage II posterior tibial tendon dysfunction, custom Arizona devices provide superior hindfoot control, and all devices they included can raise the medial arch; however, no device can completely correct flatfoot deformity, particularly forefoot abduction.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Tibiotalocalcaneal arthrodesis is an acceptable treatment for stage IV AAFD; however, the patient is not a good surgical candidate, given his history of severe symptomatic aortic stenosis.
Answer 2: Triple arthrodesis is indicated for stage III AAFD.
Answer 4: Triple arthrodesis with a deltoid ligament reconstruction is an acceptable treatment in patients with stage IV AAFD with correctable ankle valgus deformity.
Answer 5: A custom-molded in-shoe arthrosis with a medial forefoot post is more appropriate for patients with stage I or stage II AAFD with forefoot varus.
