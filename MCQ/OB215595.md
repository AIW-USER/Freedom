## Question

A 32-year-old patient presents with multiple orthopedic injuries after being involved in a motor vehicle accident. Upon arrival, the patient was found to be GCS 8 and is unable to participate in a clinical exam. EMS stated the patient was extricated from a 1970s vehicle model. Based on the clinical finding in Figure A, what injury should be suspected?

![[q215595-a-seatbelt-sign.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. L5 transverse process fracture
2. Burst fracture
3. Flexion-distraction injury
4. Lumbar pars fracture
5. Lumbar herniated disc

## Correct option

**3. Flexion-distraction injury**

## Answer explanation

The patient is presenting with a seatbelt sign after a motor vehicle accident, which may suggest a concomitant flexion-distraction injury (Chance fracture) of the thoracolumbar spine.

Chance fractures are the result of a flexion-distraction mechanism of the spine that can result in either purely ligamentous, purely bony, or a combination of bony and soft tissue injuries. Classically, flexion-distraction injuries occur in conjunction with lap seatbelt injuries, which leads to a center of rotation anterior to the spinal column during high-speed deceleration. Abdominal injuries are common with flexion-distraction injuries and should be ruled out when a Chance fracture is identified.

*Grossbach et al. prospectively studied 39 patients with flexion-distraction injuries of the thoracolumbar spine treated with either open posterior instrumentation and fusion or minimally invasive (MIS) percutaneous pedicle screw fixation.* **They reported** a lower blood loss and shorter intraoperative time with MIS fixation with no difference in kyphotic alignment at final follow-up. **They concluded** MIS pedicle screw fixation is equally effective as open posterior instrumentation and fusion in treating flexion-distraction thoracolumbar injuries.

*Mikles et al. reviewed posterior instrumentation for thoracolumbar fractures.* **They recommended** treatment of flexion-distraction injuries associated ligamentous disruption with posterior pedicle instrumentation and fusion to reconstitute the posterior tension band.

Figure A is a clinical photograph of an abdomen with bruising around the waistline that is characteristic of a seatbelt sign.

Incorrect answers
Answer 1: L5 transverse process fractures are seen in unstable pelvic ring injuries when the iliolumbar ligaments are avulsed from the L5 transverse process.
Answer 2: Burst fractures are the result of axial compression with a flexion moment. These can occur from motor vehicle accidents but have an axis of rotation that is in the middle column leading to compression failure of the anterior and middle columns.
Answer 4: Lumbar pars fractures are uncommon in acute trauma, but can occur as stress fractures in athletes with frequent hyperextension movements such as gymnasts and linebackers.
Answer 5: Lumbar herniated disc can occur from overloading the intervertebral disc leading to a defect in the annulus fibrosis. This may precipitate from lifting a heavy object with the lumbar spine in a flexed position.
