## Question

A 28-year-old male, with a history of IV drug abuse, presents to the emergency department with pain and swelling in his ankle and difficulty with range of motion for the past 2 days. Temperature is 100.5°F and remainder of vital signs are within normal limits. Sterile ankle joint aspiration shows synovial white blood cell count (WBC/mm3) of 95,000, and polymorphonuclear differential of 90% with evidence of negatively birefringent crystals. What is the next best step in management?

## Options

1. Await culture results
2. IV antibiotics for 24 hours followed by reassessment
3. Multimodal pain management
4. Obtain serum ESR, CRP, uric acid
5. Proceed to the OR urgently for incision and drainage of ankle

## Correct option

**5. Proceed to the OR urgently for incision and drainage of ankle**

## Answer explanation

This patient’s clinical presentation and synovial fluid analysis with WBC count of 95,000 are both suggestive of septic arthritis. Treatment for septic arthritis is urgent incision and drainage for joint preservation.

Septic arthritis can occur in any synovial joint, with the knee, hip, shoulder, elbow, and ankle joints affected most commonly, in that order. Patients normally present with an erythematous, painful joint and significant pain with any passive or active range of motion. The gold standard of diagnosis is joint aspiration with synovial fluid analysis for cell count with differential, gram stain, culture, and crystals. In a native joint, cell count with >50,000 WBC is considered diagnostic for septic arthritis and operative irrigation and drainage of the joint is required in an urgent manner as delayed treatment can cause extensive cartilage damage.

*Movassaghi et al. review septic ankle arthritis and reiterate its severity.* **They report** that patients typically present with warm, swollen joints which have minimal range of motion, and joint aspiration should be performed in this setting. **They conclude** by stating septic arthritis of the ankle is an urgent orthopedic condition with the risk of significant damage to the ankle joint, for which surgical drainage should not be delayed.

*Holtom et al. retrospectively reviewed hematogenous septic arthritis of the ankle to explore diagnostic lab values, common pathogens, and associated conditions.* **They reported** that CRP, ESR, and WBC were consistently elevated, with Staphylococcus aureus being the most common pathogen isolated and 30% of patients having osteomyelitis in the bones surrounding the ankle joint. **They conclude** that septic arthritis of the ankle is a severe problem and clinicians must be aware of the associated complications.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1-4: With a synovial fluid analysis showing WBC count >50,000, septic arthritis of the ankle is presumed, and no further testing should delay urgent incision and drainage of the joint. Concomitant gout/pseudogout does not eliminate the need for appropriate operative management of septic arthritis.
