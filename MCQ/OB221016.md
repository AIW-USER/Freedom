## Question

A 22-year-old collegiate distance runner presents with 6 weeks of progressively worsening heel pain that began insidiously during peak training. The pain is activity-related, worsens with running and jumping, and improves with rest, but has recently begun with walking. On physical exam, there is focal tenderness over the posteromedial calcaneal body, pain reproduced with calcaneal squeeze testing, and discomfort with a single-leg heel raise. Plain radiographs are normal, and an MRI of the hindfoot is obtained. Which of the following MRI findings would most likely be expected?

## Options

1. Bone marrow edema with a linear low-signal fracture line on T1-weighted images
2. Diffuse plantar fascia thickening with increased signal at the calcaneal insertion
3. Focal osteochondral defect of the posterior subtalar facet
4. Increased signal and partial tearing at the Achilles tendon insertion
5. Low-signal serpiginous line with central fatty marrow, consistent with avascular necrosis

## Correct option

**1. Bone marrow edema with a linear low-signal fracture line on T1-weighted images**

## Answer explanation

The patient’s clinical presentation is classic for a calcaneal stress fracture with insidious heel pain in a high-mileage runner, focal tenderness over the calcaneal body, and pain reproduced with calcaneal squeeze testing. The expected finding on MRI would include bone marrow edema with a linear low-signal fracture line on T1-weighted images (Answer Choice 1).

MRI is the most sensitive modality for diagnosing calcaneal stress fractures, particularly when radiographs are normal. The earliest and most consistent MRI finding is bone marrow edema within the calcaneal body, best seen on T2-weighted or STIR sequences. As the injury progresses, a linear low-signal fracture line on T1-weighted images may appear, confirming a true stress fracture rather than a stress reaction.

*Filho et al.* **identified** clinical and demographic risk factors for foot and ankle insufficiency fractures in postmenopausal sedentary women. **The authors found** that osteoporosis, vitamin D deficiency, higher BMI, and delayed diagnosis were significantly associated with insufficiency fractures, with the calcaneus and metatarsals being the most common sites. **They concluded** that insufficiency fractures should be strongly considered in postmenopausal women with atraumatic foot or ankle pain.

*Chimutengwende-Gordon et al. evaluated MRI findings in patients presenting with plantar heel pain and correlated imaging features with underlying pathology.* MRI showed that plantar fasciitis was most commonly associated with plantar fascia thickening and perifascial edema, while calcaneal bone marrow edema and stress fractures were also frequent contributors to heel pain. **The authors concluded** that MRI is valuable in differentiating plantar fasciitis from alternative causes such as calcaneal stress fractures, particularly in patients with atypical symptoms or refractory pain.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Plantar fascia thickening and insertional signal changes are characteristic of plantar fasciitis.
Answer 3: Osteochondral defects of the subtalar joint typically cause deep ankle or hindfoot pain with joint loading rather than focal calcaneal tenderness.
Answer 4: Achilles insertional pathology causes posterior heel pain and tenderness at the tendon insertion.
Answer 5: Avascular necrosis shows serpiginous margins with preserved fatty marrow and is not associated with focal squeeze tenderness or overuse history.
