## Question

A 15-year-old otherwise healthy female presents with progressive scoliosis. She has an apex-right thoracic curve measuring 55° that corrects to 25° on side bending, and an apex-left lumbar curve measuring 30° that corrects to 10°. On the lateral view radiograph, you note a thoracolumbar kyphotic deformity involving the T8-L2 levels, with 70% of the L2 vertebral body resting posterior to the posterior sacral vertebral line (PSVL), and 40% of the L3 vertebral body resting posterior to the PSVL. The posteroanterior (PA) radiograph additionally reveals that the L4 vertebrae is tilted to the right. To reduce the risk of distal junctional kyphosis (DJK) and need for revision to extend the fusion, which of the following is the most appropriate lowest instrumented vertebra (LIV) level?

## Options

1. T12
2. L2
3. L3
4. L5
5. Pelvic fixation is indicated.

## Correct option

**3. L3**

## Answer explanation

With improper selection of the LIV, patients with Adolescent Idiopathic Scoliosis (AIS) are at risk of developing curve progression or postoperative DJK, requiring reoperation to extend their fusion construct further distally (i.e., "adding-on"). This patient has two risk factors for adding-on: a thoracolumbar kyphotic deformity, and a Lenke 1A-R subtype curve. Ending the fusion construct above the stable sagittal vertebra (SSV), in this question the L2-L3 level, significantly increases the risk of the eventual development of DJK. Additionally, the presence of a right-tilted L4 vertebra, explained further below, places them at higher risk for postoperative curve progression and the need for adding-on. Choosing L3 as the lowest instrumented vertebra (LIV) is appropriate and effectively minimizes the risk of distal junctional kyphosis (DJK) and coronal plane lumbar curve progression, while preserving some lumbar spine mobility and avoiding fusion at transitional regions of the spine.

In adolescent idiopathic scoliosis (AIS), selecting the proper LIV is crucial to optimize postoperative spinal mobility while reducing the need for reoperation. There are multiple factors to consider when deciding the appropriate level. One such factor is the presence of thoracolumbar kyphosis. Including the first SSV, a concept first described in the thoracic hyperkyphosis (i.e., Scheuermann's Kyphosis) literature and later adopted to the AIS literature where it was defined as the level at which 50% of the vertebral body is in front of the posterior sacral vertical line, is an important consideration in selecting the LIV. Furthermore, in Lenke 1A-R curves, where the L4 vertebra is tilted right, there is a greater risk of postoperative adding-on if the fusion does not extend sufficiently. Whereas a left-tilted L4 vertebra suggests a more compensatory function to the main thoracic curve, a right-tilted vertebra suggests that the lumbar deformity may play a more active role in overall spinal balance. As such, this curve subtype has been shown to have a higher risk of postoperative curve progression and need for adding-on. Ensuring the fusion extends to or below the sagittal stable vertebra (SSV), as well as to or near the last substantially touched vertebra (LSTV), have been supported by the AIS literature as strategies to minimize the risks of postoperative DJK, curve progression, and ultimately, adding-on.

*Segal et al. performed a retrospective review of 498 patients with AIS treated with posterior spinal fusion, who had discordance between their lowest touched vertebra (LTV) on PA radiographs and SSV on lateral radiographs.* The LTV was chosen as it has historically been utilized as one method for selecting the LIV. At 5-year follow-up, **they found** that in cases where the SSV was below the LTV and the fusion construct had therefore ended proximal to the SSV, there was a significant increase in the risk of development of DJK, particularly when the fusion ended at T12 or T11, and less so L1 or L2. **They concluded** that including the SSV in the fusion construct is an important factor in minimizing DJK risk, with particularly high caution needed when considering constructs ending near the thoracolumbar junction.

*Cho et al. investigated which Lenke 1A curves are at the greatest risk for adding-on and why.* **They noted** that compared to Lenke 1A-L curves, 1A-R curves are more susceptible to adding-on due to the lumbar curve's tendency to progress if not adequately included. Additionally, **they noted** that despite the overall lower revision rate for Lenke 1A-L, certain populations, such as younger and less skeletally mature patients with this deformity type, remain at an elevated risk. **They concluded** that to prevent adding-on for 1A-R curves; one should fuse distally to 1 level above the neutral vertebra or 1 to 2 levels above the stable vertebra.

*Segal et al. examined whether fusions ending above the SSV affect outcomes in AIS patients.* *At 5-year follow-up,* **they found** a significant difference in the risk of developing DJK depending on the location of the LIV in relation to the SSV. **They reported** that patients with fusion constructs extending to below the SSV developed DJK at a rate of 2.2%, compared to 6.5% for those with fusions ending at the SSV and 15% for those with fusions with LIV above the SSV (p < 0.001). **They concluded** that although there was no statistically significant difference in revision rates, including the SSV in the fusion is important to achieve optimal sagittal alignment and reduce the risk of DJK development.

*Yang et al. performed a multicenter retrospective review of 113 AIS patients with Lenke type 1-3 curves, with either a B or C lumbar modifier, with mean follow-up of 3 years.* **They found** a significantly higher rate of DJK in constructs that did not include the SSV (8/46, 17%), compared to cases where the LIV was chosen at or below the SSV (0/67, 0%). **They concluded** that although LIV selection is complex, selecting an LIV at or below the SSV is a simple rule to minimize the risk of DJK.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer choice 1: Ending the fusion at T12 places the LIV at the thoracolumbar junction, increasing mechanical stress and the risk of DJK due to transitional anatomy. It additionally ends the fusion construct proximal to the SSV.

Answer choice 2: Stopping at L2 may not sufficiently address the thoracolumbar kyphotic deformity, as the question stem notes that the majority of this vertebral body is posterior to the PSVL, and suggests that the L3 vertebra is the first SSV. Fusing short of the SSV has been shown to increase the risk of DJK.

Answer choice 4: Extending the fusion to L5 unnecessarily limits lumbar motion and may predispose the patient to early degenerative changes at the lumbosacral junction without significant additional benefit.

Answer choice 5: Pelvic fixation is excessive for this case, as it is typically reserved for patients with pelvic obliquity or sacropelvic pathology, and would unnecessarily increase surgical morbidity.
