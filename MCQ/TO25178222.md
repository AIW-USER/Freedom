Chapter: Trauma MCQs — Sacral Fracture

## Question

A 25-year-old female presents with a "U-shaped" sacral fracture (bilateral Zone II vertical fractures connected by a transverse fracture through S2). She has loss of bladder control. What is the biomechanically superior construct to allow for both neurological decompression and early weight-bearing?

## Options

1. Bilateral percutaneous S1 iliosacral screws.
2. S1 laminectomy followed by posterior tension band plating.
3. Triangular Osteosynthesis (Lumbopelvic fixation + Trans-sacral screw).
4. Trans-iliac trans-sacral screw at S1 and S2 only.

## Correct option

**3. Triangular Osteosynthesis (Lumbopelvic fixation + Trans-sacral screw).**

## Answer explanation

U-shaped fractures cause lumbopelvic dissociation. The spine is essentially "floating" from the pelvis. Isolated screws (A or D) cannot resist the vertical shear and axial loads of the spine. Triangular Osteosynthesis transfers the load from the L4/L5 pedicles directly to the iliac wings, bypassing the fractured sacrum entirely. This provides the stability needed to perform a laminectomy safely and allows the patient to mobilize immediately.

Answer: 3
