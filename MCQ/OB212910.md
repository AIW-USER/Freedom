## Options

1. Anterior interosseous nerve (AIN); observation
2. AIN; neurolysis
3. Median nerve; observation
4. Median nerve; neurolysis
5. Ulnar nerve; observation

## Correct option

**1. Anterior interosseous nerve (AIN); observation**

## Answer explanation

This patient sustained an extension-type supracondylar fracture. The most likely nerve deficit is the AIN and the next best step in treating the nerve injury is observation.

Supracondylar humerus fractures are common in children. Thorough neurologic assessment may be difficult because of pain, anxiety, or poor cooperation with the examination, but is imperative. A vascular examination of the limb is critical in these injuries as well. AIN palsy is the most common nerve palsy seen with extension-type injuries. The AIN, a branch of the median nerve, is principally a motor nerve and innervates the flexor digitorum profundus (index and middle), flexor pollicis longus and pronator quadratus. It is usually tested by having the child give an OK sign. This confirms flexion of the index digit distal interphalangeal (DIP) joint and flexion of the thumb interphalangeal (IP) joint. AIN palsies typically recover spontaneously and therefore may be observed.

*Cramer et al. performed a retrospective review of 101 supracondylar humerus fractures in children.* *6 had an isolated AIN palsy and 4 had an AIN palsy with another nerve injury producing a sensory deficit.* *2 had a complete median nerve palsy.* **They conclude** that because an AIN palsy results in motor loss only, this injury may be easily overlooked.

*Abzug et al. performed a review on the management of supracondylar humerus fractures in children.* **They report** that in patients with an extension-type injury, AIN palsy is the most common followed by median, radial, and ulnar nerve injuries. The ulnar nerve is most commonly injured with flexion-type injuries. **They conclude** that in most patients, neurologic deficit identified at the time of injury resolves in 6-12 weeks.

Figures A and B are AP and lateral radiographs of the elbow demonstrating an extension-type supracondylar humerus fracture.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 2, 3, 4, & 5: AIN palsy is the most common palsy with extension-type supracondylar fractures and should initially be treated with observation.
