Chapter: Arthroscopy MCQs — Meniscus Injuries

## Question

Double PCL sign is seen in (FNB Pattern Paper 2020)

## Options

1. Anterior horn of medial meniscus
2. Posterior horn of medial meniscus
3. Bucket handle tear of medial meniscus
4. Anterior horn of lateral meniscus

## Correct option

**3. Bucket handle tear of medial meniscus**

## Answer explanation

Double anterior horn sign (double meniscus sign): it indicates flipped meniscal tear

![[TO17119237-1.png]]

Empty meniscus sign (ghost meniscus): it indicates complete radial tear

![[TO17119237-2.png]]

Double PCL sign: indicates bucket handle tear of medial meniscus

![[TO17119237-3.png]]

Vacuum phenomenon: A vacuum phenomenon is caused by negative pressure within the joint due to the position of the patient, which results in the accumulation of nitrogen gas. It looks like a tear within a discoid meniscus.

![[TO17119000-1.png]]

Loss of bowtie sign: The normal meniscus resembles a bow tie on sagittal images on MRI. The absent bow tie sign on the sagittal image is present when fewer than two distinct bow tie segments of the meniscus are seen on sequential images. As the normal meniscus laterally measures 9-12 mm in width, it should be seen on a number of adjacent images (2-3 images if imaged with 3 mm thick slices with a 1 mm skip). So absence of bow tie on 2 consecutive sagittal images indicates loss of meniscal volume indicating bucket handle tear.

This sign has a higher accuracy rate than other findings common with bucket-handle tears, such as the double PCL sign.

Meniscus within meniscus sign: Sometimes extensive triangular or wedge-shaped high signal intensity can be encountered that does not reach the surface of the meniscus.
This is sometimes referred to as meniscus within the meniscus sign.
Since this meniscal abnormality does not reach the meniscal surface, it does not fulfill the criteria for a meniscal tear.

![[TO17119000-2.png]]

Cleft sign: it indicates radial meniscal tear.

![[TO17119000-3.png]]

Answer: 3
