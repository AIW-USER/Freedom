## Question

A 21-year-old woman presents to the ED with a complaint of left knee pain after falling during a basketball game. Figure A demonstrates a radiograph of the left knee. Following successful reduction, the patient asks what the chances are of this happening again. Which of the following is a protective factor for recurrent dislocation in this patient?

![[q2207-a-patellar-dislocation-xray.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Age >20
2. Prior instability episode
3. High and lateral position of the patella on of the unaffected knee
4. Passive lateral hypermobility of the unaffected knee
5. Dysplastic vastus medialis obliquus muscle on the unaffected knee

## Correct option

**1. Age >20**

## Answer explanation

A patient who sustains a primary patellar dislocation episode over the age of 20 has a lower rate of recurrence compared to a primary patellar dislocation under the age of 20.

Patella instability and dislocation is often seen in young, active individuals following a traumatic event. Predisposition to dislocation can be due to a combination of bony (trochlear dysplasia, high Q-angle, high TT-TG) and soft tissue (weak VMO, patella alta, tight lateral structures) factors. Systemic laxity such as Ehlers Danlos or family history of laxity can similarly put patients at risk for dislocation. Predicting which patients will go on to repeat dislocation is critical, as these patients would likely benefit from surgical intervention. It appears clear that second time dislocators, or those with prior instability episodes, are at risk to continue to dislocate. Those patients who experience a first time dislocation under the age of 20 also appear to be at risk for recurrence compared to those over 20. The remainder of the architectural and systemic factors that can contribute to instability likely predispose individuals to recurrence.

*Cash et al. reviewed the charts of 399 patients diagnosed with an acute patellar dislocation over a 30 year period.* **They found** outcomes were improved in patients who had no signs of congenital predisposition to dislocate on their contralateral knee. Signs of congenital predisposition to dislocate included high and lateral position of the patella, passive lateral hypermobility, and a dysplastic vastus medialis obliquus (VMO).

*Larsen et al. reviewed 78 cases of patellar dislocation treated non-operatively.* **They found** no single factor caused more dislocation. **They also state** that recurrence rates were significantly higher in those patients who sustained their index dislocation at <20 years of age compared those who dislocated at >20 years of age.

Figure A shows an AP radiograph with a lateral dislocation of the left patella. Illustration A shows a clinical photograph of a patient with a left patellar dislocation as evident by the lateral soft tissue bulging.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: A prior instability episode is one of the best predictors of recurrent instability.
Answer 3: A high and lateral position of the patella on the unaffected knee suggests the patella is congenitally positioned in a position at risk for dislocation.
Answer 4: Lateral instability of the unaffected knee suggests a congenital predisposition to dislocate and puts the patient at risk for recurrent dislocation.
Answer 5: A dysplastic VMO places the knee at risk due to a lack of medial pull allowing the patella to laterally displace or dislocate.
