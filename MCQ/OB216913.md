## Question

A 45-year-old cruise director slipped while at work injuring his left non-dominant shoulder. On exam, he's unable to actively lift his arm away from his side. An MRI obtained after two weeks of physical therapy demonstrates a full thickness rotator cuff tear. Which of the following is true as it relates to outcomes following surgical repair of this injury?

## Options

1. Surgery within 4 weeks from injury is associated with poorer clinical outcomes
2. Work related injuries are associated with poorer clinical outcomes
3. This patient's age is associated with poorer clinical outcomes
4. Non-dominant arm injuries are associated with improved clinical outcomes
5. Operative time >45 minutes is associated with improved clinical outcomes

## Correct option

**2. Work related injuries are associated with poorer clinical outcomes**

## Answer explanation

Patients undergoing rotator cuff surgery for a work related injury have been found to have poorer clinical outcomes than those undergoing surgery for non-work related injuries.

Rotator cuff injuries are among the most commonly encountered work related injuries seen by orthopedic surgeons and are the second most common reason for manual laborers to miss work. Many patients ultimately require surgical fixation in order to achieve function and return to work. When compared to patients who sustained non work related injuries, several studies have demonstrated the worse recovery (i.e. physical measures, quality of life), patient satisfaction and longer time periods to return to employment or full activities following work related rotator cuff injuries. Other factors including age, timing of surgery and operative time have not been linked to worsening outcomes in this cohort.

*Bhatia et al review early return to work following arthroscopic rotator cuff repair in workers' compensation patients.* **They report** that workers' compensation patients have a high likelihood of returning to full duty at a mean time of 7.6 months. **They conclude**, however, that subjective outcomes are inferior in these patients compared to those who sustain non work related injuries.

*Kemp et al review rotator cuff tears in workers' compensation patients.* **They report** that although workers' patients experience substantial benefits from various treatments for full-thickness rotator cuff tears, disparities exist between them and their non-WC counterparts. **They conclude** that further research examining compensation status, patient characteristics and the nature of employment as primary outcomes are necessary in order to clarify the mechanisms responsible for these inferior outcomes.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Performing surgery for acute rotator cuff tears within 3 moths of injury has been linked to improved outcomes in some studies
Answer 3: Age >70 years had been linked with inferior clinical outcomes, however, the patient's stated age of 40 is not a risk factor for poor clinical outcomes.
Answer 4: No difference has been found in the functional outcomes of rotator cuff repair between dominant and non-dominant side surgery.
Answer 5: Longer operative time has been linked with poorer short-term outcomes including required overnight hospital stay and surgical site infections.
