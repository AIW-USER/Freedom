## Question

What is the best first line treatment for plantar fascial rupture?

## Options

1. Urgent primary repair
2. Delayed primary repair
3. Cast immobilization for 3-6 weeks
4. Supervised stretching program
5. Corticosteroid injections

## Correct option

**3. Cast immobilization for 3-6 weeks**

## Answer explanation

Plantar fascia rupture can be treated effectively with cast immobilization for 3-6 weeks.

The plantar fascia is a nonelastic, multilayered fibrous structure that acts via a windlass mechanism to support the foot arch in the late stage of stance phase. Rupture of the plantar fascia has been shown to occur acutely in athletes, or as part of a degenerative process in patients with pre-existing plantar fasciitis. Treatment is almost exclusively non-operative in a cast immobilization for 3-6 weeks.

*Kim et al. performed a retrospective chart review of 120 patients who received corticosteroid injection for plantar fasciitis.* **They found** four patients (2.4%) that suffered plantar fascia rupture following an average of 2.67 injections. The average BMI of these patients was 38.6 kg/m². **They concluded** that corticosteroid injection therapy appears to have a relatively low incidence of plantar fascia rupture.

*Acevedo et al. evaluated 765 patients with a clinical diagnosis of plantar fasciitis.* *51 patients suffered plantar fascia rupture.* *44 of these ruptures were associated with corticosteroid injection.* Rupture resulted in lateral and dorsal midfoot strain, lateral plantar nerve dysfunction, stress fracture, hammertoe deformity, and long term swelling.

Incorrect Answers
Answer 1,2: There is no good evidence to support plantar fascia repair as first-line treatment.
Answer 4: Supervised stretching programs should begin after a period of immobilization.
Answer 5: Corticosteroid injections are a known risk factor for rupture and would not be used in the setting of tears or rupture.
