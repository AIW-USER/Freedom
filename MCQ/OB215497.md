## Question

Which of the following would reduce the risk of osteolysis in total knee arthroplasty?

## Options

1. Use of an all-polyethylene tibial component
2. Decreasing congruency of polyethylene
3. Avoiding use of highly cross-linked polyethylene (HCLPE)
4. Use of varus-valgus constrained implant
5. Use of a metal tibial baseplate with modular polyethylene insert

## Correct option

**1. Use of an all-polyethylene tibial component**

## Answer explanation

Using an all-polyethylene tibial component decreases the risk of osteolysis in total knee arthroplasty (TKA) because this design avoids backside wear commonly seen between the polyethylene and modular metal baseplate.

Osteolysis in TKA can occur for a number of reasons, but is ultimately secondary to debris being disseminated into the joint space and causing activation of macrophages. Macrophages activate RANK-L leading to osteoclast-driven bony degradation and may cause aseptic loosening. Factors which affect osteolysis include type of polyethylene used, presence of backside wear with modular components, the degree of constraint, and the design and alignment of the components. Patients usually complain of localized pain and osteolysis can be identified on radiographs or CT. If the osteolysis progresses, these patients may eventually require revision TKA.

*Browne et al. review the use of all-polyethylene tibial components with modern TKA.* **They note** that there are advantages to a metal-backed tibial component including ease in revision settings and possible biomechanical superiority, however, no clinical or radiographic benefits have been found and all-polyethylene tibial components may decrease backside wear. **They conclude** that all-polyethylene tibial components are non-inferior and given lower cost, continue to have a role in modern TKA.

*Gioe et al review the use of all-polyethylene tibial components in primary TKA and* **note** that past studies have found them to be similar to modular tibial components with regards to clinical and radiostereometric data. There are certain advantages to the all-polyethylene design including lower cost, less backside wear, and greater final polyethylene thickness. **They conclude** that despite a lack of modularity, all-polyethylene tibial components represent a cost-effective solution in modern TKA.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Decreasing the congruency of the polyethylene component increases sliding of the femoral component on the polyethylene and may increase adhesive wear, leading to debris and subsequent osteolysis
Answer 3: Highly cross-linked polyethylene (HCLPE) is thought to decrease the amount of debris produced, therefore decreasing osteolysis
Answer 4: Increasing the constraint of the implant may place more stress through the joint articulation, leading to increased debris
Answer 5: The use of a metal tibial baseplate and modular polyethylene insert leads to backside wear and debris, causing an increased amount of osteolysis
