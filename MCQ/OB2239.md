## Question

A 4 year-old male patient with cerebral palsy, GMFCS IV, presents for routine hip surveillance. On examination, the patient has bilateral hip abduction of 20 degrees. He has x-rays depicted in figure A. The patient undergoes bilateral hip adductor tenotomies to address hip subluxation on the right and a hip at risk on the left. For patients treated for this condition with adductor tenotomies, which of the following is associated with an increased risk of treatment failure?

![[q2239-a-hip-migration-index.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Acetabular index <27 degrees
2. Preoperative hip migration percentage >40%
3. Lack of postoperative bracing
4. Performing an adductor tenotomy without an obturator neurectomy
5. Increasing age

## Correct option

**2. Preoperative hip migration percentage >40%**

## Answer explanation

Patients with cerebral palsy undergoing adductor tenotomy alone for hip subluxation and adduction contractures have a higher treatment failure if there is a preoperative hip migration >40%.

Cerebral palsy is the result of a central nervous system insult at birth that leads to pathologically increased muscle tone affecting motor function. This can lead to debilitating joint contractures. In the spastic hip, this can lead to increased subluxation and dysplasia that can reduce mobility and prevent the ability to sit in a chair. Adduction contractures may require an adductor tenotomy and flexion contractures may require iliopsoas lengthening. Studies have shown hip migration index >40% may predispose patients to an increased treatment failure when an adductor tenotomy is performed alone.

*Miller et al. performed a retrospective study of patients with cerebral palsy with hip abduction <30 degrees or hip migration >25% on AP hip radiographs with open adductor release and iliopsoas lengthenings followed by immediate postoperative mobilization.* Following treatment, 54% of patients had good (migration percentage <25%), 34% fair (migration percentage 25-39%), and 12% poor outcomes (migration percentage >40%). **The authors concluded** that with early detection and treatment these patients can have an 80% good to fair outcome rate.

*Cornell et al. performed a retrospective study of 56 hips in 37 children with cerebral palsy undergoing an adductor tenotomy alone or in combination with an obturator neurectomy.* **The authors reported** an 83% reduction rate in patients with a preoperative migration index <40%, a 77% subluxation or dislocation rate in patients with a preoperative migration index >40%, and unsuccessful treatment in 13/15 patients with acetabular index >27 degrees. The inclusion of obturator neurectomy was as effective of adductor tenotomy alone and age did not have an impact on treatment failure. **The authors concluded** that preoperative migration percentage was the only significant factor associated with treatment failure in this group.

Figure A is the AP pelvis radiograph with bilateral hip subluxation >40%. Illustration A depicts how to measure acetabular index and hip migration.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: An acetabular index >27 degrees has been associated with increased treatment failure with adductor tenotomy. Patients with an acetabular index <27 degrees do not have the same amount of treatment failure.
Answer 3: The use of postoperative bracing has not been associated with improved clinical or radiographic outcomes.
Answer 4: Performing an obturator neurectomy in addition to an adductor tenotomy does not improve treatment effectiveness for CP hip adduction contractures and hip subluxation.
Answer 5: Age at the time of treatment does not affect the success of treatment in these patients.
