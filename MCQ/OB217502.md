## Question

A 32-year-old patient is hit by a car while riding his bicycle and experiences immediate pain and swelling to the left lower extremity. His injury is depicted in Figures A and B. When treating this injury surgically, an intramedullary nail is placed and blocking screws are used to prevent the most commonly associated deformity. Placement of a blocking screw placed in the posterior proximal fragment would prevent which deformity?

## Options

1. Malrotation
2. Procurvatum
3. Recurvatum
4. Varus
5. Valgus

## Correct option

**2. Procurvatum**

## Answer explanation

Blocking screws in the posterior portion of the proximal third tibia metaphysis in proximal 1/3 tibial shaft fractures prevent procurvatum deformity of the tibia.

Proximal third tibial shaft fractures are often the result of high-energy mechanisms. Surgical treatment options include open reduction and internal fixation with a plate construct or intramedullary nailing. However, with traditional nailing techniques, there have been high incidences of valgus and procurvatum deformities due to the pull of the patellar tendon and anterior compartment musculature. Incorporation of blocking screws in the metaphyseal fragment function to direct the nail into a direct trajectory down the medullary canal and prevents malalignment.

*Ricci et al. performed a prospective study of 12 patients with proximal third tibial shaft fractures treated with an intramedullary nail and blocking screws.* **They reported** that all patients had less than 5 degrees of angular deformity postoperatively and 91% of patients maintained alignment at final follow-up. **They concluded** blocking screws can maintain fracture alignment of proximal third tibial shaft fractures treated with an intramedullary nail.

*Freeman et al. biomechanically compared nail stability in proximal third tibial shaft fractures with a varying number of locking screws and interlocking screws.* **They found** the nail construct with four screws in the proximal fragment was significantly more stable than those with only three screws. **They concluded** proximal segment stability can be improved with an increasing number of screws and with a locking configuration.

Figures A and B are the AP and lateral radiographs of the tibia and fibula with a proximal third tibial shaft fracture.

Incorrect answers
Answers 1, 2, 3, and 4: Proximal third tibial shaft fractures are associated with procurvatum and valgus deformities due to the pull of the patellar tendon on the proximal fragment and lateral shortening vector of the anterior compartment muscles. The placement of blocking screws in the proximal metaphysis function to counteract these forces.
