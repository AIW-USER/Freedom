## Question

A 36-year-old man presents with acute onset of pain in his left shoulder and arm. The pain began after a pick-up football game 10 days ago. He localizes the pain to the lateral and posterior aspect of his arm and describes it as an aching sensation. He also reports numbness over the dorsal forearm and long finger. On physical exam, his pain is alleviated when abducting and elevating his arm. He also has weakness with long finger extension. Radiographs are shown in Figures A-D. ## Options

1. C6 radiculopathy, left paracentral disc at the C5/C6 level
2. C6 radiculopathy, left paracentral disc at the C6/C7 level
3. C7 radiculopathy, left paracentral disc at the C6/C7 level
4. C7 radiculopathy, left paracentral disc at the C7/T1 level
5. C8 radiculopathy, left paracentral disc at the C7/T1 level

## Correct option

**3. C7 radiculopathy, left paracentral disc at the C6/C7 level**

## Answer explanation

The patient's presenting symptoms are consistent with a C7 radiculopathy. Given the patient's young age and normal oblique radiographs, an MRI would most likely show a left-sided paracentral soft disc herniation at the C6/C7 level.

Cervical radiculopathy is a common cause of acute arm pain. It must be differentiated from shoulder pathology and peripheral neuropathies. The diagnosis can usually be confirmed by physical exam alone. A C7 radiculopathy is characterized by diffuse pain in the posterior arm and dorsal forearm. Tingling in the ring and index fingers is another important feature and also helps to differentiate it from a C6 radiculopathy, which primarily involves the thumb. Weakness of the triceps is the most easily identified finding on physical exam and is usually the most indicative weakness of a C7 radiculopathy; however, weakness of long finger extension may be present as well. Provocative tests such as the shoulder abduction test and the Spurling test can help differentiate it from shoulder pathology and peripheral neuropathy.

*Woods et al. review the treatment of acute cervical radiculopathy.* **They report** that all patients should be treated conservatively for at least 6 weeks in the absence of myelopathy or significant muscle weakness. Conservative treatment consists of immobilization, anti-inflammatory medications, physical therapy, cervical traction, and epidural steroid injections.

*Caridi et al. review the anatomy and pathoanatomy of cervical disc herniations.* **They note** that the neural foramen is made up of the facet joint posteriorly and the intervertebral disc anteriorly. The superior and inferior borders are comprised of the pedicles of the vertebral bodies above and below, respectively. The foramina are largest in the upper cervical spine and gradually narrow distally, with the C7/T1 foramina being the most narrow. The most common causes of nerve root compression are spondylosis of the facet joint and herniation of the intervertebral disc. Hypermobility of the facet joint may lead to ligamentous hypertrophy as well as bony hypertrophy.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: While a C6 radiculopathy would be caused by paracentral disc at the C5/C6 level, it would present with numbness in the thumb and weakness to elbow flexion in neutral pronation/supination (thumb pointing up).
Answer 2: C6 radiculopathy would not be caused by a paracentral disc at the C6/C7 level. It would be caused by a disc at C5/C6.
Answer 4: C7 radiculopathy would not be caused by a paracentral disc at the C7/T1 level. It would be caused by a disc at C6/C7.
Answer 5: While a C8 radiculopathy would be caused by paracentral disc at the C7/T1 level, it would be characterized by numbness in the small finger and weakness with finger flexion, hand grip, and thumb extension.
