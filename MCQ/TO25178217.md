Chapter: Trauma MCQs — Chronic Regional Pain Syndrome (CRPS)

## Question

A 45-year-old patient presents 4 months after a total knee arthroplasty with persistent burning pain, significant edema, and increased hair growth. MRI shows diffuse bone marrow edema. According to recent literature, which of the following is the most appropriate first-line pharmacological treatment for this specific clinical phenotype?

## Options

1. Sympathetic Nerve Block
2. Long-term Opioid Therapy
3. Intravenous Bisphosphonates (e.g., Neridronate)
4. High-dose oral Vitamin C

## Correct option

**3. Intravenous Bisphosphonates (e.g., Neridronate)**

## Answer explanation

The patient in the scenario is presenting with the "Warm Phenotype" of CRPS. This is the acute inflammatory phase (usually <6 months post-injury/surgery).

1. Pathophysiology of the Warm Phenotype

In early CRPS, there is a massive release of pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-1beta, IL-6, TNF-alpha) and neuropeptides (Substance P). This leads to:

 - Vasodilation: Causing the redness and warmth.

 - Bone Turnover: Increased osteoclast activity leads to regional migratory osteoporosis, which is visible as Bone Marrow Edema (BME) on MRI.

2. Why Bisphosphonates?

Historically used for osteoporosis, bisphosphonates (specifically Neridronate, Alendronate, and Pamidronate) are now recognized for their potent anti-inflammatory and osteoclast-inhibiting effects in CRPS.

 - Mechanism: They reduce the acidic environment in the bone (acidosis triggers nociceptors) and inhibit the release of inflammatory mediators from osteoblasts and macrophages.

 - Evidence: Multiple RCTs (notably the Neridronate trials) have shown significant pain reduction and functional improvement, making them first-line for patients with MRI-proven bone marrow edema.

Answer: 3
