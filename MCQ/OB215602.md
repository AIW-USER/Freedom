## Question

A 35-year-old male presents with worsening back pain over the previous year. On examination, the patient ambulates with a slight kyphotic alignment with limited spinal range of motion. There are no deficits on neurologic examination. His current imaging is demonstrated in Figure A. Which of the following is true regarding this condition?

![[q215602-a-as-xr.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. More commonly seen in females
2. Associated with diabetes mellitus
3. Sacroiliac joint is rarely involved
4. There is often involvement of the entire spine
5. Oral steroids are more effective than IV steroids in preventing disease progression

## Correct option

**4. There is often involvement of the entire spine**

## Answer explanation

The described clinical presentation and x-ray image are most consistent with ankylosing spondylitis (AS), which often involves the entire spine. While diffuse idiopathic skeletal hyperostosis (DISH) preferentially involves the thoracic spine, it can involve the cervical and lumbar spine as well. The patients age, worsening back pain on presentation, and x-rays demonstrating sacroiliac and intervertebral disc involvement is what distinguishes AS from DISH in this clinical scenario.

Ankylosing spondylitis is a seronegative autoimmune disorder that results in gradual arthrodesis of the spinal segments through bridging syndesmophytes. It commonly presents in males in their 3rd decade of life and is often associated with HLA-B27. Due to the increased rigidity of the spinal column and relative osteoporosis, there is an increased risk of unstable spinal fractures with ground-level falls. If these injuries are missed there is a high risk of neurologic deterioration from epidural hematomas and fracture displacement.

*Werner et al. reviewed the diagnosis and management of spinal fractures in ankylosing spondylitis patients.* **The authors stated** anterior fixation alone for the treatment of AS-related fractures is associated with high failure rates. **The authors concluded** complications related to these injuries can be minimized if a high index of suspicion is exercised when evaluating these patients.

*Arun et al. performed a retrospective study of 31 patients with ankylosing spondylitis with sagittal plane imbalance treated with three different lumbar osteotomies.* **They reported** a mean kyphosis correction of 38° with pedicle subtraction osteotomies, 28° with polysegmental posterior opening wedge osteotomies, and 30° with Smith-Peterson osteotomies. **They concluded** pedicle subtraction osteotomies and Smith-Peterson osteotomies achieved greater radiographic correction at the cost of increased intraoperative blood loss and larger constructs.

Figure A is an AP radiograph of the lumbar spine with aforementioned changes consistent with ankylosing spondylitis.

Incorrect answers
Answer 1: Ankylosing spondylitis has a classical clinical manifestation in males with rigid kyphotic ankylosis of the spine. This manifestation is not seen commonly in female patients.
Answer 2: Diffuse idiopathic skeletal hyperostosis (DISH) is associated with diabetes mellitus and HLA-B8.
Answer 3: DISH rarely involves the SI joint. SI involvement is a hallmark characteristic of ankylosing spondylitis.
Answer 5: Oral and IV steroids are not preferred for symptomatic treatment of ankylosing spondylitis and have not been associated with prevention of disease progression.
