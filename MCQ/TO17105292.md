## Question

Which nerve causes painful neuroma after TKR?

## Options

1. infrapatellar branch of saphenous nerve
2. superomedial genicular nerve
3. inferomedial genicular nerve
4. inferolateral genicular nerve

## Correct option

**1. infrapatellar branch of saphenous nerve**

## Answer explanation

After exiting the subsartorial canal, the saphenous nerve branches into its two terminal branches, the infrapatellar branch, which innervates the infrapatellar fat pad as well as the rest of the anteromedial knee, and the descending branch which provides the distal sensory innervation to the skin and fascia on the anteromedial aspect of the leg and foot to the first metatarsal, as well as articular branches to the knee and ankle.

![[TO17105292-1.png]]

Neuroma of the infrapatellar branch of saphenous nerve (ISBN) may occur after midline parapatellar approach for total knee replacement. Injury to IBSN has more commonly been reported after anterior cruciate ligament reconstruction, arthroscopy, tibial nailing, but it may also complicate postoperative period of TKA.

Patient feels sharp pain in the medial part of the right knee, and it may decrease range of motion. Tenderness is found at the medial knee compartments but not the lateral compartment. Tinel’s sign may be positive.

Treatment : if conservative treatment fails then surgical excision of neuroma and nerve graft is the procedure of choice.

Answer : a
