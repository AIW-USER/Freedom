## Question

## Options

1. Initiate IV antibiotics
2. T8 and T9 anterior corpectomy with strut grafting with T6 -L2 posterior instrumentation
3. T8-9 posterior decompression with T6-L1 instrumented fusion
4. T8-9 laminectomy with thoracic interbody fusion
5. Obtain blood cultures

## Correct option

**5. Obtain blood cultures**

## Answer explanation

The patient is presenting with vertebral discitis and osteomyelitis without signs of sepsis or neurologic deficits. The first step in management would be to identify the offending organism by blood culture.

Vertebral osteomyelitis, also known as spondylodiscitis, occurs most commonly in the lumbar and thoracic spine. The goal of treatment are to diagnose the infection and identify the microorganism, (2) extirpate the infection, (3) protect neurologic structures and prevent deterioration of deficit or new deficits (4) maintain stability and (5) optimize nutritional state and host immunity. Diagnosis and pathogen identification are paramount among these and allow for the administration of appropriate antibiotic therapy, which is recommended in all cases whether or not surgery is undertaken.

*Carragee et al. retrospectively reviewed 103 cases of pyogenic vertebral osteomyelitis (PVO) to characterize the utility of MRI.* MRI obtained within 2 weeks of the onset of symptoms correctly diagnosed PVO in 55% of cases and suggested PVO in 36% of cases. **They concluded** that MRI helps confirm the diagnosis of PVO and should be obtained earlier rather than later as most other series report a 2- to a 6-month delay in diagnosis.

*Dunbar et al. reviewed confirmed spinal infections to identify cases in which early MRI did not show typical findings consistent with the diagnosis.* **They found** four cases in which imaging characteristics typical of spinal infection were not evident at initial MRI. These cases had subtle edema, disc degeneration and mild endplate changes. Subsequent MR examination clearly identified discitis and osteomyelitis. **The authors concluded** that repeat MRI may be necessary to fully identify PVO.

Darouiche reviews the clinical symptoms, MRI findings, treatment recommendations for epidural abscesses. Darouiche emphasizes that most pitfalls are due to delay in diagnosis or inadequate treatment. It is important to rule out abscess and not ascribe all symptoms to osteomyelitis as the two may coexist.

Figures A, B, and C are the sagittal T1, T2, and T1 fat-suppressed with gadolinium contrast MRI images of the thoracic spine with T8-9 discitis and osteomyelitis. Illustration A is a diagram with treatment and diagnostic recommendations for vertebral osteomyelitis.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Initiating IV antibiotic treatment should be delayed until a causative organism is identified. This can be done either by blood cultures or CT-guided aspiration of the disc. Initially, the organism should be identified by blood cultures, and if negative, a CT-guided aspiration should be used.
Answers 2-4: Surgical management of vertebral discitis and osteomyelitis is recommended when there are neurologic deficits, spinal instability, progressive deformity, and refractory cases. In this case, initial management with IV antibiotics following organism identification is appropriate initial management.
