## Question

A 40-year-old man presents with an 6-year history of a forearm mass and episodic, sharp forearm pain. MRI scans are seen in Figures A and B. What is the most likely diagnosis?

![[q4904-a-fig-a-t1-axial.jpg]]
*Figure A*

![[q4904-b-fig-b-coronal-stir.jpg]]
*Figure B*

## Options

1. Soft tissue sarcoma
2. Soft tissue metastasis
3. Hematoma
4. Abscess
5. Nerve sheath tumor

## Correct option

**5. Nerve sheath tumor**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a benign nerve sheath tumor of the median nerve (either Schwannoma or neurofibroma). Schwannomas and neurofibromas are the most common form of nerve sheath tumors.

Schwannomas arise from the Schwann cells of a peripheral nerve, and are found ECCENTRIC to the nerve fibers and is encapsulated by perineurium. Solitary neurofibromas are fusiform lesions with a CENTRALLY entering and exiting nerve. They lack a capsule and cannot be separated from normal nerve fibers.

*Bhargava et al. reviewed the target sign in T2-weighted MR images of 23 neurofibromas or malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumors.* The target sign was seen in all 12 neurofibromas and 1 of the 11 malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumors.

*Uetani et al. reviewed intraneural ganglion cysts in 3 patients.* This lesion was most common in the common peroneal nerve, presents with signs and symptoms of compression of the involved nerve and has low signal on T1-weighted images, and high signal on T2-weighted images. They tend to be long (2-15cm).

Figure A is a T1-weighted axial MRI showing a well-defined heterogeneous soft tissue mass (arrow, Illustration A) in the location of the median nerve that is isointense to slightly hyperintense to adjacent muscle. Figure B is a coronal STIR image showing a fusiform, diffusely hyperintense soft tissue mass (arrow, Illustration B) surrounded by a thin low signal intensity margin (arrowheads). Illustration C is a diagram depicting the difference between Schwannoma and neurofibroma. Illustration D ("nerve continuity sign") is a coronal STIR image showing a fusiform tumor (arrows) in continuity with the neurovascular bundle (arrowheads). Illustration E ("split fat sign") is a sagittal T1-weighted image showing a fusiform tumor surrounded by a thin margin of fat (arrowheads) because the mass within the neurovascular bundle enlarges and displaces the adjacent intramuscular fat. Illustration F ("target sign") is a coronal STIR image showing a lesion of high signal intensity peripherally and low signal centrally.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1, 2, 3, 4: The lesion does not demonstrate MRI features of these conditions.
