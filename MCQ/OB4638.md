## Question

## Options

1. Thoracolumbar kyphosis
2. Genu valgum
3. Dural ectasia
4. Carpal tunnel syndrome
5. Atlantoaxial instability

## Correct option

**3. Dural ectasia**

## Answer explanation

This patient has mucopolysaccharidosis (MPS) which is associated with kyphosis, genu valgum, carpal tunnel syndrome, and atlantoaxial instability. MPS is not associated with dural ectasia, which can be found in Marfan syndrome, Ehlers-Danlos syndrome, neurofibromatosis type I and ankylosing spondylitis.

Patients with MPS have deficient enzymes responsible for degradation of intracellular glycosaminoglycans (GAGs). GAGs accumulate within lysosomes of chondrocytes and the extracellular matrix (ECM) of cartilage and synovium. Intravenous enzyme replacement therapy and hematopoetic stem cell transplantation (HSCT) improve cardiac, respiratory and somatic function, but they do not penetrate osteocartilaginous tissue and thus have no impact on skeletal abnormalities. These patients usually require multiple orthopaedic procedures.

*White et al. review MPS disorders in orthopaedic surgery.* **They report** orthopaedic manifestations include but are not limited to hip dysplasia, shortened ulna with Madelung deformity of the distal radius, shortened metacarpals with proximal tapering, broad clavicles, oar-shaped ribs, carpal tunnel syndrome (from thickening of the flexor retinaculum and tenosynovium), genu valgum, trigger finger, cervical spinal stenosis, and atlantoaxial instability.

*Link et al. reviewed orthopaedic manifestations in Hunter syndrome (Type II MPS).* *They advise that in this patient population, short neck, atlantoaxial instability, lung impairment and cardiovascular problems may complicate general anesthesia in patients with MPS.*

Figure A comprises lateral flexion-extension radiographs showing platyspondyly but no instability or odontoid hypoplasia. Figure B is a pelvic radiograph illustrating hip dysplasia in MPS including coxa valga with disruption in Shenton's line, irregular ossification and progressive absorption of femoral head (leading to head distortion/fragmentation) and poor ossification of the acetabulum. Figure C is a wrist radiograph showing Madelung's deformity. Illustration A shows atlantoaxial instability from odontoid hypoplasia (arrow).

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Thoracolumbar kyphosis with beaking of L1-L2 is may be present in MPS.
Answer 2: Genu valgum arises because of lateral dysplasia of the proximal tibial metaphysis.
Answer 4: Carpal tunnel syndrome is common in MPS (especially MPS I Scheie). The deposition of GAGs leads to thickening of the flexor retinaculum and tenosynovium. They present with pain, tingling, numbness, and difficulties with fine motor tasks.
Answer 5: Atlantoaxial instability (AAI) is common in MPS (especially MPS IV). AAI exacerbates spinal stenosis at the occipitocervical junction. Unsteady gait, upper and lower extremity weakness, dysesthesias, and urinary retention are indications for surgery.
