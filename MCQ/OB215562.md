## Question

A 12-year-old female presents to your office for evaluation of suspected scoliosis following a school screening exam. Radiographs demonstrate a Risser stage of 0. Which of the following curves is best treated with a thoracolumbar sacral orthosis (TLSO)?

## Options

1. 30-degree scoliosis with an apex at T6
2. 15-degree scoliosis with an apex at T6
3. 30-degree scoliosis with an apex at T12
4. 15-degree scoliosis with an apex at T12
5. 53-degree scoliosis with an apex at T6

## Correct option

**3. 30-degree scoliosis with an apex at T12**

## Answer explanation

TLSO is the mainstay of treatment for patients with adolescent idiopathic scoliosis (AIS) who are at a risk of progression (ie. Risser stage 0, curve magnitude 25-45 degrees) with an apex at or below T7.

AIS is the most common type of scoliosis and presents most commonly as a right sided thoracic curve (named for the apex and convexity of curve) in patients between the ages of 10 and 18 years. Treatment is largely based on the risk of curve progression. Skeletally immature patients with curves 25-45 degrees and an apex at or below T7 are best treated with a TLSO. Treatment with the brace is dose dependent with an observed 50% reduction in the need for surgery with compliant brace wear of at least 13 hours a day. Patients with curves measuring >45-degrees are candidates for posterior spinal fusion, while those with curves measuring <25-degrees can be observed.

*Weinstein et al. review the effects of bracing in adolescents with AIS.* **They report** a treatment success of 72% after bracing compared to 48% after observation for patients with AIS curves between 20 and 40 degrees. The effects of brace wear were found to be dose dependent with increased benefits provided with longer hours of wear. **They conclude** that bracing significantly decreased the progression of high-risk curves to the threshold of surgery in patients with AIS.

*Zhang et al performed a meta-analysis evaluating bracing as a treatment option for AIS.* **They report** that significant differences exist between bracing and observation groups as it pertains to successful outcomes, quality of life, and adverse events. **They conclude** that bracing is a safe and efficient treatment for AIS.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Patients with a scoliotic curve with an apex at or proximal to T6 should be treated in a Milwaukee brace (ie. cervicothoracolumbosacral orthosis).
Answer 2: Curves <25-degrees can be treated with observation.
Answer 4: Curves <25-degrees can be treated with observation.
Answer 5: Patients with curves >45-degrees are candidates for surgical stabilization via posterior spinal fusion.
