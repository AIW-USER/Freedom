## Question

A 14-year-old male presents to the trauma bay after a fall off a horse. His AP chest reveals multiple rib fractures and his AP pelvis is shown in Figure A. A CT of the abdomen and pelvis is obtained and a selected cut is shown in Figures B. Which of the following is the most appropriate management of his injuries?

## Options

1. Non-operative and touch down weight-bearing on the left lower extremity
2. Anterior symphyseal plate fixation only
3. Posterior open sacroiliac screw fixation only
4. External fixation
5. Anterior symphyseal plate fixation with posterior percutaneous sacroiliac screw fixation

## Correct option

**5. Anterior symphyseal plate fixation with posterior percutaneous sacroiliac screw fixation**

## Answer explanation

The patient has sustained a significantly displaced pubic symphysis diastasis with a left posterior sacroiliac joint widening. The most appropriate management would be anterior pelvis open reduction and internal fixation (ORIF) with percutaneous posterior sacroiliac screw fixation.

Pediatric pelvic ring fractures are the result of high-energy trauma and often are accompanied by chest injuries (~63%), long bone fractures (~50%) and spine injuries (~25%). The majority of these injuries can be managed non-operatively. Indications for operative management are displaced posterior sacroiliac joints, iliac fractures > 2 cm displacement, and anterior pelvis disruption leading to vertical instability or rotatory instability.

*Amorosa et al. review pediatric pelvic and acetabular fractures.* **They note** that these injuries are almost always the result of a high-energy injury mechanism. **They note** that previously, it was recommended that almost all of these injuries be treated nonoperatively. However, **they state** that given the lack of remodeling in highly displaced fractures, in the presence of instability or significant displacement, operative fixation by a pelvic and acetabular fracture specialist should be considered to allow the best possible outcome.

*Tile reviewed the anatomy and management of acute pelvic ring fractures.* **He noted** that while anterior pelvis structures contribute approximately 40% to the stiffness of the pelvis, clinical and biomechanical studies have shown that the posterior sacroiliac complex is more important to pelvic-ring stability. **He notes** that unstable posterior ring fractures are almost always caused by high-energy severe trauma associated with motor vehicle accidents, falls from a height, or crushing injuries. **He also notes** that because of the complexity of injuries that most often result in acute pelvic fractures, they should be considered in the context of polytrauma management, rather than in isolation.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1, 2, and 3: The patient has sustained a significantly displaced pubic symphysis diastasis with left posterior sacroiliac joint widening. He should be managed surgically with anterior and posterior surgical fixation.
Answer 4: External fixation is generally reserved for the treatment of patients with hemodynamic instability in the setting of severely displaced anterior pelvic ring injuries.
