## Question

A 42-year-old diabetic male smoker presents with neck pain and several contusions on his left side after crashing his motorcycle 2 hours prior. He was helmeted at the time of the accident. He currently denies any pain or weakness. On examination of his spine, there is pain with limited motion of his neck. The motor examination does not reveal any upper or lower extremity weakness and there are no sensory deficits. The patient is placed in a rigid C-collar and undergoes imaging. Figure A is the current CT scan. The patient is eventually cleared for surgery and undergoes the treatment depicted in Figure B two days later. What is associated with this treatment?

## Options

1. Greater loss of motion compared to posterior fixation
2. Increased rate of infection compared to posterior fixation
3. Acceptable reduction regardless of fracture morphology
4. High likelihood of successful fracture union with few treatment complications
5. Increased risk of nonunion due to 48-hour delay to fixation

## Correct option

**4. High likelihood of successful fracture union with few treatment complications**

## Answer explanation

The patient is a diabetic and smoker presenting with a Grauer type IIB odontoid fracture, which places him at risk for nonunion with nonoperative treatment. Of the answer choices given, there is a high likelihood that there will be a union of the fracture with little treatment complications is the preferred response.

Odontoid fractures are the result of blunt trauma to the head with a hyperflexion or hyperextension injury mechanism. These fractures have been classified by Anderson and D'Alonzo and Grauer, with type II fractures occurring in the vascular watershed region at the waist of the odontoid. As a result, these are prone to non-union, especially in the setting of increased fracture displacement, tobacco use, and diabetes. Treatment involves halo vest immobilization for young patients with no risk factors of nonunion. Surgical stabilization is preferred with patients that are at higher risk for nonunion, such as smokers and diabetics, with fracture displacement >6 mm, and treatment delay greater than 4 days. Anterior odontoid screw fixation can be performed in patients with acceptable fracture morphology, specifically, type IIB of the Grauer classification. Otherwise, surgical treatment with posterior spinal fusion for type IIA and IIC for those requiring stabilization.

*Shilpakar et al. reviewed operative techniques for the treatment of type II odontoid fractures.* **They recommended** the use of anterior odontoid screw fixation in acute type II fractures as well as rostral type III fractures. In cases of chronic type II fractures or with transverse ligament disruption, **they recommended** posterior transarticular facet screw fixation supplemented with bone graft and interspinous C1-2 wiring.

*Subach et al. retrospectively studied patients treated with anterior odontoid screw fixation that presented with acute type II and rostral type III odontoid fractures.* **They found** at 30-months follow-up there was a 96% fusion rate with no complications related to the surgical technique. Of the included patients, **they reported** one patient requiring external immobilization due to poor screw placement and one requiring posterior stabilization due to insufficient fixation of the anterior screw. **The authors concluded** that single screw anterior odontoid screw fixation is an effective surgical treatment option with high fusion rates and low complication rates in type II and rostral type III odontoid fractures.

*Fountas et al. retrospectively studied the long-term fusion rates of patients undergoing anterior odontoid screw fixation for patients with type II and shallow type III odontoid fractures.* **They reported** a fusion rate of 87.1% with the remaining 12.9% of patients developing a pseudoarthrosis. Of the 12.9% that developed pseudoarthrosis 9.6% were stable and 3.2% with instability. **The authors concluded** that anterior odontoid screw fixation is a safe and effective therapeutic option for type II and shallow type III odontoid fractures.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: One of the benefits of anterior odontoid screw fixation over posterior stabilization is the preservation of the cervical spine range of motion, namely rotation at the atlantoaxial joint which contributes approximately 50% to the overall rotatory motion of the cervical spine.
Answer 2: Posterior spinal fusion procedures have been associated with greater infection rates compared to anterior procedures.
Answer 3: Anterior odontoid screw fixation is preferred in patients with oblique fracture patterns that are perpendicular to the screw trajectory (posterior obliquity) to facilitate a lag effect across the fracture site. Transverse or anterior oblique patterns tend to shear with anterior screw fixation. Patients with a large body habitus and short necks furthermore make surgical exposure difficult and appropriate screw trajectory difficult and in some instances impossible.
Answer 5: While the risk of nonunion in type II odontoid fractures is increased by diabetes and smoking, treatment delay <48hrs is not. Generally, treatment delay >4-7 days has been identified as a risk factor for nonunion.
