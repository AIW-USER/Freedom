## Question

## Options

1. Dorsiflexion osteotomy of the medial cuneiform
2. Isolated talonavicular fusion
3. No further maneuvers are indicated
4. Derotation of the forefoot through the transverse tarsal joints
5. Derotation of the forefoot through the calcaneocuboid alone

## Correct option

**4. Derotation of the forefoot through the transverse tarsal joints**

## Answer explanation

This patient has stage 3 posterior tibial tendon dysfunction. Derotation through the transverse tarsal joints is necessary to create a plantigrade foot and avoid lateral border foot pain.

In stage 3 posterior tibial tendon dysfunction, there is degeneration of the subtalar, the talonavicular and the calcaneocuboid joints. The latter two joints compose the transverse tarsal joint. As the deformity progresses, abduction occurs through the transverse tarsal joints. Compensatory forefoot varus develops in an attempt to reestablish the mechanical tripod of the foot. The operative treatment of choice for stage 3 posterior tibial tendon dysfunction is a triple arthrodesis. After the subtalar joint is realigned, the forefoot varus will be unmasked if present. Derotation through the transverse tarsal joints as part of the arthrodesis procedure can address the forefoot varus and prevent lateral border foot pain.

Johnson and Yu describe arthrodesis techniques in the management of adult-acquired flat foot. Although a triple arthrodesis is indicated in stage 3 posterior tibial tendon dysfunction, procedures to correct residual forefoot varus include a Cotton osteotomy, a first tarsometatarsal joint fusion or a naviculo-medial cuneiform fusion.

Figure A is an AP radiograph of the foot demonstrating significant abduction through the talonavicular joint and talar head uncoverage. Figure B is a lateral radiograph of the foot with degenerative changes seen in the subtalar and talonavicular joints. This is collapse of the medial longitudinal arch consistent with a flat foot deformity. Degeneration is also seen at the level of the midfoot. Figure C is a clinical photo demonstrating the presence of forefoot varus.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1:A dorsiflexion osteotomy is not indicated in this clinical situation
Answer 2: An isolated talonavicular fusion is not indicated when there are degenerative changes in the subtalar joint as well.
Answer 3: The forefoot varus needs to be addressed
Answer 5: Derotation needs to occur through the transverse tarsal joints, including the talonavicular joint. Forefoot abduction can not be addressed via only the calcaneocuboid joint.
