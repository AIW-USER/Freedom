Chapter: Arthroplasty MCQs — Wear And Osteolysis (Hip)

## Question

which is true statement regarding stress shielding around femoral stem

## Options

1. Cemented stems are associated with less stress shielding than uncemented stems.
2. Fully coated stems are associated with a decreased cortical bone stress shielding compared with proximally coated porous stems.
3. In extensively coated stems, osteolytic lesions are more likely to occur distally
4. In patch‐coated stems, osteolysis occurs more proximally.

## Correct option

**1. Cemented stems are associated with less stress shielding than uncemented stems.**

## Answer explanation

Pathogenesis of stress shielding and osteolysis after THR:

Stress shielding: Stress shielding or stress protection occurs when the higher stiffness of the implant results in bone loss as a result of decreased physiologic loading of the bone. Stress shielding is the redistribution of load that occurs when the femoral head is replaced by the metal stem. As much of the load is carried in the femoral metal stem, stress on the femoral cortex is reduced.

With extensively porous coated stems, up to 25-30% of proximal femurs will demonstrate some stress shielding; and atrophy of the proximal femur is substantially greater with stiff cementless femoral components than with cemented components.

In fact, when a cementless prosthesis has undergone extensive osseointegration, it is common to see proximal stress shielding (rounding off of the proximal-medial calcar femorale), osteopenia, and more distal oblique struts of bone connecting the cortex to the distal porous coating, the so-called spot welds.

IMPORTANT POINTS TO REMEMBER:

- Cemented stems are associated with less stress shielding than uncemented stems.
- Studies have shown that hydroxyapatite fully coated stems are associated with an increased cortical bone stress shielding compared with proximally coated porous stems.
- In extensively coated stems, osteolytic lesions are more likely to occur proximally and do not always result in loosening of the implant. This is in contrast to patch‐coated stems, which allow the joint fluid to reach the diaphysis, thus resulting in osteolysis more distally.

Answer: 1
