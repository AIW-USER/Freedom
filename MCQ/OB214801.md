## Question

Figure A is the anteroposterior elbow radiograph of an 8-month-old boy who had swelling and decreased use of his left elbow. His neurovascular examination is intact and he has no fever or chills. What is the most appropriate definitive treatment?

![[q214801-a-88.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Sling
2. Apply a long-arm splint
3. Closed reduction with pinning
4. Open reduction with pinning
5. Open reduction and internal fixation

## Correct option

**3. Closed reduction with pinning**

## Answer explanation

This child has a displaced transphyseal distal humerus fracture. This is most appropriate definitive treatment is closed reduction and pinning.

Distal humerus physeal separations (also referred to as transphyseal distal humerus fractures) are traumatic fractures usually seen in children under the age of three. The fracture pattern should raise suspicion of child abuse due to a rotational or twisting force required to produce the injury. These injuries are often missed but are characterized radiographically by posteromedial displacement of the radial and ulnar shafts relative to the distal humerus. Standard treatment is closed reduction and pinning in anatomic alignment.

*Shrader performed a review of pediatric elbow fractures.* **The author noted** that the unique radiographic anatomy of the pediatric elbow, along with the potential for neurovascular compromise, often provokes anxiety in orthopedic surgeons. **The authors noted** that the most common fractures of the distal humerus in children are supracondylar humerus fractures, lateral condyle fractures, medial epicondyle fractures, and transphyseal distal humerus fractures. **The author concluded** that with prompt diagnosis, and early management of these injuries, good outcomes can be expected.

*Skaggs reviewed pediatric elbow anatomy and associated injuries.* *The author stresses the importance of understanding the ossification and fusion of the secondary growth centers about the elbow to avoid overlooking these injuries and to optimize treatment.* Early neurologic and vascular complications are not uncommon and must be recognized before fracture reduction. **The author concludes** that anatomic reduction of articular surfaces, restoration of physeal anatomy, and near-anatomic alignment of fracture fragments in the frontal plane are the cornerstones of successful treatment of pediatric elbow fractures.

Figure A is a radiograph showing posteromedial displacement of the radial and ulnar shafts relative to the distal humerus consistent with a transphyseal distal humerus fracture.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1 and 2: There is no role for non-operative management for a displaced transphyseal distal humerus fractures.
Answers 3 and 4: Closed reduction and pinning is generally successful in the management of transphyseal distal humerus fractures
