## Question

A 55-year-old woman has a painful flexible acquired flatfoot deformity and nonsurgical management has failed to provide relief. Examination reveals that the ankle joint dorsiflexion is -5 degrees with the knee in extension and +10 degrees of dorsiflexion with the knee in flexion. In addition to performing a correction of the flatfoot deformity, treatment should include

## Options

1. open lengthening of the Achilles tendon.
2. percutaneous lengthening of the Achilles tendon.
3. open gastrocnemius muscle lengthening.
4. release of the posterior ankle joint capsule.
5. peroneus longus to brevis tendon transfer.

## Correct option

**3. open gastrocnemius muscle lengthening.**

## Answer explanation

Ankle joint range of motion in this patient differs when the knee is in flexion versus extension. When the knee is in flexion, the gastrocnemius muscle is placed at rest and ankle joint dorsiflexion is possible. Open gastrocnemius muscle lengthening is the treatment of choice because this is the muscle that has been shown to be tight on the patient's physical examination. Achilles stretching alone with a flatfoot deformity typically will not result in significant stretching of the muscle. Open lengthening of the Achilles tendon is reserved for significant deformities when both the gastrocnemius and soleus muscles are tight. Percutaneous lengthening of the Achilles tendon is used when both the gastrocnemius and soleus muscles are tight. Posterior capsule release is not indicated in this condition because the ankle joint can be brought into dorsiflexion as evidenced by the range of motion. Peroneus longus to brevis transfer is a procedure used for correction of a cavovarus foot deformity.
