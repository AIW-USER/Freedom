## Question

The procedure depicted in Figure A would be most indicated in a 66-year-old male with which of the following?

![[q216236-a-shoulder.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Capsulorrhaphy arthropathy
2. Coracoacromial ligament deficiency
3. Charcot arthropathy
4. Humeral head osteonecrosis
5. Rheumatoid arthritis

## Correct option

**4. Humeral head osteonecrosis**

## Answer explanation

Figure A depicts the use of a shoulder hemiarthroplasty. Of the choices listed above, the most appropriate indication for its use would be a patient with osteonecrosis without glenoid involvement.

The indications for a shoulder hemiarthroplasty include primary arthritis, rotator cuff arthropathy, osteonecrosis without glenoid involvement, and proximal humerus fractures. Contraindications to this procedure include infection, a neuropathic joint (ie. Charcot arthropathy secondary to a syrinx), an unmotivated patient, rheumatoid arthritis, capsulorrhaphy arthropathy, and coracoacromial ligament deficiency as this provides a barrier to humeral head proximal migration in the case of a rotator cuff tear and superior escape will occur if the coracoacromial ligament and rotator cuff are deficient.

*Iannotti et al. evaluated the influence of an operatively confirmed full-thickness tear of the rotator cuff, the severity of preoperative erosion of glenoid bone, and the severity of preoperative loss of the passive range of motion on the outcome of total shoulder arthroplasty and hemiarthroplasty.* **They reported** that severe or moderate eccentric glenoid erosion that was treated with total shoulder arthroplasty resulted in significantly better passive total elevation and active external rotation as well as a trend toward significantly better active forward flexion than did hemiarthroplasty. **They concluded** that a glenoid component should be used in shoulders with glenoid erosion.

*Hettrich et al. reviewed the functional outcome of shoulder hemiarthroplasty for diagnoses other than acute fracture.* **They found** that preoperative absence of erosion of the glenoid was associated with greater improvement in shoulder function and level of comfort. **They also noted** that shoulders with rheumatoid arthritis, capsulorrhaphy arthropathy, and cuff tear arthropathy had the least functional improvement, whereas those with osteonecrosis and with primary and secondary degenerative joint disease had the greatest improvement. **They concluded** that functional improvement following humeral hemiarthroplasty is related to factors that are identifiable before surgery.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1-3,5: These are all contraindications to use of a shoulder hemiarthroplasty
