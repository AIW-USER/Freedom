## Question

A 14-year-old male with left knee pain presents to your office. He complains of lateral sided knee pain with occasional feelings of the knee "locking” during sports. MRI shows findings suspicious for a Watanabe Type I discoid meniscus with associated radial tearing. What is the most appropriate next step in management?

## Options

1. Observation
2. Partial meniscectomy +/- repair
3. Meniscal repair only
4. Complete menisectomy
5. Meniscal transplant

## Correct option

**2. Partial meniscectomy +/- repair**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a complete discoid meniscus (Watanabe I) with mechanical symptoms and is indicated for saucerization +/- repair if the associated tear pattern is amenable.

A discoid meniscus is an abnormal meniscal variant caused by hypertrophic meniscal tissue, most commonly affecting the lateral meniscus. On radiographs, squaring of the lateral condyle is seen and on MRI, three or more consecutive sagittal cuts are seen with meniscal continuity. The Watanabe classification defines three types of discoid mensici. Type I are complete, meaning the abnormal meniscus covers the entire tibial plateau, type II are partial with the meniscus covering less than or equal to 80% of the tibial plateau, and type III discoid menisci lack peripheral meniscal attachments, making them hypermobile and unstable. In most cases, if mechanical symptoms are not present, observation is appropriate. However, in the setting of mechanical symptoms and persistent pain, saucerization of the discoid variant and repair of associated tearing is the treatment of choice.

*Klingele et al. discuss lateral discoid meniscus tears and review their classification and management.* **They noted** that associated meniscal tears were present intraoperatively in 69.5% of patients, with 28% having peripheral rim instability, most commonly at the anterior third meniscal attachment. Peripheral rim instability was more common in complete discoid menisci and younger patients.

*Kramer et al. reviewed the diagnosis and treatment of discoid menisci in children.* **They note** that the most common presentation of a symptomatic discoid meniscus is "snapping knee syndrome." Treatment is dictated by symptoms, with those who are symptomatic being managed with saucerization and repair with good results.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: This patient is having persistent pain and mechanical symptoms, and therefore should be treated surgically.
Answer 3: Saucerization (partial meniscectomy) should be performed in addition to meniscal repair to prevent future tearing of the discoid portion of the meniscus.
Answer 4: Complete meniscectomy should be avoided, particularly in young patients with discoid menisci. Partial meniscectomy/saucerization is preferred.
Answer 5: Meniscal transplant is not indicated in this patient as he is not lacking viable meniscal tissue.
