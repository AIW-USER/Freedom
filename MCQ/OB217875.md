## Question

A 53-year-old male has elbow osteoarthritis. On examination, he has limited motion secondary to osteophytes and capsular scarring. Both open and arthroscopic osteocapsular arthroplasty (OCA) are discussed with him. Which of the following is true regarding arthroscopic osteocapsular arthroplasty?

## Options

1. Decreased postoperative pain compared with open OCA
2. Equal occurrence of postoperative heterotopic ossification to open OCA
3. More limited access to the anterior and posterior compartments compared with open OCA
4. Radial nerve palsy is the most common postoperative complication
5. Ulnar nerve subluxation is an absolute contraindication

## Correct option

**1. Decreased postoperative pain compared with open OCA**

## Answer explanation

Arthroscopic treatment results in decreased postoperative pain compared with open OCA.

Elbow arthritis is degenerative joint disease of the elbow that can be broken into three main types: osteoarthritis, post-traumatic arthritis, and inflammatory arthritis. Diagnosis can be made with plain radiographs of the elbow. Treatment can be nonoperative or operative depending on patient activity demands, severity of elbow pain, and degree of elbow dysfunction. Elbow arthroscopy is indicated for loose body removal, osteophyte debridement, synovectomy, capsular releases for stiffness, osteochondritis dissecans of capitellum, lateral epicondylitis, and debridement for septic arthritis.

*Dodson et al. reviewed elbow arthroscopy indications.* **They reported** that the most common indications for elbow arthroscopy include removal of loose bodies, synovectomy, débridement and/or excision of osteophytes, capsular release, and the assessment and treatment of osteochondritis dissecans. **They concluded** that expanded indications include fracture management (eg, radial head fractures) and the treatment of lateral epicondylitis.

*Kroonen et al. reviewed basic arthroscopic elbow anatomy and the most common procedures, including diagnostic arthroscopy, loose body removal, and arthroscopic osteocapsular and ulnohumeral arthroplasty.* **They reported** that in these cases, there is pain at the terminal extents of motion, and imaging shows osteophyte formation at the tips of the coronoid and olecranon processes and thickening of the bone between the coronoid and the olecranon fossae. **They concluded** that complications do occur and commonly include persistent drainage and nerve injury, but the actual incidence of such complications remains low.

*Cohen et al. reviewed the effectiveness of open and minimally invasive techniques in the debridement of osteoarthritis of the elbow, by comparing the Outerbridge-Kashiwagi (O-K) procedure with an arthroscopic modification in which arthroscopic debridement and fenestration of the olecranon fossa was performed.* **They reported** that patients treated by arthroscopic debridement and fenestration of the olecranon fossa achieved better relief of pain. **They concluded** that in the treatment of osteoarthritis of the elbow, arthroscopic debridement and fenestration of the olecranon fossa may be a more suitable procedure when painful symptoms predominate.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Arthroscopic treatment results in less joint ankylosis/heterotopic ossification than open OCA.
Answer 3: Arthroscopic treatment allows easy access to both the anterior and posterior compartments of the elbow and better visualization of the articular cartilage.
Answer 4: Radial nerve palsy is the second most common postoperative nerve palsy (after ulnar nerve palsy). It is at risk from a standard anterolateral portal.
Answer 5: Ulnar nerve subluxation is not an absolute contraindication, but it should be identified prior to surgery, especially with prior submuscular or intramuscular transposition.
