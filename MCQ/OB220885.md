## Answer explanation

The patient has Osgood-Schlatter Disease, which is associated with decreased patellar tendon strain ratio. Plain radiographs of patients with this condition demonstrate irregularity and fragmentation of the tibial tubercle (Answer choice 2).

Osgood-Schlatter disease, or traction apophysitis of the tibial tubercle, is a clinical condition most common in boys involved in jumping activities. Patients typically present with anterior knee pain, tenderness over the tibial tubercle, and pain with resisted knee extension. Patients with this condition have been demonstrated to have decreased patellar tendon strain ratio, indicating a higher rate of force transmission to the bone-tendon junction at the tibial tubercle due to a less compliant patellar tendon. The mainstay of treatment for this diagnosis is conservative management. Ossicle excision may be considered in refractory cases.

*Guldhammer et al. performed a retrospective review of patients with Osgood-Schlatter’s disease to assess long-term prognosis.* **They found** that, at a median follow-up of 3.75 years, 60.5% of patients reported continued knee pain, which reduced their sports participation and decreased quality of life scores. **They concluded** that the dogma that Osgood-Schlatter’s disease is self-limited may not be entirely accurate, as evident in the persistent pain observed in a large subpopulation of patients.

*Enomoto et al. compared the mechanical properties of the muscles and tendons of patients with Osgood-Schlatter disease and those without.* *They performed elastography of 18 legs with Osgood-Schlatter and 42 legs without and found that the strain ratio (defined as the strain rate of the muscle or tendon divided by a reference material) of the patellar tendon was lower in Osgood-Schlatter patients compared to normal controls.* **They concluded** that low strain ratios, indicative of less extension of the tendon under muscular force, lead to a higher rate of force transmission to the bone-tendon junction, which may, in turn, contribute to the presence of Osgood-Schlatter disease.
