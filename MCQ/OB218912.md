## Question

A 56-year-old female presents to your office with complaints of progressively worsening foot pain and arch collapse. She has failed conservative measures including orthotics and therapy. Radiographs demonstrate a negative Meary's angle. You recommend a reconstruction of her deformities using a combination of tendon transfers and osteotomies. What specific deformity would be addressed by the addition of an opening wedge osteotomy of the medial cuneiform?

## Options

1. Forefoot abduction
2. Forefoot pronation
3. Forefoot supination
4. Hindfoot equinus
5. Hindfoot valgus

## Correct option

**3. Forefoot supination**

## Answer explanation

A medial cuneiform opening wedge osteotomy (Cotton) is performed to address forefoot supination in the setting of a flexible flatfoot deformity.

The most common cause of an acquired flatfoot deformity is posterior tibial tendon insufficiency (PTTI). Functional failure of this complex leads to collapse of the medial longitudinal arch and causes a classic pattern of foot deformity. Deformities include pes planus, hindfoot valgus, forefoot supination/varus, and forefoot abduction. Hindfoot valgus deformity is addressed with tendon transfers (flexor digitorum longus) to reconstitute the arch as well as a medializing calcaneal osteotomy. Forefoot abduction can be addressed with a lateral column lengthening. There is almost always a concomitant heel cord contracture and lengthening of the achilles tendon or gastrocnemius is usually necessary. With longstanding hindfoot valgus, a compensatory forefoot supination/varus deformity develops in order for the foot to remain plantigrade. Once the hindfoot is correct intraoperatively, the forefoot supination must be corrected or the residual deformity will be problematic.

*Gross and Jackson reviewed the importance of the medial column in collapsing foot deformities.* *They discuss the complex nature of acquired flatfoot disorder and note that a series of osteotomies in both the hindfoot and forefoot is usually required to provide complete correction.* *They go into more depth with regards to the technique and indications for the Cotton osteotomy.*

*Conti et al. reviewed outcomes of flexible adult acquired flatfoot deformities.* *They discuss specific techniques including FDL transfer, medializing calcaneal osteotomy, lateral column lengthening, Cotton osteotomy, 1st tarsometatarsal fusion and spring ligament reconstruction.* **They note** that most patients have significant improvements after operative intervention and they go into detail about the nature of the different osteotomies that can provide this correction.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Forefoot abduction with talonavicular uncoverage can be addressed with a lateral column lengthening, typically performed with an opening wedge osteotomy of the calcaneus.
Answer 2: Forefoot pronation is a deformity found in a cavovarus foot as the 1st ray attempts to maintain a plantigrade foot.
Answer 4: Hindfoot equinus is addressed by performing a gastrocnemius or achilles release.
Answer 5: Hindfoot valgus is addressed by performing a medializing calcaneal osteotomy (static) and flexor digitorum longus tendon transfer (dynamic).
