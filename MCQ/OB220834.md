## Question

## Answer explanation

Transphyseal distal humerus fractures (Figure A) are considered highly specific for nonaccidental trauma (NAT) in children 3 years of age and younger (Answer 1).

Pediatric NAT is a critical consideration for orthopedic surgeons, as fractures can be one of the most objective signs of abuse. Injuries with the highest specificity include metaphyseal corner fractures (bucket-handle fractures), posterior rib fractures, scapular fractures, and transphyseal distal humerus fractures. Transphyseal distal humerus fractures (or distal humeral physeal separations) carry a markedly higher risk of NAT than supracondylar humerus fractures in children under 4 years old. In non-ambulatory infants, femur fractures and humeral shaft fractures are also suspicious. It should be noted that no fracture is pathognomonic for pediatric abuse. Any and all fractures may be associated with pediatric abuse. Workup begins with a thorough, well-documented history and physical examination, noting any inconsistencies between the mechanism and developmental age. Imaging includes dedicated views of the injury and a skeletal survey radiographic series, with repeat survey in 2 weeks to detect healing injuries. Early multidisciplinary involvement, including pediatrics, radiology, child protective services, and social workers, is essential to ensure accurate diagnosis, appropriate management, and protection of the child. As a reminder, reporting suspected child abuse is mandatory.

*Manan et al. published a review on the diagnosis and multidisciplinary approach to NAT.* **The authors emphasize** that NAT remains a preventable yet serious contributor to pediatric mortality and morbidity. This study highlights the necessity of distinguishing abusive injuries from accidents and medical conditions that can mimic abuse. **The authors advocate** for a multifactorial evaluation and a multidisciplinary management (healthcare providers, social workers, and authorities). **The authors conclude** that diagnostic accuracy is paramount, as any missed diagnosis can have devastating consequences for the child.

*Crowe et al. published a retrospective study reviewing transphyseal distal humerus fractures and supracondylar humeral fractures in children under three years of age.* *Out of 23 cases of transphyseal distal humerus fractures, 6 cases (26%) were attributed to NAT.* By comparison, only 4 of 205 supracondylar humerus fractures (2%) were attributed to NAT. **The authors concluded** that the risk of NAT was 13 times higher for children with transphyseal distal humerus fractures versus those with supracondylar fractures.

*Sink et al. published a review of the role of the orthopedic surgeon in NAT.* **The authors emphasize** that although skin injuries are more commonly seen, fractures are the most frequent radiographic finding in abused children. **The authors emphasize** that no single fracture type or location is diagnostic of abuse, making contextual evaluation essential. **The authors report** that transphyseal distal humerus fractures have a moderate specificity for NAT, while rib fractures and classic metaphyseal fractures (corner fractures) have a high specificity for NAT. The authors mention clavicular fractures and long-bone fractures, which have a lower specificity for NAT. In conclusion, **the authors recommend** that orthopedic surgeons have a meticulous history and physical examination accompanied by appropriate imaging and suspicion to safeguard a child's well-being.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Clavicle fractures are a common injury in pediatric patients. Isolated clavicle fractures are rarely indicative of abuse unless there are other suspicious injuries or qualifying factors.
Answer 3: This transverse olecranon fracture in a skeletally immature patient is much more commonly seen in the setting of osteogenesis imperfecta than in NAT. While olecranon fractures can be associated with NAT, they are less specific for NAT than transphyseal distal humerus fractures.
Answer 4: Supracondylar humerus fractures are the most common pediatric elbow fractures and are almost always accidental. They can be associated with abuse when a history is inconsistent or the injury is in a very young, nonmobile infant. However, transphyseal distal humerus fractures are more specific for NAT.
Answer 5: A nondisplaced spiral fracture of the tibia (termed a toddler's fracture) is a common injury that can result from low-energy twisting injuries in ambulatory toddlers. This fracture is not highly specific for abuse.
