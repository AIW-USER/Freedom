## Question

A 13-year-old male presents with persistent knee pain that worsens with activity. He has not had any specific treatment for this. He is very active and plays multiple sports throughout most of the calendar year. His current radiographs are shown in Figure A. Which of the following factors has the greatest impact on prognosis for this condition?

![[q214087-a-ap-mfc-ocd.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Concomitant tibial apophysitis
2. Degree of sport participation
3. Ethnicity
4. Family history
5. Skeletal immaturity

## Correct option

**5. Skeletal immaturity**

## Answer explanation

This patient has an ostochondral dissecans (OCD) lesion of the knee. Of the choices available, skeletal immaturity, as gauged by the open physes, has the greatest impact on prognosis.

Osteochondal dessicans of the knee is a common complaint among youth athletes. Symptoms are often vague but activity-related knee pain and frequent effusions are common complaints. The cause of these lesions remains unclear but it is believed that mechanical disruptions (i.e. discoid meniscus or tibial spine impingement) may have a hand in this. Whereas adult OCD lesions rarely heal spontaneously, juvenile OCDs lesions have great potential for resolution without surgical intervention. Factors which positively correlate with self-healing potential include location on the medial femoral condyle (compared with patella or lateral condyle), skeletal immaturity, lack of sclerosis on radiographs, and a lack of fluid signal deep to the lesion on MRI (lower stage).

Crawford and Safran provided an overview of OCD management. They discuss the role MRI now plays in discerning between which OCDs are stable versus unstable OCD lesions. They note that lesions with fluid signals interposed between the OCD lesion and the subchondral osseous bed are considered unstable and require one of many different surgical techniques to stabilize the fragment and/or induce a healing response.

*Andriolo et al. performed a systemic review of the different conservative treatment strategies for OCDs lesions.* Though the review was comprised of Level 3 and 4 evidence, **they found** larger and more advanced lesions were were more likely to fail conservative measures. Interestingly, they did not find evidence to support formal physical therapy, immobilization, or limited weight-bearing as beneficial.

Figure A is an AP radiograph showing the classic appearance of an OCD lesion on the medial femoral condyle. Illustration A is an algorithm for management of knee OCD lesions, proposed by Crawford and Safran.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1, 2, 3, and 4: None of these have been shown to have any impact on the prognosis or healing potential of OCD lesions.
