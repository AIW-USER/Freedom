## Question

During a supine examination, passive elevation of the lower extremity between 30-70 degrees causes radiating pain from the patient's low back to distal to the knee. This physical examination maneuver is known as:

## Options

1. Lhermitte's sign
2. Hoffman's test
3. Straight leg raise test
4. Spurling's test
5. Femoral nerve stretch test

## Correct option

**3. Straight leg raise test**

## Answer explanation

A positive straight leg raise (SLR) test is when pain is generated from a patient's back to past the knee upon elevation of the affected extremity while supine.

Specifically, passively elevating a patient's affected extremity from 30-70 degrees places tension on the L5-S1 nerve root exacerbating pain and indicating radiculopathy.

*Tawa et al. performed a systematic review summarized the results of 12 studies testing the accuracy of diagnostic neurologic physical examination maneuvers.* While overall inter-observer agreement for motor examinations exhibited poor to moderate agreement, sensitivity and specificity for SLR was 0.84 and 0.78, respectively.

Video 1 exhibits and summarizes the proper method to perform the SLR.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: Lhermitte's sign is positive when cervical flexion or extension leads to shockline sensation radiating down spinal axis and into arms and/or legs. A positive test is specific but not sensitive for cervical spinal cord compression and myelopathy
Answer 2: Hoffman's test is performed in one of two ways: hold and secure the middle phalanx of the long finger and then flick the distal phalanx into an extended position; Involuntary contraction of the thumb IP joint is a positive test.OR hold and secure the distal phalanx of the long finger and then flick the distal phalanx into an extended position; Involuntary contraction of the thumb IP joint is a positive test. A positive test is sensitive but not specific for cervical myelopathy.
Answer 4: Spurling's test is performed by rotating head toward the affected side, extending the neck, and then applying and axial load (applying downward pressure on the head); the test is considered positive if pain radiates into the ipsilateral arm when the test is performed for 30 seconds. This foraminal compression test that is specific, but not sensitive, in diagnosing acute radiculopathy in the cervical spine.
Answer 5: Femoral nerve stretch test is performed with the patient in the prone position and the knee is passively flexed to the thigh and the hip is passively extended; the test is positive if the patient experiences anterior thigh pain. This test is usually strongly positive in patients with protrusions at L2–L3 and L3–L4, slightly positive or negative in L4–L5 disc protrusions and negative in cases with a lumbosacral protrusion.
