## Options

1. Immediate closed reduction
2. Immediate MRI cervical spine
3. Immediate open reduction via posterior approach
4. Immediate open reduction via anterior approach
5. Immediate open reduction via combined anterior/posterior approach

## Correct option

**2. Immediate MRI cervical spine**

## Answer explanation

The patient has sustained a C7-T1 facet fracture-dislocation and has a GCS of 10. As such, the next best step in management is to proceed with an MRI to evaluate for an anterior disc protrusion prior to operative management.

Cervical facet dislocations occur due to flexion and distractions forces on the cervical spine. The majority of the dislocations occur in the subaxial spine (C3-C7) with 17% of the injuries being reported to occur at C7-T1. The treatment of such injuries is multi-factorial and depends on the mental status of the patient, neurological injury and MRI findings. If a patient is awake and alert, immediate closed reduction is recommended while carefully monitoring for neurological change during the reduction. After reduction, an MRI should be obtained to assess for anterior disc protrusion prior to operative intervention, since such finding would require an anterior approach to address. If the patient is not alert or oriented, a closed reduction should not be performed and one should proceed with an immediate MRI followed by surgical stabilization.

*Coe et al. performed a cadaveric biomechanical study to evaluate the rigidity of various posterior and anterior constructs (sublaminar, Rogers', and Bohlman's triple-wire wiring; AO hook plate fixation; and Caspar anterior plate fixation) in six subaxial human cervical spine specimens with bilateral facet dislocations.* **The authors noted** no significant difference in flexural stiffness and torsional stiffness between any of the constructs tested; however, there was a significant increase in the posterior strain during flexion and axial loading tests between the Caspar plate construct and all other tested constructs. **The authors concluded** that Caspar anterior plating is clearly an inferior method of treating distractive flexion injuries of the cervical spine when compared with all posterior fixation techniques.

*Grauer et al. performed a questionnaire study of 25 fellowship-trained spine surgeons to determine the variation in the timing and influence of MRI on the management of patients with traumatic cervical facet dislocations.* **They noted** that after reviewing the MRI, orthopedic surgeons were significantly more likely to choose a closed versus open reduction and neurosurgeons were significantly more likely than orthopedic surgeons to order an MRI before open or closed treatment. **The authors concluded** that the timing and utilization of MRI for patients with traumatic cervical facet dislocations remains variable.

Figures A through C are the sagittal CT cuts of a patient with unilateral C7-T1 facet fracture-dislocation most predominately noted in Figure B. Illustration A is a marked version of Figure B showing the location of the facet dislocation.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Immediate closed reduction of a facet dislocation is reserved for patients who are awake and cooperative. This patient has a GCS of 10.
Answer 3-5: An MRI should be performed prior to operative management of facet dislocations to assess for disc herniation that would require an anterior approach and discectomy to address.
