## Question

A 72-year-old female is involved in a motor vehicle collision. Radiographs in the emergency room demonstrate a posterior fracture-dislocation of the hip, and CT imaging demonstrates significant articular injury, acetabular and femoral head impaction, and loose osteochondral fragments within the joint. Which of the following is true regarding treatment for this patient when comparing operative fixation (ORIF) and total hip arthroplasty (THA)?

## Options

1. ORIF has equivalent outcome scores to THA
2. THA has a higher complication rate compared with ORIF
3. Rate of conversion from ORIF to THA is <10%
4. Rate of conversion from ORIF to THA is approximately 50%
5. Rate of conversion from ORIF to THA is >75%

## Correct option

**4. Rate of conversion from ORIF to THA is approximately 50%**

## Answer explanation

Patients with a fracture-dislocation of the acetabulum with significant articular injury, acetabular and femoral head impaction, and loose osteochondral fragments have a 50% rate of conversion to THA if treated initially with ORIF alone.

Acetabulum fractures are pelvis fractures that involve the articular surface of the hip joint and may involve one or two columns, one or two walls, or the roof within the pelvis. Diagnosis can be made radiographically with dedicated pelvis radiographs (including Judet views) but frequently requires a CT of the pelvis for surgical planning. Treatment can be nonoperative for non-displaced fractures, but displaced injuries require anatomic open reduction and internal fixation to minimize the development of post-traumatic osteoarthritis. Fracture-dislocations may be associated with significant articular injury, acetabular and femoral head impaction, and loose osteochondral fragments. When fractures with all of these characteristics are treated with open reduction and internal fixation (ORIF) alone, there has been shown to be a nearly 50% rate of eventual conversion to total hip arthroplasty (THA).

*Firoozabadi et al. retrospectively reviewed the risk factors for early conversion to THA.* **They reported** that acetabular fractures with posterior wall involvement are associated with a significantly higher rate of conversion to THA if the reduction is not near-anatomic. **They reported** a 50% rate of conversion to THA. **They concluded** that the aforementioned combination of clinical/radiographic findings is associated with poorer reductions and a higher rate of conversion to THA.

*Weaver et al. retrospectively reviewed the rate of revision surgery in elderly patients with acetabular fractures treated with ORIF or THA.* **They reported** that acute reconstruction of acetabular fractures with THA in the geriatric population seems to compare favorably with ORIF with improved pain scores. **They concluded** that in addition, there was a high rate of conversion to THA within 2 years of injury when patients were treated with ORIF alone.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: There are improved functional outcome scores in those treated with THA compared with ORIF.
Answer 2: THA has a lower complication rate in this specific patient population.
Answers 3 & 5: The rate of conversion from ORIF to THA is approximately 50% when the aforementioned radiographic/clinical findings are seen in combination.
