## Question

A 32-year-old female presents with swelling and pain in the right wrist. The symptoms have progressed over the last 6 months and she has noted a decreased range of motion of the wrist joint. Figures A and B are the radiographs and MRI of the right wrist and Figure C shows the histology. What is the best treatment option for this patient?

## Options

1. Radiation
2. En bloc excision with structural allograft reconstruction
3. Curettage with application of demineralized bone matrix and iliac crest bone marrow aspirate
4. En bloc excision with wrist fusion using vascularized fibular autograft
5. Curettage, application of liquid nitrogen, and polymethylmethacrylate

## Correct option

**5. Curettage, application of liquid nitrogen, and polymethylmethacrylate**

## Answer explanation

The patient is presenting with a giant cell tumor (GCT) of the right distal radius with preservation of the cortices and joint line. The best treatment option would be extensive curettage with adjuvant liquid nitrogen and polymethylmethacrylate (PMMA).

GCT is a common benign bone tumor that has some malignant characteristics. Approximately 5% of patients have metastasis to the lungs, which should prompt routine chest radiographs. Surgical treatment usually involves extensive curettage with local adjuvant therapy, which includes liquid nitrogen, phenol, argon beam, and PMMA. Utilizing PMMA had the added benefit of being employed with other local adjuvants prior to placement, as well as the potential to reveal any local recurrence as a lytic lesion at the bone-cement interface.

*Malawer et al. performed a retrospective review of 102 patients with GCT treated with curettage, burring of the tumor walls, and cryotherapy.* **The authors reported** a 98% cure rate, with complications including pathological fracture, skin necrosis, and degenerative changes. **They recommended** curettage with liquid nitrogen in the treatment of GCT due to the high success rate and joint preservation.

*Skubitz et al reviewed the pathophysiology, diagnosis, and treatment of GCTs.* **He reported** that treatment with extensive curettage alone had a high rate of recurrence in comparison to en bloc resection, but the use of local adjuvant therapy decreased recurrence rates. Effective adjunctive therapies included denosumab, bisphosphonates, and radiation therapy if patients are not surgical candidates, however, there are concerns about potential long term complications with these approaches.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Radiation is a treatment option in patients that are poor surgical candidates or that are refractory to multiple surgical attempts. However, radiation has the potential to result in radiation-induced sarcoma, which has a very poor prognosis.
Answer 2: En bloc excision and reconstruction with allograft is an option in some cases, especially those with lesions in the distal femur. In this patient, however, joint-preserving procedures would be preferred.
Answer 3: Use of demineralized bone matrix with bone marrow aspirate would likely heal the void after curettage, but would likely result in a higher recurrence rate.
Answer 4: The cortices and joint are preserved for this patient; fusion should therefore be a last resort after failure of other joint salvage options.
