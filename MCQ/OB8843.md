## Options

1. BMI over 25
2. Age over 10 years
3. Socioeconomic status
4. Surgery to dominant extremity
5. Male gender

## Correct option

**2. Age over 10 years**

## Answer explanation

Worse outcomes after surgical fixation of radial neck fractures have been demonstrated in individuals over the age of 10.

This patient sustained a radial neck fracture, often seen with a fall onto an outstretched hand with an extension and valgus moment. Many classification systems exist; the O'Brien classification is based on the angulation of the superior articular surface from the horizontal. Type 1 has under 30 degrees angulation and is treated with a short period of immobilization followed by a range of motion. Type 2 and 3 fractures have displacement over 30 degrees and require closed reduction. If this displacement is not reasonably corrected with closed methods, percutaneous with judicious selection of an open procedure is required.

*Zimmerman et al. reviewed outcomes after surgical treatment for 151 radial head and neck fractures over a 10-year period in a pediatric population.* **They found** that 31% of these patients had unsuccessful outcomes due to either heterotypic ossification, osteonecrosis, premature physeal arrest, neuromuscular injury, or radiocapitellar instability. These complications were all correlated with an age over 10 years, more severe fractures, and more invasive procedures.

*Gutierrez-de la Iglesia et al. retrospectively reviewed a series of radial neck fractures among pediatric patients over a 16-year period.* The surgical techniques used included casting, percutaneous intramedullary nailing, and open intramedullary nailing. **They found** no difference in functional outcome between the techniques but saw worse outcomes with more displaced fractures. **They concluded** that poor outcomes were associated with severe fractures rather than the use of open techniques.

Figure A shows a displaced radial neck fracture with 60 degrees of angulation. Figure B shows a reduced fracture using a K-wire as intramedullary fixation. Illustrations A and B show the Métaizeau technique for intramedullary reduction and fixation.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: BMI is not correlated with surgical outcomes
Answers 3 and 4: No data supports a correlation between outcome and handedness or socioeconomic status
Answer 5: Unsuccessful surgical outcomes have not been shown to favor either gender
