## Question

## Options

1. Chondroblastoma
2. UBC
3. Giant Cell Tumor
4. Osteoid Osteoma
5. Non-ossifying fibroma

## Correct option

**1. Chondroblastoma**

## Answer explanation

The clinical presentation, imaging, and histology are consistent with a chondroblastoma. Chondroblastomas can be located in the epiphysis and they may cross the physis into the metaphysis. Radiographically they appear as lytic lesions with a thin rim of sclerotic bone that is sharply marginated. Histologically they appear as chondroblasts arranged in a “cobblestone” pattern, with “chicken wire” calcification. Multinucleated giant cells may be present. They are benign cartilage lesions and are most commonly found in the distal femur, proximal tibia, and proximal humerus. They are treated with curettage and bone grafting. The lesion is linked to mutations in chromosomes 5 and 8. It rarely metastasizes to the lungs (2%).
