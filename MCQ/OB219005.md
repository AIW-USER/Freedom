## Answer explanation

Postaxial polydactyly has an autosomal dominant pattern of inheritance.

Polydactyly is the most common congenital hand malformation, which is characterized by an extra digit. The diagnosis is made clinically and is classified based on the location of the extra digit as preaxial (thumb), central, or postaxial (small finger). Postaxial polydactyly is most commonly seen in African Americans and has an autosomal dominant inheritance pattern. The presence of postaxial polydactyly in Caucasian individuals is rare and should warrant further workup for associated syndromes including Chondroectodermal Dysplasia or Ellis-van Creveld Syndrome. Pre-axial polydactyly, however, is more common in Caucasians. Surgical treatment includes formal reconstruction for well-formed digits or amputation for rudimentary/vestigial digits.

*Umair et al provide a review of the genetic and molecular features of polydactyly.* **They highlight** recent genes and loci that have been identified for non-syndromic polydactyly, as well as the complex genetic mechanisms resulting in polydactyly.

*Scott-Emuakpor et al analyzed polydactyly in the Nigerian population.* **They found** a slight male preponderance with an autosomal dominant inheritance pattern with 65% penetrance. They did not observe phenotypic differences between individuals who were homozygous versus heterozygous for the gene.

*Ortiz-Cruz et al reviewed a large, multicenter database to perform an epidemiologic analysis of polydactyl.* **They noted** it is more common in males, hands than feet, left than right, and Type B (75%) more than A (25%). **They also noted** that prematurity and forceps use were more common in polydactyly cases than in the control group.
