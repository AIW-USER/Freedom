## Question

A 63-year-old male patient with a history of rheumatoid arthritis presents with neck pain that has progressively worsened. Flexion and extension radiographs are depicted in figure A. The decision is made to treat the patient with C1-2 posterior instrumented fusion with a Goel-Harms construct. The patient is found to have osteoporosis preoperatively and the treating surgeon decides to place bicortical C1 lateral mass screws. During the screw placement, a 10° medial trajectory is used for the screws. What structure is avoided with this screw trajectory?

![[q217395-a-dynamic-xray-atlantoaxial-subluxation-small-moved.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Vertebral artery
2. Esophagus
3. Internal carotid artery
4. Spinal cord
5. C2 nerve root

## Correct option

**3. Internal carotid artery**

## Answer explanation

A 10° medial trajectory protects the internal carotid artery during C1 lateral mass screw placement, especially if the screws are placed bicortically.

C1 lateral mass screws provide rigid fixation for posterior cervical arthrodesis. Complications include vertebral artery injury, internal carotid artery injury, spinal cord injury, and C2 neuritis. A 10° medial trajectory potentially avoids the internal carotid artery, especially if bicortical screw fixation is used.

*Currier et al. reported a case of internal carotid artery impingement following C1-2 transarticular screw placement and evaluated three cadaveric specimens for corresponding anatomical considerations.* **The authors stated** medial trajectories appear to improve the margin of safety with respect to the internal carotid artery. **They concluded** the internal carotid artery is at risk with C1 lateral mass and C1-2 transarticular screw placement.

*Estillore et al. retrospectively reviewed 160 CT angiograms of the cervical spine and assessed the risk of internal carotid artery injury with C1 lateral mass and C1-2 transarticular screw placement.* **They reported** the internal carotid artery was 3.7 mm from the most ideal screw location and is posterior to the most anterior cortex of the C1 arch and medial to the transverse foramen. **They concluded** bicortical screw fixation of C1 poses a risk for internal carotid artery injury.

*Murakami et al. retrospectively reviewed 149 cervical spine CT scans and assessed the risk of internal carotid artery injury with C1 lateral mass screw placement.* **They reported** internal carotid artery injury can occur with a medial trajector up to 8.6 degrees. **The authors recommended** a 10-degree medial trajectory to avoid internal carotid artery injury.

Figure A is a flexion-extension radiograph depicting significant atlantoaxial instability.

Incorrect answers
Answer 1: The vertebral artery is not specifically being avoided by a 10-degree medial trajectory.
Answer 2: The esophagus is not present at the C1 level.
Answer 4: The spinal cord is at risk with excessive medial screw trajectories.
Answer 5: The C2 nerve root is not at risk for the screw trajectory, but rather the starting point.
