## Question

A 61-year-old female who reports left hip pain is seen in the emergency department. Figure 41a shows a radiograph obtained at that time. Ten months later, she reports excruciating left hip pain with ambulation. She notes that the pain has markedly worsened over the past several weeks. Figures 41b and 41c show a current radiograph and a coronal inversion recovery MRI scan of the pelvis. What is the most likely diagnosis?

## Options

1. Infection of the hip
2. Fracture of the hip
3. Osteoarthritis of the hip
4. Osteonecrosis of the hip
5. Rheumatoid arthritis of the hip

## Correct option

**4. Osteonecrosis of the hip**

## Answer explanation

The initial radiograph shows subtle flattening of the left femoral head, suggestive of osteonecrosis but without significant subchondral sclerosis. Figure 41b shows marked collapse in the left head over the intervening 10 months, and the MRI scan reveals collapse, significant edema in the head, and low signal intensity in the superior segment, all suggestive of osteonecrosis. Note that the right hip shows MRI changes, suggesting bilateral disease in this patient.
