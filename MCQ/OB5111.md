## Question

An 11-year-old female presents to as a trauma patient to the emergency department after being struck by a car while walking across the street. Examination reveals an isolated injury, exhibited in Figure A. In the operating room, closed reduction on a fracture table is performed, and the lateral fluoroscopic image is shown in Figure B. What is the next most appropriate step?

## Options

1. Spica cast immobilization and protected weight bearing
2. Percutaneous pinning and protected weight bearing
3. Open reduction and screw stabilization
4. Placement of lateral entry cephalomedullary nail with immediate weight bearing
5. Placement of a sliding hip screw device with immediate weight bearing

## Correct option

**3. Open reduction and screw stabilization**

## Answer explanation

Fractured hips in a child demand anatomic reduction with compression fixation. Following the reduction attempt, the fracture is not anatomically reduced, warranting open reduction and internal fixation with screw stabilization.

*Ju et al. retrospectively reviewed 48 femoral neck fractures fixed and followed them for a mean of 35.1 months.* Significantly lower rates of osteonecrosis along with improved function was seen in those patients with anatomic reduction. "Acceptable" fracture reduction resulted in significantly higher rates of osteonecrosis and worse function.

*Spence et al. reported a 29% rate of osteonecrosis in all operative pediatric femoral neck fractures.* *Of 70 patients, 20 were complicated by a diagnosis of osteonecrosis with the most significant predictive factors including initial fracture displacement and time to treatment (after 24 hours).* Regardless of fracture location, osteonecrosis was reported as a common complication.

Figure A exhibits an injury AP hip radiograph exhibiting a displaced femoral neck fracture in a skeletally immature patient. Figure B exhibits a lateral fluoroscopic image following attempted closed reduction on a fracture table. Illustration A reviews the Delbet Classification and overall osteonecrosis rate depending on fracture location.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: For a displaced fracture and for an 11-year-old, hip spica immobilization alone is inappropriate.
Answer 2: Percutaneous pinning may be an option with an anatomic reduction, however, this reduction is not anatomic.
Answers 4,5: A lateral entry nail or a sliding hip screw device is not appropriate in this situation, especially without achieving an open, anatomic reduction. Furthermore, immediate weight bearing is appropriate for the geriatric patient, but not for the young/adolescent patient.
