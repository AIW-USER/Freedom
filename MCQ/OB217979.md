## Question

A 40-year-old male with a past medical history of inflammatory arthritis presents to your office for evaluation of left elbow pain. He denies any injury to the elbow. He has exhausted nonoperative management and is interested in proceeding with surgical intervention. Radiographs reveal end-stage arthritis involving the ulnohumeral and radiocapitellar joints without subchondral collapse. Examination reveals limited motion including a flexion-extension arc of 75° and a pronation-supination arc of 70°. He has no laxity with varus and valgus testing. Which of the follow surgical interventions would be most appropriate in this patient?

## Options

1. Anterior and posterior tendon lengthening
2. Anconeus interposition arthroplasty
3. Achilles interposition arthroplasty
4. Unconstrained total elbow arthroplasty
5. Semi-constrained total elbow arthroplasty

## Correct option

**3. Achilles interposition arthroplasty**

## Answer explanation

This is a young patient with end stage inflammatory arthritis of the elbow. Of the surgeries listed, an Achilles interposition arthroplasty would be indicated.

Elbow arthritis is degenerative joint disease of the elbow that can be broken into three main types: osteoarthritis, post-traumatic arthritis and inflammatory arthritis. The diagnosis can be made with plain radiographs of the elbow. Treatment may be nonoperative or operative depending on patient activity demands, severity of elbow pain and degree of elbow dysfunction. Interposition arthroplasty is indicated in young, high demand patients with end stage arthritis (OA, RA, post-traumatic arthritis who would otherwise have received TEA if they were older); this procedure does not require lifting restrictions like TEA. It is imperative to know that elbow instability is a contraindication to performing an interposition arthroplasty.

*Nolla et al. retrospectively reviewed younger patients with severe post-traumatic elbow arthrosis treated with interposition arthroplasty and temporary hinged external fixation.* **They reported** that in some patients, treatment was considered to have failed because of early postoperative instability, and their results were classified as poor. **They concluded** that interposition arthroplasty can improve elbow motion and function but at the expense of elbow stability despite hinged external fixation.

*Larson et al. retrospectively reviewed interposition arthroplasty for the treatment of severe inflammatory and post-traumatic arthritis of the elbow.* **They reported** that interposition elbow arthroplasty should be considered a salvage procedure as it neither completely eliminates pain nor restores full function. **They concluded** that it may be indicated for young active patients with severe inflammatory or post-traumatic arthritis, especially those with limited elbow motion. Furthermore, the authors do not recommend this procedure when patients present with preoperative instability on physical examination.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Tendon lengthening would not adequately address his underlying end stage arthritis
Answer 2: An anconeus interposition arthroplasty would be an acceptable treatment modality is the arthritis was isolated to the radiocapitellar joint
Answer 4: In young, high demand patients with end stage arthritis, an interposition arthroplasty is preferred to TEA. An unconstrained TEA requires competent collateral ligaments, soft tissue envelope, and good bone quality
Answer 5: In young, high demand patients with end stage arthritis, an interposition arthroplasty is preferred to TEA. A semicontrained TEA utilized a "sloppy hinge" to allow for some varus-valgus and rotational laxity. It reduces stress on bone-cement interface, which ultimately reduces the incidence of component loosening
