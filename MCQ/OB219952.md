## Question

A 21-year-old professional baseball player is evaluated in the clinic for worsening right shoulder pain and decreased batting power. Examination is significant for positive findings on Kim and jerk testing and subsequent MRI confirms a posterior labral tear. Which of the following options is the most likely etiology of the patient’s pathology?

## Options

1. Recurrent anterior instability events
2. Posterior capsular tightening
3. Acute anterior subluxation event while throwing
4. Acute posterior subluxation event while batting
5. Anabolic steroid use

## Correct option

**4. Acute posterior subluxation event while batting**

## Answer explanation

Batter’s shoulder involves an acute posterior labral tear incurred during a posterior subluxation event while swinging a bat (Answer 4).

The patient presents with right shoulder pain and decreased batting power, with examination and imaging consistent with a posterior labral tear. In baseball players or athletes performing similar forceful swinging motions, posterior labral tears can occur during an acute posterior subluxation event caused by a posteriorly directed force on an adducted lead shoulder while swinging a bat.

*O'Keefe et al. reported a retrospective case series that included five patients with batter’s shoulder injuries and a minimum of one year follow-up.* *All patients were treated with arthroscopic repair and could return to play at their previous level without limitation.* *Functional outcome scores obtained at the final follow-up revealed minimal residual limitations after treatment, and there were no significant losses in strength and range of motion.* **The authors concluded** that batter’s shoulder injuries carry an improved prognosis regarding return to play compared to other causes of posterior labral tears.

*Kang et al. reviewed the etiology and management of Batter's shoulder.* *They described the typical mechanism of injury involving an increased force on the posterior capsulolabral complex while swinging and missing on a pitch.* *They described an expected return to play six to seven months after arthroscopic repair.* **They concluded** that good to excellent results can be expected following the appropriate treatment of batter's shoulder injuries.

Incorrect Responses:
Answer 1: Batter’s shoulder typically occurs following an acute posterior subluxation event during a forceful swing.
Answer 2: Posterior capsular tightening more commonly results in glenohumeral internal rotation deficit (GIRD).
Answer 3: An acute anterior subluxation event is less likely to result in a batter’s shoulder injury to the posterior labrum.
Answer 5: There is no reported association between anabolic steroid use and batter’s shoulder injuries.
