## Question

All of the following are characteristic findings in a patient with arthrogryposis EXCEPT:

## Options

1. Normal cognition
2. Internal rotation contractures of the shoulder
3. Symmetrical extremity deformity
4. Progressive joint contractures which peak at skeletal maturity
5. Bilateral hip dislocation

## Correct option

**4. Progressive joint contractures which peak at skeletal maturity**

## Answer explanation

Athrogryposis is a congenital disorder characterized by multiple joint contractures and normal cognition, however, the joint contractures are not progressive by nature. The etiology is unknown and is most likely multi-factorial. There does not appear to be a genetic predisposition. The disease is not progressive, and contractures do not worsen with growth. Common contractures include adduction and internal rotation of the shoulders, hip flexion and subluxation/dislocation, knee rigidity, and club feet. Symmetric contractures are common. *Yau et al reviewed 19 patients (38 hips) with dislocated or subluxed hips in patients with arthrogryposis with an average 20 year follow-up.* Thirteen of the 19 were community ambulators. Surgical reduction did not improve clinical outcomes. Illustration A is a clinical example of an arthrogrypotic baby.
