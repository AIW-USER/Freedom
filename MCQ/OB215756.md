## Answer explanation

Figure C represents a Zone 1 fracture to the base of the 5th metatarsal consistent with a "pseudojones" type injury. These fractures are best treated with immediate weight bearing in a postoperative shoe.

Fractures to the base of the 5th metatarsal are divided into three zones based on their anatomic relationship to the 4-5 metatarsal articulation. Fractures proximal to this juncture are classified as Zone 1 injuries, have an excellent blood supply and can be treated with weight bearing in a postoperative shoe or walking boot. The risk of symptomatic non-union is rare. Zone 2 injuries are those that involve the 4-5 metatarsal articulation and are either treated with non-weight bearing in a cast for 6-8 weeks in the non-athlete or with an intramedullary screw in athletic patients trying to return to sport. Lastly, Zone 3 injuries involve the proximal diaphysis and are distal to the 4-5 metatarsal joint and are best immobilized and kept non weight bearing.

*Dameron et al review the anatomy and treatment of the 5th metatarsal.* *They report on the circulatory and healing differences between the three zones of the 5th metatarsal.* **They conclude** that Zone 1 injuries may be treated with immediate weight bearing, whereas, fractures distal to this region should be treated more conservatively given the poor blood supply and healing potential.

*Shahid et al review the treatment of zone 1 fifth metatarsal fractures.* **They report** that return to pre-injury function occurred after 9 weeks for those patients treated in a walking boot, whereas it took 12 weeks for those treated in a walking cast. **They conclude** that, less is more for these injuries, and that patient had a faster return to function with treatment in a walking boot.

Figure C demonstrates a Zone 1 fifth metatarsal base fracture. Figure D shows a fracture to the base of the 2nd metatarsal consistent with a lisfranc injury. Figure E is an example of a Zone 3 fifth metatarsal fracture.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1 and 4: Lisfranc injuries are very unstable and should not be treated conservatively with weight bearing as tolerated in shoe.
Answer 2: Displaced 1st metatarsal fractures should be treated surgically as there is no support provided by an inter metatarsal ligament.
Answer 5: Zone 2 injuries should be treated with non-weightbearing immobilization if non-operative treatment is elected.
