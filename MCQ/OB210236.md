## Question

A 10-year-old girl presents for a routine pre-sport participation physical examination. She is active in soccer but has complained of lateral ankle and foot pain after activities over the past few years. She has no laxity on examination with anterior drawer or talar tilt testing, but intrinsic wasting of the hands are noted. A standing clinical view of her feet are shown in figure A. There is a family history of similar foot positioning abnormalities. What is the next step in making a diagnosis of the most common underlying condition?

*Figure A*

## Options

1. Muscle biopsy
2. Nerve biopsy
3. Creatinine phosphokinase and aldolase levels
4. Electrodiagnostic studies
5. Echocardiogram

## Correct option

**4. Electrodiagnostic studies**

## Answer explanation

The clinical presentation and family history are concerning for Charcot-Marie-Tooth(CMT). Following a thorough neurologic physical examination, electrodiagnostic studies (EMG/NCS) should be performed to confirm the diagnosis.

The cavovarus foot in a child has multiple different etiologies with the underlying pathophysiology being linked to a muscular imbalance. The most common etiology is a progressive neurological condition. When the condition is bilateral, it is most often linked to a congenital or hereditary condition with Charcot-Marie-Tooth (Hereditary Motor Sensory Neuropathy) being the most common diagnosis. The diagnostic algorithm for CMT generally dictates a thorough neurologic assessment followed by electrodiagnostic studies and then genetic testing; although, immediate genetic testing may be warranted if a family member already has known CMT.

*Noguiera et al. present a review of the cavus foot deformity presentation, diagnosis, and treatment.* **They note** that the cavus foot is usually due to a neurologic condition that then requires a complete clinical and imaging evaluation. The spine exam is of particular importance as the presence of scoliosis is suggestive of CMT and evidence of spinal dysraphism suggests a neural tube defect. The discovery of a neurological cause will often change the treatment course for the patient.

*Schwend and colleagues present a review of the cavus foot deformity in children.* *Their review of the topic notes that the cavovarus deformity is the most frequent type of cavus foot, and presents with an elevated medial longitudinal arch and first ray plantarflexion.* The most common progressive neurological cause is CMT, but non-progressive causes include cerebral palsy and poliomyelitis.

Figure A demonstrates the clinical picture of a patient with bilateral cavovarus feet.
Illustration A is an example of an electrodiagnostic print out showing slowed nerve conduction velocity. In CMT the expected electrodiagnostic findings would be a diffuse, uniform delayed conduction velocity in both the motor and sensory nerves.

Incorrect Answers
Answer 1: Muscle biopsy is generally not indicated for the diagnosis of CMT, but is sometimes part of the workup in muscular dystrophies
Answer 2: Nerve biopsy is sometimes used in the diagnosis of CMT after EMG/NCS and genetic testing results are inconclusive
Answer 3: CPK and aldolase levels are elevated before symptom onset in muscular dystrophies, but are not part of the diagnostic testing in CMT.
Answer 5: An echocardiogram is not part of the CMT diagnostic testing.
