## Answer explanation

A Type II anterior oblique fracture pattern has a fracture line is perpendicular to anterior screw trajectory.

Odontoid fractures are relatively common fractures of the C2 (axis) dens that can be seen in low energy falls in elderly patients and high energy traumatic injuries in younger patients. The diagnosis can be made with standard lateral and open-mouth odontoid radiographs. Some fractures may be difficult to visualize on radiographs and require a CT scan to diagnose. MRI is rarely indicated as these fractures are usually not associated with neurologic symptoms. Treatment may be nonoperative or operative depending on the Anderson and D'Alonzo type and risk factors for nonunion. Patient older than 80 have a high morbidity and mortality regardless of nonoperative or operative treatment.

*Carvalho et al. reviewed odontoid fractures.* **They reported** that these injuries are associated with a high incidence of nonunion, morbidity, and mortality. **They concluded** that anterior odontoid fixation can be used in reducible fractures with ideal fracture patterns.

*Grauer et al. modified the Anderson and D'Alonzo classification of odontoid fractures based on current clinical treatment options.* **They reported** that the reproducibility of this system was demonstrated by the moderate agreement observed when applied to odontoid fractures. **They concluded** that the proposed utility of this system is its ability to guide clinical decision making in the treatment of odontoid fractures.

Figure A is a Type I odontoid fracture. Figure B is a posterior oblique Type II odontoid fracture. Figure C is a transverse Type II odontoid fracture. Figure D is an anterior oblique odontoid fracture. Figure E is a Type III odontoid fracture. Illustration A depicts the Anderson and D'Alonzo Classification. Illustration B depicts the Grauer subclassification of Anderson and D'Alonzo classification.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: This is a Type I odontoid fracture and is not amendable to a single anterior screw fixation.
Answer 2: This is a Type II odontoid fracture with a posterior oblique pattern and is not amendable to a single anterior screw fixation.
Answer 3: This is a Type II odontoid fracture with a transverse pattern and is not amendable to a single anterior screw fixation.
Answer 5: This is a Type III odontoid fracture and is not amendable to a single anterior screw fixation.
