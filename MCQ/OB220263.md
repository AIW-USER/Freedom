## Question

A 55-year-old female presents to the emergency department with pain and swelling in the right knee after she lost her footing and slipped down stairs earlier today. Plain films of the right knee are shown in figures A & B. Per your institution's protocol, the patient undergoes both a CT scan and an MRI to evaluate for fracture pattern and concomitant soft tissue injury. MRI shows a meniscocapsular avulsion of the lateral meniscus. During preoperative counseling, you inform the patient she has both a soft tissue and bony injury to the right knee which will be addressed surgically, and she asks how the soft tissue injury will affect her outcome. Compared to a fracture pattern without a meniscal injury, her lateral meniscus injury has what effect on her outcome at 1 year?

## Options

1. Increased risk of conversion to arthroplasty
2. Decreased knee range of motion
3. Increased Kellgren-Lawrence grade of osteoarthritis
4. Increased risk of needing additional arthroscopic surgery to address meniscal tear
5. Similar knee function and quality of life

## Correct option

**5. Similar knee function and quality of life**

## Answer explanation

This patient suffered a lateral tibial plateau fracture (Schatzker Type II). Concomitant sutured lateral meniscus tears or untreated medial meniscus tears have not been found to affect outcome data at one year (Answer 5).

Tibial plateau fractures are a complex group of injuries that often manifest with a severe ligamentous or meniscal injury that can be challenging to manage in the setting of open reduction with internal fixation. Meniscal tears are common, and entrapment may hinder the anatomic reduction of the plateau. Tears involving the lateral meniscus are more common than the medial meniscus and are most commonly associated with Schatzker II fracture patterns, > 10 mm articular depression, and > 6 mm condylar widening. Medial meniscal tears are more commonly associated with Schatzker IV fractures. The most common type of lateral meniscus tear is a meniscocapsular avulsion. A lateral sub-meniscal arthrotomy can be used to assess the articular reduction and address concomitant lateral meniscus injuries. Prospective data on tibial plateau fractures has shown no difference in outcomes at one year when comparing tibial plateau fractures with sutured lateral meniscus tears and no meniscal tear.

*Warner et al. performed a prospective cohort study of 82 patients to determine if ligamentous and meniscal injuries as determined by initial magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) altered clinical outcomes after the fixation of tibial plateau fractures.* **They reported** that on injury MRI, 73% of patients had injuries to at least one soft tissue structure and that patient-reported outcomes and range of motion assessments were not significantly different in patients with and without medial meniscal tears, sutured lateral meniscal tears, and complete medial collateral ligament (MCL) ruptures. **They conclude** that sutured lateral meniscal tears, untreated medial meniscus tears, and complete MCL ruptures did not significantly affect clinical outcomes and that obtaining a preoperative MRI in patients with tibial plateau fractures to diagnose soft tissue injuries may not alter the surgical treatment.

*Gardner et al. performed a prospective cohort study of 103 patients to determine the incidence of injury to soft tissue structures of the knee in tibial plateau fractures scheduled for surgery.* **They report** that 77% sustained a complete tear or avulsion of one or more cruciate or collateral ligaments, 91% had evidence of lateral meniscus pathology, 44% had medial meniscus tears, 68% had tears of one or more of the posterolateral corner structures of the knee. **They conclude** that the incidence of complete ligamentous or meniscal disruption associated with operative tibial plateau fractures was higher than previously reported.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: A repaired lateral meniscus tear in the setting of a lateral tibial plateau fracture is not associated with increased conversion to arthroplasty.
Answer 2: Patients with or without meniscal tears have the same reported range of motion at 1-year follow-up
Answer 3: A repaired lateral meniscus tear in the setting of a lateral tibial plateau fracture is not associated with increased severity of Kellgren-Lawrence grade osteoarthritis at 1-year follow-up.
Answer 4: A repaired lateral meniscus tear in the setting of a lateral tibial plateau fracture is not associated with an increased risk of needing additional arthroscopic surgery to address the meniscal tear.
