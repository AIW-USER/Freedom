## Answer explanation

This patient sustained an incomplete spinal cord injury suggestive of central cord syndrome (Figure C)

Central cord syndrome is the most common incomplete cord injury. It occurs often in the elderly with minor extension injury mechanisms due to anterior osteophytes and posterior infolded ligamentum flavum. It is believed to be caused by spinal cord compression and central cord edema with selective destruction of lateral corticospinal tract white matter. Symptoms include weakness with hand dexterity most affected, as well as hyperpathia (burning in the distal upper extremity). On examination, these patients will demonstrate a motor deficit worse in UE than LE (some preserved motor function); the hands have a more pronounced motor deficit than the arms.

*Wagner et al. reviewed traumatic central cord syndrome.* **They reported** that this is an incomplete spinal cord injury characterized primarily by disproportionate motor and sensory loss in the upper extremities compared with the lower extremities. **They concluded** that central cord syndrome is generally treated surgically, but the timing and surgical approach depend on injury characteristics, including the injury pattern, neurologic examination and its evolution, and patient comorbidities, and that early surgery may be performed safely in the appropriate patient, but there is little evidence to support the notion that earlier surgery affects neurologic outcomes.

*Kepler et al. reviewed the changes in the American Spinal Injury Association motor score (AMS) in the 1st week after traumatic central cord syndrome (CCS) to identify predictors of improved early outcome in patients treated with early versus delayed surgical intervention.* **They reported** that early treatment of patients with CCS remains controversial. **They concluded** that although some long-term neurological recovery is expected in patients with a CCS, surgeons and patients should not expect early neurological improvement with or without early operative intervention.

Figure A depicts a complete spinal cord injury. Figure B depicts anterior cord syndrome. Figure C depicts central cord syndrome. Figure D depicts Brown-Sequard syndrome. Figure E depicts posterior cord syndrome. Illustration A-E is the corresponding labeled spinal cord injuries

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: This depicts a complete spinal cord injury that affect bilateral extremities below the level of the injury
Answer 2: Anterior cord syndrome is a condition that is characterized by motor dysfunction and dissociated sensory deficit below the level of spinal cord injury. This results in anterior spinal artery injury which affects the anterior 2/3 spinal cord
Answer 4: Brown-Sequard syndrome is caused by complete cord hemitransection
usually seen with penetrating trauma. It results in ipsilateral motor deficits with contralateral pain and temperature lost
Answer 5: Posterior cord syndrome is very rare but leads to a loss of proprioception but preserved motor, pain, light touch
