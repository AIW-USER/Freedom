## Question

A 73-year-old female underwent total knee arthroplasty (TKA) several years ago. This evening, she fell and sustained the injury depicted in Figure A. It is a closed injury and she is neurovascularly intact. When comparing plate fixation to intramedullary nail fixation, which of the following statements is most true?

![[q220456-a-fig-a.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Intramedullary nail fixation has a lower rate of malreduction
2. Plate fixation has a higher rate of complications
3. Union rate is equivalent
4. Intramedullary nail fixation has a slower time to full weight bearing
5. Intramedullary nail fixation has a faster time to union

## Correct option

**3. Union rate is equivalent**

## Answer explanation

When comparing intramedullary nail (IMN) fixation to plate fixation in the treatment of distal femoral periprosthetic fractures, systematic reviews have found an equivalent union rate (Answer 3).

Periprosthetic distal femur fractures generally occur in elderly patients with an incidence of 0.3-2.5%, with poor bone quality being a strong risk factor. There are several classifications that have been proposed that generally include the location of the fracture, degree of displacement, and stability of components. Treatment options include nonoperative, open reduction internal fixation (ORIF) with plate or IMN, revision to long stem prosthesis, and distal femur replacement (DFR). Nonoperative treatment is uncommon and is generally reserved for non-displaced fractures with stable implants. ORIF with IMN or plate is indicated for displaced fractures with stable implants. Retrograde IMN requires a TKA with an open box design to accommodate the nail. Arthroplasty options such as revising to a long-stem prosthesis or DFR are generally indicated for loose components and/or poor bone stock that precludes adequate fixation. DFR offers early weight-bearing with comparable rates of complication, reoperation, and return to preoperative ambulatory status when compared to ORIF.

*Shah et al. performed a systematic review on the fixation of distal femur periprosthetic fractures.* **They found** that the union rate between IMN fixation and plate fixation was equivalent. **They also found** that plate fixation had a lower rate of complications and reoperation, whereas IMN had a quicker time to full weight-bearing. Additionally, a greater proportion of patients treated with IMN returned to preinjury activity. **They concluded** that each treatment has its advantages.

*Shin et al. performed a meta-analysis on fixation of distal femur periprosthetic fractures.* **They found** that IMN had a shorter operation time and a higher Knee Society Score (KSS) compared to plate fixation. **They also found** that the time to union was similar. **They concluded** that outcomes are comparable between IMN and plate fixation for distal femur periprosthetic fractures.

Figure A has AP and lateral plain radiographs demonstrating a distal femoral periprosthetic fracture.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: IMN fixation has a higher rate of malreduction.
*Answer 2: Plate fixation has a lower rate of complications, as found by Shah et al.*
Answer 4: IMN fixation has a faster time to full weight bearing.
Answer 5: Faster time to union is not an advantage of IMN fixation.
