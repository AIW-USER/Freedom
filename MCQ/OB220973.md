## Question

A 38-year-old male presents with soft tissue swelling of the left thigh for approximately 3 months. The patient subsequently undergoes a core needle biopsy, which is shown in Figure A. Using the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) staging system specific to these tumors, which of the following pairings of tumor stage and patient/tumor characteristics, if present, is most accurate?

![[q220973-a-pleomorphic-liposarcoma.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Stage IV - Low grade histology, widest tumor dimension of 7 cm, no regional lymph node metastasis, no distant metastasis.
2. Stage IIA - intermediate grade histology, widest tumor dimension of 11 cm, no regional lymph node metastasis, no distant metastasis
3. Stage IIIA: high grade histology, widest tumor dimension of 8 cm, no regional lymph node metastasis, no distant metastasis
4. Stage IIIB: high grade histology, widest tumor dimension of 18 cm, positive regional lymph node metastasis, no distant metastasis
5. Stage IB: intermediate grade histology, widest tumor dimension of 7 cm, no regional lymph node metastasis, no distant metastasis

## Correct option

**3. Stage IIIA: high grade histology, widest tumor dimension of 8 cm, no regional lymph node metastasis, no distant metastasis**

## Answer explanation

A patient with liposarcoma (soft-tissue sarcoma) with high-grade histology and a maximum tumor diameter of 8 cm, without regional lymph node or distant metastases, can be classified by the AJCC 8th edition staging system as Stage IIIA (answer 3).

The AJCC staging system for soft tissue sarcomas is a useful classification system that prognosticates patients with this subset of musculoskeletal tumors. This classification accounts for four patient/tumor characteristics: size, regional lymph node involvement, distant metastasis, and histologic grade. The most recent version of this staging system (8th edition) introduced new T3 and T4 groupings, designated for tumors measuring 10-15 cm and>15 cm, respectively. Criticisms of the AJCC staging systems include the lack of consideration of tumor depth, a factor addressed in other classification systems, such as the Vanderbilt staging system.

Jawad and Scully discussed the evolution of musculoskeletal tumor staging systems in a brief review. They cover the Enneking staging system for malignant and benign tumors and contrast it to the American Joint Committee for Cancer staging system for soft tissue sarcomas. They additionally cover the limitations of these staging systems, particularly covering the inability to apply these schemata to marrow or reticuloendothelial system tumors that involve the bone, the limited applicability to spinal tumors, and the Enneking system’s lack of consideration of lesion size.

*Fisher et al. performed a retrospective review of 26,144 patients with soft tissue sarcoma to assess the prognostic capabilities of the 8th edition American Joint Committee on Cancer staging system, which further subdivided the tumor category component into four groups with new classifications for tumors between 10 and 15 cm in size (T3) and those greater than 15 cm (T4).* **They found** that the new staging system led to an increase in the number of patients with stage III tumors, but that there was little difference in overall survival between patients with T3 and T4 tumors. **They concluded** that use of the T stage more accurately predicts overall survival, but that the effect of tumor size on survival may be relevant only up to a specific size.
