Chapter: Spine MCQs — Scoliosis Kyphosis

## Question

In Adolescent idiopathic scoliosis aged 13 years, correct statement regarding treatment with bracing is

## Options

1. Boston brace will be useful when lesion is below T12
2. Milwaukee brace will be useful when lesion above T7
3. Minimum wear time has to be 8 hours per day
4. Bracing success is defined as correction > 5 degree

## Correct option

**2. Milwaukee brace will be useful when lesion above T7**

## Answer explanation

Answer: 2

BRACE WEAR IN SCOLIOSIS

Bracing for a case of Adolescent Idiopathic Scoliosis is meant to stop progression (not to correct deformity). Hence a brace would be indicated in the following scenario:

- cobb angle ranging from 25° to 45°
- flexible deformity in skeletally immature patient (Risser 0, 1,2)

Wear time: Recommended for 16-23 hours/ day until skeletal maturity or surgical intervention deemed necessary (wear minimum 12 hours/ day is required to slow progression; 50% reduction in need for surgery can be expected)

Poor prognosis with brace treatment is associated with:

- Poor patient compliance
- hypokyphosis (relative contraindication)
- males
- obese patients
-

Sanders staging system uses anteroposterior hand radiograph and curve magnitude (Lenke classification) to assess risk of progression despite bracing. The system predicts the risk of curve progression despite bracing to > 50 degrees in Lenke type I and III curves.

Brace types: -

Curves with apex above T7

- Cervico-thoraco-lumbo-sacral orthosis
- Milwaukee brace (extends to neck for apex above T7)

Curves with apex at T7 or below

- Thoraco-Lumbo-Sacral Orthosis
- Boston-style brace
- Charleston Bending brace (night brace only)

Bracing failure:

- Bracing success is defined as <5° curve progression
- Bracing failure is defined

- 6° or more curve progression at orthotic discontinuation (skeletal maturity)
- absolute progression to > 45° either before or at skeletal maturity, or discontinuation in favor of surgery

- Skeletal maturity is defined as

- Risser 4
- <1cm change in height over 2 visits 6 months apart
- 2 years post menarche
