#TO #trauma #brachialplexusinjuries
Chapter: Nerve Injury MCQs — Brachial Plexus Injuries

## Question

Not seen in a pre-ganglionic brachial plexus palsy

## Options

1. Absent motor conduction on NCV studies
2. Absent sensory conduction on NCV studies
3. Wallerian degeneration is present
4. Recovery is poor

## Correct option

**2. Absent sensory conduction on NCV studies**

## Answer explanation

Injuries of the plexus can be either preganglionic or postganglionic. Avulsion of the nerve root from the spinal cord is a preganglionic lesion, i.e. disruption proximal to dorsal root ganglion. Rupture of a nerve root distal to ganglion or of a trunk or peripheral nerve is a postganglionic lesion. It is important to differentiate the two lesions as they have prognostic significance with preganglionic lesions seldom showing recovery.

![[TO17138122-1.png]]

 Diagnostic Studies (EMG) can differentiate between pre and postganglionic injuries. In preganglionic brachial plexus injury, denervating potentials are seen in the segmental paraspinal muscles innervated by the posterior primary rami. In preganglionic brachial plexus injury, afferent sensory fibers will not undergo Wallerian degeneration following nerve root avulsion because cell bodies of afferent sensory fibers are located in dorsal root ganglion which resides distally, so nerve conduction studies (NCS) show absent motor conduction with intact sensory conduction.
If nerve conduction velocity demonstrates absence of both sensory and motor conduction then lesion is post ganglionic.

Answer: 2
