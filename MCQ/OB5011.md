## Question

A 5-year-old boy presents with a clumsy gait. The referring pediatrician notes normal intelligence. On further work-up, the patient is noted to have increased keratan sulfate in his urine. Figure A and B exhibit flexion-extension cervical spine radiographs. What is the diagnosis?

## Options

1. San Filippo Syndrome
2. Pseudoachondroplasia
3. Down's Syndrome
4. Morquio Syndrome
5. Hurler's Syndrome

## Correct option

**4. Morquio Syndrome**

## Answer explanation

With increased keratan sulfate in the urine along with atlantoaxial instability caused by odontoid hypoplasia, Morquio Syndrome is the correct diagnosis. Flexion/extension lateral C-spine radiographs are necessary in the preoperative evaluation of patients with Morquio Syndrome.

Patients with Morquio Syndrome fall within the mucopolysaccharidoses class of enzyme deficiency with autosomal recessive inheritance. Diagnosis is facilitated by noting increased urinary keratan sulfate. Characteristically with normal intelligence, atlantoaxial instability due to odontoid hypoplasia is a hallmark presentation, which can cause myelopathic symptoms. Stenosis can also occur due to accumulation of glycosaminoglycans (GAG) behind the dens.

*McKay et al. performed a systematic review to summarize all congenital causes of cervical instability.* For those in the mucopolysaccharidoses class, particularly those with Morquio Syndrome, atlantoaxial instability with subsequent further stenosis due to GAG accumulation is so common that it has led some to advocate for prophylactic decompression and fusion. Close monitoring and activity restriction is recommended.

*Solanki et al., in a comprehensive review on the spine presentation in Morquio patients, note that the most common presenting symptoms is C1-2 instability with unsteady/waddling gait.* **They emphasize** the need for close monitoring, if possible with advanced imaging as often times, radiographs are inadequate. Dynamic CT or MRI can help to better assess the true degree of stenosis, which can often be worse than just the instability, due to the accumulation of GAGs behind the dens.

Illustrations A and B exhibit extension and flexion cervical spine radiographs that show gross instability as well as a hypoplastic dens. Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: San Filippo Syndrome is characterized by increased heparan sulfate in the urine.
Answer 2: Pseudoachondroplasts also have cervical instability secondary to odontoid hypoplasia, however, have normal urine studies.
Answer 3: While Down's syndrome also has upper cervical instability, it is typically not associated with a hypoplastic dens. Also, Down's patients do not have normal intelligence and have normal urine studies.
Answer 5: Hurler Syndrome is characterized by increased dermatan sulfate in the urine.
