## Question

A 25-year-old male is running and trips over a curb, sustaining a twisting injury to his ankle. He notes obvious deformity to his ankle. He is taken to the nearest trauma center by ambulance, where the injury is noted to be closed and his neurovascular status intact. Radiographs reveal a trimalleolar ankle fracture/dislocation. After closed reduction and splinting in the emergency department, he is scheduled for open reduction and internal fixation the following day. A CT scan of the ankle is obtained and based on these findings, you plan to place the patient prone and perform dual posterolateral and medial approaches to address all of the fracture fragments. ## Answer explanation

Fixation of the posterior malleolus restores the anatomic function of the posterior inferior tibiofibular ligament (PITFL) and significantly improves syndesmotic stability.

The distal tibiofibular syndesmosis consists of 4 primary ligaments including the anterior inferior tibiofibular ligament (AITFL), posterior inferior tibiofibular ligament (PITFL), inferior transverse ligament (ITL), and the interosseous ligament (IOL). The syndesmosis is responsible for resisting primarily rotational and axial forces at the ankle. Syndesmotic injury in combination with an ankle fracture is quite common and can be measured radiographically by evaluating the tibiofibular clear space and overlap. More recently, the importance of the PITFL has been elucidated in ankle fractures where there is posterior malleolus involvement. Along these lines, it has been suggested that the stiffness of the syndesmosis is restored to 70% of normal with fixation of the posterior malleolus versus 40% with isolated syndesmotic fixation.

*Ogilvie-Harris et al. studied the biomechanical contributions of the syndesmotic ligaments by individually sectioning them and testing the syndesmotic resistance to diastasis afterwards.* **They noted** the stability conferred from the individual ligaments was as follows: AITFL 35%, IOL 22%, PITFL 41%. **They argue** that both the anterior and posterior syndesmotic ligaments should be directly evaluated for injury and addressed as necessary.

*Miller et al. prospectively compared postoperative stability between posterior malleolus fixation and syndesmotic screw fixation in unstable ankle fractures.* **They found** that postoperative outcome scores were similar in both groups, but tibiofibular clear space was decreased in the posterior malleolus fixation group. **They conclude** that fixing the posterior malleolus on its own is a reasonable way to restore syndesmotic stability after ankle fractures.

*Irwin et al. review posterior malleolus fractures including morphology and associated anatomy.* **They note** the importance of the PITFL for tibiotalar load transfer, posterior talar stability and rotational ankle stability. They finish by saying that current indications for fixing posterior malleolus fractures are still not well elucidated, but there is evidence that the presence of a posterior malleolus fracture in general worsens the patient's prognosis.
