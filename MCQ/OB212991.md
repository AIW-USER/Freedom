## Question

A 44-year-old patient is presenting with right dorsal foot pain, loss of sensation, and weakness that started 3 weeks ago after moving large heavy boxes from his friend's apartment. Coughing and the Valsalva maneuver make the pain worse. Which physical exam finding would suggest peroneal nerve palsy instead of L5 radiculopathy?

## Options

1. Weak ankle plantarflexion
2. Weak ankle dorsiflexion
3. Intact ankle inversion
4. Intact ankle eversion
5. Weak knee extension

## Correct option

**3. Intact ankle inversion**

## Answer explanation

Peroneal nerve palsy would present with intact ankle inversion strength with weak ankle dorsiflexion, weak toe extension, and weak ankle eversion.

Peroneal nerve palsy is the result of peripheral compression of the common peroneal nerve or the peroneal branch of the sciatic nerve. Patients can present with similar complaints with L5 radiculopathy, including foot drop, dorsal foot pain, and dorsal foot numbness. The tibialis posterior is innervated by the L5 nerve root and the tibial nerve, which would be weakened in the setting of L5 radiculopathy. Additionally, tibialis anterior (L4-S1) and tibialis posterior (L5) provide inversion

*Baima et al. reviewed the etiology, evaluation, and management of peroneal nerve injuries.* **They reported** based on the literature that nerve lesions at the knee are the most common with traumatic causes being the most frequent. Other muscles that are innervated by the L5 nerve root, including the tibialis posterior, should be examined to determine if the patient is presenting with radiculopathy as opposed to peroneal nerve palsy.

*Anderson et al. performed a retrospective chart review of postoperative neuropathic complications following foot and ankle surgery.* **They reported** a 5% complication rate associated with popliteal nerve blocks and 0.7% of patients having unresolved symptoms at the final follow-up. **They concluded** that nerve injury following a popliteal nerve block is more common then previously expected.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: Weak ankle plantarflexion would be the result of S1 radiculopathy and wouldn't present with dorsal foot pain.
Answer 2: Weak ankle dorsiflexion would be present with peroneal nerve palsy, but the tibialis anterior is also innervated by the deep peroneal nerve, which receives contributions from the L5 nerve root. In both L5 radiculopathy and peroneal nerve palsy, weak ankle dorsiflexion can be present.
Answer 4: Ankle eversion is initiated by the peroneus brevis and longus which are innervated by the superficial peroneal nerve, and specifically S1 nerve root contributions. Therefore, intact ankle eversion would be present with L5 radiculopathy, but not with peroneal nerve palsy.
Answer 5: Weak knee extension would be the result of L4 radiculopathy or higher.
