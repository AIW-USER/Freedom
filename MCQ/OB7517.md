## Options

1. Lymphoma of bone
2. Ewing's sarcoma
3. Chondrosarcoma
4. Metastatic adenocarcinoma
5. Osteogenic sarcoma

## Correct option

**1. Lymphoma of bone**

## Answer explanation

Lymphoma is a destructive lesion of bone, often with a large soft-tissue mass. Histology shows basophilic cells of varying size with little background stroma. Ewing's sarcoma histology also shows uniform sheets of small blue round cells, but typically occurs in childhood and shows CD99 positivity on immunohistochemistry. Chondrosarcoma demonstrates malignant cartilage, adenocarcinoma demonstrates a glandular arrangement of epithelial cells, and osteogenic sarcoma is a mixed lytic/blastic lesion demonstrating malignant cells producing immature osteoid.
