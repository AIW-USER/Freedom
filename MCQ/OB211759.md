## Question

Which of the following is a contraindication to an osteochondral autograft transplantation of the talus?

## Options

1. Bipolar kissing lesions
2. Displaced lesion
3. Prior failed drilling procedure
4. Prior failed marrow stimulation procedure
5. Size > 1cm

## Correct option

**1. Bipolar kissing lesions**

## Answer explanation

Of the choices listed, only the presence of bipolar kissing lesions is a contraindication to an osteochondral autograft transplantation of the talus. Other contraindications include diffuse ankle arthritis and advanced osteonecrosis of the talar dome.

Osteochondral lesions of the talus are the result of focal injuries to the talar dome with variable involvement of the subchondral bone and cartilage. They may occur secondary to a traumatic event or result from repetitive microtrauma. These lesions are often located in either the medial or lateral talar dome. An acute nondisplaced fragment with an incomplete fracture is treated conservatively with immobilization and non-weight bearing. Arthroscopy with removal of the loose fragment, debridement and marrow stimulation is indicated for chronic lesions, size < 1cm, and a displaced smaller fragment with minimal bone on the osteochondral fragment (poor healing potential). Retrograde drilling and/or bone grafting is indicated for a lesion size > 1cm with an intact cartilage cap. Osteochondral grafting (ie. osteochondral autograft transplantation, autologous chondrocyte implantation, bulk allograft) are indicated for lesions > 1cm, displaced lesions, or as a salvage for failed marrow stimulation or drilling; contraindications include diffuse ankle arthritis, bipolar kissing lesions, or advanced osteonecrosis of the talar done.

*Ahmad et al. prospectively evaluated and compared the long-term clinical and radiographic outcomes of using osteochondral autograft and allograft to manage either recurrent or large osteochondral lesions of the talar dome (OLT).* **They found** that the patients who received osteochondral autograft had measurable improvements in outcomes scores with a decline in pain. Moreover, no patients who received osteochondral autograft developed ankle degenerative changes or knee complications. **They concluded** that the use of distal femoral osteochondral autograft is a reasonable option for treating recurrent or large OLTs and has a greater healing rate than fresh talar allograft.

*Flynn et al. reviewed autologous osteochondral transplantation for the treatment of osteochondral lesions of the talus.* *They examined the functional and MRI outcomes following this procedure.* **They reported** improvement in functional outcome scores and noted that the Magnetic Resonance Observation of Cartilage Repair Tissue (MOCART) score was negatively correlated with lesion size. **They also reported** that functional outcomes were similar in patients irrespective of whether they had previous microfracture or concomitant procedures. **They concluded** that AOT was an effective treatment for large OCLs of the talus and MOCART scoring indicated good structural integrity of the graft.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 2-5: These are all indications for an osteochondral autograft transplantation of the talus
