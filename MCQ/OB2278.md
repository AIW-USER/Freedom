## Answer explanation

In patients with osteopetrosis, radiographs of the spine classically demonstrate a rugger jersey appearance.

Osteopetrosis is a defect of osteoclast resorption and has three forms (Illustration A) with varying life span and medical treatment types. The above patient has the autosomal dominant benign form, which is often found post-fracture or due to low back pain or joint arthritis. This form is associated with a high risk of cranial nerve palsy (optic, auditory, and facial). The infantile form is associated with severe anemia, hepatosplenomegaly due to extra-medullary hematopoiesis and classically mandibular osteomyelitis. Radiographs demonstrate increased bone density, and classically Erlenmeyer flask-shaped proximal humerus and distal femur (though not pathognomonic as this is also seen in Gaucher disease).

*Kaplan et al., report their results with treating the infantile form of osteopetrosis with a bone marrow transplant.* **They report** the successful 5-year survival of a young child following a bone marrow transplant (BMT) with preserved hearing. **They conclude** that BMT may provide a cure for a previously fatal condition.

*Faden et al. review the Erlenmeyer Flask Bone Deformity (EFD) in Skeletal Dysplasias.* **They report** that there are three types of EFD: type 1 (absent di-metaphyseal modeling with normal trabecular bone such as Pyle-type metaphyseal dysplasia), type 2 (absent di-metaphyseal modeling with abnormal trabecular bone appearance such as osteopetrosis), and type 3 (marrow expansion which leads to abnormal modeling such as Gaucher disease). **They conclude** that EFD is seen in many skeletal dysplasias, and narrowing the differential diagnosis relies on the radiographic appearance of the trabecular bone.

Figure A are the radiographs of an osteoporotic patient with cortical thinning and lack of trabeculae
Figure B is an L3-4 burst fracture
Figure C is a rugger-jersey spine characteristic of osteopetrosis and metabolic disorders such as renal osteodystrophy
Figure D demonstrates vertebra plana as seen in eosinophilic granuloma
Figure E demonstrates L5-S1 spondylolysis

Incorrect Answers
Figure A: this would be seen in a patient with osteoporosis
Figure B: L3-4 burst fractures are not more common in patients with osteopetrosis than in the general population
Figure D: vertebra plana is seen in eosinophilic granuloma
Figure E: spondylolysis is seen in patients with osteopetrosis; however the vertebral body density characteristics are not consistent with osteopetrosis
