## Question

An 18-year-old male with a known articular defect in his right knee presents to your clinic for a preoperative discussion. Figure A is his current MRI. Which of the following would be an indication to perform osteochondral allograft transplantation over MACI alone for this patient?

![[q218853-a-ocd.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Patient age
2. Bipolar lesion
3. Location of lesion
4. Size larger than 2 cm^2
5. Subchondral bone loss > 1cm

## Correct option

**5. Subchondral bone loss > 1cm**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a large osteochondral defect of the medial femoral condyle with subchondral bone involvement. Osteochondral allograft (OCA) transplantation would be indicated over MACI alone in the presence of >1cm bone loss.

Osteochondritis Dessicans is characterized by separation of articular cartilage and subchondral bone which can result in degenerative joint disease long-term. If nonoperative treatment fails, surgical treatment is indicated based on size, location, and osseous involvement. Smaller cartilage defects (<2 cm^2) are typically treated with chondroplasty, microfracture, or osteochondral autograft transfers (OATs). Large cartilage defects >2 cm^2 are treated with OCA transplant or MACI. OCA transplantation has the added benefit of restoring bone stock and is therefore indicated with subchondral bone loss, as well as in the setting of revision procedures. MACI procedures are generally indicated when there is minimal subchondral bone loss and should be supplemented with bone graft if there is greater than >1cm bone loss.

*Sherman et al provided a broad and comprehensive review of osteochondral allograft (OCA) transplantation.* *They cited the main indications include large chondral or osteochondral defects and salvage of previously failed cartilage repair.* Challenges in using OCA include storage and size matching, availability, chondrocyte viability, and an immunologic graft response.

*Perkins et al review the evaluation and management of failed OCDs.* Long leg alignment films are critical during the preop evaluation and the malalignment should be corrected prior to or simultaneous with the cartilage restoration procedure. **They note** that there are good short and medium-term outcomes with OCA transplantation, but long-term outcomes are still needed.

Figure A is a T2 coronal view demonstrating a contained unstable OCD lesion of the medial femoral condyle with a high-intensity signal surrounding the lesion (rim sign) as well as cystic changes deep to the OCD lesion indicating subchondral bony involvement.

Incorrect Answers
1: Patient age is not a contraindication to the use of an OCA or MACI procedure.
2: Bipolar lesions generally result in poorer outcomes compared to unipolar lesions regardless of the procedure.
3: Both procedures can be performed for femoral condyle lesions.
4: Larger defects (>2 cm^2) would be an indication for either procedure.
