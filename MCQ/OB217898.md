## Question

A 22-year-old male collegiate football player sustains a left ankle injury during a game. He notices immediate pain and an inability to bear weight on the affected extremity. Radiographs of the left ankle are depicted in Figure A. When surgically treating this injury, which of the following techniques has the lowest rate of malreduction?

![[q217898-a-syndesmosis.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Clamp reduction placed at the distal tips of the medial and lateral malleoli
2. Suture-button fixation
3. Single syndesmotic screw
4. Two syndesmotic screws
5. Reduction with the ankle in complete dorsiflexion

## Correct option

**2. Suture-button fixation**

## Answer explanation

The use of a suture-button construct has a lower rate of malreduction than the other described methods.

Syndesmotic injuries require reduction and fixation to ensure good long-term outcomes. Malreduction of the syndesmosis has been reported as a cause for persistent ankle pain and decreased function following ankle fractures. Several studies have reported the superiority of suture-button fixation for achieving and maintaining syndesmotic reduction over screw-only constructs. Reduction of the syndesmosis should be done with the ankle at neutral given how the anterior aspect of the talus is wider than the posterior aspect. Therefore, reduction with the ankle in complete dorsiflexion would cause the syndesmosis to remain widened. The proper placement of clamps, if utilized, is paramount for accurate reduction. The correct placement has been described as the middle of the medial tibial ridge and the lateral fibular ridge approximately 1-2 cm proximal to the mortise.

*Naqvi et al. compared suture button fixation and syndesmotic screws for the treatment of syndesmotic injuries in 46 patients.* **They reported** that suture-button fixation provided better reduction and faster return to weight bearing than syndesmotic screw fixation with no difference in functional outcome scores. **They concluded** suture button fixation provides a more accurate syndesmotic reduction which can independently affect clinical outcomes.

*Thornes et al. prospectively studied 32 patients that were treated with suture-button or screw fixation for syndesmotic injuries.* **They reported** significantly better American Orthopedic Foot and Ankle Society (AOFAS) scores, faster return to work, and no need for secondary surgery in patients with suture-button fixation. **They concluded** suture button fixation provides improved outcomes and faster rehabilitation than syndesmotic screw fixation.

*Zhang et al. systematically reviewed suture-button versus syndesmotic screw fixation for syndesmosis injuries.* **They reported** greater AOFAS, lower implant removal, lower implant failure, lower syndesmotic malreduction, and greater cost savings with suture-button fixation. **They concluded** based on the body of literature that suture-button fixation appears to have superior outcomes for the treatment of syndesmotic injuries.

*Andersen et al. performed a randomized controlled trial comparing suture-button versus syndesmotic screw fixation in 45 patients treated for syndesmotic diastasis.* **They reported** that suture-button fixation resulted in better functional outcomes, less pain during walking and rest, and higher maintenance of syndesmotic reduction than syndesmotic screw fixation. **They concluded** that suture-button fixation may be better than syndesmotic screw fixation for syndesmotic injuries.

Figure A is an AP radiograph of the left ankle with a syndesmotic injury.

Incorrect answers
Answer 1: Placement of the clamp at the distal tips of the malleoli would result in a malreduction of the syndesmosis.
Answers 3 and 4: Syndesmotic screw fixation has a higher malreduction rate than suture-button fixation.
Answer 5: Reduction of the syndesmosis with the ankle in full dorsiflexion will resulting the syndesmosis remaining widened.
