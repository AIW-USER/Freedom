## Options

1. Bilateral hip open reduction and spica casting
2. Bilateral varus derotational osteotomies and pelvic osteotomies
3. Bilateral proximal tibia osteotomies
4. Bilateral extra-articular ACL reconstruction
5. Radiographs of the cervical spine

## Correct option

**5. Radiographs of the cervical spine**

## Answer explanation

Bilateral knee dislocations are a classic sign of Larsen's syndrome. Prior to addressing dislocations surgically the cervical spine should be imaged given the high incidence of cervical kyphosis, cervical instability, and potential for iatrogenic spinal cord injury with endotracheal tube placement.

Larsen's syndrome is characterized by multiple joint dislocations and abnormal facial features. It has been described as an autosomal dominant or autosomal recessive inheritance pattern. Dislocations are often bilateral and can include hips, knees, and elbows with other findings including equinovarus feet and extra ossification of carpal bones. Facial features include depressed nasal bridge, prominent forehead, and telecanthus. In the spine, patients may have scoliosis and cervical kyphosis. Cervical kyphosis and instability are of particular concern as missed diagnosis can lead to iatrogenic spinal cord injury. Radiographs and/or MRI should be obtained prior to the patient undergoing manipulation of the cervical spine. Early recognition and operative treatment to stabilize the spine should take precedence over other orthopedic procedures to avoid potential neurologic morbidity and mortality.

*Johnston et al. reviewed 9 cases of Larsen's syndrome, 4 of which were treated operatively for cervical kyphosis.* **They found** that patients treated with solid posterior fusion alone were able to gradually grow out of their kyphosis. **They conclude** that early recognition and treatment of cervical kyphosis in Larsen's syndrome should be a priority and can lessen the potential neurologic morbidity and mortality.

*Latta et al. presented a case report of a patient diagnosed with Larsen's syndrome.* *They were able to document the various findings in a patient with Larsen's syndrome such as bilateral elbow, hip, and knee dislocations, flat thumbs, short palms, double ossification center of the calcaneus, small facial bones, and abnormal spine curvatures.* **They concluded** that the nature of the various findings stem from a generalized mesenchymal disorder.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1 and 2: Attempts at relocation of the hips should be avoided until proper evaluation and treatment of the cervical spine have been performed.
Answers 3 and 4: Attempts at relocation of the knees should be avoided until proper evaluation and treatment of the cervical spine have been performed.
