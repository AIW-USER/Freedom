## Question

A 2-year-old male is brought to the emergency room after a witnessed fall on to his right arm. On exam the patient has tenderness to palpation over his wrist. His radiographs can be seen in Figures A and B. Which of the following is true regarding this injury?

## Options

1. Annual outpatient monitoring for physeal arrest is warranted
2. Close outpatient follow-up is needed to ensure maintained alignment
3. No cast or clinical follow-up is needed
4. Rigid casting lowers the chance of re-fracture compared to removable wrist bracing
5. Rigid casting provides superior pain relief

## Correct option

**3. No cast or clinical follow-up is needed**

## Answer explanation

Distal radius buckle fractures can be effectively managed with a removable wrist splint without outpatient follow-up.

Distal radius fractures are the most common forearm fracture in children. A buckle or torus fracture is a unicortical non-displaced fracture. These fractures have a very low risk of refracture. Treatment options include either a soft removable wrist splint or a rigid cast. The benefits of a removable wrist splint include the ability to get the arm wet, avoiding the use of a cast saw, and avoidance of a follow-up appointment for cast removal. Similar pain relief and re-fracture rates can be seen with either treatment option.

*Kuba et al. prospectively collected data on 42 consecutive patients with a distal forearm buckle fracture treated with a removable brace an no additional clinical follow-up.* **They found** that 100% of children returned to full normal function and all would choose to have the same treatment again. Pain control was equivalent between the two groups. **They concluded** that utilizing a removable wrist brace with no clinical follow-up results in excellent patient and parent satisfaction.

*Witney-Lagen et al. reported their outcomes of 232 children treated for buckle fractures of the distal radius.* *Soft cast was the treatment for 117 and rigid cast was used for 102 children.* There were no refractures in either group. Parents in both groups preferred treatment in a soft cast as this allowed them to get the cast wet, avoided the plaster saw, and avoiding time off of work for a cast removal.

Figures A and B are an AP and lateral of the distal forearm in a skeletally immature patient demonstrating a buckle fracture.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: There is no risk for physeal arrest with a metaphyseal buckle fracture.
Answer 2: No follow-up is required for distal radial buckle fractures.
Answer 4: There is no difference in re-fracture rate between soft and rigid casting.
Answer 5: Soft and rigid casting have similar pain relief.
