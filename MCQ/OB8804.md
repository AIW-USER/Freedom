## Question

Which of the following concussion assessment tools is paired with its correct description?

## Options

1. Balance Error Scoring System (BESS): Assesses integrity of the entire balance system by testing the vestibular, visual and somatosensory systems, which are responsible for postural stability and maintenance of balance.
2. Immediate Postconcussion Assessment and Cognitive Testing (ImPACT): A computerized test that calculates composite scores for verbal and visual memory, processing speed, reaction time, impulse control and concussive symptoms.
3. King-Devick (K-D) Test: Evaluates orientation (ex. What month is it?), immediate and delay memory (ex. Repeating 5 words over 3 trials and 1 trial at conclusion of assessment), and concentration (ex. Repeat string of 5 numbers backwards).
4. Sensory Organization Test (SOT): Tests balance and postural stability by having the athlete perform 3 stances – feet together, one foot, tandem stance – for 10 seconds each.
5. Standard Assessment of Concussion (SAC): Allows examination of saccadic eye movements, language and concentration by having the athlete rapidly read numbers off a card from left to right for 3 successive tests.

## Correct option

**2. Immediate Postconcussion Assessment and Cognitive Testing (ImPACT): A computerized test that calculates composite scores for verbal and visual memory, processing speed, reaction time, impulse control and concussive symptoms.**

## Answer explanation

The Immediate Postconcussion Assessment and Cognitive Testing (ImPACT) is a computerized test that calculates composite scores for verbal and visual memory, processing speed, reaction time, impulse control and concussive symptoms.

Concussions have become increasingly recognized over the past decade. Proper recognition and management of concussion is crucial to avoid dire consequences in both the short-term (i.e. second hit syndrome) and long-term (i.e. chronic traumatic encephalopathy). The current standard of care mandates that an athlete diagnosed with a concussion be removed from play immediately and subject to a full neuropsychiatric assessment. Same day return to play is prohibited.

*Okonkwo et al reviewed the sideline assessment tools available for concussion evaluation.* The presence of a trained concussion specialist on the sidelines is critical to early assessment and effective management of athletes immediately after concussion. There are a variety of sideline assessment tools, some of which have been transformed into applications for smart phones. Many of these assessment tools still need to be validated in long term studies.

*Schatz et al performed a prospective case series of 95 collegiate varsity athletes, who did not sustain concussions, to determine the long-term test-retest reliability of baseline cognitive performance scores using ImPACT.* Intraclass coefficient estimates for visual memory, processing speed and reaction time remained stable over the 2-year time period, while verbal memory and symptom scale score were more variable. Only a small percentage of participants’ scores showed significant change on the composite scores (0-6%) or symptom scale (5-10%). This suggests stretching the time between baseline assessments from 1 to 2 years may have little effect on the clinical management of concussions.
