## Question

A 50 years old male with spastic quadriplegia, dysdidokokinesia has presented to you. Good prognostic parameter for the patient would be [FNB 2023]

## Options

1. PADI > 13 mm
2. ADI > 5 mm
3. Basilar invagination < 0.5 mm
4. Sub axial subluxation < 3.5 mm

## Correct option

**1. PADI > 13 mm**

## Answer explanation

Ans: A
EXPLANATION: -
First understand,
Anterior atlantodental interval (AADI) is the distance between C1 anterior arch and C2 of the dens. The normal value of AADI in adults should be less than 3mm. The larger AADI, the greater risk of spinal cord compression. Some authors consider that AADI > 5 mm is a sign of clinically significant Atlanto axial instability, and AADI > 8 mm is an indication for surgical treatment. It should be noted that these values apply to adults – the normal value of AADI in children is up to 5 mm.
Posterior atlantodental interval (PADI) is the distance between the dens and C1 posterior arch. The distance is the width of the spinal canal on the C1–C2 level. As the spinal cord at this level is 10 mm in diameter, 1 mm is needed for the dura and 1 mm for CSF, PADI should not be smaller than 14 mm. Literature findings indicate that the reduction of this dimension below 14 mm negatively impacts the outcomes of surgical treatment.
AADI and PADI values are used to evaluate anterior and posterior subluxation and can be measured on lateral radiographs.

For given question: Basilar invagination (BI), atlanto-axial subluxation and subaxial subluxation are part of same disease process. Basilar invagination has occurred here leading to cerebellum impingement causing spastic quadriplegia and dysdidokokinesia.
• Now here, pre operative neurological status of patient would carry highest prognostic power, over and above any radiological parameter.
• Amongst radiological parameters, the only well defined parameter carrying prognostic significance is PADI and hence choice A is best pick (Ref: Mańczak M, Gasik R. Cervical spine instability in the course of rheumatoid arthritis - imaging methods. Reumatologia. 2017;55(4):201-207. doi: 10.5114/reum.2017.69782. Epub 2017 Aug 31. PMID: 29056776; PMCID: PMC5647537).
• Regarding Basilar invagination being answer; according to Chamberlain line, BI is dens projecting > 6mm. So, it’s not fitting in option C and hence ruled out as the answer.
