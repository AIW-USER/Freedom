## Options

1. Osteosarcoma
2. Osteomyelitis
3. Ewing sarcoma
4. Eosinophilic granuloma
5. Lymphoma

## Correct option

**2. Osteomyelitis**

## Answer explanation

The patient has subacute osteomyelitis of the distal tibia metaphysis.

Pediatric osteomyelitis without any direct trauma is most likely to have occurred via a hematogenous route. Bacterial seeding is thought to occur due to transient bacteremia from otitis media, pharyngitis, or dental care. The risk of osteomyelitis is higher in the setting of trauma and at the metaphyseal region due to the sharp turns capillaries make near the physes. Identifying the causative organism can be difficult with blood cultures being positive in only 30-50% of patients and bone cultures positive 50-85% of the time. Treatment typically involves 4-6 weeks of IV or oral antibiotics.

*Ceroni et al. retrospectively review 14 children with subacute epiphyseal osteomyelitis.* Half of the children had an elevated ESR, most had a normal CRP and none of them had an elevated WBC. All children had negative blood cultures and only 1/14 had identifiable bacteria by standard bone culture techniques. PCR allowed for identification of bacteria in an additional 8 children. Kingella Kingae was the most common organism in younger children (<4 years) and staph aureus was more common in older children.

*Street et al. reviewed the record of 813 cases of pediatric acute hematogenous osteomyelitis.* **They found** the most common organism was staphylococcus aureus and the incidence of MRSA was only 2%. The femur was the most common location (31%) followed by the tibia (22%). Most patients were treated non-operatively with 3-6 weeks of IV or oral antibiotics, but 44% did require an average or 2 surgical operations.

Figures A and B demonstrate a permeative, radiolucent lesion at the medial tibial metaphysis. Figure C is a coronal STIR MRI image demonstrating extensive bone marrow edema in the distal tibial metaphysis with medial sequestration. Figure D demonstrates mixed inflammatory cells combined with marrow elements. Figure E again demonstrates non-malignant appearing inflammatory cells adjacent to bone.
Illustration A demonstrates the Langerhan's cells present in eosinophilic granuloma consisting of mononuclear histiocyte-like cells with an indented, coffee-bean shaped nucleus and eosinophilic cytoplasm

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Osteosarcoma typically has osseous matrix formation on plain radiographs and malignant spindle cells mixed with osteoid on histologic examination.
Answer 3: Ewing sarcoma would have monotonous small round blue cells on histology.
Answer 4: Eosinophilic granuloma can be excluded by the absence of Langerhan's cells on histology.
Answer 5: Lymphoma can be excluded on histology, which would demonstrate a small round blue cell infiltrate.
