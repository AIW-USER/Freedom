## Question

An orthopaedic trauma team is performing an open reduction and internal fixation of a tibial plateau. The resident is sixteen weeks pregnant and is attempting to reduce her intraoperative radiation exposure during the case. Where will the highest scatter radiation be in the operating room when a lateral radiograph is taken?

## Options

1. Sitting at the circulator's desk
2. Standing 3 feet away from the collector side
3. Standing 6 feet away from the collector side
4. Standing 3 feet away from the emitter side
5. Standing 6 feet away from the emitter side

## Correct option

**4. Standing 3 feet away from the emitter side**

## Answer explanation

Scatter radiation increases with proximity to the c-arm and is higher on the side of the emitter (Answer 4).

When a lateral X-ray is taken in the operating room, scatter radiation exposure varies depending on whether one is positioned on the side of the X-ray emitter or the image receptor (collector). Generally, scatter radiation is higher on the side of the X-ray emitter. This is because the patient's body absorbs and attenuates the radiation, reducing the intensity of scatter that reaches the collector side. Studies have shown that the exposure on the emitter side can be significantly higher, sometimes by a factor of two or more, depending on patient size, beam energy, and positioning. Similarly, in cases such as a supracondylar humerus fracture where the C-arm is inverted, the surgeon is exposed to greater radiation (by 5-8%) because the emitter is closer to the body. Healthcare personnel should prioritize standing further from the emitter when possible.

*Igrek et al. compared radiation exposure during pediatric supracondylar humeral fracture surgery using two different C-arm positioning techniques.* **The findings indicated** that radiation exposure to the surgeon's neck was significantly higher when the emitter was positioned above the operating room table for the entire procedure. **The authors concluded** that optimizing C-arm positioning can reduce radiation exposure for both patients and surgical staff, emphasizing the importance of technique selection in clinical practice.

*Dorman et al. evaluated radiation exposure to staff from scatter radiation in the orthopedic operating room.* **The findings revealed** that radiation doses were highest near the X-ray emitter and decreased with distance from the source. Staff positioned on the same side as the X-ray tube, especially close to the patient and the surgical field received the highest exposure. In contrast, those on the image receptor (collector) side experienced significantly lower doses. **The authors concluded** that adhering to radiation safety protocols, including maintaining distance, using lead aprons and shields, and optimizing positioning on the collector side, is crucial to minimizing staff exposure to scatter radiation.

Incorrect answers:
Answers 1-3, 5: These locations will not have as much scatter radiation compared to standing close to the emitter.
