## Question

A 55-year-old women presents with worsening pain at the hallux metatarsophalangeal joint. The pain is predominantly during mid-range motion. The physical examination reveals a large, tender, medial and dorsal eminence. Range of motion of the joint was limited to 10 degrees of dorsiflexion and 15 degrees of plantar flexion. Intra-operative views of the joint are seen in Figure A. What would be the best option for surgical treatment?

![[q5621-a-mtp.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Cheilectomy
2. Distal bunionectomy with cheilectomy
3. Resection arthroplasty
4. Interpositional arthroplasty
5. Arthrodesis

## Correct option

**5. Arthrodesis**

## Answer explanation

This patient presents with advanced arthritis at the hallux metatarsophalangeal joint. The best surgical treatment option would be joint arthrodesis.

Hallux rigidus is degenerative arthritis of the 1st MTP joint. This disease is staged using the Coughlin and Shurnas system. Stage 4 disease is characterized by significant stiffness and pain at mid-range of motion as well as the extremes of motion. Radiographs typically show severe dorsal osteophytes and >50% joint space narrowing. Currently, the best option for surgical treatment for this condition is arthrodesis.

*McNeil et al. reviewed the operative treatment of hallux rigidus.* **They concluded** that there is fair evidence (grade B) in support of arthrodesis for treatment of hallux rigidus. There is poor evidence (grade C) in support of cheilectomy, osteotomy, implant arthroplasty, resection arthroplasty, and interpositional arthroplasty for treatment of hallux rigidus.

*O'Malley et al. looked at a series of eighty-one patients with advanced hallux rigidus that were treated with cheilectomy and phalangeal osteotomy.* **They showed** that the average American Orthopaedic Foot & Ankle Society scores improved significantly (p < 0.05) from 67.2 points preoperatively to 88.7 points with this procedure postoperatively. **They concluded** that this procedurere could be an alternative to arthrodesis.

Figure A shows advanced hallux rigidus with degeneration of >50% of the articular cartilage, substantial osteophyte formation, and joint space narrowing. Illustration A shows the Coughlin and Shurnas system for classifying hallux rigidus.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1,2,3,4: The highest level of evidence supports the treatment of advanced hallux rigidus with arthrodesis. There is poor evidence in support of the other options.
