## Question

A 16-year-old high school soccer player presents with a 1-year history of intermittent ankle pain and recurrent sprains during athletics. On physical examination his foot appears flat, with mild valgus angulation of the hindfoot. A radiograph taken in the office today is seen in Figure A. The patient can perform a single leg heel rise, but this does not improve the deformity. What initial management is most appropriate?

![[q5662-a-45-degree-oblique.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. No intervention needed, patient may play soccer regardless of symptoms
2. Trial of activity modification and possible immobilization
3. Surgical resection alone
4. Surgical resection with soft tissue interposition
5. Triple arthrodesis

## Correct option

**2. Trial of activity modification and possible immobilization**

## Answer explanation

Initial treatment of a symptomatic calcaneo-navicular coalition should be a trial of nonoperative intervention including activity modification and possible immobilization.

Tarsal coalitions (most commonly calcaneonavicular or talo-calcaneal) are a common cause of symptomatic flat feet in adolescents. These coalitions should initially be treated with non-operative interventions such as activity modification, immobilization, and weight-bearing restrictions. If these interventions fail then surgical intervention can be considered.

*Carli et al. provide a review of the treatment of pediatric tarsal coalitions.* The first line treatment should be non-operative, options include: activity modification, anti-inflammatory medications, shoe inserts or orthotics to elevate the medial arch, and/or immobilization in a below-the-knee walking cast. Operative intervention should only be considered after non-operative treatments are exhausted.

*Khoshbin et al. present a comparison of outcomes of talocalcaneal (TC) and calcaneonavicular (CN) coalition resections.* No difference was noted between the CN and TC groups with respect to outcome scores. Furthermore, no association was noted between the size of TC coalition or hindfoot valgus angle with respect to outcome scores.

*Mubarak et al. present a retrospective review of calcaneonavicular coalitions following resection and fat graft interposition.* *At one year follow-up 87% of patients had returned to sport, while 5% had re-ossification that required repeat excision.* **They report** their rates are favorable compared to other published reports using extensor digitorum brevis (EDB) interposition (rather than fat graft).

Figure A is an oblique radiograph of the foot showing a calcaneonavicular coalition. This finding is commonly referred to as the "ant-eater" sign since it resembles the long snout of an ant-eater.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: With a 1-year history of symptoms including limitation of activity some intervention is needed, although not yet surgical.
Answer 3: Surgical intervention should be done only after non-operative interventions are attempted and have failed. Also, resection alone may be associated with a higher rate of recurrence of the coalition.
Answer 4: Surgical intervention should be done only after non-operative interventions are attempted and have failed. If surgical intervention is being considered, this would be the correct intervention.
Answer 5: Triple arthrodesis is a common treatment for a rigid acquired flatfoot deformity in adults, but is not the treatment of choice in an adolescent with a tarsal coalition.
