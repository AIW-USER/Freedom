## Question

## Options

1. FHL tendon tear
2. FHB tendon tear
3. Medial sesamoid fracture
4. Rupture of the plantar plate
5. Metatarsal head fracture

## Correct option

**4. Rupture of the plantar plate**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a turf toe injury as represented by rupture of the first MTP joint plantar plate / intersesamoid ligament on MRI in combination with his clinical exam.

Turf toe is an injury to the plantar plate and sesamoid complex. It is associated with artificial turf and more flexible cleats and is seen most commonly in football players. It is recommended that an MRI is performed for hyperdorsiflexion injuries in the presence of radiographic abnormalities or if there is clinical evidence of partial or complete tear of the plantar plate. MRI can define the extent of injury to the plantar plate complex in addition to identifying injury to the FHL, FHB, sesamoids, and articular surface of the MTP. The injuries are graded I-III and most can be managed nonoperatively with rest, NSAIDs, taping, and a stiffer shoe. Grade III injuries in an elite athlete or patients who fail nonoperative treatment may require surgical intervention.

*Anderson et al. reviewed the management of sports-related foot and ankle injuries, including turf toe injuries.* Grade 1 and 2 injuries were managed through taping the MTP and rehabilitation, with Grade 2 frequently requiring time off from sport due to pain. Orthotics limiting extension were also used for Grade 2 injuries. Grade 3 injuries were also able to be treated non surgically through splinting in plantarflexion, however, they cite high return to sport rate and performance outcomes in elite-level athletes undergoing direct repair as evidence for more aggressive treatment.

*Kadakia et al. review injuries to the first MTP joint and sesamoid complex.* *They review current evidence on turf toe injuries and note that there is fair evidence to support the nonoperative treatment of grade I and II injuries and insufficient evidence to support operative treatment for grade III injuries.*

Figure A is a normal radiograph of the right foot. In severe turf toe injuries, you may be able to see proximal migration of the sesamoids. Figures B and C are sagittal and axial MRIs of the foot which reveal rupture of the first MTP joint plantar plate / intersesamoid ligament. Osteochondral injury and bony contusion of the plantar medial aspect of the first metatarsal head directly adjacent to the distal aspect of the medial sesamoid. Avulsion of the distal attachment of the dorsal capsule of the first metatarsophalangeal joint allowing slight plantar subluxation of the proximal phalanx.

Incorrect answers:
Answers 1-3 and 5: These injuries are not demonstrated on the radiographs/MRI in this patient.
