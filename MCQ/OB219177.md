## Question

A 72-year-old female presents to the emergency department as a polytrauma patient. Her orthopedic injuries include superficial abrasions and a left tibial plateau fracture. She undergoes temporizing external fixator placement with post-operative images shown in Figures A and B. After multiple general surgery trauma procedures, she is stabilized and recovers well. She has a prolonged inpatient stay and her soft tissues appear amenable for definitive fixation two weeks later. Which of the following operative intervals would be ideal for performing open reduction internal fixation of the labeled fracture fragment?

## Options

1. Between the medial gastrocnemius and pes anserine tendons
2. Between the tibial tuberosity and the tibialis anterior
3. Between the lateral gastrocnemius and biceps femoris
4. Between the medial and lateral gastrocnemius
5. Between the patella and vastus medialis oblique

## Correct option

**1. Between the medial gastrocnemius and pes anserine tendons**

## Answer explanation

The figures display a bicondylar tibial plateau fracture with a labeled posteromedial fragment. A safe operative approach that provides direct visualization of the posteromedial tibial plateau is the posteromedial approach (i.e. Lobenhoffer approach), which involves utilizing the interval between the medial gastrocnemius and pes anserine tendons (Answer 1).

Bicondylar tibial plateau fractures most often require dual plating to achieve adequate fixation and restoration of the articular surface. The posteromedial approach is intended for treating posteromedial tibial plateau fractures. In this approach, the fascia of the medial gastrocnemius is incised and the muscle belly is retracted laterally. The pes anserine tendons are identified and retracted medially (may also be released and later repaired). The underlying popliteus is then elevated in a subperiosteal fashion, allowing for direct visualization of the posteromedial tibial plateau (Illustration A). Importantly, the popliteus can be carefully released further lateral in a subperiosteal fashion to allow for safe dissection beneath the popliteal neurovascular structures. This allows further visualization to fix direct posterior or some posterolateral fractures. The most common fixation construct involves utilizing a “T” small fragment plate as a buttress to provide primary stability of the articular surface (Illustration B). A separate anterolateral approach can then be performed to restore the remainder of the articular surface with adjunctive rim plates as needed (Illustration C).

Hake and Goulet provided a brief technique guide of the posteromedial approach. The authors highlight that the knee can be placed in extension and valgus to assist in the reduction of posterior and posteromedial fracture fragments. The authors conclude that utilization of this approach maximizes the dissection typically needed for isolated posteromedial and/or bicondylar fractures of the tibial plateau.

*Weil and colleagues performed a retrospective case series and technique guide on their use of the Lobenhoffer posteromedial approach via supine positioning.* **They state** that their supine positioning allows for posteromedial fracture fixation first followed by anterolateral plating without the need to flip the patient and re-drape the sterile field. Of their 27 patients included, they achieved an anatomic reduction in 75% of patients without any wound complications or flexion contractures. **The authors conclude** that the posteromedial approach is effective and reproducible for reducing and stabilizing posteromedial tibial plateau shear fractures.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: The interval between the tibial tuberosity and tibialis anterior describes the anterolateral approach. It involves a curvilinear incision centered over Gerdy’s tubercle in which subperiosteal elevation of the anterior compartment and sub-meniscal arthrotomy allows direct visualization of the anterolateral joint surface (Illustration D).
Answer 3: The interval between the lateral gastrocnemius and biceps femoris describes the posterolateral approach. The common peroneal nerve lies within the intermuscular plane and must be protected throughout the case. The underlying popliteus muscle can be elevated in a subperiosteal fashion off the tibia, allowing for direct visualization of the posterolateral tibial plateau (Illustration E).
Answer 4: The interval between the medial and lateral gastrocnemius muscles describes the direct posterior approach. This involves direct visualization and manipulation of the popliteal artery, small saphenous vein, and common peroneal nerve (Illustration F). Given the risks of damaging these neurovascular structures, the Lobenhoffer approach would be more appropriate and most often utilized for direct posterior fracture fragments.
Answer 5: The medial parapatellar approach can treat anterior-medial fractures, which are highly uncommon and most often captured with anterolateral plating. The main purported advantage of this approach is that the same incision can be used again if performing a total knee arthroplasty in the future.
