## Question

A 20-year-old volleyball player comes to clinic complaining of 1 year of low back pain that is worse with serving. Her neurologic exam is normal and her radiologic exam shows a bilateral pars defect and spondylolisthesis at L5-S1. Which of the following relationships represents the pelvic parameter that most correlates with the severity her existing disease?

## Options

1. Pelvic incidence + pelvic tilt
2. Pelvic tilt + sacral slope
3. Pelvic incidence - pelvic tilt
4. Pelvic incidence - sacral slope
5. Pelvic tilt - sacral slope

## Correct option

**2. Pelvic tilt + sacral slope**

## Answer explanation

The pelvic parameter that most correlates with the severity of spondylolisthesis is pelvic incidence. Pelvic incidence is pelvic tilt + sacral slope.

There are several pelvic parameters used to assess the morphology of the pelvis and define the pelvis' relationship to the spine: pelvic incidence, pelvic tilt, sacral slope, and lumbar lordosis. Pelvic incidence is the only parameter that is based on the morphology of the pelvis and does not change with positioning. It can be quantified directly by measuring it from a standing lateral radiograph of the spine. It is also the summation of the sacral slope and the pelvic tilt, and thus can be indirectly obtained. Increased pelvic incidence has been shown to be associated with more severe isthmic spondylolisthesis.

*Labelle et al. reviewed relevant radiologic measurements for the evaluation of spino-pelvic balance and its relation to developmental spondylolisthesis.* The key points that the paper reiterated were that pelvic incidence determines the position of the sacral endplate, lumbar lordosis is the spine's way to adapt to pelvic morphology, and developmental spondylolisthesis is associated with abnormal pelvic morphology and spino-pelvic balance.

*Hanson et al. performed a radiologic analysis on 40 patients with matched controls in order to assess the correlation between pelvic incidence and isthmic spondylolisthesis.* **The investigators found** that there were age-related changes in pelvic incidence, and that there was a significant correlation between pelvic incidence and severity of isthmic spondylolisthesis. **They concluded** that pelvic incidence is likely a predictive factor for both high and low grade spondylolisthesis and should be considered in assessment for risk progression.

Answers 1,3-5: None of these pelvic parameter relationships will provide the pelvic incidence.
