## Question

Which is an absolute contraindication of UKA [FNB 2023]

## Options

1. Flexion contracture of 10 degree
2. Fixed Valgus deformity of 10 degree
3. Fixed Varus deformity of 10 degree
4. ACL deficiency

## Correct option

**2. Fixed Valgus deformity of 10 degree**

## Answer explanation

Ans: B

EXPLANATION: -

Indications for UKA
- isolated medial or lateral compartment OA
- osteonecrosis of the knee
- patients over 60 years old
- patients’ weight under 82 kg
- angular deformity < 15o and passively correctable to neutral
- flexion contracture < 5o
- ROM > 90o

Contraindications for UKA
- Inflammatory arthritis
- ACL deficiency (relative CI as ACL can simultaneously be reconstructed)
- Fixed varus deformity > 10 degrees
- Fixed valgus deformity > 5 degrees
- Restricted motion (Arc of motion < 90°)
- Flexion contracture of > 10°
- Previous meniscectomy in other compartment
- Tricompartmental arthritis (diffuse or global pain)
- Younger high activity patients and heavy laborers
- Grade IV patellofemoral chondrosis (anterior knee pain)

Complications of UKA

Traditionally, UKA has been an effective treatment for single- compartment disease of the knee; a list of its complications simply outlines the causes of early and late failure.

Modes of failure for all patients and for patients who failed within five years after index surgery
