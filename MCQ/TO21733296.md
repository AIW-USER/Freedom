Chapter: Regional Orthopedics MCQs — Peroneal Tendon Tear And Instability

## Question

Most commonly done procedure for chronic peroneal dislocation is

## Options

1. SPR repair and groove deepening procedure
2. Distal fibular osteotomy and rerouting the CFL
3. Rerouting of CFL
4. d. Complete fibular osteotomy with posterior displacement (Bony block)

## Correct option

**1. SPR repair and groove deepening procedure**

## Answer explanation

Ans: A
EXPLANATION: -
Conservative treatment can be attempted after acute dislocation and primarily consists of repositioning the tendons back into the retromalleolar groove, followed by immobilization in a lower leg cast for 6 weeks while the foot is slightly plantar flexed and inverted.If conservative treatment fails and re-dislocation occurs, surgical therapy is required. Some authors advocate surgical repair even in acute PTD cases because of the high failure rate of conservative treatment, especially in athletes.
In patients with chronic dislocation, conservative treatment failure rates have been reported in over 50 %. In these cases, or when the tendons are irreducibly dislocated in the acute setting, surgical management is recommended. More than 20 surgical techniques have been described in the literature, all with the primary purpose of (re-)stabilizing the tendons in the retromalleolar groove by attempting to restore the natural anatomy. In general, five categories can be identified: 1. SPR repair or replacement 2. Deepening of the retromalleolar groove 3. Boneblock/osteotomy procedures 4. Enhancement of the SPR by rerouting of other soft-tissue structures 5. Tendon graft procedures
SPR repair and groove deepening procedures: SPR repair and groove deepening have both demonstrated excellent outcomes and satisfaction rates. SPR repair aims to restore the structural physical restraint that keeps the peroneal tendons from dislocating, whereas groove deepening provides a more stable, anatomically configured bed for harboring the tendons over the course of their distal excursion.
Several soft-tissue and bone techniques have been described, with the mainstay of treatment being direct repair of the SPR.
Direct repair
The chronically injured SPR is often more redundant than in the acute injury, allowing for a “pants-over-vest” technique. The anterior retinacular flap is double breasted over the posterior flap. High rates of success have been reported, but attention must be given to the shape of the retromalleolar groove and a groove deepening procedure considered.
Retromalleolar groove deepening
Both direct and indirect techniques have been described. The direct method involves elevating the cartilaginous floor of the retromalleolar groove, exposing the cancellous bone beneath. This is then curetted or burred to a point of sufficient concavity to allow tendon excursion. The floor of the groove is then tamped down, the tendons replaced and the SPR repaired. The indirect method involves passing a 3.5 mm drill through the fibular tip parallel to the retromalleolar groove, into the subchondral bone. This preserves the floor of the groove, which is tamped down into the drilled tunnel, thus deepening it.
Bony procedures:
Osteotomy techniques involve partial or complete fibular osteotomy with posterior displacement or rotation of the most lateral fragment to serve as a physical barrier to prevent anterior subluxation of the tendons. Kelly initially described a partial-thickness distal fibular osteotomy, rotated posteriorly to deepen the fibular groove. Osteotomy techniques have the advantage of stabilizing the peroneal tendons more firmly than the soft tissue techniques described below. However, the osteotomy procedure has several shortcomings such as fractures, delayed/nonunion of bony fragments, postoperative tendon irritation, and tendon adherence to the underlying bone.

Rerouting of other soft tissues
The most used rerouting procedure involves the use of the calcaneofibular ligament (CFL). Platzgummer’s method involves separation of the CFL near the attachment of the fibula and re-suturing it through the peroneus tendon underneath it. Other rerouting techniques which have been described include-
-Cutting of peroneus tendons and resuturing them after passing them beneath CFL
- Osteotomy of the distal fibula, including the CFL, passing the peroneus tendon underneath CFL and reattaching the distal fibula

Due to heightened complication rates—including nonunion, tendon adherence to the underlying bone, sural nerve neuropathy, tendon irritation and ankle instability in bone-block procedures and rerouting of other soft-tissue, these procedures should be considered a salvage or revision.
Tendon graft reconstruction
The use of the Achilles, peroneus brevis and plantaris tendon have all been deszcribed in order to re-inforce the SPR using a transosseous fibular tunnel with varying success.
