## Question

A 68-year-old woman sustains the injury seen in Figure 189 following a fall. Careful neurologic and vascular examinations reveal no associated injury. What is the most common complication of surgical fixation with a locked plate and screw construct through a deltopectoral approach?

![[q7777-a-189.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Infection
2. Axillary nerve palsy
3. Peri-implant fracture
4. Deltoid insertion detachment
5. Screw penetration of the articular surface

## Correct option

**5. Screw penetration of the articular surface**

## Answer explanation

Several studies have documented screw penetration through the articular surface as the leading complication with locked plate and screw fixation for displaced proximal humerus fractures. Axillary nerve palsy is rare but can be seen with a deltoid split approach. Whereas some loss of range of shoulder motion is expected following this injury, infection rates about the shoulder are in the 1% to 5% range. While a portion of the deltoid insertion is commonly taken down, complete deltoid insertion detachment has not been described.
