Chapter: Orthopedics Oncology MCQs — Hemangioma

## Question

Mottled appearance on MRI is seen in (FNB Pattern Paper 2018)

## Options

1. Vertebral hemangioma
2. Bone marrow edema syndrome
3. CML
4. Bony metastasis

## Correct option

**1. Vertebral hemangioma**

## Answer explanation

High T1 signal of vertebral hemangiomas is related to the presence of fatty stroma, whereas the vascular component of the lesion is mostly responsible for the high T2 signal. On T2 fat-suppressed/STIR images, typical hemangiomas retain some high signal due to their vascular component. This is in contradistinction to foci of fatty marrow, which exhibit marked signal dropout on such sequences . Other than high T1 signal intensity, the more specific additional feature of hemangioma at MRI is the presence of linear and/or punctate signal-void areas representing the reinforced trabeculae. Although specific, this sign is not as frequently seen as its CT equivalent, the polka dot sign. The signal-void areas, when present against a background of high T1 and high T2 signal, give vertebral hemangiomas a characteristic “mottled” appearance, highly suggestive of vertebral hemangioma.

Remember: Vertebral hemangioma gives

Jailhouse appearance on plain X ray

Corduroy appearance on sagittal CT scan

Polka dot appearance on axial CT scan

Mottled appearance on MRI

Answer: 1
