## Question

A Division 1 college hockey player presents to your office with complaints of right hand pain. Radiographs reveals that he has a spiral oblique metacarpal fracture of the ring finger that is minimally displaced. Which of the following is true of open reduction and internal fixation (ORIF) of this fracture compared with non-operative management?

## Options

1. No difference in time to union or return to play
2. No difference in time to union but faster return to play
3. Faster time to union but slower return to play
4. Faster time to union but no difference in return to play
5. Faster time to union and return to play

## Correct option

**2. No difference in time to union but faster return to play**

## Answer explanation

This athlete has a metacarpal fracture. ORIF will not result in faster bone union, but it will result in a faster return to play.

Treatment of metacarpal fractures is based on which metacarpal is involved, the location of the fracture, and the rotation/angulation of the injury. Most metacarpal fractures can be successfully managed nonoperatively. However, in competitive athletes, their level of play, timing of their season, their position, and rules for playing with a splint use may encourage ORIF in this cohort. ORIF for fractures that are minimally displaced and stable will not result in faster bony union, increased grip strength, or range of motion at final follow-up; however, ORIF is likely to result in faster return to play.

*Cotterell et al. reviewed metacarpal and phalangeal fractures that occur during athletic events.* **They reported** that although an acceptable and stable reduction remains a key principle in this patient group, early mobilization and return to play may be higher priorities than in other patient populations. **They concluded** that implant designs and techniques are allowing for an accelerated postoperative course with an early range of motion and return to play.

*Wong et al. reviewed the various treatment modalities for metacarpal fracture.* **They reported** that all modalities are acceptable strategies for treating metacarpal fractures and the ultimate goal should be to maximize hand function with minimal morbidity. **They concluded** that although the current literature supports a wide array of treatment strategies, high-level evidence to guide fracture management remains lacking.

*Wahl et al. reviewed metacarpal and phalangeal fractures in athletes.* **They reported** unstable or intra-articular fractures may require operative fixation. **They concluded** that ORIF may afford the most stability while allowing for early rehabilitation and faster return to play.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1, 3-5: ORIF for fractures that are minimally displaced and stable will not result in faster bony union, increased grip strength or range of motion at final follow-up but is likely to result in faster return to play.
