Chapter: Orthopedics Oncology MCQs — Osteoblastoma

## Question

Which is true regarding treatment of osteoblastoma

## Options

1. Magnetic resonance-guided focused ultrasound surgery (MRgFUS) penetrates deeper than RFA
2. MRgFUS is less invasive than RFA
3. Disappearance of bone edema after ablation is better with MRgFUS
4. RFA has shown less damage to chondral tissue in juxtaarticular lesion

## Correct option

**2. MRgFUS is less invasive than RFA**

## Answer explanation

Answer: 2
Both Radiofrequency ablation (RFA) and MRgFUS are minimally invasive techniques for treatment of benign bone lesions like osteoblastoma, chondroblastoma and osteoid osteoma.
RFA is a widely tested technique applied in various fields other than musculoskeletal intervention. Usually, a CT-guided procedure is performed, which requires a single-day hospitalization. The procedure is performed employing a fine needle (about 17 G or little more), which causes minimal injury to the healthy tissues encountered when reaching the lesion.
MR-guided ultrasound surgery (MRgFUS), is a novel technique which, compared with RFA, does not require the use of needles to reach the lesion. The ablation is performed using energy delivered through an ultrasound beam, which, if the procedure is correctly performed, causes insignificant damage to the healthy tissues.
Magnetic resonance-guided focused ultrasound surgery (MRgFUS) has recently emerged as another innovative alternative treatment, providing tumor ablation through a needleless and ionizing radiation-free modality. In addition, this technique has the ability to guarantee a very precise and controlled increase in temperature, delivering small amounts of energy that can accurately destroy only the lesion, avoiding healthy surrounding tissues.
In keeping with literature, both techniques are safe and effective. In cases of deep lesions treated with MRgFUS: sometimes the beam cannot effectively reach the lesion itself. With an RFA needle, instead, the lesion can be easily reached and ablated.
The most common sign found around the lesions is bone oedema, which mainly indicates a reaction of the healthy bone to the growth of the lesion. Bone oedema is the cause of pain and its post-treatment disappearance indicates that the lesion is no longer active. Both techniques are equally effective in treating bone edema around the lesion.
Another typical sign observed after procedures of thermal ablation (RFA, MRgFUS and cryoablation) is the “halo” or “ring”. The halo or ring sign generally appears as a round central area characterized by low signal intensity surrounded by a peripheral isohyperintense rim; the CT pattern is very similar and appears as a central hypodense zone surrounded by a rim with higher density. The central part is probably the result of ablation (i.e. the necrotic area, rich in adipose tissue) and the surrounding zone, included in the peripheral rim, is the result of a sub-lethal thermal injury.
 The hyperintense rim indicates the presence of a reactive interface between the ablated region and the healthy tissue. The extension of this zone must be carefully evaluated to assess the presence of thermal injury of the tissues close to the lesion treated. This is of particular importance when lesions are treated close to the joints or other sensitive structures. With MRgFUS, it is possible to control in real time the temperature reached in the region of interest, in order to avoid or minimize complications. For this purpose, the thermal map obtained through PRF sequences in the MRgFUS system is helpful and should be closely monitored during treatment.
