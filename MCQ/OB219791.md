## Question

A 32-year-old male is undergoing a posterolateral approach for open reduction and internal fixation of a distal humerus fracture. During the approach, the radial nerve is seen and protected. At about 14 cm proximal to the lateral epicondyle, a branch of the radial nerve is identified. This branch courses superficial to the lateral intermuscular septum, continuing distally to the forearm. What would be the neurological deficits associated with damage to this radial nerve branch?

## Options

1. Sensory deficits to the lateral forearm
2. Sensory deficits to the posterior arm and forearm
3. Motor deficits displaying weak wrist extension
4. Sensory deficits to the medial forearm
5. Sensory deficits to the dorsoradial distal forearm and hand

## Correct option

**2. Sensory deficits to the posterior arm and forearm**

## Answer explanation

The posterior antebrachial cutaneous nerve (PABCN) is a sensory branch of the radial nerve supplying the posterior aspect of the distal arm and forearm (answer choice 2).

Identifying the radial nerve is a challenging and critical aspect during posterior-based approaches to the humerus. When performing a posterolateral approach, a reliable method is to identify the posterior antebrachial cutaneous nerve (PABCN) and trace this proximally to the radial nerve. The PABCN will reliably be found superficial to the lateral intermuscular septum on the lateral aspect of the distal arm. The PABCN will branch off the radial nerve at the lateral aspect of the humerus proximal to the radial nerve, diving deep to the lateral intermuscular septum. The posterior aspect of the forearm and distal arm receive sensory innervation from the PABCN, while the medial and lateral antebrachial cutaneous nerves (MABCN & LABCN) supply the remaining forearm. Unlike the PABCN, the MABCN and LABCN are not branches of the radial nerve.

*Scolaro et al. performed a retrospective review on 40 patients after undergoing posterolateral plate fixation of extra-articular distal humerus fractures.* In their surgical technique, the authors use a triceps-reflecting posterolateral approach to identify the PABCN on the posterior aspect of the lateral intermuscular septum and trace this proximally to identify the radial nerve. In postoperative follow-up (average 88 weeks), no new nerve deficits were found. 8 of the 17 preoperative radial nerve palsies had resolved completely at final follow-up. **The authors report** good success with this approach and fixation and recommend identifying the PABCN as a reliable tool to locate the radial nerve.

*Starr et al. performed a cadaveric study of the anatomy of the posterior antebrachial cutaneous nerve (PABCN).* *28 cadavers were dissected and the branching pattern, course, and measurements relative to bony landmarks were recorded.* **The authors report** the PABCN branching from the radial nerve at a mean of 14.2 cm proximal to the lateral epicondyle. The PABCN consistently coursed with the radial nerve, becoming more superficial as the radial nerve dives into the lateral intermuscular septum. Multiple variations of terminal PABCN branching exist with all containing at least 1 longitudinal branch and most cadavers containing an epicondylar branch. **The authors conclude** that knowledge of the PABCN course and location is critical for surgeons operating in the posterolateral arm and forearm.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer choice 1: This describes the lateral antebrachial cutaneous nerve (LABCN), a terminal sensory branch of the musculocutaneous nerve after exiting between the biceps and brachialis muscles.
Answer choice 3: This describes damage to the posterior interosseous nerve (PIN), the deep branch of the radial nerve. The PIN branches from the radial nerve 1-3 cm distal to the lateral epicondyle, splits the supinator and supplies the extensor muscles in the forearm (except for the mobile wad). The PIN terminates as a sensory branch to the dorsal wrist joint in the floor of the fourth dorsal compartment.
Answer choice 4: This describes the medial antebrachial cutaneous nerve (MABCN), a direct branch from the medial cord of the brachial plexus providing sensation to the medial forearm.
Answer choice 5: This describes the superficial branch of the radial nerve (SBRN), a terminal sensory branch of the radial nerve. After branching near the elbow, the SBRN continues in the forearm deep to the brachioradialis before emerging through antebrachial fascia 6-9 cm proximal to the radial styloid.
