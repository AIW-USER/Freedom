## Question

A 76-year-old female with severe cervical myelopathy presents with the radiographs depicted in Figure A. She undergoes the procedure depicted in Figure B. ## Options

1. Application of topical tranexamic acid (TXA) anterior to ACDF construct
2. Application of local corticosteroid anterior to ACDF construct
3. Utilization of a posterior-only construct
4. Preoperative utilization of intravenous TXA
5. Utilization of a longitudinal incision during the anterior approach

## Correct option

**2. Application of local corticosteroid anterior to ACDF construct**

## Answer explanation

This patient underwent a multilevel combined anterior and posterior cervical decompression and fixation for multilevel cervical stenosis in a rigid kyphotic spine and is experiencing postoperative dysphagia, which is not uncommon. Multiple studies have demonstrated the application of local corticosteroids in the retropharyngeal region prior to wound closure decreases the rate of postoperative dysphagia.

Postoperative dysphagia is a common complication after anterior cervical spine surgery, with rates of 30-50% cited in the literature. While many studies have reported on the use of different techniques (i.e. modification of dissection planes, alteration of endotracheal cuff pressures, modification in plate design) to reduce the rate of dysphagia following anterior cervical spine surgery, there have been varied success rates reported. The administration of corticosteroids both systemically and locally has been shown to reduce the rate of postoperative dysphagia.

*Cancienne et al. investigated the rates of postoperative dysphagia, length of stay, infection, and wound complications after ACDF in patients who received local intraoperative steroids and those who did not.* **They reported** a significantly lower incidence of dysphagia in patients who received local steroids in the long segment ACDF group compared to the control group who did not. However, this trend was not observed in the short segment ACDF cohorts. **They concluded** that local intraoperative steroid application is associated with a significantly reduced rate of postoperative dysphagia after long ACDF without any increase in postoperative infection or wound complications.

*Koreckij et al. investigated the effect of retropharyngeal steroids on postoperative dysphagia scores and clinical outcomes following multilevel ACDF.* **They reported** significant improvement in dysphagia outcome measures including the Bazaz-Yoo Dysphagia Scoring System and Eat Assessment Tool. **They concluded** that the use of retropharyngeal steroids resulted in decreased rates of dysphagia following multilevel ACDF, without increased perioperative complications.

*Jeyamohan et al. performed a randomized controlled trial on the effect of steroid use in ACDF.* **They reported** that the severity of dysphagia was significantly lower in the steroid group compared to the placebo group up to 1 month postoperatively. **They concluded** that steroid administered perioperatively significantly improved swallowing function and airway edema postoperatively, without affecting pain, functional outcomes, or long-term swallowing status.

Figure A is a cervical spine radiograph depicting a kyphotic deformity in the setting of cervical spondylosis. Figure B is a cervical spine radiograph depicting combined anterior and posterior decompression and fixation from C3-C7.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Application of topical TXA has not been demonstrated to significantly decrease the rate of postoperative dysphagia.
Answer 3: While avoidance of anterior cervical spine surgery would decrease the rate of postoperative dysphagia, an anterior procedure was important in her case to correct her kyphosis and remove the anterior compressive lesions in the setting of myelopathy.
Answer 4: Preoperative use of intravenous TXA has not been demonstrated to significantly decrease the rate of postoperative dysphagia.
Answer 5: There is a lack of literature to support the use of longitudinal incision to decrease postoperative dysphagia.
