## Question

A 45-year-old patient with a history of diabetes presents with increasing shoulder stiffness over the course of the past several months. She has tried non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, but they have not alleviated her pain. On examination she has global pain with passive range of motion, forward elevation of 100 degrees, external rotation to neutral, and internal rotation to her iliac crest. Radiographs of the shoulder are normal. An MR arthrogram is most likely to show which of the following?

## Options

1. Massive retracted rotator cuff tear
2. Proliferative synovial process with hypertrophied synovium and extensive papillary projections
3. Decreased intra-capsular volume
4. Subscapularis tear with long head of the biceps subluxation
5. Fluid extravasation down the humerus

## Correct option

**3. Decreased intra-capsular volume**

## Answer explanation

Based on the clinical information and imaging, the patient has a diagnosis of adhesive capsulitis. On an MR arthrogram, a decrease of the intra-capsular volume would be expected.

Adhesive capsulitis or frozen shoulder is associated with fibroblastic proliferation of capsular tissue leading to contracture of the soft tissues. It is associated with several endocrinopathies (including diabetes) and largely involves the rotator interval. The first line of treatment is non-operative, in the form of physical therapy, intra-articular steroids, and anti-inflammatory medications. Operative treatment may be indicated when non-operative measures have failed.

*Sheridan et al. present a review on etiology and management of adhesive capsulitis.* **They indicate** that the majority of patients presenting with adhesive capsulitis are females. The mainstay of initial treatment is benign neglect, physical therapy and intra-articular steroids.

*Marx et al. present a study looking at the effect of intra-articular steroid injections on the early stages of idiopathic adhesive capsulitis.* **They found** that use of steroid injections for patients with early frozen shoulder allowed recovery of motion at a median time of 3 months.

*Carette et al. studied the effect of physical therapy vs steroid injections in the treatment of adhesive capsulitis.* *Patients were randomized to one of four treatment groups (steroid + physical therapy, steroid alone, saline injection + physical therapy, saline injection alone).* Pain/disability scores improved more significantly in the groups that received steroid injections. **They recommend** use of steroid injections coupled with home exercise programs to improve pain/function in the setting of adhesive capsulitis.

Illustration A shows a coronal sequence of a T2 weighted MRI of the shoulder. Findings of edema in the axillary recess, as seen here, are consistent with a diagnosis of adhesive capsulitis.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The clinical picture is not consistent with a massive rotator cuff tear
Answer 2: The clinical history is not suggestive of PVNS
Answer 4: The clinical picture is not consistent with a tear of the subscapularis with biceps subluxation
Answer 5: Adhesive capsulitis would not lead to tracking of fluid down the humerus. This is more consistent with an avulsion of the inferior glenohumeral ligaments (HAGL).
