## Question

A 35-year-old woman presents to your fracture clinic with a distal radius fracture after reporting she "fell down the stairs." She appears withdrawn, avoids eye contact, and seems hesitant to provide details about the fall. During your examination, you notice several old, healed bruises of varying ages on her upper arms, inconsistent with a typical fall mechanism. She quickly pulls down her sleeves and changes the subject when you ask about them. She is accompanied by her partner, who frequently interrupts when she attempts to speak and seems overly attentive to her responses. Given these observations, what is the MOST appropriate initial step for the orthopaedic surgeon to take in this patient's management?

## Options

1. Directly confront the patient's partner about your suspicions of abuse
2. Document the patient's reported mechanism of injury, treat the fracture, and discharge her with routine follow-up
3. Offer to speak with the patient alone, in a private and non-judgmental manner, to screen for intimate partner violence and provide resources
4. Refer the patient for a mandatory psychological evaluation due to inconsistent history
5. Report your suspicions directly to law enforcement, citing potential domestic abuse

## Correct option

**3. Offer to speak with the patient alone, in a private and non-judgmental manner, to screen for intimate partner violence and provide resources**

## Answer explanation

Given the subtle indicators and prevalence of intimate partner violence (IPV) in orthopaedic settings, directly and privately screening the patient for abuse is the most appropriate initial step to ensure her safety and offer support.

IPV is a significant public health problem with a high prevalence, including within orthopaedic patient populations, where musculoskeletal injuries are common outcomes of abuse. Orthopaedic surgeons are often uniquely positioned to identify potential victims, as these patients may present with injuries that are inconsistent with their stated mechanism, or they may exhibit behavioral cues indicative of abuse. The most ethical and effective approach is to create a safe space by offering to speak with the patient alone, ensuring privacy, and asking direct, non-judgmental questions about her safety. If IPV is identified, providing access to appropriate resources (e.g., domestic violence hotlines, social work services) is crucial. This approach respects patient autonomy while prioritizing their safety and well-being.

*Gotlib Conn et al. evaluated orthopaedic surgery residents' perceived barriers to screening female patients for intimate partner violence.* **They noted** common barriers included lack of training, time constraints, discomfort, and uncertainty about how to proceed if abuse was disclosed. **They concluded** that addressing these barriers through education and systemic support is crucial for improving IPV screening rates in orthopaedic settings.

*Heikel et al. discussed IPV as a significant practice development issue for orthopaedic nurses.* *They reviewed the prevalence of IPV, the types of injuries sustained, and the crucial role of orthopaedic nurses in identifying and supporting victims.* **They concluded** that comprehensive training and clear protocols are essential for orthopaedic healthcare professionals to effectively address IPV.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: This is dangerous for the patient and inappropriate, potentially escalating the violence and jeopardizing the patient's safety.
Answer 2: This misses critical cues for potential abuse and fails to address the patient's safety and underlying needs.
Answer 4: This is an inappropriate initial step, as it stigmatizes the patient and does not directly address the immediate safety concern or provide an opportunity for disclosure of abuse.
Answer 5: While mandatory reporting laws vary, directly involving law enforcement as the initial step without assessing the patient's wishes or safety can put the victim at greater risk and erode trust.
