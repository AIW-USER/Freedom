## Options

## Correct option

**1. osteopetrosis**

## Answer explanation

Paget's disease is caused by abnormal osteoclasts. Osteopetrosis is an inherited metabolic bone disease caused by defective osteoclastic resorption of immature bone.

Pagetic bone lesions have increased osteoclastic bone resorption, marrow fibrosis, increased bone vascularity, and increased disorganized bone formation, producing a mosaic (woven and lamellar bone) appearance on histology. Osteoclasts are increased in number and size in Pagetic bone lesions and contain more nuclei (up to 100) than normal. They contain characteristic nuclear inclusion bodies that resemble virus particles. These inclusion bodies are also noted in osteopetrosis and pycnodysostosis (another osteoclast disease).

Two forms of osteopetrosis exist. The autosomal recessive (AR) form is lethal and causes death in infancy. The autosomal dominant (AD) form is more commonly seen and is compatible with life. Known mutations include carbonic anhydrase II, vacuolar proton pump and chloride channel 7. Manifestations include cranial nerve palsies, osteomyelitis, long bone fractures, coxa vara, and anemia (from marrow encroachment). Radiographs show increased radiodensity of bone with characteristic features: "erlenmeyer flask" proximal humerus and distal femur, "rugger jersey spine" with very dense bone, loss of medullary canal "bone within a bone" appearance and block femoral metaphysis.

*Ralston et al. reviewed the pathogenesis and management of Paget's disease.* The UK has the highest prevalence of Paget's disease worldwide. The most important gene mutations include Sequestosome 1 (SQSTM1) (autosomal dominant with high penetrance), a scaffold protein in the NF?B signalling pathway. Patients with SQSTM1 mutations have severe Paget’s disease. Environmental factors such as paramyxovirus infection is thought to contribute. Other potential triggers include dietary calcium deficiency and repetitive mechanical loading.

Kaplan and Singer review the pathophysiology, diagnosis, and management of Paget's disease. They state that measurement of serum alkaline phosphatase is the most useful biochemical test and correlates with the extent of skeletal involvement and disease activity. The earliest lesions are osteolytic and occur in the skull (osteoporsis circumscripta) and long bones. Indications for drug therapy (calcitonin and bisphosphonates) include bone pain, preparation for orthopedic surgery (reducing disease activity to 50% pretreatment ALP levels reduces blood loss), medical complications (hearing loss, spinal stenosis, high output heart failure), and prevention of fracture/deformity.

Figures A through C show bowing of the tibia from Paget's disease of bone.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Multiple myeloma is a plasma cell malignancy characterized by the accumulation of monoclonal plasma cells in the bone marrow and the presence of osteolytic lesions. Further, it is thought that in MM, osteocyte-derived sclerostin contributes to the inhibition of the bone forming activity of osteoblasts.
Answer 3: Osteoporosis is the decrease in bone mass secondary to uncoupling of osteoclast-osteoblast activity. It is not inherited.
Answer 4: Osteogenesis imperfecta is a disease of the osteoblast. The osteoblast produces an abnormal matrix that does not respond to mechanical loads.
Answer 5: Osteochondromas (exostosis) arise because of abnormal chondrocytes.
