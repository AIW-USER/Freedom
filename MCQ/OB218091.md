## Question

Figure A is a radiograph of a 42-year-old male who is presenting for shoulder pain. The decision is made to proceed with nonoperative management for shoulder impingement and rotator cuff tendonitis initially. Which location most accurately describes this patient's underlying condition?

![[q218091-a-9.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Meta-acromion
2. Pre-acromion
3. Junction of meso- and meta-acromion
4. Junction of meso- and pre-acromion
5. Junction of meta- and pre-acromion

## Correct option

**3. Junction of meso- and meta-acromion**

## Answer explanation

This patient's os acromiale involves the junction of meso- and meta-acromion.

An os acromiale is an unfused secondary ossification center of the acromion that can be associated with shoulder impingement and rotator cuff tendonitis. The most common location is the junction of meso- and meta-acromion. A meso-acromion is associated with rotator cuff tendonitis and full thickness tears (in 50%). Diagnosis is made with axillary lateral radiographs of the shoulder. Treatment is observation in the majority of cases with operative management reserved for patients who have persistent shoulder impingement or rotator cuff disease that have failed nonoperative management.

*Abboud et al. reviewed 19 consecutive patients treated surgically for meso-os acromiale and subacromial pathology.* **They reported** that the outcome of surgical management of symptomatic meso-os acromiale with concomitant rotator cuff pathology was satisfactory in 4 of 8 patients in their study group. **They concluded** that 80% achieved satisfactory results (compared with only 22% in their workers' compensation group).

*Harris et al. reviewed the optimal surgical treatment for symptomatic os acromiale that has failed nonoperative management.* **They reported** that rotator cuff repair was the most common concurrent surgical technique (performed in 59% of the surgically treated shoulders), followed by distal clavicle excision (25%). **They concluded** that all surgical techniques resulted in improvement in clinical outcomes and that surgical management of symptomatic os acromiale that has failed nonoperative measures may predictably lead to improved outcomes.

*Sammarco et al. attempted to better define the frequency and anatomy of os acromiale in the general population.* **They reported** that os acromiale was more frequent in blacks than in whites (13.2 compared with 5.8 percent; p < 0.001) and in men than in women (8.5 compared with 4.9 percent; p = 0.09). **They concluded** that a fused os acromiale, which has not been described previously, might be mistaken for a free ossicle in the clinical setting.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Meta-acromion (base) involves the origin of posterior portion of deltoid.
Answer 2: Pre-acromion (tip) involves the origin of anterior deltoid fibers and coracoacromial ligament.
Answers 4&5: This patient's os acromiale is located at the junction of meso- and meta-acromion.
