## Options

1. The deformity will progressively worsen and likely requires surgical correction
2. Was likely due to an insufficient period of immobilization
3. The deformity has likely reached its maximum but will remain permanent
4. The deformity usually reaches its maximum around 18 months and slowly resolves over a period of 3 years
5. The affected leg will be shorter than the contralateral leg

## Correct option

**4. The deformity usually reaches its maximum around 18 months and slowly resolves over a period of 3 years**

## Answer explanation

The radiographs in Figures A and B demonstrate a left-sided fracture of the proximal tibial metaphysis, which has been associated with the late development of post-traumatic genu valgum, or a Cozen phenomenon. This phenomenon typically reaches its maximum deformity around 18 months and resolves by 3 years post-injury with nonoperative treatment.

Proximal tibia fractures in the pediatric population are usually seen in patients aged 3 to 6, and are treated nonoperatively with closed reduction and casting except in very rare cases. They have a tendency to develop a late-onset self-limiting genu valgum deformity, known as a Cozen phenomenon. Due to the lack of large studies examining the specific prevalence, various studies have quoted a rate between 14-100%. Post-traumatic genu valgum usually worsens until an average of 18 months following the injury, then gradually self-resolves over the course of ~3 years. Most cases of Cozen phenomena may be treated with observation alone, although rare cases of severe deformity greater than 15-20 degrees near skeletal maturity may be candidates for surgical intervention in the form of wedge osteotomies or guided growth.

*Papamerkouriou et al. reviewed the long-term outcomes of 33 patients treated for proximal tibial fractures.* *They evaluated whether age at the time of injury influenced the development of a Cozen phenomenon, the occurrence of ipsilateral extremity overgrowth, and the presence of residual deformity at long-term follow-up.* *Nearly half (15/33) of the patients developed a Cozen phenomenon within 2 years, and 24 developed ipsilateral limb overgrowth, which occurred in all 15 patients who developed late-onset genu valgum deformities and in 9 who did not.* **They concluded** that age was not associated with either the development of a Cozen phenomenon or limb overgrowth.

*Yang et al. presented a large case series examining the prevalence, epidemiology, and characteristics associated with Cozen phenomenon.* *They describe a low percentage of post-traumatic genu valgum (14.3%), however, 70% of patients who did develop late-onset genu valgum remodeled to their preinjury state without persistent deformity.* **They conclude** that Cozen phenomenon is uncommon in their studied population and that regular follow-up to screen for this deformity should be reserved only for those at high risk (initial valgus deformity >4 degrees with either an ipsilateral fibula fracture or medial metaphyseal gap).

*Burton et al. retrospectively reviewed the effectiveness of four weeks of long leg cast immobilization with varus mold at the time of initial injury in preventing late-onset Cozen phenomenon.* *They followed 14 patients for an average of 16.2 months (range 6.7 to 42.8 months), with 7 of them developing an ipsilateral valgus deformity of 5 degrees between 5 and 15 months post-injury.* **The authors conclude** that immobilizing these fractures in extension with a varus mold is an effective treatment for mitigating the severity of Cozen phenomenon.

Figures A and B depict a minimally displaced fracture of the left proximal tibial metaphysis. Figure C is a clinical image of a child with Cozen phenomenon of the left lower extremity.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Cozen deformity usually resolves over time and is not progressive. The vast majority of cases do not require surgical correction.
Answer 2: Duration of immobilization has not been associated with the development of a Cozen deformity. Cozen deformity reaches a maximum deformity around 18 months, well beyond the period of initial immobilization.
Answer 3: The average time for a Cozen deformity to reach its maximum degree of genu valgum is 18 months.
Answer 5: If there is a resulting limb length discrepancy, the affected leg is usually longer than the contralateral, unaffected leg.
