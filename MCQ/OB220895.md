## Question

A young patient is found to have a very rare and unique genetic mutation that makes his body unable to produce two key mediators in fracture healing, stromal cell-derived factor-1 and granulocyte colony-stimulating factor. What process in the body’s healing response to trauma is primarily affected with the absence of these two factors?

## Options

1. Mesenchymal stem cell migration to the fracture site
2. Neovascularization
3. Stimulation of osteoclasts
4. Down-regulation of the inflammatory response
5. Transformation of mesenchymal stem cells into osteoblasts

## Correct option

**1. Mesenchymal stem cell migration to the fracture site**

## Answer explanation

This question essentially is asking what the function of stromal cell-derived factor-1 (SDF-1) and granulocyte colony-stimulating factor (G-CSF) is in the inflammatory response to trauma. Both of these factors have been shown to stimulate mesenchymal stem cell (MSC) migration to the site of trauma (Answer 1).

The body’s response to trauma is complex. Regarding bone healing, there are three phases of healing: the inflammatory phase, the repair phase, and the remodeling phase. The inflammatory stage is characterized by hematoma formation and the influx of hematopoietic and inflammatory cells, which begin to release various growth factors and cytokines that initiate the healing process. A key component of this stage is the migration of MSCs to the site of injury. Two factors involved in this process include SDF-1 and G-CSF, and dysregulation or absence of these factors has been proposed to be a mechanism for progression to non-union. After the inflammatory phase, the repair stage involves the formation of callus. Soft callus is gradually converted to hard callus. In the remodeling phase, the new woven bone is slowly remodeled via interplay between osteoblasts and osteoclasts. Ongoing research continues to explore the body’s healing mechanisms to injury, and may provide clinical therapies in the future to help stimulate effective healing after trauma.

*Cook et al. conducted a study analyzing the degree of G-CSF and hematopoietic stem cell mobilization after trauma.* *They collected blood samples from trauma patients and compared these to healthy controls.* **They found** a 50x increase in plasma levels of G-CSF in the trauma patients. Similarly, **they noted** hematopoietic cell mobilization was sustained for roughly 10 days after trauma. The authors speculated that G-CSF was the driving factor in this stem cell mobilization. **They also noted** a correlation with persistently elevated levels of G-CSF and post-traumatic anemia, infection, and need for transfusions, suggesting G-CSF may be a valuable marker to track as a patient recovers from trauma.

*Huber-Lang et al. authored a review article of MSCs and their role as it relates to healing after trauma.* *They discuss the various molecular and chemical factors that drive MSC differentiation and specifically mention that SDF-1 and G-CSF help mobilize MSCs to the site of injury.* *The authors speculate that in the future, we may be able to influence the mobilization and differentiation of stem cells on both a systemic and local level to help influence healing after injury.*

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2-5: SDF-1 and G-CSF do not specifically affect these processes. Neovascularization is thought to result from VEGF, RANK-L stimulates osteoclasts, IL-10 is an immunosuppressive cytokine, and differentiation of mesenchymal stem cells is largely due to bone morphogenic proteins.
