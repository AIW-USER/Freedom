## Question

## Correct option

**3. Diagnosis is not made by macroscopic appearance**

## Answer explanation

ALVAL leads to solid pseudo tumor mass formation that can be seen macroscopically

The metal debris are cytotoxic to macrophages and hence lymphocyte predominant lesions (due to macrophage depletion). So macrophage depletion is what leads to lymphocyte predominance.

Metal-on-metal pseudotumor or aseptic lymphocyte-dominated vasculitis-associated lesions (ALVAL) are large focal solid or semi liquid masses around metal-on-metal THR bearing prostheses. These are sterile inflammatory lesions. ALVAL is a hypersensitivity reaction leading to formation of a pseudotumor around the prosthesis that eventually results in subsequent failures. The mechanism involved is thought to be a type IV delayed hypersensitivity reaction. In ALVAL, it is hypothesized that metal ions are slowly released from the prosthetic bearing surfaces as a by-product of normal wear. The delayed-type hypersensitivity response is cell mediated and characterized histologically by the presence of lymphocytes, histiocytes, and, in some cases, multinucleated giant cells. Recruitment of inflammatory cells into affected tissue involves a release of chemotactic factors and cytokines. Tissue damage, which can be extensive, is a result of the combined effects of cytotoxic T cells and activated monocyte/macrophages.

It is hypothesized that metal ions are released from the prosthesis and form haptens with native proteins that elicit a type IV hypersensitivity response in the local soft tissues. Histopathologic descriptions of ALVAL are similar to those of failed arthroplasty in general, with the addition of a dense perivascular inflammatory infiltrate that is the hallmark of ALVAL. The inflammatory infiltrate, composed of lymphocytes and histiocytes, appeared centered on small vessels.

The clinical features of ALVAL are nonspecific, but patients complain of pain and may develop “pseudotumor.” MRI may show Periprosthetic cystic and solid masses. The clinical diagnosis can reliably be made only after histological examination. Metal ions, Chromium and cobalt in particular have been demonstrated in elevated quantities in the blood and urine of patients with loose prostheses.

Answer: 3
