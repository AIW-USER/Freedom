## Options

1. The diagnosis is giant cell tumor. Bone scan is necessary.
2. The diagnosis is giant cell tumor. Chest radiograph is necessary.
3. The diagnosis is giant cell tumor. No further staging study is necessary.
4. The diagnosis is giant cell-rich osteosarcoma. Chest radiograph is necessary.
5. The diagnosis is giant cell-rich osteosarcoma. CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis is necessary.

## Correct option

**2. The diagnosis is giant cell tumor. Chest radiograph is necessary.**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a large distal femur giant cell tumor (GCT) of bone. Staging workup should include a chest radiograph (or chest CT) to detect lung metastases which are usually benign.

GCT are tumors composed of mononuclear stromal cells and characteristic multinucleated giant cells
that exhibit osteoclastic activity. They are lytic lesions with well-defined, nonsclerotic margins that are eccentric, extend to the articular surface/subchondral bone, and occurs in patients with closed physes. Aggressive features include cortical expansion/ destruction with a soft-tissue component. Fluid levels may be seen in 14% and are consistent with secondary ABC formation. Lung metastases occurs in 1-6% because of hematogenous seeding, usually after treatment. They are histologically benign and appear similar to the primary bone tumor. Malignant transformation (<1%) to high grade sarcoma has a poor prognosis.

*Raskin et al. reviewed GCT.* The giant cells are characterized by multiple nuclei (>50), nuclei identical to stromal cells, and central nucleoli. The cell of origin is the fibroblastic-osteoblastic mononuclear cell (not the giant cell). Wide resection is an option for recalcitrant/recurrent/aggressive cases especially in expendable bones (clavicle, distal ulna, proximal to mid fibula).

Figures A and B show an eccentric metaphyseal-epiphyseal lytic lesion with narrow zone of transition. The differential diagnosis included GCT and telangiectatic osteosarcoma. Osteosarcoma will usually have a wider zone of transition, and mineralization in the soft tissue and bone. Figure C is a low power slide showing multiple characteristic giant cells with multiple nuclei that resemble surrounding stromal cells. Illustration A shows a chest CT image (part of the staging work-up) showing a benign lung metastasis. Illustration B is an intraoperative photograph showing complete destruction of the lateral margin of the lateral femoral condyle and the presence of a soft-tissue mass (arrows).

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Bone scan is not indicated.
Answer 3: Chest imaging is necessary to exclude pulmonary metastases.
Answers 4 and 5: The diagnosis is GCT rather than giant cell-rich osteosarcoma. There is no evidence of bone formation to suggest osteosarcoma.
