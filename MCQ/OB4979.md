## Correct option

**5. Figure E**

## Answer explanation

Pediatric supracondylar humerus fractures are typically treated with closed reduction and percutaneous pinning. A construct of 3 lateral pins, as shown in Figure E, provides the greatest angular and rotational stability while minimizing the risk of iatrogenic nerve injury.

Preferred constructs vary across institutions, but adequate evidence exists to support that 3-pin constructs are more stable than 2-pin constructs. If no comminution is present, and anatomic reduction is obtained then 2-pin constructs maybe adequate, particularly in type II fractures. As instability increases, particularly with medial comminution present, a 3-pin construct is recommended. Placement of a medial pin carries an increased risk of iatrogenic injury to the ulnar nerve.

*Bloom et al. present a biomechanical study testing different constructs and their stability.* *They tested each construct for angular (varus/valgus) and rotational stability.* In the comminuted group a 3-pin construct was needed for adequate stability. There was no significant difference in stability between 3 lateral pins and 2 lateral pins with a crossing medial pin.

*Babal et al. present a meta-analysis of nerve injuries related to supracondylar humerus fractures.* Iatrogenic injury to the ulnar nerve occurred as a weighted event in 3.4% of lateral only pinning, while the introduction of a medial pin increased this incidence to 4.1%. This was found to be statistically significantly different.

Figure A shows a single pin which is not a stable construct. Figure B and Figure C are 2-pin constructs, which are not as stable as 3-pin constructs. Figure D is a 3-pin construct with 2 lateral and one medial pin. While this is as stable as 3 lateral pins, it has a higher risk of iatrogenic nerve injury, and thus does NOT minimize the risk of complications.

Incorrect answers:

Answers 1, 2, 3, 4: Figures A-D do not show the optimal construct, which consists of 3 lateral pins.
