## Question

A 3-year-old male with Down syndrome presents with complaints of left hip pain and a recent decrease in his activity level. On examination, he has a positive Trendelenburg gait with no limb-length deformity. He has full symmetric motion of the hips bilaterally except for decreased abduction on the left side. A standing left AP hip radiograph reveals mild subluxation of the left hip without osseous changes. What is the most appropriate treatment?

## Options

1. Abduction brace
2. Capsulorrhaphy with pelvic and femoral osteotomies
3. Observation
4. Nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory medication
5. Shoe lift

## Correct option

**1. Abduction brace**

## Answer explanation

This Down syndrome patient is presenting with a left hip subluxation. Given his young age and mild subluxation without bony changes, an abduction brace would be most indicated.

Hip instability in Down syndrome is caused by ligamentous laxity and muscle hypotonia. It occurs in 5% of patients between the ages of 2-10 years and manifests as subluxation or dislocation. With time, degenerative arthritis of the hip will ensue. In a young patient without dislocation or osseous changes, conservative management with the use of an abduction brace is undertaken to prevent osseous changes from occurring.

*Greene et al. reported on three dislocated hips in two patients with Down syndrome that were treated conservatively using the principle of prolonged immobilization or bracing.* **They report** that these two patients (three hips) became stable and well-contained with this protocol. **They concluded** that nonoperative management of hip dislocation in Down syndrome can be successful and avoids the complications associated with operations recommended for these patients who have more advanced disease.

*Shaw et al. reviewed the clinical and roentgenographic examination of the hip in 114 patients with Down syndrome.* **They found** an increased external rotation of the hip with an acetabulum of a patient with Down syndrome being deep, more horizontally placed, and having an increased anteversion. **They concluded** that in this patient cohort, 7.9% have some hip abnormality, including dysplasia, dislocation, avascular necrosis, or slipped capital femoral epiphysis.

*Aprin et al. reported on 10 hips in patients with Down syndrome that were treated for dislocation.* *They compared patients with dislocatable hips and normal acetabula that were treated with capsular plication and/or proximal femoral osteotomy with those patients with dislocatable hips and dysplastic and insufficient acetabula that were treated with capsular plication, Salter osteotomy, and proximal femoral osteotomy.* **They concluded** that these procedures resulted in satisfactory results in most patients.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Capsulorrhaphy with pelvic and femoral osteotomies may be the treatment of choice in symptomatic older patients. Surgery in these patients is associated with a high rate of complications.
Answer 3 and 4: Observation and nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory medication will not help prevent the gradual progression of hip subluxation and associated degenerative changes.
Answer 5: Shoe lift is not indicated as this patient has symmetric limb lengths.
