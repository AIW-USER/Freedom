## Question

A 38-year-old male laborer presents with complaints of worsening left elbow pain. He reports that 14 years prior he sustained a distal humerus fracture that was treated with open reduction and internal fixation of the distal humerus that utilized an olecranon osteotomy following a motor vehicle accident. Radiographs now reveal advanced degenerative changes of the ulnohumeral joint with no intra-articular hardware. Which of the following would serve as a relative contraindication to performing an arthroscopic debridement and capsular release in this patient?

## Options

1. Mechanical symptoms from loose bodies
2. Presence of an elbow contracture
3. Presence of a bony block to motion
4. History of an olecranon osteotomy
5. Ulnar nerve transposition

## Correct option

**5. Ulnar nerve transposition**

## Answer explanation

The presence of a prior ulnar nerve transposition is a relative contraindication to performing an arthroscopic debridement and capsular release of the elbow.

Elbow arthritis is a degenerative joint disease of the elbow that can be broken into three main types: osteoarthritis, post-traumatic arthritis and inflammatory arthritis. Diagnosis can be made with plain radiographs of the elbow. Treatment can be nonoperative or operative depending on patient activity demands, severity of elbow pain and degree of elbow dysfunction. Indications for performing an arthroscopic debridement and capsular release include mechanical symptoms from loose bodies, stiffness related to capsular contracture, stiffness related to bony block to motion, and is preferred in patients with >90° of motion. Contraindications include a prior ulnar nerve transposition as the exact location of the ulnar nerve is unknown, which places it at higher risk of injury.

*Poonit et al. reviewed the literature regarding elbow OA and its treatment options and associated outcomes.* **They reported** that their review could not provide an insight on which surgical procedure is superior to the other due to the poor orthopedics literature. **They concluded** however, that open and arthroscopic debridement procedures seem to be safe and effective in the treatment of elbow OA.

*Pooley et al. reviews TEA use for elbow arthritis.* **They report** that in contrast to other total joint replacements, TEA use has declined since reaching a peak in the 1990s. **They concluded** that this decline is likely secondary to controversy about implant design, the relatively high reported complication rates associated with TEA, and the difficulties encountered in revision surgery.

*Raval et al.retrospectively reviewed the outcome of ulnohumeral arthroplasty for the management of problematic primary elbow OA.* **They reported** improvement in pain scores and in the flexion extension arc. No complications were reported. **They concluded** that ulnohumeral arthroplasty can yield good pain relief and moderate improvement in the range of movement in patients with primary degenerative OA of the elbow.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1-3: These are indications to performing an arthroscopic debridement and capsular release of the elbow
Answer 4: The presence of a prior olecranon osteotomy is not a contradiction to performing an arthroscopic debridement and capsular release of the elbow
