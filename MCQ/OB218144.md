## Question

A 45-year-old male develops progressive right hip pain with activity. He denies any clear inciting events and denies trauma. He has been trying to stretch more, as his hip feels very stiff, but it is not helping. Before this current incidence of pain, he had not had any pain in his hip. A review of his past medical history is unremarkable. You discuss with the patient that his current pathology could be treated with either hip arthroscopy or open debridement, with similar outcomes. What is the underlying process in this patient's hip?

## Options

1. Neoplasia
2. Metaplasia
3. Reactive arthritis
4. Osteoarthritis
5. Vascular insufficiency

## Correct option

**2. Metaplasia**

## Answer explanation

Synovial chondromatosis is a metaplastic process when synovial tissue develops into cartilaginous tissue.

Synovial chondromatosis (SC) is a proliferative metaplastic process of the synovium of any joint, which often results in multiple loose intra-articular bodies. Often presenting in the 4th to 6th decades of life, it is twice as common in males and most commonly affects the knee joint. The metaplastic process results in intra-articular bodies from soft synovial tissue to hard cartilaginous bodies of various sizes. Patients present with insidious onset pain and swelling with mechanical symptoms and stiffness. Mild symptoms can be treated conservatively, while more severe symptoms can be treated with arthroscopic versus open debridement.

*De Sa et al. systematically reviewed 14 studies on hip arthroscopy to treat SC.* *In 197 patients after arthroscopic loose body removal and synovectomy, there was a recurrence rate of 7.1% with a 1% neuropraxia complication rate.* Time to recurrence was, on average, 3.5 years. A total of 40.1% of patients required additional surgical procedures, though not all due to SC. **They conclude** that arthroscopy is a safe and effective means of treatment for these patients.

*Lee et al. reported on a series of 24 patients with a mean follow-up of 41 months treated with hip arthroscopy for SC.* On average, VAS went from 8.1 before to 3.1 after surgery, with a return to weight bearing at two days postop. Good or excellent outcomes occurred in 75% of their patients, with recurrence in 16.7% (4 patients). **They conclude** that arthroscopy is an effective treatment with quick rehabilitation postoperatively.

*Startzman et al. systematically reviewed SC and pigmented villonodular synovitis of the hip.* When combining both pathologies, **they conclude** that open and arthroscopic choices are successful without clear differences in outcomes. Arthroscopic surgery has the benefit of decreased hospital stay length, less postoperative pain, fewer complications, and faster recovery. At the same time, open debridement allows for an easier full synovial debridement and ease of removing large loose bodies.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Neoplastic processes underly cancer development.
Answer 3: Reactive arthritides often result in joint and bony destruction, not development of chondral loose bodies as seen in synovial chondromatosis
Answer 4: Osteoarthritis can result in chondral loose bodies within the joint; however, the magnitude is significantly less than in synovial chondromatosis.
Answer 5: Vascular insufficiency and avascular necrosis can cause loose bodies to develop secondary to femoral and acetabular fragmentation and collapse; however, this patient doesn't have evidence of that on imaging.
