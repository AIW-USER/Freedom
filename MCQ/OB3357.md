## Question

A 7-year-old boy developed a limp with atraumatic right leg pain that started five days ago. Denies any recent illness or sick contacts. On exam today, he is unable to bear weight and is lethargic with chills. His temperature is 38.0 degrees centigrade. He points to his posterior right hip as the source of his discomfort. He winces with compression of his pelvis and denies pain in his groin. Lab studies reveal a white blood cell count of 11,400/ul, CRP of 0.9 mg/dL (normal < 1.0 mg/dL), and erythrocyte sedimentation rate of 55 mm/h. A pelvis radiograph is shown in Figure A. Ultrasound-guided aspiration of the right hip joint yields 10 mL of straw-colored fluid with 9,000 leukocytes per mL and zero RBCs. What is the most appropriate next step in management?

*Figure A*

## Options

1. MRI of the pelvis
2. Irrigation and debridement of the right hip joint
3. Repeat aspiration of the hip joint
4. Percutaneous screw fixation of the proximal femoral physis
5. Nonsteroidal anti-inflamatory medications and observation

## Correct option

**1. MRI of the pelvis**

## Answer explanation

This patient has clinical signs of infection with symptoms localized to the pelvis with a positive Gaenslen test (pain with pelvic compression) on the exam. He has 2 out of 4 Kocher criteria present; however, with only 9,000 leukocytes/mL in his right hip joint, this excludes a septic joint and warrants further imaging, like an MRI (answer choice 1), to investigate for acute osteomyelitis.

The differential diagnosis of an infectious presentation with a child unable to ambulate should include discitis, sacroiliitis, transient synovitis, septic hip arthritis, osteomyelitis, and an iliopsoas abscess. This patient presents with an infectious presentation including lethargy, chills, and a fever. When these symptoms are present with an inability to ambulate secondary to pain and a leukocytosis is also present, this should warrant further workup for a joint infection. After proper labs (including WBC, CRP, and ESR) have been obtained and joint infection is still near the top of the differential, a joint aspiration should be performed next.
An appropriate workup has been completed for septic arthritis in this case. The Kocher criteria are used to evaluate for septic arthritis and include fever >38.5 degrees centigrade, inability to bear weight, ESR>40 mm/h, and WBC count >12,000/ul. In this case, 2 out of 4 of the criteria are positive (inability to bear weight and ESR>40mm/h), which indicates approximately a 40% likelihood of septic arthritis. Synovial fluid analysis is used to either confirm or reject the hypothesis of suspected septic arthritis; an aspiration of < 50,000 leukocytes per mL essentially rules out sepsis of the joint and warrants further workup to confirm the proper diagnosis. With a negative hip aspiration, acute osteomyelitis can be evaluated with advanced imaging modalities such as MRI or a bone scan. An important reason to evaluate this patient with further advanced imaging is that acute osteomyelitis can be successfully treated with IV antibiotics instead of operative debridement.

*The paper by Beaupre et al discusses that iliac osteomyelitis is a rare cause of pediatric hip pain, and it can usually be effectively treated with antibiotics alone.* Repeat aspiration of the joint is indicated if there is suspicion of a poorly done procedure. Notably, a joint infection secondary to osteomyelitis is possible in the pediatric hip as a result of the synovial reflections facilitating bacterial migration from the metaphysis to the joint space. The synovial fluid analysis is critical in identifying the presence or absence of joint infection.

Figure A is a normal AP pelvis radiograph without any sign of avascular necrosis or a slipped capital femoral epiphysis.

Incorrect answer choices:
Answer choice 2: Since the hip aspiration synovial fluid analysis resulted in 9,000 leukocytes (< 50,000), this is not a septic joint infection.
Answer choice 3: If the initial hip aspiration was performed with a suboptimal technique or there is continued worsening of the hip pain with a high index of suspicion, a repeat aspiration can be performed.
Answer choice 4: There is no sign of a slipped capital femoral epiphysis on the radiograph in Figure A.
Answer choice 5: NSAIDs and observation can be performed in those with transient synovitis. However, further imaging (such as an MRI) needs to be performed for a timely diagnosis.
