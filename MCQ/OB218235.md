## Question

A 65-year-old man presents to your clinic with chronic, progressive knee pain. Figure A is a radiograph of his right knee. He has not had any treatment to this point and would like to pursue non-surgical treatment first. Based on the most updated 2021 AAOS clinical practice guidelines for the treatment of symptomatic knee arthritis, which of the following strategies would you advise the patient is supported by "strong evidence"?

![[q218235-a-4359-99-262-medial-patellar-osteophytes-pictures.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Oral tramadol
2. Supervised exercise program
3. Massage and acupuncture
4. Hyaluronic acid injections
5. Lateral wedge insoles

## Correct option

**2. Supervised exercise program**

## Answer explanation

The updated 2021 AAOS clinical practice guidelines (CPG) "strongly" recommend a supervised exercise program for the management of symptomatic knee osteoarthritis (OA), given its studied benefits in improving pain and function.

Symptomatic knee OA is widespread, with an incidence of 240 people per 100,000. The AAOS CPG provide recommendations by a large group of experts in the care of knee OA and were most recently updated in 2021. Unchanged from previous guidelines, a supervised exercise program is recommended in order to improve pain and function in patients with OA. This recommendation is supported by multiple high quality studies. Conversely, prior recommendations for oral tramadol have changed and the new guidelines provide a strong recommendation against tramadol and other narcotics in its class. Other treatment strategies with strong recommendations for use include oral and topical NSAIDS and oral acetaminophen.

*Brophy et al. published a systematic review outlining the recent 2021 AAOS clinic practice guidelines for the management of knee OA.* *They review all 29 recommendations, with 8 of them being strong recommendations.* Finally, **they note** there is more investigation needed into the use of intra-articular corticosteroid injections, hyaluronic acid injections and platelet-rich plasma injections.

*de Rooij et al. reviewed the efficacy of exercise therapy on physical function in patients with knee OA compared to controls.* **They found** statistically significant differences in WOMAC scores and the 6-minute walk test in favor of the therapy group. **They concluded** that the differences in physical function were clinically relevant and offered no adverse outcomes, therefore, leading them to recommend this strategy to knee OA patients.

Figure A represents a right knee radiograph with significant medial compartment OA and varus deformity.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The 2021 AAOS CPG recommends strongly against tramadol and other narcotic pain medication due to increased adverse events and lack of effectiveness in improving pain or function in knee OA.
Answer 3: The 2021 AAOS CPG provided only limited recommendations for massage and acupuncture therapies, indicating that these may be useful treatments in addition to other strongly recommended therapies in knee OA. However, evidence was limited to provide a moderate or strong recommendation.
Answer 4: The 2021 AAOS CPG have a moderate strength recommendation against the use of hyaluronic acid injections for knee OA, as it is felt that patients do not demonstrate a clinically relevant difference when compared to controls.
Answer 5: The 2021 AAOS CPG strongly recommends against lateral wedge insoles, noting no significant clinical improvement.
