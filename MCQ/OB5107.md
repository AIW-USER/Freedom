## Question

A 7 year-old boy is referred to your office for evaluation of scoliosis. All of the following are indications to obtain a screening MRI EXCEPT:

## Options

1. Male gender
2. Age younger than 11
3. Absence of thoracic apical segment lordosis on clinical exam
4. Right thoracic curve on AP radiograph
5. Hyperreflexia and decreased sensation over bilateral forearms and flanks

## Correct option

**4. Right thoracic curve on AP radiograph**

## Answer explanation

Male gender, age younger than 11, absence of thoracic apical segment lordosis, and abnormal neurologic findings on clinical exam have all been associated with increased risk of abnormal MRI findings and therefore require screening MRI.

Radiographic curve characteristics associated with spinal cord abnormalities which require screening MRI include left thoracic curve, absent pedicle, and congenital bar. A syrinx should be suspected when abnormal abdominal reflexes are present. A clinical absence of thoracic apical segment lordosis has been found as one of the highest risk factors correlating with abnormal MRI findings. A right thoracic curve is typically not associated with intraspinal anomalies.

*Davids et al. reviewed over 2000 patients who were evaluated for scoliosis, of which approximately 2% had abnormal MRI findings.* Factors that correlated with abnormal findings included absence of thoracic apical segment lordosis, followed by atypical curves (i.e. left thoracic curve), and abnormal neurologic examination. Pain alone or in combination with other findings were not predictive.

*Nakahara et al reviewed a screening cohort of 472 patients, 3.8% of which had abnormal MRI findings leading to operative intervention.* Increased risk factors, correlated by regression analysis, noted that male gender, age under 11 years old, and abnormal superficial abdominal reflexes correlated the highest.

Incorrect answer(s):
Answers 1,2,3, and 5: Male gender, age < 11 years, absence of thoracic lordosis, and abnormal neurologic findings are risk factors for intraspinal anomalies when seen in patients with scoliosis.
