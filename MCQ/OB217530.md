## Question

## Options

1. This patient is not indicated for selective thoracic fusion given her apical vertebral translation ratio of 3.0
2. This patient is indicated for thoracolumbar fusion given her apical vertebral translation ratio of 0.33
3. This patient is indicated for selective lumbar fusion given her apical vertebral translation ratio of 0.33
4. This patient is indicated for selective thoracic fusion given her apical vertebral translation ratio of 3.0
5. This patient is indicated for thoracolumbar fusion given her apical vertebral translation index of 33%

## Correct option

**4. This patient is indicated for selective thoracic fusion given her apical vertebral translation ratio of 3.0**

## Answer explanation

This patient has an apical vertebral translation (AVT)ratio of 3.0 with evidence of a more flexible lumbar curve relative to her thoracic curve. Based on the information provided, the patient would be a candidate for selective thoracic fusion.

The goal of selective thoracic fusions is to allow for as many mobile vertebral segments as possible while still achieving spontaneous correction of minor curves. It is critical to be able to identify patients that may or may not be candidates for selective thoracic fusion. There exist several parameters that can be used to help evaluate candidacy. These include relative apical vertebral translation (which is translated into the apical vertebral translation ratio), apical vertebral rotation, Cobb angle, and flexibility of the two curves, as well as sagittal plane assessment of the thoracolumbar junction. The thoracic AVT is the distance between the C-7 plumb line and the center of the apical vertebral body of the thoracic curve (Figure A). The thoracolumbar/lumbar AVT is the distance between the center of the apical vertebral body of the thoracolumbar/lumbar curve and the center sacral vertebral line. A ratio of thoracic AVT to thoracolumbar/lumbar AVT larger than 1.2 indicates a more translated (20% or more) thoracic curve that may be treated with selective thoracic fusion.

*Lenke et al. performed a retrospective review of 50 patients with AIS and either King Type II or III curves treated with fusion in order to determine risk factors for postoperative decompensation.* **They report** that proper identification of King Type II curves, which may be successfully treated with selective thoracic fusion, requires careful analysis of the standing preoperative coronal radiograph as well as the side benders. **They conclude** that Type II curves are based on the differential between the thoracic and lumbar curve magnitude, apical vertebral deviation from the midline, and apical vertebral rotation on the standing coronal radiograph in addition to a positive flexibility index.

*Crawford et al. performed a multicenter retrospective analysis of prospectively collected data evaluating radiographical and clinical characteristics of patients undergoing a selective thoracic fusion (STF) for Lenke 1C curves versus nonselective fusions.* **They reported** that those undergoing an STF had smaller TL/L Cobb angles, less TL/L clinical deformity, and larger main thoracic: TL/L ratios. **They concluded** that despite the recommendation to fuse only the structural thoracic curve in a 1C curve, only 49% of patients were treated with an STF.

Figure A is a bending radiograph demonstrating a flexible lumbar curve that nearly corrects to neutral (which suggests that the thoracic curve is the major curve, and the lumbar curve is the minor curve). Figure B is a PA radiograph demonstrating the thoracic AVT, line A, which is the distance between the C-7 plumb line and the center of the apical vertebral body of the thoracic curve. Line B is the thoracolumbar/lumbar AVT, which is the distance between the center of the apical vertebral body of the thoracolumbar/lumbar curve and the center sacral vertebral line.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: A ratio of thoracic AVT to thoracolumbar/lumbar AVT larger than 1.2 indicates a more translated (20% or more) thoracic curve that may be treated with selective thoracic fusion
Answer 2: The apical vertebral translation ratio is calculated by dividing the thoracic AVT by the lumbar AVT.
Answer 3: This patient would not be a candidate for a selective lumbar fusion given that the thoracic curve is the major curve.
Answer 5: An apical vertebral translation index has not been described.
