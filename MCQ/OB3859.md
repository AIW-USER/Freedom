## Question

A 34-year-old male undergoes cavus foot reconstruction after failed nonoperative treatment. During the procedure, you plan to correct the fixed deformity shown in figure A. Which surgical technique best addresses this deformity?

![[q3859-a-screen-shot-2019-08-21-at-6-21-45-pm.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. EHL transfer to the proximal phalanx
2. EHL transfer to the metatarsal neck
3. EHL transfer to the metatarsal neck with interphalangeal joint fusion
4. FHB tenotomy
5. Metatarsal head resection

## Correct option

**3. EHL transfer to the metatarsal neck with interphalangeal joint fusion**

## Answer explanation

The modified Jones procedure best addresses this patient's fixed claw toe of the hallux, which is an EHL transfer to the metatarsal neck with interphalangeal (IP) joint fusion.

Cavovarus deformity can occur in pediatric or adult populations and may present with pain on the lateral or medial borders of the foot. Claw toes result from an imbalance between the toe intrinsic and extrinsic musculature. The primary deformity is hyperextension at the MTP joint which results in metatarsal head depression. Patients can symptomatically present with calluses over the IP joint and metatarsalgia. The modified Jones procedure can successfully treat fixed claw toe deformity of the hallux.

*Elias et al. performed a cadaveric comparison of the modified Jones procedure vs. an FHL transfer for the treatment of hallux claw toe deformity.* Both procedures produced equivalent results in terms of deformity correction and plantar pressure modification under the metatarsal. The authors do note that the FHL transfer does not require fusion of the hallux, which is considered an undesirable co-morbidity of the modified Jones procedure.

*Breusch et al. performed a cross-sectional study in 51 patients (81 feet) with a clawed hallux in association with a cavus foot after a modified Robert Jones tendon transfer.* Every patient underwent a concomitant associated surgery for their cavus foot. The overall rate of patient satisfaction was 86% and the deformity of the hallux was corrected in 80 feet. The deforming force of extensor hallucis longus is effectively eliminated by the Jones transfer but the mechanics of the first metatarsophalangeal joint are altered.

Incorrect answers:
Answers 1 and 4: Transferring the EHL to the proximal phalanx or performing a tenotomy of the FHB tendon would worsen the MTP hyperextension.
Answer 2: This is the originally described Jones procedure, but it failed to adequately address the claw toe.
Answer 5: This describes the Girdlestone-Taylor procedure which is employed with flexible claw deformities of the lesser toes.
