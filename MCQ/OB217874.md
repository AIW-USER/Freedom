## Question

A 22-year-old male presents to your office after a twisting knee injury that occurred while playing flag football. He complains of pain localized over the medial joint line and he feels an occasional locking sensation. He has had a persistent effusion since the injury occurred 2 weeks ago. Radiographs and MRI were obtained and demonstrated an unstable medial femoral condyle articular cartilage lesion measuring 1 x 1.5cm with a free flap. Intra-operatively, you are unable to fixate this piece and decide to perform a single procedure that stimulates a combination of type 1 and 2 collagen. What procedure was most likely performed?

## Options

1. Osteochondral autograft
2. Osteochondral allograft
3. Matrix-associated autologous chondrocyte implantation
4. Particulated juvenile cartilage allograft
5. Microfracture

## Correct option

**5. Microfracture**

## Answer explanation

Microfracture and other marrow-stimulation techniques stimulate the filling of cartilage defects with a combination of type 1 and type 2 collagen.

Articular cartilage defects in the knee occur after acute trauma or repetitive loading. They frequently cause mechanical symptoms and persistent effusions, especially in lesions that are unstable. MRI is important to best evaluate the size and stability of these lesions. These factors are important in a treatment algorithm for articular defects. Microfracture is an attempt to penetrate the subchondral bone in order to release marrow elements into the bony defect. This technique results in a mixture of type 1 and type 2 collagen, although it is primarily type 1 collagen that forms; this is thought to be part of the reason that cartilage forming after microfracture may have decreased longevity. In contrast, other cartilage restoration techniques like osteochondral autografts (OATS) and osteochondral allografts rely on primarily type 2 collagen.

*Krych et al. compared activity levels after OATS and microfracture for articular cartilage defects of the knee in 96 patients.* *Both groups demonstrated improved outcomes, however, the OATS group demonstrated greater improvement in Marx activity scores at two-, three- and five-year time points.* **They concluded** that OATS performed better with regards to returning to a higher level of activity at all time points in the study.

*Redondo et al. reviewed microfracture and OATS with regard to cartilage restoration in cases of patellofemoral cartilage defects.* **They note** that microfracture leads to fibrocartilage formation with the perforation of the subchondral plate and they compare it to the OATS procedure which replaces the defect with native hyaline cartilage plugs. They further discuss the indications, techniques, and outcomes of these two procedures.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Osteochondral autograft transfer, known as an OATS procedure, uses an osteochondral plug from a different spot of the knee to replace the weight-bearing defect. Therefore, the cartilage plug would still contain type 2 cartilage.
Answer 2: Osteochondral allograft takes an appropriately sized fresh graft from a cadaver in order to restore the articular cartilage. This would contain type 2 cartilage.
Answer 3: Matrix-associated autologous chondrocyte implantation (MACI) involves a two-stage procedure where cartilage cells are cultured and multiplied in vitro and then re-implanted in order to stimulate type 2 cartilage growth (with some type 1 cartilage growth as well).
Answer 4: Particulate juvenile cartilage allograft involves transplantation of juvenile cartilage pieces containing live cells within native extracellular matrix which stimulates type 2 cartilage growth.
