## Question

According to the American Academy of Orthopedic Surgeons (AAOS) clinical practice guidelines, all of the following have been shown to decrease venous thromboembolism risk following total knee arthroplasty, except?

## Options

1. Early mobilization
2. Aspirin
3. Coumadin
4. Neuraxial anesthesia
5. Pneumatic compression boots

## Correct option

**4. Neuraxial anesthesia**

## Answer explanation

From the American Academy of Orthopedic Surgeons (AAOS) clinical practice guidelines, the use of neuraxial anesthesia does not alter the risk of venous thromboembolism (VTE) in elective total hip and knee arthroplasty.

Deep vein thrombosis following total joint arthroplasty is clinically silent in 20% of cases and is potentially fatal. The use of pharmacologic and/or mechanical compression, the use of neuraxial anesthesia, and the discontinuation of antiplatelet medications prior to surgery received moderate recommendations. However, the use of neuraxial anesthesia and discontinuation of antiplatelet medications 1 week prior to surgery has not been found to decrease VTE rates but rather reduces intraoperative blood loss.

*Lewis et al. conducted a prospective study of 3289 patients undergoing hip and knee arthroplasty procedures using either aspirin 325 mg twice daily for six weeks or 40 mg low-molecular-weight heparin daily for 7-12 days as set forth by the AAOS guidelines in 2009.* **The authors reported** a 90-day VTE rate of 1.1%, with total hip arthroplasty patients (0.56%) having less VTE rates than total knee patients (1.46%). **The authors concluded** that the AAOS guidelines provide are effective at reducing VTE rates following arthroplasty procedures.

*Mont et al. presented the AAOS clinical practice guidelines for preventing VTE in patients undergoing elective hip and knee arthroplasty.* *They issued a moderate strength recommendation for the use of pharmacologic prophylaxis in patients without increased bleeding risks and/or mechanical compression.* No specific pharmacologic or mechanical compression recommendations were given.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Early mobilization was given a consensus strength recommendation by the AAOS due to low-cost, minimal risk, and being consistent with current practice.
Answer 2: The AAOS did not give specific pharmacologic prophylaxis recommendations, but aspirin has been shown in several studies to decrease VTE rates in hip and knee arthroplasty patients while minimizing wound complications.
Answer 3: The AAOS did not give specific recommendations regarding pharmacologic prophylaxis. However, they gave a moderate strength recommendation for the use of pharmacologic prophylaxis and/or mechanical compression in patients without a history of bleeding disorders and/or active liver disease.
Answer 5: The use of mechanical compression devices has received a moderate strength recommendation by the AAOS as this has been shown in studies to prevent the development of DVT and subsequent VTE.
