## Question

A 60-year-old male presents with a large necrotic ulcerative lesion over the anterior tibia over the past 6 months. He admits to a remote history of a midshaft tibia fracture treated with open reduction and internal fixation complicated by a chronically draining wound near the location of the lesion. Biopsy of the ulcerative lesion reveals squamous cell carcinoma. Which of the following is the likely precursor of this malignant lesion?

## Options

1. Adamantinoma
2. Osteofibrous Dysplasia
3. Chronic osteomyelitis
4. Eosinophilic granuloma
5. Cortical desmoid

## Correct option

**3. Chronic osteomyelitis**

## Answer explanation

The patient has developed a Marjolin's ulcer (squamous cell carcinoma) due to a chronically draining sinus tract in the setting of chronic osteomyelitis (likely due to infected underlying hardware).

Squamous cell carcinoma (SCC) is the most common type of malignant tumor deriving from chronic osteomyelitis. The most frequently affected site is the tibia, followed by the femur. Diagnosis is generally confirmed by biopsy at all suspicious wound sites. The rate of chronic osteomyelitis transformation to SCC is low with most studies citing a ~1% rate. The recommended treatment is generally amputation since many experts agree that the carcinomatous transformation of chronic bone infections can lead to progressive metastasis and a high mortality rate.

*Li et al. performed a retrospective study of 8 cases of SCC arising from chronic osteomyelitis.* *The patients had an average age of 55 years, with a male predominance (6 men and 2 women).* **The authors noted** a mean delay between the initial injury and the diagnosis of malignant transformation of 28 years (range 8 to 50 years) with SCC resulting from tibia osteomyelitis in six cases, femur in one case and ankle in one case. Curative amputation was performed in 7 patients (one patient refused) with no recurrence or metastasis occurring during the follow-up time. **The authors conclude** that amputation appears to be an effective treatment method for SCC secondary to chronic osteomyelitis.

*Jiang et al. performed a systematic review of SCC arising from extremity chronic osteomyelitis in 60 studies of 176 patients.* **The authors noted** that chronic osteomyelitis mostly occurred following trauma (73%) with the tibia being the most frequent site (61%), and a sinus tract being the most common symptom (61%). The mean duration from chronic osteomyelitis to SCC was 27 years. Limb amputation was performed in 80.5% of the patients. **The authors concluded** that SCC arising from extremity osteomyelitis had an overall local recurrence, metastasis, and SCC-related death that exceeded 10% and recommend patients with local lymphadenopathy be followed up closely.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1, 2, 4 and 5: Given the history, physical exam, and biopsy findings, the patient most likely had a diagnosis of chronic osteomyelitis that transformed into a malignant SCC
