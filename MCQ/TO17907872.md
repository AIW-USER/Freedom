## Question

A 15 years old boy has come with a proximal tibia lytic lesion in epiphyseal area. X ray is showing soap bubble pattern. The likely possibility includes

## Options

1. Chondroblastoma
2. Osteoid osteoma
3. Chondromyxoid fibroma
4. GCT

## Correct option

**1. Chondroblastoma**

## Answer explanation

Ans : A

Osteoid osteoma id diaphyseal lesion and Chondromyxoid fibroma is metaphyseal lesion so they can be ruled out.

Now, both GCT and Chondroblastoma are epiphyseal lesions (Mnemonic ECG= Epiphysis Chondroblastoma Giant cell tumor).

GCT will be less likely option as common age group for GCT is 20-40 years (occurs after the closure of growth plates in skeletally mature people).

Chondroblastoma is an epiphyseal lesion and adolescent age group is befitting (10-25 years most common). So best answer is A. Soap bubble appearance although is classically taken up for GCT, however Chondroblastoma also comes in differential diagnosis of soap bubble appearance.

SOAP BUBBLE APPEARANCE describes a multiloculated bubbly appearance seen in expansile lytic lesions (usually benign or locally aggressive lesions) with internal trabeculations but intact cortex.

D/Ds to consider for Soap Bubble Appearance: -

- aneurysmal bone cyst (ABC)
- giant cell tumor (osteoclastoma)
- chondroblastoma
- enchondroma
- chondromyxoid fibroma
- fibrous dysplasia
- ameloblastoma of the jaw
- plasmacytoma
- chordoma

All can present with multiloculated lesions with preserved cortex.
