## Question

A newborn child is born with the condition seen in Figure A. The bilateral feet correct passively with manipulation but also actively on observation. The ankle has full supple range of motion in plantarflexion and dorsiflexion. Which of the following is the next best step in management?

![[q2218-a-metatarsus-adductus-infant.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Serial manipulations and casting
2. Tarsometatarsal capsulotomies and achilles lengthening
3. Ponseti method casting and Achilles tenotomy
4. Parental manipulation and stretching
5. Parental reassurance and observation

## Correct option

**5. Parental reassurance and observation**

## Answer explanation

The patient has correctable metatarsus adductus for which the best treatment is parental education/reassurance and observation.

Metatarsus adductus is a congenital foot deformity of unknown etiology and pathogenesis. It may be related to "packing" of the infant in the uterus and due to intrauterine positioning. Classification is based on the heel bisector line with a more lateral line representing a more severe deformity. Deformities that are flexible can be treated with observation with a 90-95% rate of spontaneous correction. Rigid deformities should undergo serial manipulation and casting prior to 6 months of age. Surgery is reserved for patients older than 3 years of age or those with rigid deformities that have not responded to serial manipulation.

*Farsetti et al. followed 31 patients (45 feet) with metatarsus adductus treated nonoperatively for an average of 32 years and 6 months.* **They found** that all passively correctable deformities resolved spontaneously and had no poor results. **They concluded** that operative intervention is not needed in patients with mild or moderate metatarsus adductus deformities.

Figure A shows a photograph of a newborn infant with metatarsus adductus. Illustration A shows an illustration demonstrating the difference between a normal foot and metatarsus adductus.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Serial manipulation and casting is reserved for instances when the foot is rigidly fixed in a severely adducted position and is not correctable.
Answer 2: Tarsometatarsal capsulotomies may be performed in children aged 2-4 years of age who have failed to improve since birth. Achilles lengthening would only be indicated if there was the presence of a tight heel cord.
Answer 3: The Ponseti method is used primarily for clubfoot deformities.
Answer 4: Parental manipulation could be used if the foot could only be corrected passively.
