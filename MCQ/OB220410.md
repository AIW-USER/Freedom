## Question

## Answer explanation

Excision of nonreconstructable patellar fracture fragments is permissible in certain situations. The inferomedial quadrant of the patella provides the highest arterial contribution and would, therefore, lead to the largest decrease in perfusion to the remaining patella (answer choice 4).

The blood supply to the patella arises from a peripatellar anastomotic ring comprising five genicular arteries (supreme, superomedial, superolateral, inferolateral, and inferomedial) and the anterior tibial recurrent artery. These vessels join to form a vascular ring that feeds two main vessel systems: the mid-patellar system and the radial system, shown in Illustration A. Multiple studies have evaluated the vascular patterns of the patella and have found that the majority of the patella's arterial supply enters inferomedially. This has clinical significance in treating patella fractures and surgical approaches around the knee. Destruction of the inferior patella or the inferomedial blood supply can lead to avascular necrosis or nonunion.

*Gadinsky et al. published a cadaveric study of skeletally mature and immature patellae, comparing the blood supply distribution between skeletally mature and immature patellae.* *Five immature and five mature cadaveric knees were studied using gadolinium-enhanced MRIs.* **The authors found** arterial contributions to be similar between immature and mature patellae. When comparing the arterial contribution of the four quadrants of the patella, the inferomedial quadrant had the highest arterial contribution in both groups. **The authors concluded** that maintenance of the inferomedial blood supply in surgical procedures is paramount to preserving vasculature to the patella and preventing complications.

*Lazaro et al. also investigated the vascular supply of the patella using gadolinium-enhanced MRI.* *The authors included 20 matched years of cadaveric knees and injected arterial contributions to the patella with gadolinium.* After MRI, **the authors found** the largest arterial contribution to the patella arose from the inferior pole in all the specimens and the inferomedial quadrant in 80% of specimens. Furthermore, the authors performed transverse osteotomies of the patella and found a 36% decrease in perfusion to the proximal fragment in 36%, indicating a predominantly inferior blood supply. **The authors concluded** that vasculature to the patella was predominantly fed from the inferomedial quadrant and cautioned against distal-pole patellectomies to prevent avascular necrosis of the remaining patella.

Figures A through E demonstrate different areas or quadrants of the patella. Figure A is the superior pole of the patella. Figure B is the inferolateral quadrant. Figure C is the superomedial quadrant. Figure D: inferomedial quadrant. Figure E is the superolateral quadrant. Illustration 1 demonstrates the vascular supply of the patella, forming an anastomotic ring, from Lazaro et al.

Incorrect Answers:
Incorrect Answer 1: Figure A highlights the superior pole of the patella. Excision of the superior pole would not create significant perfusion deficits in the remaining patella, as the greatest contributors to patellar perfusion arise from the inferior pole of the patella, specifically inferomedially.
Incorrect Answer 2: Figure B highlights the inferolateral quadrant of the patella. The inferolateral quadrant is similarly well-perfused, but less perfused than the inferomedial quadrant. The inferomedial quadrant provides the majority of vascular contribution to the patella.
Incorrect Answer 3: Figure C highlights the superomedial quadrant of the patella. The superomedial quadrant is relatively less vascularized compared to the inferomedial and inferolateral quadrants. Excision of this area would not provide a large decrease in perfusion to the remaining patella, as the inferomedial quadrant would.
Incorrect Answer 5: Figure 5 highlights the superolateral quadrant of the patella. The superolateral quadrant provides the least arterial contribution to patella vasculature.
