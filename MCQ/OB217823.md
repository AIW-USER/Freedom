## Question

You are performing a cadaveric biomechanical study regarding elbow stability conferred by the coronoid under axial loading. You decide to test elbow stability under axial load before and after creating a coronoid fracture. Compared with an intact coronoid, which statement regarding the coronoid is true?

## Options

1. There would be increased posterolateral displacement at 30 degrees of flexion with >50% of the anteromedial facet fractured
2. There would be increased posterolateral displacement at 60 degrees of flexion with >50% of the anteromedial facet fractured
3. There would be increased anterior axial displacement at 30 degrees of flexion with 25% of the coronoid process fractured
4. There would be increased posterior axial displacement at 60 degrees of flexion with 25% of the coronoid process fractured
5. There would be increased posterior axial displacement at 30 degrees of flexion with >50% of the coronoid process fractured

## Correct option

**5. There would be increased posterior axial displacement at 30 degrees of flexion with >50% of the coronoid process fractured**

## Answer explanation

Coronoid process fractures with >50% involvement have demonstrated significant differences across all flexion positions, in posterior axial displacement between an intact elbow.

The coronoid process provides an anterior and varus buttress to the ulnohumeral joint. It is important in resisting posterior subluxation beyond 30 degrees of flexion. Biomechanical studies have demonstrated that compared with intact coronoid processes, there is no significant increase in posterior axial displacement when the less than 50% of the coronoid process is fractured; this would include Regan-Morrey types I&II fractures. Type III fractures have demonstrated a loss of elbow stability compared with an intact coronoid process.

*Closkey et al. performed a cadaveric study testing elbow stability under axial loading before and after a fracture of the coronoid process.* **They reported** no significant difference, at any flexion position, in posterior axial displacement between the intact elbows and the elbows in which 50% or less of the coronoid process was fractured (type I and type II). **They noted** significant differences, across all flexion positions, in posterior axial displacement between the intact elbows and the elbows in which more than 50% of the coronoid process was fractured (type III). **They concluded** that in response to axial load, elbows with a fracture involving more than 50% of the coronoid process displace more readily than elbows with a fracture involving 50% or less of the coronoid process, especially when the elbow is flexed 60 degrees and beyond. Coronoid ORIF can be fixed through radial head defect laterally. It may be repaired with sutures, suture anchors, screws, and/or plate fixation.

*O'Driscoll et al. reviewed complex elbow fractures.* **They reported** that coronoid fractures can be classified according to whether the fracture involves the tip, the anteromedial facet, or the base (body) of the coronoid. **They noted** that anteromedial coronoid fractures are actually varus posteromedial rotatory fracture subluxations and are often serious injuries. **They concluded** that these patterns of injury predict associated injuries and instability as well as surgical approach and treatment.

*Budoff et al. compared the biomechanical characteristics of screw versus plate versus both screw and plate fixation for large, type 3 O'Driscoll coronoid fractures.* **They reported** that the combination of a plate and screw had significantly greater energy to failure (83 Nm), force required to cause failure (634 N), and stiffness at failure (387 N/mm) compared to either an isolated plate (38 Nm, 474 N, 237 N/mm, respectively) or a screw (10 Nm, 279 N, 149 N/mm, respectively). **They concluded** that for type 3 O'Driscoll coronoid fractures or nonunions when both a screw and a plate can be placed, the combination of these two fixation devices appears to produce significantly greater biomechanical stability than either fixation device alone.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1&2: Fractures of the anteromedial facet of the coronoid would result in anteromedial rotatory instability
Answers 3&4: No significant difference, at any flexion position, in posterior axial displacement has been shown between intact elbows and type I or type II (<50%) coronoid process fractures
