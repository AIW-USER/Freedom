## Options

1. aneurysmal bone cyst
2. chondroblastoma
3. giant cell tumor of bone
4. intramedullary osteosarcoma
5. unicameral bone cyst

## Correct option

**3. giant cell tumor of bone**

## Answer explanation

This 29-year-old has a right wrist mass, and imaging demonstrates an expansile, lytic mass within the distal radius. A biopsy revealed mononuclear cells expressing RANKL. This presentation suggests a giant cell tumor of bone (Answer 4).

Giant cell tumors (GCT) of bone are benign but aggressive tumors found in the epiphysis and metaphysis of long bones, with a predilection for females between the ages of 30 and 50. Patients often present with a painful mass, while radiographs reveal a lytic epiphyseal/metaphyseal lesion that appears dark on T1 and bright on T2-weighted MRI sequencing. Histologically, the mononuclear stromal cell is considered the neoplastic cell, which overexpresses RANKL and causes multinucleated osteoclasts to develop, resulting in the diffuse osteolysis seen on radiographs. Following this discovery, the incorporation of denosumab (monoclonal antibody targeting the RANKL pathway) has become an integral adjunctive treatment in addition to surgery for this condition.

*Basu-Mallick & Chawla reviewed recent developments in advanced/metastatic GCT of bone,* **noting** a paradigm shift from predominantly surgical management to medical management following the development of denosumab. **The authors recommend** interdisciplinary management to include denosumab administration in GCT of bone cases where surgical resection may not be appropriate secondary to concerns for increased morbidity.

*Wu et al. reviewed the RANKL-RANK-osteoprotegerin pathway associated with giant cell tumors of bone.* **They note** the RANK pathway is essential in developing multinucleated osteoclasts, ultimately leading to bony resorption. **They conclude** the identification of this pathway has allowed the incorporation of newer therapies (denosumab) to facilitate better management and prevent recurrence.

Figures A and B reveal orthogonal radiographs of the wrist that demonstrate an expansile, lytic lesion of the distal radius. Figures C and D are T2-weighted imaging demonstrating an enhancing homogenous, expansive lesion throughout the epiphysis and metaphysis.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Aneurysmal bone cysts, while also lytic and expansile in radiographs, exhibit fluid-fluid levels on MRI and are driven by upregulation of the ubiquitin-specific protease-6
Answer 2: Chondroblastomas, while also lytic and confined to the epiphysis, do not demonstrate upregulation of the RANKL pathway and instead possess benign proliferations of immature chondroblasts.
Answer 4: While osteosarcoma can possess giant cells, GCTs do not exhibit the degree of cellular atypia intramedullary osteosarcoma (high nuclear-to-cytoplasmic ratio, abnormal mitotic cells).
Answer 5: Unicameral bone cysts are also expansile but typically do not cause cortical destruction as observed with GCTs. Meanwhile, one would observe a cystic appearance with a thin fibrous lining on histology.
