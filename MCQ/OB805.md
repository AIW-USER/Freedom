## Question

A 12-year-old girl presents with 6 months of moderate but persistent lower back pain. She has increased pain when she stands upright on one leg as well as shooting pain when she tries to bend down to touch her toes. A straight leg raise test is negative but it is noted that her hamstrings are tight. She has tried NSAIDs and PT, which have provided her with limited relief. Upright radiographs are shown in Figure A. What is the next most appropriate treatment?

![[q805-a-lumbr-l5-s1-spondy.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Repair of L5-S1 pars with tension wiring
2. Posterior instrumentation and fusion from L5-S1 without reduction of the deformity
3. Posterior instrumentation and fusion from L5-S1 with reduction of the deformity
4. Posterior instrumentation and fusion from L4-S1
5. A trial of TLSO for 6 weeks

## Correct option

**4. Posterior instrumentation and fusion from L4-S1**

## Answer explanation

The radiograph reveals a high-grade L5-S1 spondylolisthesis with roughly an 80% anterior slip (Meyerding grade IV). The most supported treatment for pediatric high-grade spondylolisthesis (grade III-V), having failed conservative management, is posterior instrumented fusion from L4-S1.

Treatment of pediatric spondylolisthesis remains a controversial topic, with an abundance of literature supporting both non-operative and operative treatment modalities of both low-grade and high-grade slips. Patients who are asymptomatic and have low-grade slips (Meyerding grade I and II) are generally managed with observation and no activity restriction. Patients with low-grade slips and who ARE symptomatic are given a trial of non-operative management with activity modification, physical therapy (to include hamstring stretches due to associated hamstring tightness) and potentially TLSO bracing for 6-12 weeks. Patients with progressive symptoms, neurological deficits, or high-grade slips are considered surgical candidates. Patients with high-grade spondylolisthesis have been shown to benefit from a 2-level PSIF more so than those with low-grade slips who undergo single-level PSIF. Whether or not a full, partial, or any reduction should be performed at the time of instrumentation remains a topic of controversy as well given the risk of associated neurologic injury.

*Lenke et al. evaluated the management of high-grade isthmic spondylolisthesis in children and young adults.* **They noted** that patients with high-grade slips (Meyerding classification grade III or higher) had the best outcomes following surgical management, advocating for appropriate central and foraminal decompressions at the L5-S1 level followed by lumbosacral fusion. **The authors further supported** that partial reduction aimed at improving the slip angle (lumbosacral kyphosis) was beneficial and provided less risk to the L5 nerve roots than complete reduction of the translational component of the slip. **They concluded** that solid anterior and posterior spinal fusion at L5-S1 provided the best long-term results.

*Bourassa-Moreau et al. compared surgical to non-surgical outcomes in 28 patients with high-grade spondylolisthesis.* *The authors compared pre-and post-operative Scoliosis Research Society (SRS-22) questionnaire scores, evaluating the neurological exam, radiographs and slip grade.* **They found** that the SRS-22 scores increased most significantly in the surgical cohort, while there was no significant improvement in quality of life in the non-surgical cohort. However, **they found** no neurologic or slip deterioration in the non-surgical group. This leads them to conclude that surgery reliably improved quality of life, more so in patients with worse baseline quality of life, but that observation was appropriate in patients with high-grade slips and NO neurologic symptoms.

Figure A is a lateral radiograph of the lumbar spine demonstrating a high-grade L5-S1 isthmic spondylolisthesis with approximately 80% slippage (Meyerding grade IV).

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: For high-grade slips (Meyerding stage III-V), the recommended treatment is PSIF. Tension wiring is generally recommended for nondisplaced or minimally displaced pars defects that have failed non-operative management
Answer 2 and 3: A 2-level instrumented fusion is recommended for high-grade slips. L5-S1 PSIF would be recommended in the setting of a low-grade slip (Meyerding I and II) with progressive pain and/or neurological defects that had failed non-operative management
Answer 5: The indication for treatment with a TLSO is an isthmus spondylolysis with or without a low-grade spondylolisthesis that has failed non-operative management with PT, activity restriction and pain medication.
