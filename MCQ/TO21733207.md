## Question

After nerve injury to a muscle electrodiagnostic studies are conducted which show polyphasic MUPs. What is your interpretation?

## Options

1. Acutely denervated muscle
2. Early reinnervation
3. Poor potential for surgical reinnervation
4. Prominent loss of innervated MEPs

## Correct option

**2. Early reinnervation**

## Answer explanation

Ans: B

 EXPLANATION: -

A motor unit action potential (MUAP) is the electrical field generated by muscle fibers within a motor unit, recorded by a needle electrode. It is the sum of the extracellular potentials of muscle fiber action potentials of a motor unit. The waveform is dictated by the inherent properties of the motor unit and the spatial relationships between the needle and individual muscle fibers.
Analysis of the MUAP size, shape, and recruitment pattern can help in distinguishing a myopathy from a neuropathy.
Motor unit action potentials (MUPs) represent connected MEPs with intact muscle activity and are described qualitatively based on their recruitment and morphology. When an acutely, partially denervated muscle is activated, additional MUPs are not recruited and, therefore, those MUPs that are available fire faster to attempt to generate the desired amount of force (to maintain clinical function) in the muscle fibers innervated by that intact motor unit. On an EMG, this incremental increase in MUPs appears as decreased recruitment (mild to severe) of fewer MUPs, and the MUPs that are present will fire faster.
In a muscle recovering from neural injury (reinnervation), collateral sprouting of adjacent, uninjured axons reinnervate the injured axons and MUPs of longer duration, large amplitude and increased polyphasia are seen.
Thus, in the context of traumatic nerve injury, the percentage of polyphasic MUPs is an indicator of the degree of ongoing, spontaneous reinnervation. With time, reinnervated MUPs lose their polyphasic morphology but maintain the larger amplitude and, to a lesser degree, the longer duration morphology. Thus, chronic MUP morphology indicates muscle that has been successfully reinnervated.

Interpretation of electrodiagnostic findings:
Decreased SNAP and CMAP Represent axonal loss and are quantitative.
Decreased conduction velocity Severely reduced velocity represents demyelination. Mild slowing of conduction velocities can be seen in primary axon loss.
Presence of fibrillations and PSW Represents acutely denervated muscle
Reduced or absent insertional activity Suggests a poor potential for surgical reinnervation.

MUPs Represent innervated MEPs within muscle.
Polyphasic MUPs Represent early reinnervation

Chronic MUPs
 Represent longer standing reinnervated muscle from collateral sprouting and nascent axons

Decreased recruitment Represents a loss of innervated MEPs with severe implying prominent loss and mild implying less prominent loss.
