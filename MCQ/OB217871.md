## Question

A 42-year-old male presents to your office for evaluation of left shoulder pain. Radiographs demonstrate glenohumeral osteoarthritis. He has failed an extensive course of nonoperative management and would like to proceed with surgical intervention. Which of the following statements is true regarding a hemiarthroplasty (HA) or anatomic total shoulder arthroplasty (TSA)?

## Options

1. HA revision rate is equal to TSA with eccentric glenoid wear
2. HA revision rate is less than TSA with concentric glenoid wear
3. HA outcomes are superior to those of TSA with concentric glenoid wear
4. HA outcomes are equal to those of TSA with eccentric glenoid wear
5. HA outcomes are inferior to those of TSA with eccentric glenoid wear

## Correct option

**5. HA outcomes are inferior to those of TSA with eccentric glenoid wear**

## Answer explanation

This young patient has glenohumeral arthritis. Compared with a TSA, HA has been shown to have inferior outcomes when eccentric glenoid wear is present.

Glenohumeral arthritis is a degenerative joint disease of the shoulder characterized by damage to the articular surfaces of the humeral head and/or glenoid. Treatment is observation, NSAIDs, and corticosteroids for minimally symptomatic patients. Shoulder arthroplasty is indicated for progressive symptoms with severe degenerative disease. A shoulder HA is a procedure in which the humeral articular surface is replaced with a stemmed humeral component. HA outcomes are worse in patients with eccentric glenoid wear, as compared to those with a concentric joint. Moreover, HA has inferior functional outcomes compared with TSA. Lastly, the revision rate is equal, or greater than, TSA at mid to long term follow-up, and worsening glenoid erosion may occur.

*Levine et al. reviewed use of HA and TSA for glenohumeral osteoarthritis.* **They reported** that only 25% of patients with glenohumeral osteoarthritis treated with shoulder HA are satisfied with their outcome at an average of seventeen years after the operation. **They concluded** that patients with concentric glenoid wear and primary osteoarthritis have better outcomes than those with eccentric glenoid wear and secondary osteoarthritis do, but patients in both groups experienced deterioration of results over time.

*Neyton et al. retrospectively reviewed shoulder arthroplasty in young patients (<60 years old) with primary glenohumeral osteoarthritis.* **They reported** that revision arthroplasty was performed in 16% shoulders, with glenoid erosion as the cause for revision in 80%. **They noted** that HA survivorship analysis demonstrated 84% free from revision at 5 years and 79% at the final follow-up. **They concluded** that in young patients with primary glenohumeral osteoarthritis, TSA resulted in significantly better functional and subjective outcomes with no significant difference in longitudinal survivorship compared with patients treated with HA.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1&2: HA revision rate is higher than TSA with eccentric glenoid wear
Answers 3&4: HA outcomes are inferior to those of TSA with eccentric glenoid wear
