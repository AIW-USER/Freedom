## Question

A 63-year-old woman with history of rheumatoid arthritis underwent tibiotalocalcaneal fusion 3 years ago following failed total ankle arthroplasty. She is referred to your office because of persistent pain that is aggravated by weight bearing and relieved by rest. Radiographs are seen in Figures A and B. There are no signs of infection. What is the best next step?

![[q5731-b-fig-b-new.jpg]]

## Options

1. Conversion to total ankle arthroplasty
2. Augmentation of fusion construct with supplemental screws
3. Revision tibiotalocalcaneal arthrodesis
4. Talar resection and tibiotalocalcaneal arthrodesis
5. Total contact casting

## Correct option

**3. Revision tibiotalocalcaneal arthrodesis**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a failed tibiotalocalcaneal (TTC) fusion with unstable ankle joint pseudoarthrosis. Revision TTC arthrodesis is indicated.

TTC fusion is indicated for global avascular necrosis of the talus, and combined ankle and subtalar arthrosis, Charcot neuroarthropathy, significant hindfoot bone loss (failed total ankle arthroplasty, failed arthrodesis, rheumatoid arthritis and flail ankle. Intramedullary nail TTC is useful in conditions of either distal tibial and talar bone loss or when conventional screw fixation is suboptimal.

*Hammett et al. reviewed 52 TTC fusions using a retrograde locking intramedullary humeral nail for combined ankle and subtalar arthritis or complex hindfoot deformities.* At 34 months, 82% were satisfied and had pain relief. **They conclude** that this is an effective technique.

*Chou et al. retrospectively reviewed 55 TTC fusions for ankle and subtalar disease including arthritis, posttraumatic injury, failed surgery, talar avascular necrosis and rheumatoid arthritis.* *Fusion was achieved in 48 ankles at 19 weeks.* There was 2 degrees of dorsiflexion and 5 degrees of plantarflexion. **They concluded** that this is an effective salvage procedure.

*Jeng et al. retrospectively examined TTC fusion with bulk femoral head allograft in 32 patients.* Fusion was found in 50%, with the presence of diabetes as a strong predictive factor for nonunion. Ultimately, 19% of the patients went on to require a below-knee amputation. Including patients with asymptomatic nonunion, the rate of functional limb salvage was 71% . **They recommend** that TTC arthrodesis with femoral head allograft is a salvage procedure with a high complication rate.

Figures A and B show nonunion/pseudoarthrosis across the tibiotalar and subtalar joints. Illustrations A and B are examples of TTC fusion with an intramedullary nail. Illustration C is an algorithm for salvage of failed ankle arthrodesis (Cooper, PS. Complications of ankle and tibiotalocalcaneal arthrodesis. Clin Orthop Relat Res. 2001 Oct;(391):33-44.) Illustrations D and E show TTC fusion with a bulk femoral allograft to augment talar bone stock. Illustration F shows the procedure of TTC fusion with a femoral head allograft.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Conversion back to total ankle arthroplasty is contraindicated in the presence of subtalar arthrosis and poor talar bone stock.
Answer 2: Supplemental screws alone are unlikely to provide sufficient stability for fusion across both tibiotalar and subtalar joints. Removal of existing screws and re-arthrodesis is necessary.
Answer 4: Talar resection is unnecessary and will only require more dissection and place soft tissue at risk. Preserving the talus preserves surrounding vascularity and provides local bone graft to facilitate union. If the talus is insufficient, femoral head allograft augmentation is an option.
Answer 5: Total contact casting is indicated for non-healing neuropathic ulcers rather than rheumatoid arthritis. It will not lead to stable union without addressing nonunion in this example.
