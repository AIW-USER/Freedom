## Question

A 21-year-old male college football player sustains a right shoulder dislocation after being tackled which was successfully reduced in the field. Several days later presents with a recurrent dislocation after he slipped in the shower. On physical exam, there is external rotation to 80° of the affected arm and a negative Gagey test. Figure A is the current radiograph. What is the patient's Instability Severity Score?

![[q211568-a-screen-shot-2020-03-16-at-8-28-55-pm.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. 2 points
2. 3 points
3. 4 points
4. 5 points
5. 6 points

## Correct option

**4. 5 points**

## Answer explanation

The patient is presenting with a recurrent anterior shoulder dislocation with an Instability Severity Score (ISS) of 5 (2 points - competitive sport, 1 point contact sport, 2 points Hill-Sachs lesion on AP external rotation radiographs).

Traumatic anterior shoulder dislocations are one of the most common shoulder injuries that have recurrence rates at a younger age at the time of the first dislocation. The ISS score stratifies high-risk recurrent dislocation patients based on other factors including competitive sports participation, contact sports, radiographic abnormalities, and soft tissue laxity. Scores higher than 6 are better treated with an open Bankart repair or Latarjet, due to a 70% failure rate with arthroscopic stabilization. ISS scores less than or equal to 6 allows for arthroscopic stabilization given that there is an acceptable recurrence risk of 10%.

*Hovelius et al. performed a prospective study of 118 patients for the development of dislocation arthropathy following Bristow-Latarjet procedures.* **They reported** that 35% of shoulders developed mild dislocation arthropathy and 14% developed severe arthropathy. For those that developed severe arthropathy, first-time dislocation with age >23 years was the only risk factor for the development of severe arthropathy. They stressed the importance of having the same radiographic views to assess the incidence of dislocation arthropathy appropriately.

*Balg and Boileau performed a prospective case-control study of 131 patients with recurrent shoulder instability after arthroscopic stabilization.* **They reported** that patients under the age of 20 at the time of arthroscopic repair, contact sports, forced overhead activities, hyperlaxity, Hill-Sachs lesion on external rotation AP radiographs and loss of inferior glenoid contour were risks factors for recurrent dislocation. The authors developed a 10-point scoring system, in which patients scoring 6 or greater had an unacceptably high risk of recurrent dislocation (70%), **suggesting** these patients should be stabilized with an open procedure.

*Rollick et al. performed a systematic review of 28 studies consisting of 1652 repairs for recurrent shoulder dislocations.* **The authors reported** an estimated redislocation rate of 15.1% following arthroscopic stabilization, 7.7% following open Bankart repair, and 2.7% following Latarjet procedure with significantly higher complication rates seen following Latarjet stabilization. **The authors concluded** that regardless of the procedure there are similar subjective instability symptoms and radiographic arthritis at 5 years post-op.

Figure A is the AP external rotation view radiograph of the right shoulder with a Hill-Sachs lesion present (superiorly) and a normal glenoid contour. Illustration A is the ISS as proposed by Balg and Boileau. Illustration B is a clinical photograph demonstrating shoulder external rotation with >85° suggesting anterior shoulder hyperlaxity. Illustration C is a clinical photograph demonstrating shoulder hyperabduction suggesting inferior shoulder hyperlaxity, also known as the Gagey test (hyperabduction >120°).

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1-3, and 5: The patient has an ISS score of 5 for participation in a competitive sport (2 points), participation in contact sports (1 point), and Hill-Sachs visible on AP external rotation radiographs (2 points), age >20 years (0 points), normal shoulder laxity (0 points), normal glenoid contour on AP radiographs (0 points).
