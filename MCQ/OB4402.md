## Correct option

**3. Figure C**

## Answer explanation

Figure C shows a radiograph of a bipartite patella. The treatment of a symptomatic bipartite patella involves conservative treatment in the majority of cases.

Bipartite patellas are normal patellar variants representing a failure of fusion. They are often confused with patellar fractures. Bipartite patellas are characteristically located superolaterally and have rounded borders, unlike fractures. Patients with bipartite patellas may have similar findings on a contralateral knee radiograph.

*Atesok et al. looked at the treatment options for symptomatic bipartite patellas which can be a cause of anterior knee pain following trauma or a result of overuse or strenuous sports activity.* Although most patients improve with nonsurgical treatment, surgery should be considered when nonsurgical treatment fails. Surgical options can include excision of the fragment or lateral retinacular release.

*Oohashi et al. review the clinical features and classification of bipartite or tripartite patella.* *They based their classification based on both location and number of fragments.* Bipartite or tripartite patellae were classified by evaluating location and number of fragments. Out of 139 knees, 115 knees (83%) were classified as supero-lateral bipartite type, 16 (12%) were lateral bipartite type, 6 (4%) were supero-lateral and lateral tripartite type, and 2 (1%) were supero-lateral tripartite type.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Figure A shows a dislocated patella and would require reduction of the patella, and possible operative management of torn medial patellofemoral ligament and chondral injuries.
Answer 2: Figure B shows patella alta from patellar tendon rupture, which would require extensor mechanism repair.
Answer 4: Figure D shows a Segond fracture indicating a likely anterior cruciate ligament rupture. This would likely call for ACL reconstruction in a collegiate athlete.
Answer 5: Figure E shows a likely Giant cell tumor of the distal femur, which would require further imaging workup followed by biopsy and possible surgical management.
