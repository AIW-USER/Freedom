## Question

A 42-year-old weightlifter presents to you office with worsening right shoulder pain. Radiographs obtained today demonstrate end-stage glenohumeral arthritis. When discussing treatment options, which of the following is true regarding either an anatomic total shoulder arthroplasty (TSA) or a non-prosthetic glenoid shoulder arthroplasty ("Ream-and-run") in this aged cohort?

## Options

1. TSA will require a more extensive postoperative therapy
2. Ream-and-Run is contraindicated in patients with capsulorrhaphy arthropathy
3. Ream-and-Run is contraindicated in post-traumatic arthritis
4. Ream-and-Run provides patient with a level of activity beyond that recommended for a TSA
5. TSA has a greater rate of humeral loosening

## Correct option

**4. Ream-and-Run provides patient with a level of activity beyond that recommended for a TSA**

## Answer explanation

The Ream-and-Run procedure provides the patient with the opportunity for a level of activity beyond that recommended for a total shoulder arthroplasty (TSA).

A Ream-and-run procedure is a shoulder hemiarthroplasty with concentric glenoid reaming (non-prosthetic glenoid arthroplasty).It avoids the potential limitations associated with a prosthetic glenoid component seen with TSA. Additionally, it provides the patient with the opportunity for a level of activity beyond that recommended for a total shoulder arthroplasty and is ideal in a young active and motivated individual. With this procedure, the glenoid face is reamed to a single smooth concavity using a nubbed spherical reamer.

*Getz et al. reviewed hemiarthroplasty with concentric glenoid reaming (known as "Ream-and-Run") for treating glenohumeral arthritis in younger patients with a biconcave glenoid.* **They reported** that modest results were achieved with a hemiarthroplasty and concentric glenoid reaming in young patients with end-stage glenohumeral arthritis and a biconcave glenoid. **They concluded** that given the high revision rate and the percentage of patients who had unsatisfactory results, this procedure should be performed only after careful patient selection. Patients who are willing and able to undergo aggressive physical therapy focused on achieving early range of motion are more likely to have a satisfactory outcome after humeral hemiarthroplasty with concentric glenoid reaming.

*Somerson et al. reviewed the functional outcomes and clinical implant survival of patients that underwent a Ream-and-Run procedure.* **They reported** that 16% of the 176 patients had a subsequent procedure. **They concluded** that improvement in function and comfort derived from the ream-and-run procedure can be sustained at the time of mid-term follow-up.

*Matsen et al. reviewed the Ream-and-Run technique for shoulder arthroplasty.* **They reported** that a technically well done ream and run procedure can restore high levels of comfort and function to carefully selected patients with osteoarthritis, capsulorrhaphy arthropathy, and post-traumatic arthritis. **They concluded** that patients considering the Ream-and-Run procedure should understand that this technique avoids the risks and limitations associated with a polyethylene glenoid component, but that it requires strong motivation to follow through on a rehabilitation course that may require many months. The outcome of this procedure depends on the body's regeneration of a new surface for the glenoid and requires rigorous adherence to a daily exercise program.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Therapy is critical in obtaining a good clinical outcome for patients that undergo a Ream-and-Run
Answers 2&3: A well done ream and run procedure can restore high levels of comfort and function to carefully selected patients with osteoarthritis, capsulorrhaphy arthropathy, and post-traumatic arthritis
Answer 5: TSA carries the risk of prosthetic glenoid component failure that is avoided with the Ream-and-Run procedure
