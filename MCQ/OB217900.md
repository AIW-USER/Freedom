## Question

During preoperative planning for a cruciate-retaining total knee arthroplasty case, you're discussing the finer details of the component design with some junior residents. For your case, you plan on using Figure A instead of Figure B. What is an advantage of the implant shown in Figure A compared to Figure B?

![[q217900-b-screenshot-2022-08-21-160348.jpg]]

## Options

1. It is easier to make adjustments to polyethylene thickness after implantation
2. It provides a more stable knee to varus and valgus stress
3. It results in less osteolysis formation
4. It requires less bone removal for preparation and implantation
5. It has a more even load distribution on the proximal tibia

## Correct option

**3. It results in less osteolysis formation**

## Answer explanation

A monoblock polyethylene tibial component results in less osteolysis, likely secondary to the lack of backside wear which occurs in modular tibial systems.

Total knee arthroplasty components can be broadly categorized by intrinsic constraint, modularity, and fixation method. The tibial component can be a modular design meaning the polyethylene attaches to a separately implanted tibial tray, or it can be monoblock where the entire tibial component is a single implanted piece. The main benefit of the modular system is that the polyethylene can be exchanged without needing to remove the entire tibial component. However, this creates a second backside articulation and subsequent wear of the polyethylene. This extra amount of wear predisposes to osteolysis. A monoblock implant is often cheaper and has decreased osteolysis, however, it requires complete tibial revision if there are any issues with it.

*Callaghan et al. evaluated modular cruciate-retaining TKA with modular tibial trays at 20 years after implantation in 101 TKAs.* **They found** a 6% rate of revision, with all revisions resulting from polyethylene wear. Overall survival was 91%. 7 of 16 (43.8%) patients at the 20-year follow-up showed evidence of osteolysis on imaging.

*Long et al. reviewed outcomes in patients undergoing TKA who were under age 55 at the time of implantation.* **They found** that at 30 years of follow up monoblock components demonstrated lower rates of aseptic revision of the tibial or femoral components compared to modular (92.3% vs 68.3% rates of retention). Overall survivorship however was not different between groups (p=0.23). Additionally, the two most common reasons for revision overall were polyethylene wear and osteolysis.

Figure A shows a monoblock tibial component. Figure B demonstrates a modular tibial component of a TKA.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: A modular system is easier to exchange polyethylene for different sizes if needed.
Answer 2: Stability is based upon the design of how the femoral and tibial components articulate, and not if the tibial component is modular or monoblock.
Answer 4: Monoblock tibial components typically have the same intraoperative tibia preparation as modular systems.
Answer 5: Biomechanical studies have shown that load distribution is more even with modular tibial components. However, with equivalent long-term outcomes, the impact of this is likely not significant.
