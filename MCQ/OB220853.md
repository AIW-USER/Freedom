## Options

1. Bone marrow biopsy
2. MRI of the contralateral thigh to look for metastatic disease
3. An echocardiogram and a hearing screen
4. Lymph node biopsy
5. A pulmonary function test

## Correct option

**3. An echocardiogram and a hearing screen**

## Answer explanation

This patient presents with a moth-eaten, aggressive lesion about the left femur. The biopsy confirms osteosarcoma. The most common chemotherapy regimen includes methotrexate, doxorubicin, and cisplatin. An echocardiogram and hearing screen is required before starting these medications, as doxorubicin can cause cardiomyopathy and cisplatin can cause hearing loss (Answer 3).

Osteosarcoma is an aggressive bone cancer that most commonly affects adolescents during the time of peak growth velocity, but it can also affect older individuals. It typically occurs in the metaphysis of long bones, most commonly the distal femur, proximal tibia, and proximal humerus. Treatment is similar to other high-grade bone cancers and includes neoadjuvant chemotherapy for 10-12 weeks, wide resection, and adjuvant chemotherapy. The most common chemotherapy regimen includes methotrexate, doxorubicin, and cisplatin. Occasionally, ifosfamide or other agents are included. It is essential to understand the common side effects of these medications to effectively counsel patients and to facilitate further workup and treatment. Doxorubicin is known to cause cardiomyopathy, and cisplatin can cause hearing loss. As such, a baseline echocardiogram and hearing screen should be completed before starting chemotherapy.

*Geller & Gorlick authored a review of osteosarcoma.* **They highlight** the diagnosis and treatment strategies for osteosarcoma and touch on several future therapies currently being explored to improve survival.

*Meltzer & Helman also reviewed the diagnosis and management of osteosarcoma, and discussed recent advances in medical therapies for osteosarcoma.* **They highlight** that chemotherapy remains the mainstay of medical management, but immunotherapies and targeted T-cell therapies are also potentially promising therapies that may allow for more targeted and patient-specific treatment options.

Figure A shows an AP radiograph of the left femur and demonstrates a moth-eaten lesion. Note the Codman’s triangles, as well as the abnormal bone production within the medullary canal of the femur. Figures B and C are the corresponding MRI cuts, which show marrow replacement and the associated soft tissue mass of the lesion. Figure D is a histologic slide showing pleomorphic, atypical cells producing a lacy osteoid matrix, consistent with osteosarcoma.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Bone marrow biopsies are not required for workup for osteosarcoma, but often are obtained for Ewing’s sarcoma.
Answer 2: A PET-CT or bone scan, not a contralateral lower extremity MRI, is obtained for staging and to assess for metastatic disease.
Answer 4: Osteosarcoma rarely metastasizes to lymph nodes, and a lymph node biopsy is very seldom indicated. A PET-CT or bone scan may show reactive lymph nodes secondary to associated inflammation.
Answer 5: A pulmonary function test is not necessary before starting the standard chemotherapy regimen for osteosarcoma.
