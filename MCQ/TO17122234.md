Chapter: Congenital & Developmental Musculoskeletal Disorders MCQs — Muscular Dystrophies

## Question

All are true about Duchenne muscular dystrophy except

## Options

1. Positive Meryon sign
2. Dystrophin testing of the muscle biopsy specimen is 100% confirmatory
3. Steroid delay progression of scoliosis
4. Raised CPK

## Correct option

**2. Dystrophin testing of the muscle biopsy specimen is 100% confirmatory**

## Answer explanation

Duchenne muscular dystrophy:

Diagnosis: A dramatically elevated level of creatine kinase (50 to 100 times normal) and genetic testing of blood samples confirm the diagnosis. Muscle biopsy shows variations in fiber size, internal nuclei, split fibers, degenerating or regenerating fibers, and fibrofatty tissue deposition. Dystrophin testing of the muscle biopsy specimen will help confirm the type of muscular dystrophy but is not 100% confirmatory

Clinical features: onset is between 2 to 6 years. Proximal musculature weakens before the distal muscles. Common clinical features include the tendency to toe-walk; a widely based, lordotic stance; a waddling Trendelenburg gait; and a positive Gower test indicative of proximal muscle weakness. Calf pseudohypertrophy is caused by infiltration of the muscle by fat and fibrosis. The posterior tibial muscle retains its strength for the longest time, this causes equinovarus deformity of the foot. Eventually all voluntary muscles are affected including heart and lungs. Survival is uncommon beyond early thirties.

Gower sign- decrease in the ability to rise from the floor without assistance of the upper extremities.

Meryon sign: it is elicited by lifting the child with one arm encircling the child’s chest. Most children contract the muscles around the shoulder to increase shoulder stability and facilitate lifting. In children with muscular dystrophy, however, the arms abduct because of the lack of adductor muscle tone and severe shoulder girdle muscle weakness until the child eventually slides through the examiner’s arms unless the chest is tightly encircled.

Treatment: The use of prednisone and deflazacort has been shown to preserve or improve strength, prolong ambulation, and slow the progression of scoliosis. Orthopaedics treatment requires tendon lengthening, contracture release and spinal fusion to improve ambulation and posture.

Answer: 2
