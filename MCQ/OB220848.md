## Question

A 20-year-old male arrives at the trauma bay following a motor vehicle collision. He sustained a right open lateral subtalar dislocation, right proximal humerus fracture, and a closed right tibial plateau fracture. He is grossly neurovascularly intact in all extremities. When speaking with the patient about reducing his open subtalar dislocation, which of the following statements is true?

## Options

1. Lateral subtalar dislocations are more common
2. Medial subtalar dislocations are more commonly open
3. The most common complication is post-traumatic arthritis
4. The peroneal tendons commonly cause a block to reduction in lateral subtalar dislocations
5. The most common complication is osteonecrosis of the talus

## Correct option

**3. The most common complication is post-traumatic arthritis**

## Answer explanation

This patient sustained polytraumatic injuries following a high-speed motor vehicle collision. The most common complication following a subtalar dislocation is post-traumatic arthritis of the subtalar joint (answer choice 3).

Subtalar dislocations are rare (1% of all dislocations), hindfoot dislocations that commonly occur secondary to high-energy trauma. Diagnosis is made through a combination of history, physical examination, and radiographs. The midfoot can either dislocate medially (more common) or laterally. Up to 25% of these injuries are open, most commonly occurring with lateral dislocations. With medial subtalar dislocations, the foot becomes locked in supination, and the peroneal tendons, extensor digitorum brevis, and talonavicular joint capsule may become a block to reduction. With lateral dislocations, the foot becomes locked in pronation, and the reduction can become blocked by the posterior tibialis, flexor hallucis longus, or flexor digitorum longus tendons. Treatment depends on the presence of associated fractures and the ability to reduce these in a closed manner. Otherwise, operative treatment includes fixation of any associated fractures and open reduction if closed reduction is not possible.

*Sharma et al. wrote a JAAOS review on subtalar dislocations.* **They note** that medial subtalar dislocations are typically the result of low-energy mechanisms and are easier to reduce in a closed manner; whereas, lateral subtalar dislocations normally occur secondary to high-energy trauma, have associated foot injuries, and are more likely to require an open reduction. **They conclude** that post-traumatic arthritis is a common finding, even after anatomic reduction, and is a result of the initial cartilage injury.

*Lugani et al. wrote a narrative review on subtalar dislocations.* *They analyzed 47 articles (389 cases) within the last 30 years and found the incidence of medial subtalar dislocations was 68.1% vs. 27.7% for lateral dislocations.* **They also report** that lateral dislocations were more associated with open injuries (44.5%), associated lesions (44.5%), and the need for an open surgical reduction (48.2%) compared to others. **They conclude** that it's important to keep a high index of suspicion for increased difficulty and the need for an open reduction with lateral subtalar dislocations.

Incorrect answer choices:
Answer 1: Medial subtalar dislocations are more common than lateral dislocations due to the fibula acting as a buttress.
Answer 2: Lateral subtalar dislocations are more commonly associated with open injuries than medial dislocations.
Answer 4: The peroneal tendons are commonly a block to reduction in medial subtalar dislocations.
Answer 5: Osteonecrosis of the talus can occur following subtalar dislocations; however, post-traumatic arthritis is a more common complication following these injuries.
