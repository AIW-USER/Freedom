Chapter: Trauma MCQs — Fracture Of Distal End Radius

## Question

Post reduction acceptability criteria for fracture distal end radius are all except (FNB Pattern Paper 2017)

## Options

1. Radial shortening <5 mm
2. Dorsal tilt up to 7 degrees
3. Change in radial inclination up to 5 degrees
4. Articular step-off up to 2 mm

## Correct option

**2. Dorsal tilt up to 7 degrees**

## Answer explanation

Normal anatomy of distal end radius:

1. Volar (palmar) tilt: The angle between the longitudinal axis of radius and a line tangential to the slope of the dorsal-to-volar surface of the radius. Normal value is 11°.

 2. Radial inclination (RI): The angle between a line drawn perpendicular to the long-axis of radius and a line drawn from the tip of radial styloid process to the ulnar corner of distal end of the radius. This is normally 22°.

3. Radial length (RL): Radial styloid process is about 1-cm distal to the styloid process of ulna. Radial length is measured on the PA radiograph as the distance between two lines perpendicular to the long-axis of the radius. One line passes through the distal tip of the radial styloid and one line passes through the most distal point on the articular surface of the ulnar head. On an average it is 11 mm.

4. Ulnar variance (UV): It is the difference in levels of the distal articular surfaces of radius and ulna. Normal ulnar variance is negative, i.e. ulnar articular surface is slightly proximal to that of the articular surface of the radius. A positive ulnar variance means ulna projects more distally than radius.
