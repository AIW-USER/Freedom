## Question

A 12-year-old boy presents with difficulty walking for 5 days. He also complains of pain and stiffness in his back. The patient and his brothers recently suffered from a diarrheal illness with associated flu-like symptoms. On examination, the patient has asymmetric weakness affecting the bilateral lower extremities without evidence of sensory deficit. He subsequently develops flaccid paralysis and his disease process is attributed to an enterovirus. Which of the following is accurate concerning his infection?

## Options

1. Seroconversion rate of >90% of household contacts
2. Dorsal column of the spinal cord is affected
3. Majority of patients exposed will develop symptoms
4. Most patients who develop paralysis do so as a result of active infection
5. Older children (>5 years) are at higher risk of infection

## Correct option

**1. Seroconversion rate of >90% of household contacts**

## Answer explanation

The patient presents with paralytic polio infection with proximal, asymmetric muscle weakness in the absence of sensory deficits. The seroconversion rate after exposure to the poliovirus is 90 to 100% among individuals living in the same household (answer choice 1).

Polio is a viral illness affecting the anterior horn cells in the spinal cord and brain stem motor nuclei that can lead to muscular paralysis in approximately 1% of infected individuals. The disease is transmitted via the fecal-oral route, putting patients living in the same household at very high risk, with approximately 4-7 secondary infections expected from one patient with polio. Despite rapid spread, the vast majority (approximately 75%) of patients will remain asymptomatic or develop mild flu-like symptoms. When paralytic polio develops, patients will present with progressive weakness affecting the proximal musculature. Associated pain and stiffness are common, with 5-15% of individuals developing respiratory failure. Sensory function is not disturbed. The majority of patients with paralytic polio will have persistent lifelong motor dysfunction despite a transient infection, and up to 50% may develop postpolio syndrome.

*Pucchio et al. published a brief update on the poliovirus.* While the virus was thought to be eradicated, a case of vaccine-derived polio in an adult in 2022 was the second community transmission of the virus reported since 1979. **The authors note** that the oral polio vaccine is used internationally; individuals receiving the vaccine shed the virus in their stool for weeks, placing under-immunized contacts at risk of developing infection. **They report** a 3-6 day incubation period, with paralysis onset in 7–21 days in affected individuals. Clinical presentation varies widely, with a majority of individuals remaining asymptomatic. **They caution** the reader that the poliovirus should be considered in all cases of flaccid paralysis, even in the modern world.

*Martini et al. discuss the surprising reappearance of the poliovirus in some Western countries, with trace detection of polio derived from a vaccine strain in wastewater in the UK.* As a result of these reports, the CDC launched a campaign to reinitiate vaccination against polio in an effort to prevent infection by the wild-type and vaccine-derived forms of the virus. **The authors confirm** the CDC’s recommendation, stating that under-vaccinated individuals, particularly children, are at risk for infection and possible poliomyelitis.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: The poliovirus affects the anterior horn motor neurons of the spinal cord, as well as the motor nuclei of the brain stem. The dorsal columns are spared by the infection.
Answer 3: The majority of patients exposed (approximately 75%) will remain asymptomatic.
Answer 4: Patients with poliomyelitis are transiently affected by the virus. Post-polio syndrome is not a result of chronic infection, but rather concomitant aging of the neurologic system with prior polio insult, leading to progression of dysfunction.
Answer 5: Younger children (age <5) are at higher risk of infection.
