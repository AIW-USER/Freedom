# TO17099764

#TO #recon #thailiopsoasimpingement
Chapter: Arthroplasty MCQs — Complications of THR

## Question

3 months after total hip arthroplasty a patient complains of groin pain. X ray hip AP and lateral views, CBC, ER, CRP all are within normal limits. Active straight leg raise is painful. Pain increases with activity and walking, especially during hip extension. Appropriate next step for management would be (FNB Pattern Paper 2016)

## Options

1. Dynamic Hip MRI to rule out implant loosening
2. PET scan to rule out infection
3. Trochanteric bursa aspiration to rule out infection
4. Cross- table lateral view X-ray

## Correct option

**4. Cross- table lateral view X-ray**

## Answer explanation

Iliopsoas tendinitis is a cause of groin pain after THR. Diagnosis should be considered in patients complaining of pain in the groin during activities which require active hip flexion, such as walking upstairs, lifting the leg in and out of a car. Physical examinations include difficulty in raising the leg to the examination couch, painful SLR or not possible and passive ROM and passive SLR is pain free. Cross- table lateral view X-ray should be done to confirm prominence of the acetabular component over the anterior aspect of the rim, which may irritate the iliopsoas tendon. Painful snapping of iliopsoas tendon may also occur over the anterior lip of the cup in some cases.

Diagnosis: Cross- table lateral view X-ray should be done to confirm prominence of the acetabular component over the anterior aspect of the rim. Relief after USG/CT guided local anesthetic injection into iliopsoas tendon sheath can confirm the diagnosis.

Treatment: CT guided corticosteroid injection and stretching exercises. If conservative treatment fails then iliopsoas tenotomy relieves the symptoms.

Answer: 4
