## Question

A 60-year-old male sustains an injury to his lower extremity while playing soccer 4 weeks ago. Examination reveals the findings seen in the video. The diagnosis is confirmed with further imaging. What is the next best step?

## Options

1. Right tendon repair and possible graft augmentation
2. Left tendon repair and possible graft augmentation
3. Right VY advancement and possible turndown flap
4. Right proximal recession and end-to-end repair
5. Left tendon repair and possible hallux flexor tendon augmentation

## Correct option

**2. Left tendon repair and possible graft augmentation**

## Answer explanation

This patient has rupture of the left tibialis anterior (TA) tendon. Treatment involves primary tendon repair with possible graft augmentation.

Atraumatic tibialis anterior ruptures occur in older men >45years following eccentric loading against a plantar flexed ankle, such as with a missed step. Findings include: (1) pseudotumor at the anteromedial ankle, (2) loss of TA contour, (3) excessive firing of the EHL and EDL to dorsiflex the ankle (leading to hyperextension of the toes), (4) eversion of the foot on dorsiflexion because of unopposed EHL and EDL action (the foot normally inverts with dorsiflexion because of TA contraction), (5) steppage or foot slapping gait or toe dragging

**Sammarco et al. reviewed the treatment of 19 ruptures with repair**. Early and late repairs were performed at 3 days-6 weeks and 7 weeks-5 years, respectively. They performed direct repair in 4/8 and 3/11 early and late repairs, respectively, and interpositional autogenous tendon graft (plantaris, Achilles, EDL, or EHB) in 4/8 and 8/11 early and late repairs, respectively. Functional dorsiflexion and gait improvement was seen in 18/19 cases. They advocate surgical repair even for delayed cases.

*Kopp et al. retrospectively reviewed 10 operatively treated TA ruptures (5 primary repair, 3 with EHL transfer and 2 with EDL transfer).* *8/10 and 9/10 had improved pain and function respectively.* **They conclude** that operative repair gives a high satisfaction, but normal ankle dorsiflexion and hindfoot inversion strength may not return.

The Video shows classic findings of a left TA rupture. These include anterior pseudotumor, loss of TA contour, toe hyperextension on ankle dorsiflexion and hindfoot eversion on dorsiflexion.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The injury is on the LEFT.
Answer 3: VY advancement or turndown flaps are advocated for Achilles tendon rupture with gapping that cannot be successfully repaired primarily.
Answer 4: Proximal gastrocnemius recession and end-to-end TA repair is possible but should be undertaken for the LEFT leg.
Answer 5: FHL augmentation is used for chronic Achilles tendon ruptures. It is out of phase and not long enough for TA ruptures. TA ruptures may be augmented with extensor tendons (EHL and EDL).
