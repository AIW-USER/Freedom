## Question

## Options

1. Synovial sarcoma
2. Plantar fibromatosis
3. Angiosarcoma
4. Morton's Neuroma
5. Rhabdomyosarcoma

## Correct option

**1. Synovial sarcoma**

## Answer explanation

Based on the clinical, histological, and imaging studies, the most likely diagnosis would be synovial sarcoma of the right foot.

Synovial sarcoma is the most common soft-tissue sarcoma found in young adults and more commonly affects males than females. Patients typically present with an enlarging mass in the lower extremity. MRI will show a heterogeneous mass that is dark on T1 weighted images and bright on T2. Histology can show a high-grade lesion with monophasic spindle cells or biphasic appearance of both fibrous/spindle sarcomatous cells and epithelial cells. Treatment is wide excision +/-chemotherapy. It is not always common to have "high grade" features, e.g. anaplasia, mitoses, etc.

*Bakri et al. reviewed the common and uncommon manifestations of synovial sarcoma.* **They reported** that patients will usually present with a large, noninfiltrative, well-circumscribed mass adjacent to joints, often with punctuate calcifications seen on CT and x-ray.

Figure A shows AP and oblique X-rays of a left foot without any identifiable pathology. Figure B shows a T2 sagittal MRI with a hyperintense heterogeneous mass adjacent to the 1st MTPJ and T1 sagittal MRI on the right of the same cut where the mass is hypointense. Figure C shows a histologic slide with a high nuclear to cytoplasmic ratio and cellular atypia consistent with monophasic synovial sarcoma cells.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Plantar fibromatosis is a disease characterized by the development of painful fibrous nodules in the plantar fascia.
Answer 3: Angiosarcomas are rare malignancies and very aggressive tumors that derive from the endothelium of blood vessels. They more commonly affect elderly patients and rarely show heterogeneous masses on MRI.
Answer 4: Morton's neuromas result from perineural fibrosis with entrapment of the interdigital nerve typically between the 3rd and 4th digits of the foot.
Answer 5: Rhabdomyosarcomas are most common in children, but may present in adults. They manifest mostly in the head/neck, genitourinary system, or retroperitoneum.
