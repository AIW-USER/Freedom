## Question

You are asked to evaluate a 2 week old child referred from her pediatrician for an abnormal hip exam. You find her knees to be at different levels with the hips flexed to 90 degrees and adducted. The left hip makes a palpable clunk when moved from adduction to wide abduction. There are no other physical exam abnormalities. You order an ultrasound which confirms your diagnosis and you decide to place the child in a Pavlik harness. What is the next step?

## Options

1. Ultrasound in Pavlik immediately
2. Full-time Pavlik followed by ultrasound in Pavlik in 7-10 days
3. Night-time Pavlik followed by ultrasound in Pavlik in 7-10 days
4. Full-time Pavlik followed by ultrasound out of Pavlik in 7-10 days
5. Night-time Pavlik followed by ultrasound out of Pavlik in 7-10 days

## Correct option

**2. Full-time Pavlik followed by ultrasound in Pavlik in 7-10 days**

## Answer explanation

This patient has unilateral hip dysplasia with a dislocated but reducible (Ortolani positive) hip on one side. Initial treatment of choice is full time Pavlik harness. The next ultrasound exam should occur in approximately one week in order to confirm maintanance of reduction.

The Pavlik harness is a dynamic reduction tool. It utilizes the infant's active muscle contraction to promote hip reduction by holding the hips in flexion and abduction. As such, some time in the Pavlik is necessary before repeating an ultrasound exam to assess efficacy of treatment.

*Murnaghan et al. performed a retrospective review of 1218 patients who underwent Pavlik treatment for hip dysplasia and* **identified** 30 cases of femoral nerve palsy (2.5%). **They found** these palsies to be more likely to occur in older, larger patients with more severe dysplasia. This complication was predictive of failure of Pavlik treatment, as patients with femoral nerve palsy had a 47% success rate with Pavlik treatment, compared to 94% in randomly selected controls.

*Swaroop et al. describe two cohorts of Ortolani-positive patients treated for developmental dysplasia of the hip (DDH) over two separate 10 year periods.* The chronologically second cohort's treatment differed in the implementation of routine serial office-based ultrasonography in all patients and the use of a rigid abduction orthosis in patients whose hips remained unstable after 3 weeks of Pavlik treatment. This resulted in an improvement in treatment success from 85% to 93%.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: Immediate ultrasound will not add any diagnostic information. The efficacy of Pavlik harness treatment needs to be evaluated but only after it has had time to work.
Answers 3 & 5: Full-time treatment is indicated.
Answer 4: Ultrasound should be performed while the child is in the Pavlik harness.
