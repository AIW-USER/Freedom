## Question

A collegiate volleyball player is referred to you from her trainer following numerous ankle sprains. She has been treated with physical therapy and immobilization throughout her career, but has decided to stop playing volleyball to deal with this issue. Which of the following is true of the Gould modification to the Brostrom repair?

## Options

1. Constructed from the superior extensor retinaculum
2. Results in no biomechanical difference in ankle stability
3. Adds reconstruction of the posterior talofibular ligament
4. Increases incidence of iatrogenic nerve injury
5. Decreased ankle range of motion 6 weeks after surgery

## Correct option

**2. Results in no biomechanical difference in ankle stability**

## Answer explanation

A Brostrom procedure is the shortening and reinsertion of the anterior talofibular (ATFL) and calcaneofibular ligaments (CFL). The Gould modification, through numerous biomechanical studies, has been shown to have no difference in ankle stability postoperatively.

Low ankle sprains involving injury to the ATFL and CFL are common reason for missed athletic participation. After failure of conservative measures, reconstruction of the lateral ligamentous complex, also known as the Brostrom procedure, may be performed. The Gould modification to this surgery consists of reinforcing the repair with the inferior extensor retinaculum and distal fibular periosteum. No studies to date have demonstrated a statistically significant difference in initial ankle stability with inclusion of the Gould modification or augmentation of the repair. Regardless of the Gould modification, Brostrom anatomic reconstruction demonstrates good to excellent results in 90% of patients.

*Wainright et al. review in vivo tibiotalar kinematics in patients who underwent modified Brostrom-Gould reconstructions.* **They report** that surgical repair resulted in statistically significant decreases in anterior translation of the talus compared to kinematics before surgery. **They conclude** that the modified Brostrom-Gould repair results in improved joint motion compared to preoperative function.

*Behrens et al. performed a biomechanical analysis of Brostrom vs Brostrom-Gould repairs for ankle instability.* **They report** no significant difference between the stability of intact ankles and those of either repair. **They conclude** that there is no difference in ankle stability conferred by augmenting the traditional Brostrom repair with the Gould modification.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The Gould modification involves reinforcing the repair with the inferior extensor retinaculum.
Answer 3: The Brostrom-Gould procedure involves reconstruction of the ATFL and CFL.
Answer 4: There is no increased risk of iatrogenic nerve injury with the Gould modification.
Answer 5: No study has demonstrated a statistically significant difference in postoperative ankle motion with the addition of the Gould modification.
