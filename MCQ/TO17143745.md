Chapter: Orthopedics Oncology MCQs — Simple Unicameral Bone Cyst

## Question

A 5 years old patient has SBC in proximal humerus and has landed up with a pathological fracture. Next immediate management step should be (FNB 2022)

## Options

1. Conservative
2. Curettage with grafting
3. ORIF with plating
4. Nailing with grafting

## Correct option

**1. Conservative**

## Answer explanation

Ans: A

Simple bone cysts (SBCs) usually appear as a centrally located, radiolucent, well-marginated and slightly expansile lesion in the proximal metaphysis of long bones with humerus being the most common site.

The lesion can reliably be differentiated from its counterpart the Aneurysmal Bone Cyst (ABC) by getting an MRI. In MRI scans in SBC, non or only a few septae and fluid-fluid levels compared to aneurysmal bone cysts can be seen. Double density fluid level, septation, and low signal on T1 images and high signal on T2 images strongly suggest the bone cyst in question is an ABC, rather than an SBC.

Urgent biopsy is not indicated because the lesion appears benign radiologically and the histology of fracture callus (in cysts with pathological fractures) may be misinterpreted as osteosarcoma.

The majority of patients with SBCs are asymptomatic and the diagnosis is mainly incidental. In such scenario, the classical indication for treating a simple bone cyst is usually the risk of fracture. Pathological fractures may occur due to thinned and weakened cortex which can be predicted based on three parameters: the bone cyst index, the bone cyst diameter, and the minimal cortical thickness.

The bone cyst index (BCi) was described by Kaelin and MacEwen and is often used as an easy method to assess the mechanical resistance of the cyst wall. The BCi is obtained by dividing the cyst area by the diameter of the diaphysis squared (on either AP or Lateral view). SBCs with a BCi of more than 4 for the humerus and more than 3.5 for the femur are at high risk for fracture.

A second predictor for fracture is the bone cyst diameter (BCD), as reported by Ahn and Park; this is expressed as the percentage of bone occupied by the cyst in the transverse plane. SBC is considered to be at a high risk for fracture when the BCD reaches more than 85% in both the anteroposterior and lateral radiographs. A third predictor of fracturing is the minimal cortical thickness (MCT), with a MCT inferior to 2 mm being an indicator of a high fracture risk. Off late, these methods have been found to be NOT very accurate predictor of a future fracture due to low intra-observer and inter-observer reliability, however, BCi measurements (on MRI better than x ray) remain the best to opt.

Once the SBCs become symptomatic, pain caused by intercurrent pathological fractures is often the first symptom, and almost 63–87% of the cysts eventually land up with pathological fractures.

TREATMENT OPTIONS

Among the therapeutical options for symptomatic SBCs or those with impending fractures are simple observation, decompression of the cyst with screws or nails, intracavitary injection of steroids and autologous red bone marrow, intralesional curettage and defect reconstruction with bone substitute or elastic intramedullary nailing with or without use of bone marrow or bone substitute.

More invasive procedures, such as curettage, Rush rod fixation, or cannulated screw decompression, have been described but are rarely necessary for treatment of upper extremity cysts.

For a unicameral bone cyst with a pathologic fracture (in the proximal humerus specifically), treatment is immobilization in a plaster cast. Once the fracture heals, active SBC`s (cyst that communicates with the physis) can be treated with a methylprednisolone acetate injection if it hasn’t resolved spontaneously. Curettage and bone grafting should be considered if there is no spontaneous healing despite the injection. Cysts basically on being fractured, undergo an involutional process and heal on most occasions. If an initial period of immobilization is performed, internal fixation can usually be avoided. The exception is when the fracture is located in a high-stress weight-bearing area, such as the femur or the fracture is completely dislocated, joint affecting, unstable or open whereby surgical treatment is advisable. In symptomatic SBCs affecting the lower extremity also surgical treatment (internal fixation) is recommended in most cases with pathological fracture to regain the ability to mobilize timely.
