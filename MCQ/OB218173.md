## Question

## Options

1. 0-10º
2. 10-15º
3. 20-30º
4. 30-40º
5. 50-70º

## Correct option

**5. 50-70º**

## Answer explanation

Fifth metacarpal neck fractures without significant shortening or rotational deformity can tolerate large amounts of angulation in the range of 50-70º without operative intervention.

Small finger metacarpal neck fractures without rotational deformity are generally stable, usually do not require manipulation, and heal reliably after nonsurgical treatment, with good outcomes. Acceptable degrees of shaft shortening range from 2-5 mm, as long as there is no clinical scissoring indicative of rotational deformity. Operative treatment is typically indicated when these parameters are exceeded, in the setting of open or intra-articular fractures, and/or in a multiply injured hand with multiple metacarpal fractures.

*Strub et al. performed a prospective study comparing "intramedullary splinting" (k-wire fixation) and conservative treatment for displaced fractures of the little finger metacarpal neck.* *Forty patients were enrolled and* **the authors found** no statistically significant differences in range of motion or grip strength between the two groups with 30to 70º of angulation. **They concluded** that intramedullary splinting for displaced fractures of the little finger metacarpal neck offers an aesthetic, but not a functional advantage.

*Westbrook et al.* **showed** that the angulation did not affect the outcome of 105 metacarpal neck fractures or 113 metacarpal shaft fractures treated non-surgically without manipulation. The outcomes were equivalent to those seen in patients treated with surgery. **They concluded** that the DASH scores and the aesthetic outcomes were actually better for the fractures treated non-surgically than for the fractures treated surgically.

*Kollitz et al. reviewed metacarpal treatment and complications.* **The authors note** that while many metacarpal fractures do well without surgery, there is a paucity of literature and persistent controversy to guide the treating physician on the best treatment algorithm. While acceptable results can be expected for small finger metacarpal neck fractures with angulation as high as 70°, **they conclude** that rotation is poorly tolerated as it is magnified with flexion and often results in scissoring, which interferes with grip; thus, the surgeon must be well-versed in the variety of treatment options available and choose the most appropriate treatment according to each patient's presentation.

Figures A and B are PA and lateral radiographs of the hand demonstrating a fifth metacarpal neck fracture with an acceptable degree of angulation for non-operative management.
