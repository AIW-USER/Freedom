## Answer explanation

This patient experienced severe trunnionosis that resulted in metal debris accumulation in and around the hip joint. In this situation, the body’s respective immune response to the debris results in a lymphocyte (Figure B) mediated chronic inflammatory process.

Aseptic lymphocyte-dominant vasculitis-associated lesions (ALVAL; also referred to as pseudotumors) typically occur in one of two settings within total hip arthroplasty: (1) trunnionosis or (2) metal-on-metal (MOM) articulation hip replacements. The chronic inflammation within the hip joint and surrounding soft tissues results in debilitating pain, elevated serum ion levels (Co to Cr ratio on the order of approximately 5:1 in trunnionosis and approximately 1-2:1 in MOM hips), abductor deficiency, and instability. The goal of revision surgery is to remove the failing articulation where metal debris is being generated. This is either at the head-neck junction in trunnionosis or between the MOM femoral head and acetabular components. In either case, it is imperative to perform adequate soft tissue debridement to remove metal debris and devitalized tissue to prevent recurrence. In addition, conversion to ceramic-on-polyethylene articulation implants has been shown to impart the lowest risk for recurrence of metal debris generation from the trunnion.

*Vajapey and colleagues provided a review article on trunnions and modularity in total hip arthroplasty.* **They discuss** that the etiology of trunnionosis is multifactorial in nature, most commonly resulting from:
(1)Implant-related factors (high head-neck ratio, dissimilar metals, stems with low rigidity)
(2)Surgical-related factors (poor head-trunnion impaction, fluid left in trunnion during impaction)
(3)Patient-related factors (high BMI, increased cyclical loading of the joint)
The authors emphasize that once trunnionosis develops, it must be addressed with implant revision. They conclude by providing a treatment algorithm to assist intraoperative decision-making.

*Cooper and colleagues present a case series of patients experiencing clinically significant trunnionosis with metal-on-polyethylene implants.* **They reported** that irrigation, debridement, and head and liner exchange resulted in significant improvement in patient-reported clinical outcomes. **They noted** that some patients experienced abductor insufficiency from either the initial inflammatory process of trunnionosis or from the debridement. **The authors conclude** that the adverse local tissue reactions in trunnionosis are akin to those of metal-on-metal implant reactions.

Figure A demonstrates severe wear at the trunnion of a femoral stem implant. Figure B demonstrates a cell with large spherical nuclei and condensed chromatin, consistent with a lymphocyte. Figure C demonstrates a white blood cell with multi-lobular nuclei, consistent with a neutrophil. Figure D demonstrates arrows pointing to sickled red blood cells. Figure E demonstrates arrows pointing to platelet cells. Figure F demonstrates a large, round cell that contains a central round nucleus and vacuolated cytoplasm, consistent with a macrophage.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Neutrophils are mediators of acute inflammation, particularly in the setting of peri-prosthetic joint infection.
Answer 3: Sickle cell anemia is associated with intraosseous blood clots which serve as a potential cause of avascular necrosis of the femoral head.
Answer 4: Platelets are a mediator of deep vein thrombosis after total hip arthroplasty.
Answer 5: Macrophages are mediators of osteolysis as a result of polyethylene debris, not metal debris.
