## Question

What is not true for thromboembolism prophylaxis (FNB Pattern Paper 2016)

## Options

1. Aspirin is recommended as prophylaxis
2. Rivaroxaban is not recommended after hip fracture surgery
3. Routine coagulation monitoring is not necessary for fondaparinux
4. Patients for total hip replacement have moderate risk for venous thromboembolism.

## Correct option

**4. Patients for total hip replacement have moderate risk for venous thromboembolism.**

## Answer explanation

VTE prophylaxis

Risk stratification: The ACCP (2008) classified in-hospital orthopedic patients who have undergone hip or knee arthroplasty or sustained a hip fracture, major trauma or SCI as high risk patients for venous thromboembolism.

Pharmacologic VTE prophylaxis:

- Aspirin: The American association of orthopedic Surgery (AAOS) guidelines (2007) and the 2012 ACCP guidelines do recommend the use of aspirin as VTE prophylaxis for patients undergoing THR, TKR or hip fracture surgery. The SIGN guidelines (2010, updated in 2015) are clearer about aspirin. According to SIGN, ‘as other agents are more effective for prevention of VTE, aspirin is not recommended as the sole pharmacological agent for VTE prophylaxis in orthopedic patients.
- Vitamin K antagonists: VKAs are recommended by the latest ACCP guidelines for VTE prophylaxis in patients undergoing THR, TKR or hip fracture surgery. A therapeutic INR range of 2.0 to 3.0 with a target INR of 2.5 is recommended rather than a lower or higher range.
- Unfractionated heparin: UFH is recommended by the latest ACCP guidelines for VTE prophylaxis in patients undergoing THR, TKR or hip fracture surgery, but they have been replaced by LMWH in all clinical applications. When administered in therapeutic doses, the anticoagulant effect of UFH is usually monitored using the activated partial thromboplastin time (aPTT or APTT).
- LMWH: low molecular weight heparin is better for DVT prophylaxis in TKR compared with warfarin or aspirin or UFH. LMWH should be considered the gold standard for VTE prophylaxis in orthopedic patients. These are given subcutaneously for 10-14 days. The activated partial thromboplastin time (aPTT) does not need to be monitored. Immune-mediated thrombocytopenia is not associated with short-term use of low-molecular-weight heparin, and the risk of heparin-induced osteoporosis may be lower than the risk with the use of standard heparin.
- Fondaparinux: (anti-Xa inhibitor). The dose of fondaparinux is 2.5 mg daily for VTE prophylaxis. Due to the predictable pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic profile of fondaparinux, routine coagulation monitoring is not necessary.

- Rivaroxaban: Rivaroxaban is an orally administered direct inhibitor of activated factor X (Xa).It is administered in a fixed daily oral dose of 10 mg FOR 14 days or more for VTE prophylaxis after elective THR and TKR. Rivaroxaban is currently not recommended for hip fracture surgery.

- Apixaban: Apixaban is a direct factor Xa inhibitor that has been approved for VTE prophylaxis following THR and TKR but not for hip fracture surgery. The current recommended dosage for VTE prophylaxis after THR and TKR is 2.5 mg twice daily starting 12 to 24 hours after surgery and continuing for 35 days for THR and 12 days for TKR. Currently it is not recommended for hip fracture surgery.
