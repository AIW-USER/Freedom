Chapter: Trauma MCQs — Terrible Triad Of Elbow

## Question

Which of the following describes the biomechanical rationale for prioritizing the repair of even a small (Regan-Morrey Type I) coronoid fracture in the context of a Terrible Triad injury?

## Options

1. The coronoid tip serves as the primary attachment for the brachialis muscle, preventing elbow flexion weakness.
2. Coronoid repair is required to prevent the development of heterotopic ossification in the brachialis muscle.
3. It restores the anterior bony buttress and the insertion of the anterior capsule to prevent posterior subluxation
4. Fixing the coronoid is necessary to provide an isometric point for the later reconstruction of the LUCL

## Correct option

**3. It restores the anterior bony buttress and the insertion of the anterior capsule to prevent posterior subluxation**

## Answer explanation

Answer: 3
The coronoid process, pyramidical in shape, forms the anterior part of the greater sigmoid notch and accounts for about 60% of the anterior surface of the articulation with the humerus. It provides a bony buttress to posteriorly directed translation forces to the ulna, while it carries the attachment site for the anterior capsule (at an average of 5.9mm from coronoid tip), the brachialis tendon (volar base of coronoid) and the anterior bundle of the MCL (sublime tubercle).

 The coronoid and its associated capsular attachments act as the primary constraint against posterior translation of the ulna relative to the humerus.

If the fragment is large, use an anterior-to-posterior screw. If it is a small tip avulsion (common), use a suture anchor or a "lasso" technique through the anterior capsule. Coronoid fixation restore the anterior buttress to prevent posterior subluxation.
