## Question

A 42-year-old female presents with a cavovarus deformity that has caused recurrent ankle sprains and a recent 5th metatarsal base fracture. After a discussion with the patient, the decision is made for surgical correction of her foot deformity to prevent further complications. On examination, the patient's hindfoot varus completely corrects with Coleman block testing. Radiographs reveal no arthritic changes in the tibiotalar, subtalar, or midfoot joints. Which of the following should be included in the surgical plan?

## Options

1. Triple arthrodesis
2. Medial calcaneal slide osteotomy
3. Dorsiflexion osteotomy of the 1st ray
4. Lateral column lengthening
5. FDL transfer to the posterior tibial tendon

## Correct option

**3. Dorsiflexion osteotomy of the 1st ray**

## Answer explanation

A dorsiflexion osteotomy of the 1st ray should be included in the surgical plan for this patient because the cavovarus deformity is forefoot driven.

Cavovarus foot deformity can be the result of neurologic or traumatic disorders. Often, there is a lack of muscular tone of the tibialis anterior and increased force through the posterior tibial tendon with Achilles tendon shortening. Surgical treatment depends on the rigidity of the hindfoot deformity and the presence of arthritic changes in the affected joints. Patients with a flexible hindfoot (hindfoot corrects with Coleman block test) should be treated with a forefoot procedure consisting of 1st ray dorsiflexion osteotomy.

*Krause et al. reviewed neuromuscular issues in cavovarus foot deformities.* **The authors stated** patients may present with sequelae related to the deformity, such as calluses, stress fractures, claw toes, metatarsalgia, ankle instability, and peroneal tendonitis. **They emphasized** surgical treatment should focus on releasing soft tissue contractures, bone deformity correction, muscle balance optimization, and prevention of recurrence.

*Younger and Hansen reviewed adult cavovarus foot deformities.* **They highlighted** that lateral border overload can lead to multiple secondary issues including peroneal tendonitis, stress fractures, and recurrent ankle instability. **They stated** muscle imbalance can be corrected with tendon transfer, corrective osteotomies, and fusion.

Incorrect answers
Answer 1: Triple arthrodesis is reserved for patients with rigid or arthritic deformities.
Answer 2: A medial calcaneal slide osteotomy should be performed on a patient with a flat foot deformity and hindfoot valgus.
Answer 4: Lateral column lengthening is performed on patients with excessive forefoot abduction with pes planovalgus.
Answer 5: FDL transfer to the posterior tibial tendon is a reconstruction technique for posterior tibial tendon insufficiency.
