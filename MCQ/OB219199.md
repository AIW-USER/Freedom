## Question

A 17-year-old male is brought to the emergency department after a motorcycle crash with an obvious deformity to his right leg. Radiographs confirm a grade III open midshaft tibia fracture. As the orthopaedic resident on-call, what should you ensure is the first thing done for this patient?

## Options

1. IV antibiotic administration
2. Superficial irrigation and debridement in trauma bay
3. Fracture immobilization
4. CT angiogram of right lower extremity
5. Formal irrigation and debridement and tibial intramedullary nailing

## Correct option

**1. IV antibiotic administration**

## Answer explanation

IV antibiotic administration within the first hour of hospital admission is the most important factor in decreasing infection in the setting of open fracture.

Open fractures are usually associated with high-energy trauma and are often associated with other injuries. They are graded based on the degree of overlying soft tissue injury. Open fractures are associated with a higher rate of infection compared to closed fractures. Infection rate can be decreased with antibiotic administration, especially within 1 hour of hospital admission. Prompt irrigation and debridement (I&D) as well as fracture stabilization/soft tissue coverage are also important factors in managing these injuries.

*Lack et al. looked at the relationship between the timing of antibiotic administration and the rate of deep infection in type III open tibia fractures.* **They found** that a > 1-hour delay in antibiotic administration and >5 days to wound coverage were the two factors that independently predicted infection. The rate of infection was 2.8% when antibiotics were given within 1 hour, 10% when they were not, and 40.5% when antibiotics were not given and the wound was not covered within 5 days.

*Pollak et al. examined the time to surgical debridement and its effect on infection incidence after open lower extremity trauma.* **They noted** that earlier admission to a trauma center was the primary factor that decreased infection risk. They presumed that this meant antibiotics may have been given earlier to these patients compared to the group in which antibiotic administration was delayed.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 2-5: Superficial I&D, fracture splinting, and eventual tibial intramedullary nailing with formal debridement are all important steps, but antibiotic administration should be performed immediately within 1 hour of presentation to the hospital. CT angiogram is not necessary without overt concern for vascular injury.
