## Question

A 32-year-old male injures his right foot after he fell off a ladder. He notices immediate pain and the inability to bear weight. There is no soft tissue disruption, but significant swelling. The fracture is closed reduced and splinted in the trauma bay. Which of the following factors potentially improve the patient's long-term outcomes following treatment of this injury?

![[q218569-a-screen-shot-2022-12-21-at-7-56-02-am.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Surgical treatment within 48 hours of injury
2. Use of minimally invasive techniques
3. Primary bone healing at the articular surface
4. Early weight bearing protocol
5. Use of relative stability construct at the articular surface

## Correct option

**3. Primary bone healing at the articular surface**

## Answer explanation

Achieving primary bone healing with anatomic reduction of the articular surface through absolute stability constructs is necessary for improved long-term outcomes.

Talus fractures are typically the result of high-energy mechanisms with the hindfoot in supination or pronation. Post-traumatic arthritis of the subtalar joint is common following these injuries and can be quite debilitating. When the patient's soft tissues are stable, fixation of the fracture to achieve an anatomic reduction of the articular surface minimizes the risk of post-traumatic arthritis.

*Vints et al. retrospectively studied the long-term outcomes of 84 patients treated surgically for talus fractures.* **They reported** 41% of patients did return to normal activities, 56% complication rate, and radiographic arthritic changes correlated with poor function. **They concluded** talus fractures negatively affect patients' long-term quality of life and articular anatomic reduction should be achieved to reduce the risk of post-traumatic arthritis.

*Fortin and Balazsy reviewed the evaluation and treatment of talus fractures.* **They stated** outcomes are dependent on the degree of fracture displacement and articular comminution. **They recommended** surgical treatment for all displaced fractures.

Incorrect answers
Answer 1: Early treatment of talus fracture has not been associated with improved long-term outcomes.
Answer 2: Minimally invasive techniques are not associated with improved functional outcomes.
Answer 4: Early weight-bearing protocols are not associated with improved long-term outcomes.
Answer 5: Relative stability constructs result in secondary bone healing which is not ideal for articular fractures.
