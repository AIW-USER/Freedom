## Question

A 5-year-old male presents for follow-up of infantile idiopathic scoliosis that has been treated with serial Mehta-Cotrel casts. His current radiographs are depicted in figures A and B. His lung function is characterized by FEV1/FVC of 0.80 which is decreased from 0.88 one year ago. What would be the ideal treatment to address the patient's scoliosis and lung function at this time?

## Options

1. Continue Mehta-Cotrel casting
2. Switch to thoracolumbosacral orthosis at least 13 hours per day
3. Continue to monitor curve and refer to pulmononology for bronchodilator treatment since lung function is unlikely to decline
4. Traditional or magnetic growing rod construct
5. T2-pelvis posterior instrumented fusion

## Correct option

**4. Traditional or magnetic growing rod construct**

## Answer explanation

The patient has a significant coronal and sagittal deformity of the spine at a young age that is consistent with early-onset scoliosis. Treatment with a growing rod construct will allow for continued spinal growth and improved lung function by allowing improved thoracic volume and development.

Infantile idiopathic scoliosis is defined as scoliosis that is present before the age of 3. This is considered to be a domain of early-onset scoliosis and carries significant treatment implications. Uncontrolled curve progression can impair thoracic and lung development leading to thoracic insufficiency syndrome. Typically, the lungs develop by increasing the number of alveoli before the age of 8. Severe scoliosis can lead to restrictive lung disease and possibly obstructive disease in later stages. Obstructive disease characteristics are theorized to result from impingement or constriction of the mainstem bronchus by the thoracic deformity. As such, early treatment consists of growth-friendly techniques including casting and bracing. However, patients that are refractory may require a growing-rod construct that allows for serial lengthening to facilitate lung and thoracic development. Definitive fusion is delayed until 10 years of age with a goal of 22 cm of thoracic growth. Implementation of this treatment has been shown to increase forced vital capacity (FVC) alone.

*Jiang et al. performed a retrospective study of eight patients that were treated with growing rod constructs for early-onset scoliosis.* **The authors reported** patients who had undergone lengthening and definitive fusion or were in the process of lengthening had a significant increase in forced vital capacity (FVC) that was not dependent on radiographic changes.

*Redding et al. performed a retrospective study of persistent and progressive airway obstruction of 49 patients with early-onset scoliosis.* **They reported** 54% of patients demonstrated progressive airway obstruction with a significant decline in FEV1/FVC over a 6 year period and was inversely correlated with coral curve magnitude. **The authors concluded** progressive airway obstruction in early-onset scoliosis may require new treatment strategies to prevent declining lung function over time.

Figures A and B are the AP and lateral radiographs of the spine with a significant left-sided thoracolumbar curve and thoracic kyphosis.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: The patient has progressive scoliosis despite Mehta-Cotrel casting with declining lung function. Continuing with this treatment is likely to lead to a continued decline in the patient's lung function and worsening of his curve during the critical years of lung development.
Figure 2: Transitioning into a TLSO brace would be less effective at preventing the patient's curve from progressing than Mehta-Cotrel casting.
Figure 3: The patient does have pulmonary function tests that are consistent with obstructive lung disease. However, the obstruction is likely from mainstem bronchus constriction from poor thoracic cavity development.
Figure 5: Performing a definitive posterior instrumented fusion is likely required for the patient, however, this is deferred until the patient is at least 10 years old. This allows for the lungs to continue developing, which is ideally achieved with 22 cm of T1-12 height.
