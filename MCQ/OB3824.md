## Question

A 55-year-old male construction worker sustained a left foot injury while working on the job 6 months ago. He has had persistent foot pain and swelling since the time of injury. He is currently taking prescription narcotics for symptom relief. His examination reveals a mild planus deformity with generalized tenderness over the midfoot and forefoot. Radiographs of the foot are shown in Figure A. What would be the most appropriate treatment?

![[q3824-a-stress.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Return to work without restriction
2. Physiotherapy, orthotics and non-opioid analgesics
3. Cast immobilization and weight-bearing as tolerated for 6 weeks
4. Open reduction and internal fixation
5. Open reduction and primary arthrodesis

## Correct option

**5. Open reduction and primary arthrodesis**

## Answer explanation

This patient presents more than 6 months after a Lisfranc injury with continued instability and arch collapse. The most appropriate treatment at this stage would be open reduction and primary arthrodesis.

The indication for primary fusion after a Lisfranc injury include severe intra-articular comminution, complete ligamentous dislocations, delayed treatment, and/or chronic deformity (most commonly, planus or planovalgus deformities associated with forefoot abduction). Treatment may also include bone autograft to address structural bone loss. Defects may be addressed with tricortical bone graft from the ipsilateral iliac crest, and less commonly with local bone graft.

*Ly et al. performed a prospective, randomized study comparing primary arthrodesis with open reduction and internal fixation for primarily ligamentous Lisfranc joint injuries.* *In 41 patients,* **they found** that primary arthrodesis of the medial two or three rays appears to have a better short and medium-term outcome than open reduction and internal fixation of ligamentous Lisfranc joint injuries.

*Rammelt et al.* **found** that primary treatment by open reduction and internal fixation of tarsometatarsal fracture-dislocations leads to improved functional results, earlier return to work and greater patient satisfaction than secondary corrective arthrodesis. However, **they conclude** that arthrodesis remains a useful salvage procedure providing significant relief of pain and improvement in function in delayed presentations.

*Sangeorzan et al. reported on sixteen patients with fracture-dislocations of the Lisfranc joint who failed initial treatment and were salvaged by arthrodesis.* **They state** that best outcomes were dependent on accurate reduction and early treatment of these injuries.

Figure A shows a Lisfranc injury with widening of the first and second TMT joint with stress views. Illustration A shows arthrodesis of the first, second and third tarsometatarsal joints using cortical lag screws after failed ORIF of a ligamentous Lisfranc injury.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1 and 2: Non-operative treatment would be considered in patients unable to undergo operative intervention relating to medical co-morbidities.
Answer 3: Cast immobilization would be appropriate for a diabetic who is insensate or patients whose medical conditions preclude surgery.
Answer 4: Most fracture-dislocations of the Lisfranc joint are best treated with formal open reduction and internal fixation. However, late presenting injuries are best managed with primary arthrodesis.
