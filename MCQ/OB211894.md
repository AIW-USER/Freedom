## Question

A 72-year-old 2-pack-per-day smoker presents to your office with increasingly severe right hip pain that is now exacerbated by any weight-bearing. Physical exam is significant an antalgic gait but otherwise a full painless range of motion, good strength, and an absence of neurologic symptoms. A radiograph is shown in Figure A. ## Options

1. Acral involvement
2. High rate of complications with intralesional treatment requiring revision surgery
3. Hypervascularity requiring pre-operative embolization
4. Sensitivity to radiation and cytotoxic chemotherapy
5. Variable median life expectancy, potentially as short as 6 months

## Correct option

**4. Sensitivity to radiation and cytotoxic chemotherapy**

## Answer explanation

The radiographic imaging and histology are consistent with a diagnosis of metastatic renal cell carcinoma (RCC). RCC is generally resistant to radiation and cytotoxic chemotherapy.

RCC is associated with bony metastases in nearly half of the cases. Similar to lung cancer, RCC is associated with primarily lytic lesions and may demonstrate acral involvement secondary to arterial tree metastases. RCC is relatively resistant to radiation and cytotoxic chemotherapy. As a result, solitary bony lesions are often managed with en bloc excision and endoprosthetic reconstruction. A cemented long-stem prosthesis should be used due to the progressive natures of the disease. Intralesional treatment has a high risk of disease progression and complications, often necessitating revision surgery. Impending pathologic fracture with multiple sites of synchronous metastases may be treated with prophylactic stabilization of the whole bone. However, pathologic fractures have demonstrated a moderate healing potential (44%) which is on par with most other metastatic carcinomas. RCC has a variable median life expectancy, though often comparable to that of non-small-cell lung carcinoma with as short as 6mo.

*Quraishi et al. evaluated the effect of embolization of metastatic RCC causing spinal cord compression on the intra-operative blood loss and surgical outcomes.* Blood loss was on average 1.7L, and **the authors found** no difference between immediate versus delayed surgery following embolization or grade of embolization on blood loss. Instead embolization was paradoxically associated with a trend toward increased blood loss. **The authors concluded** that this may be the result of rebound reperfusion or simply more extensive tumor burden in these patients.

*Johnson et al. review the contemporary management of appendicular skeletal metastases.* *The authors discuss and compare the most common primary tumors, including RCC.* **They note** that skeletal metastases are most often lytic, and that acral involvement is pathognomonic of both renal and lung carcinomas. Pathologic fractures have been shown to have a moderate change of healing, however given that RCC is relatively insensitive to both radiation and chemotherapy, **the authors recommend** that solitary bony lesions be segmentally resected with subsequent endoprosthetic reconstruction. Due to the hypervascularity of the lesions, pre-operative embolization is emphasized so as to mitigate intraoperative blood loss.

Figure A is an AP pelvic radiograph demonstrating synchronous poorly-circumscribed lytic lesions of the right proximal femur. Figure B is a histologic specimen demonstrating pathology consistent with clear cell renal carcinoma.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Acral involvement is rare but characteristic of both RCC and metastatic adenocarcinoma of the lung as a result of arterial tree metastases.
Answer 2: Due to the aggressive and progressive nature of the disease, intralesional treatment is associated with a high risk of complications and revision surgery.
Answer 3: Pre-operative embolization is recommended in general for renal and thyroid carcinoma as a result of the significant vascularity which may otherwise cause substantial intra-operative bleeding.
Answer 5: RCC has a variable median life expectancy (6mo - 4yr), though often considered to be the second worst and only marginally better than that of non-small-cell lung carcinoma (which is cited at as short as 6mo in the setting of skeletal metastases).
