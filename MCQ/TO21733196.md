## Question

In safe surgical dislocation of hip joint which capsulotomy is done

## Options

1. Anterolateral and anteroinferior capsulotomy and hip is dislocated anteriorly
2. posterolateral and posteroinferior capsulotomy and hip is dislocated posteriorly
3. Anterolateral and anteroinferior capsulotomy and hip is dislocated posteriorly
4. Anteromedial and anterosuperior capsulotomy and hip is dislocated anteriorly

## Correct option

**1. Anterolateral and anteroinferior capsulotomy and hip is dislocated anteriorly**

## Answer explanation

Ans: A

EXPLANATION: -

To prevent damage to the deep branch of the medial femoral circumflex artery, in safe surgical dislocation the capsule is first incised anterolaterally along the long axis of the femoral neck since incision in this area avoids injury to the deep branch of the MFCA. An anteroinferior capsular incision is then made. The capsulotomy must remain anterior to the lesser trochanter in order to avoid damage to the main branch of the MFCA, which lies just superior and posterior to the lesser trochanter. Elevation of the anteroinferior flap allows visualisation of the labrum. The first capsular incision is then extended towards the acetabular rim where it is sharply turned posteriorly parallel to the labrum reaching the retracted tendon of piriformis. Care must be taken not to damage the labrum. The hip can now be dislocated anteriorly by flexing and externally rotating the hip joint.
