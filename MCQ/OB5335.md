## Question

A 4-year-old girl presents with left foot turning in since she began to walk at 13 months. Hip range of motion is 60 degrees of external rotation and 40 degrees of internal rotation bilaterally. The measurement depicted in Figure A is recorded as -10 degrees on the Left and +5 degrees on the Right. What is the most appropriate treatment at this time?

![[q5335-a-thigh-foot-axis-2-moved.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Derotational supramalleolar tibial osteotomy
2. Observation and parental education
3. Bracing
4. Serial casting
5. Derotational femoral osteotomy

## Correct option

**2. Observation and parental education**

## Answer explanation

The patient has in-toeing secondary to internal tibial torsion as evidenced by her thigh-foot-angle measurements. Observation and parental education is the most appropriate treatment for internal tibial torsion in young patients, as most cases spontaneously resolve by 6 to 8 years of age.

Internal tibial torsion is the most common cause of in-toeing gait. Other potential causes include femoral anteversion and metatarsus adductus. The exact etiology of internal tibial torsion is unknown, but is thought to be due to intra-uterine positioning or extensive tightness of the medial ligamentous structures of the knee.

*Kling et al review the angular and torsional malalignments of the lower extremity in children.* **The authors note** that calcaneovalgus foot, internal tibial torsion, genu varum, genu valgus, and femoral anteversion are normally observed in infants and are usually corrected with growth.

*Staheli et al present the method of calculating the transmalleolar axis for assessment of tibial torsion in which the child is seated and the position of the malleoli are marked against against a backboard.* **The authors conclude** that the transmalleolar axis averages about 5 degrees of external rotation in the first year, 10 degrees during mid-childhood, and 14 degrees in older children and adults.

Figure A demonstrates the measurement of the thigh-foot-angle. The test is performed with the patient in the prone position and the knees flexed to 90-degrees. The angle is measured by line bisecting the foot relative to a line bisecting the thigh.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1 - Derotational supramalleolar tibial osteotomy is performed in rare cases of internal tibial torsion in symptomatic adolescent patients with a thigh-foot-angle of >15 degrees.
Answer 3 - Bracing does not alter the course of internal tibial torsion.
Answer 4 - Serial casting is a treatment for rigid metatarsus adductus, but is not used for tibial torsion.
Answer 5 - Derotational femoral osteotomy is a treatment for femoral anteversion, which is another possible cause of in-toeing gait. This surgical option is indicated for symptomatic adolescent patients with <10 degrees of external rotation of the hip.
