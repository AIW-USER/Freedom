## Question

An 18-year-old girl comes to your office complaining of deep, ill-defined bilateral knee pain for years. The pain becomes worse during long car rides. It is also worse when getting out of a deep-seated position and descending stairs. She recalls no injury. She was seen by her PCP and sent for 4 weeks of physical therapy focusing on closed chain quadriceps strengthening exercises, but the pain has persisted. She has a normal ligamentous examination with no evidence of meniscal pathology and no effusion. Her images are shown in figure A. What is the next step in management?

## Options

1. Continued physical therapy with addition of hip exercises
2. Corticosteroid injection
3. MRI of the knees
4. Diagnostic arthroscopy
5. Patellar taping

## Correct option

**1. Continued physical therapy with addition of hip exercises**

## Answer explanation

Low impact exercise-based physical therapy focusing on the quadriceps and hips is most effective in treating patellofemoral syndrome.

Patellofemoral pain is an extremely common disorder, especially in female athletes. There is, however, no true consensus as to the cause and appropriate treatment for patellofemoral pain. Evaluation of a patient with patellofemoral pain requires a physical examination and plain radiographs. In patient evaluation it is imperative to assess the rotational alignment of the tibia and femur - miserable alignment syndrome, which may result in patellofemoral pain, results from increased femoral anterversion and excessive tibial external rotation. Appropriate examination of all structures around the knee is critical to rule out other diagnoses. An MRI is useful for evaluating intra-articular or intra-osseous lesions, if clinical suspicion is suggestive of this. Treatment is predominantly conservative, with focus on low impact exercises that maximize aerobic conditioning.

*Petersen et al. perform a review of the literature on patellofemoral pain syndrome.* **They note** that it is predominantly an adolescent female syndrome possibly resulting from dynamic valgus - defined as an abnormal movement pattern visually characterized by excessive medial movement of the lower extremity during weight bearing. They add that the treatment of patellofemoral syndrome is almost exclusively non-operative.

*van der Heijden et al. performed a systematic review of the literature on exercise therapy for treating patellofemoral syndrome.* **They note** that the overall quality of the literature is lacking. This review found consistent evidence that exercise therapy results in a clinically important reduction in pain and improvement in functional ability, as well as enhancing long-term recovery.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2-5: These are not indicated at this time. Patellar taping can be considered as an adjuvant to the treatment of patellofemoral syndrome, but literature has consistently shown that exercise-based therapy to be most efficacious in recovery.
