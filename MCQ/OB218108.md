## Question

A 62-year-old male presents with chronic bilateral knee pain after being referred by his primary care physician with imaging shown in Figure A. He states the right side is much worse than the left. The patient endorses having made considerable life changes over the last year and now regularly exercises and has lost 30 lbs. Other than hypertension, he denies any past medical history. He is taking oral meloxicam 7.5mg twice per day with added 650mg acetaminophen as needed. He has received three corticosteroid injections over the past 2 years with the most recent being three weeks ago. The patient states his most recent injection only provided improvement for approximately 10 days. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step in management?

![[q218108-a-figure-a.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Refer patient to a durable medical equipment store for lateral shoe inserts
2. Discontinue his current ibuprofen and Tylenol usage and convert to topical NSAIDs
3. Prescribe oral tramadol every 4-6 hours as needed
4. Provide another intra-articular corticosteroid injection in clinic today
5. Engage in discussion of the risks and benefits of total knee arthroplasty

## Correct option

**5. Engage in discussion of the risks and benefits of total knee arthroplasty**

## Answer explanation

This patient has bilateral genu valgus alignment with significant osteoarthritic changes on the right side compared to the left. He has failed a reasonable attempt at nonoperative management and is therefore indicated to undergo a right total knee arthroplasty (TKA).

Osteoarthritis of the knee is a progressive degenerative condition that can result in functionally limiting pain affecting activities of daily living. Patients may be predisposed to this condition due to prior traumatic knee injuries (i.e. meniscal injuries, collateral ligament injuries, osteochondral defects), increasing body mass, certain occupations, or hereditary vulnerability. In general, there presides a stepwise treatment protocol that begins with oral/topical NSAID usage, weight loss, and an exercise program. Patients that fail to obtain adequate pain relief may then undergo intra-articular injections including corticosteroids or hyaluronic acid. If patients continue to fail conservative treatment, they should be considered for total knee arthroplasty (TKA).

The American Academy of Orthopaedic Surgeons (AAOS) provided an updated clinical practice guideline (CPG) summary for the conservative management of osteoarthritis of the knee. Their working group performed an in-depth systematic review of the available literature to produce consensus-approved, research-backed treatment guidelines. Some of their strong recommendations included utilizing oral and topical NSAIDs, oral acetaminophen, and engaging in exercise programs. They strongly recommended against utilizing lateral shoe inserts or oral narcotic medications. Moderate recommendations included the utilization of canes for ambulatory assistance, providing corticosteroid injections, or engaging in weight loss programs. The authors acknowledge that if patients fail a reasonable attempt at conservative management, they should be considered for total knee arthroplasty.

*Ranawat and colleagues provided a retrospective review article for the surgical treatment of knee osteoarthritis with severe valgus deformity.* *They acknowledge that 10% of patients requiring total knee arthroplasty have a clinically significant valgus deformity (>10°) which warrants special consideration.* *The authors described an inside-out technique for soft tissue release to include releasing the posterolateral aspect of the joint capsule with additional pie-crusting of the IT band in order to obtain balanced, rectangular spaces for cemented, posterior stabilized implants.* **They report** a significant improvement in patient-reported functional outcomes without the need for revision due to delayed instability at 5-year follow-up. **The authors concluded** that their technique for valgus deformity patients undergoing primary TKA is reproducible and provides excellent long-term results.

*Rajgopal and colleagues provided a similar case series reporting on their institutional outcomes using strategic soft tissue balancing techniques in the setting of TKA with genu valgus deformity.* **They reported** significantly improved Hospital for Special Surgery Scores, alignment, and range of motion in all 78 patients at a mean of 10-year follow-up. Further, the only causes for revision were one patient for deep infection and two patients for periprosthetic fracture. **The authors concluded** that adequate lateral soft-tissue release is the key to long-term successful TKA in valgus knees.

Figure A demonstrates AP radiographic imaging of this patient’s bilateral knees with genu valgus alignment. The right side is notably osteoarthritic with loss of the lateral compartment joint space, the presence of subchondral sclerosis, and osteophyte formation.

Incorrect Answer Choices:
Answer 1: In accordance with the updated 2022 AAOS CPG for the management of osteoarthritis of the knee, there is a strong recommendation for NOT using lateral shoe inserts in the management of this patient.
Answer 2: While topical NSAIDs are strongly recommended for use in the 2022 AAOS CPG, this patient has already attempted oral NSAIDs which are failing to provide adequate relief. Topical NSAIDs can be considered as a primary treatment in patients with knee osteoarthritis who are not able to take oral NSAIDs (i.e. chronic kidney disease or peptic ulcer disease).
Answer 3: The 2022 AAOS CPG strongly recommends AGAINST using oral narcotics (including tramadol) in the management of osteoarthritis of the knee. This is because there is a notable increase in adverse events and narcotics have not shown adequate efficacy at improving pain or function in patients with knee osteoarthritis.
Answer 4: This patient has received multiple corticosteroid injections with the most recent being within the last month. It would not be prudent to provide another corticosteroid injection in such a short time period (risk for infection). Further, the last injection only provided a short period of clinical efficacy. The most definitive management to provide lasting pain relief and functional improvement would be to proceed with total knee arthroplasty.
