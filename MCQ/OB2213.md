## Question

A 10-year-old male presents with refusal to bear weight on his right lower extremity. He reports falling off his bicycle 5 days prior. His right knee is warm to the touch and an effusion is noted. He has a temperature of 103.4°F and his laboratory values are remarkable for elevated ESR, CRP, and WBC. Radiographs of the knee are normal. A knee aspiration is performed. Aspiration reveals 1,000 WBC with 30% PMNs. What is the best next step?

## Options

1. Urgent irrigation and debridement of the right knee
2. Close monitoring with repeat labs in 24 hours
3. MRI of the right lower extremity
4. Long leg cast immobilization
5. Oral antibiotics with office follow-up the next day

## Correct option

**3. MRI of the right lower extremity**

## Answer explanation

This patient’s knee aspiration does not suggest septic arthropathy. However, osteomyelitis and/or a subperiosteal abscess has not been fully evaluated.

This patient has several concerning symptoms for a septic arthropathy, osteomyelitis, and/or a subperiosteal abscess. His inability to bear weight on the involved extremity, fever, elevated inflammatory markers, and warmth of the knee warrants a thorough investigation. Of his workup so far, only a septic arthropathy has been ruled out. An MRI of the right lower extremity would be the next best step to evaluate for the presence of osteomyelitis or a subperiosteal abscess. When osteomyelitis is diagnosed early before a subperiosteal abscess has formed, antibiotics alone may be adequate to treat the infection. When a subperiosteal abscess has formed, an incision and drainage, in addition to IV antibiotics, is merited to remove devitalized tissue and to enhance the efficacy of the antibiotics.

*Scott et al. reviewed 116 cases of acute hematogenous osteomyelitis in the absence of septic arthropathy.* **They report** that early presentation demonstrates evidence of local tenderness with an elevated ESR. **They conclude** that acute hematogenous osteomyelitis is successfully managed with an average IV antibiotic treatment time of 2 weeks followed by 4 weeks of oral therapy.

*Vaughan et al. reviewed 60 cases of acute osteomyelitis.* *They reported on two subgroups, those treated with 3 weeks of IV antibiotics and those treated with 8 days of IV antibiotics followed by 4 weeks of oral therapy.* **They concluded** that the incidence of chronic infection was more dependent on the time interval between the presentation of symptoms and the onset of treatment rather than the route of antibiotic administration.

Incorrect Answer:
Answer 1: The knee aspiration does not suggest septic arthropathy. Irrigation and debridement without the knowledge of a subperiosteal abscess would inadequately treat this patient.
Answer 2: Re-checking labs is important, but it should not slow further evaluation such as obtaining an MRI.
Answer 4: This patient has concerning symptoms for infection. Immobilization alone without further investigation could lead to the failure to treat an infection.
Answer 5: A thorough work-up for the cause of this patient’s infection has not been performed. Discharging on oral antibiotics is inappropriate.
