## Question

A 19-year-old cross-country runner is brought to the medical tent after collapsing during a race on a hot and humid day. He is sweating profusely, complaining of dizziness and nausea, and appears fatigued. His rectal temperature is 39.2°C (102.6°F), and he is alert but feeling weak. Another athlete is brought in shortly after with confusion, dry skin, and a rectal temperature of 41.3°C (106.3°F). What key feature differentiates heat stroke from heat exhaustion?

## Options

1. Elevated core body temperature above 38°C (100.4°F)
2. Hypotension and dehydration
3. Muscle cramps and dizziness
4. Presence of central nervous system (CNS) dysfunction
5. Profuse sweating

## Correct option

**4. Presence of central nervous system (CNS) dysfunction**

## Answer explanation

Heat stroke is defined by temperature greater than 104°F and central nervous system (CNS) dysfunction.

Heat stroke and heat exhaustion are both forms of heat illness, but they differ in severity. Heat stroke is a medical emergency defined by a core temperature >40°C (104°F) and CNS dysfunction, such as confusion, delirium, seizures, or coma. In contrast, heat exhaustion presents with intact mental status, excessive sweating, dehydration, and weakness but lacks significant CNS impairment. While both conditions may feature elevated body temperature, profuse sweating is not a reliable differentiator, as some heat stroke patients may have dry skin. Hypotension and muscle cramps are common in both conditions but do not distinguish them. Recognizing CNS dysfunction is critical for diagnosing heat stroke and initiating rapid cooling measures to prevent fatal complications.

*Garcia et al.* (2022) discussed the pathophysiology and risk factors of exertional heat stroke, **emphasizing** that it results from a failure of thermoregulation due to excessive heat production and inadequate dissipation. **They highlight** that CNS dysfunction is a defining feature and that immediate cooling, particularly via ice-water immersion, is crucial for survival.

*Navarro et al.* (2017) focused on the clinical presentation and management of exertional heat stroke, differentiating it from heat exhaustion by the presence of altered mental status and a core temperature above 40°C (104°F). They reinforce that rapid cooling and intravenous fluids are key to reducing morbidity and mortality, while slow cooling or delaying treatment significantly worsens outcomes.

Incorrect answers:
Answer choice 1: Heat stroke is defined by a core temperature above 40°C (104°F), whereas a temperature of 38°C is more consistent with heat exhaustion.
Answer choice 2: These are common in both heat exhaustion and heat stroke, but they are not distinguishing features between the two.
Answer choice 3: These are hallmarks of heat exhaustion as dizziness is not a symptom of CNS dysfunction (such as confusion or altered mental status).
Answer choice 5: While sweating can occur in both conditions, heat stroke often presents with anhidrosis or diminished sweating, especially in later stages.
