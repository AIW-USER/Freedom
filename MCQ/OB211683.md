## Question

A 27-year-old female presents to your clinic for 3 month follow-up after involvement in an MVC. She sustained multiple injuries including a type IIIa open right both bone forearm fracture, closed right femur fracture, bilateral pulmonary contusions, and a grade 3 splenic laceration. She underwent operative stabilization of her orthopedic injuries the following day without complication. Post-operatively, she transitioned to an inpatient rehab facility for two months. During an office visit, she tells you that for the past month she becomes very anxious when riding in a car and has been avoiding driving. Her past medical history is significant for hypertension, anxiety, and obesity. Social history includes a 15 pack-year smoking history and consumption of 3 glasses of wine per day. Which of the following is a risk factor for developing post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) in this patient?

## Options

1. Injury Severity Score
2. Social history
3. Timing of surgery
4. History of tobacco use
5. Duration of inpatient rehabilitation

## Correct option

**1. Injury Severity Score**

## Answer explanation

The Injury Severity Score (ISS) is a trauma scoring system used to correlate risk of morbidity, mortality, and hospitalization time and a high ISS is a risk factor for developing post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) in the orthopedic trauma patient. An ISS >15 classifies this patient as a major trauma, or polytrauma.

Post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) may develop in the orthopedic trauma patient that sustains serious injuries, such as multiple fractures. Orthopedic trauma survivors may experience anxiety, depression, and post-traumatic stress syndrome that affects their quality of life and functional rehabilitation. Early identification of patients who are at risk for developing PTSD, and early intervention, may shorten the time to physical and psychological recovery. Additionally, rehabilitation by a multi-disciplinary team, such as physical therapist, occupational therapist, PM&R physicians, and social support groups has been shown to mitigate the risk of developing PTSD in the orthopedic trauma patient.

*Starr et. al. performed a prospective, level-1 study on patients who sustained orthopedic trauma.* **They found** that 51% of patients met diagnostic criteria for PTSD. **They noted** that patients with PTSD were more likely to have higher Injury Severity Score (ISS), Extremity Abbreviated Injury Score (EAIS), and longer duration of time since the injury than those who didn't develop PTSD. **They concluded** that orthopedic trauma patients are at an increased risk for developing PTSD and should be evaluated further if there is any clinical suspicion of PTSD.

*Vincent et. al. reviewed psychological distress experienced by orthopedic trauma patients.* **They found** that approximately 50% of orthopedic trauma patients suffered from anxiety, depression, and stressors that limited their psychological and physical recovery. **They concluded** that early, multi-disciplinary intervention from rehab specialists and social support groups can lead to improved patient outcomes and quality of life.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Social history of alcohol, tobacco, or substance abuse has not demonstrated an increased risk for developing PTSD in the orthopedic trauma patient
Answer 3: Early orthopedic intervention has not demonstrated an increased risk of developing PTSD in the orthopedic trauma patient.
Answer 4: Prior tobacco use has not demonstrated an increased risk for developing PTSD in the orthopedic trauma patient.
Answer 5: Duration of inpatient rehabilitation (IPR) has not demonstrated an increased risk for developing PTSD in the orthopedic trauma patient.
