## Options

1. Having no children
2. Older age
3. Cohabitation
4. Relationship of >10 years
5. Married and living together

## Correct option

**3. Cohabitation**

## Answer explanation

Cohabitation is a risk factor for IPV in patients who present to an orthopedic clinic for evaluation.

Intimate partner violence (IPV) is considered a major cause of non-fatal injuries to women worldwide. Risk factors include young age, short-term relationships, lower socioeconomic status, pregnancy, cohabitation, alcohol and drug abuse, and female gender. Patterns that may suggest IPV in the right scenario include multiply injured patients, various stages of healing, repeat injuries, and a long delay between injury and seeking treatment. The orthopedic provider should be aware of these risk factors and patterns and properly coordinate care to protect and ensure the safety of the victims.

PRAISE trial investigators performed a cross-sectional analysis of roughly 3000 female patients in orthopedic fracture clinics. They found 1 in 6 women had experienced IPV in the past year and 1 in 3 were victims of IPV at some point in their life. They also found women in short-term relationships were at increased risk of IPV and physical abuse in the past 12 months. They conclude that 1 in 50 women presenting to an orthopedic fracture clinic is a direct result of IPV. They call for improved identification and care coordination for these victims of IPV.

*Hackenberg et al. studied 29 self-reported victims of intimate partner violence at two major trauma centers in Finland.* **They found** roughly 50% of the patients required hospitalization or surgical procedures with 17% being male. **They concluded** IPV leads to significant injuries across all age groups and gender.

*Wong et al. reviewed the charts of 1011 women in Hong Kong who were victims of IPV.* **They found** cohabitating women were about 2 times more likely to experience injuries to the head, neck, or face, present with multiple injuries in different locations, and experience >1 method of physical abuse. **They concluded** that the changing social changes and structure may mean women who are cohabitating should be screened for IPV.

Figure A shows an x-ray of the right forearm showing a healing distal ulna fracture. Figure B shows an x-ray of the left wrist showing a displaced distal radius fracture. Figure C shows a middle third tibial shaft and fibular shaft fracture.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: There is a higher prevalence of IPV in patients with children who present to an orthopedic clinic for evaluation.
Answer 2: Younger age (20-29) is a risk factor for IPV.
Answer 4: A short-term relationship of <10 years is a risk factor for IPV.
Answer 5: Cohabitation is a risk factor for IPV.
