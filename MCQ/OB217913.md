## Question

A 24-year-old male presents as a trauma transfer after being involved in a motorcycle accident. The patient is complaining of excruciating right upper extremity pain. Other than road rash, he has no other injuries. Figures A and B are radiographs of his right arm from the referring institution. Which of the following treatment options may result in chronic instability?

![[q217913-b-druj-injury.jpg]]

## Options

1. Temporary external fixation
2. Open reduction internal fixation radial head and neck
3. Radial head arthroplasty
4. Radial head excision
5. Open reduction internal fixation distal radius and cross pinning the radius and ulna

## Correct option

**4. Radial head excision**

## Answer explanation

This patient has ipsilateral radial head/neck and Galeazzi fractures which functionally serve as an Essex Lopresti injury disrupting the longitudinal stability of the radius and ulna. It is imperative to restore the length of the radius and stabilize the distal radioulnar joint to prevent the chronic instability classically seen in patients treated with radial head excision.

Essex Lopresti injuries, also referred to as longitudinal radioulnar dissociation (LRUD), is a disabling injury in which the longitudinal stability of the radius and ulna are disrupted. The pattern of injury typically involves a fracture of the radial head, disruption of the distal radioulnar joint, and rupture of the interosseous membrane. In the acute setting, treatment includes re-establishing the radiocapitellar joint and reducing the DRUJ. In the chronic setting, treatment includes restoration of radius length, leveling of the DRUJ, and reconstruction of the central band of the interosseous membrane with ligamentous grafting.

*Schnetzke et al. retrospectively reviewed 37 patients with Essex Lopresti injuries treated at their institution.* **They found** that patients diagnosed and treated early (within days of injury) had significantly improved outcomes over patients treated with chronic injuries (>4 weeks from injury). **They conclude** that patients with radial head fractures are at high risk for Essex Lopresti injuries and careful examination of the wrist is crucial to avoid missed diagnoses.

*Masouros et al. reviewed Essex-Lopresti injuries as well as updated diagnosis and treatment strategies.* **They note** that an ulnar shortening osteotomy is often required in the chronic setting in order to level the DRUJ. However, acute injuries traditionally involve fixation of the radial head and DRUJ reduction. **They conclude** that patients who develop chronic longitudinal radioulnar dissociation can be treated with reconstruction of the central band of the interosseous membrane with early studies showing promising results.

Figure A shows a radial head/neck fracture-dislocation. Figure B displays an ipsilateral Galeazzi fracture-dislocation with disruption of the DRUJ indicated by gross displacement of the distal ulna.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Temporary external fixation is a reasonable option for upper extremity injuries with significant surrounding soft tissue trauma or in the setting of polytrauma. The second stage procedure would focus on addressing the injury thus preventing chronic instability.
Answer 2: Open reduction internal fixation of the radial head and neck would help restore the radiocapitellar joint and restore length to the radius. This would be one part of the appropriate operative management for an acute Essex Lopresti injury.
Answer 3: Radial head arthroplasty is sometimes necessary when the radial head is highly comminuted. While not ideal in a young patient, a radial head arthroplasty would similarly restore the radiocapitellar joint and prevent chronic instability.
Answer 5: Open reduction internal fixation distal radius and cross pinning the radius and ulna would be required in this injury in order to restore the DRUJ and prevent chronic instability.
