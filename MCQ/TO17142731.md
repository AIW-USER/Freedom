Chapter: Orthopedics Oncology MCQs — Chondrosarcoma

## Question

Most important prognostic factor in chondrosarcoma is

## Options

1. Histological grade
2. Site of lesion
3. Calcification in the matrix
4. Age at the time of presentation

## Correct option

**1. Histological grade**

## Answer explanation

Explanation: Chondrosarcoma is a cartilaginous tumor which is seen in older people (40-70 years) with slight male predominance. Most common sites are pelvis, femur (proximal>distal) ribs and proximal shoulder.

Chondrosarcoma can be primary and secondary. Primary chondrosarcomas include low-grade, high- grade, dedifferentiated chondrosarcoma, clear cell chondrosarcoma and mesenchymal chondrosarcoma. Secondary chondrosarcomas arise in preexisting benign cartilage lesions.

Risk of malignant transformation to secondary chondrosarcoma in different cartilaginous lesions:

- Exostosis (osteochondroma) (<1% risk of malignant transformation)
- Enchondroma (1%)

- Multiple hereditary exostosis (1-10% risk of malignancy)
- Ollier's disease (multiple enchondromatosis) (5-50% risk of malignant transformation)
- Maffucci syndrome (presence of multiple enchondromas associated with multiple cavernous hemangioma and lymphangioma). (variably reported from 25-100%)

Low grade chondrosarcomas are difficult to be differentiated from an enchondroma on a pure histological basis. Tumor grading is the most important prognostic feature. Cartilaginous tumors in the distal part of the skeleton (hands, feet), in a circumscribed subperiosteal location, or in the lining of joint capsule are almost certain to be benign whereas tumor of ribs and pelvis (central tumors) are mostly malignant.

Grading of chondrosarcomas: Chondrosarcomas are mainly graded on the cellularity of the neoplasm and cellular atypia. Mitotic activity is uncommon and not used in the grading.

Histologic grade correlates with survival.

Grade I : relatively hypercellular compared with enchondromas and have moderate cytological atypia. Grade 1 chondrosarcomas are generally slow growing.

90% 5-year survival

Grade II: even more cellular than grade 1 and cytological atypia is more pronounced. 60-70% 5-year survival

Grade III: extreme cellularity, hypercellular stroma consisting of characteristic "blue-balls" of a cartilage lesion which permeate the bone trabeculae, large bizarre nuclei, and small foci of spindling at the periphery of the lobule. Grade 3 chondrosarcomas are extremely uncommon. 30-50% 5-year survival

Poor prognostic variables:

- High grade lesion (most important)
- Axial and proximal extremity lesions
- Pelvic location
- Advanced patient age,
- Inadequate surgical marginsIncreased telomerase activity (Correlated with recurrence)

X ray - Intra-lesional "popcorn" mineralization may be seen. It is described as rings, arcs, and stipples of mineralization.

Needle biopsy is not indicated for cartilage tumors due to difficulties with diagnosis. Lesions are often heterogeneous and difficult to diagnose on histology alone. Diagnosis requires clinical, pathologic, and radiographic correlation along with characteristic histology. Enchondromas of the hand, Ollier's disease, Maffucci's disease, periosteal chondromas, and chondrosarcoma may all have similar histology.

Treatment

Chondrosarcomas are generally resistant to chemotherapy and radiation. Surgery is the mainstay of treatment.

- Intralesional curettage -grade 1 lesions in the extremities (minimal rate of metastasis) are treated by intralesional curettage. Treatment of grade 1 lesions located in the pelvis or axial skeleton is wide excision due to high rate of recurrence

- Wide surgical excision- grade 2 or 3 lesions. Grade 1 lesions in pelvis.

Wide surgical excision combined with multi-agent chemotherapy- mesenchymal chondrosarcoma

Answer: 1
