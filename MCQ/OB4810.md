## Question

A 50-year-old recreational tennis player complains of increasing pain in his big toe. Radiographs are seen in Figure A. He has tried abstaining from sports for 1 year, and is using the orthotic shown in Figure B. He wishes to return back to sports. What is the most appropriate treatment plan?

## Options

1. Cheilectomy and Moberg procedure
2. Interpositional arthroplasty using joint capsule, and flexor hallucis brevis release
3. Metatarsal hemiarthroplasty
4. Total joint arthroplasty
5. Arthrodesis

## Correct option

**5. Arthrodesis**

## Answer explanation

This patient has Stage 4 hallux rigidus. He has failed activity modification and orthosis. The most reliable option is arthrodesis.

Hallux rigidus is degenerative arthritis of the 1st MTP joint. This disease is staged using the Coughlin and Shurnas system. Stage 4 is characterized by radiographic findings of severe dorsal osteophyte and greater than 50% joint space narrowing. Stage 4 disease is treated with arthrodesis. Keller resection arthroplasty may be attempted for elderly patients with low functional demands.

*Deland et al. reviewed hallux rigidus treatment.* **They advocate** cheilectomy or cheilectomy and proximal phalanx osteotomy for early to mid-stage hallux rigidus and arthrodesis and arthroplasty for late-stage disease.

Figure A shows AP and lateral radiographs of Stage 4 hallux rigidus. Figure B shows a Morton extension piece. Illustration A shows total joint arthroplasty with peri-implant radiolucencies and subsidence of the phalangeal component. Illustration B shows metatarsal hemiarthroplasty with lateral subsidence. Illustration C shows a cheilectomy and Moberg proximal phalanx dorsal closing wedge procedure. Illustration D shows the Coughlin and Shurnas classification of hallux rigidus.

Incorrect Answers
Answer 1: Cheilectomy and Moberg procedure is appropriate for early stage disease (Stage 1-2).
Answer 2: Interpositional arthroplasty is gaining popularity in younger patients <60 years with late-stage disease. However, limited toe function is noted postoperatively and is not suitable for athletic individuals.
Answers 3 and 4: Only short-term results are available on total joint arthroplasty and hemiarthroplasty, which are plagued with complications such as radiolucencies and subsidence. Arthrodesis has better outcomes and patient satisfaction than does arthroplasty with current implants.
