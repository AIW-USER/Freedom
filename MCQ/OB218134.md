## Question

A 20-year-old collegiate female tennis player presents to your office with complaints of sensations of knee instability after an injury several weeks ago where she twisted her knee. On examination, she has a positive, 2B Lachman test with no additional cruciate or collateral instability noted. MRI confirms the suspected anterior cruciate ligament (ACL) injury. When considering reconstruction, what is the femoral insertion of the portion of the ACL that controls rotatory stability with the knee near full extension?

## Options

1. Anterior to lateral intercondylar ridge, distal to bifurcate ridge
2. Anterior to lateral intercondylar ridge, proximal to bifurcate ridge
3. Posterior to lateral intercondylar ridge, distal to bifurcate ridge
4. Posterior to lateral intercondylar ridge, proximal to bifurcate ridge
5. Posterior to lateral intercondylar ridge, directly over bifurcate ridge

## Correct option

**3. Posterior to lateral intercondylar ridge, distal to bifurcate ridge**

## Answer explanation

The posterolateral (PL) bundle of the anterior cruciate ligament (ACL) is responsible primarily for resisting rotatory knee forces near full extension and attaches just posterior to the lateral intercondylar ridge (LIR) and distal to the bifurcate ridge (BR) on the lateral femoral condyle.

ACL injuries are quite common and the associated anatomy is imperative for surgeons to understand before performing reconstruction in order to recreate the native structure of the ligament. The ACL measures approximately 32mm in length and varies in diameter size between 7-12mm. Two bundles have been described, the anteromedial (AM) bundle, and the PL bundle. The anteromedial bundle is more isometric and primarily responsible for preventing anterior tibial translation. The AM bundle attaches just posterior to the LIR, but proximal to the BR. These are important landmarks to consider in order to recreate both anterior-posterior and rotatory instability after reconstruction. Interestingly, the AM and PL bundles are named for their tibial insertion sites between the intercondylar eminences.

*Zantop et al. reviewed ACL anatomy and function as it relates to surgical techniques attempting to produce the most anatomic reconstruction possible.* **They note** that close to full extension, the AM bundle is moderately loose and the PL bundle is tight and as the knee flexes, the AM bundle tightens and the PL bundle looses because of the ACL's more horizontal nature in this orientation. They describe the rotatory stability responsibility that the PL bundle carries and also note that the femoral ACL insertion is 3.5x larger than the mid substance of the ACL, important for consideration during reconstruction.

Petersen and Zantop describe the double bundle nature of the ACL, again describing the two major bundles, their biomechanics, and function. They add that the AM bundle is seen at the 10:30 position, while the PL bundle is at the 9:30 position in the frontal plane. They go on to describe the tibial footprint and the fact that the tibial insertion fans out with the AM bundle located anteriorly and the PL bundle posteriorly.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1,2,4,5: The PL bundle of the ACL attaches posterior to the LIR and distally to the BR.
