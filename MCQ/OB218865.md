## Question

A 20-year-old female collegiate runner presents to the athletic training department with complaints of loss of performance due to end-of-race fatigue. After further discussion, she appears to be feeling fatigued regularly and has had irregular ovulatory cycles for the past six months. She states that her regular diet has not changed, however, she has been increasing her training intensity and duration because she wants to be captain next year. Which hormonal changes would be expected in response to low energy availability in the athletic population?

## Options

1. Increased cortisol, increased free T3, increased luteinizing hormone
2. Increased cortisol, increased free T3, decreased luteinizing hormone
3. Increased cortisol, decreased free T3, decreased luteinizing hormone
4. Decreased cortisol, decreased free T3, decreased luteinizing hormone
5. Decreased cortisol, increased free T3, decreased luteinizing hormone

## Correct option

**3. Increased cortisol, decreased free T3, decreased luteinizing hormone**

## Answer explanation

Low energy availability (LEA) is the cornerstone causation for relative energy deficiency in sport (RED-S) and the female triad. The characteristic hormonal changes include increased cortisol, decreased free T3, and decreased luteinizing hormone.

In 2014, the International Olympic Committee highlighted the consequences of RED-S, classically referred to as the female athlete triad. The original terminology of the female athlete triad (anorexia athletica) encompassed a narrow group of female athletes who specifically had amenorrhea, insufficient energy availability, and osteoporosis. RED-S is considered generally more comprehensive, includes males, and describes the broader pathologic response to lower energy availability. It is now considered a spectrum of disease with three core pathologic changes within the body: (1) low energy availability, (2) reproductive hormonal dysfunction due to hypothalamic-pituary-adrenal axis disruption, and (3) low bone mineral density due to increased bone turnover. Some of the early disease manifestations are subtle with decreased athletic performance, while later stages can result in fragility fractures requiring surgical intervention. Newer investigations have also found potentially devastating long-term consequences to cardiovascular health from dyslipidemia and increased visceral adiposity, which accelerates precocious coronary artery atherosclerosis. Early recognition is prudent with multidisciplinary care to include the team physician, athletic trainer, sports dietician, and mental health provider if needed.

*Vopat and colleagues provided a comprehensive review of relative energy deficiency in sports from the orthopaedic surgery perspective.* *The authors discussed the multi-organ dysfunctions that can result from LEA and their multidisciplinary treatment guidelines.* *They acknowledge the need for further research is needed in specific high-risk groups, however, continued education of coaches, parents, and athletes should remain a priority.*

*Gordon and colleagues provided the Endocrine Society’s exhaustive list of clinical practice guidelines for the diagnosis and treatment of functional hypothalamic amenorrhea.* *They discuss the breadth of treatment options including simple outpatient counseling and nutritional support to more intensive inpatient guidelines for vital and electrolyte stabilization and hormonal repletion.* **These authors also emphasized** a multidisciplinary approach as functional hypothalamic amenorrhea is chronic and requires continuous monitoring from various health professionals.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1,2,4,5: When the human body has low energy availability, it utilizes innate defense mechanisms aimed at shutting down and conserving energy to divert energy stores to more vital organ systems. Higher cortisol levels help maintain functional blood glucose levels. Decreased T3 levels help lower the individual's basal metabolic rate as a response to low energy availability. The lower luteinizing hormone helps preserve energy for the individual rather than towards their reproductive capabilities.
