## Question

A 19-year-old male presents to your clinic for evaluation of an enlarging right femoral mass. He subsequently undergoes an open biopsy, which is concerning for malignancy. If the patient’s tumor falls into the most common presenting stage for malignant tumors using the Enneking/Musculoskeletal Tumor Society staging system, which of the following patient and tumor characteristics might be present?

## Options

1. High grade Osteosarcoma confined to the bone with metastatic disease to the lungs
2. High grade Chondrosarcoma confined to the bone without metastatic disease
3. High Grade Osteosarcoma with extension into the surrounding soft tissues but no metastatic disease
4. High grade Chondrosarcoma with extension into the surrounding soft tissues and metastatic disease to the liver.
5. Low grade Chondrosarcoma confined to the bone without metastatic disease.

## Correct option

**3. High Grade Osteosarcoma with extension into the surrounding soft tissues but no metastatic disease**

## Answer explanation

The most common presenting stage for a malignant bone tumor is an Enneking Stage IIB. A patient with a high-grade and extracompartmental tumor without metastatic disease best fits this description (Answer 3).

Primary bone tumors may be classified using the Enneking Staging System. This system is based on three components: tumor grade, extracompartmental involvement, and the presence of metastasis. The most common Enneking stage at time of diagnosis for malignant tumors is Stage IIB, which is characterized as a high-grade tumor extending beyond the bone cortex but without any metastatic disease. Alternative systems for bony tumors also include a separate Enneking Staging System for benign lesions and the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) Staging System. The AJCC System, like the Enneking system, takes into account tumor grade and metastatic disease, but also considers tumor size, depth of the tumor, and nodal involvement.

*Enneking et al. published a system for staging musculoskeletal sarcomas.* *They reviewed the prior staging systems and described the components of their system, which include lesion grade, surgical site, and presence of metastatic disease.* *They then describe applications of their staging system in aiding in the development of surgical plans.* **They conclude** that the Surgical Staging System offers valuable prognostic information that can inform surgical decision-making in patients with musculoskeletal sarcoma.

Jawad and Scully reviewed the Enneking classification for musculoskeletal tumors. They discussed the Enneking and the American Joint Committee for Cancer staging systems and described the components of each. They concluded that the Enneking system is efficacious in classifying musculoskeletal tumors, but that its use must be limited to tumors of mesenchymal origin.

*Bloem et al. reviewed the utility of various imaging modalities for the staging of primary bone sarcomas by correlating imaging with gross tumor findings.* **They found** that MRI outperformed CT and bone scintigraphy in predicting tumor length and was as efficacious in defining tumor involvement of the joint and cortical bone. **They concluded** that MRI is recommended for evaluating primary bone sarcoma.

Answer choice 4: This patient would have a Stage 3 tumor.
