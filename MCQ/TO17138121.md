Chapter: Nerve Injury MCQs — Brachial Plexus Injuries

## Question

Least important to reconstruct in brachial plexus injury (FNB Pattern Paper 2017)

## Options

1. Shoulder abduction
2. Elbow flexion
3. Elbow extension
4. Sensation over medial border of hand

## Correct option

**3. Elbow extension**

## Answer explanation

Brachial plexus injury involves nerve roots from C5 to T1. As a simple thumb rule C5

C6 represent the shoulder and elbow function, mainly of the deltoid and the biceps. Patients with C5-C6 involvement will show weak shoulder abduction and elbow flexion. C7 involvement in addition to C5 and C6 causes loss of elbow and wrist extension. However, C7 is an interesting root, in that it is not specific to any particular muscle or muscle group. Patients with weakness of mainly hand functions have lesions of C8-T1. If a patient has a flail upper limb, then all roots are involved. Important functions which need to be restored are shoulder abduction, elbow flexion and wrist flexion and finger grip. Elbow extension has least priority and in fact C7 nerve root can be harvested from the contralateral healthy side in case of a total palsy with very minor deficit resulting in the donor limb.

In lower trunk or global involvement of brachial plexus, sensory loss on the ulnar border of the hand and in the small and ring fingers results in losses in protective hand sensation. Restoration of sensation over the medial border of the hand is necessary to avoid trophic ulcerations, burns, and self-mutilation. For this commonly done procedures include transfer of intact median nerve fascicles in the distal forearm to the ulnar sensory nerve and dorsal sensory branch of the ulnar nerve. Recently transfer of the proximal lateral antebrachial cutaneous nerve into the dorsal sensory branch of the ulnar nerve using a 15 cm sural nerve interpositional graft has also been reported.
