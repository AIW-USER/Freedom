## Question

A 35-year-old male polytrauma patient presents to the trauma bay after a high-speed motor vehicle collision, and his injuries include a closed bicondylar tibial plateau fracture. He is taken to the operating room for an emergent exploratory laparotomy, at which time a knee-spanning external fixator is placed on the right lower extremity. The external fixator remains in place for several weeks before the patient is stabilized and cleared for definitive fixation. Initial placement and the subsequent duration of external fixation stabilization in this patient is associated with an increased risk for which of the following complications after definitive open-reduction and internal fixation?

## Options

1. Poor wound healing
2. Nonunion
3. Post-traumatic arthritis
4. Return to the operating room
5. Compartment syndrome

## Correct option

**4. Return to the operating room**

## Answer explanation

The patient sustained a bicondylar tibial plateau fracture treated with temporizing external fixation prior to definitive open-reduction and internal fixation (ORIF). The use of temporizing external fixation in this scenario is associated with an increased risk of arthrofibrosis requiring secondary intervention after definitive ORIF, and the duration of external fixation is also positively correlated to this risk.

Temporizing external fixation in the setting of complex tibial plateau fractures is an effective and often necessary strategy to achieve interim stabilization of the injury and surrounding soft tissues. Among its benefits, external fixator placement in the acute post-injury period allows for restoration of length and alignment at the knee joint and allows time for soft tissue swelling to improve before definitive fixation, which can help to avoid wound healing complications compared to early definitive ORIF. In addition to pin site complications, the risk of arthrofibrosis development is an important consideration in the use and planned duration of external fixation. The literature has established an increased risk of arthrofibrosis requiring secondary intervention after definitive ORIF in patients treated with temporizing external fixation. The duration of external fixation is also correlated to the risk of requiring intervention to treat arthrofibrosis, such as manipulation under anesthesia, quadricepsplasty, or arthroscopic lysis of adhesions.

*Haller et al. performed a retrospective study assessing the risk factors for arthrofibrosis development after tibial plateau fracture ORIF in 186 patients.* **They found** that the use of temporizing external fixation was significantly associated with developing postoperative arthrofibrosis requiring secondary intervention and that each additional day of external fixation carried a 10% increase in arthrofibrosis risk. **They also identified** the postoperative use of a continuous passive movement machine as a protective factor against arthrofibrosis development and concluded that this could be a potential intervention to mitigate the risk in patients treated with prolonged external fixation.

*Reahl et al. also retrospectively assessed the risk factors for arthrofibrosis development requiring secondary intervention after tibial plateau fracture fixation.* *They compared 110 patients who underwent either MUA, quadricepsplasty, or arthroscopic lysis of adhesions for stiffness after tibial plateau ORIF to 319 controls and identified the presence of bilateral tibial plateau fractures and the total number of weeks in an external fixator as significant predictors for arthrofibrosis intervention.* **They concluded** that surgeons should consider this risk when treating patients with temporizing external fixation and determining the time to definitive fixation.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Temporizing external fixation allows for soft tissue rest to decrease swelling before definitive ORIF, with the goal of decreasing postoperative wound healing complications compared to early definitive fixation.
Answer 2: The use of temporizing external fixation does not directly increase the risk of nonunion after definitive ORIF.
Answer 3: The patient’s complex injury pattern may place them at an elevated risk for arthritis development, but this risk is not directly increased by the use of temporizing external fixation.
Answer 5: The use of temporizing external fixation does not increase the incidence of compartment syndrome.
