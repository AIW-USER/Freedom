## Question

A 42-year-old IV drug user presents to the emergency department for evaluation of right index finger pain. The patient notes an insidious onset of pain and swelling over the past 12 hours. On examination, there is circumferential swelling with exquisite tenderness over the dorsum of the hand. Repeat examinations show increasing erythema with bullae formation. Labs show a WBC of 26 (x10,000/µL), CRP of 28 mg/dL (normal: < 15 mg/dL), hemoglobin of 10.5 g/dL, sodium of 139 mEq/L, creatinine of 1.8 mg/dL, and a glucose of 110 mg/dL. Vitals remain stable. He is emergently taken to the operating room. Which of the following is true concerning the treatment of this condition?

## Options

1. A trial of antibiotics and observation is always required
2. All cases can be successfully treated with one surgical debridement
3. Delayed surgical intervention beyond 24 hours increases mortality risk
4. Hyperbaric oxygen has minimal utility in treatment of this condition
5. The condition is most appropriately managed by a monodisciplinary team

## Correct option

**3. Delayed surgical intervention beyond 24 hours increases mortality risk**

## Answer explanation

This 42-year-old IV drug user presents with a necrotizing soft tissue infection and a Laboratory Risk Indicator for Necrotizing Fasciitis (LRINEC) score of 10 (Illustration A). Delaying surgical intervention beyond 24 hours has been shown to significantly increase mortality risk (Answer 3).

Necrotizing soft tissue infections (NSTI) is a term that encompasses several life- and limb-threatening conditions. The entity is notorious for its aggressive nature, which most often occurs in those with compromised immune systems, like diabetes, HIV/AIDS, or cancer. These conditions can evolve quickly, necessitating prompt diagnosis and treatment. Antibiotics and close observation can be trialed for ambiguous presentations, with a low threshold for taking the patient to the operating room. For fulminant infections, emergent incision and debridement or amputation are required for source control of the infection. The current literature recommends taking these patients for immediate surgical intervention to prevent unnecessary mortality risk, with time-to-intervention surpassing 24 hours as the threshold for an increase in risk.

Choueka and De Tolla provide a synopsis of necrotizing infections in the hand and upper extremities, detailing the presentation, workup, and treatment. The authors note the diagnosis largely remains clinical. With sufficient suspicion, the primary treatment involves emergent surgical incision and debridement of any involved skin, fascia, and muscle to reduce bacterial load and arrest ongoing necrosis. The authors recommend intervening within 24 hours, as outside of this timeframe increases mortality risk four-fold. Multiple trips to the operating room are often required to fully eradicate the infection. The authors conclude that a multidisciplinary team is necessary to optimally manage both the complexity of the patient and the infection.

*Osterman et al. review numerous acute hand infections, detailing their presentation, workup, and treatment.* NSTIs/necrotizing fasciitis are considered surgical emergencies, and immediate surgical intervention is required. Until the infection has been eradicated, a return to the OR every 24-48 hours is necessary. **They note** that mortality rates range from 23% to 76%, with delay in diagnosis and delay in surgical debridement being key factors in increasing mortality, in addition to two or more comorbidities being present.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Always trialing nonoperative treatment is not suggested, particularly in severe infections, as this increases mortality risk
Answer 2: Some cases require multiple trips to the OR for repeat debridement to eradicate the infection completely
Answer 4: Hyperbaric oxygen has shown utility in combating NSTIs, particularly with anaerobic organisms, as they elevate leukocyte oxygen levels thereby killing bacteria, increasing tissue perfusion/oxygenation, and increasing antibiotic uptake.
Answer 5: Given the complexity of the condition, a multidisciplinary team is required to optimally manage these patients.
