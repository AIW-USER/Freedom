## Question

Various approaches are commonly utilized during the performance of total hip arthroplasty (THA), with each approach having a distinct set of risks and benefits. Which of the following intervals is utilized in the approach that is widely cited as having the highest risk for femoral-sided complications?

## Options

1. Inter-nervous plane between the femoral nerve and the obturator nerve
2. Inter-nervous plane between the femoral nerve and superior gluteal nerve
3. Inter-nervous plane between the superior gluteal nerve and inferior gluteal nerve
4. No inter-nervous plane, splits muscles innervated by the femoral nerve and the superior gluteal nerve
5. No inter-nervous plane, splits muscles innervated by the inferior gluteal nerve

## Correct option

**2. Inter-nervous plane between the femoral nerve and superior gluteal nerve**

## Answer explanation

The direct anterior approach (DAA) to the hip has a higher rate of femoral-sided complications (i.e. fracture, subsidence) when compared to the other classic approaches. Both the superficial and deep exposures for the DAA utilize the interval between the femoral nerve and superior gluteal nerve (Answer 2).

Total hip arthroplasty (THA) is one of the most commonly performed orthopaedic procedures in the United States. While classically indicated for the management of osteoarthritis, THA also has indications in the setting of trauma, dysplasia, and/or oncology. A variety of approaches are available, and the approach utilized is often dictated by surgeon preference, the presence of prior incisions, patient body habitus, the baseline risk for dislocation, the surgeon's preferred implant, and the degree of deformity present.

Currently, a majority of THAs performed utilize one of four approaches: the direct anterior (DAA), the anterolateral (AL), the direct lateral, or the posterolateral/posterior. Recently, the direct anterior approach has gained popularity. Surgeons in favor of this approach cite the decreased dislocation rate, the preservation of the abductor mechanism, and the potential for decreased pain and quicker recovery as benefits to this approach. The DAA utilizes a superficial and deep interval, both of which are inter-nervous between the femoral and superior gluteal nerve. Superficially, the surgeon develops the interval between the sartorius and tensor fascia lata. Deep, the surgeon utilizes the interval between the rectus femoris and gluteus medius (Illustration A).

*Post et al. provide a comprehensive review of the DAA.* **The authors caution** that while short-term evidence lends support to many of the claims surrounding this approach, there is still a paucity of long-term outcome data to support many claims. Furthermore, the DAA has a steep learning curve, and appropriate training in this approach often requires a surgeon to seek out specific fellowship mentors. Furthermore, **the authors highlight** the unique risks inherent to this approach, namely femoral-sided complications including fracture and injury to the lateral femoral cutaneous nerve, but do note that complications decrease as surgeon experience increases.

*Hartford et al. investigated risk factors for the development of periprosthetic femur fractures in cementless THA performed through a DAA using a fracture table.* *Their study included 500 primary THAs.* **The authors note** that 23 hips (4.6%) incurred fractures, with 13 (2.6%) occurring intraoperatively and 10 (2.0%) occurring postoperatively. The bivariate analysis identified female sex and BMI > 40 as risk factors for the development of periprosthetic fracture. Overall, **the authors conclude** that femoral-sided complications are a common and potentially devastating complication of the DAA, and note that female sex and BMI > 40 are specific risk factors for the development of this complication.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: This is not a described approach. The medial approach to the hip, however, splits muscles innervated by the obturator nerve superficial, and takes advantage of the dual innervation of the adductor magnus (obturator nerve/sciatic nerve) deep.
Answer 3: This is not a described approach. The anterolateral approach is between two muscles (tensor fascia lata and gluteus medius) which are both innervated by the superior gluteal nerve.
Answer 4: This describes the direct lateral approach to the hip, in which the gluteus medius (superior gluteal nerve) and vastus lateralis (femoral nerve) are split. The DAA has a much higher rate of femoral-sided complications compared to the direct lateral approach.
Answer 5: This describes the posterior approach to the hip, which splits the gluteus maximus. While the posterior approach has a higher rate of instability compared to the DAA, it has a lower rate of femoral-sided complications.
