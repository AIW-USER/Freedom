## Question

A 10-year-old boy tripped as he was running down a hill, felt a painful pop in his right knee, and was unable to bear weight on the involved lower extremity. Examination reveals a tense effusion and an extensor lag of the right knee. Figures 36a and 36b show AP and lateral radiographs. Management should consist of

## Options

1. long leg casting in 30 degrees of flexion for 6 weeks.
2. a long leg cast in full extension for 6 weeks.
3. knee arthroscopy to rule out internal derangement.
4. physical therapy for range of motion and quadriceps strengthening.
5. open reduction and internal fixation.

## Correct option

**5. open reduction and internal fixation.**

## Answer explanation

The examination and radiographs are consistent with a sleeve fracture of the patella, which is an avulsion fracture of the distal pole of the patella with a disruption of the extensor mechanism. Treatment is open reduction and internal fixation of the patella, and repair of the extensor mechanism. The distal fragment can be much larger than it appears on the radiographs because it consists largely of cartilage.
