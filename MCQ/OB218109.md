## Question

A patient presents to your office with complaints of left ulnar-sided wrist pain for the past two years. Radiographs reveal an ulnar positive variance with abutment of the ulnar head with the triquetrum and subchondral sclerosis of the ulnar styloid. The decision is made to proceed with surgical intervention. Which of the following is true regarding an ulnar shortening osteotomy?

## Options

1. An oblique osteotomy has a significantly higher risk of nonunion compared with a transverse osteotomy
2. Nonunion rate is approximately 6% in smokers
3. BMI >30 has a 10-fold increase in the risk of nonunion
4. Volar placement of the plate has a significantly higher risk of nonunion compared with ulnar plate placement
5. Low bone mineral density has not been shown to increase the risk of nonunion

## Correct option

**2. Nonunion rate is approximately 6% in smokers**

## Answer explanation

Smoking is associated with a higher rate of nonunion in patients undergoing ulnar shortening osteotomy (USO).

Ulnar styloid impaction syndrome is a common cause of ulnar-sided wrist pain secondary to impaction between the ulnar styloid tip and the triquetrum. Diagnosis is made by PA wrist radiographs which reveal positive ulnar variance with subchondral sclerosis of the ulnar styloid and/or triquetrum. Treatment is a course of rest, NSAIDs, and splinting. In refractory cases, operative ulnar shortening osteotomy is indicated. Nonunion following ulnar shortening osteotomy has been estimated to occur in 4% to 8% of cases.

*Cha et al. retrospectively reviewed the variables affecting osseous consolidation after USO in patients with ulnar impaction syndrome.* **They reported** that delayed union or nonunion occurred in about 10% of patients treated with USO. **They concluded** that it may be preferable to perform USO in nonsmokers, patients with normal bone density, and those without restricted wrist motion.

*Gaspar et al. reviewed the potential risk factors for the development of delayed or nonunion following elective USO using a dedicated osteotomy plating system.* **They reported** that despite the use of an osteotomy-specific plating system, smokers and diabetics were at significantly higher risk for both delayed union and nonunion following elective USO. **They concluded** that other known risk factors for suboptimal bony healing were not found to have a deleterious effect.

*Owens et al. systematically reviewed all available literature reporting nonunion rates of USO used for the treatment of ulnar impaction syndrome and compared those rates among transverse versus oblique cuts for the osteotomy.* **They reported** that the average rate of nonunion among all osteotomies was 4.0%; 4.16% and 3.86% in transverse osteotomies and oblique osteotomies, respectively. **They concluded** that there is no difference in the rate of nonunion between transverse and oblique osteotomies.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: A transverse and oblique osteotomy has a nonunion rate of 4.16% and 3.86%, respectively.
Answer 3: BMI has not been associated with higher rates of nonunion following ulnar shortening osteotomy.
Answer 4: The placement of the plate (volar versus ulnar) has not been found to affect the rate of union.
Answer 5: Low bone mineral density has been associated with higher rates of nonunion following ulnar shortening osteotomy.
