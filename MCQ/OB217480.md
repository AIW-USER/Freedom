## Question

As part of your professional responsibilities, you provide team coverage for a local semi-professional soccer team. The team's equipment sponsor offers two footwear options, with one utilizing a new cleat design. Your athletic trainer mentions that he has heard reports that this cleat design increases the risk of anterior cruciate ligament (ACL) injury. You decide to conduct a study in which you will report the incidence of ACL injury among athletes wearing either the classic cleat design or the new cleat design, after allowing the athletes to chose their own footwear. You will initiate the study before the season starts and follow the athletes over the course of the season. Which of the following represents the level of evidence of this study?

## Options

1. Level I
2. Level II
3. Level III
4. Level IV
5. Level V

## Correct option

**2. Level II**

## Answer explanation

The vignette is describing a prospective cohort study, which is level II evidence (Answer 2).

Clinical trial design is an important concept for orthopedic surgeons to grasp, as it aids not only those who conduct research but anyone who critically appraises research as well. Trial design ultimately impacts the final level of evidence that can be assigned to a study, with higher levels of evidence representing a higher "clinical value" of the study. As a study increases in level, bias and confounders are minimized, and tools such as randomization are often implemented. In terms of clinical studies, the most common types encountered in the orthopedic literature are randomized controlled trials (RCTs), prospective cohort studies, retrospective cohort studies, case-control studies and case series. Prospective cohort studies represent a study in which two patient groups are separated non-randomly by either exposure or treatment to determine what effect, if any, the exposure or treatment has on the outcome of interest. Prospective cohort studies differ from retrospective cohort studies in two ways: first, in prospective studies, the exposure occurs after the initiation of the study, and second, prospective studies represent level II evidence, versus level III for retrospective studies. The data gathered from cohort studies are typically reported as incidence and/or relative risk.

*Checkoway et al. provide a guide for selecting the appropriate study design to answer specific research questions.* **They note** that cohort studies are useful for determining the incidence of outcomes in a population. **They go on to state** that prospective cohort studies are especially useful for determining short-term phenomena i.e. in-season injury rates with new versus old equipment. These studies are appealing as they follow the temporal sequence of exposure followed by presence of absence of outcome.

*Dunn et al. offer a primer on research methodology, specifically as it relates to orthopedic evidence-based medicine.* **They note** that conclusion validity is dependent on the minimization of bias, which can be achieved through study design, implementation, and/or analysis. **The authors go on to state** that as a study is more effectively able to minimize bias, the level of evidence will tend to increase.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Examples of level I evidence include randomized controlled trials or meta-analyses of randomized controlled trials with homogenous results.
Answer 3: Examples of level III evidence include retrospective cohort studies (in which the exposure occurs before the study is initiated), case-control studies or meta-analyses of level III studies.
Answer 4: A case series represents level IV evidence.
Answer 5: A case report, expert opinion or personal observations represent level V evidence.
