## Options

1. Physical therapy
2. Chemotherapy
3. Closed intramedullary nailing
4. Radiation therapy
5. Closed intramedullary nailing with postoperative radiation

## Correct option

**5. Closed intramedullary nailing with postoperative radiation**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a metastatic lesion in the femoral diaphysis extending along a significant length of the femur without bony defects. Internal fixation with an intramedullary device is indicated.

Skeletal stabilization of pathologic fractures, or impending pathologic fractures improves quality of life and enables mobilization. Internal fixation of lower extremity pathologic fractures is indicated in patients with life expectancy of at least six to twelve weeks. Mirels rating system may be used for predicting pathologic fracture when considering prophylactic stabilization.

Peabody discusses the approach to presumed bone metastases with an emphasis on separation of strategies for diagnosis from those for fixation and reconstruction.

*Quinn et al. discuss diagnosis, indications and treatment strategies for metastatic bone disease by anatomic location.* For the femoral shaft, **they recommend** closed intramedullary nailing for small lesions, open curettage, PMMA and internal fixation for large lesions with open section defects, an intercalary prosthesis in "few" cases of large diaphyseal lesions and total femur replacement in rare cases. Routine treatment of the entire bone (e.g. including fixation into head even if the lesion is small and diaphyseal) is controversial but should be considered in patients with prolonged life expectancy or multiple myeloma.

Figures A and B show a diaphyseal femoral lesion with endosteal scalloping. Figure C is a pathology slide showing metastatic carcinoma. Illustration A shows a table from Quinn et al. illustrating Mirels rating system for pathologic fracture risk.

2. Skeletal stabilization is indicated due to the impending pathologic fracture.
3. Post operative radiation is indicated in addition to nailing.
