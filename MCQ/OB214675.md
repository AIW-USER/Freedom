## Question

A previously highly functioning 83-year-old male falls while on a walk and is found to have a Vancouver B3 fracture. Among the below options, what would an appropriate treatment for him be?

## Options

1. Nonoperative treatment with protected weight-bearing
2. Proximal femur replacement
3. Open reduction internal fixation (ORIF)
4. Resection arthroplasty
5. Revision hip arthroplasty with a metaphyseal-engaging, pressfit stem

## Correct option

**2. Proximal femur replacement**

## Answer explanation

Among these options, a proximal femur replacement (PFR) would be the best treatment. For patients who are surgical candidates with Vancouver B3 fractures, options include PFR, a diaphyseal-engaging cementless femoral component, or an allograft prosthesis composite.

Periprosthetic femur fractures adjacent to a femoral stem are described by the Vancouver classification system. Vancouver B fractures are adjacent to the tip of the femoral stem, or just below it. B1 fractures have a well-fixed stem, B2 fractures have a loose stem, and B3 fractures have both a loose stem and poor proximal bone stock (either of poor quality or severely comminuted). In younger patients, B3 fractures may be treated with an allograft prosthesis composite, while in older patients these injuries are best treated with a proximal femur replacement.

*Parvizi et al. performed a retrospective review of 48 patients who underwent PFR for non-neoplastic reasons.* *Patients had significant functional improvement and 51% of patients had an excellent or good outcome.* Implant survivorship was 87% at 1 year and 73% at 5 years. **The authors conclude** that PFR is an option for patients with severely compromised bone stock.

*Haidukewych et al. described the indications, techniques, and results of revision for periprosthetic fractures of the hip and knee.* *They describe B3 fractures as taking place around the tip of the femoral stem, around a loose implant with poor remaining proximal bone stock.* **They conclude** that for Vancouver B3 fractures, a tumor prosthesis or megaprosthesis is necessary.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: for a healthy patient who could tolerate it, surgery is preferred in order to maximize mobility.
Answer 3: ORIF for a loose implant portends the fracture for nonunion and early failure.
Answer 4: resection arthroplasty would not be an appropriate option for a highly functioning patient with a Vancouver B3 periprosthetic femur fracture.
Answer 5: in a B3 fracture the proximal, metaphyseal bone stock is deficient. A metaphyseal-engaging stem in this setting would provide inadequate implant fixation.
