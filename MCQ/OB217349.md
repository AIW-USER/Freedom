## Question

A 12-year-old male presents to the emergency department with a 2-week history of unilateral knee pain and swelling. He reports that he previously had a rash 3 weeks ago that has since resolved. He has been able to ambulate on the knee since the swelling started. The patient and his family were previously on a camping trip in Connecticut 2 months ago. On examination he has a unilateral knee effusion and is able to flex his knee from 0-90 degrees. On laboratory studies, he has no leukocytosis but has an elevated ESR and CRP. A knee aspiration is performed which revealed a WBC of 25,000. Which of the following would be most representative of the causative bacteria in this patient?

## Answer explanation

This patient's symptoms are suggestive of Lyme disease, which is caused by Borrelia burgdorferi (depicted in Figure A)

Lyme Disease is a systemic infection caused by tick-born B. burgdorferi which usually presents with flu-like symptoms, a characteristic rash, and occasionally acute joint effusions. Diagnosis is confirmed by a history of travel to a tick-heavy regions, presence of erythema migrans rash, and positive lyme titers on an ELISA test. Patients with Lyme disease often present with a spontaneous knee effusion after the rash has resolved. Joint aspiration will likely reveal fewer WBCs compared with septic arthritis with less of a left shift; however, this is not always true. Another finding that may help to support this diagnosis is the ability to ambulate (less likely with septic arthritis). Treatment is usually oral doxycycline for an acute or mild disease.

*Matzkin et al. reviewed Lyme disease.* **They reported** that transient episodes of spontaneous knee effusion are common early in the progression of Lyme disease, and, if left untreated, 60% of patients diagnosed with the disease develop Lyme arthritis. **They concluded** that this disease is easily treated with antibiotics.

*Smith et al. reviewed Lyme disease and* **noted** it as the most common tick-borne disease in the United States and Europe. **They reported** that the epidemiology and pathophysiology of Lyme disease are directly related to the Borrelia burgdorferi spirochete and its effects on the integumentary, neurologic, cardiac, and musculoskeletal systems. **They concluded** that Lyme arthritis is a common clinical manifestation of Lyme disease and should be considered in the evaluation of patients with monoarticular or pauciarticular joint complaints in a geographic area in which Lyme disease is endemic.

Figure A is Borrelia burgdorferi appearance in dark field microscopy. Figure B depicts stained Corynebacterium cells. The "barred" appearance is due to the presence of polyphosphate inclusions called metachromatic granules. Note also the characteristic "Chinese-letter" arrangement of cells. Figure C is the microscopic image of Escherichia coli. Figure D is a gram stain of S. saprophyticus cells, which typically occur in clusters. Figure E a low-resolution photomicrograph of gonococcal urethritis.

Incorrect Answers:

Answer 3: Escherichia coli is a Gram-negative, facultative anaerobic, rod-shaped, coliform bacterium
Answer 4:Staphylococcus saprophyticus is a Gram-positive coccus that is a common cause of community-acquired urinary tract infections
