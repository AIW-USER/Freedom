## Answer explanation

Figure B is the lateral radiograph of a McKeever type II tibial eminence injury in a pediatric patient. Due to the intact posterior hinge, these injuries may successfully reduce with extension or hyperextension of the knee.

Tibial eminence fractures, also referred to as tibial spine fractures, are intra-articular fractures of the bony attachment of the ACL on the tibia. They are most common in children ages 8-14 years. The McKeever classification is used to classify these injuries. A McKeever type II injury demonstrates a minimally displaced fracture fragment with an intact posterior hinge. Several studies have found that most of these fractures will reduce with extension or hyperextension of the knee. Often, an aspiration is utilized to evacuate the hemarthrosis to help with the reduction. The knee is often immobilized in either full extension or in 20° of flexion.

*Meyers et al. described a classification system for fractures of the intercondylar eminence of the tibia.* Type I injuries are non-displaced (< 3mm); Type II injuries are displaced but have an intact posterior hinge and are amendable to closed reduction; Type III injuries are completely displaced; and Type IV injuries are completely displaced, rotated, and comminuted. **They concluded** that this classification is reliable and helps guide decision making.

*Wiley et al. review fractures of the intercondylar eminence of the tibia in pediatric patients.* **They report** that the eminence consists of two projecting tibial spines with the anterior cruciate ligament being attached to the medial spine. **They noted** no significant clinical ACL laxity following these injuries regardless of the type of fracture, method of treatment, or mode of injury. However, use of computed instrumental testing of these knees revealed measurable degrees of residual ACL laxity despite the absence of patient symptoms. **They concluded** that treatment by closed or open methods is directed toward reattachment of the loose fragment and achieves of some degree of joint congruity rather than restoration of cruciate integrity.

*Janarv et al. review long-term follow-up with a mean of 16 years in 61 children with tibial eminence fractures.* **They reported** that subjective knee function was excellent or good in 87% of the subjects and fair in 13%, with pathological knee laxity found in 38%. **They also noted** that age at the time of injury did not influence the outcome and there is no indication that young children can eliminate slackness of the ACL with further growth. Only in type III fractures was there a correlation between fracture displacement after healing with knee laxity, and between knee laxity and outcome score. **They concluded** that arthroscopy-guided or open reduction and internal fixation seems to be a worthwhile procedure only in dislocated type III fractures.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: A displaced Saller-Harris II distal femur fracture may be successfully reduced with closed methods, however, they typically require fixation as this fracture pattern is unstable.
Answer 3: A McKeever III tibial eminence injury is unlikely to be successfully reduced and requires open or arthroscopic reduction and internal fixation
Answer 4: A >2mm displaced Salter-Harris 4 proximal tibial epiphyseal injury is an indication for open reduction and internal fixation
Answer 5: A displaced Ogden type IV tibial tubercle injury describes a fracture through the entire proximal physis and requires open reduction and internal fixation
