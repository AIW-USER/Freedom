## Question

A five-year-old male is referred to your clinic by his pediatrician. A chest and pelvis radiograph are available for review in Figures A and B, respectively. This patient's inability to perform normal intramembranous ossification is most likely secondary to a mutation in which gene?

![[q219376-b-coxa-vara.jpg]]

## Options

1. COL1A1/COL1A2
2. DTDST
3. FGFR2
4. RUNX2/CBFA1
5. SOX9

## Correct option

**4. RUNX2/CBFA1**

## Answer explanation

This patient has absent clavicles and bilateral coxa vara, common findings in cleidocranial dysplasia (CCD). CCD is caused by an autosomal dominant mutation in the RUNX2/CBFA1 gene, which affects osteoblasts and leads to abnormal intramembranous ossification (Answer 4).

CCD affects approximately 1 in every 1,000,000 live births. The symptoms of CCD occur secondary to disrupted intramembranous ossification, which leads to a failure of the formation of midline structures. The characteristic anatomic abnormality seen in patients with CCD is hypoplastic/absent clavicles. CCD is caused by an autosomal dominant mutation in the RUNX2/CBFA1 gene, which is a transcription factor involved in osteoblastic differentiation.

Patients with CCD typically demonstrate proportionate dwarfism, clavicle dysplasia/aplasia, wormian bones, frontal bossing, coxa vara, and the delayed eruption of primary teeth. Secondary to clavicular abnormalities, patients will often demonstrate shoulder hypermobility. Patients with CCD are observed, as most manifestations associated with CCD do not need intervention. Patients with CCD and coxa vara, with a neck shaft angle of < 100°, may require a reorienting intertrochanteric osteotomy.

Ballock and O'Keefe provide an overview of the biology of the growth plate. Concerning matrix mineralization, the authors note that chondrocytes secrete and maintain a highly specialized matrix that promotes the calcification of cartilage and serves as a template for bone formation. Any mutation in the genes that promote osteoblast activity can interfere with this bone formation.

Hosseinzadeh and Milbrandt provide an overview of the normal and fractured physis. The authors note that both intramembranous and endochondral ossification requires osteoblast activity and that genetic aberrations that interfere with osteoblast activity can have a marked effect on bone formation.

Lian and Stein provide a seminal paper on the role of RUNX2/CBFA1 on bone formation. The authors note that this family of transcription factors is essential for osteoblast differentiation and bone formation. The authors highlight that this pathway may have potential as a therapeutic target for the promotion of bone formation.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: mutations in the COL1A1/COL1A2 genes are implicated in the development of osteogenesis imperfecta (OI). Patients with OI typically present with multiple fractures, short stature, and scoliosis. While they can demonstrate coxa vara, they do not experience clavicle aplasia.
Answer 2: mutations in the diastrophic dysplasia sulfate transporter (DTDST) gene are implicated in the development of diastrophic dysplasia. Patients with DD do not present with absent clavicles or coxa vara.
Answer 3: mutations in FGFR2 are implicated in the development of Apert syndrome. Patients with Apert syndrome will present with bilateral complex syndactyly of the hands and feet. Patients do not typically experience coxa vara or clavicle aplasia.
Answer 5: SOX-9 is a transcription factor implicated in limb development. The constellation of bilateral absent clavicles and coxa vara is not associated with a specific SOX-9 mutation.
