## Question

Which of the following is a commonly seen radiographic sequela of non-operative management of the injury noted in Figure A?

![[q211952-a-scaphoid-pa.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Distal pole avascular necrosis
2. Capitate-lunate joint arthritis
3. Humpback deformity
4. Radius-lunate joint arthritis
5. Subluxation of proximal carpal row with active radial deviation.

## Correct option

**3. Humpback deformity**

## Answer explanation

Figure A shows a displaced scaphoid fracture. Of the options above, non-operative management of a displaced scaphoid fracture may lead to a humpback deformity where the distal scaphoid becomes excessively flexed compared to the proximal fragment.

Scaphoid fractures are prone to non-union because of retrograde blood supply, limited cancellous bone, limited soft tissue attachments, delayed diagnosis, and subsequent delayed treatment. Studies cite up to 10% of scaphoid fractures go on to non-union. Proximal pole fractures have the highest rate of non-union due to retrograde blood flow. Other associated conditions related to scaphoid fractures include avascular necrosis and scaphoid-nonunion advanced collapse (SNAC). Scaphoid nonunions can present with or without avascular necrosis of the proximal pole and may show a humpback deformity on the lateral radiograph.

*Kawamura and Chung performed a review of scaphoid fracture non-unions.* **They note** that despite the advent of newly developed fixation techniques, the nonunion rate for scaphoid fractures remains as high as 10% after surgical treatment. If left untreated, scaphoid nonunions can progress to carpal collapse and degenerative arthritis. **They note** that surgical treatment is directed at correcting the deformity with open reduction and internal fixation WITH bone grafting. Recently, vascularized bone grafts have gained popularity in the treatment of scaphoid nonunions, particularly in cases with avascular necrosis.

*Buijze et al. review the risk factors and management for scaphoid non-unions.* **They state** that while the primary risk factor for nonunion is displacement/instability, other risk factors also include delayed or missed diagnosis, inadequate treatment, fracture location, and blood supply. The diagnosis of nonunion is made on radiographs, but CT and MRI scans can be useful to assess deformity and blood supply. **The authors conclude** that it is not clear that surgery can change the natural history of scaphoid non-union and degenerative wrist arthritis, even if fracture union is attained.

Figure A shows a displaced transverse scaphoid waist fracture. Illustration A shows a normal lateral figure of the scaphoid (A) as well as a displaced scaphoid fracture with humpback deformity (B).

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The proximal pole or proximal fragment (not distal) is generally the part of the scaphoid that undergoes avascular necrosis due to the retrograde blood supply.
Answer 2: Capitate-lunate arthritis is a finding noted in Stage III of scapholunate advance collapse (SLAC)
Answer 4: Radius-lunate joint arthritis is not generally observed following the non-operative management of scaphoid fractures.
Answer 5: Subluxation of the proximal carpal row with active radial deviation is generally seen with non-dissociative carpal instability
