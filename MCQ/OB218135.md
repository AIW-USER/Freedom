## Question

Figure A is the radiograph of a 72-year-old female who presents with worsening left shoulder pain, 8-months after her reverse total shoulder arthroplasty. She reports that she was doing initially, but has had worsening shoulder pain since a fall in her garden 4 weeks ago. Which of the following is true regarding the likely cause of this patient's shoulder pain?

![[q218135-a-acomial-fracture.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. It has a reported incidence of approximately 15%
2. Chronic pain with nonoperative management is very likely
3. Osteoporosis is a known risk factor
4. Rate of nonunion is less than 5% with nonoperative management
5. Nonoperative management is associated with an equal rate of revision surgery compared to those without this complication

## Correct option

**3. Osteoporosis is a known risk factor**

## Answer explanation

Osteoporosis is a known risk factor for an acromial stress fracture after reverse total shoulder arthroplasty (RSA).

RSA has a center of rotation that is moved inferiorly and medialized which allows the deltoid muscle to act on a longer fulcrum and have more mechanical advantage. RSA is indicated for conditions such as rotator cuff tear arthropathy, comminuted 4-part proximal humerus fractures and prior failed shoulder arthroplasty. Acromial or scapular spine fractures are known complication with an incidence of around 4-8% after RSA. Proposed risk factors include female sex, osteoporosis, and excessively medialized preoperative center of rotation. Treatment is often controversial with conservative management leading to a 40-50% union rate. Operative management with ORIF or tension band wiring of acromial fractures have increased union rates.

*Otto et al. reviewed the sensitivity of plain radiographs to detect scapular fractures after RSA and identify risk factors.* **They reported** that osteoporosis is a significant risk factor for scapular fractures after RSA. **They concluded** that advanced imaging may be needed to confirm the diagnosis.

*Teusink et al. retrospectively reviewed the effects of acromial fractures on RSA patient outcomes.* **They reported** that postoperative scapular fractures may occur at any point postoperatively. **They concluded** that this complication leads to inferior clinical results and may have increased risk of revision.

*Hamid et al. reviewed patients who sustained a fracture of the acromion after RSA.* **They reported** that 74% of ASES respondents treated these patients nonoperatively and that 53% of respondents thought that acromial fractures after RSA led to reduced shoulder function, but without persistent pain. **They concluded** that the natural history of nonoperative management is characterized by reduced global shoulder function and a high rate of nonunion. However, most of the patients who experienced this complication did not report chronic pain.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The reported incidence of an acromial stress fracture after RSA is approximately 5%.
Answer 2: Most patients with acromial fractures after RSA may have inferior clinical outcomes but most do not have chronic pain.
Answer 4: Nonoperative management has a high rate of nonunion.
Answer 5: Scapular fractures may lead to a higher rate of revision surgery.
