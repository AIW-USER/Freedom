## Question

A 63-year-old male has a long-standing history of neck pain. Recently he developed pain radiating into his right shoulder and arm as well as numbness and tingling in his right small finger. He also reports decreased grip strength. On physical exam he has a positive shoulder abduction provocative test, weakness with distal phalanx flexion of the right middle and index fingers, and weakness to thumb extension. At which vertebral level is there likely pathology compressing the nerve root?

## Options

1. C4/5
2. C5/6
3. C6/7
4. C7/T1
5. T1/T2

## Correct option

**4. C7/T1**

## Answer explanation

This patient presents with a radiculopathy of the C8 nerve root caused by a disc herniation at the C7/T1 level.

A sensory and motor examination of the hand can be an important method to differentiate between the level of a cervical radiculopathy, and to differential between a cervical radiculopathy and a peripheral neuropathy. A C7 radiculopathy will present with paresthesias of the index and middle finger. It also may present with weakness of elbow extension, wrist flexion, and wrist pronation. C8 radiculopathy presents with paresthesias in the small finger, weakness with distal phalanx flexion of the middle and index fingers, and thumb extension weakness.

*Rhee et al. provided a review of cervical radiculopathy.* **They report** that cervical nerve root compression may occur due to multiple etiologies. An acute soft disk herniation may impinge on the exiting nerve root at its take-off from the spinal cord or as it traverses the neuroforamen. Chronic disk degeneration with disk height loss may cause annular bulging without frank herniation. Disk height loss may result in loss of foraminal height; combined with superior migration of the superior facet joint from the subjacent vertebra, can lead to subsequent foraminal root compression.

*Viikari-Juntura et al. performed a study to validate the utility of clinical tests in the diagnosis of root compression in cervical disc disease.* *They evaluated neck compression, axial manual traction, and the shoulder abduction test.* **They found** all tests were highly specific but had low sensitivity. **They concluded** that despite low sensitivity, these tests are a valuable aid in the clinical examination of a patient with neck and arm pain.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: A herniation at the C4/5 level would involve the C5 nerve root and would likely present with lateral arm pain, weakness in shoulder abduction, and a diminished biceps reflex.
Answer 2: A herniation at the C5/6 level would involve the C6 nerve root and would likely present with radial forearm pain, weakness with elbow flexion and wrist extension, and a diminished brachioradialis reflex.
Answer 3: A herniation at the C6/7 level would involve the C7 nerve root and would likely present with middle finger pain, weakness in elbow extension and wrist flexion, and a diminished triceps reflex.
Answer 5: A herniation at the T1/2 level would involve the T1 nerve root and would likely present with ulnar forearm pain and weakness of hand intrinsics.
