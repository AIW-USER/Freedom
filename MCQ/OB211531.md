## Question

A 43-year-old man presents with left arm pain and numbness involving the wrist and thumb that began when moving furniture 5 weeks ago. He denies any gait abnormalities. On physical examination, neck extension, rotation and bending towards the left shoulder reproduces his symptoms. Abducting his left shoulder for more than several minutes improves his symptoms. He has weakness to wrist extension on the left. He has 3+ bilateral patellar reflexes. His MRI is depicted in Figures A and B. ## Options

1. Prophylactic immunosuppresants (interferon beta) may slow the disease progression
2. EMG and NCS are required to determine further management
3. His symptoms will most likely resolve with nonoperative treatment, including physical therapy and NSAIDs
4. Due to the location of the pathology, the affected nerve root would exit below the pedicle of the same numeric level (C6 nerve root exits below C6 pedicle)
5. Physical examination will likely show a positive Hoffman sign

## Correct option

**3. His symptoms will most likely resolve with nonoperative treatment, including physical therapy and NSAIDs**

## Answer explanation

This patient's clinical history, examination (Spurling sign), and imaging studies are consistent with left sided C6 radiculopathy from C5-6 disc herniation. The natural sequelae of this condition is generally favorable, with reported success of over 75% with nonoperative treatment.

Cervical radiculopathy is manifested by dysfunction of cervical nerve roots and presents as pain radiating from the neck into the distribution of the affected root. In the acute setting, cervical radiculopathy generally has a self-limited clinical course, with nonoperative treatment being the appropriate initial approach for most patients. Surgical decompression is reserved for failure of nonoperative management.

*Rhee et al. published a review article on cervical radiculopathy.* **They reported** that over 75% of patients will respond to nonoperative management with spontaneous improvement, and that surgical decompression relieves arm pain in 80-90% of cases with either anterior or posterior approaches. **They concluded** that in the absence of severe or progressive neurologic deficits, nonoperative treatment should be the initial treatment for most patients with cervical radiculopathy.

*Rao et al. published an instructional course lecture on degenerative cervical spondylosis.* **They reported** that cervical spondylosis can be categorized broadly into three clinical syndromes including axial neck pain, cervical radiculopathy, and cervical myelopathy. Cervical radiculopathy refers to pain, sensory findings, or a neurologic deficit in a dermatomal distribution in the upper extremity, with or without neck pain. **They concluded** that most patients with cervical radiculopathy respond well to an initial trial of nonoperative management.

*Cook et al. published on the reliability and diagnostic accuracy of clinical special tests in patients seen for cervical dysfunction.* **They reported** that in the diagnosis of cervical dysfunction, the patella reflex had a sensitivity and specificity of 56% and 33%, respectively. **They concluded** that none of the tests evaluated in their study, when performed individually or in combinations were effective for screening.

Figures A and B are cervical MRI sagittal and axial cuts depicting left-sided C5-6 disc herniation.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Prophylactic interferon beta may slow disease progression in patients with multiple sclerosis. This patient is exhibiting signs of cervical radiculopathy.
Answer 2: While EMG and NCS may be helpful in differentiating symptoms caused from peripheral neuropathy verses a cervical radiculopathy, they are not necessary and are infrequently required to diagnose and treat cervical radiculopathy.
Answer 4: Due to the location of the pathology, the affected nerve root would exit ABOVE the pedicle of the same numeric level (C6 nerve root exits ABOVE C6 pedicle)
Answer 5: A positive Hoffman sign is suggestive of cervical myelopathy. This patient is exhibiting signs of cervical radiculopathy, not myelopathy.
