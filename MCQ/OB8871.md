## Options

1. Clear cell sarcoma; t(12;22)
2. Myxoid liposarcoma; t(12;16)
3. Synovial sarcoma; t(X;18)
4. Alveolar rhabdomyosarcoma; t(2:13)
5. Ewing sarcoma; t(11;22)

## Correct option

**2. Myxoid liposarcoma; t(12;16)**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a myxoid liposarcoma which is associated with a t(12;16) translocation.

Myxoid liposarcomas occur in patients aged 30-60 and are typically intermediate grade. However, high-grade liposarcomas (especially with >10% round cells) can metastasize. Skeletal metastasis of myxoid liposarcoma can be unusual and extrapulmonary sites such as the retroperitoneum, bone marrow, and spine can be involved.

*Noble et al.* **identified** 8 patients that developed skeletal metastases from myxoid liposarcoma. **They found** a high false negative rate of detecting skeletal metastases utilizing x-ray, CT, or bone scan. MRI was found to be the most sensitive study. The authors remark that MRI can assess bone marrow (which was the site of metastasis) as opposed to x-ray, CT, and bone scan, which are more sensitive for changes in cortical bone.

*Schwab et al. report on 33 patients with known skeletal metastasis from myxoid liposarcoma.* PET scan and bone scan were 16% and 14% sensitive for detection of skeletal metastasis, respectively. **They recommend** whole-spine MRI for the detection of early spinal metastasis as part of the staging process. This could potentially identify early metastatic lesions before the onset of back pain or neurologic compromise.

Figure A is a lateral knee radiograph with evidence of posteromedial soft tissue swelling. Figure B is a coronal T1-weighted MRI demonstrating an ovular mass in the medial gastrocnemius, hypointense compared to surrounding fat. Figure C is a coronal T2-weighted MRI demonstrating the same heterogeneous mass with hyperintensity compared to the surrounding tissue. Figure D demonstrates an intermediate grade tumor with proliferating lipoblasts in a myxoid stroma.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1, 3, 4, 5: The histology and clinical history suggests myxoid liposarcoma, which is associated with a t(12;16) translocation, and can also be less commonly be associated with a t(12;22) translocation.
