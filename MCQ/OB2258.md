## Question

A 4-month old male is currently being treated with serial casting for a congenital equinovarus deformity of his right foot. Development of which of the following foot deformities is associated with failed nonoperative treatment and indicates the need for posteromedial release?

## Options

1. Rocker-bottom
2. Metatarsus adductus
3. Calcaneovalgus
4. Skew foot
5. Planovalgus

## Correct option

**1. Rocker-bottom**

## Answer explanation

A rocker-bottom deformity occurs when serial casting is continued in the setting of a very tight gastrocnemius-soleus complex and an uncorrected hindfoot. In this setting, a posteromedial release and tendoachilles lengthening are needed.

Muscle contractures contribute to the commonly seen equinovarus deformity. These follow the mnemonic “CAVE”: Cavus (tight intrinsics, FHL, FDL), Adductus of the forefoot (tight tibialis posterior), Varus (tight tendoachilles, tibialis posterior, tibialis anterior), and Equinus (tight tendoachilles). Treatment of congenital club foot begins with the Ponseti method of serial manipulations and casting. The Ponseti method has a success rate of >90%, however, sometimes posteromedial releases are needed. The development of rocker-bottom deformity is seen with failed nonoperative management when correction of equinus is attempted prior to fully correcting hindfoot varus. This causes dorsiflexion to occur through the midfoot rather than the hindfoot, which results in the predictable deformity.

*Dobbs et al. review the long-term follow up of patients with clubfeet who were treated with extensive soft tissue releases.* **They report** that the majority of patients had significant limitations in foot function, most notably in range of motion and quality-of-life measures. **They conclude** that the ultimate degree of functional impairment is correlated with the extent of soft tissue release.

*Morcuende et al. review the rate of corrective surgery after the advent of the Ponseti method in the treatment of congenital idiopathic clubfoot.* **They report** a 98% success rate of nonoperative treatment when using the Ponseti method with an average time to full correction of only 20 days. **They conclude** that the Ponseti method is a safe and effective treatment for congenital idiopathic clubfoot and radically decreases the need for surgical correction.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2 and 4: Metatarsus adductus is typically a self-limiting condition. However, if surgical intervention is necessary, metatarsus adductus and skew foot may be treated with a lateral column shortening, medial column opening osteotomy, and numerous metatarsal osteotomies in an attempt to correct the deformity.
Answer 3: Calcaneovalgus deformities resolve with stretching and serial casting. A posteromedial release is not part of the treatment for this deformity.
Answer 5: Pes planovalgus (flatfoot) is typically treated conservatively, however, rarely, a calcaneal lengthening osteotomy may be used for refractory cases.
