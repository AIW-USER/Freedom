## Question

A 63-year-old man presents with tricompartmental knee osteoarthritis and undergoes a primary total knee arthroplasty. He does well for 6 years, however, aseptic loosening develops and undergoes revision total knee arthroplasty. The surgeon is having difficulty exposing the retained implants and is considering performing a tibial tubercle osteotomy (TTO). Which of the following is true regarding this approach in revision total knee arthroplasty?

## Options

1. An absolute contraindication to TTO includes its use in the setting of infected revision cases
2. It has higher risk for extensor mechanism lag compared to quadricep V-Y turndown
3. It is the most common method to increase exposure after a standard medial parapatellar approach
4. Long tibia stems preclude its use due to inability to fix the TTO back to the tibia with screws
5. TTOs display very low rates of nonunion at 1-year follow-up

## Correct option

**5. TTOs display very low rates of nonunion at 1-year follow-up**

## Answer explanation

Tibial tubercle osteotomy (TTO) is considered a complex surgical approach to total knee replacement (TKA) in which the patellar tendon insertion site is mobilized through a tibial tubercle bone block and later repaired after final implants are placed. TTOs display a surprisingly high union rate, often at or near 100%.

There are various techniques in order to gain further exposure to the joint space in complex primary or revision total knee arthroplasty. The TTO approach is particularly utilized in the setting of rheumatoid arthritis, patellar baja, valgus deformities, or when the patella cannot be subluxed to facilitate access to the knee joint. The TTO is not without caveats, as it requires post-operative restrictions in range of motion until the osteotomy site heals roughly 4-6 weeks after surgery. Despite this, TTOs display a surprisingly low complication rate with union rates consistently reported at or near 100% in various studies.

Dr. Abdel and Dr. Della Valle discuss the indications for performing a TTO during total knee arthroplasty procedures. They report their operative technique and methodology of fixing the osteotomy site using 16-gauge wires. They recommend utilizing a TTO if the quadriceps snip fails to provide adequate exposure, as the VY turndown characteristically leads to some degree of postoperative extension lag.

*Divano and colleagues performed a systematic review regarding outcomes and complications of TTOs in the setting of revision TKAs.* **They report** radiographic healing near 100% even in a two-staged fashion for periprosthetic joint infection (i.e. performing TTO during stage 1, fixing back to the native bone during stage 2). **They conclude** that a strict surgical technique can minimize complications and lead to better results.

*Punwar and colleagues review their institution’s utilization of the TTO in the setting of revision TKA.* Their technique included the utilization of an extended tibial tubercle osteotomy fragment (7cm in length) without a step cut for both single-stage (noninfected) and two-stage (infected) cases. All patients went onto union without extension lag, however with a slower return to function in two-staged cases. **They conclude** that TTO is a safe and reproducible procedure when exposure needs improving in revision knee arthroplasty.

Incorrect Answers:
Incorrect Answer 1: The TTO approach has been well described in the setting of stage 1 and stage 2 revisions for knee peri-prosthetic joint infections with good outcomes and surprisingly low complication rates even when performed in a staged fashion.
Incorrect Answer 2: V-Y turndown is an extensive approach and can be performed if the quadriceps snip (quadriceplasty) does not provide the required exposure for implant removal. However, V-Y turndown approaches are notorious for the expected postoperative extension lags due to the relative lengthening of the quadricep tendon.
Incorrect Answer 3: The most commonly utilized method to obtain increased exposure in total knee arthroplasty is the quadricep snip because it is inconsequential to function outcomes and no need for changes to post-operative rehabilitation.
Incorrect Answer 4: Long tibial stems do not preclude the use of TTO for surgical approach. The tibial tubercle can be fixed with diverging angled screws or by utilizing cerclage wires through holes made in the tubercle and native tibia harvesting site.
