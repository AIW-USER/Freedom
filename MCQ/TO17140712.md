## Question

2. CDP

## Correct option

**1. MED**

## Answer explanation

Bipartite patella is known as a developmental anomaly and is usually regarded as a variant of normal ossification. It is seen in about 2 percent of the population and is more common in males (male-to-female ratio is 9: 1). It is bilateral in most patients and usually asymptomatic. It can, however, also be a cause of anterior knee pain.

Eric Saupe described the most frequent types of bipartite patella. In type 1, the patella is divided by a transverse split in the lower third. In type 2, there is a longitudinal split which divides the patella in an outer quarter bone fragment and an inner three-quarter bone fragment. In type 3, there is an oblique split which divides the patella into a larger inferomedial fragment and a smaller superolateral fragment.

Double layered patella, however, is another, more rare form of bipartite patella. It was first described by Buttner in 1925 and is now considered as a pathognomonic sign for multiple epiphyseal dysplasia (MED). It consists of two layered patellar segments with a coronal septum dividing them. The anterior segment is embedded in the quadriceps and patellar tendons, and it possesses its own layer of hyaline cartilage facing the posterior segment. The posterior segment forms the articulating surface facing the trochlea

![[TO17140712-2.png]]

DLP may cause a variety of clinical problems, including anterior knee pain, clicking or locking, and patellar dislocation. Often there is a delayed and painful movement of the posterior patellar segment that has no tendinous insertions, which causes a painful snapping of the patella.

When symptomatic, a surgical intervention should be considered. Fusion of the two patellar parts appears to be the most successful method of treatment, safeguarding the function of both the anterior and the posterior segment. Another option is the resection of either the anterior or the posterior segment.
