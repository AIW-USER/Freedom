## Answer explanation

Displaced tibial tubercle fractures present with acute knee pain and swelling, with the inability to straight-leg raise. These are typically treated with isolated screw fixation as depicted in Figure D (answer 4).

Tibial tubercle fractures are predominantly seen in the adolescent male athlete population and are caused by concentric activation of the quadriceps during jumping or eccentric contraction during forced knee flexion. Patients typically present with knee swelling, pain over the tibial tubercle, and, in some instances, a disrupted extensor mechanism. Fractures are classified by the Ogden classification, which groups fractures based on their degree of propagation (Illustration A). Type I injuries are those with minimal displacement and may be treated without surgical intervention. Types II, III, and IV fractures require open reduction and internal fixation with either partially threaded cannulated screws or headless compression screws. An arthrotomy or arthroscopy may also be utilized to assess adequate reduction of the intra-articular surface.

*Haber et al. published a retrospective review of 236 tibial tubercle fractures to describe the presentation and outcomes of this particular injury.* **They found** that patients with higher BMIs had more complex fracture patterns, while those with Osgood-Schlatter disease had lower complexity fractures. **They found** that a majority of patients underwent reoperation, mostly for hardware removal, and a minority of patients had residual pain at the tibial tubercle postoperatively. Only 2% of patients developed compartment syndrome. **They concluded** that the treatment of tibial tubercle fractures can be non-operative or operative, depending on the fracture pattern, but regardless, most patients can return to sport.

*Pretell-Mazzini performed a systematic review of 23 articles and 336 tibial tubercle fractures to assess the presentation, treatment strategies, and outcomes of this fracture pattern.* **They reported** that the majority of fractures were Type 3, that there was no difference in Osgood-Schlatter incidence between fracture patterns, and 94% of patients returned to sport at an average of 29 weeks post-injury. The most common complication was hardware irritation resulting in patellar bursitis. **They concluded** that current treatment strategies for this injury are effective in facilitating return to sport and that fractures with intra-articular involvement may warrant advanced imaging due to their association with additional intra-articular structures.

Images:
Figure A is an MRI demonstrating a mid-substance ACL tear.
Figure B is a radiograph demonstrating patella baja and swelling superior to the patella, which suggests the presence of a patellar sleeve avulsion fracture or a quadriceps tendon rupture. There is additionally a small fracture fragment from the superior patella that supports this diagnosis.
Figure C is a radiograph demonstrating fragmentation at the tibial tubercle, which is consistent with a diagnosis of Osgood-Schlatter disease.
Figure D is a radiograph demonstrating a displaced tibial tubercle fracture with intra-articular extension through the epiphysis.
Figure E is a radiograph demonstrating a patellar dislocation without an osteochondral fracture. There is a small bony fleck consistent with a medial patellofemoral retinaculum avulsion fracture.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: This imaging is consistent with an injury to the ACL and would not be best treated with screw fixation
Answer 2: This demonstrates patella baja and swelling superior to the patella, suggestive of a quadriceps tendon rupture. This would not be best treated with screw fixation solely
Answer 3: This imaging demonstrates findings consistent with Osgood-Schlatter disease, which is a more chronic condition that does not typically present with loss of knee extension.
Answer 5: This imaging is consistent with a patellar dislocation, which, in the setting of no other history of dislocation and no osteochondral fracture, would typically be initially treated with non-operative management.
