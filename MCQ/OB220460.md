## Question

A 45-year-old right-hand dominant man presents to your clinic with persistent pain over the lateral aspect of his right elbow for the past six months. He reports the pain is exacerbated by gripping and wrist extension activities, such as opening jars and lifting objects. Physical examination reveals tenderness to palpation over the lateral epicondyle and pain with resisted wrist extension with the elbow in extension. There is no evidence of instability or neurological deficits. Based on the likely diagnosis and understanding of the underlying pathophysiology of this condition, which of the following represents the MOST appropriate initial management strategy?

## Options

1. Corticosteroid injection directly into the lateral epicondyle
2. Initiation of a nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID) regimen
3. Occupational therapy focusing on activity modification, eccentric strengthening, and pain management techniques
4. Surgical release of the extensor carpi radialis brevis origin
5. Ultrasound-guided platelet-rich plasma (PRP) injection

## Correct option

**3. Occupational therapy focusing on activity modification, eccentric strengthening, and pain management techniques**

## Answer explanation

Given the understanding of lateral epicondylosis as a chronic tendinopathy rather than an inflammatory condition, initial management focuses on addressing biomechanical factors and promoting tendon healing through targeted therapy (Answer Choice 3).

The current understanding of lateral epicondylosis consists of a degenerative process with angiofibroblastic dysplasia and increased nerve sensitization, rather than acute inflammation. Therefore, initial treatment should target the underlying biomechanical imbalances, promote tendon adaptation through eccentric exercises, educate the patient on activity modification to reduce aggravating factors, and employ pain management strategies. Occupational therapy provides a comprehensive approach to address each of these treatment arms.

*Stegink-Jansen et al. synthesize the current knowledge on the pathology of lateral epicondylosis, its impact on tendon function, and the rationale for various tissue-level treatments and ergonomic interventions.* **The authors emphasize** the chronic degenerative nature of the condition and the importance of addressing biomechanics to promote tendon healing. **They conclude** that the understanding of lateral epicondylosis as a tendinopathy requires treatment (including therapy and ergonomic modifications) that addresses tissue-level changes and functional deficits.

*Regan et al. evaluated histopathological evidence from patients with chronic refractory lateral epicondylitis,* **demonstrating** the absence of significant inflammatory cell infiltration. **The authors highlight** degenerative changes, vascular proliferation, and collagen fiber disorganization at the common extensor origin. **They conclude** that chronic lateral epicondylosis is primarily a degenerative tendinopathy rather than an inflammatory condition, influencing the rationale for treatment.

*Nirschl et al. described the surgical treatment for lateral epicondylitis and introduced the classic term "angiofibroblastic dysplasia" to characterize the pathological findings, including the presence of vascular and fibroblastic proliferation rather than acute inflammation.* **They concluded** that the distinct pathological features of lateral epicondylosis shift it away from a purely inflammatory model, influencing modern treatment approaches.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: While corticosteroid injections may provide short-term pain relief, they do not address the underlying tendinopathy and have not been shown to provide sustained benefit, with potential risks of tendon attenuation and rupture.
Answer 2: Given the understanding that lateral epicondylosis is not primarily an inflammatory condition, NSAIDs are unlikely to provide significant or lasting relief and have an increased risk of gastrointestinal side effects.
Answer 4: Surgery is typically reserved for cases that have failed a prolonged course of non-operative management.
Answer 5: While showing some promise in research, the long-term efficacy and widespread consensus on PRP for initial management of lateral epicondylosis are still evolving, and it is not typically the first-line treatment.
