## Correct option

**4. Figure D**

## Answer explanation

Figure D represents a metaphyseal corner fracture, which has a high specificity for non-accidental trauma.

Physicians are mandated to report cases of non-accidental trauma, and orthopedic surgeons should always have a high suspicion for these injuries. Corner fractures (Figure D) are injuries that occur as discrete avulsions at the metaphysis and are highly specific for non-accidental trauma. Other fractures with the same associations are metaphyseal lesions that occur at the junction of the metaphysis and physis, bucket handle fractures (Illustration A), long bone fractures in a nonambulatory child, and posteromedial rib fractures (Illustration B). A skeletal survey should be performed on any patient in which there is a concern for non-accidental trauma.

*Kocher et al. review the orthopedic aspects of child abuse.* **They report** that fractures are the second most common presentation of child abuse cases, with skin lesions being the most common. **They conclude** that the most commonly presenting fractures are metaphyseal corner fractures, multiple fractures in various stages of healing, posterior rib fractures, and long bone fractures in children < 2-years-old.

*Kleinman et al. review the radiographic and histopathologic findings in infants with metaphyseal lesions secondary to abuse.* **They report** that characteristic lesions such as “corner fractures” and “bucket-handle fractures” occur through the most immature portion of the metaphyseal bone. **They conclude** that not all lesions may be seen radiographically and that postmortem analysis of potentially involved metaphyses should be evaluated in cases of suspected infant homicide.

Figure A demonstrates an extraphyseal distal radius fracture. Figure B is a displaced Salter-Harris II fracture of the distal tibia with an associated fibula fracture. Figure C shows a Salter-Harris IV fracture of the proximal phalanx of the index finger. Figure D demonstrates a proximal tibial metaphyseal corner fracture, which is highly concerning for non-accidental trauma. Figure E is a Gartland Type III supracondylar humerus fracture. Illustration A shows a distal femoral bucket handle lesion in a patient who was a victim of child abuse. Illustration B represents posterior rib fractures, that are also commonly a sign of non-accidental trauma.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1-3, 5: The radiographic appearance of these lesions are not highly specific for non-accidental trauma.
