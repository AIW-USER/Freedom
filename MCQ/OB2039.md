## Question

Which of the following would be the most appropriate treatment for a patient with asymptomatic Paget's disease?

## Options

1. Supportive therapy
2. Calcitonin
3. Vitamin D and Calcium
4. Bisphosphonates
5. Monoclonal-B antibody

## Correct option

**1. Supportive therapy**

## Answer explanation

Asymptomatic patients with Paget's disease are initially treated with observation and supportive therapy.

Paget's disease is the second most common chronic bone-remodelling disorder in the US after osteoporosis. Asymptomatic patients do not require pharmacological therapy as long as they remain asymptomatic. Supportive therapies include physiotherapy, orthoses, and over-the-counter anti-inflammatories. Although bisphosphonates have been touted as a first line treatment for symptomatic Pagets, their efficacy compared to symptomatic supportive treatment alone has been called into question

*Langston et al. conducted a randomized trial that compared the results of symptomatic treatment versus intensive bisphosphonate therapy in patients with Paget's disease.* *Clinical fractures occurred in 46 of 661 patients (7.0%) in the treatment group compared with 49 of 663 patients (7.4%) in the symptomatic treatment group.* **They concluded** that bisphosphonates did not show a significant beneficial impact on pain, quality of life or fracture incidence.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Calcitonin is generally only indicated in symptomatic patients who are unable to tolerate bisphosphonates.
Answer 3: Calcium and vitamin D supplementation are used to prevent hypocalcemia when taking bisphosphonates or calcitonin
Answer 4: Bisphosphonates are recommended as first-line drug treatment for patients with symptomatic Paget's disease
Answer 5: Immunotherapy is not used in the treatment of Paget's disease
