## Question

A 59-year-old man comes to the clinic with a chief complaint of foot pain that radiates along his arch with toe-off. The patient sustained an injury to his foot during a motorcycle accident in his early 20s that was not fixed surgically. A cut from his CT scan at the time of his injury is shown in Figure A. You counsel him that his exam and clinical symptoms are most consistent with post-traumatic midfoot arthritis and suggest that he may need a midfoot arthrodesis. Which of the following is most closely associated with the clinical presentation of midfoot arthritis?

![[q218651-a-screenshot-2023-07-22-at-6-26-58-am.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Peroneus brevis tendon contracture
2. Forefoot abduction
3. Hindfoot varus
4. Maintenance of longitudinal arch with weightbearing
5. Loss of talocrural angle

## Correct option

**2. Forefoot abduction**

## Answer explanation

The patient sustained fractures of the midfoot and is now presenting with the sequelae of midfoot arthritis, which typically includes midfoot and longitudinal arch collapse, forefoot abduction, and hindfoot valgus.

Midfoot arthritis, when not due to primary osteoarthritis, is often post-traumatic in nature and typically involves the metatarsal cuneiform, intercuneiform, and naviculocuneiform joints. Patients present with arch pain with toe-off due to midfoot collapse in the setting of an abducted forefoot and hindfoot valgus. As this process occurs over time, the Achilles tendon can shorten and cause concomitant equinus contracture. Treatment begins with anti-inflammatory medications, activity modification, and orthotic prescriptions, which can include longitudinal arch supports, cushioned heel inserts, and/or stiff-soled shoes with rocker-bottom soles. When these measures fail, a midfoot arthrodesis can be performed with or without an Achilles lengthening and a hindfoot realignment procedure to correct the pathologic valgus.

Kurup, et al. provide a current concepts review of midfoot arthritis. The authors note that although post-traumatic arthritis and primary osteoarthritis are the most common pathologies encountered, surgeons need to rule out inflammatory causes and neuropathic etiologies before starting treatment. They conclude that successful union is linked to a satisfactory outcome in up to 90% of patients following arthrodesis if the principles of careful patient selection, pre-operative planning, adequate joint preparation, and stable fixation are followed.

Nemec, et al. reviewed outcomes for midfoot arthrodesis in cases of midfoot arthritis. They noted significant improvement in AOFAS midfoot scores as well as patient satisfaction with follow-up averaging 4 1/2 years. They also noted high rates of union (92%) and very few complications. The authors concluded that primary or degenerative arthritis of the midfoot with refractory symptoms was treated with midfoot arthrodesis with good results and a low incidence of nonunion.

Figure A is a cut from a CT scan of the foot demonstrating fractures of the 1st thru 4th metatarsals and cuboid.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Equinus contracture of the Achilles may coexist as part of the pathologic picture of symptomatic midfoot arthritis, and can be addressed at the same time as midfoot arthrodesis. Peroneus brevis tendon contracture is not a frequent association.
Answer 3-5: Midfoot and longitudinal arch collapse with weightbearing, in conjunction with an abducted forefoot and hindfoot valgus, represent the common findings in patients with symptomatic midfoot arthritis. The talocrural angle is used to assess fibular length and is not used in assessing the pathologic changes seen with progressive midfoot arthritis.
