## Question

A 35-year-old male presents for evaluation of a painful hallux valgus deformity. They would like to proceed with surgical intervention. Which of the following imaging and patient characteristics would suggest that a Lapidus procedure is needed?

## Options

1. Hallux Valgus Angle (HVA): 8; Intermetatarsal angle (IMA): 4; History of diabetes
2. HVA: 25 ; IMA: 10; Congruent metatarsophalangeal joint
3. HVA: 42; IMA: 24; Tarsometatarsal (TMT) joint hyper-mobility
4. HVA: 18; IMA: 12; Arthritis at the metatarsophalangeal joint
5. HVA: 35; IMA: 10; History of spastic cerebral palsy

## Correct option

**3. HVA: 42; IMA: 24; Tarsometatarsal (TMT) joint hyper-mobility**

## Answer explanation

The presence of ligamentous laxity at the TMT joint in the setting of severe hallux valgus deformity suggests that a Lapidus procedure would be indicated (Answer 3).

Hallux valgus, also known as a bunion, is a foot deformity characterized by valgus malpositioning of the first phalanx. Initial management consists of nonoperative modalities, including shoe modification or orthoses. Those who fail conservative measures may be indicated for surgical intervention, which encompasses varying techniques whose indications depend on the severity of the deformity and other patient characteristics. The Lapidus procedure is one such surgical technique and involves arthrodesis of the TMT joint. It is indicated in those with severe disease (defined as HVA> 40 degrees, IMA > 15 degrees) or arthritis/ligamentous laxity of the first tarsometatarsal joint.

*Shah et al. performed a retrospective review of patients who underwent a modified Lapidus procedure to determine its efficacy in correcting increased distal metatarsal articular angle (DMAA).* **They found** significant decreases in DMAA, HVA, and IMA after undergoing a modified Lapidus procedure. **They concluded** that the modified Lapidus procedure is effective in correcting deformity without the need for additional osteotomies.

*Santrock and Smith reviewed the modified Lapidus approach.* **They highlighted** the indications, procedural steps, biomechanics, and outcomes of this particular procedure in depth. **They concluded** that this particular procedure offers the potential to achieve substantial correction in patients with hallux valgus, in addition to offering benefits to a wide range of disease severity.

*Do et al. performed a systematic review of modern Lapidus procedure techniques and their outcomes.* **They found** in their review that the most common complication was hardware pain and that the Lapdius procedure led to reductions in the IMA and the HVA. **They concluded** that there is still a paucity of literature describing the outcomes of the Lapidus procedure, which warrants further investigation.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: These radiographic findings fall within normal ranges and would not indicate the patient for a Lapidus procedure.
Answer 2: These radiographic findings indicate mild disease and would indicate the patient for a distal metatarsal osteotomy, such as a Chevron or Mitchell osteotomy.
Answer 4: The presence of arthritis at the first metatarsophalangeal joint would indicate the patient for metatarsophalangeal arthrodesis, not a Lapidus procedure.
Answer 5: A history of spasticity in a patient with a painful hallux valgus deformity would indicate the patient for a metatarsophalangeal arthrodesis.
