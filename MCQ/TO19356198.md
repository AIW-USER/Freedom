Chapter: FNB 2023 — 2023 MCQs

## Question

In CTEV, ideal bracing position [FNB 2023]

## Options

1. ER 15 degrees, abduction 70 degree
2. Dorsiflexion 10-15 degrees, ER 15 degree
3. Abduction and ER 70 degrees and dorsiflexion 15-20 degree
4. Dorsiflexion 5 degrees with ER of 20 degree

## Correct option

**3. Abduction and ER 70 degrees and dorsiflexion 15-20 degree**

## Answer explanation

Ans: C

EXPLANATION: -

Congenital talipes equinovarus, or clubfoot, is a common deformity where the affected foot is turned inward. It occurs in every 1.2 in 1000 live births and is the most common musculoskeletal congenital birth defect. Males are more commonly affected than females and up to 50% of cases are bilateral.
The main stay of treatment is serial casting and bracing. Failures of conservative treatment where surgical options are chosen also require bracing to ensure maintenance of correction.
There are three major categories of brace designs: ankle foot orthosis (AFO), Wheaton brace, and foot abduction brace (FAB).
When the most important demand is maintenance of the clubfoot correction, the outcome is best achieved by using a foot abduction brace (FAB). A FAB consists of two shoes connected by a bar. The shoes are straight laced, which has no curvature, and they can go on either foot. The distance between the inside edges of the heels of the shoes is equivalent to the child’s shoulder width. This distance is the most comfortable for the child and prevents knee or hip problems.
Foot abduction brace that is recommended to the child should have the following features:
• Shoes attached to bar at 60 - 70˚ abduction on the affected or both sides
• Dorsiflexion 10 - 15˚
• Straight medial border
• Heels as far apart as shoulder width
• Low posterior cut (holds the foot in the shoe, prevents heel slip)
• Well-rounded heel counter or heel cup
FABs that are commonly used are:
• Steenbeek foot abduction brace
• Mitchell brace
• Dobbs brace
• Iowa brace
• Dennis Brown brace

If the deformity is unilateral, the external rotation on the affected foot should be set to 60 to 70° and on the unaffected foot to 30 to 40°. The bar should be of the length between the child's shoulders and should be bent to allow for 10-15° of dorsiflexion.
