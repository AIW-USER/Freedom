## Question

Patient is having an ACL tear, high grade tear of LCL, Popliteus and biceps femoris injury. PCL is thickened but intact. Ideal recommended treatment is (Arthroscopy- knee arthroscopy- multiligamentous knee injuries)

## Options

1. ACL reconstruction plus PLC repair
2. Brace and LCL reconstruction
3. PLC reconstruction and delayed ACL reconstruction after 6 weeks
4. ACL + PLC reconstruction

## Correct option

**4. ACL + PLC reconstruction**

## Answer explanation

This is a case of ACL tear combined with posterolateral corner (PLC) tear.

The PLC consists of three major static stabilizing structures and several secondary dynamic and static stabilizers which collectively provide both the primary restraint against varus translation and also resist posterolateral rotation of the tibia relative to the femur. The three major static stabilizers are the fibular collateral ligament (FCL or LCL), the popliteus tendon (PLT), and the popliteofibular ligament (PFL). PLC injuries have been reported to account for up to 16% of all knee ligament injuries and are commonly associated with cruciate ligament injuries.

 The most common mechanisms of injury to the PLC involve a posterolateral-directed force to the anteromedial tibia, knee hyperextension, and/or severe external rotation of the tibia while the knee is partially flexed.

Classification of posterolateral corner injuries-

Fanelli Classification
Type A: 10° increase in external rotation of the tibia.
PFL, PLT

Type B: 10° increase in external rotation of the tibia. Slight varus relaxation (5–10 mm in varus load test)
PFL, PLT, FCL

Type C: 10° increase in external rotation of the tibia. Severe varus relaxation (> 10 mm in varus load test)
PFL, PLT, FCL, capsular avulsion, cruciate ligament

Treatment : combined ACL plus PLC injuries are best treated by concurrent ACL and PLC reconstruction within 3 weeks. End-to-end isolated midsubstance repair of the acutely injured PLC structures has higher failure rates in comparison to reconstruction so PLC repair is not recommended.

 Currently, repairs of PLC structures is reserved for

1. Injuries that involve avulsions, especially of structures avulsed off the fibular head (i.e., FCL, the PFL, and the biceps tendon) or PLT, which need to be reattached to the bone.

 2. Injuries involving the capsule and the lateral meniscocapsular ligaments that are anchored and sutured to the underlying bone.

Answer: 4
