## Question

An 80-year-old female with a T score of -3.0 presents with acute and severe back pain following a ground level fall 6 weeks ago. Since the fall she has been largely sedentary secondary to pain. She is unable to go on daily walks as she was able to do prior to the fall. She is dependent on daily narcotic medications for pain control, resulting in severe constipation. On physical exam, she has tenderness over the T11 spinous process. The patient is otherwise healthy and the remainder of her physical exam is unremarkable. ## Options

1. Percutaneous instrumentation with pedicle screws and a rod from T9 to T11
2. Percutaneous T11 vertebral augmentation
3. Percutaneous biopsy, CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis.
4. Blood cultures, ESR, CRP, and CT guided aspiration of T10/11 disc space with cultures
5. Oral steroid taper and further observation

## Correct option

**2. Percutaneous T11 vertebral augmentation**

## Answer explanation

The clinical presentation is a subacute T11 osteoporotic compression fracture that continues to cause debilitating pain despite 6 weeks of nonoperative treatment. At this time a vertebral augmentation procedure is indicated.

Cement augmentation of painful osteoporotic compression fractures consists of percutaneous stabilization of the vertebral body with PMMA. Vertebroplasty is performed by injecting PMMA into the fractured vertebra while kyphoplasty uses an inflatable balloon to create vertebral body cavity prior to the injection of cement. Pain relief is thought to be from the elimination of micromotion. In 2010, the AAOS strongly recommended against the use of vertebroplasty, but concluded that kyphoplasty was an option for the management of painful osteoporotic compression fractures.

*Savage et al. in a review, report that kyphoplasty provides the added advantage of vertebral body expansion and correction of kyphosis prior to the injection of cement.* Kyphoplasty may also provide with pain relief for a longer duration. Complications of cement augmentation relate to cement extravasation, embolization of cement, and new fractures. Increased stiffness from cement augmentation leads to higher loads on adjacent segments and may increase the risk of fracture to these segments.

*Manson et al. reviewed techniques for techniques for the treatment of osteoporotic compression fractures.* **They report** that restoration of spinal alignment with kyphoplasty improves the functional capacity of the respiratory, cardiac, gastrointestinal, and musculoskeletal systems. **They conclude** that kyphoplasty is effective to alleviate fracture pain.

*Kallmes et al. performed a multi-center trial which randomly assigned 131 patients with one to three painful osteoporotic vertebral compression fractures to undergo either vertebroplasty or a simulated procedure (placebo).* **They found** no difference in improvements in pain and pain-related disability scores between the two groups at one month. At one month vertebroplasty does not improve clinical condition compared to placebo.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Surgical stabilization is not indicated in this patient with as her spine is stable and she is neurologically intact. Instrumentation should also extend past the level of the fracture.
Answer 3: This patient has no signs of malignancy (constitutional symptoms, weight loss). Radiographs may reveal a lytic process, a blastic process, or a combination of the two. MRI would be used to evaluate for neurologic compression and may reveal a mass.
Answer 4: The clinical presentation and imaging studies are not consistent with a discitis.
Answer 5: Oral steroid taper will not improve this patients condition. Observation is not appropriate as the patient is dependent on narcotic medications and has become severely constipated.
