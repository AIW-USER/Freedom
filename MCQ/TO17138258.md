## Question

Given below is a radiograph of a child whose parents presented to you with complain of left leg deformity. On examination there is an anterolateral bowing of the leg. There is no history of trauma or focal tenderness or abnormal mobility. At this stage what would be the ideal surgical option offered to the child?

![[TO17138258-1.jpg]]

## Options

1. Osteotomy, deformity correction and intramedullary nailing
2. . Ostetomy deformity correction and plating
3. 4 in 1 procedure
4. Growth modulation

## Correct option

**4. Growth modulation**

## Answer explanation

Above radiograph is an anterolateral bowing in a child presenting in the early age with cystic changes in the tibia without any fracture or pseudoarthrosis formation which is confirmed by clinical findings and the radiograph.

A recent study published by Laine et al in JBJS, 2020 have studied the effect of guided growth in congenital pseudoarthrosis of Tibia where fracture or pseudoarthrosis has not formed. Distal tibia anterolateral growth modulation by 8 plate is the latest in the management of CPT. Results have shown resolution of the cystic lesion along with deformity correction gradually over years.

Osteotomy and internal fixation are not indicated early in CPT because of their tendency to go into non union. 4 in 1 procedure is the treatment of choice when a fracture has occurred or pseudoarthrosis has formed.

Classifications of CPT:

1. Andersen's classification

Andersen's classification of CPT (1973) differentiates the morphology of the pseudarthrosis as dysplastic, cystic, or sclerotic types, in addition to a clubfoot type that arises because of accompanying abnormalities

2. Boyd's classification

The Boyd's classification (1982) identifies six types of CPT. In type I there is anterior bowing with tibial defect. Type II and III are characterized by pseudarthrosis with hour glass constrictions and bony cyst, respectively. In type IV, pseudarthrosis is present with sclerotic segments and march fracture. Type V is characterized by a complementary dysplastic fibula. In type VI, an intraosseous neurofibroma or schwannoma is evident.

3. Crawford's classification

Crawford's classification of CPT (1986) identifies four types of CPT all with anterolateral bowing of the tibia. In type I, the medullary canal is preserved and cortical thickening at the apex of the deformity might be observed; patients with this type usually have a good prognosis; some may not even have a fracture. Type II is defined by presence of thinned medullary canal, cortical thickening, and trabeculation defect. The dominant finding in type III is a cystic lesion, which may be fractured; patients with this type of pseudarthrosis tend to experience early fracture and, therefore, require early treatment. In type IV, pseudarthrosis is present with tibial and possibly fibular nonunion.

4. EI-Rosasy–Paley–Herzenberg's classification

El-Rosasy's et al. classification (2007) considers patient's condition at time of presentation (relation to previous treatment), radiological (atrophic or hypertrophic), and mobility of pseudarthrosis site (stiff or mobile). Three types of CPT are identified. Type I is defined by atrophic bone ends, mobile pseudarthrosis without previous surgical intervention. Type II is defined by atrophic bone ends, mobile pseudarthrosis, previous surgical intervention with or without retained hardware, while type III is defined by broad bone ends, stiff pseudarthrosis and with or without previous surgical intervention.

Role of fibula: Role of fibula

Pseudarthrosis or hypoplasia of the fibula is associated with CPT in two-thirds of the cases.Yet the need for fibular surgery remains controversial. Based on findings from Johnston, resection of fibular pseudarthrosis is necessary in order to achieve optimal limb alignment and union. If the fibula is intact, it is necessary to perform a fibular osteotomy. Dobbs et al. reported a higher rate of union in patients in whom fibular pseudarthrosis was resected as compared with those in whom fibular pseudarthrosis was not resected.

Choi et al. classified fibula in two types: Type A (mild-A1, moderate-A2) includes a fibula of normal integrity in the presence of established atrophic-type CPT, whereas type B (mild-B1, moderate-B2, severe-B3) has atrophic-type CPT with concomitant fibular pseudarthrosis.

4 in 1 treatment is the most practiced algorithm for management of CPT with B2 and B3 type of fibula. 4 in 1 procedure was described by Choi et al. It consists of resection of the sclerotic ends on both sides and onlay graft with cortical chip in front and behind with an aim to achieve healing mass between proximal and distal segments of the tibia and fibula. This technique is supplemented with concomitant osteotomy and lengthening with ilizarov assistance if expected shortening is more than 2-3 cms.

Answer: 4
