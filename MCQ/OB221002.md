## Question

An 11-year-old female with a high-lumbar myelomeningocele presents with progressive difficulty sitting and recurrent pelvic pressure sores. Her medical history includes hydrocephalus managed with a ventriculoperitoneal shunt, neurogenic bowel and bladder, mild intellectual disability, and feeding dysfunction. She is being evaluated for surgical correction of a progressive spinal and pelvic deformity. Given her complex medical and functional needs, she is referred to a multidisciplinary clinic including orthopaedics, neurosurgery, urology, rehabilitation medicine, social work, and nutrition. What is a benefit of a multidisciplinary team evaluation in this patient?

## Options

1. Increased perceived independence and self-efficacy
2. Improved long-term psychosocial well-being
3. Lower rates of surgical revision
4. Reduced progression of spinal deformity
5. Reduced postoperative complications

## Correct option

**5. Reduced postoperative complications**

## Answer explanation

Multidisciplinary team management reduces postoperative complications in patients with myelomeningocele undergoing spine surgery (Answer choice 5).

A multidisciplinary team evaluation improves surgical outcomes by optimizing medical comorbidities, nutritional status, bladder and bowel function, skin integrity, and shunt function prior to surgery, all of which are major risk factors for postoperative complications in patients with myelomeningocele. Coordinated preoperative planning also improves perioperative decision-making, anesthetic safety, wound management, and discharge planning, reducing risks such as infection, wound breakdown, and prolonged hospitalization.

In medically complex pediatric patients, complications often arise from non-orthopaedic factors such as poor nutrition, unmanaged neurogenic bladder, pressure ulcer risk, or untreated shunt issues. A team-based approach ensures that these risk factors are addressed proactively, leading to lower complication rates, fewer unplanned readmissions, and safer surgical care, even though it does not fundamentally change neurologic function or long-term deformity progression.

*Bradko and colleagues described a multidisciplinary framework for managing scoliosis in adolescents and adults with spina bifida.* **The authors highlight** that coordinated care among orthopaedics, neurosurgery, rehabilitation, urology, and pulmonology improves surgical planning, complication management, and long-term functional outcomes. Their main conclusion is that a team-based, patient-centered approach is essential to optimizing deformity correction while minimizing medical and surgical risks in this complex population.

*Bradko and colleagues also published a systematic review to evaluate the benefits, risks, and outcomes of scoliosis surgery in adolescents and adults with myelomeningocele.* **The authors found** that surgery can improve sitting balance, posture, and quality of life, but is associated with high complication rates, including infection, hardware failure, and wound problems. The main conclusion is that scoliosis surgery may offer meaningful functional benefits in select patients, but careful patient selection and risk counseling are critical due to substantial perioperative morbidity.

*Conklin and colleagues sought to establish evidence-based orthopedic care guidelines across the lifespan for individuals with spina bifida.* Their guidelines address management of hip instability, foot deformity, scoliosis, fractures, mobility optimization, and prevention of skin and overuse complications. Their main conclusion is that standardized, proactive orthopedic surveillance and intervention can improve mobility, independence, and long-term musculoskeletal health in patients with spina bifida.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: Increased perceived independence and self-efficacy — Perceived independence is largely determined by baseline neurologic function and functional mobility rather than care model.
Answer 2: Improved long-term psychosocial well-being — Psychosocial outcomes in myelomeningocele are influenced more by social environment and cognitive factors than by multidisciplinary clinic structure.
Answer 3: Lower rates of surgical revision — Revision rates are primarily driven by deformity severity, growth, and implant-related factors rather than team-based evaluation.
Answer 4: Reduced progression of spinal deformity — Multidisciplinary care improves monitoring and timing of intervention but does not alter the natural history of deformity progression.
