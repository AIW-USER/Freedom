## Question

Figure A is a lateral radiograph of a knee with an asterisk over a specific anatomic landmark with the pre-operative template lines used to locate it. Reconstruction of what ligamentous structure should have its femoral insertion anatomically recreated at this point?

![[q9026-a-lateral-knee-xray-lines-star.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. The anteromedial bundle of the anterior cruciate ligament
2. The posterolateal bundle of the anterior cruciate ligament
3. The posterior cruciate ligament
4. The lateral collateral ligament
5. The medial patellofemoral ligament

## Correct option

**5. The medial patellofemoral ligament**

## Answer explanation

The asterisk is sitting on a point about 1 mm anterior to a line extending from the posterior femoral cortex, 2.5 mm distal to the posterior origin of the femoral condyle, and proximal to Blumensaat's line, which is the anatomic femoral insertion of the medial patellofemoral ligament (MPFL).

The MPFL is the primary restraint to lateral patellar subluxation, and appropriate reconstruction of the MPFL can restore proper tracking to an unstable patella. Correct graft placement is vital to ensure appropriate tracking and prevent excess tension across the graft through a range of motion. Radiographic parameters have been described to help ensure intraoperatively that grafts are placed at the anatomic insertion for best outcomes. Grafts should be inserted at this anatomic point and tensioned at 30° of flexion.

Schöttle et al. provide a review of the anatomic insertion site of the MPFL on the medial femoral condyle by cadaveric dissection. They assert that a reproducible radiographic landmark for the MPFL insertion is 1 mm anterior to a line extending from the posterior femoral cortex, 2.5 mm distal to the posterior origin of the medial femoral condyle, and proximal to the posterior point of Blumensaat's line.

*Wijdicks et al. provide a cadaveric review of the medial structures of the knee, including the MPFL.* **They noted** that on an AP radiographic view, the MPFL attaches 42.3 +/- 2.1 mm from the femoral joint line. On a lateral projection the insertion is 8.9 +/- 2.0 mm from adductor tubercle and within the anteroproximal quadrant

Figure A is a lateral radiograph of the knee with three lines drawn on it. One line is an extension of the posterior femoral cortex from proximal to distal. The other two lines are perpendicular to the first, and one intersecting the origin of the femoral condyle, and the other intersecting the posterior-most aspect of Blumensaat's line. These lines help block out the area of the femoral insertion of the MPFL as identified by the yellow star. Illustration A is a lateral knee radiograph with an oval over the approximate femoral origin of the ACL. Illustration B is a lateral knee radiograph with an oval over the approximate femoral origin of the PCL. Illustration C depicts attachment sites for the LCL and popliteus tendon on the lateral side of the knee.

Incorrect answers:
Answers 1 and 2: The two bundles of the ACL insert on the medial aspect of the lateral femoral condyle. On a lateral radiograph the femoral origin of the ACL would be posterior and distal to Blumensaat's line (within the notch). See Illustration A.
Answer 3: The PCL inserts on the lateral aspect of the medial femoral condyle and would be posterior to Blumensaat's line (within the notch). See Illustration B.
Answer 4: The LCL originates from the lateral femoral condyle as seen in Illustration C.
