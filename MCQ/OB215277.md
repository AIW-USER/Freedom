## Options

1. Intramedullary nailing
2. Begin workup for metastatic disease
3. Wide excision with reconstruction
4. Interventional radiology guided biopsy
5. Curretage and bone grafting

## Correct option

**5. Curretage and bone grafting**

## Answer explanation

Recurrence of unicameral bone cysts (UBC) often are treated without surgery, however, patients with symptomatic lesions may benefit from curettage and bone grafting to help the UBC heal.

Unicameral bone cysts are benign, serous fluid-filled bone lesions commonly found in the metaphysics of bones, most commonly the proximal humerus. Approximately 50% of these lesions will present with pathologic fracture, however are typically asymptomatic. Multiple treatment options exist for symptomatic or UBCs associated with pathologic fracture including immobilization alone, steroid injection, curettage, and bone grafting, and a combination of these options. There is no ‘gold standard’ treatment for these lesions and they often recur regardless of what treatment is chosen.

*Sung et al. reviewed 167 patients 20 years or younger who had unicameral bone cysts treated with either steroid injection, curettage, and bone grafting, or a combination of steroids, demineralized bone matrix, and bone marrow aspirate.* The recurrence rate for each treatment was 84%, 76%, and 63%, respectively after the initial treatment. Steroid treatment alone had statistically higher rates of failure compared to curettage and combination therapy. Age was also an independent predictor of failure after one treatment.

*Canavese et al. reviewed 46 children with unicameral bone cysts treated with either autologous bone marrow injection, methylprednisolone acetate injection, or percutaneous curettage alone.* *At the 2 years follow-up, adequate healing was identified in 70% of the percutaneous curettage group, 41% of the methylprednisolone acetate injection group, and 21% of the autologous bone marrow injection group.* **They conclude** that percutaneous curettage provides better healing likely due to mechanical disruption of the cyst membrane.

*Haidar et al. retrospectively reviewed 19 children with UBCs who underwent various treatments to determine the relationship of UBC distance from the growth plate and healing.* Seven children with cysts within 2cm of the physis and 12 greater than 2cm from the physis. They had successful healing in 10 children: one within 2cm, and nine greater than 2cm. **They conclude** that the healing of UBCs is more likely the further they are from the physis after surgical treatment.

*Wilkins wrote a review article on UBCs.* *He discusses clinical features, pathogenesis, and treatment options.* Treatment options include various injection and surgical interventions. The most common complication per the review is the recurrence of the lesion.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Intramedullary nailing would not be indicated in this patient.
Answer 2: This patients imaging and clinical story is consistent with a UBC, which does not require metastatic disease workup.
Answer 3: Wide excision and reconstruction is not indicated in treatment of a benign UBC
Answer 4: Biopsy is not required at this time as the patient has a clinical story and imaging consistent with a UBC.
