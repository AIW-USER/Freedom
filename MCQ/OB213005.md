## Question

A 70-year-old high functioning female is referred to your office from her primary care for an acute "foot drop" and anterior ankle pain. She states this started one week prior after hearing a loud "pop" with pain in the front of her left ankle. Figure A shows her active dorsiflexion against gravity bilaterally. She is otherwise neurovascularly intact. An MRI of her left ankle is obtained and shown in Figure B. What associated procedure is most likely indicated at the time of surgical treatment of her condition?

## Options

1. AFO
2. Posterior tibial tendon (PTT) transfer
3. Primary repair with possible gastrocnemius recession
4. Flexor hallucis longis (FHL) tendon transfer
5. Superior peroneal retinaculum (SPR) repair and fibular groove deepening

## Correct option

**3. Primary repair with possible gastrocnemius recession**

## Answer explanation

The diagnosis of an acute tibialis anterior rupture in an active patient should be treated with primary repair and possible gastrocnemius lengthening prior to tensioning the repair to achieve adequate dorsiflexion.

Acute anterior tibialis tendon ruptures are an uncommon injury and often treated surgically to facilitate a return to pre-injury functional dorsiflexion and gait. When performing an end to end direct tendon repair prior to tensioning the repair ankle dorsiflexion should be assessed with the knee in full extension. If the patient is unable to achieve at least 5° of passive dorsiflexion it is recommended that a gastrocnemius recession be performed.

*Ellington et al. present their results of surgical repair of an acute anterior tibial tendon repair or tendon transfer in 15 patients with an average age of 70.6.* **They found** a lower average dorsiflexion strength in the operative versus non-operative limb and greater dorsiflexion range of motion in the operative limb compared to the native limb in the patients who received a gastrocnemius recession. **They conclude** the surgical repair or tendon transfer of anterior tibialis ruptures leads to satisfactory strength and range of motion with a low complication rate.

*Sammarco et al. reviewed their surgical outcomes of 19 tibialis anterior tendon ruptures.* **They found** recovery of functional dorsiflexion and improvement in gait was found in 18/19 cases. **They concluded** that surgical treatment of anterior tibialis ruptures can be beneficial to most patients regardless of age, sex, comorbidities, or delay in diagnosis.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Failure to identify a tendon rupture and treatment with an AFO would likely lead to a lower level of function and permanent gait disturbance.
Answer 2: A PTT transfer would be indicated in cases of permanent foot drop
Answer 4: For a chronic tibialis anterior tendon rupture one can consider an EDL or EHL tendon transfer or interpositional graft however the use of FHL for this injury has not been described.
Answer 5: There is no indication of peroneal tendon instability so no SPR is indicated.
