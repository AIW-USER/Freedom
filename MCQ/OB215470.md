## Question

Which of the following is true of the posterior oblique ligament in the knee?

## Options

1. Provides static resistance to valgus load with the knee in full extension
2. Provides external rotational stability when the knee is in >85 degrees of flexion
3. Is the most anterior structure originating on the medial femoral condyle
4. Resides in the most superficial layer of the medial knee
5. Is the most posterior structure attaching to the fibular head

## Correct option

**1. Provides static resistance to valgus load with the knee in full extension**

## Answer explanation

The posterior oblique ligament is part of the posteromedial knee and provides static restraint to valgus load when the knee is in full extension.

Collectively, the posterior oblique ligament (POL), semimembranosus tendon, oblique popliteal ligament, posteromedial joint capsule, and the posterior horn of the medial meniscus comprise the posterior medial corner of the knee. The posterior oblique ligament originates from the adductor tubercle and inserts on the posteromedial tibia near the articular margin. It provides static restraint to valgus load and internal rotation with the knee in full extension.

*LaPrade et al. review the anatomy and biomechanics of the medial side of the knee.* **They report** that the superficial medial collateral ligament, posterior oblique ligament, and the deep medial collateral ligament are the three most important ligaments in maintaining medial sided stability. **They conclude** that while the medial patellofemoral ligament and hamstring tendons do provide some stability, the most biomechanically important structures resisting valgus force are the three previously mentioned.

*Warren et al. review the supporting structures and layers of the medial side of the knee.* **They report** the anatomic structures of the medial knee are found consistently in a 3-layer pattern. **They conclude** that the posterior oblique ligament is located within layer 2 with the semimembranosus, superficial MCL, and MPFL.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: The posterior oblique ligament mainly resists valgus and internal rotation in full extension.
Answer 3: The posterior oblique ligament originates posterior to the MCL.
Answer 4: The most superficial layer contains the sartorius and the patellar retinaculum.
Answer 5: The posterior oblique ligament is part of the posteromedial corner and does not insert on the fibula.
