## Question

A 15-year-old female high school soccer player presents to your clinic with right knee pain, stating she had a collision with an opponent one week ago where she twisted the knee and felt it "pop out of place". She is currently using crutches to get around. On examination, she has a large effusion and tenderness to palpation over the medial femoral epicondyle and medial patellar facet. There is no joint line tenderness. Lachman and posterior drawer testing are stable, and there is no varus/valgus laxity at 0/30°. McMurray and Thessaly testing do not cause pain. Her patella translates two quadrants with apprehension near the final endpoint. A radiograph is seen in Figure A. What is the next best step?

*Figure A*

## Options

1. NSAIDs and physical therapy
2. Aspiration and cortisone injection
3. Magnetic resonance imaging
4. Open reduction and internal fixation
5. Diagnostic arthroscopy with loose body removal +/- MPFL repair/reconstruction

## Correct option

**3. Magnetic resonance imaging**

## Answer explanation

This patient has an osteochondral avulsion fracture from the medial patella, and an MRI should be performed prior to intervention to better characterize the lesion and the other structures of the knee.

Patellar instability is a common condition, especially in younger athletes, that occurs as a result of trauma in the setting of predisposing ligamentous and bony abnormalities. The medial patellofemoral ligament (MPFL) is the most important ligamentous restraint to lateral patellar translation and is usually injured in the setting of a lateral patellar dislocation. An osteochondral avulsion of the medial patella may also occur at the attachment point of the MPFL. In cases without fracture where patients' effusions quickly recede and they are able to regain motion and weight-bearing capabilities, physical therapy and anti-inflammatories are appropriate, although an MRI can be obtained to rule out any chondral injuries or loose bodies. In cases of osteochondral avulsion fractures, mechanical symptoms, or large persistent effusion, an MRI should be obtained to characterize the osteochondral lesion in addition to any other joint injuries before surgery is recommended. Bony fixation of the lesion or fragment removal and MPFL repair/reconstruction have both been described to treat these osteochondral avulsions.

*Seeley et al. reviewed osteochondral injury after patellar dislocation in children and adolescents in 46 patients ranging from age 11-18 years.* **They noted** that 76% of osteochondral fractures were located at the patella, with the rest originating from the lateral femoral condyle and 44% being missed by plain radiographs. Concomitant injury to the MPFL was noted in 98% of participants, and 68% ultimately underwent surgery. **They concluded** that IKDC (International Knee Documentation Committee) scores were better after patellar injuries compared to lateral femoral condyle injuries.

*Lee et al. looked at adolescent patellar dislocations and subsequent patellar osteochondral fractures in nine patients of average age 14.6 years.* **They noted** that after MRI, four of the nine patients underwent fixation of the fragment, while five had loose body removal and microfracture. **The authors concluded** that the IKDC scores for the fixation group were lower compared to the removal and microfracture group.

Figure A is a merchant radiograph demonstrating an osteochondral avulsion fracture of the medial patella after a lateral patellar dislocation.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Given the displaced osteochondral fragment and the presence of a large effusion, an MRI should be obtained to better understand the nature of the injury before nonoperative treatment is decided upon.
Answer 2: With an acute injury and the presence of an osteochondral fragment on radiographs, aspiration, and injection should not be performed until further imaging is done.
Answers 4 & 5: An MRI should be performed prior to any surgery in this patient population to confirm the presence of additional injuries and evaluate the severity/location of the injury being treated.
