Chapter: Arthroscopy MCQs — Multiligamentous Knee Injuries

## Question

A soccer player after sustaining a twisting injury complaining of knee instability. His examination reveals increased external rotation on the dial test at both 30 degree and 90 degrees. Which is a correct option for the management?

## Options

1. Surgical management with arthroscopic PCL reconstruction
2. Surgical management with PLC reconstruction
3. Arthroscopic PCL reconstruction with LaPrade reconstruction
4. Larson’s technique of anatomical PCL reconstruction.

## Correct option

**3. Arthroscopic PCL reconstruction with LaPrade reconstruction**

## Answer explanation

Explanation: Positive dial test (> 10° external rotation asymmetry compared to opposite leg) at both 30°and 90° is consistent with PLC and PCL injury. This patient thus, needs both PCL and posterolateral corner (PLC) reconstructions. Two most commonly used methods of PLC reconstruction are Larson’s reconstruction and LaPrade reconstruction. Three most important components of posterolateral corner reconstruction are lateral collateral ligament (LCL), popliteus tendon (PT) and popliteofibular ligament (PFL).

Larson technique reconstructs LCL and popliteofibular ligaments. LaPrade technique is more anatomical and gives robust reconstruction of all the three main components of PLC namely LCL, PT and PFL.

Answer: 3
