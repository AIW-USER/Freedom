## Question

A 30-year-old female is brought to the emergency department after a high-speed motor vehicle collision. She has an obvious deformity to the right ankle with significant swelling. At the time of her definitive surgery, you identify a concomitant syndesmotic injury. Which of the following is true regarding the presence of syndesmotic injury in the setting of this fracture?

![[q219026-a-screen-shot-2023-05-01-at-4-41-45-pm.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. The incidence is approximately 50%
2. There is less likely to be a syndesmotic injury when there is a segmental fibula fracture
3. There is a higher nonunion rate
4. There is a higher amputation rate
5. It does not impact infection risk

## Correct option

**4. There is a higher amputation rate**

## Answer explanation

A concomitant syndesmotic injury in the setting of a tibial plafond fracture indicates a high-energy injury. It has been associated with higher rates of deep infection, unplanned reoperation, and amputation (Answer 4).

Tibial pilon fractures are high-energy injuries. They often involve comminution, articular involvement, and significant soft tissue injury. Treatment traditionally entails ankle-spanning external fixation, followed by definitive open reduction and internal fixation once the soft tissues permit. They are fraught with complications including wound healing problems, infection, nonunion, and arthritis. These outcomes have been shown to be even worse in the setting of a concomitant syndesmotic injury. It is felt that a concomitant syndesmotic injury indicates a higher energy injury that is more prone to complications.

*Purcell et al. performed a retrospective matched cohort study looking at the outcomes of tibial shaft and pilon fractures with associated syndesmotic injury versus those without.* *They matched 30 patients with a syndesmotic injury to 60 patients without.* **They found** that those with syndesmotic injuries had higher rates of deep infection, compartment syndrome, unplanned reoperation, and amputation. **They found** the presence of a segmental fibula fracture was much more common in patients with a syndesmotic injury.

*Herzog et al. retrospectively reviewed the outcomes of 30 patients with traumatic proximal tibiofibular dislocations.* This injury was seen in conjunction with both tibial shaft and tibial plateau fractures. It was associated with a 29% risk of compartment syndrome and a 36% risk of peroneal nerve palsy, with 70% of these palsies not recovering.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The incidence of syndesmotic injury was reported to be approximately 3.4%.
Answer 2: Patients with syndesmotic injuries are more likely to have a segmental fibula fracture.
Answer 3: There was no difference in union rates between those with syndesmotic injury versus those without.
Answer 5: Patients with syndesmotic injuries were more likely to develop a deep infection.
