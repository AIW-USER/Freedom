## Question

A 40-year-old female returns with increasing posterolateral ankle pain and swelling. She has previously attempted physical therapy, bracing and custom orthotics however has persistent and limiting pain. Physical examination demonstrates 3 of 5 eversion strength against resistance with no evidence of peroneal instability. The position of her hindfoot does not correct with the Coleman block test. ## Options

1. Platelet rich plasma injection
2. Peroneus brevis debridement and repair
3. First ray dorsiflexion osteotomy, peroneus brevis debridment and repair
4. Calcaneal osteotomy, first ray dorsiflexion osteotomy, peroneus longus to brevis transfer
5. Peroneus brevis debridement and repair with amniotic tissue augmentation

## Correct option

**4. Calcaneal osteotomy, first ray dorsiflexion osteotomy, peroneus longus to brevis transfer**

## Answer explanation

The patient presents with a symptomatic peroneus brevis tear and cavovarus alignment that does not completely correct with the Coleman block test. In a patient with peroneal pathology or ankle instability in the presence of hindfoot varus, correction of the structural deformity must be considered in conjunction with soft tissue repair to minimize the risk of recurrence.

Cavovarus alignment frequently leads to increased stress on lateral tissue structures including the lateral ligaments and peroneal tendons. When non-surgical measures have failed, surgical treatment must address the underlying structural deformity including hindfoot varus and forefoot/midfoot cavus. This can be performed via either lateral closing wedge (Dwyer) osteotomy or lateral closing wedge subtalar arthrodesis based on the rigidity of the deformity and presence of subtalar arthritis.

Deben and Pomeroy review the presentation and management of the subtle cavus foot, including management of associated peroneal tendon pathology. The authors emphasize the importance of careful preoperative examination, including the Coleman block test to assist with operative treatment of the subtle cavus foot.

*Grasset et al. present a small case series (17 patients) who were managed surgically for peroneal tendinopathy.* **The authors note** that 6 of the 17 patients required a concomitant hindfoot osteotomy to address associated hindfoot varus malalignment

Figures A and B demonstrate weight-bearing lateral and axial X-rays with evidence of cavus alignment based on the horizontal position of the talus and varus alignment of the calcaneal tuberosity. Figures C and D are axial and coronal MRI images demonstrating significant peroneal tendinopathy with cystic changes of the peroneus brevis. Images A and B are the postoperative X-rays after lateral closing wedge calcaneal osteotomy and first ray dorsiflexion osteotomy performed in combination with peroneal debridement and longus to brevis transfer

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1. Platelet-rich plasma has not been shown to have a role in the management of peroneal pathology.
Answer 2: Peroneal treatment is indicated but this does not address the hindfoot varus.
Answer 3: The patient does not correct with the Coleman block test, indicating that addressing the first ray in isolation will not correct the hindfoot varus.
Answer 5: Peroneal treatment is indicated however this option does not address hindfoot varus. Biologic augmentation of soft tissue repair in the foot and ankle has yet to be supported with rigorous studies.
