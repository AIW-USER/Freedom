## Question

A 14-year-old girl presents to your pediatric orthopaedic clinic with concerns that she is no longer fitting her shoes for the last 5 months. A clinical image of her foot is depicted in Figure A. Radiographs are obtained and her physes are closed. Which of the following statements regarding her condition and the appropriate management is most accurate?

![[q2243-a-screen-shot-2020-01-30-at-7-42-42-pm.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. She should undergo a biopsy prior to any treatment
2. Her condition will improve with antifungal medications
3. The cosmetic result after surgical intervention is often favorable
4. Complete amputation of her second ray decreases recurrence rate
5. Epiphysiodesis of the affected ray results in the best overall prognosis

## Correct option

**4. Complete amputation of her second ray decreases recurrence rate**

## Answer explanation

This patient's clinical history and image is consistent with local gigantism of her second toe, which is preventing her from properly fitting her shoes. In local gigantism, complete ray amputation decreases recurrence rate of the hyperplastic toe and would allow her to properly fit her shoes.

Local gigantism of the foot is a deformity characterized by overgrowth of some or all tissue elements in the foot. It is most commonly a nonhereditary disorder, characterized by a disproportionate overgrowth of only one toe, often the second, with normal toe nails. Initial treatment is observation, with close monitoring of the affected region. The major objectives of surgical intervention are for improved cosmesis and function. While epiphysiodesis techniques may be successful in patients with open physes, patients with closed physes should undergo bony reduction procedures and/or ray amputation to prevent recurrence.

*Turra et al. presented a series of patients with local gigantism of the foot.* **They reported** that the pathogenesis of gigantism relates to nerve trunk disease and that many cases of foot gigantism may represent undiagnosed neurofibromatosis with limited clinical findings. **They highlighted** that complications of surgery include deformity recurrence, cutaneous necrosis, and infection, with often poor cosmetic result.

*Guidera et al. investigated limb overgrowth and the diagnosis of Klippel-Trenaunay-Weber or Proteus syndromes in a case series.* **They reported** that these syndromes compromise of orthopedic conditions including localized gigantism, angular deformities, scoliosis, and skin anomalies. **They recommended** conservative management be the mainstay of orthopedic care, and complete ray resection to prevent forefoot enlargement and deformity recurrence.

Figure A depicts local gigantism of the second toe.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Local gigantism of a toe does not warrant a biopsy.
Answer 2: This patient has local gigantism of her second toe, not a fungal infection.
Answer 3: The cosmetic result after the preferred surgical intervention (ray resection) is often poor.
Answer 5: A Syme amputation (ankle disarticulation) would be excessive for second toe local gigantism and would have no significant effect on overall prognosis.
