## Question

A 17-year-old lacrosse player is taken out of a playoff game after colliding with another player and sustaining a concussion. During his off-field assessment, he is found to have a headache, photophobia, and mild nausea. If his concussion was sustained on a Friday, which of the following is the most appropriate course of action for his trainers to pursue on Monday?

## Options

1. Allow light aerobic activities even if headache is present as long as photophobia and nausea are resolved
2. Continue cognitive rest and refrain from all physical activity until complete symptom resolution
3. Enter an accelerated return-to-play protocol including sport-specific training drills
4. Restrict return-to-sport for a minimum of seven days regardless of symptom resolution
5. Start sub-symptom threshold aerobic exercise after baseline symptom reassessment

## Correct option

**5. Start sub-symptom threshold aerobic exercise after baseline symptom reassessment**

## Answer explanation

The patient has sustained a concussion with several baseline symptoms after initial contact. After reassessment of his symptoms, it is appropriate to begin sub-symptom threshold aerobic exercise to improve recovery following a sports-related concussion (Answer 5).

Concussions represent a subset of mild traumatic brain injury (mTBI) characterized by acute transient impairment of neurologic function secondary to an impulsive force transmitted to the head. Treatment typically involves immediate removal from play, followed by cognitive and physical rest for 24-48 hours, and subsequent completion of a graduated return to play protocol. Sub-symptom threshold aerobic exercise is now widely accepted as a way to improve recovery in athletes post sports-related concussion (SRC). While unrestricted activity resumption is not indicated until all symptoms have resolved, given that athletes are still vulnerable to a second injury while recovering from a concussion, it is important to select safe aerobic exercise with a low risk of re-injury that allows the athlete to remain below symptom exacerbation thresholds in order to obtain the benefits of exercise without worsening symptoms.

*Leddy et al. published on exercise as medicine for concussions.* **The authors note** that sport-related concussion (SRC) is a physiological brain injury that produces cerebral and systemic effects, including exercise intolerance, with exercise intolerance after concussion believed to be the result of autonomic nervous system (ANS) dysfunction. **They conclude** by presenting evidence that sub-threshold aerobic exercise normalizes this cerebrovascular physiological dysfunction and is “medicine” for patients with concussion and persistent post-concussive symptoms (PPCS).

*Lal et al. published a systematic review and meta-analysis reviewing the effects of physical exercise after a concussion.* *The authors included 14 studies and found that exercise significantly decreased the PCSS score, percentage of patients with symptoms of a concussion, and days off work (17.7 days vs 32.2 days) compared with control.* **They also found** that exercise improved the reaction time component of the Immediate Post-Concussion Assessment and Cognitive Testing (ImPACT) score without affecting the Balance Error Scoring System (BESS) score and neuropsychological parameters. **They concluded** that physical exercise appears to improve the PCSS score and symptoms in patients with a concussion; however, high-quality RCTs evaluating different intensities of exercise at different time points, for different durations after a concussion, for different races/ethnicities, and for sex needs to be conducted to evaluate a clear effect of exercise in patients with a concussion.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Light aerobic activities should be encouraged if the patient has no symptoms; however, if these activities reproduce any of his initial symptoms, he should not be allowed to continue.
Answer 2: The athlete does not need to refrain from all physical activity at this stage and can be allowed to progress along the return-to-play protocol if symptoms are not reproduced.
Answer 3: Unrestricted activity resumption should not be allowed after a concussion. The athlete should progress along the return-to-play protocol (Illustration A) as guided by his/her symptoms.
Answer 4: Return-to-sport does not need to be baselessly restricted to 7 days, and should progress along the protocol shown in Illustration A.
