## Options

1. Myxoid liposarcoma
2. Synovial sarcoma
3. Atypical lipomatous tumor
4. Pigmented villonodular synovitis
5. Myxoma

## Correct option

**1. Myxoid liposarcoma**

## Answer explanation

The clinical presentation and histology are most consistent with a myxoid liposarcoma.

Myxoid liposarcoma is a rare soft tissue sarcoma. A high-grade myxoid liposarcoma has a significant proportion of round cell morphology, vacuolated fat, lipoblasts, branching ("chicken wire") capillaries, and a myxoid stroma. Myxoid liposarcomas are associated with a translocation between chromosomes 12 and 16. This gives rise to the fusion protein CHOP-TLS. In contrast to myxomas (Illustration A), myxoid liposarcomas are significantly more cellular and have obvious lipoblasts.

*Kilpatrick et al. correlated the MRI characteristics with the clinical and histological characteristics of 7 patients with myxoid liposarcoma.* *All 7 cases occurred in the lower extremities.* **They demonstrated** that histologically, myxoid liposarcomas contain less than 10% mature fat and a large myxoid component, consistent with their overall low T1 signal and heterogeneous increase in T2 signal, and ultimately appearing very dissimilar to a lipoma.

Figure A is an Axial T1-weighted MRI demonstrating a hypointense soft tissue mass in the anterior compartment Figure B is an axial T2-weighted MRI sequence demonstrating the same heterogeneous, hyperintense soft tissue mass in the anterior compartment. Figure C is a histology slide of a myxoid liposarcoma. There are round cells, sparse mono-vacuolated lipoblasts, and branching capillaries set on a myxoid stroma.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 2: Synovial sarcomas are often peri-articular. They are typically hypercellular and spindle cell dominated.
Answer 3: Atypical lipomatous tumors typically demonstrate more mature fat, but no myxoid stroma
Answer 4: PVNS may have a similar appearance as shown on the MRI, however, the histologic specimen would show lipid-laden macrophages and hemosiderin deposits.
Answer 5: A myxoma would have this MRI appearance but would be much less cellular on the biopsy specimen.
