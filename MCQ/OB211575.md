## Question

Which of the following is true regarding patients undergoing surgical decompression for cervical spondylomyelopathy?

## Options

1. Postoperative C5 palsy is a permanent deficit that rarely improves
2. Surgical decompression does not result in any clinical improvement
3. Patients that develop dysphagia rarely experience improvement
4. Placement of anterior cervical plate >5 mm from adjacent disc space increases the risk for adjacent segment degeneration
5. Posterior cervical decompression is associated with higher infection rates than anterior based procedures

## Correct option

**5. Posterior cervical decompression is associated with higher infection rates than anterior based procedures**

## Answer explanation

Patients that undergo posterior cervical decompression have higher infection rates than patients who have anteriorly based procedures.

Surgical treatment of cervical spondylomyelopathy is aimed at the prevention of progressive neurologic deterioration that is characteristic of the natural disease course. Many patients undergoing surgical decompression experience significant improvement in patient-reported outcome measures, such as the modified Japanese Orthopedic Association (mJOA) score. However, posterior cervical decompression has been associated with higher infection rates compared to anterior surgical procedures.

*Fehlings et al. performed a large multicenter prospective study looking at outcomes of spinal cord decompression in patients with myelopathy.* **They reported** there was significant improvement across all patient-reported outcome measures at twelve-months postop with on the mJOA score having a difference in functional improvement from mild, moderate, and severe myelopathy patients. **The authors concluded** that surgical treatment of cervical myelopathy directly improves patient functional outcomes.

*Lim et al. performed a retrospective study on postoperative C5 palsy 710 patients undergoing surgical treatment for cervical myelopathy.* **They found** that C5 palsy occurred in 5.1% of cases with risk factors being greater than 3 surgical levels. Further, **they reported** that prolonged recovery occurred in patients with a C5 motor grade less than or equal to 2, multilevel paresis, and sensory involvement with intractable pain. **The authors concluded** that patients with C5 palsy will tend to recover within 6 months from the onset of symptoms, but the findings of this study may prognosticate patients who may require a prolonged period of recovery.

*Tetreault et al. performed a large multicenter international study of 614 patients undergoing surgical treatment for cervical myelopathy to determine preoperative factors to predict patient outcomes.* The multivariate analysis revealed 6 factors that correlated with mJOA score ≥16 which included baseline mJOA score, impaired gait, age, comorbidity score, smoking status, and duration of symptoms. **The authors concluded** that their prognostic model is not designed to determine which patients would benefit the most from surgery but to guide presurgical expectations during the consent process.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: Postoperative C5 palsy resolves in nearly all cases by 1-2 years postop.
Answer 2: Studies have demonstrated that patients with cervical myelopathy demonstrate significant clinical improvement at 1-year postop.
Answer 3: Postoperative dysphagia is a common complication of cervical spine surgery but the vast majority of cases will resolve with observation.
Answer 4: Placement of an anterior cervical plate within 5 mm of the adjacent disc space will lead to adjacent segment degeneration.
