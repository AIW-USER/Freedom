## Question

Hereditary motor-sensory neuropathy (HSMN) primarily affects the peripheral nervous system. Which of the following are features of the most common type of HMSN

## Options

1. Death of both upper and lower motor neurons in the motor cortex of the brain, the brain stem, and the spinal cord leads to muscle twitching and atrophy
2. Impaired oxidation of branched chain fatty acids leads to neurologic damage, cerebellar degeneration, and peripheral neuropathy
3. Genetic mutations in axons or myelin protein leads to severe, rapidly progressive neurological damage by adolescence and complete loss of ambulation
4. Genetic mutations in axons or myelin protein leads to leg muscle atrophy, loss of sensation and proprioception in early adulthood
5. Movement disorder characterized by degeneration of midbrain neurons

## Correct option

**4. Genetic mutations in axons or myelin protein leads to leg muscle atrophy, loss of sensation and proprioception in early adulthood**

## Answer explanation

Charcot-Marie-Tooth (CMT) disease is the most common type of hereditary motor-sensory neuropathies. It is characterized by genetic mutations in myelin proteins that lead to leg muscle atrophy and loss of sensation and proprioception in early adulthood.

Hereditary motor-sensory neuropathies (HSMN) include Charcot Marie Tooth (CMT) disease (Types 1 & 2) and Dejerine Sottas syndrome. CMT is inherited autosomally dominant (most common), recessive or X-linked. It is more common in males, but females will usually have worse symptoms of disease. Physical exam findings reveals symmetric elevation of arches, plantar flexed first ray, hindfoot varus, claw toes, decreased ankle jerks, and a flatfoot or toe-heel gait.

*Bertorini et al. reviewed CMT, including the phenotypes of the hereditary motor sensory neuropathies.* The basis of CMT neuropathy is an abnormal myelin protein leading to pathologic changes in the motor and sensory peripheral nerves. A weakened tibialis anterior/peroneus brevis is overpowered by a stronger peroneus longus/posterior tibialis and results in a plantar flexed first metatarsal and pronated forefoot. Hindfoot varus deformity develops secondarily and as the deformities become more fixed, an equinus deformity often occurs.

*Faldini et al. reviewed the surgical treatment of cavus foot in Charcot-Marie-Tooth Disease.* **They state** that a plantar fasciotomy, midtarsal osteotomy, the Jones procedure, and dorsiflexion osteotomy of the first metatarsal yielded adequate correction of flexible cavus feet in patients with Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease in the absence of fixed hindfoot deformity.

Illustration A shows the clinical findings of CMT. There is elevation of arches, plantar flexed first ray, and claw toes. A similar pattern can be seen in the hands as shown in Illustration B. Hands are less commonly affected but the disease is equally as debilitating.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Description of Amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS), which is a progressive motor neuron disorder characterized by involvement of anterior horn cells of the spinal cord. The cause is mostly unknown, although a small percentage (~5%) of patients have familial form of the disease.
Answer 2: Description of Refsum's disease, which is an inherited condition that causes vision loss, absence of the sense of smell (anosmia).
Answer 3: Description of Dejerine Sottas syndrome, which is an early onset demyelinating motor and sensory neuropathy that falls under the umbrella of hereditary motor-sensory neuropathies. It is not as common as CMT.
Answer 5: Description of Parkinson's disease, which is a degenerative disorder of the central nervous system mainly affecting the motor system. Parkinson's disease in most people is idiopathic.
