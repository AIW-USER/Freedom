## Question

A 65-year-old female presents to your office with complaints of dull/achy pain in the arch of her foot. The pain is worse with weight-bearing and is slowly becoming more consistent. She has tried oral and topical NSAIDs. Radiographs demonstrate joint space narrowing at the 2nd and 3rd tarsometatarsal joints. She asks why her pain is worse with activity. You explain to her that symptoms of midfoot arthritis are normally exacerbated by which phase of the gait cycle as shown in Figure A?

![[q218894-a-gait-cycle.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. A
2. B
3. C
4. D
5. E

## Correct option

**5. E**

## Answer explanation

The toe-off phase of the gait cycle exacerbates midfoot arthritis most compared to other phases of gait.

Midfoot arthritis can affect the tarsometarsal joints, the inter-cuneiform joints, or the naviculocuneiform joint. It is most commonly idiopathic but can be seen after a Lisfranc injury or other foot trauma. Patients typically report pain towards the toe-off phase of the gait cycle as the load is transferred from the hindfoot, through the midfoot, to the forefoot. NSAIDS, activity modification, and insoles/rocker bottom shoes are first-line treatments, with arthrodesis usually reserved for refractory cases.

*Chambers and Sutherland published a guide to gait analysis looking at the complex interactions of force going through the body during gait.* *They go into depth about the different elements of gait analysis which include the measurement of gait kinematics, kinetics, muscular activity, foot pressure, and energetics.* Understanding this data allows physicians to tailor certain procedures to the needs of their patients to maximize function.

*Yi et al. discussed the effect of carbon fiber insoles on lower limb kinetics in patients with midfoot arthritis.* **They noted** that in "mild," midfoot arthritis there was little change in VAS scores or loading patterns of the foot, while in "moderate," arthritis of the midfoot there was a shorter gait cycle, reduced VAS scores, and reduced tibialis anterior muscle activity. **They concluded** that these full-length carbon fiber insoles may be useful in improving pain and kinetics in patients with moderate midfoot arthritis.

Figure A demonstrates the phases of the gait cycle including (A) heel strike, (B) foot flat, (C) midstance, (D) heel off, and (E) toe-off.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1-4: Other phases of the gait cycle would not exacerbate midfoot arthritis as much as the toe-off phase.
