## Question

A 75-year-old male presents to the ED with posterior neck pain following a ground-level fall. On physical exam, he is tender to palpation along the posterior cervical spine. He is ambulatory and neurologically intact. Radiographs of the cervical spine are negative except for the finding of marginal syndesmophytes. What is the most appropriate next step in management?

## Options

1. Cervical immobilization, CT of the cervical spine, and X-rays of the thoracic and lumbar spine
2. Placement in a hard collar, NSAIDs, ice, rest, and 1-week outpatient follow-up
3. Flexion-extension radiographs of the cervical spine
4. Obtain X-rays of the thoracic and lumbar spine
5. MRI of the cervical spine

## Correct option

**1. Cervical immobilization, CT of the cervical spine, and X-rays of the thoracic and lumbar spine**

## Answer explanation

This patient has ankylosing spondylitis (AS) and requires immediate cervical immobilization and a CT scan of the cervical spine in order to rule out a fracture, which may not be visible on plain films in up to 50% of cases.

Ankylosing spondylitis places patients at an elevated risk for spinal fractures due to a high prevalence of osteoporosis and spinal rigidity (attributed to marginal osteophytes) resulting in long lever arms. Even in the absence of trauma, AS patients with acute back or neck pain should be assumed to have a fracture until proven otherwise. Special attention should be taken with positioning and transferring these patients, as fractures are often unstable. Due to the long lever arms in these patients, concomitant spinal injuries are not uncommon, and imaging the entire spine is recommended. Due to the difficulty in detecting these fractures alone, CT and/or MRI should be utilized to aid in diagnosis.

*Weinstein et al. retrospectively reviewed 105 cases of AS.* There was a 12% incidence of spinal fractures in this population, 8 of which resulted in paralysis. **The authors emphasize** the need for accurate radiographic assessment, immobilization, and prompt decompression and fusion when warranted to prevent neurologic injury.

*Werner et al. provided a review article on the etiology, diagnosis, and management of spinal fractures in AS patients.* **They note** the difficulty in radiographic assessment and stress the use of advanced imaging to pan-scan the entire spine. **They also note** the high complication rates associated with both operative and non-operative management and suggest consideration of both anterior and posterior fixation.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: It is inappropriate to discharge a patient with AS without obtaining advanced imaging in this setting.
Answer 3: Obtaining flexion-extension radiographs in an AS patient with a potentially unstable fracture may have catastrophic neurological consequences.
Answer 4: While imaging the entire spine is appropriate, the cervical spine should be further investigated with advanced imaging.
Answer 5: MRI is often combined with CT to diagnose spine fractures in AS patients, however, the initial screening modality should be a CT scan of the entire spine.
