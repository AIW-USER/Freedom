## Question

A 76-year-old healthy male presents to your office for follow-up. He previously underwent reverse total shoulder arthroplasty (rTSA) 1 year ago, which was performed by your partner who has since retired. In reviewing his operative note and post-operative radiographs, you see that he used a 155º rTSA prosthesis and the baseplate was implanted in the upper half of the glenoid. Which of the following complications should you be concerned with this patient experiencing in the future?

## Options

1. Acromial stress fracture
2. Dislocation
3. Infection
4. Scapular notching
5. Axillary nerve dysfunction

## Correct option

**4. Scapular notching**

## Answer explanation

Scapular notching is a possible complication after reverse total shoulder arthroplasty (rTSA), especially in cases of superior glenoid baseplate positioning in combination with the use of a 155º Grammont style prosthesis.

rTSA functions by moving the shoulder's center of rotation medially and inferiorly to allow for the deltoid to power the arm. While it is effective in improving pain and function, complications are also possible with rTSA. One of these complications is scapular notching, but the risk can be minimized with optimization of surgical technique. Positioning of the glenoid baseplate along the inferior rim of the glenoid is one of the most important technical points in order to prevent notching. Other ways include increasing the diameter and lateralization of the glenosphere, adding inferior tilt to the baseplate and using a humeral component with a more varus neck-shaft angle.

*Cheung et al. published a review of complications in rTSA.* *They started by discussing general indications for rTSA and provided an overview of potential complications.* As for scapular notching, **they note** that the incidence seems particularly dependent on glenosphere offset, glenosphere position, bone loss and the time the prosthesis is in place for.

*Gutierrez et al. evaluated the effects on abduction range of motion and inferior scapular impingement with different glenosphere diameters, offsets, glenoid baseplate positions, and humeral component neck-shaft angles.* **They noted** that a more lateral center of rotation (still medial to normal shoulder) increased the impingement free range of motion the most. **They also found** that decreasing the humeral neck-shaft angle and positioning the glenoid component more inferior had the greatest effect on minimizing scapular notching.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Acromial stress fracture risk would increase if the construct center of rotation was distalized (more inferior baseplate, larger humeral tray/poly, etc) as this places more tension on the deltoid and its insertion.
Answer 2: Dislocation risk would not be directly affected by placing the glenoid baseplate more superior or using a 155º prosthesis.
Answer 3: Positioning of the glenoid baseplate would not affect the risk of infection in an otherwise healthy patient.
Answer 5: Axillary nerve injury is possible at the time of surgery due to either direct laceration during glenoid implantation or neuropraxia secondary to over-lengthening of the construct.
