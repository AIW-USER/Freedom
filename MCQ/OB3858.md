## Question

A 55-year-old male without significant medical history presents with worsening pain from a 7-day-old puncture wound located on the plantar aspect of his forefoot. On exam, there is surrounding erythema and purulent drainage. Imaging demonstrates soft tissue edema, no identifiable foreign bodies, and no evidence of osteomyelitis. The organism most likely responsible for this patient’s soft tissue infection has which of the following characteristics?

## Options

1. Coagulase negative, non-hemolytic gram positive cocci
2. Coagulase positive, hemolytic gram positive cocci
3. Gram negative rod with a single polar flagellum
4. Gram negative rod with multiple flagella
5. Spore-forming gram positive rod with multiple flagella

## Correct option

**2. Coagulase positive, hemolytic gram positive cocci**

## Answer explanation

Staphylococcus aureus - which is coagulase-positive, hemolytic gram positive cocci - is the most common cause of soft tissue infections following a foot puncture wound. Pseudomonas, a gram-negative rod with a single flagellum, is the most common cause of osteomyelitis.

Foot puncture wounds are relatively common injuries that lead to soft tissue infection in approximately 10% of cases and osteomyelitis in 1-2% of cases. Staphylococcus aureus is the most common pathogen to cause soft tissue infection in these injuries while pseudomonas is the most common cause of osteomyelitis. Infection may have a delayed presentation (i.e., weeks). Thorough examination and imaging workup are essential to identify evidence of retained foreign bodies. MRI should be considered to rule out osteomyelitis. Tetanus booster should be administered to patients with outdated or unknown immune status.

*Lavery et al. reported on infected puncture wounds in 77 diabetic and 69 nondiabetic adults.* **The authors found** Staph aureus was the most common cause of soft tissue infections in both diabetic and nondiabetic adults, accounting for 69% and 36% of infections, respectfully. **The authors conclude** Staph aureus is the most common cause of soft tissue infection following foot puncture wounds.

*Laughlin et al. studied the prevalence of soft tissue infections, the prevalence of osteomyelitis, and the type of pathogens implicated after puncture wounds in 44 nondiabetic children (39 soft tissue infections, 8 bone infections).* *Staph aureus was implicated in 20 cases (54%) of soft tissue infection followed by Staph epidermidis in 7 cases (19%) and Pseudomonas in 6 cases (16%).* *Regarding osteomyelitis, pseudomonas was implicated in 6 cases (86%), Staph aureus in 1 case (14%), and Staph epidermidis in one case (14%).* **The authors conclude** Staph aureus is the most common cause of soft tissue infection following a foot puncture wound.

Incorrect Answers
Answer 1: Staphylococcus epidermidis is a non-hemolytic, coagulase-negative gram positive coccus commonly associated with periprosthetic joint infections.
Answer 3: Pseudomonas aeruginosa is a gram-negative rod with a single polar flagellum that produces a blue-green pigment in vitro. This organism is the most common cause of osteomyelitis after a foot puncture wound.
Answer 4: Salmonella is a gram-negative rod with multiple flagella (peritrichous) known to be a common cause of osteomyelitis in patients with sickle cell disease.
Answer 5: Clostridium tetani is a gram-positive rod with multiple flagella that cannot grow in the presence of oxygen. It produces tetanospasmin, which prevents the release of inhibitory neurotransmitters glycine and gamma-aminobutyric acid at motor nerve endings – leading to widespread activation of motor neurons.
