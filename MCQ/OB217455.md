## Question

A research fellow is presenting his most recent project to your group. He reviews his confidence interval calculation for the normally distributed population to be the mean +/- 2.58 standard deviations. Which of the following confidence intervals does this calculation represent?

## Options

1. 85%
2. 90%
3. 95%
4. 98%
5. 99%

## Correct option

**5. 99%**

## Answer explanation

Assuming a normal distribution, the 99% confidence interval is the mean +/- 2.58 standard deviations.

A confidence interval is a range of values, bounded above and below the statistic's mean, that likely would contain an unknown population parameter. Confidence level refers to the percentage of probability, or certainty, that the confidence interval would contain the true population parameter when you draw a random sample many times. The most commonly utilized confidence intervals are 95 and 99%, which are calculated based on the mean +/- 1.96 and 2.58, respectively.

*Greenland et al review statistical tests, P values, confidence intervals, and power analyses.* **They report** that there has been a shift in emphasis from hypothesis testing to estimation. **They conclude** that this is a simple and relatively safe way to improve statistical practices.

*Kocher et al review clinical epidemiology and biostatistics.* **They report** that the 95% confidence interval provides a range of values that allows the reader to interpret the implications of the results compared to p-values, which are often deemed "significant" or "non-significant". **They conclude** that the confidence interval allows for appropriation of clinical and statistical significance as well as precision.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1-3 & 5: 1.44, 1.65, 1.96 and 2.32 standard deviations represent 85%, 90%, 95% and 98% confidence intervals, respectively
