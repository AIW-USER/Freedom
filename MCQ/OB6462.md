## Question

Figure 33 shows the radiograph of a 4-year-old child who has a knock-knee deformity on the left side after sustaining a proximal tibia fracture on the same side 9 months ago. Treatment should consist of

![[q6462-a-33.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. observation.
2. bracing with a knee-ankle-foot orthosis.
3. stapling of the left medial proximal tibial physis.
4. physeal bar resection on the left proximal tibia.
5. osteotomy of the left tibia.

## Correct option

**1. observation.**

## Answer explanation

Fractures of the proximal tibia in children (also known as a "Cozen fracture") between 2 and 10 years of age often overgrow into valgus. Treatment of posttraumatic tibia valga in children consists of observation. The knock-knee will begin to spontaneously improve within 12 to 24 months after injury. Keys to preventing valgus angulation after a fracture are: 1) an anatomic reduction, (casting the fracture in extension to prevent valgus angulation of the fracture); and 2) checking to ensure that soft tissue is not trapped within the fracture site by close scrutiny of the postreduction radiographs The parents should be warned about this possibility at the beginning of treatment.
