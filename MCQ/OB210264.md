## Question

A football player experiences less than 15 minutes of confusion and headache after a direct blow to the helmet. He is told he will follow the 6-step graduated return to play protocol shown in Figure A. Which of the following is the correct minimum duration that should elapse prior to advancing to the next step of the return to play protocol?

![[q210264-a-return-to-play.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. 12 hours
2. 24 hours
3. 48 hours
4. 72 hours
5. 96 hours

## Correct option

**2. 24 hours**

## Answer explanation

This patient sustained a concussion and should spend a minimum of 24 hours in each step of the graduated return to play (RTP) protocol beginning the day after the injury.

Immediate RTP following a concussion has been found to put patients at risk of acute second impact syndrome and chronic sequelae such as headaches and depression. Proper decision making regarding RTP is therefore critical to prevent additional irreversible brain damage in these young and otherwise healthy patients. RTP should be guided by a physician experienced in concussion management and should follow the graduated RTP protocol. Further evaluation is necessary in patients who experience loss of consciousness (LOC), a prior Grade 1 concussion in the same season, symptoms lasting > 15 minutes, a positive exertional stress test, amnesia, or postconcussion syndrome. It is recommended that each stage of the graduated RTP protocol should be maintained for at least 24 hours, meaning 1 week is the earliest an athlete should RTP, even if symptoms resolve rapidly.

*Echemendia et al. reviewed and presented the pros and cons of the guidelines presented by the Concussion In Sport Group (CISG), the American Academy of Neurology (AAN), the National Athletic Trainers' Association, and the 2013 Team Physician Consensus Statement Update.* **They found** that consensus statements and formal evidence-based medicine literature are generally very similar and in agreement with each other. **They conclude** clinicians should cautiously follow consensus statements and continue to use detailed study designs in the study of concussions.

*Putukian reviewed concussion diagnosis, on-field management, and RTP guidelines.* **She states** that most patients (80-90%) return to play in 7-10 days. **She also reports** that trends in RTP have started to err on the side of caution as sustaining a secondary injury can lead to a devastating outcome. Particular attention should be paid to the concussion history of the patient.

*McCrory et al., in the 3rd Consensus Statement on Concussion in Sport, noted that all athletes less than 18 years of age and all non-elite athletes, as well as “elite” athletes greater than 18 years of age who do not have team personnel with experience in concussion management and sufficient resources (neuropsychologists, neuroimaging) should RTP using a graduated RTP protocol.* This graduated RTP protocol is found in the "Consensus Statement” and consists of six stages. The athlete should spend at least 24 hours in each stage, meaning that the earliest RTP in most instances should be one week after sustaining a concussion.

Figure A shows the 6 step graduated RTP protocol as described by McCrory et al.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: 12 hours is not a sufficient amount of time prior to advancing to the next step in the protocol.
Answers 3-5: 48 hours or more is not the minimum recommended time in each step prior to advancing to the next step of the protocol.
