Chapter: Sports Medicine MCQs — Principles Of Training And Recovery

## Question

A coach visits your clinic with his elite sprinter who has had recurrent hamstring injuries and says that he has been rested well and is currently rehabilitating and painfree, returning to training. Which of the following is not an advise you would give his coach

## Options

1. Assess and Work on his hamstring strength
2. Assess position of his hips during running
3. Assess his lumbo-pelvic control
4. Avoid training at higher speeds, else his injury will recur

## Correct option

**4. Avoid training at higher speeds, else his injury will recur**

## Answer explanation

Ans: D

EXPLANATION: -

The question needs evaluation of each choice keeping a runner/ sprinter in mind.

Choice A: During running, during Terminal swing following contralateral toe off, the body is thrust into the second 'float' phase. During this time the ipsilateral swinging hip reaches maximal flexion (approximately 30°), before being brought under control by the hamstring and gluteal musculature. The same hamstring activity slows the rapidly extending knee in preparation for heel strike. At the same time, the hip adductors, which have been working eccentrically to control abduction of the swinging limb, begin to work concentrically to adduct the hip and bring it toward the midline. Eccentric hamstring strengthening here is very important along with the glutes and hip abductors to prevent hamstring from getting injured during these eccentric contractions, a common injury during this phase of running.

Choice B: Excessive anterior tilting of the pelvis may place excessive strain on the lumbar spine and/or hamstring musculature, and may impair gluteal function. Hence assessing and guiding on appropriate position of hips in runner is important.

Choice C: Lumbo-pelvic control is important to maintain the pelvis from tilting anteriorly and therefore can prevent further hamstring injuries.

Choice D: A sprinter has to run fast and hence has to train at higher intensities post recovery from injuries. An adequate rehabilitation and biomechanical corrections will reduce injuries in the future.
