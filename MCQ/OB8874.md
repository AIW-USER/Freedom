## Question

A 49-year-old male has persistent deep shoulder pain that has not improved with conservative measures. He undergoes the procedure depicted in Figure A for persistent, chronic shoulder pain without complications. Postoperatively, he has weakness of shoulder abduction, forward elevation and external rotation as well as decreased muscle bulk and tone in the area above and below his scapular spine. What nerve was most likely injured?

![[q8874-a-figure-a.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Axillary nerve
2. Suprascapular nerve at the suprascapular notch
3. Suprascapular nerve at the spinoglenoid notch
4. Spinal accessory nerve
5. Dorsal scapular nerve

## Correct option

**2. Suprascapular nerve at the suprascapular notch**

## Answer explanation

This patient underwent arthroscopic suprascapular nerve release at the suprascapular notch for suprascapular neuropathy with iatrogenic injury to the released nerve.

The suprascapular nerve passes under the transverse scapular ligament at the suprascapular notch before turning back medially around the spinoglenoid notch. The nerve innervates the supraspinatus and infraspinatus, with fibers to infraspinatus branching after passing the spinoglenoid notch to enter the infraspinous fossa. Injury or compression at the suprascapular notch will affect both muscles.

*Scully et al. review iatrogenic nerve injuries about the shoulder.* **They note** that suprascapular nerve injury or neuropathy can occur at the suprascapular notch after massive rotator cuff repair, as advancement of the tendon may place pressure on the nerve at its relatively fixed position at the notch.

*Boykin et al. review suprascapular neuropathy.* **They note** that initial treatment in the absence of a mass lesion or a rotator cuff tear is typically non-operative. Good results have been demonstrated with traditional open treatment at the suprascapular notch but arthroscopic techniques have shown promising early results.

Figure A shows a diagram of the arthroscopic technique of suprascapular nerve decompression at the suprascapular notch after Lafosse. The arthroscope is in the lateral portal and the instrument is in the suprascapular nerve decompression portal (medial to Neviaser's). Illustration A shows a table of characteristic findings after peripheral nerve injuries about the shoulder from Scully et al.

Incorrect answers:
Answers 1, 4, and 5. The suprascapular nerve is depicted in Figure A.
Answer 3. Injury at the spinoglenoid notch would result in only infraspinatus palsy.
