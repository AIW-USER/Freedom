## Question

A 16-year-old male presents to the ED for evaluation of a finger injury. The patients caregiver reports that he bit the tip of his finger off early this morning when unsupervised. The patient has a history of cognitive impairment with documented aggressive behaviors. He was seen for similar behavior approximately 6 months ago. The patient is known to have a condition that affects purine metabolism, which of the following is the most likely diagnosis?

## Options

1. Gaucher
2. Lesch-Nyhan
3. Charcot-Marie-Tooth
4. Apert
5. Leri-Weill

## Correct option

**2. Lesch-Nyhan**

## Answer explanation

Lesch-Nyhan syndrome is a rare X-linked recessive disorder that is characterized by self mutilation and aggressive behavior.

X-linked diseases usually occur in males, as they only have one X chromosome. A single recessive gene on that X chromosome typically causes the disease. Lesch-Nyhan syndrome is one such disease characterized by cognitive impairment, self mutilating and aggressive behaviors. The absence of the Hypoxanthine-guanine phosphoribosyltransferase (HPRT) enzyme, which is involved in purine metabolism, is the main etiology of this particular syndrome.

*Guidera et al review the orthopedic manifestations in congenitally insensate patients.* **They report** that the common orthopaedic problems and complications included fracture, self-mutilation, autoamputation, osteomyelitis, septic arthritis, Charcot joints, scoliosis, and dislocation. **They conclude** that it's the abnormal interpretation of painful stimuli or lack of normal pain avoidance that leads to the characteristic self-inflicted damage.

*Sponseller et al review the orthopedic problems in Lesch-Nyhan syndrome.* **They report** that Lesch-Nyhan syndrome is an X-linked disorder of purine metabolism, which results in orthopedic problems including hip subluxation or dislocation, fractures, autoamputation, infections, minor scoliosis, and contractures. **They conclude** that these patients can successfully undergo most orthopedic procedures, however, they are at an increased risk of developing heterotopic bone.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Gaucher disease is a congenital lysosomal storage disorder caused by an autosomal recessive mutation.
Answer 3: Charcot-Marie-Tooth is rarely inherited in an x-linked recessive pattern and typically presents with muscle weakness and sensory changes.
Answer 4: Apert syndrome is an autosomal dominant condition characterized by bilateral complex syndactyly of hands/feet and a dysmorphic face.
Answer 5: Leri-Weill dyschondrosteosis presents as bilateral Madelung's deformity and is inherited in a sex linked dominant fashion.
