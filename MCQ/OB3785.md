## Question

A 65-year-old male is undergoing lumbar total disc arthroplasty. If the components shown in Figure A are used as opposed to those shown in Figure B, which of the following is true of the expected lab abnormalities?

![[q3785-a-metal-on-metal-disc-arthroplasty.jpg]]
*Figure A*

![[q3785-b-poly-lumbar-disc-arthroplasty.jpg]]
*Figure B*

## Options

1. Acutely elevated serum cobalt and chromium that becomes negligible over time
2. Elevated serum cobalt and chromium, similar to levels found after metal-on-metal total hip arthroplasty
3. Elevated serum cobalt similar to levels found after metal-on-metal total hip arthroplasty, but normal serum chromium
4. Elevated serum cobalt and chromium levels are neutralized in the liver
5. Serum cobalt and chromium may be elevated but is of negligible clinical value

## Correct option

**2. Elevated serum cobalt and chromium, similar to levels found after metal-on-metal total hip arthroplasty**

## Answer explanation

Metal-on-metal disc arthroplasty components release metal ions systemically at a similar concentration to that observed in metal-on-metal total hip arthroplasty (THA).

Metal-on-metal component design was an attractive design to minimize the amount of wear debris and prevent polyethylene particle-induced osteolysis, which can lead to loosening of prosthetic components. A disadvantage of metal-on-metal components is the local and systemic release of metal (cobalt, chromium) ions. Metal-on-metal THA implants have been associated locally with aseptic lymphocyte-dominated vasculitis-associated lesion (ALVAL), pseudotumor, and metallosis. The systemic safety threshold for cobalt and chromium ions is not well-defined and there is historic concern that elevated cobalt levels are associated with cardiomyopathy, hypothyroidism, polycythemia, cognitive dysfunction, and neuropathy.

*Zeh et al. performed a cross-sectional study in which they evaluated the serum cobalt (Co) and chromium (Cr) ion concentrations following metal-on-metal lumbar disc arthroplasty.* **They determined** Co and Cr levels were elevated at 4.75 µg/L and 1.10 µg/L, respectively. **They concluded** that the postoperative Co/Cr serum levels after metal-on-metal lumbar disc arthroplasty are similar to the levels after metal-on-metal THA.

*Zeh et al. also evaluated whether Co/Cr levels remained elevated at independent time points postoperatively after metal-on-metal lumbar disc arthroplasty.* **They found** Co/Cr levels were elevated at both 14.8 and 36.7 months postoperatively. **They concluded** that a persistent release of Co/Cr ions must be considered when evaluating which implant design a surgeon wishes to utilize.

*Witzleb et al. evaluated serum Co/Cr levels after hip resurfacing and metal-on-metal THA.* **They determined** that both hip resurfacing and metal-on-metal THA elevate serum Co/Cr levels. **They concluded** that both hip resurfacing and metal-on-metal THA elevate serum Co/Cr.

Figure A shows metal-on-metal lumbar disc arthroplasty components. Figure B shows a lumbar disc arthroplasty with a polyethylene bearing.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Co/Cr levels remain elevated indefinitely after metal-on-metal component implantation.
Answer 3: Both serum Co and serum Cr are elevated after metal-on-metal component implantation.
Answer 4: Serum Co/Cr levels become elevated after metal-on-metal component implantation, but they are excreted by the kidneys.
Answer 5: Serum Co/Cr levels are typically high after metal-on-metal component implantation and can have serious clinical implications.
