## Question

A 24-year-old female presents to your clinic with six months of gradually worsening right forefoot pain. On physical exam, pain is localized to the plantar forefoot underlying the second metatarsophalangeal joint. You suspect a process that involves bone infarction of the second metatarsal head. Which radiographic finding would best support definitive treatment with polyvinyl arthroplasty?

## Options

1. Normal radiographs with subchondral fracture only visible on MRI
2. Isolated dorsal articular surface collapse
3. Dorsal metatarsal head collapse with an intact plantar surface and medial and lateral bony spurring
4. Diffuse metatarsal head collapse with arthritic joint changes
5. Polyvinyl arthroplasty is not approved for use in lesser toes

## Correct option

**5. Polyvinyl arthroplasty is not approved for use in lesser toes**

## Answer explanation

Diffuse metatarsal head collapse with arthritic joint changes represents advanced-stage Freiberg’s disease with complete articular destruction, making joint-sacrificing procedures the only reasonable surgical option; however, polyvinyl arthroplasty is not approved for use in the lesser toes and therefore is not typically considered an appropriate definitive treatment (Answer choice 5).

Freiberg’s disease is an osteochondrosis of the metatarsal head characterized by subchondral infarction that progresses through predictable radiographic stages. Early stages demonstrate subchondral fracture and focal dorsal collapse, while intermediate stages show progressive dorsal involvement with relative plantar preservation. Advanced disease is marked by diffuse metatarsal head collapse, joint space narrowing, and secondary arthritic changes consistent with end-stage degeneration. Treatment is guided by stage: early-stage disease is treated conservatively, progressing to joint-preserving osteotomies in intermediate stages, and interposition arthroplasty is reserved for advanced arthrosis. Implant arthroplasty with polyvinyl devices is not approved for lesser metatarsophalangeal joints.

*Carmont et al. reviewed Freiberg’s disease in a current concepts article outlining its pathophysiology, radiographic progression, and treatment strategies.* **They reported** that early disease may be managed nonoperatively or with joint-preserving procedures, whereas advanced stages with collapse and degenerative change require more aggressive surgical intervention. **They concluded** that the extent of articular involvement and structural collapse should guide operative decision-making.

Çevik et al. evaluated patients with advanced Freiberg’s disease treated with extensor digitorum brevis tendon interposition arthroplasty in a retrospective case series. They found significant improvements in pain, range of motion, and functional scores following surgical treatment of late-stage disease. They concluded that interpositional arthroplasty is a reliable option for advanced metatarsal head collapse with arthritic degeneration.

Incorrect answers:
Answer choice 1: A subchondral fracture visible only on MRI represents early-stage disease that is typically managed nonoperatively or with joint-preserving strategies rather than implant arthroplasty.
Answer choice 2: Isolated dorsal articular surface collapse corresponds to an intermediate stage where joint-preserving osteotomy may still be appropriate.
Answer choice 3: Dorsal metatarsal head collapse with an intact plantar surface and medial and lateral bony spurring reflects partial articular involvement and does not represent end-stage degeneration requiring joint-sacrificing intervention.
Answer choice 4: Although diffuse collapse with arthritic change represents advanced disease, polyvinyl arthroplasty is not approved in the lesser toes.
