## Question

A 57-year-old businesswoman presents to your office with complaints of right foot pain which is located about her transverse arch. She states it feels like she is "stepping on a pebble," and she is able to further localize the tenderness to the plantar aspect of her 2nd metatarsophalangeal joint. She has attempted wearing wider toe box shoes, as her high heels tend to make the pain worse, but to this point nonoperative measures have not improved her pain. An MRI is ordered which demonstrates a degenerative plantar plate tear. Which of the following conditions is most likely to develop?

## Options

1. Mallet toe
2. Hammer toe
3. Tarsometatarsal arthritis
4. Freiberg's disease
5. Hallux valgus

## Correct option

**2. Hammer toe**

## Answer explanation

A hammer toe may develop after plantar plate rupture, as the metatarsal head translates plantarly, leading to overpull of the extrinsic extensors at the metatarsophalangeal (MTP) joint.

Hammer toe deformity usually occurs in middle aged women and the 2nd toe is most likely to be involved. One of the causes of a hammer toe is a plantar plate rupture, which changes the mechanics of the MTP and PIP joints. With a plantar plate rupture, the metatarsal head is no longer suspended and can move plantarly, causing the MTP joint to move into relative extension. This muscular imbalance leads to overpull of the extensor digitorum longus and causes a flexion deformity at the PIP joint in order to keep the toe in a plantigrade position. This often leads to painful calluses on the dorsal aspect of the PIP joint and pain over the plantar aspect of the 2nd MTP, where patients describe a sensation of standing on a pebble.

*Coughlin et al. reviewed MTP joint pathology with crossover toe deformity, which can occur secondarily after plantar plate rupture along with a hammer toe deformity.* **They noted** that in their cadaver study, patients with crossover toe deformities were more likely to have have transverse tears of the plantar plate along with midsubstance ruptures of the MTP collateral ligaments. Ultimately, the additional presence of the collateral ligament injury contributes significantly to the crossover deformity.

*Reddy et al. discuss advancements to treatment in lesser MTP instability.* **They note** that there are newer techniques to treat these issues including minimally invasive surgery, synthetic augmentation, and arthroscopy. **They conclude** that clinical outcomes appear to be equivalent with these newer techniques despite their minimally invasive nature.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: A mallet toe occurs when there is fixed flexion deformity of the DIP joint secondary to FDL contracture or EDL rupture of the lesser toes.
Answer 3: Tarsometatarsal (TMT) would not occur secondary to plantar plate rupture at the metatarsophalangeal (MTP) joint, although a middle age woman who wears high heels consistently may still be at higher risk.
Answer 4: Freiberg's disease describes infarction and eventual collapse of the metatarsal head, most commonly the 2nd, and would not been seen secondary to a plantar plate rupture.
Answer 5: Hallux valgus can develop secondary to a plantar plate rupture which contributes to toe deformity but this is not the most likely deformity to be seen as a direct result of the plantar plate injury.
