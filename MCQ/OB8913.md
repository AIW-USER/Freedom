## Options

1. Repeat evaluation after denosumab treatment
2. Repeat curettage, placement of allograft, and internal fixation
3. Local debridement, bone culture, placement of antibiotic beads, and IV antibiotics
4. Wide resection and reconstruction
5. Repeat curettage, placement of cement, and internal fixation

## Correct option

**4. Wide resection and reconstruction**

## Answer explanation

Figure F represents a fibrosarcoma, which is best treated with wide resection, reconstruction, and possibly adjuvant treatment with chemotherapy.

Giant cell tumor recurrence after treatment with curettage and bone grafting is known to occur at a rate of approximately 15-25%. Recurrence should be suspected with increased lucency at the bone-cement interface or new lucencies on radiographs (in the case of bone grafting). It is important to perform a biopsy and not assume recurrence is the cause of radiographic changes, as malignant, sarcomatous transformation can occur at a rate of 1.4%-6.6%. Malignant transformation is more common if the initial tumor was initially treated with radiation, but there are case reports of late-onset malignant transformation without radiation therapy. After malignant transformation has taken place, the prognosis is poor, and the patient should be treated with wide resection and possibly chemotherapy.

*Li et al. present a case report of a 35-year-old male with malignant transformation of a giant cell tumor to a high-grade spindle cell sarcoma 15 years after curettage and bone grafting.* Despite initial adequate en bloc resection and endoprosthesis reconstruction, the patient died of metastatic disease. **The authors highlight** the need to be suspicious of malignant transformation in any giant cell tumor recurrence, despite low rates of malignant transformation (1.4-6.6%).

*Saito et al. present a case of a 46-year-old male who was treated with curettage and grafting of a distal femoral giant cell lesion without radiation and later developed secondary malignant transformation to a fatal metastatic high-grade spindle cell lesion.* The patient was found to have a p53 gene mutation in the sarcomatous area, which is theorized to potentially help with the identification of malignant giant cell tumors.

Figures A and B are the AP and lateral radiographs of the left knee demonstrating a large, radiolucent lesion in the distal femur. Figure C is a histology slide demonstrating numerous giant cells amidst a sea of mononuclear stromal cells, consistent with giant cell tumor. Figures D and E are AP and lateral radiographs of the distal femur demonstrating an expansile lytic lesion with some cortical erosion. Figure F is a histology slide showing spindle cells with elongated nuclei consistent with a high-grade fibrosarcoma.

Answer 3: This case represents a high-grade sarcoma, not osteomyelitis
