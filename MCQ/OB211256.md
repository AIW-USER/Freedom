## Question

The use of topical vancomycin powder in open spine surgery would lead to which of the following?

## Options

1. An increase in overall surgical site infections (SSI)
2. If infection does occur, a higher rate of vancomycin-resistant SSI
3. An increased rate of non-union of the fusion site
4. An increase in infection-related medical costs
5. If infection does occur, a higher rate of gram-negative SSI

## Correct option

**5. If infection does occur, a higher rate of gram-negative SSI**

## Answer explanation

Topical vancomycin powder in spine surgery has been shown to decrease the rate of surgical site infections (SSI). However, when an infection does occur, there is a higher rate of gram-negative SSI.

SSIs following spine surgery has been estimated to occur at a rate of approximately 2.1%. The rate of SSI varies based on patient and surgical risk factors, including operative time, blood loss, and prior SSI. The use of topical vancomycin powder has been shown to decrease the rate of SSI in spine surgery and is therefore of significant interest. The rates of gram-negative SSI and culture-negative fluid collections increases with the use of topical vancomycin.

*Khan et al. performed a meta-analysis of 9 retrospective cohort studies and 1 randomized controlled study looking at the use of topical vancomycin powder in spine surgery.* **They found** that vancomycin powder led to a pooled absolute risk reduction and relative risk reduction of 2.8% and 68%, with a NNT of 36 to prevent 1 SSI. **They concluded** that vancomycin powder in open spine surgery may be protective against SSI.

*Boody et al. reviewed SSI in spine surgery.* **They report** the overall rate of SSI in spine surgery to be 2.1%. **They state** that patient risk factors for SSI include diabetes, obesity, smoking, poor nutrition, and nasal colonization with methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus (MRSA). The authors' preferred treatment for SSI is incision and drainage in the operating room with targeted antibiotic therapy.

*Chotai et al. reviewed 2802 patients who had intrawound vancomycin powder applied during open spine surgery.* **They found** vancomycin to have a lower rate of deep SSI (1.6% compared to 2.5%). Of the patients who developed a subsequent SSI despite the use of vancomycin, none were found to have infections with organisms resistant to vancomycin. **They concluded** that the use of vancomycin during index spine surgery was protective against SSI and did not appear to create vancomycin-resistant organisms after SSI.

*Godil et al. reviewed 110 patients with traumatic spine injuries that were treated with instrumented spinal fusion either with (treatment group) or without (control group) topical vancomycin.* **They found** a significantly lower SSI rate in the treatment group (0% vs. 13% in the control group). They found each infection cost an average of $33,705 or a total cost savings of $438,165 per 100 posterior spinal fusions for traumatic spine injuries treated with topical vancomycin. **They concluded** that topical vancomycin leads to a reduction of SSI and can lead to cost savings in traumatic spine injuries treated with posterior spinal fusion.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Multiple studies have shown a decreased rate of SSI with topical vancomycin powder in open spine surgery.
Answer 2: Topical vancomycin powder has not been shown to increase the rate of vancomycin-resistant SSIs.
Answer 3: No study has shown topical vancomycin powder to lead to an increased rate of non-union in spine surgery.
Answer 4: Infection-related medical costs have been studied and have shown to decrease with topical vancomycin powder in spine surgery.
