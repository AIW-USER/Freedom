## Question

A 35-year-old mechanic falls from a ladder and sustains a closed spiral humeral shaft fracture that meets operative criteria despite closed reduction attempts with immobilization. After discussing the treatment options, he elects to undergo surgery for his injury. The surgeon plans on using a posterior approach with the Gerwin modification. In this approach, which nerve is first identified to assist the surgeon in identifying the radial nerve?

## Options

1. Inferior lateral brachial cutaneous nerve
2. Motor branch of the triceps brachii
3. Posterior brachial cutaneous nerve
4. Posterior interosseous nerve
5. Superficial branch of the radial nerve

## Correct option

**1. Inferior lateral brachial cutaneous nerve**

## Answer explanation

During the posterior approach to the humerus with the Gerwin modification, the inferior lateral brachial cutaneous branch of the radial nerve is first identified and leads the surgeon to the radial nerve.

*The Gerwin modification (Gerwin et al., 1996 in JBJS) to the posterior approach to the humerus begins with retracting the triceps muscle medially to expose the inferior lateral cutaneous branch of the radial nerve.* The nerve is then followed proximally to where the radial nerve passes through the intermuscular septum. The septum can then be divided to allow for mobilization of the nerve.

Radial nerve palsies result in weakness or inability to perform wrist, thumb, and finger extension. In addition, it results in numbness over the dorsal-radial hand. Nerve recovery generally occurs at a rate of 1 mm per day. Nerve recovery can be tested clinically by the return of function of muscles in the order of their innervation. The earliest muscle to recover is the brachioradialis and the last to recover is the extensor indices proprius. Most radial nerve palsies resolve spontaneously within six months. In cases of non-recovery, electrodiagnostic exams can be performed. Additional procedures such as tendon transfers or nerve repair/grafting may be considered to restore function.

*Chaudhry and colleagues* **highlighted** several key findings regarding radial nerve injuries. For closed injuries, conservative treatment often yields favorable outcomes. **The authors note** that early surgical exploration may be beneficial in specific scenarios, such as open injuries or when there is no recovery after a period of observation. **They conclude** that despite advancements in imaging and diagnostic techniques, further research is needed to determine the optimal care for these injuries.

*Carroll and colleagues provided an in-depth review of the management of humeral shaft fractures, focusing on both nonoperative and operative options.* Nonoperative treatment, typically using functional bracing, is effective for most cases and results in high union rates and satisfactory outcomes. However, surgery is recommended in cases involving open fractures, vascular injuries, or failure of nonoperative treatment. **The authors emphasize** the importance of individualized treatment plans based on fracture characteristics, patient factors, and surgeon expertise to optimize outcomes.

Incorrect answers:
Answers 2-5: These are branches of the radial nerve, but are not the initial landmark used to trace back to the radial nerve in this approach.
