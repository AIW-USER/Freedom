## Question

A mother notices that her 6-year-old obese, uncoordinated child has some difficulty moving his right forearm as well as his left. Upon further discussion, she relays that the boy's development was somewhat delayed in terms of both motor and verbal milestones. Examination reveals that the right forearm is fixed in 80 degrees of pronation, although he has full ulnohumeral motion. Which of the following chromosome abnormalities has been associated with this condition?

![[q2261-a-cap-avn.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. 45 XO
2. 47 XXY
3. Trisomy 21
4. Trisomy 18
5. Partial chromosome 15 deletion

## Correct option

**2. 47 XXY**

## Answer explanation

The boy presents with congenital radio-ulnar synostosis (CRUS). Although this may be inherited as an autosomal dominant fashion in chromosomally normal individuals, CRUS has also been linked to duplication of sex chromosomes such as in Klinefelter's syndrome (47 XXY).

CRUS is thought to result during the 7th week in utero from a failure of differentiation. The condition is more common in males (3:2), 60% are bilateral, and there is some genetic association. However, one-third of cases are associated with syndromes including Apert syndrome, Arthrogryposis, and Klinefelter's syndrome. The diagnosis is most often made at 6 years of age when the child demonstrates fixed pronation and compensates with shoulder abduction. Most often, especially in unilateral asymptomatic cases, non-operative management is indicated. Slight pronation is often tolerated well as this is the position of typing on a keyboard. Synostosis excision and soft tissue interposition yields only mild improvements in motion. In bilateral cases, a forearm derotational osteotomy may be indicated.

*Cleary and Omer reviewed 23 patients (36 elbows) with CRUS and an average of 30 degrees of fixed pronation.* *The authors made no attempt at surgical correction given that the position of the forearm was not related to any functional limitations or employment status.* **They found** that most patients had no limitations despite that they were employed in jobs that required the use of the forearm. **The authors concluded** that treatment for CRUS is rarely indicated.

*Simcock et al. reviewed 26 patients (31 elbows) with CRUS who underwent derotational osteotomy.* *The mean age was 6.8 years and pronation averaged 85 degrees prior to correction.* *The authors achieved a mean correction of 77 degrees (to 8 degrees from neutral) and found that all osteotomies healed by 8 weeks with a 12% rate of complications (2 transient AIN palsies, 1 transient radial nerve palsy, 1 symptomatic muscle herniation).* **They concluded** that derotational osteotomy was a safe and efficacious procedure for CRUS.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: 45 XO, or Turner syndrome, is associated with malignant hyperthermia, sexual infantilism, idiopathic scoliosis, short 4th metacarpal, and a webbed neck.
Answer 3: Trisomy 21, or Down's syndrome, is associated with advanced maternal age, short stature, C1-2 instability, hip instability, patellofemoral instability, and scoliosis.
Answer 4: Trisomy 18, or Edward's syndrome, is associated with cardiac defects and generally, only 5% survive past one year.
Answer 5: A Partial chromosome 15 deletion is associated with Prader-Willi Syndrome. This leads to obese hypotonia with an insatiable appetite, growth retardation, hip dysplasia, and juvenile-onset scoliosis.
