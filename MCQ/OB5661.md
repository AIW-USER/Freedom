## Question

## Options

1. Triple arthrodesis and achilles tendon lengthening
2. Tibiotalocalcaneal arthrodesis and gastrocnemius recession
3. First tarsal-metatarsal joint arthrodesis, subtalar arthrodesis, posterior tibial tendon debridement +/- lateral column lengthening
4. Medial cuneiform dorsal opening wedge osteotomy, medializing calcaneal osteotomy, and flexor digitorum longus tendon transfer
5. First tarsal-metatarsal arthrodesis, medializing calcaneal osteotomy, flexor digitorum longus tendon transfer and lateral column lengthening

## Correct option

**5. First tarsal-metatarsal arthrodesis, medializing calcaneal osteotomy, flexor digitorum longus tendon transfer and lateral column lengthening**

## Answer explanation

This patient presents with a progressive flatfoot deformity with posterior tibialis tendon dysfunction (PTTI), first TMT instability, forefoot abduction, and flexible hindfoot. The next best step would be first tarsal-metatarsal arthrodesis, medializing calcaneal osteotomy, and flexor digitorum longs (FDL) tendon transfer.

Treatment for PTTI is stage dependent. Surgery is indicated for stages II-IV. Stage II disease is substratified based on the degree of talonavicular uncoverage. Lateral column lengthening is added if there is >40% or >30 degrees uncovering of the talar head as FDL transfer and medial slide osteotomy will not correct severe forefoot abduction. LCL is performed either through the calcaneocuboid joint or the anterior calcaneus. First tarsal-metatarsal arthrodesis versus medial cuneiform dorsal opening wedge osteotomy depends on first TMT pathology. Significant arthritis or instability is the primary indication for arthrodesis.

*McCormick et al. reviewed medial column procedures in the correction of adult acquired flatfoot deformity.* **They state** that the first NC or the first TMT joint should be examined for evidence of instability, hypermobility, or arthritic changes. If present, the surgeon should consider arthrodesis to correct residual forefoot varus, as compared to the standard cotton osteotomy.

*Greisberg et al. examined 37 symptomatic flat feet in their study to locate the area of deformity along the medial column of the arch.* **They showed** that medial column arch collapse occurred through the talonavicular joint or the medial naviculocuneiform joint, but rarely through both. In addition, the location of deformity did not correlate with joint degeneration.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Triple arthrodesis and Achilles lengthening would be indicated for a rigid foot (stage III) or for arthritis. A rigid flatfoot, irrespective of arthritis of XR is a stage 3 flatfoot.
Answer 2: Tibiotalocalcaneal arthrodesis and gastrocnemius recession would be indicated in fixed deformities with stage IV disease with a rigid hindfoot, valgus angulation of the talus, and tibiotalar and subtalar arthritis, as well as a Silfverskiold test showing gastrocnemius tightness. Stage IV disease is characterized by ankle involvement. This involves incompetence of the deltoid ligament and/or ankle arthritis.
Answer 3: First tarsal-metatarsal joint arthrodesis, subtalar arthrodesis, lateral column lengthening, and posterior tibial tendon debridement would be considered in stage IIB disease with severe subtalar arthritis. In general, it is uncommon to perform a subtalar arthrodesis and lateral column lengthening together. This is often accomplished with a calcaneal-cuboid arthrodesis. Moreover, PTT debridements have not been shown to be significantly beneficial.
Answer 4: Medial cuneiform dorsal opening wedge osteotomy, medializing calcaneal osteotomy, and flexor digitorum longus tendon transfer would be considered if there was no evidence of instability, hypermobility, or arthritic changes at the first TMT joint.
