## Question

A men’s college basketball team is undergoing pre-season physicals during training camp. Several team members have been enrolled in a randomized controlled trial to determine the utility of additional workup in the identification of hypertrophic cardiomyopathy. Which of the following is true regarding athletic heart syndrome and hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM)?

## Options

1. Athletic heart syndrome has the same risk of sudden cardiac arrest as HCM
2. In Athletic heart syndrome, left ventricular hypertrophy may mimic hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM)
3. Athletic heart syndrome typically affects the right ventricular, while HCM consistently affects the left.
4. In both athletic heart syndrome and HCM, the left ventricle cavity size is diminished
5. Athletic heart syndrome can be distinguished by the presence of T wave inversions primarily in the lateral leads of the electrocardiogram (ECG)

## Correct option

**2. In Athletic heart syndrome, left ventricular hypertrophy may mimic hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM)**

## Answer explanation

Athletic heart syndrome is characterized by left ventricular hypertrophy (LVH), typically in response to physiologic exercise, and may mimic hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (Answer 2)

Athletic heart syndrome refers to the range of physiological cardiac adaptations that occur in response to prolonged and intense exercise training. These adaptations can include electrical, structural, and functional changes, such as LVH. A key challenge in sports cardiology is distinguishing these benign physiological changes from pathological conditions, most notably hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM), as physiological LVH can sometimes mimic mild phenotypes of HCM. While athletes with physiological LVH are typically asymptomatic with a normal physical exam, features like exertional symptoms, a family history of cardiomyopathy or sudden cardiac death, or a systolic murmur raise suspicion for pathology. Therefore, a comprehensive evaluation utilizing these tools is essential to differentiate the physiological adaptations of athletic heart syndrome from the pathological features of HCM.

*In their review, Augustine et al. address the challenge of distinguishing between physiological left ventricular hypertrophy (LVH) in athletes and pathological hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM).* **They highlight** that cardiac adaptations in athletes can mimic HCM, making accurate diagnosis crucial to prevent sudden cardiac death or unwarranted disqualification from sports. Ultimately, the review provides a systematic approach for clinicians to assess LVH in athletes, considering factors like age, gender, ethnicity, and sports discipline to guide appropriate investigations and diagnosis

*Huston et al. delve into the physiological adaptations that occur in the cardiovascular systems of athletes due to intense and regular physical training.* *They explore how the heart and blood vessels remodel themselves to efficiently meet the increased demands of exercise, a phenomenon often referred to as the "athlete's heart." Ultimately, the authors aim to provide a better understanding of the cardiac changes in athletes for accurate clinical evaluation and management.*

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: Athletic heart syndrome is a physiologic response to prolonged exercise and does not carry any additional risk of sudden cardiac death.
Answer 3: Both conditions affect the left ventricle, and physiological LVH may overlap with mild phenotypes of HCM.
Answer 4: This is the distinguishing feature of HCM vs athletic heart syndrome. In the latter, cavity volume is maintained.
Answer 5: T wave inversions in the lateral leads are more concerning for pathology, including HCM.
