## Question

A 14-year-old male with prior history of distal femoral physeal fracture presents for evaluation of his leg length discrepancy. You are trying to determine the remaining growth expected from the contralateral limb to determine the best treatment modality. In the adolescent age group, what has been shown to be most accurate in predicting remaining growth?

## Options

1. Father’s final height
2. Chronological age
3. Skeletal age
4. Tanner stage
5. Paley method

## Correct option

**3. Skeletal age**

## Answer explanation

Determination of final leg length discrepancy (LLD) is an important consideration when determining which treatment to utilize. Skeletal maturity has been shown to be the best predictor of expected limb length during adolescence.

LLD can occur from a variety of different pathologies including trauma, congenital defects, and paralytic disorders. A variety of methods have been proposed to predict final LLD based on either chronological or skeletal age. In general, non-operative management is recommended for LLD <2 cm, shortening of longer limb is recommended for 2-5 cm discrepancies, and lengthening of the shorter limb is recommended when LLD >5 cm.

*Sanders et al performed a retrospective review to determine whether chronological age or skeletal maturity provided more accurate limb-length predictions.* When considering all ages, chronological age outperformed skeletal maturity. However, during adolescent years, skeletal maturity measures provided better predictions.

*Makarov et al studied the accuracy of different methods of prediction of ultimate leg lengths.* *Based on the 77 patients* **they identified**, the prediction accuracy was improved by using skeletal age over chronological age. **They conclude** that this should be considered for timing of epiphysiodesis to manage leg-length inequality.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Using the height of the parent of the same sex has not been shown to be an accurate predictor of final leg length.
Answer 2: Chronological age has been shown to be inferior to skeletal age in the adolescent time period.
Answer 4: Tanner staging is an objective classification system to track secondary sex characteristics during puberty, but it has not been documented to outperform skeletal maturity in regard to predicting final limb length.
Answer 5: The Paley method utilizes a multiplier based on chronological age. This has been shown to be accurate when considering all age groups, but during adolescence, the skeletal age is more accurate.
