## Question

A 68-year-old female presents for a three-month follow-up appointment after anatomic total shoulder arthroplasty for osteoarthritis via a deltopectoral approach. She initially had an uncomplicated recovery, but now complains of increasing pain and decreased range of motion. On physical exam, there is diffuse erythema and a draining sinus adjacent to the surgical incision. If present, which of the following factors placed this patient at an increased risk for this complication?

## Options

1. Performing an anatomic instead of reverse total shoulder arthroplasty
2. Female gender
3. Corticosteroid injection two months prior to surgery
4. Patient age >65 years
5. Initial indication of glenohumeral osteoarthritis

## Correct option

**3. Corticosteroid injection two months prior to surgery**

## Answer explanation

The patient presents with a prosthetic joint infection (PJI) after total shoulder arthroplasty (TSA) with worsening pain, erythema, and development of a sinus tract. A corticosteroid injection within three months of shoulder arthroplasty is known to increase PJI risk (Answer 3).

PJI following TSA is a rare but potentially devastating complication. Multiple risk factors for PJI have been defined, including male gender, age <65 years at index surgery, reverse TSA (versus anatomic), and the presence of inflammatory arthritis. Diagnosis is often more challenging than lower extremity PJI, as Cutibacterium acnes (C. acnes) is commonly implicated and tends to be less virulent than organisms typically affecting hip and knee prostheses. Importantly, prolonged incubation time for aspiration or tissue cultures (up to 14 days) is required for optimal recovery of C. acnes. Infection should be suspected in all cases of painful or dysfunctional TSA; however, the traditional signs of infection may not be present. Consequently, accurate diagnosis relies on a multimodal approach, including history, serologic studies, and microbiologic analysis. Ultimately, management is determined by infection chronicity, organism, and host factors. Treatment options include antibiotics with irrigation, debridement, and component retention for acute infections, and single-stage versus two-stage revision with placement of an antibiotic cement spacer.

*Werner et al. published a Medicare database study assessing the timing of preoperative shoulder injections for infection risk after arthroscopy and arthroplasty.* *The authors compared cohorts of patients who received an injection within 3 months, 3-6 months, and 6-12 months before their surgery.* *Patients who received an injection within 3 months of surgery experienced a 2.2- and 2.0-fold increase in odds of developing postoperative surgical site infection compared to patients who did not receive an injection.* **They concluded** that both arthroscopy and arthroplasty procedures should be delayed beyond 3 months after an injection to minimize infection risk.

*Stadecker et al. completed a database study analyzing the timing of preoperative corticosteroid injection with the risk of revision in TSA and rTSA.* *Patients who received an injection within 3 months of their arthroplasty procedure were at higher risk of revision (OR 2.61).* However, this increased odds of revision dissipated if the patient had an injection >3 months before their procedure. **The authors concluded** that there is a time-dependent association between preoperative steroid injection and revision risk after TSA and rTSA. Arthroplasty should be delayed beyond 3 months from joint injection.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Patients undergoing rTSA have been shown to be at a higher risk of PJI relative to patients who undergo an anatomic TSA.
Answer 2: Male patients are at a higher risk of shoulder PJI for several factors, including a higher density of sebaceous glands with greater colonization of C. acnes. Additionally, males are more likely to undergo TSA for post-traumatic arthritis or rotator cuff arthropathy, both of which increase PJI risk.
Answer 4: Younger patients are at a higher risk of PJI due to increased propensity for C. acnes colonization. Patients <65 years are also more likely to be indicated for TSA due to post-traumatic arthritis or prior failed rotator cuff surgery. Finally, younger patients may have higher mechanical demands, leading to inflammation around the prosthesis and an increased risk of PJI.
Answer 5: Inflammatory arthropathy, rather than osteoarthritis, places patients at an increased risk of shoulder PJI.
