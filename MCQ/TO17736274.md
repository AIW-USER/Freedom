## Question

In autologous chondrocyte implantation true about periosteal patch is (arthroscopy- knee arthroscopy- cartilage injury)

## Options

1. Cambium layer faces towards chondral defect
2. Cambium layer faces away from chondral defect
3. Periosteal patch is sewn into chondral defect
4. Periosteal patch is mixed with cartilage matrix and seeded into chondral defect

## Correct option

**1. Cambium layer faces towards chondral defect**

## Answer explanation

Autologous chondrocyte implantation (ACI):

Autologous chondrocyte implantation (ACI) is a biologic technique in which autologous cells grown in culture are transplanted into chondral and osteochondral defects. Since its first use in the mid 1990s its technique has changed from use of autologous chondrocytes and two procedures (first generation) to one stage procedure and use of autologous or allogeneic stem cells (third generation).

Different generations of ACI:

First generation of ACI: First generation of ACI is a two staged procedure. In the first procedure 200 to 300 mg of cartilage from a less loaded area in the knee is harvested. These cartilage cells are processed in a lab where the isolated chondrocytes are expanded. After 2-3 weeks chondrocytes are injected into the chondral defect in a second procedure. Before transplantation, chondral defect is prepared and a slightly oversized periosteal patch is harvested from the proximal tibia and distal femur. The periosteal patch is placed over the defect with the cambium layer down to the bone and sewn in a watertight manner to the surrounding normal cartilage with multiple interrupted 6-0 absorbable sutures. Margins of periosteum are sealed to the edges of the defect with fibrin glue. After watertight sealing, the cultured cartilage cells are injected into the defect beneath the periosteal cover. Apart from providing a water tight sealing to the chondral defect, periosteum patch has been shown to be chondrogenic and provides a paracrine effect to chondrocyte growth.

With evolution of first-generation ACI further, surgeons have substituted the periosteal patch with collagen membranes to avoid side effects associated with in situ periosteal patch hypertrophy and morbidity of periosteum harvest.

Second generation ACI: Second generation ACI is also known as MACI (matrix induced autologous chondrocyte implantation. It involves the incorporation of a scaffold or substrate to promote chondrocyte expansion. Many autologous chondrocytes Scaffold have been used like, hydrogel, fibrous scaffold, decellularized ECM, or composite etc.

The Histogenics NeoCart technique represents further advancement of MACI-based therapies. It uses a tissue bioreactor to introduce mechanical loading and a proprietary matrix is used with autologous chondrocytes to prepare cartilaginous tissue.

Minced AC (Cartilage Autograft Implantation System) is another novel second-generation, single-stage cartilage repair technique. It mixes minced autograft cartilage in a carrier substrate.

Third-generation ACI: It is a single stage procedure that uses allogenic tissue or autologous stem cells (instead of chondrocytes) for cartilage regeneration. Third generation ACI technique has revolutionized the cartilage repair surgeries by avoiding two stage procedures and morbidity of autogenic cartilage harvest.

De Novo ET (Third generation ACI) uses a chondro conductive/inductive matrix and minced juvenile allograft donor cartilage to fill chondral defects. CARTISTEM, another example of third generation single stage ACI uses mesenchymal stem cells from umbilical cord blood to culture a hyaluronate-based gel for one-stage implantation.

Also know:

Microfracture induces the growth of a repair tissue that consists of type-I, type-II, and type-III collagen and varies from fibrocartilage alone to a mixture of fibrocartilage and hyaline-like cartilage. In contrast, ACI can restore the integrity of damaged chondral areas with hyaline-like cartilage, a hybrid of fibrocartilage and hyaline-like tissue or with fibrocartilaginous material containing type-I and type- II collagen.
