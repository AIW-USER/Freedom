## Question

A 25-year-old male presents to clinic for chronic knee pain. He has had recurrent knee effusions and previously underwent surgery for this condition, but unfortunately, his problem has recurred. The surgeon is planning to start him on a medication that targets the colony stimulating factor-1 receptor. What condition can be treated with this medication?

## Options

1. Gout
2. Hemophilia A
3. Rheumatoid arthritis
4. Synovial chondromatosis
5. Tenosynovial giant cell tumor

## Correct option

**5. Tenosynovial giant cell tumor**

## Answer explanation

Tenosynovial giant cell tumor (TGCT), formerly known as pigmented villonodular synovitis, is a benign tumor that causes recurrent effusions and can be treated with a CSF-1 receptor antagonist in refractory cases (Answer 5).

TGCT of the knee is a rare, typically benign tumor that arises from synovium, bursae, or tendon sheaths. It commonly presents with knee pain, swelling, stiffness, and recurrent joint effusions. The treatment of TGCT primarily involves surgical intervention, with arthroscopic or open synovectomy to remove the abnormal synovial tissue.

Pexidartinib is an oral CSF1R (colony-stimulating factor 1 receptor) inhibitor approved for the treatment of TGCT, particularly in refractory cases or where surgery would result in severe morbidity. TGCT is driven by overexpression of colony-stimulating factor 1 (CSF1), leading to an accumulation of CSF1R-expressing inflammatory cells. Pexidartinib blocks this pathway, helping to reduce tumor growth and associated symptoms. Clinical trials, such as the ENLIVEN study, demonstrated that pexidartinib significantly reduced tumor size and improved physical function and range of motion in patients with advanced TGCT. However, the use of pexidartinib requires careful monitoring due to the risk of hepatotoxicity, including severe, sometimes irreversible liver injury. Patients must undergo baseline and regular liver function tests, and the medication is available only through a Risk Evaluation and Mitigation Strategy (REMS) program.

*West et al.'s study found that TGCT exhibits a "landscape effect," where a translocation-induced overexpression of CSF1 occurs in only a minority of tumor cells.* This localized overexpression leads to the recruitment of non-neoplastic inflammatory cells expressing the CSF1 receptor, contributing to tumor growth. **Their findings suggest** that targeting the CSF1 pathway could be a potential therapeutic strategy for treating TGCT.

*Tap et al.'s ENLIVEN phase 3 trial demonstrated that pexidartinib, a selective CSF1R inhibitor, significantly improved the overall response rate in patients with advanced TGCT compared to placebo.* Pexidartinib treatment led to notable reductions in tumor size and improvements in patient function and symptoms. The study concluded that pexidartinib offers a promising therapeutic option for TGCT patients with symptomatic or advanced disease not amenable to surgery.

Incorrect answers:
Answers 1-4: These conditions can cause recurrent knee effusion. However, they are not treated with a CSF-1 inhibitor.
