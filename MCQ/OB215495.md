## Question

Which of the following is true regarding patient specific instrumentation (PSI) compared to standard instrumentation in total knee arthroplasty?

## Options

1. Lower cost
2. Improved coronal and sagittal alignment
3. Improved postoperative function and range of motion
4. Fewer instrument trays needing to be processed
5. Improved postoperative pain and patient satisfaction

## Correct option

**4. Fewer instrument trays needing to be processed**

## Answer explanation

Patient specific instrumentation (PSI) decreases the number of instruments needing to be processed pre- and post-operatively compared to total knee arthroplasty (TKA) performed with standard instrumentation.

PSI has been advertised as an opportunity to improve operating room efficiency, decrease operative times, improve post-operative pain and function, and improve post-operative TKA alignment. However, there has not been strong evidence to support these initial claims. The only agreed upon benefit of PSI is the decrease in the number of instrument trays needing to be processed perioperatively. Thus, the current literature does not provide clear support for the use of PSI at this time.

*Sassoon et al reviewed PSI in TKA in order to evaluate its effects on post-operative alignment, knee function, and surgical efficiency.* **They noted** no difference in limb alignment and varying effects on operative times. There was also no evidence to suggest an improvement in post-operative function; ultimately, the group was not able to recommend the use of PSI.

*Thienpont et al performed a meta-analysis using PSI in TKA and* **noted** that there was some improvement in the mechanical axis and coronal femoral alignment. However, this came at the expense of tibial malalignment in some cases. **They noted** minimal improvements in OR time and blood loss in the PSI group. Ultimately, they could not justify the use of PSI.

*Nunley et al explored the cost-effectiveness of PSI to hospitals and* **noted** a slight improvement in operating room efficiency in the PSI group. However, there were no differences in radiographic alignment post-operatively and the group did not feel routine PSI use was necessarily cost-effective at this time.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1-3,5: These benefits have not been identified with the use of PSI over standard TKA instrumentation.
