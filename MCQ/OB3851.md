## Question

A 45 year-old male presents to your office for a painless foot mass that has now become irritating with shoe wear preventing him from going into public. On T1 non-contrast MRI image is a 1.5 x 3.0 cm well circumscribed isointense mass compared to the subcutaneous fat just superficial to the muscle of extensor digitorum brevis, that is homogeneous and isointense compared to subcutaneous fat on T2 reconstructions that does not enhance with contrast. ## Options

1. ganglion, wide resection
2. lipoma, wide resection
3. lipoma, marginal excision
4. liposarcoma, wide excision
5. fibroma, marginal excision

## Correct option

**3. lipoma, marginal excision**

## Answer explanation

The patient is presenting with a lipoma on the dorsum on the foot, which is best treated with marginal resection when they are symptomatic.

Lipomas most frequently appear on the dorsum of the foot but also may occur in muscle bellies. They are usually subcutaneous and are identifiable by the soft feeling of a mobile lesion with a distinct grape-like edge. They are typically painless unless compressed by shoe wear. Lipomas are a proliferation of fat cells that grossly and histologically resemble normal fat. The mainstay of treatment is observation, but surgical excision can be performed when the lesion becomes painful or is rapidly growing.

*Bos et al.* **state** that lipomas are well encapsulated and marginal excision are adequate. Local recurrence is rare if the thin capsule of the lesion is left intact during excision.

*Johnson et al. in a review of lipomatous tumors state lipomas are defined as "determinate" because they are diagnosed with the MRI findings: consistent with superficial fat on all image reconstructions and therefore do not require a biopsy before treatment.* MRI findings such as heterogeneous signal intensity, lacking isointense signal compared with subcutaneous fat, post-contrast enhancement, and necrosis should alert the clinician to consider other diagnoses.

*Papp et al show lipomas have a characteristic appearance on magnetic resonance imaging corresponding to that of adipose tissue.* These benign tumors appear bright on T1-weighted imaging (Fig. 1-A) and moderate to bright on T2-weighted images (Fig. 1-B). In all sequences, the lesion appears isointense compared with the subcutaneous fat.

Figure A shows a cut gross specimen showing homogenous lobular yellow tissue consistent with a lipoma.
 Figure B shows a histology slide with bland acellular storms and no signs of atypia consistent with a lipoma. Illustration A shows a T1 and T2 image of a lipoma with a signal that is isointense to subcutaneous fat in all sequences.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1 - Ganglion, while the most common soft tissue lesion in the foot and ankle will enhance on T2 weighted images suggesting the cystic or fluid-filled character.
Answer 2 - Lipomas do not require wide resection, and this image description is determinant indicating marginal excision.
Answer 4 - The image description, pathology, and histology are compatible with a benign lesion, due to the well-circumscribed nature compatible with fat on all series, yellow homogeneous gross specimen, and histology demonstrating mature adipocytes with eccentric nuclei without atypia or signs of hyperactivity, the correct treatment would depend on biopsy subtype.
Answer 5 - Fibroma would show histologically, well-differentiated fibroblasts with a large amount of dense collagen fibers, with the correct treatment being marginal excision.
