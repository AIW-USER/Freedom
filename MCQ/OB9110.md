## Question

A 50-year-old woman with a history of metastatic breast cancer reports progressive back pain and lower extremity weakness over the past 2 weeks. The weakness has progressed to a point where she can only ambulate short distances with a walker. A sagittal T2-weighted MRI scan is shown in Figure A, and a sagittal contrast-enhanced T1-weighted MRI scan is shown in Figure B. Her cancer is found to be positive for a chromosomal translocation and the oncology team expects her to respond well to targeted therapy with a life expectancy of more than 3 years. What would be the most appropriate definitive treatment for this patient?

## Options

1. Palliative chemotherapy and radiation
2. Targeted radiation therapy
3. Laminectomy alone with posterior instrumented fusion with immediate pre-operative and post-operative radiation therapy
4. Corpectomy and anterior column reconstruction, followed by posterior instrumented fusion
5. Kyphoplasty with TLSO bracing, followed by biopsy-targeted medical management

## Correct option

**4. Corpectomy and anterior column reconstruction, followed by posterior instrumented fusion**

## Answer explanation

The clinical presentation is consistent with metastatic disease to the thoracic spine in a patient with a progressive neurologic deficit. Given anterior spinal cord compression and favorable oncological prognosis, corpectomy with reconstruction and posterior instrumented fusion is the most appropriate treatment.

Management of metastatic lesions to the spine is determined by the degree of instability, neurological deficit, and oncological prognosis. Biopsy is not required in patients with known metastatic disease. In the thoracic spine, lesions from the vertebral body commonly lead to anterior spinal cord compression and require either partial, total, or multilevel corpectomy for adequate decompression. The decision for an anterior transthoracic or lateral thoracotomy approach versus a posterior only approach for corpectomy and reconstruction is based on the degree of pathology and surgeon preference. The advancement of expandable cages has broadened the use of the posterior only approach for anterior decompression through a transpedicular or costotransversectomy approach. Regardless of the approach used, anterior spinal cord compression must be addressed surgically.

*Patchell et al. published a prospective randomized study comparing patients with metastatic cancer-related spinal cord compression who received surgery followed by radiation versus patients who only received radiation.* The results were so dramatically in favor of the combination of surgery and radiation that the study was terminated early. **They found** an increased ability to ambulate, longer duration of ambulatory ability, and decrease in opioid consumption in the surgical group. Of note, patients with very radiosensitive tumors such as multiple myeloma were excluded from the study.

*Kim et al. completed a systematic review of the literature comparing surgery with or without radiation to radiation alone for metastatic disease to the spine.* *In a review of 33 studies,* **they found** surgical patients to have improved neurological function and decreased pain. In the subset of patients with paraplegia, the surgical group had a four-fold increase in return to ambulation.

*Tokuhashi et al. published a scoring system to help develop a treatment algorithm for patients with metastatic spinal lesions.* *They used a patient's general condition, the number of extraspinal bone metastases, the number of metastases in the vertebral body, the presence of metastases to other major internal organs, the primary site of the tumor, as well as the degree of neurologic compromise to determine life expectancy.* **They recommend** against surgical intervention for patients with less than six months to live.

Figure A and B are T2 sagittal and T1 post-contrast sagittal MRI images, respectively, that show a metastatic lesion in two contiguous vertebral bodies in the lower thoracic spine with anterior spinal cord compression. Illustration A shows the Tokuhashi scoring system.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: This would be appropriate in patients with less than 6 months survival, severe medical comorbidities, or in those who refuse surgery.
Answer 2: Radiation therapy will not address the anterior spinal cord compression that is causing the progressive neurologic deficit.
Answer 3: Laminectomy without corpectomy will not address the anterior spinal cord compression in this patient. Posterior fusion will help to stabilize the spine but only in combination with anterior spinal cord decompression.
Answer 5: Kyphoplasty is not indicated in a patient with metastatic disease causing bony destruction and progressive neurologic impairment.
