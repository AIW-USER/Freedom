## Question

During total hip replacement for dysplastic hip sudden massive bleeding occurs immediately after the junior surgeon places the retractor deep to transverse acetabular ligament. What can be the likely source of this bleeding?

## Options

1. External iliac vein
2. External iliac artery
3. Obturator artery
4. Medial femoral circumflex artery

## Correct option

**3. Obturator artery**

## Answer explanation

- Obturator artery and vein: Errant retractor placement inferior to the ligament can cause damage to obturator artery and vein. Transverse acetabular ligament extends between the two cotyloid pads at the inferior aspect of the acetabulum.
- External iliac artery and veins: Acetabular anterior-superior quadrant is considered the zone of death. Screws and/or drill that penetrate too far risk laceration to the external iliac artery and veins.
- Medial femoral circumflex artery: Located underneath quadratus femoris muscle or gluteus maximus tendon. Cutting deep to this area risks laceration to the vessel.
- Sciatic nerve: Eighty percent of nerve injuries following THA are to sciatic nerve; 20% involve femoral nerve. Compression is the most common pathologic mechanism of injury. Sciatic nerve travels closest to the acetabulum at the level of ischium. During surgery, the most common reason for sciatic nerve injury is errant retractor placement causing excess compression to nerves. Peroneal nerve division is most often involved because this part of the nerve is closest to the acetabulum.

Answer: 3

Risk factors for nerve injury 1. Female 2. Post-traumatic arthritis 3. Revision surgery 4. Developmental dysplasia of the hip. 5. Leg lengthening more than 3 cm.
