## Question

A 56-year-old female presents for evaluation of chronic left ankle pain. She has a history of an ankle fracture treated non-operatively 20 years prior and was doing well until 5 years ago when she developed ankle pain after long periods of ambulation. She has limited ankle dorsiflexion and plantarflexion on examination but full eversion and inversion. Her imaging is shown in Figures A through C. The patient is currently only interested in non-operative management. Which of the following should be recommended for potential symptomatic relief?

## Options

1. Lateral hindfoot post
2. Arizona brace
3. Hinged ankle-foot orthosis (AFO)
4. Double rocker bottom shoe
5. CROW boot

## Correct option

**2. Arizona brace**

## Answer explanation

The patient has evidence of tibiotalar osteoarthritis (OA). She would benefit from an Arizona, or gauntlet ankle, brace to provide symptomatic improvement of her pain.

Tibiotalar ankle arthritis is most commonly secondary to trauma, which can complicate treatment in younger, more active patients. The clinical symptoms, examination, and radiographs of this patient are consistent with ankle arthritis. Regardless of patient age, the most appropriate nonsurgical intervention to minimize the symptoms for the patient is a gauntlet ankle brace, also known commercially as an Arizona brace, which immobilizes the ankle and hindfoot, minimizing the pain. Physical therapy may worsen the pain and is not indicated. Platelet-rich plasma injections have not proven to be effective and hinged AFO would not immobilize the ankle joint. Surgical management of isolated tibiotalar arthritis generally involves tibiotalar arthrodesis or total ankle arthroplasty (TAA).

*Jastifer et al. performed a prospective study on 77 consecutive patients who underwent TAA (61 patients) and ankle arthrodesis (16 patients) with 12 months postoperative follow-up.* **The authors noted** both TAA and ankle arthrodesis groups had high patient satisfaction, improvement in VAS pain scores, AOFAS Ankle Hindfoot scores, and functional scores (all P values < .05). TAA patients had a significantly better outcome than the arthrodesis patients in ankle range of motion (ROM) and walking uphill/downhill. **The authors conclude** that while both TAA and ankle arthrodesis provides excellent pain relief and patient satisfaction, patients undergoing TAA have improved motion and a more efficient ability to ambulate at 12 months.

*Haddad et al. performed a systematic review to evaluate the intermediate and long-term outcomes of TAA and ankle arthrodesis.* **They identified** 49 primary studies, 10 of which evaluated TAA in a total of 852 patients and 39 of which evaluated ankle arthrodesis in a total of 1262 patients. **The authors noted** a revision rate following TAA of 7% with the primary reason for the revisions being loosening and/or subsidence (28%). The revision rate following ankle arthrodesis was 9% with the main reason for the revisions being nonunion (65%). **The authors conclude** that the intermediate outcome of TAA appears to be similar to that of ankle arthrodesis but comparative studies are needed to strengthen this conclusion.

*Hahn et al. compared pre-surgical and post-surgical gait measures in patients undergoing TAA versus ankle arthrodesis for ankle arthritis.* **They noted** 18 patients (9 who underwent TAA and 9 who underwent arthrodesis) with similar pre-operative demographics. **The authors concluded** that pain reduction and gait function improves 12 months after surgery for both treatments but TAA appears to regain more natural ankle joint function, with increased ROM.

*Coester et al. performed a clinical and radiographic review to determine the effect of ankle arthrodesis on the development of OA in other lower-extremity joints.* Twenty-three patients who had an isolated ankle arthrodesis were followed for a mean of 22 years. OA of the ipsilateral subtalar, talonavicular, calcaneocuboid, naviculocuneiform and tarsometatarsal were consistently more severe than the OA of the joints on the contralateral side. OA did not develop more frequently in the ipsilateral knee or lesser metatarsophalangeal joints than it did on the contralateral side. **The authors conclude** that although ankle arthrodesis may provide good early relief of pain, it is associated with premature deterioration of other joints of the foot and eventual arthritis, pain, and dysfunction.

Figures A through C show AP, oblique and lateral radiographs of the ankle showing severe tibiotalar arthritis with sparing of the subtalar joint.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Lateral hindfoot post orthosis is used for the treatment of a cavovarus foot deformity
Answer 3: Hinged AFO would be used for the management of foot drop but it does not immobilize the ankle or hindfoot.
Answers 4 and 5: The CROW boot (Charcot Restraint Orthotic Walker) and double rocker bottom show decrease stress on the midfoot and are recommended for Charcot foot deformities and midfoot OA.
