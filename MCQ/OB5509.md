## Question

An 18-year-old college football player is tackled on the field with a blow to the head during his first game of the season. According to NCAA guidelines, all of the following signs require him to be out of play for at least 5-10 days and progress through a stepwise rehabilitation protocol without experiencing symptoms EXCEPT:

## Options

1. Momentary loss of consciousness
2. Inability to recall events prior to tackle
3. Blank stare
4. Gait instability
5. Cervical muscle tenderness

## Correct option

**5. Cervical muscle tenderness**

## Answer explanation

All of the listed items are contraindications to return to play for 5-10 days EXCEPT for cervical muscle tenderness which is not a sign or symptom of a concussion and thus, does not require initiation of a return-to-play concussion protocol. It would, however, require proper evaluation for cervical spine injury, but muscle tenderness alone after complete evaluation does not restrict play for 5-10 days.

Sports-related concussions are estimated to occur 1.6-3.8 times annually and contribute to approximately 6% of all NCAA football injuries. Any athlete with any signs or symptoms of a concussion must be removed from play immediately, and guidelines from the NCAA, some national sports leagues as well as some state laws strictly prevent return to play on the day of injury. There remains ongoing debate regarding best methods for the initial diagnosis of concussion as well as reliable metrics for safe return to play.

Several screening tools exist for sideline assessment of an injured athlete, specifically the Standardized Concussion Assessment Tool (SCAT3) which includes the Standardized Assessment of Concussion (SAC) as well as the Maddocks Questions. These tools can be helpful but all agree that concussion diagnosis is based on clinical judgment and can be particularly challenging given the slow development of some symptoms, often absence or transience of neuromotor deficits, and athlete pressure to return to play. Thus, players noted to actually have any signs or symptoms suggesting concussion after head strike are automatically removed from play and must undergo stepwise, graduated return-to-play protocols.

*Ma et al. review the assessment and management of sports-related concussions.* *They describe the elements of a concussion, including duration, physical signs/symptoms, cognitive deficits, emotional lability, and sleep disturbance.* *They again emphasize the need for First Aid evaluation of airway, breathing, and circulation as well as cervical spine stabilization and/or clearance prior to a focused concussion evaluation.*

*McCrory et al. report the now fourth revised International Consensus Guidelines on concussion management.* In addition to reviewing presentation, they outline the return-to-play protocol of graduated progression through 1. no activity, 2. light aerobic activity, 3. sports-specific exercises, 4. non-contact training drills, 5. full-contact practice, 6. return to play. Athlete must be symptom-free through each step which is usually monitored for at least 24 hours. Analogous pathways exist for cognitive "return-to-learn" programs to begin schoolwork again.
