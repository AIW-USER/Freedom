## Question

Long follow through in bowling (sports medicine- biomechanics of common sports- phases of throwing )

## Options

1. Increase chances of muscle injury
2. Decreases chances of muscle injury
3. Increases muscle fatigue
4. Has no relation with muscle injury

## Correct option

**2. Decreases chances of muscle injury**

## Answer explanation

The biomechanics of throwing: Throwing can be divided into four phases

1. Wind-up (500-1000 ms): during this phase shoulder muscles are relatively inactive.

2. Cocking( 500-1000 ms): shoulder moves into abduction, extension and external rotation so, at the end of cocking anterior static restraints like anterior –inferior glenohumeral ligament and anterior-inferior capsule develop maximum strain. Windup and cocking together account for 80 % of the throwing cycle. Maximum external rotation is seen in this phase with peak muscle activation of rotator cuff muscles. Glenohumeral internal rotation defect (GIRD) and internal impingement are associated with this phase.

3. Acceleration (50ms): it is the shortest explosive phase and two forces release the ball. First, forceful contraction of internal rotators and second, elastic recoil of tight anterior capsule. Peak muscle activation is seen in elbow extensor(triceps) and then in shoulder internal rotators( pectoralis major and latissimus dorsi). With release of the ball enormous valgus force is applied on the elbow.

4. Deceleration/follow-through: Eccentric contraction of shoulder external rotators, posterior deltoid and scapular stabilizers decelerates the rapid internal rotation and arm motion. It is the highest torque phase and considered the most harmful phase. SLAP tear and biceps injuries are associated with this phase.

Late cocking and deceleration phases put greatest force on the shoulder.

Answer: 2
