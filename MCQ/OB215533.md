## Question

Which of the following is true regarding complications related to distal biceps repair?

## Options

1. The posterior interosseous nerve (PIN) is the most common nerve injured.
2. Using the interval between ECU and EDC increases the risk of heterotopic ossification with the dual-incision technique.
3. The most common complication of the single-incision technique is injury to the lateral antebrachial cutaneous nerve.
4. Load-to-failure is highest with interference screw fixation alone.
5. Risk of radioulnar synostosis is eliminated by using the single-incision technique.

## Correct option

**3. The most common complication of the single-incision technique is injury to the lateral antebrachial cutaneous nerve.**

## Answer explanation

The most common complication of a distal biceps repair is a postoperative neuropraxia of the lateral antebrachial cutaneous nerve, which causes numbness over the radial aspect of the forearm.

Distal biceps repairs are intended to improve the supination and flexion strength at the elbow as well as mitigate the deformity caused by excessive retraction of the biceps muscle belly proximally (i.e. reverse Popeye deformity). However, complications are not uncommon, regardless of fixation method. Single-incision techniques have become more popular due to a lower rate of heterotopic ossification because of less periosteal stripping during dissection compared to the dual-incision approach. However, regardless of fixation method, the most common complication is injury to the lateral antebrachial cutaneous nerve. Other nerves, including the PIN/radial nerve, can also be injured, although are less often affected.

*Ford et al. reviewed complications after distal biceps repairs in a cohort of 970 cases.* **They note** an overall 7.5% rate of complication with lateral antebrachial cutaneous nerve injury being most common in patients treated with a single-incision approach and hetertopic ossification being most common after the dual-incision approach.

*Matzon et al. prospectively evaluated a group of 212 distal biceps repairs postoperatively and* **noted** 30% of patients had at least one complication. Rates of complication were higher (45%) in the single-incision group compared to the dual-incision group (15%). Sensory neuropraxia was the most common complication, however, 95% were noted to resolve early in the postoperative period.

*Dunphy et al. evaluated outcomes and complications after a large group of distal biceps repairs.* **They noted** the most common overall complication was lateral antebrachial cutaneous nerve injury, which was more common in the single-incision group. The dual-incision group had higher rates of PIN palsy, heterotopic bone formation, and reoperation.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: While injury to the posterior interosseous nerve (PIN) is possible and more likely with the dual-incision technique, the rate of injury remains low compared to the lateral antebrachial cutaneous nerve.
Answer 2: The interval between ECU/EDC is suggested when performing the dual-incision technique, as this avoids periosteal stripping of the ulna, which is thought to increase the risk for heterotopic ossification.
Answer 4: Failure of fixation is a possible complication after distal biceps rupture and interference screw fixation alone has the lowest load-to-failure, not the highest
Answer 5: Risk of radioulnar synostosis is decreased using the single-incision technique, however, it is not eliminated, as there can still be some periosteal stripping that stimulates bony overgrowth and synostosis.
