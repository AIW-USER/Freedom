## Question

A 21-year-old college lacrosse defender is struck in the abdomen with a shot during practice. After initially recovering, the player now complains about left upper quadrant pain with radiation to his left shoulder. You are concerned about an intraabdominal injury and recommend the player be sent to the emergency department. En route, he loses consciousness and is found to be hemodynamically unstable in the trauma bay. The subsequent FAST exam is positive for free fluid. What is the next step in his management?

## Options

1. Exploratory laparotomy
2. Abdominal CT with contrast
3. Supportive IV Fluids
4. Angiography
5. Monospot test

## Correct option

**1. Exploratory laparotomy**

## Answer explanation

The patient suffered abdominal trauma and is now hemodynamically unstable, with free fluid found on the FAST Exam; an exploratory laparotomy is indicated (Answer 1).

A splenic rupture is a potentially life-threatening injury often associated with blunt trauma to the abdomen, which can occur during high-impact sports such as football, rugby, or skiing. The spleen is a highly vascular organ, and a rupture can lead to significant internal bleeding, hypovolemic shock, and, if untreated, death. Athletes may initially present with vague symptoms such as abdominal pain, left shoulder pain (Kehr's sign), or fatigue, which can delay diagnosis. Prompt recognition and treatment, including possible surgical intervention, are crucial to preventing severe complications. Coaches and medical staff should maintain a high index of suspicion for splenic injury in athletes following abdominal trauma.

*Rifat et al. review blunt abdominal trauma in sports.* Because some abdominal traumas are highly injurious, even life-threatening, it is crucial for physicians present at sporting events to be familiar with the signs and symptoms of injury to the abdominal wall, liver, spleen, stomach, intestines, and pancreas. **The authors emphasize** the importance of side-line physicians to differentiate benign from potentially life-threatening abdominal injuries. In the event of significant trauma or suspected hematoma, the patient should be transported to a hospital for further evaluation by general surgery trauma.

*Gannon et al. review splenic injuries in athletes.* Fortunately, a relatively rare complication, splenic injuries can have serious, if not fatal, consequences due to the organ's high vascularity. Because of the wide variety of possible mechanisms of injury, it is crucial to consider splenic injury in the differential diagnosis of any injured athlete presenting with abdominal pain or discomfort. An athlete presenting with abdominal pain requires frequent assessment and possible transport to the hospital for imaging, regardless of how trivial the initial injury may appear.

Incorrect Answers:

Answers 2-4: While these tests may be appropriate in a stable patient, an ex-lap is indicated, given the hemodynamic instability and evidence of intra-abdominal fluid on the FAST exam.
Answer 5: Splenomegaly is common in mononucleosis, which is diagnosed with a mono-spot test. This may have been helpful prior to the injury but is not currently clinically indicated.
