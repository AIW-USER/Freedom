## Question

What is the best treatment for a 4 year old obese child with Blounts disease with MD angle 18 degree and depression of medial tibial articular surface. (pediatric orthopedics- Blount's disease)

## Options

1. Observation
2. Proximal tibial osteotomy with correction to neutral mechanical axis
3. Proximal tibial osteotomy with correction to 10 degree valgus
4. Physeal bar resection

## Correct option

**3. Proximal tibial osteotomy with correction to 10 degree valgus**

## Answer explanation

Blounts disease/Tibia vara- Tibia vara is considered an acquired disease of the proximal tibial metaphysis. The exact cause is unknown, but endochondral ossification seems to be altered. It is characterized by varus and internal torsion of the tibia and genu recurvatum. Blount distinguished, according to age at onset, two types of tibia vara: infantile, which begins before 8 years of age, and adolescent, which begins after 8 years of age but before skeletal maturity. It does not occur in nonambulatory patients, and the relationship of early walking and obesity to Blount disease has been clearly documented.

X Ray features: In tibia vara, characteristically the medial half of the epiphysis as seen on radiographs is short, thin, and wedged; the physis is irregular in contour and slopes medially. The proximal metaphysis forms a projection medially that is often palpable. Medial metaphyseal fragmentation is pathognomonic for the development of a progressive tibia vara.

MRI studies reveal other soft-tissue abnormalities: (1) increased thickness of the chondroepiphysis of the proximal medial aspect of the tibia, (2) increased height and width of the medial meniscus (increased sign in the posterior horn), and (3) abnormal medial femoral epiphysis.

In young children blount's disease needs to be differentiated from physiological varum. A metaphyseal-diaphyseal angle of more than 11 degrees warrants close observation for development of blount's disease.

Management: many reports suggest that this generally is a self-limiting condition that corrects spontaneously and that severe progression should be documented before valgus osteotomy is done. The proximal tibial physis has the potential to correct the deformity in the adjacent metaphysis, depending on the age of the patient and the severity of the deformity. Osteotomy is indicated only for significant deformity in an older child when spontaneous correction no longer can be expected.

Generally, observation is indicated for children between ages 2 and 5 years, but progressive deformity usually requires osteotomy.

Indications for use of orthotics /braces in Blount's disease: The indications include children under the age of three years and unilateral disease Langenskiöld stage I or II. Patients over four years old, with weight to age percentile > 90, presenting bilateral disease or progressive deformity do not show good performance with nonsurgical treatment.

For progressive deformity early tibia/fibula valgus osteotomy (between 2-4 years) produces best results and least recurrence rates. Many osteotomies described in the literature include metaphyseal osteotomy, metaphyseal and epiphyseal osteotomy, intra epiphyseal osteotomy and hemi elevation of intra epiphysis osteotomy. Overcorrect into 10-15° of valgus is done because medial physeal growth abnormalities persist.

Lateral tibial epiphysiodesis can be done, with or without osteotomy, after the age of 9 years but before skeletal maturity. In unilateral involvement, epiphysiodesis of the uninvolved leg may be indicated to correct leg-length discrepancy.

Physeal bar resection: One cause of recurrence of the deformity after osteotomy is a physeal bar. Following criteria have been suggested for deciding if CT studies should be done preoperatively to determine if a bony bar is present: (1) age older than 5 years, (2) medial physeal slope of 50 to 70 degrees, (3) Langenskiöld grade IV radiographic changes, (4) body weight greater than the 95th percentile, and (5) black girls who meet the previous criteria. Bony bridge resection should be considered in children with remaining growth potential and can be done in conjunction with tibial osteotomy if angulation is significant.

In the question articular depression suggests stage 3 or more. MD angle 18 and age 4 all warrant for corrective osteotomy and over correction to 10 degree valgus.

Answer : c
