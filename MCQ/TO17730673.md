## Question

A 29 years male undergoes surgery for cervical myelopathy and radiculopathy. Below given is his postoperative x-ray. 12 hours after surgery he complains of difficulty in breathing, swallowing, and talking, which exacerbates in the supine position. He also exhibits tense incision line. However, his oxygen saturation remains normal. What is the likely cause and management? (spine- cervical disc herniation and myelopathy)

![[TO17730673-1.png]]

## Options

1. Retropharyngeal hematoma and immediate evacuation
2. Retropharyngeal hematoma and supportive treatment
3. Angioedema and intravenous corticosteroids
4. Pharyngo-laryngeal edema and cricothyrotomy.

## Correct option

**1. Retropharyngeal hematoma and immediate evacuation**

## Answer explanation

This is a case of anterior cervical discectomy and fusion.

Respiratory distress in the early postoperative period may present in various ways after ACDF surgery. In the early postoperative period between 6 to 12 hours angioedema is the most important cause and requires immediate intervention and IV corticosteroids. Between 6 to 24 hours retropharyngeal hematoma is the most common cause and warrants immediate evacuation. Pharyngolaryngeal edema usually presents between 12 to 72 hours and may require urgent cricothyrotomy.

Angioedema: Patients present with difficulty in breathing with neck swelling extending into the face and oropharynx. They may also have a massively swollen tongue protruding from their mouth. Most cases of angioedema are idiopathic, and these cases are treated with intravenous (IV) corticosteroids, antihistamines, and/or subcutaneous (or IV) epinephrine.

Retropharyngeal hematoma: Patients who develop a retropharyngeal hematoma typically form a tense mass under the incision, swelling of the anterior neck, and possible tracheal deviation. At the time of diagnosis, a retropharyngeal hematoma should be immediately evacuated through the existing surgical incision to prevent further progression of respiratory distress.

Pharyngolaryngeal edema: It may progress slowly with difficulty talking and subtle changes in voice quality. Dyspnea may be exacerbated in supine position. Some patients may suddenly collapse with respiratory arrest. Patients with pharyngolaryngeal edema may pose a significant challenge for reintubation, and potential emergency cricothyrotomy should always be considered.

Answer: 1
