## Question

A 48-year-old male is brought into the emergency department with an obvious deformity to his right lower extremity after a mountain biking accident. Radiographs are obtained, shown in Figures A and B. The orthopedic surgeon is consulted and made aware of a neurologic deficit secondary to the injury. ## Options

1. Decreased sensation over the medial leg and foot
2. Weakness with ankle dorsiflexion
3. Weakness with ankle plantarflexion
4. Loss of sensation over the posterolateral leg
5. Loss of toe plantarflexion and loss of sensation to plantar foot

## Correct option

**2. Weakness with ankle dorsiflexion**

## Answer explanation

Common peroneal nerve injuries are the most common of the associated neurovascular injuries in knee dislocations. Common peroneal nerve injuries clinically present with decreased sensation over the anterolateral leg and dorsum of the foot and loss of ankle dorsiflexion (Answer choice 2).

Knee dislocations are rare injuries that result in complete disruption of the tibiofemoral joint with high rates of associated injuries. Knee dislocations have a particularly high rate of neurovascular injuries, with common peroneal nerve (CPN) injuries being the most common (up to 40% of cases). The CPN is particularly vulnerable due to its superficial course around the fibular neck, making it susceptible to traction, compression, or direct trauma during dislocation. Clinically, patients will present with the inability to dorsiflex at the ankle, weakened toe extension, and impaired eversion of the foot. The CPN also provides sensory function to the anterolateral leg, dorsum of the foot, and first web space. Injury to the CPN after a knee dislocation carries a poor prognosis, with some reports of less than 1/3 of patients regaining the ability to dorsiflex the ankle.

*Kahan et al. published a retrospective study analyzing 123 patients treated for multiligamentous knee injuries (MLKIs) at a single level-1 trauma center.* *The study compared 45 patients with documented knee dislocations to 78 patients without documented dislocations to assess the severity of these injuries.* Compared to the non-dislocated group, patients with documented knee dislocations had significantly higher rates of CPN injuries (38% vs 14%) and vascular injuries (18% vs 4%). **The authors concluded** that MLKIs and knee dislocations are separate, distinct injuries with differing risks of neurovascular compromise.

*Worley et al. published a retrospective study to examine the incidence and outcomes of CPN injuries in patients with MLKIs.* *The study included 357 patients, of which 68 (19%) had CPN injuries.* Compared to patients without nerve injuries, those with CPN injuries had reduced postoperative knee range of motion (121° vs 127°), and lower return-to-work rates (73% vs 81%). Despite this, **the authors found** no difference in observed pain levels, activity resumption, or functional scores between the two groups. **The authors concluded** that MLKIs carry a high rate of CPN injuries that result in decreased knee mobility and return-to-work rates.

Figures A and B show AP and lateral radiographs of the right knee demonstrating an anterior knee dislocation without any accompanying fractures.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer choice 1: Medial leg and foot sensation is provided by the saphenous nerve (L3, L4), which branches proximally from the femoral nerve to travel medially down the lower leg. The saphenous nerve is not likely to be injured in a knee dislocation.
Answer choice 3: The tibial nerve (L4-S3) provides motor function to the superficial and deep posterior compartments of the leg, supplying the muscles responsible for ankle plantarflexion. The tibial nerve is less likely injured in a knee dislocation than the common peroneal nerve.
Answer choice 4: The sural nerve (S1-S2) is a purely sensory nerve that provides sensation to the posterolateral leg, lateral ankle, and lateral foot. It is formed by cutaneous branches of both the tibial and common peroneal nerves. The sural nerve is much less commonly injured in knee dislocations than the common peroneal nerve.
Answer choice 5: Toe plantarflexion and sensation to the plantar foot is supplied by the tibial nerve (via the terminal medial and lateral plantar branches). The tibial nerve is less likely injured in a knee dislocation compared to the common peroneal nerve.
