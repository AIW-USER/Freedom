## Question

A 17-year-old male is referred to your office with right foot pain and numbness that has been ongoing for the past two months. The patient underwent marginal resection of a posterior knee mass by his local orthopaedist three months ago, and he has had difficulty dorsiflexing his foot since the procedure. You are able to obtain his pre-surgical MRI and recent histology slides, which are shown in figures A-C. What is the next best step?

## Options

1. Physical therapy
2. Ankle foot orthosis and observation
3. Staging and re-resection of tumor bed
4. Posterior tibial tendon to anterior tibial tendon transfer with nerve exploration
5. Common peroneal nerve exploration

## Correct option

**3. Staging and re-resection of tumor bed**

## Answer explanation

The patient has an epithelioid sarcoma which requires wide resection. Due to the fact that the patient only underwent marginal resection, the next best step is to perform a wide resection after repeat staging studies are completed.

Resection of a high-grade malignancy without following oncologic principles typically results in high rates of residual tumor. Current literature supports rates of residual tumor in 50-60% of patients that undergo unplanned resection of a soft tissue sarcoma. Furthermore, these patients with residual disease are at greater risk of metastasis and typically will require more mutilating surgeries, highlighting the need for referral to a specialist prior to any intervention. Re-excision of the tumor bed with pre or post-operative radiation is recommended for the best possible oncologic outcome.

*Thway et al. discuss the clinical features and treatment for epithelioid sarcoma.* *They describe epithelioid sarcoma as a rare sarcoma (<1% of all soft tissue sarcomas), presenting with a peak incidence young adult males, but noting that it can occur at any age.* It typically presents as a slow-growing, superficial lesion in the hands or arms and treatment is wide surgical resection with adjuvant radiotherapy.

*Asano et al. retrospectively reviewed the clinicopathologic features of 44 patients with epithelioid sarcoma.* **They found** that patients with the proximal subtype of epithelioid sarcoma had significantly more aggressive features (compared to the classic subtype) such as more proximal location, distant metastases, higher tumor grade, vascular invasion, and decreased survival.

Figure A is a T1-weighted axial MRI of the distal femur demonstrating a large, heterogeneous posterior soft tissue mass adjacent the neurovascular bundle. Figure B is a T2-weighted sagittal MRI of the same mass. Figure C demonstrates the histology of epithelioid sarcoma. This tumor has cells that appear similar to epithelial cells ("epithelioid") and are generally ovoid or polygonal. Additionally, these cells demonstrate significant atypia and significant intracytoplasmic vacuolization. Epithelioid sarcoma can also demonstrate a granuloma-type pattern where the cells cluster around a necrotic area, but this is not seen in this slide.

Incorrect answers:
Answers 1 ,2, 4, 5: While physical therapy, AFO, tendon transfer, or nerve exploration may be beneficial, the most important next step is an appropriate oncologic margin.
