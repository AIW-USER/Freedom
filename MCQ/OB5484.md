## Question

An 8-year-old girl falls off a trampoline and lands awkwardly. She presents with left knee pain, intermittent popping and swelling for 1 week and difficulty straightening her knee. Examination reveals a mild knee effusion. Arthroscopic examination findings are shown in the Video. Which of the following MRI or radiographic imaging findings is most consistent with this condition?

## Options

1. High cartilage signal on T2- or proton density weighted MR images
2. Double anterior horn sign
3. Visualization of a second smaller posterior cruciate ligament lying anteroinferior to the native posterior cruciate ligament
4. Cystic lesion within the meniscus demonstrating high signal intensity on T2-weighted imaging
5. Widening of the lateral joint space, squaring of the lateral condyle, cupping of lateral tibial plateau and hypoplasia of the lateral tibial spine.

## Correct option

**5. Widening of the lateral joint space, squaring of the lateral condyle, cupping of lateral tibial plateau and hypoplasia of the lateral tibial spine.**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a discoid lateral meniscus. Radiographic findings can include lateral joint space widening, squaring of the lateral condyle, cupping of lateral tibial plateau and hypoplasia of the lateral tibial spine.

Discoid menisci occur in 3-5% of the population. They are usually lateral and may be bilateral in 25-50%. Sagittal MRI images showing meniscal continuity in three 5mm sagittal images ("bow-tie sign") is diagnostic. If there is pain, mechanical symptoms, meniscal tear or detachment, arthroscopic debridement and saucerization is indicated. The aim is to preserve a rim of normal meniscus.

*Lee et al. reviewed 23 partial meniscectomies and 20 subtotal/total meniscectomies in children <15years.* **They found** more arthritic changes at final followup in the subtotal/total meniscectomy group than the partial meniscectomy group. **They recommended** early diagnosis and greater caution in the treatment of torn discoid lateral menisci in children.

*Carter et al. retrospectively reviewed 33 patients who underwent saurcerization, and 24 who underwent saucerization and stabilization for discoid meniscus.* **They found** no difference in self-reported outcomes between the 2 groups. **They concluded** that stabilization does not affect outcomes or complication rates.

Incorrect Answers
Answer 1: This finding is consistent with chondromalacia patellae. These findings are seen on patellar articular cartilage.
Answer 2: These findings are consistent with bucket handle tear of a meniscus. Other signs include double PCL sign (Answer 3), flipped meniscus sign and disproportional posterior horn sign.
Answer 3: The double PCL sign is seen with a bucket-handle tear of a meniscus (usually medial).
Answer 4: This is consistent with a meniscal cyst.
