## Question

A 50-year-old male presents with 2 years of isolated low back pain and the sagittal T2-weighted MR image seen in Figure A. He has a normal neurologic examination. When comparing fusion surgery to cognitive and exercise therapy, which of the following statements is the most accurate?

![[q5720-a-l5s1-ddd.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Clinical outcomes showed statistically significant improvement with fusion surgery compared to cognitive and exercise therapy
2. Clinical outcomes showed statistically significant improvement with cognitive and exercise therapy compared to fusion surgery
3. Clinical outcomes showed equivalent improvements following either intervention
4. Clinical outcomes showed equivalent worsening following either intervention
5. Clinical outcomes were unchanged following either intervention

## Correct option

**3. Clinical outcomes showed equivalent improvements following either intervention**

## Answer explanation

In a patient with a history of at least one year of low back pain and degenerative disc disease, both surgery and cognitive or exercise therapy show equivalent improvements in outcomes scores.

Patients with isolated chronic low back pain and degenerative disc disease remain challenging to treat. Surgical procedures and/or lumbar fusion remain relatively controversial as outcomes are variable and unpredictable. For these reasons interest exists for interventions with lower risk profile including guided physiotherapy programs and cognitive therapy. Randomized studies have shown similar short (1 year) and long-term (10 year) improvements in Oswestry Disability Index (ODI).

*Mannion et al. randomized 473 patients with chronic low back pain for 1 year to either surgery or cognitive behavioral therapy and exercise.* **They found** no statistically or clinically significant difference in ODI scores when comparing the two groups at an average of 11 years of follow up. **They conclude** that given no deterioration of symptoms in the nonoperative group and the inherent risk of surgery, multidisciplinary cognitive-behavioral and exercise programs should be considered.

*Brox et al. randomized 64 patients with L4/5 or L5/S1 degenerative disc disease and >1 year of back pain to either surgery or cognitive therapy and exercise.* At 1 year, **they found** both groups had improvements in ODI score with no statistical difference identified. **They concluded** that both treatment modalities showed equal improvements in patient reported scoring.

Figure A shows an sagittal MRI of the lumbar spine with degeneration of the disc at the L5-S1 level.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1 and 2: Both interventions of surgical fusion or cognitive/exercise therapy show equivalent improvements in ODI.
Answers 4 and 5: Randomized studies have shown outcomes improve equally following either treatment.
