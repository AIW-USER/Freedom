## Question

A 20-year-old patient undergoes anterior cruciate ligament (ACL) reconstruction. Figure A is a picture taken with a 30° arthroscope and the knee in 90° of flexion, showing the native femoral attachment site of the ACL after the remnant ACL was debrided. Which of the following correctly identified the structures labelled in Figure A?

![[q210093-a-acl.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. A = bifurcate prominence, B = medial intercondylar ridge
2. A = bifurcate ridge, B = lateral intercondylar ridge
3. A = lateral intercondylar ridge, B = bifurcate ridge
4. A = lateral intercondylar ridge, B = resident’s ridge
5. A = medial intercondylar ridge, B = bifurcate prominence

## Correct option

**3. A = lateral intercondylar ridge, B = bifurcate ridge**

## Answer explanation

The anatomy of the native anterior cruciate ligament (ACL) femoral attachment is characterized by two osseous ridges: the lateral intercondylar ridge (A), defining the anterior edge of the ACL, and the bifurcate ridge (B), separating the anteromedial (AM) and posterolateral (PL) bundle attachments.

Inaccurate tunnel placement is a common cause of ACL reconstruction (ACLR) failure. Soft tissue and osseous landmarks can be utilized to guide anatomic tunnel position. The lateral intercondylar ridge (LIR), also called “resident’s ridge”, runs proximo-distal along the medial wall of the lateral femoral condyle (LFC) and is a reliable landmark demarcating the anterior extent of the ACL femoral insertion. The bifurcate ridge (BR) runs antero-posterior, roughly perpendicular to the LIR, defining the separate AM and PL bundle attachments.

*Dhawan et al. reviewed a variety of surgical techniques for anatomic tunnel placement in ACLR, including use of anatomic landmarks, intraoperative fluoroscopy and a malleable ruler device.* Using arthroscopic landmarks for single-bundle ACLR, the center of the femoral tunnel should be placed posterior to the LIR, 8mm anterior to the posterior articular margin and 1.7mm proximal to the BR. If the BR is not visible, the ruler device can be used to determine the point that is ~50% of the distance proximo-distal (usually ~10mm proximal to the inferior articular margin).

*Ferretti et al. studied the anatomy of the ACL femoral attachment in both cadaveric specimens and patients undergoing ACLR.* *Arthroscopic and cadaveric evaluation found the LIR was present in all knees and runs proximo-distal along the anterior edge of the ACL footprint, terminating at the articular margin.* The BR, though not present in all knees, extends perpendicularly from the LIR and runs antero-posterior, separating the AM and PL femoral attachments.

*Markatos et al. reviewed the importance of anatomic ACLR.* Grafts placed near the center of the native ACL femoral attachment tend to reproduce the oblique orientation of the ACL and better restore normal knee kinematics compared to anterior femoral tunnels with vertically-oriented grafts. Anteriorly placed grafts may also lead to notch impingement and graft stretching. The transtibial technique of femoral tunnel placement may not permit centering of the graft near the native ACL insertion due to constraints of the established tibial tunnel. Independent drilling of the femoral tunnel allows for more accurate and reproducible anatomic tunnel placement.

Figure A is an arthroscopic picture with the knee in 90° flexion showing the LIR (A) and the BR (B). Illustration A is graphic depicting the orientation of the LIR, running proximo-distal coursing slightly posterior, and the BR, running antero-posterior.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1 + 5: The medial intercondylar ridge and bifurcate prominence are osseous landmarks located on the lateral wall of the medial femoral condyle at the femoral attachment of the posterior cruciate ligament.
Answer 2: A is the lateral intercondylar ridge and B is the bifurcate ridge.
Answer 4: The lateral intercondylar ridge is also called resident’s ridge.
