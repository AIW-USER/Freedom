## Question

An infant with an Ortolani positive hip has been treated in a Pavlik harness for 3 weeks, but his hip remains Ortolani positive. Attempting the use of a semi-rigid abduction brace is associated with:

## Options

1. Potential to achieve a stable hip without need for surgical intervention
2. Erosion of the posterolateral acetabulum
3. Femoral nerve palsy
4. A higher risk of femoral head osteonecrosis than open reduction
5. Requiring general anesthesia is the operating room for application

## Correct option

**1. Potential to achieve a stable hip without need for surgical intervention**

## Answer explanation

A trial of semi-rigid abduction bracing has been shown in some patients to achieve a reduced and stable hip in infants who have failed Pavlik harness treatment.

For an infant born with a dislocated but reducible (Ortolani positive) hip the first step is placement of a Pavlik harness. The harness should be worn for 23 hours per day and the hip position is monitored with serial ultrasounds. If Pavlik harness treatment has failed to achieve a reduced hip after 3-4 weeks, the next step is to place a semi-rigid abduction brace in effort to achieve reduction. If the semi-rigid abduction brace fails, then closed vs open reduction with spica cast in the operating room can be planned.

Swaroop and Mubarak present a study using a protocol of initial Pavlik harness treatment with weekly ultrasound assessment for 3 weeks. If the hip remained unstable but reducible, then semi-rigid abduction bracing with continued weekly ultrasounds were done for an additional 2-3 weeks. This treatment protocol had a 93% success rate, which compared favorably to protocols not including the abduction brace. Reduction was achieved with the Pavlik harness alone in 39 (87%) of the 44 hips. In the 3 hips requiring transition to a hip abduction brace, reduction was achieved in 2 (67%).

*Guille et al. provide a review of the pathoanatomy, natural history, treatments, and outcomes of infants with developmental dysplasia of the hip.* *They describe using a Pavlik harness as initial treatment, and if this fails moving straight to closed vs. open reduction and spica casting.* *This review was written before several studies describing the use of a semi-rigid abduction orthosis as an intermediate step that has since decreased the need for operative intervention.*

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Erosion of the posterolateral acetabulum, or "Pavlik Harness Disease" is associated with prolonged use of a Pavlik harness in a persistently dislocated hip.
Answer 3: Femoral nerve palsy is seen during Pavlik harness use with excessive hip flexion (beyond 90° - 100°).
Answer 4: Femoral head osteonecrosis is a potential outcome for any infant with DDH regardless of treatment. Use of a semi-rigid abduction brace is not known to increase this risk. Known risks include excessive abduction beyond the "safe zone", or with repeat surgical interventions.
Answer 5: The abduction orthosis may be applied in the office and does not require anesthesia.
