## Answer explanation

The injury depicted in Figure A represents a sagittal band rupture and correct initial treatment involves extension splinting or yoke splinting (Figure E).

Sagittal band ruptures are most common in the middle finger and are commonly found in boxers (acute/subacute ruptures) and patients with Rheumatoid Arthritis (chronic ruptures). The radial sagittal band is most commonly involved. In a more acute/subacute rupture, the injury results in an inability to extend the finger from a flexed position, but it can be held extended if passively placed. Initial treatment for acute/subacute injuries involves extension or yoke splinting with the MCP in extension and allowing the IP joints to move. Operative treatment consists of repair or reconstruction for chronic injuries or the professional athlete.

*Matzon and Bozentka reviewed the pertinent anatomy, evaluation, and classification and treatment of injuries of the extensor tendons of the hand and forearm.* In summary, sagittal band injuries involve Zone V of the extensor tendon. **They state** that acute injuries that are less than 6 weeks old can be treated with extension splinting for 8 weeks with the MCP joint in 25-35 degrees of hyperextension. Chronic injuries are treated with either repair or reconstruction. Finally, they reviewed the rehabilitation following extensor tendon injuries.

*Lin and Strauch reviewed the anatomy, clinical presentation, and treatment of common extensor tendon injuries.* *They discussed Mallet, Boutonniere and Sagittal Band injuries.* In regards to sagittal band injuries **they state** that those with tendon subluxation diagnosed within 3-4 weeks should be treated with MCP flexion blocking splint for 8 weeks and chronic injuries or acute injuries that fail conservative management require surgery.

Figure A is a clinical photo that depicts extensor tendon subluxation of the middle finger MCP joint.
Figure B is a clinical photo that depicts a volar based finger splint.
Figure C is a clinical photo that depicts buddy taping.
Figure D is a clinical photo that depicts a dorsal based blocking splint.
Figure E is a clinical photo that depicts a Yoke Splint, which can also be referred to as MCP flexion block splint or MCP extension splint.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: This is a volar based finger splint and is not used in the initial treatment of acute sagittal band ruptures. This splint can be used with phalanx fractures or volar phalanx dislocations.
Answer 2: This is buddy taping and is not indicated in the initial management of acute sagittal band ruptures. It is commonly used for phalanx fractures, simple stable phalanx dislocations or conservative management of collateral ligament injuries.
Answer 3: This is a dorsal blocking splint and is not indicated for acute sagittal band ruptures. It is commonly used with phalanx fractures or dorsal phalanx dislocations.
Answer 5: This is an intrinsic plus splint and is not indicated for acute sagittal band ruptures. This splint is commonly used following phalanx and metacarpal fractures.
