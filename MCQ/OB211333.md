## Question

A 53-year-old male with a history of coronary artery disease presents with progressively worsening back pain associated with fevers and chills. On physical examination, there are no sensory or motor changes with stable vital signs. Labs are significant for C-reactive protein of 80 mg/L, ESR of 72 mm/hr, white blood cell count of 11500 cells/mL, and blood cultures positive for methicillin-susceptible Staphylococcus aureus (MSSA). ## Options

1. Discharge home with oral linezolid for 6 weeks
2. Admission and bracing with IV cefazolin for 6 weeks
3. Lumbar laminectomy, followed by IV vancomycin for 6 weeks
4. Anterior lumbar interbody fusion, followed by IV vancomycin for 6 weeks
5. Lumber laminectomy with instrumented fusion, followed by IV vancomycin for 6 weeks

## Correct option

**2. Admission and bracing with IV cefazolin for 6 weeks**

## Answer explanation

The patient is presenting with a spinal epidural abscess that is neurologically intact with few risk factors for treatment failure. In the setting of a normal neurological examination and lack of sepsis nonoperative treatment with symptomatic care and appropriate IV antibiotic therapy is the preferred treatment option.

Spinal epidural abscess is an infection of the epidural space resulting in granulation tissue or pus, which may lead to spinal cord compression symptoms. The mainstay of treatment consists of IV antibiotics in patients with normal neurological examinations. Medical treatment failure has been associated with patients >65 years of age, MRSA infection, diabetes, positive blood cultures, neurologic deficits, CRP > 115 mg/L, and WBC count 12 k/mL. However, the presentation with neurologic changes has been the most strongly associated with medical treatment failure. After initiating antibiotics and a good response has been observed, outpatient IV antibiotic therapy can be arranged for 6 weeks.

*Baker et al. performed a retrospective study of 39 patients that were treated for a spinal epidural abscess.* **The authors reported** that 20 patients presented with acute symptoms, of which the 5 that did not undergo surgical intervention died related to complications of the infection. They described the progression of symptoms from spinal ache, to root pain, to weakness, and finally followed by paralysis. **They concluded** that patients presenting with back pain and fever must be aggressively worked up for this diagnosis due to the severe complications.

*Kim et al. performed a retrospective study of 142 patients that presented with a spinal epidural abscess treated nonoperatively.* **The authors reported** that 42 patients required surgical treatment due to poor response to medical management, whereas 87 patients were successfully treated. Multivariate analysis revealed that neurological deficits are the most significant risk factor for medical treatment failure and age >65 years, diabetes, and methicillin-resistant staphylococcus aureus being independent risk factors for treatment failure. **The authors concluded** that patients with these risk factors have a high risk for medical treatment failure.
