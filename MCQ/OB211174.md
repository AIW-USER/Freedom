## Question

Figure A is the radiograph of an otherwise healthy 33-year-old female soccer player with a history of hip dysplasia. She reports 6 weeks of left groin pain that has not improved with physical therapy. She endorses snapping and clicking in the left hip with certain movements. On exam, she has pain if the hip is brought from a flexed, externally rotated and abducted position to a position of extension, internal rotation, and adduction. An intraarticular steroid injection temporarily improved her symptoms. What is the most likely diagnosis?

![[q211174-a-l-hip-dysplasia-orthobullets.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Labral tear
2. Femoral neck stress fracture
3. Trochanteric bursitis
4. Avascular necrosis of the femoral head
5. Sacroiliitis

## Correct option

**1. Labral tear**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a 6-week history of mechanical hip pain with a history of dysplasia. The provocative maneuver of taking the hip from a flexed, externally rotated and abducted position to that of extension, internal rotation and adduction cause a painful catching sensation in patients with anterior labral tears.

Hip labral tears are most commonly seen in patients with a history of hip dysplasia. In this scenario the labrum is often redundant and floppy, leaving it more susceptible to tearing. Patients often have mechanical hip pain or a snapping sensation within the hip. The provocative test of taking the hip from a flexed, externally rotated, and abducted position to extension, internal rotation, and adduction is that of an anterior labral tear. Intraarticular steroid injections can be both diagnostic and therapeutic, but ultimately hip arthroscopy with labral debridement or repair may be required for those who fail conservative measures. Illustration A is the MRI of a patient with an anterior labral tear of the left hip.

*Bogunovic et al review the demographics, etiology of failure, and type of revision surgery for 58 failed hip arthroscopies.* **They report** that patients with revision preservation procedures were younger, had a lower BMI and a lower Tönnis grade compared to those patients that underwent replacement. The most common causes of failure were residual femoroacetabular impingement (68%) and acetabular dysplasia (24%). **They conclude** that unaddressed structural deformity and underlying osteoarthritis are the most common causes of failed hip arthroscopy.

*Clohisy et al review the recommendations of the ANCHOR (Academic Network of Conservational Hip Outcomes Research) study group regarding the radiographic evaluation of the adult hip.* The report that each of the following views: AP pelvis, cross-table lateral, a 45° and 90° Dunn view, frog-leg lateral, and false profile views all provide discrete information regarding the structural anatomy of the hip and should be considered when evaluating patients for hip pathology. **They conclude** that while radiographic evaluation is important it should always be paired with a detailed history and physical exam.

Figure A is the AP pelvis radiograph demonstrating acetabular dysplasia of the left hip.
Illustration A is the axial MRI of a patient with an anterior labral tear.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: While a femoral neck stress fracture would cause groin pain, these patients do not typically have mechanical symptoms, nor is it more common in the setting of hip dysplasia.
Answer 3: Trochanteric bursitis presents with isolated tenderness over the greater trochanter, not groin pain.
Answer 4: This patient does not have any risk factors or radiographs findings consistent with AVN.
Answer 5: While sacroiliitis may cause referred pain to the groin, this patient’s pain was improved with an intraarticular steroid injection, confirming intraarticular pathology.
