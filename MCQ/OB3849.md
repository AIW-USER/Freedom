## Question

A 48-year-old female presents with a posterior ankle mass. She reports discomfort with certain footware. On physical exam percussion of the mass leads to a radiating pain into the foot. Figure A shows a sagittal T1 MRI of the ankle. Which of the following is true about the diagnosis in this patient?

![[q3849-a-tibial-nerve-schwannoma.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Associated with inactivation of the NF-1 gene
2. Has verocay bodies on histology
3. Has a mucin filled synovial cell lined sac
4. May transform into malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumor
5. Risk of recurrence is high after excision

## Correct option

**2. Has verocay bodies on histology**

## Answer explanation

The clinical presentation is consistent with a neurilemmoma (schwannoma) of the tibial nerve. Verocay bodies on histology are a characteristic finding in a neurilemmoma.

Neurilemmoma is a well-encapsulated solitary tumor of nerve sheath origin that presents as a slow-growing mass from which pain radiates distally if the mass is compressed or percussed. It is sometimes associated with the NF-2 gene. The tumor spreads out nerve fibers over its surface and can be excised intracapsularly with a low chance of recurrence. Verocay bodies, which are composed of two rows of aligned nuclei in a palisading formation, are pathognomonic.

*Bos et al. performed a review of foot masses and tumors.* **They report** that Loupe magnification and careful dissection may enhance removal of the tumor from the uninvolved normal nerve fibers with sparing of the nerve. **They also report** that neurofibromas present with local pain with compression and rarely with radiating pain. The neurofibroma arises from within the nerve and disrupts distal function. Excision of the neurofibroma can be expected to cause loss of function distally and malignant degeneration is possible.

*Kakkar et al. performed a review of the MRI appearance of neurogenic tumors.* **They report** that neurofibromas are characterized by a fusiform enlargement of the nerve with the tapered ends of the lesion towards the parent nerve. Neurofibromas may also have the target sign which is seen on axial images as a central area of hypointensity with a peripheral hyperintensity. They are located in the middle of the parent nerve and are inseparable from it. In contrast, schwannomas are located eccentrically in relation to the nerve.

Figure A is an MRI demonstrating a schwannoma of the tibial nerve. Illustration A demonstrates the appearance of verocay bodies, while Illustration B is a histology slide demonstrating the same. Illustration C is a T2 MRI demonstrating the target sign.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: An inactivation of the NF-1 gene causes neurofibromatosis. Neurilemmomas have been associated with the NF-2 gene.
Answer 3: Ganglion cyst histology is characterized by a mucin filled synovial cell lined sac. Ganglion cysts are most commonly located over joints or a tendon sheath.
Answer 4: Neurofibromas may transform into malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumors; a malignant transformation of neurilemmomas is exceedingly rare.
Answer 5: Neurofibromas have a high risk of recurrence after excision while neurilemmomas have a low risk of recurrence after excision.
