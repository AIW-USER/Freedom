## Question

You are a sports medicine physician who is covering a high school football game. One of your players sustains a hit to his knee and is down on the field. His knee appears deformed and feels grossly unstable on examination, so he is transported to the nearby trauma center. A knee dislocation is confirmed on radiographs and closed-reduction is performed. MRI performed after reduction demonstrates a KDIIIL type knee dislocation. Follow up examination demonstrates a neurovascular deficit. Which of the following is most likely to be seen on this patient's examination?

## Options

1. Numbness of plantar foot
2. Numbness of dorsolateral ankle
3. Weakness of ankle dorsiflexion
4. Weakness of ankle plantarflexion
5. Weakness of great toe plantarflexion

## Correct option

**3. Weakness of ankle dorsiflexion**

## Answer explanation

This patient sustained a knee dislocation with injury to the peroneal nerve, which may lead to weakness of ankle dorsiflexion.

Knee dislocations in young, active individuals are typically high-energy injuries that cause injury to the ligamentous structures of the knee. *Schenck classified these injuries based on the ligamentous structures that are injured, with KD III injuries involving the ACL, PCL and either the MCL (KDIIIM) or the LCL/PLC (KDIIIL).* Neurovascular injuries are also commonly associated with knee dislocations, as these structures track intimately about the tibia and femur. Peroneal nerve injury, specifically, occurs in about 25% of knee dislocations, most commonly in KDIIIL patterns. Injury to the peroneal nerve can lead to lack of ankle dorsiflexion (foot drop) and numbness over the dorsum of the foot.

*Ockuly et al. reviewed the evaluation and classification of knee dislocations.* **They note** that these patients present in wide spectrum, from overt dislocation to those who may have spontaneously reduced in the field. Algorithmic approach is important to achieving best outcomes, and this includes urgent reduction and attention to the neurovascular status of the extremity prior to definitive fixation.

*Van den Bergh et al. reviewed the anatomy of the peroneal nerve with regards to both normal and pathologic MRI findings.* *They make note of several anatomic features that can predispose to peroneal neuropathy about the knee, which include: a paucity of epineural tissue, biceps femoris tunnel, bifurcation level of the nerve, superficial course around the fibula, fibular tunnel, and additional nerve branches.* *They also discuss the pathologic conditions that are associated with nerve compression or damage and the result of these lesions.*

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Numbness of the plantar foot wound indicate injury to the tibial nerve and/or it's branches, the lateral/medial plantar nerves.
Answer 2: Numbness of the dorsolateral ankle would be caused by an injury to the sural nerve which is independent of peroneal nerve function.
Answer 4: Ankle plantarflexion is controlled by the gastrocsoleus complex, which is innervated by the tibial nerve.
Answer 5: Great toe plantarflexion is controlled by the flexor hallicus longus (FHL), which is innervated by the tibial nerve.
