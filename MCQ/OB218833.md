## Question

A 92-year-old female sustains a displaced femoral neck fracture after a ground-level fall. When treating these injuries in this patient population, which of the following variables has been associated with improved mobilization in the short and medium-term post-operative period?

## Options

1. Use of general anesthesia
2. Open reduction and internal fixation
3. Cemented hemiarthroplasty
4. Preoperative cardiac work-up
5. Direct lateral approach

## Correct option

**3. Cemented hemiarthroplasty**

## Answer explanation

The use of cemented hemiarthroplasty is associated with improved short- and medium-term mobility for the treatment of displaced femoral neck fractures in elderly patients.

Femoral neck fractures are common injuries in elderly patients that are often treated with arthroplasty due to the high revision rates associated with internal fixation. Hemiarthroplasty is the preferred treatment in this patient population unless that patient has pre-existing hip arthritis or higher activity levels. The use of cement fixation has been associated with decreased fracture and revision rates in some studies. Other research has found cement fixation provides better mobility and pain control than cementless fixation.

*Parker et al. performed a prospective, randomized controlled trial of 400 patients with displaced intracapsular hip fractures, comparing outcomes of treatment with cemented Thompson hemiarthroplasty versus an uncemented Austin-Moore prosthesis.* **They reported** that the patients treated with a cemented prosthesis had less residual pain and better mobility than the uncemented group, but no significant differences were found in terms of mortality, complications, re-operations, or post-operative medical complications. **They concluded** the use of a cemented Thompson hemiarthroplasty was more effective in reducing pain and improving mobility with no increase in complications.

*Taylor et al. performed a randomized controlled trial comparing the outcomes of elderly patients (70 years or older) with displaced femoral neck fractures treated with hemiarthroplasty using a cemented Exeter implant vs. an uncemented Zweymüller Alloclassic implant.* **They reported** that the visual analog pain score at rest did not differ significantly between the groups, but the uncemented group had a higher number of complications, including subsidence and fractures, compared to the cemented group. **They concluded** the mortality rate and quality of life measures did not differ significantly between the groups, but the cemented group showed trends toward better mobility and function.

Incorrect answers
Answer 1: The use of general anesthesia is associated with increased delirium in the elderly patient population.
Answer 2: Open reduction and internal fixation of displaced femoral neck fractures in the geriatric population is associated with a high failure and revision rate.
Answer 4: Preoperative cardiac workup, including echocardiogram, is not associated with improved outcomes and often delays surgery. Surgical delays have significant consequences on patient outcomes as it delays patient mobility and recovery.
Answer 5: The direct lateral approach for hemiarthroplasty is not associated with improved mobility. However, there is some consideration that it may provide lower dislocation rates in elderly patients that cannot adhere to weight bearing precautions.
