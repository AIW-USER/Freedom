## Question

A 62-year-old male presents to your office with complaints of long-standing ankle pain. He has a history of a talar body fracture that was treated nonoperatively 10 years ago. He is being considered for either a total ankle arthroplasty (TAA) or ankle arthrodesis. Which of the following is true regarding these treatment modalities?

## Options

1. Arthrodesis is likely to result in a loss in gait function 12 months after surgery
2. Arthrodesis is unlikely to demonstrate improved performance walking on uneven surfaces 12 months after surgery
3. Subtalar arthritis is more common following TAA than arthrodesis on long-term follow-up
4. TAA is better indicated in the presence of talar osteonecrosis
5. TAA is likely to result in greater outcome scores when walking uphill

## Correct option

**5. TAA is likely to result in greater outcome scores when walking uphill**

## Answer explanation

TAA patients often have higher scores than the ankle arthrodesis patients walking upstairs, downstairs, and uphill.

Ankle arthritis is a degenerative joint disease of the tibiotalar joint that can be broken into three main types: osteoarthritis, post-traumatic arthritis and inflammatory arthritis. Diagnosis is primarily made with plain radiographs of the ankle. Treatment can be nonoperative or operative depending on patient age, patient activity demands, severity of arthritis, and presence of tibiotalar deformity. Indications for ankle arthrodesis include posttraumatic or inflammatory arthritis, malalignment (with osteotomy), and/or a young, high demand laborer. Indications for a TAA include posttraumatic or inflammatory arthritis, and/or elderly patient. Contraindications include an uncorrectable deformity, severe osteoporosis, talus osteonecrosis, Charcot joint, ankle instability, obesity, and young laborers which increase the risk of failure and revision

*Jastifer et al. reviewed TAA and ankle arthrodesis for the treatment of ankle arthritis.* **They reported** that patients with TAA and ankle arthrodesis had improved performance walking on uneven surfaces at 12 months of follow-up compared to preoperatively. **They concluded** that TAA patients had higher scores than the ankle arthrodesis patients walking upstairs, downstairs, and uphill.

*Hahn et al. compared pre-surgical and post-surgical gait measures in patients undergoing either TAA or ankle arthrodesis.* **They reported** pain reduction and improved gait function 12 months after surgery for both treatments. **They concluded** that TAA appears to regain more natural ankle joint function, with increased ROM.

*Coester et al. reviewed ankle arthrodesis for the treatment of end-stage ankle arthritis.* **They reported** that at a mean of twenty-two years, the majority of the patients had substantial, and accelerated, arthritic changes in the ipsilateral foot but not the knee. **They concluded** that although ankle arthrodesis may provide good early relief of pain, it is associated with premature deterioration of other joints of the foot and eventual arthritis, pain, and dysfunction.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Ankle arthrodesis is likely to result in improved gait function 12 months after surgery
Answer 2: TAA and ankle arthrodesis have improved performance walking on uneven surfaces at 12 months of follow-up compared to preoperatively
Answer 3: Long term studies demonstrate subtalar arthrosis
Answer 4: Talar osteonecrosis is a contraindication to TAA
