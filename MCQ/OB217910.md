## Question

A 17-year-old high school baseball pitcher presents to your office with concerns of increased right elbow pain and recent decrease in velocity. His elbow pain is located primarily on the medial side of the elbow. On examination, he has pain and laxity with valgus stress and a slight flexion contracture. He is neurovascularly intact and has no numbness or tingling while throwing. There are no signs of ulnar nerve subluxation on exam. An MRI is obtained and findings are consistent with his history and physical examination. Which of the following is true of the surgical management of this patient's condition?

## Options

1. Gracilis autograft decreases postoperative laxity compared to palmaris longus autograft
2. Docking technique is associated with lower patient reported outcomes than other techniques
3. Ulnar nerve transposition at the time of surgery is not routinely indicated
4. Mid-substance tearing of the injured structure is indicated for primary repair
5. Flexor pronator mass detachment has equivalent outcomes compared to muscle-splitting approaches

## Correct option

**3. Ulnar nerve transposition at the time of surgery is not routinely indicated**

## Answer explanation

This patient has an ulnar collateral ligament (UCL) tear. In cases without evidence of preoperative ulnar nerve symptoms/subluxation, routine ulnar nerve transposition is not routinely indicated.

UCL tears are becoming increasingly appreciated in high school, collegiate, and professional baseball players as well as other overhead throwers. When identified, patients are often indicated for UCL reconstruction. The primary goal of the procedure is to repair/reconstruct the anterior band of the UCL with the hope of returning these athletes to play at a similar level of performance. UCL repair is limited mostly to avulsion tears, rather than those mid-substance tears. Other controversies have included the graft and repair technique with the humeral docking technique having been demonstrated as superior with regards to patient outcomes and return to sport. Historically, the ulnar nerve was routinely transposed in all UCL reconstructions, but this is falling out of favor as a higher rate of ulnar neuropathy has been appreciated postoperatively.

*Vitale et al. performed a systematic review of UCL reconstruction in overhead athletes.* **They noted** that 83% of patients had an excellent results, while there was a 10% rate of complication with the most common complication being ulnar neuropathy in 6%. **They also noted** that muscle splitting approaches and abandonment of routine ulnar nerve transposition favorably affected patient outcomes.

*Marshall et al. evaluated graft type, UCL reconstruction technique, and tear characteristics on MLB pitching performance post-operatively.* **They noted** overall return to play was 96%, with 82% returning to MLB play. **They showed** that those who were reconstructed with palmaris grafts compared to gracilis grafts played longer after surgery and returned to play more consistently.

*Chang et al. performed a comparison of surgical techniques for UCL reconstruction.* **They recommended** the use of a docking technique through muscle-splitting approach rather than flexor pronator mass detachment. **They also recommended** minimal handling of the ulnar nerve, consistent with the abandonment of routine ulnar nerve transposition.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Both gracilis and palmaris autografts have been shown to have high degrees of success in UCL reconstruction. There is some suggestion that palmaris longus reconstructions may have a higher rate of return to high level sports, however.
Answer 2: The docking technique is preferred and has demonstrated superior outcomes compared to the figure-8 and Jobe techniques for UCL reconstruction.
Answer 4: Mid-substance tears are not indicated for UCL repair; rather, repair is best suited for avulsion-type injuries.
Answer 5: A muscle-splitting approach through the flexor-pronator mass is recommended compared to complete detachment of flexor-pronator mass, as this improves outcome and post-operative rehabilitation protocols.
