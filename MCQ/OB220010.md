## Question

A 46-year-old avid cyclist presents to the orthopedic clinic with a chief complaint of numbness over the small and ringer finger. The patient reports that he initially felt numb in the small and ring fingers only while doing long bike rides on the weekends. However, his sensation has diminished over the past few months even while doing daily activities. On physical exam, he has 5/5 strength with index finger abduction. His 2-point discrimination measures 10mm over the volar small and ring finger with no change in sensation over the ulnar dorsal hand. What is the most likely combination of peripheral nerve and location of compression?

## Options

1. Ulnar nerve between the two heads of the flexor carpi ulnaris
2. Ulnar nerve at the medial intermuscular septum
3. Ulnar nerve at the palmar carpal ligament
4. Superficial branch of the ulnar nerve at the pisohamate ligament
5. Deep branch of the ulnar nerve at the hook of the hamate

## Correct option

**4. Superficial branch of the ulnar nerve at the pisohamate ligament**

## Answer explanation

This patient presents with isolated sensory changes in the small and ring finger. His physical exam is best explained by compression of the superficial branch of the ulnar nerve in zone 3 of Guyon's canal.

The location of compression dictates the physical exam findings associated with an ulnar nerve neuropathy. Compression of the ulnar nerve at Guyon's canal and distal will result in no sensory changes to the ulnar dorsal aspect of the hand because the dorsal ulnar sensory nerve branches proximal. Guyon's canal begins at the proximal extent of the transverse carpal ligament and ends at the aponeurotic arch of the hypothenar muscles. At the level of the pisohamate ligament, the ulnar nerve divides into the deep motor branch and the superficial sensory branch (zones 2 and 3, respectively). Common causes of compression in zone 3 are ulnar artery thrombosis or aneurysm. Compression in zone 3 will present with sensory changes in the small and ring finger with preservation of motor function.

*Kenney et al. published a review article regarding the physical examination of the hand.* **The authors emphasize** that a thorough examination is essential when caring for a patient with any hand condition. **They recommend** both monofilament and 2-point discrimination testing and review the distribution of paresthesia associated with different locations of ulnar nerve compression.

*Chen et al. published a review article regarding ulnar tunnel syndrome.* **The authors note** that compression at the ulnar (Guyon's) tunnel is far less common than cubital tunnel compression. Electromyography or nerve conduction studies can be used as a confirmatory study and will show decreased velocity at the site of compression. **The authors recommend** surgical ulnar tunnel release when there is a compressive lesion, motor deficit, or a failure of non-operative care.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1 and 2: Either of these sites would result in potential dorsal ulnar nerve sensory changes and intrinsic muscle weakness.
Answer 3: This would result in small and ring finger sensory changes, decreased intrinsic muscle strength, and preserved dorsal ulnar hand sensation.
Answer 5: This would result in motor weakness in the hand's intrinsic muscles.
