## Question

A 14-month-old child with a neglected, rigid clubfoot is being considered for surgical intervention after failing an initial trial of Ponseti casting. Which anatomical objective is the primary mechanical rationale for performing the 'Master Knot of Henry' release during a formal posteromedial release (PMR)?

## Options

1. To allow for the lengthening of the Tibialis Posterior tendon at its insertion into the navicular bone.
2. To provide access for a percutaneous Achilles tenotomy through a medial approach.
3. To decompress the medial plantar nerve and prevent postoperative tarsal tunnel syndrome.
4. To facilitate the reduction of the talonavicular joint by releasing the tethering of the long flexors to the spring ligament and sustentaculum tali.

## Correct option

**4. To facilitate the reduction of the talonavicular joint by releasing the tethering of the long flexors to the spring ligament and sustentaculum tali.**

## Answer explanation

The Master Knot of Henry is a fibrous convergence where the long flexors tether the midfoot; releasing it is essential to restore the relationship between the talus and the navicular.

The Master Knot of Henry is the fibrous crossroads where the Flexor Digitorum Longus (FDL) and Flexor Hallucis Longus (FHL) tendons cross and are tethered to the underlying bone and ligaments.

The Problem in Clubfoot:

In a neglected clubfoot, the midfoot is locked in a position of extreme supination and adduction. The FDL and FHL become tight "strings" on a bow. Because they are tethered at the Master Knot, they act as a singular unit that pulls the navicular and the entire forefoot medially and plantarly.

The Surgical Principle:

 - De-tethering: By releasing the Master Knot, you decouple the long flexors from their fixed point on the sustentaculum tali and the spring ligament.

 - Reduction of the Talonavicular (TN) Joint: The navicular in a clubfoot is displaced medially and often articulates with the medial side of the talar head. You cannot "slide" the navicular back to its anatomical position in front of the talus if the FDL/FHL are still anchored to it.

 - Spring Ligament Access: Releasing the knot exposes the spring ligament (calcaneonavicular ligament), which is often severely contracted and must be released to allow the navicular to rotate laterally.

Answer: 4
