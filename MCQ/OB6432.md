## Question

A 47-year-old woman with a history of Ollier's disease reports right knee pain and a mass along the lateral aspect of her right distal thigh. Figures 22a through 22c show radiographs, MRI scans, and a biopsy specimen. Assuming that staging studies reveal no evidence of metastases, what is the most appropriate treatment?

## Options

1. Chemotherapy only
2. Chemotherapy followed by wide resection
3. Wide resection only
4. Wide resection followed by radiation therapy
5. Radiation therapy only

## Correct option

**3. Wide resection only**

## Answer explanation

The patient's history, imaging studies, and histology are consistent with a diagnosis of chondrosarcoma. Most can arise from a precursor such as an enchondroma, especially in patients with enchondromatosis. As with this patient, these are often low to intermediate grade, and are treated with complete surgical excision. Chemotherapy and radiation therapy have very limited efficacy against chondrosarcoma, and are not typically used to treat low to intermediate grade varieties of this tumor.
