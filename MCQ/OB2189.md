## Question

## Options

1. Unstable, Osteochondral allograft
2. Stable, Period of modified weightbearing
3. Unstable, Arthroscopically-aided internal fixation
4. Stable, Arthroscopically-aided internal fixation
5. Unstable, Period of modified weightbearing

## Correct option

**2. Stable, Period of modified weightbearing**

## Answer explanation

This patient has history and exam consistent with a symptomatic OCD lesion. The imaging demonstrates a stable appearing OCD with a characteristic appearance on the medial femoral condyle without sclerosis or cystic changes of the underlying bone. The best course of treatment would be a period of modified weight bearing.

Osteochondritis dessicans is a common condition among the skeletally immature. The underlying cause is not clear, however, it is thought that microtrauma or other insulting processes may cause diminution of the subchondral bone leading to softening and separation of the articular cartilage. Symptoms may range from vague pain with activity to mechanical symptoms if the lesion is unstable or loose. Treatment is directed based on the stability of the lesion assessed on MRI. For stable, mildly symptomatic lesions a course of limited weight bearing and activity modification is indicated.

Cahill in 1995 reviewed treatment outcomes for OCD lesions. He noted that much of the data available at that time did not distinguish juvenile from adult OCD and treatment approaches were broad and across a large spectrum. What was evident was the effectiveness of limited/partial weight-bearing for stable lesions, and that an open physis provided an improved prognosis.

*Carey et al. published a case series and Appropriate Use Criteria to illustrate the AAOS Clinical Practice Guidelines put forth by Chambers et al in 2016.* A consensus was obtained regarding offering surgery to immature and mature patients for unstable/salvageable lesions; **they suggested** the patients should receive physical therapy and be followed closely clinically/radiographically in the post-operative period.
