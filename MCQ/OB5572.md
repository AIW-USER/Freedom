## Question

An infant child is referred to you for a femur fracture. The mother reports that the child sustained an unwitnessed fall but the actual circumstances surrounding the fall are unclear. When comparing pediatric femur fractures arising from non-accidental injuries (NAI) to accidental injuries (AI), which of the following statements is true?

## Options

1. Distal metaphyseal corner fractures are the most common fracture pattern in NAI. Mid-diaphyseal fractures are the most common fracture pattern in AI. The child sustaining NAI is likely to be older.
2. Mid-diaphyseal fractures are the most common fracture pattern in both groups. The child sustaining NAI is likely to be younger.
3. Distal metaphyseal corner fractures are the most common fracture pattern in NAI. Mid-diaphyseal fractures are the most common fracture pattern in AI. There is no age difference between groups.
4. Mid-diaphyseal fractures are the most common fracture pattern in both groups. There is no age difference between groups.
5. Mid-diaphyseal fractures are the most common fracture pattern in both groups. The child sustaining NAI is likely to be older.

## Correct option

**2. Mid-diaphyseal fractures are the most common fracture pattern in both groups. The child sustaining NAI is likely to be younger.**

## Answer explanation

Midshaft fractures are the most common pattern in both non-accidental injuries (NAI) and accidental injuries (AI). The NAI group tends to be younger.

Femur fractures in children <3 years is strongly suggestive of NAI. NAI comprises up to 35% of femur fractures <3-years-old, and between 40-90% of cases <1-year-old. A skeletal survey (CXR, skull, extremities) is necessary in children <2 years old if abuse is suspected. Lateral thoracolumbar XR is indicated for children <12 months. Head CT scan should be performed on victims <1-year-old or if neurological symptoms are present. If shaking is suspected, an ophthalmologic exam is also indicated.

*Hui et al. retrospectively reviewed femur fractures in children <3years.* The percentage of NAI was 11%. Risks for NAI include age <12months, nonambulatory, delayed presentation, unwitnessed/inconsistent mechanism of injury and other associated injuries. **They recommend** a high index of suspicion for patients <3years with femur fractures.

*Baldwin et al. retrospectively compared 70 NAI and 139 AI pediatric femur fractures .* **They found** that history suspicious for abuse, physical or radiographic evidence of prior injury, and age < 18 months were risk factors for abuse. Patients with no risk factors, 1 risk factors, 2 risk factors and 3 risk factors had 4%, 29%, 87% and 92% chance of NAI respectively.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1, 3, 4, 5: Mid-diaphyseal fractures are the most common pattern in both NAI and AI. While metaphyseal corner/bucket-handle fractures are specific for NAI, they are not common. Children involved in NAI femur fractures tend to be younger, and may even be pre-ambulatory.
