## Question

Colleagues of a 40-year-old arthroplasty surgeon have noticed worsening behavioral problems in the workplace. He has a reputation of being verbally abusive to medical students and residents, but this irritability has recently worsened. The surgical techs have also noticed he has been forgetting his procedural steps. His history is significant for playing collegiate football and suffering numerous concussions. What is the most likely diagnosis?

## Options

1. Postconcussion syndrome
2. Early-onset dementia
3. Parkinson’s disease
4. Prion disease
5. Chronic traumatic encephalopathy

## Correct option

**5. Chronic traumatic encephalopathy**

## Answer explanation

These signs and symptoms are consistent with chronic traumatic encephalopathy (CTE) as the result of repeated football-related head injuries.

Chronic traumatic encephalopathy (CTE) is a progressive neurodegenerative disorder seen in athletes participating in certain sporting activities that cause repetitive head trauma to include boxing, football, hockey, and wrestling. The incidence and prevalence of CTE are currently unclear and likely varies by sport, position, duration of exposure, and age at the time of initial or subsequent head trauma. CTE presents insidiously years or decades following recovery from the acute or post-acute effects of head trauma, usually in an individual’s midlife. The early manifestations of CTE affect behavior and are classically described by family and friends as being more irritable, angry, apathetic, or having a shorter fuse. In some cases, they may even present with cognitive difficulties, most commonly poor episodic memory and executive function. Neuropathological examination of brain tissue is the only way to confirm diagnosis as there are no consensus criteria for a clinical diagnosis.

*Gavett et al. present a comprehensive review of CTE including the diagnosis, neuropathology, preventive factors, and implications of CTE.* **They found** that the characteristic neuropathological features of CTE include extensive tau-immunoreactive inclusions throughout an atrophied cerebral cortex. Currently, research efforts are underway to identify biomarkers to detect the disease and monitor its progression with the hopes of developing therapies to slow or reverse its course.

*McKee et al. reviewed 48 cases of neuropathologically verified CTE in an athletic population.* The majority of athletes with CTE were boxers (85%) followed by football players (11%) with initial symptom development at a mean of 42.8 years. **They advocate** for guidelines for return to play after a concussion or mild TBI, as it may decrease the frequency of sports-related CTE.

Incorrect Answers
Answer 1: Postconcussion syndrome occurs when concussion symptoms of headache, lethargy, and confusion last beyond the expected recovery period. Unlike CTE this is a self-limiting disorder.
Answers 2,3,4: These disorders are not consistent with the patient’s history and clinical presentation.
