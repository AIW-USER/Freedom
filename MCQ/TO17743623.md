## Question

Which nerve innervates adductor pollicis ? (general orthopedics- anatomy , basic science and pathology)

## Options

1. Ulnar nerve
2. Radial nerve
3. Median nerve
4. Both ulnar and median nerve

## Correct option

**1. Ulnar nerve**

## Answer explanation

muscles supplied by ulnar nerve-

In the forearm- All flexors of the forearm are supplied by the median nerve except one and a half flexors supplied by ulnar nerve—the one flexor is FCU, the half flexor is medial half of the FDP.

In hand- All the thenar muscles are supplied by the median nerve except, one and a half muscles—the adductor pollicis and deep head of FPB which are supplied by ulnar nerve. Ulnar nerve innervates all the interossei muscles and 2 medial lumbricals.

Muscle supplied by median nerve-

In the forearm- Pronator teres and all the flexors except FCU and medial half of the FDP. These include the superficial flexors, viz. PL, FCR and the intermediate group, viz. FDS and the deep flexors (more specifically, these are supplied by the anterior interosseous branch given off in the proximal forearm), viz. FPL, pronator quadratus and the lateral two tendons of FDP.

In hand- All the thenar muscles (except adductor pollicis and deep head of FPB) i.e.FPB (superficial head), abductor pollicis brevis and opponens pollicis. It also supplies 2 lateral lumbricals.

Muscle supplied by radial nerve-

In the arm-

Before spiral groove: Triceps (long and medial head)

In the spiral groove: Lateral heads of triceps, anconeus

Below the spiral groove: Brachialis (only sensory supply, motor supply is from musculocutaneous nerve), BR and ECRL.

In the forearm-

The PIN supplies supinator and all six extensor compartment muscles on the dorsal aspect of the forearm, viz. abductor pollicis longus (APL), extensor pollicis brevis (EPB), extensor carpi radialis brevis (ECRB), extensor indicis proprius (EIP), EDC, extensor digiti minimi (EDM) and extensor carpi ulnaris (ECU).

Answer: 1
