## Question

A 64-year-old female presents to the clinic with a traumatic rotator cuff tear that is indicated for surgical fixation. She has a history of diabetes, hypothyroidism, and a BMI of 39 kg/m². She undergoes rotator cuff repair in the beach chair position. Intraoperatively, significant tendon retraction was encountered and the surgeon opted to convert to a mini-open procedure. Postoperatively, she complains of numbness in her anterolateral left thigh. She denies any weakness with hip flexion and does not have a foot drop. Which factor places the patient at highest risk for the complication she is experiencing?

## Options

1. Patient age over 50
2. BMI > 35 kg/m²
3. The nature of surgery performed
4. Presence of diabetes
5. Foam padding placed between the pannus and the thighs

## Correct option

**2. BMI > 35 kg/m²**

## Answer explanation

This patient is suffering from lateral femoral cutaneous nerve (LFCN) palsy as a result of beach chair positioning during shoulder arthroscopy. Of the answers listed, an elevated BMI is the greatest risk factor for the development of LFCN palsy (Answer 2).

LFCN palsy is a well-known but infrequent complication of shoulder arthroscopy in the beach chair position. As the LFCN crosses near the anterior superior iliac spine (ASIS), the nerve can be compressed resulting in postoperative numbness, tingling, or burning in the anterolateral thigh. Risk factors for LFCN palsy in the beach chair position include obesity, prolonged operative times, and inadequate padding over the ASIS. To mitigate the risks related to positioning and padding, it is recommended to ensure the hips are centered at the break of the bed with hip flexion of about 45 degrees, significant padding below the wide restraining belt, and padding between the pannus and thighs if necessary. Most cases are self-limited and may resolve in weeks to months but the symptoms can be distressing to patients.

*Holtzman et al. published a retrospective review on 397 patients who underwent shoulder arthroscopy in the beach chair position.* Overall, LFCN palsy occurred in 5.5% of patients with the majority being transient palsies. Patient weight and BMI were the two patient characteristics found to be statistically significantly associated with an increased LFCN palsy. Patients with a BMI > 30 were significantly more likely to develop LFCN palsy. Although not statistically significant, longer operative time was associated with an increased risk as well. **The authors concluded** that LFCN palsy is a fairly uncommon complication in the beach chair position but can be minimized by recognizing risk factors such as elevated BMI.

*Levy et al. published a study to expand on the findings in the Holtzman study.* *The authors of this study evaluated the effectiveness of a standardized positioning and padding protocol in reducing LFCN palsy in obese patients during shoulder arthroscopy.* *The protocol included specific patient positioning and increased padding across the thighs to avoid pressure on the ASIS.* With this implementation, **the authors found** no patients developed LFCN palsy, even in patients with BMI > 40. **The authors concluded** that standardized positioning and padding protocol can effectively reduce LFCN palsy in obese patients during shoulder arthroscopy in beach chair positioning.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Patient age is not associated with the development of LFCN palsy in patients in the beach chair position.
Answer 3: The nature of surgery (arthroplasty, arthroscopic, open, etc.) has not been shown to influence the rate of LFCN palsy.
Answer 4: The presence or absence of diabetes has not been shown in the literature to influence the rate of LFCN palsy. Diabetes was evaluated in the Holtzman study but did not have an association with LFCN palsy.
Answer 5: Placing foam padding between a patient's pannus and thighs has been shown to decrease the rate of LFCN palsy in the beach chair position. This is one of the specific recommendations in the Levy article to decrease the rate of nerve injury.
