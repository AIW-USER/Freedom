## Options

1. Intramuscular myxoma
2. Myxoid liposarcoma
3. Neurofibroma
4. Ganglion
5. Metastases

## Correct option

**1. Intramuscular myxoma**

## Answer explanation

This patient has an intramuscular myxoma.

A myxoma is a benign mesenchymal neoplasm without malignant potential and is most frequently found in 40 to 70-year-old patients, with a slight female predilection. It is composed of bland undifferentiated stellate cells with thin collagen fibers within a myxoid stroma. It is most commonly found in the thigh, upper arm, calf and buttock. MRI will often show a circumferential rind of fatty tissue adjacent to at least one of the poles of the mass that will demonstrate high signal on T1 sequences.

*Petscavage-Thomas et al. reviewed imaging and pathologic characteristics of myxomatous lesions.* **They state** that these lesions are characterized by mucoid/myxoid ECM with high water content, which results in distinctive cross-sectional imaging features (high-signal on T2 MRI, low attenuation on CT, and hypoechoic to anechoic on ultrasonography. Myxoid soft-tissue tumors may be confused with cysts. Benign myxoid lesions include intramuscular myxoma, synovial cyst, bursa, ganglion, and benign peripheral nerve sheath tumors (BPNSTs), such as neurofibroma and schwannoma. Malignant entities include myxoid liposarcoma, myxoid leiomyosarcoma, myxoid chondrosarcoma, ossifying fibromyxoid tumor, and myxofibrosarcoma.

Figure A is a sagittal T2-weighted MR image showing high-signal-intensity lesion with peripheral edema most pronounced at the poles. In contrast, sarcomas in general do not produce much peripheral edema. Figure B is a sagittal contrast-enhanced T1-weighted fat-saturated MR image showing thin septal and peripheral enhancement, which plus the intramuscular location and near-uniform high T2 signal is the key to diagnosis. Fig C is an axial T2-fat saturated image of the lesion. Figure D is a high power photomicrograph showing uniform spindle cells with small nuclei, few in number relative to the abundant pale myxoid background. See below for description of illustrations.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Illustration A is a photomicrograph showing the classic "pulmonary edema pattern" of myxoid liposarcoma, where adipocyte cytoplasm is markedly expanded and a myxoid stroma predominates. The key to differentiating the two (myxoid liposarcoma and myxoma) on MRI is the pattern of contrast enhancement: liposarcoma almost always has intense diffuse enhancement. The inset section is cystically dilated spaces lined by thin septa and containing capillary-sized vessels with adjacent smaller adipocytes. Illustration B is a T2-weighted fat-suppressed MRI image showing an intramuscular lesion (arrow) with areas of low signal intensity with fat saturation, and nonenhancement that correspond to foci of intralesional fat (arrowhead). The lipid component is as much as 10% of the total tumor volume
Answer 3: Illustration C is a photomicrograph of neurofibroma. The tumor is composed of cytologically bland ovoid and spindle cells arranged haphazardly within a myxoid stroma. Illustration D is an MRI STIR image showing fusiform heterogeneous high-signal-intensity lesion that follows the path of the neurovascular structures with an entering-and-exiting-nerve sign (arrowheads).
Answer 4: Illustration E is a photomicrograph of a ganglion showing a dense wall of compact fibrous connective tissue (arrow) surrounding a collection of myxoid material that contains scattered stellate and spindle-shaped cells. Illustration F is a sagittal T2-weighted fat-suppressed MRI of a ganglion lesion (curved arrow) with internal debris, which appears to originate at the proximal tibiofibular articulation (straight arrow).
Answer 5: The appearance is not suggestive of metastatic thyroid carcinoma. Soft tissue metastases is unusual.
