## Question

A 20-year-old man falls while skiing and complains of knee pain. When he presents to the office 2 weeks later his physical exam is normal and his pain has resolved. Radiographs are shown in Figures A & B. What is the next most appropriate step in management?

![[q207-a-nof-femur-ap-xr.jpg]]
*Figure A*

![[q207-b-nof-femur-lat-xr.jpg]]
*Figure B*

## Options

1. Reassurance and weightbearing as tolerated
2. Needle biopsy
3. Incisional biopsy
4. Excisional biopsy
5. Wide resection and reconstruction

## Correct option

**1. Reassurance and weightbearing as tolerated**

## Answer explanation

The radiographs show a bony lesion on the lateral distal femur with a well circumscribed sclerotic border and no signs of periosteal reaction, suggesting a benign lesion, likely a non-ossifying fibroma. This is an incidental finding, not related to the patient's injury and it needs no further follow-up.

*Dunham et al describe the developmental defects in the distal femoral metaphysis, as a spectrum of benign appearing incidental radiographic findings.* *Based on the imaging characteristics of these lesions, one can infer they are benign and require no further follow-up.* One common location of such defects is the site of the adductor magnus muscle insertion and likely represents either an old or acute avulsion injury of this muscle from bone.
