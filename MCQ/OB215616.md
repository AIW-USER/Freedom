## Question

You are a new attending who is working with residents. A 2nd-year resident comes to you concerned they are becoming depressed and no longer enjoying medicine. They have been working 80 hours/week consistently for the last 6 months and now dread coming to work. You are concerned they may be suffering from burnout. Which of the following is true regarding physician burnout?

## Options

1. Decreasing rates of burnout have been noted over recent years
2. Rates of burnout are similar across specialities
3. Physicians have similar work life balance satisfaction compared to the general population
4. Average rates of physician burnout are 45-55%
5. Physicians are four times as likely to suffer from burnout than the general population

## Correct option

**4. Average rates of physician burnout are 45-55%**

## Answer explanation

Rates of physician burnout have been reported from 45-55% encompassing all specialties.

Of late, burnout is increasingly recognized as a severe problem for all specialties, affecting 1 out of every 2 US physicians. Burnout is characterized by a loss of enthusiasm for work, feelings of cynicism and diminished feelings of personal accomplishment. Studies have demonstrated that burnout adversely affects the quality of patient care delivered. Work-life balance is recognized as an important component of burnout, and surgical specialties are more likely to rate their work-life balance as poor. These findings have strong implications on lifestyle and overall happiness and have correlations with drug/alcohol use, relationship difficulties and suicide.

*Shanafelt et al. reviewed burnout and work-life satisfaction, comparing US physicians to the employed general public.* *They used the Masalach Burnout Inventory,* **noting** 46% of physicians had a least one symptom of burnout, and noting that certain specialities like family medicine, internal medicine and emergency medicine were at higher risk. **They also noted** that compared to a non-physician cohort, physicians were significantly more likely to report burnout and to be unhappy with their work-life balance.

*Shanafelt et al. reviewed changes in burnout and work-life balance in physicians and general public between 2011 and 2014.* **They noted** that over the 4 year span, rates of burnout increased from 45% to 55%, without finding a similar increase in the general population. **They concluded** that, compared to the general population, physicians are twice as likely to suffer from burnout and 30% less likely to be satisfied with their work-life balance.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Physician burnout, as well as work-life balance, appear to be increasing, not decreasing in prevalence.
Answer 2: While rates of burnout are high across all specialties, certain specialties have a higher risk including emergency medicine, internal medicine, neurology, family medicine, otolaryngology, and orthopedic surgery.
Answer 3: Work-life balance has been consistently rated more poorly by physicians compared to a cohort of employed non-physicians.
Answer 5: Physicians are 1.5-2 times more likely to suffer from burnout than the general population.
