## Question

A 25-year-old man has had low back pain for the past 4 months. An AP pelvis radiograph, CT scan, MRI scan, and biopsy specimen are shown in Figures 91a through 91d. What is the most likely diagnosis?

## Options

1. Giant cell tumor
2. Chordoma
3. Osteosarcoma
4. Hemangioma
5. Rectal adenocarcinoma

## Correct option

**1. Giant cell tumor**

## Answer explanation

Imaging shows a lesion in the sacrum, with extension out of the bone. Histology shows multinucleated giant cells with deposits of hemosiderin. This is consistent with giant cell tumor. Chordoma commonly occurs in the sacrum as a midline lesion of notocord remnants, but the histology would show physaliferous cells. Osteosarcoma occasionally occurs in the sacrum, but histology would show malignant cells and osteoid. Hemangiomas in the spine are typically small, intraosseus lesions with vertical striations. Rectal adenocarcinoma would originate anterior to the sacrum and show a glandular pattern on biopsy.
