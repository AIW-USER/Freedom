## Question

You're asked to evaluate a 2-week old patient in the hospital with several lower extremity deformities. The patient has a unilateral deformity of the tibia and fibula that are apex posterior and medial. On exam, the foot has a significant dorsiflexion deformity relative to the tibia. With manipulation this is passively correctable to neutral. Which of the following most likely represents the present foot deformity?

## Options

1. Calcaneovalgus
2. Cavovarus
3. Club foot
4. Skew foot
5. Congenital vertical talus

## Correct option

**1. Calcaneovalgus**

## Answer explanation

Calcaneovalgus foot deformity is associated with posteromedial tibial bowing.

Posteromedial tibial bowing is a condition caused by intrauterine positioning. Radiographically, bowing of the tibia is always named for the apex of the deformity, thus the apex in this scenario will be posterior and medial. The mild nature of this deformity can be remembered by the mnemonic "PosteroMedial = ProbablyMild". Posteromedial tibial bowing is not surprisingly associated with other conditions thought to be caused by intrauterine positioning such as calcaneovalgus foot deformity, which is a benign soft tissue contracture of the hindfoot. Characteristically, there is passively correctable dorsiflexion of the hindfoot that can result in the dorsum of the foot contacting the anterior tibia. Treatment typically consists of passive stretching of the foot and resolution usually occurs by 3-6 months.

*Kaufman et al. review limb lengthening in congenital posteromedial bow of the tibia.* **They report** that posteromedial bowing is most commonly unilateral, not associated with any other abnormality, and accompanied by an initial calcaneovalgus deformity of the foot and decrease in ankle motion that does not improve with age. **They conclude** that of the present deformities, limb length discrepancy has the potential to cause long term issues.

*Yu et al. review residual calcaneovalgus deformity.* **They report** calcaneovlagus deformity to be a postural deformity, which is present at birth and is characterized by marked dorsiflexion and valgus position of the foot in relation to the leg. **They conclude** that in most cases, the deformity is highly responsive to conservative therapy consisting of manipulation and casting.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Cavovarus deformity is most often associated with neurologic conditions and would not be expected in an otherwise healthy patient with posteromedial tibial bowing.
Answer 3: Clubfoot deformity can be associated with intrauterine positioning, but does not have a dorsiflexion deformity at the ankle.
Answer 4: Skew foot is another congenital foot deformity resulting from first metatarsal adduction and mid foot abduction.
Answer 5: Congenital vertical talus may have a similar appearance, but is not passively correctable.
