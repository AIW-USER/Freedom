## Question

A 46-year-old male police officer complains of chronic and severe low back pain, without radiation of pain into the buttocks or legs, of three-year duration. Examination reveals posterior midline tenderness to palpation, and mid-line back pain aggravated by flexion and relieved by extension. Neurological examination is normal. Radiographs reveal no instability. A sagittal T2-weighted MRI scan is shown in Figure A. Extensive nonoperative treatment over 24 months has failed. Surgical options are discussed. Which of the following is true regarding total disk arthroplasty versus fusion in the treatment of isolated low back pain in the presence of advance single-level degenerative disc disease?

![[q5615-a-ddd.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Total disk arthroplasty has been shown to have safety and efficacy comparable to fusion at 2-year followup
2. Total disk arthroplasty has a longer hospitalization than fusion
3. Total disk arthroplasty has a greater reoperation rate than fusion
4. Total disk arthroplasty has poorer restoration of disk height than anterior lumbar interbody fusion
5. Total disk arthroplasty has greater subsidence than anterior lumbar interbody fusion

## Correct option

**1. Total disk arthroplasty has been shown to have safety and efficacy comparable to fusion at 2-year followup**

## Answer explanation

Several studies have shown total disk arthroplasty has safety and efficacy comparable to fusion at 2-year followup for isolated degenerative disc disease in select patients.

The diagnosis of discogenic pain and it's most appropraite treatment remains controversial. The traditional surgical treatment of discogenic back pain, if indicated, has been interbody fusion (posterior, anterior, or 360° fusion). Total Disc Replacement (TDA) is a motion-sparing alternative to ALIF designed to reduce the risk of adjacent-segment disease. Its primary indication is axial discogenic back pain caused by DDD without instability. Contraindications include pars defects, vertebral fracture, facet arthrosis/ankylosis, spondylolisthesis, posterior element insufficiency and previous laminectomy, scoliosis.

*Madigan et al. reviewed the treatment options for DDD.* **They state** that TDA gave better disk height restoration and less subsidence than ALIF, shorter hospitalization and lower rate of reoperations, with better 3-month and 2-year patient outcomes.

*Wei et al. performed a meta-analysis comparing TDA with fusion for DDD.* **They showed** that TDA has safety and efficacy comparable to fusion at 2-year followup. A higher percentage of TDA patients had satisfactory outcomes compared with fusion patients. **They recommend** TDA on a large scale, with interbody fusion as a salvage procedure for failed TDA.

*Sears et al. reported risk factors for adjacent segment disease in over 900 patients.* Risk factors identified were multi-level surgery, age > 60 years, and an adjacent level laminectomy. Overall, the average incidence of adjacent segment disease requiring surgery was 2.5% per year.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: TDA has a shorter hospitalization than fusion.
Answer 3: TDA has a lower rate of reoperation than fusion.
Answer 4: TDA has greater restoration of disk height than ALIF.
Answer 5: TDA has less subsidence than ALIF.
