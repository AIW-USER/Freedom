## Question

A 35-year-old male, heavy laborer presents to your office 3 months following 1st metatarsal phalangeal fusion on the right for significant and symptomatic hallux rigidus. He states that since he began weight-bearing he has progressive lateral foot pain and developed calluses on the lateral side of his foot that have become painful. During the gait examination in the office, you notice that during the terminal stance phase he is standing on the outside of his foot. On shoe examination, you notice excessive lateral sole wear. The toe is most likely fused in what position?

## Options

1. Toe is fused is dorsiflexion
2. Toe is fused in valgus
3. Toe is fused in varus
4. Toe is fused in plantarflexion
5. Toe is excessively shortened

## Correct option

**4. Toe is fused in plantarflexion**

## Answer explanation

The patient is placing excessive lateral pressure on his foot because his great toe metatarsal-phalangeal joint is fused in plantar flexion or too straight of a position.

Hallux rigidus is arthritis of the first metatarsal phalangeal joint. Arthrodesis is usually indicated in more advanced arthritis. The toe should be fused in 10-15 degrees of valgus in relation to the metatarsal shaft and 15 degrees of dorsiflexion in relation to the floor. The strongest construct is a dorsal plate with a compression screw. Fusing the toe in excessive plantarflexion (too straight) does not allow normal rollover and people compensate by avoiding the medial side of the foot and walk on the lateral side of the foot.

*Coughlin and Shurnas reviewed 110 patients who previously had either a cheilectomy or arthrodesis for symptomatic hallux rigidus.* **They found** that the cheilectomy group had significant improvement in dorsiflexion and total motion. Both the arthrodesis and cheilectomy group had a significant improvement in postoperative pain and AOFAS score and 97% had good or excellent results. **They concluded** that cheilectomy has predictable success in grades 1 and 2 cases and arthrodesis is successful in patients with grades 3 and 4.

*Lau and Daniels retrospectively reviewed 19 patients with grade 2 and 11 patients with grade 3 who underwent cheilectomy and interpositional arthroplasty, respectively.* **They found** that the cheilectomy group had higher mean AOFAS scores. However, post-operative motion, VAS pain scale, and SF-36 scores were similar. Interpositional arthroplasty was more likely to have weakness in the great toe. **They concluded** that cheilectomy is a reliable method of pain relief for moderate hallux rigidus and that interpositional arthroplasty for severe should be considered a salvage procedure with less reliable results.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: A toe fused in excessive dorsiflexion can cause pain at the tip of the toe, IP joint and over the first metatarsal head
Answer 2: A toe fused in excessive valgus can cause pressure on the second toe and lead to the formation of a soft corn or ulceration on the second toe.
Answer 3: A toe fused in varus would not create problems with rolling over the toe, but could lead to problems with shoe wear especially shoes with a narrow toe box.
Answer 5: A toe that is fused in an excessively shortened position can cause interphalangeal joint degeneration and varus of the 2nd metatarsophalangeal joint.
