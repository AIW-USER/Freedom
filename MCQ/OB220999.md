## Question

A 5-year-old female presents to the emergency room with left upper extremity pain after a fall during recess. The emergency department staff attempts a closed reduction and requests your opinion with post-reduction radiographs shown in Figures A and B. Which of the following is the next best step?

## Options

1. Casting in current alignment
2. Closed reduction and intramedullary fixation
3. Closed reduction in the operating room under anesthesia
4. Obtain dedicated radiographs of the elbow
5. Open reduction and plate fixation of ulnar shaft

## Correct option

**4. Obtain dedicated radiographs of the elbow**

## Answer explanation

This patient presents with a fracture of the ulnar shaft. In patients with ulnar shaft fractures, it is prudent to obtain dedicated radiographs of the elbow to rule out a Monteigga fracture-dislocation (answer choice 4).

Pediatric Monteggia fracture-dislocations are proximal ulna complete fractures or plastic deformation with associated dislocation of the radial head. Their peak incidence in children ranges from ages 4 to 10. These fractures are classified with the Bado classification, with type I (anterior dislocation of the radial head) having the highest incidence in children. When evaluating children with proximal ulna fractures, it is important to obtain biplanar radiographs of the entire forearm, including the wrist and the elbow. A missed Monteggia fracture-dislocation can lead to increased difficulty of treatment and long-term sequelae.

*Ramski et al conducted a multicenter retrospective study of patients who presented to two trauma centers with pediatric Monteggia fracture dislocations.* **The authors found** that when complete ulna fractures were present, nonoperative management led to a failure rate of 33 percent. **They concluded** by recommending operative management in patients with complete ulna fractures.

Dr. Donald Bae published a review article on the treatment of Monteggia injuries. During his review article, he highlights the importance of obtaining orthogonal films of the entire forearm, wrist, and elbow joints when treating fractures of the ulna.

Figures A and B demonstrate a complete fracture of the ulna with apex anterior alignment. The wrist or elbow joints are not visible.

Illustration A demonstrates a lateral view of the elbow with anterior dislocation of the radial head (Bado type 1).

Incorrect Answer Choices:
Answer choices 1,2,4,5: Prior to pursuing treatment of this patient, a dedicated radiograph of the elbow should be obtained to correctly diagnose the injury.
