## Question

A new arthroplasty-trained surgeon is setting up his first OR day in his practice. He was only exposed to a single implant company during his training but has been approached by multiple implant companies regarding their total knee arthroplasty (TKA) products. He quickly finds a plethora of polyethylene insert options. Which of the following is true regarding the different tibial insert designs?

## Options

1. The anterior-stabilized tibial bearing is designed to have an anterior lip to replace the resected ACL.
2. The dual-pivot design is a newer design that attempts to recreate a medial-pivot in early flexion and a lateral-pivot in deep flexion.
3. The medial-pivot knee is designed to allow rotatory and sliding motion at the interface of the tibial baseplate and polyethylene insert.
4. A posterior-stabilized implant aims to minimize femoral rollback during knee flexion
5. The standard cruciate retaining bearing can cause paradoxical anterior femoral translation.

## Correct option

**5. The standard cruciate retaining bearing can cause paradoxical anterior femoral translation.**

## Answer explanation

In normal knee kinematics, as the knee moves from extension to flexion, the center of rotation of the femur translates posteriorly, allowing for greater knee flexion. The standard cruciate retaining bearing can have a paradoxical anterior translation of the femur during flexion, likely due to the lack of ACL and a more posterior resting position in extension (Answer 5).

Total knee arthroplasty (TKA) bearing components are designed to replicate the normal kinematics of the native knee. In a healthy knee, six degrees of freedom occur during movement, controlled by the articular congruity of the knee and the cruciate ligaments. Notably, the femur posteriorly translates (femoral roll-back) during flexion to allow deep flexion (beyond 120°). Tibial bearing options in TKA aim to recreate the normal kinematics of the knee while ensuring long-term implant function. Many different variations of tibial bearings exist, and the ideal implant is dependent on patient demands, soft tissue balance, and surgeon preference.

*Krumme et al. published a systematic review and meta-analysis of different contemporary fixed tibial inserts in TKAs.* *The study included cruciate retaining (CR), posterior stabilized (PS), anterior-stabilized (AS), medial pivot (MP), bicruciate retaining (BR), and bicruciate stabilizing (BCS) inserts.* *The study found that PS bearings provided a statistically significant increase in knee flexion by 3 degrees compared to other bearings, however, the authors do not consider this clinically significant.* Patient satisfaction, patient-reported outcome measures (PROMs), and complications were not different amongst the different tibial bearings. In conclusion, **the authors suggest** that no particular tibial insert provides superior functional outcomes or patient satisfaction in TKA.

*Sandberg et al. published a retrospective study comparing patient outcomes with traditional non-conforming TKA bearings to dual-pivot bearings.* *The authors matched 183 dual-pivot TKAs to 183 traditional TKAs.* The motivation for the study stems from a more modern understanding of knee kinematics demonstrating an early lateral and late medial pivot pattern. Patients with dual-pivot knees were found to have less walking pain, **demonstrating** a potential benefit in early flexion activities. However, overall patient satisfaction did not differ notably between the two groups. **The authors concluded** that lateral-pivot motion in early knee flexion may benefit ambulation, but further research is needed to fully understand its impact on patient satisfaction.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Anterior-stabilized (ultra-congruent or deep-dish) tibial bearings have an elevated anterior lip to prevent the femur from rolling forward when the PCL is compromised or weak.
Answer 2: The dual-pivot bearing is based on recent kinematic studies demonstrating a more lateral-based pivot in early flexion and a medial-based pivot in deep flexion. The dual-pivot bearing replicates these kinematics by pivoting laterally in early flexion and medially in deep flexion.
Answer 3: The medial-pivot bearing is designed with a highly congruent medial compartment and a less congruent lateral compartment. A component that allows rotatory and sliding motion between the polyethylene bearing and tibial baseplate is described as a mobile bearing component.
Answer 4: Posterior-stabilized implants feature a polyethylene post that engages with the cam of the femoral component to promote femoral rollback during knee flexion, not minimize it.
