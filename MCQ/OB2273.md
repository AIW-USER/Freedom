## Question

A 9-year-old male presents to your office for evaluation of left hip and knee pain. The patient reports progressive left hip pain for the last 3 months that has gotten acutely worse in the last 24 hours. He is now unable to bear weight on his left leg, even with crutches. Radiographs are shown in Figure 1. Which of the following would NOT increase your concern that this patient will develop a contralateral slip?

![[q2273-a-559959230f391936a5d8de7c5c9eb5-jumbo.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Diagnosis <10-years-old
2. Height >95th percentile
3. Open triradiate cartilage
4. Obesity
5. Hypothyroidism

## Correct option

**2. Height >95th percentile**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a left unstable slipped capital femoral epiphysis (SCFE). Of the options listed, height >95th percentile has not been shown to increase the liklihood of a contralateral slip.

Slipped Capital Femoral Epiphysis (SCFE) occurs through the hypertrophic zone of the physis and leads to the metaphysis slipping anteriorly and externally rotating relative to the epiphysis. Treatment typically consists of percutaneous screw fixation. Following surgery, slippage of the contralateral hip is one of the most common complications. While the indications for prophylactic contralateral fixation are controversial, there are several risk factors that increase the likelihood of developing a contralateral slip including age <10-years-old, open triradiate cartilage, obesity and endocrine disorders (hypothyroidism, renal osteodystrophy, growth hormone deficiency, panhypopituitarism).

*Loder et al reviewed the epidemiology of bilateral SCFEs in children in Michigan.* *They report on 224 children without underlying metabolic or endocrine disorders and found that 37% had bilateral slips.* **They conclude** that patients who went on to have subsequent slips had a shorter duration of symptoms, were younger at the time of diagnosis and tended to be more obese.

*Aronsson et al reviewed the evaluation and management of stable SCFEs.* **They report** that stability is based on the presence or absence of gross instability between the physis and epiphysis. **They conclude** that the treatment of choice for a stable chronic slip is stabilization in situ with a single screw.

*Hurley et al reviewed the prevalence of late contralateral slips in patients with SCFE.* **They report** that 36% of patients who were treated with operative stabilization went on to have a contralateral slip. **They conclude** that patients should be followed closely after hip pinning to monitor for contralateral slips.

*Riad et al review the predictive factors for developing a contralateral slip in patients with SCFE.* **They report** that chronological age, <10 in girls and <12-years-old in boys, is a predictor of developing a contralateral slip. **They conclude** that patients with a unilateral SCFE who are under these ages should undergo strong consideration for prophylactic fixation of the contralateral side.

Figure 1 shows the AP pelvis radiograph of a patient with a left sided SCFE.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1, 3-5: Age <10-years-old, open triradiate cartilage, obesity and hypothyroidism are all risk factors for developing contralateral slips.
