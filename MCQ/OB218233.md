## Question

The pain is worse on her morning commute to work and when she exercises. Anti-inflammatories are no longer controlling her pain. ## Answer explanation

This patient has pincer type femoroacetabular impingement (FAI), which is supported by radiographic findings of a "crossover sign," which indicates acetabular retroversion.

FAI is defined generally by abnormal contact between the femur and acetabulum at the hip which can lead to varying degrees of capsulolabral, chondral and bony injury. There are two primary types of FAI: cam impingement and pincer impingement. Most often, however, these occur in combination. Pincer impingement, specifically, is more common in middle aged females and occurs when anterosuperior acetabular over coverage is present. This is further complicated by a constellation of bony findings including acetabular retroversion (as seen by the crossover sign), acetabular protrusio, and coxa profunda in some cases. These anatomic changes lead to the femoral neck impinging on the labrum more easily, especially in positions of flexion and internal rotation. This can then lead to labral degeneration and tearing, cartilage damage, and is thought to play a role in arthritic hip degeneration.

*Byrd et al. reviewed the pathoanatomy and diagnosis of athletes with FAI,* **noting** that it is a common cause of hip pathology, with ties to hip osteoarthritis. **They note** the different mechanics that cause each type of FAI to occur and review the different history and physical examination findings associated with each. **They conclude** that regardless of type, it is important that FAI is caught and treated early to avoid arthritic degeneration.

*Byrd et al. discuss arthroscopic management of FAI in athletes, which included primarily those with cam-type impingement (159 cam, 31 combined, and 10 pincer).* **They noted** a high degree of return to sport (85-95%) and a low rate of complication. **They concluded** from these findings that arthroscopic management of FAI was successful in returning high-level athletes to play with a low risk for complication.
