## Question

A 5-year-old girl with cerebral palsy presents for evaluation of spastic diplegia. She is ambulatory without the use of assistive devices and reports slow, involuntary, convoluted, writhing movements of the fingers, hands, toes, and feet. Which of the following interventions is contraindicated in this patient?

## Options

1. Botox injections
2. Selective dorsal rhizotomy
3. Upper extremity tendon transfers
4. SEMLS (Single-Event, Multi-Level Surgery)
5. Intrathecal baclofen injections

## Correct option

**2. Selective dorsal rhizotomy**

## Answer explanation

The presence of athetosis is a contraindication to selective dorsal rhizotomy. The ideal candidate for selective dorsal rhizotomy is an ambulatory patient aged 4-8 years old with spastic diplegia and no evidence of athetosis.

A selective dorsal rhizotomy is a neurosurgical procedure performed to reduce spasticity in children with cerebral palsy. It involves the selective resection of the L2-S1 dorsal nerve roots that do not show a myographic or clinical response to stimulation. The most appropriate candidate for selective dorsal rhizotomy is a patient with spastic diplegia who is between the ages of 4 to 8 years and who has a stable gait pattern that is limited by lower extremity spasticity. A selective dorsal rhizotomy is not recommended in patients with athetosis because of unpredictable results, and should also be avoided in nonambulatory patients with spastic quadriplegia, as it is associated with significant spinal deformities.

*Peacock et al. performed a study on outcomes after selective dorsal rhizotomy in 60 children with cerebral palsy.* **They found** that the patients whose function improved most dramatically following rhizotomy were purely spastic and intelligent, were significantly more affected by spasticity in the lower than the upper limbs, had some degree of forward locomotion, and could side-sit independently. **They also found** that patients with severe athetosis or marked contractures improved the least.

*Oppenheim published a review on selective dorsal rhizotomy for spastic cerebral palsy.* **He reports** that the ideal patient is an intelligent spastic diplegic with no fixed contractures, who is already ambulatory but hopes to improve the gait pattern and endurance. **He states** that contraindications to selective posterior rhizotomy include rigidity, hypotonia, dystonia, weakness, ataxia, athetosis, and prior spinal fusion.

*Mooney et al. published a study on spinal deformity after selective dorsal rhizotomy in patients with cerebral palsy.* **They found** that progressive spinal deformity may occur after selective dorsal rhizotomy with patients with spastic quadriplegia, whose primary activity level is sitting, at higher risk than patients with diplegia who are ambulatory. **They found** that other predisposing factors may include preexisting hip flexion contractures, preexisting abnormal lordosis or scoliosis, and possible athetosis.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Athetosis is not a contraindication to botox injection. Botox is a competitive inhibitor of pre-synaptic cholinergic receptors and is most effective in dynamic contractures.
Answer 3: Involuntary muscle control, not athetosis, is a relative contraindication for upper extremity tendon transfers. Tendon transfers in the upper extremity show the best improvement in function in patients with voluntary motor control.
Answer 4: Athetosis is not a contraindication to SELMS. SELMS is most successful when combined with a thorough gait lab assessment that predicts improvement in function with multiple level surgical interventions
Answer 5: Athetosis is not a contraindication to intrathecal baclofen injections. Intrathecal baclofen injections are most effective in non-ambulatory patients with CP.
