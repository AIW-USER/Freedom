## Question

Which of the following arm positions is most associated with internal impingement in the throwing athlete?

## Options

1. Adduction and internal rotation
2. Abduction and internal rotation
3. Flexion and external rotation
4. Abduction and external rotation
5. Flexion and internal rotation

## Correct option

**4. Abduction and external rotation**

## Answer explanation

Internal impingement occurs during humeral head abduction and maximal external rotation.

Internal impingement is a cause of shoulder pain in overhead athletes caused by repetitive impingement of the articular surface of the rotator cuff. This generally occurs in the late cocking phase of throwing with humeral head abduction and maximal external rotation. The specific impingement occurs between the posterior articular sided supraspinatus tendon and the posterosuperior aspect of the glenoid. This is in contrast to the more common extrinsic/subacromial impingement which occurs on the bursal side of the rotator cuff.

*Heyworth and Williams reviewed internal impingement of the shoulder.* **The authors noted** that internal impingement is a pathologic condition characterized by excessive or repetitive contact of the greater tuberosity of the humeral head with the posterosuperior aspect of the glenoid when the arm is abducted and externally rotated. **They note** that this arm positioning leads to rotator cuff and glenoid labrum impingement by the bony structures of the glenohumeral joint. **The authors concluded** that internal impingement is a disease state that affects overhead athletes and is characterized by the development of articular-sided rotator cuff tears and posterosuperior labral lesions.

*Paley et al. described the outcomes of an arthroscopic examination and pathology in symptomatic shoulders of 41 professional overhand throwing athletes.* With the arm in the position of the relocation test (abduction and external rotation), 100% of the subjects had either contact between the rotator cuff undersurface and the posterosuperior glenoid rim or osteochondral lesions. **The authors also noted** undersurface cuff fraying in 93%, posterosuperior labral fraying in 88%, and anterior labral fraying in 36% of the subjects. **The authors concluded** that internal impingement of the posterior cuff undersurface with the posterosuperior glenoid rim is a common finding in the overhand throwing athlete with shoulder pain.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1,2,3 and 5: Internal impingement most commonly occurs with humeral head abduction and maximal external rotation.
