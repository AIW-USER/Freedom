## Options

1. Giant cell tumor of bone, observation
2. Osteosarcoma, neoadjuvant chemotherapy with wide resection.
3. Aneurysmal bone cyst, wide resection with radiation therapy
4. Chondroblastoma, chemotherapy
5. Osteosarcoma, wide resection alone

## Correct option

**2. Osteosarcoma, neoadjuvant chemotherapy with wide resection.**

## Answer explanation

This patient's history, imaging, and biopsy are concerning for osteosarcoma, specifically the telangiectatic sub-type. The treatment for osteosarcoma is typically neoadjuvant chemotherapy followed by wide resection.

Telangiectatic osteosarcoma is a malignant osteogenic tumor that commonly affects the knee, proximal humerus, and femur. It is characterized by histology containing lakes of blood mixed with osteogenic malignant cells. They can appear similar to aneurysmal bone cysts and differentiation between the two is very important for both treatment and prognosis. Treatment typically neoadjuvant chemotherapy followed by wide resection and limb salvage if possible. A good prognostic factor is 98% necrosis with chemotherapy. Associated genes include Rb-1, p53, HER2/neu, c-myc, and c-fos.

*Messerschmitt et al. reviewed osteosarcoma-class tumors.* *The authors discuss all stages from presentation to treatment and prognosis for patients with osteosarcoma.* *They cover multiple sub-types to include conventional, telangiectatic, small-cell, parosteal, and periosteal.* Staging for osteosarcoma patients includes Xrays, affected extremity MRI, chest CT, and whole-body bone scan. Treatment is preoperative and postoperative chemotherapy with wide surgical resection. **They conclude** that the future of osteosarcoma treatment is in more target-selective treatments.

Lewis, Morris, and Parsons created an instructional article discussing common bone tumors. They discuss the radiographic evaluation of common benign and malignant bone tumors. For osteosarcoma, they explain that it is the most common primary malignancy of bone and often present in the 2nd decade of life. For telangiectatic osteosarcoma 25% of patients present with pathologic fractures.

*Weber et al. thoroughly discuss malignant bone tumors in their article.* This includes sections on grading, biopsy, and specific tumor types. A large portion of the article is regarding limb-salvage options for both the upper and lower extremities and the pelvis.

Figure A demonstrates a proximal tibia lytic lesion on a lateral radiograph with the destruction of the cortex. Figures B and C are T1 and T2 weighted MRI images showing an expansile heterogeneous lesion with the destruction of the cortex and expansion into the surrounding soft tissues. Figure D is a T2 weight MRI that shows an axial cut of the lesion. Figure E is a biopsy with large lakes of blood and malignant appearing cells with an osteoid matrix.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Although often presenting in the proximal tibia, giant cell tumors of bone typically present in females in the 3rd to 5th decades of life. Biopsy shows multi-nucleated giant cells. Treatment is initially with intralesional curettage and reconstruction.
Answer 3: Aneurysmal bone cysts can appear very similar to telangiectatic osteosarcoma with lakes of blood. However, ABCs will not have malignant cells on biopsy. Symptomatic ABCs are treated with curettage and bone grafting.
Answer 4: Chondroblastoma can present with an epiphyseal lytic lesion in a pediatric patient. A biopsy will show chondroblasts in a cobblestone pattern with a background of a chondroid matrix. Treatment is initially with intralesional curettage and bone grafting.
Answer 5: The treatment for osteosarcoma as discussed above is with neoadjuvant chemotherapy and wide excision.
