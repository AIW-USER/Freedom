## Question

A 34-year-old man presents to clinic with 2 months of lateral right elbow pain. He reports that it has limited his golf game which he has been playing 5 times per week recently. On examination, he is tender over the lateral aspect of the elbow and has pain with resisted wrist extension. Which of the following is true regarding the most commonly involved structure with this condition?

## Options

1. Has the largest cross-sectional area of any of the elbow flexors
2. Inserts into the dorsal base of the second metacarpal
3. Forms the lateral border of the cubital fossa
4. Innervated by fibers of the sixth and seventh cervical nerves
5. May be identified as the first fleshy fibers observed proximal to the common extensor tendon

## Correct option

**4. Innervated by fibers of the sixth and seventh cervical nerves**

## Answer explanation

The patient clinical examination is suggestive of lateral epicondylitis, which typically involves the origin of the extensor carpi radialis brevis (ECRB). The ECRB is innervated by fibers of the sixth and seventh cervical nerves of the radial nerve.

Lateral Epicondylitis (also known as tennis elbow) is an overuse injury caused by eccentric overload at the origin of the common extensor tendon, leading to tendinosis and inflammation of the ECRB. The ECRB is innervated by the deep branch of the radial nerve (fibers of the sixth and seventh cervical nerves) and inserts on the base of the 3rd metacarpal. Histological analysis typically shows vascular hyperplasia (or angiofibroblastic hyperplasia) and disorganized collagen. Diagnosis is made clinically with tenderness over the lateral epicondyle made worse with resisted wrist extension. Treatment is primarily nonoperative with NSAIDs, activity modification, bracing, and/or injections. Rarely, operative management is indicated for patients with persistent symptoms who fail nonoperative management.

*Brummel et al. reviewed the clinical presentation and management options for lateral epicondylitis.* Symptoms typically came on acutely in younger patients with more chronic symptoms in older patients. **They concluded** that NSAIDs, extensor stretching, and activity modification are the mainstay of nonsurgical treatment.

*Bunata et al. studied 85 cadaver elbows to determine anatomic factors contributing to tennis elbow.* **They reported** that the average site of origin of the ECRB on the humerus lay slightly medial and superior to the outer edge of the capitellum. **They concluded** that the ECRB tendon has a unique anatomic location that makes its undersurface vulnerable to contact and abrasion against the lateral edge of the capitellum during elbow motion.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The brachialis has the largest cross-sectional area of any of the elbow flexors
Answer 2: The extensor carpi radialis longus (ECRL) inserts into the dorsal base of the second metacarpal
Answer 3: The brachioradialis forms the lateral border of the cubital fossa. It crosses the elbow joint with the greatest mechanical advantage of any elbow flexor
Answer 5: The ECRL originates from the supracondylar bony column just below the origin of the brachioradialis and may be identified as the first fleshy fibers observed proximal to the common extensor tendon
