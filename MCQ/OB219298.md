## Question

A 53-year-old male undergoing active treatment for renal cell carcinoma presents with progressive right thigh pain that acutely worsened after stepping out of his truck earlier that morning. His presenting radiograph is shown in Figure A, and a CT scan demonstrates the lesion extending into the lateral femoral condyle. Further workup reveals he has lytic lesions within his thoracic spine, ilium, sacrum, cranium, multiple ribs, and other visceral metastases. His prior oncologic treatments include a nephrectomy eight months ago with ongoing immunotherapy. When compared to intralesional curettage and intramedullary stabilization of this lesion, treating this patient with en-bloc resection of his femoral metastatic lesion and endoprosthesic reconstruction would provide which of the following benefits?

![[q219298-a-figure-a.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Lower risk for local recurrence
2. Improved survival benefit
3. Lower implant cost
4. Decreased intraoperative blood loss
5. Higher risk for reoperation

## Correct option

**1. Lower risk for local recurrence**

## Answer explanation

There have been various studies showing that en-bloc, wide resection of skeletal metastases with reconstruction provides improved local control of bone metastases compared to intralesional curettage and internal fixation (Answer 1).

When treating impending or fractured skeletal metastatic lesions, ideally a single surgical intervention should be performed to allow unrestricted weight bearing with an implant that outlives the patient. For most of these cases, the common practice includes intralesional curettage, bone void filling with PMMA cement (if needed), and fixation with locking plates or intramedullary nails. Alternatively, skeletal metastases can be removed via en-bloc, wide resection like that of soft tissue sarcoma treatment. This has been shown to improve local tumor control and decrease the risk of local tumor recurrence. However, the benefit of lower local recurrence must be weighed against the increased morbidity and risks associated with a larger resection and reconstruction. Patients with diffuse, polymetastatic disease (as in this case) and shorter life expectancy often benefit from less invasive treatments, as they are likely to succumb to disease before requiring revision surgery from local recurrence (Illustration A). In select cases, performing wide metastasectomy with curative intent may impart improved long-term survival over standard intralesional curettage.

*Johnson and colleagues provided a comprehensive review article on the contemporary management of appendicular skeletal metastases by various carcinomas.* *They discuss some of the most common cancers and special considerations for each when managing skeletal metastases.* **The authors conclude** that surgeons must take the primary cancer type, potential responsiveness to radiation/chemotherapy, overall metastatic burden, anatomic location, patient expectations, and functional capacity into account when determining the most appropriate treatments.

*Krishnan and colleagues performed a retrospective analysis of their institutional patient cohort (n=301) who underwent surgery for bone metastases to determine potential factors associated with local recurrence.* **They reported** a local recurrence rate of 16% with a significantly lower risk for local recurrence in the group treated with en-bloc resection (5/66) compared to the group treated with intralesional curettage (44/235). Multivariate analysis showed that en-bloc negative surgical margins and breast or prostate cancers were independent factors associated with longer local recurrence-free survival rates. **The authors conclude** that the risk for local recurrence should be considered when treating skeletal metastatic lesions.

*Langerhuizen and colleagues performed a retrospective analysis of their institutional patient cohort (n=183) to assess the outcomes of various treatments for renal cell carcinoma (RCC) bony metastases.* **They found** the local recurrence rate was highest after stabilization only (39%) followed by intralesional curettage (22%) and wide metastasectomy (12%; p=0.003). However, **they report** no difference in the reoperation rate amongst any of these groups (p=0.847). When performing subgroup analysis, **the authors noted** recurrence rates were lower in patients with negative-margin, wide metastasectomy (and improved survival rates) compared to those with a positive margin (p<0.001). **The authors concluded** that, for RCC patients: (1) the risk of developing local recurrence is higher among patients undergoing intralesional curettage compared to wide metastasectomy and (2) patients with oligometastatic disease may have an improved mortality rate when treated with negative margin metastasectomy.

*Steensma and colleagues performed a retrospective analysis of their institutional patient cohort (n=298) who underwent surgical treatment for pathologic proximal femur fractures to determine outcomes in various treatment types.* **They report** that the number of treatment failures and the need for revision surgery in the endoprosthetic reconstruction group was significantly lower than in the intramedullary nailing and open reduction internal fixation groups. **The authors conclude** that endoprosthetic reconstruction is associated with greater implant durability and lower revision rates than fixation techniques for proximal femur skeletal metastases.

Figure A demonstrates a pathologic fracture of this patient’s right distal femur through a lytic renal cell carcinoma metastatic lesion. He underwent local curettage and retrograde intramedullary nailing. Illustration A demonstrates a radiograph taken three months later during a readmission where he succumbed to his disease secondary to other complications. Of note, updated radiographs at the time demonstrated aggressive local recurrence and metastatic spread to his proximal femur possibly secondary to the initial retrograde nailing procedure.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Wide resection of isolated, oligometastatic renal cell carcinoma bone metastases has been shown to improve mortality rates. However, this patient has aggressive, polymetastatic disease in which wide resection has not shown improved survival outcomes given the widespread cancer burden.
Answer 3: Although cost variations exist among differing hospital-vendor contracts, endoprosthetic implants are generally more expensive than those used for stabilization (i.e. intramedullary nails and plates). However, endoprosthetic reconstruction may offer a more durable implant with lower rates of revision, particularly in the proximal femur.
Answer 4: Performing metastasectomy with endoprosthetic reconstruction results in a pragmatic increased risk for potential morbidity (larger dissection, increased blood loss, prosthesis-related complications) as compared to limited intralesional treatment with stabilization.
Answer 5: Despite a lower risk for local recurrence with wide resection of skeletal metastases, the reoperation rates are generally equivalent to intralesional curettage for various cancer types. This is likely confounded by patients with more advanced disease (i.e. shorter life expectancy) being treated with the less morbid, intralesional curettage and succumbing to their cancer before revision would be required.
