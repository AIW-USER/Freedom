## Question

A 35-year-old waitress sustained an ankle injury 5 months ago when she slipped on a wet surface at work. She went to an urgent care center and was told she had an "ankle sprain." She was given a removable splint and subsequently discharged. The patient continues to experience pain with walking, standing, and has feelings of instability. She has tenderness to palpation over the anterolateral ankle. She has normal ankle and hindfoot motion and is stable on anterior drawer testing. Radiographs can be seen in Figures A and B. What is the next best treatment step?

## Options

1. Nonsteroidal Anti-Inflammatory Drugs (NSAIDs)
2. Observation
3. Immobilization in walking boot
4. Proprioception and peroneal strengthening
5. Operative treatment

## Correct option

**4. Proprioception and peroneal strengthening**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a chronic, untreated ankle sprain; therefore, first-line treatment would be proprioception and peroneal strengthening.

Under-treated ankle sprains can result in muscle weakness as well as chronic pain and instability. Patients with instability may describe a feeling of the ankle “giving way.” Chronic ankle instability refers to repetitive episodes of instability resulting in recurrent ankle sprains. If a patient has not been treated adequately, a structured program of functional rehabilitation is warranted focusing on proprioception and peroneal strengthening. Surgical treatment is reserved for patients who continue to have mechanical instability and have failed non-operative treatment.

*Lephart et al. in a review, define proprioceptive rehabilitation as a specialized rehabilitation that focuses on joint movement and joint position.* **They report** that after injury, a neuromuscular feedback mechanism becomes interrupted. It approaches restoration after rehabilitation is completed. Rehabilitation programs should be designed to include a proprioceptive component that addresses the following three levels of motor control: spinal reflexes, cognitive programming, and brainstem activity.

*Maffulli et al. in a review, report that chronic, untreated ankle sprains should initially undergo a structured program of functional and prophylactic rehabilitation.* *Patients who fail this modality should be considered for surgical management.*

*Van Ochten et al. in a systematic review report in patients who have complaints with chronic ankle pain and instability, a training program gives them better results in pain relief and a reduced rate of recurrent ankle sprains.* **They conclude** that this was more effective than observation.

Figures A and B demonstrate normal ankle radiographs.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: NSAIDs may be used for symptomatic control, however, are not the treatment of choice for chronic instability.
Answer 2: Observation is not appropriate in this case as the patient is symptomatic and observation is likely not going to improve her course.
Answer 3: Immobilization is used briefly after acute sprains for symptomatic control.
Answer 5: Operative treatment is reserved for cases who fail a full course of non-operative treatment.
