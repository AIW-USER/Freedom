## Answer explanation

Strict instructions to avoid adduction and internal rotation of the operative shoulder would be part of the acute postoperative protocol of a repaired posterior labrum, which is depicted in Figure C.

Posterior labral injuries are commonly observed in individuals that have repeated posteriorly-directed stress across their glenohumeral joint, like football players and bench-pressing weightlifters. The Jerk and Kim tests are specialized shoulder maneuvers that may be utilized to elicit pain and instability from posterior labral injuries. The Kim test is more sensitive for posterior-inferior labral tears and is elicited by pain with forward flexion of the shoulder to 45 degrees while simultaneously applying axial load on the elbow, and posterior-inferior force on the upper humerus. When repaired, motions that would place increased stresses across the posterior labral repair include internal rotation and adduction, and thus these motions should be avoided in the acute postoperative period.

*Kim et al. investigated the results after arthroscopic posterior capsular repair and capsular shift for traumatic unidirectional recurrent posterior shoulder instability.* **They reported** that at final follow-up, all patients had improved shoulder function and pain scores. **They concluded** that when used to treat traumatic unidirectional recurrent posterior instability, this operation is reliable with respect to providing stability, pain relief, and functional restoration.

*Kim et al. described the Kim test as a novel diagnostic test for detecting a posteroinferior labral lesion of the shoulder and compared it to the Jerk test.* *They compared the Kim test sensitivity (80%), specificity (94%), and positive predictive value (PPV) (0.73), to the Jerk test sensitivity (73%), specificity (98%), and PPV (0.88).* **They concluded** that the Kim test was more sensitive in detecting a primarily inferior labral lesion, whereas the Jerk test was more sensitive in detecting a primarily posterior labral lesion.

Figure A demonstrates a subscapularis tendon tear. Figure B demonstrates an anterior labral tear. Figure C demonstrates a posterior labral tear. Figure D demonstrates calcific tendonitis about the supraspinatus tendon without tear. Figure E demonstrates distal clavicle osteolysis. Illustration A demonstrates the Kim test.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: After subscapularis repair, external rotation would increase stress across repair site.
Answer 2: After anterior labral repair, external rotation would increase stress across repair site.
Answer 4: Given the risk of shoulder stiffness with operative treatment, postoperative instructions after arthroscopic decompression for calcific tendonitis without rotator cuff tear would not include avoidance of internal rotation and adduction.
Answer 5: Postoperative instructions after arthroscopic distal clavicle resection for distal clavicle osteolysis would not include avoidance of internal rotation and adduction.
