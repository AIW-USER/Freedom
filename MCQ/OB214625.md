## Question

A 27-year-old male professional football player presents with right hip pain for several weeks that has progressively worsened. The pain is localized in his groin and is worsened with hip flexion. The patient denies any mechanical symptoms. Physical exam reveals pain that is reproduced with hip flexion, adduction, and internal rotation. Internal rotation is limited to 20° on the right compared to 45 degrees on the left. Current imaging is demonstrated in Figures A-C. What is the most appropriate initial treatment?

## Options

1. Physical therapy to increase hip internal rotation motion
2. A period of activity modification and NSAIDs
3. Open hip dislocation with femoral neck osteoplasty and labral debridement
4. Isolated arthroscopic labral repair
5. Periacetabular osteotomy

## Correct option

**2. A period of activity modification and NSAIDs**

## Answer explanation

The patient is presenting with right hip cam-type femoroacetabular impingement (FAI) without previous treatment. The most appropriate initial treatment consists of nonoperative modalities including activity modification and NSAIDs.

FAI due to cam-deformity is the result of mechanical conflict between the anterosuperior labrum and femoral neck, resulting in chondral delimitation with chondrolabral separation. The loss of sphericity of the femoral head in cam-type impingement leads to abutment with the anterosuperior labrum with hip flexion and internal rotation. Initial treatment involves nonoperative modalities, including activity modification, physical therapy directed at strengthening, and NSAIDs. Surgical treatment is directed at removing the cam lesion and repair of chondrolabral lesions, either arthroscopically or through open surgical hip dislocation.

*Espinosa et al. performed a retrospective study of 52 patients (60 hips) undergoing either labral repair or labral resection via an open surgical hip dislocation for FAI.* **They reported** patients undergoing labral repair had significantly better pain scores at both 1-year and 2-years follow-up compared to labral resection, whereas radiographic degenerative changes were significantly more prevalent in the labral resection group at 2-years follow-up. **The authors concluded** that labral repair appears to have superior clinical and radiographic outcomes at 2-years follow-up and is their recommended treatment.

*Parvizi et al. reviewed the literature regarding FAI.* **They stated** initial treatment consists of activity modification, including avoidance of athletic activities, and avoidance of therapy directed at increasing passive range of motion. **They concluded** that open surgical hip dislocation and hip arthroscopy can successfully treat FAI provided that surgical treatment is employed early in the disease process.

*Trousdale et al. performed a review of acetabular osteotomies for the treatment of FAI and developmental dysplasia.* **They noted** that acetabular osteotomy is the treatment of choice for young, active patients with structural abnormalities of the acetabulum and lack of degenerative changes and symptomatic FAI.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Physical therapy is part of the initial management of FAI, but attempting to increase passive ROM, especially internal rotation, can result in increased impingement and further chondrolabral lesions.
Answer 3: Open hip dislocation is a treatment option for FAI, but is associated with increased surgical morbidity. However, a trial of nonoperative treatment should be attempted prior to proceeding with surgical management.
Answer 4: The patient does have an anterosuperior labral tear that is evident on the coronal T2 MRI images. However, a repair of the labrum alone may not resolve the patient's underlying bony pathology as there is evidence of cam deformity.
Answer 5: The patient does not have apparent structural abnormalities of the acetabulum or the pelvis to necessitate treatment with a periacetabular osteotomy.
