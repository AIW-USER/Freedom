## Question

A 71-year-old male presents to your clinic with knee pain for two years that has affected his daily activities. He has persistent pain and has failed a course of conservative management and is interested in TKA. The patient asks regarding his risk of revision knee arthroplasty. What can you tell him regarding the etiology of early (<2 years) and late (>2 years) revisions?

## Options

1. polyethylene wear-related failure is the most common reason for late failure
2. aseptic loosening is the most common reason for early failure
3. instability is a more common reason for late failure than infection
4. infection is a more common cause for early failure than stiffness
5. polyethylene wear-related failures tend to occur early

## Correct option

**4. infection is a more common cause for early failure than stiffness**

## Answer explanation

Infection is one of the leading causes of early (<2 years) failures of TKA and is more common than stiffness.

TKA revisions are generally grouped into early (<2 years) and late (>2 years) failures. Common causes of early failures include infection, instability and stiffness. Common causes of late failures include aseptic loosening, infection, and instability. Prosthetic joint infections can be a devastating complication and is imperative to preoperatively optimize modifiable risk factors (obesity, diabetes, smoking, etc). TKA instability can be as a result of gap imbalance, collateral or cruciate (PCL in CR TKA) ligament injury or attenuation, and component malpositioning. Polyethylene wear-related failure is less common due to improvements in manufacturing, sterilization, and storage. Stiffness is a common reason for revision and can be multifactorial with preoperative range of motion being one of the most important determinants.

*Le et al performed a retrospective review of 253 first-time revision TKAs at a single institution to assess the current causes of early and late failures after TKA.* Early failure accounted for 46% of all revisions with infection (24%), instability (26%), and stiffness (18%) being the leading causes. Late failure accounted for 54% of all revisions with infection (25%), instability (18%), and stiffness (14%) being the most common causes. Polyethylene wear-related failure is now a relatively uncommon mode of failure (2% of early cases and 9% of late cases).

*Pitta et al conducted a retrospective review of 16,083 patients with a minimum 5-year follow-up at a single institution to determine the most common modes of failure following TKA and identify risk factors for failure.* The two most common reasons for failure within the first 2 years were infection and stiffness. Preoperative risk factors for TKA failure included drug abuse, preoperative deformity, constrained condylar knee implant over posterior-stabilized implant, post-traumatic, and younger age.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Polyethylene wear-related failure is uncommon due to improvements in manufacturing, sterilization, and storage.
Answer 2: Aseptic loosening is a more common cause of late failures.
Answer 3: Infection is a more common cause of late failure than instability.
Answer 5: Polyethylene wear-related failures occur more commonly late than early.
