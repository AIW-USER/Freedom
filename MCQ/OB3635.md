## Options

5. Figure E

## Correct option

**4. Figure D**

## Answer explanation

Buttress plating is best indicated for simple partial articular fractures, such as shown in Figure D. Buttress plates can support a metaphyseal fragment and neutralize the shear and compressive forces across the cancellous bone. The actual buttress effect is only on the side of the plate. Pre-shaped plates are frequently used as buttress plates, as they conform to local anatomy, however exact contouring of the plate is necessary. Isolated buttress plating is not appropriate for articular depression fractures, such as those shown in Figures A, C, and E. Furthermore, buttress plating would provide inadequate fixation for a metadiaphyseal fracture, such as that shown in Figure B.

Illustration A shows an example of a buttress plate used to treat a tibial plateau fracture.
