## Answer explanation

OPEN VS CLOSED KINETIC CHAIN EXERCISES

Open kinetic chain exercises involve movements of a combination of successively arranged joints, in which the terminal segments can move freely in space. This means the distal segment of the extremity is free to move in space, for example, waving a hand, moving the foot during the swing phase of gait, or doing a seated knee extension.

CHARACTERISTICS OF OPEN KINETIC CHAIN

- Open kinetic chain exercises are typically characterized by a rotary stress pattern at the joint. For example, when doing a seated knee-extension the primary stress to the joint is the rotation of the distal tibia on the proximal femur even though other accessory movements like rolling and translation occur.
- Open kinetic chain movements occur in one primary axis. During the seated knee extension exercise, the motion primarily occurs in the sagittal plane.
- The number of simultaneously moving segments is typically limited to one. "One segment of the joint (i.e. femur) remains stationary during the knee-extension exercise, while the other segment that forms the joint (i.e. tibia) is mobile." This adds control to the exercise because of the stability of the stationary segment (i.e. femur).
- These types of exercises allow for more isolated muscle activation because less muscle co-contraction is used to perform the movement.

Closed Kinetic Chain is when the distal segment meets “considerable” external resistance that prohibits free movement. Therefore, it is a system where neither the proximal nor the distal segments can move.

So closed kinetic chain is when “resistance is placed through the distal aspect of the extremity such that distal extremity remains fixed to the resistance.”

E.g., Standing squat (feet remain fixed to the ground and the surface produces considerable resistance in response to the athlete’s bodyweight).

CHARACTERISTICS OF CLOSE KINETIC CHAIN

- Linear stress pattern is produced at the joint due to axial joint loading
- Movement occurs at multiple joints and multiple-joint axes,
- An increase in muscular co-contraction is required to stabilize and control the movements across the joints in the chain.

STATIC VS DYNAMIC EXERCISES

In static pushups, you maintain a plank position with your body in a straight line from head to heels, engaging your core and stabilizing muscles without the up-and-down motion. This is an isometric exercise that primarily focuses on building endurance and stability.

Dynamic pushups, on the other hand, involve the classic up-and-down movement. This targets a broader range of muscles, including chest, shoulders, triceps, and core. The dynamic nature adds the element of muscle contraction and extension, contributing to strength and muscle development.

When using a bench for pushups, it can increase the range of motion, emphasizing chest muscles more than standard pushups. On the contrary, incorporating a Swiss ball/ Weight Plate introduces instability, requiring additional effort from stabilizing muscles, particularly the core. This instability forces your body to work harder to maintain balance during the exercise, enhancing overall muscle engagement and strengthening your core.

Now, in the picture it’s mentioned the patient is doing push ups on a weight plate. So, the closed kinetic chain exercise here is dynamic in nature. The term "dynamic" here indicates the movement involved, even though the instability from the ball or plate adds an extra challenge to engage stabilizing muscles. So, it remains a dynamic push-up variation only and hence best answer is choice B.
