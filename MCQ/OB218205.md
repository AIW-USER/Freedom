## Question

You are attending a grand-rounds lecture on the management of proximal interphalangeal (PIP) joint arthritis. Several surgical options are discussed in great detail. Which of the following patients would be most appropriately managed with a PIP joint arthrodesis?

## Options

1. 44-year-old laborer with index finger PIPJ arthritis
2. 50-year-old male laborer with a predominant contracture and minimal joint involvement
3. 64-year-old female with collateral deficient PIP joint arthritis of the ring finger
4. 70-year-old female with intact collaterals and PIP joint arthritis of the middle finger
5. 80-year-old male with recurrent infections of the PIP joint with significant bone loss

## Correct option

**1. 44-year-old laborer with index finger PIPJ arthritis**

## Answer explanation

A young laborer with index finger PIP joint arthritis, an arthrodesis is preferred for its durability over implant arthroplasty.

Arthritis of the DIP and PIP joints are very common forms of osteoarthritis seen in the hand and can be associated with pain and deformity. Diagnosis is made radiographically with joint space narrowing seen in the DIP and PIP joints of the fingers. Mucous cysts are often present on clinical inspection of the DIP joint. Treatment is observation if patient is minimally symptomatic. Historically, the treatment of the index finger alone is a relative contraindication for arthroplasty of the PIP joint as it experiences more ulnar deviation forces during key pinch. Other surgical options include denervation, implant arthroplasty (silicone or pyrocarbon), and arthrodesis. In a young laborer, arthrodesis of the index finger PIP joint is preferred for its durability over implant arthroplasty.

*Arauz et al. reviewed the effect of simulated proximal interphalangeal (PIP) joint arthrodesis on distal interphalangeal (DIP) joint free flexion-extension (FE) and maximal voluntary pinch forces.* **They reported** that the PIP joint arthrodesis angle affects DIP joint extension. **They noted** a minimal overall impact from simulated PIP arthrodesis in muscle activity and pinch force of the index finger was observed. **They concluded** that a PIP arthrodesis at 0° to 20° of flexion leads to a more natural finger posture during tip and chuck pinch.

*Milone et al. performed a systematic review and meta-analysis of the literature to assess whether the 3 ulnar digits may bear a similar instability and complication profile.* **They reported** that instability-related deformity and complication rates of long finger PIPJ arthroplasty may not be different from that of the index finger. **They concluded** that treatment of the long finger may be a relative contraindication to PIPJ arthroplasty.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Collateral ligament excision, volar plate release, osteophyte excision are indicated with a predominant contracture and minimal joint involvement.
Answer 3: This patient may be best treated with a silicone arthroplasty as it is a single implant and not dependent on the integrity of the collateral ligaments or the volar plate.
Answer 4: This patient may be best treated with an arthroplasty option; with intact collateral ligaments and volar plate, she may be appropriately treated with a pyrocarbon implant.
Answer 5: This patient may be a candidate for a resection arthroplasty with a history of recurrent infections and significant bone loss.
