## Question

A 70-year-old female with congestive heart failure sustains a displaced femoral neck fracture. Which of the following factors increases the patient's risk of dislocation when performing a hemiarthroplasty using the posterior approach?

## Options

1. Femoral anteversion of 20 degrees
2. Increasing femoral offset
3. Lengthening the leg by 1.5 cm
4. Use of a cementless implant
5. Posterior acetabular wall insufficiency

## Correct option

**5. Posterior acetabular wall insufficiency**

## Answer explanation

Posterior acetabular wall insufficiency is a risk factor for subsequent hemiarthroplasty dislocation.

Patients who undergo a hemiarthroplasty through the posterior approach are at greater risk of a posterior hip dislocation post-operatively if there are insufficient posterior bony and soft tissue constraints. Situations where these structures can become compromised intraoperatively include lacerating the posterior labrum during the approach, fracturing the posterior wall while dislocating the femoral head, and failure to perform a sufficient posterior capsular repair at the end of the procedure. Patients who dislocate for a structural reason may ultimately require conversion to a total hip arthroplasty.

Converting a hip hemiarthroplasty to THA due to recurrent hip instability or acetabular erosion generally yields positive outcomes. Studies show that conversion to THA after a dislocation reduces the risk of recurrent dislocations, as both the acetabular version and femoral components can be adjusted along with additional liners that can increase constraint. Long-term outcomes are favorable, with many patients achieving improved mobility and quality of life.

*The study by Sarpong et al. on the conversion of failed hip hemiarthroplasty to THA found that the procedure significantly improved pain relief and function in most patients.* However, it was associated with higher complication rates, including dislocation and infection, compared to primary THA. **The authors concluded** that while conversion to THA is a viable option for managing failed hemiarthroplasty, careful patient selection and surgical planning are critical for optimizing outcomes.

*The study by Gill et al. investigated the management and outcomes of dislocated hip hemiarthroplasties,* **highlighting** that dislocation usually occurs in the early postoperative period. **The authors found** that closed reduction was successful in most cases. However, repeat dislocations and the need for revision surgery were frequent in patients with underlying instability or inadequate soft tissue repair. **They concluded** that while dislocation can be managed effectively in many cases, attention to surgical technique and patient selection is essential to reduce recurrence.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: femoral anteversion of 20 degrees is reasonable for a hip hemiarthroplasty done through the posterior approach as it decreases the risk of a posterior dislocation.
Answer 2 and 3: increasing offset and leg length increases soft tissue tension and thus increases hip stability.
Answer 4: cementless stems increase the risk of a femur fracture but do not increase the risk of a hip dislocation
