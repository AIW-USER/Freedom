## Question

A 3-year-old well appearing child has refused to walk for the past 2 days. His parents report no known injuries. Examination in the emergency department reveals a temperature of 102.2 degrees F (39 degrees C) and limited range of motion of the left hip. An AP pelvic radiograph is normal. Laboratory studies show a WBC count of 9,000/mm3, an erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR) of 50 mm/h, and a C-reactive protein level of 5 mg/dL (normal <1mg/dL). What is the next most appropriate step in management?

## Options

1. Trial of NSAID's
2. CT left hip
3. IV antibiotics
4. Ultrasound left hip
5. Discharge with close follow-up

## Correct option

**4. Ultrasound left hip**

## Answer explanation

Examination reveals an irritable hip, creating the differential diagnosis that includes transient synovitis versus pyogenic hip arthritis. The best next step in the work-up of this non-toxic appearing patient is an ultrasound of the left hip to evaluate for effusion.

*Kocher and associates describe four criteria to help predict the presence of septic hip arthritis: inability to bear weight, fever, ESR > 40 mm/h, and a peripheral WBC count of more than 12,000/mm3.* This patient has 3 out of 4 Kocher criteria in addition to an elevated CRP. A CRP >2 mg/dL is another independent risk factor, which when combined with an inability to bear weight leads to a 74% probability of septic arthritis. Given these findings, the most appropriate step is an ultrasound of the left hip to evaluate for an effusion. This is both cost-effective and of diagnostic utility. If an effusion is present, the patient will need an aspiration to confirm the diagnosis.

*Beccaro et al. review the tests for making the diagnosis of septic arthritis vs transient synovitis.* **They report** that patients with septic arthritis had a significantly higher initial temperature (38.1°C), mean ESR (44 mm/hr) and mean WBC (32,000/mm3). However, **they conclude** that there is a significant overlap of these findings in patients with both septic arthritis and transient synovitis and that patients with an ESR > 20mm/hr and a temperature of more than 37.5°C should be considered for hip aspiration.

*Kocher et al. review a clinical-based algorithm for differentiating between septic arthritis and transient synovitis of the hip in children.* **They report** that patients with septic arthritis different from those of transient synovitis with regard to ESR, serum WBC, and differential, weight-bearing status, history of fever. **They conclude** that patients with 0 out of 4 predictors had a probability of having septic arthritis of less than 0.2%, whereas, those with 4 out of 4 had a 99.6% predictive value. *These findings were later validated in another study by Kocher et al. on a new patient population.*

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: NSAID challenge would be the treatment of choice for a patient with transient synovitis. This patient has 3/4 Kocher criteria, including an inability to bear weight and an elevated CRP, which necessitates evaluation.
Answer 2: CT scan of the left hip would likely require sedation in this age group and comes with a significantly increased radiation dose that is unnecessary in this clinical scenario.
Answer 3: IV antibiotics can be held until the aspiration is performed as the patient is non-toxic appearing and has stable vital signs.
Answer 5: Given the concern for infection, discharge with follow up is not appropriate.
