## Question

A 47-year-old male arrives as a transfer from an urgent care center 2 hours after he reportedly fell from a tree. He reports neck pain and worsening subjective weakness in his lower extremities. On physical exam he is alert, oriented, and following command calmly. His motor exam shows he is unable to do a straight raise against gravity. He has abrasions and lacerations on his right flank. He arrived with the radiographs shown in Figure A. An emergent MRI is performed which shows severe cervical canal stenosis due to retropulsion of bone. Which of the following should be performed prior to definitive surgical treatment?

![[q3765-a-spine-trauma.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Administration of IV methylprednisolone
2. CT of the cervical spine
3. CT of the entire spine
4. Flexion/extension views of the cervical spine
5. MR angiography of the cervical spine

## Correct option

**3. CT of the entire spine**

## Answer explanation

This patient sustained a burst fracture at C5. The next most appropriate step in management is a CT of the entire spine to rule out noncontiguous spinal fractures.

Subaxial cervical spine injuries are common, and one of the most frequently made mistakes is missing an injury on initial evaluation. Necessary radiographs for clearance of the cervical spine include AP, lateral, and open-mouth odontoid view. The radiograph should include the top of the T-1 vertebrae. If a patient sustains a fracture that is visualized, a CT scan of the entire spine should be obtained next. Noncontiguous spinal fractures have been shown to be present in as high as 16% of cases.

*Kwon et al. performed a review of subaxial spine trauma.* **They report** that the use of CT has become popular because of the excellent resolution of osseous anatomy and its rapid accessibility in most emergency departments. It is especially effective at visualizing the occipitocervical and cervicothoracic junctions which are difficult to image with X-ray. **They conclude** that the superior imaging of bony anatomy and the widespread availability of CT scanning have compelled some radiologists to suggest a CT be obtained in lieu of conventional radiographs.

Figure A is a lateral radiograph of the cervical spine demonstrating a C5 burst fracture. Illustration A is a labeled lateral radiograph of the cervical spine and illustration B is a lateral radiograph of the cervical spine demonstrating the four lines to assess for cervical alignment.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Methylprednisolone is no longer a supported treatment for acute spinal cord injury. Current literature has demonstrated an increased risk of complications with no clear evidence of benefit.
Answer 2: CT of the thoracic and lumbar spine should be obtained in addition to the cervical spine due to the risk of noncontiguous spinal fractures, cited in as many as 16% of cases.
Answer 4: Flexion/extension views of the cervical spine would not be recommended in this case as there already is proven instability.
Answer 5: **the next most appropriate step would be to obtaina CT of the entire spine evaluate for any other spinal fractures which would require intervention.
