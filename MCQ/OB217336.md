## Question

A 74-year-old female presents to the office for evaluation of a painful right total hip arthroplasty that was performed 8 years ago. She reports having dislocated this hip 3 times. After obtaining Judet views you suspect that you'll have to utilize a triflange component for her reconstruction. Which of the following findings most likely led you to this decision?

## Options

1. History of multiple dislocations
2. Intraprosthetic dislocation
3. Loosening of the acetabular component
4. Pelvic discontinuity
5. Paprosky 2B defect

## Correct option

**4. Pelvic discontinuity**

## Answer explanation

Triflange reconstruction components are utilized in the setting of severe acetabular bone loss and pelvic discontinuity.

Pelvic discontinuity, or separation of the ilium superiorly from the ischiopubic segment inferiorly, is a challenging complication following total hip arthroplasty. Several treatment techniques exist including the use of cup-cage reconstructions, pelvic distraction or a triflange component. Triflange cups are custom-designed, porous and/or hydroxyapatite-coated, titanium acetabular components with iliac, ischial, and pubic flanges. These flanges allow for intimate contact between the implant and the host bone for initial stability until biologic fixation can be achieved. Prior to going down the path of reconstruction, however, its of paramount importance to rule out infection first by obtaining inflammatory markers.

*Abdel et al review pelvic discontinuity associated with total hip arthroplasty.* **They report** that most pelvic discontinuities result from a chronic stress fracture through severely deficient acetabular bone. **They conclude** that treatment consists of ruling out infection followed by reconstruction utilizing jumbo cups with posterior column plating, cup-cage constructions, pelvic distraction or custom triflange implants.

*Berasi et al review the use of custom triflange components for severe acetabular bone loss.* *They report on 28 patients who underwent this treatment for Paprosky 3B defects.* Only 4 revision surgeries were performed, none of which were for loosening. **They report** that the use of a custom triflange component is viable option for patients with severe acetabular bone loss.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1 and 3: A history of multiple dislocations or a loose acetabular component both may require revision of the acetabular component, but would not necessitate the use of a custom triflange.
Answer 2: Intraprosthetic dislocations can occur between components that are not properly united, but would not typically require revision of the acetabular component.
Answer 5: Paprosky 2B defects have intact anterior and posterior columns with moderate bone loss. A jumbo cup is typically used for reconstruction and a custom triflange would not be warranted.
