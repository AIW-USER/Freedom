## Question

A 32-year-old male presents with a 7-week history of lower back pain, leg pain, and associated weakness after lifting a heavy box. He has undergone a course of physical therapy and corticosteroid injections around the affected nerve root, but there is minimal improvement in his symptoms. The decision is made to proceed with a lumbar microdiscectomy to address his pathology. Physical exam reveals left great toe extension weakness. His pain is reportedly in the anterolateral leg and dorsum of the foot. what pathologic process would most likely cause this patient's presenting symptoms?

## Options

1. Left L3-4 paracentral disc herniation
2. Central L4-5 disc hernation
3. Left L5-S1 paracentral disc herniation
4. Left L4-5 paracentral disc herniation
5. Left far lateral L4-5 disc herniation

## Correct option

**4. Left L4-5 paracentral disc herniation**

## Answer explanation

The patient is presenting with a left paracentral herniated nucleus pulposus at the L4-5 level that is likely compressing the L5 nerve root.

Lumbar disc herniation is a common cause of lumbar radiculopathy with a peak incidence in the 4th and 5th decade of life. The natural history of the disease is often favorable, but surgical treatment should be considered for patients with motor deficits and continued pain despite adequate nonoperative treatment (>6 weeks of PT and corticosteroids). The presentation is dependent on the affected nerve root. If the L5 nerve root is affected there will be dermatomal pain in the corresponding distribution (anterolateral leg and dorsal foot). Also, muscles innervated by the L5 nerve root will likely be weak (hip abductors and extensor hallicus longus).

*Aeschbach and Mekhail reviewed common nerve blocks for the treatment of chronic pain.* **They stated** the literature supports a 95% positive correlation rate with selective nerve root blocks and successful surgical outcomes at 1-year. **They concluded** the practice of regional anesthesia is a specific diagnostic and therapeutic treatment modality for chronic pain management.

*Beynon et al. performed a systematic review of the utility of diagnostic selective nerve blocks in the management of lumbar radiculopathy.* *The authors included six studies, which were all deemed high-risk for bias and substantial heterogeneity.* **They concluded** there is limited evidence to support the utility of selective nerve root blocks, but the test remains relatively safe to perform.

Incorrect answers
Answer 1: A left paracentral disc herniation at L3-4 would likely compress the traversing L4 nerve root leading to tibialis anterior weakness.
Answer 2: A central disc herniation at L4-5 would not likely compress the L5 nerve root. However, large herniations may lead to cauda equina syndrome.
Answer 3: A left paracentral disc herniation at L5-S1 would lead to lateral recess stenosis and compress the traversing S1 nerve root. This would present with gastrosoleus weakness.
Answer 5: A left far-lateral disc herniation at L4-5 would compress the L4 nerve root at the level of the neural foramina and cause tibialis anterior weakness.
