## Question

For which of the following conditions would a physician use the Beighton-Horan scale during the physical examination of a patient?

## Options

1. Cerebral palsy
2. Ehlers-Danlos
3. Myelodysplasia
4. Arthrogryposis
5. Kniest's dysplasia

## Correct option

**2. Ehlers-Danlos**

## Answer explanation

The Beighton-Horan scale is used to evaluate patients with joint laxity and Ehlers-Danlos syndrome.

Five criteria are scored on a scale from zero to 9, with a higher score representing greater generalized joint hypermobility. A score of > 3 indicates generalized joint hypermobility. However, there is no universal agreement on a threshold for this score, some researchers use a Beighton scale score of 5/9, other researchers use a score of 6/9 and still others use a modified score of 3/9. Elements that are included in the physical exam include hyperextension of the fifth MCP joint, apposition of the thumb, hyperextension of the elbow and knee, and forward flexion of the trunk. The Beighton test has high intra- and interrater reliability and reproducibility.

*Wolf et al. review the impact of joint laxity and hypermobility on the musculoskeletal system.* **They discuss** that orthopaedic surgeons should be familiar and comfortable with the treatment of patients with hypermobility, as they frequently present with orthopaedic complaints such as shoulder and patella instability. Additionally, **they discuss** that treatment is usually best suited through a proprioceptive and strengthening program.

Beighton and Horan review the orthopaedic manifestations of Ehlers-Danlos syndrome. They find that the occurrence of joint dislocations and the incidence of osteoarthritis is directly related to the degree of hypermobility.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1, 3, 4, 5: The Beighton-Horan scale is not useful for these conditions.
