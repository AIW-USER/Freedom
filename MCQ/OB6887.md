## Question

Six weeks after onset, what is the most clearly accepted indication for surgical management for lumbar disk herniation?

## Options

1. Stable sensory loss
2. Stable motor weakness
3. Refractory radicular pain
4. Size of the herniation
5. Lost time at work

## Correct option

**3. Refractory radicular pain**

## Answer explanation

In the absence of a cauda equina syndrome or progressive weakness, the best indication for surgical management is refractory radicular pain. Surgical decision-making should not be based on the size of the herniation. Large extruded herniations tend to resolve more predictably than smaller herniations. Stable motor weakness and numbness resolve similarly in both surgical and nonsurgical management, although surgery hastens the process. When intractable radicular pain is the strict indication for surgery, surgical intervention provides substantial and more rapid pain relief than nonsurgical care.
