## Correct option

**2. Figure B**

## Answer explanation

Figure B demonstrates a cortical desmoid, which is typically found in the distal posteromedial femur and results from tendinous traction via the medial head of gastrocnemius or adductor magnus.

Cortical desmoids are self-limiting, benign fibro-osseous lesions occurring most frequently in active adolescents (ages 10-15). These lesions usually are the result of chronic injury, but may be acutely worsened with activity or a discrete traumatic event. Patients may be asymptomatic or present with localized pain at the site of the lesion. The supracondylar femur is the most commonly implicated location for these lesions, however, they may occur at other tendon insertion sites in the body, including the proximal humerus (pectorals insertion or deltoid insertion). Diagnosis is made with imaging. On plain radiographs, these lesions appear as geographic with a central lucent region and sclerotic margins. They may show a “bite-like” cortical irregularity. MRI features include low to intermediate signal on T1-weighted images and high signal intensity on T2-weighted images. Cortical desmoids usually enhance heterogeneously with contrast MRI, distinguishing them from more aggressive lesions.

*Tscholl et al. present a case series with two active adolescent athletes with nonspecific knee pain.* *The authors discuss the imaging findings on conventional radiographs and MRI of the knee, which demonstrated a pathognomonic cortical defect of the posteromedial femoral condyle.* *Given self-limiting natural history,* **they note** that follow-up is not required in asymptomatic patients.

*Mester et al. present a retrospective review of 23 patients with cortical desmoid of the posteromedial femoral condyle.* *The authors assessed the presence of symptoms, symptom duration, associated pathology, prior MRI evaluation, patient sport, and prior interventions.* **They found** that 100% of patients reported knee symptoms at initial presentation, with 52% localizing their pain to the posteromedial knee. The majority (70%) of patients were highly active, with an average of 10.5 weeks of symptoms. Downtime from competitive sport was 14 weeks, with a return-to-sport rate of 81%.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Figure A demonstrates a chondroblastoma, a rare, benign lesion affecting adolescents and young adults. These are typically well-circumscribed, lytic lesions in the epiphysis.
Answer 3: Figure C shows a benign, medullary-based lytic lesion with localized pain and swelling. Radiographic findings include geographic bone destruction with well-defined, sclerotic edges.
Answer 4: Figure D shows an osteoid osteoma, which may also present with localized pain, worsened at night, and responsive to non-steroidal anti-inflammatories. Radiographs demonstrate a lucent nidus with a zone of reactive sclerosis.
Answer 5: Figure E demonstrates an osteochondroma, the most common benign bone tumor, often with an associated cartilage cap. These characteristically arise from the metaphyseal region of long bones in children or adolescents. They are usually asymptomatic but may cause pain or mechanical symptoms.
