## Question

A 65-year-old female presents to your office with the chief complain of right great toe pain. She has pain during the push-off portion of the gait cycle. On physical exam, she has pain with 1st metatarsophalangeal joint grind test. The patient is a very poor surgical candidate and would like to pursue conservative management. Which orthotic would provide her the best relief?

## Options

1. UCBL
2. Arizona brace
3. Morton's carbon fiber extension
4. Medial flare shoe modification
5. Negative rocker heel

## Correct option

**3. Morton's carbon fiber extension**

## Answer explanation

The patient has hallux rigidus as evidence by the radiographs and physical exam findings. While 1st MTP arthrodesis is the golden standard treatment for this patient, conservative management with a Morton’s carbon fiber extension would provide pain relief with ambulation.

Hallux rigidus is the result of arthritis at the first MTPJ. Patients typically have pain with toe-off and a decreased ability to dorsiflex the great toe. Conservative treatment starts with activity modification and Morton’s extension orthotics. The role of the orthotic is to provide rigid support for the hallux and to decrease plantar loading forces across the MTP joint. Ultimately, treatment for advanced 1st MTP arthritis is arthrodesis. In patients who are extremely low demand, Keller (resection arthroplasty) can also be considered.

*McNeil et al review the current body of literature on operative interventions for hallux rigidus.* *They report on 135 articles and reviewed their level of evidence and grade of recommendations.* **They conclude** that there is fair (grade B) evidence to support arthrodesis, whereas, there is poor (grade C) evidence to support cheilectomy, osteotomy, implant arthroplasty, resection arthroplasty, and interposition arthroplasty for the treatment of hallux rigidus.

*Coughlin et al review the demographics, etiology, and radiographic assessment of patients treated surgically for hallux rigidus.* *They report on 114 patients, 93 of which were treated with cheilectomy and 34 treated with 1st MTP arthrodesis.* **They conclude** that patients with hallux rigidus are more likely to have hallux valgus interphalangeus, bilateral involvement with a family history, and unilateral involvement in the setting of trauma.

Figures A and B are the AP radiograph of the right foot and great toe demonstrating Coughlin and Shurnas grade 3 first MTPJ arthritis. Figure C is a lateral radiograph of the same MTP joint showing significant dorsal osteophytes.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The University of California Biomechanics Laboratory (UCBL) orthosis is used to treat flexible pes planovalgus, not hallux rigidus.
Answer 2: The Arizona brace is also used to treat flexible pes planovalgus, and is a combination of the UCBL and a lace-up ankle brace.
Answer 4: Medial flare shoe modifications provide a wider base of support and typically benefit patients with ankle instability.
Answer 5: Negative heel rocker increases plantarflexion during terminal stance, and would not benefit a patient with hallux rigidus.
