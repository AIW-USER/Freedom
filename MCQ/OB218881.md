## Question

A 32-year-old male presents to your office after a knee injury and relates that he continues to have pain and recurrent effusions despite the injury occurring several weeks ago. On advanced imaging, the structure inserting between the adductor tubercle and medial epicondyle is identified as having been injured. What is the primary function of this structure?

## Options

1. Restraint to lateral patellar translation from 0-30°
2. Restraint to knee valgus stress at 30°
3. Restraint to external tibial rotation and posterior tibial translation
4. Assist with flexion of knee
5. Adduction of hip/leg

## Correct option

**1. Restraint to lateral patellar translation from 0-30°**

## Answer explanation

The primary function of the medial patellofemoral ligament (MPFL) is to resist lateral patellar translation from 0-30° of flexion.

The MPFL is an important medial knee structure which originates on the proximal half of the medial patella and inserts on the medial femoral condyle between the adductor tubercle and the medial epicondyle. It's main function is to prevent lateral patellar translation before the patella engages the trochlea around 30° of knee flexion. MPFL injuries often occur after patellar dislocations or subluxation events and reconstruction is often indicated in cases of chondral/bony injury or persistent instability. Reconstruction involves recreating the normal MPFL anatomy with regards to both the femoral and patellar attachments.

*Placella et al. reviewed the anatomy of the MPFL based on previous literature.* *They looked at the normal size, length, and thickness of the ligament as well as normal attachment patterns.* **They noted** the MPFL is consistently present, although the size and specific insertion patterns do have some variation within the overall parameters discussed above.

*Zaffagnini et al. reviewed the influence of the MPFL on patellofemoral joint kinematics and patellar stability.* *They looked at kinematics with and without an applied lateral load from 0-90° of flexion and noted that the MPFL pulled the patella medially especially during the first degrees of knee flexion.* **They concluded** that a deficient MPFL affected both the kinematics and stability of the patellofemoral joint up to 30-60° of flexion.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: The medial collateral ligament (MCL) acts as the primary restraint to valgus stress at 30° of flexion and a persistent effusion would not be seen due to its extra-articular nature.
Answer 3: The posterior oblique ligament (POL) assists in preventing excessive external tibial rotation and posterior tibial translation and injury can lead to anteromedial rotatory instability.
Answer 4: The medial gastrocnemius tendon assists with flexion of the knee as well as plantarflexion of the ankle, as it crosses both days.
Answer 5: The adductor magnus crosses the hip joint and attaches at the adductor tubercle of the medial knee, primarily functioning to adduct the hip.
