## Answer explanation

The patient likely sustained a Neer type IIB distal clavicle fracture, disrupting the coracoclavicular (CC) ligaments (Answer 3).

The coracoclavicular ligaments – the conoid and trapezoid – provide vertical stability to the distal clavicle. The conoid ligament inserts 4-4.5 cm from the distal end of the clavicle and is most important for stability. The trapezoid ligament inserts more laterally, approximately 2-3 cm from the distal end of the clavicle. Disrupting the CC ligaments in this fracture pattern leads to vertical instability of the medial fracture fragment, which has been shown to serve as a significant factor in the development of a nonunion. To mitigate this risk, operative intervention is typically recommended.

*Banerjee et al. reviewed the management of distal clavicle fractures and* **determined** that most clavicle fractures heal without complication. However, distal clavicle fractures have a reported nonunion rate of 10% to 44%, with Type II fractures being the most prone to nonunion due to vertical instability and subsequent displacement. The clinical significance of nonunion in distal clavicle fractures is debated, as clinical outcome scores do not differ significantly. However, other studies included in their review indicate that distal clavicle nonunions are symptomatic on physical examination in 20% to 34% of patients and eventually require surgical fixation. **The authors conclude** that the decision to operate may be influenced by the amount of initial fracture displacement, the individual needs of the patient, and the development of a symptomatic nonunion. However, no surgical technique has demonstrated superiority.

*Kim et al. reviewed current concepts for classifying and treating distal third clavicle fractures.* **They found** that distal clavicle fractures make up 10% to 30% of all clavicle fractures, with some treated conservatively. However, unstable fractures typically require surgery to mitigate nonunion rates. Numerous fixation options have been utilized, including pre-contoured locking plates, hook plates, and tension band wiring. Previous systematic reviews suggest pre-contoured locking plates offer superior clinical results and fewer complications, but no consensus or randomized studies have established a gold standard fixation technique. Therefore, **the authors conclude** that unstable distal clavicle fractures are a viable surgical indication, and the fixation method should be selected based on what approach the patient and surgeon find most appropriate.
