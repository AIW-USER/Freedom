## Question

Along with a detailed history and physical, you obtain radiographs, which are depicted in Figures A and B. ## Options

1. X-linked recessive
2. X-linked dominant
3. Autosomal recessive
4. Autosomal dominant
5. Mitochondrial

## Correct option

**4. Autosomal dominant**

## Answer explanation

Osteopoikilosis is a benign skeletal condition inherited in an autosomal dominant (AD) fashion and characterized as a "spotted bone" appearance of the skeleton, primarily in a juxta-articular location.

Osteopoikilosis is an asymptomatic sclerotic bony dysplasia inherited in an AD fashion. It was first described by Albers-Schoenberg (same Albers-Schoenberg who discovered AD osteopetrosis) in 1915, with the incidence being equal in males and females. It can be associated with other systemic disorders such as scleroderma, autoimmune disorders, and endocrine abnormalities. The differential diagnosis for similar-appearing bone lesions includes chondrosarcoma, osteoblastic metastases, and tuberous sclerosis.

*Waddell et al describe several benign bony conditions with characteristic radiographic appearance.* **They caution** that multiple conditions can mimic each other, but diligent radiographic review and a review of inheritance patterns in the patient's relatives can help determine a diagnosis.

*Panchal et al wrote a case report of a patient with osteopoikilosis presenting with bilateral hip and shoulder pain.* **They state** in most instances it is an incidentally found condition, but in a rare subgroup of patients, they can have articular pain and effusions. Similar to other case reports, **they caution** that osteopoikilosis can mimic other more concerning diagnoses, specifically chondrosarcoma and metastatic carcinoma.

Figures A and B are ankle and wrist radiographs of a patient with osteopoikilosis. Note the "spotted bone" nature with multiple sclerotic bony islands in a juxta-articular location.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1-3, 5: Osteopoikilosis is inherited in an AD fashion primarily. There are cases of sporadic inheritance, but it has not been shown to be inherited as an x-linked, autosomal recessive, or mitochondrial condition.
