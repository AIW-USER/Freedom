## Question

A patient of necrotizing fasciitis developed sudden onset low back pain. On MRI there is a hypointense lesion in epidural space. The likely cause is [FNB 2023]

![[TO19356191-1.png]]

## Options

1. Epidural abscess
2. Pneumorrachis
3. Ewing sarcoma spine
4. Perineural cyst

## Correct option

**2. Pneumorrachis**

## Answer explanation

Answer: 2

EXPLANATION: -
Best pick is B. Intra spinal gas collections mostly result from infectious diseases caused by gas forming organisms, viz. emphysematous osteomyelitis and necrotising fasciitis (the condition the patient in question has). They are close differentials of Pneumorrhachis which refers to presence of “air” in spinal canal.

PNEUMORRHACHIS
The presence of air (not gas) in spinal canal (either intradural or extradural) is called as “Pneumorrhachis/ Aerorachia/ Epidural Emphysema”. It can be Traumatic, Iatrogenic or Spontaneous.
In literature, most of the cases of air in spinal canal occur due to trauma, pneumothorax, or pneumomediastinum, and from surgical procedures (iatrogenic like after epidural anaesthesia). Nontraumatic, noniatrogenic spontaneous spinal air is an uncommon case.
Pathophysiology: Peripheral alveoli burst due to the increased pressure in alveoli in the case of trauma, asthma, pneumothorax, or pneumomediastinum. Air pass to the mediastinum and then to retropharyngeal space and reaches to epidural space.
In general, they are mostly seen in cervical space but to a low frequency in thoracic and lumbar spinal space.

![[TO19356191-2.jpg]]

Clinical features: Lumbar and radicular pain has been reported in some cases although most of them are asymptomatic. CT is the best diagnostic tool.

Differential diagnosis: Intra spinal gas collections remain the closest differential of Pneumorrhachis and CT cannot separate the two. Etiology is a good guide; however; best way is to check gas-chromatographically. In cases associated with the gas collection, sample contains up to 92% nitrogen combined with oxygen, carbon dioxide and other trace gases, which is in contrast to atmospheric air (pneumorrhachis) with a lower nitrogen (78%) and a higher oxygen composition (21%).
Treatment: In general, spontaneous resolution occurs so that conservative treatment is suggested.
