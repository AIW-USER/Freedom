## Question

A 53-year-old female sustains a proximal humerus fracture after falling off of a porch. Your attending physician asks you about risk factors for humeral head ischemia following these injuries. Which of the following is most predictive of fracture-induced humeral head ischemia?

## Options

1. 9mm calcar segment length attached to humeral head
2. 8mm displacement of the lesser tuberosity
3. 40 degrees of angulation of the humeral head
4. 2-part fracture
5. Disrupted medial periosteal hinge

## Correct option

**5. Disrupted medial periosteal hinge**

## Answer explanation

This patient sustained a proximal humerus fracture. A disrupted medial periosteal hinge is a known risk factor for humeral head AVN.

The humeral head receives its blood supply from the anterior and posterior humeral circumflex arteries. Disruption of these vessels may result in humeral head AVN. Established risk factors for humeral head AVN after proximal humerus fractures include <8mm of intact calcar attached to the articular surface, a displaced medial hinge >2mm, and/or anatomic neck fracture. Fracture patterns with long medial metaphyseal segments (>8 mm) preserve the vascular supply to the humeral head.

*Hertel et al. prospectively reviewed the predictors of fracture-induced humeral head ischemia.* **They reported** that good predictors of ischemia were the length of the metaphyseal head extension (calcar segments <8 mm), the integrity of the medial hinge, and the basic fracture pattern; moderate and poor predictors of ischemia were fractures consisting of four fragments, angular displacement of the head (>45 degrees), the amount of displacement of the tuberosities (>10 mm), glenohumeral dislocation, head-split components, and fractures consisting of three fragments. **They concluded** that the most relevant predictors of ischemia were the length of the dorsomedial metaphyseal extension, the integrity of the medial hinge, and the basic fracture type determined with the binary description system.

*Neer et al. proposed a classification system for proximal humerus fractures.* *They reported on four parts including: the humeral head, the greater tuberosity, the lesser tuberosity, and the humeral shaft.* A fracture part is considered displaced if angulation >45 degrees or if the fracture is displaced by more than 1cm. **They concluded** that those 4-part fractures have a high incidence of osteonecrosis.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: >8mm of medial metaphyseal calcar is protective against humeral head AVN
Answer 2: Displacement of the tuberosities (>10 mm) is a poor predictor of ischemia
Answer 3: Angular displacement of the head (>45 degrees) is a poor predictor of ischemia
Answer 4: 2-part fracture is not by itself predictive of humeral head ischemia
