## Question

In trendelenburg test the position of opposite limb (which is not being tested) is (Regional orthopedics- hip- clinical examination and gait)

## Options

1. Foot plantigrade and hip neutral
2. Foot neutral and off the ground, hip 30 degree flexion
3. Foot neutral off the ground and hip in slight extension
4. Foot plantigrade and hip 45 degree flexion

## Correct option

**3. Foot neutral off the ground and hip in slight extension**

## Answer explanation

Ans: C

Hip should be in slight extension as flexion may activate iliopsoas altering results.

Trendelenburg Test and Gait (Gluteus Medius Gait)

It is seen in the weak abductor mechanism. An intact abductor mechanism requires fulcrum (hip joint proper), lever arm (head and neck) and power (abductors). Common causes are:

Defect in fulcrum: Hip dislocation, advanced cases of TB hip and septic arthritis of hip joint, developmental dysplasia of the hip, Perthes disease, advanced cases of AVN hip.

Defect in lever arm: Fracture head or neck of femur, coxa vara and trochanteric fractures.

Defect in power: Abductor paralysis due to poliomyelitis or superior gluteal nerve injury, tensor fascia lata and iliotibial tract palsy and L5 radiculopathy.

When one stands on a single leg ( i.e. left leg), contraction of abductors (mainly gluteus medius )on the left side lift the right side pelvis off the ground. In case of defective abductor mechanism on the left side (i.e. abductor palsy) the right side pelvis will dip down (positive trendelenburg sign). To prevent dipping of pelvis patient lurches on the defective or same side (i.e. left side in this case) So in trendelenburg test the limb being tested remains off the ground with knee in flexion, hip slight extension and other limb remains plantigrade on the ground with hip and knee in extension.
