## Question

An 11-year-old male with adolescent Blount disease presents to your clinic with a worsening deformity despite bracing. His mechanical axis deviation is -43 mm. He undergoes hemiepiphysiodesis to correct his mechanical axis. At his 4-week follow-up appointment, he presents with the X-ray in Figure A. Which of the following is a primary contributor to this complication?

![[q218702-a-broken-8-plate.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Later onset of disease
2. Increased severity of deformity
3. Solid screws
4. Lower BMI
5. Staple constructs

## Correct option

**2. Increased severity of deformity**

## Answer explanation

This patient presents with adolescent Blount’s disease and has experienced a failure of his hemiepiphysiodesis hardware. Of the listed answer choices, increased severity of deformity is a primary contributor towards hardware failure.

Adolescent Blount’s disease (tibia vara) is thought to be a disease resulting in increased stress on an abnormal medial proximal tibia physis. Onset of the deformity typically occurs between the ages of 4 and 10 with diagnosis of adolescent Blount's occurring after age 10. Blount’s typically affects obese patients and is more common in males. Without correction, persistent angular deformity and abnormal forces across the knee may lead to early osteoarthritis. While proximal tibia vara is the primary deformity, there may also be associated distal femoral varus or valgus as well. The deformity can be described as mild, moderate, or severe on the basis of the mechanical axis deviation (MAD). Mild deformity in the setting of remaining longitudinal growth may be amenable to guided correction via hemiepiphysiodesis, although temporary hemiepiphysiodesis via staples or 8-plates carry the risk of hardware failure, especially in obese patients, those with severe deformity, and those with earlier onset of disease.

*Schroerlucke et al. published a 2009 study describing their experience with the Orthofix 8-plate in the setting of angular deformity correction.* *The group retrospectively reviewed the records of 23 patients with 31 angular deformities of the lower extremities: 16 patients had Blount disease (18 extremities), 2 patients had genu varum from other causes (4 extremities), and 5 had genu valgum (9 extremities).* Implant failure occurred in 8 (26%) of 31 constructs. All 8 failures occurred in patients with Blount disease and involved breakage of the tibial implant’s metaphyseal screw. **The authors found** that all of the failures were in Blount patients, and the constructs consistently failed at the tibial metaphyseal screws.

*Funk et al. published a 2016 study comparing hemiepiphysiodesis implants for late-onset tibia vara and evaluating patient characteristics that may predict surgical failure.* *The authors retrospectively reviewed late-onset tibia vara patients treated with temporary hemiepiphysiodesis.* Surgical failure occurred in 57.9% of patients. Greater BMI and more severe deformity predicted higher rates of surgical failure. Younger age predicted higher rates of implant failure. There were no differences in surgical or implant failure between staple and plate systems.

Figure A is a radiograph of a hemiepiphysiodesis performed using an 8-plate, which has since experienced hardware failure at the inferior screw.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: A younger age at presentation is associated with an increased risk of hardware failure.
Answer 3: Cannulated screws are associated with an increased risk of hardware failure.
Answer 4: Higher BMI is associated with an increased risk of hardware failure.
Answer 5: Staple and screw-plate constructs had equivalent risks of hardware failure.
