## Question

A 17-year-old girl with severe adolescent idiopathic scoliosis requires an osteotomy in the lumbar spine to correct her sagittal imbalance. Figure A shows the pre-operative plan and surgical technique for this procedure. What would be the most common complication with this type of spinal column osteotomy?

![[q3732-a-pso1.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Spinal cord ischemia
2. Descending aorta injury
3. Pseudoathrosis
4. Pseudomeningocele
5. Neurologic deficit

## Correct option

**3. Pseudoathrosis**

## Answer explanation

The procedure shown is a pedicle subtraction osteotomy (PSO). Pseudoarthrosis is the most common complication with this procedure, cited to occur in as many as 25-30% of cases.

PSOs are posterior-based osteotomies that require resection of the posterior elements, pedicles, and decancellation of the vertebral body through a transpedicular corridor. The osteotomy hinges on the anterior column with closure of the middle and posterior columns. PSOs are most often performed in the lumbar spine, usually at L2 or L3. Note, three Smith Peterson osteotomies accomplish approximately what is accomplished with one pedicle subtraction procedure. However, blood loss is greater with a pedicle subtraction procedure.

*Kim et al. retrospectively reviewed 35 patients, 5 years after pedicle subtraction osteotomy for fixed sagittal imbalance.* **The authors found** that ten pseudarthroses (29%) occurred in 8 patients and were revised between 2 and 5 years postoperative. **They concluded** that restoration and maintenance of sagittal vertical axis <8 cm was important to outcome scores.

*Buchowski et al. reviewed the neurologic complications in patients undergoing pedicle subtraction osteotomy (PSO).* *A total of 108 PSOs were performed.* *Intraoperative and postoperative deficits (defined as motor loss of 2 grades or more or loss of bowel/bladder control) were seen in 12 patients (11.1%) and were permanent in 3 patients (2.8%).* *The authors suspected that the deficits were due to a combination of subluxation, residual dorsal impingement, and dural buckling.*

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Spinal cord ischemia is rare after lumbar spine osteotomies.
Answer 2: Major vascular injury such as injury to the aorta is cited at <1-2%.
Answer 4: Pseudomeningocele is not a known complication associated with this procedure.
Answer 5: Neurologic deficit is present in <10% of cases and not nearly as common as post-operative pseudarthrosis.
