## Question

Figure A is the radiograph of a 68-year-old man presents to your office with complaints of elbow pain. He reports that 9 years prior he underwent an interposition arthroplasty for end stage osteoarthritis of the ulnohumeral joint. He now reports that for the two past years he has had increasing pain and elbow instability. There is no clinical evidence of infection, and radiographs show no new bony process. What is the best definitive treatment for this patient?

![[q216478-a-elbow-arthritis.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Conservative care to include physiotherapy
2. Arthrodesis
3. Revision interposition arthroplasty
4. Conversion to distal humerus hemiarthroplasty
5. Conversion to total elbow arthroplasty

## Correct option

**5. Conversion to total elbow arthroplasty**

## Answer explanation

This patient underwent an ulnohumeral distraction interposition arthroplasty for end stage ulnohumeral arthritis at the age of 59 years. Now at the age of 68, with increasing pain and instability, the best definitive treatment would be conversion to a total elbow arthroplasty (TEA).

Elbow arthritis is a degenerative joint disease of the elbow that can be broken into three main types: osteoarthritis, post-traumatic arthritis, and inflammatory arthritis. Treatment may be nonoperative or operative depending on the patient's activity demands, severity of elbow pain, and degree of elbow dysfunction. Ulnohumeral distraction interposition arthroplasty is indicated in young, high demand patients with end stage arthritis (OA, RA, post-traumatic arthritis) who would otherwise have received a TEA if they were older. Indications for a TEA include older patients >65 years with severe elbow arthritis (Larsen stage 3-5), complex distal humerus fracture in elderly with poor bone stock, distal humerus nonunion or malunion in elderly, lower demand post-traumatic arthritis, and failed interposition arthroplasty.

*Blaine et al. reviewed the results of conversion of an interpositional elbow arthroplasty to a semiconstrained total elbow arthroplasty in a series of patients.* **They report** significant improvements in pain, functional outcomes, and patient satisfaction. **They concluded** that semiconstrained total elbow arthroplasty can be performed successfully in patients with a prior interposition arthroplasty with reliable pain relief and a satisfactory results being achieved in most patients.

*Cheng et al. reviewed patients with mobile painful arthritic elbows that were treated by distraction interposition arthroplasty using fascia lata.* **They report** that an elbow distractor/fixator was applied for three to four weeks to separate the articular surfaces and to protect the fascial graft. **They found** that 69% of patients had satisfactory relief from pain, while 62% of patients had an excellent or good result by the objective criteria of the Mayo Elbow Performance score. **They concluded** that although less reliable than prosthetic replacement, distraction interposition arthroplasty is a useful option in the treatment of young, high-demand patients with arthritis of the elbow.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Conservative management in the setting of worsening pain and instability 9 years after an interposition arthroplasty is unlikely to provide significant improvements long-term
Answer 2: Arthrodesis is a salvage procedure and is typically reserved for such cases as a neuropathic (Charcot) joint in which elbow pain and instability persist after having failed conservative management
Answer 3: At the age of 68 years, conversion to a TEA is likely to provide more reliable long-term improvement than revision interposition arthroplasty
Answer 4: With end stage arthritis of the ulnohumeral joint, a TEA is indicated to address the ulnar-sided arthritis
