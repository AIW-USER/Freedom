## Question

A 32-year-old male presents to the emergency department with right-hand pain after being involved in a bar fight, and he is unable to make a composite fist. Injury films can be seen in Figures A and B. What is the most appropriate definitive management for this patient's injury?

![[q217957-a-carpometacarpal-fracture-dislocation.jpg]]
*Figure A*

![[q217957-b-carpometacarpal-fracture-dislocation-3.jpg]]
*Figure B*

## Options

1. Closed reduction and splinting in intrinsic plus position.
2. Fourth carpometacarpal joint fusion
3. Closed reduction and percutaneous pinning.
4. Open reduction and intramedullary fixation.
5. Splinting and observation in resting position.

## Correct option

**3. Closed reduction and percutaneous pinning.**

## Answer explanation

The patient in the vignette sustained a fourth carpometacarpal fracture-dislocation. The recommended treatment for this injury pattern is reduction (closed or open) with surgical fixation.

Fracture-dislocation of carpometacarpal joints comprises about one percent of all hand and wrist injuries. The most common mechanism of injury is forceful hyper-flexion or axial loading of the metacarpal head onto the carpi. A high index of suspicion with a thorough physical examination is warranted as a diagnosis can be missed and delayed due to the high likelihood of severe concomitant injuries. Acute injuries are best treated surgically with reduction and fixation.

*Kato and Fukumoto published a surgical technique article on suspension arthroplasty for old fracture-dislocations of the fifth carpometacarpal joint.* *The authors describe creating a suspension bridge by utilizing a partial slip of the extensor carpi ulnaris tendon.* *The authors write that fracture-dislocations of the fourth carpometacarpal joint should be a contraindication to suspension arthroplasty of the fifth carpometacarpal joint.* Additionally, the authors assert that surgical fixation remains the gold standard for carpometacarpal joint fracture-dislocations.

*Storken et al. reported on a case series of four patients who sustained ulnar carpometacarpal joint dislocations that were subsequently treated with closed reduction and splinting within 8 hours of injury.* **The authors report** that all patients recovered without functional impairment and secondary dislocation.

*Smith et al. published a case report of a patient sustaining traumatic carpometacarpal dislocations of the ulnar four joints.* *The authors discuss the need for acute reduction and stabilization of these injury patterns.*

Figure B: Oblique and lateral radiographs demonstrating fracture with dorsal subluxation of the 4th CMC joint

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1. Closed reduction and splinting is an inappropriate definitive treatment for this unstable fracture pattern
Answer 2. Carpometacarpal joint arthrodesis is the best option for patients with severely comminuted fractures of the base of the metacarpal. Patients with chronic fracture-dislocations may be best treated with carpometacarpal joint arthroplasty.
Answer 4. Intramedullary metacarpal fixation is an appropriate method to fix length-stable metacarpal neck or shaft fractures, but is not an effective method to stabilize a metacarpal carpal fracture dislocation
Answer 5. This will be an inappropriate treatment option for this injury.
