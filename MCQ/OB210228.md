## Question

A 15-year-old male presents to your office complaining of knee pain one day after an injury. On physical examination he exhibits tenderness to palpation over the lateral joint line, has a large effusion, and range of motion is limited. Imaging is shown in Figures A & B. ## Options

1. Physical therapy followed acute/early surgical reconstruction
2. Immediate arthroscopy and surgical reconstruction
3. Physical therapy followed by delayed open reduction and internal fixation of injury
4. Immediate open reduction and internal fixation
5. Physical therapy followed by gradual return to activity

## Correct option

**1. Physical therapy followed acute/early surgical reconstruction**

## Answer explanation

Figures A & B are images consistent with an anterior cruciate ligament (ACL) rupture which is best treated with therapy to regain full range of motion followed by early surgical reconstruction.

The increased incidence of ACL tears among children and adolescents has intensified the debate about optimal treatment. Historically these injuries were treated non-operatively with physical therapy, bracing and activity restriction. Delaying surgery has been found to result in recurrent instability, secondary meniscal damage, and degenerative changes. The preferred treatment is currently physical therapy to regain strength and range of motion, followed by reconstruction of the ACL.

*Anderson et al. performed a study to assess the risk of meniscal and chondral injuries with a delay of ACL reconstruction in children younger than 17 years.* *They were assigned to surgical groups: acute, subacute, and chronic.* Time to surgery had a bivariate association with lateral and medial meniscal tears. Compared with acute reconstruction, subacute and chronic reconstruction patients were at higher risk of sustaining both medial and lateral meniscal tears. **They conclude** that delayed ACL reconstruction increased the risks of secondary meniscal and chondral injuries in this population of pediatric patients.

*Ramski et al. performed a meta-analysis to analyze the aggregated data from the literature to determine if a benefit exists for either nonoperative or early operative treatment for ACL injuries in the pediatric patient.* **They found** that early surgical stabilization led to better outcomes when compared to nonoperative treatment. Patients after nonoperative and delayed treatment experienced more instability/pathological laxity and inability to return to previous activity levels than did patients treated with early surgical stabilization.

Figure A is a lateral radiograph demonstrating the deep sulcus sign of the lateral femoral condyle which is a depression on the lateral femoral condyle at the terminal sulcus, a junction between the weight bearing tibial articular surface and the patellar articular surface of the femoral condyle. Figure B is a T2-weighted sagittal MRI demonstrating bone bruising in the middle third of the lateral femoral condyle and the posterior third of the lateral tibial plateau, which is seen in a rupture of the ACL. Illustration A demonstrates how the characteristic bone bruise pattern develops after rupturing the ACL. Illustration B has a red arrow indicating the deep sulcus sign.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 2, 3, 4, & 5: The preferred treatment for ruptures of the ACL is physical therapy to restore strength and range of motion, followed by surgical reconstruction.
