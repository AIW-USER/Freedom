Chapter: Pediatric Orthopedics MCQs — Sprengel's Deformity

## Question

Cavendish classification is used for (FNB 2021)

## Options

1. Congenital elevation of scapula
2. Congenital muscular torticollis
3. Congenital dislocation of radial head
4. Congenital pseudoarthrosis of the clavicle

## Correct option

**1. Congenital elevation of scapula**

## Answer explanation

Congenital elevation of the scapula or ‘Sprengel’s shoulder’ is an anomaly of the shoulder girdle that is associated with abnormal descent, and altered position and anatomy of the scapula in relation to the thoracic cage. The deformity is usually associated with hypoplastic scapula, muscle hypoplasia or atrophy, and a combination of these factors results in disfigurement and functional limitation of the shoulder.

Associated anomalies: Other congenital anomalies may be present, such as cervical ribs, malformations of ribs, and anomalies of the cervical vertebrae (Klippel Feil syndrome); rarely, one or more scapular muscles are partly or completely absent. Syndromes associated with Sprengel’s deformity include teratological conditions such as iniencephaly (a triad of occipital defect, spina bifida of cervical vertebrae, and fixed retroflexion of the head) and the Klippel-Feil syndrome.

In about half of patients, an extra ossicle, the omovertebral bone, is present; this is a rhomboidal plaque of cartilage and bone lying in a strong fascial sheath that extends from the superior angle of the scapula to the spinous process, lamina, or transverse process of one or more lower cervical vertebrae

Demographics:

Age – Mostly noticed at birth

Gender – Equal distribution in both sexes

Side – Left side more common than right, bilateral only in 10%

Clinical presentation:

In KFS, most commonly the cervical fusion is between C2-C3 vertebras (71.0 %), followed by C5-C6 (67.7 %), C6-C7 (67.6 %), and C3-C4 (29.0 %)

Klippel – Feil syndrome: KFS, also known as synostosis of cervical spine is a rare condition, presented by fusion of the cervical vertebrae due to failure of normal segmentation of the cervical somites during embryological development. KFS is characterized by classical triad found in around 50% of the patients and consisting of a short neck, low posterior hairline and restricted neck mobility.

Etiology: Maternal alcoholism -50% incidence of cervical vertebral fusions has been found on radiographs of infants with fetal alcohol syndrome. Familial Klippel-Feil syndrome gene locus has been identified on the long arm of chromosome 8.

Cervical spine involvement: apart from cervical fusion occipitalization of the atlas, hemivertebrae, and basilar impression occur frequently in patients with Klippel-Feil syndrome.

Other associations: Scoliosis is seen in 60 % of cases (Most frequent orthopedic complication) renal abnormalities, ventricular septal defect and other cardiac anomalies, deafness, synkinesis (mirror movements), Respiratory Anomalies (Failure of lobe formation, Ectopic lungs, Restriction of lung function by shortened trunk, scoliosis, rib fusion, or deformed costovertebral joints). Sprengel deformity is seen in 20 % cases.

Treatment: in symptomatic patients’ surgical stabilization (posterior fusion of C3-C7) with or without decompression may be required.

Answer: 1
