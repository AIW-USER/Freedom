## Question

A 52-year-old female presents to your clinic for follow-up regarding her left bunion deformity. She has undergone a prior surgery to correct her deformity. She now complains of pain under the second and third metatarsal heads that has been present for the past month. She states "it feels like I have a rock in my shoe." Examination reveals tenderness to palpation with keratosis of the plantar aspects of the second and third metatarsal heads. Which of the following previous surgical procedures, if performed incorrectly, may have contributed to this patient's current presentation?

## Options

1. Cotton osteotomy
2. Akin osteotomy
3. Lateral sesamoidectomy
4. Modified McBride procedure
5. Modified Lapidus procedure

## Correct option

**5. Modified Lapidus procedure**

## Answer explanation

This patient presents with signs and symptoms of transfer metatarsalgia of the 2nd and 3rd metatarsal heads. Transfer metatarsalgia is a potential complication associated with an incorrectly performed modified Lapidus procedure if the first ray is shortened.

*Lapidus originally described a fusion between the bases of the first and second metatarsal and the first intercuneiform joint.* A modified Lapidus procedure involves fusion of the first tarsometatarsal (TMT) joint with concomitant distal soft tissue procedure, often a modified McBride.

If the first ray is shortened after a modified Lapidus procedure, a greater amount of the weight-bearing load is distributed to the lesser metatarsals, causing increased stress and pain known as transfer metatarsalgia. This complication can be avoided by reducing bone loss with minimal bony resection of the first metatarsal base. Other boney procedures involving the first metatarsal, such as a Chevron osteotomy, may lead to metatarsalgia if the first ray is shortened. Transfer metatarsalgia may also occur following a modified Lapidus if a dorsiflexion malunion of the first metatarsal occurs.

*Li et al. reviewed the modified Lapidus procedure surgical indications, techniques, and complications.* **They report** that malunion, nonunion, and shortening of the first ray are considerable complications associated with this procedure. **They concluded** that improved understanding of first TMT joint biomechanics, as well as the use of certain fixation methods like nitinol staples, have strengthened TMT joint fixation, and may prevent complications such as transfer metatarsalgia.

*Thompson et al. reviewed fusion rates of the first TMT joint after modified Lapidus procedure.* **They report** a nonunion rate of 4% following this procedure. **They noted** that risk factors for nonunion included previous bunion surgery or recurrent deformity. **They concluded** the modified Lapidus procedure is a useful technique in the treatment of hallux valgus deformity with first TMT arthritis or hypermobility of the first ray.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: A Cotton, or plantarflexion medial cuneiform osteotomy, is used to correct forefoot varus in flatfoot deformity. Transfer metatarsalgia is uncommon following this procedure.
Answer 2: An Akin, or proximal phalanx medial closing wedge osteotomy, is unlikely to cause transfer metatarsalgia.
Answer 3: Lateral sesamoidectomy has largely been abandoned due to the risk of hallux varus associated with this procedure and is unlikely to cause transfer metatarsalgia.
Answers 4: Soft tissue procedures alone, such as the modified McBride, do not cause shortening of the metatarsal ray and would not lead to transfer metatarsalgia.
