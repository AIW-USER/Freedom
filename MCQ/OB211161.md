## Question

A 21-year-old collegiate distance runner presents with continued right hip and groin pain despite a 1-month cessation from running and impact activities. The pain bothers him at all times of the day. Ibuprofen has helped tremendously but he is taking it nearly four times a day. On exam, he endorses pain with flexion to 110 degrees but none with log-roll or in the flexed, adducted, and internally rotated position. ## Options

1. Diagnostic intra-articular corticosteroid injection
2. MR arthrogram
3. MRI
4. Percutaneous radiofrequency ablation
5. Physical therapy focusing on eccentric strengthening and core stability

## Correct option

**4. Percutaneous radiofrequency ablation**

## Answer explanation

This is a classic presentation for an osteoid osteoma, which would best be treated with radiofrequency ablation.

Osteoid osteomas are common among patients in their teens and twenties, often causing night pain that is relieved by NSAIDs. These lesions can have variable symptomatology and cause significant delays in diagnosis, with some studies showing a mean of 14 months. These lesions were traditionally resected surgically but radiofrequency ablation has shown high rates of success with low rates of recurrence.

*Boscainos et al. discuss the natural history of osteoid osteomas.* These lesions are self-limited and resolve in 6-15 years, but this may be expedited to 2-3 years with NSAID use. If these lesions are not well-tolerated, radiofrequency ablation, thermocoagulation, or trephine excision may be performed.

*Klontzas et al. reviewed MR imaging of a series of patients with hip pain of unknown cause.* **They found** bone marrow edema that formed a half-moon shape in all cases of osteoid osteomas in the femoral neck. This half-moon sign had 100% sensitivity and 95% specificity and is a useful diagnostic tool.

Figure A is an AP radiograph showing sclerosis of the medial femoral neck. Figure B is an axial CT showing characteristic nidus of osteoid osteoma. Illustration A a coronal T2 MRI sequence showing the half-moon sign described by Klontzas et al.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Diagnostic injections are helpful to delineate pain generators when multiple pathologies are present, but his symptoms and imaging are consistent with an osteoid osteoma
Answers 2 and 3: Either of these would be reasonable if you have a high suspicion for intra-articular pathology or a stress fracture.
Answer 5: While physical therapy has no detrimental effect, the osteoid osteoma should be addressed first.
