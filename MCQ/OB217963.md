## Question

You are performing an anatomic total shoulder arthroplasty on a 71-year-old female. Pre-operatively, you note there are several large osteophytes creating deformity along the inferomedial aspect of the humeral neck. During your approach, you attempt to perform an aggressive capsular release to maximize your exposure but the humeral neck osteophytes are obscuring the normal anatomy and making this process difficult. Using the inferior border of the subscapularis as a landmark in these cases can be helpful to prevent injury to what structure(s)?

## Options

1. Axillary nerve
2. Musculocutaneous nerve
3. Supraspinatus tendon
4. Conjoint tendon
5. Pectoralis major tendon

## Correct option

**1. Axillary nerve**

## Answer explanation

The inferior border of the subscapularis tendon can be used as a landmark for identifying and protecting the axillary nerve as it passes from anteriorly to posteriorly inferior to the glenohumeral joint.

Capsular release during total shoulder arthroplasty (TSA) is important to gain exposure and improve post-operative range of motion. As the capsule is released along the humeral neck, the risk of neurovascular damage can increase, principally to the axillary nerve as it traverses from anterior to posterior underneath the glenohumeral joint and through the quadrangular space posteriorly. The inferior border of the subscapularis tendon can provide a frame of reference to identify the axillary nerve during this procedure and avoid injury.

*Nove-Josserand et al. discussed exposure of the glenoid in shoulder arthroplasty,* **noting** that inferior glenohumeral release is an important stem allowing the humerus to be retracted backwards/downwards to allow for glenoid exposure. **They also note** that osteophyte resection, partial pectoralis major release and posterior capsulectomy can be used to improve exposure in difficult cases.

*Walley et al. described the anatomic safe zone of the lower subscapular nerve during an open deltopectoral approach.* **They found** that the safe zone of the subscapular nerve was within a range 32mm medial to the conjoint tendon. Further, the safe zone was within 10mm inferior and and 15mm medial to the axillary nerve.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: The musculocutaneous nerve courses from the lateral cord of the brachial plexus, just medial to the coracoid process; the subscapularis would not provide a valuable anatomic reference.
Answer 3: The supraspinatus tendon attaches superior to the subscapularis, at the greater tuberosity.
Answer 4: The conjoint tendon attaches to the coracoid process and is oriented vertically.
Answer 5: The pectoralis major tendon attaches to the intertubercular groove of the proximal humerus, and the upper 1/3 of the tendinous attachment can safely be taken down to improve exposure during TSA.
