Chapter: Musculoskeletal Infections MCQs — Tuberculosis of Hip,Knee and Peripheral Joints

## Question

False statement regarding TB hip

## Options

1. TB hip joint mostly ends in fibrous ankylosis
2. TB hip is second most common form of osteoarticular TB
3. Shanmugasundaram classification only applies to pediatric hip TB
4. In advanced stage Iliofemoral ligament involvement leads to FABER deformity

## Correct option

**3. Shanmugasundaram classification only applies to pediatric hip TB**

## Answer explanation

Ans: C
EXPLANATION: -
The first two statements are fully correct. Regarding last statement, Iliofemoral ligament prevents hip extension and external rotation. In advanced stage of TB hip arthritis normally there is FADIR deformity but complete iliofemoral ligament resorption can very well lead to FABER deformity. So best pick is choice C.

Classifications of TB Hip
The process evolved over time from a morphological x ray based pattern to a clinico-radiological combined pattern in an attempt to better align with clinical management principles.
Morphological classification: -
Historically, in 1983, Shanmugasundaram introduced a radiological classification for hip TB based on findings seen on plain x-rays, which was applicable for lesions in “both children and adults”. His viewpoint was that the radiological presentation of the hip ultimately forecasts the final clinical outcome. With this regards, morphological types based on the destructive pattern were classified into seven types;
- Normal: disorder chiefly includes synovium.

- Travelling/wandering acetabulum: Here the lesion is in the roof. The joint space is narrowed, with the progressive upward displacement of the femoral head.

- Dislocating type: In the 'dislocating' type, the head dislocates posteriorly, or subluxes because of laxity of ligaments and capsular distension.

- Perthes type: the epiphysis here is fragmented, dense and flattened. The neck of the femoral is broadened. It is similar to Perthes' disease and is likely due to embolic episodes.

- Protrusio acetabuli: the lesion here is frequently on the floor of the acetabulum. With progression, medial displacement of the floor occurs through femoral head pressure.

Agarwal et al observed that some hip involvements cannot be classifiable by traditional types. Consequently, 8th type- “Unclassified” was added to Shanmugasundaram radiological classification.
In the unclassified type, three different patterns were added: triradiate, pseudarthrosis coxae and ankylosed. The Triradiate pattern has a prime focus on the acetabular floor. The lesion develops and persists initially confined to the lower acetabulum. Rest of the hip remains unaffected. The pseudarthrosis coxae pattern is likely a consequence of the primary femoral epiphyseal focus resulting in the complete loss of femoral head and occasionally the femoral neck. This pattern indicates loss of cervicocephalic articulation. It is disparate from the dislocating type because it lacks a femoral head. The third pattern, the ankylosed hip is an old or healing disease with ankylosis.

Clinico-radiological classifications: -
Although TB hip is marked by painful limp, there can be deformity, shortening of the limb, swelling, pathological dislocations, and sinuses developing over the course of the disease. So, to integrate the clinical spectrum with radiological picture and stage them together, in 2002, Babhulkar and Pande proposed a novel classification, coupling the clinical stages of TB with radiological picture.

![[TO21733218-9.jpg]]

In the same year, Tuli et al introduced a minor modification to this classification presenting the final modified classification as the newest classification of the research era.

![[TO21733218-10.jpg]]

By now, the two patterns of classification were well in use across the world. While the morphological Shanmugasundram classification was all about destructive patterns, clinic-radiological classifications were much better linked to disease progression. In 2012, Moon et al. presented their working classification. They classified hip TB in children with regards to joint stability, cephalocotyloid morphology and its anatomical relationship and then compared their classification with the traditional Shanmugasundram classification. They elucidated that the hips in Perthes type, atrophic hip, and protrusio acetabuli were stable, whereas, the travelling acetabulum, mortar and pestle, and dislocating hip types were unstable.
