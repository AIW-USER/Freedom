## Options

1. Fourth metatarsophalangeal joint (MTPJ) fusion
2. Fourth ray resection
3. Brief period of immobilization and NSAIDs
4. Extra-depth shoe modification
5. Fourth metatarsal lengthening

## Correct option

**4. Extra-depth shoe modification**

## Answer explanation

The patient has 4th metatarsal brachymetatarsia which has become symptomatic. Initial treatment would be conservative management with extra-depth or wide toe shoes which generally resolves most symptoms.

Brachymetatarsia is a congenital hypoplasia of one or more metatarsals. Shortening of the fourth metatarsal is the most common form of brachymetatarsia and is often bilateral. Shoe modifications, such as extra-depth or extra-wide shoes, generally will improve symptoms and should be the first step in management. Taping and manipulative reduction attempts are ineffective, and extensor tenotomy and capsulotomy are not likely to sufficiently correct the deformity. If pressure and trauma persist, metatarsal lengthening or amputation may be indicated.

*Calis et al. review the functional outcomes of 7 patients who underwent distraction for the correction of bilateral congenital brachymetatarsia.* **The authors note** that many patients complain of forefoot pain which may limit their daily activities. **The authors conclude** that, when indicated, distraction appears to be an effective and safe approach for the simultaneous correction of the shortness of the first ray and medial angulation of the great toe.

*Jones et al. performed a systematic review of the 3 surgical techniques used to treat brachymetatarsia: single-stage lengthening with a bone graft, callus distraction, or a combination of bone grafting and callus distraction.* The incidence of major postoperative complications after callus distraction, single-stage lengthening, and the combination procedure was 12%, 4%, and 33%, respectively. The healing time was 16 weeks, 9 weeks, and 14 weeks for callus distraction, single-stage lengthening, and the combination procedure, respectively. **The authors conclude** that the single-stage lengthening with bone graft was associated with fewer complications and faster healing times than callus distraction but with lesser gains in length.

Figure A through C are the AP, oblique and lateral foot radiographs that reveal 4th metatarsal brachymetatarsia. Illustration A shows an example of metatarsal lengthening with distraction osteogenesis and external fixation.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: MTPJ arthrodesis is ineffective in treatment of symptomatic brachymetatarsia.
Answer 2: Fourth ray resection would be a form of treatment for a patient with brachymetatarsia who has failed conservative management.
Answer 3: Extra-depth or extra-wide shoes are the preferred first management for the treatment of symptomatic brachymetatarsia.
Answer 5: Fourth metatarsal lengthening would be the treatment of choice for a patient who has failed conservative management.
