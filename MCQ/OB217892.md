## Question

A 72-year-old male presents to the emergency department after he felt a sudden pop in his right hip with the inability to ambulate. A review of his recent labs show cobalt level of 15.2 µg/L (normal: 0.7-3.4 µg/L) and chromium level of 3.1 µg/L (normal: 0.5-2.5 µg/L). His presenting radiograph is shown in figure A. Which of the following contributed to this patient's underlying condition?

![[q217892-a-xr-only.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Failure to observe posterior hip precautions in the acute post-operative period
2. Isolated osteolysis secondary to macrophage activation
3. Mechanically assisted crevice corrosion
4. Metal on metal total hip arthroplasty implant
5. Seeding of the joint with staphylococcus aureus

## Correct option

**3. Mechanically assisted crevice corrosion**

## Answer explanation

Clinically significant trunnionosis is uncommon and results from mechanically assisted crevice corrosion (MACC; combined fretting/crevice) at the head-neck junction of hip arthroplasty implants. In severe cases, trunnionosis can lead to pseudotumor formation and catastrophic implant failure requiring revision surgery.

Trunnionosis, if left untreated, results in an adverse reaction to metal debris (ARMD) and an aseptic lymphocyte-dominant vasculitis-associated lesion (ALVAL; also referred to as pseudotumor). Clinically, this pseudotumor formation typically occurs in one of two settings: (1) trunnionosis or (2) metal-on-metal (MOM) articulating hip replacements.

*Weiser et al. discuss the importance of recognizing clinically important trunnionosis early to prevent catastrophic failure.* *They review causes related to the patient, implant, and surgeon-specific factors.* *Patients demonstrate a differential elevation of serum Co to Cr ratio on the order of approximately 5:1, as compared to the relative 1:1 ratio seen in MOM implants.* **They conclude** by suggesting the avoidance of mixed metal bearing implants and paying meticulous attention to the intraoperative assembly of the head on the trunnion.

*Kwon et al. proposed a risk stratification algorithm in the setting of MOM arthroplasties.* *They stress the importance of obtaining a full systematic workup of patients with symptomatic MOM arthroplasties.* **They conclude** that these results can delineate other causes of the patient’s painful total hip and stratify whether the patient should continue being monitored versus needing revision surgery.

Figure A (left) shows AP imaging of a right hip with catastrophic hardware failure. There is significant wear along the femoral neck with a tapered appearance consistent with late-stage trunnionosis.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Failing to observe posterior hip precautions in the acute postoperative period has been assumed to place patients at risk for early postoperative dislocation. However, some newer trials have brought this into question. This patient did not dislocate their hip, rather, had a catastrophic failure of the implant's head-neck junction.
Answer 2: Osteolysis secondary to macrophage activation does play a role in the prolonged setting of trunnionosis and is seen in this patient. However, it is not the isolated cause of this patient’s symptoms.
Answer 4: Metal on metal total hip arthroplasty implants are classically associated with ARMD and pseudotumor formation. However, this patient does NOT have a metal on metal hip. The lucent appearance between the metal femoral head and the acetabular cup indicates a liner, which was made of polyethylene in this patient’s case.
Answer 5: Seeding of joint with staphylococcus aureus would result in a periprosthetic joint infection (PJI). It is important to note that it is possible to have a superimposed PJI on top of trunnionosis, however, that was not indicated in this patient’s case.
