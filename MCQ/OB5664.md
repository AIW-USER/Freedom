## Options

1. Patients often present afebrile, with mild pain, and a normal WBC count
2. Radiographs characteristically show a solitary, localized radiolucency with sclerotic margins
3. Most lesions are metaphyseal
4. Treatment consist of wide surgical resection and chemotherapy
5. Treatment consists of surgical debridement and postoperative antibiotics

## Correct option

**4. Treatment consist of wide surgical resection and chemotherapy**

## Answer explanation

The clinical presentation and investigations are consistent with Brodie’s abscess. Children with Brodie’s abscess often appear less ill and most frequently present afebrile, with mild pain, elevated ESR, and a normal WBC count. The infection is best treated with surgical debridement and postoperative antibiotics.

Brodie’s abscess is a form of subacute pyogenic osteomyelitis. As a result, children typically have milder pain, display less toxicity, and present later than children with acute osteomyelitis. It is most commonly found in the metaphysis and characterized by a localized purulent collection surrounded by a sclerotic wall that usually responds well to surgical debridement and postoperative antibiotics.

*Kanoun et al. present a retrospective series of 20 patients successfully treated for an isolated bone abscess with surgery, cast immobilization, and antibiotics.* Erythrocyte sedimentation rate was normalized within 10 weeks and there were no recurrences.

*Dormans et al. review the presentation, diagnosis, and treatment of pediatric hematogenous osteomyelitis.* Authors note that there is now wider recognition of subacute and chronic bone infections with advances in imaging techniques (bone scanning and magnetic resonance imaging) as well as improved understanding of the disease have led to improved management.

*Blickman et al. review the acute, subacute, and chronic forms of osteomyelitis and contrast it with available imaging modalities.* *They describe Brodie’s abscess characteristically as a lucent well-circumscribed lesion in the metaphyses of long bones with well-defined dense margins, most commonly seen in the tibia and femur.*

Figure A is a radiograph showing a well-circumscribed lesion in the distal metaphysis of a pediatric left ankle with sclerotic borders, consistent with Brodie's abscess. Figures B and C are low and high power histologic sections, respectively, showing subacute inflammation, a mixture of lymphocytes and plasma cells as well as fibrosis. Illustration A shows a labeled high power histology section with degenerate bone, fibrosis, neutrophils, and plasma cells.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Patients with Brodie's abscess have been found to present most often afebrile, with only mild pain, and a normal WBC count. ESR however is often elevated.
Answer 2: Radiographically, Brodie's abscess appears as a well-circumscribed, solitary radiolucent lesion with dense, well-defined margins.
Answer 3: Purportedly due to the increased metaphyseal vascularity, Brodie's abscess and other subsets of osteomyelitis are more common in the metaphyses of the tibia and femur.
Answer 4: Surgical resection and chemotherapy is the typical treatment for some malignant tumors of the bone. While surgical resection is appropriate for chronic sequestered infection within the bone, antibiotics and not chemotherapy are appropriate post-operatively.
