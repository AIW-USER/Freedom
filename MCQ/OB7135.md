## Question

The hip joint allows motion in different anatomic planes. How many degrees of freedom does the hip joint provide and in which anatomic planes?

## Options

1. 2 degrees of freedom in compression and tension
2. 2 degrees of freedom in flexion and extension
3. 3 degrees of freedom in flexion-extension, abduction-adduction, and axial rotation
4. 3 degrees of freedom in anterior-posterior translation, medial-lateral translation, and axial motion
5. 6 degrees of freedom in flexion-extension, abduction-adduction, axial rotation, anterior-posterior translation, medial-lateral translation, and axial motion

## Correct option

**3. 3 degrees of freedom in flexion-extension, abduction-adduction, and axial rotation**

## Answer explanation

Degrees of freedom of a joint are the set of independent rotations and displacements that the joint allows. The hip is a ball-on-socket joint and, as such, allows all rotations but completely resists or "constrains" all translations. In anatomic terms, the hip joint provides three degrees of freedom: flexion-extension (rotation in the sagittal plane), abduction-adduction (rotation in the frontal plane), and axial rotation (rotation in the transverse plane).
