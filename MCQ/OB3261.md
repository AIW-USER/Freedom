## Question

A 29-year-old male volleyball player presents with a one year history of right shoulder weakness and deep aching pain. He denies any history of trauma or prior shoulder problems. A clinical photograph and representative sagittal MRI image are shown in Figures A and B respectively. He is diagnosed with a ganglion cyst of the shoulder. Based on the images provided, where is the cyst located?

*Figure A*

![[q3261-b-r-should-sagittal-mri.jpg]]

## Options

1. Suprascapular notch
2. Spinoglenoid notch
3. Quadrangular space
4. Subscapular recess
5. Triangular interval

## Correct option

**2. Spinoglenoid notch**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a spinoglenoid notch cyst which is causing compression of the distal branches of the suprascapular nerve and the isolated infraspinatus atrophy shown in Figures A and B.

Suprascapular nerve innervation to the supraspinatous m. occurs before the nerve enters the spinoglenoid notch, and therefore cysts occuring at the spinoglenoid notch cause denervation and atrophy of the infraspinatous only. Ganglion cysts more proximally at the suprascapular notch affect both the supraspinatous and infraspinatous, shown in Illustration A. Suprascapular nerve compression can often result in vague shoulder pain and muscle weakness, particularly with overhead activities.

Safran's review article discusses the anatomy, pathophysiology, presentation, and treatment of suprascapular and axillary nerve injuries about the shoulder in athletes.

*Cummins et al provide a Current Concepts Review which summarizes the relevant literature to date regarding the pathoanatomy of the suprascapular nerve and its clinical relevance.*

Illustration C shows the MRI image with the rotator cuff muscles labelled: 1=supraspinatus, 2=infraspinatus showing atrophy, 3=teres minor, 4=subscapularis.

Incorrect Answers:
1:Suprascapular nerve innervation to the supraspinatous m. occurs before the nerve enters the spinoglenoid notch, and therefore cysts occuring at the spinoglenoid notch cause denervation and atrophy of the infraspinatous only.
3:The quadrangular space contains the axillary nerve and posterior humeral circumflex artery. Compression at this location would not affect the infraspinatous muscle.
4: The subscapular recess is anterior to the subscapularis and inferior to the coracoid and often harbors intraarticular loose bodies.
5:The contents of the triangular interval include the radial nerve and profunda brachii artery. The anatomic boundaries of this space and interval are shown in Illustration B. Compression at this location would not affect the infraspinatous muscle.
