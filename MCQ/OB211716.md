## Question

An 87-year-old male presents with left hip pain after a fall while going to the bathroom at night. His injury is demonstrated in Figure A. Which of the following treatment factors has been associated with decreased patient morbidity?

![[q211716-a-ap-pelvis.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Preoperative echocardiogram
2. Early weight-bearing as tolerated and mobility
3. Use of internal fixation (IF) over arthroplasty
4. Use of general over neuraxial anesthesia
5. Post-op patient controlled analgesia (PCA) pain control

## Correct option

**2. Early weight-bearing as tolerated and mobility**

## Answer explanation

Early weight-bearing to tolerance and early mobilization have been associated with reduced patient morbidity and reduced postoperative complications.

Femoral neck fractures in geriatric patients are associated with relatively high mortality rates one year after injury. Early surgical treatment with immediate mobilization is paramount for reducing inpatient mortality and postoperative complications. In the geriatric population, ideal treatment consists of hemiarthroplasty or total hip arthroplasty (THA) due to the lower reoperation rates and increased functional status compared to IF. Postoperative pain control regimens that minimize opioid use and avoidance of general anesthesia can increase postoperative mobility and recovery.

*Bernstein et al. retrospectively studied 258 geriatric hip fractures for the influence of preoperative testing on hospital stay, time to surgery, influence on treatment decisions.* **They reported** that 27% of the patients underwent preoperative testing and resulted in a >48-hour time-to-surgery in 63% of the patients compared to 20% of the patients without testing and a 3 day longer hospital stay. **The authors concluded** that preoperative testing is associated with greater time to surgery, increased length of hospital stay, and did not affect treatment decisions.

*Chammout et al. performed a randomized controlled trial in 100 patients with displaced intracapsular femoral neck fractures treated with THA or IF.* *At 17 years follow-up,* **they reported** Harris Hip Scores to be 14.7 points higher in the total hip arthroplasty cohort and a significantly lower risk for re-operation (23% versus 53%) compared to the IF group. **They concluded** that THA provided better function and decreased re-operation risk in elderly patients with femoral neck fractures.

*Kubiak et al. reviewed early weight-bearing after lower extremity fractures.* **They stated** the literature supports early weight-bearing with low risk of complications in patients treated for ankle fractures, femoral shaft fractures, femoral neck fractures, and intertrochanteric fractures. However, periarticular fractures are better treated with protected weight-bearing or delayed weight-bearing.

Figure A is an AP pelvis radiograph with a displaced and varus-angulated left femoral neck fracture.

Incorrect answers
Answer 1: Preoperative echocardiograms have been associated with delay of definitive surgical treatment which has been associated with increased patient morbidity with prolonged preoperative bed rest.
Answer 3: IF is associated with decreased functional status and increased re-operation rates in this patient population.
Answer 4: Use of a general anesthetic can lead to increased mental status changes and delirium episodes which can lead to increased postoperative complications.
Answer 5: Use of opioid pain medications is associated with increased delirium and decreased ability to work with physical therapy. Postoperative pain regimens minimizing reliance on opioids can mitigate these issues.
