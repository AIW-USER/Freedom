## Question

Knee joint is an example of( general orthopedics- anatomy, basic science and pathology)

## Options

1. Ginglymus
2. Condyloid
3. Saddle
4. pivot

## Correct option

**1. Ginglymus**

## Answer explanation

Body joints can be divided in three main types: Fibrous, Cartilaginous and Synovial

1. Fibrous joints (synarthrosis): These are immobile joints and are bound by tough fibrous tissues. They are further sub classified into sutures, gomphoses and syndesmoses.

Sutures (Synarthrosis): Immovable joints ex. bones of the skull.

There is little movement till 18-20 years of age when these joints fuse.

Gomphoses: Immovable joints, between teeth and their socket

Syndesmoses: Slightly movable. Two bones are held together by an interosseous membrane. e.g. the middle radio-ulnar and middle tibiofibular joint.

2. Cartilaginous (amphiarthrosis): Here bones are attached by fibrocartilage or hyaline cartilage. These joints allow for only limited mobility. There are two main types – primary cartilaginous (synchondroses) and secondary cartilaginous (symphyses) joints.

Synchondroses

Two bones are joined by hyaline cartilage. Temporary joints in the epiphyseal plates in children. First rib attachment to sternum (Other costal cartilages are joined to sternum by synovial joints)

Symphyses: Two bones are joined by fibrocartilage. e.g. two pubic bones are joined by pubic symphysis with interpubic disc. Two vertebrae are joined by an intervertebral disc.

3. Synovial joints: These are the main joints of the body. They are filled with synovial fluid and provide huge movement (A synovial joint is a joint filled with synovial fluid). These joints tend to be fully moveable (known as diarthroses). These joints are further classified according to their movement.

Ball & Socket Permits movement in several axes; a rounded head fits into a concavity. e.g. glenohumeral joint, hip joint

Hinge (ginglymus): Permits movement in one axis only i.e. flexion and extension. e.g. elbow, knee and IP joints.

Saddle: Concave and convex joint surfaces unite at saddle joints, e.g. CM joint of thumb

Plane: Permit gliding or sliding movements, e.g. Acromioclavicular joint

Pivot: Allows rotation due to movements between a round bony process and a socket. e.g, the atlantoaxial joint & proximal radio-ulnar joint

Condyloid (ellipsoidal): Permits flexion, extension, adduction, abduction and circumduction e.g. Metacarpophalangeal joint, wrist joint.

Answer: 1
