## Question

## Options

1. Continue nonoperative management with additional hip abduction brace
2. Open reduction internal fixation of the posterior acetabulum alone
3. Open reduction internal fixation of the femoral head and posterior acetabulum
4. Open reduction internal fixation of the posterior acetabulum and total hip arthroplasty
5. Total hip arthroplasty alone

## Correct option

**4. Open reduction internal fixation of the posterior acetabulum and total hip arthroplasty**

## Answer explanation

This patient presents with an acute hip dislocation with fractures of the acetabular posterior wall and femoral head. After reduction, the most appropriate treatment in the older population includes open reduction internal fixation (ORIF) of the acetabulum with total hip arthroplasty (THA) during the same procedure.

Acetabular fractures in the elderly population are complex injuries with various treatment options. While ORIF is the mainstay of treatment in the younger population, preexisting arthritis and concomitant injuries make acute ORIF with combined THA an attractive treatment option in older patients. Elderly acetabular fractures have poor outcomes with ORIF alone when there is posterior wall comminution. These patients can be safely and appropriately treated with single-stage acetabular ORIF and THA.

*Butterwick and colleagues provided a comprehensive review regarding the management of acetabular fractures in the elderly.* **They discuss** that radiographic signs associated with a poor outcome include posterior wall comminution, marginal impaction, the so-called gull sign representing superomedial roof impaction, hip dislocation, impaction injuries to the femoral head, and preexisting arthritic changes. They stress that during fixation prior to THA, the goal is not to achieve anatomic reduction, but rather to reduce large fragments and/or both columns such that they will provide stability to the acetabular component. **The authors conclude** that ORIF alone for elderly acetabular fractures can provide acceptable outcomes if restoration of a concentric and congruent joint is achievable, however, there remains a high (22%) conversion rate to total hip arthroplasty within the first two years.

*Daurka and colleagues performed a systematic review of acetabular fractures in patients over the age of 55.* Interestingly, **they found** that associated both-column variants were the most frequent fracture pattern (23-26%) in the elderly followed by the more classically known anterior column-posterior hemitransverse pattern (15-19%). **They conclude** by discussing various treatment options and expected outcomes, citing overall high complication rates and mortality in acetabular fractures in the older population.

Figure A shows an acetabular fracture secondary to acute hip dislocation. Figures B and C are post-reduction CT images showing the comminuted femoral head fracture and a large posterior wall fragment with marginal impaction.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1. It would be inappropriate to pursue nonoperative management with an additional hip abduction brace in a baseline ambulatory patient who works.
Answer 2. Open reduction internal fixation of the posterior acetabulum alone would not address the comminuted femoral head fracture.
Answer 3. Open reduction internal fixation of the femoral head and posterior acetabulum would be considered appropriate in a young patient. However, given this patient's age and high risk for progression to AVN, she should be treated with ORIF of the acetabulum and THA to provide the most comprehensive, single-operation treatment.
Answer 5. Total hip arthroplasty alone would not be appropriate without adequate fixation of the acetabulum to facilitate the cup component.
