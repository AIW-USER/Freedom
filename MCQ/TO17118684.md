## Question

Your resident operated right knee of a 28 years old male and did an ACL reconstruction. After preparing the lateral wall, he couldn’t see much of remnants, so he centred femoral tunnel 10 mm anterior to the intercondylar ridge at 11’0 clock position. Native ACL footprint on the tibial side was visible, so tibial tunnel was centred on native footprint only. Rest of the surgery was successfully completed without creating any significant complications. What physical findings you expect in the patient on post op clinical examination? (NEW)

## Options

1. . Negative pivot shift and knee ROM 0-110o
2. Positive pivot shift and knee ROM 0-70o
3. Negative pivot shift and knee ROM 0-130o
4. Positive pivot shift and knee ROM 20-120o

## Correct option

**2. Positive pivot shift and knee ROM 0-70o**

## Answer explanation

Ans: B

Anatomical ACL reconstruction is defined as the functional restoration of the ACL to its native dimensions, collagen orientation and insertion sites and for achieving this goal, ideal positioning of bone tunnels in ACL reconstruction is the most critical step. Hence, one must know the appropriate landmarks to avoid tunnel mal-placement.

Ideally, the remnants of broken ligament, if visible is taken as the gold standard for tunnel placement, which mostly is feasible in fresh injuries. In chronic tears, the remnants are mostly resorbed and one has to depend on nearby bony landmarks.

For FEMORAL tunnel

Ideal placement of the femoral tunnel should be between 9:30 to 10 o’clock on the lateral wall of a right knee (Fig A) with the knee flexed to 90o, leaving approximately 2 mm of bone between the tunnel and the posterior (edge) wall of the femur. In AP dimension, centre your tunnel on bifurcate ridge (see Fig B, blue circle).

For TIBIAL tunnel

Centre the tunnel in between the tibial spines making it either: -

- 15 mm posterior to anterior tibial edge
- 7 mm anterior to PCL (best guide)
- In line with posterior edge of anterior horn of lateral meniscus (most commonly used landmark)

EFFECT OF MALPOSITIONED TUNNELS IN ACL RECONSTRUCTION

- Tibial tunnel placed too anterior- Extension limited due to graft impingement on to intercondylar notch (NUTCRACKER KNEE).
- Tibial tunnel placed too posteriorly- Graft length shortens and impingement against PCL can occur and hence, flexion gets restricted.
- Femoral tunnel placed too anteriorly- Graft length shortens and flexion gets restricted (CAPTURED KNEE).
- Femoral tunnel placed too posteriorly- This is a positive error as idea is hv a femoral tunnel as posterior as possible. Only issue can be posterior blow out that can be a hassle of Interference screw is being used on femoral side (uncommon though)
- Vertical femoral tunnel- Rotational instability (pivot) may persist despite a negative Lachman test.

Now, in given question the femoral tunnel is too anterior and vertical. Hence, ROM is likely to be restricted and Pivot might remain elicitable. So, expected finding will be a Positive pivot shift and decreased knee ROM (0-70o).
