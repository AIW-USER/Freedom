## Question

An 11-year-old female presents to the pediatric orthopedic clinic for evaluation of spinal asymmetry after a prominent rib hump was noted during routine school screening. Pelvis and hand radiographs are available and demonstrate no ossification of the iliac crests and open physes throughout the interphalangeal joints with well-formed epiphyses, respectively. A posteroanterior (PA) spine radiograph demonstrates an apex-right thoracic curve with a Cobb angle of 32º. You recommend bracing with a thoracolumbar spinal orthosis. Which of the following is correct regarding management considerations?

## Options

1. Physical therapy with scoliosis-specific exercises produces equivalent results to bracing
2. No additional benefit of bracing is conferred when braces are worn for greater than 6 hours
3. Surgical management should be considered given the patient’s skeletal immaturity
4. Curves less than 35º in a patient with these characteristics will likely not progress with observation alone
5. At least 12 hours of brace wear is necessary to achieve efficacious slowing of curve progression, with more hours correlating with greater success

## Correct option

**5. At least 12 hours of brace wear is necessary to achieve efficacious slowing of curve progression, with more hours correlating with greater success**

## Answer explanation

This patient is skeletally immature, as indicated by her pelvic and hand radiographs, with a curve magnitude that is amenable to nonoperative treatment. This is achieved with bracing, with at least 12 hours of wear recommended to prevent curve progression, and correlation of increased success rates with increased hours of bracing (Answer 5).

Adolescent Idiopathic Scoliosis (AIS) is a prevalent multiplanar spinal deformity most commonly observed in adolescent girls between 10 and 18 years of age, with diagnosis typically confirmed through full-length standing spinal radiographs. Treatment decisions are individualized, dependent on the patient's skeletal maturity, curve magnitude, and progression, and include observation, bracing, or surgical management. Bracing is a cornerstone of non-operative treatment, indicated for curves ranging from 25 to 45 degrees in skeletally immature patients, aiming primarily to prevent further curve progression rather than to achieve correction. High compliance with brace wear, typically 16-23 hours daily, has been shown to significantly reduce the need for surgical intervention, although efficacy is observed with a minimum of 12 hours per day. For more severe deformities, generally exceeding 45 degrees, surgical options such as posterior spinal fusion are often pursued to correct the deformity and mitigate long-term complications.

*Weinstein et al. reviewed the efficacy of bracing in adolescents with idiopathic scoliosis who were at risk for curve progression requiring surgery.* **They found** that bracing significantly increased the rate of treatment success (skeletal maturity without curve progression to ≥50 degrees), with a 72% success rate in the braced group compared to 48% in the observation group in the combined cohort, and a significant positive association existed between hours of brace wear and treatment success. Specifically, brace wear for at least 12.9 hours per day correlated with a 90-93% success rate, significantly higher than the 41% success rate observed with 0-6 hours of daily wear. **They concluded** that bracing significantly decreases the progression of high-risk curves to a surgical threshold in patients with adolescent idiopathic scoliosis, and this benefit is directly correlated with longer hours of brace wear.

*Dolan et al. reviewed factors leading to higher brace failures in a cohort of North American patients with adolescent idiopathic scoliosis compared to their peers in Italy.* **They found** that the Italian Scientific Spine Institute (ISICO) cohort exhibited significantly longer average hours of brace wear (18.31 hours/day) and a lower brace failure rate (12%) compared to the Bracing in Adolescent Idiopathic Scoliosis Trial (BrAIST) cohort (11.76 hours/day, 39% failure rate). **They concluded** that the lower brace failure rate observed at ISICO was primarily explained by longer average hours of brace wear.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer Choice 1: Scoliosis-specific exercises have not been shown to be efficacious in preventing progression of deformity.
Answer Choice 2: As mentioned above, studies suggest that the benefit of bracing begins when worn for at least 12 hours per day.
Answer Choice 3: Surgical management is typically not recommended until curves have reached at least 45-50º in magnitude.
Answer Choice 4: Curves of greater than 25º have been shown to progress to reaching surgical indications at an unacceptably high rate when managed with observation alone.
