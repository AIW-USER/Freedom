## Question

A four-year-old with a history of sleep apnea is referred to the clinic for evaluation of short arms and legs. Upon examination, the proximal segments in all four limbs are found to be considerably shorter than the distal segments. He is also noted to have shortened digits and frontal bossing. Which zone is affected in this condition as represented in Figure A?

![[q219374-a-growth-plate.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. A
2. B
3. C
4. D
5. E

## Correct option

**3. C**

## Answer explanation

This four-year-old has a presentation highly suggestive of achondroplasia. The condition is associated with a mutation of the fibroblast growth factor receptor-3 (FGFR3) gene, which results in increased inhibition of chondrocyte proliferation in the proliferative zone of the growth plate (Answer C).

Achondroplasia is the most common skeletal dysplasia, representing 80-90% of all dwarfism. Patients exhibit a myriad of clinical findings, namely short stature with rhizomelic limb shortening, macrocephaly with frontal bossing, several spinal manifestations (foramen magnum stenosis, lumbar hyperlordosis, thoracolumbar kyphosis), fingers of the same length with divergent ring and middle fingers (trident hands), as well as others. The aforementioned manifestations observed in the condition can be attributed to the activating mutation of the FGFR3 gene, which increases inhibition of chondrocyte proliferation in the proliferative zone of the physis, ultimately creating deficiencies in endochondral bone formation.

*Ballock and O'Keefe reviewed the growth plate,* **highlighting** each zone and paying particular attention to the biological activity of each, as well as the regulatory mechanisms that modify growth plate activity. **They note** parathyroid hormone-related peptide, Indian hedgehog, and transforming growth factor-beta are the key factors in chondrocyte proliferation and differentiation. **The authors ultimately conclude** that a thorough understanding of the regulatory processes associated with endochondral ossification is integral to possessing the capability to treat genetic conditions associated with abnormal bony development.

Hosseinzadeh and Milbrandt similarly performed a review detailing the anatomy of the growth plate, highlighting the zones and the surrounding anatomy, as well as the role several factors have on chondrocyte proliferation, hypertrophy, and calcification. The authors also examined traumatic physeal injuries and their propensity to produce bar formation, noting the proximal tibia and distal femur as the most common sites. Ultimately, the authors recommended resection for bar formations <50%, while formations >50% require bar removal with interposition of some material/tissue, such as fat or polymethylmethacrylate.

Figure A represents the growth plate with hematoxylin/eosine/alcian blue staining.

Answer 1: A represents the epiphyseal bone, which can be pathologic in the setting of multiple epiphyseal dysplasia
Answer 2: B represents the reserve zone, and is affected by Gaucher's and diastrophic dysplasia
Answer 4: D represents the zone of hypertrophy, and is associated with SCFEs and physeal fractures
Answer 5: E represents primary spongiosa, which is commonly associated with metaphyseal corner fractures and scurvy
