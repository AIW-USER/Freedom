## Answer explanation

This patient sustained a Vancouver B2 periprosthetic femur fracture which is properly treated with revision of the femoral component to a long porous-coated cementless stem with fixation of the fractured segments (answer 3).

The Vancouver classification system is a simple, validated method to classify periprosthetic hip fractures, widely employed owing to its utility in helping dictate treatment based on the classification types. It classifies fractures by the location of the fracture, stability of the prosthesis, and bone quality. One challenge to this classification system is the difficulty differentiating between Vancouver B1 and B2 subtypes based on radiographs alone. The incidence of postoperative periprosthetic hip fractures is about 0.1-3% in cementless total hip arthroplasty (THA) procedures. Risk factors associated with fracture include poor bone quality, cementless femoral stems, compromised bone stock, and revision procedures. These are typically diagnosed with plain radiographs and can be further evaluated with advanced imaging such as a CT scan.

The Vancouver classification system is broken down into A, B, and C types, which can be further subdivided. Type A fractures, subclassified as AG and AL, are isolated fractures of the greater and lesser trochanter, respectively. Type B fractures occur around the stem: B1 fractures are defined by a well-fixed stem and good proximal bone stock; B2 fractures have prosthesis loosening present, with good proximal bone stock; B3 fractures present with a loosened femoral component with poor proximal bone stock. Type C fractures occur below the femoral stem. Most type A fractures are treated nonoperatively with early protected weight-bearing. Type B1 fractures can be treated with open reduction internal fixation (ORIF), often with cerclage cables and/or a long lateral locking plate. Type B2 fractures require revision of the femoral component to a long porous-coated cementless stem bypassing the fracture site, with fixation of the fractured segments. Type B3 fractures require femoral component revision with a proximal femoral allograft or a proximal femur replacement. Type C is treated via ORIF with plating of the fractured segments while leaving the prostheses alone.

González-Martín et al. reviewed the current literature on ORIF vs revision arthroplasty (RA) in Vancouver B2 fractures and developed a treatment algorithm. They found the low-medium functional demand patients, ASA ≥ 3, many comorbidities (Charlson Comorbidity Index (CCI) ≥ 5), anatomical reconstruction possible, and no prior loosening/hip pain were relative indications to perform ORIF. Whereas high functional demand patients, ASA < 3, few comorbidities (CCI < 5), comminuted fractures or those involving ≥ 2 zones, and prior loosening/hip pain were relative indications for RA. They conclude that paying close attention to the overall health of the patient before surgery is important in Vancouver B2 fractures, as ORIF alone has also proven to be an effective treatment option for B2 fractures in older, more frail patients.

*Abdel et al. reviewed evidence-based management of periprosthetic femur fractures.* **They noted** periprosthetic femur fractures should first be assessed based on stem stability and bone quality, as well-fixed stems can be treated with ORIF alone, whereas loose stems typically require RA. **They conclude** that the Vancouver classification system serves as a useful tool to help dictate treatment. Lastly, **they note** these fractures can be clinically challenging since the treatment is based on the fracture, prosthesis, and the patient.

*Brady et al. developed the Vancouver classification system for periprosthetic fracture of the hip.* *They created this system to incorporate different factors such as location, status of stem fixation, and quality of bone stock.* **They conclude** that when these factors are considered, they allow for a more rational approach to treatment.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Vancouver AG fractures are typically treated with nonoperative management and early protected weight-bearing. Significantly displaced fracture may require ORIF.
Answer 2: Vancouver B1 fractures are typically treated with ORIF, often with cerclage cables and/or a long locking plate.
Answer 4: Vancouver B3 fractures require femoral component revision with proximal femoral allograft or a proximal femur replacement
Answer 5: Vancouver C fractures require ORIF with plating of the fracture distal to the tip of the stem.
