## Question

While on your orthopedic trauma rotation you encounter several patients with pelvic ring injuries. You are questioned about pelvic ring fixation options. When comparing the treatment in Figure A with that in Figure B for antero-posterior compression (APC) 2 pelvic ring injuries, which of the following is true?

*Figure A*

![[q219154-b-5.jpg]]
*Figure B*

## Options

1. Figure A has a decreased rate of anterior plate failure
2. Figure A has a greater rate of malunion
3. Figures A&B have equivalent rates of anterior plate failure
4. Figures A&B have equivalent rates of malunion
5. Figure B allows for earlier time to ambulation

## Correct option

**1. Figure A has a decreased rate of anterior plate failure**

## Answer explanation

APC2 injuries treated with anterior and posterior fixation decreases the rate of anterior plate failure and malunion compared with use of an anterior plate alone.

Pelvic ring fractures are high-energy fractures of the pelvic ring which typically occur due to blunt trauma. Diagnosis is made radiographically with pelvic radiographs and further characterized with a CT scan. Treatment is typically operative fixation depending on the degree of pelvis instability, fracture displacement, and patient activity demands. APC2 injuries treated with anterior and posterior fixation decreases the rate of anterior plate failure and malunion compared with the use of an anterior plate alone. However, adding posterior fixation may prevent anterior plate fixation failure but will not improve patient outcomes or assist with the reduction of the anterior symphysis.

*Wojahn et al. reviewed the treatment of anterior pelvic ring injuries involves both acute stabilization during the initial resuscitation and definitive fixation.* **They reported** that symphyseal plating is the preferred technique for stabilization of symphyseal diastasis because of superior stability and low morbidity. **They concluded** that regardless of fixation strategy, posterior ring reduction and stabilization is crucial.

*Avilucea et al. reviewed whether the use of an anterior symphyseal plate alone is adequate to control sagittal and coronal plane rotation and prevent malunion of an anteroposterior compression type-2 (APC-2) pelvic ring injury.* **They concluded** that the use of an anterior plate and a supplemental posterior screw for fixation of APC-2 pelvic ring injuries significantly decreases the rate of anterior plate failure and malunion compared with the use of an anterior plate alone.

Figure A is a pelvic radiograph of anterior and posterior fixation. Figure B is a pelvic radiograph of anterior-only fixation.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 2-4: Anterior and posterior fixation decreases the rate of anterior plate failure and malunion compared with the use of an anterior plate alone.
Answer 5: Anterior-only fixation would not lead to an earlier time to ambulation.
