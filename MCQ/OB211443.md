## Question

A 28-year-old male is involved in a high-speed motor vehicle collision. Upon arrival in the trauma bay, ATLS protocol and aggressive fluid resuscitation is started for hypotension and bradycardia. On exam, he cannot move either lower extremity and lacks sensation below the umbilicus, but no deficits are noted in the upper extremities. There is no rectal tone when the foley catheter is tensioned. How would you classify his injury based on the ASIA classification?

## Options

1. ASIA A
2. ASIA E
3. ASIA D
4. Cannot determine at this time
5. ASIA B

## Correct option

**4. Cannot determine at this time**

## Answer explanation

This patient is in spinal shock as evidenced by flaccid paralysis below the T10 level and no bulbocavernosus reflex. Given this, no ASIA classification may be assigned at this time.

Traumatic spinal cord injuries have a bimodal distribution, consisting of young males with high-energy mechanisms and elderly individuals with previous spinal pathology. Acutely, this injury causes the spinal neurons to become polarized and unresponsive, which is termed shock. Two different patterns of injury occur. Neurogenic shock involves the autonomic pathways causing bradycardia and hypotension, which requires intervention to maintain cerebral and end-organ perfusion. Spinal shock is complete unresponsiveness of all neurons at the level of injury; causing muscle paralysis, blocking reflex arc activity, and often involving the autonomic pathways as well. Spinal shock will mask the true degree of neural damage so no grading or classification of neural deficits can be made until it has resolved. Resolution of spinal shock is signified by the return of the bulbocavernosus reflex.

*Schouten et al. reviewed the initial management of spinal injuries.* Following initial evaluation following ATLS protocol, a complete neurologic evaluation should be performed to ensure prompt recognition and treatment of spinal injuries. They reinforce that during spinal shock will mask true neurologic injury and the bulbocavernosus reflex heralds shock resolution.

*Sekhon and Fehlings reviewed blood pressure management following acute spinal cord injuries.* **They noted** the deleterious effects of hypotension (systolic <90mmHg) on cord perfusion. Given this, **they recommended** maintaining a mean arterial pressure at or above 85-90mmHg for the first 7 days following injury.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1, 2, 3, 5- The ASIA classification cannot be applied at this time given spinal shock. Upon resolution of shock, this would be a useful means of communicating the extent of injury amongst surgeons, internists, physiatrists, and therapists.
