## Question

A 13-year old male injured his knee while jumping to catch a pass during football practice. He currently is complaining of severe pain in his leg and requires increased analgesia. His injury radiograph is shown in Figure A. Injury to which vessel shown in Figure B is causing his condition?

![[q210101-a-tibial-tubercle-fracture.jpg]]
*Figure A*

![[q210101-b-proximal-tibia-vasculature.jpg]]

## Options

1. A
2. B
3. C
4. D
5. E

## Correct option

**4. D**

## Answer explanation

Figure A is a radiograph demonstrating a type 3 tibial tubercle fracture. Letter D corresponds to the recurrent anterior tibial artery, which may cause a compartment syndrome if injured.

Tibial tubercle fractures have been associated with the development of compartment syndromes, with rates as high as 10% reported in the literature. It is thought to arise from a disruption of branches of the recurrent anterior tibial artery, which is located on the lateral border of the tubercle. When the tubercle fractures, the artery may be injured and retract under the fascia, leading to excessive bleeding within the anterior compartment. Patients who develop compartment syndrome should be taken for emergent fasciotomy. Associated injuries may include meniscal tears, but cruciate injuries are uncommon.

*Pandya et al. performed a retrospective study evaluating the complications of tibial tubercle fractures.* *They analyzed 41 tibial tubercle fractures in 40 children with a mean age of 15.* At presentation, compartment syndrome or vascular compromise was seen in nearly 10% of patients. **They conclude** that tibial tubercle fractures represent high-energy injuries with potentially devastating complications such as compartment syndrome and/or vascular compromise.

*Pretell-Mazzini et al. performed a systematic review of the literature to determine the outcomes and complications of tibial tubercle fractures in pediatric patients.* *They analyzed 23 articles reporting 336 fractures with a mean follow up of 33.6 months.* **They reported** the associated injury rate was 4.1% and most common in type 3 fractures (4.7%). Compartment syndrome was present in 3.6% of cases.

Figure A is a lateral radiograph of the knee demonstrating a type 3 tibial tubercle fracture. Figure B is an illustration of the vasculature surrounding the knee. Illustration A is a labeled diagram of the vasculature surrounding the knee.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Letter A shows the medial inferior genicular artery.
Answer 2: Letter B shows the lateral inferior genicular artery.
Answer 3: Letter C shows the peroneal artery.
Answer 5: Letter E shows the anterior tibial artery.
