## Question

A 3-month-old infant is brought in for a routine well-child evaluation. Physical exam uncovers moderately limited hip abduction bilaterally. The child is otherwise developmentally normal with sonographically normal hips. Which of the following is accurate regarding this clinical pathology?

## Options

1. Adductor tenotomy is recommended in place of observation to mitigate the risk of developmental hip dysplasia.
2. Developmentally normal infants without hip dysplasia rarely demonstrate limitations in hip abduction.
3. The majority of infants with hip dysplasia do not demonstrate limited hip abduction.
4. Unilateral limitation of hip abduction is a very specific clinical predictor DDH, while bilateral limitation in hip abduction is not.
5. Untreated limitation in hip abduction in sonographically normal infants increases risk of developing hip dysplasia.

## Correct option

**4. Unilateral limitation of hip abduction is a very specific clinical predictor DDH, while bilateral limitation in hip abduction is not.**

## Answer explanation

Unilateral limitation of hip abduction has been shown to be a very specific (90%) and moderately sensitive (70%) clinical predictor of hip dysplasia. Bilateral limitation in hip abduction however is not very sensitive or specific.

Limited hip abduction is present in the majority of infants with hip dysplasia but is also a common finding, albeit to a lesser extent, in developmentally normal infants without sonographic evidence of hip dysplasia. Treatment of limited hip abduction in infants with sonographically normal hips involves observation, as almost all cases have been found to resolve spontaneously and not to have significant correlation with the development of hip dysplasia among this cohort. In patients with limited abduction and sonographic evidence of hip dysplasia, the treatment algorithm typically begins with a Pavlik harness when the presenting age is under 6 months.

*Castelein et al. evaluated the relationship between limited hip abduction and DDH in infants.* **The authors found** that limited abduction was present in roughly half (46%) of infants with sonographically normal hips, while the majority (69%) of infants with hip dysplasia had limitations in abduction. In the sonographically normal cohort, limited abduction was managed with observation only, and upon re-examination on an average of 5 years later all children had clinically and radiographically normal hips.

*Jari et al. prospectively evaluated the relationship between limited hip abduction and sonographic hip dysplasia in infants.* **The authors found** that unilateral limitation was present in only 5.9% of neonatal dislocatable hips, but in 87.5% of late dislocated hips after 6mo. Bilateral limitation in hip abduction was not a sensitive finding for hip dysplasia, however unilateral limitation was very specific and moderately sensitive. **They concluded** that unilateral limitation in abduction in an infant over 3 months should warrant sonographic evaluation.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: In a patient with limitations in hip abduction, especially when bilateral, observation is warranted given that most resolve spontaneously and do not have a significant impact on development of hip dysplasia.
Answer 2: Limited hip abduction was found to be present in one-half (46%) of infants with developmentally normal hips.
Answer 3: In infants with sonographically proven hip dysplasia, nearly 70% clinical demonstrated limitations in abduction.
Answer 5: Treatment of limited hip abduction in infants with sonographically normal hips is unnecessary. This population is not at increased risk of developing hip dysplasia when left untreated.
