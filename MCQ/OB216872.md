## Question

A 43-year-old male presents to your office for evaluation of medial sided knee pain. He reports the pain to be worse with weight bearing activities and improved with rest. Radiographs show medial joint space narrowing with marginal osteophytes and subchondral sclerosis. On exam he has tenderness to palpation over the medial joint line and corrects to neutral alignment. In discussing your plan with the patient he inquires about a brace. Which of the following braces would most benefit this patient?

## Answer explanation

Figure A represents a medial unloader brace, which would most benefit this patient with varus degenerative knee arthritis.

Conservative treatment for knee arthritis typically consists of non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, weight loss, bracing, physical therapy and injections (i.e.. corticosteroid, hyaluronic acid). It's important that all patients fail conservative treatment prior to proceeding with total knee arthroplasty. In this otherwise young patient with evidence of medial joint space narrowing radiographically as well as medial sided pain and a correctable deformity, a medial unloader brace is a reasonable conservative treatment. Pain relief is thought to be mediated by distracting the involved compartment via an external valgus forces applied to the knee. In doing so, tibiofemoral alignment is improved, and the load is shifted off the degenerative compartment. The 2021 AAOS clinical practice guidelines have a moderate recommendation for the use of bracing to improve function, pain and quality of life in patients with knee arthritis.

*Kirkley et al. review the effect of bracing on varus arthritis.* **They report** a significant difference between the unloader-brace group and the neoprene-sleeve group with regard to pain after both the six-minute walking test (p = 0.021) and the thirty-second stair-climbing test (p = 0.016). **They conclude** that valgus producing bracing may significantly benefit patients with varus knee arthritis.

*Matsuno et al. review bracing for knee osteoarthritis.* **They report** 19 of the 20 patients answered that they experienced significant pain relief while ambulating with a medial unloader brace. Further, the overall tibiofemoral alignment was improved in 12 patients. **They conclude** that medial unloader bracing is a valid treatment options for patient with medial knee osteoarthritis.

Figure A is demonstrative of a medial unloader brace. Figure B shows a lateral unloader brace that would be used for primary valgus knee osteoarthritis. Figure C is indicative of a Chopat strap which can be used for patellar tendonitis. Figure D is representative of an ACL brace used postoperatively for support following ACL reconstruction. Figure E simply shows a neoprene knee sleeve.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: This is a varus producing brace and would help for valgus, not varus arthritic patterns.
Answer 3: Chopat braces offload the patellar tendon and do not correct overall alignment.
Answer 4: While ACL braces would provide improved support, it would not lessen forces along the medial compartment.
Answer 5: Knee sleeves allow for compression of the knee, which can allow for symptomatic improvement. However, a medial unloader would help offload the diseased compartment and would be a better option for this patient.
