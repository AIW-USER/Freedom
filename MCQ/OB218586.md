## Options

1. Germ-line mutation of p53
2. Mutation in EXT gene
3. Failure of normal endochondral ossification
4. Increased local concentration of prostaglandin E2 and COX1 & 2 expression
5. Genetic rearrangement affecting chromosome 6 (position q13)

## Correct option

**3. Failure of normal endochondral ossification**

## Answer explanation

This patient is most likely to have Ollier's disease (multiple enchondromatosis). This skeletal dysplasia occurs with the failure of normal endochondral ossification.

Enchondromas are benign chondrogenic tumors composed of hyaline cartilage that typically occur in the medullary cavity of the diaphysis or metaphysis, most commonly in the hands. Patients typically present between the ages of 20-50 with an asymptomatic lesion discovered incidentally on radiographs. Ollier's disease (multiple enchondromatosis) has a sporadic inheritance with no genetic predisposition. It results in skeletal dysplasia with failure of normal endochondral ossification. In this condition, enchondromas occur throughout the metaphysis and diaphysis of long bones. The involved bones can be dysplastic, with shortening and bowing. The risk of malignant transformation is upward of 30%, with chondrosarcoma being the most common malignant lesion.

*Angelini et al. reviewed the different surgical treatments and evaluated the outcome and possible prognostic factors of leg-lengthening patients with Ollier's disease.* **They reported** there is no consensus on the optimal surgical technique and implants for the correction of limb deformities in patients with Ollier's disease. **They concluded** that external fixators, most commonly circular, are the most commonly used implants; however, complications do occur.

*Gouk et al. reported on a 6-year-old boy with Ollier's disease.* **They reported** that Ollier's disease most commonly presents with shortening of the affected limb (asymmetric dwarfism). **They noted** that a skeletal survey is an important diagnostic tool. **They concluded** that there are complications associated with Ollier disease, most notably pathological fracture, and chondrosarcoma.

Figure A shows AP and lateral radiographs of the left index finger with an expansile lesion within the diaphysis and stippled calcifications, consistent with an enchondroma. The MRI images in Figure B show a lesion that is dark on T1 and bright on T2, with internal calcification consistent with an enchondroma. Figure C is the AP and lateral radiograph of the proximal tibia showing a lesion with internal calcification but no concerning features such as cortical erosion, periosteal reaction, or fracture. Figure D shows T1 and T2 weighted MRI images of the same tibial lesion with findings consistent with an enchondroma. Figure E shows low and high-power photomicrographs of enchondroma showing a clustering pattern of chondrocytes residing in lacunar spaces without nuclear pleomorphism or size variation.

Illustrations A-C are representative images of a more severe presentation of Ollier's Disease.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Germ-line mutation of p53 found in 15-20% of periosteal osteosarcomas
Answer 2: Mutation in the EXT gene affects pre-hypertrophic chondrocytes of the growth plate in osteochondromas & multiple hereditary exostoses (MHE)
Answer 4: Osteoid osteomas are attributed to the increased local concentration of prostaglandin E2 and COX1 & 2 expression
Answer 5: Genetic rearrangement affecting chromosome 6 (position q13) is most descriptive of a chondromyxoid fibroma
