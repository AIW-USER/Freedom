## Options

1. Rate of injury is greater on right-sided versus left-sided approaches
2. The left side courses around the Subclavian artery
3. Immediate re-exploration is required
4. Rate of injury is greater with revision surgery
5. It originates from cervical branches C1-C3

## Correct option

**4. Rate of injury is greater with revision surgery**

## Answer explanation

This patient in question has undergone a C4-7 anterior cervical discectomy and fusion. During the surgery, the recurrent laryngeal nerve was injured, which is the most commonly injured structure during this approach. Overall, the rate of injury is greater with revision surgery.

The recurrent laryngeal nerve (RLN) is a branch of the Vagus nerve (CN X) and is the most common nerve injury through an anterior cervical spine approach. The overall estimated incidence is approximately 1%. The course of the recurrent laryngeal nerve varies between the right and left. The left curves around the aortic arch and the right around the subclavian artery and has found to be shorter. There is a theoretical risk of increased injury with right-sided anterior cervical spine approaches due to the variable nerve course, but no studies have substantiated that theoretical increased risk. The rate of RLN injury has been found to be increased with revision surgery with rates reported up to 9.5%. Evidence of injury to the RLN does not necessitate immediate surgical re-exploration as most dysphonia (hoarseness) is temporary and will resolve. Patients that do not have improvement warrant referral to ENT in a delayed fashion.

*Eskander et al. looked at vertebral artery anatomy using MRI on 250 patients.* **They identified** three main groups of anomalies being intraforaminal anomalies with midline migration, extraforaminal anomalies(entering in the foramen at a different level), and arterial anomalies. **They found** that 7.6% of patients had midline migration of the vertebral artery and 92% of vertebral arteries were in their transverse foramen at the C6 level. Ultimately, **they concluded** that vertebral artery anatomy needs to be carefully considered to avoid injury.

*Currier et al. studied the anatomic relationship of the Internal Carotid artery (ICA) to C1 using CTs with contrast.* *They looked at 50 CTs with contrast to determine the distance of the ICA to C1, which was 2.88mm on the left and 2.89mm on the right.* **They found** that it was at moderate risk of injury in 46% of cases and high risk in 12% of cases. **They concluded** that anytime C1 will have a screw placed a CT with contrast is recommended to determine ICA location, risk of injury, and the need to switch to unicortical fixation.

*Nassr et al., retrospectively reviewed 750 multilevel cervical spine decompression surgeries by a single surgeon to identify the risk of C5 palsy.* *630 patients were included in the analysis and* **they found** that the mean incidence of C5 palsy was 6.7% with a trend for a higher incidence for the laminectomy and fusion and higher in males. Ultimately **they concluded** that the incidence of C5 palsy was not statistically significant based on the surgical procedure.

*Beutler et al. retrospectively looked at 328 anterior cervical spine procedures.* **They found** the rate of Recurrent Laryngeal nerve (RLN) injury was 2.1% with anterior cervical discectomy, 3.5% with corpectomy, 3% with instrumentation, and 9.5% with revision surgery. **They concluded** that there was a significant increase in rate of RLN injury with revision surgery and side of approach had no association with incidence of RLN injury.

Figures A and B are lateral cervical spine radiographs of a C4-7 anterior cervical decompression and fusion.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: There is no difference in the risk of injury to the RLN between right and left-sided approaches. The theoretical risk due to the short and more variable course of the right RLN has not been substantiated in the literature.
Answer 2: The left RLN does not course around the Subclavian artery. In fact, it courses around the Aortic arch.
Answer 3: Immediate re-exploration is not required. Most patients only have temporary symptoms. Those who have persistent symptoms are referred to ENT.
Answer 5: The RLN does not have contributions from C1-C3. It is a branch of the Vagus nerve (CN X).
