## Question

A 65-year-old woman with diabetes undergoes a left total knee arthroplasty 2 years ago. Her initial postoperative radiograph is depicted in Figure A. She presents to your office with a recurring wound dehiscence and drainage. The diagnosis of a prosthetic joint infection is made and the decision is made to undergo revision surgery. ## Answer explanation

This patient suffers from an anteromedial wound dehiscence after a left total knee arthroplasty (TKA). As this cannot be closed primarily, the most commonly used flap for this area of skin compromise is the pedicled medial gastrocnemius local flap. The primary blood supply for this flap is the medial sural artery, which is labeled C in Figure C.

Wound healing complications after TKA can be potentially devastating, thus multiple strategies exist for restoring a well-vascularized soft tissue envelope around TKA components to prevent infection by allowing antibiotic delivery and facilitating wound closure. The local pedicled gastrocnemius muscle flap is the preferred flap for anteromedial wounds since its musculature is larger and exhibits greater mobility compared to the lateral gastrocnemius.

*Houdek et al. reviewed long-term outcomes after pedicled gastrocnemius flaps in TKA.* **They reported** a 10-year revision and amputation-free survivorship of 68% and 79%, respectively, with increased risk of implant failure correlated with morbid obesity and multiple operations. **They concluded** that the gastrocnemius flap can reliably provide coverage of TKA components.

*Dusseldorp et al. investigated the pattern of the intra-muscular course of the medial sural artery perforator flap in cadavers.* **They identified** three types of arterial branching patterns within the medial gastrocnemius muscle, with a dominant medial sural artery perforator present in 92% of cadavers.

Figure A is a postoperative radiograph after left TKA. Figure B depicts an anteromedial wound defect. Figure C depicts the vascular anatomy of the knee, viewing from posterior. Illustration A is the labeled version of Figure C.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Label A depicts the popliteal artery.
Answer 2: Label B depicts the superior medial geniculate artery.
Answer 4: Label D depicts the inferior medial geniculate artery.
Answer 5: Label E depicts the middle geniculate artery.
