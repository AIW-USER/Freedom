Chapter: Trauma MCQs — Hip Dislocation

## Question

Most common association with anterior dislocation of hip is

## Options

1. Femoral nerve injury
2. Obturator nerve injury
3. Femoral head fracture
4. Anterior wall acetabulum fracture

## Correct option

**3. Femoral head fracture**

## Answer explanation

Associated complications to hip dislocations:

Posterior dislocation of hip is associated with posterior wall acetabular fracture (most common complication), femoral head fractures, sciatic nerve injuries and ipsilateral knee injuries (up to 25% cases).

In posterior hip dislocations, femoral head fractures are less common than acetabular fractures, with an incidence rate of 5%-15%. Acetabular fractures most commonly occur from posterior dislocations resulting from trauma or closed reduction methods as the femoral head is brought hard against the posterior rim of the acetabulum. The incidence rate of acetabular fractures is as high as 70%. A broad range of ipsilateral knee injuries can be seen, especially following native dislocations. Significant knee injuries include effusion (37%), bone bruise (33%), and meniscal tears (30%). Posttraumatic arthritis represents the most common long-term sequela of simple posterior dislocation, with an incidence rate of approximately 20%. Sciatic nerve palsy (peroneal component) is the most common neurologic structure damaged as a result of the femoral head stretching the nerve during dislocation or surgical scarring. The reported incidence rate of sciatic nerve palsy is 10%-15%. Because this injury is also time sensitive, delay in reduction may permanently impair nerve function, and patients may only see partial recovery. AVN can occur from prolonged dislocation following trauma or repeated attempts at reduction. The incidence rate of AVN following hip dislocation is approximately 2%-10%, with increasing rates past 6 hours. Heterotrophic ossification results in the presence of bone in soft tissue following repeated attempts at closed reduction. The incidence rate of heterotrophic ossification ranges from 2.8%-9%.

Anterior hip dislocations: Impaction fractures of the femoral head (most common complication) commonly complicate anterior hip dislocation. These fractures result from impaction of the femoral head on the anteroinferior rim of the acetabulum after the femoral head exits the acetabulum. Impaction injuries are located in the superolateral aspect of the femoral head and may appear radiographically as a depressed fracture (similar to a Hill-Sachs deformity of the humerus) or simply as a mild flattening of the femoral head.

Other associated skeletal injuries seen with anterior hip dislocation include ipsilateral fractures of the acetabulum, femoral neck, greater trochanter, and femoral shaft. Avulsion of the anterior inferior iliac spine also may occur, particularly in the superior type of anterior hip dislocation. Arthritis is the most common complication of anterior hip dislocation (17%). Although avascular necrosis has been reported to occur in 4% of patients with anterior hip dislocation, the prevalence may be higher in patients with reduction delayed for more than 24 hr. Osteonecrosis rates are less (4% reported for anterior dislocations) compared with high rates in posterior dislocations. This is explained by the pattern of blood supply to the hip. Less common complications in anterior dislocations of the hip include vascular injury- the femoral artery and vein being involved- and nerve injury- the femoral nerve being involved.

Answer: 3
