## Question

A 76-year-old female presents to the level 1 trauma center after tripping over her dog at home. She has significant pain in her right knee and an obvious deformity. She has a history of a primary total knee arthroplasty (TKA) performed 6 years prior. Radiographs in the emergency department are shown in Figures A and B. Later that day, she underwent surgical fixation by way of a retrograde intramedullary nail (rIMN). After surgery, radiographs are taken of the operative femur, and a malalignment is noted. What is the most likely malalignment associated with this fracture pattern and treatment?

## Options

1. Shortening
2. Flexion deformity
3. Extension deformity
4. Varus malalignment
5. Valgus malalignment

## Correct option

**3. Extension deformity**

## Answer explanation

The femoral component of a TKA will force the start point of a retrograde intramedullary nail (rIMN) posterior in the intercondylar notch. This more posterior start point will push the distal fragment anteriorly when the nail is inserted, resulting in the most common malalignment in this scenario, an extension deformity (Answer 3).

Periprosthetic distal femur fractures (PPDFFs) are typically a result of low-energy trauma and can occur in up to 2% of postoperative total knee arthroplasty (TKA) patients. Treatment of these injuries is determined by the fracture pattern, implant stability, and remaining bone stock/bone quality. Surgical fixation of these fractures involves rIMNs, open reduction internal fixation (ORIF) with locked plating, or a combination of the two. When the femoral component is well fixed, with adequate distal segment for fixation, rIMN can be employed. Nailing allows for a biomechanical advantage of intramedullary implants while minimizing soft tissue disruption. However, rIMN of PPDFFs is associated with malalignment, particularly an extension deformity of the distal segment (defined as >5° deformity relative to anatomic axis of the femur). This occurs due to the femoral component of the TKA forcing the nail start point more posterior than ideal in the intercondylar notch. This is demonstrated in Illustration A. Hyperextension of the distal femur in TKA can alter patellofemoral joint mechanics and potentially increase polyethylene wear and create instability, leading to earlier revision. Recent studies suggest that rIMNs with a 10° distal bend can improve alignment outcomes and potentially mitigate the drawbacks of using a rIMN when treating PPDFFs.

*Kerr et al. published a retrospective study of PPDFFs treated with rIMN to evaluate the post-operative sagittal alignment between rIMNs with and without a 10° distal bend.* *A total of 50 patients were included in the study; 23 were treated with a rIMN with a 10° distal bend, and 27 were treated with a standard rIMN with a 5° distal bend.* *This study found a significantly greater amount of outliers in sagittal plan alignment in the 5° bend group (41%) than the 10° group (13%).* *Only one patient in the 10° bend group had a post-operative extension deformity, compared to 11 patients in the 5° bend group.* **The authors concluded** that the 10° distal bend in rIMNs provided significantly better sagittal alignment and recommended the use of this type of nail when treating PPDFFs.

*Gausden et al. published a retrospective study on 97 patients with PPDFFs treated with either rIMN or ORIF to compare the outcomes of treatment.* *The study included 74 patients who underwent ORIF and 23 patients who underwent rIMN.* *Extension deformity was present in 10 patients in the ORIF group (14%) and 10 patients in the rIMN group (44%).* Reoperating rates were equivalent (16%) between the two treatment groups. **The authors concluded** that rIMN was associated with an increased risk of malalignment, most commonly an extension deformity, however, this did not lead to an increase in reoperation compared to ORIF.

Figures A and B are the AP and lateral radiographs of a right knee, demonstrating a supracondylar periprosthetic distal femur fracture.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: A shortening deformity is possible with rIMN due to significant comminution. However, an extension deformity of the distal segment is the most commonly reported malalignment with rIMN for PPDFFs.
Answer 2: A flexion deformity is not common when treating PPDFFs with rIMN. Due to the femoral implant forcing the rIMN posterior in the intercondylar notch, an extension deformity will arise once the nail has advanced to the proximal intramedullary canal.
Answer 4: Varus malalignment can occur with rIMN, but it is far less common than an extension deformity.
Answer 5: Valgus malalignment can also occur with rIMN, but it is far less common than an extension deformity.
