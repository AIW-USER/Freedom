## Question

A 43-year-old male presents to your clinic with complaints of left hip pain. He explains he was a former gymnast and has had intermittent hip pain for years. He now works as a truck driver and the pain is worse with prolonged periods of driving. On exam, pain is worst with flexion, adduction and internal rotation of the hip. Radiographs demonstrate an asphericity of the femoral head/neck junction with an alpha angle of 65º. MRI demonstrates a labral tear with femoral head subchondral cystic changes and edema. Physical therapy was prescribed by his PCP without much benefit. You discuss surgical options including hip arthroscopy with femoral osteoplasty and labral repair. Which one of following is a poor prognostic indicator for the success of hip arthroscopy in this patient population?

## Options

1. Age < 45
2. Alpha angle >60º
3. Associated labral tear undergoing repair
4. Lateral center edge angle = 25º
5. Subchondral bone cysts seen on MRI

## Correct option

**5. Subchondral bone cysts seen on MRI**

## Answer explanation

In patients with femoroacetabular impingement (FAI), subchondral bone cysts and edema noted on the MRI are predictive of poorer outcomes after treatment with hip arthroscopy.

FAI is a common hip pathology, particularly in patient populations which participate in repetitive activities at the extremes of hip motion. Two primary types of FAI are recognized, including cam and pincer impingement. There are often associated labral tears or labral pathology noted on MRI evaluation. Hip arthroscopy has become one of the primary methods of operative management in this population and usually entails femoral osteoplasty to improve the femoral head/neck offset as well as management of the intra-articular and labral pathology. While hip arthroscopy has been successful in managing this pathology in many patients, certain populations may be prone to poorer outcomes. These include patients >45 years old, elevated BMI >25-30, those with increased lateral center edge angles (LCEA)>35º, subchondral bone edema on MRI, and evidence of arthritic changes. In these patient populations, symptomatic management and eventual total hip arthroplasty represent the best recommendations at this time.

*Nakashima et al. evaluated the clinical and radiographic predictors for unsalvageable labral tears at hip arthroscopy and divided patients into fixation and reconstruction groups.* **They noted** that risk factors for unsalvageable tears and worse outcomes included age >45, BMI >24 kg/m^2 and vertical center anterior angle > 36º. **They conclude** that with at least 2 of these risk factors, patients have a high likelihood of having a worse outcome.

*Krych et al. evaluated the outcomes of patients with subchondral edema or cystic change who underwent hip arthroscopy for FAI.* **They found** decreased Harris Hip Scores for all patients with subchondral bony edema or cystic changes and success rates of 67% in this group compared to 85% in the group without these changes on MRI. **They conclude** that the presence of this edema or cystic change places patients at risk for inferior outcomes after arthroscopic treatment of FAI, as they likely indicate a full-thickness cartilage lesion.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Age >45 is a risk factor for poorer outcomes after hip arthroscopy.
Answer 2: Increased alpha angle does not represent a known risk factor for poorer outcomes after hip arthroscopy as this is part of the pathology being treated with femoral osteoplasty.
Answer 3: Associated labral tears that undergo debridement only with no repair have been shown to lead to worse outcomes than those that are repaired at the time of hip arthroscopy.
Answer 4: Increased LCEA >35º has been shown to lead to poorer outcomes as the acetabular overhang is difficult to modify during hip arthroscopy.
