## Question

You are in charge of performing preseason physicals for your local college athletic department. In an attempt to educate the student-athlete population, you provide them with resources about risks associated with concussions. Which of the following is thought to most increase the risk of sustaining a concussion?

## Options

1. Male gender
2. Poor quality protective equipment
3. History of previous head injury
4. Increasing age
5. History of anxiety/depression

## Correct option

**3. History of previous head injury**

## Answer explanation

A history of previous head injury places an athlete at a 2-6x higher risk for subsequent concussion/head injury.

Concussions are common in competitive sports, with reported totals per year nearing 4 million. A concussion represents a transient brain injury that disrupts normal physiologic functioning of the brain. Concussions can be associated with other serious head and neck injuries like cervical fracture, spinal cord injury, brain bleeding, etc. Appropriate sideline management is important and includes immediate removal from play without same day return, and appropriate history and physical examination with neurocognitive evaluation. Equally important is developing concussion prevention protocols which stem from identifying risk factors for concussion. Known risk factors include history of previous concussion, multiple concussions, female gender, younger age, and type/position of sport.

*Harmon et al. published a position statement for the American Medical Society for Sports Medicine regarding concussion in sport.* This statement extensively reviews the pathophysiology, incidence, risk factors, and proper management of concussions. While their review is thorough, they do note that there continues to be research needed in this field to validate and improve assessment tools and identify new neuroimaging techniques to better quantify brain tissue injury.

*Scopaz et al. reviewed risk factors for concussion and prolonged recovery in sports.* **They noted** history of prior concussion, collision sports, female sex, younger age, and women's soccer participation as the strongest risk factors. **They conclude** by noting that more research is needed to clarify the specific role of these risk factors in the overall management of concussion patients.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Females have been shown to to be at higher risk for concussion than males. This may relate to estrogen circulation and cerebral blood flow or other anatomic reasons that have not been fully delineated yet.
Answer 2: A relationship between quality of equipment and concussion risk has not yet been established.
Answer 4: Younger athletes are at higher risk for concussion due to differences in pediatric/adolescent brain development. Younger athletes may have less "cognitive reserve," than older counterparts.
Answer 5: History of anxiety and depression can complicate diagnosis and management of concussion but on its own, has not been reliably proven as a risk factor to this point.
