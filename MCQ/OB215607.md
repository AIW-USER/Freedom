## Question

A 54-year-old female with long-standing hallux valgus decides to undergo corrective surgery. Preoperatively she is found to have an HVA of 42 degrees and an IMA of 17 degrees. She subsequently undergoes proximal and distal corrective osteotomies performed through a dorsomedial incision. At her postoperative visit 2 weeks following surgery she endorses severe neuritic type pain along the medial hallux. Which of the following is true of the injured nerve?

## Options

1. Terminal branch of the deep peroneal nerve
2. Terminal branch of the superficial peroneal nerve
3. Traverses posterior to the medial malleolus
4. Crosses the achilles tendon 10cm proximal to its insertion
5. Divides in to anterior and posterior branches 3cm proximal to the medial malleolus

## Correct option

**2. Terminal branch of the superficial peroneal nerve**

## Answer explanation

The dorsomedial cutaneous nerve is a branch of the superficial peroneal and must be identified and protected during hallux surgery performed through a dorsomedial incision.

Treatment of hallux valgus commonly involves corrective osteotomies performed on the medial aspect of the great toe. During dissection, care must be made to protect the dorsomedial cutaneous nerve (DMCN), which is the terminal branch of the superficial peroneal nerve. Injury to the DMCN may result in severe medial-sided neuritic pain or numbness to this same region. Most cases of dorsomedial cutaneous nerve syndrome are the result of a neuropraxia and resolve spontaneously with time. In recalcitrant cases, revision surgery may be required in which the DMCN is resected and buried into the proximal aspect of the first metatarsal or cuneiform.

*Miller et al review dorsomedial cutaneous nerve syndrome and its treatment in cases resistant to nonoperative management.* **They report** significant symptomatic improvement following DMCN resection with burying of the nerve into the proximal metacarpal or cuneiform. **They conclude** that care must be taken to protect this nerve during hallux valgus correction.

*Solan et al review surgical anatomy of the dorsomedial cutaneous nerve of the hallux.* **They report** that a branch of the DMCN is consistently present and in line with the dorsomedial incision commonly used for hallux surgery. **They conclude** that surgeons should consider utilizing a mid-medial incision instead, in an attempt to avoid postoperative neuroma formation.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The medial branch of the deep peroneal nerve resides on the lateral aspect of the hallux and provides sensation to the first web space. This is not commonly injured with corrective osteotomies.
Answer 3: The tibial nerve terminates into the medial and lateral plantar branches, neither of which are encountered during corrective osteotomy of the hallux.
Answer 4: The sural nerve supplies sensation to the skin of the lateral foot and lateral lower ankle.
Answer 5: The saphenous nerve provides sensation to the medial ankle and should not be encountered during hallux surgery.
