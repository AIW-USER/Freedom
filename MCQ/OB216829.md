## Question

A 19-year-old collegiate javelin thrower presents to your office. He complains of pain over the medial aspect of the elbow and difficulty throwing over the last several days after feeling a "pop." On examination, he is tender at the elbow over the medial epicondyle and moving valgus stress test elicits pain. You suspect he has torn his ulnar collateral ligament (UCL). While explaining treatment options, you explain to him the anatomy of the UCL and how that affects reconstruction. Which of the following statements regarding the anatomy in Figure A is true?

![[q216829-a-ucl-anatomy.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Structure A is subdivided into 2 bands which are both isometric throughout elbow range of motion
2. Structure A provides greatest resistance to valgus stress at 70º of flexion
3. Structure B contributes to stability in full flexion
4. Structure C is tightest in full extension
5. Structure C and elbow capsule provide primary restraint to valgus stress at 30º of flexion

## Correct option

**2. Structure A provides greatest resistance to valgus stress at 70º of flexion**

## Answer explanation

This javelin thrower has a torn ulnar collateral ligament (UCL) which has 3 bundles, the most important of which is the anterior oblique ligament. The anterior oblique ligament provides the primary valgus restraint to the elbow from 30-120º of flexion.

The UCL is an important medial elbow structure and helps create stability throughout the full elbow range of motion. The UCL consists of 3 bundles - the anterior, transverse and posterior. The anterior bundle is the most important stabilizer to valgus stress, primarily functioning to stabilize the elbow from 30-120º. It is subdivided further into anterior and posterior bands. The bands are relatively isometric throughout elbow range of motion (ROM), except that the posterior band tightens during deeper degrees of flexion and the anterior band tightens during end range extension. The transverse bundle provides no meaningful contribution to elbow stability. The posterior bundle experiences a change in tension and becomes tighter as the elbow goes from extension to flexion, where it provides more stability. UCL reconstruction attempts to reconstruct the native ligament, primarily the anterior oblique ligament, by mimicking its anatomic attachments.

*Bryce et al. reviewed the anatomy and biomechanics of the elbow.* **They note** that the elbow joint is highly constrained by a group of static and dynamic structures in order to maximize stability. They review the specific anatomy and function of these structures in order to better understand and treat elbow injuries.

*Morrey et al. reviewed the ligamentous anatomy of the elbow based on cadaveric dissections of 10 specimens.* *They discussed the significance of lateral elbow structures including the lateral ulnar collateral ligament and medial structures including the ulnar collateral ligament and its 3 bundles.* **They note** that the anterior bundle of the UCL remained relatively isometric throughout an elbow range of motion while the posterior bundle tightens in flexion.

Figure A depicts the medial side of the elbow, particularly the ulnar collateral ligament bundles, labeled as follows: (A) anterior bundle (B) transverse bundle (C) posterior bundle.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: While the anterior bundle of the UCL is subdivided into anterior and posterior bands, the posterior band is not isometric and tightens during deeper flexion.
Answer 3: The transverse bundle provides minimal stability in resisting valgus stress at the elbow.
Answer 4: The posterior bundle is tightest in elbow flexion.
Answer 5: The anterior, not posterior, bundle provides the primary restraint to valgus stress at 30º of elbow flexion.
