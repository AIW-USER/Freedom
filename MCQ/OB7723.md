## Question

During spinal deformity surgery, which of the following is the most sensitive early indicator of an intraoperative injury to the spinal cord?

## Options

1. Somatosensory-evoked potentials (SEPs)
2. Transcranial motor-evoked potential monitoring (MEPs)
3. Transcutaneous electroencephalogram neuromonitoring
4. Stimulus-evoked transpedicular electromyography (EMG)
5. Brainstem auditory-evoked responses (BAERs)

## Correct option

**2. Transcranial motor-evoked potential monitoring (MEPs)**

## Answer explanation

When monitoring for spinal cord injury (SCI), the modality with the highest sensitivity is desired. Of which, transcranial motor-evoked potentials (MEPs) provide the most sensitive early indicator of an intraoperative spinal cord injury. The other tests like SEPs are more specific and are done in conjunction with MEPs to confirm SCI. Somatosensory-evoked potentials are routinely used but do not have the sensitivity of motor-evoked potentials. EMG evaluations are routinely used for root evaluation following pedicle screw placement. BAERs are typically used in monitoring brain surgery.
