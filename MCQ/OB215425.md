## Question

A 47-year-old male sustains the injury depicted in figure A during a motorcycle accident. If the treating surgeon elects to perform a primary arthrodesis, which of the following statements is true regarding the patient's prognosis?

![[q215425-a-lisfranc.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Decreased quality of reduction compared to ORIF
2. Higher wound infection rates compared to ORIF
3. Decreased overall reoperation rate compared to ORIF
4. Better functional outcomes are seen with medial and lateral column arthrodesis
5. Improved functional outcomes compared to ORIF

## Correct option

**3. Decreased overall reoperation rate compared to ORIF**

## Answer explanation

The patient has a Lisfranc injury. If treated with primary arthrodesis, this would result in decreased overall reoperation rate compared to open reduction and internal fixation (ORIF).

Lisfranc injuries involve the midfoot tarsometatarsal articulations, most commonly due to high-energy mechanisms. These injuries can easily be missed due to the subtle changes representing the injury on foot radiographs. The initial evaluation should consist of bilateral weight-bearing AP radiographs to improve injury detection. Treatment is almost always surgical and consists primarily of ORIF or primary arthrodesis. Several studies have not found a difference in either treatment option with respect to functional outcomes or complications. However, there has been a decreased overall reoperation rate due to decreased hardware removal with primary arthrodesis.

*Henning et al. performed a randomized controlled trial of 40 patients with Lisfranc injuries treated either with primary arthrodesis or ORIF.* **They reported** a 78.6% rate of planned and unplanned secondary surgeries in the ORIF group compared to the arthrodesis group with no difference in outcomes with respect to Short Form-36 and Short Musculoskeletal function assessment scores. **The authors concluded** that primary arthrodesis results in a significant reduction in repeat surgical procedures without an effect on outcome scores.

*Smith et al. performed a systematic review and meta-analysis of Lisfranc injuries treated with either ORIF or primary arthrodesis.* **They reported** a risk ratio of 0.23 (p < 0.001) for hardware removal (favoring arthrodesis), a risk ratio of 0.36 (p = 0.18) for other revision surgery, and no difference in patient-reported outcomes. **The authors concluded** that the treating surgeon should consider the increased risk of hardware removal associated with ORIF when treating Lisfranc injuries.

Figure A in an AP radiograph of the right foot with widening between the base of the 1st and 2nd metatarsals, consistent with a Lisfranc injury. An incidental finding of a bipartite medial sesamoid is also noted.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: No difference in the quality of reduction between ORIF and primary arthrodesis has been reported. However, overall outcomes are dependent on the quality of reduction.
Answer 2: There have been no studies reporting an increased infection risk with primary arthrodesis compared to ORIF.
Answer 4: Preservation of the mobility of the lateral column can lead to improved functional outcomes in patients undergoing primary arthrodesis.
Answer 5: No study has reported a difference in functional outcomes in patients treated with either primary arthrodesis or ORIF for the treatment of Lisfranc injuries.
