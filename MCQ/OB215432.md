## Question

Figure A is the AP radiograph of a 51-year-old male who presents to the ED after sustaining a fall in which he landed on his right shoulder. Given the appearance of the proximal humerus, a biopsy was obtained as demonstrated in Figure B. Which of the following is the most appropriate implant to treat this patient’s injury?

*Figure A*

![[q215432-b-renal-cc.jpg]]
*Figure B*

## Options

1. Proximal humerus locking plate after curettage and grafting
2. Proximal humerus locking plate with fibular strut
3. Antegrade humeral nail
4. Reverse shoulder arthroplasty
5. Proximal humerus replacement

## Correct option

**5. Proximal humerus replacement**

## Answer explanation

This patient presents with a pathologic proximal humerus fracture secondary to metastatic renal cell carcinoma, which would best be treated with proximal humerus resection and reconstruction.

Metastatic bone disease represents the second most common form of bone lesions in adults >40-years-old. Breast, lung, thyroid, kidney, and prostate are among the most common cancers to metastasize to bone. Upregulation of the osteoclast lineage is responsible for local bone destruction in the setting of metastatic disease. Management of metastatic renal cell carcinoma commonly involves preoperative embolization, wide resection and reconstruction, as it is not radiosensitive. This patient has significant destruction of the proximal humerus, further making proximal humerus resection and reconstruction the best option.

*Nota et al. reviewed functional outcomes and complications after oncologic reconstruction of proximal humerus lesions.* **They report** a greater than 50% prosthesis survival rate in this subset of patients with a higher risk of failure found in those patients receiving osteoarticular allografts. **They conclude**, however, that reconstruction using osteoarticular allografts, endoprosthes, or allograft-prosthetic composites all provide comparable outcomes in terms of function.

*Abdeen et al. reviewed allograft prosthesis composite reconstruction of the proximal part of the humerus.* **They report** only one glenohumeral dislocation in 36 patients. Further, **they report** that resection of the deltoid, either partial or complete, decreased overall range of motion but did not compromise Musculoskeletal Tumor Society Scores. **They concluded** allograft prosthesis composites result in a durable construct with acceptable complication rates.

Figure A demonstrates a pathologic fracture of the proximal humerus. Figure B is a histology slide demonstrating cells with clear cytoplasm consistent with clear cell renal carcinoma.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1-3: Metastatic renal cell carcinoma is best treated with resection and reconstruction.
Answer 4: Standard reverse shoulder arthroplasty would not provide adequate reconstruction following wide resection of the tumor.
