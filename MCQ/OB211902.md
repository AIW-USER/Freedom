## Question

Which of the following portends the highest risk of a diabetic patient developing the wound depicted in figure A?

![[q211902-a-screen-shot-2021-01-31-at-11-48-01-am.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Absent plantar sensation with 5.07-gauge monofilament testing
2. Transcutaneous oxygen tension of 25 mmHg
3. Ankle-brachial index of 0.60
4. Albumin of 3.0 g/dL
5. Improved ankle dorsiflexion with knee flexion

## Correct option

**1. Absent plantar sensation with 5.07-gauge monofilament testing**

## Answer explanation

Sensory dysfunction with a resultant loss of protective sensation is the primary risk factor for the development of a diabetic foot ulcer. The 5.07-gauge Semmes-Weinstein monofilament test is used to screen for diabetic neuropathy.

The primary risk factor for diabetic foot ulcer development is the loss of protective sensation, which is identified using a 5.07-gauge Semmes-Weinstein monofilament. Once an ulcer is present various diagnostics are used to determine healing potential. These include non-invasive vascular studies, such as ankle-brachial index (ABI) and transcutaneous oxygen tension, and nutrition labs such as serum albumin. The presence of gastrocnemius or Achilles tendon tightness can help guide treatment with either a gastrocnemius recession or tendoachilles lengthening (TAL), respectively.

*Dros et al. investigated the accuracy of monofilament testing to diagnose peripheral neuropathy in a systematic review.* **They reported** that the sensitivity ranged from 41%-93% and specificity ranged from 68%-100%. **They highlighted** that electrodiagnostic tests can be complex, expensive, and time-consuming, which hampers their widespread use, especially in primary care, where for most patients peripheral neuropathy is diagnosed and treated.

*Feng et al. reviewed the Semmes-Weinstein monofilament examination as a screening tool for diabetic peripheral neuropathy.* **They reported** that the Semmes-Weinstein test had a sensitivity ranging from 57%-93% and specificity 75%-100%. **They recommended** that to maximize the diagnostic value of Semmes-Weinstein testing, a three-site test involving the plantar aspects of the great toe, the third metatarsal, and the fifth metatarsals should be used.

Figure A depicts a diabetic ulcer about the lateral aspect of the foot.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Transcutaneous oxygen tension is used to assess wound healing potential and is not the most sensitive test for peripheral neuropathy. Pressures > 30 mmHg (or 40 mmHg depending on source cited) would portend good healing potential
Answer 3: The ankle-brachial index is used to assess wound healing potential and is not the most sensitive test for peripheral neuropathy. A value >0.60 is typically needed to heal an ulcer.
Answer 4: Resolution of foot erythema with elevation can help differentiate Charcot arthropathy from infection, but is not the most sensitive exam finding in patients with peripheral neuropathy.
Answer 5: Silverskiöld testing can help guide the treatment of diabetic foot ulcers by differentiating Achilles tendon and gastrocnemius tightness. This test is not used to diagnose peripheral neuropathy.
