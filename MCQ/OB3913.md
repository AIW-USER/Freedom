## Question

A 56-year-old woman presents 23 months following the forefoot procedure for painful arthritis and hallux valgus shown in Figure A (her current radiographs). She reports pain and irritation of her dorsal great toe with closed-toe shoewear. She had her hardware removed 8 months ago due to persistent dorsal symptoms. Despite this, her symptoms have persisted. What is the most likely cause of her symptoms?

![[q3913-a-screen-shot-2020-01-03-at-11-57-18-am.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Neuropathy
2. Nonunion
3. Transfer metatarsalgia
4. Plantarflexion malunion
5. Dorsiflexion malunion

## Correct option

**5. Dorsiflexion malunion**

## Answer explanation

The patient has developed a dorsiflexion malunion following a 1st MTP arthrodesis for hallux rigidus.

Arthritis of the first metatarsophalangeal joint may occur for a variety of reasons: trauma, hallux valgus (bunion), and an elevated first metatarsal are the most typical. An arthrodesis is considered for severe disease and the union rate is around 94%. Malunion may occur in the coronal or sagittal plane. Patients with a dorsiflexion malunion will often still be functional but have issues with shoewear. Patients who are fused in plantarflexion often have more difficulty with ambulation and supinate their foot to accommodate.

*Coughlin et al. published their results on operations in patients with hallux rigidus and their well-known hallux rigidus grading system.* *Cheilectomy was used with predictable success to treat Grades 1, 2, and selected Grade 3 cases.* In patients with Grade 4 hallux rigidus or Grade 3 with <50% of the metatarsal head cartilage remaining at the time of surgery, **they recommended** arthrodesis.

*Lau et al. reviewed two commonly used alternative procedures for patients with hallux rigidus: cheilectomy and interposition arthroplasty.* Patient satisfaction was 87.5% in cheilectomies and 72.7% in interpositional arthroplasties. Interpositional arthroplasty resulted in a weak push-off of the hallux. Pedobarographic analysis demonstrated a decreased load under the great toe with increased weight transfer to the lesser metatarsal heads in all patients.

Figure A shows a lateral radiograph of a dorsiflexion malunion of the 1st MTP joint following a plate and screw construct with subsequent hardware removal. Illustration A is the Coughlin hallux rigidus grading scheme.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: There is no evidence from the stem that this patient had neuropathy.
Answer 2: Patients with a nonunion classically have activity-related pain and persistent swelling, it is often relieved with stiff-soled shoes. The radiographs in this patient also show a union.
Answer 3: Patients with transfer metatarsalgia describe plantar pain over the second metatarsal head.
Answer 4: When the 1st MTP joint is fused in a plantarflexed position, gait becomes difficult as the push-off position is altered. Some patients will walk on the outside of their foot when this occurs.
