## Question

Most patients with intrinsic brainstem gliomas initially present with

## Options

1. Cranial neuropathies
2. Headache
3. Hydrocephalus
4. Nausea and vomiting

## Correct option

**1. Cranial neuropathies**

## Answer explanation

Ans: A (cranial neuropathies)

- BRAIN STEM GLIOMAS (BSG) are one of the three most common brain tumors in pediatric age groups comprising ≈ 10–20% of pediatric CNS tumors. They mostly tend to occur during childhood and adolescence (77% are < 20 yrs. old, they comprise 1% of adult tumors).
- The initial symptoms in most patients with brainstem gliomas are cranial neuropathies followed by weakness or ataxia.
- Headache, nausea and vomiting, and papilledema usually occur later in the course of the illness.
- Upper brainstem tumors tend to present with cerebellar findings and hydrocephalus, whereas lower brainstem tumors tend to present with multiple lower cranial nerve deficits and long tract findings. Due to their invasive nature, signs and symptoms usually do not occur until the tumor is fairly extensive in size.
- MRI can differentiate malignant from benign lesions.
- Trend: lower grade tumors tend to occur in the upper brainstem, and higher-grade tumors in the lower brainstem/medulla.
- Most are malignant, have poor prognosis, and are not surgical candidates.
- Role of surgery is primarily limited to dorsally exophytic lesions and shunting (when there is hydrocephalus).
