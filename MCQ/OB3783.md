## Question

Figures A and B are the sagittal and axial MRI images of a 74-year-old female that presents to the office with neck pain and progressive change in her handwriting. On exam, she has a positive Hoffmann sign and ambulates with a wide-based and unsteady gait. What is the most likely outcome following surgical intervention?

## Options

1. Improvement in gait disturbance
2. Halt progression of gradual, continuous neurologic decline
3. Improvement in sensory deficit
4. Halt progression of stepwise neurologic decline
5. Resolution of MRI based spinal cord signal change

## Correct option

**4. Halt progression of stepwise neurologic decline**

## Answer explanation

In patients with cervical myelopathy, the primary intent for surgery is to halt the progression of neurologic decline.

The natural history of cervical myelopathy is a stepwise decline with periods of neurologic stability. When considering surgery, the clinical severity of myelopathy is the most important factor. For patients with moderate myelopathy, severe neuroradiologic findings (smaller cord area, cord signal changes indicative of myelomalacia, kyphotic deformity, etc.) suggest a need for earlier surgery. Following decompression cessation of neurologic decline is the most reliable outcome with variable improvements in gait, sensory, and motor exam.

Emery. et al. reviewed the diagnosis and treatment of patients with cervical spondylotic myelopathy. They report that approximately 15% of patients with severe myelopathy do not have neck pain, and preferentially involve the proximal muscle groups of the lower extremity more than distal motor groups (opposite in lumbar stenosis patients where distal motor groups are more affected than proximal motor groups). They conclude that operative intervention in cervical myelopathy (regardless of approach) is reliable for neurologic preservation.

*Boden et al. studied the incidence of neuroradiologic findings of cervical disease in asymptomatic patients.* **They report** that 20% of asymptomatic patients had abnormal results, and that disc degeneration was present at one or more levels in 60% of patients older than 40. **They conclude** that operative indication should be made on both imaging findings and physical exam.

Figure A is a sagittal slice of an MRI scan of a patient with multilevel cervical stenosis and a kyphotic deformity. Figure B is an axial MRI image demonstrating narrowing in the space available for the spinal cord.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Improvement in gait disturbance is possible but not reliable
Answer 2: While decompression for cervical myelopathy halts neurologic decline, cervical myelopathy patients classically have a stepwise decline, not a gradual, continuous type
Answer 3: Sensory deficits variably improve following decompression
Answer 5: Spinal cord signal change may regress, but this is not the most reliable outcome
