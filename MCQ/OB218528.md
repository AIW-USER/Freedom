## Question

A 72-year-old female patient presents with severe lower back pain and decreased activity levels over the last 5 days. Her primary care physician ordered an X-ray and diagnosed her with an acute osteoporotic vertebral compression fracture. Based on the AAOS Clinical Practice Guidelines, which of the following is a supported treatment for this patient?

## Options

1. Vertebroplasty
2. Kyphoplasty
3. Prolonged best rest followed by gradual return to activities
4. Activity modification with use of calcitonin
5. Initial trial with opioid analgesics and bracing

## Correct option

**4. Activity modification with use of calcitonin**

## Answer explanation

According to AAOS Clinical Practice Guidelines (adopted 9/24/2010), the only treatment that received a moderate recommendation is the use of calcitonin for 4 weeks. Specifically, this should only be given if the patient presents within 5 days of the onset of symptoms. Calcitonin use is noted to decrease pain and improve these patients' activity levels.

According to AAOS Clinical Practice Guidelines for osteoporotic spinal compression fractures, the only "strong recommendation" was AGAINST the use of vertebroplasty. The use of calcitonin for acute injuries (presenting within 5 days of symptom onset) received a "moderate recommendation." Calcitonin directly inhibits osteoclast activity by binding to cell-surface receptors. The following modalities of treatment either had limited evidence or were simply inconclusive: ibandronate or strontium use, bed rest and opiate analgesics, L2 nerve block for an L3 or L4 fracture, exercise program, electrical stimulation, kyphoplasty, use of a brace, improvement of kyphosis angle, any treatment in a patient with concomitant neurological deficits.

*Esses et al. reviewed the treatment of osteoporotic vertebral compression fractures.* **They note** the only strong evidence available is against the use of vertebroplasty. The only moderate evidence available is for the use of calcitonin for 4 weeks following a compression fracture. All other treatments have limited or inconclusive evidence.

*Kallmes et al. performed a multicenter trial that randomly assigned 131 patients with one to three painful osteoporotic vertebral compression fractures to undergo either vertebroplasty or a simulated procedure without cement.* **They found** no difference in improvements in pain and pain-related disability between the two groups.

*Funayama et al. prospectively studied the effects of therapeutic 2-week bed rest for the treatment of osteoporotic vertebral compression fractures.* **They reported** a significantly decreased rate of surgical treatment in patients with poor-prognostic MRI findings (localized high signal and diffuse low signal on T2-weighted images) that were treated with initial 2-week bedrest. **The authors recommended** initial bed rest for patients with an osteoporotic vertebral compression fracture and poor-prognostic MRI findings.

Incorrect answers:
Answers 1 and 2: Vertebroplasty is strongly recommended against and kyphoplasty received an inconclusive recommendation from the AAOS.
Answer 3: Although recent studies have found benefits from initial 2-week bed rest for certain compression fractures. Prolonged bed rest has not been supported for the routine treatment of compression fractures.
Answer 5: Initial treatment with bracing and opioid analgesics is not recommended.
