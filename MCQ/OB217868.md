## Question

A 71-year-old male presents with worsening right knee pain and decides to undergo a revision total knee arthroplasty. He previously underwent a revision 10 years ago. Prior to proceeding with surgery, the patient is informed that a tibial tubercle osteotomy may be needed. Which of the following is likely present?

## Options

1. Patellar maltracking
2. Extensive metaphyseal bone loss
3. Extensor lag
4. Presence of a well-fixed tibial cone and cemented stem
5. Elevated joint line

## Correct option

**4. Presence of a well-fixed tibial cone and cemented stem**

## Answer explanation

A tibial tubercle osteotomy can afford excellent exposure in revision total knee arthroplasty to remove a well-fixed cemented long tibial stem.

Revision total knee arthroplasty can require extensile approaches due to scar tissue formation, capsular contractures, and stemmed implants. Previous knee surgeries, such as high tibial osteotomy, can cause patellar tendon contracture leading to patellar baja. This can manifest as anterior knee pain during flexion. A well-fixed cone and cemented long tibial stem can pose a challenge for complete removal. Performing a tibia tubercle osteotomy can provide extensile exposure of the prosthesis to facilitate removal.

Abdel and Della Valle reviewed surgical approaches for revision total knee arthroplasty. They stated that in most cases a standard medial parapatellar approach is sufficient to achieve surgical goals. However, a tibial tubercle osteotomy may be necessary when removing a long cemented tibial stem.

*Della Valle et al. retrospectively studied 126 revision total knee arthroplasty cases performed through a medial capsular approach.* **They reported** nine cases that required a quadriceps snip. **They stated** if a more extensible approach was needed a tibial tubercle osteotomy or V-Y quadricepsplasty was performed.

*Whiteside retrospectively studied 136 total knee arthroplasties performed with a tibial tubercle osteotomy.* **The author reported** a 93.7º average range of motion and two knees had extension lag that was present preoperatively. The author urged caution when performing manipulations to improve knee range of motion.

Incorrect answers
Answer 1: A tibial tubercle osteotomy is unnecessary to address patellar maltracking.
Answer 2: Metaphyseal bone loss is addressed with cones or stem fixation.
Answer 3: Extensor lag is addressed with extensor mechanism reconstruction.
Answer 5: Restoration of the joint line is addressed with distal femoral augments.
