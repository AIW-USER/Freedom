## Question

In children, a diagnosis of osteomyelitis with concomitant deep venous thrombosis (DVT) has a high association with which causative organism?

## Options

1. Kingella kingae
2. Mycobacterium tuberculosis
3. Methicillin resistant Staphylococcus aureus
4. Methicillin sensitive Staphylococcus aureus
5. Salmonella

## Correct option

**3. Methicillin resistant Staphylococcus aureus**

## Answer explanation

DVT in association with musculoskeletal infection is most likely caused by methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus (MRSA).

Risk factors for DVT in the pediatric population include age older than 8 years old, CRP> 6mg/L, presence of MRSA associated infection, as well as a need for surgical intervention. While a malignant process remains on the differential, with a relatively acute onset presentation, fever, and the associated positive blood cultures; infection (specifically, osteomyelitis) remains at the top of the differential.

*Hollmig et al. retrospectively assessed 212 children with a diagnosis of osteomyelitis over a 2-year period.* Eleven of the children had both DVT and osteomyelitis and when compared to the non-DVT group, had significantly higher CRP (>6mg/L), were older (>8 years old), and had a higher rate of MRSA as the causative agent (73% vs. 24%).

*Gonzalez et al. prospecitvely followed 9 children with concomitant DVT and MRSA-associated osteomyelitis.* Notable associations were increased ICU admissions, invasive procedures, and a propensity to cause septic emboli. Also, the most common location for DVT was the femoral vein with extension into the popliteal system.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1,2,4,5: These organisms have not been shown to have a high association with concurrent DVT and musculoskeletal infection, however, can each be causes of osteomyelitis.
