## Options

1. Chondroblastoma
2. Giant Cell Tumor
3. Aneurysmal Bone Cyst
4. Osteoblastoma
5. Chondromyxoid fibroma

## Correct option

**1. Chondroblastoma**

## Answer explanation

Based on the history, imaging and histology provided, the diagnosis is chondroblastoma.

Chondroblastoma is a benign chondrogenic lytic lesion that often is found in the epiphysis of young patients. This typically occurs in the talus, but can occur in the calcaneus. Patients may have tenderness over the affected bones. On plain radiographs, there is often a well-defined epiphyseal lytic lesion. Bone cysts are more common in anterior process of the calcaneus. Histologically, chondroblastomas show focal areas of chondroid matrix with scattered multinucleated giant cells. This is in addition to the classic chondroblastic appearance.

Douis and Saifuddin review the radiographic appearance of chondroblastoma lesions. On plain radiographs, the lesion is often 1-4 cm in dimension and lobular in nature. It is often located adjacent to the growth plate. On CT scan, sclerotic margins, mineralization of the matrix and extraosseous involvement may be seen. Furthermore, MRI scans may be used to look for possible recurrence of a previously treated lesion as indicated by the presence of edema within the lesion.

Figure A shows a lateral radiograph of a right foot with a lytic lesion in the calcaneus. Note the surrounding sclerosis. Figure B shows an axial CT scan sequence demonstrating the size and extent of lysis of the calcaneus in the anterior-posterior dimension. Note the calcifications on the axial sequences that are suggestive of chondroblastoma. Figure C shows an axial T2 weighted MRI sequence with significant edema noted in the region of the lesion. Figure D shows the histologic representation of a chondroblastoma lesion.

Incorrect Answers
Answer 2: Giant cell tumors can cause lytic lesions of bone. Histologic evaluation reveals multiple giant cells with neoplastic mononuclear stromal cells.
Answer 3: Aneurysmal bone cysts can cause lytic lessons of bone. Histologic evaluation reveals cavernous spaces filled with blood without an endothelial lining.
Answer 4: Osteoblastoma can cause a lytic lesions of bone. Histologic evaluation reveals a nidus of immature osteoid and osteoblasts amongst a fibrovascular stroma.
Answer 5: Chondromyxoid fibroma can cause a lytic lesion of bone. Histologic evaluation reveals lobules of fibromyxoid tissue that contain stellate shaped cells
