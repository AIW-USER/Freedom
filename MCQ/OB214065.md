## Question

A prospective, randomized controlled trial of 100 patients undergoing reverse total shoulder arthroplasty is performed to determine if lateralization of the glenoid baseplate increases external rotation compared with a medialized Grammont design. The study found no difference in external rotation motion between groups (p = .42). Since then, several multicenter prospective, randomized controlled trials have demonstrated a significant increase in external rotation motion with the use of a lateralized design (p = .03). Which of the following errors most likely occurred in the first study?

## Options

1. Alpha error
2. Measurement error
3. Sampling error
4. Type I error
5. Type II error

## Correct option

**5. Type II error**

## Answer explanation

The first study that was performed accepted the null hypothesis, when in fact it is false. This is a type-II (beta) error.

Several types of study errors may occur. Type-I (alpha) errors reject the null hypothesis when it should have been accepted. The probability of a type I error is determined by the alpha level error, which is significant at 0.05. Therefore, a finding that has less than a 1/20 chance that the observed association is by chance alone, is accepted. Conversely, Type-II (beta) errors accept the null hypothesis when it should be rejected. In this example, the null hypothesis is that lateralization of the glenoid baseplate does not improve external rotation. The initial study showed no statistically significant difference, therefore the null hypothesis is accepted. However, several studies have disproved the initial study's findings. Since the null hypothesis should have been rejected, a type II (beta) error occurred, likely to the initial study being underpowered.

*Fosgate et al. reviewed how sample size influences the likelihood that a study will yield statistically significant results.* **They found** that the reasons inadequately sized studies do not achieve statistical significance are failure to perform sample size calculations, selecting sample size based on convenience, insufficient funding for the study, and inefficient utilization of available funding. **They noted** that Type I (alpha) and type II (beta) errors are associated with rejection of the null hypothesis when it is true and the nonrejection of the null hypothesis when it is false, respectively. **They concluded** that sample sizes are directly dependent on the assumptions used for their calculation.

*Lochner et al. reviewed randomized trials involving fracture care to evaluate type-II error rates and study power (1 - beta) for the primary outcomes.* **They found** that the mean overall study power among the 117 trials was 24.65%, and the type-II error rate for primary outcomes was 90.52%. **They concluded** that the mean type-II error rates in the orthopaedic trauma trials exceeded accepted standards and that investigators can reduce type-II error rates by performing power and sample-size calculations prior to conducting a trial.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1&4: Type-I (alpha) errors reject the null hypothesis when it should have been accepted
Answer 2: Observational error (or measurement error) is the difference between a measured value of a quantity and its true value
Answer 3: Sampling errors are incurred when the statistical characteristics of a population are estimated from a subset, or sample, of that population
