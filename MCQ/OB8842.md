## Question

The technique shown in Figure A is used to identify and protect the following structure?

![[q8842-a-screen-shot-2017-03-28-at-5-22-32-pm.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Saphenous nerve
2. Saphenous vein
3. Superficial peroneal nerve
4. Peroneal artery
5. Tibial nerve

## Correct option

**3. Superficial peroneal nerve**

## Answer explanation

Figure A shows transillumination of the lateral ankle during ankle arthroscopy. Transillumination helps to identify the SPN by projecting its shadow against the skin.

The rate of nerve injury with ankle arthroscopy varies in the literature from 3.5-10%. Injury most commonly occurs during placement of the ankle portals. For example, the superficial peroneal nerve can be injured during placement of the anterolateral portal. Techniques to reduce injury include trying to palpate the nerve, using blunt dissection techniques and transilluminating the joint to visualize the nerve deep to the skin. .

*Young et al. reviewed the complications of ankle arthroscopy.* An overall complication rate of 6.8% was observed. In 16/20 patients the complications were neurologic. Of which, 6/20 were related to the anterolateral portal. They comment on the importance of good technique and blunt dissecting techniques.

*Barber et al. reviewed the complications of ankle arthroscopy.* Wound and post-operative infection rates are extremely low, at less than 1%, respectively. Pain at the portal sites may be related to sensitive scar formation, nerve compression or painful neuroma.

Figure A shows a cadaveric ankle with a ankle arthroscopy in the anteromedial portal. The light from the scope is illuminating the lateral ankle allowing for the neurovascular structures to project a shadow against the skin, which allows the surgeon to minimize the risk of injuring these structures during portal placement.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1, 2: The saphenous vein and nerve can be injured with placement of the anteromedial portal.
Answer 4,5: The peroneal and tibial artery is not usually at risk with placement of the anterolateral portal.
