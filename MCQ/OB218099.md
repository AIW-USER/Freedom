## Question

A 37-year-old male mountain biker presents to your clinic with a chief complaint of left shoulder pain. He notes that he sustained a fall while riding this past weekend, and his shoulder has appeared "swollen" since then. Radiographs are obtained, and available for review in Figure A. His contralateral shoulder is pain-free and has a normal radiographic appearance. Which of the following best describes the orientation and role of the injured ligaments most likely responsible for the appearance of his proximal and middle clavicle on his radiograph?

## Options

1. The conoid ligament attaches approximately 2-3 cm medial to the lateral edge of the clavicle, is anterolateral to the trapezoid ligament, and is most important for the vertical stability of the clavicle relative to the coracoid
2. The conoid ligament attaches approximately 4-4.5 cm medial to the lateral edge of the clavicle, is anterolateral to the trapezoid ligament, and is most important for the vertical stability of the clavicle relative to the coracoid
3. The conoid ligament attaches approximately 4-4.5 cm medial to the lateral edge of the clavicle, is posteromedial to the trapezoid tubercle, and is most important for the vertical stability of the clavicle relative to the coracoid
4. The trapezoid ligament attaches approximately 4-4.5 cm medial to the lateral edge of the clavicle, is posteromedial to the conoid tubercle, and is most important for the vertical stability of the clavicle relative to the coracoid
5. The trapezoid ligament attaches approximately 2-3 cm medial to the lateral edge of the clavicle, is anterolateral to the conoid tubercle, and is most important for the vertical stability of the clavicle relative to the coracoid

## Correct option

**3. The conoid ligament attaches approximately 4-4.5 cm medial to the lateral edge of the clavicle, is posteromedial to the trapezoid tubercle, and is most important for the vertical stability of the clavicle relative to the coracoid**

## Answer explanation

This patient has sustained a distal-third clavicle fracture and has superior displacement of the proximal and medial clavicle relative to the coracoid, indicating injury to the coracoclavicular (CC) ligaments. With respect to the CC ligaments, the conoid ligament, which attaches to the clavicle approximately 4.5 cm medial to the lateral edge and posteromedial to the trapezoid tubercle, is the most important ligament for maintaining verticle stability of the clavicle (Answer 3).

Distal clavicle fractures are traumatic injuries typically resulting from direct shoulder trauma. These are more common in adult than pediatric patients and account for 10-25% of clavicle fractures. Fracture displacement corresponds to various factors including fracture location, fracture pattern, the integrity of the CC ligaments and the patient's age.

The stability of the clavicle relative to the acromion and coracoid is dependent on the integrity of the acromioclavicular (AC) and CC ligaments, respectively (Illustration A). The AC ligament provides anterior/posterior stability and inserts approximately 6mm medial to the clavicle's distal end with the ligament's posterosuperior aspect contributing the greatest to AC joint stability. The CC ligamentous complex is comprised of the conoid and trapezoid ligament. The conoid attaches to the clavicle posteromedial to the trapezoid ligament, approximately 4-4.5 cm medial to the lateral edge of the clavicle, and is the most important contributor to the vertical stability of the clavicle relative to the coracoid.

*Dawson et al., in a biomechanical study, investigated the relative contribution of the AC joint capsule and the CC ligaments to AC joint stability.* *Their study utilized 6 cadaver-matched pairs, and either the AC joint capsule or CC ligaments were transected, and various measurements of displacement were taken.* Overall, transection of the AC joint capsule led to significantly increased anteroposterior (AP) translation, and transection of the CC ligaments led to significantly increased superior translation. **The authors conclude** that the AC joint capsule is the primary restraint to AP translation of the clavicle relative to the acromion and that the CC ligaments are the primary restraint to superior translation of the clavicle relative to the acromion and coracoid.

*Debski et al. investigated ligament mechanics during three-degree-of-freedom motion at the AC joint.* *Their study utilized 11 cadaver-matched pairs.* Overall, **the authors found** that in response to an anterior load, the force in the AC joint capsule was far higher than that seen in the CC ligaments and that in response to a superior load, the load was greatest in the conoid ligament. **The authors conclude** that with respect to resistance to superior displacement, the conoid ligament appears to provide the primary stabilizing effect.

Figure A is an anteroposterior radiograph demonstrating a distal third clavicle fracture with > 100% superior displacement of the clavicle, indicative of injury to the CC ligaments. Illustration A is a graphical representation of the ligamentous stabilizers of the clavicle. Note the orientation of the conoid and trapezoid ligaments, with the conoid attaching posteromedial relative to the trapezoid.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The conoid ligament attaches 4-4.5 cm medial to the lateral edge of the clavicle, not 2-3 cm medial.
Answer 2: The conoid ligament is posteromedial, not anterolateral, to the trapezoid ligament.
Answers 4 and 5: The conoid, not the trapezoid, ligament is the primary restraint to the superior displacement of the clavicle.
