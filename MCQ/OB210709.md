## Question

A 6-year-old boy presents to the emergency room after a fall from his bicycle with an isolated left elbow injury. A radiographic series is obtained and the fracture line is only appreciated on the internal oblique view. The greatest distance between the humerus and the fracture fragment is measured to be 2 mm. The patient's mother is strongly opposed to operative intervention and he is placed in a cast. However, the resident explains to her that this injury has the highest rate of nonunion about the elbow and that close follow-up will be necessary. Which of the following injuries is described in this scenario?

## Options

1. Medial condyle fracture
2. Lateral condyle fracture
3. Radial neck fracture
4. Transphyseal separation
5. Supracondylar humerus fracture

## Correct option

**2. Lateral condyle fracture**

## Answer explanation

Lateral condyle fractures have a higher risk of nonunion when treated non-operatively compared to other pediatric elbow fractures.

Pediatric lateral condyle fractures have a higher rate of nonunion than other elbow fractures. This may be due to minimal metaphyseal bone on the distal fragment, intra-articular location, fracture motion by the pull of the extensor tendons, tenuous blood supply, or instability in a cast. Displaced lateral condyle fractures >2mm are recommended to be treated operatively to achieve anatomic articular reduction and pinning. Other less common complications are stiffness, osteonecrosis, tardy ulnar nerve palsy, and fishtail deformity. Close-followup of these fractures is therefore recommended.

*Pirker et al. retrospectively reviewed 51 minimally displaced pediatric lateral condyle fractures (<2mm displacement) to determine the rate of displacement over time.* All were treated with long arm splints, repeat radiographs after 3-7 days, surgical treatment if displaced at that time, or casting for 3 weeks if not displaced. **They found** 12% of fractures displaced within the 1st week and none displaced after that, so they consider conservative management acceptable for these minimally displaced fractures.

*Finnbogason et al. prospectively followed 112 children with minimally displaced lateral condyle fractures (<2mm), also treated with long-arm plaster splinting, but categorized the fractures into 3 grades of fracture severity.* **They found** 0% of Type A fractures (no gapping or intra-articular extension), 17% of Type B (extension to articular cartilage), and 42% of Type C (equal displacement throughout fracture length) fractures displaced during 2-4 weeks of follow-up. They do not make clear treatment recommendations, but caution that a subgroup of minimally displaced fractures do have a high rate of subsequent displacement.

*Flynn* **noted** the risk of nonunion in even minimally displaced lateral condyle fractures. 23 cases of "slightly displaced" lateral condyle fractures developed a nonunion and were therefore deemed to have been inadequately treated. All were initially treated non-operatively. Only 11 of the 23 had good outcomes after subsequent surgery after nonunion.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Medial condyle fractures are less likely to achieve nonunions than lateral condyle fractures.
Answer 3: An internal oblique image radiograph does not aid in the diagnosis of a radial neck fracture, radiocapitellar view is used for this diagnosis.
Answer 4: Transphyseal separations are more common in children <3 years; these injuries can be confused with elbow dislocations and an arthrogram can aid in diagnosis.
Answer 5: Supracondylar humerus fractures are not prone to nonunion but can result in malunion if inadequately treated; treated is largely dictated by fracture type based on the Gartland Classification.
