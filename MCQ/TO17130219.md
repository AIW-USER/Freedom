Chapter: Metabolic Bone Disorder MCQs — Osteoporosis and Fragility Fractures

## Question

Which is true about anti osteoporotic treatment?

## Options

1. Effects of 2 years teriparatide are long lasting and after that antiresorptive treatment (bisphosphonates) need not be given
2. Coadministration of denosumab with TPTD blunts the response of teriparatide and is not recommended.
3. Effect of denosumab on BMD is short lived and after therapy bisphosphonates should be considered to prevent the rebound increase in bone turnover
4. Romosozumab, a fully humanized monoclonal antibody directed against RANKL, is effective in preventing osteoporosis.

## Correct option

**3. Effect of denosumab on BMD is short lived and after therapy bisphosphonates should be considered to prevent the rebound increase in bone turnover**

## Answer explanation

Teriparatide: Teriparatide (TPTD) is the 1–34 fragment of PTH. It is an effective treatment for osteoporosis when administered subcutaneously in a dose of 20 μg daily. Teriparatide stimulates bone remodeling but the balance of effect is to stimulate bone formation more than resorption, resulting in an increase in bone density.

In one study coadministration of alendronic acid with TPTD was found to blunt the anabolic effect of PTH in men by inhibiting bone formation56; although in another small study, alendronic acid did not appear to blunt the anabolic effects of TPTD in postmenopausal women as judged by measurements of bone formation markers.35 There is evidence from larger-scale studies that previous antiresorptive therapy does blunt (but does not abolish) the BMD response to TPTD

The recommended duration of therapy is 2 years at which point patients should be given an antiresorptive agent to prevent loss of the bone that has been newly formed. Raloxifene, alendronic acid, and zoledronic acid have all been reported to be effective at maintaining the increases in bone mass in patients previously treated with PTH.

 It is of interest that concomitant administration of intravenous zoledronic acid with PTH does not blunt the BMD response to TPTD, and that combined therapy with denosumab and TPTD results in an enhanced response of BMD.

Denosumab: It is a fully humanized monoclonal antibody directed against RANKL, a key stimulator of bone resorption. It has powerful inhibitory effects on bone resorption and is given subcutaneously in a dose of 60 mg every 6 months in the treatment of osteoporosis. Unlike bisphosphonates, the effect of denosumab on bone resorption is relatively short-lived and when treatment is stopped there is a rebound increase in bone turnover with significant bone loss. In some studies, an increased risk of vertebral fractures has been reported on cessation of treatment. Accordingly, treatment with denosumab must be given on a continuing basis for a sustained therapeutic effect and when treatment is stopped it is recommended that a bisphosphonate should be considered to prevent the rebound increase in bone turnover.

Newer antiresorptive agents:

Romosozumab: Romosozumab is a monoclonal antibody that neutralizes the effects of sclerostin, thereby increasing bone formation and suppressing bone resorption. It is administered by subcutaneous injection in a dose of 120 mg once monthly for a period of 12 months.

Abaloparatide: Abaloparatide is the 1–34 fragment of parathyroid hormone related protein (PTHrP). It acts in a similar manner to teriparatide to increase bone remodeling, stimulating bone formation more than bone resorption. The pharmacodynamic effect of abaloparatide differs from that of teriparatide, however; in that, it seems to preferentially bind to the PTH 1 receptor in a conformation that gives a more transient activation of cyclic AMP.

Answer : C
