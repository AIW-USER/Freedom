Chapter: Arthroplasty MCQs — Unicondylar and Patellofemoral Arthroplasty

## Question

What is the primary significance of the 'Anteromedial Osteoarthritis' (AMOA) concept in the success of UKA?

## Options

1. It justifies using a fixed-bearing.
2. It eliminates patellofemoral symptoms.
3. It ensures the deformity is correctable.
4. It allows for PCL sacrifice.

## Correct option

**3. It ensures the deformity is correctable.**

## Answer explanation

AMOA implies that the wear is confined to the anterior and medial aspects and that the ligaments (especially the ACL and MCL) are intact. Because the ligaments are intact, the varus deformity is typically intra-articular and correctable once the cartilage height is restored.

The Concept of Anteromedial Osteoarthritis (AMOA)

The modern indication for a medial UKR relies entirely on the concept of AMOA (first described by White et al.). The fundamental rule is that the anterior cruciate ligament (ACL) must be functionally intact.

When the ACL is intact, it prevents anterior translation of the tibia during flexion. Consequently, the femoral condyle rolls and glides normally, confining cartilage wear to the anterior and middle thirds of the medial tibial plateau.

If the ACL is deficient, the tibia subluxes anteriorly, causing the femur to articulate further back on the tibia. This shifts the wear pattern to the posterior tibial plateau. Therefore, posterior tibial erosion is an absolute contraindication for a standard UKR, as it indicates ACL deficiency.

In a true AMOA knee:

 - There is bone-on-bone contact in extension.

 - The joint space may appear partially preserved in deep flexion (because the posterior cartilage is spared).

 - The lateral compartment has full-thickness cartilage.

Answer: 3
