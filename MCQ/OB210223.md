## Question

The Pavlik harness has been integral in the treatment of developmental dysplasia of the hip (DDH). If the practitioner is not mindful of hip positioning, complications may occur. Which of the following correctly pairs hip positioning with its associated complication?

## Options

1. Excessive flexion: osteonecrosis; Excessive abduction: femoral nerve palsy
2. Excessive extension: osteonecrosis, Excessive abduction: obturator nerve palsy
3. Excessive adduction: osteonecrosis; Excessive extension: sciatic nerve palsy
4. Excessive flexion: compartment syndrome; Excessive extension: loss of pulses
5. Excessive flexion: femoral nerve palsy; Excessive abduction: osteonecrosis

## Correct option

**5. Excessive flexion: femoral nerve palsy; Excessive abduction: osteonecrosis**

## Answer explanation

Excessive hip flexion is associated with femoral nerve palsy while excessive hip abduction is associated with osteonecrosis of the femoral head.

The Pavlik harness is a dynamic positioning device that allows the child to move freely within the confines of its restraints. It is composed of a circumferential chest strap with shoulder straps that allow sites of attachment for lower-extremity straps. The anterior lower-extremity straps flex the hips while the posterior lower-extremity straps prevent adduction to the hips. Hip flexion should not exceed 90 degrees due to the risk of developing a femoral nerve palsy. Abduction of the hips should not exceed 70 degrees, which is associated with avascular necrosis due to impingement of the posterosuperior retinacular branch of the medial femoral circumflex artery.

*Murnaghan et al. performed a retrospective review of all patients who underwent Pavlik harness treatment for DDH within a seventeen-year period.* **They identified** 30 infants with femoral nerve palsy, an incidence of 2.5%. 87% of palsies presented within a week of harness application. It was also more likely in older, larger patients in whom dysplasia was of higher severity. **They found** that patients whose palsy resolved within 3 days had a 70% chance of treatment success and those who had not recovered by 10 days had a 70% chance of treatment failure.

*Guille et al. reviewed DDH.* **They report** that indications for use of the harness include the presence of a reducible hip in an infant who has not made attempts to stand. The child's family must be able to follow instructions on its use. When radiographs of the hips and pelvis in flexion and abduction indicate that the femoral neck axis and head are directed toward the triradiate cartilage but the hip is not fully reduced, the Pavlik harness may be used. **They conclude** that if the hips are not reduced within 2-3 weeks, other methods of treatment must be employed.

Illustrations A, B, and C (Guille et al.) demonstrate the anterior, posterior, and lateral views of correct harness positioning. Illustration D is a chart detailing the protocol for treating DDH (Guille et al.).

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1, 2, 3, & 4: Treatment of DDH with a Pavlik harness may incur risks of femoral nerve palsy (excessive flexion) and osteonecrosis (excessive abduction). Other listed complications are not known to Pavlik harness treatment.
