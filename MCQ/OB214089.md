## Options

3. Chemotherapy alone

5. Radiation therapy

## Correct option

**4. Curettage and bone grafting**

## Answer explanation

Chondroblastoma is a benign tumor of the epiphysis that is typically treated with curettage and packing alone.

Chondroblastoma is a benign chondrogenic lesion that typically presents patients around the age of 12 as insidious pain around the knee. Imaging demonstrates a well-circumscribed epiphyseal lytic lesion with a thin sclerotic rim of bone. Histology classically demonstrates "chicken wire" or "cobblestone" pattern of chondroblasts with areas of chondroid matrix. These can be treated often with intralesional curettage and packing, and if the lesion is small enough, radiofrequency ablation (RFA). There is a very low rate (<1%) of pulmonary metastasis with these lesions.

*Rybak et al. retrospectively reviewed 14 patients with chondroblastomas that were treated with RFA.* *All patients had immediate improvement in their symptoms with 12 having a complete resolution of their symptoms.* The patient with the largest lesion did require later surgery and residual tumor cells were identified. **They conclude** that RFA is a viable option for small lesions, although they do not specify a maximum size.

Chen and DiFrancesco review chondroblastomas. They discuss clinical features to include diagnosis at an average age of 19-23 years and having a male predominance. Radiologic features noted include well-demarcation, eccentric location, and lytic nature. Pathology will show sheetlike proliferation of small and intermediate-sized polygonal cells. with a variable number of giant cells. Primary treatment is surgical with curettage with or without bone grafting an en bloc resection. They cite a recurrence rate of 14-18% at an interval of 6 months to 8 years.
