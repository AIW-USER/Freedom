## Answer explanation

Surgical treatment of giant cell tumor (GCT) of bone consists of extensive curettage and reconstruction with adjuvant treatments. Figure D demonstrates numerous giant cells consistent with this diagnosis.

Giant cell tumor (GCT) of bone is a benign aggressive tumor found in the metaphysis and epiphysis of long bones, most commonly about the knee. Radiographs typically reveal eccentric lytic lesions that extend to the subchondral border. Evaluation of the chest, most commonly with a chest radiograph is required because pulmonary metastases are present in 1-6% of patients. Histopathology demonstrates numerous giant cells (Figure D) with up to 50 nuclei per cell. Nonoperative management consists of radiation alone for lesions that are inoperable or multiply recurrent. Medical adjuvants such as bisphosphonates or denosumab may also be used. Lesions in locations amenable to surgery should undergo extensive curettage with adjuvants (phenol, hydrogen peroxide, cement, liquid nitrogen or argon beam) and reconstruction.

*Malawer et al. review the long-term outcomes of cryotherapy as a treatment for GCT.* *They investigated 102 patients with GCT who underwent curettage and cryotherapy using liquid nitrogen.* A direct pouring technique was used. **They report** an overall recurrence rate of 7.9%. **They conclude** that cryotherapy has the advantages of excellent functional outcome and low recurrence rate and recommend it for the treatment of most GCT.

*Skubitz reviewed the current treatment options for GCT.* **He reports** that the malignant stromal cells of GCT secrete RANKL, which upregulates the production of osteoclasts. This finding has led to the use of denosumab, which has shown clinical benefit. **He concludes** that in patients in which surgery is not possible or would be associated with significant morbidity that denosumab is a prudent treatment option.

Figure A is the AP radiograph demonstrating an eccentric lytic lesion within the epiphysis and metaphysis of the distal femur consistent with GCT. Figure B demonstrates a small round blue cell tumor (Ewing’s sarcoma). Figure C is that of fat cells consistent with a lipoma. Figure D shows numerous giant cells (GCT). Figure E depicts numerous highly malignant cells (osteosarcoma) with atypia and a high nuclear to cytoplasmic ratio. Figure F shows cells with cigar-shaped nuclei arranged in fascicles consistent with leiomyosarcoma.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1-2, 4-5: Ewing’s sarcoma, lipomas, osteosarcoma, and leiomyosarcoma are not appropriately treated with curettage and phenol.
