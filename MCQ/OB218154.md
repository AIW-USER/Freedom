## Question

A 42-year-old patient is being seen in your clinic with a chief complaint of right knee pain. His radiographs are available for review in Figure A. The patient is a competitive cyclist and medical school biochemistry professor, and he is adamant that he does not want knee surgery, as his racing season is set to begin within the next few months. The patient brings with him multiple studies that note that antagonism of the interleukin-1 receptor (IL-1r) can lead to reductions in knee pain. Which of the following orthobiologic substances has been shown to have the highest concentration of IL-1 receptor antagonist?

![[q218154-a-kl2.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Bone Marrow Aspirate Concentration (BMAC)
2. Hyaluronic Acid (HA)
3. Leukocyte-Poor Platelet Rich Plasma (LP-PRP)
4. Leukocyte-Rich Platelet Rich Plasma (LR-PRP)
5. No difference between the concentration of IL-1ra between orthobiologic substances

## Correct option

**1. Bone Marrow Aspirate Concentration (BMAC)**

## Answer explanation

Interleukin-1 (IL-1) is a potent proinflammatory cytokine. IL-1 receptor antagonist (IL-1ra) blocks the binding of IL-1 to its receptor and can provide a robust anti-inflammatory effect. Bone marrow aspirate concentrate (BMAC) has been shown to have higher concentrations of IL-1ra compared to a variety of orthobiologics (Answer 1).

The realm of orthobiologics is a rapidly growing area of research within orthopaedics. These substances would be an attractive addition to the armamentarium of orthopedic surgeons as they, if proven to be safe and effective, could potentially delay or obviate the need for season- or career-ending surgery in recreational, competitive, and professional athletes. Preclinical studies have demonstrated great potential for enhanced healing and injury recovery in the presence of these substances. Promisingly, short-term clinical studies have also demonstrated the efficacy of these substances in the reduction of pain and activity-limited symptoms for a variety of pathologies.

The underlying rationale for the investigation into the clinical use of orthobiologics stems from the limited healing capacity of tissues often implicated in athlete injury (i.e. cartilage, muscle, tendon, ligament, meniscus). A variety of orthobiologic substances have been studied and proposed including, but not limited to, platelet-rich plasma (PRP), bone marrow aspirate concentrate (BMC), and adipose-derived mesenchymal stromal cells. Furthermore, substances that amplify the native properties of tissues (hyaluronic acid for viscosupplementation) have also been introduced as potential treatment modalities. Multiple clinical trials investigating these substances are in various phases of recruitment or follow-up, and much of the current literature draws conclusions from lower-powered and/or methodologically-flawed studies. There do exist, however, consensus statements on the use of these agents in the management of symptoms in various groups of professional athletes.

*Cassano et al. investigated the concentrations of IL-1ra and cell distribution in both BMAC and PRP.* *Their study included 29 patients.* Overall, platelet counts were not significantly different between BMAC and PRP, but the concentration of IL-1ra was greater in BMAC (13,432 pg/mL) than in PRP (588 pg/mL). Overall, **the authors note** that the potent anti-inflammatory effect of IL-1ra could represent a strength of BMAC when compared to other orthobiologic substances.

*Ziegler et al. performed a prospective analysis in order to characterize the growth factors, cytokines, and chemokines in BMAC and PRP.* Overall, **the authors found** that BMAC had a significantly higher IL-1ra concentration than bone marrow aspirate, whole blood, and both leukocyte-rich and poor PRP. **The authors conclude**, similar to Cassano et al., that the high concentration of this potent anti-inflammatory may make BMAC an effective means by which to control pain and symptoms secondary to heightened inflammatory states (i.e. osteoarthritis, acute injury).

*Cole et al. authored the 2020 NBA orthobiologics consensus statement in order to provide comprehensive guidance in the utilization of these substances in the management of high-level athletes.* **The authors note** that autologous orthobiologic preparations (i.e. PRP, BMAC) are inherently safe, and confer no risk of transmitted disease or major immune reactions). With regards to knee osteoarthritis, **the authors note** that PRP, BMAC, and adipose-derived mesenchymal stromal cells can be safely used for symptomatic relief in NBA players with evidence of knee osteoarthritis, and that leukocyte-poor PRP, at the time of publication, had the most supportive evidence.

Figure A demonstrates a weight-bearing AP radiograph of the right knee demonstrating osteophyte formation with possible joint space narrowing consistent with Kellgren-Lawrence Grade 2 knee osteoarthritis.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 2: Hyaluronic acid is an injectable viscosupplementation formula that is thought to help restore synovial fluid to a more natural state. It does not exhibit anti-inflammatory effects.
Answers 3 and 4: BMAC has demonstrated higher concentrations of IL-1ra than both leukocyte-poor and rich PRP.
Answer 4: BMAC has been demonstrated to have higher concentrations of IL-1ra than HA, LP-PRP and LR-PRP.
