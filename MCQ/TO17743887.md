## Question

A cricket player after sustaining a knee injury developed swelling around the knee joint. He was not able to continue the game. His X-ray is given below. What is not a true statement regarding management? (Arthroscopy- knee arthroscopy- PCL)

![[TO17743887-1.png]]

## Options

1. Long leg cast in full extension for 4 weeks
2. MRI should be done to guide the management
3. Surgical fixation is required
4. Most common mode of injury is posteriorly directed force on flexed knee

## Correct option

**1. Long leg cast in full extension for 4 weeks**

## Answer explanation

This is a case of PCL avulsion from tibia. Most commonly it happens when a posterior directed force is applied to the proximal tibia when the knee is flexed (dashboard injury). The second most frequent mechanism is sports-related trauma, in which the mechanism is knee hyperextension. Tibial-sided PCL avulsion fractures are the most common form of isolated bony PCL disruption. However, concomitant injuries can occur quite frequently and MRI is essential to plan the management of concomitant injuries.

Displaced PCL avulsion fractures (>5mm displacement) as in this case, are best managed by surgical (arthroscopic or open) fixation.

Answer: 1
