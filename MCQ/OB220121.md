## Question

A 63-year-old rheumatologist with knee osteoarthritis has failed nonoperative measures and is now searching for a total knee arthroplasty surgeon. He has had discussions with two different experienced, high-volume arthroplasty surgeons. One surgeon uses only traditional instrumentation and another surgeon performs robotic-assisted total knee arthroplasty (RA-TKA). After the patient performs a thorough literature review, which of the following is found to be true?

## Options

1. RA-TKA provides an increased accuracy of limb alignment but involves a longer surgical time.
2. Traditional techniques result in the same accuracy of alignment with fewer complications compared to RA-TKA.
3. RA-TKA results in improved implant positioning and limb alignment, but no difference in long-term outcomes
4. RA-TKA has been shown to increase implant longevity and decrease revision rates.
5. No difference has been shown between RA-TKA and conventional TKA in regard to alignment, short-term outcomes, or complications.

## Correct option

**3. RA-TKA results in improved implant positioning and limb alignment, but no difference in long-term outcomes**

## Answer explanation

Compared to conventional instrumentation, robotic-assisted total knee arthroplasty has been shown to improve the accuracy of implant position and limb alignment. However, there has been no consistent difference regarding long-term outcomes, complications, revision rate, or surgical time (Answer 3).

Robotic-assisted total knee arthroplasty (RA-TKA) has grown in popularity over the last decade. With the increased use of RA-TKA, the literature has found consistent improvements in the accuracy and precision of the surgical procedure, particularly when making bone cuts, predicting implant sizes/positioning, and restoring limb alignment. Despite the promising radiological outcomes thus far, the data has not been able to find a demonstrable difference in long-term outcomes, major complications, or revision rates. Added operative time can be expected in surgeons during the learning phase of RA-TKA. However, the majority of studies support a short learning curve of under 15-20 cases before operative times normalize to conventional techniques.

*Wang et al. published a 2022 study to evaluate the trends in computer-navigated TKA (CN-TKA), RA-TKA, and traditionally-instrumented TKA.* *The authors also evaluated the differences in complication rates and post-operative pain, measured by opioid consumption by procedure type.* *A five-year study period (2015-2020) included 847,496 patients and found an increase in CN-TKA usage from 5.64% to 6.41% and an increase in RA-TKA usage from 0.84% to 5.89%.* Compared to traditional TKA, an association was found for technology-assisted TKA to result in decreased opioid consumption, a reduction in hospitalization duration, and a reduction in certain postoperative complications. **The authors concluded** that the adoption of CN-TKA and RA-TKA is increasing and has the potential to improve patient outcomes.

*Li et al. published a prospective cohort study to evaluate the accuracy of RA-TKA for bone resection, limb alignment, and implant position and size prediction in 36 patients.* **The results demonstrated** that 91.7% of bone resections were within 1 mm of the preoperative plan and 75% of the final limb alignments were within 1° of the planned alignment. Femoral and tibial component sizes were accurately predicted in 100% and 97.22% of cases, respectively. **The authors concluded** that RA-TKA can precisely execute preoperative plans, leading to accurate bone resections, limb alignment, and implant placement.

*Siddiqi et al. published a comprehensive review, discussing the evolution, advances, and outcomes of RA-TKA.* **The authors note** several studies reporting improved accuracy and precision of bone cuts and alignment with RA-TKA. Despite data demonstrating improved short-term patient-reported outcomes with RA-TKA, this review notes the lack of conclusive difference in patients' long-term outcome scores. The authors consider the cost-effectiveness of RA-TKA, given the large upfront costs. Finally, **the authors emphasize** the importance of longer-term studies to truly assess implant survivorship and complications. **The authors conclude** that while RA-TKA offers promising improvements in surgical precision and early patient satisfaction, careful consideration is necessary when selecting and implementing the appropriate robotic system for clinical practice.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer choice 1: Although RA-TKA does result in improved accuracy in alignment, experienced robotic surgeons do not have increased surgical times compared to conventional techniques. Surgeons implementing robotics in their practice can expect increased surgical times early on. However, the majority of the literature supports a learning curve of less than 15-20 cases before a surgeon approaches surgical times comparable to traditional techniques.
Answer choice 2: Recent literature has not found a demonstrable difference in the complication profile between RA-TKA and conventional TKA. Furthermore, the majority of literature supports the notion of RA-TKA resulting in improved accuracy of alignment compared to conventional TKA.
Answer choice 4: Although RA-TKA has been shown to result in improved alignment restoration, the literature is inconclusive on long-term implant survivorship and revision rates. Currently, the data does not suggest an improved longevity or decreased revision rate with RAS.
Answer choice 5: RA-TKA has been shown to be superior to conventional techniques regarding limb alignment.
