## Question

A 32-year-old male presents with an atrophic nonunion of his radius following open reduction internal fixation (ORIF) of a both bone forearm fracture treated 12 months ago. On radiographs an obvious fracture gap in the radius is appreciated. Which of the following is necessary for achievement of successful union in addition to revision ORIF with cancellous autograft?

## Options

1. Fracture gap less than 3 cm
2. Supportive soft tissue envelope
3. Healed ulna fracture
4. BMP-7
5. 4.5 mm plates

## Correct option

**2. Supportive soft tissue envelope**

## Answer explanation

In an atrophic forearm nonunion with a good soft-tissue envelope, adequate plating with cancellous bone graft can be used to treat defects of up to 6 cm.

Atrophic nonunion of the forearm is rarely encountered with current plating techniques. However, when it does occur, it is most commonly associated with open injuries or inadequate fixation. The mainstay of treatment consists of revision ORIF with some degree of bone grafting procedure. When the soft-tissue envelope is compliant, has limited scar, and consists largely of healthy muscle with a good vascular supply, autogenous cancellous bone-grafting and stable internal plate fixation results in a high rate of union and improved upper limb function in patients with diaphyseal nonunion of the radius. Conversely, if a healthy soft tissue envelope is not present, vascularized bone grafting with soft tissue transfer may be required.

*Ring et al. reviewed forearm nonunions treated with plate fixation and cancellous autografts.* **They report** that suboptimal fixation and/or open fractures were most commonly identified in patients developing nonunion. **They conclude** that revision ORIF with cancellous autograft leads to a high union rate as long as the soft tissue envelope is intact and healthy.

*Kloen et al. review the current concepts in treatment of forearm nonunions.* **They report** that vascularization of corticocancellous autograft typically occurs within a few weeks if the soft tissue envelope is compliant and well-vascularized. **They conclude** that revision plating with the addition of cancellous autograft is adequate for patients with defects <6 cm and adequate soft tissues.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Fractures gaps up to 6 cm can be treated with this technique.
Answer 3: A healed ulna fracture is not a prerequisite to treatment with revision ORIF and cancellous autograft.
Answer 4: While BMP-7 may be an appropriate adjunct for some nonunions, its not required for successful treatment.
Answer 5: 3.5 mm plates are adequate and much less bulky than 4.5 mm plates.
