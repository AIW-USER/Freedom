## Answer explanation

The EXT1/2 gene mutations are associated with multiple hereditary exostoses (MHE) as seen in Figure E.

MHE is a predominately autosomal dominant disorder leading to the production of multiple osteochondromas throughout the body. Patients with MHE have a genetic mutation in the EXT1, EXT2, and EXT3 genes that encode for the production of heparin sulfate at the physis. Heparan sulfate plays an important role in physeal signaling, and its absence leads to chondrocyte disorganization and exostosis development. Most common sites include the proximal tibia, distal femur, proximal humerus, scapula, pelvis, and wrist. Malignant transformation can occur in an exostosis in any location; however, pelvic exostoses have been shown to have a higher rate of malignant transformation.

*Phan et al. review the 2016 advance in MHE following the international MHE conference.* **They state** that osteochondromas are benign tumors that have about a 2-5% chance of malignant transformation. Surgical options consist of the removal of symptomatic tumors or major skeletal defects. Many patients undergo multiple surgeries throughout life, so efforts are being directed at researching ways to prevent osteochondroma formation.

*Pedrini et al. evaluated 529 patients with MHE from 2 different European referral centers and looked at the phenotype variation among the patient.* The presence or absence of deformities and functional limitations in the patients led to a breakdown of the severity of the phenotype: mild (the absence of both aspects), intermediate, or severe (the concurrent presence of both aspects). In their cohort of patients, **they noted** that female sex, fewer than 5 areas of exostosis, and the EXT2 mutation were associated with a mild phenotype; in contrast, a severe phenotype was associated with male sex, EXT1 mutations, and more than twenty affected skeletal sites.

Figure A shows a radiograph demonstrating anterolateral bowing of the tibia seen in association with neurofibromatosis 1. Figure B shows an axial CT scan of an eccentrically based area of reactive bone with a radiolucent nidus most consistent with an osteoid osteoma. Figure C shows a radiograph of a skeletally immature patient with a metaphyseal eccentric "bubbly" lytic lesion surrounded by a sclerotic rim most consistent with a non-ossifying fibroma (NOF). Figure D shows a radiograph of the left hand with a lytic lesion at the base of the proximal phalanx of the long finger with cortical expansion and thinning most consistent with an enchondroma. Figure E shows multiple osteochondromas consistent with multiple hereditary exostoses (MHE).

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Anterolateral bowing can be seen in patients with Neurofibromatosis type 1, which is associated with the NF1 gene on chromosome 17q21.
Answer 2: Osteoid osteomas are not associated with an EXT1/2 gene mutation.
Answer 3: Non-ossifying fibromas are the most common benign bone tumor of childhood and are not associated with the EXT1/2 gene mutation.
Answer 4: Enchondromas are benign chondrogenic tumors that are not associated with EXT1/2 gene mutations.
