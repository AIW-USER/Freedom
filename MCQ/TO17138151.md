Chapter: Pediatric Orthopedics MCQs — Slipped Capital Femoral Epiphysis (SCFE)

## Question

When treating slipped capital femoral epiphysis (SCFE), which of the following might be the sole indication for subcapital osteotomy?

## Options

1. Metaphyseal blanch sign
2. Southwick angle >60 degree
3. Avascular necrosis (AVN)
4. Femoral retroversion

## Correct option

**2. Southwick angle >60 degree**

## Answer explanation

The most frequently quoted and used Southwick angle is measured on a frog lateral view of both hips and defined as the difference in the head-shaft angles following SCFE.

(An anteroposterior (AP) angle has also been reported but is used less commonly.) The

head-shaft angle is determined by the intersection of a line perpendicular to the axis

between the anterior and posterior tips of the epiphysis at the physis and a line along the anatomical axis of the femur. The value for the normal side is then subtracted from that of the slipped side and the angle calculated determines the severity. Mild is <30 degree, moderate is 30–60 degree and severe is >60 degree. 12 degree is the normal control value and can be used in the case of bilateral involvement. There is some controversy in the literature as to whether 50 degree or 60 degree is the ‘cut-off’ between moderate and severe degree, this probably relates to whether or not the ‘normal’ side has in fact been subtracted from the value for the abnormal side.

The term ‘slip angle’ is commonly used but is rarely defined: it usually refers to the Southwick angle (with or without subtraction of the normal side). Severe slips treated with pinning in situ show poorer results than mild and moderate slips. The metaphyseal blanch sign is a radiographic sign of SCFE seen on the AP pelvic radiograph. It can be seen even with minor slips. Recent publications suggest that AVN complicates 20% of acute, severe unstable slips. AVN is also a complication of subcapital osteotomy. It is not an indication for such an osteotomy. Femoral retroversion puts stress across the physis and is a mechanical factor associated with development of a SUFE. Endocrinopathy is a potential cause of SCFE and may guide the surgeon towards prophylactic pinning of the unaffected hip.

Answer: 2
