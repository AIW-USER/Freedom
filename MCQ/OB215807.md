## Question

A 33-year-old alcoholic male presents with right foot pain and progressively worsening flat foot deformity. He states this has been present for several years, but cannot recall a specific injury. He does, however, note waking up one morning with an extensively swollen foot and plantar ecchymosis. He eventually "walked this off," but his foot never felt the same. He has not tried physical therapy, medications, orthoses, or activity modifications. He appears to have tenderness on the dorsal and plantar aspects of his medial midfoot. He has a supple hindfoot and 5/5 strength in all his major muscles groups. His current imaging is in Figures A and B. What is the most appropriate treatment at this time?

## Options

1. Physical therapy
2. AFO orthosis
3. Midfoot arthrodesis
4. Medializing calcaneal osteotomy with tendon transfers and lateral column lengthening
5. Triple arthrodesis

## Correct option

**3. Midfoot arthrodesis**

## Answer explanation

The patient is likely presenting with a chronic Lisfranc injury and progressive pes planovalgus. This can be effectively treated with midfoot arthrodesis.

Lisfranc injuries result in the disruption of the Lisfranc ligament, which stabilizes the 2nd metatarsal base to the medially cuneiform. Acute treatment consists of open reduction and internal fixation or primary arthrodesis of the 1st, 2nd, and 3rd TMT joints. Due to the subtle radiographic abnormalities present, these injuries can often be missed even with an immediate presentation in an emergency setting. Chronic injuries lead to progressive flat foot deformity and midfoot arthritis. In these instances, the salvage treatment is midfoot arthrodesis.

*Alcelik et al. performed a systematic review and meta-analysis of the treatment of acute Lisfranc injuries with primary arthrodesis or open reduction and internal fixation.* **They reported** no significant differences in functional outcomes between the two treatment groups, but a higher rate of hardware removal and secondary arthrodesis in the ORIF group. **The authors concluded** patients should be counseled regarding the increased reoperation rates with ORIF.

*Fan et al. performed a retrospective study of 176 patients with Lisfranc injuries and associated multiple metatarsal fractures treated surgically.* **They reported** higher AOFAS scores in patients treated with primary arthrodesis and a nearly 25% incidence of post-traumatic arthritis in patients treated with ORIF. **The authors concluded** treatment of Lisfranc injuries with multiple metatarsal fractures treated with primary arthrodesis results in better outcomes than ORIF.

Figures A and B are the AP and lateral radiographs of the left foot with midfoot degenerative changes and planus alignment consistent with a chronic Lisfranc injury.

Incorrect answers
Answers 1and 2: Physical therapy and AFO orthosis are first-line treatment of pes planovalgus that is due to posterior tibial tendon insufficiency. However, this patient has a chronic Lisfranc injury which is best treated with a midfoot arthrodesis.
Answer 4: A medializing calcaneal osteotomy with tendon transfers and lateral column lengthening is appropriate for flexible flatfoot deformity that is refractory to bracing and therapy.
Answer 5: Triple arthrodesis is reserved for flatfoot deformity associated with a rigid hindfoot and extensive arthritic changes.
