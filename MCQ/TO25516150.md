Chapter: Trauma MCQs — Fracture of Femur and Tibia Shaft

## Question

A 68-year-old patient sustains an incomplete atypical femoral fracture with intractable thigh pain and a prominent 'dreaded black line' on the lateral cortex. She undergoes successful prophylactic intramedullary nailing. The patient has been receiving biannual subcutaneous denosumab injections for the past 7 years. Following surgical stabilization, what is the most appropriate, evidence-based pharmacological strategy to optimize fracture healing while preventing catastrophic systemic skeletal complications?

## Options

1. Institute an immediate and permanent 'drug holiday' from all metabolic bone therapies to allow the paralyzed osteoclast population to naturally recover and initiate fracture callus remodeling.
2. Continue denosumab therapy at a reduced dose to prevent rebound osteoclastogenesis, while simultaneously initiating teriparatide to drive local osteoblastic bone formation at the fracture site.
3. Discontinue denosumab and immediately initiate an anabolic agent (e.g., teriparatide) to drive fracture union, with a mandatory plan to administer a short-acting bisphosphonate upon completion of the anabolic course.
4. Transition immediately from denosumab to high-dose intravenous zoledronic acid to lock in the cortical bone mass and provide a stable, hyper-mineralized mechanical environment for the intramedullary nail.

## Correct option

**3. Discontinue denosumab and immediately initiate an anabolic agent (e.g., teriparatide) to drive fracture union, with a mandatory plan to administer a short-acting bisphosphonate upon completion of the anabolic course.**

## Answer explanation

This strategy utilizes the 'anabolic
window' to heal the mechanically stabilized fracture by restarting osteoblast
activity with teriparatide. However, because denosumab does not embed in the
bone matrix, its abrupt withdrawal leads to a massive rebound in osteoclast
activity and rapid bone loss. Following the anabolic course with a
bisphosphonate is mandatory to 'lock in' the new bone mass and prevent the
devastating rebound multiple vertebral compression fractures associated with
denosumab withdrawal. A simple drug holiday (Option A) is therefore
contraindicated. Continuing denosumab (Option B) or switching immediately to a
bisphosphonate (Option D) will maintain the severe suppression of bone
turnover, paralyzing the initial osteoclastic resorption necessary for fracture
healing and blunting any anabolic effects.

Answer: 3
