## Question

Which of the following is an absolute contraindication to the procedure depicted in Figures A and B?

![[q5878-a-tcc-ap.jpg]]
*Figure A*

![[q5878-b-tcc-lat.jpg]]
*Figure B*

## Options

1. Osteonecrosis of the talus
2. Severe peripheral vascular disease
3. Normal subtalar joint
4. Substantial bone loss secondary to failed total ankle arthroplasty
5. Severe peripheral neuropathy

## Correct option

**2. Severe peripheral vascular disease**

## Answer explanation

Severe peripheral vascular disease is an absolute contraindication for tibiotalocalcaneal arthrodesis (TTCA) with a retrograde intramedullary nail.

TTCA is a salvage procedure for severe hindfoot conditions, including end-stage ankle and subtalar arthritis, charcot arthropathy, significant hindfoot bone loss, osteonecrosis of the talus and severe acute trauma. Fixation methods for TTCA include Steinmann pins, plates and screws, external fixators and retrograde intramedullary nails. Absolute contraindications to retrograde intramedullary nailing for TTCA include the presence of active infection, profound vascular disease and severe malalignment of the tibia.

*Rammelt et al performed a retrospective case series evaluating the outcomes of patients treated with the hindfoot arthrodesis nail for severe ankle and foot deformities.* The fusion rate was 84% and rate of superficial wound infection was 2.4% with no deep infections. Patients reported low pain levels and all patients who were unable to work prior to surgery were able to fully return to work.

*Thomas et al authored a review article on the use of intramedullary nails for TTCA.* Optimal positioning of the hindfoot is 0° of dorsiflexion, 5° heel valgus and 5-10° of foot external rotation relative to the tibia. Specific contraindications to TTCA include the presence of sepsis or active infection, severe vascular disease, and notable malalignment of the tibia as a result of previous trauma.

Figures A and B demonstrate a TTCA with a retrograde intramedullary nail.

Incorrect Responses:
Answer 1: Osteonecrosis of the talus is an indication for TTCA with a retrograde nail.
Answer 3: While the presence of an intact subtalar joint may be a relative contraindication to TTCA, in patients who have difficulty complying with weight bearing restrictions and diabetic patients with Charcot neuroarthropathy, the subtalar joint may be sacrificed to provide improved stability of the construct.
Answer 4: Substantial bone loss secondary to failed total ankle arthroplasty or previous ankle arthrodesis is an indication for TTCA with a retrograde nail.
Answer 5: Severe peripheral neuropathy with Charcot arthropathy is an indication for TTCA with a retrograde nail.
