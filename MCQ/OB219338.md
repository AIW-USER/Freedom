## Question

A 14-year-old male presents to your office to discuss an irregularity that was found on MRI imaging. An MRI of the involved upper extremity revealed a “coaxial cable” appearance on axial view showing the distribution of fat interspersed between the thickened axons of an involved nerve. The patient is diagnosed with a fibrolipomatous hamartoma. Which nerve is most commonly affected in this condition?

## Options

1. Axillary
2. Median
3. Radial
4. Suprascapular
5. Ulnar

## Correct option

**2. Median**

## Answer explanation

A fibrolipomatous hamartoma is a condition that most commonly affects the median nerve.

A fibrolipomatous hamartoma is a benign proliferative mass colloquially referred to as an intraneural lipoma. While it has been most commonly reported to affect the median nerve, it has been described in other major nerves as well. It is thought to be a congenital condition although is often identified after a traumatic event. MRI imaging has several unique features in this condition including a “coaxial cable” appearance on axial view representing even distribution of fat interspersed between the thickened axons, and a spaghetti-like appearance on the coronal or sagittal MRIs. Additionally, the fat will have high signal intensity on T1-weighted images and low signal intensity on T2-weighted sequences. Asymptomatic patients are most commonly managed with observation. For symptomatic patients, treatment generally involves decompression of the median nerve at the carpal tunnel with or without epineurolysis.

*Nilsson et al. reviewed fibrolipomatous hamartoma of the median nerve.* **They reported** that they are usually located distally in the forearm and may have characteristic features on MR imaging. **They concluded** that an MRI should be performed in cases with nerve dysfunction without an obvious cause after a thorough clinical examination.

*Marom et al. reviewed the MR imaging characteristics, presenting symptoms, age and nerve distribution of fibrolipomatous hamartoma.* **They reported** that imaging characteristics were serpiginous low-intensity structures representing thickened nerve fascicles, surrounded by evenly distributed fat, high signal intensity on T1-weighted sequences, and low signal intensity on T2-weighted sequences. **They concluded** that the MR imaging characteristics of fibrolipomatous hamartoma are pathognomonic, obviating the need for a biopsy for diagnosis.

*Gilet et al. reviewed fibrolipomatous hamartoma of the nerve.* **They reported** that these are rare soft tissue tumors that most commonly occur in the forearm and the wrist, particularly within the median nerve. **They concluded** that careful preoperative planning is necessary for the optimal treatment by distinguishing whether it is a resectable or non-resectable tumor based on the clinical and radiological findings because they have characteristic findings in each other.
