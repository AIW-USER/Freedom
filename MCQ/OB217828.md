## Question

A 45-year-old male presents to your office for a second opinion after being told he has avascular necrosis of the femoral head with collapse. You indicate him for total hip arthroplasty. Given his young age, he is very concerned about undergoing this procedure and his range of motion postoperatively. Which of the following is most closely related to increased range of motion postoperatively?

## Options

1. Anterior based approach
2. Larger femoral head
3. Extended offset femoral neck
4. Acetabular anteversion >18 degrees
5. Acetabular abduction angle <35 degrees

## Correct option

**2. Larger femoral head**

## Answer explanation

Increasing the diameter of the femoral head improves the primary arc of motion prior to impingement.

Stability following total hip arthroplasty is dependent on several factors including component design, component positioning, soft-tissue tensioning and soft tissue function. As it relates to component design, increasing the diameter of the femoral head effectively increases the head to neck diameter ratio, which increases component impingement free range of motion. Further, this also increases the jump distance as the femoral head is deeper in the acetabulum. As the acetabular shell increases in diameter you have the option to increase femoral head size. The size at which the components change is system dependent. Surgeons should be aware of the nuances of the system they use.

*Lavernia et al review the use of large heads in total hip arthroplasty.* **They report** that as a larger head is used there is subsequent increased forces transmitted to the trunion regardless of head material. **They conclude** that the trend of larger head size may continue to lead to tribocorrosion and ion release.

*Cooper et al review the use of larger femoral heads and their benefits.* **They report** that as femoral head size increases, stability is augmented, secondary to an increase in impingement-free range of movement. **They conclude** that larger femoral heads decrease the rate of dislocations following THA.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Range of motion is not significantly influenced by approach.
Answer 3: While switching to an extended offset neck may decrease the likelihood of soft tissue impingement and subsequent dislocation, it is not as closely linked to range of motion as femoral head size.
Answer 4: Increasing anteversion angles will decrease overall range of motion as it will cause posterior impingement and subsequent anterior dislocation.
Answer 5: Similarly to increased anteversion, low abduction angles runs the risk of lateral impingement with abduction and subsequent dislocation.
