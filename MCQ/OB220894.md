## Question

A 11-year-old male basketball player presents to your clinic with a three-month history of left heel pain. The pain is worse with activity. ## Answer explanation

This patient has Sever’s disease, which may demonstrate fragmentation of the calcaneal apophysis on a lateral radiograph (Figure C).

Sever’s disease is a very common condition in immature athletes caused by overuse. High levels of activity may precipitate microtrauma and subsequent traction apophysitis of the calcaneus. On exam, patients have pain over the posterior heel, a tight Achilles tendon, and tenderness with medial/lateral compression of the calcaneus. Radiographs, when obtained, may demonstrate fragmentation of the calcaneal apophysis. Treatment is non-operative and is primarily comprised of activity modification and stretching of the heel cord.

*Frank et al. published a review on lower extremity injuries in pediatric and adolescent athletes.* *They provide a broad overview of Sever’s disease and its relationship to overuse leading to calcaneal metaphysical microtrauma as well as tight posterior leg compartment musculature.* **They highlight** the non-operative treatment of this condition, which predominantly consists of stretching and strengthening exercises. **They concluded** that the pediatric and adolescent sports-related injury are common and require multidisciplinary approaches to prevent and to treat injuries when they occur.

*Rachel et al. performed a review of pediatric and adolescent patients to determine the presence of radiographic abnormalities in patients with Sever’s disease.* **They found** that only five of their 98 patients with Sever’s disease had abnormal radiographic findings, which included bone cysts primarily and two calcaneal stress fractures. **The authors concluded** that the work-up of Sever’s disease in the patient with heel pain should include lateral radiographs in order to lower the risk of missing a lesion not consistent with a diagnosis of Sever’s disease.
