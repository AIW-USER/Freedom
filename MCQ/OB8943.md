## Question

A 11-year-old female soccer player had a non-contact injury in which she felt a pop about her right hip. She has been able to bear weight since then, but has pain with activities. On physical exam she has point tenderness anteriorly just proximal to her hip. She is coming in today for an initial evaluation. Figure A is an AP pelvis radiograph obtained in the office today. The avulsed muscle is innervated by which nerve?

![[q8943-a-asis-avulsion.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Sciatic
2. Femoral
3. Obturator
4. Pudendal
5. Inferior Gluteal

## Correct option

**2. Femoral**

## Answer explanation

The radiogaph in Figure A shows an avulsion fracture of the right anterior superior iliac spine (ASIS) apophysis due to pull from the sartorius muscle which is innervated by the femoral nerve.

There are several possible pelvic avulsion fractures that can occur in adolescents, as seen in Illustration A below. These are typically visible on pelvic radiographs and can be treated with rest, protected weight bearing, and gradual return to activities. The ASIS is the insertion site of the sartorius (femoral nerve) and tensor fascia lata (superior gluteal nerve) muscles, and an over-pull of these muscles during an activity such as soccer can lead to an avulsion injury. Treatment of an ASIS avulsion includes ice, anti-inflammatory medications, and activity modification until symptoms subside. Physical therapy can help improve strength and flexibility as the condition improves.

*Holden et al. provide a review on pediatric pelvic fractures secondary to high energy trauma (not sports related avulsion fractures).* **They note** that CT scans are needed to fully assess the extent of a fracture, but that most pelvic fractures in children can be treated non-operatively.

*Rossi et al. provide a review of pelvic avulsion fractures in adolescent athletes.* **They note** that the most common sports leading to these injuries are soccer and gymnastics. An ischial tuberosity avulsion was the most common location, and iliac crest avulsions were least common.

Figure A is an AP pelvis radiograph showing an avulsion fracture of the right ASIS. Illustration A is a diagram showing common sites of pelvic avulsion fractures and their associated musculature.

Incorrect answers:
Answers 1, 3-5: These nerves do not innervate the sartorius or tensor fascia lata, which are the avulsed muscles in an ASIS avulsion fracture.
