## Options

1. Insulin-like growth factor 1
2. Glutamate
3. Adenosine triphosphate
4. Prostaglandin I2
5. Prostaglandin E2

## Correct option

**5. Prostaglandin E2**

## Answer explanation

The patient has an osteoid osteoma, which produces painful prostaglandin E2 (PGE2). Non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) are cycloxygenase inhibitors that decrease the synthesis of prostaglandin and are the first line of pain control for these patients.

Prostaglandins are inflammatory mediators from the eicosanoid family synthesized from arachidonic acid. The synthetic pathway is catalyzed by the enzymes cyclooxygenase-1 and cyclooxygenase-2 (COX1, COX2). The COX1 and COX2 enzymes are the therapeutic target of the NSAIDs. NSAIDs themselves act to inhibit the COX enzymes through competitive binding with the enzyme active site (e.g., ibuprofen, naprosyn) or through irreversible acetylation (aspirin). Other important pain mediators like glutamate, adenosine triphosphate, insulin-like growth factor, and prostaglandin I2 are released from damaged tissue, but not directly synthesized by osteoid osteoma.

Makley and Dunn were the first to report the synthesis of prostaglandins by osteoid osteoma tissue. They evaluated three patients by incubating the osteoma an adjacent normal bone in Hank’s medium, and then measuring prostaglandin presence by radioimmunoassay. They found significant increases in prostaglandin synthesis, primarily PGE2, which they argued was probably the cause of the intense focal pain associated with the lesion.

*Amaya et. al. reviewed the common pain mediators.* PGE2 is synthesized by COX-1 and COX-2. After injury and in osteoid osteoma, COX-2 expression increases, which upregulates PGE2 synthesis.

Figure A demonstrates a mild right-sided scoliosis. Figure B demonstrates an axial CT cut through the lumbar spine with evidence of a well-circumscribed lesion with a sclerotic central nidus which is characteristic of an osteoid osteoma.

Illustration A demonstrates the prostaglandin synthesis pathway and the location of the COX enzymes within it.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: Insulin-like growth factor 1 (IGFR1) is synthesized from dermal fibroblasts, promotes Schwann cell proliferation and myelination of the peripheral nerves, and is also a mediator of pain after soft tissue injury. It is not a pain mediator in osteoid osteoma.
Answer 2: Glutamate is a mediator of pain during the acute inflammatory response, and acts via NMDA, AMPA, and kainate ionotropic receptors. It is released from damaged tissue and from primary afferent neurons in response to painful stimuli. It is not a pain mediator in osteoid osteoma.
Answer 3: Adenosine triphosphate (ATP) is released from damaged tissues, inducing pain via purinergic receptors in primary afferent neurons. It is not a pain mediator in osteoid osteoma.
Answer 4: Prostaglandin I2 is synthesized by COX2 within endothelial cells, and functions to inhibit platelet aggregation and promotes vasodilation. It is not a pain mediator synthesized by osteoid osteoma.
