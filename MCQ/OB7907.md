## Question

A 35-year-old construction worker continues to have weakness with lifting overhead 2 years after he was treated with physical therapy for a "chest muscle" tear. An obvious deformity noted in his axilla worsens with resisted extension and adduction. A clinical photograph and MRI scan are shown in Figures 119a and 119b. What is the most appropriate treatment?

## Options

1. Allograft reconstruction with semitendinosis weave to the humerus
2. Latissimus dorsi tendon transfer
3. Electrical stimulation
4. Shoulder arthrodesis
5. Arthroscopic pectoralis major tendon repair

## Correct option

**1. Allograft reconstruction with semitendinosis weave to the humerus**

## Answer explanation

This scenario describes a chronic, symptomatic pectoralis major tendon rupture in a young laborer. Direct repair is difficult at this time; therefore, allograft reconstruction is a good alternative to recover strength. Tendon transfers, electrical stimulation, shoulder arthrodesis, and arthroscopy are not indicated in this patient. They will not offer proper reconstruction of the lost muscle tendon unit and/or cosmetic repair.
