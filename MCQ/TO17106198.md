Chapter: Arthroplasty MCQs — Surgical Approaches - Knee Arthroplasty

## Question

While doing TKR in a patient with previous multiple incisions over the knee, the surgeon finds two longitudinal incisions 2.5 cm apart over anterior patella and one transverse incision. All scars are healed and dry. Which incision should be used to decrease the wound related complications? (FNB Pattern Paper 2020)

## Options

1. Medial
2. Lateral
3. Transverse as there will be skin slough with either of longitudinal ones
4. A new midline incision

## Correct option

**2. Lateral**

## Answer explanation

Wound problems can often be prevented with careful planning. When transverse incisions are used for knee surgery many years prior to any anticipated knee arthroplasty, no major problems are typically encountered with a conventional, anterior longitudinal incision. When confronted with multiple previous incisions, surgeons would best use the most recently healed or the most lateral.

Since most of the cutaneous vasculature arises from the medial genicular arteries, the skin flap at greatest risk of necrosis is the lateral one (medial skin incision).

In the presence of a single previous incision, the old scar, providing it is suitable, can be used. A horizontal previous incision must be crossed perpendicularly. In the presence of multiple previous incisions, use the most lateral one.

If it is not possible to incorporate the previous scar, there must be an at least 5-cm bridge of skin between the two incisions

Answer: 2
