## Question

A 47-year-old female presents with persistent left great toe pain. Two years prior, she underwent hallux valgus corrective surgery with a Chevron osteotomy. Figure A exhibits her most recent radiograph. What is the most likely diagnosis and appropriate treatment?

![[q3818-a-figure-a.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Post-traumatic arthritis of the metatarsal head and MTP arthrodesis
2. Osteonecrosis of the metatarsal head and intraarticular osteotomy
3. Osteonecrosis of the metatarsal head and MTP arthrodesis
4. Hallux varus and lateral capsular imbrication
5. Hallux valgus recurrence and conservative management

## Correct option

**3. Osteonecrosis of the metatarsal head and MTP arthrodesis**

## Answer explanation

Although rare, osteonecrosis of the metatarsal head can occur and is classically linked to distal Chevron osteotomy. Appropriate management for subsequent collapse is metatarsophalangeal (MTP) joint arthrodesis.

Distal Chevron osteotomy with lateral capsular release for hallux valgus correction is classically associated with potential risk of osteonecrosis of the MT head. The laterally based blood supply to the MTP head warrants careful lateral soft tissue handling along with required protection during bony osteotomy cuts. Primary fusion as a salvage procedure is recommended for symptomatic collapse of the MTP head.

*Shariff et al. prospectively followed 39 patients who underwent distal Chevron osteotomy and lateral capsular release for hallux valgus deformity.* *At a mean follow-up of 14 months, bone scintigraphy noted a 7.7% osteonecrosis rate, however, none were clinically significant and did not require further treatment.* **The authors concluded** that despite presence of osteonecrosis, lack of clinical significance did not warrant any need for subsequent procedures.

*Easley et al. summarized the existing literature on osteonecrosis of the MTP head and* **noted** that despite classic teaching, overall prevalence is relatively low. Overall, studies have shown blood flow reconstitution and the rate of clinically significant MTP head death remains even lower. The rate of severe, advanced collapse is very rare, and for this diagnosis, arthrodesis is recommended.

Grimes and Coughlin report their outcomes on 33 MTP arthrodeses for failed hallux valgus procedures. With a range of follow-up from 1-22 years, the authors noted reliable, consistent improvements in clinical outcomes, patient satisfaction and pain with minimal complications.

Figure A is an AP radiograph exhibiting collapse of the medial metatarsal head due to osteonecrosis.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: MTP arthrodesis is the correct treatment, but the diagnosis is incorrect. While there is joint space narrowing, minimal osteophyte formation and with evidence of collapse makes osteonecrosis correct.
Answer 2: The diagnosis is correct, however, an intra-articular osteotomy will not be appropriate for this clinical scenario.
Answers 4,5. Hallux varus and valgus recurrence are potential complications following hallux valgus correction surgery, however, this patient's overall alignment is maintained; capsular imbrication and conservative management in combination with the diagnoses listed are incorrect.
