## Answer explanation

Figure A demonstrates a Morton extension orthotic, which is used for conservative treatment of hallux rigidus. Figure F demonstrates classic radiographic features of hallux rigidus including first metatarsophalangeal joint narrowing, osteophytes, and subchondral sclerosis.

Hallux rigidus is characterized by osteoarthritis of the first metatarsophalangeal joint (MTPJ). Patients often present with pain during toe-off while walking. Conservative treatment is often with anti-inflammatories, activity modification, and a Morton extension orthosis. This orthosis, shown in Figure A, prevents movement of the 1st MTPJ and decreases forces through the MTPJ.

*Smith et al. reviewed 24 feet with an average follow-up of 14.4 years who underwent nonoperative treatment for hallux rigidus.* At final follow up 92% of patients said their pain had not substantially changed and 73% would still choose nonoperative treatment. Radiographically, 67% had a loss of cartilage space, and 67% had development of lateral osteophytes not initially present.

*Yee and Lau reviewed hallux rigidus and its various treatment options.* For nonoperative treatment they discuss the use of orthoses, shoebox modifications, and anti-inflammatory modalities. The goal of such orthoses is to reduce motion and mechanical stresses on the joint, thus reducing pain. They continue on to discuss operative treatment including cheilectomy, phalangeal and metatarsal osteotomies, arthroplasty, and arthrodesis.

*Ho et al. provide a review of the epidemiology, pathophysiology, and treatment recommendations for hallux rigidus.* **They state** the progression of great toe arthritis is associated with pain and loss of motion. Non-surgical intervention begins with shoe modifications and orthotics designed to limit MTP motion, such as a Morton's orthosis. Those with refractory symptoms are offered surgical interventions based on their individual grade of disease.

Figure A shows a Morton extension orthosis. Figures B-F all demonstrate AP radiographs of the foot with different pathologies. Figure B is hallux varus, Figure C is a ligamentous lisfranc injury, Figure D is hallux valgus, Figure E is a pseudojones fracture, and Figure F is hallus rigidus.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: Hallux varus is treated conservatively with shoe modifications and/or splinting.
Answer 2: Ligamentous Lisfranc injuries typically are treated with operative intervention.
Answer 3: Conservative treatment of hallux valgus is with shoe modifications, pads, and spacers.
Answer 4: Pseudo Jones fractures can often be treated with protected weight-bearing in a stiff-soled shoe, boot, or cast.
