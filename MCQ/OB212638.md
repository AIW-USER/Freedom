## Question

a 12-year-old boy presents with worsening right foot pain for 7 days. He is exquisitely tender over the 1st metatarsal. MRI of the right foot can be seen in Figure A. After surgical decompression and antibiotics, which of the following is the best indicator of response to treatment?

![[q212638-a-mri-foot-osteomyelitis.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. An elevation in procalcitonin
2. A decrease in procalcitonin
3. Negative blood cultures
4. A decrease in erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR)
5. A decrease in c-reactive protein (CRP)

## Correct option

**5. A decrease in c-reactive protein (CRP)**

## Answer explanation

Decreasing C-reactive protein (CRP) levels are the most reliable method of tracking response to treatment in acute hematogenous osteomyelitis.

Acute hematogenous osteomyelitis (AHO) in children most commonly affects the metaphyseal region of bone given the sluggish blood flow in the region. Patients present with pain, erythema, swelling, and functional limitations at the involved area. CRP is elevated in 98% of patients with AHO. A sharp decrease in an elevated CRP level signifies an effective response to the given treatment with complete normalization occurring in 1 month. No other laboratory markers have been shown to be more predictive of a response to treatment for AHO.

*Unkila-Kallio et al. performed a study to compare the clinical value of the erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR), C-reactive protein (CRP), and white blood cell (WBC) count in diagnosis and follow-up of acute hematogenous osteomyelitis in children.* **They found** that CRP increased and decreased significantly faster than ESR and WBC. **They concluded** that CRP is a better measure for the effectiveness of therapy compared to ESR and WBC.

*Dormans et al. reviewed acute hematogenous osteomyelitis (AHO) and subacute osteomyelitis, including Brodie's abscess, subacute epiphyseal osteomyelitis, and chronic recurrent multifocal osteomyelitis.* **They recommended** various imaging modalities and ultimately biopsy to clarify the diagnosis. The goals of treatment were defined as (1) identification of the organism, (2) proper antibiotic selection, (3) adequate delivery of antibiotics and (4) arrest of tissue destruction.

Figure A shows a T2 MRI of the foot with increased signal intensity of the first metatarsal with surrounding edema and a small abscess consistent with osteomyelitis. Illustration A is a curve depicting ESR and CRP initial response and resolution/normalization to a stimulus.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Procalcitonin would be expected to decrease following effective treatment.
Answer 2: Procalcitonin is currently thought to be slightly less sensitive and specific than CRP for acute osteomyelitis and is under further investigation in musculoskeletal infections.
Answer 3: Blood cultures are often negative at presentation and not a reliable method of response to treatment.
Answer 4: ESR takes longer (3 weeks) to normalize and is therefore not as useful for monitoring treatment response.
