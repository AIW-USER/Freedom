## Question

A 65-year-old man undergoes total knee replacement and is found to have deep vein thrombosis two days later. What molecule is thought to be involved in this process when it is released during surgical dissection?

## Options

1. Prothrombin
2. RANKL
3. IL-1b
4. Thromboplastin
5. Factor XI

## Correct option

**4. Thromboplastin**

## Answer explanation

Thromboplastin is also known more commonly as Tissue Factor (TF), which is involved in the Extrinsic Pathology of the coagulation cascade.

During surgical dissection, insults occur to the endothelial walls of blood vessels. There are three ways in which the body reacts to form a clot so that the patient does not bleed excessively. One is via vessel contraction, another is by collagen release, and a third is by tissue thromboplastin release. Thromboplastin release is part of the extrinsic coagulation pathway (see Illustration A). Thromboplastin release activates Factor VII which activates Factor X which converts prothrombin to thrombin. Thrombin is the catalyst for converting fibrinogen to fibrin which induces clot formation. While this is useful for decreasing bleeding, it is the same mechanism by which a deep venous thrombosis (DVT) develops.
