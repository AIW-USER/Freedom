## Question

Which of the following skeletal deformity can we expect in a patient having sex linked mutation of the SHOX (Short stature Homeobox) gene?

## Options

1. Radial Club Hand / Radial Dysplasia
2. Blount’s disease
3. Madelung’s disease
4. Congenital pseudo arthrosis of Tibia

## Correct option

**3. Madelung’s disease**

## Answer explanation

In Radial club hand, likely related gene is Sonic Hedgehog gene

There is believed to be a genetic component associated with Blount’s disease, no specific named gene has been specified

Congenital pseudo arthrosis of tibia can be associated with NF 1 gene

Congenital Madelung deformities are associated with a mutation or absence of the short stature homeobox (SHOX) gene in the pseudo autosomal region of sex chromosomes. The same genetic deficiencies are found in patients with Leri Weill dyschondrosteosis, a condition associated with Madelung deformities.

Madelung’s deformity

An abnormality of the palmar ulnar part of the distal radial physis in which there is progressive ulnar and volar tilt of the distal radial articular surface with dorsal subluxation of distal ulna.

![[TO17905699-1.png]]

Progressive ulnar and volar tilt of radial distal end causes dorsal subluxation of ulna

This deformity was first described by Malgaigne and later by Otto Madelung in 1878.

Etiology:

It is believed to be a congenital disorder, although it’s not obvious until late childhood or adolescence. Cause is unclear, although it has an Autosomal Dominant Pattern.

Soft tissue causes:

- Ligament of Vickers

Vickers described an abnormal ligament that tethers the lunate to the distal radius proximal to the physis. This ligament is believed to impede the growth of the Ulno-palmar aspect of Distal Radius and is commonly known as Ligament of Vickers.

Genetic causes:

Congenital Madelung deformities are associated with a mutation or absence of the short stature homeobox (SHOX) gene in the pseudo autosomal region of sex chromosomes. The same genetic deficiencies are found in patients with Leri Weill dyschondrosteosis, a condition associated with Madelung deformities.

Other associated conditions are Turner’s syndrome and Achondroplasia

Apart from true Madelung deformities, Madelung-like deformities are seen in conditions including multiple hereditary exostoses and Ollier disease

Classification:

Vender and Watson Classification:

- Post traumatic
- Dysplastic e.g. dyschondrosteosis, Diaphyseal achalasia
- Genetic e.g. Turner syndrome
- Idiopathic

Clinical features:

Volar subluxation of hand

Prominence of distal ulna and volar and ulnar angulation of distal radius

Commonly bilateral, Girls>Boys

Family history of deformity (+)

Manifests in late childhood or early adolescence

Decreased ROM, Minimal pain

As growth occurs, deformity worsens

Deformity is painless

Advanced case, pain may limit function

X-ray

- Steep ulnar slope and deficient ulnar margin of radius
- Lunate uncovered.
- The carpus subluxates ulnar and palmar ward and appears wedge shaped (lunate lies at the apex of the wedge)
- Increased width between the distal radius and ulna.
- Relatively long ulna compared to radius (positive ulnar variance).
- Decreased carpal angle.
- Triangularization of the distal radial epiphysis.
- Carpus migrates more proximal into the increasing diastasis between the radius and the ulna

Treatment:

In recent or acute cases, dorsiflexion of the wrist-maintained by a full arm plaster for 4 weeks.

- Indications for surgery: Acute pain and deformity

-

- Epiphysiolysis: (Resection of abnormal portion of radial physis) + release of Viker’s ligament – In skeletally immature patients, reduces pain and gradually improves deformity during growth
- Distal radial osteotomy with ulnar shortening– In skeletally immature patient with marked deformity
- Most patients present in adolescence, in them physiolysis with ligament resection and dome osteotomy is the preferred option.

- In skeletally mature patients,

- Ligament resection + dome osteotomy
- Radial closing wedge osteotomy + ulnar shortening (MILCH RESECTION)
- Radial opening wedge osteotomy
- Radial osteotomy and distal ulnar resection (DARRACH’S RESECTION)
- Radial osteotomy and Suave kapandji procedure
