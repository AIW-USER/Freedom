## Question

What laboratory findings would you expect to find in a patient newly diagnosed with renal osteodystrophy?

## Options

1. Decreased PTH secretion, hypophosphatemia, and hypocalcemia
2. Increased PTH secretion, hyperphosphatemia, and hypocalcemia
3. Decreased PTH secretion, hypophosphatemia, and hypercalcemia
4. Increased PTH secretion, hyperphosphatemia, and hypercalcemia
5. Hypophosphatemia and hypocalcemia

## Correct option

**2. Increased PTH secretion, hyperphosphatemia, and hypocalcemia**

## Answer explanation

Renal osteodystrophy is characterized by bone mineralization deficiency due to the electrolyte and endocrine abnormalities associated with chronic kidney disease. The pathogenesis of renal osteodystrophy is multifactorial. Hypocalcemia occurs due to the inability of the damaged kidney to convert vitamin D3 into its active form, calcitriol. The hypocalcemia and lack of phosphate excretion by the damaged kidney causes hyperparathyroidism and secondary hyperphosphatemia.
