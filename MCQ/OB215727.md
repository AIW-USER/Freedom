## Question

A 37-year-old recreationally active male presents to the clinic for evaluation of chronic pain in his right heel cord. He states that he experienced an audible pop in his ankle 3 months ago while snowboarding. On exam, there is a palpable defect along the mid substance of the Achilles tendon and the patient is unable to actively plantarflex the ankle against gravity. He elects to pursue operative intervention. During surgery, 3.5cm of scar tissue is resected. It is decided to augment the reconstruction with a local tendon autograft. Which of the following correctly describes the anatomy of the tendon that is most suitable for augmentation?

## Options

1. It is found lateral to the FDL at the level of the ankle and courses plantar to the FDL in the midfoot
2. It is found medial to the FDL at the level of the ankle and partially inserts on the medial aspect of the navicular
3. It is found medial to the FHL at the level of the ankle and courses dorsal to the FHL in the midfoot
4. It is found lateral to the FDL at the level of the ankle and courses dorsal to the FDL in the midfoot
5. Originates on the lateral condyle of the femur and is found just medial to the achilles tendon prior to inserting on the calcaneus

## Correct option

**4. It is found lateral to the FDL at the level of the ankle and courses dorsal to the FDL in the midfoot**

## Answer explanation

In the setting of a chronic Achilles tendon rupture, the FHL is a local tendon autograft that can be used to augment repairs. The FHL originates on the interosseous membrane and the distal two-thirds of the fibula, is found lateral to the FDL at the level of the ankle, and courses dorsal to the FDL at the Knot of Henry in the midfoot.

Chronic Achilles tendon ruptures can be treated with a variety of surgical techniques, including end-to-end repair, fasciaplasty, tendon transfers or rarely with the use of allograft. The size of the defect can help guide surgical techniques. When end-to-end repair is not feasible, a VY advancement alone may be adequate when the defect is <3cm. For larger defects, tissue augmentation may necessitate the use of local tendon autograft. The concept of using the flexor tendons of the foot to augment the Achilles tendon began with the FDL, described in 1991. The more convenient anatomy of the FHL was subsequently recognized, and the technique of FHL augmentation for chronic Achilles tendon rupture was first described in 1993. The transfer of the flexor hallucis longus (FHL) tendon for chronic Achilles tendon disorders has been found to reduce pain and improve function. It is typically performed first by releasing the FHL at the Knot of Henry. A tenodesis between the distal FHL stump and the FDL is then typically performed. The FHL is then re-routed proximally towards the proximal stump of the resected Achilles tendon to which it is tenodesed. In doing so, the tendon transfer allows both the FHL muscle and the triceps surae to act on the calcaneus (as opposed to simply imbricating the FHL into the proximal and distal stumps of the Achilles in-situ).

*Alhaug et al.* *2018 performed a retrospective review of 21 patients treated with open FHL transfer for chronic Achilles tendon rupture.* *They reported on the evaluation of strength, endurance, function and PROMs.* **They concluded** that patients achieve almost normal maximal strength after open FHL, however, endurance is notably lower.

*Hahn et al.* *2008 evaluated 13 patients that underwent Achilles tendon augmentation with FHL transfer.* **They reported** that all patients had a significant reduction of pain. The operated side had a torque deficit of 35% for plantar flexion. Ten patients returned to their former level of activity and **they noted** hypertrophy of the FHL of more than 15% in eight patients on MRI. **They concluded** that FHL augmentation has high patient satisfaction without donor site morbidity and that the primary benefit of the operation is pain relief and increased muscle strength.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The FHL courses DORSAL to the FDL at the Knot of Henry in the midfoot.
Answer 2: This describes the posterior tibialis tendon, which would be inappropriate for harvest as this would promote the development of pes planus.
Answer 3: While the use of the FDL has been described in this setting, the FDL courses plantar to the FHL at the Knot of Henry.
Answer 5: While the plantaris could be used as part of a tendon augmentation, its biomechanical contribution is relatively insignificant, and it must be supplemented with a more robust donor such as the FHL.
