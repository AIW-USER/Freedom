## Question

When compared with primary arthrodesis for the treatment of ligamentous Lisfranc injuries, open reduction and internal fixation has which of the following?

## Options

1. Higher overall cost of treatment
2. Higher rate of deep infection
3. Improved patient reported outcomes
4. Lower implant removal rates
5. Lower revision surgery rate

## Correct option

**1. Higher overall cost of treatment**

## Answer explanation

Lisfranc injuries are often treated with either open reduction and internal fixation (ORIF) or primary arthrodesis (PA). Patients treated with ORIF have a higher overall reoperation rate and cost compared with PA.

A Lisfranc injury is a tarsometatarsal fracture dislocation characterized by traumatic disruption between the articulation of the medial cuneiform and base of the second metatarsal. Surgical treatments include ORIF or PA. Indications for ORIF include any evidence of instability (>2mms shift) and bony fracture dislocations. Indications for a PA include purely ligamentous injury, delayed treatment or chronic deformity. Level 1 evidence has demonstrated equivalent functional outcomes and decreased rate of hardware removal with PA. This leads to an overall decreased cost in patients treated with PA.

*Albright et al. reviewed the cost effectiveness of ORIF vs primary arthrodesis (PA) for Lisfranc injuries.* **They found** that ORIF was always associated with greater costs compared with PA and was less effective in the long term. They noted that the group undergoing PA spent, on average, $43,192 less than the ORIF group, and PA was overall a more effective technique. **They concluded** that PA would clearly be the preferred treatment strategy for predominantly ligamentous Lisfranc injuries and dislocations.

*Henning et al. evaluated whether performing a PA resulted in improved functional outcome and fewer subsequent surgeries as compared to primary ORIF.* **They found** that the rate of planned and unplanned secondary surgeries, including hardware removal and salvage arthrodesis, between ORIF and PA groups was 78.6% vs. 16.7%, respectively. **They concluded** that PA of tarsometatarsal joint injuries resulted in a significant reduction in the rate of follow-up surgical procedures.

*Buda et al. evaluated whether reoperation rates, excluding planned hardware removal, differ between ORIF and PA.* **They found** that patients treated with ORIF had a significantly higher rate of return to the operation room compared to those in the PA group, 75.5% and 31.5%, respectively. However, when excluding planned hardware removal, there was no difference in reoperation rates between the 2 groups. **They concluded** that when excluding planned removal of hardware, patients with Lisfranc injuries treated with ORIF did not demonstrate a higher rate of reoperation compared with those undergoing PA.

*Smith et al. reviewed whether ORIF or PA led to fewer reoperations, higher patient outcome scores, and more frequent anatomic reduction.* **They reported** more hardware removal surgeries for ORIF than PA. However, for other revision surgery, there was not any difference between groups. Similarly, neither was favored using patient-reported outcomes. **They concluded** that there is an increased risk of hardware removal along with its associated morbidity in patients treated with ORIF of Lisfranc injuries.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: There is not any difference between groups with respect to rates of deep infection.
Answer 3: There is not any difference between groups with respect to patient reported outcomes.
Answer 4: ORIF has not been shown to have a lower rate of implant removal.
Answer 5: ORIF has not been shown to have a lower rate of revision surgery.
