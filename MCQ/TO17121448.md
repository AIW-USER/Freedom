Chapter: Arthroscopy MCQs — Shoulder Instability[Unidirectional and Multidirectional]

## Question

Which of the following statements is true regarding the Putti-Platt (imbrication of the anterior capsule and subscapularis tendon) procedure for anterior shoulder instability?

## Options

1. Redislocation has not been reported.
2. Glenohumeral arthrosis in the setting of a prior Putti-Platt procedure is associated with compromised range of motion.
3. The objective of the procedure is to rotate large Hill-Sachs lesions posterolaterally by increasing humeral head retroversion.
4. Injury to the musculocutaneous nerve is a common complication.

## Correct option

**2. Glenohumeral arthrosis in the setting of a prior Putti-Platt procedure is associated with compromised range of motion.**

## Answer explanation

The Putti-Platt procedure is of historical importance in the management of anterior shoulder instability. It is characterised by imbrication of the anterior capsule and subscapularis tendon. Two common complications of the procedure are glenohumeral arthrosis (due to excessive tightening of the joint) and loss of external rotation.

Also know:

The Bristow procedure involves transfer of a portion of the coracoid process to the anterior glenoid rim.The transferred coracobrachialis and short head of the biceps theoretically serve as dynamic stabilizers to hold the humeral head posteriorly, and the transferred portion of coracoid provides a static bone block effect.

 Injury to the musculocutaneous nerve is an infrequent complication of the Putti-Platt procedure. Musculocutaneous nerve damage is more common after the Bristow procedure because of potential tethering of the nerve by the transferred soft tissues. Both the Putti- Platt and the Bristow procedures can produce internal rotation contractures.

Answer: 2
