## Question

An 11-year-old male presents to the ED following a left elbow injury while playing football. Radiographs demonstrate a medial epicondyle fracture with 5mm of displacement. The elbow joint is well reduced on all views. You plan to treat the patient with a brief immobilization in a long-arm cast. Which of the following is true of this treatment strategy compared to operative intervention?

## Options

1. Increased risk of nonunion
2. Increased risk of cubitus varus
3. Worse functional outcomes
4. Higher incidence of fishtail deformity
5. Higher incidence of ulnar nerve neurapraxia

## Correct option

**1. Increased risk of nonunion**

## Answer explanation

Patients with displaced medial epicondyle fractures treated with immobilization alone have a higher incidence of nonunion than those treated surgically. The odds of fracture union are 9 times higher with operative stabilization.

Medial epicondyle fractures most commonly occur in children between the ages of 9 and 14-years-old following valgus stress or elbow dislocation. In those patients who sustain elbow dislocations, the location of the medial epicondyle fragment is paramount, as it can often remain incarcerated in the joint. For patients with an adequately reduced (<5mm) medial epicondyle fracture, treatment consists of immobilization in a long-arm cast. Compared to operative stabilization, nonoperative management has a significantly higher incidence of nonunion, however, the majority are asymptomatic. Functional outcomes at final follow up have been shown to be equivalent between patients treated operatively and nonoperatively.

*Farsetti et al. review the long-term results of operatively and nonoperatively treated medial epicondyle fractures.* *They report on 42 patients who were divided into three groups: immobilization alone, open reduction internal fixation, and fragment excision.* **They conclude** that patients with displacement of <15mm who were treated nonoperatively demonstrated good long-term clinical results despite the high rate of radiographic nonunion.

*Josefsson et al. review the long-term outcomes of unreduced epicondylar fractures in children.* *They report on 56 cases at an average follow up of 35 years.* **They conclude** that pseudarthrosis is common in patients with 1-15mm of displacement, however, this did not affect function or range of motion.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Cubitus varus may occur with malunion of supracondylar humerus fractures, not medial epicondyle fractures.
Answer 3: Functional outcomes have been shown to equivalent between immobilization and ORIF for medial epicondyle fractures with <15mm of displacement.
Answer 4: Fishtail deformity is seen with lateral condyle fractures when the area between the medial ossification center and lateral condyle fails to develop.
Answer 5: There is not an increased incidence of ulnar neurapraxia with nonoperative treatment of medial epicondyle fractures.
