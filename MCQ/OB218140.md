## Question

A 42-year-old female presents to your clinic with 1 year of hip pain. Upon chart review, you find that she had a total hip arthroplasty for hip dysplasia 5 years ago by an outside surgeon. The operative report states that a modular neck hip stem was used. On exam, she has a limb-length discrepancy but otherwise normal gait. On serum labs, erythrocyte sedimentation rate is 1.1 mm/h, C-Reactive Protein is 0.9 mg/L, Cobalt level is 6.1 ng/mL (normal <0.9 ng/mL), Chromium level is 2.3 ng/mL (normal <0.3 ng/mL). Radiographs show a stable modular neck hip stem implant without subsidence or surrounding lucency. MRI with metal-artifact reduction sequence (MARS) is performed and shows capsular thickening with intracapsular fluid.

What is the most likely etiology of her symptoms?

## Options

1. Adherent surface oxide coating on implants
2. Bacterial adherence to implant surface with glycocalyx formation
3. Electrochemical energy transfer between two dissimilar metals
4. Isolated histiocyte response to wear particles
5. Micromotion at femoral head-neck or stem-neck junction

## Correct option

**5. Micromotion at femoral head-neck or stem-neck junction**

## Answer explanation

This patient had a dual modular designed (or modular neck hip stem) total hip arthroplasty (THA) which has been historically susceptible to fretting corrosion. In the absence of infection, fretting corrosion is the most likely cause of symptoms in this patient. Fretting corrosion results from micromotion at junction interfaces (answer choice 5).

Dual modular (or modular neck hip stem) components feature a separate diaphyseal segment and metaphyseal segment that can be independently fitted and instrumented to fit the patient. These systems are beneficial for recreating the femoral version, limb length, and offset in settings of complex THA. The drawback in the creation of two junctions (the head-neck junction and the neck-stem junction), is an increased risk of corrosion. In particular, fretting corrosion is the most common cause of corrosive failure in these components. Fretting corrosion occurs when micromotion at a modular junction disrupts the protective surface oxide layer. The patient described above demonstrated low concern for infection but has elevated serum metal ion levels. Patients can also develop an adverse local tissue reaction, demonstrated by a MARS MRI with increased capsular fluid. Treatment typically involves revision of the entire femoral component to a monoblock stem if feasible.

*Meftah et al. investigated the rate of corrosion-related failure of a specific modular THA system.* *The authors specifically studied 104 patients who underwent THA with the Rejuvenate hip system (97 modular, 26 nonmodular), which has since been recalled due to early failures.* *The study found higher Co and Cr levels and revision rates (28% at 2 years) in modular implants.* Higher metal ion levels and pain were predictors of revision surgery. The authors theorize that fretting corrosion was the main cause of corrosive failure. Overall, **the authors found** an alarmingly high rate of corrosion-related revisions in the Rejuvenate modular hip system.

*Srinivasan et al. published a review article on modular femoral components in THA in 2012.* *The review highlights the benefits of modular implants while also highlighting the complications.* In regards to dual modular implants, **the authors note** the increased amount of corrosion secondary to the additional junction. **The authors report** that fretting corrosion is the major source of failure in dual modular components.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer choice 1: Both titanium and Cobalt alloy components can form an adherent oxide coating that decreases corrosion, called self-passivation. Despite this, both materials are still susceptible to corrosion as the protective layer will be disrupted with repetitive micromotion. Self-passivation is protective against corrosion and is therefore not the correct answer for this patient's symptoms.
Answer choice 2: Bacteria form an extracellular matrix (glycocalyx) during the chronic stage of a prosthetic joint infection. This is less likely the cause of the patient's symptoms given the normal serum inflammatory labs.
Answer choice 3: Galvanic corrosion is an electrochemical reaction occurring between two dissimilar metals in a conductive medium. It is most likely to occur when titanium alloy stems are mated to a CoCr head in the modular hip setting. Although this can occur, it is less likely than fretting corrosion in modular hips.
Answer choice 4: This process describes osteolysis. Osteolysis can occur as a downstream effect of corrosion and increased metal debris. However, this is not the primary isolated cause of the patient's symptoms and was not described in this patient who had normal radiographs.
