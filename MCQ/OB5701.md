## Options

1. Tenotomy
2. Osteotomy
3. Resection arthroplasty
4. Arthrodesis with particulate bone graft
5. Arthrodesis with structural bone graft

## Correct option

**5. Arthrodesis with structural bone graft**

## Answer explanation

Arthrodesis is the best salvage procedure for failed metatarsophalangeal (MTP) joint hemiarthroplasty with shortening. Structural bone graft is indicated to restore length.

Arthrodesis is the gold standard for end-stage hallux rigidus. Hemi-/total joint arthroplasty has high loosening rates and poor patient satisfaction. For minimal bone loss, particulate graft will suffice. Structural grafting is indicated when there is 1st MT shortening that cannot be adequately rebalanced with a lesser metatarsal osteotomy (usually shortening > 5 mm) or significant bone loss of the proximal phalanx with inadequate remaining bone for fixation without compromising the IP joint, or resultant severe shortening with loss of medial support of the second toe predisposing to varus at the 2nd MTP joint.

*Garras et al. performed a retrospective review of 21 hemiarthroplasties converted to 21 fusions.* Local bone graft was used in 12 and 6 required iliac crest. All achieved fusions but at a longer interval than primary fusion. **They concluded** that fusion for failed hemiarthroplasty has similar success although it takes longer and may require regional bone graft for small defects.

*Brodsky et al. retrospectively reviewed 12 cases of MTP fusion with iliac crest bone graft (ICBG) because of failed MTP arthroplasty (8), AVN (2), fusion nonunion (1) and osteomyelitis (1).* *They used plate and screws, plate and crossed screws and/or K wires.* All fused except one patient. All had pain relief, good alignment and improved satisfaction and function.

Figures A and B show a cock-up toe deformity. Figures C and D show failed hemiarthroplasty (loose implant) with metatarsal and phalangeal sided bone loss.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Cock-up deformity arises primarily because of bone loss and disrupted tendon length-tension relationships on the plantar/dorsal aspect. Surgery should address the primary defect (failed hemiarthroplasty and shortening) rather than the consequence (cock-up toe).
Answer 2: Osteotomies will not correct the primary defect, which is bone loss and implant loosening.
Answer 3: Resection arthroplasty reduces the weight-bearing function of the great toe and increases the peak pressures under the lesser metatarsal heads.
Answer 4: Structural graft is indicated to address massive bone loss.
