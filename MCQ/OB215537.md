## Question

A 28-year-old male is scheduled to undergo intramedullary nailing for a comminuted, mid-shaft femur fracture. You plan to utilize non-steroidal-anti-inflammatories (NSAIDs) as part of his multi-modal pain control regimen post-operatively. Which of the following is true regarding NSAIDs?

## Options

1. Mechanism of action is inhibition of phospholipase A2
2. Prolonged use decreases endochondral ossification
3. Selective inhibition of cyclooxygenase-1 (COX-1) decreases GI side effects
4. Adult patients are at lower risk for nonunion or delayed union than pediatric patients
5. Primary bone healing is more likely to be effected than secondary bone healing

## Correct option

**2. Prolonged use decreases endochondral ossification**

## Answer explanation

The effect of NSAID use on fracture healing in animal studies has demonstrated increased risk of nonunion due to decreased endochondral ossification because NSAIDs decrease chondrogenic differentiation.

NSAID use, and its' effect on bone healing, is controversial in the management of orthopedic injuries. While NSAIDs are effective as an analgesic, anti-inflammatory, and anti-pyretic medication, the potential risk for delayed union or nonunion is important to understand when using these medications in the orthopedic patient. While no consensus exists regarding the ideal therapeutic regimen, several recent studies have attempted to provide insight into the use of NSAIDs.

*Giannoudis et al. reviewed the physiology of fracture healing, as well as the effect of NSAIDs on bone healing.* *They discuss the mechanism of inhibition of bone healing is through blockage of stem cell chondrogenic differentiation, thus inhibiting endochondral ossification (i.e. callus formation).* **They conclude** that constructs that rely on callus formation (secondary bone healing) are at higher risk of nonunion than those that rely on primary bone healing (absolute stability).

*Wheatley et al. performed a meta-analysis reviewing the effect of NSAIDs on bone healing rates.* **They found** NSAID exposure increased the risk of delayed union or nonunion except in pediatric patients, low dose exposure, and short duration exposure. **They conclude** prolonged NSAID usage post-operatively in adult patients has a negative effect on bone healing.

Illustration A is a diagram demonstrating the mechanism of action of NSAIDs and steroids.

Answer 1: NSAIDs inhibit COX-1 and 2. Steroids (e.g. prednisone) inhibit phospholipase A2.
Answer 3: Selective inhibition of COX-2 (e.g. celecoxib), not COX-1, decreases GI side effects.
Answer 4: Prolonged NSAID use has been associated with delayed union and nonunion in adult patients, not pediatric.
Answer 5: NSAIDs inhibit callus formation via blockage of chondrogenic differentiation. Thus, secondary bone healing is most likely to be affected
