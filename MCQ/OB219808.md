## Question

A 26-year-old professional basketball player becomes a free agent at the end of the season and undergoes a diagnostic knee arthroscopy. During surgery, a meniscal tear is diagnosed and repaired. In subsequent contract negotiations with another team, the player is questioned about their medical history, and the name of their treating physician is requested. Which of the following describes the responsibility of physicians when treating professional athletes and interacting with teams?

## Options

1. A private physician is obligated to disclose information to potential future teams
2. A team physician is obligated to disclose all findings to their team of employment
3. A team physician can disclose information as determined by the player in question
4. HIPPA protects athletes from any type of disclosure without consent
5. Surgical reports for professional athletes are open to evaluation by prospective teams

## Correct option

**2. A team physician is obligated to disclose all findings to their team of employment**

## Answer explanation

Private physicians are not obligated to disclose patient information regardless of the requesting enterprise. In contrast, physicians employed by teams are considered agents of the team and obligated to provide accurate representations (Answer 2).

The role of a team physician presents unique ethical challenges, particularly regarding the confidentiality of a player's medical information. When a team employs a physician, they are considered an agent of that team, meaning they must prioritize the team's needs. Consequently, the medical information of a player under the care of a team-employed physician becomes part of the player's employment record and is not protected under HIPAA (Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act). This creates a potential conflict of interest, as the physician must balance the team's interests with the player's well-being. In contrast, independent physicians who care for athletes adhere to HIPAA regulations and are not required to disclose a player's medical information to team officials, thereby ensuring the player's privacy is maintained. This distinction highlights the ethical responsibility of team physicians to navigate their dual obligations carefully while advocating for the best interests of the athlete.

*Bernstein et al. surveyed professional ethicists and collegiate and professional team physicians to better define ethical norms and ideals in sports medicine.* Respondents were provided with 6 hypothetical cases covering advertising, conflicting healthcare goals, confidentiality, innovative treatments, enabling dangerous behavior, and treating children and asked to agree with statements regarding each case. **The authors found** agreement between respondents on 18/23 statements and concluded while there is significant agreement there was substantial variation amongst groups, suggesting there remain unresolved issues within the field

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: Private physicians are not obligated to disclose anything information to prospective teams unless instructed to do so by the athlete in question.
Answers 3 & 4. A team physician is considered an agent of the team and is obligated to represent the needs of their employer, not the athlete. As athletes are employed by professional teams their medical record is considered as an employment record and is not protected by HIPPA.
Answer 5: A team physician would be required to inform their team of employment of any intraoperative findings but in this scenario, the player was under the care of a private physician.
