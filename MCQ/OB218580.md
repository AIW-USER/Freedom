## Question

A 21-year-old professional ballerina presents to your office for evaluation of posterior ankle pain that has been present for about 6 months. The patient reports mastering en pointe dancing last year which was showcased in her winter recital. She has no history of injury and reports the pain began insidiously. NSAIDs and therapy have not provided relief. On exam, she has pain over the posteromedial ankle with associated crepitus. She has pain with forced plantarflexion of the ankle. There is no tenderness over the metatarsal head. With toe rise, the affected heel inverts. Which of the following structures is most closely linked to the patient's symptoms?

## Options

1. Extensor hallucis longus tendon
2. Flexor hallucis longus tendon
3. Tibialis posterior tendon
4. Tibialis anterior tendon
5. Achilles tendon

## Correct option

**2. Flexor hallucis longus tendon**

## Answer explanation

Posterior ankle impingement syndrome (PAIS) results in scarring and tendinopathy of the flexor hallucis longus tendon.

The FHL tendon originates from the posterior fibula, and travels between posteromedial/posterolateral tubercles of the talus in a fibro-osseous tunnel before passing beneath the sustentaculum tali. In PAIS, there is impingement of the FHL and resultant tendinopathy that occurs with activities requiring maximal plantarflexion such as en pointe dancers. Patients commonly report posteromedial ankle pain and crepitance. Locking and catching with great toe motion may also occur. Treatment typically consists of rest, activity modifications, and physical therapy. For those patients with recalcitrant symptoms, the FHL can be released from the fibro-osseous tunnel and a tenosynovectomy or tendinous repair can be performed.

*Hamilton et al review pain in the posterior aspect of the ankle in dancers.* *They report on a total of 37 dancers that were treated surgically for posterior impingement/FHL tenosynovitis.* **They conclude** that good or excellent results were obtained in 28/34 professional dancers compared to only 2/6 amateur dancers.

*Maquirriain et al review posterior ankle impingement syndrome.* **They report** that posterior ankle impingement syndrome is a clinical disorder characterized by posterior ankle pain that occurs in forced plantar flexion and is often caused by pathology of the os trigonum or FHL tenosynovitis. They further outline that the diagnosis is made primarily from physical exam and history taking. **They conclude** that symptoms typically improve with nonsurgical management, but surgery may be required in refractory cases.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1 & 4: The EHL and tibialis anterior tendons are on the opposite side of the foot and is not related to PAIS.
Answer 3: The patient has evidence of a functioning tibialis posterior as evidenced by heel inversion with toe rise.
Answer 5: While in the vicinity, the Achilles tendon is not the cause of discomfort with PAIS and remains normal.
