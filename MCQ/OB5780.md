## Question

A healthy 8-week-old infant with unilateral developmental dysplasia of the hip has been prescribed full-time Pavlik harness. At the 3 week followup, the left hip is positioned in the harness with 120 degrees of flexion and 30 degrees of abduction. No active knee extension is noted. Ultrasound imaging shows 50% femoral head coverage with an alpha angle of 52 degrees. What is the MOST LIKELY reason for this patients current presentation?

## Options

1. Undiagnosed genetic syndrome
2. Peripheral neuritis
3. Failure to initiate treatment at the time of birth
4. Poor compliance with treatment
5. Improper positioning of the pavlik harness

## Correct option

**5. Improper positioning of the pavlik harness**

## Answer explanation

This patient has clinical symptoms consistent with a femoral nerve palsy likely relating to excessive flexion of the hip in the pavlik harness. The ultrasound findings demonstrate a reasonably seated femoral head with immature acetabular development (normal head coverage >50%; alpha angle >60 degrees).

Positioning of the Pavlik harness is critical for management of DDH. The correct hip position should be 90-100 degrees of flexion and 20-40 degrees of abduction. Excessive flexion should be avoided to reduce the incidence of femoral nerve palsy. Excessive abduction should be avoided as this can increase the incidence of hip avascular necrosis. Indications to discontinue a Pavlik harness early include hips that do no reduce within 3 to 4 weeks (as this can cause erosion of the posterosuperior acetabulum known as "Pavlik harness disease"), femoral nerve palsy, and systemic comorbidity (e.g. early spinal/thoracic/cardiac surgery, etc.).

*Guille et al. reviewed development dysplasia of the hip from birth to six months.* **They state** that the Pavlik harness is contraindicated in patients with muscle imbalance (cerebral palsy, myelodysplasia), contractures (arthrogryposis), abnormal connective tissue laxity (Ehlers-Danlos Syndrome). X-rays are not indicated until 4-5 months of age as this is the average age for proximal femoral ossification.

*Murnaghan et al. reviewed 30 babies with femoral nerve palsy from Pavlik harness.* *87% presented in the first week.* These were more likely in larger babies with more severe DDH. **They showed** that femoral nerve palsy strongly predicts treatment failure.

Illustration A demonstrates the alpha and beta angles used to measure hip formation/dysplasia. In a normal hip, the femoral head coverage should be >50%, alpha angle >60 degrees, and beta angles <50 degrees.

Incorrect Answer:
Answers 1,2: There is no features in this clinical stem that would suggest genetic syndromes or peripheral neuritis.
Answer 3: Pavlik harness treatment for DDH should ideally be initiated in the first 12 weeks of life. This patient had started their treatment within the appropriate time interval.
Answer 4: Poor compliance with brace use would have avoided pressure on the nerve and likely prevented a femoral nerve palsy.
