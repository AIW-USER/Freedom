## Question

An 8-year-old girl was playing a pickup game of street kickball when she fell while running the bases. After falling she had difficulty walking and was brought to the emergency department by her mother, after which she was referred to your clinic. In your office, you obtained X-rays shown in Figure A. On exam, she is most tender just proximal to the knee joint on the medial side. What is the ideal treatment strategy?

![[q216814-a-2-figure1-1.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Smooth K-wire fixation with long leg cast and non-weight bearing
2. Weight bearing as tolerated in knee immobilizer
3. Closed reduction with lateral bridge plating
4. Long leg cast with crutch mobilization
5. Open reduction and screw fixation

## Correct option

**4. Long leg cast with crutch mobilization**

## Answer explanation

This patient sustained a physeal injury to her medial distal femoral physis. Without displacement, this can be treated in a cast with protected weight-bearing.

Physeal injuries to the distal femur are common pediatric injuries that occur after direct trauma to the knee of a skeletally immature patient. Most commonly these occur as Salter-Harris II fractures, and typically can be diagnosed off plain Xrays. Stress radiographs are not advised in these injuries. Treatment depends on displacement and the fracture pattern. Nondisplaced fractures can be treated with cast immobilization alone, while displaced fractures typically require closed versus open reduction with internal fixation or percutaneous fixation. With these injuries, there is a high rate of growth disturbance due to the anatomy of the physis.

Dr. Lewis Zionts published a review discussing various pediatric fractures around the knee to include distal femoral and proximal tibia physeal fractures, tibial tubercle and spine fractures, and patellar fractures. For each diagnosis, he covers treatment and expected outcomes. For distal femoral physeal fractures it is recommended for nondisplaced fractures to perform 4-6 weeks of immobilization in a cast.

*Arkader et al. retrospectively reviewed physeal fractures of the distal femur in 73 patients.* **They found** 59% were salter harris II fractures, with 41% of all fractures non-displaced. Conservative treatment with cast or splint immobilization resulted in satisfactory results in 39 patients. The two main factors associated with outcomes were Salter-Harris classification and displacement of the fracture. The degree and direction of displacement were not associated with outcomes.

Figure A shows an AP and lateral x-ray of a right knee of a skeletally immature patient with widening of the medial aspect of the distal femoral physis.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1,5: For a non-displaced physeal injury, cast immobilization is sufficient treatment. Displaced injuries may require closed versus open reduction with smooth k-wire fixation or screw fixation that do not cross the physis.
Answer 2: This patient would require the most rigid immobilization and protected weight-bearing in order to prevent displacement at the physis while healing.
Answer 3: Lateral bridge plating with a closed reduction may be useful in a comminuted femoral shaft fracture, but not likely beneficial in this physeal injury.
