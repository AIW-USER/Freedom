## Question

A 40-year-old female presents to your office with the radiographs in Figures A and B. The patient is very active and is worried about her overall function following the treatment of the injury. Which of the following factors is the most important predictors of a successful clinical outcome?

*Figure A*

![[q214830-b-finger-pre-op-1.jpg]]

## Options

1. Anatomic articular surface reduction
2. Stable fracture fragment fixation
3. Early motion
4. Repair or reconstruction of the volar plate
5. Congruent gliding joint

## Correct option

**5. Congruent gliding joint**

## Answer explanation

Figures A and B depict a middle phalanx palmar lip fracture as a result of a dorsal PIP dislocation. A congruent gliding joint reduction and early motion are the most important factors for a successful clinical outcome.

Dorsal Proximal Interphalangeal Joint (PIPJ) dislocations are a result of hyperextension and longitudinal compression. They can be simple or complex (block to reduction). Commonly, a palmar lip fracture of the middle phalanx is seen. These palmar lip fractures are classified as stable (< 30%), tenuous (30-50%), or unstable (>50%). Treatment is dependent on stability. Stable dislocations can be treated with buddy tape and early motion. Tenuous dislocations that stay reduced with less than 30 degrees can be treated with extension block splinting. Unstable dislocations require operative treatment with either volar plate arthroplasty, ORIF, dynamic external fixator, or hemi-hamate. The most important factors for a successful outcome include restoring gliding of the middle phalanx around the proximal phalanx, early motion, and less importantly anatomic joint surface restoration.

*Kiefhaber and Stern reviewed fracture-dislocations of the proximal interphalangeal joint.* *They discussed the typical fracture patterns seen, treatment guidelines, classification systems, and treatment options.* **They found** that the most important factor for a successful outcome is restoring the gliding motion of the middle phalanx around the proximal phalanx. The next is early motion. Finally, anatomic joint reduction is ideal, but not essential to a good outcome.

*Shah and Sommerkamp reviewed fracture-dislocations of the finger joints.* *They reviewed assessment and treatment options for MCP, PIP, and DIP volar and dorsal fracture-dislocations.* In regards to PIP joint dorsal dislocations, they discussed using fluoroscopy to ensure that there is no hinging at the fracture site ("V-sign" - Illustration B) and those with concentric gliding motion can likely be treated non-operatively and have less risk of post-traumatic arthrosis and stiffness.

Figure A is an AP radiograph of the left hand demonstrating an angular deformity at the PIP joint of the index finger. Figure B is a lateral radiograph of the index finger demonstrating a palmar lip fracture of the middle phalanx with dorsal subluxation of the PIP joint.

Illustration A depicts a classification system of dorsal PIP joint fracture-dislocations.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Anatomic articular joint reduction is not essential for a successful outcome.
Answer 2: Stable fragment fixation is not the most essential for a successful outcome.
Answer 3: Early motion is important for a successful outcome, but is not the most important predictor.
Answer 4: Repair or reconstruction of the volar plate is not essential for a successful outcome.
