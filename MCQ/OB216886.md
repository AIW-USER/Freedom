## Question

A 19-year-old college lacrosse player presents to your office complaining of recurrent patellar dislocations that have not been formally evaluated by an orthopedist. On examination, he has a suprapatellar effusion and tenderness to palpation anteriorly. Significant apprehension to lateral translation is present. Radiographs and MRI are obtained and the patient is started in physical therapy. Based on the imaging findings and lack of progress in therapy, you recommend a diagnostic knee arthroscopy with MPFL reconstruction. For which of the following sets of imaging parameters would this be an appropriate treatment plan?

## Options

1. Insall-salvati ratio = 1.2, TT-TG distance = 13 mm, sulcus angle = 150º, positive crossing sign
2. Insall-salvati ratio = 1.15, TT-TG distance = 15 mm, sulcus angle = 138º, negative crossing sign
3. Insall-salvati ratio = 1.0, TT-TG distance = 21 mm, sulcus angle = 140º, negative crossing sign
4. Insall-salvati ratio = 1.3, TT-TG distance = 13 mm, sulcus angle = 145º, negative crossing sign
5. Insall-salvati ratio = 1.25, TT-TG distance = 20 mm, sulcus angle = 150º, positive crossing sign

## Correct option

**2. Insall-salvati ratio = 1.15, TT-TG distance = 15 mm, sulcus angle = 138º, negative crossing sign**

## Answer explanation

A patient undergoing an isolated medial patellofemoral ligament (MPFL) reconstruction should ideally not have patella alta (Insall-salvati ratio <1.2), with a TT-TG distance <20 mm, sulcus angle <140º, and a negative crossing sign.

Patellar instability is a condition underlined by a constellation of bony and ligamentous abnormalities that may contribute to episodes of recurrent subluxation/dislocation. Osseous factors contributing to instability include patella alta (Insall-salvati ratio >1.2), trochlear dysplasia (sulcus angle >140º), excessive lateral patellar tilt (lateral patellofemoral angle <11º), elevated Q-angle (TT-TG distance >20 mm), and excessive lateral femoral condyle hypoplasia. The MPFL is the primary ligamentous restraint to lateral patellar instability, particularly in the first 20º of knee flexion, while the bony structures account for stability in deeper knee flexion. MPFL reconstruction is generally quite successful in decreasing the rate of recurrent instability, however, patient selection is important and patients with bony abnormalities are more likely to have recurrences if these issues are not directly addressed by means of potential tibial tubercle osteotomy, trochleoplasty, etc.

*Iobst et al. reviewed acute knee injuries in skeletally immature athletes.* *They make note of the fact that hemarthrosis in this patient population cannot be ignored and may relate to a meniscal tear, ACL rupture, tibial tubercle avulsion or cartilage lesion.* *They go on to discuss physical examination findings and treatment algorithms for different knee injuries in this population.*

*Fithian et al. discuss the epidemiology and natural history of acute patellar dislocation in 189 patients.* **They noted** the highest risk of dislocation is in 10-17-year-old females. **They concluded** that this population was at particularly high risk (OR = 6.6) for re-dislocation if they had a history of a prior instability event.

*Schepsis et al. wrote a technique review detailing MPFL reconstruction.* *They first discuss the indications and anatomic justification for MPFL reconstruction.* *They go on to detail their technique and pearls for performing a successful reconstruction to reduce episodes of recurrent instability.*

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Given the degree of trochlear dysplasia this patient has, they would have a higher risk for failure with an isolated MPFL reconstruction.
Answer 3: With a TT-TG distance >20 mm, an anteromedializing tibial tubercle transfer should be considered.
Answer 4: This patient has a significant patella alta in addition to a sulcus angle concerning for trochlear dysplasia (>140º), therefore an isolated MPFL reconstruction would have a higher rate of failure.
Answer 5: This patient has patella alta, elevated TT-TG distance, and evidence of trochlear dysplasia and would be a poor candidate for isolated MPFL reconstruction.
