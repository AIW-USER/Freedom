## Question

A 20-month-old male presents for evaluation of a lower extremity deformity. Radiographs demonstrate bowing of the femur and tibia along with widening with widening/cupping of the lower extremity physes. The parents endorse following a vegetarian diet and highlight their son’s strict adherence to their food preferences. The patient is subsequently sent for laboratory studies. Which of the following combination of findings is most likely to be present in this patient?

## Options

1. Elevated vitamin D, increased calcium, elevated parathyroid hormone, elevated alkaline phosphatase
2. Low vitamin D, low calcium, elevated parathyroid hormone, elevated alkaline phosphatase
3. Elevated vitamin D, decreased calcium, decreased parathyroid hormone, decreased alkaline phosphatase
4. Low vitamin D, elevated calcium, elevated parathyroid hormone, elevated alkaline phosphatase
5. Low vitamin D, low calcium, low parathyroid hormone, low alkaline phosphatase

## Correct option

**2. Low vitamin D, low calcium, elevated parathyroid hormone, elevated alkaline phosphatase**

## Answer explanation

The patient has radiographic findings consistent with a diagnosis of rickets. Nutritional Rickets, a subtype of Rickets, is caused by inadequate vitamin D intake and can be seen in infants with vegetarian diets Laboratory findings in these patients include low vitamin D, low calcium, elevated parathyroid hormone, and elevated alkaline phosphatase (Answer 2).

Rickets is a metabolic disease that affects calcium and phosphate homeostasis, which in turn hampers matrix calcification in growing bone. Clinical manifestations include bowing of long bones, muscle hypotonia, and abnormal dental findings. Radiographs may demonstrate long bone physeal widening, metaphysical cupping, and rachitic rosaries (rib head prominence at the osteochondral junction). Though rare due to the fortification of milk with Vitamin D, nutritional Rickets may be seen in patients with malabsorption syndromes or specific dietary patterns (i.e., no milk, vegetarian diet). Patients with this specific subtype will have low Vitamin D and calcium levels. Parathyroid levels will be elevated in response to the low calcium levels and subsequently elevate alkaline phosphatase levels.

*Horan et al. published a review of the role of Vitamin D in the pediatric orthopedic population.* *They discuss the metabolic pathway of Vitamin D and its role in calcium homeostasis, abnormalities that may lead to metabolic and bony pathology, the large prevalence of patients with Vitamin D deficiency, and the moderate evidence that exists demonstrating that vitamin D deficiency does not generally contribute to fracture risk outside of rickets.* **They conclude** that outside of the parameters defined for vitamin D supplementation to prevent rickets, there is limited literature regarding optimal vitamin D levels and the role of supplementation to maintain bone health.

*Clarke et al. published a review discussing the literature assessing vitamin D deficiency in pediatric patients.* *They cover the presentation of patients with vitamin D-deficient rickets, the high prevalence of vitamin D deficiency in patients with cerebral palsy, which is associated with an increased risk of pathologic fracture specifically in this patient population, and the role that vitamin D has in optimizing outcomes in pediatric patients who undergo orthopaedic intervention.* **They conclude** that an understanding of the role that vitamin D has in the pediatric population is necessary to treat metabolic bony disease and minimize the potential impact that metabolic abnormalities have in patients postoperatively.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1, 3, 4, and 5: Nutritional Rickets is associated with low levels of Vitamin D and calcium, and elevated parathyroid hormone and alkaline phosphatase.
