## Question

A 48-year-old female underwent correction of a hallux valgus deformity by an outside surgeon. In reviewing her preoperative radiographs, the hallux is visibly pronated. You measure her initial intermetatarsal angle (IMA) to be 11 degrees, the hallux valgus angle (HVA) to be 38 degrees, distal metatarsal articular angle (DMAA) to be 12 degrees, and the hallux valgus interphalangeus 9 degrees. The treating surgeon elected to proceed with an isolated distal first metatarsal osteotomy for correction. Which of the following is most likely to remain abnormal following surgical correction with this technique?

## Options

1. Intermetatarsal angle (IMA)
2. Hallux valgus angle (HVA)
3. Distal metatarsal articular angle (DMAA)
4. Hallux valgus interphalangeus (HVI) angle
5. Pronation of the first metatarsal

## Correct option

**5. Pronation of the first metatarsal**

## Answer explanation

Hallux valgus correction with a distal metatarsal osteotomy is able to address some degree of deformity in the 1,2 intermetatarsal angle, distal metatarsal articular angle, and uncoverage of the sesamoids, but it is not able to correct for first metatarsal rotation.

First metatarsal rotation plays an important role in hallux valgus deformity. Plantar and lateral migration of the abductor hallucis force causes plantar flexion and pronation. Rotation of the metatarsal is most commonly evaluated on AP standing radiographs. In neutral rotation the lateral aspect of the metatarsal head appears flat, however, as increased pronation occurs the rounded portion of the metatarsal head becomes visible laterally, giving the distal metatarsal a rounded contour laterally. Surgical fixation for hallux valgus is most commonly determined by radiographic parameters. The distal metatarsal osteotomy is utilized for mild disease in which the IMA is <13 degrees and the HVA is less than or equal to 40 degrees. While commonly used, the distal metatarsal osteotomy in isolation is unable to adequately correct rotational deformities.

*Wagner et al review rotational deformity as part of the evaluation for hallux valgus.* **They report** that determining the rotational profile should be performed for each patient and is as important as measuring preoperative angles. **They conclude** that rotational deformity is most accurately addressed with fusion or proximal metatarsal osteotomies.

*Yasuda et al review the proximal supination osteotomy for hallux valgus.* **They report** that the rates of occurrence of a positive round sign and incomplete reduction of the sesamoids significantly decreased postoperatively utilizing this technique. **They conclude** that the proximal supination osteotomy is an effective tool for treating hallux valgus and correcting rotational deformity.

*Wagner et al review pronation in hallux valgus.* **They report**, based on CT scans, that 87% of patients presenting with hallux valgus have some degree of pronation deformity. **They conclude** that this pronation is the reason for the rounded lateral metatarsal head appearance that has previously been linked to the recurrence of deformity.

Illustration A demonstrates the rounded appearance of the lateral metatarsal head, which is indicative of pronation deformity.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1-3: Distal metatarsal osteotomies have the ability to correct the IMA, HVA and DMMA.
Answer 4: HVI of <10 degrees is considered normal.
