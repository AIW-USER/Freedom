## Question

McPherson rule for correction of extra articular deformity in Total Knee Replacement states that (NEW)

## Options

1. When coronal deformity is within the distal one fourth of the femur or proximal one fourth of tibia, and deformity is 20o or more, the recommended treatment is concomitant osteotomy and TKR.
2. The farther the extra articular coronal bone deformity to the knee joint, the greater mechanical mal alignment exists at the knee joint.
3. For any given magnitude, a deformity farther away from the knee, the greater is the intra articular bone cut needed to correct the mechanical alignment.
4. An extra articular deformity that lies within the distal one fourth of the femur or proximal one fourth of tibia, is easier to correct by making bone cuts at the knee joint

## Correct option

**1. When coronal deformity is within the distal one fourth of the femur or proximal one fourth of tibia, and deformity is 20o or more, the recommended treatment is concomitant osteotomy and TKR.**

## Answer explanation

Ans: A

TOTAL KNEE REPLACEMENT IN A PATIENT WITH CORONAL PLANE DEFORMITY IN FEMUR/ TIBIA

A general rule is, the closer the coronal plane extra articular deformity is to the knee joint, the greater is the mal-alignment that exists at the knee. Now, in case this extra articular deformity is within distal one fourth of femur or proximal one fourth of tibia (means it’s quite close to knee), mal-alignment is high and hence, the standard bone cuts of TKR will be inadequate to achieve correction. If still one would try, the end result will be too large bone resections that could compromise collateral attachments or too large ligament releases that could make ligaments incompetent. So, better in this scenario is to do osteotomy to lessen deformity to make TKR more comfortable.

Hence, McPherson rule states: -

When coronal plane deformity is within the distal one fourth of the femur or proximal one fourth of tibia, and deformity is 20o or more, the recommended treatment is concomitant osteotomy and TKR. The osteotomy should preferable be a closed wedge osteotomy and for TKR, surgeon should use a long diaphyseal fit stem. This would obviate the need for any supplementary fixation for osteotomy making it concomitant with the procedure of TKR
