## Options

1. Occult injuries are rare following trivial trauma
2. Spinal fractures most commonly involve the lumbar spine
3. Delay in diagnosis of spinal fractures is uncommon
4. Computed tomography of the entire spinal axis should be performed
5. There is a low rate of adverse events and mortality following spinal fractures

## Correct option

**4. Computed tomography of the entire spinal axis should be performed**

## Answer explanation

The patient is presenting with a cervical spinal fracture in the setting of ankylosing spondylitis (AS), which can often be missed with plain radiographs and can occur in multiple locations. Patients with AS who sustain spine trauma should undergo a computed tomography scan of the entire spinal axis.

Ankylosing spondylitis is an inflammatory spondyloarthropathy that leads to the formation of syndesmophytes of the annulus fibrosis. This results in a stiff spinal column that is prone to catastrophic fractures following low energy falls. Fractures often involve all three columns making the injury unstable and prone to neurologic compromise. Injuries are often missed since fractures can be subtle on initial radiographic evaluation. Regardless of the mechanism, the entire spinal column should undergo CT imaging as there are occult fractures in multiple locations in 13% of cases.

*Rusman et al. performed a review of the gender manifestations of AS.* **The authors reported** different levels in serum and tissue inflammatory markers between males and females, with females having a delay in diagnosis due to higher extra-articular manifestations. **They concluded** that women have a higher disease burden due to diagnostic delays and resistance to certain anti-rheumatic agents.

*Lukasiewicz et al. performed a retrospective study of patients with spinal fractures in the setting of AS using the National Inpatient Sample database.* **They reported** that of the 939 patients admitted for spinal fractures, 21% presented with a spinal cord injury, 13% presented with fractures in more than 1 region, 29% experienced an inpatient adverse event, and 6.6% expired during admission. **They concluded** that AS can complicate spinal fractures resulting in higher adverse events and mortality rates.

*Rustagi et al. conducted a systematic review of 21 studies that evaluated spinal fractures in the AS spine.* **They reported** that diagnosis delay occurred in up to 40% of patients, the cervical spine was the most common site of injury and a 1-year mortality rate of up to 32%. **They concluded** that patients with AS are at high risk for significant spine injuries, even after trivial trauma, and are prone to postoperative complications.

Figure A is a lateral radiograph of the cervical spine with AS. Figure B is an AP radiograph of the lumbar spine with the characteristic "bamboo" appearance and fusion of the sacroiliac joints. Figure C is a lateral radiograph of the thoracic spine with AS demonstrating increased thoracic and cervical kyphosis that is characteristic of advanced disease. Figure D is a sagittal CT image of the cervical spine with AS and a 3-column fracture at the C3-C4 level.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Spinal fractures can commonly occur following trivial trauma.
Answer 2: The cervical spine is the most commonly fractured segment in patients with AS, occurring in >50% of cases.
Answer 3: Diagnostic delay can occur in up to 40% of cases due to the low-energy mechanism in many cases and the subtle injury pattern.
Answer 5: Adverse events are common following spinal fractures with mortality rates as high as 32% in some studies.
