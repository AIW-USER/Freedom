## Question

A 32-year-old male presents to the emergency department with severe lower back pain, without radiation into bilateral lower extremities. On exam, he is neurologically intact. Current imaging is shown in figure A. It is determined that the posterior ligamentous complex is intact. What would be the most likely mechanism of injury for this fracture pattern?

![[q215089-a-burst.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Extension distraction
2. Flexion distraction
3. Axial load
4. Torsional
5. Shear

## Correct option

**3. Axial load**

## Answer explanation

The patient has a thoracolumbar burst fracture with an intact posterior logamentous complex, which is most commonly due to an axial loading mechanism to the involved level.

Thoracolumbar burst fractures are typically the result of high-energy injuries, with an axial loading mechanism across the involved vertebrae. Most of these injuries present without neurologic deficits and with stable fracture patterns (i.e. intact posterior ligamentous complex). Surgical treatment is recommended if there are clear neurologic deficits, signs of instability, or a Thoracolumbar Injury Classification and Severity Score (TLICS) >4 points. Nonsurgical treatment has been associated with lower complications and equivalent outcomes in neurologically intact patients.

*Kato et al. reviewed the current literature regarding the effect of surgical intervention and timing on neurologic recovery in thoracolumbar burst fractures.* **They recommended** surgical decompression in patients with neurologic deficits more than unilateral solitary radiculopathy, as nonsurgical treatment is associated with poor neurologic outcomes. Additionally, the cited literature did result in improved neurologic recovery with early surgical decompression in lumbar burst fractures. **The authors concluded** that surgical stabilization should occur as soon as the patient is medically stable.

*Urquhart et al. reported on the 5- and 10-year outcomes of a previously published randomized controlled trial, comparing thoracolumbosacral orthosis (TLSO) to no bracing for thoracolumbar burst fractures.* **The authors reported** no difference between bracing and no bracing with respect to the Roland Morris Disability Questionnaire (RMDQ), kyphosis, back pain, and treatment satisfaction in the original study that remained unchanged at 5- and 10-years in the current study. **The authors concluded** treating thoracolumbar burst fractures without bracing and early ambulation does not appear to have long-term consequences compared to TLSO bracing.

Figure A is the sagittal CT of the lumbar spine with an L2 burst fracture and no significant widening of the interspinous process space.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Extension-distraction injuries of the thoracolumbar spine are rare and are characterized by widening on the anterior column, typically through the disc space.
Answer 2: A flexion-distraction injury is a three-column injury to the spine. It is characterized by a center of rotation anterior to the spinal column with the distraction of the posterior elements and has a high association of viscus injuries.
Answers 4 and 5: Torsional and shearing injuries are characterized by a rotational or asymmetric injury pattern on imaging.
