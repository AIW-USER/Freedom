## Question

Figures A and B are the radiographs a 73-year-old female with recalcitrant knee pain that actively smokes. Her cardiopulmonary exam is normal and she is scheduled for total knee arthroplasty. What is true about the outcomes of this patient compared to a non-smoker?

![[q213955-a-screen-shot-2020-01-20-at-5-39-46-pm.jpg]]
*Figure A*

![[q213955-b-screen-shot-2020-01-20-at-5-39-37-pm.jpg]]
*Figure B*

## Options

1. Improved SF-36 physical function score
2. No difference in risk of surgical site infection
3. Higher risk of readmission
4. No difference in risk of manipulation under anesthesia
5. Improved pain relief

## Correct option

**3. Higher risk of readmission**

## Answer explanation

In patients undergoing outpatient total joint arthroplasty, smoking status is the most important risk factor for 30-day readmission and reoperation.

The adverse effects of smoking on total joint arthroplasty (TJA) outcomes are well known. Smoking status predicts major complications, including deep and superficial wound breakdown, reoperation, and implant loosening. Conversely, smoking cessation four to six weeks before and after TJA reduces complications by 50%. The outcomes improvement is attributed to improved blood oxygen-carrying capacity.

*Courtney et. al. reviewed the 30-day outcomes of 1220 patients who underwent outpatient total joint arthroplasty.* **They found** that advanced age, malnutrition, cardiac disease, and diabetes were risk factors for complications, readmission and reoperation. **They conclude** that smoking is the single greatest risk factor predicting 30-day readmission and reoperation in patients undergoing TJA.

*Sahota et al. reviewed the 30-day outcomes of 1251 smokers undergoing TJA.* **They found** that smoking status predicted higher 30-day readmission and surgical complications, including deep surgical site infection. **They conclude** that medical complications were similar between smokers and non-smokers.

*Issa et. al. reviewed the risk factors for manipulation under anesthesia following primary total knee arthroplasty.* The report that diabetes, hypercholesterolemia, pre-operative range of motion <100 degrees, arthroplasty for osteonecrosis, and smoking, were associated with the highest odds of post-operative manipulation. **They conclude** that knee stiffness after TKA is multifactorial.

Figures A and B are lateral and AP knee radiographs, respectively, that demonstrate advanced arthritis of the knee.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1 and 5: SF-36 and pain scores are not known to be improved in smokers.
Answers 2 and 4: Smokers are at increased risk of surgical site infection and manipulation under anesthesia following total knee arthroplasty.
