## Question

## Correct option

**1. Figure A**

## Answer explanation

Figure A shows an avulsion fracture of the proximal lateral tibia referred to as a Segond fracture. This is an anterolateral ligament (ALL) avulsion fracture and is highly correlated with having an underlying anterior cruciate ligament (ACL) disruption.

The first step in managing a sports-related knee injury is obtaining an appropriate history and physical examination. Pivoting knee injuries that result in acute effusion are concerning for ACL injury, whereas slow-growing effusions not appreciated initially may suggest meniscal injury. A knee exam is paramount in keying into potential underlying injuries. While MRI is the most appropriate imaging modality for evaluating ligamentous knee injuries, obtaining initial radiographs is prudent. There are various avulsion-type fractures that can help establish an early diagnosis and justify obtaining further imaging. In this case, the patient presents with a Segond fracture which is highly suggestive of underlying ACL disruption. Obtaining an MRI would definitively establish the diagnosis thereafter.

*Kushare and colleagues performed an institutional retrospective review of their patients diagnosed with a Segond fracture.* **The authors found** that commonly associated injuries included an underlying ACL tear (73.6%), meniscal injury (69.8%), or tibial spine fracture (17.0%). **The authors concluded** that an MRI should be obtained in all patients presenting with a Segond fracture to characterize the underlying intra-articular injuries.

*Slagstad and colleagues performed an institutional retrospective cohort analysis of patients with Segond fractures undergoing ACL reconstruction.* **They found** that there was no significant prognostic impact on rates of subsequent revision ACL reconstructive surgeries when compared to standard ACL reconstruction surgery patients without a Segond fracture. **The authors concluded** that while Segond fracture osseous healing rates are low, the presence of the fracture did not represent a higher risk of undergoing revision surgery.

*Musahl and colleagues provided a comprehensive review article on the anatomy of the anterolateral ligamentous complex (i.e. anterolateral ligament) of the knee and its functional role.* *They discussed the importance of the anterolateral ligamentous complex in resisting tibial internal rotation, citing an injury to this area as being a potential cause for a persistent positive pivot shift after ACL reconstruction.* **They conclude** that more research is needed in this area, however, lateral extra-articular tenodesis may have a role in both primary and revision ACL reconstruction surgery.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Figure B shows an avulsion of the medial femoral condyle that ossifies over time referred to as a Pellegrini-Stieda lesion. This is indicative of a chronic medial collateral ligament injury.
Answer 3: Figure C shows an osteochondral lesion of the posteromedial femoral condyle, which is the most common location for these lesions to occur within the knee.
Answer 4: Figure D shows an avulsion of the tip of the fibular head referred to as the Arcuate Sign. This is indicative of an underlying posterolateral corner injury.
Answer 5: Figure E shows widening of the lateral joint space, squaring of the condyle, and cupping of the lateral tibial plateau. These findings are indicative of an underlying discoid meniscus.
