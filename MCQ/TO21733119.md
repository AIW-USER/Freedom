Chapter: Sports Medicine MCQs — Age Estimation in Sports Men

## Question

You were asked by the sports authority for bone age estimation of a wrestler. You employed the Tanner Whitehouse method. The radiograph you would order for same

## Options

1. Left wrist
2. Right wrist
3. Right ankle
4. Pelvis

## Correct option

**1. Left wrist**

## Answer explanation

Ans: A

EXPLANATION: -

Bone age assessment methods such as the Greulich-Pyle and Tanner-Whitehouse involve left hand and wrist radiographs.
Reasons for using left hand and wrist radiographs for bone age assessment-
1. Right hand is more likely to be injured than the left hand as most people are right-handed.
2. Physical measurements should be performed on the left side as decided at the conferences of physical anthropologists in the early 1900s.

Although no absolutely accurate method is available for determining skeletal maturity as an adolescent progresses through puberty, the common method employed depend on assessment of bone age at the hand and wrist and development of the iliac apophysis (Risser sign), triradiate cartilage, olecranon apophysis ossification, PHV (peak height velocity) and digital ossification.
The Risser sign proceeds from grade 0, no ossification, to grade 4, in which all four quadrants of the apophysis have ossification. Risser grade 5 is when the apophysis has fused completely to the ilium when the patient is skeletally mature. The Risser sign may not be as useful for predicting maturity accurately because of variations in ossification pattern amongst individuals.

![[TO21733119-1.png]]

The PHV has been reported by several authors to be a better maturity indicator than the Risser sign, chronologic age, or menarchal age. PHV is calculated from serial height measurements and is expressed as centimeters of growth per year. Average values of PHV are 8 cm per year in girls and 9.5 cm per year in boys.
The Sauvegrain method determines skeletal age from left elbow radiographs. It is a 27-point system based on four anatomic structures about the elbow: lateral condyle, trochlea, olecranon apophysis, and the proximal radial epiphysis. Charles et al. reported a simple reliable method to assess maturity based on the olecranon apophysis and allowed skeletal age to be determined at regular 6-month intervals from the age of 11 to 13 years in girls and from 13 to 15 years in boys. They found that this information complemented the Risser grade 0 and triradiate cartilage closure information.

![[TO21733119-2.png]]

The Tanner-Whitehouse-III Radius-Ulna-Short Bones (RUS) score, based on the radiographic appearance of the epiphyses of the distal radius, ulna, and small bones of the hands, and the digital skeletal age maturity scoring system, based on the metacarpals and phalanges, highly correlate with PHV and curve progression. However, these systems are cumbersome.
Sanders reported a classification based on the epiphyses of the phalanx, metacarpal, and distal radius. This method reliably predicted maturity and probability of progression to scoliosis surgery.
