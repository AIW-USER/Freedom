Chapter: Arthroplasty MCQs — Total Shoulder Arthroplasty

## Question

True about total shoulder arthroplasty are all except

## Options

1. Most common instability pattern in TSA is pull off of rotator cuff
2. Humeral stem is positioned in 25-45 degree of retroversion
3. Convex back side of glenoid is better than flat back side
4. Peg design of glenoid is better than keel design

## Correct option

**1. Most common instability pattern in TSA is pull off of rotator cuff**

## Answer explanation

Explanation:

Instability after TSA would mostly result from pull off of Subscapularis repair. However, the word Rotator cuff is more or so synonymous with Supraspinatus. So, choice A is best pick. All other statements are fully correct.

Normal glenoid and humeral stem version and inclination:

Normal glenoid version in most studies has been reported close to 0°, sometimes with slight anteversion but more often slight retroversion with values typically less than 10° in either direction. Normally glenoid is superiorly inclined by average 4 degrees (ranges between 7 inferior-15.8 superior inclination)

In shoulder arthroplasty, glenoid position should recreate the neutral version (normally implanted in neutral to slight anteversion and neutral inclination). Glenoid retroversion should be avoided.

Humeral stem is positioned in retroversion. 25 to 45 degrees retroversion is accepted range.

Glenoid implanted in a superiorly or inferiorly inclined position have the highest probability of failure, and retroverted glenoid are more susceptible to loosening than anteverted components.

Shoulder instability after TSA and RTSA

- Pull-off of subscapularis and anterior capsule results in anterior shoulder instability. This is most common instability pattern after TSA
- Dislocation of a reverse TSA most commonly is anterior. Mechanism of dislocation is shoulder hyperextension and external rotation. In this position, the humeral socket levers posteriorly and dislocates

Indications for TSA

- pain and inability to perform activities of daily living due to gleno-humeral arthritis
- Preferred over hemiarthroplasty for osteoarthritis and inflammatory arthritis

Contraindications: irreparable rotator cuff tear, glenoid bone loss, deltoid dysfunction, infection and brachial plexus palsy

Technical considerations for TSA:

- Glenoid component
- Peg design is better than keel design
- Convex backside of glenoid is better than flat back
- Polyethylene-backed components superior to metal-backed components
- Uncemented glenoid is better than cemented (low rates of loosening) Humeral stem fixation
- Position of humeral stem should be 25-45° of retroversion Avoid valgus positioning of humeral stem
- Avoid overstuffing the humeral head

Ans: A
