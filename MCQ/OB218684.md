## Question

A 14-year-old male basketball player has entered his peak growth phase, growing an additional four inches in the last nine months. He just finished playing a travel basketball season and has been working with an agility coach daily in preparation for his upcoming high school basketball tryouts. Over the last two weeks, he has noted increasing pain, erythema, and swelling over his heel that gets worse when running drills. On exam, he has tenderness with a squeeze test over the affected area and has difficulty performing a single-leg heel raise when compared to the unaffected extremity. A radiograph of his symptomatic extremity is shown in Figure A. What is the most likely diagnosis?

*Figure A*

## Options

1. Achilles tendinopathy
2. Haglund's deformity
3. Heel Fat Pad Syndrome
4. Retrocalcaneal bursitis
5. Sever's Disease

## Correct option

**5. Sever's Disease**

## Answer explanation

The patient is exhibiting signs, symptoms, and classic radiographic findings consistent with Sever's disease.

Sever's disease is a common idiopathic condition caused by an overuse injury to the calcaneal apophysis in a growing child. It is most typically seen in skeletally immature running/jumping athletes who have just entered their peak growth phase. Patients most often present with posterior heel pain that may or may not be accompanied by erythema, swelling, and warmth. The exam usually reveals a positive "squeeze test" with pain on medial-lateral compression over the calcaneal tuberosity, as well as difficulty with a single-leg heel raise and pain exacerbation on the stretch of the triceps surae. Radiographs frequently show sclerosis and fragmentation of the calcaneal apophysis (Illustration A).

*Rachel et al. provide a review of the necessity of radiographic evaluation for children with a clinical diagnosis of calcaneal apophysitis (Sever's Disease).* *The clinical records and radiographs of 98 children between the ages of 4 and 17 years who presented with a chief complaint of heel pain were retrospectively reviewed, and* **the authors found** that the rate of abnormal radiographic findings in the 96 patients was 5.1%. **They concluded** that despite concern about exposure to ionizing radiation and the cost of medical imaging, routine lateral radiographs appear to be justified for screening of pediatric patients with heel pain, given that a lesion requiring more aggressive treatment could be missed.

*Ramponi et al. provide a review of Sever's disease,* **noting** that the primary risk factors in pediatric athletes are obesity and high levels of physical activity, with the most commonly involved sports being soccer, track, cross-country, gymnastics, tennis, and ballet. Clinical diagnosis can be confirmed by performing a “squeeze test” of the heel on physical examination, while diagnostic imaging findings often include increased sclerosis and fragmentation of the calcaneal apophysis on plain radiographs. **The authors conclude** that ice, activity restriction, stretching, nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, immobilization, and heel cups are all methods that can be utilized in treating the pain caused by Sever’s.

Figure A is a lateral radiograph of the foot in a skeletally immature individual showing the classic stigmata of Sever's disease. Illustration A is the same radiograph with the sclerotic, segmented calcaneal apophysis marked with a red arrow.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Achilles tendinopathy can coexist with Sever's disease but would not present in the same fashion in isolation or exhibit the radiographic findings of Sever's disease shown in Figure A.
Answer 2: A Haglund's deformity represents a symptomatic osseous prominence of the posterolateral corner of the calcaneus resulting in posterior heel pain and swelling around the insertion of the Achilles Tendon. It is most commonly seen in females aged 15-35 who wear high heels.
Answer 3: Heel Fat Pad Syndrome (HFPS), or fat pad atrophy, is a condition that happens as a result of changes in the elasticity and/or the thickness of the heel fat pad that is often misdiagnosed as plantar fasciitis.
Answer 4: Retrocalcaneal bursitis represents inflammation of the bursa located between the calcaneus and the anterior surface of the Achilles tendon and does not exhibit the classic radiographic findings of Sever's disease shown in Figure A.
