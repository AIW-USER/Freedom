## Question

A 20-year-old male presents after slamming his right index finger into a car door earlier today. On exam of his index finger, the proximal interphalangeal (PIP) joint appears dislocated dorsally. Radiographs are obtained for further evaluation, which are shown in one of the figures included. You decide to perform a digital block and closed reduction. However, you quickly realize it is unstable and difficult to reduce due to persistent dorsal PIP joint subluxation. Based on the radiographs included, which of these injuries closely resembles this case and would warrant a hemi-hamate arthroplasty?

## Answer explanation

With this patient's mechanism of injury and the inability to reduce the proximal interphalangeal (PIP) joint, a PIP joint fracture dislocation with dorsal subluxation correlates most closely (answer choice C). This particularly unstable fracture pattern warrants a hemi-hamate arthroplasty to restore the joint surface adequately while providing joint stability.

Proximal interphalangeal joint fracture dislocations typically result from hyperextension and axial compression injuries. With hyperextension of the PIP joint, the extensor and flexor tendons surrounding the joint are placed on stretch, and the volar plate is avulsed off the volar lip of the middle phalanx. In high-energy situations, larger volar lip fragments can result due to the additional axial load. They can be difficult conditions to treat and are commonly broken down by percentage of stability to help determine treatment strategies. Those with <30% of articular surface involvement are considered stable and can be treated with closed reduction alone in most cases. Those with 30-50% involvement are situation-dependent and may require surgical treatment depending on stability following closed reduction. Lastly, those with >50% articular involvement are unstable and require surgical treatment in most cases.

*Kibar et al. investigated the outcomes following hemi-hamate autograft arthroplasty (HHA) in acute palmar lip middle phalanx injuries with persistent PIP joint dorsal subluxation.* Thirteen patients were included, and all demonstrated stable PIP joints at 1 year postoperatively. Range of motion was similar to the contralateral side at the final follow up and grip strength was, on average, 93% of the uninjured side. **They conclude** that HHA is an appropriate treatment option for acute dorsal PIP subluxation following palmar lip injuries with an intact middle phalanx dorsal cortex.

*Frueh et al. wrote a systematic review analyzing hemi-hamate autograft arthroplasty in PIP joint reconstruction.* *Thirteen articles with 71 cases were included in the review with a mean follow-up of 36 months.* The overall mean PIP joint range of motion was 77 degrees, with a mean complication rate of 35%. Approximately 50% of patients demonstrated PIP joint osteoarthritis (OA) at final follow-up, however, few complaints regarding pain or restricted motion were recorded. **They note** contraindications to HHA include fractures involving the dorsal cortex of the middle phalanx, proximal phalangeal head fractures, and preoperative PIP joint OA. **They concluded** that HHA is a viable treatment option for acute and chronic PIP joint fracture-dislocations with joint involvement >50%.

*Calfee et al. sought to determine if hemi-hamate resurfacing arthroplasty was a successful treatment option for PIP joint fracture-dislocations.* Thirty-three patients were included in this study, with >50% of the middle phalanx palmar articular surface involved. The average active PIP joint motion was 70 degrees, and the mean active DIP joint motion was 54 degrees. Grip strength was measured to be around 95% of the healthy side, and one revision was performed. **They concluded** that HHA is a valuable and reliable procedure in acute and chronic PIP joint fracture-dislocations.

Incorrect answer choices:
Answer choice 1: This radiograph depicts a pilon fracture involving the base of the middle phalanx. This is a highly unstable injury in all directions and would not benefit from a HHA given the dorsal cortex is not intact.
Answer choice 2: This radiograph depicts a palmar lip injury with less than 50% of articular involvement, and most commonly is stable following closed reduction alone.
Answer choice 4: This radiograph demonstrates a simple PIP joint dislocation without a fracture present. This would likely be stable following closed reduction alone.
Answer choice 5: This radiograph shows a dorsal lip fracture involving the middle phalanx. This would not benefit from an HHA, given that the dorsal cortex is injured.
