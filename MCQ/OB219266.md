## Question

A 57-year-old female is water-skiing during a summer vacation and catches her ski awkwardly, causing her to fall. She has immediate pain in her right buttocks region. She is unable to ambulate after getting out of the water and develops significant ecchymosis that evening in the proximal posterior thigh. On exam, she is neurovascularly intact but does have weakness in resisted knee flexion. Radiographs demonstrate a bony avulsion of the ischial tuberosity. You recommend prompt surgical repair. Which of the following orientations correctly represents the origin of the respective hamstring tendons at the ischial tuberosity?

*Figure A*

## Options

1. 1 = semimembranosus, 2 = semitendinosus, 3 = biceps femoris
2. 1 = semitendinosus, 2 = biceps femoris, 3 = semimembranosus
3. 1 = semitendinosus, 2 = semimembranosus, 3 = biceps femoris
4. 1 = biceps femoris, 2 = semitendinosus, 3 = semimembranosus
5. 1 = biceps femoris, 2 = semimembranosus, 3 = semitendinosus

## Correct option

**4. 1 = biceps femoris, 2 = semitendinosus, 3 = semimembranosus**

## Answer explanation

The semimembranosus tendon attaches most lateral at the ischial tuberosity, with the biceps attaching more proximal than the semitendinosus along the medial footprint.

Hamstring injuries are becoming increasingly recognized and those involving the proximal hamstring footprint are more frequently being surgically repaired because of better strength and functional outcomes postoperatively. The footprint at the ischial tuberosity is made up of 3 tendons including the long head of the biceps femoris, semitendinosus, and the semimembranosus. The biceps and semitendinosus make up the conjoint tendon and attach more medially than the semimembranosus which attaches more laterally and slightly proximal. The sciatic nerve lies just deep and lateral to the ischial tuberosity at the site of fixation. A thorough understanding of these relationships are important for ensuring fixation of the tendons is performed in the most anatomic fashion.

*Sonnery-Cottet et al. published a case report of a proximal hamstring avulsion in a professional soccer player, which* **they note** is rare in an elite athlete. **They recommend** prompt surgical repair in this population to allow them to return to sports at a high level. In this particular case, successful repair was confirmed on MRI at 5 months post-op and the patient was allowed to return to sport at 6 months.

*Pombo et al. reviewed technical notes on the surgical repair of proximal hamstring injuries which they cite are becoming more common due to better outcomes with repair.* *They go over their technique, which involves protecting the sciatic nerve and placing 5 suture anchors in the ischial tuberosity footprint which allows for an 'X' shaped repair configuration.* *They flex the knee to 30-60° to take tension off the repair before tying it down and place the patient in a postoperative hip-knee orthosis to limit hip flexion to 15° but do not limit knee motion.*

Figure A demonstrates the proximal hamstring footprint at the ischial tuberosity and is subsequently labeled in Illustration A as follows: (1) biceps femoris, (2) semitendinosus, (3) semimembranosus.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1,2,3,5: The biceps femoris and semitendinosus (conjoint) attach more medially to the semimembranosus, with the biceps attaching more proximal than the semitendinosus.
