## Question

A 64-year-old male laborer with primary left elbow osteoarthritis is indicated for a lateral column procedure after failing nonoperative means, which included physical therapy and dynamic splinting. His preoperative elbow range of motion is 30 to 90 degrees of flexion with pain and crepitus. Pronation and supination are to 70 degrees in each direction and without crepitus. Extensive anterior and posterior capsular releases are performed intraoperatively, in addition to diffuse cheilectomy. Which of the following procedures should be included in the operation?

## Options

1. Release of the anterior band of the medial ulnar collateral ligament
2. Release of the lateral ulnar collateral ligament
3. Radial head resection
4. Ulnar nerve decompression with possible transposition
5. Tensor fascia lata interposition

## Correct option

**4. Ulnar nerve decompression with possible transposition**

## Answer explanation

This 64-year-old male exhibits primary elbow osteoarthritis recalcitrant to nonoperative means. Because of his significant limitation in range of motion, concomitant ulnar nerve decompression with possible transposition is indicated in addition to his lateral column procedure (Answer 4).

Elbow arthritis is an uncommon entity, but can stem from a myriad of conditions. The condition can be broken down into three categories: primary, secondary (primarily post-traumatic), and inflammatory. Nonetheless, all three conditions can create significant stiffness, leading to considerable reductions in quality of life. Elderly, active patients with severe osteoarthritis can be challenging to treat, given the limited functionality total elbow arthroplasty allows for following the procedure. As such, motion-restoring surgeries like the lateral/medial column procedure, olecranon fossa debridement (Outerbridge-Kashiwagi), and interposition arthroplasties are performed with the goal of restoring motion and delaying the need for replacement. Regardless of the procedure performed, those with significant limitations in elbow range of motion (extensor lag of > 60 degrees, elbow flexion limited to < 100 degrees) should undergo concomitant ulnar nerve decompression with possible transposition to prevent postoperative ulnar nerve dysfunction.

Strubel and Cohen examine the indications, contraindications, workup, and treatment technique for open surgical release in elbow contractures. The authors note medial and/or lateral approaches can be utilized in order to release the capsule. However, they note the lateral approach is most commonly performed, as it can provide access to all three joints of the elbow. Nonetheless, the authors detail both medial and lateral approaches. The authors conclude that significant increases in range of motion can be seen following open release, with elderly patients more reliably experiencing restoration of motion than those under 20 years of age.

*Bionna et al. report on delayed-onset ulnar neuritis following the release of elbow contractures.* *The authors retrospectively reviewed 565 patients who underwent arthroscopic elbow contracture release,* **noting** an 11% ulnar neuritis rate, most of which fell into the category of rapidly progressive (58%) sensorimotor ulnar neuropathy, all of which were treated with urgent cubital tunnel decompression. **The authors recommend** having a high index of suspicion in patients with progressive loss of motion and end-range of motion pain as evidence for subclinical ulnar neuritis.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: releasing the anterior band of the medial ulnar collateral ligament is not recommended, as the structure is the primary stabilizer to valgus stress
Answer 2: releasing the lateral collateral ligament would not be recommended, as the ligament is the primary stabilizer of varus stress
Answer 3: radial head resection is not indicated as the patient possesses a full, painless range of motion with pronation and supination
Answer 5: This procedure, while indicated in those with end-stage elbow osteoarthritis, is generally reserved for patients younger than 50 years of age
