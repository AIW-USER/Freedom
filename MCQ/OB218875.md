## Question

A 56-year-old female presents to your clinic approximately 24 months after undergoing an uncomplicated total hip arthroplasty. The surgery was done in another city, and the patient recently moved after her husband retired. Upon completion of your physical examination and history, you are concerned that the patient is suffering from anterior iliopsoas impingement. Which of the following best describes the most likely physical exam finding and history elements gathered from this patient?

## Options

1. Groin pain worse during initiation of gait which began approximately 20 months after surgery
2. Pain which is constant and worse at night, associated with fevers and chills, which began approximately one week ago
3. Pain exacerbated by passive hip extension and resisted hip flexion which began approximately 20 months after surgery
4. Pain exacerbated by passive hip extension and resisted hip flexion which began 2 weeks after surgery
5. Thigh pain worse during initiation of gait which began 20 months after surgery

## Correct option

**3. Pain exacerbated by passive hip extension and resisted hip flexion which began approximately 20 months after surgery**

## Answer explanation

Iliopsoas impingement is a possible complication after total hip arthroplasty (THA). The average time from surgery to the onset of pain is 20 months, and patients typically describe pain made worse with passive hip extension and active hip flexion (Answer 3).

THA iliopsoas impingement is an under-recognized cause of recurrent groin pain after THA. While it is often caused by acetabular component malposition, it can also be caused by retained cement, limb length discrepancy and/or excessive screw length. Diagnosis is often made clinically, with patients reporting pain with resisted hip flexion and/or passive hip extension. The average time from index procedure to symptom onset is 20 months. Advanced imaging is helpful, and a CT scan will typically show an anterior cup overhang (Illustration A). A psoas tendon sheath injection can be helpful during workup, as it has the potential to be both diagnostic and therapeutic. Treatment is dependent on acetabular component position. In the setting of an appropriately placed component, the treatment is typically an arthroscopic psoas tenotomy. If the symptoms are secondary to component malposition component revision, however, may be necessary.

*Buller et al. performed a retrospective study to investigate the epidemiology, risk factors for, and treatments of psoas impingement following THA.* *Their study included 518 patients (559 hips), and 32/559 (5.7%) hips had experienced psoas impingement.* The average time from index procedure to symptom onset was 20 months. Nonoperative management was successful in 46.9% of patients and, of those patients indicated for surgery, 62.5% experienced symptom relief. Female gender, large acetabular components relative to head size, and any measurable overhang were identified as risk factors for the development of psoas impingement.

Chalmers. et al identified 49 patients at their high-volume institution with findings consistent with psoas impingement. Of those 49 patients, 21 underwent acetabular component revision, 8 underwent tenotomy and 20 underwent nonoperative management. At final follow-up, 50% of patients in the nonoperative group and 24% of patients in the operative group had persistent pain. In patients with > 8 mm of component prominence, an acetabular revision was far more successful than tenotomy. Overall, the authors conclude that nonoperative management only leads to symptom relief in 50% of patients and that the optimal surgery is dependent on the acetabular cup position.

Illustration A is an axial cut of a CT scan in a patient status-post total hip arthroplasty with anterior overhang.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: startup pain located in the groin is more consistent with aseptic loosening of the acetabular component.
Answer 2: constant pain, worse at night, associated with fevers and chills is more consistent with a prosthetic joint infection (PJI)
Answer 4: while pain with passive hip extension and resisted flexion described psoas impingement, the average time from surgery to symptom onset is far longer than 2 weeks
Answer 5: startup pain located in the thigh is more consistent with aseptic loosening of the femoral component.
