## Question

A 49-year-old male presents with several months of worsening left arm pain and weakness. He states that there is a great deal of pain over his lateral shoulder that sometimes radiates down his upper arm and neck with associated tingling. Figures A and B are the current MRI images. ## Options

1. C6-7 ACDF
2. 1-week course of oral methylprednisolone
3. Physical therapy
4. Obtain further history and physical examination of the patient
5. Right C7 selective nerve root injection

## Correct option

**4. Obtain further history and physical examination of the patient**

## Answer explanation

The patient is presenting with left shoulder symptoms, but with radiographic findings of C7 nerve root compression which would correspond to pain and tingling down the lateral aspect of the arm, down the dorsum of the forearm, and into the long finger. The patient's complaints do not correlate with the MRI findings and further history and physical examination should be performed to determine the etiology.

Cervical radiculopathy is the result of degenerative changes of the cervical spine that leads to irritation of the nerve roots innervating the upper extremities. Up to 19% of asymptomatic patients can have MRI findings consistent with cervical nerve root compression. Thus treatment must be tailored to clinical findings that corroborate with radiographic findings.

*Rhee et al. review cervical radiculopathy.* **They report** that acute cervical radiculopathy has a self-limited clinical course with up to 75% of patients experiencing spontaneous improvement. Nonsurgical treatment is the appropriate initial approach. When this fails or when there is a significant neurologic deficit, surgical decompression becomes appropriate. Surgical outcomes for pain relief range from 80-90%.

Figures A and B are the sagittal and axial T2 MRI images of the cervical spine that demonstrate a right-sided bulging disc at C6-7 that would potentially result in right-sides C7 radiculopathy. Illustration A is a table detailing the most common cervical radiculopathy patterns.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The patient has a history more consistent with a left rotator cuff tear rather than right C7 radiculopathy. Prior to prescribing a treatment regimen, further history and physical examination should be performed to determine the cause of the symptoms.
Answer 2: Oral methylprednisolone will likely result in the temporary relief of the patient's symptoms. However, it may not result in added diagnostic information in this case.
Answer 3: Physical therapy can be beneficial in patients with both cervical radiculopathy and shoulder pathology. However, prior to prescribing a treatment regimen, the causative pathology should be identified.
Answer 5: A selective nerve root injection can be useful in patients with ambiguous findings. In this cause, a thorough examination of the patient's left shoulder should be performed to determine if a diagnostic and therapeutic shoulder injection is needed.
