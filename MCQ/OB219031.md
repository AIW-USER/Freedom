## Question

A 73-year-old female presents to your office for a routine follow-up for a distal radius fracture managed non-operatively. She recently had a DEXA scan with a T-score of -3.0 and inquires about anti-sclerostin drug therapy. What is the mechanism of action for this class of medication?

## Options

1. induces osteoclast apoptosis
2. inhibits osteoclast through RANK-RANKL pathway
3. inhibits an antagonist of Wnt/Beta-Catenin pathway
4. inhibits osteoclast farnesyl pyrophosphate synthase enzyme
5. inhibits resorption by binding to estrogen receptors on osteoclast

## Correct option

**3. inhibits an antagonist of Wnt/Beta-Catenin pathway**

## Answer explanation

The mechanism of action of anti-sclerostin medications is by inhibiting sclerostin, an antagonist of the Wnt/Beta-Catenin pathway.

Osteoporosis is a decrease in bone mass that occurs most commonly in elderly women. There are various classes of medications for the treatment of osteoporosis including bisphosphonates, estrogen-agonists, calcitonin, PTH agonists, anti-sclerostin, and more. Sclerostin is produced by osteocytes and is an antagonist of the Wnt/Beta-Catenin pathway. The Wnt/Beta-Catenin pathway induces osteoblastogenesis when activated. Romosozumab, an anti-sclerostin medication, is a monoclonal antibody that binds to sclerostin, inhibiting its antagonistic effect on osteoblastogenesis and resulting in a net increase in osteoblast function.

*Palumbo et al reviewed the function of osteocytes.* *They are former osteoblasts that become entrapped in the matrix they produce.* *They help maintain bone and cellular matrix by functioning as a mechano-sensor, as well as through signaling molecules such as sclerotin.*

*Compton et al also review the function of osteocytes and the role of sclerostin.* Osteocytes produce sclerostin which is a negative regulator of bone mass. Normally, sclerostin acts on osteoblasts, and production is influenced by mechanical loading, inflammatory molecules (prostaglandin E2), and hormones (PTH and estrogen). The imbalance of sclerostin has been linked to osteoporosis-related fractures, failure of osseous integration, and metastatic cancer. An antibody against sclerostin has been shown to increase BMD in phase III clinical trials at the time of this article and has since been approved by FDA.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: This describes the function of non-nitrogen-containing bisphosphonates.
Answer 2: Denosumab inhibits osteoclast function by inhibiting RANKL from binding to RANK.
Answer 4: This describes the function of nitrogen-containing bisphosphonates.
Answer 5: This describes the function of raloxifene, an estrogen agonist.
