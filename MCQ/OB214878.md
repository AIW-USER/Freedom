## Question

A 16-year-old male presents to the emergency department one day after injuring his ankle during a high school football game. The patient sustained a large abrasion over his lateral ankle after being tackled on a turf field. He reports progressive redness, swelling, and purple spots on his leg that have advanced over the last several hours. In the emergency room, his temperature is 102.8° F, his heart rate is 120 bpm, and his systolic blood pressure is 98 mmHg. On physical exam, he is ill-appearing and diaphoretic. The leg is erythematous, edematous, and several purple bullae are present. Which of the following is true regarding this condition?

## Options

1. Urgent advanced imaging (CT/MRI) is indicated to evaluate extent of the infection
2. Polymicrobial infection is more common than monomicrobial infection
3. Staphylococcus Aureus is the most common etiologic organism
4. Mortality rate is higher in patients who undergo amputation during surgical management
5. Mortality rate is inversely related to patient age

## Correct option

**2. Polymicrobial infection is more common than monomicrobial infection**

## Answer explanation

This patient's clinical presentation is consistent with necrotizing fasciitis as evidenced by hemodynamic instability in the setting of a rapidly progressing soft tissue infection. This devastating, and often fatal condition, is most commonly caused by polymicrobial infection.

Necrotizing fasciitis is a rapidly progressive soft tissue infection that is characterized by pain, hemodynamic instability (tachycardia and hypotension), fevers, ascending erythema, edema, and/or hemorrhagic bullae. Traumatic skin abrasions, recent surgical intervention, and other causes of open skin lesions may lead to this severe condition. Obesity and immune suppression (e.g. diabetes and cancer) are risk factors that place patients at increased risk for necrotizing fasciitis. Polymicrobial infection with anaerobes (e.g. clostridia), non-group A strep (e.g. strep. viridians), and gram-negative rods (enterobacteriaceae) are the most common cause of this condition. However, monomicrobial infections are more characteristic of necrotizing fasciitis in young, healthy patients. Orthopedic management consists of resuscitation, immediate broad-spectrum IV antibiotics (e.g. vancomycin, clindamycin, and cefepime), and emergent surgical debridement.

*Ozalay et al. analyzed the clinical presentation and factors associated with mortality in a retrospective study of 22 patients with necrotizing fasciitis.* **They noted** that patients who are immunocompromised (e.g. chronic liver disease and diabetes) are at increased risk for developing necrotizing fasciitis. **They also found** that patients who are advanced in age are at increased risk for mortality whereas those who undergo amputation to treat the infection don't have an increased mortality rate compared to those whose limb is salvaged.

*McCarthy et al. reviewed the etiology, presentation, diagnosis, and treatment of necrotizing fasciitis.* **They found** that polymicrobial infection occurs in approximately 80% of all cases and treatment should include emergent surgical debridement with immediate broad spectrum intravenous antibiotics.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Although MRI and CT scans are useful adjuncts to demonstrate edema in the soft tissue, it does not provide the definitive diagnosis and will delay surgical debridement which should be performed emergently.
Answer 3: Polymicrobial infection is the most common etiology. In cases of monomicrobial infection, group A strep is the most common infectious organism.
Answer 4: Mortality rate is similar between patients who undergo amputation compared to those who undergo limb salvage.
Answer 5: The mortality rate is higher in older patients, not younger patients.
