## Question

An 7-year-old female presents to the clinic after being told she has scoliosis by her school nurse. She denies any family history of scoliosis, associated back pain, weakness, or numbness and tingling. After reviewing her imaging and performing a clinical exam, she was told that an MRI of the entire spine was necessary. Which of the following is present to make this recommendation?

## Options

1. Thoracic hypokyphosis
2. A type C lumbar curve
3. Symmetric abdominal reflexes
4. No family history of scoliosis
5. 25-degree right thoracic curve

## Correct option

**5. 25-degree right thoracic curve**

## Answer explanation

Juvenile onset scoliosis (JIS) has a higher risk of spinal column abnormalities which requires further evaluation with MRI imaging for curves >20 degrees.

JIS is defined as scoliosis that presents between the ages of 4 and 10. This can often occur with neuraxial abnormalities including syringomyelia, Arnold-Chiari syndrome, tethered cord, dysraphism, and spinal cord tumor. Neuraxial abnormalities can be suspected in patients with asymmetric abdominal reflexes, left-sided thoracic curves, and significant spinal deformity (>20 degrees before age 10).

*Zadeh et al. reported on absent superficial abdominal reflexes in 12 patients with scoliosis.* **They stated** 10 of the 12 patients were found to have syringomyelia and reflex abnormalities on the side of the convex deformity. **They recommended** further evaluation, including an MRI of the entire spinal axis, in patients with reflex abnormalities and scoliosis.

*Yngve evaluated reflex abnormalities in patients with idiopathic scoliosis.* **The author reported** asymmetric abdominal reflexes were not present in any normal study subject. **The author concluded** asymmetric abdominal reflexes may warrant further work-up of the spinal deformity.

Incorrect answers
Answer 1: Thoracic hypokyphosis is a common finding in idiopathic scoliosis and is not associated with any neurologic risks. However, hyperkyphosis may lead to myelopathy is severe cases.
Answer 2: A type C lumbar curve is not a concerning element for MRI investigation.
Answer 3: Symmetric abdominal reflexes are a normal finding. Asymmetric reflexes have a high correlation with a spinal cord defect such as syringomyelia.
Answer 4: The lack of family history of scoliosis is not a concerning element in the patient's history.
