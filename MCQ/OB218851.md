## Question

Two patients are being treated with an upcoming total knee arthroplasty (TKA). Patient A is a 62-year-old male with no significant PMH. Patient B is a 63-year-old male who has a cell-mediated immune response involving an IgM antibody against native IgG antibodies, the presence of anti-cyclic citrullinated peptide, and is being treated with a folate analogue medication. Which of the following is true regarding venous thromboembolism (VTE) prophylaxis in these patients?

## Options

1. Patients A&B have an equal risk of VTE and standard VTE prophylaxis is indicated for both patients
2. Patient A does not require VTE prophylaxis while Patient B requires standard VTE prophylaxis
3. Patient A requires use of a direct Factor Xa inhibitor while Patient B requires standard VTE prophylaxis
4. Patient A may be treated with standard VTE prophylaxis while Patient B does not require VTE prophylaxis
5. Patient A may be treated with standard VTE prophylaxis while Patient B requires use of a direct Factor Xa inhibitor

## Correct option

**1. Patients A&B have an equal risk of VTE and standard VTE prophylaxis is indicated for both patients**

## Answer explanation

Patient B has rheumatoid arthritis (RA). Studies have shown no increased risk for VTE in patients with RA.

Rheumatoid arthritis is a chronic systemic autoimmune disease caused by IgM cell-mediated immune response against soft tissues, cartilage, and bone. Patients present with insidious onset of morning joint stiffness, polyarthropathy, subcutaneous nodules, and joint arthritis. Diagnosis is made with a combination of physical examination, characteristic radiographs, and labs to evaluate for presence of RF and anti-CCP antibodies. For RA patients being treated with a TKA, studies have demonstrated that when age and comorbid factors are controlled, there is no increased risk for VTE in patients with RA. Therefore, standard VTE prophylaxis would be indicated in patients with either RA or OA.

*Niki et al. reviewed VTE risk after TKA compared between patients with RA and those with osteoarthritis (OA).* **They reported** that the incidence of DVT after TKA was equivalent in the two groups when the patients were matched for age and NSAID use. **They concluded** that these findings may allow us to reconsider a prophylactic regimen for VTE in patients with RA.

*Izumi et al. compared the incidence of VTE following TKA between patients with RA and those with OA.* **They reported** no differences in the incidence of VTE (symptomatic/asymptomatic DVT plus PE) or bleeding. **They concluded** that despite some differences in demographic data, patients with RA and OA have equivalent risks of VTE and bleeding following TKA.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 2-5: Standard VTE prophylaxis is appropriate for either Patient A or B as no increased risk for VTE has been demonstrated in patients with RA
