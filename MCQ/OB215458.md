## Question

A 12-year-old Welsh squash champion injured his right middle finger during a heated match with his arch-rival. He felt immediate pain, swelling, and inability to move the distal interphalangeal joint (DIPJ). In a reprieve from the action and in a moment of compassion, his arch-rival attempted several unsuccessful reduction attempts. Radiographs of the finger are shown in figures A and B. Which of the following correctly describes the anatomical cause for the Welshman's current finger deformity?

## Options

1. Rupture of central slip
2. Rupture of one collateral ligament, with the other remaining intact allowing a condyle to buttonhole between the central slip and the lateral band
3. Rupture of one collateral ligament, with the other remaining intact
4. Metacarpal head becomes entrapped by the displaced natatory ligaments distally and the transverse metacarpal ligament proximally
5. Entrapped volar plate

## Correct option

**5. Entrapped volar plate**

## Answer explanation

A dorsal DIPJ dislocation can cause entrapment of the volar plate. This can prevent closed reduction attempts.

The DIPJ is stabilized by proper and accessory collateral ligaments, the latter of which insert directly onto the volar plate. These ligaments act as a restraint against radial and ulnar deviation. The volar plate inserts on the base of the distal phalanx and acts as a restraint against hyperextension. Dorsal DIPJ dislocations are far more common than volar and can be reduced by a combination of longitudinal traction, direct pressure on the dorsal aspect of the distal phalanx, and DIPJ flexion. If closed reduction cannot be achieved, open reduction with the release of the interposed volar plate is necessary.

*Banerji et al report a case of an irreducible dorsal DIPJ dislocation caused by an interposed FDP, which required an open release.* **The authors highlight** that the most common block to reduction is the volar plate.

*Chen et al report on 30 cases of DIPJ dislocations.* Dorsal dislocations frequently have interposed volar plate while volar DIPJ dislocations are far rarer but maybe more often associated with avulsion of the dorsal lip and terminal tendon.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: This describes a simple volar proximal interphalangeal joint (PIPJ) dislocation.
Answer 2: This describes a rotatory PIPJ dislocation.
Answer 3: This describes a lateral PIPJ dislocation.
Answer 4: This describes a metacarpal phalangeal joint dislocation.
