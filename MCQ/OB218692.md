## Question

A 13-year-old male baseball pitcher presents to your office for initial evaluation of ongoing lateral elbow pain in his throwing arm for the past 3 weeks. An MRI of his elbow is obtained and demonstrates intact articular cartilage of the capitellum with signal changes of the subchondral bone. Which of the following is the most appropriate initial treatment for this patient's most likely underlying condition?

## Options

1. Cessation of activity for 6 weeks
2. Arthroscopic microfracture
3. Arthroscopic debridement and loose body excision
4. Fixation of lesion
5. Osteochondral autograft or allograft transplantation surgery (OATS)

## Correct option

**1. Cessation of activity for 6 weeks**

## Answer explanation

This patient's history and MRI findings are most consistent with a osteochondritis dissecans (OCD) type I lesion of the capitellum. Initial treatment involves cessation of activity for 3-6 weeks followed by slow progression back to activities over next 6-12 weeks.

OCD of the elbow is a localized injury and subsequent separation of articular cartilage and subchondral bone of the capitellum. It is most commonly seen in gymnasts and pitchers. The diagnosis may be made with plane radiographs, but MRI studies can be helpful to evaluate for the size of lesion and extent of bony edema. Treatment can be nonoperative or operative depending on size of the lesion, stability of the lesion, and presence of loose bodies.

*Camp et al. reviewed capitellar OCD in adolescent athletes.* **They reported** that it typically occurs in gymnasts and overhead throwers and presents along a wide spectrum of severity. **They noted** that stable lesions can typically be treated with conservative therapy; however, those presenting with instability, fragmentation, or loose bodies generally require surgical intervention. **They concluded** that while there are a number of described surgical options used to treat capitellar OCD lesions, microfracture is one of the most commonly performed and well-studied.

*Takahara et al. reviewed the long-term outcomes of OCD lesions involving the capitellum.* **They reported** that 50% of patients who were treated conservatively and 46% of patients who were treated by surgical removal of the fragment were found to have residual elbow symptoms associated with daily living activities (poor outcome). **They concluded** that these long-term results suggest that the residual elbow symptoms associated with daily living activities in approximately 50% of patients may be associated with advanced lesions, osteoarthritis of the elbow, and a large osteochondral defect.

*Bae et al. reviewed the use of osteochondral autologous transplantation surgery (OATS) for OCD lesions of the adolescent capitellum.* **They reported** that 93% of patients had complete graft incorporation on follow-up MRI They concluded that a single-plug OATS is safe and effective in improving pain and elbow function in adolescents with unstable OCD, with high return to sports rates and little donor-site morbidity.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Arthroscopic microfracture or drilling of the capitellum is indicated in unstable type I lesions and stable type II lesions.
Answer 3: Arthroscopic debridement and loose body excision is indicated in unstable type II lesion and type III lesions.
Answer 4: Fixation of the lesion is indicated in large lesions that are incompletely displaced.
Answer 5: Osteochondral autograft or allograft transplantation surgery (OATS) is indicated in large type II and III capitellar lesions which engage the radial head.
