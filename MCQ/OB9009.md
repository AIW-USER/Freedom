## Question

Expectant parents are in your office for consultation. An ultrasound was performed which revealed a lower extremity deformity, seen in Figure A. Which of the following statements is true regarding this condition?

![[q9009-a-clubfoot.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. This condition has no genetic predisposition
2. With treatment the child will most likely be able to walk and run
3. A majority of cases are unilateral
4. The prevalence of the condition is equal in males and females
5. The child will most likely need to wear a prosthesis until the completion of puberty.

## Correct option

**2. With treatment the child will most likely be able to walk and run**

## Answer explanation

This child will be born with clubfoot (congenital talipes equinovarus). With treatment, most children with clubfoot will be able to walk and run.

Clubfoot is the most common musculoskeletal deformity in children. It is more common in males and half of the cases are bilateral. A genetic etiology is strongly suggested in some patients. There is a characteristic deformity of midfoot cavus, forefoot adductus, hindfoot varus, and hindfoot equinus. It can be diagnosed prenatally with ultrasound; however, this may have a high false positive rate. It can be diagnosed as early as 12 weeks of gestational age. Treatment is usually performed with the Ponseti method (serial manipulation and casting).

*Lohle-Akkersdijk et al. performed a cross-sectional study and* **found** that patients with clubfoot were able to walk. However, their mean walking capacity was decreased to 79%. This was not influenced by whether the deformity was unilateral or bilateral.

*Kenmoku et al. objectively assessed the athletic ability of school-age congenital clubfoot patients.* Athletic ability was evaluated by calculating Z-scores for the patients' scores in 5 physical fitness tests routinely performed nationwide at elementary schools: 50-meter run, standing long jump, repetition side steps, 20-meter shuttle run, and sit-ups **They concluded** that congenital clubfoot with satisfactory treatment did not significantly impair the athletic performance of children.

Figure A is an ultrasound in-utero demonstrating a clubfoot.
