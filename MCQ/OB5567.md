## Answer explanation

This patient has a displaced extension-type pediatric supracondylar fracture (Gartland III). Fixation 3 laterally placed K-wires is recommended.

Closed reduction and percutaneous pinning is the mainstay of treatment for displaced pediatric supracondylar fractures (Gartland II and III). Lateral pinning with 2-3 wires is recommended (2 wires for Garland II, and 3 wires for Gartland III). Three-pin constructs are biomechanically stronger than 2-pin constructs in bending and torsion and are indicated when there is comminution. With crossed pins, the medial pin increases the risk of iatrogenic ulnar nerve injury and this makes lateral pins the construct of choice.

*Howard et al. provided recommendations in the AAOS Clinical Practice Guideline on pediatric supracondylar fractures.* **They recommend** lateral pinning with 2-3 wires is recommended and avoidance of medial pins, and immobilization alone for Type I fractures. **They recommend** emergent closed reduction and percutaneous pinning (CRPP) for decreased perfusion and open exploration for absent pulses and underperfused hand after CRPP, and open reduction for varus malposition when closed reduction fails. If the medial pin is necessary, open placement is recommended.

Figure A shows a Gartland III extension type supracondylar fracture. Figure B shows crossed pins. Figures C and D show 2 lateral and 2 medial pins respectively. Figure E and F show 2 medial/1 lateral and 2 lateral/1 medial pin respectively.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1, 3, 4, 5: Medial pins are less desirable because of the risk of iatrogenic injury to the ulnar nerve.
