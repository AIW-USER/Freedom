## Question

A 65-year-old male presents to the hand clinic with severe basilar thumb arthritis that has failed conservative management, including medication, bracing, and an injection. You decide to schedule him for a thumb carpometacarpal (CMC) suspension arthroplasty via a Wagner approach. Before making your incision, you explain the radial artery's anatomy and its course through the wrist and hand to the medical student. At which point during this procedure via the Wagner approach is the radial artery at the lowest risk of injury?

## Options

1. Dissection of the dorsoradial capsule proximal to the trapezium
2. Retraction of the abductor pollicis longus tendon dorsally
3. Retraction of the flexor carpi radialis (FCR) tendon with small homan retractors just proximal to the trapezium prior to removal
4. Dissection at the level of the 1st metacarpal base
5. The radial artery is not at risk during this approach

## Correct option

**4. Dissection at the level of the 1st metacarpal base**

## Answer explanation

This patient is undergoing a thumb CMC suspension arthroplasty to treat his basilar thumb arthritis. When utilizing the Wagner approach, the radial artery is most at risk during dissection of the dorsoradial capsule proximal to the trapeziometacarpal (TMC) joint and less at risk (more ulnar) at the level of the TMC joint, most specifically the 1st metacarpal base (Answer 4).

Basilar thumb arthritis, also known as TMC joint arthritis or 1st CMC joint arthritis, is a common hand condition. It is more common in women and seen in 25% of men and 40% of women >75 years old. Risk factors include female gender, increasing age, and ligamentous laxity. It presents as chronic pain in the base of the thumb with radiographic evidence of osteoarthritis in the TMC joint. Treatment involves conservative management initially with NSAIDs, thumb spica bracing, and corticosteroid injections. Surgical treatment depends on the stage of the disease, with trapeziectomy +/- ligament reconstruction being the most commonly performed procedure. Different surgical approaches can be utilized with this procedure. The at-risk neurovascular structures include the superficial sensory branches of the radial nerve and the radial artery.

*Pickrell et al. reviewed basilar thumb arthritis and* **noted** it most frequently affects the elderly and postmenopausal women. Initial conservative treatment is first utilized with activity modification, orthoses, and corticosteroid injections. **They conclude** that surgical treatment is pursued in those with persistent symptoms and depends on the patient's goals and functional demands, the surgeon's experience, and the patient's preference.

*Cohen-Shohet et al. performed a technique review on trapeziectomy with hematoma arthroplasty in advanced CMC joint arthroplasty.* **They note** this technique involves removing the trapezium and allowing a hematoma to fill the void. This technique is technically simple, inexpensive, and provides less morbidity to the patient. **They conclude** multiple high-quality studies have demonstrated similar outcomes when compared to ligament reconstruction and tendon interposition techniques.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: Dissection of the dorsoradial capsule proximal to the trapezium is when the radial artery is at most risk of injury.
Answer 2: The radial artery, along with its dorsal carpal branch, is located adjacent to the APL tendon on the dorsal aspect of the wrist, especially proximal to the level of the trapezium.
Answer 3: The superficial branch of the radial artery courses over the FCR tendon proximal to the trapezium.
Answer 5: The radial artery is at risk during this procedure and approach as described above.
