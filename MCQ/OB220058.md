## Question

Figure A displays the preoperative radiographs of a patient undergoing total knee arthroplasty after failing conservative measures. Intraoperatively, the surgeon unintentionally lacerates the medial collateral ligament (MCL) during tibial plateau resection. They opt to utilize a nonhinged constrained implant to account for this and place the patient in a hinged knee brace postoperatively. Following surgery, what is the most appropriate next step for the surgeon to take in discussing the operation with the patient?

![[q220058-a-screen-shot-2025-01-13-at-12-35-33-am.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Inform them that their native MCL was found to be grossly incompetent, thus adjustment to the initial surgical plan was necessary
2. The surgeon may defer disclosing this intraoperative complication
3. Inform them that their previous knee surgery necessitated the change in implant choice
4. Inform them that their MCL was injured intraoperatively, thus the surgical plan was adjusted to provide for a stable knee postoperatively
5. Explain that the knee brace is a comfort measure that some patients prefer postoperatively

## Correct option

**4. Inform them that their MCL was injured intraoperatively, thus the surgical plan was adjusted to provide for a stable knee postoperatively**

## Answer explanation

Intraoperative complications are an inevitable facet of surgical practice. In cases where a complication occurs secondary to surgeon error, surgeons have a moral and ethical responsibility to disclose this honestly to their patients (Answer 4).

Surgical complications are an unavoidable reality in surgery, presenting complex challenges that often extend beyond the operating room. These complications have significant impacts on patients, their families, and the surgeons themselves, who frequently experience substantial emotional distress. Effectively addressing these situations requires a multifaceted approach that includes prompt and transparent communication with patients, a robust system for documenting and reporting adverse events, and available avenues for psychological support for surgeons. Navigating the aftermath of surgical complications necessitates a careful balance of medical, ethical, and legal considerations, ultimately aiming to improve patient safety, support healthcare providers, and foster a culture of learning and continuous improvement within healthcare institutions.

*Cardenas reviewed the field of surgical ethics, outlining the multifaceted interplay between the technical capabilities, knowledge, and judgment of the surgeon.* *They discussed multiple key issues within surgical ethics, including informed consent, the surgeon-patient relationship, and the moral implications of surgical innovation and decision-making.* **They concluded** that a strong ethical framework, based on the commonly accepted principles of autonomy, beneficence, non-maleficence, and justice, is crucial for guiding surgeons in navigating complex cases and clinical scenarios.

*Adedeji et al. reviewed the ethical considerations surrounding surgical complications, defining a surgical complication as any undesirable, unintended, and direct result of surgery affecting the patient that would not have occurred had the surgery gone as well as could reasonably be hoped.* **They found** that while surgeons might be tempted to downplay or disguise complications, ethical principles, such as respect for autonomy and justice, require honest disclosure of errors and complications to the patient, as this information is rightfully theirs. **They concluded** that full disclosure, even when challenging, is crucial for maintaining patient trust, fulfilling the surgeon's moral obligations, and allowing for proper legal recourse, such as compensation, should the complication be due to an error.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer choices 1, 2, 3, and 5: These responses all represent dishonest communication with the patient, and would not be considered appropriate ethical practice following the commitment of a surgical error.
