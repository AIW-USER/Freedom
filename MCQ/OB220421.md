## Question

A 60-year-old male presents to your office with significant pain in the base of his right thumb. He admits to falling onto his outstretched right hand yesterday while playing basketball with his grandson. Radiographs in the office demonstrate a simple dorsal right thumb carpometacarpal joint dislocation without a fracture. Based on the image in Figure 1, which ligament is the primary stabilizer to prevent this injury from occurring?

![[q220421-a-screenshot-2025-03-18-at-9-14-07-pm.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Dorsal intermetacarpal ligament
2. Posterior oblique ligament
3. Dorsoradial ligament
4. Volar oblique ligament
5. Trapeziotrapezoid ligament

## Correct option

**3. Dorsoradial ligament**

## Answer explanation

The dorsoradial ligament is the primary stabilizer preventing dorsoradial subluxation of the carpometacarpal (CMC) joint (answer choice 3).

The CMC joint is a biconcave saddle joint with multiple surrounding ligaments for stabilization. This includes the volar oblique (volar beak), dorsoradial, intermetacarpal, and posterior oblique ligaments. Historically, the volar oblique ligament was thought to be the primary stabilizer of this joint, however, recent studies have found that this ligament only becomes taut in the hitchhiker position. The dorsoradial ligament is the strongest and thickest ligament and is now widely accepted to be the primary stabilizer preventing dorsoradial subluxation of the thumb CMC joint.

*Edmunds reviewed the anatomy of the thumb trapeziometacarpal (TMC) joint.* **They note** the final phase of thumb opposition creates torque rotation of the volar aspect of the 1st metacarpal in the trapezium recess. This creates tension on the dorsal ligamentous complex, which produces stability for pinch and power grip. **They conclude** that compressive shear forces on the 1st CMC joint can lead to osteoarthritis of this joint over time.

*D'Agostino et al. performed an anatomical study on 13 fresh cadaver thumbs, evaluating the dorsoradial (DRL) and anterior oblique (AOL) ligaments of the TMC joint.* **They found** that the DRL was significantly thicker and stiffer than the AOL. **They conclude** that the DRL is the strongest and stiffest ligament in the TMC joint, and the AOL is relatively weak and compliant.

*Halilaj et al. reviewed the functions of the AOL and DRL using in vivo joint kinematics data on 44 fresh cadaver thumbs.* **Their results demonstrated** that AOL recruitment was lower than 91% across the CMC range of motion, whereas DRL recruitment was higher than 91% in abduction and flexion. **They conclude** that the DRL provides stabilization to the CMC joint in abduction and flexion.

Figure A is an anatomical image depicting the volar (left) and dorsal (right) anatomy of the hand. The 5 ligaments surrounded by boxes are the primary stabilizers of the 1st CMC joint.

Incorrect answer choices:
Answer 1: The dorsal intermetacarpal ligament is the primary restraint to radial translation of the 1st metacarpal.
Answer 2: The posterior oblique ligament is not the primary stabilizer of dorsoradial subluxation in the 1st CMC joint.
Answer 4: The volar oblique ligament was previously believed to be the primary stabilizer to dorsal subluxation of the 1st CMC joint. However, the dorsoradial ligament has been identified as the stronger ligament as further biomechanical studies have emerged.
Answer 5: The trapeziotrapezoid ligament helps to stabilize the trapeziotrapezoid joint, not the 1st CMC joint.
