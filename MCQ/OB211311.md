## Question

A 27-year-old male rock climber presents with the injury shown in Figures A and B following a fall from 20 feet. What variable on presentation and advanced imaging would make the greatest numeric contribution to the Thoracolumbar Injury Classification System (TLICS) score?

*Figure A*

![[q211311-b-figure-2-sagittal-lumbar-burst-fracture.jpg]]

## Options

1. Disruption of the posterior ligamentous complex
2. Complete spinal cord injury
3. Burst morphology
4. Near-complete canal compromise
5. All of the variables have equal weight in the TLICS

## Correct option

**1. Disruption of the posterior ligamentous complex**

## Answer explanation

The patient has sustained a stable thoracolumbar burst fracture. Of the variables listed, a disrupted posterior ligamentous complex integrity has the highest numeric contribution towards operative treatment, with a score of 3 points.

The Thoracolumbar Injury Classification System (TLICS) score guides management of thoracolumbar injuries and is determined based on the sum of the three elements: fracture morphology, PLC integrity, and neurologic status. A composite score of >4 predicts the need for surgical management of the injury, while injuries with a score of <4 may often be managed conservatively. Canal compromise, loss of vertebral body height, and kyphosis do not factor in to the TLICS score. While some studies hold that they are predictive of neurologic injury and PLC integrity, their impact on management remains controversial.

Patel and Vaccaro review the shortcomings of the existing thoracolumbar classifications systems and discuss the TLICS score and its potential contribution to improving medical decision making in the setting of thoracolumbar trauma. The authors discuss the high inter- and intra-observer reliability of the system and presents case examples.

*Joaquim et al., in a follow-up study, prospectively applied the TLICS scoring system to 65 patients with thoracolumbar trauma.* Two patients of 37 in the non-operative arm with a TLICS score <4 underwent surgical intervention for axial back pain and mild focal kyphosis, both without any significant improvement in pain post-operatively. No patient experienced neurologic deterioration during the follow-up. **The authors concluded** that the TLICS could be safely used to guide management.

*Hitchon et al. evaluated the outcomes of nonoperative management in the setting of neurologically intact thoracolumbar burst fractures with a TLICS score of 2.* **The authors found** that 18 of 68 patients (26.4%) of patients failed nonoperative management as a result of pain limiting mobilization, and correlated failure with a greater degree of kyphosis, stenosis, and fracture fragmentation.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 2: Complete spinal cord injury contributes 2 points for neurologic status.
Answer 3: Burst fracture contributes 2 points for morphology.
Answer 4: Canal compromise does not factor into the TLICS score and is of controversial prognostic value.
Answer 5: PLC disruption contributes 3 points and has the most significant contribution on surgical decision-making.
