## Question

A 70-year-old man has a metastatic lesion of the thoracolumbar region with mild cord compression but no neurologic compromise. He undergoes a corpectomy and cage reconstruction utilizing a wide anterolateral approach. Following surgery, he has impaired motor function in his legs but has preserved sensation. Postoperative studies reveal no cord compression. What is the most likely cause of his new motor deficits?

## Options

1. Injury to a left-sided structure commonly located between T5 and T9
2. Injury to a left-sided structure commonly located between T9 and L1
3. Injury to a right-sided structure commonly located between T5 and T9
4. Injury to a right-sided structure commonly located between T9 and L1
5. Injury to a right-sided structure commonly located between L2-L5

## Correct option

**2. Injury to a left-sided structure commonly located between T9 and L1**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a new motor deficit without sensory deficit following an anterolateral approach to the spine. The most likely injured structure is the artery of Adamkiewicz, which is most commonly located on the left side between T9 and L1.

This pattern on clinical examination is suggestive of anterior cord syndrome. The artery of Adamkiewicz has likely been injured during the anterolateral exposure of the spine. Importantly, the spinal canal is found to be patent, which further supports this diagnosis. The artery of Adamkiewicz commonly arises on the left side between T9 and L1, although in rare cases, it may occur at other levels or even be absent.

*Charles et al. reviewed the preoperative topography of the artery of Adamkiewicz and the blood supply of the lumbosacral spinal cord in patients who underwent spinal surgery utilizing anterior approaches.* **They report** that the Adamkiewicz artery was always located between T8 and L3, at T9 or T10 in 50%, and arising from the left side in 75% of the cases. **They concluded** that spinal angiography safely determines the topography of the Adamkiewicz artery, and a contralateral approach or selective surgical techniques without vessel ligation may avoid damage to the Adamkiewicz artery if the pathology does not dictate the side and the extent of the surgical approach.

*Taterra et al. reviewed postoperative neurological deficit due to spinal cord ischemia following injury to the artery of Adamkiewicz.* **They report** that the artery of Adamkiewicz was present in 85% of the population, with most patients having a single vessel (87%) on the left side (77%) originating between T8 and L1 (89%). **They concluded** that the artery of Adamkiewicz is present in the majority of the population, and caution should be taken during vascular and endovascular surgical procedures to avoid injury or ensure proper reconstruction.
