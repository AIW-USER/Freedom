## Question

As defined by the Enneking Surgical Staging System, a stage IIB sarcoma would be:

## Options

1. low-grade, intracompartmental, without metastases
2. high grade, intracompartment, without metastases
3. low-grade, extracompartmental, without metastases
4. high grade, extracompartment, without metastases
5. high grade, extracompartment, with regional or distant metastases

## Correct option

**4. high grade, extracompartment, without metastases**

## Answer explanation

A bone tumor staged as IIB is defined by the Enneking Surgical Staging System as high grade, extracompartment, and without metastases. Stage IIB is most frequent stage of osteosarcoma at presentation.

The prognosis of bone cancer depends on the following variables: (1) overall stage of disease (i.e. Enneking System), (2) presence of metastasis (regional skip (discontinous) lesions or distant lesions), (3) histologic grade, (4) tumor size, and (5) tumor type. The two most common staging systems used are, (1) MSTS or Enneking System, (2) AJCC Staging System.

*Enneking et al. created two staging systems for malignant and benign lesions.* The malignant system uses Roman numerals (e.g. I, II, III) and benign uses arabic numbers (1,2,3). The stages in the MSTS (Enneking) Staging System are:

IA = Low Grade, intracompartmental, no Metastases
IB = Low Grade, extracompartment, no Metastases
IIA = High Grade, intracompartmental, no Metastases
IIB = High Grade, extracompartment, no Metastases
III = Any Grade, Distant Metastases

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: stage IA = low-grade, intracompartmental, without metastases
Answer 2: stage IIA = high grade, intracompartment, without metastases
Answer 3: stage IB = low-grade, extracompartmental, without metastases
Answer 5: stage III = high grade, extracompartment, with regional or distant metastases
