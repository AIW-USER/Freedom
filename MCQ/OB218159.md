## Question

You are on rotation with a high volume elbow surgeon. He sees several patients in clinic with a variety of elbow pathology. Which of the following patients would most likely benefit from an arthroscopic osteocapsular arthroplasty?

## Options

1. 70-year-old female with post-traumatic arthritis with pain throughout motion after comminuted distal humerus fracture
2. 50-year-old male with limited and painful end-range of motion
3. 62-year-old female with significant joint destruction secondary to rheumatoid arthritis
4. 74-year-old female with severe elbow arthritis with pain throughout motion
5. 42-year-old laborer with end stage arthritis

## Correct option

**2. 50-year-old male with limited and painful end-range of motion**

## Answer explanation

This patient likely has motion that is limited by capsular contracture osteophytes. Arthroscopic osteocapsular arthroplasty (OCA) allows the removal of impinging osteophytes and release of hypertrophied capsule and has been shown to be effective at relieving pain and improving motion in patients with mild to moderate osteoarthritis and pain at end-range of motion.

Elbow arthritis is a degenerative joint disease of the elbow that can be broken into three main types: osteoarthritis, post-traumatic arthritis and inflammatory arthritis. Diagnosis can be made with plain radiographs of the elbow. Treatment can be nonoperative or operative depending on patient activity demands, severity of elbow pain, and degree of elbow dysfunction. Arthroscopic OCA allows removal of impinging osteophytes and release of hypertrophied capsule and has been shown to be effective at relieving pain and improving motion in patients with mild to moderate osteoarthritis. It is best indicated in patients with painless mid-arc of motion but painful end-range of motion.

*Galle et al. evaluated the results of elbow arthroscopic OCA and* **determined** which factors influence the outcome in a large group of patients with primary osteoarthritis. **They reported** that elbow OCA is a safe, efficacious treatment for patients with mild to moderate osteoarthritis. **They concluded** that significant improvement in elbow motion, pain, and clinical outcomes may be expected.

*Sochacki et al. reviewed whether arthroscopic debridement of primary elbow osteoarthritis results in a statistically significant and clinically relevant improvement in (1) elbow range of motion and (2) clinical outcomes with (3) low complication and reoperation rates.* **They reported** that elbow arthroscopic debridement for primary degenerative osteoarthritis results in a statistically significant and clinically relevant improvement in elbow range of motion and clinical outcomes. **They concluded** that there are low complication and reoperation rates.

*Van Zeeland et al. reviewed restricted elbow motion.* **They reported** that most patients can regain a functional arc of motion with a combination of physical therapy and a splinting regimen. **They concluded** that arthroscopic elbow capsule release has emerged as a safe and reliable, though technically demanding, method to restore elbow motion.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Post-traumatic end-stage arthritis is likely best addressed with a total elbow arthroplasty.
Answer 3: Severe rheumatoid joint destruction is often outside of the limits of OCA.
Answer 4: This patient would benefit more from a total elbow arthroplasty.
Answer 5: Young, high-demand patients with END-STAGE arthritis may benefit more from an ulnohumeral interposition arthroplasty.
