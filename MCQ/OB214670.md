## Question

You are performing a ligament reconstruction and tendon interposition (LRTI) with the plan to utilize the flexor carpi radialis tendon (FCR) on a 53 year old Silesian seamstress with advanced left basilar thumb arthritis. After performing a trapeziectomy, you notice the FCR tendon is found to be completely avulsed. Which of the following procedures is the next best step?

## Options

1. Carpometacarpal (CMC) joint arthrodesis
2. CMC arthroplasty with pyrocarbon implant
3. CMC arthroplasty with silicone implant
4. LRTI with extensor carpi radialis longus (ECRL) tendon
5. LRTI with flexor pollicis longus (FPL) tendon

## Correct option

**4. LRTI with extensor carpi radialis longus (ECRL) tendon**

## Answer explanation

This patient is undergoing LRTI with the plan to utilize FCR; however, the FCR is found to be completely avulsed. LRTI with ECRL is the best salvage option listed.

There are many surgical options for 1st carpometacarpal OA. This includes volar ligament reconstruction and closing wedge dorsal extension osteotomy for early Stage I disease. Late disease is treated with trapeziectomy with or without metacarpal base stabilization, CMC fusion, or prosthetic arthroplasty. LRTI is most commonly performed using the FCR tendon but the ECRL and APL tendons can also be used if the FCR is not available.

*Jones et al. reviewed salvage options for FCR disruption during LRTI.* For partial FCR injury, **they recommend** using the remaining tendon, or the entire FCR for LRTI (if enough length is available). For complete FCR avulsion, **they recommended** using the ECRL as a salvage.

Conolly reviewed 17 patients undergoing revision for CMC OA (12 silastic arthroplasty, 4 trapeziectomy, 1 arthrodesis). Revision options comprised implant removal or replacement, soft tissue arthroplasty and fusion. 53% had good results, 30% had poor results. They conclude that trapeziectomy relieves pain but late proximal migration and metacarposcaphoid arthritis occur and soft tissue interposition may prevent this.

Illustration A shows a post-operative radiograph of the ECRL-LRTI procedure augmented with a suture button. Illustrations B and C are diagrams showing the usage of the ECRL tendon for an LRTI procedure. Illustration D is a diagram showing the usage of the FCR tendon for an LRTI procedure.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: At this point in the surgery, the trapezium has already been resected and a CMC arthrodesis is no longer able to be performed.
Answers 2 and 3: CMC prosthetic arthroplasty (with either a silicone or a pyrocarbon implant) has been shown to have inferior outcomes to LRTI. Thus, it would not be the best bailout option in this situation.
Answer 5: When the FCR is completely avulsed, LRTI with the ECRL or APL tendon (not FPL) are the next best options.
