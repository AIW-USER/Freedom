## Question

A 50-year-old T3 paraplegic is taken to the emergency department by his caretaker with chest pain, bradycardia, hypertension, headache, shivering, agitation, and diaphoresis. He is poorly supervised and recently his caretaker noticed increased swelling isolated to his right thigh. He is otherwise afebrile. A diagnosis of autonomic dysreflexia is suspected. Which of the following is the most likely potential cause?

## Options

1. Sarcoma
2. Pulmonary embolism
3. Occult fracture
4. Suprapubic urinary catheter
5. Recent viral illness

## Correct option

**3. Occult fracture**

## Answer explanation

The patient is a high-thoracic paraplegic that is presenting with symptoms consistent with autonomic dysreflexia. While there are many possible causes, occult fracture is the only choices which is the most consistent with his clinical presentation.

Autonomic dysreflexia is a potentially lethal sequela of spinal cord injuries (SCI) and is the result of sympathetic dysregulation. It may occur spontaneously in patients with SCI above T5-6 and can be caused by a wide variety of stimuli to include visceral irritation (such as bowel or bladder distension, gastrointestinal conditions), occult infections, skin irritation secondary to pressure or injury, decubitus ulcers, or unrecognized lower extremity fractures. Initial management should involve the identification and prompt treatment of the causative stimuli.

*Schulte et al. reviewed the literature regarding long bone fractures in patients with spinal cord injuries.* **The authors noted** that SCI patients have pathologically weak bone that predisposes them to fractures in addition to poor soft tissue quality, which makes fracture care difficult. **They report** that fractures may generally be treated with non-operative modalities, but that open reduction and internal fixation can be considered in highly active wheel-chair athletes as well as in fractures that prevent effective transfers or upright sitting in a chair.

*Jain et al. performed an epidemiological study of patients in the US with spinal cord injuries from 1993-2012.* **The authors found** that the incidence of SCI remained the same over the span of the study period, but there was a significant increased incidence in men over 65 years of age and injuries associated with falls. **The authors concluded** that the incidence of SCI remains stable, but the total number of cases is expected to increase due to population growth.

*Carbone et al. performed a retrospective cohort study examining mortality rates in SCI patients after long bone fractures of the lower extremity.* **They found** that older men with SCI and lower extremity long bone fractures involving the femur, tibia, and fibula had higher mortality rates at 5 years than younger patients with SCI. Further, **they found** that higher Charleson Comorbidity Index scores were associated with greater mortality risk. The authors stipulated that both the fracture and the high level of comorbidities are the most significant contributors to mortality in this patient population.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: Sarcoma may present with painless swelling but has not been associated with the development of autonomic dysreflexia.
Answer 2: Pulmonary embolism resulting from venous thrombosis would not be associated with isolated swelling of the thigh and additionally has not been associated with the development of autonomic dysreflexia.
Answer 4: Urinary retention is associated with the development of autonomic dysreflexia. However, the presence of a suprapubic catheter would likely prevent an episode of urinary retention from occurring.
Answer 5: Viral illnesses are not associated with the development of autonomic dysreflexia.
