## Question

An orthopaedic resident evaluates a 25-year-old male presenting to the clinic with right wrist pain provoked by prolonged periods of cell phone use. After a thorough history and examination, the resident correctly identifies the isolated involvement of the extensor pollicis brevis (EPB) and abductor pollicis longus (APL) tendons within the patient’s causative pathology. Which of the following examination findings most likely led to this conclusion?

## Options

1. Dorsoradial sided pain made worse with resisted wrist extension
2. Ulnar sided pain with a painful snapping sensation while moving the wrist from pronation to supination
3. Radial sided pain provoked with Phalen testing
4. Radial sided pain provoked with Finkelstein testing
5. Tenderness at the thumb carpometacarpal (CMC) joint and a positive CMC grind test

## Correct option

**4. Radial sided pain provoked with Finkelstein testing**

## Answer explanation

De Quervain’s tenosynovitis involves thickening and swelling of the first dorsal compartment extensor retinaculum, which contains the extensor pollicis brevis (EPB) and abductor pollicis longus (APL) tendons. Symptoms are provoked with Finkelstein testing (Answer 4).

The patient presents with wrist pain provoked by excessive cell phone use. De Quervain’s tenosynovitis commonly presents with radial-sided wrist pain made worse with Finkelstein testing, which involves grasping the patient’s thumb and quickly moving the wrist to a position of ulnar deviation or having the patient make a fist around their thumb and ulnarly deviating the wrist. The condition involves the EPB and APL tendons in which the overlying extensor retinaculum becomes pathologically thickened leading to symptomatic tendon friction within the first dorsal compartment. De Quervain’s tenosynovitis can be worsened by repetitive thumb movements associated with prolonged cell phone use.

*Wang et al. used surface electromyography (EMG) to assess EPB and abductor pollicis brevis (APB) muscle function in twelve healthy volunteers playing a smartphone game for thirty minutes.* Using median EMG frequency as a marker for muscle fatigue, **they found** significant change after ten minutes of gameplay in the EPB muscle, and after 20 minutes in the APB muscle. Discomfort levels among the participants significantly increased after thirty minutes. **The authors concluded** that prolonged smartphone gameplay may result in chronic, symptomatic muscle injury.

*Ilyas et al. reviewed the pathophysiology, diagnosis, and management of de Quervain’s tenosynovitis.* **They noted** that the associated symptoms and functional impairment are the result of resisted gliding of the APL and EPB tendons within a narrowed first dorsal compartment secondary to thickening and swelling of the overlying extensor retinaculum. The authors described the Finkelstein maneuver as essentially pathognomonic for the condition and expounded on a myriad of treatment strategies ranging from rest and bracing to corticosteroid injection and surgical release of the first dorsal compartment, with nonsurgical management being the mainstay of effective treatment.

Incorrect Responses:
Answer 1: These findings are more consistent with intersection syndrome, which is an inflammatory condition occurring at the junction of the first and second dorsal compartments (involving the EPB, APL, extensor carpi radialis longus, and extensor carpi radialis brevis tendons).
Answer 2: These findings are more consistent with a snapping extensor carpi ulnaris tendon.
Answer 3: Phalen testing involves continuous wrist flexion against gravity. A positive test produces symptoms in patients with carpal tunnel syndrome.
Answer 5: These findings are more consistent with basilar thumb arthritis.
