## Question

An otherwise healthy 83-year-old male presents to the ED for evaluation after a ground-level fall. He underwent right total knee arthroplasty five years ago utilizing a cruciate-retaining femoral component. In the ED, he's found to have a periprosthetic distal femur fracture with medial and lateral cortical comminution. The treating surgeon elects to utilize both a lateral locking plate and a retrograde intramedullary nail for fixation. Which of the following is true of this technique compared to dual plating?

## Options

1. Greater axial stiffness
2. Greater torsional stiffness
3. Allows for earlier weight bearing
4. Results in more biologic injury
5. Higher incidence of postoperative infection

## Correct option

**3. Allows for earlier weight bearing**

## Answer explanation

Utilization of a retrograde intramedullary nail (rIMN) in conjunction with a lateral distal femoral locking plate allows for early weight bearing in patients sustaining distal femur fractures that require supplemental fixation.

The use of supplemental fixation has increased in patients with poor bone quality and comminuted distal femur fractures. While dual plating and nail plate constructs have both been shown to lead to improved rates of healing, differences exist between the two techniques. Earlier weight bearing has been shown to be safe and effective following the combination of a rIMN and a lateral distal femoral locking plate, which may lead to improved patient satisfaction and outcomes. Dual plating, however, has been shown to result in a more stiff construct, both in axial compression and torsion. Understanding the difference in these techniques can assist the surgeon in selecting the construct that is most appropriate for each patient.

*Liporace et al review the use of nail plate constructs for distal femur fractures.* **They report** that using the nail plate combination technique offers a stable, balanced fixation following distal femur fractures in patients with poor quality bone. **They conclude** that this technique may be used and safely allow early weight-bearing and mobilization following surgery.

*Wright et al review supplemental fixation of supracondylar distal femur fractures in a biomechanical model.* **They report** that dual plating provides increased stiffness in both axial compression and torsion as compared to nail plate constructs. **They conclude**, however, that in the clinical setting, fracture morphology, desired healing mode, surgical approach, and implant cost should be considered when implementing these fixation strategies. Both techniques allow for adequate fixation of these difficult to treat injuries.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1 and 2: Dual plating has been shown to result in increased stiffness in both torsional stress and axial compression compared to nail plate constructs.
Answer 4: Dual plating is associated with a larger medial dissection and more soft tissue insult than the addition of a retrograde nail to a lateral locking plate.
Answer 5: No change in infection rate has been reported with either technique.
