## Question

A 16-year-old male who just obtained his driver's license is traveling at highway speeds and is rear-ended when traffic quickly stops. He was wearing a lap belt at the time of the accident. He presents to the emergency department as a trauma complaining primarily of back pain. Radiographs and a CT scan are performed, with a sagittal cut from the CT scan shown in Figure A. The associated MRI does show posterior column involvement. What is the most likely associated injury?

![[q218708-a-chance-fracture.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Closed head injury
2. Gastrointestinal injury
3. Pulmonary contusion
4. Pelvis fracture
5. Genitourinary injury

## Correct option

**2. Gastrointestinal injury**

## Answer explanation

Based on the diagnosis of a Chance fracture, a gastrointestinal injury is the most common additional injury given the flexion/distraction mechanism.

Chance fractures are traumatic fractures of the thoracolumbar spine which typically occur in the transitional zone of this area, between T12 and L3. They are characterized by bony and ligamentous injury where the anterior column fails via compression and the middle and posterior columns fail under tension. In the setting of motor vehicle collisions, the lap belt is often responsible for creating this flexion moment. There is a published rate of gastrointestinal injury of around 50% due to the anterior flexion moment arm mentioned, so the abdomen should be thoroughly evaluated in the emergency department. Chance fractures are usually unstable, especially if the posterior elements are involved, and require surgical decompression and stabilization.

*Andras et al primarily evaluated the degree to which Chance fractures are misdiagnosed in a pediatric population.* **They note** that out of 26 patients who had an appropriate mechanism, 27% had a Chance fracture and 73% had compression fractures with the mean age of the Chance fracture group being 14.6 years and all of them being located between T12 and L3. Five of the seven Chance fractures were initially misdiagnosed, with an average delay in correct diagnosis of approximately 95 days.

*Daniels et al. reviewed pediatric thoracolumbar spine trauma,* **noting** that the mechanism of injury may influence the incidence of additional visceral and musculoskeletal injuries. **They emphasize** that early and accurate diagnosis is important in making sure patients recover quickly and fully. Spine injuries highlighted in the article include compression fractures, fracture-dislocation injuries, apophyseal fractures, and others.

Figure A is a sagittal CT of the thoracolumbar spine demonstrating an L1 chance fracture with involvement of all 3 spinal columns.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1 and 3-5: Gastrointestinal injury is the most common injury seen in flexion-distraction type seatbelt injuries.
