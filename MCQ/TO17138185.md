Chapter: Pediatric Orthopedics MCQs — Obstetric Brachial Plexopathy (Erb s, Klumpke_s Palsy)

## Question

most common long-term sequel of obstetric brachial palsy is

## Options

1. anteverted glenoid with scapular hypoplasia
2. glenoid hypoplasia with retroversion
3. glenoid hypoplasia with anteversion
4. anterior subluxation of humeral head with glenoid hypoplasia and excessive anteversion

## Correct option

**2. glenoid hypoplasia with retroversion**

## Answer explanation

characteristic long-term sequels of obstetric brachial palsy are:

Glenoid hypoplasia and increased glenoid retroversion, humeral head flattening, posterior humeral head subluxation.

Obstetric brachial palsy results in characteristic deformities of glenohumeral joint. The shoulder becomes flexed, internally rotated, and slightly abducted; active abduction of the joint decreases; and external rotation disappears. Abnormal muscle forces across the shoulder lead to early changes in the glenoid. These changes include flattening of the posterior glenoid creating a pseudo glenoid. As the deformity progresses, the glenohumeral joint center becomes more posterior and the glenoid becomes more retroverted and flattened or even convex (hypoplastic and retroverted glenoid). This leads to progressive posterior glenohumeral subluxation and eventual dislocation with the humeral head becoming flattened against the glenoid. These advanced glenohumeral changes can occur early and have been described by the age of 2 years.

Answer: 2
