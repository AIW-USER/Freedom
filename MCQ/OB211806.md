## Options

2. 54-year old male with 2 years of worsening leg and back pain with walking, his pain is relieved when leaning forward, and MRI shown in Figure B
3. 58-year-old female with coronary artery disease, 3 years of low back pain, leg pain that is worse with activity, and relieved when standing in place but unchanged when leaning forward, and an MRI depicted in Figure C
4. 36-year-old female with 4 days of severe lateral leg pain, normal perianal and genital sensation, 4 out of 5 weakness to EHL and tibialis anterior, and a MRI shown in Figure D

## Correct option

**1. 36-year-old male with 3 months of radicular symptoms without weakness, no improvement with physical therapy and epidural corticosteroid injection, and imaging shown in figure A**

## Answer explanation

Unilateral foraminotomy, partial ligamentum flavum resection, medial facetectomy, and disk resection are describing the steps of a microdiscectomy. This is indicated in patients with lumbar disc herniations that do not have significant symptomatic improvement with physical therapy and corticosteroid injections.

Microdiscectomy is a surgical procedure where a small laminotomy is performed and the herniated part of the disc is removed without instrumentation or fusion. Indications for this include persistent disabling pain lasting longer than 6 weeks with failure to improve with PT, progressive weakness, and cauda equina syndrome. Around 90% of patients improve with physical therapy alone, however, and that should remain the initial treatment in most lumbar herniation patients.

*Soliman et al retrospectively reviewed 54 patients who underwent microdiscectomy for an average follow-up of 7.2 years.* **They found** that 89% of patients had good or excellent outcomes with microdiscectomy. 90% had improvement in back pain and 88% had improvement in leg pain. Reoccurrence occurred in 11% of patients. Only 1 required later arthrodesis. **They concluded** that microdiscectomy is an effective method of treating lumbar disc herniations.

*Millhouse et al. reviewed the preoperative indications and contraindications for microdiscectomy of a paracentral disc herniation.* Indications included imaging showing herniation with compression, symptoms for 6 weeks, and failure of conservative measures. Contraindications included alternative pathology that explains symptoms, such as peripheral neuropathy from diabetes mellitus or a systemic disease like multiple sclerosis. They then discuss the surgical steps of positioning, incision and exposure, approach, disc excision, and closure.

Figure A shows a T2 MRI sagittal L4-L5 paracentral disc herniation. Figure B is a T2 sagittal MRI that shows multi-level spinal stenosis with L4-L5 degenerative anterolisthesis. Figure C shows a normal-appearing T2 sagittal MRI. Figure D shows a T2 MRI of the lumbar spine with disc herniation at level L5-S1. Figure E shows a T2 MRI with anterolisthesis of L5 on S1.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: This patient's imaging demonstrates multi-level stenosis and degenerative anterolisthesis of L4-L5. This is best treated with wide decompression and fusion.
Answer 3: This patient's imaging demonstrates lumbar stenosis of L4-L5 due to hypertrophy of ligamentum flavum and/or facet capsule. Her clinical picture however is consistent with vascular claudication and would likely benefit from vascular surgery intervention.
Answer 4: This patient has a disc herniation, however, 90% of herniations improve with non-operative treatment and that would be indicated in this patient before surgical intervention.
Answer 5: Spondylolisthesis causing nerve root compression is better treated with decompression and instrumentation.
