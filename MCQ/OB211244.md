## Answer explanation

The condition demonstrated in Figure A is Multiple Hereditary Exostoses (MHE),
characterized by the development of multiple osteochondromas. The inheritance pattern of MHE is autosomal dominant (AD), represented in Figure B.

MHE involves a mutation in either EXT1, EXT2, or EXT3 tumor suppressor genes, all of which are transmitted in an autosomal dominant fashion. This mutation leads to unregulated growth and thus the characteristic exostoses. Patients with mutations in EXT1 have been found to demonstrate higher rates of sarcomatous change (~10%) when compared to those with a defect in either EXT2 or EXT3 (~1%).

*Czajka et al. investigated the proportion of MHE patients who underwent malignant degeneration to sarcoma in a heterogeneous international cohort.* **The authors reported** a 2.7% rate of malignant transformation, with the most common sites including the pelvis and scapula. **They advocated** that this estimated risk of malignant degeneration may be used to appropriately counsel and surveil patients with MHE.

*Pedrini et al. evaluated the relationship between severity of MHE and genetic background.* **The authors found** that the risk factors for the most severe MHE phenotype were male gender, EXT1 mutations, and >20 affected skeletal sites. **They concluded** that an understanding of these risk factors is useful for clinical management and follow-up of patients with MHE.

*Huegel et al. reviewed the role of heparan sulfate (HS) in skeletal development, growth, and MHE.* **They reported** that the formation of the osteochondromas in MHE is the result of changes in HS-dependent signaling pathways, including BMP and Indian hedgehog (Ihh) pathways, combining to create pro-chondrogenic responses and proliferative environments along the border of growth plates. The authors theorized that pharmacological targeting of HS may mitigate exostosis formation.

Figure A demonstrates AP and lateral radiographs of a patient with MHE.
Figures B-F depict an autosomal dominant (AD), autosomal recessive (AR), X-linked recessive (XR), X-linked dominant (XD), and mitochondrial disease inheritance patterns, respectively.
