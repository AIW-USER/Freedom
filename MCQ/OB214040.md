## Options

1. Posterior vertebral body wedging for three consecutive vertebrae
2. Lumbar hypolordosis
3. Marginal syndesmophytes
4. Schmorl's nodes
5. Preserved disc height and lack of degenerative disc changes

## Correct option

**4. Schmorl's nodes**

## Answer explanation

The patient has Scheurmann's kyphosis which is associated with herniation of disc material into the vertebral endplate, also known as Schmorl's nodes.

Scheuermann's kyphosis is characterized by thoracic hyperkyphosis (>45°) and anterior vertebral wedging >5° for three consecutive vertebrae. Scheuermann's kyphosis has an association with HLA-B27. Most patients are asymptomatic, but back pain and cosmesis are the most common complaints. Patients with kyphosis >100° may have compromised lung function. Most patients are treated with physical therapy, activity modifications, and hyperextension bracing. Patients with kyphosis >60° and persistent back pain are treated with posterior instrumented fusion with or without Ponte osteotomies.

*Halanski et al. performed an experimental study of maternal vitamin D deficiency in a porcine model.* **They reported** a higher kyphotic Cobb angle in the maternal vitamin D deficient diet pigs at all time points compared to the control animals. **They concluded** that maternal dietary vitamin D deficiency increased the development of hyperkyphosis in their offspring.

*Sheehan et al. reviewed the effects of the HLA-B27 on seronegative spondyloarthritides.* **They reported** that patients positive for HLA-B27 have a 16-fold risk of developing ankylosing spondylitis. **They concluded** that further research is needed to determine the mechanism by which HLA-B27 leads to spondyloarthropathies.

*Wood et al. reviewed the diagnosis and management of Scheurmann's kyphosis.* *They cited literature stating surgical correction should be performed for patients with kyphosis >60° and pain that does not resolve with nonsurgical care.* *They referenced studies reporting a high percentage of patients being satisfied with the cosmetic appearance of surgical correction, but a poor correlation with pain relief.*

Figure A is an AP standing radiograph of the spine with no significant coronal plane deformity. Figure B is a lateral standing radiograph of the spine with significant thoracic kyphosis and compensatory lumbar hyperlordosis. Figure C is the sagittal T2 MRI of the thoracic spine with significant kyphosis, anterior wedging of three consecutive vertebrae, and no spinal cord signal changes.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: Scheurmann's kyphosis would be associated with anterior vertebral wedging of >5° for at least three consecutive vertebrae.
Answer 2: In Scheurmann's kyphosis there is a compensatory lumbar hyperlordosis.
Answer 3: Marginal syndesmophytes are characteristic findings of ankylosing spondylitis.
Answer 4: Scheurmann's kyphosis is associated with disc height narrowing.
