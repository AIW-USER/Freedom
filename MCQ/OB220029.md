## Question

## Answer explanation

Figure E demonstrates the V-Y turndown, which requires bracing, restricted ROM, and partial weight bearing for 6 weeks.

During total knee arthroplasty (TKA), especially in the revision setting, extensile exposures may be desirable in order to increase visualization and ease of instrumentation. Extensile exposures include the quadriceps snip, V-Y turndown, and tibial tubercle osteotomy. With a V-Y turndown, at the proximal extent of the medial parapatellar arthrotomy, the incision is turned distally and laterally at a 45-degree angle along the tendinous portion of the vastus lateralis. This forms an inverted V of tissue that may be lengthened at the time of closure, forming a Y shape. Although this technique affords excellent exposure in ankylosed knees, patients often are left with an extensor lag.

*Kelly et al. explore the challenges and techniques for performing total knee replacement surgery on patients with stiff or immobile knees.* *They described the quadriceps snip, the V-Y turndown, the tibial tubercle osteotomy (TTO), and the femoral peel as options to aid in the exposure of a stiff knee.* **They cautioned** that while the V-Y turndown can provide excellent exposure, patients are often left with an extensor lag.

*Barrack et al. reported on a series of 123 revision TKAs, of which 14 required quadriceps turndown, 15 underwent TTO, and 31 a quadriceps snip.* **They found** that the patients who underwent quadriceps snip achieved postoperative function similar to the group with a standard medial parapatellar approach. Meanwhile, the V-Y turndown and TTO groups had equal function to one another, but worse than the medial parapatellar group. **They concluded** that these extensile exposure techniques are successful but may have functional consequences.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: Medial parapatellar approach can be treated with standard postoperative rehab protocol (weight bearing as tolerated + range of motion as tolerated).
Answer 2: Lateral parapatellar approach can be treated with standard postoperative rehab protocol (weight bearing as tolerated + range of motion as tolerated).
Answer 3: Midvastus approach can be treated with standard postoperative rehab protocol (weight bearing as tolerated + range of motion as tolerated).
Answer 4: When a quadriceps snip is utilized to increase the exposure afforded by the standard medial parapatellar approach, the patient can be treated with standard postoperative rehab protocol (weight bearing as tolerated + range of motion as tolerated).
