## Answer explanation

The patient presents with a cutaneous lesion suspicious of non-accidental trauma. Figure D is a radiograph of a metaphyseal corner lesion, a radiographic finding consistent with child abuse.

The most common physical exam findings in a child suspected of being the victim of non-accidental trauma are cutaneous findings (e.g. bruising, cigarette burns, belt markings), followed by fractures. Fractures are divided into high (long bone in nonambulatory child, metaphyseal corner, transphyseal separation, posteromedial rib, scapula, sternum, spinous process) and moderate (multiple fractures in various stages of healing, vertebral body, digits, complex skull) specificity. Diaphyseal fractures are more common than metaphyseal fractures, and the most commonly fractured long bone is the humerus, followed by the tibia and femur. Physicians are mandated reporters and are granted immunity from civil and criminal liability if they report in good faith, but may be charged with a crime for failure to report. Early involvement of social workers and pediatricians is essential, as missed or unreported cases carry a 30-50% of repeat abuse and 5-10% chance of death from abuse.

*Ranade et al. published a JAAOS review on the role of orthopedic surgeons in identifying and managing nonaccidental trauma.* *The authors describe the risk factors for abuse, sentinel signs on clinical examination, and questionable fracture patterns related to NAT that enable providers to better identify and address children at risk of abuse or neglect.* Fractures are the second most common injury caused by NAT after skin lesions; among infants and young children, 12% to 20% of fracture injuries are attributed to NAT. In 2017, 674,000 children were confirmed victims of abuse, including 1,720 fatalities. As an evaluator of young infants and children with bony injury, the orthopaedic surgeon is critically positioned to detect abuse, and it is essential that the orthopaedic surgeon has sound, general knowledge about NAT. When possible, identification of sentinel lesions and notification of authorities as a mandated reporter may prevent ongoing abuse and even fatality.

*Loder and Feinberg published a 2007 study examining the demographic and injury characteristics of children hospitalized with nonaccidental trauma as a causative factor using the national 2000 Healthcare Cost and Utilization Project Kids’ Inpatient Database (KID)5.* *Of the approximate 2.5 million pediatric hospitalization cases in the database, 1794 musculoskeletal injuries were identified as caused by nonaccidental trauma, and in children aged 2 years or younger, these are often abuse related.* Two-thirds of abused hospitalized children come from a disadvantaged situation, most often on Medicaid. The most common fractures from abuse occur in the skull, femur, ribs/sternum, and humerus, in descending order. In addition, this study revealed that 17% of abused children had concomitant psychiatric or neurological conditions, mostly in children aged 3 to 20 years.

Figure A is a photograph of a patterned iron burn on the thigh. Figure B is a photograph of ectopia lentis, superior dislocation of the lens seen in those with Marfan syndrome. Figure C if a radiograph of anterolateral tibial bowing, associated with NF1. Figure D is a radiograph of a metaphyseal corner lesion of the distal femur. Figure E is a radiograph of posteromedial tibial bowing. Figure F is a radiograph of zebra lines, seen in patients with osteogenesis imperfecta receiving bisphosphonate therapy. Illustration A is a photograph of pinna ecchymosis, another sign of child abuse. Illustration B is a loop imprint on the thigh, a patterned injury that results from whipping.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: This is ectopia lentis, characteristic of Marfan syndrome.
Answer 2: This is a radiograph of anterolateral tibial bowing, characteristic of neurofibromatosis type 1.
Answer 4: This is a radiograph of posteromedial tibial bowing, often associated with calcaneovalgus foot deformity. This deformity usually resolves spontaneously over the course of 5-7 years; however, it carries a risk of limb length discrepancy which may require contralateral epiphysiodesis of the longer limb.
Answer 5: This is a radiograph of zebra lines, characteristic of bisphosphonate therapy in patients with osteogenesis imperfecta. These are radiographically similar but distinct from Park-Harris growth arrest lines, which are an indicator of trauma or illness resulting in transient growth arrest. Park-Harris lines persist for a longer duration and remain steadfastly parallel to the physis when compared to zebra lines.
