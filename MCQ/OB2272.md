## Question

A 16-year-old male is undergoing T4 to L3 posterior spinal fusion for idiopathic scoliosis. Neuromonitoring is ordered for the case. Which of the following is true regarding neuromonitoring during spine surgery?

## Options

1. Somatosensory evoked potentials (SSEPs) are highly sensitive to anesthetic type
2. Motor evoked potentials (MEPs) work via afferent signal conduction
3. MEPs afferently monitor the integrity of the ventral corticospinal tract
4. SSEPs work via efferent signal conduction and monitor the lateral corticospinal tract
5. SSEPs monitor the dorsal column pathways

## Correct option

**5. SSEPs monitor the dorsal column pathways**

## Answer explanation

During adolescent idiopathic scoliosis (AIS) cases with neuromonitoring, SSEPs afferently monitor the dorsal column sensory pathways.

Neuromonitoring during spine surgery is done via monitoring of sensory afferent pathways (dorsal column, spinothalamic tract) and motor efferent pathways (lateral and ventral corticospinal tract). SSEPs are generally unaffected by anesthetic choice but are not reliable to measure the anterior spinal cord function. In contrast, MEPs measure the anterior spinal cord integrity but are highly sensitive to anesthetic choice. Triggered electromyography (EMG) can also be used intra-operatively to determine breech when placing pedicle instrumentation.

*Padberg et al. evaluated the reliability of SSEP and MEP to detect intraoperative injury.* **They report** that the sensitivity of combined SSEP and MEP in predicting post-operative neurologic status was 99%, and the specificity of normal SSEP and MEP predicting normal post-operative exam was 100%. **They conclude** that SSEP and MEP monitoring can be used in lieu of intra-operative wake-up test when reliable data are obtained.

*Schwartz et al. report on the use of SSEP to detect prone positioning based peripheral nerve palsy.* **They report** that changes in ulnar nerve SSEPs were seen in 18/500 patients, and repositioning resulted in zero post-operative brachial plexopathies. **They report** that positioning based palsy can be greatly reduced by peripheral nerve neuromonitoring.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: MEPs not SSEPs are highly sensitive to the anesthetic choice
Answer 2: MEPs work via EFFERENT signal conduction
Answer 3: While MEPs do monitor the lateral and ventral corticospinal tract they work via EFFERENT signal conduction
Answer 4: SSEPs work via AFFERENT signal conduction and monitor the dorsal column
