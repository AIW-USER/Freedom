## Question

Understanding the strengths and limitations of the various statistical tests is paramount when critically evaluating the literature and creating research study plans. Which of the following best describes the difference between a one-way and two-way analysis of variance (ANOVA)?

## Options

1. A one-way ANOVA is used when comparing two groups and a two-way ANOVA is used when comparing 3 or more groups
2. A one-way ANOVA is used for normally distributed data and a two-way ANOVA is used for non-normally distributed data
3. A one-way ANOVA is used when comparing 3 or more groups of ordinal data and a two-way ANOVA is used when comparing 2 groups of ordinal data
4. A one-way ANOVA is used when comparing 3 or more groups with one independent variable and a two-way ANOVA is used when comparing 3 or more groups with two independent variables
5. A one-way ANOVA is used when comparing two independent groups and a two-way ANOVA is used when compared two paired groups

## Correct option

**4. A one-way ANOVA is used when comparing 3 or more groups with one independent variable and a two-way ANOVA is used when comparing 3 or more groups with two independent variables**

## Answer explanation

Analysis of variance (ANOVA) is used to compare the means of three or more independent groups in which the data are normally distributed. A one-way ANOVA is used when there is one independent variable of interest, and a two-way ANOVA is used when there are two independent variables of interest (Answer 4).

Multiple statistical tests are available when performing hypothesis testing, and the appropriate test(s) is/are dependent on many variables. Data can be classified as categorical, ordinal, or continuous. Categorical data can be considered counts, and the categories do not represent an underlying order. Often this is represented as the presence or absence of an finding. Data with an inherent order among categories represents ordinal data (examples: race, hair color, pathologic diagnosis). Continuous data are observations or measurements on a continuum in which the differences between values have meaning on a numerical scale (examples: time, length, volume). Different distributions can also characterize data (i.e., normal, skewed or bimodal), and considering the distribution is critical when selecting the appropriate statistical test.

The appropriate analysis also depends on the number of groups being compared and whether these groups will be treated as independent groups or as paired samples. Altogether, multiple considerations must be made when deciding on the appropriate statistical test (Illustration A), and an understanding of biostatistical tools is essential for performing and interpreting clinical research.

Kocher and Zurakowski provide a primer of clinical epidemiology and biostatistics for orthopaedic surgeons. In this scoping review, the authors outline a litany of statistical definitions and tools, and succinctly outline the various tests frequently used in orthopaedic research.

*Araoye et al. performed a survey investigating the knowledge of medical statistics among orthopaedic residents.* The survey was sent to 10 institutions and was ultimately completed by 178/279 (64%) of eligible residents. Overall, while 53% had prior biostatistics training, 47% could not determine a study's design, 38% could not apply the concept of sensitivity and specificity, and 83% could not assess the strength of a relationship using odds ratios. **The authors concluded** that notable deficits exist in orthopaedic residents' biostatistical knowledge.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1 and 3: One- and two-way ANOVA are used when comparing 3 or more groups involving normally distributed data.
Answer 2: An ANOVA is used for normally distributed data. The Kruskal-Wallis or Friedman test is used for non-normally distributed data.
Answer 5: A student t-test is used when comparing 2 groups of normally distributed data, while a paired t-test is used when comparing paired 2 group samples of normally distributed data.
