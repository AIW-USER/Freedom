Chapter: Regional Orthopedics MCQs — Subacromial Impingement

## Question

Which is a contraindication for superior capsular reconstruction?

## Options

1. Irreparable supraspinatus and infraspinatus tear with Hamada grade I glenohumeral arthropathy
2. Irreparable subscapularis tear
3. Pseudoparalysis
4. Massive rotator cuff tear with failed conservative treatment

## Correct option

**2. Irreparable subscapularis tear**

## Answer explanation

ANSWER-B

Explanation: Superior capsular reconstruction: Several treatment options exist for massive irreparable cuff tears, such as reverse shoulder arthroplasty and tendon transfers. However, there exists significant concern for high complication rates and unpredictable clinical outcomes following such treatments. Superior capsular reconstruction (SCR) represents a viable alternative in treating irreparable cuff tears, providing biomechanical stability and improving pain related to this pathology by reducing superior humeral instability and subacromial impingement

In the massive irreparable and retracted rotator cuff, upward migration of the head of the humerus occurs due to deficient cuff above. This results in reduced acromial humeral distance and suboptimal tensioning of the deltoid muscle. Superior capsular reconstruction is done with either a folded fascia lata graft (6-8 mm in thickness) or acellular dermal graft (3-4 mm in thickness). The graft is secured arthroscopically or through a mini open incision with anchors on the superior glenoid rim and on the medial border of the tuberosity.

Superior capsular reconstruction works by providing the glenohumeral joint with a passive constraint to superior humeral-head translation, optimizing the otherwise dysfunctional rotator cuff force couples.

Indications:

- Failed non-operative treatment, of massive, irreparable rotator cuff tears (typically involving supraspinatus and infraspinatus tendons), with minimal to no rotator cuff arthropathy (Hamada Grade 1 or 2), with an intact or reparable subscapularis tendon, and with a functional deltoid muscle.
- Massive irreparable rotator cuff tear with intolerable pain
- Massive irreparable rotator cuff tear with pseudoparalysis (inability to elevate the arm over 90° in the setting of a rotator cuff tear/ some authors suggest it should be over 45 degree)

Contraindications

Superior capsular reconstruction is contraindicated in patients with rotator cuff tears associated with moderate to severe rotator cuff arthropathy (Hamada Grade ≥3), patients with glenohumeral osteoarthritis, and patients with a torn or irreparable subscapularis.

Hamada classification of rotator cuff arthropathy:

- Stage 1: Acromiohumeral interval greater than 6 mm.
- Stage 2: Acromiohumeral interval less than 7 mm.
- Stage 3: Acromiohumeral interval less than 7 mm with acetabulization of acromion.
- Stage 4a: Acromiohumeral interval less than 7 mm with glenohumeral arthritis without acetabulization.
- Stage 4b: Acromiohumeral interval less than 7 mm with acetabulization and glenohumeral arthritis.
- Stage 5: Acromiohumeral interval less than 7 mm with osteonecrosis of humeral head
