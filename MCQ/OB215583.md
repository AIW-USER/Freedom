## Question

A 68-year-old female presents to the office after feeling a snap in her foot while walking at her grandson's baseball game. She is unsure of the position of her foot or ankle when she felt the snap. Which of the following findings is most indicative of a peroneus longus rupture?

## Options

1. Avulsion fracture at the base of the 5th metatarsal
2. Tenderness to palpation over the lateral ankle
3. Inability to plantarflex the ankle
4. Proximal migration of the os peroneum
5. Pes planovalgus deformity

## Correct option

**4. Proximal migration of the os peroneum**

## Answer explanation

Proximal migration of the os peroneum is pathognomonic for rupture of the peroneus longus.

An os peroneum is a small accessory bone located at the lateral plantar aspect of the cuboid within the substance of the peroneus longus tendon as it arches around the cuboid. Proximal migration of the os peroneum is seen in the setting of peroneus longus rupture, and is considered pathognomonic by many authors. This finding is best seen on oblique and lateral views, as seen in Illustration A and B. Additionally, comparison views of the contralateral foot may also be obtained to help further evaluate displacement of this os.

*Sammarco et al. review acute and chronic peroneus longus tears.* **They report** that 86% of peroneus longus tears occurred distal to the turn of the cuboid groove, and thus would be distal to the os peroneum, if present. **They conclude** that patients with acute onset of symptoms typically have an improved outcome compared to those with a more chronic presentation.

*Bianchi et al. review imaging and appearance of the os peroneum.* **They report** that the os peroneum can often demonstrate morphologic change coinciding with peroneus longus pathology. **They conclude** that rupture of the peroneus longus tendon may result in proximal migration of the os peroneum.

Illustration A and B are oblique and lateral radiographs, respectively, demonstrating proximal migration of an os peroneum in a patient with a peroneus longus rupture.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The peroneus brevis, not longus, inserts on the base of the 5th metatarsal.
Answer 2: While patients with peroneus longus ruptures may have tenderness over the lateral ankle, this is not as suggestive of rupture as migration of the os peroneum. Lateral ankle tenderness is more likely to be caused by an ankle sprain than peroneal rupture.
Answer 3: The peroneus longus functions as a secondary plantar flexor at the ankle and everts the foot. However, even in the setting of rupture, the gastrocsoleus complex would still function to plantarflex the ankle.
Answer 5: Pes planovalgus deformity most commonly occurs following dysfunction of the posterior tibial tendon, not the peroneus longus.
