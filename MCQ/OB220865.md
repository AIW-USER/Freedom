## Question

A 24-year-old man undergoes wide resection of a proximal tibial sarcoma with cemented endoprosthetic reconstruction and patellar tendon reconstruction using a medial gastrocnemius flap. Two years later, he ambulates independently with mild stair-climbing difficulty, knee flexion to 105°, and an 8° extensor lag. Radiographs show a well-fixed prosthesis and patella alta. What is the patient's expected long-term outcome?

## Options

1. Most patients develop progressive patella alta with >30° extensor lag and frequent need for amputation within 5 years due to local recurrence
2. Over 90% achieve near-normal knee function with complication rates similar to primary arthroplasty
3. Gastrocnemius flap coverage increases mechanical loosening risk compared with direct tendon reattachment
4. Good knee function is common, but deep infection, extensor mechanism failure, and reoperation are significant risks
5. Functional outcomes remain poor in most patients regardless of reconstruction method

## Correct option

**4. Good knee function is common, but deep infection, extensor mechanism failure, and reoperation are significant risks**

## Answer explanation

The patient undergoes limb-preserving sarcoma resection, followed by endoprosthetic reconstruction with extensor mechanism reconstruction using a medial gastrocnemius flap. Most patients achieve >90° flexion and can ambulate independently, but infection and extensor mechanism problems (lag, patella alta, re-rupture) are the most common complications, often requiring future revision (Answer 4).

Long-term complication rates for patellar tendon repair with medial gastrocnemius flap augmentation and cemented endoprosthetic reconstruction after proximal tibial sarcoma resection are substantial. Deep infection rates range from 12% to 19% at 5–10 years, representing the most frequent cause of limb loss and reoperation. Extensor mechanism failure occurs in approximately 3–17% of cases, often requiring secondary procedures. Alternative methods for extensor mechanism reconstruction include the utilization of a polyethylene terephthalate (Tevira) tube, polypropylene (Marlex) mesh, or allograft. Functional outcomes are generally good to excellent in most patients who retain their limb, with median active flexion between 98° and 110°, and extensor lag typically less than 10°. Patella alta develops in up to 70% of patients and is associated with increased extensor lag and slightly reduced functional scores.

*Cipriano et al completed a retrospective review assessing outcomes of tendon repair with gastrocnemius flap augmentation after proximal tibial sarcoma resection.* **They note** restoration of active knee extension and excellent or good range of motion in most patients. However, there are substantial complication rates due to including deep infection and extensor mechanism failure, with 12 out of 16 patients requiring reoperation. **The authors conclude** that gastrocnemius flap augmentation remains a suitable option for extensor mechanism reconstruction in this challenging clinical scenario due to the limited alternatives available.

*Schwartz et al report on the long-term outcomes of cemented endoprosthetic reconstruction of the proximal tibia following tumor resection.* *In a retrospective cohort of 52 patients, the study found that overall prosthesis survival rates at 5, 10, 15, and 20 years were 94%, 86%, 66%, and 37%, respectively, with modular implants demonstrating a trend toward improved longevity compared to custom-designed components.* *Functional outcomes were generally favorable, with a mean Musculoskeletal Tumor Society score of 82% of normal function at the latest follow-up, and* **the authors concluded** that cemented endoprosthetic reconstruction is a reliable method for limb salvage in this setting.

*Grimer et al. retrospectively reviewed 151 patients who underwent endoprosthetic replacement of the proximal tibia following tumor resection over a 20-year period.* **The authors report** that limb-salvage surgery was achieved in 88% of cases, but the procedure was associated with a high rate of complications, including infection (initially 36%, reduced to 12% with medial gastrocnemius flap coverage), implant loosening, and breakage. The probability of requiring further surgical procedures was 70% at ten years, and the risk of amputation was 25%. However, **the authors conclude** that good functional outcomes can be achieved in successful cases.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Most patients do not develop severe progressive patella alta with >30° lag.
Answer 2: Outcomes are not comparable to primary arthroplasty, as complication and reoperation rates are significantly higher.
Answer 3: Use of a gastrocnemius flap does not increase loosening risk.
Answer 5: Functional outcomes are not uniformly poor; many patients achieve satisfactory ambulation with mild limitations, though risks of infection and extensor mechanism issues remain.
