## Question

A multicenter prospective study of 6,300 patients that assesses differences in re-rupture rates after rotator cuff repair with and without 6 weeks of immobilization shows a decreased rate of re-rupture with longer immobilization. A follow-up study done at a single institution of 150 patients fails to show any difference in re-rupture rates based upon the duration of immobilization. If the multicenter trial is assumed to be accurate, which statistical error occurred in the follow-up study?

## Options

1. Alpha
2. Beta
3. Minimal Clinically Important Difference (MCID)
4. Selection bias
5. Statistical Inference

## Correct option

**2. Beta**

## Answer explanation

Type-II errors, or beta errors, occur when the null hypothesis is accepted and should have been rejected. An underpowered study is at risk of this type of error. Power is defined as 1-probability of a type-II (beta error), and this is generally set at a level of 80% for most studies.

A Type II error (beta) is defined as a false negative difference that can occur by detecting no difference when there is a difference or accepting a null hypothesis when it is false and should be rejected. It is calculated as: power = 1 - (type-II error). The clinical significance is that of a study that fails to find a difference, which may be because there actually is no difference or the study is not adequately powered. Conversely, a Type I error (alpha) is defined as rejecting a null hypothesis even though it is true. The clinical significance is by definition, alpha-error rate is set to .05, means that there is a 1/20 chance a type-I error has occurred.

*Lochner et al. reviewed randomized trials involving fracture care to evaluate type-II error rates and study power (1 - beta) for the primary outcomes and to identify whether investigators clearly identified the primary and secondary outcomes.* **They reported** that the mean overall study power among the 117 trials was 24.7% (range, 2% to 99%), and the type-II error rate for primary outcomes was 90.5%. **They concluded** that mean type-II error rates in the orthopaedic trauma trials exceeded accepted standards, and that investigators can reduce type-II error rates by performing power and sample-size calculations prior to conducting a trial.

*Wang et al. reviewed fundamental statistical concepts and methods frequently used in the analysis of randomized trials.* *They reported on descriptive statistics, statistical inferences, techniques for comparing means or proportions from two samples, correlation, and regression analysis methods.* **They concluded** that these methods are associated with pitfalls that are avoidable.
