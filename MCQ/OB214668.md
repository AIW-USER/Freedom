## Answer explanation

The radiographs in Figure D would be the most indicated for a valgus high tibial osteotomy due to the isolated medial arthritis.

A valgus-producing high tibial osteotomy (HTO) is a treatment option in young, active individuals with isolated medial compartment arthritis and localized symptoms. General contraindications to HTO include inflammatory arthritis, degenerative changes in the lateral and/or patellofemoral compartments, range of motion of less than 90 degrees, flexion contracture of greater than 15 degrees, and ligamentous instability. A valgus high tibial osteotomy can be done by a lateral closing wedge, medial opening wedge, or a focal dome osteotomy technique.

*Billings et al. reviewed the outcomes of 64 valgus high tibial osteotomies performed with a calibrated cutting guide and rigid internal fixation.* **They found** that 21 knees went onto have a total knee arthroplasty at an average of 65 months and the remaining 43 had a good or excellent clinical result. The survival rate was 85% at 5 years and 53% at 10 years. **They concluded** that a valgus high tibial osteotomy is a good option for patients that want to continue an active lifestyle.

*Rossi et al. reviewed the role of the high tibial osteotomy (HTO) for the varus knee.* *They discussed the indications and contraindications and pre-operative planning for a HTO.* *They reviewed the medial open wedge, lateral closing wedge, and other less commonly used techniques their outcomes, and complications.* Finally, they reviewed the role of an HTO with varus alignment in cases of instability, chondral damage, and meniscectomy history.

Figure A is an AP and lateral radiograph of isolated lateral compartment arthritis. Figure B is an AP and lateral radiograph of a normal knee. Figure C is an AP and lateral radiograph of isolated patellofemoral arthritis. Figure D is an AP and lateral radiograph of isolated medial compartment arthritis. Figure E is an AP and lateral radiograph of tricompartmental arthritis.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1. Figure A is an AP and lateral radiograph of isolated lateral compartment arthritis, which would be a contraindication to a valgus producing high tibial osteotomy as it would further load the lateral compartment.
Answer 2: Figure B is an AP and lateral radiograph of a normal knee, which would not be indicated for a high tibial osteotomy.
Answer 3: Figure C is an AP and lateral radiograph of isolated patellofemoral arthritis. Isolated patellofemoral arthritis is a contraindication to a high tibial osteotomy.
Answer 5: Figure E is an AP and lateral radiograph of tricompartmental arthritis, which would be a contraindication to a high tibial osteotomy.
