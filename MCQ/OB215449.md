## Question

A 51-year-old male retired from the dockyards because of his persistent elbow discomfort and stiffness. His stiffness and discomfort are so severe that he can no longer perform heavy manual labor and he fills his time day trading cryptocurrencies. He finally has time to see the local hand surgeon who performs a physical exam and reviews his most recent radiographs (Figures A and B). On exam, he has an elbow arc of motion from 50-90 degrees, mild joint effusion, but only mild tenderness. His hand surgeon plans to perform an osteocapsular arthroplasty to address his elbow flexion deformity. What complication, specific to this procedure and patient, will the surgeon discuss with the patient?

## Options

1. Ulnar neuritis
2. Radial neuritis
3. Implant loosening
4. Implant wear and osteolysis
5. Infection

## Correct option

**1. Ulnar neuritis**

## Answer explanation

While an osteocapsular arthroplasty can effectively treat degenerative elbow arthritis in a young male, correction of this degree of flexion contracture carries a risk of post-operative ulnar neuritis.

Elbow arthritis occurs secondary to osteoarthritis, inflammatory arthritis, or it can be post-traumatic. Osteoarthritis is most common among middle-aged, male, laborers on their dominant hand and is associated with osteophytosis, capsular contracture, and loose bodies. Patients often complain of pain at the end range of motion, loss of terminal extension, and catching or locking of the elbow. Up to 50% of patients can have concomitant ulnar neuropathy. Such patients can be candidates for osteocapsular arthroplasty (OCA) which consists of removal of osteophytes on the coronoid, the olecranon, the elbow fossas, followed by a capsular release. Patients with a severely limited pre-operative range of motion (flexion < 100 degrees) are at risk for post-operative ulnar nerve dysfunction.

*Ersen et al. reviewed 5 patients who underwent distraction arthroplasty with interposition of Achilles allograft and collateral ligament reconstruction with follow-up of 87 months.* Post-operatively patients gained 57 degrees of flexion-extension, subjective outcome scores improved tremendously. No patient had elbow instability. **The authors report** that Achilles allograft protects the joint space in the long-term.

O'Driscoll and Blonna describe an 8 step approach for arthroscopic osteocapsular arthroplasty of the elbow to improve motion and reduce pain. First, establish arthroscopic visualization from the posterior compartment. Second, remove loose debris and excise the fat pad. Third, remove osteophytes. Fourth, release the capsule according to the severity of flexion loss. Fifth, establish a view on the anterior side of the elbow. Sixth, strip the anterior capsule. Seventh, remove osteophytes and reshape the coronoid. Eighth, excise the anterior aspect of the capsule. In a retrospective review of 502 patients treated with this technique, only 4.8% of patients experienced a transient nerve injury.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Tension on the radial nerve would decrease with increased elbow extension.
Answer 3: Implant loosening is not possible without an implant.
Answer 4: Implant wear and osteolysis are not possible without an implant.
Answer 5: The infection risk is not particularly more than with any other surgery.
