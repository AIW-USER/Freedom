## Question

A patient undergoes serial casting for the foot abnormalities shown in Figure A and achieves excellent correction. Unfortunately, the patient returns at 22 months of age with a recurrence which will require repeat casting and posteromedial releases. What is/are the risk factor(s) for recurrence in this condition?

![[q212938-a-screen-shot-2019-05-04-at-11-18-24-am.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Noncompliance with brace wear
2. Patient's race
3. Patient's gender
4. Parent's level of education
5. Answers 1 and 4

## Correct option

**5. Answers 1 and 4**

## Answer explanation

Noncompliance with foot abduction orthosis (FAO) and parental education level of high school or below are risk factors for recurrence after correction of idiopathic clubfoot using the Ponseti method.

The Ponseti method of serial manipulation and casting is >90% successful in correcting clubfoot and preventing the need for surgical releases other than achilles tenotomy. After serial casting and tenotomy, the patient is fitted for a foot abduction orthosis (FAO) which is worn for 23 hours per day for the first few months after casting and then nightly until around age 4 year. Compliance with FAO usage is the most important factor in preventing clubfoot recurrence and this is influenced by the parent's education level.

*Goksan et al. reviewed risk factors affecting compliance with FAO usage.* **They found** that bracing difficulties were found in 122/153 patients. The most important variable was the child's adaptation to the orthosis treatment without an adverse reaction. Additionally, better compliance was found in children with higher-educated parents.

*Dobbs et al. reviewed 86 consecutive idiopathic clubfeet in 51 patients treated with the Ponseti casting method.* Noncompliance was the factor most related to the risk of recurrence, with an odds ratio of 183 (p < 0.00001). Parental educational level (high-school education or less) also was a significant risk factor for recurrence (odds ratio = 10.7, p < 0.03).

Figure A is a clinical example of a patient with bilateral clubfeet.
Illustration A is an example of a FAO otherwise known as "boots and bars".

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 2 and 3: Patient race and gender have not been shown to be risk factors for clubfoot recurrence.
