## Question

A 38-year-old non-obese female presents to your clinic with a primary complaint of medial knee pain. Past medical history is significant for psoriatic arthritis. On standing examination, you note varus malalignment compared to the contralateral side. There is no limp or gait abnormality. She extends the knee 10 degrees short of full extension and flexes to 130 degrees. Full length lower extremity radiographs demonstrate 10 degrees of varus malalignment. MRI demonstrates a 1.5 x 1.5cm chondral defect on the medial femoral condyle. You consider recommending a high-tibial osteotomy (HTO) to correct her alignment. Which of the following is a contraindication to HTO in this patient?

## Options

1. Degree of correction necessary
2. Medial femoral condyle chondral lesion size
3. Age
4. Lack of terminal knee extension
5. History of inflammatory arthropathy

## Correct option

**5. History of inflammatory arthropathy**

## Answer explanation

Inflammatory arthritis, in this case psoriatic arthritis, is a contraindication to high tibial osteotomy (HTO) due to high failure rates seen in this population.

HTO is a useful procedure in younger individuals who have unicompartmental arthritis or focal chondral lesions, as it may help to unload the affected compartment. Varus malalignment with medial compartment changes are most commonly seen and can be treated with a lateral closing wedge or medial opening wedge osteotomy. Inflammatory arthritis is a contraindication to HTO given its affect on other compartments, despite their mechanical unloading. Other contraindications include BMI>35, flexion contracture >15 degrees, knee flexion <90 degrees, >20 degrees correction needed, patellofemoral or multicompartmental arthritis, and ligamentous instability on exam.

*Frank et al. review the management of complex cartilage and meniscal pathology in young patients.* **They note** that management often consists of multiple, concomitant procedures that address cartilage lesions, ligamentous insufficiency, and malalignment. They reinforce that all these procedures are necessary in order to protect the long-term integrity of the knee.

*Rossi et al. review HTO as management for varus knee malalignment.* *They discuss multiple surgical indications and mention that both coronal and sagittal alignment must be corrected.* **They feel** that correcting the alignment of the knee is of utmost importance in achieving good results with concomitant procedures and producing satisfactory long-term results.

*Gomoll et al. review a combined technique using meniscal allograft transplantation, chondral repair, and HTO to treat medial compartment arthritis in young patients.* *They included 7 patients and noted significant improvement in knee outcome scores at 2 years post-operatively,* **concluding** that this combination treatment algorithm is effective in managing these difficult clinical scenarios.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: HTO is typically able to correct up to 20 degrees of varus deformity.
Answer 2: The size of associated chondral defect is not an contraindication to HTO, as one of the goals of HTO is to unload this compartment, often in combination with other cartilage preserving procedures.
Answer 3: Patients older than 50-55 are better treated with total knee arthroplasty (TKA) in most cases, however this patient is 38, making her a reasonable candidate for HTO from this perspective.
Answer 4: This patient has a 10 degree flexion contracture, but only those with flexion contractures >15 degrees are thought to be contraindicated.
