## Question

A 27 year-old male suffers a bimalleolar ankle fracture that requires operative fixation of the fibula with a long one-third tubular plate. A subcutaneous structure is found during the proximal dissection of the distal fibula. Which of the following is true of the most likely encountered structure?

## Options

1. Becomes a subcutaneous structure just distal to the fibular neck
2. Has only two terminal branches
3. Provides sensory function to the first dorsal web space of the foot
4. Provides motor function to ankle dorsiflexors
5. Comprised of L4-S2 nerve roots

## Correct option

**2. Has only two terminal branches**

## Answer explanation

During a direct lateral approach of the fibula the superficial peroneal nerve (SPN) may be encountered which has two terminal sensory branches, the medial dorsal cutaneous nerve, and intermediate dorsal cutaneous nerve.

While the lateral approach to the fibula is generally safe, there are several anatomical considerations to be aware of. The peroneal tendons are posterior and just distal to the tip of the fibula. Distally, the sural nerve and the short saphenous vein may be encountered. During proximal dissection, the SPN can be seen crossing the surgical field. The SPN is comprised of L4-S1 nerve roots and branches off the common peroneal nerve at the level of the fibular neck. It travels in the lateral compartment providing motor innervation to the peroneus longus and brevis, both of which assist in the eversion of the foot. The SPN then pierces the deep crural fascia into the subcutaneous tissue and can be seen crossing the surgical field from posterior to anterior about 5 to 7 cm proximal to the tip of the lateral malleolus, with variations described up to 3 to 12 cm from the lateral malleolus. The SPN provides cutaneous innervation to the dorsum of the foot through two terminal branches, medial dorsal cutaneous nerve, and intermediate dorsal cutaneous nerve.

*Redfern et al conducted a retrospective review to investigate the incidence of SPN injury for both nonoperative and operatively treated ankle fractures.* **They found** that patients who underwent ORIF had a higher incidence of SPN injury when compared to the nonoperative group (21% vs 9%, p<0.05). The authors advised that surgeons should take care to not injury the SPN when performing a lateral approach to the fibula.

*Halm et al present two cases of surgically treated ankle fractures that were complicated by SPN injury, as well as reviewing current literature.* The SPN can be found crossing the fibula from posterior to anterior about 5-7 cm proximally from the lateral malleolar tip. **They note** that the incidence of SPN injury is about 10-15% for surgically treated ankle fractures and negatively influences outcomes.

Incorrect Answers
Answer 1: The SPN travels in the lateral compartment and then pierces the deep crural fascia to become a subcutaneous structure in the lower third of the leg.
Answer 3: The deep peroneal nerve provides sensation to the first dorsal webspace.
Answer 4: The SPN provides motor innervation to the peroneus longus and brevis, which function to evert the foot.
Answer 5: The SPN is made up of L4-S1 nerve roots.
