## Question

## Options

1. Her injury occurred in the primary weight bearing surface of the tibial plateau
2. This fracture pattern has a low rate of concomitant soft tissue and/or intraarticular injury
3. A staged procedure is necessary to include urgent external fixation placement
4. Bone grafting with calcium sulfate will be required at the time of definitive fixation
5. She is approximately five times more likely to undergo knee arthroplasty in the future

## Correct option

**5. She is approximately five times more likely to undergo knee arthroplasty in the future**

## Answer explanation

Patients who sustain a tibial plateau fracture have a reported 3-7 times increased risk of undergoing conversion to total knee replacement (TKA) in the future compared to the general population.

Tibial plateau fractures are typically high-energy mechanism injuries in younger patients, as compared to low-energy injuries in elderly cohorts. Displaced, intra-articular tibial plateau fractures are considered a risk factor for subsequent osteoarthritis. It is paramount to ensure that the limb mechanical axis is restored after operative fixation with near anatomic reduction and restoration of normal condylar width. Despite appropriate operative fixation, patients are still at an increased risk of developing osteoarthritis within the knee joint requiring conversion to total knee arthroplasty (TKA). While rates vary among different countries, patients who undergo tibial plateau operative fixation are approximately five times more likely to undergo a total knee replacement to the affected knee compared to the general population.

*Wasserstein and colleagues performed a matched cohort analysis using the Canadian administrative health databases comparing the rates of TKA in patients who underwent tibial plateau operative fixation versus matched general population controls.* **They found** that ten years after tibial plateau fracture surgery, 7.3% of patients had gone onto TKA, which corresponded to a 5.3-times increased rate compared to the general population. The multivariable analysis resulted that older patients (>48yrs), more severe fractures, and increased medical comorbidities were the greatest predictors of subsequent TKA by 10 years follow-up.

*Elsoe and colleagues performed a similar matched cohort study using the Denmark administrative health database.* *With a mean follow-up of 13.9 years,* **they found** that patients with tibial plateau fractures had a 3.5-times higher hazard ratio to undergo TKA and a 5-times higher hazard ratio to undergo knee arthroscopic procedures compared to the general population. **The authors conclude** that these findings highlight the long-lasting risk of chronic knee pain and decreased knee function in patients following tibial plateau fractures.

Figure A shows AP and lateral radiographic imaging of a Schatzker II (split/depression) tibial plateau fracture. Figure B includes coronal plane CT scan imaging showing the extent of the articular surface injury. The patient was placed in a knee immobilizer and underwent open reduction internal fixation two weeks later. Illustration A shows their post-operative imaging with near anatomic restoration of the articular surface and restoration of condylar width. Cancellous bone chips were utilized for backfilling the metaphyseal void after tamping up the articular surface.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1. Her injury occurred along the lateral tibial plateau. The primary weight-bearing surface of the tibial plateau is the medial plateau.
Answer 2. This fracture pattern has a high rate of concomitant soft tissue injuries, particularly lateral meniscal injuries. Some case series have reported lateral meniscal injuries in over 75% of displaced Schatzker II patients.
Answer 3. Not all tibial plateau fractures require external fixation. In length stable fracture patterns (as seen in this patient) where most of the articular surface is intact, patients can be treated with immobilization with a delay in definitive surgery to allow soft tissue swelling to subside. External fixation is not without its own risks and should be intentionally utilized.
Answer 4. Tibial plateau fractures with metaphyseal voids often require bone grafting for structural support. Calcium phosphate and packed cancellous bone chips have high compressive strength, whereas calcium sulfate lacks structural stability.
