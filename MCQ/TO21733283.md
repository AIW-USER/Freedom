Chapter: Nerve Injury MCQs — CPN Palsy

## Question

In a patient of foot drop, examination shows involvement of deep peroneal nerve only. Which will be the ideal procedure for him?

## Options

1. Flexor digitorum longus (FDL) transfer along with the tibialis posterior
2. Anterior transposition of the lateral gastrocnemius muscle along with neurotization of the peroneal nerve
3. Rerouting the peroneus longus
4. Use of foot drop splint

## Correct option

**3. Rerouting the peroneus longus**

## Answer explanation

Answer: 3
In a patient of foot drop when both the anterior and lateral groups of muscle are involved (complete CPN palsy) the choice of motor is the tibialis posterior muscle. This not only removes the unopposed inversion force but also helps to restore dorsiflexion when it is inserted onto the dorsum of the foot. Other choices for motor are flexor digitorum longus (FDL) used along with the tibialis posterior to power the extensor digitorum and hallucis tendons as a double transfer. Anterior transposition of the lateral gastrocnemius muscle along with neurotization of the peroneal nerve is another option which has also been successfully done for CPN palsy.
In patients with isolated deep peroneal nerve involvement, where the peroneus longus and brevis are spared then it is better not to transfer the tibialis posterior. Removal of the tibialis posterior will eliminate the balancing inversion force against the peroneal evertors of the foot which are not paralyzed. Rerouting the peroneus longus is a better procedure in such conditions.
