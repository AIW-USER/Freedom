## Question

A 71-year-old female with osteoporosis suffers a periprosthetic hip fracture and undergoes revision total hip arthroplasty to a proximal femoral replacement (PFR). The patient’s abductors are fixed to the implant, but the surgeon is worried about future instability and opts to use a dual mobility liner. This implant provides greater stability using which of the following mechanisms?

## Options

1. Mechanical constraint of the femoral head within the polyethylene via a locking ring
2. Increasing the arc of motion of the femoral neck before impingement
3. Decreasing the jump distance required for dislocation via a small ceramic inner head ball
4. Increasing the jump distance required for dislocation utilizing a raised lip in the area of concern
5. The cup is angled by 10 degrees to allow adjustment of the version

## Correct option

**2. Increasing the arc of motion of the femoral neck before impingement**

## Answer explanation

Dual mobility implants have a more favorable head-neck ratio and allow for a greater range of motion before impingement and dislocation can occur.

Managing instability in patients undergoing revision total hip arthroplasty (THA) is very challenging. When the soft tissue envelope surrounding the hip joint is deficient or inadequate, revision to a more stable construct is necessary. As such, patients are traditionally treated with either a large femoral head, dual mobility cup, or constrained liner. Large femoral heads increase both the effective arc of motion before impingement and jump distance. Dual mobility liners increase the effective arc of motion before impingement occurs, and have been shown to prevent hip dislocation in high-risk patients with abductor mechanism deficiency. Constrained liners theoretically increase stability through the mechanical constraint of the femoral head within the polyethylene (PE) liner through femoral head over-coverage.

*Heckman et al. reviewed their series of 27 proximal femoral replacements for non-oncologic indications in revision THA.* **They report** that the average number of operations before PFR implantation was 3.1 ± 2.1 (range: 0-7). In their series, three patients had a dislocation, but none had a dual mobility implant in place. **They conclude** that modern PFR is an excellent option for non-oncologic indications during revision THA but special consideration of bearing implant is paramount to outcomes.

*De Martino et al. reviewed their series of 41 PFRs placed in the setting of femoral revision surgery.* **They report** survivorship at five years of 95.1% for revision of the femoral stem for aseptic loosening. **The authors conclude** PFR to have acceptable survivorship in non-oncologic revision hip arthroplasties for severe proximal femoral bone loss and recommend constrained liners to mitigate the risk of instability.

Illustration A: This is a depiction of the effective arc of motion between a standard liner and a dual mobility liner. Compared with standard liners, dual mobility liners allow for an increased arc of motion before impingement and subsequent dislocation.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: Mechanical constraint via locking mechanism describes constrained liners.
Answer 3: Decreasing jump distance would increase the risk of dislocation. Although DM liners have a small ceramic head back they are impacted into a large polyethylene liner effectively creating a large combined diameter and increasing the jump distance required for dislocation.
Answer 4: Lipped liners provide an elevated surface that can be rotated in the direction of instability.
Answer 5: A face-changing liner is angled to allow for version adjustment.
