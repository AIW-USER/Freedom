## Question

A 78-year-old female reports knee pain after a ground-level fall. On examination, there are no wounds and she is neurovascularly intact, however, there is a deformity about the knee. Figure A demonstrates a radiograph obtained in the ED. You review surgical options including ORIF and distal femur replacement. Which of the following is true regarding distal femur replacement compared to ORIF?

![[q219148-a-dff.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Lower rate of return to preoperative ambulatory status
2. Higher reoperation rate
3. Better functional outcomes
4. Better range of motion
5. Similar complication rate

## Correct option

**5. Similar complication rate**

## Answer explanation

Distal femur replacement (DFR) has a similar rate of complications compared to ORIF for periprosthetic distal femur fractures (Answer 5).

Periprosthetic distal femur fractures generally occur in elderly patients with an incidence of 0.3-2.5% with poor bone quality being a strong risk factor. There are several classifications that have been proposed that generally include the location of the fracture, degree of displacement, and stability of components. Treatment options include nonoperative, ORIF with plate or IMN, revision to long stem prosthesis, and distal femur replacement. Nonoperative treatment is uncommon and is generally reserved for non-displaced fractures with stable implants. ORIF with IMN or plate is indicated for displaced fractures with stable implants. Retrograde IMN requires a TKA with an open box design to accommodate the nail. Arthroplasty options such as revising to a long-stem prosthesis or DFR are generally indicated for loose components and/or poor bone stock that precludes adequate fixation. DFR offers early weight-bearing with comparable rates of complication, reoperation, and return to preoperative ambulatory status when compared to ORIF.

*Salazar et al conducted a systematic review to compare patient outcomes following two treatment options for geriatric distal femur fractures, namely primary DFR and ORIF.* *Two studies were Level II or III evidence, and the other 28 studies were Level IV evidence.* **They found** no significant differences in complication rates or knee range of motion between the two treatment strategies. Both ORIF and DFR demonstrated similar overall complication rates and knee range of motion.

*Wadhwa et al conducted a systematic review comparing the complications and functional outcomes between primary DFR and ORIF.* *The included 58 studies comprising 1,484 patients.* **The results showed** that complications and reoperation or revision rates were similar between the DFR and ORIF cohorts. However, the mean knee range of motion was greater in the ORIF group. Notably, the Knee Society Score (KSS) and the return to preoperative ambulatory status were similar between the two treatment methods.

*Quinzi et al performed a systematic review that evaluated treatment outcomes for periprosthetic distal femur fractures using three different approaches: ORIF with periarticular locking plates, retrograde intramedullary nail (IMN), and DFR.* *A total of 52 studies were included, encompassing 1,205 cases for ORIF, 272 cases for IMN, and 353 cases for DFR, with a median follow-up of 30 months.* **They found** no significant differences in major complication rates or reoperation rates among the three groups. However, the DFR group had a higher incidence of deep infection compared to the IMN and ORIF groups, and malunion rates were higher in the IMN group compared to the ORIF group.

Incorrect Answers
Answer 1: Both treatment methods generally have similar rates of returning to preoperative ambulatory status.
Answer 2: Systematic reviews have shown no difference in reoperation rates between DFR and ORIF.
Answer 3: Systematic reviews have demonstrated similar functional outcomes between DFR and ORIF.
Answer 4: Depending on the study referenced, DFR generally has similar or slightly reduced ROM compared to ORIF, but not greater.
