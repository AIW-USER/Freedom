## Question

A previously healthy 51-year-old male presents with 3 months of progressive upper extremity clumsiness and gait instability. He currently smokes a pack of cigarettes a day. Examination reveals a positive inverted brachioradialis reflex and difficulty with toe-to-heel walking. A flexion and extension radiograph is seen in Figure A, and MR image in Figures B. Of the treatment options listed, which treatment would provide the best functional outcomes?

## Options

1. Anti-inflammatory medication and physiotherapy
2. Posterior laminoplasty at C4-7
3. Posterior laminectomy and fusion at C4-7
4. Cervical disk arthroplasty at C5-6
5. C6 corpectomy with anterior plate placement

## Correct option

**4. Cervical disk arthroplasty at C5-6**

## Answer explanation

This patient presents with symptoms of cervical myelopathy. Imaging shows single level disease with no fixed kyphotic deformity. The most appropriate treatment option, from the answers above, would be cervical disk arthroplasty at C5-6.

Cervical disk arthroplasty (CDA) is a potential alternative to anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) in patients with single levels cervical myelopathy. Implants have the potential to preserve motion, avoid limitations of fusion, and allow patients a quicker return to routine activities. A number of randomized controlled trials comparing CDA with ACDF have shown better functional recovery and reduced the risk of re-operations with CDA. Long-term studies are needed.

*In a systematic review of comparative studies, Zindrick et al. analyzed the difference in outcomes between patients undergoing CDA and ACDF.* *In their review,* **they showed** that most studies were inconclusive or unreliable with regards to clinical outcomes, revision and/or complication rates comparing CDA and ACDF. However, a few studies suggested that single level CDA may be superior to fusion in terms of better pain relief and lower neck disability scores.

*Fehlings et al. performed a prospective study to examine the efficacy of surgical decompression in patients with cervical spondylotic myelopathy.* **They found** significant improvement in functional, disability-related, and quality-of-life outcomes at one year after decompressive surgery in these patients. Interestingly, the degree of improvement did not depend on the severity of their preoperative symptoms.

*Upadhyaya et al. looked at cervical single-level disease treated with fusion or disc replacement.* At two years **they found** excellent results in both treatment groups, but arthroplasty was associated with a lower rate of secondary surgery and a slightly higher rate of neurological success at 2 years.

Figure A shows lateral flexion and extension radiographs of the cervical spine. There is no fixed kyphotic deformity. Figure B shows a T2-weighted MRI of the cervical spine showing single level disease at C5-6. Illustration A shows a C5/6 cervical disc arthroplasty.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1,2: Conservative treatment options would be considered in mild disease with no functional impairment.
Answer 3: Multilevel posterior laminectomy and fusion would be most appropriate in multi-level disease with no kyphotic deformity. Posterior cervical approaches have shown to have higher neck disability index.
Answer 5: Multilevel combined anterior and posterior decompression and fusion would be most appropriate in multilevel disease with a kyphotic deformity.
