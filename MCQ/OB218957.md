## Options

1. CSF1
2. FGFR3
3. P53
4. RUNX2/CBFA1
5. This pathology is spontaneous, and not secondary to an underlying genetic aberration

## Correct option

**1. CSF1**

## Answer explanation

This patient has radiographic and intraoperative findings consistent with tenosynovial giant cell tumor (TGCT, formally known as pigmented villonodular synovitis or PVNS). This locally aggressive neoplastic synovial tissue disease is caused by overexpression of the colony-stimulating factor (CSF) 1 gene (Answer 1).

TGCT is characterized by joint effusions, synovial expansion and potential bony erosion. While it is not a true neoplasm, it is locally aggressive and the symptoms associated with synovial hyperplasia can be extremely debilitating to patients. TGCT is rare, and typically affects adults between the ages of 30-40, although it can occur in patients of any age. The anterior knee is the most common site of involvement, but TGCT has been observed in the hip, ankle, shoulder, and elbow. Over expression of the CSF1 gene has been implicated in the development of TGCT. This leads to the formation of clusters of aberrant cells which create focal areas of soft tissue hyperplasia in the synovial cells lining joints.

TGCT can be described as localized or diffuse (Illustration A). It is important to note that recurrence rates following synovectomy for the diffuse subtype are much higher than the localized subtype. Treatment for symptomatic TGCT is operative, although administration of a CSF-1 receptor antagonist (Pexidartinib) was approved in 2019 for use in patients with extensive disease who are unlikely to benefit from surgery. There are high rates of hepatotoxicity with this medication, so it is reserved for functionally debilitating disease in patients who otherwise could not undergo surgery. Since TGCT has a predilection for the anterior knee, synovectomy can typically be performed arthroscopically. The presence of brownish/reddish inflamed synovium in a frond-like pattern of papillary projections represents the hallmark arthroscopic findings in cases of TGCT.

*Bernthal et al. provide a review of the management of TGCT/PVNS.* **The authors note** that arthroscopic and/or open synovectomy remains the gold-standard surgical treatment for TGCT and that historically radiation therapy has demonstrated efficacy as an adjuvant modality. **They highlight** recent investigations into the CSF1 pathway and possible usage of CSFR1 antagonists in certain cases of TGCT. **The authors conclude** by stating that additional research is required with respect to new adjuvant agents in order to identify the most effective, efficient and safe treatment regimen for patients.

*Cannarile et al. provide a review of CSFR1 inhibitors in the field of cancer therapy.* **They note** that these agents are generally well-tolerated by patients and that given the underlying CSF1 overexpression endemic to TGCT, they may serve a valuable role in the treatment of patients with this pathology. **The authors conclude** by highlighting the importance of continued research into this pathway in order to determine the optimal means by which to utilize this therapeutic pathway.

Figure B is an arthroscopic image of the patellofemoral joint demonstrating brownish inflamed synovium in a frond-like pattern of papillary projections, consistent with TGCT/PVNS. Incorrect Answers
Answer 2: a gain of function mutation in FGFR3 is implicated in the development of achondroplasia. Achondroplasia is not associated with atraumatic knee effusions and synovial proliferation.
Answer 3: germline mutations in P53 have been implicated in the development of breast carcinoma, bony sarcomas and soft-tissue sarcomas (i.e osteosarcoma). The patient's radiographic and arthroscopic findings are much more consistent with TGCT/PVNS as opposed to a sarcoma/carcinoma.
Answer 4: mutations in RUNX2/CBFA1 have been implicated in the development of cleidocranial dysplasia, a condition hallmarked by proportionate dwarfism with hypoplastic/absent clavicles.
Answer 5: overexpression of CSF1 has been implicated in the development of TGCT/PVNS.
