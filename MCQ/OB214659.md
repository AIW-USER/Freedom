## Question

A 26-year-old male is involved in a high-speed motorcycle accident. He sustains a grade IIIB open tibia fracture as seen in Figure A. Examination demonstrates intact perfusion to the limb but does show an insensate foot. Which of the following statements is true regarding the management of his injury?

![[q214659-a-screen-shot-2021-04-07-at-7-16-47-pm.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Limb salvage should be attempted, as this improves functional outcomes at 2 years
2. Severity of associated soft tissue injury is most predictive of eventual amputation
3. Early amputation should be considered to allow earlier return to work
4. Limb salvage will improve psychosocial outcomes at 7 years
5. Amputation should be performed given his insensate foot

## Correct option

**2. Severity of associated soft tissue injury is most predictive of eventual amputation**

## Answer explanation

In open tibial shaft fractures, the severity of the associated soft tissue injury is most predictive of eventual amputation.

The Lower Extremity Assessment Project (LEAP) data show that the outcomes of limb salvage and amputation are functionally equivalent at both two and seven years after injury. The severity of soft tissue injury and level of amputation, when necessary, were found to significantly impact outcomes, however there were no differences between limb salvage and amputation groups with regard to physical/psychosocial measures, or return to work time. Of note, absence of plantar sensation at initial evaluation did not show significantly different long term outcomes in the salvage versus amputation groups, which highlights the lack of immediate need for amputation in this setting.

*Bosse et al. performed a prospective trial of 569 patients looking at long-term outcomes between limb reconstruction and amputation in patients with severe leg injuries.* Their primary outcome was the Sickness Impact Profile (SIP) and there were no significant differences in these scores between groups at two years, but patients who underwent reconstruction were more likely to be re-hospitalized. **They concluded** that in patients with significant lower limb injuries, both limb salvage and amputation are reasonable initial treatments, resulting in similar outcomes at two years.

*MacKenzie et al. reviewed specific factors that affected outcomes in LEAP study patients.* *They allude to findings that two- and seven-year outcomes between amputation and limb salvage groups were similar in the LEAP study and state that outcomes may also be influenced by an individual's economic, social, and personal situation.* *They finish by recommending a dedicated framework for decision-making with consideration to the patient's psychosocial situation when deciding between limb salvage and amputation.*

*MacKenzie et al. published an extension of the initial two-year LEAP trial data examining similar outcomes after seven years.* *397 patients were included at this time and* **the authors found** that the severity of the associated soft tissue injury was correlated with a worsening SIP score, however, there were no other significant differences between the limb salvage and amputation groups. **They concluded** that, overall, there was significant long-term disability (~50%), but this was not different between the two cohorts.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Limb salvage and amputation were shown by the LEAP data to have functionally similar outcomes at 2-years, therefore neither limb salvage, nor amputation is definitively indicated for this reason.
Answer 3: Return to work times were not found to be significantly different between limb salvage and amputation groups.
Answer 4: Psychosocial outcomes were not found to be significantly different at seven years between limb salvage and amputation groups.
Answer 5: Insensate foot on initial examination is not an indication for immediate amputation because at 2 years follow-up in the LEAP study, both groups had an equal proportion (55%) of patients with normal plantar sensation and functionally both groups were equivalent.
