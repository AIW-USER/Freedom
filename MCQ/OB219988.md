## Question

A 50-year-old man presents to the clinic after sliding on his knees into home base during a softball match and feeling a pop in his right knee. He is unable to perform a straight leg raise and has a palpable defect at the superior pole of the patella. Quadriceps tendon repair is recommended and the patient inquires about whether or not metal anchors will be used. What can the surgeon tell the patient about transosseous repair techniques compared to suture anchor repair?

## Options

1. Transosseous repair techniques demonstrate decreased gap formation in biomechanical testing
2. Transosseous repair techniques result in increased load to failure in biomechanical testing
3. Transosseous repair techniques have a higher complication rate
4. Transosseous repair techniques are associated with worse patient reported outcomes measures
5. Transosseous repair techniques lead to equivalent functional outcomes

## Correct option

**5. Transosseous repair techniques lead to equivalent functional outcomes**

## Answer explanation

Transosseous repair techniques have been shown to have clinical outcomes comparable to suture anchor repair in the treatment of quadriceps tendon ruptures (Answer 5).

Both transosseous repair techniques and suture anchors can be utilized in the treatment of patellar and quadriceps tendon ruptures. Transosseous techniques typically involve drilling trans-patellar bone tunnels for suture passing and tying, while suture anchor techniques involve the direct placement of anchors in the superior or inferior pole of the patella to facilitate tendon repair. Biomechanical testing has demonstrated that suture anchors result in decreased gap formation under cyclical load, as well as increased overall load to failure. Despite this, clinical outcomes have remained equivalent.

*Onggo et al. performed a systematic review of suture anchor and trans-patellar suture repair techniques for patellar and quadriceps tendon rupture.* *Across 212 knees (114 quadriceps ruptures), suture anchors yielded smaller gap formation, and in 2 of the 5 studies reviewed, higher load to failure.* **The authors suggest** that biomechanically, suture anchor fixation may result in better retention of tendon approximation after repair.

*Mehta et al. performed a systematic review of clinical outcomes following quadriceps tendon repairs utilizing transosseous tunnels versus suture anchors.* **They found** that transosseous tunnel patients had higher ROM after repair, and lower complication rates (however not re-rupture rates). However, **they found** no difference in Lysholm scores between groups. **They suggest** that there is no difference in functional outcomes between techniques, but surgeons must weigh the increased complication rate of suture anchor techniques when choosing a repair strategy.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Suture anchor techniques lead to decreased gap formation in biomechanical testing.
Answer 2: Suture anchor techniques lead to increased load to failure in biomechanical testing.
Answer 3: Transosseous repair techniques have a lower complication rate.
Answer 4: Transosseous and suture anchor techniques have equivalent functional outcomes.
