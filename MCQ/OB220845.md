## Question

A 10-year-old girl presents with chronic medial foot pain, especially after activity, and a noticeable flexible flatfoot deformity. Physical examination reveals tenderness and a prominent bump along the medial midfoot, and the patient has a flexible pes planus with hindfoot valgus. Radiographs confirm the presence of a symptomatic Type II accessory navicular. What is the most appropriate surgical treatment for this patient to address both the symptomatic accessory navicular and the underlying flexible flatfoot deformity?

## Options

1. Flexor Digitorum Longus (FDL) tendon transfer to the navicular
2. Isolated excision of the accessory navicular (Kidner procedure variant)
3. Lateral closing wedge calcaneal osteotomy
4. Medializing calcaneal osteotomy combined with accessory navicular excision and Posterior Tibial Tendon (PTT) reattachment
5. Subtalar arthrodesis

## Correct option

**4. Medializing calcaneal osteotomy combined with accessory navicular excision and Posterior Tibial Tendon (PTT) reattachment**

## Answer explanation

Definitive treatment of a symptomatic accessory navicular in the setting of flexible pes planus requires accessory bone excision, PTT advancement/reconstruction, and correction of the hindfoot valgus with a medializing calcaneal osteotomy.

A symptomatic accessory navicular is frequently associated with flexible pes planus and hindfoot valgus, often due to the accessory bone interfering with the proper insertion and function of the Posterior Tibial Tendon (PTT). Surgical management must address all three components for a durable correction. The medializing calcaneal osteotomy shifts the pull of the Achilles tendon medially, correcting the hindfoot valgus and improving the arch. Excision of the accessory navicular (Kidner procedure) removes the pain source, and reattachment/advancement of the PTT ensures the tendon can effectively support the arch, thereby correcting the overall deformity.

*Vaughan and Singh reviewed cases of ongoing pain and deformity following isolated excision of the accessory navicular (Kidner procedure).* **They noted** that failure often occurs because the underlying, associated pes planus and hindfoot valgus were not simultaneously addressed. **They concluded** that isolated excision of the accessory navicular alone carries a high failure rate if a coexisting foot deformity is present.

*Cao et al. evaluated the use of a medial displacement calcaneal osteotomy combined with posterior tibial tendon reconstruction for flexible flatfoot patients who also had a symptomatic accessory navicular.* **They noted** that this comprehensive approach effectively corrected the hindfoot deformity and provided stable function, leading to excellent clinical outcomes. **They concluded** that medializing calcaneal osteotomy is a vital component of the surgical correction when treating symptomatic accessory navicular in the presence of flexible flatfoot.

*Kim et al. examined the outcomes of revision surgery following a failed isolated Kidner procedure (accessory navicular excision).* **They noted** that the most common reason for failure of the initial surgery was the persistence of the underlying flatfoot deformity. **They concluded** that patients with an accessory navicular and flexible flatfoot require surgery that addresses the osseous deformity in addition to the accessory bone to achieve successful outcomes.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: FDL transfer is a salvage procedure used for PTT rupture or severe insufficiency, not a primary correction for a young patient whose PTT is intact but functionally impaired by the accessory bone.
Answer 2: This is associated with a high rate of failure because it does not correct the underlying flexible flatfoot deformity and hindfoot valgus.
Answer 3: This procedure would push the hindfoot into more valgus (everted) or not sufficiently correct the underlying deformity, which is in the wrong direction for this planus foot.
Answer 5: This procedure sacrifices joint motion and is an overly aggressive intervention reserved for severe, painful, and rigid flatfoot deformities.
