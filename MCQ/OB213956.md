## Question

A 73-year-old male with bilateral hip osteoarthritis also has lower back pain and the x-ray findings seen in Figure A. He has failed conservative measures and you discuss bilateral total hip arthroplasties (THA). In preoperative counseling, you should tell him that he is at elevated risk of which of the following?

![[q213956-a-as-spine.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Abductor lurch
2. Infection
3. Leg length discrepancy
4. Periprosthetic hip dislocation
5. Sciatic nerve palsy

## Correct option

**4. Periprosthetic hip dislocation**

## Answer explanation

Patients with ankylosing spondylitis (AS) undergoing THA are at elevated risk of periprosthetic hip dislocation.

Patients with AS are often HLA-B27 positive and RF negative. Orthopaedic manifestations include bilateral sacroiliitis, progressive spinal kyphotic deformity, cervical spine fractures, and large joint arthritis to the hip and shoulder). They, like patients with lumbar spinal fusions or other L-spine pathology, have an altered spinopelvic relationship. This elevates the risk for prosthetic hip dislocation.

*Gu et al. reviewed radiographs of patients with AS who had undergone THA in order to determine the relationship of pelvic incidence (PI), pelvic tilt (PT), and sacral slope (SS).* **They confirmed** that pelvic incidence is equal to the pelvis tilt plus the sacral slope, and that using the skeletal anatomical landmarks of the pelvis as intraoperative references results in larger acetabular inclination and greater anteversion. **They recommended** preoperative assessment of the relationship of sagittal pelvic/spinal parameters in patients with AS.

*Blizzard et al. reviewed 1006 patients with AS undergoing THA.* **They determined** that AS patients are at increased risk of component breakage, periprosthetic hip dislocation, and THA revision. **They concluded** that patients with AS are at increased risk for complications after THA due to altered biomechanics from a rigid, kyphotic spine.

Figure A is an AP radiograph of the lumbar spine showing ankylosing spondylitis with classic bamboo spine appearance.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: If the lateral approach were used for THA, then this patient would be at elevated risk of abductor lurch. AS alone does not increase the risk of abductor lurch.
Answers 2 and 3: AS is not an independent risk factor for leg length discrepancy or periprosthetic infection.
Answer 5: Pre-existing hip dysplasia is a risk factor for postoperative sciatic nerve palsy while AS has not been associated with sciatic nerve palsies.
