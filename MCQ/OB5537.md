## Question

A 48-year-old female who presents to your office with a slow growing mass over her anterior shoulder. She reports that she has had this mass for over five years without any symptoms until a recent backpacking trip that irritated the mass. She denies any weight loss, fevers or chills. On physical exam the mass is soft, subcutaneous, mobile and non-indurated. MRI is shown in Figure A and excisional biopsy performed. Which of the following histology images would most likely be found?

## Correct option

**3. Figure D**

## Answer explanation

Based on history, clinical exam, and MRI, the diagnosis is likely lipoma, which is histologically characterized by adipose surrounded by bland stroma without any cellular atypia, as shown in Figure D.

*Damron et al. in their instructional course review, outline and summarize soft tissue tumors, both benign and malignant, going over key components in the history, physical and work-up to rule out malignancy.*

*Gaskin et al. reviewed 126 MRIs and corresponding histological reports.* **The authors found** MRI to be highly sensitive in diagnosing well-differentiated liposarcomas and highly specific in diagnosing benign lipomas.

Figure A is an axial MRI cut exhibiting a homogenous, mass consistent with lipoma. Figure B is histology corresponding with myxoid-type liposarcoma. Figure C corresponds to chondrosarcoma. Figure D represents lipoma. Figure E represents osteosarcoma. Figure F represents Ewing's sarcoma.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Figure B is representative of myxoid-type liposarcoma characterized by pleomorphic cells with mitotic cells in a hypercellular environment with interlacing fine vessels.
Answer 2: Figure C is representative of low grade chondrosarcoma, characterized by an unorganized cartilage base with mitotic figures interspersed with some pleomorphic cells
Answer 4: Figure E is representative of osteosarcoma characterized by tumor cells with significant atypia, and produce "lacey" osteoid along with stromal cells that show malignant characteristics with atypia, high nuclear to cytoplasmic ratio, and abnormal mitotic figures
Answer 5: Figure F is a high powered view of Ewing's sarcoma, highlighted by small, round blue cells.
