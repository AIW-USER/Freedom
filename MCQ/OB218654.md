## Question

A 22-month-old ambulatory child comes to ER complaining of left elbow pain following a fall off a chair. He is holding his elbow in a flexed position against his body and refuses to use the arm. The patient had no pain prior to the fall. Physical exam reveals obvious swelling around the elbow but no open wounds, and the patient is neurovascularly intact. Figure A is a radiograph of the child’s elbow. In a normal, healthy child of this age, which of the following is true?

![[q218654-a-gartland1.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Anterior humeral line intersects the middle and posterior 1/3 of the capitellum in roughly equal proportions
2. Anterior humeral line intersecting the posterior 1/3 is always indicative of a displaced fracture
3. Anterior humeral line intersects the anterior 1/3 of the capitellum more often than the posterior 1/3
4. Anterior humeral line intersects the anterior 1/3 of the capitellum more often than the middle 1/3
5. Capitellum is not yet ossified

## Correct option

**3. Anterior humeral line intersects the anterior 1/3 of the capitellum more often than the posterior 1/3**

## Answer explanation

In children younger than 5 years of age, the anterior humeral line (AHL) generally intersects the anterior and middle 1/3 in roughly equal proportions, with a smaller percentage of children having an AHL crossing the posterior 1/3.

The capitellum’s ossific nucleus becomes radiographically apparent beginning around age 12 months. It is eccentrically located within the cartilaginous anlage and undergoes eccentric ossification with increasing age. Because of this eccentric ossification pattern, there is wide variability in the location of the osseous capitellum in children under age of 5 years. The AHL is a commonly utilized line of reference to determine following trauma to the elbow to detect occult supracondylar fractures of the distal humerus - depending on where the AHL intersects the capitellum, the patient in whom a supracondylar humerus fracture is highly suspected may be a candidate for surgical intervention. Most children under 5 years have an AHL that intersects the anterior and middle thirds of the capitellum in roughly equal proportions; however, a small percentage of normal children have an AHL that intersects the posterior third. The variability in where the AHL intersects the capitellum in younger patients should be taken into account when evaluating lateral views of the elbow in the setting of recent trauma. Obtaining contralateral films of the uninjured side can be helpful for surgeons to both 1) determine what is the normal position of the capitellum relative to the AHL, and 2) detect otherwise normally positioned capitella that may be falsely interpreted as flexion- or extension-type supracondylar fractures.

*Herman et al. retrospectively reviewed all lateral elbow radiographs in sixty children that were interpreted as normal by pediatric radiologists for children ranging from four months to nine years of age.* Thirty children ranging in age from four months (the earliest age that the capitellar ossification center was apparent radiographically in our sample) to three years and eleven months comprised one study group. Thirty children ranging in age from four to nine years comprised the second study group. The authors recorded the location of the anterior humeral line within the capitellum on all lateral radiographs of the elbow and found that 40%, 42%, and 18% intersected the anterior, middle, and posterior 1/3 of the capitellum, respectively. They speculated that this variability is a reflection of anatomic variations of the location of the capitellar ossification center with age or technical difficulties in obtaining adequate lateral radiographic views for the younger child.

*Ryan et al. reviewed 124 true lateral elbow radiographs of normal pediatric elbows, and retrospectively identified and examined them for the relationship of the AHL to the capitellum.* The percentage of AHLs falling outside the middle third of the capitellum was compared among different age and sex groups. 100% of children over 5 years of age had their AHL intersect the middle 1/3 of the capitellum. Significant variability was seen in children younger than 5 years of age, however - 25% of patients were found to have an AHL that fell outside the middle 1/3 of the capitellum. For their cohort of patients younger than 2 years of age, 30% intersected the anterior 1/3, 63% intersected the middle 1/3, and only 7% intersected the posterior 1/3.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: In children younger than 5 years of age, the anterior humeral line intersects the middle 1/3 far more often than the posterior 1/3.
Answer 2: Intersection of the anterior humeral line with the posterior 1/3 of the capitellum is a normal radiographic finding in a smaller percentage of patients.
Answer 4: Depending on the literature read, the anterior humeral line intersects the anterior 1/3 either less often or in roughly equal frequency compared to the middle 1/3. However, most data agree that the anterior humeral line does not intersect the anterior 1/3 more than the middle 1/3.
Answer 5: The capitellum ossifies around age 1 year and should be present on a radiograph of a 22-month-old child.
