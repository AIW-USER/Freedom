## Question

A 17-year-old female high school cross-country runner presents with an acute onset of pain in her anterior tibia after increasing her mileage rapidly. Radiographs reveal a non-displaced tibia stress fracture. Her medical history is significant for amenorrhea (no menstrual period for the last 6 months) and a Body Mass Index (BMI) of 17. She reports frequently skipping meals to "improve her running performance." What is the most critical initial step in the management of this patient, given the high suspicion of the Female Athlete Triad?

## Options

1. Initiate treatment with a nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID) and crutches for pain control
2. Prescribe oral estrogen and progesterone supplements to restore menstrual function
3. Refer for multidisciplinary evaluation focused on nutritional and psychological counseling to correct energy deficit
4. Recommend a full 6-week period of complete rest with no physical activity
5. Schedule a Dual-Energy X-ray Absorptiometry (DEXA) scan to determine bone mineral density (BMD)

## Correct option

**3. Refer for multidisciplinary evaluation focused on nutritional and psychological counseling to correct energy deficit**

## Answer explanation

Addressing the underlying low energy availability through nutritional and psychological intervention is the primary treatment for healing stress fractures and preventing future injuries in the Female Athlete Triad (Answer 3).

The patient presents with all components of the Female Athlete Triad: low energy availability (disordered eating/low BMI), menstrual dysfunction (amenorrhea), and impaired bone health (stress fracture). The fundamental cause of all three issues is energy deficit. Correcting this deficit through proper nutritional rehabilitation is the primary treatment, as it restores the hormonal balance required for bone formation and the return of menses. Therefore, the most critical step is a multidisciplinary approach involving a physician, a registered dietitian, and a mental health professional to address the root causes of the energy deficiency.

Daily and Stumbo provided a review of the Female Athlete Triad, outlining its components: amenorrhea, disordered eating, and osteoporosis. They noted that the foundation of the Triad is an energy imbalance (low energy availability), which drives the hormonal and bone deficits. They concluded that treatment is centered on reversing the energy deficit through nutritional and behavioral intervention.

*Mountjoy et al. published the International Olympic Committee (IOC) consensus statement, expanding the concept from the Female Athlete Triad to Relative Energy Deficiency in Sport (RED-S).* **They noted** that low energy availability affects multiple physiological functions beyond the original three, impacting metabolic rate, immunity, and cardiovascular health. **They concluded** that nutritional rehabilitation and correcting the energy deficit are the primary treatments for restoring health and function.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: NSAIDs may impair bone healing, and while crutches provide rest, this does not address the underlying systemic cause of the fracture.
Answer 2: Hormone replacement does not address the underlying energy deficit and is no longer recommended as the primary treatment for functional hypothalamic amenorrhea.
Answer 4: Rest is necessary for fracture healing, but without addressing the nutritional deficit, the underlying cause of low BMD persists, leading to recurrence.
Answer 5: While important for baseline assessment, diagnostic testing should not take priority over initiating the essential nutritional intervention to stop bone loss and promote healing.
