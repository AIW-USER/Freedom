## Question

Which patient uses “Swing through gait” for ambulation? (regional orthopedics- hip and pelvis- clinical examination and gait)

## Options

1. Spinal cord injury with paraparesis
2. Spinal cord injury with quadriplegia
3. AK amputation of one limb
4. BK amputation of one limb

## Correct option

**1. Spinal cord injury with paraparesis**

## Answer explanation

Gait pattern in paraplegics:

- swing-through gait: This is a faster gait, but requires more energy and has a higher risk for falling than the swing-to gait. The crutches are advanced and the legs are swung between and ahead of the crutches. Starts in a Balanced Standing posture, lifts both crutches, and moves them both forward. Patient leans on the crutches and then extends the elbows, depresses and protracts the scapula, and tucks the head, to lift the pelvis and enable the feet to leave the ground. When the heels strike the floor, retracts the scapula, throws the head back, and pushes on the crutches to push the pelvis forward and resume Balanced Standing, in preparation for the next step

- Swing-To Gait - This gait, which is slower but has a smaller risk of falling than the swing-through, is identical to the Swing-Through, except the patient steps to the crutches, and not through them.

![[TO17745650-3.png]]

- Four point gait: starts in a Balanced Standing posture, shifts the weight off one crutch, lifts the unweighted crutch, and moves it forward, while balanced on both feet and one crutch. Shifts the weight off the swing foot (the foot on the side of the body opposite to the crutch that was just moved), presses down on both crutches, elevates the swing side of the pelvis, tucks head down and laterally away from the swing leg, extends the elbow on the swing side to lift the shoulder, and swings (steps) with the swing foot. Lifts the head and retracts the scapula to push the pelvis forward, regains balanced standing posture, repeats the process, and steps with the other leg.
