## Question

A 34-year-old male police officer presents to the clinic after feeling a pop in his right heel while playing pickleball. Plain films reveal no fracture. A physical exam shows ecchymosis around the heel, and a positive Thompson squeeze test is conducted (no plantar flexion with compression of the gastrocnemius/soleus complex). A sagittal image of his ankle MRI is displayed in Figure A. The patient does not engage in competitive sports and has no significant comorbidities. Which of the following is appropriate for recommending surgical intervention using a minimally invasive technique?

![[q220406-a-achilles-midsubstance.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Less overall cost to the healthcare system when compared to functional rehabilitation.
2. Improved functional outcomes when compared to functional rehabilitation.
3. A quicker return to work when compared to functional rehabilitation.
4. A lower rate of re-rupture when compared to rehabilitation.
5. A higher rate of return to sport when compared to rehabilitation.

## Correct option

**3. A quicker return to work when compared to functional rehabilitation.**

## Answer explanation

This patient presents with a mid-substance Achilles tendon tear. Achilles tendon repair with a minimally invasive technique is associated with a lower complication rate and quicker return to work (Answer 3).

An acute mid-substance Achilles tendon tear typically occurs following an eccentric load, and is most commonly seen in men aged 30-40. Functional rehabilitation, which involves functional orthoses, early weight bearing, and an accelerated exercise regime, has been found to have equivalent rates of re-rupture and 1-year outcomes with fewer complications compared to open end-to-end repair techniques. Percutaneous techniques were developed to optimize tendon healing while limiting surgical complications. While there is a higher risk of sural nerve damage, percutaneous repairs have an overall lower risk of complication resulting in surgery than either functional rehabilitation or open repair. Surgical repairs are also associated with a quicker return to work.

*Meulenkamp et al. published a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials of acute Achilles tendon ruptures.* *Nineteen studies involving 1,316 patients were included in the final analysis.* **The authors report** that percutaneous surgical repair is associated with a lower complication rate compared to open repair and functional rehabilitation.

*Hutchison et al. published outcomes from following the Swansea Morriston Achilles Rupture Treatment (SMART) program.* *Over 6 years, a total of 273 patients were treated for acute Achilles tendon ruptures (62 surgical, 211 functional rehabilitation).* **The authors concluded** that open surgical repair had an increased cost with similar re-rupture rates and outcome data compared to the functional rehabilitation group.

*Kadakia et al. published a review article regarding the treatment of acute Achilles tendon ruptures.* **The authors report** functional rehabilitation, open surgery, and minimally invasive surgery have similar re-rupture and 1-year outcome data. **The authors also conclude** that surgical treatment has been shown to provide an earlier return to work.

Figure A shows a sagittal MRI of a mid-substance Achilles tendon tear.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Surgical repairs are associated with increased healthcare costs.
Answer 2: Although some studies find an increase in plantar flexion strength with surgical repair, patient-reported outcomes are consistently equivalent between surgical repair and functional rehabilitation.
Answer 4: Surgical repair and functional rehabilitation have similar rates of re-rupture.
Answer 5: Surgical repair and functional rehabilitation have similar rates of return to sport.
