## Question

A 58-year-old man returns to your clinic complaining of 2 months right-sided buttock and lateral thigh and leg pain. He previously underwent lumbar laminectomy for similar symptoms. Figure A shows his T2 axial MRI sequence obtained prior to his initial surgery. He is scheduled for new MRI tomorrow. If his new MRI shows similar pathology, what is the most appropriate treatment at this time?

![[q3374-a-intraspinal-synovial-cyst.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. NSAIDs and physical therapy
2. Cyst aspiration and epidural steroid injection
3. Revision lumbar laminectomy
4. Revision lumbar laminectomy with right-sided foraminotomy
5. Revision lumbar laminectomy and instrumented fusion

## Correct option

**5. Revision lumbar laminectomy and instrumented fusion**

## Answer explanation

The patient presents with right-sided radicular symptoms in the setting of a recurrent synovial cyst. The most appropriate treatment would be revision laminectomy and instrumented fusion.

Synovial cysts most commonly occur at the L4-5 level secondary to microinstability that leads to extrusion of synovium through the facet capsule. They can be associated with degenerative spondylolisthesis. In patients with radiculopathy, treatment typically consists of lumbar laminectomy and cyst removal. Given the pathophysiology, namely resulting from instability, some surgeons will advocate for fusion even in primary cysts. In the case of a recurrent synovial cyst, revision decompression and fusion is indicated.

*Lyons et al. retrospectively reviewed 194 patients with symptomatic lumbar synovial cysts who underwent surgical treatment.* Over 90% of patients reported good pain relief. Concomitant fusion was performed in 18 cases that showed overt evidence of instability.

*Khan et al. retrospectively reviewed 39 patients with lumbar synovial cysts treated with surgery.* **They found** that more than 80% of patients had an excellent or very good outcome. **They recommend** decompression with or without fusion for surgical management of this condition.

*Xu et al. retrospectively reviewed 167 patients who underwent surgical treatment of intraspinal synovial cysts.* **They found** an increased risk of recurrent synovial cyst and back pain in patients undergoing laminectomy compared to those who undergo laminectomy and instrumented fusion. The risk of recurrence or back pain was reduced to baseline levels in the subgroup of patients who underwent fusion during revision surgery.

Figure A is a T2 axial MRI sequence showing a right-sided facet cyst at the L4-5 level.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1 and 2: Conservative management is unlikely to improve this patient's symptoms in the setting of a recurrent synovial cyst.
Answers 3 and 4: Decompression alone is appropriate for a first-time synovial cyst without evidence of instability. In the setting of recurrence, fusion is indicated.
