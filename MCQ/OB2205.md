## Question

A local physical therapist refers to you a 17-year-old female with right hip pain that has persisted despite 8 months of physical therapy. She is an avid track runner, however, she has not been able to participate for the past month due to her pain. At first, she found partial relief with naproxen but currently it does not provide symptomatic relief. What is the next best step in further evaluation of this patient?

![[q2205-a-ewing-sarcoma-of-right-hemipelvis.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. No further work up required at this time
2. Right hip arthrogram
3. Pelvic ultrasound
4. MRI of the pelvis
5. Referral to a local chiropractor for manipulation

## Correct option

**4. MRI of the pelvis**

## Answer explanation

Figure A has imaging concerning for malignancy with a peri-acetabular osteolytic process occurring. This patient should undergo pelvic MRI to further characterize the lesion. This particular patient has Ewing sarcoma of her right hemipelvis.

If a lesion concerning for malignancy is identified on Xray, the next step in workup is an MRI. Required staging studies include a bone scan, chest CT and specifically for Ewing Sarcoma is a bone marrow biopsy to look for metastatic lesions. Treatment typically involved chemotherapy with radiation and/or surgical excision depending on the location of the sarcoma. Ewing sarcoma is a small round blue cell sarcoma that often presents in patients in the second to third decades of life. Commonly it presents in the pelvis and diaphysis of long bones. Genetically it is associated with t(11;22) translocation creating a fusion protein EWS-FLI1. A biopsy will demonstrate sheets of small round blue cells with minimal cytoplasm and prominent nuclei. Immunostaining will often show CD99 positivity. Xrays will show a moth-eaten appearance of the bone with associated sunburst pattern periosteal reaction.

*Burchill reviewed molecular abnormalities of Ewing sarcoma and their implications.* **He states** Ewing sarcoma makes up 10-15% of all primary bone tumors, with the primary gene rearrangement being t(11;22) with the next most common being t(21;22). RT-PCR can be used to further break t(11;22) translocations down by specific breakpoints in each protein which is believed to have prognostic value. **He concludes** that further research needs to be completed to identify targets for specific therapies.

*Carvajal and Meyers wrote a very extensive review of Ewing sarcoma and primitive neuroectodermal family of tumors.* *They discuss the clinical features, genetics, treatment options for metastatic and non-metastatic patients, and risk for secondary malignancy and relapse.*

*Thacker et al. reviewed current standards in treatment.* Current chemotherapy treatment can have a 5-year event-free survival of around 60-69%. Radiation therapy with surgery can improve local reoccurrence. Surgical margins should be 2-3cm. Surgical treatment can include en-bloc resection with autograft reconstruction, rotationplasty, allograft, and endoprosthesis reconstruction.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer A: This patient has a concerning lesion on Xray. She requires further workup to identify its etiology.
Answer B: Right hip arthrogram would be beneficial for evaluating soft tissues that may be intra-articular. This patient's lesion is extra-articular.
Answer C: Pelvic ultrasound would be beneficial for the evaluation of intra-abdominal etiologies of pelvic pain, such as the ovaries and uterus.
Answer E: This patient has an osteolytic lesion on Xray. Chiropractic evalaution would likely not provide more information about the concerning lesion.
