## Question

A 13-year-old male with Gross Motor Function Classification System (GMFCS) IV cerebral palsy presents to the ED following a fall from his wheelchair. This is his second such fall in 3 months. His current radiographs are shown in Figure A. He is started on a medication that has the mechanism of action shown in Figure B. ## Options

1. Limb length discrepancy
2. Refracture
3. Nonunion
4. Malunion
5. Joint contracture

## Correct option

**2. Refracture**

## Answer explanation

This nonambulatory child with cerebral palsy (CP) presents with a fragility fracture of the distal femur, seen in Figure A. Studies have shown that administration of pamidronate, a nitrogen-containing bisphosphonate, prevents refractures in children with CP. Nitrogen-containing bisphosphonates inhibit farnesyl pyrophosphate synthase, a key protein of the mevalonate pathway (seen in Figure B), leading to induced osteoclastic apoptosis.

Children with CP are at especially high risk of fragility fractures, due to decreased ambulation, nutritional deficits, anticonvulsant therapy, prolonged immobilization, and presence of contractures. The prevalence varies widely (6-50%) due to fewer fractures in children with less severe disease and more frequent fractures in those with more severe disease, especially in patients with quadriplegic CP. Bisphosphonates have been shown to have a significant clinical benefit in increasing bone mineral density (BMD) and reducing the number of refractures. Children with CP not on bisphosphonate therapy have an annual fracture incidence of 4%, increasing to 7% if a patient has already sustained a previous fracture. Nitrogen-containing bisphosphonates (pamidronate, alendronate) inhibit the mevalonate pathway, leading to osteoclastic apoptosis. Non-nitrogen-containing bisphosphonates (clodronate, etidronate) produce toxic ATP analogs, also resulting in osteoclastic apoptosis. These medications bind to the hydroxyapatite within bony surfaces, especially those actively undergoing osteoclastic resorption, which explains their extremely long half-life. Bisphosphonates also interfere with the osteoclasts’ ability to form their ruffled border, decrease their adherence to the bony surface, and impair their ability to secrete protons for continued bony resorption.

*Bachrach et al. published a 2010 retrospective review of 25 children with spastic quadriplegic (GMFCS IV and V) CP undergoing pamidronate treatment to evaluate the effect of pamidronate therapy on the pre-treatment and post-treatment fracture incidence.* *All patients in the current study received 15 doses over a mean of 13.6 months.* The fracture incidence decreased from 0.34 to 0.1 (p=0.035), which persisted after adjusting for sex. **The authors conclude** that 1 year of treatment may be sufficient for some but not all patients, as Z-scores from DEXA scans of the lateral distal femur returned to baseline within 2 years of cessation of treatment, but only a minority of patients sustained additional fractures following the treatment regimen.

*Harcke et al. published a 2011 retrospective review testing the hypothesis that when fractures occur after cyclic pamidronate, they will be at the proximal or distal end of a pamidronate band, a theoretic stress riser.* *The authors reviewed the charts of 53 children with one or more complete courses of pamidronate treatment (five cycles over a 12-month period) and found that 14 of the 53 patients had sustained ≥1 fracture after completing treatment.* *Of the 19 fractures, 15 occurred in the femur and 4 in the tibia, with 63% occurring at the junction with the pamidronate bands.* **The authors conclude** that the current cyclic dosing regimen is prone to creating stress risers between the pre- and post-treatment bone at the pamidronate bands, and propose that alternative dosing regimens might be feasible to reduce that risk.

*Sees et al. published a 2016 retrospective review evaluating the outcomes of pamidronate treatment in children with cerebral palsy (CP) with frequent fractures due to low bone mineral density.* *They reviewed the charts of 32 patients with GMFCS III (2 patients), IV (3 patients), and V (27 patients) who were treated with 5 courses of pamidronate.* **The authors found** that there were 102 fractures (mean duration 2.5 years) pre-treatment and 28 fractures pos-ttreatment, decreasing the fracture rate per year from 2.35 to 0.1. They did not observe any complications (abnormal bone remodeling, osteonecrosis of the jaw) associated with pamidronate treatment.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1, 3, 4, 5: Bisphosphonates have not been shown to prevent limb length discrepancy, nonunion, malunion, or joint contractures in the setting of fractures in children with cerebral palsy.
