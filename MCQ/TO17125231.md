Chapter: General Orthopedics MCQs — Material Properties And Orthopedic Implants

## Question

What is not recommended to reduce the fracture risk associated with stress riser around plate or prosthesis

## Options

1. End screw should be unicortical
2. End screw should be placed in oblique manner
3. Tip of plate for distal fracture fixation should overlap the stem of prosthesis proximally
4. A non locking end screw in an all locking screws construct

## Correct option

**1. End screw should be unicortical**

## Answer explanation

Implant related periprosthetic fracture:

If a plate is used to stabilize the fracture, the fracture risk increases when a gap exists between the plate and the prosthetic stem. As the distance between the end of the fixation plate and the hip or knee stem decreases, the strain in the uninstrumented bone increases during loading, increasing the fracture risk. In the case of locked lateral plates, a distance of less than 8 cm between the top of the plate and the femoral stem significantly decreases the force required to cause a fracture. In both locked and unlocked constructs, extending the plate beyond the tip of the implant significantly reduces the bone strain and fracture risk.

The use of a stemmed total knee implant below a total hip implant produces an uninstrumented segment of bone in the midshaft of the femur that is at risk for an interprosthetic fracture. The risk of fracture between two well-fixed stems depends on multiple biomechanical factors, including the distance between the implants and the quality of cortical bone. Even in weak bone, normal walking does not generate forces capable of causing an interprosthetic fracture. There is some evidence that an interprosthetic distance less than 110 mm may increase the risk of fracture in weak bone under bending loads

End screw related fracture: Periprosthetic fractures also occur at the end of plate constructs. Rates of fractures near the end screw are 1% to 3% for nonlocking screws and 2.6% for locking screws. A plate is a load bearing implant on the surface of the bone and is not aligned with the neutral axis. Bending forces induce a stress riser at the screw hole at the end of the plate, increasing the risk of peri-implant fracture.

- Although a unicortical screw at the end of the plate allows for a more gradual stress distribution, it is not recommended as the use of a unicortical end screw results in lower strength than a conventional bicortical screw.
- The use of a nonlocking screw at the end of an otherwise all-locked construct has been shown to significantly decrease the risk of fracture without affecting axial strength.
- Using an angled screw in both locking and nonlocking applications was shown to decrease pullout strength and end screw fracture risk in strong bone models. Oblique screw places the near and far cortex screw holes at different levels, presumably decreasing the stress concentration at the end of the plate and placing the stress risers generated by the screw holes at different levels in the bone.
- When using hybrid fixation with locked and unlocked screws, unlocked screws are used before locking screws to prevent preloading.

Answer: 1
