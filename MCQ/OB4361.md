## Question

Figure A is an arthrosopic image from a right shoulder in the lateral decubitus position as viewed from the posterior portal. Which one of the following rehabilitation techniques should be avoided in the patient that is 2 weeks post-operative from the surgical repair shown in Figure A?

![[q4361-a-slap-repair.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Active assisted elevation in the scapular plane
2. Passive forearm pronation
3. Passive external rotation at 90 degrees of abduction
4. Open chain passive elbow flexion
5. Passive assisted elevation in the scapular plane

## Correct option

**3. Passive external rotation at 90 degrees of abduction**

## Answer explanation

The clinical presentation is consistent with a shoulder SLAP repair. Passive external rotation at 90 degrees of abduction and resistive biceps exercises should be avoided in this early postoperative period. However, complete immobilization in a sling should also be avoided as passive- and active-assisted elevation in the scapular plane should be encouraged.

SLAP tears present with vague deep shoulder pain, mechanical symptoms, or pain with overhead activity. SLAP tears are often amenable to repair with sutures next to the biceps anchor. The initial passive motion is often limited from 0 to 90 degrees of forward elevation during the first 4 weeks. Active motion is then initiated in a limited arc of motion to re-strengthen the shoulder. Sport specific activities are progressively trained from 8 to 16 weeks post-operatively.

*Shepard et al conducted a biomechanical experiment of 16 cadaveric shoulders.* **They found** that the superior labrum and biceps anchor was significantly weaker when loaded with a posterior vector. This finding is suggests that the superior labrum may be most vulnerable to injury in late cocking.

Figure A is an arthroscopic image of a SLAP repair. The humeral head is seen in the top right, there is a probe in the rotator interval, the biceps tendon in the top left, and there is a white suture posterior and a blue suture anterior to the biceps anchor.
