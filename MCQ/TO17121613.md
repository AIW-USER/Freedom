Chapter: Arthroscopy MCQs — Shoulder Instability[Unidirectional and Multidirectional]

## Question

Thermal capsulorrhaphy is used in

## Options

1. Shoulder instability
2. Adhesive capsulitis
3. Dural tear sealing of spine
4. Stiff elbow

## Correct option

**1. Shoulder instability**

## Answer explanation

Monopolar radiofrequency (RF) thermal capsular shrinkage (thermal capsulorrhaphy) for stabilization of the shoulder joint was introduced clinically in 1996.

Mechanism of action: Basic science studies have showed that joint capsular tissue can be shortened by thermal energy at the temperature range of 65°C to 80°C. Thermal energy causes a significant decrease in the mechanical properties of the tissue, collagen denaturation, and cell necrosis. Thermally treated tissue is repaired by remaining fibroblasts and vascular cells, with subsequent improvement of mechanical properties. The shortened tissue stretches with time if subjected to significant loading immediately after surgery. Leaving viable tissue between heat-treated regions (grid or stripe technique) improves the healing process. Loss of afferent sensory stimulation due to the destruction of sensory receptors in the capsule may also play a role in clinical improvement.

Most studies suggest that the most successful outcomes of this procedure occur in patients with mild instability, unidirectional anterior instability, and thermal procedures in combination with arthroscopic suture or tack repairs of the capsule and/or labrum. Volume reduction of the capsule is achieved with arthroscopic capsular plication (average of 19.0%) and thermal capsular shrinkage (26–36%).

Thermal capsular shrinkage as an isolated treatment for instability has an unacceptably high incidence of failure and recommended only in combination with labral repair for shoulder instability.

Complications: Failure of procedure (most common), post-surgical chondrolysis axillary nerve injury, capsular attenuation.

Answer: 1
