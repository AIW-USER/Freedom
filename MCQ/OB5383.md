## Options

1. Long arm cast immobilization for 2 weeks followed by transition to hinged elbow brace
2. Closed reduction and percutaneous pinning
3. Open reduction internal fixation using brachialis/triceps plane
4. Open reduction internal fixation using triceps/brachioradialis plane
5. Open reduction internal fixation using brachialis/brachioradialis plane

## Correct option

**3. Open reduction internal fixation using brachialis/triceps plane**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a displaced medial epicondyle fracture that is incarcerated in the joint. This would benefit most from open reduction internal fixation (ORIF) using the medial approach to the elbow through the brachialis/triceps plane.

Medial epicondyle fractures comprise 20% of elbow fractures in the pediatric population, 60% of which are associated with elbow dislocation. Surgery is recommended for incarcerated fragments. ORIF with screw fixation is most commonly performed, as it allows anatomic reduction, rigid fixation and early mobilization. A medial approach to the elbow is performed utilizing the internervous plane between the brachialis (musculocutaneous N.) and triceps (radial N.). Additionally, care must be taken to protect the ulnar nerve during this approach.

*Lee et al performed a retrospective study of 25 patients with medial epicondyle fractures who underwent surgical fixation.* There was no significant correlation between outcome and injury mechanism, displacement, time from injury to surgery, dislocation, fixation method or duration of immobilization. Residual radiographic deformity included hyperplasia or hypoplasia of the medial epicondyle, occurring in 11 patients (44%) and 1 patient (4%), respectively. This did not compromise cosmetic appearance or clinical results (pain/function). **The authors concluded** that operative treatment is a suitable choice for managing these fractures in the pediatric population.

*Gottschalk et al authored a review article on medial epicondyle fractures.* Nonsurgical management consists of immobilization in a long arm cast for 2-4 weeks with the elbow flexed to 90°. Absolute indications for surgery include open fractures and fragments incarcerated in the joint. Relative indications include displacement > 5mm, ulnar nerve dysfunction, valgus instability of the elbow and patients with high-demand upper extremity function.

Figure A is a lateral radiograph of the left elbow showing a posterior dislocation with a medial epicondyle fracture. Figure B is a lateral radiograph of the left elbow post-reduction showing the medial epicondyle fracture is incarcerated in the joint. Illustrations A and B are postoperative AP and lateral radiographs, respectively, following ORIF of the medial epicondyle with a single cannulated screw. Illustration C is a figure from the AO Foundation illustrating the medial approach to the elbow. The ulnar nerve runs between the triceps and the medial intermuscular septum and must be identified and protected during this approach.

Incorrect Responses:
Answer 1: Nonoperative management is reserved for minimally displaced fractures (< 5mm) that are not incarcerated in the elbow joint.
Answer 2: Open reduction is recommended to ensure anatomic reduction.
Answer 4: The lateral approach to the elbow through the triceps/brachioradialis plane is commonly used for lateral condyle ORIF but would not allow access to the medial epicondyle.
Answer 5: The anterolateral approach to the elbow through the brachialis/brachioradialis plane would not allow access to the medial epicondyle.
