## Question

A 22-year-old male presents with right shoulder pain that has been worsening over the last 4 months. He reports a history of taking high-dose corticosteroids for graft-versus-host disease. He denies alcohol use. After reviewing his radiographs you diagnose him with avascular necrosis of the humeral head. Which of the following would be most indicated for treatment with a hemiarthroplasty?

## Options

1. Sclerotic lesion of the humeral head with an intact rotator cuff
2. Sclerotic lesion of the humeral head with an irreparable rotator cuff tear
3. Subchondral fracture of the humeral head with an intact rotator cuff
4. Subchondral fracture of the humeral head with an irreparable rotator cuff tear
5. Subchondral fracture of the humeral head and cystic changes of the glenoid with an intact rotator cuff

## Correct option

**3. Subchondral fracture of the humeral head with an intact rotator cuff**

## Answer explanation

Hemiarthroplasty is the treatment of choice in a young patient with an intact rotator cuff and isolated collapse of the humeral head.

Avascular necrosis of the humeral head my occur following high dose corticosteroid use, alcoholism, or a myriad of other etiologies that negatively impact the blood supply to the head. Treatment is similar to that in the hip, and is based on subchondral collapse. Lesions without collapse may be treated with core decompression, whereas those involving collapse of the head require arthroplasty. If the glenoid is not involved and the rotator cuff is intact, hemiarthroplasty is the treatment of choice.

*Franceschi et al review the surgical management of osteonecrosis of the humeral head.* **They report** that core decompression is a safe and effective option for treating low-grade osteonecrosis of the humeral head, while hemiarthroplasty and total shoulder arthroplasty should be considered for high-grade osteonecrosis. **They conclude** that treatment should be based on the presence of subchondral collapse.

*Ristow et al review outcomes of shoulder replacement in humeral head avascular necrosis.* **They report** that at a mean follow-up of 3.9 years (range, 1-8.5 years), all patients who underwent operative intervention for humeral head AVN showed statistically significant improvement in functionality measurements. **They conclude** that both hemiarthroplasty and total shoulder arthroplasty are safe and effective treatments for this disease process.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1&2: Decompression should be performed if there is no evidence of subchondral fracture.
Answer 4: Hemiarthropalsty should not be performed in the setting of an irreparable rotator cuff tear.
Answer 5: Disease involving both the humeral head and the glenoid is best treated by resurfacing both sides of the joint.
