## Question

A 19-year-old military recruit presents to your office with complaints of lateral ankle pain approximately seven centimeters proximal to the ankle joint for the past three weeks. You are concerned that the patient has a fibular stress fracture. Which of the following describes the most likely radiographic findings for this injury at the time of presentation?

## Options

1. Distal fibular avulsion fragment
2. Heterotopic bone formation between the tibia and fibula at the level of the syndesmosis
3. Partially calcified lesion without cortical destruction or periosteal reaction
4. Osteophytic spur formation at the anterior tibial margin
5. Periosteal reaction along the outer aspect of the distal fibular shaft

## Correct option

**5. Periosteal reaction along the outer aspect of the distal fibular shaft**

## Answer explanation

A distal fibular stress fracture would most often present as a periosteal reaction along the outer aspect of the shaft (Answer 5).

Distal fibular stress fractures are common among highly active individuals, including athletes and military recruits. Risk factors, including female sex, rapid changes in training regimen, and advanced age, have been shown to predispose patients to this injury. Treatment of distal fibular stress fractures is largely nonoperative with immobilization and limiting activity. This leads to predictable healing at 6 to 12 weeks from the start of treatment and return to activity at an average of 9 weeks. Most patients with distal fibular stress fractures present with either negative radiographs or a periosteal reaction at the cortex.

*Encinas et al. published a systematic review on distal fibular stress fractures.* *The authors included a total of 3 studies with 45 distal fibular stress fractures.* *All patients in these studies went on to heal with conservative management.* **The authors conclude** that conservative management of distal fibular stress fracture leads to reliable outcomes with return to activity at an average of 9 weeks.

*Takkar et al. conducted a prospective review of military recruits undergoing training.* *They monitored a total of 8794 recruits, of which seven developed distal fibular stress fractures.* The most common radiographic presentation was periosteal reaction, followed by normal radiographs. **They conclude** by stressing the importance of overuse injury prevention in training military recruits.

*Mayer et al. conducted a review on stress fractures of the foot and ankle in athletes.* **The authors conclude** that distal fibular stress fractures as relatively low-risk stress fractures and can be safely treated with partial weight bearing with or without immobilization and gradual return to activity.

Incorrect Answer Choices:
Answer choice 1: A distal fibular avulsion fracture would be reflective of an ankle sprain.
Answer choice 2: This would be indicative of a remote high ankle sprain.
Answer choice 3: This describes an enchondroma.
Answer choice 4: These would be present in cases of anterior ankle impingement.
