## Question

A 19-year-old male college basketball player presents to the emergency department after twisting his ankle during practice. Physical examination reveals an antalgic gait in the exam room. There is moderate soft tissue swelling present along the lateral aspect of the ankle. Range of motion of the ankle elicits pain with inversion and eversion of the hindfoot. There is pain with palpation of the distal fibula and first metatarsal base. Anterior drawer test of the ankle is equivocal. Which physical exam finding is an indication to obtain ankle radiographs?

## Options

1. Ankle swelling
2. Antalgic gait
3. Pain with hindfoot motion
4. Pain with palpation of distal fibula
5. Pain with palpation of first metatarsal base

## Correct option

**4. Pain with palpation of distal fibula**

## Answer explanation

Pain with palpation of the distal fibula is an indication to obtain ankle radiographs in patients who present with an acute ankle injury. Lateral and/or medial malleolar tenderness to palpation is an indication for radiographs based on the Ottawa ankle rules.

*The Ottawa ankle rules were established in 1993 by Ian Stiell and associates to decrease the use of unnecessary radiographs for patients presenting with acute ankle pain.* **They determined** that patients who present with subjective pain in the malleolar region, and point tenderness along the posterior border/tip of the lateral malleolus, medial malleolus, or inability to bear weight immediately and in the ED, should obtain ankle radiographs to rule out a fracture. **They found** the Ottawa ankle rules are 96%-99% sensitive in ruling out an ankle fracture.

*Maffulli et al. reviewed the management of acute and chronic ankle instability.* **They found** that acute ankle injuries are best treated with brief immobilization and functional rehabilitation in most patients, with surgery reserved for high-demand athletes. Chronic ankle instability is multi-factorial and challenging to treat. Patients who fail rehabilitation may benefit from surgical ligamentous repair or reconstruction procedures. **They determined** that acute ankle instability is best managed nonoperatively, whereas ligamentous repair/reconstruction procedures are beneficial in the patient with chronic instability who fails rehabilitation.

*Philbin et al. reviewed peroneal tendon injuries and the role of the peroneal tendons in patients with ankle instability.* **They found** that 40% of patients who sustain an ankle sprain develop subsequent chronic lateral ankle pain. Peroneal tendon pathology, such as tears, subluxation, or dislocation, is one of several causes of chronic lateral ankle pain. **They determined** that proper diagnosis and management of peroneal tendon pathology in the patient with chronic lateral ankle pain is associated with favorable outcomes.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Ankle swelling is not an indication to obtain ankle radiographs
Answer 2: Antalgic gait is not an indication to obtain ankle radiographs. However, an inability to bear weight is an indication for radiographs.
Answer 3: Pain with hindfoot motion may indicate ligamentous injury, but is not an indication for radiographs
Answer 5: First metatarsal base tenderness may indicate peroneus longus injury, but is not an indication for radiographs
