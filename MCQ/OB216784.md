## Answer explanation

Medial subluxation of the biceps tendon is suggestive of a subscapularis tear. The bear hug test (Figure D) tests the integrity of the subscapularis, specifically the upper border.

A subluxated biceps tendon on imaging is highly suggestive of a concomitant subscapularis tear. These tears are not always obvious on MRI, and close inspection of the leading edge/upper border of the subscapularis tendon at the time of arthroscopy is necessary. Excessive passive external rotation, an internal rotation lag sign, or a positive belly press, lift-off, or bear hug test are all suggestive of a subscapularis tear.

*Shi et al. reviewed the relationship between the long head of the biceps tendon (LHBT) subluxation and full-thickness tears of the subscapularis.* **They reported** that LHBT subluxation on MRI was predictive of a full-thickness subscapularis tear with a reported sensitivity of 82%, specificity of 80%, positive predictive value of 35%, and negative predictive value of 97%. **They concluded** that if the LHBT is not subluxated, it is unlikely that a full-thickness tear of the subscapularis tendon is present.

*Koh et al. reviewed the angle between the long head of the biceps tendon (LHBT) and the glenoid during arthroscopic surgery and its correlation with biceps subluxation on magnetic resonance imaging (MRI).* **They report** that in the subluxation group, 85% of patients had a subscapularis tendon tear and all shoulders showed biceps tendon pathology. **They concluded** that shoulders with subluxation of the biceps tendon on the preoperative MRI revealed more pathology in the subscapularis tendon and biceps tendon during arthroscopy.

Figure A is an AP radiograph of the shoulder with no osseous abnormalities. Figure B depicts the relocation test, which is used to evaluate for shoulder instability and can be positive in patients with labral pathology. Figure C depicts the empty can test is used to evaluate supraspinatus pathology. Figure D depicts the bear hug test which is used to evaluate subscapularis pathology. Figure E depicts the Hawkins impingement test. Figure F depicts the Neer impingement test.
