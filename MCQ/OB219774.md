## Question

A 64-year-old male with rheumatoid arthritis presents with Larsen grade 4 arthritic changes of the right elbow. Despite non-operative management, the patient has significant pain and loss of function. He requests surgical intervention and is indicated for a right total elbow arthroplasty. Compared to a triceps-sparing para-olecranon approach, a triceps-off approach has been shown to have which of the following results?

## Options

1. Increased final flexion and extension range of motion
2. A higher likelihood of adequate cement mantle around the ulnar component
3. Delayed initiation of elbow range of motion exercises.
4. Equivalent rate of triceps failure
5. Lower rate of ulnar nerve palsy

## Correct option

**3. Delayed initiation of elbow range of motion exercises.**

## Answer explanation

Total elbow arthroplasty can be performed through three general approaches: triceps-splitting, triceps-reflecting, and triceps-sparing. Patients who undergo a triceps-sparing approach can begin early range of motion, while those who undergo a triceps-reflecting approach require additional time for the triceps repair to heal (Answer 3).

Regardless of implant type, total elbow arthroplasty is performed with a posterior approach to the elbow. The Bryan-Morrey approach was described in 1982 and involves reflecting the anconeus and triceps in a medial to lateral direction, with repair at the end of the case. Although the initial series of 49 patients had no triceps discontinuity or significant weakness, follow-up studies showed a higher rate of triceps failure. Due to concern for post-operative triceps-related complications, triceps-sparing approaches have been developed. While a downside of the triceps-sparing approach is decreased visualization of the ulna, it allows earlier post-operative range of motion because the extensor mechanism remains intact.

*Horneff et al. published a 2019 JAAOS review comparing the three categories of surgical exposure for total elbow arthroplasty.* **They report** the triceps-sparing approach in rheumatoid patients is associated with greater range of motion arc, greater extension torque, and lower risk of triceps rupture.

*Dachs et al. published a retrospective JSES review of 83 total elbow arthroplasty cases,* **reporting that** the cement mantle was adequate in 70% of the triceps-reflecting group and 92% in the triceps-sparing group. **They also reported** a significantly improved elbow range of motion in flexion, extension, and pronation in patients who underwent arthroplasty for inflammatory arthropathy.

*Oizumi et al reported on a case series of 23 patients treated with a triceps-sparing total elbow arthroplasty.* *The patients were followed for a mean of 42 months and no patients developed triceps insufficiency.* **They conclude** that a triceps sparring approach allows for satisfactory alignment, preserved triceps strength, and satisfactory short-term results.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: The triceps-sparing approach is associated with an increased range of motion.
Answer 2: Available literature shows no significant difference in the adequacy of the cement mantle between the two approaches.
Answer 4: The triceps-reflecting approach is associated with a high rate of triceps failure.
Answer 5: The two approaches have no reported difference in ulnar nerve palsy.
