## Question

An 8-year-old male patient presents to your clinic with 2 months of left mid-foot pain that is worse with activity. X-rays are demonstrated in figure A. What is the natural progression of this disease if you do not provide any treatment?

![[q2241-a-kohlers-disease.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Resolution of symptoms by 6 months
2. Progressive collapse of the mid-foot
3. Development of talonavicular osteoarthritis
4. Complete absorption of the navicular
5. Resolution of symptoms in 1-3 years

## Correct option

**5. Resolution of symptoms in 1-3 years**

## Answer explanation

Kohler's disease is a self-limited disease and typically resolves in 1-3 years without intervention.

Avascular necrosis of the tarsal navicular occurs due to being a vascular watershed area. Additionally, the navicular is the last bone to ossify, which increases its susceptibility to mechanical compression and injury. This process is called Kohler's Disease. It is most common in male children 4-7 years old. 25% of the time it is bilateral. It is a self-limited disease without long-term sequelae, even if no treatment is performed. However, immobilization in a short leg walking cast can shorten the amount of time a patient is symptomatic. This is the main treatment if NSAIDs do not provide sufficient symptomatic relief.

*Borges et al. looked at 16 feet with Kohler's Disease.* Short-leg cast immobilization decreased the duration of symptoms, however, type and length of treatment did not affect outcomes. Two feet were symptomatic at the final follow up and found to have a talocalcaneal coalition in one and an accessory navicular in the other. All other patients were asymptomatic. **They conclude** that patients can expect to have a normal foot at adulthood, and if they are persistently symptomatic other causes of foot pain should be investigated.

*Williams et al. reviewed Kohler's Disease detailing the development and vascular pattern of the tarsal navicular, and the natural history of disease progression.* *They discuss 20 patients with an average follow up of 9 1/2 years.* At final follow up all patients were asymptomatic and had reconstitution of the navicular on radiographs. No evidence of arthritis was identified. **They conclude** that the treatment method does not affect final outcome as all patients do well.

Figure A demonstrates bilateral AP weight-bearing radiographs of the patient's feet. You can see the classic sclerotic, flattening and fragmentation of the tarsal navicular of the patient's left foot.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Symptoms typically last longer than 6 months.
Answer 2: No deformities are associated with this disease.
Answer 3: These patients do not develop arthritis
Answer 4: There is reconstitution of the navicular as part of the natural history of the disease.
