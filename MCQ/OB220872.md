## Question

A 56-year-old female with a history of bipolar disorder presents for follow-up after sustaining a left distal radius fracture. Her fracture was subsequently fixed with a volar locking plate. The patient reports that she has been reading about potential postoperative complications and has started taking Vitamin C to prevent the occurrence of one particular complication. Which of the following is most consistent with the complication that the patient is attempting to prophylactically treat?

## Options

1. Development of purulent drainage from the incision.
2. Diminished sensation along the volar aspects of the thumb, index finger, and long finger.
3. Inability to flex the interphalangeal joint of the thumb
4. Pain out of proportion with increased sweating of her left wrist compared to her right.
5. Persistent lucency and pain at the fracture site on radiographs after 1 year.

## Correct option

**4. Pain out of proportion with increased sweating of her left wrist compared to her right.**

## Answer explanation

Prophylactic supplementation with Vitamin C has been shown to lower the postoperative incidence of Chronic Regional Pain Syndrome (CRPS), which may present with pain out of proportion at the site of injury or surgery and increased sweating compared to the contralateral side (Answer 4).

CRPS, also known as reflex sympathetic dystrophy, is a condition characterized by an aberrant inflammatory response, vasomotor dysfunction, and maladaptive neuroplasticity. Patients typically present with pain out of proportion, trophic skin changes, excessive sweating, swelling, and skin discoloration. Two subtypes exist: Type 1, which has no identifiable nerve injury, and Type 2, where a specific nerve injury has been identified. First-line treatment is non-operative with physical therapy and pharmacological modalities. Patients who fail this line of treatment may subsequently require nerve blocks or chemical sympathectomy. Surgical decompression is reserved for patients with Type 2 CRPS who have failed all non-surgical interventions. Several studies have demonstrated that Vitamin C supplementation can prevent the development of this complication, specifically in patients with distal radius fractures and those who have undergone foot and ankle surgery.

*Besse et al. retrospectively compared patients who underwent foot or ankle surgery to determine the impact of daily vitamin C supplementation on the prevention of Type 1 CRPS.* **They found** that 18 of 185 patients who did not receive vitamin C supplementation developed CRPS compared to only 4 of 235 patients who did receive supplementation. Logistic regression analysis revealed a lower risk of developing CRPS in patients who had vitamin C treatment and a higher risk for those with a prior history of CRPS. **They concluded** that vitamin C appeared to be an effective strategy for CRPS prevention in patients who have undergone foot or ankle surgery. However, the exact dosing regimen remains to be determined.

*Hernigou et al. performed a prospective randomized controlled trial assessing the efficacy of vitamin C supplementation in preventing CRPS in patients who underwent foot or ankle surgery.* **They found** that daily 1-gram vitamin C supplementation was associated with a lower odds of developing CRPS compared with controls. **They additionally identified** alcohol abuse and cast immobilization as other factors related to increased CRPS risk. In conclusion, **the authors recommended** daily vitamin C after foot and ankle surgery to mitigate the risk of CRPS development.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Development of purulent drainage from the incision is suggestive of a surgical site infection. Vitamin C supplementation has not been demonstrated to reduce the risk of this complication.
Answer 2: Diminished sensation along the volar aspects of the thumb, index finger, and long finger is suggestive of a median nerve neuropathy. Vitamin C supplementation has not been demonstrated to reduce the risk of this complication.
Answer 3: Inability to flex the interphalangeal joint of the thumb is consistent with a flexor pollicis longus rupture. Vitamin C supplementation has not been demonstrated to reduce the risk of this complication.
Answer 5: Persistent lucency and pain at the fracture site on radiographs after 1 year are more consistent with a nonunion. Vitamin C supplementation has not been demonstrated to reduce the risk of nonunion.
