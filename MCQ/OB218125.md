## Question

A 30-year-old man is involved in a motorcycle accident and is thrown over 20 feet. He sustained a purely ligamentous, unilateral scapulothoracic dissociation without vascular injury. His MRI and physical examination are consistent with a left-sided brachial plexus injury, concomitant Horner syndrome, and a flail arm. His only retained voluntary movement is performing a shoulder shrug. His MRI shows left-sided pseudomeningoceles at the roots of C5-T1. After 3 months of recovery from other injuries, he is indicated to undergo staged peripheral nerve transfers. His peripheral nerve surgeon proceeds with performing an intercostal to musculocutaneous nerve transfer. The resident inquires as to why an ulnar double fascicular to musculocutaneous nerve transfer was not performed. Which of the following principles of nerve transfer procedures does the intercostal to musculocutaneous nerve surgical treatment emphasize?

## Options

1. Donor nerve lies near the target motor end plate
2. Donor nerve is expendable and has functional motor strength
3. Donor nerve functions synergistically with recipient nerve
4. Donor nerve lies near targeted sensory receptors
5. Donor nerve is a pure motor nerve

## Correct option

**2. Donor nerve is expendable and has functional motor strength**

## Answer explanation

This patient sustained a brachial plexus injury that would best be described as a preganglionic, pan-plexal injury with avulsion of the nerve roots of C5-T1. His overall prognosis is poor and any attempts at restoring functionality would require utilizing extra-plexal, functioning donor nerves for transfer procedures (Answer 2).

The vast majority of brachial plexus injuries occur in the setting of obstetric shoulder dystocia and are more often self-limiting within the first few months of the baby’s life. Adult traumatic brachial plexus injuries have drastically different outcomes and almost always require surgical intervention to restore functional movement in the upper extremity. Historically, persistent deficits were treated with either neurolysis or neuroma excision of all affected nerves and sural nerve cable grafting. However, there has been a gradual shift towards treating these patients with peripheral nerve transfers (also referred to as neurotization procedures) with purported improved outcomes when compared to sural nerve grafting. A deep understanding of nerve anatomy and the principles of nerve transfer surgery (Illustration A) are required prior to tackling these procedures. Patients with upper trunk injuries (C5-C6) are often treated with intra-plexal, double fascicular ulnar/median nerve transfers to the musculocutaneous nerve to restore elbow flexion. However, pan-plexal injuries render the ulnar/median nerve useless as donors, as they also lack any motor functionality in this setting. Therefore, extra-plexal nerve transfers are required to obtain functional motor donor nerves. While the intercostal nerves are not ideal donors for the restoration of elbow flexion (they do not in direct proximity to the recipient nerve, lack the synergistic motor function of the recipient nerve, etc), they are functional and expendable donors that have been shown to restore some degree of elbow flexion with inconsequential changes resulting from the donor nerve harvest.

*Acharya and colleagues performed a retrospective review correlating the diagnostic accuracy of MRI for traumatic adult brachial plexus injuries when compared to intraoperative findings.* **They found** that the sensitivity of detecting pre-ganglionic root avulsions was only 39%, however with a specificity of 75%. **They noted** that pseudomeningoceles detected on MRI were highly predictive of root avulsions in surgery with an accuracy of 96%. Conversely, post-ganglionic injury MRI findings displayed a sensitivity of 87% but with a specificity of 26%. **The authors conclude** the importance of correlating MRI findings with the patient’s physical examination, nerve conduction studies, and electromyograms as no study in and of itself serves as the best diagnostic modality.

Colbert and Mackinnon, two well-known pioneers in peripheral nerve transfer surgery, provided a comprehensive review of nerve transfers utilized for brachial plexus reconstruction. They briefly reviewed the important anatomy and implications for surgical options. They emphasize that peripheral nerve transfers effectively reduce high nerve injuries to low nerve injuries, hasten muscle reinnervation, and preserve muscle integrity and function better than that nerve grafting procedures. They conclude that there is still room for improvement, nerve transfers for the treatment of adult brachial plexus injuries have provided the ability to restore functional results for patients in ways not previously achievable.

*Wahegaonkar and colleagues provided a technique guide to performing intercostal nerve harvest and transfer for various neurotization procedures.* **They report** that intercostal nerve transfer displayed superior functional outcomes to that of using the spinal accessory nerve donors for pan-plexal injuries. **The authors conclude** that neurotization using intercostal nerves is a very viable procedure in avulsion brachial plexus injuries, even if there is an ipsilateral phrenic nerve palsy.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The intercostal nerves lie far from the target motor endplates of the musculocutaneous nerve. Double fascicular transfers lie in much closer proximity and serve a synergistic function, hence why it is normally the preferred neurotization procedure for the treatment of upper trunk injuries.
Answer 3: The intercostal nerves serve to contract the intercostal muscles for assistance with respiration. This is not synergistic with the musculocutaneous nerve’s motor functionality of elbow flexion.
Answer 4: Similar to answer 1, the intercostal nerves do not lie in close proximity to the targeted sensory receptors of the musculocutaneous nerve and its terminal branch of the lateral antebrachial cutaneous nerve (LABCN).
Answer 5: The intercostal nerves are not pure motor nerves. They also provide sensation to the anterolateral thoracic and abdominal skin.
