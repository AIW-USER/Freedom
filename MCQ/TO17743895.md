Chapter: FNB 2019 — MCQ 2019

## Question

which one is not true (regional orthopedics- knee- anatomy)

## Options

1. Medial meniscus is larger than lateral meniscus
2. Lateral meniscus covers more articular surface than medial one
3. Type 2 collagen predominates in red-red zone
4. In the medial meniscus the posterior horn is larger than the anterior horn.

## Correct option

**3. Type 2 collagen predominates in red-red zone**

## Answer explanation

Morphological specifications of menisci :

The meniscofemoral ligaments, also known as the Humphrey and Wrisberg ligaments, connect the posterior horn of the lateral meniscus to a location near the insertion site of the posterior cruciate ligament on the medial femoral condyle. Though only 46% of people have both of these ligaments, 100% of people have at least one of them.

The medial meniscus is semicircular, 40 to 45 mm long, approximately 27 mm wide, and covers 51% to 74% of the medial articular surface. The lateral meniscus is shorter at 32 to 35mm and almost circular in shape. The lateral meniscus covers a larger area of the tibial articular surface at 75% to 93%. In the medial meniscus the posterior horn is substantially larger than the anterior horn whereas in lateral meniscus both horns are equal in size.

(Medial meniscus is longer but covers less articular surface, lateral meniscus is shorter but covers more articular surface)

Vascular supply to the menisci originates predominantly from the inferior and superior lateral and medial geniculate arteries via the perimeniscal capillary plexus. Following birth, the menisci become increasingly avascular, so by maturity only the peripheral 10% to 25% of the tissue is perfused.

Within the red-red zone, type I collagen is predominant similar to ligaments, tendons and other fibrous connective tissues. Conversely, the extracellular matrix of the white-white zone contains an abundance of type II collagen fibers, which are more similar to hyaline articular cartilage.

Answer: 3
