## Question

A junior orthopedic surgery resident is performing a study in which a group of patients who experienced one episode of dislocation of their total hip arthroplasty prosthesis could be compared with a group of patients in whom the hip prosthesis did not dislocate over a certain period of time. All patients in the study had their surgery performed during the same period. The goal of the study is to determine which factors are related to the presence of dislocation. What research model best describes this study design?

## Options

1. Case-control study
2. Case series
3. Cross-sectional study
4. Cohort study
5. Randomized clinical trial

## Correct option

**1. Case-control study**

## Answer explanation

This scenario best describes a case-control study. In this model, the study examines the desired outcome in a select group of cases compared with that in a control group and looks for a particular exposure.

A case-control study is defined as a retrospective study in which patient groups are separated by the current presence (cases) or absence (controls) of disease and examined for the prior exposure of interest. As the number of patients is increased there is a lesser chance of observing a random result and therefore is prone to less bias than a study without such a control group. Without controls, the real effect of potential factors could not be identified conclusively because they could be present in subjects who did not experience dislocation.

*Jolles et al. discussed that a case-control study is a retrospective case series to which a control group is added.* *They illustrate the value of a control group.* For example, a group of patients who experienced one episode of dislocation of their THA prosthesis could be compared with a group of patients in whom the hip prosthesis did not dislocate and who had their surgery performed during the same period to determine factors related to the presence of dislocation.

*Busse et al.* **noted** many surgical questions are not amenable to investigation with a randomized controlled trial; thus, investigators must select an appropriate observational study design. Case-control studies are relatively inexpensive and can be conducted in comparatively little time. Although there are important methodological limitations associated with this trial design, case-control studies can provide important insight into the association between one or more exposures and a specific outcome. They are particularly useful when the outcome of interest is rare or when the time to develop the outcome is long. We present an overview of the case-control study, with a focus on trial design and interpretation of results.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: A case series refers to a study involving patients with a single pathology; it does not compare two groups of patients
Answer 3: A cross-sectional study looks at the outcome at the same time as the exposure and is typically descriptive
Answer 4: A cohort study exposes (prospectively or retrospectively) a group of patients with a particular characteristic to treatment, and the outcome is measured
Answer 5: A randomized clinical trial (RCT) is considered the gold standard design for clinical research because randomization remains the only way to minimize selection bias. The control of all potential confounding factors is enhanced and unknown factors will be distributed more equally between the two groups
