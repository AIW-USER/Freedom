## Options

1. Coalition excision and autogenous fat graft placement
2. Open reduction and internal fixation
3. Cast immobilization
4. Continued activity modification
5. Obtain MRI evaluation

## Correct option

**3. Cast immobilization**

## Answer explanation

This patient has an accessory navicular as seen on x-rays. The initial treatment after activity modification has failed is a period of immobilization.

An accessory navicular is an enlargement of the navicular bone plantar medially. It can be either a separate piece of bone or an ossified extension of the navicular. It can be seen in up to 12% of people as a normal variant. In most patient's it is not symptomatic. When symptomatic, however, it presents with medial arch pain in the foot with tenderness in the area. Treatment starts with activity and shoe modification to decrease pressure on the prominence. If these fail, brief cast immobilization can also be successful in treatment. The final step is a surgical intervention with excision of the accessory navicular.

*Drs. Sella, Lawson, and Ogden published in 1986 a case series of 7 patients, aged 12 to 52 years old, who had accessory naviculars that underwent surgical excision.* *All patients had type II naviculars.* *All patients underwent ossicle excision with the repair of posterior tibialis.* Histologically, the excised tissue demonstrated proliferative vascular and cartilage tissues, fibrocartilage, and bone remodeling. **They conclude** that symptoms result from chronic repetitive injury. All seven patients had complete relief of symptoms and return to normal physical activity.

*Bennett et al. performed a retrospective review of 75 feet with accessory naviculars that failed conservative measures.* *The procedure performed was ossicle or protuberance excision with the repair of the posterior tibialis tendon.* *They did not move the insertion of the posterior tibialis tendon.* Overall they had 90% good and excellent results with this technique.

Figure A-C show AP, lateral and oblique Xrays showing a type II accessory navicular. Illustration A shows the Geist classification of accessory naviculars.

Incorrect Answer:
Answer 1: This patient does not have a coalition, but rather a accessory navicular.
Answer 2: ORIF would be appropriate if there were a navicular fracture, however, the imaging shows an accessory navicular.
Answer 4: This patient has failed activity modification after an extended period of time. The next step should be immobilization.
Answer 5: No further imaging studies are needed in this patient's workup.
