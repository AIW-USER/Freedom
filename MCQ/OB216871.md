## Question

You perform a total elbow arthroplasty in a 60-year-old female patient with a chronic history of elbow pain in the setting of rheumatoid arthritis. At her six-week follow-up appointment, she has an inability to extend her elbow. You believe this is secondary to the failure of your soft-tissue repair. What approach did you use for her total elbow arthroplasty?

## Options

1. Tricep-sparing approach
2. Tricep-on approach
3. Bryan-Morrey approach
4. Trans-olecranon approach
5. Alonso-Llames approach

## Correct option

**3. Bryan-Morrey approach**

## Answer explanation

The Bryan-Morrey approach is a triceps reflecting approach and failure of your tricep repair can result in a lack of elbow extension after total elbow arthroplasty (TEA).

Total elbow arthroplasty is a motion-preserving surgery for multiple debilitating elbow pathologies to include inflammatory arthritis, osteoarthritis, and distal humerus fractures. Survivorship for TEAs can be greater than 80% at 10-years, however requires a 5-10 pound lifting restriction for life to prevent implant loosening. Multiple designs exist from unlinked/unconstrained, linked/semi-constrained, and constrained. The best outcomes are with linked/semi-constrained designs.

*Dr. Lee published a review on linked TEAs.* *He discusses the general implant design, indications for arthroplasty, and surgical techniques.* As part of the surgical technique discussion, he explains a tricep reflecting technique to include approach, ulnar and humeral preparation, tricep reconstruction, and closure. Finally, **he concludes** with outcomes broken down by indications, and common complications.

*Voloshin et al. performed a systematic review of complications in total elbow arthroplasty.* *They included 64 studies and 2,983 procedures.* The overall complication rate was 24.3%, with the most common being symptomatic loosening, instability, deep infection, and intraoperative fracture. **They found** a significant difference in aseptic loosening that occurred in 13.7% of linked designs and 10.1% of unlinked components. The overall tricep complication rate was 2.4% between all approaches.

*Kim et al. published a review of complications associated with TEAs.* *In their review,* **they state** implant loosening is the most common complication and occurs in 7-15% of cases. Tricep insufficiency occurs in 3-8% of cases. Overall revision occurs in 19-32% of cases.

Incorrect answer:
Answer 1: A tricep sparing approach retains the tricep insertion on the olecranon.
Answer 2: A Tricep-on approach involves soft tissue releases with dislocation of the elbow joint for preparation for elbow arthroplasty without the release of the tricep from the olecranon. It is not associated with tricep insufficiency.
Answer 4: The question stem states there is a concern for the failure of a soft-tissue repair, hinting to the reader a trans-olecranon approach was likely not used. Furthermore, a trans-olecranon approach should never be used for total elbow arthroplasty as this would jeopardize the stability of the ulnar component. The trans-olecranon approach involves an osteotomy with retention of the triceps insertion on the olecranon and would rely on osseous healing for restoration of elbow extension. It is used to assist articular surface reduction for ORIF of distal humerus fractures.
Answer 5: Alonso-Llamas approach is an example of a triceps sparing approach.
