Chapter: Trauma MCQs — Pediatric Supracondylar Fractures

## Question

Fishtail deformity is seen in

## Options

1. old supracondylar humerus fractures
2. old femur neck fractures
3. old supracondylar femur fractures
4. proximal humerus fractures

## Correct option

**1. old supracondylar humerus fractures**

## Answer explanation

Fishtail deformity is characterized by a central deficiency of the distal humeral epiphysis. This contour abnormality results when the lateral trochlear ossification centre fails to develop or resorbs. The medial ossification centre of the trochlea and the capitellar ossification centre continue to grow normally and the distal humerus assumes an inverted V shape, which resembles that of a fish’s tail.

In the immature distal humerus, the early development of fishtail deformity is difficult to recognize.

The capitellum usually ossifies as a single nucleus by the age of one year. The trochlea ossifies at around the age of nine years (range seven to eleven years). The capitellar and trochlear ossific nuclei coalesce between the age of ten and fourteen years, and subsequently fuse to the metaphysis of the distal humerus by the age of sixteen. Thus, fishtail deformity is difficult to recognize radiographically before about nine years of age, which may lead to a delay in diagnosis.

Fishtail deformity has been described as a complication of any fracture around the elbow in childhood, except an epicondylar fracture. It most commonly follows a supracondylar fracture, but has also been described following lateral condylar fractures, medial condylar fractures and Salter Harris 1 epiphyseal fractures. The patient’s age at the time of the fracture has been reported to range from 13 months to 13 years old.

The blood supply to the trochlea is from two distinct arterioles which are essentially end arterioles. These arise from medial and lateral humeral vessels. The blood supply of the distal humerus suggests that the trochlear groove, trochlear apex and the lateral aspect of the medial crista are potentially vulnerable to disturbances in blood supply and to the development of avascular necrosis. These are the precise areas of the distal humerus that are involved in fishtail deformity. It has therefore been proposed that fishtail deformity is the result of a ‘type A’ avascular necrosis, of the distal humerus, which is the result of injury to the posterior vessels that supply the lateral trochlea. If the lateral trochlear ossification centre development is disturbed and the remaining normal physis continues to grow, the distal humerus assumes a typical V shape: the fishtail

Also know: osteochondrosis of the humeral trochlea is known as Hegemann’s disease.

Answer: 1
