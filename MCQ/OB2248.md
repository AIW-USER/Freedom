## Question

A 17-year-old boy began having right leg pain several days after the start of football practice. The pain is worse at night and not activity-related. He denies any specific injuries. Radiographs were obtained and demonstrate cortical thickening in the proximal medial tibia. A CT scan was subsequently obtained and shown in Figures A and B. Laboratory analysis reveals a normal CBC, ESR, and CRP. You prescribe him an oral medication that greatly improves his symptoms. Which of the following mechanisms of action is responsible for this result?

## Options

1. Cyclooxygenase inhibition
2. Mu-opiod receptor agonist
3. Bacterial protein synthesis inhibition by binding to the 30S ribosomal subunit
4. Transpeptidase inhibition
5. Acetylcholine receptor agonist

## Correct option

**1. Cyclooxygenase inhibition**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a benign appearing bone lesion consistent with an osteoid osteoma. NSAIDs are the first line of treatment for these patients, as they decrease prostaglandin E2 level by inhibiting cyclooxygenase (COX).

Osteoid osteomas are benign bone lesions that most commonly present in young patients (<30-years-old). Patients typically present with localized bone pain that is worse at night and not activity related. The pain from these lesions is due to an increased concentration of prostaglandin E2, which is produced by the COX enzyme. Thus, nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAID), which inhibit COX, are the first line of treatment. For those patients who fail medical management with NSAIDs, percutaneous radiofrequency ablation has a 90% success rate following 1-2 treatments.

*Donahue et al review CT guided percutaneous excision of osteoid osteomas.* *They report on 21 patients in which the nidus was successfully excised using a coaxial technique with a power drill.* Excision of the lesion was confirmed on post-procedure CT scans. **They conclude** that this treatment is an effective and safe method for the treatment of osteoid osteomas.

*Rosenthal et al review radiofrequency coagulation of osteoid osteomas compared with operative treatment.* *They report on recurrence and persistent symptoms in 87 patients who underwent operative excision and 38 patients who underwent percutaneous radiofrequency ablation.* **They conclude** that there was no detectable difference in the rate of recurrence between the two techniques. However, 30% of patients had persistent symptoms following excisions, compared to 23% following radiofrequency ablation.

*Makley et al review the mechanism by which osteoid osteomas produce increase prostaglandin E2.* *They incubated osteoid osteomas in Hank’s medium and were able to measure via radioimmunoassay the amount of prostaglandin E2.* **They conclude** that this increased concentration is the cause of focal bone pain found in patients with osteoid osteomas.

Figure A and B are the axial (A) and coronal (B) cuts of a CT scan demonstrating an eccentrical lesion with a lucent center (nidus) and a sclerotic border consistent with an osteoid osteoma.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: This is the mechanism of action for hydrocodone, which is not indicated for the treatment of osteoid osteomas.
Answer 3: Inhibition of cell wall synthesis by binding to the 30S subunit is the mechanism of aminoglycoside antibiotics and are not indicated in this case.
Answer 4: Penicillin works by inhibiting transpeptidase and is not used for the treatment of osteoid osteoma.
Answer 5: Depolarizing muscle relaxants are acetylcholine receptor agonist, and are not the first line of treatment for osteoid osteoma.
