## Options

1. Observation and close follow up
2. Radiation therapy
3. Intramedullary nail stabilization with cement augmentation and post-operative whole-bone radiation therapy
4. Wide resection and surgical reconstruction
5. Marginal resection with currettage

## Correct option

**4. Wide resection and surgical reconstruction**

## Answer explanation

The clinical presentation is consistent with a dedifferentiated chondrosarcoma. The best treatment option would be wide resection and hip endoprosthetic reconstruction.

Dedifferentiated chondrosarcoma is a rare, highly malignant variant of chondrosarcoma, comprising up to 1 to 10% of all chondrosarcomas. They typically present as isolated lesions. The dedifferentiated component can demonstrate various features, such as osteosarcoma, malignant fibrous histiocytoma(MFH), angiosarcoma, fibrosarcoma, rhabdomyosarcoma, leiomyosarcoma, anaplastic spindle cell sarcoma, and giant cell tumor. Surgical resection with disease-free margins and limb salvage remain a preferred treatment for dedifferentiated chondrosarcoma.

*Albergo et al. examined 39 pathological fractures involving femoral chondrosarcoma.* **They showed** that survival of patients with grade 1 chondrosarcoma with fracture was significantly less than those without fracture (p = 0.02) but there was no difference in those with grade 2-3 (p = 0.49) and dedifferentiated tumours (p = 0.09). The local recurrence rate of the entire series was 27%. Only dedifferentiated chondrosarcomas with an associated pathological fracture had a significantly higher rate of local recurrence.

*Mermerkaya et al. reviewed 21 patients with intramedullary low-grade chondrosarcoma of long bones treated by intralesional curettage and adjuvant therapy comprising high-speed burring, thermal cauterization, and cementation.* *At the average follow-up time point of 58.4 (range, 26-85) months, no patient had developed local recurrence and no distant metastases were observed.*

Figures A and B are plain-film radiographs of the right hip demonstrating a poorly-defined lytic lesion with cortical erosion superimposed on an area of intra-osseous calcification within the proximal femur. This is concerning for a malignant process and is best investigated with advanced imaging. Figures C is a coronal MRI image consistent with an aggressive pervasive lytic lesion within the proximal femur. There is evidence of endosteal scalloping. Figure D is a histologic specimen with the characteristic biphasic appearance of a dedifferentiated chondrosarcoma, with a low-grade chondrosarcoma adjacent to a non-chondroid high-grade spindle-cell sarcoma.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: A dedifferentiated chondrosarcoma requires aggressive treatment. Observation with close follow-up would not be appropriate.
Answer 2: While the high-grade component may be somewhat responsive to radiation, dedifferentiated chondrosarcomas require surgical management.
Answer 3: Intramedullary stabilization would provide stability to the proximal femur but at the expense of disseminating the sarcoma throughout the femur. Radiation therapy is not effective at tumor control.
Answer 5: Dedifferentiated chondrosarcomas require a wide resection with reconstruction. Marginal resection would result in incomplete resection and predispose the patient to a high recurrence rate.
