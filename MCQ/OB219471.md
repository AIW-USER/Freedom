## Answer explanation

This patient suffered a syndesmotic injury which requires anatomic reduction and stabilization. The most accurate reduction of the tibiofibular joint is with direct visualization of the articular surface at the anterior lateral distal tibia and the anterior medial fibula.

Injuries to the syndesmosis occur in 10% of all patients with fractures around the ankle. While there is considerable debate regarding the most appropriate methodology for assessing syndesmotic reduction, it is widely accepted that the quality of the reduction correlates to patient outcomes. To improve the quality and consistency of reduction, several authors have proposed different radiographic and open techniques to assess syndesmotic reduction. The most reliable technique shown for assessing the reduction is direct visualization of the ankle joint at the anterolateral joint line, where the tibia plafond, talar dome, and lateral malleolus meet (Figure D).

*Tornetta III et al reported on a cadaveric study comparing the reduction of the syndesmosis while using either the anterior lateral articular surface of the distal tibia to the anterior medial fibular articular surface or the location of the fibula within the incisura.* *7 surgeons (2 attendings and 5 chief residents) completed a combined total of 70 syndesmotic reductions, divided 50/50 between techniques.* Malreduction was defined as displacement greater than 2mm. The malreduction rate in the incisura alone cohort was 20% while the articular cohort was 7% (p=0.046). **The authors conclude** that direct visualization at the level of the joint is a more accurate visual cue of reduction than visualization of the fibula in the incisura.

*Bejarano-Pineda et al published a review article regarding the diagnosis and management of syndesmotic injuries.* **The authors recommend** weight-bearing films (AP, lateral, and mortise of the ankle) with a low threshold for attaining contralateral comparison views. They also discuss operative evaluation of syndesmotic stability using arthroscopy and stress exams. A Cotton test with diastasis of greater than 3 mm is suggestive of instability. Despite the historical focus on stability in the coronal plane, the sagittal plane has been shown to be more sensitive and specific for diagnosis of instability. Fibular motion greater than 2 mm in the anterior or poster direction while a force is applied suggests instability. The authors do not compare reduction techniques in their paper; however, they do discuss rigid versus dynamic fixation. **They conclude** either option is likely appropriate if the syndesmosis is first anatomically reduced.

Figure A is a right ankle mortise plain film. Figure B is a right ankle AP plain film. Figure C is a right ankle lateral plain film. Figure D is a clinical picture showing anatomic reduction of the articular surface at the anterior medial fibula and the anterior lateral tibial plafond. Figure E is a clinical picture showing the fibula with an arrow pointing to the point where anterior visualization of the fibula in the incisura is seen.

Incorrect answers:
Answers 1,2,3: Radiographic parameters are not as reliable for anatomic reduction as direct visualization at the level of the joint.
Answer 5: Visualization of the fibula in the incisura is not as reliable in ensuring anatomic reduction of the syndesmosis when compared to direct visualization at the level of the joint.
