## Question

Figures A is a lateral radiograph of a 40-year-old male who presents following an ATV accident. He undergoes a successful closed reduction in the emergency department, and post-reduction imaging is shown in Figure B. Which of the following should be recommended for the radial head fracture in this patient?

*Figure A*

![[q214113-b-terrible-triad-ct.jpg]]
*Figure B*

## Options

1. Fragment excision
2. Open reduction and internal fixation
3. Radial head arthroplasty
4. Radial head resection
5. Radiocapitellar arthroplasty

## Correct option

**3. Radial head arthroplasty**

## Answer explanation

This patient sustained a terrible triad injury. Due to the degree of radial head comminution, radial head replacement is indicated.

The degree of fracture displacement, comminution, and radial head involvement directs the treatment of radial head fractures. ORIF has shown to result in worse outcomes in fractures with 3 or more fragments as compared to those with fewer, and as a result radial head arthroplasty is recommended for these cases. In the setting of a terrible triad injury, radial head resection is contraindicated as it may result in instability.

*Heijink et al. reported on the short-term results of 6 patients who were treated by radiocapitellar prosthetic arthroplasty for isolated radiocapitellar degenerative arthritis in the ligamentous-intact elbow.* Several of these patients had been previously treated with ORIF for fractures of the elbow. **They reported** an implant survival rate of 100% with improvements in motion. **The authors concluded** that the short-term follow-up results of radiocapitellar prosthetic arthroplasty for isolated radiocapitellar degenerative arthritis in the ligamentous-intact elbow and forearm seem favorable.

*Acevedo et al. reviewed the use of radial head arthroplasty for the treatment of complex radial head fractures, elbow instability, and arthritic conditions.* **They report** that press-fit devices restore stability and improve pain and motion but are associated with a greater likelihood of implant loosening, leading to revision surgery. **The authors concluded** that adequate knowledge of the surgical indications, types of implants, and surgical technique are essential for a satisfactory outcome when a radial head prosthesis is used for the treatment of nonreconstructable radial head fractures.

Figure A is the radiograph demonstrating a terrible triad elbow injury with radial head fracture, coronoid fracture, and elbow dislocation. Figure B is the CT scan better demonstrating a significantly comminuted radial head fracture. Illustration A is the Mason classification for radial head fractures.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Fragment excision may be appropriate for small radial head fractures that do not contribute significantly to stability and which are otherwise not amenable to ORIF. Due to the severe comminution of the radial head in this patient, this is not an appropriate treatment modality.
Answer 2: ORIF may be appropriate for radial head fractures with a few (<3) larger fragments. However, this patient has significant radial head comminution and >50% joint involvement and would benefit more from a radial head replacement.
Answer 4: Radial head resection is reserved as a salvage treatment typically reserved for the low-demand sedentary patient and is contraindicated in patients with elbow instability, such as terrible triad injuries.
Answer 5: The indications for radiocapitellar replacements are limited, but typically reserved for patients who suffer from post-traumatic or primary radiocapitellar osteoarthritis and who have failed other treatment options.
