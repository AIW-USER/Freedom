## Answer explanation

The appropriate starting point for in situ screw fixation of a slipped capital femoral epiphysis (SCFE) is on the anterior surface of the femoral neck as identified in Figure C.

The most common direction of displacement of a SCFE is with the neck displacing anteriorly while the epiphysis stays within the acetabulum. For in situ fixation, the screw must start on the anterior surface of the neck in order to cross the physis perpendicularly, and enter the epiphysis. Depending on the grade of the slip, this screw position may need to be altered to ensure that the screw can cross perpendicular to the physis and into the epiphysis.

*Aronsson et al. present a review on the current concepts in evaluating and treating a SCFE.* **They note** that single screw in situ fixation has become the most commonly accepted treatment. They describe that the screw should be started on the anterolateral femoral neck and ideally advanced so that 8-10 mm (5 threads) of screw is within the epiphysis.

Figures A, B, and C are an anterior view of a model femur with varying starting points identified. Figures D and E are a posterior view of a model femur with varying starting points identified. Illustrations A and B are radiographs of a patient with a SCFE of the right hip. On these images (particularly the frog leg lateral) you can see that the epiphysis is now posterior to the femoral neck. Illustrations C and D are fluouroscopic images of the same hip following in situ screw fixation. Notice that the screw must be started on the anterior surface of the neck in order to pass perpendicularly to the physis and end centered in the epiphysis.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1, 2, 4, 5: Images A and B are not appropriately positioned anteriorly. As noted above, answer 4 & 5 depict figures D & E which are on the posterior neck.
