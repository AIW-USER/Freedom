## Options

## Correct option

**2. Figure B**

## Answer explanation

Mutations in the tumor suppressor genes EXT1 and EXT2 are responsible for multiple hereditary exostoses (MHE), which is seen in Figure B.

MHE is a disorder characterized by multiple osteochondromas. It is caused by a mutation in the EXT tumor suppresor family of genes. Mutations have been found in both the EXT1 and EXT 2 genes. Individuals with the EXT1 mutation have a more severe presentation than those with a mutation in the EXT2 gene.

*Stieber et al. reviews MHE and reports that linkage analysis has implicated mutations in the EXT gene family.* It is thought this mutation results in an error in the regulation of normal chondrocyte proliferation and maturation that leads to abnormal bone growth. **They report** that patients with MHE have a slight risk of sarcomatous transformation of the cartilaginous portion of the exostoses.

*Pannier et al.* **noted** that HME is a genetically heterogeneous disorder that is associated with mutations in EXT1 or EXT2 genes. EXT genes encode glycosyltransferases, termed 'exostosins', which are involved in the biosynthesis of heparan sulphate.

Figure B shows a typical radiograph of MHE. Notice the pedunculated lesions extending from the surface of bone. The cortex of the lesion is continuous with the cortex of the bone, and the cavity of the lesion is continuous with the medullary cavity of the bone.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Figure A shows cauliflower ears which is a characteristic finding of diastrophic dysplasia. Diastrophic dysplasia is caused by a mutation in the DTDST gene (sulfate transporter protein).
Answer 3: Figure C shows a clinical photo of a child with a dysmorphic face and complex syndactyly consistent with Apert's syndrome. Apert's syndrome is caused by a mutation of the FGFr2 gene.
Answer 4: Figure D shows a radiograph with findings consistent with achondroplasia, including a champagne glass pelvis. Achondroplasia is caused by a mutation in the FGFR3 gene.
Answer 5: Figure E shows a radiograph with findings consistent with cleidocranial dysplasia, a condition characterized by failure of formation of midline structures and absent clavicles. It is caused by a mutation in the RUNX2/CBFA1 gene.
