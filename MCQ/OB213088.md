## Question

A 17-year-old gymnast presents with increasing lower back pain and lower extremity radiculopathy over the course of 1 year. The patient has attempted physical therapy, bracing, and steroid injections but continues to have constant pain. The patient opts to proceed with surgery. Which of the following statements is true?

## Options

1. L5-S1 posterior spinal fusion with instrumentation has the highest fusion rates
2. L4-S1 posterior spinal fusion with instrumentation and spondylolisthesis reduction has the lowest L5 nerve root injuries
3. L5-S1 anterior lumbar interbody fusion has the best functional outcomes
4. L5-S1 transforaminal lumbar interbody fusion has the lowest dural tear rates
5. Surgeon preference with most techniques having similar outcomes

## Correct option

**5. Surgeon preference with most techniques having similar outcomes**

## Answer explanation

The patient is presenting with a high-grade Meyerding 3 isthmic spondylolisthesis with persistent symptoms which requires surgical stabilization and fusion. The optimal surgical technique is surgeon specific with comparative studies reporting similar outcomes.

Isthmic spondylolisthesis is due to disruption of the pars interarticularis, usually at L5, resulting in anterolisthesis of the L5 body on S1. Hyperextension athletes such as weightlifters, football lineman, and gymnasts. However, there is believed to be a genetic component due to the high incidence seen with Inuits. Initial treatment consists of non-operative modalities including bracing, activity modification, physical therapy, and injections. Patients with persistent symptoms may improve with surgical reduction and fusion, however, the literature does not specifically support one technique over another with most techniques having similar outcomes.

*Endler et al. performed a retrospective registry study examining outcomes following fusion for adult isthmic spondylolisthesis.* **The authors found** that there improved short-term outcomes in patients undergoing interbody fusion or posterolateral fusion with instrumentation compared to posterolateral fusion without instrumentation with differences diminishing at 7 years follow-up. There were significantly higher reoperation rates in patients undergoing instrumentation or interbody fusion. **The authors concluded** that instrumentation and interbody fusion techniques do not provide any outcome advantage for patients undergoing surgery for isthmic spondylolisthesis.

*Thirukumaran et al. performed a retrospective study examining national trends in surgical treatment for adult isthmic spondylolisthesis.* **They found** that fusion surgeries were performed 4.33 times more often in 2011 than in 1998 with significant increases in anterior spinal fusions, interbody fusions, and combine anterior-posterior fusions. Complications and hospital charges were reported to be significantly higher with combined anterior-posterior fusions with lower rates seen in females and elective admissions. **The authors concluded** that interbody fusion became the most preferred approach during the study period.

Figures A and B are the standing AP and lateral radiographs of the lumbar spine with Meyerding grade 3 isthmic spondylolisthesis.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: L5-S1 posterior spinal fusion with instrumentation has comparable fusion rates to interbody techniques.
Answer 2: Reduction of a spondylolisthesis can potentially lead to a traction injury of the L5 nerve root.
Answer 3: Interbody techniques have similar outcomes as posterior spinal fusion and combined anterior and posterior spinal fusion.
Answer 4: Complication rates vary between techniques, but transforaminal interbody techniques have been reported to have higher nerve root injuries and dural tears due to the surgical approach and retraction necessary for placement of the interbody device.
