Chapter: Spine MCQs — Spondylolisthesis

## Question

Pelvic incidence correlates with

## Options

1. Sacral slope
2. Optimum level of lumbar lordosis
3. Neck shaft angle of femur
4. Coronal alignment

## Correct option

**1. Sacral slope**

## Answer explanation

Ans A

The relationship between spinopelvic alignment and spondylolisthesis has been an area of intense evaluation in recent years. This is true particularly for dysplastic spondylolisthesis although to an extent also for other types of spondylolisthesis. Hence, an understanding of these relationship between sacropelvic morphology and spinopelvic balance are important in evaluating such cases especially in young adolescents and planning management.

The most important radiographic parameter in this context tends to be Pelvic Incidence (PI) that provides information regarding the sagittal orientation of the pelvis.

Pelvic incidence is defined as the angle between a line perpendicular to the sacral endplate at its midpoint extending caudally and a line joining the midpoint of the sacral endplate to the hip axis

Pelvic incidence (static parameter) = Sacral slope + Pelvic Tilt (dynamic parameters)

Native to understanding of PI is to understand its related parameters also viz. Sacral Slope (SS) and Pelvic Tilt (TT). While PI is a morphologic measurement, meaning it is determined by the individual anatomy and it is not affected by the position of the sacropelvis in space, the measurements of SS and PT are determined by pelvic orientation on a standing lateral radiograph and their values are dependent on the position of the sacropelvis in space.

Sacral Slope (SS) is the angle formed by a line parallel to the sacral endplate and a horizontal line, and Pelvic Tilt (PT) is the angle formed by a vertical line passing through the hip axis and a line from the hip axis to the mid-point of the sacral endplate.

The equation SS +PT=PI is true for a given individual in the standing position.

Pelvic incidence is significantly increased in patients with degenerative spondylolisthesis and dysplastic spondylolisthesis, and the relative increase in pelvic incidence correlates directly with the severity of the slip. The pelvic incidence does increase slightly with growth before stabilizing in adulthood. Patients with spondylolisthesis have higher than normal values and the higher the grade of the spondylolisthesis the higher the pelvic incidence.

Huang et al. found, however, that pelvic incidence does not predict the probability of spondylolisthesis progression. This finding is in contrast to the findings of Enyo et al., who found that increased pelvic incidence was a relative risk factor for the development of degenerative spondylolisthesis in a 15-year longitudinal study of 200 patients. Labelle et al. concluded that because pelvic incidence is a constant anatomic pelvic variable specific to each individual and strongly determines sacral slope, pelvic tilt, and lumbar lordosis, which are position-dependent variables, pelvic anatomy has a direct influence on the development of a spondylolisthesis.
