## Question

A 2-year-old boy presents to the emergency department with his stepfather. The stepfather states the boy fell off a stool at home last night and has not been able to walk or crawl today. Radiographs demonstrate a femoral shaft fracture. Which of the following is the most common presenting finding for child abuse?

## Options

1. A metaphyseal corner fracture
2. Posteromedial rib fractures
3. Scapular body fracture
4. Multiple bruises in various stages of healing
5. Complex head injury in a non-ambulatory child

## Correct option

**4. Multiple bruises in various stages of healing**

## Answer explanation

While all the listed injuries are highly associated with non-accidental trauma (NAT), skin lesions such as bruising in various stages of healing are the most common presenting sign associated with NAT (Answer 4).

Non-accidental trauma is rare but cannot be missed. In fact, physicians are mandated reporters of suspected NAT and can face criminal charges for failure to report. The orthopedic surgeon should, at the very least, be able to identify atypical fracture patterns or red flags from the patient’s history and exam to engage the appropriate social worker or welfare services. Several fractures have been identified with a high specificity of NAT, including high-energy scapula fractures, spinous process fractures, sternal and posteromedial rib fractures, and metaphyseal corner fractures. When a child less than three years of age presents with a femur fracture, NAT should also be considered. Other injury patterns with moderate specificity for NAT include transphyseal distal humerus injuries, digital fractures, and multiple fractures in various stages of healing. Skin lesions, however, are the most common finding in NAT, and a thorough head-to-toe exam is warranted when NAT is suspected. All injuries should be documented in the electronic medical record and photographed.

*Jayakumar et al. reviewed pediatric NAT and specifically focused on the role of the orthopedic surgeon.* *They describe the various classic injury patterns associated with NAT and discuss the assessment and actions that the orthopedic surgeon can take when dealing with NAT.*

*Ranade et al. similarly reviewed the role of the orthopedic surgeon in the work-up and management of NAT.* **They emphasize** the importance of a thorough history and physical exam, looking for inconsistencies with the reported mechanism and the injuries. They also provide recommendations based on guidelines from the American Academy of Pediatrics and Radiology for when to obtain a complete skeletal survey.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1-3, 5: While these injuries are classically associated with NAT, skin lesions such as bruising are the most common presenting finding for NAT.
