Chapter: Arthroscopy MCQs — ACL

## Question

Which is not a risk factor for increased incidence of ACL tear in females? (FNB Pattern Paper 2017)

## Options

1. Small intercondylar notch
2. Increased Q angle
3. Increased external rotation at Landing
4. Females are more quadriceps dominant

## Correct option

**3. Increased external rotation at Landing**

## Answer explanation

Causes of increased incidence of ACL tear in females:

- During decelerating from high-speed females take longer to generate maximum hamstring torque, and have more quadriceps than hamstrings activation than their male counterparts, predisposing them to ACL injury. A male athlete is more likely to use the hamstring to decelerate during running or sprinting.
- Females tend to land from a jump in a more erect position than males (insufficient knee and hip flexion), and also have greater hip internal rotation and hip adduction when decelerating or landing.
- Wider pelvis and greater Q angle
- Greater ligament laxity
- Smaller size of intercondylar notch
- Smaller ACL within the smaller notch
- Estrogen and relaxing hormones may play a role. Although a direct relationship is unlikely, hormones may play a role through the neuromuscular system.

Answer: 3
