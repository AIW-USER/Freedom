## Question

A 59-year-old woman presents for initial evaluation of medial-sided right foot pain that has been present for 18 months and has not responded to extensive physical therapy and ankle foot orthosis treatment. On exam, she is unable to perform a single leg heel raise. She has pes planus with a 'too many toes' sign but her hindfoot is supple. With her talonavicular joint held in a reduced position she can be passively dorsiflexed to negative 10 degrees. This does not correct when her knee is flexed. Her radiographs show arch collapse, with 50% uncoverage of the talus but no subtalar or tibiotalar arthritis. What is the most appropriate stage and treatment for her condition?

## Options

1. Stage I: debridement of the posterior tibial tendon
2. Stage IIA: transfer of flexor digitorum longus (FDL) to the medial navicular with medializing calcaneal osteotomy (MCO)
3. Stage IIB: transfer of FDL to the medial navicular with MCO, lateral column lengthening and tendo-Achilles lengthening (TAL)
4. Stage III: triple arthrodesis and TAL
5. Stage IV: tibiotalocalcaneal arthrodesis

## Correct option

**3. Stage IIB: transfer of FDL to the medial navicular with MCO, lateral column lengthening and tendo-Achilles lengthening (TAL)**

## Answer explanation

This patient has posterior tibial tendon insufficiency Stage IIB, treated with transfer of FDL to the medial navicular with MCO and lateral column lengthening. Physical examination demonstrates an equinus contracture that does not correct with the knee flexed, therefore TAL is indicated.

Reconstruction of Stage IIB deformity typically involves:
1) reconstruction of the arch (FDL transfer to medial navicular)
2) correction of hindfoot valgus (MCO). Although her hindfoot is supple, MCO is performed to protect the soft tissue arch reconstruction and prevent early failure
3) correct forefoot abduction (lateral column lengthening).
4) correct equinus (TAL or gastrocnemius recession).

Several adjunctive procedures are typically performed in reconstruction of stage IIB posterior tibial tendon insufficiency. In particular many patients develop a compensatory forefoot varus or supination in response to collapse of the arch. Once the hindfoot is corrected this can typically be addressed with either a medial cuneiform (Cotton) osteotomy or first metatarsocuneiform arthrodesis (Lapidus).

*Bluman et al. propose a more detailed and comprehensive version of Johnson and Strom's 1989 classification to include more varieties of deformity that may be encountered in posterior tibial tendon insufficiency.* Their classification is detailed in Illustration A.

*Chi et al. review their indications, technique and results for medial and lateral column procedures in the treatment of posterior tibial tendon insufficiency.* Their treatment algorithm and indications are shown in Illustrations B and C.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1. There is no deformity in Stage I disease
Answer 2. There is no forefoot deformity in Stage IIA. This patient has significant talar uncoverage indicating a forefoot abduction deformity.
Answers 4 and 5. This patient has no arthritic findings and the hind foot is supple.
