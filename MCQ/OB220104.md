## Question

A 36-year-old office worker presents to the orthopedic clinic after a snowboard crash where he landed on his left shoulder. He had immediate pain and was evaluated at a local urgent care, with his injury radiograph shown in Figure A. Upon physical exam, he has a prominent left AC joint that can be reduced with direct pressure. You counsel the patient on the risks and benefits of operative and non-operative management. Which of the following statements is most accurate regarding non-operative management of this injury relative to operative?

![[q220104-a-ac-sep.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Higher patient-reported shoulder function scores at 1-year
2. Lower rate of return to former athletic activities at 1-year
3. Better function 6 weeks from date of injury
4. Lower quality of life scores at 1-year
5. Higher risk of adverse events over the first year

## Correct option

**3. Better function 6 weeks from date of injury**

## Answer explanation

This patient presents with an acute grade III AC separation. Several randomized controlled studies have shown greater function 6 weeks out from injury with non-operative management.

AC joint dislocation typically occurs after direct high-energy trauma to the shoulder and is classified according to the Rockwood and Young system (Illustration A). Available literature and expert opinions have reached a consensus that grade I and II injuries can be managed non-operatively; however, there is still significant debate regarding the management of higher-grade injuries. Acute AC stabilization is typically performed in the first 2 weeks. Surgical options include a hook plate, AC joint synthetic cerclage, CC screw, and flexible CC fixation. Recent surgical techniques, including arthroscopic assisted fixation, have improved biomechanical stability and decreased soft tissue dissection. Despite these advances, the available literature reports similar outcomes at 1 year with operative and non-operative treatment.

*Via et al. published a meta-analysis of 5 studies (1 randomized controlled trial and 4 retrospective studies) comparing non-operative to surgical management of acute type III AC joint dislocations.* *There was a wide range of follow-up (29.6-229 months); however, the only significant difference between the 2 groups was the final CC difference.* **The authors recommend** a personalized treatment strategy based on patient education and preference.

*Tamaoik et al. published a Cochran database systematic review of surgical versus non-surgical treatment of AC joint dislocation.* *The final analysis included 5 randomized trials and 1 quasi-randomized trial.* **The authors found** that the 2 treatment cohorts showed no difference in 1-year shoulder function, pain scores, 1-year return to activity, or quality of life scores. However, non-operative management was associated with quicker recovery and a lower complication rate. **They recommend** more trials to evaluate the topic with standardized modern techniques.

Figure A shows bilateral clavicle x-rays with a left grade III AC joint dislocation.

Illustration A displays the Rockwood and Young AC joint dislocation classification system.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Prospective studies show no difference in shoulder outcome scores at 1 year.
Answer 2: Non-operative and operative management have similar return-to-activity rates.
Answer 4: Non-operative and operative management have similar quality of life scores at 1 year.
Answer 5: Operative management is more prone to adverse events, with hardware complications being the most common.
