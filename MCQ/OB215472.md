## Question

A 50-year-old male is undergoing a right primary total knee arthroplasty (TKA). He reports long-standing pain, and has failed conservative measures, including a hyaluronic acid injection 6 months ago and a steroid injection 3 months ago. He has no significant past medical history. Which of the following is true regarding the use of tranexamic acid (TXA) in this patient?

## Options

1. Intravenous dosing leads to a more significant reduction in blood loss if given within 2 hours of incision compared to oral TXA
2. Similar reduction in blood loss with intravenous and oral dosing
3. Direct topical application for at least 2 minutes is more effective than IV dosing
4. Increased risk of DVT with the use of intravenous TXA
5. Increased risk of DVT with the use of PO TXA

## Correct option

**2. Similar reduction in blood loss with intravenous and oral dosing**

## Answer explanation

Tranexamic acid (TXA) is now widely used to reduce blood loss around arthroplasty cases. Oral TXA provides a significant cost savings compared to IV formulations and has been shown to have similar reduction in expected blood loss.

TXA is an antifibrinolytic medication used to reduce blood loss and transfusion requirements following arthroplasty. Route of administration varies with IV, topical, and oral formulations available. With the importance of cost-conscious healthcare coming to the forefront, oral TXA has been found to be significantly cheaper with the same expected reduction in blood loss and transfusion requirements as IV forms. Further, no difference in adverse events, including DVT or intramuscular venous thrombosis, has been identified with various routes of administration.

*Wei et al. review the safety of oral and intravenous administration of TXA for primary TKA and total hip arthroplasty (THA).* **They report** no significant difference in terms of total blood loss, decline in hemoglobin, length of hospital stays, incidence of DVT/PE, or transfusion rates between groups. **They conclude** that oral TXA yields similar outcomes and is much less expensive.

*Kayupov et al review the use of oral and IV TXA during THA.* They report that oral TXA provided equivalent reductions in blood loss at a cost of $14 compared with $47-108 for IV formulations. They conclude that oral TXA should be considered in place of IV formulations, as this change could equate to savings of between $23 and $67 million per year.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Similar reductions in blood loss and hemoglobin are seen with oral and IV TXA.
Answer 3: Topical and IV applications have shown to be equally effective.
Answer 4 & 5: DVT rates are similar with both oral and IV administration of TXA.
