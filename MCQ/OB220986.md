## Question

A 63-year-old presents to the foot and ankle clinic with chronic right foot and ankle pain. On physical examination, the patient is unable to perform a single-limb heel rise and has a loss of the medial longitudinal arch of the foot. What other finding is likely on physical exam?

## Options

1. Hindfoot varus
2. Forefoot adduction
3. Forefoot valgus
4. Hindfoot valgus
5. Forefoot pronation

## Correct option

**4. Hindfoot valgus**

## Answer explanation

Inability to perform a single limb heel rise and loss of the medial longitudinal arch are consistent with adult-acquired flatfoot deformity. The classic deformities associated with adult-acquired flatfoot deformity are hindfoot valgus (answer choice 4), forefoot abduction, forefoot supination, and pes planus.

Adult-acquired flatfoot deformity is most commonly associated with posterior tibial tendon insufficiency (PTTI), which leads to progressive collapse of the medial longitudinal arch (pes planus). PTTI leads to a loss of dynamic stabilization of the medial longitudinal arch, contributing to attritional failure of the static stabilizers (e.g., Spring ligament complex, plantar fascia, etc.). As the disease progresses, patients can develop hindfoot valgus, forefoot abduction, and forefoot supination. Understanding these deformities informs the classification and treatment of adult-acquired flatfoot deformity.

*Myerson et al. reviewed the classification and nomenclature associated with adult-acquired flatfoot deformity.* **They noted** that historically, different names and classification systems have been used. Their consensus group recommended renaming the disease pathology to "progressing collapsing foot deformity," which represents a complex 3D pathology characterized by varying degrees of hindfoot valgus, forefoot abduction, and midfoot varus.

*Johnson et al. evaluated the clinical signs, symptoms, and radiographic findings associated with posterior tibial tendon dysfunction.* **They noted** characteristic signs, symptoms, and radiographic findings that correlate with different disease stages. **They concluded** that understanding the different stages of disease helps to guide treatment.

Incorrect Answers
Answer choice 1: In adult-acquired flatfoot deformity, there is hindfoot valgus, not varus
Answer choice 2: In adult-acquired flatfoot deformity, there is forefoot abduction, not adduction
Answer choices 3 & 5: In adult-acquired flatfoot deformity, there is forefoot varus or supination, not valgus or pronation
