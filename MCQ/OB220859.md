## Question

A 36-year-old male presents for his first postoperative visit following an isolated wide resection for osteosarcoma. Which of the following osteosarcoma subtypes would indicate the patient for wide resection alone?

## Options

1. Conventional intramedullary
2. Periosteal
3. Parosteal
4. Telangiectactic intramedullary
5. Secondary

## Correct option

**3. Parosteal**

## Answer explanation

This 36-year-old male underwent wide resection alone as the definitive treatment of his osteosarcoma. The only subtype that is indicated for this treatment in isolation is the parosteal subtype (Answer 3).

Osteocarcoma is the most common primary bone tumor, accounting for 20% of cases. The disease has multiple subtypes, including conventional intramedullary (most common), telangiectatic, periosteal, and parosteal. While most subtypes require neoadjuvant chemotherapy, wide resection, and adjuvant chemotherapy, parosteal osteosarcoma requires only wide resection alone, as it is considered a low-grade lesion characterized by cellular atypia in the setting of regularly appearing osseous trabeculae (vs. conventional, which exhibits significant cellular atypia intermixed with lacey osteoid matrix).

*Nouri et al. performed a retrospective review examining the outcomes of 18 patients (mean follow-up, 34.5 months) with parosteal osteosarcoma treated at a single institution with surgical resection with or without chemotherapy.* **The authors noted** a poor histological response to chemotherapy in all cases, while 33% experienced local recurrence and 44% had metastasis to the lungs; 33% succumbed within one year, all of which had high-grade lesions. **The authors concluded** that the grade of malignancy, not the location, should determine the treatment.

*Schwab et al. retrospectively reviewed outcomes in 59 patients with low-grade intramedullary or juxtacortical/parosteal osteosarcoma who were treated with wide resection, with or without chemotherapy, at a single institution.* *The patients had an average age of 31, a slight preponderance of males, and a minimum follow-up of one year.* **The authors noted** no differences in outcomes concerning recurrence rates, metastasis, dedifferentiation, and survivorship.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1, 2, 4, and 5: All of these subtypes of osteosarcoma necessitate neoadjuvant chemotherapy, wide resection, and adjuvant chemotherapy.
