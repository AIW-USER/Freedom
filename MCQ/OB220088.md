## Question

A 17-year-old high school quarterback is tackled during the state playoffs and suffers a concussion. He is desperate to return to his team in time to continue playing. Which of the following statements is MOST accurate regarding the diagnosis and management of sport-related concussion (SRC)?

## Options

1. A definitive diagnosis of SRC can be made using helmet-based sensor systems that accurately measure the impact to the brain.
2. The most effective approach for managing SRC involves a prolonged period of strict cognitive and physical rest lasting several weeks.
3. The SCAT5 is a comprehensive diagnostic tool that can be used as a standalone measure for the ongoing management of SRC.
4. While cognitive deficits, balance, and symptoms typically improve rapidly within the first two weeks after a concussion, a small percentage of individuals may experience persistent symptoms.
5. Post-injury neuropsychological (NP) testing is required for all athletes following a suspected SRC to determine return-to-play decisions.

## Correct option

**4. While cognitive deficits, balance, and symptoms typically improve rapidly within the first two weeks after a concussion, a small percentage of individuals may experience persistent symptoms.**

## Answer explanation

While cognitive deficits, balance, and symptoms typically improve rapidly within the first two weeks after a concussion, a small percentage of individuals may experience persistent symptoms (Answer 4).

A sport-related concussion (SRC) is a traumatic brain injury induced by biomechanical forces. It may result from a direct blow to the head, face, neck, or elsewhere on the body with an impulsive force transmitted to the head. SRC typically results in a rapid onset of short-lived impairment of neurological function that resolves spontaneously, but in some cases, signs and symptoms evolve over minutes to hours. The acute clinical signs and symptoms largely reflect a functional disturbance rather than a structural injury, and therefore, no abnormality is seen on standard structural neuroimaging studies. SRC results in a range of clinical signs and symptoms that may or may not involve loss of consciousness, and the resolution of clinical and cognitive features typically follows a sequential course, although symptoms may sometimes be prolonged. So long as symptoms remain, the athlete is ineligible to return to competition. The clinical signs and symptoms cannot be explained by other factors such as drug, alcohol, medication use, other injuries, or other comorbidities. It is important to note that SRC is an evolving injury in the acute phase with rapidly changing clinical signs and symptoms and is considered to be among the most complex injuries in sports medicine to diagnose, assess, and manage.

*This 2017 Concussion in Sport Group (CISG) consensus statement by McCrory et al. updates guidelines for managing sport-related concussions (SRC).* It provides a comprehensive SRC assessment and management overview, including recognition, removal from play, re-evaluation, rest, rehabilitation, referral, recovery, return to sport, and risk reduction strategies. The statement emphasizes a multi-faceted approach involving clinical judgment, standardized assessment tools (like SCAT5), and graded return-to-play protocols. It also highlights the need for further research into long-term effects and effective prevention strategies. The document aims to guide healthcare providers in managing SRC while acknowledging the evolving nature of the field.

*Jackson et al. review the current understanding and management of concussions, also known as mild traumatic brain injuries (mTBI).* It highlights the public health crisis presented by concussions, **emphasizing** the need for rapid identification and appropriate management to minimize long-term consequences, particularly the risk of recurrent concussions. The text details a multidisciplinary approach to diagnosis and treatment, focusing on symptomatic relief and a graduated return to activity protocol for cognitive and physical recovery. The article underscores the importance of differentiating concussion from other conditions with overlapping symptoms and advocates for a cautious approach to return-to-play decisions based on objective assessment and symptom resolution to prevent re-injury.

Incorrect Choices:
Answer 1- While helmet-based systems and other sensors can provide information on head impacts, these systems do not necessarily reflect the impact on the brain itself. The use of helmet-based or other sensor systems to diagnose or assess SRC clinically cannot be supported at this time.
Answer 2- While rest is a part of concussion management, the sources state that prolonged strict rest is not the most effective approach. A brief period of 24-48 hours of rest is recommended, followed by a gradual and progressive return to activity below symptom exacerbation thresholds.
Answer 3- While the SCAT5 is a well-established tool for sideline assessment of SRC, it is designed for rapid screening and is not meant to replace a comprehensive neurological evaluation. It is not a standalone tool for the ongoing management of SRC.
Answer 5- Post-injury NP testing is not required for all athletes. It may be useful, but the ultimate return-to-play decision should remain a clinical decision. Furthermore, while NP testing can be helpful, it should not be the sole basis of management decisions.
