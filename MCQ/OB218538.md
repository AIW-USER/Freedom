## Question

A 13-year-old female presents to the clinic with a concern for scoliosis after she was evaluated by her primary care doctor for a sports physical. Current radiographs reveal a 32-degree right thoracic curve and her hand radiograph reveals she is Sanders stage II. A recommendation is made to begin bracing. Which of the following statements are true regarding this patient?

## Options

1. Bracing greater than 12 hours a day is associated with a high rate of needing surgical treatment
2. The patient is skeletally mature and bracing is not necessary
3. Treatment of four patients with this condition can prevent one patient from requiring surgery
4. Bracing is not effective in delaying surgery in skeletally immature patients with severe scoliosis
5. The risk of progression is low in this patient

## Correct option

**3. Treatment of four patients with this condition can prevent one patient from requiring surgery**

## Answer explanation

Bracing skeletally immature patients with adolescent idiopathic scoliosis is effective and for every four patients treated with bracing one patient will avoid having surgery (NNT=4).

Adolescent idiopathic scoliosis is the most common type of scoliosis and mostly affects teenage females. Initial treatment with bracing in curves 25 to 45 degrees is highly effective in preventing later need for surgery provided the brace is worn adequately (minimum >12 hours per day). However, in large curves (>45 degrees) bracing can be useful in delaying the need for surgery until the patient reaches skeletal maturity. *A systematic review of the literature (Sanders et al.) has demonstrated that for every four compliant patients treated with bracing, one patient will avoid having surgery for scoliosis.*

*Sanders et al. reviewed the treatment of adolescent idiopathic scoliosis with bracing.* **They reported** number needed to treat to prevent surgery was nine patients and in highly compliant patients the number needed to treat was four. **They concluded** that bracing is effective, but the current data was based on nonrandomized cohorts.

*Weinstein et al. described the design of the randomized controlled trial performed in the Bracing in Adolescent Idiopathic Scoliosis Trial (BrAIST) study.* **They stated** the BrAIST study was performed in 25 sites in North America with subjects treated in thoracolumbosacral orthosis or watchful waiting until they reached skeletal maturity. Patients included in the study experienced 72% treatment success rate with 18 hours of bracing per day compared to 48% success rate with observation alone. **They concluded** the BrAIST study will provide useful prognostic information regarding the treatment of scoliosis and improve surgical decision-making.

*Verhofste et al. performed a retrospective case series assessing the efficacy of bracing in skeletally immature patients with moderate to severe idiopathic scoliosis.* **They reported** that 57% of patients had curve progression, 32% of patients stabilized, and 11% of patients improved while 58% of patients required surgery. **They concluded** nonoperative treatment is effective in 42% of patients with this degree of scoliosis and bracing can be effective at delaying surgery in patients with curves >40 degrees.

*Dolan et al. performed an effective analysis of time-in-brace for the treatment of adolescent idiopathic scoliosis using the BrAIST and Italian Scientific Spine Institute (ISICO) cohorts.* **They stated** similar to the BrAIST study, the ISICO cohort had lower failure rates in patients wearing braces >12 hours per day. **They further reported** the adjusted odds of failure were higher in males, a lower BMI, and increased Cobb angle, but lower in younger patients and increased hours per day in a brace. **They concluded** the lower failure rate in the ISICO cohort was associated was explained by a longer amount of time per day spent in a brace.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: Wearing a TLSO greater than 12 hours per day has been associated with a high success rate at preventing curve progression and later need for posterior instrumented fusion.
Answers 2 and 5: The patient is Sanders 2 which suggests the patient is skeletally immature and nearing peak height growth velocity.
Answer 4: Despite the need for surgery in severe scoliosis, bracing is effective at delaying surgery until the patient is skeletally mature.
