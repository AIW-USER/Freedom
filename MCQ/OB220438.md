## Question

A 52-year-old male and a 55-year-old male were both brought into the emergency department from an injury at a job site. The patients were finishing a roofing job when the scaffolding collapsed, and both men fell approximately 20 feet. Both patients suffered isolated, displaced intra-articular calcaneal fractures with decreased Bohler's angles. The two patients have nearly identical fracture patterns and similarly deny a smoking history or pertinent medical history. After discussion with both patients, Patient A opts for nonoperative management while Patient B would like to undergo open reduction and internal fixation. What is the expected outcome difference between the two patients at long-term follow-up?

## Options

1. Patient B will have a superior functional outcome at 5 years.
2. Patient A will report increased satisfaction at long-term follow up.
3. Patient B will have an increased risk of additional surgical procedures.
4. Patient A will demonstrate increased walking difficulty.
5. Patient B will have improved shoe-fitting.

## Correct option

**3. Patient B will have an increased risk of additional surgical procedures.**

## Answer explanation

Multiple randomized controlled trials have found that surgical fixation of displaced, intra-articular calcaneal fractures offers no improvement in outcomes but bears an increased risk of additional surgeries (Answer 3).

Management of displaced intra-articular calcaneal fractures remains a controversial topic, with both operative and nonoperative treatments showing comparable overall functional outcomes in large randomized controlled trials. Open reduction and internal fixation (ORIF) has the advantage of potentially restoring anatomy and alignment but carries risks of wound complications, infection, and hardware failure. ORIF in particular has worse outcomes in smokers, diabetics, males, workers' compensation cases, active jobs, and patients older than 50. Nonoperative management may lead to malunion, chronic pain, and increased risk of subtalar arthritis. The decision for operative versus nonoperative management is individualized to the patient, depending on patient-specific factors, functional demands, and fracture characteristics. Specifically, clinical outcomes are better with operative management for patients with the following characteristics: injury not involved in workers' compensation, female sex, age less than 29 years, and a moderately flattened Bohler's angle (0-14°).

*Griffin et al. published a multicenter randomized controlled trial to evaluate the outcomes of ORIF vs nonoperative treatment for displaced, intra-articular calcaneal fractures, termed the UK Heel Fracture Trial (HeFT).* *Twenty-two hospitals enrolled a total of 151 patients who were randomly assigned to either surgical (73) or nonoperative (78) management.* The primary outcome of the study was a patient-reported outcome score, and secondary outcomes included complications, quality of life, and functional measurements such as walking speed and gait symmetry. **The authors found**, at the two-year mark, no significant difference in the primary outcome between operative and nonoperative arms. All secondary outcomes showed no significant difference, except for complications and reoperations, which were both higher in the operative group. **The authors concluded** that ORIF for intraarticular calcaneal fractures offered no increased benefit over nonoperative management and is associated with an increased risk of complications.

*Dickenson et al. published more long-term outcomes of the UK Heel Fracture Trial (HeFT), mentioned above.* *Of the 151 patients originally enrolled in the HeFT study, 118 were available for analysis at 60 months.* **The authors report** no difference in patient-reported outcomes (measured with the Kerr-Atkins score) at final follow-up. Furthermore, there were no differences between the two groups in difficulty walking or shoe-fitting problems. The ORIF group had significantly higher rates of additional surgical procedures (10 vs 4). In summary, **the authors concluded** that ORIF of displaced intra-articular calcaneal fractures showed no difference in outcomes at 5 years compared to conservative management, with an increased risk of additional surgeries.

*Buckley et al. published another randomized, controlled multicenter trial comparing outcomes of operative versus nonoperative management of displaced intra-articular calcaneal fractures.* *Conducted across four trauma centers, the trial enrolled 424 patients with 471 such fractures.* *Participants were randomized to receive either ORIF or nonoperative care, with outcomes assessed using the Short Form-36 (SF-36) health survey and a disease-specific visual analog scale over a follow-up period ranging from two to eight years.* **The results indicated** no statistically significant difference in functional outcomes between the two groups; the mean SF-36 scores were 68.7 for the operative group and 64.7 for the nonoperative group (p = 0.13), while the visual analog scale scores were 68.6 and 64.3, respectively (p = 0.12). **The authors concluded** that ORIF does not provide superior functional outcomes compared to nonoperative treatment for these fractures, suggesting that the choice of treatment should be individualized based on patient-specific factors.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer choice 1: Patient B, who underwent ORIF, will not have superior functional outcomes at long-term follow up. Studies have found no difference in functional outcomes with ORIF vs conservative management, particularly when both patients have similar demographics, fracture pattern (depressed Bohler's angle), and medical comorbidities.
Answer 2: Patient A, who underwent nonoperative management, will not have increased satisfaction at long-term follow-up. Studies show that patients undergoing conservative management have no difference in satisfaction scores at long-term follow-up compared to patients undergoing ORIF.
Answer 4: Randomized controlled trials have shown no difference in difficulty walking at 5-year follow-up between displaced intra-articular calcaneal fractures being treated with ORIF or conservative management.
Answer 5: Randomized controlled trials have shown no difference in shoe-fitting problems at 5-year follow-up between displaced intra-articular calcaneal fractures being treated with ORIF or conservative management.
