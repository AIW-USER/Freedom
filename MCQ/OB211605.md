## Question

A 7-year-old boy presents with 3-week history of pain in the dorsomedial midfoot, which is improved with rest. A foot radiograph is obtained and depicted in Figure A. He is placed in a short-leg cast and is made non-weight-bearing for 6 weeks. This treatment completely alleviates his symptoms. Regarding his pathology, which of the following is most accurate?

![[q211605-a-kohler-xr.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Plain radiographs will show cyst formation, fragmentation, and widening of tarsal navicular
2. The bone affected is the first tarsal bone to ossify
3. Core decompression is indicated in the majority of cases
4. Theorized etiology is mechanical compression of the navicular from the ossified talus and cuneiforms
5. When compared to a short-leg cast, immobilization in a long-leg cast results in quicker return to activity

## Correct option

**4. Theorized etiology is mechanical compression of the navicular from the ossified talus and cuneiforms**

## Answer explanation

This patient is presenting with osteonecrosis of the tarsal navicular bone (Kohler's disease). The etiology of Kohler's disease is theorized to be from mechanical compression of the navicular by the ossified talus and cuneiforms.

Kohler's disease is idiopathic navicular osteonecrosis with bony cellular death from lack of circulation as the vascular supply to the navicular is tenuous with a central watershed. On plain radiography, the navicular appears sclerotic, fragmented, and flattened. The majority of cases achieve complete resolution of symptoms and normal appearance of the navicular on radiography.

*Shastri et al. published a case report of foot pain from Kohler's disease.* **They reported** that the disease is rare and self-limiting, and that the pathology is explained by navicular mechanical compression, which is related to the delay in its ossification in the midfoot. **They concluded** that radiographic findings normalize 6-18 months after onset of symptoms and that most patients recover excellent function, regardless of treatment rendered.

*DiGiovanni et al. reviewed osteonecrosis in the foot.* **They reported** that osteonecrosis in the foot is most common in the talus, navicular, and first/second metatarsals. Regarding the navicular, they reviewed its limited vascular supply, with the dorsalis pedis providing several perforating branches dorsally, and the medial plantar artery supplying the plantar aspect. **They concluded** that the increased risk of osteonecrosis is secondary to the centripetal nature of the intraosseous blood supply which results in a watershed region.

Figure A is an AP foot radiograph demonstrating Kohler's disease, with characteristic sclerosis, flattening, and fragmentation of the navicular.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Plain radiographs will demonstrate sclerosis (not cyst formation), fragmentation, and flattening (not widening) of the navicular.
Answer 2: The navicular is the last tarsal bone to ossify in the midfoot, not the first.
Answer 3: The majority of cases are self-limited, with complete resolution with nonoperative management.
Answer 5: Most patients recover excellent function, regardless of casting technique.
