## Question

A 4-month-old male presents to your clinic for evaluation of a left upper extremity deformity. The parents note that he has had this deformity since birth. Radiographs of the affected extremity are shown in Figure A. Which of the following findings is most likely to be also found in this patient?

![[q220963-a-screenshot-2025-12-22-at-9-29-35-pm.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Absence of the pectoralis major
2. Craniosynostosis
3. Lisch nodules
4. Ash-leaf spots
5. Leukocoria

## Correct option

**1. Absence of the pectoralis major**

## Answer explanation

Symbrachydactyly, a congenital upper extremity deformity, is characterized by syndactyly, finger formation failure, usually at the central hand plate, and the resultant formation of digit nubbins. This condition is associated with Poland syndrome, where patients may also have underdeveloped/absent chest musculature (answer choice 1).

Poland syndrome is a congenital disorder characterized by symbrachydactyly and unilateral chest wall hypoplasia/pectoralis major absence. Syndactyly in these patients tends to be simple in nature and involves all digits, whereas the brachydactyly predominantly affects the middle phalanges. Though the etiology of this condition is not clearly understood, it is thought that subclavian artery hypoplasia is responsible for this constellation of findings. Treatment of these patients’ symbrachydactyly is variable due to its rarity. However, the described reconstructive efforts focus on achieving daily functional requirements through promoting radial and ulnar opposition via first webspace release and improving aesthetics via syndactyly separation or toe-to-hand transfers.

*Patel et al. published a review on congenital hand and upper extremity limb deformities and the advancements in their treatment.* *They begin by discussing the current understanding of genetic contributors to upper extremity deformities made possible by next-generation sequencing and reviewing recent updates to the Oberg, Manske, and Tonkin system for classifying upper limb differences.* *They cover the presentation and treatment options of multiple upper extremity deformities, including polydactyly, congenital absence, syndactyly, synpolydactyly, symbrachydactyly, thumb hypoplasia, curved digits, macrodactyly, and constriction band syndrome.* **They concluded** that more condition-specific outcome systems are needed to accurately assess the success of reconstructive efforts and inform evolving surgical techniques.

*Ireland, Okayama, and Flatt published a brief report on 43 cases of Poland’s syndrome.* *They describe the most common features, which include syndactyly and hypoplasia/absence of the middle phalanges.* Findings outside the upper extremity include chest wall abnormalities such as hypoplasia of the nipple, breast, upper ribs, and chest musculature. Patients are most commonly male. **They concluded** that while the hand may remain dysplastic after surgery, reconstructive efforts can improve cosmesis and function, though to a limited capacity.

Figure A demonstrates an X-ray of an upper extremity with hypoplastic digits, absent carpal bone formation/ulnar metacarpal ossification, and malformed/hypoplastic radial-sided metacarpals consistent with a diagnosis of symbrachydactyly.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Craniosynostosis is a finding seen in Apert syndrome. Hand findings in these patients are typically characterized by bilateral complex syndactyly
Answer 3: Lisch nodules are associated with neurofibromatosis 1, which typically presents with numerous soft tissue masses
Answer 4: Ash-leaf spots are a finding associated with tuberous sclerosis. Patients with this condition typically present with ungual fibromas and seizures, not with upper extremity deformities
Answer 5: Leukocoria is a finding seen in patients with a mutation in the retinoblastoma gene. This condition is not commonly associated with upper extremity deformities, but is associated with osteosarcoma
