## Question

Which of the following doesn’t indicate a ruptured ACL

## Options

1. ACL angle > 15 degree
2. Deep lateral femoral sulcus sign > 2 mm
3. Bone marrow edema involving anterior proximal tibia
4. Posterior cruciate ligament index < 3

## Correct option

**3. Bone marrow edema involving anterior proximal tibia**

## Answer explanation

Ans: C

EXPLANATION: -
Edema in ACL injury due to pivoting is in postero lateral tibia and LFC.

PREDICTING POSSIBILIT OF NON-FUNCTIONING ACL
The accuracy of MRI in the detection of acute ACL disruption ranges from 90% to 95%.
The “ACL Blumensaat line angle” is normally ≤15º. It is calculated by drawing a line parallel to the roof of the intercondylar notch of the femur (Blumensaat line) and one parallel to the distal portion of the anterior cruciate ligament. The angle is calculated at the intersection of these two lines. An angle of >15º indicates an abnormal course and probable anterior cruciate ligament tear .

Indirect MR Signs of ACL Disruption
• Bone contusion sign: lateral femoral condyle and posterior tibial plateau (pivot-shift injury) (Figures 6 A and B).
• Deep sulcus sign: lateral femoral condyle impaction (more than 2- mm deep) (Figure 7).
• Segond fracture: capsular avulsion fracture of the lateral tibial plateau (Figure 8).
• Kissing contusions: anterior tibia and femur (hyperextension injury).
• Anterior drawer sign: anterior translation of tibia relative to femur.
• Buckling of PCL: nonspecific.
• Acute hemarthrosis: nonspecific.

The posterior cruciate ligament (PCL) index is a measurement of the curvature of the PCL, which is the main stabilizer of the knee's posterior side. It's calculated using a magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) scan as the ratio of B to A, where:
• B: is the length of the line between the PCL's posterior-inferior tibial attachment and its superior-anterior femoral attachment (marked with number 2)
• A: is the maximum perpendicular distance from this line to the PCL center (marked with number 3)

![[TO21733120-8.jpg]]

The PCL index in affected knee with reference to control knee is used to assess the success of anterior cruciate ligament (ACL) reconstruction and to diagnose ACL tears. It's also correlated with the control of rotational kinematics after ACL reconstruction.
