## Question

A 53-year-old male with prior total hip arthroplasty (THA) develops a periprosthetic joint infection (PJI). You treat him with irrigation and debridement + head and liner exchange. His cultures grow Candida. His recovery from surgery is unremarkable and he is started on antifungal therapy with the Infectious Disease specialists. Based on the causative pathogen, what do you counsel your patient?

## Options

1. Fungal infections have similar outcomes to bacterial infections
2. Irrigation and debridement with head and liner exchange is the standard of treatment for fungal infections
3. Candida is a rare cause of fungal infections
4. Fungal infections have better outcomes compared to bacterial infections
5. Irrigation and debridement with head and liner exchange has a very high failure rate for fungal infections

## Correct option

**5. Irrigation and debridement with head and liner exchange has a very high failure rate for fungal infections**

## Answer explanation

Fungal periprosthetic joint infection (PJI) has worse survival with débridement, antibiotics, and implant retention compared to bacterial PJI, which is why two-stage exchange is the standard for fungal PJI.

Fungal PJI is uncommon. Risk factors include immune suppression and systemic illness, such as diabetes and chronic renal failure. Candida species are usually the causative organism. The infection is usually indolent, and systemic symptoms (e.g., fever) may be absent. ESR and CRP may be only minimally elevated. Two-stage exchange arthroplasty is the standard of care.

*Nace et al. review the diagnosis and management of fungal PJIs.* *They outline the options for treatment of fungal PJI, including débridement, antibiotics, implant retention, one-stage exchange arthroplasty, prosthetic articulating spacers, and two-stage exchange arthroplasty.* **They caution** that fungal infections develop robust biofilms which limit the utility of débridement, antibiotics, and implant retention. **They conclude** that thorough irrigation and débridement are essential, and adjunctive local and systemic antifungal therapy is critical.

*Gross et al. performed a systematic review of fungal PJIs.* **They found** that Candida was the most common fungal pathogen (85%) and that 30% had concomitant bacterial infection. Two-stage exchange had a success rate of 65%. Debridement with implant retention had 15% success. **They concluded** that two-stage exchange is the favored approach to treating fungal PJI.

*Brown et al. reported their results on a series of 31 fungal PJIs treated at their institution from 1996-2014.* **They found** that 2-year survivorship free from reinfection was 38% for hips and 76% for knees. **They concluded** that fungal PJIs are rare and that clinical outcomes are poor with a high complication rate.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1, 2, and 4: Fungal PJI has worse survival with débridement, antibiotics, and implant retention compared to bacterial PJI, which is why two-stage exchange is the standard for fungal PJI.
Answer 3: Candida is the most common causative agent of fungal PJI.
