## Question

A 67-year-old patient presents with a several year history of chronic right medial ankle pain that has failed conservative treatment. Despite the use of orthotics and Arizona bracing, the patient's foot has become flatter over time. There is tenderness along the joint line and pain with passive dorsiflexion and plantarflexion of the tibiotalar joint. Weakness and pain are appreciated with resisted foot inversion. The patient has a rigid hindfoot and is unable to perform a single heel raise. Figures A, B, and C are current radiographs. What is the most appropriate treatment?

## Options

1. Medial slide calcaneal osteotomy, FDL transfer to the navicular, lateral column lengthening, cotton osteotomy, calcaneonavicular ligament reconstruction with peroneus longus tendon, deltoid ligament reconstruction
2. Supramalleolar osteotomy
3. Pantalar arthrodesis
4. Total ankle arthroplasty
5. Triple arthrodesis with deltoid ligament reconstruction

## Correct option

**3. Pantalar arthrodesis**

## Answer explanation

The patient is presenting with stage IV posterior tibial tendon insufficiency (PTTI) with symptomatic osteoarthritis of the tibiotalar joint and a rigid hindfoot. A pantalar fusion is the most appropriate treatment choice at this time.

PTTI can be divided into four stages, which are characterized by the presence of a rigid or supple hindfoot and progressive foot deformity. Stage IV PTTI is characteristic of deltoid ligament insufficiency with tibiotalar tilting. In the absence of tibiotalar arthritis, a triple arthrodesis and deltoid ligament reconstruction can be performed to preserve the joint. In the presence of tibiotalar arthritis, an arthrodesis procedure (such as a pantalar fusion) that addresses the arthritic joint and also the forefoot deformity can be performed. In a stage IV variant with a supple hindfoot, intact PTT strength, and no forefoot deformity, a total ankle arthroplasty could be performed.

*Queen et al. performed a prospective observational study that examined the outcomes of two third-generation TAA systems in 103 patients with either normal, mild, or severe tibiotalar malalignment.* *Patients with severe tibiotalar malalignment (>15 degrees varus or valgus) had similar outcomes to those with mild or neutral alignment.* Additionally, they experienced the same improvement in timed up go test, walking speed, and improved AOFAS scores.

*Jeng et al. performed a prospective study that tested a novel joint sparing technique for stage V PTTD consisting of a triple arthrodesis with deltoid reconstruction in eight patients.* **They found** that all patients had less than 10 degrees of valgus malalignment of the tibiotalar joint and no tibiotalar arthritis. At 36-months of follow-up, five of the original eight had good outcomes. **The authors concluded** that this remains a viable option for patients that fit the indications in their study.

*Bluman et al. proposed a new classification system for posterior tibial tendon insufficiency.* Their new classification scheme dictates the ideal treatment for the given stage. The classification system divides stage IV disease into two groups based on the presence of a rigid or supple hind foot. For a rigid hindfoot, a tibiotalocalcaneal fusion or pantalar fusion is recommended. For a supple hindfoot, realignment surgery with deltoid reconstruction is recommended.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Corrective alignment osteotomy with soft tissue transfers is a viable option in early disease. However, none of these approaches addresses the malalignment of the tibiotalar joint and tibiotalar arthritis.
Answer 2: Supramalleolar osteotomy is an effective method to treat tibiotalar tilt if the pathology stems from the distal tibia. However, in the case of stage IV disease, the malalignment stems from the insufficiency of the deltoid ligament.
Answer 4: Total ankle arthroplasty could be a good choice for PTTI in select patients. However, the deformity would need to be reconstructed first, with arthroplasty done in a 2nd stage procedure or possibly in conjunction.
Answer 5: Trriple arthrodesis with deltoid ligament reconstruction would be a good option if the patient did not have ankle arthritis.
