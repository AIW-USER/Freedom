## Question

A 64-year-old patient is scheduled to undergo a right total knee replacement. During the preoperative indications conference, the surgeon discusses the case with the resident on service. He states that he would like a constrained condylar knee prosthesis available for the case. Which of the following clinical factors likely reflects this decision?

## Options

1. History of rheumatoid arthritis
2. Knee recurvatum 22°
3. Leg length discrepancy >3 cm
4. 27° valgus deformity
5. Chronic patella dislocation of the affected knee

## Correct option

**4. 27° valgus deformity**

## Answer explanation

The presence of a severe valgus deformity suggests a high likelihood of an incompetent MCL which can be effectively addressed with a constrained condylar prosthesis.

The choice for certain total knee arthroplasty prosthesis designs depends on the inherent stability of the knee and the deformity that is present. In primary total knee arthroplasty, coronal and sagittal plane deformities can be corrected by intraarticular bone cuts and soft tissue releases. However, in severe deformities, there is attenuation of critical stabilizers, such as the collateral ligaments. Typically, coronal deformities >20° are too severe for primary prosthesis designs with ligamentous balancing. Constrained condylar designs offer more reliable stability.

*Kim et al. performed a retrospective study of 194 patients undergoing revision total knee arthroplasty with a constrained condylar knee prosthesis.* **They reported** a significant improvement in Knee Society Scores and Western Ontario and McMaster Universities Osteoarthritis index scores at 14.6 years follow-up. **They concluded** the constrained condylar design has high (>90%) long-term survivorship.

*Maynard et al. retrospectively studied 127 constrained condylar knees at 7-year follow-up.* **They reported** a 97.6% survivorship during the study period with no revisions for aseptic loosening.

Incorrect answers
Answer 1: A history of rheumatoid arthritis alone is not an absolute indication of a constrained prosthesis. Depending on the degree of deformity, rheumatoid patients can be treated with standard primary implants.
Answer 2: Knee recurvatum can compromise the stability of traditional implants and would require a hinged prosthesis.
Answer 3: The presence of a leg length discrepancy does not impact the decision for a more constrained prosthesis design.
Answer 5: A condylar constrained prosthesis design is not a requirement for a chronic patella dislocation.
