## Question

You are the team physician for a local high school football team. A 16-year-old male presents to your clinic with complaints of sore throat and fever. You note splenomegaly on abdominal examination. Complete blood count is obtained which demonstrates a lymphocytosis. Which of the following is true regarding return to play for this athlete?

## Options

1. May return after extended period of rest and resolution of splenomegaly
2. Must have negative heterophile antibody test before returning
3. May return when fatigue and sore throat improve
4. May return 48 hours after completion of antibiotic course
5. May return when lymphocytosis resolves

## Correct option

**1. May return after extended period of rest and resolution of splenomegaly**

## Answer explanation

This athlete has infectious mononucleosis (mono) given his clinical symptoms, splenomegaly and lymphocytosis. He should be held out of activity for 3-5 weeks to allow for resolution of splenomegaly, as splenic rupture is the most feared complication in athletes.

Infectious mononucleosis is a viral infection caused by the Epstein Barr virus (EBV) and usually consists of symptoms like fever, sore throat, and lymphadenopathy. Splenomegaly is also common, but can be more difficult to assess. It is usually diagnosed with a heterophile antibody test, which is both sensitive and specific. Treatment is supportive but withdrawal from activity is recommended for 3-5 weeks to allow for splenomegaly to resolve, as most splenic rupture occurs within the first 3 weeks after diagnosis.

*Becker et. al performed a review noting that mono usually affects adolescents and young adults and the risk for splenic rupture and chronic fatigue make return to play protocols difficult.* **They note** that mono can affect multiple organ systems and supportive care is indicated, with no medications offering clinical benefit. **They note** that physical examination can be unreliable in following splenomegaly, making individualized return to play protocols important.

*Auwaerter et. al review the symptoms of mono including fever, pharyngitis, fatigue, and lympadenopathy.* *They also describe diagnosis with heterophile antibody testing in addition to other generalized management strategies.* **They conclude** that the risk of splenic rupture, though rare, should guide return to play recommendations, with at least 3-4 weeks between diagnosis and return preferred.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: A negative heterophile antibody test is not required for return to activity as this may remain positive after diagnosis. Additionally, this test does not address the risk of splenic rupture which can occur for weeks after diagnosis.
Answer 3: Fatigue and sore throat may last for 4-8 weeks but may resolve before splenomegaly, therefore, should not be used as return to play criteria.
Answer 4: Antibiotics are not used to treat mono, as it is a viral illness. However, secondary streptococcal pharyngitis may require antibiotic therapy, but would not change return to play timing.
Answer 5: Resolution of lymphocytosis would be a poor indicator of ability to return to play as this does not necessarily mean the risk for splenic rupture has decreased.
