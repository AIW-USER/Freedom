## Question

A 30-year-old patient undergoes open reduction internal fixation of an unstable ankle syndesmosis. During the procedure, the ankle to taken through a range of motion to check the reduction. Which of the following maneuvers has been shown to result in external rotation of the fibula?

## Options

1. Ankle plantarflexion
2. Ankle dorsiflexion
3. Ankle inversion
4. Ankle eversion
5. Ankle supination

## Correct option

**2. Ankle dorsiflexion**

## Answer explanation

As the ankle transitions from plantarflexion to dorsiflexion, the wider anterior talus engages the ankle mortise leading to external rotation of the fibula as well as proximal and posterolateral translation.

Syndesmotic injuries commonly occur in ankle fractures, and recent literature has demonstrated that syndesmotic malreduction is much more common than initially believed. While the ideal reduction methods have yet to be established, ankle dorsiflexion is one maneuver that can assist with restoring rotational alignment of the fibula as ankle dorsiflexion causes the distal fibula to externally rotate. Although controversial, some authors recommend dorsiflexing the ankle prior to screw insertion to prevent dorsiflexion restriction after fixation due to the trapezoidal anatomy of the talus.

*Tornetta et al. performed a cadaveric study evaluating the position of the ankle during screw insertion to determine if dorsiflexion is required prior to screw insertion.* **They found** that fixing the syndesmosis with the talus in a plantarflexed position did not limit dorsiflexion range of motion after fixation, in spite of the trapezoidal nature of the anterior talus. **They conclude** that while dorsiflexion of the ankle causes external rotation of the fibula as the wide anterior talus engages the mortise, maximal dorsiflexion of the ankle prior to screw insertion is not required.

*Gardner et al. provide a comprehensive review of diagnosis and management of syndesmotic injuries associated with ankle fractures.* *They describe syndesmotic reduction techniques and note that a clamp applied from the lateral malleolar ridge of the fibula to the center of the AP width of the tibia resulted in the most consistent and accurate reduction of the syndesmosis.* **They conclude** anatomic reduction of the fibula within the incisura leads to improved functional outcomes.

*Liu et al. conducted a comprehensive CT study on quantitative and qualitative descriptive measures to evaluate fibular incisura morphologies and fibular orientation.* **They identified** five morphologic variations of the fibular incisura: crescentic, trapezoid, flat, chevron, and widow's peak. The most common fibular incisura morphology was crescentic (61.3%) and least common was flat (3.1%).

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Ankle plantarflexion leads to internal rotation of the distal fibula.
Answers 3/4: Ankle inversion and eversion motion primarily occurs through the subtalar joint and is less likely to influence fibular rotation.
Answer 5: Ankle supination is a combination of ankle plantarflexion, hindfoot inversion, and forefoot adduction. This would cause internal rotation of the distal fibula.
