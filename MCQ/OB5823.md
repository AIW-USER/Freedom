## Question

Which percentage best describes the relative contribution of the distal humeral growth plate to overall humeral length?

## Options

1. 5 %
2. 20 %
3. 40 %
4. 60 %
5. 80 %

## Correct option

**2. 20 %**

## Answer explanation

The distal humerus growth plate contributes approximately 20% of growth to overall humeral length.

Growth disturbance after distal humeral physeal fractures is an important complication, despite being rare, as the distal physis contributes ~3mm/yr of longitudinal humeral growth.

*Abzug et al reviewed transphyseal fracture of the distal humerus.* It was found that these injuries most commonly occur in children younger than 3 years of age. The most common etoliogy is secondary to birth trauma, nonaccidental trauma, or fall from small height. Complications include cubitus varus caused by a malunion, osteonecrosis of the medial condyle, or growth arrest. A corrective lateral closing wedge osteotomy can be performed to restore near normal alignment.

*Pritchett et al review the growth plate activity in the upper extremity.* *They reviewed 200 male and female middle-class Americans, mostly of northwest European descent to assess the contribution of each growth center to the total length of the humerus.* **They showed** that 80% of growth in the humerus occurs at the proximal growth plate, although growth plate activity is not constant or average throughout the growth period. Before the age of two years, less than 75% of growth occurs at the proximal growth plate, increasing to 85% at the age of eight and remaining constant at 90% after the age of 11.

Illustration A shows a skeletal homunculus depicting the relative contributions of the growth plates to the longitundinal growth of the upper and lower limb. Note the proximal humerus will contribute to 40% of overall upper extremity growth, where as the distal humerus and proximal radius/ulna together contribute to 20% of overall growth, or 10% each. Therefore, the proximal physis:distal physis growth ratio is 4:1 to overall humeral longitudinal growth, which can be otherwise stated as 80/20.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1, 3-5: Literature shows that the distal humerus growth plate contributes 20% of growth to overall humeral length.
