## Question

Incorrect statement regarding Vertical Expandable Prosthetic Titanium Rib (VEPTR) is

## Options

1. Indicated for early onset scoliosis (EOS) with rib anomalies
2. Since there are pelvis anchors at bottom, its use for infants with thoracic insufficiency syndrome (TIS) is difficult
3. Incidences of crouched gait have been observed in some ambulatory children where VEPTR system was used
4. Apart from being indicated in scoliosis it can also be used to maintain thoracic volume in other neuromuscular disorders

## Correct option

**2. Since there are pelvis anchors at bottom, its use for infants with thoracic insufficiency syndrome (TIS) is difficult**

## Answer explanation

Ans: B

VEPTR (Vertical Expandable Prosthetic Titanium Rib) is used in early onset scoliosis (EOS) with rib anomalies to maintain thoracic volume in order to prevent thoracic insufficiency syndrome (TIS). Although introduced for EOS with TIS, its used has also been extended to maintain thoracic volume in some other neuromuscular disorders like spinal muscular atrophy etc.

The central VEPTR treatment principle is to correct volume depletion deformity of the thorax associated with the pathological process, and maintain the correction until skeletal maturity. It was first described by Campbell et al. to treat TIS associated with congenital scoliosis and fused ribs. The VEPTR system (see pic below) is basically a titanium alloy longitudinal rib distraction device. The VEPTR device can be placed through three small incisions that allow for attachment of rib hooks (mostly bilaterally) at the upper end and through pelvic hooks at the distal end, providing distraction forces to correct the deformity while allowing for growth. The ribs are used as anchor points in addition to spine or pelvis anchor points at the base of the construct to address the three-dimensional thoracic deformity with or without expansion thoracoplasty.

The VEPTR system is different from traditional growing rods in that it is attached to the ribs at top of the device and then it also has spine and hip anchor points. This innovation particularly facilitates the treatment in infants and small children.

Although initial studies showed correction of Cobb angles, increment in the space available for the lungs, and a better total spine growth but some recent reviews have raised a caution. In children pelvis plays an important role in mobility, so if pelvis is fixed to rib cage using VEPTR, mobility could deteriorate. There have been reports of incidences of crouched gait developing where VEPTR was used in ambulatory children. So many surgeons, limit the use of pelvic anchors to non-ambulatory children, in ambulatory children, pelvic anchor is used only if there is no option to attach the distal fixation to the spine.
