## Question

A 55-year-old patient presents with to your clinic with long-standing knee pain. Radiographs reveal severe tricompartmental osteoarthritis and you discuss total knee arthroplasty with the patient. You review risks and benefits of the procedure and obtain informed consent before proceeding. Six weeks after surgery, the patient presents to the ED with purulent drainage noted to be coming from his incision. You discuss the need for irrigation and debridement and the patient becomes very angry that he needs another surgery. Several weeks later you find out that the patient has filed a formal complaint accusing you of malpractice. Which of the following is true regarding formal complaints filed against a physician?

## Options

1. Malpractice investigations are governed by federal regulations
2. A physician's license is automatically suspended until the formal complaint can be resolved
3. The physician should provide a written response to the medical board with all relevant records
4. Physicians should contact the patient's attorney directly to negotiate a settlement
5. Physicians are required to retain an attorney to address formal complaints

## Correct option

**3. The physician should provide a written response to the medical board with all relevant records**

## Answer explanation

When a formal complaint is filed against a physician, that physician should provide a written response with any relevant records detailing the case and medical decision making process.

It is important for the physician/surgeon to know how to manage a formal complaint. These complaints can be filed with the state medical board by patients who are concerned they have been treated inappropriately. If the complaint is deemed within the jurisdiction of the state medical board, an investigation is initiated and all parties involved are contacted. At this point, it is important for the treating physician to provide records regarding the medical decision making process as well as a response to the formal complaint. The case is then reviewed by other experts in the same field and action is taken based on the conclusions of the investigation. The treating physician is not required to retain an attorney, although counsel may be beneficial in some situations.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Malpractice regulations are governed by state medical boards, not the federal government.
Answer 2: In rare cases where there is an imminent threat to the public, a physician's license can be suspended but automatic suspension is not standard practice when a formal complaint is filed.
Answer 4: Physicians are not encouraged to communicate directly with the patient's attorney in order to negotiate a settlement. This should be done as a part of the formal state medical board investigation.
Answer 5: Physicians are not required to retain an attorney because a complaint was filed, although seeking counsel may be beneficial in some settings.
