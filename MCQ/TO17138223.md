## Question

A 3-year-old boy was treated in infancy with Ponseti casting demonstrates an abnormal walking pattern. He lands on the lateral border of foot with supination of the affected foot during swing phase of gait.

His ankle has 20 degrees of active and passive dorsiflexion and 45 degrees of plantar flexion. What is the most appropriate treatment?

## Options

1. Corrective casting to achieve 60* abduction
2. Tibialis anterior transfer
3. Tibialis posterior transfer
4. Mid tarsal ostetotomy to correct the supination.

## Correct option

**2. Tibialis anterior transfer**

## Answer explanation

The above mentioned examination findings suggest a presence of dynamic deformity in a static corrected foot. Though the end point of casting in infants is 60* abduction this is not the case as the child grows older. The child presently has a dynamic supination deformity.

Dynamic supination is one of the common residual issue after Ponseti casting of a clubfoot child. Dynamic supination is a known residual problem after the Ponseti casting. Ponseti applied the principles of the Garceau procedure and transferred the entire anterior tibialis tendon to the lateral cuneiform. The anterior tibialis continues to act as a dorsiflexor of the foot but with a more advantageous trajectory.

Likewise, the posterior tibialis tendon transfer through the interosseous membrane will not address the supinating force of the anterior tibialis. It is a surgical option when tibialis anterior is paralyzed.

Tibialis tendon transfer:

The indications for tibialis tendon transfer in the setting of residual clubfoot are poor first ray ground contact, weight-bearing on the lateral border of the foot, and dynamic supination or inversion of the forefoot. In the surgical treatment of dynamic supination following Ponseti treatment, the accepted sites of insertion for anterior tibial tendon transfer are the lateral cuneiform (more common) and cuboid.

Tibialis tendon transfer to the dorsum of the foot should be performed to address dynamic supination when the lateral cuneiform has ossified. This is typically after at least 2 years of age and usually not before age 3.

The Dynamic supination once noted is likely to remain, so there is no role in waiting till an adolescent age before surgery.

Ans. B
