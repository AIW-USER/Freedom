## Options

1. Arthroscopic suprapectoral tenodesis is associated with less cosmetic deformity and improved supination strength
2. Arthroscopic suprapectoral tenodesis and open subpectoral tenodesis are superior to tenotomy with regard to patient-reported outcomes
3. Open subpectoral tenodesis is superior to arthroscopic suprapectoral tenodesis in terms of cosmetic deformity but not supination strength
4. Tenotomy alone is associated with increased risk of cosmetic deformity, but is not inferior to suprapectoral or subpectoral tenodesis in terms of patient reported outcomes
5. There are no differences in strength or patient reported outcomes between suprapectoral and subpectoral tenodesis, but both are superior to tenotomy alone

## Correct option

**4. Tenotomy alone is associated with increased risk of cosmetic deformity, but is not inferior to suprapectoral or subpectoral tenodesis in terms of patient reported outcomes**

## Answer explanation

All modes of either long head of the biceps (LHB) tenodesis or tenotomy have shown no difference concerning patient-reported clinical outcome scores in the literature. However, tenotomy alone has been associated with the occurrence of Popeye deformity post-operatively.

Biceps tendinitis is a well-recognized source of anterior shoulder pain that may be associated with subscapularis tears and subacromial impingement. It is made worse with provocative tests such as the Speed and Yergason's tests. Treatment involves an initial trial of NSAIDs, activity modification, and physical therapy; however, when extensive conservative management fails, arthroscopic versus open biceps tenodesis or tenotomy can be performed to help alleviate debilitating pain. In terms of choice of surgical treatment, multiple randomized controlled clinical trials have demonstrated that there is no difference concerning patient-reported clinical outcome scores in those who undergo either tenotomy or tenodesis.

Van Durzen and the Bite Study Group performed a multi-center, non-inferiority RCT to show that LHB tenotomy is not inferior to suprapectoral tenodesis in arthroscopic repair of non-traumatic rotator cuff tears. The authors recruited 100 patients and randomized them to receive either suprapectoral LHB tenodesis or LHB tenotomy. They found and concluded that although their results were statistically "inconclusive" regarding non-inferiority of the Constant-Murley Score (CMS) at 1-year follow-up, any observed differences between patients with LHB tenotomy and those with LHB tenodesis in all outcome scores were small.

*Zhu et al. performed a meta-analysis of level I evidence comparing tenotomy versus tenodesis in the management of biceps tendinosis.* *The authors reviewed a total of 5 studies (227 tenotomy and 227 tenodesis patients) and found postoperative improvement across all outcomes regardless of surgical treatment.* A pooled analysis led them to conclude that no statistically significant difference for Constant-Murley Shoulder Outcome Score, ASES, pain, or flexion strength was observed, though tenodesis was superior to tenotomy in reducing the risk of Popeye deformity.

*Van Durzen et al. performed a systematic review and meta-analysis of the literature surrounding suprapectoral versus subpectoral tenodesis for LHB tendinopathy.* *The authors reviewed seven comparative studies with 409 patients and found a significant, but clinically irrelevant difference in ASES and no significant difference in CMS, VAS for anterior shoulder pain, Popeye deformity, or persistent bicipital pain.* **They concluded** that comparable results were found concerning outcome scores, pain in the bicipital groove, and avoiding a Popeye deformity.

Figures A-C demonstrate consecutive axial PD fat-saturated MRI sequences of a shoulder with a significantly inflamed biceps tendon. Figure D is a clinical photograph demonstrating a "Popeye" cosmetic deformity.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1-3 and 5: There are no differences in strength, functional outcomes, or range of motion between tenotomy and tenodesis, though tenotomy alone is associated with an increased rate of cosmetic deformity ("Popeye deformity"). In terms of suprapectoral versus subpectoral tenodesis, similar outcomes in residual pain, bicipital groove pain, Popeye deformity, range of motion, and patient-reported outcomes have been reported.
