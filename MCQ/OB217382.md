## Question

A 33-year-old male is playing recreational soccer when she sustains an inversion ankle injury. She is able to continue playing but afterward notices swelling as well as numbness and tingling over the dorsum of the foot. She denies any significant weakness at this time although pain from her injury limits her examination. She does report having worsening numbness with passive plantarflexion. Which of the following represents the most common site of compression for the affected nerve?

## Options

1. At musculotendinous junction of gastrocnemius muscle and achilles tendon
2. Beneath inferior extensor retinaculum
3. Beneath superior peroneal retinaculum
4. Beneath flexor retinaculum
5. Proximal to lateral malleolus exiting fascia of anterolateral leg

## Correct option

**5. Proximal to lateral malleolus exiting fascia of anterolateral leg**

## Answer explanation

This patient's ankle injury has caused a neuropraxia of the superficial peroneal nerve (SPN), which is most commonly compressed 10-12 cm proximal to the lateral malleolus as it exits from the lateral to the anterior compartment.

The course of the SPN near the ankle is important to understand given its relevance in surgical anatomy. Not only can the SPN be injured iatrogenically, but it may also be compressed or damaged secondary to trauma. The most common site of compression is 10-12 cm proximal to the lateral malleolus as the nerve exits the lateral compartment to enter the anterior compartment. Patients complain of paresthesias over the dorsum of the foot which may worsen with passive plantarflexion and inversion. Some patients may describe weakness in eversion secondary to denervation of the peroneal tendons. Observation is usually preferred, however, in some cases, exploration and neurolysis are required.

*Barrett et al. reviewed the path of the SPN and its clinical implications.* *They evaluated a total of 75 cadaver specimens, of which the SPN was identified in the lateral compartment adjacent to the fascial septum in 72% of cases and the anterior compartment in 23% of cases.* These results reiterate that it remains important to dissect with care in the lateral and anterior compartment of the distal leg, as there is significant variability to the SPN.

*Rosson et al. also evaluated SPN nerve variability and its effect on surgical technique.* **They noted** that entrapment is thought to be due to the position of the SPN in the lateral compartment, although they found the SPN in the anterior compartment in almost 50% of cases. Therefore, **they noted** that in cases of compression or neuroma, both the anterior and lateral compartments should be explored and the nerve thoroughly decompressed.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The gastrocnemius/Achilles musculotendinous junction represents one of the more common sites of sural nerve compression in the calf which would cause lateral leg and ankle paresthesias.
Answer 2: The deep peroneal nerve may be compressed under the inferior extensor retinaculum but would cause paresthesias in the 1st webspace.
Answer 3: The superficial peroneal retinaculum overlies the peroneal tendons, but would not affect any local nervous structures.
Answer 4: The flexor retinaculum overlies the tarsal tunnel and can cause compression of the tibial nerve which supplies the medial and plantar aspects of the foot.
