## Question

A 38-year-old female presents to your office with complaints of right shoulder pain that is aggravated with activity. Physical examination reveals full range of motion, positive O'Brien’s active compression test, Speed's test, and Yergason's test. What is the most appropriate initial treatment for this patient?

## Options

1. Rest with rehabilitation focusing on scapular mechanics and rotator cuff strengthening
2. Aggressive rehabilitation focusing on concentric strengthening of the biceps tendon
3. Arthroscopic debridement with repair of the labrum
4. Arthroscopic debridement with biceps tenotomy
5. Arthroscopic debridement with biceps tenodesis

## Correct option

**1. Rest with rehabilitation focusing on scapular mechanics and rotator cuff strengthening**

## Answer explanation

Rest from sports followed by physical therapy and NSAIDs is indicated as first line treatment for SLAP injuries and should focus on addressing GIRD if present. Rehabilitation should focus on scapular mechanics and rotator cuff strengthening for all patients

A SLAP lesion (Superior Labrum from Anterior to Posterior tear) generally occurs as result of overuse injury to the shoulder in overhead athletes or traumatic falls in older patients and can result in deep shoulder pain and biceps tendonitis. Diagnosis generally requires MRI studies to assess the superior labrum and the integrity of the biceps tendon. Treatment may be nonoperative or operative depending on patient age, activity levels, severity of symptoms and associated instability.

*Provencher et al. prospectively analyzed the clinical outcomes of the arthroscopic treatment of type 2 SLAP tears in a young, active patient population.* **They reported** that arthroscopic SLAP repair provides a clinical and statistically significant improvement in shoulder outcomes. **They noted**, however, that a reliable return to the previous activity level is limited; 37% of patients had failure, with a 28% revision rate. **They concluded** that age greater than 36 years was associated with a higher chance of failure.

*de Sa et al. reviewed the outcomes of SLAP repair versus biceps tenodesis for the index treatment of isolated type II SLAP tears.* **They reported** that SLAP repair and biceps tenodesis are both acceptable as index treatment for isolated type II SLAP tears. **They concluded** that SLAP repair remains the most commonly performed index procedure; however, biceps tenodesis appears equally efficacious and may represent an attractive alternative.

*Hurley et al. systematically reviewed the comparative studies in the literature to ascertain whether biceps tenodesis or labral repair results in superior clinical outcomes in the treatment of SLAP tears.* **They reported** that biceps tenodesis resulted in higher rates of patient satisfaction and return to sport in the studies published in the literature. **They concluded** that biceps tenodesis and SLAP repair resulted in similar functional outcome scores.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Aggressive rehabilitation focusing on concentric strengthening of the biceps tendon would aggravate the patient's symptoms.
Answers 3-5: Rest with rehabilitation focusing on scapular dyskinesia and rotator cuff strengthening is the most appropriate INITIAL treatment for this patient.
