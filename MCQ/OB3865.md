## Options

1. Midfoot arthrodesis
2. Open reduction internal fixation
3. Triple arthrodesis
4. Irrigation and debridement
5. Total contact casting

## Correct option

**5. Total contact casting**

## Answer explanation

The patient has a diabetic Charcot neuropathy with the best next step in management being total contact casting.

Charcot arthropathy is a destructive process, most commonly affecting joints of the foot and ankle in diabetics with peripheral neuropathy. Affected individuals present with swelling, warmth, and erythema, often without a history of trauma. On radiographs bony fragmentation, fracture, and dislocation progress to foot deformity, bony prominence, and instability.
 Diabetic Charcot neuropathy progresses through the Eichenholtz stages and first-line treatment includes immobilization progressing from total contact casting, Charcot restraint orthotic walker (CROW), to accommodative footwear and orthoses.

*de Souza et al followed 27 patients with Stage 1 or early Stage 2 Charcot arthropathy of the foot and ankle treated with total contact casting.* **They found** no patient suffered skin ulceration or bony deterioration with weight-bearing in total contact casting. **They conclude** weight-bearing in total contact casting appears to be safe in the acute Stage 1 Charcot arthropathy of the foot and ankle.

*van der Ven et al review the current concepts for evaluation and management of Charcot arthropathy.* The article states nonoperative management progresses from total contact casting (Eichenholtz Stage 1) to Charcot restraint orthotic walker (Stage 2), and later to accommodative shoe and insert. **They state** elevation helps differentiate Charcot arthropathy from infection with the former showing a decrease in erythema. Similarly, they discuss advanced imaging such as an indium bone scan that will be cold in Charcot arthropathy and hot in infection.

Figures A and B show a lateral and AP of the foot with midfoot and distal fibula coalescence of fragments and absorption of fine bone debris consistent with Eichenholtz stage 2 Charcot neuropathy. Figure C shows a cold indium WBC scan. Illustration A shows an example of Charcot restraint orthotic walker (CROW)

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1, 2, & 3: Midfoot arthrodesis, ORIF, or triple arthrodesis would only be indicated once the patient has failed nonoperative treatment or develops gross deformity or ulceration.
Answer 4: Operative debridement is not indicated as the patient has no signs of infection and a negative bone scan.
