## Question

You are rounding on a patient who sustained a common peroneal nerve palsy following an injury to the knee. While on rounds, your attending begins discussing the gait cycle of an uninjured extremity. He asks you in which phase of the gait cycle does eccentric contraction of the tibialis anterior occur?

## Options

1. Loading response
2. Mid-stance
3. Terminal stance
4. Pre-swing
5. Initial swing

## Correct option

**1. Loading response**

## Answer explanation

During the gait cycle, the tibialis anterior eccentrically contracts during heel strike (initial contact) during the loading response phase (initial double limb support).

The gait cycle is measured from heel-strike to heel-strike and consists of a stance phase and a swing phase. The stance phase is the period of time that the foot is on the ground. Approximately 60% of one gait cycle is spent in stance. During stance, the leg accepts the body weight and provides single limb support. Conversely, the swing phase is the period of time that the foot is off the ground moving forward. Approximately 40% of the gait cycle is spent in swing. Stride is the distance between consecutive initial contacts of the same foot with the ground. Step is the distance between initial contacts of the alternating feet. Electromyography during walking reveals the tibialis anterior eccentrically contracts during heel strike.

*Winter reviewed biomechanical analysis of walking patterns at slow, natural, and fast cadences.* **They report** that the ankle has two mechanical power phases: a negative work phase during weight acceptance, followed by a dominant burst of positive work at push-off. **They also found** the knee has four power phases: a negative work phase at weight acceptance, a small positive work phase during mid-stance, a major negative work burst at push-off and early swing, and a final energy-absorbing phase at the end of swing. **They concluded** that the energy absorption by the ankle plantarflexors during weight acceptance remains fairly constant at all walking speeds, and the absorption by the knee flexors at end of swing drops only slightly as cadence decreases.

*Wootten et al. reviewed the human gait cycle.* *They report on the numerical representation of electromyographic data combined with cluster analysis to obtain patterns of dynamic phasic activity of 10 muscles of the lower extremity.* **They concluded** that two to four patterns are developed for each of the muscles and the physiological significance of the patterns are discussed.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: The tibialis anterior is inactive during mid-stance, while the gluteus medius and calf muscles undergo eccentric contraction
Answer 3: The tibialis anterior is inactive during terminal stance, while the toe flexors and tibialis posterior contract
Answer 4: The tibialis anterior is inactive during pre-swing, while the hip flexors contract to propel advancing limb
Answer 5: The tibialis anterior contracts concentrically during initial swing, while the hip flexors concentrically contract to advance the swinging leg
