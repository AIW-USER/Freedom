## Question

Figure A is the radiograph of a 30-year-old female that is presenting to your office regarding a "bump" that she has on the lateral aspect of her foot. She denies any pain about the deformity other than irritation with rubbing against her shoes. Which of the following is the best initial treatment for this patient?

![[q218555-a-foot.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. NSAIDs and shoe wear modification
2. Lateral condylectomy alone
3. Lateral condylectomy with distal metatarsal osteotomy
4. Oblique diaphyseal rotational osteotomy
5. Metatarsal head resection

## Correct option

**1. NSAIDs and shoe wear modification**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a Bunionette deformity with an increased intermetatarsal angle (IMA). Nonoperative management with NSAIDS, shoe wear modification, orthotics, keratosis padding, and/or callous shaving is indicated as first-line treatment of all types of Bunionette deformities

Bunionette deformities, commonly called tailor's bunions, are prominences on the lateral aspect of the 5th metatarsal head that most commonly occur as a result of compression of the forefoot. Diagnosis is made clinically with the presence of prominence on the lateral aspect of the 5th metatarsal head, often associated with pain and callus formation. Treatment can be nonoperative with shoe modifications for mild and minimally symptomatic cases. Surgical management is indicated for progressive deformity and difficulty with shoe wear.

*Cohen et al. reviewed the bunionette, or tailor's bunion.* **They reported** that when symptomatic, these deformities often respond to nonsurgical treatment methods, such as wider shoes and padding techniques. **They concluded** that when these methods are unsuccessful, surgical treatment is based on preoperative radiographs; patients with a lateral bow to the fifth metatarsal are treated with a distal chevron-type osteotomy and a widened 4-5 IMA angle often requires a diaphyseal osteotomy for correction.

*Weil et al. reviewed the variety of surgical osteotomy procedures that have been described for treating bunionette deformities.* **They reported** that metatarsal osteotomies narrow the forefoot, maintain the length of the metatarsal, and preserve the function of the metatarsophalangeal joint. **They noted** that distal metatarsal osteotomies produce less correction and reduce postoperative disability; however, they pose a risk of inadequate correction because of the small width of the fifth metatarsal head and transfer lesions if shortened or dorsiflexed excessively. Diaphyseal osteotomies are indicated when greater correction is needed; however, they require more dissection and there is greater postoperative convalescence with non–weight bearing for several weeks. **They concluded** that proximal base osteotomies may be used to address significantly increased 4–5 IMAs or when a large degree of sagittal plane correction is required.

Figure A is the radiograph of a foot with a Bunionette deformity with an increased IMA.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Lateral condylectomy is indicated in symptomatic Type I deformities
Answer 3: Lateral condylectomy with distal metatarsal osteotomy is indicated in cases with a mild widening of the fourth and fifth IMA (≤12 degrees)
Answer 4: Oblique diaphyseal rotational osteotomy is indicated in symptomatic Type 2 and 3 with IMA >12 degrees
Answer 5: Metatarsal head resection is indicated as a salvage procedure only and leads to unacceptable instability of MTP joint
