## Question

A 55-year-old woman presents with severe forefoot pain localized to both the second and third web spaces, consistent with a dual diagnosis of Morton's neuroma. She is scheduled for surgical management after failing conservative measures. What is the most appropriate surgical plan to maximize symptomatic relief while minimizing the risk of digital artery injury and subsequent toe ischemia in the third web space?

## Options

1. Resect both the second and third web space neuromas via two separate dorsal incisions
2. Resect the second web space neuroma and perform neurolysis of the third web space by releasing the deep transverse metatarsal ligament (DTML)
3. Resect the second web space neuroma and leave the third web space unaddressed
4. Resect the second web space neuroma and perform a Flexor Digitorum Longus (FDL) tendon transfer to enhance circulation to the third and fourth toes
5. Use a single plantar approach to resect both the second and third web space neuromas

## Correct option

**2. Resect the second web space neuroma and perform neurolysis of the third web space by releasing the deep transverse metatarsal ligament (DTML)**

## Answer explanation

Neurolysis (decompression) of the third web space is as effective as resection for symptomatic relief while being a safer procedure that protects the critical blood supply of the middle toes, which is particularly vulnerable during dual web space surgery (Answer 2).

When addressing multiple neuromas in adjacent web spaces, the risk of compromising the blood supply to the central toes increases because the digital arteries (medial and lateral) are highly vulnerable during neurectomy. The third web space (supplying the third and fourth toes) is especially critical. Current evidence suggests that neurolysis (decompression) of the nerve by releasing the deep transverse metatarsal ligament (DTML) provides equivalent symptomatic relief as neurectomy. Therefore, the safest and most effective approach in this high-risk scenario is to resect the more straightforward second web space neuroma and perform decompression alone on the third web space neuroma to address symptoms while preserving the third and fourth digital arteries.

*Arshad et al. conducted a systematic review on the management of multiple Morton's neuromas in the same foot.* **They noted** that treating contiguous neuromas significantly increases the risk of digital artery injury and subsequent toe ischemia compared to single neuroma resection. **They concluded** that surgical planning for multiple neuromas must prioritize minimizing vascular injury, suggesting less aggressive techniques like neurolysis in critical web spaces.

*Villas and associates performed a study comparing neurectomy (resection) to neurolysis (decompression via DTML release) for the treatment of Morton's neuroma.* **They noted** that at the final follow-up, there was no statistically significant difference in symptomatic relief between the two surgical methods. **They concluded** that neurolysis alone is an effective treatment for Morton's neuroma, suggesting it may be preferred to avoid the risk of neurectomy complications, such as a painful stump neuroma.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: This maximal surgical approach carries the highest risk of injury to the adjacent digital arteries, particularly in the third web space.
Answer 3: This fails to address the patient's symptomatic third web space neuroma, which is necessary to maximize symptomatic relief.
Answer 4: FDL transfer is a reconstructive procedure and is not an appropriate, immediate strategy for managing acute surgical vascular risk.
Answer 5: While a plantar approach offers better visualization, using a single incision for adjacent web spaces is not optimal, and resection still carries the risk of arterial injury and toe ischemia.
