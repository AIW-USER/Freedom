## Question

A 23-year-old male is referred to your clinic by his primary care provider. The patient reports that he was playing in an intramural flag football game 3 days prior when he sustained a twisting injury to his left ankle. His radiographs are available for review in Figures A and B. You appropriately indicate the patient for surgery and perform reduction and tetracortical screw fixation of his injury, with closed reduction of the syndesmosis. Which of the following radiographic parameters, if found on the postoperative radiograph, would best be followed up with a CT scan?

## Options

1. Medial clear space of 3 mm on a mortise view
2. Tibiofibular clear space of 3mm on a mortise view
3. Tibiofibular overlap of 3 mm on a mortise view
4. Tibiofibular overlap of 2 mm on an AP view
5. Tibiofibular overlap of 7 mm on an AP view

## Correct option

**4. Tibiofibular overlap of 2 mm on an AP view**

## Answer explanation

The patient has sustained a Maisonneuve-type ankle fracture. Anatomic reduction of the ankle syndesmosis is a critical step of the surgical treatment of this injury, and the risk of malreducing the syndesmosis is increased if a closed (versus open) syndesmotic reduction is performed. A tibiofibular overlap of 2 mm (Answer 4) on an AP view indicates possible syndesmotic malreduction, and should be followed up with a CT scan to critically evaluate the quality of syndesmotic reduction.

Maissoneuve-type ankle fractures are characterized by syndesmotic disruption and concomitant proximal-fibula fracture. These injuries often occur secondary to the forced external rotation of a planted and pronated foot. This force causes the talus to externally rotate against the lateral malleolus, transferring energy through the syndesmotic ligaments, interosseous membrane, and eventually out through the proximal fibula. Treatment of these injuries is almost always operative, via reduction and stabilization of the syndesmosis. While a variety of fixation constructs have been described, anatomic reduction of the syndesmosis is critical, as malreduction has been correlated with poor postoperative outcomes. If malreduction is suspected, a CT scan should be obtained in order to critically evaluate the syndesmosis,

*Sagi et al. investigated a series of patients with ankle fractures requiring syndesmotic reduction.* *Their study included 68 patients, and all patients had a minimum two-year follow-up.* *27 (39%) of syndesmotic injuries were malreduced, with 44% of those patients who underwent closed (as opposed to open) syndesmotic reduction having evidence of malreduction.* **They found** that patients with a malreduced syndesmosis had worse outcome scores on both the Short Form Musculoskeletal Assessment and Olerud/Molander questionnaires when compared to those patients with anatomic reduction. Overall, **the authors conclude** that syndesmotic malreduction is a risk factor for poor postoperative outcomes following ankle fracture open-reduction and internal fixation, and that a CT scan should be obtained if syndesmotic malreduction is suspected.

Wake and Martin provided a comprehensive review of syndesmotic injuries. The authors highlight the importance of recognizing these injuries, as syndesmotic injury can lead to marked functional limitations and dysfunction if maltreated. They provide a review of the normal ankle radiographic parameters and offer recommendations regarding tine placement for accurate syndesmotic reduction. In order to most accurately reduce the syndesmosis, when using reduction forceps, the lateral clamp tine should be placed on the fibular ridge and the medial clamp tine should be on the anterior third of the tibial line in the lateral view. The authors conclude by echoing the findings of Sagi, stressing that anatomic syndesmotic reduction is of paramount importance, and that a CT scan can assess for the presence or absence of syndesmotic malreduction.

Figure A is a mortise of the left ankle, demonstrating increased medial clear space, decreased tibiofibular overlap and increase tibiofibular clear space. Figure B is a lateral of the left knee demonstrating a proximal fibula fracture. Illustration A is an axial CT scan of a left ankle demonstrating syndesmotic malreduction.

Incorrect Answers
Answer 1: Medial clear space should be less than or equal to 4 mm on the mortise radiograph.
Answer 2: Tibiofibular clear space should be less than 6 mm on both the AP and mortise radiograph.
Answers 3 and 5: Tibiofibular overlap should be greater than 6 mm on the AP and greater than 1 mm on the mortise radiograph.
