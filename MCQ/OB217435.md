## Question

A 37-year-old male presents to the emergency department with knee pain after a motor vehicle collision. Radiographs demonstrate a Schatzker V tibial plateau fracture. He is neurovascularly intact at the time of presentation and there is no evidence of open injury. He undergoes external fixation of the fracture on the day of injury. Ten days later, he undergoes definitive ORIF to place lateral and posteromedial plates. On post-operative radiographs the joint is congruent and the mechanical axis appears to be restored. Which of the following complications is most likely to occur within the first 6 months post-operatively?

## Options

1. Arthrofibrosis
2. Post-traumatic arthritis
3. Neurovascular compromise
4. Deep infection
5. Knee instability

## Correct option

**1. Arthrofibrosis**

## Answer explanation

Arthrofibrosis is the most common complication after tibial plateau fracture fixation, occurring in about 15% of cases when an external fixator is used to temporize the fracture initially.

Tibial plateau fractures in young people are usually associated with high-energy trauma. In fractures which are length unstable (Schatzker IV-VI) or those with significant soft tissue injury, temporizing external fixator is often employed. Despite the benefits of external fixation in this clinical scenario, it does have some negative effects, principally, increasing the rate of arthrofibrosis in the first 6 months after definitive open reduction and internal fixation (ORIF). One study found that for each day in the external fixator, the odds of requiring manipulation under anesthesia or quadricepsplasty increased by 10%. In order to combat this, some authors have recommended use of continuous passive motion (CPM) devices, which have decreased the risk of developing arthrofibrosis by up to 70%.

*Kugelman et al. evaluated the predictors and outcomes of knee stiffness after OTA-41 tibial plateau fractures.* **They noted** that independent predictors of knee stiffness included open fracture, prior external fixator application, polytrauma involvement, tibial spine involvement, nonwhite ethnicity, increasing age, and deep infection. **They concluded** that if stiffness did occur, outcomes and knee range of motion did improve with operative intervention.

*Haller et al. aimed to review the incidence and management of arthrofibrosis after tibial plateau fractures.* *They retrospectively reviewed 186 patients and using a multivariate regression analysis, noted that external fixator application prior to fixation was associated with a 4.63 times higher risk of arthrofibrosis.* **They concluded** that CPM machines and early, appropriate use of manipulation under anesthesia should be employed in order to prevent and treat arthrofibrosis, respectively.

Incorrect Answer:
Answer 2: Risk of post-traumatic arthritis is increased after complex tibial plateau fracture but typically does not occur within the first 6 months. Risk factors include axial malalignment, infection, instability and failure to establish congruent joint.
Answer 3: Injury to the nerves or vasculature around the knee can occur at the time of injury in length-unstable plateau fractures or at the time of fixation during dissection, but is less common than arthrofibrosis.
Answer 4: Infection after tibial plateau ORIF is not uncommon, occurring with varying incidence (6-12%), however, the balance of studies shows it to be less likely than arthrofibrosis, particularly after the use of a temporizing external fixator.
Answer 5: Instability can occur after tibial plateau fractures, especially those that masquerade as knee dislocations. However, this is far less common than arthrofibrosis after ORIF.
