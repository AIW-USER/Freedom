## Question

When performing an medial patellofemoral ligament (MPFL) reconstruction, placing the femoral tunnel too proximal would cause which of the following issues?

## Options

1. Graft is tight in extension
2. Excessive lateral patellar tilt
3. Patella baja
4. Graft is tight in flexion
5. Graft is loose in flexion

## Correct option

**4. Graft is tight in flexion**

## Answer explanation

When performing a medial patellofemoral ligament (MPFL) reconstruction, placing the femoral tunnel too proximal causes the graft to be over tightened which may over constrain the patella in the trochlear groove.

Patellar instability is common, particularly in younger athletes, and is typically caused by a non-contact twisting injury. The MPFL is the most important soft-tissue structure to be disrupted during dislocation. Most commonly, non-operative measures including rest, NSAIDs, and physical therapy are employed first. However, in cases with persistent symptoms or recurrent instability events, MPFL reconstruction may be indicated. Graft positioning is important, as placing the graft too proximally on the femur causes it to be too tight in flexion, while placing it too distally causes it to be too loose with flexion. Schottle’s point (Illustration A) is used intra-operatively to standardize femoral sided graft placement in order to obtain appropriate tension.

*Burrus et al review the correct positioning of the MPFL during reconstruction to ensure anatomic graft placement.* **They note** that failing to place the graft in an appropriate position can cause stiffness, anterior knee pain, and patellofemoral chondrosis. They discuss the “high and tight,” (too proximal) and “low and loose,” (too distal) femoral positioning mistakes and reiterate that the knee should be taken through a full range of motion before the graft is secured to ensure proper knee biomechanics are restored.

*Burrus et. al discuss the anatomy and biomechanical importance of the MPFL.* *They again note that femoral-sided graft positioning in MPFL is important in maintaining normal function of the ligament.* **They conclude** by reinforcing that proximal femur graft placement causes the graft to be too tight and distal femur graft placement causes the graft to be too loose.

Illustration A demonstrates Schottle’s point which rests just anterior to a line tangent to the posterior femoral cortex and between horizontal lines between Blumensaat’s and the superior aspect of the femoral condyle. Illustration B shows the anatomic location of the femoral sided MPFL insertion between the medial epicondyle and adductor tubercle.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Proximal femoral-sided graft placement would not cause the graft to be too tight in extension as there is minimal tension on the MPFL in full extension.
Answer 2: Placing the graft too proximally would not cause lateral patellar tilt, as this would tighten the graft and over constrain the patella medially.
Answer 3: Placing the graft too proximally would not cause patella baja.
Answer 5: Placing the graft too distally, not proximally, would cause it to be loose in flexion.
