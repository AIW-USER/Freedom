## Question

A 29-year-old male presents to the clinic after sustaining an injury while playing flag football three days prior. On physical exam, he has a palpable defect in his Achilles tendon, approximately 6cm from the calcaneal tuberosity. Compression of his posterior compartment with the knee in flexion while in a prone position does not produce any plantar flexion. He is counseled extensively on operative and non-operative treatment and requests surgical repair. You recommend a percutaneous repair because it offers what difference as compared to an open repair?

## Options

1. Increased plantar flexion strength
2. More rapid return to sport
3. Lower rate of tendon re-rupture
4. Lower incidence of wound complications
5. More rapid return to running

## Correct option

**4. Lower incidence of wound complications**

## Answer explanation

Percutaneous Achilles tendon repair is associated with a lower incidence of wound complications when compared to an open technique (Answer 4).

Achilles tendon ruptures are typically due to a sudden eccentric load placed across the tendon. The most common site of rupture is 4-6cm proximal to the calcaneal insertion, as this is an area of hypovascularity. A mid-substance Achilles tendon tear can be managed with or without surgery based on shared decision-making with the patient. Percutaneous techniques use a smaller surgical incision and are associated with a lower incidence of wound complications. However, there is a higher reported sural nerve injury rate. This is thought to be due to the passage of needles without direct visualization of the nerve.

*Henriquez et al. published their results from a retrospective review of 32 surgically treated patients with Achilles tendon ruptures.* **They observed** similar values of plantar flexion strength and range of motion. There was an increase in scar length and wound complications in the open group.

*Caolo et al. published a retrospective study of 185 patients who underwent percutaneous or open repairs.* *The 2 groups in this cohort study showed statistically similar rates of complications and equivalent improvement in patient-reported outcomes.* **The authors conclude** that surgical techniques should be based on patient and surgeon preferences.

*Attia et al. performed a meta-analysis of 10 randomized controlled trials, including a total of 522 patients.* The reported sural nerve injury rate was 3.4% in the percutaneous group and 0% in the open group. The superficial infection rate was 0.4% in the percutaneous group and 6.0% in the open group. Both of these were statistically significant.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Percutaneous and open techniques have similar reported post-operative plantar flexion strength.
Answer 2: Both techniques have similar return to sport timelines.
Answer 3: Both techniques have a similar re-rupture rate.
Answer 5: Both techniques have a similar time of returning to running.
