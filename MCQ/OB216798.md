## Question

A collegiate football player sustains a closed head injury during a game. His cervical spine is stabilized on the field and he taken to the ED where imaging reveals diffuse axonal injury. With respects to returning to football, how should the patient and his family should be counseled?

## Options

1. May return as soon as he is asymptomatic
2. May return when he is asymptomatic for 6 months
3. May return when he is asymptomatic for 1 year
4. May return when he is asymptomatic for 2 year
5. No return to football

## Correct option

**5. No return to football**

## Answer explanation

This patient sustained a severe closed head injury. Second impact syndrome is catastrophic cerebral edema resulting from loss of autoregulation of the brain blood supply and carries an increased mortality risk.

Concussions are a subset of mild traumatic brain injury (mTBI) characterized by acute transient impairment of neurologic function secondary to an impulsive force transmitted to the head. Diagnosis is made by careful neurological evaluation of an athlete following head trauma with a focus on cognitive function. Treatment is immediate removal from play followed by cognitive and physical rest for 24-48 hours, and subsequent completion of a graduated return to play protocol. Second impact syndrome is a second head trauma that occurs before the symptoms of a concussion have resolved. This may lead to catastrophic cerebral edema resulting from loss of autoregulation of the brain blood supply. It has an increased rate of death and disability. It is associated with male gender, young age and American football.

*Durand et al. reviews head injuries in collision sports.* *They report on second impact syndrome, which represents a severe cerebral autoregulatory dysfunction that can lead to death in an athlete who sustains a second (often minor) closed head trauma while still symptomatic from a previous head injury.* **They concluded** that generally, athletes who have suffered a severe closed head injury should not return to play; exceptions include athletes asymptomatic for 1 year who return to a noncontact sport and those who recover completely from an epidural hematoma without underlying brain injury.

*Cahill et al. reviews sports-related concussions.* **They report** that orthopedic surgeons, particularly those who are fellowship-trained in sports medicine, must have a current knowledge base of what a concussion is, how a concussion is diagnosed, and how a concussion should be managed. **They concluded** that orthopedic surgeons should understand the pathophysiology, assessment, and management of concussion so that they have a basic comprehension of this injury.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1,2,3,4: Second impact syndrome has a high rate of death and disability, with an increased mortality rate. It is recommended that someone who has sustained diffuse axonal injury should not return to contact sports
