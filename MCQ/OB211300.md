## Question

Which factor has been associated with worse patient-reported outcomes for those undergoing surgical correction of adult spinal deformity?

## Options

1. Lack of patient reported allergies
2. Ethnicity
3. Obesity
4. Preoperative Oswestry Disability Index scores
5. Preoperative sagittal vertical axis >+5 cm

## Correct option

**3. Obesity**

## Answer explanation

Complications and worse patient-reported outcomes in ASD corrective surgery have been associated with obesity.

Adult spinal deformity is the result of either residual adolescent idiopathic scoliosis or degenerative scoliosis leading to coronal and sagittal imbalance; with back pain the most common complaint. Sagittal imbalance is associated with poor patient functioning and quality of life pre- and post-operatively. The goals of surgical treatment are to restore sagittal vertical axis to within 5 cm of the posterior superior point of the S1 vertebral body and increasing lumbar lordosis to within 9 degrees of pelvic incidence. Surgical correction has been associated with many complications; with four or more medical comorbidities, insufficient sagittal correction, pseudoarthrosis, and obesity as predictors unsatisfactory surgical results.

*Smith et al. performed a retrospective review of 206 patients from the Adult Deformity Outcomes multi-institutional database for complications and outcomes associated with operative treatment based on patient age.* **They found** that increased patient age was associated with a worse preoperative level of function and pain as well as more perioperative complications. However, at 2-years follow-up there were no differences in outcomes compared to younger cohorts, **suggesting** that advanced age patients have the most to gain from ASD corrective surgery.

*Theologis et al. performed an analysis on a consecutive series of 267 patients through a multicenter ASD database to ascertain the effect of preoperative depression on outcomes at 2-years.* A greater degree of depression was found to be associated with greater preoperative pain and deformity. However, at 2-years post-op there was no difference in outcomes with regards to pre-op depression when comorbidities were taken into account.

*Soroceanu et al. performed a retrospective review including 241 patients through a multicenter database examining the effect of obesity on outcomes and complications following ASD corrective surgery.* *Patients that were obese were more likely to experience wound complications and infections than non-obese patients.* Furthermore, obese patients had a lower level of improvement at 2-years on patient-reported outcomes.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The absence of allergies has not been associated with poor outcomes following spine surgery. Observational studies have correlated the presence of patient-reported allergies with poor treatment response in lumbar spine patients.
Answer 2: Ethnicity has not been shown in the literature as a risk for surgical complications and poor outcomes in ASD corrective surgery.
Answer 4: Preoperative ODI scores have not been shown as a risk factor for poor outcomes and post-surgical complications. Worse ODI scores imply more severe ASD.
Answer 5: Preoperative SVA >+5 cm suggests a sagittal imbalance, which can be a cause of severe disability in the presence of ASD. However, if this is adequately corrected at the time of surgery it should have little impact on the outcome for the patient.
