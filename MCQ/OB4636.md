## Question

A 43-year-old women presents with a long-standing history of pain at the tip of her right non-dominant middle finger. Pain is exacerbated by cold weather. She denies systemic symptoms or preceding trauma. She has seen a number of physicians but no formal diagnosis as been made. Physical examination reveals no distinct mass or color change. Pain is reproduced by palpating over the ulnar nail bed of her distal middle finger. Radiographs and routine laboratory results are within normal limits. MRI images are shown in Figures A-C. What is the most likely diagnosis?

## Options

1. Raynaud's phenomenon
2. Epidermal inclusion cyst
3. Osteomyelitis
4. Glomus tumor
5. Osteoid osteoma

## Correct option

**4. Glomus tumor**

## Answer explanation

The clinical picture and MRI imaging is consistent with a diagnosis of glomus tumor.

Glomus tumors are benign hamartomas that most commonly present in the hand. The classic clinical presentation is the triad of fingertip pain, tenderness, and sensitivity to the cold. These lesions occur most common in females in the third and fourth decades. There are no known risk factors.

*Nazerani et al. reported 8 patients with a diagnosis of glomus tumor.* **They found** a incidence rate of 0.26% of all patients referred to a hand specialist. All were treated with excision with no recurrence

*Paliogiannis et al. reported 4 patients with a diagnosis of glomus tumor.* Intense point pain and hypersensitivity to cold was observed in all cases. All were treated with excision with no recurrence.

Figure A and B are proton-weighted coronal and STIR coronal MRI images, respectively. There is a well-defined nodular lesion in the distal middle finger. The small lesion (usually <7mm) is mildly hyperintense on proton density-weighted image and markedly hyperintense on T2-weighted scans. Figure C shows a contrast-enhanced fat-suppressed T1-weighted axial image of the distal middle finger. T1 imaging without contrast would show diffusely low signal intensity. Adding contrast, the lesion demonstrates intense, homogeneous enhancement in the subungual region. Illustrations A-C show intra-operative resection of a glomus tumour.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Raynaud's phenomenon would present with cold insensitivity, however no mass would be seen on imaging.
Answer 2: Epidermal inclusion cysts are most commonly associated with trauma. Point tenderness and sensitivity to cold are less likely to occur with epidermal inclusion cysts.
Answer 3: Chronic osteomyelitis would likely be picked up on radiography and MRI, and may show changes in laboratory markers or systemic symptoms.
Answer 5: Osteoid osteomas are benign bone lesions. Imaging would show radiolucent nodules surrounded by reactive sclerosis in the cortex of the bone.
