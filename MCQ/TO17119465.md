## Question

A professional bicyclist complains of knee pain while cycling. On examination tenderness is present on the under surface of the medial facet of patella. Deep flexion and full extension are painful. Axial fat-saturated spin-echo proton density–weighted MR images demonstrate a hypointense band (arrow) that covers the anterior surface of the medial femoral condyle. What is the diagnosis and treatment?

![[TO17119465-1.png]]

## Options

1. MPFL cyst and hydrocortisone injection
2. Sakakibara type c synovial plica and arthroscopic excision
3. Infrapatellar bursa and quadriceps eccentric exercises
4. Sakakibara type b synovial plica and arthroscopic ablation

## Correct option

**2. Sakakibara type c synovial plica and arthroscopic excision**

## Answer explanation

This is thickened and inflamed medial patellar plica (sakakibara type c).

Plica syndrome: In the human embryo knee is divided into partitions by synovial folds. By the end of 3-4 months these folds are absorbed and the knee becomes a single cavity. Partial resorption of these folds lead to persistence of knee septations. These embryonic membranes are known as synovial plicae. These are visible during arthroscopy. Mere presence of plica does not necessarily lead to symptoms. When inflamed, plica becomes fibrotic, thickened and inelastic. A thickened and inflamed plica may snap over femoral condyle or under patella producing pain and chondromalacia patellae. Four different types of synovial plicae have been described.

The most commonly encountered plicae of the knee (in descending order) are the infrapatellar plica (ligamentum mucosum), the suprapatellar plica, and the medio patellar plica.

The embryological work of Ogata and Uhthoff showed that the medio patellar plica is not a remnant of a septum of a distinct compartment during the development stage. Instead, it seems to be linked to the lateralized position of the patella and constitutes a remnant of mesenchymal tissue that was larger at the medial side of the patellofemoral region.

At arthroscopy, a complete suprapatellar plica is suggested only by the presence of a short suprapatellar pouch. The diagnosis is almost never made at arthroscopy, except when the irrigation needle is inserted into the bursa instead of the knee joint cavity.

1. Suprapatellar plica: This spans from the anterior surface of femoral metaphysis to the medial wall of the joint.

2. Medio-patellar plica (Lino band): This is the most common cause of plica syndrome. This spans over the medial wall of the joint from suprapatellar origin to infrapatellar fat pad.

3. lateral plica: It is least common among all described plicae. It spans along the lateral wall from the lateral patellar facet to the infrapatellar region.

4. ligament of mucosum or infrapatellar plica: This is most frequently encountered plica (most commonly present) during arthroscopy. It is rarely symptomatic. It extends from intercondylar notch to infrapatellar fat pad (Hoffa’ fat pad)

Sakakibara arthroscopic classification scheme for medio patellar plica:

- Type A: cordlike elevation in the synovial wall
- Type B: Shelf like appearance but does not cover the anterior surface of the medial femoral condyle
- Type C: which is large with a shelf like appearance and covers the anterior surface of the medial femoral condyle
- Type D: plica has a central defect (fenestrated plica).

Types A and B are not likely to produce symptoms. Types C and D can be trapped between the medial condyle and the patella, becoming thickened and hard and even causing internal damage to the knee joint. They can symptomatically impinge on the articular cartilage of the medial patellar facet in flexion or the cartilage of the medial femoral condyle in extension. In such cases, arthroscopic resection is the treatment of choice.

Answer: 2
