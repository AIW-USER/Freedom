## Question

Multiple physical ability assessments have been studied and identified as a predictor of post-operative function and risk for various complications. Following hemiarthroplasty for femoral neck fracture, an increased time to complete the timed Up and Go (TUG) Test portends which of the following at two years post-operative?

## Options

1. Decreased patient mortality
2. Higher likelihood of independent living
3. Higher likelihood of sustaining a fall
4. Increased need for a walking aid
5. Increased patient mortality

## Correct option

**4. Increased need for a walking aid**

## Answer explanation

The Timed Up and Go (TUG) test has been identified as a validated predictor for the need for a walking aid following hip hemiarthroplasty for femoral neck fracture (Answer 4).

Proximal femur fractures are common injuries, especially in aging populations. These injuries most commonly affect Caucasian females, and the United States has been identified as having the highest incidence of these fracture rates worldwide. It is therefore unfortunate that these injuries serve as a harbinger for a variety of complications. The baseline mortality rate of these injuries is 25-30% at one year, and these fractures represent the most expensive fracture to treat on a per-person basis. Most patients experience at least a one-level loss of independence (i.e those with a cane will require a walker). Recovery is dependent on timely surgical intervention and safe and appropriate rehabilitation. In order to aid rehabilitation, a variety of functional assessments have been proposed, studied and identified as predictors of post-operative function, need for assistive devices and/or mortality.

*Laflamme et al. conducted a prospective study evaluating the utility of the TUG test to predict functional outcomes in patients undergoing hip hemiarthroplasty for femoral neck fracture.* The TUG scores were significantly higher at 4-days and 3-weeks post-operatively in patients requiring a walking aid compared with patients walking independently at two-years. Patients who performed the test in >58s at 4-days post-operatively had an eightfold more significant risk of needing an assistive device.

*Springer et al. prospectively analyzed the unipedal stance test (UPST) with eyes open and closed in healthy subjects to establish normative values for the test across age and gender groups.* Performance on the test was found to be age-specific and not related to gender. Overall, the USPT represents a reliable method of quantifying static balance ability, and lower values on this test have been identified as a risk factor for post-operative fall.

*Kristensen et al studied the relative and absolute inter-tester reliability of TUG in patients with hip fractures.* **The authors found** that the TUG has high interobserver reliability and an improvement of 6.2 seconds for a patient with a baseline of 20s indicates a change in functional mobility. *Overall, their findings build upon those of Laflamme et al. in establishing the TUG as a reliable predictor of post-operative mobility and the need for an assistive device.*

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1 and 5: The American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) score has been validated as a predictor of post-operative mortality following surgical intervention for proximal femur fractures
Answer 2: The TUG test does not predict the likelihood of independent living.
Answer 3: The UPST has been validated as a predictor for the likelihood of sustaining a fall post-operatively due to loss of balance.
