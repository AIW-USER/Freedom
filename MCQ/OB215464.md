## Options

2. 60-year-old active male with the radiograph seen in Figure B

## Correct option

**3. 67-year-old active male with radiograph seen in Figure C**

## Answer explanation

The fracture seen in Figure C is a Vancouver B2 fracture and should be treated with ORIF of the femur fracture followed by revision of the femoral stem with a long porous-coated cementless stem.

Postoperative periprosthetic femur fractures occur in up to 3% of total hip arthroplasties. The Vancouver classification (Illustration A) is useful in guiding management of these injuries based on the location of the fracture relative to the femoral stem, presence of stem loosening, and quality of proximal femoral bone. In cases of Vancouver B2 fractures, the fracture is adjacent to the stem with loosening of the femoral stem. However, adequate bone stock allows for femoral fixation and revision. Femoral component revision is necessary to bypass the fracture with a long, cementless stem in order to obtain diaphyseal fixation. Additionally, ORIF of the femur fracture with cerclage wires or locking screw fixation proximally and bicortical screws distally provides adequate stability.

*Khan et al. reviewed Vancouver B2 and B3 periprosthetic fractures and associated best treatment practices.* **They noted** that B2 and B3 fractures treated without revision of the femoral component had a higher rate of reoperation. **They conclude** that these fracture patterns must be recognized and appropriately managed, ideally with revision of the femoral stem +/- ORIF.

*Moreta et al. evaluated functional outcomes and radiographic results in periprosthetic femur fractures.* Vancouver B type fractures were most common and risk factors for fracture included osteoporosis, osteolysis, and stem loosening. **They found** excellent or good radiographic results in 71% of patients but noted that there was a high rate of complications and over half of the patients did not regain their pre-fracture level of function.

Figure A demonstrates a Vancouver B1 periprosthetic femur fracture. Figure B depicts a broken cementless femoral stem. Figure C shows a Vancouver B2 periprosthetic femur fracture. Figure D demonstrates a Vancouver C periprosthetic femur fracture. Figure E represents a Vancouver AG periprosthetic femur fracture.

Illustration A shows a chart depicting the Vancouver classification and associated treatment recommendations.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Figure A shows a Vancouver B1 periprosthetic femur fracture given the fracture location adjacent to the stem and well-fixed appearance proximally. This fracture would be best treated with ORIF of the femur fracture.
Answer 2: Figure B demonstrates a fracture of the cementless femoral stem, not a periprosthetic femur fracture. This would best be treated by prosthetic revision.
Answer 4: Figure D shows a Vancouver C periprosthetic femur fracture with the fracture distal to the femoral stem. This would be best treated with ORIF of the femoral stem only.
Answer 5: Figure E depicts a Vancouver AG periprosthetic femur fracture of the greater trochanter. This would be best treated with ORIF of the displaced greater trochanter fragment given displacement >2 cm.
