## Question

A 33-year-old male presents to the office with low back pain radiating down his right leg. He reports the pain has gotten worse over the last several weeks. He is fairly active and plays pick-up basketball at his local gym 3 times per week. He takes an anti-inflammatory daily which provides mild relief of his symptoms. On physical exam, he has weakness with right great toe extension and relatively tight hamstrings. With straight leg raise he does have moderate discomfort in his low back bilaterally. His pedal pulses are palpable and he has a normal adult hair pattern on his bilateral lower extremities. Which of the following pathologies is most likely to be causing his symptoms?

## Options

1. Central disc herniation L3-4
2. Far lateral disc herniation L4-5
3. Central disc herniation L5-S1
4. Grade 1 isthmic spondylolisthesis L3-4
5. Grade 1 isthmic spondylolisthesis L5-S1

## Correct option

**5. Grade 1 isthmic spondylolisthesis L5-S1**

## Answer explanation

Adult isthmic spondylolisthesis at L5-S1 often causes compression of the exiting L5 nerve root in the L5-S1 foramen.

Adult isthmic spondylolisthesis is caused by a defect of the pars interarticularis and results in anterior translation of the vertebral body relative to the vertebral body below it. It is most commonly due to repetitive microtrauma and occurring at L5-S1 (82%). Compression of the L5 nerve root as it exits the foramen may be caused by hypertrophic fibrous tissue in the pars defect, uncinate spur formation on the L5 body, or bulging of the L5-S1 disc. On exam, patients are likely to present with axial back pain, dysesthesias to the lateral lower leg, and/or EHL weakness. Most patients are treated nonoperatively with lifestyle modifications and therapy, however, decompression and fusion are recommended for those with incapacitating pain who fail 6 months of conservative measures.

*Petraco et al reviewed the effects of the reduction of L5-S1 spondylolisthesis on the L5 nerve root.* **They report** that injury to the L5 nerve root with reduction of a high-grade spondylolisthesis not linear; with 71% of the total L5 strain occurring during the second half of reduction. **They conclude** that partial reduction may be significantly safer in the surgical treatment of this adult spondylolisthesis.

*Jones et al reviewed adult isthmic spondylolisthesis.* **They report** most symptomatic cases occur at L5-S1 and that most are successfully managed without surgery, however, patients with intractable pain or neurologic symptoms may benefit from decompression and fusion. **They conclude** that surgical intervention has shown >80% success in appropriately selected patients with a low incidence of complications.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1 & 2: Great toe extension is controlled by the L5 nerve root (EHL). A central disc herniation at L3-4 and a far lateral disc herniation at L4-5 would both cause symptoms related to the L4 nerve root.
Answer 3: A central disc herniation at L5-S1 would cause symptoms related to S1 such as lateral foot dysesthesias and weakness with ankle plantar flexion and eversion.
Answer 4: Spondylolisthesis at L3-4 is unlikely to involve the L5 nerve root.
