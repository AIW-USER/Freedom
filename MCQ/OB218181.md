## Question

A 25-year-old male presents to the trauma bay after an all-terrain-vehicle accident where it rolled on top of him. He complains primarily of left forearm pain and radiographs demonstrate a midshaft both bone forearm fracture. You recommend surgical intervention with open reduction and internal fixation. Which construct would provide the greatest bending rigidity?

## Options

1. 3.5mm stainless steel dynamic compression plate for ulna & one-third tubular plate for radius
2. 3.5mm titanium dynamic compression plate for radius & one-third tubular plate for ulna
3. 3.5mm titanium dynamic compression plate for radius & 3.5 mm stainless steel dynamic compression plate for ulna
4. 3.5mm stainless steel dynamic compression plates for both radius & ulna
5. One-third tubular plates for both radius & ulna

## Correct option

**4. 3.5mm stainless steel dynamic compression plates for both radius & ulna**

## Answer explanation

The greatest degree of bending rigidity during open reduction internal fixation (ORIF) of both bone forearm fractures is accomplished by using 3.5mm stainless steel dynamic compression plates compared to one-third tubular plates.

Both bone forearm fractures are usually caused by significant trauma to the forearm and, in adults, require ORIF to provide sufficient stability and healing. These fractures are good examples of the importance of using basic fracture principles to provide rigid fixation. They also underline the importance of understanding available implant choices. To achieve optimal bending rigidity, plate material and structural properties must be considered. 3.5mm dynamic compression plates are thicker and more rigid than one-third tubular plates. Stainless steel plates also have a higher Young's modulus compared to titanium, causing them to be stiffer with more rigidity. Therefore, the best option for ORIF in these cases is with 3.5mm plates on both the ulna and radius. This is typically done through a two-incision technique, as one-incision fixation tends to have a higher rate of radioulnar synostosis.

*Schulte et al. published a review looking at the management of both bone forearm fractures in adults with a particular focus on instrumentation and biomechanics of fracture fixation.* **They noted** that 3.5mm plates were preferred given their stiffness compared to smaller plates (i.e. one-third tubular) and their ability to be covered by soft tissue compared to 4.5mm plates. They reinforce that the primary goal of fixation is rigid compression at the fracture site and 3.5mm plates provide a high rate of healing with lower rates of complication than others.

*Bauer et al. reviewed forearm fractures treated with ORIF, of which 89 were both bone fractures.* *Of the 65 treated with a two-incision approach, only 1 (0.02%) went on to synostosis, compared to 5/12 (41.7%) of cases treated with one-incision techniques.* This highlights the importance of considering the most appropriate approach and instrumentation in these cases.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1-3,5: Stainless steel 3.5mm plates for both the radius and ulna would provide the greatest bending rigidity for the overall construct. Titanium has less rigidity than stainless steel and one-third tubular plates have less rigidity than 3.5mm dynamic compression plates
