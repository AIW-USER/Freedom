## Question

Which of the following structures is at risk during proximal dissection of a single lateral perifibular approach for compartment syndrome of the leg?

## Options

1. Common peroneal nerve
2. Superficial peroneal nerve
3. Deep peroneal nerve
4. Anterior tibial artery
5. Lateral inferior genicular artery

## Correct option

**2. Superficial peroneal nerve**

## Answer explanation

The perifibular approach is carried out through a straight lateral incision beginning just posterior and parallel to the fibula distal to the fibular neck to the tip of the lateral malleolus. At the proximal end of the incision, the superficial peroneal nerve must be identified and protected. Elevation of the soleus off the posterior fibula ensures proper deep compartment release. The anterior edge of the incision is then retracted to expose the anterior and lateral compartments, and at this point, care must be taken to avoid the superficial peroneal nerve as it exits the fascia of the lateral compartment and runs anteriorly in the distal third of the leg.

The referenced article by Whitesides is a review of compartment syndrome pathology, diagnosis, and treatment.

Incorrect answers:
1) The proximal extent of the fasciotomy does not extend up to the fibular neck, and is distal to the split of the common peroneal nerve in this technique.
3) Deep peroneal nerve is in the anterior compartment and away from this incision.
4) The anterior tibial artery is in the anterior compartment and away from this incision.
5) The lateral inferior genicular artery is more midline and away from this incision.
