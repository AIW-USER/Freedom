## Question

While covering the local under-18 youth soccer team, a player comes to you after a routine urinalysis demonstrated microscopic hematuria. You're initially concerned for exercise-induced hematuria, as the team just finished competing in the national championship series. After 72 hours of rest, a repeat urinalysis demonstrates persistent microscopic hematuria. Which of the following is a possible etiology of this patient's persistent hematuria?

## Options

1. Pre-workout use
2. Mononucleosis
3. Rhabdomyolysis
4. Sickle cell trait
5. None of the above are responsible

## Correct option

**4. Sickle cell trait**

## Answer explanation

Sickle cell disease can cause persistent hematuria due to papillary necrosis.

Exertional hematuria is a condition where gross hematuria can be prompted by high-intensity or long-duration workouts. The condition can be confirmed by a repeat urinalysis after cessation of higher intensity activity. Hematuria that persists for greater than 72 hours after cessation of exercise should be investigated further. The differential for persistent hematuria includes papillary necrosis in sickle cell trait/disease patients, urinary tract infections, sexually transmitted diseases, kidney stones, and kidney contusions.

*Jones et al. conducted a review of sports-related hematuria in 1997.* A combination of 64 published articles was used to find an incidence of 38% of microhematuria in asymptomatic athletes. Proposed mechanisms for hematuria included foot-strike hemolysis, bladder/kidney trauma, dehydration-induced hematuria, as well as increased circulation rate and temperature.

*Lippi et al. wrote an opinion paper outlining the definition, epidemiology, diagnostic and clinical considerations for exertional hematuria.* The definition proposed was the “painless appearance of erythrocytes in urine after recent physical exercise, not directly attributable to external traumatic injuries to the genitourinary system, and spontaneously resolving with rest.” If hematuria cannot be temporally associated with exercise, a further diagnostic investigation is recommended.

*Shephard conducted a review of sports-related hematuria in 2016.* Risk factors associated with hematuria included exercise intensity, posture, age, heat load, altitude, and kidney disease. Mechanisms for exercise-induced hematuria included vascular changes, hypoxia, lactate accumulation, oxidant stress, and hormonal changes. Progression to permanent renal damage was found to be rare.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Pre-workout is thought to make people more susceptible to exercise-induced hematuria, however does not cause hematuria independently.
Answer 2: Mononucleosis is associated with splenomegaly, generalized fatigue, body aches, and fever. Hematuria is not associated.
Answer 3: Rhabdomyolysis causes urine dipstick to be positive for blood, however, microhematuria is not present. This is because rhabdomyolysis causes myoglobinuria, and not true hemaglobinuria.
Answer 5: Sickle cell is a known source for persistent hematuria.
