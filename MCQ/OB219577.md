## Question

A 67-year-old male presents to the clinic for evaluation of a painless right thigh mass that he notes has been slowly growing in size over the last two to three years. He denies any fever, chills, or weight loss. Radiographs of the right femur are obtained, which fail to demonstrate any bony or soft tissue abnormality. He is sent for MRI, as shown in Figures A-C. ## Options

1. t(2;13) translocation
2. MDM2 and CDK4 amplification
3. ETV6-NTRK3 amplification
4. t(12;16) translocation
5. SYT-SSX1 amplification

## Correct option

**2. MDM2 and CDK4 amplification**

## Answer explanation

This 67-year-old male has evidence of a large right thigh mass. Murine double minutes 2 (MDM2) and cyclin-dependent kinase 4 (CDK4) amplification would suggest an atypical lipomatous tumor, which requires marginal excision alone (Answer 2).

While the overall incidence of liposarcoma is low in the general population, the entity is a commonly seen soft tissue sarcoma, comprising 20% of all soft tissue sarcomas in adults. Liposarcoma possesses a spectrum of sub-types that range in severity, with the well-differentiated/atypical lipomatous tumor (ALT) being the most benign-appearing, followed by the intermediate myxoid subtype (most common subtype) and finally, the higher grade tumors, which include round cell, pleomorphic, and dedifferentiated subtypes. Aside from distinguishing histologic features, ALTs are unique, considering they express MDM2 and CDK4, whereas higher-grade liposarcomas and benign lipomas typically do not. With ALTs, management includes marginal resection alone, while intermediate and high-grade subtypes require wide resection with adjuvant radiotherapy +/- chemotherapy (for myxoid).

*Knebel et al. performed a retrospective review of 113 patients (66 diagnosed with lipomas, 47 with ALTs), examining each tumor's characteristics (location, MRI signaling, septae thickness, size).* **The authors noted** ALTs were more commonly in the lower extremities and more likely to be larger, intramuscular, possess thick septae, and enhance on MRI. **The authors conclude** the use of standard MRI can aid in the diagnosis of benign-appearing lipomatous tumors.

*Brisson et al. performed a retrospective review of 87 patients diagnosed with either lipomas (n=54) or ALTs (n=33), investigating patient and MRI characteristics of each tumor.* **The authors noted** patients over 60, with lesions greater than 10 cm, lower limb location, and the presence of non-fatty areas on MRI increased the likelihood of ALT significantly. **The authors conclude** that lesion size and lipomatous content are the most reliable MRI characteristics differentiating between lipomas and ALTs.

*Ryan et al. performed a retrospective review of 49 patients, 18 of which were diagnosed with ALT and 31 with lipomas, examining the diagnostic accuracy of fellowship-trained physician expertise (two orthopaedic oncologists and two musculoskeletal radiologists) and a formula based on various MRI tumor characteristics (margins, presence of thick septae, nodularity, enhancement).* The level of accuracy was then compared to MDM2-confirmed diagnoses. **The authors found** physician reading and formula accuracy were 73.5% and 71%, respectively, which was not significantly different (p=0.71). Similar sensitivities (83% for both, p=1.000) and specificities (64.5 vs. 67.7%; p=0.659) were also noted between the formula and expert observers, respectively, when detecting WDLs. **The authors conclude** that while MRI provides valuable additional diagnostic information, MDM2 detection remains the gold standard for the diagnosis of ALTs.

Figures A-C represent coronal, sagittal, and axial T1-weighted MR sequencing of the right thigh, demonstrating a large, intramuscular, homogenous lipomatous-appearing mass.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Biopsy showing a t(2;13) translocation suggests alveolar rhabdomyosarcoma, which necessitates wide surgical resection.
Answer 3: Amplification of ETV6-NTRK3 represents infantile fibrosarcoma, which requires wide surgical resection.
Answer 4: As above, a t(12;16) translocation suggests myxoid liposarcoma, which necessitates wide resection and adjuvant therapies.
Answer 5: Amplification of the SYT-SSX1 fusion gene represents synovial sarcoma and requires wide resection.
