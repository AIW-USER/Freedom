## Question

Loss of bow tie sign on MRI indicates (arthroscopy- knee arthroscopy- meniscus injury)

## Options

1. Flipped meniscal tear
2. Bucket handle tear of meniscus
3. Cartilage fragment from medial femoral condyle
4. Osteochondral defect of medial femoral condyle

## Correct option

**2. Bucket handle tear of meniscus**

## Answer explanation

The normal meniscus resembles a bow tie on sagittal images on MRI. The absent bow tie sign on the sagittal image is present when fewer than two distinct bow tie segments of the meniscus are seen on sequential images. As the normal meniscus laterally measures 9-12 mm in width, it should be seen on a number of adjacent images (2-3 images if imaged with 3 mm thick slices with a 1 mm skip). So, absence of bow tie on 2 consecutive sagittal images indicates loss of meniscal volume indicating bucket handle tear.

This sign has a higher accuracy rate than other findings common with bucket-handle tears, such as the double PCL sign.

Answer: 2
