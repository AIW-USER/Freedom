A 6 year old boy has presented to your clinic with history of a some injury at the left leg which was managed with casting but failed to unite. ## Options

1. Anteromedial bowing of tibia
2. Anterolateal bowing of tibia
3. Posteromedial bowing of tibia
4. Isolated anterior bowing.

## Correct option

**2. Anterolateal bowing of tibia**

## Answer explanation

The boy in the picture is showing café-au-lait spots, typical feature of neurofibromatosis.

Anterolateral bowing and congenital pseudarthrosis of the tibia are congenital conditions, most associated with NF1. Patients with NF1 may have physical manifestations such as café-au-lait spots. With NF1, the gene encoding the protein neurofibromin found on chromosome 17 most often undergoes a spontaneous mutation. Diagnosis is confirmed with radiographs of the tibia. Treatment is nonoperative with bracing for patients who are weight-bearing without pseudarthrosis or fracture. Surgical correction is indicated in the presence of pseudarthrosis or fracture.

Anteromedial bowing is seen in fibular deficiency. Posteromedial bowing is physiologic which tends to resolve and rarely fractures.

Answer: 2
