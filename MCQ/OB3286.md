## Question

A 15-year-old cross country runner complains of vague shin pain. A radiograph of the tibia is seen in Figure A. A biopsy is performed and is shown in Figure B. Which of the following chromosomal translocations is most likely associated with this condition?

![[q3286-a-case-b-tibia-xray-a-parsons.png]]
*Figure A*

![[q3286-b-ewings3.jpg]]
*Figure B*

## Options

1. t(10;20)
2. t(11;22)
3. t(X;18)
4. t(9;22)
5. t(2;13)

## Correct option

**2. t(11;22)**

## Answer explanation

The clinical presentation, images, and biopsy are consistent with Ewings sarcoma. Ewings sarcoma is a pediatric round cell bone tumor resulting from a translocation of chromosomes 11 and 22 which leads to the formation of a fusion protein (EWS-FLI 1). Radiographs demonstrate a lytic lesion with periosteal reaction (“onion skin” type). An MRI is useful to evaluate soft-tissue involvement (see Illustration A). The tumor has low signal intensity on T1-weighted images compared with the normal high signal intensity of the bone marrow. On T2-weighted images, the tumor is hyperintense compared with muscle. Histologically, uniform sheets of small round blue cells are seen with round or oval nuclei of uniform size as demonstrated in Figure B.

Incorrect Answers:

Synovial sarcoma is associated with t(X;18).
Extraskeletal myxoid Chondrosarcoma is associated with t(9;22).
Rhabdomyosarcoma is associated with t(2;13).
