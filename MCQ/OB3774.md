## Question

A 55-year-old patient presents with lower lumbar back pain. A lateral radiograph of the L3 vertebral bone is shown in Figure A. Figure B and C are pre-operative biopsies. From the following options, what is the most likely diagnosis?

![[q3774-a-gct-spine.jpg]]
*Figure A*

![[q3774-b-gct-distal-femur-8.jpg]]
*Figure B*

![[q3774-c-03-62c.jpg]]
*Figure C*

## Options

1. Osteoblastoma
2. Osteoblastic osteosarcoma
3. Chondrosarcoma
4. Ewing's Sarcoma
5. Giant Cell Tumor

## Correct option

**5. Giant Cell Tumor**

## Answer explanation

The image demonstrates an expansile lytic lesion in the L3 vertebral body. Histology shows clustering of giant cells. The most likely diagnosis is giant cell tumor of the spine.

The proper evaluation of lytic bone lesions of the spine is critical. Metastatic carcinoma is the most common malignant tumor of the spine, and any bone lesion of the spine in older patients should raise concern for metastasis and be properly evaluated. Primary tumors are more rare. The most common primary tumors found in the spine are osteoid osteoma, osteoblastoma, aneurysmal bone cysts, hemangiomas, eosinophilic granuloma, and giant cell tumors. Chondrosarcoma, Ewing's sarcoma, and osteosarcomas are uncommon in the spine.

*Ciftdemir et al. reviewed tumors of the spine.* **They state** that hemangiomas and exostoses are the most common primary tumors of the spine, with an estimated prevalence between 11% and 14%.

*Messerschmitt et al. reviewed osteosarcomas.* Compared to the metaphysis of the distal femur, less common sites for osteosarcoma include the pelvis, the spine, and the craniofacial bones.

*Donthineni et al. reviewed the metastatic behaviour of GCT of the spine.* *A total of 51 cases of GCT of the spine were identified, of which 7 (13%) had metastatic disease of the lung.* *At final follow-up (ranging from 18 to 126 months), two patients had died of the disease, two had no evidence of the disease, and three were alive with disease.*

Figure A is a lateral radiograph of the lumbar spine showing an expansile lytic lesion in the vertebral body. Figures B and C show histology consistent with giant cell tumor (Numerous osteoclast-like giant cells uniformly distributed throughout tumor, spindle and round / oval mononuclear cells also present with interspersed hemorrhage)

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Osteoblastoma is common in the posterior elements of the spine. They usually present in younger population. Histology shows anastomosing trabeculae of osteoid and woven bone rimmed by a single layer of benign activated osteoblasts, numerous osteoclasts, loose fibrovascular stroma between bone trabeculae, and intralesional hemorrhage/secondary ABC is common.
Answer 2: Osteosarcoma only occurs in the spine in 3% of all osteosarcomas. The histopathology would show pure spindle cell growth with minimal matrix.
Answer 3: Chondrosarcoma lesions may arise in the vertebral body (5%), the posterior elements (40%), or both (45%), since there are three growth centers in each vertebra from which the tumor originates. Plain radiographs demonstrate spinal chondrosarcoma as a well-defined mass with internal calcification. Histology is variable depending on the grade but shows a malignant cartilage-forming tumor that does not produce osteoid, often adjacent to a lower grade bland hyaline cartilage.
Answer 4: Ewing's Sarcoma of the spine would show dense, small round blue cells on histology.
