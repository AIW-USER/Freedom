## Question

A large 2-week-old old infant presents to the pediatric orthopaedic clinic for evaluation. The mother reports that delivery was difficult and her labor was prolonged. On examination the patient's shoulder is adducted and internally rotated, his forearm is pronated, and his elbow is extended. Given the patient's diagnosis, you recommend observation and daily passive stretching exercises by the parents. Which of the following is most accurate regarding the outcomes of this condition?

## Options

1. Early twitch biceps activity is suggestive of less-favorable outcomes
2. Only 30-40% of patients who present with Horner's syndrome (ptosis, miosis and anhydrosis) recover spontaneous motor function
3. The patient's palsy is the most common type observed, and is due to involvement of C7 and C8
4. Total recovery is possible if the biceps and deltoid demonstrate anti-gravity strength by 3 months
5. Up to 50% of cases resolve without surgical intervention

## Correct option

**4. Total recovery is possible if the biceps and deltoid demonstrate anti-gravity strength by 3 months**

## Answer explanation

This patient has findings consistent with Erb's Palsy, a type of obstetric brachial plexus palsy (OBPP). Often, complete recovery is possible if the biceps and deltoid demonstrate anti-gravity strength by 3 months of age (Answer 4).

OBPP is an injury to the brachial plexus that occurs during birth, usually secondary to a traction-type mechanism. It occurs in 1 to 4 per 1,000 live births but is occurring less frequently due to improved obstetric care. Risk factors for OBPP include macrosomia, multiparous pregnancy, difficult presentation, shoulder dystocia, forceps delivery, breech position, and prolonged labor. The most common type of OBPP is Erb's Palsy, secondary to injury to the C5 and C6 nerve roots. Patients will typically present with an adducted and internally rotated shoulder, a pronated forearm, and an extended elbow, colloquially known as the "waiter's tip position" (Illustration A).

The initial treatment for patients with OBPP is observation and daily passive exercises by the parents (Illustration B) for the first three months of life. Up to 90% of patients will have spontaneous return of shoulder abduction, elbow flexion, and wrist and finger extension. Return of biceps function is a positive prognostic sign, and patients with biceps return are managed with physical therapy and observation. On the other hand, Horner's syndrome is a poor prognostic sign as it indicates damage to the sympathetic chain (i.e. pre-ganglionic injury root avulsion). Patients with Horner's syndrome are treated with microsurgery and brachial plexus reconstruction or peripheral nerve transfers. Early (<3 months) versus delayed (>3 months but < 1 year) surgery is commonly debated.

*Lagerkvist et al. performed a prospective, population-based study of the incidence, recovery, and residual impairment following OBP.* *Of the 38,749 children born between 1999-2001 in western Sweden, 114 (70 males, 44 females) had OBBP.* Return of biceps function by 3 months of age was associated with complete recovery in 100% of patients. Overall, 18 (15.8%) of patients experienced persistent OBPP, but most could perform functional activities. In summary, **the authors concluded** that most children with OBPP experience complete, spontaneous recovery, and that the presence of biceps function by 3 months is highly predictive of complete recovery.

*Dodwell et al. reported on a technique for combined glenoid anteversion osteotomy and tendon transfer(s) for OBPP.* *Their study included 32 patients with severe glenohumeral dysplasia who underwent the aforementioned procedure.* **The authors note** that the axillary, radial, thoracodorsal, and suprascapular nerves and vessels are all at risk and must be protected, and that contractures can occur with improper tensioning. **The authors note** that these procedures are not indicated in patients below four years of age, highlighting the role of appropriate early recognition and management in newborns born with OBPP.

*Abzug et al. provide a review of the evaluation and management of OBPP.* **They note** that physical examination is reliable in establishing the diagnosis and that the initial treatment for all patients is observation, passive range of motion exercises, and tactile stimulation. Surgery is reserved for patients who do not experience spontaneous resolution and typically consists of microsurgical nerve surgery, tendon transfers, and/or osteotomies.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Early twitch biceps activity is a favorable prognostic sign, and is classically associated with the potential for full, spontaneous recovery.
Answer 2: Only 10% of patients who present with Horner's syndrome recover spontaneous motor function.
Answer 3: While Erb's palsy is the most common type of OBPP, it is due to the involvement of C5 and C6.
Answer 5: Up to 90% of all cases are resolved without surgical intervention.
