## Question

A patient has come with a Meniscal injury. On examination you observed that pain occurs in deep flexion and external rotation of knee. Likely injury is

## Options

1. Tear of posterior horn Medial Meniscus
2. Tear in body of Medial Meniscus
3. Discoid lateral meniscus with a tear
4. Tear of anterior horn of Lateral meniscus

## Correct option

**1. Tear of posterior horn Medial Meniscus**

## Answer explanation

Ans: A
EXPLANATION: -
The question is based on understanding of the popular meniscal injury diagnostic test- The McMurray’s test.
The McMurray's test, as described in Corea et al (named after Thomas Porter McMurray who was a British orthopaedic surgeon), was designed to detect tears in the posterior segment of the meniscus. It is performed by placing the knee beyond 90° of flexion and then rotating the tibia on the femur into full internal rotation to test the lateral meniscus, or full external rotation to test the medial meniscus. The same maneuvers are performed in gradually increasing degrees of knee flexion to progressively load more posterior segments of the menisci. No valgus or varus stress is applied. During the maneuver, the joint line is palpated both medially and laterally. A positive test is considered to be a thud or click that can sometimes be heard but can always be felt.

Now, in given question pain is observed on deep flexion which means the posterior segments of the menici involved has been loaded. Since external rotation is producing pain, medial meniscus is likely housing the pathology.
