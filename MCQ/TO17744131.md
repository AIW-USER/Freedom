## Question

Which is an incorrect statement regarding proximal humeral fracture [TRAUMA-Proximal Humerus Fracture]

## Options

1. When treated nonoperatively, varus posteromedial fractures have a better outcome than valgus impacted fractures.
2. In an active young person GT fixation is required for displacement equal or more than 5 mm
3. Reverse shoulder arthroplasty is a viable option for AVN head with GT non-union
4. Medial comminution is a risk factor for failure of plate fixation

## Correct option

**2. In an active young person GT fixation is required for displacement equal or more than 5 mm**

## Answer explanation

Proximal humeral fractures:

Conservative management:

Most patients sustaining minimally displaced fractures achieve good results in a high

percentage of shoulders.

As a group, when treated nonoperatively, varus posteromedial fractures have a better outcome than valgus impacted fractures. Patients with displaced fractures obtain worse results compared to those with undisplaced ones when treated conservatively.

Indications for Surgical fixation:

 As such, surgery is considered for fracture-dislocations, head splitting (HS) fractures, head depression fractures involving more than 20% to 25% of the articular surface, unstable surgical neck fractures, fractures with tuberosity displacement over 1 cm, and severe varus or valgus displacement of the humeral head, open fractures, fractures associated with disruption of the suspensory

complex of the shoulder, fractures associated with vascular injuries, and pathologic

fractures.

Factors associated with locking plate failure in proximal humeral fractures

Open reduction and internal fixation using periarticular precontoured locking plates represents the workhorse for surgical management of proximal humeral fractures. However, many studies have reported complications (in up to 50% of the cases), following locking plate fixation including screw backout, screw cut-out, plate failure, malreduction, malunion, and nonunion. Intra-articular screw penetration is the most frequent complication reported, and can be the result of intraoperative insertion of excessively long screws by mistake (primary screw penetration) or fracture collapse with loss of reduction leading to late screw penetration.

Several factors have been associated to loss of reduction with locking plates, including advanced age, osteoporosis, smoking, preoperative varus deformity, varus malreduction, medial comminution and inadequate medial support, and failure to incorporate the rotator cuff to the construct with tension bands.

A nearly anatomic reduction, correct positioning of the plate in reference to the head segment, drilling only the lateral cortex, use or relatively short screws and avoidance of primary penetration, restoration of the calcar region, calcar support, neutralizing rotator cuff forces with sutures, and reinforcement of the reconstruction with structural bone graft, osteoconductive cements, or acrylic cement are all strategies aimed to decrease complication rates.

Indications for intramedullary nailing: The main indications for proximal humerus interlocking IM nailing are displaced two-part surgical neck fractures, especially those with extension into the humeral diaphysis, and pathologic fractures. Three-part greater tuberosity fractures may also be amenable to fixation with IM nailing. While IM nailing of four-part fractures has been reported, poorer outcomes can be expected in this patient group and most studies do not recommend its use in these cases. IM nailing is not recommended in varus posteromedial fractures with both tuberosities fractured or in head split fractures.

Surgical Indications for tuberosity fractures:

Displacement over 1 cm or tuberosity head overlapping over 20% represents our indications

for internal fixation in the majority of tuberosity fractures. Suture fixation is usually best, and it can be performed with arthroscopic assistance. Elderly individuals may tolerate up to 2 cm of displacement and 40% of overlap, as long as the anticipated loss in motion and function is considered acceptable.

Indications for reverse total shoulder arthroplasty in proximal humeral fracture:

For patients with isolated humeral head AVN and no other fracture-related issues (no tuberosity complications, nonunion, or malunion), hemiarthroplasty is the procedure of choice. However, if there is glenoid cartilage wear, anatomic total shoulder arthroplasty may be considered.99 Reverse arthroplasty is considered for patients who are relatively older and present with the combination of AVN and tuberosity nonunion, malunion, or resorption.

Answer: 2
