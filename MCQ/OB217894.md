## Question

A 15-year-old varsity team baseball catcher presents for left knee pain in the off-season. His MRI images are shown in Figures A and B. The patient is made non-weight bearing for eight weeks and thereafter completes a four-month return-to-play protocol. ## Options

1. Allow him to continue to play the season with daily administration of NSAIDs
2. Continue physical therapy for quadricep specific training exercises
3. Further restrict activity modifications for an additional 6 months
4. Attempted salvage of the osteochondral fragment
5. Fragment removal and two staged MACI procedure

## Correct option

**4. Attempted salvage of the osteochondral fragment**

## Answer explanation

This patient presents with osteochondritis dissecans (OCD) of the medial femoral condyle. He has completed a reasonable attempt at nonoperative management with unchanged symptoms and should undergo operative management with attempted osteochondral fragment salvage.

OCD is a focal idiopathic injury of the subchondral bone with a risk of instability/disruption of adjacent articular cartilage. It is most often seen in active adolescent patients affecting the knee, elbow, or ankle. Radiographic images may reveal contour abnormalities or radiolucency along the articular surface. MRI is the mainstay to characterize osteochondral lesions. If the lesion is stable on MRI, a period of non-operative management is reasonable. If the patient remains symptomatic with unchanged MRI findings despite attempted nonoperative management, they should undergo attempted salvage of the osteochondral fragment (i.e. antegrade or retrograde drilling vs screw fixation). Unstable lesions are defined by a disruption of the articular surface, as depicted by fluid "breaching" through the cartilage surface with surrounding edema. The consensus is to attempt operative salvage of the osteochondral fragment in skeletally immature patients, as they have a significantly better chance of successful operative intervention compared to skeletally mature patients.

*Crawford and colleagues provided a comprehensive review regarding the management of OCD of the knee.* *They stress that two principle factors (skeletal maturity at symptom onset and contiguity of the subchondral and bone-cartilage surface) remain the most important determinants of choosing treatment.* **They conclude** that skeletally immature patients have a better chance of successful nonoperative management and successful osteochondral fragment salvage compared to skeletally mature patients.

*Nepple and colleagues provided an update on the treatment of OCD incorporating the recommendations from the Research in OCD of the Knee (ROCK) multicenter study group.* **They discussed** that highly unstable lesions or free-floating osteochondral fragments should undergo operative management as nonoperative management has a high rate of failure. If the chondral fragment cannot be repaired, then salvage options remain viable alternative treatments. These can include autograft osteochondral transfer in small lesions, fresh osteochondral allograft transfer in larger lesions, or newer sandwich techniques with bone graft and autologous chondrocyte implantation.

Figures A and B display the radiographic and MRI images of an unstable medial femoral condyle OCD lesion. There is significant hyperintensity edema surrounding the lesion. Illustration A from Crawford et al. further characterizes the findings of an unstable lesion.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Allowing the patient to continue to play the season with daily administration of NSAIDs would be inappropriate when he has failed a reasonable attempt at nonoperative management.
Answer 2. Continued physical therapy for quadricep-specific training exercises would be an appropriate pre- or post-operative protocol for patients undergoing ACL reconstruction, however, has not shown long-term beneficial outcomes in the setting of OCD.
Answer 3. Further restricted activity modifications for six months would likely not provide benefit after the patient has already failed a trial of nonoperative management.
Answer 4. Fragment removal and two staged MACI procedures would be considered appropriate salvage in irreparable lesions or unstable lesions of skeletally mature individuals. However, skeletally immature patients have a greater chance of osteochondral fragment salvage which should be attempted prior to MACI salvage procedures.
