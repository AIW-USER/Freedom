## Question

A 58-year-old man presents with a gradually enlarging, painless mass in the distal thigh that he first noticed 8 months ago. He denies trauma, systemic symptoms, or neurologic complaints. Physical examination reveals a firm, non-tender soft-tissue mass deep to the fascia with no overlying skin changes. MRI demonstrates a well-circumscribed lesion without surrounding edema or neurovascular encasement. Biopsy confirms a benign and well-circumscribed mass. Which tumor is typically treated with marginal excision?

## Options

1. Aneurysmal bone cyst
2. Desmoid tumor
3. Intramuscular myxoma
4. Myxofibrosarcoma
5. Solitary fibrous tumor

## Correct option

**3. Intramuscular myxoma**

## Answer explanation

Intramuscular myxomas are treated with marginal excision. Intramuscular myxomas are benign, well-circumscribed soft-tissue tumors characterized histologically by hypocellular myxoid stroma with bland spindle cells and no mitotic activity. It demonstrates non-infiltrative growth, lacks metastatic potential, and has a very low risk of recurrence when excised with marginal margins. Because it does not exhibit aggressive local behavior or microscopic satellite lesions, wide excision is unnecessary, making marginal excision both oncologically safe and functionally preserving (Answer 3).

There are four descriptors used in orthopedic oncology to describe tumor excision. Intralesional excision removes the tumor from within its pseudocapsule and is associated with the highest recurrence risk. It is appropriate only for select benign lesions. Marginal excision removes the tumor along its reactive zone or capsule and is appropriate for benign or low-risk tumors like intramuscular myxoma. Wide excision includes a cuff of normal tissue around the tumor to reduce recurrence and is required for most malignant sarcomas. Radical excision removes the entire involved anatomic compartment and is reserved for highly aggressive tumors when limb-sparing margins cannot be achieved.

*Luna et al. aimed to characterize the MRI features of intramuscular myxomas and correlate imaging findings with histologic appearance to improve diagnostic accuracy.* **They found** that intramuscular myxomas typically appear as well-circumscribed, homogeneous lesions with very high T2 signal intensity, low T1 signal, and minimal enhancement, corresponding to their hypocellular myxoid stroma. **The authors concluded** that recognition of these characteristic MRI features can help distinguish intramuscular myxoma from malignant myxoid tumors and reduce unnecessary aggressive surgical management.

*Kawaguchi et al. sought to define and formalize the concept of a “curative margin” in the surgical treatment of bone and soft tissue sarcomas to optimize oncologic outcomes while preserving function.* **They demonstrated** that local recurrence risk is strongly influenced by the quality of surgical margins, emphasizing that margins should be planned based on biologic tumor behavior rather than purely anatomic distance. **The authors concluded** that achieving a biologically appropriate curative margin is critical to local control and should guide surgical strategy in musculoskeletal oncology.

*Baltu et al. aimed to describe the clinical presentation, imaging features, surgical management, and outcomes of patients with intramuscular myxoma.* **They found** that these tumors typically present as slow-growing, painless soft-tissue masses and can be effectively treated with marginal excision, with very low recurrence rates. **The authors concluded** that intramuscular myxomas are benign lesions with predictable behavior and excellent prognosis when appropriately diagnosed and managed with conservative surgical margins.

Incorrect Answers
Answer 1: Aneurysmal bone cysts are expansile, osteolytic bone lesions that typically require curettage with adjuvant treatment rather than marginal excision of a soft-tissue mass.
Answer 2: Desmoid tumors are benign but highly infiltrative and have high local recurrence rates, typically treated non-operatively.
Answer 4: Myxofibrosarcomas are malignant soft-tissue sarcomas with infiltrative margins, requiring wide surgical excision to reduce recurrence risk.
Answer 5: Solitary fibrous tumors are often benign, but are generally treated with wide excision due to potential recurrence or malignancy.
