## Question

A 61-year-old male undergoes revision right arthroscopic rotator cuff repair. Anesthesia is provided by ultrasound guided interscalene block with good effect. The surgical procedure is uneventful. In the recovery room, the patient has no shoulder pain, but does endorse right sided inspiratory chest pain, as well as difficulty breathing. On room air, his SpO2 is 91%. A chest x-ray obtained in the recovery room is shown in Figure A. Which of the following would be most likely to contribute to the development of this complication?

![[q5786-a-chest.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Diabetes mellitus
2. 35-pack-year smoking history
3. Revision procedure
4. Obesity
5. Subscapularis repair

## Correct option

**2. 35-pack-year smoking history**

## Answer explanation

This patient sustained an iatrogenic pneumothorax after interscalene block. Smokers with pulmonary disease have hyperinflated lungs and an elevated pleural dome, which increases the risk of injury.

The patient's pneumothorax is suggested by his acute onset chest pain, dyspnea, and hypoxia, and an x-ray demonstrating a visible pleural edge with no pulmonary markings peripheral to it (right lung field). This is a rare complication of interscalene and supraclavicular blocks as well as shoulder arthroscopy itself. Many of the reported cases are thought to have been facilitated by an elevated pleural dome due to hyperinflated lungs in the context of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease. Of note, dyspnea post-operatively can also be caused by diaphragm paralysis after a block, but a chest x-ray is obtained to confirm in this case a pneumothorax.

*Mandim et. al. reported a case of iatrogenic pneumothorax which occurred after ultrasound guided interscalene block in a 49-year-old male with COPD and a 30-pack-year smoking history.* The patient manifested symptoms of dyspnea, chest pain, and hypoxia in the recovery room, with no suggestion of complication during surgery itself.

*Bryan et. al. performed a retrospective analysis of 144 consecutive interscalene catheters placed for post-operative anesthesia following amulbatory orthopaedic surgery.* *Among the 144 patients, the only major complication was one pneumothorax in a 67-year-old man who underwent uneventful catheter placement and surgery, but post-operatively complained of chest pain.* This patient did not require invasive treatment.

*Lee et. al. reported a series of three patients who underwent arthroscopic subacromial decompression and developed subcutaneous emphysema, pneumomediastinum, and bilateral pneumothoraces, and one tension pneumothorax.* *All patients were diagnosed intra-operatively and required placement of chest tubes.*

Figure A is a chest x-ray demonstrating a right sided pneumothorax as evidenced by a visible boundary between the visceral pleura and the peripheral lung field and a lack of lung markings in the peripheral field.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Diabetes mellitus generally increases the risk for wound healing complications and peripheral neuropathy, but is not known to increase the risk of pneumothorax after regional anesthesia.
Answer 3: Revision shoulder arthroscopy may be more technically challenging than the index procedure, but increased rates of pneumothorax have not been reported after revision cases.
Answer 4: Obesity also has not been reported to be a risk factor for pneumothorax after shoulder arthroscopy or regional nerve block.
Answer 5: The specific type of arthroscopic shoulder procedure performed is not known to increase the risk of pneumothorax.
