## Question

Figure A is the radiograph of a 5-year-old boy who presents to the office for evaluation of dorsal foot pain. The patient's mother reports the pain has been present for about 2 months and seems to be worse with activity. The patient does endorse having his foot kicked several times at soccer camp, but he is unable to identify any other trauma. The patient and mother also deny any recent illnesses or constitutional symptoms. Which of the following is the best next step in treatment?

![[q218587-a-ff346b4a5b97c34ff7d2a630360cfb-gallery.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Physical therapy
2. Application of a walking cast
3. Open reduction internal fixation
4. Obtain blood work (i.e. ESR, CRP, WBC)
5. MRI of the foot

## Correct option

**2. Application of a walking cast**

## Answer explanation

This patient has Kohler's disease, which is best treated with immobilization in a walking cast in patients who have pain.

Kohler's Disease is a rare idiopathic condition caused by avascular necrosis of the navicular bone that occurs in young children and presents with pain on the dorsal and medial surface of the foot. The diagnosis is made with radiographs of the foot showing sclerosis, fragmentation, and flattening of tarsal navicular bone. Treatment typically consists of a short course of NSAIDs to decrease symptoms as well as immobilization in a walking cast if the patient has pain that is worsened with activities. Surgery is not indicated for this condition as it is self limiting within 1-3 years.

*Borges et al reviewed Köhler's disease and* **noted** that the type and length of treatment did not affect the final outcome; however, short-leg cast immobilization did decrease the duration of symptoms. **They conclude** that patients with Köhler's bone disease can be expected to have a normal foot at adulthood, and if they become symptomatic other sources should be investigated.

*Chan et al* **reported** that long-term outcomes for Köhler disease are favorable regardless of the type of treatment. **They conclude**, however, that a short term period of immobilization with a short leg walking cast may reduce the duration of symptoms.

Figure A is the lateral radiograph of a pediatric foot demonstrating sclerosis and flattening of the tarsal navicular bone consistent with Kohler's disease.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Physical therapy does not aid in the recovery of Kohler's disease as it is a self limiting problem.
Answer 3: Open reduction internal fixation is not warranted as there is no fracture.
Answer 4: While this may be confused with infection, the diagnosis can be easily obtained with the associated history and imaging. Further, the patient has no recent history of illness or constitutional symptoms.
Answer 5: MRI of the foot is not warranted as the diagnosis of Kohler's disease can be made on plain films alone.
