## Answer explanation

Medial sesamoidectomy can lead to the development of a hallux valgus deformity. An example of a medial sesamoidectomy is demonstrated in the radiographs within Figure D (Answer 3).

Hallux sesamoid pathology encompasses a variety of conditions that can be challenging to treat. Stress fractures of the sesamoids are the most common issue, with the medial or tibial sesamoid being the most frequently affected. Initial radiographs are often negative, so diagnosis depends heavily on a thorough history and physical examination. Surgical intervention should only be considered after at least 3 months of unsuccessful conservative management. Sesamoidectomy has proven to be an effective treatment option, although it carries a moderate risk of complications, including pain, nerve injury, infection, and deformity. Hallux valgus is the most commonly reported deformity following tibial sesamoid excision and may occur due to inadequate medial capsular repair and/or reefing.

*Kane et al. reviewed the radiographic outcomes and clinical results following sesamoidectomy for fractures.* *They discovered that patients with medial sesamoid pathology had higher pre-existing hallux valgus and intermetatarsal angles, which increased post-operatively to a statistically significant but clinically insignificant degree.* In contrast, patients who underwent lateral sesamoidectomy experienced a net decrease in both radiographic parameters. Despite these differences, **the authors concluded** that patient outcomes did not significantly differ between the two groups.

*Bichara et al. evaluated the outcomes of sesamoidectomy for hallux sesamoid fractures.* *After an average follow-up of three years,* **the authors found** that 92% of patients were able to return to their activities within an average of 12 weeks. **They reported** a significant improvement in both pain and function, although 4% of cases developed a postoperative hallux valgus deformity. **The authors concluded** that a meticulous surgical repair of the medial capsule following excision may help reduce this risk.

Figure A shows a clinical photograph of a hallux valgus deformity. Figure B is an anteroposterior (AP) radiograph of the foot that demonstrates an accessory navicular, which has since been excised. Figure C presents a lateral radiograph of the foot illustrating a dorsal osteophyte on the first metatarsal head, followed by a dorsal cheilectomy. Figure D depicts an AP radiograph of the foot, which shows a medial sesamoidectomy. Figure E features an axial radiograph and fluoroscopic view of the foot displaying a lateral sesamoidectomy. Finally, Figure F is an oblique radiograph of the foot that demonstrates the excision of an Os Vesalianum.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Surgical excision of an accessory navicular is not associated with the development of hallux valgus.
Answer 2: Dorsal cheilectomy is not associated with the development of hallux valgus.
Answer 4: Lateral sesamoidectomy is associated with the development of a hallux varus deformity, not hallux valgus.
Answer 5: Os Vesalianum excision is not associated with the development of hallux valgus.
