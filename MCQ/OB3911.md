## Question

A 42-year-old heavy laborer had a fusion of his left first metatarsophalangeal joint for severe arthritis approximately 9 months ago. He states that the pain in his great toe has resolved, but he has developed progressive pain at the plantar aspect of his second metatarsophalangeal joint and has noticed a new deformity to the second toe. In what position was the first toe fused?

## Options

1. Toe is fused in a varus position
2. Toe is fused in a valgus position
3. Toe is fused in a plantarflexion position
4. Toe is fused in a shortened position
5. Toe is fused in a dorsiflexion position

## Correct option

**4. Toe is fused in a shortened position**

## Answer explanation

The patient is demonstrating signs of transfer metatarsalgia due to the great toe being fused in a shortened position.

Hallux rigidus is arthritis of the first metatarsal phalangeal joint. Arthrodesis is usually indicated in more advanced arthritis. The toe should be fused in 10-15 degrees of valgus in relation to the metatarsal shaft and 15 degrees of dorsiflexion in relation to the floor. The strongest construct is a dorsal plate with a compression screw. Fusing the toe in an excessively shortened position leads to increase pressure on the second metatarsal (transfer metatarsalgia) as well as a varus deformity of the 2nd toe secondary to loss of medial support from the great toe.

*Coughlin and Shurnas reviewed 110 patients who previously had either a cheilectomy or arthrodesis for symptomatic hallux rigidus.* **They found** that the cheilectomy group had significant improvement in dorsiflexion and total motion. Both the arthrodesis and cheilectomy group had a significant improvement in postoperative pain and AOFAS score, and 97% had good or excellent results. **They concluded** that cheilectomy has predictable success in grades 1 and 2 cases, while arthrodesis is successful in patients with grades 3 and 4.

*Lau and Daniels retrospectively reviewed 19 patients with grade 2 who underwent cheilectomy and 11 patients with grade 3 who underwent interpositional arthroplasty.* **They found** that the cheilectomy group had higher mean AOFAS scores. However, post-operative motion, VAS pain scale, and SF-36 scores were similar. Interpositional arthroplasty was more likely to have weakness in the great toe. **They concluded** that cheilectomy is a reliable method of pain relief for moderate hallux rigidus and that interpositional arthroplasty for severe hallux rigidus should be considered a salvage procedure with less reliable results.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: A toe fused in varus would not create problems with transfer metatarsalgia, but could lead to problems with shoe wear especially shoes with a narrow toe box.
Answer 2: A toe fused in excessive valgus can cause pressure on the second toe and lead to the formation of a soft corn or ulceration on the second toe.
Answer 3: A toe that is fused in an excessively plantarflexed position can cause excessive pressure at the tip of the toe, as well as, lead to altered gait mechanics as he is unable to roll over the toe leading to excessive lateral foot pressure.
Answer 5: A toe fused in excessive dorsiflexion can cause pain at the tip of the toe, IP joint and over the first metatarsal head
