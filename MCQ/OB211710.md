## Question

A 55-year-old carpenter sustains an injury to his right dominant middle finger. Three months following the injury and treatment, he is able to fully flex his middle finger to his palm, however, he cannot bring his other fingers into his palm. Which of the following injury scenarios would best explain these findings?

## Options

1. FDP laceration with repair
2. Nail bed laceration and repair
3. Oblique spiral proximal phalanx fracture
4. FDS laceration with repair
5. Dorsal MCPJ dislocation

## Correct option

**1. FDP laceration with repair**

## Answer explanation

The patient has quadriga, which may result from over-advancement (>1cm) of the FDP tendon during repair, use of too short of a tendon graft, or tendinous adhesions.

Over-tensioning during an FDP repair can potentially result in quadriga due to the shared FDP muscle belly. When a single tendon is excessively advanced, this creates relative slack in the adjacent finger FDPs. Thus the injured finger may be able to reach the palm in flexion, but the adjacent fingers may not be able to touch the palm in the same motion due to the relative laxity. The treatment for quadrigia is therefore tendon lengthening, tenolysis, or release the offending FDP tendon.

Schreuders reviews the quadriga phenomenon. The authors notes that quadriga is precipitated by FDP interconnections (in addition to the common muscle belly), which makes them less able to move independently (as compared to the FDS). He discusses the impact on strength testing and assessment of digit motion.

*Malerich et al. performed a cadaveric analysis of FDP lacerations.* **They found** that advancement up to 1cm was acceptable, but that any further advancement of the ulnar three profundi caused a flexion lag of the other fingers. **The authors recommend** not advancing a flexor tendon repair beyond 1 cm.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Fingertip amputations that involve a DIPJ disarticulation may cause a lumbrical plus finger, but not quadriga.
Answer 3: An oblique fracture of the proximal phalanx may cause malrotation and shortening, but not quadriga.
Answer 4: FDS tendons have separate muscle bellies and repair does not cause quadriga.
Answer 5: A dorsal MCPJ dislocation requires a volar plate release, but this does not quadriga.
