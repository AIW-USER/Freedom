## Question

For which of the following patients is Pavlik harness treatment appropriate?

## Options

1. 4-month-old with a diaphyseal femur fracture and a neuromuscular disorder with lower extremity spasticity
2. 4-month-old with a displaced spiral mid-diaphyseal femur fracture
3. 4-month-old with a diaphyseal femur fracture and concomitant closed head injury and chest trauma
4. 9-month-old with a diaphyseal femur fracture with <2cm shortening
5. 9-month-old with a diaphyseal femur fracture with >2cm shortening

## Correct option

**2. 4-month-old with a displaced spiral mid-diaphyseal femur fracture**

## Answer explanation

Treatment with a Pavlik harness or spica cast are options for patients <6 months of age with diaphyseal femur fractures.

Because of the significant remodeling potential and fast healing of diaphyseal femur fractures in children younger than 6 months, treatment focuses on providing comfort and avoiding complications. Pavlik harnesses provide adequate pain control for the short time required for healing in this age group. Similarly, spica casting results in stable union without longterm sequelae but have a higher risk of minor skin complications. Pavlik harness treatment is therefore more often recommended for patients in this age group.

*Flynn et al. reviewed the management of pediatric femoral shaft fractures.* **They recommend** that, for children 6 months of age or younger, treatment with a Pavlik harness is preferred over a spica cast and can be supplemented with a simple splint, thereby avoiding the skin complications seen with spica casting.

*Kocher et al. published the AAOS Clinical Practice Guideline for the treatment of pediatric diaphyseal femur fractures.* For diaphyseal femur fractures in children less than 6 months of age, they provide a Grade C recommendation that Pavlik harness and spica casting are both acceptable treatment options.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Pavlik harnesses are not recommended for patients with neuromuscular disorders.
Answer 3: Pavlik harness treatment is not recommended for polytrauma patients.
Answers 4 and 5: Children older than 6 months of age are more appropriately treated with modalities other than a Pavlik harness.
