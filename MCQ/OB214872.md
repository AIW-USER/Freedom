## Question

Which of the following is not a contraindication to perform a reverse total shoulder arthroplasty?

## Options

1. C5-C6 nerve root avulsion
2. Acromial deficiency
3. Osteomyelitis of the glenoid
4. Rheumatoid arthritis
5. Posterior cord nerve palsy

## Correct option

**4. Rheumatoid arthritis**

## Answer explanation

Rheumatoid arthritis (RA) is not a contraindication to perform a reverse total shoulder arthroplasty.

The reverse total shoulder arthroplasty (RSA) is a type of shoulder arthroplasty that uses a convex glenoid (hemispheric ball) and a concave humerus (articulating cup) to reconstruct the glenohumeral joint. While initially, patients with RA were not candidates for this procedure, recent data has shown excellent short and midterm functional and radiographic outcomes with the newer implants. RA affecting the shoulder is typically associated with rotator cuff compromise and severe glenoid erosion, make RSA a viable option since it can address both conditions. Axillary nerve palsy, global deltoid dysfunction, active infection, and acromion deficiency are considered contraindications to performing an RSA.

*Young et al. performed a review of the postoperative outcomes of 18 primary RSA in 16 patients with RA involving the shoulder.* **The authors noted** the mean Constant score improved from 22.5 to 64.9 points at a mean of ~4 years postop. The patients were either very satisfied or satisfied with the outcome of the surgery in 17/18 (94%) shoulders. The most common complication was scapular notching (10/18, 55%). There were no cases of dislocation, infection, or component loosening and none of the patients required revision surgery for any reason. **The authors concluded** that good short to intermediate-term outcomes can be expected after RSA in patients with RA.

*Mangold et al. performed a retrospective review of the clinical and radiographic outcomes of 91 patients with RA who underwent RSA with an average follow-up of 4 years.* **The authors noted** two and five-year implant revision-free survival of 99%. The two and five-year re-operation-free survival was 97%. **The authors noted** two cases of glenoid loosening with gross changes in position. Overall, patients experienced significant pain relief with a 92% satisfaction rate. **The authors concluded** that patients with RA can have excellent midterm outcomes following RSA with a low rate of revision surgery.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: A C5-C6 nerve root avulsion would lead to complete deltoid palsy which is necessary for a functioning RSA.
Answer 2: Acromial deficiency is considered a relative contraindication to performing an RSA.
Answer 3: Active infection is a contraindication to performing an RSA.
Answer 5: A posterior cord nerve palsy (gives off the axillary nerve) would lead to complete deltoid palsy which is necessary for a functioning RSA
