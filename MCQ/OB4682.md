## Options

1. Child abuse
2. Neurofibromatosis
3. Metastatic neuroblastoma
4. Adamantinoma
5. Langerhans cell histiocytosis

## Correct option

**5. Langerhans cell histiocytosis**

## Answer explanation

This child has Langerhans cell histiocytosis (LCH). He has the classic triad of Hand-Schuller-Christian disease with multiple lytic skull lesions, diabetes insipidus and exopthalmos.

Langerhans cell histiocytosis comprises a spectrum of conditions including eosinophilic granuloma (EG, single lesion), Hand-Schuller-Christian disease (HSC, multiple bony and visceral lesions), and Letterer-Siwe disease (LSD, fatal, occurs in young children). They present with rapidly destructive bony lesions, and may have associated soft tissue masses.

*Hoover et al. reviewed the treatment for Langerhans cell histiocytosis (LCH).* For eosinophilic granuloma (EG) **they recommend** curettage and implantation of allograft bone chips or cement or injections of steroids. For HSC and LSD **they report** most treatment is not effective. They discuss options including corticosteroids, chemotherapy (methotrexate and vinblastine, for HSC), growth hormones (for HSC), and bone marrow transplantation.

Figure A shows exopthalmos of the left eye with marked facial asymmetry. Figure B shows multiple punched-out lytic skull lesions. Figure C shows a lytic lesion in the fibular shaft. Figure D shows vertebra plana. Illustration A is a coronal MRI image showing an osteolytic lesion in the side wall affecting the lateral and inferior rectus muscles, leading to exopthalmos. Illustration B is a coronal MRI image showing infiltration of the pituitary stalk. Illustration C shows the histological appearance of LCH with mononuclear histiocyte-like cells with a prominent nuclear groove (coffee bean or reniform nuclei, yellow box). Cytoplasm is prominently eosinophilic.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Lymphoma is unlikely to present with exopthalmos, diabetes insipidus or vertebra plana. Lymphoma bone lesions are lytic and appear moth eaten/permeative on radiographs.
Answer 2: Type 2 neurofibromatosis may have tibia bowing/pseudoarthrosis and scoliosis, and will likely have typical cutaneous signs (neurofibromas, cafe au lait spots). Lytic lesions and vertebra plana are unlikely.
Answer 3: Although metastatic lesions may occasionally produce vertebra plana, they are unlikely to present as mass lesions causing exopthalmos or diabetes insipidus.
Answer 4: Adamantinoma presents with lesions in the mid tibia with a soap-bubble/sclerotic appearance and may have bowing and cortical disruption. They are unlikely to involve the vertebrae and skull, and will not cause exopthalmos or diabetes insipidus.
