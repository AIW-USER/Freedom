## Question

## Options

1. Peroneal nerve palsy is common with coronal plane correction
2. Constrained implants may be needed due to posteromedial bone loss and requirement for extensive posteromedial releases
3. Given the varus instability this patient is best treated with a primary knee arthrodesis
4. Reefing of the lateral ligaments can afford sufficient stability
5. Arthroplasty followed by corrective osteotomy is the most effective treatment

## Correct option

**2. Constrained implants may be needed due to posteromedial bone loss and requirement for extensive posteromedial releases**

## Answer explanation

Patients with varus arthritis due to untreated Blount's disease may require constrained implants, significant posteromedial releases, and reconstruction of posteromedial bone loss.

Blount's disease is a disorder that affects the growth of the tibia, leading to varus deformity and knee arthritis. The most common treatment for Blount's disease is a proximal tibial osteotomy to realign the knee and prevent degeneration of the native joint. In some cases, if the osteotomy does not provide adequate relief of symptoms or if the arthritis is severe, a total knee arthroplasty (TKA) may be recommended. TKA has been found to be successful in providing pain relief and improved function, but it is associated with higher complication rates. Due to the deficiency of posteromedial bone and extensive posteromedial releases, use of constrained implants is preferred.

*Natoli et al. examined the outcomes of total knee arthroplasty in five patients with Blount disease or Blount-like deformity.* *The study found that despite technical challenges, patients with these deformities can have successful outcomes after total knee arthroplasty.* *The study suggests that surgeons should be prepared to address posteromedial tibial bony defects and consider constrained arthroplasty at the index procedure.*

*Kernkamp et al. reviewed and performed a meta-analysis of the current literature on the conversion of knee arthrodesis (AD) to total knee arthroplasty (TKA).* *The study found that while good clinical results can be expected after conversion, the risk of postoperative complications is high and the most frequent complications were skin necrosis, arthrofibrosis, infection, and revision.* *The study concludes that further series are necessary to draw definitive conclusions.*

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: Correction of valgus knee deformities is more commonly associated with peroneal nerve palsies due to the stretching of the nerve.
Answer 3: Knee arthrodesis is an option in certain patients with non-reconstructable knees. However, better functional outcomes are obtained with a reconstructed knee that permits a stable and painless range of motion.
Answer 4: Reefing of the lateral ligaments may temporarily improve the soft tissue tension, but the structural deformity of the knee would likely result in attenuation of the ligaments over time. A constrained prosthesis would provide adequate long-term stability to the reconstruction.
Answer 5: In severe angular deformities, a corrective osteotomy may be needed to mechanically align the limb. However, this is either done at the time of the arthroplasty procedure or prior.
