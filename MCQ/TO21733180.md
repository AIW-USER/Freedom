Chapter: Arthroplasty MCQs — Unicondylar and Patellofemoral Arthroplasty

## Question

The overall commonest cause of failure of patello femoral arthroplasty is

## Options

1. Patello femoral instability
2. Patellar tendon ruptures
3. Progression to tibio femoral osteoarthritis
4. Aseptic loosening

## Correct option

**3. Progression to tibio femoral osteoarthritis**

## Answer explanation

Ans: C

EXPLANATIONS: -
Patellofemoral arthroplasty (PFA) as a treatment option for isolated patellofemoral disease. Patellofemoral implants attempt to reproduce the complex kinetics of patellofemoral joint during knee flexion, allowing patellar movements in the sagittal, axial, and coronal planes. They are indicated in younger patient with isolated patellofemoral arthritis. PFA represents an option for improved function with faster recovery times, bone preservation, maintenance of ligamentous proprioception, and the ability to delay total knee arthroplasty.
The two main types of patellofemoral implants are based on the trochlea preparation method: resurfacing implants and anterior cut implants.
Resurfacing (first-generation) implants simply replace worn cartilage without significantly changing the shape of the subchondral bone (inlay technique). Since they are embedded in subchondral bone, their positioning depends on the anatomy of the native trochlea.

Anterior cut (second-generation) implants use the same anterior femoral cuts as total knee arthroplasty (TKA); these implants are often designed based on the femoral trochlear groove of TKA implants. So, instead of replacing only the cartilage lost due to wear, they completely replace the anterior compartment of the knee.

In a candidate planned for PFA, the initial radiographic evaluation should include standing anteroposterior and lateral knee radiographs, Rosenberg (45o knee flexion AP view) view, patellar skyline (axial or merchant) view and standing full-leg radiographs.

COMPLICATIONS
Early post op complications
The rate of early postoperative complications is higher for patellofemoral arthroplasty than total knee arthroplasty. Early postoperative complications include persistent anterior knee pain (mostly from misalignment of the extensor mechanism and an over-sized femoral component), patellar catching or snapping, and extensor mechanism rupture.
Poor positioning of the femoral component can be further complicated by patellar maltracking or even patellar instability. Improper femoral component sizing and positioning generally brings about high rates of early revision.
Late complications
Newer implant designs have reduced the number of complications due to patellar maltracking, the main cause of revision surgery overall tends to be progression of tibiofemoral osteoarthritis. The reported revision rate for tibiofemoral osteoarthritis is up to 22% after 5 to 15 years of follow-up
Loosening is a relatively rare complication. Lonner et al. Reported that fewer than 0.5% of revisions were due to loosening after a seven-year follow-up.
