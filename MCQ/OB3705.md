## Answer explanation

The use of a posterior approach alone for decompression and fusion of the myelopathic cervical spine is indicated in patients with preserved cervical lordosis and multilevel cervical stenosis, which is exemplified by figure D.

Cervical myelopathy is characterized by a progressive stepwise decline in neurologic function. Treatment is dependent on the number of involved levels as well as the sagittal spinal alignment. In patients with multilevel (>2 levels) cervical stenosis with myelopathy requires a posterior decompression due to the greater area of decompression available with this approach. However, when cervical kyphosis >10 degrees an anterior based procedure, such as an anterior cervical discectomy and fusion, is indicated as it can correct the kyphotic alignment of the spine.

*Albert et al. reviewed the biomechanics, risk factors, diagnosis, and treatment of post-laminectomy kyphosis.* **They note** that multilevel decompressions, the addition of any amount of facet resection, preoperative sagittal instability, anterior pseudarthrosis, or skeletal immaturity all necessitate strong consideration of fusion in addition to laminectomy.

*McAllister et al. performed a systematic review of outcomes for cervical myelopathy following laminectomy alone nad laminectomy with posterior spinal fusion.* The literature indicated that there are no differences in outcomes between the two treatment approaches in the short term. However, at long term follow-up, there is a greater degree of post-laminectomy kyphosis and neurologic deterioration, 47% and 39% respectively. **The authors concluded** that longterm results of laminectomy alone for the treatment of cervical myelopathy do not support it's use.

Figure A is a T2 MRI demonstrating a C5 teardrop fracture with central stenosis, local kyphosis, and changes within the cord.
Figure B illustrates a sagittal T1 MRI of the cervical spine with multilevel involvement with cervical canal stenosis and kyphotic alignment.
Figure C shows a C6-7 central disc protrusion with cord effacement and associated signal changes within the cord.
Figure D demonstrates multi-level disease with cervical central stenosis and lordotic alignment.
Figure E shows an anterior epidural mass associated with C5 spondylodiscitis at the C4-T3 level.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1. A teardrop fracture would be a contraindication to isolated posterior decompression and fusion.
Answer 2. Multi-level disease with cervical central stenosis and kyphotic alignment would necessitate combined anterior and posterior procedures.
Answer 3. The lordotic skip-level disease is best treated with an anterior skip-level ACDF.
Answer 5. Anterior epidural abscesses would necessitate anterior decompression.
