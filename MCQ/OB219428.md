## Question

Repeat films were taken six weeks later and are shown in Figure B. ## Options

1. Priniciple of hydraulic stiffening
2. Wolff's Law
3. Hooke's Law
4. Hueter-Volkmann Law
5. Delpech's Law

## Correct option

**2. Wolff's Law**

## Answer explanation

This patient presents with decreased bone mineral density after being non-weightbearing to the bilateral lower extremities for 6 weeks. Disuse osteopenia is a common issue in non-weightbearing patients, and is best explained by the principles of Wolff's Law.

Bone is a dynamic tissue that is constantly adapting and remodeling secondary to stress. This principle was first proposed in the literature by Dr. Julius Wolff in the 19th Century. Wolff's Law denotes that bone will become denser over time when placed under continued or repetitive loading stress. Conversely, bone becomes less dense over time with decreased loading forces. There are characteristic areas that resemble this phenomena, such as the dense proximal femur calcar through which the majority of body weight is transmitted during ambulation. Conversely, long periods of lower extremity immobilization after fracture fixation result in diffuse ipsilateral extremity osteopenia (i.e. disuse osteopenia).

*Chen et al. published a review article on the current understanding of how local osteoprogenitor cells respond to and remodel bone secondary to local stress.* Mechanical regulation between the cellular pathways is quite complex; however, it is initially driven by stress delivered to the bone. The decreased bone mineral density in the aforementioned example of a patient's bilateral calcaneal fractures is expected after a prolonged period of non-weightbearing.

*Walley et al. performed a retrospective chart review on patients who were admitted to the hospital with a lower extremity fracture and were placed on a non-weightbearing status while inpatient.* *They then compared the CT scans of 17 patients from before injury to those taken on average 7.3 days after being placed on non-weightbearing restrictions.* *They used the Hounsfield unit (HU) as a surrogate for bone mineral density and found an average decrease in HU of 7.4 per day.* **They report** that clinically relevant osteopenia is reported in HU scores under 100 with a drop under 100 HU correlating to 15 days of non-weightbearing in this studied population. **The authors conclude** that disuse osteopenia can be measured radiographically and is of particular concern in the proximal femur.

Figure A shows lateral foot radiographs at the time of injury in bilateral calcaneus fractures with minimal articular disruption. Figure B shows lateral foot films of bilateral calcaneus fractures after 6 weeks of non-weightbearing with interval callous formation and signs of speckled proximal bone indicative of disuse osteopenia.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Hydraulic stiffening represents a mechanism that can increase the upper range of dynamic loads tolerated by the skeleton. The fluid in cellular tissues acts as a hydraulic fluid under stress and can stiffen the entire skeleton. This principle increases (not decreases) the strength of bone.
Answer 3: Hooke's Law is related to the stress-strain relationship of a solid, not disuse osteopenia.
Answer 4: The Heuter-Volkmann Law relates to longitudinal bone growth and suggests that compressive forces inhibit growth.
Answer 5: Delpech's law is also related to bone growth across the growth plate and is not related to disuse osteopenia.
