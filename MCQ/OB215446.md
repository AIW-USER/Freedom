## Question

During shoulder arthroscopy, the posterior lever push maneuver is performed in order to improve visualization of which structure seen in Figure A?

![[q215446-a-sa-pic-numbers.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Structure #1
2. Structure #2
3. Structure #3
4. Structure #4
5. Structure #5

## Correct option

**5. Structure #5**

## Answer explanation

The posterior lever push maneuver (Illustration A) involves internal rotation of the arm and posterior stress on the humeral head in order to better visualize the subscapularis insertion on the humerus.

Subscapularis tears are more common than previously thought and affect the dynamic stability of the glenohumeral joint in the axial plane. Undersurface or non-retracted tears can be difficult to identify, particularly at their insertion on the lesser tuberosity of the humerus. For this reason, the posterior lever push is utilized intraoperatively in order to position the arm in a manner that tensions the subscapularis and allows better visualization of its humeral footprint.

*Lee et al. reviewed the anatomy and diagnosis of subscapularis tears,* **noting** they are being increasingly recognized and surgically addressed. **They found** that the Lafosse classification (Illustration B) is especially helpful in deciding the best surgical technique for arthroscopically repairing the subscapularis tear. Intraoperatively, they describe the posterior lever push maneuver and its usefulness in obtaining visualization. **They conclude** that most subscapularis tears can be repaired successfully with arthroscopic intervention, although in more severe cases tendon transfers or reverse total shoulder arthroplasty might be considered.

*Denard et al. discuss arthroscopic techniques for appropriately recognizing and repairing subscapularis tears.* *They review techniques for diagnostic arthroscopy to avoid missing occult or non-retracted subscapularis tears, including the posterior lever push maneuver.* *They also discuss a series of surgical steps to provide a reliable subscapularis repair.* **They conclude** that recognition and repair of subscapularis tendon tears leads to good functional outcomes when performed arthroscopically.

*Koo et al. focus on mid to distal subscapularis tendon tears in their review.* **They note** that a high degree of clinical suspicion must exist for these tears and arthroscopic examination of the medial biceps sling and bicipital groove should be performed using a 70-degree arthroscope. If abnormalities in these structures are noted, biceps tenodesis and subscapularis repair are performed, which improves function.

Figure A depicts the glenohumeral joint during shoulder arthroscopy. Labeled structures include: (1) Biceps tendon (2) Anterior labrum (3) Humeral head (4) Middle glenohumeral ligament (5) Subscapularis

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1-4: Structure #5 in Figure A depicts the subscapularis, for which visualization is improved by the posterior lever push maneuver.
