## Question

## Answer explanation

The highest degree of stress is placed on the ulnar collateral ligament (UCL) during the late cocking/early acceleration phases of the throwing cycle.

Ulnar collateral ligament injuries of the elbow occur more commonly in overhead throwers given the significant stresses placed on the medial elbow, particularly during the late cocking/early acceleration phase of the baseball pitch. The anterior bundle of the UCL is the primary stabilizer to valgus stress within the UCL complex and primarily functions from 30-120° of flexion. Patients complain of medial elbow pain, loss of velocity, and loss of control and usually have tenderness over the UCL origin. The milking maneuver (Figure A) and moving valgus stress tests are dynamic evaluations which provide the clinician with a better understanding of the pathology occurring at the elbow.

*Bruce et al. published a review of UCL injuries in throwing athletes,* **noting** that the repetitive stresses placed through the elbow joint lead to the structural damage that affects the UCL. They go on to discuss the efforts involved in UCL injury prevention as well as nonoperative treatment strategies. Further, they discuss UCL reconstruction as an option that allows for high levels of return to play for throwing athletes.

*Safran reviewed the diagnosis and treatment of UCL injuries in the overhead athlete,* **noting** that UCL injuries are more common than what has been previously suggested. **They note** that UCL injuries are often associated with secondary complaints which include symptoms of ulnar neuritis, flexor-pronator mass strains, valgus extension overload syndrome, and medial epicondylitis. Additionally, they review the nonoperative and operative management of athletes who have have a confirmed UCL tear.

*Safran et al. also reviewed the diagnosis and management of UCL tears.* **They note** that sports requiring repetitive overhead or side-arm motions like tennis, baseball, racquetball, or squash have a higher rate of UCL injury due to the mechanics affecting the medial elbow. They also review the different examination techniques and testing available for assisting with diagnosis as well as the treatment of these injuries.
