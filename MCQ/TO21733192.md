Chapter: General Orthopedics MCQs — Fracture Healing

## Question

Which is not a detrimental factor for fracture healing

## Options

1. Peripheral vascular disease
2. Diabetes
3. Anaemia
4. Nephrotic syndrome

## Correct option

**4. Nephrotic syndrome**

## Answer explanation

Ans: D

EXPLANATION: -
Factors affecting fracture union: factors affecting bony union are multifactorial and may be related to patient, injury, fracture or treatment.

Patient- or host-related factors:
Age: elderly, due to weak immune system increased systemic pro-inflammatory status, have a lower capacity for fracture healing when compared to their younger population.
Malnutrition: Albumin less than 3.5 mg/dL and lymphocytes less than 1,500 cells/mL indicates poor healing potential. Vitamin D and calcium deficiency also badly affects fracture healing.
Smoking/tobacco abuse: nicotine inhibits angiogenesis and forms weak calluses with an overall delay in the fracture healing process.
Alcohol abuse: Excessive alcohol consumption can slow down bone healing. Alcohol can affect the composition of the matrix that bone cells form to heal, which can suppress the formation of osteoblasts or reduce their ability to respond to signals that trigger bone formation.
Systemic diseases: In diabetes mellitus weak callus is formed due to low cellular content which affects the fracture healing process. Endochondral ossification is delayed, and fracture healing is generally prolonged compared to the general population. Cancer, metabolic bone diseases, osteomalacia, Cushing's disease, obesity all decrease the rate of fracture healing.
Anaemia- Iron is required for function of the electron transport system within the cell and for hydroxylation of proline in collagen formation. Iron deficiency delays bone healing with a decrease in the rate of union and loss of strength. There is poor mineralisation of the callus.
Peripheral vascular disease-Peripheral vascular disease adversely affects the blood flow to the tissues, including the bone and the surrounding soft-tissue envelope. This will impair delivery of oxygen, inflammatory cells and nutrients to the fracture site.
Drugs: Nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs), antineoplastic drugs, corticosteroid therapy, bisphosphonates, quinolones all delay fracture healing. NSAIDs can result in delayed union due to COX enzyme inhibition. Systemic corticosteroids are detrimental to fracture healing. Prolonged systemic administration of corticosteroids causes osteoporosis and increased risk of fracture as a result of the inhibitory effects on the production of IGF-1 and TGF-β.
Long-term use of bisphosphonates has been associated with osteoporotic fractures such as subtrochanteric femoral insufficiency fractures. Quinolones have been reported to be toxic to chondrocytes with the consequent compromise of the fracture healing process.
Radiation therapy- Irradiation of the intact bone prior to fracture resulted in delay in activation of the repair process and in delayed and incomplete healing.
Infection: Studies have shown that infection impairs callus formation, wherein fibrous tissue is formed instead, affecting woven bone deposition and thereby decreasing mechanical stability and overall osseus healing.

Local factors related to injury which delay fracture healing:
Open fractures (excessive periosteal stripping and soft tissue damage)
Intra-articular fracture
Denervation of bone
Bone loss, segmental fracture
High velocity injury, severely comminuted fracture
Intact fellow bone (intact fibula may prevent apposition of fracture fragments in case of fracture tibia)
Soft tissue interposition
Fracture-specific factors:
Vicarious blood supply: Some fractures are prone to non-union due to the vicarious blood supply of the bone. Examples of bones with vicarious blood supply include- Fracture neck of femur, Fracture proximal pole and waist of scaphoid, Fracture body of talus, Fracture lateral condyle in children, Fracture base of fifth metatarsal, Fracture distal one-third of tibia and distal one-third of the ulna.
Pathological fractures and neuropathic fractures have poor healing potential.

Factors related to treatment:
Inadequate reduction
Inadequate internal fixation: Implant too short, construct too stiff.
