## Question

A 13-month old male with arthrogryposis has little active motion of his elbows. He appears to have good strength of his triceps. Which treatment is most indicated at this time?

## Options

1. Corrective osteotomy of the humerus
2. Electromyogram and nerve conduction study of the upper extremities
3. Observation
4. Passive manipulation and serial casting
5. Soft tissue release with tendon transfer

## Correct option

**4. Passive manipulation and serial casting**

## Answer explanation

Arthrogryposis is a nonprogressive congenital disorder involving multiple rigid joints leading to severe limitation in motion. Passive manipulation and serial casting are the recommended first lines of treatment.

Arthrogryposis leads to several orthopedic manifestations, including upper extremity deformity, teratologic hip subluxation and dislocation, knee contractures, clubfoot or vertical talus, neuromuscular scoliosis and fractures. Treatments attempt to optimize function by providing the ability to control an electric chair and use computer assisted devices, while maintaining at least one elbow in extension for perianal care and the other in flexion to allow for feeding. Conservative management with passive manipulation and serial casting is the first line of treatment. Soft tissue releases, tendon transfers, and osteotomies are generally considered after following a trial of conservative management and generally performed after the age of 4 years old (although some advocate for earlier intervention) to allow for independent eating and performing ADLs.

*Van Heest et al. reviewed surgical treatment of arthrogryposis of the elbow and compared tendon transfer results using ROM criteria with functional use criteria.* The 18 tendon transfers evaluated by strength and ROM criteria showed 9 triceps to biceps transfers and 5 pectoralis to biceps transfers. **They concluded** that optimal surgical candidates for tendon transfer are children older than 4 years who have full passive ROM of the elbow in the dominant arm and at least grade 4 strength of the muscle to be transferred.

*Carroll et al. reviewed 15 patients that underwent triceps to radius transfers to restore active flexion to the paralyzed elbow.* **They reported** that results are generally predictable in the paralytic and traumatic cases, however, in arthrogrypotic patients, the results are less predictable. Nonetheless, **they conclude** that the results are still encouraging enough to justify further clinical use.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1&5: Soft tissue releases, tendon transfers, and osteotomies are generally considered after the age of 4 years old to allow for independent eating and performing ADLs. As this patient is only 13-months old, a trial of conservative management is first indicated
Answer 2: Electromyogram and nerve conduction study of the upper extremities are not indicated at this time as passive motion with serial casting should be attempted initially
Answer 3: Observation alone is not recommended as this will not help to improve elbow extension deformity. Passive motion and serial casting are recommended at initial management with surgical interventions considered in an older patient
