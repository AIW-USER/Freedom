## Answer explanation

An isolated femoral shaft fracture in a 2-month-old can be effectively managed with application of a Pavlik harness.

Pediatric femoral shaft fractures in patients up to 6 months of age are frequently treated with a Pavlik harness. Any femoral shaft fracture in a child who is not ambulatory should raise concern for non-accidental trauma and appropriate evaluation by child protective services should be performed. The potential for bony remodeling is so dramatic in this young age group that clinically significant residual angulation is rare. Care should be taken to avoid excessive hip flexion or abduction when applying a Pavlik harness, given the risks of femoral nerve palsy and femoral head osteonecrosis, respectively.

*Rush et al. reviewed 10 patients under the age of 6 months who sustained isolated femoral shaft fractures treated with a Palvik harness.* At average follow up of 5.2 years of age, **they found** an average of 3 degrees valgus and 5 degrees procurvatum residual angulation with no complications noted. **They concluded** that these patients have excellent clinical outcomes with minimal complications.

*Podeszwa et al. compared children under 1 year treated with a Pavlik harness versus spica casting for isolated femoral shaft fractures.* **They found** no differences in radiographic outcomes but a higher rate of skin complications with spica casting. **They concluded** that patients up to 1 year of age are candidates for Pavlik harness application for treatment of isolated femoral shaft fractures.

Figure C shows an external fixator used for a pediatric femoral shaft fracture. Figure D is an example of a spica cast placed for a femoral shaft fracture in a child > 6 months of age. Figure E is an example of Bryant traction. Figure F shows a Pavlik harness.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Flexible intramedullary nails would expose this young child to an unnecessary procedure.
Answer 2: External fixation in an isolated femoral shaft fracture would expose the child to an unnecessary procedure.
Answer 3: A spica cast would allow for healing but would require an anesthetic event and has higher rates of skin complications and is not necessary for a child less than 6 months old.
Answer 4: Traction is rarely used and would subject this young child to an unnecessary procedure.
