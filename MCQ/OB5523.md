## Question

A 9-year-old boy presents to clinic 2 weeks after a fall playing basketball with his injury radiographs shown in Figures 1 and 2, measuring with 18 degrees of deformity on lateral image. He was initially seen at an outside hospital emergency room without orthopedic services where no reduction was performed. What is his expected long-term outcome with no further reduction?

## Options

1. Malunion with deformity requiring osteotomy and correction
2. Chronic deformity with functional deficit requiring physical therapy
3. Chronic deformity with painful range of motion
4. Functional deficit with no deformity
5. No long-term deformity or functional deficit

## Correct option

**5. No long-term deformity or functional deficit**

## Answer explanation

A mild to moderate deformity of the metaphyseal distal radius in a young child is expected to correct with no long-term deficits.

In the skeletally immature wrist, there is high remodeling potential at the distal radial physis and metaphysis, especially for deformity "in the plane of the joint" (i.e. for wrist, an apex volar or dorsal deformity, seen best on the lateral view Xray). A greater amount of deformity is tolerated at injury for younger children (typically defined as less than 8 or 10 years old). Younger children can typically remodel apex volar/dorsal deformities up to approximately 20 degrees, and up to 10 degrees for older children. Radial/ulnar deformity (seen on AP Xray) is less tolerated, and rotational deformity is minimally tolerated.

*Houshian et al analyze remodeling after Salter-Harris II fractures of the distal radius in children and report 86% complete radiologic remodeling with initial post-reduction deformities averaging 10 degrees.* Near full remodeling was seen in all patients up to age 10, and patients of all ages had no residual deficits in grip strength or wrist range of motion.

*Larsen et al in their classic paper evaluate 70 children with distal forearm fracture and median follow up of 3.5 years.* **They report** near complete remodeling without surgery in patients up to age 10 with deformity angles up to 28 degrees.

Figures 1 and 2 are the AP and Lateral Xrays of a skeletally immature distal radius demonstrating metaphyseal fracture with 15 degrees of angulation.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1-3: Do not expect long-term malunion or deformity
Answer 4: Do not routinely expect long-term deficits from this injury if appropriately immobilized.
