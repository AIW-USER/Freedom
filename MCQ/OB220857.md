## Question

A 27-year-old patient presents after a motor vehicle collision with the closed fracture shown in Figures A and B. Which of the following immediate treatment options has been demonstrated to decrease soft tissue complications while also optimizing outcomes?

![[q220857-a-f-a-20a.jpg]]
*Figure A*

![[q220857-b-screenshot-2025-10-04-at-8-26-32-pm.jpg]]
*Figure B*

## Options

1. Ankle spanning external fixation with staged reconstruction
2. Closed reduction and casting
3. Immediate tibiotalar fusion
4. Open reduction internal fixation of the tibia and fibula
5. Open reduction internal fixation of the tibia with staged fixation of the fibula

## Correct option

**1. Ankle spanning external fixation with staged reconstruction**

## Answer explanation

External fixation of the tibia followed by delayed definitive reconstruction after soft tissue swelling (usually in 1-2 weeks) is associated with decreased soft tissue complications (Answer 1).

Tibial plafond fractures are intra-articular injuries involving the distal tibial articular surface and are frequently associated with significant soft tissue trauma. Historically, early attempts at definitive fixation in the acute setting led to high rates of wound complications. A staged management approach, including initial temporary external fixation to allow for soft-tissue recovery, maintenance of alignment, and wound care, followed by delayed open reduction and internal fixation, has been shown to reduce soft tissue complications while achieving satisfactory alignment and functional outcomes.

*Sirkin et al. published a landmark study on staged treatment of tibial plafond fractures.* *They evaluated a total of 56 pilon fractures.* *All patients underwent external fixation with staged reconstruction.* *They had a total of two deep wound infections in their entire cohort, both patients with open fractures.* **They concluded** that this treatment protocol has resulted in a decreased rate of soft tissue complications.

*Kottmeier et al. reviewed the treatment of tibial plafond fractures.* Throughout their review, **they emphasize** the importance of soft tissue management in decreasing complications. **They conclude** by stating that despite staging treatment of tibial plafond fractures, infection is still a feared complication that further challenges treatment of this injury.

*Swords et al. conducted a study on staged treatment and primary arthrodesis when treating tibial plafond fractures.* **They conclude** that primary arthrodesis can be used in patients with nonreconstructible articular injuries, delayed presentation, significant medical comorbidities, or peripheral neuropathy.

Figures A and B demonstrate an AP and lateral ankle radiograph of a tibial plafond fracture with significant metaphyseal comminution. An ipsilateral calcaneus fracture is also noted.

Incorrect Answer Choices:
Answers 2-5: These treatment options have not been associated with a decreased rate of soft tissue complications when treating tibial plafond fractures.
