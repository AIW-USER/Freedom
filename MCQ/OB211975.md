## Options

1. This specific injury pattern is often associated with ACL tears
2. Excision has shown improved functional outcome scores compared to repair in this patient's age group
3. This injury is associated with an accelerated pattern of medial compartment osteoarthritis
4. There is no anticipated functional improvement with repair compared to excision in this patient's age group
5. There is a 30% chance the patient will require a total knee arthroplasty (TKA) in 5 years regardless of current management.

## Correct option

**3. This injury is associated with an accelerated pattern of medial compartment osteoarthritis**

## Answer explanation

The patient in the vignette has a medial meniscus posterior root tear (MMPRT) with significant meniscus extrusion. These injuries have been shown to lead to an accelerated rate of medial compartment osteoarthritis.

Posterior meniscus root tears are a subset of meniscus tears that have high associated morbidity when not repaired. These tears often lead to meniscus extrusion out of the joint space and essentially functionally equivalent to a complete meniscectomy. As such, they often lead to accelerated joint space narrowing, cartilage wear, and progressive knee osteoarthritis. Patients with these tears who undergo non-operative management or partial meniscectomy progress to total knee arthroplasty (TKA) at a 30% rate at 5 years followup. As such, it is recommended that patients with posterior meniscus root tears undergo prompt repair to help slow down the rate of osteoarthritis.

*Krych et al. performed a retrospective review of the functional outcome scores of 52 patients with MMPRT with a minimum 2-year follow-up.* **They noted** 16 patients (31 %) underwent TKA at a mean of 30 months followup with higher Kellgren-Lawrence grades associated with increased rates of arthroplasty. Overall, 87 % of patients failed non-operative treatment. **The authors concluded** that the non-operative treatment of MMPRT is associated with poor clinical outcomes, worsening arthritis, and a relatively high rate of arthroplasty at a 5-year follow-up.

*Chung et al. compared the clinical and radiologic outcomes of partial meniscectomy (n = 20) with those of repair (n= 37) in patients with MMPRT at a minimum 5-year follow-up.* **The authors noted** the width of the medial joint space and K-L osteoarthritis grade worsened significantly in both groups. **They noted** that the repair group showed significantly less K-L grade progression (P = .005) and less medial joint space narrowing (P < .001) than the meniscectomy group. Furthermore, the rate of conversion to TKA was 35% in the meniscectomy group, whereas there was no conversion to TKA in the repair group. **The authors concluded** that with respect to MMPRT, repair was much more effective than partial meniscectomy in terms of the clinical and radiologic outcomes and survival for at least 5 years' follow-up.

*Chung et al. evaluated the extent that meniscus extrusion in MMPRTs affected the clinical and radiographic outcomes in 39 patients who underwent repair with a 5-year follow-up.* Two groups were identified on MRI studies: Group A (increased extrusion; n= 23) and Group B (decreased extrusion; n = 16). **They noted** that the KL arthritis grade significantly progressed and the medial joint space significantly decreased in Group A but not in Group B. The authors noted that in patients with MMPRTs, decreased meniscus extrusion at 1-year post-op had more favorable clinical scores and radiographic findings compared to those with increased extrusion at 1 year. **The authors concluded** that one of the main goals of the repair of MMPRTs is to reduce meniscus extrusion as much as possible.

*LaPrade et al. compared patient outcomes after repair of posterior root tears between patients <50 and those ≥50 years of age.* *The authors looked at 45 knees in 45 patients with an average age of 38 who were available for followup.* *Three of 45 (6.7%) required revision surgery with all 3 patients being <50 years old.* No significant difference in failure was found based on age or laterality. For both age cohorts, Lysholm and WOMAC scores demonstrated significant postoperative improvement. **The authors conclude** that the outcomes after meniscus root repairs significantly improved postoperatively and patient satisfaction was high, regardless of age or meniscal laterality.

Figures A and B are normal AP and lateral radiographs of the left knee. Figures C and D are successive T2-weighted coronal MRI cuts showing a medial meniscus posterior root tear with significant medial meniscus extrusion. The fibula is also noted in Figure C indicating the medial meniscus is injured. Figure E shows a T1-weighted sagittal MRI that shows a "Ghost sign" which is the absence of an identifiable meniscus in the sagittal plane or an increased signal replacing the normally dark meniscal tissue indicative of a meniscal root tear. Illustration A is a labeled version of Figure E with the "Ghost sign" labeled.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: LATERAL, not medial, meniscus root tears are often associated with ACL tears.
Answers 2 and 4: Repairing a MMPRT has shown significantly improved functional outcomes as compared to resection/partial meniscectomy.
Answer 5: While patients with MMPRTs who undergo partial meniscectomy or non-operative management have a ~30% rate of TKA at 5 years follow-up, those who undergo repair have shown to have significantly less chance (0% on some studies) of requiring TKA at 5-year follow-up.
