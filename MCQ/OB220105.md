## Question

A trauma attending receives a consult for an acetabular fracture at the Level 1 trauma center. After reviewing the patient's chart and imaging, he notifies his arthroplasty colleague of the case. The plan is for acute open reduction and internal fixation (ORIF) with acute total hip arthroplasty (THA). Which of the following scenarios best meets indications for acute THA with ORIF?

## Options

1. 69-year-old with an isolated anterior column fracture and no antecedent hip pain
2. 28-year-old with an infrafoveal femoral head fx and a 20% posterior wall fracture
3. 78-year-old with a 10% posterior wall fracture
4. 58-year-old with a transverse acetabular fracture and good bone quality
5. 72-year-old with a posterior column fracture with a gull sign and a femoral head fracture

## Correct option

**5. 72-year-old with a posterior column fracture with a gull sign and a femoral head fracture**

## Answer explanation

Many different indications exist for treating acetabular fractures with acute THA and ORIF. Of the options above, this treatment is indicated in the elderly patient with a posterior wall fracture, marginal impaction (gull sign), and concomitant femoral head fracture (answer choice 5).

Acetabular fractures create significant trauma and insult to the hip joint. Outcomes of acetabular fractures are dependent on the quality of reduction and ability to decrease the risk of post-traumatic arthritis in the hip joint. Many factors (older age, poor reduction, poor quality, etc) can increase the risk of a patient receiving a delayed THA for the salvage of failed operative fixation. On the contrary, THA is indicated in acute acetabular fractures in certain settings. THA, with or without fracture fixation, is considered for elderly patients with poor bone quality, severe comminution, preexisting hip arthritis, significant femoral head damage, marginal impaction, posterior dislocation, and significant posterior wall involvement. Acute THA combined with fracture fixation is preferred in these cases to provide a more durable and reliable outcome than fracture fixation alone. Multiple studies have demonstrated adequate outcomes with acute THA for acetabular fractures in this group. The indications are fairly variable in the literature, with the indications expanding over time. Ultimately, the decision to treat acetabular fractures with THA is determined by factors such as patient age, bone quality, fracture type, and functional demands of the patient.

*Salama et al. published a retrospective review to evaluate the outcomes of simultaneous ORIF with acute THA in elderly displaced acetabular fractures.* *The author's indications for acute THA with ORIF were marked comminution, significant articular injury or impaction, concomitant femoral neck fracture, or preexisting symptomatic arthritis.* *The authors studied 18 patients, averaging 66 years of age, who underwent combined THA + ORIF for an average follow-up of 22 months.* Regarding clinical outcomes, thirteen patients reported excellent Harris Hip Scores, and five patients reported good results. All patients in this study achieved independent ambulation, and all fractures healed without delayed unions or non-unions. There were no signs of prosthetic loosening, although two patients developed heterotopic ossification that did not require reoperation. Overall, **the authors concluded** that acute THA with ORIF is an effective treatment option for specific acetabular fractures in elderly patients.

*Sierra et al. published a review of the role of THA in acetabular fractures.* *The authors outline specific indications for acute THA, including young patients with both femoral head and acetabular comminution that preclude reconstruction and elderly patients with severe comminution.* Regarding THA for post-traumatic arthritis, **the authors note** several unique challenges. Residual deformity, acetabular bone loss, osteonecrotic bone fragments, retained hardware, heterotopic ossification, and sciatic nerve involvement are several of the mentioned challenges that make salvage THA more difficult than a standard primary THA. **The authors note** the good outcomes of THA for post-traumatic arthritis while acknowledging the history of problematic acetabular component fixation. In conclusion, **the authors note** unique challenges in these cases while expecting the already adequate outcomes to improve with more modern implants.

*De Bellis et al. published a systematic review of six studies evaluating the outcomes of acetabular fractures undergoing acute THA.* **The authors state** the indications for primary THA include poor bone quality, concomitant femoral neck fractures, pathologic fractures, and concurrent hip arthritis. Combined ORIF and THA include longer surgical times, higher infusion rates, and multiple technical challenges. This review demonstrated satisfying outcomes concerning clinical assessment and walking ability after acute THA. **The authors concluded** that acute THA for acetabular fractures can be successful in elderly patients with osteoporosis, concomitant femoral neck fractures, or pathological fractures.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer choice 1: An isolated anterior column fracture without joint degeneration or femoral head damage can be successfully managed with ORIF. Isolated anterior column fractures are not a current indication for acute THA.
Answer choice 2: Concomitant femoral head and acetabular fracture is an indication for acute THA (with or without acetabular ORIF) for most surgeons in elderly, osteoporotic patients. However, given this patient's young age, ORIF should be attempted.
Answer choice 3: A smaller posterior wall fracture and well-preserved femoral head typically do not require acute THA. For a posterior wall fracture of this nature, stability of the hip joint should be assessed, with ORIF being indicated for hip instability.
Answer choice 4: A transverse acetabular fracture in a patient with adequate bone quality can be successfully treated with ORIF. Without femoral head damage, and assuming an anatomic reduction can be achieved, THA is not necessary.
