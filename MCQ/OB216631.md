## Question

A 65-year-old man is referred to you for a heel ulcer that has been present for 5 weeks and refractory to wound care. He has noted an increasing amount of exudation requiring twice-daily dressing changes instead of daily dressing changes. Examination reveals a plantar heel ulcer measuring 1.5cm in diameter with surrounding erythema. He is afebrile. His morning blood sugar log is shown in Figure A. White blood cell count, C-reactive protein, and erythrocyte sedimentation rate are minorly elevated. Radiographs do not reveal any fracture, osteolysis, gas, foreign body, or joint effusion. Which of the following is not indicated?

![[q216631-a-fig-a.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Surgical debridement
2. Tighten glycemic control
3. Imaging
4. Total contact casting
5. Antibiotics

## Correct option

**4. Total contact casting**

## Answer explanation

It is not appropriate to place an infected diabetic heel ulcer in a total contact cast.

This patient has a diabetic foot infection and a heel ulcer. Infection is indicated by increased wound exudate, elevated lab markers (WBC, ESR, CRP), and worsening blood glucose readings. Diabetic foot infection is limb-threatening and is the cause of amputation in 25-50% of patients. Clinical signs are reduced in diabetics because of diminished WBC function, peripheral arterial disease (PAD), poor metabolic control, and neuropathy. Common signs include increased exudation and worsening of glycemic control. Probing to bone is highly suggestive of osteomyelitis. In such cases, further evaluation with MRI, CT, or bone scan may be of value.

*Apelqvist et al. reviewed diagnostics and treatment of the diabetic foot.* **They report** that duration of ulcer >30 days, recurrent ulcer, trauma, probing to bone, and co-existing PAD are factors related to the development of wound infection (osteomyelitis or soft tissue infection). **They state** that in 50% of patients, increased WBC, ESR, CRP, and body temperature were absent.

*Butalia et al. performed a review of features suggesting osteomyelitis in patients with diabetes confirmed on MRI.* **They found** that ulcers >2 square cm, positive "probe-to-bone", ESR >70mm/h, and abnormal plain radiograph had a positive likelihood ratio of 7.2, 6.4, 11, and 2.3 respectively. A positive and negative MRI had likelihood ratios of 3.8 and 0.14 respectively, and an accuracy of 89%. **They found** that no single historical or physical examination finding reliably excludes osteomyelitis.

Incorrect Answers
Answer 1: Given this chronic wound is refractory to wound care and now infected, surgical debridement would be appropriate.
Answer 2: Worsening glycemic control is an indicator of worsening infection. It would be appropriate in the perioperative period to obtain better blood glucose control.
Answer 3: The chronicity of the wound, as well as the appearance of the wound, may push toward obtaining an MRI.
Answer 5: In addition to surgical debridement, antibiotic therapy is indicated for this infected diabetic ulcer. The specific antibiotic agent should be directed by cultures taken intraoperatively.
