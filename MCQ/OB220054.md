## Question

A 45-year-old male presents to the orthopedic clinic with a chief complaint of right hand pain that is limiting his function and quality of life. The patient is a rancher and reports that he has significant pain whenever he is lifting heavy machinery or moving hay bales. His plain films reveal right thumb trapeziometacarpal osteoarthritis without the involvement of the scaphotrapeziotrapezoidal (STT) joint. Three steroid injections have given him significant but short-term relief, and he is now seeking a longer lasting solution. What is the most appropriate next step in treatment?

## Options

1. Trapeziectomy with suture anchor suspensionplasty
2. Trapeziectomy with tendon interposition
3. Thumb carpometacarpal joint arthroplasty
4. Thumb carpometacarpal arthrodesis
5. Repeat steroid injection and immobilization

## Correct option

**4. Thumb carpometacarpal arthrodesis**

## Answer explanation

This manual laborer has trapeziometacarpal (TMC) arthritis without STT involvement. Thumb carpometacarpal arthrodesis is the most appropriate management (Answer choice 4).

TMC arthritis is more common in women over 75; however, it can also present in younger patients with greater physical demands. Management of this common condition depends on disease progression and patient factors. In a young manual laborer, TMC arthrodesis relieves arthritic pain while preserving grip strength. However, there is a relatively high non-union rate (8%-21%). A relative contraindication to arthrodesis is significant arthritic changes in the adjacent STT joint. Other treatment options, including trapeziectomy with or without suspensionplasty or interposition arthroplasty, are not durable treatment options in this population.

*Rizzo et al. published the results of a retrospective review of 126 patients who underwent TMC arthrodesis.* **They reported** a 17% non-union rate, with 96% of patients reporting they were very satisfied or satisfied with their outcome. **They also reported** a significant decrease in pain scores and a significant increase in grip and pinch strength. **The authors conclude** that arthrodesis reduces pain, improves function, and results in excellent patient satisfaction.

*Goldfarb et al. published a review article regarding the indications and techniques for TMC fusion.* **The authors report** that arthrodesis provides a strong and stable thumb which is well-suited to heavy laborers. They recognize that multiple fixation options are reasonable, but they prefer a 2.0 mm T plate.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1 and 2: Trapeziectomy with or without interposition or suspensionplasty is not recommended in young manual laborers. There is concern regarding loss of grip strength and lack of salvage procedures.

Answer 3: Although several arthroplasty options have been proposed, the clinical results do not support their use in young manual laborers. Arthroplasty can also result in significant bone loss, which complicates salvage procedures.

Answer 5: This patient has trialed three steroid injections and is seeking a long-term solution. A repeat injection will not likely produce a long-term solution.
