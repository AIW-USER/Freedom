## Question

A 35-year-old, healthy female presents with paroxysmal episodes of fingertip discoloration. She reports that, on occasion, the fingertips of her right hand turn white with associated pain. Symptoms seem to be provoked in cold weather but can persist even with warming and glove wear. Physical examination and radiographs during today’s visit are benign, with no digital ulcerations present. She was previously seen by an outside physician for this issue with no improvement after over one year of treatment with multiple medications including a calcium channel blocker, an angiotensin II receptor blocker, and a topical nitrate. She reports that the symptoms are substantially impacting her work as a courtroom stenographer and asks about any additional treatment options that may be available. Which of the following options would be the next most appropriate recommendation?

## Options

1. Continued medical management
2. Botulinum toxin A injections
3. Thorascopic sympathectomy
4. Microvascular reconstruction
5. Discontinuation of all medications and clinical monitoring

## Correct option

**2. Botulinum toxin A injections**

## Answer explanation

Botulinum toxin A injections (Answer 2) are the next most appropriate treatment option for Raynaud’s disease which has failed medical management.

The patient presents with symptoms and a history consistent with Raynaud’s disease or primary Raynaud’s syndrome, which is a vasospastic disease of the digital arteries with no known underlying cause. This pathology is often seen in premenopausal women. In cases that have not responded to medical management, off-label use of botulinum toxin A injections is the next most appropriate treatment option.

*Porter and Murray reviewed Raynaud’s phenomenon, delineating this entity from Raynaud’s disease due to a known underlying etiology such as scleroderma, in the authors’ associated case example.* *They detailed treatment options ranging from initial management with oral calcium channel blockers and brachial plexus injections to peripheral sympathectomy and arterial bypass grafting in patients who fail conservative measures.* *The authors cited the previously reported efficacy of botulinum toxin A injections in the hand web spaces in patients with Raynaud’s phenomenon, with resultant improvement in Doppler blood flow, ulcer healing, and symptoms.*

*Valdovinos and Landry reviewed the evaluation and management of patients with Raynaud’s syndrome.* *They detailed initial treatment measures to include warming, avoidance of cold exposures, and medical management with calcium channel blockers, alpha 1-adrenergic blockers, ACE inhibitors, and angiotensin II receptor blockers, among others.* **The authors highlighted** more invasive measures used in patients with recalcitrant symptoms after medical management, including botulinum toxin A injections, noting its reported efficacy in cases involving both vasospastic and obstructive disease.

Incorrect Responses:
Answer 1: The patient’s symptoms have persisted despite over one year of medical management. Continuing this strategy alone is unlikely to result in clinical improvement.
Answer 3: Thorascopic sympathectomy is less often utilized due in part to the risk of a rebound hypersympathetic response.
Answer 4: Microvascular reconstruction may be indicated in rare cases involving arterial occlusion.
Answer 5: Clinical monitoring alone is unlikely to result in clinical improvement.
