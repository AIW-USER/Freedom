Chapter: Arthroplasty MCQs — Wear And Osteolysis (Hip)

## Question

Which technique is not associated with increased chance of cemented femoral stem loosening

## Options

1. Failure to remove the soft cancellous bone from the medial surface
2. Removal of all trabecular bone from the canal
3. Failure to prevent stem motion while the cement is hardening
4. Canal preparation with hydrogen peroxide

## Correct option

**4. Canal preparation with hydrogen peroxide**

## Answer explanation

The following are technical problems that contribute to stem loosening:

- Failure to remove the soft cancellous bone from the medial surface of the femoral neck; consequently, the column of cement does not rest on dense cancellous or cortical bone and support the stem. The cement is subjected to greater forces and fractures more easily.
- Failure to provide a cement mantle of adequate thickness around the entire stem; a thin column cracks easily. The tip of the stem should be supported by a plug of cement because this part of the stem is subjected to axial loading.
- Removal of all trabecular bone from the canal, leaving a smooth surface with no capacity for cement intrusion or failure to roughen areas of smooth neocortex that surrounded previous implants.
- Inadequate quantity of cement and failure to keep the bolus of cement intact to avoid lamination.
- Failure to pressurize the cement, resulting in inadequate flow of cement into the interstices of the bone.
- Failure to prevent stem motion while the cement is hardening.
- Failure to position the component in a neutral alignment (centralized) within the femoral canal.
- The presence of voids in the cement as a result of poor mixing or injecting technique or allowing blood or fragments of bone to be mixed in the cement.

Answer: 4
