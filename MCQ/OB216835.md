## Answer explanation

The conoid ligament attaches 4.5 cm medial to the distal end of the clavicle and connects the clavicle and the coracoid. It's one of the primary vertical stabilizers of the distal clavicle.

Stability of the distal clavicle is primarily provided by the acromioclavicular (AC) and coracoclavicular (CC) ligaments. The CC ligaments are the conoid and the trapezoid, which insert 4.5 and 3 cm medial to the distal end of the clavicle, respectively. When rupture of these ligaments occurs in the setting of fracture or AC separation, the clavicle is superiorly displaced. This displacement is often an indication for surgical intervention as the distal end of the clavicle is destabilized.

*Hyland et al review the anatomy of the shoulder, upper limb, and clavicle.* **They report** that the lateral clavicle serves as the attachment site for the deltoid and trapezius as well as the conoid and trapezoid ligaments. **They conclude** that the lateral portion of the clavicle is primarily stabilized by the conoid and trapezoid ligaments.

*Kim et al review the current concepts for classification and treatment of distal clavicle fractures.* **They report** that the Neer classification system is commonly used and based on the fracture location in relation to the CC ligament on simple anteroposterior radiographs and its involvement. Many fixation options for the treatment of distal clavicle fractures exist including precontoured locking plate, hook plate, CC fixation with a screw or flexible material, arthroscopic technique, tension band wiring, and transacromial fixation. **They conclude** that no gold standard exist for the fixation of distal clavicle fractures.
