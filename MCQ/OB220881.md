## Question

A 45-year-old male with a history of type 2 diabetes presents for evaluation of multiple non-healing diabetic ulcers on the right forefoot with necrotic tissue present; they have not improved despite multiple years of wound care. After discussion, the decision was made to proceed with a transmetatarsal amputation due to the severity of his ulcers. He undergoes a preoperative work-up, which is significant for a hemoglobin of 12.5 g/dL, total lymphocyte count of 1700 cells per microliter, albumin of 2.9 g/dL, and total protein of 6.9 g/dL. Vascular studies reveal a right toe pressure of 55. Which of the aforementioned lab values puts the patient at a higher risk for wound-healing complications?

## Options

1. Albumin of 2.9 g/dL
2. Hemoglobin of 12.5 g/dL
3. Total protein of 6.9 g/dL
4. Total Lymphocyte count of 1700 cells per microliter
5. Right toe pressure of 55 mmHg

## Correct option

**1. Albumin of 2.9 g/dL**

## Answer explanation

An albumin of less than 3.0 g/dL is a risk factor for wound healing complications in the amputation population (Answer 1).

Diabetic foot ulcers are a common complication in the diabetic patient population that largely form as a result of sensory neuropathy. Deep ulcers, especially those that are non-healing or have necrotic tissue, predispose patients to infection and may warrant amputation to mitigate this risk. Factors associated with improved amputation healing include an albumin > 3.0 g/dL, transcutaneous oxygen tension > 30 mmHg, toe pressures > 40 mmHg, total lymphocyte count > 1500 cells per microliter, hemoglobin > 11.0 g/dL, and total protein > 6.2 g/dL. When possible, patients should be optimized before surgery with these guidelines in mind.

*Armstrong et al. published a review on diabetic foot ulcers.* *They cover the underlying etiology, risk factors, classification schemata, prevention tactics, wound management strategies, choice of dressings, utility of a multidisciplinary approach, prognosis, and outcomes of this condition.* **They conclude** that this condition affects a large proportion of the population worldwide and requires a multifaceted approach to avoid severe complications such as amputation or death.

*Berli published a review on the role of midfoot and hindfoot amputations in the diabetic population to prevent the spread of infection and preserve as much of the native body as possible.* *They review the techniques, outcomes, and required prosthetics of midfoot (i.e., Lisfranc, Bona-Jaeger, and Chopart) and hindfoot amputation (Boyd, Spitzy-Pirogoff, Syme) strategies.* **They concluded** that midfoot amputations may offer a reasonable alternative to more major and proximal amputations in the diabetic foot population without the high rate of complications seen with hindfoot amputations.

Incorrect Answers
Answer 2: A hemoglobin level less than 11 g/dL is a risk factor for wound healing problems in amputations. 12.5 g/dL is above this threshold.
Answer 3: Total protein less than 6.2 g/dL is a risk factor for wound healing problems in amputations. 6.9 g/dL is above this threshold.
Answer 4: A total lymphocyte count less than 1500 cells per microliter is a risk factor for wound healing problems in amputations. 1700 cells per microliter is above this threshold.
Answer 5: A toe pressure less than 30 mmHg is a risk factor for wound healing problems in amputations. 55 mmHg is above this threshold.
