## Question

Figure A is the radiograph of a 72-year-old female who underwent bilateral THA approximately 10 years ago. The left hip was subsequently revised secondary to the development of a pseudotumor. At the time of revision surgery the femoral trunion was found to be in good condition and the surgeon elected to proceed with a head and liner exchange with a ceramic head of a different size and a polyethylene insert. 3 weeks after revision surgery the patient sustains an atraumatic dislocation. Which of the following is the most likely cause of instability in this patient?

![[q217360-a-mom-hip.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Lack of offset
2. Utilization of a larger femoral head
3. Abductor insufficiency
4. Vertical acetabular component
5. Bony impingement

## Correct option

**3. Abductor insufficiency**

## Answer explanation

Aseptic lymphocyte-dominant vasculitis associated lesions (ie. pseudotumor) is a unique complication associated with metal on metal hip arthroplasty that results in significant soft tissue damage. This can result in abductor insufficiency and an increased risk of dislocation following revision surgery.

While not all patients with a metal on metal articulation will develop adverse local soft tissue reactions, this is a potentially devastating complication that may occur. Local wear debris as well as hypersensitivity to metal ions have both been implicated as the cause for soft tissue destruction. The resultant pseudotumor often has a dark pigmentation secondary to the local metal reaction. The amount of metal debris, soft tissue necrosis, type and number of inflammatory cells differ from patient to patient. The primary concern with local soft tissue destruction is the integrity of the abductors following debridement.

*Rahman et al review the outcomes of revision of THA in patients following failed metal on metal articulations.* **They report** that severe soft tissue destruction and abductor dysfunction were common findings. **They conclude** that the use of a constrained liner may be required in these patients.

*Crawford et al review mid-term outcomes following revision of failed metal on metal THA.* **They report** that pain levels significantly improved from 17.2 pre-revision to 32.8 post-revision. Reoperation was required in 14% of hips. **They conclude** that revision of failed metal on metal hips results in improved clinical outcomes, but presents many surgical complications especially in the setting of a soft tissue destruction.

Figure A is an AP pelvis radiograph demonstrating bilateral total hip arthroplasties with metal on metal articulations.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: No information is given to suggest that the offset has changed following revision surgery, lack of abductor integrity is a more likely cause of instability following revision of metal on metal components.
Answer 2: Metal on metal articulations allowed for the use of much larger femoral heads than what can be achieved with ceramic and polyethelene for the same acetabular size.
Answer 4: The acetabular component was not changed and appears to be well positioned in the radiograph.
Answer 5: Bony impingement would not be expected to occur following a head and liner exchange if it did not occur preoperatively.
