## Question

## Options

4. Incomplete ASIA B
5. Complete ASIA C

## Correct option

**4. Incomplete ASIA B**

## Answer explanation

The clinical presentation is consistent with an Incomplete ASIA B spinal cord injury due to a bilateral C6-7 facet dislocation.

Fifty percent of spinal cord injuries result from cervical spine trauma. This is most commonly associated with motor vehicle accidents. Assessment of the degree of injury is completed after spinal shock has resolved. The ASIA scale is used to grade the severity of spinal cord injuries. Any perineal/perirectal sensation automatically indicates that the injury is not complete (cannot be an ASIA A). ASIA B refers to an incomplete injury where sensation is spared, but motor for function is not. ASIA C is an incomplete injury where less than 50% of motor groups have 3/5 strength.

*Furlan et al. conducted a retrospective review to evaluate the psychometric aspects of the ASIA scale to determine motor and sensory function in acute spinal cord injury cases.* **They note** that the ASIA standards are appropriate to evaluate patients with SCI in long-term fashion.

Figures A, B and C show representative sagittal CT scan sequences through a C6-7 facet dislocation. Figure D is an axial CT scan sequence showing the anterior location of the C6 inferior facets as result of the trauma. Illustration A is an summary chart of the ASIA impairment scale. Illustrations B and C are the front and back pages of the ASIA examination sheet.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Given that this patient has intact sensation, he is an ASIA B at the very least.
Answer 2: An ASIA A is a complete injury by definition
Answers 3, 5: There is no “complete” ASIA B or ASIA C on the ASIA scale
