## Question

A 52-year-old female with metastatic breast carcinoma presents with moderate left shoulder pain. Figures A through C are radiographs, CT scan and bone scan images of her shoulder. Examination reveals mild tenderness but no swelling around the proximal humerus. Examination is negative for other causes of shoulder pain. According to the Mirels' scoring system, what is the most appropriate next step in treatment?

## Options

1. Observation is appropriate because her Mirels' score is 4.
2. Radiation therapy is appropriate because her Mirels' score is 6.
3. Prophylactic internal fixation with a proximal humeral locking plate is appropriate because her Mirels' score is 8.
4. Prophylactic fixation with an intramedullary nail is appropriate because her Mirels' score is 7.
5. Proximal humeral replacement is appropriate because her Mirels' score is 8.

## Correct option

**2. Radiation therapy is appropriate because her Mirels' score is 6.**

## Answer explanation

This patient has metastasis to the proximal humerus. The lesion has a Mirels' score of 6 (moderate pain = 2; upper extremity = 1; 1/3 to 2/3 of the diameter = 2; blastic = 1). Nonoperative treatment is indicated. Radiation therapy is a good option for pain control.

Twenty percent of skeletal metastases occur in the upper extremity, with 50% occurring in the humerus. The surgical options are as follows: For proximal humerus (Bickel's Type I) lesions, endoprosthetic replacement is recommended; for diaphyseal (Type II) lesions, intramedullary rods, intercalary spacers, or plates and screws are options; for distal lesions (Type III), flexible nails or elbow replacement are options.

*Mirels proposed a scoring system for diagnosing impending fractures.* **He found** that risk of fracture was 33% at 6 months for a score of 9. **He proposed** that lesions with scores of 8 or higher require prophylactic internal fixation prior to irradiation.

*Evans et al. reviewed the Mirels' rating system for fracture prediction in humeral metastases.* Although the original literature defined a cut-off of 9 points or greater for prophylactic stabilization for all long bone lesions, **they recommend** a score of 7 or greater for identifying impending pathologic fractures of the humerus.

Figure A is a radiograph showing a blastic lesions in the left humeral head. Figure B is an axial CT image showing the same lesion occupying 1/3 to 2/3 of the humeral head diameter. Figure C is a technetium methylene diphosphonate bone scan showing increased tracer uptake in the left humeral head. Illustration A shows the Bickel's classification of humerus metastasis (left, Type I, proximal humerus; center, Type II, diaphysis; right, Type III, distal humerus). Illustration B shows the Mirels' classification of long bone metastasis.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Mirels' score is 6. Some form of pain control is necessary. Radiation therapy is a viable option.
Answer 3: Plate fixation is ineffective because of thinned, compromised cortical bone. Surgery is not indicated in this case.
Answer 4: Intramedullary nails are less effective for the proximal humerus because solid fixation cannot be achieved around the metaphyseal flare. These are options for diaphyseal lesions, and if the Mirels' score is high.
Answer 5: Proximal humeral replacement is the treatment of choice for proximal humeral lesions with high Mirels' scores.
