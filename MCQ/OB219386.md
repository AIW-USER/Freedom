## Options

1. Nonossifying fibroma
2. Aneurysmal bone cyst
3. Chondromyxoid fibroma
4. Atypical cartilaginous tumor
5. Chondrosarcoma

## Correct option

**3. Chondromyxoid fibroma**

## Answer explanation

This young adult patient has a large, eccentric metaphyseal lesion containing a sclerotic rim of bone with an overall lobulated appearance. When considered in conjunction with his biopsy results, his tumor is most consistent with a chondromyxoid fibroma (Answer 3).

Chondromyxoid fibromas (CMF) are benign tumors most encountered in the second or third decade of life with a predilection for males over females. These tumors appear as a well-marginated focal bone lesion with a sclerotic rim, often with a lobulated appearance. Radiographically, they often are indistinguishable from a nonossifying fibroma, except CMF is often larger and persistently active past skeletal maturity. On histologic examination, they demonstrate lobules of hypocellular fibromyxoid tissue within a chondromyxoid matrix (Illustration A). As with other benign cartilage lesions, treating symptomatic lesions involves intralesional surgical curettage and bone grafting or reconstruction as needed.

*Lersundi and colleagues performed a retrospective case series review regarding the features and treatments for their institutional cohort of 30 CMF patients.* **The authors highlight** that CMF is commonly found in the metaphysis of lower extremity long bones and is expansile but does not grossly violate the outer cortical bone. **They note** that curettage and bone grafting had a very low risk for recurrence with overall good outcomes. **The authors concluded** that these tumors are important to consider in the differential diagnosis of expansile metaphyseal tumors in the lower extremity.

*Sono and colleagues performed a 10 patient case series review regarding distinctive features of CMF of the pelvis.* **The authors discuss** that while CMF has a peak incidence in the second-third decade of life, typically in the metaphysis of long bones of the extremity, there is a recognized second peak incidence within the fifth to second decades of life, where they may be found in the central pelvis. **They discuss** that in this older cohort, pelvic CMF appears very similar to chondrosarcoma, being large tumors with soft tissue extension. **The authors conclude** that a biopsy is the only reliable way to distinguish CMF from chondrosarcoma in these cases.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1&2: While this tumor resembles a large nonossifying fibroma or even an aneurysmal bone cyst, its pathologic features resemble a chondromyxoid fibroma.
Answer 4: Grade 1 chondrosarcomas of the extremities are now classified as atypical cartilage tumors (ACTs) by the World Health Organization. They appear most similar to large enchondromas with intralesional calcifications and endosteal scalloping (Illustrations B&C).
Answer 5: Chondrosarcoma is the most common primary bone sarcoma in the United States and is classically seen in older individuals (age 50+). It is often seen as a large, lytic bone lesion with gross cortical erosion and associated soft tissue mass (illustration D).
