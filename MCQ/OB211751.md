## Question

In counseling a patient undergoing a lumbar microdiscectomy, you inform them that the rate of reoperation is which of the following?

## Options

1. 5% at 8 years with or without an incidental durotomy at the time of surgery
2. 15% at 8 years with or without an incidental durotomy at the time of surgery
3. 50% at 8 years with an incidental durotomy, 15% at 8-years without a durotomy
4. 50% at 8 years with or without an incidental durotomy
5. A repeat microdiscectomy is not likely to be successful and should not be attempted without spinal fusion

## Correct option

**2. 15% at 8 years with or without an incidental durotomy at the time of surgery**

## Answer explanation

According to the Spine Patient Outcomes Research Trial (SPORT), the 8-year reoperation rate following a lumbar microdiscectomy is 15%; an incidental durotomy sustained at the time of surgery has not been demonstrated to adversely affect long-term outcomes.

The treatment of symptomatic lumbar disc herniation is non-operative for at least 6 weeks (unless the presentation is cauda equina syndrome), as 90% of patients will have an improvement in symptoms during this period of time. Operative treatment involves removing a portion or all of the lamina and a part of the disc. The rate of an incidental durotomy at the time of surgery is 1%. Other surgical complications include recurrent herniations, discitis, and vascular catastrophe.

*Leven et al. performed a sub-analysis of the 8-year SPORT data looking at risk factors for reoperation in patients treated surgically for intervertebral disc herniation.* *They analyzed baseline patient characteristics and follow-up times.* The reoperation rate was 15%; 62% underwent reoperation due to a recurrent disc herniation, 25% because of a complication or other factor, and 11% because of a new condition.

*Desai et al. also analyzed the SPORT data to determine if incidental durotomy had any effect on long-term outcomes in cases of spinal stenosis.* *The average patient follow-up was 43.8 months.* **They found** that long-term outcomes were not affected by an incidental durotomy in a first-time lumbar laminectomy for spinal stenosis; these can reasonably be extrapolated to disc herniation.

*Lee et al. provide a review article detailing recurrent lumbar disc herniation.* *They define this condition as recurrent back and/or leg pain after a pain-free period of at least 6-months from the time of index surgery.* During revision surgery, fusion was only indicated in the presence of segmental instability or significant foraminal stenosis from disc space collapse.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1, 3, and 4: The reoperation rate for lumbar microdiscectomy, regardless of an incidental durotomy at the time of surgery, has been shown to be 15% at 8 years.
Answer 5: Revision microdiscectomy outcomes have been shown to be as good as primary discectomy; motion sparing procedures should always be attempted when indicated.
