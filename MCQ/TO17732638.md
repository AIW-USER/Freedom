## Question

A bowler presents with wrist pain and difficulty in bowling. On examination, he is found to have pain over the radial aspect of the wrist that is aggravated by flexing the thumb and by applying ulnar flexion. What is the most likely diagnosis? (regional orthopedics- hand and wrist joint- De Quervain’s tenosynovitis)

## Options

1. De Quervain tenosynovitis
2. Tennis elbow
3. Golfer’s elbow
4. Lunate AVN due to repetitive microtrauma

## Correct option

**1. De Quervain tenosynovitis**

## Answer explanation

De quervain’s tenosynovitis: De Quervain’s tenosynovitis predominantly involves the abductor pollicis longus (APL) and the extensor pollicis brevis (EPB) tendons, in the first dorsal compartment of the wrist. This stenosing tenosynovitis is caused by myxoid degeneration and thickening of tendon sheath of the first dorsal compartment. This is an overuse syndrome caused by repetitive and continued strain of the APL and EPB tendons as they pass under a thickened and swollen extensor retinaculum.

Patient presents with wrist pain which exacerbates on thumb movement and radial or ulnar deviation of the wrist.

Finklestein’s test: In a patient with De quervain’s tenosynovitis, the thumb is flexed (MP joint flexion) within a closed fist and wrist is ulnar deviated. It causes pain over the styloid process of the radius.

Treatment: Conservative treatment includes activity modification, NSAIDS, Ice massage, thumb spica splint and corticosteroid injection in the sheath of APL and EPB. If conservative treatment fails then surgical release of the first dorsal compartment is done.

Answer: 1
