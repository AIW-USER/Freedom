## Question

You’re evaluating an 13-year-old new patient in your office for scoliosis, which was initially noticed on an annual school physical. Upon initial questioning, they endorses mild activity-related back pain that has been present since the start of soccer season 2 weeks ago. On exam, there is an obvious curvature with Adam’s forward bending test, normal symmetric reflexes, and no skin lesions. PA radiograph of the spine is shown in Figure 1. Which of the following factors may warrant MRI evaluation of the spine in this patient?

![[q2269-a-screen-shot-2021-07-11-at-11-17-19-am.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Age
2. Left sided thoracic curve
3. Risser stage
4. Presence of back pain
5. Degree of scoliotic curve

## Correct option

**2. Left sided thoracic curve**

## Answer explanation

Left-sided scoliotic curves are rare and warrant MRI evaluation to rule out a cyst or syrinx.

Adolescent idiopathic scoliosis occurs in patients age 10-18-years-old, and most commonly involves right-sided curves. While most cases of adolescent scoliosis are idiopathic, certain situations warrant MRI for further evaluation. Atypical curve patterns such as left-sided curves, left-sided thoracolumbar curves, short angular curves, and apical kyphosis, as well as curves with rapid progression or neurologic symptoms should be investigated further with an MRI. Advanced imaging should extend from the posterior fossa to the conus and is used to rule out intraspinal abnormalities.

*Nohria et al. reviewed the presentation, diagnosis, and management of 43 patients with Chiari I malformations over a 12-year period.* **They report** that the widespread use of MRI around 1985 led to a significantly faster diagnosis in this group of patients. **They conclude** that patients without hydrosyringomyelia were unlikely to develop scoliosis.

*Goldberg et al. reviewed the patterns and diseases associated with left thoracic scoliotic curves.* **They report** that in their patients, older boys were more likely to have a left thoracic curve and have an underlying disease. **They conclude** that all new scoliosis patients should be investigated meticulously, and that the need for MRI is based on the patient’s entire picture, of which, curve laterality is only a part.

*Barnes et al review atypical scoliotic curves and the need for MRI.* **They report** atypical features to be left sided thoracic and thoracolumbar curves, rapid progression, neurologic symptoms and pedicle thinning. **They conclude** that there is a very high association with hydrosyringomyelia and left sided thoracic and thoracolumbar curves.

Figure 1 is a PA radiograph of the spine demonstrating a left-sided thoracic scoliosis.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Adolescent idiopathic scoliosis is diagnosed between the ages of 10-18 years-old. An MRI is not needed because of the patient's age.
Answer 3: Risser staging is used to estimate the future growth potential in patients with scoliosis, this does not impact whether or not an MRI is obtained.
Answer 4: Idiopathic scoliosis does not typically cause back pain. Pain that is not functional disruptive, present only with activities and only for a short time should be noted, but does not require further imaging.
Answer 5: Degree of the curve, without evidence of rapid progression, is not an indication for MRI.
