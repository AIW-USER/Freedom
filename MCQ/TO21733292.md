#TO #hand #seymourfracture
Chapter: Trauma MCQs — Fracture Dislocations of Hand

## Question

True about Seymour fracture is all except

## Options

1. seen in adults following hyperextension injury to thumb during sports
2. seen in children when finger catches in the door
3. mallet finger deformity
4. injury to nailbed

## Correct option

**1. seen in adults following hyperextension injury to thumb during sports**

## Answer explanation

Answer: 1
EXPLANATION: -

Seymour Fractures are displaced distal phalangeal physeal fractures with an associated nailbed injury in children. These usually result from direct trauma (fall of heavy object) or crush injury ( finger caught in door). Middle finger is most commonly involved.

![[TO21733292-1.png]]

Diagnosis is made clinically with the presence of nail plate lying superficial to the eponychial fold and radiographs potentially showing widened physis or displacement between the epiphysis and metaphysis. Radiologically, they can involve the epiphysis (Salter-Harris [SH] types I and II) or the metaphysis just 1 to 2 mm distal to the physis (juxta-epiphyseal). Nailbed injury may involve nailbed laceration and nail plate subluxation.
Similar to mallet finger in adults, injury may cause imbalance between flexor (inserts into metaphysis of distal phalanx) and extensor tendons (inserts into epiphysis of the distal phalanx) and flexed posturing of the distal phalanx.
Treatment: minimally displaced, closed fracture can be splinted. Displaced closed fracture may require close/open reduction (in case of soft tissue interposition) and k wire fixation across DIPJ. Nail bed repair is required in case of nailbed injury.
