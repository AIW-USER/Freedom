## Question

A 27-year-old male who sustained a bimalleolar ankle fracture one year ago reports continued ankle pain despite sufficient physical rehabilitation and progressive weightbearing. His immediate postoperative radiographs are depicted in figure A. Which of the following is the most likely reason for the patient's continued pain?

![[q218583-a-fibular-shortening.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Fibular nonunion
2. Fibular malunion
3. Use of a syndesmotic screw instead of dynamic fixation
4. Unrecognized deltoid ligament injury
5. Osteochondral injury of the talus

## Correct option

**2. Fibular malunion**

## Answer explanation

The immediate post-operative radiographs reveal a malreduced fibula fracture with significant shortening and syndesmotic malreduction.

Ankle fractures are common orthopedic injuries that are the result of rotational injury mechanisms. Treatment of unstable ankle fractures with syndesmotic injury is almost always surgical. Restoring fibular length is critical to reestablishing the ankle mortise and reducing the syndesmosis. In cases where the fibula has malunited and shortened, an oblique fibular osteotomy to restore length and rotation can salvage the syndesmotic reduction.

*Sinha et al. retrospectively studied 7 patients that were treated with fibular lengthening for fibular malunions.* **They reported** all patients had complete restoration of fibular length and ankle mortise with no cases of nonunion at 11 months follow-up. **They concluded** fibular lengthening is effective for treating fibular malunion and ankle joint alignment.

*Egger and Berkowitz reviewed the operative treatment of fibular fracture malunions.* **They stated** many fibular malunions can be addressed with osteotomies that restore the length and rotation of the distal fibula. **They concluded** the oblique fibular osteotomy is preferred for SER-type fibular fracture malunions.

*Laflamme et al. performed a randomized double-blind controlled trial evaluating outcomes following dynamic or static stabilization of syndesmotic injuries.* **They reported** significantly better outcomes at 3 and 12 months with Olerud-Molander scores with suture button fixation, but higher rates of implant failure (36.1% vs 0%), and reduction loss (11.1% vs 0%) with static screw fixation. **They concluded** dynamic stabilization of ankle syndesmotic injuries achieves better clinical and radiographic outcomes.

Figure A is an AP radiograph of the ankle with a fibular malreduction with shortening and syndesmotic malreduction.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The patient may have sustained a nonunion of a fibula fracture, but given the post-operative radiographs, a malunion is more likely.
Answer 3: The use of dynamic fixation for the syndesmosis is unlikely to have affected whether the patient developed ankle pain symptoms or not given the postoperative radiographs demonstrating malreduction.
Answer 4: There is a possibility that a deltoid ligament injury could have been unrecognized. But given the high degree of fibular shortening and syndesmotic malreduction, a fibular malunion remains the most likely cause of his symptoms.
Answer 5: An osteochondral injury of the talus may have occurred at the time of injury, but again, a fibular malunion remains the most likely cause of his symptoms.
