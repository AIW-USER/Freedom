## Question

A 17-year-old female presents for evaluation of a limb length discrepancy. While standing, a 10mm block is placed under her short leg resulting in a leveled pelvis. She has no lower extremity contractures. Which of the following next steps would be most appropriate following her initial screening?

## Options

1. Observation
2. Perform a CT scanogram
3. Epiphysiodesis of the longer limb
4. Femoral lengthening of the shorter limb
5. Physeal bar excision

## Correct option

**1. Observation**

## Answer explanation

This patient is skeletally mature and has a <2cm limb length discrepancy (LLD). Recommended treatment for a LLD <2cm at maturity is a shoe lift or observation.

This patient underwent the appropriate initial screening for her LLD. While standing, she required a 10mm (1cm) block under the shorter limb to result in a leveled pelvis. This method is an accurate, simple, and inexpensive way to assess LLD. Differences of less than 2cm require a shoe lift or observation. Surgical intervention may be indicated with a greater LLD or if skeletally immature.

*Stanitski et al. review the assessment and treatment of limb-length inequality in the growing child.* **They report** that in the immature patient, consistent longitudinal data are essential to avoid pitfalls in the projection of ultimate length difference. They discuss common therapeutic options ranging from no treatment to surgical shortening or lengthening procedures. **They conclude** that the indication for lengthening is a disparity exceeding 5 to 6 cm, while epiphysiodesis or femoral shortening is useful for smaller discrepancies or residual differences following a contralateral lengthening.

*Blair et al. retrospectively reviewed 67 patients with distal femoral and proximal tibial epiphysiodesis over 14 years for the correction of leg length inequality.* **They reported** that the vast majority of failures were secondary to errors in timing surgery due to incorrect use of Green and Anderson growth prediction tables. **They concluded** that the primary causes of failure would have been decreased by more careful follow-up and the use of the Moseley Graph.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: A CT scanogram is the most accurate method for assessing LLD in the presence of lower extremity contractures
Answer 3: Epiphysiodesis of the longer limb may be required in the skeletally immature patient
Answer 4: Femoral lengthening is typically reserved for LLD >5-6 cms
Answer 5: Physeal bar excision may be indicated if there is a bony bridge involving <50% of the physics with at least 2 years of growth remaining
