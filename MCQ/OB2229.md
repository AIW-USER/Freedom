## Question

An 8-year-old boy with cerebral palsy presents to your clinic for evaluation. His mother is concerned that he gets very tired during ambulation. On gait exam, the child demonstrates a crouch gait. The combination of which of the following leads to this pathologic gait?

## Options

1. Hip flexion, knee extension, ankle eversion
2. Hip abduction, knee flexion and ankle plantar-flexion
3. Hip extension, knee extension, and ankle plantar-flexion
4. Hip flexion, knee flexion, ankle dorsiflexion
5. Hip adduction, knee extension and ankle dorsi-flexion

## Correct option

**4. Hip flexion, knee flexion, ankle dorsiflexion**

## Answer explanation

Crouch gait is defined as excessive ankle dorsiflexion, knee flexion and hip flexion during the stance phase.

Crouch gait is a common gait deviation, often seen among ambulatory diplegic and quadriplegic patients with cerebral palsy, once they reach the pubertal growth spurt. This often results in a declining strength to weight ration resulting in weak muscles that can no longer support an upright walking pattern. This form of gait is highly inefficient and compromises walking ability. Treatment generally involves surgical corrections directed at multiple levels, ideally during a single-stage multi-level surgery (SEMLS) event used to address each of the pathological components of this gait pattern. These components commonly include psoas lengthening to address hip flexion contracture, hamstring lengthening or distal femur extension osteotomy for knee flexion contracture, and bony osteotomies and foot reconstruction for lever arm dysfunction.

*Kedem et al. perform an overview of the pathoanatomy, natural history, and treatment of crouch gait.* **They note** that hamstrings are often not shortened in patients with crouch gait and patella alta must be addressed if surgery is performed. Surgical correction of joint contractures and lever arm dysfunction can be effectively achieved through a single-event multilevel surgery. **The authors conclude** that crouch gait is multifactorial, and surgery should be tailored to meet the individual's specific anatomic and physiologic abnormalities.

*Joseph et al. describe a different protocol for treatment aimed at correction of the flexion deformity of the knee, weakening of the hamstrings, and augmenting the power of the knee and hip extension, which was used on 17 children with severe crouch.* *The authors describe the 2-stage procedure which involved shortening of the femur, plication of the patellar tendon, transfer of the semitendinous to the back of the femur, and fractional lengthening of the other hamstrings.* **They noted** improvement in gait, increased power of the quadriceps, improved knee range of motion and correction of patella alta in all patients. **The authors conclude** that this method of treatment of severe crouch gait outlined in this study seems to be an effective and safe method of dealing with this difficult problem.
