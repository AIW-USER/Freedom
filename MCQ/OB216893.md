## Question

A 41-year-old male construction worker presents with chronic right elbow pain. He sustained a radial head fracture about 10 years ago that was treated conservatively. He developed worsening pain and stiffness that required a Kashiwagi-Outerbridge procedure but has persistent pain that prevents him from performing his job. His orthopedic surgeon recommends an interposition arthroplasty. Which of the following would be a contraindication for this procedure?

## Options

1. Post-traumatic arthritis
2. Inflammatory arthritis
3. Elbow instability
4. Young patient age
5. Previous elbow debridement

## Correct option

**3. Elbow instability**

## Answer explanation

Elbow instability is a contraindication for interposition arthroplasty.

Elbow arthritis is most commonly the result of inflammatory causes such as rheumatoid arthritis. This can be a difficult entity to treat in younger, high-demand patients due to the poor outcomes of total elbow arthroplasty. Initial treatment can consist of joint debridement, capsular release, and osteophytectomy to improve range of motion and decrease pain. In patients that are refractory to joint debridement, an interposition arthroplasty with autograft or allograft is an effective salvage treatment. However, in the presence of joint instability, an interposition arthroplasty is likely to fail.

*Celli and Morrey retrospectively studied 49 patients under the age of 40 years that underwent primary total elbow arthroplasty.* **They reported** at an average of 91-months follow-up there was a 22% reoperation rate and 92% excellent-good outcomes on the Mayo Elbow Performance score. **They concluded** TEA in young patients is associated with high revision rates, especially for those treated for post-traumatic arthritis.

Blaine, et al. retrospectively studied the outcomes of 12 patients that underwent TEA after elbow interposition arthroplasty. They reported a significant reduction in pain and improvement in elbow function by the Mayo Elbow Performance Score with 2 patients requiring revision surgery. They concluded TEA can be successfully performed in patients after interposition arthroplasty.

*Larson and Morrey retrospectively studied 38 patients treated with interposition arthroplasty for elbow arthritis.* **They reported** a significant increase in elbow flexion arc of motion and significant improvement in patient-reported outcomes while patients with preoperative instability were associated with poor outcomes. **The authors concluded** interposition arthroplasty is an effective salvage procedure for young patients with inflammatory and post-traumatic arthritis without elbow instability.

Incorrect answers
Answers 1 and 2: Post-traumatic and inflammatory arthritis are not contraindications for interposition arthroplasty.
Answer 4: Younger patient is not a contraindication for interposition arthroplasty.
Answer 5: Previous surgery is not a contraindication for elbow interposition arthroplasty.
