## Question

A patient presents to your office for evaluation for her bunion. The decision is made to proceed with surgical intervention as she has failed an extensive period of conservative management. In which of the following scenarios would a Lapidus procedure be most appropriate?

## Options

1. HVA of 37, IMA of 18, and TMT joint hypermobility in young patient
2. HVA of 20, IMA of 11, and no TMT joint hypermobility in young patient
3. HVA of 28, IMA of 14, and no MTP joint hypermobility in young patient
4. HVA of 38, IMA of 17, and MTP joint hypermobility in older patient
5. HVA of 23, IMA of 12, and no MTP joint hypermobility

## Correct option

**1. HVA of 37, IMA of 18, and TMT joint hypermobility in young patient**

## Answer explanation

A Lapidus procedure is a first TMT joint arthrodesis with distal soft tissue procedures (medial eminence removal, first web space release of adductor hallucis, lateral capsule release). Hypermobility of the TMT joint on examination is an indication for first TMT joint arthrodesis.

Hallux valgus, commonly referred to as a bunion, is a complex valgus deformity of the first ray that can cause medial big toe pain and difficulty with shoe wear. Radiographs of the foot are obtained to identify the severity of the disease and for surgical planning. Treatment can be nonoperative with shoe modifications for mild and minimally symptomatic cases. Surgical management is indicated for progressive deformity and difficulty with shoe wear. A Lapidus procedure may be performed with severe deformity and/or hypermobility of the first ray.

*Easley et al. review the etiology, pathomechanics, degrees of severity, and treatments for hallux valgus deformity.* **They reported** that multiple proximal first metatarsal procedures, when combined with a distal soft-tissue procedure, appear to provide satisfactory treatment for moderate-to-severe hallux valgus deformity. **They concluded** that first MTP joint arthrodesis appears to offer satisfactory outcomes in patients with severe hallux valgus.

*Perera et al. reviewed the first ray and its inherently unstable axial array that relies on a fine balance between static (capsule, ligaments, and plantar fascia) and dynamic stabilizers (peroneus longus and small muscles of the foot) to maintain its alignment.* **They reported** that in some feet, there is a genetic predisposition for a nonlinear osseous alignment or a laxity of the static stabilizers that disrupt this muscle balance and that many inherent or acquired biomechanical abnormalities are identified in feet with hallux valgus. **They concluded** that treatment (conservative or surgical) should be tailored to the individual.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 2&5: A chevron osteotomy would be appropriate in mild hallux valgus deformity following failed conservative treatment
Answer 3: A chevron or a modified McBride + Akin would be appropriate in moderate hallux valgus deformity following failed conservative treatment
Answer 4: TMT (not MTP) joint hypermobility would be adequately addressed by a Lapidus procedure
