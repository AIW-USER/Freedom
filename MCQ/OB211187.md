## Question

A 32-year-old male is diagnosed with a hereditary motor sensory neuropathy resulting from a partial duplication within the gene for peripheral myelin protein 22. In the setting of this disease, each of the following characteristic deformities is accurately represented by the pathologic process responsible EXCEPT:

## Options

1. Claw toes result from both strong extrinsic flexors and extensors overpowering atrophied lumbricals and interossei
2. First metatarsal plantarflexion is driven by the hypertrophic peroneus longus overpowering a weak tibialis anterior
3. First metatarsophalangeal joint hyperextension is driven by recruitment of the extensor hallucis longus in place of a weak tibialis anterior
4. Forefoot supination is driven by the relatively stronger peroneus longus indirectly overpowering a weak peroneus brevis
5. Hindfoot varus is driven by the preserved tibialis posterior overpowering a relatively weaker peroneus brevis

## Correct option

**4. Forefoot supination is driven by the relatively stronger peroneus longus indirectly overpowering a weak peroneus brevis**

## Answer explanation

All of the above answers are characteristic deformities and respective responsible pathologic processes involved in the development of the cavovarus foot deformity in Charcot-Marie-Tooth (CMT) with the exception of answer #4. The forefoot is brought into pronation and not supination by deforming forces.

CMT results in peripheral neuropathy that potentiates the characteristic foot deformity through the variable effects on the musculature of the lower extremity. The peroneus longus and tibialis posterior are generally spared early along, while the intrinsics of the foot, peroneus brevis, and tibialis anterior are directly affected. With intrinsic atrophy, the relative strength of the long extensor tendons affects interphalangeal joint flexion and metatarsophalangeal joint hyperextension (an intrinsic minus position of the foot). It is generally agreed upon that the cavovarus deformity in CMT is forefoot-driven. It begins with plantarflexion of the first metatarsal by the hypertrophic but architecturally normal peroneus longus over its antagonist, the tibialis anterior. The extensor hallucis longus is recruited to facilitate dorsiflexion, but in the absence of a functional tibialis anterior further drives down the first metatarsal through the windlass mechanism. The relatively stronger tibialis posterior, antagonized by a weak peroneus brevis, drives the hindfoot into a compensatory varus and contributes to forefoot pronation.

*Younger et al. review the presentation and management of the characteristic foot deformities seen in CMT.* *The authors discuss the contribution of both the involved and initially unaffected musculature toward the progression of the disease.* **They note** the importance of understanding the underlying disease process in order to adequately address the deformities. Supple deformities can often be treated with soft tissue procedures, while progression to rigid deformity necessitation combining this with bony reconstruction.

*Wines et al. review the spectrum of foot deformities associated with heredity motor and sensory neuropathies (HSMN) based on their institutional experience.* **The authors discuss** that while cavovarus was the most common in general, planovalgus deformity was the next most common and that this was dictated related to the type of HMSN. Corrective surgery was more often undertaken for the cavovarus foot and more often constituted combined bony and soft tissue procedures.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The intrinsic musculature of the foot is the first to be affected by peripheral neuropathy in CMT. Claw toes result from the pull of the extrinsic flexors and extensors, which are relatively stronger early in the disease process.
Answer 2: The essential deformity in this disease process is first metatarsal plantarflexion, or cavus. This is driven by a strong and hypertrophic but architecturally normal peroneus longus which overpowers its direct antagonist, the tibialis anterior.
Answer 3: First metatarsophalangeal joint hyperextension results from over-pull of the extensor hallucis longus as a weak dorsiflexor compensating for a deficient tibialis anterior. Interestingly, the cavus is thought to be further exacerbated by the extensor hallucis longus which additionally drives down the first metatarsal head via the windlass mechanism on the medial plantar fascia.
Answer 5: The varus component of the cavovarus deformity is attributable to the hindfoot, which is affected by a strong and unaffected tibialis posterior that overpowers its antagonist, a relatively weaker peroneus brevis.
