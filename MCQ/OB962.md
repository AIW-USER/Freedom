## Question

Patients display a Trendelenburg gait to compensate for weakness in which of the following muscle groups?

## Options

1. hip adductors
2. hip abductors
3. hip flexors
4. knee extensors
5. hip extensors

## Correct option

**2. hip abductors**

## Answer explanation

In the stance phase of gait in the hip of a normal hip individual, the hip joint force is produced by the body weight times its moment arm (distance from the center of gravity of the body to the hip joint) which is balanced by the force exerted by the abductors. However, in individuals with hip joint problems, functional adaptations take place to reduce stimulus such as pain, muscle weakness or instability. Trendelenberg gait is one adaptation where avoidance of stress on the hip abductor muscles is achieved by shifting the body weight over the center of the hip joint, effectively decreasing the moment arm and requiring proportionally less effort from the abductors. This is shown in Illustration A. Therefore, a patient with a Trendelenberg lurch are effectively decreasing the hip joint forces as a method of adaptation.
