## Question

A skeletally mature 15-year-old girl with juvenile hallux valgus presents to your clinic for a second opinion. She has attempted nonoperative management with shoewear modifications and is interested in surgical intervention. She has seen an outside orthopaedic surgeon who has recommended a distal first metatarsal osteotomy. In your discussion with her, you recommend a double first metatarsal osteotomy. Which of the following is considered an indication to perform a double first metatarsal osteotomy to treat juvenile hallux valgus?

## Options

1. Neuromuscular disease
2. Hallux valgus angle of 32 degrees and distal metatarsal articular angle of 12 degrees
3. Skeletal immaturity
4. Hallux valgus angle of 32 degrees and distal metatarsal articular angle of 25 degrees
5. Hypermobility of the first tarsometatarsal joint

## Correct option

**4. Hallux valgus angle of 32 degrees and distal metatarsal articular angle of 25 degrees**

## Answer explanation

Patients with juvenile hallux valgus deformity with an elevated Distal Metatarsal Articular Angle (DMAA) are most appropriately treated with a double first metatarsal osteotomy.

Initial management of juvenile hallux valgus is similar to adult hallux valgus and mainly consists of shoewear modifications. Surgical correction can be offered to patients who fail nonoperative management. Surgical intervention is typically delayed until skeletal maturity to decrease the risk of recurrence, the most common complication. In patients who are skeletally immature with severe disease, a first cuneiform osteotomy is often employed. In patients with moderate (>25 degrees) to severe (>40 degrees) Hallux Valgus Angle (HVA) and an elevated DMAA (> 15 degrees), a double first metatarsal osteotomy can be used to correct the deformity.

*Peterson et al. describe 15 juvenile hallux valgus cases treated with a double first metatarsal osteotomy and pin fixation.* **They reported** an average of 24-degree improvement in HVA and 8-degree improvement in Intermetatarsal Angle at the final follow-up. There were no recurrences of deformity or nonunions in this group. **The authors conclude** that the double first metatarsal osteotomy effectively treats juvenile hallux valgus.

*Pentikainen et al. conducted a study to determine preoperative radiographic risk factors for the recurrence of hallux valgus after treatment with a distal first metatarsal osteotomy.* *They evaluated 100 consecutive patients and found that DMAA and preoperative congruency of the 1st metatarsophalangeal joint were both risk factors for recurrence.*

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Patients with neuromuscular disease are more appropriately treated with fusion procedures rather than osteotomies.
Answer 2: In a patient with a moderate HVA and an incongruent 1st metatarsophalangeal joint a distally based procedure would be appropriate to correct the deformity.
Answer 3: Patients with skeletal immaturity are more appropriately treated with first cuneiform osteotomies.
Answer 5: Hypermobility of the Tarsometatarsal joint in the setting of hallux valgus is best treated with a Lapidus procedure.
