## Question

Figure A is the radiograph of a 40-year-old-male who presents to the ED with persistent pain in his distal thigh, which is warm and swollen. When questioned, he also reports recent history of 30-pound weight loss. He denies any history of malignancy. Based on his radiographs, an MRI is obtained and demonstrates diffuse lytic lesions and a large soft tissue mass. What is the most appropriate next step in the management of this patient?

![[q215430-a-xr-lymphoma.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Chemotherapy
2. Marginal resection
3. Wide resection
4. Bone marrow aspiration and biopsy
5. Prophylactic cephallomedullary nailing

## Correct option

**4. Bone marrow aspiration and biopsy**

## Answer explanation

Primary lymphoma of bone is a small round blue cell tumor that can lead to pathologic fracture. With no known primary malignancy, the lesion must be better characterized with bone marrow aspiration and biopsy.

Primary lymphoma of bone is usually a non-Hodgkin’s B-cell variant that occurs more commonly in middle aged males, and can lead to a pathologic fracture. Basic labs and radiographs should be ordered initially for evaluation. Radiographically, there are characteristic large, mottled, lytic lesions. Bone marrow aspiration and biopsy are always required for staging and should be performed before any definitive bony stabilization in cases where there is no known primary malignancy. Histology will show small round blue cells, with immunohistochemical stains being positive for CD20, CD45, and lymphocyte common antigen.

*Dada reviewed the diagnosis and management of follicular non-Hodgkin lymphoma (NHL).* **They found** that prompt diagnosis and risk stratification are important in identifying patients in need of immediate treatment initiation. **They concluded** that follicular NHL has an excellent overall survival rate, despite the disease process being incurable.

*Jawad retrospectively reviewed patients diagnosed with primary lymphoma of bone based on their extent of disease.* **They found** that the 5- and 10-year survival rates were 58% and 45%, respectively, with younger adult patients and those with only local disease having a survival advantage. **They concluded** that diagnostic classifications must differentiate between limited and systemic bony lymphoma in order to guide treatment most appropriately.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Chemotherapy is an important component of treatment in primary bone lymphomas but in this case, bone marrow aspiration and biopsy should always be performed first.
Answer 2-3: Resection of a primary lymphoma of bone is not first line management
Answer 5: The use of intramedullary nailing may be appropriate for prophylaxis of this primary lymphoma of bone, however, biopsy and aspiration should always be performed first to better characterize the lesion in order to guide treatment.
