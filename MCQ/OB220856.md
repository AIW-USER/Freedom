## Question

A 36-year-old mechanical laborer presents with a right proximal both-bone forearm fracture after falling off a ladder. During the surgery, a volar Henry approach is utilized to gain access to the fracture site. If the patient’s forearm is positioned in pronation during the entirety of the procedure, which of the following postoperative complications is the patient at an increased risk of?

## Options

1. Diminished sensation over the first dorsal webspace
2. Weakness with thumb opposition
3. Weakness with thumb extension
4. Diminished sensation of the thumb, index finger, long finger, and radial half of the ring finger
5. Weakness with finger abduction

## Correct option

**3. Weakness with thumb extension**

## Answer explanation

During the volar approach to the forearm, pronation of the forearm positions the posterior interosseous nerve (PIN) closer to the lateral border of the radius and subsequently, at a greater risk of injury. Insult to the PIN is associated with weakness of the common and digital extensors (Answer 3).

The posterior interosseous nerve enters the supinator beneath the arcade of Frohse and is at risk of injury during procedures with approaches to the proximal forearm. Considerations to mitigate the risk of PIN injury include dissecting the supinator off the radius, minimizing the utilization of retractors on the radial side of the proximal radius, and positioning the forearm in supination to move the supinator and its contents (PIN) out of danger when using a volar approach. The PIN provides predominantly motor function to the extensors of the forearm and digits.

*Schulte et al. reviewed the management of adult diaphyseal both-bone forearm fractures.* *They describe the native biomechanics of the forearm, approaches for surgical management (Thompson and volar Henry), implant options, utility of bone grafting, outcomes seen with surgical intervention, and potential complications.* **They concluded** that surgical intervention is recommended for these injuries due to its generally good outcomes and consists of plate/screw fixation or intramedullary nailing to restore native forearm anatomy.

*Cross et al. performed a comparison of the degree of bony exposure and ease of PIN identification with volar and dorsal approaches to the proximal forearm in a cadaveric study.* **They found** that the Thompson approach exposed an average bony area of 7.31 centimeters squared compared to the Henry approach which on average, exposed an average bony area of 6.37 centimeters squared (p = 0.173) They found that the PIN was located, on average, 3.3 cm from the radiocapetellar joint during the Thompson approach and 2.8 cm from the radiocaptellar joint during a Henry approach (0.409). **They concluded** that exposure with either the Henry or Thompson approach has no significant difference in the area of bone exposed or distance of the PIN from the radiocapitellar joint when located.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Sensation over the first dorsal webspace is supplied by the superficial branch of the radial nerve, not the PIN.
Answer 2. Opposition of the thumb is supplied by the recurrent branch of the median nerve, not the PIN
Answer 4. Sensation to the thumb, index finger, long finger, and radial half of the ring finger is supplied by the median nerve
Answer 5. Finger abduction is supplied by the ulnar nerve, not the PIN.
