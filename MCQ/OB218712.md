## Question

A 6-year-old girl is brought to an urgent care by her mother while on a beach vacation because she won't put weight on her right knee. The knee is mildly swollen and she cries when the knee is passively ranged beyond 20° of flexion. Radiographs show no acute osseous pathology, she is afebrile with a temperature of 37.5° C, and laboratory studies reveal a white blood cell count (WBC) of 10,500/mm3, an erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR) of 22 mm/hr, and a CRP of 1.0 mg/dL. Based on a Kocher criteria score of 1/4 in this patient, which of the following statements is most true about the best next step in her care?

## Options

1. She has < 0.2% probability of having a septic knee and should be diagnosed with transient synovitis
2. She should have a knee aspiration given that Kocher criteria are unreliable in predicting septic knees
3. She has a 3% probability of having a septic knee and should be empirically treated with antibiotics
4. She does not require a knee aspiration given that Kocher criteria can reliably predict transient synovitis
5. She has a 3% probability of having a septic knee and should be diagnosed with transient synovitis

## Correct option

**2. She should have a knee aspiration given that Kocher criteria are unreliable in predicting septic knees**

## Answer explanation

The Kocher criteria are predictive of septic arthritis of the hip when attempting to differentiate from transient synovitis, but the predictive value does not translate to septic arthritis of the knee and should not be applied in this setting. The patient should have a knee aspiration to direct further treatment.

The Kocher criteria, as originally described, include fever >38.5° C, refusal to ambulate, elevated WBC > 12,000, and elevated ESR >40. These values were derived only from cases of septic arthritis of the hip in pediatric patients. The predictive values had not received validation outside of pathology involving the hip. Thus, in settings of ambiguous clinical and laboratory findings, the next best step in treatment should be an aspiration with synovial fluid analysis given that it is easily achievable with minimal morbidity and can help to differentiate between septic and non-septic pathology (i.e., transient synovitis, Lyme arthritis, or other inflammatory etiologies), which remain high on the differential diagnosis list and are conditions that do not require surgical intervention.

*Obey et al. provide a review of pediatric septic arthritis of the knee and* **note** that the commonly used predictors of septic hip do not apply. The authors retrospectively reviewed 104 patients with later confirmed septic arthritis. Patients presented with elevated C reactive protein >20 mg/L (75%), fever (65%), non–weight-bearing status (64%), erythrocyte sedimentation rate> 40 mm/h (60%), and/or WBC > 12,000 cells/mm3 (49%). According to the Kocher criteria of the hip, at 3 or more criteria the probability of septic arthritis becomes 93% with a sensitivity of 0.84 provoking many physicians to use this cutoff in their assessment of hip pain. The authors of this study concluded that if these same criteria were applied to their included septic knee pediatric patients, 52% of cases could be missed.

*Baldwin et al. published in JBJS on predictive factors for differentiating between septic arthritis and Lyme Disease of the knee in children.* *The authors reviewed 189 patients and found that although septic arthritis of the knee and Lyme monoarthritis share common features that can make them difficult to distinguish clinically, the presence of pain with short arc motion, C-reactive protein of >4.0 mg/L, patient-reported history of fever, and age younger than 2 years were independent predictive factors of septic arthritis in pediatric patients.* **They concluded** that the more factors that are present, the higher the risk of having septic arthritis.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Having 0/4 Kocher criteria would infer a <0.2% risk of septic arthritis of the hip, the same cannot be confidently inferred in the knee.
Answers 3-5: Though having 1/4 Kocher criteria would infer a 3% risk of septic arthritis, these criteria are only sensitive in the setting of pediatric hip septic arthritis and should not be applied to the pediatric septic knee. The patient should have a knee aspiration performed.
