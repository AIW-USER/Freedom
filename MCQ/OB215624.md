## Answer explanation

This patient has Duchenne's muscular dystrophy, which follows an X-linked recessive inheritance pattern. In X-linked recessive conditions, a female carrier usually passes the diseases to 50% of male children and affected male parents pass the carrier gene on to all female children.

Duchenne's muscular dystrophy is a congenital disease caused by the absence of the dystrophin protein. It affects young males only and is usually noted between ages 2 and 6. The absence of dystrophin leads to increased muscle breakdown with replacement by fatty and fibrous tissue which causes weakness that typically begins proximally. There are a multitude of associated orthopedic conditions, but cardiac and pulmonary issues ultimately contribute to decrease in function and eventual death.

*Read et al. review the diagnosis of Duchenne muscular dystrophy by orthopedic surgeons.* The initial diagnosis of Duchenne's was missed in all (37) cases where the patient saw an orthopedic surgeon initially, causing an average delay in treatment of 2 years. **They conclude** that timely diagnosis is important for clinical and social reasons and should be considered by orthopedic surgeons in cases of abnormal gait, or unexplained lower extremity deformities.

*Sussman wrote a review on the orthopedic manifestations of Duchenne muscular dystrophy, beginning with its epidemiology and pathophysiology.* While there is no cure, treatment includes corticosteroids and rehabilitation to delay muscle and strength loss that leads to functional decline. **The author suggests** that scoliosis should be treated with early spinal fusion to maintain cardiac and pulmonary function.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: This pedigree demonstrates autosomal dominant inheritance in which if one parent has the disease, 50% of children will inherit it. Orthopedic conditions with autosomal dominant inheritance include achondroplasia, apert's syndrome, charcot marie tooth (CMT), and osteogenesis imperfecta (OI) types 1/4.
Answer 2: This pedigree demonstrates mitochondrial inheritance which affects all children if the mother is affected. However, an affected male child will not pass this disease to his offspring.
Answer 4: This pedigree demonstrates X-linked dominant inheritance. All daughters of an affected male will have the disease. There is no male to male transmission, however, meaning males can only inherit the disease if their mother is affected. Orthopedic conditions with an X-linked dominant inheritance include hypophosphatemic rickets.
Answer 5: This pedigree demonstrates autosomal recessive inheritance which is not sex-lined. If both parents are carriers, there is a 25% chance that the child would be affected. Orthopedic conditions with an autosomal recessive inheritance include diastrophic dysplasia, friedrich's ataxia and OI types 2/3.
