## Question

A 9-year-old child presents to your office after he felt a painful snap in his arm while throwing a baseball. He is otherwise in good health and has no family history of disease. A radiograph of his arm is shown in Figure A. What is the next step in your management?

![[q449-a-sbc-humerus-ap.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Sling for comfort
2. Tumor staging
3. Biopsy
4. Open reduction with internal fixation
5. Intramedullary nailing

## Correct option

**1. Sling for comfort**

## Answer explanation

The clinical presentation and radiograph is consistent for a pathologic fracture through a latent (cyst does not communicate with the physis) proximal humerus unicameral bone cyst. The most appropriate treatment is immobilization.

For a unicameral bone cyst with a pathologic fracture in the proximal humerus, treatment is immobilization. Once the fracture heals, active UBC`s (cyst that communicates with the physis) should be treated with a methylprednisolone acetate injection if it does not resolve spontaneously. Curettage and bone grafting be considered if there is no spontaneous healing or the injections fail.

*Betsy et al. report if fracture alignment is acceptable, and the lesion is benign, conservative management is appropriate.* After healing, if the lesion continues to grow or cause problems, curettage and grafting may be necessary

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2 and 3: If the lesion were more aggressive appearing, then staging and biopsy are indicated
Answer 4 and 5: ORIF and intramedullary nailing are not indicated for this child as nonoperative management is the first line of treatment.
