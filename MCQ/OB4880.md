## Question

Medial opening wedge supramalleolar osteotomy is considered a treatment option for ankle osteoarthritis. In which of the following scenarios would this procedure be most appropriate?

## Options

1. Complete obliteration of the ankle joint space with bone-on-bone contact; valgus ankle alignment
2. No joint-space narrowing, but early ankle joint sclerosis and osteophyte formation; valgus ankle alignment
3. Symptomatic narrowing of the ankle joint space medially; varus ankle alignment
4. Symptomatic narrowing of the ankle joint space laterally; neutral ankle alignment
5. Obliteration of the medial joint space that extends to the roof of the talar dome; varus ankle alignment

## Correct option

**3. Symptomatic narrowing of the ankle joint space medially; varus ankle alignment**

## Answer explanation

Medial opening wedge supramalleolar osteotomy would be most appropriate in patients with symptomatic narrowing of the ankle joint space medially and varus ankle alignment.

Medial opening wedge supramalleolar osteotomy is considered a joint-preserving surgical technique. It is indicated for the treatment of ankle osteoarthritis in patients with minimal talar tilt and varus heel alignment. The Takakura classification system classifies varus-ankle arthritis into 4 stages. Supramalleolar osteotomy has been shown to be effective for patients in stage 2 or stage 3a, but clinical results for patients with stage 3b are unsatisfactory.

*Lee et al reviewed the indications for supramalleolar osteotomy.* **They believe** that by shifting the ankle joint load line lateral, this will re-distribute the medial concentration of stress within the ankle joint.

*Pagenstert et al. followed 35 consecutive patients with posttraumatic ankle osteoarthritis.* Using realignment techniques, including supramalleolar osteotomy, **they showed** improvements in patient walking ability and the functional parts of the American Foot and Ankle Society score.

Illustration A shows the 4 stages of the Takakura classification system for varus-ankle arthritis. Stage I: shows no joint-space narrowing, but early sclerosis and osteophyte formation. Stage 2: shows narrowing of the joint space medially. Stage 3: shows obliteration of the joint space with subchondral bone contact medially. Stage 4: shows obliteration of the whole joint space with complete bone contact. Note: Stage 3a shows obliteration of the joint space that is limited in the facet to medial malleolus. Stage 3b shows obliteration of the joint space that has advanced to the roof of the talar dome. Illustration B (left panel) shows a varus ankle as shown with by the tibial anterior surface angle (TAS) and talar tilt angle (TT). On the right panel, a postoperative radiograph is shown that illustrates a supramalleolar opening wedge osteotomy which was made to normalize the TAS and TT angle.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: This would be stage 4 arthritis. Ankle arthrodesis would be most appropriate.
Answer 2: This would be stage 1 arthritis. Conservative, symptomatic treatments would be recommended.
Answer 4. This would be stage 2 arthritis. Lateral joint space narrowing with normal ankle alignment would not benefit from supramalleolar osteotomy. The different causes of isolated lateral joint space narrowing would need to be further investigated (e.g. knee/hip alignment, etc)
Answer 5: This would be stage 3b arthritis. Studies have shown poor outcomes with supramalleolar osteotomy in this patient population.
