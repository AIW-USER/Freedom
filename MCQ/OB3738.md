## Question

A 45-year-old patient has a stage 3 giant cell tumor of the spine with suspected metastasis. What imaging study would most likely detect the location of metastasis?

## Options

1. MRI brain
2. CT brain
3. CT abdomen
4. CT chest
5. MRI spine

## Correct option

**4. CT chest**

## Answer explanation

The lungs are the most common site for metastasis in giant cell tumor of bone. A CT chest would be the best choice.

Giant cell tumor of bone (GCTB) accounts for 3-5% of primary skeletal tumors. It has the highest prevalence in people aged between 20 - 50 years old, with a slight female predominance. GCBT are usually considered benign lesions, however they are known to metastasize to the lung. The metastatic rate for GCT is approximately 3%. Higher risk for pulmonary metastasis includes local tumor recurrence, high grade lesions and spine location of primary lesions.

*Donthineni et al. have reported a pulmonary metastasis ratio of 13.7% in patients with GCTB which occur primarily in the spine.* The treatments for the lung nodules consisted of metastectomy in two patents and chemotherapy in six patients. At final follow-up, 2 out of 7 patients with pulmonary metastasis died from progression of metastatic disease.

*Tunn et al. evaluated 87 patients with giant cell tumors.* *Local recurrence was observed in 11 patients (12.6%).* Three patients with local recurrence also had synchronous pulmonary metastases. Seven patients in total had metastatic disease. 3 out of 7 patients with malignant GCT died from progression of metastatic disease.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1-3, 5: Giant cell tumors of the spine can metastasize to many areas of the body including, the lung, lymph nodes, liver, soft tissues, brain, mediastinum, scalp, kidney and penis. The most common site is the lung.
