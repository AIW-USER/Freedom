## Question

The graph below shows the change in Respiratory Exchange Ratio (RER) as exercise intensity (% VO₂ max) increases.

Based on the concept of the crossover point, which of the following statements is most accurate?

![[TO21733244-1.png]]

## Options

1. At intensities below the crossover point, the body primarily relies on fat oxidation for energy.
2. At intensities above the crossover point, the body primarily relies on protein metabolism for energy.
3. The crossover point occurs at the same exercise intensity for everyone, regardless of training status.
4. Beyond the crossover point, fat oxidation increases while carbohydrate oxidation decreases.

## Correct option

**1. At intensities below the crossover point, the body primarily relies on fat oxidation for energy.**

## Answer explanation

Ans: A

EXPLANATION: -

The respiratory exchange ratio (RER) is the ratio of carbon dioxide produced to oxygen consumed. It's a measure of how the body uses energy and oxygen.
RER is calculated by dividing the amount of carbon dioxide produced by the amount of oxygen consumed.
RER is measured using a respiratory gas analyser.
• Low RER (0.7-0.8) means the body is using more lipids
• High RER (1.0) means the body is using more carbohydrates
• High RER (above 1.0) significant contribution from anaerobic metabolism
As exercise intensity increases, the Respiratory Exchange Ratio (RER) also increases, meaning that with higher intensity exercise, the body relies more on carbohydrates for energy and less on fat, resulting in a higher RER value, typically moving closer to 1.0; at low intensities, RER is closer to 0.7, indicating greater fat utilization.

Why RER changes with intensity:
• Substrate availability:
When exercising at low intensity, the body has more time to mobilize and utilize fat stores, while at high intensity, readily available carbohydrates are preferentially used for energy.
• Muscle fibre recruitment:
During high-intensity exercise, fast-twitch muscle fibres are recruited, which primarily rely on carbohydrates for fuel.

The “crossover point” occurs during exercise when one transitions energy substrate from fat to carbohydrate predominance. At this point 70% energy comes from carbohydrate while 30% comes from fat oxidation.

Training can shift the cross over point to the right indicating higher reliance on fat. However, Sympathetic nervous system activity can shift the cross over point to the left indicating higher carbohydrate oxidation.
