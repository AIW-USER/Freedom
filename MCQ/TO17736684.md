## Question

Most common cause of failed back surgery syndrome at 3 weeks is (spine- miscellaneous)

## Options

1. Wrong diagnosis
2. Spinal infection
3. Adjacent segment disease
4. Spinal instability

## Correct option

**2. Spinal infection**

## Answer explanation

Failed back surgery syndrome (FBSS) is a term used to describe a clinical entity when the outcome of lumbar spinal surgery does not meet the pre-surgical expectations of the patient and surgeon”

Causes of FBSS: Most common cause is improper patient selection for spine surgery. It has been suggested that the most frequent etiologies of FBSS include “wrong diagnosis,” “wrong surgeon,” and/or inappropriate patient selection

Patient related factors:-

- It’s common in those claiming workers' compensation or receiving disability support pension.
- Smoking
- Obesity
- Stress disorder

Operative factors:-

- Infection (discitis)
- A single-level decompressive laminectomy in the presence of unknown multi-level spinal involvement
- Etiology of back pain (foraminal stenosis>disc herniation)

- Adjacent segment disease (also known as ASD or transitional syndrome) is a potential cause of failed back surgery syndrome after spinal fusion surgery. Because fusion is intended to stop movement at a spinal segment, the segments above and below it may overcompensate by moving more. This excessive movement can cause the adjacent levels to degenerate at an accelerated rate and become unstable, causing pain.

- Decompressive surgery in the presence of predominantly axial/mechanical pain is unlikely to lead to a satisfactory outcome
- Patients undergoing lumbar fusion are at risk of loss of sagittal balance, particularly when more than one level is involved.
- Those with reduced sacral tilt after surgery have been found to exhibit loss of natural lumbar lordosis with a resultant increase in stress transfer to the sacroiliac joints
- Hematoma formation in the epidural or subdural space, infection, pseudomeningocele, and nerve injury
- Progression of degenerative changes (new onset foraminal stenosis, new/recurrent disc herniation)

Most common cause of FBSS are:

In the immediate or early period: incorrect diagnosis

Within a few weeks after spine surgery: A postoperative spinal infection

Few months after spine surgery may be caused by a recurrent diagnosis (eg, herniated disc), epidural fibrosis (scar tissue forms around spinal nerve roots), or arachnoiditis.

Years after spine surgery:

Pain years after your spine surgery may be caused by degenerative changes in the spine, spinal instability (eg, spondylolisthesis) or spinal stenosis.

Answer: 2
