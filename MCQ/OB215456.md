## Question

A 42-year-old veteran lumberjack enters into an amateur bare-knuckle boxing bout in the upper peninsula of Michigan one snowy winter evening. At work the next week, the patient notices that he cannot extend his left middle finger. However, he is able to maintain extension if he first passively extends this finger. The camp hand surgeon does not believe he will need surgery if he is compliant with a low profile, finger-based, splint. In what position would this splint put the metacarpal phalangeal joint (MCPJ) and proximal interphalangeal joint (PIPJ) respectively?

## Options

1. MCPJ - 0° extension; PIPJ - 0° extension
2. MCPJ - 0° free; PIPJ - 0° extension
3. MCPJ - 30° hyperextension; PIPJ - free
4. MCPJ - 30° flexion; PIPJ - 0° extension
5. MCPJ - 30° flexion; PIPJ - 0° free

## Correct option

**3. MCPJ - 30° hyperextension; PIPJ - free**

## Answer explanation

Sagittal band injuries can be treated with a custom, low-profile, finger-based splint which places the MCPJ at 30° hyperextension and leaves the PIPJ free for full motion.

Sagittal band injuries are also called Boxer's knuckles and can be caused by striking an object with a closed fist. The radial sagittal band is far more commonly affected than the ulnar sagittal band, which allows the extensor tendon to sublux ulnarly. This pattern can also occur in the setting of rheumatoid arthritis. Injuries can typically be treated with extension splinting; however, recalcitrant cases can be treated surgically with the direct repair of the sagittal band or extensor tendon centralization.

*Hame and Melone reviewed 8 professional athletes who had 11 Boxer's knuckles that underwent surgical repair.* The ulnar two digits were most commonly affected. Each athlete had regained full range of motion and returned to sport at 5 months. No patient required additional surgery.

*Kleinhenz and Adams reviewed closed sagittal band injuries of the MCPJ.* **The authors note** this uncommon injury occurs when the extensor tendon subluxes over the metacarpal head, most frequently after a direct blow. These injuries are typically treated by extension splints. Recalcitrant cases are treated with repair or reconstruction of the sagittal band to prevent further subluxation.

Illustration A is a relative motion splint that can be used for sagittal band injuries, proximal interphalangeal flexor lag, and extensor tendon lacerations. The MCPJ is hyperextended 30° as compared to the neighboring fingers while the PIPJ is free for unrestricted range of motion.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1,2,4, and 5: The correct position for the relative motion splint in the treatment of sagittal band injuries is MCPJ in 30° hyperextension and PIPJ without any range of motion restriction.
