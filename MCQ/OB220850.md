## Answer explanation

Following biopsy of the left thigh mass in this 66-year-old male, Figure E (atypical lipomatous tumor; ALT) of the included slides would require marginal resection without any adjuvant treatment (Answer 5).

Liposarcoma is a common entity encountered in older individuals, greater than 50 years of age. While the entity can occur anywhere in the body, the tumor most commonly occurs in the extremities (75%). There are multiple subtypes of liposarcoma, which include ALTs/well-differentiated tumors (WDT), myxoid, round cell, pleomorphic, and dedifferentiated. All of the aforementioned subtypes can be stratified into a continuum in terms of severity, with ALT/WDT being low grade, myxoid being intermediate, and the latter three being high grade. Low-grade tumors can be reliably treated with marginal resection, whereas intermediate- and high-grade tumors require wide resection and the inclusion of radiotherapy.

*Kooby et al. provide a review of 91 WDT/ALTs, with the primary objective of determining the frequency of local recurrence.* **They noted** 5- and 10-year recurrence rates of 100% and 78%, respectively. **They also reported** a 3% dedifferentiation rate at ten years. When recurrence occurs, **the authors recommend** function-preserving re-excision with the consideration of radiotherapy.

*Pérez-Losada et al. examined the common chromosomal translocations observed in liposarcoma, which include the t(12;16)(q13;p11), creating the FUS-CHOP fusion gene.* **The authors note** that when the FUS domain is lacking in association with the CHOP protein, minimal tumorigenicity is present. **The authors highlight** the necessity of the FUS domain for the development of liposarcoma.

Mentzel and Fletcher provide a synopsis on the diagnosis, clinical features, and treatment of soft tissue lipomatous tumors, ranging from benign lesions to high-grade malignancies. The authors note ALTs and WDTs are essentially identical lesions, while myxoid and round cell liposarcoma represent a morphological spectrum of a single entity. They conclude that a thorough understanding of the morphology of lipomatous tumors aids in establishing a foundation for understanding treatment paradigms.

Figure A represents myxoid liposarcoma, as demonstrated by the presence of lipoblasts in a myxoid stroma matrix.
Figure B represents de-differentiated liposarcoma, which is often a result of transformation from ALTs/WDL, and is classically characterized by having both well- and de-differentiated components.
Figure C shows undifferentiated pleomorphic sarcoma, which is characterized by pleomorphic spindle-shaped and epithelioid cells in the absence of any identifiable line of differentiation.
Figure D represents pleomorphic liposarcoma, which is considered a high-grade tumor with lipoblasts and atypical nuclei.
Figure E illustrates an atypical lipomatous tumor as evidenced by fatty stroma with atypical lipoblasts with minimal cellularity.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: This slide represents myxoid liposarcoma, which is often treated with wide resection and radiotherapy or chemotherapy, as this tumor is more responsive to these therapies than other forms of liposarcoma.
Answer 2: This slide represents de-differentiated liposarcoma, which necessitates wide resection with adjuvant radiotherapy.
Answer 3: This slide represents an undifferentiated pleomorphic sarcoma, which is best treated with wide resection and adjuvant radiotherapy.
Answer 4: This slide represents pleomorphic liposarcoma, which requires wide resection with adjuvant radiotherapy.
