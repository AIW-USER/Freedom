## Question

A 54-year-old male is involved in a motor vehicle collision. He sustains an open tibia fracture with a large bone void. The decision is ultimately made to proceed with bone grafting using femoral autograft obtained with reamer-irrigator-aspirator. Which of the following is true regarding the capacities of this choice of bone grafting?

## Options

1. Osteogenic only
2. Osteoinductive and osteoconductive only
3. Osteoconductive and osteogenic only
4. Osteoinductive and osteogenic only
5. Osteogenic, osteoconductive and osteoinductive

## Correct option

**5. Osteogenic, osteoconductive and osteoinductive**

## Answer explanation

Autograft is osteogenic, osteoinductive, and osteoconductive capacities.

Bone graft is a material with either osteoconductive, osteoinductive, and/or osteogenic properties. Osteoconductive material acts as a structural framework for bone growth (ie. demineralized bone matrices (DBMs)). Osteoinductive material contains factors that stimulate bone growth and induction of stem cells down a bone-forming lineage (ie. bone morphogenetic protein (BMP)). Osteogenic material directly provides cells that will produce bone including primitive mesenchymal stem cells, osteoblasts, and osteocytes. Bone graft is used routinely to assist in healing of fractures, delayed unions, or nonunions, arthrodeses, spinal fusions, and/or to replace bone defects from trauma or tumor.

*Wenisch et al. reviewed the biological characteristics of human reaming debris (HRD) generated in the course of surgical treatment of long bone diaphyseal fractures and nonunions.* **They reported** that cells started to grow out from small bone fragments and established a confluent monolayer within 20-22 days. **They concluded** that cells differentiate along the osteogenic pathway after induction and have prompted interest in the use of reaming debris-derived stem cells in cell and bone replacement therapies.

*Schmidmaier et al. reviewed large bony defects and non-unions treated with bone graft material produced during intramedullary reaming.* **They reported** that in comparing platelet-poor plasma (PPP), platelet-rich plasma (PRP) and reaming irrigation, the bony reaming debris is a rich source of growth factors with a content comparable to that from iliac crest. **They concluded** that the irrigation fluid from the reaming also contains growth factors.

*Dawson et al. prospectively compared patient outcomes after Reamer-Irrigator-Aspirator (RIA)-harvested bone grafting with the current gold standard, either anterior or posterior iliac crest bone graft (ICBG).* **They reported** that when compared with autograft obtained from the iliac crest, autograft harvested using the RIA technique achieves similar union rates with significantly less donor-site pain. **They concluded** that RIA also yields a greater volume of graft compared with anterior ICBG and has a shorter harvest time compared with posterior ICBG.
