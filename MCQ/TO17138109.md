Chapter: Nerve Injury MCQs — Miscellaneous Compression Neuropathies

## Question

A patient presented to OPD with complaints of posterior shoulder pain. On examination pain aggravates in external rotation in abduction. His MRI shows a paralabral cyst in quadrilateral space. Which examination finding will likely be present in this case? ( FNB Pattern Paper 2015)

## Options

1. Deltoid atrophy
2. Supraspinatus atrophy
3. Positive apprehension test
4. Positive dynamic labral shear test

## Correct option

**1. Deltoid atrophy**

## Answer explanation

The quadrangular space (or quadrilateral space) is located in the posterior compartment of the arm, along with the lateral triangular space and medial triangular space.

Boundaries

Medial: lateral border of the long head of the triceps brachii muscle

Lateral: medial cortex of the surgical neck of humerus

Superior: inferior border of the teres minor muscle

Inferior: superior border of the teres major muscle

Contents

Axillary nerve

Posterior circumflex humeral artery and vein

Mechanism of injury: Compression of quadrangular space due to tight fibrous bands, muscular hypertrophy, paralabral cysts (most commonly inferior labral tears), trauma (scapular fracture, shoulder dislocation), benign or malignant masses may cause axillary nerve palsy. In athletes maximum compression of this space occurs in throwing position (late cocking phase, abduction and external rotation)

Clinical features: Pain at the posterolateral aspect of the shoulder. Pain worsens in the night and during throwing activities. There may be atrophy of the deltoid and teres minor and paresthesia along the lateral aspect of the upper arm. Pain increases in resisted external rotation in abduction.

Treatment: Mostly conservative with NSAIDS and activity modification. Surgical decompression is required in recalcitrant cases.

Answer: 1
