## Question

A 72-year-old female had a prior left total hip arthroplasty (THA) 1 year ago and in the time since has had 3 dislocations. On examination, she is noted to have a trendelenburg gait. Her left leg is slightly longer than her right on exam. Given these findings, you schedule her for revision THA. Which of the following implant changes would be helpful in improving stability without further increasing her leg length?

## Options

1. Utilizing femoral neck skirt
2. Increase in femoral neck length
3. Use of more varus femoral stem
4. Use of more valgus femoral stem
5. Medialization of acetabular cup

## Correct option

**3. Use of more varus femoral stem**

## Answer explanation

This patient likely did not have enough offset restored at the time of her primary total hip arthroplasty (THA), and therefore using a high-offset, more varus stem at the time of revision would help increase stability without increasing the leg length.

THA is a common and generally reliable procedure for alleviating pain and increasing function in patients with hip arthritis. However, instability can a significant problem in particular cases and may need to be addressed with revision of the THA prosthesis. Important variables to assess before revision include component design, component positioning, soft tissue tensioning and soft tissue function. Some of these variables are intertwined with regards to the type of implant used. For example, increasing the femoral neck length increases leg length and femoral offset, while medialization of the acetabulum primarily decreases offset. Using a stem with a lower neck-shaft angle (more varus), can increase the degree of offset with minimal effect on leg-length.

*Sculco et al. published a review on avoiding hip instability and leg length issues after THA.* **They note** that patient risk factors, as well as clinical exam findings such as pelvic obliquity help to quantify risk before surgery. Further, **they discuss** that intraoperative changes such as larger femoral head sizes, elevated liners, high offset stems and dual mobility devices can be used to prevent instability.

*Rath et al. reviewed the causes for instability after THA,* **noting** implant malpositioning, impingement, inadequate offset and gluteal insufficiency as reasons. They discuss how failure to restore offset leads to instability, pain and dislocation. They also discuss how, along these lines, abductor insufficiency can contribute to gait abnormalities, pain and instability.

*Brooks reviewed causes and solutions for dislocation following THA.* *He discussed patient risk factors for dislocation including age >70, medical comorbidities, female gender, ligamentous laxity, revision surgery, abductor insufficiency, and patient education.* With regards to operative intervention, **he noted** that preoperative identification of patients with high offset was crucial in order to restore soft tissue tension and stability of the THA.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The use of a femoral neck skirt does not affect leg length or offset but would decrease the femoral head-neck ratio, decreasing the stability of the THA.
Answer 2: Increasing the femoral neck length would increase both offset and leg length.
Answer 4: The use of a more valgus stem would decrease offset.
Answer 5: Further medialization of the acetabular component due to aggressive acetabular reaming decreases the offset of the THA.
