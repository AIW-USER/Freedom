## Question

True about Buford complex is all but( arthroscopy- shoulder arthroscopy- instability)

## Options

1. Thickened Medial glenohumeral ligament
2. Absent labrum
3. Associated with recurrent shoulder dislocation
4. Normal variant of labrum

## Correct option

**3. Associated with recurrent shoulder dislocation**

## Answer explanation

Know all the labral variants:

- Sub labral foramen: A sublabral foramen or sublabral hole is an unattached anterosuperior labrum at the 1-3 o'clock position. It is seen in 11% of individuals.
- Sub labral recess: A sublabral recess is located at the 12 o'clock position (site of attachment of the biceps tendon) and does not extend distally to the 1-3 o'clock position where the other normal variants are located. It may be confused with SLAP tears.
- Buford complex: It is characterized by absence of labrum in the 1-3 o'clock position and the middle glenohumeral ligament is usually thickened. It is present in approximately 1.5% of individuals.
