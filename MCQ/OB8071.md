## Question

A 45-year-old woman with type 2 diabetes (BMI 38, Hgb A1c 7.4) has a grade II ulcer under the first metatarsal head. Previous treatment with a custom orthosis and total contact casting has provided only temporary healing. Her ankle-brachial index is 0.95, she has no foot deformity, and there is no evidence of infection. What is the next most appropriate step in management?

## Options

1. Custom-molded Plastizote orthotics
2. Gastrocsoleus recession and peroneus longus to brevis tendon transfer
3. Resection of the first metatarsal head
4. First-ray amputation
5. Transmetatarsal amputation

## Correct option

**2. Gastrocsoleus recession and peroneus longus to brevis tendon transfer**

## Answer explanation

Ulceration in neuropathy is a consequence of increased pressure. When it occurs in the forefoot, it is frequently associated with a tight heel cord. In addition, lesions under the first metatarsal often have an associated overpull of the peronus longus, plantar flexing the first metatarsal. Before bony resections are contemplated, a fractional lengthening of the heel cord (gastrocsoleus recession) as well as a peroneal longus to brevis tendon transfer will decrease the forefoot pressures and leads to decreased ulcer recurrence rates. Orthotics alone will not be successful because they would have been used after the initial trial of total contact casting.
