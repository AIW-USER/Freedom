## Question

Figure A shows a 4-year-old girl with bilateral tibia vara. She has been treated with valgus positioning knee-ankle-foot orthosis (KAFO) bracing for 12 months. Radiographs of the left leg show worsening varus, medial metaphyseal beaking and depression without bar formation. The metaphyseal-diaphyseal angle measures 25 degrees. What would be the most appropriate next step for the left leg?

![[q5279-a-blounts-disease1.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Discontinue bracing and observation only
2. Continue bracing
3. Convert to hip knee ankle foot orthosis
4. Proximal tibia/fibula osteotomy
5. Proximal tibial and fibular hemiepiphysiodesis

## Correct option

**4. Proximal tibia/fibula osteotomy**

## Answer explanation

This patient has infantile Blount's disease. Operative treatment with proximal tibia/fibula osteotomy would be considered in this patient as she has progression of varus and medial depression.

Patients between the ages of 0-3 with persistent, progressive and/or severe genu varum are considered to have Infantile Blount's Disease. This is a progressive pathologic knee malaignment that is almost exclusively centered at the tibia (unlike adolescent Blount's). Severity of disease is measured via the mechanical axis and evaluation of the joint line.

*Sabharwal et al. reviewed Blount's disease.* **They state** that the knee deformity is more multiplanar with tibial varus, procurvatum, and internal torsion along with limb shortening. When a patient has early-onset disease (e.g. Infantile Blount's), a realignment tibial osteotomy before the age of four years decreases the risk of recurrent deformity.

*Loder et al. also reviewed Infantile Blount's disease.* **They state** that metaphyseal-diaphyseal angles >16° are considered abnormal and have a 95% chance of progression. Whereas metaphyseal-diaphyseal angles <10° are more consistent with physiologic bowing and have a 95% chance of natural resolution of the bowing.

Figure A shows genu varus of the left knee. Illustration A shows (1) tibiofemoral angle (left), which is a radiographic angle measured between the longitudinal axis along the tibia and femur; and (2) metaphyseal-diaphyseal angle (right), which is an angle bisected between a line drawn perpendicular to the longitudinal axis of the tibia and a line draw horizontal through the two beaks of the tibia metaphysis. Illustration B shows radiographic changes in the infantile form of Blount's disease described by Langenskiöld.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1-3: This patient has Langenskiöld stage I-II disease that has been resistant to bracing and a metaphyseal-diaphyseal angles >20°. Severe angles >16° are considered abnormal and have a 95% chance of progression. Children who are too old for bracing, morbidly obese, and/or have advanced Blount's disease are candidates for proximal tibial osteotomy.
Answer 5: Proximal tibial and fibular hemiepiphysiodesis is another operative treatment option for this condition. Most commonly, this is used in adolescent Blount's disease. If selected for this patient, it would be bilateral.
