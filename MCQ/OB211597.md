## Question

A 65-year-old patient develops worsening right deltoid weakness after undergoing a C4-7 posterior laminectomy and fusion for cervical spondylomyelopathy. Which factor would suggest a prolonged recovery from this condition?

## Options

1. Single-level C5 involvement
2. Diabetes diagnosis
3. Duration of myelopathy symptoms
4. Motor grade </= 2
5. Isolated motor deficits without sensory changes

## Correct option

**4. Motor grade </= 2**

## Answer explanation

The patient is presenting with postoperative C5 palsy following posterior cervical laminectomy and fusion. Prolonged recovery has been associated with motor grades ≤2.

Postoperative C5 palsy is a potential complication of cervical decompression with rates estimated to be 4.6% with higher incidences reported in males. Currently, the mechanism of injury is not fully elucidated but speculation surrounds increased posterior cord migration causing tension on the C5 nerve root, ischemic damage, and direct injury to the nerve roots. The prognosis for recovery is good, with a a majority of cases experiencing a full recovery by 2 years post-op. Prolonged recovery (symptoms persistent beyond 6 months) has been correlated to multilevel paresis, motor grade ≤2, and associated sensory changes.

*Fehlings et al. performed a large multicenter prospective study looking at outcomes of spinal cord decompression in patients with myelopathy.* **They reported** there was significant improvement across all patient-reported outcome measures at twelve-months postop with on the mJOA score having a difference in functional improvement from mild, moderate, and severe myelopathy patients. **The authors concluded** that surgical treatment of cervical myelopathy directly improves patient functional outcomes.

*Lim et al. performed a retrospective study on postoperative C5 palsy 710 patients undergoing surgical treatment for cervical myelopathy.* **They found** that C5 palsy occurred in 5.1% of cases with risk factors being greater than 3 surgical levels. Further, **they reported** that prolonged recovery occurred in patients with a C5 motor grade less than or equal to 2, multilevel paresis, and sensory involvement with intractable pain. **The authors concluded** that patients with C5 palsy will tend to recover within 6 months from the onset of symptoms, but the findings of this study may prognosticate patients who may require a prolonged period of recovery.

*Tetreault et al. performed a large multicenter international study of 614 patients undergoing surgical treatment for cervical myelopathy to determine preoperative factors to predict patient outcomes.* The multivariate analysis revealed 6 factors that correlated with mJOA score ≥16 which included baseline mJOA score, impaired gait, age, comorbidity score, smoking status, and duration of symptoms. **The authors concluded** that their prognostic model is not designed to determine which patients would benefit the most from surgery but to guide presurgical expectations during the consent process.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: Multilevel paresis rather than single-level involvement is a prognostic sign for a prolonged recovery
Answer 2: Diabetes does not appear to affect the recovery time of postoperative C5 palsy.
Answer 3: The duration of myelopathy symptoms does not appear to impact the recovery time of postoperative C5 palsy.
Answer 5: Motor deficits that are accompanied by sensory changes and intractable pain are prognostic signs for a prolonged recovery.
