## Question

A 25-year-old ballerina complains of right forefoot pain of insidious onset 2 months after starting practice for a major performance. ## Options

1. Biopsy
2. Observation
3. Protected weight-bearing
4. MRI
5. Bone scan

## Correct option

**3. Protected weight-bearing**

## Answer explanation

This patient has right second metatarsal shaft stress fracture. These fractures will heal if the fracture site is stabilized. Protected weight-bearing will provide stability and minimize pain.

Stress fractures arise when there is a disruption of the balance between accumulation of microdamage and bone repair. Stress fractures occur in watershed areas where there is poor blood supply and areas of high stress. Pain is typically insidious (2-3 weeks) and may correlate with a change in training habits. Radiographs typically lag behind clinical symptoms by weeks. Repeat radiographs 2 weeks later may reveal the fracture.

*Shindle et al. reviewed stress fractures about the tibia, foot and ankle.* **They recommend** elucidating a history including endocrinopathies, autoimmune and eating disorders, depression, malabsorption, bariatric surgery, and dietary history. Management depends on the fracture location. High-risk fractures (anterior tibia, navicular, proximal fifth metatarsal, medial malleolus) may require surgery in high level athletes who need to return to sport early.

*Welck et al. reviewed stress fractures of the foot.* **They state** that of metatarsal fractures, the 2nd and 3rd metatarsals are most commonly involved. Fractures of the 2nd metatarsal are often seen in ballet dancers because of extreme plantar flexion. Other risks include prominent 2nd metatarsal, pronated foot and poor ankle plantar flexion with compensation at the metatarso-cuneiform joint.

Figure A is a normal oblique radiograph of the right foot. Figure B is the same foot 2 weeks later. There is marked mineralized callus formation indicative of a healing stress fracture.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Biopsy is not necessary. There are no red flags to indicate a malignant process. With a history of repetitive loading, stress fracture is a likely diagnosis.
Answer 2: Observation alone (without treatment) will not provide pain relief and will not provide a sufficiently stable environment to facilitate bony union.
Answer 3: MRI is useful if diagnosis is urgent, or for diagnosis of radiographically occult stress fractures. Fluid-sequences are sensitive for endosteal marrow edema and periosteal edema (the earliest features of stress fractures). In this case, the diagnosis is already clear on plain radiographs.
Answer 4: Bone scan is indicated if there are no clear radiographic findings. Current radiographs confirm the diagnosis. Bone scan is unnecessary. Bone scan is useful if diagnosis is urgent, and for identifying potential areas of noncontiguous, simultaneous fractures (eg, second metatarsal and tibia midshaft).
