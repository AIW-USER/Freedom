## Question

A 55-year-old male has a full thickness rotator cuff tear involving the supraspinatus and infraspinatus with retraction to the level of the glenoid and Goutallier Grade 1 muscle atrophy. You schedule him for arthroscopic rotator cuff repair in the beach chair position. Which peripheral lower extremity nerve palsy is most likely to occur?

## Options

1. Femoral nerve
2. Lateral femoral cutaneous nerve
3. Obturator nerve
4. Pudendal nerve
5. Sciatic nerve

## Correct option

**2. Lateral femoral cutaneous nerve**

## Answer explanation

Lateral femoral cutaneous nerve (LFCN) palsy is a documented complication after shoulder arthroscopy in the beach chair position.

Beach chair positioning is ideal for shoulder arthroscopy procedures including rotator cuff repairs, but does not come without risk. Traditionally, operating room staff has been attentive to padding areas of prominence to reduce neuropraxias to the peripheral nerves such as the peroneal nerve. However, it has been increasingly recognized that nerve injury to the LFCN occurs in this position in up to 2% of cases. Typically the neuropraxia resolves within 6 months, but this risk may be minimized with adequate padding, appropriate seatbelt positioning and slight reverse Trendelenburg during the operation. It is also thought that body mass index (BMI) >35 is a risk factor for LFCN palsy.

*Bowley et al. reviewed entrapment neuropathies of the lower extremities,* **noting** that accuracy in identification and management is vital. The authors review patterns of pain, sensory loss, coordination changes and muscle weakness. **They also note** common patterns of particular peripheral nerve entrapment syndromes.

*Levy et al. evaluated ways to reduce LFCN palsy in obese patients in the beach chair position.* After a certain date, they started placing foam padding over the patient's thighs with an extra wide safety strap underneath their abdominal pannus for protection. They noticed a significant decrease in the amount of LFCN palsy cases, despite the fact that all cases resolved within 6 months regardless.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Femoral nerve is not thought to be easily injured in beach chair positioning.
Answer 3: Obturator nerve injury is not thought to occur in the beach chair position.
Answer 4: The pudendal nerve is not thought to be easily injured during beach chair positioning. However, it can be injured with a perineal post on a traction table or during hip arthroscopy.
Answer 5: Sciatic nerve is at risk during many procedures surrounding the hip but not thought to be easily disturbed by beach chair positioning.
