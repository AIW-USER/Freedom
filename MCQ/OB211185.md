## Question

A 30 year old patient is referred to you complaining of 5 months of activity-related hip pain. It has not improved after a brief period of rest followed by 2 months of physical therapy. Her primary physician ordered an MRI which shows an anterolateral labral tear. Which of the following pre-surgical factors is a general contraindication for hip arthroscopy?

## Options

1. Alpha angle of 60 degrees
2. Femoral head-neck offset ratio of 0.2
3. Tobacco use
4. Tönnis angle of 5 degrees
5. Tönnis grade 3

## Correct option

**5. Tönnis grade 3**

## Answer explanation

Arthritic changes graded as Tönnis 2 or higher are contraindications for hip arthroscopy.

Hip arthroscopy is an increasingly common procedure in treating both intra- and extra-articular hip pathology. Early adoption of this often led to variable outcomes. Subsequent analysis of these have delineated clear and relative contraindications. Arthritic hips showing Tönnis grade 2 and 3 on radiographs, portend a worse prognosis and have shown higher rates of failure and conversion to hip arthroplasty.

*Bogunovic et al. reviewed their series of patients who failed primary hip arthroscopy.* Of those who required a secondary procedure following arthroscopy, 37% had underlying osteoarthritis, 43% had under-treated FAI, and 15% had acetabular dysplasia. All of these findings are visible on imaging studies and highlights the need for diligent assessment of pre-operative imaging studies.

*Clohisy et al. outline a systemic method for radiographic assessment of the hip.* *They describe proper imaging techniques in order to create quality images necessary to allow accurate and reproducible measurements.* *They add that these images should always be interpreted in the context of patient symptoms and physical exam findings.*

Illustration A demonstrates the Tönnis classification grades 0 through 3, respectively. Illustration B demonstrates how to measure the alpha angle while Illustration C demonstrates measurement of the femoral head-neck offset ratio. Illustration D shows the Tönnis angle measurement.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1- Alpha angles over 42 degrees denote a femoral-based Cam deformity which is not a contraindication for hip arthroscopy.
Answer 2- A femoral head-neck offset ratio under 0.17 would denote femoral-based Cam deformity, which is not a contra-indication for hip arthroscopy.
Answer 3- Smoking has an increased risk of poor outcomes but is not a contraindication.
Answer 4- The Tönnis roof angle assesses the acetabular inclination, with 0-10 degrees within the normal range. Excessive angles would denote dysplasia.
