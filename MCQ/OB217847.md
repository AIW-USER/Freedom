## Question

A 42-year-old male presents to the emergency room after falling from a roof and sustaining an injury to his elbow. You note obvious deformity to the elbow and radiographs demonstrate a simple posterolateral elbow dislocation. You perform a reduction under sedation and note the elbow to be unstable from 0-60º of extension. Given his residual instability, you discuss repair of the most likely torn ligament. Which of the following is true about this patient's injury & subsequent surgery?

## Options

1. Ligament is most commonly ruptured off ulnar attachment
2. Should avoid shoulder abduction post-operatively
3. Should avoid position of valgus stress & pronation post-operatively
4. Rates of recurrent instability are >10% after repair
5. Usually occurs with coronoid anteromedial facet fractures

## Correct option

**2. Should avoid shoulder abduction post-operatively**

## Answer explanation

After lateral ulnar collateral ligament (LUCL) repair or reconstruction, it is important patients avoid shoulder abduction, because gravity produces a varus stress that can disrupt the surgical outcome.

Posterolateral rotatory instability of the elbow is commonly caused by trauma and can be simple (without fracture), or complex (associated with fracture). In most cases of posterolateral elbow dislocations, the LUCL is disrupted, typically rupturing off its humeral attachment. If the elbow remains unstable after reduction, LUCL repair or reconstruction may be necessary to restore innate stability to the elbow. The forearm is typically most stable in pronation and activities involving supination and varus stress should be avoided immediately after surgery.

*Karbach et al. reviewed the anatomy, biomechanics, and physical exam findings of elbow instability,* **noting** a variety of bony and ligamentous stabilizers which contribute to elbow stability. There are several examination maneuvers that are helpful in identifying injuries to the lateral ligament complex, including the lateral pivot shift test, posterolateral drawer test, and the chair push-up test. **They note** that with injuries to the LUCL (primary lateral stabilizer), the elbow is typically most unstable in supination.

*Anakwenze et al. reviewed surgical management of posterolateral rotatory elbow instability in 130 patients.* *They performed LUCL reconstruction on all the patients in this cohort and 91% had good or excellent results.* Recurrent instability was noted to be 8% in this study.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The LUCL is most commonly ruptured off its humeral origin just anterior and distal to the lateral epicondyle.
Answer 3: After surgical repair of the LUCL, patients should primarily avoid varus stress to the elbow, as it may jeopardize the repair.
Answer 4: Rates of recurrent instability are less than 10% after repair of the LUCL.
Answer 5: While injuries to the LCL are frequent, anteromedial facet fractures of the coronoid occur in cases of posteromedial elbow instability.
