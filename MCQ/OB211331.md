## Options

1. Between the superficial MCL and medial head of the gastrocnemius
2. Between the sartorius and soleus
3. Between the gracilis and semitendinosus
4. Between the posterior oblique ligament and soleus
5. Between the pes anserinus and medial head of the gastrocnemius

## Correct option

**5. Between the pes anserinus and medial head of the gastrocnemius**

## Answer explanation

This patient has sustained a high-energy bicondylar tibial plateau fracture, with a large posteromedial displaced fragment. The correct interval for the definitive reduction and fixation of this posteromedial fragment is between the pes anserinus and the medial head of the gastrocnemius.

For optimal definitive reduction and fixation of bicondylar tibial plateau fractures, a dual-incision approach is often utilized. For fractures that require posterior or posteromedial fixation, the correct interval is between the pes anserine and the medial head of the gastrocnemius. The posteromedial sheared fracture piece is often difficult and sometimes impossible to achieve appropriate stable fixation with a single lateral locking plate, as there will be limited screw purchase and fixation into the posteromedial fragment.

*Bhattacharyya et al. investigated the fracture patterns and early results of posterior shearing tibial plateau fractures.* **They reported** that all fractures healed after the index surgery, with minimal complications (1 wound dehiscence and 1 flexion contracture) which were managed nonoperatively. **They concluded** that with the posterior approach, the quality of articular reduction is a primary factor that influences short-term functional outcomes.

*He et al. presented a case series of patients with posterior bicondylar tibial plateau fractures treated by direct exposure and buttress plate fixation through a posterior inverted L-shaped approach.* **They reported** satisfactory reduction in all cases except one, and average radiographic bony union time and full weight-bearing time of 11.5 and 13.8 weeks, respectively. **They concluded** that this approach is a safe and effective way for the treatment of posterior bicondylar tibial plateau fractures.

*Carlson et al. evaluated patients with posterior bicondylar tibial plateau fractures treated by direct fracture exposure and fixation through dual incisions.* **They reported** that all patients returned to near full activities with range of motion averaging 2-121 degrees. **They concluded** that the dual-incision approach to these challenging fractures can result in good to excellent knee function for these patients.

Figures A-E demonstrate a displaced bicondylar tibial plateau fracture with a large posteromedial plateau fragment.
