## Question

As part of the diagnostic work-up of the lesion shown in Figure A, you are asked to perform a surgical biopsy. What would be the best approach?

![[q5836-a-1408455491.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Direct anterior approach within rectus femoris muscle
2. Anteromedial approach between the interval of rectus femoris and vastus medialis muscle
3. Posterolateral approach between the interval of vastus lateralis and bicep femoris muscle
4. Posteromedial approach within vastus medialis muscle
5. Direct posterior approach medial to the neurovascular bundle

## Correct option

**4. Posteromedial approach within vastus medialis muscle**

## Answer explanation

Figure A shows a distal femur osteosarcoma. The safest approach to the distal femur for biopsy in this patient is through the posteromedial portion of vastus medials. The alternative would be the posterolateral portion of vastus lateralis. The ideal modality would be a needle biopsy of the soft-tissue or bone component of the lesion.

The goal of biopsy is to obtain a representative sample of tissue that will allow for diagnosis without jeopardizing the chance of subsequent limb salvage. The principles of biopsy include discussion of the case and plan with a tumour surgeon and a pathologist. The biopsy should be planned in such a way that it only includes areas that will be removed as part of future limb salvage. It is important to avoid developing planes and to make try to work within a single muscle belly. Neurovascular structures should not be exposed. Use an oval bone window if one is required. Send a sample to the pathologist and a sample for tissue culture.

*Damron et al. reviewed the specific approaches for anatomical biopsy.* The best approaches include:
o Wrist = dorsal 2nd compartment
o Distal Humerus = lateral triceps, brachialis muscle
o Proximal Humerus = anterior deltoid muscle
o Iliac = thru superior abductor muscles
o Supracetabular = Smith-Peterson approach through TFL and rectus femoris muscle
o Femoral Neck and Head = transtrochantlric osseous approach
o Distal Femur = posteromedial or posterolateral border of vastus medialis/lateralis muscle
o Tibia = subcutaneous border of tibia

Figure A is an AP radiograph of the distal femur showing a non-geographic, expansile and penetrative lesion with an associated soft-tissue component. Illustration A shows the best approach for biopsy of the distal femur.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1,2: The anterior approach to the femur will contaminate the extensor mechanism, which is important for gait mechanics and knee function. The principle of biopsy is to traverse within a single compartment or muscle.
Answer 3: This approach would contaminate both the anterior and posterior compartments. The principles of biopsy advocate that entry be at the edge of a single compartment - not between them.
Answer 5: The posterior approach would contaminate the posterior neurovascular structures.
