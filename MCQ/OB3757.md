## Question

A 47-year-old woman presents to the emergency room with acute back pain of 3 days duration. She states that she has been having difficulty walking and describes lower extremity weakness. On physical exam, she has 3/5 strength in her hip flexors, and knee extensors and flexors. She has 2/5 right ankle dorsiflexion and plantarflexion and 1/5 left ankle dorsiflexion and plantar flexion. Patellar reflexes are 3+ bilaterally, and there is clonus with ankle dorsiflexion. She has a normal upper extremity exam. Figures A and B demonstrate the results of her spine MRI. Which surgical approach for thoracic disc removal is associated with the greatest neurologic morbidity?

## Options

1. Lateral extracavitary
2. Lateral costotransversectomy
3. Posterior laminectomy
4. Anterior transthoracic
5. Video assisted thorascopy

## Correct option

**3. Posterior laminectomy**

## Answer explanation

The patient presents with abnormal neurologic symptoms and upper motor neuron signs in the lower extremities with a normal upper extremity examination due to a thoracic disc herniation. Posterior laminectomy and discectomy is associated with significant neurologic morbidity when used to treat this injury.

For patients with thoracic disc herniation and myelopathy, surgery is generally done through either an anterior, lateral or posterior approach. The anterior approach can be either transsternal or transthoracic and provides exposure from T4 to T12. The lateral approach can be either extra-cavitary or with a costotransversectomy. The advantage of this approach is that there is no violation of the pleura (thus no postoperative chest tube is needed) and that T12-L1 surgery does not require the takedown of the diaphragm. The posterior pediculofacetectomy or transpedicular approach has been described but may not provide optimal visualization.

*Vanichkachorn et al. thoroughly describe the diagnosis and treatment of thoracic disc disease.* **The authors report** that the posterior approach with laminectomy and discectomy is associated with a 28% rate of neurologic worsening. **The authors conclude** that permanent paraparesis or worsening neurologic function is the most commonly reported major perioperative complication.

*Stillerman et. al. review the clinical outcomes of 71 patients with 82 herniated thoracic discs.* **Their study found** that most thoracic disc herniations are centerolateral, and that pain, spasticity and sensory changes were all likely to improve (>84%). Motor impairment improved in only 58% of treated patients. **They concluded** that anterior and lateral approaches are indicated for densely and partially calcified centrolateral discs.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Lateral extracavitary approach is associated with significant bone resection and potential blood loss but decreased risk of neurologic deficit compared to the posterior approach.
Answer 2: Lateral costotransversectomy is associated with difficulty in the removal of calcified disks or large posterior osteophytes but decreased risk of neurologic deficit compared to the posterior approach.
Answer 4: Anterior transthoracic approach is not associated with greater neurologic morbidity compared to posterior laminectomy. It does require diaphragmatic takedown at T12-L1.
Answer 5: Video-assisted thoracoscopy is associated with intercostal neuralgia and visceral injury from trocar placement.
