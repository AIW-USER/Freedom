## Question

A 55-year-old patient presented to the arthroplasty clinic with progressive left-sided hip pain. He previously underwent left hip replacement surgery approximately five years prior. Based on the radiographs shown in figures A-C, which of the following is the most appropriate surgical option?

## Options

1. Triflange acetabular reconstruction
2. Cementing a new liner into the current cup
3. Converting to a constrained liner
4. Impaction grafting
5. Structural allograft reconstruction

## Correct option

**1. Triflange acetabular reconstruction**

## Answer explanation

The radiographs demonstrate proximal and posterior migration of the left acetabular component, creating a pelvic discontinuity. Of the options provided, a triflange acetabular reconstruction is the best surgical option (Answer 1).

Acetabular bone loss is a common and challenging issue in revision total hip arthroplasty (THA), often resulting from implant wear, osteolysis, or infection. Proper classification of bone defects is critical for selecting the appropriate reconstruction strategy. Options for managing acetabular bone loss include using structural grafts, porous metal augments, or highly porous acetabular components to enhance biological fixation. In cases of severe bone loss, custom triflange components or cage constructs may be required to restore the hip center position and achieve durable fixation. Addressing acetabular bone loss effectively is key to ensuring implant stability, optimizing patient function, and reducing the risk of re-revision.

*Sheth et al. review the evaluation and management of Acetabular bone loss in revision total hip arthroplasty.* Patient evaluation begins with a thorough history with emphasis on prior surgery and infection. Next, three-dimensional imaging is often employed to better characterize bony deficits and operative planning. The authors then review several options for reconstruction based on the degree of bone loss. These techniques included impaction grafting, bulk structural allograft, metal augmentation, cup-cage combinations, and triflange reconstruction. **The authors emphasize** that successful acetabular revision surgery requires thoughtful evaluation, meticulous preoperative planning, and skillful execution.

*Deirmengian et al. also reviewed the management of acetabular bone loss in revision hip arthroplasty.* **The authors advocate** that most acetabular revisions can be managed with a hemispherical component with screw fixation. In the setting of segmental bone loss, structural allograft or second-generation porous metal augments are often employed. Finally, **the authors recommend** acetabular cages in the management of massive bone loss and pelvic discontinuity but warn that otherwise, they have limited application.

Figures A-C are the AP and Judet views demonstrating posterior superior migration of the acetabular component, creating a divide between the superior and inferior aspects of the hemipelvis (pelvic discontinuity). Illustration A is the immediate post-operative radiograph showing the triflange acetabular component in place.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 2-3: The patient has a pelvic discontinuity with significant component migration and failure. This requires complete revision, including stabilization of the superior and inferior hemipelvis.
Answer 4: Acetabular impaction grafting restores bone stock with tightly impacted, well-contained cancellous graft and a cemented polyethylene cup. This technique is suitable for simple cavitary defects but not in the setting of pelvic discontinuity.
Answer 5: Bulk structural allograft is an option in cases with inadequate host acetabular bone for component fixation and stability but not in pelvic discontinuity.
