## Question

A 79-year-old female sustains a ground level fall and presents to the emergency department with a contusion on her forehead. Physical exam shows bilateral upper extremity weakness (right greater than left), patchy numbness in bilateral upper extremities, and urinary retention. Her lower extremities show normal muscles strength, and she demonstrates the ability to walk. An MRI is obtained and shown in Figure A. Which of the following best explains the pathophysiology of her neurologic injury?

![[q3054-a-ccssag1.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Vascular injury to the anterior spinal artery
2. Vascular injury to the posterior spinal artery
3. Selective injury to the central component of lateral corticospinal tract
4. Selective injury to midline component of the posterior columns
5. Selective injury to peripheral component of the ventral spinothalamic tract

## Correct option

**3. Selective injury to the central component of lateral corticospinal tract**

## Answer explanation

The clinical presentation is consistent with a central cord syndrome. The pathophysiology of central cord syndrome is believed to involve selective injury to the central component of lateral corticospinal tract.

Central cord syndrome commonly occurs in individuals with cervical stenosis who sustain a hyperextension injury to the cervical spine. The spinal cord becomes "pinched" between anterior osteophytes and posterior hypertrophic ligamentum flavum. This is believed to cause central cord edema with selective destruction of lateral corticospinal tract white matter. Based on the topographic anatomy of the spinal cord this causes upper extremity weakness with relative sparing of lower extremity motor function. Patchy changes in sensation are also expected.

*Harrop et al. performed a comprehensive literature review to evaluate the potential pathophysiology, current treatment options, and management of central cord injuries.* **They found** that medical management and surgical decompression and stabilization provide improved neurologic recovery.

*Epstein et al. evaluated a unique group of 23 patients with cervical spinal stenosis and myelopathy without fracture or dislocation.* **They reported** a positive correlation between pre-existing cervical canal stenosis and degree of neurologic deficit following trauma.

Figure A is a sagittal T2 MRI of the cervical spine demonstrating degenerative changes at C3/4/5 level causing spinal stenosis. Illustration A depicts a cross section of the spinal cord highlighting the areas affected by central cord syndrome.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: This is the pathophysiology of anterior cord syndrome
Answer 3: Central cord syndrome does not primary involve an injury to the posterior spinal artery.
Answer 4: This is the pathophysiology of posterior cord syndrome
Answer 5: Central cord syndrome does not primary involve an injury to the ventral spinothalamic tract.
