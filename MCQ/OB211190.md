## Question

A 69-year-old female undergoes a thoracolumbar fusion for a sagittal plane deformity that has severely impacted her quality of life and ability to perform basic activities of daily living. During the procedure, polymethylmethacrylate is injected to the caudad and cephalad vertebrae in conjunction with pedicle screw fixation. Which of the following most accurately describes the purpose of this technique?

## Options

1. Decreases the number of levels fused
2. Increases fusion rate
3. Decreases the rate of junctional failure
4. Both 1 and 3 are correct
5. Both 2 and 3 are correct

## Correct option

**5. Both 2 and 3 are correct**

## Answer explanation

The use of polymethylmethacrylate (PMMA) augmentation at the end vertebra of pedicle screw constructs has been associated with improved correction maintenance, increased fusion rates, decreased junctional failure, and increased screw pull-out strength.

Osteoporosis is often concomitant in adult spinal deformity patients, making successful corrective surgery challenging. Optimization of bone quality is essential to strong fixation. Medical management remains challenging, but recent studies have shown teriparatide to contribute to increased bone mineral density and fusion rates when utilized in the perioperative and postoperative period. Intraoperative techniques to maximize construct rigidity include the use of long constructs incorporating fusion to the sacrum and pelvis, pedicle screw constructs, at least three points of fixation above and below the deformity, and PMMA augmentation of the end vertebrae. The use of PMMA augmentation has been associated with improved maintenance of deformity correction, increased fusion rates, and increased screw pull-out strength with no added complications.

*Lehman et al. performed a review of osteoporosis management in spine surgery.* Medical management with teriparatide has shown promise as it increases bone mineral density, increased fusion rates, and decreased pedicle screw loosening in osteoporotic patients. Surgically, **the authors recommend** using longer fusion constructs with at least three fixation points about and below the deformity. Additionally, under-tapping pedicle screws increase the insertional torque and bicortical screw purchase enhances pullout strength.

*Ponnusamy et al. performed a review of instrumentation techniques in osteoporotic spines.* **The authors recommended** the use of PMMA augmentation of the end vertebrae with pedicle screw constructs as it has been shown to decrease junctional failure, increase pull-out strength, and improve maintenance of correction. Other recommended techniques include bicortical screw fixation, hydroxyapatite screw coating, double pedicle screws, and expandable screws.

*Sawakami et al. performed a retrospective cohort study of osteoporotic patients undergoing posterior spinal fusion with pedicle screw constructs either with or without cement augmentation.* **The authors reported** a significantly lower lucency rate around the pedicle screws, improved sagittal plane alignment, and increased fusion rate. **They concluded** that the use of PMMA to reinforce pedicle screws is an effective option for osteoporotic spines.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: PMMA augmentation of the end vertebrae of a fusion construct has not been shown to decrease the number of levels included in the fusion. In osteoporotic spines, at least three points of fixation are required above and below the deformity, which is unaffected by cement augmentation.
Answer 2: PMMA augmentation results in higher fusion rates BUT also increases the maintenance of sagittal plane correction by reducing the risk of junctional kyphosis.
Answer 3: PMMA augmentation results in better maintenance of correction BUT also higher fusion rates.
Answer 4: Junctional kyphosis risk is reduced with PMMA augmentation of the end vertebrae, it does not reduce the number of levels fused or instrumented.
