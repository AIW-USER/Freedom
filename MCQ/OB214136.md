## Question

Which of the following would favor the diagnosis of neurogenic claudication over vascular claudication?

## Options

1. Exacerbation of symptoms when walking upright
2. Exacerbation of symptoms when leaning forward
3. Exacerbation of symptoms when leaning backwards
4. Relief of symptoms when sitting
5. Relief of symptoms when standing stationary

## Correct option

**3. Exacerbation of symptoms when leaning backwards**

## Answer explanation

Lumbar extension, as experienced with walking down stairs, exacerbates symptoms in neurogenic claudication.

Differentiating between vascular and neurogenic claudication can be challenging. Both forms of claudication present with pain, achiness and fatigue throughout the buttock and leg. Moreover, both share similar characteristics, such as exacerbation of symptoms while walking upright and improvement in symptoms while sitting. Etiologically, vascular claudication symptoms occur as a failure of the musculature to meet its oxygen requirements, usually secondary to impeded blood flow from peripheral vascular disease. Characteristic symptoms which favor the diagnosis of vascular claudication include relief of symptoms while standing stationary. Conversely, neurogenic claudication is most commonly the result of lumbar spinal stenosis. Neurogenic claudication often presents with improvement by postural changes that flex the lumbar spine (ie. leaning forward, ascending stairs, or riding a stationary bike). This occurs since lumbar flexion increase the cross sectional area of the central canal relieving pressure on the affected area.

*Pearson et al. reviewed surgical and nonoperative outcomes in spinal stenosis patients.* **They report** significant improvement in outcomes in most patients treated with surgery than with nonoperative treatment. **They concluded** that with the exception of smokers, patients who met strict inclusion criteria improved more with surgery than with nonoperative treatment.

*Singh et al. reviewed the symptoms and treatment of lumbar spinal stenosis.* **They report** that non-operative measures such as activity modification, exercise, NSAIDs and epidural injections may be beneficial in most patients. However, **they concluded** that operative intervention should be performed in patients with intractable pain, neurologic compromise, or mechanical instability.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: BOTH vascular and neurogenic claudication symptoms are exacerbated with walking upright
Answer 2: Neurogenic claudication has improvement with lumbar flexion
Answer 4: BOTH vascular and neurogenic claudication symptoms are improved with sitting
Answer 5:Relief of symptoms when standing stationary is expected with vascular claudication.
