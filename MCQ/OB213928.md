## Answer explanation

This patient's clinical history of a painful, blastic, and destructive distal femur mass in the setting of a p53 inactivating mutation most likely represents osteosarcoma. The histological section of osteosarcoma is depicted in Figure C.

The p53 protein is an important tumor suppressor and central mediator of vital responses to stress including DNA damage, oncogene activation, viral infections, or telomere formation. Inactivating mutations of p53 correlate with about half of all sporadic human malignancies. Patients who carry homozygous mutations in the p53 protein gene have a 100% chance of developing some form of malignancy. Osteosarcoma is the most common primary bone tumor associated with p53 gene mutations. Mutations in the retinoblastoma (Rb) gene are also highly associated with the development of osteosarcoma.

*Overholtzer et al. investigated p53 mutations and genomic instability in human osteosarcomas.* **They reported** that the p53 gene is a critical tumor suppressor gene that is inactivated in a majority of cancers (including osteosarcoma), as a result of the disruption of checkpoint responses to DNA damage. **They concluded** that reducing the levels of wild-type p53 protein may contribute to osteosarcoma tumorigenesis.

*Walkley et al. investigated the loss of the p53 protein in mouse osteosarcoma.* **They reported** that osteosarcoma development depends on the loss of p53 and is potentiated by loss of the Rb protein. **They demonstrated** the striking resemblance of mouse osteosarcoma to human osteosarcoma and concluded that the development of osteosarcoma was dominantly correlated with p53 mutation.

Figure A demonstrates a left distal femur osteosarcoma. Figure B depicts a chondroblastoma (chondroblasts arranged in cobblestone pattern). Figure C depicts an osteosarcoma (characteristic lacey osteoid with atypical cells, high nuclear to cytoplasm ratio). Figure D depicts multiple myeloma (round plasma cells with eccentric nuclei). Figure E depicts fibrous dysplasia (characteristic "Chinese letters" appearance with fibroblastic proliferation within islands of woven bone). Figure F depicts osteoid osteoma (uniform immature osteoid woven bone with osteoblastic rimming). Illustration A demonstrates the pathway of p53 gene activation and its target mechanisms.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Figure B is a chondroblastoma, which often presents in the epiphysis and does not have a high association with a p53 mutation.
Answer 3: Figure D demonstrates multiple myeloma, which is not common in this age group and not associated with a p53 mutation.
Answer 4: Figure E demonstrates fibrous dysplasia, which would not present with a blastic and destructive appearance, and is not highly associated with a p53 mutation.
Answer 5: Figure F demonstrates osteoid osteoma, which generally wound present as a nidus (not a destructive blastic lesion) and is not highly associated with a p53 mutation.
