## Question

Which of the following pathologic findings will help determine medical intervention of newly diagnosed breast cancer?

## Options

1. Human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)/neu receptor
2. RANK ligand receptor
3. BRCA gene expression
4. Cancer antigen 125 (CA-125)
5. t11:22 translocation

## Correct option

**1. Human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)/neu receptor**

## Answer explanation

Metastatic disease of the breast requires coordinated treatment by both orthopaedic surgeons and oncologists. HER2/neu receptor positivity is a feature of breast cancers that allows a unique therapeutic target for medical treatment.

Breast tumors may express three significant pathologic markers which guide treatment: HER2, ER, and progesterone receptor (PR). These biomarkers guide treatment in that ER+ tumors may be treated with anti-estrogenic drugs such as tamoxifen or aromatase inhibitors such as letrozole. HER2+ can be targeted with monoclonal antibodies such as trastuzumab.

*Buzdar performed a literature review to identify the value of chemotherapy and HER2-targeted biologic therapies as adjuvants for treating HER2+ breast cancers.* His findings demonstrate that the ER and HER2 pathways interact with each other, ER+/HER2+ tumors demonstrate resistance to hormonally-directed therapies such as tamoxifen, and that dual regimens of aromatase inhibitors and anti-HER2 monoclonal antibodies should be used for ER+/HER2+ tumors.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: The RANK ligand (RANKL) is an essential mediator of osteoclast activity. It is secreted by osteoblasts, and binds to receptors on osteoclast precursors triggering their maturation and subsequent bone resorption. In the setting of neoplasia, PTH can stimulate the RANK receptor, but this is not typically a therapeutic target for breast cancer.
Answer 3: The BRCA1 and 2 genes are tumor suppressor genes which, when mutated, have a high associated risk of breast and ovarian cancer. These genes however are not yet therapeutic targets, however there is evidence to support the role of prophylactic double mastectomy for BRCA1 and BRCA2 mutation carriers. *Kaas et. al. reviewed 147 women treated with bilateral prophylactic mastectomy and* **found** a subsequent breast cancer rate of 0.2%/women-year.
Answer 4: CA-125 is a serum marker expressed by multiple neoplasias including breast cancer and does have diagnostic importance, however it is not a treatment target.
Answer 5: The t11:22 translocation is the mutation responsible for Ewing Sarcoma, not breast cancer.
