## Question

A 45 years old male with unicompartmental arthritis presents with severe pain. His MRI shows an ACL tear with intact lateral meniscus and grade 4 arthritic changes limited to the medial compartment of the knee. On examination he has 10 degrees of varus deformity which is passively correctable. Best treatment for him is

## Options

1. HTO
2. UKA
3. ACI
4. TKA

## Correct option

**1. HTO**

## Answer explanation

Although ACL deficiency and Grade 4 OA will be Contra indications for HTO, however, these are relative contraindications.

ACL can be simultaneously reconstructed during HTO so it won’t be strict hinderance to go with HTO.

Grade 4 OA will decrease outcome but doesn’t mean a strict contraindication either. However, if there is significant bone loss, it’s definitely a strict contraindication.

HTO > UKA here as there is varus (although that’s boderline for UKA). UKA is more preferred in people with varus < 10 degree”

Explanation: Contraindications for unicondylar knee arthroplasty are:

- Inflammatory arthritis
- Fixed deformity: That can’t be corrected passively
- Previous meniscectomy in opposite compartment
- ACL deficiency
- Flexion contracture greater than 10 degrees
- Bicompartmental or Tricompartmental arthritis

Contraindications for HTO are:

- Inflammatory arthritis
- Lack of flexion—minimum of 90 degrees needed
- Flexion contracture over 10 degrees
- Ligament instability (Especially varus thrust gait), but Not contraindicated in cruciate-ligament insufficiency
- Lateral tibial subluxation greater than 1 cm
- Medial compartment bone loss
- Lateral compartment joint narrowing (bicompartmental /tricompartmental arthritis)
- Smoking, age>60 degrees and varus deformity more than 10 degrees are relative contraindications.

In this patient as he has an ACL tear so UKA is contraindicated.

Answers: A
