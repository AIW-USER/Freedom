## Question

A 65-year-old female well known to your clinic with painful bunions recalcitrant to nonoperative management. The decision is made to proceed with a corrective procedure. Pre-operatively you plan to make cut "B" labeled in Figure A for a medial eminence resection combined with a modified McBride procedure, but intraoperatively you make the cut labeled "A". What complication is associated with making cut "A" instead of "B"?

![[q212917-a-screen-shot-2019-04-17-at-8-55-09-am.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Post operative recurrence of hallux valgus
2. Cock up toe deformity
3. 2nd metatarsal transfer metatarsalgia
4. Hallux varus
5. Metatarsal head avascular necrosis

## Correct option

**4. Hallux varus**

## Answer explanation

Excessive resection of the medial eminence, usually due to lateral misplacement of the sagittal saw blade, can result in iatrogenic hallux varus.

Factors implicated in the development of hallux varus after hallux valgus (HV) surgery or bunionectomy include: fibular sesamoid resection or detachment of adductor hallucis, overcorrection of the intermetatarsal angle, excessive removal of the medial eminence, medial tibial sesamoid subluxation, excessive plantar lateral release, excessive postoperative bandaging, osteoarthritis of the 1st metatarsophalangeal (MTP) joint, and malunion of a distal 1st metatarsal osteotomy.

*Devos Bevernage et al. authored a review on hallux varus.* *This article focuses on iatrogenic hallux varus following bunion surgery, but the same principles apply to other causes of acquired hallux varus.* *They discuss the iatrogenic causes of hallux varus discussed in the above paragraph and treatment options once hallux varus has occurred.*

*Richardson et al. also authored a review on the treatment of hallux varus.* *They again discuss some of the iatrogenic causes but focus on the formation of a treatment algorithm of hallux varus.* **They state** that there is a paucity of quality literature on treatment and recommend an individualized approach that focuses on joint flexibility, joint integrity, soft tissue balance, and bony deformity.

Figure A is a weight-bearing foot radiograph demonstrating hallux valgus. Lines A and B have been superimposed on the image to represent potential medial eminence resection. Line A represents an overly-aggressive medial eminence resection. Illustration A is an example of an iatrogenic hallux varus deformity.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: There are many reasons for hallux valgus recurrence, usually from inadequate correction, but excessive metatarsal head resection is not one of them.
Answer 2: Cock up toe deformity is usually due to FHL injury or a sequela of Keller arthroplasty.
Answer 3: Transfer metatarsalgia following hallux valgus surgery is usually due to 1st metatarsal shortening.
Answer 5: Capital segment necrosis may develop after disruption of the blood supply to that portion of the metatarsal but not after lateral placement of the saw blade.
