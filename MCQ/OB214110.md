## Question

A 65-year-old female who presents to your office for evaluation of left hip pain. She has failed conservative measures including PT over the last 6 months and is ready for hip replacement surgery. She asks about the differences in the anterior, lateral, and posterior approaches. Which of the following is true?

## Options

1. No difference in gait biomechanics at 3 months postoperatively
2. Dislocation rates are reduced when utilizing the posterior approach
3. Violation of the abductor mechanism occurs with the posterior approach
4. Paresthesias over the anterolateral thigh can be expected after the direct lateral approach
5. The lateral approach is the only approach that utilizes both an internervous and a intermuscular plane

## Correct option

**1. No difference in gait biomechanics at 3 months postoperatively**

## Answer explanation

When comparing approaches used for total hip arthroplasty, there is no difference in gait biomechanics at 3 months postoperatively.

Total hip arthroplasty is one of the most commonly performed orthopedic procedures and can be done through a variety of approaches. Currently, the posterior, direct lateral, and direct anterior are the most commonly utilized approaches. Differences in patient outcomes based on approach have been evaluated in countless studies. The anterior approach has gained popularity recently due to its muscle sparing nature, which utilizes an internervous and intermuscular plane. Even with this muscle sparing approach, gait biomechanics at 3 months postoperatively has been shown to be similar amongst these approaches.

*Meneghini et al review gait patterns following total hip arthroplasty performed through 3 minimally invasive approaches.* **They report** that all patients had improvement in gait parameters at 6 weeks postoperatively, however, the anterolateral approach was associated with a decrease in vertical ground reaction force at mid-stance. There was no difference in the posterior and 2-incision approaches. **They conclude** that the anterolateral approach showed a gait pattern consistent with abductor muscle injury.

*Moyer et al review postoperative gait mechanics after total hip arthroplasty.* **They report** minimal differences overall, and a small increase in step length after the posterior approach compared to the anterolateral approach. Additionally, there was a slight increase in the hip adduction moment following the posterior approach. **They conclude** that there are no significant differences in early or late follow up between the posterior, anterolateral, and lateral approaches in regards to gait mechanics.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: The posterior approach has the highest rate of dislocation for THA.
Answer 3: The lateral and anterolateral approaches violate the abductor mechanism.
Answer 4: Injury to the lateral femoral cutaneous nerve may occur during the anterior approach.
Answer 5: The anterior approach is performed through both internervous and intramuscular planes.
