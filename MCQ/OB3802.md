## Options

3. Denosumab
4. Teriparatide
5. Methotrexate

## Correct option

**3. Denosumab**

## Answer explanation

This patient has giant cell tumor (GCT) of the distal femur. Denosumab is FDA approved for treatment of GCT of bone.

Giant cell tumor of bone is a benign, aggressive tumor typically found in the metaphysis and epiphysis of long bones. It is more commonly found in females aged 30-50. Most commonly it is found in the distal femur>proximal tibia>distal radius>sacral ala. 50% of them occur around the knee either in the distal femur or the proximal tibia. Treatment is usually centered around extensive curettage and reconstruction (with adjuvant treatment). Denosumab is a monoclonal antibody against RANK-ligand and recent trials have suggested that it may decrease the size of the bone defect in GCT.

*Raskin et al. in a review of GCT, report that surgery remains widely accepted as the mainstay of therapy, but medical management using denosumab has been recently approved as well.* **They report** that osteoclastic giant cells express RANKL and denosumab effectively targets these cells.

*Xu et al. in a review of GCT, report that denosumab has been found to be more effective than bisphosphonates in reducing skeletal events due to GCT.* **They also report** that denosumab was given to patients with both surgically salvageable and unsalvageable GCT and it inhibited disease progression and reduced the requirement for surgical treatment. It has been found to induce at least 90% tumor necrosis histologically.

*Karras et al. report the case of a 10-year-old girl that was found to have GCT of the patella.* At the time of diagnosis, she had over 30 lung nodules. They treated her with denosumab for 1 year and noted the disappearance of giant cells and found new bone deposition on biopsy. Her pulmonary disease also did not progress during that time. She continued to have a normal growth velocity for her age and gender as well. **They concluded** that denosumab is tolerable and effective in peripubertal children.

Figures A and B demonstrate a distal femoral metaphyseal lesion extending to the epiphysis. Figure C demonstrates the characteristic giant cells of GCT.
