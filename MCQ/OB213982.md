## Question

A 51-year-old female with a history of scleroderma presents to your office for evaluation of non-healing ulcer to the tips of her index and middle fingers. You decide to attempt nonoperative treatment with a topical medication. What is the mechanism of action of this drug?

## Options

1. Increase cGMP in vascular smooth muscle
2. Inhibition of cyclooxygenase
3. Inhibition of phospholipase A2
4. Decreasing nerve cell membrane permeability to sodium ions
5. Blockade of L-type calcium channels

## Correct option

**1. Increase cGMP in vascular smooth muscle**

## Answer explanation

This patient has Raynaud’s phenomenon secondary to scleroderma. Initial treatment consists of topical nitrates, which cause vasodilation by increasing cGMP in local vascular smooth muscle.

Raynaud’s phenomenon or syndrome is a vasospastic disease with a known underlying cause; scleroderma in this case. Patients have a characteristic triphasic color change of the digits with white from vasospasm, blue due to cyanosis and venous stasis, and finally red from rebound hyperemia. Ulcerations of the finger pulps may develop and represent a difficult aspect of the disease to treat. Patients should avoid modifiable factors that cause vasoconstriction such as smoking and cold exposure. Nitrates have emerged as the only topical medication used in the treatment of Raynaud’s phenomenon. The use of oral medications including calcium channel blockers, aspirin, and Sildenafil have also been described. Digital sympathectomy and microvascular reconstruction are surgical interventions that are reserved for recalcitrant cases.

*Curtiss et al review the effects of topical nitrates in the treatment of both primary and secondary Raynaud’s phenomenon.* **They report** a moderate-to-large improvement of measured digital blood flow compared to placebo. **They conclude** that topic nitrates provide therapeutic benefit without a serious side effect profile.

*Porter et al reviewed the current evidence for the treatment of Raynaud's phenomenon.* *They discuss their evidence-based approach, which begins with low dose nifedipine.* Ultimately, **they conclude** that sympathectomy should be performed over arterial bypass grafting when needed.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Topical NSAIDs are not used in the initial treatment of Raynaud’s phenomenon.
Answer 3: Topical steroids are not used in the treatment of Raynaud’s’.
Answer 4: Local anesthetics such as lidocaine are not used topically in the initial treatment of Raynaud’s phenomenon.
Answer 5: Calcium channel blockers are used orally, but not topically for the treatment of Raynaud’s phenomenon.
