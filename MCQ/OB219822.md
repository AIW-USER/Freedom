## Question

A 25-year-old Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu practitioner presents to your clinic complaining of dominant hand index finger pain. The patient reports participating in a sparring session three days prior when he felt a pop in the affected digit. An ultrasound is performed, and you diagnose the patient with a Grade II pulley injury and recommend taping of the affected pulley(s). Which of the following injuries is consistent with a Grade II pulley injury?

## Options

1. Complete rupture of A2
2. Complete rupture of A3
3. Complete rupture of A4
4. Complete rupture of A2 and A3
5. Complete rupture of A2 with associated collateral ligament trauma

## Correct option

**3. Complete rupture of A4**

## Answer explanation

Grade II pulley injuries are defined as complete rupture of A4, or a partial rupture of A2 or A3 (Answer 3). These injuries are treated with taping of the affected pulley.

The flexor sheath-pulley system is a complex series of structures that allows for normal and efficient flexor tendon function. The pulleys help to form a fibro-osseous channel that serves to keep the tendons adjacent to the phalanges, allowing for the efficient transfer of force to the phalanges during finger flexion. In the non-thumb digits, there are 5 annular (A1-A5) and 3 cruciate (C1-C3) pulleys that run in descending order from proximal to distal (Illustration A). The A2 and A4 pulleys insert directly into the bone of the proximal and middle phalanges, respectively, and serve to prevent bowstringing of the tendon during flexion.

Injuries to the flexor-pulley system can occur during sports or activities where an extending force is applied to a flexed digit (i.e. rock climbing, Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu). Approximately 75% of rock climbers will suffer an upper extremity injury during his or her career, and 30%-50% of these injuries will involve a closed injury to the flexor-pulley system. *Injuries of the flexor-pulley system can be described through the classification system proposed by Schoffl et al.* Grade I injuries represent strains, Grade II injuries represent complete rupture of A4, or partial rupture of A2 or A3, Grade III injuries represent complete rupture of A2 or A3 and Grade IV injuries represent multiple ruptures of A2/A3 or A2/A3/A4, or a single rupture of A2 or A3 combined with lumbrical muscle or collateral ligament trauma. Grade I-III injuries are typically treated through closed means, while grade IV injuries are treated surgically with pulley reconstruction.

*Cole et al. provide a comprehensive review of rock climbing injuries.* **The authors note** that upper extremity injuries are common in these athletes and that hand/finger injuries represent most of the upper extremity injuries sustained in this patient population. **The authors note** that participation in rock climbing has skyrocketed since its introduction as an Olympic sport in 2020, and it is important that surgeons recognize the injuries associated with this sport.

*Zafonte et al. provide a review of the flexor-pulley system with an emphasis on evidence-based treatment.* **The authors note** that while Grade I-III injuries are typically managed through closed (nonoperative) means, grade IV injuries typically require open pulley reconstruction. **The authors highlight** that athletes in sports which require prolonged finger flexion are at risk of these injuries. Overall, **the authors conclude** that the goals of open treatment for Grade IV injuries are to prevent flexion contractures and decreased range of motion caused by flexor tendon bowstringing.

*King et al. provide a review of flexor tendon pulley injuries in rock climbers.* Like previous authors, **they highlight** the increased risk of sustaining these injuries in rock climbers. **The authors note** that sequential pulley ruptures are required for tendon bowstringing to occur and that isolated pulley ruptures are often managed successfully with early functional rehabilitation.
