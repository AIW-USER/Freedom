## Question

A 45 Years male patient presented to OPD with complaints of pain in the heel bone. On examination there is no swelling and tenderness is present on deep palpation. X ray of lateral view of ankle is given below. Fast spin-echo sequences of an MRI shows high signal both in T1- and T2-weighted images. What is the most likely diagnosis?

![[TO17143378-1.png]]

## Options

1. ABC
2. SBC
3. Intraosseous lipoma
4. Osteoblastoma

## Correct option

**3. Intraosseous lipoma**

## Answer explanation

X ray given in the question shows well-defined lucent lesion of the calcaneum with a central calcification also known as "cockade sign". This "cockade sign" describes the classic appearance of a calcaneal intraosseous lipoma. SBC , ABC, GCT, Osteoblastoma and pseudotumor of the calcaneum are the main differential diagnoses. MRI helps to confirm the diagnosis.

In fast spin-echo sequences of an MRI, adipose tissue signal is high both in T1- and T2-weighted images, and becomes extinguished in fat-suppression sequences.

Intraosseous lipomas constitute very rare bone tumours. Histologically, these tumours are composed of mature adipose cells, which are slightly larger than the non-tumorous ones, and may include single spindle cells, regressive lesions (foci of fat necrosis, cystic spaces, and dystrophic calcifications) and bone trabeculae undergoing resorption.

Milgram divided these lesions into three stages, depending on the histopathological appearance.

Stage I includes lesions composed of viable, mature lipocytes, similar to the cells of subcutaneous adipose tissue.

In stage II, necrotic foci are present in the lesion; foamy macrophages and foci of reactive osteogenesis may also appear.

Stage III is a completely necrotic lesion with focal calcifications and cystic degeneration that resembles a bone infarct, the only features differentiating it from lipoma being lack of trabecular resorption or possible bone expansion.

Location: The lesions tend to locate within the lower limb, with calcaneum being the most common location (about 30% of cases). Other frequent locations include: subtrochanteric femur, as well as femur and tibia in the region of the knee joint.

Age group: Intraosseous lipomsa may develop around all ages but most commonly seen in middle age group ( around the age of 40)

Radiology: Intraosseous lipomas usually present as cystic lesions with an increased radiolucency, surrounded by a sclerotic rim. Inside the cysts, internal trabeculations may be present, while in lipomas with regressive lesions (stage II and III according to Milgram) – foci of calcifications, usually located centrally. An image of a cystic lesion in the calcaneus with a central calcification is pathognomonic for intraosseous lipoma.

Ans: C
