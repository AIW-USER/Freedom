## Question

A 34-year-old male prisoner decides to jump from the top floor of the jail and falls about 20 feet. He lands on his feet and immediately screams in pain. He is unable to ambulate and complains of significant pain in his left foot. He is taken to the emergency department by officers where radiographs are obtained as seen in Figure A. You are called around 11:00 pm to the emergency department to evaluate the patient and you immediately note that he needs urgent operative intervention. The anesthesiologist on call asks why the case cannot be delayed until the following day. What is the reason for surgical urgency in this particular case?

![[q215606-a-tongue-type-calc.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. To decrease the risk of nonunion
2. To improve the fracture reduction
3. To decrease the risk of soft tissue necrosis
4. To decrease the risk of compartment syndrome
5. To improve post-operative function of the gastrocnemius-soleus complex

## Correct option

**3. To decrease the risk of soft tissue necrosis**

## Answer explanation

This patient has sustained a tongue-type calcaneus fracture, which places the overlying skin at high risk for necrosis and must be addressed urgently.

Calcaneus fractures normally occur with a traumatic axial load, such as a fall from height. Tongue-type fractures involve the posterior facet and exit posteriorly through the calcaneal tuberosity. Tongue-type variants occur due to the strong avulsive forces of the gastrocnemius-soleus complex. Displaced tongue-type fractures can place the overlying posterior skin at risk for necrosis. This is a complication that can occur very quickly and these patients should be splinted in 30 degrees of plantarflexion and taken to the operating room in an urgent manner for reduction and fixation. Skin necrosis is a devastating complication as options for soft tissue coverage in this area are very limited.

*Chhabra et al. performed a review of tongue-type calcaneus fractures, initially describing the pathoanatomy behind the injury and its effects on the overlying soft tissues.* **They note** published rates of skin necrosis of >20% in this fracture pattern, with soft-tissue coverage or amputation being needed in 29% of this group. They go on to reiterate that both emergency physicians and orthopedic surgeons must quickly recognize and treat these injuries to avoid this complication.

*Banerjee et al. discuss management for calcaneal tuberosity fractures in general,* **noting** that they are uncommon but can have significant soft tissue implications. **They suggest** that these injuries should be quickly recognized and fixed surgically. They go on to discuss fixation techniques to improve outcomes in this injury pattern.

Figure A demonstrates a lateral radiograph showing a displaced tongue-type calcaneus fracture.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Urgent/emergent fixation does not affect the rate of nonunion.
Answer 2: There is no difference in the ability to achieve an anatomic reduction with regards to urgent and delayed (hours to days) fixation.
Answer 4: Risk for skin necrosis, not compartment syndrome, is the reason why surgical urgency is necessary.
Answer 5: While fixation of the tongue-type fragment does restore the function of the gastrocnemius-soleus complex, this is not the reason that this case requires surgical urgency.
