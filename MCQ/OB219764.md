## Question

A 15-year-old male presents to your clinic with a three-month history of right knee pain. The patient notes that he was previously asymptomatic until he fell while playing soccer a few months ago. Radiographic studies are consistent with the diagnosis of Osteochondritis Dissecans (OCD). Which of the following locations, as labeled in Figure A, represents the most likely site of pathology?

![[q219764-a-ocdkneeloc.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. A
2. B
3. C
4. D
5. E

## Correct option

**4. D**

## Answer explanation

Osteochondritis Dissecans (OCD) of the knee most commonly affects the posterolateral aspect of the medial femoral condyle (Answer 4).

OCD is a pathologic lesion that affects articular cartilage and subchondral bone. The juvenile form of this pathology typically affects children between the ages of 10-15, while the physis is still open. The most common location for OCD around the knee is the posterolateral medial femoral condyle (70% of lesions).

The etiology of OCD is multifactorial and includes hereditary components, traumatic injury, or vascular injury. The pathophysiologic cascade begins with the softening of the overlying articular cartilage, followed by early articular cartilage separation, partial detachment of the lesion, and finally, osteochondral separation with loose body formation. Symptoms of OCD include pain, mechanical symptoms and recurrent effusions. Treatment depends on numerous factors, including lesion stability, physeal status, and lesion size. Overall, prognosis is better in skeletally immature patients, as these patients have a much higher capacity for success with nonoperative treatment.

*Zanon et al. provide an overview of OCD of the knee.* **The authors note** that the incidence of this pathology is increasing and that the most common location of OCD around the knee is the posterolateral aspect of the medial femoral condyle. **The authors highlight** that while nonoperative management is often successful in patients with open physis, adults with OCD will often necessitate surgical intervention.

*Pareek et al. performed a population-based study of OCD of the knee, which included 302 individuals.* Overall, **the authors found** that the incidence was higher in males than females and that the highest incidence was seen in patients between the ages of 11 and 15.

Incorrect Answers
Answers 1-3,5: the most common location for OCD around the knee is the posterolateral aspect of the medial femoral condyle. The other answer choices are much less common locations of this pathology.
