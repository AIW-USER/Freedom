## Question

A 2-year-old male presents to the ED with fevers, irritability, and refusal to bear weight on the left leg for the past 24 hours. The patient’s mother states that he had a trip and fall while playing outside two days ago but only sustained minor abrasions. The patient is holding the left hip in a slightly flexed and externally rotated position and cries with any attempt to move the hip. Laboratory studies are ordered and pending. Figure A is a radiograph of the patient’s pelvis. Which of the following laboratory values would support the patient's diagnosis?

![[q218656-a-septic-arthritis-hip.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Serum white blood cell count of 10,000 cells/mm3
2. Erythrocyte sedimentation rate of 38 mm/hour
3. C-reactive protein of 22 mg/L
4. Temperature of 38.2º Celsius
5. Synovial white blood cell count of 40,000 cells/mm3

## Correct option

**3. C-reactive protein of 22 mg/L**

## Answer explanation

This patient is presenting with septic arthritis of the left hip. The original Kocher criteria were as follows: refusal to bear weight, a history of fever (an oral temperature >38.5°C or 101.3°F), a white blood cell count of >12,000 cells/mm3, and an erythrocyte sedimentation rate of >40 mm/hr. *This was further supported by Caird et al. in 2006, who demonstrated that a CRP of >20 mg/L was a strong independent risk factor as well.* Therefore, a CRP of 22 mg/L is the most supportive listed finding for diagnosing septic arthritis of the pediatric hip.

Pediatric septic arthritis is a surgical emergency, as prompt diagnosis and emergent surgical intervention are crucial to the preservation of the articular cartilage. Missed cases can result in complete destruction of the articular cartilage, osteonecrosis of the femoral head, and resulting limb length discrepancy. A careful physical exam, complete laboratory and imaging workup, and high clinical suspicion are required to diagnose septic arthritis in the child, especially if the child is very young and unable to vocalize their specific complaints. In the setting of hip pain in the child with or without a history of trauma, the Kocher criteria are a well-established, highly predictive set of clinical and laboratory data that can assist with the diagnosis of pediatric septic arthritis.

*Kocher et al. established four independent clinical and laboratory criteria in their 1999 retrospective study as a method for differentiating between septic arthritis and transient synovitis of the hip in children.* *Using laboratory values, imaging data, and the clinical course of 282 patients over 17 years, the group established a probability algorithm for differentiation between septic arthritis and transient synovitis on the basis of independent multivariate predictors.* The predictors were described as follows: history of fever (>38.5º Celsius), non-weight-bearing, ESR >40mm/hour, and serum WBC count of >12,000 cells/mm3. The predicted probability of septic arthritis was determined for all sixteen combinations of these four predictors: <0.2% for zero predictors, 3% for one, 40% for two, 93.1% for three, and 99.6% for four.

*Caird et al. performed a subsequent, prospective 2006 study evaluating clinical data on 53 children in whom septic arthritis was suspected.* *Their study was different from previous work in that data was collected prospectively, C-reactive protein levels were recorded, and the study’s focus was on children in whom the findings were so suspicious for septic arthritis that hip aspiration was performed.* Positive cultures on aspirate were used as the gold standard for diagnosis. In addition to validating the Kocher criteria’s usage in predicting the probability of septic arthritis, the group noted that a C-reactive protein (CRP) level of >20 mg/L was a strong independent predictor as well, with a diagnostic probability of 97.5% with all five factors (fever, non-weight bearing, ESR >40, WBC >12, and CRP >20) present.

*Erkilinc et al. reviewed the current concepts in the diagnosis and management of pediatric septic arthritis.* In the initial examination of the child in whom septic arthritis is suspected, concerning findings on history and physical as well as laboratory studies should prompt appropriate imaging studies in the form of AP pelvis X-rays and ultrasound of bilateral hips. Positive findings on imaging studies are followed by aspiration of the hip joint. Synovial fluid with a cloudy or purulent synovial fluid appearance, WBC count >50,000 cells/mL with more than 90% polymorphonuclear neutrophils, and a glucose level of 50 mg/dL less than serum glucose levels, all support the diagnosis of infection. Of note, the release of proteolytic enzymes in the joint leads to articular damage within 8 hours of symptoms onset. Therefore, urgent irrigation and debridement coupled with the administration of empiric intravenous (IV) antibiotics is essential.

Figure A is an AP radiograph of a 2-year-old child with septic arthritis of the left hip, evidenced by the lateral displacement of the femoral head ossification center (suggestive of a hip effusion) in comparison to the contralateral side.

Incorrect Answers:

Answer 1: A serum white count of 10,000 cells/mm3 is below the published Kocher criterion of 12,000 cells/mm3.
Answer 2: Erythrocyte sedimentation rate of ≥40 mm/hour is a positive predictor according to the Kocher criteria.
Answer 4: Temperature of ≥38.5º Celsius is a positive predictor according to the Kocher criteria.
Answer 5: Synovial white blood cell count is not part of the original Kocher criteria. However, the original Kocher article describes the septic arthritis group as having a synovial WBC >50,000 cells/mm3, whereas the transient synovitis group presented with a synovial WBC <50,000 cells/mm3.
