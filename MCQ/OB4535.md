## Options

1. Neoadjuvant chemotherapy, surgical excision, adjuvant radiation therapy
2. Surgical irrigation and debridridement followed by 6 weeks of culture directed intravenous antibiotics
3. Surgical excision followed by adjuvant chemotherapy and radiation therapy
4. Neoadjuvant chemotherapy, surgical excision, adjuvant chemotherapy
5. Surgical irrigation and debridridement with antibiotic cement spacer placement followed by 6 weeks of culture directed intravenous antibiotics

## Correct option

**4. Neoadjuvant chemotherapy, surgical excision, adjuvant chemotherapy**

## Answer explanation

The history, images, and histology all suggest a high grade osteosarcoma. The appropriate treatment includes neoadjuvant chemotherapy, surgical excision, and adjuvant chemotherapy.

Osteosarcomas are the most common malignant bone tumor in children. Osteosarcomas are associated with mutations in the retinoblastoma gene (RB) and p53. The most common chemotherapy agents include adriamycin (doxorubicin), cis-platinum, methotrexate, and ifosfamide. Surgical resection of the primary osteosarcoma is key to the treatment and is generally performed 3 to 4 weeks after the last dose of preoperative chemotherapy. Long-term survival has a 5x increase with complete resection of both primary and metastatic sites compared with resection of the primary tumor alone.

*Quan et al. looked at a series of patients with periarticular osteosarcomas.* **They found** there was no evidence of penetration across the entire thickness of articular cartilage into the joint cavity in all of the 27 cases. **They propose** articular cartilage is a relative barrier to tumor invasion and if pre-operative MRI does not show definite evidence of intra-articular tumor involvement, it is likely to be safe to proceed with intra-articular resection.

*Mankin et al. looked at their 30 year experience with osteosarcoma and looked at variables that influence the survival outcome.* **They found** patient gender had no effect, but age of the patient was correlated with survival data, with the poorest survival for the older patients. The Musculoskeletal Tumor Society (MTS) stage of the lesion, the presence of metastases or local recurrence, and the chemotherapeutic treatment all had a profound effect.

Figures A and B are radiographs demonstrating a blastic and destructive lesion with periosteal elevation (Codman's triangle) present. Figure C is a bone scan demonstrating increased uptake at the distal femur. Figures D and E are coronal MR sequences exhibiting a heterogenous mass crossing the distal femoral physis with surrounding edema. Figure F demonstrates malignant cells with significant atypia and surrounding "lacey" osteoid.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1 & 3: Radiation plays no role in the standard treatment of osteosarcoma.
Answer 2 & 5: The presentation is most consistent with osteosarcoma and not osteomyelitis.
