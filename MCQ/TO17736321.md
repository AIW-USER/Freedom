Chapter: FNB 2017 — 2017 MCQs

## Question

External rotation lag sign is suggestive of (regional orthopedics- shoulder- clinical examination)

## Options

1. Subscapularis tear
2. Teres minor tear
3. Infraspinatus tear
4. Adhesive capsulitis

## Correct option

**3. Infraspinatus tear**

## Answer explanation

Clinical tests for rotator cuff tear:

Subscapularis tear:

Belly-press test (Gerber)/Napoleon sign: Patient places his hand on the belly with elbow in front of the trunk and press the abdomen, If subscapularis is normal, the patient presses the abdomen by hand by internal rotation of the shoulder and elbow remains in front of the trunk. If the subscapularis is weak, the patient presses the abdomen by extending the shoulder and dropping the elbow behind the trunk (Belly press test) or by flexing the wrist (Napoleon sign, named after the peculiar posture in which Napoleon is depicted in many photographs).

Lift-off test (Gerber): Patient’s arm is internally rotated with the dorsum of the hand touching the back. In case of subscapularis tear the patient will not be able to lift his hand off the back.

Bear hug test (Burkhart and De Beer): This test is more sensitive than above tests and can detect subscapularis tears when less than 50% of the muscle is torn. In such situations above two tests may fail to recognize subscapularis tear. Patient places the hand on the contralateral shoulder and the examiner tries to raise the hand off the shoulder and the patient resists it. A positive test is indicated by failure of the patient to resist the examiner.

Supraspinatus tear:

Jobe’s empty can sign: Elevate the shoulder to 90° abduction in scapular plane (30° forward flexion from coronal plane of body) and internally rotate (thumb faces towards floor) Weakness in elevating the arm against resistance indicates a tear of supraspinatus.

 Drop arm test (Codman): Patient’s arm is elevated overhead and then he is asked to bring it down. Onset of acute pain or if the patient is unable to smoothly control the lowering motion suggest supraspinatus tear.

Infraspinatus and Teres Minor tear:

External rotation resistance stress test: The test checks the tear of infraspinatus and teres minor. Patient keeps his arms by his side in 0° abduction-flexion with elbows bent to 90°. Now the shoulders are externally rotated to 60°. Patient resists the pressure of the examiner on the dorsum of hands as the examiner tries to internally rotate both arms. Pain or weakness suggests insufficiency of external rotators.

External rotation lag sign (ERLS): This test is specific for infraspinatus. To elicit the ERLS, the patient is seated on a chair with elbows bent to 90°. The examiner elevates the patient’s shoulder by 20° in the scapular plane and rotates them externally to full extent. The patient is asked to maintain this position. The test is positive when angular drop occurs.

Drop sign: This sign also tests the integrity of infraspinatus. The procedure is absolutely similar as in ERLS, however, in this test the shoulders are elevated to 90° in the scapular plane. Sensitivity as well as specificity has been reported to be slightly more with the ERLS.

Hornblower sign (Patte): This sign predicts more accurately the integrity of teres minor. With elbows bent to 90°, the patient's arm is elevated to 90° in the scapular plane. The patient is then asked to laterally rotate the shoulder. Weakness or pain on trying to do so suggests insufficiency of teres minor.

Answer: 3
