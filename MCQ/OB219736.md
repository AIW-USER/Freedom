## Question

A 22-year-old female soccer player presents to the clinic for bilateral leg pain when participating in practice. She reports intense aching in her legs with paresthesias in her feet when playing, which improves with rest. Which of the following examination findings would most likely be present on examination in clinic?

## Options

1. Positive tinel's sign over the fibular neck
2. Firm anterior and lateral compartments
3. Diminished sensation in the webspace of the great toe
4. Weakness with ankle dorsiflexion
5. Normal examination

## Correct option

**5. Normal examination**

## Answer explanation

This 22-year-old female presents to the clinic with bilateral leg aching and paresthesias while participating in physical activity, which subsequently resolves with rest. The most likely diagnosis is exertional compartment syndrome, and clinical examination in the outpatient clinic setting most commonly yields a normal, unremarkable examination (Answer 5).

Exertional compartment syndrome (ECS) is a common exercise-induced condition that creates temporary ischemia in the affected compartment, leading to pain and occasionally muscular weakness and neurologic deficits that resolve with activity cessation. Because of the transient nature of the condition and its relationship with physical activity, patients most commonly demonstrate no abnormal examination findings at rest. As such, the gold standard of diagnosing the condition includes serial compartment measurements at rest and at one and five minutes post-exercise. Resting pressures greater than 15 mmHg, one-minute post-exercise greater than 30 mmHg, or five-minute post-exercise pressures greater than 20 mmHg meet the criteria for diagnosing ECS.

*George and Hutchinson published an article providing an overview of ECS,* **noting** the entity to be most common in physical activities involving repetitive physical activity, like in sports involving a large amount of running, jumping, or cutting. Despite this, **the authors also note** the condition can be seen in sedentary individuals who experience leg pain while performing rather innocuous activities and in the absence of claudication.

*Waterman et al. performed a retrospective review examining the risk factors of 4,100 military service members who received a diagnosis of ECS.* Epidemiologically, **the authors found** an overall incidence rate of 0.49 per 1000 person-years. Moreover, **the authors determined** that increasing age, females, white race, junior ranking officers, and those enlisted in the branch of the Army are significant risk factors for ECS.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1-4: while these findings are associated with exertional compartment syndrome, they are likely to be absent on examination when the patient is at rest in the clinic setting.
