## Question

Which of the following is true regarding closed, unstable ankle fractures in diabetic patients?

## Options

1. Complication rates after ORIF are higher with absent pedal pulses than with peripheral neuropathy
2. ORIF with standard techniques is recommended in the absence of peripheral neuropathy and vasculopathy
3. Tibiotalocalcaneal arthrodesis is contraindicated in those with poor glycemic control
4. Tibiotalocalcaneal nailing is recommended in those with a sensate foot
5. Weightbearing total contact casting should be used for fracture stabilization

## Correct option

**2. ORIF with standard techniques is recommended in the absence of peripheral neuropathy and vasculopathy**

## Answer explanation

Diabetic patients have higher complication rates than non-diabetic patients with ankle fractures. However, patients without peripheral neuropathy or vasculopathy may be treated successfully with standard ORIF techniques.

Patients with neuropathy or vasculopathy are at increased risk for delayed union, nonunion, and poor wound healing. However, in the absence of peripheral neuropathy or vasculopathy, standard ORIF techniques with enhanced fixation should be employed as fewer complications occur postoperatively compared with those with neuropathy or vasculopathy. Postoperative non-weightbearing duration is often increased in the diabetic patient.

*Wukich et al. reviewed complications following both open and closed management of ankle fractures in patients with diabetes mellitus.* **They report** that diabetic patients with neuropathy or vasculopathy have higher complication rates than both diabetic patients without these comorbidities and nondiabetic patients. **They concluded** that unstable ankle fractures in diabetic patients without neuropathy or vasculopathy are best treated with ORIF with use of standard techniques. Patients with neuropathy or vasculopathy are at increased risk for both soft-tissue and osseous complications, including delayed union and nonunion and rigid internal fixation is crucial to obtaining a good outcome. Prolonged non-weight-bearing followed by subsequent protected weight-bearing are recommended following both operative and nonoperative management of ankle fractures in patients with diabetes.

*Costigan et al. reviewed diabetic patients who underwent ORIF using standard fixation techniques for acute, closed ankle fractures.* **They reported** that 33% of patients with preoperative evidence of peripheral neuropathy developed Charcot arthropathy. **They noted** that patients who had absent pedal pulses preoperatively and those with peripheral neuropathy developed complications in 83% and 92%, respectively. **They concluded** that most patients with diabetes can undergo open reduction and internal fixation of acute ankle fractures without complications, but those with absent pedal pulses or peripheral neuropathy are at increased risk for complications.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Complication rates are increased with both vasculopathy and peripheral neuropathy in diabetic patients. Studies have shown that patients who had absent pedal pulses preoperatively and those with peripheral neuropathy developed complications in 83% and 92%, respectively
Answer 3: Tibiotalocalcaneal (TTC) arthrodesis and nailing with locking screws has been shown to be an optimal salvage procedure. Long nails should be used to avoid stress fractures proximally
Answer 4: TTC nailing is a reasonable option in a diabetic patient with peripheral neuropathy or vasculopathy, but standard ORIF techniques are recommended in those without neuropathy or vasculopathy
Answer 5: Total contact casting is helpful in achieving healing of soft tissues but often does not stabilize the fracture
