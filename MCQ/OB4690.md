## Options

1. The transverse ligament is disrupted, the fracture is unstable and should be treated with either a rigid orthosis, halo immobilization, or surgical stabilization
2. The transverse ligament is intact, the fracture is stable and can be treated in a soft cervical collar
3. It is classified as Anderson and D'Alonzo Type II because the fracture extends into the C1/C2 facet
4. It is classified as Anderson and D'Alonzo Type III because the fracture extends into the C1/C2 facet
5. The imaging findings are relatively common and represent a congenital incomplete formation of the posterior arch and not a traumatic injury

## Correct option

**1. The transverse ligament is disrupted, the fracture is unstable and should be treated with either a rigid orthosis, halo immobilization, or surgical stabilization**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a Jefferson fracture with a ruptured transverse ligament as evident by a sum of lateral mass displacements of 8.3 mm. This fracture pattern is unstable and should be treated with rigid immobilization (either rigid orthosis, halo immobilization, or posterior surgical stabilization).

Atlas fractures arise from a hyperextension, axial loading force, such as occurs during a head-first dive. If the sum of lateral mass displacements > 7 mm (or 8.1mm with magnification) then a transverse ligament rupture is present and the injury pattern is unstable. Stable fractures can be treated with a hard cervical orthosis. Unstable fracture patterns need to be treated with either a rigid cervical orthosis, halo immobilization, or a posterior C1-2 fusion. There is no clear evidence to support one over the other, so it is up to patient characteristics (degree of instability seen on flexion-extension radiographs), patient preference, surgeon judgement, and available resources.

*Tan et al. performed C1 lateral mass-C2 pedicle screws and crosslink fixation on 11 cases of unstable atlas fractures.* *They used cross-links to aid reduction of the displaced lateral masses and provide rotational stability.* There were no vertebral artery or neurologic complications.

*Spence et al. reviewed burst fractures with ruptured transverse ligaments.* In an experimental study, **they found** that the total excursion of lateral masses from resting width to ligament rupture was 6.3mm (range, 4.8-7.6mm). **They concluded** that if spread was <5.7mm, conservative therapy was indicated. If spread >6.9mm, then the ligament was probably torn and surgery was indicated.

Incorrect Answers
Answer 2: This Jefferson fracture is classified as unstable due to a sum of lateral mass displacements of > 8.1 mm which indicates a disrupted transverse ligament.
Answers 3 and 4: The Anderson-D'Alonzo classification is used for fractures of the odontoid process.
Answer 5: Imaging findings of displaced lateral masses is indicative of traumatic disruption of the ring of the atlas.
