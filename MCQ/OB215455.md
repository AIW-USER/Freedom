## Question

A 38-year-old competitive curler, enraged after sending his stone off course, strikes the ice with a closed fist. The winter-sport enthusiast has immediate pain and swelling. The next day he visits his team's preferred hand surgeon, who diagnoses the patient with an isolated, closed, minimally displaced, 4th metacarpal shaft fracture. On exam, the curler has tenderness over the fracture site, but no rotational deformity and no shortening. Non-operative management is recommended. Which of the following best describes the effect of the transverse intermetacarpal ligament in this case?

## Options

1. Limit shortening of the affected digit
2. Limit rotation of the affected digit
3. Limit dislocation of the metacarpal phalangeal joint (MCPJ) of the affected digit
4. Limit dislocation of the carpometacarpal joint (CMCJ) of the affected digit
5. Reduce likelihood of fracture of the affected digit

## Correct option

**1. Limit shortening of the affected digit**

## Answer explanation

The transverse metacarpal ligament is the primary restraint against the longitudinal shortening of metacarpal shaft fractures.

Metacarpal fractures account for 40% of all hand injuries and occur most commonly in men aged 10-29. The metacarpals are concave on the palmar surface and are further stabilized by the transverse intermetacarpal ligament. Long oblique fractures are prone to shortening and malrotation, which can be assessed by comparing the flexion cascade to the uninjured hand. Operative indications include open fractures, those involving multiple metacarpals, significant shortening, or malrotation.

*Al-Qattan et al. prospectively reviewed 42 patients with 54 spiral metacarpal fractures treated with non-operative management.* Extension lag was initially seen in all digits but recovered by one year. Grip strength at one year was also found to be 94% of the contralateral hand. Conservative management with immediate mobilization, even in length-unstable fractures, yielded good results.

*Strauch et al performed a cadaveric analysis evaluating metacarpal phalangeal joint (MCPJ) lag after metacarpal fractures.* **The authors found** a 7-degree extensor lag at the MCPJ for every 2mm of metacarpal shortening. **The authors noted** that the ability of the MCPJ to hyperextend likely compensates for this loss in a clinical setting.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Malrotation can be assessed best after a local block and can be assessed by lining up the fingernail of the fractured finger in partial flexion and full flexion. This, however, is not affected by the transverse intermetacarpal ligament.
Answer 3: The transverse intermetacarpal ligament is well proximal to the MCPJ and does not affect its stability in this case.
Answer 4: The transverse intermetacarpal ligament is well distal to the CMCJ and does not affect its stability in this case.
Answer 5: The transverse intermetacarpal ligament does not confer rigidity or reduce the likelihood of metacarpal fracture.
