## Question

A 55-year-old man presents with a lesion of his right thumb. A clinical photograph and biopsy of the lesion are shown in Figures A and B. Lesion thickness is 0.6mm. Which treatment is recommended to provide the best chance of cure while preserving function?

![[q4848-b-fig-b-thin-melanoma.jpg]]

## Options

1. Chemotherapy to downsize the tumor, followed by resection with a 1-2mm margin.
2. Moh's micrographic surgery
3. Disarticulation at the interphalangeal joint and sentinel lymph node biopsy
4. Disarticulation at the metacarpophalangeal joint and sentinel lymph node biopsy
5. Ray amputation, lymph node dissection and hyperthermic isolated limb perfusion

## Correct option

**3. Disarticulation at the interphalangeal joint and sentinel lymph node biopsy**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a thin subungual acral lentiginous melanoma. IP joint disarticulation with sentinel node biopsy will provide the best chance of cure while preserving thumb function.

Acral lentiginous melanoma occurs on the palm and in subungual areas (usually the thumb). Depth is the most important prognostic factor for survival. Subungual melanomas tend to present later, be thicker, and be ulcerated. For subungual melanomas, amputation through the proximal phalanx of the thumb and at the level of the PIP joint of a finger is recommended.

*Plate et al. reviewed the treatment of malignant tumors of the hand and wrist.* **They recommend** the size of the excision margin depends on the thickness of the tumor. On a finger digit, a thin melanoma (<0.76mm) can be treated with disarticulation while a thick melanoma (>4mm) requires a ray resection. For thin melanomas elsewhere on the body, **they recommend** a 1-2cm margin. For thicker melanomas, the margin should be greater than 3cm.

Figure A shows a subungual acral lentiginous melanoma of the thumb. Figure B shows aggregates of melanocytes invading into the dermis, demonstrating marked cellular atypia and hyperchromatic nuclei with prominant nucleoli.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: A 1-2mm margin is insufficient for melanoma resection. Thin melanomas require a 1-2cm margin. Thick melanomas require a >3cm margin. Neoadjuvant chemotherapy for downstaging/downsizing is not used.
Answer 2: The use of Moh's surgery in melanoma is controversial. It is accepted treatment for squamous cell carcinoma and basal cell carcinoma.
Answers 4,5: Distal amputation (at the IP joint) with sufficient margins has similar recurrence rates and survival to proximal (metacarpophalangeal or ray) amputations. Isolated limb perfusion is not indicated.
