## Question

Which of the following patients is most likely to benefit from triple arthrodesis?

## Options

1. 14-year-old female with flatfoot deformity, hindfoot valgus, hindfoot varus with double-leg heel rise, and no talonavicular uncoverage on AP radiographs
2. 63-year-old female with flatfoot deformity, hindfoot valgus, hindfoot varus with double-leg heel rise, equinus contracture, and 25% talonavicular uncoverage on AP radiographs
3. 57-year-old male with flatfoot deformity, hindfoot valgus, hindfoot valgus with double-leg heel rise, and 25% talonavicular uncoverage on AP radiographs
4. 59-year-old female with flatfoot deformity, hindfoot valgus, hindfoot varus with double-leg heel rise, and 50% talonavicular uncoverage on AP radiographs
5. 51-year-old male with flatfoot deformity, hindfoot valgus, hindfoot varus with double-leg heel rise, normal ankle dorsiflexion, and 25% talonavicular uncoverage on AP radiographs

## Correct option

**3. 57-year-old male with flatfoot deformity, hindfoot valgus, hindfoot valgus with double-leg heel rise, and 25% talonavicular uncoverage on AP radiographs**

## Answer explanation

Triple arthrodesis of the hindfoot is indicated in patients with rigid flatfoot deformity. Patient's with rigid hindfoot valgus, demonstrated by inability to correct with heel rise, are classified as stage III adult-acquired flatfoot deformity.

Triple arthrodesis includes arthrodesis of the subtalar, talonavicular, and calcaneocuboid joints. Triple arthrodesis is the gold standard for surgical management of patients with rigid, stage III flatfoot deformity which consists of rigid hindfoot valgus and/or rigid forefoot abduction. Triple, or double, hindfoot arthrodesis is also indicated in patients with flatfoot deformity and severe subtalar arthritis.

*Pinney et al. reviewed acquired adult flatfoot deformity.* *They described the constellation of foot deformities that may be present in this disease including: collapse of the midfoot arch, hindfoot valgus, forefoot abduction, forefoot supination, equinus contracture, and deltoid ligament incompetence.* *They also described nonoperative and operative management of various stages of flatfoot deformity.* **They note** flexible deformities are amenable to reconstruction whereas rigid deformity is best managed with arthrodesis.

Kelly and Easley reviewed treatment of stage III adult acquired flatfoot deformity. They noted that stage III disease occurs when chronic posterior tibial tendon insufficiency results in rigid hindfoot valgus or rigid forefoot abduction. They concluded that corrective arthrodesis is the treatment of choice and triple arthrodesis is the gold standard for surgical correction of stage III flatfoot deformity.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Flexible pes planovalgus is a common finding in the pediatric population due to ligamentous laxity that is best treated conservatively
Answer 2: Stage IIA (flexible hindfoot, normal forefoot) disease is best treated with reconstruction procedures. Equinus contracture should be treated accordingly based on silfverskiold testing results.
Answer 4: Stage IIB (flexible hindfoot, abducted forefoot) disease is best treated with reconstruction as long as the forefoot deformity is flexible
Answer 5: Stage IIA (flexible hindfoot, normal forefoot) disease is best treated with reconstruction procedures
