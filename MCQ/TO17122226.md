Chapter: Congenital & Developmental Musculoskeletal Disorders MCQs — Facioscapulohumeral dystrophy

## Question

A 25 years old patient presents with inability to flex and abduct both arms. He also has difficulty in pursing the lips and inability to whistle. Choose the false statement.

## Options

1. Inability to abduct the arm is due to weakness of shoulder abductors
2. Bilateral scapulothoracic arthrodesis is the treatment of choice
3. Diagnosis is Facioscapulohumeral muscular dystrophy
4. Autosomal dominant inheritance

## Correct option

**1. Inability to abduct the arm is due to weakness of shoulder abductors**

## Answer explanation

Facioscapulohumeral muscular dystrophy: It is an autosomal dominant condition with characteristic weakness of the facial and shoulder girdle muscles. Onset of the disease may be in dystrophin early childhood, in which case the disease runs a rapid, progressive course, confining most children to a wheelchair by age 8 to 9 years; alternatively, onset may occur in patients 15 to 35 years old, in which case the disease progresses more slowly.

Clinical features: The most striking clinical manifestation is facial weakness with an inability to whistle, purse the lips, wrinkle the brow, or blow out the cheeks. The greatest functional impairments are the inability to abduct and flex the arms at the glenohumeral joints and winging of the scapula, both caused by progressive weakness of the muscles that fix the scapula to the thoracic wall, whereas the muscles that abduct the glenohumeral joint remain strong. As the disease progresses, weakness of the lower extremities, especially in the peroneal and the anterior tibial muscles, results in a foot drop that requires the use of an ankle-foot orthosis.

The inability to flex and abduct the shoulder functionally usually is treated by stabilization of the scapula, with scapulothoracic arthrodesis

Answer : a
