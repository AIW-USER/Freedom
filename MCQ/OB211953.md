## Correct option

## Answer explanation

Figure A reveals a subscapularis tendon tear with a dislocated bicep tendon. A rotator cuff repair and bicep tenodesis would be appropriate in this patient.

The subscapularis serves as an internal rotator of the shoulder. A subscapularis tear most commonly presents as an acute avulsion in younger patients with a hyperabduction/external rotation injury. One pathognomic finding on MR imaging that suggests a subscapularis tendon tear is the presence of a subluxed or dislocated biceps tendon. In the abscense of significant glenohumeral arthritis, rotator cuff repair and bicep tenodesis is recommended. The bicep tenodesis addresses the painful dislocated biceps tendon. Clinical examination findings which support the diagnosis of a subscapularis tendon tear are excessive external rotation, positive belly press test, positive lift off test, and internal rotation lag.

*Boileau et al. evaluated the clinical and radiographic outcomes of isolated arthroscopic biceps tenotomy or tenodesis as treatment for persistent shoulder pain and dysfunction due to an irreparable rotator cuff tear associated with a biceps lesion.* **They report** that 78% were satisfied with the result with improvements in motion and outcome scores. **They concluded** that both arthroscopic biceps tenotomy and arthroscopic biceps tenodesis can effectively treat severe pain or dysfunction caused by an irreparable rotator cuff tear associated with a biceps lesion.

*Creech et al. reviewed the reported indications for tenodesis of the long head of the bicep tendon.* **They found** that the most common indications for LHB tenodesis were partial tearing (51 %), instability (49 %), tenosynovitis (44 %). **They concluded** that despite tearing, instability, and tenosynovitis being the most common reasons for a tenodesis, other indications include clinical exam indicating pathology, SLAP tears and subjective shoulder pain.
