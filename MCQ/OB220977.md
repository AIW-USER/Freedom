## Question

A 32-year-old badminton player presents for evaluation of a 5-month history of right ankle pain and swelling without any inciting event. He denies any other joint pain. An MRI of the right ankle reveals lobulated T1 and T2 hypointensities extending to the posterior extra-articular aspect of the ankle as well as erosive changes of the tibiotalar joint. A total synovectomy is planned, and brown pigmented synovium is encountered intraoperatively. Which of the following histology findings is most consistent with the patient’s imaging, planned surgery, and intraoperative findings?

## Options

1. Abundant plasma cells and lymphocytes, forming follicular like structures
2. Caseating granulomas
3. Hemosiderin stained multi-nucleated giant cells
4. Nodules of mature chondrocytes
5. Osteoclast-like cells with multiple nuclei and hemosiderin laden macrophages in a background of stromal cells.

## Correct option

**3. Hemosiderin stained multi-nucleated giant cells**

## Answer explanation

This patient presents with findings consistent with tenosynovial giant cell tumor (TsGCT). Patients with this condition have histology that demonstrates hemosiderin-stained multinucleated giant cells (answer 3).

TsGCT is a rare neoplastic disease caused by overexpression of the CSF1 gene, which results in aberrant synovial hyperplasia. Patients classically present with joint swelling, erythema, and chronic pain (most commonly involving the knee, but may present in other joints). Synovial specimens will demonstrate hyperplastic synovial cells, hemosiderin-stained multinucleated giant cells, and lipid-laden histiocytes. Treatment for patients with this condition is predominantly surgical and can consist of partial synovectomy or total synovectomy +/- arthroplasty or arthrodesis in the setting of severe degenerative changes. Patients who are unlikely to benefit from surgery may be treated with Pexidartinib (CSF1 receptor antagonist).

*Guo et al. performed a retrospective review of 31 patients with TsGCT of the ankle joint to determine the risk of recurrence following synovectomy.* **They found** that surgery significantly improved patient AO Foot and Ankle Society scores, and only 5 recurrences occurred, all of which had preoperative extra-articular involvement of the tendon sheaths. **They concluded** that patients with PVNS may be successfully treated with synovectomy and radiotherapy, but that those with extra-articular involvement may be at a higher risk of recurrence.

*Rollo and Wapner published a case report of a patient with TsGCT of the ankle joint.* *They discussed the diagnostic work-up and intra-operative findings of this patient’s neoplastic disease, which was highlighted by a high volume of brown pigmented synovium that had invaded and replaced the articular surface.* **They concluded** that among the various treatment modalities described for this condition, complete operative excision is the most efficacious, though postoperative recurrence rates remain high.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: These histology findings are more consistent with a diagnosis of rheumatoid arthritis, which would not be associated with brown pigmented synovium
Answer 2: This histology finding is more consistent with a diagnosis of intra-articular tuberculosis, which would not present with brown pigmented synovium.
Answer 4: This histology finding is more consistent with a diagnosis of synovial chondromatosis. This rarely presents in the ankle and is not typically treated with total synovectomy.
Answer 5: This histology finding is more consistent with a diagnosis of osteosarcoma. This is treated with neoadjuvant chemotherapy followed by wide resection and then adjuvant chemotherapy.
