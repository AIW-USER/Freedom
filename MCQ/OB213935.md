## Question

A 59-year-old female who recently moved to the area presents to your office for evaluation of a painful right total knee arthroplasty that was performed by another surgeon 10 months ago. Infectious workup, including alpha-defensin and synovial fluid analysis, are negative for infection. When asked, she does endorse a history of skin reaction to cheap metal jewelry. What is the pathophysiology of metal hypersensitivity reactions in TKA?

## Options

1. Type I IgE mediated
2. Type II IgG mediated
3. Type III immune complex mediated
4. Type IV delayed T-cell mediated
5. Type V IgG mediated

## Correct option

**4. Type IV delayed T-cell mediated**

## Answer explanation

Metal allergy, most commonly nickel, following TKA may cause increased pain, persistent effusion and stiffness. This is caused by a Type IV delayed, T-cell mediated hypersensitivity reaction.

Nickel, found in cobalt-chromium alloys used in TKA components may lead to metal hypersensitivity reactions. The metal wear from the prosthesis becomes lodged in the soft tissues, which must react with proteins to initiate a T-cell mediated hypersensitivity reaction. There is no consensus on the diagnosis of such reactions, however, patch testing, lymphocyte transformation tests, and T-lymphocyte rich immunohistopathology have all been suggested. Ultimately, treatment consists of implant exchange for hypoallergenic components.

*Zeng et al. reviewed the relationship between metal allergy and postoperative pain following lower extremity joint arthroplasty.* *They reported on 87 patients who underwent preoperative skin patch testing before TKA or THA.* The overall metal allergy rate was 51.1% (Nickel>Cobalt>Manganese). **They found** no relationship between metal allergy and post-surgical pain in this cohort.

*Roberts et al. reviewed hypersensitivity reactions in orthopedic implants.* **They report** a 10-15% incidence of cutaneous metal sensitivity across the population. **They confirm** that most of these reactions are type IV or delayed-type hypersensitivity reactions, with nickel being the most common inciting material. **They conclude** that hypersensitivity is a diagnosis of exclusion and infection must always be ruled out first.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Type I, IgE mediated reactions are those of quick onset after exposure such as bee stings and anaphylaxis.
Answer 2: Type II, IgG mediated reactions are antibody-mediated such as hemolytic reactions and Goodpasture syndrome.
Answer 3: Type III reactions are immune complex-mediated such as those resulting in systemic lupus erythematosus and polyarteritis nodosa.
Answer 5: Type V hypersensitivity reactions are stimulatory reactions and are IgG mediated.
