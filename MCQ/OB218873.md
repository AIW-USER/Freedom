## Question

An orthopedic resident is trying to conduct a research study. His research mentor is performing a study that is a cohort study design. Which of the following most accurately describes a cohort study design?

## Options

1. A study in which patient groups are separated non-randomly by exposure, with exposure occurring either before or after the initiation of the study
2. A study in which patient groups are separated by the current presence or absence of disease and examined for the prior exposure of interest
3. A study in which patients are randomly assigned to the treatment or control group and are followed prospectively
4. A retrospective account of multiple patients with the same injury or treatment with no control or comparison group
5. A study group is analyzed at a given time with no follow-up

## Correct option

**1. A study in which patient groups are separated non-randomly by exposure, with exposure occurring either before or after the initiation of the study**

## Answer explanation

A cohort study design is where patient groups are separated non-randomly by exposure or treatment, with exposure occurring after (prospective), or before (retrospective), the initiation of the study.

A cohort study is defined as a study in which patient groups are separated non-randomly by exposure or treatment. When the exposure occurs after the initiation of the study, this is considered a prospective cohort study (a Level II study by design), whereas if it occurs before then it is considered a retrospective cohort study (a Level III study by design). The analysis of a cohort study is usually reported as relative risk. An example of a cohort study would be a study that is trying to determine if smoking is a risk factor for the development of lung cancer. You identify a group of smokers and a group of non-smokers, and follow them over time measuring the desired outcome, in this case, lung cancer (more specifically, this would be a prospective cohort study - if the group identified patients already with lung cancer and then separated patient groups by a history of smoking, then it would be retrospective).

*Rouleau et al. reviewed the designs of clinical studies in orthopedic traumatology.* *They reported on observational studies, such as case-control studies, prospective cohort studies, and cross-sectional studies.* **They concluded** that these studies have a role to play in improving scientific knowledge of orthopedic trauma.

*Kooistra et al. reviewed several study designs.* **They reported** that case series is a descriptive study that follows a group of patients who have a similar diagnosis or who are undergoing the same procedure over a certain period of time. **They concluded** that the results of case series can generate hypotheses that are useful in designing further studies, including randomized controlled trials; however, no causal inferences should be made from case series regarding the efficacy of the investigated treatment.

Incorrect Answer:
Answer 2: This describes a case-control study design.
Answer 3: This describes a randomized controlled trial design.
Answer 4: This describes a case series study design.
Answer 5: This describes a cross-sectional study design.
