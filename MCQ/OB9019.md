## Question

A 79-year-old man with a 30-pack-year smoking history presents to the office with 3 weeks of right hip pain. His radiographs are shown in Figure A. His biopsy specimen is shown in Figure B. He undergoes resection of the primary tumor and no other skeletal or visceral lesions are found. Wide resection and endoprosthetic reconstruction (EPR) is the preferred treatment compared to intra-lesional resection and intramedullary nailing for which of the following reasons?

![[q9019-a-metastasis-proximal-femur.jpg]]
*Figure A*

![[q9019-b-renal-cell-carcinoma.jpg]]
*Figure B*

## Options

1. Lower risk of revision surgery
2. Increased patient survival
3. Decreased overall complication rate
4. 1 and 2
5. 1, 2 and 3

## Correct option

**5. 1, 2 and 3**

## Answer explanation

The patient has a solitary metastatic right proximal femur lesion from renal cell carcinoma (RCC). Endoprosthetic reconstruction (EPR) is preferred to intra-lesional resection for all of the listed reasons.

Patients with metastatic renal cell carcinoma experience a substantial failure rate with intra-lesional resection and bony stabilization procedures alone. Resection and EPR decreases the need for revision surgery and can prolong survival. Complete resection of metastatic RCC sites can improve median survival from 1.3 years to 4.8 years. Additionally, wide resection reduces the risk of local recurrence. Furthermore, immediate EPR allows for immediate weight-bearing and improved mobility.

*Laitinen et al. performed a retrospective review of 268 patients that underwent skeletal reconstruction for metastatic renal cell carcinoma.* **They found** an overall complication rate of 17%. Mechanical failure was found in 7.1% of patients, infection in 4.9% of patients (more common in EPR), and tumor progressing in 3.7% of patients. **The author found** that endoprosthetic reconstruction led to the lowest rate of complications overall compared to other treatment options such as intramedullary nailing or plate fixation.

*Hwang et al. retrospectively reported on 135 patients with metastatic renal cell carcinoma treated with resection and endoprosthetic replacement.* Survival at 1, 3, and 5 years was 72%, 45%, and 28%, respectively. Endoprosthetic revision rate was 7% overall. **The authors suggest** that because the majority of patients survive beyond 1 year and the risk of revision is low, that endoprosthetic reconstruction should be considered.

*Miller et al. performed a retrospective review of 112 intramedullary nails placed for metastatic disease.* *10 failures occurred and the most common reasons for failure were surgeon error, tumor progression, nonunion, and hardware failure.* *20% of the renal cell carcinoma patients had treatment failure, all due to progression of disease.* **The authors suggest** that curettage or resection and reconstruction, particularly of renal cell carcinoma, may help avoid treatment failures of metastatic disease.

Figure A is a radiograph of the right proximal femur with a radiolucent lesion in the peritrochanteric area. Figure B demonstrates the appearance of clear cell renal cell carcinoma. The cells have abundant cytoplasm and variable nuclear atypia. The cells are arranged in nests bordered by blood vessels.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: EPR is associated with a lower risk of revision surgery
Answer 2: EPR is associated with increased patient survival when performed for a solitary osseous metastasis.
Answer 3: Overall complication rate is lower with EPR
