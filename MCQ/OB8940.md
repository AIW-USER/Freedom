## Question

Figure A is an illustration of a patient in the beach chair position. Failure to appropriately positon the patient and pad the areas marked in Figure A may result in a compressive nerve palsy. Which labelled area is correctly paired with its resultant neuropraxia?

![[q8940-a-beach.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. A – Ptosis, mydriasis and diplopia.
2. B – Paresthesias over the ear, posterior auricular area and angle of the mandible.
3. C – Ipsilateral tongue deviation, dysphagia and dysarthria.
4. D – Weakness of the flexor pollicis longus and flexor digitorum profundus to index and long digits.
5. E – Weakness of the quadriceps femoris, paresthesias over anterior thigh and medial leg.

## Correct option

**2. B – Paresthesias over the ear, posterior auricular area and angle of the mandible.**

## Answer explanation

The great auricular nerve arises from the cervical plexus (C2-C3) and can be compressed as it travels subcutaneously over the mastoid process (Figure A, label B), resulting in paresthesias over the ear, posterior auricular area and angle of the mandible.

Proper placement and padding of the patient in the beach chair position is crucial to avoid soft tissue injury and neuropraxias. With the cervical spine in a neutral position, the head is secured to the head rest. The forehead and mastoid process area should be padded to protect the supraorbital nerve and great auricular nerve, respectively. The contralateral arm is positioned in an arm rest with the elbow padded to avoid ulnar neuropraxia. A lateral abdominal post or belt is placed to further secure the patient and should be padded to protect the lateral femoral cutaneous nerve (LFCN).

*Guss et al. reviewed the perioperative management of obese patients.* Safe positioning and padding is imperative, as these patients have a significantly greater risk of perioperative nerve palsies. Postoperatively, obese patients have higher rates of deep vein thrombosis, infection, wound breakdown, hardware/prosthetic failure and mortality. They also differ from non-obese patients in supplemental oxygen requirements and medication dosing.

*Moen et al. reviewed complications of shoulder arthroscopy.* In addition to compressive neurologic injuries from the beach chair positioner, the seated upright position also places the patient at risk for cerebral hypoperfusion and subsequent stroke. Neck hyperextension and rotation narrows blood vessels in the neck and increases the risk of stroke. Shoulder arthroscopy with regional anesthesia and intravenous sedation has a substantially lower rate of cerebral desaturation events.

Figure A is an illustration of a patient in the beach chair position. A tight and/or poorly padded face mask over the forehead (A) can compress the supraorbital nerve, a terminal branch of the trigeminal nerve (CN V), resulting in paresthesias over the forehead and anterior scalp. The area of the mastoid process (B) should be properly padded to avoid compression of the great auricular nerve, as discussed above. Skin breakdown involving the lip and chin can occur if the chin strap (C) is applied too tightly or improperly padded. The elbow of the contralateral nonoperative arm (D) should be protected to avoid ulnar neuropraxia. The lateral abdominal support belt (E) can compress the LFCN, particularly in obese individuals (compounded by weight of the pannus), resulting in paresthesias over the proximal lateral thigh. Illustration A depicts the sensory innervation of the face.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Oculomotor nerve (CN III) palsy manifests as ptosis (eyelid drooping), mydriasis (pupil dilation) and diplopia (double vision). Oculomotor nerve palsy can occur due to vascular disorders, demyelinating disease (multiple sclerosis) and autoimmune conditions (myasthenia gravis), but compressive neuropathy with beach chair use has not been reported.
Answer 3: Hypoglossal nerve (CN XII) palsy after surgery in the beach chair position has been reported; however, it is thought to be due to neck malpositioning, specifically neck hyperextension with resultant nerve traction and internal compression by the angle of the mandible, rather than external compression by the face mask.
Answer 4: Anterior interosseous nerve (AIN) palsy presents as weakness of the flexor pollicis longus (FPL) and radial flexor digitorum profundus (FDP) with no sensory abnormalities. AIN palsy has not been reported with beach chair use.
Answer 5: Femoral nerve palsy manifests as quadriceps femoris weakness and sensory abnormalities over the anterior thigh (anterior femoral cutaneous nerve) and medial leg (saphenous nerve). Femoral nerve palsy has not been reported with beach chair use.
