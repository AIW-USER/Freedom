Chapter: Regional Orthopedics MCQs — Rheumatoid Arthritis Involvement of The Hand

## Question

Regarding the pathogenesis of ulnar drift at the MCP joints, what is the 'wind-swept' effect primarily attributed to in the rheumatoid hand?

## Options

1. Hypertrophy of the abductor digiti minimi
2. Radial deviation of the metacarpals
3. Ulnar displacement of the extensor tendons and radial collateral ligament attenuation
4. Primary contracture of the ulnar collateral ligaments

## Correct option

**3. Ulnar displacement of the extensor tendons and radial collateral ligament attenuation**

## Answer explanation

The extensor carpi ulnaris (ECU) often subluxates volarly and ulnarly, while the radial collateral ligaments fail, allowing the intrinsic and extrinsic forces to pull the fingers ulnarly.

Ulnar Drift (MCP Joint)

Pathoanatomy

A multi-factorial "wind-swept" collapse at the MCP level.

 - Sagittal Band Failure: The radial sagittal band attenuates, allowing the EDC tendon to slip ulnarly into the "valleys" between the metacarpal heads.

 - Collateral Ligament Laxity: The radial collateral ligament fails, losing its resistance to the ulnar-directed pull of the intrinsics.

 - The Zigzag Effect: Often starts at the wrist. If the wrist settles into radial deviation, the long extensors must pull at an angle to reach the fingers, creating an "ulnar bowstring" force.

Surgical Management

 - Soft Tissue: Synovectomy, Cross-intrinsic transfer (moving the ulnar intrinsic of one finger to the radial side of the adjacent finger), and extensor tendon realignment.

 - Articular: Silastic MCP Arthroplasty (Swanson implants) remains the gold standard for fixed deformities, providing pain relief and a "functional" (though not full) range of motion.

Answer: 3
