## Question

A 49-year-old accountant presents with symptomatic bunions that were previously treated by a podiatrist. She notes that her right side has healed well, but her more recently done left side has become more painful over the last three months with stiffness and loss of dorsiflexion at the first metatarsophalangeal joint. She "feels like a marble is stuck" between her second and third metatarsal heads when she puts weight on the foot and wants to be evaluated for revision surgery. The operative report states that a proximal crescentic metatarsal osteotomy was performed and fixed with two smooth wires that were pulled in the clinic. Which of the following is the most likely complication the patient has suffered that would account for her current chief complaints?

## Options

1. Avascular necrosis of the metatarsal head
2. Cock up toe deformity
3. Dorsiflexion malunion
4. Hallux varus
5. Painful neuroma

## Correct option

**3. Dorsiflexion malunion**

## Answer explanation

Moderate-to-severe hallux valgus that is treated with a proximal metatarsal osteotomy (i.e., crescentic) is at risk for complication by a dorsiflexion malunion of the first metatarsal, especially when temporary smooth pin fixation is used to secure the osteotomy. This frequently results in painful loss of first MTPJ dorsiflexion and transfer metatarsalagia.

Hallux valgus represents a complex valgus deformity of the first ray that varies in the severity of its presentation. Mild deformities can often be treated with distal metatarsal osteotomies, while more severe deformities with hallux valgus angles (HVA) >25-40 degrees typically require proximal metatarsal osteotomies to allow for greater degrees of correction. Care must be taken when performing proximal metatarsal osteotomies to not significantly shorten the first ray, which can exacerbate a dorsiflexion malunion and lead to worsening transfer metatarsalgia. Treatment for a dorsiflexion malunion involves performing a corrective plantar flexion metatarsal osteotomy to restore the kinematics of the first ray and alleviate compensatory pressure on the lesser metatarsal heads.

*Varner et al. reviewed screw versus plate fixation of the proximal first metatarsal crescentic osteotomy in the treatment of hallux valgus.* **The authors note** that the intrinsically unstable proximal crescentic osteotomy relies on operative fixation for stability. In their cadaveric study, **the authors found** that all of the measures of fixation strength were statistically greater in specimens treated with a plate rather than with screw fixation, with plate fixation providing approximately twice the resistance to disruption of the osteotomy under cyclic loading conditions. **They concluded** that a dorsal plate is biomechanically more stable than a single cancellous screw when applied to proximal crescentic osteotomies.

*Brodsky et al. reviewed surgery for hallux valgus with proximal crescentic osteotomy as a cause for variable postoperative pressure patterns with weight bearing.* *The authors prospectively evaluated the results of plantar pressure measurements in 32 patients who had a proximal crescentic osteotomy of the first metatarsal and found that average peak pressures increased postoperatively under the second metatarsal head at a mean follow-up of 29 months.* **The authors concluded** that despite the procedure's reliable correction of hallux valgus deformity in the transverse plane and overall high patient satisfaction, a proximal crescentic osteotomy is highly variable and unpredictable in positioning the first ray in the sagittal plane and results in up to 20% rate of persistent or new areas of increased pressure under the second metatarsal.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: AVN of the metatarsal head more commonly occurs with distally based osteotomies, not proximal metatarsal osteotomies, with the medial capsulotomy being the primary insult to the blood flow to the metatarsal head.
Answer 2: A cock up toe deformity is caused by damage to the FHL not by proximal metatarsal malunion and would not present with primary MTPJ stiffness and transfer metatarsalgia.
Answer 4: Hallux varus is often the result of overcorrection of the first intermetatarsal angle with over-resection of the medial first metatarsal head and/or excessive lateral release. It is not due to dorsal metatarsal malunion.
Answer 5: Though Morton's neuroma can occur in the primary setting, it typically does not occur as a result of bunion surgery. Bunion surgery can result in painful incisional neuromas of the medial dorsal cutaneous nerve (terminal branch of the SPN), but the pain described in the question stem is more consistent with transfer metatarsalgia.
