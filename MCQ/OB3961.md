## Question

A 24-year-old laborer caught his foot in an industrial machine at work and sustained the injury illustrated in Figure A. A radiograph of the injured extremity is shown in Figure B. What is the blood supply of the tissue labeled (*) at baseline?

## Options

1. Medial plantar artery
2. Lateral calcaneal artery
3. Medial calcaneal branch of the posterior tibial artery
4. Artery of the sinus tarsi
5. Lateral calcaneal branch of the peroneal artery

## Correct option

**3. Medial calcaneal branch of the posterior tibial artery**

## Answer explanation

This patient has sustained a heel pad avulsion secondary to a high-energy injury. This injury is frequently associated with a compromised blood supply to the heel pad, which is primarily supplied by the medial calcaneal branch of the posterior tibial artery.

Heel pad avulsions, although rare, can be devastating injuries. The tight honeycomb-like structure of fibrous septa that typically secure the fat pad to the calcaneus and oppose migration, in addition to the specialized epidermal and dermal cells of its exterior make this region of the foot irreplaceable. As a result, heel pad avulsions are difficult to treat and salvage is often unsuccessful.

Libermanis, described successful revascularization and replantation of an avulsed heel pad in a 17 year old patient using the ipsilateral saphenous vein as a graft.

*Cichowitz et al. performed arterial studies on cadaveric specimens which demonstrated that the blood supply to the heel was primarily derived from the medial calcaneal branch of the posterior tibial artery.* **They also noted** that there was a minor contribution from the lateral calcaneal branch of the peroneal artery.

Figure A shows a complete heel pad avulsion, which extends in the proximal-to-distal direction. Figure B shows a lateral radiograph of the foot demonstrating the avulsed heel pad and minimal bony injury. Illustration A demonstrates the vascular supply to the heel, highlighting the major blood supply to the heel pad, the medial calcaneal branch of the posterior tibial artery. Illustration B exhibits the location of the cutaneous perforators (including the medial calcaneal branch of the posterior tibial artery) that supply the heel pad and the remainder of the hindfoot and posterior lower leg.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The medial plantar artery is one of the terminal branches of the posterior tibial artery given off under the flexor retinaculum and supplies the medial side of the foot.
Answer 2: The lateral calcaneal artery is a branch of the peroneal artery and is one of the three arteries (the other two being the lateral malleolar and lateral tarsal arteries) that provides blood supply to the lateral flap that is typically created during the standard lateral approach to the calcaneus.
Answer 4: The artery of the sinus tarsi is a branch of the perforating peroneal artery and along with the tarsal canal artery supplies the talus.
Answer 5: The lateral calcaneal branch of the peroneal artery makes a MINOR contribution to the blood supply of the heel.
