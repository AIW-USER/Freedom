## Question

A 12-year-old male presents with 6 months history of persistent right knee pain. He denies symptoms of infection, swelling or instability. Physical examination reveal an antalgic gait, as well as increased pain when the knee is forced into full flexion while in the supine position. Apart from activity modification at school, he has not received formal treatment for his knee pain. A radiograph of his knee is shown in Figure A. His growth chart is seen in Figure B. What would be the next best step in the management of this patient?

## Options

1. MRI of knee
2. Chest radiograph and full laboratory work-up
3. Dedicated hip and pelvic radiographs
4. Physiotherapy, patella sleeve brace and anti-inflammatory medication
5. Referral to child therapist

## Correct option

**3. Dedicated hip and pelvic radiographs**

## Answer explanation

The next best step in the management of this patient would be hip and pelvic radiographs. This would be required to rule out hip pathology, specifically slipped capital femoral epipysis (SCFE).

SCFE should always be considered in overweight adolescent patients presenting with knee pain. Physical examination findings suggestive of SCFE include antalgic gait, increased foot progression angle, obligatory external rotation during passive flexion of hip, and/or pain with hip flexion (hip flexion occurs secondarily when the knee is fully flexed with a patient lying in the supine position). The most important investigation should be radiographs of the hip and pelvis. Common radiographic findings would include blurring of proximal femoral metaphysis, epiphysis appearing posterior to the metaphysis, and/or epiphysiolysis.

*Matava et al. looked retrospectively at 106 patients referred for slipped capital femoral epiphysis (SCFE).* *15% of patients had distal thigh or knee pain or both as their chief complaint at presentation.* These patients tended to experience a longer delay to diagnosis, have high grade slips and more often require a proximal femoral osteotomy as part of their management.

*Kocher et al. examined factors for delay in diagnosis of slipped capital femoral epiphysis (SCFE).* **They found** that patients who present with primarily knee or distal-thigh pain, patients with Medicaid coverage, and patients with stable slips have longer delays in diagnosis of SCFE.

Figure A shows a normal knee X-Ray in a skeletally immature patient. Figure B shows a national standard growth chart for 'height-and-weight'. The adolescent boy is plotted in the 95th percentile for weight and 50th percentile for height. This would classify him as overweight.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: An MRI knee would be needed for suspected soft tissue or cartilaginous knee pathology. This patient my have chondromalacia patellae, but hip imaging should be ordered first.
Answer 2: Chest radiographs and full laboratory work up would be required if one was concerned of systemic disease or pathological findings on X-Ray.
Answer 4: Physiotherapy, patella sleeve brace and anti-inflammatory medication would be appropriate if this was confirmed knee pain. A full work up of the presenting complaint is necessary to make a diagnosis.
Answer 5: There are no concerning features for referral to a child therapist.
