## Question

A 12-month-old male presents with symmetric, progressive weakness that is more profound in lower extremity than upper extremity. On physical exam, he can sit but cannot walk. He has tongue fasciculations and absent deep tendon reflexes. Serum creatine phosphokinase and aldolase levels are normal. Which of the following genetic mutations or deletions is responsible for his condition?

## Options

1. Dystrophin gene
2. PMP22 gene
3. Androgen receptor gene
4. SMN-1 gene
5. Frataxin gene

## Correct option

**4. SMN-1 gene**

## Answer explanation

The child described has Type II spinal muscular atrophy (chronic Werdnig-Hoffman disease). The genetic mutation involves a deletion of the telomeric gene survival motor neuron 1 (SMN1) located on chromosome 5.

There are two copies of SMN genes (telomeric and centromeric). Spinal muscular atrophy involves telomeric deletion of SMN1 gene. The SMN protein is critical to RNA metabolism, and patients with SMA have a shortage of SMN protein, leading to motor neuron death. The severity of disease is then mitigated by the number of copies of SMN2 gene in each cell. Multiple copies of SMN2 gene allow for production of SMN proteins, reducing disease severity, and allowing the disease to present later in life.

*Sucato et al. examined spine deformity in SMA.* The type of scoliosis in SMA is a long, c-shaped, right sided thoracolumbar curve which is more flexible and progresses more rapidly than an idiopathic curve. Surgery is indicated for progressive deformity in spite of orthotics, and curves >50-60deg.

*Mesfin et al. review the types of SMA are differentiated by severity and age of presentation.* Type I presents at <6mths and patients die by 2years of age. Type II presents at 6-12mths and patients live to their 5th decade. Type III presents at 2-15yrs and patients have normal life expectancy.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The dystrophin gene, found on chromosome X, connects the cytoskeleton of muscle fibers to the surrounding ECM. A dystrophin gene mutation leads to Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy. Creatine phosphokinase levels (CPK-MM) are elevated. Deep tendon reflexes are present.
Answer 2: Duplication of the peripheral myelin protein 22 gene (PMP22) on chromosome 17 leads to Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease. This disease is characterized by cavovarus foot and claw toe deformities.
Answer 3: Spinobulbar muscular atropy involves mutation in the androgen receptor gene on chromosome X. The mutation is a trinucleotide (CAG) expansion.
Answer 5: Friedreich's ataxia involves a trinucleotide repeat expansion mutation of the frataxin gene, which reduces the amount of frataxin protein in cells. This protein affects normal mitochondria function.
