## Question

A 4 years old girl is brought to OPD by her parents with complaints of painful tilting of neck to one side for 3 weeks. On examination the girl also has facial asymmetry and change in voice. Her birth history reveals a normal vaginal delivery with no perinatal complications. Apart from that the young girl had an upper respiratory tract infection one month ago, past history is insignificant. The most likely diagnosis is:

## Options

1. Muscular torticollis
2. Grisel syndrome
3. Os odontoideum Pseudosubluxation of C 2 on C 3
4. Sandifer syndrome

## Correct option

**2. Grisel syndrome**

## Answer explanation

Grisel syndrome: This syndrome is characterised by atlanto axial dislocation / subluxation. Patients with this syndrome manifest with torticollis with a history of upper airway infection. Patients with this syndrome classically present with: Painful head tilt (Cook­Robin Look). The chin is turned towards one side while the neck is laterally flexed to the opposite side. These patients have limitations in neck mobility. Attempted corrective rotation causes pain. Children are frequently affected because they are more prone for adenoid inflammation. There may be associated rhinopharyngitis and secretory otitis media due to eustachean catarrh. This is actually a more dangerous complication of tonsillectomy. Sudden change in nasal resonance due to reduction in the size of nasopharynx caused by subluxation of atlantoaxial joint may be seen.

Treatment:

![[TO17133903-1.png]]

Type I Grisel syndrome: Soft collar

Type II Grisel syndrome: Hard collar

Type III Grisel syndrome: Halo fixation

Type IV Grisel syndrome: Open fixation This is resorted to when neurological complications arise.
