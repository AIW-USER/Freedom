Chapter: Regional Orthopedics MCQs — Snapping Hip Syndromes

## Question

Internal snapping of hip is common in (FNB Pattern Paper 2015)

## Options

1. Gymnast
2. Long distance runner
3. Cyclist
4. Dancers

## Correct option

**4. Dancers**

## Answer explanation

Answer: Snapping hip syndrome: Snapping hip syndrome (coxa saltans/dancer’s hip) is a rare disease and most commonly seen in ballet dancers and athletes requiring repetitive and extreme hip flexion and abduction. It can be intraarticular or extraarticular. Extra Articular type is more common and further divided into internal and external forms. In the internal type patients complain of pain and audible clicking sounds anterior to hip joint due to impingement of iliopsoas muscle or its tendon against iliopectineal eminence. External form is more common and patients complain of lateral pain and clicking sounds due to rubbing of iliotibial band (ITB) over the greater trochanter. Intra Articular snapping occurs due to lesions inside the joint, viz. labral tears and loose bodies. Diagnosis is mainly clinical but dynamic USG or MRI may be helpful in clinching the diagnosis of intra-articular snapping. Treatment is mostly conservative and involves NSAIDS, ice application and stretching of involved muscle or tendon. Arthroscopic loose body removal and labral repair is the treatment of choice in intra articular snapping. Some cases of extra articular snapping may require local corticosteroid injection into involved tendon sheath. Resistant cases may require surgical release and lengthening of iliopsoas tendon and ITB.
