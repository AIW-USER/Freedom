## Question

You are consulted to the emergency department to evaluate a 27-year-old male who sustained a fall from a 12-foot high obstacle during an extreme obstacle course. The patient's left ankle is swollen, and the injury is closed. Radiographs are obtained and available for review in Figures A and B. As part of the informed consent process, you go over the possible complications associated with this injury pattern and operative fixation. Which of the following best approximates the long-term rate of development of radiographic post-traumatic osteoarthritis following this injury?

![[q218948-b-lateral-ankle.jpg]]

## Options

1. <5%
2. 5-15%
3. 15-45%
4. 50-85%
5. 85-100%

## Correct option

**4. 50-85%**

## Answer explanation

This patient has sustained a "log-splitter" ankle fracture. This injury is associated with a significant risk for the development of post-traumatic osteoarthritis, which develops in approximately 54-84% of patients (Answer 4).

Ankle fractures are common injuries, which occur at an annual incidence of 187 per 100,000 adults. Risk factors for sustaining an ankle fracture include younger age, male sex, obesity, tobacco use, and alcohol consumption. The incidence of ankle fractures which occur secondary to high-energy mechanisms, however, is on the rise. *The "log-splitter" type of ankle fracture, defined as a trans-syndesmotic ankle fracture-dislocation in which the talus is violently embedded into the distal tibiofibular syndesmosis, was first described by Bible et al. in 2014.* Since then, a litany of research into this unique injury pattern has been performed. A grossly-unstable ankle fracture-dislocation, treatment is almost always operative. Secondary, however, to the high-energy nature of the injury, and the cartilage damage sustained at the time of injury, patients with "log-splitter" type fractures develop post-traumatic osteoarthritis at a rate of between 54-84%, even in the setting of anatomic reduction and stable internal fixation. Other less common complications following the operative treatment of these injuries include infection (15-17%) and nonunion (15-17%).

*Doherty et al. provide a review of the treatment and prevention of acute and recurrent ankle sprains.* *Their study ultimately included 46 papers.* **The authors note** that overall, in the treatment of acute ankle sprains, there is strong evidence for NSAIDs and early mobilization. **The authors also state** that proprioceptive training and bracing are supported in the prevention of chronic ankle instability.

*Zhang et al. provide a review of the "log-splitter" ankle fracture.* *Their study ultimately included 199 patients from 7 unique studies.* **The authors note** that the most common mechanisms of injury were fall from height (52.8%) and motor vehicle accidents (29.6%). **The authors conclude** that there is a lack of consensus regarding the diagnosis and optimal treatment paradigm for these injuries and that further research is necessary in order to optimize patient outcomes following this devastating injury.

*Wang et al. report on the treatment and outcomes of patients with "log-splitter" ankle fractures.* *Their study population consisted of 41 patients with a mean age of 41.46 years.* *The authors subdivided their patients into those with typical (axial load) and atypical (rotational stress) injury mechanisms.* **The authors note** that at an average final follow-up of 78.54 weeks patients in the atypical group had lower rates of osteoarthritis when compared to the typical group (54.5% atypical versus 84.2% typical); however, this complication was by far the most common when compared to others including nonunion (15.8% typical, 0% atypical) and infection (15.8% typical, 0% atypical).

*Sidhu et al. report on the injury characteristics, radiographic outcomes and factors influencing the rate of post-traumatic osteoarthritis in "log-splitter" ankle fractures.* *This was a retrospective study reporting on 27 adult patients treated at an academic level I trauma center.* **The authors note** that at one-year postoperative, 14/20 (70%) patients demonstrated signs of radiographic post-traumatic osteoarthritis. The reoperation rate was 51.9%. Overall, **the authors conclude** that a "log-splitter" injury should be considered separately from conventional ankle fractures and that patients should be counseled on the probability of chronic pain and the likelihood of additional surgical procedures.

Figure A demonstrates an AP radiograph of a left ankle with a trans-syndesmotic fracture-dislocation consistent with a "log-splitter" type injury. Figure B is a lateral radiograph of the injury demonstrated loss of tibiotalar congruity consistent with an ankle fracture-dislocation.

Incorrect Answers
Answers 1-3: the rate of infection and nonunion is approximately 15%.
Answer 5: the rate of posttraumatic osteoarthritis is approximately 50-84%.
