## Question

A 19-year-old male undergoes open reduction and internal fixation of his left ankle fracture shown in Figure A. Due to concern for injury to the syndesmosis, fixation is performed with a suture button construct depicted in Figure B. A repeat stress examination is performed and the surgeon notes persistent talar tilt on fluoroscopy. Which additional ligament injury is present?

## Options

1. Interosseus
2. Deltoid
3. Bifurcate
4. Calcaneofibular
5. Posterior talofibular

## Correct option

**2. Deltoid**

## Answer explanation

This patient is presenting with persistent talar tilt on stress examination following syndesmotic reconstruction, suggesting an incompetent deltoid ligament (Answer 2).

The tibiotalar articulation is stabilized by many ligamentous structures that act as restraints to rotational, translational, and axial motion. In a high ankle sprain, there is injury to the distal tibiofibular ligaments, including the anterior-inferior tibiofibular ligament (AITFL), posterior-inferior tibiofibular ligament (PITFL), interosseous membrane (IOM), interosseous ligament (IOL), and inferior transverse ligament (ITL). Compromise of these ligaments will cause destabilization of the syndesmosis and alter tibiotalar joint mechanics. In association with syndesmosis disruption, the deltoid ligament is often affected. The deltoid ligament is comprised of superficial and deep components, providing support to the medial ankle. The superficial deltoid functions to restrain ankle eversion, while the deep deltoid prevents excessive external rotation. Inadequate reduction of the syndesmosis can lead to chronic instability and increase the risk of post-traumatic arthritis.

*Massri-Pugin et al. performed a cadaveric study with differential transection of the distal tibiofibular and deltoid ligaments.* **The authors found** no significant syndesmotic instability after transection of an isolated distal tibiofibular ligament (either AITFL or PITFL). Diastasis at ONLY the posterior margin of the incisura was found when all distal tibiofibular ligaments were transected (AITFL, PITFL, and IOL). However, diastasis at BOTH the anterior and posterior incisura was noted only with complete transection of the distal tibiofibular AND deltoid ligaments.

*Wake et al. discuss implementing a standardized approach for surgical planning of syndesmosis repair, including consistent physical exam and radiographic workup.* Reconstruction should progress from proximal to distal, focusing on incompetent structures, starting with the IOL and AITFL. **The authors recommend** repairing the deltoid ligament if medial talar tilt or syndesmosis injury persists after AITFL reconstruction.

Figure A demonstrates a Weber C ankle fracture with associated high ankle sprain and syndesmosis disruption. Figure B is a schematic representing open reduction and internal fixation of the lateral malleolus with suture button use.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The interosseous ligament is presumably incompetent with this injury, but reconstruction was addressed by adding the suture button construct.
Answer 3: The bifurcate ligament, also called the Chopart ligament, stabilizes the subtalar and midtarsal joints. It does not contribute to the integrity of the syndesmosis.
Answers 4 & 5: The calcaneofibular and posterior talofibular ligaments comprise a portion of the lateral ligament complex. They do not contribute to the integrity of the syndesmosis.
