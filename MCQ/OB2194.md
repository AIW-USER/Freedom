## Question

A 13-year-old nonambulatory male with spinal muscular atrophy presents with his mother for evaluation of scoliosis. He is also complaining of progressive right hip stiffness. Physical exam reveals a flexible spine deformity, absent deep tendon reflexes in bilateral lower extremities, and limited passive range of motion of his right hip. Current radiographs demonstrate a 68º right thoracolumbar curve that has quickly progressed despite bracing therapy, and subluxation of his right hip. What is the next best step in management?

## Options

1. Posterior spinal fusion
2. Continued bracing
3. Surgical reduction of right hip
4. Anterior and posterior spinal fusion
5. Growing rod instrumentation

## Correct option

**1. Posterior spinal fusion**

## Answer explanation

This patient has an advanced, progressive scoliotic curve in the setting of spinal muscular atrophy. The best treatment option at this time is posterior spinal fusion.

Scoliosis is a very common manifestation of SMA, and tends to be progressive and refractory to conservative measures. Surgical intervention is indicated in patients with progressive curves of >30-40º that are not responsive to bracing. The primary goal of surgical correction of scoliosis in nonambulatory patients with SMA is to improve sitting balance and the ability to sit in a wheelchair, halt curve progression, and minimize restrictive lung disease. As this patient is nearing skeletal maturity and crankshaft phenomenon is not a concern, anterior fusion and growing rods are not indicated. The best treatment for this patient is posterior spinal fusion with instrumentation extending to the pelvis.

*Aprin and colleagues describe a series of patients with SMA undergoing posterior or anterior spinal fusions.* **They found** 87.5% of wheelchair-dependent subjects had improved sitting balance and were able to sit unsupported post-operatively. Despite objective functional outcomes improving in only 3 patients, 86% of all patients were happy with their outcome, as they no longer required their arms to support their trunk.

*Daher et al. also studied a series of patients with SMA undergoing posterior spinal fusion.* The average scoliotic curve corrected to 47º from 91º, a correction of 48%. Patients with preoperative pelvic obliquity showed a 5º loss of curve correction at follow-up as compared to subjects with balanced pelvises.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: This patient has already failed a trial of bracing therapy. Additionally, bracing has been shown to be largely ineffective in halting curve progression in patients with SMA.
Answer 3: Surgical attempts at reducing dislocated or subluxated hips in SMA patients have proven largely unsuccessful, with high recurrence rates and no differences in function.
Answer 4: This patient is nearly skeletally mature, so crankshaft phenomenon is not a concern. Additionally, patients with SMA are unlikely to require anterior fusion as their curves tend to be highly flexible.
Answer 5: Growing rods are not indicated, as this patient is nearly skeletally mature.
