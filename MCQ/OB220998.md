## Question

A 72-year-old patient presents to your clinic for evaluation of medial forefoot pain. He underwent surgery for hallux rigidus two years prior and, for the past six months, has had a gradual worsening of pain underlying the first metatarsal head. Physical exam reveals a fixed first metatarsophalangeal joint (MTPJ) with tenderness to palpation over the plantar surface overlying the hallux sesamoids. Which of the following procedures would most likely lead to this clinical presentation?

## Options

1. Dorsal cheilectomy with 10% of dorsal metatarsal head resection
2. Dorsal cheilectomy with 30% of dorsal metatarsal head resection
3. First MTPJ arthrodesis in 10 degrees of valgus in relation to the metatarsal shaft, 15 degrees of dorsiflexion in relation to the floor
4. First MTPJ arthrodesis in 5 degrees of valgus in relation to the metatarsal shaft, 30 degrees of dorsiflexion in relation to the floor
5. First MTPJ arthrodesis in 5 degrees of varus in relation to the metatarsal shaft, 15 degrees of dorsiflexion in relation to the floor

## Correct option

**4. First MTPJ arthrodesis in 5 degrees of valgus in relation to the metatarsal shaft, 30 degrees of dorsiflexion in relation to the floor**

## Answer explanation

Excessive dorsiflexion at the time of MTPJ arthrodesis increases plantar pressures under the first metatarsal head, which can present clinically as progressive plantar and sesamoid pain localized beneath the first metatarsal head (answer choice 4).

Hallux rigidus is degenerative osteoarthritis of the first metatarsophalangeal joint that commonly presents with pain and decreased range of motion, often worsened with dorsiflexion due to dorsal osteophyte impingement. Operative treatment depends on disease severity, with dorsal cheilectomy used in less severe disease (with up to 30% dorsal metatarsal head resection) and arthrodesis serving as the mainstay of treatment for advanced disease. First MTP arthrodesis alignment is typically planned to achieve approximately 5-10 degrees of valgus relative to the metatarsal shaft, and 10-15 degrees of dorsiflexion relative to the floor—dorsiflexion position is often achieved using a footplate intraoperatively, aiming for approximately 5 mm of elevation of the pulp of the hallux. Malposition can produce various postoperative symptoms: varus alignment may cause medial hallux irritation; excessive dorsiflexion may cause sesamoid and plantar pain; excessive valgus may cause contact with the second toe and callosity; and inadequate dorsiflexion causes the tip of the toe to strike the ground during gait.

*Aas et al. reviewed 35 patients (39 first MTP fusions) treated for hallux rigidus at an average of 8 years of follow-up and evaluated clinical outcomes, radiographic fusion position, and plantar pressure distribution using in-shoe sensors.* **They reported** generally high satisfaction and pain relief, measured weight-bearing extension angles across a wide range, and found significantly increased local pressure under the pulp of the great toe in operated feet. **They concluded** that clinical outcome correlated only weakly with arthrodesis position and that intraoperative estimation of extension can be aided by measuring toe clearance from a flat board placed under the foot.

*Drago et al. reviewed a systematic approach to atraumatic first metatarsophalangeal joint pain and* **highlighted** hallux rigidus as a common etiology characterized by progressive pain and loss of motion related to dorsal osteophytes and altered joint mechanics. They described the first MTP fusion as a benchmark surgical treatment for severe compromise and emphasized that load transfer after fusion varies with final dorsiflexion position. **They concluded** that higher dorsiflexion angles increase plantar pressures under the first metatarsal head, while lower angles increase pressures beneath the hallux, underscoring the importance of fusion positioning when evaluating persistent plantar forefoot pain after arthrodesis.

Incorrect answer choices:
Answer choices 1 & 2: Dorsal cheilectomy with ≤30% of metatarsal head resection is a safe procedure and typically employed for early-stage disease with reliable results.
Answer choice 3: This is a reasonable arthrodesis position and would not be expected to cause postoperative complications.
Answer choice 5: Varus alignment may cause medial hallux irritation postoperatively but is not typically implicated in sesamoid pain.
