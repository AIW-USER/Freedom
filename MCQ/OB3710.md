## Question

## Answer explanation

This patient has sustained an undisplaced type III odontoid fracture. Rigid cervical collar immobilization is the most appropriate treatment.

Type III odontoid fractures occur extend into the body of C2 and often involves the C1-C2 facets. The risk of nonunion is low due to the good vascularity in this region of C2. Rigid collar immobilization for 6-12 weeks is first line treatment. If the fracture goes on to nonunion, posterior C1-C2 fusion is warranted.

*Koech et al. looked at type II fractures in elderly patients that were treated nonsurgically.* In patients treated with collars, fusion was noted in 50% and fracture stability was attained in 90% of cases. In patients treated with halo vests, fusion was noted in 37.5% and fracture stability was attained in 100% of cases. **They demonstrated** that nonoperative treatment of elderly patients is an acceptable option, and that stable fibrous union may be adequate in this group.

*Majercik et al. examined halo vest immobilization in elderly patients.* **They found** that elderly patients treated in halo vests had higher mortality (40%) than young patients (2%), in spite of younger patients having greater injury severity. Elderly patients treated with halo vest immobilization also had higher mortality than those treated with surgery or cervical collar immobilization.

Figure A comprises coronal and sagittal CT images of a Type III odontoid fracture.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: There is no advantage of halo vest immobilization (Figure B) over rigid cervical collar immobilization. Halo vests are poorly tolerated in the elderly and barrel-chested patients with underlying lung disease.
Answer 2: Posterior C1 lateral mass-C2 pedicle fixation (Figure C) is an option for fixation of type III fractures that have gone on to nonunion. They are also indicated for fixation of displaced type II fractures in young patients, or in elderly patients (displaced and undisplaced Type II) who are surgical candidates.
Answer 3: Posterior transarticular screw fixation (Figure D) is an option for fixation of displaced type II fractures in young patients, or in elderly patients (displaced and undisplaced) who are surgical candidates.
Answer 5: Anterior odontoid screw fixation (Figure F) is an option for Type II fractures with favorable configuration (oblique or transverse, perpendicular to screw trajectory, corresponding to Grauer Types A and B).
