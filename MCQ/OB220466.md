## Question

A multicenter, randomized controlled trial compared two rehabilitation protocols (Protocol A and Protocol B) following arthroscopic rotator cuff repair in a large cohort of patients. At 12 months postoperatively, both groups demonstrated statistically significant improvements from baseline in the American Shoulder and Elbow Surgeons (ASES) score (p<0.01 for both). The mean improvement in the ASES score for Protocol A was 22 points, while the mean improvement for Protocol B was 27 points. The p-value for the difference between the two groups was 0.03. A systematic literature review reported a range of 10 to 15 points for the Minimal Clinically Important Difference (MCID) of the ASES score in this patient population. Considering the reported MCID for the ASES score with respect to arthroscopic rotator cuff repair, which of the following conclusions is MOST appropriate regarding the clinical implications of these study results?

## Options

1. Both rehabilitation protocols resulted in clinically meaningful improvements, and Protocol B should be preferentially adopted due to its statistically superior outcome.
2. While Protocol B demonstrated a statistically significant advantage, the 5-point difference between the protocols may not represent a clinically meaningful difference for most patients.
3. Neither rehabilitation protocol achieved a clinically meaningful improvement, as the mean changes in ASES score for both groups exceeded the upper limit of the reported MCID range.
4. The statistically significant difference between the groups definitively indicates that Protocol B provides a clinically relevant benefit that warrants its widespread implementation.
5. Further research on patient-reported satisfaction rather than the ASES score is needed to determine the true clinical impact of these rehabilitation protocols.

## Correct option

**2. While Protocol B demonstrated a statistically significant advantage, the 5-point difference between the protocols may not represent a clinically meaningful difference for most patients.**

## Answer explanation

Although Protocol B showed a statistically significant improvement over Protocol A, the magnitude of this difference (5 points) is smaller than the established MCID range and suggests that patients may not perceive a clinical difference between treatments.

This scenario highlights the critical need to differentiate between statistical significance at the group level and clinical significance at the individual patient level, as defined by the MCID. While Protocol B achieved a statistically superior mean outcome, the 5-point difference falls below the established threshold for a clinically meaningful change. This implies that, despite the statistical finding, the average patient may not experience a noticeable benefit from Protocol B compared to Protocol A that would necessitate a change in practice, especially considering potential differences in cost, complexity, or accessibility of the protocols.

*Maredupaka et al. reviewed the MCID of commonly used patient-reported outcome measures in total knee arthroplasty and reinforced the broader principle of MCID by examining its application to various patient-reported outcome measures.* **They noted** the importance of understanding the context-specific MCID values for different outcome tools and patient populations when interpreting research results. **They concluded** that the MCID is a crucial metric for assessing the real-world impact of interventions across different orthopaedic conditions and outcome measures.

*Bloom et al. reviewed the MCID and its role in bridging the gap between statistical findings and practical clinical implications in sports medicine research.* **They noted** that a statistically significant result does not automatically equate to a meaningful benefit for patients, and the MCID provides a patient-centered threshold for interpreting the relevance of observed changes. **They concluded** that the critical role of the MCID is in translating research findings into clinically meaningful insights, ensuring that treatment decisions are guided by outcomes that patients perceive as beneficial.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: While both showed improvements exceeding the lower end of the MCID range, the difference between them may not be clinically meaningful.
Answer 3: Both protocols demonstrated mean improvements well above the reported MCID range, indicating likely clinical benefit from both.
Answer 4: Statistical significance does not guarantee clinical significance, and the magnitude of the difference must be considered in relation to the MCID.
Answer 5: While patient satisfaction is important, the ASES score is a well-validated outcome measure, and the MCID provides a benchmark for its clinical relevance.
