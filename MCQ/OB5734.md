## Question

A 28-year-old female has prolonged labor during a spontaneous vaginal delivery. The child is born with a brachial plexus palsy which gradually recovers. At age 6, his physical examination reveals active elbow range of motion from 35 - 130 degrees. What is the recommended initial treatment for his flexion contracture?

## Options

1. Biceps tenotomy
2. Three weeks in a long-arm cast
3. Subscapularis slide procedure
4. Serial nighttime elbow extension splinting
5. Open anterior capsular release of the elbow

## Correct option

**4. Serial nighttime elbow extension splinting**

## Answer explanation

The patient has developed a 35 degree elbow flexion contracture in the setting of a prior brachial plexus birth palsy. The recommended initial treatment for elbow flexion contractures <40 degrees after brachial plexus birth injury is serial extension splinting.

Brachial plexus birth palsy is the result of birth trauma affects the plexus trunks and causes differential neuromuscular deficits and resultant joint contracture. Risk factors include shoulder dystocia, breech delivery, foreceps delivery, and prolonged labor. Elbow flexion contracture is thought to be caused by persistent triceps weakness from C7 root injury with relatively normal biceps strength. The treatment of elbow contracture is dependent upon its severity. Contracture of <35 degrees can be treated with serial nighttime extension splinting, >40 degrees can be treated with serial casting. Persistent contractures can be surgically managed with anterior capsular release and concomitant lengthening of the biceps and brachialis tendons.

*Ho et. al performed a retrospective review of their institutional brachial plexus injury database and* **found** patients with elbow flexion contractures related to post-obstetrical plexopathy. **They recommended** a treatment protocol which involved stretching and serial nighttime splinting for 20-40 degree contractures, and the use of serial weekly plaster or fiberglass casting for >40 degree contractures.

*Sheffler et. al. performed a retrospective review of patients brachial plexus birth palsy and* **found** an incidence of elbow flexion contracture of 48% which increases with patient age. In this study, patients with contracture 30 degrees or less were treated with serial nighttime splinting. This did not improve the magnitude of deformity but did significantly decrease its risk of progression.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Biceps tenotomy is not indicated as first line treatment for mild elbow flexion contracture
Answer 2: Static casting for three weeks is not indicated for elbow flexion contractures. Serial casting (fiberglass or plaster) is an option for severe elbow flexion contracture >40 degrees
Answer 3: Subscapularis slide procedure is a treatment option for internal rotation contracture of the shoulder after brachial plexus birth palsy
Answer 5: Open anterior capsular release is not indicated as first line treatment for mild elbow flexion contracture
