## Question

A 58-year-old male presents to your office for evaluation for TKA. He has failed conservative treatments including anti-inflammatories, physical therapy and corticosteroid injections. He is indicated for surgery, and has several questions pertaining to tourniquet use. Which of the following is true about the use of a tourniquet-less TKA?

## Options

1. Improved knee flexion at one year postoperatively
2. Longer operative times
3. Increased intraoperative blood loss
4. Lower rates of DVT
5. Less self reported pain in male patients

## Correct option

**3. Increased intraoperative blood loss**

## Answer explanation

TKA performed without the use of a tourniquet has consistently been shown to have increased blood loss compared to cases performed with a tourniquet.

There is much controversy about the use of a tourniquet during TKA. It's been reported that 90% of surgeons throughout the US and Europe utilize a tourniquet during TKA. The tourniquet allows for a drier field and potentially better visualization during surgery. Many patients, however, report significant pain postoperatively around the thigh. This has caused many surgeons to explore tourniquet-less TKA. While there are many conflicting reports in regards to pain and outcomes with or without the use of a tourniquet, increased intraoperative blood loss without the use of a tourniquet has been consistently reported.

*Kheir et al review pain and functional outcomes following tourniquet-less TKA.* **They report** improved pain scores in females undergoing tourniquet-less TKA, however, no difference was found in male patients. Further, **they report** less intraoperative blood loss with the use of a tourniquet. **They conclude** that avoiding tourniquet use during TKA for females may be a relatively risk-free adjunct to minimize opioid consumption during hospitalization.

*Alcelik et al review outcomes with and without a tourniquet in total knee arthroplasty.* **They report** no difference in surgery duration, deep vein thrombosis, pulmonary embolus, and minor/major complications. They did, however, report a lower intraoperative blood loss with the use of a tourniquet. **They conclude** that tourniquets are beneficial during TKA.

*Goel et al review tourniquet use in TKA.* **They report** an increase in blood loss and a more difficult time with surgical field visualization in the non-tourniquet group compared to those in which a tourniquet was utilized. **They conclude** that tourniquet use during TKA significantly decreases blood loss and does not adversely affect early postoperative outcomes.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: No difference has been shown in knee flexion measured at 12 months between patients undergoing TKA with or without a tourniquet.
Answer 2: Operative times have not shown to be statistically different when comparing based on tourniquet use.
Answer 4: Rates of DVT following TKA do not differ with or without the use of a tourniquet.
*Answer 5: While female patients have been shown to have less pain without the use of a tourniquet, Kheir et al reported no difference in pain scores with or without the use of a tourniquet in male patients.*
