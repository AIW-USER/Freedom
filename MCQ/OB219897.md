## Question

A 64-year-old female with left knee osteoarthritis is referred to your clinic. She has a body mass index (BMI) of 47 kg/m2, diabetes, and had open heart surgery this past year. She has previously had steroid injections that provided her temporary relief. She is currently trying to lose weight to improve her risk profile. Your clinic offers radiofrequency ablation (RFA). What is most true of this modality?

## Options

1. Contraindicated in patients with diabetes
2. Inferior treatment benefit compared to hyaluronic acid injections
3. Superior treatment benefit compared to corticosteroid injections
4. Unproven treatment benefit
5. Unsafe risk profile

## Correct option

**3. Superior treatment benefit compared to corticosteroid injections**

## Answer explanation

Radiofrequency ablation (RFA) is a promising nonoperative treatment of knee osteoarthritis (OA). Multiple randomized controlled trials (RCTs) have shown RFA to have superior outcomes compared to corticosteroid injections.

Thermal radiofrequency ablation (RFA) is the application of heat to ablate the superior lateral, superior medial, and inferior medial genicular sensory nerve branches around the knee to reduce the pain associated with OA. RFA is a safe and effective treatment for knee OA. Geniculate nerve RFA can be used in patients with contraindications to NSAID use, such as diabetes, renal disease, cardiovascular risk, and gastrointestinal risks.

*Chen et al. performed a systematic review on thermal nerve radiofrequency ablation (RFA) for the nonsurgical treatment of knee osteoarthritis.* *They included seven randomized controlled trials in their review.* **They found** that geniculate nerve RFA to provide statistically significant outcome improvement compared with control or sham procedures regarding pain, function, quality of life, and composite scores. When compared with corticosteroids and hyaluronic acid, geniculate nerve RFA also provided notable improvement in pain, function, and composite scores. **They concluded** with geniculate nerve thermal RFA to be a superior nonsurgical treatment of knee OA compared with NSAIDs and intra-articular corticosteroid injections.

*Zhang et al. performed a systematic review and meta-analysis looking at non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID) induced acute kidney injury (AKI).* **They found** that in the general population, the pooled odds ratio (OR) of AKI for current NSAID exposure was 1.73 (95%CI 1.44 to 2.07). While in people with chronic kidney disease (CKD), individual study OR of AKI due to current NSAID exposure ranged from 1.12 to 5.25, with pooled estimate OR 1.63 (95% CI 1.22 to 2.19). When looking particularly at the elderly, **they found** a higher risk of AKI (OR 2.51, 95%CI 1.52 to 2.68).

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: RFA is not contraindicated in patients with diabetes.
Answer 2: RFA has superior benefit in comparison to hyaluronic acid injections.
Answer 4: RFA has been shown in multiple RCTs to have significant clinical benefit in the nonoperative treatment of knee OA.
Answer 5: RFA has a safe risk profile.
