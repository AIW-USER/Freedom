## Question

A 68-year-old female presents to your office with complaints of right shoulder pain. She denies any specific trauma but reports over the last several months she has had increasingly significant pain over the anterolateral aspect of the shoulder that is worse with overhead activities. She does report some weakness in certain positions but pain is her primary complaint at this time. On examination, she achieves 160° of active range of motion when forward flexing her right upper extremity. She has a positive empty can test, pain with resisted external rotation with the arm adducted, and a negative Hornblower's test. Supraspinatus and infraspinatus strength are 4/5, while subscapularis strength is 5/5. She is diagnosed with a chronic rotator cuff tear and does not wish to be treated surgically. Which of the following is true regarding the nonoperative treatment of chronic rotator cuff tears?

## Options

1. Partial thickness tears progress more rapidly and have higher degrees of fatty atrophy
2. Linear correlation exists between size of tear and rate of tear progression
3. Symptoms develop within 3 years in 20% of asymptomatic patients with a known tear
4. 10-15% of full-thickness tears increase in size by 2 years
5. Development of pain does not correlate well with progression of tear

## Correct option

**2. Linear correlation exists between size of tear and rate of tear progression**

## Answer explanation

Larger rotator cuff tears tend to continue to progress in size at a higher rate than smaller tears.

Rotator cuff tears are one of the most common orthopedic shoulder conditions seen and tend to occur increasingly as patients age due to chronic degeneration of the rotator cuff tendons. Symptoms include pain over the lateral shoulder with overhead activities that tends to cause difficulty sleeping at night. Approximately 30% of those >60 years old have a full-thickness tear with that number increasing to 65% at >70 years old. In those who have asymptomatic tears, a tear tends to progress in about 1/3 of cases within 2-3 years. In those with symptomatic tears, progression is linked to the size of the tear, and increases in pain can be used as a marker for progression. Studies have shown that about 50% of full-thickness tears increase in size by 5 years.

*Maman et al. used MRI to look at the outcomes of nonoperative management for the treatment of rotator cuff tears.* **They noted** that 48% of tears followed for >18 months progressed in size, while only 19% of those followed for <18 months progressed in size. **They also noted** that 17% of patients <60 years old saw progression, while this occurred in 54% of patients >60 years old. **They concluded** that factors associated with tear progress included age >60, full-thickness tearing, and fatty infiltration of the muscle.

*Safran et al. looked at the natural history of symptomatic rotator cuff tears treated non-operatively in those <60 years old.* *At 2-3 years of follow-up, 49% of full-thickness tears had increased in size, and 25% of shoulders that were initially intact had a newly identified rotator cuff tear.* **They noted** that patients who had more pain on follow-up ultrasound were more likely to have had progression in the size of their tear.

*Moosmayer et al. studied the natural history of asymptomatic rotator cuff tears over 3 years in 50 patients, with 18/50 (36%) developing symptoms during the follow-up period that correlated with an increase in tear size.* Additionally, in the group that was newly symptomatic, there was a higher degree of muscle atrophy and concomitant biceps pathology.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Full-thickness tears progress more quickly and have a higher degree of atrophy given that their tendinous insertion is compromised.
Answer 3: Between 30-50% of asymptomatic rotator cuff tears confirmed on imaging become symptomatic over a 2-3 year time period.
Answer 4: The size of a full-thickness rotator cuff tear tends to increase in approximately 20-50% of patients around 2 years, with > 50% having progression at 5 years.
Answer 5: Patients who display increasing degrees of pain at follow-up have been shown to have a greater degree of tear progression.
