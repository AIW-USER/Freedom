## Question

Historically, ACL reconstructions were performed using an "over-the-top" position where the graft was placed around the posterior aspect of the lateral femoral condyle rather than drilling a femoral tunnel. What effect might such graft positioning have on the tension observed in the graft?

## Options

1. Tight in flexion and tight in extension
2. Tight in flexion and lax in extension
3. The graft will remain isometric
4. Lax in flexion and lax in extension
5. Lax in flexion and tight in extension

## Correct option

**5. Lax in flexion and tight in extension**

## Answer explanation

An "over-the-top" position results in a graft that is lax in flexion and tight in extension. This is due to the fact that femoral portion of the graft is affixed in a position that is much more posterior than the anatomic origin of the ACL.

While the over-the-top position is largely historical, this technique has been advocated for use in pediatric ACL reconstructions to avoid transphyseal drilling of the femoral tunnel. The resulting excessively posterior position of the graft on the femur relative to the native origin of the ACL, however, may result in anisometric cycling, with the graft becoming lax in flexion and taut in extension. This may, in turn, lead to increased graft laxity and risk of failure. Modern ACL reconstruction techniques have been developed for the pediatric patient in order to avoid physeal injury and potential growth disturbance while restoring knee stability and kinematics.

*Jeremy et al. review ACL reconstruction in the skeletally immature athlete.* *The authors discuss the special surgical considerations to include growth disturbance in this patient population and describe the current physeal-sparing all-epiphyseal, partial transphyseal and complete transphyseal surgical techniques.* *Partial transphyseal techniques such as the transtibial over-the-top technique, described in the vignette, have been found to avoid any significant growth disturbance but at the expense of mixed results to include increased graft laxity and generally inferior functional outcomes at long-term follow-up.*

*Andrews et al. evaluated the outcomes following partial transphyseal ACL reconstruction in skeletally immature athletes.* *The authors used fascia lata or Achilles tendon allograft with a transtibial over-the-top technique.* *At an average 5-year post-operative follow-up,* **they found** nearly 40% of patients to have >3mm of clinical laxity with 13% reporting poor results. **They reported** no significant limb-length discrepancy and concluded that this represented a viable option in the skeletally immature athlete.

*Zantop et al. review the structural and biomechanical properties of the ACL.* *The authors describe the functional tensioning of the anteromedial (AM) and posterolateral (PL) bundles as well as their respective contributions to anterior laxity and rotatory stability.* **They note** the while the center of the origin of the AM bundle is close to the over-the-top position, that of the PL bundle is closer to the anterior and inferior chondral margin.

*Markolf et al. characterized the effect of femoral tunnel placement on knee laxity and resulting forces on the ACL graft.* **The authors found** that varying the placement of the graft by 1 hour from the standard 11 o’clock position did not affect knee laxity significantly but that anterior or posterior translation of the femoral tunnel by even 5.0mm did have a significant effect. **They concluded** that anteroposterior positioning of the graft was more important to laxity and restoring normal graft force than the clock face position.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: A tibial tunnel that is too anterior will result in a graft that is tight in flexion and may impinge on the notch in extension.
Answer 2: A femoral tunnel that is too anterior will result in a graft that is tight in flexion and lax in extension.
Answer 3: Anatomic ACL reconstruction results in the most isometric graft. The over-the-top position is non-anatomic, therefore the isometry of the graft is compromised.
Answer 4: Generally graft tunnel position will not result in a graft that is lax in both flexion and extension. This may result from improper graft tensioning (i.e. an under-tensioned graft).
