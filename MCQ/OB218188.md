## Question

A patient is 2 years status post left total knee arthroplasty (TKA) done with standard instrumentation. He presents to the clinic for evaluation for a right TKA. He is satisfied with the left side but has read about custom cutting guides (CCGs) and requests the use of this technology for his upcoming right knee. If the patient undergoes a right TKA with custom cutting guides, how will his outcomes differ from his left knee?

## Options

1. Improved alignment, better outcome scores
2. Improved alignment, no change in outcomes
3. Increased range of motion, no change in outcomes
4. Decreased range of motion, decreased outcome scores
5. No difference in range of motion, alignment, or outcome scores

## Correct option

**5. No difference in range of motion, alignment, or outcome scores**

## Answer explanation

Custom cutting guides (CCGs; often referred to as patient-specific instrumentation or PSI) in total knee arthroplasty (TKA) have not been shown to significantly improve patient outcomes including range of motion, alignment, or outcome scores (answer 5).

The use of custom cutting guides is a newer technique in attempts to further improve the clinical success of total knee arthroplasty. CCGs use preoperative 3-D imaging to manufacture patient-specific cutting blocks unique to the patient's anatomy. Theoretically, PSIs could decrease operative time, provide the ability to preoperatively plan for component size, and improve alignment for patients. However, the only benefit that has been born out in literature is the use of fewer instrument trays required to perform the procedure.

*Nam et al. performed a retrospective study comparing the outcomes of 95 patients who underwent a standard TKA and 95 patients who underwent a TKA with the use of CCGs.* *At a 2-year follow-up, the study showed no differences in range of motion (ROM), outcome scores, or postoperative alignment between the two cohorts.* **The authors concluded** that the use of CCGs does not provide a clinical advantage over standard TKA instruments.

*Thienpont et al. performed a systematic review and meta-analysis of 16 studies reporting alignment outcomes in TKA using PSI.* *The study showed no significant difference in overall mechanical alignment with PSI versus standard TKA instrumentation.* *The authors of this study concluded that CCGs/PSI do not improve the alignment of components relative to conventional TKA instruments.*

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: CCGs have not proven to improve alignment or outcome scores.
Answer 2: CCGs have not been shown to improve alignment or outcome scores.
Answer 3: There has not been a proven increase in ROM in patients after TKA with CCGs.
Answer 4: There has not been a proven difference in ROM or outcome scores in patients after TKA with CCGs.
