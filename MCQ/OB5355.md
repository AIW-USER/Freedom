## Question

Figures A and B exhibit radiographs of a 2-year-old boy who is healthy with normal milestones. What is the most commonly associated complication?

![[q5355-a-figure-a.jpg]]
*Figure A*

![[q5355-b-figure-b.jpg]]
*Figure B*

## Options

1. Persistent foot deformity
2. Limb length discrepancy
3. Fibular hemimelia
4. Frequent insufficiency fractures
5. Neurofibromatosis

## Correct option

**2. Limb length discrepancy**

## Answer explanation

The radiographs exhibit posteromedial tibial bowing.

Posteromedial tibia bowing typically does not require bracing or serial casts. Surgery is not indicated for correction of the bowing as most deformities correct over time. However, residual limb-length discrepancies may require treatment at a later age.

*Shah et al. retrospectively reviewed 20 patients with posteromedial bowing from birth until skeletal maturity,* **noting** that the majority of deformities resolved without intervention by the age of 4. The most rapid correction occurred at the level of the physis during the first year of life. The authors still recommended close follow-up into adolescence to identify and address any limb-length discrepancy, which was the most common deformity requiring intervention.

*Pappas followed 33 patients from birth to skeletal maturity and* **noted** the increased risk of a greater limb length discrepancy with a greater initial posteromedial bow at birth. Typically, the posterior bow disappears, but a mild medial bow remain for those who reach skeletal maturity without discrepancy.

Figures A and B are radiographs that exhibit posteromedial tibial bowing.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: Metatarsus adductus is not associated with posteromedial bowing, but forefoot adductus is a component of club foot
Answer 3: Fibular hemimelia is a associated with an anteromedial bowing
Answer 4: Frequent insufficiency fractures is associated with certain conditions such as osteogenesis imperfecta, or Ricket's
Answer 5: Neurofibromatosis is associated with anterolateral bowing.
