## Question

An 18-year-old male presents for evaluation of gradually worsening left thigh pain. A firm mass is palpated at the level of the femoral diaphysis, and the patient subsequently undergoes a core needle biopsy. An 11;22 chromosomal translocation is detected on fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH). Which of the following findings is most likely to be present on histology?

## Options

1. Numerous lobulated fat cells without cellular atypia
2. Osteoclast-like cells with multiple nuclei and hemosiderin laden macrophages in a background of stromal cells.
3. Sheets of small round blue cells that stain positively for CD99.
4. Stromal cells with high nuclear to cytoplasm ratio in a background of lacy osteoid
5. Uniform spindle cells with thin, wavy nuclei.

## Correct option

**3. Sheets of small round blue cells that stain positively for CD99.**

## Answer explanation

Ewing’s Sarcoma is associated with an 11;22 chromosomal translocation, and biopsy will demonstrate sheets of small round blue cells that stain positively for CD99 (Answer 3).

Ewing’s Sarcoma is one of the most common malignant primary pediatric bone tumors and presents frequently in the metadiaphysis of long bones. Patients can present with chronic pain, night pain, erythema, swelling, weight loss, and fever. Biopsy of suspected masses will yield sheets of small, round blue cells that stain positively for CD99. Further molecular analysis using FISH or PCR will demonstrate an 11:22 chromosomal translocation in 85%-95% of cases, which encodes the EWS-FLI1 fusion protein.

*Nakano and Takahashi reviewed the role chromosomal translocations have in soft tissue sarcoma and the current state of genetic therapies targeted at these abnormalities.* *They cover the most frequently observed chromosomal translocation in specific sarcoma diagnoses and the role of the new fusion protein in promoting oncogenesis.* *They then discuss the technologies used to detect chromosomal translocations (i.e., fluorescence in situ hybridization, polymerase chain reaction, next-generation sequencing) and specific interventions for translocation-related sarcomas, such as trabectedin, an alkylating agent, and CRISPR-Cas9 genetic editing technologies.* **They conclude** that continued technological advancement has yielded further information about translocations involved in the development of sarcomas and potential avenues for targeted intervention.

*May et al. performed a basic science study assessing the impact of the EWS-FLI1 fusion protein/11;22 chromosomal translocation characteristic of Ewing Sarcoma.* **They found** that transducing NIH 3T3 cells with EWS-FLI1 induced rapid growth, confirming its oncogenic potential. Additionally, deletion of the EWS or FLI1 domains precluded the transformation of 3T3 cells, indicating that the oncogenic potential of the 11;22 translocation is directly related to the formation of a fusion protein between these two domains. **They concluded** that this example of chimeric proteins formed secondary to chromosomal translocations suggests that other similar mechanisms may be present in different pathologies.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: This histologic finding is most consistent with a lipoma, which is not associated with an 11;22 chromosomal translocation.
Answer 2: This histologic finding is most consistent with a giant cell tumor, which is not associated with an 11;22 chromosomal translocation.
Answer 4: This histologic finding is most consistent with osteosarcoma, which is not associated with an 11;22 chromosomal translocation.
Answer 5: This histologic finding is most consistent with a peripheral nerve sheath tumor, which is not associated with an 11;22 chromosomal translocation.
