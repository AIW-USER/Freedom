## Question

A healthy 34-year-old male presents to the trauma bay following a MVC. Radiographs reveal a displaced femoral neck fracture with no other injuries. The decision is made to proceed with open reduction and internal fixation (ORIF). Which of the following variables has the greatest impact on optimizing outcomes and function in this patient?

## Options

1. Approach used
2. Presence of medical comorbidiites
3. Time to reduction
4. Type of fixation
5. Quality of the reduction

## Correct option

**5. Quality of the reduction**

## Answer explanation

A femoral neck fracture in a young patient (<50 years old) should be treated urgently. However, the most important factor in optimizing outcomes and function with ORIF is the quality of the reduction.

Femoral neck fractures are common injuries to the proximal femur associated with increased risk of avascular necrosis and high levels of patient morbidity and mortality. Treatment is generally operative with open reduction and internal fixation (ORIF) versus arthroplasty depending on the age of the patient, activity demands and pre-injury mobility. Indications for ORIF include displaced fractures in young or physiologically young patients; ORIF is indicated for most patients <50 years of age. The reduction method and quality have a more pronounced effect on healing than surgical timing.

*Chan et al. reviewed femoral neck fractures in young patients.* **They report** that the quality of reduction, more than time to surgery, has the most impact on optimizing outcomes and function. **They conclude** that femoral neck shortening and varus collapse are the most common challenges of current fixation options.

*Swart et al. reviewed treatment with ORIF or arthroplasty for a displaced femoral neck fracture in a patient ≤65 years old using a decision analytic model.* **They report** that THA was found to be a cost-effective option for a displaced femoral neck fracture in an otherwise healthy patient who is >54 years old, a patient with mild comorbidity who is >47 years old, and a patient with multiple comorbidities who is >44 years old; clinical outcomes of THA and ORIF were otherwise similar for patients 40 to 65 years old. Notably, for all ages and cases, hemiarthroplasty was associated with worse outcomes and higher costs. **They concluded** that compared with ORIF, primary THA can be a cost-effective treatment for displaced femoral neck fractures in patients 45 to 65 years of age, with the age cutoff favoring THA decreasing as the medical comorbidity and risk of ORIF fixation failure increase.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The approach used for ORIF has not been shown to have a significant impact on the outcomes and function following a femoral neck fracture in a young patient
Answer 2: The presence of medical comorbidities may impact the decision to proceed with ORIF or arthroplasty, but has not been shown to have a greater impact on outcomes and function than quality of the reduction when proceeding with ORIF
Answer 3: Time to surgery is controversial. However, the reduction method and quality has a more pronounced effect on healing than surgical timing
Answer 4: Type of fixation may provide additional benefits such as compression of the fracture, but has not been shown to have a greater impact on outcomes and function than quality of the reduction
