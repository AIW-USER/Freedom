## Question

A 25-year-old male presents to the sports clinic with a mass that has gradually increased in size over the past year. A physical exam revealed a mass lateral and distal to the knee joint and groin lymphadenopathy. MRI shows a heterogeneous 4 cm mass adjacent to the lateral plateau with low intensity on T1 and high intensity on T2. A core needle biopsy is performed and molecular pathology confirms a SYT-SSX1 fusion protein. A lymph node biopsy is positive, but chest CT shows no lung metastasis. What is the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) 8th Edition stage?

## Options

1. II
2. IIIA
3. IIIB
4. IVA
5. IVB

## Correct option

**4. IVA**

## Answer explanation

This patient presents with a synovial sarcoma with regional lymph node involvement. The AJCC staging for this presentation is IVA.

Synovial sarcomas are soft tissue sarcomas that typically present adjacent to joints. Soft tissue sarcomas most often metastasize to the lungs, but lymph node is the next most common location. The AJCC staging system was developed to establish a standard staging system to facilitate multidisciplinary care and help guide treatment options. As part of the ongoing development process of the AJCC staging system, the eighth edition was published in 2017 (Illustration A). The four factors that affect staging are tumor size, grade, regional lymph node metastasis, and distant metastasis. Any lymph node involvement automatically elevates the tumor to stage IVA. Any distant metastasis elevates the stage to IVB.

*Steffner et al. published a review covering the staging of bone and soft tissue sarcomas.* *They discuss both the Musculoskeletal Tumor Society [MSTS] and the AJCC staging systems.* **They note** that the MSTS has not changed since 1980, while the AJCC undergoes periodic multidisciplinary updates.

*Gazendam et al. published a clinical review of synovial sarcoma.* *They discuss the presentation, imaging, pathology, and treatment of the condition.* **They note** that synovial sarcomas do not have a higher rate of lymph node metastasis than other soft tissue sarcomas, and typically, only physical examination is needed to screen for lymph node involvement.

Illustration A shows the AJCC staging system [eighth edition] for soft tissue sarcomas.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1,2,3: Lymph node involvement automatically elevates the stage to IV
Answer 5: No distant metastases preclude this stage.
