## Options

1. Embolization
2. Laminectomy with posterior spinal fusion
3. External beam radiation
4. Anterior column reconstruction with posterior stabilization
5. Hemilaminectomy

## Correct option

**3. External beam radiation**

## Answer explanation

The patient is presenting with painful metastatic lesions to the lumbar spine without evidence of neurologic deficit or cord compression. The best treatment would be palliative external beam radiation to control pain.

Metastatic disease is the most common cause of cancer involving the spine. Common origins involve the breast, lung, thyroid, kidney and prostate carcinomas. In cases where there are no neurologic deficits radiation and chemotherapy is the preferred treatment. When there are signs of instability or signs of neural compression with deficits surgical treatment is considered.

*White et al. reviewed the treatment of metastatic disease to the spine.* **The authors stated** that the mainstay of treatment for painful metastatic lesions without neurologic compression is radiation with or without chemotherapy. Surgical treatment should be performed in patients with a life expectancy greater than 3 months, and those with nerve compression symptoms. Goals of surgical care are to reduce pain, protect neural structures, and greater independence of living.

*Cernoch et al. performed a retrospective study looking at blood loss and outcomes related to pre-operative embolization of several metastatic tumors to the spine.* **They found** that 33% of cases that were embolized involved renal cell carcinoma with no difference in intraoperative blood and plasma transfusion with embolization technique and average transfusion volume is 241 mL. **The authors concluded** that pre-operative embolization effective controls intraoperative blood loss and is a safe technique for tumor surgery of the spine.

Figure A is the lateral radiograph of the spine without any evidence of lytic lesions or abnormalities. Figure B is a T1 sagittal MRI of the lumbar spine with multiple hypointensities in the vertebral bodies consistent with metastatic lesions. Figure C is a T1 with contrast sagittal MRI of the lumbar spine with multiple intense lesion in the vertebral bodies and spinous processes consistent with metastatic disease. Figure D is a T2 sagittal MRI of the lumbar spine without evidence of neural compression. Figure E is the histology a breast adenocarcinoma.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: Embolization is preferred for patients with hypervascular tumors, such as renal cell carcinoma, before surgical treatment to reduce intraoperative blood loss.
Answers 2, 4, and 5: Surgical treatment of metastatic disease of the spine is preferred when there are neurologic deficits or there are signs of instability due to the tumor.
