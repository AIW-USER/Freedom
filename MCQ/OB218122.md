## Question

A 16-year-old ballet dancer comes to the clinic with a chief complaint of posterior ankle pain when in the "en pointe" position. She was diagnosed as having a symptomatic os trigonum two years prior, has tried non-operative management including anti-inflammatory use, activity modification, and physical therapy, and is now planning to undergo endoscopic resection. When counseling the patient and her mother, you discuss the possibility of permanent numbness after the procedure. Which of the following ankle arthroscopy portals depicted in Figure A places the sural nerve at the greatest risk?

![[q218122-a-screenshot-2023-07-26-at-11-27-31-am.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Portal 1
2. Portal 2
3. Portal 3
4. Portal 4
5. Portal 5

## Correct option

**5. Portal 5**

## Answer explanation

Portal 5 represents the posterolateral portal used during ankle arthroscopy. It places the sural nerve and short saphenous vein most at risk.

An os trigonum is a secondary ossicle that represents the separated posterolateral tubercle of the talus (Illustration A). It is present in 10-25% of people but is uncommonly symptomatic. Ballet dancers and patients performing activities in extreme plantarflexion may cause repetitive microtrauma to the ossicle, resulting in a stress fracture, which may be accompanied by FHL tendon tenosynovitis or entrapment. Treatment is typically conservative, but in recalcitrant cases, surgical excision can be performed, either through an open lateral approach or via posterior ankle arthroscopy. Resection occurs through the posterolateral ankle portal, which places the sural nerve most at risk.

*Hsu et al. provide a review article on extended indications for foot and ankle arthroscopy.* **The authors note** that arthroscopy of the posterior ankle, subtalar joint, and first metatarsophalangeal joint and tendoscopy can be used to address common foot and ankle ailments, with the potential benefits of decreased pain, fast recovery, and low complication rates. In terms of posterior ankle arthroscopy, **they conclude** that incorrect creation and instrumentation of the posterolateral portal in the prone approach is the most likely cause of sural nerve damage.

*van Dijk et al. published a review of advancements in ankle arthroscopy,* **noting** that important progress has been made in the last 30 years. **The authors conclude** that for posterior ankle pathology, the two-portal hindfoot endoscopic technique with the patient in the prone position provides excellent access to the posterior ankle compartment and to posteriorly located extra-articular structures.

Figure A represents the common arthroscopy portals used, and is labeled as follows: Portal 1 (Anteromedial), Portal 2 (Anterolateral), Portal 3 (Anterocentral), Portal 4 (Posteromedial), Portal 5 (Posterolateral). Illustration A is a lateral ankle radiograph depicting an os trigonum.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Portal 1 is the Anteromedial portal, which is the primary viewing portal for ankle arthroscopy and is placed medial to the tibialis anterior tendon and lateral to the medial malleolus. It places the saphenous nerve and vein at risk, as well as the tibialis anterior tendon if not placed carefully.
Answer 2: Portal 2 is the Anterolateral portal, which is placed just lateral to the peroneus tertius tendon and medial to the lateral malleolus. It places the dorsal intermediate cutaneous branch of the superficial peroneal nerve at risk.
Answer 3: Portal 3 is the Anterocentral portal, which is placed medial to EDL and lateral to EHL, but is not commonly used due to the significant risk to the dorsalis pedis artery.
Answer 4: Portal 4 is the Posteromedial portal, which is placed just medial to the Achilles tendon, and places the posterior tibial artery most at risk.
