Chapter: Arthritis MCQs — Sexually Acquired Reactive Arthritis

## Question

A 34-year-old patient with confirmed sexually acquired reactive arthritis (SARA) has been managed with continuous, maximum-tolerated doses of two different non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) over the past 14 weeks. Despite this, and following successful eradication of his initial urogenital infection, he continues to experience persistent, severe peripheral oligoarthritis restricting his mobility. He has no significant axial involvement or isolated, severe enthesitis. According to current step-up treatment algorithms for peripheral spondyloarthritis, what is the most appropriate next pharmacological intervention?

## Options

1. Immediate initiation of a TNF-alpha inhibitor (e.g., adalimumab), as conventional synthetic DMARDs lack efficacy for the peripheral manifestations of reactive arthritis.
2. Initiation of sulfasalazine, as it possesses the most established clinical evidence among conventional synthetic DMARDs for managing chronic peripheral reactive arthritis.
3. Transition to oral systemic corticosteroids at a dose of 1 mg/kg/day for 4 weeks to induce rapid remission before tapering.
4. Initiation of methotrexate, as it is considered superior to all other csDMARDs for both the axial and peripheral manifestations of the spondyloarthritis spectrum.

## Correct option

**2. Initiation of sulfasalazine, as it possesses the most established clinical evidence among conventional synthetic DMARDs for managing chronic peripheral reactive arthritis.**

## Answer explanation

Sulfasalazine is considered the first-line conventional synthetic disease-modifying antirheumatic drug (csDMARD) for patients with peripheral reactive arthritis who have failed continuous NSAID therapy and local intra-articular injections.

Answer: 2
