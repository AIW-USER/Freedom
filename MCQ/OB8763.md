## Question

A 65-year-old female returns to the office with continued medial and lateral hindfoot pain. She has attempted bracing, physical therapy and non-steroidal anti-inflammatories with limited relief. The patient is unable to perform a single-leg heel rise. The position of her right hindfoot and talonavicular joint are not passively correctable. She has pain with attempted inversion against resistance. Figures A-C are weight-bearing foot radiographs. Which of the following is the most appropriate treatment option?

## Options

1. Charcot resistant orthotic walker
2. Medializing calcaneal osteotomy, posterior tibial tendon debridement
3. Medializing calcaneal osteotomy, posterior tibial tendon debridement with flexor digitorum longus transfer
4. Medializing calcaneal osteotomy, lateral column lengthening, posterior tibial tendon debridement with flexor digitorum longus transfer
5. Talonavicular and subtalar arthrodesis

## Correct option

**5. Talonavicular and subtalar arthrodesis**

## Answer explanation

The patient has failed non-operative measures and has a rigid hindfoot deformity. Hindfoot arthrodesis is the most appropriate treatment option to relieve pain and correct the underlying deformity.

In patients with stage 3 Posterior Tibial Tendon Dysfunction (rigid hindfoot deformity), arthrodesis is the most accepted surgical treatment option. Traditionally this was performed with a triple arthrodesis through two incisions. Recent attention has focused on the correction of deformity with a fusion of only the subtalar and talonavicular joints, which can be performed via a single medial incision. In patients without substantial arthritic changes at the calcaneocuboid joint this is an acceptable treatment option.

*Saville et al. present a retrospective study of 18 patients with rigid hindfoot valgus deformity corrected via a medial approach.* *The authors achieved fusion in all but one patient with a modest correction of subtalar valgus from an average of 32 to 17 degrees.*

*Anand et al. present a similar retrospective study of 18 patients with hindfoot arthrodesis via medial approach.* **The authors reported** no wound complications with 1 nonunion and 2 malunions.

*Sammarco et al.* **found** a substantial improvement in alignment and AOFAS Hindfoot scores in 14 patients treated with a double arthrodesis through a medial approach. Similar to prior studies **the authors report** no wound complications.

Figures A-C demonstrate weight-bearing images of a patient with a rigid hindfoot deformity with significant hindfoot valgus

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: Charcot resistant orthotic walkers (CROW) are used for neuropathic patients with severe deformity
Answers 2-4: Joint sparing approaches are not typically utilized in patients with rigid hindfoot deformity. Although they may correct alignment they do not reliability relieve arthritic joint pain and have a high risk of loss of correction.
