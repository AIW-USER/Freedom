## Question

A 32-year-old male is involved in a motor vehicle collision where he is the restrained driver. He has to be extracted from the vehicle and his knees appeared to have directly impacted the dashboard. He complains of pain in the right hip upon arrival to the trauma bay. Radiographs and CT scans reveal an T-type acetabular fracture. He undergoes open reduction and internal fixation the following day through an extended iliofemoral approach and receives post-operative external beam radiation the same day. At his 3-month follow-up radiographs are obtained and there is already a noticeable amount of heterotopic ossification (HO) developing. Out of the answer choices listed, what is the most likely reason for HO development?

## Options

1. Fracture pattern
2. Surgical approach
3. Use of radiation postoperatively
4. Injury severity score (ISS) = 16
5. Debridement of the gluteus minimus muscle

## Correct option

**2. Surgical approach**

## Answer explanation

The risk of developing heterotopic ossification (HO) after acetabular fracture open reduction and internal fixation (ORIF) is increased with an iliofemoral approach.

Acetabular fractures occur most commonly in younger patients with traumatic accidents or elderly patients with ground-level falls. The type of fracture seen is dependent on the mechanism of injury incurred. Many of these fractures require ORIF, and complications such as post-traumatic osteoarthritis can still occur. Additionally, HO is a known complication after operative treatment of these fractures with a reported incidence of around 25%. Rates are highest with the use of the extended iliofemoral approach, and both indomethacin and external beam radiation have been evaluated in the past as methods for decreasing HO, with varying rates of effectiveness noted. Traditionally, debridement of the gluteus minimus muscle has been thought to decrease HO development but this has been more recently called into question.

*Firoozabadi et al. reviewed HO in acetabular fracture surgery for the JAAOS.* *They discuss different risk factors for the development of HO, which include head trauma, male sex, intra-articular debris, delay to surgery, and prolonged mechanical ventilation.* *They go on to discuss methods for prophylaxis including NSAIDS and radiation,* **noting** that radiation seems to have a stronger effect than NSAIDS in preventing HO development.

*Rath et al. looked at gluteus minimus necrotic muscle debridement and its effect on HO after acetabular ORIF.* **They note** that in 29 patients significant HO was only noted in 3 patients (10%). **They conclude** that the debridement of necrotic gluteus minimus muscle is safe and may be effective in reducing the degree of HO postoperatively.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: While posterior wall fractures have been linked to higher rates of post-traumatic arthritis, evidence is lacking to state one specific fracture pattern is responsible for higher rates of HO.
Answer 3: External beam radiation has been shown in some studies to decrease the rates of HO, although this is still somewhat controversial.
Answer 4: Elevated injury severity scores have not been linked to higher rates of HO after acetabular fracture ORIF.
Answer 5: Debridement of the gluteus minimus muscle would not lead to HO development. Some authors have suggested debridement may decrease HO rates, while others have noted the effects of debridement may be minimal.
