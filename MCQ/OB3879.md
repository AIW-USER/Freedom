## Question

A 33-year-old male reports a persistent 2-month history of forefoot pain and swelling after he stepped on a nail while jogging and wearing rubber-soled athletic shoes. He went to the emergency department and was given tetanus prophylaxis and the appropriate prophylactic antibiotics. Examination reveals a healed puncture wound under the plantar forefoot measuring 2 mm in diameter that is moderately tender. There is no erythema or fluctuance. Lab values are within normal limits. ## Options

1. Bone Scan
2. IV antibiotics
3. Expectant management
4. Percutaneous drainage and IV antibiotics
5. Surgical wound exploration, debridement, and IV antibiotics

## Correct option

**5. Surgical wound exploration, debridement, and IV antibiotics**

## Answer explanation

The MRI demonstrates an abscess caused by a retained foreign body. The next best step in treatment would be surgical wound exploration, debridement, and IV antibiotics.

Puncture wounds to the foot are relatively common injuries which may present weeks to months after the initial injury. The most common cause of soft tissue infection is S. aureus, while the most common cause of osteomyelitis is Pseudomonas. Following a puncture to the foot, a tetanus booster should be given along with coverage for Pseudomonas. A fluoroquinolone is usually given for this purpose. Indications for surgical debridement are delayed presentations with deep infection with or without osteomyelitis and foreign body removal. Postoperatively, IV antibiotics are given which may be converted to oral antibiotics after the clinical condition improves.

*Chang et al. reported on 8 cases of puncture wounds of the foot associated with rubber foreign bodies and resultant abscess formation.* **They report** that the patient's symptoms were only able to be resolved after foreign body removal and IV antibiotics. Only 3/8 patients were found to have osteomyelitis. **They conclude** that a history of wearing rubber-soled shoes at the time of injury should alert the provider of the possibility of a retained foreign body causing a soft tissue abscess.

*Saha et al. report a case of Pseudomonas osteomyelitis after a plantar puncture wound through a rubber sandal.* **They suggest** that there is something unique to the construction of an athletic shoe that predisposes to Pseudomonas colonization. **They report** that Pseudomonas grows in the moist inner layers of the sole of an actively used athletic shoe, whereas it does not grow in new or very old shoes. The microbe is then inoculated into the foot along with the rubber sole. **They conclude** that appropriate antibiotic therapy should be given and surgical treatment should be considered for patients with continued symptoms.

Figure A is a normal radiograph of a foot. Figures B and C are T1-weighted post-contrast coronal and axial MRI slices demonstrating an abscess with a retained foreign body. There is no abnormal signal within the bones.
 Illustrations A and B are T1 and T2-weighted sagittal MRI slices demonstrating osteomyelitis. There are exudates and edema within the medullary space which give a low signal on T1-weighted images and high signal on T2-weighted images. There is a sequestrum present within the femur.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: A bone scan is not indicated at this time as the patient has no indication of osteomyelitis on imaging.
Answer 2: IV antibiotics alone will not be sufficient in treating this patient's condition.
Answer 3: Expectant management is not appropriate as the patient has an abscess on imaging that will not resolve until decompression with foreign body removal has been performed.
Answer 4: A percutaneous stab incision will be too small to sufficiently explore the area and retrieve the foreign body.
