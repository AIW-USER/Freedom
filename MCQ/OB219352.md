## Question

A 34-year-old male presents to your clinic with complaints of pain over the anterolateral aspect of his knee that has developed over the last year. He states he has noticed a mass associated with the pain in this area and has paresthesias that travel into the dorsum of his foot. The mass is palpable and mobile just distal to the anterolateral joint line, and he reports it has gotten progressively larger over the last 6 months. There is no knee joint effusion and his knee ligamentous exam is stable. Radiographs are negative for fracture or calcified masses. A representative axial MRI sequence is shown in Figure A. What is the next best step in management to decrease recurrence?

*Figure A*

## Options

1. Activity modification and non-steroidal anti-inflammatories
2. Aspiration of cyst
3. Surgical decompression and excision of cyst
4. Proximal tibiofibular joint arthrodesis
5. Fibular head resection

## Correct option

**3. Surgical decompression and excision of cyst**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a proximal tibiofibular joint ganglion cyst with neurologic symptoms and should be managed with surgical decompression and cyst excision.

Ganglion cysts of the proximal tibiofibular joint are uncommon but typically noted more in middle-aged males and are thought to occur due to degeneration of this small joint. The cyst is mucin-filled without a true epithelial lining. When it becomes symptomatic, patients typically complain of pain and fullness, and a palpable mass can often be present distal to the lateral joint line which is usually mobile. Paresthesias may also be present as the cyst can compress the peroneal nerve. Radiographs are typically normal but MRI will show cystic material which is high signal on T2 sequences. In patients who are asymptomatic or newly symptomatic, observation, NSAIDS, and aspiration are the initial treatment. There is a high reported rate of recurrence with aspiraton alone (~25-80%), and in cases where nerve symptoms are present, surgical management with decompression and cyst excision is indicated.

*Gulati et al. reviewed the surgical treatment of recurrent proximal tibiofibular joint ganglion cysts.* *In this study, they treated recurrences with proximal fibular excision and noted good results in 9 patients.* **They concluded** that in cases where there are recurrences and/or progressive nerve involvement, surgical intervention should be indicated.

*Miskovsky et al. looked at 13 patients with proximal tibiofibular joint ganglion cysts and their outcomes with different treatments.* **They note** that first-time decompression and cyst excision was successful in avoiding recurrence in 7/8 patients; however, 3/3 patients who were undergoing repeat excision experienced another recurrence. Two patients underwent proximal tibiofibular joint fusion and did not have any episodes of recurrence. Their conclusion was that excision was successful overall but repeat excision was not effective and arthrodesis should be considered in this population.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1 & 2: Observation, NSAIDs, and cyst aspiration are first-line steps in management with a low rate of success. Aspiration has recurrence rates as high as 25-80%. This patient has associated neurologic symptoms which should be managed more aggressively with surgical intervention.
Answers 4 & 5: Proximal tibiofibular joint arthrodesis and fibular head resection are typically reserved for cases where there is a recurrence after decompression and cyst excision.
