## Question

A 34-year-old female undergoes open reduction internal fixation of a left posterior malleolar ankle fracture. The fracture involves 25% of the articular surface and is reduced and fixed through a posterolateral approach. There is a resultant 2mm articular step-off on postoperative imaging. The patient is seen at her two-week follow-up visit and asks about the risk of developing posttraumatic arthritis. Which of the following statements most appropriately characterizes the associated risk of arthritis after this injury and treatment?

## Options

1. Fracture involving 25% of the articular surface is protective against arthritis development
2. Fracture involving 25% of the articular surface is a risk factor for arthritis development
3. Postoperative articular step off of 2mm is protective against arthritis development
4. Postoperative articular step off of 2mm is a risk factor for arthritis development
5. The posterolateral approach is a risk factor for arthritis development

## Correct option

**4. Postoperative articular step off of 2mm is a risk factor for arthritis development**

## Answer explanation

Postoperative articular step off of ≥ 2mm is a risk factor for the development of ankle arthritis after open reduction internal fixation of posterior malleolus fractures.

Surgical fixation of displaced posterior malleolus fractures can restore stability and articular congruity of the ankle joint to maintain function and reduce the risk of posttraumatic arthritis. Indications for operative intervention have evolved, and fracture fragment size and articular displacement have often been cited as factors to consider when determining appropriate treatment options. Irrespective of fracture fragment size, articular step-off of 2mm or greater has been identified as an independent risk factor for the development of arthritis.

*Irwin et al. reviewed the evaluation and treatment of posterior malleolus fractures and* **noted** that exact indications for surgical intervention are unclear. Factors to consider include the fracture fragment size, articular impaction, comminution, and syndesmotic stability. **The authors noted** that fixation of posterior malleolus fractures can have many benefits, including the restoration of rotatory joint stability and articular congruity, and the prevention of posterior talar translation.

*Rammelt and Bartonicek also reviewed the management of posterior malleolus fractures and* **highlighted** the importance of obtaining an anatomic articular reduction when surgical treatment is utilized. Discussed benefits of an anatomic reduction included the facilitation of associated distal fibula fracture reduction, restoration of the incisura, and restoration of the posterior malleolus that could potentially obviate the need for additional syndesmotic fixation. The authors also discussed potential risk factors for subsequent arthritis, **noting** that there are mixed results within the literature regarding fracture fragment size and arthritis risk, with more recent studies finding no significant correlation. **They concluded** that the resultant articular step off of ≥2mm is an established risk factor independent of the fracture fragment size.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1,2&5: The recent literature has not established a clear association between fracture fragment size and the risk of arthritis. Displacement and articular incongruity are more closely associated with this risk.
Answer 3: ≥2mm of articular step-off is an independent risk factor for the development of arthritis.
