## Question

A 20-year-old female soccer player presents to your clinic with complaints of repeated patellar dislocations. She reports these episodes primarily occur during non-contact, twisting episodes while playing soccer. She denies any mechanical symptoms at this time but is having recurrent effusions. She has failed physical therapy. An MRI is obtained which demonstrates an MPFL rupture, TT-TG distance of 20mm, Insall-Salvati ratio = 1.15 and no signs of trochlear dysplasia. You discuss operative management with the patient and recommend MPFL reconstruction and Fulkerson osteotomy. If this procedure were performed, patellofemoral contact pressures would be most increased in which quadrants?

## Options

1. Distal & lateral
2. Distal & medial
3. Medial
4. Proximal & lateral
5. Proximal & medial

## Correct option

**5. Proximal & medial**

## Answer explanation

A Fulkerson osteotomy is an anteromedializing tibial tubercle osteotomy that incidentally increases patellofemoral contact forces medially and proximally.

Patellar instability is a complicated orthopedic condition which can lead to episodes of repetitive patellar subluxation/dislocation. Anatomic risk factors for recurrent instability include patella alta, trochlear dysplasia, femoral anteversion, genu valgum, and an increased TT-TG interval (tibial tubercle-trochlear groove). A TT-TG interval > 20mm is thought to excessively increase the Q-angle of the lower extremity which leads to inappropriate lateral force on the patella. There are a variety of tibial tubercle osteotomies that can be utilized to improve the line of pull. One of the primary osteotomies used is the Fulkerson osteotomy, which is an anteromedializing osteotomy. This leads to increased contact pressures in the medial and proximal quadrants of the patellofemoral joint and significant chondrosis in these areas can be a contraindication for the procedure.

*Ramappa et al. reviewed the effects of medializing and anteromedializing tibial tubercle osteotomies on patellofemoral mechanics.* With abnormally increased Q-angles, **they found** lateral patellar contact pressures were increased. **They noted** that a pure medializing tubercle osteotomy corrected lateral patellar contact pressures more than the anteromedializing osteotomy.

*Sherman et al. evaluated tibial tubercle osteotomies and the associated indications, techniques, and outcomes.* *They discuss the different versions of these tibial tubercle osteotomies and their evolution, up to the current anteromedializing Fulkerson osteotomy.* *They go into further detail regarding the surgical technique and clinical pearls.*

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1-3 & 5: Patellofemoral contact pressures are most increased medially and proximally after an anteromedializing tibial tubercle (Fulkerson) osteotomy.
