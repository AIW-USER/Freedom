## Question

A 68-year-old female presents to the ED after falling off of her exercise bike. Radiographs demonstrate a displaced femoral neck fracture. She is a community ambulator who does not use any assistive devices at baseline. You discuss treatment options with the patient, including total hip arthroplasty (THA) and hemiarthroplasty. Which of the following is true of THA compared to hemiarthroplasty in this scenario?

## Options

1. Increased stability
2. Lower risk of infection
3. Lower mortality
4. Longer hospital length of stay
5. Improved functional outcome

## Correct option

**5. Improved functional outcome**

## Answer explanation

Total hip arthroplasty (THA) has demonstrated modestly improved outcomes and functional scores compared to hemiarthroplasty in appropriately indicated patients with displaced femoral neck fractures.

Femoral neck fractures are extremely common in older patients as bone quality begins to diminish. Displaced femoral neck fractures in patients >50 years old are thought to be best treated with arthroplasty, but debate has persisted as to whether THA or hemiarthroplasty provides best results. Age cutoffs vary based on specific studies, but it is generally thought that patients aged <75-80 have better functional scores (i.e WOMAC) with THA compared to hemiarthroplasty. Rates of mortality and adverse events have been shown to be similar between THA and hemiarthroplasty. Conversely, hemiarthroplasty is more stable with dislocation rates around 2%, with THA dislocation rates closer to 5%.

*Bhandari et al. performed a prospective trial comparing hemiarthroplasty with THA in 1495 patients >50 years old.* The primary endpoint, a secondary procedure within 24 months, was no different in the hemiarthroplasty (8.3%) and THA (7.9%) groups. There was a modest improvement in WOMAC functional scores in the THA group, while there were no notable differences in mortality or serious adverse events.

*Florschutz et al. review general principles of femoral neck fracture management.* *They review surgical options depending on the classification of the fracture which include ORIF, hemiarthroplasty and THA.* **They note** that optimal management of femoral neck fractures is not entirely agreed upon among orthopedic surgeons and the goal of the study is to provide indications and guidelines with respect to appropriate management.

*Lewis et al. performed a systematic review comparing hemiarthroplasty to THA in the management of displaced femoral neck fractures.* *In their study, THA was superior with regards to functional outcomes and risk for re-operation, while dislocation rate was higher in the THA group.* *They also did not note a significant difference in mortality or infection between cohorts.*

*Voskuijl et al. looked at outcomes for hospitalized patients after femoral neck fracture, comparing those who had hemiarthroplasty and THA.* **They noted** no differences in hospital length of stay or in-hospital mortality. However, they did identify that hemiarthroplasty had a 40% higher risk for adverse events during the immediate post operative period than THA.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Hemiarthroplasty has demonstrated a lower rate of dislocation compared to THA.
Answer 2-4: Risk of infection, mortality rate, and hospital length of stay have not demonstrated significant differences between THA and hemiarthroplasty.
