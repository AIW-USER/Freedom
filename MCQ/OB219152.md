## Question

A 45-year-old male is involved in a motor vehicle collision and presents to the trauma bay with the radiograph seen in Figure A. He is neurovascularly intact and does not have any other injuries. He is a construction worker and avid pickleball player. You recommend surgical management for his injury. What is the best surgical option for this patient to maximize the chances for fracture union and minimize complications?

![[q219152-a-subtroch.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Reamed statically locked intramedullary nail
2. Unreamed statically locked intramedullary nail
3. Unreamed dynamically locked intramedullary nail
4. Sliding hip screw
5. Blade plate

## Correct option

**1. Reamed statically locked intramedullary nail**

## Answer explanation

A reamed, statically locked intramedullary nailing provides the highest rates of fracture union while offering a low risk for complications relative to other fixation strategies.

Subtrochanteric femur fractures occur in a bimodal distribution with younger patients' fractures usually occurring as a result of high-energy trauma. Due to the well-described deforming forces about the proximal hip girdle, obtaining and maintaining reduction of these fractures can be challenging. Therefore, choosing the most appropriate implant for fixation is paramount in achieving the best patient outcomes. A statically locked intramedullary nail is a load-sharing implant that is the best choice in these unstable, comminuted fracture patterns and provides biomechanical superiority over laterally based plate options that are load-bearing. Additionally, reamed nails have been shown to increase vascularity and union rate compared to unreamed nails without increasing the risk for pulmonary complications or blood transfusion.

*Yoon et al. looked at the use of 10mm intramedullary nails for femur fractures and the subsequent time to union.* *In 287 patients who had nail sizes between 10 and 13mm, there were no differences in union rate or time to union.* **They concluded** that based on these findings a 10mm nail with interlockers was appropriate for most patients and its standardized use may decrease reaming, offer shorter operative times, and remove unnecessary inventory stock.

*Imerci et al. compared the use of nailing or plating for subtrochanteric femur fractures in adults.* **They noted** that the plating group had a longer time to fracture union, longer fluoroscopy times, and lower Harris hip scores. These findings led them to conclude that the intramedullary nailing system had distinct advantages over plating relative to healing in the management of these fractures.

Figure A shows an AP radiograph of the right hip demonstrating a comminuted subtrochanteric femur fracture.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 2 & 3: Reamed nailing has demonstrated higher union rates than unreamed nailing without an increase in complication.
Answer 4: A sliding hip screw has a high rate of failure and malunion when used for subtrochanteric fractures.
Answer 5: A blade plate is thought to act as a tension band to load the medial cortex, but in a comminuted, unstable subtrochanteric fracture this fixation is inferior and leads to higher rates of failure.
