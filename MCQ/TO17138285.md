## Question

A 5 year old child, GMFCS III has been diagnosed with spastic diplegic cerebral palsy. He has been on regular physiotherapy. On examination he has Jump knee type gait pattern. Static examination shows the following finding:

Ankle DF R1: -10 R2 +20 KF +30 on both sides.

Popliteal angle: R1: 80 R2: 40 on both sides.

Abduction at hips with Knee flexion is 45; with knee extended is 20.

What would be the management?

## Options

1. Botox: Gastrocnemius; Gracilis, Semitendinosus, Semimembranosus.
2. Botox: Gastrocnemius; Semitendinosus, Semimembranosus; Hip Adductor
3. Surgery: Gastrocnemius recession, Medial hamstring lengthening, Hip adductor release
4. Botox: Gastrocnemius; Semitendinosus, Semimembranosus; Hip adductor release

## Correct option

**1. Botox: Gastrocnemius; Gracilis, Semitendinosus, Semimembranosus.**

## Answer explanation

ANSWER-A

GMFCS is Gross Motor Function Classification System. It’s basically a system that categorises gross motor skills of children (< 18 years) with Cerebral palsy into five levels.

A Level 3 child is capable of walking with support and climbing stairs using railing.

Now, spasticity in CP children is graded using following measurements

R1: Angle of muscle reaction

R2: Passive ROM

R2-R1: gives dynamic component of spasticity and a difference of more than 20 is significant.

The child is 5 years of age, is GMFCS III and while being on physiotherapy has developed a Jump knee gait. This will prevent him from mobilizing and even wearing of orthosis so he will be benefitted from intervention.

His examination findings denote a dynamic spasticity in the Gastrocnemius and hamstring muscles. When more than 20 degrees of difference exists between R1-R2 it is significant and can be helped with botulinium injection. The hip in this case can be abducted till 45degrees which is the end point of surgical release. The hip abduction is restricted only in hip extension which is suggestive of a positive Phelps test which denotes a tightness/ spasticity of the Gracilis as the cause.

So, option 1 would be the ideal treatment of choice in this case.
