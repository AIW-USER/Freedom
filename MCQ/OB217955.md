## Question

A 42-year-old female with a past medical history significant for rheumatoid arthritis presents to your office with complaints of long-standing right elbow pain. She follows with her rheumatologist for management of her disease-modifying anti-rheumatoid drugs. She complains of diffuse joint pain and swelling. On examination, she has a large joint effusion, elbow flexion arc of 50°, and crepitus with forearm rotation. She demonstrates an inability to extend the thumb on neurovascular testing. Her elbow radiograph reveals some radiocapitellar arthritis. An MRI reveals a large synovitis encircling the radial head and neck. If the patient is to undergo synovectomy with radial head resection, which of the following would be the most likely complication following this procedure?

## Options

1. Loss of pronation
2. Loss of supination
3. Longitudinal instability of the forearm
4. Rapid progression of ulnohumeral arthritis
5. Ulnar nerve palsy

## Correct option

**4. Rapid progression of ulnohumeral arthritis**

## Answer explanation

This is a young patient with rheumatoid arthritis. Radial head resection may result in rapid progression of ulnohumeral arthritis.

Rheumatoid arthritis is an inflammatory arthropathy that may result in progressive synovitis of the elbow joint. As the synovitis progresses, this leads to cartilage destruction resulting in symmetric joint space narrowing. In younger patient with a relatively well-preserved joint space, open or arthroscopic synovectomy provides successful improvement for the vast majority of patients. In most cases, radial head resection is not required. However, synovitis that encircles the radial head and neck that results in painful crepitus with forearm rotation and when posterior interosseous nerve findings are apparent, radial head resection may be indicated. Preserving the radial head prevents the rapid progression of wear at the ulnohumeral joint.

*Lee et al. reviewed 14 arthroscopic synovectomies of the elbow in 11 patients with rheumatoid arthritis.* **They reported** that 93% achieved a short-term rating of excellent or good on the Mayo Elbow Performance Score; at the most recent assessment at an average of 42 months, however, only 57% maintained excellent or good results; four had required total elbow replacement. **They concluded** that although rehabilitation is facilitated by an arthroscopic procedure the results deteriorate more rapidly than after open synovectomy which may be due to the limitations of the arthroscopic technique.

*Copeland et al. reviewed 30 synovectomies of the elbow for rheumatoid arthritis.* **They reported** satisfactory relief of pain in 27 elbows with improved range of motion. **They noted** comparable results between those that underwent excision of the radial head from those that did not They concluded that the results of synovectomy do not significantly deteriorate with time up to ten years and the operation can be done with good results, especially in respect of relief of pain, even in elbows with relatively advanced rheumatoid disease.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1&2: A synovectomy with radial head resection would not be expected to cause a significant loss to pronosupination
Answer 3: Longitudinal instability of the forearm would not be expected in someone with an intact interosseous ligament of the forearm
Answer 5: If the elbow drifted into a significant amount of valgus, it is possible that an ulnar nerve palsy may occur. However, this would be much less likely that rapid progression of ulnohumeral arthritis
