## Question

You've indicated a patient for total knee arthroplasty after they have failed conservative treatment. They inquire about different knee prosthesis designs, and specifically why you select a posterior stabilized design. Which of the following is true of posterior stabilized knees compared to cruciate retaining designs?

## Options

1. Less bone resection
2. Higher incidence of patellar clunk
3. Better survivorship
4. Lower incidence of intraoperative fracture
5. Ability to perform cementless fixation

## Correct option

**2. Higher incidence of patellar clunk**

## Answer explanation

Patellar clunk is a condition seen in posterior stabilized designs, but not in cruciate retaining total knee arthroplasties.

Patellar clunk is a condition unique to posterior stabilized total knee designs that occurs secondary to a scar nodule that develops on the posterior distal portion of the quadriceps tendon/superior pole of the patella. As the knee is taken from a position of flexion to extension, a painful and sometimes audible "clunk" occurs as the scar gets stuck in the femoral box. This engagement most commonly occurs at about 40 degrees of flexion. The reason for the formation of the scar nodule is poorly understood, but hypothesized to be from injury to the quadriceps tendon or an undersized patellar button. Treatment consists of observation for mild symptoms or arthroscopic resection of the scar nodule for those who have more severe symptoms.

*Meneghini et al review the need for a cam/post mechanism in modern TKA.* **They report** that anterior-lipped and more conforming CR bearings, such as ultracongruent, medial pivot, and dual-pivot designs, have been used to substitute for the posterior cruciate ligament and obviate the need for the cam-post mechanism of a traditional PS design. **They conclude** that with the elimination of the PS design would also eliminate the complication of patellar clunk.

*Scott et al review PS TKA with condylar stabilized (CS) designs.* **They report** that the comparison of the clinical scores and radiographic results between both groups were essentially equivalent with no statistically significant differences at the final 5-year evaluation, although the CS knee group had significantly fewer episodes of postoperative mechanical sensation. **They conclude** that the CS knee provides excellent clinical, functional, and radiographic outcomes that are comparable to the results achieved with PS knees.

*Dennis et al performed a control-matched evaluation of painful patellar crepitus (PC) after total knee arthroplasty.* **They report** the incidence of PC correlated with a greater number of previous knee surgeries, decreased patellar component size, decreased composite patellar thickness, shorter preoperative and postoperative patellar tendon length, increased posterior femoral condylar offset, use of smaller femoral components and thicker tibial polyethylene inserts, and placement of the femoral component in a flexed posture. **They conclude** that these factors may increase quadriceps tendon contact forces against the superior aspect of the intercondylar box, increasing the risk of fibrosynovial proliferation and entrapment within the intercondylar region of the femoral component.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: PS designs require more bone resection given the femoral box.
Answer 3: There has been no documented difference in survivorship of PS and CR designs.
Answer 4: Intraoperative fracture risk is higher in PS designs compared to CR.
Answer 5: Both PS and CR designs are currently available in cementless options.
