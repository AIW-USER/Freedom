## Question

A 6-month-old female presents the emergency department with her aunt. Her aunt states that she has been favoring her left leg since yesterday after falling from her highchair. On physical exam, her thigh appears to be swollen and tender to palpation. A radiograph of her femur is shown is shown in Figure A. What is the next step in management?

![[q210130-a-screen-shot-2018-03-22-at-5-59-21-pm.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Placement in a Pavlik harness
2. Immediate notification of child protective services
3. Placement in a spica cast
4. Computed tomography
5. Genetic testing for metabolic bone disease

## Correct option

**2. Immediate notification of child protective services**

## Answer explanation

Any concern for non-accidental trauma (NAT) requires the immediate notification of child-protective services. A femoral shaft fracture in a non-ambulatory infant is highly suggestive of NAT.

The most common cause of femur fractures in non-ambulatory patients is NAT. Red flags concerning for NAT include long-bone fractures in non-ambulatory patients, age younger than 18 months, delayed presentation for treatment, and unwitnessed injuries. Children with disabilities are at increased risk for abuse. A skeletal survey may reveal other fractures at different stages of healing. However, it is important to keep in mind that skin lesions, not fractures, are the most common presenting signs of child abuse. Physicians are legally required to report suspected child abuse and neglect in all states.

*Kocher et al. provided an overview of the treatment of pediatric diaphyseal femur fractures.* When considering treatment, it is important to take into account fracture characteristics, age, and social factors. **They conclude** that, despite the recent trend towards surgical fixation in this population, there remains controversy and lack of conclusive evidence in the treatment of these fractures.

*Baldwin et al. performed a retrospective review at their institution in children younger than age 4 presenting with femur fractures to determine factors suggestive of NAT.* *Seventy patients were identified meeting these criteria with fractures suggestive of abuse and 139 patients were identified with fractures identified as accidental.* **They found** that, when comparing these groups, a history suspicious for abuse, physical or radiographic evidence of prior injury, and age younger than 18 months were significant predictors of abuse; patients with all three risk factors has a 92% chance of NAT.

Figure A is an anteroposterior radiograph of a left femur showing a transverse diaphyseal fracture.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1 and 3: While placement in a Pavlik harness or early spica casting are both appropriate treatment strategies with this injury in a 6-month-old, this should not delay immediate notification of child protective services.
Answer 4: Computed tomography will not change treatment and will expose the child to unnecessary radiation.
Answer 5: The history and exam do not suggest metabolic bone disease, and such testing should not delay the immediate notification of child protective services if warranted.
