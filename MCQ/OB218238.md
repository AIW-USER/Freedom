## Options

1. Anatomic total shoulder arthroplasty
2. Comprehensive arthroscopic management procedure
3. Glenoid osteotomy and interposition arthroplasty
4. Ream-and-run hemiarthroplasty
5. Reverse total shoulder arthroplasty

## Correct option

**5. Reverse total shoulder arthroplasty**

## Answer explanation

The reverse total shoulder arthroplasty (RSA) has the highest risk of postoperative acromial/scapular spine fracture; approximately 5%.

Glenohumeral arthritis is degenerative joint disease of the shoulder characterized by damage to the articular surfaces of the humeral head and/or glenoid. Diagnosis is made radiographically with true AP shoulder ("Grashey") and axillary lateral radiographs. Treatment is observation, NSAIDs, and corticosteroids for minimally symptomatic patients. Shoulder arthroplasty is indicated for progressive symptoms with severe degenerative disease. RSA is a type of shoulder arthroplasty that uses a convex glenoid hemispheric ball and a concave humerus articulating cup to reconstruct the glenohumeral joint. The center of rotation is moved inferiorly and medialized which allows the deltoid muscle to act on a longer fulcrum and have more mechanical advantage. One known postoperative risk factor is scapular spine fractures, which has a reported incidence of 4-5% after RSA; known risk factors include female sex, osteoporosis, and a medialized preoperative center of rotation.

*Iannotti et al. evaluated the influence of an operatively confirmed full-thickness tear of the rotator cuff, the severity of preoperative erosion of glenoid bone, preoperative radiographic evidence of subluxation of the humeral head, and the severity of preoperative loss of the passive range of motion on the outcome of total shoulder arthroplasty and hemiarthroplasty.* **They reported** that humeral head subluxation was associated with a less favorable result regardless of the type of shoulder arthroplasty and must be considered in preoperative planning and counseling. **They concluded** that severe loss of the passive range of motion preoperatively was associated with a decreased passive range of motion postoperatively.

*Otto et al. reviewed the sensitivity of plain radiographs to detect scapular fractures after RSA to test the reliability of a proposed classification, and to evaluate risk factors.* **They reported** that osteoporosis is a significant risk factor for scapular fractures after RSA. **They concluded** that advanced imaging may be needed to confirm the diagnosis, and it appears that most scapular spine fractures occurred from a screw.

Figures A-D are radiographs and CT images that reveal osteoarthritis with posterior subluxation and posterior bone loss.

Answers 1-4: RSA has the highest risk of postoperative scapular spine fractures, which is believed to be secondary to the altered mechanics of the prosthesis, pull of the deltoid, and/or secondary to a stress-riser created from baseplate screw fixation
