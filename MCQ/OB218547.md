## Question

A 25-year-old active-duty male presents to the clinic as a follow-up to discuss his noninsertional Achilles tendinopathy. He was initially evaluated by you in the clinic 6 weeks ago when the diagnosis was made. You provided a referral for physical therapy to begin eccentric strengthening exercises at that time. Which of the following is true regarding the management of non-insertional Achilles tendinopathy?

## Options

1. PRP injections in addition to eccentric exercise training is more likely to lead to worse outcomes than eccentric training alone
2. Eccentric exercises training must be supplemented with an orthotic device in order to provide significant improvements in outcomes
3. Patient outcomes are only improved with operative modalities of this condition
4. PRP injections in addition to eccentric exercise training may lead to improved outcomes in those that do not respond to eccentric exercise training alone
5. PRP injections have universally shown to be of no benefit in the treatment of this condition

## Correct option

**4. PRP injections in addition to eccentric exercise training may lead to improved outcomes in those that do not respond to eccentric exercise training alone**

## Answer explanation

While eccentric exercise training is the mainstay of non-operative treatment of noninsertional Achilles tendinopathy, the addition of PRP injections may lead to improved outcomes in those that do not respond to eccentric exercise training alone.

Achilles tendinopathy is generally classified by its anatomic location as insertional or noninsertional. Insertional tendinopathy occurs at the calcaneal insertion of the Achilles tendon and appears to be less common. Noninsertional (midportion, midsubstance) tendinopathy occurs in the body of the tendon and is thought to be more common. Multiple modalities of nonoperative management have been studied, with eccentric exercise training having the most robust evidence in support of its use in the current literature. Multiple adjuncts have been evaluated, including orthotics, PRP, extracorporeal shock wave therapy (ESWT), topical glyceryl trinitrate, polidocanol injection, blood injection, hyaluronic acid injection, intense pulsed light, NSAIDs, and corticosteroids.

*Jarin et al. performed a systematic review on the treatment of noninsertional Achilles tendinopathny (NIAT).* *Regarding PRP injection, they report on 6 studies that have been published since 2010.* Four of these found significant improvements in VISA-A scores with PRP injection. They do highlight one randomized control trial that did not report a significant difference of PRP compared to placebo saline injections at 3 months. **They further note** that while recent prospective and retrospective studies have shown promising results, more high-quality evidence is needed to fully support PRP injections in treating NIAT.

*Boesen et al. performed a randomized double-blinded prospective study evaluating the effect of high-volume injection (HVI), PRP and sham treatment in chronic noninsertional Achilles tendinopathy.* **They reported** that VISA-A scores improved significantly in all groups with greater improvements in the HVI group vs. PRP and placebo. Furthermore, VAS scores improved in all groups at all time points with significantly greater decrease in HVI and PRP versus placebo at all time points. Tendon thickness showed a significant decrease only in HVI and PRP groups during the intervention, and this was greater in the HVI versus PRP and placebo groups at 6 and 12 weeks and in the HVI and PRP groups versus the placebo group at 24 weeks. **They conclude** treatment with HVI or PRP in combination with eccentric training in chronic AT seems more effective in reducing pain, improving activity level, and reducing tendon thickness and intratendinous vascularity than eccentric training alone.

*LaPrade et al. provide a review on the American Academy of Orthopaedic Surgeons research symposium in November 2015 on the current state-of-the-art biologic treatments of articular cartilage, muscle, tendon, and bone injuries.* *This review outlines the findings of the symposium and summarizes the consensus reached on how best to advance research on biologic treatment of orthopaedic injuries.*

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: While more high-quality evidence is needed to fully support PRP injections in treating NIAT, current evidence suggests that patients may have improved outcomes, not worse.
Answer 2: Current evidence has found that there is no improvement in outcomes with eccentric exercises in addition to orthotic devices compared to eccentric exercises alone.
Answer 3: Non-operative management is the mainstay of treatment, as there is a multitude of evidence supporting improved patient outcome with non-operative modalities.
Answer 5: There have been several studies as discussed above that have found significant benefit with use of PRP.
