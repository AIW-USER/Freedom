## Question

## Correct option

**2. Figure C**

## Answer explanation

Figure C demonstrates metastatic breast cancer with carcinoma nests invading the bone. A patient with this pathology at this impending fracture site can be treated with surgical stabilization and post-operative radiation of the entire bone.

Metastatic carcinoma is overwhelmingly the most common cause for a destructive bone lesion in this patient age group. Myeloma and lymphoma are the next most common causes. Despite the rarity of primary malignant tumors in this age group, it is important to perform the proper imaging and biopsies to rule out a primary malignant tumor. The most appropriate workup for this patient would include complete femur radiographs, labwork (CBC, CMP, SPEP/UPEP, ESR), CT scan of the chest/abdomen/pelvis, bone scan, and CT scan vs. MRI of the left femur with contrast. Following these imaging studies, a biopsy can be performed to determine the etiology of the lesion.

*Weber et al. review the evaluation of an adult patient with a destructive bone lesion.* In patients presenting with a pathologic femur fracture, tibial skeletal traction can be useful to reduce pain and maintain bone length during workup. If metastatic disease, myeloma, or lymphoma are diagnosed, stabilization with a intramedullary nail can be performed. If a sarcoma is discovered, then surgery should be delayed while neoadjuvant therapy is performed.

*Adams et al. retrospectively reviewed 8 patients that underwent internal fixation of an undiagnosed primary malignant bone tumor without referral to an orthopaedic oncologist.* *6/8 patients required subsequent amputation and 2/8 were able to undergo limb salvage.* **The authors identified** the most common errors by the initial practitioner leading to these complications as improper biopsy technique, incomplete radiographs, and incomplete patient histories.

Figure A is an AP and lateral radiograph of a left tibia with an impending pathologic fracture.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Figure B demonstrates lacey osteoid and significant cellular atypia, typical of osteosarcoma, which would require wide resection.
Answer 3: Figure D demonstrates biphasic histology with low-grade chondroid component adjacent a high-grade spindle cell area, characteristic of dedifferentiated chondrosarcoma, which would require wide resection.
Answer 4: Figure E demonstrates atypical spindle cells in a herringbone pattern characteristic of fibrosarcoma, which would require wide resection.
Answer 5: Figure F demonstrates vacuolated physaliferous cells, characteristic of chordoma, which requires wide resection.
