## Question

A 12-year-old boy falls 8 feet from a tree limb and lands on his outstretched hand. He complains of elbow pain and a displaced radial neck fracture is noted on radiographs. Closed reduction is performed under sedation in the ER. A post-reduction radiograph is provided in Figure A. Which of the following actions should be taken?

![[q3200-a-ped-radial-neck-fx.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Immobilization in a sling until pain subsides
2. Immobilization in a long arm cast for 6 weeks to allow for callus formation and subsequent bony remodeling
3. CT scan to further evaluate the fracture and physis
4. Open reduction and internal fixation
5. Further reduction and percutaneous fixation in the operating room with elastic stable intramedullary nailing (ESIN)

## Correct option

**5. Further reduction and percutaneous fixation in the operating room with elastic stable intramedullary nailing (ESIN)**

## Answer explanation

The radiograph demonstrates a radial neck fracture with greater than 30 degrees of residual angulation following closed reduction. The next best step in treatment would be a trip to the operating room for closed reduction and percutaneous ESIN or pinning.

Pediatric radial neck fractures account for 5-10% of all pediatric elbow fractures. The majority of pediatric radial neck fractures can be treated with closed reduction. If angulation following closed reduction is <30 degrees a short course of immobilization and early range of motion is indicated. If there is >30 degrees of angulation following attempts at closed reduction, percutaneous pinning should follow. If these methods fail and angulation is >45 degrees open reduction is indicated. Open reduction leads to significantly worse outcomes and should only be reserved for rare instances when the fracture cannot be reduced by closed or percutaneous methods.

*Tarallo et al. revied 20 patients with radial neck fractures, 12 treated with percutaneous pinning and 8 treated with ESIN.* **They found** similar outcomes scores in the two groups but greater range of motion in the ESIN group. **They concluded** that ESIN was the ideal approach given better range of motion and the absence of complications.

*Dormans et al. summarize the evaluation and treatment of radial head and olecranon fractures in pediatric patients stating that most radial head fractures can be treated with closed reduction.* As the age of the child increases, the necessity for open reduction also increases.

Figure A shows a radial neck fracture status post reduction with residual angulation greater than 30 degrees. Illustration A shows a right elbow x-ray status post elastic stable intramedullary nailing.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1 & 2: Further attempts at reduction should be performed given significant residual angulation.
Answer 3: CT scan would not help with management in this injury and subject the patient to unnecessary radiation.
Answer 4: Open reduction should be reserved for rare cases when closed reduction and percutaneous pinning fails.
