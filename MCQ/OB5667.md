## Question

## Options

1. Myxoma
2. Hematoma
3. Neurofibroma
4. Soft tissue sarcoma
5. Fibroma

## Correct option

**4. Soft tissue sarcoma**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a soft-tissue sarcoma (STS). The histology slides (Figures D and E) shows a leiomyosarcoma.

Soft tissue sarcomas arise from mesoderm (connective tissue, muscle, adipose, neural, vascular, lymphatic). The most common are malignant fibrous histiocytoma, liposarcoma, leiomyosarcoma and synovial sarcoma. They grow centripetally with a pseudocapsule and compress surrounding structures. Rhabdomyosarcoma, angiosarcoma, clear-cell sarcoma, epithelioid sarcoma and synovial sarcoma (RACES) may metastasize to the lymph nodes. Of note, while leiomyosarcomas can have lymphatic spread, they still primarily metastasize via the hematogenous route, making pulmonary metastases more common than lymphatic metastases.

*Gilbert et al. reviewed soft tissue sarcoma.* **They recommend** pre- or postoperative radiation with limb-sparing wide local excision to improve local control. Route of delivery (brachytherapy or external beam) remains controversial. **They recommend** chemotherapy for high risk (high-grade, large, deep lesions, i.e. AJCC stage III or IV) tumors although the use of chemotherapy is controversial.

Figure A shows axial T1-weighted MRI scan of a large soft tissue mass in the medial thigh with intermediate T1 signal and heterogenous high T2 signal suspicious for soft tissue sarcoma. There are areas of low signal intensity and the tumor does not resemble surrounding subcutaneous fat. Figure B shows an axial T2-weighted MRI scan demonstrating heterogeneity within the tumor and high signal intensity. Figure C shows an axial T1-weighted image with intravenous gadolinium demonstrates large geographic areas without enhancement suggesting necrosis, a typical finding in high grade sarcomas. Figures D and E are low and high power micrographs showing marked pleomorphism characteristic of STS.

Incorrect Answers
Answer 1: Illustration A is a myxoma. It is characterized by paucicellular, often finely granular myxoid stroma with few cells, usually macrophages and bland spindle cells.
Answer 2: Illustration B is a hematoma. There is a cystic cavity containing necrotic debris, fibrin, and blood clots.
Answer 3: Illustration C is a neurofibroma. There is involvement of not only dermal but also subcutaneous nerves. There are multiple discoid proliferations of peripheral nerves in deep dermis and subcutaneous adipose tissue.
Answer 5: Illustration D is a fibroma. It is characterized by poorly marginated proliferation of hypocellular collagenous tissue.
