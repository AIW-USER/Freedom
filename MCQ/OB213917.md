## Options

1. Chondroblastoma
2. Osteosarcoma
3. Osteoblastoma
4. Giant cell tumor of bone
5. Neurilemmoma

## Correct option

**2. Osteosarcoma**

## Answer explanation

Malignant transformation of fibrous dysplasia may occur decades after the original diagnosis and is most commonly found to be osteosarcoma.

Fibrous dysplasia is a bone disease in which the normal lamellar bone is replaced by an abnormal fibro-osseous connective tissue. The onset of disease is most common in patients <30-years-old, with the majority of lesions becoming dormant as the patient ages. However, malignant transformation does occur and should be considered in patients with new-onset pain and the absence of trauma. Patients with polyostotic disease such as those with McCune-Albright or Mazabraud syndrome are more likely to undergo malignant transformation. Osteosarcoma occurs most commonly, but fibrosarcoma and malignant fibrous histiocytoma have also been reported.

*Qu et al review malignant transformation in those patients with monostotic fibrous dysplasia.* *They report on 10 patients, 7 of which were diagnosed with osteosarcoma, 2 with fibrosarcoma and one with transformation to malignant fibrous histiocytoma.* **They conclude** that an increase in pain or rapid change in radiographic appearance should alert the clinician to malignant change in patients with fibrous dysplasia.

*Murphey et al review the radiographic and clinical appearances of various types of osteosarcoma.* Specifically, they report on the different types of primary intramedullary osteosarcoma, such as high grade, telangiectatic, low grade, small cell, and osteosarcomatosis. **They conclude** that being able to recognize these lesions allows for improved patient assessment and is vital for appropriate clinical management.

Figure A is the AP radiograph of the left tibia demonstrating an osteolytic lesion within the "ground glass" appearance of fibrous dysplasia. Figure B is the microscopic specimen consistent with osteosarcoma; malignant transformation with anaplastic malignant tumor cells and neoplastic osteoid tissue.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Chondroblastomas are benign cartilage lesions found in the epiphysis of skeletally immature patients, and are not related to fibrous dysplasia.
Answer 3: Osteoblastomas are aggressive benign tumors of bone and can have a similar radiographic appearance to osteosarcoma, but are not related to fibrous dysplasia.
Answer 4: Giant cell tumors of bone are aggressive benign lesions commonly found in the metaphysis of long bones, and are not related to fibrous dysplasia.
Answer 5: Neurilemmomas are benign tumors composed of Schwann cells, and are not related to fibrous dysplasia.
