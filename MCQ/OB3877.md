## Question

Figure A shows a 45-year-old male underwent excision of interdigital neuroma through a dorsal approach 1 year ago. The operative report states "neuroma was excised 5mm proximal to the intermetatarsal ligament." He continues to have web space pain with a well healed incision and a positive Tinel's sign in the area of previous neuroma resection. What is the most likely cause of his persistent pain?

![[q3877-a-normal-right-foot-xray.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Painful scar formation
2. Infection
3. Freiberg's disease
4. Metatarsophalangeal arthritis
5. Inadequate proximal resection

## Correct option

**5. Inadequate proximal resection**

## Answer explanation

The most likely reason for persistent pain after interdigital (Morton's) neuroma is the formation of a stump neuroma due to inadequate proximal resection.

Recurrence of interdigital neuroma presents with similar symptoms of primary neuroma with localized pain and Tinel's sign in the intermetatarsal region. Inadequate proximal resection is thought to be secondary to residual plantarly directed nerve branches (PDNB). These may lead to recurrence by tethering the remaining nerve preventing proximal retraction out of the weight-bearing portion of the metatarsal head. Similarly, traumatic neuroma stump formation may occur in these remaining branches. Based on this anatomy, current recommendations are excision at least 3cm proximal to the intermetatarsal ligament.

*Amis et al. performed cadaveric dissections to further examine the common digital nerve to help explain high rates of surgical failure for Morton's neuroma excision.* **They found** a high concentration of plantarly directed nerve branches proximal to the bifurcation of the proper digital branches. **They concluded** these plantarly directed nerve branches account for the high recurrence rate due to the failure of proximal retraction or traumatic neuroma formation. **They conclude** excision should be at least 3 cm proximal to the intermetatarsal ligament.

*Johnson et al. looked at 37 feet who had reoperation for persistent interdigital neuroma pain.* **They found** 67% of patients had complete relief and histology showed elements of primary interdigital neuroma or amputation-stump neuroma. **They conclude** that reoperation is a successful procedure for recurrent interdigital neuroma.

Figure A shows a normal AP radiograph of the foot. Illustration A shows an authors depiction of the location of the plantarly directed nerve branches.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Painful scar formation is more common following plantar based incisions for neuroma excision.
Answer 2: The patient has no signs of infection with a well-healed wound.
Answer 3: Freiberg's disease would show flattening of the involved metatarsal head and joint space narrowing.
Answer 4: Figure A shows a normal AP radiograph of the foot without signs of arthritis.
