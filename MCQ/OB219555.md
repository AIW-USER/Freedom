## Question

Corticosteroids, both orally and as injection components, are frequently utilized by orthopaedic surgeons and other medical professionals due to their potent anti-inflammatory effects. Which of the following is most accurate regarding the possible complications of corticosteroid use?

## Options

1. Adverse skeletal effects do not occur with less than one year of oral glucocorticoid use
2. Bisphosphonate therapy is contraindicated in patients at high-risk of fracture who require prolonged oral glucocorticoid therapy
3. Daily doses between 2.5 and 5.0mg have not been found to increase the rate of adverse skeletal effects
4. Epidural corticosteroid injections are associated with a loss of lumbar bone mineral density (BMD)
5. Knee intra-articular corticosteroid injections are associated with a loss of lumbar bone mineral density (BMD)

## Correct option

**4. Epidural corticosteroid injections are associated with a loss of lumbar bone mineral density (BMD)**

## Answer explanation

While corticosteroids, both orally and as components of injections, have relatively low complication rates, their use as a component of an epidural steroid injection (ESI) has been associated with the loss of lumbar bone mineral density (BMD), most notably in post-menopausal women.

The downstream effects of corticosteroids are numerous and mediated through various pathways. Concerning the metabolic effects, these hormones primarily serve to increase glucose availability, hence why caution must be taken when indicating these medications for diabetic patients, as they can lead to transient hyperglycemia. For immune effects, corticosteroids interact with the glucocorticoid receptor to upregulate the expression of anti-inflammatory proteins and downregulate the expression of pro-inflammatory proteins. Furthermore, through interaction with the RANK-L pathway, these molecules can also interfere with local bone mineralization homeostasis.

*Florance et al. investigated the effect of intra-articular corticosteroid injections (CSI) on lumbar BMD.* *Their study included 50 patients who had undergone at least 3 medium or large joint CSIs and a subsequent non-contrast abdominal CT within 5 years of the first injection.* *These patients were matched to 126 patients who did not undergo CSIs.* Overall, **the authors found** no significant differences in lumbar BMD between cases and controls and no cumulative dose-related effect of multiple injections on lumbar BMD.

*Kerezoudis et al. performed a systematic review on the effect of epidural steroid injections (ESI) on lumbar BMD and vertebral fracture risk.* *Their study ultimately pooled data on 7,233 patients.* In multiple studies, **the authors found** that ESI was associated with significantly decreased BMD. **The authors found** that cumulative ESI doses of less than 200 mg for postmenopausal women and 3000 mg for healthy men were not associated with this loss of BMD. **The authors also found** that anti-resorptive therapy protected against this BMD loss. Ultimately, **the authors conclude** that ESIs should be cautiously recommended, especially in patients at risk for osteoporotic fracture.

Van Staa provides an overview of the pathogenesis, epidemiology, and management of corticosteroid-induced osteoporosis in patients receiving oral glucocorticoids (GC). He notes that daily doses as low as 2.5 mg prednisone-equivalent have been associated with an increase in the risk of fractures and that the risk is dose-dependent and can occur as early as three months following initiation of therapy. He also notes that after cessation of GC treatment, the fracture risk may normalize unless the patient had previously been on high-dose oral GC therapy. Ultimately, he suggests that patients started on oral GC therapy also receive calcium and vitamin D and that patients at high risk of fracture receive antiresorptive therapy.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Adverse skeletal effects often occur within three months of treatment initiation.
Answer 2: Bisphosphonate/antiresorptive therapy is indicated in patients at high risk of fracture receiving prolonged oral GC medications.
Answer 3: Daily doses as low as 2.5 to 5.0 mg are associated with developing adverse skeletal effects.
Answer 5: IA knee CSIs are not associated with loss of lumbar BMD.
