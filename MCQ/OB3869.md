## Question

A 60-year-old woman presents with the condition seen in Figure A following corrective forefoot surgery 6 years ago. She has attempted to manage her condition with shoe wear modifications, taping and splinting but has consistent pain. Her metatarsophalangeal (MTP) joint is not completely reducible on exam. Which of the following treatment options offers her predictable alignment and pain relief?

![[q3869-a-untitled.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. MTP joint hemiarthroplasty
2. Lateral metatarsal opening wedge osteotomy
3. First metatarso-cuneiform arthrodesis
4. MTP joint arthrodesis
5. MTP joint interpositional arthroplasty

## Correct option

**4. MTP joint arthrodesis**

## Answer explanation

The patient presents with iatrogenic hallux varus with with early arthritic changes. Hallux MTP joint arthrodesis provides the most predictable results in terms of pain relief and alignment.

Hallux valgus surgery may result in iatrogenic hallux varus. Surgical management of hallux varus is predicated on determining the level of deformity, the activity of the patient, and the presence of arthritic changes.

*Grimes et al. retrospectively studied a group of 29 patients (33 feet) who had undergone MTP arthrodesis for failed hallux valgus surgery.* **The authors found** substantial improvements in pain relief and alignment, however patient reported outcomes were significantly worse in the revision setting compared with primary arthrodesis for hallux valgus deformity.

Figure A demonstrates iatrogenic hallux varus following distal metatarsal osteotomy with an incongruent MTP joint and early arthritic changes

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: MTP joint arthroplasty would not allow for predictable alignment and has significantly high rates of failure when used for hallux rigidus
Answer 2: Lateral metatarsal opening wedge osteotomy would not correct the arthritic, incongruent MTP joint
Answer 3: First metatarsal cuneiform arthrodesis (Lapidus) can be used as a salvage for failed hallux valgus correction, however would not offer predictable pain relief or alignment in this scenario
Answer 5: MTP joint interpositional arthroplasty would not allow for realignment
