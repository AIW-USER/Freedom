## Question

A patient with a distal radius fracture and a sizable metaphyseal void undergoes open reduction and internal fixation with the use of a synthetic augment. One year later, radiographs confirm the successful integration of the graft material into the host bone. What is the probable graft material used?

## Options

1. Calcium phosphate
2. Calcium sulfate
3. Tricalcium phosphate
4. Osteogenic protein-1
5. Freeze-dried cancellous allograft

## Correct option

**1. Calcium phosphate**

## Answer explanation

Calcium phosphate (CaP) possesses osteoconductive and osteointegration capabilities allowing for incorporation into host bone over time (Answer 1).

CaP, which is available as an injectable with chemical characteristics similar to those in the mineral phase of bone, can be utilized in distal radius fracture fixation to fill osseous voids. Despite disparate outcome data regarding its efficacy in restoring volar tilt, enhancing Disabilities of the Arm, Shoulder and Hand (DASH) scores, bolstering grip strength, or promoting improved healing potential, radiographic evidence consistently illustrates the incorporation of CaP into the host bone approximately 12 months postoperatively.

*Tosti et al. report that utilization of injectable CaP can be associated with improved range of motion, grip strength, and pain reduction in patients with distal radius fractures with large osseous voids.* However, these results were established at 6-8 weeks postoperatively, without significant differences between groups at 1 year. This review also commented that a series of distal radius fractures are difficult to compare because of the heterogeneity of fracture patterns, graft type, adjuncts, surgical approaches, fixation devices, and postoperative protocols. Among the controlled series, only short-term or radiographic benefits have been shown, with no difference in long-term outcomes.

*Ozer and colleagues reviewed the use of bone grafts and substitutes in the treatment of distal radius fractures and* **found** CaP ranks as the second most commonly tested material, following autograft, in treating distal radius fractures. CaP cement, moldable and compatible with bone defects, shares chemical and physical characteristics with the bone's mineral phase allowing for its osteoconductive and osteointegration capabilities.

*Kim et al. aimed to assess the efficacy of augmenting volar locking plate fixation with CaP bone cement in elderly patients with unstable distal radial fractures.* *Forty-eight patients participated, with procedures randomized between volar locking plate alone (Group 1) and volar locking plate with CaP bone cement (Group 2).* *Clinical and radiographic evaluations at 3 and 12 months showed no significant differences in outcomes, however, all patients in Group 2 who returned for the 12-month follow-up visit had radiographic evidence of incorporation of the CaP bone cement into the host bone.*

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Calcium sulfate is osteoconductive and usually resorbs within 4-12 weeks in vivo, making it less likely to be identified on radiographs 1 year postoperatively.
Answer 3: Tricalcium phosphate, possesses qualities similar to that of the mineral phase of bone. Despite this similarity, its faster biodegradation rate compared to hydroxyapatite may induce an inflammatory reaction, leading to observable osteolysis around the graft material on radiographs.
Answer 4: Osteogenic protein-1 (OP-1), or rhBMP-7, an osteoinductive growth factor, has been linked to osteolysis and delayed union in distal radius fractures. It has not demonstrated successful radiographic incorporation into host bone one year postoperatively.
Answer 5: Freeze-dried cancellous allograft is completely depleted of bone morphogenetic protein, is purely osteoconductive, and is not a synthetic augment.
