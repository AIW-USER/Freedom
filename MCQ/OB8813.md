## Question

A 67-year-old man feels a pop and has an immediate onset of pain and swelling after awkwardly stepping off of a curb. Preoperative Xrays are shown in figure A. He undergoes the surgery depicted in Figure B. What would be the most likely finding pre-operatively?

## Options

1. Insall-Salvati ratio of 1
2. Insall-Salvati ratio of 0.7
3. Insall-Salvati ratio of 1.3
4. MRI showing complete quadriceps tendon rupture
5. Maintained ability to perform a straight leg raise

## Correct option

**3. Insall-Salvati ratio of 1.3**

## Answer explanation

The patient has undergone a patellar tendon repair and an Insall-Salvati ratio of >1.2 would be expected pre-operatively.

Patellar tendon ruptures typically occur in patients younger than 40 years old. Most ruptures occur at the junction of the tendon and distal pole of the patella. Primary surgical repair within 2 weeks of injury is recommended to prevent extensor mechanism contracture. Lateral radiographs demonstrates patella alta (Insall-Salvati ratio greater than 1.2) which in this setting is indicative of a patellar tendon rupture. Clinical findings include an inability to extend the knee against gravity, a large hematoma, and a palpable gap below the inferior pole.

*Matava reviewed patellar tendon ruptures.* *He stressed the importance of a timely anatomic repair, including reconstitution of tendon integrity, retinacular tissue, placement of a reinforcing cerclage suture, and early aggressive postoperative therapy.* While patients with delayed repairs often gain a functioning extensor mechanism, persistent quadriceps atrophy and diminished flexion can compromise optimal results.

*Brooks reviewed extensor mechanism ruptures following total knee arthroplasty.* **He indicated** that blood supply to the extensor mechanism may become disrupted during the surgical approach, which may limit healing potential compared to the native knee. Primary repair is indicated in acute ruptures, whereas reconstruction with allograft may be required in chronic cases.

Figure A demonstrates patella alta with an IS ratio >1.2. Figure B is an intraoperative photograph of a patellar tendon rupture from the distal pole of the patella being fixed with a running locked stitch configuration. Illustration A demonstrates how to measure the Insall-Salvati ratio. Illustration B is the Caton-Deschamps index which is defined by the ratio between the articular facet length of the patella and the distance between the distal articular facet of the patella and the anterior corner of the superior tibial epiphysis. With the Caton-Deschamps ratio, patella alta is >1.2 and patella baja <0.6. This ratio as another common and increasingly popular ratio utilized.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: An Insall-Salvati ratio of 1 is considered normal.
Answer 2: An Insall-Salvati ratio of <0.8 is considered patella baja and would be consistent with a quadriceps tendon rupture.
Answer 4: The intra-operative photograph demonstrates fixation of the patella tendon, not the quadriceps tendon. An MRI is usually not required to make the diagnosis of a complete patella or quadriceps tendon ruptures.
Answer 5: A patient with a complete patella tendon rupture would be unable to perform a straight leg raise.
