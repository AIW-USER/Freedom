## Question

A lateral wall thickness of 25 mm represents an independent risk factor for which of the following complications following fixation of an intertrochanteric femur fracture with a sliding hip compression screw?

## Options

1. Anterior perforation of the distal femur
2. Anterior spike malreduction
3. Implant cut-out
4. Postoperative lateral wall fracture
5. None of the above

## Correct option

**5. None of the above**

## Answer explanation

A sliding hip compression screw is contraindicated in patients with a lateral wall thickness of less than 20.5 mm, due to a risk of postoperative lateral wall fracture. A lateral wall thickness of 25 mm does not portend a higher risk of this complication.

Intertrochanteric femur fractures are a common subtype of hip fractures and occur frequently among the elderly and/or those with osteoporosis. They occur more frequently in women than men, and patients are typically of an older age than patients sustaining femoral neck or subtrochanteric hip fractures. Intertrochanteric femur fractures are typically fixed with a sliding hip compression screw or cephalomedullary nail. Sliding hip compression screws are appropriate for stable fracture patterns, as these devices rely on an intact lateral wall for fixation. Cephalomedullary nails are useful for both stable and unstable fracture patterns. Arthroplasty is reserved for fractures with extensive comminution or in bone that is too osteoporotic to support internal fixation.

*Hsu et al. performed a retrospective review of 208 patients (mean age 78) who underwent sliding compression hip screw fixation of AO/OTA 31-A1 and 31-A2 intertrochanteric femur fractures.* **They found** that a lateral wall thickness of less than 20.5 mm was an independent risk factor for the development of postoperative lateral wall fracture. **They concluded** that fractures with a lateral wall thickness of less than 20.5 mm should not be treated with a sliding hip compression screw alone.

*Palm et al. performed a retrospective review of prospectively collected data of 214 patients (mean age 83) who underwent four-hole sliding hip compression screw fixation of intertrochanteric femur fractures.* **They found** that postoperative fracture of the lateral wall was the most common reason for re-operation following fixation of intertrochanteric femur fractures with a sliding hip compression screw. **They concluded** that fractures with involvement of the lateral wall preoperatively or that have a potential to fracture the lateral wall intraoperatively should not be managed with sliding hip compression screws.

Incorrect Answers:

Answer 1: Anterior perforation of the distal femur can occur with intramedullary nailing of an intertrochanteric femur fracture. This complication is typically associated with a starting point that is too posterior, or if there is a mismatch in the radius of curvature between the nail and femur.
Answer 2: Anterior spike malreduction can occur during fixation of an unstable left-sided intertrochanteric femur fracture with a sliding hip compression screw due to screw torque.
Answer 3: Implant cut-out can occur when the tip-apex distance is above 25.0 mm and is not specifically associated with any lateral wall thickness threshold.
Answer 4: Postoperative lateral wall fractures typically occur when the lateral wall thickness is less than 20.5 mm.
