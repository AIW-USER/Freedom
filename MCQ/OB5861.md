## Answer explanation

Figure B shows a synovial sarcoma (SS), which is associated with a t(X;18) translocation.

Synovial sarcoma account for 5-10% of all soft tissue sarcomas, and 30% of soft tissue sarcomas in childhood. They are characterized by a t(X;18;p11;q11) translocation (>90%) and keratin positivity and can be monophasic or biphasic. The biphasic pattern is most common and contains both epithelial and spindle components. The epithelial component shows large pale or columnar cells and may rarely form glands. The spindle component comprises sheets/fascicles of uniform small elongate plump cells with scant cytoplasm with indistinct cell margins. The stroma is comprised of collagen bundles and vessels. Synovial sarcomas with a monophasic pattern contain the spindle component alone.

*Ladanyi et al. performed a retrospective multi-institutional review of 243 patients with synovial sarcoma to determine if SYT-SSX fusion type (SYT-SSX1 vs SYT-SSX2) affected outcome.* **They found** that SYT-SSX1 was more common than SYT-SSX2 (61% vs. 37%). SYT-SSX1 tumors were larger, presented more often with metastases, and were biphasic. SYT-SSX2 tumors were more common in females (2:1), had better survival rates, and were monophasic. **They concluded** that SYT-SSX fusion type was the most important prognostic factor.

*Kawai et al. examined SYT-SSX fusion transcripts in 45 synovial sarcomas with RT-PCR.* **They found** that biphasic tumors had SYT-SSX1 while monophasic tumors had SYT-SSX2. Patients with SYT-SSX2 had better metastases-free survival. Histological subtype alone (monophasic vs. biphasic) was not prognostically important. **They concluded** that the type of fusion transcript correlates with histological subtype and clinical behavior.

*Chotel et al. reviewed the variability of SS presentation in 35 children.* **They found** that 49% presented with a painless mass, 30% had no mass, and 21% had joint contractures. In 45%, a diagnosis was obtained after unplanned excision. Tumors around the elbow/knee were associated with longer symptoms and longer doctor delay; however, delayed diagnosis was not shown to lead to poor prognosis. They advise greater awareness of this tumor as a potential diagnosis.

Figure A shows alveolar rhabdomyosarcoma. Figure B shows synovial sarcoma. Figure C shows clear cell sarcoma. Figure D shows myxoid liposarcoma. Figure E shows Ewing sarcoma.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Figure A demonstrates the histology of an alveolar rhabdomyosarcoma, which is associated with t(2;13). Alveolar rhabdomyosarcoma demonstrates aggregates of poorly differentiated round cells with multinucleated giant cells surrounded by dense fibrous septae.
Answer 3: Figure C demonstrates the histology of a clear cell sarcoma, which is associated with t(12:22). CCS is a soft tissue neoplasm exhibiting evidence of melanocytic differentiation. The slide shows irregular nests of cells separated by fibrous septa. Cells are round to fusiform and non-pleomorphic. There is moderate to abundant clear to granular eosinophilic cytoplasm.
Answer 4: Figure D demonstrates the histology of a myxoid liposarcoma, which is associated with t(12:16). MLS is a fatty neoplasm characterized by myxoid stroma, signet ring lipoblasts, regular small round cells and/or a prominent arborizing vascular pattern. The histological slide shows paucicellularity, sheets of signet ring lipoblasts and large fat cells, and an arborizing, plexiform capillary network (chicken wire appearance).
Answer 5: Figure E demonstrates the histology of Ewing's sarcoma, which is associated with t(11:22). ES is a peripheral primitive neuroectodermal tumor (small, round, blue cell neoplasm) with a spectrum of appearance from undifferentiated to forming rosettes. Small round cells are present, with numerous mitotic figures and nuclear atypia.
