## Question

A 63-year-old female presents with left knee pain and lateral swelling that has progressively worsened over the past year. Her current radiograph is depicted in Figure A. An MRI revealed T2 signal hyperintensity within the trabecular bone without expansion beyond the cortex. A biopsy is performed and depicted in Figure B. Which of the following treatment options are preferred to provide the optimal outcome and knee function?

## Options

1. Currettage and bone grafting
2. Currettage, liquid nitrogen local adjuvant, and reconstruction of the void with bone cement
3. Denosumab
4. Wide resection and reconstruction of the LCL and biceps tendon
5. Wide resection and multiagent chemotherapy

## Correct option

**4. Wide resection and reconstruction of the LCL and biceps tendon**

## Answer explanation

The patient presents with chondrosarcoma of the proximal fibula, which is treated with wide resection and then reconstruction of the lateral collateral ligament (LCL) and biceps femoris tendon to maintain knee stability.

Chondrosarcoma is a malignant bone tumor that is slow growing. Due to the low mitotic rate, chemotherapy regimens and radiation are ineffective. Wide resection is the mainstay of surgical treatment to achieve local control and prevent local recurrence. With the involvement of the proximal fibula, resection of the proximal fibula can lead to varus instability and knee flexion weakness due to the insertion of the LCL and biceps femoris tendon. Reconstruction of the LCL and biceps femoris tendon can improve knee function after surgical resection. The LCL and biceps femoris tendon can be reconstructed by transposing to the lateral tibia with staple fixation. This location mimics the original insertion's varus stabilizing and knee flexion moment.

*Bickels et al. retrospectively studied 24 patients who underwent resection of the proximal fibula for tumors and assessed their knee stability and functional outcome.* *The authors utilized a reconstructive technique to minimize knee instability, involving stapling the lateral collateral ligament to the lateral tibial metaphysis, followed by cast immobilization and protected weight bearing.* **They reported** that most patients achieved a stable knee, with better outcomes observed in those with less radical resections (Type I) than in more radical resections (Type II). **The authors concluded** stapling the lateral collateral ligament proved to be a reliable method for reconstruction in these cases.

*Zhao et al. retrospectively compared knee stability and functional outcomes in patients with proximal fibula tumors with the reconstruction of the lateral collateral ligament (LCL) and biceps femoris tendon using a suture anchor (18 patients) and no reconstruction (11 patients).* **They reported** a higher rate of knee stability and better Musculoskeletal Tumor Society (MSTS) functional scores with ligament reconstruction compared to the non-reconstruction group. **They concluded** reconstruction of the LCL and biceps femoris tendon was deemed a safe and reliable technique after proximal fibula tumor resection.

Figure A is an AP radiograph of the left knee with an expansile and lytic lesion of the proximal tibia. Figure B is a histologic slide depicting chondrosarcoma.

Incorrect answers
Answer 1: Curettage and bone grafting is an option for enchondromas and non-ossifying fibromas.
Answer 2: Curetteage and local adjuvant treatment with liquid nitrogen and bone cementing is a surgical treatment for giant cell tumors.
Answer 3: Denosumab is a first-line treatment for giant cell tumors.
Answer 5: Wide resection and chemotherapy is the standard treatment option for osteosarcoma.
