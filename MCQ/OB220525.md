## Question

A 24-year-old male presents to the orthopedic clinic for follow-up. 3 years prior, the patient was injured in an improvised explosive device, resulting in a right radius and ulna diaphyseal fracture, ulnar nerve injury, and a large soft tissue deficit. The patient was treated with plate fixation across the radius and ulna, and a free flap. The initial free flap lost perfusion early and had to be revised. The patient has chronic pain and a nonunion of the radius. Which of the following aspects of the patient's presentation has been associated with an increased risk of late amputation?

## Options

1. Upper extremity injury
2. Fracture non-union
3. Nerve injury
4. Initial flap failure
5. Diaphyseal fracture

## Correct option

**2. Fracture non-union**

## Answer explanation

Outcomes after limb salvage treatment for high-energy extremity trauma are often poor. Fracture non-union has been associated with late amputation after limb salvage surgery (answer choice 2).

Mangled extremities present a unique challenge for both patients and treating surgeons. The Lower Extremity Evaluation program (LEAP) study was a multicenter prospective observational cohort study of adults with severe lower extremity trauma in the United States. The LEAP study reported no clear functional differences between early amputation versus reconstruction at 2-year follow-up. The Military Extremity Trauma Amputation/Limb Salvage (METALS) study is an observational retrospective cohort study of US military personnel who were treated for mangled extremities. The METALS study showed that salvage showed higher functional outcomes, more pain, and higher return to military duty rates. Despite successful early salvage, many patients go on to late amputation. Lower extremity periarticular fractures, fracture non-union, limb dysfunction, and chronic pain are associated with late amputation.

*Harrington et al. published a retrospective review of 307 patients who underwent flap reconstruction from 2003-2014 in the National Capital Region.* **The authors found** that approximately 14% of patients underwent late amputation after limb salvage. Lower extremity periarticular fractures, fracture non-union, limb dysfunction, and chronic pain are associated with late amputation.

*Mitchell et al. published a retrospective cohort study that utilized data from the METALS study for a subset of 155 individuals with mangled upper extremities.* *Patients reported moderate to high levels of physical and psychosocial disability.* **The authors reported** that long-term outcomes are equivalent for those treated with amputation or limb salvage.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Upper extremity injuries have a significantly lower rate of late amputation after successful limb salvage.
Answers 3,4,5: Nerve injury, initial flap failure, and diaphyseal injuries have not been associated with late amputation after successful limb salvage.
