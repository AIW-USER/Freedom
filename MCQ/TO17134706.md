Chapter: Spine MCQs — Klippel Feil Syndrome

## Question

what would you advise to an adolescent boy with short neck and renal aplasia, who comes for fitness regarding playing football. His CT shows C4-C5 fusion.

## Options

1. No participation
2. Can play
3. Can play after surgery
4. Surgery and cannot play even after surgery

## Correct option

**2. Can play**

## Answer explanation

Klippel-Feil Syndrome: It is a rare congenital condition caused by failure of normal segmentation or formation of cervical somites at 3-8 weeks gestation. It is characterized by multiple abnormal segments of the cervical spine. Patient typically has congenital fusion of 2 or more cervical vertebrae

Associated conditions

- congenital scoliosis

- Sprengel's deformity (33%)

- renal disease (aplasia in 33%)

- deafness (30%) congenital heart disease / cardiovascular (5-30%)

- synkinesis (mirror motions)

- brainstem abnormalities

- congenital cervical stenosis

- basilar invagination

- atlantoaxial instability (~50%)

- adjacent level disease (100%)

classic triad (seen in fewer than 50%) includes- low posterior hairline, short webbed neck and limited cervical ROM

TREATMENT:

Non-Operative and can participate contact/ collision sports
Non-Operative but cannot participate contact/

collision sports
Operative (decompression and

fusion)

Indication
Asymptomatic patients with fusions of 1–2-disc spaces below C3
asymptomatic patients with fusion involving C2
basilar invagination chronic pain myelopathy associated atlantoaxial instability

counseling important to avoid activities that place the neck at high risk of injury

contact sports, gymnastics, football,

wrestling, trampoline

Answer: 2
