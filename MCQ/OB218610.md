## Question

## Answer explanation

Elevated leptin levels have been associated with the development of slipped capital femoral epiphysis (SCFE) (Answer 2). The other conditions depicted in the figures are not associated with elevated leptin levels.

SCFE is a common condition of the proximal femoral physis that leads to slippage of the metaphysics relative to the epiphysis. This condition is most often encountered in adolescent obese males, and it is the most common disorder affecting adolescent hips. Diagnosis is made through plain-film radiographs of the hips, and treatment is with percutaneous pin fixation. Contralateral pinning is indicated in patients at high risk for slip, including those patients with an initial age at slip < 10 years, obese males and those patients with endocrine disorders. Recently, elevated leptin levels have been identified as a risk factor for the development of SCFE, and this elevated risk appears to be independent of obesity, sex and race.

*Halverson et al. investigated the contribution, if any, that elevated leptin levels had on the development of SCFE.* *Their study included 40 patients with a known SCFE and 30 BMI-matched control patients.* The odds of developing SCFE were increased by an odds ratio of 4.9 in patients with elevated leptin levels, regardless of obesity status, sex and race. When grouped by obesity status, both obese and non-obese patients with SCFE had higher leptin levels (17.9 ng/ml, 5.8 ng/ml, respectively) compared to their non-SCFE counterparts (10.5 ng/mL and 1.7 ng/mL, respectively). Furthermore, serum leptin levels were found to increase in association with BMI (0.17 ng/mL per MRI percentile point).

*Witbreuk et al. performed a systematic review of the literature investigating risk factors for the development of SCFE.* *Their study ultimately included 56 individual studies.* Overall, **the authors conclude** that SCFE is most likely the result of a multi-factorial event during adolescence during periods of increase in patient height and weight. **They highlight** the dearth of screening and/or diagnostic tests, although they do not that specific biomarkers (leptin, thyroid hormone, etc) may serve as both independent risk factors and targets future diagnostic or screening tests. Overall, **they conclude** that further research is necessary in order to both optimize the screening and possible prevention of this condition.

Figure A demonstrates obliteration of the proximal femoral physis consistent with late-stage Legg-Calve-Perthes Disease (LCPD). Figure C demonstrates loss of femoral head-neck offset consistent with CAM-type femoroacetabular impingement (FAI). Figure D demonstrates a traumatic proximal femur fracture. Figure E demonstrates bilaterally increased femoral neck-shaft angles consistent with developmental coxa valga and dysplasia.

Incorrect Answers
Answers 1, 3,4,5: LCPD, FAI, traumatic proximal femur fractures and developmental coxa valga have not been found to be associated with elevated leptin levels.
