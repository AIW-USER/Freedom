## Question

A 28-year-old female presents to the clinic for continued evaluation of low back pain, that occurred acutely after an intense weightlifting session 8 weeks ago. Since her previous examination, she has now noticed weakness with left great toe dorsiflexion. An MRI is obtained, as demonstrated in Figures A and B. ## Options

1. Extracellular matrix proteolysis
2. Maintain structural integrity of surrounding tissue
3. Induction of cellular apoptosis
4. Provide compression strength through water retention
5. Regulate collagen fiber diameter

## Correct option

**1. Extracellular matrix proteolysis**

## Answer explanation

This 31-year-old female presents with continued management of a lumbar disc herniation. The most abundant molecule produced in cells of herniated intervertebral disks includes matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs), which function in extracellular matrix proteolysis (Answer 1).

Disc herniations are commonly encountered in spine practices and can be a prominent source of pain and debilitation for patients. Intervertebral discs are comprised of a nucleus pulposis and annulus fibrosis, both of which contain their own unique molecular profiles. The former is predominantly type II collagen and water, and serves as a 'cushion' between vertebral segments, whereas the latter is fibrocartilaginous, comprising type I collagen and serving to encase the nucleus pulposis. Disc herniations stem from compromised or pathologic annulus fibrosis, which can be secondary to a number of etiologies (e.g., increased torsion and endplate lesions). The pathophysiology behind this condition includes the development of MMPs as a byproduct of the inflammatory response to the inciting factor, predisposing one to herniation.

*Wang et al. performed a cadaver study examining 600 vertebral endplates and 313 intervertebral discs to identify a possible link between endplate morphology and the presence or absence of lesions (e.g., schmorl nodes, trauma-related fissures, and compressions) and intervertebral disc degeneration.* **The authors noted** a significant association between the presence of lesions and disc degeneration, and larger-sized lesions were associated with more severe disc degeneration. **The authors concluded** endplate morphology aberrancies are likely directly linked to disc degeneration.

*Kang et al. compared 18 herniated lumbar discs to 8 normal lumbar disc specimens to delineate the biochemical profile of herniated intervertebral discs.* **The authors noted** a significant increase in MMP activity, nitric oxide, prostaglandin E2, and interleukin-6. However, interleukin-1-alpha, interleukin-1-beta, tumor necrosis factor-alpha, and substance P did not have increased activity. **The authors postulate** the roles of the aforementioned molecules may contribute or, at a minimum, predispose one to the onset of disc herniations.

*Bachmeier et al. performed a molecular analysis of 37 herniated disc specimens (average age: 53.8), attempting to quantify levels of MMPs in the pathologic tissue.* **The authors found** several MMP subtypes being expressed, with MMP-3 (previously termed stromelysin-1) followed by MMP-7 being the most highly expressed subtype. **The authors conclude** these MMPs may serve as future targets for therapeutic intervention to treat or slow the degenerative cascade.

Figures A & B represent T2-weighted MRI exhibiting a large paracentral disc herniation at the L4-5 disc level.

Incorrect Answers
Answers 2 & 4: Chondroitin sulfate and Aggrecan are extracellular matrix molecules degraded by MMPs.
Answer 3: Caspases are similarly proteolytic enzymes but have a prominent role in cellular apoptosis and have not been found in increased amounts.
Answer 5: Decorin assists in regulating the diameter of tendon collagen fibers and is not found in excess concentrations.
