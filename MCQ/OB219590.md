## Options

1. 25-hydroxyvitamin D of 45 ng/mL
2. 25-hydroxyvitamin D of 35 ng/mL
3. 1,25-hydroxyvitamin D of 35 ng/mL
4. 25-hydroxyvitamin D of 25 ng/mL
5. 1,25-hydroxyvitamin D of 25 ng/mL

## Correct option

**4. 25-hydroxyvitamin D of 25 ng/mL**

## Answer explanation

This 40-year-old female sustained a mid-shaft humerus fracture with minimal callus formation at four months post-injury. A patient with a 25-hydroxyvitamin D level between 20-30 ng/mL is considered "vitamin D insufficient" (Answer 4).

Vitamin D is a fat-soluble steroid known to have a myriad of effects on the body. Most relevant to orthopedics, vitamin D plays a crucial role in calcium homeostasis, functioning to increase calcium levels through increased resorption of bone via osteoclast activation (in addition to increased intestinal calcium absorption). Chronically low vitamin D levels result in lower bone density and have been found to compromise bone healing in the setting of fractures, resulting in atrophic nonunions. When evaluating potential atrophic nonunions, vitamin D levels are measured via serum 25-hydroxyvitamin D. Levels between 20 and 30 ng/mL are considered "insufficient," while levels below 20 ng/mL are considered "deficient."

*Patton et al. reviewed vitamin D and its relevance to orthopedics, beginning with the hormone's source and metabolism.* *They then discussed its musculoskeletal implications when patients become insufficient or deficient,* **noting** poorer rates of fracture healing and fusion healing following spinal instrumentation. Ultimately, **the authors recommend** aiming for a 25-hydroxyvitamin D level between 30 and 40 ng/mL for those with low vitamin D levels.

*Arshi et al. examined the implications of vitamin D concerning periprosthetic joint infections by systematically reviewing the previously published literature on the matter.* The authors determined screening at-risk patients for 25-hydroxyvitamin D preoperatively amassed $1,504,857 in savings per 10,000 cases. **The authors conclude** that 25-hydroxyvitamin D screening and repletion would likely be a beneficial cost-saving mechanism by reducing the incidence of periprosthetic joint infections.

*Amrein et al. reviewed the current worldwide concepts of low vitamin D levels,* **highlighting** the inconsistencies regarding the definition of vitamin D deficiency, the lack of robust data demonstrating the clinical benefit of vitamin D supplementation, and poorly defined guidelines for recommended daily dosing. However, **the authors note** several benefits of vitamin D use shown throughout the literature, including reduced mortality, anti-infective properties, several benefits for pregnant patients (reduced incidence of gestational diabetes, low birth weight, and preeclampsia), and anti-cancer properties in addition to the clear benefits on the musculoskeletal system. Ultimately, **they conclude** vitamin D supplementation may be beneficial and advocate its use, given its benign side-effect profile.

Figures A and B represent orthogonal Xray views of a left humerus, demonstrating a transverse mid-shaft humerus fracture. Figures C and D demonstrate orthogonal Xray films, again demonstrating the aforementioned fracture with minimal callus formation.

Incorrect answers
Answers 1 & 2: These values represent normal vitamin D levels
Answers 3 & 5: 1,25-hydroxyvitamin D is considered the active form of vitamin D and has been shown to estimate vitamin D stores poorly; thus, it is not the appropriate form of vitamin D to be measured.
