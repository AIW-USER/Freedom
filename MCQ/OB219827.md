## Question

A 44-year-old man presents to your clinic after sustaining an injury while deadlifting at the gym. The patient reports that he felt a pop in his right elbow. Physical examination and ultrasound confirm the diagnosis of a distal biceps rupture. Which of the following strategies can be used to reduce the risk of developing radioulnar synostosis following distal biceps repair?

## Options

1. 7.5mg oral indomethacin taken once daily for 6 weeks following surgery
2. 75mg oral indomethacin taken once daily for 6 weeks following surgery
3. A one time 500 cGy dose of radiation to the area 24 hours prior to surgery
4. A one time 500 cGy dose of radiation to the area 24 hours after surgery
5. Utilization of a two incision technique

## Correct option

**2. 75mg oral indomethacin taken once daily for 6 weeks following surgery**

## Answer explanation

Radioulnar synostosis secondary to heterotopic ossification (HO) is a known complication following distal biceps repair and can lead to profound loss of pronation/supination. Oral indomethacin (75 mg) taken once daily for 6 weeks has demonstrated a protective effect regarding the development of HO following distal biceps repair (Answer 2).

HO is the formation of bone in atypical, extraskeletal tissue that may occur following localized trauma, in the setting of a neurological injury, or as a post-surgical complication. Patients will typically present with painless loss of motion of the affected joint. The diagnosis is made radiographically and hallmarked by soft-tissue ossification with a sharp demarcation from surrounding soft tissues. The treatment focuses on prevention with oral indomethacin or perioperative radiation.

In patients with symptomatic HO, the definitive treatment is surgical resection. While the optimal timing for surgical intervention remains hotly debated. Historically, the best results were seen when resection was performed between 1 and 2 years after onset. Surgery within one year of onset is safe if the patient's radiographs demonstrate bony maturation (i.e., well-defined margins and bridging trabeculi). Discrete waiting periods should be avoided (i.e., > 12-24 months), as the restoration of adjacent joint motion should remain the priority, and earlier intervention can help to prevent soft-tissue contractures.

*Bergeron et al. provide a review of the management of posttraumatic radioulnar synostosis.* **The authors note** that loss of pronation/supination can significantly interfere with the performance of activities of daily living. **The authors highlight** that normal active forearm rotation in healthy subjects is 76° pronation and 82° supination. In contrast, on average, patients with radioulnar synostosis exhibit a pronation/supination arc of 14° to 4°, **highlighting** the marked consequences of this phenomenon.

*Costopoulos et al. report on the utilization of indomethacin to prevent postoperative radioulnar synostosis after distal biceps repair.* *Their study included 124 patients, 112 of which met inclusion criteria.* Seven patients underwent a single-incision repair, and 105 underwent a two-incision repair. Of those 112 patients, 104 received indomethacin. The post-operative synostosis rate was 0.96% for those who received prophylaxis versus 37.5% in the untreated group (p > .001). Overall, **the authors conclude** that oral indomethacin is a safe and effective modality for preventing radioulnar synostosis.

*Streubel et al. review the open surgical releases for contractures of the elbow.* **The authors note** that flexion is recovered by releasing posterior tethers and removing anterior impinging structures. Extension is recovered by releasing anterior tethers and removing any impingement between the olecranon tip and fossa. **The authors note** that identification and transposition of the ulnar nerve is recommended in the presence of long-standing nerve dysfunction or when correction of profound loss of elbow flexion is anticipated.

Incorrect Answers
Answer 1: The optimal dose for HO prophylaxis is 75 mg daily for 10 days - 6 weeks.
Answers 3-4: Doses less than 550 cGy are ineffective in preventing HO.
Answer 5: A single-incision technique is associated with a lower rate of postoperative radioulnar synostosis.
