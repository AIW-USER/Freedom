## Question

A 5-year boy with a history of a challenging birth due to macrosomia presents to your clinic for evaluation of his right shoulder. He holds his right arm in adduction with his hand close to his stomach. He is able to actively obtain 5 degrees of shoulder external rotation. His deltoid strength is 4/5, and he has normal hand function. Which of the following is a contraindication to performing the Hoffer procedure (transfer of the Latissimus dorsi and teres major to the posterior rotator cuff) in this patient?

## Options

1. Severe internal rotation contracture
2. No motor function of subscapularis
3. Positive Hornblower's test
4. Glenoid anteroversion
5. Convex glenoid

## Correct option

**5. Convex glenoid**

## Answer explanation

This patient has Erb's palsy with resulting internal rotation (IR) contracture. The Hoffer procedure (transfer of the Latissimus dorsi and teres major transfer to the posterior superior rotator cuff ) to assist with external rotation (ER) is contraindicated in patients with glenohumeral dysplasia.

The Hoffer procedure, which is a variation of the Severe-L'Episcopo procedure, is a transfer of the Latissimus dorsi and teres major to the posterior and superior rotator cuff to assist with external rotation in the setting of internal rotation contracture and weakness of the external rotators. This is generally a sequala of obstetric brachial plexus palsy (most commonly Erb's) in which the external rotators (teres minor, infraspinatus and posterior portion of deltoid) are nonfunction or very weak. Tendon transfer can induce glenohumeral remodeling in the growing child whereas patients with advanced glenohumeral dysplasia (convex or absent glenoid, complete dislocation, humeral head flattening) may not be able to remodel sufficiently. As such, contraindications to this procedure are severe glenohumeral dysplasia (for which proximal humerus derotational osteotomy is performed), weakness of teres major/latissimus dorsi (less than 4/5) and significant deltoid weakness (less than 2/5).

*Strecker et al. performed a retrospective review of 16 patients who underwent Sever-L'Episcopo transfers for obstetrical birth palsy with a follow-up of 2-6 years.* **The authors noted** a substantial improvement in shoulder external rotation, as well as subjective functional improvement in all patients. They did note 3 transient and 1 permanent axillary nerve palsies resulting from the procedure. **The authors conclude** that the procedure is an excellent method to increase ER in patients with obstetrical brachial plexus palsy.

*Hoffer et al. described a modification to the Sever L-Episcopo transfer in 11 patients with obstetrical brachial plexus palsy.* *They describe the transfer of the Latissimus dorsi and teres major to posterior to the long head of the triceps and sutured to the rotator cuff as high as possible.* **The authors noted** that the procedure significantly increased ER (average of 45 degrees) and abduction (average of 64 degrees) in their patient population. **The authors concluded** that the procedure has the advantage of increasing both ER and abduction with good long term outcomes.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: A severe internal rotation contracture would be an INDICATION to perform the Hoffer procedure.
Answer 2: A functional subscapularis is not necessary to perform the Hoffer procedure. In fact, some practitioners routinely partially release the subscapularis to help offset the internal rotation contracture.
Answer 3: A positive hornblower's test is indicative of a weak teres minor which is to be expected in this patient. A functional teres MAJOR is needed to perform the Hoffer procedure.
Answer 4: Severe glenoid dysplasia noted by glenoid retroversion, a convex glenoid, persistent posterior humeral head subluxation, and humeral head flattening is a contraindication to performing a Hoffer procedure.
