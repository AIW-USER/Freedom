## Question

A 32-year-old runner presents with persistent left great toe pain that has been ongoing for 6 months. She's tried a rigid-sole running shoe, anti-inflammatories, and orthotics with no relief. She has pain at extremes of dorsiflexion and with pushoff, but no midrange pain. She has 10 degrees of dorsiflexion. She decides to take time off running and undergo a dorsal cheilectomy. Figures A and B show her preoperative images. Intraoperatively the surgeon removes the dorsal 30% of the metatarsal head and is able to achieve 20 degrees of dorsiflexion. What is the next best step in management?

## Options

1. Wound closure and weight bearing as tolerated in a post op shoe
2. Flexor digitorum longus to extensor digitorum longus tendon transfer
3. Moberg procedure
4. Keller procedure
5. Metatarsophalangeal joint arthrodesis

## Correct option

**3. Moberg procedure**

## Answer explanation

When cheilectomy fails to achieve at least 30 degrees of dorsiflexion in runners a Moberg procedure should be performed to increase dorsiflexion while sacrificing plantarflexion.

In grade II hallux ridigus patients will complain of pain during terminal dorsiflexion, pain with pushoff, and dorsal shoe irritation. Nonsurgical treamtent should be attempted first including activity modification, shoe modification, anti-inflammatories, and orthotics. When these fail surgical management with cheilectomy with or without dorsal closing wedge osteotomy of the proximal phalanx (Moberg procedure). The goal is to maximize intraoperative dorsiflexion as some range of motion may be lost postoperatively. Moberg procedure is indicated when cheilectomy fails to provide at least 30-40 degrees of dorsiflexion. The Moberg procedure sacrifices some plantarflexion in exchange for improved dorsiflexion.

*Coughlin et al. retrospectively reviewed the outcomes of ninety-three feet at a mean of 9.6 years following cheilectomy.* **They found** significant improvement in dorsiflexion, total motion, postoperative pain, and AOFAS scores. **They conclude** that cheilectomy can provide predictable success in Grade I, II, and select Grade III hallux ridigus.

*Waizy et al. prospectively following 60 patients with symptomatic hallux rigidus who underwent cheilectomy with or without additional Kessel-Bonney osteotomy.* **They found** that long-term follow-up shows pain reduction and improved dorsiflexion. **They conclude** joint preserving procedures should be used in grade I and II hallux ridigus.

Figure A and B show lateral and AP radiographs of a patient with dorsal osteotphytes and <50% joint space narrowing. Illustration A shows a depiction of a cheilectomy with a dorsal closing wedge osteotomy of the proximal phalanx

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Wound closure would be inappropriate in this instance as the patient hasn't achieved adequate dorsiflexion.
Answer 2: Tendon transfer would not be indicated or improve the range of motion.
Answer 4: Keller resection arthroplasty would not be indicated in a high demand patient with preserved joint space.
Answer 5: Metatarsophalangeal joint arthrodesis would be indicated in cases of severe arthritis.
