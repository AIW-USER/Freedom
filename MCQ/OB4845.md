## Answer explanation

This patient has a lipoma. This corresponds with Figure C (Answer 2).

The differentiation of lipoma from well-differentiated liposarcoma is sometimes a diagnostic dilemma. On imaging, lipomas demonstrate homogeneous fatty attenuation on CT and homogeneous signal intensity identical to fat on MR imaging.

*Rougraff et al. evaluated the accuracy of MRI in diagnosing lipomatous masses.* **They found** that MRI characteristics associated with benign disease include: smaller tumor size, uniformly homogeneous signal, homogeneous high T1 and T2 signals and low STIR signal comparable to normal fat. **They concluded** that MRI can accurately predict a benign lipoma.

Figure A is a lipoma in the supinator muscle. The lesion has a signal intensity and character identical to that of subcutaneous adipose tissue. Illustration A is a T1-weighted MRI showing a well-differentiated liposarcoma in the posterior thigh compartment. There are thin (arrowhead) and thick (arrows) septae, and a nodular area of nonadipose tissue (*). Illustration B is a coronal STIR MR image showing Ewing sarcoma of the left distal femur with prominent extramedullary soft tissue component. Illustration C is a coronal STIR T2-weighted MR image of chondrosarcoma showing a well-defined lobulated hyperintense lesion of the proximal humerus. It demonstrates fibrous septae and involves soft tissue on medial and lateral aspects (arrows).

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Figure B shows myxoid liposarcoma. Note the increased cellularity, myxoid stroma and fine network of vascular channels.
Answer 3: Figure D shows chondrosarcoma demonstrating increased cellularity and cellular atypia, amidst a chondroid stroma.
Answer 4: Figure E shows Ewing sarcoma, characterized by small, round blue cells.
Answer 5: Figure F shows normal lung tissue, characterized by alveolar spaces.
