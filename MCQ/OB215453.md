## Question

A 35-year-old male sustains a fall onto his outstretched small finger during a flag-football playoff game. He notes immediate pain and deformity to the digit, and he is brought to the local emergency department by his wife. Radiographs are obtained and demonstrate a dorsal dislocation of his fifth metacarpophalangeal joint without any associated fracture. The patient is seen by the orthopedic resident, who is able to successfully reduce his injury. What is the recommended reduction maneuver for this injury?

## Options

1. Direct pressure over the dorsal aspect of the proximal phalanx with the wrist in extension
2. Direct pressure over the dorsal aspect of the proximal phalanx with the wrist in flexion
3. Direct pressure over the volar aspect of the metacarpal with the wrist in flexion
4. Longitudinal traction with the wrist positioned in extension
5. Longitudinal traction with the wrist positioned in flexion

## Correct option

**2. Direct pressure over the dorsal aspect of the proximal phalanx with the wrist in flexion**

## Answer explanation

The patient sustained a simple dorsal dislocation of the fifth metacarpophalangeal joint (MCPJ). The appropriate closed-reduction technique for a dorsal MCPJ dislocation is direct pressure over the dorsal aspect of the proximal phalanx with the wrist in flexion in order to relax the intrinsic and extrinsic flexors.

MCPJ dislocations are rare injuries and are usually caused by a fall and subsequent hyperextension injury to the MCPJ of the affected digit. The most common direction of dislocation is dorsal. Most simple dislocations can be managed with closed reduction. Care should be taken to avoid longitudinal traction during closed reduction as this may displace the volar plate or flexor tendon(s) into the joint and convert a simple dislocation into an irreducible injury. Aside from iatrogenic injury, other indications that the injury may be irreducible include deviation of the digit toward the adjacent central digit with a palpable palmar prominence, skin dimpling in the proximal palmar crease, interposed sesamoid on radiographs, or bayonet positioning of the proximal phalanx.

*Dinh et al. performed a review of metacarpophalangeal joint dislocations.* **They noted** that the appropriate closed reduction maneuver is wrist flexion to relax the flexors followed by a dorsal-to-volar directed force over the proximal phalanx. **They highlighted** the importance of avoiding longitudinal traction during closed reduction.

*Rubin et al. reviewed 5 patients who underwent open reduction of complex MCPJ dislocations.* *The mean follow-up was 13 years.* **They reported** that all patients had a full function in the hand at the latest follow-up. They did note, however, that there was a mild loss in MCPJ flexion in 2/5 patients, that grip strength was reduced in 2/5 patients, and that 2/5 patients who had a metacarpal head fracture treated with screw fixation demonstrated arthritic changes. **The authors conclude** that early appropriate treatment of MCPJ dislocations leads to excellent outcomes.

*Vadala and Ward reviewed 22 MCPJ dislocations that required surgical reduction.* **They reported** that operative time was 14 minutes shorter in patients who were treated with a dorsal approach. Among those treated with a volar approach, 42% required a second dorsal approach. **The authors conclude** that a dorsal approach leads to shorter surgical time and a decreased need for a secondary approach.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The wrist should be placed in flexion, not extension, in order to relax the intrinsic and extrinsic flexors.
Answer 3: Pressure should be applied to the dorsal aspect of the proximal phalanx, not the volar aspect of the metacarpal, in order to close reduced a simple dorsal dislocation.
Answers 4 and 5: Longitudinal traction should be avoided, as this can pull the volar plate into the joint and convert the injury into an irreducible injury.
