## Question

A 1-year-old female presents to your clinic with a trunk asymmetry noted by her parents. Radiographs confirm a thoracic scoliosis with a Cobb angle of 35 degrees. Further radiographic evaluation reveals a rib vertebral angle difference (RVAD) greater than 20 degrees and a rib at the apex of the curve that crosses the midline (Phase 2 rib). Based on these findings, what is the MOST appropriate initial management for this patient?

## Options

1. Bracing with a thoracolumbosacral orthosis (TLSO).
2. Observation with serial radiographs every 6 months.
3. Spinal fusion with instrumentation.
4. Serial derotational casting with a specialized scoliosis cast.
5. Vertebral body tethering (VBT).

## Correct option

**4. Serial derotational casting with a specialized scoliosis cast.**

## Answer explanation

A young patient with a progressive, early-onset scoliosis is best treated with serial derotational casting to correct the deformity while allowing for continued spinal growth (Answer 4).

The patient has signs of a progressive early-onset scoliosis, specifically a Cobb angle over 25 degrees, a high rib vertebral angle difference (RVAD), and a Phase 2 rib. For very young children with these progressive curves, the goal of treatment is to control the curve without stopping spinal growth, as growth is essential for pulmonary development. Serial derotational casting is the standard non-operative treatment that uses a specialized cast to correct the deformity in three dimensions over time. This approach has been shown to slow or even halt curve progression and, in some cases, can result in complete resolution, avoiding the need for a definitive fusion surgery later in life.

*Fedorak et al. evaluated the long-term outcomes of Mehta casting in children with idiopathic early-onset scoliosis.* **They noted** that casting was effective in preventing curve progression and often avoided the need for surgical fusion. **They concluded** that casting can be a successful treatment for early-onset scoliosis, with outcomes sustained over at least 5 years.

*Fedorak et al. conducted a multicenter review on the age-stratified outcomes of Mehta casting for idiopathic early-onset scoliosis.* **They noted** that younger patients, particularly those under 2 years of age, had the highest success rates with casting. **They concluded** that early intervention with serial derotational casting is most effective in this very young patient population.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Bracing is typically used for older children with larger, but less rapidly progressive curves, and is not as effective as casting for this age group.
Answer 2: Observation is inappropriate for a progressive curve with a Cobb angle greater than 25 degrees and poor prognostic radiographic signs.
Answer 3: This is a major surgical procedure that halts spinal growth and is reserved for older children with severe, unresponsive curves.
Answer 5: VBT is a surgical growth-sparing technique, but it is not appropriate for a 1-year-old and is typically performed on older children with significant remaining growth.
