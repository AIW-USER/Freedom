## Question

A 17-year-old female presents to your clinic with a primary complaint of right wrist contracture, which she and her parents state has been present for many years. She does have a diagnosis of spastic cerebral palsy and she is concerned that she is no longer able to use the extremity in a functional manner because of the significance of the contracture. You discuss treatment options with the patient and ultimately settle on performing a wrist arthrodesis. Which of the following is true regarding wrist arthrodesis as definitive management for this condition?

## Options

1. There is a higher failure rate in patients with spastic subtypes of cerebral palsy
2. Union rates are < 75% despite high rates of patient satisfaction
3. Should not be performed concomitantly with proximal row carpectomy
4. Provides best correction in patients who lack ability to passively correct deformity
5. Should be performed only after failure of FCU to ECRB tendon transfer

## Correct option

**4. Provides best correction in patients who lack ability to passively correct deformity**

## Answer explanation

Wrist arthrodesis can be used as a treatment strategy for patients who have cerebral palsy (CP) or other neuromuscular conditions that cause wrist contractures that are not passively correctable.

Wrist deformities in patients with CP can be quite complex. The most common wrist deformity in these patients is flexion and ulnar deviation, given the innate deforming forces crossing the joint. These patients typically have weakness and difficulty with wrist extension and forearm pronation. In assessing these patients, it is crucial to document their ability to passively correct the deformity as the lack of a supple wrist may preclude them from having success with stretching, splinting, botox and other non-invasive options. FCU to ECRB or FCU to EDC tendon transfers can also be used but are not good options in patients without a supple wrist. Wrist fusion, usually performed with proximal row carpectomy, can be a good option in these patients in order to correct deformity and provide an improvement in daily function and hygiene. It is typically performed using a dorsal plate with high rates of union and patient satisfaction.

*Alexander et al. review 19 patients for which wrist arthrodesis was used to correct flexion and ulnar deviation deformities in CP patients.* **They noted** significant functional improvement in 84% of patients. **They concluded** that, when performed in the correct patient population, wrist arthrodesis provided a great deal of functional improvement.

*Duquette et al. discuss the management of spasticity in the forearm and wrist.* *They talk about different causes of spasticity in this region and discuss basic principles regarding surgical management.* *They go on to talk about what procedures are indicated in certain clinical situations and how to manage postoperative outcomes and expectations.*

*Van Heest et al. performed another review of wrist arthrodesis in CP patients.* *They performed a dorsal-based wrist arthrodesis in 41 patients with spastic wrist deformities and noted a union rate of 98% with a relatively low rate of complication.* Additionally, **they noted** significant improvements in outcome scores related to appearance, function and hygiene.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: There is no evidence of a higher failure rate in patients who have spastic CP. In fact, wrist arthrodesis is appropriate for this patient subset if they are suffering from significant contracture.
Answer 2: Union rates are thought to be >95%, as is patient satisfaction.
Answer 3: Proximal row carpectomy is commonly performed alongside wrist arthrodesis because it decreases soft tissue tension and provides bone graft for the fusion.
Answer 5: FCU-ECRB tendon transfer is indicated in patients who have voluntary control of their wrists with better sensibility; these patients must have the ability to passively correct the wrist deformity in order for this procedure to be successful.
