## Question

A 10-year-old girl presents to your office with complaints of bilateral hip and leg pain over the last year. Her parents tell you she also has a history of a seizure disorder which was diagnosed two years ago. A radiograph of her lower extremities obtained in your office is shown in Figure A. You have suspicion for a bony dysplasia and obtain blood work which reveals severe anemia. What enzymatic/genetic defect is the most likely etiology of this patient's symptoms?

![[q219284-a-osteopetrosis.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. PMP22
2. FGFR2
3. COMP
4. DTD sulfate transport protein
5. Carbonic anhydrase II

## Correct option

**5. Carbonic anhydrase II**

## Answer explanation

Osteopetrosis is most commonly caused by a defect in the carbonic anhydrase II enzyme that causes osteoclast dysfunction (Answer choice 5).

Osteopetrosis is an uncommon disorder that leads to abnormal bone turnover. It is most commonly caused by the inability to acidify Howship's lacunae due to a defective carbonic anhydrase II enzyme or a chloride channel dysfunction within osteoclasts. This leads to dense, sclerotic bone and the obliteration of the medullary canal. This disease has both orthopedic and systemic manifestations. Systemically, this leads to pancytopenia due to the destruction of bone marrow and cranial nerve palsies as the skull is commonly affected. Orthopedically, low back and hip pain are common as is osteoarthritis development in these areas. Fractures also occur in about half of patients with this diagnosis and coxa vara is frequently seen. It is important to understand there are different types of osteopetrosis, all with a spectrum of disease severity, and that symptoms can be variable.

*Armstrong et al. reviewed the orthopedic management of osteopetrosis,* **noting** its multiple different inheritance patterns. They reviewed a variety of different fracture management strategies for the lower extremities, **noting** that many were treated appropriately by closed means. Similarly, most upper extremity fractures healed well with closed reduction and casting in this population.

*Landa et al. published a JAAOS review article looking a the orthopedic management of patients with osteopetrosis.* *They went over the common radiographic features including bony sclerosis, rugger jersey spine appearance, and end bone formation.* Clinically, **they discuss** that cranial nerve palsies and pancytopenia are common and orthopedically patients often have fractures, coxa vara, arthritis, and osteomyelitis.

*Strickland and Berry reviewed total joint arthroplasty for patients with osteopetrosis.* These patients are more apt to develop arthritis and the authors sought to find out if there was an increased risk of complication in this population. During their case study of only five patients, they did encounter some technical challenges including the difficulty of reaming the hard, sclerotic bone which increased operative time but overall noted that these patients can be successfully managed with arthroplasty.

Figure A is the AP radiograph of a skeletally immature individual with diffuse osteopetrosis.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: PMP22 gene abnormality is present in Charcot-Marie-Tooth (CMT) disease which is caused by a duplication of chromosome 17.
Answer 2: FGFR2 gene mutations are responsible for Apert's syndrome which is characterized by complex syndactyly of the hands/feet, craniosynostosis, and hypertelorism.
Answer 3: COMP (cartilage oligometric matrix protein) mutations can cause abnormal type II collagen function and lead to the delay of epiphyseal ossification, known as multiple epiphyseal dysplasia (MED).
Answer 4: Mutations in the DTD sulfate transport protein are present in dystrophic dysplasia which is a rhizomelic form of dwarfism best known for the characteristic "cauliflower ears," and hitchhiker's thumb.
