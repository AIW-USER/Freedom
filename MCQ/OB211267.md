## Options

1. On physical exam he will likely have hyper-reflexic patellar tendon reflexes
2. Tumor cells localize to vertebral body via binding osteocalcin
3. Osteolytic lesions are due to local release of endothelin-1
4. Radiation should be performed followed immediately by surgical decompression
5. Embolization should be performed before surgical decompression

## Correct option

**1. On physical exam he will likely have hyper-reflexic patellar tendon reflexes**

## Answer explanation

The patient is presenting with metastatic colon cancer with thoracic spinal cord compression and associated neurologic deficits. Of the answer choices, the patient will most likely have hyper-reflexive patellar tendon reflexes due to metastatic epidural spinal cord compression resulting in upper motor neuron signs.

Metastatic disease is the most common cause of destructive bone lesions in adults. The thoracic spine is most commonly involved. Treatment of painful lesions without spinal cord compression involves palliative radiation. In cases of metastatic disease with neurologic deficits, the ideal treatment involves spinal cord decompression with or without adjuvant radiation. Separation surgery allows for the safe delivery of stereotactic radiation by developing space between the spinal cord and the tumor. This permits a high dose of radiation to be delivered to the metastatic lesion while limiting exposure to the spinal cord and neural elements.

*Rose et al. reviewed the literature regarding the evaluation and management of metastatic disease of the thoracic and lumbar spine.* *The authors cited literature that supported the management of metastatic lesions with chemotherapy, steroids, and radiotherapy when there were no neurologic symptoms or instability.* Lesions that presented with neurologic deficits and instability were reportedly better managed with surgical decompression and stabilization. The authors summarized that increased metastatic disease of the thoracic spine is to be expected due to decreased cardiovascular mortality and improved cancer management.

*Laufer et al. performed a retrospective review of patients with metastatic epidural spinal cord compression treated with separation surgery and either low-dose or high-dose hypofractionated stereotactic radiosurgery.* **The authors found** a 4.1% recurrence rate a 1-year following high-dose hypofractionated stereotactic radiosurgery compared to 22.2% with low-dose hypofractionated therapy. **They concluded** that high-dose hypofractionated therapy following separation surgery was a superior treatment in the setting of metastatic epidural spinal cord compression.

Figure A is the sagittal T2 MRI of the thoracic spine with a pathologic vertebral compression fracture at T7 and spinal cord compression from a metastatic lesion without visible CSF consistent with grade 3 compression, referencing the epidural spinal cord compression scale. Figure B is the histologic section of small cell carcinoma.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 2: Osteocalcin is the most abundant protein in the bone matrix next to collagen. Tumor cells that metastasize to vertebral bodies bind to endothelial cells via integrin adhesion molecules.
Answer 3: Osteolytic lesions are the result of local upregulation receptor activator nuclear kappa-B. Endothelin-1 had been implicated in the development of osteoblastic lesions commonly seen with metastatic prostate cancer, and less common breast cancer.
Answer 4: When patients present with neurologic deficits surgical decompression should be performed prior to radiation. This allows for safer delivery of radiation without harming the neural elements.
Answer 5: Embolization prior to surgical decompression is usually performed in lesions involving renal carcinoma due to the high tumor vascularity and to minimize surgical related blood loss. Metastatic colon cancer does not require preoperative embolization prior to decompression and stabilization.
