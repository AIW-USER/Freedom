## Question

A 72-year-old male with no significant past medical history presents to the clinic with the chief complaint of right shoulder pain. The patient states he underwent reverse total shoulder arthroplasty two years prior without any issues. He had a single dislocation event one year ago (Figure A) that was well reduced under sedation. However, he continues to endorse increasing shoulder pain. Radiographs confirm his shoulder remains well reduced and his labs now show an ESR of 80 mm/hr (normal 0-22 mm/hr) and CRP of 2.3 mg/dL (normal <0.5mg/dL). Which of the following is the most commonly reported organism involved in this patient’s current presentation?

## Answer explanation

This patient presents with increasing shoulder pain and elevated inflammatory markers, consistent with a deep periprosthetic joint infection (PJI). The most identified organism in shoulder PJI is Cutibacterium acnes, a gram-positive rod-shaped organism (Figure E).

Deep periprosthetic joint infections within the shoulder are difficult conditions to manage with currently no standardized treatment guidelines. The overall incidence is low, occurring in 0-5% of patients after shoulder arthroplasty. Cutibacterium acnes is a low-virulence organism that poses a diagnostic challenge given that traditional diagnostic tests have low sensitivity due to the lack of an inflammatory response. Many patients can present with insidious onset of pain with deceptively low inflammatory markers. While joint aspiration can aid in the diagnosis, it has low sensitivity when compared to synovial tissue biopsy. This patient presented with increasing shoulder pain and elevated inflammatory markers years from surgery, which is consistent with a chronic shoulder PJI. Patients who have multiple medical comorbidities and cannot undergo surgical treatment can be treated with antibiotic suppression alone. Debridement with antibiotics and implant retention (DAIR) is considered appropriate in the setting of an acute periprosthetic joint infection within 5-6 weeks after initial arthroplasty placement. In general, two-stage revision is the most widely accepted treatment option for chronic PJIs in healthy patients who are fit to undergo multiple surgeries.

*Brown and colleagues performed a retrospective review of their management of shoulder PJIs utilizing two-stage revision over the course of a decade.* Their technique involved a first-stage synovial debridement, implant removal, and antibiotic spacer placement. After a 6-week course of antibiotics, a two-week drug holiday was utilized followed by joint aspiration to ensure no active infection. The second stage involved the removal of the spacer and the placement of new implants. **They reported** an 80% infection eradication with a mean of three-year follow-up. **The authors concluded** that a two-staged treatment for chronic shoulder PJI is supported by their institutional protocol and long-term results.

*Buchalter and colleagues performed a similar case series and literature review regarding two-stage revision arthroplasty for shoulder PJI.* **They reported** a 75% infection eradication using this approach with good to fair long-term American Shoulder and Elbow Society (ASES) functional outcome scores. **They concluded** that 2-stage revisions are an effective treatment for shoulder PJIs.

Figure A shows radiographic imaging of this patient’s anteriorly dislocated reverse total shoulder arthroplasty. Figure C shows gram-positive cocci arranged in chains, consistent with beta-hemolytic streptococci species. Figure D shows gram-negative rods, consistent with Escherichia coli (E. coli). Figure E shows gram-positive rods, consistent with cutibacterium acnes (C. acnes). Figure F shows budding yeast cells, consistent with Candida albicans. Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Staphylococcus aureus or Staphylococcus epidermidis are considered the most common organisms found in hip and knee periprosthetic joint infections, however, they are not the most common organisms in shoulder periprosthetic joint infections.
Answer 2: Streptococci species are rarely encountered as the cause of shoulder periprosthetic joint infections.
Answer 3. E. coli is similarly a rarely encountered cause of shoulder periprosthetic joint infections.
Answer 5: Fungal periprosthetic joint infections are exceedingly rare and difficult to treat.
