Chapter: Pediatric Orthopedics MCQs — Congenital Vertical Talus (CVT)

## Question

Which is not true about congenital vertical talus? (FNB Pattern Paper 2015)

## Options

1. Talocalcaneal angle is > 40°
2. Calcaneal gait
3. Surgery should be done before 2 years of age
4. Rigid rocker bottom foot deformity with concave surface

## Correct option

**4. Rigid rocker bottom foot deformity with concave surface**

## Answer explanation

Congenital convex pes valgus or congenital vertical talus is a condition characterized by multiple soft-tissue, joint and osseous abnormalities which result in a severe rigid flat-foot deformity. It is more correctly described as “teratologic dorsolateral dislocation of the talocalcaneonavicular joint”. In around 50% cases it is associated with neuromuscular disease or a syndrome. Common diseases associated with congenital VT are-

- Myelomeningocele
- Arthrogryposis
- Diastematomyelia
- congenital dislocation of the hip
- cerebral palsy
- spinal muscular atrophy

Oblique talus is anatomic variant that can be differentiated by the reduction of talonavicular joint on forced plantar flexion of the foot.

Physical examination: Patient presents with rigid rocker bottom deformity of the foot with convex plantar surface. Hind foot is fixed in equino-valgus and the forefoot is abducted and dorsiflexed. Soft tissue contracture is a prominent feature of CVT and includes contractures of tendo Achilles, peroneal tendons, EHL, EDL and Tibialis anterior tendons.

Gait: weak push off produces calcaneal gait (excessive heel contact, limited forefoot contact)

Radiological examination: Lateral X ray shows vertical talus and dorsal dislocation of the navicular. Meary's angle > 20° (between line of longitudinal axis of talus and longitudinal axis of 1st metatarsal).

 On AP view talocalcaneal angle is > 40° (20-40° is normal). Forced or stress plantar flexion lateral view is diagnostic. Diagnosis is made if persistent dorsal dislocation of the talonavicular joint is seen on the forced view.

Treatment:

In the early period after birth- Cast by reverse ponseti technique. Surgical treatment is preferred before the age of 2 years. If treatment is delayed beyond 2 years of age, more aggressive procedures must be employed.

In a child younger than 2 years: Extensive release with lengthening of tendons, reduction of talonavicular joint and fixation procedures.

In a child with a neural tube defect, younger than 2 years of age: Extensive release with tendon transfer procedures, reduction of talonavicular joint and fixation procedures is the treatment of choice.

In a child older than 2 years of age: Extensive release with tendon transfer procedures is preferred. Bony procedures are reserved for older children>4 years old.

Answer: 4
