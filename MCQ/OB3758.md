## Question

Which of the following pelvic parameter equations as seen in Figure A is independent of the position of the pelvis?

![[q3758-a-pelvic-incidence.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. A+B
2. A+C
3. B+C
4. A-C
5. B-A

## Correct option

**2. A+C**

## Answer explanation

Pelvic incidence can be calculated by adding the pelvic tilt (Angle A) to the sacral slope (Angle C). Pelvic incidence does not vary with the position of the pelvis.

Pelvic incidence can be defined as the angle between a line drawn from the midpoint of the S1 endplate to the center of the femoral heads and a perpendicular line to the midpoint of the S1 endplate (Angle B). Pelvic incidence is a constant anatomic relationship and is independent of the position of the pelvis. Sacral slope can be defined as the angle between the superior S1 endplate and a horizontal line (Angle C). Pelvic tilt can be defined as the angle between a line from the midpoint of the S1 endpoint to the center of the femoral heads and a vertical line (Angle A). The mathematic association between these three angles can be defined as: sacral slope + pelvic tilt = pelvic incidence.

*Legaye et al. looked at pelvic incidence in a normal adult population as well as an adult population with scoliosis.* **They found** that pelvic incidence was one anatomic and constant parameter while other parameters varied depending on patient positioning. **They concluded** that pelvic incidence appears to be the main axis of sagittal balance of the spine.

*Rose et al. followed forty patients who underwent pedicle subtraction osteotomy.* **They found** that sum of pelvic incidence, lumbar lordosis, and thoracic kyphosis less than or equal to 45 degrees showed high sensitivity for predicting ideal sagittal balance. **They conclude** that pelvic incidence and thoracic kyphosis can help when correcting sagittal imbalance through pedicle subtraction osteotomy.

Figure A shows a lateral of the lumbosacral spine with pelvic tilt (Angle A), pelvic incidence (Angle B), and sacral slope (Angle C). Illustration A shows a depiction by Legaye et al of the various pelvic parameters including pelvic incidence.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The sum of pelvic tilt (Angle A) and pelvic incidence (Angle B) does not represent a clinically useful angle
Answer 3: The sum of the pelvic incidence (Angle B) and sacral slope (Angle C) does not represent a clinically useful angle
Answer 4: The difference of the pelvic tilt (Angle A) and sacral slope (Angle C) does not represent a clinically useful angle.
Answer 5: The difference of the pelvic incidence (Angle B) and pelvic tilt (Angle A) represents the sacral slope.
