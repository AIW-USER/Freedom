## Question

A 20-year-old left-hand dominant downhill skier presents with the injury shown in Figure A. After a thorough discussion of treatment options with him, he elects to undergo operative management. Which of the following is the primary goal of treating this fracture surgically?

![[q218215-a-screenshot-2023-12-29-at-12-08-29-pm.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Decreasing time to pain-free fracture union
2. Eliminating cosmetic deformity
3. Improving functional strength for elite activities
4. Reducing overall rates of complication
5. Reducing the risk of nonunion

## Correct option

**5. Reducing the risk of nonunion**

## Answer explanation

When comparing operative fixation of displaced clavicle fractures with non-operative treatment, ORIF results in lower rates of nonunion with similar functional outcome scores.

Clavicle fractures represent common traumatic injuries caused by direct impact to the shoulder girdle. Treatment can either be operative or non-operative based on patient activity and functional demands, along with the degree of initial displacement, shortening, and comminution. In general, midshaft clavicle fractures displaced >100%, with >2cm of shortening, and significant comminution have greater rates of nonunion occurring in up to 15-20% of cases. Predictors of nonunion at six weeks of non-operative treatment include palpable motion at the fracture site, lack of radiographic callous, and DASH score >40, with any combination of 2-3 of these factors increasing the risk of progression to nonunion by 60%. In general, operative treatment has primarily been cited in the literature to decrease the risk of nonunion, with successful union achieved in >94% of cases. Conflicting evidence suggests similar to improved functional scores with reduced time to union (average of 16.4 weeks when treated operatively vs. 28.4 weeks when treated non-operatively), though with increased risk of complication, including infection, neurovascular insult, and the need for additional surgery.

*Qin et al. published a meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials (RCTs) comparing open reduction and plate fixation of displaced mid-shaft clavicle fractures with non-surgical treatment.* *The authors included data from 9 RCTs and found that ORIF is advantageous over non-surgical treatment in terms of nonunion rate, malunion rate, appearance dissatisfaction rate, and shoulder appearance defect rate, with non-operative treatment showing lower rates of complication and no significant differences with respect to functional outcomes.* **They concluded** that open reduction and plate fixation yielded better efficacy than conservation treatment for displaced midshaft clavicle fractures from the perspectives of fracture healing and appearance.

*Woltz et al. performed a meta-analysis of RCTs comparing plate fixation with non-operative treatment of displaced midshaft clavicle fractures.* *The authors included 6 RCTs with 614 patients and found that the risk of nonunion was lower in the operatively treated patients.* **They concluded** that plate fixation significantly reduces the risk of nonunion, but does not have a clinically relevant advantage regarding final functional outcome.

Figure A is an AP radiograph of a left shoulder demonstrating a clavicular shaft fracture with >200% displacement, >2cm shortening, and comminution.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Though time to bony union has been suggested in the literature to be decreased with operative treatment, the time to pain-free union has not been quantified and is not the primary goal of operative treatment.
Answer 2: Eliminating cosmetic deformity from malunion is a benefit of operative intervention, but it is not the primary goal of operative treatment, as post-operative scarring and plate prominence would also be expected with operative intervention.
Answer 3: The majority of published literature demonstrates similar functional scores for both operatively and non-operatively treated clavicle fractures, without statistically significant differences in strength improvement for return to elite activities.
Answer 4: Operative treatment, in general, comes with an increased risk of overall complications, namely infection, neurovascular insult, and the need for additional surgery.
