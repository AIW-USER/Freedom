## Question

You are seeing a new 13-year-old patient in the clinic with a known diagnosis of spina bifida for evaluation of their left hip. The patient is able to ambulate around his home with mild assistance. Radiographs demonstrate a left hip dislocation. On examination of his left lower extremity, the patient has full passive range of motion, can flex and adduct the hip and actively flex and extend the knee with full strength, but cannot actively extend or abduct his hip. What is the function level classification and what is the treatment?

## Options

1. L3; contracture release
2. L3; open hip reduction
3. L4; no further intervention
4. L4; open hip reduction
5. L5; no further intervention

## Correct option

**3. L4; no further intervention**

## Answer explanation

This patient with spina bifida (myelomeningocele) has active knee extension but no active hip extension, classified as L4 level based on functional level. Current recommendations for dislocated hips in patients with spina bifida are to focus on range of motion, avoiding surgery unless contracture releases are needed, making answer choice 3 correct.

Patients with spina bifida often have hip deformities resulting from muscle imbalance and paralysis around the hip joint. Patients may present with contracture, subluxation, or dislocation. It is not uncommon for a patient to have a nonreduced hip while still maintaining an appropriate level of ambulation in the setting of spina bifida. Previously, surgeons made efforts to achieve a concentrically reduced hip in spina bifida patients with osteotomies and muscle releases. However, current recommendations focus on maintaining hip range of motion with contracture releases as needed and avoiding futile attempts at maintaining reduction.

Determining the level of myelomeningocele lesion is important in order to determine treatment and ambulatory prognosis. Classification of myelodysplasia level is based on the lowest functional level. Important hallmarks of functional levels include hip flexion and adduction (L3), knee extension (L4), and hip extension and abduction (L5). Determining the level of lesion will help treating physicians with providing appropriate orthosis and ambulation prognosis.

Swaroop and Dias provide a two-part review on orthopedic manifestations and management of spina bifida. In regards to hip pathology in patients with spina bifida, the authors make note of the current recommendations for emphasis on maintaining hip range of motion with contracture release as needed. The authors concluded that concentric hip reduction does not lead to increased ambulatory status in this population.

*Gabrieli et al. performed a study on twenty patients with lower lumbar myelomeningocele.* *The authors sought to study the effect of hip dislocation or subluxation and hip contractures on gait patterns via three-dimensional gait analysis.* Results showed gait asymmetry corresponded with the presence of hip contractures, not the presence of a dislocated or subluxated hip. **The authors concluded** that hip status (reduced, subluxated, or dislocated) should not be addressed surgically as this will not alter a patient's ambulatory status.

Answer 2: This patient's lesion is at the L4 level, given his ability to flex and adduct his hip and extend his knee but his inability to extend his hip. Open hip reduction is not indicated in these patients due to the high-risk profile and inability to maintain concentric reduction due to muscular imbalances. Furthermore, patients with appropriate ambulatory status have been shown to perform better without surgery.
Answer 4: Although the level of lesion is correct, the treatment is incorrect. Current recommendations state that a patient who is appropriately ambulating with dislocated hips should not undergo an open hip reduction.
