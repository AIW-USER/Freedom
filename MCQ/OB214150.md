## Options

1. External fixation
2. Closed reduction and syndesmosis fixation
3. Open reduction and internal fixation of proximal fibula fracture
4. Fibular osteotomy and syndesmosis open reduction internal fixation
5. Tibiotalar arthrodesis

## Correct option

**4. Fibular osteotomy and syndesmosis open reduction internal fixation**

## Answer explanation

The patient presents with a chronic Maisonneuve syndesmosis injury with evidence of proximal fibula fracture callus formation and significant fibular shortening. The next best step in management is fibular osteotomy to restore fibular length and syndesmosis open reduction and internal fixation (ORIF).

Displaced ankle fractures with evidence of syndesmosis injury should be managed operatively to restore fibular length, restore the ankle mortise, and reduce the syndesmosis. Malalignment or malunion will generally lead to progressive tibiotalar eccentric loading. Historic studies have shown that even 1 mm of a lateral talar shift will lead to a ~40% decrease in the tibiotalar contact area. In the absence of significant tibiotalar osteoarthritis, the treatment of ankle fracture malunion should be osteotomy with ORIF.

Weber and Weber discussed ankle fracture malunion. They note that malunions lead to joint instability and pathologic load transfer which ultimately may lead to arthritic joint destruction. The authors describe corrective osteotomies of the medial malleolus, lateral malleolus, posterior malleolus, and the posteromedial corner. The authors conclude that corrective osteotomies for malleolar malunions show good results in the majority of mid- and long-term studies with secondary arthrodesis needed in only 10-15% of patients.

Egger and Berkowitz describe the pathoanatomy and management of fibular malunions. They note that fibular malunion results in altered ankle joint biomechanics which often leads to the development of pain, stiffness, and premature joint degeneration. The authors describe the oblique fibular osteotomy for the reconstruction of SER-type fibular fractures. They note good functional outcomes for this procedure that allows patients to avoid arthrodesis in the majority of cases.

Weber and Simpson describe fibular shortening in the setting of ankle fractures. They note that once this fibular shortening has been recognized, a lengthening and rotational osteotomy can be conducted with the use of a special compression/distraction device and bone graft. The authors performed the procedure in 23 patients and demonstrate that reconstructive lengthening osteotomy is well worthwhile when there is absent or minimal osteoarthritic change, irrespective of the time from the original injury.

Figures A and B are the AP and lateral ankle radiographs that reveal displaced syndesmosis injury with significant fibular shortening. Figure C reveals a proximal fibula fracture with interval callus formation. Difure D reveals a normal contralateral AP of the right ankel. Illustration A reveals post-operative AP and lateral ankle fluoroscopy showing an oblique distal fibular osteotomy and syndesmosis ORIF.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1 and 2: Given the significant displacement and chronicity of the injury, an open reduction is required to restore fibular length and reduce the syndesmosis.
Answer 3: Open reduction and internal fixation of the proximal fibula fracture will not restore the syndesmosis.
Answer 5: The patient has not yet developed evidence of tibiotalar osteoarthritis and a malunion correction should be offered prior to an arthrodesis.
