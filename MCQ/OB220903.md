## Question

A 10-year-old male presents to the emergency department after a fall at a trampoline park with the elbow injury seen in Figure A. You discuss the treatment options with the patient’s father and elect to proceed with a trial of nonoperative management. Which of the following, if present, would be an indication to proceed with surgical management instead?

![[q220903-a-ob-figure-a-medial-epicondyle.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. An ipsilateral buckle fracture of the distal radius
2. The child dislocated his elbow during the fall
3. Fracture displacement measures 1.9 mm
4. Tingling on the dorsum of the hand with weakness when extending his thumb
5. The patient plays competitive video games

## Correct option

**2. The child dislocated his elbow during the fall**

## Answer explanation

This patient has sustained a medial epicondyle fracture. The management of these injuries can be controversial, but a history of elbow dislocation and instability is an indication for operative management of these fractures (Answer 2).

Medial epicondyle fractures are the third most common elbow injuries seen in the pediatric population. The mechanism is typically a fall onto the outstretched arm with a valgus load applied at the elbow. Pull of the flexor-pronator mass musculature then avulses the epicondyle from the humerus and contributes to the displacement of the fracture. The medial epicondyle is typically the last structure to fuse in the pediatric elbow (roughly fusing between 16–18 years of age) and can occur in teenagers. Management of medial epicondyle fracture is somewhat controversial. Some surgeons believe nonoperative management can be pursued even with displacement > 5 mm. Non-union can be common after nonoperative management, with some reporting only a 28% union rate with casting alone, though most of these non-unions may be asymptomatic. Absolute indications for surgery include an incarcerated fragment in the ulnohumeral joint after a dislocation, open injury, and extension into the articular surface. Relative indications for surgery include presence of ulnar nerve palsy, injury in a throwing athlete, and history of elbow instability/dislocation. Fixation of medial epicondyle fractures after elbow dislocation is thought to help restore medial elbow stability.

*Magee et al. conducted a case series of 26 pediatric patients who presented with an elbow fracture-dislocation, 16 of whom had an associated medial epicondyle fracture.* *All patients with a medial epicondyle fracture underwent surgical management.* The mean time to union for medial epicondyle fractures was roughly 40 days. Roughly 85% of patients had either an “excellent” or “good” outcome as measured by Flynn’s Criteria for Outcomes of Elbow Fractures. **The authors recommended** operative management for all medial epicondyle fractures associated with elbow instability.

*Pezzutti et al. conducted a systematic review of management of medial epicondyle fractures.* *They included 37 studies with 1022 patients.* Outcomes were generally good with either nonoperative or operative management, but **the authors noted** that union rates were significantly higher in the surgery group (96% vs 28%). **The authors concluded** that significant heterogeneity existed within the included studies in terms of standardized operative indications, particularly related to displacement, but advocated for operative management if instability or ulnar nerve symptoms were present.

Figure A shows an AP radiograph demonstrating a medial epicondyle fracture.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The presence of a buckle fracture may suggest a higher degree of energy associated with the injury, but does not provide an indication for fixation of a medial epicondyle fracture.
Answer 3: The degree of fracture displacement which necessitates operative management is controversial, though most would suggest displacement <2 mm can be treated nonoperatively.
Answer 4: This describes a radial nerve palsy which is not typically associated with medial epicondyle fractures. An ulnar nerve palsy would be a relative indication to proceed with surgery.
Answer 5: Surgery may be indicated in throwing or upper extremity athletes to restore valgus stability to the elbow.
