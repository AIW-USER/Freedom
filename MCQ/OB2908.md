## Question

A 22-year-old female with hereditary osteochondromas has difficulty supinating and pronating her forearm. Radiographs are shown in Figure A. Which of the following procedures would most effectively improve forearm rotation in this patient?

![[q2908-a-mhe-radial-bowing.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Combined radius and ulna corrective osteotomy
2. Radial shaft osteotomy
3. Radial head resection
4. Ulnar osteotomy and lengthening
5. Osteochondroma excision

## Correct option

**5. Osteochondroma excision**

## Answer explanation

Osteochondroma excision is one of the most effective ways to improve motion in patient with limited supination and pronation due to MHE.

*Akita et al retrospectively reviewed 23 patients with MHO treated with various procedures and* **found** the following: 1. All complications were associated with ulnar lengthening, 2. Corrective osteotomy did not lead to improved range of motion, and 3. Simple lesion excision lead to the most improvement in range of motion.

*Shin et al retrospectively reviewed 29 patients with MHO treated with various procedures and* **found** that no improvement from ulnar lengthening was seen. Best results were found with Sauvé-Kapandji procedure and simple excision.
