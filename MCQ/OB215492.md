## Question

A 37-year-old male presents to the trauma bay with left hip pain after falling 10 feet off a ladder. Radiographs demonstrate the injury seen in Figure A. Which of the following is the most important predictor of fracture healing after operative stabilization?

![[q215492-a-screen-shot-2021-04-13-at-11-11-02-am.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Surgery within 12 hours
2. Quality of reduction
3. Open reduction
4. Capsular decompression
5. Surgical approach

## Correct option

**2. Quality of reduction**

## Answer explanation

This patient presents with a femoral neck fracture. Anatomic reduction is the most important predictor of fracture healing.

Femoral neck fractures in the physiologically young patient, typically <50 years old, are best treated with anatomic reduction and internal fixation. Osteonecrosis and nonunion are the two most common complications associated with these injuries. Anatomic reduction has been demonstrated to influence fracture healing the most. Cannulated screws and sliding hip screws are commonly used devices used to stabilize femoral neck fractures. Device selection is dictated by fracture pattern, fracture location, and surgeon preference.

*Haidukewych et al. retrospectively reviewed a cohort of 73 femoral neck fractures in patients <50 years old who underwent internal fixation.* **They found** that 23% of patients developed osteonecrosis and 8% developed a nonunion which required a secondary procedure. **They conclude** that osteonecrosis was the primary complication in this cohort and results were influenced by the quality of reduction and initial fracture displacement.

*Krischak et al. retrospectively reviewed reduction quality of 39 patients who underwent internal fixation for femoral neck fractures by analyzing postoperative alignment on x-ray.* **They found** that 0/8 patients with varus malreduction healed the fracture. **They concluded** that nonanatomic reduction in a varus position increased the risk for failure.

*Weinrobe et al. retrospectively reviewed radiographic alignment of 47 patients who underwent internal fixation for femoral neck fractures.* **They found** that varus angulation and inferior offset of the fracture fragments correlated with increased risk for re-displacement. **They conclude** that nonanatomic reduction with varus angulation or inferior offset is a strong predictor of failure.

Figure A is an AP x-ray of the left hip demonstrating a femoral neck fracture.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Although controversial, recent literature has shown that timing of surgery may not affect fracture healing.
Answer 3: There is no consensus on whether open or closed reduction is superior. Although, multiple unsuccessful closed reduction attempts increases AVN risk.
Answer 4: Decompression of the intracapsular hematoma may lower the risk of AVN but has not shown to increase rate of fracture healing
Answer 5: The anterior Smith-Peterson and lateral Watson-Jones approach are the most common surgical approaches utilized. However, neither has been shown to be superior for fracture healing.
