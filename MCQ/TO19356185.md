## Question

Not a test for Carpal tunnel syndrome [FNB 2023]

## Options

1. Phalen
2. Murphy
3. Durkan
4. Semmes Winstein test

## Correct option

**2. Murphy**

## Answer explanation

Answer: 2

EXPLANATION: -
Carpal tunnel syndrome (CTS) occurs when the median nerve is compressed as it traverses the carpal tunnel. The primary factor contributing to the onset of CTS is the elevated pressure (> 30 mm-Hg) within the carpal tunnel. Risk factors that directly or indirectly contribute to this pathophysiology include: -
o female sex
o obesity
o pregnancy
o hypothyroidism
o rheumatoid arthritis
o trauma (acute CTS) - distal radius fractures or malunions, dislocation/or subluxation of the carpus
o repetitive motion activities
o acromegaly
o advanced age
o menopause
o chronic renal failure
o space-occupying lesion (e.g. ganglion cyst, neoplasm)
o use of oral contraceptives
o congestive heart failure
o diabetes
o smoking
o alcoholism
o mucopolysaccharidosis (children)

Commonest causes include idiopathic > hypothyroidism > pregnancy.

CLINICAL FEATURES: -
The typical initial signs of CTS include pain, numbness, and paraesthesia’s, which affect the first 3 digits and the lateral half of the fourth digit. Thenar atrophy may ensue with time. Patients with Riche-Cannieu anastomosis that connect the deep branch of the ulnar nerve to the recurrent motor branch of the median nerve, however, may have preserved thenar strength despite severe CTS.

Clinical tests for CTS
Sensory tests: Two-point discrimination (failure to discriminate two points held 5 mm apart), Semmes-Weinstein monofilament test, vibrometry, and texture discrimination and self administered hand diagram are important sensory tests for defining the anatomic distribution of sensory changes.

Semmes Weinstein monofilament is the most sensitive sensory test for detecting early carpal tunnel syndrome while Hand diagram is the most specific.

Provocative tests: -

Tinel’s sign involves lightly tapping over the nerve to see if it generates a tingling sensation.
Phalen’s test involves pushing the dorsal surface of hands together and holding 30 – 60 seconds to see reproduction of symptoms.

Durkan’s direct nerve compression test: Here one applies pressure with thumbs over the median nerve within the carpal tunnel, located just distal to the wrist crease. The test is positive if the patient responds with numbness and tingling within 30 seconds.

INVESTIGATIONS:
Radiographs are of limited value although MRI may be indicated when a space occupying lesion is suspected.
Ultrasound rather is much more useful where increased cross-sectional area of the median nerve >10 mm² at the level of the pisiform/proximal edge of transverse carpal ligament is strongly associated with CTS. Regarding Electro diagnostic studies (EDS), EMG traditionally has been considered gold standard; however, AAOS clinical practice guideline currently hold a limited recommendation for its use.
Pathological EDS findings are as follows: -
In NCV
• prolonged latencies (slowing)
o distal sensory latency of > 3.5 ms
o motor latencies > 4.5 ms
• slower conduction velocities
o velocity of < 52 m/sec is abnormal
o slower conduction velocity are less specific than prolonged latencies
 In EMG
• increased insertional activity
• sharp waves
• fibrillations
• fasciculations
• complex repetitive discharges
