## Question

A 21-year-old male college football running back is hit in the lower half during a game and is down on the field. He has an obvious deformity to his leg with an open wound. He is taken to the emergency department by ambulance and radiographs there demonstrate a grade II open distal third tibial shaft fracture. He is taken to the operating room urgently for irrigation and debridement and tibial intramedullary nailing. He is recovering well on rounds post-op day one and is discharged after 24 hours. Which of the following is he at the highest risk of developing in the post-operative period?

## Options

1. Depression
2. Tibiotalar arthritis
3. Malunion requiring revision surgery
4. Hardware failure
5. Worsening VAS pain scores

## Correct option

**1. Depression**

## Answer explanation

In the setting of open fractures, patients are at high risk of developing psychiatric symptoms of depression post-operatively (Answer 1).

Orthopaedic trauma can have significant consequences on a patient's happiness and function. Recently, the role of orthopaedic trauma on a patient's psychiatric function is being increasingly studied. Several studies have demonstrated patients with pre-existing depression have worse outcomes after sustaining a fracture than those without. Additionally, open tibia fractures have been shown to be a risk factor for the development of depression post-operatively. As the emphasis on patient satisfaction and whole-patient healthcare intensifies, it is important for orthopaedic surgeons to recognize these symptoms and be able to refer care appropriately.

*Crichlow et al. looked at the prevalence and severity of depression in orthopaedic trauma patients.* **They noted** that depression after significant orthopaedic trauma was common, with 26% of patients reporting moderate-to-severe or severe depression after injury. Open fractures were the only injury-specific factor that correlated with the development of depression (OR 4.58).

*Weinberg et al.* **reported** that psychiatric illness was common in orthopaedic polytrauma patients and outcomes were poorer in those who reported having these conditions. **They noted** that depression was an independent predictor of complications developing with an OR of 2.96. Their conclusion was that greater attention should be paid to appropriate treatment of those with pre-existing psychiatric illness in the setting of orthopaedic trauma.

*Graham et al. looked at measurements of patient satisfaction in orthopaedic surgery.* **They noted** that understanding patient satisfaction is complex and requires context on the type of treatment, the setting, and the patient's expectations. **They found** that these measurements of patient satisfaction are especially important in evaluating established procedures and processes to understand their value and patient expectations in this setting.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Tibial shaft fractures do not directly increase the risk of developing tibiotalar arthritis. Tibial plafond fractures do increase the risk of tibiotalar arthritis.
Answer 3: The risk of malunion is related to surgical technique and fracture pattern, not the fact there is an open injury.
Answer 4: Hardware failure has not been directly linked to the presence of an open injury.
Answer 5: After I&D and operative stabilization, VAS pain scores should improve, not worsen.
