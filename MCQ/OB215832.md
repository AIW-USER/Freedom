## Question

## Options

1. Cast immobilization in 30 degrees of plantarflexion
2. Fragment excision with distalization of the Achilles tendon
3. Open reduction and internal fixation (ORIF)
4. Percutaneous reduction and Kirschner wire fixation
5. Subtalar arthrodesis

## Correct option

**3. Open reduction and internal fixation (ORIF)**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a displaced calcaneal tuberosity fracture causing skin threatening. Definitive management should consist of urgent open reduction and internal fixation (ORIF) to minimize the risk of skin necrosis.

The calcaneus is the most common fractured tarsal bone and is associated with a high degree of morbidity and disability. Urgent ORIF should be performed for a displaced calcaneal tuberosity fracture to mitigate necrosis of the overlying soft tissue. Until ORIF is able to be performed, the ankle should be splinted in approximately 30° of plantar flexion to reduce pressure on the threatened skin.

*Beavis et al. reviewed calcaneal tuberosity avulsion fractures.* *They reported on 3 types of avulsion fractures of the calcaneal tuberosity.* **They concluded** that these fractures types often occur in osteopenic or osteoporotic patients and have been reported to have an association in those with diabetes.

*Hess et al. reviewed a subset of calcaneal fractures that should be addressed urgently.* **They report** that calcaneal tuberosity avulsion fractures often compromise the thin posterior skin that covers the insertion of the Achilles tendon. **They concluded** that these patients are at risk for skin breakdown of the posterior heel and tissue necrosis if they do not receive urgent treatment.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: This patient has skin threatening following a displaced calcaneal tuberosity fracture and urgent ORIF should be performed following initial splinting in a plantarflexed position
Answer 2: Excision of the avulsed fragment should be avoided due to its large size
Answer 4: The strength of the Achilles tendon would overpower percutaneous Kirschner wire fixation leading to failure
Answer 5: ORIF is indicated urgently in this patient to minimize the risk of skin breakdown
