## Question

A 14-year-old male presents to your clinic for evaluation of his right knee. He is an avid basketball player but states that he has "dislocated" his right knee several times over the last several years. He has noted that it has happened more frequently over the last year with the most recent episode 6 weeks prior. Exam reveals a positive J-sign. His current imaging studies are shown in Figures A through C. His TT-TG distance is 20 mm and his sulcus angle measures 150 degrees. ## Options

1. MPFL reconstruction by itself will not lead to good clinical outcomes in this patient
2. Insal-Salvati ratio of 1.2 places him at decreased risk of recurrent instability
3. His age places him at decreased risk for future instability episodes
4. Presence of trochlear dysplasia places him at risk for recurrent instability
5. Tibial slope of 15 degrees places him at increased risk for recurrent instability

## Correct option

**4. Presence of trochlear dysplasia places him at risk for recurrent instability**

## Answer explanation

The patient has a history of recurrent patellar instability with his most recent episode causing a medial patellar facet MPFL avulsion fracture. Given his elevated sulcus angle (> 140 degrees, indicating flattening of the trochlea), the patient has evidence of trochlear dysplasia, which is the most important predictor of residual patellofemoral instability following isolated MPFL reconstruction.

Patellofemoral instability (PFI) is a frequent cause of knee pain and one of the most common reasons for the adolescent athlete to visit an orthopedic surgeon. It can be the result of ligamentous laxity, a prior patellar instability event, or miserable malalignment syndrome (femoral anteversion, genu valgum or external tibial torsion). Management of recurrent instability in the setting of normal mechanical alignment is managed with MPFL ligament reconstruction. Risk factors for failure are severe trochlear dysplasia, increased tibial tuberosity-trochlear groove (TT-TG) distance and decreased age (less than 20). Trochlear dysplasia can be further classified using the Dejour classification (Illustration A) and the sulcus angle (values greater than 140 degrees indicate dysplasia)

*Kita et al. performed a case-control study to evaluate the rate of recurrent patellar instability in 42 patients who underwent isolated, anatomic MPFL reconstruction using a semitendinosus autograft.* *At a mean of 2 years follow-up,* **they noted** 2 knees had experienced a redislocation (4.5%) and a positive apprehension sign was still evident in 8 knees (18.2%). **They noted** that trochlear dysplasia was independently associated with postoperative PFI by multivariable logistic regression analysis. **The authors concluded** that severe trochlear dysplasia is the most important predictor of residual PFI after isolated MPFL reconstruction.

*Matsushita et al. looked at the clinical outcomes of isolated MPFL reconstruction in 34 patients with an increased TT-TG distance.* *Nineteen patients with a TT-TG distance of >20 mm were compared with 15 patients with a TT-TG distance of <20 mm.* **The authors noted** that no patients reported re-dislocation. **The authors concluded** that overall clinical outcomes of isolated MPFL reconstruction were favorable even in patients with an increased TT-TG distance and that TT-TG distance of >20 mm may not be an absolute indication for medialization of the tibial tuberosity when performing MPFL reconstruction.

*Wagner et al. performed a prospective study to evaluate the importance of trochlea geometry, medial patellofemoral ligament (MPFL), patella height, tibial tuberosity-trochlea groove distance (TT-TG) and the extensor muscles on clinical outcomes of PFI.* **They noted** that 50 patients with chronic PFI were treated with isolated MPFL reconstruction. **The authors concluded** that addressing only ligamentous factors through MPFL reconstruction leads to satisfying clinical results and low redislocation rates in most patients.

*Nelitz et al. analyzed 37 patients with recurrent PFI after unsuccessful operative stabilization in childhood and adolescence.* **They noted** severe trochlear dysplasia as detected on MRI scans was found significantly more often in the study group (89%) than in the control group (21%). **The authors concluded** that trochlear dysplasia, therefore, seems to be a major risk factor for failure of operative stabilization in recurrent PFI.

Figures A and B show the radiographs of a laterally subluxated patella. Figures C is the T2-weighted axial MRI cut showing MPFL avulsion from the femoral insertion site. Illustration B shows the sulcus angle of the trochlea (values greater than 140 are considered elevated indicating the presence of dysplasia)

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: MPFL reconstruction by itself has shown excellent outcomes for PFI. Some studies show that a TT-TG > 20 is an indication for performing a simultaneous tibial tubercle transfer but this patient has a TT-TG of 20.
Answer 2: An Insal-Salvati (IS) ratio of 1.2 is normal and, furthermore, the IS ratio has not shown to be a significant factor in recurrent PFI following MPFL reconstruction.
Answer 3: This patient's young age (< 20) puts him at increased risk for PFI.
Answer 5: A tibial slope of 15 is normal and, furthermore, the tibial slope would not affect recurrent PFI following MPFL reconstruction.
