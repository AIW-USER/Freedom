## Question

A 15-year-old female soccer player sustains a twisting injury while running for a ball during a game. Her knee immediately becomes swollen and she is unable to ambulate. She presents to your office and you suspect a tear of the anterior cruciate ligament (ACL). You obtain radiographs of the injured knee which demonstrate a large effusion but no fractures. MRI confirms complete rupture of her ACL. When asked, she confirms that menstruation began at age 12. Which of the following is true when comparing techniques for ACL reconstruction in this patient population?

## Options

1. Non-operative management with a functional ACL brace may yield comparable results to ACL reconstruction
2. Outcomes are improved with extra-articular IT band reconstruction compared to anatomic graft reconstruction
3. Autograft yields superior results when compared to allograft tissue
4. Size of graft is not a consideration in risk for re-rupture
5. All-epiphyseal reconstruction should be performed to avoid crossing the physis

## Correct option

**3. Autograft yields superior results when compared to allograft tissue**

## Answer explanation

Despite this being an adolescent, autograft tissue still has better outcomes and lower rates of failure than allograft for anterior cruciate ligament (ACL) reconstruction.

ACL ruptures are common injuries, especially in the young, athletic population. In this population, reconstruction is currently the preferred technique in order to restore stability and prevent further damage and degeneration to the knee. In the pediatric and adolescent population, the physis must be respected, as damage to the physis via drilling or bony graft in-growth may cause deformity. The onset of menarche can be used as a determinant for skeletal maturity, with the majority of growth occurring in the 18 months following menarche. Techniques include extra-articular IT band reconstruction, all-epiphyseal reconstruction, and anatomic transphyseal reconstruction. In this patient, transphyseal reconstruction has a low risk of growth disruption given her age and timing of menarche and would be preferred compared to a non-anatomic technique. Regardless of the reconstruction technique, soft-tissue autograft reconstruction is preferred to allograft given the higher rate of graft failure with allograft.

*Fabricant et al. reviewed the management of ACL injuries in children and adolescents,* **noting** that the frequency is increasing in this age group. Risks of nonoperative management in this population include continued instability, meniscal pathology, and cartilage damage, while risks of physeal disruption include tibial recurvatum and leg-length discrepancy/angular deformity. They go on to discuss physeal sparing/respecting techniques to minimize the risk of physeal disturbance in younger, peri-pubescent age groups in order to optimize outcomes.

*Kaeding et al. compared allograft and autograft ACL reconstruction in a MOON group cohort.* **They pointed out** that graft failure rates were highest in the 10-19 year-old range, and the odds of re-rupture with allograft reconstruction were 4x higher than using autograft. **They conclude** that given these risks, autograft reconstruction is preferred, particularly in a younger cohort.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Non-operative management yields higher rates of instability, lower satisfaction rates, and theoretical increases in other knee injuries, principally to the meniscus.
Answer 2: Extra-articular IT band reconstruction is still widely used in younger, pre-pubescent children, but in children who are close to skeletal maturity, anatomic ACL reconstruction is preferred.
Answer 4: Graft size is an important consideration in decreasing rates of re-rupture. A graft >8 mm is preferred to provide the best outcomes.
Answer 5: All-epiphyseal reconstruction is another option for patients with open physes, but for those nearing skeletal maturity, crossing the physis presents a low risk for growth disturbance and may provide a more anatomic reconstruction
