Chapter: General Orthopedics MCQs — Platelet Rich Plasma

## Question

Autologous Conditioned Plasma- ACP has

## Options

1. Low platelet and low leukocyte count
2. High platelet and high leukocyte count
3. High platelet and low leukocyte count
4. Low platelet and high leukocyte count

## Correct option

**3. High platelet and low leukocyte count**

## Answer explanation

Answer: 3
Types of PRP
1. Pure Platelet-Rich Plasma (P-PRP) or Leukocyte-Poor PRP (LP-PRP)- P-PRP, also called leukocyte-poor PRP, contains a high concentration of platelets with a minimal amount of leukocytes. It is a plasma formulation that primarily focuses on the role of platelets in healing while minimizing inflammation that might be caused by white blood cells. For example, P-PRP is often used in tendon and cartilage repair since excessive inflammation could interfere with the healing process. They have lower leukocyte content to reduce potential inflammatory reactions.
2. Leukocyte-Rich PRP (LR-PRP) LR-PRP contains both a high concentration of platelets and a higher proportion of leukocytes. The presence of leukocytes adds an inflammatory component to the treatment, which can be beneficial in certain healing contexts, especially where fighting infection or managing early inflammation is necessary. This type of PRP is more commonly used in musculoskeletal and orthopedic conditions, particularly for treating acute injuries (e.g., ligament tears, muscle strains) where inflammation is a normal part of the healing process. LR-PRP may be preferred in the early stages of tissue repair to enhance the immune response. It contains a higher concentration of leukocytes, promoting a more robust inflammatory response.
3. Platelet-Rich Fibrin (PRF) Platelet-Rich Fibrin is a newer form of PRP that creates a fibrin scaffold, which allows for a slower, more sustained release of growth factors over time. Unlike PRP, PRF does not involve the use of anticoagulants, leading to the formation of a fibrin matrix that supports tissue healing for a longer duration. It has fibrin matrix that prolongs the release of growth factors, enhancing long-term healing.
4. Advanced PRP or Bio-PRP (Autologous Conditioned Plasma)- Advanced PRP formulations, sometimes called bio-PRP or autologous conditioned plasma, undergo additional steps to further enhance the concentration of growth factors or bioactive proteins. These PRP formulations may involve more complex processing techniques that can activate the platelets prior to injection, increasing their healing potential. It is used in specialized cases where high-intensity regenerative treatment is needed, often in chronic or severe conditions like advanced osteoarthritis or non-healing tendon injuries. It has enhanced concentration and activation of platelets to maximize the therapeutic effect.
5. Plasma-Rich in Growth Factors- PRGF is a specific type of PRP designed to contain a precise balance of growth factors, while minimizing the presence of leukocytes to avoid excessive inflammation. This type of PRP focuses on optimizing the release of growth factors that stimulate tissue healing. It has optimized growth factor profile with minimal leukocyte content for controlled healing.
6. Autologous Conditioned Plasma- ACP is another term used for a leukocyte-poor form of PRP. It is typically processed using a specific type of centrifuge that separates the plasma and platelets while reducing leukocytes. This type of PRP is often used when a more refined approach to tissue repair is needed without the inflammatory effects of white blood cells. It is frequently employed in treating tendon injuries, cartilage degeneration, and some forms of osteoarthritis. It focuses on platelets without significant leukocyte content to limit inflammation.
