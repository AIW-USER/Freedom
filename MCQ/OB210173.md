## Answer explanation

When present, an os trigonum lies just lateral to the flexor hallucis longus (FHL) tendon and causes FHL tendonitis and subsequent posteromedial ankle pain.

The os trigonum is the second most common accessory bone in the foot and arises from the posterolateral tubercle of the talus and often causes irritation and inflammation of the FHL. This accessory bone may be asymptomatic or cause pain particularly in patients who are doing repetitive forced plantarflexion such as dancers. Pain can be generated when the os trigonum is impinged between the distal aspect of the posterior tibia and the superior portion of the calcaneus. Pain can also be generated from irritation of the fibro-ossesous FHL tendon sheath just medial to the os trigonum. Treatment modalities initially include nonoperative measures including rest, immobilization, and anti-inflammatories. Patients who fail non-operative measures or those with recalcitrant symptoms may benefit from arthroscopic or surgical excision with or without tenolysis, debridement, or repair of the FHL tendon.

*Rungprai et al. reviews os trigonum syndrome and describes it as a disease of the FHL tendon.* **They state** repetitive or acute forced plantarflexion leads to injury of the FHL tendon through impingement of the os trigonum. Treatment options start with non-operative measures followed by either open or arthroscopic excision of the os trigonum. If using an open approach **they recommend** a lateral approach if only addressing the os trigonum but a medial approach if addressing both the os trigonum and FHL tendon.

*Hamilton reviewed common causes of posterior ankle pain in dancers.* **He states** that FHL tendonitis in dancers is so common it has been coined "dancer's tendonitis". **He states** posteromedial ankle pain in dancers is caused by FHL tendonitis, impingement from an os trigonum, or a combination of both.
