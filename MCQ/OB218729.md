## Question

You are attending a lecture while on your pediatric orthopedic rotation on tarsal coalitions. Several treatment options are discussed. Which of the following is an appropriate treatment for the indication listed?

## Options

1. Coalition resection with interposition graft for a talocalcaneal coalition that involves <50% of joint surface area
2. Subtalar arthrodesis for a calcaneonavicular coalition that involves <50% of joint surface area
3. Subtalar arthrodesis for a calcaneonavicular coalition with associated degenerative changes affecting the calcaneocuboid joint
4. Subtalar arthrodesis for a talocalcaneal coalition with associated degenerative changes affecting the talonavicular joint
5. Triple arthrodesis for a talocalcaneal coalition that involves >50% of joint surface area without hindfoot arthritis

## Correct option

**1. Coalition resection with interposition graft for a talocalcaneal coalition that involves <50% of joint surface area**

## Answer explanation

The preferred treatment for a talocalcaneal coalition with involvement of <50% of the joint is excision of the coalition with interpositional grafting.

Tarsal coalition is a common congenital condition caused by failure of embryonic segmentation leading to an abnormal coalition of 2 or more of the tarsal bones. The condition is usually asymptomatic but may present with a flatfoot deformity or recurrent ankle sprains. Diagnosis is made with plain radiographs of the foot and ankle showing a coalition, most commonly a calcaneonavicular or talocalcaneal coalition. Treatment is usually a course of casting and NSAIDs for symptomatic patients. Surgical coalition resection or joint arthrodesis is indicated for patients with persistent symptoms who fail conservative management.

*Murphy et al. reviewed talocalcaneal coalitions.* **They reported** that patients often present with complaints of flatfeet, foot or ankle pain after a minor injury, or recurrent ankle sprains. Physical examination findings include limited subtalar motion and prominence inferior to the medial malleolus. **They concluded** that resection of talocalcaneal coalitions with fat-graft interposition has superior results to primary arthrodesis.

*Bauer et al. reviewed the recent literature regarding the evaluation and management of pediatric flatfeet.* **They reported** that the treatment of tarsal coalition remains challenging, but short-term and intermediate-term outcome studies are satisfactory, whereas long-term outcome studies are lacking. **They concluded** that management of the associated flatfoot deformity may be as important as management of the coalition itself.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: A subtalar arthrodesis is considered if coalition involves >50 % of the joint surface of a talocalcaneal coalition.
Answers 3-5: Triple arthrodesis (subtalar, calcaneocuboid, and talonavicular) is indicated in advanced coalitions that fail resection and/or diffuse associated degenerative changes affecting calcaneocuboid and talonavicular joints.
