## Question

An 82-year-old female patient presents with a minimally displaced femoral neck fracture. A decision is made to treat her with internal fixation. Which of the following is true regarding treatment outcomes for her injury?

## Options

1. There is a low likelihood that she will require a walking aid after surgery
2. Her risk of requiring accommodation in an assisted living facility is similar to a patient <80 years
3. American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) class has not been associated with post operative independence
4. Preoperative ambulation without an assistive device is associated with 1 year post-op independent living
5. The use of a dynamic hip screw for internal fixation is associated with decreased post-op independence at 1-year post-op

## Correct option

**4. Preoperative ambulation without an assistive device is associated with 1 year post-op independent living**

## Answer explanation

Patients greater than 80 years sustain a high rate of institutionalization and dependence on walking aids following femoral neck fracture treatment. Factors associated with loss of independent living include age >80 years, American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) class > I, previous walking aid use prior to the injury, and current tobacco use.

Femoral neck fractures can be the result of high-energy mechanisms in young patients and low-energy, ground-level falls in the elderly. In the elderly population, there is a high rate of mortality at 1-year post-fracture, ~30%, in addition to the loss of independence. A recent study using the data collected from the Fixation using Alternative Implants for the Treatment of Hip fractures (FAITH) study determined age >80 years, American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) class > I, previous walking aid use prior to the injury, and current tobacco use was associated with loss of post-fracture independence.

The FAITH investigators performed an international, multicenter randomized controlled trial studying reoperation and complication rates in patients treated with sliding hip screws and cannulated screws for femoral neck fractures. They reported no difference in reoperation rates between sliding hip screws and cannulated screws in the pooled analysis and a higher rate of avascular necrosis with sliding hip screws. They concluded sliding hip screws may be advantageous for smokers, displaced fractures, and basicervical fractures.

*Schemitsch et al. performed a descriptive analysis of the FAITH trial patient cohort to determine the level of independence following fixation of femoral neck fractures.* **They reported** 19.81% of patients >80 years were institutionalized 12 months after surgery and 69.84% of patients >80 years required walking aids 12 months after surgery. **The authors concluded** that femoral neck fractures greatly affect patient independence with age <50 years, ASA class 1, no previous walking aid use, no current tobacco use, and acceptable implant placement being associated with increased independence.

Incorrect answers
*Answer 1: According to Schemitsch et al., there is ~70% rate of walking aid use in patients >80 years following femoral neck fracture surgery.*
Answer 2: There is a lower risk of assisted living accommodation in patients <80 years compared to patients >80 years.
Answer 3: Patients with an ASA class 1 have a higher rate of post-fracture independence.
Answer 5: The type of implant used does not affect post-fracture independence, but rather the quality of fracture reduction and implant placement.
