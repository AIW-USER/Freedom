## Question

You are a foot and ankle specialist in a metropolitan area who is giving a talk about posterior tibial tendon insufficiency. When lecturing on operative indications, which of the following patients would be most appropriately managed with a flexor digitorum longus transfer and a medializing calcaneal osteotomy alone?

## Options

1. 57-year-old female with pain, inability to perform a single leg heel raise, and flexible hindfoot valgus deformity
2. 65-year-old female with pain, inability to perform a single leg heel raise, and rigid hindfoot valgus deformity
3. 72-year-old female with pain, inability to perform a single leg heel rase, rigid hindfoot valgus deformity, and deltoid ligament compromise
4. 45-year-old male with pain but retained ability to perform a single leg heel raise
5. 55-year-old female with pain, inability to perform single leg heel raise, forefoot abduction, and flexible hindfoot valgus deformity

## Correct option

**1. 57-year-old female with pain, inability to perform a single leg heel raise, and flexible hindfoot valgus deformity**

## Answer explanation

The patient in answer choice #1 has a stage IIa flatfoot deformity and a medializing calcaneal osteotomy and a flexor digitorum longus (FDL) reconstruction would be indicated.

Adult flatfoot deformity is common, with posterior tibial tendon insufficiency (PTTI) being the most frequently identified cause. It occurs more commonly in women and in the middle-later ages. It is well described as a spectrum of disease with medial longitudinal arch collapse occurring first, followed by valgus hindfoot deformity and forefoot abduction/varus. At first, these deformities can be flexible, but without treatment, they may become rigid over time. The flexibility of the deformity is the primary driver of operative management. With a hindfoot valgus deformity that is flexible, a medializing calcaneal osteotomy can be performed. If it is rigid, a hindfoot arthrodesis is preferred. Understanding whether there is talonavicular uncoverage is also important, as this differentiates stage IIa from IIb and necessitates additional surgery in the form of lateral column lengthening and/or spring ligament reconstruction.

*Chan et al. reviewed the contribution of medializing calcaneal osteotomy on hindfoot alignment in the reconstruction of stage II flatfoot deformity.* **They found** seven different variables that affected hindfoot moment arm, which included: gender, amount of medialization of the osteotomy, presence of lateral column lengthening, first TMT fusion, spring ligament reconstruction, medial cuneiform-fifth metatarsal height, and calcaneal pitch angle. A regression analysis demonstrated that the medializing calcaneal osteotomy was the only significant predictor of the hindfoot moment arm, leading to the conclusion that correction of hindfoot valgus is primarily controlled by the medializing osteotomy.

*Maker and Cottom reviewed the surgical management of stage II adult flatfoot deformity.* *They discussed the relevant differences between stage IIa and IIb and noted that the severity and location of the deformity were important to understand before choosing a procedure.* **They also highlight** the importance of not overcorrecting the flatfoot deformity in order to prevent excess overload of the lateral column and stiffness.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Inability to perform a single leg heel raise with a RIGID hindfoot valgus deformity would be classified as a stage III deformity and would be best treated with a triple arthrodesis.
Answer 3: Inability to perform a single leg heel raise with rigid hindfoot deformity and deltoid ligament compromise would be classified as a stage IV deformity and would best be managed with a triple arthrodesis + deltoid reconstruction or a tibiotalocalcaneal arthrodesis.
Answer 4: Pain with the ability to still perform a single leg heel raise would be classified as stage I and nonoperative management should be maximized before considering posterior tibial tendon debridement.
Answer 5: Inability to perform a single leg heel raise with a flexible hindfoot and forefoot abduction would be classified as a stage IIb and requires a lateral column lengthening to correct the talonavicular uncoverage.
