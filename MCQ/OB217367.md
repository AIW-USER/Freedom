## Question

A 53-year-old powerlifter presents to the clinic with a known history of end-stage primary shoulder osteoarthritis refractory to conservative measures. After a lengthy treatment discussion, the patient wishes to proceed with surgery in the form of an anatomic total shoulder replacement. During what step of the shoulder replacement is the arcuate artery most likely to be damaged?

## Options

1. Subscapularis peel
2. Humeral neck cut
3. Elevating biceps tendon from the groove
4. Circumferential labrectomy
5. Deltopectoral approach

## Correct option

**3. Elevating biceps tendon from the groove**

## Answer explanation

The arcuate artery is a branch of the anterior humeral circumflex artery that runs up the lateral aspect of the biceps groove and can cause bleeding during the extraction of the biceps tendon.

Critically understanding the blood supply to the proximal humerus is crucial during surgical exposures and in understanding how osteonecrosis of the humeral head may occur after a fracture or surgical intervention. Branches from the anterior humeral circumflex artery and the posterior humeral circumflex artery contribute toward the vascularization of the proximal humerus. A branch off the anterior humeral circumflex artery, the arcuate artery, is the terminal branch and main blood supply to the greater tuberosity.

*Gerber et al. used a cadaveric model to anatomically study the arteries of the humeral head to determine their intraosseous distributions.* The anastomoses between the different arteries were abundant, but vascularization of all of the humeral head was possible only through the anterolateral branch of the anterior humeral circumflex artery, which was deemed to be the primary blood supply.

*Hettrich et al. refuted Gerber’s original findings that the anterior circumflex artery is the main perfuser of the humeral head.* *Using a cadaveric model,* **they determined** that the posterior humeral circumflex artery was the dominant blood supply to the humeral head providing 64% of the blood, whereas the anterior humeral circumflex artery supplied 36%. The belief that the posterior humeral circumflex artery is the dominant blood supply to the head is still widely held.

*Keough et al. performed a study to investigate the blood supply of the humeral head originating from the anterior and posterior circumflex humeral arteries.* **They observed** 7 variations in the branching pattern of the anterior circumflex humeral artery using a cadaveric model. **They concluded** that certain variations can lead to interruption of the intraosseous arterial network in the greater tuberosity with surgery and suture anchor placement.

Incorrect Answers:
1. The anterior humeral circumflex vessels are visible at the inferior margin of the subscapularis tendon and can be injured with distal dissection during a subscapularis peel.
2. During the humeral neck cut care must be taken to avoid violating the supraspinatus insertion on the greater tuberosity.
4. During inferior glenoid exposure with a labrectomy the axillary nerve has close relations with the glenoid passing just under the glenoid rim at a mean of 10-25 mm. This distance increases with the arm in an abducted and externally rotated position.
5. The cephalic vein runs within the deltopectoral groove and serves as the landmark structure to help identify the correct interval during a deltopectoral approach.
