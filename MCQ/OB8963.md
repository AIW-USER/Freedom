## Question

What nerve supplies the muscle which is most likely not functioning properly in the patient shown in Figure V?

## Options

1. Dorsal scapular nerve
2. Long thoracic nerve
3. Thoracodorsal nerve
4. Suprascapular nerve
5. Lower subscapular nerve

## Correct option

**2. Long thoracic nerve**

## Answer explanation

This patient has medial winging of the scapula due to a lesion affecting the serratus anterior. The serratus anterior is supplied by the long thoracic nerve.

The long thoracic nerve is the first nerve to branch off from the brachial plexus, taking contributions from nerve roots C5, C6 and C7. It supplies the serratus anterior, which normally holds the scapula to the chest wall, preventing medial winging of the scapula. Conversely, lesions affecting the spinal accessory nerve, which supplies the trapezius, prevent the trapezius from elevating the scapula. As a result, the scapula translates inferiorly as the shoulder droops and the inferior angle of the scapula rotates laterally (lateral winging).

*Meininger et al. review scapular winging diagnosis and treatment.* **They note** that long thoracic nerve and spinal accessory nerve lesions are the most common etiologies.

*Gregg et al. reviewed 10 cases of long thoracic nerve palsies in young athletes.* **They noted** that full recovery occurred at an average of 9 months and concluded that surgical treatment should be reserved for those who failed to regain function after two years.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1. The dorsal scapular nerve takes contributions from C4 and C5 (mostly C5) and supplies the rhomboids. The rhomboids provide scapular retraction and elevation. They may be implicated in scapular winging but less commonly than serratus anterior or trapezius lesions.
Answer 3. The thoracodorsal nerve takes contributions from C6-C8 and supplies the latissimus dorsi muscle. A small slip of the origin of the latissimus may originate on the inferior angle of the scapula but inserts on the humerus and it is not generally implicated in scapular winging.
Answer 4. The suprascapular nerve takes contributions from C5 and C6 and supplies the supraspinatus and infraspinatus. These originate but do not insert on the scapula and are not implicated in winging.
Answer 5. The lower subscapular nerve takes contributions from C5 and C6 and supplies the teres major. Teres major originates but does not insert on the scapular and is not implicated in winging.
