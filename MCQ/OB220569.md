## Question

A 7-year-old child presents to the emergency department with a non-displaced distal both bone forearm fracture. You are preparing to apply a fiberglass cast. The patient is anxious, and you want to ensure the cast is applied safely and comfortably, minimizing any risk of thermal injury. You instruct your resident to prepare the casting materials. Which of the following actions, if performed during the cast application process, would MOST significantly increase the risk of a cast burn to the patient?

## Options

1. Applying a single layer of cast padding.
2. Immersing the fiberglass roll in water at 22°C (72°F).
3. Thin layer of fiberglass used.
4. Resting the wet cast directly on a soft pillow during the curing process.
5. Using a fiberglass cast instead of a plaster cast.

## Correct option

**4. Resting the wet cast directly on a soft pillow during the curing process.**

## Answer explanation

Resting the wet cast directly on a pillow creates a thermal heat pocket that prevents heat dissipation and significantly increases the risk of a cast burn (Answer 4).

Applying cast material results in an exothermic reaction as the casting material cures, generating heat. Several factors can increase the risk of thermal injury to the patient's skin. Creating a "thermal heat pocket" by resting the wet cast directly on a pillow or other insulating surface prevents the dissipation of this heat, leading to a dangerous buildup of temperature against the skin. Other contributing factors include the thickness of the cast, the temperature of the water used to speed up the curing process (>24°C increases the risk of burns), and the amount of pressure applied during molding, which reduces the distance between the hot cast and the skin. Both fiberglass and plaster casts are capable of generating temperatures high enough to cause burns if proper precautions are not followed.

*Deignan et al. evaluated the effect of pressure applied during casting on the temperatures generated beneath casts.* **They noted** that increased pressure, particularly during molding, significantly raised the peak temperatures, likely due to reducing the insulating space and increasing the cast material's proximity to the skin. **They concluded** that excessive molding pressure is a critical factor contributing to cast burns.

*Halanski et al. evaluated the temperatures generated during contemporary cast application techniques and explored methods to reduce the risk of thermal injury.* **They noted** that certain practices, such as applying multiple layers of casting material, using warm water, or insulating the cast during curing, significantly increased temperatures to levels capable of causing burns. **They concluded** that careful attention to cast application technique, including using cooler water and avoiding heat-trapping methods, is crucial for preventing iatrogenic thermal injuries.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: While a double layer of cast padding is often preferred for comfort and protection, a single layer doesn't significantly increase burn risk if other precautions are taken.
Answer 2: Water at 22°C (72°F) is within a safe, cooler range and would decrease, not increase, the risk of burn compared to warmer water.
Answer 3: Using a thin layer of fiberglass cast material reduces the total exothermic reaction, keeping casting temperatures lower and thereby decreasing the risk of skin burns, whereas thicker casts generate more heat and increase burn risk.
Answer 5: Both fiberglass and plaster casts generate heat during the curing process and have the potential to cause burns if improper techniques are used.
