## Question

A 22-year-old collegiate football player presents with persistent left lateral ankle pain 6 months after sustaining an ankle sprain during a game. He recalls catching his foot on astroturf with a dorsiflexion and inversion moment about his ankle. He was treated with physical therapy and a controlled ankle motion boot for several weeks following the injury with minimal relief. ## Correct option

**4. 4**

## Answer explanation

The clinical presentation is consistent with subluxation or dislocation of the peroneal tendons. This is caused by disruption of the superior peroneal retinaculum (SPR), which is labeled "4" in Figure C.

Forceful inversion and dorsiflexion of the foot can disrupt the SPR, thereby allowing subluxation/dislocation of the peroneal tendons. These injuries often present late and can be mistaken for ankle sprains. Operative management of acute injuries includes direct SPR repair and possible fibular groove deepening. In chronic injuries, surgical management may involve tendon rerouting, soft-tissue repair/reconstruction, and bone block procedures. The overall alignment of the foot and ankle should be carefully evaluated to ensure that malalignment (ex. hindfoot varus)is not contributing to abnormal stress on the repair/reconstruction.

*Ogawa et al. published a review on peroneal tendon subluxation and dislocation.* **They recommend** surgical management of these injuries in both the acute and chronic settings, and describe a variety of procedures to address these injuries such as tendon repair, retinaculum repair, tendon rerouting, and groove-deepening procedures.

*Philbin et al. published a review article on peroneal tendon injuries.* **They report** that these injuries are often underdiagnosed and that peroneal tendon pathology should always be considered in individuals with chronic lateral ankle pain following a sprain. They also discuss treatment strategies, **emphasizing** relevant anatomy.

Figure A is an XR demonstrating the "fleck sign" off the fibula, indicative of an SPR avulsion. Figure B is an MRI demonstrating anterior subluxation of the peroneal tendons at the ankle. Figure C demonstrates various structures about the lateral ankle. Illustration A is a clinical photo demonstrating dislocation of the peroneal tendons. Illustration B is the AP radiograph of the ankle show in Figure A with the "fleck sign" circled (cortical avulsion of the distal tip of the lateral malleolus).

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: "1" corresponds to the peroneus brevis.
Answer 2: "2" corresponds to the peroneus longus.
Answer 3: "3" corresponds to the Achilles tendon.
Answer 5: "5" corresponds to the inferior peroneal retinaculum.
