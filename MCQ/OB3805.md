## Question

A 67-year-old woman presents for evaluation of severe back pain localizing to the thoracic spine. She denies any recent trauma or past medical history and is generally healthy, though admits to recent unintended weight loss. Physical examination is notable for tenderness of the thoracic spine and no neurological deficit. Radiographs of the spine are normal. You obtain an MRI which is shown in Figure A. In addition to laboratory studies, which of the following is the most appropriate next step?

*Figure A*

## Options

1. Oral anti-inflammatory medication, limited bedrest, early return to activity as tolerated with a core strengthening program
2. CT-guided biopsy
3. Open surgical excision
4. CT scan of the chest, abdomen and pelvis and whole body bone scintigraphy
5. Radiofrequency ablation

## Correct option

**4. CT scan of the chest, abdomen and pelvis and whole body bone scintigraphy**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a previously undiagnosed cancer with metastasis to the spine. Staging studies are necessary prior to biopsy or treatment.

Spinal metastases are the first sign of malignancy in 20% of patients with metastatic disease of the spine. A diagnostic algorithm that includes history, physical examination, plain radiography, laboratory testing, bone scintigraphy and CT scanning will identify the primary tumor in 85% of cases of spinal metastasis. Biopsy will identify another 8%.

Rose and Buchowski review the evaluation and management of spinal metastatic disease. They stress that both diagnostic and treatment strategies must be individualized to patients. Three specific categories of patients present with spinal metastasis: (1) patients with known metastatic disease, (2) patients with known malignancy but without a history of metastatic disease, and (3) patients with no prior history of malignancy. Patients in categories (2) and (3) require further evaluation prior to treatment.

Figure A shows a sagittal T2 MRI image with an enhancing T5-T6 lesion with cortical disruption and associated soft tissue mass as well as enhancing T10 and T11 lesions without cortical disruption.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1. This may be appropriate for mechanical low back pain but not for a patient over 50 with severe pain and a lesion identified on MRI
Answer 2. Staging should precede biopsy in the work up of an unknown primary cancer
Answers 3 and 5. Diagnostic work up must precede treatment.
