## Question

A 12-year-old male has an autosomal dominant genetic condition caused by a mutation on chromosome 4p. The mutation causes increased tyrosine kinase activity in the proliferative zone during enchondral bone formation at the physis. This condition is known as:

## Options

1. Achondroplasia
2. Pseudoachondroplasia
3. Larson's syndrome
4. Diastrophic Dysplasia
5. Kniest's Dysplasia

## Correct option

**1. Achondroplasia**

## Answer explanation

Achondroplasia is caused by a mutation in the FGFR3 gene on chromosome 4 leading to a gain of function of FGFR3 (turned on). This causes increased tyrosine kinase activity in the proliferative zone during enchondral bone formation at the physis, resulting in the inhibition of chondrocyte proliferation and differentiation.

Achondroplasia is the most common skeletal dysplasia. Eighty percent of mutations are sporadic, but the condition is autosomal dominant. The mutation affects the gene encoding for the receptor of fibroblast growth factor, rather than the growth factor itself. The clinical result is hypoplasia of long bones formed by enchondral ossification, with proximal limb segments more affected than distal.

*Shirley et al. reviewed achondroplasia.* **They state** this condition as the most common skeletal dysplasia, which is caused by a mutation of fibroblast growth factor receptor-3. This disorder is characterized by frontal bossing, midface hypoplasia, otolaryngeal system dysfunction, and rhizomelic short stature. In addition, they discuss foramen magnum stenosis, thoracolumbar kyphosis, lumbosacral hyperlordosis, spinal stenosis, and extremity deformities.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Pseudoachondroplasia is caused by a defect in the cartilage oligomeric matrix protein (COMP) on chromosome 19.
Answer 3: Larson syndrome has autosomal dominant (AD) and recessive (AR) inheritance patterns. The AD form is linked to a mutation of the gene encoding filamin B. The AR form is linked to carbohydrate sulfotransferase 3 deficiency.
Answer 4: Diastrophic Dysplasia is caused by a mutation in DTDST gene (SLC26A2) on chromosome 5.
Answer 5: Kniest Dysplasia is caused by a defect in COL2A1 (missense, splice or deletion). This defect leads to abnormal Type II collagen, chondrocyte degeneration, cytoplasmic inclusions.
