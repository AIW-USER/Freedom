Chapter: Trauma MCQs — Sacral Fracture

## Question

When performing percutaneous fixation of a Denis Zone II fracture in a sacrum with a severely constricted S1 corridor (Dysmorphism), what is the modern "safe harbor" alternative for transverse stabilization?

## Options

1. Placement of a 3.5 mm cortical screw in the S1 ala.
2. Utilizing the S2 trans-sacral corridor for a long screw.
3. Using an anterior symphyseal plate to indirectly stabilize the posterior ring
4. Intentional L5-S1 trans-facet screw placement

## Correct option

**2. Utilizing the S2 trans-sacral corridor for a long screw.**

## Answer explanation

In dysmorphic anatomy, the S1 corridor is often too narrow or oddly shaped to safely house a 6.5mm or 7.3mm screw. However, the S2 corridor is almost always "non-dysmorphic" and larger in these patients. Current literature suggests that in the presence of S1 dysmorphism, a trans-sacral screw through S2 (crossing from ilium to ilium) provides equivalent or superior stability for the posterior pelvic ring without the high risk of nerve injury associated with a cramped S1 corridor.

Answer: 2
