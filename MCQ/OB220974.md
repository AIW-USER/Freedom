## Question

A 35-year-old healthy male presents to the emergency department following a motor vehicle collision with the closed left ankle injury shown in Figure A. On exam, he has medial and lateral ankle pain. He denies any additional pain or injuries on secondary exam. He elects to undergo open reduction and internal fixation of his left ankle injuries. Regarding syndesmotic fixation, which of the following outcomes is true regarding screw fixation?

![[q220974-a-img-9196.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Stainless steel screws provide greater stability and lower rates of reoperation compared to titanium screws
2. Significantly lower rates of screw pullout when passing four cortices compared to only passing three cortices
3. Higher rate of malreduction with screw fixation versus flexible fixation
4. Lower rate of implant removal when compared to flexible fixation
5. No difference in rate of syndesmotic reduction with screw fixation versus flexible fixation

## Correct option

**3. Higher rate of malreduction with screw fixation versus flexible fixation**

## Answer explanation

This patient sustained a Weber C fibula fracture with complete disruption of his syndesmosis. Regarding syndesmotic screw fixation, it has been associated with a 40% rate of malreduction when compared to flexible (e.g., suture button) fixation (Answer 3).

Syndesmosis injuries are traumatic injuries that affect the distal tibiofibular ligaments, which lead to instability of the ankle joint. These occur in about 13% of all ankle fractures and most commonly occur following sudden external rotation of the ankle. The syndesmosis maintains integrity between the tibia and fibula, resisting axial, rotational, and translational forces. Diagnosis is suspected with tenderness over the syndesmosis and worsening pain with the squeeze test, a physical exam maneuver. Diagnosis can be confirmed with plain radiographs and stress views demonstrating tibiofibular space widening and/or medial clear space widening (indicating an increased risk of syndesmotic injury). Treatment of isolated syndesmotic sprains without overt instability or diastasis is nonoperative. When there is notable instability of the syndesmosis, operative treatment with screws, flexible fixation (e.g., suture button), or both is indicated.

*Sanders et al. conducted a level I prospective randomized controlled trial comparing the rate of malreduction in 103 high fibula fractures with the utilization of two screws (two 3.5-mm cortical screws) vs. 1 TightRope—a suture button fixation implant.* Overall, the rate of malreduction measured on a CT scan 3 months postoperatively was significantly lower with a TightRope versus cortical screws (15% vs 39%, respectively; p= 0.028). No differences were found in functional outcomes between the two groups. The reoperation rate (primarily implant removal) was significantly higher in the screw group compared to the TightRope group (30% vs. 4%, respectively; p= 0.02). **They conclude** that the TightRope device is favorable compared to two 3.5-mm, 3-cortex screw fixation for syndesmosis injuries.

*Pang et al. performed a cadaveric study evaluating tibiotalar contact pressure following syndesmotic fixation with two 3.5-mm cortical screws or two Tightropes.* No difference in mean contact pressure was found between the groups with pure axial load. **They reported** no change in the center of pressure in the TightRope group at any threshold; however, at every threshold tested, there was significant medial and anterior translation in the screw group relative to the intact ligamentous state. **They conclude** that fixing the syndesmosis with two Tightropes more closely matches native ankle joint mechanics compared to two 3.5-mm cortical screws.

*Igrek and Ulusoy conducted a level IV single-center retrospective cohort study evaluating 76 patients who underwent syndesmotic fixation with either screws or suture buttons for unilateral syndesmosis injuries.* No significant difference was found in malreduction between the syndesmotic screw (SS) or suture button (SB) groups when measuring the tibiofibular clear space and sagittal translation. **They found** that when the screws were sent from Zone 2 on the fibula, the malreduction rate (ST value) was significantly higher than in the SB group (p = 0.003). **They conclude** that the suture button system is a more convenient method for reduction and fixation compared to a syndesmotic screw.

Figure A is an AP radiograph of a left ankle demonstrating a Weber C lateral malleolus ankle fracture with complete disruption of the syndesmosis.

Incorrect answer choices:
Answer 1: No biomechanical differences have been found when comparing stainless steel and titanium screws for syndesmotic fixation.
Answer 2: When comparing the passage of three versus four cortices with screw fixation, no significant difference in screw pullout has been found.
Answer 4: Screw fixation has been associated with a higher rate of implant removal when compared to flexible fixation.
Answer 5: This is incorrect as there is a 40% rate of malreduction with screw fixation versus flexible (e.g., suture button) fixation.
