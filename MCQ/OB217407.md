## Question

An 18-month-old female is brought in due to concerns about toe walking on the left side. The mother's pregnancy was complicated by gestational diabetes and oligohydramnios requiring an amnioinfusion, but she was born vaginally at full term without complications. On exam, with the hips flexed to 60 degrees and the feet on the table, the right leg appears longer than the left and she has limited left hip abduction with full range of motion of the knees and ankles. Which of the following findings would most likely be seen on an x-ray of the pelvis?

## Options

1. acetabular teardrop
2. acetabular index of 20 deg
3. center edge angle of 40 deg
4. alpha angle of 60 deg
5. delayed ossification of the left femoral head

## Correct option

**5. delayed ossification of the left femoral head**

## Answer explanation

This patient's history and exam are most concerning for developmental dysplasia of the left hip. Delayed ossification of the left side is the most likely radiographic finding of these answer choices.

Developmental dysplasia of the hip (DDH) is a spectrum of abnormal development of the hip. Initial hip instability and incongruity lead to abnormal ossification of the femoral and dysplasia of the hip, which is followed by subluxation and dislocation. Patients under the age of 3 months may have a Barlow or Ortolani positive hip, as well as a positive Galeazzi sign, and ultrasound is the best diagnostic tool. Older children (>3 months) may have limited hip abduction, limb length discrepancy, toe walking, or a painless limp and radiographs are the imaging modality of choice. Radiographic findings consistent with hip dislocation include delayed ossification of the femoral epiphysis, femoral head ossification that is superior to Hilgenreiner's line and lateral Perkin's line, disruption of Shenton's line, and absence of acetabular teardrop. Radiographic findings suggestive of dysplasia include an acetabular index >25 deg in children > 6 months and a center edge angle <20 deg in children > 5 years of age.

*Herman et al provide a thorough review of the etiology and diagnostic evaluation of a limping child.* Trauma and infection are cited as the most causes of limping with inflammatory conditions, developmental diagnoses, overuse injuries, and malignancies as other potential causes. Initial blood work includes CBC, ESR, and CRP and first-line imaging modalities include x-ray and ultrasound.

*Yang et al provide an overview of the presentation, diagnostic workup, and treatment principles of DDH.* Regardless of nonoperative or operative treatment of DDH, the principle goal is to obtain and maintain a concentric reduction of the femoral head in the acetabulum. Treatment options include Pavlik harness and abduction bracing to surgical options including closed vs open reduction with spica casting, femoral osteotomy, or pelvic osteotomies depending on the age and severity.

Incorrect Answers
Answer 1: Acetabular teardrop is usually not present before hip reduction with chronic dislocations and the development of acetabular teardrop after reduction is a good prognostic indicator.
Answer 2: An acetabular index of <25 is normal in this age group and one would expect an increased AI due to underdevelopment of the acetabulum.
Answer 3: Center edge angle is only reliable in older children (>5 years) and <20 deg would abnormal
Answer 4: Alpha angle would be measured on a frog-leg lateral of the involved hip and would is used to evaluate a CAM lesion.
