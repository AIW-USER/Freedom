## Question

A 75-year-old male with 2 previous rotator cuff repairs has persistent shoulder pain and active forward elevation to 60 degrees. He has normal deltoid function with a positive lift-off test. ## Options

1. Revision supraspinatus repair
2. Shoulder hemiarthroplasty
3. Total shoulder arthroplasty
4. Reverse total shoulder arthroplasty
5. Latissimus transfer

## Correct option

**4. Reverse total shoulder arthroplasty**

## Answer explanation

The clinical and radiographic presentation is consistent with an irreparable rotator cuff tear with associated supraspinatus atrophy. In this setting, the most reliable surgical option is a reverse total shoulder arthroplasty (R-TSA).

*Wall et al. reviewed 240 patients who underwent R-TSA.* **They determined** that while the best results were seen in patients who had cuff tear arthopathy, patients undergoing revision arthroplasty and those with posttraumatic arthritis had less favorable results.

*Boileau et al. followed 45 patients and investigated the results of the Grammont reverse prosthesis in fractures, cuff tear arthritis and revision arthroplasty.* ASES shoulder score, Constant score and adjusted constant scores were all higher in the cuff tear arthropathy group. There were several deep infections which all occurred in the in the revision arthroplasty group.

Figure A is a radiograph of the shoulder that shows loss of congruency with a superior escape of the humerus and associated glenoid wear, both characteristic findings of a chronic rotator cuff tear that has failed previous surgery. Figure C and D shows and MRI with a retracted tear of the supraspinatus, superior migration, and severe fatty atrophy of the supraspinatus. Illustration A shows the postoperative radiographs of a reverse total shoulder arthroplasty (R-TSA). Video V shows the physical exam of a patient with pseudoparalysis of his left arm as a result of severe rotator cuff arthropathy.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Supraspinatus repair is unlikely to be successful after 2 prior failed attempts and, furthermore, it does not address the subscapularis.
Answer 2: Hemiarthroplasty is a reasonable option for RCTA, however, hemiarthroplasty should be avoided in a patient with subscapularis and supraspinatus insufficiency due to problems with instability and anterior-superior escape.
Answer 3: TSA would not be a suitable option in the setting of a RCTA.
Answer 5: A latissiumus transfer might be indicated in a younger patient without glenohumeral arthritis
