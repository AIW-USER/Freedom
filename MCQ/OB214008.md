## Question

A 45-year-old male with a chronic history of ulnar sided wrist pain is referred to you by a partner for a second opinion. The patient previously underwent occupational therapy, corticosteroid injection, and isolated arthroscopic TFCC debridement. Imaging obtained today in clinic is shown in Figure A. Despite these treatments, he has continued pain. What is the likely reason for continued pain in this patient?

![[q214008-a-l-ulnar-positive-xray-ap.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Inadequate TFCC debridement
2. Positive ulnar variance
3. Undiagnosed scapholunate ligament tear
4. Missed scaphoid waist fracture
5. No improvement is the expected outcome

## Correct option

**2. Positive ulnar variance**

## Answer explanation

Ulnar impaction syndrome is when a patient has a TFCC tear in the setting of ulnar positive variance. The treatment requires ulnar shortening osteotomy for long-term symptom improvement.

The triangular fibrocartilage complex is a confluence of ligaments and tendons with a central articular disc that aids in load transmission and load absorbing on the ulnar side of the wrist. In ulnar positivity, it commonly will develop a degenerative tear that can be treated with arthroscopic debridement or repair. If the patient has ulnar positivity, especially if >2mm, then an ulnar shortening osteotomy will likely be required. This can be done with a diaphyseal shortening osteotomy, or if <2mm of positivity is present a wafer osteotomy. Ulnar positivity can result from multiple etiologies to include normal anatomic variation, trauma, and as in this case Madelung deformity. Madelung deformity is a congenital dyschondrosis of the distal radius ulnar volar physis that results in a partial deficiency of growth of the distal radius.

Bickel provides a surgical technique of arthroscopic TFCC debridement with ulnar wafer resection. He describes performing the wafer resection with a high-speed burr until 2-3mm of ulnar minus variance is achieved. Postoperatively he maintains wrist immobilization for 6 weeks, or shorter if the patient is pain-free.

*Moeldner et al. compared TFCC debridement versus TFCC debridement with ulnar shortening osteotomy.* **They found** similar VAS score improvement and near-equal Mayo Modified Wrist Scores postoperatively and concluded that TFCC debridement alone is a sufficient initial intervention. However, they did find 1.8mm of positive ulnar variance as an indication for early ulnar shortening osteotomy when the patient had persistent postoperative pain.

*Kim and Song compared 27 wrists divided into three treatments of patients with ulnar impaction syndrome: ulnar shortening osteotomy alone (USO), TFCC arthroscopic debridement alone, and combined procedures.* **They found** similar DASH scores and Chun and Palmer scores between USO alone and TFCC debridement with USO, with significantly improved outcomes compared to TFCC debridement alone. **They recommend** TFCC debridement with ulnar shortening osteotomy for unstable TFCC tears and propose USO alone may be sufficient for stable TFCC tears.

*Berstein et al. retrospectively reviewed 11 patients TFCC arthroscopic debridement with USO compared to 16 patients with TFCC arthroscopic debridement with arthroscopic wafer resections.* **They found** that TFCC with arthroscopic wafer resection had similar function outcome scores as TFCC with USO. However, the complication rate was lower with arthroscopic wafer resection, and return to work was faster in this group.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Although this is possibly a reason for requiring revision surgery, the Xrays demonstrate clear positive ulnar variance which should have been addressed at the time of the initial surgery.
Answer 3: No SL widening is appreciated on imaging and the patient is complaining only of ulnar sided wrist pain.
Answer 4: No scaphoid waist fracture is seen on the Xray.
Answer 5: 90% of patients who undergo arthroscopic TFCC procedures have significant improvement in their symptoms and return to original work.
