## Question

A 38-year-old female presents with osteonecrosis of the femoral head with mild collapse. She is in excruciating pain despite trying injections, physical therapy, oral medications, and a cane. She wishes to proceed with surgery and asks about hip resurfacing arthroplasty. Which of the following is true regarding this technique?

## Options

1. Elevated metal ions indicate a well functioning prosthesis
2. Female gender is associated with increased complications
3. Presence of osteonecrosis is a contraindication
4. Higher risk of limb length discrepancy with this technique
5. Less than 50% success rate in the literature

## Correct option

**2. Female gender is associated with increased complications**

## Answer explanation

Female patients undergoing hip resurfacing arthroplasty with metal-on-metal bearings have been associated with higher complications.

Hip resurfacing arthroplasty takes advantage of metal-on-metal bearings to provide a stable implant with a large femoral head. Additionally, there is the preservation of the proximal femoral bone stock, which prevents limb length discrepancies that can be associated with traditional total hip arthroplasty. In female patients, hip resurfacing arthroplasty has been associated with increased complication rates and revisions. This has led several researchers to conclude female patients are not ideal candidates for this technique.

*Lombardi et al. reviewed an algorithmic approach for the diagnosis and management of metal-on-metal arthroplasty adverse reactions.* **They recommended** initial work-up with plain radiographs and laboratory evaluation with ESR, CRP, and serum metal ions. Further work-up should consist of aspiration with manual cell count and advanced imaging to assess for fluid collections or masses around the affected hip.

The Canadian Arthroplasty Society published their retrospective experience with hip resurfacing arthroplasty in 2773 hips. They reported a 5-year Kaplan-Meier survival of 96.4% with the most common causes of failure being femoral neck fracture and femoral component aseptic loosening. They concluded the higher rate of revision in female patients precludes routine use in young females.

*Haughom et al. systematically reviewed the difference in complication rates between genders in metal-on-metal hip resurfacing arthroplasty.* **They reported** women to have increased odds of adverse local tissue reaction, dislocation, aseptic loosening, and revision. **They concluded** women have a higher complication profile following hip resurfacing arthroplasty which may prohibit the routine use in this patient population.

Incorrect answers
Answer 1: Elevated metal ions are associated with metallosis and potential adverse local tissue reactions.
Answer 3: Osteonecrosis is not a contraindication provided there is the preservation of proximal femoral bone stock to accommodate the femoral implant.
Answer 4: There is a lower risk of limb length discrepancy with hip resurfacing arthroplasty compared to conventional hip arthroplasty.
Answer 5: Success rates and survival rates in the literature are >90%.
