## Question

A 23-year-old male presents to your office for evaluation of recurrent shoulder dislocations. The patient first reports dislocating his shoulder while attempting to dunk a basketball 7 years ago. Since that time he has sustained >10 dislocations. He has completed numerous sessions of physical therapy, but has never had surgery on his shoulder. Which of the following factors is most closely linked to failure following arthroscopic Bankart repair in this patient?

## Options

1. Age at initial dislocation
2. Age at the time of surgery
3. Use of >3 anchors
4. Glenoid bone loss
5. "On track" Hill-Sachs lesion

## Correct option

**4. Glenoid bone loss**

## Answer explanation

Patients with a history of recurrent traumatic anterior shoulder dislocations with glenoid bone loss are at an increased risk for recurrent instability following arthroscopic Bankart repair.

Traumatic Anterior shoulder instability, also referred to as TUBS (Traumatic Unilateral dislocations with a Bankart lesion requiring Surgery), are traumatic shoulder injuries that generally occur as a result of an anterior force to the shoulder while its abducted and externally rotated and may lead to recurrent anterior shoulder instability. Initial treatment is typically nonoperative with physical therapy, however, operative stabilization is recommended for recurrent instability and in those with persistent symptoms. Arthroscopic Bankart repair is a very successful operation if performed in the correct patient. Several factors including glenoid bone loss, shoulder hyperlaxity and the use of <3 anchors for repair have all been linked to higher risk of recurrent instability following arthroscopic Bankart repair in patients with recurrent traumatic anterior shoulder dislocations.

*Boileau et al. review the risk factors for recurrence of shoulder instability after arthroscopic Bankart repair.* **They report** that in their cohort at thirty-six months, fourteen patients (15.3%) experienced recurrent instability following arthroscopic Bankart repair. **They conclude** that patients with bone loss or with shoulder hyperlaxity are at risk for recurrent instability after arthroscopic Bankart repair.

*Beran et al. review the treatment of chronic glenoid defects in the setting of recurrent anterior shoulder instability.* *They report on several studies evaluating a myriad of techniques including coracoid transfer, allograft and autograft reconstruction.* **They conclude** that additional research is needed to optimize the preoperative glenoid defect assessment, further evaluate the reconstruction techniques, and follow the long-term effects of reconstruction on the development of glenohumeral arthrosis.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: While age at the time of dislocation is a predictive factor for recurrent instability, this is not the case following operative repair.
Answer 2: Age at the time of surgery has not been related to failure of reconstruction.
Answer 3: Reconstructions performed using <3 anchors has been linked to high rates of recurrent instability following surgery.
Answer 5: "On track" Hill-sachs lesions are those that do not engage the glenoid and are not linked to recurrent instability following arthroscopic bankart.
