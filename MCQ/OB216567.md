## Question

A 54-year-old male presents to the emergency department after a sudden onset of pain and inability to use his right arm after pushing himself up from a chair. X-rays are obtained and demonstrated in Figures A & B. The patient denies other medical problems. He is subsequently admitted for continued workup including a CT chest/abdomen/pelvis, which does not demonstrate any relevant abnormalities. What would be the next best step of the options below?

## Options

1. Intramedullary nailing of humerus
2. Initiate radiation therapy to entire right arm
3. Plate and screw fixation of fracture
4. Discussion of hospice care
5. Core needle biopsy

## Correct option

**5. Core needle biopsy**

## Answer explanation

The treatment of new lytic lesions without clear origin requires a biopsy prior to fixation.

The treatment of pathologic fractures requires a diagnostic workup to determine the diagnosis of the lesion. It is important to have a diagnosis prior to fixation in order to provide proper treatment. Workup for a metastatic lesion without a known primary source includes complete imaging of the involved bone (to include an MRI). Additional imaging studies include a CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis to evaluate for primary lesion location, and a technetium bone scan to evaluate for other metastatic lesions. Laboratory studies should be obtained to help in the identification of a primary lesion and provide information on cellular turnover including CBC, BMP, LFTs, ESR, LDH, UPEP/SPEP, and PSA. Finally, a biopsy should be obtained to histologically evaluate the lesion and provide a diagnosis.

*Biermann et al. provided an AAOS Instructional Course Lecture on metastatic bone disease.* Within the lecture, they discuss the diagnostic steps for metastatic bone disease. They impart the importance of completing a diagnostic workup prior to biopsy and having an accurate diagnosis before surgical intervention. Further discussion includes principles of surgical intervention for metastatic disease and minimally invasive treatment options.

Rougraff and Frassica present clinical scenarios for different patients with new evidence of metastatic disease. This includes patients with no known malignancy with or without pathologic fractures, first bony lesion with a history of cancer, and biopsy-proven carcinoma without a known primary site. In each scenario, they outline the clinical steps required to properly treat these patient examples.

Figures A and B are AP and lateral radiographs of a right humerus which demonstrate a pathologic fracture through a lytic lesion of the distal humeral shaft. Illustrations A and B demonstrate the postoperative AP and lateral X-rays for this patient demonstrating 90-90 plating providing rigid fixation and allowing early use of the extremity.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1&3: Surgical intervention should be delayed until a diagnosis is obtained to ensure proper treatment is being performed.
Answer 2: While radiation therapy may be part of this patient's care, an accurate diagnosis should be made prior to radiation therapy.
Answer 4: Hospice care discussion would be unnecessary at this time for this otherwise healthy patient.
