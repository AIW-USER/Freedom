## Options

1. Hand-Schuller-Christian disease
2. McCune-Albright syndrome
3. Maffucci's syndrome
4. Neurofibromatosis
5. Ollier disease

## Correct option

**1. Hand-Schuller-Christian disease**

## Answer explanation

The most likely diagnosis is Hand-Schuller-Christian disease

Hand-Schuller-Christian (HSC) disease is a chronic, disseminated form of Langerhans cell histiocytosis with bone and visceral involvement (lungs, spleen, liver, skin, lymph nodes). The classic triad of Hand-Schuller-Christian disease includes multiple lytic skull lesions, diabetes insipidus (increased thirst) and exopthalmos (bulging of the eye anteriorly out of the orbit). HSC often have multiple osteolytic lesions that can lead to spontaneous fractures. Symptoms of abdominal pain is often diffuse and non-specific, but usually indicative of visceral involvement.

*Velez-Yanguas and Warrier reviewed Langerhans’ cell histiocytosis.* **They report** that Langerhans cell histiocytosis comprises a spectrum of conditions including eosinophilic granuloma (EG, single lesion), Hand-Schuller-Christian disease (HSC, multiple bony and visceral lesions), and Letterer-Siwe disease (LSD, fatal, occurs in young children).

*Islinger et al. examined the presentation of Langerhans’ cell histiocytosis in patients older than 21 years.* **They report** that LCH most commonly occurs in children (60% of patients present < 20 years of age). Radiographic evaluation revealed similar margin and matrix patterns in adult and children, with a geographic lesion without sclerotic borders being the most common pattern. The location in adults more commonly affects the diaphysis than children (77% vs 56%).

Figure A shows multiple punched-out lytic skull lesions. Figure B shows a lytic lesion in the left proximal femur. Figure C shows the histological appearance of Langerhans cell histiocytosis with mononuclear histiocyte-like cells with a prominent nuclear groove (coffee bean or reniform nuclei, yellow box). Cytoplasm is prominently eosinophilic. Illustration D shows exopthalmos.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: McCune-Albright syndrome will present with polyostotic fibrous dysplasia (PFD), café-au-lait skin pigmentation, and autonomous endocrine hyperfunction (eg, precocious puberty). Other endocrine syndromes may be present, including hyperthyroidism, acromegaly, and Cushing syndrome.
Answer 3: Maffucci's syndrome presents with multiple enchondromas and soft-tissue angiomas. There is increased risk of visceral malignancies (astrocytoma, GI malignancy) with this syndrome.
Answer 4: Type 2 neurofibromatosis may have tibia bowing/pseudoarthrosis and scoliosis, and will likely have typical cutaneous signs (neurofibromas, cafe au lait spots). Lytic lesions and vertebral involvemtn are unlikely.
Answer 5: Ollier disease presents with multiple enchondromas. The enchondromas are largely found throughout the metaphyses and diaphyses of long bones. The involved long bones are usually dysplastic, with shortening and bowing.
