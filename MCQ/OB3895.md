## Question

Which of the following muscles is the primary antagonist to the structure identified by the white arrow in Figure A?

![[q3895-a-posterior-tibialis-mri.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Peroneus brevis
2. Peroneus longus
3. Anterior tibialis
4. Gastrocnemius-soleus complex
5. Extensor hallucis longus

## Correct option

**1. Peroneus brevis**

## Answer explanation

The structure identified in Figure A is the posterior tibialis tendon. Its primary antagonist is the peroneus brevis.

The primary function of the tibialis posterior is to invert the foot. It also adducts the foot, plantar flexes the ankle, and supinates the foot. Its primary antagonist is the peroneus brevis which everts the foot. The posterior tibialis is innervated by the tibial nerve and is supplied by branches of the sural, peroneal, and posterior tibial arteries.

*Pedowitz and Kovatis performed a review on the flatfoot deformity in the adult.* **They report** that the normal excursion of the posterior tibial tendon is 2 cm and its function is to invert the subtalar joint. Acting with the flexor digitorum longus and the flexor hallucis longus, it dynamically restrains the longitudinal arch. **They conclude** that the posterior tibialis is opposed mainly by the peroneus brevis and its main site of synovitis and rupture is between the medial malleolus and the navicular.

Figure A is a T-1 weighted MRI with an arrow pointing to the posterior tibialis tendon.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The antagonist of the anterior tibialis is the peroneus longus.
Answer 2: The antagonist of the peroneus longus is the anterior tibialis.
Answer 4: The antagonists of the gastrocnemius-soleus complex are the dorsiflexors of the ankle.
Answer 5: The antagonist of the extensor hallucis longus is the flexor hallucis longus.
