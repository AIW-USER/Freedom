## Question

Which amputation patient would have the highest risk of developing heterotopic ossification at the amputation site?

## Options

1. 75-year-old diabetic with an ischemic foot undergoing a transtibial amputation
2. 45-year-old woman that had a propane tank explode near her thigh undergoing a transfemoral amputation
3. 25-year-old army captain with a complex blast injury to his shin undergoing transfemoral amputation
4. 65-year-old with necrotizing fasciitis of his ankle undergoing knee transfemoral amputation
5. 35-year-old woman with a grade IIIC open distal tibia fracture who elects for a below the knee amputation

## Correct option

**2. 45-year-old woman that had a propane tank explode near her thigh undergoing a transfemoral amputation**

## Answer explanation

Heterotopic ossification (HO) most commonly occurs at the amputation site if the amputation was performed thru the zone of injury, especially if the injury was a blast mechanism.

*Potter et al reviewed 213 military amputations and* **found** heterotopic ossification was present in 63% of the residual limbs. A final amputation level within the zone of injury and a blast mechanism were risk factors for the development of heterotopic ossification. Symptomatic HO was removed successfully with a low recurrence rate (<10%).

Incorrect answers: All other answers listed describe amputations at a level above the zone of injury.
