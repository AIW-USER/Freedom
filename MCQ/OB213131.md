## Question

A 62-year-old patient presents after undergoing a lateral interbody fusion with posterior percutaneous screws for degenerative scoliosis. Since the surgery, the patient has been unable to stand upright and can walk with a walker for a few minutes at a time. Figures A, B, and C are the current images. ## Options

1. T12 pedicle subtraction osteotomy with posterior fusion and instrumentation from T6 to the pelvis
2. Multilevel Smith-Peterson osteotomies of the lumbar spine with fusion to the pelvis
3. L3 pedicle subtraction osteotomy posterior instrumentation fusion with extension to the pelvis
4. L3 vertebral column resection followed by posterior fusion and instrumentation to the pelvis
5. Multilevel Smith-Peterson osteotomies of the thoracic spine with posterior instrumentation and fusion from T2 to pelvis

## Correct option

**3. L3 pedicle subtraction osteotomy posterior instrumentation fusion with extension to the pelvis**

## Answer explanation

Correcting lumbar lordosis to within 9 degrees of pelvic incidence would improve sagittal plane alignment. In this case, it would be most effective performed with pedicle subtraction osteotomy of the lumbar spine given the previous anterior fusion.

Sagittal alignment is the most important factor for patient-reported outcomes in adult spinal deformity and requires intense preoperative planning. Loss of lumbar lordosis, which is normally 40-60 degrees or equal to pelvic incidence +/- 9 degrees, is associated with an increasingly positive sagittal vertical axis. In order to appropriately address the sagittal vertical axis a preoperative assessment of the amount of lumbar lordosis correction that is needed. Smith-Peterson osteotomies, or posterior column osteotomies, can provide approximately 10 degrees of correction per level. Pedicle subtraction osteotomies remove the pedicle and the posterior column, providing 20-30 degrees of correction and is indicated when there is a need for 30 degrees or greater in the lumbar spine. A three-column osteotomy, or vertebral column resection, there is complete removal of a vertebral body and posterior column leading to greater than 45 degrees of correction, which is usually indicated for sharper curves.

*Schwab et al. performed a review of the literature regarding spinopelvic parameters in adult spinal deformity.* The literature describes the "cones of economy" that allow an erect stance with a forward gaze without excessive energy expenditure. The authors increasing pelvic tilt as a compensatory mechanism for increasing the sagittal vertical axis and can act as a surrogate for poor patient-reported outcomes. **They proposed** that in order to improve sagittal vertical axis deviations it is necessary to correct lumbar lordosis proportionally to pelvic incidence.

*Klineberg et al. reviewed the literature regarding spinopelvic parameters and adult spinal deformity.* The literature supports that sagittal plane correction as the most important factor for patient-reported outcomes in adult spinal deformity surgery. Pelvic tilt and sacral slope are spinopelvic parameters that compensate for the increased sagittal vertical axis and are further correlated with health-related quality of life measures. **The authors stated** that consideration of the spinopelvic compensatory mechanisms is crucial for successful outcomes in correcting the sagittal plane deformity.

*Kose et al. reviewed the indications, complications, and capabilities of spinal osteotomies.* **They stated** that pedicle subtraction osteotomies can provide between 30-40 degrees or 9 cm of sagittal balance correction. Indications for pedicle subtraction osteotomy include sagittal imbalance >12 cm, previous 360° fusion, sharp angular kyphosis, and ankylosing spondylitis. Potential complications include extensive intraoperative bleeding, transient neurological deficits, and pseudoarthrosis.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: A T12 pedicle subtraction osteotomy likely improves the patient's sagittal vertical alignment, would not affect lumbar lordosis or pelvic tilt which is the goal of treatment in this case.
Answer 2: Smith-Peterson osteotomies would be ineffective in the lumbar spine since the patient is already fused anteriorly. Smith-Peterson osteotomies require a mobile disc space to correct through.
Answer 4: A vertebral column resection allows for sagittal and coronal correction. However, this approach predisposes the spine to instability, intraoperative blood loss, and high complication rates.
Answer 5: Smith-Peterson osteotomies of the thoracic spine will decrease thoracic kyphosis, which is not desired in this patient. Further, it would not address the loss of lumbar lordosis and increased pelvic tilt.
