## Question

Two 75-year-old females are sitting together in follow-up. They are similar in age and activity, and both are 3 months out from their treatment of nearly identical displaced femoral neck fractures. One patient underwent internal screw fixation and the other underwent hemiarthroplasty. Each has returned to ambulating with full weight on the surgically treated side. What is the expected outcome between these two patients from now to one year out from surgery?

## Options

1. Overall higher failure rates with internal fixation techniques
2. Overall higher failure rates with replacement techniques
3. No difference in overall failure rates between the two techniques
4. Overall higher mortality rates with internal fixation techniques
5. Overall higher mortality rates with replacement techniques

## Correct option

**1. Overall higher failure rates with internal fixation techniques**

## Answer explanation

When comparing patients treated with internal fixation or arthroplasty there is an overall higher failure rate with internal fixation at 2 years out from surgery.

Options for operative intervention for the treatment of displaced femoral neck fractures include fracture reduction with internal fixation and arthroplasty. Failure and revision rates with internal fixation techniques have historically been higher compared to arthroplasty in the postoperative period. Most of the failures occur in the first two years of follow-up. This leads to a higher overall revision and failure rate at 10 years postoperatively.

*Leonardsson et al. randomized a series of 470 patients over 70 years of age with displaced femoral neck fractures to internal fixation or arthroplasty.* At ten years there were 99 failures (45.6%) after internal fixation compared with 17 (8.8%) after replacement. However, most of these failures occurred by the 2-year follow-up (43% internal fixation; 6% arthroplasty).

*Gjertsen et al. reviewed the Norweigen registry to assess patient satisfaction, pain and quality of life 4 months after displaced femoral neck fracture in patients >70 years of age.* **They showed** that patients operated with hemiarthroplasty had less pain (VAS 27 vs. 41), were more satisfied with the result of the operation (VAS 33 vs. 48), and had better EQ-5D index score 4 months postoperatively (0.51 vs. 0.42) than patients who were operated with internal fixation. **They concluded** that displaced femoral neck fracture should be treated with arthroplasty.

*Gjertsen et al. reviewed data from the Norwegian Hip Fracture Register comparing internal fixation with arthroplasty in patients >70 years of age.* **They found** a failure rate of 22.6% with the internal fixation group and 2.9% in the arthroplasty group. **They concluded** that elderly patients with displaced femoral neck fractures should be treated with arthroplasty.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 2: There is a lower revision rate with arthroplasty when compared to internal fixation.
Answer 3: There is a difference in the overall failure rates between internal fixation versus arthroplasty for displaced femoral neck fractures in elderly patients.
Answer 4,5: There is no difference in mortality rates at all time points between fixation and arthroplasty techniques.
