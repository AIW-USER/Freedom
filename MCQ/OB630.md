## Question

A 6-year-old girl is referred for bilateral in-toeing gait. Which of the following physical examination findings would make increased femoral anterversion the most likely reason for this finding?

## Options

1. Thigh-foot angle with 15 degrees of internal rotation
2. Thigh-foot angle with 40 degrees of external rotation
3. Convex lateral border of foot
4. Hips with 80 degrees of internal rotation and 15 degrees of external rotation
5. Hips with 50 degrees of internal rotation and 30 degrees of external rotation

## Correct option

**4. Hips with 80 degrees of internal rotation and 15 degrees of external rotation**

## Answer explanation

Increased femoral anteversion is characterized by increased hip internal rotation (normal 20-60 degrees) and decreased external rotation (normal 30-60 degrees).

There are three main causes of in-toeing gait among children: excessive femoral anteversion, internal tibial torsion, and metatarsus adductus. Femoral anteversion is seen most commonly in early childhood (3-6 year olds) and is twice as common among girls than boys. Femoral anteversion is characterized by increased hip internal rotation (more than 70 degrees) and decreased external rotation (less than 20 degrees). Children with intoeing due to femoral anteversion also have internally rotated patellae when standing and are more likely to sit in the "W" position.

*Lincoln et al. reviewed common rotational variations in children.* **The authors noted** that most rotational variations in young children are benign and resolve spontaneously, but understanding normal variations is critical to identifying true structural abnormalities. **The authors also noted** that in-toeing is usually attributable to metatarsus adductus in an infant, internal tibial torsion in the toddler, and femoral anteversion in children younger than 10 years.

*Blackmur et al. retrospectively 202 patients referred to an orthopaedic clinic for in-toeing gait.* Increased femoral anteversion and internal tibial torsion were the most common diagnoses, and no patient required surgery during the time period of the study. **The authors determined** that the management and outcome for these patients was not affected by a referral to an orthopaedic clinic.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: A thigh-foot angle with greater than 10 degrees of internal rotation is indicative of internal tibial torsion
Answer 2: A thigh-foot angle with 40 degrees of external rotation is indicative of an out-toeing gait
Answer 3: A convex lateral border of the foot is usually indicative of metatarsus adducts
