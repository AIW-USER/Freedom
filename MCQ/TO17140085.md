Chapter: Regional Orthopedics MCQs — Clinical Examination (Shoulder)

## Question

Positive Hawkin’s sign is indicative of (FNB Pattern Paper 2017)

## Options

1. Rotator cuff tear
2. Subscapularis tear
3. Subacromial bursitis
4. Brachial plexus palsy

## Correct option

**3. Subacromial bursitis**

## Answer explanation

Impingement syndrome: This is characterized by pain anteriorly over the shoulder in 60–120 degrees of shoulder abduction. During shoulder abduction the greater tuberosity slides under the acromion, the movements being smoothened by presence of subacromial bursa. Any condition that compromises this space may result in painful arc syndrome due to impingement. Causes of impingement syndrome are:

- Subacromial bursitis
- A hook shaped acromion.
- Rotator cuff pathology (supraspinatus tendinitis, calcific tendinitis, partial tear)
- Fracture of greater tuberosity

Clinical tests for impingement syndrome:

Neer's impingement sign: Examiner stabilizes the scapula, and passively raises the arm in forward flexion. This causes pain in the shoulder anteriorly.

Neer’s impingement test: 5 mL of 1% lidocaine is injected into the subacromial space. After several minutes, the Neer maneuver is again performed. Absent or significantly reduced pain indicates that subacromial impingement was the cause of pain.

Hawkins-Kennedy test: This is a sensitive but less specific test. Forward flexion of shoulder to 90 degrees with forcible internal rotation reproduces the impingement pain.

Scapular assistance test: Examiner pushes the inferior medial scapular angle during shoulder elevation to assist scapular upward rotation and posterior tilt. A positive result is indicated by relief of painful symptoms related to the subacromial impingement and increased arc of motion.

Answer: 3
