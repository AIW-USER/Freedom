## Question

A 32-year-old male is involved in an unwitnessed trauma, and later pronounced dead in the trauma bay. The cause of death is ultimately determined to be a closed head injury. Which of the following fracture patterns is most closely associated with this cause of death?

## Options

1. Figure A
2. Figure B

4. Figure D
5. Figure E

## Correct option

**2. Figure B**

## Answer explanation

Figure B demonstrates a lateral compression pelvic ring injury. Closed head injuries are the most common identifiable cause of death in patients sustaining this type of injury.

Lateral compression injuries result from a lateral impact to the pelvis that rotates the pelvis toward the midline on the side of the impact. LC II injuries, as depicted in Figure B, consist of superior and inferior pubic rami fractures with a concomitant posterior ilium "crescent" fracture. The most common cause of death following these injuries is a closed head injury. In contrast, in patients with anterior posterior compression (APC) type pelvic ring injuries, visceral injuries and resulting hemorrhage are the most common cause of death.

*Burgess et al. review pelvic ring disruptions and treatment protocols.* **They report** an overall mortality rate of 8.6% following pelvic ring injuries. The most common identifiable cause of death in patients with LC type injuries was closed head injury. **They conclude** that the cause of death was directly related to the pelvic injury in less than 50% of patients.

*Smith et al. reviewed hemodynamically unstable pelvic ring fracture patients and* **found** that there is a positive association of blood replacement requirements and mortality. **They also reported** that death within the first 24 hours after admission was most often a result of acute blood loss while death after the first day was most often caused by multi-organ failure.

Figure A is a schematic demonstrating an APC III injury. Figure B demonstrates an LC II pelvic ring injury. Figure C is a Denis 2 sacral injury. Figure D shows a Pipkin 3 femoral head injury. Figure E represents a both column acetabular fracture.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: APC III injuries are most closely linked to visceral causes of death (ie. multi-organ failure, hemorrhage).
Answer 3, 4 & 5: Closed head injuries are not the most common cause of death identified in patients with sacral, femoral head and acetabular fractures.
