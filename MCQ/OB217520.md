## Question

A 29-year-old female and her husband are undergoing genetic counseling prior to starting a family. She reports a history of dysplasia epiphysealis hemimelica (DEH). Which of the following is most consistent with this condition?

## Options

1. High risk of malignant transformation
2. Exostoses developing in the upper extremities
3. 25% chance of non-viable birth if both parents are carriers
4. Asymmetric epiphyseal overgrowth
5. Autosomal dominance inheritance

## Correct option

**4. Asymmetric epiphyseal overgrowth**

## Answer explanation

Dysplasia epiphysealis hemimelicia (DEH) results in hemimelic overgrowth of the cartilage of one or more epiphyses in children.

DEH is a rare non-inherited condition, also known as Trevor's Disease, thats estimated to occur in 1 in 1,000,000. Most commonly occurring in children or teenagers, DEH results in osteocartilaginous tumors arising from one side of the epiphysis. Treatment varies from observation in asymptomatic patients with small lesions to surgical resection in those who have pain or larger tumors. Irregularities created in the articular surface can result in development of secondary arthritis. As growth continues, the bone may fuse on the contralateral side of the joint as with other exostoses.

*Stevens et al review DEH and compare it to osteochondromas.* **They report** that histologically DEH appears as chondrocyte clusters in a fibrillary matrix, with a thick disorganized cartilage. **They conclude** that Type II collagen was also clearly expressed in osteochondromas and only rarely found in DEH.

*Kuo et al review the clinical features and management of DEH.* **They report** that the lower limb was involved in all cases with the ankle (talus) and knee (distal femur) being the most common sites. Further, all cases were observed initially and showed progressive increase in size of the lesion with skeletal growth. **They conclude** that all patients in this series ultimately underwent surgical excision.

*Perl et al review the pathophysiologic aspects of DEH.* **They report** that that arthroscopic biopsy showed a cellular phenotype of clustered chondrocytes exhibiting characteristics of chondroprogenitor cells and terminally differentiated cells. **They conclude** that these findings suggesta dysregulation of resident progenitor cells as a pathophysiologic mechanism.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: There has been no reported risk of malignant transformation with DEH.
Answer 2: DEH characteristically occurs in the lower extremities.
Answer 3&5: There is no inheritance pattern or association with non-viable births in patients a history of DEH.
