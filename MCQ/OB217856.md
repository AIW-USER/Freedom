## Question

An active 78-year-old female presents to your clinic for evaluation of her debilitating hip osteoarthritis. She has trialed and failed many months of anti-inflammatories, physical therapy, and corticosteroid injections. You indicate her for surgery. During your discussing of the risks and benefits, she mentions that she would like to minimize the risk of periprosthetic fracture, while maintaining the longevity of her implants. ## Answer explanation

To minimize the risk of fracture while maintaining longevity of the implants, a hybrid total hip arthroplasty with a cementless acetabulum and cemented femoral component would be the best selection.

The incidence of periprosthetic fractures continues to rise as a result of the increase in arthroplasty procedures and increased demands of elderly patients. Most of these fractures occur intra-operatively, with a prevalence of 3.5% in uncemented and 0.4% of cemented femoral components. Uncemented acetabular components have largely become the gold standard given the risk of early loosening of cemented acetabular components.

Harris provided a review of the results of uncemented acetabular components in total hip arthroplasty. He noted that cemented components were fraught with complications, namely early loosening. He stated that only 2% of hemispheric cementless acetabular shells required revision at 12.5 years.

Abdel el al. reviewed over 32,000 primary total hip arthroplasties to determine risk factors, nature, chronology, and treatment of periprosthetic femur fractures. There was a fracture risk of 1.7% overall, with a higher rate seen in uncemented femoral components (3% vs 0.23%). Female patients and those over 65 years of age were at particular risk.

Figure A demonstrates cemented fixation of both the acetabulum and femur.
Figure B demonstrates hybrid fixation with a cemented femoral component and cementless acetabular component.
Figure C demonstrates a bipolar hemiarthroplasty.
Figure D demonstrates a cementless total hip arthroplasty.
Figure E demonstrates a hip resurfacing.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Cemented acetabular components have largely fallen out of favor due to the risk of early loosening.
Answer 3: A bipolar hemiarthroplasty would not be indicated for the treatment of primary hip osteoarthritis.
Answer 4: A press fit femoral component would increase the patient’s risk of periprosthetic fracture.
Answer 5: A hip resurfacing would not be indicated in a 78-year-old female.
