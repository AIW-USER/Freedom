## Question

Figure A is the clinical appearance of a neonate with a diagnosed brachial plexus palsy. Which of the following is true with respect to this type of brachial plexus palsy?

![[q5714-a-erbs-palsy-waiter-tip-hand.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. It is commonly associated with miosis, ptosis, and anhydrosis
2. It invovles all 5 nerve roots that contribute to the brachial plexus
3. It is the most common pattern of obstetric brachial plexus palsy
4. It requires surgical repair in approximately 75% of neonates
5. It is often referred to as "Klumpke's Palsy"

## Correct option

**3. It is the most common pattern of obstetric brachial plexus palsy**

## Answer explanation

Figure A shows an infant with his right upper extremity held adducted, internally rotated, and wrist flexed. This is the common position for an Erb's Palsy, which is the most common pattern of obstetric brachial plexus palsy (OBPP).

OBPP is typically associated with difficult vaginal deliveries and is thought to occur due to a stretching injury of the brachial plexus. The most common injury pattern is an Erb's Palsy, which involves injury to the upper trunk (C5,6) and appears clinically with a "waiter's tip hand" as seen in Figure A. Erb's Palsy has the best prognosis for recovery when compared to other patterns such as Klumpke's Palsy or a pan-plexus palsy. The worst prognosis is seen in a pan-plexus palsy with associated Horner's syndrome. These will more commonly require surgical repair for the best outcome.

*Lagerkvist et al. prospectively followed children born in western Sweden from 1999 to 2001.* **They found** an incidence of OBPP of 2.9 per 1000. 100% of children who regained elbow flexion by 3 months of age went on to complete recovery. 18 of the 114 infants with OBPP had some persistent deficits, 5 mild, 11 moderate, and 2 severe.

Incorrect Answers:

Answer 1: Miosis, ptosis, and anhydrosis describes a Horner's Syndrome, or damage of the sympathetic trunk. This is most commonly associated with a pan-plexus palsy.
Answer 2: Injury to all five nerve roots, or a pan-plexus palsy, would create a flail extremity.
Answer 4: Erb's Palsy has the best prognosis for recovery and typically does not require surgical repair.
Answer 5: Klumpke's Palsy is an injury to the lower plexus nerve roots, C8 and T1. This typically presents with a "claw hand", which has wrist extension, MCP extension, and IP flexion.
