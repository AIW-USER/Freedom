Chapter: Regional Orthopedics MCQs — Pes Planus - Flat Foot

## Question

An 18-year-old male presents with deep, poorly localized hindfoot pain exacerbated by activity and walking on uneven ground. Clinical examination reveals a rigid pes planus with a severely restricted inversion-eversion arc, which does not correct on toe-raising. A tarsal coalition is suspected. Which of the following pathological pairings correctly matches the most common site of this condition with its typical age of symptomatic presentation and primary histological interface?

## Options

1. Calcaneocuboid coalition; 16–20 years; Synostosis
2. Calcaneonavicular coalition; 12-16 years; Syndesmosis or Synchondrosis
3. Talonavicular coalition; 14–18 years; Syndesmosis
4. Talocalcaneal (middle facet) coalition; 12–16 years; Synchondrosis or Synostosis

## Correct option

**4. Talocalcaneal (middle facet) coalition; 12–16 years; Synchondrosis or Synostosis**

## Answer explanation

Answer: 4

Talocalcaneal coalitions involve the middle facet, typically become symptomatic during early adolescence (12–16 years) as the bridge ossifies, and often present as a rigid bony or fibrocartilaginous restriction.

Pathophysiological Notes

 - The Chronological Cascade: Symptoms of rigid pes planus manifest precisely when the mesenchymal bridge undergoes physiologic chondrification and subsequent ossification. Because the calcaneonavicular bridge begins ossification earlier than the talocalcaneal middle facet, CN coalitions present clinically in younger children (8–12 years) compared to TC coalitions (12–16 years).

 - Histological Transition: While congenital in etiology (failure of mesenchymal segmentation in utero), the interface is rarely completely osseous at birth. The mechanical stress of early ambulation causes micro-tears in the fibrous (syndesmosis) or cartilaginous (synchondrosis) interfaces, triggering secondary peroneal spasticity and localized inflammatory pain before definitive synostosis locks the joint into a rigid, non-reducible valgus posture.
