## Question

An industrial representative presents a new core needle biopsy kit that he states can obtain over twice the amount of tissue compared to a conventional fine needle aspiration. He shows you a pamphlet detailing that after 100 separate uses, this new core needle biopsy kit obtained on average 1.2 ± 0.3 cm3 of tissue, whereas the conventional fine needle aspiration only obtained 0.5 ± 0.2 cm3. If the study had normally distributed data with an appropriate sample size based on a priori power analysis, which of the following conclusions can be made based on the information provided?

## Options

1. Based on 95% confidence intervals, the new core-needle biopsy gathers significantly less tissue
2. Based on 95% confidence intervals, the new core-needle biopsy gathers significantly more tissue
3. Approximately 95% of core needle biopsy tissue samples were between 1 - 1.5 cm3
4. Conventional fine needle aspiration produces 0.1 - 0.9 cm3 of tissue 95% of the time
5. The new core-needle biopsy really does obtain twice the amount of tissue every time

## Correct option

**4. Conventional fine needle aspiration produces 0.1 - 0.9 cm3 of tissue 95% of the time**

## Answer explanation

The 95% confidence interval is the mean value ± 2 standard deviations.

A basic understanding of descriptive statistics is prudent for physicians and surgeons in order to appropriately interpret reported findings from academic literature and industry advertisements. Misinterpretation or, potentially worse, omission of specific details (such as the p-value of a Student’s t-test) is not uncommon. In general, academic literature descriptive reporting of means is as follows: [mean value] ± [1 standard deviation]. Each standard deviation correlates to the following (Illustration A):

1 standard deviation = 68% of all data points
2 standard deviations = 95% of all data points
3 standard deviations = 99.7% of all data points

In this example, the 95% confidence interval of each group can be calculated as the following:

Core needle biopsy: 1.2 ± 0.3cm3 [95% confidence interval range 0.6-1.8 cm3]
Conventional fine needle aspiration: 0.5 ± 0.2 cm3 [95% confidence interval range 0.1-0.9 cm3]

Importantly, overlapping 95% confidence intervals do not impart statistical significance (or a lack of significance). Formal hypothesis testing via a formal statistical test that directly compares the differences across groups would be required to make such conclusions (i.e. t-test). This is due to the potential difference in variability in the means of both groups, which is not accounted for when comparing 95% confidence intervals. However, it is accounted for in formal hypothesis testing models. In the above provided example, there are overlapping 95% confidence intervals however both groups were significantly different (p<0.005) based on a two-tailed t-test.

Kocher and Zurakowski provided a comprehensive review article regarding clinical epidemiology and biostatistics for orthopaedic surgeons. They discuss the various study designs, hypothesis testing, outcome assessments, and biostatistics utilization with real-world examples. The authors conclude that an understanding of these concepts is essential both for investigators conducting clinical research and for practitioners interpreting clinical research reports.

*Greenland and colleagues provided a similar review of basic biostatistical foundational knowledge in order to prevent interpretive errors.* *The authors provide an explanatory list of 25 common misinterpretations of p-values, confidence intervals, and power including a discussion on overlapping confidence intervals.* **They conclude** by emphasizing the importance of understanding what the p-value really means, and how to appropriately report and interpret its value.

Illustration A displays the confidence intervals of a normally distributed data set.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1&2: As discussed above, definitive conclusions on significance cannot be made based on this information alone. A formal statistical test would be required to make such definitive conclusions.
Answer 3: This answer reports only 1 standard deviation, which would correlate to a 68% confidence interval.
Answer 5: While there are certain specific data points where the core needle biopsy COULD have gathered twice the amount of tissue compared to fine needle aspiration, this would not be an expected regular occurrence.
