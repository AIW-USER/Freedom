## Question

Most common location for an Arachnoid cyst is

## Options

1. Sylvian fissure
2. Interhemispheric
3. Sellar and suprasellar
4. Cerebellopontine angle (CPA)

## Correct option

**1. Sylvian fissure**

## Answer explanation

Ans: A

ARACHNOID CYSTS (AC)

- Also known as Leptomeningeal cysts, are congenital lesions that arise during development from splitting of arachnoid membrane (thus they are technically intra-arachnoid cysts) and contain fluid that is usually identical to CSF. They do not communicate with the ventricles or subarachnoid space.
- Typically, these cysts are lined with meningothelial cells positive for epithelial membrane antigen (EMA) and negative for carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA).
- AC may also occur in the spinal canal.
- They comprise around 1% of intracranial masses.
- Male: female ratio is 4:1 and they are more common on the left side.

- Most ACs are asymptomatic and incidental findings. Those that become symptomatic usually do so in early childhood. The presentation varies with location of the cyst, but symptoms are mostly mild considering the large size of these cysts.
- Typical presentation symptoms include:

1. symptoms of intracranial hypertension (elevated ICP): Headache, Nausea/Vomiting, lethargy

 2. seizures or other focal signs/symptoms of a space occupying lesion

- A sudden deterioration of symptoms may occur at times: -

a) due to haemorrhage (into cyst or subdural compartment): middle fossa cysts are notorious for haemorrhage due to tearing of bridging veins.

 b) due to rupture of the cyst

- Suprasellar cysts may additionally present with: -

a) hydrocephalus (probably due to compression of the third ventricle)

 b) endocrine symptoms: occurs in up to 60%. Includes precocious puberty

 c) head bobbing (the so-called “bobble-head doll syndrome”)

 d) visual impairment

- MRI is better than CT in differentiating the CSF contained in arachnoid cysts from the fluid of neoplastic cysts. May also show cyst walls.

- Treatment: -

- Recommendation for incidentally discovered arachnoid cyst in adults: a single follow-up imaging study in 6–8 months is usually adequate to rule-out any increase in size. Subsequent studies only if concerning symptoms develop.
- Surgical treatment options may be considered for symptomatic cysts. This may include: -

- Drainage by needle aspiration or burr hole evacuation
- Craniotomy and excising cyst wall and fenestrating it into basal cisterns
- endoscopic cyst fenestration through a burr hole
- shunting of cyst into peritoneum or into vascular system
