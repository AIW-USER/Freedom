## Options

1. The use of two fully threaded screws
2. Primary surgery with bone graft epiphysiodesis
3. Initial treatment with hip spica
4. More horizontal screw orientation
5. Ensuring 80% of threads cross the physis

## Correct option

**1. The use of two fully threaded screws**

## Answer explanation

Figures A and B show the progressive failure of a slipped capital femoral epiphysis (SCFE) after single screw fixation. Out of the available options, two fully threaded screws would have increased fixation stability and best prevented this complication.

The mainstay treatment of treatment for SCFE is in situ fixation of the epiphysis with 1 or 2 screws. Stability of fixation has shown to be increased with larger diameter screws, increased number of screws, 32mm or fully threaded screws across the physis, and strict post-operative weight-bearing restrictions. Despite the greater potential for chondrolysis secondary to screw penetration, the biomechanical benefit of 2 screws needs to be considered

*Upasani et al. studied fixation stability as a function of screw thread distribution across the physis.* **They showed** that the greatest strength and stiffness are achieved when 40%-60% of threads engage the epiphysis. **They found** a significant decrease in strength when greater than 80% of threads cross the physis.

*Dragoni et al. compared the stability of screw thread length with either 16 mm threaded, 32 mm threaded, or fully threaded screws.* **They showed** that 16-mm threaded screws have the highest frequency of femoral neck failure.

*Kishan et al. designed a biomechanical study to compare the stability of single screw vs. 2 screw fixation for an unstable slipped capital femoral epiphysis.* **They showed** that 2 screw constructs were 66% stiffer and 66% stronger than the single screw constructs. **They concluded** that the biomechanical benefit of 2 screws needs to be considered in the face of greater violation of the physis +/- articular surface.

Figure A shows a frog-leg lateral radiograph of the hip with a single partially threaded screw for fixation of an SCFE. Figure B shows the progressive slip of the SCFE with hardware failure.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Primary surgery with bone graft epiphysiodesis involves removing a portion of residual physis through a rectangular window on the anterior aspect of the femoral neck. This procedure runs the risk of further slippage until the physis is closed.
Answer 3: Hip spica cast immobilization is an antiquated technique. Chondrolysis and further slip are common complications.
Answer 4: Screw orientation can influence stability with increased compression across the physis. However, none of the placement patterns screw constructs seem to be superior in fixation stability.
Answer 5: with partially threaded screws, the greatest strength and stiffness are achieved when 40%-60% of threads engage the epiphysis.
