## Question

A 15-year-old boy has had pain and swelling in his leg for several months. He recently complained of fever, fatigue, and unexplained weight loss. ## Options

1. Adamantinoma; t(x;18)
2. Ewing's Sarcoma; t(11;22)
3. Myxoid chondrosarcoma; t(9;22)
4. Osteofibrous dyplasia; t(11;33)
5. Osteosarcoma; t(2;13)

## Correct option

**2. Ewing's Sarcoma; t(11;22)**

## Answer explanation

This patient presents with imaging and biopsy findings consistent with Ewing's Sarcoma, which has a classic t(11;22) translocation, creating the EWS-FLI1 fusion protein (Answer 2).

Ewing's Sarcoma is an aggressive malignancy of bone, most commonly affecting patients under 30 years of age. Patients will often complain of a painful, enlarging mass along with variations of fever, chills, weight loss, and fatigue. Radiographs typically demonstrate a permeative, lytic lesion in the metaphysis or diaphysis of long bones with occasional reactive bone formation (onion skinning or Codman's triangle). MRI will often display a large soft tissue component. Diagnosis is made with a biopsy demonstrating monotonous, small, round blue cells with a high nuclear-to-cytoplasmic ratio. Ewing sarcoma stains positively for CD99. The standard treatment of a resectable tumor is neoadjuvant chemotherapy, followed by surgical resection and adjuvant chemotherapy. Prognosis is fairly poor, with a 10-year survival rate of about 75% in localized disease and about 35% in metastatic disease.

*Carvajal and Meyers published a review on Ewing's sarcoma and its related family of primitive neuroectodermal tumors.* **The authors note** the characteristic small, round blue cells on histology and the classic cytogenetic t(11;22) (and less common t(21;22)) translocation leading to the EWS-FLI1 fusion protein. The authors describe the standard of care treatment of neoadjuvant chemotherapy with surgical resection and adjuvant chemotherapy while noting that radiation therapy is employed for unresectable or inadequately resected tumors.

Balamuth and Womer provided a comprehensive review of Ewing's sarcoma in The Lancet Oncology. They discuss the common presenting signs/symptoms, imaging findings, appropriate workup, and treatment options. They provide an in-depth review of immunologic and chemotherapies that are being developed to treat the molecular pathways, such as the EWS-FLI1 fusion protein, involved in the disease process.

*Barretina et al. published an analysis of genomic aberrations of soft tissue sarcomas.* Covering many genetic alterations and translocations, **the authors highlight** the importance of identifying and understanding genomic aberrations to guide future treatments.

Figure A is the radiographs and MRI of a right distal tibia in a skeletally immature individual with characteristic findings of cortical disruption and a large soft tissue component on MRI suggestive of Ewing Sarcoma. Figure B is a histology slide of Ewing Sarcoma with monotonous small, round blue cells and a high nuclear-to-cytoplasmic ratio.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Adamantinoma, although classically found in the anterior tibia in patients under 30 years of age, typically displays sharply circumscribed "soap bubble" lesions on radiographs and will not have the periosteal reaction seen in Figure A. Histology will demonstrate biphasic nests of epithelial cells with a fibrous background. Furthermore, the t(x;18) translocation is seen in Synovial Sarcoma.
Answer 3: Myxoid chondrosarcoma, a rare subtype of chondrosarcoma, is typically seen in older patients with a predominantly lytic appearance on radiographs. Histology resembles cartilage tissue, not small, round blue cells seen in Figure B.
Answer 4: Although osteofibrous dysplasia is also seen in the tibia of younger patients, radiographs will show a multiloculated lytic defect with a well-defined sclerotic border. Histology will show a fibro-osseous lesion with rimming osteoblasts. Furthermore, osteofibrous dysplasia does not have a t(11;33) translocation.
Answer 5: Osteosarcoma should always be considered in younger patients with this presentation. Osteosarcomas will also have a large periosteal reaction seen on radiographs. However, the histology will feature atypical tumor cells producing "lacey" osteoid. Furthermore, the t(2;13) translocation is seen in alveolar rhabdomyosarcoma.
