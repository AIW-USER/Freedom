## Question

With a cavovarus foot, plantar flexion of the first ray is driven by a weak muscle "X" being overpowered by a strong muscle "Y". A tendon transfer to correct this involves transferring muscle "Y" to muscle "Z". Which muscles are represented by X, Y, and Z, respectively?

## Options

1. X = Peroneus longus, Y = Tibialis anterior, Z = Peroneus brevis
2. X = Tibialis anterior, Y = Peroneus longus, Z = Peroneus brevis
3. X = Tibialis anterior, Y = Peroneus brevis, Z = Peroneus Longus
4. X = Peroneus brevis, Y = Peroneus longus, Z = Flexor digitorum longus
5. X = Posterior tibialis, Y = Peroneus brevis, Z = Flexor digitorum longus

## Correct option

**2. X = Tibialis anterior, Y = Peroneus longus, Z = Peroneus brevis**

## Answer explanation

Weakness of the anterior tibialis with strong peroneal longus muscle tone is believed to be one of the factors causing a plantarflexed first metatarsal. The peroneus longus to brevis transfer attempts to correct this as well as the posterior tibialis/peroneus brevis imbalance.

Cavovarus foot deformity is related to an imbalance between agonists and antagonists, with tibialis anterior (TA) and peroneus brevis (PB) weaker compared to posterior tibialis (PT) and peroneus longus (PL). Agonist-antagonist relationship is PL-TA (PL stronger) and PT-PB (PT stronger). First line of treatment is non-operative, often with a lateral heel wedge and metacarpal head recess insert. If non-operative treatment fails, and the foot is still supple, tendon transfers can be considered to correct the imbalance(s).

*Olson et al. used cadaveric biomechanical modeling to examine the role of the peroneus longus (PL), extensor hallucis longus (EHL), and flexor hallucis longus (FHL) on position and pressure distribution of the first ray by simulating muscle imbalances.* **The results indicated** that the peroneus longus was statistically the greatest contributor to the elevation of plantar pressure beneath the first metatarsal, while the EHL and FHL were primarily responsible for the angular changes resulting in the clawed hallux deformity.

*Younger et al. reviewed the adult cavovarus foot.* **They note** that the combination of the relatively strong tibialis posterior and peroneus longus muscles with the weak peroneus brevis and tibialis anterior muscles results in a hindfoot varus and forefoot valgus (pronated) position. In patients with muscle imbalance, well-planned osteotomies and tendon transfers provide more reliable outcomes than does triple arthrodesis.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1, 3, 4, and 5: As noted, plantarflexion of the first ray is a result of overpull of the PL relative to the TA which can be corrected with a PL to PB transfer. Cavus deformities result in part from a relative overpull of the PT compared to the PB.
