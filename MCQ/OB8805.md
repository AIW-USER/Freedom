## Question

Figure A shows a 12-month-old boy who presented to clinic with his concerned parents about new deformity of his legs. They state now that he is standing up they have noticed his legs tend to bow and his knees do not touch when his legs are fully extended. Which of the following is most accurate regarding his condition?

![[q8805-a-physiologic-genu-varum-1.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. He has a defect in the mineralization of bone consistent with Rickets
2. Parents should be questioned as this deformity is likely due to non-accidental trauma
3. These findings are consistent with normal development
4. To prevent further bowing he will need bracing for at least two years
5. His bowing will progress and eventually need surgery

## Correct option

**3. These findings are consistent with normal development**

## Answer explanation

The patient has a physiologic genu varum and will likely go on to improve and resolve with further growth.

Genu varum, also known as bowlegs, is a common presenting complaint in a pediatric clinic and can be characterized as physiologic or pathologic. Physiologic genu varum presents in patients under 2 years of age and is a consequence of angular relationship between the femur and the tibia. Physiologic genu varum will progress to genu valgum followed by normal adult physiologic valgus by age 7. Pathologic genu varum can present as a consequence of tibia vara or a varus deformity of the proximal tibial known as Blount's disease. This is often seen after the age of two and is evident on radiographs by medial metaphyseal fragmentation, depression, and beaking. Metaphyseal-diaphyseal angle greater than 16 is considered abnormal and has a high chance of progression. Metaphyseal-diaphyseal angle less than 10 degrees has a 95% chance of natural resolution of bowing.

*Bowen et al reviewed 173 varus limbs in children under 4 years of age who presented with bowed legs.* **They found** that all patients went on to progress with both greater than 50% contribution of bowing from the tibia and metaphyseal-diaphyseal angle >16 degrees. **They conclude** using both test together is a reliable indicator of children who may benefit from surgical correction.

Brooks and Gross review the topic of genu varum including diagnosis and treatment. They state the vast majority of genu varum will revolve with growth. They describe a normal progression from genu varum until around 18 months, followed by over-correction to genu vaglum peaking at 4 years, and lastly physiologic valgus at 5 to 6 years of age.

Figure A shows an AP image of a skeletally immature child with a normal metaphyseal-diaphyseal angle and equal beaking of the distal femur and tibia. Illustration A demonstrates bowing secondary to Rickets with physeal widening, decreased density of bone, and Looser's zones (pseudofracture on compression side of bone). Illustration B shows an example of a corner fracture which may raise suspicion for non-accidental trauma. Illustration C is an example of Blount's disease characterized by medial metaphyseal fragmentation, depression, and beaking. Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: This patient's x-rays are not consistent with Rickets (Illustration A)
Answer 2: This patient's x-rays are not consistent with non-accidental trauma (Illustration B)
Answer 4 and 5: The patient does not have Blount's disease based on a low DMA, age <2, and no metaphyseal beaking and will likely naturally resolve their bowing.
