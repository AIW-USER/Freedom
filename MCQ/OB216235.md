## Question

A 65-year-old female sustains a fall onto an outstretched hand. She notes immediate pain and swelling to her wrist. She is taken to the local emergency department where she is found to have an extra-articular distal radius fracture with a large amount of volar comminution. She is close-reduced and splinted by the orthopaedic resident on duty. Post-reduction radiographs demonstrate acceptable anatomic alignment. You have a discussion with the patient regarding possible treatment options. Based solely on the information given, which of the following fixation methods is contraindicated for this patient?

## Options

1. Definitive treatment with splint/cast immobilization
2. Intrafocal (Kapandji) pinning alone
3. Intrafocal (Kapandji) pinning supplemented with external fixation
4. Open reduction and internal fixation with a dorsal bridge plate
5. Open reduction and internal fixation with a volar locking plate

## Correct option

**2. Intrafocal (Kapandji) pinning alone**

## Answer explanation

The patient has sustained an extra-articular distal radius fracture with volar comminutions. Intrafocal (Kapandji) pinning alone is contraindicated in fractures with volar comminution, as these fractures are prone to shortening and loss of reduction, and this technique results in greater stiffness than typically afforded by pin fixation alone.

Distal radius fractures are an incredibly common injury and account for 17.5% of all fractures in adults. They occur more frequently in females and have a bimodal distribution. In younger patients, they occur due to high-energy mechanisms, and in older patients due to low-energy mechanisms such as falls from standing height. 50% have intra-articular involvement. Osteoporosis is a major risk factor, and sustaining a distal radius fracture is predictive of future fragility fractures. There exist multiple treatment options for distal radius fractures, with each having discrete indications and contraindications. Intrafocal pinning involves the insertion of 2 pins into the fracture site, where they are used to book open the fracture, prior to being passed in a proximal to distal direction.

*Trumble et al. studied outcomes following the treatment of distal radius fractures with intrafocal pinning alone or with supplemental external fixation.* *They reported on seventy-three patients and broke their population down into six groups based on age and fracture pattern.* **They postulate** that older patients with extensive comminution should not be treated with intrafocal pinning alone and that these patients require supplemental external fixation in order to achieve acceptable outcomes.

*Brady et al. reported the one-year of patients who underwent intrafocal pinning for unstable distal radius fractures.* *Out of 36 patients treated surgically for distal radius fractures at their institutions, 22 were treated with intrafocal pinning, and 20 were available for follow-up.* Fracture instability was defined as the presence of one of the following: deformity in the presence of dorsal comminution, fairly to maintain reduction following reduction under general anesthesia, or a fracture that redisplaced despite appropriate cast application. While there was a higher incidence of residual dorsal angulation, there was no increase in radial shift or shortening following intrafocal pinning. Overall, in the absence of volar comminution, intrafocal pinning was found to be an acceptable treatment strategy for patients with unstable distal radius fractures.

*Weil and Trumble performed a review of the literature surrounding the treatment of distal radius fractures with intrafocal pinning and supplemental skeletal external fixation.* **They note** that in elderly patients with distal radius fractures, intrafocal pinning alone led to high rates of post-operative shortening and subsequent poor outcomes. In summary, they do not recommend intrafocal pinning alone in the treatment of osteoporotic distal radius fractures.

The patient in the vignette has an osteoporotic distal radius fracture with volar comminution. Given the fracture pattern and patient age, intrafocal pinning alone is contraindicated in the treatment of this patient.

Incorrect Answers
Answer 1: Recent data have suggested definitive nonoperative treatment as an appropriate treatment strategy for osteoporotic distal radius fractures. A trial of nonoperative management may be indicated in this patient.
Answer 3: Intrafocal pinning with external-fixator supplementation is a viable treatment option for elderly patients with comminuted distal radius fractures.
Answers 4 and 5: Both of these treatment options are appropriate definitive treatment options for patients with osteoporotic distal radius fractures.
