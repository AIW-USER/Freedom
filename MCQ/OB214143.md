## Answer explanation

Figure D is the 3-dimensional reconstruction of a CT scan in a patient with an associated both column acetabular fracture. With a segmental posterior column fracture, anatomic reduction of this pattern cannot be judged through either a ilioinguinal or a Kocher-Langenback approach alone, making the extended iliofemoral approach a good option if only one approach can be chosen.

The extended iliofemoral approach is the only single approach that allows visualization of both columns, and is thus recommended when anatomic reduction is unable to be judged through either an ilioinguinal or Kocher-Langenbeck approach alone. It exposes the entire lateral innominate bone via posterior reflection of the abductors and reflection of the short external rotators. Additionally, it can be extended anteriorly in to the first iliac window of the ilioinguinal approach, allowing direct visualization of an anterior reduction. This approach is most commonly used for both column fractures, T-type, and transverse patterns with significant posterior comminution or involvement of the SI joint. Moreover, this is the recommended approach for associated fracture patterns that are greater than 3 weeks after injury.

Stöckle et al review the treatment of complex acetabular fractures with the extended iliofemoral approach. They report an 80% anatomic fracture reduction with <1mm displacement. However, they also report many complications including 8% loss of reduction, 13% incidence of heterotopic ossification (HO) grade 3 and 4% rate of avascular necrosis of the femoral head. They conclude that this approach may be used to achieve anatomic reduction, however, there continues to be a high rate of morbidity, which may lead to a decrease in the use of extended approaches.

*Griffin et al review the safety and efficacy of the extended iliofemoral approach in the treatment of complex acetabular fractures.* *They report on 106 patients, 60% of which were both column fractures that underwent ORIF using the extended iliofemoral approach.* The achieved anatomic reduction in 72% of patients, but had an HO rate of 30%. **They conclude** that this approach may be used for complex acetabular fractures, however, the surgeon should give strong consideration to HO prophylaxis.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Simple transverse posterior wall patterns can be fixed through a posterior approach.
Answer 2: Fixation of the crescent fracture of an LC2 incjury can be performed via an anterior approach OR a posterior approach but does not require the extended iliofemoral approach.
Answer 3: The quadrilateral plate is accessed through a modified Stoppa approach.
Answer 5: Posterior wall fractures are best treated through a posterior approach.
