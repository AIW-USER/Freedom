## Correct option

## Answer explanation

Congenital scoliosis is not associated with familial inheritance, which is exemplified in Figure C.

Congenital scoliosis is the result of abnormal vertebral development that results in coronal plane deformity. Vertebral anomalies can be the result of the failure of formation, failure of segmentation, or a combination of the two. Unlike adolescent idiopathic scoliosis (AIS), congenital scoliosis is not believed to be associated with genetic inheritance. Rather, environmental exposure and vascular disruption are believed to be the cause of congenital defects.

*Giampietro et al. performed a review and synteny analysis of genetic markers involved in the development of congenital and idiopathic scoliosis.* **They reported** 27 mapped gene mutations from a mouse genome database that are plausibly involved in the development of adolescent scoliosis.

*Chang et al. performed a retrospective study of patients under the age of 10 years undergoing hemivertebrae resection and short segment fusion for congenital scoliosis.* **They reported** a 75% curve correction without crankshaft phenomenon or vascular events. **They concluded** that early posterior hemivertebrae resection with short-segment pedicle screw fusion is safe and effective for restoring normal spinal alignment.

Figure A is an AP radiograph of the entire spine with Lenke type 1 AIS. Figure B is an AP radiograph of the entire spine with Lenke type 3 AIS. Figure C is an AP radiograph of the entire spine with multiple congenital vertebral defects and associated congenital scoliosis. Figure D is an AP radiograph of the entire spine with Lenke 4 AIS. Figure E is an AP radiograph of the entire spine with Lenke 5 AIS. Illustration A is a diagram depicting the Lenke classification for AIS.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The patient's history and physical exam and radiographs suggest Lenke type 1 AIS.
Answer 2: The patient's history and physical exam and radiographs suggest Lenke type 3 AIS.
Answer 4: Plagiocephaly is not associated with spinal deformity and is believed to be related to uterine packing issues during pregnancy. The patient's history and physical exam and radiographs suggest Lenke type 4 AIS.
Answer 5: Multiple sclerosis is not associated with spinal deformity, but can manifest with neurologic deficits. This patients radiographs are consistent with Lenke 5 AIS.
