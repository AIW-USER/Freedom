## Question

A 72-year-old male presents with right hip pain and swelling 8 years after undergoing total hip arthroplasty. His current implant has been recalled by the manufacturer. Serum cobalt ions are 14 µg/L and chromium ions are 16 µg/L. Revision arthroplasty is scheduled and a gluteus maximus transfer is included in the operative plan. What is likely present for this to be included in the operation?

## Options

1. Hip flexion contracture
2. Medial acetabular component migration
3. Persistent groin pain after injections and positive Stinchfield
4. Trendelenburg gait
5. Profound weakness with hip external rotation

## Correct option

**4. Trendelenburg gait**

## Answer explanation

A Trendelenburg gait is indicative of abductor deficiency which can occur in the setting of metallosis and can be addressed with a gluteus maximus transfer.

Metallosis is the corrosion of metal-on-metal bearing total hip implants that can lead to adverse local tissue reactions. This can lead to extensive local destruction of soft tissues including the abductors. This can complicate reconstruction efforts as patients can have sustained hip instability and lower functional outcomes if not addressed. Stability can be afforded by including a constrained implant in the reconstruction plan as well as a gluteus maximus transfer. By transferring the gluteus maximus to the greater trochanter, the muscle can be converted from a hip extensor to a hip abductor.

*Whiteside et al. reported on five hips that underwent reconstruction of the greater trochanter with gluteus maximus flap transfer and compared outcomes to five patients without reconstruction and four patients with greater trochanter excision.* **They reported** the flap reconstruction group had less pain, lower incidence of Trendelenburg sign, and less need for support.

*Wyles et al. evaluated serum and synovial markers for prosthetic joint infections in failed metal-on-metal hip arthroplasty in 39 patients (4 of which were culture positive).* **They reported** neutrophil percentage >80% was 100% sensitive and 97.1% specific, whereas synovial WBC count, serum CRP, and serum ESR had lower sensitivities and specificities. **They concluded** synovial neutrophil percentage to be a highly accurate marker for diagnosing infection in metal-on-metal hip arthroplasty.

*Yi et al. performed a retrospective study of 150 revision hips that were either metal-on-metal articulations or prostheses with trunnionosis and evaluated serum and synovial markers for the accuracy of diagnosing prosthetic joint infections.* **They reported** synovial WBC count was the best test for diagnosing prosthetic joint infections (cutoff value 4350 WBC/mcL; AUC = 98%) followed by synovial WBC differential (cutoff >85%; AUC = 90%). **The authors recommended** a more aggressive evaluation for suspected prosthetic joint infection in patients with metal-on-metal implants.

Incorrect answers
Answer 1: Gluteus maximus transfer is not used to address a preoperative hip flexion contracture.
Answer 2: Medial acetabular component migration is addressed with reconstruction cup cages.
Answer 4: A positive Stinchfield test suggests joint or iliopsoas pathology.
Answer 5: Attritional tears of the short external rotators is not treated with gluteus maximus transfer.
