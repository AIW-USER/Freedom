## Question

A 50-year-old male with knee osteoarthritis presents with chronic joint pain and stiffness that has not improved with NSAIDs or physical therapy. With regard to Orthokine Therapy which of the following is true

## Options

1. Increases platelet concentration to stimulate growth factors for tissue healing.
2. Enhances anti-inflammatory cytokines, particularly IL-1 receptor antagonist (IL-1Ra), to block inflammation.
3. Injects exogenous stem cells into the joint to regenerate cartilage and reduce pain.
4. Provides corticosteroids to suppress inflammation and slow disease progression.

## Correct option

**2. Enhances anti-inflammatory cytokines, particularly IL-1 receptor antagonist (IL-1Ra), to block inflammation.**

## Answer explanation

Ans: B
EXPLANATION: -

Orthokine therapy is a new approach involves using autologous conditioned serum (ACS), which is obtained by:
1. Incubating whole blood with pyrogen-free glass spheres to stimulate leukocytes.
2. This process increases anti-inflammatory cytokines (e.g., IL-1 receptor antagonist) and growth factors (e.g., IGF-1, PDGF, TGF-β1).
3. The resulting cell-free serum is then injected into affected tissues to promote healing and reduce inflammation.
Factor Autologous Conditioned Serum (ACS) Platelet-Rich Plasma (PRP)
Source Whole blood incubated with glass spheres to stimulate leukocytes. Whole blood processed by centrifugation to concentrate platelets.
Mechanism of Action Increases anti-inflammatory cytokines (e.g., IL-1 receptor antagonist) and growth factors. Releases platelet-derived growth factors (e.g., PDGF, TGF-β, VEGF) to promote healing.
Primary function Anti-inflammatory & regenerative (reduces pain and inflammation). Tissue repair & regeneration (stimulates healing and collagen formation).
Bioactive components IL-1 receptor antagonist (IL-1Ra) → Blocks inflammation.
- IGF-1, PDGF, TGF-β1 → Support tissue repair High concentration of platelets → Release growth factors.
- VEGF, PDGF, TGF-β → Promote tissue healing and angiogenesis

TECHNIQUE FOR ORTHOKINE GENERATION: -
Step Procedure
1. Blood Collection Draw 10–60 mL blood into a syringe with glass beads.
2. Incubation Keep at 37°C for 24 hours to stimulate cytokine release.
3. Centrifugation Spin at 3000–3500 rpm to separate serum from blood cells.
4. Serum Extraction & Storage Collect cell-free serum, store at -20°C for future use.
5. Injection Inject ACS into the joint/muscle, typically once or twice a week.

How Orthokine Mimics Hypoxia (Altitude Training-Like Effect): Orthokine therapy mimics some effects of altitude training by inducing a mild hypoxic-like response in blood cells during the incubation process. This stimulates anti-inflammatory and regenerative factors, similar to how altitude training enhances endurance performance. However, Orthokine is not aimed at endurance performance.
1️. Blood Incubation at 37°C → Simulates a mild stress environment, triggering leukocyte activation.
2.Hypoxia-Like Cellular Response → Leukocytes release anti-inflammatory cytokines & growth factors, similar to altitude-induced HIF-1α (Hypoxia-Inducible Factor-1 Alpha) activation.
3️.Increased IL-1Ra Production → Counteracts inflammation, aiding cartilage repair, joint health, and muscle recovery.
4️. Improved Healing Efficiency → Similar to altitude training's ability to improve recovery.
Therefore, Orthokines:
• May support recovery and reduce inflammation, helping athletes sustain high-intensity training at altitude.
• Mimics mild hypoxia in blood cells, but does not stimulate EPO or red blood cell production like altitude training.
• Best used for injury management and joint health, rather than endurance enhancement.
Doping regulations:

• It does not enhance performance artificially but helps reduce inflammation and aid recovery.
• Athletes can use Orthokine therapy legally in professional sports without violating anti-doping regulations.
Case-by-Case Approval:
Athletes who need Orthokine therapy might require a Therapeutic Use Exemption (TUE) from their sports federation, depending on the regulations in their sport.
