## Answer explanation

Of the choices listed, an MRI would be indicated in a patient with a "winking owl" sign as shown in figure A.

A “winking owl” sign is a radiographic finding which represents a loss of the radiographic appearance of a pedicle. This finding indicates that a bony process is developing within that vertebral body. This radiographic abnormality should be further evaluated with use of an MRI. A common cause of this radiographic finding includes spinal aneurysmal bone cysts (ABCs), which often requires surgical intervention.

*Lopez-Gutierrez et al. reviewed benign tumors in the spine.* **They reported** that these benign tumors of the spine may cause local pain, radicular symptoms, neurologic compromise, spinal instability, and deformity. **They concluded** that appropriate treatment may be observational (ie. eosinophilic granuloma), ablative (ie. osteoid osteoma, neurofibroma, hemangioma), or surgical depending on the level of pain, instability, neurologic compromise, and natural history of the lesion.

*Zanonos et al. reviewed spinal ABCs in pediatric patients.* **They reported** that ABCs were located throughout the spine, with an equal number in the thoracic and lumbar spine, and rarely in the cervical spine. Furthermore, **they noted** that the majority of patients presented with back pain, but neurological deficits and spinal deformity were also common. **They concluded** that gross-total excision in conjunction with spinal stabilization, as needed, usually provides cure of the ABC and excellent long-term spinal alignment.

Figure A is a radiograph of the spine demonstrating a "winking owl" sign." Figure B is a radiograph of the spine demonstrating a right scoliotic curve measuring 35 degrees. Figure C is a radiograph of the spine demonstrating a right scoliotic curve in a skeletally mature adult. Figure D is a lateral radiograph of the spine demonstrating thoracic kyphosis suggestive of Scheuermann's kyphosis. Figure E is a radiograph of the spine demonstrating a right-sided thoracic curve. Illustration A is a radiograph of the spine demonstrating a "winking owl" sign.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 2,3&5: These figures demonstrate right-sided scoliotic curves. An MRI is not indicated from these radiographs alone
Answer 4: Figure D is a lateral radiograph of the spine demonstrating thoracic kyphosis suggestive of Scheuermann's kyphosis. An MRI is controversial as some advocate its use prior to surgery to look for associated disc herniation, epidural cyst, spinal cord abnormalities, and spinal stenosis. However, this radiograph alone does not prompt a MRI
