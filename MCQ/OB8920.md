## Question

A 20-year-old running back referred to you by his primary care physician, presenting with a one-week history of right great toe pain following a tackle. He never had any issues with this foot before but has significant pain doing anything more than walking. Exam shows a swollen and painful big toe, further exam was equivocal. ## Options

1. Bone scan
2. Taping the first MTP, beginning physical therapy and wearing a rigid orthotic
3. First MTP Arthrodesis
4. Sesamoidectomy
5. Lateral closing wedge osteotomy

## Correct option

**2. Taping the first MTP, beginning physical therapy and wearing a rigid orthotic**

## Answer explanation

The patient above has a history and workup consistent with a low-mid grade turf toe injury. Initial treatment should include conservative modalities.

Turf toe was first described in 1976 among collegiate football players. It is most commonly seeing football players on artificial surfaces, but is seen in other sports in which an axial load is applied to a plantarflexed hindfoot and dorsiflexed metatarsophalangeal joint. The phalangeal end of the plantar capsuloligamentous complex can be attenuated or completely torn, causing significant pain and swelling. Motions involving hyper-extension or push off will stress the joint and reproduce pain. On exam excess laxity on hyperextension stress may be seen but exam may be difficult due to pain. Radiographs may show displacement of the sesamoids, and MRI yields the most information on the plantar structures and cartilage surface.

*Anderson et al reviewed management of sports-related foot and ankle injuries, including turf toe injuries.* Grade 1 and 2 injuries were managed though taping the MTP and rehabilitation, with Grade 2 frequently requiring time off from sport due to pain. Orthotics limiting extension were also used for Grade 2 injuries. Grade 3 injuries were also able to be treated non surgically through splinting in plantarflexion, however they cite high return to sport rate and performance outcomes in elite-level athletes undergoing direct repair as evidence for more aggressive treatment.

Incorrect Answers
Answer 1 - No further imaging is required since the MRI was performed and negative.
Answers 3 and 5 - These would be surgical options for correcting hallux varus.
Answer 4 - Sesamoidectomy would be an option in the case of sesamoid fracture.
