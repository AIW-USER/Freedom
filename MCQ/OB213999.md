## Question

A 38-year-old male presents to your office for evaluation of right shoulder pain after he was involved in an ATV accident. On exam, the patient is unable to hold his right palm against the contralateral shoulder as you pull the arm anteriorly as shown in Figure A. Standard radiographic views of the shoulder are obtained. Which of the following is most likely to be seen on these radiographs?

![[q213999-a-bearhug.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Great tuberosity fracture
2. Lesser tuberosity fracture
3. Superior migration of the humeral head
4. Glenohumeral dislocation
5. Acromioclavicular separation

## Correct option

**2. Lesser tuberosity fracture**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a positive bear hug test, which is an isolation test for the subscapularis. Thus, a lesser tuberosity avulsion is most likely.

The lesser tuberosity is the insertion of the subscapularis. An avulsion fracture would present as weakness with internal rotation, as tested by the bear hug test, in which the patient places the ipsilateral palm on the opposite deltoid and tries to resist the examiner pulling it away anteriorly. This test is important because attempting to test internal rotation with the arm at the side can give misleading results due to contributions from the pectoralis major and latissimus dorsi. Other tests for the subscapularis include the internal rotation lag sign, increased passive external rotation, lift-off test and belly press test. Specifically, the lift-off test isolates the inferior portion of the subscapularis, whereas the belly press test is best for its superior border.

*Hippensteel et al review the physical exam tests of the cervical spine, scapula, and rotator cuff.* **They report** that the bear hug test should be considered positive when the patient is unable to hold the palm against the shoulder or exhibits greater than 20% weakness compared to the contralateral extremity. **They conclude** that a thorough and focused physical exam is crucial in determining a differential diagnosis of pathology in and around the shoulder.

*Jain et al review the clinical examination of patients with rotator cuff pathology and introduce their algorithm.* **They report** that a detailed physical exam including range of motion using a goniometer, strength testing using a dynamometer, and select special tests are all that is needed to diagnose most rotator cuff tears. **They conclude** that the bear hug test has a sensitivity and specificity of 60 and 90%, respectively, and that other tests such as the lift-off test, belly press test, and belly-off sign may also be helpful in diagnosing subscapularis pathology.

Figure A is a clinical picture of the bear hug test being performed as described in the question stem.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The supraspinatus inserts on to the greater tuberosity, these patients are more likely to present with a positive drop arm test.
Answer 3: Superior migration of the humeral head is seen with cuff tear arthropathy, not with acute subscapularis pathology.
Answer 4: In the setting of a glenohumeral dislocation the patient would not likely be able to get into the position to perform the bear hug test.
Answer 5: The bear hug test described is not used in the diagnosis of AC joint injuries.
