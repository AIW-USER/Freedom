## Question

A 20-year-old professional baseball pitcher complains of medial-sided right elbow pain which has been worsening over the last several weeks. He has failed several weeks of treatment consisting of rest and forearm strengthening. A MRI arthrogram is performed and shows a rupture of the medial ulnar collateral ligament (MUCL) from its ulnar insertion. The decision is made to proceed with MUCL reconstruction. Which of the following phases of throwing places the highest valgus load on the elbow?

## Options

1. Wind-up
2. Early cocking
3. Early acceleration
4. Decleration
5. Follow-through

## Correct option

**3. Early acceleration**

## Answer explanation

The valgus load is highest on the MUCL during the transition from the late cocking to early acceleration phase of throwing.

MUCL injuries are characterized by attenuation or rupture of the ulnar collateral ligament of the elbow leading to valgus instability in overhead throwing athletes. Diagnosis is usually made by a combination of physical exam and MRI studies. Treatment for most individuals is rest and physical therapy. Surgery is reserved for high-level overhead athletes such as baseball pitchers. MUCL reconstruction often utilizes either an ipsilateral palmaris longus, hamstring autograft, or allograft tendon.

*Bruce et al. reviewed repetitive valgus forces on the throwing elbow.* **They reported** that this stress can cause structural damage and injury to the ulnar collateral ligament. **They concluded** that failing nonsurgical management, MUCL reconstruction is a viable option to return the throwing athlete to competition.

*Erickson et al. reviewed MUCL injuries in the overhead athlete.* **They reported** that MUCL reconstruction is the current gold standard of treatment for these injuries in MLB pitchers who wish to return to sport at a high level and who have failed a course of non-operative treatment. **They concluded** that results following MUCL reconstruction in MLB pitchers have been encouraging, with multiple return to sport rates now cited at greater than 80%.

*Erickson et al. reviewed patient demographics, surgical techniques, and outcomes following MUCL reconstruction.* **They reported** that 94% of patients were able to return to sport and had good/excellent outcomes scores. **They concluded** that MUCL reconstruction resulted in good outcomes and a high return to sports rate.

*Liu et al. reviewed primary and revision MUCL reconstruction in MLB pitchers.* **They reported** that the rate of revision MUCL reconstruction surgery is substantially higher than previously reported. **They concluded** that for MLB pitchers, return to play after a revision surgery is much lower than after primary reconstruction, and the overall durability of MLB pitchers after revision MUCL reconstruction decreases significantly compared with controls matched for age and matched controls.
