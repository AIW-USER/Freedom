Chapter: Spine MCQs — Scoliosis Kyphosis

## Question

Congenital kyphosis commonly occurs at which level

## Options

1. T1-3
2. T4-6
3. T7-9
4. T11-12

## Correct option

**3. T7-9**

## Answer explanation

Ans: (C)

Congenital kyphosis is characterized by severe angular deformity with a prominent gibbus at the apex of the curve; which usually tends to be around T7-9. It’s usually seen in association with mucopolysaccharidoses or skeletal dysplasia’s like spondyloepiphyseal dysplasia congenita, spondylometaphyseal dysplasia, Morquio’s disease, pseudo achondroplasia, etc.

ETIOLOGY

Congenital kyphosis is due to either failure of formation defect or failure of segmentation defect of vertebrae. Failure of formation defect may present at birth as kyphotic deformity and worsen with growth. Failure of segmentation defect is slow to grow and usually become apparent when the child starts walking.

 - defect of formation:
 - failure of formation of the anterior elements produces the worst deformity;
 - prognosis in this category is considerably worse;
 - natural history involves relentless progression of deformity resulting in paraplegia

 - severe deformities may even lead to impairment of pulmonary function and compression of abdominal viscera

 - defect of segmentation:
 - slightly better prognosis;
 - produces a more rounded kyphotic shape;
 - deformity progresses more slowly and paraplegia is uncommon;

TREATMENT

Braces have no role in congenital kyphosis.

For progressive deformity fusion of deformed vertebrae may be required.

Current recommended treatment includes posterior arthrodesis for deformities of less than 50° to 60°, and anterior release or decompression, anterior fusion, and posterior instrumented arthrodesis for large deformities and cord compression.

Treatment options include following: -

- Posterior Fusion: In children < 5 years with curves < 55 degree, only a situ posterior arthrodesis is required since some spontaneous correction of kyphosis will occur with continued growth. A bivalve total-contact orthosis is used to support trunk until fusion is solid.

- Anterior & Posterior Fusion: Combined anterior & posterior fusion is indicated for children > 5 years, and curves > 55 degree; or those with a paralytic deformity.
 - reconstruction begins with anterior release (decompression of cord anteriorly) f/b interbody fusion by placement of a strut graft in between the vertebral bodies.
 - as a staged procedure, posterior arthrodesis (fusion) f/b spinal fixation is added

 - correction of kyphotic deformity carries a high risk of paraplegia, and hence intra operative cord monitoring if available is highly recommended.
