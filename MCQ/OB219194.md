## Question

A 34-year-old male is seen in your clinic for follow-up. You have been taking care of the patient since he sustained a Type IIIB open distal tibia fracture following a high-speed motorcycle accident. The patient is set to undergo definitive fixation after undergoing the first stage of an induced membrane technique. As part of the informed consent process, you discuss the autologous bone graft options available for utilization with the patient. Which of the following statements is true regarding autologous bone grafting?

## Options

1. Autograft harvested utilizing the reamer-irrigator-aspirator (RIA) requires more surgical time than harvesting autologous iliac crest bone grafting (ICBG)
2. ICBG is associated with fewer immediate postoperative complications when compared to utilization of the RIA technique
3. A greater volume of autograft is harvested with the RIA technique when compared to autologous ICBG
4. Union rates are higher with autograft obtained from the iliac crest when compared to the RIA technique
5. Union rates are higher with autograft obtained with the RIA technique when compared to the iliac crest

## Correct option

**3. A greater volume of autograft is harvested with the RIA technique when compared to autologous ICBG**

## Answer explanation

Autologous bone grafting is the gold standard for filling bony defects secondary to trauma or nonunion. Historically obtained from the iliac crest (ICBG), newer technology such as the reamer-irrigator-aspirator (RIA) has allowed for the harvest of autograft from the medullary canal of long bones. Use of the RIA technique is associated with equal union rates compared to autologous ICBG and confers additional benefits such as shorter harvest time, higher volume of autograft, and fewer immediate postoperative complications (Answer 3).

A bone graft is a material with either osteoconductive (the material acts as a structural framework for bone growth), osteoinductive (the material contains factors that stimulate bone growth and the induction of stem cells down a bone-forming lineage), and/or osteogenic (the material directly provides cells that will produce bone including primitive mesenchymal stem cells, osteoblasts, and osteocytes) properties. Almost 1 million bone grafting procedures are performed in the US each year, with a growth of almost 13% per year. Bone grafts are used to assist in the healing of fractures, delayed unions or nonunions, to aid in arthrodesis or spinal fusion procedures, and/or to replace bony defects left secondary to trauma or tumor.

Historically, autologous bone grafts have been harvested from either the anterior or posterior iliac crest. Autologous bone graft (fresh autograft and/or bone marrow aspirate) is favorable as it is the only graft material that contains live mesenchymal precursor cells. Harvest of bone graft from the iliac crest is not benign, and this procedure carries with it a complication rate that varies from 2% to 36%. Recently, utilization of a reamer-irrigator-aspirator (RIA) has gained support (Illustrations A and B). The RIA technique, however, is associated with greater blood loss when compared to ICBG.

*Dawson et al. performed a multicenter randomized controlled trial comparing patient outcomes following RIA-harvested bone grafting with ICBG.* *Their study randomized 133 patients to one of two treatment arms.* Outcomes collected included the amount of graft harvested, time for harvest, surgical costs, clinical and radiographic union rates, and SMFA and VAS-pain scores. Overall, 113 patients (84.9%) were followed until union. **The authors found** that when compared to ICBG, autograft obtained utilizing the RIA technique achieved similar union rates, with less donor site pain, while providing a greater volume of graft and a shorter harvest time. Furthermore, despite a greater up-front cost, the shorter harvest time and large volume obtained demonstrated a cost benefit to RIA over ICBG.

*Toosi et al. performed a comparative study to characterize the mesenchymal stem cells derived from ICBG, RIA, and adipose tissue.* *The authors utilized multiple cellular biology methods to quantify and qualify the cells harvested.* Overall, **the authors found** that the RIA-obtained cells underwent osteogenesis and chondrogenesis at a faster rate than ICBG and adipose tissue, while also having a stronger differentiation towards osteoblast and chondrocytes compared to other sources. **The authors concluded** that on a microbiological level, the mesenchymal cells derived utilizing the RIA technique may be a more suitable source of graft when compared to other sources.

*Le Baron et al. performed a comparative study investigating RIA and ICBG for patients with aseptic nonunion of the tibia or femur up to 2cm in length.* *The RIA group contained 30 patients, and the ICBG group contained 29 patients.* Overall, union rates were similar between groups, and high, while patients who underwent grafting via RIA achieved union more quickly when compared to ICBG (8.63 vs. 10.08 months, respectively, p = 0.006). In addition, ICBG was associated with a longer operative time, a higher postoperative analgesic demand, and a higher immediate postoperative complication rate.

Incorrect Answers
Answer 1: The RIA technique requires less surgical time compared to ICBG
Answer 2: ICBG is associated with more frequent immediate postoperative complications.
Answers 4 and 5: Union rates following ICBG and RIA are comparable, and high (approximately 90%).
