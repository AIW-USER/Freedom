## Question

A 14-year-old high school football wide receiver gets tackled while going up for a catch. He completes the catch but notices shoulder pain and arm weakness upon returning to the sideline. The athletic trainer diagnoses him with a "Stinger". Which of the following is consistent with this diagnosis?

## Options

1. Examination will reveal bilateral upper extremity involvement
2. Most common involved nerve root predominately innervates the triceps
3. Physical examination may show a positive Spurling's sign
4. Neck pain with cervical spine extension
5. Symptoms generally progress for 20-25 minutes before slowly resolving

## Correct option

**3. Physical examination may show a positive Spurling's sign**

## Answer explanation

The patient has been diagnosed with a stinger. Patients with stingers may have a positive Spurling's sign on clinical exam.

A stinger is a transient brachial plexus neuropraxia that is common in collision sports such as football and is generally caused by a traction injury (downward displacement of arm and bending of the neck away from the side of injury), compression (lateral head turning toward the affected side), or a direct blow to Erb's point (just superior to clavicle). The most common symptoms are unilateral paresthesias and weakness in the C5/C6 distribution that quickly resolves with time (in 1-2 minutes). While patients should have a full painless range of motion of the cervical spine, patients do occasionally have a positive Spurling test at the time of injury. Players may return to play once they have complete resolution of symptoms with normal strength and range of motion.

*Meyer et al. evaluated the relationship of cervical spinal stenosis with the occurrence of stingers in collegiate football players.* *The authors obtained preparticipation cervical spine radiographs of 266 players of which 40 players were later diagnosed with stingers.* **They noted** that the Torg ratio was less than 0.8 at 1 or more levels in 47.5% of the stinger group, and 25.1% of the non-stinger group. **They noted** that players with a Torg ratio less than 0.8 had 3 times the risk of incurring stingers. **The authors conclude** that cervical spinal stenosis increases the risk for having stingers with complicated clinical courses.

*Schroeder and Vaccaro reviewed cervical spine injuries in athletes.* **They noted** that although cervical spine injuries are most common in athletes who participate in contact and collision sports, such as American football and rugby, they also have been reported in athletes who participate in non-contact sports, such as baseball, gymnastics, and diving. **They noted** that some athletes have chronic conditions, such as congenital stenosis, that increase their risk for a serious cervical spine injury after even minor trauma. **The authors recommend** that physicians who cover athletic events must have a thorough knowledge of cervical spine injures and the most appropriate ways in which they should be managed.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Stingers are associated with UNILATERAL upper extremity involvement. Bilateral upper extremity involvement is concerning for a cervical spine injury and requires disqualification from play until further imaging studies are obtained.
Answer 2: The most commonly involved nerve root is C5, which predominately affects the deltoid and biceps. The C6 nerve root may also be affected and this predominately affects the wrist extensors. The triceps is predominately affected by a C7 palsy.
Answer 4: Cervical spine range of motion should not exacerbate symptoms of a stinger.
Answer 5: Symptoms of a stinger generally don't progress, but resolve very quickly after onset (1-2 minutes).
