## Question

Figure A is the radiograph of a 53-year-old female with rheumatoid arthritis who presents with left forefoot pain of several years duration. She has failed an extensive course of conservative treatment and is requesting surgical intervention. In addition to 1st metatarsophalangeal joint fusion, which additional procedure is indicated in this patient?

![[q215742-a-fa-foot-pre.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Bunionectomy
2. Forefoot amputation
3. Lesser MTP joint fusions
4. Lesser metatarsal head resections
5. Lesser MTP joint arthroplasties

## Correct option

**4. Lesser metatarsal head resections**

## Answer explanation

This patient has radiographic evidence of joint destruction of the foot commonly seen in rheumatoid arthritis. Surgical intervention with a 1st metatarsophalangeal (MTP) joint fusion and lesser metatarsal head resections is recommended in rheumatoid arthritis.

Rheumatoid arthritis is a chronic systemic autoimmune disease caused by IgM cell-mediated immune response against soft tissues, cartilage, and bone. The earliest manifestation of rheumatoid arthritis of the forefoot is synovitis of the MTP joints with eventual hyperextension deformity of the MTP joints including distal migration of the forefoot pad, painful plantar callosities and skin ulcerations over bony prominences. Arthrodesis of the 1st MTP joint and lesser MTP joint resections is a reasonable treatment option for these patients.

*Coughlin et al. retrospectively reviewed reconstruction of the rheumatoid forefoot with arthrodesis of the MTP joint of the great toe, resection arthroplasty of the metatarsal heads of the lesser toes, and open repair of hammer-toe deformity (arthrodesis of the proximal interphalangeal joint) of the lesser toes.* **They report** that all first MTP joints had successfully fused at an average of seventy-four months with the result of the procedure being excellent, good, fair or poor in 24, 22, 2, 0 patients, respectively. **They concluded** that arthrodesis of the first MTP joint, resection arthroplasty of the lesser metatarsal heads, and repair of fixed hammer-toe deformities with intramedullary Kirschner wire fixation resulted in a stable repair with a high percentage of successful results at an average of six years after the procedures.

*Mann et al. reviewed the surgical correction of severe rheumatoid forefoot deformities with resection arthroplasties of the lesser MTP joints and arthrodesis of the first MTP joint.* **They reported** that this resulted in a significant long-term improvement with respect to shoe wear, pain, and the ability to stand and walk in 95% of the patients. Moreover, 90% of patients had a good or excellent functional result. **They concluded** that modifications of the procedure with maintenance of the proximal phalangeal bases and K-wire fixation of the MTP joint arthroplasty and interphalangeal joints resulted in an improved cosmetic result and simplified postoperative management with an equal functional result and no increase in recurrence of deformity or complications.

Figure A is the radiograph of the foot that illustrates the common findings of a rheumatoid foot. Illustration A shows a first MTP joint fusion, resection of the 2nd through 5th MT heads and hammertoe correction with PIP joint resection and K wire fixation. Illustration B shows the preoperative and postoperative anteroposterior radiographs of hallux valgus deformity with lesser MTP joint erosions treated by 1st MTP joint fusion and lesser metatarsal head resections

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: A bunionectomy would not adequately address the arthritic state of the 1st MTPJ or the lesser toes
Answer 2: Forefoot amputation would be a very aggressive treatment and 1st MTP joint fusion and lesser metatarsal head resections have shown good results in RA patients
Answer 3: Lesser MTP joint fusion should be avoided due to the amount of motion that occurs through the lateral foot during ambulation
Answer 5: In RA patients, lesser metatarsal head resections (rather than arthroplasties) is recommended
