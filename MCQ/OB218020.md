## Question

A healthy 66-year-old male presents to the clinic with a painful right hip that has worsened over the past two months. He underwent a total hip arthroplasty 5 years prior with the most recent imaging shown in Figure A. He underwent a recent CT scan showing a small fluid collection tracking along the psoas tendon. A review of his recent labs shows:

Serum Cobalt: 2.4 µg/L (normal: 0.7-3.4 µg/L)
Serum Chromium: 1.6 µg/L (normal: 0.5-2.5 µg/L)
Serum C-reactive protein: 1.7 mg/dL (normal: 0.3 to 1.0 mg/dL)
Serum Erythrocyte Sedimentation Rate: 31 mm/hr (normal 0 to 22 mm/hr)

Which of the following is the most appropriate next step in management?

![[q218020-a-figure-a.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Continued observation and physical therapy
2. Metal artifact reduction sequence (MARS) MRI
3. Perform a hip aspiration
4. Femoral head and liner exchange to a ceramic on polyethylene articulation
5. Two-staged revision with six weeks of IV antibiotics

## Correct option

**3. Perform a hip aspiration**

## Answer explanation

This patient presents with elevated inflammatory markers and CT findings concerning for a psoas abscess five years after undergoing a primary hip replacement with a metal-on-metal (MOM) implant. These findings are concerning for periprosthetic joint infection and the patient should undergo a hip aspiration.

MOM hips are notorious for complications secondary to an adverse reaction to metal debris shed from the implant. This classically involves an aseptic lymphocyte-dominant vasculitis-associated lesion (ALVAL; also referred to as pseudotumor). These patients often present with progressive pain and display elevated serum cobalt and chromium. Importantly, these patients may initially be overlooked for underlying infection; periprosthetic joint infections should remain at the top of the differential for the chronic painful hip replacement despite the underlying implant articulation metallurgy. This patient has normal serum cobalt and chromium levels while having elevated inflammatory markers. These findings are concerning for underlying periprosthetic joint infection and the patient should undergo a hip aspiration with synovial fluid analysis to confirm the underlying diagnosis.

*Bozic and colleagues utilized the Medicare database to perform a retrospective review of complications associated with the three major types of bearing surfaces in total hip arthroplasty: (1) metal on polyethylene (2) ceramic on ceramic and (3) metal on metal.* The risk of short term-complications was nonsignificant between all implants with the exception that metal-on-metal implants imparted a significantly higher risk for developing a periprosthetic joint infection compared to ceramic on ceramic hips. **They concluded** that metal on metal hips have questionable value in the elderly population given their higher cost and higher risk for periprosthetic infection compared to other bearing surfaces.

*Parvizi and colleagues released their landmark paper defining the current, evidence-based, and validated criteria for diagnosing a periprosthetic joint infection.* *They provide major and minor criteria as well as other intraoperative tests to assist in establishing a diagnosis.* These can be appreciated in Illustration A.

Ting and Della Valle provided an algorithm-based approach for establishing the diagnosis of periprosthetic joint infection. They review all available diagnostic modalities and their clinical utility, emphasizing that arthrocentesis most often serves as the best single diagnostic tool to rule out a periprosthetic joint infection.

Figure A shows a right total hip replacement utilizing a metal-on-metal hip prosthesis. Illustration A is taken directly from the Parvizi article showing the most updated scoring-based definition for periprosthetic joint infection.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Continued observation would not be appropriate as this patient is endorsing continued pain with findings concerning for underlying periprosthetic joint infection.
Answer 2: Metal artifact reduction sequence (MARS) MRI would be considered the next best step in management if there was a concern for an adverse reaction to metal debris. These patients have elevated serum cobalt and chromium levels. If the cobalt:chromium levels were elevated at a 1:1 ratio, it would be concerning for metal-on-metal debris. If it were a 2:1 ratio, it would be concerning for trunnionosis.
Answer 3: Femoral head and liner exchange to ceramic on polyethylene articulation would be an appropriate treatment for patients with an adverse reaction to metal debris. The revised implant articulation helps to minimize the chance of recurrence by removing the metal-metal articulation and replacing it with ceramic on polyethylene.
Answer 5: Performing a two-staged revision with 6 weeks of IV antibiotics would be considered an appropriate treatment for the diagnosis of periprosthetic joint infection. However, it is important to first establish the diagnosis before undergoing treatment.
