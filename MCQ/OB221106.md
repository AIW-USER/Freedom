## Question

This is an ASES Self Assessment Exam question.

 An orthopaedic resident is positioning a patient for total shoulder arthroplasty when the attending arrives. The resident notes that the attending is acting oddly and smells of alcohol. What is the most appropriate next step for the resident to take?

## Options

1. Call the medical board to file a formal complaint
2. Offer to perform the entire case in lieu of the attending
3. Discuss the situation with the attending at the conclusion of the procedure
4. Notify the department chairman
5. Refuse to participate in the case

## Correct option

**4. Notify the department chairman**

## Answer explanation

The presentation of the attending is concerning for an impaired physician. Substance abuse represents the leading cause of medical board sanctions in the US. As rates of physician burnout and depression increase, so has the rate of alcohol dependence amongst physicians, as the two are often related. Fortunately, with appropriate treatment, physicians have a far higher success rate for recovery after instituting appropriate substance abuse rehabilitation compared to the general population.

The most appropriate step is to escalate the situation along the chain of command. The department chairman has ultimate authority and responsibility for the physician, so they should be involved prior to beginning the case (Option D). Because of the seriousness of the situation, the case should not be allowed to proceed with the attending surgeon in a compromised state (Options C and E). Likewise, the patient should not be provided with a lower level of care (Option B). While the medical board may ultimately be involved, it would be most appropriate for this step to be taken by the surgeon’s superior, and likely after some degree of investigation has taken place (Option A).
