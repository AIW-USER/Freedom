## Question

A 14-year-old male presents to your office with complaints of anterior and lateral ankle pain after a twisting injury while playing basketball several days prior. He is painful primarily over the anterolateral ankle on examination, but his ankle is stable to drawer testing. He denies any prior history of ankle pain. Radiographs are seen in Figure A. Which of the following is true of the lesion seen in the radiographs?

![[q218815-a-non-ossifying-fibroma.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Associated with trauma
2. Short leg walking cast is first line of treatment
3. Risk of pathologic fracture is high
4. Lesion typically ossifies spontaneously
5. Risk for malignant transformation is 5-10%

## Correct option

**4. Lesion typically ossifies spontaneously**

## Answer explanation

The incidental lesion shown is a nonossifying fibroma (NOF), which will typically ossify spontaneously over time.

Nonossifying fibromas are benign fibrogenic lesions that are thought to be caused by disorganized bony ossification. They most commonly occur in younger males, ages 5-15. They are typically asymptomatic and most often are found incidentally at the time of a separate trauma. Most can be observed with serial radiographs as they will usually ossify and resolve spontaneously. If the lesion fills >50% of the bony diameter, curettage and bone grafting may be indicated to mitigate the risk of pathologic fracture. If pathologic fracture does occur, most are amenable to casting.

*Yanagawa et al. published on the natural history of disappearing bone tumors and tumor-like conditions.* These included exostoses, eosinophilic granuloma, fibrous dysplasia, NOF, osteoid osteoma, and bone islands. They discuss the mechanisms of lesion regression and note that most of these lesions start to regress within a matter of months to years.

*Miller et al. reviewed malignant and benign bone tumors.* **They note** that there are classic findings of benign and malignant lesions on imaging, but they are not always pathognomonic. For this reason, it is important for radiologists and surgeons to be able to identify which lesions may require biopsy/intervention or observation alone.

Figure A is an AP ankle radiograph demonstrating a NOF which is not expanding the cortex.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: NOFs are usually incidental findings and are not typically associated with any particular traumatic event.
Answer 2: Short-leg walking casts are only indicated in cases of pathologic fracture; otherwise, NOFs can be observed.
Answer 3: The risk of pathologic fracture does increase with >50% of the cortical diameter involvement and curettage, and bone grafting can be considered in these patients.
Answer 5: NOFs are not associated with a risk of malignant transformation. However, multiple hereditary exostoses (MHE) are associated with a 5-10% risk of malignant transformation.
