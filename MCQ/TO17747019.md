## Question

Patients complain of tingling and numbness in the ring and little finger. There is no motor weakness. MR angiography shows ulnar artery aneurysm. True statement is ( peripheral nerve injuries- compression neuropathy- Guyon’s canal/ulnar tunnel syndrome)

## Options

1. Ulnar nerve compression in zone 3
2. Electrophysiological studies will be normal
3. Positive Jeane sign
4. Cervical radiculopathy is the diagnosis

## Correct option

**1. Ulnar nerve compression in zone 3**

## Answer explanation

Guyon’s canal /ulnar tunnel syndrome is a compressive neuropathy of the ulnar nerve at the level of the waist (Guyon’s canal), most commonly due to a ganglion cyst. It is common in cyclists and also known as handlebar palsy.

Causes of compression: most common cause is a ganglion. Other causes are lipoma, repetitive trauma, ulnar artery thrombosis or aneurysm, fracture/ dislocation of the hand (hamate fracture or non-union, pisiform dislocation), and RA. Cause cannot be localized in a few cases (idiopathic).

Ulnar nerve divides into superficial sensory and deep motor branches in the guyon’s canal which begins at the proximal extent of the transverse carpal ligament and ends at the aponeurotic arch of the hypothenar muscles. Symptoms depend on the site of compression. Compression proximal to bifurcation (zone 1) gives both motor and sensory symptoms. Zone 2 surrounds a deep motor branch and compression here gives only motor symptoms. Compression in zone 3 gives only sensory symptoms. Ulnar artery thrombosis,an aneurysm, is often associated with compression in zone 3. Whereas ganglion and hamate fracture cause compression in zone 1 and 2.

Clinical features: Pain and paresthesias in ulnar 1-1/2 digits. Clawing of ulnar two fingers, weakness of finger abduction/adduction and thumb adduction (weak pinch). Should the ulnar artery be occluded for several millimetres, Raynaud syndrome may be produced in the ulnar three digits because the sympathetic nerve fibers to these digits pass along the ulnar artery

Froment sign and book test: The patient is asked to hold a book by the side of thumb and medial border of palm by just adducting the thumb. When this muscle is paralyzed, the patient holds the book by flexing the thumb at the interphalangeal joint, due to action of FPL (which is supplied by the median nerve). The test is called a book test and the sign is called Froment’s sign.

Jeane’s sign : thumb IP joint flexion is accompanied by MCP joint hyperextension.

Wartenberg sign : abduction posturing of the little finger

NCS and EMG: help in diagnosis. These studies show conduction velocity <50 m/sec across elbow and low amplitudes of sensory nerve action potentials and compound muscle action potentials

Treatment consists of exploration of the ulnar nerve at the wrist and removal of any cause of compression. Segmental resection of the occluded section and replacement with a vein graft is the preferred procedure when it is feasible

Answer: 1
