## Question

Figures 81a and 81b show the radiograph and MRI scan of a 19-year-old patient who has a painful thigh. Examination reveals significant thigh swelling and tenderness. A biopsy specimen is shown in Figure 81c. Which of the following genetic abnormalities is most commonly observed in this condition?

## Options

1. p53 mutation
2. t(X;18) translocation
3. EWS/FLI1 gene rearrangement
4. Retinoblastoma gene mutation
5. HER-2 gene amplification

## Correct option

**3. EWS/FLI1 gene rearrangement**

## Answer explanation

The microscopic appearance of Ewing's sarcoma is a small round blue cell tumor with no significant stromal tissue. Radiographs commonly demonstrate a diaphyseal lesion with onion skinning periosteal reaction or cortical thickening. MRI commonly demonstrates a large soft-tissue component. In most cases, Ewing's sarcoma is found in patients between 5 and 25 years of age. Cytogenetic studies identify a chromosomal abnormality involving a reciprocal translocation between chromosomes 11 and 22 in up to 90% of cases, resulting in a fusion protein product called EWS/FLI1. p53 is associated with Li Fraumeni syndrome and the development of various cancers. t(x;18) is associated with synovial sarcoma. Retinoblastoma gene mutation is most commonly associated with osteosarcoma. HER-2 gene amplification is most commonly seen in breast cancer and observed in osteosarcoma.
