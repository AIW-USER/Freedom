## Question

A healthy 45-year-old lady sustains a high grade plantar plate rupture of her second metatarsophalangeal joint. What progressive foot deformity is she at greatest risk of developing with this injury?

## Options

1. Hallux varus
2. Hallux valgus
3. Second toe crossover
4. Freiberg's disease
5. Second mallet toe

## Correct option

**3. Second toe crossover**

## Answer explanation

The most likely foot deformity to occur with this injury is second toe crossover.

Second metatarsophalangeal joint instability (i.e., crossover toe) is diagnosed when the second toe crosses either under or over the hallux. This deformity occurs with failure of the plantar plate and the collateral ligaments, which stabilize the 2nd MTP joint. The lateral collateral ligament likely fails first, leading to medial deviation of the second toe. The plantar plate usually fails second, causing the proximal phalanx to displace dorsally with weight-bearing.

*Shirzad et al. reviewed lesser toe deformities.* **They state** that chronic inflammation or trauma is the likely etiology for failure of stabilizing structures of the 2nd MTP joint. Patients often present with pain and inflammation of the 2nd MTP joint. The sensation of a marble in the ball of their foot is a common feature of this deformity, due to swelling of the capsule and prominent metatarsal head.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Hallux varus deformity is not associated with a plantar plate rupture of the second metatarsophalangeal joint.
Answer 2: Hallux valgus may contribute to the etiology of second toe crossover or occur secondarily to second toe crossover. It does not result directly from plantar plate rupture of the second metatarsophalangeal joint.
Answer 4: Freiberg's disease is thought to be related to a disruption in the blood supply due to microtrauma and stress overloading that leads to eventual collapse of 2nd MT head.
Answer 5: Second mallet toe is associated with flexion and lateral deviation of the DIP joint. It is thought to occur because of contracture (or spasm) of flexor digitorum longus.
