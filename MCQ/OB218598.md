## Question

While performing humanitarian aid in the Marshall Islands, a 6-year-old patient presents to your clinic for evaluation due to her hips being unlevel. Her parents bring X-rays that were obtained at the local hospital recently (Figure A). Per her parents, she's always stood with unlevel hips and for the past two years, it has been causing her back, hip, and knee pain. Which physical exam finding can help you identify an associated condition that can result in knee subluxation with femoral lengthening?

![[q218598-a-pffd-l.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Dial Test
2. Lachman Test
3. Galeazzi Test
4. Thessaly Test
5. Varus Stress Test

## Correct option

**2. Lachman Test**

## Answer explanation

Proximal femoral focal deficiency is a disorder characterized by several conditions, including the absence of the ACL, which is tested for with the Lachman Test.

Proximal femoral focal deficiency (PFFD) is a congenital disorder characterized by a short femur, absent femur, absent hip, or pseudoarthrosis of the femoral neck. It is predominately sporadic in etiology. Several conditions are associated including fibular hemimelia, ACL deficiency, coxa vara, and knee contractures. The percentage of limb-length discrepancy usually remains constant with growth. ACL deficiency in these patients results in knee instability and subsequent subluxation of the knee joint with femoral lengthening.

*Szymczuk et al. compared femoral lengthening with monolateral external fixator versus magnet intramedullary nailing.* **They found** better knee range of motion earlier in lengthening and recovery in the IMN group, which equalized by final follow-up. Additionally, the monolateral external fixator had a higher rate of complications, although mostly due to pin site/superficial infections. They had similar lengthening and healing outcomes between groups.

*Morrison et al. discussed various complications that arise with intramedullary magnetic limb lengthening procedures.* Included in their study is the failure of distraction, nonunion or insufficient regenerate, device breakage, iatrogenic deformity, joint contracture, and subluxation. **They note** that knee subluxation occurs in a step-wise fashion starting with flexion contracture, translation, and finally posterior subluxation. Knee X-rays in maximal extension can help identify these changes, and **they recommend** stopping or reversing lengthening when it is identified.

Figure A demonstrates a pediatric patient with a shortened femur and coxa vara on the left consistent with PFFD.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The dial test assesses the posterolateral corner and posterior cruciate ligament, whose absences are not associated with PFFD.
Answer 3: Galeazzi Test is used to identify hip dislocations, which can occur in these patients, however, the test would be unreliable in the setting of PFFD and does not concern the knee.
Answer 4: The Thessaly test assesses for meniscal injury.
Answer 5: The varus stress test checks the competency of the lateral collateral ligament. The absence of the LCL is not specifically associated with PFFD.
