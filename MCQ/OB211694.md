## Question

An 89-year-old male presents to the emergency department with left hip pain after a mechanical fall from standing height. He is found to have the injury depicted in Figure A. Which of the following has been associated with a delay in surgical treatment?

![[q211694-a-fnf.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Fascia iliaca block in the emergency department
2. Preoperative echocardiogram
3. Admission to a geriatric medicine service
4. Hb <10 g/dL on presentation
5. Preoperative daily aspirin use

## Correct option

**2. Preoperative echocardiogram**

## Answer explanation

Preoperative echocardiograms in the treatment of geriatric hip fractures often delay the timing to definitive surgical care with little effect on treatment decisions.

Femoral neck fractures in elderly patients are associated with hip patient morbidity and mortality. Optimal treatment involves early definitive surgical treatment with early patient mobilization and weight-bearing. Despite this, patient mortality at 1-year has been reported to be as high as 25-30%. Recent studies have reported surgical delays with preoperative echocardiograms without an effect on treatment decisions.

*Bernstein et al. retrospectively studied 258 geriatric hip fractures for the influence of preoperative testing on hospital stay, time to surgery, influence on treatment decisions.* **They reported** that 27% of the patients underwent preoperative testing and resulted in a >48-hour time-to-surgery in 63% of the patients compared to 20% of the patients without testing and a 3 day longer hospital stay. **The authors concluded** that preoperative testing is associated with greater time to surgery, increased length of hospital stay, and did not affect treatment decisions.

*Chammout et al. performed a randomized controlled trial in 100 patients with displaced intracapsular femoral neck fractures treated with total hip arthroplasty or internal fixation.* *At 17 year follow-up,* **they reported** Harris Hip Scores to be 14.7 points higher in the total hip arthroplasty cohort and a significantly lower risk for reoperation, 23% versus 53%, compared to the internal fixation group. **They concluded** that total hip arthroplasty provided better function and decreased reoperation risk in elderly patients with femoral neck fractures.

*Kubiak et al. reviewed early weight-bearing after lower extremity fractures.* **They stated** the literature supports early weight-bearing with a low risk of complications in patients treated for ankle fractures, femoral shaft fractures, femoral neck fractures, and intertrochanteric fractures. However, periarticular fractures are better treated with protected weight-bearing or delayed weight-bearing.

Figure A is an AP pelvis radiograph demonstrating a left femoral neck fracture with shortening and varus.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: Administering a fascia iliaca block in the emergency department can improve pain control preoperatively, but has not been associated with a delay in surgical care.
Answer 3: Admission to a geriatric medicine service can improve patient management preoperatively and postoperatively. This has not been associated with a delay in surgical care.
Answer 4: No association with anemia and delay in the surgical care of hip fractures has been established. Patients with anemia can be treated with transfusion as needed in the perioperative period.
Answer 5: Use of antiplatelet medications is associated with greater intraoperative blood loss and transfusion requirements, but surgery should not be delayed to normalize platelet function.
