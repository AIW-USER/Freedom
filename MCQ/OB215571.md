## Question

In a circular external fixator frame, replacing orthogonally positioned half pins with orthogonally positioned and tensioned fine wires results in what biomechanical changes?

## Options

1. Increased axial compression and decreased transverse shear
2. Increased axial compression and increase transverse shear
3. Decrease axial compression and increase transverse shear
4. Decrease axial compression and decrease transverse shear
5. No difference in axial compression or transverse shear

## Correct option

**1. Increased axial compression and decreased transverse shear**

## Answer explanation

Orthogonally positioned fine wires offer more axial compression with less transverse shear compared with half pin constructs.

Fine-wire circular frame (Ilizarov) fixators have the ability to allow axial micromotion while limiting interfragmentary shear. Tensioned fine wires and half pins are two fixation methods that are used in these fixators, with the latter being more popular in modern frames. When comparing the biomechanics of the two, tensioned fine wires create increased axial compression with decreased transverse shear. The advantage of half pins, however, comes with increased fixation options and a potential increase in patient comfort, as fine wires may cause more muscle irritation. It's important to understand the biomechanical differences in these constructs when building circular frames.

*Henderson et al. review the biomechanical effects of half pin and fine wire constructs on fracture site movement with circular frames.* **They report** that with axial loading, the all-pin configuration had less axial compression but also had increased transverse shear strain. **They conclude**, however, that both all pin and all wire constructs result in an amount of motion that is amenable to fracture healing.

*Gessmann et al. review the influence of a weight-bearing platform on the biomechanical behavior of tensioned wires versus half pins.* **They report** increased translational movements with half pin constructs secondary to the asymmetrical unilateral direction of the pins. **They conclude** that half pins result in a more stiff construct, but do allow for increased translational shear at the osteotomy site.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Tensioned wires result in decreased transverse shear compared to half pins.
Answer 3-4: Tensioned wires produce increased axial compression compared to half pins.
Answer 5: Biomechanically, differences at the fracture/osteotomy site do exist between half pins and tensioned wires as outlined above.
