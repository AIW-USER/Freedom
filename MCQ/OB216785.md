## Question

A 25-year-old male semiprofessional basketball player lands on his right shoulder when he is accidentally hit by another player. The patient experiences immediate pain in right AC joint that is worsened with active ROM. When he is evaluated by the team physician, he is diagnosed with a type III AC joint injury. He is initially treated nonsurgically. At his 6 week follow-up appointment, which clinical exam finding would suggest continued pain at the AC joint?

## Options

1. Pain reproduced with resisted shoulder abduction while shoulders elevated to 90 degrees in scapular plane, elbows extended, and forearms fully pronated
2. Pain reproduced with resisted shoulder flexion while the shoulder is at 90 degrees flexion, 10 degrees adduction, and extremity fully internally rotated
3. Pain reproduced with shoulder abduction and external rotation while the patient is laying supine on an examination table
4. Weakness and pain with resisted shoulder internal rotation with elbow at side and palm on patient's abdomen
5. Pain reproduced with forward shoulder elevation above the patient's head with the arm internally rotated

## Correct option

**2. Pain reproduced with resisted shoulder flexion while the shoulder is at 90 degrees flexion, 10 degrees adduction, and extremity fully internally rotated**

## Answer explanation

O'Brien's test is sensitive in detecting superior labrum anterior-posterior (SLAP) tears and acromioclavicular (AC) joint pathology depending on the depth of the pain. This is performed with the patient in an upright position and resisted shoulder flexion while the shoulder is at 90 degrees flexion, 10 degrees adduction, and the extremity is fully internally rotated.

AC joint injuries typically occur in young athletic patients with a direct fall on the shoulder. Varying degrees of injury occur and are dependant on the involvement of the AC ligament and coracoclavicular (CC) ligaments. Nonoperative treatment is recommended for types I and II AC joint injuries while treatment of type III injuries is more controversial. Patients that are treated nonoperatively can be assessed for AC joint pain with O'Brien's test and the crossbody adduction test. Continued pain and signs of instability after nonoperative treatment can be treated with AC and CC ligament reconstruction.

*O'Brien et al. described O'Brien's test for diagnosing labral tears and AC joint pathology, and performed a prospective validation study in 318 patients.* **They reported** 53/56 patients with preoperative examinations consistent with labral tears had confirmed intraoperative findings and 55/62 patients with AC joint findings had confirmed radiographic and operative findings.

*Simovitch et al. reviewed the diagnosis and management of AC joint injuries.* *They discussed the literature supporting nonoperative treatment of type I and II injuries and surgical treatment for types IV, V, and VI injuries.* **They recommended** initial nonoperative treatment for type III injuries with surgical treatment reserved for symptomatic instability.

*Kibler et al. performed a case series of 15 patients with AC joint instability surgically treated with a Docking technique.* **They reported** 14/15 patients maintained AC joint reductions at 3 years follow-up with CC distance averaging 0.93 cm compared to 2.7 cm preoperatively. **They concluded** that anatomic CC ligament with concomitant AC joint repair/reconstruction achieves static and dynamic stability of the shoulder.

Incorrect answers
Answer 1: Jobe's test or the "empty can" test, is a physical examination maneuver to diagnose rotator cuff tears involving the supraspinatus.
Answer 3: The apprehension test is a physical exam maneuver for diagnosing anterior shoulder instability.
Answer 4: The belly press test is performed with the patient's palm on their abdomen and the examiner tests internal rotation strength by pulling the patient's palm from their abdomen. Reproduction of pain and appreciation of weakness is associated with a subscapularis tear.
Answer 5: Neer's test is performed with the patient sitting upright and the patient's arm is passively elevated in forward flexion and the arm in internal rotation. Pain reproduced with this maneuver is positive for subacromial impingement.
