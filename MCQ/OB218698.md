## Question

A 9-year-old girl presents to the emergency room with her mother febrile to 39.5°C and with a white blood cell count (WBC) of 19,500/mm3, an erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR) of 55 mm/hr, and a C-reactive protein (CRP) of 9.0 mg/dL. An MRI of her knee shows the pathology in Figure A, and she is taken urgently for operative debridement. If the cultured specimen intra-operatively reveals the most common causative organism, which of the following factors released by the organism is most likely responsible for the activation of prothrombin to thrombin that allows for clot formation and increases the patient's risk for deep vein thrombosis (DVT)?

## Options

1. Adhesins
2. Coagulase
3. Extraceullar DNA (eDNA)
4. Panton-Valentine leukocidin (PVL)
5. Von Willebrand factor (vWF) binding protein

## Correct option

**2. Coagulase**

## Answer explanation

Coagulase, a Staphylococcus aureus virulence factor, activates prothrombin to thrombin, which leads to the cleavage of fibrinogen to fibrin and subsequently promotes clot formation that can manifest as clinically significant deep vein thromboses (DVTs), even in pediatric patients.

Pediatric osteomyelitis is most often the result of hematogenous seeding of bacteria to the metaphyseal region of bone. The most common causative organism in all children is Staphylococcus aureus, with the most severe presentations often involving methicillin-resistant forms (MRSA) given the predilection for expression of the Panton-Valentine leukocidin (PVL) gene. Though PVL is associated with the most severe expressions of the disease, it is not directly the cause of coagulopathy via activation of thrombin, but instead mediates soft tissue destruction and abscess formation indirectly through neutrophil degradation. These neutrophil degradation products may in turn cause aggregation of platelets that contribute to the coagulopathic milieu, but again are not the direct cause without the presence of coagulase. The rare cases of DVT and coagulopathy that do occur are directly associated with coagulase expression from MRSA and are more commonly seen in patients older than 8 years of age, those who are in a post-operative state from a prior debridement, and those with a CRP of > 6.0 mg/dL.

*An et al. review pediatric musculoskeletal infection and the "hijacking of the acute-phase response."* **The authors note** that direct tissue injury activates the acute-phase response mediated by the liver, which promotes coagulation, immunity, and tissue regeneration. Staphylococcus aureus, in particular, expresses the virulence factors coagulase and von Willebrand binding protein to stimulate coagulation and to form a fibrin abscess that protects it from host immune-cell phagocytosis. **They conclude** that after the staphylococcal abscess community reaches quorum, which is the colony density that enables cell-to-cell communication and coordinated gene expression, subsequent expression of staphylokinase stimulates activation of fibrinolysis, which ruptures the abscess wall and results in bacterial dissemination.

*Niemann et al. published on their findings regarding PVL-induced platelet aggregation from the products of neutrophil breakdown (i.e., α-defensins and the myeloperoxidase product HOCl).* **The authors note** that PVL only activates platelets in the presence of human neutrophils, denoting again its primary role as a pore-forming substance in neutrophil breakdown. Regardless, **they conclude** that their observations may make us think about new approaches to treat and/or prevent thrombotic complications in the course of infections with PVL-producing S. aureus strains.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Adhesins are responsible for bacterial adherence to host extracellular matrix molecules and assist in biofilm formation.
Answer 3: Extracellular DNA (eDNA) has been shown to be an important factor in the creation and maintenance of bacterial biofilms.
Answer 4: Panton-Valentine leukocidin (PVL) expression causes neutrophil degradation through pore formation at the cell's surface, indirectly resulting in soft tissue destruction/necrosis in severe MRSA infections. These breakdown products may also activate platelets, but they do not directly result in thrombin activation and fibrin clot formation, as above.
Answer 5: Von Willebrand factor (vWF) binding protein activates platelets and promotes adhesion of bacteria to blood vessel walls, allowing for tissue invasion and hematogenous spread of infection.
