## Question

A 5-year-old girl presents to the emergency room after a fall off a playground with right elbow pain. She refuses to move her arm due to the pain but is neurovascularly intact distally. Radiographs are shown in Figures A and B. What is the best next step in treatment?

## Options

1. NSAIDs, rest, and immobilization with avoidance of elbow resistance exercises
2. Splint/cast immobilization
3. Elastic stable intrameduallary nailing (ESIN)
4. Open reduction internal fixation (ORIF) using a tension band construct
5. Open reduction internal fixation (ORIF) using a plate and screws

## Correct option

**4. Open reduction internal fixation (ORIF) using a tension band construct**

## Answer explanation

This patient presents with a displaced olecranon fracture, which would be best treated with ORIF using a tension band construct.

Olecranon fractures are rare injuries in the pediatric population. To identify olecranon fractures and determine the degree of displacement, the practitioner must be knowledgeable about the progressive ossification centers. In girls aged 5 years old, the capitellum, radial head, and medial epicondyle should be partially ossified while the trochlea, olecranon, and lateral epicondyle should remain fully cartilaginous. Thus, at this stage in development, any disruption in the olecranon seen on lateral radiographs is likely a fracture. In patients where ossification of the olecranon has begun, the oblique orientation of the physis can help to differentiate it from a fracture.

*Cheng et al. retrospectively assessed the sequential development of elbow ossification centers in children.* **They found** that the sequence of ossification in both boys and girls was found to be the same (i.e. the capitellum first, followed by the radial head, medial epicondyle, olecranon, trochlea, and last, the lateral epicondyle).

*Parent et al. performed a biomechanical analysis of fixation methods (tension band wiring vs. tension band suturing) for displaced olecranon fractures in children.* **They found** that suture tension bands had lower ultimate failure loads and less compression at the fracture site compared to wire tension bands. However, **they report** that the suture tension band may be an appropriate alternative to wire fixation if low loads are expected or if the fracture is easily reducible.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: NSAIDs, rest, and immobilization with avoidance of elbow resistance exercises would be appropriate for a patient with a stress fracture of the olecranon (which is not the case in this patient).
Answer 2: Splint/cast immobilization would be inappropriate for a patient with a displaced olecranon fracture.
Answer 3: Elastic stable intramedullary nailing is not recommended for olecranon fractures
Answer 5: Open reduction internal fixation (ORIF) using a plate and screws can be considered with comminuted fractures but is not required in a patient several years from skeletal maturity.
