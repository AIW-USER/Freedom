## Question

Definition of floating shoulder is (traumatology- fracture/dilocations around shoulder- scapula fracture)

## Options

1. Ipsilateral fracture of clavicle and surgical neck of humerus
2. Ipsilateral fracture of clavicle and fracture neck of scapula lateral to coracoid process
3. Ipsilateral fracture of clavicle and fracture neck of scapula medial to coracoid process
4. Ipsilateral fracture of clavicle and surgical neck of scapula and disruption of coracoacromial ligament and coracoclavicular ligaments

## Correct option

**2. Ipsilateral fracture of clavicle and fracture neck of scapula lateral to coracoid process**

## Answer explanation

The surgical neck of the scapula is medial to the base of the coracoid process whilst the anatomical scapular neck is lateral to the base of the coracoid process. According to this pathoanatomy, fractures of the anatomical neck are unstable and account for floating shoulder when associated with ipsilateral clavicle fracture. Most of the time, the scapular neck fracture is at the surgical neck and very rarely at the anatomical neck. Fractures of the surgical neck of the scapula produce the distal fragment consisting of the glenoid and the coracoid process and a proximal fragment consisting of the acromion, scapular spine and scapular body. The distal fragment is attached to the proximal fragment by the coracoacromial ligament and to the axial skeleton, through the clavicular shaft, by the coracoclavicular ligament. The indirect attachment of the distal fragment to the proximal fragment is through the acromioclavicular ligament. For the scapular neck fracture to produce a floating shoulder, there has to be damage to its attachments to the proximal fragment and to the axial skeleton as well. Fracture of the anatomical neck of the scapula produces a floating shoulder (without injury to the stabilizing ligament) due to loss of bony and ligamentous continuity with the proximal fragment and axial skeleton.

So both B and D are correct options.
