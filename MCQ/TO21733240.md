Chapter: Sports Medicine MCQs — Principles Of Training And Recovery

## Question

The type of exercise for muscle training recommended for a 24 years old tennis player

## Options

1. Slow and fast speed isokinetic
2. Ballistic resistance training
3. Plyometric
4. Fartlek training

## Correct option

**1. Slow and fast speed isokinetic**

## Answer explanation

Ans: A

EXPLANATION: -

Tennis requires well developed physical fitness (endurance, strength, agility, flexibility and hand eye coordination) as well as sound technical, tactical and psychological skills.

Isokinetic strengthening refers to performing exercise at ‘same speed’ regardless of the amount of resistance applied. This consistency allows for uniform strength gain throughout the range of motion as it allows of maximal resistance at every point of the range of motion. It can be used to train both single joint and multi joint movements as well as concentric and eccentric strength. Examples include stationary bike, treadmill running, rowing, leg extension, hamstring curls, squats, curls, push ups, shoulder press on specialised equipment (isokinetic machines). Isokinetic strength becomes important in tennis to be able to maintain muscle endurance of long rallies of play and long hours of play as well as being able to utilise the strength in full range of motion to increase the efficiency of the shots.

Now, Plyometrics and Ballistic training involve exercises that use bursts of speed and force of different movements to aggressively build muscle power. So, ballistic resistance training and plyometrics is incorporated in training schedules to improve the power of the muscle that would translate to improvements in the power of serve or shots. However, baseline physical fitness and isokinetic strength is more important in order to be able to maintain before ballistic training and plyometrics can improve power of the strokes once fatigue sets in.

Now Interval training and Fartlek training are basically specialized training regimens. Interval training involves the athlete working at near full exertion, i.e. approximately 90% of their maximal heart rate for a period of time, and then interspersed with a rest period of much lower intensity exercise or relative rest. The session is most beneficial when the work to rest ratio is approximately 1:1; that is the time of effort is similar to the rest period. As the session progresses, the athlete’s recovery will not be complete and their heart rate will not return to its normal resting level. Fartlek training just similar to interval training in that there are periods of high intensity training interspersed with low intensity periods. It is continuous training and therefore is often used by long distance runners as a training method. The session can last for up to 45 min and can, in fact, include anaerobic bursts if it is required in the athlete’s event. But to pursue to these training regimens one needs to have adequate muscle power. Then the energy systems utilised by tennis players include both aerobic and anerobic. Fartlek training targets both energy systems as it involves alternating between periods of high intensity (anaerobic system) and low intensity (aerobic system), essentially varying the speed during continuous running. However, the question specifically mentions muscle training and not energy system.
So here choice A will be best pick.

Regular core strengthening especially superficial and deep muscle of the lower back and hip joint provide a favourable environment for the power transmission to the upper limb from the lower limbs (Principle of Kinetic chain). This enhances coordination, agility, balance and power transmission between the various muscle groups. They should ideally be added to the Isokinetic exercises as well to maximize benefit.
