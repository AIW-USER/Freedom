## Question

A 65-year-old woman presents with worsening back pain, inability to stand erect, and inability to ambulate more than 1 block at a time. Current radiographs are shown in figure A. Which of the following formulas can used to correct sagittal balance?

LL: lumbar lordosis, PI = pelvic incidence, TK = thoracic kyphosis

![[q3735-a-screen-shot-2020-02-26-at-5-39-22-pm.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. LL = PI + 25°
2. LL = PI +/- 9°
3. LL = 35°
4. LL = 15° + TK
5. LL = TK

## Correct option

**2. LL = PI +/- 9°**

## Answer explanation

The patient is presenting with adult spinal deformity characterized by lumbar hypolordosis. Ideally, the patient should be treated to correct lumbar lordosis (LL) to within 9° of pelvic incidence (PI).

Adult spinal deformity is typically characterized by a sagittal vertical axis deviation greater than 5 cm, which has been strongly correlated with worse patient-reported quality of life measures. Surgical treatment typically involves osteotomies of which the degree of correction determines the technique. Smith-Peterson osteotomies provide 10° of correction per level, pedicle-subtraction osteotomies provide 35° of correction, and vertebral column resection provides >45° of correction. Preoperative planning should consist of restoring LL = PI +/- 9°. Pelvic incidence remains constant, whereas other spinopelvic and spinal parameters can be surgically modified.

*Legaye et al. performed a study of spinopelvic and spinal parameters in normal and scoliotic patients.* **They reported** that a close relationship existed between pelvic incidence and sacral slope, which determined lumbar lordosis. **They concluded** that pelvic incidence controls other spinal curves in accordance with their adaptability.

*Rose et al. performed a retrospective analysis of 40 patients that underwent pedicle subtraction osteotomy (PSO) for sagittal plane correction.* **They reported** calculating lumbar lordosis (LL) correction with the equation LL ≤ 45° - thoracic kyphosis (TK) - pelvic incidence (PI) (where LL is a negative value, TK and PI are positive values) was the most effective at predicting sagittal plane maintenance following PSO. **They concluded** that patients with a diagnosis of degenerative sagittal deformity and shorter fusion constructs lost correction overtime due to proximal junctional kyphosis and degeneration at L5-S1.

*Schwab et al. reviewed the key parameters for assessing spinal alignment and preoperative deformity correction planning.* **They stated** adult spinal deformity is the interaction between structural abnormalities and compensatory mechanisms with quality of life metrics correlating with sagittal spinal alignment. **They concluded** realignment surgery should aim to correct the sagittal vertical axis to <+5 cm, pelvic tilt <25°, and lumbar lordosis to be proportional to pelvic incidence.

Figure A is a standing lateral radiograph of the spine with severe sagittal plane imbalance and loss of lumbar lordosis.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: Correction of LL to the sum of PI + 25° would result in a hyperlordotic lumbar spine leading worse quality of life for the patient.
Answer 3: A normal range of LL is 25-45°, but simply correcting the value to a normal value may not take into consideration of other global contributors of sagittal alignment.
Answer 4: Correcting LL to TK + 15° may result in a hyperlordotic lumbar spine and may detrimentally affect the patient.
Answer 5: Correcting LL to be equivalent to TK has not been shown to result in good surgical outcomes.
