## Question

A 50-year-old male with a history of gout presents to the emergency department with two days of worsening atraumatic right knee pain, swelling, and erythema. He reports a low-grade fever at home that is subjective, along with a progressively worsening arc of motion due to pain. On examination, he has an effusion with limited range of motion. His right knee fluid is aspirated and sent for synovial fluid analysis (SFA). Which of the following options below would indicate an isolated gout flare suitable for discharge on NSAIDs and allopurinol later for future prophylaxis?

## Options

1. SFA: 30k WBC with calcium pyrophosphate dihydrate deposition
2. SFA: 15k WBC, 200k RBC, and negative crystals
3. SFA: 75k WBC with monosodium urate crystal deposition
4. SFA: 35k WBCs with monosodium urate crystal deposition
5. SFA: 80k WBC with calcium pyrophosphate dihydrate deposition

## Correct option

**4. SFA: 35k WBCs with monosodium urate crystal deposition**

## Answer explanation

This patient presents with an acute gout flare of the right knee. Of the following options, an isolated gout episode would be present in a patient with an SFA demonstrating 35k WBC with monosodium urate crystal deposition. This patient would benefit from treatment with NSAIDs and future Allopurinol prophylaxis (Answer 4).

Gout is an inflammatory arthritic condition caused by intra-articular deposition of monosodium urate crystals that can cause acute recurrent episodes of significant joint pain and swelling. This condition has primary and secondary causes for intra-articular crystal deposition (issues with nucleic acid metabolism, psoriasis, hemolytic anemia, leukemia, and chemotherapy). Diagnosis is made by joint aspiration and crystal analysis showing strongly negatively birefringent needle-shaped intracellular crystals. Treatment for acute gout attacks is NSAIDs, with chronic recurrent gout attacks treated with allopurinol and medical management to reduce uric acid.

*Afinogenova et al. reviewed recent advances in the epidemiology and treatment of gout.* **They report** that genetic factors and obesity are significant contributors to hyperuricemia and gout, while diet contributes less to elevated serum urate in those who aren’t at risk. They also explain that new trial data suggest cardiovascular risk with febuxostat, and several new drug therapies are being tested for gout management. **They conclude** that, although advances have been made in our understanding and treatment strategies for gout, further improvement is still required to optimize the management of this condition.

*Cohen-Rosenblum et al. published a JAAOS review on the orthopedic management of gout.* **They note** it is the most common form of inflammatory arthritis in the U.S. and is often associated with hypertension, obesity, and chronic kidney disease. Medical management of the acute inflammatory response and hyperuricemia is the mainstay of treatment, while surgical intervention is indicated for damage to the surrounding soft tissue, concomitant septic arthritis, symptomatic cartilage loss, or neurologic deficits. In conclusion, gout does not independently affect outcomes after total hip, knee, and ankle arthroplasty. However, the associated comorbidities leading to crystalline arthropathy are important to keep in mind, as these can play a role in outcomes.

Incorrect Answer Choices:
Answer 1: This patient had an acute pseudogout episode and therefore would benefit from NSAIDs. However, allopurinol will not help prevent future recurrent episodes of pseudogout.
Answer 2: This patient has a hemarthrosis and is not having a gout attack. Therefore, allopurinol will not help prevent future episodes.
Answer 3: This patient has superimposed, septic arthritis and gout. Therefore, they will likely need a surgical I&D and antibiotics to help properly treat their condition.
Answer 5: This patient has superimposed, septic arthritis and pseudogout. Therefore, they will likely need a surgical I&D and antibiotics to help properly treat their condition.
