## Answer explanation

The patient has developed a large focus of pigmented villonodular synovitis (PVNS) in her anterior ankle which is characterized by lipid-laden macrophages with pathognomonic, prominent hemosiderin pigment and multinuclear giant cells (Figure E).

PVNS is a neoplastic synovial disease with a slight female predominance that occurs in patients typically ages 30-50. Symptom onset is typically gradual, but can lead to joint destruction if untreated. PVNS is typically not detected on radiographs, but has characteristic MRI findings that aid in the diagnosis. MRI will demonstrate a synovial mass with a joint effusion. Due to the ferromagnetic nature of the hemosiderin deposits, PVNS will appear hypointense on both T1 and T2 MRI sequences. The gross appearance of PVNS is typically a yellow to brown stained mass proliferating from the synovium. Treatment involves arthroscopy for limited nodules or complete synovectomy for the diffuse form of the disease. External beam radiation therapy is sometimes considered to reduce the risk of recurrence.

*Auregan et al. performed a retrospective case series of 23 patients with PVNS of the knee treated arthroscopically.* Arthroscopic complete synovectomy and later chemical synovectomy was performed for diffuse PVNS and arthroscopic limited synovectomy was utilized for localized disease. **They report** excellent outcomes with this technique and recurrences only in the diffuse group, which did not recur after further surgical treatment.

*Murphey et al. review the radiologic and pathologic characteristics of PVNS.* **They state** the knee is the most common joint involved, followed by the hip. **They also report** that a gradient-echo sequence will demonstrate a specific "blooming" appearance from the enlargement of the low-signal intensity areas due to the presence of hemosiderin (Illustration A).

Figure A is a sagittal T1 MRI demonstrating a hypointense soft tissue mass anterior to the ankle that appears to arise from the synovium. Figure B is a gross specimen demonstrating the stained, brown appearance of PVNS.

Illustration A are sagittal proton density and gradient-echo sequences demonstrating enlargement of hypointense areas (arrows) that have enlarged as a result of their hemosiderin composition.

Answer 2: Figure D demonstrates proliferating lipoblasts in a myxoid stroma with occasional signet ring lipoblasts consistent with a myxoid liposarcoma
Answer 4: Figure F demonstrates a biphasic appearance with spindle cells arranged in intersecting bundles and hypocellular areas (Antoni A and B areas, respectively) consistent with a neurilemmoma
