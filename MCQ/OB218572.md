## Question

A 6-year-old boy presents to the emergency department with his mother. She states he has been complaining of left knee pain for the past week and started having a fever two days ago. She noticed he was increasingly somnolent this morning and was difficult to arouse after a nap. His blood pressure is 80/45mmHg, heart rate of 150, pulse oxygenation of 90%, respiratory rate of 24 breaths per minute, and a fever of 102.2°F. His serum WBC is 20 and CRP is 20.5mg/L with blood cultures growing methicillin-resistant staph aureus (MRSA). His imaging is seen in Figure A. Which of the following is the most appropriate treatment?

*Figure A*

## Options

1. Place on oral antibiotics and provide short term follow up
2. Aspirate the ankle and wait for final cultures in 2-3 days before beginning antibiotics
3. Start antibiotics and consult interventional radiology for bone biopsy staging
4. Initiate fluids and antibiotics with urgent operative irrigation and debridement
5. Perform guillotine below knee amputation with staged closure

## Correct option

**4. Initiate fluids and antibiotics with urgent operative irrigation and debridement**

## Answer explanation

This patient presents with a osteomyelitis, subperiosteal abscess, positive blood cultures for MRSA, and a clinical picture concerning for septic shock. The most appropriate initial management is aggressive fluid resuscitation, broad-spectrum antibiotics, and early operative debridement.

Osteomyelitis in the pediatric population often occurs as a result of hematogenous spread. The slow-flowing capillary sinusoids about the metaphyseal region of long bones result in an overall predilection for bacterial seeding. There are specific joints that have intra-articular metaphyseal segments, leading to concomitant septic arthritis (i.e shoulder, hip, elbow, ankle). Patients may present with overt septic shock, as seen in this patient, or with less aggressive subacute symptoms. Without any delay, patients presenting in septic shock require aggressive fluid resuscitation and antibiotic therapy. Establishing a diagnosis and surgical planning can be aided by joint aspiration, MRI, or other modalities. Ultimately surgical debridement of the affected bone with subperiosteal abscess drainage is often required, however, diagnostic workup should not delay the initiation of fluids and antibiotics in the setting of overt septic shock.

*Kerr and colleagues retrospectively reviewed pediatric patients presenting with life-threatening bone infections at a single institution.* **They found** that MRSA infections were more likely to require intensive care unit (ICU) monitoring, multiple operative debridement procedures, and prolonged hospital stays. The authors urged that these patients be treated with aggressive resuscitation and surgical decompression to optimize the effectiveness of treatment.

*Jauregui and colleagues present a case series of eight pediatric patients who suffered from necrotizing soft tissue infection or toxic shock syndrome from circular external fixation pins.* **They proposed** a pin site infection classification system, ranging from simple inflammation to systemic infection, and guided treatment plans. While uncommon, the authors stress early recognition and resuscitation with fluids and antibiotics in order to obtain optimal outcomes.

Figure A shows the AP imaging of a left knee in a skeletally immature individual with appreciable metaphyseal periosteal elevation. Using clinical correlation, this is concerning for osteomyelitis with adjacent subperiosteal abscess formation.

Answer 1: Placing this patient on oral antibiotics with short-term follow-up is inappropriate in overt septic shock.
Answer 2: Antibiotics should be started as soon as possible in patients with overt septic shock.
Answer 3: The patient will require operative debridement. Therefore, a biopsy can be obtained at the time of operative debridement.
Answer 4: Performing a guillotine below-knee amputation is inappropriate when this pediatric patient has not had any other form of medical/surgical management. If the patient fails exhaustive treatment measures or has chronic wounds without the ability to perform reconstructive surgeries, then amputation may be considered.
