## Question

A surgeon is performing an open medial approach to the hip in a patient with Gross Motor Function Classification System level IV cerebral palsy. During the approach, the surgeon encounters two nerve branches and performs a pinch neurectomy. Which of the following would one expect to be weakened following this procedure?

## Options

1. Hip extension
2. Hip adduction
3. Hip flexion
4. Hip abduction
5. Hip external rotation

## Correct option

**2. Hip adduction**

## Answer explanation

The surgeon has encountered branches of the obturator nerve, and a pinch neurectomy would result in weakened hip adduction (Answer 2).

During the medial (Ludloff) approach to the hip, the anterior branch of the obturator nerve lies on the adductor magnus and adductor brevis muscles. It is exposed once the adductor longus has been released. It innervates the adductor longus, brevis, and gracilis. Neurectomies of the obturator nerve can be helpful in cases of non-ambulatory patients with spastic adduction to alleviate the deforming forces contributing to hip subluxation in the setting of neuromuscular hip dysplasia.

*Khot et al. published a 2008 pilot study evaluating the short-term outcomes of a combined intervention for managing generalized lower limb spasticity, hip displacement and contractures of adductors in children with bilateral spastic cerebral palsy.* *The authors performed lengthening of the adductor longus and gracilis combined with phenolization of the anterior branch of the obturator nerve.* *Patients experienced a significant increase in hip abduction, knee extension (popliteal angle), and ankle dorsiflexion.* Mean hip migration percentage decreased from 29 to 21% (P < 0.001), and significant improvements were noted in gross motor function.

*Tarassoli et al. published a 2014 article describing the medial approach for the treatment of children with developmental dysplasia of the hip (DDH).* *The authors describe their technique as follows: the medial approach exploits the interval between adductor brevis and pectineus, with the hip in flexion and abduction.* The origin of the adductor longus is divided through a 3 cm to 4 cm transverse incision in the groin. The interval in the bed of the reflected tendon is marked by the anterior division of the obturator nerve and developed medially and laterally by incising the film-like fascia over the pectineus.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1, 3-5: These actions are not associated with muscle groups innervated by the obturator nerve.
