## Correct option

## Answer explanation

The patient in Answer 4 is a young, active patient who sustained an acute patellar dislocation resulting in a large osteochondral defect, a free-floating osteochondral fragment, and a torn medial patellofemoral ligament (MPFL). Of the given choices, this patient is the best candidate for acute fixation of the osteochondral fragment with a concomitant MPFL reconstruction.

Articular cartilage defects of the knee comprise a wide spectrum of etiologies, geographical locations, and treatment options. Generally, cartilage defects result from acute trauma or chronic degeneration; osteochondritis dissecans (OCD) is a unique pathologic lesion of unknown etiology affecting the articular cartilage and subchondral bone with variable clinical patterns. Acute cartilage defects resulting from a traumatic event may present as smaller (<2 cm2) or larger (>2 cm2) lesions, which is an important component of guiding treatment. In the setting of acute patellar dislocations, osteochondral fractures to the patella and/or lateral femoral condyle are among the most commonly affected sites. Open reduction and internal fixation (ORIF) is the optimal first treatment option for dislocation-associated osteochondral defects, especially if there is an identifiable fragment. However, in more chronic settings where an identifiable fragment may be absent, osteochondral autograft/allograft transfer surgery (OATS) may be indicated. Additionally, MPFL reconstruction is indicated in cases of a compromised medial patellofemoral ligament (MPFL), as evidenced by history and physical examination with confirmatory imaging. Either allograft or autograft may be used for reconstruction, depending on the surgeon's preference.

*Kramer et al. published a 2015 case series to evaluate the functional outcomes of surgical management of OCD lesions of the patella and trochlea in children and adolescents.* **The authors identified** patients aged 18 years and younger who were surgically treated for OCD of the patella or trochlea, then performed a chart review of each patient’s lesion data, results, complication profile, reoperation, residual pain, ability to return to sports, and Pedi-IKDC scores. Surgical treatment of patellofemoral OCDs in children and adolescents produces a high rate of satisfaction and return to sports; however, female sex, prolonged duration of symptoms, and internal fixation may be associated with worse outcomes.

*Felus et al. published a 2022 retrospective case series to evaluate midterm results of fixation of dislocation-related osteochondral fractures in adolescents and to identify predictive factors for poor outcomes.* *The authors assessed the Knee injury and Osteoarthritis Outcome Score (KOOS), patient satisfaction, and postoperative versus preoperative sports participation level.* *The median overall KOOS was 93.8, the median satisfaction score was 8/10, and 16 patients (40%) returned to sports at their pre-injury level.* MRI scans revealed a 100% rate of bone healing. Abnormalities outside the fracture area were evident on MRI scans in 86.5% of patients. Recurrence of patellar instability, even after surgical fixation, and an unstable patella at final follow-up were independent predictors of worse results after OCF fixation.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: No osteochondral defect is seen in this patient’s imaging; therefore, fragment fixation is not indicated.
Answer 2: This patient has a small, partial-thickness cartilage defect in the setting of chronic patellar instability without an identifiable fragment; fragment fixation is not indicated in this scenario.
Answer 3: This patient has a bucket handle meniscus tear and requires meniscal repair, not fragment fixation or MPFL reconstruction.
Answer 5: This patient has advanced Outerbridge Grade IV knee osteoarthritis and does not require fragment fixation or MPFL reconstruction.
