## Question

A 30-year-old female with a history of multiple recurrent sprains of the right lateral ankle presents to your office with complaints of significant pain in the anterior and lateral ankle. She has tried an extensive course of nonoperative management including bracing and NSAIDs. On examination, she has grade 2+ anterior drawer test, talar tilt test, and evidence of anterior ankle impingement. In addition to ankle arthroscopic debridement, which of the following surgical procedures would be best indicated in this patient?

## Options

1. Shortening and reinsertion of the ATFL and CFL reinforced with inferior extensor retinaculum and distal fibular periosteum
2. Shortening and reinsertion of the ATFL and PTFL reinforced with inferior extensor retinaculum and distal fibular periosteum
3. Shortening and reinsertion of the CFL and PTFL reinforced with Inferior extensor retinaculum and distal fibular periosteum
4. Shortening and reinsertion of the ATFL and CFL reinforced with superior extensor retinaculum and distal fibular periosteum
5. Shortening and reinsertion of the PTFL and CFL reinforced with superior extensor retinaculum and distal fibular periosteum

## Correct option

**1. Shortening and reinsertion of the ATFL and CFL reinforced with inferior extensor retinaculum and distal fibular periosteum**

## Answer explanation

This patient has anterior ankle impingement and persistent lateral ankle instability. Shortening and reinsertion of the ATFL and CFL reinforced with inferior extensor retinaculum and distal fibular periosteum is indicated in grade I-III low ankle sprains that continue to have pain and instability despite extensive nonoperative management

Ankle sprains are very common twisting injuries to the ankle that are the most common reason for missed athletic participation. Diagnosis can be made clinically with swelling and ecchymosis of the ankle and pain with range of motion. Treatment usually includes a brief period of immobilization followed by early functional physical therapy. Rarely, operative management is indicated in the setting of syndesmosis injury with tibiofibular diastasis or chronic ankle instability with recurrent sprains.

*Ross et al. reviewed anterior ankle impingement syndrome.* **They reported** that advancements in ankle arthroscopy have decreased the risk of complication drastically and are an effective approach to treating osseous and soft tissue impingement. **They concluded** that preoperative detection of anterior osteophytes has been correlated with outcomes and depends on factors such as joint space narrowing and large osteophytes.

*Watson et al. reviewed ankle instability and ankle impingement as a result of recurrent ankle sprain.* **They reported** that ankle instability may resolve with physical therapy but can require surgical reconstruction. **They concluded** that anatomic reconstruction is preferred whenever possible, and ankle impingement usually requires surgical treatment.
