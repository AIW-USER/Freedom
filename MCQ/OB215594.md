## Question

A 68-year-old patient undergoes a C2-T2 laminectomy and instrumented fusion for severe multilevel myelopathy. Her immediate postoperative course was uneventful. However, on post-op day 6 she develops worsening motor weakness and paresthesias in bilateral lower extremities. An emergent MRI is performed and reveals an epidural hematoma at C4-5. Which appearance on MRI would suggest an epidural hematoma in a hyperacute stage (<24 hours)?

## Options

1. Hypointense on T1 and T2 weighted images
2. Hyperintense on T1 and hypointense on T2 weighted images
3. Isointense on T1 and hypointense on T2 weighted images
4. Hypointense on T1 and hyperintense on T2 weighted images
5. Hypointense on STIR weighted images

## Correct option

**4. Hypointense on T1 and hyperintense on T2 weighted images**

## Answer explanation

Epidural hematomas have varying appearances on MRI imaging depending on the acuity of the hematoma. In the hyperacute stage (<24 hours), the hematoma will appear hyperintense on T2 and hypointense on T1.

Postoperative epidural hematomas are rare complications with an incidence of 1:1000. However, they come with grave consequences if not detected early. If missed, patients often experience permanent neurologic deficits and worse patient-reported outcomes. Immediate MRI of the spine should be performed in those who are suspected to have an epidural hematoma. The appearance of the hematoma on imaging depends on the duration. In the hyperacute stage (<24 hours) it will appear hyperintense on T2 and hypointense on T1 due to the presence of oxygenated hemoglobin in the blood.

*Kao et al. performed a case-control study of patients with symptomatic epidural hematomas following lumbar spine decompressive surgery.* **They reported** a higher risk of epidural hematoma in patients with higher preoperative diastolic blood pressure, use of gelfoam on for dural coverage, and decreased postoperative drain output. In their study, patients with a shorter time from symptom onset to hematoma evacuation had better neurologic improvement.

*Schroeder et al. performed a retrospective multicenter study of symptomatic epidural hematomas in cervical spine surgery.* **They reported** an incidence of 1 symptomatic epidural hematoma to 1000 surgical cases with a higher predilection in posterior cervical operations, but this was not a significant finding. **They concluded** early hematoma evacuation improves the chances of neurologic recovery, but patients with symptomatic epidural hematomas do not have any improvement health-related quality-of-life measures.

Incorrect answers
Answer 1: Chronic epidural hematomas (>2 weeks) have a hypointense appearance on T1 and T2 weighted images.
Answer 2: Epidural hematomas in the subacute stage (3 days - 2 weeks) will appear hyperintense on T1 and hypointense on T2 weighted images.
Answer 3: In the acute phase (1-3 days) will have an isointense appearance on T1 and hypointense signal on T2 weighted images.
Answer 5: In the hyperacute phase (<24 hours) the hematoma will appear hyperintense on STIR weighted images.
