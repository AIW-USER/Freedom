## Options

1. Increase metaphyseal contact area to promote osseous integration
2. Provide suppressive long term antibiotics
3. Provide diaphyseal fit
4. Obviate the need for cemented constructs
5. Decrease the difficulty of any subsequent revision surgery

## Correct option

**1. Increase metaphyseal contact area to promote osseous integration**

## Answer explanation

The arrows point to a metaphyseal cone. Cones and sleeves have been designed to replace bone loss during revision total knee arthroplasty (rTKA) by increasing the contact area between the femoral and tibial metaphyseal bone and the implant while providing a porous surface for long term biologic implant fixation (Answer 1).

Revision knee arthroplasty often presents significant challenges due to bone loss and the need for stable fixation. Achieving reliable fixation at both the metaphysis and diaphysis is crucial to restoring mechanical stability and optimizing implant longevity. Metaphyseal fixation is typically achieved using porous metal cones or sleeves, which provide structural support by filling bone defects and facilitating biological integration through osseointegration. Diaphyseal fixation, on the other hand, relies on long cemented or press-fit stems to achieve stability in the host bone's more intact regions. Combining metaphyseal and diaphyseal fixation ensures a well-balanced load transfer, minimizes micromotion, and enhances the success of revision procedures in patients with complex bone loss.

*Sheth et al. review the evaluation and management of bone loss in rTKA.* Patient evaluation begins with a thorough history emphasizing prior surgery and infection. Next, three-dimensional imaging is often employed to better characterize bony deficits and operative planning. The authors then review several options for reconstruction based on the degree of bone loss. This includes stem fixation, cement and screws, allograft, block augmentation, and cones and sleeves.

*Siddiqi et al. review the utilization of metaphyseal cones and sleeves in rTKA.* *The authors first review bone loss classification schemes before describing the various cones and sleeves available for implantation.* *The authors then review the clinical outcomes for both implants before finally comparing their use.* **They conclude** that both metaphyseal cones and sleeves have demonstrated excellent radiographic survivorship and clinical, functional outcomes at midterm follow-up.

Figure A demonstrates a primary TKA component. Figure B shows an articulating spacer with an all-polyethylene tibia. Figure C is the AP and lateral following rTKA, with the arrow indicating a metaphyseal cone.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 2: Metaphyseal cones allow for biological fixation but are not infused with antibiotics.
Answer 3: The purpose of this particular implant is to provide a metaphyseal fixation.
Answer 4: Although cones are press-fit, they are frequently used with short cemented stems at the discretion of the surgeon. Additionally, cement is often used to unitize the cone with the tibial baseplate.
Answer 5: Metaphyseal sleeves and cones often complicate subsequent revision surgery.
