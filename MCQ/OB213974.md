## Question

Figure A in the radiograph of a 38-year-old male laborer presenting with worsening shoulder pain for the past several years. He has completed two courses of formal physical therapy and has received multiple cortisone injections over the years. He now states that for the past 3 months he has noticed decreasing shoulder motion. A neuropraxia secondary to this patient's inferior humeral osteophyte may lead to fatty infiltration of which muscle?

![[q213974-a-osteophyte-shoulder.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Infraspinatus
2. Supraspinatus
3. Subscapularis
4. Teres major
5. Teres minor

## Correct option

**5. Teres minor**

## Answer explanation

This young active laborer has moderate glenohumeral arthritis. The presence of an inferior humeral osteophyte has been associated with axillary nerve entrapment leading to fatty infiltration of the teres minor muscle

This patient poses a unique challenge to the treating orthopedic surgeon. His young age and activity level makes arthroplasty treatment options less desirable. In this cohort, arthroscopic debridement with capsular release may be indicated to relieve pain and improve motion. This patient’s loss of external rotation is likely due to capsular contraction. The presence of a “beard” osteophyte on the humerus has been associated with axillary nerve entrapment and eventual teres minor fatty infiltration and weakness. This patient must be counseled that this is a temporizing treatment to help improve pain and motion. It is less successful in those with more rapid degenerative changes.

*Cole et al. reviewed symptomatic cartilage injuries in young, active individuals.* **He reports** that the smallest lesions are amenable to reparative strategies such as marrow-stimulation, however larger lesions and those that have failed reparative techniques may be candidates for restorative or reconstruction procedures. **They conclude** that the diagnosis and management of glenohumeral arthritis in this young cohort is challenging and they provides a treatment framework for use in these patients.

*Millett et al. reviewed glenohumeral osteoarthritis with inferior humeral osteophytes and their influence on the axillary neurovascular bundle and fatty infiltration of the teres minor muscle.* With the use of MRI, **they found** that the distance between the inferior humerus and axillary neurovascular bundle was inversely correlated to the size of the inferior humeral osteophyte. Moreover, fatty infiltration of the teres minor was greater when an inferior osteophyte was present and a correlation between the size of the humeral head spur and quantity of fat in the teres minor muscle belly was identified. **They concluded** that the axillary nerve may be entrapped by the inferior humeral osteophyte which may affect axillary nerve function and lead to changes throughout the teres minor muscle.

Figure A is the AP radiograph of the shoulder demonstrating moderate glenohumeral osteoarthritis with an inferior humeral osteophyte

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The infraspinatus is innervated by the suprascapular nerve
Answer 2: The supraspinatus is innervated by the suprascapular nerve
Answer 3: The subscapularis is innervated by the upper and lower subscapular nerves
Answer 4: The teres major is innervated by the lower subscapular nerve and or the thoracodorsal nerve (middle subscapular nerve). The nerves that innervate teres major consist of fibers from spinal nerves C5-C8
