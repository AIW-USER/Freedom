## Question

An axial CT scan image is given, showing a mottled appearance. What’s likely to be the diagnosis in this case)

![[TO17912733-1.png]]

## Options

1. Vertebral haemangioma
2. Multiple Myeloma
3. Vertebral metastasis
4. D

## Correct option

**1. Vertebral haemangioma**

## Answer explanation

Ans: A

Radiographic features are so characteristic in a vertebral haemangioma that a biopsy is almost never required.

On X-ray vertebra shows vertically oriented trabeculae giving the classic “Jailhouse appearance” or the so called “corduroy appearance”.

![[TO17912733-2.jpg]]

Figure: X ray and MRI of thoracic spine showing the classical corduroy appearance (Corduroy cloth- a thick cotton fabric that has velvety ridges running in parallel fashion)

On cross-sectional images on CT scan, the equivalent pattern is visible as the popular “polka dot pattern”, also called by some as the Salt and Pepper sign. The lesions are characteristically bright in both T1 and T2 images of MRI and are a result of the replacement of the normal cancellous bone by thickened vertical trabeculae surrounded by fat marrow or vascular lacunae.

![[TO17912733-3.jpg]]

Figure: Axial CT image showing the characteristic Polka dot pattern (Polka is a bug with multiple dots on body surface).
