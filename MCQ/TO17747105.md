## Question

A patient has ossified PLL (mixed OPLL) on MRI with cervical compressive myelopathy . Which investigation would you like to do before embarking on surgery? (spine- DISH/Fluorosis/ OPLL)

## Options

1. X-ray cervical spine lateral view and dynamic view
2. CT of cervical spine
3. Both a & b
4. None of the above

## Correct option

**3. Both a & b**

## Answer explanation

After plain radiographs are obtained, MRI is usually the next test obtained in the imaging workup for cervical myelopathy. MRI provides a noninvasive means of visualizing the entire cervical spine, including the discs, vertebra, spinal cord, and nerve roots, in multiple planes. CT-myelography plays a role when MRI is contraindicated or when osseous pathology contributes to spinal canal encroachment. In the presence of complex cervical stenosis problems, CT-myelography continues to play a significant role, especially in patients with ossification of the posterior longitudinal ligament (OPLL) where progressive mineralization of this ligament progressively narrows the diameter of the cervical spinal canal. CT images are also valuable in preoperative planning of complex spinal instrumentation procedures for assessment of potential screw fixation sites and their relationship to osseous abnormalities and critical vascular structures (e.g. vertebral artery).

X Ray cervical spine in cervical myelopathy is important to assess the cervical lordosis. Posterior cervical laminectomy can be performed only if the cervical lordosis is maintained.

Answer: 3
