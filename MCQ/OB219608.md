## Question

At the start of your intern year, your program director has arranged for the program to purchase lead protective garments for you and your classmates. As you peruse the catalog, you are overwhelmed by the configurations and color options. Which of the following is the most true?

## Options

1. Standard vests alone provide the greatest protection from irradiation to the breast
2. Anteroposterior images expose the breast to greater irradiation than lateral images
3. Lead glasses are worn to protect from developing retinoblastoma
4. Lead sleeves and axillary supplements reduce radiation to the breast
5. Breast tissue is not radiosensitive, so wearing a lead garment around your torso is not advised

## Correct option

**4. Lead sleeves and axillary supplements reduce radiation to the breast**

## Answer explanation

The breast's upper outer quadrant (UOQ) is inadequately protected with standard lead garments. Lead sleeves and axillary supplements are advised to reduce radiation exposure to the UOQ of the breast (Answer 4).

The use of fluoroscopy presents an occupational risk to orthopedic surgeons. Radiation places surgeons at elevated risk of cataracts and multiple forms of solid cancers. Precautions taken to reduce radiation exposure include but are not limited to, radiation shields (including lead aprons), lead glasses, and conscientious C-arm positioning and management. Recent literature has evaluated the protective effect of lead garments and found the upper outer quadrant of the breast inadequately protected by conventional lead vests. Axillary lead supplementation and sleeves decrease radiation exposure to the upper outer quadrant.

*Valone et al. performed a study using dosimeters and anthropomorphic torso phantoms to examine breast radiation exposure with different lead aprons.* **They found** that the upper outer quadrant (UOQ) received the most radiation, cross-back aprons provided greater protection than vests, and cross-table lateral images exposed the breast to greater radiation than AP images. **They concluded** that proper lead wear is vital to reduce radiation exposure.

*Van Nortwick et al. performed a similar study to the above Valone study.* *This study specifically examined the effect of different lead configurations (no lead, lead vest, and vest with sleeves, wings, and axillary supplements) on breast radiation exposure.* **They found** that lead sleeves and axillary supplements decreased intraoperative radiation exposure to the UOQ, with sleeves and axillary supplements providing greater protection than wings. **They concluded** that axillary supplements and sleeves are important lead augments to adequately protect the breast from radiation exposure.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: Vests provide inadequate protection to the UOQ of the breast, which is the most common site of breast cancer.
Answer 2: Lateral images expose the breast to more radiation.
Answer 3: Lead glasses are worn to protect from developing cataracts.
Answer 5: The effect of radiation on breast tissue is on par with bone marrow, colon, lungs, and stomach and is greater than its effect on the gonads and thyroid.
