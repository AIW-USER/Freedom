## Question

A 22-year-old soldier finishes a timed 2-mile run during the early summer and then collapses next to the track. The standby medical team evaluates her immediately and discovers that she has maintained consciousness. She reports feeling faint but is alert to her environment. Upon further evaluation in the medical tent, she has a rectal temperature of 37.9°C (100.2°F), blood pressure of 112/70 (standing) and 122/73 (supine), and a heart rate of 125 beats/min. After a short rest, she is more conversational and wants to know her 2-mile run time. Which of the following is the most likely diagnosis associated with her constellation of symptoms?

## Options

1. Exercise-associated collapse
2. Exertional heat stroke
3. Exercise-associated muscle cramps
4. Exercise-associated postural hypotension
5. Exercise-associated hyponatremia

## Correct option

**1. Exercise-associated collapse**

## Answer explanation

This patient collapsed shortly after an aerobic event and presents with no evidence of postural hypotension, mental status change, or temperature dysregulation. The most likely cause of her condition is exercise-associated collapse (EAC).

Any athlete who collapses during or immediately after an event should receive prompt evaluation. The results of such a collapse can vary from spontaneous recovery to death, depending on both the underlying cause and the subsequent treatment. EAC is postulated to result from a sudden loss of venous return upon the cessation of activity. The retention of consciousness, normal mental status, and normal vitals are consistent with this diagnosis. The symptoms of EAC will resolve with rest and patients can be discharged home after appropriate monitoring. Although EAC is the most common condition seen in a marathon medical tent (59-85%), providers need to be vigilant to evaluate for other life-threatening conditions.

*Asplund et al. published a review article regarding EAC.* *The authors reviewed 86 articles related to EAC and summarized the mechanism, presentation, and treatment of EAC.* *They published a treatment algorithm (Illustration 1) and recommend vigilance when evaluating any athlete who collapses during or shortly after an event.*

*Armstrong et al. published the American College of Sports Medicine's position on exertional heat illness during training and competition.* *They review the etiology, diagnosis, and treatment of exertional heatstroke, exertional heat exhaustion, and exercise-associated muscle cramps.* **The authors recommend** immediate evaluation of all heat-related illnesses.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Exertional heat stroke presents with a change in mental status and elevated core temperature.
Answer 3: Exercise-associated cramps present with severe pain in the involved muscle group.
Answer 4: Exercise-associated postural hypotension is caused by a decline in systolic blood pressure by at least 20mm Hg below supine values once standing upright.
Answer 5: Exercise-associated hyponatremia is characterized by a decrease in serum sodium of <135 and mental status changes.
