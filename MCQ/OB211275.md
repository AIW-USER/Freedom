## Question

A 31-year-old male falls while coming down the stairs and reports immediate foot pain. Figure A shows the radiographs obtained by the ER and Figure B shows a physical examination of the patient. Based on this information a high clinical suspicion should be had for an injury to which structure?

![[q211275-b-plantar-ecchymosis.jpg]]

## Options

1. The ligament running from the plantar calcaneus to the metatarsal heads
2. The ligament connecting the medial calcaneus to the navicular
3. The ligament connecting the anterolateral tibial to the anteromedial fibula
4. The ligament connecting the first metatarsal base to the medial cuneiform
5. The ligament connecting the second metatarsal base to the medial cuneiform

## Correct option

**5. The ligament connecting the second metatarsal base to the medial cuneiform**

## Answer explanation

Plantar ecchymosis should raise suspicion for a Lisfranc ligament injury. The Lisfranc ligament runs from the second metatarsal base to the medial cuneiform.

Tarsometatarsal joint fracture-dislocations necessitate early diagnosis, prompt anatomical reduction, and stable internal fixation. On physical examination, patients may present with the classic plantar ecchymosis as a sign of significant midfoot injury. Radiographs should be obtained and if initial radiographs are negative, further imaging in weight-bearing or stress radiographs should be obtained.

*Ross et al. first described the plantar ecchymosis sign (PES) as a consistent physical examination finding in patients with acute Lisfranc ligament injuries.* **They state** PES can be helpful in raising suspicion and is a strong piece of evidence to suggest a possible Lisfranc injury although not necessarily specific.

*Watson et al. performed a review of the treatment of Lisfranc injuries.* **They report** that several osseous relationships contribute to the intrinsic stability of the midfoot. The trapezoidal shape of the middle three MT bases and their associated cuneiforms produce a stable arch referred to as the “transverse” or “Roman” arch. The keystone to the transverse arch is the second TMT joint, a product of the recessed middle cuneiform, which occupies a position 8 mm proximal to the medial cuneiform and 4 mm proximal to the lateral cuneiform.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The plantar fascia runs from the plantar calcaneus towards the metatarsal heads.
Answer 2: The spring ligament runs from the medial calcaneus to the navicular
Answer 3: The AITFL component of the syndesmosis is described.
Answer 4: The first metatarsal base is connected to the medial cuneiform by its joint capsule.
