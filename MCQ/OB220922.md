## Question

A 59-year-old male presents for planned L5-S1 fusion to address his degenerative spondylolisthesis. When considering the placement of the S1 pedicular screws, which of the following nerve roots is most at risk if an excessively long screw is utilized?

## Options

1. L1
2. L2
3. L3
4. L4
5. L5

## Correct option

**5. L5**

## Answer explanation

An inappropriately long S1 pedicle screw places the L5 nerve root at risk (Answer 5).

The L5 nerve root is located over the anterior aspect of the sacral ala. An inappropriately long S1 pedicle screw that traverses the anterior cortex of the sacrum may potentially injure this nerve and lead to radiculopathic symptoms in the L5 distribution (radiating pain to the anterolateral leg and dorsum of the foot, weakness with great toe extension). Treatment of this condition ranges from conservative management to screw revision. Safe zones for the placement of S1 pedicle screw, including that which is located between the sacral promontory and internal iliac vein, as well as that which is located between the sacroiliac joint and the lumbosacral trunk.

*Inoue et al. described two cases of L5 nerve root injury during the insertion of S1 pedicle screws.* *They reviewed the course of treatment, including the reinsertion of the screw in one patient, as well as their recommendations for diagnostic work-up, which included selective nerve rootgraphy and infiltration with lidocaine to confirm the pain source.* **They concluded** that careful consideration of the L5 nerve root is needed when placing S1 pedicle screws.

*Mallepally et al. also reported on two cases of L5 radiuclopathy after S1 screw misplacement.* *They reviewed the inappropriate placement of the culprit screws in these patients and the two safe zones for S1 pedicle screws as described by Mirkovic et al.* **They concluded** that though bicortical purchase of S1 screws improves construct strength, the risk of injury to the neurovascular structures is increased.

Incorrect Answers:

Answers 1-4: These nerve roots are less likely to be affected by an excessively long S1 screw.
