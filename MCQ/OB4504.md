## Answer explanation

The patient shown in Figure C is the most skeletally immature, and therefore would have the highest risk of progression of an idiopathic scoliotic curve.

Skeletal maturity can be determined by the presence/absence of ossification and fusion of various apophyses. One of the most commonly used is the iliac apophysis, which can be assessed on an AP pelvis radiograph and is staged according to the Risser classification. When the iliac apophysis has not yet begun to ossify and the triradiate cartilages are open (Risser 0), the child has not yet entered the rapid phase of growth and therefore has the highest risk of progression of scoliosis. Risser 1 is when there is 25% ossification of the iliac crest; Risser 2 is when there is 50% ossification; Risser 3 is when there is 75% ossification; Risser 4 is when there is 100% ossification, and Risser 5 is when fusion is complete.

*DiMeglio et al. published a review of factors to consider when evaluating the potential progression of a scoliotic curve.* **They report** that Risser 0 is present during the initial two-thirds of pubertal growth and that ossification patterns of the elbow can also be used during this critical time period (Illustration A).

*Ryan et al. published a retrospective review of patients with adolescent idiopathic scoliosis (AIS) that underwent brace treatment.* **They identified** a significantly lower likelihood of progression in patients with closed triradiate cartilages at the time of bracing.

*Nault et al.* **found** limited reliability of the Risser grading system in determining the onset of the curve acceleration phase based on the digital skeletal age (DSA). **They proposed** a modified system based on the fact that Risser 0 patients with open triradiate cartilages were all seen prior to the curve acceleration phase, and that Risser 0 patients with closed triradiate cartilages and Risser 1 patients were within peak growth velocity. Risser 2 through 5 patients were all past this phase.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: This radiograph shows a patient at Risser 3, with approximately 75% ossification of the iliac crest apophysis.
Answer 2: This radiograph shows a patient at Risser 5, with complete fusion and closure of both the triradiate cartilage and iliac crest apophysis.
Answer 4: This radiograph shows a patient in the late stages of Risser 0, when the triradiate cartilage has started to fuse but the iliac crest apophysis has not yet started to ossify.
Answer 5: This radiograph shows a patient at Risser 4, with approximately 100% ossification but no fusion of the iliac crest apophysis.
