## Answer explanation

Distal chevron osteotomy of the 5th metatarsal is most appropriate in Type 2 or 3 bunionette deformities when the 4th-5th metatarsal IM angle is <12°.

Bunionette deformity involves a relative prominence of the 5th metatarsal head, due to 5th MTP joint varus alignment and concomitant forefoot widening. Bunionettes are broken down into 3 types: Type 1 has a lateral metatarsal head prominence with widening of the MT head >13mm and is treated with lateral metatarsal head condylectomy. Type 2 represents lateral bowing of the distal half of the metatarsal with a normal 4-5 IM angle (6-8°). Type 3 bunionettes involve widening of the 4-5 IM angle and when >12°, necessitate midshaft diaphyseal osteotomy.

*Cohen et al. reviewed bunionettes with a detailed description of the deformity seen.* **They note** that management should be nonsurgical initially and progress to surgical management if pain and deformity worsen. They finish by describing the different types of deformity and their associated surgical management.

*Coughlin reviewed symptomatic bunionettes that underwent longitudinal diaphyseal osteotomy along with lateral condylectomy.* **He found** good or excellent results in 93% of patients and concluded this was a useful technique for correcting bunionette deformity.

*Boyer et al. reviewed a series of distal fifth metatarsal chevron osteotomies.* *All patients had good results, high satisfaction and no serious complications.* **They concluded** that this was a useful technique in patients with bunionette deformity.

Figure A represents a type 3 bunionette deformity in addition to widening of the metatarsal head. Figure B shows a type 1 bunionette with widening of the metatarsal head but no bowing or increase in 4-5 IM angle. Figure C shows a type 2 bunionette with distal bowing of the metatarsal but no increase in 4-5 IM angle. Figure D shows a type 3 bunionette with 4-5 IM angle of 14°. Figure E shows a type 1 bunionette deformity in addition to hallux valgus deformity of the first ray.
