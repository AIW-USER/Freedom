## Question

A 28-year-old male falls from his dirt-bike and is brought to the nearest emergency department. He is found to have the closed injury shown in Figure A. Angulation measures 15° in the coronal plane and 18° in the sagittal plane. There are no other fractures noted on secondary survey. Which of the following is the most appropriate initial treatment?

![[q218966-a-humerus-shaft-1-2.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Nonoperative management with functional brace
2. Nonoperative management with long arm cast
3. ORIF w/ lag screws and neutralization plate
4. ORIF w/ bridge plate
5. Intramedullary humeral nailing

## Correct option

**1. Nonoperative management with functional brace**

## Answer explanation

This humeral shaft fracture falls within acceptable tolerances for nonoperative management and a functional (Sarmiento) brace or coaptation splint would be the preferred method of initial immobilization.

Humeral shaft fractures are common injuries and affect both young individuals after high-energy trauma, and elderly patients with poor bone quality. Union rate is high with nonoperative management, so efforts are made to treat these fractures without surgery if they fall within certain tolerances. These tolerances include less than 3 cm of shortening, less than 20° of sagittal plane angulation, and less than 30° of coronal plane angulation. In cases where nonoperative management is deemed acceptable, a coaptation splint or functional brace is placed in the emergency department and the patient is followed with repeat radiographs. Often, patients will have a slight degree of residual varus malunion, but they tolerate this well. Continued fracture motion 6 weeks after injury would represent an indication to fix the fracture at that time.

Harkin and Large evaluated union rates of 126 consecutive humeral shaft fractures in order to identify patients who might benefit from operative vs. nonoperative management. Interestingly, their early union rates were lower than initial studies on nonoperative management, with 33% of patients treated nonoperatively not achieving union. They concluded that surgical management had higher rates of union, but did not identify certain fracture characteristics that increased this risk.

*Ali et al. retrospectively reviewed nonoperative management of humeral shaft fractures in 138 cases over a 5-year span.* Their overall union rate was 83% with nonoperative management and **they noted** a higher rate of nonunion in proximal-third fractures (76%), compared with middle- (88%), and distal-third (85%) fractures. Fractures with comminution had a high degree of healing, regardless of position.

Figure A demonstrates the AP and lateral views of a right humeral shaft fracture which is within acceptable tolerances for nonoperative management.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: A long arm cast is not the preferred method of immobilization, as this can lead to elbow stiffness and other complications.
Answers 3, 4, 5: Initial operative management is not indicated in this patient, as his humeral shaft fracture is within acceptable tolerances for nonoperative management.
