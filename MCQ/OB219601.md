## Question

A 56-year-old college professor presents to the clinic with 6 months of low back pain that has been refractory to physical therapy, analgesics, and activity modifications. He is now considering radiofrequency ablation and is set to undergo a preliminary nerve block. In order to appropriately anesthetize the L4-L5 facet joint, which of the following spinal nerves must be blocked?

## Options

1. Intermediate branches of the dorsal roots of the L4 and L5 spinal nerves
2. Lateral branches of the dorsal roots of the L3 and L4 spinal nerves
3. Medial branches of the dorsal roots of the L3 and L4 spinal nerves
4. Ventral roots of the L3 and L4 spinal nerves
5. Ventral roots of the L4 and L5 spinal nerves

## Correct option

**3. Medial branches of the dorsal roots of the L3 and L4 spinal nerves**

## Answer explanation

In order to anesthetize the L4-L5 facet joint, it is necessary to block the medial branches of the dorsal rami of the L3 and L4 spinal nerves.

The L3 and L4 spinal nerves divide into a ventral and dorsal root, with the dorsal rami subsequently dividing into medial, lateral, and intermediate branches. The lateral branch supplies innervation to the iliocostalis muscle and the skin lateral to the muscle on the back, while the intermediate branch supplies innervation to the spinalis and longissimus muscles. The medial branch, meanwhile, supplies innervation to the capsules of the facet joints, as well as multisegmental motor innervation to the multifidus muscle. The cranial half of any given facet joint level is innervated by the exiting nerve root from the level above, while the caudal half is innervated by the exiting nerve root at that level. For example, the L3 medial branch traverses the medial aspect of the L4 transverse process at the base of the L4 superior articular process and supplies sensory innervation to the caudal half of the L3-L4 facet joint and the cranial half of the L4-L5 facet joint. In order to anesthetize the L4-L5 facet joint, it is necessary to block the medial branches of the dorsal rami of the L3 and L4 spinal nerves, given that the L4 nerve innervates the caudal half of the L5-L5 facet joint (the exiting nerve at that level), as well as the cranial half of the L5-S1 facet joint below.

*Cohen et al. reviewed the pathogenesis, diagnosis, and treatment of lumbar zygapophysial (facet) joint pain.* **The authors note** that facet arthropathy and resultant facet joint pain is a challenging condition affecting up to 15% of patients with chronic low back pain. **They also note** that because the facet joint is innervated by the medial branches arising from the dorsal rami of the spinal nerve at the same level and a level above the joint, LA blocks of these nerves have been advocated for diagnostic and prognostic purposes. **They conclude** that, as with other blocks, the potential for false positive and false negative responses must be considered, and steps should taken to reduce their incidence.

*Lau et al. performed a cadaveric study to demonstrate the surgical anatomy of lumbar medial branch neurotomy and the validity of placing electrodes parallel to the target nerve in lumbar radiofrequency facet denervation.* **The authors found** that, at typical lumbar levels, the tip of the electrode should lie opposite the middle two-quarters of the superior articular process. For example, at the L5 level, it should lie opposite the middle and posterior thirds of the S1 superior articular process. **They concluded** that placing the electrode parallel to the nerve has a demonstrated anatomical basis. It has been vindicated clinically, with suboptimal techniques portending suboptimal outcomes after lumbar medial branch neurotomy.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The intermediate branches of the dorsal rami provide innervation to the spinalis and longissimus muscles, not the facet joints.
Answer 2: The lateral branches of the dorsal rami provide innervation to the iliocostalis muscle and the skin lateral to the muscle on the back, not the facet joints.
Answers 4 and 5: The dorsal (not ventral) rami provide innervation to the facet joints.
