## Question

A 21-year-old competitive equestrian presents to your clinic one week after her horse fell while she was mounted. When she impacted the ground, her left foot was caught in a plantarflexed position in the stirrup. She was initially seen at an urgent care and non-weight-bearing films were read as normal. On exam, she has exquisite pain over the midfoot with bruising along the medial plantar foot and moderate swelling. Weight-bearing films of her injured foot are seen in Figure 1. As part of the shared decision-making process, you inform the patient that when compared to primary fusion, open reduction and internal fixation with rigid implants has a higher rate of which outcome?

## Options

1. Hardware removal
2. Revision fixation across the midfoot
3. Mid-term patient-reported outcomes
4. Increased stress on adjacent joints
5. Length of time non-weight bearing

## Correct option

**1. Hardware removal**

## Answer explanation

This patient presents with a ligamentous Lisfranc injury after a fall while on horseback. Treatment of this injury with open reduction and internal fixation using rigid hardware has a higher rate of hardware removal.

A Lisfranc injury is a broad term that describes a variety of injury patterns that are characterized by traumatic disruption between the articulation of the medial cuneiform and the base of the second metatarsal. Physical exam often reveals pain and swelling at the midfoot with ecchymosis along the medial plantar foot. Weight-bearing films will show increased distance between the base of the 1st and second metatarsal. Lateral films may show dorsal subluxation of the 1st or 2nd metatarsal base. This patient presents with a purely ligamentous injury with obvious malalignment of the 2nd tarsometatarsal joint. The patient should be counseled on the risks and benefits of various treatment options in order to participate in the shared decision-making process. Experts disagree regarding the ideal treatment strategy for a young active patient with a ligamentous Lisfranc injury. Treatment options include primary arthrodesis of the medial column of the midfoot, open reduction and internal fixation with rigid implants, or suture button fixation (PMID:37611870). However, the literature on this topic shows consistently higher rates of implant removal with osteosynthesis. The patient should be counseled on this known outcome during the formulation of a treatment plan.

*Ly et al reported on a prospective trial of patients with isolated primarily ligamentous Lisfranc injury who were randomized to either primary arthrodesis of the medial two or three rays or open reduction and screw fixation.* *At an average follow-up of 42.5 months patients who underwent primary arthrodesis had a higher mean AOFAS Midfoot score of 88 compared to 68 in those who underwent open reduction and internal fixation (P<.005).* *Patients who had been treated with a primary arthrodesis estimated that their postoperative level of activities was 92% of their preinjury level while the fixation group reported 65% (p<0.005).* The hardware removal rate was 80% in the fixation group and 19% in the fusion group. **The authors conclude** that primary arthrodesis has better short and medium-term outcomes than fixation and is a better primary treatment for ligamentous Lisfranc injuries; however, long-term outcomes data is needed.

*Buda et al performed a retrospective analysis of 217 with a mean follow-up of 62.5 months comparing Lisfranc injuries treated with arthrodesis and open reduction and internal fixation.* Open reduction and internal fixation had a significantly higher rate of returning to the operating room when compared to arthrodesis (75.5% v 31.5% p<.005). The rate of isolated hardware removal was 83% in the fixation group and 8.3% in the fusion group. However, when hardware removal was excluded, patients in the two cohorts had no difference in reoperation rates. This included no significant difference in revision fixation across the midfoot.

Figure A demonstrates weight-bearing films of the foot showing increased space between the first and second rays with disruption of a line drawn from the medial base of the 2nd metatarsal to the medial side of the middle cuneiform.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Revision fixation is not significantly higher when this injury pattern is treated with open reduction and internal fixation.
Answer 3: As discussed above prospective evidence shows higher patient-reported outcomes in patients treated with primary arthrodesis when compared to rigid osteosynthesis.
Answer 4: Increased load on adjacent joints is a greater concern in patients treated with primary fusion.
Answer 5: Both treatment options have equivalent immobilization and non-weight-bearing protocols.
