## Question

A 35-year-old female has a 6-month history of forefoot pain and continued swelling over the 2nd metatarsal phalangeal (MTP) joint. Radiographs are normal and MRI is ordered which is seen in Figure A. What physical exam finding would be consistent with these MRI findings?

![[q211924-a-2nd-mtp-plantar-plate-tear.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Hyperflexion of 2nd MTP joint
2. Increased sagittal translation of 2nd MTP joint
3. Flexion contracture of 2nd MTP with dorsal ulceration
4. Pain when 2nd and 3rd toes are squeezed
5. Point tenderness over distal 2nd metatarsal shaft

## Correct option

**2. Increased sagittal translation of 2nd MTP joint**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a plantar plate tear of the 2nd MTP, which causes increased sagittal translation with a drawer test on physical examination.

Plantar plate disruptions of the lesser toes are often seen in association with hyperextension injuries at the MTP joint. They can also be seen in the setting of MTP dislocation and generalized MTP ligamentous laxity. Patients often complain of point tenderness over the plantar aspect of the MTP. There may be increased laxity in the sagittal plane at the MTP joint with injury to the plantar plate. MRI is useful in identification. Nonoperative management of plantar plate injuries includes metatarsal pads, achilles stretching, taping and splinting. Painful instability in the setting of failed nonoperative management is most commonly treated with plantar plate repair through either a dorsal or volar approach, both with good results.

*Doty et al. describe the recent increase in recognition of lesser toe plantar plate injuries.* *They discuss different surgical techniques including both plantar and dorsal approaches for repair, which both improve pain, function, and help correct deformity.*

*Nery et al. reviewed 55 dorsal plantar plate repairs in combination with Weil metatarsal osteotomies in patients with lesser MTP joint instability.* The 2nd MTP was the most commonly affected joint in their study. **They concluded** that this technique resulted in improvements in pain, function, and joint stability.

*Nery et al. evaluated the outcomes for treatment of different grades of plantar plate tears and sought to come up with a treatment protocol based on these grades (Illustration A) as observed in 100 repairs.* Direct repair was generally recommended for grades 2 and 3, but as severity increased to grade 4 tears, tendon transfers were recommended. **They concluded** that all groups demonstrated improvement, but patients with grade 2 repairs had the best outcomes.

Figure A shows a sagittal MRI cut demonstrating 2nd MTP plantar plate disruption. Illustration A demonstrates the grading scale used by Nery et al. to describe plantar plate ruptures.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: This exam finding would not be seen in a plantar plate rupture. Hyperextension would lead to plantar plate rupture.
Answer 3: Flexion contracture and dorsal ulceration of the 2nd MTP would be more indicative of a hammer toe which is not suggested by the MRI.
Answer 4: Positive squeeze test would be indicative of Morton’s neuroma
Answer 5: Point tenderness over the 2nd metatarsal shaft would more likely represent stress fracture of the 2nd metatarsal, not plantar plate pathology.
