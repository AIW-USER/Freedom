## Question

First portal to be made in elbow arthroscopy is (arthroscopy- elbow arthroscopy- portals and complications)

## Options

1. Anteromedial
2. Posterolateral
3. Superomedial
4. Anterolateral

## Correct option

**4. Anterolateral**

## Answer explanation

Elbow arthroscopy can be done with the patient supine, prone, or in the lateral decubitus position. A number of portals have been described for elbow arthroscopy and usually the anterolateral portal is the first made after elbow distention. Commonly made portals for elbow arthroscopy are:

Anterolateral portals: First portal to be made. Many anterolateral portals have been described:

(1) Anterolateral or distal anterolateral portal: It is located 3 cm distal and 1 cm anterior to the LE just anterior to the radial neck and head. Many surgeons don’t favor this portal due to its close proximity to the radial nerve.

 (2) The mid anterolateral portal: It is Located directly anterior to the radiocapitellar joint, and made just proximal and approximately 1 cm anterior to the palpable radiocapitellar joint.

 (3) The proximal anterolateral portal is 2 cm proximal and 1 cm anterior to the lateral epicondyle. It is the safest anterolateral portal.

Anteromedial portals:

- Anteromedial portal : 2 cm anterior and 2 cm distal to the ME. The anteromedial portal is placed through the flexor pronator musculature and the median and medial antebrachial cutaneous nerves (MABCN) are at risk.
- The proximal medial portal (or supracondylar anteromedial portal) : This portal is located 2 cm proximal and anterior to the medial epicondyle. The PAMP is the most commonly used medial portal.

Posterior portals:

- The posterolateral portal is located 2 to 3 cm proximal to the olecranon tip and just lateral to the border of the triceps tendon along the lateral supracondylar ridge.
- Direct Posterior Portal: The direct posterior portal or posterior central or straight posterior or “trans triceps” portal. It is located 3 cm proximal to the tip of the olecranon and pierces through the tendinous portion of the triceps.

 Answer: D
