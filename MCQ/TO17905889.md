## Question

A handball player had a collision and fainted. He got up shortly and started playing again but kept on getting progressively aggressive. On asking he could not recall who had scored the last goal. What should be the appropriate step in this case

## Options

1. Allow him to play as he performs best when he is aggressive
2. Counsel him for 15 min and then let him play
3. Immediately remove him from the play area
4. Give electrolyte drink and then allow him to resume play

## Correct option

**3. Immediately remove him from the play area**

## Answer explanation

Ans: C

In the mentioned scenario, the athlete seems to have suffered a concussion. The SCAT6 is a standardized method for evaluating injured athletes for concussion and can be used for athletes aged 13 and above (for children aged 5 to 12 years, the Child SCAT6 is used). Memory assessment in SCAT6 is done by assigning a “Maddocks score”. In the above case Maddocks score assessment should be done and the player should be removed from field of play.

The Sports Concussion Assessment Tool - 6th Edition (SCAT6)

The SCAT6 is a standardized tool for evaluating concussions, designed for use by Health Care Professionals in the acute phase, ideally within 72 hours (3 days), and up to 7 days, following injury, for athletes aged 13 and above (for children aged 12 years and younger, Child SCAT6 is used). If greater than 7 days post-injury, consider using the SCOAT6/ Child SCOAT6 (Sport Concussion Office Assessment Tool 6).

Note: SCAT6 is used to support the clinical diagnosis of a medical practitioner or healthcare professional in cases an athlete is suspected to have suffered a concussion. The SCAT6 should not be used as a stand-alone method to diagnose concussion, measure recovery, or make decisions about an athlete’s readiness to return to competition. Clinical diagnosis remains the gold standard as there is no perfect diagnostic tool or method for a cent percent immediate diagnosis of concussion.

SCAT6 includes: -

Immediate Assessment/Neuro Screen:

- Step 1: Observable Signs
- Step 2: Glasgow Coma Scale
- Step 3: Cervical Spine Assessment
- Step 4: Coordination & Ocular/Motor Screen
- Step 5: Memory Assessment Maddocks Questions
