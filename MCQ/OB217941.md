## Question

A 43-year-old male presents to your office for evaluation one year after open reduction internal fixation of a closed midshaft both bone forearm fracture that was performed by an outside surgeon. The patient reports minimal pain in the arm but has difficulty pronating the forearm beyond 15 degrees. Which of the following intraoperative decisions most likely led to this?

## Options

1. Bridge plating technique on the radius
2. Use of a straight plate on the radius
3. Bridge plating on the ulna
4. Use of a straight plate on the ulna
5. Dual incision technique

## Correct option

**2. Use of a straight plate on the radius**

## Answer explanation

Functional outcome, specifically pronation is directly related to restoration of the radial bow. Proper plating of the radius requires a contoured plate.

Radius and ulnar shaft fractures, also known as adult both bone forearm fractures, are common fractures of the forearm caused by either direct or indirect trauma. The axis of rotation of the forearm runs from the radial head proximally through the ulna fovea distally. This results in the radius functionally rotating around the ulna in pronation and supination. The curved nature of the radius must be restored postoperatively to expect normal function, specifically with pronation and supination. To achieve this, contoured plates are used on the radius, while straight plates are used on the ulna for ORIF of midshaft both bone forearm fractures. This is also typically done through a two-incision approach to limit the risk of synostosis.

*Schemitsch et al review the effect of malunion on functional outcomes after plate fixation of fractures of both bones of the forearm in adults.* **They report** that a good functional result (more than 80% of normal rotation of the forearm) was associated with restoration of the normal amount and location of the radial bow. **They conclude** that the radial bow is the key to good success following plating of radial shaft fractures.

*Matthews et al review the effects of angular malalignment on pronation and supination following both bone forearm fractures.* **They report** that 10 degrees of angulation may not result in meaningful loss of pronation and supination, however, when 20 degrees of angulation is encountered patients can expect a significant loss of forearm rotation.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1 & 3: Specific plating technique is dictated by the fracture characteristics and does not influence postoperative forearm motion if normal anatomy is restored.
Answer 4: The ulna is a straight bone and does not require significant contouring of the plate to restore normal anatomy.
Answer 5: A dual incision technique is preferred. Single incision techniques are associated with a greater incidence of synostosis, which may decrease forearm pronation and supination.
