## Question

A 17-year-old high school female lacrosse player is hit in the back of the head with a thrown ball. She falls to the ground and is ultimately helped off the field by the coach and training staff. She complains of a headache and photophobia. There is no verteral tenderness and no abnormalities on a cranial nerve exam. After several minutes on the sideline, she is feeling better and asks to return to the game. Which of the following is true regarding management of this scenario?

## Options

1. She should be allowed to return to play immediately upon resolution of symptoms
2. She should be taken to the emergency department for a head CT scan and brain MRI for diagnostic purposes
3. Risk for re-injury is similar with same day return to play compared to pre-injury
4. She should be warned that return to play protocols usually involve at least two weeks of cognitive and physical rest
5. She should be held out of play for the remainder of the day

## Correct option

**5. She should be held out of play for the remainder of the day**

## Answer explanation

If there is any concern for concussion, athletes should not be allowed to return to play on the same day.

Concussions represent a subset of traumatic brain injury that is often transient due to a forceful blow to the head. This typically affects the athlete's cognitive function but is not thought to cause structural damage. Concussions are quite common in an athletic population, therefore it is important for healthcare providers to be trained in appropriate sideline and return to play management. If athletes exhibit any symptoms of a suspected concussion, they should not be allowed to return to play on the same day. Return-to-play protocols have been created to help providers progress athletes through generalized, specific, and sport-specific activities to assess their readiness to return based on symptoms and performance. Most current protocols involve a 24-hour interval for each step in the return to play protocol, with each step increasing the degree of activity and building back to return. Commonly, there are about 6 steps in the protocol which allow for athletes to return around 1 week after their injury if all symptoms have resolved (Illustration A).

*Harmon et al. published a position statement on sports concussions for the American Medical Society for Sports Medicine where they define a concussion and discuss the incidence and pathophysiology.* *They also discuss sideline evaluation through standardized tests such as the Sport Concussion Assessment Tool (SCAT), and go over return-to-play protocols.* **They note** that the athlete should be free of concussion symptoms during and after exertion before returning to play, which may take days to weeks.

*McCrory et al. also published a consensus statement on concussions in sports.* *They focus initially on sideline management and symptoms of acute concussions which include headache, loss of consciousness, amnesia, neurologic deficit, abnormal balance, cognitive impairment, and sleep disturbance.* **They note** that a player should be evaluated by a trained healthcare professional in this setting and sideline assessment tools should be used. Players should not be left alone and should not return to gameplay if a concussion is suspected.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: After a direct blow to the head and symptoms concerning for concussion, an athlete should not be allowed to return to gameplay on the same day.
Answer 2: CT scan of the head and/or brain MRI are not diagnostic for concussion and should be routinely performed unless there is concern for more significant intracranial pathology.
Answer 3: The risk for further injury to the brain is significantly higher immediately following a concussion as the biochemical structure of the brain is altered and neuroprotective mechanisms are disrupted.
Answer 4: As stated above, most return to play protocols involve staged 24 hour periods in order to achieve complete return to sport around 1 week. Depending on concussion severity, return to play may ultimately require more time in any one of these stages.
