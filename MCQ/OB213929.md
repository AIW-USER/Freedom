## Question

A 62-year-old male presented with a mass on his left thigh that was subsequently removed with marginal resection by a community orthopedic surgeon. Figure A is the histology of the mass. After a referral to a tertiary care center, the patient undergoes a wide excision. If wide surgical margins are obtained, what is associated with this treatment course compared to a planned excision?

![[q213929-a-high-grade-ups.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Increased blood loss
2. Increased need for plastic reconstruction
3. Decreased functional outcomes
4. Requirement of additional chemotherapy
5. A local recurrence rate >50% at 5-years

## Correct option

**2. Increased need for plastic reconstruction**

## Answer explanation

Patients that undergo wide resection of a previously unplanned soft tissue sarcoma excision have an increased need for plastic reconstruction of the surgical site.

Unplanned excision of soft tissue sarcomas is associated with increased surgical site morbidity. Usually, the index procedure results in marginal or intra-lesional resection of the lesion, which may have appeared benign by the initial treating surgeon. Good local control can be achieved with wide resection of the previous surgical bed with at least a 3 cm margin along the skin incision and surgical wound. This extensive soft tissue removal had resulted in a higher need for plastic reconstruction of the affected site.

*Nakamura et al. reviewed 197 patients that underwent wide resection of previously unplanned soft tissue sarcoma excisions.* **The authors reported** that wide margins were achieved in 190 patients with 53% patients requiring plastics reconstruction. The 5-year local recurrence-free rate was 91% with residual tumor present at re-excision margins being an independent risk factor of local recurrence. **The authors recommended** avoiding unplanned excisions if possible, but in many cases, sufficient local control can be achieved with wide margins.

*Arai et al. retrospectively reviewed 191 patients undergoing surgical excision of sarcomatous tumors, of which 63 patients had unplanned excisions.* **They reported** a significantly higher rate of free skin grafting or flap coverage undergoing wide re-excision. Planned excision was seen more commonly in patients with deeper and larger tumors with no differences in postoperative complications, function, and survival rates between the two groups. **The authors concluded** that additional wide resection can provide adequate local control and disease-free survival rates in patients with previously unplanned sarcoma excisions.

Figure A is the histologic slide of a high-grade undifferentiated pleomorphic sarcoma.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: Studies have shown that there is decreased blood loss associated with unplanned excisions compared to planned excision.
Answer 3: Functional outcomes after sufficiently wide resection of the previous surgical wound and surgical margins do not differ from planned excisions.
Answer 4: Additional chemotherapy does not play a role in the treatment of unplanned soft tissue sarcoma excisions.
Answer 5: Studies have shown that a 5-year local recurrence-free rate is >90% if appropriately wide surgical margins are obtained after wide resection of the surgical bed.
