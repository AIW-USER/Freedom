## Answer explanation

Calcaneovalgus foot is associated with posteromedial bowing of the tibia.

Calcaneovalgus foot is a flexible deformity thought to be a result of intrauterine packaging resulting in an excessively dorsiflexed ankle. It is most common in first-born females born to a young mother. Although imaging is not required with a calcaneovalgus foot, it can be helpful to further evaluate for posteromedial bowing of the tibia and rule out congenital vertical talus. Treatment for calcaneovalgus deformity is observation with the option of passive stretching by parents. Posteromedial bowing is treated with observation for leg length discrepancy as the child ages; the bowing typically improves.

*Heyman et al. describe the clinical entity of posteromedial bowing of the tibia as a third entity separate from anteromedial and anterolateral bowing of the tibia.* *In their series of three patients,* **they found** excellent outcomes in those patients that were simply observed. **They conclude** that conservative treatment is indicated for posteromedial bowing.

*Hofmann et al. followed thirteen patients with posteromedial bowing of the tibia clinically and radiographically.* **They found** an average of 3.1cm of leg length discrepancy on scanogram and the discrepancy was related to the initial severity of curve. **They conclude** these patients should be followed for progression of leg length discrepancy and need for possible epiphysiodesis.

*Lincoln et al. reviews different rotational deformities found in children.* With regard to calcaneovalgus foot, they cite it as one of the most common foot deformities with reports as high as 50%. **They state** the natural history of calcaneovalgus foot is not altered with treatment.

Figure A shows fibular hemimelia. Figure B shows posteromedial bowing of the tibia in an infant. Figure C shows anterolateral bowing of the tibia. Figure D shows clubfoot deformity of an infant. Figure E shows a tibial deficiency in an infant.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1, 3, 4, and 5: Fibular hemimelia, anterolateral bowing of the tibia, clubfoot, and tibial deficiency are not associated with calcaneovalgus foot.
