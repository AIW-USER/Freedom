## Question

Which of the following is true regarding necrotizing fasciitis?

## Options

1. Methicillin-resistant streptococcus aureus (MRSA) is the most common cause of monomicrobial necrotizing fasciitis
2. Gram-negative enteric bacilli are commonly involved in polymicrobial necrotizing fasciitis
3. Hyperbaric oxygen therapy is contraindicated in patients with clostridia infection
4. Laboratory Risk Indicator for Necrotizing Fasciitis (LRINEC) scoring system includes erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR), white blood cell (WBC) count, sodium, hemoglobin, creatinine, and glucose level
5. Overall mortality is approximately 15%

## Correct option

**2. Gram-negative enteric bacilli are commonly involved in polymicrobial necrotizing fasciitis**

## Answer explanation

Polymicrobial infection is the most common cause of necrotizing fasciitis. Typical pathogens include gram-negative enteric bacilli, anaerobes, and non-group A streptococcus species.

Necrotizing fasciitis is an uncommon soft-tissue infection, characterized by widespread fascial necrosis. It is accompanied by local pain, fever, and systemic toxicity and is often fatal unless promptly recognized and aggressively treated. Tissue damage and systemic toxicity are believed to result from the release of endogenous cytokines and bacterial toxins. Early and aggressive surgical debridement and fasciotomy have been associated with improved survival. Initial antibiotic therapy should include broad aerobic and anaerobic coverage.

*Bellapianta et al. review the pathophysiology of necrotizing fasciitis as well as symptoms and treatment.* **They found** the single most important predictor of mortality is time to surgical debridement. **They conclude** timely diagnosis, broad-spectrum antibiotic therapy, and aggressive surgical debridement of affected tissue are imperative in management of this potentially fatal condition.

*Green et al. provide a comprehensive review of necrotizing fasciitis.* *They describe the etiology of necrotizing fasciitis,* **noting** polymicrobial infections are most common and group-A streptococcus is the most common cause of monomicrobial infections. **They conclude** surgical debridement is indicated if the treating surgeon has a high-index of suspicion of necrotizing fasciitis.

Illustration A demonstrates the LRINEC scoring system. *A score >6 has a 92% PPV of necrotizing fasciitis infection.(Wong et. al., Crit Care Med.* 2004)

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Group A Streptococcus pyogenes is the most common cause of monomicrobial necrotizing fasciitis
Answer 3: Hyperbaric oxygen (HBO) therapy is an adjunctive treatment option for patients with necrotizing fasciitis. HBO increases the partial pressure of oxygen in tissues and is particularly useful in treating infections by anaerobic bacteria, such as clostridia.
Answer 4: LRINEC scoring system includes CRP, not ESR. The remainder of the components are correct.
Answer 5: In the largest case series to date, overall mortality rate has been found to be approximately 30%
