## Question

Knee arthrodesis is an optimal salvage treatment in which of the following patients with a history of prior total knee arthroplasty (TKA)?

## Options

1. 58-year-old diabetic male with a chronic periprosthetic infection that failed previous debridement with component retention
2. 85-year-old female with a comminuted distal periprosthetic femur fracture with loose femoral component
3. 63-year-old obese male with aseptic loosening of the tibial and femoral components with substantial metaphyseal bone loss
4. 65-year-old female with recurrent extensor mechanism disruption now complicated by recurrent periprosthetic knee infection
5. 71-year-old male with symptomatic flexion instability that has failed isolated polyethylene exchange

## Correct option

**4. 65-year-old female with recurrent extensor mechanism disruption now complicated by recurrent periprosthetic knee infection**

## Answer explanation

Knee arthrodesis is a reasonable salvage procedure for chronic periprosthetic knee infections that have failed multiple staged revisions or for recurrent extensor mechanism disruption following multiple reconstruction attempts.

In the setting of a multiply failed extensor mechanism and recurrent infection (especially in the setting of a previous mesh reconstruction), knee arthrodesis or above-knee amputation may be the most reasonable options. Although knee arthrodesis is not without potential complications, it would improve the potential for infection clearance and maintains the patient's ability to ambulate. In general, knee arthrodesis is considered a good option in patients with chronic infections that have failed staged revisions and in patients with failed extensor mechanism reconstructions.

*Kuchinad et al. describes a series of 21 patients treated with knee arthrodesis for either persistent periprosthetic joint infection or failed extensor mechanism.* *In total, 16 patients were treated with Ilizarov frames and 5 with intramedullary nails.* *Fusion was achieved in 20/21 patients (95%), with 2 patients eventually requiring amputations.*

*MacDonald et al. perform an excellent review of knee arthrodesis as a salvage procedure for patients with a painful knee not amenable to reconstruction.* *They describe multiple methods of knee arthrodesis including using external fixators, compression plates, and intramedullary rods.* **They conclude** that for the carefully selected patient with realistic expectations, knee arthrodesis may relieve pain and obviate the need for additional surgery or extensive postoperative rehabilitation.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: A chronic periprosthetic infection that has failed debridement and implant retention (DAIR) should be treated with a 2-stage revision with an antibiotic spacer.
Answer 2: A distal periprosthetic femur fracture with a loose femoral component is best treated with a distal femoral replacement.
Answer 3: Aseptic loosening with femoral and tibial bone loss is generally treated with revision arthroplasty with supplemental metaphyseal fixation (cones vs sleeves).
Answer 5: Symptomatic flexion instability should be addressed with revision arthroplasty where the flexion gap is typically addressed through posterior augments on the femur
