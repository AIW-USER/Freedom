## Question

A 72-year-old retired accountant with a history of type 2 diabetes presents after sustaining a mechanical ground-level fall while doing yard work earlier in the day. Since her injury, she has had difficulty with weight-bearing in her bilateral lower extremities due to pain. Her pain is most significant at the thoracolumbar junction. Which of the following combinations of imaging and physical findings would MOST likely lead to a recommendation for non-operative management?

## Options

1. T12 Chance fracture, intact posterior ligamentous complex, intact neurological exam
2. L2 Burst fracture, intact posterior ligamentous complex, weakness with left hip flexion
3. L2 Burst fracture, indeterminate posterior ligamentous complex integrity, weakness with right hip flexion
4. L2 Chance fracture, widening of the interspinous distance, loss of motor and sensory function below L2 level
5. T12 Burst Fracture, intact posterior ligamentous complex, intact neurological exam

## Correct option

**5. T12 Burst Fracture, intact posterior ligamentous complex, intact neurological exam**

## Answer explanation

Thoracolumbar injuries with a Thoracolumbar Injury Classification and Severity Score (TLICS) less than 4 should be managed with non-operative treatment. A burst fracture with an intact posterior ligamentous complex and an intact neurological exam fits this description with a TLICS of 2.

The TLICS is a scoring system designed to prognosticate and guide medical decision-making in patients with thoracolumbar injuries. Points are assigned in distinct sub-categories, which include fracture morphology, posterior ligamentous complex integrity, and neurological exam. Total scores less than 4 are considered for non-operative management, while those greater than 4 are considered for operative management. Scores of exactly 4 may be considered for either non-operative or operative management. Importantly, treatment recommendations should rely on surgical experience and clinical judgment, and not solely on the TLICS' guidance.

*Wenjie et al. retrospectively assessed 120 patients with thoracolumbar fractures to assess and compare the feasibility of a modified TLICS system.* **They found** a slightly higher operating rate with the utilization of the original TLICS compared to the modified TLICS system (73.3% vs 79.2%), which adjusted the grading of the PLC integrity subcategory and added a subcategory for disc injury status. **They concluded** that the modified TLICS system is an improvement on the original and should be considered for wider adoption.

*Gonzales-Portillo et al. evaluated 272 patients with thoracolumbar fractures to characterize treatment patterns and outcomes associated with the utilization of the TLICS.* **They found** that patients with TLICS < 4 predominantly received non-operative treatment, those with TLICS = 4 mostly received surgery, and the majority of patients with TLICS > 4 received operative intervention, with all patients broadly having significant improvements in Visual Analogue Scores at follow-up. Patients who received surgery had an average healthcare cost over $121,000 greater than those who received conservative treatment. **They concluded** that patients broadly had improved pain after discharge regardless of treatment modality and those receiving surgical intervention had expectedly greater healthcare costs.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1. A T12 Chance fracture with intact PLC and neurological exam has a TLICS of 4 and may be treated with either non-operative or operative management
Answer 2. An L1 Burst fracture with intact PLC but weakness with hip flexion has a TLICS of 4 and may be treated with either non-operative or operative management
Answer 3. An L2 Burst fracture with indeterminate PLC integrity and weakness with hip flexion has a TLICS of 6. Operative management is indicated for this injury.
Answer 4. An L2 chance fracture with widening of the interspinous distance suggestive of a disrupted PLC and evidence of complete spinal cord injury has a TLICS of 9. Operative management is indicated for this injury.
