## Question

Incorrect statement regarding Dynamic Anterior stabilization of the Shoulder (DAS) is

## Options

1. It has been designed to treat patients with “chronic” anterior instability of shoulder
2. Patients with poor quality of labrum and glenohumeral ligaments significantly benefit
3. Procedure involves transferring the long head of the biceps (LHB) to the anterior glenoid margin, thereby creating a “sling effect”
4. It is well suitable arthroscopic alternative to Latarjet in patients with bone loss

## Correct option

**4. It is well suitable arthroscopic alternative to Latarjet in patients with bone loss**

## Answer explanation

Ans: D

DAS is contraindicated in significant bone defects, hence it cannot be alternative to Latarjet.

DYNAMIC ANTERIOR STABILIZATION

Dynamic anterior stabilization (DAS) is a procedure designed to treat CHRONIC anteroinferior glenohumeral instability cases (but without significant bone loss) where the quality of labrum and glenohumeral ligaments is expected to be poor. The procedure involves transferring the long head of the biceps (LHB) to the anterior glenoid margin, through a split in subscapularis, thereby creating a “sling effect” by using a conservative technique (See Fig below).

![[TO17121623-1.jpg]]

Indications for DAS are anteroinferior glenohumeral instability with limited bone defects, associated with SLAP, or in overhead athletes and throwers.

Conversely, contraindication for DAS would include the following: significant bone defects that preclude a soft tissue procedure, presence of LHB lesions, associated subscapularis lesion, and previous LHB tenotomy or tenodesis.

Hence, DAS perfectly fills the gap between soft tissue procedures and Bone block procedures.

Surgical technique

- Diagnostic soulder arthroscopy is done and labral deficiency identified.
- Anterior glenoid is prepared as in Bankart surgery.
- The LHB tendon is tenotomized, then exteriorized to whipstich the end.
- Subscapularis is splint in the middle
- LHB tenodesis to the anterior glenoid is now performed as follows: a drill is used to prepare a hole at 3 o’clock from anterior to posterior within the neck of the glenoid, 1.5 to 2.0 cm deep, depending on the length of the interference screw. The LHB tendon is then passed through the subscapularis split into the predrilled hole, to establish the “sling effect,” and fixed using a Tenodesis screw.
- Labral repair is added with available capsule ligamentous structures to establish the bumper effect.
