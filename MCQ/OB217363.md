## Question

A 46-year-old female presents with right groin pain that has progressively worsened over the preceding 12 months. She has tried physical therapy and three corticosteroid injections with decreasing relief. The physician notices a positive FADIR test during the physical examination and suspects a labral tear. An MRI of the affected hip reveals a labral tear and a small cam lesion of the proximal femur. Radiographs reveal acetabular dysplasia with Tönnis grade 2 changes. When discussing the treatment options with the patient, which of the following would preclude hip arthroscopy for joint preservation?

## Options

1. Three previous steroid injections
2. Age >45 years
3. Presence of a large cam lesion with a grade 2B subpsine
4. Tönnis grade 2 changes
5. Presence of acetabular dysplasia

## Correct option

**4. Tönnis grade 2 changes**

## Answer explanation

The presence of Tönnis grade 2 changes on radiographs suggest advanced arthrosis of the affected hip and is associated with a high rate of conversion to arthroplasty.

Symptomatic hip osteoarthritis is expected to increase in the coming years due to the aging population. First-line treatment consists of physical therapy, activity modifications, NSAIDs, and intra-articular injections. Surgical treatment can involve hip arthroscopy, for patients with degenerative labral tears and lack of significant radiographic disease, or arthroplasty. Patients with Tönnis grade 2 radiographic arthrosis have a high conversion to arthroplasty following arthroscopy. Patients >50 years of age have a higher conversion to total hip arthroplasty.

*Chandrasekaran et al. performed a prospective study of 1,079 patients undergoing hip arthroscopy with and without radiographic evidence of hip arthrosis.* **They reported** a significant improvement in patient-reported outcomes measures for all patients undergoing hip arthroscopy and a 7.73 odds ratio of conversation to arthroplasty in Tönnis grade-2 patients compared to grade 0. **They concluded** there is a minimal role for hip arthroscopy in patients with Tönnis grade 2 hips.

*Rees reviewed the management guidelines of hip arthritis endorsed by the AAOS, AAHKS, Hip Society, ACR, APTA, and POSNA.* *They provided strong evidence supporting the use of NSAIDs, intra-articular steroid injections, and physical therapy for the treatment of symptomatic hip arthritis.* *They further provided strong evidence against the use of hyaluronic acid injections due to lack of clinical benefit compared to placebo for pain, stiffness, and function.*

Incorrect answers
Answer 1: Previous steroid injections are not a contraindication for hip arthroscopy.
Answer 2: Age > 45 years is not a contraindication for hip arthroscopy.
Answer 3: The presence of a large cam lesion or a grade 2B subspine is not a contraindication for hip arthroscopy and can be directly addressed by the procedure.
Answer 5: The presence of acetabular dysplasia is not a contraindication for hip arthroscopy.
