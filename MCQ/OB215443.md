## Question

An 86-year-old female sustains a femoral neck fracture and undergoes uneventful hip hemiarthroplasty. On the first post-operative day, she ambulates 50 feet with physical therapy and her foley catheter is removed. She is tolerating fluids by mouth and her vital signs are normal. However, nursing staff reports she becomes increasingly confused at night and is easily agitated. Given the development of postoperative delirium, what is she most at risk for?

## Options

1. ICU admission
2. Increased hospital length of stay
3. Inadequate pain control
4. In-hospital mortality
5. Periprosthetic fracture

## Correct option

**2. Increased hospital length of stay**

## Answer explanation

Geriatric patients who sustain hip fractures and develop delirium postoperatively are at risk of increased hospital length of stay.

Hospital delirium is a common issue, affecting around 25% of geriatric patients with hip fracture. In this patient population, delirium has been linked to longer length of stay (LOS) and need for nursing facility admission. It has also been shown to increase long-term mortality, although this may relate more to patient comorbidities than development of delirium. Risk factors for developing delirium after geriatric hip fractures include increased age, dementia, and need for nursing facility pre-injury.

*Hecht et al. attempted to establish a predictive model to identify factors influencing LOS after geriatric hip fractures.* *They performed a multivariate analysis and determined that longer time to surgery and hospital delirium were the only factors in their model that increased the LOS.* *The authors used these results to create a predictive, patient-specific model for geriatric hip fractures and concluded that, although not perfect, their model allowed them to identify patients who deviated from expected stay lengths, allowing targeted improvement.*

*Smith et al. reviewed preoperative and perioperative factors that increased risk for patients developing delirium following hip fracture fixation.* **The authors noted** patient age >80 (OR 1.77), pre-admission need for nursing care (OR 2.65), and pre-admission dementia (OR 6.07) were significant risk factors for delirium after geriatric hip fracture. **They concluded** by suggesting that clinicians should be aware of these risk factors for developing delirium after hip fracture fixation and work in a multi-disciplinary fashion to mitigate them.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1,2,4,5: These outcomes are not directly related to the development of delirium after geriatric hip fracture fixation
