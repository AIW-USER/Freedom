## Question

With regard to automobile safety in children, which of the following is a consistent recommendation among the United States National Highway Traffic Safety Administration, Transport Canada, and the American Academy of Pediatrics?

## Options

1. A 15 lbs (6.8kg) 6-month-old boy should ride in a forward-facing car seat in the rear center seat
2. A 30 lbs (13.6kg) 3-year-old boy should ride in a booster seat in the rear center seat
3. A 55 lbs (24.9kg) 7-year-old girl should ride with a seat belt only directly behind the driver
4. A 75 lbs (34.0kg) 13-year-old girl riding in the front passenger seat should have the airbag turned on
5. A 75 lbs (34.0kg) 13-year-old girl riding in the front passenger seat should have the airbag turned off

## Correct option

**5. A 75 lbs (34.0kg) 13-year-old girl riding in the front passenger seat should have the airbag turned off**

## Answer explanation

All three organizations recommend children riding in the front passenger seat weighing less than 80lbs should have the airbag turned off.

In general, children should remain in a rear-facing seat in the rear center seat until at least 20 lbs or 1 year of age. Children should then remain in a forward-facing seat until at least 40lbs or 4 years of age. Children should then remain in a booster seat until at least 60-80lbs and 8 years of age. Children from 8 to 12 years old should then remain in the rear middle seat in a properly fitting seatbelt. At the age of 13 children may sit in the front seat with the airbags turned off for kids <80lbs and turned on for those >80lbs. Specific recommendations vary among organizations (Illustration A).

*Winston et al. reviewed crash data and performed phone surveys to evaluate the risk of premature transition to safety belts in children.* Those children in motor vehicle accidents wearing a seat belt (without a booster or child safety seat) were at a higher risk of significant injury when compared to those children in a child restraint system. **They conclude** premature graduation of children aged 2-5 years of age to a seat belt alone results in a higher risk of injury when compared to children in child restraint systems.

*Truong et al. reviewed the current recommendations in the United States and Canada with respect to automobile restraints for children.* All three major organizations of the United States Nationa Highway Traffic Safety Administration (USNHTSA), Transport Canada (TA), and the American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) recommend children under 80lbs sitting in the front passenger seat should have the airbag turned off.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: A child should remain in a rear-facing car seat in the rear center seat until at least 1 years of age and weighing at least 20lbs.
Answer 2: A child should remain in a front-facing car seat in the rear center seat until at least 4 years of age or weighing at least 40lbs.
Answer 3: This child should be in more than a simple seat belt restraint. The child meets criteria to ride in a booster seat, (over 40 pounds and older than 4 years of age), and should be seated in the rear center seat.
Answer 4: Riding in the front seat is typically not recommended until the age of 13 with the caveat of any child under 80 pounds in the front seat should have the airbag turned off.
