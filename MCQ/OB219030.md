## Question

An 11-year-old male patient presents to the emergency department with a surgical site infection in the setting of a recent open reduction internal fixation of an open tibial shaft fracture. He undergoes irrigation with debridement and subsequently develops necrotizing pneumonia. The organism that grew from intraoperative cultures is found to express the Panton-Valentine leukocidin (PVL) cytotoxin. Which of the following organisms is known to express this cytotoxin?

## Options

1. Staphylococcus epidermidis
2. Streptococcus pneumoniae
3. Streptococcus pyogenes
4. Staphylococcus aureus
5. Klebsiella pneumoniae

## Correct option

**4. Staphylococcus aureus**

## Answer explanation

Panton-Valentine leukocidin (PVL) is a cytotoxin that is among the most common virulence factors expressed by Staphylococcus aureus strains.

PVL-positive methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus (MRSA) has been associated with recurrent soft tissue infections, severe tissue necrosis, pneumonia, osteomyelitis, and poorer overall outcomes due to tissue damage. Although produced by <5% of S. aureus strains, the toxin is detected in large percentages of isolates that cause necrotic skin lesions and severe necrotizing pneumonia. Though commonly associated with community-acquired methicillin-resistant S. aureus (CA-MRSA), several outbreaks due to methicillin-susceptible S. aureus (MSSA) have also been reported.

*Lynch et al. performed a systemic review to investigate the efficacy of decolonization against PVL-positive S. aureus.* **They report** that there is no high-quality evidence to show whether decolonization is effective in reducing (re)infection or long-term carriage of PVL-positive S. aureus, and the low-quality evidence available indicates it may not be effective in eradicating carriage or reducing future disease. Furthermore, there may be risks associated with decolonization, e.g., potentially increased risk of infection from other microbes, opportunity costs, and negative impacts of repeated testing for asymptomatic carriage.

*Sina et al. characterized a group of genes responsible for toxin production and antibiotic resistance of S. aureus strains isolated from skin, soft tissue, and bone-related infections.* **They reported** that PVL was the most commonly produced virulence factor (70%), followed by staphylococcal enterotoxin B (44%). **They concluded** that PVL was strongly associated with pyomyositis and osteomyelitis.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1-3 and Answer 5: These organisms have not been found to produce the Panton-Valentine leukocidin (PVL) cytotoxin.
