## Question

A 52-year-old male sustains a talus fracture that is treated with immediate reduction and internal fixation. He subsequently develops talar dome avascular necrosis and is treated with the surgery shown in Figures A and B. He shows no evidence of healing at 12 months postoperatively and has continuous pain with ambulation; his incisions are well-healed and his subtalar motion remains full and pain-free upon examination. What is the most appropriate treatment for him at this time?

## Options

1. Use of an external bone stimulator
2. Dynamization of the implants to allow controlled compression
3. Removal of the implants and placement of a hindfoot arthrodesis nail or plate
4. Injection of platelet rich plasma
5. Revision ankle arthrodesis with bone grafting as needed

## Correct option

**5. Revision ankle arthrodesis with bone grafting as needed**

## Answer explanation

Nonunion of an ankle arthrodesis with either isolated screws or a plate and screw construct can be treated with revision arthrodesis with adjunct bone grafting as needed. Success rates with revision arthrodesis are high, and this can successfully return patients to functional ambulation.

Ankle arthrodesis nonunion workup requires evaluation for infection and metabolic causes of the nonunion. Imaging for a preoperative workup would include quality plain films; adjunct CT scan can be obtained if quantification of bone healing is needed, and labeled WBC scan can also be obtained if infection is a concern. If malalignment is also an issue, quality scanograms and/or contralateral films should be obtained to adequately template and plan for any corrective alignment procedures.

*Easley et al. retrospectively reviewed 45 patients who underwent revision ankle arthrodesis with internal fixation, hindfoot nailing, or use of ring fixation.* Revision fixation went onto union in 88.9% of patients, with ring external fixation resulting in the highest union rate. All patients who failed revision arthrodesis went on to transtibial amputation.

*Plaass et al.* **reported** that anterior double plating system was shown be a reliable method to achieve solid isolated tibiotalar arthrodesis, even in cases of failed total ankle arthroplasty. **They reported** 100% union rate in 29 patients and no major complications. They did report that cases that required allograft for bone loss took significantly longer to obtain union.

*Levine et al. retrospectively reviewed the treatment of a selected group of 23 patients with pseudoarthrosis after ankle arthrodesis who underwent revision arthrodesis.* **They reported** that 21 of 23 patients went onto union and 19 of 23 would be willing to undergo the procedure again if needed. Nine of 23 patients required additional joint arthrodesis at the time of the revision procedure (subtalar or triple arthrodesis).

Figures A and B show a well-aligned ankle fusion after talar neck fracture; full union of the ankle arthrodesis is not noted.

Incorrect Answers:
1: Use of a bone stimulator at 12 months postoperatively would not likely lead to union in this patient.
2: Dynamization of the plate and screw construct would lead to ankle instability.
3: The patient does not have subtalar pain on exam, or limitations in subtalar motion; this would mean that the addition of a subtalar arthrodesis is not needed.
4: Injection of platelet-rich plasma has not been shown to increase union in an ankle arthrodesis nonunion.
