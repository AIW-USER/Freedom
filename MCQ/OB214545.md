## Options

1. The current literature does not support allograft meniscal transplantation in isolation
2. He is beyond the age where the procedure will provide lasting benefits
3. He has yet to try a course of viscosupplementation
4. His limb alignment and articular cartilage loss will likely result in increased failure of the procedure
5. Based on his age and limb alignment, an isolated medial closing wedge tibial osteotomy is all that he needs

## Correct option

**4. His limb alignment and articular cartilage loss will likely result in increased failure of the procedure**

## Answer explanation

This patient's history, physical findings, and MRI scans indicate that a total medial meniscectomy was performed. Of the above responses, the most accurate is that an isolated meniscal allograft transplantation would likely fail given his varus lower extremity mechanical alignment and articular cartilage loss.

Partial or total meniscectomy has been demonstrated to result in increased progression of clinical and radiographic arthritis compared to meniscal preservation techniques. Isolated meniscal allograft transplantation has been shown to be most effective in patients with prior total meniscectomy, age <50 years, BMI <30 kg/m2, clinical symptoms of pain in the involved tibiofemoral compartment, 2 mm or more of tibiofemoral joint space on a 45-degree weight-bearing AP radiograph, ligamentous stability, neutral mechanical alignment, absence of articular cartilage loss, and no radiographic evidence of advanced arthritis. In the presence of lower extremity malalignment, a high tibial osteotomy (HTO) is often considered before or in conjunction with meniscal transplantation to correct the malalignment.

*Hede et al. investigated long term outcomes after partial versus total meniscectomy in a prospective RCT.* **They reported** that after one year, 90% of patients with partial meniscectomy had no complaints compared with the 80% with total meniscectomy, however, in the long term there was no statistically significant difference. Furthermore, patients with partial meniscectomy had higher functional scores. **They also highlighted** that radiological signs of knee degeneration increased with no difference between the two treatment groups.

*Gomoll et al. investigated the technique of combined meniscal transplantation, chondral repair, and osteotomy in patients with meniscal deficiency, focal arthritis, and malalignment.* **They reported** that at 2 years postoperatively, patients had significant improvements in the International Knee Documentation Committee score, Lysholm, and KOOS functional scores. **They concluded** that the combined treatment of meniscal transplantation, cartilage repair, and osteotomy exhibited promising clinical results of unicompartmental arthritis treatment in young patients.

*Verdonk et al. evaluated the results of a survival analysis of the clinical outcomes after transplantation of viable meniscal allograft.* **They reported** that the mean cumulative survival time (11.6 years) was identical for the medial and lateral allografts; the cumulative survival rates for the medial and lateral allografts at ten years were 74.2% and 69.8%, respectively. **They highlighted** that after combined HTO, the mean cumulative survival time and the cumulative survival rate for the medial allografts were 13.0 years and 83.3% at ten years, respectively. **They concluded** that meniscal allograft transplantation can significantly relieve pain and improve function of the knee joint.

Figure A depicts varus lower extremity mechanical alignment. Figure B demonstrates a lateral knee plain radiograph with mild degenerative changes. Figure C and D are MRI cuts demonstrating a previous medial total meniscectomy with articular cartilage loss.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The current literature actually supports allograft meniscal transplantation when the above criteria are met.
Answer 2: This is incorrect as isolated meniscal allograft transplantation has been demonstrated to be effective in patients 50 years or younger.
Answer 3: Viscosupplementation may provide some temporary relief but is not an appropriate long-term solution.
Answer 5: This is a varus-producing HTO and would exacerbate his current deformity.
