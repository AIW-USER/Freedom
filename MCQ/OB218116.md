## Question

A 72-year-old male presents to the clinic with worsening left hip and groin pain that no longer responds to NSAIDs. He denies any traumatic events, but recently completed ten sessions of physical therapy for a side-bending gait pattern without any improvement. The patient states he had received a total hip replacement approximately 10 years ago by another surgeon and that he felt good for the first couple of years. The operative report indicates a cobalt-chromium head implant. His presenting radiographs are shown in Figure A. Metal artifact reduction sequencing (MARS)-MRI shows an appreciable fluid collection around his hip implant as shown in Figure B. Review of his recent labs shows a cobalt level of 15.2 µg/L (average: 0.7-3.4 µg/L) and a chromium level of 3.1 µg/L (average: 0.5-2.5 µg/L). Which of the following is the root cause of this patient's underlying condition?

## Options

1. Having a metal-on-metal total hip arthroplasty
2. Excessive increase of global offset from the implant
3. Corrosion at the implant’s head-neck morse taper
4. Abductor musculature avulsion off the greater trochanter
5. Femoral stem aseptic loosening with subsidence

## Correct option

**3. Corrosion at the implant’s head-neck morse taper**

## Answer explanation

This patient has worsening pain, abductor insufficiency, and an aseptic lymphocyte-dominant vasculitis-associated lesion (ALVAL; also referred to as a pseudotumor). Collectively, these are findings consistent with corrosion at the implant’s head-neck morse taper (i.e. trunnionosis, Answer 3) resulting in an adverse reaction to the resultant metal debris.

Adverse reaction to metal debris (ARMD) is a unique cause of arthroplasty failure. ARMD classically results in ALVAL, occurring secondary to the host’s cell-mediated response to metal debris. Clinically, this may occur in one of two settings: (1) trunnionosis or (2) metal-on-metal (MOM) articulation hip replacements. The goal of revision surgery is to remove the failing articulation where metal debris is being generated. This is either at the head-neck junction in trunnionosis or between the MOM femoral head and acetabular components. In either case, it is imperative to perform adequate soft tissue debridement to remove metal debris and devitalized tissue to prevent recurrence. In addition, conversion to a ceramic-on-polyethylene articulation has been shown to impart the lowest risk for the recurrence of metal debris generation from the trunnion.

*Nam and colleagues performed a systematic review to determine the advantages and disadvantages of the primary imaging modalities (ultrasound, CT, and MRI) used in detecting wear-related corrosion problems after THA.* **They found** that there is minimal use of ultrasound, CT scans are best used for determining component positioning, and MRI is best for predicting both presence and severity of adverse local tissue reactions.

*Cooper and colleagues present a case series of patients experiencing clinically significant trunnionosis with metal-on-polyethylene implants.* **They reported** that irrigation, debridement, and head and liner exchange resulted in significant improvement in patient-reported clinical outcomes. **They noted** that some patients experienced abductor insufficiency from either the initial inflammatory process of trunnionosis or from the debridement. **The authors conclude** that the adverse local tissue reactions in trunnionosis are akin to that of metal-on-metal implant reactions.

Figure A shows AP and lateral radiographic imaging of a metal-on-polyethylene left total hip arthroplasty. There does not appear to be gross implant loosening or mal-positioning. Figure B shows a coronal image of the MARS-MRI sequencing with appreciable fluid collection around the underlying implant, indicative of an ALVAL.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Patients with clinically meaningful trunnion corrosion often demonstrate a differential elevation of serum Co to Cr ratio on the order of approximately 5:1. This is as compared to the relative 1:1 ratio seen in MOM implants. Further, there is obvious radiolucency between the femoral head and the acetabular cup, which is the underlying polyethylene liner.
Answer 2: Increasing the global offset can result in over-tensioning of the abductor musculature and lateral hip pain from trochanteric bursitis. However, that would not result in increased serum cobalt chromium ion levels and ALVAL formation.
Answer 4: Abductor muscle failure would lead to the Trendelenburg gait as seen in this patient. However, the cause is secondary to attritional rupture from the soft tissue disruption of ARMD.
Answer 5: Aseptic loosening and subsidence of the femoral implant would present clinically with thigh and startup pain. Furthermore, stem subsidence or loosening would likely be visible on radiographic imaging. In any case, aseptic loosening would not explain the elevated cobalt-chromium levels and ALVAL formation.
