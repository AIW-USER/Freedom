## Question

Figure A is the sagittal MRI of a 32-year-old male who was evaluated by the orthopedic trauma resident following an MVC in which he hit a tree. While no fractures were identified, the patient was found to have a tense effusion and bruising on the anterior aspect of his knee. Which of the following is true of the injured structure shown in Figure A?

![[q211766-a-pcl-injury.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Inserts superior to the articular margin of the tibia
2. Deficiency leads to patellofemoral and lateral compartment arthritis
3. Anterolateral bundle is tight in flexion, posteromedial bundle is tight in extension
4. Anterolateral bundle is tight in extension, posteromedial bundle is tight in flexion
5. Anteromedial bundle tight in flexion, posterolateral bundle is tight in extension

## Correct option

**3. Anterolateral bundle is tight in flexion, posteromedial bundle is tight in extension**

## Answer explanation

This patient sustained a PCL injury following a dashboard injury. The anterolateral bundle of the PCL is tight in flexion, while the posteromedial bundle is tight in extension.

PCL injuries can occur following a direct blow to the anterior tibia in a flexed knee as may occur when the knee hits the dashboard during an MVC. The PCL originates on the posterior tibial sulcus below the level of the articular surface and has a large insertion on the anterolateral aspect of the medial femoral condyle. It’s composed of two bundles, the anterolateral bundle that is tightest in flexion and the posteromedial bundle which is tight in extension. The anterolateral bundle is the most important posterior stabilizer to the knee in 90 degrees of flexion.

*Zawodny et al review the complications associated with posterior cruciate ligament surgery.* **They report** that these complications arise due to several factors including the relative infrequency of PCL injuries, the lack of knowledge and experience in their treatment, its proximity to the neurovascular bundle, technically demanding nature of the reconstruction. **They conclude** that the popliteal artery is at risk when drilling the tibial tunnel as it lies just posterior to the PCL insertion on the tibia.

*Voos et al review the posterior cruciate ligament anatomy, biomechanics and outcomes following repair.* *They report on the biomechanics of the two bundles including the anterolateral bundle being tight in flexion, and the posteromedial bundle being tight in extension.* **They conclude** that while laboratory and cadaveric studies have suggested double-bundle reconstruction may be better to restore normal knee kinematics, clinical studies have not revealed a difference.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The PCL attachment on the tibia is on the posterior tibial sulcus, which is distal to the articular margin.
Answer 2: PCL deficiency leads to patellofemoral and medial compartment arthritis given the varus alignment.
Answer 4: The anterolateral bundle is tight in flexion, while the posteromedial bundle is tight in extension.
Answer 5: The anteromedial and posterolateral bundles are structures of the ACL.
