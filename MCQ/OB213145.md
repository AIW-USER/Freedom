## Answer explanation

The most common form of inherited rhizomelic dwarfism is achondroplasia, which is caused by an autosomal dominant mutation in the FGFR3 gene. Figure B is a pedigree demonstrating autosomal dominant inheritance.

Achondroplasia is the most common skeletal dysplasia. It results from a G380 mutation of the FGFR3 (fibroblast growth factor receptor 3) gene, which is located on chromosome 4P. This gain-of-function mutation increases the inhibition of chondrocyte proliferation in the proliferative zone of the physis (and is therefore a quantitative cartilage defect), leading to a defective endochondral bone formation. The mutation in achondroplasia is usually sporadic but can also be inherited in an autosomal dominant fashion, conferring a 50% risk of inheriting the condition for each child (regardless of gender). Patients with achondroplasia present with rhizomelic shortening of the extremities and may have thoracolumbar kyphosis that resolves when the child begins to walk.

*Shirley et al. published a review on the manifestations and treatment of achondroplasia.* **They report** that achondroplasia is the most common skeletal dysplasia and is caused by a mutation of fibroblast growth factor receptor-3 (FGFR3). **They state** that thoracolumbar kyphosis is seen in most infants, but typically resolves when the child begins to walk.

*Beals et al. published a review on the diagnosis of skeletal dysplasias.* **They report** that diagnosis requires a simple measurement of height and calculation of proportionality, combined with a complete physical examination, appropriate radiographs, an investigation of the family pedigree, and occasionally laboratory studies. **They state** that a definitive diagnosis allows the treating physician to project the patient's ultimate height and to prognosticate about likely deformities and the risk of the recurrence of the condition in the family.

Figures A-E are pedigrees demonstrating autosomal recessive, autosomal dominant, X-linked recessive, mitochondral (maternal), and X-linked dominant inheritance, respectively.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Figure A shows an autosomal recessive inheritance pattern. An example of an autosomal recessive condition is Diastrophic dysplasia, results from a mutation in the SLC26A2.
Answer 3: Figure C shows a X-linked recessive inheritance pattern. Duchenne and Becker muscular dystrophies result from a X-linked recessive mutation in the Xp21.2 dystrophin gene that leads to an absence or deficiency of dystrophin proteins, respectively.
Answer 4: Figure D shows a mitochondrial (maternal) inheritance pattern. MERRF (myoclonic epilepsy with ragged red fibers) syndrome is acquired via mitochondrial (maternal) inheritance and is characterized by dementia, optic atrophy, bilateral deafness, peripheral neuropathy, spasticity, and multiple lipomas.
Answer 5: Figure E shows an X-linked dominant inheritance pattern. Vitamin D-resistant rickets is an example of a X-linked dominant mutation that results in a defect in the absorption of phosphorus by the renal tubules. It is characterized by short stature, genu varum, windswept deformity, physeal widening, low serum phosphorus, elevated alkaline phosphatase, and normal or low normal serum calcium.
