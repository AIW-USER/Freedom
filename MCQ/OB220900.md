## Question

A 5-year-old male presents for evaluation of gait abnormalities with a pelvis radiograph shown in Figure A. You are concerned that the patient has spondyloepiphyseal dysplasia (SED). Which of the following disease attributes distinguishes SED from multiple epiphyseal dysplasia?

![[q220900-a-pelvis.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Irregular ossification at multiple epiphyses
2. Genu valgum
3. Precocious hip arthritis
4. Short stature
5. Spine involvement

## Correct option

**5. Spine involvement**

## Answer explanation

Multiple epiphyseal dysplasia (MED) characteristically does not have spine involvement compared to spondyloepiphyseal dysplasia (SED) (Answer choice 5).

SED congenita is a disorder characterized by a mutation of the COL2A1 gene inherited in an autosomal dominant fashion. It presents with a constellation of findings, including cervical spine instability, spinal deformity, ocular deformities, hip deformities, and early degenerative joint disease. Radiographically, it is characterized by irregular ossification at multiple epiphyses, thoracic kyphoscoliosis, and flat vertebral bodies. MED is an autosomal dominant mutation in the COMP gene and has similar findings of irregular ossification; however, it does not involve the spine.

*Bayhan et al. conducted a retrospective review of children with SED who underwent valgus hip osteotomy for coxa vara.* **They found** consistent improvements in hip range of motion, pain, and radiographic alignment. **The authors concluded** that valgus hip osteotomy in children with SED leads to maintained function and good midterm outcomes.

*Wynne-Davies and Gormley published a descriptive article of patients suffering from skeletal dysplasias in Scotland, England, and Wales, and their orthopaedic manifestations over a 30-year period.* **They found** MED to be 3–4 times more prevalent than SED.

Figure A demonstrates an AP pelvis of a patient with SED. Irregular epiphyses are seen at the bilateral femoral heads.

Incorrect Answer choices:
Answer choices 1-4: Irregular ossification at multiple epiphyses, genu valgum, precocious hip arthritis, and short stature are present in both MED and SED.
