## Question

A 68-year-old female comes to your clinic for evaluation of hip osteoarthritis (OA). Her X-rays demonstrate end-stage OA, and the patient has trialed several conservative treatment measures without any significant improvement in her symptoms. She is interested in pursuing total hip arthroplasty (THA). However, she is concerned because her brother had the procedure last year and now requires a shoe lift because his leg was lengthened by the procedure. Which of the following factors is most likely to cause an increase in leg length after THA?

## Options

1. Medialization of the acetabular component
2. Placement of a high-offset femoral stem
3. Use of a short-necked femoral component
4. Very proximal neck cut
5. Use of a cemented instead of cementless femoral stem

## Correct option

**4. Very proximal neck cut**

## Answer explanation

This patient is concerned about increasing leg length during THA, which can occur with making a long (proximal) femoral neck cut.

When performing total joint arthroplasty preoperative planning and sound surgical technique are important to restore as much native anatomy as possible. The final component position can change several patient factors including range of motion, joint stability, abductor tensioning, leg lengthening, and joint reactive force. Leg length can traditionally be adjusted by neck cut length, femoral component neck length, and acetabular component superior/inferior placement. Abductor tensioning can be adjusted with medial/lateralization of acetabular components and femoral component offset. The combination of leg length and offset can affect the abductor tensioning and stability of the implants.

*Scheerlinck published a stepwise approach to templating for a THA.* These steps are: 1. Identify anatomical landmarks; 2. Assess the quality of the radiograph; 3. Identify mechanical references; 4. Optimize implant positioning to restore hip biomechanics. **The authors propose** that by following these steps surgeons can hopefully recognize difficult hips, and minimize complications.

*Desai et al. reviewed the causes of LLD in THA and techniques to avoid it.* **They state** LLD occurs in 1-27% of cases at an average of 3-17mm. LLD can result in gait disorders, general dissatisfaction, and neuritis. Techniques to avoid LLD discussed include preoperative templating, intraoperative calipers and reference point measurements, and intraoperative fluoroscopy with various reference aids.

*Colombi et al. reviewed THA planning.* **They emphasize** the importance of proper review of the radiographs to prevent misleading plans and go in-depth on how to evaluate X-rays. Templating techniques including acetone templating, 2D digital, and 3D digital with CT are reviewed with pros and cons explained. **They conclude** with a discussion on the spine-hip relationship.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Medialization of the acetabular component and use of a high-offset stem will affect abductor tension and joint reactive forces without adjusting leg length.
Answer 2: A high-offset femoral stem will increase abductor tension by moving the femur more lateral, while not increasing leg length.
Answer 3: A short neck femoral component will shorten leg length.
Answer 5: When properly performed a cemented versus uncemented femoral component should not adjust leg length.
