## Question

A 64-year-old female with a long-standing history of peripheral neuropathy presents for follow-up for a chronic left diabetic foot ulcer. The patient has been treating this with local wound care and one previous debridement by a podiatrist. She is currently wearing a stiff-soled shoe to offload the affected region. A current photograph of the ulcer is depicted in Figure A. She denies any fevers, chills, or purulent drainage from the wound. On examination, bone cannot be probed with a sterile Q-tip. The 2nd ray has hyperextension of the MTP joint with flexion of the DIP and PIP and is passively correctable. What is the most appropriate treatment at this time?

![[q215822-a-diabetic-ulcer.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Amputation through the 2nd MTP joint
2. Ulcer debridement
3. DIP fusion
4. Percutaneous flexor digitorum longus tenotomy
5. Weil osteotomy

## Correct option

**4. Percutaneous flexor digitorum longus tenotomy**

## Answer explanation

The patient is presenting with a diabetic foot ulcer at the tip of the 2nd toe and an associated claw toe deformity. This can be effectively treated with a percutaneous FDL tenotomy.

Claw toe deformity is the result of MTP hyperextension and associated flexion of the DIP and PIP joints. This leads to excessive loading of the tip of the affected toe, which can be problematic in neuropathic patients due to ulcer pathogenesis. Treatment of diabetic foot ulcers associated with claw toe deformities can be treated with either percutaneous flexor tenotomy (flexible deformity) or arthrodesis (rigid deformity). Percutaneous procedures have the benefit of being effective at deformity correction, low infection, low complication, and high healing rates.

*Tamir et al. performed a retrospective study of 83 patients treated with 160 percutaneous tenotomies for diabetic foot ulcers.* **They reported** a very low complication rate except for transfer lesions where an adjacent toe would become involved. **They concluded** percutaneous tenotomy is effective for treating toe ulcers in diabetic and neuropathic patients.

*Scott et al. performed a systematic review of five case series assessing percutaneous flexor tenotomies for the management of diabetic foot ulcers.* **They reported** high healing rates (92-100%) with low rates of infection or new deformity. **They reported** percutaneous flexor tenotomies are effective treatments for diabetic foot ulcers, but the current literature is of lower quality evidence.

Figure A is a clinical photograph of the left foot with a claw toe deformity and associated plantar ulcer on the tip of the 2nd ray.

Incorrect answers
Answer 1: An amputation through the 2nd MTP would be preferred if there was an infection with associated osteomyelitis, but based on the history and physical this is not the case.
Answer 2: An ulcer debridement alone would not be effective in this case as this has already been unsuccessfully performed and would not address the eccentric loading of the 2nd ray tip.
Answer 3: A DIP fusion is indicated in the setting of a rigid and/or arthritic deformity.
Answer 5: A Weil osteotomy addresses the deformity by shortening the ray and translating the metatarsal head plantar. Usually, this is performed for claw toe deformity of all four lesser toes in the absence of diabetic neuropathy and has the risk of floating toes and transfer metatarsalgia.
