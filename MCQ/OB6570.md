## Question

A patient reports persistent anterior shoulder pain following a forceful external rotation injury to the shoulder. An MRI scan is shown in Figure 4. The patient remains symptomatic despite 3 months of nonsurgical management. Treatment should now consist of

![[q6570-a-04.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. repair of the superior labrum.
2. isolated supraspinatus repair.
3. biceps recentering.
4. subscapularis repair and biceps tenodesis.
5. subscapularis repair and recentering of the biceps tendon.

## Correct option

**4. subscapularis repair and biceps tenodesis.**

## Answer explanation

The MRI scan reveals a subscapularis tear with a biceps that is out of the groove. Treatment in this patient is most predictable if the subscapularis is repaired. The biceps should either be tenodesed or tenotomized since it is unstable. Recentering of the biceps has been found to be unpredictable. Treatment of these lesions has been shown to have better results if the biceps is either released or tenodesed. This prevents recurrent biceps symptoms that can be source of surgical failure.
