## Options

1. Screw fixation of of joints 1, 2, 3, 4, & 5
2. Plating of joints 1, 2, 3, & 4; K-wire fixation of joint 5
3. Plating of joints 1, 2; K-wire fixation of joints 3, 4, & 5
4. Plating of joints 1, 2, & 3; K-wire fixation of joints 4 & 5
5. Screw fixation of joints 1, 2, 3, 4; K-wire fixation of joint 5

## Correct option

**4. Plating of joints 1, 2, & 3; K-wire fixation of joints 4 & 5**

## Answer explanation

The standard treatment for Lisfranc injuries is rigid fixation of the 1, 2, & 3 tarsometatarsal, (TMT), joints with K-wire immobilization of joints 4 & 5.

Tarsometatarsal joint fracture-dislocations necessitate early diagnosis, prompt anatomical reduction, and stable internal fixation. Historically, reduction and screw fixation of the medial 3 tarsometatarsal joints was performed. There has been evidence to show that stiff immobilization of all five TMT joints should be avoided due to poor functional outcomes. The 4 & 5 TMT joints are more mobile than the medial 3 TMT joints, so they are normally immobilized with K-wires, that are subsequently removed after a period of time. Though controversy remains on whether or not primary arthrodesis of these injuries is indicated, arthrodesis is the preferred choice in the cases of failed fixation or chronic/missed injuries. To perform an arthrodesis, the joint is exposed and the cartilage is denuded prior to fixation.

*Sangeorzan et al. performed a retrospective study reporting the results of 16 patients who failed initial treatment of TMT fracture-dislocations.* These patients were treated with fusion of the medial 3 TMT joints with lag screws. **They found** 11 patients had excellent results while 5 had poor results. **They also found** that injuries that occurred in the workplace and those that incurred a long delay until treatment showed a significant negative correlation to outcome.

*Kuo et al. performed a retrospective study of patients who underwent open reduction and screw fixation of a Lisfranc injury over 7 years.* *48 patients were included in the study and 12 had post-traumatic osteoarthritis of the TMT joints.* Six ultimately required arthrodeses. **The authors conclude** that stable anatomical reduction of fracture-dislocations of the Lisfranc joint leads to the best long-term outcomes.

*Rammelt et al. performed a cohort study over five years comparing primary open reduction and internal fixation in 22 patients with secondary corrective arthrodesis in 22 patients who presented with painful malunion at a mean of 22 months after Lisfranc injury.* **They found** that treatment by open reduction and internal fixation of tarsometatarsal fracture-dislocations leads to improved functional results, earlier return to work and greater patient satisfaction than secondary corrective arthrodesis, which remains a useful salvage procedure providing significant relief of pain and improvement in function.

Figures A, B, and C are the respective AP, oblique, and lateral radiographs of the patient's foot demonstrating a homolateral Lisfranc fracture/dislocation. Figure D is a normal oblique foot radiograph with the TMT joints labeled.
