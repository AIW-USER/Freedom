Chapter: General Orthopedics MCQs — 3D Printing in Orthopaedics and Patient Specific Implants

## Question

When manufacturing patient-specific cutting guides (PSI) for total knee arthroplasty (TKA) using structural polymers, which technology and material pairing ensures the necessary dimensional stability to survive autoclave steam sterilization without warping?

## Options

1. Fused Deposition Modelling (FDM) using standard Polylactic Acid (PLA).
2. Selective Laser Sintering (SLS) or Stereolithography (SLA) utilizing medical-grade polyamide (Nylon) or biocompatible photopolymers with a heat deflection temperature (HDT) above 135°C.
3. Direct Metal Laser Sintering (DMLS) using Ultra-High-Molecular-Weight Polyethylene (UHMWPE) powder.
4. Binder Jetting utilizing a calcium phosphate ceramic base with a cyanoacrylate binder.

## Correct option

**2. Selective Laser Sintering (SLS) or Stereolithography (SLA) utilizing medical-grade polyamide (Nylon) or biocompatible photopolymers with a heat deflection temperature (HDT) above 135°C.**

## Answer explanation

Patient-specific surgical jigs must
precisely fit the patient’s native anatomy based on preoperative CT or MRI
planning. Because these guides enter the sterile field, they must undergo
sterilization—most commonly autoclave steam sterilization at 121°C to 134°C.
Material Properties: The polymer used must possess a Heat Deflection Temperature (HDT)
or glass transition temperature (Tg) safely above standard autoclaving
temperatures to prevent geometric distortion. Medical-grade polyamides
processed via SLS or specific biocompatible resins processed via SLA meet these
criteria, providing excellent fidelity, rigidity against cortical bone, and
thermal resistance.
Why others are incorrect: Standard PLA used in commercial FDM (A) has a low glass transition
temperature (~60°C) and will severely deform in an autoclave. UHMWPE (C) cannot
be processed via DMLS, which is strictly a metallic powder bed fusion
technology. Binder jetting with ceramic/cyanoacrylate (D) lacks the mechanical
shear strength required to guide oscillating surgical saws without shedding
particulate debris.
