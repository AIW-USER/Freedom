## Question

A 14-year-old immigrant from a developing country had complained of pain playing soccer 4 months prior. Radiographs at that time were read as normal and he was given a prescription for NSAIDs. More recently, his parents noticed he is limping and complaining of pain after playing games. He has had some intermittent fevers and swelling around his thigh. A complete blood workup is performed. ## Options

1. Biopsy
2. Genetic screening for EXT1 and EXT2 gene mutations
3. NSAIDS and single-dose radiation therapy
4. Intravenous antibiotics
5. Observation

## Correct option

**1. Biopsy**

## Answer explanation

This patient has intramedullary osteosarcoma. Biopsy should be performed to obtain tissue diagnosis prior to initiating treatment. Red herring injuries can delay diagnosis. Although uncommon, osteosarcoma can present with fever and night sweats.

Intramedullary osteosarcoma is the most common primary sarcoma of bone and often occurs in children and young adults. The distal femur and proximal tibia are most commonly involved. Differentials include Ewing sarcoma and osteomyelitis.

*Messerschmitt et al. reviewed intramedullary osteosarcoma.* Survival rates exceed 70% when a multidisciplinary approach is used. For high-grade osteosarcoma, treatment includes neoadjuvant chemotherapy, wide surgical resection, and adjuvant chemotherapy. The aim of chemotherapy is to treat detectable or presumed metastases and decrease the size of the primary tumor by reducing vascularity, making limb-salvage technically easier by promoting tumor demarcation from surrounding tissues.

Figure A comprises AP and lateral knee radiographs showing mineralized cloud like periosteal reaction along the medial metaphysis and the mineralized metaphyseal matrix, characteristic of osteosarcoma. Figure B comprises coronal and sagittal cut T2-weighted MRI images showing the intramedullary extent of the tumor. Illustration A shows tumor cells with significant atypia, high nuclear to cytoplasmic ratio and abnormal mitotic figures surrounded by "lacey" osteoid.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: EXT1 and EXT2 gene mutations are present in multiple hereditary exostosis.
Answer 3: NSAIDS and perioperative radiation are used for heterotopic ossification prophylaxis.
Answer 4: It would be erroneous to assume a diagnosis of osteomyelitis and initiate empiric antibiotic treatment without first excluding a malignant process with tissue biopsy.
Answer 5: Observation for a malignant process is contraindicated.
