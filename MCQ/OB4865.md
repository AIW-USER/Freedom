## Question

A 9-year-old boy fell on his outstretched right hand. He has a closed injury and is neurovascularly intact. Injury films are shown in Figures A and B. The patient undergoes an anatomic closed reduction in the emergency department. What would the expected outcome be if this patient were treated in a short arm cast compared to long arm cast?

## Options

1. High rate of post-reduction swelling
2. More likely to interfere with daily activities
3. Increased pain medication requirements
4. More likely to require re-manipulation
5. No difference in outcomes

## Correct option

**5. No difference in outcomes**

## Answer explanation

The patient has sustained an angulated distal diaphyseal both bone forearm fracture. Once successfully reduced, this patient should be treated in a cast with a short arm cast as it has similar results as a long arm cast for these fractures with fewer complications.

Most pediatric forearm fractures can be treated non-operatively with a closed reduction and cast immobilization. As stated in the articles below, short arm casts for distal both bone forearm fractures are as effective as long arm casts; however, short arm casts have fewer complications associated with them. General indications for operative management are bayonet apposition in children older than 10 years of age, angulation >15 degrees and rotation >45 degrees in children <10 years, angulation >10 degrees and rotation >30 degrees in children >10 years, and open fracture. Less angulation is accepted for proximal fractures.

*Paneru et al. randomized 42 children aged 4-12 to an above-elbow group and 43 to a below-elbow group.* **They found** that pain and swelling on the next day and one week after reduction was significantly higher in the above-elbow group. As well, patients in the above-elbow group were more likely to undergo a re-manipulation than the below-elbow group (9.5% vs. 2.3%, p=0.173).

*Webb et al. performed an RCT comparing short arm and long arm plaster casts for displaced distal both bone forearm fractures.* **They concluded** that a well-molded short arm cast was equally as effective as a long-arm cast, and interfered less with daily activity. They stressed the need for proper cast molding, indicated by a lower cast index. The ideal cast index is less than 0.8.

*Bohm et al. performed an RCT comparing below- and above-elbow casts for distal both bone forearm fractures.* **They found** that below-elbow casts were as efficacious as above-elbow casts in maintaining the reduction.

Figures A and B show AP and lateral radiographs of a distal both bone forearm fracture. Illustration A demonstrates how to measure the cast index (dividing the sagittal cast width (A) by the coronal cast width (B) at the fracture site). Illustration B demonstrates a typical reduction maneuver: 1) reproduce or exaggerate deformity to unlock fragments, 2) reduce fragments using a periosteal hinge, 3) correct the rotational deformity.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1-4: Short arm casts have been shown to be equally efficacious in treating pediatric distal both bone forearm fracture with less pain and swelling and fewer limitations on daily activities.
