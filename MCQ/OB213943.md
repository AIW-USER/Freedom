A 46-year-old male undergoes interposition arthroplasty with a synthetic cartilage implant for the condition depicted in Figures A and B. In regards to this treatment, which of the following is true?

## Options

1. Requires prolonged post-operative weight bearing restrictions
2. Shorter time to maximal pain reduction
3. Contraindicated in patients with moderate osteophyte formation and less than 50% joint space loss
4. Indicated in patients with hallux valgus of 30 degrees
5. Equivalent pain reduction compared to arthrodesis

## Correct option

**5. Equivalent pain reduction compared to arthrodesis**

## Answer explanation

The patient in this question has undergone a synthetic cartilage interpositional arthroplasty for hallux rigidus as depicted in Figures A and B. This has been shown to have equivalent pain reduction in comparison to arthrodesis.

People with hallux rigidus have pain, swelling, and limited motion at the 1st metatarsal-phalangeal joint. Initial treatment includes activity modification, Morton extension, or use of a stiff sole shoe. Operative treatments include joint debridement with synovectomy, dorsal cheilectomy, a dorsal closing wedge of the proximal phalanx, Keller procedure, Silicone arthroplasty, Interpositional arthroplasty, or arthrodesis. Interpositional arthroplasty is an option for patients wanting to maintain range of motion and has been shown to have equivalent functional outcomes and pain reduction to arthrodesis. Arthrodesis is indicated in patients with more significant deformities, failed previous arthroplasty, and a more significant stage of arthritis.

*Baumhauer et al. compared the efficacy of a hydrogel implant (Cartiva) to arthrodesis for hallux rigidus.* *They looked at 152 implants and 50 arthrodesis patients and found that there were equivalent outcomes for pain, function based on the Foot and Ankle Ability Measure (FAAM), and safety.* **They concluded** that their hydrogel implant was an excellent alternative to arthrodesis with less than 10% revision at 2 years.

*Daniels et al. retrospectively looked at 5-year outcomes of the hydrogel implant (Cartiva).* *They looked at 27 patients that underwent interpositional arthroplasty with Cartiva and found significant improvements in pain, SF-36, FAAM scores, and activities of daily living.* No patients had radiographic changes in implant position, loosening, wear, or subsidence. **They concluded** that at 5 years the Cartiva implant has excellent survivorship.

*Cassinelli et al. retrospectively looked at 60 patients with a total of 64 hydrogel synthetic cartilage implants (Cartiva) at an average follow-up of 18.5 months.* **They found** 9 were very satisfied, 18 satisfied, 13 neutral, 7 unsatisfied, and 17 very unsatisfied. Overall, there was a 20% re-operation rate with an 8% rate of arthrodesis conversion. **They concluded** that it resulted in neutral satisfaction, mild pain, and physical dysfunction on early follow-up.

Figures A and B are AP and lateral weight-bearing radiographs of a foot demonstrating degeneration of the first metatarsal phalangeal joint (hallux rigidus) with a dorsal osteophyte.

Illustration A is a Table describing the Coughlin and Shurnas Classification for Hallux Rigidus. Illustrations B&C depict the intraoperative use of synthetic cartilage interpositional arthroplasty of the 1st MTP joint.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Interpositional arthroplasty does not have prolonged weight-bearing restrictions as compared to arthrodesis
Answer 2: Interpositional arthroplasty does not have a shorter time to maximal pain reduction. In fact, it has a longer time to maximal pain reduction.
Answer 3: Moderate dorsal osteophyte and less than 50% joint space loss describes Grade 2 Coughlin and Shurnas hallucinate rigidus. Interpositional arthroplasty has not been shown to be contraindicated for Grade 2 Coughlin and Shurnas hallux rigidus.
Answer 4: Interpositional arthroplasty is not indicated in patients with significant deformity (Hallux varus/valgus greater than 20 degrees).
