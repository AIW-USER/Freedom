## Question

After receiving a cortisone injection, which of the following variables has the greatest association with postoperative infection with arthroscopic rotator cuff repair?

## Options

1. Amount of injected cortisone
2. Injection within 1 month of surgery
3. Location (intra-articular vs subacromial) of injection
4. Number of prior cortisone injections
5. Type of corticosteroid utilized

## Correct option

**2. Injection within 1 month of surgery**

## Answer explanation

A cortisone injection within 1-3 months of arthroscopic rotator cuff repair has been shown to increase postoperative infection.

Cortisone injections are frequently utilized as a means of nonoperative management for several shoulder pathologies. However, timing of its use must be considered if surgical intervention is planned. An increased rate in postoperative infection has been shown in patients who received a shoulder injection within 1-3 months of surgery; a 65% to 75% increased rate shown within 1 month of surgery. It is recommended that surgery be delayed by at least 3 months after injection, to mitigate this risk. Patients who received an injection more than 3 months prior to surgery have the same risk of infection as those who did not have an injection.

*Werner et al. reviewed the association of preoperative injection before shoulder arthroscopy and arthroplasty with the incidence of postoperative infection.* **They reported** an incidence of infection after arthroscopy or arthroplasty at 3 months and 6 months was significantly higher in patients who underwent injection within 3 months before the procedure compared with controls. **They concluded** that there was a significant increase in postoperative infection in patients who underwent injection within 3 months before shoulder arthroscopy and arthroplasty, which was not noted when shoulder arthroscopy or arthroplasty occurred >3 months after injection.

*Forsythe et al. reviewed the temporal relationship between corticosteroid injections and the risk of surgical site infection after arthroscopic rotator cuff repair.* **They report** that patients receiving an injection within 1 month prior to operative management had a significantly higher rate of surgical site infection compared with the control group. **They concluded** that injections within 1 month of arthroscopic rotator cuff repair significantly increases the risk of surgical site infection.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1,3-5: These variables have not been shown to increase the risk of postoperative infection following arthroscopic rotator cuff repair as has receiving a cortisone injection within 1-3 months of surgery
