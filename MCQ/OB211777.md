## Question

## Answer explanation

This patient has likely sustained a dashboard knee injury during the rear-end collision resulting in injury to his posterior cruciate ligament (PCL). Untreated PCL deficiency most commonly results in increased degenerative changes to the medial and patellofemoral compartments (Figure C).

PCL injuries are rare in isolation and more commonly occur in the multi-ligament injured knee. Patients with isolated PCL injuries often clinically tolerate the PCL-deficient knee, however, this deficiency results in altered loads and kinematics during functional activities. These altered kinematics include load shifts to the patellofemoral and medial (due to increased varus alignment with PCL deficiency) knee compartments, resulting in degenerative changes. Surgical reconstruction may delay this progression of degenerative arthritis when compared to nonoperative treatment.

*Zawodny et al. reviewed PCL surgery complications.* **They reported** that the most common reasons for complications include the relative rarity of PCL injuries, the lack of knowledge and experience of providers treating them, the proximity of neurovascular structures to the PCL, and the technically demanding nature of reconstructive procedures. **They emphasized** the increased danger to the popliteal artery during drilling of the tibial tunnel as it lies just posterior to the PCL insertion on the tibia.

*Voos et al. review the anatomy, biomechanics and outcomes following repair of the PCL.* **They highlighted** the biomechanics of the anterolateral bundle (tight in flexion) and the posteromedial bundle (tight in extension). **They concluded** clinical studies have not revealed a difference in single versus double-bundle reconstruction, although laboratory and cadaveric studies demonstrated better restoration of normal knee kinematics with double-bundle reconstruction.

Figure A is a T2 sagittal MRI demonstrating an isolated PCL injury. Figure B demonstrates isolated patellofemoral compartment arthritis. Figure C demonstrates medial and patellofemoral compartment knee arthritis. Figure D demonstrates isolated lateral compartment arthritis. Figure E demonstrates a normal lateral knee radiograph. Figure F demonstrates lateral compartment knee arthritis without any patellofemoral disease.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The medial (not lateral) AND patellofemoral compartments are classically involved. It would be rare for PCL insufficiency to present with isolated patellofemoral arthrosis.
Answer 3: This represents isolated lateral compartment arthritis. The medial and patellofemoralcompartments are classically involved.
Answer 4: While short term radiographs may show no degenerative changes on radiographs, medial and patellofemoral compartments are classically involved in the long term.
Answer 5: The medial and patellofemoral compartments are classically involved.
