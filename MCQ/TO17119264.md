Chapter: Arthroscopy MCQs — Multiligamentous Knee Injuries

## Question

In concomitant ACL and PCL injury repair order will be (FNB Pattern Paper 2017)

## Options

1. Simultaneous reconstruction of both PCL and ACL immediately after injury
2. ACL reconstruction after 2 weeks and PCL after 4 weeks
3. Simultaneous reconstruction after 2-4 weeks
4. Immobilize knee for 4 weeks and then ACL reconstruction

## Correct option

**3. Simultaneous reconstruction after 2-4 weeks**

## Answer explanation

Principles of sequence of tensioning and fixation in a multi-ligamentous injured knee:

1. Many biomechanical studies have reported that in a posterolateral corner deficient knee, tension during fixation of the ACL graft increased external tibial rotation of the tibia. Therefore, the posterolateral corner should be fixed prior to the ACL to avoid external tibial rotation.

2. The PCL should be fixed prior to the ACL to best restore graft forces.

 3. The preferred fixation sequence is: the anterolateral bundle of the PCL at 90° to restore the normal tibial step-off, the posteromedial bundle of the PCL in extension, (70-90 degree in single bundle PCL reconstruction) the LCL at 20°−30° of knee flexion and a slight valgus force, followed by the rest of the PLC structures at 60° of flexion and neutral rotation, the ACL at 30 degrees flexion and finally the posteromedial corner.

4. In a bicruciate reconstruction PCL is tensioned at 0 degree and fixed at 70-90 degrees first followed by ACL tensioning at 0 degree and fixation at 30 degrees.

References: Wentorf FA, LaPrade RF, Lewis JL, et al. The influence of the integrity of posterolateral structures on tibiofemoral orientation when an anterior cruciate ligament graft is tensioned. Am J Sports Med 2002; 30:796–9

Markolf KL, O'Neill G, Jackson SR, et al. Reconstruction of knees with combined cruciate deficiencies: a biomechanical study. J Bone Joint Surg Am 2003;85-A:1768–74

Levy BA. CORR Insights®: Does sequence of graft tensioning affect outcomes in combined anterior and posterior cruciate ligament reconstructions? Clin Ortho Relate Res 2015; 473:244–5

Timing of surgery in a multi-ligamentous knee injury:

No uniformity of opinion exists with respect to the optimal timing of surgery and the specifics of ligament repair/reconstruction.

1. ACL plus PCL plus collateral ligaments: Currently, most authors recommend simultaneous bicruciate and collateral ligament reconstruction/repair performed at approximately 2-4 weeks from injury.

2. ACL plus MCL injury: Most of the MCL tear can be managed conservatively.

Grade 1 MCL – It is guided by ACL injury. ACL surgery at two weeks; no pre-surgery motion restriction.

Grade 2 MCL – ACL surgery within two weeks; before surgery a seven-day period of motion limitation (brace locked at 30 degrees and after seven days the brace is unlocked to full motion).

Grade 3 MCL – ACL surgery at three weeks with a 10-day motion restriction period (brace locked at 30 degrees and unlocked after 14 days).

3. ACL plus PCL: Simultaneous ACL and PCL reconstruction after 2-4 weeks.

Answer: 3
