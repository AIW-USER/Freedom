## Question

A 45-year-old male presents to the emergency department with complaints of low back pain which developed after visiting a trampoline park with his children. He states that the pain radiates down his left leg when he bends over. When asked to describe the location of his radicular symptoms, he notes that the pain and numbness go down the anteromedial leg into the medial aspect of his foot. Using Figure A, which of the following locations of compression is most likely causing his symptoms?

![[q219302-a-lumbar-mri.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Location A at L2-3
2. Location A at L3-4
3. Location A at L4-5
4. Location B at L3-4
5. Location B at L5-S1

## Correct option

**2. Location A at L3-4**

## Answer explanation

A paracentral disc herniation at L3-4 would cause radicular symptoms in the traversing L4 nerve distribution, which would cause pain and numbness in the anteromedial leg/foot.

Lumbar disc herniations occur when there is tearing of the annulus fibrosus that leads to herniation of the nucleus pulposus. This herniation causes local compression on either the traversing or the exiting nerve root. This depends on the location of the herniation. If it occurs more centrally through the weakest part of the posterior longitudinal ligament, it usually affects the traversing nerve root whereas if the herniation occurs in the foramen it causes compression on the exiting nerve root. The relationship of the nerve roots with the adjacent discs in the lumbar spine is important to understand. In the lumbar spine, nerve roots exit below their associated disc (i.e. L3 root exits between L3-4). The exiting nerve root is affected when there is a far lateral disc herniation that causes compression in the foramen. With paracentral herniations, the traversing nerve root (L4 at L3-4) is typically affected.

*Amin et al. reviewed lumbar disc herniation including current concepts and future directions.* **They note** that minimally invasive surgical approaches continue to improve and demonstrate good mid-term outcomes and they expect these techniques to continue to evolve. They also discuss advances in molecular research concerning disc herniation which explores the roles of inflammatory and infectious alterations in the process.

*Schoenfeld and Weiner looked at the evidence-based treatment of lumbar disc herniations.* **They noted** that six weeks of conservative care were recommended but if this fails, surgical intervention may be undertaken. **They concluded** that surgical intervention resulted in more rapid relief of symptoms and restoration of function even though many patients will eventually improve with nonoperative management.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: A paracentral herniation at L2-3 would cause radicular symptoms in the L3 nerve root distribution, causing symptoms in the anteromedial thigh.
Answer 3: A paracentral herniation at L4-5 would cause radicular symptoms in the L5 nerve root distribution, causing symptoms in the dorsum of the foot and lateral leg.
Answer 4: A far lateral herniation at L3-4 would cause radicular symptoms in the L3 nerve root distribution, causing symptoms in the anteromedial thigh.
Answer 5: A far lateral herniation at L5-S1 would cause radicular symptoms in the L5 nerve root distribution, causing symptoms in the dorsum of the foot and lateral leg.
