## Question

A 52-year-old jackhammer operator has persistent wrist pain and decreased range of motion, particularly after long days on the road. His primary care physician diagnoses the laborer with a wrist sprain and places him into a short arm cast. After continued symptoms, a MRI is obtained by a local hand surgeon (Figures A and B). What is the etiology of the hireling's condition?

## Options

1. Capitate avascular necrosis (AVN)
2. Kienbock's disease
3. Preiser's disease
4. Carpal coalition
5. Kohler disease

## Correct option

**1. Capitate avascular necrosis (AVN)**

## Answer explanation

The MRI demonstrates capitate AVN.

AVN of the capitate may be secondary to trauma, vascular congestion, or hypermobility. Immobilization is the ideal initial treatment. However, symptoms that are recalcitrant to non-operative management may be treated surgically with a vascularized graft, partial excision and tendon interposition, or a limited carpal fusion. The literature has few cases and the outcomes of surgical management are guarded.

*Peters et al. reviewed 6 cases of capitate AVN.* Ultimately 4 were treated with SC fusion and 2 had a 4CF. **The authors reported** good-excellent results in 3, fair in 2, and poor in 1. **They concluded** that arthrodesis most reliably produced the best results.

*Lapinsky and Mack reviewed a single case of capitate AVN in a 20-year-old female.* Intraoperatively, **the authors found** that only the proximal pole of the capitate was cystic and non-viable. As a result, the proximal pole was excised and replaced with an interposed graft. The patient continued to have pain post-operatively and went on to an eventual wrist arthrodesis.

*Hattori et al. reported a single case of the surgical treatment of capitate AVN with a vascularized pedicled bone graft from the dorsal distal radius.* (4th extra-compartmental artery) Post-operatively at one year, **the authors reported** excellent symptomatic relief and that the patient was able to return to playing baseball.

Figures A and B demonstrate T1 and T2 MRI, respectively, of a wrist with AVN of the capitate.

Answer 2: Kienbock's disease is AVN of the lunate.
Answer 3: Preiser's disease is AVN of the scaphoid.
Answer 4: A carpal coalition is not demonstrated in the images in this question. Among carpal coalitions, LT is the most common, affecting 1% of the population, with 60% of these patients having bilateral LT coalitions. Carpal coalitions are most common among those of West African descent.
Answer 5: Kohler disease is AVN of the navicular.
