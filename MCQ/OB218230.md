## Question

A 76-year-old female presents with worsening back pain and bilateral lower extremity neurogenic claudication that has progressively worsened over the last 2 years. Additionally, she feels as if she is having increasing difficulty standing fully erect which makes it difficult for her to walk meaningful distances. Her back pain, posture issues, and neurogenic claudication are all equally bothersome. She has tried alleviating her symptoms with physical therapy, epidural steroid injections, and a monitored opioid regimen with pain management but has not achieved lasting relief. She has a history of osteoporosis that is currently treated with alendronate for the last 4 years. Her most recent DEXA was significant for a T-score of -3.2 in the left femoral neck. The patient is now considering surgical treatment for her symptoms. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step in treatment?

## Options

1. Continue alendronate and proceed with T11-pelvis posterior instrumented fusion and decompression
2. Stop alendronate and proceed with T11-pelvis posterior instrumented fusion and decompression
3. Switch from alendronate to vitamin D/calcium supplementation and proceed with T11-pelvis posterior instrumented fusion and decompression
4. Referral to bone health clinic to initiate teriparatide treatment
5. Switch to denosumab and proceed with T11-pelvis posterior instrumented fusion and decompression

## Correct option

**4. Referral to bone health clinic to initiate teriparatide treatment**

## Answer explanation

The patient is presenting with osteoporosis after being on antiresorptive treatment with neurogenic claudication related to degenerative spinal deformity. Referral to a bone health clinic to initiate anabolic bone treatment (teriparatide) is recommended to reduce osteoporosis-related complications with spinal fusion (Answer 4).

Adult spinal deformity is a complex form of spinal pathology that requires treatment of spinal alignment correction, neurologic decompression, consideration of bone density, and life expectancy/treatment goals. Treatment often includes multilevel spinal fusion with long constructs to address deformity correction and stabilize the spondylotic segments. In the presence of osteoporosis, pseudoarthrosis rates can be staggeringly high and fraught with mechanical complications. Patients with osteoporosis (T-score < -2.5) should be assessed by a bone health liaison to determine if an anabolic bone treatment regimen should be implemented prior to surgery.

*Huang et al. performed an animal study investigating the effects of alendronate on posterolateral lumbar fusion in rats.* **They showed** that alendronate inhibited spine fusion, as evidenced by lower fusion rates compared to the control group. Radiographic and histomorphometric analyses indicated that alendronate led to larger and denser fusion masses but inhibited bone graft resorption and incorporation. **The authors recommend** avoiding alendronate in patients undergoing spine arthrodesis until fusion is achieved.

*Lehman et al. studied the effect of alendronate sodium on spinal fusion in a rabbit model.* **The results showed** that alendronate sodium inhibited or delayed bone fusion, as evidenced by lower fusion rates and histological scores compared to the control group. **The authors determined** that discontinuing alendronate sodium during the acute fusion period may be necessary to promote successful fusion.

Figures A and B are the AP and lateral radiographs of the lumbar spine depicting degenerative scoliosis with hypolordosis.

Incorrect answers
Answer 1: Alendronate has been shown to have a negative effect on spinal fusion. Bisphosphonate medications should be discontinued prior to spinal fusion.
Answer 2: The patient has osteoporosis (T-score < -2.5) despite being on antiresorptive treatment. Initiating an anabolic treatment prior to spinal fusion can reduce osteoporosis-related complications.
Answer 3: Vitamin D and calcium treatment is a component of osteopenia/osteoporosis treatment, but alone is not sufficient to reduce surgical complications related to reduced bone density.
Answer 5: Switching to denosumab after prolonged bisphosphonate treatment can reduce the risk of atypical fractures. However, prior to spinal fusion an anabolic agent will enhance the chances of successful fusion and reduce mechanical complications. Decompression alone with a significant deformity is unlikely to provide durable relief.
