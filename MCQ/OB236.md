## Question

A surgeon resects a small, superficial, mobile lesion from the thigh. Pathological review of the specimen suggests it is a low-grade soft tissue sarcoma with positive surgical margins. After staging the patient, no other sites of disease are identified. What is the next most appropriate treatment for this patient?

## Options

1. Observation
2. Systemic chemotherapy
3. Radiotherapy
4. Tumor bed re-excision
5. Amputation

## Correct option

**4. Tumor bed re-excision**

## Answer explanation

Accidental excision of soft tissue sarcomas is a common reason for referral to a musculoskeletal oncologist. If such accidental excisions are accompanied with negative surgical margins, observation vs. adjuvant radiotherapy is the appropriate therapy. As positive margin resections in this instance leave residual disease within the patient, tumor bed re-excision to attain negative surgical margins is necessary. Amputation for superfical incompletely excised sarcomas usually isn't necessary.

*Rougraff et al performed a retrospective review of 46 of their patients with high-grade, nonmetastatic soft tissue sarcomas.* Treatment with local wide-margin excision and adjuvant radiotherapy resulted in approximately a 60% survival rate at a mean of 50 months. Surgical resection was accomplished using limb-salvage in 91% of cases.
