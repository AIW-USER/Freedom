## Question

A 78-year-old woman is seen in the emergency department after a fall. She has a significant bruise on her forehead and complains of mild neck pain. Her MRI is shown in Figure A. Which of the following is NOT associated with the spinal cord injury most commonly seen in this presentation?

![[q3794-a-cervicalstenosis.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Hyperextension mechanism in setting of pre-existing cervical stenosis
2. Motor weakness more pronounced in upper extremity than lower extremity
3. Dyesthesias in the lower extremities
4. Motor weakness more pronounced in hands than arms
5. Selective injury to the lateral corticospinal tract

## Correct option

**3. Dyesthesias in the lower extremities**

## Answer explanation

The patient is presenting with a hyperextension mechanism of injury to the neck with underlying cervical stenosis, which typically leads to central cord syndrome (CCS). Dyesthesias in the lower extremities are not characteristic of CCS.

CCS is the most common incomplete spinal cord injury and is typically seen in the elderly with minor hyperextension injuries. Selective injury to the central portion of the lateral corticospinal tract leads to the typical pattern of motor deficits, with upper extremities involved more than lower extremities and hands involved more than arms. Many patients also have painful burning sensations in the arms in a non-dermatomal distribution. Management is controversial, but more recent evidence suggests benefit with early decompression.

*Nowak et al. reviewed the pathophysiology, presentation, and management of CCS.* **They suggest** that CCS can present with a wide variety of symptom severity, from isolated dysesthesia to significant motor injury in all four extremities. Management has traditionally been nonoperative, but newer research is investigating the benefit of decompression.

*Brodell et al. examined national trends in the management of CCS using a large database study.* **They found** an approximately 40% rate of surgical intervention, with older patients having less surgery and higher mortality.

*Akihiko et al. studied morphological changes in the cervical spinal canal during dynamic movement using cadaver models.* Using computed tomography, **they found** significant decreases in central canal and neuroforaminal dimensions in neck extension as opposed to neutral or flexion.

Figure A is a T2 sagittal cervical spine MRI showing degenerative changes resulting in multi-level cervical stenosis.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1,2,4,5: All of these answers are associated with central cord syndrome. While the traumatic mechanism may be minor in CCS, preexisting cervical stenosis can lead to significant insult to the spinal cord, as the spinal canal diameter is significantly lowered when the neck goes in to extension.
