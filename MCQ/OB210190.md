## Answer explanation

Figure B shows an anterior inferior iliac spine (AIIS) avulsion fracture. The AIIS is where the direct head of the rectus femoris originates, and the rectus femoris inserts into the superior pole of the patella through the quadriceps tendon (Answer #2).

Anterior inferior iliac spine (AIIS) avulsion fractures represent an apophyseal injury and are seen most commonly in males between the ages of 14-17. The rectus femoris (direct head) originates from the AIIS and inserts through the quadriceps tendon into the superior pole of the patella. These avulsion injuries typically occur during eccentric contraction of the rectus femoris, such as during kicking. Most AIIS avulsion injuries in adolescents are treated non-operatively.

*Waters et al. reviewed hip and pelvic injuries in the young athlete.* **They noted** that apophyseal avulsion fractures are among the most commonly encountered skeletal injury. These injuries are usually treated with rest, anti-inflammatories, and guided physical therapy. However, the authors stress that clinicians must also be cognizant of other pathologies, such as slipped capital femoral epiphysis, Perthes' disease, congenital subluxation of the hip, malignancy, and infection.

*Rajasekhar et al. published two cases of ASIS avulsion fractures.* The first was a missed diagnosis of the injury that resulted in exostosis formation requiring excision, and the second was an acute injury with significant displacement that was treated with open reduction and internal fixation. **The authors conclude** that a high index of suspicion is required to accurately diagnose these injuries and that there is a role for surgical management in carefully selected cases.

Incorrect Answers:

Answer 1: Both the sartorius and the tensor fascia lata originate from the ASIS. The sartorius inserts at the pes anserinus on the medial aspect of the knee, while the tensor fascia lata inserts into Gerdy's tubercle via the IT band.
Answer 3: Several muscles originate from the ischial tuberosity (long head of biceps femoris, semimembranosus, semitendinosus, and adductor magnus), but none insert via the quadriceps tendon into the superior pole of the patella.
Answer 4: The iliopsoas tendon inserts into the lesser tuberosity.
Answer 5: Both the gluteus medius and gluteus minimus insert into the greater trochanter.
