## Question

![[q215416-a-laterjet-1.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. <10%
2. 20-35%
3. 40-55%
4. 75-90%
5. 100%

## Correct option

**4. 75-90%**

## Answer explanation

This patient underwent an anterior coracoid transfer. The estimated return to sports at the previous level of play is roughly 75-90% following this procedure.

The Latarjet (coracoid transfer) and Bristow procedures for glenoid bone loss are indicated with chronic bony deficiencies with >20-25% glenoid deficiency (inverted pear deformity to glenoid); recently, the indications for bony augmentation procedures have expanded to include instability in the setting of subcritical bone loss (>15%). These procedures involve transferring of the coracoid bone with an attached conjoined tendon and CA ligament. The Latarjet procedure is performed more commonly than the Bristow procedure, and provides a "triple effect" that includes bony augmentation which increases the size of the glenoid track, a sling effect in which the conjoined tendon tensions the top of the subscapularis, and it provides capsular reconstruction by way of the CA ligament. Following this procedure, the average return to sports at any level is high, but there is a reduction in return to sports at the previous level of play. Return to overhead sports is sometimes difficult following Latarjet.

*Glogovac et al. reported on the outcomes and rate of return to sport for athletes after coracoid transfer.* In most studies, **they found** that the rate of return to sport at any level averaged 80% to 100%, and the rate of return to the previous level of play ranged from 56% to 95%. Moreover, patients returned to sport at an average of 3.2 to 8.1 months, with a postoperative dislocation rate of 0% to 14%. **They concluded** that there was a high early rate of return to sport in patients who underwent coracoid transfer for anterior shoulder instability, although patients did not reliably return to the same level of play and the procedure had very favorable outcomes for treatment of instability, with low rates of recurrent dislocation and reoperation.

*Blonna et al. compared return to sports following either arthroscopic Bankart repair and open Bristow-Latarjet procedure.* **They report** that patients who underwent arthroscopic Bankart repair obtained better results in terms of return to sport and ROM in the throwing position, and they reported better subjective perception of the shoulder with no differences in the rate of recurrent instability. **They concluded** that arthroscopic stabilization using anchors provided a better return to sport and subjective perception of the shoulder compared with the open Bristow-Latarjet procedure in the population studied.

*Hurley et al. systematically reviewed the rate and timing of return to play after the Latarjet procedure.* **They report** that the overall rate of return to play was 88.8%, with 72.6% returning to the same level of play with the mean time to return to play was 5.8 months (range, 3.2-8 months). **They concluded** that the overall rate of return to play was reportedly high after the Latarjet procedure. However, almost a fifth of athletes returning to sports were not able to return at the same level.
