## Question

## Answer explanation

This patient underwent an ACL reconstruction that has now failed. A missed concomitant posterolateral corner (PLC) injury at the time of initial injury increases the risk of ACL reconstruction failure.

Failure to address a concomitant PLC injury results in undue strain on the ACL graft, ultimately leading to its failure. The PLC consists of static and dynamic stabilizers about the lateral knee. The LCL is the primary restraint to varus stress of the knee at 5° (55%) and 25° (69%) of knee flexion. An isolated PLC injury is rare, and most often occurs with concomitant PCL or ACL ruptures. The dial test is frequently used to assess clinically for a PLC injury. With the dial test, >10° of external rotation asymmetry at 30° of knee flexion suggests a PLC injury. If this asymmetry occurs at both 30° and 90° of knee flexion, then a PLC and PCL injury should be suspected. With an ACL and PLC injury, the PLC is often reconstructed at the same time as the ACL, or as the 1st stage of a 2 stage reconstruction.

*Ricchetti et al. review posterolateral corner injuries of the knee.* **They report** that these injuries rarely occur in isolation and are often associated with cruciate ligament tears or tibial plateau fractures. **They conclude** that early diagnosis may allow for primary repair of the posterolateral corner rather than delayed reconstruction.

*Fanelli et al. review the management of the acute three-ligament-injured knees (ACL-PCL-PLC).* **They report** that these injuries often occur secondary to trauma or a tibiofemoral dislocation with spontaneous reduction and that limb ischemia is a concern. **They conclude** that combined ACL-PCL-PLC complex reconstructions performed in a timely fashion have provided consistent and predictable results with few complications.

Figure A is a clinical depiction of a dial test being performed. Figure B represents a Lachman examination. Figure C represents a pivot shift maneuver. Figure D shows an anterior drawer being performed. Figure E represents a patellar apprehension maneuver.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: A Lachman examination tests the integrity of the ACL, not the PLC.
Answer 3: The pivot shift maneuver assesses for rotational stability of the ACL by isolating the posterolateral bundle.
Answer 4: The anterior drawer assesses for anterior to posterior stability of the ACL.
Answer 5: A patellar apprehension maneuver is used to evaluate for patella translation and may suggest an MPFL tear.
