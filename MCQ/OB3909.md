## Question

She ambulates without issue but admits to recurrent swelling and redness of the ankle. She is afebrile and CBC, ESR, and CRP are unremarkable. ## Options

1. Arthroscopic or open resection of the lesion
2. Chemotherapy with wide resection of the lesion
3. External beam radiation followed by radical resection of the lesion
4. Observation with serial radiographs
5. Physical therapy with a repeat MRI in 6 months

## Correct option

**1. Arthroscopic or open resection of the lesion**

## Answer explanation

This patient's clinical presentation is consistent with pigmented villonodular synovitis (PVNS) of the ankle. The recommended treatment in the symptomatic patient is open or arthroscopic synovectomy.

PVNS is an idiopathic monoarticular neoplastic synovial disease that most commonly affects adults aged 30-40 years. Patients often present with complaints of mechanical pain, swelling, erythema, limited motion, and recurrent effusions. There is minimal role for observation in the symptomatic patient. In these patients, open or arthroscopic synovectomy is indicated. The patient will have a brownish or reddish synovium with a grossly bloody aspirate and histological studies will demonstrate hemosiderin stained multinucleated giant cells.

*Tyler et al. review monoarticular PVNS and* **noted** a recurrence rate of up to 46% in the diffuse form. **They conclude** that arthroscopic synovectomy has several advantages but is associated with higher recurrence rates in diffuse PVNS.

*Lee et al. prospectively reviewed seven patients with histologically confirmed diffuse PVNS who underwent incomplete synovectomy and radiotherapy.* Six of these patients reported better function and reduced levels of pain. **They concluded** that a combined approach to a primary PVNS of the foot and ankle reduces the risk of recurrence without functional impairment and is a reasonable treatment modality in these patients.

*Rhee et al. retrospectively reviewed the long-term results of localized PVNS treated with arthroscopic excision and partial synovectomy.* **They noted** two cases with posteromedial lesion recurrence. **They concluded** that arthroscopic excision improves symptoms with the ability to return to preoperative activity levels. However, patients may develop secondary osteoarthritis after treatment.

Figure A is a sagittal MRI image showing a well-circumscribed inhomogeneous mass at the anterior ankle joint consistent with a diagnosis of pigmented villonodular synovitis (PVNS). Figure B is the histologic evaluation from the intraoperative biopsy demonstrates multiple multinucleated giant cells with hemosiderin and very few mitotic figures.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 2: Chemotherapy with wide resection of the lesion is not indicated in PVNS. Open or arthroscopic synovectomy with marginal resection is indicated in the symptomatic patient
Answer 3: While there is a role for external beam radiation when combined with total synovectomy to reduce recurrence rates, radical resection of the lesion is not indicated
Answers 4&5: There is minimal role for observation in the symptomatic patient. In these patients, open or arthroscopic synovectomy is indicated
