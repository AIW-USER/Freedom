## Question

A shoulder and elbow trained surgeon has many patients in his clinic with elbow arthritis. He offers arthroscopic and open debridement procedures as well as various arthroplasty procedures. Arthroscopic osteocapsular arthroplasty is considered for select patients to relieve pain, improve range of motion, and address mechanical symptoms associated with arthritis. Which of the following patients is the best surgical candidate for an elbow arthroscopic osteocapsular arthroplasty?

## Options

1. A 79-year-old household ambulator with severe osteoarthritis
2. A 37-year-old high school football coach with moderate osteoarthritis and a range of motion of 40-90 degrees
3. A 29-year-old soldier with moderate post-traumatic arthritis and a history of ulnar nerve transposition after multiple open procedures for a previous elbow fracture
4. A 51-year-old mailman with mild osteoarthritis and range of motion of 30-135 degrees
5. A 46-year-old mechanic with end-stage arthritis secondary to an intra-articular fracture 20 years ago

## Correct option

**4. A 51-year-old mailman with mild osteoarthritis and range of motion of 30-135 degrees**

## Answer explanation

The ideal candidate for arthroscopic osteocapsular arthroplasty is a younger (<65), active patient with an adequate range of motion (> 90-degree total arc) and mild to moderate arthritis (answer choice 4).

Many options exist for the surgical treatment of elbow arthritis, depending on a patient's age, activity level, and stage of arthritis. For younger patients with mild arthritis and adequate range of motion, arthroscopic debridement can provide adequate relief. Debridement (either arthroscopic osteocapsular arthroplasty or the Outerbridge-Kashiwagi procedure) functions by removing loose bodies, osteophytes, and/or inflamed synovium. Relative contraindications to arthroscopic procedures include previous ulnar nerve transposition, significant contracture, and prior extensive open procedures. The Outerbridge-Kashiwagi procedure (O-K procedure) is an open procedure from a posterior approach that features a fenestration in the olecranon fossa for the release of posterior impingement and access to the anterior joint. For younger patients with more severe arthritis, interposition arthroplasty can be a reliable option. Interposition arthroplasty features the same debridement as the aforementioned procedures but includes a soft tissue graft placed over the denuded cartilage to create a smooth, functional joint surface. Total elbow arthroplasty is reserved for lower demand, often elderly patients with severe arthritis who can comply with postoperative activity limitations.

*Guerrero et al. published a systematic review comparing the outcomes of arthroscopic and open osteocapsular debridement for primary osteoarthritis of the elbow.* Thirty studies were included in this review with primarily functional outcomes assessed. **The authors found** that arthroscopic debridement was associated with fewer complications, shorter recovery, and higher patient satisfaction in patients with adequate range of motion and mild arthritis. **They concluded** that arthroscopic debridement is beneficial for milder symptoms but the decision between open and arthroscopic treatment should depend on patient factors and arthritis severity.

*Cohen et al. performed a non-randomized trial to compare the clinical outcomes of open debridement versus arthroscopic debridement of elbow arthritis.* *Twenty-eight patients either underwent arthroscopic debridement (16 patients) or open debridement (12 patients) with a mean follow-up of 31 months.* Overall, surgical debridement significantly improved function and pain scores. Arthroscopic debridement was found to have better outcomes in patients with mild to moderate arthritis, while open debridement achieved greater improvements in patients with severe arthritis. **The authors concluded** that arthroscopic-based debridement is ideal for earlier stages of arthritis, with open treatment reserved for advanced stages.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: This elderly, low-demand patient with severe osteoarthritis is better indicated for a total elbow arthroplasty.
Answer 2: This patient has a flexion contracture of 50 degrees, which is a contraindication to arthroscopic osteocapsular arthroplasty. Due to the flexion contractures, this patient would be better served with an open procedure such as an olecranon fossa debridement or column procedure.
Answer 3: Prior ulnar nerve transposition and prior extensive open procedures are contraindications to arthroscopic osteocapsular arthroplasty.
Answer 5: Due to end-stage arthritis in this patient, an interposition arthroplasty may be a better treatment option rather than arthroscopic osteocapsular arthroplasty.
