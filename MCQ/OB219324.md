## Question

Trunnionosis can occur secondary to a variety of forms of corrosion. Micromotion at the modular head-neck junction of a total hip arthroplasty can lead to trunnionosis through which type of corrosion?

## Options

1. Adhesive Corrosion
2. Crevice Corrosion
3. Fretting Corrosion
4. Galvanic Corrosion
5. Pitting Corrosion

## Correct option

**3. Fretting Corrosion**

## Answer explanation

Relative micromotion between two materials which causes wear is known as fretting corrosion (Answer 3).

Total hip arthroplasty (THA) trunnionosis is a complication of THA caused by metal ion release from corrosion at the femoral head-neck interface Morse taper. This release of wear particles leads to adverse local tissue reactions and hip pain, symptoms of which may be severe enough to require patients to undergo revision THA. Various risk factors for trunnionosis have been identified, including larger diameter heads, shorter Morse taper lengths, metal on metal bearing surfaces, and mixed alloy components.

On a pathophysiologic basis, three main forms of corrosion can lead to trunnionosis. Galvanic corrosion occurs when an electrochemical transfer of electrons occurs between two dissimilar metals (i.e. cobalt-chromium alloy head on a titanium alloy stem). Fretting corrosion occurs secondary to micromotion between two materials, which causes wear and metal ion release. Crevice or pitting corrosion occurs secondary to the physicochemical interaction between a metal and its environment, which leads to altered mechanical properties and the formation of crevices or pits. This is driven when an area with a low concentration of oxygen becomes anodic, while an adjacent area with an excess of oxygen becomes cathodic. The adverse reaction to the metal debris generated secondary to corrosion may result in an aseptic lymphocyte-dominant vasculitis association lesion (ALVAL), similar to that seen in metal-on-metal hips.

*Dyrkacz et al. investigated the influence of head size on corrosion and fretting behavior at the head-neck interface of prosthetic hip joints.* *Their study included 74 retrieved implants, a cohort that included heads of both 28 and 36mm diameters.* Overall, **the authors found** greater corrosion scores with the larger heads when compared to the smaller heads, which they attributed to a greater torque acting about the Morse taper interface.

*Srinivasan et al. reviewed the literature surrounding modular THA components.* **The authors note** that modular implants allow for cost savings, and aid in the recreation of native femoral version, limb length, and offset. **The authors caution**, however, that modular junctions are susceptible to taper fretting, fatigue fracture, and local corrosion, especially when larger diameter heads are used. Overall, **the authors conclude** that while there are benefits to modular components, long-term data on the effects of these modular junctions is required before the safety profile of these implants is fully elucidated.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Adhesive wear (not corrosion) occurs when polyethylene microscopically adheres to the metal or ceramic bearing surface, leading to wear debris generation. This is a primary mediator of osteolysis, not trunnionosis.
Answers 2 and 5: Crevice/Pitting corrosion occurs in fatigue cracks due to differences in oxygen concentration. 316L stainless steel is most prone to this form of corrosion, while titanium is the least prone.
Answer 4: Galvanic corrosion occurs when two dissimilar metals are located close to each other, and subsequent electrochemical destruction occurs.
