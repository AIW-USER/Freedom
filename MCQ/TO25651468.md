## Question

A 70-year-old patient on Zoledronic acid for 3 years develops a mid-shaft femoral stress reaction on imaging. What is the most appropriate next step?

## Options

1. Switch to Denosumab immediately.
2. Continue Zoledronic acid and add Vitamin D.
3. Switch to oral Alendronate to reduce potency.
4. Discontinue Zoledronic acid and consider Teriparatide.

## Correct option

**4. Discontinue Zoledronic acid and consider Teriparatide.**

## Answer explanation

The identification of an AFF precursor requires cessation of the antiresorptive. Teriparatide may be used to stimulate bone remodeling and expedite the healing of the stress fracture.

The clinical presentation of a mid-shaft femoral stress reaction in a patient on long-term Zoledronic acid is highly indicative of an incomplete Atypical Femoral Fracture (AFF). Because these lesions have a high propensity to progress to complete, transverse, or short oblique fractures with catastrophic displacement, the management requires immediate, aggressive intervention.

Here is the detailed, step-wise management protocol for this presentation.

1. Immediate Medical Discontinuation

The absolute first step is the immediate cessation of Zoledronic acid and any other antiresorptive agents.

The pathogenesis of an AFF involves the profound, prolonged suppression of targeted bone remodeling. This prevents the physiological repair of routine microdamage, which accumulates in areas of high tensile stress (specifically the lateral cortex of the subtrochanteric or diaphyseal femur). Continuing the antiresorptive agent will only further inhibit the osteoclastic resorption necessary to initiate the healing cascade.

2. Mechanical Protection

The patient must be immediately transitioned to protected weight-bearing (toe-touch or non-weight-bearing) using crutches or a walker. This mechanical offloading is critical to prevent the stress reaction from propagating across the cortices and completing the fracture.

3. Mandatory Contralateral Imaging

You must obtain full-length orthogonal radiographs of the contralateral femur.

AFFs are bilateral in up to 20% to 50%
of patients. Even if the patient does not report prodromal thigh or groin pain on the contralateral side, the opposite lateral cortex must be scrutinized for localized periosteal or endosteal thickening ("cortical beaking") or a subtle radiolucent line. If plain radiographs are equivocal but clinical suspicion remains high, an MRI or bone scintigraphy should be obtained.

4. Stratifying Surgical vs. Conservative Management

The decision to proceed with prophylactic surgical fixation depends entirely on the presence of clinical pain and the radiographic severity of the lesion.

 Prevents catastrophic complete fracture. Prophylactic fixation has significantly lower morbidity, reduced blood loss, and fewer non-union complications compared to fixing a complete AFF.

 Patient is completely asymptomatic AND
 imaging shows only localized cortical thickening (beaking) without any lucent line.

 A trial of strictly protected weight-bearing for 2–3 months can be attempted, with serial radiographic monitoring to ensure the stress reaction is not progressing.

Surgical Pearl: If prophylactic nailing is indicated, full-length cephalomedullary reconstruction nails are preferred to protect the femoral neck. Careful attention must be paid to the anterior bowing of the femur, which is often exaggerated in these patients; over-reaming or using a nail with a smaller radius of curvature may be necessary to avoid iatrogenic anterior cortical perforation during insertion.

5. Transition to Anabolic Therapy

To facilitate healing of the stress reaction (or the surgical site post-nailing), the patient should be transitioned to an osteoanabolic agent, most commonly Teriparatide (PTH 1-34).

Because the patient's intrinsic bone remodeling is heavily suppressed by three years of Zoledronic acid, Teriparatide is utilized off-label to directly stimulate osteoblast-mediated bone formation, accelerate woven bone production, and enhance fracture callus formation.

 - Note: Abaloparatide or Romosozumab may also be considered based on availability, but Teriparatide currently has the most robust clinical literature supporting its use in expediting AFF union.

 - Absolute Contraindication:
 Denosumab must be strictly avoided, as it will reinstate the profound remodeling suppression that caused the pathology.

6. Metabolic Optimization

Ensure that the patient's fundamental metabolic parameters for bone healing are optimal. Check serum calcium, 25-hydroxyvitamin D, and PTH levels. Aggressively correct any Vitamin D deficiency (maintaining levels >30 ng/mL) and ensure adequate calcium intake to provide the necessary substrate for the anabolic phase of healing.

Answer: 4
