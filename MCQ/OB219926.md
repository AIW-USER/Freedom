## Question

A hospital performs a root cause analysis of a recent wrong-site surgery. Unfortunately, there have been several wrong site or wrong procedure surgeries at this facility in the past year. The interdisciplinary team discovered violations in the Universal Protocol established by The Joint Commission occurred in the most recent case. Which of the following violates the Universal Protocol established by The Joint Commission?

## Options

1. Delegating the procedure site marking to the orthopedic resident on service
2. The operating room nurse calls for and leads the surgical time out
3. A pre-procedure checklist is used in pre-op to identify implants needed during the case
4. Confirming the site marking with the patient in the pre-operative holding area prior to administration of anesthesia
5. Placing the site marking on the plantar aspect of the foot for a total knee arthroplasty

## Correct option

**5. Placing the site marking on the plantar aspect of the foot for a total knee arthroplasty**

## Answer explanation

The Joint Commission (TJC) mandated the Universal Protocol for the prevention of wrong site, wrong procedure, and wrong person surgery in July 2004. According to the minimum requirements of this mandate, the surgical mark must be visible in the surgical field.

Despite the Universal Protocol mandate for accredited hospitals, ambulatory care facilities, and office-based surgical facilities, wrong surgery events persist. In 2015, 120 wrong surgery sentinel events were reported to TJC. TJC requires 3 basic aspects as part of the Universal Protocol: Conduct a pre-procedure verification process, mark the procedure site, and perform a timeout. The pre-procedure verification should include a review of relevant documentation, proper display of diagnostic radiological testing, and ensuring necessary blood products, implants, and devices are available. The site marking should be at or near the procedure site and should be visible after skin preparation and draping. During the surgical timeout, all team members should agree (at a minimum) on the correct patient identity, correct site, and procedure to be done.

*Paull et al. performed a retrospective analysis of all incorrect surgical procedures between 2004 and 2013 in the Veterans Health Administration database.* **The authors identified** 308 wrong surgery events. 48 of these incorrect procedures would not have been prevented even with adherence to the Universal Protocol. **They conclude** that adherence to the Universal Protocol may decrease but will not eliminate wrong surgery events. **They recommend** additional monitoring upstream and downstream from the Universal Protocol to minimize patient harm.

*Boyum et al. published the authors' experience with a multidisciplinary team to prevent wrong surgery events.* *The authors' institution had 7 sentinel events attributable to a breakdown in the Universal Protocol in a single calendar year.* The multidisciplinary team established a standardized timeout protocol and recorded compliance over the following year. There were no wrong surgery events and timeout compliance significantly increased. **The authors recommend** a tailored standardized timeout that adheres to TJC standards.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The site marking may be delegated, in limited circumstances, to a resident, physician’s assistant, or advanced practice registered nurse. However, the licensed independent practitioner is accountable for that site marking.
Answer 2: Any designated team member can start the timeout. The timeout involves all relevant members of the procedure team.
Answer 3: A pre-procedure verification process includes ensuring that required implants are available.
Answer 4: The patient should be involved in the verification process whenever possible.
