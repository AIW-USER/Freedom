## Question

A patient is undergoing primary THA for the treatment of moderate-severe osteoarthritis. The orthopaedic surgeon asks for 1g of intravenous tranexamic acid prior to the incision. Which conditions in the patient’s history would be considered an absolute contraindication?

## Options

1. Embolic stroke
2. Deep vein thrombosis
3. Atrial fibrillation
4. Factor V Leiden
5. None of these are absolute contraindications

## Correct option

**5. None of these are absolute contraindications**

## Answer explanation

The only absolute contraindications to the use of tranexamic acid (TXA) are current subarachnoid hemorrhage, active intravascular coagulopathy, and severe hypersensitivity to TXA.

TXA is a reversible competitive plasminogen inhibitor, thereby encouraging clot stabilization. Its use in orthopedic surgery is well established, as it has been shown to decrease the need for blood transfusions without the risk of an adverse event. While further randomized control trials are needed, TXA has been shown to be safe and effective, even in patients with a history of thromboembolism. There are few absolute contraindications to its use in surgery. While some relative contraindications include pregnancy, being on birth control, a history of seizures, or having a coronary stent placed in the last 6 months. There are also groups warranting special consideration, such as patients with renal impairment requiring adjusted dosing, given it is renally excretion.

*Porter and colleagues performed a retrospective case-control study at their institution to evaluate the clotting-related complications associated with TXA use in a high-risk population undergoing elective primary hip or knee arthroplasty.* *They included pre-existing or prior conditions in their “high-risk” group: deep vein thrombosis, pulmonary embolism, myocardial infarction, lifelong prothrombotic state, atrial fibrillation, atrial flutter, coronary stent, or coronary bypass grafting.* **They found** no difference in adverse outcomes in the first 90 days post-operatively when treated with or without intraoperative TXA. **They concluded** that TXA administration in this high-risk elective joint replacement patient cohort is not associated with significantly different adverse outcomes.

*Porter and colleagues also evaluated the clotting-related complications associated with TXA use in a high-risk population undergoing arthroplasty for acute hip fracture.* *They utilized propensity score matching to compare high-risk patients treated with and without intraoperative TXA use.* **They found** no difference in adverse outcomes in the first 90 days post-operatively between groups and concluded TXA was safe to use in this patient population.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1-4: The Food and Drug Administration (FDA) only lists current subarachnoid hemorrhage, active intravascular clotting, and severe hypersensitivity as absolute contraindications to the use of TXA.
