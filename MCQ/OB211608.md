## Question

A 71-year-old patient undergoes a C3-6 posterior decompression and fusion for severe cervical spondylomyelopathy. Preoperatively, the patient's modified Japanese Orthopedic Association (mJOA) score was 10. What is the goal of surgical decompression in this patient?

## Options

1. Obtaining a mJOA score >16 at 1 year folllow-up
2. Complete neurologic recovery
3. Resolution of myelomalacia
4. Restoration of normal cervical lordosis
5. Prevention of continued neurologic decline

## Correct option

**5. Prevention of continued neurologic decline**

## Answer explanation

The goal of surgical decompression of patients with severe cervical myelopathy is the prevention of continued neurologic decline.

Cervical myelopathy is the result of chronic spinal cord compression in the cervical spine that leads to gait instability, urinary changes, motor weakness, hand clumsiness, and upper motor neuron signs. The natural history of the disease is a chronic step-wise deterioration in neurologic function. Non-operative treatment has a role in mild presentations of the disease. However, severe cases should be addressed if the patient can physiologically tolerate the procedure. The goal of surgical treatment is the prevention of further neurologic deterioration, although, significant neurologic improvement may occur.

*Fehlings et al. performed a large multicenter prospective study looking at outcomes of spinal cord decompression in patients with myelopathy.* **They reported** there was significant improvement across all patient-reported outcome measures at twelve-months postop with on the mJOA score having a difference in functional improvement from mild, moderate, and severe myelopathy patients. **The authors concluded** that surgical treatment of cervical myelopathy directly improves patient functional outcomes.

*Lim et al. performed a retrospective study on postoperative C5 palsy 710 patients undergoing surgical treatment for cervical myelopathy.* **They found** that C5 palsy occurred in 5.1% of cases with risk factors being greater than 3 surgical levels. Further, **they reported** that prolonged recovery occurred in patients with a C5 motor grade less than or equal to 2, multilevel paresis, and sensory involvement with intractable pain. **The authors concluded** that patients with C5 palsy will tend to recover within 6 months from the onset of symptoms, but the findings of this study may prognosticate patients who may require a prolonged period of recovery.

*Tetreault et al. performed a large multicenter international study of 614 patients undergoing surgical treatment for cervical myelopathy to determine preoperative factors to predict patient outcomes.* The multivariate analysis revealed 6 factors that correlated with mJOA score ≥16 which included baseline mJOA score, impaired gait, age, comorbidity score, smoking status, and duration of symptoms. **The authors concluded** that their prognostic model is not designed to determine which patients would benefit the most from surgery but to guide presurgical expectations during the consent process.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: A mJOA score >16 at 1-year postop is suggestive of a good outcome with near-normal neurologic function, which is not realistic in patients presenting with a preoperative score of 10.
Answer 2: Complete neurologic recovery in cases of severe myelopathy is highly unlikely and not the goal of treatment.
Answer 3: The resolution of myelomalacia on magnetic resonance imaging is unlikely to occur and should not be the goal of treatment.
Answer 4: While reducing cervical kyphosis is a goal of treatment in this patient, restoration of "normal" cervical lordosis is not needed and is unlikely. Ideally a patient is restored to neutral cervical alignment.
