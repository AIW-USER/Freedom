## Question

A 63-year-old female with a body mass index of 25 has left hip osteoarthritis refractory to nonoperative management and is indicated for total hip arthroplasty. She is very interested in the direct anterior approach, and is specifically wondering whether you can offer her a minimally invasive “bikini incision”. Compared to a traditional longitudinal incision, what is true of the “bikini incision”?

## Options

1. Less risk of injury to the lateral femoral cutaneous nerve
2. Greater risk of periprosthetic fractures
3. Higher rate of postoperative limb length discrepancy
4. Lower rate of heterotopic ossification
5. More wound complications

## Correct option

**4. Lower rate of heterotopic ossification**

## Answer explanation

The "bikini incision" for direct anterior (DA) total hip arthroplasty (THA) has been associated with a decreased risk for heterotopic ossification (HO) when compared to the traditional longitudinal incision.

For various reasons, the DA approach for THA has become more popular in recent years. Most commonly the DA approach is performed with a longitudinal incision. This, however, has been associated with a high rate of wound complications when used for obese patients. The "bikini incision" has been developed as an incision for DA THA that is associated with a lower risk of wound complications when used in obese patients. The "bikini incision" has the downside of being less extensile than a longitudinal approach, so caution should be taken when selecting patients.

Rüdiger et al. performed a retrospective review of 401 direct anterior DA THAs, examining risk factors for HO. They found that American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) grade, age in women, and higher body mass index (BMI) in men were associated with higher HO grades, and that the "bikini incision" was associated with a lower rate of high-grade HO compared to traditional longitudinal incisions (4% vs 12%). They concluded that the "bikini incision" is associated with a lower rate of high-grade HO compared to the longitudinal incision.

*Naylor et al. performed a retrospective comparative study in which they analyzed the effect of celecoxib on HO formation in patients who had undergone DA THA.* **They found** that patients receiving celecoxib had an overall HO rate of 4.3% and a 0.3% rate of high-grade HO, compared to patients who did not receive celecoxib, who had an HO rate of 14.3% and a 2.2% rate of high-grade HO. **They concluded** that celecoxib is protective of HO after DA THA.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Some studies have found a higher rate of lateral femoral cutaneous nerve injury with the "bikini incision".
Answer 2: There has not been a periprosthetic fracture difference established in the literature between the two incisions.
Answer 3: There has not been a limb length discrepancy difference established in the literature between the two incisions.
Answer 5: The "bikini incision" has been associated with a lower rate of wound complications, particularly for some obese patients.
