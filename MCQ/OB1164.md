## Question

A 23-year-old professional ballet dancer complains of medial ankle pain when in the en pointe position. Physical exam shows a hyper-mobile ankle with palpable crepitus along the medial ankle with active range of motion of the great toe. Six months of nonoperative management including rest, ice, NSAIDs, and arch support have failed to provide relief. What is the best next step in management?

## Options

1. Decompression of the flexor hallucis longus at the level of the ankle
2. Decompression of the flexor hallucis longus at the level of the metatarsal
3. Decompression of the peroneal tendons at the level of the ankle
4. First metatarsal osteotomy
5. Gastrocnemius recession

## Correct option

**1. Decompression of the flexor hallucis longus at the level of the ankle**

## Answer explanation

This patient is presenting with impingement of the flexor hallucis longus (FHL) with resultant tendonitis. The most appropriate treatment would be release of the FHL tendon at the level of the ankle.

Impingement of the FHL, and even rupture, at the level of the posteromedial ankle joint can be seen in dancers. Physical exam findings include: posteromedial pain and crepitus, triggering of the great toe, decreased passive extension of the great toe, and pain with resisted great toe plantar flexion. First line treatment should include rest, ice, NSAIDs, and arch support. If these modalities fail, then operative treatment is the next best step in management.

*Sammarco et al. retrospectively reviewed a population of young people with FHL tendon pathologies.* **They showed** that surgical treatment of tenosynovitis, pseudocyst, and tendon tear yielded good or excellent results in 14 of 15 dancers and 9 of 11 non dancers.

Illustration A shows the anatomy of the medial ankle. Illustration B shows an image of open decompression of the tarsal tunnel. The medial retinaculum (2) and FHL tendon (1) are shown. Contracture or compression by the retinaculum over the FHL can lead to posteromedial ankle pain.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: The pain and crepitus is at the level of the ankle, which is more common than at the distal tarsal tunnel in the midfoot.
Answer 3: Peroneal pathology would be referred to the lateral ankle, not the medial ankle.
Answer 4: A first metatarsal osteotomy would be indicated for hallux abduction/valgus deformities.
Answer 5: Gastrocnemius recession would be performed to correct an isolated gastrocnemius equinus contracture of the ankle.
