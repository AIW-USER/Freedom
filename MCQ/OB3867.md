## Question

A 62-year-old female presents with pain on the plantar surface of her foot. Figure A is a clinical photograph. The patient is scheduled for the osteotomy shown in Figure B. What is the pathophysiologic mechanism of the most common complication caused by this osteotomy method?

*Figure A*

![[q3867-b-screen-shot-2019-08-28-at-6-55-40-am.jpg]]
*Figure B*

## Options

1. Articular damage
2. Disruption of dorsal capsular artery
3. Lengthening of the metatarsal
4. Conversion of interossei from plantarflexors to dorsiflexors
5. Iatrogenic ligament instability

## Correct option

**4. Conversion of interossei from plantarflexors to dorsiflexors**

## Answer explanation

The most common complication of a Weil osteotomy is a floating toe, or a MTP dorsiflexion contracture, which results from altered mechanics of the interossei leading to conversion of interossei from plantarflexors to dorsiflexors.

Following a Weil osteotomy the interossei muscles move dorsally with respect to the axis of the MTP joint due to depression of the plantar fragment and due to the fact that the center of rotation is altered after the osteotomy. The osteotomy provides controlled shortening of the metatarsal using rigid internal fixation. In metatarsaglia, the metatarsal head is moved proximal to the callus, which achieves axial decompression. It is also used to resolve the hammer toe deformity or MTP subluxations, which may cause metatarsalgia.

*Davies et al. reviewed 50 Weil osteotomies in 33 patients.* In this article, they describe the indications, the technique, and the results of the osteotomy. Their short term results show an overall low complication rate and good success for a variety of forefoot pathologies. The average amount of shortening following a Weil osteotomy is 4mm. They did not evaluate or discuss the floating toe complication in their article.

*Trnka et al. performed a cadaveric study to evaluate dorsiflexion following Weil osteotomy.* **They found** that plantar depression of the MTP joint following the osteotomy altered the dynamic forces of the interossei converting them from plantarflexors to dorsiflexors.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1 and 2: Despite the fact that this is a distal osteotomy and commences through the articular surface of the metatarsal head, MTP arthritis and AVN is rare. This is because there is a constant plantar artery supplying the metatarsal head and the capsule of the MTP joint.
Answer 3: The average degree of shortening following a Weil osteotomy is 4mm and transfer metatarsaglia has not been shown to be the most common complication.
Answer 5: Toe instability is not the most common complication following a weil osteotomy.
