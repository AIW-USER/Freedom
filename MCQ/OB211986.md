## Answer explanation

For an isolated posterior cruciate ligament (PCL) injury, closed-chain quadriceps strengthening is the initial treatment of choice.

While isolated PCL injuries are rare, multiple studies have reported successful outcomes (patient satisfaction and return to sport) with non-operative management. However, no clear prognostic factors predict outcomes and ideal management is unclear. By strengthening the quadriceps, the concept is to dynamically counteract the increased posterior tibial translation with a PCL-deficient knee. Similarly, rehabilitation of an ACL-deficient knee would consist of hamstring strengthening. When non-operative management fails, surgery may consist of single-bundle, double-bundle, trans-tibial, or tibial inlay reconstruction techniques.

*Parolie et al. looked at the long-term results of nonoperative treatment of isolated PCL injuries in athletes.* *They examined 25 patients with a mean follow-up of 6.2 years.* *At follow-up,* **they found** that 84% of patients returned to sport without requiring surgery.

*Agolley et al. studied the return of athletes to sport following the nonoperative treatment of isolated PCL injuries.* *They prospectively studied 46 patients until return to sport-specific training and again at an average of 5.2 years from injury.* **They concluded** that non-operative management of isolated PCL injuries (Hughston grade II and III) within four weeks of injury gives excellent functional outcomes with a high proportion of athletes returning to the same or higher level of sport.
