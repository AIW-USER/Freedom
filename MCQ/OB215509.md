## Question

You are the team physician for a local high school football team. Throughout the season, you treat multiple athletes with head/neck injuries. Which of the following athletes should be allowed to return to play in the same game?

## Options

1. Hit to head, no loss of consciousness. Normal physical examination. Appears oriented but cannot repeat multiple words over the course of exam
2. Hit to head with loss of consciousness on field for 10 seconds. Following removal from play he has a normal cognitive and physical sideline examination
3. Hit to right side of neck with no loss of consciousness. Normal cognitive examination on sideline. Bilateral upper extremity parasthesias with no transient improvement
4. Hit to head/neck with no loss of consciousness. Amnestic to events of previous quarter. Physical examination does not reveal any upper or lower extremity deficits
5. Hit to left side of neck without loss of consciousness. Normal cognitive examination. Initially complains of left sided upper extremity paresthesias that resolve after several minutes on sideline

## Correct option

**5. Hit to left side of neck without loss of consciousness. Normal cognitive examination. Initially complains of left sided upper extremity paresthesias that resolve after several minutes on sideline**

## Answer explanation

The athlete who sustained a transient brachial plexopathy, or "stinger," would be allowed to return to play in the same game since their strength has returned, unlike the other athletes who have sustained concussions.

A concussion is a mild traumatic brain injury (TBI) that occurs after a traumatic force to the head/neck and can lead to metabolic changes in the brain, causing long-term effects. A high index of suspicion must be maintained for concussions after any trauma to the head, neck, or face and these players should be immediately removed from play and examined. If there are any cognitive or somatic symptoms or evidence of focal neurologic deficits the player should not be allowed to return to play and further workup should be performed as necessary. Players with transient unilateral brachial plexopathy represent a scenario where return to play on the day of injury is acceptable, provided they have complete resolution of their symptoms. An abundance of caution is vital with regards to concussion management as these athletes are vulnerable to repeat head trauma.

*Doperak et al. review sport-related concussions and provide an overview of identification and subsequent management of these injuries in athletes.* *They go on to review different physical examination and cognitive testing methods that are used in evaluation of concussions and further classify the injuries with regards to specific treatment plans based on patient symptoms.* **They note** that with targeted treatment and medication, patients usually see improvement, however, recovery period can be lengthy.

*Podell et al. review sideline assessment of athletes who have suffered a head injury concerning for concussion.* **They note** that sideline assessment can be difficult in some cases but the Sideline Concussion Assessment Tool 5 (SCAT5) remains the standard of care, with its focus on cognitive and physical components. **They conclude** by noting that diagnosis and management of concussions is a complex endeavor that requires continued reassessment of cognitive and physical symptoms.

*Cahill et al. review concussion details that are important for orthopaedic surgeons to understand.* *They review the physiology of concussions as well as the on-field and sideline assessment and diagnosis.* *They also discuss specific treatment strategies and conclude by saying that the orthopaedic surgeon must function as part of a treatment team that includes athletic trainers, physical therapists, and the athlete's coaches/family.*

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1-4: Any athlete with physical or cognitive deficits, or sustained a loss of consciousness, should not be allowed to return to play on the day of injury. Additionally, those with focal neurologic deficits or prolonged loss of consciousness should be examined emergently at a hospital.
