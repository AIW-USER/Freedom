## Question

Figure A demonstrates an arthroplasty technique commonly used for rheumatoid arthritis. This material has also been used in an attempt to alleviate the condition shown in Figure B. What has been shown to be a long term complication of this technique that complicates revision arthrodesis surgery?

![[q212993-b-screen-shot-2019-08-03-at-2-01-46-pm.jpg]]

## Options

1. Recurrent deformity
2. Infection
3. Osteolysis
4. Chronic Synovitis
5. Floating toe

## Correct option

**3. Osteolysis**

## Answer explanation

1st MTP arthroplasty with silastic implants has been shown to be associated with osteolysis and bone loss which complicates revision surgery.

In an attempt to preserve the range of motion in end-stage hallux rigidus, multiple arthroplasty techniques have been developed. These arthroplasty techniques have been fraught with complications such as loosening and osteolysis. Structural bone grafting is usually required for salvage arthrodesis following failure of these procedures due to the resulting bone loss. Loss of fixation, chronic synovitis, and pathologic fractures can also occur after placement of silastic implants.

*Yee et al. reviewed current concepts in hallux rigidus.* **They noted** that, due to the initial success of silastic joint replacement in the hand, these implants were adapted for use in the MTP joint. However, multiple level IV studies reported high complication rates which second-generation implants attempted to correct. Concerns persist regarding the potential effects of silicone debris leading to foreign-body reaction, synovitis, and bone erosion in the hallux.

*Sullivan reviewed various MTP arthroplasty techniques.* In regards to silastic implants, **he noted** the many reported complications in the literature. Failure of the implant because of wear, early fracture of the implant, dislodgement of the implant, silastic synovitis and osteolysis, and silicone debris causing foreign body reactions have been reported.

Figure A demonstrates silicone MCP arthroplasties in a patient with rheumatoid arthritis. Figure B is a lateral demonstrating the classic dorsal osteophyte associated with hallux rigidus. Illustration A is an AP radiograph of a patient with a silastic 1st MTP arthroplasty and resulting osteolysis. Illustration B demonstrates the conversion of this patient to 1st MTP arthrodesis using a structural iliac crest bone graft.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Recurrent deformity is possible if a patient had a combined angular deformity along with arthritis of the first MTP joint.
Answer 2: Silastic implants do not appear to have an increased risk of infection.
Answer 4: Chronic synovitis is a complication of silastic implants but is less likely to complicate secondary arthrodesis compared to osteolysis.
Answer 5: A cock-up toe can occur with MTP arthroplasty but floating toes are most often associated with Weil osteotomies.
