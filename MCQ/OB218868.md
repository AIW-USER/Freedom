## Question

You are seeing a 6-year-old female with a known history of spastic cerebral palsy (CP). She is accompanied by her mother. You discuss the patient's prognosis with her and her mother and, based on her clinical presentation, predict that she has between a 30-40% chance of experiencing hip subluxation by the age of 7. Which gross motor classification functional level is associated with a 30-40% chance of developing hip subluxation by the age of 7?

## Options

1. Near normal gross motor function, independent ambulator (Level I)
2. Nonambulator with global involvement, dependent in all aspects of care (Level V)
3. Severely limited walking ability, primary mobility is wheelchair (Level IV)
4. Walks independently, but difficulty with uneven surfaces, minimal ability to jump (Level II)
5. Walks with assistive devices (Level III)

## Correct option

**5. Walks with assistive devices (Level III)**

## Answer explanation

Patients with Gross Motor Function Classification System (GMFCS) level III (Illustration A) have a 30-40% chance of experiencing hip subluxation by the age of 7. GMFCS level III cerebral palsy (CP) is defined as ambulation with assistive devices (Answer 5).

CP is secondary to injury to the immature brain that leads to upper motor neuron disease which is nonprogressive and presents across a spectrum of cognitive and musculoskeletal manifestations. The diagnosis of CP is made clinically through the evaluation of developmental milestones, cognitive function and musculoskeletal abnormalities. Co-morbid hip conditions are common, and patients can present with subluxation, dislocation and/or joint degeneration. Research has demonstrated that children with more severe CP have a higher risk of experiencing hip subluxation/dislocation; in fact, patients with GMFCS V CP have a 70-80% chance of experiencing hip subluxation by the age of 7. Treatment is dependent on patient age, degree of spasticity and severity of subluxation/dislocation and can range from soft tissue procedures to bony osteotomies.

*Shrader et al. provide a comprehensive review of hip surveillance in children with CP.* **The authors note** that the severity of dysplasia correlates with more severe pathology (i.e. higher GMFCS type). **The authors note** that hip surveillance programs have helped to improve the detection of hip subluxation and aid in the initiation of early intervention to decrease the number of frank dislocations. **The authors conclude** that while a nationwide surveillance program is yet to be established in the United States, several high-volume centers and organizations have begun investigations into the efficiency and efficacy of hip screening in patients with CP.

*Palisano et al. provide a seminal paper describing the GMFCS classification system.* *The authors drew expert opinions from 48 specialists in order to construct a valid and reproducible system.* The consensus was achieved with an integrated reliability of 0.5 for children under the age of 2 and 0.75 for children 2 to 12 years of age. Overall, **the authors conclude** that the GMFCS system has applications for clinical practice, research, teaching and hospital administration, and it has since become one of the most widely-utilized classification systems for CP.

*Pruszczynski et al. performed a systematic review investigating the risk factors for hip displacement in children with CP.* *Their study ultimately drew data from 10 studies.* Based on GMFCS levels, the authors provide a surveillance schema to hopefully detect early subluxation and help to prevent frank dislocation. **The authors note** that there exists a strong correlation between GMFCS level and hip subluxation risk and based on GMFCS level, note the following risks of subluxation by age 7:
I: < 10%
II: 10-15%
III: 30-40%
IV: 50-60%
V: 70-80%

Illustration A is a table of the Gross Motor Function Classification System (GMFCS) proposed by Palisano et al.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: this describes GMFCS I. Patients with GMFCS I CP have a <10% risk of experiencing hip subluxation by age 7.
Answer 2: this describes GMFCS V. Patients with GMFCS V CP have a 70-80% risk of experiencing hip subluxation by age 7.
Answer 3: this describes GMFCS IV. Patients with GMFCS IV CP have a 50-60% risk of experiencing hip subluxation by age 7.
Answer 4: this describes GMFCS II. Patients with GMFCS II CP have a 10-15% risk of experiencing hip subluxation by age 7.
