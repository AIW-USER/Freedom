## Question

A 17-year-old male football player presents with a 3-week history of insidious right thigh pain and a limp. He denies any history of trauma. He is afebrile and WBC, ESR, and CRP are within normal limits. On examination he has pain with resisted adduction of his right hip only. The remainder of the examination is normal. ## Options

1. Activity modification and NSAIDs
2. Antibiotics
3. Irrigation and debridement
4. Prophylactic fixation
5. Biopsy

## Correct option

**1. Activity modification and NSAIDs**

## Answer explanation

This patient presents with adductor insertion avulsion syndrome. The next best step in treatment would be activity modification and observation.

Thigh splints, or adductor insertion avulsion syndrome, is a painful condition affecting the proximal to mid femur at the insertion of the adductor muscles of the thigh. Treatment revolves around activity modification, symptomatic control, and anti-inflammatory drugs. Patients should be monitored until symptoms resolve. Symptoms usually resolve within 1-2 months.

*Anderson et al. review the MRI findings of five patients with adductor insertion avulsion syndrome.* **They found** a thin rim of high signal intensity on turbo spin-echo inversion recovery images long the medial periosteum of the proximal to mid-femoral shaft in all five patients. The length of involvement varied from 4 to 9 cm, but the abnormalities were consistently present along the posteromedial aspect of the femoral shaft at the level of the adductor muscle insertions. All patients were treated conservatively and their symptoms resolved in a few months.

*Nishio et al. present the case of a 14-year-old girl to present with adductor insertion avulsion syndrome.* **They report** the differential diagnosis includes osteomyelitis and neoplasms. **They recommend** that clinical follow-up with imaging is necessary in certain patients with apparent adductor insertion avulsion syndrome. **They recommend** a trial of conservative treatment with adequate rest. However, a biopsy may be considered if pain persists or increases.

Figures A and B are normal radiograph of the femur of a skeletally mature patient. Figure C is a coronal turbo spin-echo inversion recovery image which demonstrates a linear high-intensity signal along the medial aspect of the proximal femur.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 2 & 3: The patient does not exhibit any signs or symptoms of infection. Laboratory values and imaging are not suggestive of infection.
Answer 4: Imaging and physical examination are not suggestive of a stress fracture.
Answer 5: Biopsy is not needed as this patient does not have a malignancy.
