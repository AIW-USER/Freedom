## Question

Vigorous retraction of conjoint tendon puts which structure at risk during shoulder surgery? ( General orthopedics-surgical approaches)

## Options

1. Musculocutaneous nerve
2. Axillary nerve
3. Coracoid process
4. Anterior circumflex humeral artery

## Correct option

**1. Musculocutaneous nerve**

## Answer explanation

The musculocutaneous nerve is the terminal branch of the lateral cord of the brachial plexus (C5, C6 and C7). It leaves the axilla at the inferior border of the pectoralis minor muscle. It pierces the coracobrachialis muscle 5-8 cm distal to coracoid near its point of insertion on the humerus. It gives a branch to this muscle. The musculocutaneous nerve then passes down the flexor compartment of the upper arm, superficial to brachialis but deep to the biceps brachii muscle. It innervates both these muscles and gives articular branches to the humerus and the elbow. It pierces deep fascia lateral to biceps brachii a little above the elbow and becomes lateral antebrachial cutaneous nerve (terminal branch.

Vigorous retraction of conjoint tendon (short head of biceps and coracobrachialis) can cause neuraprexia of musculocutaneous nerve.

Answer: 1
