## Question

The most likely cause behind a late onset paraplegia in TB spine is

## Options

1. Cold Abscess
2. Granulation tissue
3. Deformity
4. Sequestrum

## Correct option

**3. Deformity**

## Answer explanation

Ans: C

Tuberculosis of spine that is often referred to as Caries spine is notorious to lead to paraplegia if unattended. Caries spine with paraplegia (neurological deficit) is popularly referred to as Pott’s spine (after Percival Potts who described the condition).

The incidence of Pott’s paraplegia is 10–30% and is commonly seen in disease of the upper thoracic spine because of narrow canal space here. The common causes are listed in table below.

Griffith’s Classification of Potts’ Paraplegia

Griffith classified paraplegia into early and late onset types, to highlight the different pathological processes involved in two different situations:

1. Early onset paraplegia: It occurs during the active phase of the disease usually within 2 years of onset. It is caused due to inflammatory edema, granulation tissue, abscess, caseous tissue or ischemic lesion of cord.

2. Late onset paraplegia: It occurs after 2 years of the persistence of disease. It may be associated with recrudescence of the disease or due to mechanical pressure on the cord. It is caused due to caseous tissue, tubercular debris, sequestra, internal gibbus, canal stenosis or severe kyphotic deformity.
