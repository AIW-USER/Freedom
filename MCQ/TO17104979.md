## Answer explanation

Patellar clunk syndrome :

Patellar clunk syndrome is characterized by the formation of a fibrous nodule at the articular side of junction of the superior pole of patella and the quadriceps tendon. It is characterized by painful catching sensation or crepitus on knee extension. It is a well-recognized complication after total knee arthroplasty.

Pathology: It results from fibrosynovial hyperplasia at the junction of the superior pole of patella and quadriceps tendon. A nodule is formed which gets stuck within the intercondylar box during knee flexion. As the knee is extended this fibrosynovial nodule jumps out the box and a painful grinding sensation or crepitus is felt. The nodule is wedged into the intercondylar notch during flexion and dislodged on extension.

Causes and prevention of patellar clunk syndrome:

- It is mostly a complication of posterior stabilized (PS) knee arthroplasty- Chances of clunk syndrome are less with PS femoral components with a lower intercondylar box ratio (trochlear groove extended more posteriorly and distally)
- Undersized femoral component is more likely to cause patellar clunk syndrome
- Femoral components in a flexed position will increase the chances of development of patellar clunk.
- The neutral or posterior placement of the tibial tray may lessen the development of numerous patellar complications including crepitus and clunk.
- The patellar component should be placed as superior as possible, but not beyond the superior border of patella.
- Thin patella, implant overhang at the distal pole of patella, increased posterior offset, and forced knee flexion are the other risk factors

Diagnosis and treatment: Clinical findings can be confirmed by MRI. Treatment is open or arthroscopic excision of the fibrosynovial nodule.
