## Question

A 63-year-old patient with medial knee pain is wondering what nonoperative modalities you would recommend. According to the 2022 American Academy of Orthopaedic Surgeons Clinical Practice Guideline on Management of Osteoarthritis of the Knee, which of the following treatments was recommended and strongly supported?

![[q218820-a-pxl-20230117-171609735.jpg]]
*Figure A*

![[q218820-b-pxl-20230117-171603960-mp.jpg]]
*Figure B*

## Options

1. Acupuncture
2. Hyaluronic acid injections
3. Exercise
4. Weight loss
5. Arthroscopic lavage and/or debridement

## Correct option

**3. Exercise**

## Answer explanation

The 2022 American Academy of Orthopaedic Surgeons (AAOS) Clinical Practice Guideline (CPG) on Management of Osteoarthritis (OA) of the Knee gave a strong recommendation in support of exercise.

The goal of the AAOS CPG recommendations are to provide advice to practitioners treating these patients as to which modalities are evidence-based. In their most recent update for knee OA, they strongly recommend the following: exercise (supervised, unsupervised, and aquatic), patient education, oral/topical NSAIDS, and oral acetaminophen. They also strongly recommend against the use of oral narcotics and lateral wedge insoles. In a patient who has failed a home exercise program, medications like acetaminophen and NSAIDS (topical and/or oral) can be helpful in relieving pain and improving function.

*Brophy et al. published a systematic review outlining the recent 2022 AAOS clinic practice guidelines for the management of knee OA.* *They review all 29 recommendations, with 8 of them being strong recommendations.* Finally, **they note** there is more investigation needed into the use of intra-articular corticosteroid injections, hyaluronic acid injections and platelet-rich plasma injections.

*Bartels et al. conducted a Cochrane Database Systematic Review of randomized controlled trials on the efficacy of aquatic exercise for knee and hip osteoarthritis.* **They found** that aquatic exercise was associated with improvements in pain and quality of life. They endorsed aquatic exercise as a beneficial nonoperative modality in the treatment of knee and hip osteoarthritis.

Images A and B are are plain radiographs of the knee demonstrating osteoarthritis.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Acupuncture received a Limited recommendation that it may improve pain and function in patients with knee osteoarthritis.
Answer 2: Hyaluronic acid injections received a Moderate recommendation against.
Answer 4: Weight loss received a Moderate recommendation in support of it.
Answer 5: Arthroscopic lavage and/or debridement received a Moderate recommendation against.
