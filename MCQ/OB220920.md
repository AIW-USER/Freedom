## Question

A 31-year-old male presents to your office with a three-month history of bilateral foot pain located at the plantar aspect of the midfoot and hindfoot that is worse in the morning but improves after some activity. You recommend a trial of non-operative management, with a focus on stretching and pain control. Which of the following best describes the structure most likely causing this patient's pain?

## Options

1. Origin of the abductor hallucis
2. A connective tissue structure that originates on the medial calcaneal tubercle
3. A nerve that is the first branch of the lateral plantar nerve
4. A tendinous structure that inserts on the calcaneal tuberosity
5. A tendon that inserts on the navicular tuberosity and originates from the posterior surfaces of the tibia, fibula, and interosseous membrane

## Correct option

**2. A connective tissue structure that originates on the medial calcaneal tubercle**

## Answer explanation

The patient’s history and initial treatment strategy are most consistent with a diagnosis of plantar fasciitis. The plantar fascia is a connective tissue structure that originates from the medial calcaneal tubercle (Answer 2).

Plantar fasciitis is a condition characterized by inflammation of the plantar fascia, a connective tissue structure originating on the medial tubercle of the calcaneus and inserting on the metatarsal heads. Patients typically present with an insidious onset of plantar foot pain, predominantly located over the heel, that worsens at the beginning of the day and improves with ambulation. Treatment is non-operative for the majority of patients and consists of stretching exercises focused on the plantar fascia and Achilles tendon. Corticosteroid injections are second-line and typically avoided due to the risk of consequent fat pad atrophy or plantar fascia rupture. Plantar fasciotomies are reserved for patients who have failed extensive non-operative management and are associated with a high rate of post-operative complications.

*Pearce et al. prospectively assessed 33 patients with plantar fasciitis who were prescribed gastrocnemius stretching exercises to determine the relationship between gastrocnemius tightness and pain symptoms.* **They found** that gastrocnemius tightness was positively correlated with the severity of pain on first steps in the morning, as well as the worst pain felt. **They concluded** that, given the findings of their study, initial plantar fasciitis treatment should involve stretching of the posterior leg structures.

*DiGiovanni et al. performed a clinical trial assessing the efficacy of a plantar fascia-stretching or Achilles tendon-stretching program for patients with chronic plantar fasciitis.* **They found** that patients who underwent plantar fascia-stretching programs experienced significant improvement in symptoms. Additionally, those who initially received treatment with an Achilles tendon-stretching program but switched to a plantar fascia-stretching program showed the most improvement in their symptoms. **They concluded** that rehabilitation programs for chronic plantar fasciitis should favor stretches targeted at the plantar fascia over other soft tissue structures.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: This describes the calcaneal origin of the abductor hallucis, which is not the main structure implicated in plantar fasciitis.
Answer 3: This describes the inferior calcaneal nerve (Baxter’s nerve), which can get entrapped and produce symptoms similar to plantar fasciitis. However, symptoms typically persist after activity rather than improve.
Answer 4: This describes the Achilles tendon, which is not the main structure implicated in plantar fasciitis, though it is a target for stretching exercises used in the rehabilitation of plantar fasciitis.
Answer 5: This describes the posterior tibialis tendon, which is not the main structure implicated in plantar fasciitis.
