## Options

1. Low risk of developing osteonecrosis
2. High risk of developing osteonecrosis
3. Low risk of developing post-traumatic arthritis
4. High risk of developing valgus malunion
5. Low risk of developing a varus malunion

## Correct option

**1. Low risk of developing osteonecrosis**

## Answer explanation

This patient was treated with open reduction and internal fixation (ORIF) of a talar neck fracture. His two-month postoperative radiographs demonstrate a Hawkins sign, a subchondral lucency resulting from resorption of subchondral bone indicating intact vascularity and therefore a low risk of developing osteonecrosis.

The overall risk of osteonecrosis following talar neck fractures is approximately 31%. The presence of the Hawkins sign, or subchondral lucency of the talar dome, is best seen on a mortise radiograph and indicates viability of the talar body. Once a Hawkins sign appears, it is unlikely that that the patient will develop osteonecrosis. A Hawkins sign will typically appear at 6 to 8 weeks after the fracture, though the absence of a Hawkins sign at that time does not necessarily indicate osteonecrosis.

*Early et al. reviewed talus fractures.* **The authors report** that displaced fractures require open reduction of the major joint surfaces and rigid internal fixation. **They concluded** that despite aggressive management, complications involving osteonecrosis and posttraumatic arthritis of both the subtalar and tibiotalar joints remain frequent.

*Leduc et al. reviewed osteonecrosis of the talus following posttraumatic reconstruction of the hindfoot.* **The authors concluded** that adequate knowledge of the etiology, extent of disease, and degree of patient symptomatology are required to determine optimal treatment, and that osteonecrosis remains a well-established complication following these injuries.

*Tezval et al. retrospectively reviewed a series of displaced talar fractures that were treated surgically.* **They reported** that the Hawkins sign demonstrated a 100% sensitivity and 57.5% specificity for the development of osteonecrosis. Moreover, **the authors found** that the Hawkins sign (if present) appeared between 6 and 9 weeks after trauma. **They concluded** that the Hawkins sign was a good indicator of talus vascularity following fracture, and if a full or partial positive Hawkins sign is detected, it is unlikely that osteonecrosis will develop at a later stage after injury.

Figures A and B are the AP and lateral radiographs of the ankle demonstrating subchondral lucency indicating intact vascularity with resorption of subchondral bone (the Hawkins sign).

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: The presence of the Hawkins sign is a favorable prognostic indicator suggesting that osteonecrosis is unlikely.
Answer 3: Post-traumatic arthritis is relatively common with subtalar and tibiotalar post-traumatic arthritis being present in 50% and 33% of cases, respectively.
Answer 4: Valgus malunion is a rare complication following talar neck fractures.
Answer 5: Varus malunion may occur in up to 30% of cases and leads to decreased subtalar eversion, decreased motion with locked midfoot and hindfoot, and causes weight bearing on the lateral border of the foot.
