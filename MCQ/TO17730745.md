## Question

A young male was given interscalene brachial plexus analgesia for arthroscopic bankarts repair for recurrent shoulder dislocation. In the postoperative period analgesia was maintained with a top-up of 0.2% bupivacaine every 8 h through the interscalene catheter. During the postoperative round he complained of facial discomfort. His picture is given below. True about the diagnosis and treatment is- (general orthopedics- complications of anesthesia)

![[TO17730745-1.png]]

## Options

1. Facial nerve palsy, give Iv steroids
2. Horner syndrome, remove interscalene catheter
3. Dry eye, artificial tears
4. Facial nerve palsy, stop bupivacaine

## Correct option

**2. Horner syndrome, remove interscalene catheter**

## Answer explanation

Patient given in the picture has typical facial features of Horner's syndrome.

Complications associated with the interscalene approach are temporary paresis of the phrenic or recurrent laryngeal nerves, Horner's syndrome, pneumothorax, hematoma, epidural or spinal anesthesia, loss of function of the cervical spinal cord or injection into the vertebral artery.

Components of Horner’s syndrome are meiosis, ptosis, enophthalmia, anhidrosis, and conjunctival hyperemia. Horner's syndrome results from paralysis of the ipsilateral sympathetic cervical chain (stellate ganglion) caused by surgery, drugs (mainly high concentrations of local anesthetics), local compression (hematoma or tumor) or inadequate perioperative positioning of the patient.

Horner syndrome caused by an interscalene catheter (due to excessive local anesthetic delivery) is usually reversible after removal of the catheter.

Answer: 2
