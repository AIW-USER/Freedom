## Question

A 17-year-old male presents with left knee pain and swelling after playing football three days ago. On physical exam, he has a large effusion with limited knee flexion due to pain. His radiograph is shown in Figure A. What does the finding in the radiograph represent?

![[q213068-a-segond-fx.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Avulsion fracture of the anterior cruciate ligament
2. Avulsion fracture of the biceps femoris
3. Lateral tibial plateau fracture
4. Avulsion fracture of the anterolateral ligament
5. Avulsion fracture of the lateral collateral ligament

## Correct option

**4. Avulsion fracture of the anterolateral ligament**

## Answer explanation

The patient is presenting with an acute anterior cruciate ligament (ACL) rupture with radiographs depicting a Ségond fracture. This radiographic finding represents an avulsion fracture of the anterolateral ligament (ALL) from the tibial plateau.

Ruptures of the ACL commonly occur as a result of a non-contact pivoting injury. Females are more prone to this injury due to landing biomechanics and neuromuscular activation. The ACL prevents anterior tibial translation and tibial rotation. The anterior translation event that occurs during an acute tear can lead to a Ségond fracture, which manifests as a small avulsion fracture of the lateral tibial plateau. This fragment represents the attachment of the anterolateral ligament (ALL), which is a capsuloligamentous structure. The presence of this fracture on knee radiographs is highly suggestive of an ACL rupture.

*Hess et al. performed a retrospective study of ACL ruptures and associated Ségond fractures.* Approximately 9% of patients that presented with an acute ACL rupture had a concomitant Ségond fracture, whereas only 1% of patients presenting with knee injuries without an ACL tear had the same finding. **They concluded** that Ségond fractures seen on knee radiographs are highly suggestive of ACL ruptures.

*Shaikh et al. performed a case series of patients that presented with ACL ruptures and Ségond fractures.* MRI and CT scan findings suggested that the avulsion fragment occurs at the location of the ALL insertion on the tibia as well as the insertion points of the joint capsule and the posterior bundle of the iliotibial band. **The authors concluded** that the ALL, the lateral joint capsule insertion to the tibia, and the posterior bundle of the ITB form the anterolateral complex which is responsible for the Ségond fragment in ACL ruptures.

Figure A depicts a notch view radiograph of the right knee with a lateral avulsion fragment consistent with a Ségond fracture. Illustration A is a diagram of the ALL and the relationship to other lateral ligamentous knee structures.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: An avulsion fracture of the ACL would demonstrate the involvement of the intercondylar eminence.
Answer 2: An avulsion fracture of the biceps femoris would result in the arcuate sign, with the origin off of the fibular head.
Answer 3: A lateral tibial plateau fracture would be more extensive and through the weight-bearing region of the articular surface than what is depicted in this patient's radiograph.
Answer 5: An avulsion fracture of the LCL would also result in the arcuate sign, with the origin off of the fibular head.
