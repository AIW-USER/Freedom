## Question

Which of the following best describes the relationship of the subtalar and transverse tarsal joints during the phases of gait?

## Options

1. Eversion of the subtalar joint locks the transverse tarsal joint
2. Transverse tarsal (Chopart) joint axes are parallel during heel strike
3. The calcaneus is in inversion throughout stance phase
4. Tibialis anterior concentrically contracts during stance phase
5. During push-off the foot becomes flexible due to eversion of the calcaneus

## Correct option

**2. Transverse tarsal (Chopart) joint axes are parallel during heel strike**

## Answer explanation

The transverse tarsal (Chopart) joints consist of the talonavicular and calcaneocuboid joints. The transverse tarsal axes are parallel during most of the heel strike phase when the subtalar joint is everted.

Eversion of the subtalar joint unlocks the transverse tarsal joints so that it is flexible and provides shock absorption during heel strike. Additionally, subtalar joint inversion locks the transverse tarsal joints so that the foot is inflexible and provides a rigid lever arm for push-off.

*Erdemir et al.* **found** that plantar aponeurosis forces gradually increased during stance and peaked in late stance, with maximum tension reaching 96% of total body weight. **The authors concluded** the plantar aponeurosis transmits large forces between the hindfoot and forefoot during the stance phase of gait.

Illustration A is a drawing depicting the relationship of the subtalar joint with everted and inverted postions (left to right on the illustration). Note that the transverse tarsal axes (talonavicular and calcaneocuboid joints) are parallel during eversion and deviated with inversion.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Eversion of the subtalar joint unlocks the transverse tarsal joint.
Answer 3: During heel strike the calcaneus is mostly in eversion.
Answer 4: Tibialis anterior eccentrically contracts during stance phase.
Answer 5: During push-off the foot becomes inflexible with inversion of the calcaneus.
