## Question

A nine-year-old female weighing approximately 35 kilograms presents to the pediatric emergency room after she was involved in a motor vehicle accident. She is found to have an isolated transverse diaphyseal femur fracture with a centimeter of shortening. Which of the following treatment strategies is most appropriate for this patient?

## Options

1. Flexible intramedullary nailing
2. Rigid trochanteric start intramedullary nailing
3. Rigid lateral entry start intramedullary nailing
4. Spica casting
5. Submuscular plating

## Correct option

**1. Flexible intramedullary nailing**

## Answer explanation

Pediatric femoral diaphyseal fractures in patients between 5 and 11 years of age who weigh under 49 kilograms should be treated with flexible intramedullary nailing (Answer 1).

Pediatric femoral diaphyseal fractures are among the most common orthopedic injuries. Optimal treatment strategies largely depend on patient age and fracture characteristics. Patients under 6 months of age may be treated with a Pavlik harness or Spica casting. Those aged 6 months to 5 years may be treated with spica casting. History and physical should be conducted with care in these two age groups, as a femur fracture in this population may be a sign of child abuse, especially if the history does not adequately align with childhood milestones. Patients between 5 and 11 years of age who weigh under 49 kilograms may be treated with flexible titanium nailing, while those with comminuted or spiral fractures that are inherently length unstable may be treated with submuscular plating. Patients aged 11 years or older who weigh more than 49 kilograms may be treated with rigid intramedullary nailing. However, piriformis-start nails should be avoided in the pediatric population because of an increased risk of femoral head avascular necrosis from injury to the medial femoral circumflex artery (MFCA). Other post-operative complications include limb length discrepancy, which can result from overgrowth or shortening of the injured leg; and malunion, which is more common in older, heavier patients undergoing flexible nailing.

*Kocher et al. published a clinical practice guideline specifically for the treatment of pediatric diaphyseal femur fractures.* *They summarize the recommended treatment strategies for this injury in specific age groups and fracture presentations.* These include the use of spica casting/Pavlik harness for those 6 months and younger, spica casting for patients 6 months to 5 years in age, flexible intramedullary nailing in patients 5 to 11 years in age, perioperative regional pain management, the use of waterproof cast liners for those treated with spica casts, and child abuse screening for patients under the 3 years of age.

*Moroz et al. performed an international multicenter retrospective review of 229 patients with diaphyseal femur fractures treated with titanium elastic nailing to describe outcomes and risk factors for postoperative complications.* **They found** that 65% of patients had excellent outcomes and 25% had satisfactory outcomes. 41 major complications were reported, which included angulation exceeding guidelines at follow-up, loss reduction, nail revision, or limb length discrepancy. Patients weighing over 49 kg and those 11 years or older were at a higher risk for complications. **They concluded** that titanium elastic nailing is an acceptable treatment strategy for school-aged children with diaphyseal femur fractures.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 2 and 3: Rigid intramedullary nailing via either a trochanteric or lateral entry start point is an acceptable treatment for patients older than 11 years of age and/or weighing more than 49 kilograms.
Answer 4: Spica casting is typically reserved for patients under 5 years of age.
Answer 5: Submuscular plating for pediatric femoral nail fractures is typically reserved for patients between 5 and 11 years of age with a length-unstable fracture pattern.
