## Question

A 55-year-old female presents with low back pain. The pain has been present for 3 years, and is located in the lower back with radiation down both legs. The pain is intermittent and worsens with prolonged walking or activity. She is no longer able to work as a realtor as she cannot walk for more than 10 minutes before being limited by leg pain. Nonoperative treatment with physical therapy, anti-inflammatory, and muscle relaxant medications have not provided significant relief. Her radiographs and a transverse cut of L4/5 MRI are shown in Figures A and B. What treatment should you offer her to have the best long-term improvement in bodily pain and physical function?

## Options

1. Lumbar disc replacement
2. Minimally invasive laminectomy
3. Minimally invasive microdiscectomy
4. Open laminectomy without fusion
5. Open laminectomy with instrumented fusion

## Correct option

**5. Open laminectomy with instrumented fusion**

## Answer explanation

The patient has neurogenic claudication in the setting of central and lateral recess stenosis, as well as degenerative spondylolisthesis at L4/5. A laminectomy with instrumented fusion will provide the best long-term improvement in bodily pain and physical function.

Degenerative spondylolisthesis is a common degenerative condition of the lumbar spine. The degenerative cascade can lead to neurologic symptoms via central, lateral recess as well as foraminal stenosis. In this case, the neurogenic claudication due to central stenosis can be addressed with a laminectomy and decompression, but the instability as evident by the spondylolisthesis necessitates an instrumented fusion. Reduction of the spondylolisthesis remains an option but is not correlated with clinical outcomes.

*Abdu et al. completed the 8-year analysis of the SPORT trial, a randomized trial with a concurrent observational cohort for the treatment of lumbar degenerative spondylolisthesis.* *The as-treated analysis in both randomized and observational groups showed greater improvement with surgical treatment on all primary outcome measures through the 8-year follow-up.*

*Knaub et al. compiled an instructional course lecture reviewing the indications for fusion and spinal instrumentation in lumbar spinal stenosis.* Recommendations for arthrodesis include patients with degenerative spondylolisthesis, scoliosis, or kyphosis, as well as stenosis adjacent to a previously fused segment.

Herkowitz and Kurz completed a prospective study comparing decompression versus decompression with fusion for degenerative lumbar spondylolisthesis with spinal stenosis. At a mean follow-up of 3 years, the patients who had a concomitant arthrodesis had significantly better outcomes with respect to pain in the back and lower limbs.

Figure A shows a lateral lumbar radiograph with spondylolisthesis at the L4/5 level. Figure B is an axial MRI cut showing central stenosis and foraminal stenosis.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Lumbar disc replacement is not recommended in the setting of instability.
Answer 2 & 4: While somewhat controversial, an isolated decompression has been shown to be inferior to decompression and fusion in patients with spondylolisthesis.
Answer 3: A microdiscectomy would not completely address the source of neural compression.
