## Question

A Grammont-style reverse shoulder arthroplasty places the center of rotation of the shoulder medially and inferiorly. In addition to increased shoulder abduction, which motion gains the greatest biomechanical advantage following use of this design?

## Options

1. Adduction
2. External rotation with the arm at 90 degrees
3. External rotation with the arm at the side
4. Forward elevation
5. Internal rotation

## Correct option

**4. Forward elevation**

## Answer explanation

The Grammont reverse shoulder arthroplasty (RSA) functions by medializing and distalizing the center of rotation (COR). This tensions the deltoid and achieves a more constrained articulation, allowing for shear forces of shoulder abduction to be converted into a compressive force, increasing both shoulder abduction and forward elevation.

The RSA utilizes a convex glenoid and a concave humerus to reconstruct the glenohumeral joint. The Grammont-style RSA places the COR inferiorly and medially. This allows the deltoid muscle to act on a longer fulcrum and have more of a mechanical advantage by substituting for deficient rotator cuff muscles. This allows for improvements in shoulder abduction and forward elevation. Unfortunately, this design does not have significant contributions to shoulder internal or external rotation. Use of a latissimus dorsi transfer may be needed to assist with external rotation deficits.

*Grammont et al. first described the use of the "delta" RSA by reporting on its use in 14 patients.* *They reported on the novel concept of placing the ball of the shoulder on the glenoid and the concave matching surface on the humerus in patients with an absent rotator cuff.* **They concluded** that the deltoid muscle could compensate for an absent rotator cuff if four conditions were present: a lever arm effective from the start of movement; a fixed center of rotation; inherent stability; and maintenance of adequate external rotation.

*Boileau et al. reviewed the midterm results and potential complications of the of the Grammont-style RSA in 45 patients.* **They reported** a significant increase in active elevation (55° to 121°) but no significant change in active external rotation (7° to 11°) or internal rotation. **They found** that atrophy of severe fatty infiltration of the teres minor was associated with lower external rotation (15° vs 0°). **They concluded** that the Grammont RSA can improve function and restore active elevation in patients with incongruent cuff-deficient shoulders; active rotation is usually unchanged.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Adduction is not greatly increased with the use of the Grammont RSA
Answers 2&3:External rotation is not increased with use of the Grammont RSA. With external rotation deficits, use of a latissimus dorsi transfer may be indicated
Answer 5: Similar to external rotation, internal rotation does not receive significant biomechanical advantages with the use of a Grammont RSA
