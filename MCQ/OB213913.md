## Question

A 24-year-old man presents with increasing left thigh deformity and swelling that started at a young age. Radiographs are obtained and demonstrate peripheral bone formation with a central lucent area. He is found to have an ACVR1 mutation. Which of the following is the most likely diagnosis?

## Options

1. Melorheostosis
2. Fibrodysplasia ossificans progressiva (FOP)
3. Paget's disease
4. Fibrous dysplasia
5. Multiple hereditary exostosis (MHE)

## Correct option

**2. Fibrodysplasia ossificans progressiva (FOP)**

## Answer explanation

This patient was found to have an ACVR1 mutation, which is seen in fibrodysplasia ossificans progressive (FOP).

Fibrodysplasia ossificans progressive (FOP) is a rare disease characterized by progressive heterotopic ossification (muscles, fascia, tendons, ligaments and joint capsules) and congenital malformations of the great toe (hallux valgus, malformed first metatarsal and monophalangism). It is associated with a mutation in the ACVR1 (activin A type I receptor) gene, which is a BMP type-1 receptor. BMP4, which contributes to the formation of the skeleton in the normal embryo, is implicated in this disease.

*Barruet et al. performed a study to elucidate the biochemistry of the ACVR1 mutation found in fibrodysplasia ossificans progressiva.* **They found** that BMP can induce endothelial cell dysfunction, increase expression of fibrogenic matrix proteins, and cause differential downstream signaling of the ACVR1 mutation.

*Hildebrand et al. performed a study to evaluate the signalling responses of ACVR1 to different ligands.* **They found** that, compared to normal ACVR1 receptors, the ACVR1 receptors with the mutation found in FOP is more sensitive to a number of natural ligands. **They concluded** that the mutant receptor lost some essential inhibitory interactions with its ligands and co-receptors, thereby conferring an enhanced ligand-dependent signalling and stimulating ectopic bone formation as seen in patients with FOP.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Melorheostosis is a rare benign painful disorder of the extremities characterized by formation of periosteal new bone and is not associated with an ACVR1 mutation.
Answer 3: Paget's disease is a disorder of abnormal bone remodeling. During the sclerotic phase, it appears as bony enlargement with cortical thickening. It is not associated with an ACVR1 mutation.
Answer 4: Fibrous dysplasia appears as large lytic and expansile lesions with well-defined margins of sclerotic bone and is associated with a GNAS mutation, not an ACVR1 mutation.
Answer 5: Multiple hereditary exostosis appears as sessile (broad base) or pedunculated (narrow stalk) lesions found on the surface of bones and is associated with an EXT mutation, not an ACVR1 mutation.
