## Question

A 32-year-old male presents to the clinic two weeks after sustaining a type 3A open tibial shaft fracture while he was on vacation. He underwent irrigation and debridement, fasciotomies, and acute intramedullary nailing by an outside institution as shown in Figure A. Both iliac crest autograft and reamer-irrigator-aspirator (RIA) bone grafting are discussed with the patient. When compared to iliac crest autologous bone harvesting, which of the following is true regarding RIA?

![[q219141-a-figure-a.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Requires less expensive equipment
2. Higher incidence of lateral femoral cutaneous nerve injury
3. Obtains an overall lower volume of bone graft
4. Produces a more structurally supportive bone graft
5. Increased risk for blood loss requiring post-operative transfusion

## Correct option

**5. Increased risk for blood loss requiring post-operative transfusion**

## Answer explanation

The reamer-irrigator-aspirator (RIA) is an autogenous bone graft harvesting technique utilizing the cancellous and endosteum bone within the intramedullary canal of the femur or tibia. While it can produce a substantially larger volume of bone graft compared to autogenous graft obtained from the iliac crest, this incurs an associated increased blood loss and higher risk for requiring a post-operative transfusion.

The reamer/irrigator/aspirator (RIA) system was originally developed to reduce the risk of fat embolism and thermal necrosis associated with reaming long bone fractures. While the RIA has not been shown to live up to its originally intended purpose, it has been shown to produce an abundant volume of autogenous bone graft (~50-90 ccs per long bone). It lacks the structural support of other, cortical autogenous bone grafts (i.e. iliac crest bone graft), however, the collected intramedullary debris from the RIA system maintains the key features of an optimal graft with osteoconductive, osteoinductive, and osteogenic properties. Early critics questioned the viability of the included cells within the reamed graft debris. However, flow cytometry studies have shown >95% cell viability and cellular analysis has demonstrated the process of reaming may increase the osteogenic potential of harvested cells (via molecular signals released from necrotic cells). In general, the RIA is faster to perform, produces higher volumes of bone graft, contains higher concentrations of growth factors and mesenchymal stem cells, and is considered technically easier than that of autologous bone graft harvesting from the iliac crest (Illustration A). However, the RIA incurs significant blood loss, with up to 44% of patients requiring transfusion in some retrospective cohort studies. This potential complication should be discussed with patients during the informed consent process.

Higgins and Marchand provided a comprehensive review of the basic science and clinical application of RIA bone grafting. The authors discuss that the biochemical and cellular profile of RIA is equal or superior to that of iliac crest bone grafting. They emphasize that while RIA has a lower risk for donor site morbidity and higher reported graft volumes, it also results in a substantially higher volume of blood loss that often requires transfusion. They conclude that the RIA serves as a promising alternative to iliac crest bone grafting, however, its risks and benefits should be considered in each patient.

*Dawson and colleagues performed a randomized control trial to compare the union rates of RIA and iliac crest bone grafting when treating nonunions or post-traumatic bone defects.* **They found** that while the cost for the RIA equipment was substantially higher, it was a faster procedure to perform and therefore cost-effective. Both groups had similar union rates and time to union with no difference in reoperation rates. **The authors concluded** that RIA yields a greater volume of bone graft, shorter harvesting time, and a similar complication profile to that of iliac crest bone grafting.

Figure A shows a postoperative image of this patient’s tibia demonstrating a segmental bone defect stabilized with an intramedullary nail. Illustration A is a comparison table of different bone graft harvesting sites found in the Higgins and Marchand review article.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1. The RIA system requires more expensive equipment, however, is faster to perform than iliac crest bone harvest. It is purported to be overall cheaper due to the cost savings of shorter operative times.
Answer 2. A higher incidence of lateral femoral cutaneous nerve injury is seen with anterior iliac crest bone harvesting. The RIA approaches do not place the nerve at risk.
Answer 3. The RIA system obtains an overall HIGHER volume of bone graft.
Answer 4. The RIA involves reaming out the intramedullary canal of lower extremity long bones with the largest bone fragments obtainable being 2mm in size. These do not provide the same structural support as an iliac crest bone graft containing tricortical bone.
