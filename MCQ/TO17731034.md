## Question

Most common forearm deformity in hereditary multiple exostosis is (orthopedic oncology- benign bone tumors and tumor like lesion- Osteochondroma and multiple hereditary exostosis )

## Options

1. Ulnar exostosis with radial head dislocation
2. Radial exostosis with ulnar shortening
3. Ulnar exostosis with radial bowing
4. Radial exostosis with ulnar bowing

## Correct option

**3. Ulnar exostosis with radial bowing**

## Answer explanation

MHE is a monogenetic, autosomal dominant disorder, mainly caused by loss of function mutations in two genes: exostosin-1 (EXT1) and exostosin-2 (EXT2), located on chromosome 8 and chromosome 11 respectively.

Symptoms manifest during childhood. Exostosis most commonly involves distal femur followed by proximal tibia, fibula, and humerus. They are rarely located in ribs, carpal and tarsal bones, and never in the facial bones because they develop by intramembranous ossification.

Clinical features: Pain is experienced by the majority of patients due to compression of tendons and muscles, Sometimes, bursitis over the cartilage cup may develop causing pain. Restricted motion of the joint is often encountered due to mechanical block by osteochondroma located near the joint.

Osteochondromas found in the forearm, can lead to unbalanced shortening of the ulna, with consequent higher curved radius and subluxation or dislocation of the radial head. Masada classified forearm deformities of HME into three types

Type I- the distal ulna has the greatest exostosis formation. The ulna is shortened with bowing of the radius, but the radial head is not affected. The proximal part of the radius is not dislocated. Tapering of the ulnar head and ulnar tilt of the distal radius are both present. This deformity is the most common forearm deformity in HME.

Type II- the radial head is dislocated and the ulna is shortened. Bowing of the radius is less severe than in the type I deformity, secondary to dislocation of the radial head. In type IIa, the radial head is dislocated as a result of exostosis formation at the proximal metaphysis of the radius. In type IIb, radial head dislocation occurs in the absence of a proximal radial exostosis.

In type III, the primary exostosis formation is in the metaphysis of the distal radius, with relative shortening of the radius compared with the ulna.

In the hip most common presentation is coxa valga, due to exostoses near the lesser trochanter. Valgus deformities are also common in the knee and ankle. HME can lead to disproportionate short stature. Other complications include hemo/pneumothorax (due to costal osteochondroma), cesarean section (pelvic osteochondroma), and scoliosis.

Answer: 3
