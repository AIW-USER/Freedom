## Question

A 14-year-old boy with a known diagnosis of Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD) presents for a routine orthopedic follow-up. He has been wheelchair-bound for the past 3 years. His parents report that he has been leaning to the right side more frequently while sitting. Physical examination reveals a flexible thoracolumbar scoliosis. Recent pulmonary function tests indicate a forced vital capacity (FVC) of 55%. Radiographs demonstrate a Cobb angle of 25°. Based on the patient's diagnosis and radiographic findings, what is the most appropriate next step in management?

## Options

1. Anterior spinal fusion with instrumentation
2. Continued observation with serial radiographs
3. Custom-molded thoracolumbosacral orthosis (TLSO)
4. Posterior spinal fusion to the pelvis
5. Referral for physical therapy and core strengthening

## Correct option

**4. Posterior spinal fusion to the pelvis**

## Answer explanation

In patients with Duchenne muscular dystrophy, scoliosis curves reaching 20° to 30° are progressive and significantly impact pulmonary function, necessitating early posterior spinal fusion, as nonoperative measures are ineffective.

Duchenne muscular dystrophy is characterized by progressive muscle weakness that leads to the loss of ambulation and the subsequent development of neuromuscular scoliosis. Unlike idiopathic scoliosis, the curves in DMD are relentlessly progressive and coincide with a decline in pulmonary function. The "operative window" for these patients is earlier than in other etiologies; historically, spinal fusion was recommended once curves exceeded 20° to 30° because bracing (nonoperative treatment) has been proven ineffective in halting curve progression or preserving pulmonary function. Early posterior spinal fusion, often extending to the pelvis to address pelvic obliquity, maintains a straight spine, betters sitting balance, and facilitates care, although the direct impact on prolonging survival or halting pulmonary decline remains a topic of ongoing research.

*Garg reviewed the literature regarding the management of spinal deformities in neuromuscular conditions like DMD.* **He noted** that bracing is generally ineffective and that surgery is standardly recommended when curves progress beyond 20° to 30° to maintain sitting balance and comfort. **He concluded** that early surgical intervention with posterior spinal fusion is the preferred management to prevent severe deformity and its associated morbidity.

*Yang et al. evaluated the survival outcomes and quality of life differences between DMD patients treated surgically versus non-surgically for scoliosis.* **They noted** that while surgery reliably corrects the deformity and improves nursing care/sitting balance, the direct correlation with extended survival compared to non-surgical controls was not statistically significant in their specific cohort analysis. **They concluded** that while the survival benefit is debated, surgical correction remains indicated for the functional and quality-of-life benefits it provides to patients and caregivers.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Anterior-only approaches are generally insufficient for the long, collapsing C-shaped neuromuscular curves seen in DMD and carry higher pulmonary risks.
Answer 2: Observation is inappropriate because a 25° curve in a DMD patient is already within the operative threshold and will inevitably progress.
Answer 3: Bracing has been shown to be ineffective in halting the progression of neuromuscular scoliosis in Duchenne muscular dystrophy.
Answer 5: While supportive, physical therapy cannot overcome the progressive nature of the neuromuscular curve or the underlying muscle wasting, making it an insufficient standalone treatment for a 25° deformity.
