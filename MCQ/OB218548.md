## Question

A 14-year-old middle school female gymnast was referred by her pediatrician for treatment of new onset left knee pain. The patient states she fell during tumbling practice a week ago and it has been hurting ever since that time despite trying to practice through the discomfort. She is ligamentously stable on exam with some pain with terminal flexion. The patient’s radiographic imaging from the office is shown in Figure A noting a 1.2cm shorter left limb without any fractures. Upon discussion, the mother states she always knew her daughter had slightly offsetting limb lengths which may limit her ability to qualify for the young stars league. Which of the following is the next best step in management?

![[q218548-a-corrected-lld.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Await skeletal maturity then perform corrective left limb lengthening
2. Epiphysiodesis right distal femur for approximately 1.5 years
3. Epiphysiodesis right proximal tibia for approximately 2 years
4. Long leg cast immobilization for 6 weeks
5. Non-steroidal inflammatory medications, refrain from gymnastics for one week

## Correct option

**5. Non-steroidal inflammatory medications, refrain from gymnastics for one week**

## Answer explanation

This patient is likely experiencing an acute ligamentous strain of her left knee, for which the RICE (rest, ice, compression, elevation) protocol should be pursued. Prior to her fall, this patient had an asymptomatic limb length discrepancy of less than 2cm and should not undergo any corrective treatments at this time.

Pediatric limb length discrepancies (LLD) are most often asymptomatic and found incidentally on radiographic imaging. The majority of patients’ LLD is less than 2cm, asymptomatic, and lacks negative long-term clinical implications (as seen in this patient). If the patient begins to endorse chronic symptoms (pain, noticeable asymmetry), they should be treated initially with a shoe lift, whereas larger deformities, angular deformities, or persistent symptomatic LLDs failing conservative management should be considered for corrective procedures. Adolescents with larger (>2cm), symptomatic LLDs who have not yet reached skeletal maturity may be managed with epiphysiodesis procedures of the contralateral limb to equalize LLDs over time. Skeletally mature patients may require shortening osteotomies or limb-lengthening procedures. The patient presented was asymptomatic prior to her fall and did not previously know she had a limb length discrepancy. It is important to tailor conversations with patients and their parents regarding the inconsequential long-term outcomes of small LLDs. For the time being, she should be treated conservatively until her acute knee strain resolves.

Dr. Sabharwal and Dr. Kumar reviewed the different methods for measuring limb length inequalities, ranging from physical examination tape measurement to more advanced micro digital radiography and MRI scans. They stress that the ideal method for assessing limb length discrepancy may be different depending on equipment availability however overall imaging modalities are more appropriate than physical examination alone. They conclude that full-length standing AP computed radiographs of both lower extremities with the pelvis level, along with the use of a magnification marker, should be the primary imaging modality for the initial evaluation of limb length discrepancies in the majority of patients.

*Hubbard and colleagues performed a comprehensive review of assessing limb length inequality and appropriate treatment options considering expected final limb lengths.* *They discussed the expected adolescent growth rates used to plan corrective procedures and length of growth modulation to be as follows:*

- Proximal femur: 3mm
- Distal femur: 9mm
- Proximal tibia: 6mm
- Distal tibia: 4mm

They state that symptomatic limb inequality lengths of <2cm are most often asymptomatic. However, if these patients endorse symptoms, they should be treated initially with a shoe lift unless there is an unacceptable angular deformity in the shortened limb.

Figure A shows standing radiographic imaging of a skeletally immature female with a small limb length discrepancy (12mm longer right femur). There are no acute fractures.

Incorrect Answers:

Answer 1: Awaiting skeletal maturity then performing corrective left limb lengthening would be considered a reasonable option if this patient is near skeletal maturity with a larger LLD (>4cm).
Answer 2: Epiphysiodesis right distal femur for approximately 1.5 years would lead to an overall correction of her limb length deformity (9mm x 1.5 years = 13mm correction), however, is not indicated in this patient at this time as she was previously asymptomatic prior to her fall. Generally speaking, female adolescents reach skeletal maturity earlier than male adolescents (females ~14 years, males ~16 years). Therefore, at age 14 she is likely near skeletal maturity and skeletal aging would be prudent to determine how much expected growth she has remaining.
Answer 3: Epiphysiodesis right proximal tibia for approximately 2 years would lead to an overall correction of her limb length deformity (6mm x 2 years = 12mm correction), however, is not indicated in this patient at this time as she was previously asymptomatic prior to her fall.
Answer 4: A long leg cast would be indicated if there were a present fracture or nondisplaced physeal injury. Given that this patient can continue to perform in gymnastics, it is unlikely to be more than a ligamentous strain.
