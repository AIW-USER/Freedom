## Question

You are asked to see an 16-year-old patient by his pediatrician after a lesion is found in the child's distal fibula by radiographs taken for a sprained ankle (Figure A). The child is otherwise healthy, active, and has no pain or limitation of motion. Your management should consist of:

![[q529-a-non-ossifying-fibroma1.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Non-weight bearing short leg cast
2. Tumor staging including chest CT, bone scan, MRI of entire bone
3. Contacting local child protective services
4. Activities as tolerated, repeat radiographs in 3 to 6 months
5. Curettage and allograft bone packing to lesion.

## Correct option

**4. Activities as tolerated, repeat radiographs in 3 to 6 months**

## Answer explanation

The history and radiographs suggest a benign bony lesion, consistent with a non-ossifying fibroma. As the child is having no symptoms, conservative management consisting of activities as tolerated with repeat xrays in 3 to 6 months is the best choice. Asymptomatic benign tumors do not need tumor staging, curettage, casting, or contact of child protective services,

In the review by Rougraff, the history, diagnosis, evaluation, treatment, and management of benign tumors of bone are reviewed.

*Arata et al present 23 patients over 49 years that sustained a fracture through a NOF.* In each paitent the percentage of bone occupied by the fibroma in the transverse plane was greater than 50% on AP and lateral radiographs.
