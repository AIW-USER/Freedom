## Question

A 59-year-old male present with left ankle pain and drainage 3 years after surgery for a traumatic injury to the left ankle. He notes worsening pain over the past year. On examination ankle range of motion is limited to a 10-degree arc of motion with erythema and serous drainage from an anterior ankle incision. Figures A and B are his current radiographs. The patient requests a discussion of limb salvage surgery. Of the following, which is the best surgical plan for his condition?

## Options

1. Maintenance of prior hardware and simultaneous arthrodesis
2. Maintenance of prior hardware and staged arthrodesis
3. Removal of hardware, I&D, and simultaneous arthrodesis
4. Removal of hardware, I&D, and staged arthrodesis
5. Removal of hardware, I&D, and simultaneous ankle arthroplasty

## Correct option

**4. Removal of hardware, I&D, and staged arthrodesis**

## Answer explanation

Given the persistent wound drainage, the surgical plan should include removing the current hardware and eradication of the infection prior to placement of new hardware.

Painful post-traumatic ankle arthritis in the setting of infection is a difficult surgical problem. Patients are faced with salvage options of arthrodesis or amputation. Although no one single treatment algorithm is recommended, aggressive debridement of infected and nonviable tissue with a course of antibiotics is recommended prior to attempted arthrodesis. Patient selection is crucial to a successful outcome and preoperative education of goals of surgery, expectations, and salvage amputation should be thoroughly discussed.

*Anakwenze et al. review the various types of infections throughout the foot and ankle.* With regard to deep infection, **they state** in patients with compromised bone structure external fixation is a viable option to stabilize the limb during the treatment of infection. **They state** amputation is the salvage option if all treatment fails to eradicate the infection.

*Baumhauer et al. discuss the preoperative, intraoperative, and postoperative treatment of patients undergoing arthrodesis of the ankle or subtalar joint.* **They state** thorough irrigation and debridement with stabilization is needed to eradicate the infection. **They also recommend** liberal consultation of infectious disease, vascular, and plastic surgery teams.

Figures A and B show an AP and lateral, respectively, of the ankle demonstrating significant post-traumatic arthritis and deformity. Illustration A shows a lateral radiograph of the final arthrodesis construct after staged irrigation and debridement.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1 & 2- The prior hardware is likely infected given continued long-term drainage from the incision. This should be removed and the infection should be cleared prior to placement of new hardware.
Answer 3 & 5- The infection should be treated and cleared prior to placement of new hardware.
