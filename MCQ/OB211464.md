## Question

A 66-year-old female obtained a DEXA scan that was ordered by her PCP and now her PCP is asking for your treatment recommendations. Her DEXA scan demonstrated a T score of -2.8. She does report a history of smoking and alcohol use. Based on her FRAX score, her 10-year risk for hip fracture is 10%. This being said, you recommend treatment with teriparatide. Which of the following is true regarding the use of this medication?

## Options

1. Decreases alkaline phosphatase
2. May cause hyperphosphatemia
3. Preferentially decreases risk of non-verterbral fractures
4. Inhibits osteoclastogenesis
5. Stimulates osteoblast function

## Correct option

**5. Stimulates osteoblast function**

## Answer explanation

Teriparatide (Forteo) is a partial PTH agonist which binds to receptors on osteoblasts and stimulates bone formation. It is the medication used to treat osteoporosis with an anabolic mechanism.

Osteoporosis is a very common condition that leads to decreased bone mass for multiple reasons. It affects elderly, post-menopausal women most often and can lead to fractures of the spine, hip, and wrist, among others. It is diagnosed with a DEXA scan which demonstrates bone density > 2.5 standard deviations below the peak bone mass of an average 25-year-old (T score > -2.5). Pharmacologic treatment is indicated in cases of hip/vertebral fracture, T score > -2.5, and those with osteopenia with a high 10-year probability for hip or major osteoporotic fracture. Multiple methods of pharmacologic treatment exist, with the most common being bisphosphonates which are anti-resorptive agents that inhibit osteoclast function. Teriparatide is a medication that acts as a partial PTH agonist and when given appropriately, has an anabolic effect on bone growth, leading to activation of osteoblasts, rather than increasing osteoclast production as PTH does physiologically. It is often given for a defined period of time and then anti-resorptive medication is restarted to maintain the gains in bone mineral density.

*Black et al. reviewed the management of postmenopausal osteoporosis,* **noting** fragility fracture in this population is devastating. They discuss indications for management including a T score > -2.5 and note that bisphosphonates are typically recommended as first-line management. They also discuss use of teriparatide, **noting** its effectiveness in reducing the risk of both vertebral and non-vertebral fragility fractures.

*Ekman et al. wrote a review about the role of the orthopaedic surgeon in the management of fragility fractures.* **They note** that orthopaedic surgeons should be helping to facilitate osteoporosis treatment in those with fractures and attempting to screen for risk factors in the appropriate patient population. In cases where treatment is appropriate, surgeons should refer these patients to a trusted provider or initiate therapy, usually beginning with bisphosphonates.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Teriparatide use should increase alkaline phosphatase as this is a marker of bone formation.
Answer 2: Teriparatide is known to cause transient hypercalcemia, but has not been reported to cause hyperphosphatemia.
Answer 3: Teriparatide reduces the risk for vertebral and non-vertebral fracture. If any difference exists, it may preferentially protect against vertebral fractures.
Answer 4: Anti-resorptive osteoporosis medications such as bisphosphonates inhibit osteoclastogenesis.
