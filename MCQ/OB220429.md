## Question

A 28-year-old recreational soccer player presents to the clinic for continued management of his left ankle pain. He reports a long history of repeated ankle inversion injuries and complains of pain and instability daily. Physical therapy and over-the-counter analgesics have not improved his symptoms. On examination, there is excessive anterior translation and inversion of the talus with drawer and talar tilt testing, respectively. He is subsequently taken to surgery for a modified Broström procedure with suture tape augmentation. If over-tightened, which of the following limitations in motion would most likely be seen?

## Options

1. Inversion
2. Eversion
3. Plantarflexion
4. Dorsiflexion
5. Pronation

## Correct option

**4. Dorsiflexion**

## Answer explanation

This 28-year-old male has chronic ankle instability and is treated surgically with a modified Broström procedure with augmentation using suture tape. Over-tightening of the suture tape has been found to result in excessive constraint, leading to limited dorsiflexion (Answer 4).

Chronic (>6 weeks) ankle instability is a common condition associated with functional disability and missed time in sports and/or work. Repeated ankle inversion injuries render the lateral ankle ligaments [anterior talofibular (ATFL), calcaneofibular (CFL), posterior talofibular (PTFL)] incompetent, resulting in instability and pain. After the failure of non-operative treatment, the standard of care involves repairing or reconstructing the lateral ankle ligaments, namely the ATFL and CFL. This can be achieved in several ways, including non-anatomic and anatomic reconstructions. As a type of anatomic reconstruction, the modified Broström technique with Gould modification is the most commonly performed procedure for chronic ankle instability. Suture tape augmentation is one variant of this procedure used. This technique was first developed to reinforce the repair of native ligamentous tissue, and subsequent biomechanical studies have confirmed improved tensile strength. However, the addition of the suture tape has largely failed to improve clinical outcomes. Moreover, patients may experience loss of dorsiflexion at an average of 1.2 cm, which is considered secondary to overconstraint (i.e., overtightening) of the ligament.

*Coetzee et al. performed a retrospective review of 81 patients undergoing lateral ankle ligament reconstruction with suture-tape augmentation with a one-year follow-up, to assess functional outcomes utilizing an aggressive rehabilitation protocol (immediate weight-bearing in a short leg cast or boot followed by the initiation of therapy at four weeks).* The average return to sport was 84 days, with 67% being brace-free when returning to sport. The authors did note a reduction in dorsiflexion compared to the unaffected side (9.2 vs. 10.4 cm; p=0.34). **The authors conclude** that suture-tape augmentation can provide an accelerated return to sport with minimal complications.

*Lau et al. reviewed the various operative treatments for chronic lateral ankle instability.* *The authors provide an in-depth analysis of suture-tape augmentation, comparing it to other treatment types like suture anchors or native ligament repair.* **The authors confirm** that suture tape increases the strength of the repair to near-native strength and appears to enhance stability, but does not appear to appreciably impact clinical outcomes. **The authors conclude** that the inclusion of suture tape augmentation may be most appropriate for revision cases or patients with generalized ligamentous laxity.

*Dias et al. review the current concepts surrounding the surgical management of chronic lateral ankle instability, detailing the pathophysiology, workup, treatment, and rehabilitation of the condition.* While the modified Broström technique remains the gold standard treatment, arthroscopic repair has gained popularity with comparable clinical outcomes and similar overall complication rates. However, arthroscopic repair does have an increased incidence of nerve injury. **The authors highlight** postoperative rehabilitation as crucial to successfully returning to baseline function, but note that return-to-weight-bearing and therapy protocols remain poorly defined.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1,2,3 and 5: restrictions in motion in these directions are less commonly seen.
