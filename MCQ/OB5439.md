## Answer explanation

Borrelia burgdorferi is a spirochete which causes Lyme disease. Lyme disease commonly presents with monoarthritis or a large joint effusion. Figure D is an axial T2 MRI of a knee with a large effusion and no other obvious pathology.

Lyme disease is caused by Borrelia burgdorferi, a spirochete spread by tick bite. Patients commonly present weeks to months after the bite with a migratory mono- or poly-articular arthritis develops with large, minimally painful joint effusions, most commonly about the knee. Lyme serologies are available but can be negative in early stages, so understanding the clinical presentation of the disease is crucial to make an appropriate diagnosis.

*Jouben et al. present a review of the orthopaedic manifestations of Lyme Disease.* **They note** that in stage one of the disease serologies may be negative, and that the tick is typically never found. The bulls-eye rash may also never be seen. **They note** that large joint effusions that are minimally painful, a proper history of being in an endemic area, and any neurologic symptoms should prompt consideration of Lyme disease.

Figure A is a coronal MRI demonstrating osteonecrosis of the femoral head. Figure B is a coronal MR arthrogram of a shoulder demonstrating a humeral avulsion of the glenohumeral ligamaent (HAGL lesion). Figure C is a sagittal MRI of the spine with osteomyelitis. Figure D is an axial MRI of a knee with a large effusion and no other obvious pathology. Figure E is a coronal MRI of a wrist consistent with Keinbock's disease, or osteonecrosis of the lunate.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: Lyme disease does not typically present with osteonecrosis of the hip.
Answer 2: Lyme disease can cause effusions of the shoulder, but in the presence of pathology that explains the effusion (in this case a HAGL lesion) you would not clinically suspect Lyme disease.
Answer 3: Lyme disease does not typically present as an infection of the verterbal bodies.
Answer 5: Lyme disease does not typically present with osteonecrosis of the lunate.
