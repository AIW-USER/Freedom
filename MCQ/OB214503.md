## Question

Figure A is the radiograph of a 22-year-old male who sustained an injury to his right hand while attempting to make a catch during intramural football. Which of the following is true when comparing delayed (immobilization first to allow for fracture healing) versus immediate curettage and grafting?

![[q214503-a-enchondroma-ring.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Delayed treatment results in higher rates of consolidation
2. Delayed treatment is associated with higher recurrence rates
3. Immediate treatment results in higher DASH scores
4. Immediate treatment is associated with higher complication rates
5. No difference in outcomes

## Correct option

**5. No difference in outcomes**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a pathologic fracture through an enchondroma of the proximal phalanx of the ring finger. The only difference between delayed vs immediate treatment of these injuries is the prolonged recovery associated with first immobilizing the digit before curettage and grafting.

Enchondromas are the most common primary bone tumor of the hand, and most commonly present as a pathologic fracture following minor injury. Immediate curettage with or without grafting is the treatment of choice for these lesions. Traditionally, delayed treatment with 1-2 months of immobilization followed by curettage after fracture healing was utilized. However, one-stage procedures are now recommended as they show similar outcomes and greatly decrease the overall recovery time. No consensus exists in the literature on void augmentation, however, cancellous autograft, calcium phosphate, calcium sulfate, polymethylmethacrylate, and coralline hydroxyapatite have all been described with low complication rates.

*Zhou et al reviewed the management and timing of surgical intervention for enchondromas of the hand.* **They report** no significant difference in consolidation after surgery, recurrence rate, DASH scores or complications following immediate or delayed treatment. **They conclude** that immobilization to allow for fracture healing prior to curettage is not necessary and prolongs recovery time.

*Lubahn et al review the evaluation and management of enchondromas of the hand.* **They report** similar outcomes and complication profiles for both immediate and delayed curettage of these lesions. Furthermore, **they report** that there is no consensus on the need for grafting following curettage. **They conclude** that the current treatment of choice is that of a single-stage curettage with or without grafting following pathologic fracture of enchondromas of the hand.

Figure A demonstrates a pathologic fracture through a proximal phalanx enchondroma.

Incorrect Answer:
Answer 1-4: No statistical difference exists between rates of consolidation, DASH scores, recurrence rates, and complication rates between delayed and immediate treatment of pathologic fractures associated with enchondromas.
