## Question

You are covering a high-school football summer training camp with twice daily practices when a defensive lineman comes to the sideline initially complaining of cramps, fatigue, and nausea. During your examination, he is unable to answer basic personal identifying questions accurately. You take his temperature and note it to be 104.5°. Despite the high outdoor temperature, he lacks body sweat. Given these findings, which of the following is the most important initial step in treatment of this athlete?

## Options

1. Electrolyte replacement
2. Fluid replacement with low osmolarity solution
3. Rapid ice water immersion
4. Laying supine with legs elevated
5. Give tylenol and valium

## Correct option

**3. Rapid ice water immersion**

## Answer explanation

This athlete is suffering from heat stroke symptoms and the best initial step in treatment is rapid ice-water immersion to initiate a reduction in core body temperature.

Heat-related illness occurs on a large spectrum of severity and can affect a variety of athletes. Pathology included in this description are heat cramps, heat-related syncope, heat exhaustion, heat illness, and heat stroke. The primary differentiating factor of heat stroke is the presence of central nervous system symptoms including confusion and abnormal behavior. Heat stroke occurs due to a failure of the body's thermoregulatory mechanisms secondary to excess core body temperature. Body temperatures may be in excess of 104° and patients usually lack sweat, which further accelerates the pathologic thermoregulatory processes. Heat stroke should be aggressively treated with immediate ice water immersion on-site, fanning, and cool intravenous fluids. Transfer to the emergency department can be considered if symptoms do not improve with initial treatment.

*Sloan et al. review on-site treatment of exertional heat stroke, nothing that without prompt treatment, significant morbidity and mortality can result.* *They looked at 32 cases of heat stroke during a half-marathon over 7 years and noted that 68% were treated with on-site ice water immersion therapy and 59% were able to be discharged home from the race.* Thirty-two percent of athletes were transported to the hospital and only 40% were able to be discharged home, leading the authors to conclude that on-site treatment of athletes with heat stroke was safe and effective.

*Noonan et al. wrote a JAAOS article reviewing heat- and cold-induced injuries in athletes.* **They emphasize** the role of the team physician in preparedness and education on these exposures prior to competition. They discuss the spectrum of both heat- and cold-related illnesses and how they are best treated.

*Howe and Boden published on the management of heat-related illness in athletes,* **noting** it is the result of increased heat production and impaired dissipation. **They discuss** that heat stroke is the most severe on this spectrum and is characterized by a temperature of >104° and mental status changes. They again emphasize the importance of education of players, coaches, and training staff and note that the most important initial treatment is rapid cooling.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1 & 2: Fluids with low osmolarity (water) and electrolyte replacement fluids (sports drinks) are both effective at preventing dehydration and in treating heat cramps and heat-related syncope. It is generally accepted that water is most effective early in an exercise routine and as the duration of exercise increases and becomes more vigorous, electrolyte replacement may be effective in preventing dilutional hyponatremia.
Answer 4: Laying supine with the legs elevated increases blood flow to vital organs and, as a result, increases cardiac output, which may improve symptoms of heat-related syncope. This alone is not an aggressive enough treatment to manage heat stroke symptoms.
Answer 5: There is no evidence that tylenol use decreases core body temperature quickly and meaningfully enough in a heat stroke. Benzodiazepines are not effective in treating heat stroke, and by some accounts, can make symptoms worse.
