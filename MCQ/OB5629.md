## Correct option

## Answer explanation

A focal dome osteotomy with the center of the dome located at the center of rotation of angulation (CORA), as illustrated in Figure B, would correct limb alignment with the least translation of bone ends, least translation of anatomical axis, and minimal shortening (shown in Illustration C).

When performing osteotomies, it is important to remember the rules of osteotomy as outlined by Paley. Rule 1: The axis of correction of angulation (ACA) can pass through the CORA (ACA-CORA). If the osteotomy passes through the ACA-CORA, correction produces pure angulation at the osteotomy site (no translation). Rule 2: If ACA passes through the CORA (ACA-CORA), but the osteotomy does NOT pass through the ACA-CORA, there will be angulation and translation of the bone ends at the osteotomy site. But the axis lines are collinear (no translation of axis). Rule 3: If the osteotomy passes through the ACA, but the CORA is at a different level, there will be angulation but no translation of bone ends. The axis lines will be parallel, but translated.

*Gugenheim et al. described correction of distal femoral coronal plane deformities in 14 femora.* *They used a temporary external fixators, performed percutaneous dome osteotomy, inserted retrograde IM nails and finally removed the external fixators.* Mean healing was achieved at 13wks. The achieved normal mechanical lateral distal femoral angle 12 knees and normal mechanical axis in 10 knees. **They believe** that this technique produces accurate correction, few complications, corrects valgus/varus deformities, and allows early mobilization.

Figure A shows a Maquet dome osteotomy (concave distal). Figure B shows a focal dome osteotomy (concave proximal). Figure C shows an intra-articular uniplanar oblique osteotomy. Figure D shows an extraarticular uniplanar oblique osteotomy. Figure E shows a horizontal osteotomy. Osteotomies in Figures C-E may be opening wedge, closing wedge or neutral wedge. Illustration A shows the osteotomy rules (Panels 1 and 2 illustrate Rule 1, opening and closing wedge osteotomy respectively. Panel 3 illustrates Rule 2. Panel 4 illustrates Rule 3). Illustration B demonstrates correction seen with Osteotomy A in Figure A. Illustration C demonstrates correction seen with Osteotomy B in Figure B. This is a "concavity proximal" type of osteotomy or focal dome osteotomy. Pivoting of the distal fragment at the CORA, not the osteotomy site, results in correction of angulation with excellent bone contact.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The osteotomy in Fig A passes through the CORA but will result in secondary axis translation shown in Illustration B.
Answer 3: Intraarticular osteotomies are less desirable. Although bone end translation and axis translation is minimized, this osteotomy will provide minimal bone contact and increases the risk of chondral and cruciate ligament damage.
Answer 4: Osteotomy D in Figure D does not pass through the CORA. If the ACA coincides with the CORA (ACA-CORA), there will be translation of bone ends (even if the axis lines are collinear). If the osteotomy passes through the ACA, and the ACA is distinct from the CORA, there will be secondary translation of axis lines (even if there is no translation of bone ends).
Answer 5: A horizontal Osteotomy E in Figure E will lead to translation of bone ends and axis lines.
