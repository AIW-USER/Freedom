## Question

A 40-year-old male sustains an ankle fracture-dislocation and undergoes operative fixation. His postoperative recovery is uneventful except for persistent, limited ankle dorsiflexion. He is diagnosed with an Achilles contracture and undergoes an Achilles lengthening. Which patient would also most likely benefit from an isolated Achilles lengthening procedure?

## Options

1. A 53-year-old male who cannot dorsiflex his ankle past neutral with the knee extended or flexed
2. A 14-year-old male with cerebral palsy who can dorsiflex his ankle past neutral with knee flexion, but not when his knee is extended
3. A 30-year-old runner with anterior ankle pain and impingement when he runs
4. A 62-year-old female with a left foot drop
5. A 23-year-old female with lateral ankle pain after fall 3 months ago who can dorsiflex her ankle past neutral with the knee extended and flexed

## Correct option

**1. A 53-year-old male who cannot dorsiflex his ankle past neutral with the knee extended or flexed**

## Answer explanation

Isolated Achilles lengthening procedures are indicated for patients with evidence of an Achilles contracture on examination. Answer 1 is correct of the listed options because it describes a patient with an Achilles contracture, as evidenced by his inability to dorsiflex his ankle past neutral with either knee extended or flexed.

An equinus deformity of the ankle can result from a multitude of reasons, including either an Achilles contracture or a gastrocnemius contracture. It is important to distinguish a true Achilles contracture from a gastrocnemius contracture, which occurs only due to the gastrocnemius muscle's tightness. To differentiate these two pathologies, the Silfverskiöld test is often utilized. This test involves testing ankle dorsiflexion with the knee in extension. Dorsiflexion is then re-checked with knee flexion. In an Achilles contracture, patients cannot dorsiflex past neutral in either position. With a gastrocnemius contracture, ankle dorsiflexion will improve with knee flexion as the muscle originates on the posterior femur (knee flexion allows the muscle to relax). In patients who have an isolated gastrocnemius contracture, a gastrocnemius lengthening procedure can be performed to lengthen the tight muscle. However, a true Achilles contracture will not significantly improve with just a gastrocnemius lengthening and often requires an Achilles lengthening procedure instead.

*Baumbach et al. conducted a study involving 20 asymptomatic volunteers to identify at what degree of knee flexion the gastrocnemius muscle relaxes.* **The authors found** that, on average, 20 degrees of knee flexion fully eliminated the restraining effect of the gastrocnemius muscle on ankle dorsiflexion. **The authors concluded** that flexing the knee to about 20 degrees of flexion should be sufficient to relax the gastrocnemius when assessing for Achilles and gastrocnemius contractures.

*DiGiovanni et al. conducted a biomechanical study to evaluate the association between gastrocnemius tightness and forefoot pain.* *They assessed two cohorts: group 1 consisted of 34 patients with forefoot and midfoot pain, and group 2 consisted of asymptomatic controls.* **They found** that 65% of the symptomatic group 1 patients had evidence of a gastrocnemius contracture vs 24% in the control group. **The authors highlighted** that contractures of the gastroc-soleus complex can predispose patients to develop forefoot and midfoot pain, and a good physical exam should be performed to identify patients may may benefit from gastrocnemius or Achilles lengthening procedures.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: This patient has evidence of a tight gastrocnemius and may benefit from a gastrocnemius lengthening procedure, not an Achilles lengthening one.
Answer 3: This patient most likely has anterior ankle impingement and may benefit from an ankle arthroscopy and debridement procedure instead.
Answer 4: Patients with foot drop should not undergo an isolated Achilles lengthening, and rather may benefit from tendon transfers.
Answer 5: This patient has a normal Silfverskiöld test.
