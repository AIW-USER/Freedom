## Question

A 45-year-old male has been followed for progressive left hip arthritis and underlying femoroacetabular impingement. He has exhausted nonoperative management and is interested in pursuing surgical treatment. On review of available imaging of the left hip, there are Tonnis grade 2 arthritic changes, a tear of the ligamentum teres, and a degenerative labral tear extending into the chondrolabral junction. The alpha angle measures 52°. In counseling this patient on surgical options, which factor is the strongest contraindication to joint preserving, arthroscopic intervention?

## Options

1. Degenerative labral tear extending into the chondrolabral junction
2. Preoperative alpha angle of 52°
3. Tonnis grade 2 arthritis
4. Male sex
5. Ligamentum teres tear

## Correct option

**3. Tonnis grade 2 arthritis**

## Answer explanation

The patient has Tonnis grade II arthritis secondary to underlying femoroacetabular impingement (FAI). Arthroscopic intervention in the setting of Tonnis grade II arthritis is not recommended due to a high failure rate necessitating conversion to total hip arthroplasty (THA).

Hip arthritis in younger patients who have failed nonoperative management is a challenging clinical scenario with multiple treatment options. The Tonnis classification scheme qualitatively characterizes arthritis severity and has been used within the literature as one of several factors that can help guide management. Based on an AP x-ray, Tonnis grading ranges from zero (normal) to three (severe joint space narrowing, large cyst formation, severe femoral head deformity). Joint preserving arthroscopic procedures may be viable strategies when there is low-grade arthritic change (Tonnis Grade 1), relatively well-preserved joint space, and mild underlying deformity. In the presence of advanced arthritic change (Tonnis grade 2 or 3), there is a high risk for failure associated with arthroscopic management.

*Lynch et al. put forth best practice clinical guidelines for hip arthroscopy in the setting of FAI using validated techniques to reach a consensus among 15 hip arthroscopists in North America.* Among their recommendations, **they established** contraindications to arthroscopic management, which included Tonnis grade ≥2 arthritis, joint space narrowing to ≤2mm, and obesity. They cited poor functional outcomes and satisfaction scores in addition to a significantly increased risk for conversion to THA in support of their establishment of Tonnis grade ≥2 arthritis as a contraindication.

*Perets et al. performed a retrospective investigation assessing risk factors for re-operation in patients younger than 50 years old with FAI and labral tears undergoing hip arthroscopy.* *Patients requiring conversion to THA had a higher preoperative alpha angle (63.2° versus 58.6°), a higher BMI, were more likely to have Outerbridge grade ≥2 chondral changes, and were more likely to have undergone capsular release and labral debridement rather than repair.* Of note, the authors cited higher-grade arthritis as a well-established risk factor for failure and conversion to THA, and excluded patients with Tonnis grade >0 arthritis from the study. **They concluded** that the potential risk factors for conversion to THA should be carefully considered when selecting patients for arthroscopic surgery.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1,2,4,5: These options are not well-established contraindications to arthroscopic intervention. Among the strongest contraindications defined within the literature are the presence of higher-grade arthritis, advanced age, and obesity.
