## Question

A 59-year-old female with a history of biopsy proven metastatic renal cell carcinoma presents with a thoracic spine lesion consistent with renal cell carcinoma. She has lower extremity weakness and sustained clonus bilaterally. What is the most appropriate management prior to surgery?

## Options

1. Blood cultures
2. High dose IV methylprednisone
3. Arterial embolization
4. CT guided cryotherapy
5. Radiation therapy

## Correct option

**3. Arterial embolization**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a history of renal cell carcinoma which is one of the most common tumors that metastasizes to bone. Renal cell tumors are highly vascular tumors (along with thyroid) and can bleed heavily during biopsy or excision. As such, careful evaluation and consideration for arterial embolization is necessary prior to surgery.

*Prabhu et al reported on 51 preoperative spine embolizations and* **reported** that 80% of these lesions could be embolized effectively. Even after embolization, average blood loss was 2.5L for these cases. In conclusion, **they argue** that preoperative embolization of the feeder vessels is safe and effective and is indicated to prevent excessive blood loss in patients undergoing surgery of the spine with metastatic renal cell carcinoma.
