## Options

1. Unicompartmental knee arthroplasty (UKA) demonstrates reliable long-term outcomes when performed for this condition
2. UKA is appropriate for the spontaneous (but not secondary) form of this condition
3. UKA performed for this condition is associated greater failure rates than for osteoarthritis
4. The most common cause of failure following UKA for this condition is from tibial stress fractures
5. Total knee arthroplasty (TKA) is indicated for this patient due to high failure rates associated with UKA when performed for this condition

## Correct option

**1. Unicompartmental knee arthroplasty (UKA) demonstrates reliable long-term outcomes when performed for this condition**

## Answer explanation

The patient presents with clinical exam findings and imaging consistent with spontaneous osteonecrosis of the knee (SONK). UKA for SONK has demonstrated reliable long-term functional outcomes and is the treatment of choice for severe localized disease.

Osteonecrosis of the knee may be categorized as spontaneous (SONK), secondary, or post-arthoscopic. SONK is the most common type and typically involves the medial femoral condyle (MFC). The etiology remains controversial; however, the process is thought to result from subchondral insufficiency fractures or from meniscal root injuries, which are present in up to 80% of patents presenting with SONK. Secondary causes should be ruled out. Similar to osteochondritis dissecans (OCD), SONK most commonly involves the femoral condyles and involves subchondral collapse with later osteonecrosis (sometimes seen in advanced cases of OCD). However, OCD will more commonly affect the lateral aspect of the MFC, and the age distribution is vastly different, with OCD present in young males and SONK occurring primarily in elderly females. MRI typically shows a localized low-signal area with surrounding bony edema, as in this case. Initial management for pre-collapse SONK involves protected weight-bearing and anti-inflammatory medications, with surgery reserved for intractable symptoms or progressive disease. Once all conservative management options have been exhausted, UKA has demonstrated reliable long-term functional outcomes in the setting of SONK that are comparable to that of UKA for localized secondary osteonecrosis of the knee and OA. As a result, TKA is not required in most cases. Failure following UKA is most often due to aseptic loosening and requires revision to TKA. Contraindications to UKA include inflammatory arthropathy, fixed deformity >5-10 degrees, limited motion <90 degrees, and previous meniscectomy or symptomatic involvement of the other compartment(s).

*Parratte et al. evaluated the outcomes of UKA performed for spontaneous or secondary osteonecrosis of the knee.* **The authors found** that UKA provided reliable pain relief in 96% of cases at a minimum follow-up of 3 years. They also extrapolated a 12-year survivorship of 96.7%, **concluding** that UKA was reasonable for restoring function and limb alignment for both spontaneous and secondary osteonecrosis of the knee that is localized to one compartment.

*Heyse et al. retrospectively reviewed the outcomes of 52 patients who underwent UKA for SONK.* *At an average 10-year final follow-up,* **the authors found** that functional outcomes were good, with 97.3% (all but one patient) satisfied or very satisfied with their outcome. Survivorship was 93.1% at 10 years and 90.6% at 15 years. **The authors concluded** that this compared favorably to UKA for osteoarthritis and was as effective in treating SONK.

*Bruni et al. investigated the long-term survivorship in the largest group of 84 patients who underwent medial UKA for SONK.* **The authors found** that ten-year survivorship was 89%, with 10 revisions performed for tibial subsidence (4) and aseptic loosening (3). There were no cases with progression to the lateral compartment necessitating revision. **They concluded** that UKA was successful for SONK so long as isolated and secondary causes were ruled out.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: UKA has demonstrated comparable results when performed for either localized spontaneous or secondary osteonecrosis of the knee.
Answer 3: UKA for SONK and OA are comparable in terms of pain and functional outcomes at mid- to long-term follow-up.
Answer 4: Aseptic loosening and progression of arthritis to involve the other compartments remain the most common modes of failure of UKA, even in the setting of SONK. Stress fracture is much less common, especially in a technically sound procedure.
Answer 5: UKA is successful in treating unicompartmental SONK. TKA may be required in the setting of more diffuse osteonecrosis, which is more often secondary, or in the setting of failed UKA, though it is rarely required as primary treatment.
