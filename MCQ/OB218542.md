## Question

An 11-year-old male with a BMI of 32 presents with progressively worsening left knee pain. He states the pain has worsened over the last several weeks to the point where he is having difficulty walking. With ambulation, his left foot is externally rotated compared to the contralateral side. An examination of the left knee is unremarkable. Which of the following is true?

## Options

1. Symptoms usually resolve with physical therapy followed by progressive return to activities
2. In situ pinning is associated with poor long-term outcomes
3. Bilateral treatment is recommended in patients with endocrinopathies
4. Internal rotation tibial osteotomy results in good long-term outcomes
5. A periacetabular osteotomy will prevent progressive hip arthrosis

## Correct option

**3. Bilateral treatment is recommended in patients with endocrinopathies**

## Answer explanation

Patients with slipped capital femoral epiphysis should undergo in situ pinning of the affected hip on presentation and patients with endocrinopathies should have prophylactic pinning of the contralateral hip.

Slipped capital femoral epiphysis is the disruption of the proximal femoral physis through the hypertrophic zone leading to shortening and external rotation of the proximal femoral alignment. Early recognition of this condition is critical to prevent progression to unstable slips and a higher risk of avascular necrosis. Patients with endocrinopathies, age <10 at presentation, BMI < 55th percentile, and open triradiate cartilage have a higher risk of developing a slip on the contralateral hip. As such, these patients may benefit from prophylactic pinning of the contralateral hip at the time of treatment.

*Mathew and Larson performed a systematic review of the literature regarding the natural history of slipped capital femoral epiphysis.* **They reported** at 20-year follow-up only 5% of patients required total hip arthroplasty with avascular necrosis being the most common cause for later arthroplasty. **They concluded** the long-term data support in situ pinning with good outcomes.

*Aronsson et al. reviewed current concepts in slipped capital femoral epiphysis.* **They stated** the treatment of choice for stable slips is in-situ pinning with good long-term results. **They further postulated** unstable slips are best treated with closed reduction with single or double screw fixation.

Incorrect answers
Answer 1: Conservative treatment is usually effective in patients with Legg-Calve-Perthes disease.
Answer 2: In-situ pinning is associated with good long-term outcomes for patients with slipped capital femoral epiphysis.
Answer 4: Tibial rotational osteotomy is indicated for patients with symptomatic external tibial torsion.
Answer 5: Periacetabular osteotomies are effective in preventing progressive hip arthrosis in patients with hip dysplasia.
