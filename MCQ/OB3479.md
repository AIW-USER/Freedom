## Question

A 2-year-old child has a flexion deformity of the interphalangeal joint of his thumb as seen in Figure A. Surgical correction of this deformity places what structure most at risk as it crosses the surgical field?

![[q3479-a-peddie-trigger.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Princeps pollicis artery
2. Ulnar digital nerve
3. Oblique pulley
4. Ulnar digital artery
5. Radial digital nerve

## Correct option

**5. Radial digital nerve**

## Answer explanation

The patient in the scenario has a trigger thumb. Surgical correction of this condition requires the release of the A1 pulley. The A1 pulley is seen at the red arrow in Illustration A. During the dissection, the radial digital nerve crosses the operative field and is at risk. It must be identified and protected.

*Bae described the etiology, natural history and surgical indications in treatment of pediatric trigger thumbs.* Currently, the literature does not advocate for or against surgical management. **He concluded** that further long-term research will help guide evidence-based treatment.

Baek at al described the natural history of pediatric trigger thumbs. They concluded that trigger thumbs in children will resolve without treatment in >60% of patients. In patients who do not have full resolution, the flexion deformity can be expected to gradually improve with time.
