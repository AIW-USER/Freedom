## Question

Which of the following tumors are most likely to cause the condition seen in Figure A?

![[q3798-a-met-spine.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Breast, Renal, Osseous, Lung
2. Breast, Hepatic, Colon, Lung
3. Breast, Osseous, Renal, Hepatic
4. Breast, Renal, Prostate, Lung
5. Breast, Renal, Prostate, Colon

## Correct option

**4. Breast, Renal, Prostate, Lung**

## Answer explanation

This patient has developed spine metastases and breast, renal, prostate, and lung carcinoma are most likely to cause this condition.

Metastatic cancer is the most common reason for a destructive bone lesion in adults (>40). Bone is the third most common site for metastatic disease after the lung and liver. Common sites of metastatic lesions to bone in descending frequency: spine, proximal femur, and humerus. The most common site in the spine for metastasis is the thoracic spine. Carcinomas that spread to bone are breast, lung, thyroid, renal, and prostate.

*White et al. reviewed metastatic disease of the spine and report that the primary tumors to most likely metastasize to the spine are breast (16% to 37% of breast cancer patients develop spine metastases), prostate (9% to 15%), lung (12% to 15%), kidney (3% to 6%), and thyroid (4%).* Symptomatic lesions typically are found in the thoracic (68% to 70%), lumbosacral (16% to 22%), and cervical (8% to 15%) spine.

*Constans et al. performed a review of 600 cases of spinal metastasis causing a neurologic syndrome.* **They note** that spinal metastases occur in 5-10% of cancer patients. **They report** that the mechanism by which pure bone lesions cause neurological involvement originate with vertebral collapse. **They found** that nearly all patients, (98.4%), underwent surgery, radiotherapy, or combined treatment.

Figure A is a T2 MRI demonstrating a lesion and pathological fracture in L1. There is also tumor involvement at the level above and within the canal causing severe compression on the conus.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1, 2, 3, and 5 do not list the most common causes of spine metastases.
