## Question

A 53-year-old female presents to your clinic with end-stage osteoarthritis of her hip. She has trialed nonoperative management and is now interested in a total hip arthroplasty (THA). She is otherwise healthy and fairly active. The patient is worried about the implant's longevity and has questions regarding the implants you would use. What is the most appropriate statement to tell the patient?

## Options

1. You will use a dual mobility acetabular liner for the sole purpose of implant longevity.
2. Using a 40 mm femoral head will increase implant longevity.
3. The use of highly cross-linked polyethylene liners have been shown to be successful at 15 years in her age group.
4. You will place a polyethylene liner of <3 mm to decrease wear and increase implant longevity.
5. Placement of a vertical cup will ensure minimal wear and increase long-term survivability.

## Correct option

**3. The use of highly cross-linked polyethylene liners have been shown to be successful at 15 years in her age group.**

## Answer explanation

In a younger patient, such as this one, undergoing a total hip arthroplasty (THA), the most critical implant choice to increase the longevity of the implant is using highly cross-linked polyethylene (XLPE) acetabular liners (Answer choice 3).

Component wear in total hip arthroplasty (THA) is an important consideration in all patients undergoing surgery, particularly younger patients when implant longevity is key. The limiting factor in implant lifespan is long-term wear, causing osteolysis and loosening. One of the most impactful recent developments in this field is the advancement in polyethylene liners and the creation of highly cross-linked polyethylene (XLPE). XLPE has significantly improved implant longevity by reducing wear rates & thus the risk of osteolysis, leading to reliable long-term outcomes. With the implementation of XLPE, larger head sizes can be used without a significant increase in wear rate, thus providing stability and longevity of the implants. The benefits of XLPE have made it the gold standard for acetabular liners, particularly in younger patients.

*Roedel et al. performed a retrospective review of 105 THAs performed in patients under 50 using XLPE as the acetabular bearing.* *96 hips were followed for a minimum of 15 years, with clinical and radiographic outcomes measured.* **The results demonstrated** 0.04 mm/year of polyethylene wear and no osteolysis in any hips at the 15-year mark. **The authors concluded** that XLPE in THA results in excellent performance and longevity without signs of significant wear or loosening in patients under 50 years of age.

*De Steiger et al. published an observational study from Australian registry data evaluating the outcomes of patients undergoing THA with conventional polyethylene (CPA) vs XLPE.* *The authors' outcome measure was time to first revision, and they evaluated the effects of age, sex, method of component fixation, and femoral head size, as well as reasons for revision.* *This study found a lower rate of revisions in the XLPE group (6.2%) compared to the CPE group (11.7%) at 16 years.* **The authors concluded** that XLPE significantly improves the longevity of implants and suggest that these data may support the utilization of THA in younger cohorts.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Dual mobility (DM) liners are designed to enhance the stability of the THA. DM liners are not designed to increase the lifespan of the implant from a wear perspective. Furthermore, there is no indication that a DM liner is indicated in this patient.
Answer 2: Head sizes >40 mm have not been shown to increase the longevity of the implants. On the contrary, head sizes >40 mm have a biomechanical increase in volumetric wear, particularly when using conventional polyethylene liners. This has been refuted when using XLPE.
Answer 4: XLPE by nature has a reduced fracture toughness, which can be exasperated by thinner liners. Therefore, it is recommended to place a polyethylene liner of at least 5-6 mm thick.
Answer 5: This is incorrect, as cup malposition has been shown to increase the failure rate of the acetabular liner.
