## Question

A 25-year-old male falls from a ladder and presents with the injury seen in Figure A. Which of the following is true when comparing primary arthrodesis with open reduction and internal fixation for the first, second and third tarsometatarsal joints in this injury?

![[q5458-a-weatherford-121129-35.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Higher rates of implant removal with primary arthrodesis
2. Lower rates of post-traumatic arthritis with open reduction and internal fixation
3. Improved return to activity with primary arthrodesis
4. Higher rates of malreduction with open reduction and internal fixation
5. Worse functional outcomes with primary arthrodesis

## Correct option

**3. Improved return to activity with primary arthrodesis**

## Answer explanation

The patient presents with a Lisfranc injury with primarily ligamentous disruption of the tarsometatarsal joint articulations. Recent randomized controlled studies have found improved functional outcomes, increased return to activity and decreased rates of secondary surgery with primary arthrodesis of the medial three tarsometatarsal joints in these injuries.

Lisfranc injuries may lead to arthritis and collapse of the longitudinal arch if not addressed operatively. Regardless of the treatment method chosen, anatomic reduction and stable fixation is necessary to optimize outcome. Careful assessment of the intercuneiform and naviculocuneiform joints should be performed to avoid continued instability.

*Ly and Coetzee performed a randomized controlled study of open reduction and internal fixation (ORIF) compared with primary arthrodesis of the medial two or three rays for primarily ligamentous Lisfranc injuries.* **The authors found** significant improvement in AOFAS midfoot scores and return to activity in the arthrodesis group. **They note** that 15 of 20 patients in the ORIF group had evidence of radiographic arthritis and 5 patients underwent conversion to arthrodesis during the study period.

*Henning et al performed a randomized controlled study again comparing ORIF with primary arthrodesis of the medial two or three rays.* With the numbers available, the study found no significant difference in SF-36 or SMFA functional outcomes scores, however the the ORIF group had a significantly higher rate of secondary surgery for either painful implant removal or conversion to arthrodesis. It should be noted that the study was discontinued prior to meeting adequate enrollment to demonstrate a significant difference in the SF-36 or SMFA.

Figure A demonstrates a Lisfranc injury with lateral subluxation of the second through fifth tarsometatarsal joints. in addition the patient has a navicular fracture, likely indicating disruption of the intercuneiform joint, as well as articular impaction of the cuboid.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: Higher rates of implant removal are seen with ORIF
Answer 2: Higher rates of post-traumatic arthritis are seen with ORIF
Answer 4: No study to date has demonstrated a difference in reduction when comparing ORIF with primary arthrodesis
*Answer 5: Superior functional outcomes were seen in the study by Ly and Coetzee and equivalent outcomes were seen in the underpowered study by Henning et al.*
