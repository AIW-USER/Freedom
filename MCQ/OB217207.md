## Question

A 68-year-old right-handed male presents to the office for a follow-up evaluation regarding knee osteoarthritis. He reports worsening pain, especially when playing golf, which he plays approximately 3 times per week. The patient has failed conservative measures including anti-inflammatories, activity modifications, and corticosteroid injections. When discussing total knee arthroplasty, which of the following is true as it relates to golf after total knee arthroplasty?

## Options

1. Unable to return to sport secondary to wear
2. Unable to return to sport secondary to pain
3. Decrease in the number of rounds tolerated per month
4. Increase in drive length by >20 yards
5. Change in handicap of <5 strokes

## Correct option

**5. Change in handicap of <5 strokes**

## Answer explanation

Active golfers who undergo total knee arthroplasty typically return to sport with a modest change in their handicap of <5 strokes.

Golf is one of the most popular sports in the United States, especially amongst older individuals as this can be a low-impact activity. Numerous studies have evaluated the return to golf following both total knee and total hip arthroplasties. Trends show a slight increase or no change in handicap amongst amateur golfers undergoing these procedures. Further, subjective drive length has varied in the literature with some authors reporting an increased distance while others report a slight loss in distance. While the knee opposite of the patient's handedness can experience significant stress, most patients are able to return to sport without difficulty.

*Mallon and Callaghan studied 83 (80 of which were right-handed) active golfers who underwent TKA and* **found** that they invariably experienced a rise in their handicap (mean +4.6 strokes) and also a decrease in the length of their drives. Approximately 15% of the cohort experienced a mild ache while playing, and golfers with left TKA's had more difficulty with pain during and after play than did golfers with right TKA's. It also should be stated that statistically significant increased pain ratings occur in golfers with a TKA on the target-side knee. Finally, almost 90% of the patients in this study utilized a cart while playing post-operatively.

*Gorbaty et al review the relationship between joint arthroplasty and golf play.* **They report** that patients undergoing both total knee and total hip arthroplasties experience a rise in their handicap of 1.4 strokes after recovery based on the validated regional golf handicap database. **They concluded** that patients can expect to increase their play frequency slightly while their play quality will likely decrease by a small margin.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1 & 2: Most patients undergoing total hip or knee arthroplasty may return to sport without significant issues.
Answer 3: The majority of patients actually increased their play frequency following joint replacement surgery.
Answer 4: While patients undergoing total hip arthroplasty may see a slight increase in drive length (approximately 3 yards), those undergoing total knee arthroplasty often report a decrease in drive length by approximately 12 yards.
