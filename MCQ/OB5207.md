## Question

What nerve(s) innervate the muscle(s) most responsible for creating abduction deforming forces to the proximal fracture fragment shown in Figure A?

![[q5207-a-subbb.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Superior gluteal nerve
2. Inferior and superior gluteal nerve
3. Sciatic nerve
4. Direct branches from L1-L3
5. Femoral nerve

## Correct option

**1. Superior gluteal nerve**

## Answer explanation

Abduction of the proximal fragment is attributable to the pull of the abductor muscles (gluteus medius and minimus) at the greater trochanter. These muscles are innervated by the superior gluteal nerve.

The proximal fragment in subtrochanteric femoral shaft fractures is usually displaced into flexion, abduction, and external rotation. The pull of these muscles must be considered when reducing, fixing and/or monitoring these fractures. Non-operative treatment options include casting or traction with delayed casting. Operative options include flexible intramedullary nailing, trochanteric antegrade intramedullary nailing, bridge plating, or external fixation.

*Hosalkar et al. reviewed intramedullary nailing (IMN) of pediatric femoral shaft fractures.* **They state** the peripheral branches of the medial femoral circumflex artery (MFCA) are close to the piriformis fossa, thus increasing the chances of osteonecrosis if piriformis entry nails are used.

*Jarvis et al. retrospectively reviewed 13 adolescents (average age 13) with subtrochanteric fractures.* Eight were operatively treated with satisfactory results, compared to three patients treated non-operatively which were deemed unsatisfactory. **They conclude** that operative treatment should always be considered if surgery can be tolerated.

Figure A shows a subtrochateric fracture in a skeletally immature patient, which was treated with retrograde flexible intramedullary nailing.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: The inferior gluteal nerve innervates the gluteus maximus. Based on the fracture pattern, the gluteus maximus insertion is distal to the fracture line.
Answer 3: The sciatic nerve will innervates some of the external rotators, which contribute to externally rotating the proximal fragment.
Answer 4: Direct branches from L1-L3 will innervate the psoas muscle, which is contributes to flexing the proximal fragment.
Answer 5: The iliacus muscle is innervated by the femoral nerve, which is contributes to flexing the proximal fragment.
