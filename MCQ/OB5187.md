## Question

Which of the following statements is FALSE regarding non-accidental trauma?

## Options

1. The differential diagnosis includes osteogenesis imperfecta, accidental trauma and metabolic bone disease
2. Fractures are the most common finding on initial presentation
3. Two-thirds of child abuse victims have Medicaid as their primary payor
4. Femur fractures in non-ambulatory children should raise suspicion for non-accidental trauma
5. Children with concomitant neurologic or psychiatric conditions are at greater risk

## Correct option

**2. Fractures are the most common finding on initial presentation**

## Answer explanation

Skin lesions (bruises, lacerations, and burns) are the most common physical manifestation of child abuse, followed by fractures.

Evaluation of a child suspected to be the victim of non-accidental trauma requires a multi-disciplinary approach. Although certain fractures or patterns of fractures may raise suspicion (long bone fractures in children less than 2 years old, posterior rib fractures, multiple fractures in various stages of healing, certain metaphyseal fractures), the entire social and clinical picture should be taken into consideration. This includes age, fracture pattern, stated mechanism of injury, and psychosocial stressors that may be present.

*Kocher et al. review the orthopaedic ramifications of non-accidental trauma.* **They emphasize** a thorough and complete examination, as abuse involves the whole child. **They conclude** that recognition is the key to management, as abused children are at risk for repeated abuse and death.

*Loder et al. analyzed demographic and injury characteristics in a large national database of hospitalized children.* *Of the 2.5 million cases, 1794 (0.1%) were diagnostically coded for abuse.* The most common fractures were femur and humerus, mostly in children younger than 2 years old. Nearly half of cases in children aged 3 to 20 years had concomitant neurologic or psychiatric issues.

*Pandya et al. retrospectively reviewed 500 cases of child abuse in children 48 months of age or younger and compared their characteristics with a control group of 985 accidental trauma patients at a single level I pediatric trauma center.* **They found** that in children younger than 18 months, the following fractures occurred more frequently in non-accidental trauma victims (odds ratio in parentheses): rib (23.7 times), tibia/fibula (12.8 times), humerus (2.3 times), and femur (1.8 times). In patients older than 18 months, however, femur and humerus fractures were more likely to result from accidental trauma.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: Osteogenesis imperfecta or other metabolic bone diseases may result in multiple fractures or fractures from low energy mechanisms. Accidental trauma is always in the differential but is unlikely in non-ambulators.
Answer 3: This was confirmed in a large national sample.
Answer 4: Femur fractures may result from birth trauma but are infrequent in children under 2.
Answer 5: Unfortunately this is true, especially for older children.
