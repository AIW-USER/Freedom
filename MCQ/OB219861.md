## Question

An adult patient presents to your clinic with persistent knee pain despite conservative treatment. You obtain skyline Merchant view, weight-bearing AP and lateral knee, and weight-bearing full-length limb alignment radiographs. You mark her mechanical axis on full-length radiographs, which confirms mechanical malalignment. A lateral opening wedge distal femoral osteotomy is performed to address their knee pain and correct their alignment. Which of the following patients was most likely to have undergone this procedure?

## Options

1. A 62-year-old active female with medial joint line and direct anterior knee pain, with a mechanical limb axis that passes centrally between their medial and tibial spines.
2. A 35-year-old male long-distance runner with isolated lateral joint line pain and a mechanical limb axis that passes through their fibular head
3. A 67-year-old female active swimmer with medial and lateral joint line pain, with subchondral sclerosis and marginal osteophyte formation throughout their medial and lateral tibiofemoral compartments.
4. A 38-year-old female with remote history of MPFL reconstruction and anterior and medial tibial tubercle transfer, with progressive anterior knee pain.
5. A 50-year-old male weight-lifter with medial joint line pain, with a mechanical limb axis that passes through the peripheral third of his medial femoral condyle.

## Correct option

**2. A 35-year-old male long-distance runner with isolated lateral joint line pain and a mechanical limb axis that passes through their fibular head**

## Answer explanation

A 35-year-old male long-distance runner with isolated lateral joint line pain and a mechanical limb axis that passes through their fibular head, suggesting valgus malalignment, is the most likely candidate amongst the answer choices to have undergone a lateral opening wedge distal femoral osteotomy (Answer 2).

Distal femoral osteotomy (DFO) is a potential surgical option for correcting either varus or valgus knee deformity. In younger, more active patients with isolated lateral compartment degenerative changes, a varus-producing distal femoral osteotomy, or lateral opening wedge osteotomy, serves as a useful surgical option that may correct their mechanical axis and alleviate symptoms, without needing to perform arthroplasty. Arthroplasty is typically reserved for older patients due to the finite lifespan of implants and the challenges associated with revision arthroplasty. Creating an opening in the lateral femur effectively leads to a more varus knee alignment, shifting the mechanical axis medially (i.e., more central through the knee joint), and offloading the lateral compartment, thereby alleviating stress imparted on the lateral tibia/femur and reducing pain in that compartment.

*Sherman et al. reviewed distal femoral varus osteotomy (DFVO), detailing the pathoanatomy of valgus malalignment, as well as indications, techniques, and complications of this procedure.* **They noted** that DFVO, either with a medial closing wedge or lateral opening wedge osteotomy, has demonstrated acceptable mid-to-long-term survivorship in multiple studies, although conflicting results exist. **They concluded** that the DFVO procedure may be an effective tool to restore a neutral mechanical axis to the limb in appropriately selected patients, and can be performed either as a primary procedure in physiologically young, active patients with isolated lateral arthritis, or concomitantly in patients undergoing other soft-tissue stabilization, cartilage restoration, or meniscal replacement procedures.

*Cameron et al. reviewed a case series of thirty-eight knees in thirty-six patients who underwent lateral opening wedge distal femoral osteotomies.* **They found** that of the fifteen knees that underwent surgery for isolated lateral compartment arthritis with valgus deformity and had minimum two-year follow-up with available postoperative radiographs, only seven knees were within the correction goal of ± 3° from neutral mechanical alignment. This cohort of patients, however, did report improved pain and function scores postoperatively, with a 5-year survival rate of 74%. Therefore, **the authors concluded** that while correction accuracy was often suboptimal, the improved clinical outcomes justified this procedure​ as a viable surgical option.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: This patient is not an ideal candidate for a lateral opening wedge distal femoral osteotomy, as her pain suggests broader involvement including the patellofemoral joint, and her radiographic findings suggest a lack of significant coronal malalignment.
Answer 3: This patient presenting with both medial and lateral joint line pain and widespread joint degeneration likely suffers from tricompartmental osteoarthritis, better addressed with a total knee arthroplasty rather than osteotomy.
Answer 4: This patient has a history of procedures aimed at correcting patellar maltracking and currently presents with recurrent patellofemoral pain symptoms which would likely not be corrected by a distal femoral osteotomy alone.
Answer 5: This patient with isolated medial joint line pain and varus alignment (mechanical axis passing medially) would not be a candidate for lateral opening wedge distal femoral osteotomy, as they present with varus malalignment, not valgus.
