## Options

2. Chemotherapy
3. Radiation
4. Imaging
5. Surgery

## Correct option

**4. Imaging**

## Answer explanation

This patient had unplanned excision of myxoid liposarcoma. Further imaging needs to be performed, including CT chest, abdomen, and pelvis and MRI of the extremity. Management thereafter will include re-excision of the tumor bed and radiation therapy vs amputation.

Surgeons need to be aware of red flags that suggest malignancy/soft tissue sarcoma (STS): Masses >5cm, rapid increase in size, masses deep to fascia, pain in a previously painless mass, and recurrent swelling. Biopsy, staging and imaging should be performed in sarcoma centers. Surgical margins during re-excision need to be more extensive because of post-operative changes after the 1st surgery, and neighboring tissue would have been contaminated during the 1st surgery. CT/MRI will guide these margins. Limb salvage (tumor bed re-excision and radiotherapy) or amputation are options at revision surgery.

*Umer et al. retrospectively explored the impact of unplanned STS excision on prognosis.* *For first resections (planned surgery, 84 patients), local recurrence and metastasis rates were 14.3% and 8.3%, respectively.* *For revision surgery (after initial surgery showed unplanned sarcoma excision, 51 patients), rates were 21.4% and 13.7%, respectively.* The average duration from unplanned excision to revision surgery was 8 months. Wide re-excision and radiotherapy was attempted in 48 patients, 3 needed amputation. **They concluded** that wide resection of STS has poor outcomes.

*Potter et al. retrospectively examined local recurrence after unplanned excision of high-grade STS in patients.* Local recurrence was 6% after planned excision, and 34% after unplanned excision. Patients with unplanned excisions undergoing limb salvage were more likely to require flap coverage/skin grafting (30% vs 5%). Residual disease at the tumor bed predicted local recurrence and decreased survival.

Figures A and B are AP and lateral radiographs showing a large posteromedial soft tissue mass without bony involvement. Figures C and D are coronal T1-weighted and axial T2-weighted images showing a large solid, variegated mass consistent with soft tissue sarcoma.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Reassurance is inappropriate as this is an unplanned STS excision which has higher local recurrence and metastasis rates, and poorer survival rates than planned excisions.
Answer 2: Chemotherapy is not indicated at this stage.
Answer 3: Radiation therapy is indicated after limb salvage surgery. It should not be given prior to imaging investigations to exclude distant metastases (CT) and to assess necessary margins (MRI) at revision surgery.
Answer 5: Surgery should not be performed until proper imaging investigations are complete.
