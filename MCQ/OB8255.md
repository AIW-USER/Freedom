## Question

A 32-year-old man who is a smoker sustained an open tibial fracture and underwent a staged treatment with placement of an intramedullary nail. Four weeks after surgery, he developed a pseudomonas deep-wound infection. What is the strongest predictor of persistent infection if implants are retained until fracture union?

## Options

1. Open fracture
2. Smoking history
3. Pseudomonas organism
4. Tibia fracture

## Correct option

**1. Open fracture**

## Answer explanation

*Berkes and associates* **showed** that in a single-center retrospective study of all patients treated with open reduction and internal fixation of extremity fractures that resulted in early postsurgical deep wound infections, the only variables that showed statistical significance were history of open fracture as the initial injury or use of an intramedullary nail. Smoking history, intraoperative cultures showing pseudomonas species, and involvement of the lower extremity were not significant. *Rightmire and associates examined patients at longer follow up, and showed smoking to be a risk factor for clinical failure.*
