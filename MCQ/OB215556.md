## Question

A 35-year-old male is involved in a high-speed motor vehicle collision and sustains an anterior knee dislocation which is reduced in the trauma bay. He is subsequently noted to be unable to dorsiflex his foot. An ankle-foot-orthosis (AFO) is ordered. At a later follow-up visit, he remains unable to dorsiflex the foot despite completing a course of physical therapy. He mentions his desire to walk independently without the AFO and you suggest a tendon transfer to improve his ankle dorsiflexion. What nerve innervates the musculo-tendinous complex being transferred?

## Options

1. Deep Peroneal n.
2. Tibial n.
3. Sural n.
4. Superficial Peroneal n.
5. Saphenous n.

## Correct option

**2. Tibial n.**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a foot drop secondary to a peroneal nerve palsy. The procedure in question is a posterior tibial tendon transfer. The tibial nerve innervates the posterior tibialis.

Peroneal nerve injury can occur for multiple reasons including trauma, proximal nerve compression, laceration, local compression and systemic neuropathy. If the nerve injury occurs at the knee it can result in sensory loss to the lateral leg as well as motor deficits in eversion and dorsiflexion, causing a foot drop and making ambulation difficult. Initial treatment is an ankle-foot-orthosis (AFO) and observation. If the function of the nerve does not return within 6-12 months, consideration may be given to posterior tibial tendon transfer, which transfers the posterior tibial tendon (innervated by the tibial nerve) through the posterior interosseous membrane and to the lateral cuneiform dorsally. The new vector of pull from the posterior tibial tendon recreates the ability to dorsiflex the ankle.

*Werner et al. compared the outcomes of nonoperative management and posterior tibial tendon transfer for peroneal nerve injury after a multi-ligament knee injury.* **They noted** that the transfer group had improved dorsiflexion and sagittal plane gait mechanics. **They concluded** that posterior tibial tendon transfer is a viable option for patients wishing to obviate the need for an AFO.

*Poage et al. reviewed peroneal nerve palsy and its management.* **They note** the most common presentation seen is a foot drop with or without sensory deficits. Nonsurgical measures can be beneficial in the case of nerve compression, although surgery is sometimes required to decompress the nerve. Additionally, **they note** that nerve and tendon transfers can be useful as a salvage procedure in the setting of failed peroneal nerve recovery.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Deep peroneal nerve innervates the anterior compartment extensor musculature.
Answer 3: Sural nerve does not have any motor innervation.
Answer 4: Superficial peroneal nerve innervates the lateral compartment including the peroneals.
Answer 5: Saphenous nerve does not have any motor innervation.
