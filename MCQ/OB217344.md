## Question

You're performing a cadaveric lab on total knee arthroplasty and various soft tissue releases. During the lab, you're asked to report on the function of the posterior oblique ligament. The primary role of this ligament is to provide resistance to which of the following motions?

## Options

1. Varus
2. Valgus
3. Internal rotation
4. External rotation
5. Flexion

## Correct option

**3. Internal rotation**

## Answer explanation

The primary function of the posterior oblique ligament (POL) is to resist internal rotation.

There are three important soft tissue structures that provide support to the medial side of the knee including the deep MCL, superficial MCL, and the POL. The deep MCL is commonly released as part of the standard approach for TKA. The POL and superficial MCL work to complement each other, both providing some restraint to valgus and internal rotation. The posterior oblique ligament provides primary stability against internal rotation with secondary restraints to valgus and external rotation. The superficial MCL, however, resists primarily valgus stress as well as the combined forces of valgus and internal rotation.

*Tang et al review selective soft tissue releases as it relates to TKA.* *They describe a medial release technique that first involves the deep MCL followed by selective release of the superficial MCL or POL and finally a medial reduction osteotomy.* **They conclude** that this technique can be used to achieve stable and balanced gaps during TKA.

*Kim et al review correction of severe varus deformities in TKA.* *They report on a technique in which they achieve a balanced flexion gap by needle puncturing and spreading of the superficial medial collateral ligament.* The extension gap is then balanced by gradual extension with the trial components in place. **They conclude** that this technique is safe and effective in achieving balanced gaps without large soft tissue releases.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The primary restraint to varus stress is the lateral collateral ligament.
Answer 2: The superficial MCL is the primary restraint to valgus stress. The POL does have a secondary function to limit valgus, but this is not its primary role.
Answer 4: External rotation laxity occurs following the release of the LCL and posterolateral structures.
Answer 5: Flexion is not influenced by the POL.
