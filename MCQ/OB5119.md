## Question

Inability of osteoclasts to acidify Howship's lacuna will result in:

## Options

1. Albers-Schönberg disease
2. Rickets
3. Osteogenesis Imperfecta
4. Spondyloepiphyseal Dysplasia
5. Multiple Epiphyseal Dysplasia

## Correct option

**1. Albers-Schönberg disease**

## Answer explanation

Albers-Schönberg disease, or autosomal dominant osteopetrosis, type II (ADO II), is the most common form of osteopetrosis. This disease is caused by an inability of the osteoclast to acidify Howship's lacuna.

Defective bone resorption causes osteopetrosis, which is characterized by dense but usually fragile bones. Autosomal dominant osteopetrosis (ADO) is much more common than its recessive counterparts. Among families with ADO, two subtypes are generally reported based primarily on radiographic features. Type I features a generalized, diffuse osteosclerosis affecting especially the cranial vault. Type II manifests radiographically with a segmentary osteosclerosis, predominately at the vertebral endplates (‘rugger jersey spine’), iliac wings (‘bone within bone’ sign) and skull base.

*Landa et al. reviewed the orthopaedic management of the patient with osteopetrosis.* **They state** that osteopetrosis significantly compromises the body’s ability to acidify the Howship’s lacuna and to resorb bone and calcified cartilage. This is caused by genetic defects in the chloride channel 7 gene (ie, CLCN7), the proton pump, and carbonic anhydrase II.

*Armstrong et al. also reviewed the combined experience of 57 surgeons who treated 79 patients with osteopetrosis.* Historically most fractures in patients with osteopetrosis have been treated non-operatively. **They concluded** that surgical management of the skeletal problems is reasonable in certain circumstances and is especially indicated for coxa vara and femoral neck fractures.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Rickets is a defect in mineralization of osteoid matrix caused by inadequate calcium and phosphate.
Answer 3: Osteogenesis Imperfecta is a hereditary condition resulting from a decrease in the amount of normal Type I collagen.
Answer 4: Spondyloepiphyseal Dysplasia is caused by abnormal synthesis of Type II collagen due to mutation of COL2A1 on chromosome 12.
Answer 5: Multiple Epiphyseal Dysplasia results from a defect in the cartilage oligomeric matrix protein gene and type IX collagen. This results in type II collagen dysfunction because of type IX collagen acts as a link protein for type II collagen.
