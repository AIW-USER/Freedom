## Answer explanation

Metaphyseal corner fractures or "classic metaphyseal lesions" (CML) are most suspicious for nonaccidental trauma in pediatric femur fractures.

Any femur fracture in a nonambulatory child should raise suspicion for nonaccidental trauma. Other factors that should also raise suspicion for nonaccidental trauma in pediatric femur fractures include suspicious histories, associated injuries, and metaphyseal corner fractures. Metaphyseal corner fractures may also be referred to as classic metaphyseal lesions (CML). Diaphyseal femur fractures have been reported to be associated with a lower incidence of abuse. When there is any suspicion of child abuse it should be reported to the appropriate authorities in efforts to prevent any further abuse episodes.

*Wood et al. performed a systematic review to identify factors associated with child abuse in pediatric femur fracture patients.* **They found** in patients less than 12 months of age with a femur fracture an abuse rate between 16.7% to 35.2%. **They concluded** that these studies suggest a high rate of nonaccidental trauma in patients <12 months with a femur fracture.

*Kocher et al. reviewed the diagnosis and treatment of pediatric femoral shaft fractures.* *They give a Grade A recommendation that all children <36 months of age with a pediatric femur fracture be evaluated for nonaccidental trauma.* **They go on to state** that "evaluating" a patient should include routine elements of the examination plus reaching out to their family pediatrician, consulting child abuse professionals within the institution, and ordering a skeletal survey.

Figure A displays a typical metaphyseal "corner fracture". Figure B shows a long spiral pediatric femoral shaft fracture. Figure C shows a slipped capital femoral epiphysis (SCFE) on the left side. Figure D shows a transverse mid-diaphyseal pediatric femur fracture. Figure E shows a subtrochanteric pediatric femur fracture.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2, 3, 4, & 5: These patterns of femur fractures have a lower association with child abuse compared to a metaphyseal femur fracture.
