## Question

A 19-year-old male collegiate basketball player presents to the orthopedic clinic with a 3-month history of anterior knee pain. The pain was initially only present at the beginning of practice but now bothers him at rest. He is currently in season and sought medical care because the pain prevented him from finishing a recent practice. On physical exam, he has an intact extensor mechanism with pain at the distal pole of the patella, with the knee in full extension, which improves with knee flexion. In addition to physical therapy with eccentric exercises, what recommendation should be made to the patient?

## Options

1. Corticosteroid injection
2. Operative debridement of patellar tendon
3. Platelet-rich plasma injection
4. Rest from sport and jumping
5. Injection of sclerosing agent

## Correct option

**4. Rest from sport and jumping**

## Answer explanation

This patient presents with a history and exam consistent with patellar tendinopathy. High-quality evidence supports eccentric exercises and rest from sports for the treatment of patellar tendinopathy (Answer 4).

Patellar tendinopathy is a common cause of anterior knee pain in jumping athletes and is largely considered a degenerative disorder, rather than inflammatory. This patient has pain with activity and rest and is therefore Blazina stage 3 (Illustration A). High-quality evidence recommends eccentric exercises and rest from sports for the initial treatment of patellar tendinosis. While there is variability in treatment protocols, the available studies indicate that a treatment plan should include a decline board, some level of discomfort, and removal from sports.

*Larsson et al. published a systematic review of randomized controlled trials regarding the treatment of patellar tendinopathy.* **They report** that evidence for platelet-rich plasma and sclerosing agents is experimental. They do show that surgical intervention is beneficial in a subset of patients who fail non-operative care. They endorse eccentric exercises and rest from sports as the initial treatment.

*Figueroa et al. published a review article regarding the diagnosis and treatment of patellar tendinopathy.* *They cite a randomized controlled trial performed in 2005 that showed no significant improvement if athletes continued to participate in a competitive season.* **They conclude** that eccentric exercises and rest from sport are the cornerstones of initial management.

*Blazina et al. classified what was not considered patellar tendinitis based on clinical features alone.* While the understanding of patellar tendinopathy has advanced over the past 50 years, this classification system remains the most complete reference of patellar tendinopathy. A complete list of the classification system is found in Illustration A.

Illustration A shows the four Blazina stages of patellar tendinopathy.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Corticosteroid injections are not recommended in the management of patellar tendinopathy. This is a degenerative condition and corticosteroids carry a risk of tendon rupture.
Answer 2: Operative debridement is not recommended without a trial of non-operative care.
Answer 3: Platelet-rich plasma has inconsistent results in the literature and is considered experimental.
Answer 5: Sclerosing agents have inconsistent results in the literature and are considered experimental.
