## Question

A 62-year-old female presents to your office with complaints of worsening hand deformities over the past several years. Radiographs of the bilateral hands reveal ulnar drift and volar subluxation of the metacarpophalangeal joints. Her serum laboratory markers for anti-citrullinated peptide antibodies are abnormal. Which of the following would describe this patient's most likely underlying diagnosis?

## Options

1. IgA cell-mediated immune response
2. IgD cell-mediated immune response
3. IgG cell-mediated immune response
4. IgE cell-mediated immune response
5. IgM cell-mediated immune response

## Correct option

**5. IgM cell-mediated immune response**

## Answer explanation

This patient's radiographic findings and serum laboratory markers are suggestive of underlying rheumatoid arthritis (RA). This is an IgM antibody against native IgG antibodies.

Rheumatoid arthritis is a chronic systemic autoimmune disease caused by an IgM cell-mediated (T cell-MHC type II) immune response against soft tissues (early), cartilage (later), and bone (later). Rheumatoid factor is an IgM antibody against native IgG antibodies that leads to an immune complex that is deposited in end tissues like the kidney as part of the pathophysiology. Anti-citrullinated protein antibodies (ACPA) are useful in the diagnostic evaluation for RA. Unlike other polyarthritides, ACPA has a relatively high specificity for RA. The key difference between anti-CCP and ACPA is that anti-CCP is a subset of ACPA useful as an autoantibody marker to detect patients with rheumatoid arthritis, while ACPA is a major category of autoantibodies.

*Whiting et al. compared the accuracy of ACPA and rheumatoid factor in diagnosing RA in patients with early symptoms of the disease.* **They reported** that in cohort studies anti-cyclic citrullinated peptide antibodies (anti-CCP2) had greater specificity than rheumatoid factor (96% vs. 86%), with similar sensitivity. **They noted** that evidence was insufficient to ascertain whether the combination of anti-CCP2 and rheumatoid factor provides additional benefit over anti-CCP2 alone. **They concluded** that anti-CCP2 should be included in the work-up of patients with early symptoms of rheumatoid arthritis.

*Shiraishi et al. reviewed diagnosis and therapeutic intervention at early stages of RA and psoriatic arthritis (PsA).* *They report on the different features of RA and PsA seen in the hand on various imaging modalities, including radiography, US, MRI, and dual-energy CT, with updates on the contemporary role of imaging in diagnosis and treatment.* **They concluded** that the radiologist should have sufficient knowledge to interpret imaging findings and understand the strengths and weaknesses of each modality to recommend the appropriate imaging method and differentiate both diseases accurately.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: IgA is the major antibody in secretions found in saliva, tears, colostrum, intestinal, genital tract, and respiratory secretions. It appears in mucosa membranes and protects the epithelial surfaces of the digestive, respiratory, and genitourinary systems.
Answer 2: IgD is a monomer and has an unknown function against pathogens. IgD may play an essential role in antigen-triggered lymphocyte differentiation.
Answer 3: IgG is a monomer that is synthesized mostly in the secondary immune response to pathogens. IgG can activate the classical pathway of the complement system, and it also is highly protective. IgG is the only immunoglobulin that crosses the placenta and is thus the most abundant antibody present in newborns.
Answer 4: IgE is a monomer that protects against parasites and binds to high-affinity receptors on mast cells and basophils, causing allergic reactions. IgE is considered the most important host defense against different parasitic infections.
