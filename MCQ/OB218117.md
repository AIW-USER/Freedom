## Question

A 45-year-old secretary presents to the office with a chief complaint of several months of pain and numbness predominantly along the thumb, index, and middle fingers that typically occurs after prolonged periods of typing at her desk. On your examination, you diagnose her with carpal tunnel syndrome after obtaining a score of 12 on the Carpal Tunnel Syndrome 6 (CTS 6) questionnaire. Which of the following constitutes one of the diagnostic criteria used on this questionnaire?

## Options

1. Compensatory thumb flexion when attempting to hold a piece of paper between the thumb and index fingers
2. Hypothenar musculature atrophy
3. Inability to make an "OK" sign with the thumb and index fingers
4. Lack of nocturnal numbness
5. Loss of index finger two-point discrimination

## Correct option

**5. Loss of index finger two-point discrimination**

## Answer explanation

A score of 12 or higher on the Carpal Tunnel Syndrome 6 (CTS 6) questionnaire is considered clinically diagnostic of carpal tunnel syndrome. The questionnaire includes the following six components with their respective scores: Numbness predominantly or exclusively in the median nerve territory (3.5); Nocturnal numbness (4); Thenar atrophy and/or weakness (5); Positive Phalen's test (5); Loss of two-point discrimination in the median-innervated digits (4.5); Positive Tinel sign (4).

Carpal tunnel syndrome is a compressive neuropathy of the median nerve at the wrist. It typically presents clinically with numbness in the median nerve distribution of the hand (radial 3 1/2 digits), night pain or numbness that wakes the patient from sleep, and positive provocative tests including a Tinel sign, Phalen's test, and/or Durkan's flexion-compression test. Diagnosis is most often made solely on clinical and physical exam findings, with a score of > 12 out of 26 on the CTS 6 considered significant for the diagnosis of carpal tunnel syndrome. Though EMG/NCS can be used to confirm the diagnosis and is useful for prognostication, obtaining one is not required to establish the diagnosis.

*Fowler et al. compared the use of ultrasound and electrodiagnostic testing for the diagnosis of carpal tunnel syndrome (CTS).* *The authors tested 85 patients, calculating the sensitivity, specificity, and accuracy for diagnosis of CTS using ultrasound and electrodiagnostic testing with use of the CTS-6 as the reference standard.* **The authors concluded** that ultrasound had a sensitivity of 89% and a specificity of 90%, while electrodiagnostic testing had a sensitivity of 89% and a specificity of 80%, making ultrasound a reasonable option to confirm the diagnosis of carpal tunnel syndrome with better specificity and equal sensitivity as compared with those of electrodiagnostic testing.

*Graham et al. reviewed the value added by electrodiagnostic testing in the diagnosis of carpal tunnel syndrome.* *The probability of carpal tunnel syndrome before electrodiagnostic testing (pretest probability) was estimated with use of the CTS-6 in 143 patients, with 73 percent of the patients having a pretest probability of at least 0.80 (correlating to a score >/= 12).* **The authors concluded** that for the majority of patients who are considered to have carpal tunnel syndrome on the basis of their history and physical examination alone, electrodiagnostic tests do not change the probability of diagnosing this condition to an extent that is clinically relevant.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: This describes a positive Froment's sign, which is used to assess for ulnar nerve injury causing a lack of thumb opposition. It is not diagnostic of median nerve pathology associated with compression at the carpal tunnel.
Answer 2: Hypothenar muscular atrophy would be expected with an ulnar nerve compressive neuropathy; thenar muscle atrophy, on the other hand, is a part of the CTS 6 diagnostic criteria.
Answer 3: The inability to make an "OK" sign indicates AIN weakness, which could be associated with a more proximal AIN compressive neuropathy and represents a pure motor syndrome without sensory symptoms.
Answer 4: The presence of nocturnal symptoms, not the lack thereof, is one of the diagnostic criteria.
