## Answer explanation

This patient likely has ossification of the posterior longitudinal ligament (OPLL). Figure C is suggestive of OPLL.

Ossification of the posterior longitudinal ligament is an idiopathic cervical spine anomaly that is a common cause of cervical myelopathy in the Asian population. Diagnosis is made with lateral radiographs of the cervical spine. CT scan is the study of choice to determine the extent and thickness of the ossification. Treatment is observation in patients with minimal symptoms. Surgical decompression followed by stabilization is indicated for patients with myelopathy.

*Liu et al. reviewed multilevel cervical myelopathy resulting from cervical spondylotic myelopathy (CSM) or ossification of posterior of the longitudinal ligament (OPLL).* In their comparison of direct decompression by anterior corpectomy (CORP) versus indirect decompression by posterior laminoplasty (LAMP), **they reported** that anterior CORP and fusion is recommended for the treatment of multilevel cervical myelopathy when the involved surgical segments were <3. **They concluded** that given the higher rates of surgical complications and complication-related reoperation and the higher surgical trauma associated with multilevel CORP, it is suggested that posterior LAMP may be the preferred method of treatment for multilevel cervical myelopathy when the involved surgical segments were equal to 3 or more.

*Ma et al. performed a meta-analysis evaluating the clinical results between laminoplasty (LP) and laminectomy with fusion (LF) in the treatment of multilevel cervical OPLL.* **They reported** that both LP and LF can achieve clinical improvement in the treatment of multilevel cervical OPLL. **They concluded** that LF was superior to LP in maintaining cervical lordosis. However, LP showed lower surgical trauma than LF.

Figure D depicts a Carrot stick fracture. Figure E depicts an Anderson lesion. Illustration A is a sagittal CT image depicting OPLL.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: This is suggestive of ankylosing spondylitis (AS)
Answer 2: This is suggestive of diffuse idiopathic skeletal hyperostosis (DISH)
Answer 4: This is suggestive of a carrot stick fracture which is a complication of AS
Answer 5: This is suggestive of an Andersson lesion which is a complication of AS
