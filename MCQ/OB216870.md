## Question

A 37-year-old male presents to your office with complaints of medial right knee pain of multiple years duration. He is a construction worker and states he is now having trouble performing his daily duties. He has done physical therapy and been given a steroid injection by a different provider with little relief. On examination, his knee demonstrates varus alignment and tenderness is present primarily over the medial joint line. Radiographs are obtained which demonstrate a varus deformity of 8º of the right knee with moderate arthritis in the medial compartment only. You offer him a medial unloader brace which does passively correct his deformity and improve his pain but he is unable to tolerate it while he is working. Given this scenario, what is the best definitive treatment option for this patient?

## Options

1. Medial meniscus transplant
2. Medial opening wedge high tibial osteotomy
3. Lateral distal femoral opening wedge osteotomy
4. Unicompartmental knee arthroplasty
5. Total knee arthroplasty

## Correct option

**2. Medial opening wedge high tibial osteotomy**

## Answer explanation

Valgus producing high tibial osteotomy (HTO) is the most appropriate treatment strategy to correct this patient's varus deformity and offload his medial compartment arthritis.

Valgus producing HTO is a powerful procedure in cases of young, active patients who have medial compartment arthritis and not good candidates for receiving a unicompartmental- (UKA) or total knee arthroplasty (TKA). Full-length alignment radiographs are necessary to delineate the degree of malalignment prior to indicating the patient for HTO as well as understanding the degree of correction necessary during surgery. Patients who have passively correctable deformities when using a medial unloader brace have a higher likelihood of success with an HTO. Both lateral closing wedge and medial opening wedge techniques can be performed, each with specific advantages and disadvantages. Contraindications to valgus producing HTO include arthritis in the lateral or patellofemoral compartments, lateral tibial subluxation, and varus deformity of >10º.

*Amendola et al. review the indications and techniques surrounding knee osteotomies and meniscal transplantation.* *They discuss meniscal transplants being appropriate in patients with normal (or correctable) alignment and no arthritis and go on discuss technique.* *They also talk about specifics of knee osteotomies and how to plan and evaluate for the location and degree of correction necessary.*

*Sprenger et al. published their results using tibial osteotomies in cases of varus knee arthritis.* *They performed a lateral closing wedge osteotomy in 76 patients,* **noting** a 10-year survival rate of 90% when a correction to 8-16º of valgus alignment was achieved. **They concluded** that valgus producing HTO has a role as an alternative to TKA in younger patients with malalignment.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Given the malalignment and significant arthritis in the medial compartment, medial meniscus transplant would not be appropriate.
Answer 3: Lateral distal femoral opening wedge osteotomy would be helpful in addressing a valgus knee deformity.
Answer 4 & 5: UKA and TKA would not be appropriate options for a young laborer in this scenario.
