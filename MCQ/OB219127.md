## Question

A 26-year-old male motorcyclist is brought to the emergency department after laying down his motorcycle while lane splitting at high speeds in an effort to avoid rush-hour traffic. His knee is deformed, and a radiograph taken in the trauma bay is available for review in Figure A. You perform a closed reduction, and a subsequent ankle-brachial index for the injured extremity is 0.0, although the noisy trauma bay has you doubting your reading. Dorsalis pedis and posterior tibial pulses are difficult to palpate when compared to the contralateral limb. Which of the following best describes the most likely vascular injury sustained by this patient?

![[q219127-a-posteriorkneedis.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. A complete tear of the peroneal artery
2. A partial intimal tear of the peroneal artery
3. A complete tear of the popliteal artery
4. A partial intimal tear of the popliteal artery
5. This ABI is normal and vascular injury is unlikely

## Correct option

**3. A complete tear of the popliteal artery**

## Answer explanation

This patient has sustained a high-energy posterior knee dislocation. The rate of vascular injury in the setting of posterior knee dislocations is high (40-50%) and is most commonly a complete tear of the popliteal artery (Answer 3).

Knee dislocations are high-energy traumatic injuries characterized by a high rate of concomitant neurovascular injury. While they are rare (0.02% of all orthopaedic injuries), the true incidence is likely underreported as approximately 50% self-reduce and therefore are misdiagnosed. As mentioned, concomitant nerve or vascular injuries are common, with 25% of patients sustaining an injury to the common peroneal nerve and 5-15% of patients sustaining a vascular injury.

Knee dislocations can be classified based on the Kennedy (direction of tibia displacement) or Schenck (based on the number of ruptured ligaments) classification systems. Anterior dislocations represent 30-50% of all dislocations and vascular injury, when present, is usually secondary to an intimal tear due to traction. Posterior dislocations, which are the second most common type, have the highest rate of vascular injury, which is usually a complete tear of the popliteal artery. With regards to the Schenck classification, KD IV dislocations (with injury to the ACL, PCL, PMC and PLC) have the highest rate of vascular injury and are also fraught with other complications including nerve injury and future arthrofibrosis.

*Medina et al. provide a systemic review investigating vascular and nerve injuries in the setting of a knee dislocation.* *Their study ultimately included 862 patients, with 171 sustaining a vascular injury (18%).* The frequency of nerve injury was 25%. Overall, 80% of vascular injuries underwent repair and 12% eventually required amputation. **They found** that 25% of posterior dislocations had a concomitant vascular injury. Overall, given the high rate of concomitant vascular and nerve injury, clinicians should err on the side of caution in ruling out arterial injury.

*Weinberg et al. investigated the role of physical exam in ruling in/out vascular injury in the setting of a knee dislocation.* *A database at a level I trauma center was queried for all patients with a knee dislocation, with 110 patients meeting final inclusion criteria.* While no single physical exam maneuver had 100% sensitivity for ruling out vascular injury, a normal physical exam (consisting of palpable pulses and ABI ≥ 0.9) did have 100% sensitivity for ruling out vascular injury. Overall, **the authors conclude** that the combination of a palpable dorsalis pedis pulse and posterior tibial pulse combined with an ABI of 0.9 or greater was 100% sensitive for the detection of vascular injury based on clinical follow-up at 6 months.

Figure A is a lateral radiograph of a right knee demonstrating a posterior knee dislocation.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1 and 2: the most commonly injured artery in a knee dislocation is the popliteal artery.
Answer 3: posterior knee dislocations are associated with complete tears. Anterior dislocations are associated with intimal tears.
Answer 5: an ABI < 0.9, in the setting of weak pulses, is highly suggestive of an associated arterial injury.
