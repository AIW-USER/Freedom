## Question

A 54-year-old male presents with complaints of left small finger contracture of the MCP joint. The patient is ultimately diagnosed with a benign proliferative disorder characterized by decreased hand function caused by hand contractures and fascial nodules. When considering nonoperative intervention between the use of collagenase Clostridium histolyticum (CCH) or a percutaneous needle fasciotomy (PNF), which of the following is true?

## Options

1. CCH has a lower rate of reintervention at 2 years
2. CCH has a higher rate of reintervention at 5 years
3. Equivalent costs at 5 years when factoring in higher rate of reintervention with PNF
4. Outcomes at 1 year are equivalent between PNF and CCH
5. Greater patient-reported outcomes despite decreased range of motion with CCH compared with PNF

## Correct option

**4. Outcomes at 1 year are equivalent between PNF and CCH**

## Answer explanation

At 1 year, the outcomes of PNF and CCH injection are the same in Dupuytren's disease with predominantly metacarpophalangeal joint involvement.

Dupuytren's disease is a benign proliferative disorder involving the palmar fascia. The diagnosis can be made by physical examination which shows painful nodules in the palm with associated digital contracture(s). Treatment ranges from nonoperative passive stretching to injections, needle aponeurotomy, and operative open fasciectomy if the disease progresses or affects patients’ daily living. Injection of clostridium histolyticum collagenase (Xiaflex) causes lysis and rupture of pathologic cords. It is able to correct MCP/PIP contracture to <5° but is reportedly more successful at MCP correction than PIP correction. Needle aponeurotomy is indicated in mild contractures (at the MCP>PIP). Similarly, outcomes have been more successful for MCP contracture than PIP.

Strömberg et al. compared clinical and patient-reported outcomes between patients who had been treated with either percutaneous needle fasciotomy (PNF) versus enzymatic digestion by injectable collagenase Clostridium histolyticum (CCH) for Dupuytren's disease. They reported that there were no significant differences in the reduction of PIP contracture, range of motion, or patient-reported outcomes between the 2 treatments. They concluded that there is no advantage of CCH treatment compared with PNF in terms of clinical outcome at any time during the 2-year follow-up.

*Scherman et al. compared early and 1-year outcomes of PNF and CCH injection for Dupuytren's disease.* **They reported** that the deficits were reduced by 75% in both groups at 3 months and by 70% in both groups at 12 months. **They concluded** that at 3 months and 1 year, the outcomes of PNF and CCH injection are the same in Dupuytren's disease with predominantly metacarpophalangeal joint involvement.

Strömberg et al. compared the efficacy of collagenase treatment and needle fasciotomy for contracture of the metacarpophalangeal (MCP) joint in Dupuytren disease. They reported that 90% of the patients in both groups had full extension of the treated MCP joint. They concluded that there was no significant difference between the treatment outcomes after collagenase and needle fasciotomy treatment after 1 year.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Reintervention rates at 2 years are 24% for PNF and 41% for CCH.
Answer 2: Reintervention rates at 5 years are 61% for PNF and 55% for CCH.
Answer 3: Including all reinterventions, the cumulative costs per digit at 5 years after NA and CCH were $1,540 and $5,952, respectively.
Answer 5: A 2-year follow-up by Strömberg showed no difference in range of motion or patient-reported outcomes.
