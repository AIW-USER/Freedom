## Question

A high school defensive back is thrown out of the game for blatant targeting. On the sidelines, the team physician assesses the player given complaints of neck pain and bilateral numbness and tingling in the upper extremities that lasts for longer than 15 minutes. A cervical spine radiograph is performed and a Torg ratio of 0.80 is calculated. Which of the following is most true of the return to play status of this patient based on these findings?

## Options

1. A Torg ratio of </= 0.80 has a high positive predictive value for future injury and the player should be held out of the game
2. A Torg ratio of </= 0.80 has a low positive predictive value for future injury and the player should be allowed to return immediately
3. A Torg ratio is only useful in professional football players because they have larger body habitus
4. The player has congenital stenosis of the cervical spine which is an absolute contraindication to return to play
5. The player should not be allowed to return until advanced imaging has been obtained

## Correct option

**5. The player should not be allowed to return until advanced imaging has been obtained**

## Answer explanation

Athletes should be pain-free, completely neurologically intact, and have full strength prior to return to play, with those exhibiting bilateral neurologic symptoms being held out until appropriate workup with advanced imaging (i.e., MRI) has been performed (Answer 5).

The Torg (or Torg-Pavlov) ratio was established to measure congenital stenosis in the cervical spine. According to the original method described, the sagittal diameter of the spinal canal is divided by the diameter of the corresponding vertebral body, with an abnormal ratio being one < 0.82 (Illustration A). Though this ratio was created as a screening measure, in practice it has a very low positive predictive value for determining future injury in athletes. Thus, a ratio of <0.80 indicating congenital stenosis does not represent an absolute contraindication to return to play if there are no clinical signs of instability.

*Pavlov and Torg described their original method of calculating their eponymous ratio in 1987.* *The authors reviewed the radiographs of 23 patient-athletes with cervical spinal neurapraxia and measured the sagittal diameter of the spinal canal relative to the sagittal diameter of the corresponding vertebral body and found the average sagittal spinal canal dimension to be 14 mm in the symptomatic patients compared with 18.9 mm in the control group.* **They concluded** that a spinal canal/vertebral body ratio of less than 0.82 indicates significant cervical spinal stenosis.

*Kang et al. subsequently performed an analysis of three factors involved in fractures and dislocations of the cervical spine and their relation to the degree of injury of the spinal cord in 288 patients with and without neurologic deficits.* *The authors specifically looked at the space available for the spinal cord at the level of the injury, the sagittal diameter of the spinal canal at the uninjured levels, and the Pavlov ratio at the uninjured levels.* **They found** that the mean space available for the spinal cord at the level of the injury was 10.5mm for the patients who had a complete injury of the spinal cord, 13.1mm for those who had an incomplete injury of the spinal cord, 15.9mm for those who had an isolated nerve-root injury, and 16.7mm for those who had no neurological deficit. **They concluded** that the severity of the injury of the spinal cord was in part associated with the space available for the cord after the injury, as measured on plain lateral radiographs.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1 and 2: The Torg ratio has been shown to have poor predictive value for future injury in athletes and should not be used exclusively as a predictor for return to play.
Answer 3: A Torg ratio is less useful in larger, professional football players than in younger athletes given the presence of larger bones that falsely skew the ratio.
Answer 4: The presence of congenital stenosis (i.e., a Torg ratio < 0.80) does not preclude a player from returning to play in the absence of clinical, symptomatic instability.
