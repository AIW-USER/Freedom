## Question

A 62-year-old male patient presents with severe lower back pain radiating to his right lower abdomen and right leg. He previously underwent a right nephrectomy for renal cell carcinoma. On physical exam, he has weakness with right knee extension which is affecting his ability to ambulate. An MRI is performed and is shown in Figures A and B. Staging studies determine this to be an isolated metastatic lesion. What is the next best step in this patient's treatment?

## Options

1. Chemotherapy alone
2. Radiation alone
3. Vertebroplasty
4. Embolization, surgical decompression and stabilization followed by radiation
5. Surgical decompression and stabilization followed by radiation

## Correct option

**4. Embolization, surgical decompression and stabilization followed by radiation**

## Answer explanation

The clinical presentation is consistent with metatstatic renal cell carcinoma to the spine. To optimize his ability to ambulate and minimize blood loss intraoperatively, embolization of the tumor followed by surgical decompression/stabilization should be completed.After surgical stabilization, the patient will need to be treated radiation therapy.

Renal cell carcinoma is one of the 5 carcinomas that commonly metastasize to the bone. Metastasis to the axial skeleton occurs via Batson’s vertebral plexus and may lead to compression of the neural elements, requiring decompression. Due to the vascular nature of renal cell carcinoma, excessive intraoperative bleeding may occur, and preoperative embolization is indicated. The prognosis of metastatic renal cell carcinoma is variable depending on medical condition but may be as short as 6 months.

*Kwon et al. reviewed 25 patients who underwent arterial embolization of hypervascular tumors to minimize bleeding during surgical intervention for long bone metastatic disease.* **They found** that use of embolization helped to decrease the need for transfusion in first 24 hours after surgery. The recommend use of arterial embolization to minimize bleeding, both intra-operatively and post operatively.

*Sun et al. reviewed 16 patients with bony metastatic disease who underwent preoperative embolization.* Coils or polyvinyl alcohol were used. **They found** that blood loss was optimally controlled when tumor staining was diminished by greater than 70%.

Figures A and B show T2 weighted MRI sequences of a lower lumbar spinal lesion from a patient with metastatic renal cell carcinoma. Note the vertebral body destruction and impingement on the nerve roots in the right half of the spinal canal. Illustration A shows a biopsy of renal cell carcinoma; clear cell renal cell carcinoma (seen here) is the most common type. The cells have clear cytoplasm, uniform nuclei and a distinct cell membrane.

Incorrect Answers
Answers 1, 2, 3: None of these options are indicated for management of this patient
Answer 5: Decompression and stabilization should be completed, but after embolization of the tumor to decreased bleeding risk
