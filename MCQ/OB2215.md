## Question

A 5-year-old male presents to your office for continued evaluation of a right equinocavovarus foot. He has a known history of spastic hemiplegic cerebral palsy and has failed conservative management including bracing. On exam, he has stage 2 ulcers on the lateral aspect of his forefoot, a passively correctible equinocavovarus foot, and continuous posterior tibial tendon activity on EMG. What is the best next step in management?

## Options

1. Triple arthrodesis
2. Posterior tibial tendon transfer to the lateral cuneiform
3. Peroneal brevis and tendoachilles lengthening
4. Split posterior tibial tendon transfer
5. Calcaneus slide osteotomy

## Correct option

**4. Split posterior tibial tendon transfer**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a flexible equinocavovarus deformity of the right foot and has already failed conservative management. The treatment of choice for a flexible deformity with continuous posterior tibial activity on EMG is split transfer of the posterior tibial tendon to the peroneus brevis.

Equinocavovarus deformity of the foot is common in patients with spastic hemiplegic CP. This deformity is the result of the tibialis posterior overpowering the peroneals. An isolated posterior tibial tendon transfer is the treatment of choice for patients with CP who are between the ages of 4 and 7 with a flexible cavovarus foot. Continuous activity of the posterior tibialis on EMG dictates split tendon transfer, whereas out-of-phase activity is best managed with complete transfer of the posterior tibialis tendon to the dorsum of the foot. Furthermore, a calcaneal osteotomy is also indicated if a fixed deformity is present.

*Green et al. reviewed the outcomes of split posterior tibial tendon transfer in patients with spastic cerebral palsy at a minimum of two years.* **They report** that all varus deformities were corrected, with only two patients also requiring osteotomies of the calcaneus. **They conclude** that split posterior tibial tendon transfer is a simple procedure that reliably corrects flexible spastic varus deformities.

*Kling et al. review the treatment of equinovarus deformities in children with cerebral palsy.* **They report** that at an average of eight years, thirty patients had excellent results, four good, and three poor following split posterior tibial tendon transfers. **They conclude** that this is a prudent treatment for equinovarus deformity that does not deteriorate with time.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Triple arthrodesis may be performed in combination with a soft tissue procedure if a severe fixed deformity is present.
Answer 2: Posterior tibial tendon transfer to the lateral cuneiform is utilized if the EMG demonstrates out-of-phase activity of the tibialis posterior.
Answer 3: While tendoachilles lengthening is sometimes used in combination with split posterior tibial tendon transfer, peroneus brevis lengthening would not correct this deformity.
Answer 5: Calcaneal osteotomies are used in the setting of a fixed equinocavovarus deformity.
