Chapter: Trauma MCQs — Amputations

## Question

True about below knee amputation (FNB Pattern Paper 2018)

## Options

1. Long anterior flap in non ischemic limb
2. Long anterior flap in ischemic limb
3. Equal length of anterior and posterior flaps in non ischemic limbs
4. Long posterior flap in non ischemic limb

## Correct option

**3. Equal length of anterior and posterior flaps in non ischemic limbs**

## Answer explanation

In the nonischemic patient the flaps are fashioned approximately equal in length. Redundant soft tissues or large “dog ears” should be avoided as they create problems in prosthetic fitting and may prevent maximal function of an otherwise well-constructed stump.

In ischemic limbs fashioning of short anterior and long posterior skin flaps is done due to better vascularity of the long posterior flap.

Techniques of amputation stump closure

- myofascial closure: It encases the transected muscle by closing the outer fascial envelope over the top of the muscles. Myofascial closure in itself is not an effective means of muscle stabilization as it provides only minimal stabilization for the most superficial muscles, and provides inadequate distal attachment of the muscular tissue to the bone. It is primarily used when severe ischemia prevents more effective means of distal muscle fixation.
- Myoplasty: Myoplasty, the second technique, is one by which the surgeon brings the muscles over the end of the bone and sews them to opposing muscle groups. Motion of the antagonistic muscle as a sling over the bone end often creates a bursa, crepitus and discomfort . Because of the frequency of these complications, simple myoplasty is not recommended and the surgeon should instead attempt to secure the tissue directly to the distal end of the bone.
- Myodesis: If the muscle groups themselves are attached directly and securely to the periosteum or the bone, it is called myodesis. In myodesis, the deepest layers of muscle are typically secured directly to the bone, while the more superficial layers of muscle are sewn to each other as a myoplasty. The myofascial envelope is then closed over the top of this muscular reconstruction.

Myodesis may be contraindicated, however, in severe ischemia because of the increased risk of wound breakdown.

- Tenodesis: A final muscle stabilization technique, tenodesis, is very secure but frequently anatomically impossible. Tenodesis involves the firm distal attachment of the severed tendon down to the bone, and is the most physiologic and effective means of muscle stabilization. It is possible only when the muscle belly itself is not transected and the tendon is intact. Tenodesis is most commonly used in disarticulations, and is the primary method in knee disarticulations in which the patellar tendon is secured to the origin of the cruciate ligaments on the distal femur.

Answer : C
