## Question

A 45-year-old seamstress presents with pain and a visible bump on the lateral aspect of her right foot, which is exacerbated by wearing tight shoes. Upon examination, you notice erythema and keratosis over the lateral fifth metatarsal head. She has trialed metatarsal pads and wider shoes without relief. Radiographs show a 4-5 intermetatarsal angle of 10 degrees and a prominent lateral condyle of the fifth metatarsal. What is the most appropriate next step in management for this patient's condition?

## Options

1. Nonweightbearing and contact casting
2. Lateral eminence resection
3. Sliding distal metatarsal minimally invasive osteotomy
4. Open metatarsal diaphyseal osteotomy with internal fixation
5. Metatarsal head resection

## Correct option

**3. Sliding distal metatarsal minimally invasive osteotomy**

## Answer explanation

The patient presents with symptoms and radiographic findings consistent with a type 3 bunionette deformity. The best option to treat this patient is a sliding distal metatarsal minimally invasive osteotomy (S-DMMO) (Answer 3).

Bunionette deformity, historically known as a tailor's bunion, is a common forefoot problem leading to lateral foot pain, which arises from abnormal fifth metatarsal bony anatomy. There are three types: 1) enlargement of the lateral condyle, 2) fifth metatarsal varus, and 3) combined deformity. After conservative measures such as footwear modification, orthotics, and anti-inflammatory medications have failed, surgical intervention may be indicated. In type 1 bunionettes, lateral eminence resection can be performed as a standalone procedure. However, when there is clinically significant metatarsal varus, minimally invasive osteotomy techniques are preferred, as they treat both the deformity and avoid implant-related complications.

*Guangyu Shi et al. provide a descriptive review of bunionette deformity.* It is often multifactorial, involving both bony morphology and soft-tissue imbalance. While nonsurgical management is effective for most patients, persistent symptoms may require surgical intervention. The deformity is classified into types based on radiographic findings, guiding the choice of surgical technique. Surgical options range from lateral eminence resection to various osteotomies of the fifth metatarsal to correct the underlying bony deformity and alleviate symptoms.

*Del Vecchio et al. prospectively analyzed the clinical, functional, and radiographic outcomes of sliding distal metatarsal minimally invasive osteotomy (S-DMMO) in treating bunionette deformity.* *The study involved 74 feet from 57 patients, assessing various radiologic parameters, clinician-reported scores (AOFAS score, VAS), and patient-reported outcomes (FAAM ADL, FAAM sports, MOXFQ, patient satisfaction survey).* Results indicated significant improvements in intermetatarsal angles, metatarsophalangeal angles, and reductions in pain and disability. High patient satisfaction was reported, with 89.1% rating the procedure as excellent. Bone union was achieved at an average of 8.1 weeks. The study concluded that S-DMMO is effective in improving function and pain with a high satisfaction rate and a low incidence of complications.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The patient has already failed conservative management, and contact casting would not address the underlying deformity
Answer 2: Lateral eminence resection is indicated in type 1 bunionette deformity
Answer 4: A diaphyseal osteotomy, while historically utilized, is associated with rotational deformity, and internal fixation increases the rate of implant-related complications
Answer 5: Metatarsal head resection is reserved for failed bunionette surgical treatment as a salvage procedure with relatively poor outcomes
