## Question

Figure A is the radiograph of a 68-year-old male who presents to the ED following a MVC. He undergoes the surgery depicted in Figure B. Compared with a reverse total shoulder arthroplasty, which of the following is most likely true of this patient's outcome?

![[q216815-a-preop-shoulder.jpg]]
*Figure A*

![[q216815-b-shoulder-1.jpg]]
*Figure B*

## Options

1. Greater loss of motion
2. Greater pain relief
3. Higher incidence of humeral periprosthetic fracture
4. Higher overall complication rate
5. Longer implant longevity

## Correct option

**1. Greater loss of motion**

## Answer explanation

This patient sustained for 4-part proximal humerus fracture which was treated with a hemiarthroplasty. Greater loss of motion may be encountered following use of a hemiarthroplasty with tuberosity fixation if tuberosity displacement or resorption occurs.

Proximal humerus fractures are common fractures often seen in older patients with osteoporotic bone following a ground-level fall on an outstretched arm. In patients that are poor candidates for head preservation treatment, both hemiarthroplasty and RSA can provide successful results. Older patients may benefit more with the use of an RSA, as the anatomic healing of the tuberosities around the implant is not as required with an RSA as it is with the use of a hemiarthroplasty. With greater tuberosity displacement or resorption following a hemiarthroplasty, patients will experience a significant loss of active motion. Additionally, the level of pain relief following a hemiarthroplasty is more unpredictable than an RSA.

*Namdari et al. performed a systematic review to critically examine the outcomes of RSA and hemiarthroplasty (with the use of a fracture-specific stem) for the treatment of proximal humeral fractures.* **They reported** that compared with hemiarthroplasty, reverse arthroplasty was associated with 4.0 times greater odds of a postoperative complication. **They concluded** that patients and physicians should consider projected functional outcomes, implant costs, and complication rates when selecting an appropriate arthroplasty technique for this indication.

*Anakwenze et al. performed a systematic review detailing the demographics, outcomes, and complications of patients who undergo RSA for complex proximal humerus fractures.* **They reported** that RSA patients tended to be elderly women and still have postoperative dysfunction despite well-controlled pain. **They reported** that repair of the greater tuberosity allowed for a greater range of motion in patients.

Figure A is the radiograph of the left shoulder demonstrating a 4-part proximal humerus fracture. Figure B is the radiograph of the shoulder revealing a hemiarthroplasty. Illustration A is the radiograph of a shoulder hemiarthroplasty demonstrating reabsorption of the greater tuberosity.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: The level of pain relief following a hemiarthroplasty is more unpredictable than an RSA
Answer 3: Use of either hemiarthroplasty or RSA would have a similar rate of humeral periprosthetic fracture
Answer 4: RSA has a higher complication rate than hemiarthroplasty
Answer 5: Implant longevity is similar between hemiarthroplasty and RSA
