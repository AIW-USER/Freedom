Chapter: Arthroplasty MCQs — Reverse Shoulder Arthroplasty

## Question

Which is not an indication for RTSA? (FNB Pattern Paper 2019)

## Options

1. Massive rotator cuff tear and arthritis with presence of full active shoulder elevation
2. Cuff tear arthropathy
3. Chronic pseudoparalysis with a massive rotator cuff tear and no arthritis
4. Cuff tear arthropathy with pseudoparalysis

## Correct option

**1. Massive rotator cuff tear and arthritis with presence of full active shoulder elevation**

## Answer explanation

Indications to RTSA

1. Cuff tear arthropathy

2. Chronic pseudoparalysis with a massive rotator cuff tear and no arthritis

Chronic pseudoparalysis with a massive rotator cuff tear develops secondary to loss of the fulcrum when the rotator cuff fails. As the deltoid muscle contracts, the humeral head dynamically translates superiorly, and the deltoid loses its lever to elevate the arm. Patients present without evidence of glenohumeral arthritis and are frustrated with the inability to use their upper extremity .The first line of treatment should be rehabilitation to strengthen the remaining shoulder musculature to discover if the patient can recruit enough accessory muscles to elevate their arm. The RTSA can restore active elevation through restoration of a fulcrum for deltoid function but it is indicated only after physical therapy has failed.

Contraindications to RTSA:

- Severely impaired deltoid function,
- Isolated supraspinatus tear, and the presence of full active shoulder elevation with a massive rotator cuff tear and arthritis

RTSA in a patient with glenohumeral arthritis and an isolated supraspinatus (SST) tear : An isolated SST tear with associated arthritis will not produce an unbalanced shoulder. If the shoulder is balanced, an unconstrained total shoulder arthroplasty is appropriate

Answer: 1
