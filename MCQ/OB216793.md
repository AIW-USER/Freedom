## Question

A 23-year-old gymnast sustained a fall off the uneven bars injuring her left elbow. Her surgeon recommends surgery, as she would ultimately develop posteromedial rotatory instability (PRMI) if surgery were not performed. Fixation of which of the following fractures, if present, is most likely to prevent PMRI?

## Options

1. Radial head
2. Coronoid tip
3. Anteromedial coronoid facet
4. Olecranon tip
5. Medial capitellum

## Correct option

**3. Anteromedial coronoid facet**

## Answer explanation

The anteromedial facet of the coronoid is part of the critical weight bearing portion of the ulnohumeral joint and requires fixation in unstable elbow injuries to prevent posteromedial rotatory instability.

Posteromedial rotatory instability occurs following elbow dislocations in which a lateral collateral ligament injury and a coronoid fracture occurs. Characterization of corocoid injury is important following complex elbow trauma. Fractures involving the anteromedial facet of the coronoid should be differentiated from other patterns such as coronoid tip fractures as the anteromedial facet injury can destabilize the elbow in a posteromedial rotatory instability pattern that can deteriorate functional outcome and ultimately result in arthrosis long term. Computed tomography is helpful in delineating coronoid fracture patterns.

*Sanchez-Sotelo et al. reviewed medial oblique compression fracture of the coronoid process of the ulna.* **They reported** that this injury is much more complex than previously appreciated. **They concluded** that injury to this specific portion of the coronoid facet may lead to joint subluxation or dislocation.

*Pollock et al. reviewed the effects of anteromedial facet fractures of the coronoid and lateral collateral ligament injury on elbow stability and kinematics.* **They reported** that the size of the anteromedial coronoid fracture fragment affects elbow kinematics, particularly in varus stress. The size of an anteromedial coronoid fracture and the presence of concomitant ligament injuries may be important determinants of the need for open reduction and internal fixation.

*Bryce et al. reviewed the anatomy and biomechanics of the elbow.* **They reported** that the anterior bundle of the medial collateral ligament (AMCL) is the most discrete portion of this ligamentous complex and inserts onto the anteromedial facet of the coronoid. **They concluded** that the AMCL is able to provide significant stability against valgus force, making it one of the primary static constraints of the elbow.

*Steinmann et al review coronoid fractures.* **They report** that varus and rotation force that results in an anteromedial coronoid fracture is opposite from the force that results in a coronoid tip fracture. When this injury is missed, and left untreated, the ulnohumeral joint will tend to articulate incongruently under gravitational varus stress. **They conclude** that care must be taken to accurately identify the and classify injury patterns to the coronoid to guide treatment as once arthritis changes occur, therapeutic options are limited.

Incorrect Answer:
Answer 1&2: Radial head and coronoid tip fractures are associated with "terrible triad" injuries of the elbow, which result in posterolateral, not posteromedial rotatory instability.
Answer 4: Olecranon tip fractures are not associated with posteromedial rotatory instability, nor would fixing them provide any increased stability to the elbow.
Answer 5: Displaced fractures of the medial capitellum should be fixed if encountered, but do not result in posteromedial rotatory instability if left alone.
