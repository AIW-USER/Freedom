## Question

A 45-year-old man presents for evaluation of 6 months of radicular buttock pain. A MRI is shown in Figure A. The resected mass stains positive for S100 and the histology is shown in Figures B. What is the most likely diagnosis?

![[q1283-a-1283a.jpg]]
*Figure A*

![[q1283-b-1283b.jpg]]
*Figure B*

## Options

1. Schwannoma
2. Liposarcoma
3. Lipoma
4. Malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumor
5. Giant cell tumor of tendon sheath

## Correct option

**4. Malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumor**

## Answer explanation

The history, imaging, and histopathology are consistent with a diagnosis of malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumor (MPNST), a high grade soft tissue sarcoma arising from a nerve or neurofibroma. The MRI of MPNST shows dark T1 and bright T2, like other high grade soft tissue sarcomas, and MPNSTs can usually be traced to a major nerve. The histopathology demonstrates atypical pleomorphic spindle cells on a hypocellular stroma (Figure B). The atypical pleomorphic spindle cells differentiate this lesion from a schwannoma. S100 is a frequently tested cellular marker which is specific for melanomas, nerve tumors, and clear cell sarcomas. *Hruban et al reviewed their clinical experience with MPNSTs and* **found** that tumor size, mitotic rate, and need for amputation all portended a poor prognosis. Interestingly, diagnosis with neurofibromatosis did not appear to be associated with a worse prognosis.

To contrast, illustrations A and B show the MRI and histopathology of a simple schwannoma. The MRI shows the classic "target sign" on fluid sensitive imaging. While the histopathology can show the same linear organization of nerve fibers, the MPNST shows more malignant cellular features of the neoplastic cells.
