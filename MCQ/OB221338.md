## Question

A local high school’s varsity starting pitcher presents to clinic with pain in his throwing shoulder. It is noted that when his shoulders are placed in 90 degrees of abduction, he has approximately 30 degrees decreased internal rotation range of motion in his dominant arm. When compared to the average population, which of the following findings would be expected in this patient’s shoulder?

## Options

1. Overall decreased shoulder total motion arc
2. Increased anteversion of the proximal humerus
3. Increased internal rotation motion
4. Thickening of the posterior capsule
5. Chondral injury along the anterior-inferior glenoid

## Correct option

**4. Thickening of the posterior capsule**

## Answer explanation

Posterior capsular contraction and thickening of the posterior-inferior glenohumeral ligament (PIGHL) are characteristic adaptive responses seen in overhead throwing athletes and can result in symptomatic glenohumeral internal rotation deficit (GIRD). These result from repetitive eccentric stresses placed on the posterior shoulder structures during the follow-through phase of throwing.

Overhead athletes often develop adaptive nonpathological anatomic changes secondary to the biomechanical stresses associated with repetitive throwing. As a result of these changes, many overhead-throwing athletes demonstrate abnormal findings on MRI yet are clinically asymptomatic. Some of these changes include increased proximal humerus retroversion, thickening of the posterior capsule/PIGHL, and attenuation of the anteroinferior glenohumeral ligament and anterior capsule. Collectively, these adaptations increase the shoulder’s overall arch of motion and impart greater kinetic energy via rotational torque during the early cocking phase of throwing. As with any adaptive changes in the body, excessive compensation and repetitive microtrauma can result in symptomatic pathology. Symptomatic GIRD (i.e., >25 degrees of loss of ROM compared to the contralateral shoulder) and internal impingement (i.e., type II SLAP tears and partial articular supraspinatus tendon avulsions at the supraspinatus-infraspinatus junction) are the most encountered symptomatic pathologies in these athletes.

*Tanaka and colleagues performed a cross-sectional analysis of anatomic changes in the shoulders of Japanese baseball players to determine the correlation with throwing disability.* **They found** increased humeral retroversion, increased external rotation, and decreased internal rotation in the athletes’ throwing shoulder compared to the same athletes’ non-throwing shoulder. Further, **they found** that posterior capsular tightness with concomitant loss of internal rotation was observed only in symptomatic patients. **The authors conclude** that symptomatic GIRD may be more related to posterior capsular tightness rather than muscular elasticity.

*Gelber and colleagues provide a review article on the adaptive changes of the shoulder in throwing athletes and common injuries.* **The authors further emphasize** that normal adaptive changes do not necessarily represent pathology for an overhead athlete, and merely attempting to restore normal anatomy may reverse these adaptations and prevent return to the previous level of performance. **They conclude** that these athletes are commonly overtreated, and surgical outcomes consequently may result in worse outcomes compared to nonoperative management.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: While overhead throwing athletes often demonstrate some degree of glenohumeral internal rotation deficit (GIRD), they have a proportionally greater increase in external rotation range of motion, providing an overall increased shoulder total motion arc.
Answer 2: The proximal humerus is retroverted in utero and de-rotates until skeletal maturity. The adaptive changes seen in overhead-throwing athletes occur throughout adolescence, resulting in relatively greater proximal humerus retroversion than in the average population. Greater retroversion leads to more external rotation and less internal rotation. It is proposed that the stresses of repetitive throwing on the proximal humerus physis halt the normal process of de-rotation, leading to more retroversion and less anteversion.
Answer 3: The thickening of the posterior capsule seen in overhead athletes results in loss of internal rotation range of motion (i.e., GIRD), not increased.
Answer 5: Chondral injuries along the anterior-inferior glenoid would be expected with anterior shoulder instability. Adaptive changes of overhead athletes include a thickened/tight posterior capsule and posterior inferior glenohumeral ligament (PIGHL). Collectively, these changes shift the glenohumeral center of rotation posteriorly and superiorly, resulting in posterior-superior chondral injuries rather than anterior-inferior.
