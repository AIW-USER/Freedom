## Question

A 27-year-old male presents to your clinic after injuring his knee playing recreational soccer several days ago. He reports a twisting injury with immediate swelling. On examination, you appreciate a grade 3B Lachman test. Based on this, you order an MRI which confirms your suspicion for a complete anterior cruciate ligament (ACL) rupture. If an ACL reconstruction is performed, which of the following factors most increases his risk for re-rupture?

## Options

1. Transtibial drilling technique
2. Interference screw fixation of graft
3. Hamstring autograft usage
4. Varus laxity
5. Graft size = 9 mm

## Correct option

**4. Varus laxity**

## Answer explanation

Concomitant posterolateral corner (PLC) injury which goes unrecognized at the time of anterior cruciate ligament (ACL) reconstruction places the graft at high risk for failure.

ACL rupture is a very common knee injury in the younger, athletic population and usually occurs as a non-contact, twisting injury with immediate swelling. Treatment is generally ACL reconstruction. A thorough preoperative evaluation is important to ensure accurate diagnosis of concomitant knee injuries, as failure to address these injuries can place a higher degree of stress on the reconstructed ACL graft, leaving it at risk for re-rupture. PLC injuries can be difficult to diagnose on examination due to guarding and swelling but would characteristically demonstrate varus laxity at 30 degrees of flexion in isolated PLC injuries or at 0 and 30 degrees if there was also ACL injury. Dial test would also demonstrate increased tibial external rotation at 30 degrees of flexion with PLC injury. MRI is also a useful adjunct to diagnosis of PLC injury in addition to physical exam.

*Ricchetti et al. discussed acute/chronic management of PLC injuries.* **They note** that isolated PLC injury is rare and often occurs concomitantly with injuries to the cruciate ligaments. They reinforce that all of these ligaments must be appropriately addressed in order to prevent failure of the adjacent structures after repair/reconstruction.

*Fanelli et al. reviewed the treatment of combined ACL/PLC injuries.* **They note** that a systematic approach to these complex injuries is vital in overall success, as failure rates are higher if adjacent structures are neglected. **They conclude** that combined ACL/PLC reconstructions, if performed appropriately, are effective in recreating a stable, functional knee with low rates of complication.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: While transtibial drilling may result in a more vertical graft orientation, it is not an innate risk for re-rupture.
Answer 2: Interference screws are one of several ways of fixating an ACL graft and, if inserted appropriately, have not been shown to increase risk of graft re-rupture
Answer 3: This patient is young and plays recreational sports, making hamstring autograft appropriate. The best graft choice may be debated, but this graft would not represent his highest risk for re-rupture.
Answer 5: A graft of <8mm in diameter has been thought to increase risk for re-rupture.
