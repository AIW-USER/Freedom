## Question

A 55-year-old man is experiencing pain in the neck and right arm, subjective weakness in the right arm, and numbness/tingling in his right thumb. Physical exam is remarkable for weakness of wrist extension. His MRI is shown in Figure A. What EMG findings would you expect to find?

![[q213589-a-screen-shot-2019-07-31-at-4-58-05-pm.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Fibrillations and positive sharp waves of C6 innervated muscles
2. Prolonged H-reflex
3. Increased motor nerve amplitude
4. Increased F-wave
5. Increased distal latency

## Correct option

**1. Fibrillations and positive sharp waves of C6 innervated muscles**

## Answer explanation

Electrodiagnostic studies of patients with radiculopathy will likely reveal fibrillations and positive sharp waves in the affected root distribution (Answer 1).

Cervical radiculopathy results from compression of nerve roots as they branch from the spinal cord and exit the neuroforamen. This results in motor weakness of the muscles innervated by the nerve root, numbness, and neuropathic burning pain. In addition to MRI, electrodiagnostic tests can be used to confirm the affected nerve roots and, in some cases, to prognosticate recovery. In cases of radiculopathy, decreased compound motor action potential amplitude, decreased or absent F-waves, and fibrillations and positive short waves can be seen in the affected distributions.

*Robinson reviewed the literature regarding the use of electromyography in the diagnosis of neurological injuries and myopathies.* *The author described that fibrillations and positive sharp waves were present in patients who had lesions with axon loss, such as radiculopathy.* In chronic radiculopathy, there are increased polyphasic motor unit action potentials, which is the result of demyelinated nerve endings sprouting to reinnervate nearby denervated muscle.

*Lee et al. reviewed the literature regarding the use of electrodiagnostic tests in the diagnosis of peripheral nerve lesions.* **The authors reported** that nerve conduction can be affected by temperature, age, height, and inaccurate distance measurements. In radiculopathy, the EMG portion is most important since the sensory nerve findings are equivocal due to the dorsal root ganglia being located distal from the site of compression. Further motor conduction velocities are often unremarkable due to the site of compression being proximal to the tested sites.

Figure A is the sagittal T2 MRI of the cervical spine with a herniated nucleus pulposus at C5-6. Illustration A is an example of positive sharp waves. Illustration B is an example of a fibrillation potential.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: The H-reflex is a monosynaptic reflex of the gastro-soleus muscle complex and is the neurophysiologic response to the ankle reflex. It is only present with S1 radiculopathy or peripheral neuropathy.
Answer 3: Radiculopathy will likely result in decreased motor nerve amplitude if there is an axonal injury.
Answer 4: The F-wave is a recurrent discharge of motor neurons from antidromic stimulation of motor neurons. In severe cases of radiculopathy, a decreased or absent F-wave will result.
Answer 5: Increased distal latency will result from peripheral compression, not from radiculopathy.
