## Question

## Options

1. Observation
2. Knee-ankle-foot Bracing
3. Guided growth of the distal tibia
4. Subtroachanteric femoral rotational osteotomy
5. Supramalleolar osteotomy

## Correct option

**5. Supramalleolar osteotomy**

## Answer explanation

This patient has right external tibial torsion without femoral rotational abnormalities. The calculation for femoral neck rotational alignment is right (17-7=10°) and left (24-18=6°) of femoral anteversion. The calculation for distal tibia rotation alignment is right (70-25=45°) and left (40-8=32°) of external tibial torsion. The most appropriate treatment is a right supramalleolar tibial osteotomy.

External tibial torsion (ETT) is commonly progressive and more often requires surgical treatment than internal tibial torsion. Surgery is indicated for functional reasons if the TFA <-15° or >30° in a child aged 9 years or older (normal thigh foot angle (TFA) is between -5° and 20°). Distal tibial osteotomies have the advantages of easier dissection, easier rotation, less risk of neurovascular compromise or compartment syndrome, and easier stabilization with only K-wires. On the other hand, proximal tibia osteotomy may provide a greater contact area and a thicker soft tissue envelope for vascularity but may have a higher risk of neurovascular compromise. Anterior fasciotomy is necessary with proximal osteotomies to reduce the risk of peroneal nerve palsy from the tight anterior compartment, especially if there is both angular and rotational correction.

*Walton et al. retrospectively reviewed 43 tibiae treated with proximal tibial derotation osteotomy.* All had prophylactic anterior compartment fasciotomy. The mean age was 10 years. Mean preoperative and postoperative TFA in ITT was -14° and 8° respectively. Mean preoperative and postoperative TFA in ETT was 38° and 7° respectively. **They recommend** proximal tibial derotation osteotomy because of the larger bony surface area and soft tissue envelope for treating tibial torsion.

*MacWilliams et al. performed a multicenter study of 18 patients with abnormal tibial rotation and assessed frontal knee moments during gait.* **They reported** an increased internal valgus moment in patients with internal tibial torsion and a relative internal varus moment in patients with external tibial torsion. **They concluded** excessive tibial torsion negatively affects frontal plane knee moments.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1: The patient has been observed for nearly 6 years. Given the patient's age, this deformity is unlikely to resolve spontaneously.
Answers 2: Bracing is minimally effective for rotational deformities of the distal tibia. Further, there is little long-term benefit of this given the patient is near skeletal maturity.
Answer 3: Distal tibial guided growth techniques would likely induce a coronal plane deformity rather than correct the rotational deformity of the distal tibia.
Answer 4: The patient and 10° of right femoral anteversion and 6° of left femoral anteversion. There is no indication for a femoral rotational osteotomy.
