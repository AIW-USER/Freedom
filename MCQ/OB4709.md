## Question

A 6-year-old presents with an elbow deformity after falling from the monkey bars. The skin is intact and no evidence of puckering is seen. The patient is neurovascularly intact. Representative radiographs of the injury are shown in Figures A and B. What is the optimal initial treatment for this injury based on the AAOS guidelines?

## Options

1. Observation alone
2. Closed reduction and casting
3. Primary open reduction and internal fixation
4. Closed reduction with medial and lateral crossed pins
5. Closed reduction with two or three lateral pins

## Correct option

**5. Closed reduction with two or three lateral pins**

## Answer explanation

DISCUSSION: This patient has sustained a displaced (Type 3) supracondylar fracture of the elbow. The optimal treatment is closed reduction with two or three lateral pins

Supracondylar fractures are common pediatric elbow fractures. Displaced injuries should be treated with closed reduction and percutaneous pinning. Although crossed pin constructs (medial and lateral pins) have been shown to be the most biomechanically resistant to torsional stresses, the AAOS guidelines recommend use of lateral pins to avoid the risk of ulnar nerve injury.

*Howard et al. present the AAOS clinical treatment guidelines for pediatric supracondylar injuries.* In treatment of displaced fractures with closed reduction and percutaneous pinning, **they recommend** use of lateral pins. This avoids the risk of iatrogenic ulnar nerve injury, without compromising the integrity of the reduction.

Per the AAOS guidelines: "The AAOS guideline suggests the practitioner might use two or three laterally introduced pins to stabilize the reduction of displaced pediatric supracondylar fractures of the humerus. Considerations of potential harm indicate that the physician might avoid the use of a medial pin. Strength of Recommendation: Weak"

Figures A and B are the AP and lateral radiographs of a displaced pediatric supracondylar fracture.

Incorrect Answers
Answer 1: Observation is not indicated here
Answer 2: Closed reduction and casting could be utilized for Type 1 supracondylar (nondisplaced) fractures.
Answer 3: Open reduction/internal fixation may be used if closed reduction is unsuccessful secondary to incarceration of soft tissues. Open reduction may be utilized if there is malalignment after closed reduction
Answer 4: Per the AAOS guidelines, use of medial pins should be avoided to prevent risk of ulnar nerve injury.
