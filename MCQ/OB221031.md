## Question

A 26-year-old female marathoner presents with a four-month history of right heel pain. She describes the pain as sharp, burning, and worst at the end of the day. Physical exam reveals pain with palpation, focused at the medial-plantar heel. Which of the following best describes the anatomy of the nerve most likely affected in this patient?

## Options

1. The nerve runs superficial to the abductor hallucis
2. The nerve runs deep to the flexor digitorum brevis
3. The nerve runs deep to the quadratus plantae
4. The nerve runs lateral to the calcaneus
5. The nerve is a branch of the medial plantar nerve

## Correct option

**2. The nerve runs deep to the flexor digitorum brevis**

## Answer explanation

This patient has symptoms and a physical exam consistent with entrapment of the first branch of the lateral plantar nerve (Baxter’s nerve). This nerve runs deep to the abductor hallucis and flexor digitorum brevis, superficial to the quadratus plantae, and medial to the calcaneus (Answer choice 2).

Baxter nerve entrapment refers to compression of the first branch of the lateral plantar nerve, typically between the abductor hallucis fascia and the quadratus plantae or at the point where the nerve runs just anterior to the medial calcaneal tuberosity. The nerve passes deep to the abductor hallucis, then travels between the quadratus plantae and flexor digitorum brevis before innervating the abductor digiti quinti. Patients present with pain over the medial aspect of the heel. They may additionally have abductor digiti quinti atrophy due to chronic nerve compression. Treatment for this condition is initially conservative, consisting of rest, ice, and physical therapy. Patients with persistent symptoms despite non-operative management for three months may benefit from surgical decompression.

*Kaur et al. published a case report describing entrapment of the first branch of the lateral plantar nerve and discussed its association with plantar fasciitis.* *They describe the anatomy of the nervous structures in the foot and review MRI findings that may be present when such nerves are compressed.* **They concluded** that this diagnosis, though responsible for approximately a fifth of hindfoot pain cases, is widely missed and requires growing awareness.

*Lareau et al. published a review on the diagnosis and management of plantar and medial heel pain.* *They review possible diagnoses and their treatments, including plantar fasciitis, heel pad atrophy, baxter nerve entrapment, calcaneal stress fractures, and tarsal tunnel syndrome.* **They conclude** that medial and plantar heel pain is common and that treatment for its various pathologies is rarely operative.

*Baxter and Pfeffer published a retrospective review of 53 patients with chronic heel pain who underwent release of the first branch of the lateral plantar nerve.* **They found** good to excellent results in 89% of patients with minimal complications (persistent heel pain requiring additional surgery, neuroma, superficial site infection, reflex sympathetic dystrophy). **They concluded** that patients with entrapment of the first branch of the lateral plantar nerve should first be treated with conservative measures and that recalcitrant cases may be treated surgically with excellent expected outcomes.

Incorrect answers:
Answers 1, 3, 4, and 5: Baxter’s nerve is a branch of the lateral plantar nerve that runs deep to the abductor hallucis and flexor digitorum brevis, superficial to the quadratus plantae, and medial to the calcaneus.
