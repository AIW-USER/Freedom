## Question

A 6-year-old female patient with short stature is undergoing growth modulation for genu valgum. For which of the following conditions is an MRI of the entire spine recommended as part of the preoperative work-up?

## Options

1. Hurler syndrome
2. Kniest dysplasia
3. Psuedohypoparathryoidism
4. Schmid dysplasia
5. Turners syndrome

## Correct option

**1. Hurler syndrome**

## Answer explanation

Patients with Hurler syndrome are at risk for odontoid hypoplasia and atlantoaxial instability. Therefore, they should undergo a spine MRI before operative intervention (Answer 1).

Hurler syndrome, also known as mucopolysaccharidosis type I, is an autosomal recessive inherited disorder characterized by a deficiency in alpha-L-iduronidase. The prognosis of Hurler syndrome is quite poor, but lifespan can be extended with hematopoietic stem cell transplantation. It has many musculoskeletal manifestations, including carpal tunnel syndrome, genu valgum, severe hip dysplasia, acute thoracolumbar kyphosis (gibbus deformity), and finally, severe global spinal stenosis. Before operative intervention, patients with Hurler syndrome should undergo spinal cord evaluation.

*White et al. aimed to establish the best practices in perioperative management of patients with skeletal dysplasia.* Using a Delphi process, **they established** 19 recommendations. These include a comprehensive neurological examination, full spinal MRI, and flexion-extension imaging as part of the perioperative workup.

*Jackson et al. conducted a retrospective review of patients with spinal anomalies who underwent neuromonitoring for non-spine cases.* *Over a nine-year period, 102 patients underwent non-spine procedures with intraoperative neuromonitoring.* There were no cases of permanent neurological deficits in these patients. **The authors conclude** that intraoperative neuromonitoring should be employed in patients with spinal anomalies undergoing nonspine procedures.

*White et al. published a review article regarding the mucopolysaccharide disorders.* **They highlight** that these patients often have a combination of C1 ring hypoplasia, dural thickening, glycosaminoglycan accumulation, and atlantoaxial instability, placing them at risk for severe cervical cord injury.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2-5: These conditions are all associated with short stature but do not commonly present with spine abnormalities.
