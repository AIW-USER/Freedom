## Question

You are starting a new research project at your large academic medical center where you want to compare the treatment of non-displaced femoral neck fractures with either hemiarthroplasty or percutaneous screw fixation. While designing the study you decide to use computer randomization, single-surgeon, and blinded follow-up assessments where outcome scores are obtained by someone who doesn't know what surgery was performed. What is the reasoning for computer randomization?

## Options

1. To prevent data analysis from being skewed by your personal opinion
2. To allow for automated follow up surveys to be sent
3. To increase heterogeneity of patients in the study
4. To improve data collection and analysis of the involved subjects
5. To keep patient placement into each study group unbiased

## Correct option

**5. To keep patient placement into each study group unbiased**

## Answer explanation

Computer randomization is used to prevent selection bias, which is described in answer choice 5.

Research study design and implementation can be a complicated process that can be biased at various points throughout. Bias in orthopedic research can change the final outcome of the study and can be from patient selection, treatment choice, acquiring and analyzing data. When designing a new study, it is important to plan measures to prevent bias within your study.

*Spindler et al. provided a guide to understand the basics of orthopedic literature.* Within their article, there is a description of study designs, levels of evidence, and various types of biases. Furthermore, they expand on statistics and the interpretation of data. **They conclude** with their recommended approach to literary interpretation.

*Poolman et al. reviewed 32 randomized control trials (RCT) that were published in JBJS American between 2003 and 2004.* **They found** that only 3.4% of published articles in that time period were RCTs. When comparing blinded versus unblinded RCTs **they found** that the reported outcome score treatment effect was larger in unblinded studies versus blinded studies. **They concluded** that unblinded studies may have exaggerated benefit effectiveness.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: This describes confirmation bias, which is when data interpretation is biased to confirm the ideas or opinions the researchers hold.
Answer 2 & 4: Computers may help with automated surveys being sent and improve data collection and data analysis, however, computer randomization specifically prevents selection bias.
Answer 3: This describes sampling bias, which is when only a homogenous population is included in your study, which limits the generalizability of your results.
