## Question

Figure A shows the radiographs of a 35-year-old male with a history of hypertension presents to the emergency department after a high-energy motor vehicle collision. He has significant ankle pain, but no open wounds. He is a single man who never graduated high school. After ten days, he is definitively treated with open reduction and internal fixation. Which of the following patient factors has been shown to be associated with inferior outcomes?

![[q217813-a-pilon-fracture-xray.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Closed injury
2. History of hypertension
3. Marital status
4. Education level
5. Open reduction and internal fixation as definitive treatment

## Correct option

**4. Education level**

## Answer explanation

Tibial plafond (pilon) fractures are devastating injuries that have long-term functional implications for patients. Those with two or more co-morbidities, married patients, lower income/education, and definitive treatment with an external fixator are associated with inferior outcomes.

Pilon injuries are high-energy injuries of the distal end of the tibia. High-speed MVCs and falls from significant heights are possible mechanisms. They are frequently associated with comminution/impaction, intra-articular extension, and significant soft tissue injury. Radiographs and CT are usually obtained to evaluate the fracture. On CT scan, you can see common fragments including Chaput's, Volkmann's, and medial malleolus fragments. Temporary external fixation is common to allow the soft tissues to heal prior to definitive open reduction and internal fixation. Other potential treatments include Ilizarov frame, ankle fusion, and hindfoot nailing/fusion.

*Pollak et al. retrospectively reviewed the outcomes of 80 patients who sustained a tibial plafond fracture.* Complication rates were high with 35% having stiffness, 29% having persistent swelling, and 33% having ongoing pain. Of the employed patients, only 47% returned to work. SF-36 scores were significantly poorer than age and gender-matched norms. Two or more comorbidities, being married, lower income, not having attained a high-school diploma, and having been treated with external fixation were associated with inferior outcomes.

*Kottmeier et al. wrote a review on preventing complications after tibial plafond fractures.* **They state** that respect for soft tissue injury is paramount, but still may not prevent many complications. They discuss additional techniques such as early fixation, upgrading, primary arthrodesis, staged fixation, acute shortening, and trans-syndesmotic fibular plating as potential solutions to minimize these complications.

Figure A demonstrates an AP, lateral, and oblique radiograph of a comminuted intra-articular tibial plafond fracture with valgus angulation.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1, 2, 3, and 5: Two or more comorbidities, being married, lower income, not having attained a high-school diploma, and definitive treatment with external fixation was associated with inferior outcomes. Open injuries typically do worse than closed injuries.
