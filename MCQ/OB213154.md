## Question

A 40-year-old male presents to the ED with significant swelling and bruising about the right anterior elbow. He reports that he was attempting to achieve a personal record lift at the gym when he felt a pop in his right arm. On exam he has ecchymosis over the anterior elbow with diffuse tenderness to palpation. A positive hook test is also appreciated. Radiographs are normal. Which of the following best describes the type of muscle contraction that likely led to this injury?

## Options

1. Muscle remains the same length with contraction
2. Muscle shortens during contraction
3. Muscle lengthens during contraction
4. Muscle contract with constant speed
5. Rapid lengthening followed by contraction of the muscle group

## Correct option

**3. Muscle lengthens during contraction**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a ruptured distal biceps tendon. Eccentric contractions put the most stress on the muscle and are most closely linked to tendon injuries.

Distal biceps ruptures most commonly occur following a forced eccentric load. This type of contraction is one in which the muscle lengthens during the contraction such as would occur during the "negative" of a lift. Muscle contractions are described most commonly based on what happens to the muscle during the contraction. Concentric contractions for instance are those in which the muscle shortens during the contractile phase as would occur while lifting the weight during a bicep curl. Isotonic contractions are those in which force remains constant for the entire range of motion.

*Tjoumakaris et al. review distal biceps injuries.* **They report** that these injuries most commonly in males following forced eccentric contraction of the biceps. **They conclude** that most patients who sustain distal biceps ruptures should undergo operative repair to minimize strength and functional loss.

*Kraemer et al. review progression models in resistance training for healthy adults.* **They report** that the optimal characteristics of strength-specific programs include the use of both concentric and eccentric muscle actions and the performance of both single- and multiple-joint exercises. **They conclude** that strength programs should sequence exercises to optimize the quality of the exercise intensity (large before small muscle group exercises, multiple-joint exercises before single-joint exercises, and higher intensity before lower intensity exercises).

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Isometric contractions are those in which the muscle remains the same length during contraction.
Answer 2: During concentric contractions the muscle shortens during contraction.
Answer 4: Isokinetic motions are those in which the muscle contracts at a constant speed.
Answer 5: Plyometric exercises involve rapid lengthening followed by contraction of muscle groups.
