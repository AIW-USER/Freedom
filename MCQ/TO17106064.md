Chapter: Arthroplasty MCQs — Post TKR Infection

## Question

True regarding prosthetic joint infection caused by Propionibacterium acnes is

## Options

1. P acnes is a gram-negative bacterium
2. P acnes has slow onset of symptoms
3. P acnes takes 48 hours to grow on culture media
4. P acnes infection is associated with high WBC count

## Correct option

**2. P acnes has slow onset of symptoms**

## Answer explanation

Propionibacterium spp. is an anaerobic Gram-positive rod-shaped bacterium (Anaerobic diphtheroid), which is commonly found in the pilosebaceous follicles of the human skin, oral cavity, conjunctiva, respiratory and intestinal tract and external ear canal. Although it is often considered not pathogenic, Propionibacterium spp. can be responsible for severe infections including endocarditis, meningitis and brain abscess, endophthalmitis, conjunctivitis, osteomyelitis or spondylodiscitis and prosthetic joint infection.

PJI are classified as early (<3 months after surgery), delayed (3–24 months after surgery) and late (>24 months after surgery). Most Propionibacterium spp. infections are diagnosed as delayed or late PJI with consistent pain as most common complaint followed by radiological loosening and persistent sinus tract.

Pathology: In their planktonic form, Propionibacterium spp. is sensible to the host’s immune response and antibiotic treatment. But in contact with implants such as a prosthetic joint they will immediately form an extracellular matrix, the so-called biofilm. Well protected underneath this sheath of biofilm they won’t be reached by the immune system and most antibiotics (except for rifampicin) are not able to penetrate the biofilm.

Investigations: Slow growth rate and low virulence delay presentation of specific signs of infection. First line investigation is represented by serologic tests, such as measurement of white blood cell count and CRP. They generally have good sensitivity and lower specificity. In presence of PJI with Propionibacterium spp, both parameters are often not or little elevated and therefore not helpful in confirming or rejecting diagnosis of infection. CRP may be elevated in case of coexistent infection with other bacteria (mostly coagulase negative staph spp.)

Propionibacterium, as a facultative anaerobic rod is difficult to culture and identify, needing anaerobic culture media and incubation periods of 7–15 days and even intraoperative cultures, although considered as the gold standard, may be negative for some patients with clinical proven prosthetic infection. Sonication of the removed implants and molecular diagnostics can improve the rate of accurate diagnosis.

Answer: 2
