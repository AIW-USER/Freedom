## Question

A 69-year-old female presents for evaluation secondary to pain at multiple sites throughout the body. Radiographs of the upper and lower extremities demonstrate multiple lytic lesions. There is concern for multiple myeloma. The patient has a serum protein electrophoresis and a bone marrow aspiration performed. Of the values provided, which of the following is the minimum amount of plasmacytosis that would indicate a positive bone marrow aspiration for making the diagnosis of multiple myeloma in this patient?

## Options

1. 5%
2. 10%
3. 35%
4. 50%
5. 75%

## Correct option

**3. 35%**

## Answer explanation

This patient is believed to have multiple myeloma. The percentage of plasma cells on bone marrow aspirate is one major criteria used to distinguish plasmacytoma (10-30% plasma cells) vs. multiple myeloma (>30% plasma cells).

Multiple myeloma is a neoplastic proliferation of plasma cells that commonly results in multiple skeletal lesions, hypercalcemia, renal insufficiency, and anemia. Patients typically present at ages >40 with localized bone pain or a pathologic fracture. Diagnosis is made with a bone marrow biopsy showing monoclonal plasma cells ≥30% and/or serum protein electrophoresis which may be positive for beta2-microglobulin. Treatment is usually chemotherapy and radiation. Surgical management is indicated for associated fractures.

*Brigle et al. reviewed the role of the genetic changes and bone marrow microenvironment on the development, progression, and staging of multiple myeloma.* **They reported** that the acquisition of genetic changes and the bone marrow microenvironment in which myeloma cells develop both influence the pathogenic potential of these malignant cells and is reflected in staging of the disease, risk of progression, and predicted response to treatment. **They concluded** that treatment of multiple myeloma is largely dependent on risk factors in which specific genetic alterations play a large role.

*Dimopoulos et al. reviewed imaging in multiple myeloma.* **They reported** that among all imaging modalities, MRI provides the most useful information about the accurate staging of solitary bone plasmacytoma, the prediction of progression of asymptomatic multiple myeloma and the prognosis of symptomatic multiple myeloma. **They concluded** that MRI contributes to the differential diagnosis of compression fractures in patients with myeloma and can be used for assessment of response to treatment.

*Mulligan et al. reviewed the importance of imaging in the diagnosis, initial staging, follow-up, and restaging of patients with multiple myeloma.* **They reported** that the staging system for myeloma patients has been updated and now includes findings from advanced imaging modalities. **They concluded** that currently, conventional radiographic skeletal surveys, MRI, and F-18 FDG PET/CT examinations are the most useful instruments.
