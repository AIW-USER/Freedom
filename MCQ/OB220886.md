## Question

A 28-year-old male presents with a complete Achilles tendon rupture sustained while playing basketball. He underwent surgical repair 7 days ago. During the initial postoperative visit, you review the principles of tendon healing with him. Which of the following cellular events is most characteristic of the inflammatory phase of tendon healing in the first week following injury?

## Options

1. A predominance of M2 macrophages, promoting tissue remodeling
2. An increase in type I collagen deposition and neovascularization
3. A transition from fibroblasts to fibrocytes for scar formation
4. High levels of neutrophil and M1 macrophage infiltration
5. Widespread resolution of the initial hematoma

## Correct option

**4. High levels of neutrophil and M1 macrophage infiltration**

## Answer explanation

The initial inflammatory phase of tendon healing is characterized by the recruitment of neutrophils and M1 macrophages to clear debris and promote the initial repair process (answer choice 4).

The first phase of tendon healing is the inflammatory phase, which begins immediately after injury and lasts for approximately one week. This phase is crucial for preparing the injury site for subsequent repair and remodeling. The process is initiated by the formation of a hematoma, followed by the recruitment of inflammatory cells. Neutrophils and M1 macrophages are the key cellular players in this early phase, working to remove necrotic tissue and debris. This M1-dominant environment promotes inflammation and the initial scaffold for repair. As the healing progresses, a shift to a more M2-dominant state occurs, which then facilitates the later proliferative and remodeling phases. M1 macrophages are pro-inflammatory cells that initiate the healing process, while M2 macrophages are anti-inflammatory cells that promote tissue repair and remodeling.

*Leong et al. provided a review of the mechanisms of tendon and ligament healing and* **highlighted** contemporary approaches to regeneration. **They noted** the critical role of the initial inflammatory phase and the subsequent transition of macrophage phenotypes in orchestrating the healing cascade. **They concluded** that a detailed understanding of these biological processes is essential for developing effective regenerative therapies.

*Thomopoulos et al. evaluated the basic mechanisms underlying tendon injury and repair.* **They noted** that the healing process involves three overlapping phases—inflammatory, proliferative, and remodeling—each with distinct cellular and molecular events. **They concluded** that the coordinated action of various cell types, including macrophages, is crucial for successful tendon healing.

*Sharma and Maffulli reviewed the biological and mechanical aspects of tendon injury and repair.* **They noted** that the healing process, from the initial inflammatory response to final remodeling, is a complex biological cascade. **They concluded** that the principles of healing must be understood to guide appropriate clinical management.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: This is characteristic of the proliferative and remodeling phases, not the initial inflammatory phase.
Answer 2: This describes the proliferative phase of healing, which follows the inflammatory phase.
Answer 3: This is a part of the proliferative and remodeling phases, as fibroblasts are the key cells for synthesizing new collagen.
Answer 5: Resolution of the hematoma occurs as the inflammatory phase concludes and is a precursor to the subsequent phases, not the primary event within the first week.
