## Options

1. Immobilization in long leg cast for 4 weeks
2. Hamstring strengthening exercises
3. Quadricep strengthening exercices
4. Acute double-bundle PCL reconstruction
5. Delayed double-bundle PCL and PLC reconstruction

## Correct option

**3. Quadricep strengthening exercices**

## Answer explanation

This patient has sustained an isolated posterior cruciate ligament (PCL) injury, without a posterolateral corner (PLC) injury, as exhibited by the negative Dial's test on examination. Initial treatment for PCL injuries is non-operative, with a focus on quadriceps strengthening.

PCL injuries are rare in isolation and more commonly occur in the multi-ligament injured knee. The most common mechanism of PCL injury is a direct blow to the proximal aspect of the tibia, occurring in athletes during a fall onto the flexed knee with the foot in plantarflexion, which places a posterior force on the tibia and leads to PCL rupture. The initial treatment of a PCL injury is non-operative, with early range of motion initiated. While quadriceps strengthening is essential, resisted hamstring exercises are generally avoided initially because they pull the tibia in a relatively posterior position. Operative intervention for PCL injuries are indicated in combined ligamentous injuries or in chronic cases with recurrent posterior instability.

*Shelbourne et al. investigated the natural history of acute, isolated, non-operatively treated PCL injuries.* **They observed** no correlation between radiographic joint space narrowing and grade of laxity, subjective knee scores and time from injury, and that 50% of patients returned to the same sport at the same or higher level. **They concluded** that active patients with acute isolated PCL injuries treated non-operatively achieved a level of objective and subjective knee function that was independent of the grade of laxity.

*McAllister et al. reviewed the diagnosis and treatment of acute PCL injuries.* **They report** that PCL injuries may manifest by a spectrum of symptoms and disability, and that the factors that predict which patients with isolated PCL injuries will develop degenerative changes remain understudied. **They concluded** that the ideal treatment algorithm for these injuries remain a matter of debate.

Figure A demonstrates normal knee plain radiographs. Figure B is an MRI depicting an isolated PCL injury. Figure C demonstrates an examiner providing the Dial test.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Immobilization in a long leg cast for 4 weeks would result is unnecessary and would result in stiffness.
Answer 2: Hamstring exercises are generally avoided initially because they pull the tibia in a relatively posterior position.
Answer 4: Initial nonsurgical treatment is indicated for this patient. If he exhibits persistent instability, PCL reconstruction may be considered.
