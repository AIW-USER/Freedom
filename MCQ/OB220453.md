## Answer explanation

The patient has MRI evidence of spontaneous osteonecrosis of the knee (SONK), which is most commonly associated with a subchondral fracture of the medial femoral condyle and a tear of the posterior horn of the medial meniscus (Answer 2, Injury C).

Spontaneous osteonecrosis of the knee (SONK) is an idiopathic condition that leads to the development of a crescent-shaped osteonecrosis lesion, most commonly in the epiphysis of the medial femoral condyle. The pathology most commonly occurs in middle-aged females (>55 years old) and involves the sudden onset of severe knee pain and effusion, with 99% of patients having only one joint involved. The pathophysiological cascade typically represents a subchondral insufficiency fracture of the medial femoral condyle with osteonecrosis and flattening, followed by a degenerative posterior horn medial meniscus tear. It is essential to differentiate this from osteochondritis dissecans, which more commonly occurs on the lateral aspect of the medial femoral condyle in adolescent males, and transient osteoporosis, which is more common in young to middle-aged men. Treatment is generally non-operative and symptomatic, as most cases are self-limiting, with surgical management involving arthroplasty reserved for progressive cases that fail conservative management.

*Bhatia et al. reviewed the significance, diagnosis, and treatment of meniscal root tears.* **The authors note** that the meniscus root attachment aids meniscal function by securing the meniscus in place and allowing for optimal shock-absorbing function in the knee. When lost, this attachment impairs the transmission of circumferential hoop stresses and leads to meniscal extrusion. **They conclude** that this pathophysiologic process alters knee biomechanics and kinematics and significantly increases tibiofemoral contact pressure. This leads to a condition biomechanically similar to a total meniscectomy, thereby highlighting the importance of early diagnosis and treatment.

*Serrano et al. published a review of spontaneous osteonecrosis of the knee.* **The authors note** that there are, in general, three types of osteonecrosis that can affect the knee are secondary, post-arthroscopic, and spontaneous osteonecrosis of the knee (SONK). **They conclude** that the non-operative options for treatment at the moment include observation, nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory medications (NSAIDs), protective weight-bearing, and analgesia if needed, with joint arthroplasty being the best course of action whenever there is significant joint surface collapse or there are signs of degenerative arthritis.

Figure A is PD fat-saturated coronal MRI sequence of a knee showing flattening of the articular surface of the medial femoral condyle with volume loss and marrow edema consistent with SONK. Figure B, representing injury B, is a T2-weighted sagittal MRI showing a complete tear of the posterior cruciate ligament (PCL). Figure C, representing Injury C, is a T2-weighted sagittal MRI sequence showing a large horizontal tear of the posterior horn of the medial meniscus. Figure D, representing Injury D, is a PD fat-saturated sagittal MRI sequence showing complex horizontal and radial tears of the lateral meniscus. Figure E, representing Injury E, is an axial PD fat-saturated MRI sequence showing surface fissuring and fraying of the medial patellar cartilage, as well as mild cartilage loss of the femoral trochlea consistent with patellofemoral arthritis. Figure F, representing Injury F, is an AP radiograph of the knee showing a non-displaced avulsion fracture of the lateral femoral epicondyle at the expected location of the proximal attachment of the lateral collateral ligament.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Injury B represents a complete tear of the PCL, which would be unexpected to occur concurrently in the setting of SONK.
Answer 3: Injury D represents a complex lateral meniscus tear, which is less likely to occur than a medial meniscal tear in the setting of SONK, given the predilection for the medial compartment and the pathophysiologic cascade that starts with a subchondral fracture of the medial femoral condyle.
Answer 4: Injury E represents patellofemoral arthritis, which is not likely to result from the rapid onset of medial compartment wear seen in SONK.
Answer 5: Injury F shows an avulsion of the attachment site of the LCL, which, again, is unlikely to occur in the setting of SONK.
