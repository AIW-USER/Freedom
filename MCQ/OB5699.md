## Question

Figure A is a radiograph of 5-year-old boy with an acute left elbow injury. On physical exam, he was found to have absent radial and ulnar pulses and capillary refill greater than 5 seconds. Initial radiographs are shown in Figure A. Immediately after anatomic closed reduction and pinning, the hand is cool and pale. What is the next best step?

![[q5699-a-figure-1.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Surgical exploration of the antecubital fossa.
2. Remove all pins and stabilize fracture with external fixation
3. Continue to observe for neurovascular status improvements overnight
4. Remove fixation and perform repeat closed reduction and percutaneous pinning
5. Proceed with emergent fasciotomy

## Correct option

**1. Surgical exploration of the antecubital fossa.**

## Answer explanation

The patient has a pulseless and underperfused limb despite reduction and pin fixation after sustaining a displaced supracondylar humerus fracture. In this situation, surgical exploration of the antecubital fossa is indicated.

Vascular injury after a supracondylar humerus fracture can result in a Volkmann’s ischemic contracture if ischemia is not resolved. Vascular compromise is indicated by lack of pulses, absent dopplerable signals, cool hand, pale limb, and delayed capillary refill. For a pulseless, underperfused limb after closed reduction and percutaneous fixation, treatment is immediate exploration. The vascular injury or entrapment is often at the site of the fracture and therefore angiography has been found to be an unnecessary delay in resolution of compromise.

*Garbuz et al. retrospectively reviewed 326 pediatric supracondylar humerus fractures with 22 without pulses on admission.* *Sixteen of the 22 patients had well perfused pulses before closed reduction and percutaneous pinning, and afterwards most had pulses return.* *The remaining 6 patients presented with a cold pulseless hand.* *After closed reduction, only 2 patients had persistent underperfused, pulseless hands and underwent surgical exploration.* **The authors stated** that an absent pulse is not an absolute indication for exploration of the brachial artery, however, an under perfused hand after closed reduction and percutaneous pinning does warrant exploration.

*Howard et al.* **stated** that a patient with an under perfused hand and absent pulses after closed reduction and percutaneous pinning should undergo immediate exploration of the antecubital fossa. Despite the lack of level I evidence, the consensus was that the benefit of immediate exploration outweighs the “catastrophic risks of persistent inadequate perfusion.”

Incorrect Answers
2 and 4: The vascular compromise is unlikely to be secondary to the fixation as it was present before surgery. Thus changing fixation would not be of benefit.
3: The vascular compromise is an emergency.
5: Fasciotomy is not required as the patient has not developed compartment syndrome. Prophylactic fasciotomy is not needed if the vascular compromise is addressed in a timely manner.
