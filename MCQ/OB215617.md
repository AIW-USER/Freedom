## Correct option

**2. Greater prevalence in females compared to males**

## Answer explanation

This patient has Freiberg's disease/infraction. This condition is far more common in females (~ 4:1).

Freiberg's disease is a rare foot condition characterized by infraction and fracture of the metatarsal head. The second metatarsal is most commonly involved, followed by the third metatarsal. The lateral metatarsals and the first metatarsal are rarely affected. Diagnosis is made radiographically with plain radiographs showing subchondral sclerosis, flattening of the involved metatarsal head and eventual joint destruction in advanced disease. Magnetic resonance imaging studies may be needed to detect early disease. Initial management includes activity modification and non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs. Surgical management is indicated for progressive pain, joint destruction and joint deformity.

*DiGiovanni et al. reviewed osteonecrosis of the foot.* **They report** that Freiberg's disease commonly occurs on the dorsal and anterior aspects of the metatarsal head and that collapse is caused by microfracture and necrosis of the subchondral bone. **They concluded** that nonsurgical management, including activity modification, limited weight bearing with crutches, orthosis use, and shoe modifications that off-load the affected metatarsal head and limit affected metatarsophalangeal joint motion are effective in the early stages of the disease.

*Sproul et al. retrospectively reviewed 11 cases of Freiberg's infraction treated surgically.* **They report** that surgery consisted of metatarsophalangeal joint debridement, except in one patient where the metatarsal head was resected. **They concluded** that all patients had improvement of their symptoms and 80% of normal joint range of motion was restored, with no patient having joint space narrowing or major arthritic changes on follow-up roentgenographic studies.

Figures A and B are the frontal and oblique radiographs of the right foot demonstrating subchondral lucency with sclerosis and flattening of the second metatarsal head seen, consistent with Freiberg's disease/infraction.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1: Concurrent first metatarsal involvement is uncommon
Answer 2: Initial management should include nonoperative treatment such as a short leg walking cast or boot for 4-6 weeks or stiff-soled shoe with metatarsal bars or pads typically used after period of casting
Answers 4 and 5: The second metatarsal is most commonly involved, followed by the third metatarsal. The lateral metatarsals follow behind. The first metatarsal is not typically affected
