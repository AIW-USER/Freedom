## Question

A 29-year-old male sustains an acetabular fracture after a motorcycle crash. This is an isolated orthopedic injury and is classified as an anterior column acetabular fracture. Based on standard imaging, which of the following would be an expected radiographic finding with this fracture pattern?

## Options

1. Disrupted iliopectineal line best visualized on iliac oblique view
2. Ilioischial line disruption on iliac oblique view
3. Fracture of the iliopectineal line on the obturator oblique view with extension into the sciatic notch on the iliac oblique view
4. Fracture through the iliopectineal line with undisturbed posterior acetabulum on obturator oblique view
5. Spur sign seen on obturator oblique view

## Correct option

**4. Fracture through the iliopectineal line with undisturbed posterior acetabulum on obturator oblique view**

## Answer explanation

Anterior column fractures have disruption of the iliopectineal line on the obturator oblique Judet view with no fractures of the posterior wall (answer choice 4).

Acetabular fractures are complex pelvis fractures of the articular surface with a variety of presentations. In 1964, Judet first set out to devise a classification system for these complex fractures that has since been refined and revised. Today, the Judet and Letournel classification system is most commonly used and divides fractures into 10 types and two subgroups, elementary and associated (Illustration A). Although the radiographic pattern within each fracture varies, the features remain the same. Anterior column fractures are a small subset of acetabular fractures (3-5%) that feature a separation of the anterior border of the innominate bone from the intact ilium. Variation can exist in regards to the height the fracture exits from the anterior aspect of the ilium. Like all acetabular fractures, a standard AP pelvis radiograph and Judet radiographs should be performed. Judet radiographs are 45° internal and external rotation views of the pelvis that allow for enhanced visualization of bony landmarks. An obturator oblique (OO) view is taken with the hemipelvis of interest rotated 45° towards the x-ray beam, providing clearer visualization of the posterior wall and anterior column (Illustration B). The iliac oblique (IO) view is taken with the hemipelvis of interest tilted 45° away from the x-ray beam for better visualization of the anterior wall and posterior column (Illustration C). A diagnosis of acetabular fracture patterns can be made with standard radiographs, but CT imaging has proven to be instrumental in further classification of these fractures and surgical planning.

*Letournel et al. provide a 1980 review of acetabular fracture types and surgical approaches.* *The authors review the 10 acetabular fractures and the common radiographic features expected on AP and Judet films.* *The authors continue to describe approaches and fixation strategies recommended for the various acetabular types.*

Beaulé et al., in their 2003 study, sought to evaluate the reliability of the Letournel classification system. The authors took orthopedic surgeons of various familiarity with acetabular surgery and assessed their ability to correctly classify fracture patterns with and without CT. The authors found significant intraobserver and interobserver reliability (Kappa > 0.70) of the classification system with and without CT. The authors concluded that an appropriate diagnosis of acetabular fractures can be made by radiographs alone.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer choice 1: Although disruption of the iliopectineal line is a feature of anterior column fractures, this is best viewed on the obturator oblique view.
Answer choice 2: This can describe multiple different fracture patterns that include disruption of the posterior column. However, an anterior column fracture will not have disruption of the posterior column.
Answer choice 3: This is describing a transverse fracture pattern that will feature disruption of iliopectineal and ilioischial lines on pelvic radiographs.
Answer choice 5: A spur sign, which is viewed on the obturator oblique, is a projection of the caudal portion of the intact ilium. This is a pathognomonic finding for associated both column fractures and would not be seen in an isolated anterior column fracture.
