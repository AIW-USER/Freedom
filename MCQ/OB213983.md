## Question

A 28-year-old male triathlete presents to your office for evaluation of numbness and weakness in his right hand, which began 5 weeks ago following a charity race. On exam, he has paresthesias to his volar small finger and ulnar half of his ring finger, difficulty crossing his index and middle fingers, and a weakened grip. Dorsal hand sensation is normal, as is the sensation to the volar aspect of the forearm. Froment’s sign is present. An EMG is most likely to demonstrate fibrillations in which of the following muscles?

## Options

1. Adductor pollicis
2. Flexor carpi ulnaris (FCU)
3. Extensor carpi ulnaris (ECU)
4. Flexor pollicis longus (FPL)
5. Flexor digitorum profundus (FDP) to small finger

## Correct option

**1. Adductor pollicis**

## Answer explanation

The patient has signs consistent with ulnar nerve dysfunction. Sensation to the dorsum of the hand is intact, however, which localizes the lesion to Guyon’s canal. Of the options listed, only the adductor pollicis receives its innervation distal to Guyon's canal.

Ulnar tunnel syndrome is commonly seen in cyclists from direct compression of the ulnar nerve in Guyon’s canal (handlebar palsy). Symptoms can be similar to that of cubital tunnel syndrome including decreased sensation to the small fingers and ulnar half of the ring finger. Additionally, patients may demonstrate weakness in the hand as the deep branch of the ulnar nerve innervates the hypothenar muscles, the interosseous muscles, the third and fourth lumbricals, the adductor pollicis and the deep head of the flexor pollicis brevis. The dorsal ulnar cutaneous nerve provides sensation to the dorsal ulnar aspect of the hand and branches 5cm proximal to Guyon’s canal. For this reason, ulnar tunnel syndrome can be differentiated from cubital tunnel syndrome by assessing dorsal hand sensation.

*Lee et al. review clinical nerve conduction and needle electromyography studies.* **They report** that peripheral nerve entrapment syndromes result from focal demyelination, which slows conduction velocities across that site. Ultimately, **they conclude** that the interpretation of these results requires a thorough understanding of the anatomic structures and entrapment sites.

*Strohl et al. review the peripheral nerve entrapment syndromes including ulnar tunnel syndrome.* **They report** that the ulnar nerve may be compressed as it travels through Guyon’s canal, which is referred to as ulnar tunnel syndrome. **They conclude** that electrodiagnostic studies may aid surgeons in making the correct diagnosis, however, a thorough physical exam is paramount to localizing the correct site of entrapment.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 2: While the FCU is innervated by the ulnar nerve, it receives its innervation proximal to Guyon's canal and would not be impacted.
Answer 3: ECU is innervated by the posterior interosseous nerve.
Answer 4: FPL is innervated by the anterior interosseous nerve.
Answer 5: While the FDP to the small finger is innervated by the ulnar nerve, it receives its innervation proximal to Guyon's canal and would not be impacted.
