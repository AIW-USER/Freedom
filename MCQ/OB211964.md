## Question

A 17-year-old high-school football player presents to your clinic for evaluation of right hip pain. He has had off-and-on pain for over 4 years mainly during football season. He is a linebacker who weighs 200 pounds (BMI 32). The pain is deep in the groin and worsens with hip flexion. His hip x-rays are noted in Figure A. Subsequent magnetic resonance arthrogram (MRA) of his right hip shows a posterior labral tear. Which of the following factors in this patient will most likely to lead to poor outcomes after arthroscopic right hip surgery?

![[q211964-a-right-hip-ap-pelvis.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Age 17
2. Football player
3. BMI of 32
4. Acetabulum subchondral cyst
5. Posterior labral tear

## Correct option

**4. Acetabulum subchondral cyst**

## Answer explanation

The presence of an acetabulum subchondral cyst would be indicative of hip osteoarthritis (at least Tonnis grade 2) and would be the worst prognostic factor of this patient after arthroscopy right hip surgery.

Hip arthroscopy for management of femoroacetabular impingement (FAI) and labral pathology has gained increasing support over the last 10-20 years. Professional and recreational athletes have noted improvement in symptoms, and function and have been able to return to sport at an excellent (>90%) rate following surgery. However, a subset of patients with evidence of early arthritis have been noted to have inferior results after hip arthroscopy. These patients have also been noted to be at increased risk of earlier conversion to a total hip arthroplasty (THA). As such, patients with evidence of hip osteoarthritis and FAI should be counseled on the possible limited benefit of hip arthroscopy for the management of their FAI.

*Ng et al. performed a systematic review of the surgical management of FAI to determine if a certain subset of patients benefit most from hip arthroscopy and if treatment alters the natural progression of hip osteoarthritis.* *The authors reviewed 970 cases in 23 studies and noted that all studies demonstrated improvement in patient symptoms.* **They noted** 30% of patients required THA and that those patients with preoperative radiographs showing greater than Tonnis grade I osteoarthritis will have worse outcomes with treatment for FAI. **The authors concluded** that surgical treatment for FAI reliably improves patient symptoms in the majority of patients without advanced osteoarthritis or chondral damage and it is too soon to predict whether the progression of osteoarthritis is delayed.

*Philippon et al. performed a review of FAI to define associated pathologies and determine if an arthroscopic approach to treating FAI can allow professional athletes to return to high-level sport.* *They reviewed 45 professional athletes who underwent hip arthroscopy for the decompression of FAI.* **They noted** an average time to follow-up of 1.6 years with forty-two (93%) athletes returning to professional competition following arthroscopic decompression of FAI. Three athletes did not return to play; however, all had diffuse osteoarthritis at the time of arthroscopy. **The authors concluded** that arthroscopic treatment of FAI allows professional athletes to return to professional sport.

*Byrd and Jones performed a prospective study of the functional outcomes of 200 athletes who underwent hip arthroscopy for the management of FAI.* **The authors noted** a 100% followup rate at an average of 19 months. There were 23 professionals, 56 intercollegiate, 24 high school, and 97 recreational athletes in their cohort. **They noted** that 95% percent of professional athletes and 85% of intercollegiate athletes were able to return to their previous level of competition. There were 5 transient neurapraxias (all resolved) and 1 minor heterotopic ossification. **The authors concluded** that the treatment of symptomatic FAI with hip arthroscopy is very successful with few complications and most athletes were able to resume activities.

*Fabricant et al. performed a retrospective review of 27 hips in 21 patients who were 19 years or younger that underwent arthroscopic treatment for FAI.* **The authors noted** that the modified harris hip score (HHS) improved by an average of 21 points, and the sports outcome subset of the hip outcome score (HOS) improved by an average of 32 points. **The authors concluded** that hip arthroscopy in patients younger than 19 led to short-term improvements in HOS and HHS with no complications for arthroscopic treatment of FAI.

Figure A is the AP of the right hip showing mild degenerative changes with lateral joint line sclerosis as well as a right acetabulum subchondral cyst. Illustration A notes the subchondral cyst in Figure A.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Studies have shown that adolescents undergoing hip arthroscopy have shown good short-term improvements without complications following treatment of FAI or labral pathology.
Answer 2: Progressional, collegiate, and high school football players have all shown excellent outcomes following treatment of FAI or labral pathology.
Answer 3: While there are some studies that have shown BMI > 30 to be associated with decreased symptomatic improvement after hip arthroscopy, hip osteoarthritis is still a much worse prognostic indicator.
Answer 5: Posterior labral tears have not been shown to be a negative prognostic indicator for arthroscopic hip surgery.
