## Question

Articular cartilage is unique in its ability to withstand high cyclic loads, demonstrating little or no evidence of damage or degenerative change. One reason for this ability is its viscoelastic nature. Which of the following best describes viscoelasticity?

## Options

1. The modulus of elasticity changes over time based on the duration and rate of an applied load
2. The material can undergo significant plastic deformation before failure
3. The direction of the applied load changes the materials’ mechanical property
4. The material undergoes elastic deformation until failure
5. The material exhibits equal mechanical properties independent of direction of applied forces

## Correct option

**1. The modulus of elasticity changes over time based on the duration and rate of an applied load**

## Answer explanation

Viscoelastic materials exhibit a stress-strain relationship dependent on the applied load's duration and rate (Answer 1).

Articular cartilage is a thin layer of specialized connective tissue with unique properties exhibiting elastic and viscous behavior when deformed (viscoelastic). Articular cartilage consists of 2 phases: fluid and solid phases. Due to this biphasic mechanism, mechanical tests have a characteristic appearance where the modulus appears to change over time in response. The viscoelasticity of articular cartilage is governed by two mechanisms: flow-dependent and flow-independent. Flow-dependent behavior arises from the frictional drag of interstitial fluid, referred to as biphasic viscoelastic behavior. Flow-independent behavior is due to the inherent movement within the collagen-proteoglycan matrix. The fluid pressure contributes significantly to load support, reducing the stress on the solid cartilage matrix.

*Fox et al. reviewed the basic science of articular cartilage with an emphasis on structure and composition.* *The authors systematically describe the zones, regions, and components of articular cartilage, including chondrocytes, extracellular matrix, collagen and water composition, proteoglycans, and finally, non-collagenous proteins and glycoproteins.* Additionally, the authors summarize the metabolism, biomechanical function, and aging process of articular cartilage. Importantly, **they highlight** the biphasic nature of cartilage and its viscoelastic property, which provides significant load support and reduces the stress on the matrix.

Flandry and Hommel review the normal anatomy and biomechanics of the knee. The authors emphasize the coordinated efforts of static ligaments, dynamic muscular forces, meniscocapsular aponeurosis, bony topography, and joint load to maintain joint stability. They conclude that surgeons must possess intricate knowledge of knee anatomy before performing any reconstruction attempt.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 2: This describes a ductile material
Answer 3: This describes anisotropic materials
Answer 4: This describes a brittle material that undergoes a linear stress-strain relationship until failure with little plastic deformation.
Answer 5: This describes an isotropic material
