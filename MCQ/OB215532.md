## Question

She reports activity-related pain in the left leg for several months prior to her fall. Figures A-C are her current radiographs. What is the most appropriate treatment for this patient?

## Options

1. Cylinder cast and non-weightbearing for 8 weeks
2. ORIF with strut grafting
3. ORIF with intramedullary nail
4. ORIF with revision total knee arthroplasty
5. Proximal tibial replacement

## Correct option

**4. ORIF with revision total knee arthroplasty**

## Answer explanation

*The patient has sustained a Felix and associates type IIIB (loose component) periprosthetic tibia fracture, which is best treated with combined revision arthroplasty and open reduction internal fixation.*

Periprosthetic tibial fractures are rare, but challenging injuries to treat and often occur in the perioperative setting or after acute injuries. Soft tissue complications are common with surgical treatment due to the tenuous tissue coverage of the tibia. Nonoperative treatment can yield good results with stable implants and good fracture alignment. Patients with loose implants and poor bone stock are amenable to surgical treatment with revision arthroplasty.

*Konan et al. reviewed the management of total knee arthroplasty periprosthetic fractures.* **The authors proposed** a revision arthroplasty in the setting of extensive bone loss, poor bone stock, and implant loosening. **They stated** revision arthroplasty is associated with worse outcomes and increased complications compared to primary arthroplasty procedures.

*Born et al. reviewed the diagnosis, classification, and management of periprosthetic tibial fractures.* **They recommended** internal fixation or nonoperative treatment consisting of casting for fractures with stable implants. Fractures with loose implants should be treated with revision arthroplasty.

Figures A and B are the AP and lateral radiographs of the left knee with a loose tibia component that has collapsed in varus with a fracture distal to the keel of the tibial component. Figure C is a lateral tibia radiograph demonstrating a fracture of the tibia distal to the keel of the prosthesis and no bone abnormalities distally.

Incorrect answer
Answer 1: Fractures with stable implants, good alignment, and minimal displacement can be treated with casting and non-weightbearing with good results and low complication rates.
Answers 2 and 3: Fractures with stable implants and good bone stock can be treated with internal fixation or with intramedullary nailing. The use of strut grafting is influenced by the need to reconstitute cortical bone with internal fixation.
Answer 5: A proximal tibial replacement is a salvage option for severe bone loss in both oncologic and non-oncologic reconstruction, but comes with significant morbidity, and is less ideal for the extensor mechanism.
