## Question

A design surgeon has created a new polyethylene acetabular liner for total hip arthroplasty (THA). Early clinical outcomes are comparable to conventional THA. At 4-year follow-up, radioisometric analysis reveals 0.03 mm of annual wear in the surgeon's trial patients. Which of the following statements can be made from this data?

## Options

1. Osteolysis and subsequent component loosening will be minimal if the wear rate continues
2. Osteolysis and subsequent component loosening will be greater than conventional hip arthroplasty if the wear rate continues
3. Component positioning should have no effect on wear rates of these bearings
4. This annual wear rate is significantly lower than that seen in conventional THA
5. Patient satisfaction will be greater at 10 year follow up

## Correct option

**1. Osteolysis and subsequent component loosening will be minimal if the wear rate continues**

## Answer explanation

Polyethylene wear rates are associated with osteolysis and subsequent component loosening. Wear rates above 0.1 mm per year are at significant risk of osteolysis. In this patient with a wear rate of 0.03 mm, osteolysis and subsequent component loosening will be minimal if this rate continues (Answer 1).

Periprosthetic osteolysis is a histiocytic response to wear debris, primarily polyethylene particles, which leads to bone resorption and implant loosening. As studies have shown, the rate of polyethylene wear is the most significant predictor of the development of osteolysis in total hip arthroplasty (THA). The introduction of highly cross-linked polyethylene (HXLPE) has significantly reduced volumetric wear rates compared to conventional ultra-high molecular weight polyethylene (UHMWPE). In fact, since the introduction of HXLPE, there has been a decrease in wear rates, which has led to a decrease in the incidence of clinically significant osteolysis at intermediate and long follow-up intervals.

*Dumbleton et al performed a meta-analysis of the literature concerning wear rates and osteolysis.* **They concluded** that osteolysis was rarely observed when the wear rate was less than 0.1 mm per year.

*Randall et al performed a systematic review and meta-analysis in patients <50 years old who underwent a THA using HXLPE.* *This included 18 studies (1,587 hips) with a mean follow-up of 14.1 years.* Overall, revision-free survivorship was 98%, and the mean improvement in the Harris Hip score was 43.7 points. The average linear wear rate in this meta-analysis was 0.024mm/year (95% CI: 0.013-0.035), and all wear rates in this study remained well below the 0.1mm/year threshold regardless of femoral bearing material or size. **The authors conclude** that this study supports the continued use of HXLPE among young patients to maximize revision-free survival.

*Sochart et al. reviewed 235 total hip arthroplasties performed by Charnley, with 20-year follow-up.* Osteolysis and revision were significantly associated with polyethylene wear rates. Average wear rates for hips that did not proceed to revision were 0.09 mm per year, compared with 0.19 mm per year for hips that were subsequently revised for loosening.

Incorrect Answer Choices:
Answer 2: Osteolysis and subsequent component loosening will not be greater than that of conventional THA since the threshold is 0.1mm/year of wear, and this only reveals 0.03mm/year. Plus, conventional wear rates in THA are reported to be 0.02-0.04mm/year, similar to this new polyethylene.
Answer 3: This is incorrect as component positioning will affect the wear rates of these bearings.
Answer 4: This is incorrect because a wear rate of 0.03mm/year is similar to conventional THA, with an average reported annual wear rate between 0.02mm and 0.04mm/year.
Answer 5: With similar wear rates to conventional THA, the patient satisfaction rates are similar.
