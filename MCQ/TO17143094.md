## Question

Which of the following statements with regards to biopsy in bone tumours is true?

## Options

1. Open biopsy carries a higher risk of local recurrence after definitive surgery as compared to needle biopsy
2. It is advisable to divide the biopsy sample into two and send to two different pathologists to be doubly sure of the diagnosis
3. When doing open biopsy, meticulous dissection should be done to raise subcutaneous flaps
4. FNAC is preferred over needle biopsy for diagnosis of malignant bone tumours since it yields early results thereby saving time.

## Correct option

**1. Open biopsy carries a higher risk of local recurrence after definitive surgery as compared to needle biopsy**

## Answer explanation

There is a higher chance of contamination of normal tissues after open biopsy as compared to needle biopsy and hence higher risk of local recurrence after open biopsy as compared to needle biopsy.

Many a times tumours are heterogenous and if a sample is divided, there are chances that one pathologist may get a tissue with high grade and another with low grade giving discrepancy of report adding to the confusion. So, biopsy samples SHOULD NEVER BE DIVIDED. In case of doubt, slides and blocks can be reviewed from a different pathologist

Flaps should not be raised while doing open biopsy because that would contaminate the planes and will increase the risk of local recurrence after definitive surgery. It is recommended to get to the bone directly without raising flaps.

FNAC carries no role in the diagnosis of bone tumours and hence should not be done. Since bone tumours are paucicellular, aspirated contents from the tumour may not be representative. Moreover, we cannot perform immunohistochemistry tests on FNAC aspirated contents. Also , FNAC is done by a pathologist who may not be well versed with oncological incisions for definitive surgery .

Answer A
