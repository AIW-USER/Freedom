## Question

## Options

1. None required
2. Akaline phosphatase
3. Erythrocyte sedimentation rate
4. Serum IgG
5. Lactate dehydrogenase

## Correct option

**1. None required**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a unicameral bone cyst (UBC) of the right distal femur. Specific laboratory tests are not a routine part of the workup for this pathology.

Unicameral bone cysts (UBC) are non-neoplastic, serous fluid-filled bone lesions that are thought to result from temporary failure of medullary bone formation near the physis. They are usually found in patients under the age of 20. Most are asymptomatic unless fracture occurs. The diagnosis of a UBC is usually given on the basis of the lesion's typical radiographic appearance. Confirmation is made when cyst fluid is demonstrated on biopsy, although this is almost never required.

*Jain et al. reviewed the radiographic approach to a patient with hip pain.* Conventional radiography is the initial investigation of choice and is frequently the only imaging technique required in these patients. Computed tomography (CT) is of limited value because of the risks associated with ionizing radiation. Therefore, its use is limited to assessment of complex bone lesions only.

*Canavese et al. retrospectively reviewed 46 children and adolescents with UBC treated with autologous bone marrow injection, methylprednisolone acetate injection or percutaneous curettage alone.* **They showed** the greatest rate of healing occurred with percutaneous curettage (70%) compared to bone marrow injection (21%) and methylprednisolone acetate injection (41%) (P = 0.03).

Figures A and B show a well-defined lytic lesion that is centrally located within the metaphysis and abuts the physis. The margins are well defined and demonstrate a thin sclerotic border. There is no periosteal reaction nor matrix production. These findings are consistent with a simple bone cyst (UBC). Illustration A shows the typical histological finding of a UBC including thin non-epithelial lining of pseudocyst with bland spindle cells and osteoid in wall.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Alkaline Phosphatase is a marker for bone turnover and an increased level indicates tumor activity. Elevated levels are seen in osteosarcoma, rickets and heterotrophic ossification.
Answer 3: Erythrocyte sedimentation rate is a marker for acute inflammatory reaction. This would be elevated in osteomyelitis, Lyme disease, transient synovitis, septic arthritis, multiple myeloma, and other inflammatory conditions.
Answer 4: Serum IgG is a marker for polyclonal immunoglobulin production. This would be elevated in multiple myeloma and multiple sclerosis.
Answer 5: Lactate dehydrogenase is an enzyme found in almost all body tissue. Elevated levels are seen in Ewing's sarcoma and intramedullary osteosarcoma.
