## Question

## Options

1. Deep peroneal nerve and superficial peroneal nerve
2. Superficial peroneal nerve and tibial nerve
3. Tibial nerve and deep peroneal nerve
4. Tibial nerve and saphenous nerve
5. No true internervous plane

## Correct option

**3. Tibial nerve and deep peroneal nerve**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a pilon fracture with central impaction, with the primary fracture line most accessible from an anteromedial approach, which utilizes the internervous plane between the deep peroneal nerve and the tibial nerve (Answer 3).

Pilon fractures are highly complex tibial plafond injuries that often have significant metaphyseal comminution and central impaction. The most direct access to central impaction is typically made through an anterior-based approach centered over the primary fracture line. The anterolateral approach utilizes the internervous plane between the deep and superficial peroneal nerves, while the anteromedial approach utilizes the plane between the deep peroneal and tibial nerves. The direct anterior approach, less commonly utilized, takes no true internervous plane through the anterior compartment and is less extensile as a result. Posterior-based approaches are often utilized to address additional fracture planes in complex pilon fractures, but do not provide as direct access to central impaction as an anterior-based approach in most cases.

*Cole et al. performed a retrospective radiographic review of 38 pilon fractures and* **found** that a consistent Y-shaped major fracture pattern was identified in all patients, involving the tibiofibular joint and resulting in three main bone fragments. Comminution was present in 95% of cases, predominantly in the central and anterolateral regions. **They emphasize** that an understanding of the primary fracture planes and location helps guide the surgical approach.

*A comprehensive review by Assal et al. discusses the strategies for surgical approaches in open reduction internal fixation (ORIF) of pilon fractures.* **They emphasize** the importance of soft tissue management and the principle of delaying definitive fixation to decrease complications. They outline various anterior, lateral, and posterior surgical approaches, each with specific indications based on the fracture pattern and the involved columns of the tibia, and highlight the need for careful preoperative planning with CT scan evaluation to achieve anatomic reduction of the articular surface and stable fixation.

Incorrect Answers:
