## Question

An 82-year-old female presents to the emergency department after a ground-level fall with the injury shown in Figure A. She undergoes open reduction internal fixation (ORIF) with a lateral locking plate. At one year, she presents to your clinic with persistent thigh pain with ambulation. What findings would represent the most common reason for failure in these patients?

![[q218876-a-figure-a.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Persistent fracture line
2. Periprosthetic lucency with startup pain and normal lab values
3. Advanced polyethylene wear
4. Lucency around the locking plate screws with draining sinus tract
5. TKA periprosthetic dislocation

## Correct option

**1. Persistent fracture line**

## Answer explanation

The most common reason for failed ORIF of a distal femur TKA periprosthetic fracture is fracture nonunion.

Periprosthetic fractures are often devastating injuries with a detrimental impact on patients' quality of life after total knee replacement. Most of these fractures present with a stable implant and subsequently undergo ORIF using various operative techniques (locked lateral plating, retrograde intramedullary nailing, etc.). While uncommon (~10%), the most common reason for revision surgery thereafter is fracture nonunion. These can be treated with revision ORIF or conversion to distal femur replacement (DFR). There are many who support utilizing a DFR as the primary treatment for some of these periprosthetic femur fractures over attempting ORIF. A primary DFR is advantageous in the setting of segmental or highly complex fractures, very distal fractures, and in patients with poor bone healing potential. Ultimately, these injuries pose a unique challenge, and each should be handled on a case-by-case basis. Patients’ overall health, fracture location, fracture morphology, and implant stability collectively determine the most appropriate treatment plan.

*Chen and colleagues performed a retrospective case series regarding patients at their institution who sustained a periprosthetic TKA fracture.* **They reported** the most common causes for failed ORIF requiring revision to DFR were nonunion (53.8%), infection (30.8%), aseptic loosening (7.7%) and refracture (7.7%). **The authors conclude** that primary DFR is considered appropriate in patients with very distal femur periprosthetic fractures, osteolysis, osteoporosis, inability to maintain protected weight-bearing, or in patients who may not tolerate multiple surgeries.

*Ebraheim and colleagues performed a systematic review regarding distal femur periprosthetic TKA fractures and surgical treatment options.* **They found** the most reported periprosthetic TKA fracture was classified as Rorabeck type II (displaced fracture with stable implant). This subgroup of patients had similar union rates when comparing locked plating (87%) to retrograde intramedullary nailing (84%), however, the retrograde nailing group had a higher revision surgery rate overall due to nonunion. **The authors concluded** that patients’ overall health, fracture morphology, and surgeon experience should guide the surgical treatment plan, as a gold standard has yet to be established.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Aseptic loosening of the TKA implant has been reported, however, is not the most common cause for failure after ORIF.
Answer 3: While changes in the mechanical axis of the limb and osteolysis due to polyethylene wear are possible after ORIF, they are not the most common reason for failure of TKA periprosthetic fracture ORIF.
Answer 4: Periprosthetic joint infection and other deep infections are always a concern after ORIF. While it is a common cause of failure, fracture nonunion remains the most common reason for revision surgery.
Answer 5: TKA Implant dislocations are among the lowest causes of failure after ORIF.
