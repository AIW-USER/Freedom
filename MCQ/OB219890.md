## Question

A 47-year-old male with a history of Dupuytren’s disease presents with progressive hand disability and difficulty with fine motor tasks. After examination and discussion with the patient, you elect to perform partial palmar fasciectomy with sequential Z-plasty. Using this technique for the incision will help address which of the following?

## Options

1. Skin closure
2. Volar Nodule
3. Dorsal Nodule
4. Recurrence
5. Neurovascular symptoms

## Correct option

**1. Skin closure**

## Answer explanation

Z-plasty is performed to address the contractures associated with Dupuytren’s disease in the event surgery is performed (Answer 1).

Dupuytren’s disease is a progressive condition affecting the palmar fascia, leading to abnormal fibrous tissue formation and thickening under the skin of the palm. This process causes the tissue to contract over time, leading to flexion contractures primarily in the fourth and fifth fingers, though other fingers can be involved too. The disease often starts with a nodule in the palm that gradually develops into cords, pulling the fingers into a flexed position while limiting extension, which may ultimately interfere with hand function.

Treatment varies depending on the severity and functional impact of the contracture. Mild cases may be managed conservatively, while more advanced contractures often require intervention. In severe cases, surgical fasciectomy may be necessary to excise the diseased fascia. Post-procedure therapy, including stretching and splinting, is crucial to restore range of motion and minimize recurrence.

*McGregor reviewed the use of Z-plasty in hand surgery, specifically when managing Dupuytren contractures.* Beyond its function of increasing length, **the author emphasizes** the secondary role of Z-plasty to provide exposure to deeper structures within the hand. In the case of Dupuytren contracture, Z-plasty is often employed for a single-ray contracture and allows exposure of the pathologic structures without performing a longitudinal incision. The author also describes using Z-plasty in contracted scars and emphasizes proper planning and technique to achieve the best results.

*Brown et al. review post-burn contractures and outline the spectrum of deformity leading to significant hand dysfunction.* **The authors emphasize** that appropriate care during the acute burn phase can help limit the severity of the contracture. Surgical release with graft or flap coverage is the primary treatment modality, with postoperative splinting, hand therapy, and scar care all vital to overall outcomes.

Incorrect answers:
Answers 2-3: One of the first signs of Dupuytren’s disease is painless nodules in the palm. These typically do not require surgery unless they progress into palpable cords.
Answer 4: Z-plasty is required to address the contracture associated with pathological cords. This technique allows for the closure of the wound once the fasciectomy is completed. Recurrence is related to the technique utilized (injection, needle aponeurotomy, or surgery) rather than the skin incision technique.
Answer 5: Neurovascular symptoms may be present in patients with Dupuytren’s disease and require surgery but are not the indication for performing Z-plasty.
