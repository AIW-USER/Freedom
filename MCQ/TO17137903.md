Chapter: Trauma MCQs — Proximal Humerus Fractures

## Question

Which is not a predictor of humeral head ischemia after proximal humeral fracture

## Options

1. Disruption of the medial “hinge”
2. Fractures through the anatomic neck
3. Metaphyseal extension of the head fragment of 8 mm or less
4. Valgus displacement of head

## Correct option

**4. Valgus displacement of head**

## Answer explanation

Several morphologic fracture features have been proposed to estimate the possibility of the disruption of this blood supply and hence predict the risk of AVN. These features include varus displacement of the head, size of metaphyseal extension of the humeral head, and medial displacement of the humeral shaft in relation to the humeral head. Hertel et al. studied 100 intracapsular proximal humerus fractures (i.e., those in which at least one component of the fracture was proximal to the anatomic neck) undergoing operative treatment. Several fracture characteristics as possible predictors for humeral head ischemia were studied and correlated with intraoperative assessment of humeral head perfusion. Distal metaphyseal extension of the head fragment of 8 mm or less, disruption of the medial “hinge” between the humeral head and the shaft at the level of the calcar, and fractures through the anatomic neck were independent predictors for humeral head ischemia.

Answer: 4
