## Question

A middle-aged obese female with a history of uncontrolled diabetes presents to the office after undergoing physical therapy for a right ankle sprain. She also notes difficulty sleeping secondary to atraumatic left shoulder pain and stiffness that has been ongoing for 3 months. What shoulder physical exam finding is most likely present in this patient?

## Options

1. Active forward elevation 100 degrees, passive forward elevation 150 degrees
2. Symmetric loss of both active and passive range of motion
3. Pain with 0-30 degrees of active abduction, full passive range of motion present
4. Positive empty can test with pain, weakness and full passive range of motion present
5. Positive Speed's test with full passive and active range of motion

## Correct option

**2. Symmetric loss of both active and passive range of motion**

## Answer explanation

In patients with uncontrolled diabetes and insidious onset of shoulder pain, the most likely associated condition is adhesive capsulitis, which presents with loss of both active and passive range of motion (Answer 2).

Adhesive capsulitis is an inflammatory shoulder condition that leads to a reduction in both passive and active range of motion. It is most commonly associated with diabetes (type I and type II) and hypothyroidism. Initial treatment is the medical management of the underlying disease, physical therapy and injections. If these fail to improve symptoms, manipulation under anesthesia or arthroscopic capsular release are the next treatment options.

*Zreik et al. performed a meta-analysis looking at the prevalence of adhesive capsulitis in diabetes mellitus and to see if the type of diabetes or insulin therapy influenced it.* **They found** that people with diabetes are 5 times (95% CI 3.2-7.7) more likely than people without diabetes to have adhesive capsulitis. No significant difference was found between the different diabetic treatment regimens.

*Rai et al. compared the prevalence of prediabetes and diabetes among patients with adhesive capsulitis.* Each participant underwent a 2-hour oral glucose tolerance test to categorize them as normal, prediabetic, or diabetic. **They found** that 15.5% were prediabetic, and 27.4% were diabetic. **They recommend** that each patient with idiopathic adhesive capsulitis of the shoulder be screened for diabetes for early detection and management.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Decreased active range of motion with normal passive range of motion is not indicative of adhesive capsulitis.
Answer 3: Pain during the initial 0-30 degrees of shoulder abduction is more commonly found in supraspinatus tendinosis/tears.
Answer 4: These findings are more common in rotator cuff tears while the passive range of motion is still intact.
Answer 5: These findings are more common with biceps tendinopathy.
