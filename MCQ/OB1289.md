## Question

What is the correct treatment for an incompletely excised high grade superficial sarcoma on the volar aspect of the forearm?

## Options

1. Chemotherapy
2. Radiation therapy
3. Marginal resection of the tumor bed to spare the neurovascular bundles
4. Wide resection of the tumor bed and coverage as necessary
5. Palliative care

## Correct option

**4. Wide resection of the tumor bed and coverage as necessary**

## Answer explanation

The correct treatment for an incompletely excised high grade sarcoma on the volar aspect of the forearm is wide resection of the tumor bed and coverage as needed. It is always important to gain appropriate imaging, biopsy, and staging of a patient with a mass to correctly excise the tumor during the index surgery. Incomplete excision requiring tumor bed wide excision is a morbid procedure that potentially may affect the patients survival.

*Davis et al review 239 patient with "unplanned excision" of high-grade sarcomas and* **showed** that positive tumor at the index surgery correlated with increased risk of local recurrence.

*Noria et al retrospectively reviewed the charts of 65 patients to determine the prevalence of residual tumor after unplanned excision and to identify factors that predict which patients will have residual tumor following such an excision.* All sixty-five patients had a repeat resection of the wound bed and histological evidence of sarcoma was identified in a total of twenty-three (35 percent) patients. Even after the second excision, the rate of local recurrence was significantly higher than other patients treated at this cancer center. These data indicate that it is not possible to predict which patients will have residual tumor at the site of the operative wound and therefore repeat excision is advised.
