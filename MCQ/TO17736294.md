## Question

Tied mark in human articular cartilage is between (General orthopedics- anatomy, basic science and pathology)

## Options

1. Calcified layer and subchondral bone
2. Superficial and transitional layer
3. Transitional and calcified layer
4. Radial and calcified zone

## Correct option

**4. Radial and calcified zone**

## Answer explanation

Articular cartilage: Articular cartilage is 60–80% water by weight. Stiffness and durability of cartilage depend on the extracellular matrix. Collagen (mainly type II), proteoglycans and glycoproteins are main constituents of matrix. Chondrocytes make less than < 10% of the total volume.

Zones of articular cartilage: Articular cartilage is typically depicted in four zones.

![[TO17736294-1.png]]

The superficial zone (zone 1): This zone makes the gliding surface of articular cartilage. Chondrocytes and collagen fibrils are arranged parallel to the articular surface. This zone contains relatively inactive chondrocytes although they contain endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi membranes, and mitochondria.

Transitional zone (zone 2): In the transitional zone collagen fibers are larger than those of superficial zone and their orientation changes from parallel to columnar. Active chondrocytes contain endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi membranes, mitochondria, glycogen, and intracytoplasmic filaments.

Deep zone (radial zone or zone 3): Chondrocytes are organized in a columnar pattern perpendicular to the joint surface. The largest collagen fibrils of articular cartilage and the highest content of proteoglycans are present in this zone.

Tidemark: Tidemark is a basophilic line which forms the boundary between calcified and noncalcified cartilage. It’s considered to be a part of Zone 4, i.e. calcified zone only.

Calcified zone (zone 4) : This is the deepest zone of articular cartilage and it separates the softer cartilage from underlying subchondral bone. The chondrocytes in the calcified zone connect the articular cartilage to the underlying bone and contain little cytoplasm and almost no endoplasmic retinaculum.

Also know:

As the number of proteoglycans increases, the amount of water decreases from the superficial to the deep zone.

Age-related changes in the articular cartilage : Water content, number of chondrocytes, and proteoglycan content decrease with increasing age due to increased enzymatic degradation and accumulation of pentosidine, an advanced glycation end product.

Answer: 4
