## Question

The emergency department consults the orthopaedic surgery service with concern for septic arthritis in the shoulder of a 4-year-old boy. They have already obtained blood cultures prior to consultation. The patient’s labs show WBC is 16,000 cells per microliter, ESR is 72mm/hr (normal: 0-20 mm/hr) and CRP is 5.6 mg/dL (normal: 0.8-1.0mg/dL). Which of the following is true regarding the management of pediatric septic arthritis?

## Options

1. MRSA imparts a low risk for concomitant osteomyelitis
2. MRI prior to surgery may decrease rates of repeat surgery
3. Kingella Kingae is an infrequent pathologic organism in this patient’s age group (<5 years)
4. A negative synovial polymerase chain reaction effectively rules out septic arthritis
5. Septic arthritis is nonurgent and may be managed on an outpatient basis

## Correct option

**2. MRI prior to surgery may decrease rates of repeat surgery**

## Answer explanation

In the pediatric population, several joints (i.e., shoulder, hip, elbow, and ankle) have intra-articular metaphysis, placing them at increased risk of septic arthritis resulting in adjacent osteomyelitis. In these cases, a pre-treatment MRI often decreases the risk of return trips to the OR by identifying concomitant pathology that can be addressed at the time of the index procedure.

Septic arthritis (SA) in the pediatric population is considered an urgent surgical condition, as cartilage degeneration may occur as early as 8 hours after bacterial inoculation. Reaching the diagnosis is often difficult, particularly in newborns and infants who have not developed the ability to talk. Cases that involve concomitant SA and osteomyelitis are associated with longer hospital stays, more severe infections requiring multiple irrigation and debridement procedures, osteonecrosis, and leg length discrepancies. Pre-treatment MRI should be considered in cases involving major joints that are at high risk for adjacent osteomyelitis due to intra-articular metaphyses (i.e., shoulder, hip, elbow, and ankle). Obtaining a pre-treatment MRI may serve both diagnostic and treatment-determining purposes, as it can identify concomitant osteomyelitis which also requires debridement. For this reason, obtaining a pre-treatment MRI has been associated with shorter hospital stays and decreased need for repeated trips to the OR. Importantly, obtaining an MRI should not delay treatment in clinically unstable patients. In high-efficacy centers, an MRI is obtained en route to the OR via a single-anesthesia event between the scanner and surgical debridement.

Song and Sloboda provided a review article on the management of pediatric osteomyelitis. They discuss the importance of early organism identification to allow for tailored antibiotic therapy. They emphasize that isolated osteomyelitis may be treated with antibiotics alone unless: (1) patients fail to rapidly respond to antibiotics, (2) there is adjacent septic arthritis or (3) there is an obvious area of necrotic bone (i.e. sequestrum). The authors conclude that with early identification and appropriate treatment, generally good outcomes with few long-term complications can be expected.

*Erkilinc and colleagues provided a review article on the management of pediatric SA.* *The authors discuss the etiology, differential diagnoses, common organisms, and optimal treatment modalities to optimize outcomes.* *They stress that urgent irrigation and debridement be performed with a pre-treatment MRI in high-risk cases for adjacent osteomyelitis.* **The authors conclude** that appropriate treatment with targeted antibiotic therapy results in the best outcomes.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: MRSA imparts the HIGHEST risk for concomitant osteomyelitis compared to other organisms.
Answer 3: Kingella Kingae has been increasingly identified as a pathologic organism with newer studies suggesting it is the most common organism in SA patients between 3 months to 5 years old.
Answer 4: A negative synovial polymerase chain reaction does NOT effectively rule out septic arthritis, although it makes it less likely. Kingella Kingae is a low-virulence organism that is the most identified pathogen in PCR-negative cases.
Answer 5: Due to the rapid production of cartilage degenerating enzymes from the host’s autoimmune reaction to bacterial inoculation, septic arthritis imparts an urgent surgical indication.
