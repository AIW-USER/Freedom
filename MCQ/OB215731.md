## Question

A 47-year-old male presents to the trauma bay following a motor vehicle collision. He sustains a grade III open tibia fracture with an extensive soft tissue defect. On physical examination, he has no plantar sensation and no palpable or Doppler-able dorsalis pedis or posterior tibial pulses. The decision is made to proceed with a below knee amputation. What is the most important factor of patient satisfaction after management of this injury?

## Options

1. Ability to return to work
2. Cosmetic appearance of the extremity
3. Formal psychiatric counseling
4. Normal walking speed
5. Postoperative pain level

## Correct option

**1. Ability to return to work**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a grade III open fracture of the lower leg with significant soft tissue injury. The strongest predictor of outcomes postoperatively is the ability to return to work.

The decision to proceed with amputation or limb salvage is often dependent on the severity of soft tissue injury. SIP (Sickness Impact Profile) and return to work is not significantly different between amputation and reconstruction at 2 years in limb-threatening injuries. However, the strongest predictor of patient-reported outcomes after trauma-related lower extremity amputation is the patient's ability to return to work.

*Bosse et al. retrospectively reviewed limb salvage following severe trauma.* **They report** that at two years, there was no significant difference in scores for the Sickness Impact Profile between the amputation and reconstruction groups, and patients who underwent amputation had functional outcomes that were similar to those of patients who underwent reconstruction. **They concluded** that patients with limbs at high risk for amputation can be advised that reconstruction typically results in two-year outcomes equivalent to those of amputation.

*MacKenzie et al. performed the Lower Extremity Assessment Project (LEAP), which is a multi-center study of severe lower extremity trauma in the US civilian population.* **They report** that at 2- and 7-year follow-ups, no difference in functional outcome between patients who underwent either limb salvage surgery or amputation was found. **They concluded** that outcomes were more affected by the patient's economic, social, and personal resources than by the initial treatment of the injury.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 2-5: These factors play a lesser role than the ability to return to work in predicting patient-reported outcomes after trauma-related lower extremity amputation.
