## Question

Figure A represents the radiograph of a 17-year-old female soccer player who complains of intermittent anterior knee pain. On physical examination, her Beighton score is 6/9. She is noted to have approximately 70 degrees of hip internal rotation and slight genu valgum bilaterally. Based on these findings, what knee injury is she most at risk for?

![[q215554-a-patella-alta.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. ACL rupture
2. Patellar tendon rupture
3. Patellar instability
4. Medial femoral condyle osteochondral lesion
5. Lateral bucket-handle meniscus tear

## Correct option

**3. Patellar instability**

## Answer explanation

This patient is at risk for patellar subluxation/dislocation based on her radiographic findings of patella alta as well as her physical examination findings consistent with femoral anterversion, genu valgum and generalized ligamentous laxity.

Patellar instability is common and usually occurs with an acute traumatic injury. Patient risk factors include younger age, female sex, ligamentous laxity and previous dislocations. Predisposing anatomic risk factors include genu valgum, femoral anteversion and external tibial torsion (miserable malalignment syndrome), in addition to patella alta, trochlear dysplasia and lateral patellar tilt. The Insall-Salvati ratio is used to differentiate patella alta (>1.2) and baja (<0.8). Additionally, the Blackbourne-Peel method or the Caton Deschamps method can be used for classification of patella alta.

*Diduch et. al review relevant patellofemoral anatomy as it relates to patellar instability.* *They review the decision making process for operative management with regards to soft tissue procedures (MPFL reconstruction) versus bony procedures (tibial tubercle osteotomy and trochleoplasty).* **They conclude** by reviewing the specific indications and technique of each procedure.

*Sheehan et. al evaluated the correlation of knee measurements and testing (Q-angle, J-sign, and lateral hypermobility testing) with patellar maltracking/instability.* *They were able to identify two patient groups with distinct maltracking patterns but no correlation existed with any aforementioned test or measurement.* **They concluded** that a better understanding of the three-dimensional patellofemoral motion was needed for improved diagnosis and treatment of patellar maltracking.

Figure A is a lateral knee radiograph depicting patella alta.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: While this patient does have some risk factors for ACL rupture, there is significant patella alta on radiographs. In combination with her coronal alignment, she is more at risk for patellar instability.
Answer 2: While patellar tendon injuries do occur more frequently in younger patients (<40) compared with quadriceps tendon injuries (>40), patella Alta would occur after a distinct injury not as a risk factor.
Answer 4: With patellar dislocation, lateral femoral condyle osteochondral lesions are normally seen, not medial.
Answer 5: This patient is less likely, based on the describe risk factors, to have a bucket-handle meniscus tear compared to patellar instability.
