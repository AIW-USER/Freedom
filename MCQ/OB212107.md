## Question

Which of the following statements regarding the management of atraumatic full-thickness rotator cuff tears is true?

## Options

1. Pain on presentation correlates with the severity of the tear
2. Pain on presentation does not correlate with patient demographic
3. Patient expectation regarding the effectiveness of rehabilitation is the strongest predictor of the need for surgery
4. Smoking status is the strongest predictor of the need for surgery
5. Activity level is the strongest predictor of the need for surgery

## Correct option

**3. Patient expectation regarding the effectiveness of rehabilitation is the strongest predictor of the need for surgery**

## Answer explanation

Patient expectations regarding physical therapy is the strongest predictor of need for eventual surgery for atraumatic full-thickness rotator cuff tears.

Atraumatic full-thickness rotator cuff tears are common, reported in up to 28% and 65% of patients aged >60 and >70 years of age, respectively. Physical therapy, NSAIDS, and subacromial corticosteroid injections remain the first line of treatment for most of these cases. Physical therapy focuses on aggressive strengthening of the rotator cuff and scapular stabilizers.

*Dunn et al. evaluated the correlation between pain level and the severity of atraumatic symptomatic full-thickness rotator-cuff tear treated with physical therapy.* **The authors found** that increased comorbidities, lower education level, and race were significant factors associated with pain on presentation. **They concluded** that anatomic features defining the severity of atraumatic rotator cuff tears were not associated with the degree of pain.

*Dunn et al. also prospectively reviewed the indications for surgery of full-thickness rotator cuff repair by identifying predictors of failure with nonoperative treatment.* **They found** that patient expectations regarding physical therapy was the strongest predictor of surgery. However, higher activity level and non-smoking status were also significant predictors of surgery. **The authors concluded** that a patient's decision to undergo surgery was influenced more strongly by low expectations regarding the effectiveness of physical therapy than by patient symptoms or anatomic features of the rotator cuff tear.

Incorrect Answer:
Answer 1: Anatomic features defining the severity of atraumatic rotator cuff tears are not associated with the pain level.
Answer 2: Increased patient comorbidities, lower education level and race were significant variables associated with pain on presentation.
Answer 4 and 5: While higher activity level and non-smoking status are both significant predictors of eventual surgery, patient expectations is the strongest predictor.
