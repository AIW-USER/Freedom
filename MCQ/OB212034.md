## Question

Figure A is the radiograph of a 14-year-old male who presents to the clinic with recurrent of recurrent ankle sprains despite nonoperative treatment. What is the appropriate interval for surgical resection for this patient?

![[q212034-a-cn-coalition.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Between the extensor and peroneal tendons
2. Posterior between the Achilles and peroneal tendons
3. Posterior between the Achilles and flexor hallucis longus (FHL)
4. Medial between posterior tibial tendon and flexor digitorum longus (FDL)
5. Medial between FHL and FDL

## Correct option

**1. Between the extensor and peroneal tendons**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a calcaneonavicular coalition. The interval for surgical resection of a calcaneonavicular coalition is through an incision laterally over the sinus tarsi between the extensor and peroneal tendons.

Tarsal coalitions are a structural anomaly between two or three tarsal bones causing a rigid flatfoot. The pathophysiology of this condition occurs at the embryological level when there is a failure of mesenchymal segmentation leading to coalition between two or three tarsal bones. These coalitions are initially treated conservatively, but may require resection with possible graft interposition following failed nonoperative management. When surgical resection is undertaken, calcaneonavicular coalitions are approached through a longitudinal incision laterally over the sinus tarsi. Talocalcaneal coalitions are typically approached medially, between the FDL and FHL (at the level of the sustentaculum tali).

*Mubarak et al. retrospectively reviewed symptomatic calcaneonavicular coalitions treated with resection and fat graft interposition.* **They reported** that only 5% had symptomatic regrowth requiring repeat resection. **They concluded** that reossification and reoperation rates with fat graft interposition were lower than in most published reports of EDB interposition.

*Gantsoudes et al. retrospectively reviewed outcomes of patients treated for symptomatic talocalcaneal coalition with resection and interposition of fat graft.* **They report** only a 3% recurrence, which was treated with repeat excision. **They concluded** that a symptomatic talocalcaneal coalition can be treated with excision and fat graft interposition, and achieve good to excellent results.

Figure A is the radiograph of the foot demonstrating a calcaneonavicular coalition.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 2-4: The interval used to perform a calcaneonavicular coalition resection is laterally through the sinus tarsi
Answer 5: The interval between the FHL and FDL is used to perform a talocalcaneal coalition resection
