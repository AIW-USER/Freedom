Chapter: Orthopedics Oncology MCQs — Ewings Sarcoma

## Question

6 years old girl has had H/o episodic fever for a month and she feels a palpable mass in her mid-thigh that is painful. Her TLC is 12,400; Hb-7.6; ESR-110. Her X ray that was done shows periosteal reaction with bone destruction. The BIOPSY of lesion that was taken by the surgeon and depicted small blue round cells. Parents are concerned with the prognosis. Which of the following “does not” indicate bad prognosis?

## Options

1. Increased LDH levels
2. Recurrent fever episodes
3. High Grade
4. Age < 12 years

## Correct option

**3. High Grade**

## Answer explanation

Ans: (C)

The diagnosis seems to be Ewing's sarcoma

Prognosis depends on a number of factors. The most unfavorable prognostic factor in Ewing’s sarcoma is the presence of distant metastasis (high stage) at diagnosis. Other unfavorable prognostic factors include an age older than 10 years, a size larger than 8 cm, more central lesions (as in the pelvis or spine) and poor response to chemotherapy. The histological grade is of no prognostic significance, as all Ewing’s sarcomas are of high grade. Fever, anemia, and elevated white blood cell (WBC) count, ESR, and lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) values have been reported to indicate more extensive disease and a poorer prognosis. The presence of trl (11;22), which is present in 90% cases of Ewing sarcoma, however, does not seem to affect the clinical course.
