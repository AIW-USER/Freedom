## Question

A 15-year-old male presents to your clinic with atraumatic knee pain, swelling, and mechanical symptoms. After obtaining a knee MRI, you recommend a synovectomy for definitive management. The most likely diagnosis has a mutation in what gene?

## Options

1. APC
2. C-myc
3. CSF-1
4. EXT
5. Retinoblastoma

## Correct option

**3. CSF-1**

## Answer explanation

This patient has tenosynovial giant cell tumor (TGCT, formerly known as pigmented villonodular synovitis), which is caused by a mutation in the colony stimulating factor-1 (CSF-1) gene.

TGCT is a benign proliferative disease that arises from joint synovia, bursae, and tendon sheaths most commonly within the knee joint. Symptoms are nonspecific to include joint pain and effusion, often necessitating advanced imaging (MRI) or tissue biopsy for diagnosis. Symptomatic cases are classically treated with synovectomy, however, newer drugs inhibiting the overexpression of CSF-1 (i.e pexidartinib) have demonstrated promise in improving disease burden for recurrent or unresectable cases.

*Mastboom et al. compared the incidence rate and natural history of tenosynovial giant cell tumor (TGCT) in a pediatric population to an adult population.* Overall, the incidence of pediatric TGCT was found to be lower than that seen in adults, but with similar presenting symptoms of swelling, pain, and limited ROM with a median time before diagnosis of 12 months. In addition, **they found** recurrent disease after surgical treatment to be comparable between children and adults.

*Gouin et al. reviewed the main points for understanding TGCT and described it as a benign entity, with relatively nonspecific symptomatology and slow progression.* **They state** that the diagnosis always requires MRI and often histologic confirmation. **The authors conclude** that primary surgical resection is of paramount importance to prevent local recurrence.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: A mutation in the APC gene is commonly seen in individuals with familial adenomatous polyposis (FAP) leading to an increased risk for the development of desmoid tumors.
Answer 2: A mutation in the C-myc oncogene can be seen in aggressive forms of giant cell tumors (GCT). This tumor is classically found in the distal femur and is treated with curettage, adjuvant treatment, and reconstruction.
Answer 4: A mutation in the EXT gene is found in patients with multiple hereditary exostoses (MHE). Treatment is generally non-operative in the form of observation, however, symptomatic osteochondromas can be treated with marginal resection.
Answer 5: A mutation in the Retinoblastoma tumor suppressor gene (Rb) is the driving force in certain types of osteosarcoma. High-grade osteosarcoma is typically treated surgically in conjunction with pre-operative and post-operative chemotherapy.
