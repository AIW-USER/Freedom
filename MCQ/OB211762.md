## Question

Figure 1 is the axial MRI of an active 32-year-old male who presents to your office for evaluation of new-onset low back pain and left leg pain. He reports the pain started while he was helping a friend move last weekend and has not improved. On physical exam, he has difficulty extending his left great toe and has paresthesias to the anterolateral left calf. Which of the following is responsible for this patient’s symptoms?

![[q211762-a-spine18eckq060f2.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. L3-4 paracentral disc herniation
2. L3-4 far lateral disc herniation
3. L4-5 paracentral disc herniation
4. L4-5 far lateral disc herniation
5. L5-S1 paracentral disc herniation

## Correct option

**3. L4-5 paracentral disc herniation**

## Answer explanation

Weakness of great toe extension and paresthesias to the anterolateral calf are consistent with compression of the L5 nerve root. Figure 1 demonstrates a paracentral disc herniation, thus an L4-5 paracentral herniation is responsible for the patient’s symptoms.

In the lumbar spine, paracentral disc herniations affect the traversing nerve root, whereas far lateral herniations affect the exiting nerve root. Thus, paracentral and far lateral disc herniations at the L4-5 disk space would affect the L5 and L4 nerve roots, respectively. Treatment starts with conservative measures including anti-inflammatories, physical therapy and rest, of which 90% of patients improve without surgery. The second line of treatment consists of epidural steroid injections followed by microdiscectomy if symptoms fail to improve.

*Millhouse et al. review the management of paracentral lumbar disc herniations.* **They report** that the natural outcomes following radiculopathy due to disc herniations are favorable and mostly consist of nonoperative management. Furthermore, they review in detail the surgical technique for microdiscectomy. **They conclude** that this procedure is reserved for patients who fail conservative care for 6 weeks, have progressive motor deficits or bowel/bladder dysfunction.

*Soliman et al. review the long-term outcomes following lumbar microdiscectomy.* **They report** that long-term improvement of back and leg pain was seen in 89 and 88% of patients, respectively. Additionally, the recurrence rate following this procedure was 11%, all of which were successfully treated with fragmentectomy. **They conclude** that minimally invasive microdiscectomy, with the removal of the fragment only, is an effective way to treat lumbar disc herniations.

Illustration B is a schematic demonstrating which nerve root is affecting with paracentral and far lateral disc herniations in the lumbar spine.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1 and 4: An L3-4 paracentral disc herniation and L4-5 far lateral disc herniation would present with symptoms affecting L4 such as ankle dorsiflexion weakness and paresthesias to the anterolateral thigh and medial calf.
Answer 2: An L3-4 far lateral disc herniation would affect L3, and would present with weakness in knee extension and medial thigh paresthesias.
Answer 5: An L5-S1 paracentral disc herniation would present with S1 symptoms such as weakness with ankle plantarflexion and decreased sensation to the lateral foot.
