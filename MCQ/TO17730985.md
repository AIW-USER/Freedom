## Question

A patient with ankle injury presents with the following X ray. On examination there is no effusion and anterior ankle joint line tenderness is present. What is FALSE regarding diagnosis and management? (FNB Pattern Paper 2014)

![[TO17730985-1.png]]

## Options

1. On AP view tibio-fibular clear space > 6 mm indicates syndesmotic injury
2. Weight bearing stress X ray guide the management
3. Tear of deltoid ligament along with AITFL is an indication for surgery
4. On mortise view tibio-fibular overlapping less than 6mm indicates unstable injury

## Correct option

**4. On mortise view tibio-fibular overlapping less than 6mm indicates unstable injury**

## Answer explanation

Syndesmotic ankle injuries (high ankle sprains): -

The term syndesmotic injury is used to describe a lesion of the ligaments that connect the distal fibula and the tibial notch surrounded on both sides by the anterior and posterior tibial tubercles, with or without an associated injury of the deltoid ligament. Its most commonly associated with external rotation injuries.

Syndesmosis anatomy: Syndesmosis maintains the integrity of distal tibia and fibula resisting all kind of stresses. It includes four major ligaments: the anterior inferior tibiofibular ligament (AITFL, which runs from Chaput tubercle on the tibia to Wagstaffe tubercle on the fibula) limits the fibular external rotation; the interosseous ligament (IOL), which limits the lateral translation of the fibula; the posterior inferior tibiofibular ligament (PITFL, which runs from Volkmann tubercle and attaches to the posterolateral aspect of the fibula), prevents the posterior fibular translation; and the inferior transverse ligament (ITL, which is distal to PITFL), limits posterior talar displacement.

Fig: Anatomy of Syndesmosis

Clinical examination: In syndesmotic injuries gross swelling is usually not visible. Pain occurs on forced dorsiflexion and external rotation of the ankle joint. Tenderness is present at anterior tibiofibular syndesmosis and may be present over deltoid ligament. The Squeeze test (compression of tibia and fibula at midcalf level causes pain at syndesmosis) is a diagnostic test for syndesmosis injury. Fibular translation test, Cotton’s test and External rotation stress test may also come out to be positive.

The ‘fibular translation’ test is performed by drawing the ﬁbula forward and backward with the tibia stabilized, while the ‘Cotton’ test is performed while attempting translation of the talus from side-to-side with the ankle in a neutral position; increased translation as compared with the contralateral side accompanied by pain, make these tests positive. In External rotation stress test, pain over syndesmosis is elicited with external rotation/dorsiflexion of the foot with knee and hip flexed to 90 degrees.

X ray: In Syndesmotic assessment AP view is best, mortise view doesn’t give much information, although it gives better information on ankle joint congruity. Syndesmotic injury is characterized by increased tibioﬁbular clear space and decreased tibioﬁbular overlap. Both parameters should be measured 1 cm above the tibial plafond. The first parameter (TF clear space) is represented by the space between the medial border of the fibula and the lateral border of the posterior tibial prominence. This distance in both AP and mortise views should be < 6 mm with an intact syndesmosis. The other parameter (TF overlap) is the maximal overlap between the medial border of the fibula and the lateral border of the distal tibia. Normal tibiofibular overlap for the AP view is > 6 mm.

On a mortise view, the ankle articular surfaces should appear symmetrical. The superior surface of Talus should be congruous with the tibial plafond, the medial and lateral articular spaces should be equal and comparable to superior surface. The subchondral bone that forms the Shenton’s line should be intact. There should also be a continuous curve between the lateral talus and the recessed tip of fibula (also known as Dime’s sign). The medial joint space should be less than 4 mm (increased value indicates a lateral talar shift), and the superior joint space should be within 2 mm medially of its width laterally.

![[TO17730985-6.jpg]]

Fig: Dime’s sign: The “ball” or “dime sign” is described on the AP view as an unbroken curve connecting the recess in the distal tip of the fibula and the lateral process of the talus. Broken curve is indicative of pathology.

Weight bearing stress x rays are important to suggest instability in obscure cases. MRI is the gold standard for the diagnosis of syndesmotic injuries. Lamda sign identifiable on coronal MRI images as > 2mm of diastasis, is very sensitive and specific for diagnosis.

Classification: Injuries can be divided into acute<6 weeks, subacute> 6 weeks, and chronic >6 months. They can further be divided into stable (tear of AITFL with or without IOL tear with intact deltoid ligament) and unstable (tear of AITFL with IOL and deltoid tear suggested by > 5 mm medial clear space).

Treatment: Stable syndesmotic sprains should be treated non-operatively as they usually heal after conservative management. Conservative treatment includes rest, ice compression and non-weight bearing immobilization for two weeks. It is followed by functional mobilization in a brace, range of motion and strength exercises. Once full Rom and strength is achieved training of neuromuscular control and proprioception exercises are started.

Unstable injuries are best managed by surgery. Aim of surgery is to maintain reduction of syndesmosis until ligament heals by syndesmotic screw or a suture-button device with or without AITFL repair.

Answer: 4
