## Question

A 42-year-old Jehovah's Witness undergoes outpatient elective total hip arthroplasty for idiopathic avascular necrosis of the right hip. Which of the following medications given in the perioperative period decreases the amount of active plasmin in this patient's blood?

## Options

1. Epoetin alfa
2. Aspirin
3. Tranexamic acid
4. Dexamethasone
5. Celecoxib

## Correct option

**3. Tranexamic acid**

## Answer explanation

Tranexamic acid (TXA) competitively inhibits the conversion of plasminogen to plasmin, which has been shown to decrease blood loss during total hip and knee arthroplasty.

TXA is routinely used during total hip and knee arthroplasty and is available in topical, oral and IV formulations. Competitive inhibition of the lysin receptor found on plasminogen blocks its conversion to plasmin, which ultimately prevents fibrin degradation. This antifibrinolytic medication has been shown to decrease blood loss and transfusion requirements without significantly increasing the risk of thromboembolic events in total joint replacement surgery.

*Fillingham et al performed a meta analysis reviewing the safety profile of TXA.* **They report** that moderate evidence supports the safety of TXA in patients undergoing total knee arthroplasty with an ASA score of 3 or greater. **They further conclude** that use of TXA in patients with a history of prior thromboembolic disease does not appear to increase the incidence of venous thromboembolic events including stroke.

*Dastrup et al review the risk of cardiovascular events and death following the use of TXA in elective arthroplasty.* *They report on a comparative analysis of 45,000 patients undergoing elective THA, of which 38,000 received preoperative TXA.* **They conclude** that TXA is not associated with an increased risk of cardiovascular events or death.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Epoetin alfa (ie. Epogen) stimulates erythropoiesis and can be used to increase a patient's hemoglobin concentration prior to elective arthroplasty procedures.
Answer 2: Aspirin is an irreversible non-selective COX inhibitor that is commonly used for DVT prophylaxis postoperatively.
Answer 4: Dexamethasone is a corticosteroid that is thought to decrease postoperative pain following elective arthroplasty procedures.
Answer 5: Celecoxib is a selective COX2 inhibitor.
