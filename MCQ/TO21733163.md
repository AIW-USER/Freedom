## Question

A patient had Unicondylar knee arthroplasty (UKA) done 7 years back. He recently sustained a twisting knee injury to same knee. Correct statement in context of design of UKA design used is

## Options

1. Lateral UKA has more linear wear
2. Medial UKA has more dislocation rate
3. Mobile bearing UKA has more dislocation chances
4. Fixed bearing UKA has less linear wear

## Correct option

**3. Mobile bearing UKA has more dislocation chances**

## Answer explanation

Ans: C

EXPLANATION: -
UKA is a mode of treatment for isolated unicompartmental osteoarthritis of the knee (medial or lateral). It has two designs fixed bearing and mobile bearing based on if the polyethylene liner is designed to move in the anterior-posterior direction on the tibial tray with ROM or stay in place.
Mobile bearing UKA
Mobile bearing UKA is designed to replicate tibiofemoral biomechanics better than fixed bearing. In Mobile Bearing implants, the polyethylene insert is mobile, allowing some degree of tibial rotation over the femur, there is more congruent bearing surfaces with a large contact area and generates less contact stresses, this decreases the risk of aseptic loosening, polyethylene wear, and implant revision in the long term. However, the Mobile Bearing prosthesis is technically more difficult to implant. Without precise alignment and ligament balancing, it can lead to bearing dislocation or impingement which is the most common complication in mobile bearing UKA.

![[TO21733163-1.png]]

Fixed bearing UKA
Fixed Bearing prosthesis often has a flat tibial articular surface, is easier to implant and there is no risk of bearing dislocation. In Fixed Bearing implants, the polyethylene inlay is fixed into the metal tibial plateau, allowing flexion, extension, and roll-back motion over a fixed insert. The Fixed Bearing prosthesis is less conforming as flexion occurs and can lead to point loading due to the flat tibial articular surface which can accelerate wear rates.

![[TO21733163-2.jpg]]

So mobile bearing UKA is prone to bearing dislocations and impingements while fixed bearing theoretically has more wear rates; which indirectly means mobile bearing-uka tends to fail in early post- operative years whereas fixed bearing-uka mostly fails in later postoperative years.
Lateral vs Medial UKA
In UKA options are available for both medial as well as isolated lateral compartment OA. Among the two Lateral UKA has been traditionally found to have more dislocation and failure rates. There are several biomechanical and anatomical characteristics in the lateral knee compartment that may be responsible for this: the lateral collateral ligament allows distraction of the lateral compartment in flexion of up to a mean of 6.7 mm, compared with 2 mm in the medial compartment. Furthermore, the anatomical shape of the tibial condyle in the medial compartment is flat, whereas the lateral tibial condyle is convex. Additionally, in high flexion, the centre of the femoral condyle in the medial compartment stays central on the tibial plateau, whereas the lateral femoral condyle subluxes posteriorly and inferiorly off the back of the tibial plateau. These factors make Lateral UKA susceptible for dislocation. However, the advent of newer implant designs have provided equivalent practical results of both medial and lateral UKA. Latest studies mostly conclude with no significant differences observed between the two treatment options with respect to survival rate, pain relief, and functional improvement.

HTO vs UKA
There are two procedure options available for the treatment of unicompartmental osteoarthritis of the knee when nonsurgical treatment methods fail: high tibial osteotomy (HTO) or unicompartmental knee arthroplasty (UKA). HTO has traditionally been used for correction of a knee angular deformity or metaphyseal tibial malalignment in patients less than 60 years old where following pre requisites are met: -

- active patient
- mild-to-moderate varus knee (5 to 15 degrees)
- less than grade 3 medial compartment involvement
- intact lateral and patellofemoral compartments
- knee flexion >120 degrees
- no joint laxity or instability

Over time indications for HTO have expanded to include posterolateral laxity and varus hyperextension thrust, anterior cruciate ligament (ACL) deficiency (slope modification is used to the advantage of reducing translation).
However, in scenarios where there is a radiological bone to bone kissing lesion of the condyles with established cartilage wear UKA is preferred over HTO in the setting of a good ligamentous status and stability in monocompartmental disease. And if there is KL grade 4 OA with involvement of other compartments, ligamentous instability or gross coronal malalignement, TKA is the preferred choice.
