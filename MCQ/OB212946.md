## Question

An 8-month-old boy was brought in by his parents for refusing to use his right arm after a fall off of the couch earlier in the evening. The patient's hand is otherwise well-perfused. Current imaging is shown in Figure A. After immobilization of the extremity which of the following is the next best step in treatment?

![[q212946-a-transphyseal-separation.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) of the elbow
2. Computed tomography (CT) of the elbow
3. CT of the head
4. Emergent fixation in the operating room
5. Contacting child protective services

## Correct option

**5. Contacting child protective services**

## Answer explanation

This patient sustained a distal humeral physeal separation. The fracture pattern should raise suspicion for non-accidental trauma, therefor child protective services should be contacted next.

Transphyseal distal humerus fractures typically occur in children younger than 3 years of age. Non-accidental trauma must always be considered as a possible etiology, as this has been cited as the most common mechanism of injury. Prior to ossification of the capitellum, this injury can be identified by recognizing that the forearm is not aligned with the humerus and is often displaced posteromedially on plain radiographs. Prompt recognition and closed reduction with percutaneous pinning is necessary in order to optimize outcomes. Long-term complications include cubitus varus, osteonecrosis of the medial condyle, and growth arrest.

*Supakul et al. reviewed 16 cases of distal humerus epiphyseal separation.* **They found** that the diagnosis was missed on plain radiographs of over one-half of the patients. With the addition of ultrasound, diagnostic accuracy was increased to 100%. **The authors also noted** that non-accidental trauma was the most common mechanism of injury. **They concluded** that the diagnosis of distal humeral physeal separation is often missed on radiographs and ultrasound should be considered as an adjunct modality to improve the diagnostic accuracy of these injuries.

*Abzug et al. performed an instructional course review on transphyseal distal humerus fractures.* **They noted** that the usual mechanisms of injury were birth trauma, nonaccidental trauma, and fall from a small height. **They noted** a prompt and accurate diagnosis is necessary to optimize outcomes and that surgical management should be performed with the aid of an arthrogram. **They concluded** that cubitus varus was the most common complication of this injury.

Figure A is a radiograph of a transphyseal distal humerus fracture. The key to the diagnosis is noting the forearm is not aligned with the humerus.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: MRI of the elbow is not necessary for the diagnosis or treatment of this injury. Additionally, pediatric patients would typically require sedation.
Answer 2: CT of the elbow is not required for the diagnosis or treatment of this injury. Furthermore, performing a CT poses the risk of unnecessary radiation exposure to a pediatric patient.
Answer 3: CT of the head is not routinely performed in pediatric patients but may be ordered if there was a concern for head trauma.
Answer 4: Transphyseal humeral separations do not require emergent treatment in the absence of open injuries or neurovascular compromise.
