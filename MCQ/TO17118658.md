Chapter: Arthroscopy MCQs — ACL

## Question

True about revision ACL reconstruction is

## Options

1. Staged reconstruction for tunnel dilatation more than 10 mm
2. Removal of femoral screw is necessary
3. Poor graft selection is most common cause
4. Improper tunnel placement is most common cause

## Correct option

**4. Improper tunnel placement is most common cause**

## Answer explanation

Revision ACL reconstruction: technical considerations-

- Staged revision surgery should be considered if there is a problem with knee motion, specifically a lack of 5 degrees of extension or 20 degrees of flexion, when stability is not the problem.
- If large bony defects are present that cannot be adequately treated at the time of revision surgery, staged procedures should be considered. It is recommended that a staged ACL reconstruction should be done with bone graft of the tunnel in the first stage for a tunnel more than 15 mm wide.
- Previous incisions should be used or extended if possible. Skin bridges of less than 7 cm wide should be avoided to prevent problems with wound healing.
- Fixation should be removed only if necessary. Unnecessary removal creates defects that require treatment.
- A bioabsorbable screw can still be present 2 years or more after surgery because the
- Polylactic acid used to make the screws is not readily absorbable within this short period. Attempts to remove a bioabsorbable screw may cause fragmentation. It is best to leave it intact if possible or to ream through part or all of it if it is not readily removable.
- Prosthetic ligaments should be removed end bloc to avoid fragmentation of synthetic graft
- Generous revision notchplasty may be indicated. Adequacy of the notchplasty should be assessed after graft placement.
- The most frequently cited reason for failure of the primary anterior cruciate ligament reconstruction is inappropriate tunnel positioning.

Answer: 4
