## Answer explanation

Lymphocytes, the primary cell type in lymphomas, stain positively for CD20 and CD45 and are characterized by small, round, blue cells on histology (Answer 1).

Lymphomas are hematopoietic malignancies most commonly seen in patients between the ages of 35 and 55, characterized by small, round blue cells on histology. Patients can present with pain not relieved by rest, pathologic fractures, and B symptoms (fevers, chills, night sweats, and weight loss). Lymphoma of bone on radiographs can demonstrate mottled and lytic lesions. A biopsy is required to confirm the diagnosis and will show small, round blue cells that stain positive for CD20 and CD45. Treatment predominantly consists of chemotherapy +/- radiation and does not typically require surgical intervention except in the setting of pathologic or impending fracture.

*Ventre et al. performed a retrospective review of 161 patients with diffuse large B-cell lymphoma of the bone to determine the most appropriate treatment for this patient population.* **They found** that patients who were treated with primary chemotherapy regardless of receipt of subsequent radiotherapy had improved outcomes compared to those who received primary radiotherapy with or without subsequent chemotherapy. Additionally, **they found** that patients who received primary chemotherapy did not have significantly improved outcomes if they received additional radiotherapy thereafter. **They concluded** that patients who received anthracycline-based chemotherapy had the most favorable prognosis.

*Ferreri et al. reviewed 20 patients with primary bone lymphoma to determine the outcomes of Adriamycin-based chemotherapy and subsequent radiotherapy.* **They reported** that all patients with monostotic disease achieved complete remission compared to four of eight with polyostotic disease. Survival rate for patients with monostotic disease was 92% at 50 months compared to 25% at 40 months for those with polyostotic disease. **They concluded** that chemotherapy and radiotherapy in monostotic disease were more effective in preventing local relapse compared to the use of radiotherapy alone.

*Rezazadeh et al. published a retrospective review of patients with primary bone diffuse large B-cell lymphoma to determine the impact of additional radiotherapy following rituximab therapy.* **They found** that there were no differences in overall survival or relapse-free survival in patients who underwent radiotherapy compared to those who did not after rituximab chemotherapy. Notably, five of six patients who received radiotherapy after achieving only a partial response to immunotherapy subsequently reached a complete response. **The authors concluded** that radiotherapy following rituximab therapy was not associated with a broad survival benefit.
