## Question

Which of the following factors has been shown to be the strongest predictor of screw cutout of a sliding hip screw used for an intertrochanteric femur fracture?

## Options

1. Age of the patient
2. Intrinsic stability of the fracture
3. Tip-apex distance
4. Quality of reduction
5. Angle of the sideplate

## Correct option

**3. Tip-apex distance**

## Answer explanation

Illustration A found below depicts the method to calculate tip-apex distance (TAD). The tip-apex distance is the sum of the distances from the tip of the lag screw to the apex of the femoral head as seen on the AP and lateral radiographs.

*Baumgaertner et al* **reported** that he had no screws cut out if the tip-apex distance was less than 25mm. Tip-apex distance was the strongest predictor of cutout. The increasing patient age, poor reduction, use of a high-angle sideplate, and unstable fracture were weaker predictors of cutout.

*Kyle et al* **demonstrated** that obtaining an anatomic reduction using a sliding hip screw with intertrochanteric fractures leads to the best radiographic and clinical outcomes.
