## Correct option

**3. Figure C**

## Answer explanation

Figure C is a chest radiograph showing multiple posterior rib fractures with callus formation in different stages of healing. This is highly suggestive of non-accidental trauma, the updated term reflecting the entity previously referred to as child abuse.

Abuse is the second most common cause of death in children after sudden infant death syndrome (1-5mths) and accidental injury ( >1year). The most common manifestation is skin trauma (bruising, burns), the 2nd most common is fractures. The most common mechanism is shaking. The most common cause of death is head trauma. Signs of abuse include multiple fractures, especially rib fractures (70% chance of abuse), femur fractures before walking age, humerus shaft fracture <3 years, and an infant or toddler with a skull fracture.

*Sink et al. reviewed orthopedic manifestations of child abuse.* Risk was greatest for first-born children, unplanned children, premature infants, stepchildren, handicapped children, <1year old, male (59%), and those with Medicaid or no insurance. Mean age of abuse was 6.6 months. Most abusers were male and 45% were biologic fathers. Shaking is the most common cause of intracranial injuries. Other highly specific fractures include fractures of the posterior ribs, scapular process, spinous process and sternum. Diaphyseal fractures are more common than metaphyseal fractures in abuse (but less specific).

*Pandya et al. retrospectively compared 500 cases of child abuse to 985 cases of accidental trauma at a pediatric trauma center.* Child abuse victims were younger than accidental trauma patients. In patients <18 months, the odds of rib, tibia/fibula, humerus and femur fractures were significantly higher than the non-abuse group. In patients >18 months (walking age), the risk of humerus and femur fractures was higher in the accidental trauma group.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Figure A is an AP radiograph of the knee showing growth plate widening and flaring and fraying of the metaphyses of the knee. This occurs in rickets because of defects in mineralization.
Answer 2: Figure B is a lateral skull radiograph showing wormian bones (intrasutural bones). This is seen in normal skulls (10% in Caucasians, 80% in Asians), osteogenesis imperfecta and rickets.
Answer 4: Figure D is a lateral radiograph of the knee showing bisphosphonate or Park Harris lines. This occurs following treatment for osteogenesis imperfecta.
Answer 5: Figure E is a lateral leg radiograph showing infantile cortical hyperostosis (Caffey's disease). This condition is characterized by solid wavy periosteal reaction along the shafts of long bones, clavicles, mandible, and scapulae.
