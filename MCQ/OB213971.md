## Question

A 65-year-old woman presents to your clinic for evaluation of her right shoulder. She tripped and fell on ice 2 weeks prior and sustained an anterior shoulder dislocation. She underwent a reduction at a local emergency room on the day of the injury and now presents to your clinic in a sling. Which of the following structures is most likely to be injured in this patient?

## Options

1. Axillary nerve
2. Suprascapular nerve at the spinoglenoid notch
3. Suprascapular nerve at the suprasccapular notch
4. Infraspinatous
5. Subscapularis

## Correct option

**4. Infraspinatous**

## Answer explanation

The most common injury in the elderly population following a traumatic anterior glenohumeral shoulder dislocation is a rotator cuff tear. Of the 4 rotator cuff muscles, the supraspinatous, infraspinatous and teres minor (SIT) are the most likely to sustain an acute injury following a dislocation event.

Rotator cuff tears in the elderly population are generally noted to be due to degenerative conditions, chronic impingement and acute trauma with or without a glenohumeral dislocatIon. In the setting of an anterior glenohumeral dislocation in a patient > age 40, a rotator cuff tear is most likely to involve the SIT muscles. The subscapularis can also be torn but this is more likely in the younger patient due to hyperabduction and external rotation force. Axillary nerve injuries may also occur after a glenohumeral dislocation event (7-10% cases), although in a much lesser frequency than a rotator cuff injury.

*Paxton et al. reviewed glenohumeral instability in patients over the age of 40.* **They note** that glenohumeral dislocations in older patients often present with complex injury patterns, including rotator cuff tears, fractures, and neurovascular injuries. **The authors conclude** that glenohumeral instability in older patients requires a stepwise decision-making process necessary to treat this injury pattern.

*Neviaser et al. reviewed 31 patients who sustained a primary anterior dislocation of the glenohumeral joint who were subsequently found to have a ruptured rotator cuff.* All of the patients were more than 35 years old with 29 initially presumed to have axillary nerve injury (only 4 actually had an axillary nerve injury). The association between primary anterior dislocation of the glenohumeral joint and rupture of the rotator cuff in the older patient who cannot abduct the arm after reduction is poorly appreciated and is often missed. Overall, **the authors concluded** that the incidence of injury to the axillary nerve was 7.8% in their cohort, as compared with 100% for the rupture of the rotator cuff.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: While axillary nerve injuries do occur after a glenohumeral dislocation, these are far less common than rotator cuff injuries.
Answers 2 and 3: Suprascapular nerve injuries are not common after glenohumeral dislocations.
Answer 5: While subscapularis tears may occur after glenohumeral dislocations, these injuries are less common than tears to the other 3 rotator cuff muscles.
