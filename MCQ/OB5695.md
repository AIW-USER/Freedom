## Question

Which of the following is INCORRECT regarding closed reduction and percutaneous pinning of a pediatric supracondylar humerus fracture using a lateral entry technique with 2 or 3 pins?

## Options

1. It is the preferred first line treatment for a displaced supracondylar humerus fracture
2. It has Level 1 evidence to support its superiority over the use of both medial and lateral entry pins
3. It has equal radiographic outcomes compared to the use of both medial and lateral entry pins
4. It has equal range-of-motion outcomes compared to the use of both medial and lateral entry pins
5. It causes fewer ulnar nerve injuries than the use of both medial and lateral entry pins

## Correct option

**2. It has Level 1 evidence to support its superiority over the use of both medial and lateral entry pins**

## Answer explanation

Level 1 evidence in the form of a prospective randomized controlled trial (RCT) comparing lateral entry pinning with medial and lateral pinning demonstrates no difference on all outcome measures.

Displaced supracondylar humerus fractures are amongst the most common pediatric fracture patterns. Completely displaced, Gartland Type III, fractures are indicated for closed reduction and percutaneous pinning as first line treatment, with many advocating for operative management of displaced Type II fractures as well. Open treatment may be required if unable to achieve adequate reduction closed. The preferred fixation method is 2-3 laterally introduced divergent pins to achieve stability. Retrospective, prospective, and RCT studies have all been conducted showing no worse outcomes with lateral entry pinning, and there is a presumed lower risk of ulnar nerve injury. However, especially in cases of medial column comminution, there may be some biomechanical advantage and need for a medial entry pin in some minority of cases.

*Skaggs et al in 2004 reported a prospective series of 124 consecutively managed children including both Gartland Type II and Gartland Type III fractures.* **They report** no patients with loss of reduction, no clinically evident deformity or loss of motion, and no iatrogenic nerve palsies.

*Kocher et al in 2007 reported a prospective RCT comparing lateral entry vs lateral and medial entry pinning.* *This study was powered to detect a 10% difference in loss of reduction rate, and similar to prior prospective work, found no major loss of reduction, no statistically significant rate difference in minor loss of reduction (6 out of 28 lateral entry group, 1 out of 24 cross-pinning group), no iatrogenic nerve injury, and no significant differences in range of motion or clinical deformity outcomes.*

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1, 3, 4, 5 are all true based on the reported literature.
Answer 2 is the Correct answer because there is no Level 1 Evidence demonstrating superiority of lateral pinning over cross-pinning, though lateral pinning has become the standard of care and preferred technique in most places due to the theoretically reduced risk of ulnar nerve injury.
