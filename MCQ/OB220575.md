## Question

A 35-year-old male presents for preoperative discussion before transfemoral amputation reconstruction with an osseointegration implant. Which of the following is the most common postoperative complication?

## Options

1. Inadequate osseointegration
2. High-grade infection requiring debridement or implant removal
3. Low-grade infection that can be managed without surgical debridement
4. Peri-implant fracture
5. Soft tissue redundancy

## Correct option

**3. Low-grade infection that can be managed without surgical debridement**

## Answer explanation

A low-grade soft tissue infection is the most common postoperative complication following an amputation reconstruction with osseointegration. These can typically be managed with oral antibiotics (Answer 3).

Osseointegration is a technique in which an implant is inserted directly into the residual bone of an amputee and then passed transcutaneously to a prosthesis. These have been described primarily for transtibial and transfemoral amputations. This has the advantage of eliminating socket-related problems as the prosthesis is anchored directly in bone. Patients who undergo osseointegration have reported increased prosthesis wear, improved mobility, quality of life, and extremity proprioception compared to those with socket prostheses. The most common postoperative complication is soft tissue infection. These can most often be treated with simple measures, and severe infections are overall rare.

*Hoellwarth et al. published a technique article on implantation and soft tissue management of press-fit osseointegration implants for patients with transfemoral amputations.* *They go on to comment that the most common postoperative complication following this procedure is a low-grade soft tissue infection that can be managed with oral antibiotics.*

*Marano et al. published a retrospective review of patients undergoing osseointegration for transfemoral amputation using their technique of soft tissue management, which included purse-string muscular closure and soft tissue contouring.* *They reviewed a total of fourteen patients.* Two cases of low-grade soft tissue infection were managed with oral antibiotics, and no cases of severe infection that required surgical management were reported. **The authors concluded** that proper soft tissue management and planning are critical in the care of patients undergoing osseointegration procedures.

*Al Muderis et al. conducted a prospective observational study of patients undergoing two-stage osseointegration procedures for transfemoral amputations.* *They enrolled a total of 86 patients with 91 implants.* The most common complication was a low-grade soft tissue infection that developed in a third of patients. This was defined as cellulitis with signs of inflammation. Other complications included: stoma hypergranulation, soft-tissue redundancy, proximal femoral fracture, inadequate osseointegration, and implant failure.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1-2,4-5: While these complications can occur following osseointegration procedures, their incidence is lower than that of low-grade infections.
