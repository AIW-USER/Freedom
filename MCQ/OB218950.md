## Question

A 21-year-old male snowboarder presents to your clinic one week after returning from vacation. He states that he sustained a bad fall on the last day of the trip when he caught an edge snowboarding. He notes that his foot twisted within his snowboarding boots and bindings, and since the fall, he has had difficulty bearing weight. A select image from his CT scan is available for review in Figure A. Given his injury and radiographic findings, which of the following is the best course of treatment for this patient?

![[q218950-a-ltpf.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Bracing and initiation of physical therapy with a focus on proprioception
2. Immobilization in a hard-soled shoe for 6 weeks
3. Immobilization in a short-leg cast for 6 weeks
4. Fragment excision
5. Operative fixation

## Correct option

**5. Operative fixation**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a displaced fracture of the lateral process of the talus (which has been coined a snowboarder's fracture). Given the degree of displacement and lack of comminution, the optimal treatment for this patient is operative fixation (Answer 5).

While the talar neck is the most common site of fracture of the talus, fractures can also occur in the body, lateral process, posterior process and/or head. Injuries to the talar body, which constitute 13-23% of all talus fractures, typically occur secondary to high-energy trauma. Lateral process fractures, which account for approximately 10% of all talus fractures, occur secondary to forced dorsiflexion, axial load, and inversion with an external rotation force. Given the position and relative immobility of the foot in snowboard boots and bindings, these injuries commonly occur in snowboarders and have been coined "snowboarder's fractures". Lateral process fractures are often misdiagnosed as ankle sprains, and a failure to carefully evaluate ankle radiographs can lead to this injury being missed. Computerized tomography (CT) is the advanced imaging modality of choice for determining the degree of displacement and comminution present or absent in lateral process fractures. Optimal treatment is dependent on the degree of displacement and the presence or absence of communication. Nondisplaced (<2mm displacement) fractures are treated with immobilization in a short-leg cast for 6 weeks. It is important that the cast is molded to support the longitudinal arch. Displaced fractures (>2mm displacement) are treated with ORIF/Kirschner wire fixation and comminuted fractures are often treated with fragment excision. Although these fractures may appear benign on imaging, posttraumatic subtalar arthritis develops in approximately 45% of patients, regardless of the treatment modality chosen. Thankfully, with prompt diagnosis and immediate initiation of the appropriate treatment, patients demonstrate favorable functional outcomes independent of the development of radiographic osteoarthritis.

*Kirkpatrick et al. provide one of the earliest studies of injury patterns in snowboarders.* *Their study population consisted of 3,213 snowboarding injuries that occurred between 1988 and 1995.* Overall, 491 (15.3%) of injuries involved the ankle and 58 (1.8%) involved the foot. Of the foot injuries, 33 (57%) were fractures. **The authors noted** that an unexpectedly high number of fractures of the lateral process of the talus were observed and that these injuries (74 total) represented 2.3% of all snowboarding injuries and 34% of all foot/ankle fractures. **The authors conclude** that the appropriate diagnosis of these fractures is paramount and that CT is often required to fully identify and characterize these injuries.

*McCrory et al. provide a review of fractures of the lateral process of the talus.* **The authors note** that this injury most likely occurs secondary to a combination of dorsiflexion and inversion of the ankle and that these fractures are often missed and misdiagnosed as ankle sprains. **The authors conclude** that secondary to the long-term risk of subtalar osteoarthritis, aggressive early management of these injuries is warranted.

Figure A demonstrates a fracture of the lateral process of the talus with 3mm of displacement.

Incorrect Answers
Answers 1 and 2: This is inappropriate for a lateral process of the talus fracture. The appropriate immobilization, if this fracture was displaced <2mm, would be immobilization in a short-leg cast.
Answer 3: This would be the correct treatment for a minimally (<2mm) displaced lateral process fracture.
Answer 4: A displaced, non-comminuted lateral process of the talus fracture is best treated with operative fragment fixation. This would be the appropriate treatment for a highly comminuted and displaced lateral process fracture.
