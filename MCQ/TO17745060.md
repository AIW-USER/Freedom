## Question

During TKR, the surgeon finds a tight extension gap and loose flexion gap. Most appropriate measure for this is (arthroplasty- knee arthroplasty- coronal plane balancing)

## Options

1. Downsizer femur and slope tibia
2. Distal femoral cut and thicker poly
3. Downsize femur and thicker poly
4. Resect tibia and thicker poly

## Correct option

**2. Distal femoral cut and thicker poly**

## Answer explanation

Troubleshooting in TKR: In gap balancing, ligaments are released before femoral bone cuts are made.

Steps of gap balancing:

-Remove all osteophytes

- Precise tibial cut

- Ligaments are released before femoral cut

 Remember:

- Distal femoral cut affects only extension gap

- Posterior femoral cut affects flexion gap

- Tibial cut affects both flexion and extension cut

- Increasing/decreasing the size of the femoral component only changes the AP diameter and therefore affects the flexion gap only.

Flexion and extension gaps should be rectangular and equal to each other.

Tight in flexion
Balanced in flexion
Loose in flexion

Tight in extension
Cut more proximal tibia
Cut more distal femur

Release of posterior capsule
Cut additional distal femur and use thicker tibial spacer

Balanced in extension
Cut more posterior tibial slope

Downsize femoral component and use same tibial spacer
You are done
-Cut additional distal femur and use thicker tibial spacer

-Increase size of femoral component and use same size tibial spacer

-Recut tibia with less posterior slope and use thicker tibial spacer

Loose in extension
Down size femoral component and use thicker tibial spacer
-Down size femoral component and use thicker tibial spacer
