## Question

A 56-year-old left-handed computer programmer from rural New Hampshire presents with 1 year of right-hand discomfort and progressive flexion contracture. While he had usually enjoyed LARPing, he has not had an interest in social activities since he lost all of his money on a short-squeeze of a popular gaming stock. He does not recall a specific traumatic event. On exam, the entire hand is tender to palpation. Any type of passive motion elicits a significant amount of pain throughout the hand and volar forearm, but all compartments are soft and supple. (Figure A) All imaging and electrodiagnostic studies are normal. The wise hand surgeon recommends simultaneous aggressive therapy and psychiatric support. What diagnosis is most likely in this unfortunate patient?

*Figure A*

## Options

1. Clenched fist syndrome
2. Volkmann contracture
3. Cerebral Palsy
4. Lyme Disease
5. Larsen's Syndrome

## Correct option

**1. Clenched fist syndrome**

## Answer explanation

Clenched fist syndrome (CFS) is a non-organic somatoform disorder.

CFS is a rare clinical condition that presents with fixed flexion contractures of the fingers and is often associated with psychiatric pathology. Associated conditions may include self-cutting, lymphedema of the hand, and other psychologically motivated problems. CFS can be difficult to differentiate from Complex Regional Pain Syndrome (CRPS). However, unlike CRPS, CFS may present with a clenched fist, macerated and foul-smelling palm, and paradoxical stiffness.

*Petrella et al. review 20 patients with the diagnosis of CFS.* *The average age was 56.* *The authors classified three types: 1.* Metacarpal Phalangeal and Proximal Interphalangeal Joint (MCPJ and PIPJ) contractures of the ulnar two digits without index or thumb involvement; 2. Distal Interphalangeal Joint (DIPJ) and PIPJ contractures of the ulnar digits; 3. Flexion of all of the long fingers; 4. flexion of all of the fingers and the thumb; 5. isolated flexus-adductus of the thumb; 6. Flexion of digits with associated upper extremity contractures. Correction of the deformities was obtained in all cases and maintained over time.

Simmons and Vasile review CFS, which can be seen in both dominant and non-dominant hands and often follows a minor incident event and is associated with pain, swelling, dysfunction, and significant stiffness. The condition is associated with a psychiatric disorder and the authors note a poor prognosis.

Figure A demonstrates a typical presentation of Clenched fist syndrome.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Volkmann contracture is associated with compartment syndrome of the forearm.
Answer 3: Cerebral Palsy is a nonprogressive upper motor neuron disease due to injury to an immature brain.
Answer 4: Lyme Disease is a systemic infection with the spirochete Borrelia burgdorferi transmitted by the bite of an infected deer tick. Lyme disease may cause intermittent arthritis, joint swelling, but not the symptoms in this patient.
Answer 5: Larsen's Syndrome is a rare genetic disorder with characteristic findings of ligamentous hyperlaxity, abnormal facial features, and multiple joint dislocations.
