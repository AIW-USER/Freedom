## Question

A 12-year-old boy with a diagnosis of Fragile X syndrome presents with bilateral flexible flatfeet and persistent pain despite conservative management with orthotics and physical therapy. Physical examination reveals an abducted foot posture, collapse of the medial arch, and hindfoot valgus alignment. Radiographs show normal skeletal anatomy without evidence of bony coalition or deformity. An isolated calcaneal slide osteotomy is planned. What would be the expected outcome if a medial cuneiform osteotomy is not performed concurrently?

## Options

1. Correction of both hindfoot valgus and forefoot supination
2. Improved arch height with lateral column shortening
3. Persistent forefoot supination
4. Persistent heel valgus
5. Reduction in pain with no change in foot alignment

## Correct option

**3. Persistent forefoot supination**

## Answer explanation

Persistent forefoot supination would result if a medial cuneiform osteotomy is not performed with a medial calcaneal slide osteotomy in this patient (answer choice 3).

For flexible pes planovalgus, early conservative management with orthotics and physical therapy is appropriate. Surgical intervention is appropriate when non-operative methods fail. An isolated calcaneal slide osteotomy effectively corrects hindfoot valgus but often leads to persistent forefoot supination due to an unbalanced plantar pressure distribution. To prevent this, a medial cuneiform osteotomy is performed to plantarflex the first ray, ensuring proper weight-bearing during the gait cycle. This combination helps to redistribute plantar pressures and improve the weight-bearing surface during gait.

*Bouchard et al. focuses on the surgical management of pediatric flatfoot deformity,* **emphasizing** the importance of proper patient selection and surgical techniques. It highlights that isolated soft-tissue procedures, such as tendon transfers, are rarely effective, and combining them with an osteotomy is often necessary for optimal correction. The calcaneal slide osteotomy is effective for correcting hindfoot valgus, but it can lead to forefoot supination when performed in isolation. Therefore, plantarflexion of the first ray via a medial cuneiform osteotomy is recommended to achieve a balanced plantar pressure distribution during the gait cycle.

*Davids et al. investigated the orthopedic manifestations of Fragile X syndrome, with a particular focus on flexible flatfeet, a common condition in affected individuals.* **The authors emphasize** the importance of early diagnosis and conservative management, such as orthotics and physical therapy, but also address the role of surgical intervention in cases of persistent pain. The study highlights the effectiveness of a calcaneal slide osteotomy combined with a medial cuneiform osteotomy to correct persistent forefoot supination and ensure proper weight-bearing alignment during gait.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: Correction of both hindfoot valgus and forefoot supination requires additional procedures like a medial cuneiform osteotomy.
Answer 2: Improved arch height with lateral column shortening is not the function of a calcaneal slide osteotomy.
Answer 4: Persistent hindfoot valgus would not occur, as the medial calcaneal slide osteotomy corrects this valgus deformity.
Answer 5: Reduction in pain with no change in foot alignment is unlikely, as the procedure directly alters foot mechanics.
