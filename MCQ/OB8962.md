## Question

Figure A shows a clinical photograph of an ambulatory 55-year-old diabetic male who presents with a persistent neuropathic plantar forefoot ulceration despite shoe modification and local wound care. Exam reveals a loss of protective sensation with 5.07 Semmes-Weinstein monofilament, less than 5 degrees of ankle dorsiflexion, a palpable posterior tibial pulse, and exposed tendon but no exposed bone. Which of the following is true with regard to total contact casting with percutaneous Achilles tendon lengthening compared total contact casting alone in this clinical scenario?

![[q8962-a-pt1112online2a.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Higher rate of neuropathic plantar forefoot ulcer recurrence at two years
2. Lower rate of neuropathic plantar forefoot ulcer recurrence at two years
3. Same rate of neuropathic plantar forefoot ulcer recurrence at two years
4. Higher rate of below knee amputation
5. Lower rate of heel pad skin breakdown

## Correct option

**2. Lower rate of neuropathic plantar forefoot ulcer recurrence at two years**

## Answer explanation

The patient has a Wagner stage II ulceration and limited ankle dorsiflexion. Ulceration recurrence rates are lower with Achilles tendon lengthening in conjunction with total contact casting compared to total contact casting alone.

Forefoot diabetic foot ulcers are thought to arise from a combination of loss of protective sensation as well as increase plantar pressures on the forefoot. Lack of ankle dorsiflexion leads to cyclical trauma to the forefoot with ambulation and ultimately presents as ulceration under the metatarsal heads. Important examination findings include depth of ulceration, vascular status, infection, and Achilles tendon contracture. Initial management consists of shoe modification, offloading, and local wound care. Total contact casting leads to reliable healing of forefoot ulcers by distributing peak pressures away from the forefoot. Those with limited dorsiflexion have lower rates of recurrence with Achilles tendon lengthening in addition to total contact casting.

*Mueller et al. randomized 64 subjects into two treatment groups, immobilization in a total-contact cast alone or combined with percutaneous Achilles tendon lengthening.* All ulcers healed in the Achilles tendon lengthening group, and the risk for ulcer recurrence was 75% less at seven months and 52% less at two years than the risk of recurrence in the total-contact cast alone group. **They concluded** that Achilles tendon lengthening decreases the risk of recurrent ulcers in patients with neuropathic plantar forefoot ulcers with limited dorsiflexion.

*Armstrong et al. measured forefoot mean peak pressures on 10 diabetic patients at risk for ulceration following percutaneous Achilles tendon lengthening.* **They found** a reduction in forefoot pressures and increased dorsiflexion following percutaneous Achilles lengthening. **They conclude** that forefoot mean peak pressures are decreased in diabetics at risk for ulceration following percutaneous Achilles lengthening.

Image A shows a clinical image of a Wagner stage II neuropathic plantar forefoot ulceration.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1 and 3: Achilles tendon lengthening in conjunction with total contact casting has been shown to decrease rates of recurrent plantar foot ulcers.
Answer 4: There is not an increased rate of below-knee amputation following Achilles tendon lengthening.
Answer 5: Achilles tendon lengthening may result in increased peak pressures in the heel and lead to higher rates of heel pad skin breakdown or irritation compared to total contact casting alone.
