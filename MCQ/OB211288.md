## Question

A 65-year-old patient with a history of type 2 diabetes presents to clinic after sustaining a distal radius fracture secondary to a ground-level fall. Upon evaluation of the patient’s risk factors for osteoporosis, you note that they are taking a thiazolidinedione as part of their treatment regimen for diabetes. What is the mechanism of action of this medication in relation to bone mineral density (BMD)?

## Options

1. Inhibition of PPAR-gamma leads to decreased osteoblast function and a decrease in BMD
2. Activation of PPAR-gamma leads to a greater allocation of adipocyte formation from mesenchymal stem cells and suppression of osteoblastogenesis with an overall decrease in BMD
3. Inhibition of PPAR-gamma leads to increased RANKL formation and an increase in BMD
4. Inhibition of GSK-3-mediated b-Catenin phosphorylation leads to decreased osteoblast differentiation and a decrease in BMD
5. Activation of ATF4 which leads to inhibition of Runx2 and a decrease in BMD from suppression of osteoblastogenesis

## Correct option

**2. Activation of PPAR-gamma leads to a greater allocation of adipocyte formation from mesenchymal stem cells and suppression of osteoblastogenesis with an overall decrease in BMD**

## Answer explanation

Thiazolidinediones (TZDs) are agonists of peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor-γ (PPAR-γ) which has been reported to lead to an increase in bone marrow adiposity and a decrease in osteoblastogenesis, leading to an overall decrease in BMD.

TZDs have been demonstrated to improve insulin sensitivity and currently represent a widely prescribed elective treatment for type 2 diabetes. They increase insulin sensitivity by their binding to the nuclear receptor and transcription factor, PPAR-γ. Activation of PPAR-γ by TZDs causes an increase in bone marrow adiposity and a decrease in osteoblastogenesis, resulting in reduced bone formation. Recent clinical data have found that intake of thiazolidinediones by older patients with type 2 diabetes correlates with both the decrease of bone mineral density in the femoral neck and hip and a higher risk for fractures.

Falchetti, Masi, Brandia 2007 provided a review of the literature of TZDs and their effect on bone. They report their action is mediated by the binding to PPAR-γ, which regulates the activity of other transcription factors in the adipogenic differentiation and inflammatory response pathways. They conclude that health care providers should carefully check the existence of risk factors for osteoporosis and fractures in their patients before selecting them for thiazolidinediones treatment.

Marie 2008 published an excellent review summarizing transcription factors that control osteoblastogenesis. The roles of Runx2, the principal transcriptional regulator of osteoblast differentiation, Osterix, beta-Catenin, and ATF which act downstream of Runx2, and other transcription factors that contribute to the control of osteoblastogenesis including the AP1, C/EBPs, PPAR-γ and homeodomain, helix-loop-helix proteins are discussed.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1 and 3: Thiazolidinediones (TZDs) are agonists of PPAR-γ, not antagonists
Answer 4: While the mechanism of action of TZDs regarding osteoblastogenesis suppression is not fully understood, it is thought that transcription factors that upregulate osteoblast formation are inhibited. Nonetheless, inhibition of GSK-3-mediated b-Catenin phosphorylation would lead to an INCREASE (not a decrease) in b-Catenin accumulation, which would increase osteoblast formation and thus BMD.
Answer 5: Activation of ATF4 would lead to an increase in osteoblastogenesis (not a decrease) through its interaction with Runx2.
