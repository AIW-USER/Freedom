## Question

All are poor prognostic factors for Osteosarcoma, except

## Options

1. Presence of heat shock protein in urine
2. High level of Proteoglycan C in serum
3. No change in DNA ploidy following chemotherapy
4. More than 90 percent tumour necrosis present in resected specimen

## Correct option

**4. More than 90 percent tumour necrosis present in resected specimen**

## Answer explanation

Answer: 4

The histologic response of osteosarcoma to (neoadjuvant) chemotherapy is commonly cited as an important prognostic factor. Among the evaluation indexes of chemotherapy effect, the tumour necrosis rate (typically graded as the percent necrosis of the tumor at the time of surgical resection) has the advantage of being able to be accurately evaluated and with good sensitivity. There is a significant correlation between tumour necrosis rate and prognosis of osteosarcoma and hence the best answer is D.

PROGNOSTIC FACTORS OF OSTEOSARCOMA

Osteosarcoma is a highly malignant bone tumor, characterized by its rapid growth rate, high local aggressiveness, and early metastasis to the lungs and distant bones. It has a predilection for adolescents and young adults aged between 15 and 25, and is more frequently diagnosed in males. Although osteosarcoma is a rare disease with an annual incidence of about 1–5 cases per million population, it is the most common primary (non-haematological) malignant bone cancer with a low overall survival (OS) rate.

The introduction of adjuvant and neoadjuvant chemotherapy have now led to 5-year survival rates of more than 60%. Among the possible prognostic factors, tumor size, metastatic disease at the time of diagnosis, histological grade, histologic response to neoadjuvant chemotherapy, and adequate surgical margins have consistently shown a strong correlation with survival in osteosarcoma patients.

Poor prognostic factors include: -

- advanced stage of disease (most predictive of survival)
- age > 40 at the time of diagnosis
- response to neoadjuvant chemotherapy (as judged by percent tumor necrosis of resected specimen)

- > 90% necrosis is a good prognostic indicator

- male sex
- tumour site and size
- expression of P-glycoprotein
- high ALP/LDH (significantly high LDH on initial labs may indicate the presence of metastases)
- vascular involvement
- positive surgical margins
