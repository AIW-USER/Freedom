## Question

A 9-year-old child is on follow-up for a lateral condyle fracture of the distal humerus. AP radiographs are taken on the date of injury and at 1 year postoperatively, shown in Figures A and B respectively. Which of the following statements is true?

## Options

1. The final range of motion of the elbow is not influenced by the size of the lateral spur.
2. Lateral spurring is common in patients treated surgically because of surgical disruption of the overlying periosteum.
3. Younger patients are more likely to develop larger spurs compared with older patients because of greater growth potential.
4. The size of the lateral spur is independent of the amount of initial fracture displacement.
5. Arthroscopic or open trimming of the lateral spur is recommended to prevent late cubitus varus tarda.

## Correct option

**1. The final range of motion of the elbow is not influenced by the size of the lateral spur.**

## Answer explanation

Lateral spurring of the elbow is common after lateral condyle fractures. Neither the size of the spur nor its presence influences the final ROM of the elbow.

Lateral condyle fractures are the 2nd most common pediatric elbow fractures (after supracondylar fractures). Fracture treatment depends on fracture displacement: (1) <2mm can be treated nonoperatively (but watched closely) and > 2mm require surgery. Bony overgrowth, or “lateral spurring,” is common and is associated with greater initial fracture displacement. Possible etiology includes inadequate reduction, lateral condylar physeal stimulation, or rotation of the distal fragment laterally, displacing a periosteal flap.

*Skak et al. reviewed deformity after 28 displaced lateral condyle fractures.* All humerii demonstrated increased width. Varus deformity was present in 21% and valgus deformity was present in 11%.

*Pribaz et al. examined the presence of spurring in 212 lateral condyle fractures.* Spurs formed in fractures with mean initial displacement of 3.3 mm vs 1.1 mm in those that did not. Those with severe spurring had greater initial fracture displacement. Spur formation did not influence the final arc of motion.

Figure A shows the initial width at 3 weeks. Figure B shows an increase in width at 1 year, indicative of lateral spurring.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Lateral spurring is more common in patients with larger initial fracture displacement and this cohort of patients are usually treated surgically. Spurring is present because of the larger fracture displacement, and is not a result of surgery.
Answer 3: The development of spurs is independent of patient age.
Answer 4: There is a correlation between initial fracture displacement and the size of the spur.
Answer 5: Asymptomatic spurring should not be treated. Spurring itself does not predispose to cubitus varus.
