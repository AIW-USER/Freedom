## Correct option

## Answer explanation

Transverse femoral shaft fractures are considered a fracture pattern predictive of nonaccidental trauma (NAT) amongst patients 3 or under (Figure D).

Pediatric femur fractures are one of the most common fractures noted in the pediatric population and are the most common reason for pediatric hospitalization due to an orthopedic injury. These injuries in children 3 or under should raise suspicion for NAT, with a special emphasis on children who are non-ambulatory. There are specific patterns within the femur that further raise suspicion for NAT: including fractures at the junction of metaphysis and physis, metaphyseal corner fractures and transverse shaft fractures.

*Murphy et al. evaluated 95 patients (ages 3 or younger) who sustained femur fractures, of which 51 had either a child protective service (CPS) or a child assessment program (CAP) consult due to suspected NAT.* Thirteen (25%) were found to have NAT as determined by CPS/CAP and seven (14%) had indeterminate CPS/CAP investigations. Patients who had NAT had significantly decreased mean anteroposterior fracture ratios compared with those who had confirmed accidental trauma (p < 0.0001). **The authors conclude** that while not diagnostic, the presence of a transverse diaphyseal femoral fracture in a young child should raise the index of suspicion for NAT.

*Scherl et al. performed a retrospective review of 207 patients younger than 6 years of age (average age 2.7) who sustained non-pathologic diaphyseal femur fractures with an emphasis on accidental trauma versus NAT.* Seventy-six cases were investigated for child abuse with 13 investigations being positive. Overall, the morphologic features of the fractures were transverse (38%), spiral (27%), and oblique (17%) and in the investigated group, 27% were transverse, 39% were spiral, and 15% were oblique. In those cases with positive NAT results, 36% of the fractures were transverse, 36% were spiral, and 7% were oblique. **The authors concluded** that while spiral fractures are viewed as particularly suspicious, they were over-represented in the cohort of cases investigated and they may lead to missed cases of NAT in children with transverse fractures.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1, 2, 3, 5: Long oblique, comminuted and spiral pediatric femoral shaft fractures are not as predictive of NAT as transverse femur shaft fractures.
