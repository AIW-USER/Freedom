## Answer explanation

Symptomatic enchondroma of the distal phalanx can be treated with curettage and bone grafting alone.

Enchondromas are benign cartilaginous tumors of the bone located within the medullary canal. They're most commonly found in the hands and feet. Typically these do not require any form of treatment, as they are benign, however, they can become rarely symptomatic and can cause pathologic fractures. In these instances, curettage and bone grafting are indicated. Local recurrence is unusual after treatment.

*Lubahn and Bachoura reviewed enchondromas of the hand.* **They discuss** that the mainstay of treatment is curettage with bone grafting. Also, **they argue** that distinguishing between low-grade chondrosarcoma and an enchondroma is unnecessary as treatment is the same. Common postoperative complications include joint stiffness and soft-tissue deformity, however, recurrence and malignant degeneration are both uncommon. Patients typically have full recovery postoperatively.

*Shimizu et al. reviewed 5 cases of distal phalanx enchondromas, which they treated in three cases with curettage and bone grafting via a palmar approach, one case with curettage and grafting via a dorsal approach, and one with DIPJ disarticulation.* *They had good results in their palmar approach with no tenderness or pain for those patients.* Additionally, the scar was not a limitation to daily activity.

*Ramos-Pascua et al. presented a case series of 11 distal phalanx symptomatic enchondromas with 5 patients undergoing surgery and 6 choosing nonoperative management.* *9 patients had pathologic fractures on presentation.* All fractures healed with or without surgery. Between groups **they found** similar pain scores at the final follow up, with all patients returning to work without limitation. **They conclude** that nonsurgical treatment of distal phalanx enchondromas is an option.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Ewing sarcoma should be treated with chemotherapy and wide resection.
Answer 2: A subungual exostosis can be treated with nail removal and marginal excision (exostectomy)
Answer 3: High-grade chondrosarcoma should be treated with wide resection and in this case likely ray resection.
Answer 5: Osteochondromas can be treated with marginal resection
