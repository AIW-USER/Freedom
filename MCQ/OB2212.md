## Question

Figure A shows a 10-year-old patient who presented following a pedestrian versus motor vehicle collision. A proximal tibial traction pin is placed in attempt to maintain length while the patient is stabilized for surgery. The patient later undergoes successful fracture fixation using retrograde flexible titanium nails with restoration of length, alignment, and rotation. Which of the following outcomes may result from this injury or treatment?

![[q2212-a-femoral-shaft-fracture.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Genu recurvatum deformity
2. Genu procurvatum deformity
3. Genu valgum deformity
4. Genu varum deformity
5. Patellar tendon rupture

## Correct option

**1. Genu recurvatum deformity**

## Answer explanation

This skeletally immature patient has a midshaft femur fracture and a proximal tibial traction pin placed; both of which may lead to tibial tubercle growth arrest and subsequent genu recurvatum deformity.

Femoral shaft fractures in pediatric patients can lead to tibial tubercle growth arrest and recurvatum deformity. This is thought to occur as either an iatrogenic injury to the proximal tibial physis following traction pin placement or as a Salter V type injury to the physis at the time of injury. Compression injuries to the nearby tibial physis are unavoidable but placing traction pins in the distal femur can help avoid iatrogenic injury to the proximal tibial physis. Subsequent deformities can be clinically significant and require epiphysiodesis to stop progression or osteotomy to correct the deformity.

*Hresko et al. reviewed 7 patients with physeal arrest associated with non-physeal injuries to the lower extremity.* **They found** 5 patients had proximal tibial growth arrest and 2 patients had distal femoral growth arrest. **They concluded** that nearby physes are at risk for unsuspected injury and knowledge of these patterns can help with prevention or early recognition of deformity.

*Bowler et al. report on two patients with proximal tibial growth arrest without the use of proximal tibial traction pins.* **They conclude** that recurvatum deformity is not necessarily iatrogenic from proximal tibia traction pin and may be from physeal injury at the time of original injury.

Figure A shows a midshaft femur fracture in a skeletally immature patient. Illustration A and B shows a clinical image and a radiograph of a patient with tibial tubercle growth arrest and subsequent genu recurvatum.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 2 and 3: Genu procurvatum and valgum are not commonly seen following femoral shaft fractures
Answer 4: Varus deformity of the fracture can occur if external fixation is used to treat these fractures.
Answer 5: Patellar tendon ruptures are not associated with femoral shaft fractures.
