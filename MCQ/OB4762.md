## Question

A 65-year-old community-ambulatory patient complains of constant left hip pain that affects her activities of daily living. Staging studies confirm multi-focal disease. ## Options

1. Radiotherapy.
2. Curettage, cemented dynamic hip screw fixation and radiotherapy.
3. Curettage, cancellous bone grafting, cephalomedullary fixation and radiotherapy.
4. Proximal femoral resection, replacement with allo-prosthetic composite and radiotherapy.
5. Cemented hemiarthroplasty and radiotherapy.

## Correct option

**5. Cemented hemiarthroplasty and radiotherapy.**

## Answer explanation

This patient has multiple myeloma with a large lytic lesion in the left femoral head and neck with a Mirels score of 12 (functional pain = 3; lytic mets = 3; greater than two-thirds diameter = 3; peritrochanteric region =3). Because her Mirels score is greater than or equal to 9, and her life expectancy is greater than 3 months, hemiarthroplasty or total hip arthroplasty is the most appropriate treatment.

The femur is the 3rd most common site of bone metastases (after spine and pelvis) and most common site of pathological fracture (50% femoral neck, 30% subtrochanteric, 20% intertrochanteric). The goal of surgical treatment is to create an immediately stable construct that will allow immediate full weight bearing and normal function.

*Bickels et al. reviewed the surgical management of metastatic bone disease.* The aims of surgery are local tumor control (usually with radiotherapy) and immediate mechanical stability. They prefer removal of tumor through a cortical window using curets and high-speed burrs, followed by cemented intramedullary fixation devices (preferred because of their superior ability to withstand mechanical load). If there is extreme cortical destruction, **they advocate** resection and reconstruction with a cemented tumor prosthesis.

Figure A is a slide showing multiple myeloma. Figures B and C show a large lytic defect of the femoral neck and head. Illustration A shows a placement of a cemented cephalomedullary device after curretage through a bone window. Illustration B shows a cemented bipolar hemiarthroplasty for a femoral neck lesion. Illustration C shows DHS failure when used for peritrochanteric metastasis.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Radiotherapy alone will not protect the femoral neck from fracture. Radiotherapy is used as an adjunct to operative fixation to reduce the risk of recurrence. Radiotherapy may be used alone for palliation if life expectancy is <3months.
Answer 2: Dynamic hip screws have a high failure rate when used for malignant lesions in this region.
Answer 3: In the presence of malignant bone disease, PMMA cement is used to provide immediate structural support. Autogenous bone graft incorporation is unreliable.
Answer 4: Allograft-prosthetic composites rely on bone-healing and will not provide immediate structural stability.
