## Question

An 8-year-old female presents with lower thoracic-level myelomeningocele. She has a history of recurrent skin ulcers over the apex of her deformity. Currently, her skin shows no evidence of breakdown. Radiographs reveal progressive neurogenic scoliotic and kyphotic curves. MRI demonstrates no tethering of the cord. What is the most appropriate management of this patient’s spinal deformity?

## Options

1. Anterior and posterior spinal fusions with pelvic fixation and kyphectomy
2. Anterior release and fusion using a rib strut graft
3. Bracing
4. Kyphectomy alone
5. Physical therapy for hip stretching exercises

## Correct option

**1. Anterior and posterior spinal fusions with pelvic fixation and kyphectomy**

## Answer explanation

This myelomeningocele patient has progressive neurogenic scoliotic and kyphotic curves. Anterior (ASF) and posterior (PSF) spinal fusions with pelvic fixation are indicated in a progressive scoliotic curves, while kyphectomy with fusion and posterior instrumentation is indicated for the progressive kyphotic deformity.

Several orthopedic manifestations are associated with this condition including pathological fractures, scoliosis, kyphosis, hip dysplasia, knee deformities, and foot deformities. Survival and neurological impairment depend on the level of spinal segment involvement, with L3 or above being mostly confined to a wheelchair. Scoliosis may result from either a muscular imbalance of a congenital malformation (hemivertebrae). ASF and PSF with pelvic fixation are indicated in progressive scoliotic curves as bracing is not effective. The ASF is necessary due to the dysplastic posterior elements which may impair the posterior fusion. Congenital kyphosis leads to difficulties with sitting position, compression of internal organs, and chronic skin breakdown. Kyphectomy and posterior fusion with instrumentation are indicated in progressive kyphotic curves.

*Sharrard et al. review the use of an osteotomy-excision of the spine for lumbar kyphosis in older children with myelomeningocele.* *He reports on the indications for operative correction of kyphosis in older children including those with chronic or recurrent skin ulcerations.* Contraindications include severe uncontrolled hydrocephalus or recurrent ventriculitis, cardio-respiratory failure or incipient renal failure. **They conclude** that this procedure may relieve ulcerations, allow the possibility of limited walking with calipers or sit in a wheel-chair.

*Guille et al. review the treatment of spinal deformities in children with myelomeningocele* **They report** that the two most challenging problems are paralytic scoliosis and rigid lumbar kyphosis. The indications for surgical intervention are multifactorial, and the proposed benefits must be weighed against the potential risks. **They conclude** that newer spinal constructs now allow for fixation of the spine in areas previously difficult to the instrument.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Anterior release and fusion using a rib strut graft would not adequately address the progressive scoliotic and kyphotic curves
Answer 3: Bracing is not effective in patients with a progressive curve
Answer 4: Kyphectomy alone would help to address the progressive kyphotic curve, but posterior instrumentation would be needed
Answer 5: Physical therapy for hip stretching exercises would be an inadequate treatment for a patient with progressive curves
