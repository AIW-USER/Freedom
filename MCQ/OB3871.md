## Question

A 16-year-old boy presents for evaluation of his right foot. A plain radiograph is shown in Figure A. His condition was previously treated with a cast but remains symptomatic. Which of the following is most likely to result from surgical management of his condition?

![[q3871-a-figure-a.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Inability to fully participate in sports
2. Decreased subtalar motion
3. Decreased plantar flexion
4. Need for a repeat of the index procedure
5. Full return to sport

## Correct option

**5. Full return to sport**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a calcaneonavicular coalition. After resection with tissue interposition, return to full function including sport can be expected.

Initial management of tarsal coalition is nonoperative but in cases in which this fails, operative treatment with coalition resection and tissue interposition can provide pain relief and restoration of function in most patients. Patients with subtalar coalitions that fail this, have significantly large coalitions, or have degenerative changes may be candidates for hindfoot fusion procedures.

*Mubarak et al. retrospectively reviewed 96 feet treated surgically for tarsal coalition.* *87% returned to sport.* *5% required repeat excision.* *74% had improved subtalar motion.* *82% had improved plantarflexion.*

*Lemley et al. review the etiology, presentation, imaging and treatment of tarsal coalition.* **They concluded** that when choosing between arthrodesis and resection, CT or MRI was more accurate in identifying arthrosis of the surrounding joints.

Figure A shows a calcaneonavicular coalition with a characteristic "anteater" sign.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1. Return to sport is more likely (87% in Mubarak's study) than restriction of participation.
Answer 2. Subtalar fusion would be an inappropriate treatment. Bar resection is likely to lead to increased subtalar motion. In Mubarak's study, 74% had improved subtalar motion and none had decreased subtalar motion.
Answer 3. Increased, not decreased plantar flexion is expected after bar resection (82% in Mubarak's study).
Answer 4. Repeat resection may be necessary in 5%. Return to sport is more likely (87%).
