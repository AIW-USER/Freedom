## Question

A 56 year-old male underwent a tibiotalar joint fusion six months ago. He presents for a second opinion due to chronic pain and difficulty walking. His current radiographs are shown in Figure A. The patient has a history of alcoholic induced neuropathy, type 2 diabetes, and had a previous nonunion of his left femur from an unrelated injury. His tibiotalar arthrodesis was completed for treatment of post-traumatic arthritis and his infection workup is currently negative. Which of the following places the patient at greatest risk for persistent nonunion with revision surgical fixation?

![[q210266-a-failed-ankle-fusion.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Neuropathy
2. Diabetes
3. Prior femoral nonunion
4. Post-traumatic arthritis
5. Initial fixation method

## Correct option

**1. Neuropathy**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a tibiotalar fusion nonunion which will require a revision fusion operation. His history of neuropathy places him at the greatest risk for revision surgery failure.

Arthrodesis is a common operative procedure used to manage arthritis and deformity in the foot and ankle. Nonunion is a possible and undesirable outcome in any arthrodesis surgery. Rates of nonunion in the foot and ankle literature range from 0% to 47% depending on the patient population and joint involved.

A revision fusion procedure for this patient is the best option given his current clinical situation. He is not a candidate for total ankle arthroplasty given his prior fibular resection, and he has no obvious evidence of adjacent joint disease which would preclude the need for the extension of the fusion to the hindfoot or midfoot.

*O'Connor et al. reviewed case logs of three foot and ankle surgeons to identify nonunion arthrodesis revision cases.* The patient factors reviewed included diabetes, inflammatory arthropathy, tobacco use, history of infection, nonunion elsewhere, neuropathy, Charcot arthropathy, posttraumatic arthritis, and prior attempt at revision arthrodesis at the same site. Eighty-two cases of revision arthrodesis were identified with an average follow-up of 16 months. The overall nonunion rate was 23%. Neuropathy and prior attempts at revision were identified as significant risks (P < .05) for persistent nonunion.

*Tenenbaum et al. conducted a study to test the hypothesis that arthrodesis of both the ankle and the hindfoot joints produces an objective improvement of function as measured by gait analysis.* *Twenty-one patients with severe ankle and hindfoot arthritis who underwent unilateral tibiotalocalcaneal arthrodesis with an intramedullary nail were prospectively studied.* There was a small loss of sagittal plane motion in the affected limb postoperatively. There were marked increases in gait velocity, ankle moment, and hip motion and power, documenting objective improvements in ambulatory function.

*Clare et al. describe an anatomic compression arthrodesis technique with anterior plate augmentation for ankle arthrodesis.* **The authors feel** this is an effective technique that results in a high union rate.

Figure A shows radiographs of a failed tibiotalar fusion with collapse and nonunion.

Incorrect Answers:
2-5: These factors were not found to place the patient at as high of risk for nonunion as neuropathy
