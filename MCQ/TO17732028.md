## Question

Essex-Lopresti lesion in upper limb involves ( trauma- fractures of radius and ulna)

## Options

1. Radial head fracture with distal radio-ulnar joint disruption.
2. Radial shaft fracture
3. Injury to interosseous membrane
4. Radial shaft fracture with proximal radioulnar joint dislocation

## Correct option

**3. Injury to interosseous membrane**

## Answer explanation

Essex-Lopresti fracture:

It is a fracture of the radial head with the splitting of interosseous membrane and distal radioulnar joint dislocation. This is an unstable fracture and requires ORIF of the radial head with a small headless Herbert screw or a radial head replacement to maintain the length of the radius. If in this fracture the radial head is excised, the radius will migrate proximally into the elbow restricting movements of the joint. If at all excision of the head is planned in such cases, it should always be delayed for 3 weeks to allow the interosseous membrane to heal up.
