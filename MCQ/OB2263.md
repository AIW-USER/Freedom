## Question

You’re asked to evaluate a 7-day-old infant in the NICU for decreased movement of his right upper extremity since birth. On chart review, you see that he is 97th percentile for head circumference, 84th percentile for height, and 88th percentile for weight, and underwent a prolonged vaginal delivery. On exam, the right upper extremity is adducted with internal rotation of the shoulder, extension of the elbow and pronation of the forearm. Which of the following factors has a poor prognosis for spontaneous recovery?

## Options

1. Age >8 days at diagnosis
2. Lack of biceps function by 3 months
3. Ipsilateral clavicle fracture
4. Post-ganglionic injuries
5. Head circumference >95th percentile

## Correct option

**2. Lack of biceps function by 3 months**

## Answer explanation

Patients with Erb's palsy with a lack of biceps function against gravity by 3-months-old have a poorer prognosis and a lower chance of complete spontaneous recovery.

Erb’s palsy is the most common obstetric brachial plexopathy and involve the upper brachial plexus (C5-6) and results from lateral flexion of the head towards the contralateral shoulder with depression of the ipsilateral shoulder. Treatment typically consists of observation and passive exercises to maintain motion while waiting for nerve function to return. 90% of patients will recover spontaneously but this may take up to two years. Early twitch biceps activity suggests a favorable outcome, and patients who have anti-gravity function of the biceps and deltoid by 3 months have a good prognosis for complete recovery. Poor prognostic indicators are a lack of biceps function by 3 months and preganglionic injuries (Horner’s Syndrome).

*Waters et al. review the natural history and outcomes following microsurgical repair of patients with obstetric brachial plexopathies.* **They report** that patients who had a return of biceps function by 3 months had a significantly improved prognosis compared to those who saw biceps function return during the fourth, fifth, or sixth months. **They conclude** that it is rare for patients who have late (>3 months) return of biceps function to have a complete recovery.

*Greenwald et al. review the incidence and prognosis of birth-related brachial plexus palsies.* **They report** that an incidence of 2/1,000 live births, with an excellent prognosis and full recovery in 95.7% of patients. **They conclude** that the severity of such palsies has lessened and that early recovery is typical.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Age at diagnosis has not been shown to correlate with prognosis.
Answer 3: Ipsilateral clavicle fractures are commonly associated with Erb’s palsy, but do not demonstrate correlation to prognosis.
Answer 4: Pre-ganglionic injuries, not post-ganglionic, have the worst prognosis and will not spontaneously recover motor function.
Answer 5: Head circumference is not related to prognosis following Erb’s palsy.
