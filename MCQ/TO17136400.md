## Question

Which Amino Acid is required during oxidation process in body for Protein metabolism in endurance athlete?

## Options

1. Arginine
2. Lysine
3. Leucine
4. Isoleucine

## Correct option

**3. Leucine**

## Answer explanation

Acute endurance exercise results in the oxidation of several amino acids. The total amount of amino acid oxidation during endurance exercise amounts to only 1-6% of the total energy cost of exercise. The branched chain amino acid, leucine, has been most often studied in relation to endurance exercise. Leucine is oxidized by the enzyme, branched-chain oxo-acid dehydrogenase (BCOAD). BCOAD is relatively inactive at rest (approximately 4-7%) and is activated at the onset of exercise by dephosphorylation (to about 25%).

In resting human individuals, the recommended protein daily allowance is about 0.8 g (dry weight) to 1 gm/ kg body weight per 24 h. Healthy athletes (and heavy workers) should have a common diet of 1.2 g /kg body weight to 1.6 gm/kg body weight per 24 h to compensate the exercise training muscle protein degradation and their resynthesis within the following hours. A nitrogen-balance assay would be recommended to avoid any excessive intake of protein.

The liver and, to a lesser extent, the kidney are the principal sites of amino acid metabolism in humans.

When ingesting excess protein, amino acid amounts larger than needed for synthesis of proteins and other nitrogenous compounds cannot be stored or excreted, and the surplus is oxidized or converted to carbohydrate and lipid.

During amino acid degradation, the α−amino group is removed and the resulting carbon skeleton is converted into a major metabolic intermediate. Most of the amino groups of amino acids are transformed into pyruvate, acetyl-CoA or one of the intermediates of the tricarboxylic acid cycle.

 Answer: c
