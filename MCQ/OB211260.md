## Question

A 34-year-old recreational hockey player collides with the goalie during a game and injures his knee. He presents to your clinic for evaluation. A radiograph is shown in Figure A. He is diagnosed with an isolated ligamentous injury. During the pivot shift examination, the iliotibial band contributes to:

![[q211260-a-segond.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Reduction of the medial tibial plateau with knee extension
2. Reduction of the lateral tibial plateau with knee extension
3. Reduction of the lateral tibial plateau with knee flexion
4. Subluxation of the lateral tibial plateau with knee extension
5. Subluxation of the lateral tibial plateau with knee flexion

## Correct option

**3. Reduction of the lateral tibial plateau with knee flexion**

## Answer explanation

During the pivot-shift examination, the knee is brought into extension, internally rotated, and a valgus force is applied, anteriorly subluxating the lateral tibial plateau and impinging it against the lateral femoral condyle. As the knee is forcibly flexed, the resistance is overcome and the iliotibial band becomes a reducing force, facilitating the reduction of the lateral tibial plateau past 30-40 degrees of knee flexion.

Pathologic motion elicited by the pivot shift depends on the degree of subluxation and is graded as normal (grade 0), a glide (grade I), clunk (grade II), or clunk with locking (grade III), though grade I and occasionally grade II exams may be appreciated in normal knees in patients with physiologic laxity. While the anterior drawer and Lachman test evaluate translational instability, the pivot shift chiefly evaluates rotatory instability and is thought to furthermore serve as a better gauge of functional outcomes following ACL reconstruction.

*Lane et al. review the pivot-shift examination as a combined means by which to assess both rotatory and translational instability of an ACL-deficient knee.* **The authors emphasize** the importance of the pivot shift as an indicator of functional stability and patient outcomes. **They conclude** that assessment of the pivot shift is confounded by varied techniques, the subjectivity of the evaluation, and the coexistence of other soft tissue injuries in ACL-deficient knees.

*Galway and MacIntosh reviewed the pivot shift as a means for assessment of lateral compartment instability secondary to ACL deficiency.* *The authors describe the pathomechanics of the maneuver which involves anterior subluxation of the lateral tibial plateau and discuss the meniscal and chondral injuries which may result from persistent instability.* **They conclude** with the importance of the pivot shift as an indicator of functional instability.

Figure A demonstrates an AP radiograph of a right knee with a Segond fracture, which is pathognomonic for ACL disruption.

Incorrect answers:
Answers 1, 2, 4, and 5: Pathologic motion elicited by the pivot shift maneuver in an ACL-deficient knee involves anterior subluxation of the LATERAL tibial plateau with the knee in extension, followed by REDUCTION of the lateral tibial plateau with knee flexion, facilitated by the iliotibial band.
