## Question

Doing hip surgery your incision extended 5 cms proximal to GT. You speculate some nerve has got injured. How will you test it post op [FNB 2023]

## Options

1. Asking patient to stand on operated limb
2. Asking patient to stand on non operated limb
3. Asking patient to dorsiflex ankle
4. Asking patient to plantarflex ankle

## Correct option

**1. Asking patient to stand on operated limb**

## Answer explanation

Ans: A
EXPLANATION: -
Likely the surgeon in question used the lateral approach. Extending the incision 5 cms proximally in this case is likely to injure the Superior Gluteal Nerve and this would lead to paralysis of Gluteus Medius manifesting as Trendelenburg gait.

SURGICAL APPROACHES TO HIP
ANTERIOR APPROACHES
Patient position: Supine

ILLIO FEMORAL (SMITH PETERSON) APPROACH
Skin incision: Middle of iliac crest to ASIS, then downwards pointing in line towards medial border of patella. The vertical limb of incision is aimed to stay medial to medial border of patella.

![[TO19356230-1.jpg]]

Plane: Tensor Fascia Lata (TFL) laterally and Sartorius plus Rectus femoris medially

![[TO19356230-2.jpg]]

Protect: Ascending branch of Lateral circumflex femoral artery (5 cm below hip) and Lateral femoral cutaneous nerve (just below ASIS).

![[TO19356230-3.jpg]]

The lateral femoral cutaneous nerve pierces the deep fascia close to the intermuscular interval between the TFL and the Sartorius.

Schabel modification: Osteotomy of iliac crest that has attached TFL.

SOMERVILLE APPROACH
Used for Open reduction in cases of DDH.
Incision: Bikini incision (Middle of iliac crest to point mid-way between ASIS and mid-line of Pelvis)

![[TO19356230-4.jpg]]

Plane: TFL (laterally) and Sartorius (medially)
Next: Detach iliac apophysis from ilium. Then release TFL and then sartorius/ rectus femoris à 1 cm distal to AIIS.
Next: Identify psoas on superior pubic ramus and retract with retractor
Next: T-incision in capsule
Next: Use ligament teres to find acetabulum

![[TO19356230-5.jpg]]

ANTERO-LATERAL APPROACH
Patient position: Supine

SMITH PETERSON APPROACH
Exposes femoral neck as well as the acetabulum.
Skin Incision (shape of 7): Anterior third of iliac crest à Then go along anterior border of TFL to its insertion at IT band (can go up to 10 cms below base of greater trochanter)

![[TO19356230-6.jpg]]

Plane: Make a flap by dividing muscle attachments between ASIS and acetabular labrum. Flap consists of TFL, Gluteus minimus and anterior fibers of Gluteus medius.

![[TO19356230-7.jpg]]

 Protect: Lateral femoral cutaneous nerve between Sartorius and TFL.

LATERAL APPROACHES
Patient position: Supine

WATSON JONES APPROACH
Skin incision: Start 2.5 cms distal and lateral to ASIS à Curve distal and posterior over the lateral aspect of Greater trochanter à Carry down over lateral surface of femoral shaft for around 5 cms.

Plane: Between TFL (medially) and Gluteus medius (Laterally)
Differentiation between TFL and gluteus medius is made by starting separation midway
 between ASIS and GT
Dissection carried out proximally to expose superior gluteal nerve
Capsule is incised longitudinally along anterosuperior surface of femoral neck
Extension –Proximal by detaching anterior fibres of gluteus medius from GT
 Distal by reflecting the vastus lateralis distally or by spliting it longitudinally

![[TO19356230-8.jpg]]

![[TO19356230-9.jpg]]

![[TO19356230-10.png]]

HARRIS APPROACH TO HIP
Make a U-shaped skin incision, with its base at the posterior border of the GT . Begin the incision about 5 cm posterior and slightly proximal to the ASIS, curve it distally and posteriorly to the posterosuperior corner of the GT.

![[TO19356230-11.png]]

Beginning distally, divide the iliotibial band in line with the skin incision
GT Osteotomy done. Direct the osteotomy superiorly and medially from a point 1.5 cm distal to the tubercle of the vastus lateralis to the superior surface of the femoral neck. Reflect distally the origin of the vastus lateralis Place an instrument between the abductor muscles and the superior surface of the joint capsule

HARDINGE APPROACH (Direct lateral transgluteal approach)
Place the patient supine with the greater trochanter at the edge of the table and the muscles of the buttocks freed from the edge
Lazy-J lateral skin incision.

![[TO19356230-12.png]]

Plane: Tensor fasciae lata retracted anteriorly, and gluteus maximus is retracted posteriorly. Gluteus medius is exposed head side and vastus lateralis is exposed on lower side.

![[TO19356230-13.jpg]]

![[TO19356230-14.png]]

Incise the tendon of the gluteus medius obliquely across the greater trochanter, leaving the posterior half still attached to the trochanter. Carry the incision proximally in line with the fibers of the gluteus medius at the junction of the middle and posterior thirds of the muscle. This gluteus medius split should be no farther than 5 cm from the tip of the greater trochanter or less than 2 cms above lateral lip of acetabulum (Frndak et al.) to avoid damage to the superior gluteal nerve and artery.
Incision through gluteus medius tendon is outlined. Posterior half is left attached to greater trochanter.
Anterior joint capsule is exposed and hip is dislocated anteriorly.

![[TO19356230-15.png]]

 During closure, repair the tendon of the gluteus medius with nonabsorbable braided sutures.

POSTERO LATERAL APPROACHES
Patient position: Lateral

GIBSON AND MODIFIED GIBSON APPROACHES
Gibson’s approach is derived from Kocher Langenbeck approach of Acetabulum and involves dislocating the head anteriorly after cutting Gluteus medius and minimus at apex of greater trochanter. Modified Gibson (Marcy and Fletcher) on the other hand cuts the short external rotators to go from the posterior side thereby dislocating the hip posteriorly.

GIBSON’S APPROACH
Incision: Begin 6-8 cms anterior to PSIS and just distal to iliac crest  Extend incision along anterior border of Gluteus maximus going along anterior edge of greater trochanter and then along femoral shaft for 15-18 cms

![[TO19356230-16.png]]

Next,
In Proximal incision  Locate sulcus at anterior border of gluteus maximus by abducting the hip and extend incision proximally along this line
In Distal incision  Incise ilio tibial band till greater trochanter

![[TO19356230-17.jpg]]

Gluteus medius attached to apex of greater trochanter is now fully visible.

Next  Cut Gluteus medius and minimus at their insertions into tip of greater trochanter. Retract these muscles and anterior joint capsule will be seen. Excise capsule along anterior surface of femoral neck to expose the hip and dislocate anteriorly by flexing, abducting and externally rotating the hip.

MODIFIED GIBSON
All same as above except in place of Gluteus medius and mimimus, short external rotator are cut to dislocate the hip posteriorly.

POSTERIOR APPROACHES
Patient position: Lateral

KOCHER LANGENBECK APPROACH
This is actually a posterior approach to pelvis but understanding is imperative for understanding the posterior approaches to hip.

Incision: Begin 6-8 cms anterior to PSIS and just distal to iliac crest  Extend incision along anterior border of Gluteus maximus going along anterior edge of greater trochanter and then along femoral shaft for 15-18 cms
Next,
In Proximal incision  Plane is made through the gluteus maximum by separating the same in line with the fibres
In Distal incision  Incise ilio tibial band till greater trochanter

![[TO19356230-18.png]]

Next  Incise the short external rotators at their insertion in to the greater trochanter and reflect them medially along with the sciatic nerve

![[TO19356230-19.png]]

Next  Elevate gluteus medius and minimus subperiosteally from posterior and lateral ilium. Retraction of these muscles is maintained by hammering two Steinman pins into ilium just proximal to the greater sciatic notch. Ensure to protect superior gluteal nerves and vessels.

![[TO19356230-20.png]]

![[TO19356230-21.jpg]]

Entire posterior acetabulum wall and posterior column can be seen.
OSBORN APPROACH
This is just on lines of Kocher Langenbeck with slight modifications, primarily skin incision extent that is more suited to expose hip than pelvis. Also, gluteus medius and minimus are not stripped from iliac crest as acetabular posterior column exposure is not required as this approach is ideally used for hip surgeries (not pelvic).
Incision: Start 4.5 cms distal and lateral to PSIS  Go along posterior border of GT and then along the shaft

![[TO19356230-22.png]]

Plane: Separate through fibres of Gluteus maximus and cut ilio tibial tract distally

![[TO19356230-23.png]]

![[TO19356230-24.jpg]]

Next, cut short external rotators and retract posteriorly

![[TO19356230-25.jpg]]

Next, Incise capsule longitudinally to expose posterior surface of femoral neck along with joint capsule

MOORES APPROACH (SOUTHERN APPROACH)
The approach is same as Osborn except with slight difference in the description of the skin incision.
Start the incision approximately 10 cm distal to the posterior superior iliac spine and extend it distally and laterally parallel with the fibres of the gluteus maximus to the posterior margin of the greater trochanter. Direct the incision distally 10 to 13 cm parallel with the femoral shaft

![[TO19356230-26.jpg]]

Expose and divide the deep fascia in line with the skin incision.
Plane: By blunt dissection, separate the fibers of the gluteus maximus no more than 7 cm from the tip of the trochanter to avoid injury to the branches of the inferior gluteal artery and nerve. Distally cut the ITB.

![[TO19356230-27.jpg]]

Expose and divide the gemelli and obturator internus and, the tendon of the piriformis (if desired) at their insertion on the femur, and retract the muscles medially. Tag these for later reattachment to the trochanter if desired.

![[TO19356230-28.jpg]]

![[TO19356230-29.png]]

The posterior part of the joint capsule is now well exposed. Incise it from distal to proximal along the line of the femoral neck in H shape as shown above in diagram (green dotted lines) to the rim of the acetabulum.
Flex the thigh and knee 90 degrees, internally rotate the thigh, and dislocate the hip posteriorly.

MEDIAL APPROACHES
Patient position: Supine

LUDLOFF APPROCH [1908]
Medial skin incision
Superficial plane is between Sartorius and Adductor longus
Deep plane is between Pectinius and Ilio-psoas

FERGUSON, HOPPENFIELD AND DEBOER APPROCH
Incision: 2.5 cms distal to pubic tubercle  Go along medial aspect of thigh
Superficial plane: Gracilis and Adductor longus
Deep plane: Adductor magnus and Adductor brevis
Protect Posterior branch of Obturator nerve and NV bundle of gracilis
