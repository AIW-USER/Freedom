## Question

A 55-year-old female is being seen in your clinic for a progressive foot deformity with symptoms recalcitrant to the appropriate nonoperative management. You are discussing the risks, benefits and alternatives of your planned surgical intervention with the patient. A fifth metatarsal stress fracture secondary to lateral column overload is a common complication following the surgical correction of which of the following pathologies?

## Options

1. Ligamentous Lisfranc
2. Moderate Hallux Valgus
3. Stage I Posterior Tibial Tendon Insufficiency (PTTI)
4. Stage IIa Posterior Tibial Tendon Insufficiency (PTTI)
5. Stage IIb Posterior Tibial Tendon Insufficiency (PTTI)

## Correct option

**5. Stage IIb Posterior Tibial Tendon Insufficiency (PTTI)**

## Answer explanation

The treatment of stage IIb PTTI often requires an opening-wedge osteotomy of the anterior calcaneus in order to correct the abduction seen at the talonavicular joint (Answer 5). This procedure has been associated with lateral column overload.

PTTI is the most common cause of adult-acquired flatfoot deformity. This pathologic process is hallmarked by attenuation and tenosynovitis of the posterior tibial tendon, which leads to medial arch collapse and, without treatment, fixed degenerative changes across the midfoot, hindfoot, and tibiotalar joints. The deformity imparted by PTTI consists of pes planus, hindfoot valgus, forefoot varus, and forefoot abduction. The posterior tibial tendon functions as the primary dynamic support for the arch, and acts as a hindfoot inverter. During the stance phase of gait, it serves to adduct and supinate the forefoot and also acts as a secondary plantar flexor of the ankle. The treatment of PTTI is dependent on a variety of factors, including both the extent and flexibility of the deformity. With excessive forefoot abduction, excessive talonavicular uncoverage occurs. This deformity, in the absence of rigid and/or degenerative changes, is often combated with an anterior calcaneus lateral opening-wedge osteotomy. This procedure, however, has been associated with lateral column overload, which can manifest as a fifth metatarsal stress fracture or lateral column stiffness.

*Thordarson et al. provide a consensus statement on the indications of lateral column lengthening for progressive flatfoot deformity.* **The authors recommend** the utilization of this procedure (i.e. calcaneus opening wedge osteotomy) when the talonavicular joint uncoverage is above 40%. **The authors caution** that careful titration of the amount of correction is of paramount importance, as hypercorrection (typically > 5-10mm) can lead to lateral column overload.

*Baxter et al. investigated the impact that graft shape had on the amount of deformity correction and forefoot loading in the setting of a lateral column lengthening for flatfoot deformity.* *The authors created a flatfoot deformity in 18 cadaveric specimens and used three different graft shapes (triangular, trapezoidal, and rectangular).* Overall, **the authors note** that graft shape has no effect on the correction of talonavicular abduction or dorsiflexion, but did affect forefoot loading mechanics. **The authors recommend** the utilization of graft with a large taper in order to lower the incidence of lateral column overload.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: A common complication following the treatment of a Lisfranc injury is the development of a planovalgus deformity secondary to malreduction. In addition, these patients often develop post-traumatic midfoot arthritis. Lateral column overload is not a well-described complication following the treatment of a Ligamentous Lisfranc.
Answer 2: A common complication following the surgical correction of hallux valgus is iatrogenic hallux varus and/or subsequent second transfer metatarsalgia. Lateral column overload is unlikely.
Answer 3: Stage I PTTI with failure of immobilization, is treated with a posterior tibial tendon tenosynovectomy, which does not cause lateral column overload.
Answer 4: Stage IIa PTTI, due to normal talonavicular coverage and forefoot abduction/adduction, does not require lateral column lengthening.
