## Question

What is the most common anatomical site for unicameral bone cysts in children?

## Options

1. Distal humerus
2. Proximal humerus
3. Distal femur
4. Proximal tibia
5. Tibial shaft

## Correct option

**2. Proximal humerus**

## Answer explanation

Unicameral bone cysts are found most commonly in the proximal humerus in children.

Unicameral bone cysts are non-neoplastic, serous fluid-filled bone lesions that are thought to result from temporary failure of medullary bone formation near the physis. They are usually found in patients under the age of 20. Most are asymptomatic unless fracture occurs.

*Scaglietti et al. reported the outcomes of methylprednisolone acetate use for the treatment of bone cysts.* *They opined the use of corticosteroids as they exert a destructive action on the pathological tissue of the lesion, thus favoring a progressive process of repair and avoiding surgery.*

*Cho et al. compared the initial treatment of unicameral bone cysts using steroid injections vs. bone grafting in 58 patients.* **They showed** a lower success rate with steroid treatment. That rate of recurrence after initial treatment with steriods or autologous bone marrow was 41.7% and 13.3% (p<0.05), respectively.

Illustration A shows an AP radiograph of a left shoulder. There is a central, lytic, well-demarcated metaphyseal lesion indicative of a unicameral bone cyst. Within the lesion, there is an associated pathologic fracture.

Incorrect Answers:
1,3,4,5: These are not the most common sites of UBCs, the next most common sites are the proximal femur, distal tibia, ilium, calcaneus, and occasionally metacarpals, phalanges, or distal radius.
