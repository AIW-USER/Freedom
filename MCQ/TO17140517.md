Chapter: Regional Orthopedics MCQs — Transient Osteoporosis of the Hip Joint

## Question

The radiographs and MR images of a 37-year-old woman who has a 3-month history of severe right hip pain are given below. She does not recall any trauma prior to the pain onset and denies any past steroid exposure. She has 3 children and is not currently pregnant. The pain is located in her groin and the onset was sudden. The pain is refractory to anti-inflammatory medications. What is the most appropriate treatment?

![[TO17140517-1.png]]

## Options

1. Toe-touch weight-bearing activity and supportive care
2. Core decompression and femoral head grafting
3. Periacetabular osteotomy
4. Hemiarthroplasty

## Correct option

**1. Toe-touch weight-bearing activity and supportive care**

## Answer explanation

The patient’s MR images are consistent with transient hip osteoporosis. No signs suggest avascular necrosis. She has no joint narrowing. Transient osteoporosis of the hip is characterized by bone marrow edema in the femoral head and neck. This condition affects more men than women and is sometimes seen in the third trimester of pregnancy but can be seen in women who are not pregnant as well. This is a self-limiting condition, and the treatment is limited weight-bearing activity until the symptoms resolve. There is no indication for arthroplasty or osteotomy in this scenario. Generally resolves on it`s own in 6-8 months
