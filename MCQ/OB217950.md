## Question

A third year orthopedic resident is studying the effects of bone density on fusion rates. When discussing the methodology with his attending there are concerns about a type II error. Which of the following modifications would reduce this type of error in the study?

## Options

1. Setting a = 0.01 instead of 0.05
2. Increasing the study sample size
3. Blinding the data analysts
4. Increasing ß to 0.9 instead of 0.8
5. Randomizing the study groups

## Correct option

**2. Increasing the study sample size**

## Answer explanation

Increasing the sample size will decrease the risk of a type II error, which is accepting the null hypothesis when it is in fact false.

A type II error occurs when the study fails to reject the null hypothesis when it is in fact false. In order to minimize this error, a study must be adequately powered. This can be accomplished by increasing the sample size of the study.

*Wason and Mander reported on minimizing the maximum expected sample size in two-stage Phase II clinical trials with continuous outcomes.* *They claimed the study design is effective for a variety of treatment effects.* **They concluded** this design is superior to other models with respect to sample size properties.

*Ioannidis, Hozo, and Djulbegovic reported on the optimal type I and type II error pairs with fixed sample sizes.* **They proposed** equations that provide optimal type I and type II errors with fixed sample sizes.

Incorrect answers
Answer 1: α is the probability of a type I error which is rejecting the null hypothesis when it is in fact true. Reducing α to 0.01 would reduce the risk of a type 1 error.
Answer 3: Blinding data analysts would reduce the risk of bias from those that are evaluating the data.
Answer 4: β is the probability of a type II error, which is failing to reject the null hypothesis when it is in fact false. Increasing β would increase the risk of a type II error.
Answer 5: Randomizing the study groups would reduce the risk of selection bias.
