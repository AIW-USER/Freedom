## Question

A 22-month-old girl presents to your clinic due to parental concern for back asymmetry. Radiographs of the spine demonstrate a left thoracic curve measuring 48° with the apex at the T10 vertebra. The rib vertebrae angle difference measured at the apical vertebra shows a difference of 24°. Neurologic examination is normal, and spinal MRI shows no intraspinal anomaly. Regarding the management of this patient's spinal deformity, which statement is most accurate?

## Options

1. Initiating serial Elongation-Derotation-Flexion (EDF) casting at this age offers a higher chance of achieving a curve <15° than starting casting after age 4 years
2. Full-time bracing alone provides superior outcomes to casting for progressive early-onset scoliosis
3. Most children with this presentation require immediate growing-rod instrumentation to protect pulmonary development
4. Early posterior spinal fusion is most appropriate to prevent thoracic insufficiency in this age group
5. Success after casting is unrelated to initial curve magnitude or early in-cast correction

## Correct option

**1. Initiating serial Elongation-Derotation-Flexion (EDF) casting at this age offers a higher chance of achieving a curve <15° than starting casting after age 4 years**

## Answer explanation

Early initiation of EDF (Mehta) casting is associated with a higher likelihood of reaching a small residual curve, and outcomes decline when casting is started at older ages. Starting before age 4—particularly before 18 months—yields the best results, whereas starting after age 4 has a significantly lower rate of curve correction and reduction of needing surgical management (answer A).

Infantile idiopathic scoliosis is a coronal plane deformity that typically presents at ≤3 years of age. Evaluation requires standing (or supine) radiographs, as well as an MRI to exclude neural axis abnormalities such as the presence of a syrinx, tumor, or tethered spinal cord. Management depends on skeletal maturity, magnitude, and documented progression; options include observation, bracing, serial casting, and surgical strategies. Larger curves may impair pulmonary development and thoracic insufficiency, with cardiopulmonary compromise becoming more frequent as curve magnitude increases, underscoring the importance of growth-friendly management in early childhood.

*Fedorak and colleagues performed a multicenter retrospective review stratifying outcomes of Mehta casting by age at first cast in idiopathic early-onset scoliosis.* **They reported** that 75% of children cast before 18 months had a final major curve <15°, with progressively worse results at older ages; only 9.1% of those starting at ≥4 years reached <15°, and 64% in this age group underwent surgery or had curves ≥50° at follow-up.

*Fedorak et al. published a single-surgeon series of patients with idiopathic early-onset scoliosis treated with serial casting, with a minimum 5-year follow-up.* **They found** that 49% of children had curves ≤15° at the latest follow-up, and 73% improved their curves by ≥20°. **They concluded** that predictors of sustained small curves included lower initial Cobb angle, better traction radiograph correction, and a smaller first-in-cast Cobb angle and rib vertebrae angle difference (RVAD), highlighting the importance of early curve response and initial magnitude.

Incorrect answer choices:
Answer choice B: Casting, not bracing alone, is the established first-line modality for progressive early-onset idiopathic curves; bracing is typically used as an adjunct after casting or in cases with later presentations with maintained spine flexibility.
Answer choice C: Immediate growing-rod instrumentation is not the default intervention at this age because effective casting can delay or obviate surgery in many cases, reducing the burden of repeated lengthenings and complications.
Answer choice D: Posterior spinal fusion in toddlers sacrifices spinal and thoracic growth and risks restrictive lung disease; growth-friendly nonfusion strategies are preferred initially.
Answer choice E: Early radiographic responses and initial curve magnitude correlate with outcomes; better first-in-cast correction and smaller initial Cobb angles predict sustained smaller curves at follow-up.
