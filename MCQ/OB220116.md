## Question

A 21-year-old patient presents to your office with recurrent anterior shoulder instability with a moderate sized Hill-Sachs lesion after an arthroscopic Bankart repair. You proceed with treatment using a bone block augmentation of the glenoid. Which risk factor for recurrence after Bankart repair most justifies this treatment?

## Options

1. Concomitant superior labrum anterior and posterior (SLAP) lesion
2. Dominant-sided extremity
3. Greater than five preoperative dislocations
4. On-track lesion
5. "Postage stamp" fractures of the anterior glenoid rim

## Correct option

**5. "Postage stamp" fractures of the anterior glenoid rim**

## Answer explanation

"Postage stamp" fractures of the anterior glenoid rim are a known complication after Bankart repair, often resulting from improper suture anchor placement or underlying bone loss. Bone block augmentation is appropriate in this case as it restores glenoid bone stock, reducing the risk of recurrent instability (Answer choice 5).

Bone block transfer is recommended when glenoid bone loss is greater than 20% to 25% or when the Hill-Sachs lesion is off-track. Postage stamp fractures are commonly seen in patients who are male, age 25 years or younger, and participants in sporting activities. They are also seen in fractures initially stabilized with 3 or more anchors, conventional knot-tying anchors, or in patients that experienced osteolysis around anchor sites. Significant predictors of recurrent instability following a Bankart procedure includes patients younger than 20 years of age, a Hill-Sachs lesion, a glenoid bone lesion, shoulder hyperlaxity, and an off-track lesion.

*Provencher et al. reviewed the diagnosis and management of traumatic anterior shoulder instability, focusing on clinical evaluation, imaging, and treatment strategies.* *They discuss both nonoperative and surgical options,* **emphasizing** patient-specific factors such as age, activity level, and risk of recurrence. The article highlights advancements in arthroscopic and open stabilization techniques, as well as rehabilitation protocols aimed at optimizing outcomes.

*Zhang et al. conducted a systematic review and meta-analysis to identify risk factors associated with recurrence after Bankart repair.* **Their findings suggest** that younger age, hyperlaxity, high-demand sports participation, and glenoid bone loss significantly increase recurrence risk. **They emphasize** the importance of patient selection and potential adjunctive procedures to improve surgical success rates.

*Woolnough et al. systematically reviewed cases of anterior glenoid rim fractures, known as "postage stamp" fractures, occurring after Bankart repair.* *They identify key patient and suture anchor characteristics contributing to these fractures, including anchor placement, bone quality, and fixation techniques.* *The study underscores the need for careful surgical planning to mitigate the risk of postoperative complications.*

Incorrect answers:
Answer choices 1-3: Dominant side, a SLAP lesion, and more than five preoperative dislocations are not significant risk factors for recurrence.
Answer choice 4: On-track lesions, as opposed to off-track lesions, are not a significant predictor of recurrent instability.
