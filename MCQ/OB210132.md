## Question

A high school quarterback presents to your office complaining of activity-related right foot pain. Five months ago, he was diagnosed with a "foot fracture" and was treated by an outside provider for 8 weeks in a controlled ankle motion boot and was made non-weightbearing. His foot pain had mostly resolved following this period of immobilization and he did not follow-up. His season resumed 2 weeks ago and he is now practicing 6 days a week. Since this time, he states his pain has returned and his performance is beginning to deteriorate. On physical exam, the only significant finding is tenderness to deep palpation about the proximal fifth metatarsal. Radiographs are obtained and presented in Figures A and B. When counseling him and his parents, which of the following treatment options is most associated with early return to play, successful union, and the highest patient reported outcome scores?

## Options

1. Midfoot fusion
2. Open reduction internal fixation with plate and screws
3. Surgical fixation with an intramedullary compression screw
4. Fragment excision
5. 6-8 weeks of non-weight bearing with use of a bone stimulator

## Correct option

**3. Surgical fixation with an intramedullary compression screw**

## Answer explanation

The treatment of choice for a Jones fracture (Zone 2) in an athlete is with early surgical fixation with an intramedullary compression screw; symptomatic non-unions in this cohort initially treated with a trial of non-weight bearing also show promising results with surgery, although union is slightly delayed compared with early fixation.

True Jones fractures involve a vascular watershed area, and non-union is reported to be as high as 15-30%. The treatment of choice is with an intramedullary compression screw. Although a trial of non-operative intervention can be considered in Zone 2 fifth metatarsal base fractures, early operative fixation in athletes has been shown to be effective in promoting healing, early return to play, and is associated with improved patient reported outcome scores compared to non-operative management.

*Rosenberg et al. provide a review article on the management of fifth metatarsal base fractures.* *They divide the treatment of these fractures based on the three anatomic locations described by Quill: tuberosity avulsions (Zone 1), fractures at the meta-diaphyseal junction extending into the intermetatarsal articulation (Zone 2), and proximal diaphyseal fractures (Zone 3).* **They state** that a trial of non-operative management of Zone 2 fractures, in the form of non-weight bearing for 6-8 weeks, can be successful, but these fractures are prone to non-union. Early intramedullary-screw fixation should be considered in high-performing athletes with acute Jones fractures.

*Japjec et al. further discuss the treatment of proximal fifth metatarsal fractures in athletes.* *The authors looked at 42 athletes with Zone 1 and 2 injuries treated with acute intramedullary screw fixation (33) versus non-operative management (9).* **They found** that athletes treated with acute intramedullary screw fixation with cannulated compression screws had higher Modified Foot Scores, faster return to play, and complete recovery.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Midfoot fusion would result in loss of motion and is not indicated in this scenario.
Answer 2: Percutaneous fixation with an intramedullary compression screw is the treatment of choice.
Answer 4: Fragment excision may be an effective treatment option for the symptomatic non-union of a fifth metatarsal base avulsion fracture (Zone 1), not a Zone 2 fracture.
Answer 5: Continued non-operative management will likely result in continued symptoms and non-union in an athlete with a chronic Jones fracture.
