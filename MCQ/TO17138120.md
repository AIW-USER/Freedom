## Question

Which nerve does not meet at Erb’s point (FNB Pattern Paper 2017)

## Options

1. Suprascapular nerve
2. Nerve to subclavius
3. Long thoracic nerve
4. C5 root

## Correct option

**3. Long thoracic nerve**

## Answer explanation

Erb’s point: Nerve to subclavius, suprascapular nerve, C5 root and C6 root meet at a point known as Erb’s point. After convergence these nerves divide into anterior and posterior divisions of C5 and C6. Injury to Erb's point is commonly sustained at birth or from a fall onto the shoulder. The nerve roots normally involved are C5, C6 and C7 resulting in Erb’s palsy.

![[TO17138120-1.png]]

Also know: Erbs palsy

Erb’s palsy results from injury to nerve roots C5 and C6, with C7 also affected in 50% of instances. The classical sign of Erb’s palsy is the ‘waiter’s tip hand’ or policeman tip deformity. The arm hangs by the side of the chest with internal rotation and pronation of forearm, extension of elbow and flexion of wrist and finger. Palsy of C5 and C6 affects the strength of deltoid, biceps, brachialis, infraspinatus, supraspinatus, and serratus anterior muscles. Also involved are the rhomboids, levator scapulae, and supinator muscles. Sometimes there are sensory deficit over the C5 and C6 dermatomes (lateral and anterior aspects of arm and forearm and radial aspect of hand and fingers)
