## Question

A 23-year-old rookie quarterback in the NFL takes a hit behind the line of scrimmage and is pulled from the game when a spotter notifies the head trainer and sideline physician of a concern for concussion. Which of the following best describes the Immediate Post-Concussion Assessment and Cognitive Testing battery (ImPACT) that is used to assess the player in the locker room?

## Options

1. A balance and postural stability test that involves having the athlete perform 3 stances for 10 seconds each
2. A standardized neuropsychological test that can also be used on the sideline for athletes aged 13 years or older
3. A computer-based neurocognitive test that assess verbal and visual memory, processing speed, reaction time, and impulse control
4. An exam based on saccadic eye movements, language, and concentration performed by having the athlete rapidly read numbers off a card from left to right for 3 successive tests
5. An exam that assesses the integrity of the entire balance system by testing the vestibular, visual and somatosensory systems

## Correct option

**3. A computer-based neurocognitive test that assess verbal and visual memory, processing speed, reaction time, and impulse control**

## Answer explanation

The ImPACT battery is a computer-based neurocognitive test that has proven useful in dictating and standardizing the management of concussions sustained during athletic activities (Answer 3).

Concussions are a subset of mild traumatic brain injury (mTBI) characterized by acute, transient impairment of neurologic function secondary to an impulsive force transmitted to the head. Diagnosis is made by careful neurological evaluation of an athlete following head trauma with a focus on cognitive function, with numerous different exams having been developed to more accurately diagnose and treat concussion-related pathology. The ImPACT battery was designed to be conducted off-field in a quiet and controlled atmosphere, and tests attention, working memory, and processing speed. Its particular usefulness rests in the fact that the results are then compared to the patient's baseline (an ImPACT test performed prior to participation in sport) or to historical controls to guide treatment and return-to-play decisions. The downsides are that it is a fee-based service that requires a computer and/or tablet, a quiet off-field space, and 30 minutes of time, so it used more commonly in the high-level collegiate and professional sports. The SCAT5, conversely, is widely used among high school athletes given that it is free, does not require special software or technology to administer, and can be done on-field in less than 15 minutes.

*Cahill et al. review what orthopaedic surgeons need to know regarding concussion management in sports.* **The authors note** that although orthopaedic surgeons are not expected to replace sports medicine physicians and neurologists with regard to the management of concussions, orthopaedic surgeons, particularly those who are fellowship-trained in sports medicine, must have a current knowledge base of what a concussion is, how a concussion is diagnosed, and how a concussion should be managed. **They conclude** that although most concussions resolve within the first 2 weeks postinjury, a substantial subset of patients who sustain a concussion have symptoms that persist beyond the standard recovery period, and postconcussion syndrome may develop in some patients who sustain a concussion, with symptoms lasting for months after the concussion, underlining the importance of this understanding to improve how orthopaedic surgeons work in concert with and assist sports medicine physicians, athletic trainers, and physical therapists in providing comprehensive care for athletes with a concussion.

*Merritt et al. evaluated the test-retest reliability of symptom indices associated with the ImPACT post-concussion symptom scale (PCSS).* *The authors included 38 undergraduate students (50.0% male) who underwent neuropsychological testing as part of their participation in their psychology department's research subject pool and found that paired-samples t-tests revealed no significant differences between test and retest for any of the symptom variables.* **They concluded** that evaluating additional symptom indices beyond the total symptom score from the PCSS is beneficial, with the findings from this study being applicable to athlete samples to assess reliable change in symptoms following concussion.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: This describes the BESS (Balance Error Scoring System) test, which involves testing bipedal, unipedal, and tandem stance.
Answer 2: This describes the Sports Concussion Assessment Tool 5 (SCAT5), which is an on-field, non-computerized assessment.
Answer 4: This describes the King-Devick (K-D) Test, which examines saccadic eye movements, language, and concentration.
Answer 5: This describes the Sensory Organization Test (SOT).
