## Question

A 21-year-old male college hockey player presents to your clinic with complaints of right index finger pain after a hit. You examine him and obtain radiographs of the hand. A finger fracture that heals in a shortened position at which of the following locations would be expected to cause a proximal interphalangeal joint extensor lag?

## Options

1. Distal phalanx
2. Middle phalanx distal to FDS insertion
3. Middle phalanx proximal to FDS insertion
4. Proximal phalanx
5. Metacarpal neck

## Correct option

**4. Proximal phalanx**

## Answer explanation

A proximal phalanx fracture that heals in a shortened position may lead to an extensor lag at the proximal interphalangeal joint (PIP).

Proximal phalanx fractures tend to fall into apex volar angulation due to the proximal fragment being flexed by the interossei and the distal fragment being extended by the central slip. Extra-articular proximal phalanx fractures with <10° angulation, <2mm of shortening, and no rotational deformity can be treated non-operatively but those falling outside these parameters may require closed reduction percutaneous pinning vs. open reduction internal fixation (ORIF). If the fracture heals in an angulated or shortened position, this may lead to an extensor lag at the PIP joint due to the effective shortening of the extensor tendon. Soft tissue releases or a corrective osteotomy may be required in this case if symptomatic.

*Beekman et al. reviewed a technique to correct PIP joint extensor lag after proximal phalanx fractures that heal in a shortened or angulated position.* **They note** that malunions with 2.5mm and 5.0mm of shortening lead to an average of 6.2 and 26° of extensor lag, respectively. In order to correct the extensor lag they transected both sagittal bands, EDC insertion, and juncturae tendinae, with improvement in lag.

*Vahey et al. looked at the effect of proximal phalanx fracture deformity on extensor tendon function in cadavers.* **They noted** a linear relationship between the degree of proximal phalanx shortening and the extensor lag. There was an average of 12° of extensor lag for each millimeter of bone-tendon discrepancy in the study.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Fractures of the distal phalanx typically involve the nail bed but rarely shorten significantly to the point where joint motion would be threatened. Further, the PIP joint would not be affected.
Answer 2: Apex volar angulation is seen with middle phalanx fractures distal to the FDS insertion. If this fracture were to heal in a shortened position, there would be potential for an extensor lag at the DIP joint.
Answer 3: Apex dorsal angulation is seen with middle phalanx fractures proximal to the FDS insertion and would not cause an extensor lag at the PIP.
Answer 5: Metacarpal neck fractures do not typically result in extensor lag. Regardless, this would not occur at the PIP joint.
