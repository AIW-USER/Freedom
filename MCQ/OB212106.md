## Question

A 61-year-old female presents to your office 6 weeks after having her left wrist casted for a non displaced distal radius fracture. Radiographs demonstrate a well healed fracture. Upon removal of the cast, her hand and wrist are red and very sensitive. You suspect Complex Regional Pain Syndrome. Which of the following differentiates CRPS Type I from CRPS Type II?

## Options

1. No identifiable peripheral nerve involvement
2. Autonomic dysfunction in the affected hand
3. Trophic changes to the dorsum of the hand
4. Increased functional impairment that interferes with activities of daily living
5. Positive three-phase bone scan

## Correct option

**1. No identifiable peripheral nerve involvement**

## Answer explanation

Complex regional pain syndrome (CRPS) Type 1 occurs without an identifiable peripheral nerve component, whereas CRPS Type 2 includes the same findings with identifiable nerve involvement.

CRPS is defined as sustained sympathetic activity in a perpetuated reflex arc which leads to pain out of proportion to physical exam. Other commonly known names for this condition include reflex sympathetic dystrophy (RSD) and causalgia. The International Association for the Study of Pain classifies CRPS in to two groups, both of which have similar symptoms including pain out of proportion, autonomic dysfunction, trophic changes, and functional impairment. CRPS Type I is more common and occurs without evidence of identifiable nerve damage. Causes of CRPS Type I include trauma, casting or tight dressing, but no specific nerve injury is present. CRPS Type II, however, occurs following nerve injury and is less responsive to sympathetic blocks.

*Patterson et al. review complex regional pain syndrome of the upper extremity.* *They report on the similarities of CRPS Type I and Type II and highlight that CRPS Type I occurs in the absence of a specific nerve injury.* **They conclude** that CRPS should be on the differential for any patient with excessive or seemingly inappropriate pain and that early pharmacologic treatment should be considered with one of many different classes of drugs including corticosteroids, anti-depressants anticonvulsants, membrane-stabilizing agents, adrenergic agents, and calcium channel blockers. It is important to note, however, that none of these medications are labeled by the FDA for the treatment of CRPS.

*Borchers et al. performed a comprehensive and critical review on CRPS.* **They report** that CRPS is an umbrella term used to describe a variety of disorders centered on autonomic and motor disturbances. **They conclude** that CRPS is divided into two subgroups, Type I which relates to RSD and similar disorders without a nerve injury, and Type II, which occurs after damage to a specific peripheral nerve.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2, 3, and 4: Autonomic dysfunction, trophic changes, and increased functional impairment are hallmarks of both CRPS Type I and Type II and cannot be used to differentiate the two.
Answer 5: A positive three-phase bone scan can be seen in both Type I and Type II CRPS and cannot be used to differentiate the two. However, a negative test has a high negative predictive value for ruling out CRPS Type I.
