## Answer explanation

Thrombocytopenia Absent Radius (TAR) syndrome is the most likely diagnosis in a patient with complete loss of the radius and an intact thumb.

TAR is a rare congenital disorder characterized by complete absence of the radius and a dramatically decreased platelet count. Severe thrombocytopenia can lead to life threatening hemorrhage. Platelet transfusions are utilized as needed in these patients, and the severity of the thrombocytopenia decreases with age. TAR can be differentiated from radial club hand by the presence of the ipsilateral thumb.

*Toriello et al review TAR syndrome.* **They report** that the diagnosis of TAR syndrome is established in a proband with bilateral absent radii, present thumbs, and thrombocytopenia. **They conclude** that treatment for these patients consists of platelet transfusions and orthopedic procedures as needed to increase function of the limb.

*de Ybarrondo et al review TAR syndrome.* **They report** that the diagnosis classically is based on clinical characteristics, and that newborns born with bilateral radial aplasia who have thumbs should be evaluated for thrombocytopenia. **They conclude** that platelet measurements should be repeated whenever there is physical evidence of increased bleeding (eg, bruising, petechiae).

Figure A demonstrates a patient with fibular hemimelia. Figure B shows a patient with TAR syndrome. Figure C is an example of a hypoplastic thumb. Figure D is an example of congenital scoliosis. Figure E demonstrates pediatric clubfoot.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer A: Fibular hemimelia is not associated with a high incidence of thrombocytopenia.
Answer C: This radiograph demonstrates a hypoplastic thumb, which is not linked with thrombocytopenia.
Answer D: Congenital scoliosis is not associated with thrombocytopenia.
Answer E: There is no specific link between pediatric club foot and thrombocytopenia.
