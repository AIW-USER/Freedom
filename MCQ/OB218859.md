## Question

A 40-year-old right hand dominant male presents for evaluation after a high-pressure injection injury to his left index finger. He sustained the injury while spraying paint two hours prior to presentation. He has minimal to no pain at the injection site. Radiographic evaluation is unremarkable. Which of the following is true regarding this injury?

## Options

1. Antibiotics should be given prior to operative intervention
2. Injections at a pressure of 80 PSI require urgent surgical debridement
3. Management should include a digital block in the ER to prevent progression of the pain
4. The injected material tracks along the flexor sheath
5. There is no difference in outcomes between early and delayed surgical debridement

## Correct option

**1. Antibiotics should be given prior to operative intervention**

## Answer explanation

High-pressure injections of the hand are medical and surgical emergencies that require early broad-spectrum antibiotics, tetanus prophylaxis, and early surgical debridement. Prompt delivery of antibiotics is crucial and should not be delayed prior to surgery.

Injection injuries to the hand can cause extensive soft tissue damage, despite a usually benign appearing entry wound. It is most common in young male laborers involving their non-dominant index finger. The most important prognostic variables include time to definitive management, force of the injection, volume injected, and type of solution injected. Management consists of early antibiotic and tetanus administration. With injections of water and air, close monitoring in the hospital may be attempted. Injections of paints, grease, or solvents require urgent operative debridement. Complications are common after this injury and can consist of chronic stiffness, infection, and amputation.

*Cannon reviewed the management of high-pressure injection injuries of the hand.* *He discusses the factors that play a role in the prognosis including the type of material injected, location of injection, involved pressure, and timing to surgical decompression and debridement.* *He discusses the importance of early broad-spectrum antibiotics, tetanus, and surgical debridement in the management of these injuries.*

*Bean et al. reviewed outcomes on 19 patients with high-pressure water injections of the hand.* Ten were treated with early surgery and nine were treated without early debridement. In the group without early surgery, 3 required re-operation including a debridement of flexor tenosynovitis, fingertip amputation, and compartment release. **They conclude** that these injuries are not benign and require early medical treatment and consideration for early surgical debridement.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: The pressure required to break the skin is 100 PSI.
Answer 3: A digital block has the potential to decrease perfusion to the digit and is not indicated in this setting.
Answer 4: High-pressure injections tend to track along the neurovascular bundles.
Answer 5: In the setting of high-pressure injection of paints and solvents, early debridement (< 6 hours) results in fewer complications.
