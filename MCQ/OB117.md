## Question

A 24-year-old male suffered an inversion injury 6 months ago. Since then he has had persistent numbness and tingling across the dorsum of his foot that is worse with plantar flexion and inversion of foot. A release of the involved nerve, shown in Figure V, is performed. Which of the following most likely describes the cause of his symptoms?

## Options

1. Compression of the superficial peroneal nerve by fascial band proximal to lateral malleolus
2. Compression of the superficial peroneal nerve by the superior extensor retinaculum
3. Compression of the deep peroneal nerve by the inferior extensor retinaculum
4. Compression of the superficial peroneal nerve by a ganglion cyst
5. Compression of the sural nerve 2cm proximal to the tip of the fibula

## Correct option

**1. Compression of the superficial peroneal nerve by fascial band proximal to lateral malleolus**

## Answer explanation

The clinical scenario is consistent with nerve entrapment of the superficial peroneal nerve (SPN), which is most commonly caused by a fascial band approximately 12 cm proximal to the lateral malleolus.

The common peroneal nerve branches into the deep peroneal nerve and superfical peroneal nerve. The superficial peroneal nerve runs in the lateral leg compartment, supplies the peroneus longus and brevis, and then provides sensation to the dorsum of the foot. While rare, entrapment has been reported and patients may benefit from fascial release.

*Styf et al. diagnosed SPN entrapment in 19 legs (17 patients), and performed releases in 14 patients and local fasciectomies in 3 patients.* **They reported** 80% symptom free/satisfied with result.

*Yang et al. report a case of SPN entrapment associated with herniation through a fascial defect.* Fascial defects are commonly associated with SPN entrapment. Closure of the fascial defect worsens symptoms and release, rather than closure, is indicated.

Video V shows the superficial peroneal nerve following release of the proximal fascia. Illustration A shows a diagram of the site of SPN entrapment. Illustrations B and C (Yang et al. J Neurosurg 2006) show clinical photos of SPN release.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 2. The most common site of entrapment is 12 cm proximal to the lateral malleolus.
Answer 3. Deep peroneal nerve entrapment sensory symptoms would localize to the first dorsal web space
Answer 4. Fascial compression is more common than mass effect lesion.
Answer 5. Sural nerve entrapment can occur at any site along the course of the nerve. In the foot, this would localize to the lateral dorsum and heel.
