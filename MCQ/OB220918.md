## Question

A 9-year-old female presents to your office with right ankle pain after she sprained it during recess 1 week ago. She notes a history of recurrent bilateral ankle sprains. Also notes chronic right knee pain and clumsiness that has been ongoing for years without an extensive workup. On physical examination, she notes tenderness and has mild swelling in the right medial ankle. An MRI scan demonstrates the expected finding, as shown in Figure 1. Also, on physical exam, she has significant laxity without an endpoint during Lachman testing of the right knee. Of the following options, which additional physical exam finding is most likely present and associated with this patient's condition?

## Options

1. Cafe-au-lait spots
2. Posteromedial tibial bowing
3. Leg length discrepancy
4. Pes planovalgus
5. Pain with hip flexion, adduction, and internal rotation

## Correct option

**3. Leg length discrepancy**

## Answer explanation

Patient has a talocalcaneal coalition that was diagnosed on an MRI following her most recent ankle sprain. She also has anterior cruciate ligament (ACL) insufficiency. Both of these conditions are associated with fibular deficiency and, therefore, a significant leg length discrepancy (answer 3).

Fibular deficiency (previously known as fibular hemimelia) is a congenital condition caused by a shortening or absence of the fibula. It is the most common congenital long bone deficiency and has been associated with many other conditions including anteromedial tibial bowing, ankle instability (ball-and-socket ankle), clubfoot, tarsal coalition (50% incidence), absent lateral rays, coxa vara, hip dysplasia, cruciate ligament deficiency, genu valgum, and a significant leg length discrepancy (shortened femur and/or tibia). Diagnosis is based on physical exam and radiographs or advanced imaging. Treatment ranges from observation and bracing or shoe lifts to limb lengthening/shortening procedures or amputation in the nonfunctional extremity.

*Hamdy et al. wrote a JAAOS review on congenital fibular deficiency (CFD).* **They note** that the etiology of this condition is still unclear. They explain that the Birch classification is helpful as it guides treatment to achieve normal weight bearing, a functional plantigrade foot, and equal limb length. **They conclude** that outcomes following amputation for severe forms of CFD have shown favorable results and fewer complications compared with those of limb lengthening.

*Roux et al. conducted a retrospective cohort study investigating cruciate ligaments in children with CFD.* *This included 69 knees, of which 95% and 60% had aplasia/hypoplasia of the ACL and PCL, respectively.* **They conclude** that an active search for the presence of congenital cruciate deficiency in patients with CLD is important to prevent knee subluxation during femoral lengthening procedures.

*Stevens et al. conducted a case series of 20 patients with CFD (also known as postaxial hypoplasia of the lower extremity).* **They found** significant leg length discrepancies in addition to a wide spectrum of other musculoskeletal conditions involving the ipsilateral hip, femur, knee, tibia/fibula, ankle, and foot. **The authors advocate** for a systematic approach combining limb lengthening with hemiepiphysiodesis of the distal femur and/or ankle to correct valgus alignment and establish a neutral mechanical axis with the ultimate goal to achieve stable and symmetric joints at skeletal maturity. **They conclude** that this approach has led to satisfactory outcomes in 10 of their patients who have reached skeletal maturity.

Figure 1 is a coronal MRI demonstrating a tarsal coalition between the talus and calcaneus.

Incorrect answer choices:
Answer 1: Cafe-au-lait spots are commonly seen in a patient with Neurofibromatosis type 1, not fibular deficiency.
Answer 2: Posteromedial tibial bowing is not commonly seen in fibular deficiency; anteromedial tibial bowing is.
Answer 4: Pes planovalgus is not associated with fibular deficiency; talipes equinovarus is.
Answer 5: Pain with hip flexion, adduction, and internal rotation (FADIR test) is commonly seen in femoroacetabular impingement, which is not associated with fibular deficiency. Developmental dysplasia of the hip is a more commonly associated condition with fibular deficiency.
