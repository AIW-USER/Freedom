## Question

A 3-year old female with spastic right hemiparesis and toe walking is currently being treated with AFO bracing. Her parents are concerned that the brace is not fitting well and is not improving the toe walking. Right ankle dorsiflexion is 10 degrees short of neutral position. Left ankle dorsiflexion to +15 degrees. She received a Botulinum injection into the right gastrocnemius one month ago. What is the best next step in treatment?

## Options

1. Continue brace treatment for Right foot and ankle only
2. Repeat Right calf botox injection
3. Serial casting of Right foot and ankle
4. Implantation of intrathecal baclofen pump
5. Right percutaneous heel cord lengthening

## Correct option

**3. Serial casting of Right foot and ankle**

## Answer explanation

This patient is unable to achieve a right plantigrade foot. Serial casting is an effective treatment method for patients less than 6 years old with mild spasticity and should allow her to resume bracing once the foot is plantigrade.

Equinus deformity of the ankle may be due to abnormal shortening of the muscle, an exaggerated stretch reflex, or a combination of these pathologies. The goal of both non-operative and operative treatment is to achieve and maintain a plantigrade foot, prevent skeletal deformity, and improve gait efficiency. Non-operative treatments for equinus include serial casting, physical therapy, intramuscular injections of botulinum toxin A, and ankle-foot-orthoses (AFO). Surgical treatment of equinus deformity should be delayed until the patient is at least 6 years old to prevent recurrence.

*Cottalorda et al. performed a retrospective study analyzing the value of serial corrective casts in the management of toe-walking children aged less than six years with cerebral palsy.* After removal of the casts all patients showed improved function with plantigrade gait. Mean passive dorsiflexion was 20 degrees with knee in extension and 28 degrees with the knee in flexion after cast removal.

*Brouwer et al. examined the neuromuscular function and gait in children with cerebral palsy (CP) and idiopathic toe walking (ITW) (mean age 7.1yrs) treated with serial casting for 3 to 6 weeks.* After casting, both groups were found to have increased dorsiflexion range, decreased resistance to passive stretch, and produced maximal plantar flexor torques in dorsiflexed positions. **The authors noted** that the effects of serial casting may be longer lasting in patients with ITW.

*Boyd et al. prospectively studied the effects of botulinum toxin A (BTX/A) on the gastroc-soleus cycle in ambulatory patients with CP.* *All children demonstrated improvements in sagittal ankle kinematics.* Using 3-D gait analysis the authors also devised two new measures of ankle kinetics: ankle moment quotient (AMQ) and ankle power quotient (APQ). There was a statistically significant improvement in AMQ and APQ, which **the authors concluded** provides evidence of 'biomechanics transformation of muscle'.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The patient has failed brace treatment and currently has an unbraceable foot (ie. unable to dorsiflex to neutral). Continuing brace treatment will be ineffective at this stage.
Answer 2: Botulinum toxin A is effective when used in conjunction with serial casting inpatients <6 years of age. Injections should not be given more frequently than every 3 months.
Answer 4: Baclofen pumps are used for diplegia CP, not hemiplegic.
Answer 5: Percutaneous heel cord lengthening for toe walking is not recommended in patients in this age group.
