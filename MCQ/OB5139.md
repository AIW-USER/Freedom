## Question

Figure A shows a radiograph of a 16-year-old female complaining of significant left hip pain after a low energy fall. She has a past medical history of precocious puberty and cafe-au-lait spots. A follow-up CT scan shows an undisplaced pathologic fracture. What would be the recommended treatment?

![[q5139-a-fibrous-dysplasia-femur.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Observation and restricted weight-bearing for 6 weeks
2. Radiation therapy and restricted weight-bearing for 6 weeks
3. Biopsy, chemotherapy and wide excision
4. Biopsy, curettage, bone grafting and internal fixation
5. Percutaneous screw fixation and post-operative radiation therapy

## Correct option

**4. Biopsy, curettage, bone grafting and internal fixation**

## Answer explanation

This patient has polyostotic fibrous dysplasia with associated pathologic fracture. The lesion is greater than 2/3rds the diameter of bone. The most appropriate treatment would be biopsy, curettage, bone grafting and lesion spanning internal fixation.

Fibrous dysplasia can be monostotic (only a single bone is affected = most common), or polyostotic (more than one bone is involved). Replacement of normal bone in fibrous dysplasia often is associated with pain, misshapen bones, and risk of fracture.

*Ippolito et al. reviewed the surgical treatment of coxa vara in polyostotic fibrous dysplasia.* The femoral neck-shaft angle averaged 83° before surgery and was corrected to a mean of 130° using an intertrochanteric osteotomy and proximal blade plate or screw-plate construct.

*Parekh et al. reviewed fibrous dysplasia.* **They state** coxa vara usually worsens with growth, causing progressive shepherd’s crook deformity and valgus deformity of the distal aspect of the femur. This may cause pain, limping, reduced range of motion and increased risk of fracture.

Figure A shows an isolated hip lesion consistent with fibrous dysplasia. Illustration A shows an isolated pertrochanteric lesion in the left hip with associated coxa vara. Illustration B shows intertrochanteric osteotomy with blade plate fixation.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Hip fractures are generally treated with open reduction and internal fixation.
Answer 2: Radiation therapy would not be indicated with fibrous dysplasia.
Answer 3: Chemotherapy and wide resection would be indicted in malignant disease.
Answer 5: Percutaneous screw fixation would not stabilize this lesion. The lateral cortex bone is soft and would not provide enough stability to hold the screws. Radiation therapy would not be indicated with fibrous dysplasia.
