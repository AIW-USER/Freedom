## Question

Post THR a patient has landed with periprosthetic fracture as shown in X ray. Ideal management for this case would be

![[TO21733116-1.png]]

## Options

1. Conservative
2. Fracture fixation with Encirclage/ plating
3. Revision with long stem
4. Girdlestone

## Correct option

**3. Revision with long stem**

## Answer explanation

Ans: C
EXPLANATION: -
Shown is a type B2 periprosthetic hip fracture according to Vancouver classification and revision with long stem would be best pick.

![[TO21733116-2.jpg]]

![[TO21733116-3.png]]

*Unstable prosthesis is one which has shown position change (varus/ valgus neck angle) or subsidence after fracture as compared to preoperative scenario.

Treatment:
* Type A fractures are associated with osteolysis, which requires treatment of the factor causing Osteolysis
* Type B1 periprosthetic fractures are treated surgically by ORIF. Cortical strut grafts, cerclage cables, or Locking Compression Plates may be used
* Type B2 fractures: are usually treated with revision of the femoral component and fixation of the fracture fragment.
* Type B3 fracture: Requires femoral component revision along with proximal femoral allograft or proximal femoral replacement
* Type C are usually treated with ORIF. The hip replacement components are, left alone.
