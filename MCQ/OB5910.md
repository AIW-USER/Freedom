## Question

Compared to a 60-year-old male with the same medical comorbidities, a 75-year-old male with an incomplete cervical spinal cord injury has an increased risk of:

## Options

1. More severe neurological deficits at presentation
2. Chronic pain
3. Epidural hematoma
4. Paralysis
5. Mortality

## Correct option

**5. Mortality**

## Answer explanation

Geriatric patients with cervical spine injuries have a higher mortality rate than matched adult patients. In contrast, geriatric patients are less likely to present with neurologic deficits or paralysis and are not at an increased risk of developing an epidural hematoma or chronic pain.

The incidence of cervical spinal cord injuries in the elderly population is increasing. The mechanism of injury is usually of lower energy as compared to the younger population, and often involves a fall from standing height with an associated head-strike. Plain radiographs should be obtained with further evaluation with CT and MRI if there is a concern for fracture or spinal cord involvement. The management of stable injuries includes immobilization in a rigid cervical collar and operative intervention (posterior or anterior/posterior decompression and fusion) for unstable injuries.

*Fassett et al. retrospectively reviewed a spinal cord injury (SCI) database and compared parameters including admission American Spinal Injury Association (ASIA) motor score, level of SCI, mechanism of injury, and mortality data between patients 70 years of age and older, with a younger cohort.* **They found** that geriatric patients were found to be less likely to have severe neurological deficits. However, mortality rates, both during hospitalization and during the first year after injury, were much higher in the geriatric population. The in-hospital mortality rate was 3.2% for adult patients younger than 70 years of age and 27.7% for geriatric patient, while the 1-year mortality rate was 5.4% for younger adult patients and 44.4% for geriatric patients.

*Irwin et al. performed a retrospective review of pre-injury patient factors, injury and treatment patterns, and in-hospital versus 60-day mortality in adult and geriatric spinal injury patients.* **They found** that geriatric patients had fewer cervical injuries, lower energy injuries, less severe overall injuries, decreased paralysis, increased preexisting conditions, decreased treatment at level 1 and 2 treatment centers, and decreased odds of surgical treatment. Geriatric patients also had increased 60-day in-hospital mortality and increased mortality associated with cervical spine injury.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Geriatric patients have been found to have a lower incidence of neurologic deficits as compared to adults.
Answer 2: Chronic pain has not been found in higher incidence after cervical spine trauma in geriatric patients.
Answer 3: Geriatric patients have not been found to have a higher incidence of epidural hematomas than the general population following cervical spine trauma.
Answer 4: Paralysis was found to be less common following cervical spinal cord injury among geriatric patients as compared to their matched adult counterparts.
