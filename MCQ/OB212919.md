## Question

## Options

1. Midfoot arch collapse
2. Hindfoot varus
3. Hallux MTP dorsiflexion
4. Hallux MTP plantarflexion
5. Hallux MTP adduction

## Correct option

**3. Hallux MTP dorsiflexion**

## Answer explanation

This patient has hallux rigidus and is using a Morton's extension orthosis, which limits 1st MTP dorsiflexion.

Hallux rigidus is a term to describe the condition characterized by pain and limited motion, particularly dorsiflexion, at the 1st metatarsophalangeal joint. Pain and limitations in dorsiflexion lead to forefoot accommodation through internal rotation leading to a subsequent decrease in push-off strength and transfer metatarsalgia. Surgical treatment is indicated when nonoperative measures have failed to provide relief and is tailored to each patient based on the degree of arthritis at the MTP joint. NSAIDs and a dorsiflexion-limiting orthosis for the 1st MTP joint are the first steps in nonoperative treatment.

*Yee et al. authored a current concepts review of hallux rigidus.* *They discuss the controversies regarding the various surgical techniques used for management but urge a trial of nonoperative treatment, as some patient have a benign natural history to the condition.*

*Coughlin et al. retrospectively reviewed characteristics of 114 patients who underwent cheilectomy or arthrodesis for hallux rigidus.* **They found** no association between hallux rigidus and primus elevatus, first ray hypermobility, long first metatarsal, achilles or gastrocnemius tightness, abnormal foot posture, symptomatic hallux valgus, adolescent onset, shoeware, or occupation. They did find an association between hallux rigidus and hallux valgus interphalangeus, bilateral involvement in patients with a family history, unilateral involvement following a history of trauma, and female gender.

*Deland et al. reviewed hallux rigidus treatment.* **They advocate** cheilectomy or cheilectomy and proximal phalanx osteotomy for early to mid-stage hallux rigidus and arthrodesis and arthroplasty for late-stage disease.

Figure A is an AP radiograph demonstrating joint space narrowing with osteophyte formation within the hallux MTP joint. Figure B is a photograph of a morton's extension. Illustration A are AP and lateral radiographs demonstrating hallux rigidus, with a significant dorsal osteophyte seen on the lateral view.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1, 2, 4, and 5: The Morton's extension orthosis is designed to limit MTP dorsiflexion, as dorsal osteophyte impingement causes the pain seen in hallux rigidus.
