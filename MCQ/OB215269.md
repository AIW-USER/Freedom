## Question

Which of the following represents the median for life expectancy in a patient with primary lung carcinoma with metastatic bone disease?

## Options

1. 1 month
2. 7 months
3. 16 months
4. 24 months
5. 48 months

## Correct option

**2. 7 months**

## Answer explanation

At a median of roughly 7 months survival, the prognosis of primary lung carcinoma with metastatic bone disease is the worst among the carcinomas that are most likely to spread to bone(breast, lung, thyroid, kidney, and prostate).

The life expectancy of patients with metastatic skeletal lesions is an important concept to understand in order to correctly counsel patients regarding expectations. The life expectancy of patients with metastatic lung carcinoma is shorter than all the other cancers presented above, with roughly 50% of patients surviving more than 6 months from the diagnosis of skeletal metastasis. In general, patients with breast, thyroid, and prostate carcinoma live longer than patients with other metastatic lesions and patients with renal cell carcinoma have a variable life expectancy, depending on their medical condition at the time of diagnosis. The median survival in patients with various types of metastatic bone disease is 1) lung: 6 months; 2) renal: variable depending on medical condition but may be as short as 6 months or as long as 4-5 years with isolated bone metastasis; 3) breast: 24 months; 4) prostate: 40 months; 5) thyroid: 48 months.

Coleman reviews the features of metastatic bone disease. The author states the prognosis of breast CA is measured in months while the other carcinomas that spread to bone have a prognosis measured in years. He states patients with coexisting metastasis to bone and extraosseous sites have a poor prognosis as well.

Coleman reviews the skeletal complications of malignant bone disease discussing pathogenesis, pain generators, and potential treatment regimes. Specifically, patients with bone metastases tend to outlive patients with visceral involvement. Management aimed at the prevention of fracture or treatment of bone-related cancer pain can significantly improve the cancer-related morbidity associated with their diagnosis. Aggressive treatment with bisphosphonates has been shown to improve quality of life and decrease skeletally related events.

*Sugiura et al reviewed the survival and prognostic factors in 118 patients with bony metastases and primary lung carcinoma.* **They found** mean survival was roughly 9 months and median survival was 7.2 months in this group of patients. **They found** a survival rate of 59.9% at 6 months. **They also found** a favorable prognosis in females, PS 1 or less, adenocarcinoma, patients with solitary bone mets, no mets to appendicular bones, no pathologic fracture, use of systemic chemotherapy, and use of gefitinib. **They conclude** that survival after bone metastasis is improved with the use of EGFR inhibitors.

Incorrect Answer:
Answer 1: A patient with primary lung cancer and metastatic bone disease would be expected to survive longer than one month.
Answer 3: The median survival in a patient with primary lung cancer and bony metastatic disease falls closer to 6 months than 16 months.
Answer 4: Breast cancer with metastatic bone disease has a survival of roughly 24 months.
Answer 5: Thyroid cancer with metastatic bone disease has a survival of roughly 48 months.
