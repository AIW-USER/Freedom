## Question

A 68-year-old female presents to your clinic with long-standing right shoulder pain. She reports having a fall from a ladder 6 years ago and having "issues" with her shoulder since that time. Figure A demonstrates her examination when asked to abduct her shoulder. However, you are able to passively abduct her shoulder to 160°. She can externally rotate to 30° with the arm at her side and can internally rotate to her right hip. She denies any paresthesias. She has done 6 weeks of physical therapy and had one corticosteroid injection without any pain relief or change in function. Radiographs obtained in your office demonstrate acetabularization of the acromion and moderate arthritis of the glenohumeral joint. You discuss with her that her best option is reverse total shoulder arthroplasty (rTSA), due to the cause of her shoulder dysfunction. Which of the following best describes this pathologic process?

![[q218146-a-pseduoparalysis-right-arm.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Fibroblastic proliferation and thickening of joint capsule
2. Suprascapular nerve injury
3. C5 radiculopathy
4. Inability of rotator cuff to depress humeral head
5. Inferior humeral head subluxation

## Correct option

**4. Inability of rotator cuff to depress humeral head**

## Answer explanation

Pseudoparalysis of the arm is characteristic of superior rotator cuff insufficiency and its inability to depress the humeral head as the deltoid powers the shoulder into abduction (Answer 4).

Rotator cuff arthropathy is characterized by a high-riding humeral head secondary to superior rotator cuff insufficiency. Normally, the rotator cuff functions to center the humeral head on the glenoid and provide a compressive force throughout a range of motion as the deltoid powers the arm into abduction and forward elevation. Without these forces intact, the humerus subluxes superiorly with overhead motion. Given the inability to keep the humeral head reduced, the deltoid lever arm is significantly diminished and leads to a pseudoparalytic appearance on exam (Figure A). Secondarily, this leads to eccentric wear, degenerative changes of the glenohumeral joint, and abnormal wear ("acetabularization") of the undersurface of the acromion. In cases of a chronic rotator cuff tear with pseudoparalysis and/or rotator cuff arthropathy, reverse total shoulder arthroplasty (rTSA) is the primary surgical option, as it alters the center of rotation of the shoulder to allow the deltoid to power the shoulder throughout its range of motion.

*Drake et al. looked at indications for rTSA in rotator cuff disease,* **noting** that rTSA implants restore deltoid tension and recreate a functional range of motion to the cuff-deficient shoulder. In their study, rTSA improved pain and function in patients with both pseudoparalysis and rotator cuff arthropathy. **They concluded** that in this population, rTSA could offer significant improvements in lifestyle and function and patient satisfaction was high.

*Patzer looked at rTSA in patients with rotator cuff arthropathy who had rotator cuff tears that were retracted with muscle atrophy.* **They note** that rTSA was their procedure of choice due to its ability to move the center of rotation medial and inferior and optimize the deltoid lever arm. They go on to discuss specific techniques for rTSA in this patient population in order to maximize function and stability.

Figure A demonstrates right shoulder pseudoparalysis as the patient attempts to abduct the shoulder.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Fibroblastic proliferation and joint capsule thickening describe the pathologic process which takes place in adhesive capsulitis, not rotator cuff arthropathy. Additionally, the patient has normal passive shoulder range of motion.
Answer 2: Injury to the suprascapular nerve would cause weakness to both the supra- and infraspinatus. Pseudoparalysis is not normally an examination finding as the superior cuff remains intact and exerts a downward force on the humeral head as the deltoid powers the shoulder into abduction.
Answer 3: C5 radiculopathy can lead to weakness of the deltoid and superior rotator cuff muscles but does not present with pseudoparalysis and usually has associated paresthesias.
Answer 5: Superior humeral head subluxation occurs in patients with rotator cuff arthropathy as the superior rotator cuff is functionally absent and is not able to exert a depression force as the deltoid powers the shoulder into abduction.
