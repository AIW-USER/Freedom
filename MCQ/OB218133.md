## Question

A 21-year-old male fell off of his mountain bike and sustained the injury shown in Figure A. He is counseled that he should have an open reduction and internal fixation given his fracture type and amount of displacement. Which of the following muscles provide the deforming forces on the fracture and make closed reduction less favorable in the setting of significantly displaced intra-articular fracture-dislocations of the base of the thumb?

![[q218133-a-screenshot-2023-07-28-at-5-26-07-am.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Abductor pollicis brevis, flexor pollicis brevis, opponens pollicis
2. Adductor pollicis, flexor pollicis brevis, opponens pollicis
3. Abductor pollicis longus, extensor pollicis longus, adductor pollicis
4. Adductor pollicis, flexor pollicis longus, abductor pollicis brevis
5. Abductor pollicis longus, extensor pollicis longus, opponens pollicis

## Correct option

**3. Abductor pollicis longus, extensor pollicis longus, adductor pollicis**

## Answer explanation

The patient has sustained a Bennett fracture, the displacement of which is affected by the deforming forces of the abductor pollicis longus (APL), extensor pollicis longus (EPL), and adductor pollicis (Illustration A).

Bennett fractures represent intra-articular fractures of the base of the thumb and can be a challenging subset of injuries to treat. Imperfect reductions and the deforming forces of the surrounding muscles and tendons lead to increased joint contact pressures and a subsequent predisposition to early arthritis, while excessive angulation may lead to malunion with a metacarpophalangeal joint hyperextension deformity. In general, operative fixation is recommended in the setting of intra-articular fractures with > 1mm of displacement.

Carlsen, et al. published a current concepts review of Bennett fractures, Rolando fractures, and ulnar collateral ligament injuries. The authors note that in intra-articular fractures of the base of the thumb, anatomic congruity is more important than the type of fracture fixation technique for long-term success. They conclude by recommending that open reduction should be considered in those circumstances where greater than 2mm of articular step-off persists despite closed reduction attempts.

Rivlin, et al. provide a case study of a patient with a Bennett fracture and a review of the literature. The authors make a case for performing a closed reduction and percutaneous pinning (CRPP), given that early radiographic signs of arthritis seem common, even after open reduction and internal fixation. They conclude that prospective randomized trials would help determine if open or arthroscopic-assisted reduction and internal fixation have any advantages over percutaneous pin fixation in terms of alignment, arthrosis in the short and long term, symptoms and disability, adverse events, and resource utilization.

Figure 1 is a radiograph depicting a Bennett fracture, demonstrating an intra-articular fracture of the base of the thumb metacarpal with a dislocation of the first CMC joint. Illustration A is a pictorial demonstration of the deforming forces.

Incorrect answers:
Answers 1,2,4,5: Though the extensor pollicis brevis (EPB) may also contribute a small deforming force, none of the additional muscles listed cause the prototypical dorsal, proximal, and radial dislocation of the thumb metacarpal from the volar ulnar fracture fragment (which, itself, is held in place by the anterior oblique ligament, or volar "beak" ligament).
