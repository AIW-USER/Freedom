## Question

A 34-year-old male presents to the emergency department after cutting his wrist on a piece of glass while punching a glass window. His laceration is just proximal to the distal wrist crease. On exam, the patient has 6 mm two-point discrimination along the ulnar hand and small finger, with greater than 10 mm two-point discrimination over the thumb and index finger. He can abduct his index finger and oppose his thumb. Intraoperative exam confirms complete median nerve laceration just proximal to the carpal tunnel. Which anatomical variant explains the physical exam findings in this patient?

## Options

1. Riche-Cannieu anastomosis
2. Marinacci anastomosis
3. Martin-Gruber anastomosis
4. Berrettini anastomosis
5. Anconeous epitrochlearis

## Correct option

**1. Riche-Cannieu anastomosis**

## Answer explanation

This patient presents with a laceration of the median nerve and preserved thenar function. Riche-Cannieu Anastomosis (RCA) carries motor fibers from the deep branch of the ulnar nerve to the recurrent branch of the median nerve (answer choice 1).

Patients with median and ulnar nerve interconnections can present with irregular physical exams and electrodiagnostic findings. There are four main interconnections reported between the median and ulnar nerve. Martin-Gruber and Marinacci anastomose in the forearm, while Riche-Cannieu and Berettini anastomose in the hand. RCA is often referred to as an “all ulnar hand”. This is because the deep branch of the ulnar nerve connects with the recurrent branch of the median nerve, resulting in thenar innervation from the ulnar nerve. In this anatomic variant, the median nerve retains sensory innervation in the radial aspect of the volar hand.

*Smith et al. published a review article regarding the 4 nerve anastomoses listed above.* **The authors report** RCA occurs in the hand with a pooled prevalence of approximately 55%. Noting, it may be feasible to consider RCA a normal anatomic variant. **The authors conclude** that knowledge of this variant is essential to the management of traumatic and compressive median nerve injuries.

*Martin et al. published a review article on the 4 interconnections mentioned above between the median and ulnar nerve.* *The review article discusses the anatomy, physical exam, and electrodiagnostic studies associated with each anastomosis.* **The authors note** that the thenar muscles in a hand with an RCA may have dual innervation from the recurrent branch of the median nerve and deep branch of the ulnar nerve. **The authors recommend** careful physical and electrodiagnostic studies when evaluating median nerve injury.

Illustrations A-D show the following anastomoses in order: Martin-Gruber, Marinacci, Riche-Cannieu, and Berettini.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 2: A Marinacci anastomosis travels from the ulnar nerve to the median nerve in the forearm, and would not present with preserved thenar function.
Answer 3: The Martin-Gruber anastomosis occurs between either the anterior interosseous nerve (AIN) or median nerve proximally, with a connection to the ulnar nerve in the forearm
Answer 4: Berrettini anastomosis is a sensory interconnection between the hand's ulnar and median nerve, and would not present with preserved thenar function.
Answer 5: Anconeous epitrochlearis can present with compression of the ulnar nerve at the cubital tunnel, which would have combined sensory and motor deficits in the ulnar nerve.
