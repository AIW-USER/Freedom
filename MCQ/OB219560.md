## Question

A professional hockey player presents to your office after a recent shoulder dislocation. He has a history of a prior bankart repair two years ago from which he healed uneventfully. MR arthrogram of the shoulder demonstrates a Hill-Sachs lesion with 16% glenoid bone loss. You recommend an open Latarjet stabilization procedure. You discuss the possibility of neurovascular injury during the informed consent process. Which of the following structures is at the greatest risk for injury at the inferior aspect of the glenoid with the arm in external rotation?

## Options

1. Thoracoacromial artery
2. Axillary artery
3. Musculocutaneous nerve
4. Radial nerve
5. Ulnar nerve

## Correct option

**2. Axillary artery**

## Answer explanation

Open shoulder surgery can place neurovascular structures at risk, with the axillary artery being particularly vulnerable at the inferior aspect of the glenoid with the arm in external rotation.

Shoulder instability is a common problem in young, contact athletes. In patients who have failed soft tissue procedures (labral repair, capsulorrhaphy) or have increasing degrees of glenoid bone loss, a Latarjet coracoid transfer can be a useful surgical technique to restore stability. However, with any open shoulder surgery, there are risks of injury to the neurovascular structures of the shoulder. The main structures at highest risk include the axillary nerve, axillary artery, and the musculocutaneous nerve. It is important to understand the trajectory of these structures and also understand that their relationships with the associated bony structures may change as the arm moves in space. Injury to the axillary artery can be particularly devastating, and the artery is most at risk at the inferior aspect of the glenoid with the arm in 90° or more of external rotation as this tends to place tension on the neurovascular structures and bring them closer to the inferior glenoid. Care should be taken to avoid damaging these important structures with cautery, sharp tools, or retractors.

*Stone et al. reviewed the surgical anatomy of the axillary artery and implications for open shoulder surgery via the dissection of 18 cadaver shoulders.* The axillary artery consistently measured at least 2 cm from the coracoid base and tip, while the distance from the glenoid face decreased progressively with increasing degrees of shoulder external rotation. **They noted** that the axillary artery was closest to the inferior glenoid at 90° of external rotation, measuring about 4.1mm, compared to with the arm at neutral rotation (1-1.8cm).

*Freehill et al. looked at neurovascular alterations after Latarjet open coracoid transfer.* **They noted** that the musculocutaneous nerve consistently changed positions in a superior/inferior direction after Latarjet and that the musculocutaneous and axillary nerves were often more lax. **They also noted**, importantly, that the position of the axillary artery relative to their defined landmarks was unchanged after Latarjet.

*McFarland et al. examined the relationship of the brachial plexus and axillary artery to the glenoid.* **They noted** that the brachial plexus and axillary artery were within 1-2 cm of the glenoid rim, with the axillary artery as close as 5mm in some cases. **They noted** that abduction did not seem to change the relationship of the neurovascular structures relative to the glenoid.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The thoracoacromial artery branches from the axillary artery around the coracoid and does not run inferior to the glenoid despite it giving off four separate branches (deltoid, clavicular, acromial, pectoral).
Answer 3: The musculocutaneous nerve is the terminal branch of the lateral cord of the brachial plexus and pierces the coracobrachialis medial to the coracoid process. It may be intimately associated with the glenoid rim medially but typically not inferiorly.
Answer 4: The radial nerve is the terminal branch of the posterior cord of the brachial plexus and exits the axilla posteriorly through the triangular interval. It begins its posterior course medial to the glenoid and is not in danger at the inferior glenoid.
Answer 5: The ulnar nerve is the terminal branch of the medial cord of the brachial plexus and courses through the axilla and into the medial intermuscular septum of the upper arm but is not in danger at the inferior glenoid.
