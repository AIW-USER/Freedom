## Question

Which of the following statements is true when comparing Low-molecular-weight heparin (LMWH) and aspirin for deep venous thrombosis (DVT) prophylaxis in total hip and knee arthroplasty?

## Options

1. LMWH results in a significantly increased risk of major bleeding events postoperatively
2. No major difference in mortality rates
3. Aspirin is noninferior to LMWH in regards to the rate of symptomatic DVT within 90 days postoperatively
4. Aspirin results in a lower rate of symptomatic DVT within 90 days after surgery
5. LMWH results in a significantly higher rate of joint-related admission within 90 days

## Correct option

**2. No major difference in mortality rates**

## Answer explanation

When comparing LMWH and Aspirin for DVT prophylaxis following total hip and knee arthroplasty, no major difference has been found in mortality rates between the two medications (Answer 2).

There are various types of anticoagulation and antiplatelet medications used for DVT prophylaxis in elective and non-elective orthopedic procedures. The most common medications used for venous thromboembolism (VTE) prophylaxis include low molecular weight heparin (LMWH), Aspirin, Coumadin (Warfarin), Apixaban (Eliquis), and Rivaroxaban (Xarelto). Recently, studies have been comparing the various anticoagulation and antiplatelet (Aspirin) medications regarding the rate of symptomatic DVT or pulmonary embolism (PE) postoperatively. These medications have been found to significantly reduce the rates of VTE postoperatively in total hip and knee arthroplasty when compared to no chemoprophylaxis.

*Borazjani et al. conducted a systematic review and meta-analysis of 19 studies evaluating the efficacy of Aspirin in preventing DVTs after primary or revision knee and hip arthroplasty.* **They found** that the all-cause mortality rate comparing Aspirin (0.18%) to all other anticoagulants (0.14%) was not significantly different (OR = 1.25; 95% CI = 0.79 to 1.97). Aspirin reduced the risk of pulmonary emboli by 0.58%, which was similar to other anticoagulants (0.6%) without statistical significance (OR = 1.26; 95% CI = 0.76 to 2.07; I2 = 94%). **They concluded** that Aspirin is a safe chemoprophylactic option for DVT prophylaxis following primary and revision hip and arthroplasty procedures.

The CRISTAL Study group conducted a crossover, randomized, multi-center trial including 9,711 patients and evaluating whether Aspirin is noninferior to LMWH in preventing symptomatic VTE following total hip and knee arthroplasties. They used a noninferiority margin of 1% in this study and reported that the symptomatic VTE rate with Aspirin vs LMWH was 3.45% vs 1.8% (estimated difference, 1.97%; 95% CI, 0.54%-3.41%). This resulted in Aspirin failing to meet the noninferiority margin of 1% and thus LMWH demonstrated significant superiority (p=0.007). All-cause mortality rates, joint-related admission rates, and major bleeding events were not significantly different between the two groups. They concluded that Aspirin resulted in a significantly higher rate of symptomatic DVTs compared to LMWH within 90 days.

Incorrect Answer Choices:
Answer 1: LMWH has not resulted in a significant difference in major bleeding events when compared to Aspirin.
Answer 3: The CRISTAL group study reported that Aspirin is not noninferior to LMWH regarding the rate of symptomatic DVT within 90 days postoperatively.
Answer 4: Aspirin resulted in a higher rate of symptomatic DVT within 90 days after surgery compared to LMWH in the CRISTAL group study.
Answer 5: No significant difference between the Aspirin or LMWH groups was found regarding joint readmission within 90 days.
