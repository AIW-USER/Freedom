## Question

A 17-year-old male high school baseball pitcher presents to your office with medial elbow pain which has been present for the last 2 months. His father states he has noticed a loss in velocity over this time period. On examination, he has pain over the posteromedial aspect of the elbow and pain with the test seen in Figure A. Which phase of the throwing motion places the most stress on the structure which is most likely to be injured?

*Figure A*

## Options

1. Wind-up
2. Early cocking
3. Late cocking
4. Deceleration
5. Follow through

## Correct option

**3. Late cocking**

## Answer explanation

The ulnar collateral ligament (UCL) is under the greatest degree of stress in the late cocking/early acceleration phase of throwing.

The UCL originates just anterior and distal to the medial epicondyle on the humerus and inserts at the sublime tubercle of the ulna. Injury to the UCL is common in overhead throwers who experience a significant degree of valgus loading at the elbow. In baseball players specifically, this load occurs during the late cocking and early acceleration phases of the throwing motion. The microtrauma form recurrent valgus stress may eventually lead to rupture of the ligament and posteromedial elbow pain. Other physical examination finding include a positive valgus stress at 20-30º, a positive milking maneuver, and a positive moving valgus stress test.

*Cain et al. discussed treatment of UCL injuries with reconstruction in 743 athletes.* Average time to throwing was 4.4 months, with the average return to competition being 11.6 months. The complication rate was 20%, and **they concluded** that UCL reconstruction was effective in correcting valgus elbow instability and allowing return to sport.

*Dodson et al. reviewed the results UCL reconstruction in throwing athletes using a docking technique.* **They noted** that 90% of the patients were able to return to sport at an equal or higher level. *There were a total of only 3 complications, leading them to a similar conclusion as Cain et al.,* **noting** that UCL reconstruction was quite effective.
