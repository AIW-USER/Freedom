## Question

A 47-year-old rock climber presents after he sustained a fall directly on his buttock while scaling a boulder. He complains of pain when attempting to walk. He also reports bladder and bowel dysfunction ever since the injury. He was originally told at an outside hospital that he had a sacral fracture known to be associated with a high rate of symptoms that he is currently experiencing. Which of the following fracture patterns does the patient most likely have?

## Options

1. Sacral fracture located through the sacral foramen (Denis Zone II fracture)
2. Sacral fracture located medial to the sacral foramen (Denis Zone III fracture)
3. Sacral fracture located lateral to the sacral foramen (Denis Zone I fracture)
4. Sacral fracture located medial to the sacral foramen (Denies Zone I fracture)
5. Sacral fracture located lateral to the sacral foramen (Denis Zone III fracture)

## Correct option

**2. Sacral fracture located medial to the sacral foramen (Denis Zone III fracture)**

## Answer explanation

Sacral fractures are often classified using the Denis classification. Denis Zone III sacral fractures are located medial to the sacral foramina into the spinal canal and are at a higher risk of neurological injury compared to Denis Zone I or II fractures (Answer 2).

Sacral fractures are orthopedic injuries of the pelvic ring and can be associated with neurologic dysfunction. As such, history taking and physical exam should include a thorough examination to identify symptoms or findings indicative of a neurological insult. Imaging work-up typically consists of radiographs of the pelvis (AP, inlet, and outlet views) and computed tomography of the pelvis. The Denis classification is frequently utilized to describe and characterize sacral fractures into three specific subtypes (Illustration A). Denis Zone I fractures are located lateral to the sacral foramina, Denis Zone II fractures are located through the sacral foramina, and Denis Zone III fractures are located medial to the foramina and into the spinal canal. Rates of neurologic injury correspond to the locations of injury, with Zone III fractures having the highest rate of associated neurological insult, followed by Zone II fractures, and then by Zone I fractures.

*Denis et al. retrospectively reviewed 236 sacral fractures and sought to classify and better understand injury characteristics associated with neurological symptoms.* *They distinctly characterized three groups of sacral fractures based on their location (Zone 1: in the region of the sacral ala, Zone 2: in the region of the sacral foramina, and Zone 3: in the region of the central sacral canal) and found increasing rates of neurological insult with more medial sacral fractures, especially those involving the central sacral canal.* **They concluded** that patients with severe neurological compromise may most benefit from surgical decompression compared to conservative management and that this should be done with minimal delay.

*Gibbons et al. retrospectively reviewed 44 patients with sacral fractures and sought to describe the morphological and neurological characteristics of these fractures.* **They found** that sacral fractures involving the central canal were more likely to cause neurological injuries (Incidence of neurological injury: 57-60%) and were more likely to be bilateral and severe compared to fractures involving the sacral ala (Zone I; Incidence of neurological injury: 24%) or the foramina (Zone II; Incidence of neurological injury: 29%). **They concluded** that the incidence and neurological deficit severity of sacral fractures can be predicted with a classification system based on fracture morphology.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The incidence of neurological injury is highest in Denis Zone III Sacral Fractures, followed by Denis Zone II fractures, and then Denis Zone I fractures.
Answer 3: Denis Zone I fractures have the lowest incidence of associated neurological deficits.
Answer 4: Sacral fractures located medial to the sacral foramen are classified as Denis Zone III fractures, not Denis Zone I.
Answer 5: Sacral fractures located lateral to the sacral foramen have the lowest incidence of associated neurological deficits and are classified as Denis Zone I fractures, not Denis Zone III fractures.
