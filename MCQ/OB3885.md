## Question

A 53-year-old man has bilateral hallux valgus that has failed non-operative treatment. He undergoes bilateral staged hallux valgus corrections. Following a proximal osteotomy, his right foot has a squared lateral metatarsal head and the tibial sesamoid is noted to migrate under the metatarsal head. On the left foot, he has a rounded metatarsal head and the tibial sesamoid overlaps the lateral border of the metatarsal head. At 1 year, recurrent painful hallux valgus is noted on the right side. This likely occurred secondary to:

## Options

1. The post-operative metatarsal head rotation
2. Post-osteotomy alignment of the tibial sesamoid
3. Increased preoperative 1-2 intermetatarsal angle on the right compared to the left
4. Increased medial eminence resection on the right
5. Increased preoperative hallux valgus angle on the left compared to the right

## Correct option

**3. Increased preoperative 1-2 intermetatarsal angle on the right compared to the left**

## Answer explanation

Increased preoperative 1-2 intermetatarsal angle is a risk factor for recurrence following surgical correction of hallux valgus (HV).

Hallux valgus deformities that require surgical correction are indicated in symptomatic patients with an IMA > 10° and HVA of > 20°. There are various surgical techniques used for correction of HV ranging from soft tissue releases to osteotomies to fusions. There is no one preferred technique. The optimal management relies upon patient-specific factors and surgeon preference. The reported rate of recurrence is 4-11% with proximal osteotomies, and while there is no universally accepted algorithm of treatment, the surgical technique chosen must address risk factors for complications and recurrence. Risk factors for recurrence include a rounded first metatarsal head (versus squared), residual lateral displacement of the sesamoids following correction, and increased preoperative HVA/IMA.

*Okuda et al.* (2009) analyzed the risk of recurrence based upon residual lateral sesamoid displacement following hallux valgus correction. Postoperative incomplete reduction of the sesamoids can be a risk factor for the recurrence of hallux valgus. The feet without a reduced sesamoid were found to have 10x the odds of recurrence. The identification of incomplete reduction of the sesamoids intraoperatively may allow modification of surgical procedures and improvement of the surgical results.

*Okuda et al.* (2007) also analyzed the influence of the metatarsal head shape on hallux valgus recurrence. **They found** that there is a significant relationship between a round-shaped lateral edge of the first metatarsal head and hallux valgus, and a positive round sign after a proximal first metatarsal osteotomy can be a risk factor for the recurrence of hallux valgus. A metatarsal that is pronated with appear rounded on an AP view of the foot, while the metatarsal is properly rotated will appear to have a squared lateral border. The actual metatarsal morphologies are not different.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: A squared lateral border, as viewed on the AP foot radiograph, of the metatarsal head represents normal metatarsal rotation. When you see a lateral border that is rounded, this represents a pronated toe, which is a risk factor for HV recurrence.
Answer 2: Reduction of the tibial sesamoid under the metatarsal head demonstrates an appropriate reduction, which would be protective from recurrence. The sesamoids not being fully reduced, as occurred in the LEFT foot, would be one of the greatest risk factors for HV recurrence.
Answer 4: Increased medial eminence resection is a risk factor for iatrogenic hallux varus.
Answer 5: Increased preoperative HVA is a risk factor for recurrence. This scenario would result in the left side being at a higher risk for recurrence compared to the right.
