## Question

An 18-year-old male with cerebral palsy presents to your clinic. He has functioning wrist flexors with a dynamic wrist flexion deformity, a flexible wrist joint, mildly weak finger extensors, and nearly absent wrist extensors. What treatment offers him the best chance at the greatest degree of improvement of motion, functional, and satisfaction?

## Options

1. Botulinum toxin injection
2. Pronator Teres rerouting
3. FCU to ECRB tendon transfer
4. Flexor-pronator slide
5. Pronator Teres to ECRB transfer

## Correct option

**3. FCU to ECRB tendon transfer**

## Answer explanation

An FCU to ECRB tendon transfer is indicated in a supple wrist with weak wrist extensors with resting ulnar deviation. The transfer will also help improve forearm supination as a secondary effect.

The most common position of the upper extremity affected by CP is elbow flexion, forearm pronation, wrist flexion, and thumb in the palm. The intervention aims to improve position, function, ease of hygiene, and patient self-esteem. Wrist flexion deformity decreases the ability to grasp and release. The most common surgical treatment is the Green's transfer - FCU-ECRB transfer which improves wrist extension and forearm supination. This transfer is contraindicated in those patients without a functioning FCR or if the wrist joint is rigid.

*Van Heest et al. randomized 39 children with CP with upper extremity involvement.* Children either underwent tendon transfer, botulinum toxin injection, or therapy. The surgery cohort had the greatest improvements in the wrist range of motion, quality of life inventory, and satisfaction as compared to the botulinum toxin and therapy cohorts. **The authors concluded** that for children with upper extremity CP, tendon transfers are superior to non-operative management.

*Patterson et al. reviewed 25 FCU to ECRB for children with CP.* *Late deformities developed in 12/25 children between 10-105 months postoperatively including extension deformity (8), supination deformity (1), recurrent flexion deformity (3).* Revision surgery was required on 9/12. **The authors advocate** not performing tendon transfers in CP before the age of 13.

*Cheema et al. reviewed 3 tendon transfers for supination of the forearm in a biomechanical analysis: FCU-ECRB, PT rerouting, and BR rerouting.* All transfers improved supination, however with the PT transfer - the forearm never reached the neutral position. The FCU-ECRB transfer was the most effective for producing supination in cadavers.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: Botulinum toxin injection achieves inferior results compared to tendon transfers.
Answer 2: Rerouting the PT converts the muscle into a supinator. It may be indicated in patients with CP - but the best answer in the case is the Green's transfer.
Answer 4: This is indicated to release ischemic muscles after compartment syndrome typically. In addition, it can be used to release a severe, fixed wrist and finger flexion deformity in CP.
Answer 5: PT can be used to power wrist extension when there is a contraindication to using a wrist flexor. In this case, this patient has functioning wrist flexors - so FCU can be transferred.
