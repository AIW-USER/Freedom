## Options

1. Elevated serum phosphate and serum creatinine
2. Mutation in ACVR1 gene
3. HLA B27 antigen
4. Mutation in EXT gene
5. Diabetes

## Correct option

**2. Mutation in ACVR1 gene**

## Answer explanation

The clinical presentation is consistent with Fibrodysplasia ossificans progressiva (FOP, Stone Man Disease), which involves a mutation of the ACVR1 gene.

FOP is a rare disease involving the ACVR1 gene (activin A type I receptor gene, a BMP type-1 receptor). There are 2 hallmark characteristics: progressive heterotopic ossification (muscles, fascia, tendons, ligaments and joint capsules) and congenital malformation of the great toe (hallux valgus, malformed first metatarsal and monophalangism). BMP4, which contributes to the formation of the skeleton in the normal embryo, is implicated in this disease.

*Carvalho et al. discuss screening of ACVR1 gene in Brazilian FOP patients.* **They found** that all 16 patients with the classic FOP phenotype had the classic mutation in ACVR1 gene.

Figure A shows a "frozen" spine with complete loss of motion. Figure B shows malformed great toes. Figure C shows exostosis and heterotopic ossification around the knee. Figure D shows heterotopic ossification around the left hip. Figure E shows ossification of soft tissues and paraspinal ligaments and fusion of the vertebral bodies give rise to a “bamboo spine” appearance (usually used to describe ankylosing spondylitis if used in isolation). Illustration A shows the clinical appearance of patients with FOP. Illustration B shows the appearance of the skeleton in FOP, showing widespread extraskeletal ossification.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Calcinosis of renal failure is associated with hyperphosphatemia and long-term dialysis.
Answer 3: HLA-B27 antigen is strongly associated with ankylosing spondylitis (90% positive).
Answer 4: Mutations in EXT1, EXT2 or EXT3 present with multiple hereditary exostosis (MHE).
Answer 5: Diabetes is associated with diffuse idiopathic skeletal hyperostosis and ossification of the posterior longitudinal ligament. It has no association with FOP.
