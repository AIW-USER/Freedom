## Question

Two patients, both aged 58-years-old males, present for evaluation for end-stage elbow arthritis. Patient A has osteoarthritis, while Patient B has rheumatoid arthritis. Which of the following is true if both of these patients undergo a linked total elbow arthroplasty?

## Options

1. Patient A has a higher likelihood of aseptic loosening
2. Patient B has a higher likelihood of aseptic loosening
3. Patient A and Patient B have an equal likelihood of aseptic loosening
4. Patient A has a higher likelihood of instability
5. Patient B has a higher likelihood of instability

## Correct option

**1. Patient A has a higher likelihood of aseptic loosening**

## Answer explanation

Patients with primary elbow osteoarthritis tend to be more active and place greater demands on a total elbow arthroplasty (TEA). These patients have a higher rate of stem fracture and aseptic loosening compared to those with rheumatoid arthritis.

TEA is an increasingly used motion-preserving modality for the treatment of many debilitating elbow pathologies. Primary indications include rheumatoid arthritis, post-traumatic arthritis, and intra-articular distal humeral fractures in the elderly with poor bony quality. Semiconstrained implants have the best longevity and most optimal functional outcomes. Aseptic loosening (radiographic 17%, clinical 6%) is the most common mode of failure for more constrained implant designs.

*Gill et al. reviewed 78 elbows who had rheumatoid arthritis that were managed with a Coonrad-Morrey TEA.* **They reported** that at the latest clinical follow-up evaluation, according to the Mayo elbow performance score, excellent,good, fair, and poor results were reporting in 43, 26, 7, and 2 patients, respectively. **They concluded** that the rate of survival of the prosthesis was 92%, with 86% good or excellent and 14% fair or poor results.

*Gramstad et al. reviewed primary elbow osteoarthritis>* **They reported** that elbow osteoarthritis typically affects middle-aged men who engage in strenuous manual activity. **They concluded** that arthroplasty is rarely indicated for primary osteoarthritis of the elbow and should be reserved for elderly individuals with low demands for whom other treatment options have failed.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 2&3: Patients with elbow OA tend to be more active and more likely to be involved in manual activities that result in increases stress to the TEA and a higher likelihood of aseptic loosening.
Answers 4&5: Because the chosen implant was linked, the rate of instability is equivalent in both patients. Patients with RA should not undergo unlinked TEA because of risk of tendon injury and subsequent instability.
