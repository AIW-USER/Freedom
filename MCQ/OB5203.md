## Question

Which of the following best describes the vascular supply to the femoral epiphysis in children under the age of 18 months old?

## Options

1. Artery of ligamentum teres
2. Medial femoral circumflex artery
3. Medial and lateral femoral circumflex artery
4. Medial femoral circumflex artery and artery of ligamentum teres
5. Medial and lateral femoral circumflex artery, and artery of ligamentum teres

## Correct option

**5. Medial and lateral femoral circumflex artery, and artery of ligamentum teres**

## Answer explanation

In children under the age of four years, the femoral epiphysis is supplied by the medial and lateral femoral circumflex arteries, as well as the artery of the ligamentum teres (branch of the obturator artery).

The medial femoral circumflex artery (MFCA) and lateral femoral circumflex artery (LFCA) constitute the main blood supply to the femoral head in early bone development. In adolescents and after maturity, the femoral head receives its blood supply mainly from the MFCA, with the posterosuperior and posteroinferior retinacular branches from this artery being the most important.

*Boardman et al. reviewed pediatric hip fractures.* **They state** that after the age of four years old, the blood supply through the artery of the ligamentum teres and LFCA diminishes. This leaves the MFCA as the principal blood supply to the femoral head via its posterosuperior and posteroinferior retinacular branches.

John Treuta (1957) studied the normal vascular anatomy of the human femoral head. He commented that all three vessels are functional in the femoral head up until the age of 18-36 months, when the ligamentum teres artery is the first to become non-functional. By age 6, the medial femoral circumflex artery is the dominant blood supply.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1-4: Before age four years, the vascular supply to the femoral head is supplied by the medial and lateral femoral circumflex arteries as well as the artery of the ligamentum teres.
