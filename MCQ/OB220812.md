## Question

A 40-year-old man who works at a concrete plant sustains a closed calcaneus fracture after falling from a ledge at work. A CT scan cut through the posterior facet demonstrates two fragments with 8 mm of displacement at the articular surface. He is treated with an extensile lateral approach and fixation with independent lag screws and a neutralization plate. Which of the following factors is a positive prognostic indicator for long-term outcomes for this patient compared to other patients with displaced intra-articular calcaneus fractures?

## Options

1. Sex
2. Age
3. Employment
4. Surgical approach
5. Sanders classification

## Correct option

**5. Sanders classification**

## Answer explanation

This patient sustained a displaced intraarticular calcaneus fracture (DIACF) with a Sanders classification of II. Patients with minimal comminution at the posterior facet are more likely to have a good long-term functional outcome (Answer 5).

DIACFs are devastating injuries that contribute to significant surgical challenges and patient morbidity. A multitude of studies have been performed investigating prognostic factors for poor outcomes after open reduction and internal fixation of these fractures. Patient factors, including age over 30 years, male sex, labor-intensive employment, and associated litigation or workers' compensation, have all been associated with poor outcomes in the treatment of these injuries. An anatomical reduction of the articular surface is critical in the treatment of these fractures, with the extensile lateral and sinus tarsi approaches being the most common surgical approaches employed for ORIF. The Sanders classification, which is based on preoperative CT coronal cuts through the posterior facet, is prognostic for treatment failure in DIACF, with higher classifications having an exponentially increased risk for post-traumatic arthritis and subsequent subtalar arthrodesis at long-term follow-up.

*Sanders et al. perform a long-term analysis of a prospectively collected sample of 108 DIACFs with over 10-year follow-up.* *95% of fractures had an anatomic reduction of the articular surface, as determined by postoperative CT scan.* *Patients with Sanders III fractures were 4 times more likely to have a poor outcome requiring hindfoot arthrodesis than patients with Sanders II fractures.* **The authors highlight** the difference in prognosis based on injury pattern alone, despite anatomic reduction and stable fixation in nearly all cases, which is critical in counseling patients on their long-term outcomes.

*Sharr et al. provide a review of the current management options for DIACFs.* *The authors focus on four main treatment categories: non-operative management, open reduction and internal fixation (ORIF), minimally invasive reduction and fixation, and primary ORIF with subtalar arthrodesis.* *They discuss prognostic factors influencing the treatment of DIACFs, with poor outcomes seen in patients over 30 years of age, male laborers, and patients with higher Sanders classification and intra-articular comminution.*

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1, 2: Female sex and patients under 30 years of age are associated with improved outcomes
Answer 3: Patients with sedentary jobs have been shown to have improved outcomes
Answer 4: The surgical approach has not been shown to influence long-term outcomes
