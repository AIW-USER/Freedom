## Question

Which of the following hallux valgus pre-operative measurements would call for a surgical plan involving both a proximal metatarsal osteotomy and a distal metatarsal medial closing wedge osteotomy?

## Options

1. HVA 30, IMA 10, HVI 8, congruent MTP
2. HVA 30, IMA 16, HVI 14, incongruent MTP
3. HVA 45, IMA 12, HVI 8, DMAA 8
4. HVA 30, IMA 16, HVI 8, DMAA 16
5. HVA 45, IMA 16, HVI 14, incongruent MTP

## Correct option

**4. HVA 30, IMA 16, HVI 8, DMAA 16**

## Answer explanation

A patient with an IMA (intermetatarsal angle) > 13° and a congruent MTP with a DMAA (distal metatarsal articular angle) > 10° would call for a surgical plan involving a proximal 1st metatarsal osteotomy to correct the IMA and a distally-based metatarsal medial closing wedge osteotomy to correct the DMAA.

The correction of moderate to severe hallux valgus can present as a treatment challenge, and there are controversies as to whether single, double, or triple osteotomies should be utilized. Hallux valgus deformities are congruent when the articular surface of the 1st metatarsal head matches the articular surface of the base of the proximal phalanx (necessitating an increased DMAA). If the DMAA is not corrected and the proximal phalanx is reduced to a neutral position, then the joint will become incongruent which increases the risk for stiffness, pain, and recurrence of deformity.

*Smith et al. present a series of patients with increased DMAA using double and triple osteotomies.* **They note** that correcting the orientation of the articular surface is most commonly done with a medially-based closing wedge osteotomy of the distal aspect of the first metatarsal. This reorients the articular surface into a neutral position, thus correcting the DMAA.

*Siekmann et al. present thirty-two patients who underwent operative correction of hallux valgus with a double osteotomy of the 1st metatarsal using an opening wedge proximally and a closing wedge distally.* **They note** good overall results with a low complication rate and conclude that this approach has clear advantages over isolated opening wedge procedures including potentially better correction, especially in those bunions associated with an increased distal metatarsal articular angle.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: This is mild HV and does not require a proximal osteotomy.
Answer 2: A proximal osteotomy is indicated in this patient but an incongruent articular surface suggests a low DMAA, therefore a distal osteotomy to correct this would not be required. An Akin ostoeotomy would be considered in this patient with an increased HVI. An increased hallux valgus interphallangeus (HVI) > 10° is often treated with an Akin osteotomy, which is a closing wedge osteotomy of the proximal phalanx of the great toe.
Answer 3: A DMAA < 10° would not require a distal correction. Moderate-severe hallux valgus with an IMA > 13° and/or HVA > 40° is best treated with a proximal osteotomy. If these measurements are not met, then a distally based chevron osteotomy is often indicated.
Answer 5: A proximal osteotomy is indicated in this patient, but an incongruent articular surface suggests a low DMA and therefore a distal osteotomy to correct this would not be required. An Akin ostoeotomy would be considered in this patient with an increased HVI.
