## Answer explanation

Of the plain radiographs above, the fracture pattern that would be most appropriately treated with a dynamic external fixator is a complete intraarticular middle phalanx base fracture, otherwise referred to as a pilon fracture (Figure D).

The fracture pattern at the base of the middle phalanx depends on the direction of the force and position of the joint. A combination of longitudinal load and either hyperextension or hyperflexion causes dorsal or volar fracture-dislocations, respectively. A predominantly axial load disrupts both the dorsal and volar cortices of the middle phalanx base, creating, by definition, a pilon fracture. Due to the configuration of pilon fractures, these patterns are unstable and prone to collapse if not supported. While a variety of surgical techniques have been described for PIP fracture-dislocations, fracture pattern characteristics and surgeon experience often dictate the treatment chosen. Generally speaking, fracture-dislocations that have a congruent joint after closed reduction and are stable through 30-90 degrees of flexion may be successfully treated with extension block splinting (presuming it was a dorsal fracture-dislocation). If a congruent joint reduction cannot be obtained by closed measures, an ORIF may be chosen if the fragment(s) is large enough for adequate fixation purchase. In the setting of significant joint surface comminution and/or the joint is dynamically unstable, a dynamic external fixator is likely necessary. Of note, static external fixators have fallen out of fashion in favor of dynamic ones that allow early active motion to achieve better clinical outcomes.

*Colegate-Stone et al.* *2015 evaluated the use and outcomes of a dynamic external fixator (specifically the Ligamentotaxor external fixator) in 12 patients with displaced intra-articular fractures of the base of the middle phalanx.* **They reported** an average range of motion of the PIPJ was 63° along with an average end-of-care-cycle quick-DASH score of 9.1. **They concluded** the outcomes seen support the use of the Ligamentotaxor in the management of middle phalanx intra-articular fractures.

*Sastravaha et al.* *2020 performed a study evaluating the results of an alternative dynamic external fixator technique for unstable PIP joint injuries.* The alternative fixator used a plastic syringe to maintain distraction force instead of the commonly used rubber bands. **They reported** that at 6 months after the operation, the mean active ROM of the injured PIP joint was 89° (range, 70-104°), and the mean Quick-DASH score was 9.78 (range, 0-40.9). **They concluded** that their results were comparable to other dynamic external fixation techniques, however, the advantages of this technique were the strength of the system, procedure simplicity, the compact design, and relatively low cost.

Figures A & E represent a PIP dorsal fracture-dislocation. Figure B is a malunion of a prior PIP dorsal fracture-dislocation. Figure C is a PIP volar fracture-dislocation. Figure D represents a PIP pilon fracture of the middle phalanx base.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: This is a dorsal fracture-dislocation. Given the dorsal subluxation at rest, it is likely that this will remain unstable after closed reduction and fixation would likely be necessary. This may include extension block pinning (which involves closed reduction and drilling of a k-wire into the distal, dorsal aspect of the proximal phalanx to block the terminal extension and prevent dorsal dislocation), percutaneous pinning of the volar fragment, open reduction and pinning of the fragments, or a combination thereof. There is likely not enough osseous real estate to use a buttress plate.
Answer 2: This patient has a malunited PIP dorsal fracture-dislocation. Given the arthritic changes and lack of congruity at the volar lip, this patient would be a candidate for a hemi-hamate replacement arthroplasty.
Answer 3: This patient has a volar fracture-dislocation with an associated dorsal lip fracture. Operative strategies follow the same principles as dorsal fracture-dislocations with focus on early ROM. These include splinting in extension, closed reduction and percutaneous pinning, K-wire transfixion of the PIPJ, miniplate or hook plate fixation, and/or screw fixation.
Answer 5: Similar to the patient in Figure A, this patient sustained a PIP dorsal fracture-dislocation. However, this patient has a large articular piece that would be amenable to ORIF with either a buttress plate or screws.
