## Question

A 17-year-old track athlete is presenting to your office for right knee pain, and radiographs of the knee reveal a bipartite patella. He is initially treated with nonoperative management including NSAIDs and physical therapy with isometric stretching exercises of the quadriceps muscle in extension. Which location of the patella is the most commonly involved for this patient's condition?

## Options

1. Inferomedial
2. Inferolateral
3. Direct lateral
4. Superomedial
5. Superolateral

## Correct option

**5. Superolateral**

## Answer explanation

A bipartite patella is most often found in the superolateral region (Type III).

Bipartite Patella is a congenital knee condition caused by the failure of the patella to fuse and is often an incidental finding on radiographs. Diagnosis is confirmed radiographically with most commonly an unfused patella at the superolateral pole. Treatment is observation and most often does not require treatment as the condition is typically asymptomatic.

*Atesok et al. reviewed bipartite patella.* **They reported** that most patients improve with nonsurgical treatment. **They concluded** that surgery is considered when nonsurgical treatment fails and may include excision of the fragment or lateral retinacular release and detachment of the vastus lateralis muscle insertion

*Oohashi et al. reviewed the clinical features of the developmental anomaly of ossification type bipartite or tripartite patella.* **They reported** that the median age at onset of pain in symptomatic patients was 16 years (range, 10-51 years). **They concluded** that bipartite or tripartite patellae were classified by evaluating the location and number of fragments; 83% were classified as superolateral bipartite type, 12% were lateral bipartite type, 4% were superolateral and lateral tripartite type, and 1% were superolateral tripartite type.

*Kallini et al. retrospectively reviewed the clinical features, surgical techniques, and outcomes associated with operative treatment of symptomatic bipartite patella in pediatric and adolescent athletes.* **They reported** that patients undergoing surgical treatment were most commonly female competitive athletes with prolonged symptoms. **They concluded** that a variety of surgical techniques may be effective, with a 15% risk of persistent or recurrent symptoms warranting re-operation.

*Radha et al. reviewed symptomatic bipartite patella in 6 cases.* **They reported** that these 6 cases of painful synchondrosis in bipartite patellae were all in keen athletes following a direct blow to the anterior aspect of the knee. **They noted** that a complete rupture of the synchondrosis with evidence of retropatellar chondral separation was seen on MRI scan in all cases. **They concluded** that in cases refractory to non-operative management, successful symptomatic treatment can be achieved by operative fixation.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1-4: The most common location of a bipartite patella involves the superolateral patella.
