## Question

A 23-year-old patient underwent a routine elbow arthroscopy to remove a loose body. The patient is unable to extend his thumb IP joint immediately post-op. This complication is most commonly associated with which portal placement?

## Options

1. Direct lateral
2. Anteromedial
3. Proximal anteromedial
4. Posterolateral
5. Anterolateral

## Correct option

**5. Anterolateral**

## Answer explanation

The patient has sustained a PIN injury (lack of thumb IP extension). The most common elbow arthroscopy portal associated with a PIN injury is the anterolateral portal.

Indications for elbow arthroscopy include loose body/osteophyte removal, synovectomy, management of osteochondral lesions, capsular release, and management of elbow septic arthritis. The most frequent complication of elbow arthroscopy is damage to nearby neurovascular structures. The anterolateral portal as originally described puts the radial nerve (PIN) at risk because of its close proximity to the portal. The more proximal the portal, the lower the probability of radial nerve injury. For this reason, the proximal anterolateral portal (1 cm anterior and 1-2 cm proximal to lateral epicondyle), is more commonly used than the distal anterolateral portal (1 cm anterior and 1-2 cm distal to lateral epicondyle).

*Dodson performed a review arthroscopy of the elbow.* **They noted** that elbow arthroscopy was originally considered to be an unsafe procedure because of the small size of the elbow joint capsule and its proximity to several crucial neurovascular structures. They cite that the procedure has become safer due to a better understanding of elbow anatomy. **They note** that the most common indications for elbow arthroscopy include removal of loose bodies, synovectomy, débridement and/or excision of osteophytes, capsular release, and the assessment and treatment of osteochondritis dissecans.

*Abboud et al. performed a technique review of elbow arthroscopy.* *They cite that many surgerons create a lateral portal initially and then establish a medial portal with a spinal needle by direct visualization from within the joint.* **They state** that the anterolateral portal was originally described as being located 3 cm distal and 2 cm anterior to the lateral epicondyle. However, this portal location places the radial nerve at significant risk for iatrogenic injury. The authors cite that to decrease the risk of injury to the radial nerve, avoiding the distal placement of this portal in favor of a more proximal placement of the anterolateral portal, at the sulcus between the capitellum and the radial head is recommended.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The direct lateral portal places the lateral antebrachial cutaneous nerve at risk
Answer 2: The anteromedial portal places the medial antebrachial cutaneous and median at risk
Answer 3: The proximal anteromedial portal places the medial antebrachial nerve, ulnar nerve, and median nerve at risk
Answer 4: The posterolateral portal places the posterior antebrachial cutaneous at risk.
