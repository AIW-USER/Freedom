## Question

Which of the following structures is the most important static stabilizer to valgus forces in a throwing athlete?

## Options

1. Anterior band of the anterior bundle of the ulnar collateral ligament
2. Posterior band of the anterior bundle of the ulnar collateral ligament
3. Posterior bundle of the ulnar collateral ligament
4. Transverse bundle of the ulnar collateral ligament
5. Flexor-pronator mass

## Correct option

**1. Anterior band of the anterior bundle of the ulnar collateral ligament**

## Answer explanation

The anterior band of the anterior bundle of the ulnar collateral ligament (UCL) of the elbow provides the primary restraint to valgus stress.

Valgus stability of the elbow is primary provided by the UCL<, with the radiocapitellar joint acting as a secondary stabilizer, and the flexor-pronator mass is a dynamic stabilizer. The UCL is divided into three different components, which include the anterior bundle of the UCL, the posterior bundle of the UCL, and the transverse ligament. The anterior oblique ligament is divided into the anterior band, which is the most important valgus stabilizer and is taut from 0-85° of flexion, and the posterior band, which is taut from 55° of flexion. The posterior oblique ligament is more important for valgus stability with elbow flexion greater than 90°. The transverse ligament does not provide any significant stability to the elbow joint.

*Safran reviewed the diagnosis and treatment of UCL injuries.* *The author described the ulnar collateral ligament to be divided into three groups, of which the anterior band of the UCL being the most important valgus stabilizer, which is most taut for 0 to 85° of flexion.* **The author concluded** that the ulnar collateral ligament can be commonly injured, but may not present with gross laxity or instability, which should prompt a thorough evaluation.

*Safran et al. reviewed the diagnosis and treatment of UCL injuries of the elbow.* **The authors stated** athletes that who participate in overhead motion that imparts valgus stress to the elbow are at risk for injury due to repetitive microtears and can lead to ulnar nerve symptoms, flexor-pronator strain, radiocapitellar joint compression, and loose bodies. **They concluded** that a high index of suspicion should be present when a throwing athlete presents with symptoms potentially attributable to UCL insufficiency.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 2: The posterior bundle of the UCL does provide valgus stability of the elbow, but does not become taut until 55° of flexion.
Answer 3: The posterior oblique ligament provides valgus stability of the elbow beyond 90° of flexion.
Answer 4: The transverse ligament originates and inserts on the ulna, and as such does not provide any significant stability.
Answer 5: The flexor-pronator mass is an important dynamic stabilizer of the elbow, but does not contribute to static stability of valgus stress in a throwing athlete.
