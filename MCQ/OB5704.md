## Question

A 19-year-old female gymnast has had a 1-year history of low back pain that radiates down to her right leg. On imaging, it can be noted that the inferior endplate of the L5 vertebral body is 6 mm anterior to the superior endplate of the S1 vertebral body. The combinations of which lines are used to create a measurement angle that best correlates with this disease severity?

![[q5704-a-bk8yu3jccae0jddp2.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Lines A and B
2. Lines A and C
3. Lines B and D
4. Lines C and D
5. Lines D and E

## Correct option

**4. Lines C and D**

## Answer explanation

The patient's vignette is consistent with L5-S1 isthmic spondylolisthesis. Lines C and D are used to measure pelvic incidence which has a strong correlation with isthmic spondylolisthesis slip severity. Pelvic incidence is defined as the angle between the line perpendicular to the sacral plate at its midpoint and the line connecting this point to the femoral heads axis. “Pelvic incidence=pelvic tilt+sacral slope” because they are formed by lines perpendicular to each other.

Pelvic incidence (PI) most strongly predicts the severity of spondylolisthesis relative to the other pelvic parameters including sacral slope (SS), pelvic tilt (PT), slip angle, slip grade, Myerding slip percentage, and Wiltse-Newman slip type. PI has been shown to be relatively constant in adulthood (~57 degrees) and unlike many other parameters, is not affected by changes in posture. It is important to note that low PI angles indicate low shear forces at the lumbosacral junction.

*Labelle et al. reviewed the clinical significance of PI.* It is created by measuring the angle between a line drawn from the center of the S1 endplate to the center of the femoral head, and a second line drawn perpendicular to a line drawn along the S1 endplate, intersecting the point in the center of the S1 endplate. **The authors noted** that subjects with L5-S1 spondylolisthesis are a heterogeneous group with various adaptations of their posture. **They suggest** that reduction techniques, if to be used, should preferably be used in subjects with evidence of abnormal posture, in order to restore global spinopelvic balance and improve the biomechanical environment for fusion.

*Mehta et al. reviewed the relation of the pelvis to the spine as a contributor to sagittal balance.* In the setting of elevated PI angles, the spine will adapt with increased lumbar lordosis. These abnormal spinopelvic parameters have also shown to contribute to multiple spinal conditions including isthmic spondylolysis, degenerative spondylolisthesis, deformity, and impact outcome after spinal fusion. **The authors conclude** that it is essential to accurately assess and measure these sagittal values to understand their potential role in the disease process, and to promote spinopelvic balance during surgery.

*Hanson et al. performed a retrospective study on 40 patients with spondylolisthesis to determine the correlation between PI and disease severity.* **The authors noted** that the PI was significantly higher in patients with low- and high-grade isthmic spondylolisthesis as compared to control without spondylolisthesis. **The authors also noted** that the PI was significantly higher in the high-grade isthmic spondylolisthesis group than in the low-grade isthmic spondylolisthesis group.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The angle between lines A and B is the sacral slope.
Answer 2: The angle between lines A and C represents a 90-degree angle to the S1 superior endplate.
Answer 3: The angle between lines B and D is not a commonly described spinopelvic orientation measurement.
Answer 5: The angle between lines D and E is the pelvic tilt.
