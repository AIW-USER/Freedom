Chapter: Regional Orthopedics MCQs — Hallux Valgus Bunion

## Question

Which radiographic measurement is used specifically to assess the congruence of the first metatarsophalangeal joint?

## Options

1. Talar-first metatarsal angle
2. Intermetatarsal Angle (IMA)
3. Hallux Valgus Angle (HVA)
4. Distal Metatarsal Articular Angle (DMAA)

## Correct option

**4. Distal Metatarsal Articular Angle (DMAA)**

## Answer explanation

The DMAA measures the orientation of the articular surface relative to the shaft, helping determine if the joint is congruent or subluxated.

Radiographic measures in hallux valgus

Radiographic Findings

Weight-bearing (standing) anteroposterior (AP) and lateral radiographs of the foot are essential — non-weight-bearing films significantly underestimate the deformity. An axial sesamoid view (skyline view) assesses sesamoid position relative to the metatarsal head.

Hallux Valgus Angle (HVA): Measured between the longitudinal axes of the first metatarsal and the proximal phalanx on the weight-bearing AP view. Normal is <15°. Mild
deformity: 15–20°; moderate: 20–40°; severe: >40°. This is the primary measure of hallux valgus severity.

Intermetatarsal Angle (IMA):
Measured between the longitudinal axes of the first and second metatarsals on the AP view. Normal is <9°. Mild: 9–11°; moderate: 11–16°; severe: >16°. Increased IMA reflects metatarsus primus varus and is a critical determinant of surgical procedure selection — elevated IMA generally requires a proximal metatarsal osteotomy or Lapidus procedure rather than a distal osteotomy alone.

Distal Metatarsal Articular Angle (DMAA): Measures the relationship between the articular surface of the first metatarsal head and the long axis of the first metatarsal. Normal is <6°. An increased DMAA indicates that the articular surface is laterally tilted relative to the metatarsal shaft — a lateral-facing joint surface that drives the hallux into valgus. If the DMAA is increased, a distal metatarsal osteotomy must include correction of the articular angle (a medial closing wedge or a laterally translating osteotomy), otherwise the deformity will recur.

Sesamoid Position: Assessed on the AP view using the Hardy and Clapham classification (grades 1–7) or the simpler tibial sesamoid position (TSP) classification. The tibial (medial) sesamoid is classified relative to its position under the first metatarsal head — from fully covered (normal, position 1) to completely lateral to the metatarsal (severe subluxation, position 7). Sesamoid subluxation ≥position 4 is generally considered significant.

First MTP Joint Congruence:
The joint is classified as congruent (the articular surfaces of the metatarsal head and proximal phalanx are parallel — the hallux deviates laterally but the joint surfaces are matched, with no subluxation) or incongruent/subluxated
(the proximal phalanx has subluxated laterally on the metatarsal head — there is a mismatch between the articular surfaces). This distinction is crucial for surgical planning — congruent joints require correction of the articular angle (DMAA correction) rather than lateral soft tissue release, as releasing the lateral structures in a congruent joint would create incongruency and predispose to degenerative arthritis.

First MTP Joint Arthritis:
Assessed for joint space narrowing, osteophyte formation (particularly dorsal osteophytes that may indicate hallux rigidus component), subchondral sclerosis, and cyst formation. Significant arthritis may necessitate arthrodesis rather than osteotomy.

First Metatarsal-Cuneiform Joint: Evaluated for arthrosis and hypermobility. Instability at this joint (radiographic signs include joint line obliquity, divergence between the first and second metatarsals at the base) supports a Lapidus procedure (first tarsometatarsal arthrodesis) as the corrective osteotomy.

Hallux Interphalangeal Angle: The angle between the proximal and distal phalanges of the hallux on the AP view. An angle >10° indicates hallux valgus interphalangeus, which requires a separate Akin osteotomy (medial closing wedge of the proximal phalanx) for complete correction.

Answer: 4
