## Question

An 8-year-old child was struck by a car while crossing the street. He is unresponsive, tachycardic, and has a systolic blood pressure of 48 mmHg. He is intubated and despite adequate fluid resuscitation, he remains hemodynamically unstable. Which pelvic injury would benefit most from the application of a pelvic binder?

## Answer explanation

Rotationally unstable pelvic ring injuries have a higher incidence of intrapelvic hemorrhaging. Figure A depicts an AP compression pelvic ring injury that would benefit from the placement of a pelvic binder in a hemodynamically unstable patient.

Pediatric pelvic ring injuries are uncommon and typically occur after high energy trauma. Unlike adult injuries, in which AP compression injuries are most common, pediatric pelvic ring fractures are more commonly a lateral compression injury. Moreover, they have a higher rate of single-bone pelvic ring fractures. Pediatric patients have an increased bone elasticity, thickened periosteum and the cartilage that is capable of absorbing more energy. Additionally, there is a lower rate of hemorrhage secondary to smaller vasculature, a greater capability of vasoconstriction and less intrapelvic volume. However, in the setting of hemodynamic instability with an unstable AP compression pelvic ring injury, a pelvic binder or sling should still be placed to reduce this intrapelvic space.

*Torode et al. retrospectively reviewed 141 patients with multiple injuries that included pelvic fractures.* **They report** that the greatest morbidity, mortality, and complications from the fractures were in patients with segmental instability of the pelvis. **They recommend** the use of computed tomographic imaging be used when segmental instability is suspected and when the posterior elements of the pelvis are difficult to examine by conventional radiographs. **They conclude** that when there is segmental instability of the pelvis that cannot be controlled by conventional methods of pelvic slings or spica casts, then the treatment of choice should be an external fixation.

*Ganz et al. review unstable posterior pelvic ring disruptions and their accompanied venous bleeding and hypotension.* *They report on the use of an antishock clamp to provide direct reduction and compression of fracture-diastases about the sacroiliac joint.* **They conclude** that this device allows for application in less than ten minutes in the emergency room without interfering with the ability to carry out subsequent laparotomy or other required procedures.

Figure A is a pelvic radiograph demonstrating a rotationally unstable AP compression pelvic ring injury. Figure B demonstrates a left iliac wing fracture. Figure C demonstrates a left ischial tuberosity avulsion fracture. Figure D demonstrates the pubic rami fractures. Figure E is an AP pelvic radiograph demonstrating a right AIIS avulsion fracture.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 2: An iliac wing fracture does not require the placement of a pelvic binder
Answer 3: An ischial tuberosity avulsion fracture does not require the placement of a pelvic binder
Answer 4: Pubic rami fractures are stable pelvic fracture that would not benefit from the placement of a pelvic binder
Answer 5: An ASIS avulsion would not benefit from the placement of a pelvic binder
