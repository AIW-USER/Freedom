## Question

A 67-year-old female presents to the ED with right hip pain after standing up from a chair and feeling a pop in her hip. She states she fell to the ground and was unable to bear weight after. Radiographs are shown in Figure A. She has a past medical history of hypertension, coronary artery disease and breast cancer, which was treated with lumpectomy 3 years prior. The fracture is deemed pathologic by the radiologist. What role do matrix metalloproteases play in this type of fracture?

![[q215609-a-pathologic-subtroch.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. RANK-L activation to aid osteoclastogenesis
2. Induction of angiogenesis
3. Acidification of the extracellular matrix to aid in bony destruction
4. Extracellular matrix degradation to aid cell invasion and metastases
5. Acts as a chemokine to promote targeting of bone

## Correct option

**4. Extracellular matrix degradation to aid cell invasion and metastases**

## Answer explanation

Matrix metalloproteases (MMPs) are responsible for cancer cell invasion, which can lead to pathologic fracture, as seen in Figure A.

Matrix metalloproteases (MMPs) are a subset of proteolytic enzymes that cause local tissue degradation. MMPs are secreted by the tumor cells themselves or by host defenses in reaction to the tumor. They work by degrading the extracellular matrix, allowing tumor cell entry into the vascular system, and spread (metastasis) to distal sites. Once the cancer cells reach their target site, MMPs function similarly to allow them to degrade the extracellular matrix and invade into the local tissues.

*Wen et al. describe a techinque using mangiferin, a natural xanthone compound, to reduce osteosarcoma cell proliferation and metastasis.* The mechanism of mangiferin was to reduce invasion, adhesion and migration of tumor cells by decreasing expression of MMP-2/9. **They concluded** that mangiferin was an effective tool in decreasing growth and spread and inducing apoptosis of cancer cells.

*Zhou et al. looked at MMP-9 expression in osteosarcoma cells and its relation to tumor growth, invasion and metastasis.* **They noted** that expression of MMP-9 in cancer cells was associated with a 4.69 increased OR of metastasis and 7.19 increased OR of poor survival compared to those with negative MMP-9. **They concluded** that MMP-9 may be a useful biomarker for guiding treatment and managing expectations in osteosarcoma.

Figure A demonstrates a right hip pathologic subtrochanteric femur fracture with scalloped lucency noted in the subtrochanteric region.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: MMPs do not directly activate RANK-L. RANK-L is produced by osteoblasts and is responsible for activating osteoclasts.
Answer 2: Vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) is the component of tumor cells most responsible for angiogenesis
Answer 3: Carbonic anhydrase enzymes help to increase acidification at the ruffled border of osteoclasts in order to promote bony destruction, but this is not controlled by MMPs.
Answer 5: Chemokine ligand 12 (CXCL12) acts as a "homing" mechanism to provide a bony target for tumor cells.
