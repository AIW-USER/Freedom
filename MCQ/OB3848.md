## Question

A 21-year-old male presents with gradual foot pain over the past several months. There is no history of trauma. Figures A and B are his plain radiographs and subsequent biopsy specimen. Which of the following is the most likely diagnosis?

![[q3848-a-abc-metatarsal.jpg]]
*Figure A*

![[q3848-b-abcpatho.jpg]]
*Figure B*

## Options

1. Unicameral bone cyst
2. Enchondroma
3. Chronic osteomyelitis
4. Aneurysmal bone cyst
5. Giant cell tumor

## Correct option

**4. Aneurysmal bone cyst**

## Answer explanation

The imaging and biopsy specimen are most consistent with a diagnosis of an aneurysmal bone cyst.

Aneurysmal bone cysts (ABCs) frequently affect individuals in the first 2-3 decades of life. ABCs often present as painful slow-growing lesions. Radiographs show the typical appearance of an intramedullary expansile lesion with septae or striations. In the foot, ABCs are most often located in the metaphysis of the metatarsals. Curettage and bone grafting is the treatment of choice.

*Bos et al. review the diagnosis and treatment of foot tumors.* **They note** that ABCs usually originate in metaphyseal bone, with the metatarsals being the most common site in the foot. Curettage and bone grafting is the treatment of choice, but lesions can recur.

*Steffner et al. studied the effectiveness of adjuvant argon beam treatment versus high-speed burr alone in the treatment and prevention of recurrence in the treatment of ABC's.* **They found** a recurrence rate of 20.6% with high-speed burr alone and a recurrence rate of 7.5% with adjuvant argon beam. **They conclude** that curettage and high-speed burr may not reduce recurrence and argon beam is an effective adjuvant.

*Hegde et al. retrospectively looked at the diagnostic accuracy and complications of core needle biopsy (CNB) for ABCs performed at an outside hospital compared to the authors' home institution.* **They found** no difference in efficacy, odds ratio, or accuracy in CNB performed in house versus outside hospital. **They conclude** that CNB is a useful test for ABCs and may be used reliably from outside hospitals without having to repeat the biopsy.

Figure A shows an AP radiograph demonstrating an expansile lytic metaphyseal lesion of the medullary canal. Figure B shows a biopsy specimen with blood-filled cavernous linings, consistent with an ABC.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Unicameral bone cysts are more frequently seen in the calcaneus, are usually incidental findings that rarely require treatment.
Answer 2: Enchondromas are typically asymptomatic and usually an incidental finding. A "rings and arcs" appearance is often seen on XR due to the cartilaginous matrix. Histology would show cartilage.
Answer 3: Chronic osteomyelitis would show a permeative lytic pattern on imaging. Histology would likely show an abundance of lymphocytes.
Answer 5: Giant cell tumors are typically found in the epiphysis of long bones, with radiographs showing a radiolucent lesion with a rim of reactive bone.
