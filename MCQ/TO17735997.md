## Question

True about limb girdle muscle dystrophy (congenital and developmental musculoskeletal disorders-muscular dystrophies- limb girdle muscular dystrophy)

## Options

2. AD

## Correct option

**3. Both**

## Answer explanation

Muscular dystrophies (MD) are a group of disorders that progressively involve muscles with consequent loss of their bulk and strength. More than 30 types have been described. Common muscular dystrophies are as follows

Duchenne muscular dystrophy: Most common MD. Manifest in childhood with progressive inability to walk and child becomes wheelchair-bound by 12 years and dies of respiratory failure during adolescence. Positive gower sign is characteristic- Child uses his arms to stand from a squat position

![[TO17735997-1.png]]

Becker muscular dystrophy: Similar symptoms to Duchenne but with a later onset and slower progression; death usually occurs in the mid-forties.

Myotonic dystrophy: Most common adult-onset form. Patient becomes unable to relax a contracted muscle. Muscles of neck and face are first to get involved.

Congenital: It manifests before 2 years or may be apparent at birth.

Facioscapulohumeral (FSHD): Onset can be at almost any age but is most commonly seen during teenage years. This involves muscles of the face and shoulder girdle. Winging of the scapula and open eyed sleeping are characteristic features.

Limb-girdle: This variant begins in childhood or teenage years and first affects the proximal group of muscles around shoulder and hip joints. Waddling gait, difficulty in getting up from the toilet seat, climbing stairs and overhead arm motion are common features. Two major types of LGMDs are LGMD1 and LGMD2, these two groups are autosomal dominant and autosomal recessive respectively.

Oculopharyngeal muscular dystrophy: Onset is between the ages of 40 and 70 years. Eyelids, throat, and face are first affected, followed by the shoulder and pelvis.

Answer : C
