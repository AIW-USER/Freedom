## Question

A 25-year-old male presents to your clinic after sustaining a twisting injury to his ankle 2 days ago while playing basketball. He complains of pain over the anterolateral ankle with inability to bear weight. Initial radiographs were unremarkable. On examination, there is pain with ankle dorsiflexion and external rotation of the ankle. Which imaging finding is most sensitive and specific for his diagnosis?

## Options

1. AP ankle radiograph with 4 mm of tibiofibular clear space
2. AP ankle radiograph with 6 mm of medial clear space
3. Mortise ankle radiograph with 5 mm of tibiofibular overlap
4. Coronal ankle MRI sequence demonstrating discontinuity of the deltoid ligament
5. Coronal ankle MRI sequence demonstrating hyperintense signal contiguous between mortise and tibiofibular articulation

## Correct option

**5. Coronal ankle MRI sequence demonstrating hyperintense signal contiguous between mortise and tibiofibular articulation**

## Answer explanation

This patient's examination is suggestive of an occult syndesmotic injury. The coronal MRI image will often reveal a lambda sign, a hyperintense signal communicating between the ankle joint and the syndesmosis, which is the most sensitive and specific imaging finding for syndesmotic injury.

While physical examination findings may be suggestive of syndesmotic injury, imaging significantly increases the diagnostic sensitivity. Radiographic measurements such as tibiofibular overlap and tibiofibular clear space have been described but have demonstrated limited sensitivity and predictive value for syndesmotic injuries. Additionally, reliability of these measurements on plain film radiographs may be poor. The recent description of the lambda sign on coronal MRI sequences, in addition to physical examination, has been associated with high rates of inter-rater reliability and both improved sensitivity and specificity for diagnosing syndesmotic injuries.

*Ryan et al. reviewed radiographic and MRI findings of patients with arthroscopically diagnosed syndesmotic instability and compared these patients' images to uninjured patients.* *They described the Lambda sign, which was considered present if there were findings of hyperintense signal communicating from the ankle joint to the syndesmosis on coronal MRI sequences.* **The authors concluded** that the lambda sign was not an independent predictor of syndesmotic instability, but when combined with positive physical exam findings, it was both sensitive and specific.

*Han et al. compared MRI images to arthroscopic findings in order to evaluate sensitivity and specificity of MRI in the diagnosis of syndesmotic injury.* **The authors noted** >90% sensitivity, specificity, and predictability of MRI in evaluating syndesmotic injuries. **They concluded** that MRI should be the diagnostic tool of choice for syndesmotic injury.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Tibiofibular clear space should be <6 mm on the AP or mortise ankle radiographs. This is a normal finding.
Answer 2: While medial clear space widening can be associated with syndesmotic injury and some studies have indicated it has good specificity, it’s sensitivity for syndesmotic injury is very poor. Additionally, medial clear space is best evaluated on the ankle mortise view.
Answer 3: Normal tibiofibular overlap should be >1 mm on the ankle mortise view or >6 mm on the AP radiograph, making 5 mm a normal measurement. Some studies have also questioned the diagnostic usefulness of these measurements, as sensitivity is low.
Answer 4: Deltoid ligament injury is not a good indicator of syndesmotic injury. *Han et al.* **noted** 8 patients in their study with syndesmotic injury and none of them had deltoid ligament ruptures.
