## Answer explanation

Ans: D

The intensely black shadows visible in CT image in question is “AIR” and the pathology hence seen is Tension pneumocephalus. Appropriately it is called as Mt. Fuji sign with bilateral pneumocephalus on Axial non contrast CT scan in which the two frontal poles appear peaked and are surrounded by and separated by air, resembling the silhouette of the twin peaks of Mt. Fuji.

TENSION PNEUMOCEPHALUS

- Tension Pneumocephalus is accumulation of intra cranial air that produces mass effect resulting in neurological deterioration. It is an unusual but life-threatening neurosurgical emergency, which has been described following head trauma (most common cause), epidural injections or complicating neurological, spinal, craniofacial or sinus surgery.
- Intracranial gas can develop elevated pressure in the following settings: -

1. when nitrous oxide anesthesia is not discontinued prior to closure of the dura

2. when a “ball-valve” effect occurs due to an opening to the intracranial compartment with soft tissue (e.g. brain) that may permit air to enter but prevent exit of air or CSF

3. when trapped room temperature air expands with warming to body temperature: a modest increase of only ≈ 4% results from this effect

 4. In the presence of continued production by gas-producing organisms.

- Patients may present with seizures, headaches, neurological deficits, and altered mental status.
- Since simple pneumocephalus usually does not require treatment, it is critical to differentiate it from tension pneumocephalus, which may need to be evacuated (by making burr holes) if symptomatic.
