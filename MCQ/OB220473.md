## Question

Patient A and Patient B are active 68-year-old men who both sustained displaced transcervical femoral neck fractures after a mechanical ground level fall. Patient A and Patient B undergo hemiarthroplasty and total hip arthroplasty for their injuries, respectively. When considering the outcomes of their procedures, which of the following is most accurate?

## Options

1. Patient B is more likely to have a periprosthetic fracture
2. Post-operative self-reported walking distances for both patients are expected to be similar to pre-injury levels
3. Patient B is more likely to sustain a prosthetic hip dislocation
4. Patient A is more likely to have a greater capacity for physical activity
5. Patient B is more likely to be readmitted within 30 days of his procedure

## Correct option

**3. Patient B is more likely to sustain a prosthetic hip dislocation**

## Answer explanation

Patients who undergo total hip arthroplasty (THA) for displaced femoral neck fractures are more likely to have post-operative hip dislocations compared to those who undergo hemiarthroplasty (answer choice 3)

Hemiarthroplasty and THA are both commonly used to treat displaced intracapsular femoral neck fractures. THA is associated with a greater rate of postoperative dislocation, lower rate of short and long-term mortality, and equivalent rates of reoperation compared to hemiarthroplasty. Greater physical function scores have also been observed in THA patients compared to patients with hemiarthroplasties.

*Tohidi et al. reviewed the clinical outcomes of a cohort of approximately 50,000 patients at least 60 years old who underwent hemiarthroplasty or THA for a femoral neck fracture.* **They found** that THA patients had higher rates of dislocation at 30-day, one-year, and two-year time points, lower one and two-year mortality rates, and equivalent rates of revision surgery and 30-day readmission. **They concluded** that treatment counseling should involve discussion of these short and long-term complication risk profiles.

*Avery et al. performed a prospective randomized controlled trial comparing outcomes of 81 patients who underwent either THA or hemiarthroplasty for displaced intracapsular femoral neck fractures.* **They found** a higher rate of mortality in hemiarthroplasty patients at 100 months after surgery. Improved function and pain were observed in the THA group, but these results did not reach statistical significance. **They concluded** that THA in this patient population offers mortality and morbidity benefits compared to hemiarthroplasty.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Rates of periprosthetic fracture are comparable between these two surgical interventions.
Answer 2: Self-reported walking distances have been reported to be significantly decreased after surgery compared to pre-injury levels regardless of the type of surgical intervention received.
Answer 4: THA patients, not hemiarthroplasty patients, have been observed to have greater physical function scores after femoral neck fractures.
Answer 5: THA and hemiarthroplasty patients have comparable rates of 30-day readmissions.
