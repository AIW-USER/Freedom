## Question

A 46-year-old male presents to the trauma bay after being involved in a motor vehicle collision. Imaging reveals that he has a pelvic ring injury, and he is sent for angioembolization. Which of the following is the most common vessel for angioembolization with pelvic ring injuries?

## Options

1. Internal iliac artery
2. Deep circumflex iliac artery
3. Inferior epigastric artery
4. Superior epigastric artery
5. External pudendal artery

## Correct option

**1. Internal iliac artery**

## Answer explanation

The most common vessel for angioembolization in pelvic ring fractures is the internal iliac artery.

Pelvic ring fractures are high energy fractures of the pelvic ring which typically occur due to blunt trauma. Diagnosis is made radiographically with pelvic radiographs and further characterized with CT scan. Treatment is typically operative fixation depending on the degree of pelvis instability, fracture displacement, and patient activity demands.

*Vaidya et al. reviewed the use of angioembolization in the management of bleeding pelvic fractures.* **They reported** that the most common vessels for angioembolization are, in decreasing order, the internal iliac artery and its branches, the superior gluteal artery, the obturator artery, and the internal pudendal artery. **They concluded** that the mortality rates after angioembolization are 16% to 50%, but deaths are usually related to concomitant injuries.

*Agolini et al. reviewed the success rate of embolization in stopping hemorrhage for unstable patients with severe pelvic fractures, to calculate the time to achieve embolization, and to determine the yield from angiography.* **They reported** that only a small percentage of patients with pelvic fractures require embolization, but when it is used, embolization can be 100% effective. **They concluded** that age, time to achieve embolization, and initial hemodynamic instability appear to be important factors in survival.

*Tanizaki et al. retrospectively reviewed the relationship between survival and time from arrival to angiography for hemodynamically unstable patients with pelvic trauma.* **They reported** that patients who were embolized within 60 min of arrival had a significantly lower mortality rate (16 vs. 64%). **They concluded** that earlier pelvic embolization within 60 min may affect the survival of hemodynamically unstable patients with pelvic fractures.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 2-4: The most common vessels for angioembolization are, in decreasing order, the internal iliac artery and its branches, the superior gluteal artery, the obturator artery, and the internal pudendal artery.
