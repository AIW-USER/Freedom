## Question

A 14-year-old boy sustains a crash while mountain biking, resulting in a completely displaced, midshaft clavicle fracture with 24 mm of shortening. Neurovascular exam is normal, and the skin is intact without signs of tenting. He is treated nonoperatively in a sling. Which of the following is the most likely long-term outcome of nonoperative management?

## Options

1. Fracture malunion with significant shortening
2. Painful fracture nonunion
3. Asymptomatic fracture nonunion
4. Fracture union without significant shortening
5. Fracture malunion with significant angular deformity

## Correct option

**4. Fracture union without significant shortening**

## Answer explanation

Fracture union without significant shortening is the most likely long-term outcome after nonoperative treatment in adolescents due to substantial remodeling capacity (Answer 4).

Pediatric midshaft clavicle fractures are common injuries, typically resulting from a fall onto an outstretched arm or direct trauma to the lateral shoulder. Due to the significant remodeling potential of the clavicle, the vast majority of these fractures heal reliably with sling immobilization; surgery is reserved for open injury, threatened soft tissue, or vascular compromise. Long-term, nonoperative care yields functional results comparable to fixation while avoiding implant-related complications, and the clavicle frequently remodels shortening and deformity over time. Nonunion is rare in this age group, and clinically significant residual deformity is uncommon at late follow-up.

*Pennock et al. reviewed completely displaced adolescent clavicle fractures treated nonoperatively and quantified remodeling.* **They found** mean improvements of 61% in shortening and 31% in angulation at ~3.4 years, with only 3% having >20 mm residual shortening at final follow-up. **They concluded** that significant remodeling—including in older adolescents—likely explains the low rate of symptomatic malunion.

*Sabatini et al. prospectively stratified adolescents with comminuted “Z-type” fractures and compared nonoperative care with ORIF.* **They noted** no difference in nonunion (0% both groups), symptomatic malunion, refracture, or validated patient-reported outcomes at 2 years after adjusting for confounders. **They concluded** that surgery did not improve outcomes over nonoperative treatment in this cohort.

*Cole et al. analyzed national treatment trends and complications in adolescent clavicle fractures.* **They observed** increasing surgical fixation despite literature showing equivalent outcomes with nonoperative care and highlighted a high revision rate largely from hardware removal. **They concluded** that rising surgery rates run counter to outcome evidence favoring nonsurgical management in many adolescents.

Incorrect answer choices:
Answer choices 1,2, 3, and 5: Significant persistent shortening, nonunion, or large angular deformities are uncommon in this population, given the significant propensity for bone healing in the skeletally immature individual, and considerable remodeling potential of the clavicle.
