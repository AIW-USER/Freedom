## Question

A 60 years male came with pain in lower back and hips. X ray showed skull involvement (see pic). Alkaline phosphatase levels are markedly elevated. Likely diagnosis is

![[TO17912705-1.png]]

## Options

1. Hyperparathyroidism
2. Paget’s disease
3. Multiple Myeloma
4. Langerhans cell histiocytosis

## Correct option

**1. Hyperparathyroidism**

## Answer explanation

Ans: B

The clinical history best suits the diagnosis of Paget’s disease. The disease is a disorder of exaggerated bone turnover (increased bone resorption being balanced by increased bone formation) classically seen after the age of 40 years. There is a lytic phase where bone resorption predominates followed by a blastic phase whereby bones become sclerotic and deformed. The brittleness that results from abnormal bone remodelling, makes bones prone to pathological fractures.

Owing to increased bone turnover, alkaline phosphatase (ALP) levels are classically raised (in contrast to Multiple Myeloma where ALP is normal).

In addition, the skull image shown in the picture is classically depicting multiple cotton wool spots, peculiar of Paget’s disease. The spots result from thickened, disorganized skull trabeculae (due to abnormal bone remodelling) which leads to areas of poorly defined fluffy sclerotic patches developing in a previously lucent area of bone.

Shown below are some classical skull findings in various orthopedic disorders: -

Fig A: Salt and Pepper appearance of Hyperparathyroidism showing multiple tiny well-defined lucencies in caused by resorption of trabecular bone

Fig B: Eosinophilic granuloma (a subtype of LCH) lesion with sharp, punched out borders that are uneven across the inner and outer table (affects outer table more than the inner table), causing a “beveled edge” that gives them the characteristic double contour.

Fig C: Osteoporosis circumscripta (a finding seen in lytic phase of Paget’s disease), where there develops a highly circumscribed (focal) lytic lesion in the skull bone

Fig D: Multiple punched out lytic lesions classical of skull involvement in Multiple Myeloma that resemble raindrops hitting a surface and splashing (Rain Drop Skull).
