## Answer explanation

Figure E demonstrates a patient with diffuse idiopathic skeletal hyperostosis (DISH). Patients with DISH have a high prevalence of co-morbid diabetes mellitus as evidenced by elevated hemoglobin A1c.

Diffuse idiopathic skeletal hyperostosis is characterized by ligamentous ossification, specifically within the anterior longitudinal ligament of the spine, creating marginal osteophytosis and an anterior osseous ridge. Patients may manifest extra-spinal ligamentous ossification as well. Serum studies of DISH patients demonstrates elevated growth hormone (GH) and insulin-like growth factor 1 (IGF-1). The estimated prevalence of disease is 1.6-13% among non-diabetics compared with 13-49% in patients with diabetes.

*Smith et. al. reviewed the musculoskeletal manifestations of diabetes mellitus including DISH.* **They report** the proposed mechanism of causation, which is prolonged elevated levels of insulin, which stimulates new bone growth. Among all patients with DISH, an estimated 12-80% have impaired glucose tolerance, and 30% of those suffer from obesity. **They report** an age-related increase in the incidence of DISH which occurs at an earlier age in diabetic patients. The risk of DISH is 3-fold higher for type 1 diabetes compared with type 2.

*Denko et. al. evaluated the relationship between BMI, GH, and IGF-1 levels in patients with DISH.* **They found** elevated basal serum insulin levels in patients with BMI>28 compared with those of BMI 23-28. However, BMI did not correlate with basal serum GH or IGF-1 levels.

Figure E is a lateral cervical spine radiograph which demonstrates flowing anterior syndesmophytes with preservation of the disc spaces consistent with DISH.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Figure A is an AP pelvis radiograph which demonstrates sclerosis in the bilateral sacroiliac joints which is consistent with ankylosing spondylitis (AS). Patients with AS may demonstrate elevated serum HLA-B27.
Answer 2: Figure B is an AP radiograph of the left knee demonstrating a Pellegrini-Stieda lesion. This is consistent with a prior injury to the origin of the medial collateral ligament.
Answer 3: Figure C is a lateral radiograph of the ankle demonstrating an os trigonum.
Answer 4: Figure D is an AP radiograph of the left knee demonstrating an osteochondroma.
