## Question

An 84-year-old male with a 50-pack-year smoking history returns for 6-week postoperative follow-up complaining of persistent left hip pain and difficulty ambulating following prophylactic intramedullary nailing of his left femur. Two months prior, radiographs of his left proximal femur revealed the pathologic lesion demonstrated in Figure A. Staging at that time revealed a 5-mm lung nodule. Prostate specific antigen, serum protein electrophoresis, and urine protein electrophoresis were within normal limits. Figure B demonstrates his current left hip radiograph and Figure C is a biopsy specimen obtained at the time of surgery. What is the recommended course of treatment at this time?

## Options

1. Continued physical therapy and analgesics
2. Chemotherapy
3. Radiation
4. Proximal femur replacement
5. Amputation

## Correct option

**5. Amputation**

## Answer explanation

An intramedullary nail has inadvertently been placed through a dedifferentiated chondrosarcoma, seeding the entire femur and surgical field. Of the choices provided, amputation is the patient’s only option now.

The role of a biopsy is to confirm a suspected diagnosis, and it is important the center performing the biopsy is also capable of subsequent management/treatment. Referral to a tertiary center may be warranted. Indications for biopsy include aggressive bone or soft tissue lesions, unclear diagnosis in a symptomatic patient, solitary bone lesions in a patient with a history of cancer, soft tissue lesions larger than 5 cm deep to the fascia, or overlying bone or neurovascular structures.

*Mankin et al. commented on the findings compiled by the Musculoskeletal Tumor Society in 1982 and 1992 regarding the hazards associated with biopsies of primary musculoskeletal sarcomas.* *Despite earlier recommendations that these biopsies be performed at a definitive treatment center and referred when necessary, their follow-up study was largely unchanged.* *Comprising 597 patients submitted by twenty-five surgeons from twenty-one institutions,* **their findings demonstrated** an unacceptably high rate of diagnostic error (17.8% in which cases from referring and treatment centers were combined), often resulting in significantly increased morbidity and death.

*Traina et al. summarized current concepts in the biopsy of musculoskeletal tumors.* *They performed a literature review on twenty-one studies commenting on the diagnostic data of various biopsy modalities for bone and soft tissue tumors.* **They concluded** that the optimal biopsy technique is unclear, but suggest the use of image guidance to improve accuracy and advocate for core needle over incisional biopsy when possible due to a low risk of contamination and value.

Figure A is a left proximal femur radiograph with a pathologic lesion caused by a dedifferentiated chondrosarcoma. Figure B is a left proximal femur radiograph with a pathologic lesion status post cephalomedullary nail fixation. Figure C shows histology consistent with a dedifferentiated chondrosarcoma. Illustration A is the gross specimen of a resected proximal femur with dedifferentiated chondrosarcoma. Illustration B demonstrates limb sparing surgery in the form of a proximal femur replacement for the patient with the lesion in Illustration A.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The patient’s symptoms are unlikely to improve with these modalities and they do not address his underlying pathology.
Answer 2: Chondrosarcomas are a surgical disease and the role of chemotherapy remains unclear.
Answer 3: Radiation may play a palliative or adjuvant role in the treatment of dedifferentiated chondrosarcomas. However, definitive treatment is surgical.
Answer 4: This fails to address that the entire femur and surgical field have been contaminated at the index surgery.
