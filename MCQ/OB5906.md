## Question

A 45-year-old left-hand dominant diabetic woman presents with an atraumatic onset of right shoulder pain followed by progressive loss of motion. She undergoes an MR arthrogram, pictured in Figure A. All of the following are associated with an increased risk of this condition in diabetic patients EXCEPT:

![[q5906-a-mrshoulder.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Age > 50
2. Duration of diabetes
3. History of myocardial infarction
4. Presence of autonomic neuropathy
5. Type of diabetes

## Correct option

**5. Type of diabetes**

## Answer explanation

The clinical presentation is consistent with adhesive capsulitis. All of the variables listed are risk factors for adhesive capsulitis EXCEPT for type of diabetes. Adhesive capsulitis is elevated in both type I and type II diabetic patients.

Adhesive capsulitis (AC) is characterized by painful, gradual loss of active and passive shoulder motion most commonly affecting the nondominant arm in woman aged 40-60. Risk factors include diabetes, thyroid disorders, history of myocardial infarcation, Crohn's disease, prolonged immobilization. Diabetes mellitus is associated with a much worse prognosis and poorer outcomes of both nonsurgical and surgical treatment. Diabetic regimen (insulin dependent or non-insulin dependent) as well as glycemic control are not significantly associated with development of adhesive capsulitis.

*Neviaser et al authored a review article detailing the clinical presentation and management of AC.* Nonoperative management with stretching and heat is the mainstay of treatment, regardless of stage. Surgical options should be reserved for patients who fail conservative management and include manipulation under anesthesia and capsular release. The presence of diabetes is associated with a significantly worse prognosis, greater need for surgery and suboptimal results regardless of treatment.

*Arkkila et al performed a cross-sectional study looking at the association between AC and chronic diabetic complications.* **The authors concluded** that AC is a common disorder in type I and II diabetic patients, is associated with increased age in both types and associated with duration of diabetes in type I patients. Independent associations were found between AC and autonomic neuropathy in both types and history of myocardial infarction in type I patients.

Figure A is a coronal T1 FSE image demonstrating loss of the normal axillary recess indicative of capsular contracture. Illustration A shows a normal axillary recess versus an absent axillary recess seen in AC.

Incorrect Responses:
Answers 1-4: In patients with diabetes, older age, duration of diabetes, history of myocardial infarction and presence of autonomic neuropathy are all associated with the development of AC.
