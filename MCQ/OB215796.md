## Question

A 29-year-old male sustains the injury shown in Figure A following a dirt bike crash. Which of the following is true of an infrarapatellar (standard) approach for intramedullary nailing compared to a suprapatellar approach?

![[q215796-a-tibia.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Improved postoperative knee range of motion
2. More anterior knee pain
3. Improvement in quality of the reduction
4. Improved postoperative quadriceps strength
5. Lower rate of postoperative patellofemoral disease

## Correct option

**2. More anterior knee pain**

## Answer explanation

A standard infrapatellar approach for intramedullary nailing of tibial shaft fractures has been shown to result in higher rates of postoperative anterior knee pain.

Intramedullary nailing of tibial shaft fractures can be performed via either an infrapatellar or suprapatellar approach. The traditional infrapatellar approach requires a significant amount of knee flexion, which can make reductions more difficult. Additionally, the infrapatellar approach has been shown to result in increased complaints of anterior knee pain postoperatively. Conversely, the suprapatellar approach can be performed with the knee in a semi-extended position, which has shown to improve reductions, especially with proximal third fractures as the one shown in Figure A. Numerous studies have evaluated the patellofemoral joint with both MRI and arthroscopy and have not found a difference in injury to the patellofemoral joint between the two approaches.

*Gelbke et al. review the patellofemoral contact forces between the suprapatellar and infrapatellar nailing in a cadaveric model.* **They report** that the mean peak contact pressure on the patella and femoral condyles was 1.84 MPa (range, 1.09-2.95 MPa) and 2.13 MPa (range, 1.10-2.86 MPa), respectively, during suprapatellar nailing. **They conclude** that while these are increased compared to infrapatellar nailing they are significantly below the amount of pressure required to cause chondral damage.

*Chan et al. review the two approaches for intramedullary nailing of tibial shaft fractures in a randomized controlled pilot study.* **They report** that an improved pain score in the suprapatellar group, but no difference in range of motion or union rate. **They conclude** that the two techniques were equal as it related to outcomes during this small pilot study.

Illustration A are AP and lateral radiographs demonstrating a proximal third extra-articular tibial shaft fracture.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1 and 4: No difference in knee range of motion or quadriceps strength has been reported between the two approaches.
Answer 3: Improved reduction quality is seen with semiextended techniques utilizing the suprapatellar approach.
Answer 5: Patellofemoral disease is not increased following suprapatellar nailing as demonstrated on numerous arthroscopic and MRI based studies.
