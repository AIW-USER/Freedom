Chapter: Regional Orthopedics MCQs — Jersey Finger

## Question

A 24-year-old athlete presents 5 days after a suspected zone I flexor digitorum profundus (FDP) avulsion of the ring finger. Radiographs demonstrate a large bony fragment trapped specifically at the level of the A2 pulley. According to the Leddy and Packer classification, which of the following is the most accurate description of this injury and its surgical implications?

## Options

1. Type I: The tendon has retracted into the palm, requiring urgent surgery within 7–10 days to avoid permanent muscle contracture.
2. Type II: The tendon is held at the level of the PIP joint by the vincula, allowing for a more flexible surgical window.
3. Type III: The large bony fragment prevents retraction proximal to the A2 pulley, preserving the blood supply via intact vincula.
4. Type IV: The injury represents a double avulsion where the FDP tendon has further dissociated from the bony fragment itself.

## Correct option

**3. Type III: The large bony fragment prevents retraction proximal to the A2 pulley, preserving the blood supply via intact vincula.**

## Answer explanation

The Leddy and Packer classification is the standard classification, based on the level to which the avulsed tendon retracts. The retraction level determines the viability of the vincula (the mesotenon-like structures that provide the FDP's blood supply within the digital sheath) and therefore the time window for successful repair.

Type I — Tendon Retracts to the Palm:

The tendon avulses without a bony fragment (or with a tiny fragment that does not catch on the pulleys) and retracts all the way into the palm, at or proximal to the A1 pulley. Both the long and short vincula are ruptured during retraction, completely disrupting the tendon's intrasynovial blood supply. Without blood supply, the tendon undergoes rapid degeneration and becomes oedematous, soft, and non-viable within days. The tendon sheath also contracts rapidly.

This type requires the most urgent treatment — direct repair must be performed within 7–10 days of injury. Beyond this window, the retracted tendon becomes too swollen and friable to pass through the digital sheath, and the sheath itself contracts, making direct repair impossible. Delayed cases require tendon grafting (staged reconstruction with a silicone rod followed by free tendon graft — the Hunter technique) or DIP joint arthrodesis.

Type II — Tendon Retracts to the PIP Joint Level:

The tendon avulses with a small bony fragment that catches at the chiasma of the FDS (Camper's chiasma) at the PIP joint level, preventing further retraction. The short vinculum (vinculum breve profundus, which inserts near the FDP insertion) is ruptured, but the long vinculum (vinculum longum profundus, which arises from the FDS chiasma region) remains intact, preserving partial blood supply to the tendon.

This is the most common type. Because the vinculum longum maintains blood supply and the tendon has not retracted into the palm, the tendon remains viable for a longer period. Repair can be performed within 6 weeks of injury (some authors extend to 3 months), though earlier repair is always preferred.

Type III — Large Bony Fragment at the A4 Pulley Level:

The tendon avulses with a large bony fragment from the distal phalanx that is too big to pass through the A4 pulley (located over the middle phalanx). The tendon remains at the DIP joint level with both vincula intact, maintaining full blood supply.

This type has the best prognosis and the longest window for repair — the fragment can be reduced and fixed with a small screw or pull-out wire even weeks after injury, and the fully vascularised tendon heals reliably. If the bony fragment is large enough, ORIF with a mini-screw provides anatomical reduction of both the fracture and the tendon insertion.

Type IV (Added by Smith, 1981): A bony avulsion (Type III) combined with simultaneous avulsion of the tendon from the bony fragment — effectively a Type III fracture with a Type I or II tendon retraction. This requires both fracture fixation and tendon repair.

Type V (Al-Qattan, 2001): A variant involving avulsion of the FDP with a comminuted fracture of the distal phalanx. Management depends on the fragment configuration and tendon retraction level.

Correct Answer: C
