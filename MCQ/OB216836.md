## Question

A 22-year-old female presents to the office for evaluation of ankle pain after sustaining an injury while running track 7 days ago. She reports that she rolled her ankle at that time. She was placed into a splint and has been using crutches since. On examination, she has tenderness to palpation distal to the fibula. Which of the following is true regarding the injury evaluated with the maneuver depicted in Figure A?

*Figure A*

## Options

1. Anterior talofibular ligament injury may result in subtalar instability
2. Calcaneofibular ligament is best tested in dorsiflexion
3. Calcaneofibular ligament injury occurs prior to anterior and posterior talofibular ligament injuries
4. Maneuver requires injury to the posterior talofibular ligament to be positive
5. Anatomic reconstruction is recommended as intiial treatment in Grade II & IIII soft tissue injuries

## Correct option

**2. Calcaneofibular ligament is best tested in dorsiflexion**

## Answer explanation

This patient likely sustained an ankle sprain. The ATFL is best tested in plantarflexion, while the CFL is best tested in dorsiflexion.

Diagnosis of ankle sprains can be made clinically with swelling and ecchymosis of the ankle and pain with range of motion. Radiographs are only indicated when clinical examination meets criteria (Ottawa ankle rules). The anterior drawer test is performed with the ankle in 10° of plantar flexion, resulting in the greatest amount of translation, investigating the integrity of the anterior talofibular ligament with a key distance of translation being 8 to 10 mm. The patient should be sitting and has their knees flexed over the edge of a table or bench, while the physician uses one hand to stabilize the distal leg and with the other hand applies an anterior force to the heel in an attempt to gap the talus anteriorly from under the tibia. The anterior drawer test result reflects injury to the ATFL and a possible injury to the CFL.

*Tropp et al. compared coordination training and orthosis use for the prevention of ankle joint injuries in soccer.* **They report** that both techniques reduced the frequency of ankle sprains in soccer players with previous ankle problems. **They concluded** that the orthosis is an alternative to taping, and can be used during the rehabilitation period after injury or when playing on uneven ground, while coordination training ought to be included in the rehabilitation of ankle injuries to prevent functional instability.

*Stiell et al. prospectively compared original (first stage) and refined (second stage) clinical decision rules regarding the use of radiography in acute ankle injuries.* **They reported** that the potential reduction in radiography is estimated to be 34% for the ankle series and 30% for the foot series using the refined rules. **They concluded** that refinement and validation have shown the Ottawa ankle rules to be 100% sensitive for fractures, to be reliable, and to have the potential to allow physicians to safely reduce the number of radiographs ordered in patients with ankle injuries by one-third.

*Osborne et al. reviewed ankle sprains and prevention.* **They report** that critical evaluation of the effectiveness of ankle braces supports their use for at least 6 months following injury in athletes who have sustained a moderate or severe sprain; however, their role in primary prevention of ankle sprain is less evident. **They concluded** that functional ankle rehabilitation is the mainstay of acute ankle sprain treatment and in recent reviews has been deemed preferable to immobilization or early surgery for the initial treatment of acutely injured ankles.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Subtalar instability can be difficult to differentiate from posterior ankle instability because the CFL (not ATFL) contributes to both
Answer 3: The CFL is the 2nd most common ligament injury in lateral ankle sprains; ATFL is the most common
Answer 4: The anterior drawer test result reflects injury to the anterior talofibular ligament and a possible injury to the calcaneofibular ligament
Answer 5: For Grade I-III soft tissue ankle sprains, functional ankle rehabilitation is the mainstay of acute ankle sprain treatment
