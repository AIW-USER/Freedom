Chapter: General Orthopedics MCQs — DVT Prophylaxis

## Question

A 65-year-old female presents to the emergency department with severe, life-threatening gastrointestinal bleeding on postoperative day 5 following a Total Knee Arthroplasty (TKA). Her discharge records indicate she was prescribed a direct thrombin inhibitor for DVT prophylaxis. What is the standard maintenance prophylactic dose of this medication following TKA, and what specific targeted agent should be administered immediately to reverse its effects?

## Options

1. 10 mg once daily; reversed by Prothrombin Complex Concentrate (PCC) and Vitamin K.
2. 2.5 mg twice daily; reversed by Idarucizumab.
3. 150 mg twice daily; reversed by Andexanet alfa.
4. 220 mg once daily; reversed by Idarucizumab.

## Correct option

**4. 220 mg once daily; reversed by Idarucizumab.**

## Answer explanation

The only direct oral thrombin (Factor IIa) inhibitor widely used for VTE prophylaxis post-TKA is Dabigatran. After an initial partial dose on the day of surgery, the standard maintenance prophylactic dose is 220 mg once daily. Its specific, FDA-approved monoclonal antibody reversal agent is Idarucizumab.

Direct Oral Anticoagulants (DOACs)

DOACs have transformed thromboprophylaxis practice by offering oral administration, fixed dosing, no monitoring requirement, and efficacy comparable to LMWH.

Rivaroxaban (Xarelto): A direct Factor Xa inhibitor. Dose: 10 mg oral once daily, starting 6–10 hours postoperatively. Validated in the RECORD 1–4 trials (>12,000 patients), which demonstrated superiority over enoxaparin for total VTE prevention after hip and knee replacement with comparable major bleeding rates. Approved duration: 35 days after THR, 14 days after TKR.

Apixaban (Eliquis): A direct Factor Xa inhibitor. Dose: 2.5 mg oral twice daily, starting 12–24 hours postoperatively. Validated in the ADVANCE 1–3 trials, demonstrating non-inferiority to enoxaparin with lower bleeding rates — apixaban has the most favourable bleeding profile among the DOACs. Approved duration: 32–38 days after THR, 10–14 days after TKR.

Dabigatran (Pradaxa): A direct thrombin (Factor IIa) inhibitor. Dose: 220 mg oral once daily (110 mg for patients >75 years or with moderate renal impairment), starting 1–4 hours postoperatively with a half-dose. Validated in the RE-MODEL and RE-NOVATE trials. Approved duration: 28–35 days after THR, 10 days after TKR. Uniquely among DOACs, dabigatran has a specific reversal agent — idarucizumab (Praxbind) — a monoclonal antibody fragment that immediately and completely reverses dabigatran's anticoagulant effect.

Answer: 4
