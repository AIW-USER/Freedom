## Options

1. Aneurysmal bone cyst
2. Giant cell tumor
3. Telangectatic osteosarcoma
4. High-grade osteosarcoma
5. Osteomyelitis

## Correct option

**2. Giant cell tumor**

## Answer explanation

The photo, radiographs, MRI, and histology are consistent with giant cell tumor, a benign aggressive lesion common around the knee, pelvis, and distal radius. The histology photograph shows the bland osteocytic stroma with multi-nucleated giant cells, where all the nuclei of the stromal and giant cells are homogeneous, consistent with giant cell tumor. The extra-articular resected specimen is shown in Illustration A. While giant cell tumor is a "benign" lesion, a small percentage (~2-5%) can metastasize to the lungs. While telangectatic osteosarcoma should be considered based on the history of pain and the radiographic appearance, the histology slide is not characteristic. Telangectatic osteosarcoma histology would show dilated, blood-filled vascular spaces lined by malignant osteoblasts (Illustration B).

*Blackley et al evaluated whether curettage and reconstruction was equivalent to curettage with adjuvant treatment for giant cell tumors.* **They found** no difference in recurrence suggesting that "the adequacy of the removal of the tumor rather than the use of adjuvant modalities is what determines the risk of recurrence."

*Kocher et al reviewed their results in 24 patients with giant cell tumors treated with reconstruction using osteoarticular allografts.* **They found** an equivalent rate of tumor recurrence, little pain with common activities, and good function suggesting osteoarticular allografts can be used effectively for reconstruction in giant cell tumors.
