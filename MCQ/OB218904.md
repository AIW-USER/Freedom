## Question

A 47-year-old food truck vendor presents to the emergency room after cutting the distal tip of his ring finger off with a meat cleaver. On exam, there is an exposed distal phalanx and less than 1 cm of residual nail plate intact. When performing a revision amputation procedure at the bedside, the residual nail plate is removed and a rongeur is used to take back the exposed bone until adequate soft tissue coverage is achieved. The patient returns to the clinic unhappy, explaining that his nail has regrown with a large split down the middle (Figure A). What structure was most likely damaged to result in this type of nail deformity?

## Options

1. Eponychium
2. Germinal matrix
3. Hyponychium
4. Lunula
5. Sterile matrix

## Correct option

**2. Germinal matrix**

## Answer explanation

The germinal matrix is responsible for 90% of nail growth. Scar formation within the germinal matrix causes the absence of nail production at the location of the scar, resulting in a split nail deformity.

Fingertip injuries are among the most common hand injuries seen in the emergency department setting and can be generally divided into two broad categories: nail bed injuries (+/- tuft fractures) and partial fingertip amputations. When addressing a nail bed injury, the residual nail plate is removed, the underlying nail bed is repaired (typically with chromic suture), and the eponychial fold is splinted with the nail remnant or an aluminum foil. If the eponychial fold is not splinted open and subsequently scars to the sterile matrix, the underlying proximal germinal matrix will continue to form a new nail that cannot adhere to the distal sterile matrix, resulting in the formation of a nail inclusion cyst that is typically painful and bothersome to the patient. This can similarly occur in the setting of partial fingertip amputations that undergoes revision amputation when residual germinal matrix remains under the primarily approximated wound. In the case of the patient in this vignette, the central portion of the germinal matrix was injured either from the initial injury or the revision amputation, leading to scar tissue formation within the germinal matrix, generating a zone of nail agenesis and a resultant split nail deformity. Though often only a cosmetic deformity, treatment options include excision of the scar and nail matrix transfer with graft from an adjacent injured finger or the second toe.

Yuan. et al reviewed 43 studies in a systematic review of outcomes after revision amputation as treatment for traumatic finger amputation. The authors demonstrated that revision amputation resulted in better static two-point discrimination and overall arc of motion compared with local flaps. Patients self-reported outcomes as either "satisfactory," "good," or "excellent," with the most frequently associated complaint being cold intolerance.

Neustein. et al, in a review of treatment options for fingertip injuries, report that allowing the injuries to heal by secondary intention with revision amputation typically yields an average time to healing of 4 weeks while also decreasing time missed from work and avoiding the donor-site morbidity and long period of immobilization required for flap-based procedures.

Germann. et al, provide an update to the most commonly used flaps and semiocclusive dressing treatments for fingertip injuries. With a conservative treatment approach, the semiocclusive dressing acts as an artificial skin that preserves pH, temperature, polymorph cell accumulation, immunoglobulin concentration, and moisture, portending a healing time of 2-8 weeks on average. The pulp has been shown to regain a near-normal contour with sensibility and restitution of the fingerprint pattern, as well as the return of nail plate growth if the germinal matrix is intact. Nail plate deformities such as a "hook nail" can be encountered if the pulp is shortened too much or overtightened with suture material.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The eponychium is the skin proximal to the nail that covers the nail fold. Injury to this structure would not cause aberrant nail growth.
Answer 3: The hyponychium is the skin distal to the nail bed. Injury to this would not cause the nail plate deformity seen with a split nail.
Answer 4: The lunula is the white portion of the proximal nail that demarcates the sterile matrix from the germinal matrix.
Answer 5: The sterile matrix is the portion of the nail bed distal to the lunula to which the nail plate adheres, but is not primarily responsible for nail growth.
