## Question

Figure 118 is the radiograph of a 2-year-old boy who had an isolated injury. An immediate hip spica cast was chosen as the method of treatment. Which of the following techniques increases risk for compartment syndrome in the left leg following cast placement?

![[q8462-a-118.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Using a single-leg spica instead of a double-leg spica
2. Wrapping the left short-leg portion of the cast first, then pulling traction through the fracture site
3. Placing the cast in an emergency department setting under sedation
4. Placing the cast within 24 hours of the injury

## Correct option

**2. Wrapping the left short-leg portion of the cast first, then pulling traction through the fracture site**

## Answer explanation

Spica casting remains the standard of care for diaphyseal femur fractures in this age group. Spica casting has been shown effective when the cast is placed by experienced personnel while the patient is relaxed, whether in a setting using sedation or general anesthesia. Immediate spica casting has not been associated with a higher rate of fracture shortening or other complications compared to traction and delayed casting. To avoid the potential for compartment syndrome in the leg, the body and upper-leg portions of the cast should be placed first, followed by the lower-leg portion after the initial portion of the cast has been molded and the material has hardened.
