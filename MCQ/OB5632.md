## Question

A 62-year-old woman is on follow-up with your office for recurrent plantar ulceration for 2 years that has been refractory to offloading and total-contact casting. She has a history of insulin-dependent diabetes mellitus. Examination reveals a 1.5cm ulcer with a pink base and undermined edges with no palpable bone. She is unable to detect a Semmes-Weinstein 5.07 monofilament on multiple sites on the plantar foot. A recent clinical photograph and radiograph are shown in Figures A and B. MRI reveals no evidence of osteomyelitis. In order to reduce the risk of ulcer recurrence, treatment should include which of the following?

## Options

1. Charcot restraint orthotic walker
2. Vacuum-assisted closure
3. Midfoot exostectomy and Achilles tendon lengthening
4. Midfoot arthrodesis
5. Subtalar arthrodesis

## Correct option

**3. Midfoot exostectomy and Achilles tendon lengthening**

## Answer explanation

This patient has Charcot neuroarthropathy secondary to longstanding diabetes mellitus (DM). To prevent ulcer recurrence, Achilles tendon lengthening, midfoot exostectomy, debridement and immobilization in a total-contact cast.

Charcot neuropathic arthropathy is associated with peripheral neuropathy arising from DM, alcohol abuse, heavy metal exposure, or idiopathic reasons. It is associated with fracture, nonunion, joint dislocation, foot deformity, skin ulcers and amputation.

*Hastings et al. reviewed the progression of disease in 15 patients with Charcot arthropathy and 19 with DM and peripheral neuropathy without Charcot arthropathy.* **They found** that Meary angle, cuboid height and calcaneal pitch and hindfoot-forefoot angle worsened in patients with Charcot arthropathy compared with contralateral, uninvolved feet and patients with DM neuropathy over 2 years. Medial column changes precede lateral column changes. **They recommend** more aggressive orthopedic intervention to prevent complications from deformity.

*Pinzur et al. reviewed 26 obese diabetic patients undergoing Charcot midfoot deformity correction with a ring fixator, Achilles tendon lengthening, excision of infected bone and antibiotics.* *At 1 year, 24 of 26 patients were able to ambulate, 1 died, and 1 had transtibial amputation.* **They concluded** that morbidly obese diabetic patients can achieve correction of midfoot deformity with a ring fixator.

*Myerson et al. reviewed neuropathic fractures of the foot and ankle.* While neuropathy occurs in 30-50% of diabetics, fracture only occurs in 1-2.5%. **They recommend** prolonged immobilization for most stable, minimally displaced fractures. If considerable manipulation is needed for reduction, **they recommend** ORIF with 10 weeks of postoperative non-weight bearing, followed by 2-4 months of weightbearing in a cast.

*Mueller et al. compared 31 patients treated with immobilization and total-contact casting with Achilles tendon lengthening (Hoke triple hemisection) and 33 patients with casting alone.* There was reduction in ulcer recurrence, increase in ankle dorsiflexion, and decrease in peak plantar pressures in the Achilles tendon lengthening group. **They recommend** Achilles tendon lengthening to reduce recurrence of neuropathic forefoot ulcers.

Figures A and B are a clinical photograph and lateral foot radiographs showing rocker bottom foot and Charcot arthropathy.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: CROW walkers do not reduce the risk of ulcer recurrence.
Answer 2: Wound VAC dressings do not reduce the risk of ulcer recurrence.
Answers 4 and 5: Midfoot and subtalar fusions in-situ do not reduce the risk of ulcer recurrence.
