## Question

Management of a hypertensive intra cranial bleed in a 55 years old patient with loss of gray white differentiation and infarct of middle cerebral artery with sudden onset left sided hemiplegia

## Options

1. Decompressive Craniectomy
2. Mannitol
3. Thrombolysis
4. DSA and thrombolysis

## Correct option

**1. Decompressive Craniectomy**

## Answer explanation

Ans: A
Explanation: -
Loss of gray white differentiation here is likely pointing to malignant infarct of MCA territory “with hemorrhagic transformation”. If this was just an infarct (with no haemorrhage) then DSA with thrombolysis is best answer; however, when there is hemorrhage and patient’s GCS deteriorates then Surgery is better offered i.e. Decompressive craniectomy.

• Intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH) i.e. bleeding into brain parenchyma accounts occurs mostly in hypertensives and accounts for 15-30 % of strokes. Hemorrhagic transformation of an ischemic infarct (the scenario in above question) is estimated to occur in 43%.
• In ICH, presentation differs from ischemic infarcts because it includes headache, vomiting and altered consciousness which are signs of raised ICT due to bleeding
• Hematoma volume correlates with morbidity and mortality
• Hematoma enlarges in at least 33% of cases within first 3 hours of onset
• Angiography is always recommended except in patients older than 45 years old with preexisting hypertension and hematoma in thalamus, putamen or posterior fossa.
• Hypertensive hemorrhage sites of predilection are: basal ganglia 50% ; thalamus 15% ; pons 10-15% ; cerebellum 10% ,; cerebral white matter 10- 20% and brainstem 1-6%. Most common location for deep hematoma is putamen and is due to rupture of lenticulostriate arteries.
• The component that is released by clot and presumed to be the most likely cause of surrounding delayed edema is Thrombin
• Indications for surgery:

- ICH with significant mass effect and midline shift
- ICH volume:10-30 cc
- Deteriorating GCS
