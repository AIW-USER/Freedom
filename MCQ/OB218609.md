## Question

An orthopedic resident scrubs a Scarf osteotomy for a hallux valgus correction with her attending surgeon. During the procedure, the attending discusses the importance of reducing the sesamoids while correcting the deformity. What is the significance of this during the procedure?

## Options

1. Improved great toe extensor strength
2. Improved sagittal correction of the first ray
3. Prevents recurrence of the hallux valgus deformity
4. Supination of the 1st metatarsal
5. Lower nonunion rates

## Correct option

**3. Prevents recurrence of the hallux valgus deformity**

## Answer explanation

Reduction of the sesamoids during hallux valgus correction prevents the recurrence of the deformity.

Hallux valgus is the valgus deviation of the great toe due to pronation and abduction of the first metatarsal. Patients may experience pain, especially with certain types of shoewear, and poor cosmesis. Surgical correction is directed at aligning the great toe and reducing the prominence of the bunion to allow for accommodation in normal footwear. The deformity is often associated with the lateral translation of the sesamoids, which are incorporated in the flexor hallucis brevis tendon. A scarf osteotomy is a double chevron diaphyseal osteotomy which is more stable than other osteotomies. At the time of osteotomy, an incomplete reduction of the sesamoids will permit a persistent valgus moment to the 1st MTP joint leading to the recurrence of the deformity.

*Raikin et al. reviewed the recurrence of hallux valgus.* **They stated** that it can be caused by a combination of patient-related factors such as pre-existing anatomic predisposition, medical comorbidities and non-compliance with post-surgery instructions, and surgical factors such as choice of procedure and technical competency. With regards to sesamoid reduction specifically, **they point out** that lateral displacement has been strongly associated with recurrence and close attention should be paid to appropriate bony and soft tissue correction.

*Bock et al. evaluated the long-term results of a Scarf osteotomy for hallux valgus deformity.* **They found** that the median overall AOFAS score and radiographic measurements improved significantly from pre-surgery to final follow-up. **They concluded** the recurrence rate of hallux valgus deformity at ten years is 30% with higher pain levels associated with failure to achieve adequate bony correction/sesamoid reduction.

Incorrect answers
Answer 1: Reduction of the sesamoids does not affect the extensor strength of the hallux.
Answer 2: Sagittal plane correction is not affected by the sesamoid station. Osteotomies and fixation are responsible for sagittal plane correction.
Answer 4: Pronation of the 1st ray is common with the hallux valgus and is affected by the osteotomy rather than the sesamoid station.
Answer 5: The union rate of the osteotomy is not affected by the sesamoid station.
