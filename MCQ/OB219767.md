## Question

A parent brings her newborn son into the office for evaluation of their upper extremity limb abnormality. The newborn is a one-month-old male who was diagnosed with radial longitudinal deficiency at birth. On physical examination the thumb, first web space, intrinsic and extrinsic thumb muscles are absent as are the proximal metacarpals, trapezium and scaphoid. Which of the following is the most likely description of this patient’s radius?

## Options

1. Normal proximal and distal radius
2. Radioulnar synostosis with a distal radius >2mm shorter than the ulna
3. Hypoplasia of the proximal and distal radius
4. Hypoplasia of the proximal radius and absence of the distal radius physis
5. Absence of the proximal and distal radius

## Correct option

**5. Absence of the proximal and distal radius**

## Answer explanation

The patient has severe thumb hypoplasia (Blauth and Schnelder-Sickert Type 5) in the setting of radial longitudinal deficiency. The severity of thumb dysplasia is the greatest predictor of radial longitudinal deficiency severity. Therefore, the patient is most likely to have a type 4 Bayne and Klug radial longitudinal deficiency characterized by the absence of the proximal and distal radius.

Radial longitudinal deficiency (RLD) is a condition characterized by deficiency of the radius and is most commonly the result of spontaneous genetic mutations. This condition is associated with several syndromes including thrombocytopenia with absent radius, Fanconi’s anemia, Holt-Oram syndrome, and VATER/VACTERL syndromes. RLD exists on a spectrum of severity and is classified according to the Bayne and Klug system. Type 0 is an isolated thumb absence or hypoplasia with a normal radius. For types 1 through 4, all types have absent or hypoplastic thumbs with: type 1 - >2mm shortening of the radius and either normal proximal radius/radioulnar synostosis/radial head dislocation, type 2 – hypoplasia of the proximal and distal radius, type 3 – absence of distal radius physis and hypoplasia of the proximal radius, and type 4 – absence of the proximal and distal radius. The greatest predictor of radial longitudinal deficiency is the degree of thumb hypoplasia with greater thumb hypoplasia correlating with greater radial hypoplasia.

*James et al. performed a retrospective review of 227 affected upper extremities in 139 patients with radial longitudinal deficiency.* **The authors found** 61% of patients to have bilateral involvement, 94% of patients with an absent radius had Blauth and Schneider type 4 (27%) or type 5 (67%) thumb deficiency versus 66% of patients with a normal radius having a Blauth and Schneider type 1, type 2, or type 3 thumb. Therefore, **the authors concluded** that the severity of thumb hypoplasia was a significant predictor of the degree of radial longitudinal deficiency.

*Forman et al.* *Performed a retrospective review of 383 affected limbs from 259 patients from the Congenital Upper Limb Differences registry.* **The authors found** syndromic subjects to be twice as likely to have bilateral deficiencies and 2.5 times more likely to have radial and thumb deficiencies than patients without a concomitant syndrome diagnosis, and the severity of thumb hypoplasia to be the most significant predictor of radial hypoplasia severity. Therefore, **the authors concluded** that there is a strong correlation between the severity of thumb deficiency and radial deficiency.

*Halverson et al. performed a retrospective review comparing two cohorts of patients with radial longitudinal deficiency: a194 Japanese patients with 290 involved extremities against 104 United States patients with 174 involved extremities.* **The authors found** higher rates of concomitant diagnosis of medical syndrome in Japanese patients, higher rates of bilateral RLD and greater severity of RLD in US patients, and that the severity of RLD was associated with increased thumb hypoplasia severity. Therefore, **the authors concluded** that US patients with RLD were more likely to have more severe RLD and be bilateral, and that the severity of RLD was increased with more severe thumb hypoplasia.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1-4: These descriptions are suggestive of Bayne and Klug Type 0, Type 1, Type 2 and Type 3 Radial Longitudinal Deficiency, respectively. This patient has Blauth and Schneider Type 5 thumb hypoplasia, and it is unlikely his RDL will be less severe.
