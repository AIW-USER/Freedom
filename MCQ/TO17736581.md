Chapter: FNB 2017 — 2017 MCQs

## Question

True regarding mallet finger is (trauma- fractures around wrist and hand- mallet finger)

## Options

1. Immobilization should involve both PIP and DIP joints
2. Bony mallet should be fixed at earliest
3. Chronic mallet finger (>6 weeks) is an indication for surgery
4. Extension splitting of DIP joint only is the treatment of choice

## Correct option

**4. Extension splitting of DIP joint only is the treatment of choice**

## Answer explanation

Mallet finger: Also known as “baseball finger” or “drop finger” is caused by injury to the extensor mechanism at the distal interphalangeal (DIP) joint. This can be due to soft tissue disruption of the tendon in zone 1 or to a bony avulsion from the distal phalanx. Ulnar three fingers are most commonly involved. Untreated mallet finger can lead to a swan neck deformity from PIP joint hyperextension and DIP joint flexion

Wehbe and Schneider classification is most commonly used to classify mallet finger.

Types

 1. No DIP joint subluxation

 2. DIP joint subluxation

 3. Epiphyseal and physeal injuries

Subtypes

 1. Less than 1/3 of articular surface involvement

 2. 1/3 to 2/3 of articular surface involvement

 3. More than 2/3 of articular surface involvement

Treatment: non-operative management (extension splitting) of mallet finger is indicated in cases of all soft tissue mallet and bony mallet which are well reduced in a splint without DIP joint subluxation. Immobilization of a DIP joint alone in extension or slight hyperextension is sufficient. PIP joint immobilization is not required. Extension splitting should be continued for 6 weeks followed by night splitting for another 2-6 weeks. Chronic mallet finger (>4 weeks) without fixed deformity and without fracture fragment involving > 30% of articular surface should also be given a trial of non-operative treatment with splitting. Some extensor lag may remain with splint treatment, but this does not result in functional deficit.

Indication for Surgery in mallet finger: it is indicated in all open injuries and in injuries with a large bony mallet fragment (<30% of articular surface) with subluxation of the DIP joint.

Answer: 4
