## Question

How a third generation ACI is different from first generation ACI

## Options

1. All arthroscopic procedure
2. two stage procedure
3. Use of a collagen cover
4. Use of a periosteal sheath

## Correct option

**1. All arthroscopic procedure**

## Answer explanation

First-generation ACI, originally reported by Brittberg et al. in 1994 was defined as a two-stage (first stage arthroscopic biopsy and second stage open or mini-open arthrotomy) procedure. Autologous chondrocytes were harvested first, then grown in culture, made into paste which was then implanted into the defect after 3-6 weeks beneath a “periosteal flap” (e.g. PACI) or a “collagen membrane” (e.g. MACI).

Second generation ACI was also a two-stage procedure (either arthroscopic biopsy first stage and open arthrotomy second stage or arthroscopic biopsy first stage and arthroscopic implantation second stage) with implantation of cultured chondrocytes via cell-seeded, three-dimensional, bioabsorbable scaffolds. The 3D SCAFFOLD was hammered press fit into the defect (e.g. hyalograft C, bioseed- C). Prior chondrocyte seeding and the ability for secure graft fixation without suture precludes an open or mini-open arthrotomy and thus allowed for all-arthroscopic implantation. Different bioabsorbable scaffolds used were: porcine collagen-derived membrane, hyaluronic acid-based scaffold, fibrin gel-polymer matrix, type I collagen gel matrix, chondrocyte-pre-seeded type I/III collagen membrane, chondrocyte-pre-seeded fibrin three-dimensional matrix gel, hydroxyapatite with interconnected pores scaffold, chondrocytes seeded on tissue fibrin glue, solid agarose-alginate matrix, biphasic chondroitin sulfate-collagen scaffold, type I collagen gel matrix.

Third generation ACI has a major advantage of being a single stage procedure. Freshly isolated autologous chondrocytes are combined with bone marrow MNCs, and minced into small fragments are encapsulated into a biodegradable load-bearing scaffold that is directly implanted into the defect. Chondrocytes from fragments migrate into scaffold (made of polycaprolactone + PGA + PDS) and produce ECM. A minimal number of chondrocytes can hence produce much of tissue to a relatively larger defect. The treatment requires only 1 surgical intervention and is potentially a cost-effective alternative to earlier generations. Classical example is CAIS- Cartilage Autograft Implantation System.
