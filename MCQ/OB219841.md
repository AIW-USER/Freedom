## Question

A 58-year-old female with left knee osteoarthritis is a candidate for total knee arthroplasty. At her preoperative appointment, you describe your institution's multimodal perioperative pain management protocol. One part of that protocol is an injection in the interspace between the popliteal artery and the capsule of the posterior knee (iPACK). Which of the following best describes the iPACK block?

## Options

1. Anesthetizes multiple distal branches of the femoral nerve
2. Is not effective in controlling pain
3. Does not require ultrasound
4. Motor sacrificing nerve block
5. When combined with adductor canal block (ACB), improved pain management compared to ACB alone

## Correct option

**5. When combined with adductor canal block (ACB), improved pain management compared to ACB alone**

## Answer explanation

The iPACK (interspace between the popliteal artery and the capsule of the posterior knee) block is an appealing motor-sparing injection to help control pain as a part of a multimodal perioperative pain management approach.

Patients who undergo total knee arthroplasty (TKA) have a large amount of postoperative pain and swelling. To reduce the amount of opioids required for pain management, several strategies have been validated. Typical perioperative multimodal pain management approaches include oral nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory medications (NSAIDs), acetaminophen, glucocorticoids, ice therapy, elevation, and injections. Injections may be delivered by the surgeon (e.g. intra-incisional pericapsular), or by the anesthesia team. Often, the anesthesiologist will use ultrasound guidance to deliver their injection. Multiple peripheral injections have been validated, to include the adductor canal block and the interspace between the popliteal artery and the capsule of the posterior knee (iPACK).

*Kertkiatkachorn et al. performed a randomized controlled trial comparing an adductor canal block Combined With iPACK (interspace between the popliteal artery and the capsule of the posterior knee) block vs periarticular injection after total knee arthroplasty.* **They found** that the ACB + iPACK group had greater postoperative morphine requirements at 48 hours. **They concluded** that patients who received ACB + iPACK block may require higher amounts of opioids and have worse immediate functional performance compared to those who received ACB + PAI.

*Sankineani et al. performed a prospective control trial comparing the adductor canal block and iPACK block with the adductor canal block alone after total knee arthroplasty.* **They found** that visual analog scale (VAS) score, range of motion, and ambulation distance all favored the ACB + IPACK group. **They concluded** that the ACB + IPACK is a promising technique that offers improved pain management in the immediate postoperative period without affecting the motor function around the knee joint.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: This describes the adductor canal block.
*Answer 2: The iPACK has been validated as improving perioperative pain, though as noted by Kertkiatkachorn et al., the iPACK may be inferior to a periarticular injection.*
Answer 3: The posterior knee has important neurovascular structures. Ultrasound guidance allows the practitioner to avoid these structures.
Answer 4: The iPACK is a motor-sparing block.
