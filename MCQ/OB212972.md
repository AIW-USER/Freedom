## Question

With regards to the most common pattern of triplane ankle fractures, which of the following is true?

## Options

1. The Salter-Harris II component is seen on the AP radiograph
2. The Salter-Harris III component is seen on the AP radiograph
3. The Salter-Harris IV component is seen on the AP radiograph
4. The Salter-Harris III component is seen on the lateral radiograph
5. The Salter-Harris IV component is seen on the lateral radiograph

## Correct option

**2. The Salter-Harris III component is seen on the AP radiograph**

## Answer explanation

The Salter-Harris III component of triplane ankle fractures is seen on the AP radiograph.

Triplane ankle fractures are complex fractures with components in all three planes (coronal, axial, and sagittal). On imaging, the Salter-Harris III component is seen on the AP view, with the fracture line exiting into the ankle joint, and the Salter-Harris II component is seen on the lateral view, with a posterior metaphyseal component. The overall injury pattern of triplane is Salter-Harris IV fracture.

*Spiegel et al. published a review on triplane fractures of the distal tibial epiphysis.* **They reported** that diagnosis of the particular anatomy of each fracture is ascertained by plain radiographs; however, if the fracture is displaced 2mm or more on any view, a CT scan should be obtained.

von Laer studied the anatomical relationships in 32 transitional fractures of the distal part of the tibia. They found that the epiphyseal (SH III) component was visible on the AP view and that its location is not influenced by the mechanism of injury but is instead exclusively dependent on the maturity of the distal tibial physis.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The Salter-Harris II component is seen on the lateral view, not the AP view.
Answer 3: There is no true Salter-Harris IV component in triplane ankle fractures.
Answer 4: The Salter-Harris III component of triplane ankle fractures is seen on the AP view, not the lateral view.
Answer 5: There is no true Salter-Harris IV component in triplane ankle fractures.
