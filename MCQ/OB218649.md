## Question

A 55-year-old female presents to your clinic with chronic foot pain. She states she has retired her dress shoes in exchange for orthotics for four months but still feels like she is "walking on marbles." On examination, she has a long second metatarsal, and mild joint pain at the second metatarsophalangeal joint with a fixed dorsiflexion deformity. Given her history and objective findings, what technique should be employed to avoid the most common complication with the preferred treatment for this patient?

## Options

1. Ensure collateral ligaments are protected during approach and procedure
2. Inject plantar to intermetatarsal ligament
3. Perform osteotomy parallel to plantar surface of the foot
4. Shorten second metatarsal at least 4-7mm
5. Strict postoperative immobilization for 4 weeks

## Correct option

**3. Perform osteotomy parallel to plantar surface of the foot**

## Answer explanation

This patient has second metatarsophalangeal joint (MTPJ) instability with a long second metatarsal and a fixed deformity, therefore a distal oblique shortening metatarsal osteotomy (Weil procedure) is indicated. The most common complication with this procedure is a floating toe deformity that can be prevented by performing the osteotomy parallel to the weight-bearing surface (Answer 3).

Instability of MTPJs can present in a range of deformities from mild instability to dislocation and chronic deformities. Treatment for patients that fail nonoperative management involves soft tissue procedures for flexible deformities and bony reconstruction for fixed deformities, as in this patient. For patients with a fixed dorsiflexion deformity and long second metatarsal, a distal oblique shortening osteotomy or Weil procedure is often indicated. Floating toe deformity is the most common complication of the Weil osteotomy. This phenomenon is suspected to be a result of altered biomechanics at the MTPJ. The floating toe occurs due to the plantar displacement of the center of rotation at the metatarsal head, forcing the pull of the intrinsics to move from plantar to dorsal of the center of rotation. When this occurs, the intrinsic muscles function as dorsiflexors at the MTPJ, creating the floating toe. Researchers have attempted solutions to combat this common issue. The most commonly employed method is to ensure the osteotomy cut is parallel to the plantar surface of the foot. This technique will minimize but not completely prevent plantar migration of the distal segment. Other solutions include taking a wafer of bone from the distal metatarsal to shorten the metatarsal and minimize plantar migration or combining soft tissue procedures with isolated osteotomies.

*Migues et al. performed a study of 70 Weil osteotomies from a single institution for the evaluation of floating toe deformities.* **The authors found** a high occurrence of floating toe deformities when a concurrent proximal interphalangeal (PIP) joint arthrodesis was performed (50%). **The authors found** a lower rate of floating toe deformities (15%) when PIP joint fusion was not performed. **The authors conclude** that PIP joint arthrodesis should be avoided with concurrent Weil osteotomies to reduce the occurrence of floating toe deformities.

*Lee et al. performed a cadaveric study to evaluate surgical techniques to minimize floating toe deformities in Weil osteotomies.* *The authors performed a flexor digitorum brevis (FDB) transfer to the PIP joint after Weil osteotomy and PIP joint arthrodesis.* *In the 14 cadavers,* **the authors found** the FDB transfer helped reduce the incidence of floating toe deformities.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Protecting the collateral ligaments during surgery of the MTPJ is important to prevent avascular necrosis (AVN). AVN is a complication of these procedures but it is much less common than a floating toe deformity.
Answer 2: Although patients can have symptoms that present similar to Morton's neuroma, this patient has symptoms and exam findings consistent with MTPJ instability. A steroid injection in this setting is contraindicated because the steroid may weaken the capsule, leading to worsening instability.
Answer 4: When performing a second metatarsal osteotomy, overshortening of the metatarsal can further displace the distal fragment plantar, leading to the floating toe deformity. Most authors recommend shortening the metatarsal no more than 3 mm.
Answer 5: Immobilization for 4 weeks after a Weil osteotomy is not recommended, as this can worsen postoperative stiffness. Most authors recommend protected weight bearing in a surgical shoe with an aggressive range of motion therapy. Furthermore, stiffness is not the most common complication with a Weil osteotomy.
