## Question

A 40-year-old ambulatory male presents with complaints of bone pain in his left hip. Plain radiographs of the pelvis are shown in Figure A. You suspect that the patient’s underlying condition is caused by abnormal activity of a cell type derived from the macrophage lineage. Which of the following laboratory findings is most likely to be present in this suspected condition?

![[q220970-a-paget.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Elevated phosphate levels
2. Low alkaline phosphatase
3. Low parathyroid hormone
4. Normal urinary hydroxyproline
5. Normal calcium levels

## Correct option

**5. Normal calcium levels**

## Answer explanation

Calcium levels are typically normal in ambulatory patients with Paget’s disease (Answer 5). Those who are immobilized (not present in this patient), may develop hypercalcemia secondary to increased osteocyte sclerostin secretion.

Paget’s disease is a disease of bone characterized by excessive bone resorption and subsequent abnormal bone formation. Though many patients are asymptomatic, those that do have symptoms present with bone pain and pathologic fractures due to poor bone quality. Imaging may demonstrate varying findings depending on the phase of disease but will frequently show blastic-appearing bone, cortical remodeling, bowing of the long bones, and osteoarthritis of the hip and knee. Laboratory findings in these patients are significant for elevated serum alkaline phosphatase (ALP) and urinary collagen cross links/hydroxyproline/N-telopeptide/alpha-C-telopeptide/deoxypyridinoline. Serum calcium and phosphate levels are typically normal. Medical treatment focuses on inhibiting the abnormal osteoclast activity with bisphosphonates or calcitonin.

*Vallet and Ralston published a review on Paget’s disease, covering its pathophysiology and treatment.* *They discuss the abnormal, unregulated activity of osteoclasts in these patients and the characteristic nuclear inclusions seen in pathologic osteoclasts, which may be the result of dysfunctional autophagy pathways.* *They also cover possible contributors to the disease, including environmental triggers (such as diet, toxins, and biomechanics) and genetic factors.* *They finally discuss the treatment of this condition and emphasize that there is no evidence demonstrating that anti-resorptive interventions can prevent disease complications, but that these modalities may still offer benefit with regard to patient pain.* **They conclude** that additional studies and research is needed to identify patients with Paget’s disease at an earlier stage to facilitate earlier intervention, especially in those with predisposing genetic mutations.

*Singer published a review on Paget’s disease, discussing the presenting symptoms and characteristic findings of patients with this condition.* *He reviews the skeletal/osseous deformities present in the skull, axial, and appendicular skeleton, radiographic findings, systemic manifestations including hyperuricemia, and cardiovascular dysfunction (secondary to a decrease in peripheral vascular disease, resulting in elevated cardiac output), medical treatment options, and finally surgical treatment to address osteoarthritis and long bone deformities.* **He concludes** that the etiology of this disease is still not adequately defined and requires additional research to assess all possible disease contributors.

*Shaker published a review on the epidemiology, pathophysiology, and treatment of Paget’s disease.* *He discusses the predominance of this disease in the United Kingdom and Western European population, the disease’s abnormal osteoclastic activity and the environmental/genetic factors contributing to this cellular dysfunction, and therapeutic interventions.* An in-depth discussion of abnormal laboratory findings is also provided and highlights that hypercalcemia may be present in the immobilized or dehydrated patient but may otherwise represent a coexisting pathologic process. **He concludes** that Paget’s disease is a complex disease with a broad range of systemic manifestations that requires clinicians to have a wide breadth of knowledge regarding their respective treatments.

Figure A is an AP of a pelvis with scattered areas of blastic/sclerotic bone, particularly in the left hemipelvis, suggestive of Paget's disease.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: Phosphate levels are typically normal in Paget’s disease.
Answer 2: Alkaline phosphatase levels are typically elevated in Paget’s disease as a result of the high bone turnover.
Answer 3: Parathyroid hormone levels are usually normal in patients with Paget’s disease but may become elevated in a small subset of patients who have particularly active disease.
Answer 4: Urinary hydroxyproline, a marker of collagen breakdown, is usually elevated due to the high bone turnover.
