## Answer explanation

All of the clinical images are physical exam finding associated with neurofibromatosis type 1 (NF1) EXCEPT Figure E, which demonstrates pectus excavatum, a feature typically seen in patients with Marfan's syndrome and not neurofibromatosis type 1.

Anterolateral tibial bowing can be associated with neurofibromatosis type 1 (NF1) about 55% of the time. This condition can progress to pseudoarthrosis of the tibia, a very difficult problem to treat. NF 1 may be associated with other physical exam findings such as Lisch nodules, scoliosis, axillary freckling, and cafe-au lait spots surrounding neurofibromas.

*Feldman et al. provide a thorough review of the diagnostic criteria for NF1, which include 2 or more of the following: >6 cafe-au-lait spots, >2 neurofibromas, axillary and/or inguinal freckling, optic glioma, >2 Lisch nodules, a distinctive osseous lesion (i.e. sphenoid dysplasia or thinning of long bone with or without pseudoarthrosis), or a first degree relative with NF1.* *The authors review the specific vertebral dystrophic anomalies noted in patients with NF1, especially short segment scoliosis with a rotational component.*

*Vander Have et al. review the specifics of congenital pseudoarthrosis of the tibia, typically associated with patients with NF1 and present with clinical anterolateral bowing.* Diagnosis and treatment strategies are discussed including intramedullary rodding, the importance and efficacy of bone grafting and the use of ring fixators for large segment defects.

*Stevenson et al. characterized radiographic features of tibial dysplasia in NF1.* **They demonstrated** that patients with NFI have radiographic signs of cortical thickening and canal narrowing at the apex of the anterolateral bowing of the tibia. Most patients also have fibular dysplasia.

Figure A exhibits tibial pseudoarthrosis associated with anterolateral bowing. Figure B exhibits a young male patient with clinical scoliosis. Figure C exhibits several Lisch nodules (green spots). Figure D exhibits axillary freckling. Figure E exhibits pectus excavatum. Figure F exhibits cafe-au-lait spots surrounding several neurofibromas.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1: Scoliosis (typically short segment, thoracic with a rotatory component), is associated with NF1.
Answer 2: Lisch nodules are associated with NF1.
Answer 3. Axillary freckling are associated with NF1.
Answer 5: Cafe-au-lait spots and neurofibromas are all associated with NF1.
