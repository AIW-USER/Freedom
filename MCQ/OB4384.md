## Question

A 22-year-old right-handed semi-professional baseball player complains of right shoulder pain and progressive weakness. He denies any traumatic events. His exam is noted to have a normal "empty can" and "belly press" tests. Resisted external rotation with the arm at the side does exhibit weakness. His MRI images are shown in figure A and B. What is the most likely cause of his symptoms?

## Options

1. Suprascapular nerve entrapment at the suprascapular notch
2. Suprascapular nerve entrapment at the spinoglenoid notch
3. Axillary nerve entrapment in the posterior triangle
4. Axillary nerve entrapment in the axillary pouch
5. Axillary nerve entrapment at near inferior neck of glenoid

## Correct option

**2. Suprascapular nerve entrapment at the spinoglenoid notch**

## Answer explanation

The clinical presentation is consistent with suprascapular nerve entrapment at the spinoglenoid notch from a ganglion cyst.

Suprascapular neuropathy can be caused by nerve compression in the suprascapular notch or spinoglenoid notch. Spinoglenoid notch compression from a ganglion cyst is most commonly seen in overhead athletes and can be seen in conjunction with SLAP or posterior labral tears. Compression of the suprascapular nerve at the spinoglenoid notch would only affect the innervation of the infraspinatus muscle, as the supraspinatus has already received its innervation.

*Piatt et al. studied spinoglenoid notch ganglion cysts.* *In their study,* **they found** spinoglenoid notch cysts by magnetic resonance imaging in 73 patients. Posterosuperior labral tears were identified in 65 patients who had spinoglenoid notch cysts. Patient symptoms included weakness with external rotation (43 patients), infraspinatus atrophy (25 patients), and posterior shoulder tenderness (30 patients).

*Chen et al. looked at the arthroscopic management of spinoglenoid cysts associated with SLAP lesions and suprascapular neuropathy.* *They describe 3 cases in which preoperative and postoperative EMG and MRI documented cyst resolution and return of suprascapular nerve function after arthroscopic spinoglenoid cyst excision and labral repair.*

Figures A and B shows T2 weighted axial and coronal MRI cuts showing a ganglion cyst in the spinoglenoid notch. Illustration A depicts the course of the suprascapular nerve through the suprascapular notch and spinoglenoid notch.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Compression at the suprascapular notch would affect both the supraspinatus and infraspinatus muscles.
Answer 3,4,5: Axillary nerve compression would only affect either the deltoid or teres minor muscles, neither of which of consistent with the above clinical scenario or MRI images.
