## Question

A 52-year-old male construction worker presents to your spine clinic with complaints of new-onset low back pain with radiation into the anteromedial distal thigh, adjacent to the knee. He states it began when he bent over to pick up a heavy toolbox. He denies having prior treatment for his lumbar spine. On exam, he has a positive straight leg raise and tenderness to palpation over his lumbar paraspinal muscles. He has 2+ patellar reflexes on the right and left. Given his symptoms what is the most likely diagnosis?

## Options

1. Far lateral L2-3 disc herniation
2. Paracentral L3-4 disc hernation
3. Far lateral L3-4 disc hernation
4. Paracentral L4-5 disc hernation
5. Far lateral L4-5 disc herniation

## Correct option

**3. Far lateral L3-4 disc hernation**

## Answer explanation

A far lateral disc herniation at L3-4 would cause radicular symptoms in the exiting L3 nerve distribution.

Lumbar disc herniations are one of the most common causes of low back pain and associated radicular pain. It is caused by tearing of the annulus which leads to herniation of the inner nucleus pulposis. Nerve root compression leads to radicular symptoms secondary to mechanical compression and local inflammatory responses. It is important to understand the relationship of the nerve roots with the adjacent discs in the lumbar spine to correlate imaging and physical examination findings. In the lumbar spine, nerve roots exit below their associated disc (i.e. L3 root exits between L3-4). The exiting nerve root is affected when there is a far lateral disc herniation that causes compression in the foramen. With paracentral herniations, the traversing nerve root (L4 at L3-4) is typically affected.

*Schoenfeld and Weiner looked at the evidence-based treatment of lumbar disc herniations.* **They noted** that six weeks of conservative care were recommended but if this fails, surgical intervention may be undertaken. **They concluded** that surgical intervention resulted in more rapid relief of symptoms and restoration of function even though many patients will eventually improve with nonoperative management.

*Amin et al. reviewed lumbar disc herniation including current concepts and future directions.* **They note** that minimally invasive surgical approaches continue to improve and demonstrate good mid-term outcomes and they expect these techniques to continue to evolve. They also discuss advances in molecular research concerning disc herniation which explores the roles of inflammatory and infectious alterations in the process.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: A far lateral disc herniation at L2-3 would cause symptoms in the exiting L2 nerve root distribution which would affect a more proximal thigh (groin) dermatome.
Answer 2: A paracentral disc herniation at L3-4 would affect the L4 nerve root distribution which would cause sensory symptoms more distally over the anterior and medial aspects of the leg as well as weakness of the tibialis anterior.
Answer 4: A paracentral disc herniation at L4-5 would affect the L5 nerve root distribution which would cause sensory symptoms over the dorsum of the foot and weakness of the EHL.
Answer 5: A far lateral disc herniation at L4-5 would affect the exiting L4 nerve root distribution as detailed above.
