## Question

A 15-year-old football wide-receiver went up for a catch and sustained a fall directly onto his head upon landing. He was able to get up and run for a touchdown. While on the sidelines he was noted to have headaches and light-sensitivity and as such was removed from the game. After 1 week of rest, his headaches have finally resolved and he is free of any other concussion-related signs or symptoms. Which of the following is the correct sequence of activities the patient must complete without return of symptoms prior to returning to a football game?

## Options

1. Riding a stationary bicycle, running and catching a football, running on a track
2. Running on a track, riding a stationary bicycle, running and catching a football
3. Performing squats and leg presses, riding a stationary bicycle, running and catching a football
4. Riding a stationary bicycle, performing squats and leg presses, running on a track
5. Riding a stationary bicycle, running on a track, running and catching a football

## Correct option

**5. Riding a stationary bicycle, running on a track, running and catching a football**

## Answer explanation

The patient has sustained a concussion and has cleared the first step in the return to play (RTP) protocol which is to be asymptomatic with day-to-day activities. The next steps in the RTP protocol are light aerobic activities (i.e riding a stationary bicycle), followed by sport-specific exercise (i.e running on a track) followed by non-contact training skills/strengthening (running and catching a football, and strength training), followed by full-contact practice. Each stage of progression should be at least 24 hours before progressing to the next stage.

The RTP protocol has been initiated to ensure athletes who have sustained a concussion are completely asymptomatic prior to returning to game action. The protocol involves 5 steps with each step requiring a minimum of 24 hours to complete. Step 1 is to be completely asymptomatic during the course of a day without any sporting activity. Step 2 is performing very light aerobic exercises, such as riding a stationary bicycle, walking or swimming. Step 3 involves performing sport-specific exercises; this includes running for sports such as basketball, soccer, and football or ice skating for hockey players. Step 4 is actual non-contact training drills that are sport-specific; this is the step when strength and resistance training may also begin. And lastly, step 5 is a full-contact practice. If an athlete has symptoms at any point during a specific step, then he/she must return to the prior step and complete another 24 hours of asymptomatic activity before returning to the step in which symptoms occurred.

*Brown et al. performed a prospective cohort study of 335 patients who presented to their clinic within 3 weeks of a documented concussion to determine factors associated with the duration of symptoms.* **The authors noted** that 19% reported a loss of consciousness at the time of injury and 37% reported experiencing amnesia at the time of injury. The mean Post-Concussion Symptom Scale score at the initial visit was 30 (SD, 26). The overall mean duration of symptoms was 43 days. Of all variables assessed, only total symptom burden at the initial visit and cognitive activity level were independently associated with the duration of symptoms. **The authors concluded** that increased cognitive activity is associated with longer recovery from concussion.

*Majerske et al. performed a retrospective study of neurocognitive performance and symptom reporting in 95 student-athletes with a documented concussion.* *The authors created a regression analysis for repeated measures to evaluate the relationship of activity intensity to symptoms and neurocognitive outcomes up to 33 days after a concussion.* **The authors concluded** that activity level after concussion affected symptoms and neurocognitive recovery and further found that athletes engaging in high levels of activity after concussion demonstrated worse neurocognitive performance.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: "Running on a track" is an example of a sport-specific exercise (Step 3) and should be performed and completed asymptomatically PRIOR to "running and catching a football" which would be performed in Step 4.
Answer 2: "Running on a track" is an example of a sport-specific exercise (Step 3) that should be performed AFTER riding a stationary bicycle (Step 2).
Answer 3: "Squats and leg presses" is an example of strength training and should not be performed prior to Step 4. "Riding a stationary bicycle" (Stage 2) should be performed successfully and asymptomatically prior to strength training.
Answer 4: "Running on a track" is an example of a sport-specific exercise (Step 3) and should be performed and completed asymptomatically prior to any strengthening/resistance exercises which would be allowed in Step 4.
