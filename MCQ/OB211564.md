## Question

When considering lumbar osteotomies and other procedures for the correction of sagittal deformity, which of the following is correctly paired with the average expected correction per level?

## Options

1. Posterior column osteotomy; 15-20 degrees
2. Pedicle subtraction osteotomy; 25-35 degrees
3. Vertebral column osteotomy; 35-40 degrees
4. Transforaminal lumbar interbody fusion (TLIF) without anterior column release; 15-20 degrees
5. Transforaminal lumbar interbody fusion (TLIF) with anterior column release; 25-30 degrees

## Correct option

**2. Pedicle subtraction osteotomy; 25-35 degrees**

## Answer explanation

A pedicle subtraction osteotomy should provide 25-35 degrees of correction per level.

Historically, the focus of spinal deformity correction has been in the coronal plane. However, multiple studies have demonstrated that sagittal deformity contributes more significantly to negative patient-reported outcomes. The goals of surgery to achieve "spinopelvic harmony" include: Sagittal vertical axis <50 mm, pelvic tilt <20 degrees, and lumbar lordosis within 10 degrees of pelvic incidence. There are several ways to achieve this depending on the flexibility of the spine utilizing posterior column osteotomies, three-column osteotomies, anterior releases, and lordotic interbody cages.

*Wang et al. performed a retrospective study to evaluate the efficacy and safety of posterior-only vertebral column resection for the treatment of angular and isolated congenital kyphosis.* *Pre- and post-operative radiographic measurements were obtained in 24 patients to evaluate global sagittal alignment.* **They concluded** that posterior-only vertebral column resection is an ideal procedure for severe rigid congenital kyphosis, resulting in a substantial radiographic correction.

*Turner et al. performed a multicenter analysis looking at radiographic outcomes of anterior column realignment (ACR) for adult sagittal plane deformity.* ACR utilized a lateral transpsoas approach with anterior longitudinal ligament release and an interbody cage to achieve correction. **They conclude** that this procedure successfully restores lumbar lordosis more than lateral lumbar interbody fusion, providing even greater deformity correction when supplemented with posterior column osteotomies.

Akbarnia similarly looked at ACR for focal kyphotic deformity correction using a lateral transpsoas approach and ALL release. They retrospectively reviewed a series of 17 patients who underwent ACR and evaluated pre- and post-operative clinical and radiographic parameters. They concluded that, compared with poster-based techniques, ACR did not show additional benefit.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Posterior column osteotomies typically provide between 3-5 degrees of correction per level.
Answer 3: A VCR can provide significant deformity correction which may be limited only by the ability of the neurologic elements to accommodate stretching; some series have reported an average correction of 62 degrees in the coronal plane and 45 degrees in the sagittal plane.
Answer 4: A TLIF typically provides 8 degrees of correction per level.
Answer 5: A TLIF combined with ALL release and a hyperlordotic cage typically provides 10-15 degrees of correction; the addition of posterior column osteotomies can increase this to 22 degrees.
