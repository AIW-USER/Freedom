## Options

1. Plate fixation
2. Closed reduction
3. Open reduction
4. Pin fixation
5. Long arm splinting

## Correct option

**2. Closed reduction**

## Answer explanation

The patient has a Monteggia forearm fracture-dislocation. Prompt closed reduction with proper ulnar alignment and reduction of the radial head is required.

Often occurring due a fall onto a outstretched hand, Monteggia fractures are characterized by an ulnar fracture and ligamentous failure on the radial side and subsequent radial head dislocation. These fractures were originally classified from I-IV by Bado based on direction. Type I fractures involve anterior angulation of the ulnar with anterior dislocation of the radius and are most common in pediatrics, while type II fractures are posteriorly dislocated and more common in adults. Early reduction by using longitudinal traction, supination and flexion to 90 degrees is usually sufficient to obtain a stable reduction. Delayed diagnosis and treatment will make reduction difficult or impossible and necessitate avoidable operative intervention.

*Ring et al. reviewed Monteggia fractures among pediatric populations.* Ulnar fracture pattern may dictate treatment, with plastic deformation and incomplete fractures beingmost amenable to closed reduction and casting, while complete transverse or short oblique fractures can be amenable to closed treatment but may require internal fixation for stability. Long oblique or comminuted fractures are unstable patterns and require operative treatment.

Figures A and B are radiographs of a Bado type I Monteggia fracture with subtle anterior dislocation of the radial head.
Illustration A shows post-reduction radiographs of this injury. Restoration of the ulnar alignment usually facilitates reduction of the radial head. Illustration B shows typical post-op radiograph after intramedullary nailing for an unstable Monteggia fracture
