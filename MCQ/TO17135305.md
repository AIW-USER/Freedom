Chapter: Spine MCQs — Spinal Cord Injury

## Question

A patient has central cord syndrome after injury. Xray shows spondylosis. What is the mechanism of injury? (FNB Pattern Paper 2020)

## Options

1. Axial compression
2. Hyperflexion
3. Hyperextension
4. Rotational injury

## Correct option

**3. Hyperextension**

## Answer explanation

Central cord syndrome is the most common of spinal cord syndromes. It consists of injury to the central area of the spinal cord, including gray and white matter.

The centrally located upper extremity motor neurons in the corticospinal tracts are the most severely affected, and the lower extremity tracts are affected to a lesser extent. Generally, patients have a tetra paresis involving the upper extremities to a greater degree than the lower extremities with greater dysfunction distally in the extremities than proximally. Sensory sparing varies, but usually sacral pinprick sensation is preserved. These patients frequently show early partial recovery and may have preexisting cord compression and may not have spinal instability. Prognosis varies, but more than 50% of patients have return of bowel and bladder control, become ambulatory, and have improved hand function This syndrome usually results from a hyperextension injury in an older individual with pre-existing osteoarthritis of the spine. The spinal cord is pinched between the vertebral body anteriorly and the buckling ligamentum flavum and lamina posteriorly. It also may occur in younger patients with flexion injuries.

Answer: 3
