## Question

A 34-year-old laborer had his left foot crushed in a piece of farming equipment and sustained multiple midfoot fractures and a significant soft tissue defect. The patient undergoes limb salvage with skeletal stabilization and soft tissue reconstruction and presents to your clinic 2 years after the injury. On examination, the patient's left foot has well-healed incisions but is hyperemic and has hair loss compared to the right foot. The foot is stiff, swollen, and the patient complains of persistent diffuse foot pain throughout the course of his recovery. His ESR and CRP are normal. All of the following are risk factors for this patient's constellation of symptoms EXCEPT?

## Options

1. Smoking
2. Depression
3. Prolonged immobilization
4. Multiple surgical procedures
5. Male gender

## Correct option

**5. Male gender**

## Answer explanation

This patient has undergone reconstruction of a mangled extremity and has developed post-operative complex regional pain syndrome (CRPS). Females are more commonly affected by CRPS (4:1 ratio to males).

Crush injuries to the foot are associated with several complications including soft tissue defects requiring reconstructions, delayed union of bony fractures, osteoarthritis, chronic neuritis, and CRPS. CRPS is a sustained sympathetic response in a perpetuated reflex arc characterized by pain out of proportion to physical exam findings. Neuroischemia following substantial soft-tissue injury likely plays a role in the development of CPRS after crush injuries to the foot, either through direct trauma to the peripheral nerves or by intraneural or extraneural fibrosis. This subsequently leads to an aberrant inflammatory response and vasomotor dysfunction of the area. F

*Allen et al. performed an epidemiological study to evaluate for various demographic variables in 134 patients with CRPS.* **The authors noted** that 17% of the patients had a lawsuit and 54% had a worker compensation claim related to the CRPS. **They also noted** that 47% had a history of physician-imposed immobilization, and 56% had a myofascial component present at evaluation. **The authors concluded** that most CRPS patients are referred to a pain specialty clinic after several years of symptoms and many failed therapies.

*Myerson et al. performed a retrospective review of 58 patients with crush injuries at a mean interval of 3.3 years after injury.* **They noted** that 46% of the patients had good functional outcome, 29% had fair results, and 25% had poor results. Poor results were associated with neuritis or CRPS or if patients were involved in ongoing workers' compensation and litigation. **The authors conclude** that because crush injuries of the foot may be associated with prolonged morbidity and CPRS, initial management should be directed toward recognition and treatment of compartment syndromes, early soft-tissue coverage, and rigid skeletal stabilization to enhance soft-tissue healing.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1-4: Smoking, depression, prolonged immobilization, and multiple surgical procedures are all risk factors for the development of CPRS.
