## Question

An American orthopedic surgeon meets with an attorney because a malpractice claim has been filed against him. What procedure did the patient filing the malpractice claim most likely undergo?

## Options

1. Total hip arthroplasty
2. Ankle arthroscopy
3. Meniscal root repair
4. Posterior lumbar decompression and fusion
5. Open reduction with internal fixation of a diaphyseal femur fracture

## Correct option

**1. Total hip arthroplasty**

## Answer explanation

Arthroplasty surgeons are the most likely to face malpractice claims among all subspecialized orthopedic surgeons (Answer 1). The majority of cases are ruled in favor of the surgeon.

A previous study of malpractice claims on orthopedic surgeons reveals that osteoarthritis was the most common diagnosis associated with these suits (21% of all suits). A recent study found 29.9% of malpractice claims to be related to hip and knee arthroplasty, followed by spine surgery (22%), and trauma surgery (17.7%). The trend of increased arthroplasty-related lawsuits over the last decade may reflect the aging of the U.S. population as baby boomers enter their sixth decade of life. Fasciotomy-related lawsuits have the highest rate of successful malpractice claims (48% success rate), followed by lawsuits related to musculoskeletal infections (43%).

*Sauder et al. reviewed 164 malpractice claims against orthopedists from 2015 to 2020.* **They found** the most common cases were hip and knee surgery, followed by spine, trauma, hand, sports, foot and ankle, pediatrics, and shoulder elbow, respectively. Additionally, **they found** that 77.6% of cases were ruled in favor of the defense. When the ruling favored the plaintiff, the mean award for the plaintiffs exceeded $4.5 million. **They concluded** that hip and knee surgeons are the most likely to face malpractice claims, with nerve injury and infection significantly increasing the risk of a lawsuit.

*Wimmer et al. reviewed medical litigation from 2000 to 2017.* **They found** the main reasons for malpractice claims against orthopedists were inappropriate management (46.1%), misdiagnosis (22.6%), and poor nursing care (8.3%), with an overall decrease in litigation per 1000 cases filed in the later half of the study. Therefore, **they concluded** that although the absolute number of litigation cases increased, the cases per 1000 patients decreased, and patients were more likely to file suit after undergoing elective surgery rather than emergent surgery.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 2 through 5 involve lawsuits less frequently than total hip and total knee arthroplasty.
