## Question

Figures A and B are the AP and lateral radiographs of a 7-year-old girl who presents with elbow pain after a fall on outstretched hand. On examination her elbow is swollen and has a notable deformity. She has palpable radial pulses and her neurological exam is within normal limits except she can not flex the interphalangeal joint of her thumb. This finding is due to injury to what structure?

## Options

1. Ulnar nerve
2. Anterior interosseous nerve
3. Posterior interosseous nerve
4. Radial nerve
5. Recurrent branch of the median nerve

## Correct option

**2. Anterior interosseous nerve**

## Answer explanation

An inability to flex the interphalangeal (IP) joint of the thumb is consistent with an injury to the anterior interosseous nerve (AIN).

The images in Figures A and B represent a Gartland type III supracondylar humerus (SCH) fracture. The most common nerve injury seen in this fracture pattern is a neuropraxia of the AIN. This nerve injury would manifest as weakness or the inability to flex the IP joint of the thumb, or distal interphalangeal (DIP) joints of the index and long fingers. This finding,commonly known the "OK" sign, manifests as a triangulation of the typical O seen when making the OK sign. This nerve deficit typically resolves over time with observation after reduction and fixation of the fracture.

Otsuka and Kasser present a review on SCH fractures with a focus on treatment of type II and type III fractures. They note that closed reduction and percutaneous pinning is frequently sufficient, but note that in the presence of neurologic deficits or vascular compromise that open exploration may be required.

*Babal et al. present a meta-analysis of nerve injuries encountered following SCH fractures.* **They note** that AIN injury is significantly more common than other nerve injuries. In the setting of a flexion-type SCH fracture, an ulnar nerve palsy is more common.

Figures A and B are AP and lateral radiographs of a Gartland type III SCH fracture.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: An ulnar nerve palsy would present with an inability to abduct and adduct the fingers. This is more common with flexion type SCH fractures.
Answer 3: A PIN palsy would present with an inability to extend the index finger.
Answer 4: A radial nerve palsy would present with inability to extend the wrist.
Answer 5: A deficit of the recurrent branch of the median nerve would present with weakness of thumb opposition.
