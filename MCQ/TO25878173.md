## Question

The Kozinn and Scott criteria (1989) were historically used to select patients for UKA. According to modern evidence and the 'Oxford' criteria, which of the following traditional contraindications is no longer considered a barrier to successful UKA?

## Options

1. Lateral compartment cartilage loss.
2. High Body Mass Index (BMI).
3. Fixed flexion deformity ![[TO25878173-2.png]] .
4. Inflammatory arthritis.

## Correct option

**2. High Body Mass Index (BMI).**

## Answer explanation

Multiple modern studies have shown that
high BMI (e.g.,
![[TO25878173-1.png]]
) does not
negatively impact the mid-to-long-term survival of modern UKA components,
contradicting the original strict limits set by Kozinn and Scott.

Evolving Criteria: Kozinn & Scott
vs. Oxford

Historically, patient selection was
demographic. Today, the philosophy has shifted entirely to pathoanatomy. This
contrast is heavily tested.

 Criteria

 Kozinn & Scott (1989) - Fixed
 Bearing

 The Oxford Criteria - Mobile Bearing

 Age & Weight

 > 60 years, < 82 kg (180 lbs)

 No limits.
 (Age, weight, and activity level are irrelevant).

 Activity Level

 Low demand / sedentary only

 No restrictions.

 Deformity

 Varus < 10°, Valgus < 5°

 Varus must be intra-articular and
 manually correctable to neutral.

 Flexion arc

 Flexion > 90°, Contracture < 5°

 Flexion > 90°, Contracture < 15°

 Ligaments

 Intact ACL required

 Intact ACL and competent MCL required.

 Patellofemoral Joint

 No PFJ arthritis allowed

 PFJ arthritis is ignored, unless
 there is severe lateral facet grooving with bone loss and anterior pain.

 Lateral Compartment

 Must be pristine

 Must have full-thickness cartilage
 (confirmed by valgus stress view).

Answer: 2
