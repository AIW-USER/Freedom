## Question

You form a study group to test a recent hypothesis that daily Vitamin D consumption is protective against hip fractures at a young age. You identify a group of patients who consumed Vitamin D daily for 10 years and compare them to another group who did not. You then follow these groups over time to assess the incidence of hip fractures before age 60 in each group. What type of study is this?

## Options

1. Cross-sectional
2. Case-control
3. Prospective randomized control
4. Cohort
5. Case series

## Correct option

**4. Cohort**

## Answer explanation

This example was a retrospective cohort study assessing daily vitamin D consumption as a protective factor against the development of hip fractures at a young age (Answer 4).

Cohort studies involve retrospectively following study groups (cohorts) who are selected non-randomly and observing how exposures (such as daily vitamin D consumption) or risk factors influence outcomes (such as young hip fractures). Relative risk (RR) can be calculated from cohort studies with the formula: [a/(a+b)]/[c(c+d)]. When RR is greater than 1, the incidence of the outcome is greater in the exposed/treatment group.

*Belbasis et al. reviewed the basic epidemiological study designs, including case-control, cross-sectional, and cohort studies.* **They report** that researchers develop cohort studies by identifying participants based on exposure and following these participants to measure outcomes. **They conclude** that epidemiological studies inherently introduce selection bias, information bias, and confounding.

*Euser et al. reviewed cohort studies and compared prospective versus retrospective designs.* **They report** that the advantage of cohort study designs is that they can link associations between multiple risks/exposures and multiple outcomes. They are useful when studying rare exposures and finding their association with various outcomes. **They conclude** that if researchers actively work to minimize confounding and bias, cohort studies can produce meaningful, generalizable results.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Cross-sectional studies are observational studies that analyze data from a population at a single point in time.
Answer 2: Case-control studies are developed by separating groups on the presence or absence of the outcome (hip fracture) and then evaluating for an associated risk or exposure leading to this outcome.
Answer 3: Prospective randomized controlled study designs assess the effectiveness of a new intervention by randomly dividing participants into a treatment or control group and following along with them prospectively.
Answer 5: A case series is an observational study design used to describe the characteristics of patients with a similar condition, but it does not have a comparison group.
