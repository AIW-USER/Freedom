## Question

Which of the following is the least common physical finding in myelopathic patients?

## Options

1. Babinski sign
2. Hoffman sign
3. Biceps Hyperreflexia
4. Patellar Hyperreflexia
5. Ataxic gait

## Correct option

**1. Babinski sign**

## Answer explanation

Of the physical exam findings listed, the Babinski sign is the least commonly seen in cervical myelopathy (CM).

Cervical myelopathy can be defined as the presence of greater than one long-tract sign localizing to the spinal cord and the absence of other neurologic conditions. These signs include Hoffman's sign, Babinski's sign, clonus, hyperreflexia, crossed adductor sign, and gait dysfunction. The Hoffman sign has been found to be the most common pathologic reflex, occurring in 80% of myelopathic patients, and it is more common with increasing severity of the CM. The prevalence of hyperreflexia has been shown to be no different from the prevalence in individuals without myelopathy. Sustained clonus and Babinski signs have been shown to occur in only one third or fewer of the patients with myelopathy. The most common physical finding in CM is gait ataxia.

*Harrop et al. studied the clinicopathological correlations in cervical spondylotic myelopathy.* **They identified** gait abnormality as the most common physical sign (91%). The Babinski sign was the least common (44%). Lower-extremity hyperreflexia was more common than upper extremity (81% vs 67%). Patients >60 years of age were more likely to demonstrate at least 4 of these outward signs of myelopathy.

*Rhee et al. evaluated the diagnostic utility of common physical exam findings in cervical myelopathy.* *13% of patients with CM demonstrated an upgoing (abnormal) Babinski reflex, and 13% demonstrated sustained clonus.* Contrarily, 59% of patients with CM demonstrated a positive Hoffman reflex, and 51% demonstrated an inverted brachioradialis reflex. Importantly, 21% of patients of patients with myelopathic symptoms (upper extremity clumsiness, gait abnormalities) and imaging demonstrating cord compression had no myelopathic exam signs, though they did have symptomatic improvement following decompression.

*Sung et al.* **determined** the clinical relevance of a positive Hoffman's reflex in otherwise asymptomatic patients. **They found** that 100% of asymptomatic patients with a positive Hoffman's reflex demonstrated cervical or thoracic cord compression on MRI. However, none of these patients underwent surgery, and the present of a positive Hoffman's reflex did not appear to alter the clinical decision making or patient outcome.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Hoffman's sign is seen with a prevalence of 59%.
Answers 3, 4: Hyperreflexia is seen with a prevalence of 81% in the lower and 67% in the upper extremity.
Answer 5: Ataxic gait is the most common physical exam finding noted in CM with a prevalence of 91%.
