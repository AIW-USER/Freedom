## Question

What is the most common pattern of forearm deformity in the disease shown in X ray?

![[TO17143401-1.jpg]]

## Options

1. Short ulna with radius head dislocation
2. Short radius with ulnar bowing
3. Short ulna with bowing of radius without head dislocation
4. Radial shortening with ulna dislocation

## Correct option

**3. Short ulna with bowing of radius without head dislocation**

## Answer explanation

The X-Ray is a case of Multiple osteochondromatosis of FA (sometimes also referred to as Masada Syndrome). It is characterized by defective metaphyseal remodeling and asymmetrical retardation of longitudinal bone growth.

The FA deformity is popularly classified into three types by Masada classification.

Shown in X ray is Type 2 (Short ulna with Radial head dislocation) but examiner has asked most common type in the classification which is Type 1 actually (Short ulna, bowed radius but without dislocation of radial head).

The Masada classification for forearm deformity in multiple osteochondromatosis

Type I The main osteochondroma formation is located in the distal portion of the ulna. The ulna is shortened, and there is bowing of the radius. However, the radial head is not dislocated (this is the most common type in 55–61 % of cases).

Type II In addition to ulnar shortening, the radial head is dislocated (22–33 % of patients). In subtype IIA, the radial head is dislocated because of an additional osteochondroma at the proximal metaphysis of the radius. In subtype IIB, whilst there are osteochondromas at the distal ulna, there are none detectable in this region.

Type III The main osteochondroma formation is in the metaphysis of the distal radius, and there is relative shortening of the radius.

Treatment Recommendation:

- Masada type I deformity: Patients are usually counselled for conservative treatment for deformity. In patients with large osteochondromas which are painful despite minimal functional impairment, elective removal of the symptomatic osteochondroma is recommended as they may give rise to gradual erosion and deformity of the adjacent bone and limit rotation.
- for Masada type II, are counselled to wait till 13 years age. if functional impairment as well as instability and pain is still present, plan a 2-stage procedure (first levelling of the forearm bones and, later, a radial head resection—rarely) or a single stage complex correction with indirect reduction of the radial head using a ring fixator. In patients with Masada type 114 deformity, removal of the proximal osteochondroma (as they are painful) and, in type IIB, removal of the ulnar osteochondroma is recommended.
- In patients with type III deformity, offer to remove the painful osteochondroma but counsel most patients to wait until growth is completed. Some patients in this group need treatment for their positive ulnar variance, usually by ulnar shortening osteotomy.

Answer: 3
