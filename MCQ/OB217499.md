## Question

A 63-year-old female presents with a chronic draining sinus after undergoing a right total knee arthroplasty at an outside facility three years ago. She has a history of rheumatoid arthritis and diabetes mellitus type 2. During the discussion of her condition, the treating physician states she requires a two-stage revision to treat her infection. Which of the following makes antibiotic treatment alone unsuccessful in this scenario?

## Options

1. Antiobiotic resistant organisms
2. The presence of bacterial biofilm prevents eradication with antibiotics alone
3. Polymicrobial infection
4. Presence of diabetes
5. Bacteria in a planktonic phase

## Correct option

**2. The presence of bacterial biofilm prevents eradication with antibiotics alone**

## Answer explanation

The presence of a biofilm is associated with increased antibiotic resistance requiring a two-staged revision.

Chronic periprosthetic joint infections are classified by the presence of the infection >6 weeks. At this phase, planktonic (free-floating) bacteria adhere to the implant surface and create an extracellular matrix called the glycocalyx. This prevents antibiotic diffusion to the sessile bacteria in the matrix and also functions to modulate the host immune response. Antibiotic treatment alone would be unsuccessful due to the presence of the glycocalyx.

*Mooney et al. reviewed bacterial biofilm formation and quorum sensing in periprosthetic joint infections.* **They reported** antimicrobial resistance in periprosthetic infections is mediated by extracellular matrix (glycocalyx) that is adherent to implant surfaces and prevents diffusion of antibiotics to bacteria in the sessile stage. **They stated** formation of biofilms is reliant on quorum sensing, which may be a future target to prevent or disrupt biofilm formation.

*Gries et al. reviewed staphylococcal biofilm formation and immune polarization in periprosthetic joint infections.* **They stated** biofilms produce mediators that actively suppress the immune response as evidenced by macrophage polarization to anti-inflammatory phenotypes. **They concluded** that further understanding of the cross-talk between biofilms and the immune system is imperative for determining future periprosthetic joint infection treatments.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: Resistance to organisms can make antibiotic treatment more difficult, but the selection of appropriate antibiotics can overcome this.
Answer 3: Polymicrobial infections are associated with a poor prognosis, but combination antibiotic treatment can provide sufficient coverage.
Answer 4: Diabetes is associated with a poor prognosis in periprosthetic joint infections, but this alone will not deter the success of antibiotic treatment alone.
Answer 5: Bacteria in the sessile phase are more resistant to antibiotics than in the planktonic phase.
