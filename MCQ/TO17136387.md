## Question

Approximate daily calcium requirement in a high-performance athlete as a prophylaxis to prevent stress fractures

## Options

1. 500 mg/ day
2. 1000 mg/ day
3. 1500 mg/ day
4. 2000 mg/ day

## Correct option

**4. 2000 mg/ day**

## Answer explanation

Stress Fractures are the result of excessive stress on the bone due to a prolonged and repetitive loading. The prevalence of stress fractures is estimated to be between 6.5–9.7% among athletes of different sport disciplines. The metatarsals and the tibia are the most frequently affected locations.

Risk factors of stress fractures in athletes: Female sex, age over 40and under 18, and anatomical factors like high foot arches, leg length discrepancy, flat feet etc.

Vitamin D (25(OH)D) has a central role in the development of stress fractures. 25(OH)D is a fat-soluble vitamin and was first discovered in cod liver oil. 25(OH)D is synthesized either from diet (fortified dairy products or fish oils) or through ultraviolet irradiation of 7-dehydrocholesterol in the skin. As soon as it is converted into its biologically active form 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D it regulates the expression of over 900 gene variants. Normal blood level of Vitamin D is ≥30 ng/mL.

For stress Fractures a daily calcium and vitamin D supplement which provides 2000mg of calcium and 800 IU of vitamin D, representing 200% and 150% of daily recommended intake for each, respectively. When 25(OH)D levels are high, body fat is reduced and muscle mass is increased.

A 25(OH)D value of <75.8 nmol/L is a risk factor for a stress fracture. Prevalence of stress fractures decreased when athletes are supplemented daily with 800 IU 25(OH)D and 2000 mg calcium. Recommendations of daily 25(OH)D intake may go up to 2000 IU of 25(OH)D per day.

Answer: 4
