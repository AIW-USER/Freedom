Chapter: Arthroplasty MCQs — Anatomy, Biomechanics and Templating

## Question

Position of legs while templating for THR (FNB Pattern Paper 2020

## Options

1. 15-degree IR
2. 30-degree IR
3. 15-degree ER
4. 30-degree ER

## Correct option

**1. 15-degree IR**

## Answer explanation

To estimate the length of the femoral neck, both femora should be positioned in 15–20° of internal rotation, corresponding to the natural femoral anteversion.

Steps of total hip replacement templating:

- Radiograph analysis:

Magnification – 20 %

 AP Pelvis- centered over pubic symphysis

 AP hip- taken with 15-20 degrees of internal rotation

The surgeon needs to know the magnification of the X-ray he is working on. Assuming that the X-ray tube is at the distance of 1 meter from the table and that the film is 5 cm below the table, magnification will be approximately 20%. Magnification is directly proportional to the distance between pelvis and X-ray film, so increased magnification should be expected in obese patients and less magnification in slim patients.

- Anatomical landmarks

On femoral side- (1) femoral medullary canal; (2) greater trochanter; (3) saddle; (4) lesser trochanter

On the acetabular side- the roof and the teardrops are adequate landmarks. The main anatomical concept, which applies for the teardrop, is that it actually corresponds to the most distal and medial part of the acetabulum, that sits behind the transverse acetabular ligament (TAL) and at the superior border of the obturator foramen.

Standing AP hip X-rays suitable for templating. Anatomical Landmarks: (1) femoral medullary canal; (2) greater trochanter; (3) saddle; (4) lesser trochanter; (5) acetabular roof; (6) teardrop; (I) obturator foramen; (II) symphysis pubis; (III) sacrococcygeal joint; (IV) distance to measure for pelvic tilt observation.

To gain insight into leg length discrepancy, pelvic tilt in both frontal and sagittal plane should be observed. To exclude this effect, keep the AP radiographs in standing position with iliac spines at the same distance bilaterally. This will be confirmed when the symphysis pubis is passing right through the middle of the sacrum.

Check for pelvic tilt: To calculate pelvic sagittal tilt, distance between sacrococcygeal joint and the upper aspect of symphysis pubis is calculated. In neutral alignment it reflects a distance of 32 mm (range: 8–50 mm) in women and 47 mm (range: 15–72 mm) in men. This distance increases when the pelvis is tilted forward and the reciprocal is true as well.

- Limb length discrepancy

Make two horizontal lines one touching both ischial tuberosities (or alternatively both teardrops may be taken) and another touching proximal most point of LT. The left and right-side distance between these two lines is the limb length discrepancy to be corrected intraoperatively.

![[TO17099506-2.jpg]]

- Acetabulum templating: place appropriately sized acetabular template with roughly 40 degrees of abduction. Medial border of the cup should approximate the ilioischial line and lie close to the teardrop. Inferior border of the cup should be at the level of the inferior teardrop line.

Mark center of rotation of acetabular component.

Acetabular offset is defined as the distance between acetabular center of rotation and a vertical line drawn through the teardrop.

- Femoral templating: choose an implant that best fills the medullary canal. Insertional depth is determined to equalize the limb length by locating a new femoral head center of rotation and the acetabular center of rotation. Placing the new femoral head center of rotation superior to the acetabular center of rotation will lengthen the limb and placing the new femoral head center of rotation inferior to the acetabular center of rotation will shorten the limb.
- Mark the intended femoral neck resection level.
- Femoral offset: Femoral offset is the distance from the center of rotation of the femoral head to a line bisecting the long axis of the femur.

![[TO17099506-3.jpg]]

Restoring femoral offset is also important. If the center of rotation of the prosthetic head lies lateral to that of the cup on templating, the reconstruction will produce decreased offset. If the center of rotation of the prosthetic head lies medial to that of the cup on templating, the reconstruction will produce increased offset.

In order to keep limb length and offset the same, the acetabular and femoral offsets must overlap.

Answer: 1
