## Question

During your adult reconstruction rotation you notice that one surgeon you rotate with irrigates the wound with dilute betadine during total knee arthroplasty (TKA), while another only irrigates with normal saline. What is true of the use of dilute povidone-iodine lavage for TKA?

## Options

1. Cost effective in infection reduction
2. Ineffective at reducing infection
3. Equivalent to normal saline
4. Leads to antibiotic resistance
5. Does not need to be cleansed out of the tissues after application

## Correct option

**1. Cost effective in infection reduction**

## Answer explanation

Dilute povidone-iodine is a relatively inexpensive implement that many studies have shown helps reduce the rate of infection for total knee arthroplasty (TKA).

Povidone-iodine acts at numerous cellular targets of infecting organisms, augmenting its role as a biocidal molecule. Dilution of povidone-iodine has been shown to be effective in limiting cytotoxicity to tissues. Clinical studies have verified notable reductions in surgical site infections with a 3-minute soak of 0.35% dilute povidone-iodine irrigation. Guidelines from the World Health Organization, Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, and International Consensus Meeting on Musculoskeletal Infection support the use of prophylactic incisional wound irrigation with aqueous PVP-I to reduce and prevent surgical site infections.

*Shohat et al. performed a retrospective review on 31,331 primary TJAs and compared 8,659 cases irrigated with dilute povidone-iodine with 22,672 cases irrigated with sterile saline.* Dilute povidone-iodine had a lower rate of PJI (0.6% vs 1.3%) with a 0.73% absolute risk reduction and a number needed to treat of 137. **The authors concluded** that the routine use of dilute povidone-iodine could prevent 1 PJI for every 137 TJA patients, regardless of their preoperative risk.

*Kerbel et al. performed a break-even analysis for intraoperative Betadine lavage for the prevention of infection in total hip arthroplasty (THA) and TKA.* At their institution, dilute povidone-iodine lavage costs $2.54. **They found** that it would be cost-effective with an absolute risk reduction of infection of 0.01%. **They concluded** that at typical institutional prices, Betadine lavage can be highly cost-effective in reducing infection after joint arthroplasty.

*Brown et al. compared 1,862 cases before the introduction of povidone-iodine lavage to 688 cases after.* The rate of infection before introduction was 0.97%, while the rate after was 0.15%. **The authors concluded** that Betadine lavage before wound closure may be an inexpensive, effective means of reducing acute postoperative infection after total joint arthroplasty.

Incorrect answers:
Answers 2 and 3: Though some studies have found minimal improvement in infection rates with povidone-iodine lavage, numerous studies have shown that povidone-iodine reduces infection rates.
Answer 4: Povidone-iodine is not an antibiotic and does not lead to antibiotic resistance.
Answer 5: Povidone-iodine is cytotoxic and can lead to tissue damage; therefore, after its use, it must be cleansed out of the tissues to minimize tissue damage.
