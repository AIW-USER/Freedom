## Question

A 28-year-old male professional football player was involved in an on-field collision. He was slow to return to the sideline, but he did not lose consciousness. He denies a headache, neck pain, or nausea. He appears confused but wishes to return to play immediately. Which of the following is the most appropriate recommendation with regard to allowing the athlete to return to play on the same day?

## Options

1. Neurologic exam
2. Sport Concussion Assessment Tool, 5th edition (SCAT5)
3. Immediate Post-Concussion Assessment and Cognitive Testing battery (ImPACT)
4. King-Devick test
5. Return to play should not be allowed

## Correct option

**5. Return to play should not be allowed**

## Answer explanation

The athlete is exhibiting cognitive signs suggestive of a concussion. Athletes exhibiting signs or symptoms of a concussion should not be allowed to return to play the same day.

A concussion is a term that is used to describe clinical symptoms resulting from mild to moderate brain trauma, that may not be due to structural injury. Loss of consciousness is involved only 10% of the time. Numerous sideline assessments have been developed for evaluating athletes; however, given the dangers of a second impact syndrome, an athlete with a suspected concussion should never be allowed to return on the same day. Second impact syndrome results in rapid brain swelling that may cause death resulting from a repeat injury before the athlete has recovered from the initial injury.

*Cahill et al. performed a review on concussions.* **They report** that any athlete in whom a concussion is suspected should be removed from play and assessed by a medical provider. Providers must adhere to standard BLS principles. **They conclude** that if an athlete exhibits signs of a concussion, the athlete should not be allowed to return to play that day.

*McCrory et. al developed a consensus statement on concussion in sport.* **They report** that the symptoms may be somatic (headache), cognitive (confusion), or emotional (lability). There may be physical signs as well such as loss of consciousness. If one of the components is present, a concussion should be suspected and the player must be removed from play and evaluated. **They conclude** that the player should not be allowed to return to play that day.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Though a neurologic exam should be performed, the athlete should not be allowed to return to play the same day.
Answer 2: The SCAT5 is a standardized tool for evaluating athletes aged 13 and over for concussion. It may be utilized, but the patient is exhibiting signs of a concussion and should not return to play the same day.
Answer 3: The ImPACT test is a validated, computerized concussion management tool which may be administered before the season begins in order to obtain an athlete's baseline. In this case, the athlete should not return to play the same day as he is exhibiting signs of a concussion.
Answer 4: The King-Devick test is a portable, 2-minute test that involves reading digits on cards. Again, the athlete should not return to play the same day as he is exhibiting signs of a concussion.
