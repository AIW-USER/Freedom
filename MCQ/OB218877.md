## Question

An 83-year-old female patient is transferred from her nursing home to the emergency department after being found down. The patient is predominantly wheelchair-bound; however, she can stand for transfers and hygienic needs. She has rheumatoid arthritis, COPD and has been on dialysis for 2 years with various inpatient admissions over the last year. Her initial imaging is shown in Figure A. Which of the following information should be provided in the family discussion when obtaining consent?

## Options

1. THA would not result in significantly improved outcomes compared to HA in this patient
2. THA would incur a significant increased risk of a periprosthetic infection over HA
3. THA would be considered the more cost-effective treatment option
4. THA would have equivalent dislocation rates to HA within the first year after surgery
5. THA in contraindicated in patients with any inflammatory arthropathy

## Correct option

**1. THA would not result in significantly improved outcomes compared to HA in this patient**

## Answer explanation

THA and HA have equivalent functionality and quality of life outcomes at short-term follow-up (2 years). In the setting of a displaced femoral neck fracture in a patient with multiple significant medical comorbidities, low functional status, and overall shorter life expectancy, hemiarthroplasty should be the treatment of choice.

Both hemiarthroplasty (HA) and total hip arthroplasty (THA) have been established as treatment options for displaced femoral neck fractures in older patients. HA is considered a less complex surgery and is associated with shorter time under anesthesia, reduced intraoperative blood loss, reduced short-term dislocation rates, and lower implant costs when compared to THA. However, long-term (≥4 years) functional and quality-of-life outcomes are generally superior in patients treated with THA. Therefore, baseline patient characteristics often guide surgeons toward the most appropriate treatment for each patient on a case-by-case basis. Older patients who are generally healthy, functionally independent, community ambulators are treated with THA. On the other hand, non-ambulatory, multiple-comorbidity patients with a short life expectancy are treated with HA.

*Lewis and colleagues performed a systematic review regarding the optimal surgical management for displaced femoral neck fractures.* **They reported** improved functional outcomes and quality of life scores in semi-elderly (50-80yrs) patients treated with THA. While dislocation rates were initially higher in THA during short-term follow-up, **the authors reported** equivalent dislocation rates to HA after a 4-year follow-up. **The authors concluded** that THA should be the treatment of choice for displaced femoral neck fractures in patients aged 50-80 with a >4-year life expectancy. They further endorsed bipolar HA use in patients over the age of 80 years.

*Chammout and colleagues performed the HOPE-Trial which was a prospective, randomized, single-blinded study comparing hemiarthroplasty to total hip arthroplasty for displaced femoral neck fractures in octogenarian (80yrs+) patients.* All participating patients were ambulatory at baseline (with or without assistive devices). **They found** that at the 2-year follow-up, there was no difference in hip function, health-related quality of life, pain in the operatively treated hip, activities of daily living, or the ability to regain previous walking function. **The authors concluded** that hemiarthroplasty is considered a suitable procedure with equivalent short-term outcomes to total hip arthroplasty in this patient cohort.

Figure A shows AP pelvic radiographic imaging of this patient with a right-sided, displaced femoral neck fracture. Illustration A shows this patient's post-operative imaging after undergoing placement of a hemiarthroplasty.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: There has NOT been shown to be a significant difference in the risk of periprosthetic infection between HA and THA in this patient cohort.
Answer 3: Given the equivalent functional outcomes, HA would be considered the more cost-effective treatment option.
Answer 4: THA has a significantly increase dislocation rate compared to HA within the first year after surgery.
Answer 5: While post-operative complications have been reported as slightly higher in inflammatory arthritis when compared to general osteoarthritis, THA is not contraindicated in inflammatory arthropathy.
