## Question

A 65-year-old female half marathon runner undergoes a total knee arthroplasty. Unfortunately, 8 weeks after surgery, her range of motion is only 10-80 degrees. She undergoes a manipulation under anesthesia, during which there is an intraoperative distal femur fracture. What technical error during her original surgery could have put the patient at increased risk of this complication?

## Options

1. Failure to create equal flexion and extension gaps
2. Increased posterior femoral offset
3. Internal rotation of the femoral component
4. Undersizing the femoral component while using a posterior referencing guide
5. Use of press-fit components

## Correct option

**4. Undersizing the femoral component while using a posterior referencing guide**

## Answer explanation

Undersizing the femoral component while using posterior referencing leads to over-resection of the anterior femur. This results in notching, which increases the risks of distal femur fractures (Answer 4).

Notching during total knee arthroplasty (TKA) can lead to catastrophic distal femur fractures. Over-resection of the anterior distal femur by 3 mm significantly increases the fracture risk. Technical errors that lead to notching include improper placement of cutting guides, under sizing the femoral component, and improper technique while making the femoral cuts. Patient factors that increase the risk of post-operative fracture include older patients, those with osteoporosis, and those who perform high-impact activities. The presence of notching in patients with a periprosthetic fracture ranges from 7-50%.

Patients with post-operative stiffness can be treated with a manipulation under anesthesia (MUA). MUA can be considered in patients with less than 90 degrees of flexion or a flexion contracture that limits their functional activity. During the manipulation, the surgeon ranges the knee to break up adhesions. It is critical that aggressive physical therapy be resumed immediately to maintain gains in range of motion. On average, patients who undergo an early MUA improve by 20-30 degrees. In this case, patients who have notching are at increased risk of fracture during the manipulation. The anterior femur is under the greatest stress during maximal extension. Overall, the risk of periprosthetic fracture during a manipulation under anesthesia is less than 2%.

*Ritter and colleagues investigated the impact of anterior femoral notching on post-operative outcomes.* **The findings indicate** that femoral notching increases the risk of supracondylar femoral fractures, particularly when the notch depth exceeds 3-5 mm. Despite this increased fracture risk, the study found that mild notching does not significantly affect long-term clinical outcomes such as pain relief, range of motion, or overall function when fractures are not present. **The authors emphasize** the importance of careful surgical technique to avoid notching and recommend close monitoring and adjusted rehabilitation protocols for patients with significant notching to mitigate fracture risks.

*Zachwieja and colleagues investigated the outcomes of MUA as a treatment for stiffness following TKA.* **The findings show** that MUA is an effective intervention for improving range of motion, particularly within the first three months after surgery. **The authors concluded** that while MUA is a valuable tool for addressing stiffness after TKA, careful patient selection and early implementation are critical for maximizing benefits and minimizing complications.

Incorrect answers:
Answers 1 & 2: failing to create equal flexion and extension gaps can lead to stiffness or instability, but not necessarily distal femur fractures
Answer 3: internal rotation of the femoral component leads to patellar instability but not distal femur fractures
Answer 5: The use of press-fit components does not increase the risk of distal femur fractures compared to cemented components
