Chapter: Arthroscopy MCQs — Shoulder Instability[Unidirectional and Multidirectional]

## Question

which is most significant risk factor for recurrent instability after first time anterior shoulder dislocation?

## Options

1. Bony Bankart’s lesion
2. Young age
3. Manual occupation
4. Greater tuberosity fracture

## Correct option

**2. Young age**

## Answer explanation

Risk factors for recurrent instability following a first-time anterior shoulder dislocation (FTASD) have been described in recent systematic reviews and meta-analyses. These risk factors may be categorized as either modifiable or non-modifiable.

Modifiable risk factors include manual occupations, occupations where the upper limb is used above shoulder height, immobilization following the dislocation, involvement in collision sport and time to return to sport.

Psychosocial factors such as higher levels of pain and fear of reinjury may also be modifiable risk factors.

Non-modifiable risk factors include greater tuberosity fractures, Hill-Sachs lesions, bony Bankart lesions, axillary nerve palsy, age, gender and hypermobility.

Young age is most significant risk factor for recurrent shoulder instability with individuals younger than 25 years old are likely to re-dislocate with non-operative management. People between 14-18 years have around 90 % chances of redislocation after first episode of anterior shoulder dislocation with nonoperative management.

Use of the dominant limb in both daily and high-risk activities may explain why participants who injure their non-dominant shoulder are less likely to have recurrent instability.

The presence of a Hill-Sachs lesion is not significantly associated with recurrent instability.

Immobilization of the limb following a shoulder dislocation is found to decrease the risk of recurrent instability at follow-up.

Answer: 2
