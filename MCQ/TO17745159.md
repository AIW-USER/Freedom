## Question

All are true about ACL except (arthroscopy- knee arthroscopy- ACL)

## Options

1. Tibial attachment site is larger and more secure than Femoral side
2. Nerve supply by posterior articular nerve
3. Main blood supply is inferior medial genicular artery
4. It is extrasynovial

## Correct option

**3. Main blood supply is inferior medial genicular artery**

## Answer explanation

ACL:

Anatomy : Extrasynovial but intracapsular. ACL consists of 2 bundles. AM and PL

Anteromedial (AM)

- fibers are parallel in extension
- fibers are externally rotated in flexion
- tight in both flexion and extension

Posterolateral (PL)

- PL bundle prevents pivot shift
- prevents internal tibial rotation with knee near extension
- tight in extension, loose in flexion

Origin and insertion:

The fascicles of the AMB originate at the most anterior and proximal aspect of the femoral attachment and insert at the anteromedial aspect of the tibial attachment [6]. Conversely, the fascicles of the PLB originate at the posterior-distal aspect of the femoral attachment and insert at the posterolateral aspect of the tibial attachment. Both bundles are attached to the medial wall of lateral femoral condyle behind the residents ridge which separates the AM and PL bundle on the medial wall of lateral femoral condyle.

Tibial attachment is broader than femoral attachment (ACL fibers fan out as they approach their tibial attachment). Its length ranges from 22 to 41 mm (mean, 32 mm) and its width from 7 to 12 mm.

Near its attachment, the ACL sends a variable amount of fibers anteriorly beneath the transverse intermeniscal ligament, and some extensions may blend with both the attachment of the anterior horn of the lateral meniscus. The tibial attachment is somewhat stronger than the femoral attachment (That is why avulsion is common on the tibial side and substance tear on femoral side).

Blood supply: The main blood supply of the cruciate ligaments is provided by the middle genicular artery (MGA). A small amount of blood is supplied to the distal portion of the ACL by the infrapatellar branches of the inferior genicular arteries. The synovial vessels run obliquely and longitudinally over the entire length of the ACL, beneath the synovial membrane. The proximal part of the ACL is better endowed with blood vessels than the distal part.

Avascular zone in ACL:

The periligamentous fold of vessels is absent in a small zone approximately 5–10 mm proximal to the tibial attachment, and in this anterior fibrocartilaginous part the tissue is avascular. These two factors undoubtedly play a role in the poor healing potential of the ACL.

Innervation: posterior articular branches of the tibial nerve

Biomechanics: tensile strength: native ACL 2200 N < bone patellar bone tendon (BPTB) 3000N< quadrupled hamstring 4000N.

Function : Primarily ACL prevents anterior tibial translation on the femur. The ACL is a secondary restraint to internal rotation of the tibia and resist varus displacement at full extension.

Also know: ACL reflex

The mechanoreceptors (Ruffini, Pacini, and Golgi-like receptors) have a proprioceptive function and provide the afferent arc for signaling knee postural changes. Deformations within the ligament influence the output of muscle spindles through the fusimotor system. Hence, activation of afferent nerve fibers in the proximal part of the ACL influences motor activity in the muscles around the knee; a phenomenon called ‘‘ACL reflex. Therefore, this excitatory reflex cannot serve as an automatic protective mechanism for the ACL. The ACL reflex is an essential part of normal knee function and is involved in the updating of muscle programs. This becomes even more obvious in patients with a ruptured ACL, where the loss of feedback from mechanoreceptors in the ACL leads to quadriceps femoris weakness.

Answer : c
