## Question

An 8-year-old male falls from a tree while climbing. Evaluation in the ER demonstrates shallow breathing, a large bleeding laceration to the back of his head, and obvious deformity to his left ankle. Pulse oximetry measures 83% oxygen saturation. When you perform a sternal rub, he withdraws, however, he does not open his eyes or make noise any point during the assessment. What is the best next step in treatment of this patient?

## Options

1. Reduce and splint the patients ankle
2. CT of chest, abdomen and pelvis
3. Intubation
4. Cover the patient in warm blankets
5. Primary closure of head laceration

## Correct option

**3. Intubation**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a Glasgow coma scale (GCS) of 6 in the setting of a likely severe traumatic brain injury. With a GCS less than 8 and oxygen saturation in the 80%, the patient is unable to protect his own airway and breath adequately making intubation necessary. His GCS is based upon not opening eyes (1 point), does not make noise (1 point), and withdraws from pain (4 points).

When evaluating trauma patients it is very important to adhere to Advanced Trauma Life Support guidelines and practices. The steps of the primary survey are 1. Airway, 2. Breathing, 3. Circulation, 4. Disability, and 5. Exposure. It is important to adequately assess and intervene if necessary in each step before moving on. Pediatric patients have smaller airways, cervical anatomy that can predispose to collapse of their airway if laying on an adult spine board, and thoracic physiology that can limit the amount of compensation possible in breathing. When a patient has a low GCS score, they should be intubated to secure their airway before moving on the next steps in ATLS.

*Eichelberger et al. discussed extensively the trauma algorithm of pediatric patients.* *They break the treatment down into life support and post stabilization.* During the life support stage are the steps of ATLS (Airway, Breathing, Circulation, Disability, and Exposure), which are broken down further into specific treatments for each step in assessment. Post stabilization is the secondary survey part of ATLS which is thoroughly discussed and treatment options are again offered based upon what injuries are identified during the secondary survey.

*Kay and Skaggs reviewed the management of polytrauma pediatric patients.* In their paper, they review the management of many orthopedic and non-orthopedic injuries. **The authors highlight** in the paper that the two most common pediatric mechanisms of injury are falls and motor vehicle accidents and that the two most severe individual injuries are vertebral fractures and pelvic fractures. Vertebral and pelvic fractures have on average 5 concomitant injuries and mortality rates of 0.6% and 0.8%, respectively. The most common long term sequelae in polytrauma patients is a neurologic injury with rates up to 38% of pediatric patients.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1,3-5: Although these will likely happen during the care of the patient, evaluation and securing the patient's airway should be the first step in treatment.
