Chapter: Arthroscopy MCQs — Shoulder Instability[Unidirectional and Multidirectional]

## Question

A 25 years old university player met with an accident 2 months back and got his shoulder dislocated. It was reduced by a local GP. Following that he developed pain in overhead activities that is gradually increasing over the last two months. His MRI picture of the shoulder ( axial cut) is given below. What is the correct diagnosis?

![[TO17121341-1.jpg]]

## Options

1. Bankart’s lesion
2. ALPSA
3. SLAP
4. HAGL

## Correct option

**2. ALPSA**

## Answer explanation

This magnetic resonance image shows evidence of a detached medial anteroinferior labral tear with migration medially by an intact periosteal sleeve, which is consistent with an anterior labroligamentous periosteal sleeve avulsion (ALPSA).

Capsular lesions with glenohumeral ligament injuries in patients with primary shoulder dislocation:

- Bankart’s lesion: Avulsion of the labrum and the attached inferior glenohumeral ligament (IGHL) from the anteroinferior glenoid rim, described as the Bankart lesion, has been regarded as the essential lesion of anterior traumatic dislocations of the shoulder.
- Perthes lesion: A Perthes lesion is a labroligamentous avulsion like a Bankart, but with a medially stripped intact periosteum. On images of the shoulder with the arm in a neutral position, the torn labrum may be held in its normal anatomic position by the intact scapular periosteum.

![[TO17121341-2.jpg]]

- ALPSA: An ALPSA lesion ("medialised Bankart"). An ALPSA-lesion is an Anterior Labral Periosteal Sleeve Avulsion.

The anterior labrum is absent on the glenoid rim. Medially displaced torn labrum is still attached to the medial scapular periosteum. The arrow points to the medially displaced labroligamentous complex.

- GLAD : A GLAD-lesion is a GlenoLabral Articular Disruption. It represents a partial tear of the anteroinferior labrum with adjacent cartilage damage. The arrow points to the cartilage defect
![[TO17121341-3.jpg]]

![[TO17121341-4.jpg]]

- HAGL: HAGL is a Humeral Avulsion of the inferior Glenohumeral Ligament.There is discontinuity of the IGHL attachment on the humerus.
- Kim lesion: A Kim lesion is a concealed tear at the posterior glenoid chondrolabral junction accompanied by a concealed partial detachment of the deeper portion of the labrum from the glenoid rim.

![[TO17121341-5.jpg]]

Progression of Kim lesion. Labrum (black), cartilage (gray) and posterior joint capsule (curvilinear lines) are indicated. Glenoid hypoplasia also demonstrated, but need not always bepresent. Initially, there is partial separation of the labrum from the glenoid (glenolabral detachment indicated by vertical lines). Over time, this separation progresses outward to involve the portion of the labrum attached to the cartilage (chondrolabral detachment indicated by dots). However, the portion of the chondrolabral junction visible to the arthroscope remains intact.

Probing may reveal labral fluctuance (indicated by wavy labral contour in diagram).

All these lesions, i.e. Bankarts, perthes, HAGL, ALPSA, GLAD, are associated with recurrent anterior shoulder dislocation. Kim lesion is associated with posterior shoulder instability.

Answer: 2
