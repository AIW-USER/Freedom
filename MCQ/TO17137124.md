Chapter: Trauma MCQs — Fracture Of Distal End Radius

## Question

A patient presented with comminuted fracture of distal end of radius. Close reduction was done and fracture was casted. After some time, the patient returns with complaints of severe pain and paresthesia in the lateral digits. What is the next appropriate step?

## Options

1. Immediate release of cast
2. Immediate carpal tunnel release
3. Immediately release cast and immobilize the wrist in neutral position
4. Just assure the patient

## Correct option

**3. Immediately release cast and immobilize the wrist in neutral position**

## Answer explanation

Ans: C

Carpal tunnel syndrome (CTS) after distal radius fractures can present in 3 forms: acute, transient,
and delayed.

Acute CTS is when median nerve compression symptoms are seen immediately after fracture
reduction. This is so as the nerve has been entrapped at fracture site and this requires an emergent
nerve exploration along with a carpal tunnel release.

Some patients may manifest transient symptoms of CTS that may spontaneously resolve. Such
patients just need further observation and do not require surgical release of the carpal tunnel once
the fracture has been acceptably reduced.

For patients with Delayed CTS (as the case mentioned in question) after a distal radius fracture, all
possible causes of nerve compression should be considered and addressed in standard fashion. One
should first and foremost release the constricting bandages and cast should be loosened, and the
wrist should be extended to neutral position. Still if the median nerve symptoms persist or have not
improved after positional change, surgery (carpal tunnel release) usually is indicated.

Prophylactic carpal tunnel release in the absence of signs and symptoms of CTS after a distal radius
fracture fixation is not indicated.
