## Question

A 31-year-old male presents to your office complaining of years of right anterior groin pain. He explains that he can no longer participate in his daily cross-fit workouts. His pain is localized to the anterior hip and is worsened with flexion, adduction, and internal rotation of the hip. You obtain radiographs and an MRI which identify a CAM deformity of the proximal femur in addition to a labral tear. He explains that he has attempted physical therapy without benefit and did get several weeks of complete relief from an intra-articular steroid injection. Therefore, you schedule him for an arthroscopic labral repair and femoral osteoplasty using the setup seen in Figure A. At his first postoperative appointment, his deep groin pain is improved but he complains of numbness of the right inner thigh and hemi-scrotum. Which of the following is the most likely cause of this complication?

## Options

1. Injury to the obturator nerve due to intra-operative fluid extravasation
2. Injury to the obturator nerve during establishment of the anterolateral portal
3. Injury to the pudendal nerve due to direct compression
4. Injury to the pudendal nerve during establishment of the distal anterolateral portal
5. Injury to the lateral femoral cutaneous nerve during establishment of anterior portal

## Correct option

**3. Injury to the pudendal nerve due to direct compression**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a pudendal nerve palsy, which can occur during hip arthroscopy due to direct compression of the nerve by the traction post.

Hip arthroscopy can be used to address a variety of intra- and extra-articular hip pathologies. While it can be quite successful in decreasing hip pain and improving function, it also has a steep learning curve and a risk of unique complications. Principally, hip arthroscopy carries a risk for neurovascular damage. The structures at risk typically correspond with the portal locations. Lateral femoral cutaneous nerve (LFCN) palsy is associated with anterior portal creation, superior gluteal nerve palsy is associated with anterolateral portal creation, and sciatic nerve palsy is associated with the establishment of the posterolateral portal. Additionally, in hip arthroscopy cases where a perineal post is used to apply traction, a pudendal nerve palsy can occur. To decrease direct compression injury, traction time should be limited and the post should be well-padded. Newer, tractionless hip arthroscopy setups are now being used in order to avoid this complication.

*Habib et al. reviewed pudendal nerve palsies and their frequency after hip arthroscopy.* **They noted** that 62/3405 (1.8%) had pudendal nerve palsies postoperatively and all resolved within a period of 6 weeks - 3 months. This rate was higher (4.3%) in cases using a perineal post, and in cases where traction times were longer.

*Nakano et al. performed a systematic review of complications after hip arthroscopy.* The overall complication rate was 3.3%, with nerve injury occurring in 0.9% of cases. The most common nerve injuries occurred in the pudendal and LFCN distributions.

*Pailhe et al. published a retrospective review looking at pudendal nerve neuralgia after hip arthroscopy.* **They noted** that 2% of patients had a sensory pudendal neuralgia, and all spontaneously resolved within 3 weeks - 6 months. They were not able to identify any specific risk factors for nerve injury, but did recommend limiting traction force and duration with a well padded post >8-10cm in diameter.

Figure A demonstrates the bed setup used for hip arthroscopy which, in this case, includes a traction post.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The obturator nerve exits the pelvic brim adjacent to the medial border of the psoas and provides sensation to the medial thigh as well as motor innervation primarily to the hip adductors. The obturator nerve does not provide innervation to scrotum.
Answer 2: The obturator nerve courses deep and medial to the anterolateral portal and would not be at risk.
Answer 4: Based on its normal course, the pudendal nerve is not at risk when establishing a distal anterolateral portal in hip arthroscopy.
Answer 5: The LFCN is at risk for injury when establishing the anterior portal in hip arthroscopy but would cause anterolateral thigh numbness, rather than perineal numbness.
