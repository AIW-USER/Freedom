## Question

A 65-year-old man presents to the emergency department after sustaining a ground-level fall. He complains of severe right thigh and knee pain, and a radiograph of the right femur is presented in Figure A. When comparing the constructs shown in Figures B and C, which of the following is true of the construct depicted in Figure C?

![[q220407-c-nail-plate-combo.jpg]]

## Options

1. Decreased risk of reoperation
2. Decreased risk of infection
3. Increased risk of conversion to distal femur replacement
4. Decreased rates of immediate weightbearing
5. Increased risk of nonunion

## Correct option

**1. Decreased risk of reoperation**

## Answer explanation

This patient has sustained a distal femur fracture. Nail plate combination (NPC) has been shown to have a lower risk of reoperation than lateral locked plating (LLP) in the treatment of distal femur fractures (Answer 1).

Distal femur fractures are increasingly common fractures that pose significant difficulty in fixation. Historically, many implant options were considered, including fixed-angle devices such as blade plates and dynamic condylar screws, as well as dual-plating constructs. However, modern fixation has shifted primarily towards the use of lateral locked plating (LLP), retrograde intramedullary nails (rIMN), nail plate combination (NPC), and dual plating constructs. Several series investigating distal femur fracture treatment have shown that NPC constructs have a lower reoperation rate compared with LLP, which has a rate of nonunion of approximately 20%.

*Shi et al. compared NPC with LLP for distal femur fractures.* *The study involved 33 patients treated with NPC and 867 with LLP across ten Level 1 trauma centers.* The main outcomes measured were unplanned reoperation rates and the presence of varus collapse. Results showed that NPC patients had significantly lower unplanned reoperation rates for reasons other than infection compared to LLP patients (3% vs. 23%, P = 0.023), and no varus collapse was observed in the NPC group. The study concluded that NPC resulted in high union rates and good functional outcomes even in complex fractures, **suggesting** that it may be a more reliable option than LLP alone.

*Espey et al. provide a systematic review that evaluates the use of NPC constructs for treating native distal femoral fractures.* Four studies suggest NPCs allow earlier weight bearing and have lower non-union and implant failure rates compared to single constructs without increasing infection rates. **The authors note** that the four series reviewed were highly heterogeneous and better series are necessary.

Figure A is the radiograph of a comminuted complete articular distal femur fracture. Figures B and C are the AP radiographs of a distal femur fracture fixed with LLP and NPC constructs, respectively.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: The reported infection rate between NPC and LLP is equivalent
Answer 3: There is no current literature investigating conversion rates of distal femur fractures to distal femur replacement, which is rare
Answer 4: Can do immediate weightbearing of isolated plating or NPC
Answer 5: NPC has a lower reported rate of nonunion
