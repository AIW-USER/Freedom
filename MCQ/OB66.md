## Question

Figure A is a photograph of a newborn's hand. Genetic testing reveals a mutation in the SLC26A2 gene on Chromosome 5q and both the parents were carriers. What condition does this child have?

![[q66-a-hitchhikers-thumb.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Achondroplasia
2. Osteogenesis imperfecta
3. Hypochondroplasia
4. Diastrophic dysplasia
5. Ehlers-danlos syndrome

## Correct option

**4. Diastrophic dysplasia**

## Answer explanation

The clinical image and genetic testing results are consistent with diastrophic dysplasia.

Diastrophic dysplasia is an autosomal recessive genetic disorder with the mutation mapped to chromosome 5q called SLC26A2. Clinical manifestations of this mutation include disproportionate, short-limbed dwarfism (generally diagnosed at birth), dysmorphic ear lobes and palate, generalized limitation of joint movement, clubfoot, and other skeletal abnormalities including "Hitchhiker" thumb. "Hitchhiker" thumb is a result of a shortened 1st metacarpal and radial subluxation of the MCP joint.

Ryöppy et al looked at the different foot manifestations seen in infants with diastrophic dysplasia. They found the most common deformities were tarsal valgus deformity (43%) and metatarsus adductus (37%).

Figure A shows a classic example of a "hitchhiker" thumb.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Achondroplasia is the most common form of disproportionate dwarfism and is caused by a mutation in fibroblast growth factor receptor 3 (FGFR3).
Answer 2: Osteogenesis imperfecta is characterized by bone fragility and short stature resulting from a defect in type I collagen (COL1A2 gene) that causes abnormal cross-linking and leads to decreased collagen secretion.
Answer 3: Hypochondroplasia is similar to achondroplasia and is caused by an autosomal dominant mutation in FGFR3 in a different location than achondroplasia.
Answer 5: Ehlers-Danlos syndrome is a connective tissue disorder with several subtypes including the classic form resulting from a mutation in COL5A1 and COL5A2, the genes for type V collagen.
