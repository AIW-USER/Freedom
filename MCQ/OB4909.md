## Question

A 60-year-old female presents with a 2 week history of low back pain. The pain is described as a dull, persistent ache and radiates down to her buttocks but not past the horizontal gluteal crease. She has a history of Type 2 diabetes mellitus and urinary incontinence. Examination reveals loin tenderness, paravertebral muscle spasm and diminished reflexes but full strength and sensation in the lower extremities. ## Options

1. These are Modic Type I changes and treatment will involve NSAIDS, a short period of bed rest and physical therapy
2. These are Modic Type I changes and treatment will involve a lumbar corset and physical therapy
3. This is spondylodiscitis and treatment will involve intravenous antibiotics and a thoracolumbosacral orthosis (TLSO).
4. This is spondylodiscitis and treatment will involve needle biopsy, intravenous antibiotics and a thoracolumbosacral orthosis (TLSO).
5. This is spondylodiscitis and treatment will involve anterior decompression and stabilization, and intravenous antibiotics.

## Correct option

**4. This is spondylodiscitis and treatment will involve needle biopsy, intravenous antibiotics and a thoracolumbosacral orthosis (TLSO).**

## Answer explanation

This patient has discitis and osteomyelitis of the adjacent vertebrae. The next step involves obtaining a tissue biopsy culture, followed by empiric antibiotics, to be later tailored to the specific pathogen. A TLSO is also provided for immobilization.

Bacterial spondylodiscitis in adults is most common in the lumbar spine. The most common organism is Staphylococcus aureus (>50%). Pyogenic infections start at the endplate and spread into the adjoining disk and progress into the next vertebral body. Needle biopsy is the first step and accuracy is about 70%. An open biopsy is indicated when needle biopsy is inadequate or impossible and accuracy is >80%.

*Tay et al. reviewed spinal infections.* **They state** that the goals of treatment are establishing a diagnosis and identifying the pathogen, preventing neurological deficit, maintaining spinal stability. Establishing a diagnosis and identifying the pathogen is most important. **They recommend** that if the initial core needle biopsy is inadequate, a second biopsy should be considered before an open biopsy is done.

*Cousins et al. reviewed MRI imaging of the spine.* *They acknowledged that an increase in vertebral body T2 signal is not specific for osteomyelitis and recommended adding contrast and using fat-suppression images to aid in the diagnosis.* **They further suggested** evaluating the appearance of the disk, end plates, epidural space, and paraspinal tissues to differentiate infection from degenerative changes.

Figure A is a T2-weighted image showing degenerative changes with an increased signal at the L2-3 disc. Figure B is a T1-weighted image showing the destruction of adjacent endplates. Figure C is a T1 contrast-enhanced image showing enhancement of the disc, endplates, and adjacent bone marrow. There is no collection. Illustration A summarizes Modic endplate changes.

Incorrect Answers
Answers 1 and 2: Modic Type I changes are degenerative signal abnormalities (hypointense on T1, hyperintense on T2). The difference between Type I changes and diskitis can be assessed on T2-weighted images. Diskitis presents with increased T2 signal intensity, whereas a degenerated, desiccated disc with Type I changes presents with normal/hypointense signal. Modic Type I changes will also not have erosion of the endplates, paravertebral soft tissue mass, and abnormal ESR and CRP.
Answers 3: It is necessary to first establish a diagnosis (with biopsy) and identify the pathogen before initiating antibiotics to prevent false-negative results.
Answer 5: Surgical intervention will be necessary only in the following scenarios: to establish tissue diagnosis after a failed needle biopsy, drainage of an abscess, presence of neurological deficits, instability, or for failed medical management. As this patient currently demonstrates none of the above complications, a non-operative course is preferred and the primary objective is still to establish the diagnosis with a needle biopsy.
