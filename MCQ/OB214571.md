## Question

A 65-year-old male presents with persistent shoulder pain and limited range of motion. He has a history of recurrent shoulder dislocations requiring a Bankart repair 20 years ago. He has undergone several months of physical therapy and corticosteroid injections without improvement in his pain. On exam, the patient has forward flexion and abduction to 80° with external rotation limits to 25° with the elbow at his side. Figures A through C are the current radiographs and MRI. What is the most appropriate treatment option at this time?

## Options

1. Humeral head arthroplasty
2. Total shoulder arthroplasty
3. Reverse shoulder arthroplasty
4. Arthroscopic debridement/capsular release
5. Corticosteroid injection and physical therapy

## Correct option

**3. Reverse shoulder arthroplasty**

## Answer explanation

The patient is presenting with end-stage right shoulder glenohumeral arthritis after pre-existing shoulder instability treated with a Bankart repair. Given the fatty atrophy of the supraspinatus on the T1 sagittal MRI, posterior humeral head subluxation and erosion of the posterior glenoid, a reverse total shoulder arthroplasty would be the most appropriate treatment.

Shoulder instability can lead to advanced degenerative changes over time, also known as dislocation arthropathy. This is usually the result of articular cartilage damage as a result of the dislocation episode with further eccentric loading of the joint from persistent instability. Similarly, historical procedures, such as the Putti-Platti procedure, have resulted in accelerated degeneration of the glenohumeral joint due to increased loading stress. Patients typically present in the 5th decade with debilitating pain and functional limitations of the affected shoulder. Arthroplasty has proven to be a successful treatment option for these patients, with reverse total shoulder arthroplasty indicated in the setting of rotator cuff deficiency.

*Hovelius et al. performed a prospective study of 118 patients for the development of dislocation arthropathy following Bristow-Latarjet procedures.* **They reported** that 35% of shoulders developed mild dislocation arthropathy and 14% developed severe arthropathy. For those that developed severe arthropathy, first-time dislocation at age >23 years was the only risk factor for the development of severe arthropathy. They stressed the importance of having the same radiographic views to assess the incidence of dislocation arthropathy appropriately.

*Green and Norris performed a retrospective study of 19 patients that developed advanced glenohumeral arthritis following anterior instability repair.* **They reported** 17 patients underwent arthroplasty treatment at a mean age of 45 years with 94% of patients reporting pain relief and improvement in shoulder function. **The authors concluded** that advanced glenohumeral arthritis as a late-stage complication of shoulder instability which can be effectively treated with total shoulder arthroplasty.

*Hawkins and Angelo retrospectively studied 10 patients that developed osteoarthritis of the glenohumeral joint after Putti-Platt repair.* **They reported** symptom onset an average of 13.2 years from the procedure and resulted in a substantial limitation of range of motion. **They concluded** that some patients could be treated conservatively whereas an anterior release improved range of motion.

Figure C is the sagittal T1 MRI of the shoulder with fatty atrophy of the supraspinatus. Illustration A is an AP radiograph depicting a humeral head arthroplasty. Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: Humeral head arthroplasty is a bone stock preserving procedure that addresses the humeral head alone. This will likely result in persistent post-operative pain since there is advanced degeneration of the glenoid.
Answer 2: The patient has fatty atrophy of the supraspinatus on the sagittal T1 MRI images. Anatomic total shoulder arthroplasty is not indicated given the high failure rate in the setting of an incompetent rotator cuff.
Answer 4: Given the advanced degeneration of the glenohumeral joint an arthroscopic debridement and capsular release will be unsuccessful in giving the patient long-term relief of his symptoms.
Answer 5: Further treatment with steroid injections and physical therapy is unlikely to improve his symptoms given that he has attempted an adequate course.
