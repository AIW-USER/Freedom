## Question

A 36-year-old avocado field worker presents to the clinic four years after having his left forearm fixed surgically in Mexico. He states that roughly two years ago a "pimple" erupted over the ulnar border of his forearm and drained for a few weeks but eventually closed up on its own. Since then, he has had difficulty with numerous similar lesions that intermittently open and close along his scar, as shown in Figure A. His current radiographs are shown in Figures B and C. Lab studies demonstrate a c-reactive protein (CRP) of 6.4 mg/L (normal < 4.9 mg/L) and an erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR) of 24 mm/hr (normal < 22 mm/hr). Which of the following represents the next best step in the management of this patient's condition?

## Options

1. Administer chronic suppressive empiric oral antibiotics until the fractures are healed
2. Culture the biopsy site in the clinic prior to surgical intervention
3. Perform an MRI to evaluate for any complex fluid collections
4. Schedule for debridement and irrigation with hardware removal
5. Urgent admission for operative debridement and intravenous antibiotics

## Correct option

**4. Schedule for debridement and irrigation with hardware removal**

## Answer explanation

The patient has radiographic and laboratory evidence of chronic osteomyelitis with multiple draining sinus tracts. The patient should be scheduled for debridement and irrigation with hardware removal, followed by admission for culture-directed intravenous antibiotics.

Chronic osteomyelitis in the setting of infected hardware is a difficult problem to treat successfully. Despite surgical debridement and long-term antibiotics, recurrence rates of chronic osteomyelitis in adults approach 30%. Regardless, the first step in management in a medically stable patient who is not systemically ill is a non-urgent debridement and irrigation with hardware removal. All devitalized tissue and non-essential hardware should be removed, and deep wound cultures should be taken to allow for culture-directed intravenous antibiotics that can be tailored to an oral regimen of sufficient duration for discharge. Depending on the amount of skin that must be removed due to sinus tract involvement, negative pressure wound vacuum therapy versus subsequent soft tissue coverage procedures may be indicated. After removal of hardware, the stability at the fracture site should be tested, and if a nonunion is present, splint/cast immobilization or the placement of cement antibiotic spacers in the presence of large bony defects may be required until the infection is eradicated and definitive fixation can be pursued.

Rao, et al. reviewed the treatment of osteomyelitis with antibiotics and surgery. The authors note that osteomyelitis is best managed by a multidisciplinary team, as it requires accurate diagnosis and optimization of host defenses, appropriate anti-infective therapy, and bone debridement with reconstructive surgery. They conclude that intraoperative cultures, optimally obtained from bone with the patient off antibiotics, are very useful for selecting appropriate antimicrobial therapy.

Hogan, et al. reviewed the different treatment options for osteomyelitis at any stage: antibiotics in the acute and chronic stage for conservative treatment or radical debridement, bone fenestration, reaming, bone troughing, the Masquelet-technique, segmental resection with callus distraction, bone grafting and even amputation as surgical therapy. The authors concluded that the treatment of osteomyelitis should be done in centers with expertise in the treatment of this challenging disease and that good results are expected with every technique available on the condition that a radical debridement is performed.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Chronic suppressive antibiotics would not be an appropriate definitive treatment option for a young, healthy laborer. This would only be considered in recurrent bouts of chronic osteomyelitis in patients in whom surgical intervention is not feasible due to significant comorbid risks.
Answer 2: Culturing sinus tracts has not been shown to be useful as it often yields spurious confounding results due to contaminants. Deep wound cultures should be taken instead during a surgical debridement in order to appropriate direct antibiotic management post-operatively.
Answer 3: An MRI would not change the management or the extent of the debridement in this particular patient unless there was concern for malignancy or additional pathology.
Answer 5: Urgent admission is not required in the setting of a stable patient with chronic osteomyelitis who is not systemically ill. Elective debridement and scheduled post-operative admission can be performed for culture-directed antibiotics and infectious disease consultation.
