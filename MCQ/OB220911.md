## Question

A 49-year-old woman presents to the clinic with a painful bony prominence on her left medial forefoot. Radiographs are displayed in Figure A. Symptoms persist despite shoe modifications and orthoses, and she is scheduled for elective surgical correction. She is otherwise healthy and takes no prescription medications. Postoperatively, you plan to allow immediate heel-weight-bearing in a CAM walker boot. Which postoperative venous thromboembolism strategy is most appropriate for this patient?

![[q220911-a-screen-shot-2025-11-11-at-5-12-11-am.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Aspirin 325 mg daily for 2–4 weeks
2. Low–molecular-weight heparin (LMWH) 40 mg subcutaneously daily for 2 weeks
3. No routine chemical prophylaxis
4. Direct oral anticoagulant (DOAC) once daily for 35 days
5. Aspirin 81 mg twice daily for 6 weeks

## Correct option

**3. No routine chemical prophylaxis**

## Answer explanation

This patient, undergoing elective forefoot surgery with immediate weight-bearing, would be considered low risk for venous thromboembolism (VTE). Although management should be individualized based on patient-specific risk profiles, current consensus guidelines recommend no routine chemical prophylaxis for low-risk patients (Answer 3).

Anticoagulation fundamentals are grounded in Virchow’s triad—venous stasis, endothelial injury, and hypercoagulability—which explains why orthopaedic procedures can increase the risk of VTE. Multiple anticoagulant classes exist with differing mechanisms and trade-offs, and remain a cornerstone of appropriate perioperative management of many orthopedic conditions. Importantly, routine chemical prophylaxis may not be indicated in certain isolated below-knee scenarios, underscoring the need for risk stratification rather than blanket protocols.

The International Consensus Meeting on Venous Thromboembolism (ICM-VTE) Foot & Ankle recommendations report that routine chemoprophylaxis is not advised for low-risk elective forefoot/midfoot surgery—especially with immediate weight-bearing—and stress individualized risk assessment. They also note that non-weight-bearing is an independent VTE risk factor mitigated by >50% load-bearing, but available data do not justify routine prophylaxis selection changes solely on this basis.

*Sullivan et al. prospectively screened low-risk elective hindfoot/midfoot cases with postoperative ultrasound and* **found** a 25.4% DVT prevalence, the majority asymptomatic and distal, with most detected at two weeks and a substantial proportion at six weeks. **They noted** that silent DVTs are far more common than clinically apparent events, emphasizing surveillance sensitivity rather than clinical symptom detection. **They concluded** that the clinically silent DVT prevalence is higher than previously appreciated in non-weight-bearing cohorts.

Incorrect Answers:

Answers 1 & 5: Although aspirin has become increasingly popular for thromboembolism prophylaxis in many orthopedic conditions, its use is not recommended in patients with a low-risk profile as described.
Answers 2 & 4: Neither routine LMWH nor DOAC use is supported for low-risk elective forefoot/midfoot surgery with immediate weight-bearing.
