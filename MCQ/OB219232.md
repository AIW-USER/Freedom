## Question

A 55-year-old former college football player presents to your spine clinic with complaints of localized low back pain without radicular symptoms. He has attempted physical therapy and has been taking NSAIDs without much relief. An MRI is ordered which demonstrates degeneration of his L4/5 disc. Which of the following changes occurs in the setting of disc degeneration?

## Options

1. Increase in ratio of chondroitin sulfate to keratin sulfate
2. Decrease in metalloproteinases
3. Decrease in aggrecan
4. Increase in water content
5. Increase in hydrophilic proteoglycans

## Correct option

**3. Decrease in aggrecan**

## Answer explanation

Aggrecan is the most plentiful proteoglycan in the intervertebral disc and its presence decreases over time, causing the disc's water content to decrease (answer 3).

The intervertebral discs help to link the vertebral bodies of the spine together and they contribute to spinal height, stability, and motion. The disc is composed of the annulus fibrosus and the nucleus pulposus, with the latter being composed primarily of type II collagen, water, and proteoglycans providing the disc its compressibility. As the disc ages over time, it undergoes many changes including a loss of water content. This is related to the loss of hydrophilic proteoglycans, the most plentiful of which is aggrecan. The loss of proteoglycans relates to an increase in degradative enzyme activity that occurs with aging and repetitive wear. This can be appreciated on MRI as "dark disc disease," with a decrease in fluid signal on T2 sequences. These degenerative changes alone typically cause localized back pain but if the annulus fibrosus is also compromised and the disc material herniates, radicular symptoms may also be appreciated.

*Kepler et al. published a JAAOS review article evaluating intervertebral disc degeneration and related biological treatments.* **They note** that in the setting of disc degeneration, there is an increase in degradative enzyme activity which accelerates cellular apoptosis mechanisms and depletes matrix proteins. Newer techniques are focused on injectable and percutaneous techniques including growth factors and mitogens that may help reverse enzymatic changes related to disc degradation - however, these techniques still lack clinical support at this point.

*Matsui et al. looked at the involvement of matrix metalloproteinases and the role of inflammation in the herniation of lumbar discs.* **They noted** that in specimens where disc degeneration had occurred, there was an increase in metalloproteinases and inflammatory cells. This caused them to conclude that there was a causal relationship between an increase in metalloproteinases and tissue degradation in the setting of disc herniations.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: In the setting of disc aging, there is typically an increase in the keratin sulfate:chondroitin sulfate ratio.
Answer 2: Metalloproteinases are degradative enzymes that increase their activity in the setting of disc aging.
Answer 4: Water content decreases with an associated decrease in aggrecan which is a hydrophilic proteoglycan.
Answer 5: Hydrophilic proteoglycans decrease in content with disc aging, which causes the water content to decrease and be more sensitive to breakdown.
