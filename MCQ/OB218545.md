## Question

A female patient presents to your office with complaints of long-standing right ankle pain. She previously sustained a right pilon fracture twelve years ago. She has obvious tibiotalar arthritis on radiographs, and is being considered for a supramalleolar osteotomy. Which of the following findings would be the best indication to perform a supramalleolar osteotomy in this patient?

## Options

1. 30% preserved tibiotalar joint surface
2. Age >70 years
3. Inflammatory arthritis
4. Neuroarthropathy
5. Varus heel alignment

## Correct option

**5. Varus heel alignment**

## Answer explanation

Varus heel alignment is an indication for a supramalleolar osteotomy.

Ankle arthritis is a degenerative joint disease of the tibiotalar joint that can be broken into three main types: osteoarthritis, post-traumatic arthritis, and inflammatory arthritis. Diagnosis is primarily made with plain radiographs of the ankle. Treatment can be nonoperative or operative depending on the patient age, patient activity demands, the severity of arthritis, and presence of tibiotalar deformity. A supramalleolar osteotomy may be indicated in patients with near normal arc of motion, minimal talar tilt, a varus heel alignment, and medially focused ankle arthritis (stage 2 or 3a according to the Takakura-Tanaka classification for varus-type osteoarthritis).

*Hintermann et al. reviewed supramalleolar osteotomies.* **They reported** that the primary goal of the procedure is to realign the spatial relationship between the talus and tibia and thereby normalize joint loading within the ankle. **They concluded** that clinical studies of supramalleolar osteotomy demonstrate that correction of the altered biomechanics associated with asymmetric arthritis improves functional outcomes.

*Pagenstert et al. reviewed realignment surgery for asymmetric (varus or valgus) ankle osteoarthritis.* *They reported on 35 consecutive patients with posttraumatic ankle osteoarthritis treated with lower leg and hindfoot realignment surgery.* **They noted** that at a mean follow-up of 5 years, pain decreased by an average of 4 points on a visual analog scale and range of ankle motion increased by an average of 5 degrees. **They concluded** that realignment surgery for patients with asymmetric ankle osteoarthritis may be a successful treatment option.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1-4: Indications for a supramalleolar osteotomy include near normal arc of motion, minimal talar tilt, a varus heel alignment, and medially focused ankle arthritis (stage 2 or 3a according to the Takakura-Tanaka classification for varus-type osteoarthritis). Contraindications to this surgery include less than 50% preserved tibiotalar joint surface, age greater than 70, hindfoot instability that cannot be managed with ligament reconstruction, significant vascular disease, inflammatory arthritis, and neuroarthropathy.
