## Question

A 32-year-old female is returning to your office for her left hip pain. One month ago she underwent the MR arthrogram seen in Figures A and B, in addition to an intra-articular steroid and lidocaine injection which relieved all of her pain but only for one day. She had no hip pain until 7 months ago and has not improved despite taking a month off from training for her upcoming marathon, working with physical therapy, and taking anti-inflammatories. What is the best next step in treatment for this patient?

## Options

1. Repeat intra-articular injection using fluoroscopy followed by further therapy
2. Percutaneous pinning for non displaced femoral neck fracture
3. Arthroscopic treatment of labral tear and possible femoroacetabular impingement
4. Arthroscopic loose body removal
5. Peri-acetabular osteotomy

## Correct option

**3. Arthroscopic treatment of labral tear and possible femoroacetabular impingement**

## Answer explanation

The patient is suffering from persistent hip pain due to a labral tear. The most appropriate next step would be to undergo hip arthroscopy to address the labral tear and likely femoroacetabular impingement.

Acetabular labral tears are see in 69% of the general asymptomatic population, with symptomatic tears commonly seen in active females. The labrum adds 22% to the articular surface area and 33% to the volume of the hip, providing further stability. Also it creates a negative pressure suction effect, sealing the central compartment to retain synovial fluid and thus creates frictionless motion at the articular surfaces. Tears occur most commonly at the anterosuperior aspect, likely due to the low collagen content and parallel orientation of the labral fibers to the chondrolabral junction.

*Register et al. reviewed MR imaging of 45 individuals with no prior history of hip pain to evaluate for hip pathology.* **They found** 73% of subjects had some degree of hip pathology, 69% had a labral tear, and increasing incidence of cartilage degeneration associated with age. This highlights the fact that though labral tears can be a source of pain and disability, they can also remain asymptomatic and require no treatment

Figure A is an AP radiograph of the pelvis with evidence of retroversion. Figures B and C are fat-saturated T1 coronal and axial images of an MR arthrogram showing an anterosuperior labral tear. Illustration A shows the knotless suture anchor repair technique preserving the overall anatomy of the labrum.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The patient has already failed conservative management with injections and PT.
Answer 2: There is no edema or fracture line seen on the images
Answer 4: There is no evidence of loose bodies on the images
Answer 5: The patient has no previous history of hip issues, and her imaging studies do not reveal evidence of hip dysplasia for which a peri-acetabular osteotomy would be indicated.
