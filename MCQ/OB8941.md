## Question

Figure A shows a 50-year-old female who is one year status post hallux valgus correction of her right foot. Her preoperative pain is gone but he reports 1 month of "walking on marbles" and pain walking barefoot. She has no paresthesias or neurologic findings. She has a large callus under the second metatarsal head with focal tenderness in that area. She has dorsomedial deviation of the second toe in relation to the metatarsal. She has >50% subluxation of the second metatarsophalangeal joint with a drawer test. What is the next best treatment?

![[q8941-a-ap-mtpj.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Orthotics with metatarsal pads, shoe modification, taping, and NSAIDS
2. Corticosteroid injections
3. Revision hallux valgus correction
4. Shortening osteotomy and plantar plate repair
5. Metatarsophalangeal joint fusion

## Correct option

**1. Orthotics with metatarsal pads, shoe modification, taping, and NSAIDS**

## Answer explanation

The patient has 1 month of symptomatic metatarsophalaneal joint instability best initially treated with orthotics with metatarsal pads, shoe modification, taping, and NSAIDs.

MTPJ instability is a common cause of lesser toe pain and recently has been attributed to deficiency of the plantar plate. Patients may complain of a sensation of walking on marbles. Following failure of the plantar plate a combination of intrinsic and extrinsic forces leads to a dorsiflexed position of the proximal phalanx. The drawer test is performed by holding the proximal phalanx in a neutral position and applying a dorsally directed force to assess instability. Operative treatment options include Weil shortening osteotomy, plantar plate repair or reconstruction, flexor to extensor tendon transfer, or EDB transfer under intermetatarsal ligament.

*Mizel et al. review the various disorders that can present as lesser MTPJ pain.* **They found** that instability of the MTPJ is affected by intrinsic and extrinsic muscle forces as well as static stabilization of the plantar plate. **They conclude** nonoperative treatment followed by appropriate surgical intervention disabling pain and deformity can be solved.

*Doty et al. review MTPJ instability including presentation, exam findings, classification, and treatment.* **They found** that multiple techniques focus on repair of the plantar plate. **They conclude** that plantar plate repair or reconstruction leads to promising results in pain relief and deformity correction.

Figure A shows an AP of a right foot status post hallux valgus correction. Illustration A shows a clinical image of dorsomedial deviation of the second toe representing metatarsophalangeal joint instability. Illustration B shows an image of the drawer test. Illustration C shows taping to prevent dorsal subluxation of a second metatarsophalangeal joint.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Corticosteroid injections are not indicated in this scenario and can lead to further capsular and plantar plate attenuation.
Answer 3: Revision hallux valgus correction is not indicated as the patient has achieved a stable correction.
Answer 4: Shortening osteotomy and plantar plate repair would not be indicated until a failed course of conservative measures.
Answer 5: Metatarsophalangeal joint fusion may be considered after failed conservative measures and a rigid joint deformity.
