## Correct option

## Answer explanation

Hereditary multiple exostoses, shown in Figure C, is inherited in an autosomal dominant pattern.

Hereditary multiple exostoses is an autosomal dominant condition caused by mutations in EXT family of tumor suppresor genes. Responsible mutations have been found in EXT1, EXT2, and EXT3 genes. Individuals with the EXT1 mutation have a more severe presentation compared to patients with the EXT2 mutation including a higher rate of chondrosarcoma and more exostoses.

*Hecht et al. report that hereditary multiple exostoses (EXT) is an autosomal dominant disorder characterized by the formation of cartilage-capped prominences that develop from the growth centers of the long bones.* **They report** five mutations in the EXT1 gene have been identified including four germ-line mutations, and one somatic mutation. Four of the mutations identified in EXT1 resulted in frameshifts and premature termination codons, while the fifth mutation resulted in a substitution of leucine for arginine.

Figure C shows typical radiographic findings of hereditary multiple exostoses. Notice the sessile (broad base) or pedunculated (narrow stalk) lesions found on the surface of bones. The lesions usually grow away from the involved joint, and the cortex of the lesion is continuous with the cortex of the bone. Illustration A shows the characteristic histology which shows a cartilage cap composed of hyaline cartilage, normal primary trabeculae, and linear clusters of active chondrocytes.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Figure A shows the typical MRI findings associated with bone infarcts. These are associated, in this case, with sickle cell disease. This condition is inherited in an autosomal recessive pattern.
Answer 2: Figure B shows the typical radiographic findings associated with diastrophic dysplasia, specifically the "hitchhiker thumb" deformity. This disease is inherited in an autosomal recessive fashion.
Answer 4: Figure D shows the classic radiographic findings associated with fibular hemimelia. This condition is not associated with a known inheritance pattern.
Answer 5: Figure E shows advanced bony changes in the setting of Ollier's disease. This condition does not have a defined inheritance patter and is sporadic.
