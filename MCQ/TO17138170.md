## Question

A 13-year-old boy presents to the clinic with inability to elevate his right shoulder and flex his elbow. He sustained injury to his shoulder in a RTA 5 months ago. Sensations are decreased along the distribution of axillary nerve. Tinel sign is positive around the clavicle. Horner signs are absent and his arm lies against the body. EMG report shows fibrillation potential in the weak muscles. The plan of management in this boy 5 months after injury with no clinical improvement should be:

## Options

1. Neurotization
2. Continued observation
3. Exploration and nerve grafting
4. Tendon transfers

## Correct option

**3. Exploration and nerve grafting**

## Answer explanation

Neurotization is appropriate in preganglionic lesions. Continued observation will not cause any change. Around 5-6 months is ideal for plexus exploration. Exploration and nerve grafting is the ideal treatment at this stage. Tendon transfers are reconstructive procedures which are done at a later stage

Answer: 3
