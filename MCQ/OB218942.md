## Question

A 36-year-old marble quarry worker presents to the clinic with 9 months of medial-sided knee pain and feelings of his knee giving way while working. Imaging demonstrates isolated medial compartment arthritis with a native posterior tibial slope of 14 degrees. The patient elects to undergo a high tibial osteotomy (HTO), during which the surgeon neglects to address the patient's native tibial slope. What physical exam finding would the patient most likely present to your clinic with post-operatively?

## Options

1. Medial pseudolaxity
2. Positive Lachman test
3. Positive quadriceps active test
4. Posterior sag sign
5. Varus thrust

## Correct option

**2. Positive Lachman test**

## Answer explanation

The patient presents with varus gonarthrosis in the setting of apparent ACL insufficiency with an increased posterior tibial slope of 14 degrees. When performing the HTO, if the surgeon does not decrease the tibial slope in conjunction with the mechanical axis correction, instability will likely remain in the form of increased anterior tibial translation and a positive Lachman on physical exam post-operatively.

Varus malalignment secondary to isolated medial compartment arthritis in the young, active patient has been traditionally addressed with an HTO. The malalignment creates both a constant, static adduction moment during stance phase and a pathologic, dynamic adduction moment during gait. These forces aberrantly load the ACL, as well as the lateral and posterolateral soft tissues, which can lead to attenuation and laxity. In the setting of concomitant ACL insufficiency, this increased load results in increased anterior translation of the tibia relative to the femur and, thus, increased subjective feelings of instability. An increased posterior tibial slope beyond that of a normal knee (usually 9-11 degrees medially and 6-8 degrees laterally) further increases the tendency for anterior tibial translation and instability. Therefore, correcting the mechanical alignment may improve coronal soft tissue balance and limb alignment, but it will not completely address the sagittal instability unless the posterior tibial slope is also addressed.

Herman, et al. provide a review of the use of HTO to treat medial compartment osteoarthritis in the ACL-deficient knee. The authors suggest that the posterior tibial slope should be decreased when it is greater than 13 degrees and is associated with anterior tibial translation of at least 10mm compared to the contralateral knee. They note that for each 1mm of osteotomy closure that is made anteriorly, 2 degrees of decreased slope can be expected.

Noyes, et al. present a series of 41 young patients with ACL deficiency and varus gonarthrosis who were treated with high tibial osteotomy and staged ACL reconstruction. A 100% follow-up was obtained at 4.5 years, with 71% of the cohort demonstrating clinically significant reduction in pain, 85% reduction in feelings of instability, and reduction of the mean adductor moment from 35% higher than controls prior to osteotomy down to below normal values post-operatively.

Incorrect Answers:
1. Medial pseudolaxity is the result of articular cartilage loss in the medial compartment rather than true MCL laxity, and would not be expected on exam after adequate correction of the mechanical axis in the coronal plane.
3. A positive quadriceps active test occurs when a patient with PCL insufficiency attempts to extend a flexed knee at 90 degrees and anteriorly reduces a posteriorly subluxated tibia. There is no indication that this patient has PCL insufficiency.
4. A posterior sag sign occurs in the setting of PCL insufficiency when a patient lies supine with the knees and hips flexed to 90 degrees and the medial tibial plateau, which normally rests 10mm anterior to the medial femoral condyle, is either absent or a posteriorly-directed tibial step-off is palpated. Again, there is no indication that the patient has an insufficient PCL.
5. A varus thrust occurs in the setting of LCL laxity, resulting in bowing out of the knee during gait. This would not be expected after adequate correction of the mechanical axis in the coronal plane.
