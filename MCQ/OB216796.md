## Question

While in clinic, a 26-year-old patient is found to have an osteochondral defect of his medial femoral condyle. On examination, he has a ligamentously stable knee with negative McMurrays test. Mechanical alignment is within normal limits. When discussing treatment options with your attending, which of the following is true regarding the use of an osteochondral allograft in this patient?

## Options

1. Freeze dried grafts are used to retain chondrocyte viability
2. Poor results for larger defects >2 cm x 2cm
3. Replaces cartilage defect with live chondrocytes in mature matrix with underlying bone
4. Uses cell therapy with the goal of forming autologous "hyaline-like" cartilage
5. Utilizes cells that are cultured and embedded in a matrix or scaffold

## Correct option

**3. Replaces cartilage defect with live chondrocytes in mature matrix with underlying bone**

## Answer explanation

Osteochondral allograft transplantation is performed with the goal of replacing cartilage defect with live chondrocytes in mature matrix along with underlying bone.

Articular cartilage defects of the knee comprise of a spectrum of disease entities from a single, focal defects to advanced degenerative disease of articular (hyaline) cartilage. Diagnosis generally requires an MRI to accurately assess the location of specific defects. Treatment can be nonoperative or operative depending on patient age, degree of symptoms and the size of the lesion. Conservative management with rest, NSAIDs, physiotherapy, and weight loss are indicated as first line treatment when symptoms are mild. Surgical management is reserved for defects measuring over 2 x 2 cm that failed nonoperative management, or those with acute osteochondral fractures resulting in full-thickness loss of cartilage.

*Bedi et al. reviews the various treatment modalities for osteochondral defects.* **They report** that transplantation of an osteochondral allograft consisting of intact, viable articular cartilage and its underlying subchondral bone offers the ability to address large osteochondral defects of the knee, including those involving an entire compartment. **They concluded** that the primary theoretical advantage of autologous chondrocyte implantation is the development of hyaline-like cartilage rather than fibrocartilage in the defect, which presumably leads to better long-term outcomes and longevity of the healing tissue.

*Krych et al. reviewed return to sport and outcomes following various cartilage restoration techniques.* **They report** that cartilage restoration surgery had a 76% return to sport at mid-term follow-up. **They concluded** that osteochondral autograft transfer offered a faster recovery and higher rate of return to preinjury athletics.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Fresh, refrigerated grafts are used which retain chondrocyte viability
Answer 2: Poor results for larger defects >2 cm x 2cm with marrow stimulation techniques
Answer 4: Autologous chondrocyte implantation (ACI) uses cell therapy with goal of forming autologous "hyaline-like" cartilage
Answer 5: Matrix-associated autologous chondrocyte implantation (MACI) uses cells are cultured and embedded in a matrix or scaffold
