## Question

Figures A and B are anteroposterior and lateral standing stereoradiographic images of a 55-year-old male with chronic back pain and gait abnormality. Surgical correction of which parameter will be most important for improving his health-related quality of life (HRQOL) scores?

![[q3731-a-balance-coronal.jpg]]
*Figure A*

![[q3731-b-balance-sagittal.jpg]]
*Figure B*

## Options

1. Apical vertebral rotation
2. C7 sagittal vertical axis
3. Coronal cobb angle
4. Pelvic incidence
5. Coronal plumb line

## Correct option

**2. C7 sagittal vertical axis**

## Answer explanation

Correction of the patient's sagittal balance is most important for improving HRQOL. Sagittal balance can be quantified using the C7 sagittal vertical axis (C7 SVA).

The patient presents with compensated sagittal spinal deformity (SSD). Patients with SSD compensate for their deformity in order to maintain balance and forward gaze. Mechanisms of compensation include posterior tilt of the pelvis, hypokyphosis of the thoracic spine, hyperlordosis of the cervical spine, and posterior shift of the pelvis through knee flexion. By compensating in these ways, patients with regional spine deformities may present with apparently normal C7 SVA (less than 5cm as measured from the posterosuperior aspect of the S1 body). For this reason, regional imbalance should be investigated in patients who present with gait abnormality and evidence of compensation despite normal plumb line values.

*Glassman et al. reviewed 298 adult scoliosis patients to correlate radiographic deformity measures with patient outcomes.* *Among 172 patients who had no prior spine surgery and 126 who had prior spinal fusion, positive sagittal balance was the most reliable predictor of symptomatic scoliosis.* **The authors concluded** that restoration of sagittal balance is a critical goal of any surgery.

*Glassman et al. evaluated the importance of positive sagittal balance in 752 patients with adult spinal deformity.* **The authors found** that all measures of health status diminished as C7 plumb line deviation increased. In the setting of relative lumbar kyphosis, disability also increased.

Figures A and B are full-body AP and lateral stereoradiographs of a skeletally mature adult with spinal deformity. Figure B suggests compensated sagittal malalignment as evidenced by excessive knee flexion in an effort to maintain an upright posture.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1, 3, 4, and 5 are all necessary components of a deformity correction strategy but have not been as closely correlated to HRQOL as a sagittal imbalance.
