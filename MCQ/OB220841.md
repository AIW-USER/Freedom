## Question

## Options

4. C6-8

## Correct option

**5. C8-T1**

## Answer explanation

This patient suffered a flexion-type supracondylar humerus fracture. Ulnar nerve injuries are the most common nerve injuries associated with flexion-type supracondylar humerus fractures. The ulnar nerve originates from the C8-T1 nerve roots (answer choice 5).

A supracondylar humerus fracture (SCHF) is the most common elbow fracture in children, typically occurring between ages 5 and 7. The vast majority are extension-type injuries, which result from a fall on an outstretched hand and cause posterior displacement of the distal fragment; these are most often associated with median nerve or anterior interosseous nerve injuries, and carry a risk of brachial artery compromise. In contrast, flexion-type supracondylar fractures are rare, accounting for only about 5% of cases, and occur after a direct fall on a flexed elbow. In this pattern, the distal fragment is displaced anteriorly, and the ulnar nerve is at the highest risk of injury due to its position posterior and medial to the distal humerus. Nerve injuries are usually neuropraxias and often recover spontaneously, but their identification is critical on initial exam. Extension-type injuries are generally managed with closed reduction and percutaneous pinning, while flexion-type fractures are often more unstable and frequently require open reduction.

*Babal et al. performed a meta-analysis of nerve injuries associated with pediatric supracondylar humerus fractures, reviewing over 5000 cases from the literature.* **They found** that approximately 11% of these fractures are complicated by nerve injury, with the anterior interosseous nerve (AIN) being the most commonly affected overall. Extension-type fractures were most frequently associated with median/AIN and radial nerve injuries, while flexion-type fractures demonstrated a strong association with ulnar nerve injury (91%). **The authors concluded** that recognizing the typical fracture pattern–nerve injury associations can guide focused neurologic examination and improve clinical vigilance.

*Dormans et al. reviewed acute neurovascular complications associated with SCHFs,* **emphasizing** their frequency, patterns, and management. **They found** that vascular compromise and nerve injuries are not uncommon in these fractures, with the brachial artery and median/AIN most often affected in extension-type injuries, and the ulnar nerve more commonly involved in flexion-type injuries. The authors stressed the importance of careful initial neurovascular examination, as deficits may be present at the time of injury or develop after reduction. **They concluded** that prompt recognition and management of these complications is critical to avoid long-term functional impairment.

Figures A and B are AP and lateral radiographs of a left elbow demonstrating a flexion-type supracondylar humerus fracture in a skeletally immature individual.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer choice 1: The median nerve and the radial nerve both originate from C5-T1 roots. Both of these nerves are at risk with extension-type supracondylar humerus fractures. The median/AIN nerve is the most commonly affected with extension-type fractures, followed by the radial nerve. In flexion-type fractures, however, the ulnar nerve is most commonly affected.
Answer choice 2: C5-7 nerve roots supply the musculocutaneous nerve. The musculocutaneous nerve is much less commonly injured in flexion-type supracondylar humerus fractures than the ulnar nerve.
Answer choice 3: C5-6 nerve roots are the roots for multiple nerves, most notably the axillary nerve, which would not be at risk with this fracture, given the proximal nature of the axillary nerve.
Answer choice 4: C6-8 nerve roots are the roots for the thoracodorsal nerve, which supplies the latissimus dorsi muscle. The thoracodorsal nerve is not at risk with a flexion-type supracondylar humerus fracture.
