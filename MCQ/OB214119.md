## Question

Which of the following patients is most likely to develop an infection following shoulder arthroplasty?

## Options

1. 73-year-old female with insulin dependent diabetes following a reverse total shoulder arthroplasty
2. 61-year-old obese male following a reverse total shoulder arthroplasty
3. 35-year-old female with Rheumatoid Arthritis following a total shoulder arthroplasty
4. 45-year-old male following a reverse total shoulder arthroplasty
5. 43-year-old obese female following a total shoulder arthroplasty

## Correct option

**4. 45-year-old male following a reverse total shoulder arthroplasty**

## Answer explanation

The greatest risks for infection in shoulder arthroplasty are younger age and male and a primary reverse has a greater risk of infection than primary total shoulder arthroplasty.

Reverse Total shoulder arthroplasty (RTSA) uses a concave humeral cup to articulate with a hemispherical glenoid effectively moving the center of rotation inferior and medial. While a RTSA has been increasingly performed with increasing indications, there are also complications that are not insignificant. One of those being infections that occur in 1-2% of cases and with the most common organisms being Staph and C. Acnes. The greatest risk factors for infection are younger age and male sex. Other risks are a primary reverse TSA and arthroplasty secondary to trauma. The factors of BMI, race and ASA score, were not found to increase the risk of infection.

*Morris et al. looked at 301 primary RTSA with a minimum of 1-year follow-up for risk factors for infection.* **They found** that patients with a history of a RTSA for a failed arthroplasty and patients younger than 65 years old had a greater risk of infection. There was no increased infection risk with smoking, diabetes, rheumatoid arthritis, or obesity. **They concluded** that the greatest risk factors for infection are age younger than 65 and a history of a prior failed arthroplasty.

*Richards et al. retrospectively reviewed 3,906 patients over a mean follow-up of 2.7 years following shoulder arthroplasty for patient risk-factors for deep infection and pathogen profile.* **They found** that for every 1-year increase in age there was a 5% lower risk of infection, males had a 2.59 times greater risk of infection, primary reverse TSA had a 6.11 times greater risk of infection, and arthroplasty for traumatic reasons had a 2.98 times greater risk of infection. **They concluded** that younger, male patients are at the greatest risk of infection and that C. acnes was the most prevalent pathogen.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers, 1, 2, 3, 5: The greatest risk factors for infection have been found to be male, younger age, RTSA, and arthroplasty for traumatic reasons. Obesity, smoking, rheumatoid arthritis, and ASA class were not associated with an increased risk of infection.
