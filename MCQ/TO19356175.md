## Question

False statement regarding THR in a patient with Ankylosing Spondylitis [FNB 2023]

## Options

1. More blood loss and higher risk of infection
2. Difficult reaming
3. Decrease inclination to reduce dislocation
4. Decrease anteversion to reduce dislocation

## Correct option

**2. Difficult reaming**

## Answer explanation

Ans: B

EXPLANATION: -
Preparation of the femur in inflammatory arthritis like ankylosing spondylitis, rheumatoid arthritis usually is easy because the canal is wide, but the cortex is thin and easily penetrated or fractured.
Progressive spine stiffness associated with stiff hips in ankylosing spondylitis (AS) results in mobility restriction and reduces the quality of life in young individuals. Sacral slope change is reduced (< 10 degrees) with a predominant stuck sitting pattern and posterior pelvic tilt. Care must be exercised to reduce acetabular anteversion preventing posterior impingement and anterior dislocation. Dual mobility hips are recommended.
The surgical approach for THA should ensure the release of the tight capsular tissue and soft tissue to enable full correction at THA. The flexion deformity would require a comprehensive anterior release while the abduction extension deformities would require the release of the lateral structures including the iIliotibial band.
THA with cementless fixation would achieve a good outcome however, cemented fixation would be required in hips with capacious femoral (Dorr C) and poor bone stock.
Risks at THA in AS include intraoperative fractures, postop dislocation and heterotopic ossification.
In addition, they have been or are receiving corticosteroids and other immunosuppressive drugs; consequently, the risks of fracture during surgery and infection after surgery are greater. Multiple studies have demonstrated higher blood loss in THR in active ankylosing spondylitis.
When operations on the hip and the knee are indicated, Total knee replacement can be technically difficult in the presence of a markedly stiff arthritic hip joint. Conversely, a severe flexion contracture of the knee may predispose to dislocation of a total hip replacement. If involvement is equal, the hip arthroplasty probably should be done first.
Concerning a concurrent spine deformity, although there is no fixed guideline, the dictum is- that when both spine and hip deformity fall in operative range then spine should be fixed first. Version of cup then can be most suitably adjusted.
