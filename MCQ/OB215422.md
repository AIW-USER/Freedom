## Question

Which combination correctly matches the name and function of the structure indicated by the arrows in Figure A?

![[q215422-a-peroneus-longus-insertion.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Flexor hallucis longus; plantarflexion of great toe
2. Peroneus brevis; eversion of foot
3. Peroneus longus; eversion of foot
4. Flexor hallucis longus; inversion of foot
5. Peroneus longus; dorsiflexion of 1st ray

## Correct option

**3. Peroneus longus; eversion of foot**

## Answer explanation

The peroneus longus inserts on the plantar aspect of the first metatarsal base and the medial cuneiform and functions to evert the foot and plantarflex the first ray.

The peroneus longus tendon lies in the lateral compartment of the leg and originates from the proximal lateral fibula, inter-muscular septum, and lateral tibia. The long, tendinous portion lies posterior to the peroneus brevis in the fibular retro-malleolar groove. Its function antagonizes the tibialis anterior and is also important in the maintenance of the foot’s transverse arch. The peroneus longus tendon and associated pathology are best demonstrated on MRI.

*Choplin et al. provide an overview of three-dimensional (3D) CT scans used in the evaluation of foot and ankle tendons.* **They note** that fine anatomic details can be lost with 3D renderings and that the quality of imaging is dependent on CT technique and the operating radiologist. **They conclude** that, while 3D CT may display high-resolution tendinous structures of the foot and ankle, it should not be used in isolation and MRI remains the imaging technique of choice for muscle and tendon pathology due to its high soft-tissue contrast.

*Clarke et al. review diagnosis and imaging of peroneal tendon injuries.* **They discuss** that plain radiographs may indicate a peroneus longus tear when there is displacement or fracture of the os peroneum. With respect to CT, **the authors note** that it can be helpful in identifying peroneal tendon injuries even though it can be challenging to differentiate tendons from adjacent soft tissues. **The authors conclude** that MRI has excellent soft-tissue contrast and is the optimal imaging choice for visualization of partial intra-tendinous lesions.

Figure A depicts a sagittal MRI cut showing the peroneus longus insertion onto the base of the 1st metatarsal.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1,4: The flexor hallucis longus tendon inserts on the plantar aspect of the distal phalanx and functions as the primary flexor of the great toe; it does not play a role in inversion of the foot.
Answer 2: The peroneus brevis tendon inserts laterally at the base of the 5th metatarsal and functions to evert the foot.
Answer 5: As described above, the peroneus longus inserts on the plantar aspect of the first metatarsal base and medial cuneiform and functions to evert the foot and plantarflex, not dorsiflex, the first ray.
