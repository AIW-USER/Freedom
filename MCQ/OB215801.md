## Question

An 82-year-old female presents to your office after tripping off a curb and landing on her right side two days prior. She reports right thigh pain and a mild headache. Radiographs are obtained and normal. The patient reports being on therapy for 8 years and on chronic corticosteroids for the last 9 years. Her most recent t-score was -2.6. Which of the following is the most appropriate recommendation as it relates to her bisphosphonate treatment?

## Options

1. Continue as previously prescribed, no drug holiday
2. Discontinue for 6 months, then restart at double the dose
3. Discontinue for 6 months, then restart at same dose
4. Discontinue for 12 months, then restart at double the dose
5. Discontinue for 12 months, then restart at same dose

## Correct option

**1. Continue as previously prescribed, no drug holiday**

## Answer explanation

While a drug holiday is recommended for some patients, those who are at high risk for fracture, with either declining bone mineral density (BMD) or severe osteoporosis, should not be considered for a drug holiday.

Bisphosphonates have been shown to prevent osteoporotic fractures. They suppress osteoclastic recruitment and activity and induce apoptosis of osteoclasts. However, they have also been associated with subtrochanteric femur fractures. These fractures are typically subtrochanteric in nature, transverse, and have a large medial spike. While the timing and duration of a drug holiday are debated, it is clear, however, that patients who are at high risk for fracture, with either declining bone mineral density (BMD), severe osteoporosis, or those on chronic corticosteroids, should not be considered for a drug holiday in the first 10 years. Following 10 years of treatment, many authors recommend a drug holiday of 1-2 years or until the patient has a significant decrease in BMD or a fracture, whichever comes first.

*Watts et al. review the long-term use of bisphosphonates in osteoporosis.* **They report** that because bisphosphonates accumulate in the bone, there is a residual anti-fracture effect even after treatment is stopped. **They conclude** that patients at mild risk might stop treatment after 5 years and remain off as long as bone mineral density is stable and no fractures occur. Higher-risk patients should be treated for 10 years, have a holiday of no more than a year or two, and perhaps be on a non-bisphosphonate treatment during that time.

*Diab et al. review bisphosphonate drug holidays.* **They report** that patients who are high risk (ie. chronic corticosteroid use) should be kept on bisphosphonate therapy for 10 years, followed by a 1-2 year holiday or until the patient sustains a fracture or has a significant drop in bone mineral density, whichever comes first. **They conclude** that the strength of the evidence for fracture reduction in high-risk individuals and the rarity of long-term adverse effects indicate that the benefits of continued treatment outweigh the risks in individuals at high risk of fracture.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2-5: Bisphosphonate drug holidays are not recommended before 10 years in patients at high risk for osteoporotic related fracture.
