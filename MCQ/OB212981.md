## Options

1. Intercalary allograft reconstuction
2. Endoprosthetic reconstruction
3. Amputation
4. Van Nes rotationplasty
5. Transepiphyseal resection with bone transport

## Correct option

**4. Van Nes rotationplasty**

## Answer explanation

The patient is presenting with osteosarcoma of the distal femur with epiphyseal involvement. The most appropriate reconstruction option that provides the highest self-reported outcomes and lower revision rates for this patient is the Van Nes rotationplasty.

Osteosarcoma in pediatric patient poses treatment challenges with limb salvage approaches. Endoprosthetic reconstruction does have a high degree of success but comes with high reoperation rates to address loose prostheses and addressing limb length discrepancy (LLD) as patients age. Rotationplasty involves resection of the affected femur with the fusion of the tibia to the femoral stump in 180 degrees. The tibiotalar joint functionally becomes the knee and provides a highly functional limb to fit with a prosthesis. The nature of the procedure avoids LLD challenges. Preservation of the sciatic nerve is crucial to the success of this procedure.

*Hillman et al. compared Van Nes rotationplasty to endoprosthetic reconstruction with regards to patient-reported outcomes in pediatric patients treated for osteogenic sarcoma.* **The authors found** that both treatment options were equivalent regarding most outcomes but rotationplasty required less external ambulatory support, better role functioning scores, and less restriction of daily activities due to pain. **The authors concluded** that, despite the equivalent, if not better results with rotationplasty, patients may be deterred due to the odd appearance of the procedure.

*Levin et al. reviews limb reconstruction following tumor resection in pediatric patients.* **The authors reported** that patients undergoing rotationplasty had lower reoperation rates than endoprosthetic reconstruction with quality of life scores similar to the general population. Despite the odd appearance of the rotated extremity, **the authors suggest** this procedure to be equivalent or superior to limb salvage in many cases.

Figures A and B are the AP and lateral radiographs of the left knee demonstrating a lytic lesion in the lateral metaphysis with a wide zone of transition and periosteal elevation. Figure C demonstrates a coronal T1 MRI slice with a hypointense lesion involving the lateral metaphysis and lateral epiphysis. Figure D demonstrates a histological section of osteosarcoma with a matrix of osteoid and dysplastic osteocytes. Illustration A depicts a clinical photograph of a patient with a rotationplasty of the right lower extremity. Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: An intercalary allograft reconstruction is a potential option as there is involvement of the distal femur. Allograft reconstruction options would require the use of distal femoral allograft which has a high fracture and infection rate.
Answer 2: Endoprosthetic reconstruction has been shown to have high success rates in pediatric limb salvage, but in this highly active and young patient population there is an increased potential of prosthetic loosening requiring reoperation.
Answer 3: Amputation is not preferred as initial therapy as there is an increased oxygen requirement with ambulation as well as patient self-image. This procedure should be done as a salvage option.
Answer 5: Transepiphyseal resection with bone transport is a relatively new limb salvage option that preserves the articular surface and maintains limb length, with excellent outcomes in select patients. However, this is not a viable option given that there is epiphyseal involvement in this patient.
