## Question

A 35-year-old purple belt Jiu-Jitsu martial artist presents to the Emergency Department immediately after sustaining the injury depicted in Figure A. While attempting a double ankle grab sweep the black belt gym owner skillfully placed him in a guillotine choke causing the aforementioned injury. What is the best next step in the Emergency Department?

![[q215480-a-img-0778.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Compressive dressings and primary care referral
2. Formal surgical decompression
3. Otoscopic evaluation
4. Urgent needle decompression
5. Head MRI

## Correct option

**3. Otoscopic evaluation**

## Answer explanation

This patient is presenting with an acute auricular hematoma and the first step in management is to perform a thorough otoscopic evaluation.

Auricular hematomas result from blunt shearing forces to the ear that disrupt the tightly adherent perichondrium to the underlying cartilage. Classically, these injuries are secondary to sports trauma, most commonly in boxing, wrestling, and martial arts. Successful auricular hematoma treatment involves early recognition, early drainage, and prevention of reaccumulation, however, it is important to first assess for other concomitant intra-auricular pathologies via an otoscopic evaluation. If this hematoma is left untreated, abnormal cartilage and fibrous tissue development can occur leading to a cosmetic deformity known as a ‘cauliflower ear’.

*Greywoode et al review the management of auricular hematomas and stress the importance of evaluating patients with an otoscopic examination.* Blunt ear trauma frequently results in tympanic membrane perforation, which may require further assessment and treatment by an otolaryngologist. Ultimate treatment goals include urgent drainage and prevention of reaccumulation as untreated hematomas can result in complications such as perichondritis, infection, and necrosis.

*Cassaday et al discuss the initial evaluation of the athletes who have experienced auricular trauma and outline the necessity of performing a complete examination to rule out any life-threatening injuries, such as a skull fracture.* In addition, patients must undergo an otoscopic examination for evaluation of a possible tympanic membrane rupture.

Figure A is a clinical photo of an auricular hematoma.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Compressive dressings would be appropriate after decompression of the hematoma to prevent recurrence.
Answer 2: Formal surgical decompression with hematoma evacuation is used in recurrent auricular hematomas or those presenting in a delayed fashion.
Answer 4: While urgent needle decompression is the standard treatment for these acute injuries, it should not precede an otologic examination.
Answer 5: MRI of the patient's head would not be appropriate as the first step in management. Plain radiographs and/or CT head may be warranted if there is a suspected skull fracture or intracranial hemorrhage.
