## Question

A 70 years old male is to undergoing Revision THR. Bone stock on X ray seems very poor in the metaphyseal and diaphyseal region of femur especially, hence, you plan to go with Impaction Bone Grafting during the surgery. Incorrect statement regarding the technique would be

## Options

1. Stem used should be tapered fluted, collarless and made of flexible titanium with a roughened surface
2. In this technique particulate allograft bone is impacted into endosteal canal
3. Mechanical loading forces are transmitted as compression forces upon allograft bone
4. Some sought of subsidence is always expected after impaction bone grafting

## Correct option

**1. Stem used should be tapered fluted, collarless and made of flexible titanium with a roughened surface**

## Answer explanation

Ans: A
EXPLANATION: -
Impaction bone grafting was described by Hastings and Parker in 1975 and popularized thereafter by the Nijmegen group in the Netherlands led by Slooff et al and in the United Kingdom by the Exeter Group.
Impaction bone grafting is an established reconstruction technique to address bone loss in revision total hip arthroplasty where basically the missing bone is replaced with bone (allograft). Impaction bone grafting of the acetabulum involves packing of cavitary defects with compressed particulate graft (mostly allograft), followed by insertion of either a cemented or cementless acetabular component. Impaction grafting of the femur involves retrograde filling of the femoral canal with impacted particulate graft, creating a neomedullary canal into which a cemented femoral stem can be placed.
Technique description: Cancellous allograft bone chips of 1 mm to 2 mm size are used, and tapered into the canal with rods of increasing diameters. To impact the bone chips into the femoral canal a prosthesis dummy of the same dimensions of the definitive cemented stem is driven into the femur to ensure that the chips are very firmly impacted.
Ideal indication: for the Paprosky 3B femurs in young patients where proximal restoration of bone is desired or if distal fixation is not ideal or 4 type femurs where there is a lack of proximal bone stock and there is compromise of the isthmus.
Pros and Cons: Use of the impaction allografting technique is appealing, especially in young patients, because of its potential to restore bone stock. The technically demanding nature of the procedure, the risk of complications [dislocation (4.1%) or femoral fracture (5.4%)], and the unknown long-term fate of the impacted allograft are the usual concerns.
Additional points: -
Gie et al. described a technique for management of proximal femoral deficiencies that combines impaction bone grafting with a cemented revision stem. As per the study, some take home points: -
• Impacted morselized allograft bone is used to reconstruct both cavitary and segmental bone defects. In uncontained defects, meshes or graft struts may be used for containment.
• A small amount of subsidence is to be expected and is consistent with the intended subsidence within the cement mantle.
• There is no consensus as to if a particular type of stem can only be used for impaction grafting. Collarless polished stems and collared stems with various finishes have been used successfully with impaction grafting in multiple studies. Rather one must say that Impaction bone grafting allows for the use of a shorter cemented stem during revision surgeries which otherwise need long stems.
• Impaction grafting requires specialized instrumentation and the availability of large amounts of cancellous bone graft.
• Fresh-frozen allograft has osteoinductive properties, and the act of impaction may contribute to the process of remodelling and incorporation of bone.
