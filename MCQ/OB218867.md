## Question

A 17-year-old male presents to clinic with knee pain and swelling for 6 months. He is a starting high school catcher with aspirations of playing at the collegiate level. MRI confirms the diagnosis of osteochondritis dissecans. Where would the lesion most likely be located?

## Options

1. Anterior aspect of lateral femoral condyle
2. Posterolateral tibial plateau
3. Posterior femoral condyle
4. Inferomedial patellar facet
5. Femoral trochlear groove

## Correct option

**3. Posterior femoral condyle**

## Answer explanation

Osteochondritis dissecans (OCD) more commonly involves the posterior femoral condyle in catchers compared to position players, likely due to the repetitive and persistent loading of the knees in a hyper-flexed position.

OCD of the knee is a common cause of knee pain and dysfunction among skeletally immature and young-adult patients. Overall, OCD lesions of the knee most commonly involve the posterolateral aspect of the medial femoral condyle. Stable lesions in skeletally immature patients should initially be managed non-operatively. Unstable juvenile lesions (fluid or cyst underneath lesion), as well as, stable lesions that fail to heal with non-operative treatment require surgical treatment.

*McElroy et al retrospectively reviewed 78 patients with OCD to study the relative age and characteristics of OCD knee lesions in catchers compared with position players.* **They found** that catchers developed OCD at a younger age and in a more posterior location on the femoral condyles than non-catchers.

*Flynn et al provide a review of the etiology, clinical presentation, diagnostic evaluation, nonoperative, and operative treatments and outcomes of knee OCD.* Nonoperative management is indicated for stable lesions in skeletally immature patients. Operative treatment is indicated for unstable lesions and failure of nonoperative management.

Incorrect Answers
Answer 1: More commonly associated with ACL injuries
Answer 2: More commonly associated with ACL injuries
Answer 4-5: The patella and trochlear groove are not frequently involved.
