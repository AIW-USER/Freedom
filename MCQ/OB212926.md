## Question

A 10-year-old boy stepped on a nail approximately 1 week ago. He currently complains of heel pain and is having difficulty ambulating. On physical examination, he has erythema and tenderness to palpation over his calcaneal tuberosity. Current imaging is shown in Figures A & B. ## Options

1. It may be treated with a ceftazidime
2. It may be treated with sulfamethoxazole-trimethoprim
3. It may be treated with cephalexin
4. It may be treated with amoxicillin-clavulanate
5. It may be treated with ampicillin-sulbactam

## Correct option

**1. It may be treated with a ceftazidime**

## Answer explanation

This patient has calcaneal osteomyelitis. The offending organism is likely pseudomonas aeruginosa, which can be treated with ceftazidime.

Osteomyelitis of the calcaneus may be treated with IV antibiotics and debridement. P. aeruginosa is a very commonly implicated organism in infections after a penetrating injury through a shoe. Staphylococcus aureus can be the causative organism as well; however, it more commonly causes a soft tissue infection only. A fluoroquinolone is typically the antibiotic of choice for treating a P. aeruginosa infection to the calcaneus except in children. In those cases, ceftazidime or cefepime are preferred.

*Laughlin et al. performed a review of calcaneal osteomyelitis caused by nail puncture wounds.* **They report** that, if a puncture wound is suspected, radiographs should be obtained to look for foreign bodies. Patients should also be placed on empiric antibiotics. The most commonly encountered organism was P. aeruginosa, which has been attributed to the local environmental factors in shoes.

*Eidelman et al. produced a retrospective review of 80 children with plantar puncture wounds.* **They found** that the most common organisms causing soft tissue infection were S. aureus or Group A streptococcus. **They recommend** that patients with an established infection 24-36 hours after a plantar puncture should be admitted to hospital for parenteral antibiotic therapy, and that relapse after initial improvement suggests the presence of osteomyelitis or a retained foreign body.

Figure A is a lateral radiograph of the ankle demonstrating no obvious osseous pathology. Figure B is a T1-weighted MRI slice demonstrating osteomyelitis of the calcaneus.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Sulfamethoxazole-trimethoprim may be used to treat infections with MRSA.
Answer 3: Cephalexin may be used for superficial skin infections due to Gram-positive organisms.
Answer 4: Amoxicillin-clavulanate may be used to treat plantar puncture wounds that only involve the soft tissues. This antibiotic treats S. aureus and beta-hemolytic streptococci well.
Answer 5: Ampicillin-sulbactam is commonly used to treat polymicrobial infections due to a human bite.
