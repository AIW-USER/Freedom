## Question

A 23-year-old active duty soldier is referred to your clinic. He notes that four years ago during basic training he sustained an ankle sprain, and has had multiple recurrent sprains since. On physical examination, you note fullness about the ankle, and he states that he has pain when walking on uneven or soft terrain. There is increased laxity when compared to the contralateral side with ankle drawer testing in dorsiflexion, but not in plantarflexion. Attenuation or injury to which of the labeled structures in Figure A is most likely responsible for your physical examination findings?

![[q218559-a-ankleligs.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. A
2. B
3. C
4. D
5. E

## Correct option

**5. E**

## Answer explanation

This active-duty soldier has recurrent functional ankle instability. His anterior drawer testing demonstrates laxity with a dorsiflexed foot, indicating injury to the calcaneofibular ligament (CFL) (labeled E).

Ankle sprains are a common injury and are a leading cause of missed athletic participation among athletes. There are various patient-related and environmental-related risk factors for these injuries, and ankle sprains are the most common injury sustained by dancers. Ankle sprains can be divided into high ankle sprains, in which the syndesmotic ligaments are injured, and low ankle sprains, in which the anterior talofibular ligament (ATFL), calcaneofibular ligament (CFL), or both are injured. The first-line treatment for ankle sprains in most patients is rest, ice, compression, and elevation for acute symptomatic management, with physiotherapy focusing on proprioception to follow. Surgical intervention is reserved for patients with recalcitrant functional ankle instability, or high-level athletes.

*Bahr et al. performed a biomechanical cadaveric study investigating the contribution of the various ankle ligaments to the anterior drawer and talar tilt tests.* *Their study included 8 cadaveric specimens.* **The authors found** that with the foot in plantar flexion, the ATFL was subjected to the highest degree of force during drawer testing. On the other hand, the CFL was subjected to the highest force with the foot in dorsiflexion. **The authors conclude** that injury to these two ligaments can be selectively identified by varying the foot position during this physical examination maneuver.

*Fujii et al. performed a similar cadaveric study investigating the stability of the ankle with various laboratory-generated ligamentous injuries.* **The authors found** that transection of the ATFL caused a significant increase in internal rotation at the tibiotalar joint, a finding not seen in isolated CFL injury. However, **the authors found** that isolated CFL transection did cause a significant increase in motion during inversion (talar tilt) testing. **The authors conclude** that isolated ATFL injury was clearly identified with increased talar-tibial internal rotation, but not inversion.

Figure A is an illustration of the lateral ankle ligaments. A is the posterior tibiofibular ligament, B is the anterior tibiofibular ligament, C is the posterior talofibular ligament, D is the anterior talofibular ligament and E is the calcaneofibular ligament.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1 and 2: The posterior (labeled A) and anterior (labeled B) tibiofibular ligaments help to make up the ankle syndesmosis. Injury to these ligaments will not cause laxity with anterior drawer testing.
Answer 3: The posterior talofibular ligament is not tested with anterior drawer testing.
Answer 4: The anterior talofibular ligament is tested by performing the anterior drawer test with the foot in plantarflexion, not dorsiflexion.
