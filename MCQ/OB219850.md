## Question

A 23-year-old male presents to the emergency department after an assault which resulted in lacerations over his right antecubital fossa and torso. On exam, the patient has greater than 10 mm two-point discrimination along the thumb, index, and long finger with 4 mm two-point discrimination along the small and ulnar half of the ring finger. He cannot flex the thumb at the interphalangeal joint or abduct the index finger. He can abduct the small finger and extend the thumb interphalangeal joint. Which anatomical variant explains the physical exam findings in this patient?

## Options

1. Martin-Gruber anastomosis
2. Marinacci anastomosis
3. Riche-Cannieu anastomosis
4. Berrettini anastomosis
5. Anconeous epitrochlearis

## Correct option

**1. Martin-Gruber anastomosis**

## Answer explanation

This patient presents with a laceration over the antecubital fossa, and physical exam findings supportive of a median nerve injury. However, the patient also has no motor function to the first dorsal interossei, indicating a Martin-Gruber type II anastomosis (MGA II).

Median and ulnar nerve interconnections can present with irregular physical exams and electrodiagnostic findings. There are four main interconnections reported between the median and ulnar nerve. Martin-Gruber and Marinacci anastomose in the forearm, while Riche-Cannieu and Berettini anastomose in the hand. The Martin-Gruber occurs between either the anterior interosseous nerve (AIN) or median nerve proximally, with a connection to the ulnar nerve distally. MGA II is the most common variant, in which motor fibers from the AIN or median nerve travel with the ulnar nerve and innervate the first dorsal interosseous muscle. Laceration of the median nerves in a patient with MGA II will present with classic median nerve sensory and motor dysfunction as well as the inability to abduct the index finger, with preserved ulnar nerve sensation.

*Martin et al. published a review article on the 4 interconnections mentioned above between the median and ulnar nerve.* *The review article discusses the anatomy, physical exam, and electrodiagnostic studies associated with each anastomosis.* **The authors note** that an MGA II typically presents with a normal nerve conduction study. This is because the first dorsal interossei muscle is not routinely tested during a nerve conduction study. **The authors highlight** the necessity of a thorough physical exam when evaluating patients.

*Roy et al. published a meta-analysis regarding median and ulnar nerve anastomoses.* *A total of 10,562 upper limbs were included in the meta-analysis.* Martin-Gruber anastomosis had a pooled prevalence of 19.5%, with 57.6% originating from the AIN. **The authors recommend** a thorough exam and critical interpretation of electrophysiologic findings before surgical exploration.

Illustrations A-D show the following anastomoses in order: Martin-Gruber, Marinacci, Richi-Cannieu, and Berettini.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 2: A Marinacci anastomosis travels from the ulnar nerve to the median nerve in the forearm, and it presents paradoxical preservation of the thenar function in this clinical picture.
Answer 3: Riche-Cannieu anastomosis is a communication of motor fibers from the deep branch of the ulnar nerve in the hand to the thenar compartment. This anastomosis would present with paradoxical preserved thenar function.
Answer 4: Berrettini anastomosis is a sensory interconnection between the hand's ulnar and median nerve and would not present with abnormal motor function.
Answer 5: Anconeous epitrochlearis can present with compression of the ulnar nerve at the cubital tunnel, which would have combined sensory and motor deficits in the ulnar nerve.
