## Question

A 55-year-old male presents to your office with complaints of right foot pain and plantar ulceration. On evaluation, you note the ulcer to be superficial at the level of the midfoot. He reports having a history of type 2 diabetes with a Hgb A1c of 9.4%. You recommend conservative management with total contact casting (TCC). Which of the following is true regarding the management of diabetic foot ulcers with TCC?

## Options

1. Ulcers are at greatest risk for recurring within 1 month of total contact cast discontinuation
2. Should be replaced weekly in the setting of active infection
3. Duration of casting should be no longer than 4 weeks
4. Should not be used in patients with insensate feet due to risk for further ulceration
5. Custom shoe insoles are equally as effective in treating plantar ulcers

## Correct option

**1. Ulcers are at greatest risk for recurring within 1 month of total contact cast discontinuation**

## Answer explanation

After total contact casting (TCC), ulcers most commonly recur within the first 3-4 weeks because the healing tissue is still vulnerable to the vertical and shear stresses of ambulation.

Total contact casting (TCC) is an effective therapy for healing chronic neuropathic plantar ulcers in individuals with diabetes mellitus and other chronic sensory neuropathies. The use of TCC in the clinical management of neuropathic wounds has gained increasing acceptance over the past 30 years. Total contact casts are snug-fitting, below-knee casts that protect insensate limbs from repetitive trauma, promote ulcer healing, and allow the patient to remain ambulatory. The first month or so after discontinuation is a particularly vulnerable period for re-ulceration because the newly healed ulcer is not fully mature and is not yet able to withstand the vertical and shear stresses placed on it. Extreme caution is recommended during this period to allow complete wound consolidation and connective tissue remodeling.

*Sinacore et al. report on total contact casting for diabetic neuropathic ulcers and* **note** that skilled application and careful follow-up of the wound are necessary to avoid complications and minimize the risks for re-ulceration. **They report** that successful treatment of neuropathic ulcers using TCC results in a lower incidence of infection, hospitalization, and lost income in patients with chronic sensory neuropathies. **They highlight** the importance of close monitoring within the first several months after discontinuation of TCC, as this is the period of most vulnerability for re-ulceration.

*Mueller et al performed a randomized controlled trial on Achilles lengthening versus TCC in recalcitrant diabetic neuropathic ulcers, and* **they found** that twenty-nine (88%) of thirty-three ulcers in the total-contact cast group and all thirty ulcers (100%) in the Achilles tendon lengthening group healed after 40-60 days (no differences between groups). However, 81% of the TCC group and 38% of the Achilles lengthening group had a recurrence of the ulcer at two-year follow-up.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: TCC is contraindicated in the setting of active infection as the infection can not be effectively visualized and treated.
Answer 3: Most of the time, several months of TCC is required for complete healing of the ulcer.
Answer 4: One of the indications for TCC is a plantar ulcer in diabetic patients with an insensate foot.
Answer 5: Custom orthotics are typically used to prevent ulceration, compared to a TCC which is used to treat the ulcers after they have formed.
