Chapter: Musculoskeletal Infections MCQs — MDR:XDR Osteoarticular TB

## Question

In the modern 'All-Oral' Longer Regimen for MDR-Osteoarticular TB, which of the following combinations represents the prioritized 'Group A' drugs that should be included in the initial phase of treatment?

## Options

1. Bedaquiline, Linezolid, and a Fluoroquinolone (Levofloxacin/Moxifloxacin).
2. Amikacin, Ethionamide, and Cycloserine.
3. Pyrazinamide, Ethambutol, and Streptomycin
4. Rifampicin, Isoniazid, and Bedaquiline

## Correct option

**1. Bedaquiline, Linezolid, and a Fluoroquinolone (Levofloxacin/Moxifloxacin).**

## Answer explanation

Management of MDR-TB

 - Principles of Management

The management of DR-OATB is primarily medical, with surgery playing a supporting "adjuvant" role.

 Chemotherapy Regimens (The All-Oral Revolution)

Recent WHO updates favor all-oral regimens over older injectables (like Amikacin/Kanamycin) to reduce toxicity (ototoxicity/nephrotoxicity). Never add a single drug to a failing regimen.

 - The Shorter All-Oral Regimen (9–12 months): For RR/MDR-TB patients where resistance to fluoroquinolones has been excluded. Use 4-6 drugs from Group A and Group B.

 - The Longer All-Oral Regimen (18–20 months): Custom-tailored based on DST. Drugs are prioritized by "Groups":

 - Group A (Vital): Levofloxacin/Moxifloxacin, Bedaquiline, and Linezolid.

 - Group B (Supporting):
 Clofazimine, Cycloserine.

 - Group C (Add-ons): Ethambutol, Delamanid, Pyrazinamide.

BPaLM Regimen (The 6-month paradigm)

In some settings, the BPaLM regimen (Bedaquiline, Pretonamid, Linezolid, and Moxifloxacin) is being utilized for 6 months for MDR/RR-TB, though its specific efficacy in deep-seated bone infections is still under active study.

XDR-TB: The New Definition and Management

The definition of Extensively Drug-Resistant TB (XDR-TB) was updated by the WHO in 2021 to reflect the clinical importance of the newer "Group A" drugs.

 - Definition: MDR/RR-TB plus resistance to at least one fluoroquinolone
 (Levofloxacin or Moxifloxacin) AND at least one Group A drug
 (Bedaquiline or Linezolid).

 - Significance: This definition highlights that Bedaquiline and Linezolid are now our most potent weapons. Resistance to them, combined with fluoroquinolone resistance, makes the disease "extensively" difficult to treat.

 - Management: For XDR-TB, the regimen must be designed individually. It often requires high-dose Moxifloxacin (if the resistance is low-level), Delamanid, and older "Group C" drugs (like Clofazimine or Cycloserine) to ensure at least 4–5 effective drugs are being used.

The Shift Away from Injectables (Amikacin, Kanamycin)

The older "Standardized Shorter Regimen" (the "Bangladesh Regimen"), relied heavily on Kanamycin or Amikacin.

According to the WHO Consolidated Guidelines (2020/2022) and the National TB Elimination Program (NTEP) in India, injectables have been moved to Group C. They are now only used as a "last resort" when a patient has such extensive resistance that an effective all-oral regimen cannot be constructed.

The All-Oral Regimen Superiority: Large-scale trials (like the NIX-TB and STREAM trials) proved that all-oral
regimens containing Bedaquiline have better cure rates and fewer side effects.

Current Grouping of DR-TB Drugs (WHO 2022)

When designing a regimen for an FNB exam or clinical practice, use this hierarchy. Start with Group A and work down:
