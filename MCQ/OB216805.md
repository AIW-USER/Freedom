## Question

You are doing a cadaver lab with rotating medical students. While discussing injuries of the lateral clavicle, a medical student asks about the difference between the acromioclavicular (AC) and coracoclavicular (CC) ligaments? Which of the following statements is true?

## Options

1. Anterosuperior AC ligament is most crucial for vertical stability
2. Conoid ligament is most crucial for horizontal stability
3. Inferior AC ligament is most crucial for horizontal stability
4. Posterosuperior AC ligament is most crucial for horizontal stability
5. Trapezoid is most crucial for vertical stability due to its most posteromedial insertion on the clavicle

## Correct option

**4. Posterosuperior AC ligament is most crucial for horizontal stability**

## Answer explanation

Biomechanical studies have shown that the posterosuperior portion of the AC joint capsule/ligament complex is most crucial for maintaining anterior-posterior (horizontal) stability of the AC joint and should be preserved during AC joint resection procedures.

An acromioclavicular joint injury, otherwise known as a shoulder separation, is a traumatic injury to the acromioclavicular (AC) joint with disruption of the acromioclavicular ligaments and/or coracoclavicular (CC) ligaments. Diagnosis is made with bilateral focused shoulder radiographs to assess for AC and CC interval widening. Treatment is either immobilization or surgical reconstruction depending on patient activity levels, degree of separation and degree of ligament injury. The coracoclavicular ligaments primarily provide superior/inferior (vertical) stability to the clavicle, and the AC joint capsule/ligament complex provides anterior-posterior (horizontal) stability. Within this capsular complex, biomechanical studies have shown that the posterosuperior portion is most crucial for maintaining anterior-posterior stability of the AC joint and should be preserved during AC joint resection procedures.

*Li et al. reviewed AC joint injuries.* **They reported** that anteroposterior stability of the acromioclavicular joint is conferred by the capsule and acromioclavicular ligaments, of which the posterior and superior ligaments are the strongest. **They also noted** that superior-inferior stability is maintained by the coracoclavicular (conoid and trapezoid) ligaments. **They concluded** that outcomes after surgical reconstruction of the coracoclavicular ligaments have been satisfactory with regard to achieving pain relief and return to functional activities, but further improvements in the biomechanical strength of these constructs are necessary to avoid loss of reduction and creep with cyclic loading.

*Saccomanno et al. reviewed AC joint instability.* **They reported** that the AC joint is surrounded by a capsule that is quite thin, but has considerable ligamentous support; there are four AC ligaments: superior, inferior, anterior and posterior. **They also noted** that the CC ligament complex consists of the conoid and trapezoid ligaments and insert on the posteromedial and anterolateral region of the undersurface of the distal clavicle, respectively. **They concluded** that several biomechanical studies showed that horizontal stability of the AC joint is mediated by the AC ligaments while vertical stability is mediated by the CC ligaments.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The AC joint capsule/ligament complex provides anterior-posterior (horizontal) stability
Answer 2: The CC ligaments (conoid and trapezoid) primarily provide superior/inferior stability to the clavicle
Answer 3: The inferior AC capsule/ligament does not play a significant role in AC joint horizontal stability
