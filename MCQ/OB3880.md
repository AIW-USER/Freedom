## Question

Which of the following numbered regions on the axial CT scan of an adolescent ankle epiphysis corresponds to the displaced fragment in a Tillaux fracture, and which structure attaches to the displaced fragment?

![[q3880-a-distal-tibia.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Region 1, Anterior-inferior tibiofibular ligament (AITFL)
2. Region 2, Posterior-inferior tibiofibular ligament
3. Region 1, Anterior talofibular ligament (ATFL)
4. Region 2, AITFL
5. Region 3, Deltoid Ligament

## Correct option

**1. Region 1, Anterior-inferior tibiofibular ligament (AITFL)**

## Answer explanation

A Tillaux fracture is an avulsion fracture of the anterolateral distal tibia epiphysis. The anterior inferior tibiofibular ligament (AITFL) is attached to this fragment.

Tillaux fractures are Salter Harris type III fractures of the anterolateral distal tibial epiphysis. These fractures occur because these patients are close to skeletal maturity, and each portion of the distal tibial physis closes at a different time. The sequence of closure in chronological order is central, posterior, medial, and anterolateral. They are intra-articular fractures and occur with everting a supinated foot. The anterior-inferior tibiofibular ligament attaches to the anterior distal tibial epiphysis and causes fracture through an avulsion-type mechanism. The fracture line extends transversely through the physis and vertically through the epiphysis. CT is warranted to evaluate the degree of articular displacement.

*Wuerz et al. performed a review of pediatric physeal ankle fractures.* With regards to Tillaux fractures, **they report** that given the intra-articular nature of the fracture, displaced fragments can lead to early degenerative changes. **They report** that non-displaced fractures may be non-operatively in a cast. **They conclude** that reduction and screw fixation is indicated for fractures with either displacement >2 mm or translation >1 mm.

*Horn et al. performed a review of the radiologic evaluation of Tillaux fractures.* *They caused fractures in cadaver specimens and used radiographs and CT to determine the accuracy of each study to detect displacement.* **They found** that plain radiographs and CT were accurate within 1 mm depicting the actual fracture displacement 50% of the time. CT was more sensitive in detecting fractures that were displaced >2 mm. **They conclude** that CT is the preferred imaging modality in the assessment of Tillaux fractures.

Figure A is an axial CT slice of an adolescent distal tibial epiphysis. Illustration A is a coronal and an axial CT slice demonstrating a Tillaux fracture.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 2 and 4: The region numbered 2 corresponds to the posterolateral epiphysis. Tillaux fractures displace the anterolateral epiphysis.
Answer 3: The ATFL is the most common ligament injured in an ankle sprain and is not involved in Tillaux fractures.
Answer 5: The region numbered 3, corresponds to the medial malleolus which is not involved in a Tillaux fracture.
