## Answer explanation

The patient’s clinical history and exam are most consistent with an avulsion injury of the flexor digitorum profundus (FDP) tendon, eponymously known as a Jersey Finger. Various surgical techniques have been described, but regarding suture anchor fixation specifically, the most appropriate location and orientation is at a 45° retrograde angle (distal-volar to proximal-dorsal) in the proximal metaphysis of the distal phalanx (Answer choice D).

Jersey finger is a traumatic injury to the FDP tendon at its insertion on the distal phalanx, typically caused by forceful hyperextension of the DIP joint while the FDP muscle is maximally contracted. The ring finger is most commonly involved, representing about 75% of such injuries, likely due to the slightly more prominent position and greater force generated during gripping. Patients will present unable to actively flex their DIP joint. Diagnosis of the condition is primarily made based on a clinical examination, although radiographs may be utilized and may reveal a bony avulsion in a percentage of cases. Treatment is usually surgical, typically involving direct tendon repair or reinsertion when identified early, with various techniques and classifications existing based on the extent of tendon retraction and the presence of any associated bony fragments.

*Schreuder et al. investigated the influence of suture anchor insertion angle on the biomechanical properties of repaired FDP tendon avulsions in human cadavers.* **They found** that gap formation was lowest when anchors were inserted at a 45-degree retrograde angle, but that load to failure did not differ significantly between retrograde 45-degree, 90-degree, and antegrade 45-degree insertion angles. **They additionally found** differences in the mechanism of failure between groups, with all failures in the retrograde angle group due to failure of the suture at the eyelet, versus four of six in the other groups with this mode of failure (with the remaining two of six due to failure at the bone anchor interface).

*Ruchelsman et al. reviewed avulsion injuries of the FDP tendon, discussing the anatomy, mechanism of injury, classification, diagnosis, and treatment options.* **They reported** that treatment should be tailored to the specific injury pattern and chronicity, with early direct repair preferred for acute injuries and various options, including nonoperative management, DIP joint arthrodesis, or staged reconstruction, considered for chronic cases. Regarding suture anchor fixation, they discussed the optimal anchor insertion technique according to the "deadman angle" theory, **suggesting** a 45-degree retrograde angle relative to the bone for maximizing pullout strength and reducing gap formation. **They concluded** that proper suture anchor technique, including careful consideration of anchor placement and angle, allows for a robust repair while avoiding dorsal-sided and nail plate complications associated with other commonly described techniques.

*Geary et al. retrospectively reviewed complications associated with the transosseous repair of zone I FDP tendon injuries using a buried dorsal suture technique.* **They found** a high complication rate, with five of eight patients experiencing complications such as infection, osteomyelitis, chronic draining granuloma, and abnormal nail growth, and three patients requiring additional surgery. **They concluded** that transosseous repair of zone I FDP injuries with a buried dorsal suture carries a substantial risk of complications.

Figure A demonstrates AP and lateral radiographs of a small finger without any osseous abnormalities, which may often be the case in FDP avulsion injuries. Illustration A, referenced from Ruchelsman et al. (2011), demonstrates a schematic and radiographic example of appropriate suture anchor placement at the aforementioned “deadman angle.”

Incorrect Answers:
Answer choice 1: Although the angular orientation of “A” is reasonable, this is not the anatomic insertion of the FDP tendon.
Answer choice 2: An anchor placed in the orientation of “B” is incorrect because of its perpendicular rather than retrograde orientation, this is not the anatomic insertion of the FDP tendon, and penetration through the dorsal cortex which should be avoided during suture anchor repair.
Answer choice 3: Antegrade orientation of the suture anchor leads to greater gap formation than appropriate retrograde orientation.
Answer choice 5: Although the orientation of option “E” is correct, the location is incorrect given its violation of the articular surface.
