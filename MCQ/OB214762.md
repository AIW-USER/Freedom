## Question

Injury to which of the following nerve structures during an anterior approach to the cervical spine would lead to paralysis of the posterior cricoarytenoid muscle?

## Options

1. C5 nerve root
2. Internal branch of Superior laryngeal nerve
3. Sympathetic chain
4. Hypoglossal nerve
5. Recurrent laryngeal nerve

## Correct option

**5. Recurrent laryngeal nerve**

## Answer explanation

Injury to the recurrent laryngeal nerve leads to paralysis of the posterior cricoarytenoid muscle and abnormal vocal cord function.

The recurrent laryngeal nerve (RLN) supplies all the intrinsic muscles of the larynx with the exception of the cricothyroid muscles. The course of the nerve is different on the right and left sides of the neck. On the right, it crosses from lateral to midline more cephalad in the incision after it passes under the subclavian artery; conversely, on the left the course is more caudal because it passes under the aortic arch. Although theoretically, the nerve is at greater risk of injury with a right-sided approach, there is limited evidence to support a greater incidence of nerve injury with a right-sided approach. Initial treatment of a recurrent laryngeal nerve injury is observation. If no improvement is noted (persistent vocal hoarseness) after 6 weeks, an ENT consult is recommended.

*Miscusi et al. performed a cadaveric study to review the origin and course of both RLNs to assess their vulnerability in the anterior sternocleidomastoid approach to the cervical spine.* **The authors noted** that the origin of the RLN was at C7 in 2 cases and at T1 in 4 cases. In all cases, it arose where the vagus nerve crossed the subclavian artery and it was directed superiorly and transversely to the esophagotracheal groove. **The authors concluded** that although the discussion about the best side for the anterior cervical approach is debated, both anatomical and surgical considerations lead to evidence that the left side approach is preferred below the level of C4.

*Fard et al. performed a cadaveric study to review the anatomical considerations specific to the high anterior retropharyngeal approach to the cervical spine.* **The authors noted** certain anatomic considerations that are unique to the high anterior cervical spine including the hypoglossal nerve and the superior thyroid artery/nerve. Only the superior thyroid artery in this region has numerous anatomical variations. Awareness of other structures, including the carotid artery, RLN, and esophagus also remains important. **The authors conclude** that awareness of the anatomical structures in the anterior upper cervical spine is essential for performing safe anterior upper cervical spinal surgery, avoiding serious complications.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: C5 nerve root injury leads to weakness of deltoid and biceps.
Answer 2: Internal branch of Superior laryngeal nerve provides sensory innervation of the larynx above the vocal cords.
Answer 3: Injury to the Sympathetic chain leads to Horner's syndrome characterized by ptosis, anhydrosis and miosis
Answer 4: The Hypoglossal nerve innervates extrinsic and intrinsic muscles of the tongue. Injury to it leads to tongue will deviate to side of injury
