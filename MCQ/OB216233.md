## Question

A 73-year-old male underwent left total hip arthroplasty 15 years ago. He had an uneventful postoperative course and was doing well up until 8 months ago when he began having insidious onset left hip pain that worsens with activity. Figure A shows his most recent radiographs obtained 2 week ago. ESR and CRP at that time were normal. The patient presents to the ED today with the radiograph shown in Figure B. He reports this this is the first issue he's had with his THA. Which of the following is the MOST LIKELY reason for this patient’s late instability?

![[q216233-a-figure-a.jpg]]
*Figure A*

![[q216233-b-screen-shot-2021-05-25-at-9-35-43-pm.jpg]]
*Figure B*

## Options

1. Polyethylene wear
2. Loosening of the femoral component
3. Acetabular inclination
4. Infection
5. Impingement secondary to heterotopic ossification

## Correct option

**1. Polyethylene wear**

## Answer explanation

Polyethylene wear greater than >2mm has been shown to be one of the most common causes of late instability following THA.

Instability is one of the most common complications following total hip arthroplasty. Late instability specifically, is a primary instability event that occurs >5 years after the index procedure. In contrast to early instability episodes, which can sometimes be treated without surgery, most instances of late instability require operative intervention. Risk factors for early dislocation include female gender, older age, neurologic dysfunction, cognitive impairment, or a diagnosis of inflammatory arthritis or femoral head osteonecrosis. Late dislocations, however, are associated with polyethylene wear >2mm, progression of neurologic symptoms, abductor insufficiency or loosening/migration of components.

*Pulido et al review late instability following THA.* **They report** the most common causes of late instability are polyethylene wear, long time malpositioning of the components, trauma, deterioration in muscle mass and declining neurologic status. **They conclude** that all providers seeing patients with THA should be aware of the risk of dislocation with polyethene wear and be able to identify an eccentric femoral head radiographically.

*Van Knoch et al review late dislocations after THA.* *They report on a total of 19,680 hips with an overall dislocation rate of 2.6%, of which, 32% were late dislocation (occurring >5 years after the index procedure).* **They conclude** that late instability can be related to long standing problems such as component malpositioning and subluxation or new issues such as polyethene wear, neurologic decline and trauma.

Figure A is the AP radiograph of a left hip demonstrating an eccentric femoral head indicative of significant polyethene wear. Figure B is the radiograph of this same patient following a late dislocation in which polyethene wear was indicated as the main cause of instability.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: The femoral component in Figure A does not appear loose, making this an unlikely cause of instability.
Answer 3: While this cup does look vertical, acetabular inclination can only be truly measured on an AP pelvic radiograph. Further, if the patient hasn't had any issues with his hip for 15 years, increased inclination is not likely to be the most common reason for late instability.
Answer 4: Infection as a cause of instability is unlikely, especially in the setting of a normal ESR and CRP.
Answer 5: While this patient does have some focal heterotopic ossification (HO), it is unlikely that this amount of HO would lead to impingement and dislocation.
