## Question

A 45-year-old male presents with worsening lower back and leg pain after lifting a large object yesterday. He reports difficulty with urination since the onset of his symptoms. His leg pain radiates from bilateral buttocks and down bilateral thighs. On examination, the patient has saddle anesthesia with decreased rectal tone. Which of the following statements are true regarding the treatment of this condition?

## Options

1. Surgical decompression within 24 hours is associated with improved outcomes compared to surgical decompression within 48 hours
2. Wide laminectomy and diskectomy is associated with greater neurologic recovery than microdiskectomy
3. Long term urinary dysfunction is common following treatment
4. Sexual dysfunction is the most common presenting symptom
5. Burst fractures are the most common cause of this syndrome

## Correct option

**3. Long term urinary dysfunction is common following treatment**

## Answer explanation

The patient is presenting with cauda equina syndrome. Even with successful treatment long-term urinary dysfunction is common.

Cauda equina syndrome is an ischemic event of the caudal most nerve roots due to severe compression. The etiology of the compression can vary, but most commonly is the result of a herniated nucleus pulposus. The most common symptom is back pain with others including bilateral sciatica, urinary retention, and fecal incontinence. Even with successful treatment there is residual urinary dysfunction in approximately 50% of patients.

*Spector et al. reviewed the diagnosis and management of cauda equina syndrome.* **The authors stated** early diagnosis is difficult due to the often subtle initial symptoms. **The authors recommended** urgent surgical decompression after diagnosis to enable optimal neurologic recovery.

*Korse et al. performed a systematic review of micturition, defecation, and sexual function after cauda equina syndrome.* **They reported** at an average 17 months follow-up 42.5% of patients with micturition dysfunction, 49.6% defecation dysfunction, and 44.3% sexual dysfunction. **The authors concluded** long-term dysfunction to be common after cauda equina treatment.

*Kohles et al. reviewed the literature regarding the timing of decompression of cauda equina syndrome.* **They reported** a key meta-analysis revealed no difference in outcomes between decompression within 24 hours and 24-48 hours but discussed the flawed methodology to this finding. **They concluded** the results are easily misinterpreted as irreversible damage to the nerve roots are likely to occur in a continuous, time-dependent fashion rather than a stepwise sequence.

Incorrect answers
Answer 1: Decompression within 48 hours compared to 24 hours has been shown to have similar outcomes.
Answer 2: Laminectomy or microdiscectomy have similar outcomes for treating cauda equina syndrome. The main goal of treatment is to adequately decompress the thecal sac.
Answer 4: The most common presenting symptom is back pain.
Answer 5: Herniated nucleus pulposus is the most common cause of cauda equina syndrome.
