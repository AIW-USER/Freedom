## Question

Osseointegration of uncemented porous femur component occurs till (arthroplasty- Hip arthroplasty- implants and prosthesis design)

## Options

1. 4-6 weeks
2. 3-6 months
3. 6-12 months
4. Up to 3 years

## Correct option

**4. Up to 3 years**

## Answer explanation

Bone will grow on to the surface of metal implant if it has certain special ‘‘topography’’, called porous ingrowth or osseointegration. Osseointegration or osseointegration refers to a direct bone-to-metal implant interface without any interposition of non-bone tissue. After the implantation, it will take approximately four to twelve weeks for osseointegration and will be continued for up to three years.

Fixation of an uncemented femoral stem is classified as (1) bone ingrowth, (2) stable fibrous fixation, or (3) unstable.

Bone ingrowth and bone ongrowth

Bone growing inside a porous surface is the ingrowth and bone growing on a roughened surface is ongrowth. These are determined by the surface characteristics of an implant. A pore size between 100 and 400 mm is necessary for ingrowth and 40–50% of voids within the coating should be maintained for the mechanical strength. Sintered beads, fiber mesh, and porous metals over the surface are the ways of creating ingrowth surfaces.

 Ongrowth surfaces have a roughness ranging from 3 to 5 mm and this is created by grit blasting or plasma spraying. Hydroxyapatite is osteoconductive and enhances growth of mineralized bone onto the implant. It is a calcium phosphate compound which is plasma sprayed directly on the implant.

Also know: Radiographic signs for detecting acetabular osseointegration: (1) absence of radiolucent lines; (2) presence of a superolateral buttress; (3) medial stress-shielding; (4) radial trabeculae; and (5) an inferomedial buttress.

Answer: 4
