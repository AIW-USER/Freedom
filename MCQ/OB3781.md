## Options

1. C5-7 anterior cervical discectomy and fusion
2. Perform urgent closed reduction
3. C4-7 posterior instrumented fusion
4. C5-7 combined anterior and posterior
5. Hard cervical collar

## Correct option

**5. Hard cervical collar**

## Answer explanation

The patient is presenting with a right C6 facet fracture without instability or subluxation. This can be treated with a hard cervical collar.

Cervical facet fractures can indicate a large soft tissue injury to the supporting ligaments of the cervical spine. Isolated facet fractures can be deceivingly unstable and can potentially represent a facet dislocation that spontaneously reduced. The initial evaluation should include controlled dynamic studies, including flexion-extension radiographs or fluoroscopic assessment. Stable injuries with preserved cervical alignment can be treated with nonoperative means, such as a hard cervical collar or halo vest. Surgical stabilization should be performed for patients that fail nonoperative treatment, unstable injuries, or those with neurologic deficits.

*van Middendorp et al. performed a prospective cohort study of 239 patients treated with halo-vest immobilization.* Univariate regression analysis determined that patients older than 65 years of age were not at increased risk for pneumonia or death with halo immobilization but immobilization alone resulted in the loss of alignment at higher rates with fractures involving the facet joints. **The authors concluded** that elderly patients have relatively low rates of pneumonia and death with halo vest treatment, but is still replete with minor complications.

*Bucholz and Cheung performed a retrospective review of 124 patients treated with halo immobilization for cervical spine injuries.* **They reported** that injuries involving the facets joints, subluxation injuries, and involvement of the lower subaxial spine were risk factors for halo vest failure. **The authors concluded** that halo vest immobilization is a reliable treatment option for cervical spine injuries with a relatively low failure rate.

*Spector et al. retrospectively reviewed 24 patients with 26 unilateral cervical facet fractures diagnosed with cervical spine computed tomography (CT).* **They reported** that CT was the most effective modality for identifying unilateral facet fractures. **The authors concluded** that patients with facets fractures involving >40% of the lateral mass or an absolute height >1 cm are at increased risk for failure of nonoperative treatment.

Figures A and B are the sagittal and axial CT scans of the cervical spine demonstrating a right C6 facet fracture without significant displacement. Figure C is the sagittal MRI of the cervical spine with an intact disc at C5-6 and C6-7 without increased signal in the posterior ligamentous complex. Figures D and E are the flexion and extension radiographs of the cervical spine without apparent instability.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1, 3 and 4: The patient is presenting with a nondisplaced facet fracture without evidence of instability on the lateral flexion-extension radiographs. This injury does not require surgical stabilization and fusion.
Answer 2: The lateral mass fracture at C6 is not associated with a perched facet or dislocation, so a closed reduction is not required at this time.
