## Question

A 73-year-old female sustains the fracture depicted in Figure A, and the surgeon decides to treat the fracture with a distal femoral locking plate. When discussing the postoperative weight-bearing status of the affected extremity, which recommendation should be made to allow postoperative mobilization while limiting fixation failure and nonunion?

![[q218827-a-distal-femur.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Non-weight bearing
2. Touchdown weightbearing
3. 25% partial weightbearing
4. 50% partial weightbearing
5. Weightbearing as tolerated

## Correct option

**5. Weightbearing as tolerated**

## Answer explanation

Permitting weightbearing as tolerated with lateral distal femoral locking plates for extra-articular distal femur fractures is not associated with fixation failure or higher reoperation rates and allows for early mobility.

Distal femur fractures occur in a bimodal distribution with a high frequency in elderly osteopenic females following ground-level falls. Extra-articular can be managed with distal femoral locking plates or retrograde intramedullary nail constructs. Early weightbearing allows for rapid mobility and reduction of sequelae of recumbency. Load-bearing constructs, such as distal femoral locking plates, have traditionally come with protected weightbearing or non-weightbearing status out of concern for fixation failure and reoperation. However, recent studies have demonstrated there to be no difference in complication with early weight bearing as tolerated in osteopenic elderly patients with distal femoral extra-articular fractures.

*Poole et al. retrospectively studied the outcomes of early weight-bearing following lateral distal femoral locking plate (LDFLP) fixation of distal femur fractures in 127 elderly patients.* **They reported** a 3% rate of fixation failure with immediate unrestricted weight-bearing after LDFLP fixation, a 95% rate of clinical and radiological union, and a low rate of re-operation. **They concluded** early weight bearing is not associated with fixation failure in this population.

*Lieder et al. retrospectively compared the early complications in elderly patients with extra-articular distal femur fractures who are allowed to weight-bear as tolerated (WBAT) versus patients who are prescribed initial touchdown weight-bearing (TDWB).* **They reported** that the rate of early adverse events requiring reoperation was similar between the WBAT group and the TDWB group, and there was no difference between the groups in terms of length of stay, discharge disposition, malunion, and patient-reported outcomes. The study supports allowing carefully selected elderly patients to immediately weight-bear after operative fixation of extra-articular distal femur fractures regardless of implant choice.

Figure A is an AP and lateral radiograph of the left femur with a comminuted extra-articular distal femur fracture.

Incorrect answers
Answers 1, 2, 3, and 4: Partial or touchdown weightbearing is not associated with decreased fixation failure, reoperation, or nonunion rates that early weightbearing as tolerated. Nonweightbearing precautions do not allow for early mobility.
