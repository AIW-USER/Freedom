## Question

A 64-year old male underwent a total knee arthroplasty 10 weeks ago. Intraoperatively his knee was well-balanced and had a range of motion 0-130 degrees. Current range of motion is now 10-70 degrees. Post-op radiographs are shown in Figure A. CRP is 0.3 mg/L and ESR is 8 mm/hr. What is the next most appropriate step in management?

![[q215352-a-tka-ap-lat.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Knee aspiration
2. Cortisone injection followed by physical therapy
3. Polyethylene liner exchange
4. Manipulation under anesthesia
5. Femoral component revision

## Correct option

**4. Manipulation under anesthesia**

## Answer explanation

The history, physical examination, laboratory studies, and imaging are consistent with arthrofibrosis after total knee arthroplasty (TKA). The next most appropriate step is a manipulation under anesthesia to improve the patient's range of motion.

Post-operative knee stiffness (flexion < 90 degrees) occurs in 1.3%-12% of patients who undergo TKA. Preoperative risk factors for stiffness include decreased preoperative range of motion, age, diabetes mellitus, and socioeconomic status. Surgical factors can also contribute, including overstuffing the patellofemoral joint, tight flexion/extension gaps, and excessive tightening of the extensor mechanism. If left untreated, loss of flexion negatively impacts functional outcomes, patient satisfaction, and ability to perform activities of daily living. MUA is considered the initial management for patients with flexion less than 90 degrees.

Maloney presents Level 4 evidence discussing TKA postoperative arthrofibrosis. They report that manipulation under anesthesia was successful in improving flexion from an average of 67 degrees pre-manipulation to 111 degrees post-manipulation.

*Keating et al. report Level 4 evidence of 113 patients that underwent manipulation following TKA.* **They found** that 90% of the patients achieved an improvement of ultimate knee flexion following manipulation. The average improvement in flexion from the measurement made before manipulation to that recorded at the five-year follow-up was 35 degrees.

Figure A shows AP and lateral left knee radiographs with well-placed TKA components.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: This patient's history, exam, and inflammatory markers are consistent with arthrofibrosis, not septic arthritis.
Answer 2: A cortisone injection + additional physical therapy will not solve this patient's arthrofibrosis.
Answer 3: A polyethylene liner exchange is not the most appropriate management for early postoperative arthrofibrosis after TKA.
Answer 5: Revision arthroplasty at this early time point would not be appropriate.
