Chapter: Arthroplasty MCQs — Wear And Osteolysis (Hip)

## Question

Which of the following Mechanisms best describes the common cause of failure of the polyethylene in a total knee replacement?

## Options

1. Adhesive wear
2. Two-Body Abrasive Wear
3. Fatigue Wear
4. Third-Body Abrasive Wear

## Correct option

**3. Fatigue Wear**

## Answer explanation

Arthroplasties viz. joint replacements require insertion of a prosthesis with components made of various biomaterials that have tendency to undergo wear with use.

A biomaterial is defined as a nonviable material used in a medical device and intended to interact with biological systems. Biomaterials may be described by their acceptance within a biological system as biotolerant (encapsulated in fibrous tissue implants), bioinert (in direct contact with the surrounding bone), and bioactive (distinguished by a direct chemical bond to the surrounding bone). According to the chemical composition, biomaterials may be classified as ceramics, metals, polymers (e.g., Polyethylene), and composites

Polyethylene (PE) wear is a very popular biomaterial used as inserts in both total hip and knee arthroplasty and has been a material of concern as far as wear is concerned. Wear debris generated from the material have since long been thought of as the most important cause behind aseptic loosening of arthroplasty components.

TRIBOLOGY is the branch of science that studies such phenomena. According to the ASM International handbook, Tribology is defined as the science of interacting surfaces in relative motion. Henceforth, it studies these wear patterns and the related mechanics viz. friction and lubrication in joints.

Wear is defined as the progressive removal of material from the prosthesis due to relative motion between two surfaces, resulting in formation of particulate debris.

WEAR STAGES

- Primary stage or early run-in period, where surfaces adapt to each other and the wear-rate might vary between high and low.
- Secondary stage or mid-age process, where steady wear can be observed. Most of the component's operational life is spent in this stage. The secondary stage is shortened with increasing severity of environmental conditions, such as high temperatures, strain rates and stresses.
- Tertiary stage or old-age period, where surfaces are subjected to rapid failure due to a high rate of wear.

WEAR MODES

- Adhesive wear — Bonds get formed between two surfaces in contact under loading. As movement occurs, when sometimes one material is weaker than those bonds, wear occurs due to pulling away of particles from that weaker surface. For instance, microscopically PE sticks to prosthesis and debris gets pulled off the PE during movements.
- Abrasive wear — Occurs when a hard surface cuts through a softer surface as it slides against it creating the classical “Cheese grater effect”. For instance, a rough femoral head surface causes mechanical scratching of PE surface with loss of PE material (cheese grater effect).

Now, there are two modes of abrasive wears known as two-body and three body abrasive wear mechanisms. Two-body abrasive wear occurs when one surface (usually harder than the other) cuts material away from the other surface; this mechanism very often changes to three-body abrasion as the wear debris then acts as an abrasive substance between the two surfaces. Three-body wear occurs when particles are not constrained, but are free to roll and slide down a surface.

For instance, particles within the joint space get between the head and PE cup, causing PE to be removed from the cup surface. Third-body particle sources include Cement debris, Metal debris shed from cup or stem, Metal debris from metal corrosion at modular metal-metal interfaces (like neck stem modular junctions viz. Tunionosis), and Hydroxyapatite debris shed from implant surfaces.

Plowing, Cutting and Fragmentation are the mechanisms attributed to abrasive wear.

- Surface Fatigue wear (Delamination wear): Surface fatigue is a process in which the surface of a softer material is weakened by cyclic loading. Wear particles are generated as the cyclic loading forces exceed the fatigue limit for that surface. Wear particles are then detached by cyclic crack growth of microcracks (pits/ voids and delaminations) on the surface.
- Fretting wear: Wear due to small amplitude of oscillatory or reciprocating movement between two surfaces is known as fretting wear. It is a two step mechanism. Initially, the adhesive wear occurs due to rubbing of two surfaces and then they become oxidized due to the large quantity of energy stored in wear particles. The oxides are usually harder than the underlying materials mostly, so wear accelerates as the harder particles abrade the underlying surfaces further. Over a period of time fretting which will remove material from both surfaces in contact. In arthroplasty, fretting occurs mostly due to the rubbing between two materials that are not intended for motion, for instance, in THR between the metal shell and a PE insert (back-side wear).
- Corrosive wear: Corrosive wear occurs when sliding takes place in corrosive or oxidative environment. During dry sliding also, the oxygen from the normal environment or other gases present in the environment can react with the solid surface. The excessive presence of antiwear additives or other chemical agents also can bring corrosive wear.

Wear in TKR and THR

Hip joint is a ball and socket joint wherein forces are evenly distributed. Here predominant mode of wear (considering PE insert) is Adhesive/ abrasive whereas in TKR wear is predominantly Delamination/ Fatigue type.

WEAR PATTERNS

Linear wear is measured by the distance the prosthesis has penetrated into the liner.

Volumetric wear is main determinant of number of particles created. It is directly related to square of the radius of the head.

Small head (22 mm) will have higher linear wear and lower volumetric wear than large (32-mm head). Failure is more likely to result from wear through PE cup. Large head (32 mm) will have lower linear and higher volumetric wear than 22-mm head. Failure is more likely to result from osteolysis. Compromise head (28 mm) will have less volumetric wear than 32-mm head and Less linear wear than 22-mm head. They also have better stability than 22-mm head. Thus, most hip systems use 28-mm head size.

Ans: C
