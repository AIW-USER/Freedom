## Question

A 14-year-old female was diagnosed with scoliosis 3 years ago and was initially treated with bracing. The curve progressed to > 50 degrees and she and her family elect to proceed with posterior instrumented fusion. The patient has an uncomplicated perioperative course and is discharged home on postoperative day 3. Which of the following statements is true regarding emergency department visits following adolescent idiopathic scoliosis surgery?

## Options

1. BMI <20 is a risk factor for more frequent emergency department visits within 30 days of surgery
2. Emergency department visits are associated with Lenke IV and V curves
3. Higher number of emergency department visits are associated with post-operative Enhanced Recovery After Surgery (ERAS) pathways
4. Patients that are of older age and had more fusion levels have a higher number of emergency department visits within 6 months of surgery
5. Use of pelvic fixation and posterior column osteotomies increases the post-opeartive emergency department visit rate

## Correct option

**4. Patients that are of older age and had more fusion levels have a higher number of emergency department visits within 6 months of surgery**

## Answer explanation

Emergency department (ED) visits after adolescent idiopathic scoliosis (AIS) surgery have been associated with older age at the time of surgery and more fusion levels.

AIS is characterized by a coronal plane deformity of the thoracolumbar spine that presents between the ages of 10-18 years. The etiology of the deformity remains unknown, but several theories exist. With mild deformities (< 40 degrees) treatment consists of bracing and observation. Patients with curves >50 degrees are recommended surgery out of the concern for continued progression into adulthood. Overall complication rates following surgery remain low, but up to 10% of patients may present to the ED due to concerns related to the surgery. Most of the presenting complaints are for minor issues that can often be managed in an outpatient clinical setting. Patients that are of older age and had more levels involved in surgery have been associated with higher ED visit rates.

*Harris et al. examined the incidence of ED visits following posterior spinal fusion (PSF) in AIS patients.* **The researchers found** that approximately 10% of the patients had at least one ED visit within six months after the surgery with the most common reasons for these visits being related to pain, musculoskeletal issues, constipation, respiratory problems, and dehydration. **The authors observed** that the majority of diagnoses at these ED visits were outpatient medical issues, suggesting that serious complications were less frequent.

*Sundar et al. retrospectively evaluated the effects of a standardized Care Path on perioperative outcomes in patients undergoing posterior fusion for AIS.* **They reported** the post-op Care Path cohort showed improvements in postoperative mobilization, reduced length of stay, fewer inpatient complications, decreased rates of postoperative ileus, fewer emergency room visits, decreased postoperative infections, readmissions, reoperations, and lower oral morphine equivalents. **The authors suggested** that implementing the Care Path resulted in improved outcomes and reduced morbidity in AIS patients undergoing spinal fusion.

Incorrect answers
Answer 1: Curve type has not been associated with post-op emergency room visits.
Answer 2: Low BMI has not been identified as a risk factor for increased ED visits post-op.
Answer 3: Rapid recovery protocols have been associated with a decreased number of ED visits post-op.
Answer 5: Pelvic fixation or use of Ponte osteotomies have not been associated with post-op ED visits.
