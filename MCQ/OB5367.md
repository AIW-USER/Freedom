## Question

A 6-year-old boy presents to the emergency department after a fall from a play structure while at recess. He has notable pain and swelling about his left elbow. His neurovascular exam is within normal limits. Figures A and B are radiographs taken in the emergency department following his arrival. What is the most appropriate description of his injury?

## Options

1. Suspected simple elbow dislocation
2. Gartland type I supracondylar humerus fracture
3. Gartland type II supracondylar humerus fracture
4. Gartland type III supracondylar humerus fracture
5. Lateral condyle fracture

## Correct option

**3. Gartland type II supracondylar humerus fracture**

## Answer explanation

The presentation and radiographs are consistent with a Gartland type II supracondylar humerus fracture.

Supracondylar humerus fractures are common injuries in children after a fall from height. They are classified based on the degree of displacement, and presence of an intact posterior hinge. If the fracture is non-displaced then it is a type I. A type II fracture pattern involves angulation of the distal humeral fragment without loss of complete cortical contact. If there is complete displacement it would be considered a type III.

*Otsuka et al. present a review on supracondylar humerus fractures with a focus on treatment of type II and type III fractures.* **They report** that closed reduction and percutaneous pinning is frequently sufficient, but note that in the presence of neurologic deficits or vascular compromise, open exploration may be required.

Figure A is an AP radiograph of a type II supracondylar humerus fracture. Figure B is a lateral radiograph of a type II supracondylar humerus fracture. Illustration A shows the same lateral projection of this fracture with the anterior humeral line drawn in red. Note the line passes anterior to the capitellum indicating displacement of the fracture into an extended position.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: While simple elbow dislocations in children do occur and can spontaneously reduce (leading to a normal radiograph upon initial presentation), these radiographs clearly demonstrate a type II supracondylar humerus fracture.
Answer 2: The fracture is displaced with the anterior humeral line passing anterior to the capitellum, and thus this cannot be a type I fracture.
Answer 4: While the fracture is angulated in an extended position, the posterior cortex appears to be well-aligned and certainly doesn't appear completely displaced. Thus is not considered a type III.
Answer 5: The fracture seen in Figures A and B extends across the entire distal humerus from lateral to medial is thus is a supracondylar humerus fracture rather than a fracture of the lateral condyle.
