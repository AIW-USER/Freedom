## Question

A 4-year-old male is diagnosed with a congenital condition caused by a mutation in chromosome 11p15.5 that caused him to develop with characteristic traits of hemihypertrophy of his left lower extremity, macroglossia, and an omphalocele. For which of the following conditions should the boy be screened for every 3-6 months until he is 5-8 years of age given his genetic predisposition?

## Options

1. Hearing loss
2. Hyperglycemia
3. Pancreatic insufficiency
4. Vision loss
5. Wilms tumor

## Correct option

**5. Wilms tumor**

## Answer explanation

Beckwith-Wiedemann Syndrome is a congenital condition caused by a mutation in chromosome 11p15.5 that leads to overactivity of the IGF-2 gene (growth factor) and/or no active copy of CDKN1C (inhibitor of cell proliferation gene). Patients commonly present with hemihypertrophy, macroglossia, and abdominal wall defects such as omphalocele. The most common associated pre-disposition that must be screened for in early childhood is the development of a Wilms tumor.

Though the inheritance is sporadic in the majority of cases (85%), there is an autosomal dominant (AD) pattern with incomplete penetrance in as many as 15% of cases. The pathophysiology of some of the typical presenting features is thought to relate to pancreatic islet cell hypertrophy that causes repeated bouts of infantile hypoglycemia resulting in spasticity and altered development. Importantly, patients have a predisposition for embryonal tumors, such as Wilms tumors, which should be screened for with abdominal ultrasound every 3-6 months until the child is aged 5-8 years.

Choyke, et al. provide a review of screening for Wilms tumor in children with Beckwith-Wiedemann syndrome (BWS). Of 15 patients who were screened early, 12 had Wilms tumors and none had late-stage disease, while 25/59 (42%) of unscreened patients with Wilms tumors had late-stage disease. The authors concluded that children with BWS may benefit from screening sonograms at intervals of 4 months or less.

Miller, et al. provide an early review in the New England Journal of Medicine (NEJM) of associations with congenital hemihypertrophy. The authors noted that despite the very low frequency of congenital hemihypertrophy, 16 cases have been reported with Wilms tumor, 4 with adrenal neoplasm, and 3 with hepatoblastoma. The tumor was contralateral to the hemihypertrophy in 5 of the 16 cases described.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Though ear anomalies can occur, hearing loss is not a commonly associated finding.
Answer 2: Repeated bouts of hypoglycemia, not hyperglycemia, are associated with the development of the most commonly seen clinical traits.
Answer 3: Pancreatic islet cell hypertrophy, not insufficiency, is associated with Beckwith-Wiedemann syndrome.
Answer 4: Vision loss is not a common associated finding.
