## Question

Initially, he thought it was arthritis in his knee, but there is associated swelling on the inside of his knee that made him concerned. He denies any neurologic symptoms or unexplained weight loss. ## Options

1. Cartilage
2. Bone
3. Vascular
4. Smooth Muscle
5. Skeletal Muscle

## Correct option

**3. Vascular**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a hemangioma, a benign vascular tumor.

Hemangiomas are benign, malformed vascular lesions that occur when small blood vessels begin to multiply at an abnormal rate and form a mass. Classically they present with pain that varies by activity level, swelling and aching with engorgement with activity or being in a dependent position. The mass often demonstrates heterogeneously mixed signal intensity in both T1- and T2-weighted sequences, with a "bag of worms" appearance due to the way the blood vessels pack around one another. If biopsied, histology reveals small vascular channels lined with a single layer of endothelial cells. The histological differential for such a lesion would include an aneurysmal bone cyst (ABC). Most hemangiomas may be treated with observation and symptomatic management.

*Nozaki et al. reviewed the syndromes associated with vascular tumors and malformations.* **They reported** that while some vascular tumors and malformations develop in isolation, many others develop as part of a syndrome and that vascular tumors (neoplastic) should be distinguished from vascular malformations, which are caused by vascular structural anomalies (non-neoplastic). **They recommended** that clinicians familiarize themselves with these conditions.

*McCuaig et al. reviewed the diagnosis of the major vascular malformations and associated syndromes.* **They reported** that vascular malformations occur due to genetic mutations resulting in errors in vascular morphogenesis, often presenting with overgrowth of the bone and/or surrounding soft tissue. **They recommended** that in cases of suspected syndromes, genetic testing be performed to compare the affected tissue with blood or saliva to identify somatic mutations that are associated with these syndromes.

Figure A includes AP and lateral radiographs as well as a T2 MRI of the knee demonstrating the classic findings of phleboliths (on plain radiography) and serpiginous vascularity with a classic "bag of worms" appearance. Figure B depicts the histology of a hemangioma (small vascular channels lined with a single layer of endothelial cells).
