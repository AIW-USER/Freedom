## Question

A 15-year-old lacrosse player comes to the clinic with a complaint of persistent right medial foot pain that is typically worse after long afternoon practice sessions. He was given rigid foot insoles for arch support by his pediatrician for his flat feet, but these seem to make his pain worse. An external oblique radiograph of the affected extremity is shown in Figure A. Which of the following inserts onto the structure associated with the cause of this patient's pain and is likely contributing to worsening pain with the use of the rigid arch supports?

![[q218647-a-screenshot-2023-06-23-at-12-05-53-am.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Flexor digitorum longus
2. Flexor hallucis longus
3. Peroneus brevis
4. Peroneus longus
5. Posterior tibialis

## Correct option

**5. Posterior tibialis**

## Answer explanation

This patient has an accessory navicular, as seen in Figure A. The posterior tibialis tendon (PTT) inserts onto the medial tuberosity of the navicular (Illustration A) and can be a cause of medial arch pain due to inflammation at the insertion point of the tendon.

An accessory navicular is a normal variant that can be seen in up to 12% of the population. Most patients are asymptomatic. The diagnosis is made with plain radiographs demonstrating a plantar-medial enlargement of the navicular bone. Given the intimate association with the posterior tibialis tendon (PTT), associated conditions often include pes planus, and rarely posterior tibialis tendon insufficiency. Treatment is almost always conservative, beginning with shoe modifications, activity restrictions for athletic patients, anti-inflammatories, and if needed, a short period of immobilization in a boot. When prescribing shoe modifications, it is important to note that rigid insoles used for pes planus often rub on and irritate the enlarged navicular, so these should be avoided in favor of softer, cushioned inserts. In recalcitrant cases, surgical excision of the accessory navicular can be performed.

*Issever et al. published a case report of a young gymnast who was referred to rheumatology clinic by an orthopaedist for ankle pain that was thought to be due to her rheumatologic condition.* An MRI obtained by rheumatologists demonstrated the presence of an accessory navicular. **The authors concluded** that MRI is considered to have the highest sensitivity and specificity for the diagnosis of painful accessory navicular and that high suspicion should be held for accessory navicular when medial foot pain occurs after stress or physical exercise in young females.

*Jegal et al. reviewed the presence of accessory navicular in the athletic population versus the general population.* *The authors reviewed 79 patients (29 athletes and 50 non-athletes) and found that, overall, 34% of the non-athletes improved after conservative treatment, while only 6.9% of athletes improved.* **They concluded** that the clinical course of athletes with accessory naviculars is different than that of non-athletes, with their symptoms more often being refractory to conservative treatment, suggesting that early operative treatment could be considered in this select subgroup.

Figure A is an external oblique radiograph of the right foot demonstrating the presence of an accessory navicular. Illustrations A, B, and C demonstrate the insertions of the posterior tibialis tendon, the superficial (main) slip of which inserts onto the medial navicular (and sometimes medial cuneiform), and the plantar (minor) slip which inserts onto the plantar bases of the 2nd-4th metatarsals. Illustration D is a labeled version of Figure A, showing the accessory navicular, in red.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The flexor digitorum longus splits into four slips after passing through the medial intermuscular septum of the plantar foot, with one of each of the slips inserting onto the plantar bases of the 2nd-5th distal phalanges.
Answer 2: The flexor hallucis longus inserts onto the plantar surface of the base of the distal phalanx of the great toe.
Answer 3: The peroneus brevis inserts on the base of the 5th metatarsal.
Answer 4: The peroneus longus inserts on the plantar medial cuneiform and the lateral base of the first metatarsal.
