## Answer explanation

Figure E demonstrates histology consistent with a chordoma of the lumbar spine. Of the histologic specimens shown, this is the only one that would warrant an initial en bloc corpectomy. This is the most effective treatment to limit the recurrence of primary vertebral tumors.

Primary spine tumors are very rare, and achieving curative margins can furthermore be technically difficult. Chordomas most frequently occur in the sacrum but may also occur in the vertebral bodies leading to neurologic sequelae. Neoplastic cells originate from primitive notochordal cells. Tumors that are operable in the mobile spine are best treated with en bloc corpectomy to reduce the chances of local recurrence. Further, radiation treatment plays a role in treatment with inoperable tumors and in the setting of positive margins found on pathologic analysis.

*Boriani et al. performed a combined retrospective and prospective cohort study of patients being treated for chordoma of the spine.* *Patients treated solely with radiation or intralesional excision as well as those treated with a combination of intralesional excision with radiotherapy experienced tumor recurrences with two years.* *All patients died as a result of tumor recurrence.* *Patients undergoing en bloc resection with or without radiation had a 66% continuously disease-free state at 8 years.* Those that did recur after en bloc resection had positive margins. **They concluded** that margin free en bloc resection is treatment associated with the best continuously disease-free survival greater than 5-years.

*Laufer et al. performed a retrospective review of patients with metastatic epidural spinal cord compression treated with separation surgery and either low-dose or high-dose hypofractionated stereotactic radiosurgery.* **The authors found** a 4.1% recurrence rate a 1-year following high-dose hypofractionated stereotactic radiosurgery compared to 22.2% with low-dose hypofractionated therapy. **They concluded** that high-dose hypofractionated therapy following separation surgery was a superior treatment in the setting of metastatic epidural spinal cord compression.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Vertebral osteomyelitis is treated with IV antibiotics following a culture identification of the offending organism. Surgical treatment is usually not necessary unless there are neurologic deficits from an epidural abscess or signs of instability. In most cases, a decompression with irrigation and debridement is sufficient. An en bloc corpectomy would not be indicated with osteomyelitis.
Answer 2: Multiple myeloma is usually treated with chemotherapy and radiation. In cases of impending fracture of long bones or signs of spinal instability, surgical stabilization is indicated. An en bloc corpectomy will provide no added outcome benefit and expose the patient to extensive surgical morbidity.
Answer 3: Langerhan's cell histiocytosis treatment involves radiotherapy, corticosteroids, and bracing. In seldom cases where deformity results from the lesion, surgical correction and stabilization can be performed in addition to curettage and bone grafting.
Answer 5: Metastatic lung adenocarcinoma has a poor prognosis and a "curative" en bloc corpectomy is likely futile. The mainstay of treatment is palliative radiation when pain is the primary complaint. If there is metastatic epidural spinal cord compression, separation surgery followed by stereotactic radiation is indicated. Surgical stabilization can be performed when there are findings suggesting instability.
