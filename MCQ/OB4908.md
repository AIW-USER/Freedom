## Question

A 37-year-old healthy construction worker presents 1 week after stepping on a nail at work. He reports continued pain, fever, and drainage, and his clinical photo is shown in Figure A. Treatment with 7-days of amoxicillin - clavulanic acid did not improve his symptoms. Which additional antibiotic are you most likely to prescribe?

![[q4908-a-screen-shot-2014-10-20-at-1-33-14-pm.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Doubling the dose of Amoxicillin
2. Trimethoprim and sulfamethoxazole
3. Ciprofloxacin
4. Rifampin
5. Vancomycin

## Correct option

**3. Ciprofloxacin**

## Answer explanation

Pseudomonas aeruginosa is the most common organism to cause osteomyelitis as a result of a nail through the sole of a shoe in a healthy host (still most common to cause infection is S. Aureus). Thus, a fluoroquinolone should be prescribed.

Following puncture through the sole of a foot, a tetanus booster should be given. There is no standard prophylactic antibiotics, but coverage for Pseudomonas should be provided. Fluoroquinolones are the only class of antibiotics with an oral formulation active against Pseudomonas. Dosage should be ciprofloxacin 750mg PO q12hours or levofloxaxin 750mg PO q24hours. Patients often present after 1 week, which often leads to requiring soft tissue and/or bony debridement (if osteomyelitis present). Wounds should be packed, allowing to heal by secondary intention.

*Decoster et al. reviewed the management of traumatic injuries, including nail-puncture wounds.* Recommended management include irrigation and debridement and coverage for Pseudomonas.

Figure A depicts a puncture wound through the sole of the foot secondary to stepping on a nail.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: Amoxicillin-clavulanic acid is a first line treatment for plantar puncture wounds as staphylococcus aureus and beta-hemolytic streptococci are common causes of skin and soft tissue infections and can produce wound infections after puncture However, doubling the dose amoxicillin is not recommended.
Answer 2: Trimethoprim and sulfamethoxazole does not cover for Pseudomonas as well as fluoroquinolones.
Answer 4: Rifampin is an important component of treatment for Mycobacterium tuberculosis and does not cover Pseudomonas.
Answer 5: Vancomycin covers gram positives and is ideal for MRSA.
