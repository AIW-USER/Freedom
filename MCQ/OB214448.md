## Question

## Options

1. In situ posterior fusion with instrumentation
2. Bed rest for 8 weeks
3. Early mobilization +/- orthosis immobilization
4. Posterior decompression, followed by posterior fusion with instrumentation, with sagittal plane correction
5. Anterior decompression and partial corpectomy, with anterior instrumentation

## Correct option

**3. Early mobilization +/- orthosis immobilization**

## Answer explanation

The patient is presenting with an L1 burst fracture after a fall without neurologic deficits and relatively maintained spinal alignment. The best initial treatment involves early mobilization with or without an orthosis.

Thoracolumbar burst fractures are typically the result of an axial load with flexion mechanism resulting in a compressive force through the anterior and middle columns of the vertebral body. This can lead to retropulsion of bone fragments into the spinal canal leading to compression of the neural elements. Fracture stability is defined by an intact posterior ligamentous complex or a Thoracolumbar Injury Classification and Severity Score (TLICSS) of 3 or lower. Fractures without neurologic compromise or instability can be treated with nonsurgical methods including hyperextension casting, thoracolumbosacral orthosis, or early mobilization without an orthosis. Patients with neurologic deficits should be treated with decompression and instrumented fusion.

*Wood et al. performed a retrospective review of 47 patients that sustained a thoracolumbar burst fracture and were either treated operatively or nonoperatively with sixteen to twenty-two-year follow-up.* **They reported** that kyphosis at final follow-up in the operative group was 13° compared to 19°, which was not significant. Patients that were treated nonoperatively reported significantly better pain scores and functional scores, than operatively treated patients. **The authors concluded** that at early follow-up there are few significant differences between operatively and nonoperatively treated patients, but long-term follow-up favors nonoperative treatment with improved pain and functional scores.

*Gnanenthiran et al. performed a meta-analysis of operative and nonoperative treatment for thoracolumbar burst fractures without neurologic deficit.* *Of the studies included in the analysis, there was no difference in Roland Morris Disability Questionnaire (RMDQ) score, pain, and return to work rates between the two groups, with improved kyphosis in the surgical group associated with higher costs and complications.* **They concluded** operative treatment for thoracolumbar burst fractures does not lead to better outcomes and is associated with higher treatment costs and increased complications.

*Bailey et al. performed a prospective randomized multicenter study comparing thoracolumbosacral orthosis and no orthosis for the treatment of thoracolumbar burst fractures without neurologic injury.* **They found** no significant difference between the two groups with respect to the RMDQ score at 3 months post-injury. **The authors concluded** that treatment with early mobilization without bracing reduces the cost and deconditioning associated with bracing without affecting outcomes.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1, 4, and 5: Surgical treatment of thoracolumbar burst fractures without neurologic injury is not associated with improved outcomes and leads to greater treatment costs and complications.
Answer 2: Prolonged bed rest is not an ideal treatment due to deconditioning and medical complications with this treatment. Early mobilization has been shown to produce good results without deconditioning.
