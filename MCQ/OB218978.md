## Question

A 57-year-old Caucasian male presents to your clinic for evaluation of low back pain. The patient states that it has been present for years and that the anti-inflammatory medication provided by his primary care doctor is no longer effective in managing his pain. Select radiographs of his spine are available for review in Figures A and B, and a clinical photograph of his eyes is available in Figure C. Blood tests ordered by his primary care doctor are notable for HLA-B27 positivity. Compared to those without his genetic predisposition, this patient is at an increased risk of synovitis secondary to which of the following organisms?

## Options

1. An alpha-hemolytic, gram-positive cocci which grows in chains
2. A gram-negative diplococci
3. A gram-negative, anaerobic bacilli
4. A gram-positive, anaerobic bacteria which occupies pilosebaceous glands
5. A gram-positive cocci which grows in clusters

## Correct option

**3. A gram-negative, anaerobic bacilli**

## Answer explanation

This patient has symptoms, radiographic, and clinical findings consistent with Ankylosing Spondylitis (AS) with HLA-B27 positive serologic markers. These patients are at an increased risk of developing Klebsiella pneumoniae (KP) synovitis. KP is a gram-negative anaerobic bacilli (Answer 3).

AS is a chronic seronegative autoimmune spondyloarthropathy characterized by bridging spinal osteophyte formation, enthesitis, sacroiliitis, and uveitis. AS affects approximately 0.2% of the Caucasian population, and usually presents in the 3rd decade of life. The exact mechanism behind the clinical manifestations of AS remains unknown, but the leading theory is that it is most likely due to an autoimmune reaction to an environmental pathogen in a genetically susceptible individual. Adding to this theory is the fact that over 90% of individuals with AS are HLA-B27 positive. Of note, HLA-B27 positivity is associated with the development of not only AS, but psoriatic arthritis, inflammatory bowel disease, and reactive synovitis/arthritis. With respect to reactive synovitis/arthritis, individuals with AS have a unique susceptibility to KP, an association that has been well established.

*Hu et al. investigated blood loss during total hip arthroplasty (THA) performed in patients with AS.* *Their study included 49 patients, and this cohort, based on Ankylosing Spondylitis Disease Activity Score (ASDAS), was divided into active (ASDAS ≥ 1.3, n = 32) and stable (ASDAS <1.3, n = 17) subgroups.* Overall, blood loss was much greater in the active group (2035.04 mL) when compared to the stable (1415.31 mL) group (p = 0.006). **The authors concluded** that individuals with active AS are at high risk for increased blood loss during THA.

*Zhu et al. performed a review of the etiology, pathogenesis, and treatments for AS.* With respect to KP, **the authors note** that this bacteria, a normal inhabitant of the human gut, may play a role as an exacerbating agent in the autoimmune process of AS. While the exact relationship remains unknown, the leading hypothesis is that KP influences AS development through a complex interplay with HLA-B27.

Incorrect Answers
Answer 1: This describes streptococcus pneumoniae, the leading cause of pneumonia and meningitis. AS does not confer an increased risk of synovitis secondary to S.pneumoniae.
Answer 2: This describes neisseria gonorrhoeae, the second most common bacterial sexually transmitted infection. AS does not confer an increased risk of synovitis secondary to N.gonorrhoeae.
Answer 4: This describes cutibacterium acnes, the leading cause of shoulder PJI. AS does not confer an increased risk of synovitis secondary to C.acnes.
Answer 5: This describes staphylococcus aureus, the most common cause of lower extremity PJI. AS does not confer an increased risk of synovitis secondary to S.aureus.
