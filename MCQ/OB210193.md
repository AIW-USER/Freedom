## Question

A 22-year-old female undergoes a hip arthroscopy for persistent hip pain unresponsive to conservative treatment. The structure shown in Figure A, determined to be the source of her symptoms, is released. Which of the following pre-operative findings was likely present in this patient?

![[q210193-a-hip-scope.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Snapping sensation with active hip flexion
2. Positive Ober's test
3. Loose bodies on plain radiographs
4. Pain with flexion, adduction, and internal rotation
5. Acetabular labral tear on magnetic resonance imaging (MRI)

## Correct option

**1. Snapping sensation with active hip flexion**

## Answer explanation

The structure undergoing release in Figure A is the psoas tendon. Internal snapping hip is most commonly caused by the psoas tendon sliding over the proximal femur producing pain and a snapping sensation.

Internal snapping hip syndrome or internal-type coxa saltans is the term used to describe snapping of the iliopsoas tendon over deeper structures within the pelvis. The snapping may be asymptomatic or it may cause debilitating groin pain. A complex anatomic relationship exists between the iliopsoas tendon at the musculotendinous junction and the anterior structures of the hip joint. The femoral head, the iliopectineal ridge, the iliopsoas bursa, and the iliofemoral ligament are theorized to be most commonly involved in the snapping phenomenon. The iliacus muscle may contribute to the snapping as it overlies the anterior capsule and superior pubic rami. Snapping hip syndrome was conventionally treated with an open release, but now it is often managed arthroscopically.

*Philippon et al. performed a study to determine the anatomic variance of the iliopsoas tendon.* *They used 53 non-matched, fresh-frozen, cadavers and exposed the iliopsoas muscle via a Smith-Petersen approach.* *They incised the iliopsoas and recorded each identifiable tendon.* **They found** the prevalence of a single-, double-, and triple-banded iliopsoas tendon was 28.3%, 64.2%, and 7.5%, respectively. **They note** that the psoas major tendon was consistently the most medial tendinous structure followed by the primary iliacus tendon laterally. **They conclude** that when releasing, the iliopsoas tendon, surgeons should be mindful that more than one tendon may be present.

*Ilizaliturri et al. performed a study to evaluate the short-term results of 2 different techniques of arthroscopic iloipsoas tendon release for the treatment of internal snapping hip syndrome.* *Patients were randomized to a group with release at the lesser trochanter and a group with transcapsular psoas release from the peripheral compartment.* There was no statistical difference found when comparing Western Ontario MacMaster scores. **They conclude** that iliopsoas tendon release at the level of the lesser trochanter or at the level of the hip joint using a transcapsular technique is effective and reproducible.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: A positive Ober's test suggests tightness of the tensor fascia lata and the iliotibial band. This may be implicated in external snapping hip syndrome.
Answer 3: Loose bodies on plain radiographs may be seen with synovial chondromatosis which may cause intra-articular snapping hip syndrome. This is treated with loose body removal.
Answer 4: Pain with flexion, adduction, and internal rotation may be seen in femoroacetabular impingement (FAI).
Answer 5: Acetabular labral tears visualized on MRI may be present secondary to FAI. They are treated with labral repair versus debridement.
