## Question

A 7-year-old boy with Down syndrome is seen in your office for initial evaluation. Lateral cervical spine radiographs obtained in neutral, flexion, and extension reveal an atlanto-dens interval of 5 mm. He has no symptoms of neck pain or difficulty walking. Physical examination is normal. What is the most appropriate management?

## Options

1. Follow-up consisting of symptom surveillance, neurologic examination, and further radiographic evaluation
2. Arthrodesis of the occiput to C2
3. Anterior cervical fusion
4. A soft cervical collar to be worn during physical activities
5. Nighttime head halter traction to prevent sleep apnea

## Correct option

**1. Follow-up consisting of symptom surveillance, neurologic examination, and further radiographic evaluation**

## Answer explanation

Although the use of routine radiographic screening of patients with Down syndrome for upper cervical instability is controversial, the child with documented instability should have follow-up consisting of symptom surveillance, neurologic examination, and further radiographic evaluation. Surgical treatment is indicated in patients with progressive or acute onset of neurologic symptoms, less than or equal to 14 mm of space available for the cord on radiographs, and MRI or CT evidence of cord compression. A soft cervical collar or the use of traction is not indicated in the management of asymptomatic cervical instability in children with Down syndrome.
