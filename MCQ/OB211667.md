## Question

## Answer explanation

This patient sustained a lateral patellar dislocation that spontaneously reduced. Most commonly, osteochondral fragments occur off the medial patellar facet, as shown in Figure C.

Acute traumatic lateral patellar dislocations most commonly occur following non-contact twisting injuries with the knee extended. Many patients will self-reduce in the field following contraction of the quadriceps. The medial patellofemoral ligament (MPFL) originates between the medial epicondyle and adductor tubercle and is the primary restraint to lateral patellar subluxation in the first 30 degrees of knee flexion. With reduction of the patella, an osteochondral fracture commonly occurs off the medial patella (Figure C). Less commonly, a fracture may be seen from the lateral femoral condyle. In the setting of a large osteochondral fracture, patients should be evaluated for operative intervention, which may consist of fragment removal or open reduction and internal fixation.

Schepsis and Rogers review the indications and techniques for MPFL reconstruction. They report that the MPFL is the primary static restraint to lateral patellar subluxation. They conclude that reconstruction should be in patients who have continued mechanical instability.

*Fithian et al. review the epidemiology and natural history of acute patellar dislocations.* **They report** that patellar dislocators who present with a history of dislocation are more likely to be female, are older, and have a greater risk for subsequent patellar instability than first time patellar dislcoators. **They conclude** that these factors must be kept in mind when evaluating patients with a patellar dislocation.

Figure A is a merchant view radiograph demonstrating a lateral facet fracture of the patella. Figure B demonstrates a knee dislocation. Figure C shows a medial osteochondral fracture of the patella as occurs with lateral patellar dislocation. Figure D is a lateral femoral condyle fracture. Figure E shows a fracture of the inferior pole of the patella. Figure F is the clinical image of a patient with a lateral patellar dislocation.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Patella fractures following dislocation most commonly occur on the medial aspect of the patella, not laterally.
Answer 2: The patient’s history and physical exam are consistent with a patellofemoral dislocation, not a dislocation of the knee proper.
Answer 4: Lateral femoral condyle fracture may occur following patellar dislocation, however, they are less common than fractures from the medial patella.
Answer 5: Transverse fractures of the patella are not associated with patellar dislocations.
