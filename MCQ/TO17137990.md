## Question

Spur sign is seen in which type of acetabular fracture.

## Options

1. Both column fracture
2. Anterior column with posterior hemi transverse fracture
3. Posterior column fracture
4. Anterior column fracture

## Correct option

**1. Both column fracture**

## Answer explanation

SOME IMPORTANT SIGNS/ ANGLES IN CONTEXT OF ACETABULAR FRACTURES

- SPUR SIGN: The spur sign is produced by a triangular fragment of iliac bone that remains attached to the sacroiliac joint but is separated from the fractured acetabulum. This spur is exposed when the fractured acetabular columns are medially displaced. The spur sign is indicative of fracture in both the anterior and posterior acetabular columns (both-column fracture)

Spur sign is seen in Obturator Oblique view

Other signs and angles seen in Acetabular fracture

- SEA GULL SIGN – Injury pattern in the shape of the wings of a sea gull. Seen when there is a direct impaction of the articular cartilage which is in the weight bearing dome. Seagull sign is suggestive of Anteromedial Dome impaction. It carries a poor prognosis if not detected and treated well. The sign is pathognomic for Posterior column fractures and needs to be surgically operated.

Best seen in Obturator oblique view

![[TO17137990-3.jpg]]

- ROOF ARC ANGLE OF MATTA - Angle between vertical line through femoral head and line through fracture site on all 3 views. It is used to determine whether fracture line has violated weight bearing dome. It determines if remaining intact acetabulum can maintain a stable and congruent joint. One line is drawn from the centre of the femoral head perpendicular to the femoral head and another line from the centre of the femoral head to the apex of the fracture line. The angle formed by the two lines is the Roof Arc Angle of Matta. Matta Roof Arc angle >45 degree on AP, Iliac and obturator oblique view not involving the weight bearing area of acetabulum can be conservatively managed. This angle is not applicable for Posterior wall fractures and Both column fractures.

![[TO17137990-4.png]]

SUBCHONDRAL ROOF ARC ON CT (Olson and Matta) – On the CT scan a cranial most 10mm of the acetabular articular surface, referred to as the CT subchondral arc is equivalent to 45-degree roof arc angle.

Ans: A
