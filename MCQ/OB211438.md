## Question

A surgeon at another facility performed an arthroscopic hip procedure on a 64-year-old female 3-years ago. She now presents to you complaining of functionally limiting ipsilateral hip pain that has been refractory to physical therapy and anti-inflammatory medications. An x-ray of her hip is shown in figure A. You determine that the patient would benefit most from conversion to a total hip arthroplasty (THA). You review her records to determine what may have caused her to require a total hip arthroplasty after her arthroscopic procedure. What preoperative factor from the index arthroscopic hip procedure has the highest predictive value for conversion to THA?

![[q211438-a-l-hip-arthritis.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Ipsilateral knee osteoarthritis
2. Age > 60
3. Body mass index (BMI) of 27
4. Female gender
5. Surgeon performs < 40 hip arthroscopy cases per year

## Correct option

**2. Age > 60**

## Answer explanation

Multiple options above are predictive of conversion to THA after hip arthroscopy. Of the options listed above, however, age greater than 60 years old is the most strongly predictive of the answer choices listed.

Hip arthroscopy is an intervention that is gaining in popularity due to its ability to preserve hip joint function and delay or prevent the need for later THA. Its use has increased over 88-fold since the late 1990s. Certain factors, however, can increase the risk of progression to THA. These factors include obesity (BMI > 30), age >50-60 years old (depending on the study), female gender, hip osteoarthritis, and low surgeon case volume. Protective factors include male gender, non-white race, and high volume surgeons.

*Degen et al. reviewed 8267 hip arthroscopy cases between 1998 and 2012 from the Statewide Planning and Research Cooperative System database in New York from 1998 to 2012.* **They identified** factors for revision surgery. Overall **they found** a 13.2% revision rate at an average of 1.7 years. Revision to THA occurred in 9.6% of all cases. **They identified** hip osteoarthritis (hazard ratio 2.72), age >50 (HR 2.09), and removal of loose body CPT code (HR 1.61) as factors that increase the risk for revision surgery. Protective factors were non-white race (HR 0.74), and surgeon case volume >164 cases per year (HR 0.42).

*Kester et al. reviewed 3957 patients between 2011 and 2012 in the same statewide database of New York for factors that predict conversion to THA after hip arthroscopy.* **They found** multiple significant independent factors to include obesity (BMI>30, odds ratio 5.6), age >60 years old (OR 3.4), hip osteoarthritis (OR 2.4), a surgeon with <40 cases per year (OR 1.9), tobacco use (OR 1.9), and female gender (OR 1.8). Overall conversion to THA was 5.9% at an average of 14.7 months.
