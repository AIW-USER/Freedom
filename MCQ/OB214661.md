## Question

A 66-year-old female with a long-standing history of rheumatoid arthritis previously underwent a constrained total elbow arthroplasty several years ago. She presents for routine follow up and complains only of slight elbow discomfort. This patient should be informed that the most common cause of failure with her previously performed surgery is what?

## Options

1. Aseptic loosening
2. Bushing wear
3. Infection
4. Instability
5. Ulnar neuropathy

## Correct option

**1. Aseptic loosening**

## Answer explanation

Total elbow arthroplasty is the most established definitive surgical treatment for severe rheumatoid arthritis of the elbow. The most common complication following use of a constrained total elbow arthroplasty (TEA) is aseptic loosening.

Traditionally, TEA designs have been divided into 3 categories: fully constrained rigid-hinge design, semi-constrained hinge design, and unconstrained unlinked design. Fully constrained designs have a lack of flexibility in the coronal plane and with rotation, resulting in high shear stresses and early loosening rates (this model is no longer in use). However, if a patient previously underwent a constrained TEA, they should be consoled regarding the most likely cause of failure with this design. Conversely, unconstrained models rely on sufficient bone stock and an intact soft tissue sleeve, whereas the semi-constrained designs can be used in an elbow with bone or soft tissue deficiency. Aseptic loosening and bushing wear are common complications following use of a constrained TEA.

*Skytta et al. evaluated the survival of different TEA designs and factors associated with survival.* **They reported** that the most frequent reason for revision in the unconstrained TEA was aseptic loosening. **They also noted** that there was a 1.5-fold elevated risk of revision in unspecialized hospitals as compared to those specialized in treatment of rheumatoid arthritis. **They concluded** that inferior survival rates of the TEAs performed in unspecialized hospitals demonstrates the importance of proper indications, surgical technique, and postoperative follow-up, and endorses the need for centralization of these operations at specialized units.

*Kokkalis et al. reported on the current management, treatment options, and outcomes of elbow arthritis.* **They report** that low-demand and elderly patients with end-stage painful arthritis may be successfully managed with a total elbow arthroplasty. **They concluded** that advances in implant design have led to substantial improvements in treatment of elbow arthritis.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Bushing wear is a common mode of failure following a constrained TEA, but less common than aseptic loosening
Answer 3: Infection is reported in about 8% of TEA cases, but is still less common than aseptic loosening in constrained TEA
Answer 4: Instability is more common in unconstrained and semi-constrained TEA designs
Answer 5: Ulnar neuropathy is less common than instability following use of a TEA
