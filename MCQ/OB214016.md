## Answer explanation

This patient has sustained a knee dislocation. If the medial femoral condyle buttonholes through the medial joint capsule, a “pucker sign” may be seen.

Knee dislocations are rare traumatic injuries that have a high association with vascular injury. While approximately 50% of these spontaneously reduce, it is possible for the medial femoral condyle to buttonhole through the medial capsule. This leads to incarceration of the joint capsule and soft tissues and indicates an irreducible posterolateral dislocation. Often a single attempt at closed reduction may be performed. However, if unsuccessful, repeat closed reduction attempts should not be performed due to the risk of skin necrosis.

*Woon et al. reviewed posterolateral dislocations of the knee.* **They report** that an examination that reveals an anteromedial "pucker" sign is pathognomonic for posterolateral knee dislocation. **They note** that attempts at closed reduction of posterolateral dislocations of the knee are usually impossible because of incarceration of medial soft tissue in the intercondylar notch and may only to delay surgical management and increase the risk of skin necrosis. **They conclude** that open reduction should be performed once vascular compromise is excluded.

*Jeevannavar et al. reported on a 32-year-old policeman who presented with right knee pain following a fall and* **found** to have a knee dislocation which was irreducible by closed manipulation. The patient was noted to have “puckering” of the skin overlying the medial femoral condyle. He underwent open reduction utilizing an anteromedial approach in which the interposed medial structures were dissected out and the medial femoral condyle was reduced. **They concluded** that the “pucker sign” or “dimple sign” is an important physical sign denoting irreducibility and need for prompt open reduction, as delay in treatment can result in skin necrosis.

Figure A is an AP radiograph of the knee demonstrating a posterolateral knee dislocation. Figure B is a clinical image illustrating a “pucker sign” suggesting buttonholing of the medial femoral condyle through the joint capsule. Figure C illustrates a deficiency superior to the patella suggesting a quadriceps rupture. Figure D illustrates lateral patellar dislocation. Figure E illustrates medial patella dislocation. Figure F illustrates a PCL sag sign which may be evident with a reduced knee dislocation but does not suggest medial joint capsule entrapment.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: A deficiency superior to the patella suggests a quadriceps rupture and is unlikely with a knee dislocation
Answer 3: Lateral patellar dislocation may occur with a posterolateral knee dislocation, however this finding itself does not indicate entrapment of the medial joint capsule and soft tissues, which would prevent closed reduction
Answer 4: Medial patellar dislocation may occur with a knee dislocation, however this finding itself does not indicate entrapment of the medial joint capsule and soft tissues, which would prevent closed reduction
Answer 5: A PCL sag sign which may be evident following the reduction of a knee dislocation, but does not suggest medial joint capsule and soft tissue entrapment, which would prevent closed reduction
