## Question

For which of the following pathologies would the orthotic shown in Figure A be most appropriate?

![[q211220-a-interpod-windlass.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Hallus rigidus
2. Supple adult pes cavus
3. Mild midfoot arthritis
4. Pes planovalgus
5. Rigid pes cavovarus

## Correct option

**2. Supple adult pes cavus**

## Answer explanation

Figure A shows a full-length semi-rigid insole orthotic with a lateral post and recession for the first ray. This orthosis is used for supple adult pes cavus or cavovarus foot deformity, most often seen in the setting of Charcot-Marie-Tooth (CMT).

Pes cavovarus deformity is a dynamic process driven by progressive muscular imbalance about the foot and ankle, most often due to an underlying neurologic condition such as CMT. The process typically begins with plantarflexion of the first ray resulting from the relatively stronger pull of the peroneus longus overpowering a weaker tibialis anterior. The posterior tibialis similarly overpowers the peroneus brevis, contributing to the elevated arch, or cavus deformity. The hindfoot further decompensates into varus due to the inverted posture induced by this muscular imbalance. This deformity is initially supple but will eventually progress to rigid, at which point it no longer corrects with semi-rigid orthoses. As a result, rigid cavovarus deformities may not respond adequately to orthotics and may eventually necessitate surgery.

*Younger et al. review the pathophysiology and treatment for the adult cavovarus foot deformity.* *The authors discuss the importance of the Coleman block test in evaluating the source and flexibility of the deformity, and how this ultimately guides management.* **They note** that while orthoses may accommodate early supple deformity, early surgical correction is recommended to avoid progression.

*Janisse et al. discuss the implementation of shoe modification and orthoses in the treatment of foot and ankle pathology.* *They review the four general categories of custom orthoses: accommodative, semi-rigid, rigid, and partial foot prosthesis, and highlight the utility of each in regard to specific pathologies that they may be used to address.*

Figures:
Figure A demonstrates an orthosis with a recessed first ray, lateral post, and slight heel lift, appropriate in the setting of cavus or cavovarus foot deformity.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Hallux rigidus requires a rigid extended shank for the first ray, or "Morton's extension".
Answer 3: Midfoot arthritis is best addressed conservatively with a rocker-bottom shoe.
Answer 4: Flexible pes planovalgus necessitates medial foot posting.
Answer 5: Accommodative orthoses and bracing are not recommended for rigid deformities.
