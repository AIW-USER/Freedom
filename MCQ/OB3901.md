## Question

A 36-year-old male recreational runner presents for evaluation of right lateral forefoot pain. He notes that these symptoms have persisted for many years, and that he has had multiple ankle sprains which caused him to take time off from running. He is currently training for a marathon and would like to address his pain. Examination reveals a stable ankle with talar tilt and anterior drawer testing. He has a supple subtalar joint and his peroneal tendons are non-tender and do not subluxate with circumduction. Weightbearing foot radiographs demonstrate no fracture. A clinical photograph is shown in Figure A. Which intervention would most likely allow him to continue his marathon training and reduce his symptoms?

## Options

1. A UCBL brace
2. A lace-up ankle brace
3. An orthotic with medial hindfoot posting
4. An orthotic with medial hindfoot posting and a rigid arch support
5. An orthotic with lateral hindfoot posting and first metatarsal head recess

## Correct option

**5. An orthotic with lateral hindfoot posting and first metatarsal head recess**

## Answer explanation

This patient has cavovarus feet and would benefit from an orthotic with lateral hindfoot posting and first metatarsal head recess.

It is crucial to correct the biomechanical anomaly present in subtle cavovarus foot for nonsurgical management to succeed. In addition to other treatments prescribed, such as stretching for plantar fasciitis or proprioceptive reeducation for functional ankle instability, orthotic management may offer further benefit. A custom or over-the-counter orthosis with a recessed first ray, a lateral wedge (including the forefoot and hindfoot), and a lowered medial longitudinal arch can result in significant improvement in pain and instability events.

*Chilvers et al. describe the subtle cavus foot alignment and its association with many injuries occurring in the athletic population, most commonly ankle instability and lateral foot overload.* **They note** that the goal is to recognize the posture of the cavus foot and to prevent future recurrence.

*Manoli et al. review the subtle cavovarus foot and* **identified** three factors that explain why it has not received similar emphasis in the workup of foot pain. These factors include training bias, subtle clinical examination findings, and the absence of a simple clinical or radiographic sign.

Figure A shows the clinical photograph of cavovarus feet with high medial arches. Illustration A is a typical orthotic used in a cavovarus foot with lateral wedging and a first metatarsal recess.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: University of California Biomechanics Laboratory (UCBL) orthoses are typically used for pes planovalgus.
Answer 2: A lace-up ankle brace can be considered following an ankle sprain, but does not address the primary pathology of this patient.
Answer 3 and 4: Medial hindfoot posting with or without arch support is typically used for patients with pes planovalgus.
