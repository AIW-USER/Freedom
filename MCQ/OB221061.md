## Question

A 19-year-old collegiate basketball player presents for evaluation of lateral foot pain one week after an inversion ankle sprain. A radiograph of the injured foot is shown in Figure A. You discuss management options and ultimately recommend intramedullary screw fixation. What is the proposed benefit of this treatment modality for this injury?

![[q221061-a-jones-fraktur-61m-cr-schraeg-001.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Decreased risk of sural nerve irritation compared with casting
2. Faster time to radiographic union and earlier return to play
3. Elimination of the risk of refracture
4. Avoidance of hardware-related complications
5. Prevention of posttraumatic midfoot arthritis

## Correct option

**2. Faster time to radiographic union and earlier return to play**

## Answer explanation

Intramedullary screw fixation of a zone 2 fifth metatarsal base fracture (Jones fracture) has been shown to provide faster time to union and earlier return to sport compared with nonoperative treatment, particularly in competitive athletes (answer choice 2).

Fifth metatarsal base fractures are classified by anatomic zone, with a zone 2 injury occurring at the metaphyseal-diaphyseal junction and extending into the fourth–fifth intermetatarsal articulation. Zone 2 fractures, commonly referred to as Jones fractures, occur in a vascular watershed region and have an increased risk of delayed union and nonunion. Nonoperative management with non–weightbearing immobilization is an option, but operative fixation is recommended in high-demand or athletic patients to decrease time to union and facilitate earlier return to play. Intramedullary screw fixation is the most commonly utilized surgical technique and has demonstrated high union rates and predictable healing in this population, with a generally favorable complication profile.

*Albloushi et al. reviewed the operative management of fifth metatarsal Jones fractures in athletes.* **They reported** that nonoperative treatment is associated with higher rates of delayed union and nonunion, which may prolong time away from sport. **They concluded** that surgical stabilization in athletes is associated with higher union rates and more reliable return to play compared with conservative management.

*Bucknam et al. evaluated a case series of patients undergoing primary intramedullary screw fixation for Jones fractures.* **They found** that all fractures healed with high patient satisfaction, with a mean time to radiographic union of approximately 10 weeks and return to unrestricted activity at approximately 13 weeks. **They concluded** that primary intramedullary screw fixation is a safe and reliable technique that allows early weight-bearing and expeditious return to full activities.

*Metzl et al. reviewed the diagnosis and treatment of fifth metatarsal Jones fractures, with a detailed description of their preferred surgical technique of intramedullary screw fixation.* *They described that these fractures are at increased risk for nonunion and refracture, particularly in athletes.* **They concluded** that intramedullary screw fixation is a commonly utilized, reliable operative strategy to promote union and facilitate return to sport.

Figure A demonstrates a zone 2 fifth metatarsal base fracture.

Incorrect answer choices:
Answer choice 1: Sural nerve irritation is rare but more common with operative intervention than casting.
Answer choice 3: Refracture remains a known complication even after surgical fixation and is not eliminated by intramedullary screw placement.
Answer choice 4: Hardware-related irritation and complications may occur after intramedullary screw fixation and are not avoided by operative treatment.
Answer choice 5: Posttraumatic midfoot arthritis is not a typically noted sequela of base of 5th metatarsal fractures.
