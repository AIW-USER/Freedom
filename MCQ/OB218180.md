## Question

A 66-year-old homemaker presents 3 weeks after an elective total knee arthroplasty with a red, inflamed, and painful knee, and the findings shown in Figure A. She notes that she did fall and scrape her knee on the pavement when getting into her car while leaving the hospital after surgery. She had minimal pain initially and didn't think about it again until it started to swell and become painful last week after a bout of diverticulitis that resolved with a course of antibiotics. On exam in the office, she has a mild extensor lag with an appreciable effusion and a small healing wound adjacent to her incision with surrounding ecchymosis. Which of the following is the next best step in management?

![[q218180-a-screenshot-2024-01-24-at-2-02-20-pm.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Aspiration to evaluate for acute periprosthetic joint infection
2. CT scan to assess for occult periprosthetic fracture
3. Consent and book for debridement and irrigation with component retention and polyethylene exchange
4. Offer reassurance and close followup for presumed knee contusion
5. Ultrasound to investigate the integrity of her quadriceps tendon and extensor mechanism

## Correct option

**1. Aspiration to evaluate for acute periprosthetic joint infection**

## Answer explanation

The patient's clinical picture, post-operative history of events, and radiograph demonstrating a large effusion in the setting of a possible direct inoculation event versus hematogenous spread from recent diverticulitis make acute periprosthetic joint infection a distinct possibility that should be ruled out with an aspiration.

Acute periprosthetic joint infection has been defined by the CDC as occurring < 90 days from the time of the index joint arthroplasty procedure. From a surgical standpoint, and in more practical terms for revision arthroplasty, it is often defined as an infection occurring within 3-6 weeks of the index procedure, as this will dictate the recommendation for revision surgery. Acute infections can occur via direct inoculation (typically through wound dehiscence or formation of a sinus tract) or by hematogenous spread (i.e., dental work, abdominal infection, etc). Typically, beyond 4-6 weeks of infection, debridement and irrigation with component retention is not advised, given that biofilm production and consolidation have typically begun, often becoming irreversible by 4 weeks. Because of this, two-stage reconstruction is recommended as the gold standard revision option to best eradicate infection once irreversible biofilom production has taken place.

*Sharkey et al. reviewed the reasons that total knee arthroplasties are failing today, reflecting on 10 years of experience.* **The authors determined** the frequency and cause of failure after total knee arthroplasty (TKA) in a cohort of 781 TKA revisions and compared the results with those reported by a similar investigation conducted 10 years prior. **They noted** that the most common failure mechanisms were: loosening (39.9%), infection (27.4%), instability (7.5%), periprosthetic fracture (4.7%), and arthrofibrosis (4.5%), with infection being the most common failure mechanism for early revision (<2 years from primary) and aseptic loosening the most common reason for late revision. **They concluded** that the percentage of revisions performed for polyethylene wear, instability, arthrofibrosis, malalignment and extensor mechanism deficiency has decreased.

*Saxena et al. published on biofilm architecture, resistance, quorum sensing, and control mechanisms.* **The authors note** that the process of biofilm development can be broadly grouped into four different phases: the attachment phase (on the surface of inanimate or tissue), the sessile growth phase governed by intercellular interaction (quorum sensing (QS) factors), the biofilm maturation phase (induced exopolymeric substances (EPS) matrix synthesis), and the detachment phase (induction of disassembly factors). **They concluded** that quorum sensing (QS) plays essential roles in biofilm development, with pathogenic bacteria in biofilms utilizing QS mechanisms to activate virulence and develop antibiotic resistance.

Figure A is a lateral radiograph of a knee status post total knee arthroplasty demonstrating a large effusion with foci of gas and pre-patellar swelling.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: There is no indication of fracture on the XR, and the patient's clinical picture is more concerning for infection despite her initial fall post-operatively.
Answer 3: Though she will likely require operative intervention as such, an aspiration would be the best first step to evaluate the synovial cell count and infectious markers.
Answer 4: Reassurance is an unacceptable treatment modality in a patient who may have an acute post-operative infection, in which treatment delay could drastically alter the available treatment methods.
Answer 5: Though the patient has pre-patellar swelling and an extensor lag, her clinical presentation is much more consistent with an acute periprosthetic joint infection than an extensor mechanism rupture.
