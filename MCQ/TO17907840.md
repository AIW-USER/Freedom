## Question

Athlete is having sore throat and cervical lymphadenoathy. Most common complication associated with this condition that might affect the long-term performance of the athlete would be

## Options

1. Thrombocytopenia
2. Myocarditis
3. Chronic fatigue syndrome
4. Hepatitis

## Correct option

**3. Chronic fatigue syndrome**

## Answer explanation

Ans: C

The likely diagnosis from the history is Infectious mononucleosis (IMN). IMN is an infection caused by Epstein-Barr virus which is a Double-stranded DNA gamma herpesvirus that was first isolated in 1964 from Burkitt lymphoma tissue.

IMN is a benign lymphoproliferative disease somewhat linked to Burkitt lymphoma, Hodgkin lymphoma, and nasopharyngeal carcinoma

Clinical features: -

Classical “Triad” of IMN” includes

- Fatigue and pharyngitis are the most debilitating symptoms and often present as the chief complaint.
- Posterior cervical lymph nodes >> axillary and inguinal lymphadenopathy.
- Athlete presenting with nothing more than fatigue, lack of energy, or diminished performance.

Other features -- posterior palatine petechiae (one third of cases), jaundice (10-15% cases), exudative pharyngitis, rash, and splenomegaly.

A rash is seen in about 10% to 40% of patients. The rash is transient and generalized with maculopapular, petechial, or urticarial features.

![[TO17907840-1.png]]

Approximately 90% of patients have mildly elevated liver enzymes facilitating in the diagnosis of IMN.

Exudative pharyngitis with concomitant tonsillar enlargement (lymphoid hyperplasia and pharyngeal inflammation) are classical.

![[TO17907840-2.png]]

Splenomegaly occurs from lymphocytic infiltration enlarging the spleen beyond protection from the rib cage and creating an organ that is susceptible to rupture either spontaneously or traumatically. It generally gets resolved in 4 to 6 weeks.

The acute phase of IM can resolve as quickly as 7 days, but usually takes between 2 and 3 weeks from the onset of symptoms.

This is followed by a recovery period that may take up to 2 to 3 months.

IMN is typically self-limiting, but an acute EBV infection is a risk factor for chronic fatigue, with symptoms lasting in excess of 6 months.

List of Complications

- Sore throat (95%),
- Cervical lymphadenopathy (80%),
- Fatigue (70%),
- Upper respiratory symptoms (65%),
- Headache (50%),
- Decreased appetite (50%),
- Fever (47%) and
- Myalgia (45%).

Most findings last 10 days or less but fatigue and cervical lymphadenopathy often persist for at least 3 weeks.

Rare complications that occur in just 1% of patients include:

- Airway obstruction because of oropharyngeal inflammation,
- Streptococcal pharyngitis,
- Meningoencephalitis,
- Hemolytic anemia and
- Thrombocytopenia.

Splenic rupture may also occur in < 1% in patients but is the most feared complication, which sometimes keeps athletes out of competition for a few weeks. A reasonable recommendation rather is that athletes may resume contact sports after 3 weeks of illness as long as they have no ongoing signs or symptoms of acute EBV infection.
