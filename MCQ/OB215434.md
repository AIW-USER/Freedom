## Correct option

## Answer explanation

Osteosarcoma is best treated with neoadjuvant chemotherapy, wide resection, and adjuvant chemotherapy.

Osteosarcoma represents the most common type of bone sarcoma. It has a bimodal distribution and is most commonly found in children and young adults with a second peak occurring in older patients (>55-years-old) who have Paget’s disease. The distal femur and proximal tibia are the two most common sites of disease; however, the proximal humerus, proximal femur, and pelvis are also commonly reported. Most patients are first diagnosed as having Stage IIB (high grade, extra-compartmental, no metastases) disease. Radiographs characteristically show a blastic, destructive lesion often described as having a “sun-burst” or “hair on end” pattern. A biopsy is required for the diagnosis and will reveal significant atypia with lacey osteoid deposition. Treatment consists of neoadjuvant chemotherapy given for 8-12 weeks preoperatively, followed by wide surgical resection and maintenance chemotherapy for 6-12 months postoperatively.

*Isakoff et al. review the survival of osteosarcoma of the pelvis.* **They report** that while osteosarcoma infrequently metastasizes to the pelvis, it still requires surgery plus systemic chemotherapy. **They confirm** poor survival in patients with pelvic osteosarcoma and conclude that survival with metastatic disease in the absence of a pelvic primary tumor is similar to that for localized or metastatic pelvic osteosarcoma.

*Salunke et al. review surgical management of pelvic bone sarcoma with internal hemipelvectomy.* **They report** that the goals of pelvic sarcoma treatment are adequate oncologic local control, maintenance of optimum function while maintaining good quality of life. **They conclude** that internal hemipelvectomy provides good functional outcomes so long as proper patient selection, preoperative planning, and wide surgical margins are obtained in patients with pelvic osteosarcoma.

Figure A demonstrates a hypercellular cartilaginous tumor consistent with chondrosarcoma. Figure B shows a biphasic pattern with epithelial and fibrous mesenchymal cells consistent with an adamantinoma. Figure C is an example of tumor cells with significant atypia and lacey osteoid deposition consistent with osteosarcoma.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Chondrosarcoma is best treated with wide resection alone as it is often radio and chemo resistant.
Answer 2: Adamantinomas are best treated with wide resection and are a low-grade malignancy, thus radio and chemotherapy are not typically used.
Answer 3: Synovial sarcomas are a soft tissue sarcoma most commonly treated with wide surgical resection and radiation.
Answer 5: Myxoid liposarcoma is also a soft tissue sarcoma most commonly treated with wide surgical resection and adjuvant radiotherapy.
