## Question

A 24-year-old male sustains a left femoral shaft fracture in a motor vehicle collision. After his other non-orthopaedic injuries are stabilized, he is indicated for femoral nail fixation. What is most correct with regard to outcomes when planning the size of an intramedullary nail to fix his femur fracture?

## Options

1. Patients with larger canal diameters benefit from larger nail diameters
2. The rate of union is equivalent between 10 mm nails and larger diameter nails
3. Larger nails are beneficial irrespective of canal diameter
4. The rate of union is equivalent between 8 mm nails and larger diameter nails
5. When using retrograde nails, a larger diameter nail should be used

## Correct option

**2. The rate of union is equivalent between 10 mm nails and larger diameter nails**

## Answer explanation

Union rate of femoral shaft fractures treated with 10 mm diameter nails is equal to the union rate after treatment with larger diameter nails.

Femoral shaft fractures are treated with intramedullary nails. Historically, it has been thought that patients with larger femoral canals would be better served with larger diameter femoral nails. Studies in children have determined that flexible nail fixation is optimal when occupying >80% of the femoral canal. However, adult studies have established that when accounting for femoral canal diameter, femoral nails with a 10 mm diameter are associated with equal union rates when compared with femoral nails with larger diameters.

*Yoon et al. performed a retrospective review in which they examined time to union and union rate for 287 femoral shaft fractures stratified by intramedullary nail (IMN) diameter.* **They found** that there was no difference in time to union or union rate for 10 mm, 11 mm, or 13 mm IMNs even when accounting for the difference between the size of the IMN and the size of the femoral canal at the isthmus. **The authors concluded** that a 10 mm diameter IMN is sufficient for femoral shaft fracture treatment irrespective of canal diameter.

*Serrano et al. performed a retrospective review in which they examined nonunion after IMN of 484 femoral shaft fractures with results stratified for nail size, antegrade vs retrograde, and the difference between the size of the IMN and the size of the femoral canal at the isthmus after reaming.* **They found** similar healing rates regardless of IMN diameter, antegrade vs retrograde, and difference in nail-canal size. **They concluded** that a 10 mm femoral IMN can be considered the standard for the treatment of acute femoral shaft fractures regardless of the entry point.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1 and 3: Traditionally, it was thought to be true that larger femoral canals should be treated with larger diameter femoral nails; however, recent studies have refuted that.
Answer 4: This has not been proven to be true. In fact, many orthopaedic implant companies do not offer an adult 8 mm diameter femoral nail.
Answer 5: There is no evidence that larger diameter nails should be used for retrograde IMNs.
