## Question

Not true about Romosuzumab is

## Options

1. Approved for prevention of osteoporosis in post menopausal women
2. Romosuzumab treatment should be followed by antiresorptive therapy
3. Indicated for treatment of severe osteoporosis
4. Sclerostin inhibitor

## Correct option

**1. Approved for prevention of osteoporosis in post menopausal women**

## Answer explanation

Ans: A

EXPLANATION: -

ROMOSOZUMAB
It’s a recently introduced treatment option for osteoporosis management.
Mechanism of action: Sclerostin is an osteocyte-derived secreted glycoprotein that binds to low-density lipoprotein receptor proteins LRP5 and LRP6 (low-density lipoprotein receptor protein 5/6), preventing the activation of the Wnt/β-catenin pathway that normally stimulates osteoblasts. Thus sclerostin acts as a negative regulator of bone formation. This led to the development of romosozumab, a humanized monoclonal sclerostin-neutralizing antibody (Scl-Ab) that binds to and inhibits sclerostin. This binding inhibits sclerostin’s ability to bind to the LRP5 and LRP6 receptor proteins. Romosozumab allows the Wnt/β-catenin pathway to occur, thus promoting osteogenesis and inhibiting bone resorption to a lesser extent. This increases cortical and trabecular bone mass, which is beneficial in treating osteoporosis.
Indications for use: FDA has approved romosozumab on April 9, 2019 to treat osteoporosis in postmenopausal women who are at high risk of fracture. It has not been approved for prevention of osteoporosis. Postmenopausal women with severe osteoporosis at very high risk of fracture are defined as
• T-score <− 2.5 (at hip or spine) and
• Vertebral or hip fracture in the past 24 months or history of > 2 osteoporotic vertebral fractures,
or
• Very high fracture risk (FRAX major osteoporosis fracture > 30%, FRAX hip fracture > 4.5%)

Dose and guidelines: Romosozumab is administered as a once-a-month subcutaneous injection for 12 months. The recommended monthly dose is 210 mg, regardless of body weight. Each prefilled syringe is 105 mg/1.17 mL; thus, two prefilled syringes should be administered to reach the recommended dose of 210 mg. The healthcare provider injects with possible injection sites, including the patient’s thigh, abdomen, or upper arm. Injection sites should be altered each month. Patients receiving romosozumab should also receive adequate calcium and vitamin D supplementation.
After 12 months of monthly injections, romosozumab should be discontinued as its effects wane. Studies have shown that after one year of discontinuing romosozumab, BMD levels return to baseline; therefore, it is recommended that after discontinuing romosozumab, the patient continues osteoporosis treatment with antiresorptive therapy (alendronate/ denosumab/zoledronic acid) to maintain and continue increased bone mass density.
