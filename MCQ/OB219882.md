## Question

As part of a quality improvement project, you are tasked with creating a tiered post-op pain medication protocol for your ambulatory surgical center. You make three tiers that increase in multimodal options as well as the strength and duration of narcotics. The first tier includes only ibuprofen and acetaminophen. Which procedures would be most appropriate for receiving tier-one medications only?

## Options

1. Carpal tunnel release, trigger finger release, cubital tunnel release
2. Carpal tunnel release, trigger finger release, proximal row carpectomy
3. Trigger finger release, first dorsal compartment release, distal radius ORIF
4. Flexor tendon repair, extensor tendon repair, scaphoid non-union
5. Proximal row carpectomy, scaphoid nonunion, distal radius ORIF

## Correct option

**1. Carpal tunnel release, trigger finger release, cubital tunnel release**

## Answer explanation

Over-the-counter (OTC), narcotic-free pain management is most appropriate following soft tissue procedures (Answer 1).

In the United States, the practice of prescribing opioid analgesia for postoperative pain control has steadily increased over the last two decades. Consequently, there has been an associated increase in opioid-specific hospital admissions related to inappropriate use and overuse. In hand surgery, 2 to 5 times more opioids are prescribed than the patients consume. As a result, a concerted effort has been made to reduce opioid prescribing after hand surgery. Several studies have demonstrated that non-narcotic analgesics provide equivalent pain control to opioids for soft-tissue hand surgery.

*Weinheimer et al. conducted a prospective, randomized, double-blinded controlled trial comparing ibuprofen and acetaminophen vs. hydrocodone and acetaminophen for soft tissue hand procedures.* *Patients undergoing carpal tunnel release, trigger finger release, first dorsal compartment release, or ganglion cyst excision were randomized to either the opioid or non-opioid arm and followed for two weeks after surgery.* **The authors found** no difference in daily pain intensity scores, and there was no difference in time until pain-free, with a median of 5 days in the opioid group and 3 days in the non-opioid group. Based on these results, **the authors recommend** a combination of acetaminophen and ibuprofen as a safe and effective postoperative pain regimen for soft tissue hand surgery procedures.

*Sraj reviewed their experience with over-the-counter (OTC), narcotic-free, postoperative pain management in hand surgery.* *The authors included patients who underwent surgery using wide-awake local anesthesia only with no tourniquet (WALANT) technique.* Procedures were limited to soft tissue only and included carpal tunnel release, cubital tunnel release, trigger finger release, first dorsal compartment release, dorsal wrist ganglion cyst excision, hand or finger mass excision, percutaneous needle fasciotomy, flexor tendon repair, and extensor tendon repair. **They found** that very few patients (6%) required a prescription for pain control after any of these procedures. **The authors conclude** that patients can successfully self-manage their postoperative pain with OTC analgesics following soft tissue procedures.

Incorrect Answers 2-5: Literature supports using OTC medications for soft tissue hand procedures. Proximal row carpectomy, distal radius ORIF, and scaphoid non-union are more likely to require additional medications when compared with soft tissue-only procedures.
