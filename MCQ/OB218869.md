## Question

A 13-year-old male is thrown off the back of an all-terrain vehicle and brought to the emergency department by local emergency services. Due to the patient's inability to bear weight, radiographs are obtained and are available for review in Figure A. In discussing the risks, benefits, and alternatives of surgery with the patient's father, as part of the informed consent process, you mention that the patient's age increases his risk of a specific complication. Which of the following postoperative complications is the patient at an increased risk of experiencing secondary to his age?

![[q218869-a-type-iii.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Coxa vara
2. Infection
3. Leg Length Discrepency
4. Nonunion
5. Osteonecrosis

## Correct option

**5. Osteonecrosis**

## Answer explanation

This patient has sustained a Delbet Type III (cervicotrochanteric) proximal femur fracture. The patient's advanced age puts him at an increased risk of experiencing osteonecrosis (Answer 5).

Proximal femur fractures in the pediatric population are rare injuries. They are caused by high-energy trauma, and often occur in the setting of polytrauma. Males are more commonly affected as compared to females and, in the adolescent population, these injuries typically occur in motor/recreational vehicle accidents. These injuries are classified according to the Delbet classification (Illustration A). An important takeaway from the Delbet classification is the relationship between fracture proximity and the rate of postoperative osteonecrosis secondary to AVN; in summary, the more proximal a fracture, the higher the rate of postoperative osteonecrosis. Treatment of these injuries is usually operative, with the preferred technique dependent upon patient age and the Delbet classification of the fracture.

*Patterson et al. provide a comprehensive review of the workup, classification and treatment of pediatric proximal femur fractures.* **They note** that the Delbet classification system not only helps to guide operative management but also helps to stratify patient risk of osteonecrosis. **They note** that the goal of surgery is an anatomic reduction with stable fixation and that these injuries are fraught with complications including, but not limited to, osteonecrosis, physeal arrest, coxa vara and nonunion.

*Riley et al. performed a prospective study investigating whether time to reduction had any impact on the rate of the development of osteonecrosis.* *Their study reviewed the charts of 255 patients over a 25+ year span and ultimately included 44 fractures in their analysis.* Overall, 9/44 (20%) of patients developed osteonecrosis, and age ≥ 11 was the only statistically significant independent predictor for the development of osteonecrosis. Interestingly, the authors were unable to demonstrate that earlier time to reduction was protective against the development of osteonecrosis

*Moon et al. performed a dual-armed investigation into risk factors for the development of osteonecrosis in pediatric patients with femoral neck fractures.* *Their study included 25 cases of their own and a meta-analysis of 360 cases.* Following logistic regression analysis, **the authors found** that only age and fracture type were predictive of the future development of osteoneecrosis. In their study, older children were 1.14 times more likely to develop osteonecrosis for each year of advancing age, and Delbet I-III fractures were 15, 6 and 4 times, respectively, more likely to develop osteonecrosis than type IV fractures.

Illustration A lists the Delbet classification for pediatric proximal femur fractures, along with the associated incidence and rates of osteonecrosis and nonunion.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1-4: Coxa vara, infection, leg length discrepancy and nonunion following pediatric proximal femur fracture have not been found to be associated with patient age.
