## Question

A 12-year-old female presents to the office as a referral following a positive scoliosis screening exam at her school. On radiograph, she is found to have a right thoracic curve measuring 28 degrees. You recommend TLSO bracing. Which of the following is true as it relates to bracing in this patient?

## Options

1. Deformity correction is optimized with brace wear of between 12-15 hours/day
2. Time in the brace is most closely linked to treatment success
3. Amount of curve correction on in-brace radiographs is best indicator of treatment success
4. Physical therapy should be attempted prior to starting brace therapy
5. Brace therapy should be initiated only once curve magnitude exceeds 30 degrees

## Correct option

**2. Time in the brace is most closely linked to treatment success**

## Answer explanation

The most important factor to predict success of TLSO bracing to prevent surgical treatment is the amount of daily time a patient is in the brace.

Adolescent idiopathic scoliosis (AIS) is the most common form of scoliosis, typically presenting in females age 10-18 years. For curve magnitudes 25 to 45 degrees, patients are treated with a thoracolumbar orthosis (TLSO) in an attempt to stop curve progression. The most important factor as it relates to success of treatment is the amount of time the patient wears the brace. It has been reported that a 50% reduction in curve progression occurs if the patient is compliant with brace wear of at least 13 hours per day. Given that the success of brace treatment is dose dependent, patients who wear the brace for >17.6 hours per day have been found to have a 90% success rate in preventing curve progression to >50 degrees.

*Weinstein et al review the effects of bracing in adolescents with idiopathic scoliosis.* **They report** a success of treatment with bracing of 72% compared to 48% in those patients who were simply treated with observation. **They conclude** that the benefits of bracing increased in a dose dependent manner with increased success seen with increased brace wear.

*Dolan et al review adolescent idiopathic scoliosis bracing success.* *They compared two populations who differed primarily in the total number of hours of in-brace time.* **They conclude** that bracing is an effective treatment for AIS and success improves with increased time in the brace.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: TLSO bracing is used to stop progression and is not used to correct the deformity, regardless of how much the brace is worn.
Answer 3: Curve correction in the brace is a component to success, but is not as closely linked to success as the amount of time spent in the brace.
Answer 4: Bracing is an appropriate treatment, regardless of therapy status once curve progression exceeds 25 degrees.
Answer 5: Bracing should be prescribed when curve progression exceeds 25 degrees as this gives the best chance at slowing progression.
