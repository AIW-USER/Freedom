## Question

The American Joint Commission on Cancer (AJCC) provides the most used cancer staging systems within the United States. These staging systems are important for providing prognostic implications and determining individualized treatment plans, but also for standardization in research. In 2017, they released the AJCC Cancer Staging Manual (8th edition), which stands as their most updated edition. Regarding the updated classification for soft tissue sarcomas, which of the following major changes was made?

## Options

1. It now considers post-operative surgical margins part of the staging system
2. It now contains separate classifications for each individual histologic subtype
3. It now lumps together both soft tissue sarcomas and primary bone sarcomas
4. It now considers hospital subtype (community vs. academic) as part of the staging system
5. It now separates appendicular tumors from other anatomic sites (i.e. intra-abdominal)

## Correct option

**5. It now separates appendicular tumors from other anatomic sites (i.e. intra-abdominal)**

## Answer explanation

The AJCC 8th edition classification for soft tissue sarcomas has made some changes that are controversial within the orthopedic community. However, a universally accepted change was their separation of appendicular/truncal location tumors from other anatomic locations (i.e., head and neck, visceral abdomen/thoracic, and retroperitoneum; Answer 5).

Soft tissue sarcomas are rare entities, representing the vast minority of cancer cases reported overall. They are generally thought to arise from mesenchymal tissues and subsequently are grouped for cancer staging purposes. However, they are a diverse group of cancers with more than 50 distinct histologic subtypes, each with unique clinical features, biology, responses to therapy, and prognoses. Due to their heterogeneous complexity, no single staging system can reasonably encompass a panacea. The AJCC is a widely utilized cancer staging system in the United States and relies on traditional TNM methodology. Their most recent (i.e., 8th edition) very appropriately separated primary tumor locations, which has been widely accepted. However, they also made controversial changes that have been met with scrutiny within the orthopedic community (i.e., isolated lymph node involvement without any other distant metastases was considered equivalent to lung metastases; Illustration A).

*Fisher and colleagues at MD Anderson Cancer Center utilized the National Cancer Database (NCDB) to perform a validation study comparing the performance of the 7th and 8th editions of the AJCC staging systems for soft tissue sarcomas.* **They found** that the newer, expanded T3/T4 tumor distinction may not be clinically relevant in survival outcomes. Further, **they found** that patients with isolated nodal involvement have an intermediate survival outcome between no metastatic disease and distant metastatic disease. **The authors conclude** that the primary tumor site distinction changes were appropriate; however, the expanded T3/T4 distinction and lumping isolated nodal involvement with distant metastatic disease were based on single institutional data and likely inaccurate.

*Dr. Yoon from Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center provided an editorial response to the aforementioned Fisher and colleagues’ article.* *He commends them for their work and also notes other potential limitations within the AJCC’s staging system for soft tissue sarcomas.* **The author concludes** while primary tumor anatomic location finally has an appropriate distinction, future revisions may also need to address biomarkers.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Postoperative surgical margins are correlated to both local recurrence and overall survival (i.e., grossly positive surgical margins result in worsening morbidity and mortality). However, sstaging should be determined before tumor removal to determine prognosis and potential neoadjuvant treatments. Therefore, no widely used classification system incorporates surgical margins as part of tumor staging.
Answer 2: There are over 50 separate subtypes of soft tissue sarcoma with various clinical behaviors. The AJCC seeks to provide comprehensive staging systems and, therefore, lumps these subtypes altogether. While this helps provide broad standardized information, relevant granular differences are unable to be reasonably incorporated.
Answer 3: Soft tissue sarcomas and primary bone sarcomas are different clinical entities despite both arising from mesenchymal stem cell origins. Therefore, the AJCC appropriately separates soft tissue sarcomas from primary bone sarcomas (as do other staging systems).
Answer 4: All patients with a potential bone or soft tissue sarcoma should be referred to a musculoskeletal oncologist before performing a biopsy to avoid diagnostic and treatment errors. Many studies have shown that larger, cancer referral centers have potentially better survival outcomes than small community hospitals. However, the treatment modalities vary considerably between centers, and the AJCC does not consider hospital subtypes as part of their staging systems.
