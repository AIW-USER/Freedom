## Question

The condition shown in Figure A is associated with all of the following EXCEPT:

![[q4927-a-fibular-hemimelia.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Absent lateral rays
2. Anterior cruciate ligament deficiency
3. Quadriceps hypotonia
4. Ball and socket ankle
5. Coxa vara

## Correct option

**3. Quadriceps hypotonia**

## Answer explanation

The radiograph shown in Figure A shows congenital fibular deficiency or fibular hemimelia. This condition is associated with absent lateral rays, anterior cruciate ligament deficiency, ball and socket ankle, and coxa vara. It is not associated with quadriceps hypotonia.

Fibular hemimelia is the most common congenital long bone deficiency and usually involves the entire limb. The classic appearance is a short limb and skin dimpling over the anteromedial tibial bowing. Fibular hemimelia is associated with ankle instability secondary to a ball and socket ankle, equinovalgus foot deformity, tarsal coalition (50%), absent lateral rays, femoral abnormalities (PFFD, coxa vara), anterior and posterior cruciate ligament deficiency, and lateral femoral condyle hypoplasia.

*Yoong et al. reviewed knee abnormalities in a series of patients with fibular hemimelia.* *Findings included genu valgum (6/6 patients), trochlear dysplasia (5/6), anterior cruciate ligament deficiency (4/6), and abnormal lateral meniscus (4/6).* **They concluded** that an unstable hip, knee, or ankle must be addressed prior to any lengthening procedure.

*Oberc et al. reviewed 31 patients with fibular hemimelia.* Twenty-four patients (78%) underwent 62 orthopedic procedures, which yields approximately three procedures per patient on average. Complications occurred in almost 78% of the patients treated with distraction-based leg lengthening methods. **They concluded** that Syme’s or Boyd’s amputation should be considered in patients with a nonfunctional (2–3-toed) foot or more than 50% expected shortening of the lower limb .

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1,2,4,5: These findings are associated with Fibular Hemimelia.
