## Question

A 12-year-old male presents to the ED following a motor vehicle accident. Radiographs obtained in the trauma bay reveal a femoral shaft fracture. He undergoes surgical stabilization utilizing a retrograde intramedullary nail which violated 5% of the physis, and all distal interlocking screws were placed proximal to the physis. How should the family be counseled?

## Options

1. Peripheral physeal injury is likely due to the method of fixation
2. Central physeal injury is likely due to the method of fixation
3. Complete physeal injury is likely due to the method of fixation
4. Physeal injury is likely due to the horizontal placement of the interlocking screws
5. Physeal injury is unlikely

## Correct option

**5. Physeal injury is unlikely**

## Answer explanation

This patient sustained a femoral shaft fracture which was treated with operative stabilization that violated <7% of the central physis. Growth arrest is unlikely due to the amount (%) of physis violation.

Physeal injuries may lead to complete or partial growth arrests. Complete growth arrest results in shortening, while partial arrest leads to angulation deformities. Treatment with bar resection with interposition is indicated when there is <50% physeal involvement and >2 years or 2cm of growth remaining. An ipsilateral completion of arrest is indicated with >50% physeal involvement
and may be combined with a contralateral epiphysiodesis and/or ipsilateral lengthening. While growth arrest may occur following violation to the physis, several studies have demonstrated that <7% of physeal violation is unlikely to result in physeal arrest. Placement of the distal interlocking screws may cause physeal arrest if not placed proximal to the distal femoral physis.

Mäkelä et al. reviewed 44 rabbits that underwent violation of the intercondylar portion of the femur. They reported that 7% of cross-sectional drilling of the physis, without any implant placed, resulted in growth arrest, whereas 3% did not. They concluded that destruction of 7% of the cross-sectional area of the growth plate caused permanent growth disturbance and shortening of the femur.

*Knapik et al. reviewed retrograde femoral nailing in skeletally immature patients.* **They report** that when compared with control specimens, only operative specimens with 8% of physeal violation demonstrated significant growth discrepancy. **They concluded** that the distal femoral growth continues across the physis when 3% to 7% of the cross-sectional area of the physis is violated using a retrograde intramedullary implant.

*Court-Brown et al. reviewed 36 adolescent patients who presented with tibial diaphyseal fractures and open tibial physes treated by reamed intramedullary nailing using a hollow, slotted nail.* **They reported** that no premature physeal closure secondary to nailing damage was noted. **They concluded** that intramedullary nailing of tibial diaphyseal fractures in adolescents with open physes, using a slotted nail, appears to be safe and does not cause late growth problems.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 2-3: Physeal violation <7% is unlikely to result in physeal injury
Answer 4: Horizontal placement of interlocking screws through a physis may lead to significant physeal violation and subsequent physeal arrest. However, as these screws were placed proximal to the physis, arrest is unlikely as violation of the physis has not occurred secondary to these screws
