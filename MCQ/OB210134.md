## Question

Which of the following should be avoided in young children who have sustained trauma?

## Options

1. Pediatric cervical collar
2. Pelvic sheet
3. Halo orthosis
4. Standard backboard
5. Computed tomography (CT) scan

## Correct option

**4. Standard backboard**

## Answer explanation

Children have large heads relative to their body size that will place their cervical spine in flexion if a standard backboard is used.

Children have heads that are large when compared to their torso. The circumference of their head grows logarithmically while the circumference of their chest grows linearly. If a standard adult backboard is used, the neck of a child will be placed in relative kyphosis and their cervical spine will be flexed. This is an undesirable position as the cervical spine should remain neutral. If a standard backboard is used, it must be modified with a torso pad to raise the chest or have the head cut out to minimize flexion to the cervical spine. Pediatric backboards are available as well.

*Herzenberg et al. performed a study on emergency transport and positioning of children who have an injury to the cervical spine.* *They reviewed 10 children who were less than 7 with an unstable injury to the cervical spine.* **They noted** that they had anterior angulation or translation on a lateral radiograph when they were supine on a standard backboard. **They report** that extension should have been the proper positioning of these injured patients. **They conclude** that standard adult backboards should not be used in young children.

Sundstrøm et al. performed a review of the prehospital use of cervical collars. They report that pediatric collars are adapted to the size and anatomy of children. They also note that cervical spine flexion on spine boards is undesirable and should be avoided. They conclude that pediatric cervical collars may be used in children with cervical spine trauma.

Illustration A demonstrates modifications to a standard adult backboard by cutting a portion out of the head of the board or by adding a torso pad to raise the chest. These modifications keep the cervical spine in a neutral position.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Pediatric cervical collars may be used in young children.
Answer 2: Pelvic sheets/binders may be used in young children with pelvic injuries.
Answer 3: A halo orthosis may be used in a child with an unstable cervical spine injury. Care must be undertaken as children have thinner calvaria.
Answer 5: CT scans may be used in pediatric patients, but should not be used as a spine screening examination.
