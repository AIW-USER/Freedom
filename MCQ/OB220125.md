## Question

He noted immediate left hip pain and the inability to ambulate afterward. A 3D CT scan of the pelvis is shown in Figure A. The patient is primarily a household ambulator who utilizes a cane at baseline. He denies any antecedent hip pain. His daughter presents with him, and hip arthroplasty is discussed. What is the biggest difference in outcomes between the two surgical techniques for the femoral stem shown in Figures B and C?

## Options

1. Figure B is associated with a shorter hospital length of stay
2. Figure C is associated with an increased rate of discharge home
3. Figure B is associated with a decreased risk of periprosthetic fracture
4. Figure B is associated with decreased operative time
5. Figure C is associated with a significant decrease in postoperative delirium

## Correct option

**3. Figure B is associated with a decreased risk of periprosthetic fracture**

## Answer explanation

This patient presented with a left displaced femoral neck fracture after a mechanical fall. In regard to cemented versus cementless femoral stems in hip arthroplasty, cemented stems are associated with a decreased risk of periprosthetic fracture (answer choice 3).

Femoral neck fractures are commonly encountered in the elderly population and are associated with an increased risk of avascular necrosis and high patient morbidity and mortality. Treatment for this injury depends upon age and displacement/morphology of the fracture. With displaced fractures, the most common treatment, especially in the elderly population, is hemiarthroplasty. Total hip arthroplasty is reserved for more active and relatively younger patients with similar injury patterns. For nondisplaced femoral neck fractures, open reduction and internal fixation can be utilized. Regarding hip arthroplasty components, cemented and cementless femoral stem options exist. Cemented hemiarthroplasty is associated with decreased intraoperative and postoperative periprosthetic fractures in elderly insufficiency fractures compared to cementless stems. Collared femoral components have also more recently shown a decreased risk of periprosthetic fractures when compared to collarless stems.

*Blankenstein et al. performed a contemporary review about the use of cement in hip hemiarthroplasty.* **They note** that cementless options predominate today due to procedural efficiency, concern for DVT risk, and a historic belief that cement is the cause of osteolysis and loosening. However, **they note** a rise in intraoperative, early, and late postoperative periprosthetic fractures following cementless options. **They conclude** that an understanding of cement techniques is crucial to help mitigate the risk of periprosthetic fracture.

*Veldman et al. conducted a systematic review and meta-analysis to compare the outcomes of cemented and cementless hemiarthroplasty for femoral neck fractures.* **They found** cementless stems were associated with more implant-related complications (p=0.002), while OR time was significantly shorter for cementless stems (p<0.001). **They concluded** that cemented stems result in fewer implant-related complications and similar mortality compared with cementless stems.

*Heckmann et al. conducted a database study comparing early complication and readmission rates for cemented versus cementless femoral stems in total hip arthroplasty for the treatment of femoral neck fractures.* *Of 17,491 patients, 25.3% received cemented femoral stems and 74.7% were cementless.* **They found** that cemented fixation was performed in significantly older patients with more comorbidities than those who received cementless stems. Further analysis demonstrated that cemented fixation resulted in lower rates of periprosthetic fracture (p=0.004) at 30 days but had similar readmission rates at 30, 90, and 180 days postoperatively. **They concluded** that cemented femoral stem fixation resulted in lower rates of short-term periprosthetic fractures, higher incidence of medical complications, and similar unplanned readmission rates within 180 days postoperatively compared with cementless femoral stem fixation.
