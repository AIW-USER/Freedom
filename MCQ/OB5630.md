## Question

A 21-year-old male sustained a T12 burst fracture after a motor vehicle accident. His neurological examination reveals an incomplete spinal cord injury. Magnetic resonance imaging shows the posterior ligament complex is intact. What would be the Thoracolumbar Injury Classification System (TLICS) score and recommended treatment?

## Options

1. TLICS = 4; bed rest immobilisation
2. TLICS = 5; external thoracolumbar orthosis
3. TLICS = 5; posterior decompression and arthrodesis
4. TLICS = 4; posterior decompression and arthrodesis
5. TLICS = 6; combined anterior and posterior decompression and arthrodesis

## Correct option

**3. TLICS = 5; posterior decompression and arthrodesis**

## Answer explanation

This patient's Thoracolumbar Injury Classification System (TLICS) score adds to 5, (morphology [burst fracture] ~ 1 (+ 1) = 2, spinal cord injury = 3, posterior ligament complex = 0). The most appropriate treatment would be surgical (e.g. posterior decompression and arthrodesis).

Thoracolumbar Injury Classification System (TLICS) categorizes injury by morphology (compression, burst, translation/rotation, distraction), posterior ligamentous injury (intact, indeterminate, disrupted) and neurological injury (intact, complete, incomplete, cauda equina). The TLICS scoring helps to guide treatment option, where scores of 5 or greater are recommended surgery.

*Patel and Vaccaro reviewed thoracolumbar spine trauma classification systems.* **They state** that anatomic structures of the PLC include the supraspinous ligament, interspinous ligament, ligamentum flavum, and facet joint capsules. Disruption of the PLC is typically indicated by widening of the interspinous space or of the facet joints, empty facet joints, facet perch or subluxation, and dislocation of the spine. MRI may reveal disruption of the ligamentous structures on T1-weighted imaging.

*Zdeblick et al. review the surgical treatment of thoracolumbar fractures.* The optimal surgical approach (anterior, posterior, or combined) is suggested by the anatomical zone of injury, neurological compromise, or PLC disruption (requiring posterior tension band constructs, i.e. posterior stabilization).

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1,2,4,5: The TLICS score = 5. Scores 5 or higher require surgical intervention. A variety of surgical options exist for T12 burst fractures. Posterior decompression and arthrodesis may be considered one choice.
