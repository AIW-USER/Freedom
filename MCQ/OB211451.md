## Question

Which of these patients with lesser toe deformities would most benefit from a percutaneous flexor tenotomy?

## Options

1. 49-year-old female with rigid flexion deformity of the DIPJ
2. 54-year-old male with flexible deformity of hyper extension of MTPJ with flexion of PIPJ and DIPJ who is otherwise healthy
3. 17-year-old with flexion deformity at PIPJ and extension at DIPJ
4. 62-year-old female with A1c of 8.5 and flexible flexion deformity at PIPJ and DIPJ with ulceration at tip of toe
5. 43-year-old male with rigid deformity of hyper extension of MTPJ with flexion of PIPJ and DIPJ

## Correct option

**4. 62-year-old female with A1c of 8.5 and flexible flexion deformity at PIPJ and DIPJ with ulceration at tip of toe**

## Answer explanation

In diabetic patients with claw toes and tip ulcerations, percutaneous flexor tenotomy is a good option to decrease tip pressure and help facilitate healing.

Lesser toe deformities include claw toes, hammertoes, and mallet toes. See illustration A for characteristics of each. Claw toes can cause increased pressure and subsequent ulcerations at the distal aspect of the toes. Ulcers are a causative factor in diabetic foot amputations. In diabetic patients, it is important to minimize surgical exposures due to increased risk of infection and poor wound healing potential. Therefore, it is appropriate in these patients to perform a percutaneous tenotomy instead of the typical tendon transfers and tendon lengthenings that you would do on an otherwise healthy patient.

*Scott et al. performed a systematic review covering percutaneous flexor tenotomies for diabetic toe ulcers.* **They found** an overall healing rate of 92-100%, with 0-18% reoccurrence. The more complex the ulcer, the longer it took for it to heal. A complication of this procedure is the development of transfer ulcers onto adjacent toes.

*Tamir et al. did a retrospective review of 103 tip-of-toes ulcers treated with FDL tenotomies.* Most of these ulcers were low-grade on the Texas ulcer scale. They had a 4-week healing rate of 98% with a 0% recurrence rate. The main complication was transfer lesions, which occurred in 8% of cases. **They concluded** that FDL tenotomies are an effective therapy for tip-of-toe ulcers in neuropathic patients.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: This patient has a rigid mallet toe which requires a bony procedure to correct, typically a DIPJ fusion or resection.
Answer 2: He has a flexible claw toe, but is otherwise healthy. A better treatment for this patient is a girdlestone procedure. This includes EDB tenotomy, EDL lengthening, and FDL flexor-to-extensor transfer.
Answer 3: This patient has a hammertoe. This is treated with FDL to EDL tendon transfer.
Answer 5: He has a rigid claw toe which requires tendon transfer as well as bony resection.
