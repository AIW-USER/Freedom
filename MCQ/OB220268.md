## Question

A 80-year-old female sustains a mechanical fall while sweeping her home. She presents with severe left shoulder pain, and her 3D reconstruction CT is presented in Figure A. The orthopaedic surgeon on call elects to perform reverse total shoulder arthroplasty. Which of the following choices will contribute to improved forward elevation and external rotation postoperatively?

![[q220268-a-proxhumfxdx.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Press-fit fixation of humeral component
2. Inlay type humeral component
3. Greater tuberosity fixation
4. Lesser tuberosity osteotomy (LTO)
5. Suprascapular nerve decompression

## Correct option

**3. Greater tuberosity fixation**

## Answer explanation

This elderly patient has sustained a proximal humerus fracture (PHF) dislocation and is indicated for reverse total shoulder arthroplasty (rTSA). Fixation of the greater tuberosity has been demonstrated to lead to improved outcomes concerning forward elevation and external rotation (Answer 3).

rTSA is becoming an increasingly common treatment option for proximal humerus fractures. Comminuted 4-part fractures in the elderly are almost all treated with arthroplasty options, while select 3-part fractures may also benefit from acute arthroplasty options. While not mandatory for overall success, fixation of the greater tuberosity improves clinical outcomes, specifically regarding external rotation and forward elevation strength. Patients without greater tuberosity fixation or those who go on to nonunion or malunion can still perform forward elevation and rotation due to the mechanical advantage that rTSA places on the deltoid complex.

*Anakwenze et al. performed a systematic review to evaluate the demographics and outcomes of rTSA for acute PHFs. rTSA is mostly performed in elderly females (mean age 77.5 years) for complex 4-part fractures.* Postoperatively, patients have moderate functional limitations but satisfactory pain relief. Repair of the GT is associated with significantly higher forward elevation (FE) and ER compared to patients without repair.

*Gallinet et al. analyzed 41 patients who underwent rTSA for PHF with and without GT repair.* Anatomic union of the GT occurred in 66% of GT repairs and was associated with significantly greater shoulder ROM in all planes and better functional outcome scores compared to both GT resection and GT nonunion or malunion. There was no difference in pain. **The authors concluded** that postoperative shoulder ROM and function depend on both anatomic reduction and subsequent healing of the GT.

*Jobin et al. reviewed the use of rTSA for PHF.* Unlike hemiarthroplasty, repair and healing of the tuberosities is not a prerequisite for an acceptable clinical outcome. However, anatomic repair and union of the GT demonstrate improved shoulder ER, function, and patient satisfaction compared to GT resection, nonunion, or resorption. GT repair techniques include the use of horizontal and vertical sutures or wires +/- bone autograft from the resected humeral head.

Figure A is the AP radiograph of a proximal humerus fracture dislocation with greater tuberosity involvement

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1, 2: Press fit or inlay fixation of the humeral component is not related to functional outcome measures.
Answer 4: The lesser tuberosity is already fractured, and thus an osteotomy or subscapularis peel is not required. Additionally, LTO is not related to forward elevation or external rotation outcomes.
Answer 5: Suprascapular nerve decompression is not indicated in routine rTSA for PHF cases.
