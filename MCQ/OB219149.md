## Question

An 18-year-old male presents to the ED after being involved in an MVC. Radiographs are obtained which reveal a Neer Type IIB distal clavicle fracture. Surgical intervention is selected because of concerns of nonunion with nonoperative management. Which of the following most accurately describes this injury?

## Options

1. Fracture occurring lateral to intact CC ligaments
2. Fracture occurring medial to intact CC ligaments
3. Fracture occurring between CC ligaments with the conoid ligament torn and the trapezoid ligament intact
4. Physeal fracture with torn CC ligaments
5. Comminuted fracture pattern with intact CC ligaments

## Correct option

**3. Fracture occurring between CC ligaments with the conoid ligament torn and the trapezoid ligament intact**

## Answer explanation

According to the Neer classification, a fracture occurring between CC ligaments with the conoid ligament torn and the trapezoid ligament intact describes a Type IIB fracture.

Distal clavicle fractures are traumatic injuries usually caused by direct trauma to the shoulder from a fall in adults. Diagnosis is confirmed with standard shoulder radiographs and a 15° cephalic tilt view (Zanca view). Treatment is immobilization or surgery, depending on the displacement and stability of the distal clavicle, as determined by whether coracoclavicular (CC) ligaments (trapezoid and conoid) are intact. Type IIB injuries have two fracture patterns (1) fracture occurs either between CC ligaments (conoid ligament torn and trapezoid ligament intact) OR (2) fracture occurs lateral to CC ligaments (conoid and trapezoid ligaments torn). With Type IIB injuries there is often significant medial clavicle displacement; these injuries are unstable (up to 45% nonunion rate with nonoperative management). Some have advocated that this second Type IIB fracture pattern be deemed "extra-lateral" and have proposed an update to the Neer classification to include it as Type IIC.

*Banerjee et al. reviewed distal clavicle fractures.* **They reported** that radiographic nonunion after distal clavicle fracture occurs in 10% to 44% of patients. **They concluded** that type II distal clavicle fractures, which involve displacement, are associated with the highest incidence of nonunion.

*Robinson et al. reviewed the results of employing a policy of primary nonoperative treatment of displaced lateral fractures of the clavicle.* **They reported** that nonoperative treatment of most displaced lateral fractures of the clavicle in middle-aged and elderly patients achieves a good medium-term functional result. **They concluded** that asymptomatic nonunion does not appear to adversely affect the functional outcome in the medium term.

*Levy et al. retrospectively reviewed the efficacy of operative management for the treatment of patients with an extra-lateral distal clavicle fracture pattern.* **They reported** that all patients had very good clinical outcomes following operative management of an extra-lateral distal clavicle fracture pattern. **They concluded** that this fracture pattern be added to the current modified Neer classification as a type IIC fracture.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Type I injuries are extra-articular fractures occurring lateral to CC ligaments. The conoid and/or trapezoid ligament remains intact. There is minimal displacement. These injuries are stable and often treated with nonoperative management.
Answer 2: Type IIA injuries occur medial to the CC ligaments with the conoid and trapezoid ligaments remaining intact. These have significant medial clavicle displacement and are unstable (up to 56% nonunion rate with nonoperative management)
Answer 4: Type IV injuries are physeal fracture that occurs in the skeletally immature with INTACT CC ligaments. The lateral clavicle may be displaced superiorly through a tear in the thick periosteum (clavicle pulls out of periosteal sleeve). These injuries are often stable and are treated nonoperatively.
Answer 5: Type V injuries include a comminuted fracture pattern with intact CC ligaments. These often have significant medial clavicle displacement and are usually unstable.
