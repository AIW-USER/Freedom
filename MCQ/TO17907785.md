## Question

A patient landed up with patellar dislocation that got spontaneously reduced. X ray (skyline view) was taken and is shown below.

Best treatment for the scenario would be

![[TO17907785-1.png]]

## Options

1. Arthroscopy of knee
2. Conservative with a cylindrical cast
3. Internal fixation
4. Patellectomy

## Correct option

**3. Internal fixation**

## Answer explanation

Ans: C

X ray is showing vertical fracture (intra articular) that should be managed by ORIF.

PATELLAR FRACTURES PATTERN AND TREATMENT

Fractures of the patella can be caused by two different mechanisms, which will determine the type of fracture. The most common is a direct impaction on the anterior side of a flexed knee. The shock can occur at low energy (fall) or high energy (motorcycle-type road accident, dashboard injury). Direct sustained high-energy trauma leads to comminuted fractures often associated with osteochondral lesions caused by the patella crushing into the femoral condyles. Less frequently, the secondary contraction of the quadriceps can cause secondary displacements, it is an indirect eccentric contraction caused by forced flexion of the knee against maximal contraction of the quadriceps, leading to a disruption of the extensor mechanism.

The choice of the treatment is based on the patient’s history, the clinical findings and the configuration and displacement of the fracture. The goal is to restore the articular surface and the extensor mechanism. Nonsurgical treatment is indicated in fractures that are undisplaced or with displacement of less than 4 mm and an articular step-off of less than 2 mm, and without interruption of the extensor mechanism (patient can do active SLR). The treatment involves positioning a brace with approximately 10° of flexion and with a neutral rotation, allowing a slight tension of the extensor apparatus in order to avoid the development of a patella Baja.

Surgical treatment: Indications for surgical treatment are transverse, vertical, stellar or comminuted fractures with a displacement greater than 4mm and/or with an articular step-off greater than 2 mm, as well as interruptions of the extensor apparatus. The goal of surgical treatment is to obtain an anatomical reduction of the joint surface and to provide stable fixation to allow for early range of motion and thus restore the knee‐extensor mechanism.

Other indication for open reduction and internal fixation (ORIF)

- extensor mechanism failure (unable to perform straight leg raise)
- open fractures
- displaced articular patella gap > 3 mm
- loose bodies
- osteochondral fractures
- patella sleeve fractures in children
