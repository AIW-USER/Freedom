## Question

The 3-year-old boy shown in Figure A presents with left hip pain and inability to ambulate for the last 48 hours after a recent flu-like illness. He is febrile to 39 degrees Celsius, and bloodwork reveals a serum WBC count of 8,000/mm3, 55% neutrophils, ESR 20 mm/h, and CRP 1.9 mg/dL. Which of the following statements is most accurate?

![[q2216-a-septic-left-hip-pic.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. CRP is the most predictive factor of the Kocher criteria
2. The patient has 1 out of the 4 Kocher criteria, making post-viral transient synovitis more likely
3. The patient has 2 out of the 4 Kocher criteria, with 29% predicted probability of septic arthritis
4. The patient has 3 out of 4 Kocher criteria, with >90% predicted probability of septic arthritis
5. A hip arthrocentesis would best confirm the diagnosis given the clinical and laboratory findings

## Correct option

**5. A hip arthrocentesis would best confirm the diagnosis given the clinical and laboratory findings**

## Answer explanation

The differential diagnosis for the patient in question includes septic hip arthritis and post-viral transient synovitis. Given he has 2 out of the 4 Kocher criteria, corresponding to a 40% predicted probability of septic arthritis, a hip arthrocentesis would best confirm the diagnosis.

The differentiation between septic arthritis and transient synovitis of the hip in children with fevers and an acutely irritable hip may be challenging. The early accurate diagnosis of septic hip arthritis is critical because poor outcomes are associated with an increasing delay in diagnosis. The Kocher criteria is a predictive clinical tool which may be used to differentiate between septic arthritis and transient synovitis of the hip based on four independent, multivariate predictors including: 1) inability to bear weight, 2) history of fever >38.5 degrees Celsius, 3) WBC > 12K/mm3, and 4) ESR > 40 mm/h. The predicted probability of septic arthritis for patients with 0, 1, 2, 3, or all 4 of these findings are 0.2%, 3%, 40%, 93.1% and 99.6%, respectively. CRP was not part of the original Kocher criteria.

*Kocher et al. published a validation study of a clinical prediction rule for the differentiation between septic arthritis and transient synovitis of the hip in children.* **The authors identified** the above 4 independent predictors of septic arthritis of the hip, with area under the receiver operating characteristic curve for the patient population being 0.86, which signifies good diagnostic performance.

*Sucato et al. present a review on septic arthritis of the hip in children.* **They reported** that juvenile idiopathic arthritis (JIA) can present similarly to a septic joint with a hip effusion with high leukocyte count and >75% PMNs. **The authors concluded** that children with septic arthritis can be distinguished by a more acute onset of symptoms, more pain, refusal to weight bear, and exhibit constitutional symptoms.

*Caird et al. prospectively evaluated the efficacy of the addition of CRP to the original Kocher.* **The authors reported** that CRP test was the only independent risk factor that met statistical significance in its association with septic arthritis. **They concluded** that patients with five, four, and three predictive factors had a 98%, 93%, and 83% chance of septic arthritis, respectively.

Incorrect Answers:
*Answer 1: CRP is not included in the original Kocher criteria, but was later added to the diagnostic algorithm by Caird et al.*
Answer 2: The patient has 2 out of the 4 Kocher criteria (refusal to bear weight, fever), conveying a 40% predicted probability of septic arthritis.
Answer 3: The patient has 2 out of the 4 Kocher criteria BUT this is associated with a 40% predicted probability of septic arthritis.
Answer 4: The patient has 2 out of the 4 Kocher criteria, though 3 would convey >90% risk of septic arthritis.
