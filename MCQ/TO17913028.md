## Question

A dancer has been having foot pain since a while. He is walking by putting weight on lateral margin as his great toe MTP joint plantar flexion is painful. The likely etiology is

## Options

1. Sesamoiditis of first MTP joint
2. 1st MTP joint arthritis
3. Stress fracture of first MT head
4. AVN of first MT head

## Correct option

**1. Sesamoiditis of first MTP joint**

## Answer explanation

Answer: 1: Sesamoiditis of first MTP joint

Features in question are highly suggestive of grade 3 sprain of first MTP joint turning into chronic stage with resultant sesamoiditis because of hyperextension injuries in dancers and players.

1st MTP SESAMOIDITIS

The sesamoid bones of the first metatarsal (Medial and Lateral) exhibit a special anatomic configuration, enveloped by the tendons of the flexor hallucis brevis (fhb) and lying within the capsule of the first metatarsophalangeal joint. Normally, the first MTP joint carries approximately 119% of body weight during gait with each step. The sesamoid complex that is located centrally and plantar to the first metatarsal head, embedded within the plantar plate, transmits 50% of body weight and more than 300% during push-off.

Chronic overuse (as in dancers) may lead to sesamoiditis generating clinical features such as: -

- Transfer of weight bearing to lateral surface
- Pain and restriction of MTPJ movements
- Painful push off of great toe (evident by painful plantar flexion in question above)

In certain instances, complete tear of plantar plate, capsule-ligamentous structure may also lead to sesamoid fracture/ separation of bipartite sesamoids and in rare cases a proximal migration of sesamoids.

The imaging examination should always begin with conventional radiography, including special axial and lateromedial views of the sesamoid bones. In cases in which results of routine radiography are inconclusive, MRI is indicated.

These pathologies have to be differentiated from other painful conditions of the hallux sesamoid bones like stress fractures, avascular necrosis, osteochondral fractures, and degenerative arthritis of the 1st MTP joint (Hallux Rigidus), that are generally secondary to these large weight-bearing loads or significant trauma.

Treatment options include conservative management with orthotics and immobilization, as well as operative interventions that involves various approaches for sesamoidectomy.
