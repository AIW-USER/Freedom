## Question

A 7-year-old female sustains a right knee injury while jumping on a trampoline. The parents are unsure of the exact mechanism, but state she noticed immediate pain, swelling, and inability to bear weight. Physical exam reveals tenderness in the lateral aspect of the knee, above the joint line. The remainder of the exam is limited due to pain. AP bilateral knee radiographs are depicted in figure A, lateral and oblique views are negative. Which study is optimal for confirming the diagnosis?

![[q216812-a-normal.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. CT of the right knee
2. Hip radiographs
3. MRI of the right knee
4. Weight bearing knee radiographs
5. Stress radiographs of the right knee

## Correct option

**3. MRI of the right knee**

## Answer explanation

The patient has a suspected nondisplaced Salter-Harris (SH) I fracture of the distal femur with normal radiographs. The most appropriate diagnostic step in management is an MRI of the knee to confirm the injury.

Distal femoral physeal fractures are the result of direct trauma to the knee with some element of rotation. SH II fractures are the most common forms and typically manifest with a metaphyseal Thurston-Holland fragment on radiographs. Nondisplaced fractures may not be appreciated on plain radiographs, especially if there is a SH I fracture. Historically, stress radiographs were performed to confirm the diagnosis, but given the added need for sedation/analgesics and the potential to cause further damage to the physis, MRI has supplanted this method. Alternatively, possible SH I fractures can be immobilized in a cast with early follow up for signs of healing. Confirmed nondisplaced fractures can be treated with a 4-6 week period of long-leg casting.

*Zionts et al.* *2002 reviewed pediatric fractures around the knee.* **They reported** a SH type II is the most common type. **They recommended** treating nondisplaced type II fractures with immobilization in long leg or hip spica casts for 4-6 weeks and displaced fractures with reduction and smooth transphyseal pins.

*Arkader et al. performed a retrospective study of distal femoral epiphyseal fractures and potential predictive factors on growth and functional outcomes.* **They reported** an overall 40% complication rate which was associated with higher SH grades, fracture displacement, and surgical treatment, especially if hardware violated the physis. **They concluded** SH classification and displacement of the fracture are the most significant predictors of complications in distal femur physeal fractures.

Figure A is an AP radiograph of bilateral knees in a skeletally immature patient without significant radiographic abnormality.

Incorrect answers
Answer 1: A CT of the knee would expose the patient to unnecessary radiation and may not detect a nondisplaced fracture of the distal femoral physis.
Answer 2: The patient does not have the history or physical exam findings to suggest hip pathology.
Answer 4: In the presence of a distal femoral physis fracture, performing weight-bearing radiographs are likely to be painful and may damage the growth plate.
Answer 5: Stress radiographs may reveal excess mobility at the physics in Salter-Harris I fractures, but this exposes the patient to increased pain and risk for further physeal damage. Many clinicians have abandoned this technique out of these concerns.
