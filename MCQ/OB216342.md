## Question

A 19-year-old collegiate soccer player presents for pre-participation screening physical. She had an ACL reconstruction 16 months ago which forced her to miss her senior season. She denies any complications following surgery and completed 6 months of guided physical therapy before participating in soccer drills. Exam shows full range of motion with a grade Ia Lachman and no pivot shift. Incisions are well healed, there is no quad muscle atrophy with symmetric strength. She is concerned about future ACL injuries. Which of the following most significantly lowers her risk of ACL injury?

## Options

1. ACL-stabilizing brace
2. Participation in neuromuscular training program
3. Gender
4. Age
5. Sport selection

## Correct option

**2. Participation in neuromuscular training program**

## Answer explanation

Neuromuscular training programs have been shown reduce the incidence and likelihood of ACL injuries.

ACL injuries are common among sporting activities requiring rapid acceleration/deceleration combined with pivoting and/or jumping. These injuries were previously career-threatening but refinement in technique and rehabilitation have improved outcomes tremendously. Various neuromuscular training programs have been developed to prevent ACL injury or re-injury. All of these programs center on the core stability and synchrony of the abductors and hamstrings to avoid dynamic vaglus-moments at the knee during activity. Multiple studies have shown dramatic improvement in injury rates at nearly every level of sport, particularly soccer (i.e. FIFA 11+).

*Voskanian et al. reviewed risk factors for ACL injury in female athletes and proposed key elements for a successful ACL prevention program.* **The authors recommend** initiation of the prevention program prior to puberty, incorporation of neuromuscular training and muscle strengthening, as well as education and feedback regarding body mechanics and proper landing patterns. **They emphasized** the program should be easy to follow and require minimal equipment and time commitment; to enhance compliance.

*Zebis et al. evaluated the effects of implementing a prophylactic neuromuscular training program in female athletes.* Neuromuscular activity of the lower extremity was analyzed with electromyography (EMG) during performance of a side-cutting maneuver in 12 soccer players and 8 handball players, before and after implementation of a neuromuscular training program. Neuromuscular training increased the pre-landing and landing EMG activity for the medial hamstring muscles, while the quadriceps EMG activity remained unchanged. **They postulated** that an increased ratio between HS and quadriceps activity may decrease the risk of dynamic knee valgus and subsequent ACL tear.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1- ACL stabilizing braces have not shown to protect the ACL from injury. They may have a more important role in the ACL-deficient knee.
Answers 3, 4, and 5- All of these elevate her risk of and ACL injury. Young female soccer players the most at-risk cohort for ACL injury.
