## Question

A 24-year-old male presents to the emergency department for management of right elbow pain after falling onto an outstretched hand while playing football. He is subsequently found to have an elbow dislocation and is promptly reduced under conscious sedation. Post-reduction CT imaging is obtained, with 3D reconstruction imaging shown in Figures A & B. Which physical exam finding would most likely persist if the injury were to be treated nonoperatively and end up with nonunion?

## Options

1. Varus laxity
2. Valgus laxity
3. Positive milking maneuver
4. Mechanical block with pronosupination
5. Ring and small finger paresthesias

## Correct option

**1. Varus laxity**

## Answer explanation

This 24-year-old sustained an elbow dislocation following a varus-posteromedial moment onto the arm, which subsequently resulted in posteromedial rotatory instability. Due to the anteromedial coronoid facet fracture and lateral collateral ligament (LCL) complex disruption seen with this pattern, varus laxity would most likely be observed (Answer 1).

Coronoid fractures in isolation are fairly uncommon and typically occur with concomitant injuries in the setting of elbow dislocations. The type of coronoid fracture is often dependent on the mechanism of elbow dislocation. With posterolateral rotatory instability, coronoid pathology typically manifests as coronoid tip fractures in conjunction with radial head fractures and LCL injuries. In contrast, posteromedial rotatory instability often results in anteromedial coronoid facet fractures and LCL disruptions secondary to the varus moment associated with the mechanism. While both patterns require surgery, the latter pattern necessitates surgery as the primary restraints (anteromedial coronoid facet, LCL complex) to varus stress are lost, with the medial ulnar collateral ligament (mUCL) commonly remaining intact. Given the large coronoid fragment, open reduction with internal fixation of the fragment in addition to LCL repair would be required.

*Park et al. performed a retrospective review examining injury patterns and clinical outcomes of 11 patients sustaining isolated anteromedial coronoid facet fractures treated with open reduction, internal fixation and collateral ligament repair as indicated.* **The authors noted** all patients sustained LCL disruptions, while 54% exhibited mUCL disruptions at the time of injury. At an average follow-up of 31 months, patients exhibited a mean range of 128 degrees, and the majority (91%) achieved 'excellent' or 'good' outcomes based on Mayo Elbow Performance Scores. **The authors conclude** this injury is often associated with collateral ligament injury but can achieve good outcomes with appropriate treatment.

*Sanchez-Sotelo et al. reported on two patients who sustained anteromedial coronoid facet fractures following posteromedial rotatory instability.* One was treated with open reduction and internal fixation, while the other was treated non-operatively. The patient who received fixation went on to experience no residual instability and a near-full range of motion. **The authors concluded** that this fracture pattern requires more attention, as sequelae following a missed fracture can lead to significant disability.

Figures A and B represent 3D reconstruction imaging of a right elbow demonstrating a type II Bryan-morrey coronoid fracture with extension into the anteromedial facet.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 2 and 3: after healing of the anteromedial coronoid facet, there would likely be no mUCL-related pathology, given the ligament often remains intact.
Answer 4: there is no radial head pathology present in this injury and therefore, no block would be suspected
Answer 5: ulnar neuropathy has not been associated with anteromedial coronoid facet fractures but is more commonly observed transiently following surgical fixation
