![[q215276-b-pvns-histo.jpg]]

## Options

1. CSF-1 receptor agonist
2. CSF-1 receptor antagonist
3. Ubiquitin-specific protease 6 (USP6) agonist
4. Ubiquitin-specific protease 6 (USP6) antagonist
5. Mouse double minute-2 homolog (MDM2) agonist

## Correct option

**2. CSF-1 receptor antagonist**

## Answer explanation

This patient's history, MRI, and histology suggests a diagnosis of tenosynovial giant cell tumor (TGCT) also known as pigmented villonodular synovitis (PVNS) and giant cell tumor of the tendon sheath. Pexidartinib is a CSF-1 receptor antagonist and the first and only FDA-approved oral medication for TGCT.

PVNS is an idiopathic monoarticular neoplastic synovial disease. This condition is most commonly managed with surgical synovectomy, but it is associated with a high risk of local recurrence. Pexidartinib, a CSF-1 receptor antagonist, is indicated for the treatment of adult patients with symptomatic TGCT associated with severe disease morbidity, functional limitations and for whom surgery is not an option. This medication has been associated with serious and potentially fatal liver injury.

*Abdul-Karim et al. reviewed the DNA content and proliferative indices of TGCT subtypes.* **The authors found** that the proliferative indexes for TGCT diffuse type were significantly higher than those found in other subtypes (ie. PVNS). **They concluded** that TGCT and its subtypes are histopathologically similar but clinically distinct lesions.

*Gelhorn et al. reviewed TGCT treated pharmacologically.* **They noted** the influence of CSF1R-bearing macrophages recruited to the tumor by genetic elevation of CSF1 activity leading to joint destruction. **They reported** trends of improvement in both pain and stiffness over time and identified content-valid patient-reported outcome measures following pharmacologic treatment.

Figure A is the MRI image of a knee with TGCT. Figure B is the histology of TGCT which demonstrates hemosiderin stained multinucleated giant cells.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Pexidartinib is a CSF-1 receptor antagonist, not agonist.
Answers 3 and 4: USP6 fusion genes have been linked to aneurysmal bone cysts and more recently to nodular fasciitis.
Answer 5: MDM2 agonist has been associated with the development of low-grade and parosteal osteosarcoma as well as atypical lipomatous tumors.
