## Question

A new patient is coming to your clinic with a known diagnosis of a prosthetic joint infection (PJI) in a total knee arthroplasty (TKA). You are deciding on options for infection eradication. After further history, a knee aspiration and radiographs are obtained. Which factor would predict failure from undergoing a debridement and implant retention (DAIR) procedure?

## Options

1. Age under 65 years old
2. Ciprofloxacin susceptible E. coli infection
3. Symptom onset less than 1 week
4. Two cultures positive for Mycobacterium species
5. Well-fixed implants on radiographs

## Correct option

**4. Two cultures positive for Mycobacterium species**

## Answer explanation

In the setting of PJI, identification of the responsible microorganism can help predict success with DAIR procedures (along with many other factors). The presence of mycobacterium species predicts failure of DAIR procedures due to the indolent presentation, formation of biofilm, and resistance to antimicrobials (Answer 4).

Debridement and Implant Retention (DAIR) procedures can be used to treat prosthetic joint infections (PJIs) in specific conditions. Greater success of PJI eradication with DAIR procedure is seen in acute PJI (early postoperative or acute hematogenous), less virulent organisms, stable implants, adequate soft tissue, and amenable host factors. Specifically, age over 80 years old, male sex, COPD, chronic renal failure, and liver cirrhosis were host-specific factors predictive of DAIR failure. Furthermore, many studies have reported predictors of successful DAIR procedures amongst different causative microorganisms. Microorganisms with high antibiotic resistance (MRSA, VRE, Pseudomonas), biofilm formation (S. epidermidis), fungal species, and mycobacterium species have a higher likelihood of failure with DAIR procedures. Mycobacterium PJIs pose a rare but specific challenge for DAIR success due to their indolent nature with biofilm formation and antibacterial resistance. Identifying mycobacterium as the causative agent in PJIs is a contraindication to performing a DAIR procedure.

*Chotanaphuti et al. published an international consensus on prosthetic joint infections.* Early postoperative infections and acute hematogenous infections can be treated similarly, assuming the onset of symptoms within 4 weeks. The success of DAIR decreases if symptoms exist longer than 5 days and the presence of a sinus tract is a contraindication to undergoing DAIR in this study. **The authors conclude** that DAIR should be reserved for cases with a recent onset of symptoms (preferably < 7 days), well-fixed implants, absence of sinus tract, and an isolated organism that is sensitive to antimicrobial agents.

*Malhotra et al. published a case report on a patient with Mycobacterium abscessus PJI to the bilateral knees.* The patient eventually was definitively treated with arthrodesis of one knee and a permanent antibiotic spacer in the other knee. **The authors report** the difficulty in eradicating mycobacterial PJIs due to the formation of biofilms and indolent presentation in this bacterial species. **The authors recommend** full component removal with a prolonged antimicrobial regimen in the setting of mycobacterium PJIs.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer choice 1: Patient age under 65 would not be the greatest predictor of DAIR failure. While not the sole predictor of success with DAIR, studies have shown an association with advanced age (>80) and failure of DAIR procedures. This is likely due to the concomitant medical comorbidities, delayed presentation, and longer indwelling prosthesis leading to biofilm formation.
Answer choice 2: Studies have shown up to a 79% success rate at eradicating infection in the setting of fluoroquinolone-susceptible strains of E. coli (assuming other criteria for DAIR are met).
Answer choice 3: Symptoms persisting less than 1 week have been shown to increase the success rate of DAIR procedures. This information alone would not predict the failure of DAIR procedure.
Answer choice 5: The presence of well-fixed implants is not a predictor of DAIR failure. While having stable implants will not predict the success of DAIR procedures, the presence of unstable implants is a contraindication to DAIR procedures.
