## Answer explanation

Figure E demonstrates an AP radiograph of an ankle with a ball and socket ankle deformity. This deformity is often associated with fibular hemimelia.

Fibular hemimelia can present with a spectrum of deformities about the lower extremity. Abnormalities can include absent lateral rays of the foot, ball-and-socket ankle joint, cruciate deficient knee, tarsal coalitions, and a partially or completely absent fibula with anteromedial tibial bowing. Treatment typically depends on the functionality of the foot, and degree of limb length inequality.

*Oberc et al. present a review of 31 patients with fibular hemimelia.* **They recommend** limb elongation for patients with type Ia or Ib hemimelia, a functional foot with more than 3 rays, and shortening less than 5cm at birth. For patients requiring an amputation **they found** Syme's amputation to provide the best functional outcome.

*Birch et al. present a review of 104 patients with fibular hemimelia.* **They proposed** a classification system that could be used shortly after birth to help predict future deformity and level of function. It is based on presence of a functional foot (type I with, type II without). Type I is sub-classified based on degree of limb length inequality. Type II is sub-classified based on function of the upper extremities.

Figure A is a radiograph of a left hip slipped capital femoral epiphysis. Figure B is a radiograph of a patient with tibial hemimelia. Figure C is a radiograph of anterolateral tibial bowing consistent with tibial pseudoarthrosis. Figure D is an AP and lateral radiograph of posteromedial bowing of the tibia. Figure E is an AP radiograph of an ankle with a ball-and-socket ankle deformity.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: Figure A shows slipped capital femoral epiphysis which is not typically seen concomitant with fibular hemimelia.
Answer 2: Figure B shows a patient with a significant limb length discrepancy consistent with tibial hemimelia.
Answer 3: Figure C demonstrates pseudoarthrosis of the tibia which is often associated with conditions such as neurofibromatosis.
Answer 4: Figure D shows posteromedial bowing of the tibia. Thi is considered physiologic bowing, often associated with a calcaneovalgus foot.
