## Question

A 50-year-old male presents to the ED after falling off a roof while hanging Christmas lights on his house. Imaging is obtained and reveals a comminuted distal humerus fracture. On examination, he is neurovasculary intact. He undergoes open reduction and internal fixation of the distal humerus. Which of the following statements is true regarding the fixation depicted in Figures A compared with Figure B?

## Options

1. Figure A has a higher incidence of ulnar neuritis
2. Figure B has a higher incidence of ulnar neuritis
3. Figure A has a higher incidence anterior interosseous nerve injury
4. Figure B has a higher incidence anterior interosseous nerve injury
5. Figure A and B have an equal incidence of ulnar or anterior interosseous nerve injury

## Correct option

**3. Figure A has a higher incidence anterior interosseous nerve injury**

## Answer explanation

A comminuted distal humerus fracture treated with an olecranon osteotomy with tension band fixation places the AIN at increased risk of injury.

Treatment of distal humerus fractures may be achieved through several approaches including a triceps-splitting (Campbell), triceps-sparing (paratricipital, Alonso-Llames, medial and lateral windows), olecranon osteotomy, triceps-reflecting (Bryan-Morrey), triceps-reflecting anconeous pedicle (O'Driscoll), and lateral intermuscular interval. Use of an olecranon osteotomy with tension band fixation has been shown to have a higher incidence of AIN injury postoperatively due to the proximity of the AIN to the hardware used for olecranon fixation. Use of an olecranon osteotomy with tension band fixation has not been associated with an increased incidence of ulnar neuritis. However, patients undergoing ulnar nerve transposition have been shown to have a higher incidence of ulnar neuritis compared with those in which the ulnar nerve was not transposed (33% versus 9%, respectively).

*Parker et al. reported a case report in a patient who experienced an AIN deficit postoperatively after tension banding of an olecranon fracture.* Intraoperatively, there were multiple passes of the K-wires in an attempt to find purchase in the anterior cortex of the ulna. **The authors believed** that during these passes, the nerve was injured and concluded that placing K-wires should not occur without radiologic visualization.

*Vazquez et al. retrospectively reviewed the incidence of ulnar nerve dysfunction after open reduction and internal fixation of distal humerus fractures and identify factors associated with its development.* **They reported** that using an olecranon osteotomy for the exposure of the joint surface was not associated with an increased incidence of ulnar neuritis. Moreover, the presence of an open fracture also was not associated with postoperative ulnar neuritis. **They concluded** that ulnar nerve transposition was not protective of postoperative ulnar neuritis.

Figure A is the radiograph of a distal humerus fracture treated with ORIF and an olecranon osteotomy with tension band fixation. Figure B is the radiograph of a distal humerus fracture treated with ORIF without an olecranon osteotomy. Illustration A is the lateral radiograph demonstrating prominent anterior pin tips putting the AIN at risk of injury.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1&2: Use of an olecranon osteotomy for exposure of the joint surface in a distal humerus fracture is not associated with an increased incidence of ulnar neuritis
Answers 4&5: Use of an olecranon osteotomy with tension band fixation has a higher incidence of AIN injury
