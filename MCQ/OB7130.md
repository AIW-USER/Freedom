## Question

A 12-year-old patient with spastic hemiplegia cerebral palsy with equinovarus foot deformity is undergoing serial casting and injection of intramuscular botulinum toxin type A. Botulinum toxin type A induces muscle weakness by which of the following actions?

## Options

1. Prevention of presynaptic release of acetylcholine
2. Binding of the nicotinic acetylcholine receptor
3. Destruction of the acetylcholine receptor at the neuromuscular junction
4. Alteration of the muscle sarcolemma stability
5. Stimulation of acetylcholinesterase activity

## Correct option

**1. Prevention of presynaptic release of acetylcholine**

## Answer explanation

Botulinum toxin type A blocks neuromuscular transmission by binding to receptor sites on motor nerve terminals, entering the nerve terminals, and inhibiting the presynaptic release of acetylcholine. Prevention of the release of acetylcholine causes flaccid paralysis of the muscles via chemical denervation of the muscle and localized reduction in muscle activity. The toxin is injected into the spastic muscle belly. With time, axonal sprouting occurs with reinnervation of the muscle, thus slowly reversing the muscle denervation and toxin effects. Binding of the nicotinic receptor occurs with a nondepolarizing toxin such as curare or with depolarizing agents such as succinylcholine. Destruction of the acetylcholine receptor is representative of myasthenia gravis, an autoimmune disorder. Alteration of muscle sarcolemma stability occurs with conditions of muscular dystrophy.
