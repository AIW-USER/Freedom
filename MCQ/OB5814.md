## Question

Which of the following scenarios would femoral head resection rather than reconstruction be the best treatment for patients with cerebral palsy with symptomatic hip subluxation?

## Options

1. 8-year-old female; GMFCS [GMFCS = Gross Motor Function Classification System]IV; Reimers migration percentage 50% with femoral head irregularity
2. 15-year-old male; GMFCS V; Reimers migration percentage 25% with femoral head irregularity
3. 11-year-old male; GMFCS III; Reimers migration percentage 50% with femoral head irregularity
4. 18-year old female; GMFCS V; Reimers migration percentage 100%
5. 17-year-old male; GMFCS V; Reimers migration percentage 100% and femoral head deformity

## Correct option

**5. 17-year-old male; GMFCS V; Reimers migration percentage 100% and femoral head deformity**

## Answer explanation

Subtrochanteric valgus osteotomy and femoral head resection is an option for a symptomatic complete hip dislocation in a 17 year old male with level V cerebral palsy and femoral head deformity.

Hip displacement in CP is classically measured by Reimers’ migration percentage (MP). This describes the percentage of the femoral head positioned lateral to the acetabular margin on an anteroposterior pelvic radiograph (Illustration A). The Gross Motor Function Classification System Level (GMFCS) is used to assess motor function on a five-level ordinal scale. Level I corresponds to the highest/near-normal level of function, where as Level V is the lowest level of function/non-ambulatory patient.

*Graham et al discuss salvage hip surgery for patients with cerebral palsy.* **They note** that femoral head resection is a salvage procedure in an older patient with a hip that cannot be reconstructed.

*Larnert et al reviewed hip displacement in relation to age and gross motor function in children with cerebral palsy.* **They report** that children with GMFCS V had a significantly higher risk of hip displacement, with displacement occurring at an earlier age than children with GMFCS III or IV. At 7–8 years of age, 66 % of children at GMFCS V had developed Reimers’ migration percentage >33%.

Illustration A shows how to calculate the Reimers' migration percentage. The amount of ossified femoral head positioned lateral to the acetabular margin (Labeled A) is divided by the total width of ossified femoral head (Labeled B) on an anteroposterior pelvic radiograph, and multiplied by 100%. Illustration B shows an illustration of the McHale technique (femoral head resection and valgus-producing subtrochanteric osteotomy) for chronically dislocated hips in cerebral palsy. Illustration C shows the GMFCS levels.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1 : This patient should be managed initially with a reconstructive procedure, such as a varus-directing femoral derotational osteotomy +/- pelvic osteotomy.
Answer 2: This older, non-functional, patient could be treated symptomatically with analgesia and antispasmodics. A migration index of 25% is less likely to be symptomatic.
Answer 3: This patient should be managed initially with a reconstructive procedure, such as a varus-directing femoral derotational osteotomy +/- pelvic osteotomy.
Answer 4: Resection is not the ideal treatment if the femoral head is normal without cartilage degeneration. Femur +/- pelvic osteotomy may still be an option and does not carry the risk of continued proximal migration that is associated with resection.
