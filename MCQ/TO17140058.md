Chapter: Regional Orthopedics MCQs — Anatomy ( Shoulder)

## Question

Structure responsible for anteroposterior stability of AC joint is

## Options

1. Anteronferior part of the capsule
2. Posterosuperior part of the capsule
3. Trapezoid ligament
4. Conoid ligament

## Correct option

**2. Posterosuperior part of the capsule**

## Answer explanation

Anatomy of Coracoid –Acromion- clavicular complex:

The coracoclavicular (CC) ligament complex: It consists of the conoid and trapezoid ligaments. The former is the posteromedial portion, and the latter the anterolateral portion of the CC ligament complex.

The clavicular insertion of the conoid ligament is approximately twice as wide (medial to lateral) and thick (anterior to posterior) as its coracoid insertion, giving rise to its inverted cone shape. The trapezoid ligament is more than three times thicker at its clavicular end than at its coracoid end. The coracoid origin of the trapezoid cover the posterior half of the coracoid dorsum; the conoid origin is more posterior on the base of the coracoid, limited anteriorly by the trapezoid insertion.

The AC joint: It is a diarthrodial joint: it is surrounded by a capsule and has an intra-articular synovium and an articular cartilage interface. The hyaline articular cartilage becomes fibrocartilage on the acromial side of the joint by the age of 17 and on the clavicular side by the age of 24.

An articular disc is usually present in the joint, but this varies in size and shape. In anatomical studies, 2 types of discs have been observed: a complete disk (very rare) and a meniscoid-like disk.

Biomechanics:

The AC capsule and ligaments stabilize the AC joint, principally to restrain horizontal translation, affording three times more control in the anteroposterior (AP) direction than in the vertical direction. These structures also constrain posterior rotation of the clavicle and contribute to vertical stability at small degrees of displacement. The AC ligaments are capsular thickenings that are traditionally described as four structures: the superior, anterior, inferior, and posterior ligaments. These ligaments vary in thickness from 1 to 5 mm, with the inner margin of their osseous insertions located 3–4 mm from the joint line while the peripheral fibers insert farther distally. The superior ligament is the most robust and is the principal ligament maintaining horizontal stability. Few studies have recommended that the AC ligaments be considered as just two structures, a substantial superoposterior bundle responsible for resisting posterior clavicular translation and a thin anteroinferior bundle that assists in limiting anterior displacement

Answer : b
