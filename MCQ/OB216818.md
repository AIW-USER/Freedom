## Question

A 17-year-old male with an articular injury to his knee is being evaluated for reconstructive surgery approximately 4 weeks after his initial injury. During arthroscopy, 4 weeks later, you identify the lesion on the femoral trochlea and extending to the lateral condyle. The medial and lateral compartments appear healthy without evidence of articular damage. The footprint of the lesion measures 3cm x 2cm. When evaluating the depth of the lesion you appreciate irregular bony footprint measuring to a depth of 8mm. Ultimately, you indicate the patient for osteochondral allograft transfer. Which of the following factors indicates for the use of an osteochondral allograft transfer rather than autologous chondrocyte implantation?

## Options

1. Footprint >6cm2
2. Depth of osseous involvement
3. Location
4. Patient age
5. Time since the initial injury

## Correct option

**2. Depth of osseous involvement**

## Answer explanation

Osteochondral allograft transfer with the mosaicplasty technique (transfer of multiple plugs) would be well-suited for this large defect with significant osseous involvement.

Articular cartilage defects of the knee comprise of a spectrum of disease entities from single, focal defects to advanced degenerative disease of articular (hyaline) cartilage. Treatment is largely based on the size and depth of the lesion. Microfracture is best for lesions that are <2 cm2 without underlying osseous defects. Autologous chondrocyte implantation, can be used for lesions between 1-10 cm2, but is restricted to those without significant osseous defects (<8mm deep). Osteochondral allograft transfer is best for patients with large lesions that also have a significant amount of bone loss as is described in this patient.

*Murray et al review the management of knee articular cartilage injuries in athletes.* **They report** that the indications for osteochondral allograft transfer are full-thickness chondral defect >2 cm2 with or without osseous defect or AVN. **They conclude** that osteochondral allograft transfer procedures overcome many of the challenges of matching chondral topography and donor-site morbidity that limit autologous techniques.

*Yanke et al review the management of patellofemoral chondral injuries.* **They report** that treatment of patellofemoral chondral defects is fraught with difficulty because of the generally inferior outcomes and significant biomechanical complexity of the joint. **They conclude** that patients with large bone defects requiring osteochondral allografts generally have poorer outcomes as reported in up to 53% of patients in this study.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: While large lesions can be treated with allograft implantation, they can also be treated with autologous chondrocyte implantation (1-10cm2). Size alone is not the reason this patient was indicated for allograft implantation.
Answer 3-5: Lesion location, patient age, and time from injury are not primary reasons to indicate a patient for osteochondral allograft transfer.
