## Question

A 56-year-old male laborer presents with the deformity shown in Figure A and B. He complains of long standing pain and swelling over the medial aspect of his right forefoot only. There is minimal tenderness with full flexion and extension of the first metatarsophalangeal joint and no tarsometatarsal joint laxity bilaterally. What treatment is most appropriate for this patient?

## Options

1. Bilateral first metatarsalphalangeal arthrodesis
2. Bilateral first metatarsal osteotomy with distal bunionectomy
3. Distal biplanar Chevron osteotomy of right foot only
4. Proximal first metatarsal osteotomy combined with Akin osteotomy of right foot only
5. Bilateral Lapidus procedure combined with bilateral biplanar chevron osteotomy

## Correct option

**4. Proximal first metatarsal osteotomy combined with Akin osteotomy of right foot only**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a symptomatic bunion on the right foot and an asymptomatic bunion on the left foot. He has failed conservative treatment. Due to his radiographic measurements on the right (IMA = 16, HVA = 35 and hallux valgus interphalangeus angle = 13), the most appropriate treatment would be surgical correction with a proximal first metatarsal osteotomy combined with Akin osteotomy of the right foot only.

Surgical treatment is indicated in persistently symptomatic patients with an IMA > 10° and HVA of > 20°. Proximal or combined osteotomy are indicated in more moderate disease (IMA > 13). The proximal first metatarsal osteotomy addresses the increased IMA while the Akin procedure addresses the hallux valgus interphalangeus deformity. Patients with arthritic joints or underlying joint diseases (e.g. RA) should be considered for fusion vs corrective surgery. Cosmetic treatment of asymptomatic bunions are not considered an indication for surgery.

*Coughlin et al. reviewed the demographic of hallux valgus deformities.* **They found** that familial history of bunions, bilateral involvement, female gender, a long first metatarsal, and an oval or curved metatarsophalangeal joint articular surface were common findings with hallux valgus deformities.

*Johnson et al. reviewed the management of hallux valgus deformities in adolescents.* **They showed** that first metatarsal double osteotomy is an effective and reliable technique for treatment of severe adolescent hallux valgus as compared to conventional measures.

Figure A shows an AP radiograph of the left and right foot while laying. Figure B shows the same patient with standing radiographs, with overlying markings. There is a severe hallux valgus deformity bilaterally with little radiographic evidence of first MTP arthritis and a non-congruent metatarsophalangeal joint. The IMA angles are increased bilaterally (normal <9 deg). There is hallux valgus interphalangeus in the right foot (normal < 10deg).

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Indications for first metatarsalphalangeal arthrodesis include degenerative joint disease, forefoot inflammatory disease, subluxated or dislocated MTP joints, or failed hallux valgus surgery.
Answer 2: The IMA angle >15 requires proximal MT osteotomy. However, this patient is asymptomatic in the left foot and does not require treatment. The hallux valgus interphalangeus angle in the right foot requires attention for correction.
Answer 3: Biplanar chevron osteotomy are performed on patients with mild-to-moderate hallux valgus deformities with increased distal metatarsal articular angle (DMAA). This patients deformity requires management of the IMA angle.
Answer 5:The hallux valgus interphalangeus angle in the right foot requires attention for correction. This will not be corrected with a biplanar chevron osteotomy. This patient is asymptomatic in the left foot and does not require treatment on this side. A Lapidus procedure can be used for increased IM angles, however there is debate whether it should be used in patients that do not have TMT hypermobility, arthritis, etc.
