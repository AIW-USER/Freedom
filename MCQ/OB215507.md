## Question

A 35-year-old male presents to the emergency department with right wrist and elbow pain after a fall. X-rays of the right elbow are demonstrated in figure A. Right wrist exam demonstrates a positive Ballotment test. Patient is to undergo radial head replacement and percutaneous stabilization of his wrist injury. Which of the following is the most likely diagnosis?

![[q215507-a-screen-shot-2021-04-13-at-12-09-04-pm.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Galeazzi Fracture
2. Monteggia Fracture
3. Essex-Lopressti Injury
4. Terrible triad of the elbow
5. Floating elbow

## Correct option

**3. Essex-Lopressti Injury**

## Answer explanation

This patient presents with a comminuted radial head fracture and a DRUJ injury as indicated by a positive Ballotment test. This injury pattern is consistent with an Essex-Lopresti injury (ELI).

An ELI occurs when a patient falls onto an outstretched hand and an axial load is transmitted from the wrist to elbow, causing injury to the DRUJ, interosseous membrane, and radial head fracture. This results in DRUJ instability and longitudinal instability of the radius which is restored by fixation of the radial head. Management of DRUJ injuries in these patients is dictated by timing of the injury. Acute DRUJ injuries are treated with pinning, chronic injuries with reconstruction, and salvage procedures (i.e. Darrach procedure) are reserved for severe arthrosis.

*Schnetzke et al. reviewed a series of 31 patients who sustained an ELI and evaluated the impact of timing of diagnosis and treatment on patient outcomes.* **They found** the early diagnosis group (<4 weeks) had significantly better clinical outcomes and a lower percentage of complications. **They conclude** that early diagnosis of ELI and stabilization of the DRUJ leads to satisfactory outcomes compared to those with late diagnosis.

*Adams et al. reviewed management of interosseous membrane (IOM) injuries seen in patients with ELI.* **They found** that the radial head is the primary stabilizer to axial load in the forearm whereas the IOM is a secondary stabilizer. **They conclude** that restoring the radial head will provide radial longitudinal stability and IOM reconstruction should be reserved for patients who undergo radial head excision.

Lawler and Adams present a novel surgical reconstruction technique for the DRUJ. They surmise that if the TFCC is unrepairable, DRUJ reconstruction provides the most biomechanical stability of the DRUJ. They provide a description of the novel reconstruction technique which includes tendon graft reconstruction through radial and ulnar bone tunnels.

Figure A is an AP x-ray of a right elbow demonstrating a radial head fracture. Video A demonstrates the Balltoment test to evaluate DRUJ instability.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: A Galeazzi fracture is a distal radius fracture with a DRUJ injury.
Answer 2: A Monteggia fracture is a proximal ulna fracture with a radial head dislocation.
Answer 4: Terrible triad of the elbow includes: radial head fracture, coronoid fracture, and posterior elbow dislocation.
Answer 5: Floating elbow occurs when there is a humerus fracture with a radius and/or ulna fracture of the same extremity.
