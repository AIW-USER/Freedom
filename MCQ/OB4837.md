## Question

After arthroscopic shoulder surgery a 45-year-old male develops pain, weakness and decreased sensation over the lateral arm and shoulder. If this injury was due to portal placement, which of the following portals would be the most likely site of injury?

## Options

1. Lateral superior portal
2. Posterior superior portal
3. Anterior portal
4. Posterior inferior portal
5. Nevasier (supraspinatus) portal

## Correct option

**4. Posterior inferior portal**

## Answer explanation

The axillary nerve is a risk with low posterior (7 o'clock) portals.

The axillary nerve leaves the axilla through the quadrangular space and winds around humerus on deep surface of the deltoid muscle and passes approximately 7 cm below tip of acromion. Injury to the nerve may occur when posterior portals are made inferior. Injury to the nerve can cause pain, weakness and decreased sensation over the lateral arm and shoulder.

*Lo et al. examined the neurovascular structures at risk during placement of glenohumeral arthroscopy portals using an outside-in technique.* **They found** that all of the neurovascular structures were more than 20 mm away from all the portals evaluated. The structure most at risk was the celphalic vein with anterior portals.

*Meyer et al. looked at 12 shoulder portals and their risk of neurovascular injury.* **They showed** that the 5 o'clock portal is more at risk of injuring the axillary nerve than the standard 7 o'clock portal. The other structure most at risk during shoulder arthroscopy was the cephalic vein.

Illustration B shows a top down view of the shoulder. Portal (B) shows the position of the low posterior 7 o'clock portal.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: There are no neurological structures at risk with the lateral superior portal.
Answer 2: There are no neurological structures at risk with posterior superior portal.
Answer 3: The musculocutaneous nerve may be at risk with anterior portals.
Answer 5: The suprascapular nerve may be at risk if the Nevasier (supraspinatus) portal is made into the suprascapular notch.
