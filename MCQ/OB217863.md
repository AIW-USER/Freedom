## Question

A 64-year-old female presents with right shoulder pain and weakness nine months after she was treated surgically for a proximal humerus fracture. Examination reveals right shoulder active abduction to 30 degrees and pain with passive range of motion. Radiographs reveal a malunion of the proximal humerus. Which of the following is true regarding treatment with a reverse shoulder arthroplasty rather than a hemiarthroplasty?

## Options

1. Increased major complication rate
2. Higher reoperation rate
3. Improved pain but lower range of motion
4. Greater improvement in functional outcome scores
5. Greater postoperative weakness

## Correct option

**4. Greater improvement in functional outcome scores**

## Answer explanation

Treatment of fracture-sequelae of the proximal humerus with reverse shoulder arthroplasty is associated with greater improvement in functional outcome scores compared to a hemiarthroplasty.

Proximal humerus malunion and other fracture-sequelae can lead to significant pain and functional limitations. Treatment options include revision ORIF, corrective osteotomy, subacromial decompression, or arthroplasty. Arthroplasty consisting of a reverse prosthesis has been demonstrated to provide reliable pain relief and functional improvement when compared to a hemiarthroplasty. This is likely related to less dependence on cuff integrity for final shoulder function.

*Jarrett et al. reviewed reverse total shoulder arthroplasty.* **The authors stated** the prosthesis design is capable of decreasing pain and improving function in rotator cuff-deficient shoulders. They described the distal and medial station of the center-of-rotation and how it allows for improved deltoid mechanics.

*Alentorn-Geli et al. performed a prospective non-randomized study of 32 patients treated with either hemiarthroplasty or reverse shoulder arthroplasty for fracture sequelae of the proximal humerus.* **The authors reported** a significantly higher improvement in total Constant scores and Constant activity level with reverse shoulder arthroplasty, and lower complications than hemiarthroplasty. **The authors concluded** reverse shoulder arthroplasty may be a better option for treated fracture sequelae of the proximal humerus. Based on this and many other studies, the number of hemiarthroplasty has significantly declined in recent years for the treatment of proximal humerus fractures.

*Willis et al. studied 16 patients with sequelae of proximal humerus malunion treated with reverse shoulder arthroplasty.* **They reported** all patients required increased retroversion to accommodate the proximal humeral deformity, but still achieved significant improvement in American Shoulder & Elbow Society (ASES) scores following reverse shoulder arthroplasty without any major complications. **They concluded** reverse shoulder arthroplasty can achieve satisfactory results in fracture sequelae associated with malunion.

Incorrect answers
Answers 1, 2, 3, and 5: Reverse shoulder arthroplasty has been associated with greater improvement in functional outcomes scores, and lower reoperation rates than hemiarthroplasty.
