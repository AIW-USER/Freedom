## Question

A 57-year-old male presents to your clinic with a two year history of lower back pain. You review the patient’s MRI, which is shown in Figure A. He asks, in particular, why the L3/L4 intervertebral disc appears brighter than the L5/S1 disc. Which of the following changes in the L5/S1 disc composition is responsible for this difference in appearance?

![[q220931-a-ddd.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Decreased aggrecan content, decreased water content
2. Decreased aggrecan content, increased water content
3. Increased aggrecan content, increased water content
4. Increased aggrecan content, decreased water content
5. Unchanged aggrecan content, decreased water content

## Correct option

**1. Decreased aggrecan content, decreased water content**

## Answer explanation

Degenerative disk disease is associated with decreased aggrecan content, which leads to a decrease in water content within the nucleus pulposus (Answer 1).

Disc degeneration is a known contributor to back pain and is associated with various structural and molecular changes. Aggrecan, a major proteoglycan in the disc, is responsible for maintaining disc hydration through its hydrophilic properties and effect on osmotic pressure. In degenerative disc disease, the concentration of aggrecan is reduced as a result of increased degradation. Increased fragmentation of this proteoglycan leads to enhanced leaching from the tissues. When this occurs, the osmotic pressure of the disc falls and the disc becomes dehydrated. Additional processes present in the degenerating disc may also downregulate aggrecan synthesis, further contributing to the loss of water content. Lower water content in the intervertebral disc is reflected in a decrease in T2 signal on MRI.

Inoue and Espinoza Orias reviewed processes involved in the development of intervertebral disk degeneration. They highlight the changes associated with disk degeneration and the subsequent changes seen in viscoelasticity and segmental instability.

*Urban and Roberts published a review covering the intervertebral disc degeneration.* *They review the normal biochemistry and anatomy of the healthy intervertebral disc before covering the nutritional, mechanical, and genetic etiologies of disc degeneration and their associations with specific patterns of biochemical and molecular changes.* **They conclude** that the high prevalence of back pain warrants enhanced research efforts beyond that which currently exists to catalyze advancements in treatment strategies.

Figure A demonstrates a lumbar spine MRI with degeneration of the L4/L5 and L5/S1 intervertebral discs evident in the presence of disc height loss and loss of signal intensity. Slight bulging of the L5/S1 disc is also present.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 2, 3, 4, and 5 do not accurately reflect the changes seen with degenerative disk disease with respect to aggrecan and water.
