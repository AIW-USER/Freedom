## Question

An 11-year-old girl sustains the following injury seen in Figure A. Assuming she has complete physeal arrest, which of the following is the closest approximation to the expected limb-length-discrepancy?

![[q4678-a-distalfemur.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. 1 cm
2. 2 cm
3. 3 cm
4. 4 cm
5. 5 cm

## Correct option

**3. 3 cm**

## Answer explanation

This patient has sustained a distal femoral physeal injury. If she undergoes complete physeal arrest, a leg length difference will develop. Insufficient data is provided to perform either Paley or Moseley methods of predicting LLD. Thus, traditional assumptions that girls achieve skeletal maturity at age 14 and the distal femur grows 9-10 mm per year must be used. Therefore she will have an approximate discrepancy of 10 mm/year x 3 years = 30 mm or 3 cm.

The most common distal femur physeal injuries are Salter Harris II fractures. These injuries have a high incidence of physeal arrest and growth disturbance.

*Garrett et al. reviewed patients who sustained displaced distal femoral physeal injuries.* *Most patients were treated with closed reduction and percutaneous pinning.* Physeal arrest correlated with increasing severity based on the Salter-Harris classification.

*Arkader et al. reviewed patients who had sustained distal femoral physeal fractures.* Fifty-nine percent of fractures were displaced. Physeal arrest was the most common complication. Fracture severity and fractures displacement correlated with higher rate of complications.

Figure A shows an AP radiograph of a distal femur physeal fracture. Because of fracture extension proximally through the metaphysis, this is classified as a Salter-Harris type 2 fracture.

Incorrect Answers
Answers 1, 2, 4, 5: As growth is assumed to stop at age 14 in female patients, she has 3 years of growth remaining. Given that her distal femur contributes 9-10 mm/year, she will have an approximate discrepancy of 10 mm/year x 3 years = 30 mm or 3 cm.
