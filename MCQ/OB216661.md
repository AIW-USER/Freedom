## Question

A 52-year-old woman complains of progressive medial-sided foot pain for the past 4 years. She has tried multiple braces and completed several courses of physical therapy during this time. On examination, she has rigid forefoot abduction with "too many toes" sign, and rigid hindfoot valgus. She is unable to do a single-heel raise and has severe sinus tarsi pain. Her radiograph is seen in Figure A. Which treatment is most indicated for this patient?

![[q216661-a-stage-3.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Immobilization in walking cast/boot for 3-4 months
2. FDL transfer, calcaneal osteotomy, TAL, ± forefoot correction osteotomy
3. First TMT joint arthrodesis, calcaneal osteotomy, TAL ± lateral column lengthening ± PTT debridement
4. Hindfoot arthrodesis
5. Tibiotalocalcaneal arthrodesis

## Correct option

**4. Hindfoot arthrodesis**

## Answer explanation

This patient has Stage III posterior tibial tendon insufficiency (PTTI) or adult acquired flatfoot. The most appropriate surgical intervention for her would be a hindfoot arthrodesis.

Posterior Tibial Tendon Insufficiency (PTTI) is the most common cause of adult-acquired flatfoot deformity. It is caused by attenuation and tenosynovitis of the posterior tibial tendon leading to medial arch collapse. The diagnosis can be made clinically with loss of medial arch of the foot which may progress to hindfoot valgus, forefoot abduction and subsequent development of midfoot osteoarthritis. Treatment is nonoperative with orthotics and ankle braces in early stages. A variety of surgical options are available and indicated for progressive and rigid deformities, subtalar or midfoot arthritis, and failure of nonoperative management. Hindfoot arthrodesis is indicated in stage III disease and typically is performed as a triple arthrodesis. Hindfoot arthrodesis may also be indicated with stage II disease with severe subtalar arthritis.

*Deland et al. reviewed treatment of PTTI.* **They report** that prompt early, aggressive nonsurgical management is important, but in a patient in whom such treatment fails should strongly consider surgical correction to avoid worsening of the deformity. **They concluded** that in all four stages of deformity, the goal of surgery is to achieve proper alignment and maintain as much flexibility as possible in the foot and ankle complex.

*Myerson et al. reviewed acquired flatfoot deformity caused by dysfunction of the posterior tibial tendon.* **They report** that treatment depends on the severity of the symptoms and the stage of the disease. **They concluded** that non-operative options, such as rest, administration of anti-inflammatory medication, and immobilization are beneficial in early disease, while operative options should be considered with more advanced disease.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Immobilization in walking cast/boot for 3-4 months is indicated as the first line of treatment in stage I disease
Answer 2: FDL transfer, calcaneal osteotomy, TAL, ± forefoot correction osteotomy ± spring ligament repair ± lateral column lengthening ± medial column arthrodesis ± PTT debridement is indicated in stage II disease
Answer 3: First TMT joint arthrodesis, calcaneal osteotomy, TAL ± lateral column lengthening ± PTT debridement is indicated in stage II disease with 1st TMT hypermobility, instability or arthritis
Answer 5: Tibiotalocalcaneal arthrodesis is indicated in stage IV disease with a rigid hindfoot, valgus angulation of the talus, and tibiotalar and subtalar arthritis. This patient's radiographs show no evidence of tibiotalar arthrits or tilt, thus a TTC arthrodesis is unnecessary
