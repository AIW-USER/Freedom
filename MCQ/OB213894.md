## Question

A 40-year-old single male sustains a closed head injury with a closed left femur fracture after falling off of a ladder at work. You meet him in the trauma bay 2 hours after his injury. He is lapsing in and out of consciousness as you're discussing his treatment options with him. He is able to confirm the details of the surgery, which is intramedullary nailing, but not able to repeat the risks associated with the procedure. He remains hemodynamically stable. His 20-year-old daughter and 55-year-old mother are both in the waiting room. Which of the following is true regarding informed consent for fixation of his femur fracture?

## Options

1. You may proceed with surgical treatment without consent given the severity of his injuries
2. Given his GCS and his confirming of surgical details, he is able to consent for surgery
3. His mother should be contacted for surgical consent
4. Chaplain consent should be sought after given the urgency of the procedure
5. Delay surgery until the consent is obtained from the next of kin

## Correct option

**5. Delay surgery until the consent is obtained from the next of kin**

## Answer explanation

This patient has sustained a left femur fracture which requires surgical fixation. Given this is not a limb or life-threatening injury, surgical consent should be obtained prior to proceeding. He is not able to stay conscious or repeat risks associated with the surgery, consent should be obtained for his next of kin which in this case, is his daughter.

Surgical consent is an important component of medical ethics where the surgeon must provide to the patient all information needed for the patient to make an informed decision required prior to intervention in the cases of non-life threatening injuries. The patient, as such, needs to understand and comprehend the risks, benefits, and alternatives for the procedure. If a patient is not able to consent for surgery due to injuries or an underlying medical condition, the next of kin should be consented. The next of kin precedence that has been established is in the following order: spouse, children (in no order), parents, siblings, grandchildren

*Wenger et al. conducted a survey of 102 orthopedic surgeons who correctly answered a mean of nineteen (73%) of the 26 questions involving medical and surgical ethics.* The respondents appropriately handled questions involving economic aspects, truth-telling, confidentiality, and an incompetent colleague. However, there was less understanding of proper ethical conduct with regard to informed consent (58%), the physician-patient relationship (72%), and end-of-life decision-making (78%).

*Bhattacharyya et al. performed a closed claims analysis on malpractice claims of 28 cases where inadequate informed consent was performed by orthopedic surgeons.* *All 28 cases were elective procedures.* **The authors noted** that documentation of informed consent in the office setting was associated with a decreased indemnity risk while obtaining informed consent in the hospital ward or in the preoperative holding area was associated with an increased indemnity risk. **The authors concluded** that surgeons may be able to decrease the risk of a malpractice claim by obtaining informed consent in their offices, rather than in the hospital or in pre-operative holding.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: A closed femur fracture is a relatively urgent procedure but is not a medical emergency and as such, informed consent should be obtained.
Answer 2: Since the patient is not able to repeat the risks and is lapsing in and out of consciousness, he is not fit to consent for surgery. Even those with GCS 15, need to be able to repeat the procedure and identify the pertinent risks associated with the procedure.
Answer 3: The next of kin precedence that has been established is in the following order: spouse, children (in no order), parents, siblings, grandchildren. The patient's 20-year-old daughter should be sought for surgical consent.
Answer 4: There is no need for a chaplain consent given the patient has "next of kin" family available.
