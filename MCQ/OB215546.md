## Question

A 59-year-old male patient with severe spinal stenosis presents for follow-up. The patient has attempted physical therapy and pain medications for six months without any improvement in his symptoms. The patient decides to undergo surgical treatment for his condition. Which of the following statements are true regarding this patient's condition and the findings of the SPORT trial?

## Options

1. Nonsurgical treatment results in improved pain relief but lower patient satisfaction than surgical treatment
2. Surgical treatment results in greater pain relief and improvement in function than nonoperative treatment
3. Nonsurgical treatment results in lower narcotic usage long-term than surgical treatment, but no changes in pre-treatment functional status
4. Surgical treatment results in similar pain relief as nonoperative treatment but with greater improvement in function
5. Return to work rates were higher with nonsurgical treatment compared to surgical treatment

## Correct option

**2. Surgical treatment results in greater pain relief and improvement in function than nonoperative treatment**

## Answer explanation

Based on the findings of the Spine Patient Outcomes Research Trial (SPORT), surgical treatment of symptomatic lumbar stenosis results in greater pain relief and improvement in function than nonsurgical treatment.

Lumbar spinal stenosis is typically the result of spondylosis leading to collapsed disc spaces, bulging discs, buckling of the ligamentum flavum, and hypertrophied facets. This process leads to encroachment of the neural elements and clinically presents with neurogenic claudication. Initial management consists of physical therapy and pain medications followed by epidural steroid injections. Patients that remain symptomatic despite adequate conservative treatment for 3-6 months are candidates for surgical decompression. Studies have shown there is a greater improvement in long-term pain and function with surgical treatment than continued nonoperative treatment.

*Lurie et al. performed a combined randomized trial and observational cohort study on the outcomes of lumbar disc herniation treated surgically and nonsurgically.* **They reported** a significant improvement in SF-36 bodily pain, SF-36 physical function, and Oswestry Disability Index (ODI) scores in patients treated surgically for lumbar disc herniations. **They concluded** appropriately selected patients experienced a lasting improvement in clinical outcomes with surgical treatment.

*Weinstein et al. performed a combined multicenter randomized trial and observational cohort study on the outcomes of degenerative spondylolisthesis treated surgically and nonsurgically.* **They reported** a significant improvement in SF-36 bodily pain, SF-36 physical function, and ODI scores in surgically treated patients in the as-treated analysis. **They concluded** patients with degenerative spondylolisthesis and associated spinal stenosis have a significant improvement in pain relief and function with surgical treatment.

*Weinstein et al. performed a combined randomized trial and observational cohort study on the outcomes of spinal stenosis treated surgically and nonsurgically.* **They found** a significant improvement in SF-36 bodily pain, SF-36 physical function, and ODI scores in patients treated surgically and were maintained out to four years. **The authors concluded** surgical treatment for symptomatic spinal stenosis is associated with significant improvement in pain and function than nonsurgical care.

Incorrect answers:
Answers 1, 3, 4, and 5: Surgical treatment of symptomatic lumbar stenosis results in greater pain relief and improvement in function than nonsurgical treatment.
