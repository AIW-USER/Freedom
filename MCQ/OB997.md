## Question

A 62-year-old female sustained the injury seen in Figure A after a fall at home. Her past medical history is significant for mild hypertension only. She is not currently taking any medications. Her pre-operative assessment revealed elevated serum alkaline phosphatase. Serum calcium and phosphate were normal. What is the most likely underlying diagnosis?

![[q997-a-64.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Paget's bone disease
2. Osteoporosis
3. Osteomyelitis
4. Renal osteomalacia
5. Aneurysmal bone cyst

## Correct option

**1. Paget's bone disease**

## Answer explanation

The clinical presentation is consistent with a pathologic subtrochaneric femur fracture in a patient with Paget's Disease.

Paget's disease is a chronic bone-remodelling disorder. It causes elevated serum alkaline phosphatase with normal levels of serum calcium and phosphate. Symptomatic Paget's disease can cause symptomatic bone pain, most often in the leg, hip or back. The treatment for symptomatic Paget's bone disease includes medical management for the inhibition of osteoclasts, including bisphosphonates or calcitonin, as well as supportive symptomatic therapy with physiotherapy, NSAIDS, or oral analgesics.

*Bidner et al. studied the characteristics and outcomes of 35 hip fractures in patients with Paget's bone disease.* **They showed** that subtrochateric fractures tended to occur in younger patients. Blood loss during fixation was found to be more significant compared to subcapital and intertrochanteric fracture patterns.

*Koh et al. studied the complication of femoral cortical stress lesions in patients on longterm bisphosphonate treatment.* In their findings, **they note** that these fractures occurred in the subtrochanteric region, were preceded by "a dreaded black line," and lateral femoral cortical thickening. All patients with these fractures also reported lateral thigh pain for over one month.

Figure A shows coarsening of the trabeculae with marked cortical thickening in the proximal segment of the right proximal femur and femoral neck. There is a displaced subtrochanteric hip fracture with lateral cortex thickening.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: While 62 y/o women often have underlying osteoporosis, Paget's disease in her right hip has resulted in a pathological fracture. In addition to the radiologic features of Paget's disease seen in this patient described above, osteoporotic fractures tend to occur in compression and more commonly in the femoral neck or intertrochanteric area.
Answer 3: There are no systemic symptoms of infection.
Answer 4: Pathological fractures may occur with renal osteomalacia. However, the bone of renal osteomalacia will often have large erosions, deformity, and amyloid deposition.
Answer 5: Aneurysmal bone cysts typically show loss of normal cortex.
