## Question

Which of the following is true of reverse total shoulder arthroplasty (RTSA) when compared to hemiarthroplasty (HA) for proximal humerus fractures?

## Options

1. Higher complication rate
2. Higher revision rate
3. Greater internal rotation
4. Less forward elevation
5. Higher incidence of tuberosity resorption

## Correct option

**1. Higher complication rate**

## Answer explanation

Complex proximal humerus fractures may be treated with either reverse total shoulder arthroplasty (RTSA) or hemiarthroplasty (HA). Despite a lower revision rate, the overall complication rate is higher for RTSA.

Proximal humerus fractures represent the third most common non-vertebral fracture pattern seen in the elderly. HA remains a viable treatment option in younger patients (40-65 years old) with complex fracture-dislocations or head-splitting components that are otherwise likely to fail fixation. With HA, lesser and greater tuberosity nonunions may lead to diminished lift-off strength as well as active shoulder elevation and external rotation, respectively. Conversely, RTSA is more often utilized in lower-demand elderly individuals with non-reconstructible tuberosities and poor bone stock. Complications following RTSA include scapular notching, dislocation, glenoid loosening, deep infection, acromial or scapular spine fractures, and axillary neuropraxia.

*Sebastiá-Forcada et al. prospectively reviewed complex proximal humeral fractures in elderly patients treated with either RTSA or HA.* **They report** that RTSA patients had significantly higher outcome scores, forward elevation, and abduction, but no difference in internal rotation. **The authors noted** that in the HA group, 57% of tuberosities healed and 30% resorbed (compared to 65% and 13% in RTSA group, respectively), and functional outcomes after RTSA were irrespective of healing of the tuberosities. **They concluded** that RTSA was associated with better pain relief, functional improvement, and a lower overall revision rate.

*Namdari et al. systematically reviewed the literature regarding complex proximal humeral fractures that were treated with either RTSA or HA.* **They found** that subjective outcomes, range of motion parameters (including active forward elevation, abduction, and external rotation) were similar between the two groups, but that RTSA was associated with 4-fold greater odds of developing postoperative complications. **The authors concluded** that despite these differences, both RTSA and HA resulted in reliable improvement in function, pain, and range of motion.

*Ferrel et al. reported on the clinical and radiographic outcomes of patients treated with RTSA or HA for acute complex proximal humeral fractures.* *In their systematic review,* **the authors found** that patients treated with RTSA had improved forward flexion but decreased external rotation compared with patients undergoing HA. There was otherwise no significant difference in clinical outcome scores between cohorts. RTSA was however associated with an increased rate of clinical complications but a lower overall revision rate. **They concluded** that RTSA was an acceptable surgical option for patients with acute complex proximal humeral fractures.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: There is a higher revision rate of HA compared to RTSA.
Answer 3: Internal rotation is generally greater following HA compared to RTSA.
Answer 4: Forward elevation is generally similar or favors RTSA over HA.
Answer 5: Despite the importance of tuberosity healing toward the functional outcomes following HA, the incidence of tuberosity resorption is reportedly higher compared to RTSA.
