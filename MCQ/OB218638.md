## Question

A 12-year-old boy presents with a four-day history of progressive pain and difficulty ambulating on his right leg. His parents state that he sustained a minor laceration to his right knee last week during a soccer game. Physical exam reveals a well-developed, uncomfortable-appearing child of expected appearance for his age. There is a 3cm healing laceration over the anterior aspect of the proximal right leg. There is an area of poorly demarcated erythema surrounding the wound. There is moderate tenderness to palpation and swelling about the proximal leg, but the patella is not ballottable and there is no palpable knee effusion. Lachmann, posterior drawer, and varus/valgus stress tests reveal a stable knee. There is some pain with calf squeeze. MRI findings are shown in Figures A and B. An ultrasound of the right leg is seen in Figure C. Which virulence factor is most responsible for these associated findings?

## Options

1. Superantigens
2. Biofilm
3. Endotoxin
4. Panton-Valentine leukocidin
5. Protein A

## Correct option

**4. Panton-Valentine leukocidin**

## Answer explanation

This patient is presenting with acute osteomyelitis and an associated popliteal deep vein thrombosis (DVT). Panton-Valentine leukocidin (PVL) is a cytotoxin produced by the PVL prophage which increases the risk of DVT in MRSA osteomyelitis.

PVL is one of the major virulence factors associated with MRSA. PVL-positive MRSA strains are associated with complex infections, multifocal infections, prolonged fever, abscess, DVT, and sepsis. While infrequent, all patients should be screened through physical exam to detect the possibility of an acute DVT, especially in the presence of concomitant risk factors: CRP >6 mg/dL, age >8 years, and recent surgical treatment.

*Hollmig et al. performed a retrospective, single-center review of 212 children with osteomyelitis.* Those in whom DVT developed were compared with those in whom it did not with respect to age, diagnosis, causative organism, duration of symptoms prior to admission, laboratory values at the time of admission, surgical procedures, and length of hospitalization. The group found that DVT associated with osteomyelitis is more common in children >8 years, those with MRSA-associated infections, and those presenting with a C-reactive protein level of >6 mg/dL.

*Woods et al. established the 2021 clinical practice guideline by the Pediatric Infectious Diseases Society and the Infectious Diseases Society of America on the diagnosis and management of acute hematogenous osteomyelitis (AHO) in pediatric patients.* The panel made a strong recommendation for the use of blood cultures as part of the initial evaluation for potential AHO based on the benefits that clearly outweigh the risks. In their study, 28 of 466 children with AHO presented with a DVT, and all 28 were positive for Staph aureus. However, the panel states that data specifically addressing antibiotic treatment regimens for AHO with associated DVT are limited. The presence of DVT is concerning for, if not indicative of, endovascular infection. At the present time, the use of bactericidal versus bacteriostatic agents, as one would do for treatment of endocarditis, for the entire treatment course for AHO with associated DVT is considered prudent by many.

Figures A and B are MRI slices demonstrating increased signal and small fluid collections suggestive of an infectious musculoskeletal process. Figure C is an ultrasound cross-section of the popliteal artery and vein showing an acute deep vein thrombosis.

Incorrect Answers:

Answer 1: Superantigens are a group of 25 known enterotoxins responsible for the induction of toxic shock syndrome, which is characterized by fever, erythema, hypotension, shock, multiple organ failure, and skin desquamation. They are not associated with increased DVT risk.
Answer 2: Biofilm is a protective mechanism that has high resistance to antibiotics and host immune responses. S. aureus biofilm consists of a glycocalyx slime layer and can consist of teichoic acids, host proteins, extracellular DNA (eDNA), and polysaccharide intercellular antigen (PIA). Biofilm is not associated with increased DVT risk.
Answer 3: Endotoxins are not associated with increased DVT risk.
Answer 5: Protein A anchors binds to the Fc region of IgG, and is thought to protect staphylococci from phagocytic killing. Additional studies have shown that Protein A also triggers cross-linking of B cell surface IgM receptors, resulting in proliferative supraclonal expansion and apoptotic collapse of activated B cells. Protein A is not associated with increased DVT risk.
