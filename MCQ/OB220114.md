## Question

A 44-year-old right-hand dominant helicopter pilot presents to the orthopedic clinic with a chief complaint of right-hand pain and difficulty using hand controls within his helicopter. He was diagnosed with Dupuytren’s contracture of his right hand and underwent collagenase clostridium histolyticum (CCH) injections 12 months ago. He received some initial benefits in motion; however, the cords have returned. On exam, he has painful nodules and cords to the small and ring finger. He cannot put his hand flat on the exam table and has a 55° contracture at the ring finger metacarpophalangeal (MCP) joint and a 45° contracture at the proximal interphalangeal (PIP) joint. The patient would prefer treatment that limits having another recurrence. Which of the following history or exam findings makes the patient a more appropriate candidate for partial palmar fasciectomy?

## Options

1. Painful palmar nodule
2. Degree of PIP joint contracture
3. Hand dominance
4. Number of cords involved
5. History of prior CCH injection

## Correct option

**2. Degree of PIP joint contracture**

## Answer explanation

This patient presents with significant contractures at both the metacarpophalangeal (MCP) and proximal interphalangeal (PIP) joints and desire to limit further recurrence. The degree of his PIP contracture and goal to limit the likelihood of recurrence make him a better candidate for partial palmar fasciectomy.

Dupuytren’s Disease (DD) is a proliferative disorder that begins as painless nodules and can progress to significant contractures. Patients may be unable to place their palms flat on the table as the disease progresses. Contractures can also develop at both the MCP and PIP. Several techniques exist for treatment, all with varying degrees of invasiveness, effectiveness, and risk of recurrence. CCH causes lysis and rupture of cords by targeting collagen types I and III. Patients typically undergo CCH injection in the clinic followed by manipulation from 1 day to 1 week later. Partial palmar fasciectomy involves an open incision and removal of disease tissue in a discrete area. Open surgical treatment remains the gold standard for severe contractures, especially at the PIP, and for a lower risk of recurrence.

*Hentz et al. published a review article regarding recent advances in the use of CCH for the treatment of DD.* *The authors provide a thorough review of the mechanism of CCH and how to perform CCH with manipulation in a clinical setting.* **They note** that an MCP contracture over 50° and a PIP contracture over 40° were far less likely to achieve contracture correction within 5° of full extension.

*Yang et al. published a review article about the management of the stiff finger.* *The authors review causes for decreased range of motion in a finger, including DD, tendon adhesions, lateral band tightness, skin scar, intrinsic muscle contracture, capsule contracture, collateral ligament contracture, palmar plate contracture, and bony block.* **The authors recommend** a precise clinical exam to identify the underlying cause, prevention via judicious mobilization, and initial non-operative management.

*Boe et al. published a literature review regarding DD.* *They discuss the pros and cons of needle aponeurotomy, CCH injection, and fasciectomy.* **They report** that 5 years after CCH treatment, 66% of PIP joints that corrected to within 5° of extension had a recurrence of greater than 20°. **The authors recommend** open surgical treatment for severe contractures and the most durable long-term correction.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: A painful nodule is not an indication for either CCH injection or fasciectomy.
Answer 3: Hand dominance is not an indication of fasciectomy over CCH.
Answer 4: Two cords can be addressed in a single treatment with either CCH or fasciectomy.
Answer 5: Repeat CCH injection can be performed in the case of recurrence.
