## Question

Figure A is the radiograph of a 32-year-old male who presents to your office with complaints of persistent ankle pain. He previously underwent fixation of the ankle six months ago following a car accident. He reports that his ankle has been painful ever since surgery but has been becoming increasingly painful. On examination, he has pain with squeezing of the distal tibiofibular joint. His labs are unremarkable and there is no concern for infection. Which of the following would be the most appropriate treatment for this patient at this time?

![[q215767-a-ankle-1.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Continued observation with a period of immobilization
2. Syndesmotic fixation only
3. Revision ORIF, fibular shortening osteotomy, and syndesmotic fixation
4. Revision ORIF, fibular lengthening osteotomy, and syndesmotic fixation
5. Tibiotalar fusion

## Correct option

**4. Revision ORIF, fibular lengthening osteotomy, and syndesmotic fixation**

## Answer explanation

This young patient has a previous ankle ORIF with a malunion of the fibula with obvious shortening. Of the answer choices listed, revision ORIF of the fibula, a fibular lengthening osteotomy, and syndesmotic fixation would be most appropriate.

It is important to ensure that fibular length has been restored following a lateral malleolus ORIF. Failure to achieve this may result in fibular malunion and a persistently painful ankle. In such cases, a fibular lengthening osteotomy may be required. With respect to syndesmotic fixation, the most critical aspect is ensuring that adequate reduction has been achieved. The size (3.5mm or 4.5mm) of the screw, number of screws, number of cortices (3 vs. 4), or use of a suture button do not seem to lead to significant biomechanical advantage.

*Andersen et al. compared clinical and radiographic results between patients who underwent stabilization of an acutely injured syndesmosis with a suture button (SB) and those treated with 1 quadricortical syndesmotic screw (SS).* **They reported** that the patients treated with an SB had higher outcome scores, better pain scores, and less widening seen radiographically at 2 years than did the patients in the SS group. **They concluded** that no differences in the scores for pain at night or during daily activities were identified.

*Laflamme et al. compared the clinical and radiographic outcome after stabilization of an acute syndesmosis rupture with either a static implant (a 3.5-mm metallic screw through 4 cortices) or a dynamic device (TightRope; Arthrex).* **They reported** that dynamic fixation of acute ankle syndesmosis rupture with a dynamic device seems to result in better clinical and radiographic outcomes. **They concluded** that the implant offers adequate syndesmotic stabilization without failure or loss of reduction, and the reoperation rate is significantly lower than with conventional screw fixation.

*Egger et al. reviewed fibular malunions and malreductions of the ankle mortise.* **They reported** that many fibular malunions can be successfully salvaged using osteotomy techniques to restore anatomic fibular length and rotation. **They concluded** that the oblique fibular osteotomy is described for the reconstruction of SER-type fibular fractures; more proximal fibular malunion may be better addressed with a transverse osteotomy technique with lengthening and structural bone graft.

*Sinha et al. reviewed malunited ankle fractures.* **They reported** that fibular length and restoration of the ankle mortise is successful in achieving bony union with improvement in outcome scores. **They concluded** that a simpler fibular osteotomy may be successful in correcting ankle joint alignment for malunited fibular fractures.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: This failed ORIF merits revision surgery in a young active patient
Answer 2: Revision ORIF, fibular lengthening, and syndesmotic fixation is recommended
Answer 3: Revision ORIF and syndesmotic fixation is appropriate, but a fibular lengthening (not shortening) osteotomy should be performed
Answer 5: Tibiotalar fusion should be reserved as a salvage procedure in a young active patient
