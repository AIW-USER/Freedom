## Question

A medical student is on his orthopedic spine rotation. While performing a microdiscectomy for a herniated lumbar disc, the attending surgeon starts to ask the medical students about spinal anatomy. When discussing the intervertebral discs, which of the following statements is true of the nucleus pulposus?

## Options

1. Composed primarily of type I collagen
2. Has a high collagen / low proteoglycan ratio
3. Innervated by the dorsal root ganglion of the sinuvertebral nerve
4. Receives its nutrient supply via diffusion through the vertebral end plates
5. Rich in fibroblast-like cells

## Correct option

**4. Receives its nutrient supply via diffusion through the vertebral end plates**

## Answer explanation

The intervertebral disc is avascular with capillaries terminating at the end plates. Nutrition reaches the nucleus pulposus through diffusion through pores in the endplates.

The intervertebral disc is composed of an outer structure called the annulus fibrosis and an inner structure called the nucleus pulposus. The annulus fibrosis is composed of type I collagen, water, and proteoglycans. The inner nucleus pulposus is composed of type II collagen, water, and proteoglycans. The nucleus pulposus receives nutrition primarily through diffusion through blood vessels within the endplate.

*Roberts et al. review the histology and pathology of the intervertebral disc.* **They reported** that at birth, the cartilagenous end plates have large vascular channels through them as well as vascular channels through the annulus. Soon after birth, these vascular channels close with none remaining at the end of the first decade of life. **They concluded** that with age, more blood vessels grow into the disc from the outer annulus fibrosis in response to degenerative changes.

*Fields et al. reviewed the innervation of the vertebral end plate and intervertebral disc.* **They reported** that vertebral end-plate pathologies are more innervated than intervertebral disc pathologies and that many innervated end-plate pathologies are not detectable on MRI. Thy concluded that these findings suggest that improved visualization of end-plate pathologies could enhance the diagnostic value of MRI for chronic low back pain.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The annulus fibrosis is composed primarily of type I collagen, while the nucleus pulposus is composed primarily of type II collagen
Answer 2: The annulus fibrosis has a high collagen/low proteoglycan ratio, while the nucleus pulposus has a low collagen/high proteoglycan ratio
Answer 3: The dorsal root ganglion gives rise to the sinuvertebral nerve which innervates the superficial fibers of the annulus. No nerve fibers extend beyond the superficial fibers
Answer 5: The annulus fibrosis is rich in fibroblast-like cells, while the nucleus pulposus is rich in chondrocyte-like cells
