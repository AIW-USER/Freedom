## Question

A 35-year-old female presents to your office with pain in the lateral aspect of her foot. She works as a waitress. This has been bothering her for months despite modifications in her shoe wear and anti-inflammatory medications. You review treatment options with the patient and ultimately offer her a lateral condylectomy. Which of the following is true of this procedure?

## Options

1. Used to correct intermetatarsal angles of >10 degrees
2. Does not require weight bearing restrictions postoperatively
3. Lateral half of the metatarsal head should be resected
4. Associated with the lowest rate of recurrence of disease
5. High rate of secondary metatarsalgia postoperatively

## Correct option

**2. Does not require weight bearing restrictions postoperatively**

## Answer explanation

There are no weight-bearing restrictions following lateral condylectomy, which is a discrete advantage compared to metatarsal head osteotomies which can also be used to treat Type 1 bunionettes.

Bunionette deformities, commonly called tailor's bunion, are prominences on the lateral aspect of the 5th metatarsal head that most commonly occur due to compression of the forefoot. They are classified into 3 types - Type 1 consists of an enlarged metatarsal head with or without a lateral exostosis, Type 2 represents a congenital bow of the 5th metatarsal with a normal 4-5 IMA, and Type 3 bunionettes have an enlarged 4-5 IMA. While both metatarsal head osteotomy and lateral condylectomy can be used to treat Type 1 bunionettes, lateral condylectomy has the advantage of no weight-bearing restrictions postoperatively. Conversely, the metatarsal head osteotomy allows for a larger degree of correction than lateral resection alone.

*Shi et al review the management of bunionette deformities.* **They report** that resection of the lateral condyle is typically reserved for patients who have an isolated prominent lateral condyle, but it can also be considered for patients who cannot tolerate the limitations or comply with postoperative weight-bearing restrictions. **They conclude** that resection does not correct malalignment but instead reduces the mass effect.

*Cohen et al review bunionette deformities.* **They report** that lateral resections are appropriate in patients who are unable postoperatively to restrict weight bearing on the lateral forefoot and whose primary problem is pressure from the prominent lateral condyle. **They conclude** that patients often do very well with lateral resection.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Patients with increased 4-5 IMA should be treated with a metatarsal osteotomy rather than a lateral condylectomy.
Answer 3: It's recommended that only the lateral quarter to third of the metatarsal head be resected.
Answer 4: Lateral condylectomy has the the highest rate of bunionette recurrence following surgery.
Answer 5: Metatarsalgia is most commonly seen following metatarsal head resection, not lateral condylectomy.
