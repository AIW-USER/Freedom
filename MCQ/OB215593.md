## Answer explanation

A transverse + posterior wall fracture type (Figure C) has the highest risk of nerve injury in the setting of acetabulum fractures. Foot drop in this patient is secondary to injury to the peroneal branch of the sciatic nerve.

Acetabulum fractures vary in complexity, but have an overall high rate of morbidity and mortality associated with them. The Judet and Letournel classification is the most widely used classification system for acetabular fractures. It classifies acetabular fractures into ten major fracture patterns, which consist of five simple patterns and five complex patterns (Illustration A). Nerve injuries are highly associated with fractures of the acetabulum, occurring in 3%–33% of patients with the sciatic nerve affected most often. This nerve injury is secondary to posterior fracture displacement, especially when there is an associated dislocation of the hip. The most common manifestation of this injury is a foot drop/dorsiflexion deficit on the affected side due to involvement of the peroneal division of the sciatic nerve.

*Simske et al. retrospectively evaluated nerve injuries and their prognosis in the setting of acetabulum fractures.* **They found** an overall low incidence of nerve injury (3.3%) with 65% of sciatic nerve injuries affecting the common peroneal division only, while none were isolated to the tibial division. The most common fracture pattern associated with nerve injury was transverse + posterior wall (31% of injuries). Nerve recovery was found to occur in 72% of patients (50% partial nerve recovery, 22% had complete recovery) while 24% of patients with sciatic or common peroneal injuries had no recovery.

*Bogdan et al. performed a large multicenter series of operatively treated acetabular fractures with documented neurologic injury and tracked their neurologic recovery and ultimate outcomes.* *In total 137 patients with mostly high-energy mechanisms were included.* The most common fracture types associated with neurologic injury were transverse + posterior wall (33%), posterior wall (23%), and associated both column (23%). Neurologic deficits were primarily in the sciatic nerve distribution and identified as preoperative in 57%, iatrogenic in 19%, and 24% identified postoperatively in those whose preoperative exam was limited (i.e. obtunded patient). A total of 34% of patients had no recovery and only 60% of patients were able to return to work.

Figure A depicts a posterior wall fracture. Figure B depicts an associated both column fracture. Figure C depicts transverse + posterior wall fracture. Figure D depicts a posterior column + posterior wall. Figure E depicts a posterior column fracture. Illustration A depicts the complete Judet and Letournel classification system for acetabulum fractures.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Depicts a posterior wall fracture accounting for 9.5%-23% of nerve injuries.
Answer 2: Depicts an associated both column fracture accounting for 15.6% of nerve injuries.
Answer 4: Depicts a posterior column + posterior wall fracture accounting for 12.5%-23% of nerve injuries.
Answer 5: Depicts a posterior column fracture, which in isolation rarely has an associated nerve injury.
