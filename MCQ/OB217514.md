## Question

You are giving a lecture on hallux rigidus to a group of medical students and want to review the different treatment options available. You discuss dorsal cheilectomy, proximal phalanx osteotomy, resection arthroplasty, MTP joint arthroplasty and MTP joint arthrodesis. Of these options, which of the following patients would be the best candidate for MTP joint arthrodesis based on their physical examination findings?

## Options

1. 45-year-old female with pain at terminal dorsiflexion
2. 75-year-old male with stiffness and decreased push-off strength of her great toe but no associated pain
3. 55-year-old male with pain and shoe wear irritation when running
4. 32-year-old female gymnast with pain in full MTP dorsiflexion
5. 62-year-old female with pain throughout MTP motion

## Correct option

**5. 62-year-old female with pain throughout MTP motion**

## Answer explanation

Hallux MTP arthrodesis is most appropriate for patients with Grade 3/4 hallux rigidus, meaning that there is significant stiffness, large dorsal osteophytes, or pain throughout the full range of motion on examination.

Hallux rigidus is defined by pain and stiffness at the MTP joint of the great toe. There is a spectrum of severity with hallux rigidus, which has been classified by Coughlin and Shurnas based on the degree of pain at the mid-range or extremes of motion, combined with the degree of radiographic involvement (Illustration A). MTP joint arthrodesis should be considered in cases of Grade 3 or 4 involvement where there is pain throughout the mid-range of motion or there is significant MTP joint arthritis/bony destruction. In the appropriately indicated patient, MTP joint arthrodesis has been shown to have a high rate of fusion and patient satisfaction.

*Coughlin et al. provide a background of the demographics and radiographic assessment of hallux rigidus.* **They noted** hallux rigidus was commonly found bilaterally in those with a family history, unilaterally in those with a history of trauma, and more commonly in females. Contrarily, hallux rigidus was not associated with first ray hypermobility, a long first metatarsal, Achilles/gastrocnemius contracture, shoe wear, occupation, or hallux valgus.

*Wagner et al. reviewed the technique for hallux MTP arthrodesis.* *They review the steps for exposure, joint preparation, and arthrodesis positioning (5-10º valgus).* *They finish by noting that this technique normally results in good patient satisfaction, function, and low complication rates.*

Illustration A demonstrates the Coughlin and Shurnas classification (Grades 0-4) of hallux rigidus based on radiographic and clinical findings.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: MTP pain at terminal dorsiflexion only represents Grade 1 hallux rigidus, which is normally managed with dorsal cheilectomy if surgery is necessary.
Answer 2: MTP joint stiffness and decreased strength with no pain represent Grade 0 hallux rigidus. This can usually be successfully managed nonoperatively.
Answer 3: In a patient with pain and shoe wear irritation while running, shoe wear modifications and possibly dorsal cheilectomy would be most appropriate, as an MTP arthrodesis would likely limit his ability to run.
Answer 4: A young gymnast with pain at the end range of motion represents a Grade 1 or 2 hallux rigidus depending on their radiographic findings. This patient would be best managed nonoperatively versus with dorsal cheilectomy.
