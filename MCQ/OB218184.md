## Question

A 72-year-old female presents to clinic three months after a total knee arthroplasty. There is concerns that she is developing a prosthetic joint infection (PJI). Her incision is well-healed without drainage or dehiscence. Based on the 2018 Diagnostic Criteria for PJI defined by Parvizi and associates, what preoperative lab abnormalities in isolation would most strongly suggest an infected prosthetic joint?

## Options

1. Elevated synovial white blood cell (WBC) count & positive synovial alpha-defensin
2. Elevated serum erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR) & elevated synovial CRP
3. Elevated serum D-dimer & elevated synovial C-reactive protein (CRP)
4. Elevated synovial WBC count & elevated synovial polymorphonuclear cells (PMNs)
5. Elevated synovial PMNs & elevated synovial CRP

## Correct option

**1. Elevated synovial white blood cell (WBC) count & positive synovial alpha-defensin**

## Answer explanation

Of the provided answer choices, only elevated synovial WBC count & positive alpha defensin will provide a score of 6 points or greater, indicating infection according to the 2018 Parvizi study (answer 1).

Diagnosing a prosthetic joint infection in total hip and knee arthroplasty can be a challenging but critical endeavor. *In order to simplify and standardize diagnostic criteria, Parvizi and associates developed criteria in 2018 (Illustration A) to expand on the previous 2011 MSIS criteria.* Included in this algorithm are major and minor criteria. Major criteria (communicating sinus tract or pathogen isolated from 2 separate tissue/fluid samples) alone can confirm the diagnosis of PJI. Minor criteria include serum labs and synovial fluid analysis, with different tests being allotted different points. The total points of the minor criteria are used to determine a PJI diagnosis in the absence of major criteria. A score of 0-1 indicates no infection, 2-5 indicates an inconclusive finding, and 6 points or greater is diagnostic for infection.

*Fernandez et al. performed a prospective study of 130 patients with PJIs to assess for the accuracy of various diagnostic tests.* *The study evaluated the receiver-operating-characteristic (ROC) curves of various tests including sonicate fluid culture, synovial fluid, peri-implant tissue, C-reactive protein, and histopathology.* **The authors concluded** that sonicate fluid culture was the most sensitive individual diagnostic method but combining tests obviously increases sensitivity for all stages of infection.

*Parvizi et al. published their landmark study in 2018 to better define prosthetic joint infections.* In addition to the well-established major criteria (communicating sinus tract or two positive cultures of the same organism), the authors added a scoring system to several minor criteria. With the new scoring system, which reports a sensitivity of 97.7% and specificity of 99.5%, a pre-operative score of 6 or greater is considered infected. **The authors concluded** that this new algorithm will better help clinicians diagnose and treat prosthetic joint infections.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Elevated serum ESR is worth 1 point and elevated synovial CRP is worth 1 point as well. The combined score of 2 points would not be diagnostic of infection.
Answer 3: Elevated serum D-dimer is worth 2 points and elevated synovial CRP is worth 1 point. The combined score of 3 points would not be diagnostic of infection alone.
Answer 4: Elevated synovial WBC count and elevated synovial PMNs give a combined score of 5 points. This would qualify as possibly infected but would not be diagnostic of infection alone.
Answer 5: Elevated synovial PMNs and elevated synovial CRP give a combined score of 3 points, not diagnostic of infection.
