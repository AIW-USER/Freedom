## Question

A new arthroplasty surgeon has just graduated from an adult reconstruction fellowship and is returning to his hometown to join a private practice. He plans to use the minimally invasive approaches and multimodal pain control techniques he learned in fellowship. He is buying advertising space in the local paper to kickstart his practice. Which of the following statements is an acceptable phrase that adheres to ethical standards?

## Options

1. "Safe Surgery For All Patients"
2. "Pain-Free Hip and Knee Replacement"
3. "The Cure for All Hip and Knee Pain"
4. "Bloodless Hip and Knee Replacement"
5. "Less Invasive Hip and Knee Replacements"

## Correct option

**5. "Less Invasive Hip and Knee Replacements"**

## Answer explanation

Of the answers provided, only "Less Invasive Hip and Knee Replacements" would be deemed acceptable to advertise, as this accurately reflects their approaches, without implying a guaranteed improved outcome. (Answer choice 5).

Physicians are ethically obligated to present themselves via advertisements accurately. Because the physician-patient relationship is based on trust, it is paramount that physicians accurately present themselves and their treatments to patients. Physician advertisements should not have explicitly false or implied misrepresentations of material fact. Furthermore, physicians should be able to substantiate any claims or qualifications made in advertisements. The United States Federal Trade Commission may enact penalties for any advertisements deemed unfair, misleading, deceptive, or false.

Dr. Capozzi reviews sample advertising statements made by orthopedic surgeons and evaluates some common false or misrepresentative claims that surgeons have made. The author warns surgeons against using terms such as "bloodless" or "painless" as these are improbable outcomes that will mislead patients. Similarly, advertising a cure for an ailment that will not be entirely eradicated with the procedure, such as pain, is misleading. Finally, while using terms such as "less invasive" or "minimally invasive" is permitted, surgeons should avoid making conclusions on the outcomes of these techniques unless supported by literature.

The American Academy of Orthopaedic Surgeons (AAOS) provides an opinion on ethical advertising, which can be found online ([https://www.aaos.org/contentassets/6507ec63e5ac4ea48375ad96d154daac/1205-advertisingopiniononethicsbodapprovedjune2016.pdf).](https://www.aaos.org/contentassets/6507ec63e5ac4ea48375ad96d154daac/1205-advertisingopiniononethicsbodapprovedjune2016.pdf).) The authors stress the importance of truthful advertising that accurately represents the surgeon's qualifications, services, and expected outcomes of procedures. The authors note to avoid using terms that imply a guarantee or misrepresent outcomes or qualifications. Any terms used must be supported by evidence. Overall, the AAOS Opinion on Ethical Advertising provides guidelines to maintain ethical standards and warns that violations could result in professional consequences.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer choice 1: Using such definitive terms implies a lack of risk when undergoing procedures. This is misleading to patients, as inherent risk is involved in surgery, and the surgeon cannot guarantee the procedure will be completely safe, especially for all patients. Terms such as "relatively safe" or "one of the safer orthopedic surgeries" would be better used.
Answer choice 2: The term "pain-free" is misleading to patients. It is highly unlikely that any invasive orthopedic procedure will be completely painless in some form.
Answer choice 3: The term "cure" can be deceptive, particularly if it refers to general pain. Curing a condition would mean the condition no longer exists and will not recur. While an arthroplasty procedure may cure a patient of arthritis, it cannot guarantee that it will cure their hip and knee pain. This statement is misleading and should be avoided in this context.
Answer choice 4: Bloodless surgery does not realistically exist and presents a false impression to patients. This term should be avoided, while terms such as "relatively little blood loss" may be more acceptable.
