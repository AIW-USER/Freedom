## Question

While covering the high-school state championship football game your asked to expediently evaluate the quarterback for return to play. His team was driving and about to score the go-ahead touchdown when he sustained a blindside hit with 2-minutes left in the game. After the hit, he laid motionless on the ground for 8-seconds prior to getting up with the help of his teammates who report that he never lost consciousness. He speaks slowly, but his words and sentences make sense. His only complaint at this time is a moderate headache, and he explicitly denies numbness or tingling in any of his extremities. The player and his coach would like him to return to the game after sitting out one play as dictated by the rules. Which test must the player pass to return for the remainder of the game?

## Options

1. Standard Assessment of Concussion (SAC)
2. Immediate Post-concussion Assessment and Cognitive Testing battery (ImPACT)
3. Sensory Organization Test (SOT)
4. King-Devick (K-D) test
5. None of the above

## Correct option

**5. None of the above**

## Answer explanation

This patient sustained a concussion during this hit as evidence by his lying motionless on the ground for >5 seconds and now having a headache. He is prohibited from returning to play.

Concussions have gained a lot of attention recently due to the identification of long-term sequalae include chronic traumatic encephalopathy (CTE). Symptoms of concussion can vary greatly among athletes, but headache is the most common and present in 70% of cases. A player does not need to lose consciousness to sustain a concussion, and in fact only 10% of players have this symptom. Other commonly identified symptoms are being slow to get up, confusion or disorientation, clutching of the head, amnesia, vacant look, and motor incoordination. Players diagnosed with concussions are prohibited from returning to play and must complete a graduated return to play protocol. Several tests are used for the evaluation and management of concussions, however, none of them allow players diagnosed with concussions to return to play on the same day.

*Herring et al review the consensus statements on concussions and the responsibilities of the team physicians.* **They report** that acute clinical symptoms of a concussion demonstrate a functional disturbance rather than structural injury and may are typically self-resolving. **They conclude**, that no same-day return to play is appropriate following symptoms concerning for a concussion.

*McCrory et al review the summary and agreement statement of the 2nd International Conference on Concussion in Sports.* **They report** that concussions result in a graded set of clinical syndromes that may or may not involve loss of consciousness and represent impairment of neurologic function. **They conclude** that patients with signs or symptoms of concussions should not return to play or practice on the same day, should not be left alone, and should always undergo a medical evaluation.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The SAC test evaluates orientation, memory, and concentration. It may not be used as a RTP tool.
Answer 2: ImPACT test is a computer-based test that assesses verbal and visual memory, processing speed, reaction time, impulse control and presence of concussive symptoms.
Answer 3: The SOT assesses integrity of the entire balance system by testing the vestibular, visual, and somatosensory systems.
Answer 4: The K-D test examines saccadic eye movements, language and concentration and may not be used to assess sideline RTP.
