## Question

Which of the following pre-operative findings on electrodiagnostic studies would portend to the worst prognosis following carpal tunnel release?

## Options

1. Distal sensory latency of 3.0 ms and distal motor latency of 4.0
2. Motor conduction velocity of 55 m/s
3. Distal motor latency of 4.0 ms and distal sensory latency of 5.0 ms
4. Distal sensory latency of 3.0 ms and distal motor latency of 5.0 ms
5. Distal motor latency of 5.0 ms and distal sensory latency of 4.0 ms

## Correct option

**5. Distal motor latency of 5.0 ms and distal sensory latency of 4.0 ms**

## Answer explanation

The severity of disease as confirmed by electrodiagnostic studies is the factor most strongly associated with poor recovery after carpal tunnel release. Of the listed choices, the worst electrodiagnostic results is answer choice 5 with prolonged distal sensory latency (> 3.5 ms) and prolonged distal motor latency (> 4.5 ms).

Electrodiagnostic studies (EDS) which consist of both nerve conduction studies (NCS) and electromyography (EMG) are helpful in both the diagnosis and prognosis of those with carpal tunnel syndrome (CTS). Specific findings of CTS as noted on EDS include: distal sensory latency > 3.5 ms, distal motor latency > 4.5 ms, nerve conduction velocity < 52 m/s, and sharp waves/fasciculation/fibrillations noted in the thenar muscles on EMG. Furthermore, nerve conduction velocity is considered a less specific finding of the severity of disease compared to distal motor and sensory latency.

*Bland performed a retrospective study of 3336 patients with CTS to determine prognostic variables associated with various treatments.* **The authors noted** that of the 1268 surgical procedures that were identified, the overall surgical success rate was 69%. **They noted** that the preoperative nerve conduction study findings, greater age, lower symptom scores, longer disease duration, and male gender were significant predictors of poor outcome following CTR. **The authors also noted** that nerve conduction studies had the strongest effect, with patients with middle-grade abnormalities having better results than those with very severe abnormalities

*Kronlage and Menendez performed a retrospective review of the surgical outcomes of patients with CTS who had moderate versus severe disease on EDS.* *The authors reviewed 95 patients who underwent CTR (47 in the moderate cohort, and 48 in the severe cohort).* **They noted** that patients with moderate CTS reported, on average, no symptoms, and patients with severe disease had reduced but unresolved symptoms at 3 months followup. **The authors concluded** that patients with severe CTS experience a considerable reduction in symptoms after surgery but should be informed that recovery may be more prolonged and, in some cases, incomplete 1 year after CTR, particularly with regard to numbness.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: A distal sensory latency of 3.0 ms and distal motor latency of 4.0 are both considered normal values
Answer 2: A motor conduction velocity of 55 m/s is considered normal (abnormal is < 52 m/s)
Answer 3: While a distal sensory latency of 5.0 ms is considered abnormal, a distal motor latency of 4.0 ms is normal.
Answer 4: While a distal motor latency of 5.0 ms is considered abnormal, A distal sensory latency of 3.0 ms is considered normal.
