## Question

A 68-year-old female presents to your office with complaints of left wrist pain for years. Radiographs show advanced wrist arthritis due to scapholunate advanced collapse. Which of the following accurately describes the result of dissociation between the scaphoid and lunate?

## Options

1. Scaphoid flexion and lunate extension
2. Scaphoid flexion and lunate flexion
3. Scaphoid extension and lunate flexion
4. Scaphoid extension and lunate extension
5. No flexion or extension of either the scaphoid or lunate

## Correct option

**1. Scaphoid flexion and lunate extension**

## Answer explanation

When the scaphoid and lunate dissociate, the scaphoid flexes while the lunate extends to create a dorsal intercalated segment instability (DISI).

Scapholunate advanced collapse (SLAC) describes the specific pattern of degenerative arthritis seen in chronic dissociation between the scaphoid and the lunate. Diagnosis is made clinically with progressive wrist pain and wrist instability with radiographs showing advanced arthritis of the radiocarpal and midcarpal joints (of note, the radiolunate joint is most typically spared). Treatment involves observation, NSAIDs, and splinting in the early stages of the disease. A variety of operative procedures may be indicated depending on the severity of the disease and the patient's symptoms.

*Strauch et al. reviewed SLAC and scaphoid nonunion advanced collapse (SNAC).* **They reported** that SLAC wrist may result from wrist arthritis and/or chronic pseudogout. **They concluded** that surgical treatment options for SLAC wrist include 4-corner arthrodesis, capitolunate arthrodesis, complete wrist arthrodesis, proximal row carpectomy (PRC), denervation, and radial styloidectomy depending on disease severity and patient functional needs.

*Kitay et al. reviewed injuries to the scapholunate joint.* **They reported** that these injuries often result in carpal instability and account for a considerable degree of wrist dysfunction, lost time from work, and interference with activities. **They concluded** that although it is insufficient to cause abnormal carpal posture or collapse on static radiographs, an isolated injury to the scapholunate interosseous ligament (SLIL) may be the harbinger of a relentless progression to abnormal joint mechanics, cartilage wear, and degenerative changes.
