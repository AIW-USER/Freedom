## Question

A 21-year-old recreational runner sustained a right ankle twisting injury 10 days ago. A non-weightbearing splint was placed at the time of injury. Figures A and B show clinical and radiographic images of the ankle after the splint was removed. Examination reveals mild tenderness over the calcaneal fibular ligament, positive talar tilt and increased talar drawer test with the ankle in dorsiflexion. What would be the next best step in this patients management?

## Options

1. CT scan right ankle
2. MR imaging right ankle
3. Continue non-weightbearing casting for 2-4 weeks, followed by functional rehabilitation
4. Transition into a removable walking boot for 2-4 weeks, followed by functional rehabilitation
5. Early functional rehabilitation

## Correct option

**5. Early functional rehabilitation**

## Answer explanation

This patient has sustained a low ankle sprain. The ideal treatment after 7-10 days of immobilization is to initiate functional rehabilitation.

Low ankle sprains often occur from inversion injuries that affect the anterior talo-fibular ligament (ATFL) and calcaneal fibular ligament (CFL). The initial treatment of these Injuries include rest, ice, elevation and compression, or short periods of immobilization (approx. 7-10 days). After swelling has subsided, neuromuscular and proprioceptive training has been shown to be an important step in the functional recovery of these injuries, as well as to decrease the risk of recurrence of ankle sprains.

*Petersen et al. performed a systematic literature review of the evidence for the treatment and prevention of lateral ankle sprains.* **They showed** that a short period of immobilization (max. 10 days) in a below knee cast is advantageous in grade III injuries. For non-surgical treatment, long-term immobilization should be avoided.

*Kaminski et al. reported the recommendations of athletic trainers, and other allied health care professionals, in the conservative management and prevention of ankle sprains in athletes.* Strong evidence from this paper include multi-intervention injury-prevention programs lasting at least 3 months that focuses on balance and neuromuscular control to reduce the risk of ankle injury.

*Pihlajamaki et al. compared outcomes of surgery vs. functional treatment only for acute Grade-III lateral ligament injuries of the ankle.* *All patients in both groups had recovered their pre-injury activity level and reported that they could walk and run normally.* Findings suggest that surgery appeared to decrease the prevalence of re-injury of the lateral ligaments, but may increase the risk of secondary arthritis.

Figure A shows lateral ankle swelling and bruising. Figure B shows a normal AP and lateral radiograph of the ankle.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1, 2: Advanced imaging is not required acutely in this patient. However, it this patient continues with persistent pain and swelling, these findings may warrant advanced imaging to rule out an occult fracture or osteochondral lesion.
Answer 3: For non-surgical treatment, long-term immobilization should be avoided.
Answer 4: Transition into a functional semi-rigid brace may be considered with Grade-III tears. A rigid walking boot is not recommended.
