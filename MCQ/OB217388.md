## Question

A 28-year-old male presents to your office after sustaining a laceration over the dorsal aspect of his right index finger 1 week ago while chopping vegetables. The wound was irrigated and primarily closed at an urgent care the day of the injury and the wound was covered with bacitracin ointment and dressed. In the office, the laceration site is erythematous and blistered but is not draining or showing any signs of fluctuance. He denies fevers or a significant increase in pain. What is the most likely cause of his presentation?

## Options

1. Allergic contact dermatitis
2. Cellulitis
3. Deep bacterial wound infection
4. Dermatomycoses
5. IgE mediated urticaria

## Correct option

**1. Allergic contact dermatitis**

## Answer explanation

This patient has allergic contact dermatitis, a type IV delayed hypersensitivity reaction that results in erythema, swelling, and blistering but no purulence or drainage.

Allergic contact dermatitis represents a delayed hypersensitivity reaction that is mediated by T-cells and modulated my macrophages and monocytes. It usually takes 7-10 days to develop, compared with type 1 IgE mediated hypersensitivity reactions which occur more quickly. Bacitracin is often used after repair of lacerations to prevent infection but carries a 5-10% risk of allergic contact dermatitis. Common symptoms include erythema, edema and blistering, in contrast to infection which often demonstrates an increase in pain, drainage/purulence and fluctuance. Proper treatment includes the withdrawal of the offending agent as well as steroid creams in some cases.

*Heal et al. reviewed the use of topical antibiotics for preventing surgical site infection in wounds healing by primary intention.* **They noted** a probable decreased risk of infection with the use of antibiotics relative to antiseptics/no treatment but were unclear as to whether or not antibiotics affected rates of allergic contact dermatitis secondary to a lack of study power.

*Nguyen et al. reviewed contact dermatitis secondary to medications and skin products.* *They reviewed many different products including a subset focusing on topical antibiotics for their potential to cause contact dermatitis.* *They provide strategies for diagnosis, treatment, and prevention of contact dermatitis in these products.*

*Draelos et al. attempted to evaluate whether antibiotic-based ointments improved outcomes after dermatologic procedures.* *In their study, there was no increased efficacy in wound healing but there did seem to be an increase in subjective burning sensations at week one, with one case of allergic contact dermatitis being reported.* **They concluded** that topical antibiotics may not be necessary to achieve satisfactory wound healing and may lead to allergic contact dermatitis.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Cellulitis may show erythema and swelling but would likely be associated with an increase in pain and possibly other systemic symptoms and blistering would not be seen.
Answer 3: A deep infection would likely show erythema and fluctuance as well as an increase in pain.
Answer 4: Dermatomycoses, or ringworm, would likely occur more chronically and would be more common in a moist area with a more characteristic appearance.
Answer 5: IgE mediated urticaria represents a type I hypersensitivity reaction presenting with red, itchy hives and usually presents more acutely without associated blistering.
