## Question

A 42-year-old woman presents with pain localized to the dorsum of the great toe. She reports undergoing a bunion correction with limited internal fixation using a single screw 2 years prior, which initially helped her. She subsequently underwent implant removal one year later. She is now unable to properly fit shoes on that foot. Her current radiograph is depicted in Figure A. Of the following procedures below, which did she likely undergo?

![[q3814-a-screen-shot-2020-02-29-at-12-01-25-pm.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Akin osteotomy
2. First metatarsal proximal crescentic osteotomy
3. Modified McBride procedure
4. First cuneiform osteotomy
5. Keller resection arthroplasty

## Correct option

**2. First metatarsal proximal crescentic osteotomy**

## Answer explanation

This patient is presenting with a dorsiflexion malunion of the first metatarsal after bunion correction surgery. Of the above procedures, only the first metatarsal proximal crescentic osteotomy would cause a dorsiflexion malunion.

The first metatarsal proximal osteotomy has been described as a standard treatment in addressing moderate to severe hallux valgus deformity. The proximal crescentic osteotomy has gained popularity as it allows triplanar correction with a high rate of union. Unfortunately, due to the inherent instability, there is a relatively high rate of malunion in dorsiflexion, particularly when less rigid modes of internal fixation (i.e. K-wires, single screws) are utilized to maintain the osteotomized fragments. The treatment of a dorsiflexion malunion involves surgical correction with first metatarsal plantarflexion osteotomy.

*Varner et al. evaluated the strength of fixation of single screw versus plate for stabilization of proximal first metatarsal crescentic osteotomies for correction of hallux valgus.* **They reported** that dorsiflexion malunion is reported in up to 82 % of cases fixed using a single screw fixation with or without supplemental Kirschner wire fixation. **They highlighted** that plate rather than screw fixation was twice as strong in all measures of fixation.

*Brodsky et al. evaluated postoperative plantar pressure patterns in a series of patients after a first metatarsal proximal crescentic osteotomy.* **They reported** that the average peak pressure under the second metatarsal head increased, and observed radiographic evidence of first metatarsal elevation. **They concluded** that the osteotomy was unpredictable in positioning the first ray in the sagittal plane.

*Mann et al. investigated the results of patients with hallux valgus deformity treated with first metatarsal proximal crescentic osteotomy as well as soft tissue procedures.* **They reported** a 93% satisfaction rate, with complications including hallux varus, recurrence of hallux valgus, pain under the fibular sesamoid, and a tailor's bunion, in decreasing order.

Figure A depicts a dorsiflexion malunion of the first metatarsal.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The Akin osteotomy is a proximal phalanx medial closing wedge osteotomy and would not result in a malunion of the metatarsal.
Answer 3: The Modified McBride procedure includes the release of the adductor from the fibular sesamoid/proximal phalanx, lateral capsulotomy, and medial capsular imbrication. It does not involve an osteotomy of the metatarsal.
Answer 4: A first cuneiform osteotomy would not cause a malunion of the first metatarsal.
Answer 5: The Keller resection arthroplasty includes resection of base of the proximal phalanx and medial eminence. It would not cause a malunion of the first metatarsal.
