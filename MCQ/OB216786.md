## Question

Figure A is the radiograph of a 72-year-old righthand dominant male who presents to the emergency department following a skiing injury. He has no other injuries. Radiographic measurements reveal 7 mm of displacement. Which of the following treatments is indicated?

![[q216786-a-gt.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Nonoperative management
2. Open reduction and internal fixation
3. Fragment excision
4. Hemiarthroplasty
5. Total shoulder arthroplasty

## Correct option

**2. Open reduction and internal fixation**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a displaced greater tuberosity fracture. The recommended treatment is open reduction and internal fixation.

Most proximal humerus fractures may be properly managed with nonoperative management. However, isolated greater tuberosity fractures with >5 mm of displacement negatively alter the rotator cuff mechanics. If the greater tuberosity heals in a malunited position, this will lead to limitations in shoulder abduction or external rotation with altered rotator cuff mechanics. In active patients, it is well-accepted that greater tuberosity fracture displacement >5 mm is an indication for open reduction internal fixation (ORIF) to restore normal shoulder mechanics and prevent impingement.

*Bissell et al. reviewed the incidence of humerus fractures following snowboarding and skiing.* **They report** that the incidence of humerus fractures among snowboarders and skiers is 0.062 and 0.041 per 1000, respectively. **They concluded** that snowboarders are at a significantly higher risk of sustaining humerus fractures than skiers, while in skiers the injury most often involve the proximal humerus.

*George et al. reviewed isolated fractures of the greater tuberosity of the humerus.* **They report** that nondisplaced and minimally displaced fractures are typically treated successfully nonsurgically. **They concluded** that surgical fixation is recommended for fractures with >5 mm of displacement in the general population or >3 mm of displacement in active patients involved in frequent overhead activity.

Figure A is the AP radiograph of the shoulder revealing a displaced greater tuberosity fracture

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Greater tuberosity fracture displacement <5 mm may be successfully managed with sling immobilization followed by progressive rehabilitation
Answer 3: Fragment excision is not indicated as it serves as the attachment for the rotator cuff muscles
Answers 4 and 5: Shoulder arthroplasty is not indicated in an acutely displaced greater tuberosity fracture in a young, active patient
