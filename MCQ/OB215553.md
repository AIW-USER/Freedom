## Question

Figure 1 is the postoperative radiograph of a 44-year-old laborer with a history of long standing medial sided knee pain following a complete meniscectomy performed 15 years ago. You selected this procedure to correct his varus deformity. Which of the following is true of this procedure compared to the medial sided approach for the same indication?

![[q215553-a-screen-shot-2021-06-01-at-6-52-30-am.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Increased risk of patellar baja
2. Increased risk of popliteal artery injury
3. Less stable construct requiring more robust fixation
4. Shorter time to weight bearing
5. Decreased risk of injury to the peroneal nerve

## Correct option

**4. Shorter time to weight bearing**

## Answer explanation

Lateral closing wedge tibial osteotomies are more stable, allow early weight bearing, and have a lower chance of causing patella baja.

High tibial osteotomies (HTO) may be performed medial or laterally for the treatment of varus deformity in young patients who are not great candidates for TKA. Generally, indications for HTO consists of young (<50-years-old) active patients with good vascular status, who are not obese, and have pain localized to a single compartment. Medial sided procedures consist of an opening wedge osteotomy, which are less stable, require longer periods of non-weight bearing and are associated with an increased risk of iatrogenic patella baja. The lateral sided procedure, shown in Figure A, is a closing wedge osteotomy which is more stable, allows for earlier weight bearing and avoids the need for bone grafting.

*Rossi et al review the role of HTOs in the treatment of vaurs knees.* **They report** that the clinical indications for this procedure include varus alignment, medial compartment arthrosis, knee instability, medial compartment overload following meniscectomy and osteochondral defects requiring resurfacing procedures. **They conclude** that the most important aspect of the procedure for a durable result is correction of the coronal alignment.

*Duivenvoorden et al compare medial opening wedge and lateral closing wedge HTO in a randomized controlled trial with 6-year follow up.* **They report** no difference between medial and lateral procedures as it relates to clinical or radiographic outcomes in the subset of patients who did not require conversion to TKA. **They conclude** that medial opening wedge procedures were associated with more complications, but that closing wedge osteotomies were associated with an earlier conversion to TKA.

*Amzallag et al review patellar height modification after high tibial osteotomy by either medial opening-wedge or lateral closing wedge osteotomies.* **They report** no significant change in patellar height following lateral closing wedge osteotomies. Conversely, medial opening wedge osteotomies decreased patellar height by >20%. **They conclude** that baseline measurements of patellar height should be performed prior to high tibial osteotomies.

Figure A is the postoperative radiograph of a patient who underwent a lateral closing wedge HTO.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: A medial opening wedge HTO is associated with a higher rate of patella baja.
Answer 2: Popliteal artery injury is a very rare complication of knee surgery, and is not linked with medial or lateral approaches for HTO.
Answer 3: Lateral HTO creates a more stable construct as it is a closing wedge osteotomy and does not require bone grafting.
Answer 5: Lateral HTOs have a higher rate of injury to the peroneal nerve as compared to medial opening wedge procedures.
