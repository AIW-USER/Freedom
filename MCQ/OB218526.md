## Question

A high school football player has been encouraged by his strength and conditioning coach to add muscle mass leading up to the season. He begins taking a performance-enhancing substance that increases cellular ATP energy stores. What is a potential side effect of this substance?

## Options

1. Dehydration
2. High blood pressure
3. Tendon injuries
4. Acromegaly
5. Cardiac arrhythmias

## Correct option

**1. Dehydration**

## Answer explanation

Creatine increases cellular ATP stores and pulls water from the intravascular space into the intracellular space leading to an increased risk of dehydration.

Creatine supplementation functions to act as a phosphate storage substrate to enhance ATP stores within the cell. Multiple studies have shown mild but significant increases in body composition and muscle strength, but limited proof of enhanced performance and recovery. The increased creatine content intracellularly pulls water from the intravascular column into the cellular column which can increase the risk of dehydration.

*McGuine et al. performed a cross-sectional, multisite, anonymous survey between 37 public high schools in Wisconsin regarding creatine use in football players.* **They determined** that 30% of respondents used creatine with the perceived benefit of faster recovery and dehydration being the biggest risk. **They concluded** creatine was widely used by this population and further education is needed to warn parents, patients, and coaches about the risks of creatine use.

*Rawson et al. evaluated 23 men for muscle damage (blood levels of LDH and creatine kinase), elbow flexor strength, soreness, and arm circumference after taking 5 days of creatine or placebo.* **They reported** no differences with respect to muscle damage, strength, arm circumference, or soreness between the two groups. **They concluded** creatine does not reduce indirect markers of muscle damage to enhance recovery.

*Branch performed a meta-analysis on the effect of creatine supplementation on body composition and performance.* **The author reported** small but significant effect sizes with respect to body composition, glycolysis, and oxidative phosphorylation that was greatest during the loading cycle. **The author concluded** creatine supplementation does not appear to be effective in improving running or swimming performance.

Incorrect answers
Answer 2: Caffeine can lead to increased blood pressure.
Answer 3: Steroids can lead to tendon injuries.
Answer 4: Human growth hormone can lead to acromegaly with prolonged use.
Answer 5: Ephedra can lead to cardiac arrhythmias which can be fatal.
