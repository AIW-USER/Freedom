## Question

A 45-year-old male injured his knee while playing basketball. On physical examination, he has a large hematoma in his knee and a palpable defect proximal to his patella. ## Options

1. Figure A has a higher ultimate load to failure, and less gap formation
2. Figure A has a higher ultimate load to failure, but more gap formation
3. Figure A has less gap formation, but has a lower ultimate load to failure
4. Figure B has a higher ultimate load to failure, and less gap formation
5. Figure B has less gap formation, but has a lower ultimate load to failure

## Correct option

**4. Figure B has a higher ultimate load to failure, and less gap formation**

## Answer explanation

This patient has sustained a quadriceps tendon rupture. Figure A is an illustration demonstrating a transosseous suture repair technique while Figure B demonstrates repair with suture anchors. Suture anchor repair has been shown to have a higher ultimate load to failure with less gap formation.

Ruptures of the quadriceps tendon usually occur in patients over the age of 40 due to degenerative changes caused by reduced blood supply, repetitive microtrauma, diabetes, renal failure, use of steroids, and previous knee surgery. Patellar tendon ruptures have a similar etiology but usually, occur in patients under the age of 40. Successful repair is impeded by high mechanical loads and a poor blood supply. Increasing strands crossing the repair site, and increasing the caliber of the suture increase the repair strength, but may lead to wound complications. Classically the method of repair is sutures passing through transpatellar bone tunnels (Figure A). Recently biomechanical studies have shown superior results with suture anchor repair when assessing gap formation and ultimate load to failure.

*Petri et al. performed a human cadaveric study comparing the biomechanical properties of quadriceps tendon repair with suture anchors and transosseous sutures.* *Thirty cadaveric knees underwent tenotomy followed by repair with either 5.5-mm-double-loaded suture anchors or transpatellar suture tunnels using No. 2 suture and the Krackow whipstitch.* **They found** that tendon repairs with suture anchors yielded significantly less gap formation during cyclic loading and resisted significantly higher ultimate failure compared with transosseous sutures.

*Kindaya et al. performed a study to compare the biomechanical properties of a knotless suture anchor quadriceps tendon repair technique with transosseous and traditional, fully threaded, suture anchor repair techniques in 20 matched pairs of cadaveric knees.* **They found** that compared with transosseous repairs, quadriceps tendons repaired with knotless suture tape demonstrated significantly less displacement during cyclic loading, improved construct stiffness, and greater ultimate load to failure. **They also found** that the knotless suture anchors had decreased initial displacement during loading and increased the ultimate load to failure compared to the traditional suture anchors.

Figure A is an illustration demonstrating the classic repair of a quadriceps tendon with transosseous tunnels. Figure B is an illustration demonstrating quadriceps tendon repair with suture anchors.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1, 2, 3, and 5: Suture anchor repair of quadriceps tendon ruptures have been shown to have less gap formation and an increased load to failure when compared to the transosseous technique.
