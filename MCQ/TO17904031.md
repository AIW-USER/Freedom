## Question

True about meniscus root tear is

## Options

1. Medial meniscal root tears are more commonly seen with ACL tear
2. Lateral meniscal root tears are mostly degenerative in nature
3. Concomitant lateral meniscal posterior root tear with ACL tear should always be repaired
4. Anterior placement of ACL tibial tunnel can cause iatrogenic injury to anterior root of lateral meniscus.

## Correct option

**3. Concomitant lateral meniscal posterior root tear with ACL tear should always be repaired**

## Answer explanation

Meniscal root tears are defined as bony or soft tissue root avulsion injuries or radial tears within 1 cm of meniscus root attachment.

Lateral meniscus posterior root tears (LPRT) are more likely to occur with an ACL tear than medial meniscus posterior root tears (MPRT), which are commonly degenerative. Injuries to the anterior horns of the menisci are less common and frequently occur iatrogenically, such as during ACL tibial tunnel reaming (posterolateral placement of tunnel) and tibial nailing on tibial shaft fractures.

![[TO17904031-1.png]]

Functions: The meniscal roots have an important function for the meniscus to convert axial tibio- femoral loads into hoop stresses. The loss of meniscal anchoring to the tibial plateau results in loss of normal meniscus function, meniscal extrusion, and altered knee kinematics. This leads to unequal and abnormal distribution of knee loading, thereby decreasing tibio- femoral contact area and increasing peak contact pressures. The increased contact pressures due to meniscal root tears are detrimental to the articular cartilage, and can lead to the development of early OA if not treated adequately

In cases of ACL deficiency, the LM posterior root plays an important role in stabilizing the knee in both anterior tibial translation (ATT) and during pivoting activities. Therefore, in patients who exhibit a grade 3 Lachman and a 3+ pivot shift, a possible LPRT should be suspected.

Anatomic repair of the meniscal root should be attempted whenever possible to prevent meniscus damage and OA, except in cases in which the patient is a poor surgical candidate

Answer: 3
