## Question

A newborn infant is delivered via breech presentation. The ultrasound scan of both hips performed at six weeks of age is shown in Figure A. How would you interpret the findings?

![[q5649-a-fig-a.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. The alpha angles are normal bilaterally. Both hips are normal.
2. The alpha angles are normal bilaterally. The right hip is normal. The left hip is dysplastic.
3. The alpha angles are normal bilaterally. The left hip is normal. The right hip is dysplastic.
4. The alpha angle is normal on the left and abnormal on the right. The left hip is normal. The right hip is dysplastic.
5. The alpha angle is normal on the right and abnormal on the left. The right hip is normal. The left hip is dysplastic.

## Correct option

**5. The alpha angle is normal on the right and abnormal on the left. The right hip is normal. The left hip is dysplastic.**

## Answer explanation

The alpha angle is normal on the right and abnormal on the left. The right hip is normal. The left hip is dysplastic.

Ultrasound of the infant hip is indicated where there are abnormal examination findings, family history of developmental dysplasia of the hip (DDH), breech presentation, oligohydramnios, neuromuscular conditions and for monitoring of patients treated with a Pavlik harness or splint. Ultrasound images are classified by the Graf classification (see Illustration E) where the alpha angle determines type, and beta angle determines subtype. An alpha angle >60° is normal.

*Leba et al. performed screening for infants with risk factors but normal physical examination.* Of 217 infants, 17 (7.8%) had DDH on dynamic ultrasound evaluation and were treated with Pavlik harness. **They concluded** that selective ultrasound may be effective in infants with risk factors and normal examination findings.

*Roof et al. reviewing screening for DDH.* **They recommend** age appropriate screening for stable hips with positive risk factors (ultrasound at 6 weeks or X-ray at 4 months). If the alpha angle <50° and head coverage <40%, **they recommend** treatment. If the alpha angle >50°, and head coverage >40%, **they recommend** repeating the ultrasound in 4-6weeks as mild dysplasia might resolve spontaneously.

Figure A shows that both hips are located within the acetabulum (not subluxated). The Graf angle on the right is normal, but on the left is Graf 2C, immature hip. Illustration C shows the alpha and beta angles on coronal ultrasound. Illustration D shows the Graf classification. Illustration E is a table showing the Graf classification. Illustration F shows the ossific nucleus of the femoral head on ultrasound

Incorrect Answers
Answers 1-4: The alpha angle is normal on the right (>60°) but not on the left. This indicates that the right hip is normal, and the left is dysplastic.
