## Question

A 54-year-old female presents to your office for evaluation of right forefoot pain. She reports pain on the bottom of her foot that is worse with standing or walking. Additionally, she endorses a tingling sensation that radiates to her 3rd and 4th toes. On physical exam, a Mulder’s click is present. Which of the following is true regarding the most likely diagnosis?

## Options

1. MRI is needed to confirm the diagnosis
2. Affected nerve lies dorsal to the transverse intermetatarsal ligament
3. Pathology occurs at the confluence of the medial and lateral plantar nerves
4. Deep peroneal nerve is the structure most at risk during surgical approach
5. Pathology is due to nerve entrapment in the anterior tarsal tunnel

## Correct option

**3. Pathology occurs at the confluence of the medial and lateral plantar nerves**

## Answer explanation

This patient has signs and symptoms consistent with an interdigital (Morton’s) neuroma, which is a compressive neuropathy most commonly occurring at the confluence of the medial and lateral plantar nerves.

Interdigital (Morton’s) neuromas affect the terminal branches of the medial and lateral plantar nerve, which lies plantar to the intermetatarsal ligament. Patients most commonly have pain on the plantar forefoot with radiation of burning and/or tingling sensation into the affected toes. Diagnosis is based on history and physical exam, with MRI providing little benefit. The presence of a Mulder’s click, which is performed by squeezing the forefoot and applying dorsal pressure between the metatarsal heads, can aid in the diagnosis (Illustration A). Additionally, a common digital nerve block can be performed to confirm the diagnosis. Definitive treatment consists of a dorsal neurectomy, through which blunt dissection should initially be used to protect branches of the superficial peroneal nerve.

*Title et al review the diagnosis and management of Morton’s neuromas.* **They report** that patient history and physical exam are the most important factors in determining the diagnosis and location of the interdigital neuroma. Their preferred treatment is primary neurectomy through a dorsal incision with care to protect the superficial peroneal nerve. **They conclude** that with primary neurectomy 51-85% of patients will have successful results, however, 70% will continue to have limitations in shoe wear.

*O'Connor et al reviewed the correlation of clinical, operative and histopathologic results pertaining to interdigital neuromas.* **They report** that of 133 primary interdigital neurectomies, 5 cases had intraoperative concern for a diagnosis other than neuroma, of which surgeons accurately diagnosed cysts or rheumatoid nodules. 132 of 133 histopathology reports indicated the presence of neural tissue. **They conclude** that with a high level of clinical and intraoperative acumen in identifying a neuroma, routine histopathological evaluation is not necessary.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: MRI is not required for the diagnosis of Morton’s neuroma. It can occasionally be used to rule out other pathologies, but the primary diagnosis is based on history and physical exam.
Answer 2: The affected interdigital nerve lies on the plantar aspect of the intermetatarsal ligament, which is released during the dorsal approach.
Answer 4: Branches of the superficial peroneal nerve, not the deep peroneal nerve is at risk during the dissection.
Answer 5: Anterior tarsal tunnel syndrome entraps the deep peroneal nerve and is unrelated to interdigital neuromas.
