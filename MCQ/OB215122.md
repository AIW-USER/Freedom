## Question

Your co-worker invites you over to his house for dinner. When you arrive you notice his house is larger and more extravagant than any other on the street. During dinner, you ask him what his secret is to own such a lavish home. He tells you he refers all of his patients to a separate physical therapy company he co-owns, located several blocks from your clinic location. What is your co-worker violating through his actions?

## Options

1. Fee-Splitting Provisions
2. Certificate of Need Laws
3. Stark Law
4. Physician Payments Sunshine Act
5. Health Information Portability and Accountability Act

## Correct option

**3. Stark Law**

## Answer explanation

Stark Law, part of the Social Security Act section 1877, prohibits the referral by a physician to an entity with which he or she has a financial relationship for the furnishing of a designated health service.

In 1989, Stark Law was created to regulate self-referrals of physicians to organizations in which they have financial relationships. It was meant to clarify the anti-kickback statute of Medicare, however, it has its own convolutions that can make interpretation difficult. Violations of Stark Law can result in monetary penalties of up to $100,000. Designated health services include clinical laboratory, physical therapy, radiology services, and durable medical equipment and supplies. Exceptions exist if certain services are provided "in-office".

Glaser, a lawyer, published an extensive review of legal issues as related to ancillary services and orthopedic practice. In his review, he discusses Stark law, the Medicare anti-kickback statute, state anti-kickback, fee-splitting provisions, Certificate of Need laws, and various Medicare billing and supervision requirements.

*Manchikanti and McMahon published a review that provides an overview of the Stark law from its initial inception in 1989, through its multiple expansions and refinements.* *They provide further discussion on the implications of Stark law on physicians' practices.*

Incorrect Answer:
Answer 1: Fee-splitting provisions limit the sharing of revenue with professional colleagues in return for being sent referrals.
Answer 2: Certificate of Need laws are regulations at the state level on the expansion or establishment of health care facilities and services.
Answer 4: The Physician Payments Sunshine Act was established as a federal regulation to increase the transparency of physician-industry financial relationships by obligating public disclosure of payments and financial interests made to physicians by manufacturers of drugs, devices, biologicals, and medical supplies.
Answer 5: The Health Information Portability and Accountability Act addresses the flow of health information and protects patients' personally identifiable information from fraud and theft.
