## Question

A 20-year-old collegiate pitcher presents to your sports medicine clinic for preoperative discussion prior to elbow ulnar collateral ligament reconstruction. You plan to perform a docking technique with autograft. What is the likelihood of his return to preinjury levels of throwing?

## Options

1. 0-20%
2. 20-40%
3. 50-60%
4. 60-75%
5. 80-95%

## Correct option

**5. 80-95%**

## Answer explanation

With modern reconstruction techniques, 85-95 percent of throwing athletes return to their preinjury throwing levels after ulnar collateral ligament reconstruction (UCLR) (Answer 5).

Ulnar collateral ligament (UCL) injuries are becoming increasingly common in overhead-throwing athletes. Patients typically present with decreased throwing performance and posteromedial elbow pain while throwing. UCLR is a proven treatment option for athletes with deficient UCLs. Recent systematic reviews have demonstrated an 86% return to sport rate for all athletes following UCLR, with collegiate athletes having a 95% return to sport rate. While UCLR was originally described by Dr. Frank Jobe in 1986, there have been several modifications since its inception. The original technique described a flexor-pronator tenotomy, routine ulnar nerve transposition, and an autograft placed in a figure-of-eight fashion through ulnar and medial epicondyle drill tunnels. The modified Jobe technique describes a flexor-pronator mass-splitting approach and avoids ulnar nerve transposition with the same drill tunnels. The docking technique uses converging ulna tunnels with a single medial epicondyle tunnel. This allows for greater control of graft tensioning while avoiding excessive ulnar nerve manipulation. The docking technique has demonstrated lower complication rates and higher return to sport rates than the Jobe technique.

*Conway et al. published the first series of throwers undergoing UCLR for medial instability of the elbow.* *They reviewed 71 patients over 13 years, with 14 patients undergoing UCL repair and the remainder having UCLR.* Fifty percent of patients who underwent UCL repair and 68 percent of patients undergoing UCLR could return to the pre-injury level of sport.

*Erickson et al. conducted a systematic review to examine return to sport rates after UCLR, specifically examining the level of sport and surgical technique.* Overall, 86% of patients were able to return to sport, with collegiate athletes having the highest return to sport rates. **The authors conclude** that the docking and Andrews' sports medicine institute techniques have a higher return to sport rates than the Jobe technique.

Incorrect Answer choices:
Answer choices 1-4: The return to sport rate after UCLR in collegiate athletes with modern reconstruction techniques is high, with systematic reviews demonstrating a 95 percent success rate.
