## Question

A 5-year-old boy is seen in the emergency room with a 3 day history of limping and painful range of motion of the left hip. He has no history of trauma. Observation of his gait demonstrates the ability to bear weight, although he has a notable limp. Radiographs are seen in Figures A and B. He is afebrile and his labs include: ESR of 20 mm/h (normal less than 22 mm/h), CRP of 18 mg/l (normal less than 20 mg/l), and WBC 9.0 (reference range 4.5 - 10.0). Which of the following most likely preceded the onset of his symptoms?

*Figure A*

![[q5874-b-6-year-old-normal-frog-lat-pelvis.jpg]]

## Options

1. Hemotogenous bacterial innoculation of the hip joint
2. Infectious respiratory illness
3. Hypothyroidism
4. Exposure to second hand smoke
5. Limb length discrepancy

## Correct option

**2. Infectious respiratory illness**

## Answer explanation

This patient's presentation is consistent with transient synovitis of the hip, which is thought to occur due to a preceding viral respiratory illness.

The diagnosis of a limping child can be challenging with a broad differential. The laboratory values provided and lack of fever suggest this is not a septic hip, but rather it is consistent with transient synovitis. Transient synovitis of the hip is considered the most common cause of pediatric hip pain. It's etiology is not fully understood but is believed to follow a bacterial or viral respiratory illness.

*Nouri et al. provide a review of transient synovitis of the hip in children.* **They note** that it most commonly follows a viral illness, and presents with the absence of fever and normal laboratory markers to distinguish it from septic arthritis.

*Singhal et al. present a study comparing CRP in patients with septic arthritis vs. transient synovitis.* **They found** that only CRP and weight bearing status were independent predictors of septic arthritis vs. transient synovitis. A CRP> 20 mg/l was the strongest independent risk factor for septic arthritis with an odds ratio of 81.9 (p<0.001).

Figure A is an AP radiograph of a pelvis in a skeletally immature patient without specific pathologic findings.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: Hematogenous bacterial inoculation of the joint is the cause of septic arthritis.
Answer 3: Hypothyroidism can be a risk factor for slipped capital femoral epiphysis (SCFE). There is no evidence for SCFE in this patient.
Answer 4: Exposure to secondhand smoke has been correlated with occurrence of Legg-Calve-Perthes disease (LCPD). These is no evidence of LCPD in this patient.
Answer 5: Limb length discrepancy (LLD) would cause a painless limp, and there is no evidence of LLD in this patient.
