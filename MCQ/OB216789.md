## Question

A 22-year-old male collegiate long jumper falls forward after completing a jump, landing forcibly on a pronated, slightly flexed arm. He notices immediate pain and inability to move his elbow after the fall. He is taken to the nearest hospital where radiographs are obtained and demonstrate fracture of the ulnar medial collateral ligament attachment. Based on the scenario described, which of the following injury patterns has occurred?

## Options

1. Lateral collateral ligament disruption, coronoid tip fracture, radial head fracture, ulnohumeral joint dislocation
2. Lateral collateral ligament disruption, anteromedial coronoid facet fracture, ulnohumeral joint dislocation
3. Medial collateral ligament disruption, anteromedial coronoid facet fracture, radial head fracture
4. Olecranon fracture, ulnohumeral joint dislocation, radiocapitellar joint dislocation
5. Proximal ulna fracture, radiocapitellar joint dislocation

## Correct option

**2. Lateral collateral ligament disruption, anteromedial coronoid facet fracture, ulnohumeral joint dislocation**

## Answer explanation

In cases of varus posteromedial rotatory instability (VPMI), as described in this clinical scenario, the typical constellation of injuries includes lateral collateral ligament (LCL) rupture, anteromedial coronoid facet fracture and ulnohumeral joint subluxation/dislocation.

VPMI of the elbow has become increasingly recognized as a cause of elbow instability and involves a varus stress with axial load. This creates a cascade that causes the lateral collateral ligament (LCL) to fail in tension and avulse from the humeral attachment, followed by a fracture of the anteromedial coronoid facet which leads to subluxation or dislocation of the ulnohumeral joint. The anteromedial facet represents the bony attachment of the medial collateral ligament (MCL) and is a critical weight-bearing portion of the ulnohumeral joint. Radial head fractures are uncommon in this pattern and more likely seen in cases of valgus posterolateral rotatory instability, as the radius is compressed into the capitellum. Understanding the characteristic fracture pattern can help identify cases of subtle VPMI. Most cases of VPMI require open reduction and internal fixation of the anteromedial facet to prevent persistent subluxation and early onset of elbow arthritis.

*Sanchez Sotelo et al. describes the medial coronoid compression fracture and its mechanism/consequences.* **They note** that it can be subtle, but may also lead to incongruity of the ulnohumeral joint. They further discuss identification and classification and the importance of fixation of this structure.

*Bryce et al. review the anatomy and biomechanics of the elbow, including the bony and ligamentous restraints.* **They note** the three primary static constraints of the elbow are the MCL, LCL and ulnohumeral articulation and when these are compromised the elbow is left at risk for instability. They go on to describe the specific contributions of the static and dynamic elbow stabilizers and their biomechanic roles in elbow stability.

*Ring et al. describe appropriate fixation of anteromedial coronoid facet fractures.* **They noted** persistent varus subluxation of the ulnohumeral joint in patients where the anteromedial facet fragment was not addressed or fixation had failed. They went on to note that this group had a high rate of early progression to arthritis, while those with adequate fixation had excellent function of their elbow.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: LCL disruption, radial head fracture, coronoid tip fracture and ulnohumeral dislocation occur in cases of posterolateral rotatory instability (PLRI).
Answer 3: While medial collateral ligament injury can occur concomitantly with VPMI, radial head fracture is usually not seen as the lateral structures fail in tension and there is impaction of the medial elbow.
Answer 4: This pattern describes a transolecranon fracture-dislocation of the elbow, which typically usually leaves the collateral ligaments and their attachments intact.
Answer 5: Proximal ulna fracture with radiocapitellar joint dislocation describes a Monteggia fracture which does not have the same collateral ligament involvement as seen in VPMI.
