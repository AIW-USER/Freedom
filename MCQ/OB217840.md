## Question

A 68-year-old male presents to your clinic with severe hip osteoarthritis for which he has failed nonoperative management. You discuss treatment options and plan for total hip arthroplasty (THA) using a posterior approach. Intraoperatively, you encounter significant bleeding after acetabular retractor placement and fear you may have injured the external iliac artery. In what position was this retractor most likely placed?

## Options

1. Anterior-inferior quadrant
2. Anterior-superior quadrant
3. Posterior-inferior quadrant
4. Posterior-superior quadrant
5. Directly inferior

## Correct option

**2. Anterior-superior quadrant**

## Answer explanation

Aberrant retractor placement anterior to the acetabulum in the anterior-superior quadrant may place the external iliac vessels at risk for injury.

Total hip arthroplasty (THA) is generally a very reliable and safe procedure. However, during acetabular exposure and component placement, the risk for neurovascular injury does exist. For this reason, the acetabulum was divided into quadrants based on a line extending from the ASIS through the center of the acetabulum and another line perpendicular. The external iliac vessels run along the medial border of the psoas muscle when anterior acetabular retractors are placed, these structures can be at risk in this area. These structures can also be injured during screw placement in acetabular component fixation. Surgeons must have an understanding of the surrounding neurovascular anatomy in order to avoid potentially catastrophic injury.

*Barrack et al. published an instructional course lecture on avoiding neurovascular injuries during total hip arthroplasty.* **They note** that peripheral nerve injuries are rare during THA, with the femoral, sciatic, superior gluteal, and obturator nerves at risk. Vascular injuries are even less common, with mechanisms of injury including vascular occlusion, cement removal, and screw fixation; **they recommend** that a thorough vascular examination should be performed preoperatively in all patients.

*Parvizi et al. reviewed vascular injuries which occur during total joint arthroplasty.* **They noted** that only 0.1% of total joints performed at their institution had a vascular injury (16/13,517), with 11 occurring during TKA and 5 during THA. Specifically, in regards to THA, **they noted** direct injury with screw or retractor placement were the most common means of injury.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Aberrant retractor placement in the anterior-inferior quadrant typically leads to injury of the obturator nerve, artery, or vein
Answer 3: Aberrant retractor placement in the posterior-inferior quadrant would lead to potential injury of the sciatic nerve, inferior gluteal vessels/nerve, or internal pudendal vessels/nerve.
Answer 4: The posterior-superior quadrant is a relatively safe zone, but aberrant retractor placement can injure the superior gluteal nerve/vessels or the sciatic nerve.
Answer 5: Retractor placement directly inferior to the transverse acetabular ligament would cause potential injury to the obturator vessels/nerve.
