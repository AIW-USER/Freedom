## Question

A 5-year-old patient presents to the orthopedic clinic with shoulder asymmetry and limited abduction. The patient's parents explain this deformity has been present since birth, and now the child is unable to reach overhead and participate in play. On examination, the left scapula is elevated with medial rotation of the inferior pole. Which of the following is the most common associated finding on further imaging?

## Options

1. Absent clavicle
2. Spina bifida
3. Cervical spine abnormalities
4. Horseshoe kidney
5. Proximal humeral deficiency

## Correct option

**3. Cervical spine abnormalities**

## Answer explanation

The patient has Sprengel's deformity which is commonly seen with Klippel-Feil syndrome (congenital cervical spine abnormalities).

Sprengel's deformity is the eponym for congenital elevation of the scapula due to a failure in proper caudal migration, often with restricted motion of the scapula due to an omovertebral bone or other spino-scapular linkage. It may be bilateral in 10-30% of cases, cause a broad, short, webbed neck as in Turner's syndrome (though asymmetric if unilateral), and in some cases be associated with Klippel-Feil syndrome.

*Grogan et al. report their classic case series in Toronto of 21 cases treated with the Woodward procedure (resection of omovertebral bone and inferior transfer of muscular scapular attachments).* **They report** a 3:1 female to male ratio in cases, and 38 degree improvement in shoulder abduction and average 2 cm lowering of the scapula after surgery improving both cosmesis and function in 80% of patients.

*Farsetti et al. more recently describe a long-term follow-up series of 22 patients with average 26-year follow-up.* This cohort was mixed operative patients and non-operative. **They conclude** that surgery does improve shoulder abduction, and improvement cosmetically in scapular position, but pain was present in almost all patients at long term follow-up in both groups.

*Kadavkolan et al. present a thorough review of the pathophysiology, classification, clinical findings, and surgical options for patients with Sprengel's deformity.* **They report** an equal prevalence in boys and girls but a higher rate of left vs right sided disease.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Absent clavicle is seen cleidocranial dysplasia, not Sprengel's
Answer 2: Spina bifida is not a common association with Sprengel's deformity like Klippel-Feil
Answer 4: Renal abnormalities are not as frequently associated as Klippel-Feil
Answer 5: Humerus defects are not typically seen in children with Sprengel's deformity
