## Question

A 23-year-old male with a history of osteochondritis dissecans of the right knee treated with fragment fixation and microfracture about 3 years ago. He is now presenting with recurrent knee pain and effusions preventing him from playing recreational basketball. Recent radiographs and MRI revealed a complete displacement of the previously treated OCD fragment. He is subsequently treated with osteochondral allograft implantation of the defect. Which of the following is true regarding this treatment?

## Options

1. The articular cartilage defect is gradually replaced with fibrocartilage
2. Graft healing occurs through osteoclastic tunneling (cutting cone)
3. Results in a reliably high rate of patient satisfaction
4. Graft failure rates exceed 30% at 5-years
5. Low BMI is associated with failure to return to sports

## Correct option

**3. Results in a reliably high rate of patient satisfaction**

## Answer explanation

Osteochondral allograft (OCA) implantation of an osteochondritis dissecans (OCD) results in a high rate of patient satisfaction.

OCD lesions are articular cartilage defects of the knee that often occur during adolescence. Initial treatment can often be successful with nonoperative treatment and non-weightbearing with or without casting. However, patients with persistent symptoms require microfracture with or without fragment fixation. Recurrent lesions can be treated with lesion grafting, with larger lesions best addressed with allograft to minimize donor site morbidity. Allograft constructs provide a biologic surface that is identical to articular cartilage and the graft is incorporated via creeping substitution. Mulitple studies have reported a high rate of patient satisfaction and high survivorship at 10-years.

*Sadr et al. presented a series of 135 patients with OCD of the knee treated with OCA of the defect.* **They reported** 8% of patients experienced allograft failures at final follow-up and an OCA survivorship of 93% at 10-years. **They concluded** OCA transplantation to be an effective treatment for OCD lesions of the knee.

*Cotter et al. retrospectively reported a series of 41 patients that were treated with OCA for OCD lesions of the knee.* **They reported** a significant improvement in all outcome measures post-operatively, but with 35.9% of patients requiring secondary surgery. **They concluded** OCA provides a clinically meaningful improvement in patient-reported outcome measures and high patient satisfaction.

Incorrect answers
Answer 1: Microfracture results in a fibrocartilage replacement for the cartilaginous defect.
Answer 2: Allograft incorporation occurs through creeping substitution.
Answer 4: Graft failure rates are reportedly low with graft survivorship >90% at 10-years.
Answer 5: Higher BMI is associated with a failure to return to sports.
