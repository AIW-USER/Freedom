## Question

A 31-year-old woman is referred to your office for further evaluation of a chronic left foot pain attributed to an "abnormal left leg tendon". Her physician forwards you a CD containing an axial MRI image seen in Figure A. Regarding the abnormal tendon, which of the following is true?

![[q5716-a-fig-a.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. It originates from the tibia and fibula and is eccentrically active during terminal stance.
2. It originates from the tibia and fibula and is concentrically active during terminal stance.
3. It originates from the fibula only and is concentrically active during initial swing.
4. It originates from the fibula only and is eccentrically active during initial swing.
5. It originates from the tibia only and is concentrically active during terminal stance.

## Correct option

**2. It originates from the tibia and fibula and is concentrically active during terminal stance.**

## Answer explanation

The MRI confirms a left tibialis posterior (TP) tendon rupture. The TP is concentrically active during terminal stance and pre-swing and originates from both the tibia and fibula (and interosseous membrane).

The TP is a dynamic stabilizer of the medial longitudinal arch of the foot and the most powerful foot inverter. It arises from the posterior tibia, fibula and interosseous membrane. It has multiple insertion. The main ANTERIOR portion inserts into the navicular tuberosity and plantar surface of the 1st cuneiform. The MIDDLE plantar portion inserts into the bases of the 2nd-4th metatarsals, the 2nd and 3rd cuneiforms and the cuboid and the flexor hallucis brevis muscle. Finally, the POSTERIOR recurrent portion inserts into the sustentaculum tali. Together these contribute to its specific functions.

*Kaye and Jahss reviewed the anatomy and biomechanics of the tibialis posterior in relation to medial arch support.* During gait, the TP inverts the hindfoot and locks Chopart's joint, allowing the gastrocsoleus (GS) to act through the metatarsal heads. The TP and GS are synergistic. In the absence of the TP, the GS pulls the talar head downwards, stretching the spring ligament and in turn collapsing the medial arch.

*Semple et al. reviewed the TP in health and disease.* TP EMG activity is biphasic and occurs during initial CONTACT and MIDSTANCE and PRESWING phases. In TP dysfunction/acquired flatfoot, there is greater TP activity during the second half of stance phase than controls (RIGHT shift/delayed activation).

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1, 3-5: The TP tendon is ruptured. It is concentrically active in the terminal stance and preswing phases of gait. Of the tendons in the deep posterior compartment, the FDL takes origin from the tibia only, and the FHL takes origin from the fibula only.
