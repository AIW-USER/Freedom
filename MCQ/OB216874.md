## Question

A 39-year-old male landscaper presents with a primary complaint of right knee pain. He states the pain is located over the medial aspect of his knee only. The pain is worse with activity and has progressively worsened over the last 9 months. Knee radiographs demonstrate medial compartment arthritis without additional compartmental involvement. Full length lower extremity radiographs demonstrate 9º of varus knee alignment, which correlates with his clinical examination. He has previously failed other modes of nonoperative management including therapy, corticosteroid injections, and bracing. You recommend surgical intervention with high tibial osteotomy. Which of the following is true of this procedure?

## Options

1. Higher BMI does not influence results
2. Contraindicated in patients >40 years of age
3. Post-operative patella alta is a common complication
4. Should overcorrect knee alignment into valgus alignment
5. Outcomes of medial opening wedge are superior to lateral closing wedge technique

## Correct option

**4. Should overcorrect knee alignment into valgus alignment**

## Answer explanation

Given the patient's age and status as a laborer, a valgus producing high tibial osteotomy (HTO) would be indicated. When performed, it is recommended that knee alignment be overcorrected into valgus alignment for best results.

Valgus producing HTO is a powerful technique that can be used in young, active patients with varus knee malalignment and medial compartment arthritis. Several different techniques can be employed, including medial opening wedge and lateral closing wedge osteotomies. Lateral closing wedge osteotomies have more inherent stability but may require more precision with regards to the degree of correction. Medial opening wedge osteotomies avoid the proximal tibiofibular joint and the peroneal nerve and use a wedge of bone graft to provide the appropriate degree of correction. Regardless of the chosen technique, it is recommended that overcorrection into 8-10º of valgus be performed at the time of surgery, as this has been shown to achieve the best results with the least degree of deformity recurrence. Common complications associated with HTO include recurrence, patella baja and malunion/nonunion of the osteotomy site.

*Amendola et al. reviewed the indications and techniques surrounding knee osteotomies and meniscal transplantation.* *They discuss meniscal transplants being appropriate in patients with normal alignment and no arthritis and go on discuss technique.* *They also talk about specifics of knee osteotomies and how to plan and evaluate for the location and degree of correction necessary.*

*Sprenger et al. published their results using tibial osteotomies in cases of varus knee arthritis.* *They performed a lateral closing wedge osteotomy in 76 patients,* **noting** a 10-year survival rate of 90% when a correction to 8-16º of valgus alignment was achieved. **They concluded** that valgus producing HTO has a role as an alternative to TKA in younger patients with malalignment.

*Rossi et al. reviewed the role of HTO as a treatment strategy in the varus knee.* *They discuss specific indications for HTO as well as reinforcing the fact that both coronal and sagittal plane malalignment have to be appreciated and corrected during these procedures.* *They finish by mentioning different techniques that are employed when performing an HTO and the fact that they can be performed alone or in combination with other procedures (ACL, meniscal transplants, cartilage resurfacing).*

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: BMI >35 is generally thought to be a contraindication to performing an HTO, as it may increase the risk for deformity recurrence and failure.
Answer 2: While there is no specific age cutoff for performing an HTO, young, active individuals <50 years old are typically the most well qualified surgical candidates.
Answer 3: Post-operatively, patella baja is well-known complication, as the patellar tendon can be shortened during the procedure for a number of reasons.
Answer 5: Both medial opening and lateral closing wedge osteotomy techniques are appropriate to perform an HTO, both with advantages and disadvantages.
