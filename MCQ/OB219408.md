## Question

A 19-year-old female presents to the clinic for continued management of vague left knee pain following a soccer injury, which has been recalcitrant to physical therapy and platelet rich plasma injections. She is subsequently sent for an MRI, which demonstrates no ligamentous injury. However, a one by one-centimeter full-thickness cartilaginous defect of the lateral condyle is seen. The patient subsequently undergoes arthroscopic debridement with microfracture. Which mediator is directly responsible for the mechanism of chondrocyte death following her injury?

## Options

1. Caspases
2. Advanced glycosylation end products
3. Matrix metalloproteinases
4. Tumor necrosis factor-alpha
5. Interleukin-6-beta

## Correct option

**1. Caspases**

## Answer explanation

This 19-year-old sustained a soccer injury that resulted in a chondral injury. Chondrocytes degrade through the apoptotic pathway, mediated through caspase activation (Answer 1).

Cartilaginous defects are commonly encountered, stemming from traumatic events in some capacity, arising either from a singular event or repeated insult over time. When articular cartilage is subjected to supraphysiologic loads, chondrocyte death results. The manner in which this occurs has been subject to debate, with the literature detailing both apoptotic and necrotic pathways, which have been thought to be related to the degree of strain imparted onto the cartilage. Nonetheless, apoptosis has been regarded as the predominant pathway.

Apoptosis, depending on the stimuli, occurs through intrinsic (mitochondrial-mediated) and extrinsic (death-receptor-mediated) pathways, both of which result in caspase activation (mainly caspase-3) and ultimately cellular death. In contrast, the necrotic pathway results when an injurious stimulus compromises the cellular integrity, which in turn elicits the release of reactive oxygen species from the mitochondria, causing further fragmentation and release of cellular contents, which provokes an inflammatory reaction from the surrounding cellular space.

*D'Lima et al. ascertained 64 full-thickness cartilage samples from bovine medial femoral condyles.* The samples were then loaded at varying loads (low [7 MPa], moderate [14 MPa], high [23 MPa]) after 48 hours for 500 milliseconds to assess apoptosis rates and any load-related response. Increased rates of apoptosis were observed in loaded cartilage, which were most significant at medium and high loads. Moreover, the degree of apoptosis was found to increase substantially after six hours from the initial measurement. **The authors conclude** a window may be present for future pharmacologic intervention following cartilaginous injury to prevent cellular apoptosis.

*McKinley et al. examined the current literature detailing the pathophysiology of intra-articular fractures and the onset of post-traumatic osteoarthritis.* **The authors noted** that supraphysiologic loads onto articular surfaces with associated fractures led to significantly higher loads of chondrocyte death compared to those without. While acknowledging the prominent role of apoptosis in chondrocyte death, **the authors also note** the role of cellular necrosis following articular injury.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Advanced glycosylation products are not implicated in chondrocyte death but rather compromise the integrity of chondrocytes through the cross-linking of collagen molecules.
Answers 3, 4, and 5: While these molecules are present in abundance throughout chondrocyte death, caspases are directly responsible for cellular senescence via the apoptotic pathway.
