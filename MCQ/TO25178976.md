Chapter: Regional Orthopedics MCQs — Hallux Valgus Bunion

## Question

Which is not a risk factor for hallux valgus

## Options

1. metatarsus primus varus
2. rounded (convex) first metatarsal head
3. first metatarsal-cuneiform joint hypermobility
4. Pes cavus

## Correct option

**4. Pes cavus**

## Answer explanation

Causes of hallux valgus:
Intrinsic
Factors: Genetic
predisposition is the strongest risk factor — positive family history is
present in 60–70% of patients, with an autosomal dominant inheritance pattern
with incomplete penetrance demonstrated in several studies. Specific anatomical
variants that predispose to hallux valgus include a rounded (convex) first
metatarsal head (as opposed to a flat or chevron-shaped articular surface,
which resists lateral subluxation), metatarsus primus varus (increased
first-second intermetatarsal angle reflecting medial angulation of the first
metatarsal — this is considered the primary skeletal driver in many cases), first
metatarsal-cuneiform joint hypermobility (excessive mobility at the
Lisfranc joint allows the first metatarsal to drift medially under load), pes
planus (flat foot with hindfoot valgus and forefoot abduction increases the
valgus vector at the first MTP joint), and ligamentous laxity
(generalised hypermobility). Long first metatarsal (Egyptian foot type) may
also predispose by increasing the lever arm for deforming forces.
Extrinsic
Factors:
Constrictive, narrow-toed, high-heeled footwear is the most significant
environmental factor — it compresses the forefoot, forcing the hallux into
valgus and the first metatarsal into varus. The high incidence in women is
largely attributable to footwear patterns. However, hallux valgus occurs in
barefoot populations (though less commonly), confirming that footwear is a
contributory rather than sole causative factor.
Systemic
Conditions:
Rheumatoid arthritis (synovitis of the first MTP joint destroys the medial
capsule and collateral ligaments), neuromuscular disorders (cerebral palsy,
poliomyelitis — muscle imbalance), and connective tissue disorders
(Ehlers-Danlos, Marfan syndrome — ligamentous laxity) predispose to hallux
valgus.

Answer: 4
