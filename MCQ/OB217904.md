## Question

A 67-year-old female presents to your office with end-stage tricompartmental osteoarthritis of her right knee. She has been treated conservatively by her primary care physician and is no longer getting relief. Therefore, you schedule her for total knee arthroplasty (TKA). During tibial tray preparation, you place a sharp homan retractor posterolateral to the posterior cruciate ligament (PCL). What structure is most likely to be injured during this step?

## Options

1. Biceps femoris muscle
2. Lateral meniscus root
3. Popliteal artery
4. Semimembranosus
5. Peroneal nerve

## Correct option

**3. Popliteal artery**

## Answer explanation

The popliteal artery is at risk for injury with aberrant retractor placement posterolateral to the tibial PCL insertion.

The neurovascular anatomy surrounding the knee is very important to understand for any surgeon performing total knee arthroplasty (TKA). The popliteal artery and vein lie in a bundle with the tibial nerve just posterolateral to the tibial PCL insertion and just posterior the posterior root of the lateral meniscus. The neurovascular bundle is at risk for injury either with direct laceration or blunt damage, such as with aberrant retractor placement. Placing these retractors with the knee in flexion can help to displace the bundle posteriorly and mitigate risk for injury, however, extreme care must be maintained in order to prevent catastrophic damage.

*Rand et al. published an early case report detailing 3 instances of vascular injury during TKA over the course of the previous 15 years.* This was equivalent to a vascular injury rate of ~0.03%. **They noted** that pre-existing arteriosclerosis and flexion contracture increased the odds of this complication occurring.

*Ninomiya et al. reviewed popliteal artery injury in TKA,* **noting** that these complications are severe, sometimes resulting in the need for vascular intervention or even amputation. They performed MRI studies to evaluate the relationship of the popliteal artery to different positions relative to the knee joint line, **noting** that the popliteal artery was at risk with retractor placement posterolateral to the PCL or when a retractor was placed deeper than 1cm into the tissues. They finish by recommending that the knee be in a position of flexion when placing these retractors, rather than hyperextension.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The biceps femoris muscle inserts on the posterior fibular head and crosses the knee posterolateral to the lateral femoral condyle.
Answer 2: The lateral meniscus root rests just anterior and lateral to the tibial PCL insertion and is not in danger with retractor placement over the posterior edge of the tibia.
Answer 4: The semimembranosus has a series of attachments to the proximal posteromedial tibia and would not be damaged with retractor placement posterolateral to the PCL.
Answer 5: The peroneal nerve crosses the knee lateral to the lateral femoral condyle and is not in danger with posterior retractor placement. The tibial nerve, however, runs adjacent to the popliteal artery/vein.
