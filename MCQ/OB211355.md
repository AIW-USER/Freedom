## Question

During a C1-C2 posterior spinal fusion, while performing the dissection of posterior arch of C1 and pedicle of C2, the surgeon encounters rapid pulsatile arterial bleeding. The artery is tamponaded off,vascular surgery is consulted and an intra-operative angiogram is performed. Given adequate collateral flow, the injured artery is embolized. Post-operatively the patient's neurologic function is normal. Injury to this arterial structure could have best been avoided by minimizing dissection in which of the following areas?

## Options

1. Dorsolaterally along the C1-C2 facet, medial to the foramen transversarium
2. Ventral along the medial border of the the pars of C2
3. Cephalad to the posterior arch of C1 when > 1.5cm lateral to the midline
4. Caudal to the posterior arch of C1 when < 1.0cm lateral to the midline
5. Cephalad to the posterior arch of C1 when < 1.0cm lateral to the midline

## Correct option

**3. Cephalad to the posterior arch of C1 when > 1.5cm lateral to the midline**

## Answer explanation

The clinical presentation is consistent with an intraoperative injury to the vertebral artery in the upper posterior cervical spine. Avoiding dissection more than 1.5 cm lateral to the midline and dissecting subperiosteally on the inferior aspect of the posterior arch of C1 can help avoid injury to the verterbral artery.

The vertebral artery most commonly courses cranially through the foramen transversarium of C2 and C1, after which it courses medially along the superior aspect of the C1 arch until turning cranially into the foramen magnum. While anatomic variations are common, dissection greater than 1.5cm lateral to the midline and along the the posterior arch of C1 puts the vertebral artery at the greatest risk of injury. This risk can be mitigated by preoperative imaging evaluating for any anatomic variation combined with technical consideration for the course of the artery during exposure and instrumentation.

*Hu et al. performed an anatomic study using fine-cut CT scans to determine the optimal entry point and trajectory for C1 lateral mass screws in order to minimize risk to the vertebral artery and surrounding structures.* **The authors reported** a safe starting height of 4.3mm, 27.6mm lateral to the mid-sagittal axis, and angled 20-degrees medially.

*Molinari et al. reviewed the literature on vertebral artery injury during posterior cervical exposure.* **The authors noted** the high incidence of vertebral artery anomalies in the upper cervical spine and the coincidence of vertebral artery injuries with aberrant anatomy in the C1-C2 interval.

*Patkar et al. evaluated C1 lateral mass and C2 pedicle screw placement in 49 cases of posterior C1-C2 fusion.* **The authors noted** that an entry point for the C2 pedicle screw located 3mm below the midpoint of the C1-C2 joint and directed caudally and medially into the body of C2 best avoided injury to the vertebral artery.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1 and 2: Dissection dorsolaterally along the pars of C2 and C1-C2 facet risks injury to the C2 nerve root, which is encountered medial and prior to the vertebral artery traveling between the C2 and C1 foramen transversarium. Some surgeons advocate for routine sacrifice of the C2 nerve root during C1 screw placement.
Answer 4: Dissection ventrally past the posterior arch of C1 risks injury to the dura and spinal cord.
Answer 5: The tectorial membrane is an anterior structure and cranial continuation of the posterior longitudinal ligament. Reaching this structure from a posterior approach would require dissection past the spinal cord.
