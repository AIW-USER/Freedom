## Question

Which of the following is a change noted within the intervertebral disc with increasing age?

## Options

1. Decreased keratin-sulfate to chondroitin sulfate ratio
2. Decreased lactate
3. Increase in pH
4. Increase in water content
5. Decrease in proteoglycans

## Correct option

**5. Decrease in proteoglycans**

## Answer explanation

Aging leads to an overall decrease in the overall amount of proteoglycans within the intervertebral (IV) disc.

The intervertebral disk consists of the annulus fibrosus, nucleus pulposus, and endplate. The gross appearance of the nucleus pulposus is a clear watery gelatinous matrix in the very young disk, but with aging the nucleus pulposus becomes more opaque, less hydrated, and firm. The cellular composition of the young disk consists of many notochordal cells (embryonal remnants), but after 10 years of age notochordal cells are not seen in the disk. Aging is associated with an overall increase in lactate, degradative enzyme activity, and an increase in keratin to chondroitin sulfate ratio within the IV disk. Aging is also associated with an overall decrease in water content, proteoglycans, pH, and the overall number of viable cells within the IV disc.

*Andersson et al. performed a review of IV disc degeneration.* **The authors note** that the IV disc is a highly organized matrix, laid down by relatively few cells in a very specific manner. **They note** that in early life, some vascular supply exists within the endplates and annulus; however, these vessels recede soon after birth. With increasing age, **they noted** that matrix and cell components both change, water is lost
from the matrix, and the proteoglycan content is diminished. They also note that increased cell death occurs, along with the formation of cell clusters leading to change in the cartilaginous endplates.

*Roberts et al. performed a review of the IV disc anatomy and age-related changes.* **They noted** that at birth, the human disc has some vascular supply within both the cartilage endplates and the annulus fibrosis, but these vessels soon recede, leaving the disc with little direct blood supply in the healthy adult. They cite that with increasing age, water is lost from the matrix, and the proteoglycan content also changes and diminishes. Furthermore, with aging, more blood vessels begin to grow into the disc from the outer areas of the annulus accompanying an increase in cell proliferation.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Aging leads to an overall INCREASE in keratin-sulfate to chondroitin sulfate ratio within the IV disc
Answer 2: Aging leads to an overall INCREASE in lactate within the IV disc
Answer 3: Aging leads to an overall DECREASE in pH within the IV disc
Answer 4: Aging leads to an overall DECREASE in water content within the IV disc
