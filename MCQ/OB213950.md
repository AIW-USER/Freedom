## Question

## Options

1. The cause of failure is manifested by the eccentric position of the femoral head in the acetabulum at 2 years
2. The use of smaller femoral head components increases the risk of her complication
3. Her complication only occurs in patients with metal-on-metal total hip arthroplasty
4. The complication she is experiencing is likely from micromotion and/or electrochemical transfer of metals
5. Aspiration with automated cell counts are helpful in confirming the diagnosis

## Correct option

**4. The complication she is experiencing is likely from micromotion and/or electrochemical transfer of metals**

## Answer explanation

This patient with a history of a metal-on-polyethylene (MoP) total hip arthroplasty (THA) has suffered a dissociation at the head-neck junction, most likely due to trunnionosis. Of the above statements, the most accurate is the statement regarding the pathophysiology of trunnionosis, which occurs from micromotion and/or electrochemical transfer of metals in the presence of dissimilar metals.

While modularity in THA allows surgeons to optimize leg length and offset, the extra articulation may result in metal-related reactions known as trunnionosis, which is a relatively rare phenomenon. Initially, only metal-on-metal (MoM) bearings were reported to be at risk for these complications, however, it is now recognized that it may occur with any articulation, usually as a result of problems at the head/neck junction from micromotion. The magnitude of micromotion (and consequently trunnionosis) is mainly influenced by taper design and material (higher likelihood of corrosion when taper is smaller and made up of a more flexible alloy), the assembly condition (the force of impaction should be in line with the taper during assembly), and head size (corrosion is most significant with larger head sizes).

*Raju et al. evaluated specific implant combinations with non-traumatic trunnion failure, with two cases presenting as acute complete dissociation of the femoral head from the neck.* **They reported** that the most likely risk factors for failure were large femoral component size, high offset, a low angled neck, and a titanium alloy taper with a cobalt-chromium head. **They recommend** that surgeons scrutinize radiographs for any alignment alteration between the femoral head and neck in follow-up radiographs.

*Morlock et al. investigated the timeline, role of taper dimensions and material in gross stem-taper failure in THA.* **They reported** that material loss at the head taper increased with time, and average linear material loss above 76 μm at the head taper correlated highly with bottoming out and failure. **They concluded** that taper design with 36-mm head diameters bears a higher risk for corrosion than larger tapers made from stiffer materials like cobalt-chromium.

Figure A demonstrates dissociation of the THA at the head-neck junction. Figures B and C demonstrate normal-appearing postoperative THA radiographs. Illustration A demonstrates trunnionosis, with the cobalt-chromium femoral head and the tapered portion of the femoral prosthesis, which are both significantly corroded.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The radiographs at 2 years do not show signs of eccentric wear of the femoral head, which would result in osteolysis.
Answer 2: The use of larger femoral head components (not smaller heads) increases the risk of trunnionosis.
Answer 3: While MoM bearings are often reported in trunnionosis cases, it can also occur with MoP articulations as well.
Answer 5: Aspiration with manual (not automated) cell counts are helpful in confirming the diagnosis as the metal debris may cause a false elevation in cell count.
