## Question

You are reviewing anterior shoulder instability cases with a group of residents and explaining the complexities of the pathoanatomy involved with these injuries. You explain that treatment decisions are based on the combination of injuries noted to the labrum, glenoid, and humeral head. In which of the following patients would an isolated open bankart repair be most appropriate?

## Options

1. Anterior labral tear with 20% glenoid bone loss in college rugby player
2. Anterior labral tear with 5% glenoid bone loss and off-track Hill-sachs lesion in retired basketball player
3. Recurrent anterior labral tear after prior arthroscopic labral repair with 6% glenoid bone loss and on-track Hill-sachs lesion in college soccer player
4. Recurrent anterior labral tear after prior arthroscopic labral repair with 10% bone loss and a large, engaging Hill-sachs lesion measuring 45% of the humeral head in professional football player
5. Recurrent anterior labral tear after prior arthroscopic labral repair with 18% bone loss and on-track Hill-Sachs lesion in college wrestler

## Correct option

**3. Recurrent anterior labral tear after prior arthroscopic labral repair with 6% glenoid bone loss and on-track Hill-sachs lesion in college soccer player**

## Answer explanation

Open bankart repair and capsular shift would be most appropriate in the setting of a failed prior arthroscopic labral repair without significant bone loss in a contact athlete (Answer 3).

Anterior shoulder instability is a complex topic with many different treatment options available. First, the surgeon must understand the pathoanatomy of the shoulder and how these relationships affect the overall stability of the shoulder. Injury to the anteroinferior labrum is most common given the mechanism of shoulder subluxation/dislocation. Hill-Sachs lesions of the humeral head and anterior glenoid bone loss may also be seen. These three lesions should all be taken into account when making treatment decisions, in combination with patient factors like age and plans for return to contact/overhead throwing activities. Historically, open bankart procedures were believed to result in lower shoulder dislocation recurrence rates compared to arthroscopic repairs, however, improvements in arthroscopic techniques and implants have negated the difference in outcomes. Therefore, in patients who have minimal/no anterior bone loss and on-track lesions, isolated bankart repair is appropriate, with no differences in outcomes between those performed arthroscopically vs. open. With large degrees of anterior bone loss, bone block procedures should be considered (latarjet, distal tibial allograft, etc). With large Hill-Sachs and/or off-track lesions, a remplissage should be considered. Open bankart repair and capsular shift is a great treatment option for patients with recurrent instability and minimal glenoid bone loss who are planning to return to contact sports.

*Harris et al. looked at long-term outcomes of bankart stabilization in a systematic review.* *They did not identify significant differences in the rate of recurrent instability between arthroscopic (11%) and open (8%) repair.* *They also were not able to show any differences in the rate of arthritis development with 11-year follow-up.*

*Chalmers et al. performed a meta-analysis looking at whether arthroscopic and open stabilization procedures produced equal results for anterior shoulder instability.* **They noted** no significant differences in the failure rates of arthroscopic vs. open shoulder stabilization. Therefore, surgeons should perform the procedure they are most comfortable with achieving the best result in their hands.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: This patient would be indicated for a bony-augmentation procedure such as a latarjet given the high degree of bone loss and nature of contact sport.
Answer 2: This patient would be indicated for labral repair +/- remplissage given the off-track nature of the injury and low degree of glenoid bone loss.
Answer 4: This patient would be indicated for labral repair and bone grafting of the humeral head Hill-Sachs defect.
Answer 5: This patient who is a college contact athlete would likely benefit most from an open, bony procedure to correct the critical anterior glenoid bone loss.
