## Options

2. Figure B

4. Figure D
5. Figure E

## Correct option

**3. Figure C**

## Answer explanation

Figure C is representative of giant cell tumor (GCT) of bone. Denosumab is a monoclonal antibody for RANKL and is FDA approved for adjuvant treatment of GCT to allow joint salvage when curretage alone may otherwise require arthroplasty.

Giant cell tumor of bone is commonly treated with local curettage and bone grafting. Denosumab can be used in select cases as an adjuvant to surgical resection to potentially allow joint salvage when tumor destruction of subchondral bone may otherwise require joint replacement. Denosumab is a monoclonal antibody to RANKL, and can decrease the osteolysis seen in certain pathologic processes, and has been used to treat osteolytic lesions from metastatic disease. Giant cells are known to express RANKL (receptor activator of nuclear factor kB ligand) and are responsible for the aggressive osteolytic nature of the tumor.

*Lewin et al. present a review of GCT and its treatment including medical treatment with denosumab.* *They describe that patients with advanced or unresectable GCT have shown objective changes in tumor composition, reduced bony destruction, and clinical benefit.* **They also note** denosumab use for treatment of metastatic disease, and cite studies showing denosumab has improved benefits over bisphosphonates for the same indication.

Figure A is a histology slide characteristic of eosinophilic granuloma. Figure B is a histology slide characteristic of a small round blue cell tumor. This particular slide was from a biopsy of ewings sarcoma. Figure C is a histology slide characteristic of GCT. Figure D is a histology slide characteristic of osteosarcoma. Figure E is a histology slide charactersitic of pigmented villonodular synovitis (PVNS).

Incorrect answers:
Answers 1, 2, 4, 5: While denosumab may be used for treatment of metastatic disease from many different tumors, it is not indicated for primary treatment of any of these neoplasms.
