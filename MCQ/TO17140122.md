Chapter: Regional Orthopedics MCQs — Subacromial Impingement

## Question

35 years old male with massive rotator cuff tear presents with pseudoparalysis. MRI findings of his shoulder includes acromiohumeral humeral distance <6mm with minimal glenohumeral arthritis, massive tear of posterosuperior rotator cuff muscles and goutallier stage 3 fatty atrophy of supraspinatus muscle. Which is the least preferred treatment option for him?

## Options

1. Reverse shoulder arthroplasty
2. Latissismus dorsi transfer
3. Lower trapezius transfer
4. Superior capsular reconstruction

## Correct option

**1. Reverse shoulder arthroplasty**

## Answer explanation

ANSWER-A

Explanation: Massive and Irreparable rotator cuff tear: RCTs are defined as irreparable when the lesions cannot be repaired primarily to their insertion on the tuberosities despite conventional techniques of surgical release/mobilization, because of their size, retraction, and muscle impairment caused by atrophy and fatty infiltration. Characteristics of an irreparable cuff are:

- Cofield et al. described a massive tear as greater than 5 cm, while others consider a massive rotator cuff tear to be one involving two or more tendons.
- Posterosuperior tears involve the supraspinatus and infraspinatus and are more common than anterior superior tears, which involve the supraspinatus and subscapularis tendons.
- Goutallier stage III and IV fatty atrophy of cuff muscles
- Acromio-humeral distance less than 6 mm
- Chronic pseudoparalysis (see above question)
- Positive tangent sign (see question 30 for explanation)

In young patients with massive irreparable rotator cuff tears without significant arthritis surgical options are tendon transfer and superior capsular reconstruction (see Q. 3). Reverse shoulder arthroplasty is done in old patients with rotator cuff arthropathy.

Tendon transfer procedures are options for younger and active patients with massive irreparable posterosuperior or anterosuperior rotator cuff tears without significant glenohumeral arthritis in the setting of an intact subscapularis tendon.

Latissimus dorsi transfers are indicated for patients with retracted posterosuperior tears with grade-3 or higher fatty degeneration of the infraspinatus and supraspinatus musculature and are contraindicated for those with insufficient subscapularis function and/or poor subscapularis muscle quality and obvious deltoid muscle atrophy.

A more anatomical transfer for irreparable posterosuperior RCTs is a lower trapezius transfer, and early results are promising.

The pectoralis major transfer is the most common tendon transfer performed for anterior-superior insufficiencies to restore the anterior force couple due to supraspinatus and subscapularis insufficiency.
