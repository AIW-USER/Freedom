## Answer explanation

The clinical presentation of muscle weakness and difficulty standing is consistent with Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD). The inheritance pattern of DMD is X-linked recessive, which is depicted in Figure D (Answer 3).

DMD is caused by a mutation in the dystrophin gene (on Xp21.2), resulting in an absent dystrophin protein. X-linked recessive inheritance is a mode of inheritance in which a mutation in a gene on the X chromosome causes the phenotype to be expressed either in males who are heterozygous for the gene mutation (because they have only one X chromosome) and in females who are homozygous for the gene mutation (gene mutation on each of their two X chromosomes). Typically, DMD presents itself between 2-6 years old and patients have delayed motor milestones and develop calf pseudohypertrophy, equinovarus foot deformity, and scoliosis.

*Shieh et al. reviewed muscular dystrophies and genetic myopathies.* **They reported** that DMD is the most common muscular dystrophy. **They highlighted** that the dystrophin protein links the actin cytoskeleton to membrane-bound proteins, protecting the muscle from strain-related damage during exertion. **They recommended** steroids (improves muscle strength), ACE inhibitors (slow cardiomyopathy), and noninvasive ventilator support (improves life expectancy) for treatment.

Figure A depicts a scoliotic deformity in the setting of DMD. Figures B-F depict an autosomal dominant (AD), autosomal recessive (AR), X-linked recessive (XR), X-linked dominant (XD), and mitochondrial disease inheritance patterns, respectively.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: DMD is not transmitted by an AD inheritance pattern.
Answer 2: DMD is not transmitted by an AR inheritance pattern.
Answer 4: DMD is not transmitted by an XD inheritance pattern.
Answer 5: DMD is not transmitted by mitochondrial inheritance pattern.
