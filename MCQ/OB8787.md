## Question

A 62-year-old man with distant history of lung cancer presents with moderate left hip pain and a limp for the last 3 months. He is now dependent on walking aids and is housebound. ## Options

1. Radiation therapy
2. Cementoplasty
3. Cephalomedullary nail
4. Cemented calcar-replacing hemiarthroplasty
5. Uncemented proximal femoral replacement hemiarthroplasty

## Correct option

**3. Cephalomedullary nail**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a Mirel's score of 2 (moderate pain) + 3 (lytic) + 3 (>2/3 diameter) + 3 (peritrochanteric) = 11. Surgical fixation is indicated for lesions with Mirel's score ≥9. For a lesion in the subtrochanteric region without involvement of the neck/head, a cephalomedullary nail is preferred.

The treatment of a proximal femoral lytic lesion is as follows: Lesions in the head and neck may be best treated with hemiarthroplasty or THA (if there is acetabular involvement or preexisting arthritis). Intertrochanteric lesions may be treated either with a calcar replacing hemiarthroplasty / THA or cephallomedullary nail. Subtrochanteric lesions may be treated with cephalomedulary nail nail ± PMMA or proximal femoral replacement. For arthroplasty, a cemented femoral stem is preferred to uncemented stems

*Mirels retrospectively reviewed 38 patients with 78 metastatic lesions of long bones.* Following irradiation, 35% of lesions fractured and the remaining 65% did not. The nonfracture group had scores from 4 to 9 and the fracture group had scores from 7 to 12. **He found** that risk of fracture was 33% at 6 months for a score of 9 (Illustration B). **He proposed** that lesions with scores of ≥ 9 require prophylactic internal fixation prior to irradiation, and lesions ≤7 can be treated nonoperatively.

*Scolaro and Lackman reviewed the management of metastatic long bone fractures.* *They advise (1) a multi-disciplinary approach, (2) consideration of abnormal (or absent) healing when selecting treatment, (3) obtain a rigid mechanical construct which may involve internal fixation with PMMA cement or resection and joint reconstruction, and (4) the latter is is more reliable when insufficient bone is present for fixation.*

Figures A and B show a lytic subtrochanteric lesion occupying >2/3 the femoral cortical diameter. Figures C and D show adenocarcinoma of the lung with gland formation and mucin secretion (on bright pink with mucicarmine stain and PAS after diastase). Illustration A shows the Mirel's classification. Illustration B shows the probability of fracture at different Mirel's scores. Illustration C shows a calcar-replacing hemiarthroplasty. Illustration D shows a proximal femoral replacement hemiarthroplasty.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: With a high Mirel's score, surgery is indicated.
Answer 2: Cementoplasty may relieve pain but will not confer structural integrity to the lesion, and will not protect the entire bone.
Answers 4,5: Prosthetic replacement is a consideration if the lesion is in the intertrochanteric region proximal to the lesser trochanter and/or involves the femoral head/neck. A cemented stem is necessary to confer early stability and allow immediate weightbearing.
