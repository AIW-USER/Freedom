## Question

A 2-year-old child presents with multiple fragility fractures sustained from minimal trauma, severe bowing deformities of the long bones, and dentinogenesis imperfecta. Genetic testing reveals a heterozygous mutation in the COL1A1 gene. Which of the following molecular mechanisms best explains the severe phenotype seen in this patient compared to the classic, mild form (Type I) of Osteogenesis Imperfecta?

## Options

1. A missense mutation causing the substitution of a bulkier amino acid for glycine within the triple-helical domain, exerting a dominant-negative effect on collagen assembly.
2. A large chromosomal microdeletion encompassing the entire COL1A1 locus, leading to complete absence of pro-alpha1(I) chains and unopposed pro-alpha2(I) trimerization.
3. A homozygous loss-of-function mutation in the CRTAP or LEPRE1 genes, resulting in a complete failure of 3-hydroxylation of proline residues.
4. A nonsense mutation resulting in a premature stop codon, leading to haploinsufficiency and a 50% reduction in the total amount of structurally normal type I collagen.

## Correct option

**1. A missense mutation causing the substitution of a bulkier amino acid for glycine within the triple-helical domain, exerting a dominant-negative effect on collagen assembly.**

## Answer explanation

Severe forms of OI (Types II, III, and IV) are typically caused by 'qualitative' defects. A missense mutation replacing the crucial, small glycine residue (which normally occupies the restricted space in the center of the triple helix) with a larger amino acid disrupts the winding of the entire triple helix. Because mutant chains intertwine with normal chains, this exerts a dominant-negative effect, resulting in severely structurally abnormal collagen and a severe clinical phenotype.

Answer: 1
