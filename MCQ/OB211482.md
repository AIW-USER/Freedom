## Question

A 78-year-old man presents with bilateral knee pain for the last year, recalciltrant to anti-inflammatory medications, physical therapy, and corticosteroid injections. His radiograph is depicted in Figure A. Which of the following changes is most accurate in the pathophysiology of his diagnosis?

![[q211482-a-screen-shot-2020-08-17-at-3-54-23-pm.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Increased glycoaminoglycans
2. Decreased cartilage permeability
3. Decreased IL-6 production
4. Increased chondrocyte production of aggrecanases
5. Decreased movement of water into cartilage

## Correct option

**4. Increased chondrocyte production of aggrecanases**

## Answer explanation

This patient's diagnosis is bilateral knee osteoarthritis. Of the above, the only accurate statement is that there is increased chondrocyte production of aggrecanases in osteoarthritis.

In osteoarthritis, the changes observed in articular cartilage include increased water content (as a result of the disruption in architecture of the matrix molecules and increased permeability of water into cartilage), decreased quantity of proteoglycans (i.e. glycoaminoglycans), decreased quantity of collagen with decreases in cross-linking and stiffness associated with degradation, increased thickness of subchondral bone, increased chondrocyte production of matrix metalloproteinases and aggrecanases, and increased chondrocyte activity and proliferation of chondrocytes.

*Appleton et al. reviewed the biology of osteoarthritis.* **They reported** that inflammatory processes in synovium, chondrocytes, and synovial fluid have long been recognized in osteoarthritis, though it is not an autoimmune process. **They highlighted** that not all inflammation is bad and fine-tuning a balance within inflammatory signaling mechanisms may be a path to regain joint homeostasis in osteoarthritis.

Figure A reveals bilateral knee osteoarthritis with varus mechanical alignment.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Decreased (not increased) production of proteoglycans like glycoaminogylcans is typical in osteoarthritis.
Answer 2 and 5: Increased (not decreased) cartilage permeability and water movement into cartilage is characteristic of osteoarthritis.
Answer 3: Osteoarthritis is associated with an increase in cytokines, including IL-6.
