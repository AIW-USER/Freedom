## Question

A 25-year-old male who initially complained of new vision changes and difficulty hearing is diagnosed with osteopetrosis. You explain to him that his risk for fracture is increased given his abnormal bony resorption. In an attempt to explain the pathophysiology of his disease, you describe the process of bone metabolism. Which of the following is true regarding normal bone remodeling?

## Options

1. PTH directly stimulates osteoclasts to increase bone resorption
2. Osteoprotegrin signals increased bone turnover
3. Tartrate resistant acid phosphate released by osteocytes increases acidity to degrade bone matrix
4. Howship's lacunae are formed at the site of bone resorption
5. Initial lamellar bone is reorganized into woven bone

## Correct option

**4. Howship's lacunae are formed at the site of bone resorption**

## Answer explanation

Howship's lacunae are pits formed at the site of osteoclastic bone resorption where the ruffled border of the osteoclast interacts with the bone.

Bone remodeling is a complex process, involving multiple signaling pathways. In simple terms, osteoblasts are stimulated by PTH and Vitamin D3 and produce RANK-L, which binds to the RANK receptor on osteoclasts and stimulates the bone resorption process. After activation, the osteoclast attaches to the bone surface via integrins and contains a ruffled border. Howship's lacunae are formed at the junction of the bone and ruffled border and this area is sealed off and acidified to degrade the bone matrix, followed by digestion via proteolytic enzymes secreted from osteoclasts. After Howship's lacunae are formed and this resorption process is complete, osteoclasts are replaced by osteoblasts that form new bone matrix.

*Matsuo et al. describe the interaction between osteoclasts and osteoblasts.* **They note** communication between these cell types occurs at multiple stages during bone remodeling, including initiation, transition from resorption to formation, and termination of remodeling. They specifically make note of how the transition from resorption to formation is mediated by osteoclast factors which activate osteoblasts within the resorbed Howship's lacunae to stimulate new bone formation in this area.

*Martin et al. explore the concept of osteoclast-osteoblast coupling and their interaction in bone remodeling.* **They propose** the concept of a bone multicellular unit (BMU) that describes the bone resorption process as osteoblasts initiate the process by signaling osteoclastic resorption. They finish by explaining that **they believe** osteoclasts resorb bone, but also produce cytokines that activate bone formation in each BMU.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: PTH binds to the PTH receptors on osteoblasts, activating adenylyl cyclase and increasing production of RANK-L by osteoblasts, which eventually bind to RANK receptors on osteoclast and increase resorption.
Answer 2: Osteoprotegrin sequesters RANK-L, preventing it from binding to osteoclasts. Therefore, osteoprotegrin downregulates osteoclast function.
Answer 3: Tartrate resistant acid phosphatase is secreted by osteoclasts, not osteocytes, and functions to lower the pH in order to resorb bone.
Answer 5: Woven bone is initially formed by osteoblasts and is then remodeled to lamellar bone as a response to stresses through the bone.
