## Answer explanation

This patient has a SI fracture-dislocation (crescent fracture). The most common injury pattern resulting in this injury is a lateral compression (LC) type II fracture pattern.

SI dislocations and crescent fractures include a spectrum of injuries involving the pelvis which can lead to instability and pelvic malalignment. Diagnosis requires pelvic radiographs with CT imaging. An SI fracture-dislocation (crescent fracture) is an iliac wing fracture that enters the SI joint. The posterior ilium remains attached to sacrum by the posterior SI ligaments. The presence of a crescent fracture is consistent with a lateral compression (LC) type II injury. Treatment is generally operative with reduction and fixation of both the anterior and posterior pelvic ring as needed.

*Burgess et al. review the mechanisms of pelvic ring injuries.* **They report** the greatest need for blood replacement in APC injuries compared to LC and vertical shear (VS) injuries, 14.8 vs 3.6 vs 9.2 units, respectively. **They concluded** that the overall mortality was greatest in the APC injuries.

*Koo et al. review the interobserver reliability of the Young-Burgess and Tile classification systems for fractures of the pelvic ring.* **They report** that CT scans can improve the reliability of assessment of pelvic stability because of its ability to identify anatomical features of injury. **They concluded** that the Young-Burgess system may be optimal for the learning surgeon, while the Tile classification system is more beneficial for specialists in pelvic and acetabular surgery.

Figure A depicts a LC type I injury. Figure B depicts a LC type II injury. Figure C depicts an APC I injury. Figure D depicts an APC type II injury. Figure E depicts a vertical shear injury. Illustration A depicts the Young-Burgess fracture classification of pelvic ring injuries. Illustration B is an axial CT image demonstrating a crescent fracture, which is an iliac wing fracture that enters the SI joint.
