## Question

A 75-year-old female wishes to proceed with total hip arthroplasty (THA) for osteoarthritis. Her past medical history is significant for hypertension, hypothyroidism, and lumbar degenerative disease. She underwent uncomplicated L5-S1 posterior lumbar fusion 5 years ago. If seen on pelvic radiographs when moving from standing to sitting, which of the following parameter changes would increase her risk for postoperative THA dislocation the most?

## Options

1. Decreased lumbar lordosis
2. Decreased pelvic tilt
3. Decreased sacral slope
4. Increased acetabular anteversion
5. Increased pelvic incidence

## Correct option

**2. Decreased pelvic tilt**

## Answer explanation

A lack of change in pelvic tilt between standing and seated position is indicative of fixed spinopelvic alignment, which is common after lumbar spinal fusion or in degenerative spine conditions and may increase the risk of prosthetic hip dislocation.

Prosthetic hip dislocation is multifactorial but spinopelvic parameters are becoming a more important consideration in patients with prior lumbar spinal fusion or lumbar degenerative disease undergoing total hip arthroplasty (THA). Comparing spinopelvic parameters between standing and sitting pelvic radiographs preoperatively may be useful for intraoperative component positioning to decrease the risk of dislocation.

*Meneghini et al. review lumbar spine disease and history of lumbar spinal fusion in the setting of prosthetic hip instability.* **They found** that lumbar abnormalities may affect the spinopelvic relationship and increase the risk for THA instability by creating what he refers to as, “dynamic,” acetabular component malposition. **They conclude** that if causes for recurrent THA instability are addressed systematically, most patients can be treated successfully.

*Esposito et al. reviewed a group of THA patients who had spine-to-ankle seated and standing radiographs to determine whether spinopelvic parameters played a role in THA dislocation.* **They found** that patients who dislocated had less spine flexion, less change in pelvic tilt, more hip flexion in the seated position, lower seated acetabular inclination and anteversion. **They concluded** that patients with a smaller change in pelvic tilt from standing to sitting, including those with prior lumbar fusion, must compensate with increased hip flexion, placing them at higher risk for primary THA dislocation.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: In a flexible spine it is normal for there to be a decrease in lumbar lordosis when sitting as the pelvis rolls back and increases pelvic tilt.
Answer 3: Decrease in the sacral slope of the pelvis from standing to the seated position is normal and would not increase the risk for dislocation. Less change in sacral slope would be noted in the setting of lumbar fusion.
Answer 4: Acetabular anteversion should increase normally as the pelvis tilts posteriorly. With fixed spinopelvic alignment this motion is lacking. Increased AA would not increase the risk for dislocation.
Answer 5: Pelvic incidence is thought to be a fixed pelvic parameter and should not change significantly, even in the setting of lumbar fusion. Therefore, risk for THA dislocation should be unaffected.
