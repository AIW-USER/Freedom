Chapter: Orthopedics Oncology MCQs — Chordoma

## Question

Most appropriate treatment option for chordoma of sacrum

## Options

1. Wide resection alone
2. Wide resection + EBRT
3. Wide resection and CT
4. Curettage and bishopphonate therapy

## Correct option

**1. Wide resection alone**

## Answer explanation

Ans: A

Explanation:

CHORDOMA

Chordoma is a malignant, slow growing and locally aggressive tumor with predilection for sacrum, that arises from remnants of the notochord and accounts for just 1–4% of all primary bone tumors. Still it is the most common primary malignant tumor found in sacrum.

Males are involved more than females. Its typically seen in 5th-6th decades of life. The insidious onset and trivial symptoms caused by sacral chordoma may account for the late presentation. Symptoms are often vague but a frequent referral presenting to the orthopaedic surgeon is lower back pain worse when sitting.

Pain and radiculopathy due to compression of the sciatic nerve or iliolumbar trunk can be there in up to one third of these patients. A few cases with tumor presenting in the S1- S2 region can have unilateral weakness or total loss of L5-S1 function. Up to one third of patients can even have symptoms of urinary tract infections and 10% can present with constipation or cauda equina symptoms.

On plain film sacral chordoma appears as a large lytic lesion which crosses the midline > 50% of cases. Lesions may occur anywhere from the first to fifth sacral vertebrae with predilection to the 2nd and 3rd sacral bodies. The destruction of bone extends to the sacro-iliac joints and progresses to the ilium as well as posteriorly which may cause skin changes in the lower back.

Histologically the classic chordoma mass consists of chords of tumor cells and lobules with a mucoid matrix and extensive fibrous tissue in between. Physaliferous cells are pathognomic of chordoma and are characterized by their large size with a vesicular nucleus and vacuolated cytoplasm. High grade tumors on histology tend to carry poor prognosis. Several authors have also corelated volume of tumor (> 10 cms/ 200 cm3) with bad prognosis.

On immunohistochemistry Chordomas are positive for epithelial membrane antigen (EMA), pan-cytokeratin (panCK).

Treatment:

· Wide resection is the mainstay although excision is not without issues as Proximal sacral nerves roots often go into resection.

The tumor is slow growing so by and large it’s chemoresistant and radio resistant. However, these have a role in recurrences that are common as complete excision in most cases is not achieved.

Rather than Conventional Radiotherapy, Stereotactic Radiotherapy is employed in Recurrent cases, small lesions or where due to some factor surgical excision cannot be pursued.
