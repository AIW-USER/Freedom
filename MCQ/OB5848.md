## Question

A 56-year-old male presents with right arm pain and weakness with elbow extension that has been present for the past 2 weeks. He reports that he must sleep with his right arm above his head to remain comfortable. A positive Spurling's test is evident on physical examination. Which of the following is true regarding this patient's condition?

## Options

1. The patient's neurologic symptoms warrant surgical intervention at this time
2. The natural course of this condition is continued stepwise progression of his neurologic symptoms
3. Electromyography and nerve conduction studies are the next best step in work-up
4. The cervical level affected in this patient is C7/T1
5. This patient's pain will most likely improve with a course of nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) and physical therapy (PT)

## Correct option

**5. This patient's pain will most likely improve with a course of nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) and physical therapy (PT)**

## Answer explanation

The above patient presents with cervical radiculopathy. The only true statement about this condition is that this patient's symptoms are most likely to improve with NSAIDs and physical therapy.

Cervical radiculopathy is a degenerative cervical condition where there is compression of the nerve root leading to ipsilateral neck and arm pain. This may also be associated with muscular weakness or dermatomal numbness that corresponds to the nerve being compressed. The natural history of this condition is favorable, with only 25% of patients who will have persistent pain and worsening of symptoms. Due to the majority of patients having improvement of symptoms, initial management should be conservative. Physical therapy, NSAIDs, steroid injections, short course of immobilization, and cervical traction have all been shown to help ameliorate symptoms.

*Rhee et al. performed a review that discussed the pathoanatomy, clinical evaluation, radiographic evaluation, nonsurgical and surgical treatment, and outcomes of cervical radiculopathy.* **They concluded** that an initial trial of non-operative management is the most appropriate approach for patients; however, if there is progressive dysfunction or lack improvement, surgical treatment provides excellent and predictable outcomes.

*Dreyer et al. reviewed non-operative treatment options for neck and arm pain.* *The authors discussed sources of neck pain including bony, muscular, ligamentous, and neurologic and how to determine which source is primarily affecting the patient.* Treatment options discussed were rest, physical therapy, medications (oral steroids, NSAIDs, muscle relaxants, etc.), improving patient posture and ergonomics, and steroid injections. **They noted** that it is important to recognize and treat concomitant clinical depression that patients may have in order to improve outcomes.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Surgical management is indicated if the patient fails non-operative management or if he has signs of myelopathy.
Answer 2: A stepwise progression of neurologic symptoms describes cervical myelopathy.
Answer 3: EMG and nerve conduction tests are not the next step in the workup. The next imaging modality would be MRI.
Answer 4: Given the patient's triceps weakness, a disc herniation at the C6/7 level is the most likely pathology.
