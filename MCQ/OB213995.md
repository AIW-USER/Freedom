## Question

A 62-year-old patient presents with a brown line on the left ring finger that has increased in size over the last year. He denies any pain associated with this lesion and denies any antecedent trauma to the finger. Figure A is the clinical picture of the lesion. A biopsy is performed out of concern for what diagnosis?

![[q213995-a-screen-shot-2020-03-01-at-6-40-59-pm.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Dermatofibrosarcoma
2. Melanoma
3. Bacterial endocarditis
4. Glomus tumor
5. Epithelioid sarcoma

## Correct option

**2. Melanoma**

## Answer explanation

The patient is presenting with a longitudinal melanonychia, which can be a manifestation of subungual melanoma. Biopsy should be performed to rule out this diagnosis.

Longitudinal melanonychia is often a benign condition characterized by a linear brown streak in the nail bed. However, this can represent a malignant subungual melanoma, which is often diagnosed in very late stages due to an initial benign appearance. Lesions with bands >6 mm wide, the involvement of a single nail, bands that are wider proximally, color heterogeneity, blurry lateral borders and extension of pigment into the adjacent cutaneous fold are suggestive of malignant melanoma. A biopsy should be performed in cases where there is a concern for malignancy. In confirmed cases, amputation of the digit, either through the interphalangeal joint or the carpometacarpal joint, is the preferred treatment.

*Baran and Kechijian reviewed the diagnosis and treatment of longitudinal melanonychia.* **The authors emphasized** the importance of biopsy as subungual melanoma is often in the differential with clinical examination unable to determine the cause of longitudinal melanonychia. The authors stressed the importance of pathological diagnosis following various biopsy techniques.

*Mannava et al. reviewed the evaluation and treatment of longitudinal melanonychia.* *The authors described a higher rate of metastatic disease with melanoma presenting as longitudinal melanonychia due to a prolonged delay to diagnosis.* **They recommended** performing a punch biopsy for distal nail lesions whereas more proximal lesions are better addressed with matrix shave biopsy, lateral longitudinal excision, midline excision, or en bloc excision of all nail tissues.

Figure A is a clinical photograph of a left ring finger with longitudinal melanonychia.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: A dermatofibrosarcoma presents as a nodular lesion that is sometimes pigmented. It typically occurs on the trunk, neck and proximal extremities and does not occur as a subungual mass.
Answer 3: Bacterial endocarditis can lead to emboli in the nail bed resulting in splinter hemorrhages. If there is a concern for this diagnosis an echocardiogram, rather than a biopsy, should be performed.
Answer 4: A glomus tumor is a tumor that results in severe pain and cold intolerance. It can appear as a blue-colored mass. There is no malignant potential with this tumor.
Answer 5: An epithelioid sarcoma presents as a large mass with a necrotic center and does not cause discoloration of the nail bed.
