## Question

A minor league third baseman presents after sustaining an injury to his non-dominant hand while batting at practice. The pain is located ulnarly and is made worse with resisted flexion of the small and ring fingers. You order radiographs and a CT scan, as shown in Figure A. Which of the following is true of this CT finding?

![[q218900-a-hook-of-hamate.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Clenched fist view is the best method to evaluate radiographically
2. This is a normal anatomic variant
3. It may cause compression of a nerve that results in weakness in abductor pollicis brevis
4. Nonsurgical management results in high union rates
5. Excision of the fragment results in excellent return to play in high level athletes

## Correct option

**5. Excision of the fragment results in excellent return to play in high level athletes**

## Answer explanation

Surgical excision of the hook of the hamate is the best treatment options for high-level athletes as it results in the fastest return to play.

Hook of the hamate fractures are common injuries in baseball players and golfers. Clinically the patient will have ulnar-sided wrist pain with the hook of the hamate pull test, or DIP flexion of the small and ring finger in ulnar deviation. The diagnosis can be confirmed with a carpal tunnel view radiograph or CT scan. Non-operative management is an excellent option in less active patients, although the nonunion rate can be approximately 50%. Many of these nonunions are asymptomatic. The best surgical option for high-level athletes is a hook of the hamate excision, as it allows the fastest return to play.

*Erickson et al. performed a retrospective review looking at the return to play in professional baseball players after excision for treatment of hook of the hamate fractures.* **They found** in 261 players, 84% were able to return to play with almost all of them returning to the same or higher level of activity. On average this took approximately 48 days.

*Bansal et al. retrospectively identified 81 athletes that underwent fragment excision for hook of the hamate fractures.* **They found** that 78 of the 81 patients returned to sports at a median time of 6 weeks. **They noted** a 25% complication rate, including transient ulnar nerve dysfunction. **They conclude** that excision is still a safe treatment that allows for a rapid return to play.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Clenched fist x-rays are useful in evaluating SL ligament tears, but a carpal tunnel view is the most useful for hook of the hamate fractures.
Answer 2: Bipartite hamates are described but they would have smooth cortical surfaces.
Answer 3: Hook of the hamate fractures may compress the ulnar nerve in Guyon’s canal, but the abductor pollicis brevis is innervated by the motor branch of the median nerve.
Answer 4: Nonsurgical management is associated with a nonunion rate of about 50%.
