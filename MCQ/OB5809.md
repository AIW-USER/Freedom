## Answer explanation

This patient presents with endocarditis and new neurologic symptoms including pain and long-tract signs (clonus) concerning for thoracic spinal epidural abscess, which is demonstrated in Figure B.

When spinal infection is suggested by clinical presentation, physical exam, and laboratory findings, the most appropriate next step involves MRI with and without gadolinium contrast. MRI has high sensitivity, and the addition of gadolinium increases the specificity of the test. Gadolinium-enhanced MRI of a spinal epidural abscess characteristically demonstrates a ring-enhancing lesion with high intensity on T2.

*Schoenfeld et. al. reviewed the mortality, complications, and hospital charges associated with treatment of spinal epidural abscesses using the Nationwide Inpatient Sample from 2006 to 2011.* Among their cohort, 52% of local abscesses were found in the lumbosacral spine, 15% in the thoracic spine, and 9% in the cervical spine. **They reported** a 3% in-hospital mortality rate and a 26% rate of complications. **The authors found** that predictors of mortality included advanced age, insurance status, liver disease, paralysis, and renal failure.

*Shah et al. retrospectively reviewed patients from 1993 to 2016 admitted to their hospital with a diagnosis of spinal epidural abscess.* **They found** that 27% (99) of the 367 patients who initially underwent nonoperative management ultimately failed medical treatment and required surgical intervention. **They identified** the following variables as independent predictors of failure: a presenting motor deficit, pathologic or compression fracture in affected levels, active malignancy, diabetes mellitus, sensory changes, and dorsal location of the abscess.

Figures and Illustrations:
Figure B is a sagittal, gadolinium-enhanced T1-weighted MRI demonstrating a ring-enhancing lesion within the thoracic spinal canal. It is situated dorsally and causing thecal sac compression. This is strongly suggestive of a spinal epidural abscess.
Illustration A is another MRI series demonstrating a thoracic epidural abscess. The T2 image (left) demonstrates destruction of the disc space at T4-5 with an adjacent epidural abscess spanning the T4 and 5 vertebral bodies.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Figure A is a T2-weighted sagittal MRI that demonstrates replacement of normal bone marrow within T8 and T9 with a mass spanning the disc space, which is consistent with multiple myeloma.
Answer 3: Figure C is a T2-weighted sagittal MRI that demonstrates a thoracic disc herniation at the T6-7 disc space with canal stenosis and cord compression.
Answer 4: Figure D is a T2-weighted MRI that demonstrates an osteoporotic compression fracture. The vertebral body has lost height appears to have anterior wedging, and the lack of bone edema suggests this fracture is chronic.
Answer 5: Figure E is a T2-weighted sagittal MRI demonstrating a syrinx at the cervicothoracic junction. The fluid signal within the spinal cord parenchyma is the syrinx cyst containing cerebrospinal fluid.
