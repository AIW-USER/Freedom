Chapter: Trauma MCQs — Polytrauma Patients

## Question

In a multiply injured polytrauma patient lethal triad following bleeding include

## Options

1. Hypotension, acidosis, coagulopathy
2. Hypothermia, acidosis, coagulopathy
3. Hypotension, hypothermia, acidosis
4. Hypothermia, hypotension, coagulopathy

## Correct option

**2. Hypothermia, acidosis, coagulopathy**

## Answer explanation

The lethal triad of hypothermia, acidosis, and coagulopathy has been well-accepted as the sequela of severe haemorrhage in a polytrauma patient. The lethal triad of conditions leads to each other and eventually becomes metabolically synergistic to the point of no return. Ineffective oxygen delivery, whether due to hypotension or, more commonly, hemorrhage, leads to anaerobic respiration. Anaerobic respiration produces a biochemical state of acidosis that alters the environment for normal enzymatic processes, resulting in dysfunctional platelet aggregation and clotting factors. Further blood loss worsens the acidosis. Inability to effectively perfuse tissues leads to hypothermia and worsens the environment for enzymatic processes needed for proper coagulation, resulting in further bleeding. In an effort to support an exsanguinating patient while attempting to control hemorrhage, early blood product administration protocols have been shown to decrease utilization and to improve morbidity and mortality. Massive transfusion protocols stipulate blood product resuscitation in a 1:1:1 product ratio (fresh frozen plasma:platelets:packed red blood cells). This is rooted in the rationale that replacing coagulation factors along with volume effectively promotes coagulation and oxygen delivery

Answer: 2
