## Question

Which of the following prognostic variables of Legg-Calve-Perthes disease has been most associated with the development of degenerative arthritis of the hip in the fifth or sixth decade of life?

## Options

1. Asphericity of the femoral head
2. Bone age <6 years at presentation
3. Calcification lateral to the epiphysis
4. Low BMI
5. Male gender

## Correct option

**1. Asphericity of the femoral head**

## Answer explanation

Deformity of the femoral head with aspherical congruity at skeletal maturity typically leads to degenerative arthritis secondary to deteriorating hip function after the age of 40 years. Though calcification lateral to the epiphysis is a poor prognostic sign, its prognostic significance has not been studied as extensively as femoral head sphericity and congruence.

Legg-Calve-Perthes (LCP) is an idiopathic avascular necrosis of the proximal femoral epiphysis in children. Prognostic variables for good long term outcomes include young age (bone age) < 6 years at presentation, group A lateral pillar classification, and spherical congruency of the femoral head at skeletal maturity (Stulberg classification). Variables of poor prognosis include female gender, adduction contracture, obesity, longer duration of presentation, stiffness, calcifications lateral to the epiphysis, lateral subluxation of the femoral head, a horizontal proximal femoral epiphysis, presence of a Gage sign, and the presence of a metaphyseal cyst.

*Stulberg et al. reviewed patients with LCP and followed them for an average of 30-40 years.* *They defined five classes of deformity based on its radiographic appearance at maturity, with each class having a characteristic pattern of involvement during the active stages of the disease.* The clinical and radiographic course of an involved hip was related to the type of congruency that existed between the femoral head and acetabulum. **They concluded** that three types of congruency were recognized: (1) spherical congruency (Class-I and II hips) - in hips in this category arthritis does not develop; (2) aspherical congruency (Class-III and IV hips) - mild to moderate arthritis develops in late adulthood in these hips; and (3) aspherical incongruency (Class-V hips) - severe arthritis develops before the age of fifty years in these hips.

*McAndrew et al. reviewed 35 patients, with an average follow-up of 47.7 years, who were previously diagnosed with LCP to define the long-term impairment in hips.* **They reported** statistically significant correlations between clinical outcome measures and Catterall head-at-risk signs, femoral head-size ratio, and age at onset of the disease. **They concluded** that several of the diseased hips were treated with hip arthroplasty secondary to pain and dysfunction, with the majority of these being performed in the fifth or sixth decade of life.

Illustration A is the lateral pillar (Herring) classification. Illustration B is a Gage sign, which is a v-shaped radiolucency in the lateral portion of the epiphysis and/or adjacent metaphysis. Illustration C is the Stulberg classification.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Young age (bone age) <6 years at presentation is the most important good prognostic indicator of LCP.
Answers 3: Calcifications lateral to the epiphysis is a poor prognostic variables of LCP; however, deformity of the femoral head with aspherical congruity at skeletal maturity has been most associated with the development of degenerative arthritis secondary to deteriorating hip function after the age 40 years.
Answers 4&5: Female gender and obesity are poor prognostic variables of LCP.
