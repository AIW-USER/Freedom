## Question

A patient undergoes C4-5 cervical disk arthroplasty for radiculopathy secondary to degenerative cervical disc disease. There were no complications noted intraoperatively, and the patient awoke in the PACU uneventfully without any issues. They were discharged the following day. They present to clinic 7 days postoperatively for their first post-operative appointment. Which of the following clinical presentations would reassurance and observation alone be the most appropriate?

## Options

1. Pain and numbness in the superior shoulder and lateral arm bilaterally that began 5 days after surgery, and bilateral deltoid and biceps weakness to grade 3/5 noted on exam
2. Subjective fatigue noted over the last 24hrs, temperature of 38.6 degrees Celsius and heart rate of 91 recorded in the clinic, and minor superficial wound dehiscence noted on exam
3. New onset of gait instability since a fall one day prior, three episodes of bowel and bladder incontinence that occurred since last night, and bilateral hyperreflexia
4. Progressive dysphagia and pain while speaking over the last day, and two episodes of vomiting earlier in this morning
5. Subjective but progressive dyspnea first noted during the walk from the parking lot to the clinic this morning, pulse oximetry reading of 91%, and a heart rate of 105

## Correct option

**1. Pain and numbness in the superior shoulder and lateral arm bilaterally that began 5 days after surgery, and bilateral deltoid and biceps weakness to grade 3/5 noted on exam**

## Answer explanation

Of the clinical presentations listed above, observation and reassurance would be most appropriate for a patient presenting with signs and symptoms of a postoperative C5 palsy (Answer 1).

Transient segmental motor palsy of the upper limb is a well-recognized complication after cervical spinal decompression, and of the nerve roots contributing to the brachial plexus, the C5 nerve root is most frequently involved. C5 palsy has been reported to occur after both anterior and posterior cervical decompression procedures, but most studies have found it more common in posterior procedures. The etiology of C5 palsy remains poorly understood. Although a number of hypotheses have been suggested, the results remain inconclusive. The time at which the nerve damage occurs remains largely unknown, but most motor deficits become apparent around 2-7 days postoperatively. Time to recovery varies based on severity, but in about 80% of cases, the patient can expect to have full or partial recovery over a period of 6 months to 1 year. When occurring in the acute post-operative period and there is no concern for malpositioned or failed hardware, observation and reassurance is most appropriate. If there is any concern regarding hardware position, X-rays and/or advanced imaging may be obtained.

*Bydon et al. provide a retrospective review of patients that underwent C4-5 operations for degenerative conditions in order to determine the incidence and perioperative characteristics/predictors of C5 palsy after anterior vs posterior operations.* **They concluded** that in anterior operations, an increasing number of corpectomy levels had a higher incidence of C5 palsy; however, older age was the strongest predictor of C5 palsy. In posterior operations, C4-5 foraminotomy carried the strongest correlation.

*Laratta et al. provide a systematic review on the safety and efficacy of single- and multilevel cervical disc arthroplasty as well as hybrid constructs.* **They note** that multiple high-quality studies have shown that single-level CDA can offer equivalent clinical outcomes with a reduction in secondary procedures and total cost when compared to ACDF. However, more recently there has been an increasing prevalence of 2-level CDA and hybrid surgery. **They conclude** that although the data regarding these multilevel procedures is less robust, it appears that they may be as effective as their single-level counterparts.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: This presentation is concerning for early post-operative infection and observation alone would not be appropriate.
Answer 3: New onset of myelopathy after a fall would be highly concerning for acute cord compression. The patient should be immobilized and taken/transferred to the emergency department for workup with advanced imaging and possible acute decompression +/- revision to a fusion (i.e. ACDF).
Answer 4: This presentation would be concerning for subacute esophageal perforation. This condition can be rapidly fatal if not identified and addressed timely. The patient should be taken/transferred to the emergency department and evaluated by an otolaryngologist.
Answer 5: This presentation is concerning for pulmonary embolism. This patient should be taken/transferred immediately to the emergency department for management (imaging/d-dimer levels and treatment accordingly).
