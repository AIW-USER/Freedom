## Question

False regarding the nerve involved in Tardy ulnar nerve palsy

## Options

1. Supplies Abductor pollicis brevis
2. Treatment includes anterior nerve transposition with or without deformity correction
3. Palsy may occur several years after injury
4. May occur due to cubitus valgus secondary to Lateral condyle fracture

## Correct option

**1. Supplies Abductor pollicis brevis**

## Answer explanation

Ans: A

EXPLANATION: -

Ulnar nerve supplies Adductor pollicis. Abductor pollicis brevis is a thenar muscle supplied by Median nerve.

TARDY ULNAR NERVE PALSY

Tardy ulnar nerve palsy (first mentioned by Mouchet in 1899) is a chronic clinical condition characterized by a delayed onset ulnar neuropathy. Typically, it occurs as a consequence of nonunion of pediatric lateral condyle fractures at the elbow, which eventually lead to a cubitus valgus deformity generating chronic stretch; however, case reports have also been published after non-union of medial epicondyle humerus fractures and after varus malunion of supracondylar humerus fractures. Rarer aetiologies may include elbow arthritis, cysts around elbow, congenital anomalies, adhesions, recurrent dislocation of the elbow, olecranon, radial head and Monteggia fractures. In uncommon aetiologies the cause is mostly traction by abnormal bony spurs while in its classical form, the palsy develops secondary to cubitus valgus generally over a few months (although up to 25 years-time has been reported) due to abnormal tension with repeated trauma during movement that leads to tension neuritis and intraneural fibrosis. The cause of abnormal tension could be multifactorial: V-shaped kinking of the ulnar nerve, compression of the nerve by a fibrous band over flexor carpi ulnaris and incongruity of elbow joint due osteoarthritic changes.
Clinical assessment should include obtaining a complete history, physical examination, nerve conduction tests, and elbow imaging studies. One must remember, even minimal valgus deformity could still manifest as tardy ulnar nerve palsy.
Management: Most patients presenting with tardy ulnar nerve palsy are not candidates for conservative treatment because of the mechanical characteristics of the pathology. Treatment si mostly surgical and consists of ulnar nerve decompression, with or without corrective osteotomy. Anterior ulnar transposition alone shows very good results, so most surgeons attempt correction of bone deformity or fixation of the non-union condyle only if there is a complaint at the elbow raised by the patients.
