## Question

A patient has come with history of dorsal wrist pain since 3 months. On MRI there is sclerosis seen in Lunate and X ray shows a negative ulnar variance. Management option ideal for the case would be [FNB 2022]

## Options

1. VBG and radial shortening
2. STT fusion
3. Proximal Row Carpectomy
4. VBG with radial wedge osteotomy

## Correct option

**1. VBG and radial shortening**

## Answer explanation

Ans: A

Patient is having Kienbock’s disease Stage 2 with negative ulnar variance. VBG and radial shortening will be best answer.

KIENBOCK’s DISEASE

Kienbock’s disease is avascular necrosis involving the Lunate. Although the lunate typically has both palmar and dorsal blood supplies, 25% cases, lunate may have a singular palmar blood supply, increasing the risk for osteonecrosis. Although repetitive trauma and faulty loading that alters Lunate biomechanics have been suggested as risk factors in Kienbock’s disease, the most predominant risk factor remains to be “negative ulnar variance”.

A classic patient with Kienbock’s is a young man (20-40 years old male) who is usually a manual labourer, and who comes with progressive unilateral wrist pain. Precisely, pain is generalized dorsal wrist pain that is more with grip loading, and there could be decreased ROM. Most patients report an insidious onset with progressive symptoms without a traumatic event.

Kienbock’s disease starts with osteonecrosis changes in the lunate (earliest picked up by MRI, IOC) including sclerosis and signal changes. Further degeneration results in lunate collapse, fracture, scaphoid flexion, loss of carpal height parameters, and carpal arthritis involving the radiolunate, capitolunate, and possibly radio scaphoid joints.

Treatment options for Kienbock’s disease fall into four main categories:

- core decompression (distal radius)
- joint levelling/ lunate unloading procedures (e.g. radial shortening)
- lunate revascularization (e.g. distal radius based or pronator quadratus based vascularized bone grafting)
- salvage procedures including carpectomies and partial wrist fusions
