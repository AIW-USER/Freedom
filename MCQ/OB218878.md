## Question

A 74-year-old female presents to the emergency department after feeling a pop in her thigh when stepping down from a low curb, and presents with the fracture shown in Figure A. She endorses being treated for osteoporosis over the last five years but denies any other significant past medical history. Intraoperatively, traction is applied. What additional manipulation should be applied through the distal fragment to aid in fracture reduction?

![[q218878-a-figure-a-atypical-subtroch.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Extension and internal rotation
2. Extension and external rotation
3. Flexion and internal rotation
4. Flexion and external rotation
5. None; traction alone always ensures anatomic reduction

## Correct option

**4. Flexion and external rotation**

## Answer explanation

This patient presents with a bisphosphonate-associated atypical femur fracture located in the subtrochanteric region. The deforming forces of the iliopsoas and gluteus medius on the proximal fragment cause flexion and external rotation, respectively, and the distal fragment may be manipulated into similar positioning to facilitate the closed reduction before nailing.

Subtrochanteric femur fractures (<5cm from lesser trochanter) are notoriously difficult to fix due to the significant deforming forces acting on the hip where there are numerous muscular attachments. The proximal fragment is acted on by the gluteus medius and minimus muscles (abduction), the iliopsoas musculature (flexion), and the short external rotators (external rotation). The adductors act on the distal fragment (adduction and shortening). Placing the patient’s distal extremity into the position of the proximal segment helps assist in the reduction of the fracture for intramedullary nailing. For this reason, some authors advocate for lateral surgical positioning as the distal fragment can be more easily manipulated than when the patient is placed on a traction table.

Bedi and Toan provided a comprehensive review regarding subtrochanteric femur fractures. They discuss the various implants available and techniques to assist in the fixation of these difficult fractures. They place emphasis on the preservation of blood supply and soft tissue integrity to prevent nonunion. The authors conclude that surgeons must have an understanding of the deforming forces as work and choose an implant that they are most comfortable using.

*Afsari and colleagues reviewed their surgical technique in treating high subtrochanteric femur fractures with the aforementioned deformity.* *They describe using a small lateral incision with the introduction of large-toothed bone forceps around the proximal fragment in order to assist with the fracture reduction.* **The authors reported** that 86% of their patients treated with this technique had an anatomic reduction, while the other 14% were within 5° of anatomic reduction.

Figure A shows a right-sided, bisphosphonate-associated subtrochanteric femur fracture.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1, 2, 3: These answer choices do not correctly characterize the deforming forces involved in subtrochanteric femur fractures.
Answer 5: Traction alone would not result in appropriate closed reduction due to the aforementioned deforming forces.
