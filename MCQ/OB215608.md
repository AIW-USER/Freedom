## Question

An 18-year-old male presents to your office for evaluation of ligamentous laxity with recurrent dislocations of his shoulders. His past medical history is significant for a superior lens dislocation and a spontaneous pneumothorax. On examination it is found that his arm span is greater than his height and he has ligamentous hyperlaxity of multiple joints. Genetic testing reveals that he has elevated levels of transforming growth factor beta. With this patient's underlying diagnosis, these elevated levels have been associated with what additional finding?

## Options

1. Aortic dilatation
2. Decreased bone growth
3. Insulin dysregulation
4. Impaired osteoblastic differentiation
5. Impaired proliferation of chondrocytes

## Correct option

**1. Aortic dilatation**

## Answer explanation

This patient has an underlying diagnosis of Marfan Syndrome. Elevated levels of transforming growth factor beta (TGF-ß) has been associated with aortic dilatation.

Marfan Syndrome is a congenital connective tissue disorder caused by a mutation the fibrillin-1 (FBN1) gene that presents with long narrow limbs, skeletal abnormalities, cardiovascular abnormalities, and ocular abnormalities. Fibrillin gene mutation prevents TGF-ß binding, resulting in elevated TGF-ß levels in tissue. These elevated levels of TGF-ß are associated with aortic dilatation.

*Haller et al. reviewed Marfan syndrome and how early diagnosis may prevent life-threatening complications of aortic aneurysm.* **They report** that a pathogenic FBN1 mutation may lead to an earlier diagnosis of Marfan syndrome. **They concluded** routine genetic screening of all patients with adolescent idiopathic scoliosis for mutations in clinically actionable aortic aneurysm disease genes is not recommended on the basis of the high frequency of variants of unknown significance. Yet, clinical evaluation and family history should heighten indications for genetic referral and testing.

*Liang et al. reviewed patients with Marfan syndrome scoliosis (MSS) to analyze whether there are differences of posterior correction surgery in MSS and adolescent idiopathic scoliosis (AIS).* **They report** that the MSS group had a longer operation duration and more estimated blood loss compared with those of group AIS, while there was no significant difference in terms of fusion level, transfusion, coronal Cobb angle of the major curve at final follow-up, and the correction rate. **They concluded** that when performing posterior correction for scoliosis, the surgeons should be aware that the patients with Marfan syndrome scoliosis had more estimated blood loss and longer operation duration than AIS patients, while the correction rate was similar.
