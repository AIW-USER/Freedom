## Question

A 65-year-old male is seen in clinic with atraumatic right hip pain. He underwent a right total hip arthroplasty eight years prior at an outside institution. The operative report indicates that a metal-on-polyethylene implant was used. Laboratory evaluation reveals a white blood cell count, C-reactive protein level, and erythrocyte sedimentation rate all within normal limits. Serum cobalt and chromium levels are elevated. After additional workup, you suspect that the patient’s symptoms are secondary to an adverse local tissue reaction. Histologic evaluation of the affected tissue would most likely show which of the following?

## Options

1. Predominance of perivascular lymphocytes
2. Predominance of perivascular neutrophils
3. Lipid-laden histiocytes and occasional multinucleated giant cells
4. Inflammatory tissue with numerous neutrophils
5. Mononuclear stromal cells infiltrating the synovium

## Correct option

**1. Predominance of perivascular lymphocytes**

## Answer explanation

Head-neck junction corrosion after metal-on-polyethylene (MoP) total hip arthroplasty (THA) can lead to adverse local tissue reactions (ALTRs). Histology of ALTRs demonstrates a predominance of perivascular lymphocytes with synovial hypertrophy, pseudocapsule formation, and tissue necrosis.

The patient presents with atraumatic hip pain eight years after THA. The laboratory data obtained does not concern infection. In the setting of atraumatic pain after MoP THA, elevated serum metal ion levels can indicate the presence of an ALTR, prompting further workup and correlation with plain radiographs and advanced imaging. An ALTR after MoPTHA occurs secondary to mechanically assisted crevice corrosion at the head-neck junction. In confirmed cases, histology will demonstrate a predominance of perivascular lymphocytes with synovial hypertrophy, pseudocapsule formation, and tissue necrosis.

*Fitz et al. reviewed the literature regarding head-neck taper corrosion in MoP THA and the treatment of ALTRs.* **They noted** the similarities between the bioreactivity of metal corrosion particles released from the head-neck junction in MoP THAs and metal-on-metal THAs causing a hypersensitivity reaction that can lead to aseptic lymphocyte-predominant vasculitis-associated lesions, pseudocapsule formation, and synovial tissue hypertrophy. Identified risk factors associated with implant choice included larger femoral heads (≥36 mm), mixed alloy components, and increased stem flexibility. Suggested components of the clinical evaluation included laboratory testing (inflammatory markers and metal ion levels), hip joint aspiration, radiography, and advanced imaging when indicated (either ultrasound and/or metal artifact reduction sequence MRI). **The authors concluded** that mechanically assisted crevice corrosion leading to ALTR after MoP THA can be influenced by implant, surgeon, and patient factors, and providers should have a low threshold to consider this as a potential cause of failure in postoperative MoP THA patients.

*Fillingham et al. analyzed 44 patients with ALTRs after MoP THA to identify optimal cutoff values in serum cobalt and chromium levels to aid in diagnosis.* **They found** that a serum cobalt level of ≥ 1.0 ng/mL had a sensitivity of 100%, specificity of 90%, a positive predictive value of 96%, and a negative predictive value of 100%. Twenty-one of the included subjects had specimens available for histopathologic evaluation and were considered consistent with ALTR when demonstrating dense infiltrates of perivascular lymphocytes and areas of tissue necrosis. **The authors concluded** that the measurement of serum cobalt is an optimal tool to aid in diagnosing ALTR secondary to head-neck junction corrosion after MoP THA.

Incorrect Responses:
Answer 2: Histology of ALTRs associated with MoP THA head-neck junction corrosion demonstrates a predominance of perivascular lymphocytes rather than neutrophils.
Answer 3: These are histologic findings of pigmented villonodular synovitis.
Answer 4: Inflammatory tissue with numerous neutrophils is more consistent with prosthetic joint infection.
Answer 5: This is also a finding of pigmented villonodular synovitis.
