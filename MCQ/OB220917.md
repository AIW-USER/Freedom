## Question

A 32-year-old male presents to your office with chronic left foot pain and arch collapse after a motor vehicle collision a decade ago. At that time, he was treated in a boot and remained non-weight-bearing for six weeks. He notes chronic left foot pain since that injury, which has slowly progressed with time. He shows you images of his injury from 10 years ago in the office today, as shown in Figures A and B. In general, what is the most common complication following these injuries?

## Options

1. Nonunion
2. Midfoot arthritis
3. Planovalgus foot deformity
4. Hypermobility of the 1st tarsometatarsal (TMT) joint
5. Associated numbness over the dorsum of the foot

## Correct option

**2. Midfoot arthritis**

## Answer explanation

This patient sustained a Lisfranc injury ten years ago, as shown in Figures A and B. The most common complication following this injury is post-traumatic midfoot arthritis (Answer 2).

A Lisfranc injury is a traumatic disruption between the medial cuneiform and base of the 2nd metatarsal. These can involve a ligamentous injury (without bony involvement) or fracture. This is caused by indirect rotational forces and an axial load through a hyperplantarflexed forefoot. Diagnosis is made through a thorough history and physical examination, assessing for ecchymosis along the plantar medial aspect of the foot, as well as hypermobility of the Lisfranc articulation (piano key test) during the physical examination. Plain radiographs of the foot are the initial imaging study of choice, with weight-bearing radiographs or advanced imaging such as MRI/CT for further evaluation and surgical planning. Treatment includes nonoperative treatment in certain nondisplaced injuries that are stable with weight-bearing. Operative treatment includes either ORIF or arthrodesis, depending on stability and bony involvement. Typically, with fracture involvement, ORIF is the preferred choice of treatment, and arthrodesis for purely ligamentous injuries.

*Carter et al. reviewed the management of Lisfranc injuries.* **They reported** that appropriate imaging can reduce patient morbidity by reducing the number of missed diagnoses while helping to avoid overtreatment. **They concluded** with a treatment algorithm that includes performing a thorough initial assessment, which involves the use of weight-bearing radiographs and CT/MRI if these are subtle with ORIF being reserved for bony fracture-dislocations and primary arthrodesis for purely ligamentous or severely unstable injuries.

*Rammelt et al. reviewed chronic injuries to the Lisfranc joint complex.* These injuries can range from isolated instability to complex deformities leading to rapidly progressive post-traumatic arthritis. **They report** that for symptomatic joints, correction of the deformity with an arthrodesis of the first 3 TMT joints can lead to a significant reduction in pain as well as functional improvement. **The authors recommend** interpositional arthroplasty rather than fusion for the 4th and 5th TMT joints and possible intercuneiform or naviculocuneiform fusions in complex cases.

*Swords et al. reviewed the salvage treatment of failed Lisfranc or other midfoot injuries.* **They found** that complications encountered included post-traumatic arthritis, instability, chronic pain, infection, and functional loss. **They report** typical salvage options, highlighting arthrodesis techniques and key considerations tailored to the extent of the damage. **They conclude** with a practical framework for decision-making in complex salvage cases to help guide when and how to intervene surgically.

Figures A and B are plain radiographs of the left foot demonstrating a purely ligamentous Lisfranc injury with widening of the Lisfranc articulation on the AP radiograph and minimal subluxation of the proximal base of the 2nd metatarsal on the lateral x-ray.

Incorrect Answers:

Answer 1: Nonunion can occur with these injuries; however, post-traumatic midfoot osteoarthritis is the most common complication following these.
Answer 3: Planovalgus foot deformity can occur following these injuries; however, post-traumatic midfoot osteoarthritis is the most common complication following these.
Answer 4: Instability of the 1st TMT joint is not associated with Lisfranc injuries since the Lisfranc articulation involves the medial cuneiform and 2nd metatarsal base, not the 1st TMT joint.
Answer 5: Associated numbness over the dorsum of the foot can occur with dorsal osteophytes impinging on the superficial peroneal nerve, especially with tight shoewear following these injuries; however, post-traumatic midfoot osteoarthritis is the most common complication following these.
