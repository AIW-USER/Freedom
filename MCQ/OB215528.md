## Question

When would braking time return to normal in patients undergoing open reduction and internal fixation for right ankle fractures?

## Options

1. After return to full weight-bearing
2. Once there is complete radiographic healing
3. Once 90% plantarflexion strength has returned
4. 6 to 9 weeks post-op
5. When walking speed is at baseline

## Correct option

**4. 6 to 9 weeks post-op**

## Answer explanation

Patients that sustain right ankle fractures requiring surgical treatment experience increased braking times postoperatively. Several studies reported braking time returns to near-normal levels between 6-9 weeks.

Ankle fractures are common injuries of the lower extremity and often require surgical treatment to stabilize the tibiotalar joint. Surgical treatment in healthy patients is followed by 6 weeks of non-weight-bearing and immobilization, leading to increased braking times. Several studies have reported 6 to 9 weeks post-op to safely resume driving after surgical treatment of right ankle fractures, regardless of weight-bearing status and radiographic healing.

*Ho et al. performed a prospective study of 23 patients with right ankle fractures treated surgically to determine the safety of returning to driving at 6 weeks post-op.* **They reported** 91% of patients passed the driving test at 6 weeks, even before fractures completely healed or weight-bearing was initiated, and a significant improvement in SMFA scores and braking time from 6 to 12 weeks. **They concluded** patients can safely drive at 6 weeks post-op following surgical treatment of right ankle fractures.

*Egol et al. performed a prospective study evaluating the braking speed and driving safety in 11 healthy volunteers and 31 patients that were surgical treated for right ankle fractures.* The surgically treated patients had a braking speed of 1330, 1172, and 1160 milliseconds at 6, 9, and 12 weeks post-op compared to 1079 milliseconds in the healthy controls. **The authors concluded** braking time returns to baseline by 9 weeks post-op.

Incorrect answers
Answers 1, 2, 3, and 5: Radiographic healing, resumption of weight-bearing, plantarflexion strength, and walking speed are not established proxies for braking time or the ability to safely drive after right ankle ORIF.
