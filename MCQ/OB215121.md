## Question

A high school baseball player presents after injuring his shoulder while sliding into second base during a game last season. He completed 6 months of physical therapy and his shoulder pain had improved, but he doesn't feel confident with his shoulder anymore. He feels pain and apprehension with the physical exam maneuver shown in Figure A. Which structure is necessary to repair surgically or otherwise risk persistent instability?

![[q215121-a-kim-test-moved.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Anterior band of the inferior glenohumeral ligament (AIGHL)
2. Coracohumeral ligament (CHL)
3. Middle glenohumeral ligament (MGHL)
4. Posterior band of the inferior glenohumeral ligament (PIGHL)
5. Superior glenohumeral ligament (SGHL)

## Correct option

**4. Posterior band of the inferior glenohumeral ligament (PIGHL)**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a history and physical exam supporting posterior shoulder instability. The primary restraint to posterior instability in this position is the posterior band of the inferior glenohumeral ligament (PIGHL).

The shoulder is an unconstrained diarthrodial joint that relies on the labrum and capsule in conjunction with the rotator cuff musculature in order to maintain stability. Recognition of the importance of these structures is imperative to restore shoulder stability following a traumatic dislocation. The AIGHL and PIGHL form a hammock structure which alternatively tension as arm position changes. The PIGHL acts to restrain posterior translation with the shoulder in the forward flexed and adducted position, while the AIGHL restrains anterior translation in 90 degrees of abduction and 90 degrees of external rotation.

*Provencher et al. reviewed the management of posterior shoulder instability.* The PIGHL is thinner and less robust than the AIGHL and **the authors postulate** that glenoid retroversion may contribute to posterior instability. They discussed the importance of a diligent physical exam as the history may be vague and instability findings subtle.

*Kuhn et al. investigated the ligamentous restraints to shoulder external rotation with the shoulder fully abducted in the late-cocking position.* *They were able to demonstrate that the entire inferior capsule acts as a restraint to ER, followed by the AIGHL.* These findings further demonstrate the contribution of the AIGHL to anterior shoulder stability.

Figure A shows the Kim test, where the arm is brought into forward flexion with an axial load, which should cause pain and/or apprehension in the posterior shoulder. Illustration A depicts the capsulolabral attachments of the shoulder: (A) represents the PIGHL, (B) the MGHL, and (C) the AIGHL.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1- The AIGHL is a restraint to anterior translation in 90 degrees of abduction and 90 degrees of external rotation.
Answer 2- The CHL limits inferior translation and external rotation when the arm is adducted and limits posterior translation when the arm is flexed, adducted, and internal rotation.
Answer 3- The MGHL is a restraint at 45 degrees of abduction and 45 degrees of external rotation.
Answer 5- The SGHL restrains inferior translation and external rotation of the adducted shoulder.
