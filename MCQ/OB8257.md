## Question

Premature arrest following growth plate injury is attributed to what mechanism?

## Options

1. Proliferation of the perichondrial ring of LaCroix
2. Initiation of Wolff's law
3. Disruption of the Heuter-Volkmann principle
4. Vascular invasion across the physis

## Correct option

**4. Vascular invasion across the physis**

## Answer explanation

Salter-Harris types III and IV fractures create disruption through all physeal zones. A conduit for invasion of blood vessels not normally present between the metaphysis and epiphysis creates the potential for influx of osteoblasts and formation of a bony bar that prevents further longitudinal growth. The remaining responses do not contribute to traumatic physeal arrest. The perichondral ring of LaCroix is a fibrocartilagenous periosteal ring surrounding the physis that provides up to 50% of resistance to shear stress. According to Wolff's law, bone deposition and resorption occurs in response to the stresses placed upon it. The Heuter-Volkmann principle describes slowing of longitudinal physeal growth in response to increasing compressive force. The hypertrophic zone demonstrates a decrease in cell size and columnar density as physeal closure approaches.
