## Question

A picture of axial CT scan spine shows bony lesion at junction of pedicle and lamina. What is the probable diagnosis? (Spine- spinal tumor)

![[TO17747111-1.png]]

## Options

1. Osteoid osteoma
2. Osteosarcoma
3. Eosinophilic granuloma
4. Brodie's abscess

## Correct option

**1. Osteoid osteoma**

## Answer explanation

This is a case of osteoid osteoma of vertebra and CT is showing a typical picture of central nidus with surrounding sclerosis. Spinal osteoid osteoma mainly involves the lumbar spine with predilection for posterior elements seen in 75% of cases. Pars interarticularis is the most common site of involvement. OOs are usually seen in patients under the age of 30 with a male preponderance (sex ratio – 2–4:1). The most common clinical symptom is night pain (up to 80–100%) believed to be due to prostaglandin/prostacyclin production) and painful scoliosis (63–70%). Nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs effectively relieve pain by reducing inflammation. Radionuclide bone scanning remains the most sensitive tool for localization. It reveals focal increased uptake surrounded by a decreased uptake due to the sclerotic bone known as the “Double density” sign.

Diagnosis can be difficult because early radiographs may appear normal. Frequently, a sclerotic lesion of the pedicle is all that is apparent, and even this may be a subtle asymmetry. Later, the usual configuration of a central nidus with surrounding sclerosis may be found, but it is typical in appearance in only half of patients. Oblique radiographs can be helpful when the pedicle, facet, and pars interarticularis are studied. A radioisotope bone scan is most helpful in accurate localization, and CT often shows the nidus

Remember: Vertebral hemangiomas are the most common benign bony primary spinal tumor. Spinal epidural metastases are the most common type of spinal tumor.

Answer :a
