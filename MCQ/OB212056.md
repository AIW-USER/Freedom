## Question

Which of the following is true regarding complications following operative stabilization of ankle fractures in diabetic patients?

## Options

1. Malunion remains the most common postoperative complication
2. Delayed union rates are not influenced by the presence of peripheral neuropathy
3. Postoperative Charcot arthropathy occurs in <5% of patients with peripheral neuropathy
4. Shorter tibiotalocalcaneal (TTC) nails are recommended in salvage procedures to minimize proximal fractures postoperatively
5. Complication rates are higher in those with peripheral neuropathy than those with absent pedal pulses

## Correct option

**5. Complication rates are higher in those with peripheral neuropathy than those with absent pedal pulses**

## Answer explanation

Complications are more common in diabetic patients who undergo operative stabilization of ankle fractures. The presence of peripheral neuropathy is the largest risk factor for postoperative complications in diabetic patients.

Patients with neuropathy or vasculopathy are at increased risk for complications including delayed union, nonunion, and infection. Wound issues remain the most common postoperative complication in diabetic patients. Deep wound infections are more common in those with evidence of preoperative peripheral neuropathy or vasculopathy. Percutaneous screw fixation is encouraged when possible to minimize the risk of deep wound infections.

*Wukich et al. reviewed complications following both open and closed management of ankle fractures in patients with diabetes mellitus.* **They report** that diabetic patients with neuropathy or vasculopathy have higher complication rates than both diabetic patients without these comorbidities and nondiabetic patients. **They concluded** that unstable ankle fractures in diabetic patients without neuropathy or vasculopathy are best treated with ORIF using standard techniques. Patients with neuropathy or vasculopathy are at increased risk for both soft-tissue and osseous complications, including delayed union and nonunion and rigid internal fixation is crucial to obtaining a good outcome. Prolonged non-weight-bearing followed by subsequent protected weight-bearing are recommended following both operative and nonoperative management of ankle fractures in patients with diabetes.

*Costigan et al. reviewed diabetic patients who underwent ORIF using standard fixation techniques for acute, closed ankle fractures.* **They reported** that 33% of patients with preoperative evidence of peripheral neuropathy developed Charcot arthropathy. **They noted** that patients who had absent pedal pulses preoperatively and those with peripheral neuropathy developed complications in 83% and 92%, respectively. **They concluded** that most patients with diabetes can undergo open reduction and internal fixation of acute ankle fractures without complications, but those with absent pedal pulses or peripheral neuropathy are at increased risk for complications.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Wound issues are the most common complication postoperatively in diabetic patients
Answer 2: The presence of peripheral neuropathy increases the risk of delayed union and nonunion; rigid internal fixation is crucial to obtaining a good outcome
Answer 3: The development of Charcot arthropathy occurs more commonly than in 5% of patients with preoperative evidence of peripheral neuropathy
Answer 4: Tibiotalocalcaneal (TTC) arthrodesis and nailing with locking screws has been shown to be an optimal salvage procedure, but long nails should be used to avoid stress fractures proximally
