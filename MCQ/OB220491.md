## Answer explanation

This patient presenting with acute pain, swelling, and erythema in the first metatarsophalangeal (MTP) joint, with similar symptoms previously responsive to anti-inflammatory medications, most likely has gout. Microscopic analysis of aspirate from the involved MTP joint would most likely demonstrate needle-shaped negatively birefringent crystals (Answer 1).

Gout is a form of inflammatory arthritis resulting from the deposition of monosodium urate crystals within joints. This typically presents as acute, recurrent episodes of severe joint pain and swelling. Diagnosis is definitively established by joint aspiration and microscopic analysis demonstrating the characteristic presence of strongly negatively birefringent, needle-shaped intracellular crystals. Management of acute attacks focuses on anti-inflammatory medications, while chronic gout is treated with medications aimed at lowering serum uric acid levels to prevent further crystal formation and symptomatic flares. This condition most frequently affects men aged 40-60 and commonly involves joints in the lower limb, classically the great toe (podagra), but can also lead to crystal deposition, known as tophi, in various soft tissues. While serum uric acid levels are often elevated, a definitive diagnosis requires crystal identification, as elevated levels alone are not diagnostic, and the presentation can sometimes resemble septic arthritis.

*Perez-Ruiz et al. reviewed the current literature on uric acid, crystal deposition disease, and gout, focusing on rising prevalence and the growing recognition of its systemic impact, particularly on cardiovascular outcomes.* **They found** that advanced imaging techniques, such as ultrasonography and dual-energy computed tomography, are valuable for detecting urate crystal deposition before symptomatic onset and for objectively measuring crystal depletion during urate-lowering treatment. **They concluded** that an early treatment approach for gout and associated comorbidities is advisable, with the ultimate strategic objective being the complete dissolution of urate crystals in tissues to prevent further symptoms and structural musculoskeletal damage.

*Zhang et al. reviewed the diagnosis of gout, which relies on identifying birefringent monosodium urate (MSU) crystals in synovial fluid using compensated polarized light microscopy (CPLM),* **noting** limitations of traditional methods. **They found** that a novel lens-free polarized microscope achieved wide-field, high-resolution holographic imaging of birefringent crystals with color contrast comparable to standard CPLM, and experimental results demonstrated close agreement with gold-standard images. **They concluded** that this lens-free polarized microscopy is a promising approach to improve the efficiency and accuracy of diagnosing gout, reducing costs, and can be deployed at point-of-care settings.

Figure A is a clinical photograph demonstrating a gout flare at the left 1st MTP joint, as evidenced by increased swelling and erythema.

The remaining figures reveal the following microscopy findings:
Figure B: Needle-shaped, negatively birefringent crystals, characteristic of gout.
Figure C: Rhomboid-shaped, weakly positively birefringent crystals, characteristic of pseudogout.
Figure D: Gram-positive cocci, in this case, Staphylococcus aureus.
Figure E: Increased number of erythrocytes, as can be seen in the setting of hemarthrosis.
Figure F: Gram-negative bacilli, in this case E. coli.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Pseudogout may present similarly to gout clinically; however, large joint involvement, such as the knee, is much more common.
Answers 3 and 5: Although septic arthritis, of which Staph aureus is the most common cause, may present similarly to gout, this patient’s clinical history, characteristic 1st MTPJ involvement, and normal WBC count are more suggestive of gout.
Answer 4: Hemarthrosis in the absence of local trauma may occur in those with hemophilia or pigmented villonodular synovitis. However, these conditions are overall less likely than a gout flare in the described scenario, particularly in a patient who had similar symptoms previously that resolved with NSAIDs.
