## Question

## Options

1. Leave pins in place and continue immobilization in a long arm cast for 3 more weeks
2. Leave pins in place and continue immobilization in a long arm cast for 6 more weeks
3. Leave pins in place and provide a removable brace to begin gentle range of motion for the next 3 weeks
4. Remove medial pin only, and continue immobilization in a long arm cast for 3 more weeks
5. Remove all pins and discontinue immobilization

## Correct option

**5. Remove all pins and discontinue immobilization**

## Answer explanation

Three weeks after closed reduction and percutaneous pinning of a supracondylar humerus fracture one should consider pin removal.

Supracondylar humerus fractures are common injuries among children. For displaced fractures, initial treatment typically includes closed reduction and percutaneous pinning (CRPP) with long arm cast immobilization. After 3 weeks in the cast there is enough callus to allow removal of pins and discontinuation of immobilization. Activity restrictions remain, but beginning gentle range of motion at the elbow is allowed.

*Green et al. present a review of 71 supracondylar humerus fractures treated by CRPP with a crossed pin construct.* The medial pins were placed with a "mini-open" technique to protect the ulnar nerve. **They noted** no iatrogenic ulnar nerve injuries, and recommend use of this technique when placing a medial pin.

*Abzug et al. present a current concepts review of the treatment of supracondylar humerus fractures in children.* **They note** that standard postoperative care following CRPP involves long arm cast immobilization for 3 weeks, followed by pin removal and discontinuation of immobilization. They allowed patients to wear a sling for comfort for 1-2 weeks, but encouraged gentle range of motion.

Figures A and B are AP and lateral radiographs of a displaced supracondylar humerus fracture. Figures C and D are AP and lateral radiographs of the same elbow 3 weeks after CRPP. Three lateral pins are in place, and callus can be noted around the fracture site.

Incorrect answers:
Answers 1-3: Three weeks after CRPP the pins should be removed.
Answer 4: After 3 weeks, no further long arm cast immobilization is needed.
