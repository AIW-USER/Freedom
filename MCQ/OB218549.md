## Question

A patient presents to your office with complaints of foot pain for nearly a year. The patient denies any history of injury but complains of pain and swelling localized to the right second metatarsophalangeal (MTP) joint. On examination, there is decreased passive range of motion of the second MTP joint. Radiographs reveal subchondral sclerosis and flattening of one of the metatarsal heads. Regarding this patient's underlying condition, which of the following is true?

## Options

1. More common in females
2. More commonly involves the third metatarsal head
3. More common in patients in their 30s and 40s
4. Metatarsal head flattening is an early radiographic finding
5. Early surgical intervention is recommend to prevent disease progression

## Correct option

**1. More common in females**

## Answer explanation

Freiberg's disease occurs much more frequently in females, with a female:male ratio of 5:1.

Freiberg's disease is a rare foot condition characterized by infarction and fracture of the metatarsal head. The diagnosis is made clinically and radiographically with plain radiographs showing subchondral sclerosis, flattening of the involved metatarsal head, and eventual joint destruction in advanced disease; MRI studies may be needed to detect early disease. The incidence is higher in females than in males. This is believed to be due to a combination of vascular compromise, genetic predisposition, and altered biomechanics. Treatment is activity modification and NSAIDs in early disease to reduce pressure on the involved metatarsal head. Surgical management is indicated for progressive pain, joint destruction, and joint deformity.

*Talusan et al. reviewed Freiberg's infarction.* **They reported** that adolescent females are the "textbook" patients but both males and females may present with this condition later in life. **They noted** that the second and third metatarsals are the most commonly affected, while the involvement of the fourth or fifth is rare. **They concluded** that spontaneous healing with remodeling may occur in the early stages of the disease, while operative options are dorsal closing wedge osteotomies, osteochondral transplant, and resection arthroplasty.

*Trnka et al. reviewed Freiberg's infraction.* **They reported** that onset is usually between the 11th and 17th year of age and has a female-to-male ratio of 5:1. **They also noted** that the second metatarsal is most frequently involved (68%) followed by the third metatarsal (27%), and the fourth (3%). **They concluded** that complete resolution of pain and full return to activities in 70% after joint destructive procedures and more than 90% after joint preserving procedures.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Freiberg's disease most commonly involves the second metatarsal (68%).
Answer 3: Freiberg's disease occurs most commonly in patients aged 11-17 years old.
Answer 4: Early radiographs in Freiberg's disease are usually normal, whereas flattening of the metatarsal head would be seen later in the disease progression.
Answer 5: Nonoperative management with activity modification, NSAIDs, and immobilization is recommended in the early stage of the disease.
