## Options

1. Worsening arm pain with axial compression on an extended, laterally flexed, and rotated cervical spine
2. Involuntary flexion of the thumb and index finger with flicking of the long finger
3. Axial neck pain with motion and decreased cervical rotation
4. Electical sensation down back with cervical flexion
5. Decreased arm pain with shoulder abduction

## Correct option

**3. Axial neck pain with motion and decreased cervical rotation**

## Answer explanation

The patient has left C1-2 facet arthrosis and ankylosis of the left occipitocervical joint secondary to rheumatoid spondylitis, which may be characterized by occipital headaches and worsening axial neck pain with decreased cervical spine range of motion. Compression of the C2 nerve root (greater occipital nerve) can be linked to headaches in this scenario as well.

Cervical spondylosis is the result of disc and facet degeneration leading to axial neck pain and reduced range of motion of the cervical spine. In patients with rheumatic manifestations of the cervical spine, there can be auto-fusion of the occipital-C1 facet leading to advanced degeneration of the adjacent facet joints. Facet arthropathy can also be the result of senile degeneration or other inflammatory arthritides, such as CPPD or gout. In isolation, facet arthropathy manifests as axial neck pain, reduced range of motion, and occipital pain (if there is the involvement of the upper cervical spine). Beyond rheumatoid arthritis, occipital headaches can be a manifestation of occipital neuralgia (C2 radiculopathy), migraines, tension headaches, and myofascial pain syndromes.

*Kobayashi et al. performed a prospective study of 27 patients over the age of 50 years that presented with acute axial neck pain.* **They reported** patients experienced a significant improvement in VAS scores from 81.9 mm to 35.6 mm following a lateral atlantoaxial joint puncture, with 62.5% of the patients with synovial fluid collected having calcium pyrophosphate crystals present. **The authors concluded** that crystal-induced arthropathy of the lateral atlantoaxial joint could be a common cause of acute neck pain in elderly patients.

*Hippensteel et al. reviewed the physical examination of the cervical spine, scapula, and rotator cuff.* *The authors described the Spurling test, axial manual traction test, shoulder abduction test, Valsalva test, and upper limb tension test.* **They concluded** that the upper limb tension test is the most useful test for diagnosing cervical radiculopathy with a sensitivity of 97%.

Figure A is a sagittal CT of the cervical spine with multilevel erosive changes consistent with rheumatoid cervical spondylitis without significant neural compression.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: Spurling's sign is typically present with cervical radiculopathy. With neck extension, rotation, and axial compression reproduction of radicular pain is considered positive.
Answer 2: Hoffman's sign is often present in cervical myelopathy.
Answer 4: Lhermitte's sign is the production of electrical sensation down the spinal axis with cervical spine flexion. This is indicative of cervical myelopathy.
Answer 5: Relief of upper extremity radicular pain with arm abduction is indicative of cervical radiculopathy.
