## Question

A 14-year-old girl with osteogenesis imperfecta (Type I) falls and sustains a both-bone forearm fracture. The extremity is closed and neurovascularly intact. Closed reduction and casting are performed as seen in Figures A and B. What is the next best step in the management of this injury?

## Options

1. Continued casting with repeat radiographs in 2 weeks
2. Realignment osteotomy and intramedullary rod fixation
3. Realignment osteotomy and plate fixation
4. Intramedullary rod fixation
5. Plate fixation

## Correct option

**4. Intramedullary rod fixation**

## Answer explanation

The next best step in this scenario would be open reduction with intramedullary rod fixation given fragile bone, nearly closed growth plates (decreased ability to remodel), and residual deformity.

Osteogenesis imperfecta (OI) results in a decrease in Type I collagen and significantly affects bone quality specifically bone remodeling. Avoiding a fracture by preventative measures in patients with OI is ideal. When fracture does occur consideration to the altered bone biology should drive decision making by the treating provider. Surgical treatment should be considered for fractures with deformity that falls outside of acceptable tolerance for the location and age of the patient. Surgical treatment with plate fixation does increase the risk of periprosthetic fracture given adjacent stress shielding and poor bone remodeling. Ideally, fractures that do require surgical fixation should be treated with intramedullary rods whenever possible.

*Zeitlin et al. reviewed the seven types of osteogenesis imperfecta (OI).* **They state** in OI bone fragility stems from a combination of decreased bone mass, disorganized bone tissue, and altered bone geometry. They do note intravenous bisphosphonates decreases fracture rates and bone pain while increasing bone density and level of ambulation with minimal side effects.

*Noonan et al. reviewed pediatric forearm fractures and* **stated** that they accept bayonet apposition and no more than 30 degrees of malrotation and no more than 10 degrees of angulation. They describe their method of closed reduction and intramedullary fixation followed by 6-8 weeks of casting.

Figures A and B shows a both-bone forearm fracture in a nearly skeletally mature patient after closed reduction and casting with >10 degrees of deformity in the sagittal and coronal planes.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Continued closed reduction would likely lead to malunion and subsequent motion loss.
Answer 2: Realignment osteotomy is indicated for severe persistent deformity to reduce the risk of future fracture.
Answer 3: Realignment osteotomies in patients with OI are often stabilized with an intramedullary device given the risk of subsequent fracture near the ends of a plate.
Answer 5: Plate fixation puts the patient at risk for adjacent fracture given the stress shielding and altered bone biology of patients with OI.
