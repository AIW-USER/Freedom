## Question

Figure A is the radiograph of a 10-year-old girl who sustained a left hip injury while playing soccer. She reports the inability to bear weight on the left lower extremity. Figures B and C are the 1-week and 8-week postoperative radiographs, respectively. Which of the following is considered a risk factor for developing the complication seen in Figure C?

## Options

1. Hardware placement in the posterosuperior femoral neck
2. Use of cannulated screws
3. Having <5 screw threads engaged in the epiphysis
4. Not attempting a forceful reduction maneuver
5. Performing a capsulotomy

## Correct option

**1. Hardware placement in the posterosuperior femoral neck**

## Answer explanation

The patient has an unstable slipped capital femoral epiphysis (SCFE). Hardware placement in the posterosuperior femoral neck is a risk-factor of disrupting the vascular supply following surgical management of a SCFE.

Osteonecrosis of the femoral head is the most devastating complication of SCFE. In most cases, osteonecrosis of the femoral head is likely secondary to vascular disruption from the initial femoral head displacement. Several factors have been shown to increase the risk of femoral head osteonecrosis. Unstable slips, defined as the inability to walk even with the aid of crutches, have been shown to have a 47% incidence of osteonecrosis. However, as closed reductions are now less frequently attempted, newer studies have indicated that the risk of avascular necrosis (AVN) in an unstable SCFE to be much lower than 47%. Additionally, hardware placement in the posterosuperior femoral neck may result in injury to the vascular supply and place the hip at risk for AVN.

*Loder et al. review the presenting symptoms and radiographs of 54 patients with SCFE and reclassified them as either unstable or stable.* *They defined unstable slips as the inability to bear weight even with crutches, while stable slips were those in which the patient was able to bear weight, with or without crutches.* **They reported** that 47% of the unstable slips and 96% stable slips had a satisfactory result. **They also demonstrated** a 47% and 0% rate of AVN in unstable and stable slips, respectively. **They concluded** that a higher risk of avascular necrosis was associated with unstable slips.

*Rhoad et al. review 73 SCFEs that underwent a pretreatment planar technetium bone scan to identify the presence of ischemia of the femoral head.* **They reported** that none of the 63 stable SCFEs demonstrated ischemia by bone scan, and none developed AVN. Of the 10 unstable SCFEs, 6 demonstrated ischemia by bone scan, and 5 of these developed AVN. **They concluded** that a pretreatment bone scan is a sensitive predictor for the development of AVN in unstable SCFEs.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Use of 2 screw constructs demonstrate greater biomechanical stability than a single screw. There is a greater risk of articular surface penetration with the use of an additional screw. Cannulated screws have not been shown to have a greater risk of AVN.
Answer 3: <5 screw threads engaged in the epiphysis increases the risk of slip progression.
Answer 4: Performing a forceful reduction has been shown to increase the risk of osteonecrosis.
Answer 5: Performing a capsulotomy decreases intra-capsular pressure and may be used in an unstable SCFE. There is no clear evidence that the use of a capsulotomy reduces the risk of AVN.
