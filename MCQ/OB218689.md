## Question

A 42-year-old female patient returns to your clinic for follow-up. You have been managing her for adult-acquired flatfoot secondary to posterior tibial tendon insufficiency. Given the recalcitrant nature of her symptoms, you plan on performing a flexor digitorum longus tendon transfer, gastrocnemius lengthening, and medial displacement calcaneal osteotomy. The patient asks if you will use a minimally invasive surgical (MIS) or a traditional open approach for the osteotomy. Which of the following is true regarding these two treatment strategies?

## Options

1. A MIS technique is associated with fewer wound complications but higher rates of nerve injury when compared to an open approach
2. A MIS technique is associated with fewer wound complications but longer surgical time when compared to an open approach
3. A MIS technique is associated with fewer wound complications but higher radiation exposure when compared to an open approach
4. A traditional open technique is associated with higher wound complications and a longer surgical time when compared to the MIS technique
5. A traditional open technique is associated with improved radiographic outcomes but more frequent wound complications when compared to the MIS technique

## Correct option

**4. A traditional open technique is associated with higher wound complications and a longer surgical time when compared to the MIS technique**

## Answer explanation

Both minimal invasive surgical (MIS) and traditional open techniques can be utilized in the treatment of adult-acquired flatfoot deformity (AAFD) secondary to posterior tibial tendon insufficiency (PTTI). A MIS approach is associated with a smaller incision, reduced surgical time, lower rate of neurological injury, and lower rate of wound complications with no appreciable differences in patient outcomes or radiation exposure when compared to a traditional open approach (Answer 4).

PTTI is the most common cause of AAFD and occurs secondary to attenuation and tenosynovitis of the posterior tibial tendon. This attenuation serves as the inciting pathologic event in a pathology that often causes progressive deformity and arthritis. PTTI is more common in women and typically presents in the sixth decade of life. While the exact etiology remains unknown, a variety of risk factors have been identified including, but not limited to, obesity, hypertension, diabetes, increasing patient age, and corticosteroid use.

The management and treatment of PTTI is dependent on the stage of the disease, which takes into account the type of deformity, the patient's physical examination findings, and the radiographic sequelae of the patient's pathology. Early-stage PTTI is often initially managed nonoperatively, with surgery reserved for patients with symptoms recalcitrant to appropriate nonoperative management or those with more profound pathology. Traditionally, surgery was completed through a traditional open approach. Recently, however, surgeons have begun utilizing minimally invasive approaches, which offer benefits when compared to traditional approaches, with no appreciable deleterious effect on patient outcomes or complications.

*Kendal et al. investigated the outcomes of minimally invasive versus open calcaneal osteotomy.* *Their study included 81 patients, 50 of whom underwent a traditional open approach and 31 who received a minimally invasive approach.* Overall, **the authors concluded** that a MIS calcaneal osteotomy is a safe technique, with no difference in radiographic improvement observed between the two approaches. Promisingly, **the authors observed** significantly fewer wound and nerve complications in the MIS group.

*Waizy et al. also investigated the outcomes of minimally invasive versus open calcaneal osteotomy.* *Their study included 60 consecutive patients, 33 of whom underwent a traditional open approach and 27 who received a minimally invasive approach.* **The authors noted** a significantly shorter operative time and smaller incision in the MIS group, with no difference in radiation exposure. Furthermore, patient outcomes did not differ. **The authors conclude** that despite no difference in clinical outcomes, the shorter operating time might indicate the benefit of a MIS approach.

Incorrect Answers
Answer 1: MIS techniques have lower rates of both wound complications and nerve injury
Answer 2: MIS techniques have lower rates of wound complications and shorter surgical times
Answer 3: MIS techniques have lower rates of wound complications and no difference in radiation exposure
Answer 5: There is no difference in radiographic or patient outcomes when comparing MIS and traditional open techniques
