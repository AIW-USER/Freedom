## Question

Not true about cortical screw is( general orthopaedics- material properties and orthopedic implants )

## Options

1. The runout is the weakest part of a partially threaded screw.
2. The size of core determines the strength of screw and its fatigue resistance
3. Tensile strength of a screw depends on root diameter
4. Mechanical advantage of a screw is equal to pitch/circumference

## Correct option

**4. Mechanical advantage of a screw is equal to pitch/circumference**

## Answer explanation

Parts of a bone screw:

The shaft of the screw is that region between the underside of the screw head and the point where the threads begin. Core is the solid section from which the threads project outwards or portion of the shaft extending within the thread pattern. The size of the core determines the strength of the screw and its fatigue resistance. The size of the drill bit used is equal to the core diameter.

 The region where the shaft meets the core of the screw (starting point of the threads) is referred to as the runout. The runout is the weakest part of a partially threaded screw.

Root or minor diameter is equal to core diameter. Major diameter is equal to diameter of threads.

Pitch and mechanical advantage:

Pitch is the distance between the base of two threads in a screw and lead means the distance the screw travels in a single complete turn.

 The mechanical advantage (MA) of a screw can be found by dividing the circumference of the screw by the pitch of the screw, or MA = Circumference/Pitch

Strength of a bone screw:

Tensile strength (resistance to breaking) of a screw: depends on root diameter (diameter of the screw between the threads)
 Pull out strength: depends on the outside diameter of the threads; does not depend on the number of threads per inch, provided 5 or 6 threads are in the cortex.

Answer: 4
