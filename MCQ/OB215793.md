## Question

A 12-year-old female presents to your clinic complaining of bilateral foot pain and feelings of instability when walking. On examination, she has 5/5 strength with hindfoot inversion but 3/5 strength with eversion. She also has weakness dorsiflexing her ankles. Before deciding on a treatment strategy, you recommend obtaining an EMG and genetic testing. The EMG demonstrates decreased nerve conduction velocities and prolonged distal latencies, particularly in the nerve innervating the weak evertors. Genetic testing reveals an abnormal duplication in chromosome 17. What is the most likely diagnosis?

## Options

1. Spastic diplegic cerebral palsy
2. Friedrich's ataxia
3. Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease
4. Duchenne's muscular dystrophy
5. Marfan syndrome

## Correct option

**3. Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease**

## Answer explanation

Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease (CMT) can lead to cavovarus foot deformity because of abnormal peripheral nerve myelination due to a duplication in chromosome 17.

CMT is an autosomal dominant hereditary neuropathy that is caused by abnormal myelination of peripheral nerves, causing decreased nerve conduction velocity and prolonged nerve latencies that commonly affect the peroneal nerve. There are multiple categories of CMT, with type 1 being most common and affecting motor nerves more than sensory nerves. The cause of abnormal myelination is thought to be a duplication of chromosome 17, leading to a mutation in the peripheral myelin protein 22 (PMP22) gene that is expressed by Schwann cells. These abnormalities lead to weakness of the tibialis anterior and peroneus brevis in the foot, in addition to the intrinsic muscles of the hand and foot. Therefore, the most recognizable deformity in these patients is a cavovarus foot deformity due to the unopposed pull of the peroneus longus and tibialis posterior. Many of these patients will eventually require surgery to correct their foot deformities after bracing fails.

*Krajewski et al. reviewed the neurologic causes associated with CMT type 1.* *They reiterated that the duplication in chromosome 17 is the main issue, which leads to damage to large motor and sensory axons, as demonstrated by decreased nerve conduction velocities, reduced CMAPs and SNAPs and increased muscle weakness.* *They finish by suggesting that therapies should be directed at preventing demyelination and axonal degeneration or promoting regeneration.*

*Manganelli et al. reviewed the role of nerve conduction velocity in managing CMT1A.* **They note** that as CMT patients age, axonal degeneration worsens, as demonstrated by decreased CMAP amplitude and nerve conduction velocity. **They concluded** that age related changes in nerve conduction velocity are likely dependent on the patient's ability to remodel myelin thickness and more investigation is needed in this area.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Patients with cerebral palsy can have a cavovarus foot deformity, however cerebral palsy does not have a specific association with chromosome 17.
Answer 2: Friedrich's ataxia often has a similar cavovarus foot appearance but is caused by a GAA repeat mutation on the chromosome 9.
Answer 4: Patients with Duchenne's muscular dystrophy are more commonly affected by equinovarus foot deformities. This disease is caused by mutations in the DMD gene on the X chromosome. It is an X-linked recessive disorder.
Answer 5: Patients with Marfan syndrome typically have pes planovalgus foot deformities with abnormalities of the FBN1 gene on chromosome 15.
