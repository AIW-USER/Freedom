## Question

An 11-year-old boy sustains an elbow injury. Which of the following injuries could be appropriately managed with a long arm posterior splint for 8-12 days, followed by protected range of motion exercises?

## Options

1. Nondisplaced lateral condyle fracture
2. Acutely reduced elbow dislocation
3. Ulnar collateral ligament sprain
4. Gartland type I supracondylar fracture
5. Minimally displaced olecranon fracture

## Correct option

**2. Acutely reduced elbow dislocation**

## Answer explanation

Acute elbow dislocations can be appropriately treated with a long arm posterior splint for 10 days, followed by protected range of motion exercises.

Acute pediatric elbow dislocations are most common in 10-15 year-olds. They are classified according to the position of the proximal radio-ulnar joint in relation to the distal humerus. Posterior dislocations are most common. Treatment involves closed reduction, verification of stability, then brief immobilization with early range of motion. Operative treatment is usually indicated in open dislocations and incarcerated medial epicondyle fragments.

*Andrews et al. reviewed dislocations of the elbow joint.* **He reported** that dislocation of the elbow in the pediatric population is a common injury, which may be associated with fractures of the radius, ulna, or humerus. The majority of children's dislocations can be simply managed with a closed reduction. Following reduction, it is important to confirm that there is a stable congruent reduction with full range of motion.

*Protzman reported the effect of prolonged immobilization after elbow dislocation.* **He showed** that prolonged immobilization resulted in greater stiffness, poorer satisfaction and a slower return to normal activities. Early immobilization did not result in elbow instability.

Illustrations A and B show left elbow AP and lateral radiographs demonstrating a posterolateral elbow dislocation with an associated medial epicondyle fracture. Illustration C shows an attempted closed reduction with entrapment of the medial epicondyle in the joint.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Nondisplaced lateral condyle fractures are treated with long arm casting for a minimum of 3 weeks and often are considered for fixation with percutaneous pinning or screw fixation.
Answer 3: UCL sprain should not be treated with an elbow splint. Early protected ROM is advised.
Answer 4: Gartland type I supracondylar fractures should be treated in an above elbow splint/cast for 3-5 weeks.
Answer 5: Minimally displaced olecranon fractures should be treated in an above elbow splint for 3-5 weeks.
