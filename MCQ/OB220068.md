## Question

A 59-year-old female presents to the clinic as a follow-up with symptomatic knee osteoarthritis. After trialing physical therapy, weight loss, and oral anti-inflammatories, she would like to now discuss further options. She has been hesitant to try any injections due to her fear of needles but presents to the clinic today with questions regarding hyaluronic acid (HA) and platelet-rich plasma (PRP) injections. Which of the following is true when deciding between HA and PRP?

## Options

1. HA and PRP require different injection sites.
2. PRP is more effective for severe (Kellgren-Lawrence Grade 4) osteoarthritis.
3. Per current American Academy of Orthopedic Surgeons (AAOS, 3rd Generation) guidelines, PRP injections received a strong recommendation for symptomatic arthritis of the knee.
4. PRP has been to shown result in improved clinical outcomes when compared to HA injections for knee osteoarthritis.
5. Leuckocyte-rich PRP is safer than leuckocyte-poor PRP.

## Correct option

**4. PRP has been to shown result in improved clinical outcomes when compared to HA injections for knee osteoarthritis.**

## Answer explanation

PRP injections have been shown in multiple studies, including meta-analyses, to outperform HA injections in terms of clinical outcomes (answer choice 4). The remaining answer choices are incorrect.

Platelet-rich plasma (PRP) injections have gained favor for use in knee osteoarthritis (OA). PRP has been particularly used in patients seeking continued nonoperative management with insufficient relief from corticosteroid or hyaluronic acid (HA) injections. Many studies have found significant benefits in pain relief and functional improvement, particularly in patients with mild to moderate OA rather than advanced disease. PRP works by utilizing the body's natural healing processes. The patient's blood is drawn and processed to concentrate platelets and growth factors. When injected, these factors have been proposed to promote tissue repair, reduce inflammation, and stimulate the production of cartilage and extracellular matrix. Studies have evaluated the effect of leukocyte-poor (LP-PRP) and leukocyte-rich (LR-PRP) and found that leukocyte-poor PRP performs better, but further research is warranted.

*Belk et al. published a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials comparing the efficacy of PRP and HA injections for the treatment of knee osteoarthritis.* *In total, 18 studies were included in their analysis.* *In 5 studies with a mean follow-up of at least 12 months, the PRP group demonstrated a significantly better WOMAC score compared to the HA group (44.7% vs 12.6%, respectively, P < 0.01).* *Regarding VAS pain scores, 11 studies reported results, with 5 studies reporting significantly less pain in the PRP group compared to the HA group (P < 0.05).* Six studies reported results of the Subjective IKDC score, 3 of which studies found PRP patients to report significantly better IKDC scores when compared to HA patients (P < 0.05). **The authors concluded** that treatment of knee osteoarthritis with PRP injections produces improved clinical outcomes when compared to HA injections.

*Nie et al. published a meta-analysis on the effectiveness of platelet-rich plasma injections for osteoarthritis of the knee.* *Twenty-one randomized controlled trials were included in this analysis.* *PRP was found to significantly relieve pain compared to saline and corticosteroid injections with at least a 6-month follow-up.* When compared to hyaluronic acid, this analysis found significant study heterogeneity, and therefore, no conclusion was drawn. In conclusion, **the authors found** PRP injections to be effective for pain relief and functional improvement in knee osteoarthritis but recommended further high-quality research to confirm these findings.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer choice 1: This statement is incorrect. No study has demonstrated a difference in outcomes between the various injection sites for intraarticular knee injections for HA or PRP.
Answer choice 2: PRP is more effective for mild-moderate osteoarthritis. Studies have shown that Kellgren-Lawrence (KL) grades 2-3 experience the most benefit, while advanced arthritis (KL grade 4) may not experience relief or improvement.
Answer choice 3: According to current AAOS guidelines, PRP for the management of knee osteoarthritis has received a limited recommendation. Due to significant heterogeneity in the various studies, the AAOS guidelines have downgraded the recommendation from strong to limited.
Answer choice 5: Leukocyte-poor PRP (LP-PRP) has been shown to be more effective and has a decreased risk profile compared to leukocyte-rich PRP (LR-PRP).
