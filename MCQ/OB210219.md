## Question

A child presents to the ED with the injury shown in Figures A and B. Following a closed reduction attempt, which of the following is the strongest indication for surgical fixation?

## Options

1. Age <12
2. Age >12
3. Male sex
4. Articular step-off >2 mm
5. Any articular step-off

## Correct option

**4. Articular step-off >2 mm**

## Answer explanation

Following closed reduction, a Tillaux fracture with >2 mm of articular step-off necessitates surgical fixation.

Tillaux fractures (SH-III), along with triplane fractures (SH-IV), are transitional fractures in adolescents with incomplete physeal closure. The distal tibia physes close in the following order: central, medial, and lateral. Supination and external rotation about the ankle will often shear off an anterolateral tibial fragment before the anterior inferior tibiofibular ligament fails. If the articular step-off is <2 mm, treatment consists of cast immobilization, whereas >2 mm step-off requires ORIF or CRPP to prevent growth arrest (although rare) and post-traumatic arthritis. Closed reduction typically involves internal rotation of the ankle.

*Wuerz et al. reviewed pediatric physeal ankle fractures.* **They state** that Tillaux and triplane fractures typically arise in the 18-month transitional period prior to complete physeal closure. **They suggest** utilizing CT for surgical planning to better asses articular congruency.

Crawford discussed the indications for surgical fixation of both triplane and Tillaux fractures. He comments on the excellent outcomes of these fractures when anatomically reduced and reinforces the utility of CT in properly assessing articular congruity. Finally, he suggests using closed reduction and percutaneous fixation using a threaded cannulated screw with the goal of achieving <2 mm of articular step-off.

Figures A and B demonstrate an AP and lateral XR of a Tillaux fracture, respectively. Illustration A is an AP and lateral CT scan of a Tillaux fracture. Illustration B is a post-operative XR demonstrating CRPP of a Tillaux fracture.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1 and 2: Age is not an indication for surgical fixation in Tillaux fractures.
Answer 3: Gender does not play a role in the decision for surgical fixation in Tillaux fractures.
Answer 5: Tillaux fractures with an articular step-off of <2 mm can be treated non-operatively with cast immobilization, whereas >2 mm should be treated operatively to avoid post-traumatic arthritis.
