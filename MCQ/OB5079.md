## Question

A 10-year old boy sustains a femoral shaft fracture. Titanium elastic nailing is appropriate treatment for which of the following cases?

## Options

1. Highly comminuted, open diaphyseal femur fracture in patient that weighs 35 kilograms
2. Highly comminuted, closed diaphyseal femur fracture in patient that weighs 35 kilograms
3. Transverse, open, mid-diaphyseal femur fracture in patient that weighs 35 kilograms
4. Transverse, open, mid-diaphyseal femur fracture in patient that weighs 51 kilograms
5. Transverse, closed, mid-diaphyseal femur fracture in patient that weighs 51 kilograms

## Correct option

**3. Transverse, open, mid-diaphyseal femur fracture in patient that weighs 35 kilograms**

## Answer explanation

Titanium elastic nailing is appropriate treatment for patients older than 6 years and less than 100 pounds, with length stable (transverse or short oblique) diaphyseal femoral fractures.

Flexible intramedullary nails offer a treatment option for a large number of femoral shaft fractures in children. Although external fixation may be more appropriate in cases with more severe soft tissue injuries, flexible nails can be used in both open or closed fractures. They are limited in highly comminuted fractures, due to their limited ability to maintain length. The construct is not a strong enough for heavier children.

*Kocher et al. provide the AAOS Clinical Practice Guideline for the treatment of pediatric diaphyseal femur fractures.* Their recommendation for diaphyseal femur fractures in patients ages 5 to 11 years is Grade C, based on level III evidence. **They state** simply that flexible nails are "an option" for treatment of these fractures. Compared to traction and casting, patients treated with flexible nails return to walking and school more rapidly, and patients >11 years old or >49 kg have poorer outcomes.

*Flynn et al. review the management of pediatric femoral shaft fractures.* **They note** that flexible intramedullary nails are the preferred implant for skeletally immature patients >6 years old with transverse fractures in the middle 60% of the femur.

*Kay et al. review the management of pediatric polytrauma patients.* **They note** that intramedullary fixation has largely replaced external fixation for the fixation of open femur fractures in pediatric patients. Although both allow access to open wounds, access is even better with intramedullary implants, pin site care is not required, and scars more cosmetic.

Incorrect answers:
Answers 1 and 2: Highly comminuted fracture patterns require length stable fixation (plate, rigid nail or external fixation)
Answers 4 and 5: Weight >49 kg is associated with poor outcome, although this does not vary independently of age.
