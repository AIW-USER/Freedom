## Question

A 26-year-old female tennis player is seen by you in clinic for chronic lateral ankle instability, and she subsequently undergoes anatomic reconstruction of the anterior talofibular ligament utilizing a semitendinosus allograft. Which of the following findings is the most consistent with the most common nerve complication seen with this procedure?

## Options

1. Paresthesias in the lateral aspect of the foot
2. Paresthesias in the proximal dorsum of the foot
3. Inability to plantarflex the ankle
4. Paresthesias in the dorsal aspect of the webspace between the great toe and second toe
5. Paresthesias in the medial aspect of the hindfoot

## Correct option

**2. Paresthesias in the proximal dorsum of the foot**

## Answer explanation

Injury to the superficial peroneal nerve is the most common nerve complication following lateral ligament reconstruction (Answer 2).

Chronic lateral ankle stability may present as mechanical and/or functional instability secondary to injury to the anterior talofibular ligament and/or the calcaneofibular ligament. First-line treatment for this condition is non-operative and focuses on strengthening, proprioception, and functional rehabilitation. Immobilization is seldom utilized in favor of early mobilization. Surgical management is reserved for those who have failed non-operative management and consists of various techniques ranging from non-anatomical tenodesis procedures to anatomic repair or reconstruction. Complications of these procedures include recurrent instability and nerve damage (most commonly to the superficial peroneal nerve).

*Yasui et al. published a review covering surgical intervention for chronic lateral ankle instability.* *They discuss non-anatomic (i.e., Chrismasn-Snook, modified Brostrom, Watson-Jones tenodesis, Evans procedure) and anatomic procedures, direct anatomic repairs (Brostrom procedure, Gould modification, Karlsson modification), anatomic reconstructions with autograft or allograft, the role of arthroscopic repair, the outcomes of these interventions, and the future predisposition of patients to ankle arthritis.* **They conclude** that, apart from non-anatomic procedures, there are many different ways to approach patients with this condition, and these approaches should be tailored to individual characteristics.

*Alcale and Maffulli published an overarching review on the management of chronic lateral ankle instability.* *They cover the anatomy of the ankle joint, clinical presentations of acute and chronic lateral ankle instability, conservative management strategies and their middling benefit on chronic instability, and surgical techniques.* *They grouped surgical interventions into anatomic and tenodesis stabilization techniques, and additionally discussed the role that arthroscopy has in addressing concomitant intra-articular pathologies.* **They concluded** that there are several different approaches to this condition, though tenodesis techniques tend to be looked upon less favorably due to their high risk of complications.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The sural nerve provides sensation to the lateral aspect of the foot and is not commonly seen to be injured with lateral ligament reconstruction
Answer 3: Inability to plantarflex is likely to be due to an injury to the tibial nerve, which is not a common complication of lateral ligament reconstruction
Answer 4: Paresthesia in the dorsal aspect of the webspace between the great toe and second toe is consistent with a deep fibular nerve injury, which is not a common complication of lateral ligament reconstruction
Answer 5: Paresthesia in the medial aspect of the hindfoot is consistent with a saphenous nerve injury, which is not a common complication of lateral ligament reconstruction
