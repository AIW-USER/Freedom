## Question

A 12-year-old baseball pitcher throws 100 pitches a game and reports chronic anterolateral left shoulder pain for 4 months. On physical exam, he is noted to have normal range of motion. Which of the following imaging findings would most likely be present?

## Options

1. Chronic supraspinatus tear with atrophy and retraction to glenoid
2. Pathologic fracture with fallen cortical fragment in base of empty cyst
3. Acromion tip stress fracture
4. Widened lateral physis at the proximal humerus
5. Salter Harris II fracture of proximal humerus

## Correct option

**4. Widened lateral physis at the proximal humerus**

## Answer explanation

In a 12-year-old baseball player throwing 100 pitches per game, the most likely diagnosis is a physeal stress fracture (Little Leaguer's shoulder [LLS]), which presents on imaging studies as widened physis at the proximal humerus.

LLS is seen most frequently in male baseball pitchers between the ages of 11-14 years, a range where peak growth in the proximal physis occurs and makes it susceptible to injury. While the exact etiology is unknown, the accepted theory is that of repetitive rotational stresses, along with the frequency of pitching and poor throwing kinematics. Findings on radiographs can include widening of the physis (epiphysiolysis), metaphyseal sclerosis, osteopenia, and fragmentation. Treatment consists of activity modification and education, with the athlete refraining from throwing until symptom resolution.

*Taylor et al. reviewed the consensus and controversies of adolescent shoulder injuries.* **They reported** that repetitive microtrauma can lead to pain and disability, particularly in adolescent throwing athletes, and this risk is increased with age and the level of competition. **They highlighted** that placing limits on the number and type of pitches thrown might decrease the prevalence of shoulder and elbow injuries.

*Heyworth et al. examined the trends in the presentation, management, and outcomes of LLS.* **They reported** that this diagnosis is being recognized with increasing frequency, most commonly in male baseball pitchers, youth catchers, other baseball positions players, and tennis players. **They highlighted** that almost a third of LLS patients had glenohumeral internal rotation deficit (GIRD), and this group had approximately three times higher probability of recurrence compared with those without GIRD.

*Chen et al. reviewed shoulder and elbow injuries in the skeletally immature athlete.* **They reported** that the intensity of training and competition among young athletes can place them at increased risk of acute and chronic injuries, which occur in patterns unique to the skeletally immature athlete. **They emphasized** early recognition, coupled with appropriate treatment or prevention can help restore and maintain normal shoulder and elbow function.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: While a chronic retracted and atrophic rotator cuff tear is common in patients >40 years, this is not the most common in a 12-year-old.
Answer 2: This refers to a unicameral bone cyst, and is not the most common cause of pain from excessive pitch counts.
Answer 3: A proximal humerus physeal stress fracture (not an acromion tip stress fracture) is most common.
Answer 5: LLS does not result in a Salter-Harris II fracture of the proximal humerus.
