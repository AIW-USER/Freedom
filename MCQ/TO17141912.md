Chapter: Regional Orthopedics MCQs — Ulno-Carpal Abutment Syndrome

## Question

A 26-year-old male Mixed Martial Arts student presented to OPD with a primary complaint of increased pain following repetitive punching at a stationary bag, and had increased in intensity over this period. Opening doors and weight bearing in extended positions would also aggravate his complaint. The patient reported relief with rest. Previous trauma included a distal radius growth plate fracture and distal ulna fracture from a fall on an outstretched arm at the age of 13 years, in which the patient did not note long-term deficits. His wrist X ray is shown below. What is the best treatment of choice?

![[TO17141912-1.jpg]]

## Options

1. Arthroscopic repair of TFCC
2. Interposition arthroplasty
3. Distal ulnar osteotomy
4. Darrach procedure

## Correct option

**3. Distal ulnar osteotomy**

## Answer explanation

This is ulnar abutment syndrome caused by excessive impact stress between ulna and carpal bones (primarily lunate). X ray shows positive ulnar variance. Normally only 20% load goes to ulna, but in case of + 2 mm positive ulnar variance 40% of the load goes to the ulna. Patient usually presents with ulnar side wrist pain with associated with wrist activities.

positive ulnar variance can be seen in the setting of:

scapholunate dissociation

TFCC tears

lunotriquetral ligament tears

radial shortening from previous Colles fracture.

Treatment is ulnar shortening osteotomy. Darrach procedure (ulnar head resection) is indicated in lower demand patients. It increases the risk of proximal ulna stump instability.

Answer: 3
