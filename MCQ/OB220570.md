## Question

A 65-year-old male presents with left leg pain after a ground-level fall. On exam, the leg is shortened and externally rotated. Plain radiographs demonstrate a left intertrochanteric fracture. Which of the following patient or radiographic characteristics, if present, would suggest that the utilization of a sliding hip screw would be inadvisable?

## Options

1. A history of contralateral cephalomedullary nail
2. A history of resolved non-ossifying fibroma in the femoral neck
3. A lateral wall thickness of 20 mm
4. The patient's age
5. The standard obliquity orientation of the fracture

## Correct option

**3. A lateral wall thickness of 20 mm**

## Answer explanation

Lateral wall thickness is defined as the measurement of a line extending from a point 3 cm distal to the inominate tubercle, angled at 135 degrees to the fracture line. A measurement < 20.5 mm suggests the presence of an unstable intertrochanteric fracture, which would require a cephalomedullary nail rather than a sliding hip screw (Answer 3).

Intertrochanteric fractures are common hip fractures that most frequently require surgical intervention. Implant choice should take into account the stability of the fracture. Sliding hip screws are most frequently reserved for stable intertrochanteric as these injuries are less susceptible to varus collapse or medial displacement of the femoral shaft. Radiographic findings concerning for the presence of an unstable intertrochanteric fracture include posteromedial cortex comminution, subtrochanteric extension, reverse obliquity fracture orientation, or lateral wall thickness less than 20.5 mm (measured by drawing a line extending from a point three cm distal to the nominate tubercle, angled at 135 degrees towards the fracture line). These unstable fractures are, instead, best treated with a cephalomedullary nail.

*O’Connor and Switzer published a review article summarizing the current guidelines established by the American Academy of Orthopaedic Surgeons for the management of hip fractures.* *They summarized and graded recommendations for various aspects of managing these injuries, including surgical timing, approach, implant selection, and postoperative protocols.* **They concluded** that additional research is still warranted to address areas not addressed by these recommendations, which include the optimal treatment of osteoporosis in the fragility fracture population and outcomes of total hip arthroplasty for hip fractures.

*Hsu et al. published a review of 208 patients with intertrochanteric fractures to identify the role that lateral wall thickness has in predicting lateral wall fractures after use of a dynamic hip screw.* **They found** that a lateral wall thickness threshold of 20.5 mm had an 82.7% sensitivity and a 77.8% specificity in predicting future lateral wall fracture. **They concluded** that patients with intertrochanteric fractures and a lateral wall thickness < 20.5 mm should not be treated solely with a dynamic hip screw.

Incorrect answers:
Answers 1, 2, 4, and 5: These options do not suggest the presence of an unstable intertrochanteric fracture, which would warrant a cephalomedullary nail rather than a sliding hip screw.
