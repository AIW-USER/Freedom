## Question

## Answer explanation

A three-column fracture of an ankylosed cervical spine is a highly unstable injury that is at high risk for progressive neurologic deficits. This is best treated with posterior cervicothoracic instrumented fusion.

Fractures of the spine in patients with ankylosing spondylitis uniquely involve all three columns from relatively low-energy mechanisms. This is due to the long lever arm of the ankylosed spine resulting in greater force transfer to the affected region, most often the cervical spine. Due to the involvement of the posterior tension band, posterior instrumentation and fusion is preferred to avoid high failure rates of anterior constructs alone. Often, a long fusion construct is needed to enable adequate fixation in the setting of osteoporosis that is often associated with the disease process.

*Werner et al. reviewed the treatment of spinal fractures in patients with ankylosing spondylitis.* **They reported** nonsurgical treatment is associated with a high rate of complications and anterior alone surgical approaches are associated with high failure rates. **They recommend** maintaining an appropriate level of concern for patients presenting with low-energy trauma and a diagnosis of AS.

*Schouten et al. reviewed the initial assessment and emergency management of spine injuries.* **The authors recommended** assuming all patients with AS presenting with neck or back pain after a trivial fall have an unstable spinal fracture until proven otherwise. **They further recommended** obtaining advanced imaging of the entire spine in AS patients presenting with axial back pain after low-energy falls.

Figure A is a sagittal CT of the cervical spine demonstrating a flexion teardrop fracture of the C6 vertebral body. Figure B is an axial CT of the cervical spine demonstrating a Jefferson burst fracture without significant displacement. Figure C is a lateral radiograph of the cervical spine with a type 3 Hangman's fracture. Figure D is a sagittal CT of the cervical spine with ankylosing spondylitis and a three-column fracture. Figure E is an coronal CT of the cervical spine with a type 3 odontoid fracture.

Incorrect answers
Answer 1: Figure A depicts a flexion teardrop fracture, which is best addressed with an anterior corpectomy, strut grafting, and anterior instrumentation.
Answer 2: Figure B depicts a Jefferson burst fracture of the atlas, which can be treated with halo vest immobilization in most cases.
Answer 3: Figure C depicts a type 3 hangman's fracture, which can be treated with a C2-3 ACDF.
Answer 5: Figure E depicts a type 3 odontoid fracture, which can be effectively treated with C-collar immobilization.
