## Question

A 75-year-old male presents for his preoperative total knee arthroplasty (TKA) appointment. As part of the chart review, it is apparent that the patient is an active smoker, does not drink alcohol, has a BMI of 27, takes Apixaban, and has a hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c) of 8.1%. How should this patient be counseled before undergoing TKA?

## Options

1. Serum fructosamine levels > 292 µmol/L are not associated with higher risks for deep infection
2. Cigarette smoking is not associated with a higher rate of complications
3. The patient is at increased risk for infection because his BMI is >25
4. The patient is at increased risk for infection because his HbA1c is >8%
5. The patient does not have any risk factors for infection or poor outcomes

## Correct option

**4. The patient is at increased risk for infection because his HbA1c is >8%**

## Answer explanation

Hemoglobin A1c levels > 8% are associated with increased infection rates in total joint arthroplasty (TJA) (Answer 4).

Optimizing modifiable risk factors before surgery is crucial to reducing hospital readmissions and postoperative complications after TJA. These factors include controlling blood glucose, preventing venous thromboembolism, addressing obesity, managing vitamin D levels, quitting tobacco, moderating alcohol intake, and treating cardiovascular disease. Patients with a HbA1c level above 8% are associated with an increased risk of infection.

*Iorio et al. retrospectively reviewed the outcomes of 4,241 primary or revision total hip or total knee arthroplasties at a single institution.* **They concluded** that patients with diabetes have a higher incidence of infection after TJA compared to those without diabetes. Among infections, diabetic patients had more deep infections. The mean HbA1c level was 6.92% in noninfected diabetics and 7.2% in infected diabetics.

*Boraiah et al. developed a Readmission Risk Assessment Tool (RRAT), which allowed for risk stratification in patients undergoing elective primary TJA at their institution.* *Over a 6-year period, there were 207 readmissions among 9,930 patients, with surgical site infections being the most common cause of readmission.* The RRAT score had a significant association with readmission after TJA and could potentially be a clinically useful tool for risk mitigation.

*Shohat et al. reviewed 829 patients undergoing primary TJA; 119 had a history of diabetes, and 308 had prediabetic HbA1c levels.* Fifty-one patients had fructosamine levels ≥ 292 μmol/L; of these, 20 did not have HbA1c levels ≥7%. Patients with fructosamine levels ≥ 292 μmol/L had significantly higher risks for deep infection, readmission, and reoperation rates.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1: Serum fructosamine levels ≥ 292 μmol/L are associated with a higher risk of deep infection.
Answer 2: Cigarette smoking is associated with a higher rate of complications after TJA.
Answer 3: A BMI of 27 (< 30) is not associated with an increased risk of infection in TJA.
Answer 5: This patient does have a risk factor for infection, such as a HgbA1c > 8%.
