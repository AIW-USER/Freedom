## Question

A 55-year-old male with chronic and worsening left plantar foot pain notes tenderness on the plantar aspect of his 2nd and 3rd metatarsophalangeal (MTP) joints with an associated callus present. He notes a history of hallux valgus in the left great toe and a previous 1st MTP fusion performed three years ago, as shown in Figure 1. He denies any paresthesias. On examination, he has a positive squeeze test and notes pain with palpation of the 2nd and 3rd metatarsal heads. No click is noted in the left foot. What is the most likely cause of his left foot pain?

*Figure A*

## Options

1. Morton's neuroma
2. Frieberg's disease
3. 1st metatarsal shortening
4. 1st metatarsal nonunion
5. Hallux valgus recurrence

## Correct option

**3. 1st metatarsal shortening**

## Answer explanation

Patient presents with symptoms suggestive of transfer metatarsalgia. He previously underwent a 1st MTP fusion for hallux valgus and likely developed transfer metatarsalgia from aggressive shortening of the 1st metatarsal (MT) (answer 3).

Hallux valgus is a deformity of the 1st ray that can cause medial great toe pain and difficulty with shoe wear. This condition is more common in women (15:1) and affects up to 30% of women aged 18-65 years. Diagnosis is made clinically with the presence of the great toe that rests in a valgus and pronated position. Radiographs of the foot can also be obtained for further evaluation and for surgical planning in more severe cases. Treatment typically begins with conservative measures such as shoe modifications, like metatarsal pads, spacers, and orthoses. Surgical intervention is indicated once conservative management fails to provide pain relief. Complications following surgical correction may include recurrence of deformity, Hallux varus, avascular necrosis, malunion/nonunion, transfer metatarsalgia, neuropraxia, and a cock-up toe deformity.

Michael Coughlin published a JBJS review on hallux valgus deformities. He notes this deformity occurs with lateral deviation of the great toe and medial deviation of the first metatarsal, with progressive subluxation of the 1st MTP joint. He notes that hallux valgus deformities almost exclusively occur in shoe-wearing (constricting footwear) societies, with an extremely high prevalence in American women ages 40-60 years. He concludes that hallux valgus deformities are common in patients who wear constricting shoes, and it is important to look for this deformity in patients at risk, presenting with medial great toe pain and a cosmetic deformity.

*Lopez et al. performed a cadaveric biomechanical study evaluating the effect of hallux valgus correction on forefoot loading mechanics.* Four lower limbs were used in this study, and a pressure-sensing mat was used to assess forces through the foot following metatarsal shortening and sagittal plane displacement. Medial forefoot pressure significantly increased when comparing the control group vs the group with plantar displacement of the osteotomy (p=0.006), and lateral forefoot pressure increased dramatically with dorsal displacement (p=0.013). **They conclude** that considering metatarsal head position following the osteotomy can be useful, as a decrease in medial loading and an increase in lateral loading may lead to lateral forefoot pain and transfer metatarsalgia.

Incorrect Answers:

Answer 1: Morton's neuroma can cause lateral forefoot pain; however, it is more common to present with Mulder's click and associated paresthesias.
Answer 2: Frieberg's disease is a rare condition in the foot when the 2nd MT head develops avascular necrosis. This would be seen on radiographs, which was not the case in this patient.
Answer 4: 1st MT nonunion can cause forefoot pain, given the previous MTP arthrodesis procedure; however, radiographs would demonstrate signs of nonunion, which was not the case in this patient.
Answer 5: Hallux valgus recurrence is a common cause of failure with inadequate correction of the initial deformity. However, this would be found on radiographs, which was not the case in this patient.
