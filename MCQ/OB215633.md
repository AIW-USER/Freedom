## Question

A 52-year-old female slipped on the ice and fell down several steps. She went to the ED and radiographs revealed a simple elbow dislocation, and subsequently underwent closed reduction and splinting. She eventually underwent open reconstruction of the medial and lateral collateral ligaments with application of a hinged external fixator for persistent instability. With respect to the biomechanics of elbow stability, which of the following is true?

## Options

1. Anterior bundle of the MUCL is the primary restraint to valgus stress in maximal elbow flexion
2. Lateral ulnar collateral ligament inserts on the sublime tubercle
3. Posterior bundle of the MUCL is the most important restraint against valgus stresses
4. Radial collateral ligament originates at the axis of rotation for elbow flexion
5. Second most important restraint against to valgus stresses is the ulnotrochlear articulation

## Correct option

**4. Radial collateral ligament originates at the axis of rotation for elbow flexion**

## Answer explanation

The biomechanics of the elbow is complex. The radial collateral ligament originates at the axis of rotation for elbow flexion.

Elbow dislocations are common elbow injuries that can be characterized as simple or complex depending on associated injuries to nearby structures. Treatment is closed reduction, followed by a short period of immobilization for stable simple elbow dislocations. Surgical management is indicated for complex elbow dislocations associated with fractures or persistent instability. Primary static stabilizers of the elbow include the ulnohumeral joint (coronoid), medial (ulnar) collateral ligament (MUCL), and the lateral collateral ligament complex (LCL). The LCL complex consists of the radial collateral ligament (RCL), lateral ulnar collateral ligament (LUCL), accessory collateral ligament, and the annular ligament. Secondary static stabilizers include the radiocapitellar joint, capsule, and the origins of the flexor and extensor tendons. Lastly, dynamic stabilizers include those muscles that cross the elbow joint, such as the anconeus, brachialis, triceps, and biceps.

*Tashjian et al. reviewed complex elbow instability consisting of dislocation of the ulnohumeral joint with a concomitant fracture of one or several of the bony stabilizers of the elbow.* **They reported** that recurrent instability is not often associated with a simple dislocation, but an improperly managed complex dislocation may result in recurrent elbow instability. **They concluded** that fixation or replacement of injured bony elements, ligamentous repair, and hinged fixation may be used to successfully manage complex elbow instability.

*Wyrick et al. reviewed complex elbow fracture-dislocations.* **They reported** that chronic instability, posttraumatic arthrosis, and poor functional outcomes are frequently encountered following these injuries. **They concluded** that optimization of elbow function is achieved through the restoration of articular congruity and stability coupled with early rehabilitation.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The posterior bundle of the MUCL is the primary restraint to valgus stress in maximal elbow flexion
Answer 2: The LUCL inserts on the crista supinatoris of the proximal ulna
Answer 3: The anterior bundle of the MUCL is the most important restraint against valgus stresses
Answer 5: The second most important restraint against valgus stresses is the radial head
