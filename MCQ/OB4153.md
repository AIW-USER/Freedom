## Question

Which of the following statements is true regarding the recurrent laryngeal nerve and anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF)?

## Options

1. It is the most common nerve injury with anterior cervical discectomy and fusion.
2. Injuring the nerve leads to anhydrosis, pupil dilation, and facial drooping on the ipsilateral side of the injury.
3. The anatomic course of the nerve is symmetric on the left and the right sides.
4. It originates from the nerve roots C3, C4, and C5.
5. It runs along with the superior thyroid artery in the upper cervical spine.

## Correct option

**1. It is the most common nerve injury with anterior cervical discectomy and fusion.**

## Answer explanation

The recurrent laryngeal nerve (RLN) is the most commonly injured nerve during anterior cervical discectomy and fusion. It is more common than injury to the sympathetic chain and injury to the C5 nerve root.

The recurrent laryngeal nerve must be considered when performing anterior surgery in the cervical spine. Permanent or transient injury has been reported to occur at rates of 1-11% following ACDF and manifests with dysphonia, hoarseness, airway obstruction, vocal fatigue, persistent cough, aspiration, and dysphagia. The anatomic course of the nerve has been well-studied, and specific right- and left-sided differences should be considered. It has been suggested that right-sided approaches present a higher risk to the RLN but this has not manifested in the clinical literature.

*Haller et al. performed a cadaveric study on the anatomic course of the RLN from both the left and right sides.* On the right side, the RLN leaves the vagus trunk at or below the level of the subclavian artery at T1-T2, and passes under and then anterior to the subclavian artery before it ascends in the tracheo-esophageal fascia. On the left, the RLN leaves the vagus trunk at the level of the aortic arch, passes beneath the aortic arch at the site of the origin of the ligamentum arteriosum, and ascends within the tracheo-esophageal fascia. The right RLN invests within the fascia at C7-T1, whereas the left RLN invests within the fascia at T1-T2. Both RLN terminate at the larynx an average of 0.5cm caudal to the C6-7 disk space.

*Ebraheim et al. performed a cadaveric study comparing the right and left RLN.* *In this study, the right-sided RLN was found to ascend more obliquely in the sagittal plane than the left RLN.* In addition, the right RLN was found to enter the tracheo-esophageal groove at variable levels compared with the left. These anatomic findings conceivably put the RLN at risk during right-sided ACDF approaches.

*Beutler et al. retrospectively reviewed a case series of 328 ACDF and corpectomy procedures to assess for RLN symptoms based upon side of approach.* *173 were approached from the right and 155 were approached from the left.* *9 of 328 patients had post-operative dysphonia (5 left, 4 right).* There was no demonstrable increase in risk between approach sides or with ACDF vs. corpectomy. Revision surgery did appear to increase the risk of RLN injury (2.3% vs 9.5%, p=0.049).

*Kilburg et al. retrospectively reviewed 418 consecutive one- or two-level ACDF cases to assess the risk of RLN injury based on laterality of exposure (278 had right-sided exposures, and 140 had left-sided exposures).* There were 8 RLN injuries (1.9%), 5 of which were on the right (1.8%) and 3 of which were on the left (2.1%). **The authors concluded** that laterality of approach did not appear to influence the risk of RLN injury.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: The cervical sympathetic chain lies on top of the longus colli and can be injured if retractors are not placed under the longus colli muscle.
Answer 3: The RLN is most vulnerable on the right because it crosses from lateral to midline more cephalad in the incision after it passes under the subclavian artery; conversely, on the left the course is more caudal because it passes under the aortic arch, which is a more caudal structure.
Answer 4: The RLN originates from the vagus nerve (cranial nerve X) within the carotid sheath. The phrenic nerve originates from the roots of C3-C5.
Answer 5: The superior laryngeal nerve runs along with the superior thyroid artery in the upper cervical spine, putting it at risk during surgical procedures on the upper cervical spine, which are less commonly performed. The RLN branches interface with the inferior thyroid artery in the lower cervical spine as the nerve ascends to the larynx.
