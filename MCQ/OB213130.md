## Options

1. Originates from the posterior cord and splits the two heads of the FCU in the proximal forearm
2. Originates from the lateral cord and runs in Guyon's canal
3. Originates from the medial cord and runs deep to the transverse carpal ligament as it enters the hand
4. Originates from the lateral cord and runs medial to the associated artery at the level of the wrist
5. Originates from the medial cord and runs between the FDP and FCU in the forearm

## Correct option

**5. Originates from the medial cord and runs between the FDP and FCU in the forearm**

## Answer explanation

The ulnar nerve is at risk of entrapment in the fracture site during an open reduction internal fixation (ORIF) of a medial epicondyle fracture. The ulnar nerve originates from the medial cord and runs between the FDP and FCU in the forearm.

During the elbow medial approach for the ORIF, it is important to identify and protect the at-risk ulnar nerve. The ulnar nerve originates from the medial cord of the brachial plexus and provides motor innervation to the flexor carpi ulnaris, flexor digitorum profundus of small and ring fingers, adductor pollicis, deep head of the flexor digitorum brevis, hand volar and dorsal interrosei, 3rd and 4th lumbricals, abductor digiti minimi, opponens digiti minimi, flexor digiti minimi, as well as sensation along ulnar aspect of the hand. Entrapment of the ulnar nerve at the level of the elbow would present with wrist palmar flexion weakness and ring and small finger numbness.

*Lima et al. published a case report of an elbow dislocation associated with unrecognized fracture of the medial epicondyle and delayed ulnar neuropathy in pediatric age.* *They presented the case of a 10-year-old boy with*
a posterior dislocation of the left elbow, and a fracture of the medial epicondyle that was not recognized
until he developed ulnar nerve palsy 3 months later. They discussed that the delayed neuropathy of
the ulnar nerve was associated with a complete recovery in children, so long as it is promptly treated.

Lee at al. performed a retrospective cohort study on the operative treatment of displaced medial epicondyle
fractures in children and adolescents. They reported no significant correlation between surgical outcome and injury mechanism, displacement, interval from injury to surgery, dislocation, fixation method, or duration of immobilization. All patients reported good to excellent results after operative treatment. They concluded that operative treatment was a reasonable option for managing these fractures in children and adolescents.
