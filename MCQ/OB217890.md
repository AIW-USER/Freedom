## Question

A 21-year-old soccer player presents to your office with complaints of persistent knee instability after undergoing an ACL reconstruction 1-year prior. He denies any new injury to the knee. Radiographs of the knee are seen in Figure A. Assuming the ACL reconstruction is still intact, which of the following examination findings would be expected on examination?

![[q217890-a-acl2.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Negative Lachman and pivot shift
2. Negative Lachman and positive pivot shift
3. Positive Lachman and negative pivot shift
4. Positive Lachman and pivot shift
5. Unable to assess without an MRI

## Correct option

**2. Negative Lachman and positive pivot shift**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a vertically placed ACL femoral tunnel. This could lead to rotational instability, which is best assessed with a pivot shift test.

ACL tears are common athletic injuries leading to anterior and lateral rotatory instability of the knee. The Lachman and pivot shift are two commonly employed examination maneuvers that assess ACL integrity. The Lachman is the most sensitive exam test and assesses anterior translation of the tibia on the femur. The pivot shift is best at assessing rotational stability of the ACL. With an ACL-deficient knee in full extension and internal rotation, the lateral tibial plateau subluxates anteriorly; as the knee is flexed, the lateral tibial plateau slides posteriorly into a reduced position, causing an audible clunk. A vertically placed femoral tunnel during ACL reconstruction may lead to a knee that is anteriorly stable (negative Lachman) but rotationally unstable (positive pivot shift).

*Galway et al. reviewed the lateral pivot shift (LPS) in assessing ACL insufficiency.* **They reported** that it is characterized by anterior subluxation of the tibial plateau out from beneath the lateral femoral condyle. **They noted** that as a consequence of this form of instability, deleterious changes of the knee joint take place, including meniscal tears, and articular cartilage erosions. **They concluded** that the eventual result of continued symptomatic instability is late osteoarthritis of the knee.

*Lane et al. reviewed the Lachman and the pivot shift in the assessment of ACL instability.* **They reported** that there is mounting evidence that the pivot shift examination correlates with functional instability and patient outcomes better than does any other physical examination test. **They noted** that this test attempts to reproduce the functional combined rotary and translational instability in the ACL-deficient knee. **They concluded** that while the pathologic kinematics of the pivot shift are difficult to measure, recent technological advances have allowed more accurate and objective descriptions of the pivot shift, which have furthered our understanding of the complex motions involved.
