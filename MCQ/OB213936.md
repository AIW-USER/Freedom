## Question

A 63-year-old female underwent left total knee arthroplasty (TKA) 5 years ago. She was recently diagnosed with an infected left TKA and subsequently underwent a two-stage exchange arthroplasty. Based on the Musculoskeletal Infection Society (MSIS), which of the following, if present, can be used in isolation for the diagnosis of an infected TKA?

## Options

1. Synovial WBC count of 60,400 with 90% PMNs
2. CRP of 48 mg/L
3. Sinus tract communicating with the prosthesis
4. Purulence in the knee joint
5. ESR of 49 mm/h

## Correct option

**3. Sinus tract communicating with the prosthesis**

## Answer explanation

According to the Musculoskeletal Infection Society (MSIS) criteria for infection (Illustration A), the diagnosis of a prosthetic joint infection can be made when one major criterion or at least 6 points are achieved based on minor criteria. The two major criteria are the presence of a sinus tract communicating with the prosthesis and an isolated pathogen by culture on two separate samples from within the joint.

Periprosthetic joint infections have an incidence of 1-2% following primary TKA and 4-5% after revision TKA. The MSIS criteria outlined below are used to aid in the diagnosis of non-acute prosthetic joint infections. Acute infections, occurring <3 months from the index replacement and acute hematogenous infections defined as those occurring >3 months after surgery but who have had symptoms for <6 weeks were excluded when evaluating the MSIS criteria. The diagnosis of periprosthetic infection can be made with two positive cultures of the same organism or a sinus tract communicating with the joint. Additionally, at least 6 points based on minor criteria, as outlined in illustration A, give the diagnosis of PJI. Two-stage exchange arthroplasty is the gold standard of treatment for prosthetic joint infections that occur >4 weeks after the index procedure. In this first stage of this technique, the original prosthesis is removed, a thorough debridement is performed, and an antibiotic spacer is placed. The patient then completes 4-6 weeks of IV antibiotics prior to having the spacer removed and a TKA replanted.

*Bingham et al reviewed the Musculoskeletal Infection Society (MSIS) minor criteria and how they related to prosthetic joint infections.* *The authors reviewed 297 patients who underwent a prosthetic joint infection work-up at a tertiary care facility for failed TKA.* The predictive probability of a prosthetic joint infection from 5 minor criteria were: 3.6% for 1 positive variable, 19.3% for 2, 58.7% for 3, 83.8% for 4, and 97.8% for 5. **They concluded** that all of the minor criteria were significantly associated with having a prosthetic joint infection.

*Parvizi et al reviewed the MSIS criteria and set out to validate an updated version for the diagnosis of PJI.* **They report** that the presence of a sinus tract or two positive cultures were considered major criteria and diagnostic of infection. Furthermore, they developed an aggregate scoring system consisting of CRP, D-dimer, ESR, CRP, alpha-defensin, leukocyte esterase, polymorphonuclear percentage, and synovial fluid CRP to assist in the diagnosis of prosthetic joint infections. Patients with an aggregate score of >6 were considered infected. **They conclude** that with these new criteria a sensitivity of 97.7% and a specificity of 99.5% were achieved.

Illustration A is a chart depicting the MSIS criteria for periprosthetic joint infection.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1, 2, 5: All of these are minor criteria based on the MSIS system. While in combination, these can lead to the diagnosis of a prosthetic joint infection, none of them alone, have the validity to diagnose a prosthetic joint infection.
Answer 4: Purulence in the joint is insufficient to diagnose prosthetic joint infections because fluid from poly debris, metal on metal articulations and gout may have a similar appearance.
