## Options

1. May require removal of the adductor aponeurosis
2. More amendable to repair with delayed presentation
3. Most often results from an abduction moment of the thumb
4. Rupture most commonly occurs at the distal insertion
5. Typically presents within 2 weeks of injury

## Correct option

**2. More amendable to repair with delayed presentation**

## Answer explanation

This patient underwent repair of the thumb radial collateral ligament (RCL). Repair of the RCL are also much more amendable to primary repair even with delayed presentation.

Thumb collateral ligament injuries often lead to a decrease in effective thumb pinch and grasp. Ulnar collateral ligament (UCL) injuries are 10 times more common than RCL injuries. RCL injuries should be evaluated with radial-ulnar stress exam with the thumb in both extension and at 30° of MCP flexion to avoid allowing the phalanx to rotate. RCL repair is indicated in acute Grade 3 injuries with >15° side-to-side variation of varus/valgus instability and >30-35° of opening. Fortunately, 96% good to excellent outcomes have been reported after RCL repair.

*Taylor et al. retrospectively reviewed the demographics and presentation of patients with RCL injuries in a military health care system.* **They reported** that patients with RCL injuries were younger and more likely to require surgery than were those with UCL injuries (67% vs 40%, respectively). Moreover, patients with UCL injuries presented 2-10 weeks after injury, whereas nearly all patients with RCL injuries presented >10 weeks after injury. Radial collateral ligament injuries were more likely than UCL injuries to have resulted from an axial load (56% vs 16%), whereas UCL injuries were more likely to have been caused by an abduction-adduction moment (50% vs 22%). **They concluded** that close attention to subtle or frank instability presenting as pain in younger patients with axial loading injury mechanisms may allow early diagnosis and appropriate treatment of this injury.

*Wang et al. reviewed the management of grade III injuries of the RCL of the thumb following delayed primary repair of chronic RCL injuries without the use of tendon grafts or tendon transfers.* **They reported** that the RCL was able to be primarily repaired with suture anchors in all cases with patients being able to return to competitive play at the same pre-injury professional level. **They concluded** that delayed primary repair of the RCL is a viable option and results in satisfactory long-term outcomes, and may be more preferable to the professional athlete who wishes to avoid surgery during the sporting season.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: UCL injuries may require removal of the adductor aponeurosis
Answer 3: RCL injuries often result following an axial load, while UCL injuries most often results from an abduction moment of the thumb
Answer 4: UCL ruptures occur primarily at the distal insertion, while RCL tears are evenly distributed between proximal, mid substance, and distal insertion
Answer 5: UCL injuries often present within 2-10 weeks after injury, while RCL injuries often present >10 weeks after injury
