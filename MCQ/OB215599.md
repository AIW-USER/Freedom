## Question

A 32-year-old laborer presents with severe lower back pain after a fall from a 20-foot roof. He denies any leg pain or numbness. Examination reveals a point tenderness in the thoracolumbar junction without a palpable step-off. Motor strength is 5/5 in all the major muscles groups with sensation intact in all dermatomes. An MRI is performed and depicted in figure A. Which of the following is true regarding this injury?

## Options

1. Patients treated nonoperatively have worse 1-year post injury sagittal alignment than operatively treated patients
2. Patients treated operatively have a significantly decreased pain at 1-year post-op than nonoperatively treated patients
3. Use of a TLSO brace has significant pain relief compared to no orthosis for nonoperative treatment
4. Nonsurgical treatment is associated with lower complication rates than operative treatment with similar outcomes
5. Surgical treatment is associated with higher functional status at 1-year compared to nonoperative treatment

## Correct option

**4. Nonsurgical treatment is associated with lower complication rates than operative treatment with similar outcomes**

## Answer explanation

The patient is neurologically intact and presenting with a stable thoracolumbar burst fracture at L1. Nonoperative treatment for this is associated with lower complication rates than operative treatment.

Thoracolumbar burst fractures are the result of axial-loading mechanisms the lead to compression failure of the anterior and middle column. These injuries can be unstable if the posterior column is disrupted by tension failure of the posterior ligamentous complex (PLC). Nonoperative treatment of neurologically intact patients with stable burst fractures has been associated with similar radiographic and clinical outcomes as surgical treatment, but with lower complication rates.

*Vaccaro et al. developed a new classification system for thoracolumbar burst fractures through a consensus of leading experts in spinal trauma.* The Thoracolumbar Injury Classification System is based on the injury morphology, the integrity of the posterior ligamentous complex, and the neurologic status of the patient. **They concluded** the TLICS system can improve communication regarding patient injuries in a uniform manner and help guide treatment options.

*Pizones et al. performed a prospective study of 74 patients presenting with thoracolumbar traumatic injuries and evaluated MRI markers for posterior ligamentous complex incompetence.* **They reported** MRI evidence of supraspinous ligament disruption on MRI correlated with intraoperative PLC incompetence. **They concluded** disruption of the supraspinous ligament is the critical step in PLC incompetence.

*Wood et al. performed a 16- to 22-year follow-up study of patients treated surgically and nonsurgically for stable thoracolumbar burst fractures without neurologic deficits.* **They reported** no differences in sagittal alignment, but significantly improved pain, ODI, and Roland and Morris scores for nonoperatively treated patients. **They concluded** nonoperative treatment of stable burst fractures without neurologic deficits is superior to surgical treatment.

Figure A is a T2 MRI of the lumbar spine with a burst fracture of the L1 vertebrae and an intact PLC. They reported higher pain scores and lower functional scores (ODI and Roland-Morris) in the operative group with no differences in sagittal alignment. They concluded long-term follow-up demonstrates nonoperative treatment to be superior for stable, neurologically intact burst fractures.

Incorrect answers
Answer 1: Surgical treatment for stable and neurologically intact patients does not improve sagittal alignment at 1-year post-injury.
Answer 2: Neurologically intact patients with stable burst fractures have equivalent pain improvement with operative and nonoperative treatment.
Answer 3: Thoracolumbar orthosis with early mobilization has not been associated with improved pain scores compared to no orthosis.
Answer 5: Functional outcome scores at 1-year for neuro-intact and stable burst fractures are similar for surgical and nonsurgical treatment.
