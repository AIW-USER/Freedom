## Question

A recreational rock climber presents to the Emergency Department after an 8-foot fall while descending a precipice. He presents with isolated pain to his right heel with radiographs and CT imaging demonstrating a displaced intraarticular calcaneus fracture. After a long discussion, the patient wishes to proceed with open reduction internal fixation as he fears a calcaneus malunion will preclude him from climbing again. Which of the following variables would portend a more favorable outcome after surgical fixation?

## Options

1. Preoperative Böhler angle <0 degrees
2. Male sex
3. Age <30 years
4. Workers' compensation
5. Sanders Type IV fracture

## Correct option

**3. Age <30 years**

## Answer explanation

Of the listed choices, only the patient’s age being <30 years old is a positive predictor of improved outcomes after surgical fixation.

The calcaneus is the most commonly fractured tarsal bone, observed classically after a high energy axial load (i.e. fall from height). Over the past decade, there has been a trend toward nonoperative treatment of calcaneus fractures including prolonged immobilization of up to 3 months. This frequently leads to calcaneus malunions which can compromise lower extremity appearance and function. Potential sequelae include a widened, short, and varus heel that can lead to subtalar arthritis, subfibular impingement, shoewear difficulties, and limited ankle motion with anterior impingement. Operative indications are not clear, however, surgical intervention can potentially mitigate malunion sequelae despite higher incidences of wound complications. Functional outcomes following operative management have been shown to be worse in those who are older, obese, tobacco consumers, manual laborers, and receiving workers' compensation.

*Buckley et al performed a multicenter, prospective, randomized study of over 300 displaced calcaneus fractures comparing nonoperative versus operative treatment.* Overall, the outcomes after nonoperative treatment were not different from those after operative treatment. However, when stratifying groups, there were improved outcomes in the operative group in at least one measure after intraarticular calcaneal fractures in women, age <30, non-smoker, light work laborers, single, simple displaced intra-articular calcaneal fractures, higher pre-operative Böhler's angle, and non-workers' compensation patients.

*Bajammal et al reviewed the highest available evidence on intra-articular calcaneus fractures and* **found** Moderate Evidence (Grade B) suggesting the potential benefit of operative treatment in women, patients who are not receiving workers' compensation, younger males, patients with higher preoperative Böhler's angle, patients with a lighter workload, and those with a single, simple displaced intra-articular fracture.

*Rammelt et al review the operative treatment of calcaneal fractures.* Notably poorer outcomes are associated with open fractures, delayed reduction after more than 14 days, and individual risk factors such as high body mass index and smoking.

Illustration A depicts the Sanders Classification Types I-IV. This classification system is based on the number of intraarticular fracture lines and their location on semicoronal CT images through the posterior facet.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer A: Normal Bohler's angle is 20-40 degrees. An angle <0 degrees suggests more displacement, energy, and soft tissue trauma leading to an overall poorer prognosis.
Answer B: Men treated operatively result in inferior clinical outcomes compared to women.
Answer D: Worker’s compensation patients have inferior outcomes to non-worker’s compensation patients.
Answer E: Sanders Type IV fractures are the severely comminuted kind giving rise to worse clinical outcomes. Some surgeons even advocate for primary arthrodesis in this setting.
