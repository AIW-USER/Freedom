## Question

A 28-day-old female infant presents to your clinic. She and her mother were referred to your by her pediatrician with concerns for hip dysplasia. The patient is her mother's firstborn, and her perinatal course was complicated by a breech presentation leading to a difficult vaginal birth. The patient is in the 98th percential for weight and 95th percentile for length. There is no family history of hip dysplasia. Dynamic ultrasonography is performed in your clinic and a representative image is available for review in Figure A. Based on the patient's history and imaging findings, which of the following best describes the recommended management of this patient?

*Figure A*

## Options

1. The patient has Graf class I hips, and should be treated with a Pavlik Harness
2. The patient has Graf class II hips, and should be treated with open reduction and spica immobilization
3. The patient has Graf class II hips, and should either be followed with repeat ultrasonography in 3 weeks or treated with a Pavlik harness
4. The patient has Graf class III hips, and should be followed with repeat ultrasonography in 3 weeks
5. The patient has Graf class IV hips, and should be followed with repeat ultrasonography in 3 weeks

## Correct option

**3. The patient has Graf class II hips, and should either be followed with repeat ultrasonography in 3 weeks or treated with a Pavlik harness**

## Answer explanation

This patient has Graf class II hips on screening ultrasonography (alpha angle = 48°, beta angle = 72°). Given her breech presentation and the current imaging findings, the patient should either be followed with repeat ultrasonography in 3 weeks or treated with a Pavlik harness, depending on surgeon preference (Answer 3).

Developmental Dysplasia of the Hip (DDH) is a disorder of abnormal development resulting in dysplasia, subluxation and possible dislocation of the hip secondary to capsular laxity and mechanical instability. It represents the most common orthopaedic disorder in newborns and preferentially affects females over males in an 8:1 ratio. Risk factors for the development of DDH include being a firstborn child, female sex, breech presentation, a positive family history, oligohydramnios, macrosomia and swaddling.

Ultrasound represents the primary imaging modality from birth to 4 months of age, although it may produce erroneous results if performed too early. Current recommendations are for a screening ultrasound examination at 6 weeks in patients who are considered high risk, even in the face of a normal physical examination. Ultrasound results are classified according to the Graf classification system (Illustration A). This system derives its results from the measurement of two angles (Illustration B). The first, the alpha angle, represents the angle subtended by lines drawn across the bony acetabulum and the ilium. The second angle is the beta angle, which is the angle subtended by lines drawn across the labrum and the ilium. Normal alpha angle values are above 60° and normal beta angle values are below 55°. The optimal management of Graf II hips (which this patient has) is variable, with some surgeons initiating Pavlik harness treatment in high-risk individuals and others taking a more conservative approach with close-interval ultrasound follow-up.

*Young et al. provide a review surrounding a team-based approach to the diagnosis and management of DDH.* *The authors stress the importance of team-based care, as timely diagnosis and appropriate treatment is paramount to achieving good patient outcomes.* **The authors note** that the frequency and duration of surveillance are dependent on a variety of factors and that the appropriate treatment ranges from radiographic surveillance all the way to surgical intervention with femoral and/or pelvic osteotomies.

*Morris et al. performed an investigation on the occurrence of late hip dysplasia following initial normal ultrasonographic screening in babies born via a breech presentation.* *Their study population included ninety babies born over a 2 year period.* *All of the babies have a normal initial physical and ultrasonographic examination and 75% were available for final follow-up.* At 13 months, the overall rate of radiographic dysplasia was 7.4%. **The authors concluded** that in breech-born babies, further clinical and radiographic follow-up is indicated, as the possibility for the development of late DDH does exist.

*Brusalis et al. also investigated the incidence of DDH in breech-born infants following an initial normal ultrasound.* *Their study population consisted of 94 hips in 47 breech-born infants.* All infants had normal ultrasonographic findings at a mean age of 6.9 weeks. At a mean follow-up of 6.4 months, 10/94 (10.6%) hips met the diagnostic criteria for DDH (defined as an acetabular index ≥ 30°). **The authors conclude** that even in the setting of a normal screening ultrasound, follow-up surveillance is necessary in breech-born babies, as late dysplasia occurs at a rate of approximately 5-10%.

Incorrect Answers
Answer 1: the patient has class II hips. Based on the Graf classification, this patient would not require Pavlik harness treatment.
Answer 2: while the patient has class II hips, open reduction and immobilization is the incorrect intervention at this time. Some surgeons, however, may consider initiation of Pavlik harness treatment in Graf II hips.
Answer 4: the patient has class II, not III, hips. The correct management of a class III hip would be the initiation of Pavlik harness treatment.
Answer 5: the patient has class II, not IV, hips. The treatment of class IV hips depends on the patient's age and degree of subluxation/dislocation.
