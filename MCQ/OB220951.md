## Question

A 4-month-old infant is brought to the pediatric orthopaedic clinic for evaluation of hypotonia and generalized weakness. Genetic testing confirms a diagnosis of Spinal Muscular Atrophy (SMA) Type 1, revealing a homozygous deletion of the SMN1 gene. The parents are discussing emerging treatment options, including a therapy that is administered intrathecally and works by modifying the splicing of the SMN2 gene to increase the production of functional SMN protein. Which of the following therapeutic agents matches the mechanism of action and route of administration described by the parents?

## Options

1. Albuterol
2. Nusinersen
3. Prednisone
4. Risdiplam
5. Zolgensma (Onasemnogene abeparvovec)

## Correct option

**2. Nusinersen**

## Answer explanation

Nusinersen is an antisense oligonucleotide that modifies the splicing of the SMN2 pre-mRNA to include exon 7, increasing full-length SMN protein production, and must be administered intrathecally because it does not cross the blood-brain barrier (Answer 2).

Spinal Muscular Atrophy (SMA) is caused by a loss-of-function mutation in the SMN1 gene. A nearly identical gene, SMN2, exists but typically fails to produce sufficient functional protein due to a C-to-T transition in exon 7 that disrupts a splicing enhancer, leading to exon 7 exclusion. Nusinersen is an antisense oligonucleotide (ASO) designed to bind to a specific sequence in the SMN2 pre-mRNA. This binding masks the splicing silencer, promoting the inclusion of exon 7 and allowing the SMN2 gene to produce full-length, functional SMN protein. Because antisense oligonucleotides are large molecules that do not readily cross the blood-brain barrier, Nusinersen requires repeated intrathecal injections to reach the motor neurons in the central nervous system.

*Butterfield reviewed the landscape of SMA treatments and the impact of newborn screening on clinical outcomes.* **He noted** that the approval of therapies like nusinersen and gene therapy has transformed SMA from a fatal disease into a treatable condition, necessitating urgent diagnosis. **He concluded** that early intervention is critical, as presymptomatic treatment significantly improves motor milestones and survival.

*Nicolau et al. provided a comprehensive overview of the pathophysiology, clinical classification, and therapeutic advances in SMA.* **They noted** that nusinersen targets SMN2 splicing to increase the amount of functional protein, while onasemnogene abeparvovec (Zolgensma) uses a viral vector to replace the SMN1 gene entirely. **They concluded** that understanding the distinct mechanisms and delivery routes of these therapies is essential for optimizing patient management.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: While sometimes used as an adjunct to increase SMN protein levels, albuterol is a beta-agonist administered orally, not intrathecally, and is not a primary disease-modifying splicing therapy.
Answer 3: Corticosteroids like prednisone are used in Duchenne muscular dystrophy, but are not a standard disease-modifying treatment for the underlying genetic defect in SMA.
Answer 4: Risdiplam shares the mechanism of modifying SMN2 splicing to include exon 7, but it is a small molecule administered orally, not intrathecally.
Answer 5: Zolgensma is a gene replacement therapy delivered via a one-time intravenous infusion (for systemic distribution) that introduces a functional copy of the SMN1 gene, rather than modulating SMN2 splicing.
