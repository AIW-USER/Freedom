## Question

A 41-year-old female presents to the clinic for follow-up of her left knee arthroscopy, which involved addressing a cartilaginous defect on her medial femoral condyle. The procedure in question resulted in the formation of predominantly type I collagen within the defect. Which of the following techniques results in this collagen composition?

## Options

1. Microfracture
2. Autologous chondrocyte implantation
3. Osteochondral allograft transplantation
4. Osteochondral autograft transplantation
5. Mosaicplasty

## Correct option

**1. Microfracture**

## Answer explanation

This 41-year-old female underwent a cartilage restoration procedure resulting in predominantly type I collagen filling the defect. The procedure most consistent with this effect is microfracture (Answer 1).

Isolated cartilaginous defects are uncommon conditions, but have become increasingly common in athletes, estimated at 36%. The mechanism of injury creating these defects typically arises from traumatic etiologies. However, chronic, repetitive overload has been described as a causative factor. Regardless of origin, treatment is indicated for those who fail nonoperative management (rest, NSAIDs, therapy) or sustain defects as a result of trauma. The treatment algorithm can be dichotomized based on size, with defects less than four cm² typically being treated with debridement and microfracture or osteochondral autograft transfer, whereas defects greater than four cm² being treated with osteochondral allografts or autologous chondrocyte implantation (keep in mind this threshold does vary within the literature, with some proposing a threshold of two cm²). Of the listed treatments, microfracture results primarily in fibrocartilage (type I collagen), which possesses a higher concentration of collagen, thereby making the tissue stiffer. These mechanical differences, while better than an unfilled defect, are considered inferior to articular cartilage and typically result in clinical deterioration over time.

*Carballo et al. reviewed the architecture of articular cartilage and its four zones (superficial/tangential, middle/transitional, deep/radial, and calcified zones) and their biomechanical nuances.* *The authors then examine the role of inflammatory mediators and their impact on articular cartilage function,* **noting** IL-1β and TNF-α induce a myriad of proinflammatory cytokines, including matrix metalloproteinase-13 (MMP-13), the MMP most produced in osteoarthritis. **The authors conclude** a better understanding of the signaling pathways is required to improve technologies and more effectively treat cartilaginous lesions.

Drs. Simon and Jackson review articular cartilage, the types of injury that occur, and currently available treatments for cartilaginous defects. The authors note that articular cartilage defects that remain isolated from the blood supply and marrow space typically do not elicit a repair response, which serves as the basis for microfracture, which includes penetrating the marrow with an awl to allow for marrow extravasation into the joint, resulting in fibrocartilage formation within the defect. The authors conclude that defects, regardless of defect size and type of treatment, should be treated with ligamentous integrity and alignment in mind to improve outcomes.

*Mehran et al. performed a review seeking to examine functional outcomes after cartilaginous restoration procedures within the knee in high-level athletes.* *The authors detail the surgical options and associated outcomes with each respective technique.* **The authors noted** that with microfracture, patients tend to experience an improvement in symptoms. However, outcomes begin to falter at five years following the procedure. Nonetheless, approximately 78.6% of patients successfully return to play at an average of 293 days, with baseball players having the highest return-to-sport rates. **The authors conclude** that sports demand has a large impact on the patient's ability to return to sport.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Autologous chondrocyte implantation has been shown to result in both types I and II collagen after maturation, but type II collagen predominates.
Answers 3, 4, and 5: Osteochondral allograft transplantation and osteochondral autograft transplantation/mosaicplasty involve the direct transfer of articular (type II) cartilage into the defect
