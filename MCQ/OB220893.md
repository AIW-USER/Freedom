## Question

A 64-year-old otherwise healthy woman presents back to the office three months after undergoing a bunionectomy. She is complaining that her hallux is now drifting medially, and it is difficult for her to wear shoes; on exam, the hallux is slightly crossing midline. She has full but mildly painful range of motion at the metatarsophalangeal (MTP) joint. Her intermetatarsal angle (IMA) is 6 degrees, hallux valgus angle (HVA) is -19 degrees, and her distal metatarsal articular angle is 6 degrees. Which of the following surgical errors most likely led to her problem?

## Options

1. Tibial sesamoidectomy
2. Excessive medial capsular release
3. Excessively large proximal metatarsal medial opening wedge osteotomy
4. Inadequate resection of metatarsal medial eminence
5. Selection of distal soft tissue procedures over MTP fusion

## Correct option

**3. Excessively large proximal metatarsal medial opening wedge osteotomy**

## Answer explanation

This patient has iatrogenic hallux varus, which was likely caused by overcorrection of her intermetatarsal angle (IMA) during the index procedure. An excessively large proximal opening wedge osteotomy would lead to overcorrection of the IMA. (Answer 3).

Hallux varus is a relatively uncommon clinical condition of the foot that has several potential causes. The disease is caused by abnormal musculotendinous forces exerted across the MTP joint, leading to medial deviation of the hallux. Most commonly, this is an iatrogenic problem after hallux valgus surgery. Overtightening of the medial structures, overzealous release of the lateral structures, or overcorrection of bony anatomy may all cause hallux varus. Treatment is dependent on the time after index surgery, the presence of a rigid or flexible deformity, and the initial cause of the iatrogenic problem.

*Mohan et al. provide a literature review of hallux varus, including the pathophysiology, clinical and radiographic assessment, and treatment options.* **They emphasize** that surgical management is based on three general tenets, addressing soft tissue imbalance, bony correction, and/or MTP joint arthrodesis or resection arthroplasty. These procedures are selected based on the underlying pathology leading to hallux varus.

*Sferra et al. published an editorial article describing their expert opinion on the causes and treatment of hallux varus.* **They emphasize** that overcorrection of the IMA, overtightening of the medial structures, and overlaxity of the lateral structures, including overzealous fibular sesamoidectomy, all may lead to iatrogenic hallux varus. They describe various surgical techniques for treating this problem.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1, 2: Fibular sesamoidectomy or excessive lateral capsular release would be most likely to contribute to hallux varus
Answer 4: Overresection of the medial eminence is most likely to contribute to hallux varus
Answer 5: This patient has full MTP motion and minimal pain, and likely was not indicated for MTP fusion in the index surgery
