## Question

A professional endurance athlete is looking to optimize energy utilization, delay fatigue, and enhance recovery during prolonged training sessions. Which supplementation strategy is most evidence-based for improving endurance performance?

## Options

1. Caffeine pre-workout, sodium bicarbonate pre-workout, and BCAAs intra-workout
2. Creatine pre-workout, glutamine post-workout, and antioxidants post-workout
3. Carnitine pre-workout, BCAAs intra-workout, and glutamine post-workout
4. Sodium bicarbonate pre-workout, carnitine pre-workout, and antioxidants pre-workout

## Correct option

**1. Caffeine pre-workout, sodium bicarbonate pre-workout, and BCAAs intra-workout**

## Answer explanation

Ans: A

EXPLANATION: -

1. Energy & Performance Enhancers (For Endurance Athletes)

Caffeine: Increases fat oxidation and endurance performance by stimulating the central nervous system and reducing perceived exertion.
Sodium Bicarbonate: Helps buffer lactic acid accumulation, delaying fatigue in prolonged high-intensity efforts.

2. Muscle Recovery & Fatigue Reduction

Branched-Chain Amino Acids (BCAAs): Helps preserve muscle mass and delay central fatigue by competing with tryptophan uptake in the brain.
Glutamine: May support immune function, but its direct impact on performance is less significant.

3. Fat Metabolism & Oxidative Stress

Carnitine: Theoretically increases fat oxidation, but research shows limited direct performance benefits in trained athletes.
Antioxidants (Vitamin C, E, Polyphenols): Help reduce oxidative stress, but excessive intake may impair training adaptations by blunting mitochondrial biogenesis.

Why is Option A Correct?

Caffeine (Pre-Workout): Enhances fat oxidation and endurance capacity.
Sodium Bicarbonate (Pre-Workout): Helps buffer metabolic acidosis, delaying fatigue in intense endurance sessions.
BCAAs (Intra-Workout): Helps reduce muscle breakdown and prevent central fatigue during prolonged training.

Why Not Other Options?
Option B: Creatine is less useful for endurance athletes, as it mainly benefits short bursts of power.
Option C: Carnitine has inconsistent results in improving endurance performance.
Option D: Pre-workout antioxidants may impair adaptation rather than enhance performance.
