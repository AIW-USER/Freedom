## Question

A 5-year-old male presents with progressively worsening left genu varum that has been present since birth. Due to psychosocial reasons, the patient was unable to seek treatment until now. The current radiograph is shown in figure A. The patient undergoes a proximal tibial valgus osteotomy. At 2-year follow-up, there is a progression of genu varum to 8 degrees of varus. What is the likely reason for this progression?

![[q2190-a-blount.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Nutritional vitamin D deficiency
2. Medial tibial physeal bar
3. Overcorrection of tibia vara at the time of the surgery
4. Malpositioning of proximal tibial plate
5. Age of 5 at the time of surgical intervention

## Correct option

**2. Medial tibial physeal bar**

## Answer explanation

The patient likely has the progression of tibia vara after proximal tibia valgus osteotomy due to the presence of a medial tibial physeal bar due to severe Blount's disease.

Infantile Blount's disease is abnormal varus of the proximal tibia which occurs before 5 years of age. The etiology is unclear, but Blount's is associated with African-American, female, and obese patients. Early walking has been speculated to be a cause, leading to increased medial tibial stresses leading to deformity. Initial treatment in early Langenskiöld stages I and II is bracing before the age of 3 years. In more severe stages and ages beyond 4 years, proximal tibial valgus osteotomy and/or growth modulation are recommended treatment options. In severe cases, a physeal bar may form on the medial physis which may cause persistent or worsening varus after an osteotomy. This may necessitate bar resection and/or hemiepiphysiodesis of the lateral proximal tibia.

*Brooks et al. reviewed the diagnosis and management of genu varum in children.* **They reported** that infantile Blount's disease is highly suggestive with a metaphyseal-diaphyseal angle >16 degrees, which necessitates treatment. In severe cases of infantile Blount's disease, a physeal bar can form on the medial tibia which can result in angular deformities and limb length discrepancies in patients treated with a proximal tibial osteotomy.

*Levine and Drennan performed a retrospective study of 88 patients evaluated for infantile Blount's disease.* **They reported** that 29/30 patients with a metaphyseal-diaphyseal angle >11 degrees later developed radiographic changes consistent with tibia vara, whereas, only 3/58 patients with angles < 11 degrees developed similar changes. **The authors concluded** that the metaphyseal-diaphyseal angle is an effective early diagnostic tool for infantile Blount's disease.

Figure A is the AP standing mechanical alignment views with severe left-sided Blount's disease with beaking and fragmentation of the medial tibial plateau. Illustration A is a diagram depicting the metaphyseal-diaphyseal (Drennan) angle. Illustration B is a diagram of the Langenskiöld classification.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Vitamin D deficiency may cause tibia vara in infantile patients, but is unlikely to be the cause of deformity progression in this patient after undergoing surgical correction of the deformity.
Answer 3: Overcorrecting the deformity into 10 degrees of valgus is an ideal surgical goal as there is usually a persistent degree of varus that continues after a proximal tibial osteotomy that eventually corrects to a physiologic valgus. Overcorrecting the deformity, in this case, would not lead to progressively worsening varus following treatment.
Answer 4: Malpositioning of the proximal tibial plate would be unlikely to cause progressively worsening varus after a proximal tibial osteotomy.
Answer 5: The age at the time of surgical correction has not been found to cause progressively worsening genu varum after a proximal tibial osteotomy.
