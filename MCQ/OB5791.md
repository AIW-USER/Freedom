## Answer explanation

The video shows Gower sign, this is most classically associated with Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD). DMD is an X-linked recessive disorder (only males affected). This is represented by Figure D. Gower's sign can also be observed in other conditions of proximal muscular weakness including Becker's muscular dystrophy, spinal muscular atrophy, limb girdle muscular dystrophy, and sarcoglycanopathy.

Duchenne muscular dystrophy is characterized by absent dystrophin protein (gene on Xp21.2). Disease onset is between 2-6 years old. Patients have delayed motor milestones and develop calf pseudohypertrophy, equinovarus foot deformity, joint contractures and scoliosis. Lab tests show markedly elevated CPK levels (up to 100 times normal), while muscle biopsy shows connective tissue infiltration and absent dystrophin staining. Patients lose the ability to walk by 10-12 years of age, and life expectancy is around 25 years of age.

*Shieh reviewed muscular dystrophies and genetic myopathies.* **He states** that DMD is the most common muscular dystrophy (1:3600). Dystrophin links the actin cytoskeleton to membrane-bound proteins, protecting the muscle from strain-related damage during exertion. Treatment is with (1) steroids (improves muscle strength), (2) ACE inhibitors (slow cardiomyopathy), and (3) noninvasive ventilator support (improves life expectancy).
