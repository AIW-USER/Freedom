## Answer explanation

This patient has a joint-depressed calcaneus fracture. The most common acute, concurrent pathology associated with this injury is peroneal tendon instability due to a torn superior peroneal retinaculum.

Calcaneus fractures are the most common fractured tarsal bone and are associated with a high degree of morbidity and disability. Approximately 20% of patients with a calcaneus fracture have concurrent peroneal tendon instability. This is likely secondary to a lateral wall blowout of the calcaneus seen in typical joint-depression type fractures. Imaging may serve useful in the diagnosis as peroneal tendon dislocation may be visualized on the axial CT view.

*Toussaint et al. retrospectively reviewed peroneal tendon displacement (subluxation or dislocation) accompanying an intra-articular calcaneal fracture.* **They report** that that peroneal tendon displacement was identified in 28% of the calcaneal fracture cases, and the presence of tendon displacement was significantly associated with joint-depression fractures compared with tongue-type fractures. **They concluded** that CT images showed a 28% prevalence of peroneal tendon displacement accompanying intra-articular calcaneal fractures.

*Mahmoud et al. reviewed peroneal tendon instability associated with intraarticular calcaneal fractures.* **They report** that the overall prevalence of peroneal instability (PI) in association with a calcaneus fracture was 29.3%. An increasing prevalence of PI increased the severity of the calcaneus fractures, 5.4% in Sanders I, 19% in Sanders II, 39.4% in Sanders III, and 49.5% in Sanders IV. **They concluded** that the global reported prevalence of peroneal tendon instability associated with intraarticular calcaneal fractures is high and increases with increasing severity of the calcaneus fracture.

Figure A is the lateral radiograph demonstrating a calcaneus fracture with joint depression. Figure B is a Harris view radiograph demonstrating varus malalignment and widening of the calcaneus. Figure C is a diagram demonstrating the lateral ankle anatomy. Illustration A is the same illustration depicted in Figure C with the anatomical structures appropriately labeled.
