## Question

## Options

1. Posteroinferior acetabular cartilage lesion
2. Eburnation of the posterosuperior quadrant of the acetabulum
3. Cartilage delamination of the anterosuperior chondrolabral junction that precedes labral tearing at the chondrolabral junction
4. Cartilage fraying posterosuperior femoral head
5. Tearing of the anterosuperior labrum at the labral-capsular junction that precedes cartilage delamination in the anterosuperior quadrant

## Correct option

**3. Cartilage delamination of the anterosuperior chondrolabral junction that precedes labral tearing at the chondrolabral junction**

## Answer explanation

Cam lesions can lead to cartilage delamination at the chondrolabral junction that precedes labral tearing in the anterosuperior quadrant.

Femoroacetabular impingement (FAI) is the result of abnormal contact between the femur and acetabulum that leads to chondral and labrum wear with resulting hip pain and decreased range of motion. Cam-type lesions are the result of aspherical femoral head, usually from decreased sphericity of the anterosuperior femoral head-neck junction, that impinges on the anterior superior acetabulum. This typically leads to cartilage delamination followed by labral tearing at the chondrolabral junction in the anterosuperior quadrant. Typically, elite athletes are prone to the development of cam lesions which is linked to increased training intensity during skeletal development. Treatment involves initial conservative care with physical therapy and NSAIDs, followed by surgical hip dislocation or hip arthroscopy for femoral neck osteoplasty and labral repair or debridement.

*Frank et al. performed a systematic review of 26 studies and 2,114 patients assessing the prevalence of FAI in asymptomatic patients.* **The authors reported** an overall prevalence of a Cam lesion in 37% of patients, 54.8% in athletes, and a 67& incidence of pincer lesions in asymptomatic patients. **The authors concluded** that FAI morphology frequently occurs in asymptomatic patients with athletes being predisposed.

*Skendzel et al. performed a study of a consecutive series of 559 patients to determine the effects of joint space on outcomes following hip arthroscopy.* **They reported** an 86% THA conversion rate at 5-years follow-up for patients with joint space ≤2 mm compared to 16% conversion rate in patients with >2 mm, most of whom were found to Outerbridge grade 3 or 4 chondral lesions on arthroscopy. **The authors concluded** that hip arthroscopy affords better outcomes in patients with preserved joint space (>2 mm on radiographs).

*Ganz et al. reviewed FAI and the associated cartilage injuries potentially leading to hip osteoarthritis.* *The authors described anterosuperior chondral delamination with labral fraying in cam impingement and posteroinferior chondral injury, termed "contre-coup" injury, with anterosuperior labral degeneration with pincer impingement.* **They concluded** understanding of the mechanism by which impingement leads to osteoarthritis can better direct treatments for joint preservation.

Figure A and B are the AP and lateral radiographs of the right hip demonstrating a cam lesion of the anterosuperior femoral neck.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Cartilage lesions of the posteroinferior quadrant are associated with pincer lesions and are termed contre-coup cartilage injuries.
Answer 2: Eburnation of the posterosuperior quadrant of the acetabulum is associated with osteoarthritis of the hip.
Answer 4: Posterosuperior femoral head cartilage fraying is associated with osteoarthritis of the hip.
Answer 5: Cam lesions are generally associated with a cartilage lesion that precedes a labral injury originating at the chondrolabral junction.
