Chapter: Sports Medicine MCQs — Drugs And Dopping In Sports

## Question

Gene doping was banned by WADA in the year

## Options

1. 2001
2. 2003
3. 2006
4. 2008

## Correct option

**2. 2003**

## Answer explanation

Gene doping is a non therapeutic use of gene therapy, whereby, instead of injecting DNA into a person's body for the purpose of restoring some function related to a damaged or missing gene, DNA is inserted via vector for the purpose of enhancing athletic performance.

Gene Delivery Systems mostly employed include:

Viral vectors: Efficient carriers but immune response may reduce effect

Non Viral vectors (Bacterial plasmids): No immune response

Gene transfer via the viral vectors is called transduction while transfer via the non-viral vectors is called transfection.

The genes commonly targeted include:

- Improving Strength
- IGF-1
- Myostatin
- Improving Endurance (Stamina)
- EPO (Erythropoetin)
- PPAR ꙋ that converts fast type 1 muscle fibers to slow type 2 muscle fibers
- PEP carboxylase
- VEGF
- Enhancing tissue repair
- BMPs
- Endorphins
-

Methods of detection of Gene Doping

- Direct approach: Targets detection of doping vector
- Indirect approach: detects consequence of gene doping

- Transcriptonomics: Measures changes in concentrations of mRNA
- Proteonomics: Evaluates the protein set expressed from the genome
- Metabolomics: Profiling non protein LMW metabolites

Risks posed by Gene Doping to sports person include:

- Immune response to viral vectors (can lead to SIRS)
- Autoimmune response to a recombinant protein
- Lack of expression of artificial protein
- Insertional mutagenesis

Owing to the risks, the World Anti-Doping Agency (WADA) prohibited gene doping in 2003 and added it to the list of banned doping practices.

Ans : B
