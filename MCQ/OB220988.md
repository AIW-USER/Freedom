## Question

A 6-year-old child presents after a playground fall with elbow pain, swelling, and deformity. Radiographs show an extension-type supracondylar humerus fracture with 7 millimeters of coronal plane displacement on the anteroposterior radiograph. This degree of displacement increases the risk of which of the following?

## Options

1. Cubitus varus deformity
2. Iatrogenic ulnar nerve injury
3. Loss of reduction after closed pinning
4. Need for an open reduction
5. Vascular compromise requiring fasciotomy

## Correct option

**4. Need for an open reduction**

## Answer explanation

Coronal plane displacement increases the risk of needing an open reduction (Answer 4).

In extension-type supracondylar humerus fractures, greater coronal-plane displacement is associated with instability and difficulty achieving and maintaining anatomic reduction with closed techniques. Several studies have shown that greater medial or lateral displacement correlates with soft tissue interposition, periosteal stripping, and rotational malalignment, all of which can prevent successful closed reduction.

A threshold of approximately 7 mm of coronal displacement has been identified as a predictor of failure of closed reduction and a higher likelihood of requiring open reduction. Recognizing this risk preoperatively helps with surgical planning, family counseling, and timely decision-making to avoid repeated reduction attempts that may increase swelling and neurovascular risk.

*Latario et al. conducted a study to identify fracture characteristics associated with conversion from attempted closed reduction to open reduction in pediatric supracondylar humerus fractures.* The final multivariate analysis demonstrated that only flexion type and coronal displacement greater than 7 mm were significant preoperative factors for conversion to open reduction. **They concluded** that specific radiographic features can help predict failure of closed reduction and should inform preoperative planning and counseling.

*Sun et al. evaluated clinical and radiographic risk factors associated with failure of closed reduction in pediatric supracondylar humerus fractures.* **The authors found** that severe displacement, coronal plane translation, and rotational instability were significantly associated with unsuccessful closed reduction. **They concluded** that recognizing these factors preoperatively may help anticipate the need for open reduction and reduce intraoperative delays or complications.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: Cubitus varus deformity is most commonly related to coronal malreduction rather than the initial degree of displacement alone.
Answer 2: Iatrogenic ulnar nerve injury is more closely associated with the medial pin placement technique than with the fracture displacement magnitude.
Answer 3: Loss of reduction after closed pinning depends more on pin configuration and stability than on initial coronal displacement.
Answer 5: Vascular compromise requiring fasciotomy is typically related to fracture severity and posterior displacement, not to coronal displacement measurement.
