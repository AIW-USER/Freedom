## Question

Figure A is a C5-6 level axial MRI image of a 38-year-old male who is presenting with left-sided upper extremity symptoms. He has a negative Hoffman test. Which of the following additional physical examination findings would be expected in this patient?

![[q215543-a-herniated-cervical-disc.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Brachioradialis weakness
2. Elbow extension weakness
3. Middle and index finger distal phalanx flexion weakness
4. Middle finger paresthesia
5. Wrist flexion weakness

## Correct option

**1. Brachioradialis weakness**

## Answer explanation

This patient's MRI finding are remarkable for a C5-6 disc herniation. A C6 radiculopathy would be expected, as suggested by brachioradialis weakness.

Cervical spine nerve roots exit above their corresponding pedicle. Therefore, the C6 nerve root exits at the level of the C5-6 disk. A posterolateral or foraminal disk herniation at this level would compress the C6 nerve root. Such manifestations of a C6 nerve root compression would include brachioradialis and wrist extension weakness, diminished brachioradialis reflex, biceps weakness, and paresthesia in thumb and index fingers. Conversely, a central herniation in the cervical spine may cause both radicular symptoms, as well as signs of myelopathy (ie. gait disturbances, clumsiness, bowel/bladder symptoms, hyperreflexia).

*Rhee et al. reviewed cervical radiculopathy.* **They reported** that acute cervical radiculopathy generally has a self-limited clinical course with up to a 75% rate of spontaneous improvement; therefore, nonsurgical management is recommended as the initial treatment modality. **They concluded** that when nonsurgical treatment fails to relieve symptoms or if a significant neurologic deficit exists, surgical decompression may be necessary and provides relief of arm pain in 80% to 90% of cases.

*Davidson et al. reviewed cervical monoradiculopathies due to extradural compression in patients which had relief of radicular pain with shoulder abduction.* **They reported** that pain relief obtained with this maneuver seems to occur by decreasing tension on the nerve root. **They concluded** that this clinical sign is an indicator of significant cervical extradural compression and radicular disease.

Figure A is the axial MRI image demonstrating a large left posterior paracentral and lateral recess disc extrusion at C5/6 level resulting in indentation of thecal sac and stenosis of the corresponding left neural foramina.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Elbow extension weakness may be seen with triceps weakness suggesting a C7 radiculopathy
Answer 3: Middle and index finger distal phalanx flexion weakness would suggest a C8 radiculopathy
Answers 4&5: Middle finger paresthesia and wrist flexion weakness would be seen with a C7 radiculopathy
