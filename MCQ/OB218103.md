## Question

A 72-year-old male underwent anatomic total shoulder arthroplasty (aTSA) two months ago and has had persistent pain since the surgery. Radiographs demonstrate that the implant is in good position without evidence of hardware failure, fracture, or superior displacement of the humeral head. Aspiration is performed and suspicion for infection is confirmed. What are the microbiological characteristics of the organism most likely recovered in this scenario?

## Options

1. Gram positive cocci, facultative anaerobe, coagulase positive
2. Gram positive cocci, facultative anaerobe, coagulase negative
3. Gram positive rod, facultative anaerobe
4. Gram negative rod, facultative anaerobe
5. Gram negative encapsulated rod

## Correct option

**3. Gram positive rod, facultative anaerobe**

## Answer explanation

Cutibacterium acnes (C. acnes) is the most common organism isolated in cases of periprosthetic shoulder infection and is a gram-positive rod, which is facultatively anaerobic.

Periprosthetic shoulder infection is an uncommon, but challenging problem for orthopedic surgeons. The most common organism isolated is C. acnes, and this is thought to be due to the high number of dermal sebaceous glands concentrated in the axilla. A high degree of suspicion is important in cases with persistent shoulder pain, especially in the presence of draining sinuses, systemic signs of infection, or an abnormal degree of stiffness. Aspiration of the shoulder is an important tool in cases where infection is suspected. Unfortunately, C. acnes can be difficult to detect and the sensitivity of aspiration is low, making synovial tissue cultures the favored gold standard for diagnosis. Cultures must be held and followed for 14-21 days, as C. acnes incubation time is longer than that of other bacteria. Irrigation and debridement, 1- or 2-stage revision, or resection arthroplasty are surgical options depending on the chronicity of the infection.

*Mercurio et al. reviewed the outcomes of revision surgery after periprosthetic shoulder infection,* **noting** this is one of the more challenging aspects of shoulder arthroplasty. **They noted** C. acnes was the most common organism isolated in shoulder infection and there were high rates of eradication with 1-stage revision (96%), 2-stage revision (86%), resection arthroplasty (85%), and permanent spacer placement (93%). **They ultimately recommended** a 1-stage revision due to a high eradication rate and low number of complications.

*Weber et al. also looked at the management of the infected shoulder prosthesis.* *There were 9 patients in their series who were treated primarily with either 2-stage revision or resection arthroplasty.* **They noted** pain was similar in both groups with resection arthroplasty having improved, but still poor abduction. **They recommended** isolation of the organism followed by early surgery to eradicate the infection as soon as possible.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Staphylococcus aureus is a gram-positive cocci, which is facultatively anaerobic and coagulase positive.
Answer 2: Staphylococcus epidermidis is a gram-positive cocci which is facultatively anaerobic and coagulase-negative.
Answer 4: E. Coli is a gram-negative rod which is a facultative anaerobe.
Answer 5: Klebsiella pneumonia is a gram-negative rod which is encapsulated.
