## Question

A 50-year-old male presents to the trauma bay following a MVC. He sustained the injury depicted in Figure A. Examination prior to closed reduction of the hip reveals an inability to dorsiflex the ankle or great toe. His examination is unchanged following closed reduction of the hip. He undergoes operative fixation the following morning. His neurological deficits remain unchanged following operative stabilization. Which of the following nerve root levels is most commonly involved with this injury?

![[q215603-a-tab-fx-dislocation.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. L4
2. L5
3. S1
4. S2
5. S3

## Correct option

**2. L5**

## Answer explanation

This patient sustained an acetabulum fracture with a posterior hip dislocation. The most commonly involved nerve root associated with this injury is L5.

The sciatic nerve is the largest single nerve in the body and is made up of neurological roots from the L4, L5, S1, S2 and S3 vertebral levels. The L5 nerve root contribution is believed to supply the tibialis anterior, extensor hallucis longus, extensor digitorum brevis, and the lateral head of gastrocnemius. In patients with surgically treated acetabular fractures and neurologic injury, the L5 nerve root is predominantly affected, having the greatest impact on the function of the common peroneal nerve branch of the sciatic nerve. Isolated injury to the common peroneal nerve branch of the sciatic nerve is more common than a complete sciatic nerve injury with these injuries.

*Simske et al. retrospectively reviewed the factors associated with nerve injury after acetabular fracture.* **They report** that 3.3% of patients with acetabular fractures experienced nerve injury with 78% resulting from trauma, many of which were associated with a posterior hip dislocation. **They further found** that the most common fracture pattern associated with nerve injury was transverse posterior wall (31% of injuries). Sixty-five percent of sciatic nerve injuries were to the common peroneal division only, while none were isolated to the tibial division. **They concluded** that posterior acetabulum fracture/dislocations are associated with traumatic nerve injury, although 25% of nerve injuries were iatrogenic. More than 25% of patients had no recorded nerve recovery.

*Bogdan et al. reviewed a series of operatively treated acetabular fractures with neurologic injury and tracked sensory and motor recovery.* **They report** that the most common fracture types were transverse + posterior wall (33%), posterior wall (23%), and both-column (23%). Nerve deficits associated with the following root levels were identified: 7 in L2-3, 18 in L4, 114 in L5, and 48 in S1. Forty-eight patients wore a brace at the final follow-up, all for an L5 root level deficit. **They concluded** that peripheral neurologic injury in operatively treated acetabular fractures occurs most commonly in the sciatic nerve distribution, with L5 root level deficits having only a 26% chance of full recovery.

Figure A is an AP radiograph of the pelvis demonstrating an acetabular fracture with an associated posterior hip dislocation. Illustration A depicts the nerve root contribution to the peroneal and tibial divisions of the sciatic nerve.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1,3-5: The sciatic nerve receives innervation from L4-S3, however the most commonly involved nerve root with an acetabulum fracture with associated posterior hip dislocation is the L5 nerve root
