## Question

A 48-year-old female falls while walking into her house after an evening out with co-workers, and notes immediate pain and deformity to her elbow. She is taken to the emergency department, and her radiographs are available for review in Figure A. Under conscious sedation, you perform a closed reduction. The elbow remains concentrically reduced throughout the arc of motion, and there is no appreciable instability with valgus stress. Post-reduction radiographs demonstrate a concentric reduction with no appreciable intra-articular loose bodies. What of the following strategies will offer the patient the best chance at achieving favorable post-injury outcomes?

![[q218051-a-posterolateralelbowdislocation.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Early operative intervention with repair of injured structures
2. Initiating mobilization within five to 10 days of injury
3. Initiating mobilization only after at least 14 days of immobilization
4. Splinting the injured extremity in more than 120° of flexion for at least three weeks
5. None of the above have been found to correlate with post-injury outcomes

## Correct option

**2. Initiating mobilization within five to 10 days of injury**

## Answer explanation

This patient has sustained a simple elbow dislocation. Given the stability of the elbow to valgus stress, the medial ligamentous structures are most likely in continuity, and no fractures are present. As a result, in order to maximize the likelihood of achieving favorable outcomes, the patient should begin formal physical therapy within five to 10 days of injury with a focus on early mobilization (Answer 2).

Elbow dislocations are common elbow injuries, and represent the most common major joint dislocation second to the shoulder. These injuries represent 10-25% of injuries to the elbow, and 80% of these injuries are posterolateral in nature. Elbow dislocations can be broadly classified as simple (no associated fracture, 50-60% of dislocations) or complex (dislocation with associated fracture, 40-50% of dislocations). Injury to the surrounding ligamentous and osseous stabilizers occurs in a predictable, circular, lateral-to-medial pattern, with the lateral collateral ligament (LCL) failing first and the medial collateral ligament (MCL) failing last. Injury to the LCL is usually an avulsion-type injury from the ligament origin on the lateral epicondyle, with mid-substance tears occurring far less frequently. Acute, simple dislocations are best managed with closed reduction with the initiation of formal physical therapy within five to 10 days. Although these patients were historically managed with 5 to 10 days of splinting in at least 90° of flexion prior to the initiation of therapy, there is ample evidence to suggest that formal immobilization is not required and that avoidance of splinting may reduce the risk of prolonged immobilization.

*Mehlhoff et al. provide a case series and comprehensive review of the treatment and outcomes of simple elbow dislocations in adult patients.* *Their study included 52 adults all of who sustained a simple elbow dislocation with a mean follow-up of 34.4 months.* **The authors noted** that prolonged immobilization following reduction was strongly associated with poor outcomes, and longer immobilization was associated with both a more pronounced flexion contracture and higher pain levels. Overall, **the authors conclude** that early active motion is the key factor in rehabilitation following simple dislocation, and that initial immobilization should be for no longer than 5-10 days.

*Anakwe et al. provide a case series of 110 patients who sustained a simple elbow dislocation.* *The mean follow-up for their cohort was 88 months, and the authors collected a variety of patient-reported outcome scores.* **The authors found** that outcomes were strongly associated with elbow range of motion, and both loss of elbow flexion and female sex were independent predictors of poorer outcomes. **The authors conclude** that while patients generally report favorable long-term functional outcomes following this injury, these injuries are not benign, and improperly managed patients frequently experience residual pain and elbow stiffness. Promisingly, however, **the authors conclude** that residual functional instability is rare and, if present, rarely limits elbow function.

Incorrect Answers.
Answer 1: acute, simple elbow dislocations with a stable arc of motion do not require acute operative intervention.
Answers 3 and 4: greater than 10 days of immobilization increases the likelihood of the patient experiencing arthrofibrosis and higher pain levels, and is inappropriate in the management of simple elbow dislocations.
Answer 5: early mobilization has been found to correlate with a greater post-injury range of motion and lower patient pain levels following acute, simple elbow dislocations.
