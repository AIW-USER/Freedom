## Question

A 70-year-old male undergoes an elective Total Hip Arthroplasty (THA). To prevent venous thromboembolism (VTE), the orthopedic surgeon prescribes an oral Factor Xa inhibitor that requires a twice-daily dosing schedule. Which of the following accurately describes the correct prophylactic dose, initiation schedule, and appropriate reversal agent for this specific medication?

## Options

1. Apixaban 5 mg PO twice daily, initiated immediately after wound closure; reversed by Protamine sulfate.
2. Rivaroxaban 10 mg PO once daily, initiated 6 to 10 hours postoperatively; reversed by Idarucizumab.
3. Apixaban 2.5 mg PO twice daily, initiated 12 to 24 hours postoperatively; reversed by Andexanet alfa.
4. Dabigatran 220 mg PO once daily, initiated 1 to 4 hours postoperatively; reversed by Andexanet alfa.

## Correct option

**3. Apixaban 2.5 mg PO twice daily, initiated 12 to 24 hours postoperatively; reversed by Andexanet alfa.**

## Answer explanation

Apixaban is an oral Factor Xa inhibitor indicated for VTE prophylaxis after hip or knee replacement. The standard prophylactic dose is 2.5 mg twice daily, starting 12-24 hours after surgery to balance bleeding risk. Its specific targeted antidote is Andexanet alfa, a recombinant modified Factor Xa molecule.

While Aspirin is an antiplatelet rather than an anticoagulant, contemporary orthopedic guidelines (like AAOS and ACCP) increasingly recommend it for standard VTE prophylaxis in standard-risk patients following total joint arthroplasty due to its lower bleeding profile and ease of use.

Key Clinical Considerations:

 - Timing Cruciality: Initiation times are highly dependent on establishing surgical hemostasis. If there is ongoing bleeding or a high risk of surgical site bleeding, the initiation of these medications should be delayed.

 - Renal Function: DOACs (especially Dabigatran and Rivaroxaban) require dosage adjustments or are contraindicated in patients with significant renal impairment (e.g., low Creatinine Clearance).
