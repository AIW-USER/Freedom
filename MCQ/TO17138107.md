Chapter: Nerve Injury MCQs — Miscellaneous Compression Neuropathies

## Question

The posterior interosseous nerve can be compressed in all of the following sites except?

## Options

1. The leash of Henry.
2. The arcade of Frohse.
3. The distal margin of the supinator muscle.
4. The ligament of Struthers

## Correct option

**4. The ligament of Struthers**

## Answer explanation

The posterior interosseous nerve is a branch of the radial nerve, which comes off the posterior cord of the brachial plexus. The posterior interosseous nerve supplies motor innervation to the posterior forearm. The terminal branch of the posterior interosseous nerve travels distally into the floor of the 4th dorsal compartment of the wrist to innervate the dorsal wrist capsule. Compression neuropathies of the radial nerve distal to the elbow include radial tunnel syndrome, posterior interosseous nerve syndrome, and Wartenberg syndrome.

Five potential sites of posterior interosseous nerve compression sites in the forearm include

- fibrous tissue anterior to the radiocapitellar joint (between the brachialis and brachioradialis)
- “leash of Henry” (these are recurrent radial vessels that fan out across the PIN at the level of the radial neck)
- extensor carpi radialis brevis edge- medio-proximal edge of the extensor carpi radialis brevis
- "arcade of Fröhse" - which is the proximal edge of the superficial portion of the supinator
- supinator muscle edge- distal edge of the supinator muscle

The ligament of Struthers is a fibrous band extending from a large bony projection of the humerus, known as the supracondylar process, to the medial epicondyle. It is probably present in less than 1% of humans, and may cause median nerve compression.

Presentation: Onset is insidious, often goes undiagnosed. Patients present with pain in the forearm and wrist with weakness in finger grip and wrist and finger movements. The site of pain depends on the site of compression. There may be atrophy of the involved muscles. Resisted supination may increase the pain. When a patient is asked to extend the wrist joint it deviates to the radial side due to intact ECRL (radial nerve.) and absent ECU (PIN).

Differentials-

Wartenberg's Syndrome- It is described as the entrapment of the superficial branch of the radial nerve with only sensory manifestations and no motor deficits.

Radial tunnel syndrome- Radial Tunnel Syndrome is a compressive neuropathy of the posterior interosseous nerve (PIN) at the level of proximal forearm (radial tunnel). So it involves the same sites of compression as PIN syndrome.

Patient presents with dorsoradial proximal forearm pain with maximum tenderness at 3-5 cm distal to lateral epicondyle. There are no motor or sensory symptoms. Pain increases with resisted long finger extension, resisted supination with elbow and wrist in extension and in passive pronation with wrist flexion.

Radial tunnel syndrome- Only pain, No sensory /motor symptoms

Wartenberg syndrome- Only pain and sensory symptoms, No motor symptoms

PIN compression syndrome- Only pain and Motor sign and symptoms, No sensory symptoms
