## Question

A 19-year-old college wrestler presents to the training room with a new skin lesion on his face after a meet last week. You note an area of erythema associated with a cluster of vesicles on his face. He states he just noticed it the same day. He has not undergone any treatment to this point. Which of the following is true of ability to return to play?

## Options

1. Immediate return to play after one dose of acyclovir
2. Immediate return to play when no new lesions have developed for 48 hours
3. Return to play after 48 hours of keflex
4. Return to play after 72 hours of trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole
5. Return to play after 5 days on valacyclovir

## Correct option

**5. Return to play after 5 days on valacyclovir**

## Answer explanation

This college wrestler has herpes gladiatorum which is a herpes simplex virus (HSV) infection of the face and he should not be allowed to return to play until no new lesions have been noted for 72 hours, current lesions are fully crusted over, and antiviral medication has been taken for 5 days.

Herpes gladiatorum infection occurs secondary to direct skin-to-skin contact with infected people. Wrestlers are the athletes most commonly affected. On exam, vesicles on an erythematous base are normally seen (Illustration A), which helps differentiate it from bacterial infections which typically appear as larger pustules. HSV infections should be treated with acyclovir or valacyclovir, which inhibit viral DNA polymerase. In order to be cleared to return to sport the athlete must be on the antiviral medication for 5 days, have had no new lesions in the past 72 hours, and have all current lesions scabbed over.

*Peterson et al. published a general overview of infectious diseases in contact sports.* *They discuss bacterial skin infections, HSV, molluscum, warts, fungal infections, and other pathology.* *They discuss each of these infections specifically and note that early diagnosis and appropriate management are key in treating the athlete and preventing diffuse outbreaks.*

Johnson specifically reviewed herpes gladiatorum. They noted that skin infections account for 10-15% of time-loss injuries in athletes at the collegiate level. They discuss specific guidelines put forth by the NCAA and NFHS which note how athletes with skin lesions should be treated and address return-to-play guidelines.

*Zinder et al. also discussed herpes gladiatorum and surveyed college wrestlers and athletic trainers nationwide.* **They noted** that 19% of college wrestlers in the conference surveyed reported a history of infection. Of importance, **they noted** that wrestlers who faced an opponent positive for a cutaneous HSV lesion were at high risk for developing an infection themselves (RR: 9.4).

Illustration A demonstrates a clinical photograph showing vesicles on an erythematous base characteristic of HSV infection.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Return to play should occur only after 5 days of antiviral treatment in combination with all lesions being scabbed over and ensuring no new lesions have formed over the prior 72 hours.
Answer 2: Return to play protocol for HSV skin infections includes no new lesions developing over the prior 72 hours in combination with appropriate antiviral treatment.
Answer 3 & 4: Patients with MRSA infections can return to play after 72 hours on antibiotics, one option which would include trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole.
