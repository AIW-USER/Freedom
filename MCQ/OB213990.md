## Question

A 64-year-old woman presents with severe symptoms of right carpal tunnel syndrome and undergoes an elective carpal tunnel release. Which of the following statements below is most accurate regarding postoperative analgesia for this operation?

## Options

1. Patients undergoing endoscopic carpal tunnel release require less pain medication, on average, than those undergoing open carpal tunnel release
2. Patients taking 600mg ibuprofen and 5mg oxycodone tablets have similar pain scores
3. On average, most patients consume >10 tablets of 5mg oxycodone or its equivalent
4. Most patients report taking opioid medications for 5 days or more
5. There is no difference in the rate of adverse events between oxycodone and tylenol

## Correct option

**2. Patients taking 600mg ibuprofen and 5mg oxycodone tablets have similar pain scores**

## Answer explanation

After soft-tissue hand surgical procedures like carpal tunnel release, several reports have demonstrated that ibuprofen, tylenol and opioid pain medications provide similar analgesia.

There is increasing awareness to decrease the prescription of opioids, which has increased dramatically in recent years in the United States. The current trend suggests that as a specialty, orthopaedic surgeons overprescribe opioid analgesics. It is crucial for patients to have appropriate analgesia, because inadequately controlled postoperative pain may result in difficulty with postoperative rehabilitation and return to work. However, evidence is increasingly describing the equivalency of NSAIDs and tylenol compared to opioids for controlling postoperative pain in hand surgery. Additionally, patients who are given opioids often report taking fewer than the prescribed amount. Given the immediate side-effect profile and the long-term repercussions of opioid abuse, consideration should be given to using non-narcotic medication with carpal tunnel release.

*Ilyas et al. reviewed pain management after carpal tunnel release by performing a double-blinded trial comparing tylenol, ibuprofen and oxycodone.* **They noted** that 600mg ibuprofen, 5mg oxycodone and 500mg tylenol all provided similar levels of analgesia in both open and endoscopic carpal tunnel release. Not only were, pain levels similar postoperatively, but the number of pills consumed was also similar. Interestingly, there were more adverse events in the oxycodone group, most commonly nausea.

*Peters et al. evaluated pain control and quantified the amount of leftover pain medication after outpatient carpal tunnel release.* **They reported** that the average number of opioid tablets consumed was 10, with more than half of patients consuming 2 or fewer tablets. **They also reported** that the average number of postoperative days of analgesic consumption was 2. **They concluded** that excessive opioid prescriptions are being prescribed after carpal tunnel surgery and recommended physicians re-evaluate their current standards and adopt responsible prescription practices.

*Kim et al. investigated opioid consumption following outpatient upper-extremity surgical procedures to determine opioid utilization patterns and to develop prescribing guidelines.* **They reported** that surgeons prescribed an average of 24 pills, and patients consumed an average of 8.1 pills (34% utilization rate). Furthermore, patients undergoing soft-tissue procedures required fewer opioids (5.1 pills for 2.2 days) compared with fracture procedures (13.0 pills for 4.5 days) or joint procedures (14.5 pills for 5.0 days). **They concluded** that patients are being prescribed approximately 3 times greater opioid medications than needed following upper-extremity surgical procedures.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: There have been no reported differences in pain medication usage and pain scores between open and endoscopic carpal tunnel release
Answer 3: As highlighted in the above studies, most patients consume fewer than 10 opioids if any are prescribed after carpal tunnel syndrome.
Answer 4: As highlighted in the above studies, most patients report taking opioid medications for 2 days or fewer (not 5 days or more)
Answer 5: Oxycodone is associated with higher rates of adverse events and has well established long-term social consequences.
