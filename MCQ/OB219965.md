## Question

A 67 year-old-male presents to the clinic 2 years after undergoing primary total knee arthroplasty. The patient explains that he has had progressive pain and swelling for the last month and recently presented to an urgent care clinic. At that visit, he was given an antibiotic and has been taking it for several days. You decide to perform laboratory tests and an aspiration with the results showing normal synovial values, no isolated organisms, but elevated serum CRP and ESR. Based on these results, what is the best next course of action?

## Options

1. Discontinue antibiotics and perform a repeat aspiration
2. Continue antibiotics for a total of two weeks
3. Add an additional antibiotic with gram-negative coverage
4. Perform revision arthroplasty
5. Prescribe physical therapy

## Correct option

**1. Discontinue antibiotics and perform a repeat aspiration**

## Answer explanation

The patient is presenting with a potential periprosthetic knee infection that was prematurely treated with antibiotics, which can decrease the diagnostic yield of a synovial aspiration. The antibiotic should be discontinued, and a repeat aspiration should be performed prior to prematurely ruling out infection (Answer 1).

Periprosthetic knee infection (PKI) is a serious complication following total knee arthroplasty, occurring in approximately 1-2% of primary and up to 4% of revision procedures. It is characterized by the presence of bacteria within the joint, leading to pain, swelling, warmth, and sometimes systemic symptoms. Diagnosis typically involves a combination of clinical signs, serologic markers (elevated CRP and ESR), joint aspiration for culture and cell count, and imaging studies. Management depends on the timing and severity of the infection. Acute infections may be treated with irrigation, debridement, and component retention (DAIR) alongside targeted antibiotics. Conversely, chronic infections often require a two-stage revision involving prosthesis removal, an antibiotic spacer placement, and subsequent re-implantation after infection control. Prompt recognition and tailored treatment are critical for preserving joint function and preventing systemic complications.

*Flierl et al. review the causes of painful total knee arthroplasty and outline a systematic approach to assessing patients.* *The authors describe both intrinsic and extrinsic causes of knee pain.* Intrinsic causes of pain include pathology within the knee joint, such as infection, implant failure, implant loosening, instability, subluxation/dislocation, arthrofibrosis, impingement, or extensor mechanism disorders. Conversely, extrinsic pain after total knee arthroplasty (TKA) includes pathology outside the knee, like hip disease, spine pathology, vascular insufficiency, tendinitis, and bursitis. Diagnosis requires a careful history and physical examination and can be supplemented by additional imaging or laboratory studies. **The authors recommend** a detailed, algorithmic approach to the painful TKA, which results in the best chance of successfully determining the source of pain. Once a diagnosis is confirmed, treatment may range from nonsurgical measures to complex revision TKA.

*Barrack et al. reviewed a consecutive series of 69 knees in 67 patients where preoperative aspiration was performed.* When the results of the re-aspirations were included, the overall aspiration results improved to a sensitivity of 75%, specificity of 96%. **The results of the study support** the use of routine preoperative aspiration before total knee revision. Previous antibiotic use increases the risk of a false negative result, and re-aspiration at a later date can be expected to significantly improve the value of this test in such cases.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 2 and 3: The patient’s presentation is concerning for a PJI and should be evaluated off of antibiotics in order to establish a diagnosis.
Answer 4: Revision arthroplasty is not indicated currently without a definitive diagnosis.
Answer 5: The patient has an elevated CRP and ESR and should be completely evaluated for infection before prescribing any other interventions.
