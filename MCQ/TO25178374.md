Chapter: General Orthopedics MCQs — Bone Cement

## Question

Which of the following antibiotics is generally avoided in PMMA bone cement because its large, polycationic molecular structure leads to entrapment within the hydrophobic cement matrix, resulting in clinically negligible elution?

## Options

1. Fosfomycin
2. Amikacin
3. Polymyxin B
4. Daptomycin

## Correct option

**3. Polymyxin B**

## Answer explanation

The selection of antibiotics for addition to Polymethylmethacrylate (PMMA) bone cement is a specialized field. For an antibiotic to be effective in cement, it must be thermostable (survive the – heat of polymerization), available in powder form, and have a molecular structure that allows for elution (leaching out) from the hydrophobic cement matrix.

Polymyxin B and Colistin are large, charged molecules that bond to the PMMA matrix; they fail to leach out in therapeutic concentrations, making them ineffective for local delivery.

Below is a detailed list of antibiotics categorized by their clinical utility and evidence for use in bone cement.

1. The "Gold Standard" (Primary Use)

These are the most extensively studied, highly thermostable, and have the best elution profiles.

 - Gentamicin:

 - Spectrum: Broad-spectrum Gram-negative coverage; some Gram-positive.

 - Why: Exceptional elution kinetics and extremely heat-stable. It is the most common pre-mixed antibiotic in commercial cements.

 - Tobramycin:

 - Spectrum: Similar to Gentamicin but often more potent against Pseudomonas aeruginosa.

 - Why: Highly thermostable; frequently used in North American "spacer cocktails."

 - Vancomycin:

 - Spectrum: Essential for Gram-positive coverage, including MRSA and MRSE.

 - Why: Very stable during the exothermic reaction. It elutes well, especially when combined with an aminoglycoside (the "synergistic elution" effect).

2. Specialized Gram-Negative "Rescue" Antibiotics

Used for multi-drug resistant (MDR) Gram-negative infections when aminoglycosides fail.

 - Amikacin:

 - Spectrum: Broad Gram-negative; effective against many Gentamicin-resistant strains.

 - Why: Better elution than many other aminoglycosides and highly heat-stable.

 - Meropenem:

 - Spectrum: Carbapenem-sensitive MDR Gram-negatives (Klebsiella, Acinetobacter).

 - Why: Recent evidence shows it survives polymerization and has a strong "burst" elution in the first 48 hours.

 - Ceftazidime:

 - Spectrum: Specifically for Pseudomonas
 and other resistant Gram-negatives.

 - Why: One of the few cephalosporins stable enough for cement use, though its elution is shorter-lived than aminoglycosides.

3. Alternative Gram-Positive Coverage

 - Clindamycin:

 - Spectrum: Gram-positive aerobes and anaerobes.

 - Why: Sometimes used as an alternative for patients with severe allergies to other drugs, though its elution profile is less predictable than Vancomycin.

 - Daptomycin:

 - Spectrum: Highly potent against resistant Gram-positives (VRE, MRSA).

 - Why: It is heat-stable and elutes effectively, but its high cost often limits it to "salvage" cases.

 - Linezolid:

 - Spectrum: VRE and MRSA.

 - Why: Thermostable and elutes well, but generally reserved for cases where Vancomycin is not an option to prevent systemic resistance.

4. Antifungals

Used for rare but devastating fungal periprosthetic joint infections (PJI).

 - Amphotericin B:

 - Use: Gold standard for fungal PJI.

 - Note: Known to significantly weaken the mechanical strength of cement more than antibacterial powders.

 - Voriconazole:

 - Use: Alternative antifungal with a better safety profile.

 - Note: Shows reasonable elution and stability, but clinical data is more limited compared to Amphotericin.

5. Antibiotics with Poor or Controversial Use

These are generally avoided or used only in extreme, experimental circumstances.

 - Polymyxin B / Colistin: Large, "sticky" molecules that elute very poorly from PMMA. Most of the drug remains trapped in the cement.

 - Penicillins: Most are highly heat-labile and are degraded by the exothermic reaction of the cement.

 - Tetracyclines: Poor elution and can interfere with the polymerization process.

 - Quinolones (e.g., Ciprofloxacin):
 Generally show poor elution kinetics from PMMA compared to aminoglycosides.
