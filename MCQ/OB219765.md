## Question

A 67-year-old female presents to your office to discuss anterior hip replacement. Several of her friends have recommended anterior hip replacement but have warned her that her leg will be “asleep” after surgery. Regarding her concern, which of the following is the most appropriate response?

## Options

1. Injury to the lateral femoral cutaneous nerve is common but improves over time with no functional deficits
2. Injury to the lateral femoral cutaneous nerve is uncommon but improves over time with no functional deficits
3. Injury to the lateral femoral cutaneous nerve is uncommon and is unlikely to improve over time
4. Injury to the intermediate femoral cutaneous nerve is common but improves over time with no functional deficits
5. Injury to the intermediate femoral cutaneous nerve is common and does not improve over time

## Correct option

**1. Injury to the lateral femoral cutaneous nerve is common but improves over time with no functional deficits**

## Answer explanation

Patients undergoing anterior total hip replacement are at risk for injury to the lateral femoral cutaneous nerve, which occurs commonly but improves over time with no appreciable functional deficits (Answer 1).

The direct anterior hip approach involves a true inter-nervous plane between muscles innervated by the superior gluteal and femoral nerves. This is achieved by developing a plane between the tensor fascia latae and sartorius superficially, and between the gluteus medius and rectus femoris at a deeper level. The most associated nerve palsy following a direct anterior approach (DAA) is that of the lateral femoral cutaneous nerve (LFCN), followed by the femoral nerve. The nerve is formed by the posterior divisions of the L2 and L3 nerve roots of the lumbar plexus and eventually runs within the fascia overlying the tensor fascia latae or sartorius. While different techniques have been proposed to reduce injury, current evidence indicates that it is a common occurrence, improves over time, and has virtually no bearing on hip functional scores.

*Goulding et al evaluated 132 patients who underwent anterior total hip arthroplasty (THA) or hip resurfacing (HR) to determine the incidence of lateral femoral cutaneous nerve (LFCN) sensory deficit.* **They report** an overall incidence of 81% with a much higher prevalence amongst HR patients compared to THA: 91% vs 67%. Of the 60 patients followed for 12 months, 88% continued to have symptoms with only 6% achieving complete resolution. Despite the high rate of injury and longevity of symptoms, no patients reported functional deficits across multiple testing modalities. **The authors conclude** that LFCN neuropraxia is a common occurrence following anterior hip surgery, but it does not lead to significant disability.

*Patton et al surveyed 688 patients who underwent anterior THA to determine the duration and severity of LFCN injury.* **They report** that the most common symptom following surgery was numbness which improved with increasing intervals from surgery. **The authors conclude** that LFCN injury is common, improves with time, and does not appear to be linked to hip functional scores.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Injury to the nerve is common and patients are routinely advised to expect some level of numbness.
Answer 3: Injury to the nerve is common and improves over time.
Answers 4-5: Injury to the intermediate femoral nerve is not common during the DAA.
