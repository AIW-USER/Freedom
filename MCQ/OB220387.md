## Question

An 82-year-old female with rheumatoid arthritis presents to your office with severe right knee osteoarthritis. After failing all conservative treatment options, you schedule her for a manual right total knee arthroplasty. The plan is to cement a posterior-stabilized total knee implant. After bone preparation is performed and the patella is resurfaced, you start to insert the trial components. All of a sudden you hear a crack and the knee no longer feels balanced. Which structure is most commonly fractured intraoperatively during a primary total knee arthroplasty?

## Options

1. Lateral femoral condyle
2. Medial femoral condyle
3. Patella
4. Supracondylar femur
5. Lateral tibial plateau

## Correct option

**2. Medial femoral condyle**

## Answer explanation

This patient most likely suffered an intraoperative medial femoral condyle fracture during a primary right total knee arthroplasty (TKA) procedure (answer choice 2).

Periprosthetic fracture (PPFx) in TKA is an uncommon complication with a prevalence of 0.39%. Risk factors include osteoporosis, anterior cortical notching, chronic steroid use, advanced age, female gender, and neurologic disorders. PPFx can occur intraoperatively or postoperatively. Intraoperative fractures can occur during any stage, including exposure, bone preparation, trialing components, cementation, and seating of the polyethylene insert. The most common structure fractured intraoperatively during a primary TKA is the medial femoral condyle, followed by the lateral femoral condyle, supracondylar femur, and proximal tibia. Diagnosis can be made intraoperatively with direct visualization or implant loosening. Postoperatively it can be diagnosed with plain radiographs or a CT scan. In general, treatment can be operative or nonoperative depending on the location of the fracture, implant stability, the available bone stock, and patient comorbidities. If found intraoperatively, treatment can include open reduction internal fixation via plates and screws, hinged implants, stemmed implants, bone graft, or distal femoral replacement.

*Alden et al. reviewed 17,389 primary TKAs performed between 1985 and 2005 and* **identified** 66 patients with 67 intraoperative fractures. **They noted** intraoperative fracture occurred more commonly in women (80.6%) and the femur (73.1%) with a majority of fractures occurring during exposure and bone preparation and trialing of the components. **They concluded** that all fractures healed and Knee Society scores and functional scores improved postoperatively.

*Pinaroli et al. reviewed 1,624 primary TKAs for intraoperative fracture and ligament disruption with an average follow-up time of 36.8 months.* **They found** 40 fractures around the knee (2.2%) and 29 tendon/ligament disruptions (1.6%). **They noted** the risk of tibia fractures was statistically more significant when smaller tibial trays (size 1) were utilized (p = 0.019) or when an anterior tibial tuberosity elevation was performed (p = 0.02). **They concluded** a prevalence of 3.8% of intraoperative bone or ligament complications in their study analyzing primary PS-TKA implants, all of which could be potentially prevented with rigorous surgical technique.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The lateral femoral condyle is the second most common structure fractured during a primary TKA.
Answer 3: The patella is at an increased risk of fracture with resurfacing, especially if cut too thin.
Answers 4 and 5: The supracondylar femur and proximal tibia are the third and fourth most commonly fractured structures during a TKA, respectively.
