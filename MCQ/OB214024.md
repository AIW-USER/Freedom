## Question

A patient presents to your office with short stature and lower limb deformities. On review of patient's labs, you notice a profoundly low serum phosphate and 24-hour urinary phosphate levels are high. On discussion with the family, you discover others in the family have been diagnosed with a similar condition. You are able to develop an inheritance pattern based on your discussion (Figure A). What is the most likely disease's associated mutation?

*Figure A*

## Options

1. PHEX gene mutation
2. DMP1 gene mutation
3. FGFR3 mutation
4. COL1A1 gene mutation
5. FGF23 gene mutation

## Correct option

**5. FGF23 gene mutation**

## Answer explanation

The pedigree depicts autosomal dominant (AD) inheritance and AD Hypophosphatemic Rickets is associated with FGF23 mutation.

Vitamin D-resistant rickets or familial hypophosphatemic rickets is the most common form of heritable rickets. It usually presents at 1-2 years of age and is characterized by the inability of the kidneys to reabsorb phosphate. The autosomal dominant (AD) form is a result of FGF23 mutation, leading to decreased FGF23 degradation. FGF23 then acts to increase the urinary excretion of phosphate. The autosomal recessive (AR) form results from a dentin matrix protein 1 (DMP1) mutation that leads to impaired osteocyte maturation and bone mineralization and increased FGF23. The most common form, X-dominant (XD), is the result of a PHEX mutation that allows for increased levels of FGF23 which decreases renal phosphate absorption and suppresses 25-(OH)-1 alpha-hydroxylase activity.

*Yao et al. did an intra-articular injection of FGF18 in an animal model.* **They found** it decreases cartilage degradation, increases type II collagen, suppresses matrix metalloproteinases, promotes chondrocyte proliferation through PI3K-AKT signaling, decreases IL-1 beta apoptosis, decreases free radicals, and more. **They concluded** that FGF18 demonstrates an anti-arthritic effect.

Bitzan and Goodyear stated that the X-linked is the most common form of hypophosphatemic rickets secondary to a PHEX mutation. They stated that there is an increased risk of fractures, poor bone healing, dental complications, and others. Classic treatment includes phosphate supplementation and calcitonin. However, new treatments may target FGF23 or its receptors.

Figure A is a pedigree chart demonstrating autosomal dominant inheritance. Illustration A is a cartoon demonstrating the effects of FGF23.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The PHEX mutation is associated with X-linked dominant Hypophosphatemic Rickets. The pedigree presented depicts AD inheritance.
Answer 2: The DMP1 mutation is associated with autosomal recessive Hypophosphatemic Rickets. The pedigree presented depicts AD inheritance.
Answer 3: The FGFR3 mutation is seen in patients with Achondroplasia.
Answer 4: The COL1A1 mutation is seen in patients with osteogenesis imperfecta.
