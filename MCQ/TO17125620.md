## Question

A sports person has been complaining of painful limping on left hip for one week. He gives a history of aggressive athletic training preceding this pain. His x-ray of the hip joint is given below. What is the appropriate management? (FNB Pattern Paper 2020)

![[TO17125620-1.png]]

## Options

1. Protected weight bearing with crutches
2. Percutaneous screw fixation
3. Bed rest, calcium and vitamin D supplementation
4. Non-Weight bearing and activity modification

## Correct option

**4. Non-Weight bearing and activity modification**

## Answer explanation

Femoral neck stress fractures (FNSFs) account for 3% of all sport-related stress fractures. Higher incidences of FNSFs are seen among female athletes. Within athletic cohorts, the most commonly reported causative sports are marathon running, long-distance running, basketball, gymnastics and ballet dancing.

Risk factors: female gender and poor baseline physical fitness, decreased femoral bone mineral density (BMD), female athlete triad, namely disordered eating, amenorrhea and decreased bone mineral density.

Predisposing factors: sudden increase in training intensity, coxa vara, coxa profunda and acetabular retroversion, as seen in pincer-type femoro acetabular impingement (FAI).

Classification: The mostly commonly used classification at present is that of Fullerton & Snowdy, who categorized FNSFs into 3 groups, using both plain radiographs and bone scans. Type I fractures occurred on the tension-side(superolateral) of the femoral neck, type II fractures on the compression-side(inferomedial) and type III fractures were displaced.

Based on MRI findings of FNSFs, compression injuries are further divided into those without a fracture line, those with a fracture line less than 50% of the femoral neck width, and those with a fracture line greater than or equal to 50% of femoral neck width.

Treatment:
![[TO17125620-2.png]]

In compression type fracture (inferomedial) if fracture line extends to less than 50% it can be managed with non-weight bearing and activity modification.

Answer: 4
