## Question

A 42-year-old male presents to clinic with posterior heel pain and swelling over several weeks. He notes that his pain is aggravated with activity. He is advised to wear a heel sleeve and is referred for therapy. Which of the following exercises is most effective for this patient's condition?

## Options

1. Standing calf raise hold
2. Single-leg stance
3. Heel drop
4. Jump training
5. Heel walk

## Correct option

**3. Heel drop**

## Answer explanation

This patient's history is consistent with Achilles tendonitis which is best treated with eccentric exercises, such as heel drops. Heel drops are performed by standing on a step with the heels off and slowly lowering the foot below the level of the step producing an eccentric contraction.

Achilles tendonitis occurs as a result of repetitive trauma leading to inflammation. It typically occurs in middle-age patients with tight heel cords who present with posterior heel pain and swelling. Achilles tendonitis is initially managed nonoperatively with activity modification, shoe wear modification, and therapy. Therapy should emphasize eccentric stretching exercises.

*Verrall et al. conducted a study to assess the efficacy of a modified eccentric heel-drop program in treating athletes with chronic Achilles tendinopathy.* After 6 weeks of therapy, there was a significant improvement in pain with over 80% of patients reporting excellent satisfaction and an overall mean time to return to pre-morbid activity of 10 weeks. **The authors concluded** that a 6-week eccentric heel-drop training regimen as the only treatment for chronic Achilles tendinopathy was an effective option.

*Magnussen et al. conducted a systematic review on the current evidence for nonoperative treatment of midportion Achilles tendinopathy.* *707 abstracts and 16 RCT were reviewed.* **They concluded** that eccentric exercises have the most evidence supporting their effectiveness. More studies are needed for treatment modalities such as extracorporeal shockwave therapy, local corticosteroid treatments, and other alternatives.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Standing calf raise hold emphasizes isometric contraction of the gastrocsoleus complex by standing on the ball of the foot and holding this position
Answer 2: Single leg stance focuses on proprioception which is more effective for ankle instability
Answer 4: Jumping training involves plyometric contractions and may aggravate symptoms
Answer 5: Heel walks emphasize strengthening ankle dorsiflexors
