## Question

A 54-year-old female patient presents to your clinic. She has been between jobs and only recently obtained health insurance again. For the past several years she has had chronic pain with ambulation and a progressive deformity to her right foot. Figure A is a clinical image. She endorses a traumatic event several years ago and had severe foot pain, however she did not see a physician at the time due to her lack of insurance. The pain improved however never completely resolved. What untreated injury did she most likely sustain?

## Options

1. Navicular fracture
2. Lisfranc injury
3. Calcaneus fracture
4. Anterior talofibular ligament injury
5. Talar neck fracture

## Correct option

**2. Lisfranc injury**

## Answer explanation

This patient presents with pes planovalgus due to a chronic untreated lisfranc injury.

Lisfranc injuries often occur with axial load through a hyperplantar flexed forefoot. These will present often with medial plantar bruising, severe pain, and inability to bear weight. Imaging will demonstrate the widening of the interval between the 1st and 2nd rays with the discontinuity between the medial base of the 2nd metatarsal and the medial aspect of the middle cuneiform. There may be associated fractures of the mid-foot or purely ligamentous injury. Chronic Lisfranc injury results in progressive foot planovalgus deformity due to midfoot and arch collapse. The deformity leads to chronic pain and dysfunction for the patient.

*Weatherford, Anderson, and Bohay reviewed the treatment of tarsometatarsal injuries.* *They discuss the anatomy of the midfoot, physical exam and radiographic findings consistent with Lisfranc injuries, and management considerations.* Nonoperative management is possible in patients without midfoot instability or those that would not tolerate surgery. Surgery is typically indicated to restore the functional anatomy of the midfoot. Fixation typically proceeds from proximal to distal and medial to lateral. Instability should be assessed intraoperatively with stress testing. Surgical techniques include k-wire, screw, and plate fixation with or without arthrodesis of involved joints.

*Sangeorzan et al. performed salvage arthrodesis for chronic lisfranc injuries and failed surgical treatment in a total of 16 patients.* Preoperative symptoms included pain in all patients, progressive pes planus deformity in 75%, and lateral impingement in 31% of patients. Arthrodesis provided good or excellent results in 69% of patients, with subjective improvement in all patients except one. Accurate reduction and early treatment were associated with improved outcomes.

Figure A and B demonstrate clinical pictures of a pes planovalgus foot with the valgus alignment of the hindfoot and pes planus.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Navicular fractures are not associated with the development of a planovalgus foot.
Answer 3 and 5: These fractures are typically associated with a varus deformity, not planovalgus.
Answer 4: This would lead to ankle instability, but unlikely to result in a planovalgus foot deformity.
