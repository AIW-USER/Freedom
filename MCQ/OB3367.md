## Question

Figure A is the MRI image of a 45-year-old female who presents with low back pain and radicular leg pain radiating to the dorsum of her foot. On exam, she has weakness of her hip abductors, and her right extensor hallucis longus. Which of the following statements is true regarding L5-S1 isthmic spondylolisthesis (IS)?

![[q3367-a-screen-shot-2020-02-01-at-9-27-49-am.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Increasing lumbar lordosis reduces strain on the L5 nerve root in low grade spondylolisthesis
2. Smaller disc height produces less strain on the L5 nerve root during reduction of spondylolisthesis
3. The majority of the L5 nerve root strain occurs during the second half of the complete reduction of a high grade IS
4. There is a linear increase in nerve root strain during spondylolisthesis reduction
5. The clinical results of decompression alone are equal to decompression with instrumented fusion in high-grade IS

## Correct option

**3. The majority of the L5 nerve root strain occurs during the second half of the complete reduction of a high grade IS**

## Answer explanation

In patients undergoing a reduction of high-grade spondylolisthesis, 71% of nerve root strain occurs during the second half of a complete reduction. This is an often-cited reason for partial reduction of high-grade spondylolisthesis.

Isthmic spondylolisthesis (IS) in adults is caused by a pars defect most commonly at the L5-S1 level. Clinically patients present with axial back pain and L5 radiculopathy due to foraminal stenosis. Radiographs are necessary to quantify the slip degree via the Myerding classification (Illustration A) and pelvic incidence, as this corresponds with the severity of the disease. Most patients resolve with non-operative treatment. Surgical treatment can be anterior or posterior, and single-level or multi-level. The goal of surgical treatment is wide decompression and instrumented fusion.

*Petraco et al. performed a cadaveric study to evaluate the effect of spondylolisthesis reduction on the L5 nerve root.* **They report** that there is a marked increase in nerve root stretch after >50% slip reduction. **They conclude** that partial reduction may be clinically safer and that correction of lumbosacral kyphosis in high-grade slips may be protective for the L5 nerve root.

*Rao et al. review adult IS.* **They report** that more than 75% of patients with Myerding grade 1 or 2 adult IS will be successfully treated with non-operative treatment for 3-6 months. **They conclude** that decompression alone without instrumented fusion is associated with poor outcomes in patients undergoing surgery for adult IS.

Illustration B demonstrates the non-linear increase in L5 nerve root strain (measured via nerve length) during the complete reduction of a spondylolisthesis

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Increase lumbar lordosis is protective for the L5 nerve root in high-grade spondylolisthesis
Answer 2: Increasing disc height results in less nerve root strain during spondylolisthesis reduction
Answer 4: There is a non-linear increase in nerve root strain after >50% reduction of a high-grade spondylolisthesis
Answer 5: The clinical results of decompression alone in IS are poor in high grade IS patients
