## Question

## Options

1. Area moment of inertia
2. Elastic range on the stress-strain curve
3. Perren's strain theory
4. Radius of curvature of the nail
5. Young's modulus of elasticity

## Correct option

**3. Perren's strain theory**

## Answer explanation

Stephan Perren's interfragmentary strain (IFS) theory quantifies the relative motion produced within a fracture gap and dictates that the amount of IFS determines the subsequent differentiation of fracture gap tissue, with lower strain environments allowing for the formation of callous and the process of endochondral ossification (Illustration A).

In general, the mechanical environment of fracture healing itself is determined by a combination of the stiffness of the fracture fixation device used, the magnitude of the loads applied, the properties of the tissue in the fracture site (as focused on here), as well as the types of loads allowed by the chosen fixation device such as axial, shear, or bending moments. When relative stability constructs such as intramedullary nails are used, the process of secondary bone healing occurs through a cartilaginous intermediate via endochondral ossification. In order for this cartilage intermediate to form, strain must be kept between 2-10% (Illustration A). The Perren IFS theory states that strain varies as a function of the change in the length of the fracture gap relative to the initial length of the fracture gap. Thus, in short oblique or transverse fractures (Figure A), very little change occurs in the length of the fracture gap, maintaining a higher degree of strain at the fracture site if an absolute stability construct with cortical apposition is not used. Meanwhile, a comminuted fracture gap (Figure B) spreads the strain across the length of the many comminuted segments, effectively reducing the overall strain at the fracture gap and creating a tissue environment that is amenable to endochondral ossification. Though short oblique fractures treated with IMN will not necessarily go on to nonunion, the strain at this fracture site is higher than that of a multifragmentary fracture treated with the same device.

*Cheal et al. describe the role of interfragmentary strain in fracture healing using an ovine model of healing after osteotomy.* Cyclic bending strains were applied to tibial osteotomies in 11 sheep, and it was noted that the ingrowth of vascularized soft tissue into the interfragmentary gaps, as well as the subsequent differentiation of this tissue, occurred earlier and to a greater degree in regions of lower strain. **The authors concluded** that their findings provided support for the prevailing interfragmentary strain hypothesis made by Perren.

*Claes et al. provide support for the quantitative tissue differentiation theory of IFS by Perren, using a finite element model to show that the magnitudes of local stress and strain along bony surfaces predict the course and type of fracture healing.* **The authors concluded** that intramembranous bone formation occurs with strains < 5% and hydrostatic pressures smaller than 0.15 MPa, while endochondral ossification is associated with compressive pressures larger than 0.15 MPa and strains < 15%. All other conditions seemed to lead to connective tissue or fibrous cartilage that would be consistent with material seen in fracture non-unions.

Figure B is an AP radiograph of a comminuted distal-third femoral shaft fracture treated with a retrograde IMN, demonstrating a lower-strain fracture gap amenable to secondary bone healing through a cartilage intermediate. Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The area moment of inertia is a property of shape that is used to predict deflection, bending, and stress in beams, and is proportional to the measured length to the power of four. It is a measure of bending rigidity but does not explain how the intermediate healing tissues in a fracture gap vary as a function of the deforming forces seen across fracture gaps of varying lengths.
Answer 2: The elastic range on the stress-strain curve represents the loads at which reversible changes occur to a material without causing permanent deformation.
Answer 4: The radius of curvature of an intramedullary nail affects the implant's stiffness, or bending rigidity, but is not a function of the strain at the fracture site that affects subsequent tissue properties or differentiation.
Answer 5: Young's modulus of elasticity is the slope of the stress-strain curve and represents a measure of the stiffness of a material in the elastic zone, or rather its ability to resist deformation. Again, this is the material property and not a property of the differentiating tissues at the fracture site.
