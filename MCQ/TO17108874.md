## Question

Cause of loosening of glenoid component in Total shoulder arthroplasty (FNB Pattern Paper 2020)

## Options

1. Dysfunctional rotator cuff
2. Osteonecrosis
3. Rheumatoid arthritis
4. Osteoarthritis

## Correct option

**1. Dysfunctional rotator cuff**

## Answer explanation

Complications after total and reverse shoulder arthroplasty:

The overall complication rate after total shoulder arthroplasty is estimated to be approximately 15%. The most commonly reported complications, in order of frequency, are component (primarily glenoid) loosening, glenohumeral instability, rotator cuff tear, periprosthetic fracture, infection, implant failure including dissociation of modular prostheses, and deltoid weakness or dysfunction.

Loosening of the glenoid component is a frequent cause of failure of total shoulder arthroplasty (TSA). The etiology of glenoid component loosening is multifactorial and includes aseptic osteolysis, rotator cuff insufficiency, soft tissue instability, and infection.

Technical factors which have been postulated for increased risk of glenoid loosening are-

- Excessive reaming on the native glenoid with removal of subchondral bone
- A glenohumeral mismatch <4 mm increases the chances of glenoid component loosening
- The use of a flat-back glenoid component
- B2 glenoid had a higher risk for radiographic loosening compared to A1

Glenoid wear pattern:

Type A (central)

Type B (posterior)

Type C (excessive glenoid retroversion >25°)

Also know:

Common complications after reverse total shoulder arthroplasty are scapular notching, hematoma formation, glenoid dissociation such as baseplate failure or aseptic loosening, glenohumeral dislocation, acromial and scapular spine fractures, infection, loosening or dissociation of the humeral component, and nerve injury.

The most common intraoperative complications in shoulder arthroplasty are fracture, usually of the humeral shaft in the mid to distal diaphysis, nerve injury, most often to the axillary nerve; and mispositioning of components.
