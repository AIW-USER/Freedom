## Question

Figures A and B are the radiographs of a 62-year-old male that presents to the emergency room following a motor vehicle collision. He complains only of right knee pain. He undergoes open reduction and internal fixation for this injury when the soft tissues permit. The interval used to address the medial-sided injury is between the medial head of the gastrocnemius and what other structure?

## Options

1. Posterior oblique ligament
2. Semimembranosus
3. Soleus
4. Superficial MCL
5. Confluence of the sartorius, gracilis, and semitendinosus

## Correct option

**5. Confluence of the sartorius, gracilis, and semitendinosus**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a tibial plateau fracture with a posteromedial fragment. The correct interval for the reduction of this posteromedial fragment is between the pes anserinus (confluence of the sartorius, gracilis, and semitendinosus) and the medial head of the gastrocnemius.

Tibial plateau fractures are periarticular injuries of the proximal tibia frequently associated with soft tissue injury. Dual surgical incisions with dual plate fixation are indicated in bicondylar tibial plateau fractures. Even with the use of variable angle-locking technology, use of only a lateral locked plate alone will not provide adequate stability of the separate posteromedial fragment. Failure to address the posteromedial fragment separately results in a high failure rate.

*Carlson et al. retrospectively reviewed patients with posterior bicondylar tibial plateau fractures treated by direct fracture exposure and fixation through dual incisions.* **They report** that reduction of the posterior plateau condyles is easiest with the knee in full extension and flexion contractures can be a problem postoperatively. **They concluded** that the dual-incision approach to these challenging fractures can result in good to excellent knee function for these patients.

*Bhattacharyya et al. retrospectively reviewed fracture patterns and early results of treatment of posterior shearing tibial plateau fractures.* **They reported** that the functional outcome score was significantly related to the quality of articular reduction. **They concluded** that posterior shearing tibial plateau fractures form a consistent pattern and can be successfully managed using a posterior approach with direct reduction and buttress fixation of articular fragments.

Figures A&B are the radiographs of a bicondylar tibial plateau fracture. Illustrations A&B are the corresponding postoperative radiographs following ORIF through a dual incision approach.

Incorrect Answers:
