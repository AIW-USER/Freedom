## Question

Which of the following radiographic features is characteristic of developmental coxa vara?

## Options

1. Increased femoral anteversion
2. Increased femoral epiphyseal-diaphyseal angle of Southwick
3. Increased Hilgenreiner-epiphyseal angle
4. Short femoral neck with a horizontal physis
5. Y-shaped ossification defect with associated triangular metaphyseal fragment of the superior femoral neck

## Correct option

**3. Increased Hilgenreiner-epiphyseal angle**

## Answer explanation

Developmental coxa vara (DCV) is associated with an increased Hilgenreiner’s-epiphyseal angle.

DCV is characterized by a decreased neck-shaft angle (<120 degrees) and is thought to be due to an ossification defect of the inferior femoral neck. This results in a morphologically short femoral neck with a relatively vertical physis and decreased femoral anteversion. The Hilgenreiner’s-epiphyseal angle is used to grade the severity and likelihood of progression of the deformity as well as to assess the adequacy of deformity correction following osteotomy. Valgus overcorrection is recommended.

*Carroll et al. evaluated the surgical outcomes following valgus osteotomies for pediatric coxa vara.* **The authors found** that the overall recurrence rate was approximately 50%. However, 95% of patients with correction of the Hilgenreiner’s-epiphyseal angle to <38 degrees had no recurrence. **They noted** that head-shaft angle was not a reliable indicator of correction.

*Cordes et al. reviewed the long-term results of correction of coxa vara using a Pauwel’s Y-shaped osteotomy.* **The authors noted** that in all cases the physis was vertical and there was abnormal ossification of the inferior aspect of the femoral neck. **They found** that osteotomy was successful at correction and that recurrence of the deformity was minimized so long as the Hilgenreiner’s-epiphyseal angle was <40 degrees.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Femoral anteversion is DECREASED.
Answer 2: An increased femoral epiphyseal-diaphyseal angle of Southwick is characteristic of a slipped capital femoral epiphysis. This is measured on a frog-leg lateral view. The head-shaft angle, measured between the axis of the femoral shaft and a line perpendicular to the base of the capital epiphysis on the AP view, may be used to evaluate the degree of coxa vara but has not been shown to correlate with progression or recurrence.
Answer 4: The femoral neck is short but the physis is more VERTICAL.
Answer 5: An INVERTED Y-shaped ossification defect is present and associated with a triangular metaphyseal fragment of the INFERIOR femoral neck.
