## Question

A 7-year-old girl presents to the emergency room after a fall with right arm pain. She has full motor and sensory function. Her fingers are warm and pink with a capillary refill <3 seconds, and she is noted to have ecchymosis in her antecubital fossa. Radiographs are seen in Figures A and B. What is the most appropriate management plan?

## Options

1. Closed reduction, long arm casting, and discharge home
2. Closed reduction, long arm casting, and admission for a 24-hour observation
3. Closed reduction, percutaneous pin fixation, and discharge home
4. Closed reduction, percutaneous pin fixation, and admission for arteriography
5. Open reduction with brachial artery exploration and admission for observation

## Correct option

**3. Closed reduction, percutaneous pin fixation, and discharge home**

## Answer explanation

The radiographs display a Gartland Type II supracondylar humerus (SCH) fracture for which the current standard of care in the United States is closed reduction and percutaneous pinning (CRPP). Neither arterial exploration nor extended inpatient observation is indicated for a child with isolated ecchymosis and a well-perfused hand following surgical fixation.

A type II SCH with a well-perfused hand is recommended for closed reduction and percutaneous pinning. Extension type SCH fractures with the capitellum posterior to the anterior humeral line are by definition either Gartland Type II or Type III (if posterior cortex/periosteum displaced). Though some Canadian and other international groups are studying closed reduction and casting for Type II fractures, the standard of care in the United States is to perform closed reduction and percutaneous pin fixation. A patient with a pink, well-perfused hand before and after surgery may be monitored clinically for a brief period after surgery and then discharged home.

*Skaggs et al. performed a retrospective review of 189 children with Type II SCH fractures treated with closed reduction and percutaneous pinning.* **They found** no patients who lost reduction and no intra-operative complications. Four patients had pin tract infections, only 1 required return to the OR for irrigation and debridement.

*Bashyal et al. analyzed 622 patients at a single center with SCH fractures of all types.* *They examined antibiotic usage, prep and draping method, and complications.* **They found** only 5 infections (1%), with only 1 deep infection requiring a return to the OR. They saw no effect of draping method or preoperative antibiotic on infection. In half of their patients, a medial pin was used with only 1 ulnar nerve injury (0.3%).

Figures A and B are AP and lateral radiographs, respectively, of a Gartland Type II supracondylar humerus fracture in a 7-year-old girl. Note the anterior humeral line does not intersect the capitellum on the lateral view. Illustrations A and B are the AP and lateral radiographs, respectively, of the same patient after closed reduction and percutaneous pin fixation with 2 divergent lateral pins followed by a bivalved long arm cast.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1 and 2: CRPP is recommended for Type II SCH fractures.
Answer 4: Arteriography is not indicated in a patient with a well perfused hand, nor is it considered in a dysvascular hand prior to reduction.
Answer 5: Brachial artery exploration is not indicated in a child with a pink, perfused hand.
