## Question

A 66-year-old patient with diabetes underwent a left total ankle arthroplasty 2 years ago. The patient has developed left ankle erythema, swelling, and pain over the last 5 weeks. His radiographs are shown in Figures A and B, while a joint aspiration reveals 70K WBCs with gram-positive cocci in clusters. What is the most appropriate treatment and the expected outcome?

## Options

1. IV antibiotics; prosthesis retention
2. Irrigation and debridement with polyethylene exchange; prosthesis retention
3. Antibiotic spacer; successful re-implantation of arthroplasty components
4. Amputation; successful infection eradication
5. Antibiotic spacer; unlikely for reimplantation of prosthesis

## Correct option

**5. Antibiotic spacer; unlikely for reimplantation of prosthesis**

## Answer explanation

The patient is presenting with a chronic prosthetic joint infection which will require component explant and antibiotic spacer placement. Despite aggressive treatment measures, many patients with prosthetic ankle infections will be unlikely to have reimplantation of a prosthesis.

Prosthetic ankle infections are devastating complications associated with high patient morbidity. Chronic infections are defined as greater than 4 weeks from the index procedure and acute infections for less than 4 weeks. Irrigation and debridement with polyethylene exchange is an option for acute infections, whereas component explantation with antibiotic spacer for chronic infections. Few prosthetic ankle infections will have successful reimplantation due to either persistent infection, extensive bone loss from debridement, or high risk for recurrent infection.

*Patton et al. performed a retrospective review of patients with infected total ankle arthroplasties.* **The authors found** a higher infection risk in patients with prior ankle surgery, diabetes, and wound healing complications for greater than 14 days post-op. **The authors concluded** that caution must be exercised when performing a total ankle arthroplasty in patients with risk factors due to the profound morbidity of prosthetic joint infections.

*Myerson et al. performed a retrospective review of patients managed for an ankle prosthetic joint infection.* **They found** a 0% success rate with early prosthesis salvage and only 16% were able to successfully undergo component reimplantation. **They concluded** that few patients presenting with prosthetic ankle infections can be expected to undergo joint preserving arthroplasty.

Figures A and B demonstrate and AP and lateral radiograph of the left ankle with a total ankle arthroplasty prosthesis.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: IV antibiotics alone will be insufficient to clear the chronic infection. This will not result in infection eradication to retain the prosthesis unless long term suppression is the treatment goal.
Answer 2: Irrigation and debridement with polyethylene will insufficiently address the infection due to the formation of biofilm on the metal components.
Answer 3: Despite appropriate treatment with component explantation and antibiotic spacer, very few cases will result in successful prosthesis reimplantation.
Answer 4: Initial treatment of a prosthetic joint with amputation is undesirable. Attempts should be made to preserve the joint and then the limb, such as permanent antibiotic spacer, fusion, or successful component reimplantation.
