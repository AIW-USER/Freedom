Chapter: Spine MCQs — Scoliosis Kyphosis

## Question

All are true regarding Influence of Spinal Deformity on the Lungs except?

## Options

1. Lung development occurs with volumetric growth until approximately age 8, followed by substantial alveolar number increase.
2. The earlier the onset and larger the curve, the more likely is pulmonary failure.
3. Only infantile and juvenile curves or severely lordotic thoracic curves of high degree are likely to result in pulmonary problems,
4. Typical adolescent curves do not markedly affect long-term pulmonary function despite some restrictive disease.

## Correct option

**1. Lung development occurs with volumetric growth until approximately age 8, followed by substantial alveolar number increase.**

## Answer explanation

Large curves can result in significant restrictive lung disease and core pulmonale. Unfortunately, there are only a few high-quality studies evaluating chest and lung development and long-term pulmonary prognosis in scoliosis. Lung development occurs with substantial alveolar number increase until approximately age 8, followed by volumetric growth. Ultimately, it is probably the surface area of ventilated and perfused alveoli in relationship to oxygen demand that matters. The earlier the onset and larger the curve, the more likely is pulmonary failure. Juvenile and particularly infantile curves are much more likely to result in early to mid-adult life pulmonary restriction than adolescent curves, which rarely cause problems except in the presence of marked thoracic lordosis. A Scandinavian 20-year follow-up study found that respiratory failure occurred only in patients with a low vital capacity at earlier follow-up who also had curves greater than 110°. Because these patients, like all adults, further decreased their vital capacity through the normal aging process, they subsequently decompensated. Weinstein et al, in a long-term follow-up of untreated adolescent patients, found pulmonary function failure only in those with thoracic curves more than 100° to 120°, except in smokers who fared worse. Double curves did not demonstrate pulmonary problems although their curves were not as severe. Overall, in untreated scoliosis, only infantile and juvenile curves or severely lordotic thoracic curves of high degree are likely to result in pulmonary problems, but typical adolescent curves do not markedly affect long-term pulmonary function despite some restrictive disease.
