## Question

A 32-year-old female with a past history of asthma presents with a painless, slowly enlarging soft-tissue mass in her left thigh for the last year. On exam, the patient has a 6 cm mass located along the proximal medial thigh, and significant inguinal lymphadenopathy is noted. If this patient’s lymphadenopathy is deemed to be secondary to metastatic disease from the patient’s soft tissue mass, which of the following combinations of conditions best represents the most likely differential diagnoses for this patient’s mass?

## Options

1. Clear cell sarcoma, epithelioid sarcoma, giant cell tumor
2. Clear cell sarcoma, synovial sarcoma, rhabdomyosarcoma
3. Epithelioid sarcoma, liposarcoma, giant cell tumor
4. Lipoma, desmoid tumor, epithelioid sarcoma
5. Synovial sarcoma, rhabodymyosarcoma, liposarcoma

## Correct option

**2. Clear cell sarcoma, synovial sarcoma, rhabdomyosarcoma**

## Answer explanation

This patient presents with an enlarging soft tissue mass concerning for a soft tissue tumor. The most likely diagnoses for a soft tissue tumor with lymph node metastases are clear cell sarcoma, synovial sarcoma, rhabdomyosarcoma, angiosarcoma, or epithelioid sarcoma (Answer 2).

Soft tissue sarcomas are rare musculoskeletal tumors that encompass a wide range of malignancies. Such diagnoses include angiosarcomas, liposarcomas, malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumors, fibrosarcomas, leiomyosarcomas, epithelioid sarcomas, dermatofibrosarcoma protuberans, clear cell sarcomas, and alveolar soft part sarcomas. Patients typically present with a painless, enlarging, soft tissue mass. The AJCC staging system is frequently used to assess patients once a diagnosis is confirmed by biopsy. System components include tumor size, lymph node involvement, presence of metastasis, and tumor grade. While most soft tissue sarcomas rarely metastasize to the lymph nodes, a certain subset (rhabdomysarcomas, angiosarcomas, clear cell sarcomas, epithelioid sarcomas, and synovial sarcomas) do at a higher rate, warranting dedicated evaluation of the lymph nodes during staging.

*Constantinidou et al. published a correspondence discussing the role of aggressive surgical management combined with chemotherapy/radiation in patients with metastatic clear cell sarcoma in the setting of a recent study by Bianchi et al. advocating for such a strategy.* **They highlighted** that clear cell sarcoma is a chemotherapy-resistant disease and that their experience with operating on those with metastatic disease is associated with short-term recurrence. **They concluded** that the benefit of surgery in these patients is very hard to discern in the absence of any large randomized controlled trials.

*Bianchi et al. performed a retrospective analysis of 31 patients with clear cell sarcoma to determine the outcomes of wide resection.* **They found** an overall 10-year disease-specific survival rate of 41% that dwindled to 0% in those with lymph node/pulmonary metastasis. A higher rate of lymph node metastasis was observed in their cohort compared to historic rates seen in other high-grade soft tissue sarcomas. **They concluded** that surgical intervention is beneficial in patients without systemic spread and that additional medical advancements in chemotherapy will offer substantial benefit to this patient population.

Answers 1, 3, 4, and 5: Giant cell tumors, liposarcomas, desmoid tumors, and lipomas rarely metastasize to the lymph nodes when compared with clear cell sarcoma, epithelioid sarcoma, and rhabdomyosarcoma.
