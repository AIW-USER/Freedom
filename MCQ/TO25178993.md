Chapter: Regional Orthopedics MCQs — Jersey Finger

## Question

During the surgical reconstruction of a chronic, retracted Leddy and Packer Type I Jersey finger, a surgeon considers advancing the FDP tendon to its original insertion. Which of the following is the most critical biomechanical constraint to avoid the "Quadriga Effect"?

## Options

1. Advancement must not exceed 0.5 cm to prevent attenuation of the A4 pulley.
2. Advancement must not exceed 1.0 cm to maintain the excursion balance of the common FDP muscle belly.
3. The A2 pulley must be vented at least 50% to allow for the increased bulk of the repaired tendon.
4. The repair must be performed with a pull-out button to neutralize the forces of the Lumbrical muscles.

## Correct option

**2. Advancement must not exceed 1.0 cm to maintain the excursion balance of the common FDP muscle belly.**

## Answer explanation

The Quadriga Effect occurs because the FDP tendons of the medial three (and sometimes four) fingers share a common muscle belly. If one tendon is shortened by more than 1.0 cm during repair, that finger will reach the palm prematurely during flexion. Once that tendon "bottoms out," the shared muscle belly cannot contract further, resulting in a significant loss of active flexion and grip strength in the adjacent, uninjured fingers.

Answer: 2
