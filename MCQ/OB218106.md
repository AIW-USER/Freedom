## Question

A 17-year-old female high school soccer player sustains a non-contact injury to her knee during a scrimmage just prior to her season starting. She is unable to bear weight and reports her knee is very swollen. On examination in your clinic, there is a moderate-sized effusion. She is able to flex the knee to 90° but extension lacks 40°. Further examination reveals a 3B-Lachman, stable posterior drawer test, and no instability with varus or valgus stresses at 30°. When positioned prone, her injured knee externally rotates 15° at 30° of flexion, while her contralateral knee externally rotates 10°. McMurray's testing is positive for pain and clicking over the lateral joint line. Radiographs demonstrate no bony injury. An MRI is obtained and a sequence through the intercondylar notch is shown in Figure A. What is the next best step in management for this patient?

## Options

1. Non-operative management with functional ACL bracing followed by an aggressive hamstring strengthening program
2. ACL repair + meniscal repair
3. ACL reconstruction + meniscal repair
4. ACL reconstruction + meniscal repair + medial collateral ligament repair with internal brace
5. ACL reconstruction + meniscal repair + posterolateral corner reconstruction

## Correct option

**3. ACL reconstruction + meniscal repair**

## Answer explanation

This patient's physical examination and imaging findings reveal a mid-substance anterior cruciate ligament (ACL) rupture and a lateral meniscus bucket handle tear.

ACL rupture is a common injury and mostly occurs in a younger, athletic population. Associated injuries are common and can include posterior cruciate ligament tears, collateral ligament tears, or meniscal tears as in this patient. Physical examination findings are important as they can assist in proper diagnosis. The inability to fully extend the knee can indicate a locked, bucket-handle meniscus tear that must be dealt with more urgently in combination with an ACL reconstruction. Additional considerations prior to surgery include physeal evaluation, full-length standing radiographs to evaluate for alignment, and scrutinizing advanced imaging (MRI) to evaluate for additional chondral or intra-articular pathologies that may need to be addressed at the time of surgery.

*Bisson et al. published on bone bruising patterns in patients with ACL ruptures.* **They noted** that patients 17 and younger had more predictable bone bruising patterns and that bone bruising appeared more common and severe in younger males. **They also reported** that medial meniscal tears were associated with increasing severity of lateral tibial plateau bruising patterns.

*Kocabey et al. discussed the value of physical examination compared to MRI in diagnosing meniscal tears and ACL ruptures.* **They noted** no significant difference in the ability to diagnose meniscal tears or ACL ruptures with MRI or physical examination. Their conclusion was that qualified surgeons should rely on their physical examination skills in these cases and MRI should be reserved for more complicated cases.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Non-operative management would not be the best choice for a 17-year-old athlete with an ACL rupture and a likely bucket-handle meniscus tear. This scenario represents a high risk for further intra-articular degeneration early in her lifetime.
Answer 2: While meniscal repair is appropriate, ACL repair is not the best option in this patient with a mid-substance ACL rupture. Research regarding ACL repairs is ongoing, and at this time ACL reconstruction remains the more reliable choice for mid-substance tears. In cases of proximal ACL ruptures, ACL repair may be considered.
Answer 4: This patient's examination demonstrated stability of the medial collateral ligament (MCL) at 30°, making an MCL reconstruction unnecessary.
Answer 5: This patient's examination demonstrated a negative Dial test, with no significant difference (<10° difference) in prone external rotation between the injured and uninjured sides, meaning that posterolateral corner reconstruction would be unnecessary.
