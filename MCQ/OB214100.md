## Question

An arthrogram would serve most useful to help delineate which of the following pediatric elbow injuries?

## Options

1. Displaced medial condyle fracture in a 4-year-old
2. Displaced radial head fracture in a 5-year old
3. Displaced capitellum fracture in a 4-year-old
4. Nondisplaced medial epicondyle fracture in a 6-year-old
5. Nondisplaced trochlea fracture in a 10-year-old

## Correct option

**1. Displaced medial condyle fracture in a 4-year-old**

## Answer explanation

An arthrogram serves as a useful tool to help in the diagnosis and treatment of elbow injuries in the young patient prior to ossification. The medial side of the elbow does not ossify until the age of 5-years which may warrant the use of an arthrogram.

Medial condylar fractures of the humerus are rare injuries in children, making up 1–2% of all pediatric elbow fractures. Similar to lateral condylar fractures, these injuries are prone to nonunion and potential growth arrest. Diagnosis of these fractures can be difficult in younger children because the trochlea ossifies much later than the capitellum and is not completely ossified until around age 9. These fractures involve a metaphyseal fragment, the medial epicondyle, and the cartilaginous trochlea in the younger patient. With a small metaphyseal fragment these injuries may be misdiagnosed as a medial epicondylar fracture which may lead to poor outcome. The order and age of ossification centers about the elbow are important in diagnosing these injuries, as arthrogram or MRI may be needed and radiographs are often incomplete or not useful. The order of ossification is capitellum, radius, internal (medial) epicondyle, trochlea, olecranon, and external (lateral) epicondyle which ossify around 1, 3, 4, 5, 7, and 9 years of age, respectively. The age and order of ossification can be remembered with the mnemonic C-R-I-T-O-E.

*Ghawabi et al. reported on ten fractures of the medial condyle of the humerus.* **They reported** that these avulsion fractures are unstable and at risk of nonunion. **They concluded** that when displaced, these fractures are best treated with rigid internal fixation.

*Zhou et al. retrospectively reviewed medial condyle fracture of the distal humerus (MCFH) in 10 pediatric patients that were treated surgically.* **They reported** that four out of 10 patients had their diagnosis confirmed with the aid of MRI. **They concluded** that MRI is beneficial assisting the diagnosis and allowing effective joint restoration with mid-term good functional outcome.

*Leet et al. retrospective reviewed 21 medial condylar fractures in pediatric patients.* **They reported** a complication rate for these rare fractures to be 33%. **They also noted** that most of the minimally displaced fractures healed uneventfully with immobilization, but operative treatment is indicated if there is >2 mm of displacement at the fracture site. **They concluded** that medial condylar fractures may be difficult to diagnose in younger children and oblique views, arthrography, or magnetic resonance imaging can be helpful in confirming the diagnosis.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: The radius ossifies around the age of 3 and radiographs are likely to aid in the diagnosis. Arthrogram is unlikely to add additional information.
Answer 3: The capitellum ossifies around the age of 1 and radiographs are likely to aid in the diagnosis. Arthrogram is unlikely to be necesary
Answer 4: The medial epicondyle ossifies around the age of 5 and radiographs are likely to aid in the diagnosis. Arthrogram is unlikely to be indicated as the fracture is not intra-articular and is non displaced.
Answer 5: The trochlea ossifies around the age of 9 and radiographs are typically sufficient for diagnosis. Arthrogram is unlikely to be indicated
