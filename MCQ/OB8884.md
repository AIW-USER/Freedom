## Question

An 11-year-old male sustains a fall onto his outstretched left arm at the trampoline park. He complains of severe left arm pain, deformity, and numbness over the dorsum of his hand. His AP radiograph is seen in figure A. The nerve most likely injured by this fracture controls which of the following groups of muscles?

![[q8884-a-posteromedial.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Extensor pollicis longus and extensor carpi ulnaris
2. Opponens pollicis, abductor pollicis, and superficial head of the flexor pollicis brevis
3. Flexor pollicis longus, pronator quadratus, radial half of flexor digitorum profundus
4. Pronator quadratus, flexor pollicis longus, and superficial head of flexor pollicis brevis
5. Abductor digiti minimi and flexor carpi ulnaris

## Correct option

**1. Extensor pollicis longus and extensor carpi ulnaris**

## Answer explanation

This patient has sustained an extension-type supracondylar distal humerus fracture with posteromedial displacement. Given his exam and fracture pattern, he has likely injured his radial nerve. The deep motor branch of the radial nerve is known as the posterior interosseous nerve and innervates the extensor pollicis longus and extensor carpi ulnaris.

Neurologic injury from supracondylar fractures occurs in 10-20% of patients. The anterior interosseous nerve is most commonly injured overall in extension-type supracondylar fractures, and the ulnar nerve is most commonly injured in flexion-type supracondylar fractures. The radial nerve is typically more likely to be injured if the distal fragment is displaced posteromedially. Exploration of the nerve is rarely indicated in a closed fracture and neural recovery typically takes 2 to 3 months.

*Abzug et al review the current management of supracondylar humerus fractures and report AIN injury is the most common, followed by median, radial, and ulnar.* **They emphasize** a careful preoperative and postoperative exam and suggest that postoperative neurologic exam change may indicate iatrogenic injury and warrant operative exploration.

*Babal et al performed a meta-analysis of 5,154 supracondylar fractures and* **found** an overall neuropraxia rate of 11.3%. Of all extension type supracondylar fracture neuropraxias, median nerve, AIN, radial nerve, and ulnar nerve represented 21.3%, 34.1%, 26.6%, and 15.8% respectively. Posteromedial displacement poses a greater biomechanical risk to the radial nerve (as seen in illustration A).

Figure A is an AP radiograph of the left elbow demonstrating a supracondylar distal humerus fracture with posteromedial displacement of the distal fragment. Illustration A demonstrates the how the median or radial nerves can be injured with a supracondylar fracture.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: The opponens pollicis, abductor pollicis, and superficial head of the flexor pollicis brevis are all innervated by the recurrent motor branch of the median nerve.
Answer 3: The flexor pollicis longus, pronator quadratus, and radial half of the flexor digitorum profundus are innervated by the AIN.
Answer 4: The pronator quadratus and flexor pollicis longus are innervated by the AIN, but the superficial head of flexor pollicis brevis is innervated by the recurrent motor branch of the median nerve.
Answer 5: The abductor digiti minimi and the FCU are innervated by the ulnar nerve.
