## Question

A 50-year-old man undergoes marginal excision of a tumor in his thigh. Figure A and B are preoperative T1-weighted axial MRI imaging and a pathological slide of this tumor. Immunohistochemistry reveals stain positivity for MDM2 and CDK4. What is the most likely diagnosis?

![[q5722-b-fig-d-atyp-lipo.jpg]]

## Options

1. rhabdomyosarcoma
2. lipoma
3. atypical lipomatous tumor
4. leiomyosarcoma
5. myxofibrosarcoma

## Correct option

**3. atypical lipomatous tumor**

## Answer explanation

This patient has an atypical lipomatous tumor (also known as a well differentiated liposarcoma if found in a retroperitoneal location). MDM2 and CDK4 oncogene amplification differentiates it from other sarcomas. Benign lipomas do not stain for MDM2 and CDK4.

Atypical lipomatous tumors are a low-grade malignancy of relatively mature adipocytes that exhibit focal atypia. It is a common liposarcoma subtype (40-45% of liposarcomas) and peaks at ages 40-60 years. It is found in the lower limbs, retroperitoneum, trunk and head and neck region. Treatment is with marginal excision. It does not metastasize unless it dedifferentiates. Subcutaneous or intramuscular tumors may recur but typically do not dedifferentiate or metastasize. Retroperitoneal tumors are difficult to resect with clear margins and have poor prognosis. Ring chromosomes derived from 12q13-15 are found in almost all cases. The sensitivity/specificity of CDK4 for detecting atypical lipomatous tumors were 86% and 89%, those of MDM2 were 86% and 74%.

*Wong et al. looked at MDM2/CDK4 gene amplification in 140 large/deep-seated tumors thought to be lipomas.* **They found** that the incidence of MDM2/CDK4 amplification in this setting is low (5%) (95%CI 1.4-8.6%). Variables associated with amplification were location (thigh), size (>10cm) and presence of equivocal atypia. Gene amplification did not affect recurrence rates. **They recommend** assessing for MDM2/CDK4 amplification in lipomas which are >100 mm in size, show equivocal atypia, and arise in the thigh although the significance of this gene amplification in this setting is unclear.

Figure A shows an axial T1-weighted MRI of an atypical lipomatous tumor. There are areas of fat that are not isointense with subcutaneous fat (termed "dirty fat") consistent with the diagnosis of atypical lipomatous tumor. Figure B shows atypical lipomatous tumor. There are mature lipocytes with variation is size and shape, scattered atypical cells with pleomorphism, hyperchromatic nuclei. Fibrous tissue septa are present that contain spindle cells or highly pleomorphic cells. Illustration A shows an axial T1-weighted MRI of a mass that is isointense with fat, consistent with a lipoma. Illustration B shows a lipoma with neoplastic adipocytes that are indistinguishable from normal adipocytes.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Rhabdomyosarcomas (RMS) occur mostly in children and have the histological appearance of a "small, round, blue cell tumor". They stain for muscle-specific proteins: myogenin (highly specific), muscle-specific actin, desmin, D-myosin, and myoD1. Expression of PAX3-FKHR fusion gene occurs in alveolar RMS. They do not stain for MDM2 and CDK4.
Answer 2: Lipomas do not stain for MDM2 and CDK4.
Answer 4: Leiomyosarcomas (LMS) are rare soft tissue neoplasms. A primary LMS of the extremities is likely to have arisen from the wall of a blood vessel. They do not stain for MDM2 and CDK4.
Answer 5: Myxofibrosarcoma is a common soft tissue sarcoma of elderly patients. It stains for pan-keratin, S-100 protein, desmin, and alpha-smooth muscle actin. They do not stain for MDM2 and CDK4.
