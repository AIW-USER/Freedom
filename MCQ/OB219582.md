## Question

A 23-year-old female arrives in the trauma bay with a right 3B open mid-shaft femur fracture. Her lactate is 5, and your team decides to proceed with irrigation, debridement, and external fixator application. Before the case, you are templating potential pin placement on her contralateral femur radiographs. Which pin would be safe to place anteriorly from the options shown in Figures A and B?

![[q219582-b-femur-distal.jpg]]

## Options

1. A
2. B
3. C
4. D

## Correct option

**1. A**

## Answer explanation

The only pin that would allow for safe entry of an anterior pin out of the options demonstrated in Figure A/B is pin "A" (Answer 1).

The placement of proximal anterior half pins poses a risk to femoral nerve branches, with studies identifying these risks extending as far distally as 5.8 cm from the lesser trochanter. Similarly, distal anterior half pins may penetrate the suprapatellar pouch, posing intra-articular infection risks, with the pouch extending as proximally as 7.4 cm from the superior pole of the patella. These findings highlight the limited safe zone for anterior femoral half-pin placement.

*Haidukewych et al. describe techniques for lower extremity external fixation in the setting of complex lower extremity fractures.* **They conclude** that when approaching femoral shaft half-pin placement, the surgeon should proceed utilizing established safe windows for placement, employ percutaneous techniques with soft-tissue sleeves and pre-drilling, confirm length with fluoroscopy, and avoid clustering multiple pins to prevent stress risers.

*Beltran et al. defined the safe zone for anterior external fixator half pins in the femur as an average of 20 cm in length but can be as narrow as 12 cm.* Proximally, to avoid injury to the femoral nerve, the surgeon should avoid pin placement within 5.8 cm of the lesser trochanter. Distally, the surgeon should not place pins within 7.5 cm from the superior pole of the patella as this poses a risk of intraarticular pin placement, potentially increasing the risk of infection. Thus, **they determined** that the safest pin placement about the anterior femur is 4 cm distal to this isthmus. Given this narrow safe zone, surgeons should consider alternative anterolateral or lateral pin placement.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 2/3: Options "B" and "C" place the femoral nerve at risk. In the above-cited cadaveric study, the last branch of the femoral nerve crossed at an average distance from the anterior superior iliac spine of 174 ± 43 mm (range, 95–248 mm) and from the lesser trochanter at a distance of 58 ± 36 mm (range, 0–136 mm).
Answer 4/5: Option "D" increases the risk of intra-articular pin placement, possibly predisposing the patient to increased infection risk. In the above-cited cadaveric study, the average distance from the proximal pole of the patella to the superior reflection of the suprapatellar pouch was 46.3 ± 13.1 mm (range 20–74 mm).
