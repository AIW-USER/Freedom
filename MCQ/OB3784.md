## Question

An evaluation metric used in spinal biomechanics constituting the theoretical axis about which each functional spinal unit rotates at a point in time is known as the:

## Options

1. Center of rotation of angulation
2. Virtual center of rotation of angulation
3. Instantaneous axis of rotation
4. Vertebral incidence
5. Vertebral plumb line

## Correct option

**3. Instantaneous axis of rotation**

## Answer explanation

The instantaneous axis of rotation (IAR) is defined as a theoretical axis about which a functional spinal unit rotates at a discrete point in time. The IAR is not a fixed point but has been shown to move constantly with bending and rotational moments.

The IAR can be used to map the kinematic behavior of the spine. As a result, the IAR can then be used as a metric to compare the biomechanics of the diseased, injured, or post-operative spine to that of an intact, uninjured state. This translates to an understanding of the contribution of the various parts of the spine to pathological motion.

*Inoue et al. evaluated the impact of spinal fixation on the IAR.* *The authors compared the kinematic parameters from the injured status to those following pedicle screw fixation and quantified this in terms of the location of the applied mechanical load.* **They found** that pedicle screw instrumentation shifted the IAR and effectively reduced the mechanical load on the posterior neurologic elements.

*Haher et al. compared the behavior of the IAR in the uninjured state to that in the setting of isolated sequential destruction of the three columns.* **The authors noted** that the IAR migrated to the intact structures as the spine was sequentially released and determined the importance of the anterior structures in resisting rotational moments. **They concluded** that an understanding of the contribution of the various structures to spinal stability would influence fixation.

Illustration A depicts the IAR in one plane for a single motion at a single vertebral level. The IAR however is not a single stationary point and is more complicated to determine when accounting for more complex movements in multiple planes.

Incorrect answers:
Answers 1 and 2: Refer to principles of deformity correction. The center of rotation of angulation, or CORA, is defined as the intersection between proximal and distal mechanical axes.
Answer 4: Vertebral incidence has only been described for the L5 vertebral body in the setting of spinopelvic balance and spondylolisthesis.
Answer 5: Vertebral plumb line has only been described for the C7 vertebral body in relation to sagittal balance of the spine.
