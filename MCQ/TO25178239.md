## Question

Recent literature comparing Ilizarov distraction histogenesis to comprehensive open posteromedial release (PMR) for neglected clubfoot in older children (>3 years) suggests which of the following regarding long-term functional outcomes?

## Options

1. External fixation methods are associated with better preservation of joint mobility and reduced recurrence rates in rigid cases.
2. Both methods have identical rates of wound dehiscence and skin margin necrosis.
3. Open PMR results in superior foot power and calf muscle girth compared to distraction methods.
4. Ilizarov methods show a higher rate of avascular necrosis of the talus due to constant joint distraction.

## Correct option

**1. External fixation methods are associated with better preservation of joint mobility and reduced recurrence rates in rigid cases.**

## Answer explanation

By avoiding extensive surgical scarring (which leads to stiffness) and utilizing slow tissue expansion, distraction methods often maintain better range of motion.

The "Acute Tension" Problem in PMR:

In an open PMR, you perform a "360-degree" release. When you acutely bring a neglected foot into a plantigrade position, you create massive tension on the medial skin and neurovascular bundle.

 - Vascular Insult: Acute stretching can cause vasospasm of the posterior tibial artery, leading to the dreaded "white toe."

 - The Scar Cycle: Extensive surgery creates a large internal hematoma that heals by secondary intention (fibrosis). This thick scar tissue is what causes the "stiff, wooden foot" 10–20 years later.

The Ilizarov Principle (Distraction Histogenesis):

 - Biological Growth: Slow distraction (1mm/day) doesn't just "stretch" tissues; it stimulates neovascularization
 and collagen synthesis. The skin, nerves, and vessels actually grow longer.

 - Joint Preservation (Arthrodiatasis): By using the frame to "distract" the joint surfaces before moving them, you prevent the high-pressure "grinding" of cartilage that occurs when a tight, rigid joint is forced into a new position.

 - Stability without Scarring: Because the correction is percutaneous (pins only), the internal "lubrication" of the tarsal joints is preserved. The lack of a large medial incision means no "scar-tether" to cause recurrence as the child grows.

Answer: 1
