## Question

A 46-year-old otherwise healthy man comes to clinic with 6 months of right elbow pain. It has been nonresponsive to NSAIDs. He has pain over the lateral aspect of elbow. The pain is exacerbated with resisted wrist extension. What is the origin and insertion, respectively, of the muscle most commonly implicated in this disorder?

## Options

1. Lateral supracondylar ridge, second metacarpal
2. Lateral supracondylar ridge, third metacarpal
3. Lateral supracondylar ridge, Lister's tubercle
4. Lateral epicondyle, second metacarpal
5. Lateral epicondyle, third metacarpal

## Correct option

**5. Lateral epicondyle, third metacarpal**

## Answer explanation

The patient is presenting with signs and symptoms consistent with lateral epicondylitis, also known as tennis elbow. The implicated muscle, the extensor carpi radialis brevis (ECRB) originates on the anterior aspect of the lateral epicondyle and inserts on the third metacarpal.

Lateral epicondylitis is an overuse injury that leads to inflammation and tendon degeneration at the origin of the ECRB. Pathology specimens typically reveal disorganized collagen angiofibroblastic hyperplasia. Conservative treatment, consisting of activity modification, ECRB stretching, splinting, and corticosteroid injections, is successful in over 95% of cases. Refractory cases require release, debridement, and reattachment of the ECRB origin.

*Brummel et al. reviewed the clinical presentation and management of lateral epicondylitis.* *They make a distinction between the acute symptoms from injury in young athletes and chronic, slowly progressing symptoms in older adults.* Mainstay of treatment is conservative.

*Calfee et al. review current concepts in the management of lateral epicondylitis.* **They suggest** that the underlying joint capsule and collateral ligament complex may sometimes be implicated in severe disease. New arthroscopic and percutaneous techniques for surgical management have been shown to be effective in low level studies and case series.

Illustration A is diagram highlighting the ECRL and ECRB. Notice the higher origin of the ECRL on lateral supracondylar ridge as compared to the ECRB origin on the lateral epicondyle.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Extensor carpi radialis longus (ECRL) originates the lateral supracondylar ridge and inserts on the second metacarpal
Answer 2-4: Incorrect combination of answers. No muscle inserts on Lister's tubercle
