## Question

## Answer explanation

Proximal humeral epiphysiolysis (little league shoulder) or osteochondrosis of the proximal humeral epiphysis may result from repetitious significant external rotational torque on the humeral shaft during the late arm-cocking phase (Answer C in Figure A).

Little leaguer's shoulder (LLS) is an overuse injury occurring in young baseball pitchers resulting in epiphysiolysis of the proximal humerus (a Salter Harris Type 1 injury). The typical age at presentation is between 11 and 16 years. LLS is the result of excessive rotational and distraction forces that occur with repetitive overhead throwing. Torque in the late cocking-phase of throwing likely leads to the deformation of the proximal humeral epiphysis cartilage, leading to proximal humeral epiphysiolysis. The distraction force is believed to have a lesser role than the rotational force. Diagnosis is made with radiographs of the shoulder showing a widened proximal humerus physis in comparison to contralateral shoulder. Treatment is cessation of throwing, followed by PT and progressive throwing program after sufficient rest.

*Heyworth et al. reviewed the demographic, clinical, and diagnostic features of a population of LLS patients, with an emphasis on identifying underlying risk factors for the development and recurrence of LLS after nonoperative treatment.* **They reported** that after rest and physical therapy, recurrent symptoms may occur in a small subset of patients (7%), generally 3 to 6 months after return to sports. **They concluded** that almost one-third of LLS patients had GIRD, and this group had approximately three times higher probability of recurrence compared with those without GIRD.

*Thompson et al. reviewed the kinetics and kinematics of the youth baseball pitching motion.* **They reported** that progressive external rotation of the shoulder occurs throughout the start of the pitching motion, reaching a maximum of 166° to 178°, before internally rotating throughout the remainder of the cycle, reaching a minimum of 13° to 17°. **They concluded** that elbow valgus torque reaches the highest level (18 ± 4 N·m) just prior to maximum shoulder external rotation and decreases throughout the remainder of the pitch cycle forces on the elbow and shoulder are greater for the fastball than the curveball.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1,2,4,5: LLS is the result of excessive rotational and distraction forces that occur with repetitive overhead throwing. The pathophysiology leading to this injury is believed to be the significant external rotational torque on the humeral shaft during the final part of the arm-cocking phase, just before the acceleration phase.
