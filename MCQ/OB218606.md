## Question

A 4-year-old boy without medical comorbidities presents with progressive pain in his heel and is having difficulty ambulating. The patient is found to have osteomyelitis of the calcaneus, as shown in Figure A. Blood cultures are positive for Kingella kingae. He is admitted and eventually discharged on intravenous (IV) antibiotics administered through a peripheral inserted central catheter (PICC). When comparing uncomplicated pediatric osteomyelitis treated with 6 weeks of oral versus IV antibiotics, which of the following is true?

![[q218606-a-figure-a.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Oral antibiotics have a significantly higher rate of treatment failure
2. Oral antibiotics have a significantly higher rate of adverse events
3. PICC line administered IV antibiotics incur a lower cost of treatment
4. PICC line administered IV antibiotics incur a higher rate of rehospitalization
5. There are no differences in treatment efficacy or adverse events between both groups

## Correct option

**4. PICC line administered IV antibiotics incur a higher rate of rehospitalization**

## Answer explanation

When compared to oral antibiotics, PICC line-administered IV antibiotics for pediatric osteomyelitis have significantly higher rates of subsequent ED visits or rehospitalizations with no difference in rates of successful infection eradication.

Acute osteomyelitis in the pediatric population should initially be treated with IV antibiotics during hospitalization with/without surgical intervention. If there is an underlying abscess or sequestrum, then surgical intervention is warranted. Once clinical improvement is noted, patients can be discharged on IV or oral antibiotic therapy for typically six weeks. PICC lines are associated with inconvenient complications such as local skin irritation/pain, line disruption from a blood clot requiring localized TPA administration, and PICC infection requiring removal (Illustrations A&B). Due to these concerns, there has been a gradual shift towards the early transition to oral antibiotics upon discharge based on culture sensitivity results. Collectively, oral antibiotics have been shown to be equivalent to IV antibiotics in eradicating uncomplicated pediatric acute osteomyelitis, however, there are some selective exceptions that remain in question (i.e. if this is also true for treating more aggressive organisms like MRSA).

*Arkader and colleagues reviewed the common causes of musculoskeletal infections in pediatric patients.* *They examined the diagnostic workup, commonly involved organisms (i.e. Staphylococcus aureus), and potential contrasting differential diagnoses.* *They stress the importance of a multidisciplinary approach to treating musculoskeletal infections in the pediatric population.* These teams allow for shorter hospital stays and lower hospital readmission rates.

*Keren and colleagues utilized the Pediatric Health Information System (PHIS) in order to assess discharge antibiotic therapy protocols used in pediatric patients with acute osteomyelitis.* *They performed a retrospective propensity score-matching analysis and found no significant difference in treatment failure rates between patients discharged on oral versus PICC line-administered IV antibiotics.* However, they did find a significant difference in adverse events with PICC-related complication rates being much higher due to emergency department visits or re-hospitalization. **The authors concluded** that otherwise healthy children with acute osteomyelitis should be transitioned to oral antibiotics upon discharge.

Figure A shows sagittal T2 MRI imaging with hyperintensity within the body of the calcaneus bone consistent with osteomyelitis. This patient presented with an inability to ambulate, elevated inflammatory markers, low-grade fever, and positive blood cultures for Kingella kingae species. They were admitted for IV antibiotics and a PICC line was placed for post-discharge antibiotics. Illustration A shows an ultrasound of the same patient’s arm 3 weeks later when they presented to the emergency department with progressive swelling of their arm. A 1x1cm pocket of fluid was noted on ultrasound examination and was later found to be a small abscess that was drained (resulting in MSSA). Illustration B shows this patient’s arm after the PICC line was removed prior to abscess drainage. They were transitioned to oral antibiotics.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Oral antibiotics do NOT have a significantly higher rate of treatment failure compared to IV antibiotics in uncomplicated pediatric osteomyelitis.
Answer 2: Oral antibiotics have a significantly LOWER rate of adverse events, mainly via a lower rate of emergency department visits or re-hospitalization.
Answer 3: PICC line administered IV antibiotics incur a HIGHER cost of treatment. The cost of PICC line insertion and infusions is generally much more expensive than oral medications.
Answer 5. As discussed, while there are no differences in treatment efficacy, there ARE differences in adverse events between both groups. PICC line-administered antibiotics for acute uncomplicated pediatric osteomyelitis has a higher rate of adverse events due to PICC line-associated complications. This manifests primarily in higher rates of emergency department visits and higher rates of rehospitalization.
