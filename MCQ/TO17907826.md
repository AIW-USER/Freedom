## Question

A patient was undergoing TKR. He landed up with sudden onset of hypotonia and breathing difficulty after tourniquet deflation. The likely reason

## Options

1. Bone cement implantation syndrome
2. Anaphylaxis
3. Fat embolism
4. Myocardial infarction

## Correct option

**1. Bone cement implantation syndrome**

## Answer explanation

Ans: A

Bone cement implantation syndrome (BCIS) seems to be best pick. Other options like myocardial infarction causes central chest pain with or without hemodynamic instability and anaphylaxis due to cement is very rare. Fat embolism would generally occur on intramedullary rod insertions into medullary canals which is not corelating with tourniquet deflation.

BONE CEMENT IMPLANTATION SYNDROME

BCIS is characterized by hypoxia, hypotension or both and/or unexpected loss of consciousness with cardiac arrythmias occurring around the time of cementation, prosthesis insertion, reduction of the joint or, occasionally, limb tourniquet deflation in a patient undergoing cemented joint replacement surgery. It is an important cause of intraoperative mortality and morbidity in patients undergoing cemented hip arthroplasty and may also be seen in the postoperative period in a milder form causing hypoxia and confusion.

Pathogenesis: Embolization occurs as a result of high intramedullary pressures developing between cement and prosthesis insertion. The cement undergoes an exothermic reaction and expands between the space of the bone and prosthesis trapping air and medullary contents under pressure. The temperature of cement can increase up to 96oC. When prosthesis is put, the contents escape into the interstices of bone because of already developed intramedullary hypertension. This leads to embolization.

Severity classification of BCIS is as under:

Grade 1: moderate hypoxia (SpO2 <94%) or hypotension [fall in systolic blood pressure (SBP) >20%]

Grade 2: severe hypoxia (SpO2 <88%) or hypotension (fall in SBP >40%) or unexpected loss of consciousness.

Grade 3: cardiovascular collapse requiring CPR.

TREATMENT: Identification is the key to saving the life. IV fluids are rushed to maintain intra vascular volume and inotropes and vasopressors are given. In case still there is no hemodynamic recovery, then ECMO (Extracorporeal membrane oxygenation, that uses a pump to circulate blood through an artificial lung back into the bloodstream, removing the emboli) is considered.
