## Question

You are planning to perform total knee arthroplasty (TKA) on a 57-year-old female with knee osteoarthritis. At her preoperative appointment, you learn that she is a materials engineer. This spawns a lengthy discussion on materials used in TKA. You plan to use a highly cross-linked polyethylene insert. What effect would irradiation in an oxygen-rich environment have on the polyethylene insert?

## Options

1. Superior mechanical properties
2. Greater implant survival
3. Improved resistance to adhesive and abrasive wear
4. Creates a decreased number of free radicals
5. Lead to early failure

## Correct option

**5. Lead to early failure**

## Answer explanation

Irradiation of polyethylene in an oxygen-rich environment causes oxidation, which leads to early failure due to subsurface delamination, pitting, and cracking (answer choice 5).

The manufacturing process for the polyethylene inserts used in total joint arthroplasty is complex and has evolved significantly over the last several generations. Conventional ultra-high-molecular-weight polyethylene (UHMWPE) was introduced in the 1960s and is formed from 200,000 repeated ethylene subunits. It was traditionally sterilized with gamma radiation in the air at a dose of 2.5 Mrad; however, this was found to introduce too much oxidation, which increased fatigue wear. Thus, gamma-irradiation in inert gas was developed to reduce oxidation.

*Rimnac et al. discuss how the properties of ultra-high-molecular-weight polyethylene (UHMWPE) used in hip and knee implants influence their wear and fracture.* It explains how altering the material through processes like cross-linking can improve wear resistance but may increase susceptibility to fracture. **The authors emphasize** the need for better testing methods to evaluate new UHMWPE formulations for wear and fracture resistance to ensure long-term implant success.

*Crowninshield et al. explore how newer methods of sterilizing and processing polyethylene used in joint replacements have affected the wear rates of these implants.* It explains that while some new sterilization techniques reduce oxidation, they may not improve wear resistance compared to traditional gamma irradiation in inert environments. The development of highly cross-linked polyethylene through increased radiation doses has significantly reduced wear rates, improving joint replacements' longevity.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: Irradiation actually causes a decrease in mechanical properties (decreased ductility and fatigue resistance).
Answer 2: Irradiation in air leads to early failure.
Answer 3: Irradiation in an oxygen-depleted environment improves resistance to adhesive and abrasive deamination.
Answer 4: Irradiation in an oxygen-rich environment leads to more free radicals and oxidative degradation
