## Question

A 67-year-old male presents to the emergency department with hip pain of two days duration. He denies any recent trauma. He has a past medical history significant for metastatic lung cancer. Initial labs include an elevated the sedimentation rate and elevated alkaline phosphatase. Radiographs of the hip show no fracture with moderate osteoarthritis. A bone scan is negative for any bony metastatic lesions. You reassure the patient that his hip pain is unlikely to be metastatic disease and initiate conservative management of his hip osteoarthritis. You recommend further work up based on his laboratory abnormalities, however. What additional testing may help to explain his elevated alkaline phosphatase?

## Options

1. Carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA)
2. MRI brain
3. CT scan of hip
4. ALT/AST
5. Prostate-specific antigen (PSA)

## Correct option

**4. ALT/AST**

## Answer explanation

Alkaline phosphatase is also secreted by the liver and in cases where there is no recognizable bony pathology with an elevation in the alkaline phosphatase, liver function should be evaluated for possible metastatic lesions. Alanine aminotransferase (ALT) and aspartate aminotransferase (AST) would be part of a further workup to evaluate hepatocyte function.

Alkaline phosphatase is an enzyme that is secreted primarily by the liver and bones. Increases in alkaline phosphatase can be seen in the setting of multiple bony pathologies, usually relating to increased bone turnover and osteoblast activity. This includes, but is not limited to, Paget's disease, metastatic bone disease, and osteomalacia. Conversely, alkaline phosphatase can also be increased in cases of liver pathology including hepatitis, cirrhosis, metastatic liver disease, biliary obstruction or cholecystitis. With an elevated alkaline phosphatase, known metastatic cancer and minimal suspicion for bony metastases, liver pathology should be ruled out.

*Rao et al. evaluated levels of serum alkaline phosphatase in patients with metastatic cancer.* **They noted** that elevated levels of alkaline phosphatase may be related to more bony turnover and more distant metastasis. **They concluded** that tumor cells having increased alkaline phosphatase production was a negative prognostic factor and signified increased progression.

*Saif et al. looked at using serum alkaline phosphatase as a prognostic tool for evaluating hepatic metastasis in the setting of known cancer.* **They found** that patients with elevation of their alkaline phosphatase were 5.7 times more likely to have hepatic metastasis compared to patients with normal levels. They add that higher alkaline phosphatase levels correlated with a worse prognosis.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA) is mostly used to evaluate for colorectal cancers and would not be most useful in evaluating liver pathology in the setting of an elevated alkaline phosphatase
Answer 2: MRI of the brain would not be helpful in diagnosing metastatic liver lesions in this patient with elevated alkaline phosphatase and known metastatic cancer
Answer 3: CT scan of the hip would not be most indicated based on the elevated alkaline phosphatase as radiographs do not demonstrate fracture and bone scan did not identify any areas concerning for bony hip metastases
Answer 5: Prostate-specific antigen (PSA) could be used to screen for metastatic prostate cancer but this would not assist with diagnosis based on the patient’s laboratory values which indicate liver pathology
