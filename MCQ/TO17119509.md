Chapter: Arthroscopy MCQs — Posterolateral Corner Injuries

## Question

What is the use of the test depicted in the image?

![[TO17119509-1.png]]

## Options

1. To detect PCL injury
2. To detect meniscus injury
3. To detect PLC injury
4. To detect ACL injury

## Correct option

**3. To detect PLC injury**

## Answer explanation

The test described in the question is a dial test. A positive dial test at 30-degree knee flexion suggests posterolateral corner (PLC) injury and positive test at 90 degrees knee flexion suggest PCL tear in addition to PLC injury

Dial test:

Patient is positioned supine on the bed with leg to be examined hanging over the edge of the bed. Now the examiner stabilizes the distal femur with one hand and holds the heel with the other hand. The examiner flexes the knee to 30°, and externally rotates the leg maximally, holding it through the heel. Position of tibial tuberosity is noted. Same maneuver is repeated on the other side. If external rotation is more by 15° as compared to the other side, a posterolateral corner injury (PLC) is suspected. The test is repeated with the knee flexed further to 90°. Increased external rotation at 90 degrees (as compared to 30°) indicates concomitant PLC with PCL injury.

Same test can be done in a prone position in which both feet are held and rotated first at 30 degrees and then at 90 degrees. Angle between the sagittal plane of the sole and body is noted. Increased external rotation at 30 degrees indicates PLC injury while increased external rotation at 90 degrees indicates combined PLC and PCL injury.

![[TO17119509-2.png]]

Other tests done for this condition are:

- External rotation recurvatum
- Posterolateral drawer test
- Reverse pivot shift test

Answer: 3
