## Question

A 45-year-old male construction worker presents with right ankle pain after falling from a two-story building and landing on his right leg. He noticed immediate pain and inability to bear weight on the affected limb. Physical examination reveals diffuse soft tissue swelling around the ankle joint without any open injuries. The patient reveals he never completed a high school degree, smokes 1/2 a pack of cigarettes per day, and occasionally uses marijuana recreationally. Injury radiographs are shown in figures A and B. The patient undergoes an ankle-spanning external fixator placement for soft tissue stabilization and then undergoes definitive fixation shown in figures C and D. Which factor suggests a poor clinical outcome and failure to return to work?

## Options

1. Use of a temporizing external fixator
2. Varus collapse of the distal tibia at the time of injury
3. Recreational marijuana use
4. Lower level of education
5. Use of more than one plate for definitive fixation of the tibia

## Correct option

**4. Lower level of education**

## Answer explanation

Poor clinical outcomes and inability to return to work following tibial plafond fracture have been associated with a lower level of education.

Pilon fractures, also known as tibial plafond fractures, are high-energy injuries that commonly present with extensive soft tissue swelling. Despite successful internal fixation, these injuries can be fraught with poor clinical outcomes. Some authors have found disease impact on the quality of life to be greater than cancer or acquired immunodeficiency syndrome diagnosis. Several factors have been associated with a poor prognosis for return to work and poor clinical outcomes, including a lower level of education, increased medical comorbidities, lower-income levels, male sex, and work-related injuries.

*Patzakis and Wilkins performed a retrospective study of 1104 open fractures to determine factors affecting infection rates.* **They reported** 77 infections with early administration of antibiotics covering gram-positive and gram-negative being the single most important preventative factor.

*Tornetta and Gorup studied computed tomography scans of 22 patients with tibial pilon fractures.* **They reported** an increased number of fragments in 12 patients, increased impaction in 6 patients, and increased comminution in 11 patients. **The authors concluded** the CT scans altered the surgical plan in 64% of the patients and additional information was gained in 82%.

*Sirkin et al. performed a retrospective study of 56 AO type C pilon fractures that were treated with a staged treatment protocol consisting of initial open reduction internal fixation (ORIF) of the fibula and ankle spanning external fixation followed by definitive fixation.* **They reported** an average of 12.7 days to conversion from ex-fixation to ORIF and all closed pilon fractures surgical wounds healing with no full-thickness skin necrosis. **The authors reported** a high rate of successful healing and reduced infection complications of pilon fractures with staged ORIF.

*Mehta et al. reviewed reduction strategies through the anterolateral approach for type B and C pilon fractures and retrospectively reported the results of 131 patients treated.* **They reported** the anterolateral approach allows reduction of the entire articular surface and of the 131 patients included there were three wound infections requiring operative debridement and seven superficial peroneal nerve palsies. **They concluded** the anterolateral approach is not suitable for fractures with medial comminution, medial crush, impaction of the medial shoulder of the joint, segmental medial malleolar injuries, or varus deformity at the time of the injury.

Figures A and B are the AP and lateral radiographs of the right ankle depicting a comminuted and varus collapse tibial pilon fracture. Figures C and D are the AP and lateral radiographs of the right ankle after an open reduction internal fixation of the pilon and fibula fracture.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: The use of a temporizing external fixator has not been associated with poorer clinical outcomes and the inability to return to work. Rather, it has been associated with decreased catastrophic wound and infection complications.
Answer 2: Varus collapse of the distal tibia has not been associated with worse clinical outcomes or the inability to return to work.
Answer 3: Recreational marijuana use has not been associated with worse clinical outcomes or the inability to return to work.
Answer 5: Use of more than one plate for definitive fixation of the distal tibia has not been linked to worse outcomes.
