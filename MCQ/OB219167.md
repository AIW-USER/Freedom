## Question

A 23-year-old male presents with complaints of right arm pain after a fall off of a roof. Radiographs reveal an extra-articular fracture of the distal humeral shaft. The patient is neurovascularly intact. The decision is made to proceed with nonoperative management with use of a Sarmiento brace. What is the most likely type of deformity that results from this injury?

## Options

1. Procurvatum
2. Recurvatum
3. Valgus malalignment
4. Varus malalignment
5. Nonunion

## Correct option

**4. Varus malalignment**

## Answer explanation

Varus malalignment is the most likely result of nonoperative treatment of this injury.

Humeral shaft fractures are common fractures of the diaphysis of the humerus, which may be associated with radial nerve injury. Diagnosis is made with orthogonal radiographs of the humerus. Treatment can be nonoperative or operative depending on location of fracture, fracture morphology, and association with other ipsilateral injuries. The most likely outcome of nonoperative treatment for this injury is a healed fracture with some degree of varus alignment. Fortunately, varus alignment often falls within clinically and radiographically acceptable parameters and will rarely require a delayed osteotomy.

*Jawa et al. performed a retrospective comparison of both operative and nonoperative treatment of distal-third diaphyseal fractures of the humerus.* **They reported** that for extra-articular distal-third diaphyseal humeral fractures, operative treatment achieves more predictable alignment and potentially quicker return of function but risks iatrogenic nerve injury and infection and the need for reoperation. **They concluded** that functional bracing can be associated with skin problems and varying degrees of angular deformity, but function and range of motion are usually excellent.

*Swellengrebel et al. reviewed diaphyseal fractures of the distal humerus.* **They reported** that these injuries have a high rate of union when treated with a functional brace or an above-elbow cast (AEC). **They concluded** that closed extra-articular distal diaphyseal humerus fractures heal with both bracing and casting and there are no differences in average elbow motion or radiographic alignment.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1-3: Varus malalignment is the most common type of malignment following nonoperative management of extra-articular fracture of the distal humerus.
Answer 5: Nonunion can occur but is less common than uneventful healing.
