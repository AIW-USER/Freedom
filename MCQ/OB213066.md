## Options

1. ACL reconstruction and delayed meniscal allograft transplantation
2. ACL reconstruction and meniscectomy
3. ACL reconstruction and an all-inside meniscal repair in a horizontal mattress fashion
4. ACL reconstruction and a staged inside-out meniscal repair
5. ACL reconstruction and meniscal root repair

## Correct option

**5. ACL reconstruction and meniscal root repair**

## Answer explanation

The imaging shows an ACL rupture and medial meniscal root tear, which both should be addressed.

Meniscal injuries are common knee pathology, especially in the setting of ligamentous injury. Management of these depends on the location, chronicity, and pattern of the tear. Dispalced peripheral, longitudinal tears (which present as the "double PCL sign" on MRI) are often amenable to repair, while horizontal radial tears have poor healing potential and are often excised. Meniscal root tears destabilize the entire meniscus and should be repaired in a prompt fashion.

*Laible et al. review the management of meniscal tears.* **They note** the equivalence of all-inside, inside-out, and outside-in techniques in tear fixation. However, root tears must be repaired with either suture anchors or a trans-osseous suture repair to restore the native anatomic relationship to the plateau.

*Beaufils et al. described their approach for meniscal pathology.* **They advocate** for the repair of most longitudinal vertical tears, menisci-capsular lesion with ACL ruptures, horizontal tears in young athletes, radial and root tears. **They advocate** for judicious meniscectomy for degenerative and complex meniscal tears failing to respond to physical therapy.

Figures C and D are fluid-sensitive sagittal MR sequences showing an ACL rupture and a posterior medial meniscal root tear. Illustration A shows various patterns of meniscal tears. Illustration B shows a schematic of trans-osseous meniscal root repair.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Meniscal allograft transplantation may be an option if the meniscal root repair fails but repair should be attempted first.
Answer 2: Meniscectomy would further destabilize the meniscus.
Answers 3 & 4: These are options for peripheral tears but not for root repairs. Additionally, meniscal repairs have higher rates of healing when done at the time of ACL reconstruction.
