## Question

A 58-year-old female patient presents with long standing posterior heel pain and a tender mass over the posterior heel. She has attempted treatment with anti-inflammatory medications, physical therapy, and shoe modifications without relief. A radiograph of her heel is seen in Figure A. She proceeds with surgical intervention, which requires open debridement of 55% of the Achilles tendon centrally. The next most appropriate surgical steps involve suture anchor repair of the Achilles insertion and which of the following?

![[q5655-a-haglunds.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Primary closure of the wound
2. Wound vac placement and staged wound closure
3. Side to side repair of the achilles tendon and primary closure
4. Augmentation with flexor hallucis longus transfer followed by primary closure
5. Augmentation with posterior tibialis tendon transfer followed by primary closure

## Correct option

**4. Augmentation with flexor hallucis longus transfer followed by primary closure**

## Answer explanation

Surgical treatment of insertional achilles tendonitis involves debridement of the degenerative portion of the tendon. If this amounts to more than 50% of the tendon, then transfer of the flexor hallucis longus (FHL) may be used for augmentation.

Insertional Achilles tendonitis presents as posterior heel pain with potential progression to retrocalcaneal bursitis and progressive bony enlargement of the Achilles tendon's insertion on the calcaneus (Haglund's deformity). Initial treatment is non-operative including physical therapy for a tight heel cord, shoe modifications, and anti-inflammatory medications. If this fails, surgical debridement of the Achilles tendon and exostectomy are performed. If this debridement requires removal of more than 50% of the Achilles tendon, then transfer of another tendon (FHL, FDL, or PB) is done for augmentation.

*Kang et al. compared the height and peak angles of Haglund's deformity in patients with and without insertional Achilles tendonitis.* **They found** no difference in these measurements between the two groups.

*Nunley et al. review long term outcomes of a central incision technique for debridement and exostectomy of recalcitrant insertional achilles tendonitis.* *They had a 96% satisfaction rate, and 22 or 23 patients were pain-free at long term follow-up.*

Figure A is a lateral radiograph of a calcaneus with insertional Achilles tendonitis and resulting calcification of the tendon insertion. Illustration A is an intraoperative view of a central incision technique for exposing and debriding insertional Achilles tendonitis.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: With greater than 50% of the achilles tendon debrided, wound closure alone would not be sufficient.
Answer 2: There is no indication for a delayed wound closure with wound vac treatment.
Answer 3: Side to side repair of the achilles tendon is done when less than 50% of the central tendon is debrided.
Answer 5: The posterior tibialis tendon is not used for augmentation in this scenario.
