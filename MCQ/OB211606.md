## Question

Figure 1 is the clinical photograph of a 41-year-old male who presented to the ED at 11 PM, 3 hours following a rural zip lining accident. The patient was transferred by personal car from the scene. Within 30 minutes of his arrival to the hospital the patient was given IV antibiotics and the leg was splinted. The following morning the patient was taken to the OR for irrigation, debridement and reamed intramedullary nailing of this injury. A negative pressure dressing was initially utilized over the open wound and definitive closure was performed on hospital day 3. In this scenario, which of the following is independently linked to this patient's increased risk of infection?

![[q211606-a-screen-shot-2021-02-01-at-6-45-12-am.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Reamed nailing
2. Timing of definitive soft tissue closure
3. Personal transport from scene
4. Timing of antibiotics
5. Delayed surgical treatment

## Correct option

**4. Timing of antibiotics**

## Answer explanation

Administration of intravenous antibiotics >66 minutes after injury has been shown to be an independent risk factor for the development of infection following type III open tibia fractures.

Open fractures occur when the injured bone has direct communication to the external environment. Antibiotics should be given as soon as possible following these injuries and should be continued for 24 hours after primary closure. These injuries are classified using the Gustilo classification as depicted in Illustration A. Similarly, antibiotic prophylaxis should be given based on this classification. Following Gustilo type I and II injuries, patient should receive a 1st generation cephalosporin (clindamycin or vancomycin if an allergy exists). Type III injuries are typically given a 1st generation cephalosporin + an aminoglycoside for prophylaxis. Alternatively, vancomycin + cefepime may be given for type III injuries at some institutions.

*Lack et al. review risk factors for the development of infection following type III open tibia fractures.* **They report** that a delay of antibiotic prophylaxis of greater than 66 minutes and a delay in wound closure greater than 5 days were the two factors linked to increased rates of infection following these injuries. **They conclude** that antibiotics should be administered as soon as possible following open fractures.

*Weber et al. review the time to initial operative treatment following open fractures as a risk of developing deep infection.* **They report** a mean time to surgery of 9 hours and 4 minutes with an ultimate infection rate of 6% following all open fractures. **They conclude** that infection following open fractures most commonly occurred following tibia/fibula fractures and increased with increasing Gustilo grades, and was not related to timing of initial debridement.

Figure A is the clinical radiograph of the lower leg demonstrating an open fracture of the tibia with exposed bone. Illustration A depicts the Gustilo classification.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Reaming of open tibia fracture has not been associated with increased risk of infection.
Answer 2: Definitive soft tissue closure performed >5 days after injury is associated with increased infection risk.
Answer 3: Personal transport has no association with the risk of infection.
Answer 5: Timing of initial debridement is not an independent risk factor for infection.
