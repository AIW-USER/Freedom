## Question

Two patients present to a quaternary referral center for wide resection of sacral chordomas, shown in Figure A. With the assistance of modern technology, both patients undergo successful wide resection with 2mm of healthy surrounding tissue, denoting negative margins. Patient A required bilateral S4-5 nerve root resections, whereas Patient B required bilateral S2-S5 nerve root resections. Which of the following would be the expected post-operative outcomes?

![[q218883-a-figure-a.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Patient A has near-normal bowel/bladder function
2. Patient B has near-normal bowel/bladder function
3. Patient A has diminished bowel/bladder function, Patient B has normal bowel/bladder function
4. Both patients have a complete loss of bowel/bladder function
5. Both patients have near-normal bowel/bladder function

## Correct option

**1. Patient A has near-normal bowel/bladder function**

## Answer explanation

Patient A would retain most of the pelvic splanchnic nerves (S2-S4; S3 predominance) and would be expected to have near-normal bowel/bladder function.

Chordomas are rare, slow-growing, and locally aggressive malignant bone tumors thought to arise from vestigial or ectopic notochordal tissue. They often arise at the base of the skull or within the sacrum. Wide resection is the mainstay of treatment. Due to the location of sacral tumors, some sacral nerve roots are often sacrificed to ensure negative margins. The sacral parasympathetic plexus, or pelvic splanchnic nerves, arise from S2-S4. These nerve roots work in tandem to innervate the pelvic viscera for smooth muscle tone and peristaltic functions. In general, the S3 nerve root is most critical for preserving bowel and bladder function. Resection of the S3 nerve root and below would consequentially result in loss of bowel or bladder function.

*Barber and colleagues review the current understanding and modern treatment paradigms for chordomas.* *They discuss the low response of these tumors to radiation and chemotherapy.* Surgical resection with negative margins imparts the largest impact on patient survival. **The authors conclude** that experienced, multi-disciplinary teams should manage these patients at quaternary referral centers.

*Todd and colleagues reviewed their institutional outcomes in bowel/bladder function after sacrectomy procedures.* **They reported** that in patients with S2-S5 nerve root resections, all patients had abnormal bowel/bladder function postoperatively. Conversely, in patients treated with S4-S5 resection with at least one S3 nerve root left intact, normal bowel and bladder function was retained in 67% and 60% of patients, respectively. If both S3 nerve roots were intact, normal bowel and bladder function was retained in 100% and 69% of patients, respectively. **The authors concluded** by emphasizing the importance of the S3 nerve roots as a critical structure in optimizing bowel/bladder functional outcomes.

Figure A shows sagittal-plane T2 weighted MRI imaging of these two patients’ sacral chordomas. Patient A has a smaller, albeit still locally aggressive, tumor arising from the S4 sacral body. Patient B has a larger chordoma with obliteration of the S2 sacral body and below.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2-5: Patient A retained the S2-S3 nerve roots that serve as the pelvic splanchnic nerve majority, so they would expect to have near normal bowel/bladder function. Whereas patient B would completely lose bowel/bladder function.
