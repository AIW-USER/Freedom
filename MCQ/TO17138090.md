## Question

Following open reduction of a traumatic shoulder dislocation a patient develops numbness over the lateral aspect of the forearm. On examination he can flex and extend the elbow with full range of motion of the shoulder. Wrist and finger movements are normal. Which finding is unlikely from further investigations? (FNB Pattern Paper 2015)

## Options

1. Absent biceps reflex
2. Small amplitude lateral antebrachial cutaneous SNAP
3. Biceps atrophy
4. Weak forearm supination and pronation

## Correct option

**4. Weak forearm supination and pronation**

## Answer explanation

This is a case of musculocutaneous neuropraxia due to overzealous retraction of conjoint tendon during open reduction of the shoulder.

The musculocutaneous nerve is derived from the lateral cord of the brachial plexus and innervates the biceps brachii, brachialis, and coracobrachialis muscles (C5–C6), terminating as the lateral cutaneous nerve of the forearm. Injuries to the musculocutaneous nerve are associated with

-Weakness of elbow flexion and forearm supination (Pronation and elbow extension will be normal)

-Sensory loss over the lateral and volar aspect of the forearm.

-Weak or absent biceps tendon reflex.

EMG evaluation of the musculocutaneous nerve may include the lateral antebrachial cutaneous SNAP, a biceps brachii CMAP.

Complete division of the nerve may be overlooked because the sensory loss may be ill-defined and flexion of the elbow by the brachioradialis may be strong enough to mask biceps paralysis. In these instances, it is essential to palpate the biceps while testing its function to identify specific muscle contractions.

Answer: 4
