Chapter: Pediatric Orthopedics MCQs — Tibial Bowing

## Question

Most common complication requiring surgery in congenital posteromedial bowing of tibia (CPMBT) is

## Options

1. Limb length discrepancy
2. Calcaneovalgus foot
3. Bowing of tibia
4. Fracture through pseudoarthrosis

## Correct option

**1. Limb length discrepancy**

## Answer explanation

Answer: 1
Congenital posteromedial bowing of tibia (CPMBT): Congenital posteromedial bowing or
angulation of the tibia is one of the rare congenital anomalies of the lower limbs. It can be
diagnosed in the prenatal period at around 20 weeks of gestation but is more commonly
diagnosed at birth. If detected antenatally, the sonologist should record the position,
movement and shortening of the affected limb and compare it with the normal limb.
For years CPMBT has been considered a benign self-solving condition, due to the virtually
absent risk of fracture or pseudarthrosis and the natural tendency to the spontaneous
resolution, with minimal residual deformity. But spontaneous correction of the bowing is
sometimes incomplete, the LLD is frequently wide and a residual ankle valgus can persist at
the end of growth.
The clinical presentation at birth is a shortened and bowed leg, with angulation in two
planes, both posteriorly and medially. The bowing of the tibia is most commonly seen in the
distal or medial third of the tibia shaft, with more posterior angulation as well, and it mostly
affects only one leg. The angulation of the deformity can vary from 25 to 70 degrees.
The foot may also present with a calcaneovalgus deformity, which varies in severity and in
most severe cases, the dorsum of the foot lies on the anterolateral surface of the lower leg
and cannot be brought back into a neutral position. These two conditions may occur
together or independently of each other. The talocrural joint generally has a restricted ROM
and this tends to persist throughout life. Shortening of the leg is evident at birth and it
increases with age. Wedging of the distal tibial epiphysis with a valgus inclination and fibular
hypoplasia has been noted in some children with CPMBT.
Treatment: observation is the initial treatment of choice because spontaneously correction
over 5-7 years is expected. Associated calcaneovalgus feet may also require observation and
parental stretching. There are three reasons for surgical intervention in CPMBT: equalize the
LLD, correct the ankle valgus and correct the residual bowing of the tibia. Nonetheless, there
are no clearly defined guidelines for the surgical treatment of CPMBT. > 50% of patients end
up requiring surgical intervention for limb length discrepancy (LLD). Usual LLD at maturity in
CPMBT is 2-5 cms. Age-appropriate epiphysiodesis of long limb or limb lengthening with
ilizarov fixator is required in more than 50 % patients.
