## Question

A 15-year-old high school baseball pitcher presents to your office complaining of shoulder pain in his throwing arm that has been present now for 2 weeks. He describes the pain as deep in the anterosuperior aspect of the shoulder and it is worse when he reaches the late cocking phase of his throwing sequence. As a part of his physical examination, you measure his shoulder range of motion (ROM) with a goniometer, holding the scapula fixed. Compared to the non-dominant arm, which of the following glenohumeral ROM measurements is most associated with an increased risk of shoulder injury in overhead throwing athletes like this patient?

## Options

1. Increased external rotation
2. Increased internal rotation
3. Increased horizontal adduction
4. Decreased external rotation
5. Decreased flexion

## Correct option

**4. Decreased external rotation**

## Answer explanation

Decreased shoulder external rotation compared to the non-dominant arm is thought to create an imbalance that places overhead throwers at increased risk for shoulder injury, especially in the setting of pre-existing internal rotation deficit.

GIRD is a well established condition that occurs most commonly in overhead athletes such as baseball pitchers. In GIRD, the posterior-inferior capsule of the shoulder becomes tight and leads to posterior-superior translation of the humeral head when the shoulder is positioned in abduction and external rotation (ABER). This causes the greater tuberosity of the humeral head to pinch the posterosuperior rotator cuff against the glenoid when the arm is placed in a throwing position (ABER), causing internal impingement. More recently, it is believed that when decreases in shoulder internal rotation (IR) are balanced with increases in shoulder external rotation (ER), normal shoulder kinematics can be maintained. For this reason, shoulder ER has been more closely evaluated and a decrease in ER has been independently linked to increased injury risk in these athletes. In addition to measuring IR and ER independently, total ROM (IR+ER) has become a valuable measurement in determining whether the throwing shoulder has made adaptations that maintain normal joint kinematics.

*Shanley et al. studied preseason shoulder ROM and its effects on youth baseball pitcher's risk of injury during the season.* **They noted** side-to-side differences in cross body adduction >15º and IR >13º placed youth pitchers at 4x and 6x greater risk of injury, respectively. **They concluded** that these measurements could be useful on a larger scale in order to preemptively identify youth players at risk for shoulder injuries based on preseason ROM measurements.

*Myers et al. prospectively evaluated the role played by scapular dysfunction in high school baseball players sustaining upper extremity injuries.* **They postulated** that subtle and obvious scapular dysfunction, as measured on preseason screenings, may offer increased risk for shoulder injury. However, they were not able to identify a correlation between preseason scapular dysfunction and shoulder injury during the season.

*Wilk et al. prospectively reviewed the effects of glenohumeral range of motion deficits related to injury risk in 296 professional baseball pitchers.* **They noted** a signifiant differences in the dominant and non-dominant arm of these pitchers with regards to all range of motion measurements. With regards to injury risk, **they noted** that <5º of ER in the throwing shoulder created a 2.2x greater risk for being placed on the disabled list and a 4x greater risk of undergoing shoulder surgery.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Increased shoulder ER occurs as a compensatory mechanism in order to help maintain normal shoulder kinematics, and its role in injury risk has been recently become more emphasized.
Answer 2: Increased shoulder IR is uncommon in a population of overhead throwers.
Answer 3: Increased horizontal adduction has not been shown to increase risk for shoulder injury. However, decreased shoulder horizontal adduction has been correlated to injury risk with an unknown clinical significance.
Answer 5: Shoulder flexion plays a smaller role in the throwing sequence than shoulder rotation, therefore a decrease in flexion does not cause an increased risk of shoulder injury.
