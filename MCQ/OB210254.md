## Question

A radiograph of his cervical spine demonstrates spondylosis and a cervical MRI shows multi-level central canal stenosis with increased signal intensity on T2-weighted images. ## Answer explanation

The clinical presentation is consistent with cervical spondylotic myelopathy (CSM). The natural course of CSM is classically characterized by a slow and stepwise progression over time.

CSM is characterized by periods of relative stability with slow, stepwise progression over time in an age-dependent fashion. Presenting symptoms are highly variable, as is predicting the clinical course of individual patients. Non-operative management rarely reverses this condition, although patients may stabilize or improve over time with conservative treatment. Indications for surgery include significant functional impairments, rapid disease progression, and myelomalacia.

Nurick in a classic paper written in 1972, described the natural history of CSM, as well as the results of operative and non-operative treatment in 91 patients affected by this condition. They specifically looked at cord diameter and degree of spondylosis and their role disease progression. Patient demographics and co-morbidities were also examined.

*Karadimas et al., in a more recent article, also examined the pathophysiology and natural history of CSM in a narrative and systematic review.* **They found** that 20-60% of patients with CSM will deteriorate over time without surgical intervention. **They conclude** that surgical intervention eliminates the chance of neurologic progression and that patients should be counseled accordingly.

*Rhee et al. performed a systematic review exploring the nonoperative management of CSM.* Five studies met their inclusion criteria. **They concluded** that the evidence is sparse regarding the non-operative management of CSM, especially in patients with mild to moderate degrees of CSM.

Figures A through E are graphs depicting symptoms as a function of time. Illustration A is a T2 sagittal and axial cervical MRI demonstrating multi-level cervical stenosis with associated myelomalacia in a patient suffering from CSM.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1, 2, 3, and 5 do not illustrate the slow and stepwise progression of symptoms over time associated with the natural history of CSM.
