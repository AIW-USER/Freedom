Chapter: Trauma MCQs — Rolando And Bennett Fractures

## Question

True regarding Rolando fracture is (FNB Pattern Paper 2017)

## Options

1. Extraarticular fracture of base of 1st MC
2. Intraarticular fracture of base of 1st MC
3. Main deforming force is pull of adductor longus
4. Most common fracture dislocation of the thumb joint.

## Correct option

**2. Intraarticular fracture of base of 1st MC**

## Answer explanation

Both Rolando and Bennett’s fractures are intra-articular fractures of the base of the thumb.

Bennett’s fracture-dislocation is an oblique intra-articular fracture dislocation of the base of the first metacarpal while Rolando fracture dislocation is an intra-articular three-part fracture (V or Y or T-shaped fracture) of the base of the first metacarpal. Axial loading of the partially flexed and adducted thumb as in while punching with a clenched fist is the usual mechanism of injury in fractures of base of 1st MC.

In Rolando fracture axial force splits the base into triangular volar and dorsal fragments. Deforming forces in both of these fractures are combined pull of abductor pollicis longus (main force) and adductor pollicis. In Bennett’s fracture it displaces the diaphyseal fragment laterally, proximally and dorsally. In Rolando fracture dislocation diaphyseal fragment is usually not displaced. Volar or palmar oblique ligament prevents displacement of volar fragment.

Treatment: Small avulsion fractures can be managed by closing reduction and thumb spica immobilization for 6 weeks. Most of these fractures are unstable injuries and require close or open reduction, K wire fixa­tion and cast immobilization in a thumb spica for 4–6 weeks.

Answer: 2
