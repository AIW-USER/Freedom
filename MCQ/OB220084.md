## Question

A cross-country team in Arizona is planning their practices for the week. After last year's season, when several athletes suffered heat-related illnesses, the coaching staff decided to use the wet bulb temperature to guide when practice should be suspended. What is the threshold wet bulb temperature for postponement of sports participation?

## Options

1. greater than 75 degrees Fahrenheit
2. greater than 83 degrees Fahrenheit
3. greater than 86 degrees Fahrenheit
4. greater than 90 degrees Fahrenheit
5. greater than 93 degrees Fahrenheit

## Correct option

**3. greater than 86 degrees Fahrenheit**

## Answer explanation

Cancellation of sports participation is recommended when the wet bulb temperature (WBT) is greater than 86 degrees Fahrenheit. When the WBT is 82-86 degrees Fahrenheit, athletes should train at a 1:1 play-to-rest ratio.

WBT is measured by wrapping a thermometer in a wet cloth. Unlike standard air temperature, WBT reflects the body's ability to cool itself through sweat evaporation, making it particularly relevant in hot and humid conditions where cooling mechanisms may be impaired. Sports organizations and athletic trainers use WBT thresholds to determine safe activity levels, with guidelines often restricting or modifying practices and competitions when WBT exceeds certain limits (e.g., 82°F or higher). For instance, modifications may include shortening practice durations, increasing hydration breaks, or postponing events. Using WBT in planning helps prevent heat-related illnesses such as heat exhaustion or heatstroke. Major governing bodies like the NCAA and high school athletic associations have adopted this proactive approach to mitigate risks associated with extreme heat during training and competition.

*Cooper et al.* **found** that approximately 74% of exertional heat illness (EHI) cases among American football players were exertional heat cramps, with the highest incidence occurring during the first 14 days of preseason practice, particularly within the initial 7 days. The risk of EHI increased substantially when the wet bulb globe temperature (WBGT) reached or exceeded 82.0°F (27.8°C). **The authors concluded** that sports medicine personnel should implement preventive measures during the early preseason and under elevated WBGT conditions to mitigate EHI risk.

The American College of Sports Medicine's position on exertional heat illness (EHI) emphasizes that EHI can affect athletes during high-intensity or prolonged exercise, leading to conditions such as exercise-associated muscle cramping, heat exhaustion, or exertional heatstroke. The document highlights the importance of recognizing early signs and symptoms of EHI to prevent progression to more severe conditions. It also underscores the necessity of implementing preventive measures, including proper hydration, acclimatization, and monitoring environmental conditions, to mitigate the risk of EHI during training and competition.

Incorrect answers
Answers 1, 2, 4, 5: These are not the threshold temperature at which sports participation should be suspended
