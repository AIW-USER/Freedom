## Question

Figures A and B are the radiographs of a 68-year-old man who is well known to you for having undergone a previous two stage revision for infection. He ambulates with a cane at all times and reports new-onset pain in his knee. An aspiration is performed and demonstrates a synovial WBC count of 26k. Which of the following risk factors is associated with failure of a second two-stage revision for prosthetic infection?

![[q215463-b-screen-shot-2021-03-06-at-6-11-11-am.jpg]]

## Options

1. Preoperative synovial WBC >25k
2. Culture positive for an anaerobic cocci
3. Musculoskeletal Infection Society (MSIS) Type C host
4. Use of a semi-constrained prosthesis
5. Static, rather than dynamic spacer use

## Correct option

**3. Musculoskeletal Infection Society (MSIS) Type C host**

## Answer explanation

Patients with the an MSIS host grade of C are much less likely to clear an infection following a second two-stage revision.

The effectiveness of multiple two-stage exchanges is variable and relies on numerous factors including host health, limb condition, and virulence of the organism. Knowing which patients are likely to clear an infection following a second two stage revision is very useful as it may decrease morbidity and cost. Factors associated with good outcomes are acceptable general health, low MSIS host grade (A or B) and culture sensitive organisms. Conversely, patients with MRSA or polymicrobial infections and high MSIS grade (C) may be better treated with fusion or amputation, as the likelihood of them ever clearing the infection is low. The MSIS grading system is based on systemic host compromising factor and is shown in Illustrations A and B.

Vadiee et a.l review the effectiveness of repeat two-stage revision in the setting of a recurrent infection. They report that no patient graded as an MSIS Grade C was able to clear an infection following a second two-stage revision. They conclude that patients re-infected with high virulent organisms and poor MSIS host status should be considered for amputation or fusion rather than another attempt at revision.

*McPherson et al. report on outcomes using a staging system in the setting of total hip arthroplasty.* *They report on their results of dividing patients up in to three categories based on infection type (acute v chronic), systemic host grade. and local extremity grade.* **They found** a higher complication rate with patients who had local wound compromise and worsening medical conditions. **They conclude** that using this staging system in the evaluation of periprosthetic infections will be a useful tool in the future.

Figures A and B are the AP and lateral radiographs of a semi-constrained TKA. Illustrations A and B review the MSIS host criteria based on the work of McPherson et al.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: While highly virulent organisms have been associated with increasing failure rates, WBC count specifically, has not.
Answer 2: Anaerobic cocci are considered low virulent organisms.
Answer 4: A semi-constrained prosthesis has not been linked to increased failure for infection following two-stage revision.
Answer 5: Spacer type may effect ease of reimplantation and range of motion but does not effect likelihood of recurrent infection.
