## Question

You are performing a latissimus dorsi tendon transfer for a massive irreparable rotator cuff tear. During harvest of the latissimus dorsi tendon, the arm is internally rotated to facilitate tenotomy and minimize risk of injuring a nerve that runs immediately deep (anterior) to the latissimus dorsi tendon, approximately 3cm medial to its insertion. Injury to this structure would result in loss of innervation to which of the following muscles?

## Options

1. Deltoid
2. Extensor pollicis longus
3. Flexor pollicis longus
4. Latissimus dorsi
5. Teres major

## Correct option

**2. Extensor pollicis longus**

## Answer explanation

The radial nerve runs immediately deep (anterior) to the latissimus dorsi (LD) tendon, roughly 3cm medial to its insertion onto the humerus. Injury to the radial nerve would result in loss of innervation to the wrist and digit extensors, including the extensor pollicis longus (EPL).

LD transfer is a surgical option for massive irreparable posterosuperior rotator cuff tears with loss of elevation and external rotation, particularly in younger patients with minimal glenohumeral arthritis. LD transfer is often combined with a teres major (TM) transfer, to maximize surface area for coverage of the rotator cuff footprint. Patients with subscapularis tears and deltoid dysfunction tend to have worse outcomes. Those with glenohumeral arthritis or cuff tear arthropathy are better served with arthroplasty.

*Pearle et al. authored an anatomic study of the LD transfer in 12 cadaveric shoulders.* The radial nerve runs along the anterior surface of LD, ~2.9cm medial to the superior aspect and ~2.3cm medial to the inferior aspect of the humeral insertion and is most at risk during tenotomy. The neurovascular pedicles to the TM (lower subscapular nerve) and LD (thoracodorsal nerve) are found ~7.4cm and ~13.1cm medial to the humeral insertion, respectively (Illustration A) and are most at risk during mobilization of muscle bellies. The posterior branch of the axillary nerve runs along the deep deltoid fascia and is at risk during transfer of the tendon deep to the posterior deltoid out into the SA space.

*Morelli et al. performed an anatomic study of the LD transfer in 9 cadaveric shoulders.* Identification of a dense fibrous band (DFB) at the confluence of the LD and TM facilitates localization of the radial nerve, which crosses deep to this landmark (Illustration B). **The authors concluded** that harvest of the LD tendon can safely be accomplished by locating the DFB and releasing the tendon within 2cm of its humeral insertion.

Illustration A depicts the anatomy of the neurologic structures at risk during a LD transfer (Pearle et al.). Illustration B shows the DFB (arrowhead) at the junction of the LD and TM, with the radial nerve (arrow) coursing just deep to the LD before entering the arm via the triangular interval (Morelli et al.). Illustration C shows the thoracodorsal neurovascular pedicle (arrowhead) inserting onto the undersurface of the LD (arrow).

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The deltoid is innervated by the axillary nerve.
Answer 3: The flexor pollicis longus is innervated by the anterior interosseous nerve branch of the median nerve.
Answer 4: The latissimus dorsi is innervated by the thoracodorsal nerve.
Answer 5: The teres major is innervated by the lower subscapular nerve.
