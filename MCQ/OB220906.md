## Question

A non-ambulatory 7-year-old boy with spastic quadriplegic cerebral palsy, prematurity, and developmental delay presents with radiographic evidence of progressive hip subluxation. Most recent AP pelvis radiographs show Reimers migration percentage of 70% on the right and 60% on the left, as well as an acetabular index of 30º bilaterally. Passive abduction measures 30° on the right and 35° on the left. Operative intervention is recommended. In counseling the family, which treatment plan can you recommend that would most likely minimize resubluxation and need for reoperation over time?

## Options

1. Bilateral adductor lengthenings with psoas recession and abduction bracing
2. Bilateral proximal femoral varus derotational osteotomies (VDROs)
3. Bilateral pelvic osteotomies
4. Combined bilateral VDROs and pelvic osteotomies with adjunct soft-tissue releases
5. Closed reduction with serial radiographs

## Correct option

**4. Combined bilateral VDROs and pelvic osteotomies with adjunct soft-tissue releases**

## Answer explanation

In this patient with bilateral hip subluxation (Reimers migration index >33º) and acetabular insufficiency (elevated acetabular index >25º), combined bilateral VDROs and pelvic osteotomies with soft-tissue releases will provide the greatest improvement in containment and the lowest odds of recurrent subluxation compared with femoral osteotomies or soft-tissue procedures alone (answer choice 4).

Hip pathology in patients with cerebral palsy (CP) arises from muscle imbalance and progressive bony remodeling; risk of hip displacement increases with greater GMFCS severity and can lead to pain and functional compromise if untreated. Diagnosis relies on radiographs, commonly quantified by the Reimers migration index (also known as Migration Percentage [MP]), which measures the percentage of the femoral head without acetabular coverage. Treatment spans soft-tissue procedures to reconstructive osteotomies depending on age, spasticity, and severity. Soft-tissue procedures (adductor ± psoas releases) are primarily preventive in younger children (<4 years old) and have higher failure rates once MP exceeds ~40%, whereas reconstructive osseous procedures are indicated in children >4 years old or when MP is ≥60%, ideally addressing all pathology in a single stage. Physical therapy and abduction bracing alone do not prevent subluxation and may promote a windswept deformity. The overall goal is to maintain a mobile, located, painless hip with durable containment.

*Agarwal et al. performed a meta-analysis of treatments for CP hip subluxation.* **They found** lower odds of resubluxation/reoperation for combined femoral+pelvic osteotomies versus femoral alone, and for femoral osteotomy versus soft-tissue procedures; combined osteotomies produced the greatest improvement in MP, while Botulinum toxin A did not improve MP. **They concluded** that osteotomies are preferred over soft-tissue procedures alone to reduce recurrence.

*Hosseinzadeh et al. reviewed the management of hip disorders in patients with CP.* **They noted** that reconstructive procedures are indicated for an MP >40% and aim to provide a mobile, located, painless hip; recurrence is more common in nonambulators and when reconstruction is performed in younger patients. **They concluded** that combined femoral and acetabular reconstruction is often required, with salvage techniques reserved until reconstructive options are exhausted.

Incorrect answer choices:
Answer choice 1: Soft-tissue procedures alone have poor success when MP >40% and are primarily preventive in younger children; they do not reliably prevent progression here.
Answer choice 2: VDRO alone improves containment but has a higher resubluxation risk than combined femoral+pelvic osteotomies in this context. Additionally, this patient has signs of acetabular deficiency, suggesting they would likely benefit from pelvic osteotomy.
Answer choice 3: Pelvic osteotomy alone is not superior to femoral osteotomy alone and fails to address typical combined deformities.
Answer choice 5: Closed reduction and observation is inappropriate, as baseline muscle forces will assuredly lead to resubluxation. Reconstructive surgery is indicated when MP exceeds 40%.
