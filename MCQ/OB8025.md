## Question

Figures 3a and 3b are the current AP and oblique radiographs of a 44-year-old man who underwent nonsurgical management of a left ankle fracture 6 months ago. What is the most appropriate course of management?

![[q8025-a-3a.jpg]]
*Figure A*

![[q8025-b-3b.jpg]]
*Figure B*

## Options

1. Arizona brace
2. Ankle arthroscopy with drilling of the talar osteochondral lesion
3. Medial ankle arthrotomy and débridement with correction of the fibular malunion
4. Ankle arthrodesis
5. Ankle and subtalar arthrodesis

## Correct option

**3. Medial ankle arthrotomy and débridement with correction of the fibular malunion**

## Answer explanation

The radiographs reveal a fractured malunited, shortened fibula with deltoid instability. Corrective osteotomy with fibular lengthening has shown positive results. Nonsurgical management in an active, healthy patient will lead to rapid deterioration of the ankle joint. Without evidence of arthritis, a joint-sacrificing procedure should not be used.
