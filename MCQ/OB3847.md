## Question

A 54-year-old woman complains of persistent right great toe pain, which worsens with shoe wear. On physical examination, she has mild swelling and moderate pain around the joint with pain especially at extreme dorsiflexion. Radiographs are shown in Figures A and B. The recommended procedure is a dorsal cheilectomy. Based on the patient's physical exam, radiographs and treatment recommendation, to which grade of disease should she be most appropriately assigned?

## Options

1. Grade 0
2. Grade 1
3. Grade 2
4. Grade 3
5. Grade 4

## Correct option

**3. Grade 2**

## Answer explanation

With moderate to severe pain primarily at her end range of motion along with a mild dorsal osteophyte and <50% joint space narrowing, grade 2 hallux rigidus is the correct diagnosis.

Hallux rigidus is characterized by loss of motion of the first metatarsalphalangeal (MTP) joint in adults due to degenerative arthritis. Progressive pain with motion and at the extremes along with osteophyte formation can be classified by the Coughlin Classification (described and shown in Illustration A).

*Giannini et al. review the established and innovative treatment options for hallux rigidus including joint arthroplasty.* Reviewing the literature, **the authors find** insufficient evidence to recommend arthroplasty over fusion, and caution against its use until more data is available.

*Coughlin and Shurnas wrote the landmark paper reporting their grading and subsequent results with an emphasis on reserving cheilectomy for early stages of hallux rigidus.* Resection arthroplasty or fusion is reserved for grade 3 or above, for those who have failed conservative measures.

*Mann et al. report on 25 consecutive patients with hallux rigidus treated with dorsal cheilectomy with a 12% failure rate at mean follow-up of 5 years.* *Good pain relief was reported for the remaining 22 patients.* **The authors recommend** cheilectomy for symptomatic dorsal osteophytes with minimal osteoarthritic changes.

Figures A and B exhibit radiographs of Grade 2 hallux rigidus, characterized by a moderate dorsal osteophyte and joint space narrowing <50%. Illustration A exhibits the Coughlin and Shurnas Classification for hallux rigidus

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: Grade 0 exhibits no radiographic evidence and is just symptomatic stiffness.
Answer 2: Grade 1 on physical exam has hallmark pain at extremes of motion, with minimal radiographic changes.
Answer 4: Grade 3 disease is characterized by significant stiffness with no mid-range pain and severe pain at extremes. Radiographically, dorsal osteophytes are large and the joint space is >50% narrowed.
Answer 5: Grade 4 disease is similar to grade 3, however, also has mid-range ROM pain.
