Chapter: Pediatric Orthopedics MCQs — Congenital Vertical Talus (CVT)

## Question

A 7-month-old child presented to you with a deformity over the right foot. Following are the X rays. What is the treatment?

![[TO17138230-1.png]]

## Options

1. Casting followed by mini reduction and tenotomy
2. Casting followed by TA tenotomy
3. Stretching
4. Open reduction with extensive soft tissue release.

## Correct option

**3. Stretching**

## Answer explanation

Above X rays are Dorsiflexion and plantar flexion view of a foot of a child. The DF view shows vertically oriented talus and non collinear Talus- Metatarsal axis. However, on plantar flexion the Talus- 1st metatarsal axis becomes collinear and the orientation of talus improves. This suggests that it is a case of congenital oblique talus. Congenital oblique talus unlike its counterpart, vertical talus just needs stretching of the soft tissue and in some cases casting. Surgery is not usually required.

Vertical talus is a rigid rocker-bottom deformity of the foot wherein there is fixed and irreducible dorsal dislocation of the navicular. It is a rare condition. In 50% cases it is associated with underlying syndromic etiology or neurologic disorder.

Answer: 3
