## Question

A 14-month old male presents with syndromic early-onset scoliosis with a 43-degree curve. The treating spine surgeon recommends Mehta casting. What is the expected outcome of this treatment?

## Options

1. Complete resolution of the scoliosis
2. No need for posterior instrumented fusion
3. Continued curve progression, but allowing further spine growth
4. No further curve progression until puberty
5. Eventual requirement for growing rod construct

## Correct option

**3. Continued curve progression, but allowing further spine growth**

## Answer explanation

Mehta casting for syndromic early-onset scoliosis (EOS) can delay the need for surgery, but allows for continued spine growth (Answer 3).

Mehta casting is a technique used to treat early-onset scoliosis (EOS), particularly in patients with syndromic scoliosis. The technique involves applying a cast to the affected area to correct the deformity and delay the need for future spinal surgery. It is typically used when the patient is too young for other surgical options. Mehta casting is not a curative treatment for scoliosis, but it can be effective in slowing the progression of the disease and preventing the need for growing rod constructs. However, there are potential complications such as skin necrosis and infection that can occur with the use of Mehta casting.

*LaValva et al. evaluated the effectiveness of serial casting for patients with neuromuscular and syndromic EOS by retrospectively reviewing patients from two multicenter registries.* *The study found that there were no statistically significant differences between precasting and postcasting major and compensatory curves and spine height, however, thoracic and lumbar height were significantly greater upon casting cessation.* *The study found that 55% of the patients experienced major curve progression and 43% required surgical intervention.* *The study concludes that serial casting for neuromuscular or syndromic EOS is an effective strategy for delaying surgical intervention despite suboptimal radiographic outcomes.*

*Fletcher et al. studied the effectiveness of serial casting in delaying surgery in children with moderate-to-severe early-onset scoliosis, including those with syndromic, neuromuscular, or congenital scoliosis, or idiopathic scoliosis measuring over 50 degrees.* **They found** that the main thoracic Cobb angle before casting was corrected to 39.1 ± 16.4 degrees in a cast but increased to 76.3 ± 24.0 degrees at the final follow-up. **The authors also found** that 72.4% of the patients have avoided growing spine surgery, but 51.7% required surgical treatment for their scoliosis at the most recent follow-up. The study concludes that serial casting is a viable alternative to surgical growth-sparing techniques in moderate-to-severe early-onset scoliosis and may help delay eventual surgical intervention.

Incorrect answers
Answer 1: Complete resolution of syndromic scoliosis with Mehta casting is exceedingly rare.
Answer 2: Patients treated with casting will often experience curve progression that will require a later instrumented fusion. However, this is often delayed to allow for further spine growth.
Answer 4: Curve progression commonly occurs in childhood after bracing is completed, which usually accelerates during the adolescent years.
Answer 5: Avoidance of growing rod constructs is one of the potential benefits of Mehta casting.
