## Question

A 33-year-old active-duty soldier is being seen by you in your clinic. He underwent the procedure depicted in Figure A. A keen medical student rotating on your service asks about the implant, and you mention that it was coated with a substance that allowed for both the process essential to the prosthesis depicted in Figure A, in addition to osteoconduction. Which of the following substances was the stem most likely coated with?

![[q218069-a-oi.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Calcium Carbonate
2. Calcium Phosphate
3. Calcium Sulfate
4. Freeze-dried allograft croutons
5. Platelet-rich Plasma (PRP)

## Correct option

**2. Calcium Phosphate**

## Answer explanation

The radiograph in Figure A demonstrates an osseointegration-type prosthesis. Calcium phosphate (Answer 2), a type of synthetic bone graft, is often used to coat the stem of these implants as the material allows for both osseointegration and osteoconduction.

Bone grafting is a valuable component of any orthopaedic surgeon's armamentarium. These materials can assist in the healing of fractures, delayed unions or nonunions, can aid in arthrodesis and fusions, and can help to replace bone defects secondary to trauma or tumor. Over 1,000,000 bone grafting procedures are performed in the United States each year, with a growth of approximately 12% per year.

Bone graft materials have aspects of one or more of the following properties: (1) Osteoconductive, in which the material acts as a structural framework for bone growth (2) osteoinductive, in which the material contains factors that stimulate bone growth and the induction of stem cells down a bone-forming lineage and (3) Osteogenic, in which the material provides cells that will produce bone, including primitive mesenchymal stem cells, osteoblasts and osteocytes. Furthermore, with the advent of osseointegration, certain materials have been identified that can aid in the formation of a stable and reliable bone/metal interface.

*Lobb et al. provide a comprehensive review of bone graft substitutes.* **The authors note** that material selection is dependent on surgeon need, cost and availability, and that many synthetic materials lag behind autograft for a variety of outcome measures. The authors do highlight that calcium phosphate is an osteoconductive material with slow resorption, and can be used as a structural scaffold for filling large defects.

*Lopez-Valverde et al. provide a materials study of calcium phosphate-coated titanium implants in the setting of planned osseointegration.* **The authors note** that this material is unique in its ability to allow for osseointegration when compared to other osteoconductive materials. While the authors were unable to demonstrate a significant advantage of this coating to conventional etched surfaces, **they highlight** that calcium phosphate-coated stems are a growing area of research interest and that higher-powered studies are required to fully elucidate the risks and benefits of this design.

*Fu et al. provide a review of the effectiveness of recombinant human bone morphogenic protein-2 (rhBMP-2) in the setting of spinal fusion surgery.* *Their study included data from 13 RCTs and 31 cohort studies.* Overall, **the authors conclude** that rhBMP-2 has no proven clinical advantage over bone graft, and may be associated with complications including wound complication and dysphagia. **The authors conclude** that data derived from industry-sponsored studies must be viewed with caution, as these studies are often fraught with bias.

Figure A is an AP radiograph of the bilateral lower extremities demonstrating a left below-knee amputation with an osseointegration prosthesis.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Calcium carbonate is not capable of osseointegration, it is osteoconductive only.
Answer 3: Calcium sulfate is a quickly resorbed osteoconductive material that is not capable of osseointegration.
Answer 4: Freeze-dried allograft croutons are osteoconductive only.
Answer 5: Platelet-rich plasma (PRP) is utilized in a variety of soft-tissue-related pathologies, but it is not currently indicated as a bone-graft substitute.
