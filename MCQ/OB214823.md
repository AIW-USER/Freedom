## Question

A 21-year-old male college volleyball player presents to your office complaining of anterior knee pain at the inferior aspect of the patella that is worse during activity but does not cause discomfort at rest. Radiographs are negative for fracture or obvious effusion. He has not had any formal treatment to this point. Which of the following is true regarding this patient's most likely diagnosis?

## Options

1. Tenderness at distal pole of patella is greater in full extension than flexion
2. Occurs secondary to intra-substance inflammatory reaction
3. Strongly associated with lower extremity malalignment
4. Surgical management is indicated at this time
5. Decreased signal noted on T1 and T2 MRI is diagnostic

## Correct option

**1. Tenderness at distal pole of patella is greater in full extension than flexion**

## Answer explanation

This patient most likely has patellar tendonitis, which is noted on physical examination with pain at the inferior aspect of the patella, worse in extension.

Patellar tendonitis is most commonly seen in sports where repetitive jumping activities are performed such as volleyball and basketball. It is thought that the repetitive, eccentric contractions of the patellar tendon cause degenerative micro-tearing of the tendon insertion over time. On examination, patients complain of pain at the inferior patellar pole which is worse with extension than flexion and when brought from a flexed to extended position. MRI can be useful in chronic cases and usually demonstrates hyperintense signal on T1 and T2 sequences. Treatment is conservative, but may eventually require operative intervention in cases that do not improve or worsen to the point where the patient has significant pain at rest and is not able to participate in daily activities.

*Rath et al. reviewed the clinical signs of patellar tendonitis and correlated them with MRI and cadaveric findings.* *They performed the "passive extension-flexion sign," and "standing active quadriceps sign," tests,* **noting** that both flexion of the knee and contraction of the quadriceps helped to decrease pain at the inferior pole of the patella. **They postulate** that based on these tests, the pathogenesis of patellar tendonitis is secondary to impingement of the deep tendon fibers on the inferior pole of the patella.

*Kodali et al. reviewed the diagnosis and treatment of anterior knee pain in young athletes.* *They specifically discuss patellar tendonitis and its diagnosis and management.* **They conclude** that most cases can be managed conservatively with rest, NSAIDS, and therapy emphasizing eccentric quad strengthening and hamstring stretching.

Illustration A demonstrates the Blazina classification, which describes the stages of severity in patellar tendonitis.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Patellar tendonitis is thought to be caused by degenerative and repetitive micro-tearing of the tendinous insertion on the inferior pole of the patella.
Answer 3: Patellar tendonitis is not strongly associated with malalignment. However, malalignment is associated with generalized patellofemoral syndrome and patellar maltracking.
Answer 4: Surgical management is rarely indicated, except in Blazina stage 3, where the patient is having pain at rest and is not improving with conservative measures.
Answer 5: Increased, not decreased, signal is noted on T1 and T2 imaging at the tendinous insertion on the patella.
