## Question

Which of the following statements regarding the nerve isolated with the vessel loop in Figure A is true?

![[q3835-a-dmcn.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. It is the terminal branch of the saphenous nerve; injury leads to first MTP joint abduction weakness
2. It is the terminal branch of the superficial peroneal nerve; injury leads to first interphylangeal joint extension weakness
3. It is the terminal branch of the superficial peroneal nerve; injury leads to reduced sensation over medial aspect of great toe
4. It is the terminal branch of the deep peroneal nerve; injury leads to first interphylangeal joint flexion weakness
5. It is the terminal branch of the deep peroneal nerve; injury leads to first MTP joint abduction weakness

## Correct option

**3. It is the terminal branch of the superficial peroneal nerve; injury leads to reduced sensation over medial aspect of great toe**

## Answer explanation

Figure A shows the dorsomedial cutaneous nerve. It is the terminal branch of the superficial peroneal nerve; injury leads to reduced sensation over medial aspect of the great toe

The dorsomedial cutaneous nerve is the terminal branch of the superficial peroneal nerve and is susceptible to iatrogenic injury during bunion surgery. It is usually found 5-15 mm medial/plantar to the EHL tendon. Injury to the nerve may result in a painful neuroma and/or numbness over the medial aspect of the great toe.

*Miller et al. reviewed nerve disorders in the hallux.* **They state** nerve disorders can occur secondary to soft tissue entrapment, trauma, iatrogenic injury, or from an idiopathic basis. They must be approached by careful history and examination followed by nonoperative treatment. During workup, a positive nerve block can help to confirm the diagnosis and should be a precursor before any surgical intervention.

*Miller et al. discussed dorsomedial cutaneous nerve syndrome.* **They state** that severe neuritic pain over the medial eminence and first toe after bunion surgery is suggestive of this syndrome. In their series of 9 patients, the nerve had been either transected or bound in scar tissue. In these patients, resection and burial of this nerve appears to be a useful treatment when pain is unresponsive to nonoperative measures.

Figure A shows a medial image of the forefoot. There is a longitudinal incision over the medial MTP joint, which is used commonly for bunion surgery.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Injury to the abductor hallucis tendon would cause first MTP joint abduction weakness. This is supplied by the medial plantar nerve.
Answer 2: Injury to the extensor hallucis longus or brevis tendon would cause first IP joint extension weakness. These are supplied by the deep peroneal nerve.
Answer 4: Injury to the tibial nerve, or its branch, would lead to first interphylangeal joint flexion weakness.
Answer 5: Injury to the abductor hallucis tendon would cause first MTP joint abduction weakness. This is supplied by the medial plantar nerve.
