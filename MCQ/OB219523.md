## Question

A 19-year-old female collegiate soccer player is playing in her first game of the season when she steps awkwardly and sustains an external rotation ankle injury. Radiographs of the ankle and leg are shown in Figures A & B. You explain to her that she has a Maisonneuve fracture which involves injury to the syndesmotic ligaments. Which of the following correctly matches the syndesmotic ligament with one of its attachment sites?

![[q219523-b-maisonneuve-fracture-3.jpg]]

## Options

1. Anterior-inferior tibiofibular ligament; Volkmann's tubercle
2. Anterior-inferior tibiofibular ligament; Chaput's tubercle
3. Posterior-inferior tibiofibular ligament; Chaput's tubercle
4. Posterior-inferior tibiofibular ligament; Wagstaffe's tubercle
5. Interosseous ligament; Volkmann's tubercle

## Correct option

**2. Anterior-inferior tibiofibular ligament; Chaput's tubercle**

## Answer explanation

Maisonneuve fractures involve syndesmotic injury and affect the attachments of the anterior-inferior tibiofibular (AITFL) and posterior-inferior tibiofibular (PITFL) ligaments.

Maisonneuve fractures are relatively uncommon but typically occur with an external rotation mechanism that causes injury to the medial ankle (deltoid ligament or medial malleolus), which moves through the syndesmosis and out the proximal fibula causing a fracture. When there is an injury to the medial ankle on isolated ankle radiographs, full-length tib-fib radiographs should always be ordered to rule out a proximal fibula fracture or syndesmotic widening as this may change surgical management. It is important to understand the anatomic relationships of the syndesmotic ligaments to perform the appropriate surgery. The ATIFL originates on Chaput's tubercle of the anterior-inferior tibia and attaches to Wagstaffe's tubercle of the fibula, while the PITFL originates on Volkmann's tubercle of the posterior-inferior tibia and attaches to the posterior fibula. Open reduction and visualization of the syndesmosis provides the best possible syndesmotic reduction.

*He et al. reviewed the pathoanatomy and injury mechanism associated with Maisonneuve fractures in 41 patients seen between 2014 and 2019.* **They note** that 35 patients with injuries of the posterior ankle structures, mostly fractures of the posterior malleolus. **They note** that most of the injuries were caused by pronation-external rotation mechanisms and considered ORIF the primary treatment option, with 40/41 patients undergoing fixation.

*Bartonicek et al. reviewed Maisonneuve fractures,* **noting** a high range of variability in stability of these fractures with injury typically occurring to the proximal fibula and syndesmotic ligaments. **They also recommend** a CT scan to evaluate for additional fractures and assess the position of the fibula relative to the fibular notch. The primary goal of treatment in their eyes is the anatomic reduction of the syndesmosis under direct visualization with rigid fixation.

Figure A is an AP ankle radiograph that shows medial clear space widening and Figure B is a lateral tib-fib radiograph that shows an oblique proximal fibular shaft fracture. Combined, these radiographs are representative of a Maisonneuve fracture.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1, 3-5: The AITFL attaches to Chaput's tubercle on the tibia and Wagstaffe's tubercle on the fibula, while the PITFL attaches to Volkmann's tubercle on the posterior malleolus of the tibia and the posterior fibula.
