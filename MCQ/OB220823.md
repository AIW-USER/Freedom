## Question

A 48-year-old male presents to the trauma service after being involved in a high-speed motor vehicle accident. He is found to have several fractures requiring differing modes of non-operative and operative management. Which of the following injuries and their corresponding treatments would most likely rely on a strain environment less than 2% to facilitate ideal construct-specific healing?

## Options

1. A left comminuted humeral shaft fracture treated with an intramedullary nail
2. A left extra-articular distal femur fracture treated with a bridge plate
3. A left transverse radial shaft fracture treated with compression plating
4. A right transverse femoral shaft fracture treated with a cephalomedullary nail
5. A right extra-articular distal radius fracture treated non-operatively with a sugar tong splint

## Correct option

**3. A left transverse radial shaft fracture treated with compression plating**

## Answer explanation

A simple transverse radial shaft fracture treated with compression plating relies on primary healing, which requires an environment with < 2 % of strain (Answer 3).

Perren’s theory of strain describes the relationship between fracture site strain (fracture gap movement divided by the overall fracture gap) and bony healing. Primary healing, characterized by intramembranous healing, is best suited for simple fracture patterns and requires a low-strain environment (< 2%). Secondary healing, characterized by endochondral ossification, is best suited for comminuted fractures and requires strain between 2 and 10% at the fracture site to promote callus formation. Fracture environments where strain surpasses 10% are associated with fibrous tissue formation rather than bony healing.

*Perren et al. reviewed fracture healing biomechanics with a specific emphasis on strain.* *The authors describe the concept of strain (the amount of fracture gap movement divided by the gap width), the balance that must be achieved between strain induction and strain tolerance to permit adequate bony healing, and the avenues by which surgeons can influence strain at a fracture site through fixation methods.* **They concluded** that an understanding of fracture biomechanics, particularly strain, allows for a better understanding of fracture fixation techniques and causes of non-union.

*Goodnough et al. reviewed the basic science concepts relevant to fracture healing.* *The authors detail considerations when planning treatment, which include the benefits and risks of performing open reduction, implant choice, and the stability that each option confers to the fracture site, and the optimal strain needed to be attained to facilitate healing.* **They conclude** that building an understanding of basic science principles allows practitioners to better treat fractures through an individualized approach.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: A left comminuted humeral shaft fracture treated with an intramedullary nail relies on secondary healing, which requires an environment with strain between 2% and 10%.
Answer 2: A left extra-articular distal femur fracture treated with a bridge plate relies on secondary healing, which requires an environment with strain between 2% and 10%.
Answer 4: A right transverse femoral shaft fracture treated with a cephalomedullary nail relies on secondary healing, which requires an environment with strain between 2% and 10%.
Answer 5: A right extra-articular distal radius fracture treated non-operatively with a sugar tong splint relies on secondary healing, which requires an environment with strain between 2% and 10%.
