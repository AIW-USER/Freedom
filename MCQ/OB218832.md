## Question

A 38-year-old female presents to your office with a 5th metatarsal fracture that she sustained approximately 11 weeks ago when she twisted her foot on an icy curb. She was treated non-operatively initially in a protective post-op shoe. She comes to your office because she is still having substantial pain over the 5th metatarsal. Radiographs demonstrate a zone III 5th metatarsal fracture with sclerotic fracture edges and minimal callus formation. What is the most appropriate next step to assure reliable fracture healing?

## Options

1. Continue nonoperative management, NWB in cast
2. Continue nonoperative management, WBAT in boot
3. Order MRI to evaluate for evidence of healing or persistent edema
4. Prescribe bone stimulator
5. Open reduction internal fixation +/- bone grafting

## Correct option

**5. Open reduction internal fixation +/- bone grafting**

## Answer explanation

This zone III 5th metatarsal fracture is at high risk for nonunion based on the lack of healing at almost 3 months and the poor blood supply in the area; therefore, ORIF +/- bone grafting is the best choice to stimulate fracture healing.

Fifth metatarsal fractures are one of the more common foot fractures and zone II/III fractures are more likely to have difficulty healing due to poor localized blood supply. While zone II fractures are most frequently discussed, zone III fractures are also at high risk. Zone III fractures may be treated initially within a short leg boot/cast, but ultimately may require fixation +/- bone grafting if there are signs of sclerosis and fracture gapping at subsequent follow-ups. Patient goals and activity should also be considered when deciding about surgery, as fixation may allow earlier union and return to play for athletes.

*Le and Anderson reviewed Zone II and III 5th metatarsal fractures and* **noted** that both areas are at higher risk for nonunion due to poor blood supply. **They note** that nonoperative management can be initially attempted in lower-demand patients, with the risk of nonunion up to 30%. Given the nonunion rates, **they recommend** that in high-demand patients or athletes that ORIF be performed in order to expedite union.

*O'Malley et al. reviewed operative management of zone II and III 5th metatarsal fractures in NBA players.* **They noted** that radiographic healing occurred at an average of 7.5 weeks with a return to play at around 10 weeks. **They advocated** internal fixation with the addition of bone grafting in high-risk patients to prevent re-fracture.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1 & 2: Continued nonoperative management, regardless of weight-bearing status, is inappropriate for a zone III 5th metatarsal fracture that does not show signs of healing at almost 3 months.
Answer 3: Based on the description of radiographic findings, an MRI would not likely change management in this case.
Answer 4: A bone stimulator may be considered, but the most reliable choice would be ORIF +/- bone grafting to get this fracture to heal.
