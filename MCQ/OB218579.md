## Question

A 58-year-old female presents to your office with a warm, swollen, and erythematous foot. She reports that her symptoms have worsened over the past two weeks. She denies any history of trauma to the foot. In differentiating an infectious process from a neuropathic arthropathy, which of the following is true?

## Options

1. Early radiographic changes will show fragmentation of articular surfaces with joint subluxation in Charcot neuropathy
2. MRI is best imaging modality to differentiate infection from Charcot arthropathy
3. MRI has a low sensitivity for osteomyelitis
4. Indium WBC scan will be negative (cold) for neuropathic joints
5. Technetium bone scan may be negative (cold) for neuropathic joint

## Correct option

**4. Indium WBC scan will be negative (cold) for neuropathic joints**

## Answer explanation

This patient has diabetic Charcot Neuropathy. An indium WBC bone scan will be negative (cold) for neuropathic joints.

Diabetic Charcot neuropathy is a chronic, noninfectious and progressive disease that occurs as a result of loss of protective sensation which leads to the destruction of foot and ankle joints and surrounding bony structures. Diagnosis can be made clinically with a warm and erythematous foot with erythema that decreases with foot elevation. Radiographs often reveal obliteration of joint space and fragmentation of both articular surfaces of a joint leading to subluxation or dislocation. Bone scans may play an important role when considering infectious versus diabetic Charcot neuropathy. Treatment is a trial of total contact casting for acute Charcot deformities without skin breakdown. Operative management is indicated for recurrent infections, deformities, and severe skin breakdown.

*Strotman et al. reviewed Charcot foot arthropathy.* **They reported** that in the vast majority of current patients it is secondary to longstanding diabetes. **They concluded** that diabetes-associated Charcot foot arthropathy creates a severe negative impact on health-related quality of life for affected individuals, leading to both substantial disability and a financial burden on the health care system.

*Serino et al. reviewed the utilization of nuclear medicine as a diagnostic tool for orthopedic foot and ankle pathology.* *They reported on the indications and utility of these techniques as they pertain to specific foot and ankle conditions, including osteomyelitis, stress fractures, talar osteochondral lesions, complex regional pain syndrome, oncology, plantar fasciitis, and painful total ankle arthroplasty.* **They concluded** with a discussion of their approach to nuclear medicine with illustrative cases.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: LATE radiographic findings of Charcot neuropathy will demonstrate fragmentation of both articular surfaces of a joint leading to subluxation or dislocation.
Answer 2: It is difficult to differentiate infection from Charcot arthropathy on MRI.
Answer 3: MRI has a high sensitivity in diagnosing soft tissue abscesses and osteomyelitis.
Answer 5: Technetium bone scans rely on radiolabeled phosphate analogs which will light up on imaging in areas of high bone turnover. Thereby technetium bone scans are very sensitive for areas of high bone turnover but nonspecific for the cause of the increased turnover rate. As such, both osteomyelitis and neuropathic arthropathy have expected positive technetium bone scans.
