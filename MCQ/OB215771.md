## Question

A 32-year-old male presents with multiple injuries following a motorcycle accident. On presentation, he is hemodynamically unstable with a GCS of 8. During the primary survey, he is sent to the CT scanner for chest, abdomen, and pelvis studies where the injury depicted in figure A is revealed. Which of the following is the most appropriate surgical approach for definitive treatment of this injury?

![[q215771-a-screen-shot-2021-08-30-at-4-01-14-pm.jpg]]
*Figure A*

![[q215771-b-screen-shot-2021-08-30-at-4-01-21-pm.jpg]]
*Figure B*

## Options

1. Smith-Peterson approach
2. Southern approach
3. Modified Stoppa approach
4. Kocher-Langenbach approach
5. Iliofemoral approach

## Correct option

**1. Smith-Peterson approach**

## Answer explanation

The patient is presenting with combined posterior hip dislocation and femoral head fracture. The best approach is a Smith-Peterson approach.

Femoral head fractures commonly occur with posterior hip dislocations and pose a treatment challenge. Often, irreducible fractures require temporization with skeletal traction followed by open reduction through a Smith-Peterson or Watson-Jones approach. Anterior-based approaches have the added benefit of avoiding the medial femoral circumflex artery, which is the main vascular supply to the femoral head, thus avoiding iatrogenic avascular necrosis.

*Mareck et al. reviewed the diagnosis and treatment of femoral head fractures.* **They stated** the initial management consists of addressing the patient's life-threatening injuries first followed by open reduction and internal fixation through an anterior approach. Careful technique and anatomic reduction can minimize the risk of avascular necrosis following these injuries.

*Mehta and Routt performed a retrospective study of seven patients with irreducible fracture-dislocations of the femoral head without posterior wall fractures.* **They reported** all patients achieved satisfactory reduction and fixation following a Smith-Peterson approach with two patients ultimately requiring total hip arthroplasty for avascular necrosis. **They concluded** fracture-dislocations of the femoral head without posterior wall involvement are rare entities that require early recognition and open reduction internal fixation.

Figures A and B are axial and coronal CTs of the pelvis with posterior hip dislocation and associated femoral head fracture.

Incorrect answers
Answer 2: The Southern approach (posterior approach) to the hip comes with the added risk of disrupting the medial femoral circumflex artery which can lead to avascular necrosis.
Answer 3: The modified Stoppa approach is an intrapelvic approach to the acetabulum that is commonly used for fractures involving the quadrilateral plate.
Answer 4: The Kocher-Lagenbach approach is suitable for posterior wall fractures of the acetabulum and is an extensile version of the Southern approach.
Answer 5: The iliofemoral approach is uncommonly used for anterior or complex bicolumnar acetabular fractures.
