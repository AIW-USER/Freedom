## Question

Which of the following is not a technique for acute TA repair? (regional orthopaedics- foot and ankle- tendo achilles injuries)

## Options

1. Ma and Griffith
2. Lynn
3. Lyndholm
4. Maffulli

## Correct option

**4. Maffulli**

## Answer explanation

TA rupture:

Mechanism of injury: Pushing off with the weight-bearing forefoot while extending the knee, sudden unexpected dorsiflexion of the ankle, and violent dorsiflexion of the plantar flexed foot.

Clinical tests:

1. Thompson squeeze test: Squeeze the calf muscle just distal to its maximal girth while the patient is prone. In case of complete rupture of Achilles tendon there will be no plantar flexion of the ankle.

2. Matles test: In prone position active flexion of the knees to 90 degrees will cause plantar flexion of the foot if Achilles tendon is intact. If the foot drops into neutral or slight dorsiflexion, the Achilles tendon is likely ruptured.

3. Needle test of O’Brien: A 25-gauge needle is placed percutaneously in the midline of the proximal tendon. With a passive ankle range of motion the needle will move if continuity of the tendon is maintained.

Ultrasound-based criteria for non-operative treatment:

- gap of less than 5 mm with maximal plantar flexion, (2) gap of less than 10 mm with the foot in neutral position, and (3) more than 75% tendon apposition with the foot in 20 degrees of plantar flexion.

Re-rupture rate after operative treatment: approx. 3-4%, Return to sports rate is approx. 60%.
