## Question

A 60-year-old male former professional bodybuilder presents with chronic progressive lower back and bilateral thigh pain. He currently smokes 1 pack per week. Physical exam is remarkable for midline tenderness and a positive ipsilateral left-sided straight-leg raise. Radiographs are shown in Figures A and B. The patient elects to undergo multilevel decompression and fusion for this condition and is able to quit smoking post-operatively. Which of the following is accurate regarding the risk of nonunion in this patient following surgery?

## Options

1. Decreased to the level of nonsmokers with post-operative smoking cessation for longer than 6 months
2. Directly dependent on the quantity smoked pre-operatively
3. Increased over nonsmokers whether the patient quit smoking pre- or post-operatively
4. Related to the duration of pre-operative smoking cessation
5. Unrelated to the number of levels fused

## Correct option

**3. Increased over nonsmokers whether the patient quit smoking pre- or post-operatively**

## Answer explanation

Post-operative smoking cessation has been found to decrease the risk of nonunion following lumbar spinal fusion, though the risk still remains elevated over nonsmokers. The impact of preoperative smoking cessation on nonunion remains to be elucidated.

Smoking has been identified as a major independent risk factor for pseudarthrosis following lumbar spinal fusion. The risk of nonunion has been found to be increased in smokers over nonsmokers for multi-level lumbar fusion procedures. However, literature is mixed on the impact of the amount of smoking, duration of pre- and post-operative smoking cessation, and the effect on outcomes following single-level posterior lumbar fusion procedures. Pre-operative smoking cessation has been shown to significantly decrease the risk of wound complications and is furthermore predictive of a patient’s ability to quit post-operatively.

*Glassman et al. evaluated the impact of smoking cessation on outcomes following lumbar spinal fusion.* **The authors found** that the nonunion rate was significantly greater for smokers and remained elevated over that of nonsmokers even with post-operative smoking cessation. However, **they found** that the risk of nonunion was significantly less in patients who were able to quit smoking for longer than 6 months postoperatively, though it still remained elevated over that for nonsmokers. Quantity smoked or duration of preoperative smoking cessation did not have an influence on the nonunion rate.

*Andersen et al. investigated the effect of perioperative smoking cessation on clinical and functional outcomes after undergoing lumbar spinal fusion.* **The authors found** that the risk of nonunion was increased in patients who smoked more than 10 cigarettes per day pre-operatively and who had multi-level fusions. The success of fusion in smokers approached that of nonsmokers with smoking cessation. They did not find a significant difference in nonunion between smokers and nonsmokers for single-level procedures.

Figure A shows the lumbar spine flexion view demonstrating multi-level degenerative lumbar spondylolisthesis, most pronounced at L4-5.
Figure B shows the lumbar spine extension view with maintained L4-5 spondylolisthesis.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: Despite the significant impact of post-operative smoking cessation, most notably for 6 months or more, the risk of nonunion approaches but still remains greater than that of nonsmokers.
Answer 2: The quantity smoked pre-operatively has not been definitively shown to increase the risk of nonunion, especially in the case of this patient who smokes <10 cigarettes per day. Some studies have supported an increased risk of nonunion for multi-level procedures in patients who smoke > 10 cigarettes per day as compared to those who smoke less than this, but the relationship of smoking to nonunion risk is not directly dependent on the quantity smoked.
Answer 4: The impact of preoperative smoking cessation and the duration of this have not been definitively shown to decrease the risk of nonunion following lumbar spinal fusion. However, smoking cessation of at least 4 weeks pre-operatively has been shown to decrease the risk of wound complications.
Answer 5: The impact of smoking on nonunion following single-level lumbar fusion remains controversial, with many studies showing no difference. However, multi-level procedures have been found to have a significantly increased risk of nonunion among smokers as compared to nonsmokers.
