## Question

Which of the following is true regarding the organism that most commonly causes osteomyelitis in the nondiabetic foot after a puncture wound?

## Options

1. It does not have a flagellum
2. It is a spore-forming, Gram-negative rod
3. It secretes a pigment in vitro
4. It ferments lactose
5. It produces clear colonies on nutrient agar

## Correct option

**3. It secretes a pigment in vitro**

## Answer explanation

The most common organism that causes osteomyelitis in the foot after a puncture wound is Pseudomonas aeruginosa. It secretes a pigment in vitro.

After a puncture wound, a fluoroquinolone is given to cover for P. aeuruginosa. It is a Gram-negative rod which may be normal flora in some sites of the body. It thrives in moist environments, produces an exotoxin, and forms biofilms. In vitro, it produces a blue-green pigment and has a characteristic grape-like odor. It does not have the ability to ferment lactose.

*Lavery et al. performed a case-control study to compare bacterial pathogens in diabetic and nondiabetic adults with foot infections after a puncture wound.* **They report** that nondiabetic patients had less osteomyelitis and were infected by fewer organisms. **They found** that Pseudomonas was the most common cause of osteomyelitis among nondiabetic subjects. Infections caused by Staphylococcus aureus were more common in diabetics as was polymicrobial osteomyelitis.

*Eron wrote a review about targeting pathogens in acute and chronic wounds.* **He recommends** an oral fluoroquinolone such as ciprofloxacin for puncture wounds to target P. aeruginosa. If there is a contraindication to using a fluoroquinolone, then parenteral antibiotic therapy with ceftazidime, piperacillin, or ticarcillin-clavulanate may be required.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: P. aeruginosa has a flagellum and exhibits motility.
Answer 2: P. aeruginosa cannot form spores.
Answer 4: P. aeruginosa cannot ferment lactose.
Answer 5: P. aeruginosa produces a pigment and colonies appear blue-green on nutrient agar.
