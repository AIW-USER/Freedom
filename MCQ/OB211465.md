## Question

A 62-year-old male proceeds with surgical treatment for the condition in Figure A. Which of the following surgical treatments utilizing a standard posterior midline approach can potentially decrease intraoperative blood loss, decrease the size of the surgical incision, and provide a high rate of successful fusion?

![[q211465-a-sagital-t2-mri-lspine.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Single-level PLIF with local autograft and fixation with cortical bone trajectory screws
2. Single-level PLIF with local autograft and fixation with conventional trajectory pedicle screws
3. Single-level PLIF with bone morphogenic protein-2 and allograft and fixation with conventional trajectory pedicle screws
4. Single-level PLIF with iliac crest bone graft and fixation with conventional trajectory pedicle screws
5. Single-level PLIF with iliac crest bone graft and fixation cortical bone trajectory screws

## Correct option

**1. Single-level PLIF with local autograft and fixation with cortical bone trajectory screws**

## Answer explanation

The use of medial-to-lateral cortical bone trajectory screws in the single-level lower lumbar spine interbody fusion has been associated with lower intraoperative blood loss and surgical incision length.

Degenerative spondylolisthesis occurs at a rate of 5-9% in the population and is more common in African American females over 50 years of age. The most commonly affected level is the L4-5 segment. The vast majority of cases respond to nonoperative treatment, but those requiring surgical intervention can be treated with posterolateral instrumented fusion or interbody fusion. The use of cortical bone trajectory screws was devised to decreased that amount of surgical exposure required thus leading to decreased blood loss, smaller incisions, and decreased pain scores the 1-week postop period.

*Lee et al. performed a single-blind randomized control trial noninferiority study comparing cortical screw fixation to pedicle screw fixation in patients undergoing single-level posterior lumbar interbody fusion (PLIF) at the L4-5 and L5-S1 segments.* **They reported** that cortical screw fixation was associated with significantly less blood loss, smaller skin incisions, and less superior facet violation with no differences in functional outcomes and fusion rates to pedicle screw fixation. **The authors concluded** that cortical screw fixation is potentially a technically easier procedure that can provide similar outcomes to conventional pedicle screw constructs.

*Orita et al. performed a prospective study of 40 patients undergoing transforaminal lumbar interbody fusion for spondylolisthesis with either percutaneous pedicle screw fixation or percutaneous cortical trajectory screw fixation.* **They reported** percutaneous cortical trajectory screw fixation had significantly decreased overall incision length and fluoroscopy time with no differences in other perioperative and postoperative data compared to percutaneous pedicle screw fixation. **They concluded** that percutaneous cortical trajectory screw fixation provides similar outcomes to percutaneous pedicle screw fixation and can be combined with interbody fusion.

*Ninomiya et al. performed a retrospective study of 21 patients undergoing PLIF for degenerative spondylolisthesis with either pedicle screw fixation or cortical trajectory screw fixation.* **They reported** that both fixation techniques demonstrated significant improvement in slip percentage at final follow-up compared to preoperative radiographs with no differences between the two fixation methods. **They concluded** that cortical trajectory screws provide similar fixation as conventional pedicle screws when combined with interbody fusion for the treatment of degenerative spondylolisthesis.

*Akpolat et al. performed a cadaveric biomechanical study to evaluate the fatigue behavior of conventional pedicle screws and cortical trajectory screws.* **They found** conventional pedicle screws to have greater fatigue strength and pullout strength compared to cortical trajectory screws with cadaver bone mass index being strongly correlated to fatigue and pullout strength of both constructs. **The authors concluded** that standard pedicle screw fixation appears to be biomechanically superior to cortical trajectory screws.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Conventional pedicle screws require greater lateral exposure and thus greater blood loss and increased surgical incision length in order to obtain the screw starting point.
Answer 3: The use of BMP-2 in spine surgery is FDA approved for single-level ALIF procedures. However,, traditional trajectory screws have an inherent increased exposure to gain the starting point that necessitates a larger incision and increased blood loss.
Answer 4: Traditional trajectory pedicle screws require greater lateral exposure and subsequent blood loss to obtain the screw starting points. Additionally, harvesting iliac crest bone graft adds to surgical morbidity by further lateral exposure and added blood loss from the harvest site. The use of local autograft with interbody fusion yields high fusion rates.
Answer 5: The use of iliac crest bone graft has the advantage of providing osteogenic components to the bone graft, but comes at the cost of increased exposure with increased blood loss and large incisions.
