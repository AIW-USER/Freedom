## Question

A 23-year-old male presents with right thigh pain that has worsened over the last 6 weeks. He reports frequent low-grade fevers and decreased energy. Current radiographs are depicted in figures A and B. His current ESR is 73 mm/hr and CRP is 23 mg/L. Which of the following findings would suggest the need for surgical treatment?

## Options

1. CRP >20 mg/L
2. Gram positive organism
3. Penumbra sign on MRI
4. Long bone involvement
5. Duration of symptoms for 6 weeks

## Correct option

**3. Penumbra sign on MRI**

## Answer explanation

The presence of the penumbra sign on MRI suggests an intraosseous abscess with surrounding necrotic tissue, which is unlikely to respond with antibiotics alone.

Bone sarcomas and osteomyelitis may have similar initial presentations and can be difficult to distinguish. Misdiagnosis can have significant implications on the patient and treatment success. Initial evaluation with radiographs, laboratory analysis, and magnetic resonance imaging provides useful information but may not be able to differentiate between the two entities. Obtaining a biopsy of the involved tissue with culture is the only means to definitively diagnose the presence of sarcoma or osteomyelitis. Recent studies have demonstrated high specificity of the penumbra sign with osteomyelitis. The penumbra sign is a vascularized membrane surrounding an abscess that has a higher T1 signal than the abscess fluid.

*Lex et al. retrospectively analyzed prospectively collected data from 238 patients to identify how best to distinguish bone and soft-tissue infections that mimic sarcomas.* **They reported** that infection is frequently clinically indistinguishable from sarcoma, with no single clinical, biochemical, or radiological feature or investigation able to be relied upon for diagnosis. The study concluded that clinicians should have a low threshold for tissue biopsy and discussion in a sarcoma multidisciplinary team meeting to diagnose bone and soft tissue infections accurately.

*Huang et al. studied a series of 10 patients with osteomyelitis of the femur that was initially diagnosed as having bone tumors and were subsequently transferred to another institution for further evaluation.* **They reported** common symptoms were nocturnal pain and elevated C-reactive protein levels. Radiographic findings included permeative, moth-eaten osteolytic lesions, osteolytic lesions with sclerotic borders, and cortical destruction with pathological fracture. **They concluded** surgical biopsy was necessary to confirm the diagnosis and detect the type of bacteria responsible for the infection as MRI is not always useful for distinguishing from tumors.

*Shimose et al. retrospectively studied the imaging and laboratory findings of 244 patients to identify distinguishing factors of osteomyelitis from bone tumors.* **They reported** that the penumbra sign and high CRP level were the most helpful, with the penumbra sign having a sensitivity of 73.3% and specificity of 99.1%. **They concluded** that the penumbra sign and high CRP level may be useful in identifying patients with osteomyelitis and thus helping to exclude the presence of a tumor.

Figures A and B are the AP and lateral radiographs of the right femur with destructive changes and soft tissue mass effect. Illustration A is the sagittal T1 MRI of the knee demonstrating Brodie's abscess with a penumbra sign.

Incorrect answers
Answer 1: Elevated CRP and ESR are not independent risk factors for failed antibiotic treatment.
Answer 2: Gram-positive organisms are not associated with treatment failure. Multidrug-resistant organisms and polymicrobial infections are associated with antibiotic treatment failure.
Answer 3: Osteomyelitis of long bones does not predispose the patient to antibiotic treatment failure.
Answer 5: Infections lasting greater than 3 months are associated with medical treatment failure.
