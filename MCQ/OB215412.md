## Question

Use of bone grafting through which approach has demonstrated a high rate of union for the treatment of distal tibial nonunions?

## Options

1. Anterior
2. Anterolateral
3. Anteromedial
4. Lateral
5. Posterolateral

## Correct option

**5. Posterolateral**

## Answer explanation

High rates of union using the posterolateral approach with bone grafting for the treatment of distal tibial nonunions have been reported.

A nonunion is an arrest in the fracture repair process. The pathophysiology for the development of a nonunion is often multifactorial, but most commonly occurs secondary to inadequate fracture stabilization and/or poor blood supply. Types of nonunion include septic nonunion, hypertrophic nonunion, atrophic nonunion, and oligotrophic nonunion. In the presence of a distal tibial nonunion, bone grafting through a posterolateral approach has demonstrated high rates of union. Contrary to alternate approaches, there is a lower risk of significant soft-tissue compromise.

*Foster et al. retrospectively reviewed posterolateral bone grafting to treat nonunions of the distal two-thirds of the tibia.* **They report** that fracture union was achieved in 75% of patients without further intervention, with a mean interval to union of 9.9 months (range, 3-22). Moreover, **they noted** no wound breakdown, vascular injury, or tendon injury associated with the approach. **They concluded** that even with large bone gaps, and a history of infection, union was achieved at a relatively high rate with posterolateral bone graft.

*Konda et al. demonstrated the technique of posterolateral bone grafting with iliac crest bone graft for a distal tibial nonunion.* **They report** that atrophic and oligotrophic nonunions have poor biology at the fracture site to promote bone growth and would therefore benefit from autogenous iliac bone graft, as this technique is osteoinductive, osteoconductive, and osteogenic. **They noted** that indications for posterolateral bone grafting of the tibia include atrophic or oligotrophic nonunions, an ipsilateral same level tibia and fibula nonunion, or a tibial nonunion with a large anteromedial soft-tissue injury. **They concluded** that the technique of posterolateral bone grafting with iliac crest bone graft for distal third tibia and fibula atrophic nonunion provides a reliable method for achieving union.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1-4: Other approaches to the distal tibia do not have as robust natural soft tissue coverage as the posterolateral approach. Therefore the posterolateral approach is recommended for the treatment of distal tibial nonunions as it provides high rates of union with low complication rates.
