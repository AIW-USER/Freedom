## Question

You have been treating a 74-year-old male with a past medical history of coronary artery bypass and renal insufficiency for low back pain and claudication described as pain that radiates to the left lower extremity. He has been treated with several epidural injections of corticosteroids into the lumbar spine that have provided significant, but short-term relief. He is ultimately treated with a decompression at the L2-3 and L3-4 levels. Which of the following dermatomal findings would be MOST suggestive of these involved levels and support decompression at these levels?

## Options

1. Pain radiating into the left plantar and lateral surfaces of the foot
2. Pain radiating into the left anterolateral leg and dorsum of the foot
3. Pain that radiates into the left anterior thigh and anteromedial aspect of the left leg
4. Pain radiating in the left inguinal region
5. Pain radiating into the left posterior and lateral calf

## Correct option

**3. Pain that radiates into the left anterior thigh and anteromedial aspect of the left leg**

## Answer explanation

This patient has lumbar spinal stenosis (LSS) that is refractory to nonoperative management. Decompression at the L2-3 and L3-4 levels would be appropriate for disabling pain that radiates into the left anterior thigh and anteromedial aspect of the leg

Lumbar spinal stenosis is a degenerative spinal condition characterized by the narrowing of the lumbar spinal canal due to a variety of bony or soft tissues structures. Diagnosis is made with MRI studies of the lumbar spine. Treatment is a trial of nonoperative management with NSAIDs and physical therapy. Surgical laminotomy and discectomy is indicated for progressive disabling pain that has failed nonoperative management, and/or progressive neurological deficits.

*Weiner et al. described and analyzed a technique for lumbar microdecompression that affords an excellent decompression while minimizing damage to surrounding tissues.* **They reported** that this technique affords an excellent decompression while minimizing destruction to tissues not directly involved in the pathologic process. **They noted** that functional outcome scores doubled, and 87% of patients reported high satisfaction rates. **They concluded** that lumbar microdecompression is a minimally invasive technique that appears to provide excellent functional outcomes.

*Alimi et al. retrospectively reviewed a case series evaluating the clinical and radiographic outcomes in patients who underwent LSS and compared outcomes in patients with and without preoperative spondylolisthesis.* **They reported** that minimally invasive laminectomy is an effective procedure for the treatment of LSS. **They concluded** that the reoperation rates for instability are lower than those reported after open laminectomy, and that the functional improvement is similar in patients with and without preoperative spondylolisthesis.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Pain radiating into the plantar and lateral surfaces of the foot suggests a S1 dermatome involvement.
Answer 2: Pain radiating into the left anterolateral leg and dorsum of the foot suggests a L5 dermatome involvement.
Answer 4: Pain radiating in the left inguinal region suggests an L1 dermatome involvement.
Answer 5: Pain radiating into the left posterior and lateral calf suggests involvement of the L5-S2 dermatomes.
