## Question

A 24-month-old female presents to clinic for evaluation of her hips. She has previously been treated with a Pavlik harness. Pelvis radiograph is shown in Figure A. What is the most appropriate intervention at this time?

![[q5908-a-ddh-l-hip-ap-pelvis.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Ultrasounds of the bilateral hips
2. Rigid abduction orthosis and weekly radiographs
3. MRI of the pelvis
4. Open reduction of the left hip +/- pelvic and/or femoral osteotomy and application of spica cast
5. Closed reduction of the left hip and application of spica cast

## Correct option

**4. Open reduction of the left hip +/- pelvic and/or femoral osteotomy and application of spica cast**

## Answer explanation

The patient is presenting with hip dysplasia and evidence of hip dislocation on radiographs. Given her age, the best intervention would be open reduction of the left hip with or without pelvic and/or femoral osteotomy with application of hip spica cast. Femur shortening can be decided upon intraoperatively based upon tension of the reduced hip.

The treatment algorithm for developmental hip dysplasia depends on the age of the patient and efficacy of prior treatment. For patients less than 6 months of age with a reducible hip on clinical examination, Pavlik harness is indicated. If this fails after 3-4 weeks, a rigid abduction brace can be used for 3-4 weeks with weekly ultrasounds. Further failure then warrants either closed or open reduction and hip spica casting. Younger patients, typically less than 18 months of age, are more amenable to closed reduction. After age 2, patients typically require a femoral or pelvic osteotomy, depending on the the degree of hip dysplasia and how proximal the femoral head is prior to reduction.

*Vitale and Skaggs reviewed the management of hip dysplasia.* **They report** excellent outcome with use of Pavlik harness in patients younger than 6 months. **They recommend** more invasive intervention tailored to the specific underlying disorder leading to dysplasia rather than based on age alone.

*Murray et al. retrospectively reviewed results of closed reduction in 35 hips in 30 patients.* *Only 2 hips were unable to be closed reduced at time of attempt.* Thirty percent of hips developed evidence of acetabular dysplasia requiring later surgery and 12% of hips developed osteonecrosis.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: After 6 months of age, x-ray can be used to evaluate the hips. The left hip is dislocated on the radiograph and ultrasound is not needed
Answer 2: Rigid abduction braces may be useful up to 6 months of age
Answer 3: The left hip is dislocated on x-rays. MRI is not routinely required in this setting.
Answer 5: Closed reduction is typically not successful after 18 months of age.
