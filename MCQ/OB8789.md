## Options

1. Decompression and initiation of rifampin, ethambutol, isoniazid, and pyrazinamide
2. Anterior L3 corpectomy with posterior instrumentation
3. Early mobilization, with or without TLSO
4. Spinal traction x 6 weeks, followed by weight bearing as tolerated
5. CT-guided biopsy, en bloc spondylectomy with staged reconstruction

## Correct option

**3. Early mobilization, with or without TLSO**

## Answer explanation

This patient presents with a displaced lumbar burst fracture with a small degree of retropulsion. For neurologically intact patients with no posterior ligamentous disruption, there is no proven benefit of surgery over non-operative management with early mobilization, with or without a TLSO.

The thoracolumbar injury classification and severity (TLICS) score is helpful in determining operative vs. non-operative treatment of thoracolumbar burst fractures. Surgery is recommended for patients with a score >4 (see illustration A). For patients treated non-operatively with a TLSO, multiple studies have demonstrated that there is no difference in long-term pain, return to work, functional outcomes, or sagittal balance as compared to surgically treated patients. Additionally, surgery has been demonstrated to be 4x more expensive and have a higher rate of complications. Multiple studies have also demonstrated that retropulsed bone fragments can be absorbed over time and the surgically and non-operative patients have similar levels of canal remodeling. More recent controversy has centered over whether or not a TLSO is needed, and recent reports suggest that patients may have similar outcomes and less deconditioning without bracing.

*Bailey et al. conducted a multicenter, randomized controlled trial of T11-L3 burst fractures without neurologic deficit treated with or without TLSO and early mobilization.* **They found** equivalent disability scores between the groups at 3 months, indicating that the discomfort and deconditioning associated with brace use might be avoidable.

*Alcala-cerra et al. reported on 2 prospective randomized controlled trials in attempt to determine if a TLSO is needed for neurologically intact patients with thoracolumbar burst fractures.* **They identified** 119 patients and concluded that the available literature indicates that a spinal orthosis is not mandatory for these fractures.

*Wood et al performed the first randomized control trial comparing operative versus nonoperative treatment of thoracolumbar burst fractures in patients with no neurological deficits.* **They found** no major long-term advantages with operative treatment, and increased disability and complications with operative treatment.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: There is no evidence the patient has tuberculosis. Common MRI findings of tuberculosis are end-plate disruption, paravertebral soft tissue shadow, and sub-ligamentous spread of infection.
Answer 2: Corpectomy with strut graft and posterior decompression and fusion would be the recommended surgery if the patient had a neurologic deficit.
Answer 4: Spinal traction for 6 weeks would lead to more complications (such as decubitus ulcers) than TLSO.
Answer 5: The history and imaging does not suggest a primary spinal tumor.
