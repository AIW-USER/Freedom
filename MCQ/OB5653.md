## Question

Figure A is an axial MR image of the L4-5 disc space. A patient with the pathology seen in this image would be expected to have all of the following signs and symptoms EXCEPT:

![[q5653-a-lumbar-disc-herniation.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Numbness over dorsum of foot
2. Decreased patellar tendon reflex
3. Weakness to hip abduction
4. Weakness of great toe extension
5. Positive straight leg raise

## Correct option

**2. Decreased patellar tendon reflex**

## Answer explanation

Figure A is an axial MR image demonstrating a paracentral disc herniation at L45/5. This would compress the L5 nerve root leading to symptoms that would likely include 1) numbness over dorsum of foot 3) weakness to hip abduction 4) weakness of great toe extension 5) positive straight leg raise. A L5 radiculopathy would not be expected to lead to decreased patellar tendon reflex.

In the lumbar spine a paracentral disc herniation typically compresses the descending nerve root (at L4/5 this is the L5 nerve root which exits under the L5 pedicle). This is in contrast to a far lateral herniation which compresses the exiting nerve root (at L4/5 this is the L4 nerve root which exits under the L4 pedicle). In the cervical spine, the nerve roots do not "descend" before they exit the foramen, and therefore a disc, whether central or lateral, always affects the exiting nerve root (a C4/5 disc hernation compresses the C5 nerve root which exits above the C5 pedicle).

In the cervical spine the exiting nerve root travels above the corresponding pedicle (C5 exits above the C5 pedicle) whereas in the lumbar spine the exiting nerve root exits below the corresponding pedicle (L5 exits below the L5 pedicle). The transition from above the corresponding pedicle to below occurs because there is a C8 nerve root but no C8 pedicle (no C8 vertebral body).

*Radcliff et al. present a prospective study on the use of epidural injections for lumbar disc herniations.* **They found** no difference in short or long term outcomes between the two groups. Significant crossover occurred between groups and may have confounded their data, so they make no specific recommendations on the use of epidural injections.

*Weinstein et al. present 4 year outcomes for patients with lumbar disc herniations treated with and without surgery.* In a combined, as treated analysis, **they found** patients who had undergone surgery achieved greater improvements than those treated non-operatively.

Figure A is an axial MR image of the L4-5 disc space. A paracentral disc herniation can be seen. Illustration A is a diagram showing the anatomy of exiting nerve roots from the lumbar spine. Notice how a central or paracentral herniation would impinge on the lower nerve root, while a far lateral herniation would impinge on the upper nerve root. Illustration B shows the difference in the relations between the nerve root and corresponding pedicle between the cervical spine and lumbar spine.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: A decreaesed patellar reflex would be cause by a L4 radiculopathy. This would be cause by a far lateral disc herniation at L4/5 (L4 is exiting nerve root at this level) or a paracentral disc at L3/4 (L4 nerve root is descending nerve root at this level).
