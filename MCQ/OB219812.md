## Question

A 22-year-old soldier collapses during a weighted 10-mile March with an ambient temperature that day of 28.8°C (84°F). This was the seventh day of field exercise, during which the soldiers were required to carry their drinking water each day. Prior to collapsing, members of his platoon reported that he was behaving erratically, and he is currently unable to clearly answer simple questions. On exam, he is found to have sweat-soaked and pale skin. A rectal temperature probe is unavailable, but his temporal temperature is measured at 39.4°C (103°F). Vitals include a systolic blood pressure of 100 and an elevated respiratory rate. The battalion surgeon decides to delay transfer to the hospital and immediately begins cooling with an ice bath and intravenous fluids. Which clinical finding necessitates this immediate treatment?

## Options

1. Mental status changes
2. Temporal temperature of 39.4°C (103°F)
3. Anhidrosis
4. Elevated respiratory rate
5. Hypotension

## Correct option

**1. Mental status changes**

## Answer explanation

This patient presents with clinical findings consistent with exertional heat stroke. Any heat-related collapse with mental status changes should be treated with immediate whole-body cooling (Answer 1).

Exertional heat stroke is a life-threatening medical emergency. It is defined by hyperthermia (core body temperature greater than 40°C (104°F) associated with central nervous system disturbances and multiple organ system failure. This patient exhibited several risk factors, including an extended exercise course and dehydration. Patients with exertional heat stroke often present with sweat-soaked, pale skin. Meanwhile, non-exertional heatstroke often presents with dry and flushed skin. Exertional heat exhaustion can be difficult to distinguish from exertional heat stroke, as both conditions can present with collapse, hypotension, and tachypnea. Rectal temperature should be obtained early in the diagnosis as temporal, axillary, and auricular temperatures are unreliable in diagnosing or monitoring this condition. Without other life-threatening conditions, whole-body cooling should not be delayed, even for transport to a higher level of care.

*Armstrong et al. published the American College of Sports Medicine’s position statement on exertional heat illness.* *They reviewed the risk factors and pathophysiology of exertional heat illness.* *They also offer evidence statements regarding the prevention, diagnosis, and treatment of heat illnesses.* **The authors conclude** that exertional collapse with mental status changes should trigger immediate evaluation and treatment. A complete list of their evidence statement is found in Illustration A.

*Becker et al. published the American Family Physician position statement on heat-related illness.* *They discuss the risk factors, presentation, and treatment of heat-related illnesses.* **They report** that patients with heat stroke can often present with persistent perspiration. **They conclude** that patients with a core temperature over 40°C (104°F) should undergo immediate rapid cooling.

Illustration 1 shows the evidence statements from the American College of Sports Medicine’s position statement on exertional heat illness.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 2: The American College of Sports Medicine states that oral, temporal, axillary, and auricular temperatures should not be used for the diagnosis of exertional heat stroke.
Answer 3: Patients with exertional heat stroke often present with persistent perspiration.
Answer 4: Tachypnea is not a specific finding to exertional heat stroke.
Answer 5: Hypotension is not a specific finding to exertional heat stroke.
