## Question

You are performing a knee ligament reconstruction of a structure which has the femoral insertion denoted with the white arrow in Figure A. For this procedure, which of the following graft choices will provide a patient with the highest risk of postoperative instability?

![[q218855-a-schottle.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Allograft semitendinosus
2. Autograft gracilis
3. Autograft quadriceps tendon
4. Synthetic graft
5. All of the above have equal rates of postoperative instability

## Correct option

**5. All of the above have equal rates of postoperative instability**

## Answer explanation

Figure A demonstrates Schottle's point, which serves as a radiographic landmark for the femoral insertion of the medial patellofemoral ligament (MPFL). Current literature supports the assertion that postoperative rate of recurrent instability does not differ with regards to the listed graft choices (Answer 5).

Patellar instability is a relatively common condition that often presents with recurrent patellar subluxation or dislocation episodes. This instability can be secondary to a variety of etiologies including ligamentous injury, ligamentous laxity and/or increased quadriceps (Q) angle of the knee. Diagnosis is made clinically, and the appropriate surgical technique is dependent on a variety of patient factors including the number of dislocations, the presence/absence of associated intra articular chondral loose bodies, the trochlear morphology, the overall rotational profile of the limb and patient goals and/or expectations. For patients with recurrent instability and no significant underlying malalignment, isolated MPFL reconstruction has been shown to reliably reduce the rate of future instability events. The femoral insertion of the MPFL can be found at Schottle's point (Figure A). A variety of graft choices are available when performing a MPFL reconstruction, and recent literature suggests that the rate of recurrent instability, postoperative infection and overall patient-reported outcomes do not vary between allografts or autografts.

*Migliorini et al. provide a systematic review and meta-analysis of graft choice in the setting of primary MPFL reconstruction for patellofemoral instability.* *Overall, their study pooled data from 12 studies (474 procedures) with a mean follow-up of 42.2 months and an average patient age of 21.1 years.* **The authors postulate** that allografts represent an attractive alternative to traditional autograft for MPFL reconstruction, and yield similar patient-reported outcomes, rates of persistent instability and revision.

*McNeilan et al. also provide a systematic review and meta-analysis of graft choice in the setting of isolated MPFL reconstruction.* *Their study pooled data from 45 studies (1,504 patients) with 27 documented cases of recurrent instability.* Overall, **the authors conclude** that autograft is not superior to allograft or synthetic grafts for isolated reconstruction of the MPFL, and that rates of recurrent instability are uniformly low. Of interest, they did note that pediatric patients and patients treated with adductor tendon autograft tended to experience higher rates of recurrent instability.

Figure A is a lateral radiograph of the distal femur, with Schottle's point indicated by an arrow. Schottle's point can be found just anterior to the posterior cortex extension line, 2.5 mm distal to the posterior origin of the medial femoral condyle, and proximal to the level of the posterior point of the Blumensaat line on a lateral radiograph with both posterior condyles projected in the same plane. This point represents the isometric point of the reconstructed MPFL. Illustration A demonstrates a graphical representation of how to locate Schottle's point.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1-4: Overall, the literature supports the assertion that the rate of post-operative recurrent instability does not differ among the listed graft choices.
