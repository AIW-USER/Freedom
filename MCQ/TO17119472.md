Chapter: Arthroscopy MCQs — Portals (Knee Arthroscopy)

## Question

Gillquist manuvre was described by Gillquist to enable the arthroscopic surgeon to

## Options

1. Check Patello femoral tracking is appropriate or not
2. Visualize the ACL tibial stump via trans patellar tendon portal
3. Visualize the posterior compartment of the knee
4. visualize anterior compartment of the knee

## Correct option

**3. Visualize the posterior compartment of the knee**

## Answer explanation

For viewing the patello-femoral articulation, supero-lateral portal of the knee is the most appropriate one. It is made lateral to the quadriceps tendon and 2.5cm above the superolateral pole of patella.

Gillquist portal refers to the Central transpatellar portal that is located in midline 1 cm inferior to the lower pole of patella. It is used by some surgeons in anterior cruciate ligament (ACL) reconstruction after the graft has been harvested.

However, in 1979 he published a technique (Gillquist manuvre) to access the posterior compartment of the knee via this portal. He described introducing a 5-mm Storz arthroscope (30 degree viewing angle) through the patellar tendon in the mid-line 1 cm below the apex of the patella, passing it between the medial femoral condyle and the posterior cruciate ligament (PCL) to enter the posterior compartment. Thereafter, the 30 degree angled telescope is replaced by a 70 degree instrument for greater field of view. This provides visualization of the meniscus, its posterior horn and meniscocapsular junction, any ramp lesion, the PCL, and any loose bodies in the posterior compartment. The modified Gillquist maneuver uses the anterolateral or anteromedial portal for arthroscope introduction instead of the transpatellar tendon portal for transcondylar posterior compartment visualization.

Transcondylar insertion of the arthroscope is not always easy, especially in knees with intact cruciate ligaments and osteoarthritis changes. So, McGinnis et al. reported his preference of doing an inferior notchplasty at the lateral aspect of the medial femoral condyle to aid the advancement of arthroscope.

answer: c
