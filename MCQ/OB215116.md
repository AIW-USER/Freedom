## Question

A 22-year-old college pole vaulter has chronic anterior pain on her jumping knee. She report that this anterior knee pain was only bothersome during training initially, however, she now complains of persistent pain with and without activities. She has completed several courses of physical therapy without improvement. Her recent MRI is seen in Figure A. Which treatment modality is most indicated at this time?

![[q215116-a-knee-mri-sag-t1-pcm-tendinosis.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Aggressive concentric stretching program
2. Intra-tendinous injection of corticosteroid
3. Intra-articular corticosteroid injection
4. Arthroscopic patellar chondroplasty
5. Surgical excision of the affected tissue

## Correct option

**5. Surgical excision of the affected tissue**

## Answer explanation

This patient has Blazina Stage III patellar tendon disease with chronic pain and dysfunction. Treatment for this advanced disease in which there is persistent pain with or without activities includes surgical excision and suture repair as needed.

Patellar tendinitis is tendinopathy of the patellar tendon associated with activity-related anterior knee pain. The diagnosis is primarily made clinically with tenderness to palpation at the distal pole of patella in full extension. Treatment is generally nonoperative with rest, ice, activity modifications and physical therapy to focus on hamstring, quadriceps and core strengthening, and an emphasis on hamstring stretching. Indications for surgical excision and repair includes Blazina Stage III disease, chronic pain and dysfunction not amendable to conservative treatment, and partial tears.

*Blazina et al. reviewed the functional anatomy, pathophysiology, and management of overuse injuries of the extensor mechanism in athletes.* *They report on a classification system of these overuse injuries.* **They concluded** that surgical treatment is indicated in Stage III disease.

*Panni et al reviewed extensor mechanism injuries in athletes.* *They reported on surgically treated patients that underwent debridement in either an open or arthroscopic fashion.* **They noted** that postoperative rehabilitation involves initial immobilization in extension, with weight bearing as tolerated and progressive range-of-motion. **They concluded** that approximately 80% to 90% of athletes are able to return to play post-operatively.

Figure A is the sagittal MRI image demonstrating inflammation and mucoid degeneration seen within the patellar tendon. Illustration A is the Blazina classification.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1: Conservative treatment may include an ECCENTRIC stretching program. However, this will likely not be sufficient with this advanced disease state.
Answers 2&3: Conservative treatment modalities may be used to treat most cases of patellar tendonitis. However, surgical excision and repair is indicated in Blazina Stage III disease, chronic pain and dysfunction not amendable to conservative treatment, and partial tears.
Answer 4: Arthroscopic patellar chondroplasty would not adequately address the advanced patellar tendon pathology
