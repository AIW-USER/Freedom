## Question

A 62-year-old woman presents with progressive bunion pain and difficulty with shoe wear. Physical examination reveals a hallux valgus deformity with medial eminence prominence and forefoot metatarsalgia. Weight-bearing radiographs demonstrate a hallux valgus angle of 36°, an intermetatarsal angle of 15°, and a metatarsus adductus angle of 18°. An AP radiograph is shown in Figure A. You are considering operative correction with a proximal first metatarsal osteotomy. Your colleague suggests adding second and third proximal metatarsal abduction osteotomies to address the metatarsus adductus component. Which of the following statements regarding the management of this patient's combined deformity is most accurate?

![[q221043-a-met-adductus-and-hv.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Second and third metatarsal osteotomies are mandatory in all cases with metatarsus adductus angle >15° to prevent recurrence
2. A rotational scarf osteotomy of the first metatarsal alone may provide adequate correction without requiring lesser metatarsal procedures
3. Tarsometatarsal arthrodesis of the first three rays is the preferred approach for this degree of metatarsus adductus
4. The metatarsus adductus angle of 18° indicates that the first intermetatarsal angle measurement is artificially elevated and should be disregarded
5. Soft tissue syndesmosis procedures are contraindicated when metatarsus adductus is present due to forefoot rigidity

## Correct option

**2. A rotational scarf osteotomy of the first metatarsal alone may provide adequate correction without requiring lesser metatarsal procedures**

## Answer explanation

The patient presents with combined hallux valgus and metatarsus adductus deformities. A rotational scarf osteotomy of the first metatarsal alone will provide adequate correction for hallux valgus in the setting of mild to moderate metatarsus adductus (<20 degrees) without requiring lesser metatarsal procedures (Answer 2).

Hallux valgus with metatarsus adductus represents a challenging surgical scenario because the medially deviated lesser metatarsals narrow the first intermetatarsal space, limiting correction and increasing recurrence risk. Traditional teaching suggests that moderate to severe metatarsus adductus (MA angle ≥20°) requires combined first ray and lesser metatarsal osteotomies, while milder deformities may be treated with isolated first ray procedures such as distal chevron, rotational scarf, or proximal reverse chevron osteotomies. The specific surgical technique selected for the first ray influences outcomes, as procedures with rotational or multiplanar correction capabilities may achieve adequate results even in the presence of mild to moderate metatarsus adductus without requiring lesser metatarsal intervention. The intermetatarsal angle is another important measurement in these patients when deciding if lesser toe metatarsal intervention is required. If IMA <13, there may not be sufficient room for hallux valgus correction and can require osteotomies or fusions of the lesser toes to correct the metatarsus adductus and make space for the hallux correction.

Reddy reviews metatarsus adductus as a common congenital forefoot deformity that is frequently present in patients undergoing hallux valgus surgery and is associated with both the development and recurrence of deformity. The author outlines the major radiographic assessment methods, including the Sgarlato, Engel, and Kilmartin techniques, emphasizing that measured angles vary by method and must be interpreted carefully in surgical planning. Successful correction of hallux valgus in the setting of metatarsus adductus requires individualized surgical planning that addresses both first-ray and lesser-ray deformities, as failure to account for the global deformity increases recurrence risk.

*Shi et al. review the operative decision-making principles for adult hallux valgus,* **emphasizing** that surgical planning should be guided by radiographic parameters including the hallux valgus angle (HVA), intermetatarsal angle (IMA), and distal metatarsal articular angle (DMAA), while also accounting for associated deformities such as metatarsus adductus. **The authors conclude** that distal soft tissue procedures and distal metatarsal osteotomies (chevron) are appropriate for mild deformities, while proximal metatarsal osteotomies and first tarsometatarsal (Lapidus) arthrodesis are indicated for moderate to severe cases, with technique selection based on deformity severity and joint congruency.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The statement that second and third metatarsal osteotomies are mandatory in all cases with a metatarsus adductus angle ≥15° is not correct. Successful correction has been demonstrated in patients with MA angles in the 18–20° range using first-ray osteotomies alone.
Answer 3: Tarsometatarsal arthrodesis of the first three rays is not the preferred approach for moderate metatarsus adductus without instability. Arthrodesis is typically reserved for severe deformity, rigid multiplanar malalignment, or demonstrable TMT instability, given its greater surgical morbidity and longer recovery.
Answer 4: Although metatarsus adductus can confound the interpretation of the first–second intermetatarsal angle, this measurement should not be disregarded. The intermetatarsal angle must be interpreted in the context of the overall deformity, including the metatarsus adductus angle, hallux valgus angle, and distal metatarsal articular angle. Adjunctive measurements may assist in understanding true forefoot alignment.
Answer 5: Soft tissue syndesmosis procedures are not contraindicated in the presence of metatarsus adductus. Published series have demonstrated meaningful radiographic correction and functional improvement in patients with hallux valgus and metatarsus adductus treated with soft tissue realignment alone.
