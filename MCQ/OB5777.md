## Question

A 4-year-old girl has a history of developmental dysplasia of the right hip that required closed reduction and spica casting at 9 months of age. She presents now to your office with the image seen in Figure A. The acetabular index is 35° on the right. What is the recommended treatment at this time?

![[q5777-a-hip-dysplasia-2.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Observation with serial radiographs
2. Hip abduction bracing
3. Right hip iliac osteotomy
4. Right hip periacetabular osteotomy
5. Right hip periacetabular osteotomy with proximal femoral osteotomy

## Correct option

**3. Right hip iliac osteotomy**

## Answer explanation

The patient has residual dysplasia of the right hip and subluxation of the femoral head with lateral uncovering. She would benefit from an iliac (Pemberton or Dega) osteotomy.

Developmental dysplasia of the hip involves a poorly developed, shallow acetabulum. During early life the acetabulum has good potential for remodeling, and treatments focus on maintaining the femoral head within the acetabulum (i.e. with a Pavlik harness) to encourage this remodeling to occur. After age 2, dysplasia can be measured on AP pelvis radiographs with measurement of the acetabular index, which should be less than 25°. Residual dysplasia has been shown to increase point loading forces within the hip joint and lead to early arthritic changes. It is recommended to treat residual dysplasia (moderate-severe dysplasia, broken Shenton's line) with osteotomies designed to increase the contact surface area within the hip and prevent late sequelae. When abnormal anatomy of the proximal femur is also present, proximal femoral osteotomy may be required in addition to the pelvic procedure.

*Faciszewski et al. present a review of 52 hips with residual acetabular dysplasia treated by Pemberton osteotomy.* *Average age at time of surgery was 4 years, and average follow up was 10 years.* **They noted** that 42 of the hips had a Severin class Ia, or normally developed hip at final follow-up.

*Gillingham et al. review treatment for residual dysplasia following early treatment for DDH.* *They describe how dysplastic hips experience point loading at the edge of the shallow acetabulum.* **They recommend** Pemberton osteotomies for children between ages 2 and 10, a triple osteotomy for older patient's with open triradiate cartilage, and a Ganz periacetabular osteotomy for those with a closed triradiate cartilage.

Figure A is an AP pelvis radiograph demonstrating right sided developmental dysplasia of the hip. The acetabulum is shallow with an acetabular index of 35°, and the femoral head is laterally subluxated. Illustration A demonstrates how to measure the acetabular index, which is labeled "A". It is the angle formed by the intersection of two lines: a line connecting the triradiate cartilage to the lateral border of the acetabulum, and Hilgenreiner's line which connects the left and right triradiate cartilages. Illustration B is a flow chart summarizing a treatment algorithm for DDH. Illustrations C, D, and E are diagrams showing the locations of a Pemberton, Triple, and Ganz osteotomies respectively. Illustration F is an AP pelvis radiograph of the same patient approximately 6 months following an iliac osteotomy.

Incorrect answers:
Answers 1 and 2: Both hips demonstrate residual dysplasia and would benefit from surgical treatment.
Answers 4 and 5: Both hips have residual dysplasia and require osteotomy, but a PAO would not be the correct choice in a patient this age and with open triradiate cartilage. Also, femoral osteotomies would not be required as the proximal femur appears normal.
