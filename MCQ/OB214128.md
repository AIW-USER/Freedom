## Question

A 55-year-old male presents with severe neck pain and quadriparesis after falling off a 2-story balcony while intoxicated. The patient is GCS 9 on presentation with notable weakness of the bilateral upper and lower extremities. Current imaging is shown in figure A. The decision was made to send the patient for a cervical spine MRI prior to attempted closed reduction and surgical fixation. If a closed reduction is planned, which MRI finding would require immediate surgical treatment rather than closed reduction?

![[q214128-a-facet-dislocation-edit.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Presence of spinal cord edema at the injury level
2. Identify a herniated disc at the dislocated level
3. Disruption of the posterior longitudinal ligamentous complex
4. Presence of an occult fractures of the lamina at the affected level
5. Increased T2 signal in the anterior longitudinal ligament of the affected level

## Correct option

**2. Identify a herniated disc at the dislocated level**

## Answer explanation

Ventral compression of the spinal cord by extruded disc material can occur with bilateral facet dislocations and would require a decompression, either anterior or posterior, prior to a reduction maneuver.

Bilateral facet dislocations are the result of flexion-distraction forces across the subaxial spine. Due to the extent of anterior displacement, there is significant acute stenosis at the affected level typically leading to spinal cord injury. Prompt closed reduction is recommended for patients that are awake and able to participate in serial neurological examinations as early reduction has been associated with improved neurologic recovery. In some cases, ventral compression of the spinal cord by disc material may be present, which could lead to worsening neurological status with closed reduction. In these cases, anterior decompression and fusion should be performed in conjunction with reduction. Prereduction MRI of the cervical spine is not routinely recommended as this would lead to treatment delay and potentially worse neurological recovery. However, patients that are unable or unreliable to participate in neurologic examinations, such as intoxicated patients or those with head injuries, should undergo a pre-reduction MRI.

*Hadley et al. reviewed the literature regarding the closed reduction of cervical spine fracture-dislocation injuries.* **The authors stated** that prereduction MRI was unnecessary for awake patients as early reduction was associated with improved neurologic recovery. **They concluded** patients who are unable to participate in neurologic testing should undergo a prereduction MRI to rule out ventral compression from extruded disc material and preoperative MRI evaluation for patients who have failed closed reduction due to a higher incidence of anatomic blocks to reduction.

*Vaccaro et al. performed a prospective clinical study of 11 patients with a cervical facet dislocation that underwent a pre- and post-reduction cervical spine MRI to evaluate for intervertebral disc herniations and spinal ligament injuries.* **They reported** that two patients were found to have disc herniations before reduction, whereas five patients were found to have herniated discs after closed reduction. Despite the increase in herniated discs after closed reduction, there were no patients with worsening neurological changes. **The authors concluded** that closed reduction of facet dislocations appears to increase the incidence of intervertebral disc herniations, but this does not appear to pose harm to the awake and alert patient.

Figure A is the sagittal CT scan of the cervical spine with a C6-7 bilateral facet dislocation.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: The presence or absence of spinal cord edema identified by MRI would not deter an initial closed reduction.
Answer 3: Disruption of the posterior ligamentous complex would suggest added instability, but would not deter initial closed reduction.
Answer 4: The presence of an occult lamina fracture would not affect whether the patient undergoes an anterior or posterior procedure, nor would it pose a risk for further neurological deterioration.
Answer 5: Increased T2 signal in the anterior longitudinal ligament would not require immediate surgical fixation over initial closed reduction.
