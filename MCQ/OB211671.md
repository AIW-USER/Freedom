## Question

A 62-year-old female presents with worsening left foot pain over the last year. Her pain is located mostly in her great toe and is worse with prolonged walking. She has noticed it is more difficult to wear her normal shoe wear due to medial sided great toe pain. Physical examination reveals hallux MTP dorsiflexion of 5° and plantarflexion of 20° with pain through the arc of motion. There is a positive grind test. ## Options

1. MTP arthrodesis
2. Phalangeal dorsiflexion osteotomy
3. Lapidus procedure
4. Cheilectomy
5. Dorsal cheilectomy with metatarsal osteotomy

## Correct option

**1. MTP arthrodesis**

## Answer explanation

The patient is presenting with advanced hallux rigidus which is most reliably treated with 1st metatarsophalangeal (MTP) arthrodesis.

Hallux rigidus is the result of degenerative changes to the 1st MTP joint leading to pain, decreased range of motion, and difficulty with shoe wear. Initial treatment involves shoe wear modifications, Morton's extension orthotic, and activity modifications. However, patients that are refractory may be suitable for surgical treatment. The early-stage disease can often be with cheilectomy with good results. Late-stage disease, characterized by pain throughout range of motion and advanced arthritis on radiographs, is most reliably treated with arthrodesis. Several studies have documented varying results and high complication rates with arthroplasty of the 1st MTP joint.

*Coughlin and Shurnas performed a retrospective review of 114 patients treated for hallux rigidus.* **They reported** hallux valgus was associated with hallux valgus interphalangeus, bilateral involvement in those with familial history, unilateral involvement in those with a history of trauma, and female gender.

*Perez-Aznar et al. prospectively evaluated the outcomes of dorsal-wedge phalangeal osteotomy for the treatment of grade II and III hallux rigidus in 40 patients.* **The authors reported** the mean AOFAS score to improve from 51.7 to 88.8, mean VAS scores to improve from 7.6 to 1.9, and mean passive dorsiflexion improved from 20.3° to 55.7°. **They concluded** dorsal-wedge phalangeal osteotomy is an effective treatment for hallux rigidus.

*Coughlin and Shurnas performed a long-term follow-up study of 114 patients treated for hallux rigidus.* **They reported** an overall 97% good to excellent subjective results at a mean follow-up of 9.6 years for cheilectomies and 6.7 years for arthrodeses with 92% of patients undergoing cheilectomy have good pain relief and functional outcomes. **They concluded** cheilectomy was effective for treating grade 1-2 and select grade 3 disease, whereas grade 4 and grade 3 hallux rigidus with <50% of the metatarsal head cartilage intact are best treated with arthrodesis.

*Raikin and Ahmad compared the outcomes of arthrodesis or metallic hemiarthroplasty for the treatment of hallux rigidus in 46 patients.* **They reported** a 24% failure rate and a 38% plantar stem cut out in the hemiarthroplasty group whereas the arthrodesis group achieved a 100% fusion rate without any need for revisions. **The authors concluded** arthroplasty is more reliable for alleviating pain symptoms associated with hallux rigidus with a lower failure and revision rate.

Figure A is an AP and lateral radiograph of the left foot demonstrating severe joint space narrowing and subchondral sclerosis of the 1st metatarsophalangeal joint, consistent with grade 4 hallux rigidus.

Incorrect answers
Answer 2: A Moberg osteotomy, or a phalangeal dorsiflexion osteotomy, is a treatment option for patients with early-stage hallux rigidus and decreased dorsiflexion. Ideally, this is performed for runners or patients that have failed a cheilectomy.
Answer 3: A Lapidus procedure, or a tarsometatarsal (TMT) arthrodesis, is a treatment option for patients with severe hallux valgus, hypermobile TMT joint, or arthritic TMT joint.
Answer 4: A cheilectomy is a treatment choice for early-stage with decreased dorsiflexion and pain over the dorsum of the 1st MTP joint.
Answer 5: A dorsal cheilectomy with metatarsal osteotomy would allow for improved dorsiflexion of the MTP joint with a reduction in pain for patients with early-stage disease.
