## Question

A 49-year-old-male that is a current pack-per-day smoker underwent a lumbar L3-S1 posterior spinal fusion for severe multilevel spinal stenosis. The patient has a history of diabetes mellitus and a body mass index (BMI) of 37. Perioperatively the patient received 900 mg of clindamycin every 8 hours, which continued for 24 hours post-operatively. Intraoperatively two drains were placed, one subfascial and one suprafascial. Post-operatively there was a large volume of drainage that necessitated drains for 5 days before removal. At the patient's 5-week post-operative follow-up he was found to have a surgical site infection. Which factor is most related to developing the surgical site infection?

## Options

1. Smoking
2. Subfascial drain placement
3. Vancomycin powder applied to wound at the time of closure
4. Wound drains present beyond perioperative antibiotic coverage
5. Increased drain output

## Correct option

**1. Smoking**

## Answer explanation

Smoking has been identified as a significant risk factor for the development of surgical site infections post-operatively in spine surgery. None of the other variables have been shown to independently contribute.

Surgical site infections in spine surgery are devastating complications. Risk factors for developing surgical site infections include smoking, diabetes, increased patient age, increased blood loss, revision spine surgery, and an increased number of levels fused. Postoperatively, drains are often placed to prevent the development of an epidural hematoma. Despite the drains acting as a potential nidus for infection in the post-operative setting, expanding antibiotic coverage beyond the standard 24-hour period has not been shown to decrease the incidence of surgical site infections.

*Brown et al. performed a prospective randomized control trial comparing patients with or without placement of wound drains after undergoing extensive lumbar spine surgery.* **The authors found** no differences with respect to infections, hematomas, or neurologic changes in patients with or without drains present. The only statistically significant difference that was observed between groups was increased temperature in patients that did not have a drain placed. **The authors concluded** that the use of a lumbar drain does not appear to have a treatment benefit and the decision to use one should be left to the surgeon's discretion.

*Takemoto et al. performed a randomized comparative study of patients undergoing multilevel thoracolumbar spinal surgery with surgical drains placed and received either a standard 24-hour antibiotic prophylaxis or antibiotics until the wound drain was pulled.* **The authors found** no differences in infection rates between patients that received either 24-hours of prophylaxis or until the drain was pulled. The authors did find that infections were significantly correlated with increasing age, smoking, alcohol use, and diabetes. **They concluded** that perioperative antibiotic prophylaxis should be discontinued after 24 hours, regardless if there is a drain placed.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 2: Subfascial drain placement has not been associated with the development of deep infections.
Answer 3: Vancomycin powder applied to the wound at the time of closure has been shown to decrease infection rates in retrospective studies, but have minimal to no effect in randomized controlled trials. Additionally, there does appear to be a decrease infection rate with gram-positive organisms and increased infection with gram-negative organisms with the use of vancomycin powder.
Answer 4: Prolonged antibiotic prophylaxis for the presence of surgical wound drains has not been shown to decrease surgical site infections.
Answer 5: Increased drain output has not been associated with the development of surgical site infections.
