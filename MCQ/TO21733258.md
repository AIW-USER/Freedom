Chapter: Spine MCQs — Spinal Infections

## Question

The commonest fungal infection of the spine is

## Options

1. Aspergillosis
2. Candida
3. Cryptococcus
4. Blastomyces

## Correct option

**1. Aspergillosis**

## Answer explanation

Ans: A
EXPLANATION: -
Fungal infections of the spine are relatively uncommon, nevertheless, troublesome.
Candida and aspergillus are the commonest sources. They are normal commensals of the body and produce disease in susceptible organisms when they gain access to the vascular system through intravenous lines, during implantation of prosthetic devices, or during surgery. For the other fungi, spinal involvement usually is the result of hematogenous or direct spread of organisms from an initial pulmonary source of infection. An increment in cases was specifically observed during the COVID pandemic likely owing to steroid overuse.

Common pathogens involved: Aspergillosis, Candida, Cryptococcus, Coccidis immitis, Blastomyces (last two are mainly limited to some geographical regions).

Fungal infections of the spine range from simple discitis to severe complicated vertebral osteomyelitis. Involvement of the vertebral bodies can lead to pathological vertebral compression fractures. Spread of infection along the anterior longitudinal ligament can lead to psoas or paravertebral abscesses. These infections reported are common in males involving the lumbar spine commonly. Non invasive diagnostic tests are unavailable in such cases. Diagnosis has to be supplemented by CT guided biopsy and histopathological examination. Fungal culture is confirmatory.
Treatment relies on the prompt institution of appropriate antifungal pharmacotherapy (amphotericin, azoles etc). Resistance to medical therapy, spinal instability, and neurologic deficits are indications for debridement and stabilization with spinal fusion.
Prognosis depends on the premorbid state of the patient, the type of fungal organism, and the timing of treatment.
