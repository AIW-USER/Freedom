## Options

1. Ganglion cyst
2. Abscess
3. Hematoma
4. Giant cell tumor of tendon sheath
5. Synovial sarcoma

## Correct option

**4. Giant cell tumor of tendon sheath**

## Answer explanation

Giant cell tumor of the tendon sheath is the most common solid soft-tissue mass in the hand. These tumors are slow-growing and may be present for months or years before coming to medical attention. Patients usually report mechanical difficulties because of the size or position of the tumor. The gross appearance is that of a lobulated mass that may be multicolored; typically yellow, brown, red, and gray. Histologically the lesion consists of multinucleated giant cells, polygonal mononuclear cells, and histiocytes that may contain abundant hemosiderin or lipid.
