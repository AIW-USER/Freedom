Chapter: Arthroplasty MCQs — Reverse Shoulder Arthroplasty

## Question

Most common mechanism for RTSA dislocation is

## Options

1. Abduction and external rotation
2. Adduction and internal rotation
3. Extension and external rotation
4. Flexion and internal rotation

## Correct option

**2. Adduction and internal rotation**

## Answer explanation

Regarding complications of Reverse Shoulder arthroplasty (RSA), complication rate averages 15% overall. Scapular notching > Instability > Acromion/ Scapular spine fractures > Infection > Axillary nerve neuropraxia (more with antero superior approach) are commonest early complications while loosening (glenoid component) is commonest long term complication.

Dislocation (resulting from instability) is one of the most common cause of early failure of RSA (semiconstrained Grammont-style prosthesis; Paul Grammount, 1985). Incidence is 3.4% average (2-14% as per different studies, as there is no universal definition of dislocation/ instability owing to low number of cases). Dislocation exclusively occurs in the anterior and anterolateral direction.

Patients with RSA don’t dislocate with the arm in abduction and external rotation like a normal shoulder. They typically dislocate with the arm in internal rotation and adduction in conjunction with extension (hyperextension) as in trying to get up from a chair or tucking shirt in and hence these movements are avoided in post op rehab protocols for up to 8-12 weeks.

Factors that can influence the degree of stability of RSA are the soft tissue balance (humeral head length), glenosphere size, the inclination of the humeral articular joint line, the version of the humeral component and the position of the metaglene (glenoid base plate).

RISK FACTORS FOR DISLOCATION: -

Biomechanically, lack of compressive forces (inadequate humeral head length) followed by shallow socket depth are the main parameters associated with instability. Impingement of either bone or soft-tissue structures may also contribute to dislocation. Use of deltopectoral as opposed to superolateral approach and poor subscapularis muscle (having higher than Goutallier grade 3 fatty infiltration) have also been associated with increased risk of instability. Some important risk factors are as follows:

· Inadequate subscapularis repair (most important if deltopectoral approach was used instead of Supero-lateral)

· failed prior arthroplasty attempt

· proximal humeral fracture with nonunion

· humeral bone loss

Treatment: CR can be tried but redislocations usually occur as errors are mostly surgical. Open reduction and correction of error are the standard solution.

 Answer- B
