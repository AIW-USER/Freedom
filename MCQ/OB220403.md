## Question

A 10-year-old prepubescent boy presents after a non-contact injury while playing soccer. He reports a “pop” and immediate swelling in the knee. MRI confirms a complete ACL tear without associated physeal injury. Due to the patient’s skeletal immaturity and high activity level, surgical reconstruction is planned. The decision is made to perform a physeal-sparing ACL reconstruction using an iliotibial band (ITB) autograft. Which of the following steps best describes the critical anatomical principle of this surgical technique?

## Options

1. Central portion of the iliotibial band is passed through a transphyseal tunnel into the tibial epiphysis
2. Graft is fixed with interference screws in both femur and tibia
3. Iliotibial band graft is passed over-the-top of the lateral femoral condyle and under the anterior intermeniscal ligament
4. Patellar tendon autograft is used and secured in the epiphysis
5. Tunnel placement is centered through both femoral and tibial physes to optimize anatomic reconstruction

## Correct option

**3. Iliotibial band graft is passed over-the-top of the lateral femoral condyle and under the anterior intermeniscal ligament**

## Answer explanation

Answer choice 3 describes the core step of the physeal-sparing ACL reconstruction technique for skeletally immature patients, avoiding growth plates while restoring stability.

In skeletally immature patients, preserving the growth plates during ACL reconstruction is essential to prevent growth disturbances. The physeal-sparing "over-the-top" technique using the iliotibial band avoids drilling tunnels through the tibial or femoral physes. Instead, the graft is routed around the lateral femoral condyle (the “over-the-top” position), into the joint, passed beneath the anterior intermeniscal ligament, and then anchored to the anteromedial tibial periosteum—all outside of the physis. This technique provides effective restoration of stability while minimizing the risk of limb length discrepancy or angular deformity.

*Kocher et al., 2005 & 2006: These foundational studies describe the development and technical details of physeal-sparing ACL reconstruction using an IT band autograft in skeletally immature patients.* The technique avoids disturbing the physes by utilizing an “over-the-top” femoral position and tibial periosteal fixation, achieving good outcomes while minimizing risk to growth plates.

*Kocher et al., 2018: This outcomes study followed a cohort of skeletally immature patients who underwent physeal-sparing ACL reconstruction and* **found** excellent knee stability, return to sport, and low rates of growth disturbance, further supporting the procedure as a gold standard in this population.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Transphyseal tunnels risk physeal damage and are not used in physeal-sparing techniques.
Answer 2: Interference screw fixation would involve physeal violation, which is avoided in skeletally immature patients.
Answer 4: Patellar tendon autografts are not used in skeletally immature patients due to the risk of damaging the tibial tubercle apophysis.
Answer 5: Centering tunnels through both physes would defeat the purpose of a physeal-sparing technique and increase the risk of growth disturbance.
