## Question

A 65-year-old woman complains of thigh pain 3 years after total hip arthroplasty with a metal on metal bearing surface. She has a remote history of adenocarcinoma treated with bowel resection 10 years ago. Radiographs are shown in Figures A and B. A bone scan, chest radiograph and CT of the chest, abdomen and pelvis, and laboratory investigations were all negative for other lesions. What is the next best step?

## Options

1. Lesion curettage, adjuvent treatment with phenol, and plate fixation
2. Percutaneous biopsy
3. Observation
4. Chemotherapy
5. Plate fixation and radiotherapy

## Correct option

**2. Percutaneous biopsy**

## Answer explanation

A solitary lesion in the absence of known metastatic disease should be biopsied for histological diagnosis prior to treatment.

Patients with history of cancer and a solitary lesion should not be assumed to have metastatic disease until histological diagnosis has been obtained. The most common primary lesions to metastasize to bone are thyroid (longest survival), prostate, breast, kidney and lung (shortest survival). The Mirel's scoring system helps determine the risk for pathological fracture. Another scoring system is the Harrington criteria (cortical destruction >50%, proximal femoral lesion >2.5cm, pathological avulsion of the lesser trochanter, persistent pain despite radiation therapy).

*Weber et al. retrospectively reviewed MSK metastases to the lower extremity at 3 institutions.* The most common source was breast cancer, and the most common location was the femur. Of 225 lesions, 168 were treated surgically with internal fixation (96) and prosthetic reconstruction (72). Patients who did not have surgery died at a mean of 14.2 months, and those who had surgery died at 11.5 months. **They advocate** PMMA and adjuvant radiation in the majority of patients to supplement fixation and improve local disease control.

*Bickels et al. review the management of metastatic bone disease.* **They recommend** an algorithm for patients with aggressive bone lesions and a history of cancer that includes bone scan, followed by CT/XR for all detected lesions, and biopsy of the most accessible lesion.

Figures A and B are AP and lateral radiographs showing solitary lytic femoral shaft metastasis distal to the THA stem. Illustration A shows recommended treatment modalities for metastasis to the lower limb.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Cement-augmented plate fixation is suitable for metastatic lesions as prophylaxis against fracture. Overlap with the femoral stem will prevent a stress riser at the plate-stem junction.
Answer 3: Observation is inappropriate as the lesion is likely to be a malignant process that will lead to significant morbidity and mortality if left untreated.
Answer 4: Chemotherapy is not delivered empirically. Targeted chemotherapy can only be administered once a histological diagnosis is obtained.
Answer 5: Prophylactic fixation is secondary. Achieving a histological diagnosis should be the first priority.
