## Question

A 65-year-old female who previously underwent left total hip arthroplasty now presents with increasing pain, swelling, and warmth to her operative hip. In the setting of which additional details would debridement, antibiotics, and implant retention (DAIR) most likely be an appropriate treatment?

## Options

1. Three weeks after surgery with synovial fluid white blood cell count of 500/µL with 37% PMNs
2. Three weeks after surgery with synovial fluid white blood cell count of 10,700/µL with 91% PMNs
3. Six months after surgery with synovial fluid white blood cell count of 4,250/µL with 82% PMNs
4. One year after surgery with three months of symptoms with synovial fluid white blood cell count of 49,500/µL with 86% PMNs
5. One year after surgery with two months of symptoms with synovial fluid white blood cell count of 3,900/µL with 91% PMNs

## Correct option

**2. Three weeks after surgery with synovial fluid white blood cell count of 10,700/µL with 91% PMNs**

## Answer explanation

Debridement, antibiotics, and implant retention (DAIR) is an option for acute periprosthetic joint infections (PJI) of the hip and knee.

Infection rates after total hip arthroplasty (THA) and total knee arthroplasty (TKA) are estimated to be 1.7% and 2.5%, respectively. The chronicity of the infection determines management. If the patient has clinical symptoms of an infection within 4 weeks of surgery, it is considered to be acute. A biofilm has not had time to be created, so the treatment is debridement and irrigation, polyethylene exchange with prosthetic retention. If the patient begins to have clinical and objective signs of infection >4 weeks after surgery, the infection is now considered to be chronic. There is a very high likelihood of a biofilm on the implants and a two-stage revision is required.

*Chotanaphuti et al. summarized consensus recommendations on the treatment of PJI of the hip and knee.* **They recommended** DAIR as a reasonable surgery for acute PJI. They also discussed postoperative drainage, prevention strategies, and operative treatment of drainage lasting greater than 7 days postoperatively.

*Barros et al. performed a case-control study of 38 cases that underwent DAIR.* **They found** an 89.2% success rate. **They concluded** that DAIR for acute PJI is safe and effective.

*Parvizi et al. updated their definition of PJI in 2018.* *They kept the major criteria for a chronic periprosthetic joint infection the same (an infection is indicated when 2 positive cultures of the same organism are isolated, or a sinus tract is present), but made several changes to the minor criteria.* Namely, they assign a point criteria in which a score of ≥6 is reliably infected, 2-5 is inconclusive, and 0-1 is not infected. In the case of infection, their recommendation is to proceed with a two-stage revision. If an inconclusive score is met, the recommendation is to proceed to the OR for histologic examination, cultures, and to determine whether purulence is present. The update to the MSIS criteria provides a sensitivity of 97.7% and a specificity of 99.5%.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: This scenario is non-diagnostic for infection and would be best treated with observation.
Answer 3-5: Each of these scenarios is consistent with a chronic PJI, which would be best treated with a two-stage revision versus a single-stage all-component revision.
