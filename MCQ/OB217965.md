## Question

A 14-year-old male patient with right spastic hemiplegic cerebral palsy presents with right forearm pronation and wrist flexion spasticity. He has tried braces and botulinum toxin A injections, but the pronation persists while running and grasp is limited by wrist flexion. Examination demonstrates a 35 degree wrist flexion contracture. When discussing surgical treatment options, which of the following procedures would provide the patient with the best restoration of forearm supination?

## Options

1. Pronator teres to flexor carpi ulnaris transfer
2. Flexor carpi ulnaris to extensor carpi radialis brevis transfer
3. Pronator teres rerouting
4. Brachioradialis rerouting
5. Wrist and DRUJ arthrodesis in neutral forearm rotation

## Correct option

**2. Flexor carpi ulnaris to extensor carpi radialis brevis transfer**

## Answer explanation

A flexor carpi ulnaris (FCU) to extensor carpi radialis brevis (ECRB) transfer, also called a Green transfer, is the most effective transfer for restoring forearm supination in cerebral palsy patients.

Cerebral palsy is the result of an injury to the premature brain that affects muscle tone. The manifestation and severity can vary widely. Spasticity of the upper extremities can be detrimental to the patient's quality of life and ability to perform daily functions. Forearm pronation deformities are common due to the overpowering effect of the pronator teres. Surgical treatment with an FCU to ECRB transfer can effectively restore dynamic forearm supination in these patients.

*Cheema et al. performed a biomechanical comparison of three tendon transfer techniques for forearm supination in ten cadaver specimens.* **They reported** the (FCU) to (ECRB) reached neutral rotation with 9 N of force and reached 84 degrees of supination with 36 N of force. **They concluded** the FCU to ECRB is biomechanically superior to pronator teres rerouting and brachioradialis rerouting.

*Patterson et al. retrospectively reviewed 25 cerebral palsy patients that underwent an FCU to ECRB tendon transfer to assess for late deformities.* **They reported** 12/25 patients developed late deformities with 8 patients developing extension deformities and 9 patients requiring revision surgery. **They concluded** FCU to ECRB transfer is effective for cerebral palsy patients, but care must be taken in treating patients less than 13 years due to the risk of late deformity.

*de Roode et al. reviewed the indications, preoperative evaluation, and surgical technique for tendon transfers and releases in spastic hemiplegic cerebral palsy.* **They stated** hemiplegic cerebral palsy affects one side of the body and initial treatment consists of bracing and botulinum toxin A injections. Further treatment consists of surgery for tendon transfers or arthrodesis.

Incorrect answers
Answer 1: A pronator teres to FCU transfer would increase forearm pronation and wrist ulnar deviation.
Answers 3 and 4: Pronator teres and brachioradialis rerouting can improve forearm supination in cerebral palsy patients, but biomechanically the FCU to ECRB transfer is more effective.
Answer 5: An arthrodesis can be performed if initial treatment fails or for severe deformity.
