## Question

A 53-year-old man presents after lacerating his right arm with a machete while cutting brush. He presents to the emergency department with a large, hemostatic wound just distal to the elbow. He has full strength with palmar abduction, but weakness with index finger abduction. There is dense numbness to pinprick at the small finger. The patient is concerned about his ability to return to work. Which of the following procedures will provide the patient with the best chance of early recovery of hand function?

## Options

1. Median nerve repair
2. Median nerve repair with nerve transfer of the branch to brachialis to the anterior interosseous nerve
3. Ulnar nerve repair
4. Ulnar nerve repair with end-to-side transfer of the anterior interosseous nerve to the deep motor branch of ulnar nerve
5. Ulnar nerve repair with end-to-end transfer of the anterior interosseous nerve to the deep motor branch of ulnar nerve

## Correct option

**4. Ulnar nerve repair with end-to-side transfer of the anterior interosseous nerve to the deep motor branch of ulnar nerve**

## Answer explanation

This patient presents with a laceration at the proximal forearm, which resulted in ulnar nerve transection. Ulnar nerve repair with additional supercharged end-to-side (SETS) transfer of the anterior interosseous nerve (AIN) to the deep branch of the ulnar nerve provides the patient the best chance of regaining early intrinsic hand motor function (Answer 4).

Ulnar nerve injuries are the most common upper extremity peripheral nerve injury, and most commonly occur in working-age males. Patients with proximal ulnar nerve injury present with complete loss of intrinsic hand motor function, as well as sensory deficits in the ulnar distribution. High ulnar nerve injuries have been reported to have poor outcomes regarding hand intrinsic muscle recovery due to the immediate initiation of motor end plate degeneration. In ulnar nerve injuries associated with an open wound, acute primary ulnar nerve repair is indicated. Additionally, for lacerations in the proximal forearm or higher, the addition of SETS transfer from AIN to the deep branch of the ulnar nerve maximizes recovery of the intrinsic motor function of the hand.

Susan Mackinnon presents a review article on upper extremity nerve transfers. She describes the requirements, considerations, and surgical techniques for several types of nerve transfers. She recommends the supercharged end-to-side technique for AIN to the deep branch of ulnar nerve transfers when treating ulnar nerve injuries.

*Moore et al. review various surgical options for treating radial, median, and ulnar nerve injuries in the*
upper extremity. It is discussed that recovery of intrinsic motor function of the hand is rare in high-level ulnar nerve injuries, even with immediate repair, due to the long distance required for regeneration. They recommend using the AIN transfer to the motor branch of the ulnar nerve to improve early pinch and grip strength and decrease the risk of clawing.

*Woo et al. review the management of ulnar nerve injuries and discuss the relevant anatomy, clinical findings, diagnosis, and treatment options.* In open acute ulnar nerve injuries, immediate exploration and primary neuroplasty are recommended. Because high ulnar nerve injuries have traditionally been associated with poor outcomes concerning intrinsic muscle recovery, **they suggest** AIN transfer to the ulnar motor branch. Recent reports indicate that functional recovery has been observed in 70 to 100 percent of patients.

*Koriem et al. conducted a randomized controlled trial involving a total of 21 patients who sustained acute proximal ulnar nerve transection injuries located up to 10 cm distal to the elbow.* *The patients were randomized to receive either isolated ulnar nerve repair or ulnar nerve repair with SETS transfer of the AIN.* *It was found that patients who underwent ulnar nerve repair with SETS transfer of the AIN demonstrated better intrinsic muscle reinnervation and correction of claw deformity after an 18-month follow-up period.* **The authors proposed** that the use of SETS transfer of the AIN should be added to primary ulnar nerve repair in the treatment of proximal ulnar nerve injuries.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1 & 2: The median nerve is not injured in this case; therefore, median nerve repair is not indicated.
Answer 3: There is a high risk of intrinsic motor function loss in cases of proximal ulnar nerve injuries. Therefore, a SETS transfer of the terminal branch of the AIN is indicated to improve hand function.
Answer 5: SETS transfer of the terminal branch of the AIN allows for concomitant primary ulnar nerve repair in addition to improving reinnervation of the distal intrinsic musculature, and is thus superior to end-to-end transfer, which requires a surgical division of the deep branch of the ulnar nerve.
