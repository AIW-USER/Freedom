## Question

An 11-year-old is brought in to the urgent care with pain and swelling over his shin after being kicked by one of his classmates in his special education class. His presenting radiograph is shown in Figure A. He is able to bear weight on the extremity and has no deficits on exam, but he is noted to have numerous broad-based, brown macules on the extremities, wears glasses, and is being followed for an expansile lesion in his mandible. Which of the following conditions does the patient most likely have?

![[q219481-a-screenshot-2024-02-23-at-4-42-12-am.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Jaffe-Campanacci syndrome
2. Jaffe-Lichenstein syndrome
3. Maffucci syndrome
4. Mazabraud syndrome
5. McCune Albright syndrome

## Correct option

**1. Jaffe-Campanacci syndrome**

## Answer explanation

This patient has a large non-ossifying fibroma and clinical evidence of cognitive delay, cafe-au-lait spots, ocular defects, and an expansile lesion in the mandible representing a mandibular giant cell tumor, the constellation of which is representative of Jaffe-Campanacci syndrome.

Non-ossifying fibromas are benign fibrogenic lesions that result from dysfunctional ossification and are most commonly found in the metaphysis of long bones. The most common age of presentation is between ages 5 and 15 with an asymptomatic lesion discovered incidentally on radiographs. Radiographs characteristically demonstrate "bubbly," lytic lesions surrounded by a sclerotic rim. Important associated conditions to rule out include Neurofibromatosis and Jaffe-Campanacci syndrome, which represents a congenital syndrome of multiple NOFs, cognitive delay, cafe-au-lait pigmentation, giant cell lesions of the mandible, and associated cardiac, ocular, and gonadal defects.

*Mankin et al. provide a biologic and clinical review of non-ossifying fibroma, fibrous cortical defect, and Jaffe–Campanacci syndrome.* **The authors note** that non-ossifying fibroma of bone (NOF) is a common entity, more frequently found in male children and consisting of a solitary eccentric, lytic expanded lesion in the metaphysis of a long bone. **They conclude** that it is important to distinguish NOF from another very similar but smaller lesion, fibrous cortical defect, which is almost always asymptomatic and eccentrically located, as well as a very rarely encountered lesion known as Jaffe–Campanacci syndrome, which also occurs in children who present with typical non-ossifying fibromatous tumors but in multiple sites and must be distinguished from patients with Type 1 neurofibromatosis.

*Jamshidi et al. published a comparison of the characteristic features and local recurrence in syndromic versus non-syndromic multifocal non-ossifying fibroma (MNOF).* *The authors reviewed 35 patients with MNOF, including those without cafe-au-lait spots (non-syndromic) and those with cafe-au-lait spots (syndromic).* **They found** that after curettage and bone grafting, the lesion recurred in 6.7% of non-syndromic MNOFs and 60% of the syndromic MNOFs. **The authors concluded** that the syndromic form of MNOF is much less prevalent and is associated with a higher rate of recurrence after surgical removal; therefore, a more rigorous removal of the MNOF lesions might be necessary when presenting in a syndromic context.

Figure A is a lateral radiograph of the ankle demonstrating a "bubbly," lytic lesion in the metaphyseal distal tibia consistent with a non-ossifying fibroma (NOF).

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Jaffe-Lichenstein syndrome represents a constellation of polyostotic fibrous dysplasia (not NOF) and cafe-au-lait spot, and can be distinguished from McCune-Albright syndrome by a lack of multiple endocrinopathies.
Answer 3: Maffucci syndrome involves the presence of multiple enchondromas and associated vascular lesions/malformations (typically soft tissue angiomas).
Answer 4: Mazabraud syndrome involves a form of polyostotic fibrous dysplasia associated with the presence of multiple intra-muscular myxomas.
Answer 5: McCune Albright syndrome is a form of syndromic polyostotic fibrous dysplasia associated with cafe-au-lait macules in jagged, "coast of Maine" formation, and multiple endocrine abnormalities, typically exhibiting precocious puberty with autonomous testosterone production, excess growth hormone, and thyroid dysregulation.
