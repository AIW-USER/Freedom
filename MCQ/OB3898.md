## Options

1. Calcaneal stress fracture
2. Osteomyelitis
3. Tarsal coalition
4. Sever's disease
5. Kohler's disease

## Correct option

**4. Sever's disease**

## Answer explanation

This patient's clinical examination and imaging is consistent with calcaneal apophysitis or Sever's disease.

Calcaneal apophysitis, or Sever's disease, most commonly occurs in boys aged 12 or girls aged 11. It is often seen in athletes participating in running and jumping sports and may be due to traction apophysitis and repetitive microtrauma. Pain is localized to the heel and compression of the calcaneus may cause pain. It is usually self-limited and resolves with conservative treatment. Radiographs may show sclerosis and fragmentation of the apophysis. MRI can be useful to rule out other causes of heel pain such as osteomyelitis, stress fractures, or lytic lesions.

*Micheli et al. performed a review of 85 children and 137 feet with calcaneal apophysitis.* **They found** that both heels were affected in 61% of patients. Sixty-eight patients reported that their pain was made worse by a specific sport, most commonly soccer. All patients were treated with physical therapy and stretching of the heel cords. Soft orthotics or heel cups were used in 98% of patients. **They concluded** that all patients improved and were able to return to their sport of choice within 2 months of diagnosis with no recurrences.

*Frank et al. performed a review of lower extremity injuries in the skeletally immature athlete.* **They report** that Sever's disease is an overuse injury caused by repetitive microtrauma to the trabecular metaphyseal bone of the calcaneus and is not inflammation. **They conclude** that the pathology is also related to the adolescent growth spurt and weak ankle dorsiflexors with a tight gastrocnemius-soleus complex.

Figures A and B are AP and lateral radiographs, respectively, demonstrating increased density of the calcaneal apophysis with fragmentation. Figure C is a T1-weighted sagittal cut demonstrating signal loss in the calcaneal apophysis. Figure D is a STIR coronal cut demonstrating a hyperintense signal in the calcaneal apophysis.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: In calcaneal stress fractures, a fracture line is visualized on the MRI and at times on plain radiographs.
Answer 2: Osteomyelitis usually presents with signs and symptoms of infection. Early radiographs may be normal, while MRI exhibits diffuse marrow and soft tissue edema.
Answer 3: Tarsal coalitions present with recurrent ankle sprains. Coalitions may be seen on radiographs or MRI.
Answer 5: Kohler's disease is avascular necrosis of the navicular and presents with pain in the dorsomedial midfoot. Radiographs demonstrate sclerosis, fragmentation, and flattening of the navicular bone.
