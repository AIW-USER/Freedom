## Question

A 63-year-old male with history of several invasive knee procedures including an open meniscectomy performed many years ago presents to your office for follow up. You performed a right total knee arthroplasty (TKA) approximately 4 weeks ago. Postoperatively, the patient developed an area of skin necrosis over the distal medial portion of your midline TKA incision. You elect to perform a rotational flap for coverage as shown in Figure A. Which of the following is the arterial supply for this flap?

![[q218012-a-pmfaam17-gastrocnemius-part7.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Inferomedial geniculate artery
2. Recurrent branch of the anterior tibial artery
3. Profunda femoris artery
4. Medial sural artery
5. Descending branch of the femoral circumflex

## Correct option

**4. Medial sural artery**

## Answer explanation

Medial gastrocnemius rotational flaps are an excellent option for distal medial full thickness skin necrosis following TKA and are supplied by the medial sural artery.

Incisional planning for revision knee surgery is of paramount importance. The superficial blood supply to the anterior knee is primarily derived from the medial geniculate arteries, and thus the most lateral incision should be selected whenever possible. If skin necrosis does occur between incisions, medial and lateral rotational gastrocnemius flaps are an excellent option for coverage. The medial gastrocnemius rotational flap is supplied by the medial sural artery and is best for medial and anterior coverage, whereas the lateral gastrocnemius flap is supplied by the lateral sural artery and is better for laterally based defects.

*Markovich et al review muscle flaps in total knee arthroplasty.* **They report** that muscle flaps for TKA can be classified into 3 types - (1) prophylactic soft tissue coverage, done before definitive reconstruction; (2) treating muscle flap for infected prostheses with deficient soft tissue coverage; and (3) salvage muscle flap for wound dehiscence or necrosis in the immediate postoperative period. **They conclude** that "treating" muscle flaps are less likely to lead to a successful outcome, however, they did have 100% revascularization of the incisions in their cohort of 12 patients.

*Walton et al review the surgical technique for medial and lateral gastrocnemius flaps.* **They report** that the medial gastrocnemius rotational flap is supplied by the medial sural artery and that the lateral gastrocnemius rotational flap is supplied by the lateral sural artery. **They conclude** that these flaps have low failure rates and a distinct proximally based blood supply that allows them to be elevated and rotated up to 15 cm above the level of the knee joint.

Figure A is a clinical image of a surgeon performing a medial gastrocnemius rotational flap.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The inferomedial geniculate artery supplies the inferomedial structures of the knee including the medial tibial plateau and participates in the supply of the patella.
Answer 2: The recurrent branch of the anterior tibial artery participates in the patellar anastomosis complex but does not supply the gastrocnemius muscle.
Answer 3: Profunda femoris is much more proximal and one of the main blood supplies to the thigh musculature.
Answer 5: The descending branch of the femoral circumflex extends from the femoral neck and travels down to join the patellar anastomosis complex but does not extend distal to the knee.
