## Question

A 28-year-old male sustains extensive injuries to his right leg after an improvised explosive device detonates near him while on deployment. After the patient is stabilized and returns stateside, you take over his care. You have a thorough discussion with the patient on treatment options to include limb-salvage and amputation. If this patient were to undergo amputation compared to limb salvage, he would likely have a higher rate of what?

## Options

1. Depressive symptoms
2. Pain interfering with daily activity
3. Post-traumatic stress disorder
4. Return to vigorous sports
5. Return to work and/or school

## Correct option

**4. Return to vigorous sports**

## Answer explanation

According to the Military Extremity Trauma Amputation/Limb Salvage (METALS) study, patients that undergo amputation have a higher return to vigorous sport sports compared to limb salvage patients.

Catastrophic injuries to extremities can be very challenging injuries for an orthopedic surgeon to manage. The decision between limb salvage and amputation can be difficult and can rely on injury characteristics and resources available. Treatment also extends beyond surgical intervention involving a close relationship between rehab physicians, prosthetists, physical therapists, psychiatrists, and social workers.

*Doukas et al. published the METALS study, which was a retrospective cohort study of 324 military service members who sustained a lower extremity requiring either amputation or limb salvage.* *Both groups did significantly worse than population norms for Short Musculoskeletal Form Assessment (SFMA) questionnaire, however, the amputation group did better than limb salvage in domains.* **They found** amputation patients also had a lower likelihood of Post-traumatic stress disorder and a higher likelihood of a return to vigorous sport. One-third of patients were not working or in school and 38.4% screened positive for depression. There was no difference between cohorts for these two measures. One limitation of this study is that it involves military patients who have access to resources that may not be available to civilian patients, thus limiting the application of these findings to the general population.

*MacKenzie and Bosse published data from the Lower Extremity Assessment Project (LEAP), a multicenter study of severe lower extremity trauma in US civilians.* **They found** that in comparing amputation to limb salvage, outcomes were poor for both groups. An important conclusion by this study is that outcomes often are more affected by the patient’s economic, social, and personal resources than by the initial treatment of the injury. They offer a conceptual framework of the many factors that interplay in patient outcomes. Specifically, **they found** anxiety, pain, and depression as significant predictors of poor long-term outcomes.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Depressive symptoms are the same between limb salvage and amputation
Answer 2: Pain interfering with daily activity was not different between groups.
Answer 3: PTSD was lower in the amputation cohort compared to limb-salvage in the METALS study.
Answer 5: There was no difference in return to work and/or school at 2-years between treatment modalities.
