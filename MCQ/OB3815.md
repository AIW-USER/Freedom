## Options

1. Calcaneal osteotomy
2. Flexor hallucis longus tendon transfer
3. Flexor digitorum longus tendon transfer
4. Deltoid ligament reconstruction
5. Tendoachilles lengthening

## Correct option

**4. Deltoid ligament reconstruction**

## Answer explanation

All of the following soft tissue procedures play a role in the treatment of Stage II PTTI EXCEPT deltoid ligament reconstruction, which is only indicated for type IV PTTI.

Adult-acquired flatfoot deformity (AAFD) results from PTTI and is the result of combined hindfoot valgus and forefoot abduction. Clinically, patients are unable to perform a single heel raise and demonstrate a "too many toes" sign. Correction of the deformity in stage II PTTI is achieved through a combination of soft tissue and bony procedures. A common combination of procedures for stage II disease includes transfer of the flexor digitorum longus (FDL) or less commonly the flexor halluxis longus (FHL), tendoachilles lengthening (TAL), and a medial displacement calcaneal osteotomy.

*Pinney et. al. review the pathogenesis and treatment of adult-acquired flatfoot deformity.* **They report** that deltoid ligament reconstruction has been described for patients with tibiotalar valgus in stage IV PTTI. Alternatively, some authors have recommended tibiotalocalcaneal or pantalar arthrodesis. All treatment options currently have limited evidence to make specific recommendations for management.

*Myerson et. al. evaluated the efficacy of treating stage II PTTI with FDL transfer and medial displacement calcaneal osteotomy.* **They reported** the majority of patients had good reconstitution of the medial longitudinal arch (87%) and return to normal shoe wear without orthotics (84%). The procedure had high success rates and good patient satisfaction.

*Sands et. al. review the use of lateral column lengthening for the treatment of AAFD.* *They describe the goals of osteotomy are adducting the talonavicular joint, plantarflexing the midfoot, and de-rotating the calcaneus out of valgus.*

*Sammarco et. al. reviewed 19 patients with stage II PTTD treated with FHL transfer and medial displacing calcaneal osteotomy.* **They found** that the procedure was unable to reconstitute the medial longitudinal arch of the midfoot, but was associated with good clinical improvement and high patient satisfaction.
