## Question

A 55-year-old recreational tennis player presents to clinic after initial rehabilitation complaining primarily about pain and swelling along the posterior fibula. He states he sprained his ankle six months ago, and was treated with bracing and proprioceptive training. He notes multiple sprains in the past, but the pain from prior sprains was different and always resolved. After an MRI demonstrated a peroneus brevis tear he is taken to the operating room. During surgery you identify multiple longitudinal tears in the peroneus brevis tendon, and a 3 cm portion of the tendon with significant tendinosis in over 70% of the cross-sectional area. What is the appropriate surgical procedure?

## Options

1. Core repair and tubularization of the peroneus brevis tendon
2. Peroneal groove deepening
3. Excision of the diseased tendon without transfer
4. Excision of the diseased tendon with proximal and distal transfer to the peroneus longus
5. Arthroscopic debridement of the peroneus brevis

## Correct option

**4. Excision of the diseased tendon with proximal and distal transfer to the peroneus longus**

## Answer explanation

The patient in the question has a complex tear in the peroneus brevis tendon and the most appropriate treatment is a debridement of the tendon with tenodesis of distal and proximal ends of the brevis tendon to the peroneus longus.

Initial treatment for patients with tears in the peroneus brevis tendon can be non-operative, but surgical treatment is an excellent option when symptoms persist. Surgical options for patients with a tear in the peroneus brevis tendon are dependent on the complexity of the tear with simple tears being treated with core repair and tubularization of the peroneus brevis tendon, and complex tears being treated with either debridement of the tendon with tenodesis of distal and proximal ends of the brevis tendon to the peroneus longus or reconstruction with allograft.

*Philbin et al. published a review article on the treatment of peroneal tendon injuries and* **recommended** that once the tendon has multiple longitudinal tears and significant tendinosis (>50% of the tendon involved) the patient should be treated with debridement of the tendon with tenodesis of distal and proximal ends of the brevis tendon to the peroneus longus.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: A core repair is correct for a single longitudinal tear without significant tendinosis
Answer 2: This is the treatment for persistent peroneal tendon subluxation/dislocation.
Answer 3: While the diseased tendon may be resected, tenodesis of distal and proximal ends of the brevis tendon to the peroneus longus is recommended.
Answer 5: Arthroscopic treatment of this injury would not allow for transfer of the healthy remaining tendon to the peroneus longus
