## Question

Figures A and B are radiographs of a 64-year-old male with progressive right hip pain. He is now unable to put full weight on the affected leg. Laboratory studies show mildly elevated serum calcium, serum IgG = 3.9g/dL (normal = 0.7-1.6 g/dL) and IgA = 1.5 g/dL (normal = 0.07-0.40 g/dL), and ESR = 40 (normal <20). Tissue biopsy of the right hip is seen in Figure C. In addition to chemotherapy treatment, comprehensive care of this patient's osseous disease should include:

## Options

1. Wide marginal resection, joint reconstruction, bisphosphonates and hormone therapy
2. Currettage, bone graft and surgical stabilization
3. Surgical stabilization, radiation and bisphosphonates
4. Observation, radiation and bisphosphonates
5. Surgical stabilization and hormone therapy

## Correct option

**3. Surgical stabilization, radiation and bisphosphonates**

## Answer explanation

This patient presents with a lytic lesion of the right hip in the setting of multiple myeloma. The most appropriate treatment would be surgical stabilization, radiation and bisphosphonates.

Unless a patient has a known primary neoplasm with bone biopsy-proven skeletal metastasis, the treating surgeon should biopsy the lesion in question. With a confirmed diagnosis, the treatment of multiple myeloma involves radiotherapy, bisphosphonates +/- surgical stabilization. Surgical stabilization is utilized when there is a complete or impending fracture.

*Mirels proposed a scoring system for diagnosing impending fractures.* **He found** that risk of fracture was 33% at 6 months for a score of 9. **He proposed** that lesions with scores of 8 or higher require prophylactic internal fixation prior to irradiation.

*Scharschmidt et al. review the diagnosis and skeletal manifestations of multiple myeloma.* **They report** that IgG is the most common antibody detected in this disease. **They also state** that lytic bone disease is a hallmark of multiple myeloma, and a high percentage of patients develop pathologic fractures.

*Yeh et al. reviewed the treatment of multiple myeloma.* Radiation therapy has been a major component of antimyeloma therapy. In addition, bisphosphonate treatment should also be considered in patients with myeloma who have evidence of bone loss. New therapies targeting bone (e.g., monoclonal antibody to RANKL and inhibitors of cathepsin) may offer patients with myeloma additional therapeutic options.

Figure A shows an AP radiograph of the right hip with a peritrochanteric lytic lesion. This lesion is also demonstrated on the lateral view as seen in Figure B. Figure C shows round plasma cells with an eccentric nucleus, prominent nucleolus, and clock face organization of chromatin, which is indicative of multiple myeloma.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Wide marginal resection, joint reconstruction, bisphosphonates and hormone therapy would not be required in this patient. First, wide resection of multiple myeloma is never indicated, even solitary lesions. Secondly, hormone therapy is not an element of treatment.
Answer 2: Currettage and bone grafting would be considered in patients with a isolated lesion, such as giant cell tumour or aneurysmal bone cyst.
Answer 4: Observation, radiation and bisphosphonates would be appropriate if the Mirels score for this patient was less than 9. However, the lesion has a Mirels' score of 12 (functional pain = 3; peritrochantieric location = 3; > 2/3 in size= 3; lytic = 3). Therefore, this patient requires prophylactic internal fixation prior to irradiation.
Answer 5: Surgical stabilization and hormone therapy is not the treatment regime for this disease.
