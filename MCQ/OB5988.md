## Question

Figure 16 shows the radiograph of an otherwise healthy 62-year-old woman who fell. Management should consist of

![[q5988-a-16.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. revision total hip arthroplasty with a cemented femoral component and adjuvant fracture fixation.
2. revision total hip arthroplasty with a cementless femoral component and adjuvant fracture fixation.
3. open reduction and internal fixation of the fracture and retention of the original components.
4. removal of the components, open reduction and internal fixation of the fracture, and delayed replantation of the components when the fracture is healed.
5. resection arthroplasty and internal fixation of the fracture.

## Correct option

**2. revision total hip arthroplasty with a cementless femoral component and adjuvant fracture fixation.**

## Answer explanation

The radiograph reveals that the femoral component is grossly loose as evidenced by disruption of the cement column; therefore, retention of the original components will not yield a successful outcome. A cementless revision is the procedure of choice. A strut graft and/or plate may be added at the surgeon’s discretion. A resection arthroplasty would only be considered in a nonambulatory patient. Cemented fixation of the revision component would be problematic given the numerous fracture fragments and the inability to contain the cement.
