## Question

A 60-year-old male presents to your clinic with left hip pain that radiates through the anterior groin, down the medial thigh, and occasionally past the knee. Pain is worsened when going up stairs and walking uphill. Radiographs of his left hip demonstrate moderate osteoarthritis. He reports having tried physical therapy in the past without benefit and is eventually scheduled for total hip arthroplasty. Surgery is uneventful, but at his subsequent follow-up visits he complains of minimal pain improvement compared to preoperatively. Inflammatory markers have returned to normal and he is otherwise well-appearing. What is the next best step in management of this patient?

## Options

1. Imaging of the lumbar spine
2. Aspiration of hip joint
3. Electromyography (EMG) and nerve conduction velocity studies
4. Technetium (Tc-99) bone scan
5. CT of operative hip

## Correct option

**1. Imaging of the lumbar spine**

## Answer explanation

Spinal stenosis is often found concomitantly in patients with osteoarthritis of the hip and further lumbar imaging (radiographs and MRI) would be useful in identifying areas of stenosis or nerve impingement.

Degenerative spinal stenosis is a very common condition of the lumbar spine that is prevalent in the same population which has a high rate of hip osteoarthritis (OA). Patients with hip OA and concomitant spinal stenosis who undergo total hip arthroplasty (THA) typically show some improvement in hip pain compared to preoperatively, but to a lower degree than those without stenosis. They may continue to have limitations in function and activity level due to their lumbar stenosis, even after THA. Additionally, the presence of spinal stenosis has been suggested as a risk factor for nerve palsy in patients undergoing THA, given the existing nerve compression. There has been much debate regarding the order in which to address lumbar stenosis and hip osteoarthritis. Some surgeons suggest pain control and functional outcomes are better when lumbar stenosis is addressed first while others suggest higher rates of hip dislocation when the lumbar spine is addressed first.

*Jauregui et al. investigated the effects of lumbar stenosis on functional outcomes after primary THA.* **They noted** that both the stenosis and non-stenosis groups showed functional improvements after THA but the improvements in the stenosis group were less. **They concluded** by saying that those undergoing THA with concomitant lumbar stenosis can expect to have continued limitations and decreased satisfaction compared to those without stenosis.

*Eneqvist et al. evaluated the order of THA and lumbar spinal stenosis surgery as it relates to patient outcomes.* **They noted** that lumbar stenosis surgery before THA was correlated with higher functional improvements and pain control. They finish by saying that the order of surgery should be individualized, based on surgeon and patient discussion.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Aspiration of the hip joint is not immediately indicated in this patient with normal inflammatory markers and symptoms derived from the lumbar spine.
Answer 3: EMG and nerve conduction studies may be useful in identifying affected levels of nerve impingement, but imaging of the lumbar spine should be performed first to evaluate the severity of lumbar pathology.
Answer 4: Tc-99 bone scan would be used to identify loosening of the prosthesis, which there is low suspicion for in this patient given the fact his symptoms are similar preoperatively and postoperatively and likely not related to implant loosening.
Answer 5: CT of the operative hip could help identify occult fracture or deep fluid collection/hematoma which there is no obvious concern for as this patient does not seem to have an acute process occurring.
