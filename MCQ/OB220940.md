## Question

A 76-year-old man presents with progressive left hip pain that worsens with ambulation. Imaging demonstrates a permeative lytic lesion of the proximal femur with extensive marrow replacement, but no cortical breakthrough, as shown in Figure A. PET/CT demonstrates no other sites of disease. The patient is planned for prophylactic fixation followed by systemic chemotherapy. Which of the following pathologic findings most appropriately supports this management strategy?

![[q220940-a-screenshot-2025-12-29-at-5-12-24-pm.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Sheets of large atypical lymphoid cells that are CD20-positive, CD79a-positive, BCL-6–positive, and CD3-negative
2. Malignant spindle cells producing osteoid with marked nuclear pleomorphism and atypical mitotic figures
3. Lobules of malignant cartilage with permeative growth and binucleate chondrocytes
4. Irregular curvilinear trabeculae of immature woven bone within a fibrous stroma, associated with activating GNAS mutations
5. Sheets of small round blue cells with strong CD99 positivity and EWSR1 rearrangement

## Correct option

**1. Sheets of large atypical lymphoid cells that are CD20-positive, CD79a-positive, BCL-6–positive, and CD3-negative**

## Answer explanation

This patient presents with a destructive proximal femoral lesion, and biopsy findings consistent with primary bone lymphoma (Answer A) would be appropriately treated with systemic chemotherapy with or without radiotherapy and prophylactic fixation.

The proximal femur is a common site for primary lymphoma of bone (PLB), representing one of the most frequently affected long bones. Modern staging incorporates PET/CT imaging to define disease extent and guide management. Systemic chemotherapy remains the cornerstone of treatment, with anthracycline-based regimens combined with rituximab demonstrating improved outcomes compared with historical chemotherapy alone. The benefit of routine radiotherapy in the contemporary R-CHOP (Rituximab, Cyclophosphamide, Doxorubicin, Vincristine, Prednisone) era remains uncertain and is typically individualized. In this patient, the combination of advanced age, proximal femoral involvement, and extensive osseous destruction places him at high risk for impending pathologic fracture, and prophylactic fixation is indicated. Prophylactic stabilization of weight-bearing long bones in this setting is associated with reduced morbidity and improved functional outcomes compared with fixation after fracture, provided that surgery does not delay systemic therapy.

*Ramadan et al performed a retrospective study of 131 patients with primary bone lymphoma treated at the British Columbia Cancer Agency from 1983 to 2005.* **The authors found** that 79% had diffuse large B-cell lymphoma, with 5- and 10-year overall survival rates of 62% and 41%, respectively. Multivariate analysis identified three prognostic groups based on age and International Prognostic Index (IPI) score, with 5-year overall survival of 90% in patients younger than 60 years with IPI scores of 1–3, 61% in patients aged 60 years or older with IPI scores of 0–3, and 25% in patients aged 60 years or older with IPI scores of 4–5. The addition of rituximab to CHOP chemotherapy significantly improved 3-year progression-free survival to 88% compared with 52% for CHOP-like chemotherapy alone. Neither primary tumor location nor the presence of a pathologic fracture at presentation was associated with worse overall survival.

*Bruno Ventre et al assessed 161 patients with limited-stage (stage I–II) primary bone diffuse large B-cell lymphoma and* **demonstrated** that anthracycline-based chemotherapy, with or without radiotherapy, produced favorable outcomes, with 5-year progression-free survival of 68% and overall survival of 75%. Patients treated with primary chemotherapy had significantly better outcomes than those managed with primary radiotherapy, and the addition of consolidative radiotherapy after chemotherapy did not improve survival, nor did higher radiation doses or larger radiation fields. These findings support systemic chemotherapy as the foundation of treatment for limited-stage primary bone DLBCL, with no clear added benefit from routine consolidative radiotherapy.

*Lewis et al report a retrospective series of 28 adults with lymphoma of bone, reporting a 5-year overall survival of 57.8% with a median follow-up of 40 months, with improved survival seen in patients younger than 40 years and those with diffuse large B-cell histology.* *Most patients presented with early-stage disease, and local recurrence was uncommon, though osteonecrosis occurred in several patients after radiotherapy.* Survival did not differ significantly between patients with primary bone lymphoma and those with systemic lymphoma involving bone.

Figure A is an AP radiograph of the left hip with a permeative lytic lesion of the proximal femur, with intertrochanteric and subtrochanteric regions affected.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer B: Malignant spindle cells producing osteoid indicate osteosarcoma, which requires wide oncologic resection rather than chemotherapy-based treatment alone.
Answer C: Lobulated malignant cartilage with permeative growth is characteristic of chondrosarcoma, a tumor that is treated surgically because it is resistant to chemotherapy and radiation.
Answer D: Fibrous dysplasia is a benign fibroosseous process associated with GNAS mutation that is managed with observation and does not warrant systemic chemotherapy.
Answer E: Small round blue cells with CD99 positivity and an EWSR1 rearrangement indicate Ewing sarcoma, a predominantly pediatric/early adult malignancy requiring chemotherapy and surgical resection that is unlikely in this elderly patient.
