## Question

A 45-year-old female presents to your office with chronic right great toe pain. She is a former ballet dancer who has had multiple injuries and chronic pain involving both feet. She notes a previous fibular sesamoidectomy performed by an outside surgeon ten years ago to correct her right great toe pain. Since that procedure, she has noted a progressively worsening great toe deformity. On exam, she has a large blister on the medial aspect of her right great toe. Limited first metatarsophalangeal (MTP) joint range of motion and a deformity that is not passively correctable are present. Radiographs taken in the office show degenerative changes in the first MTP joint. What is the best treatment option for this patient?

## Options

1. Shoe modifications with a wider toe box and a pad for the medial aspect of the great toe
2. Custom splint with the great toe in a more neutral position for 3-4 weeks
3. First MTP arthrodesis
4. Transfer abductor hallucis tendon to the lateral base of the first proximal phalanx with a medial soft tissue release
5. Lateral closing wedge osteotomy

## Correct option

**3. First MTP arthrodesis**

## Answer explanation

The patient is a 45-year-old female with a chronic hallux varus deformity following a fibular sesamoidectomy performed 10 years ago. Since her radiographs demonstrate degenerative changes and she does not have a passively correctable deformity, a first MTP arthrodesis is indicated at this time (answer 3).

Hallux varus is a condition that is more commonly seen in women and is characterized by medial deviation of the hallux relative to the first metatarsal. This is most often (2-14%) due to overcorrection following procedures to correct hallux valgus deformities. This condition can also occur following a fibular sesamoidectomy, as the abductor hallucis longus tendon becomes unopposed and leads to medial tibial sesamoid translation and subsequently medial hallux translation. Diagnosis of this condition is made clinically, with close attention to stiffness and passive correction of the deformity. Plain radiographs of the foot and great toe can also be obtained for evaluation of degenerative changes in the first MTP joint and for an enhanced evaluation of the deformity. Treatment options consist of non-operative intervention with shoe modifications and tape/splinting. Operative intervention consists of correctional osteotomies and tendon transfers for earlier deformities and a first MTP arthrodesis if degenerative changes and a fixed deformity are present.

*Mohan et al. performed a literature review on hallux varus deformities.* This deformity can arise from iatrogenic (most common), post-traumatic, idiopathic, inflammatory, spontaneous, or congenital pathologies. The primary pathology in this condition arises from abnormal, unopposed medial forces (soft tissue and abductor hallucis longus tendon) pulling on the hallux and causing a varus deformity. **They conclude** that the overall goal with early surgery is to achieve deformity correction, provide pain relief and functional restoration, and maintain a stable and mobile first MTP joint to minimize the risk of arthritis.

*Crawford et al. published an article on an iatrogenic hallux varus treatment algorithm.* **They note** this condition requires a thorough assessment focusing on flexibility, joint integrity, soft tissue balance, and bony deformity. They shared their treatment algorithm, which is based on flexible vs fixed deformities following bunion correction, with treatments tailored to the time of presentation. **They note** that flexible injuries that present within 2-6 weeks can be treated with shoe modifications, while those presenting 6-12 weeks after surgery should undergo medial capsule release/Z-plasty, and those >12 weeks from surgery should undergo medial capsule release with lateral capsular repair. Whereas, the patients who present with fixed deformities and degenerative changes in the first MTP joint should undergo a first MTP joint arthrodesis. **They conclude** that their treatment algorithm helps to categorize the severity of the deformity while addressing all elements of the pathology.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Shoe modifications are a great first-line treatment in mild, flexible deformities. However, this patient has a chronic fixed deformity with degenerative changes.
Answer 2: Custom splints are useful in flexible deformities, not in fixed deformities like this patient.
Answer 4: Tendon transfers and a medial soft tissue release can correct flexible deformities. This procedure is less successful when fixed deformity and degenerative joint changes are present, as seen in this patient.
Answer 5: A lateral closing wedge osteotomy is a good surgical treatment option in deformities without signs of degenerative changes in the first MTP joint; however, this patient already has degenerative changes and therefore, an arthrodesis is indicated.
