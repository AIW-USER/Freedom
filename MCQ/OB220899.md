## Question

A 65-year-old female presents to your office with chronic pain and stiffness at the base of her right second toe near the metatarsophalangeal (MTP) joint. She was a previous collegiate athlete and notes that this pain has been present since her final collegiate volleyball season years ago. On examination, she has stiffness and pain with motion in the right second MTP joint. Radiographs taken in the office are shown in Figure 1. Patient has failed all nonoperative treatment options to this point. For surgical treatment, which finding would prevent you from performing a dorsal closing wedge osteotomy?

## Options

1. Age of patient
2. End stage osteoarthritis with severe joint collapse
3. Avascular necrosis of the 2nd metatarsal head
4. Degenerative changes of the 2nd MTP joint with articular congruity of the plantar surface
5. Collapse of the dorsal portion of the 2nd metatarsal head with moderate arthritis

## Correct option

**2. End stage osteoarthritis with severe joint collapse**

## Answer explanation

Patient has Freiberg's disease involving her 2nd metatarsal (MT) head with radiographic findings of end-stage osteoarthritis and severe joint collapse. This is a contraindication to performing a dorsal closing wedge osteotomy (DCWO) in patients with this condition because articular congruity on the plantar surface of the joint is required (answer 2).

Freiberg's disease is a rare foot condition described as an infarction and fracture of the 2nd MT head. Most commonly affects the adolescent (13-18 years) athletic female (4:1) population. The etiology of this condition is thought to be due to the disruption of the blood supply secondary to microtrauma and stress overload. Diagnosis is made radiographically, demonstrating subchondral sclerosis, flattening of the involved MT head, and eventual joint destruction in advanced disease. For the detection of early disease, an MRI can be diagnostic. Treatment begins with activity modification and NSAIDs in early disease. Surgical management is indicated for progressive pain, joint destruction, and joint deformity.

*Wax and Leland reviewed Freiberg's disease and avascular necrosis of metatarsal heads.* **They report** it is the 4th most common form of primary osteochondrosis and frequently affects the athletic adolescent female population. If this condition is treated early with conservative measures, it can be nearly self-limiting and resolve with this alone. **They conclude** that patient factors such as age, activity level, and degree of articular involvement are important to consider before surgical intervention.

*Georgiannos et al. performed a randomized controlled trial comparing the results of dorsal closing wedge metatarsal osteotomy (DCWMO) versus osteochondral (OAT) for the treatment of Freiberg's disease in an athletic population.* *27 patients with an average follow-up of 46 months were randomized in this study to receive either DCWMO (14 patients) or OAT (13 patients).* The start of training [6 weeks in the OAT group vs 8 weeks in the DCWMO group (p<0.001)] and return to full sport [10 weeks in the OAT group vs 13 weeks in the DCWMO group (p<0.05)] were significantly different between treatment groups. **They conclude** that clinically, treatment with OAT is comparable to treatment with DCWMO and that OAT is a safe, effective, and optimal treatment option in the athletic patient population with Freiberg's infarction.

Figure 1 is an AP radiograph of the right foot demonstrating severe arthritic changes with significant joint surface destruction at the 2nd MTP joint.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The age of the patient would not be a contraindication to performing a DCWO.
Answer 3: AVN of the 2nd MT head is Freiberg's disease, and therefore, an indication to perform DCWO.
Answer 4: Degenerative changes of the 2nd MTP joint with articular congruity of the plantar surface are indications to perform DCWO.
Answer 5: Collapse of the dorsal portion of the 2nd metatarsal head with moderate arthritis is an indication to perform DCWO. The dorsal surface can be affected, but the plantar surface must be uninvolved for this procedure to achieve successful results.
