## Question

You are the on-call resident at a level I pediatric hospital and receive a consult in the emergency department. When you arrive in the patient's room, members of the non-accidental-trauma team are already evaluating the patient. What is the most likely injury you have been consulted for?

## Options

1. Distal humerus physeal separation in 16-month-old
2. Midshaft both bone forearm fracture in 5-year-old
3. Type II supracondylar fracture in 4-year-old
4. Midshaft femur fracture in 10-year-old
5. Distal tibia tillaux fracture in 13-year-old

## Correct option

**1. Distal humerus physeal separation in 16-month-old**

## Answer explanation

Transphyseal distal humerus fractures should always be evaluated for non-accidental trauma (NAT), as this is one of the primary mechanisms of injury seen.

Transphyseal distal humerus fractures are traumatic injuries that usually occur in children less than 3 years old. In these cases, the distal humeral physis displaces posteromedially most commonly and remains well aligned with the forearm on radiographs. It is important to distinguish this injury from an elbow dislocation or supracondylar humerus fracture. It commonly occurs as a result of a rotational or twisting injury, which is the reason for its strong association with NAT. An appropriate NAT workup should be performed on all young children with this injury to ensure there are no risk factors for further abuse. Definitive management of these injuries involves closed reduction and pinning in the operating room. Adjunctive use of an arthrogram can be helpful intraoperatively to ensure an appropriate reduction is achieved.

*Abzug et al. published a JAAOS review of transphyseal fractures of the distal humerus.* **They note** that these typically occur in children less than 3 years old secondary to birth trauma, NAT, or a fall from height. It is important to recognize that the forearm is not aligned with the humerus on radiographs. **They recommend** closed reduction and percutaneous pinning for treatment.

*Crowe et al. compared transphyseal distal humerus fractures to supracondylar fractures with regard to the rate of NAT in children less than 3 years old.* **They note** that 26% of transphyseal distal humerus fractures resulted from NAT, compared to 2% of displaced supracondylar fractures. **They concluded** that the risk of NAT was 13x greater in transphyseal distal humerus fractures in this age group.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2-5: None of these fractures have features concerning for NAT compared a distal humerus physeal separation in a very young patient.
