## Question

A 45-year-old male sustains an open Grade 3B distal humerus fracture after a roll-over ATV accident. He initially undergoes temporizing external fixation and debridement of the injury. His distal posterior arm defect has exposed triceps tendon and articular bone and measures 10 x 13 cm. The decision is made to proceed with a latissimus dorsi myocutaneous flap. Which artery in Figure A supplies the main perforators to this flap?

![[q213986-a-labeled-angiogram1.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. A
2. B
3. C
4. D
5. E

## Correct option

**3. C**

## Answer explanation

The patient has a large posterior distal arm defect and is a candidate for a latissimus dorsi (LD) myocutaneous flap. The thoracodorsal (TD) artery (Labeled C in the angiogram) supplies the perforators to this flap.

Soft tissue defects around the elbow provide a challenge for the reconstructive surgeon. Because of bony prominences and the essential and large arc of motion of the elbow, soft tissue coverage around the elbow must be durable, thin and allow for repetitive flexion and extension of the elbow. The LD myocutaneous flap has been described as a viable option for large defects around the elbow due to this large size as well as its broad insertion. This flap is primarily supplied by the thoracodorsal artery which is a branch of the subscapular artery.

*Hacquebord et al. performed a retrospective review of 18 patients from 1998 and 2012 who underwent coverage with pedicled LD flap for defects around the elbow.* **The authors noted** an average defect size around the elbow of 422 cm^2. Overall, **they noted** 88% (14 of 16) of the patients had documented (>3-month follow-up) successful soft tissue coverage with a single-stage pedicled LD flap. **The authors conclude** that the pedicled LD flap is a reliable option for large and complex soft tissue injuries around the elbow significantly larger than previous reports.

*Elzawawy et al. evaluated the different vascular patterns of the TD pedicle in 20 cadavers.* **They found** 5 vascular patterns of TD pedicle were found with the most common type being the long vertically descending pedicle giving 3 to 4 transverse medial branches to the LD (40% of cases) and the classically described pattern of a short pedicle that terminates into a transverse medial and a long vertical branch to LD was only seen in 20% of cases. **The authors conclude** that the pattern of TD branching is unpredictable, and a preoperative ultrasound is essential to define the existing pattern and plan the best LD flap design for each patient.

Figure A is a left upper extremity angiogram. The subscapular artery (A) is the largest branch off of the axillary artery (E). The subscapular artery has 2 large arterial branches which are the circumflex scapular artery (B) and the thoracodorsal artery (C). The anterior humeral circumflex artery is labeled D.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: This refers to the subscapular artery.
Answer 2: This refers to the circumflex scapular artery.
Answer 4: This refers to the anterior humeral circumflex artery.
Answer 5: This refers to the axillary artery.
