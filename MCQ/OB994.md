## Question

A 14-year-old male high school football player complains of 3 months of low back pain that began at the onset of his last season. The pain is worse with football-related activity and subsides with rest. He is neurologically intact and has no radiation of the pain down his legs. Symptoms have not improved with activity modifications and 3 months of physical therapy. Radiographs are shown in Figure A. Corresponding MRI studies of the lumbar spine do not show any increased signal on STIR-weighted images in the area of concern. What is the next best step in management?

![[q994-a-spondylolysis.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. LSO brace
2. L4-S1 PSIF
3. L5-S1 PSIF
4. Epidural injection
5. Pars defect repair

## Correct option

**1. LSO brace**

## Answer explanation

The patient's imaging studies are consistent with chronic L5 spondylolysis (no increased signal on STIR-weighted MRI). He has failed an extensive course of physical therapy so the next best step in management is LSO bracing for 6-12 weeks.

Pediatric spondylolysis is one of the most common causes of pediatric and adolescent low back pain. These defects develop over time and are seen in 4-6% of the population. The most common cause is repetitive hyperextension seen in gymnasts, football players, and weightlifters. The incidence of progression to high-grade spondylolisthesis is around 15%. Treatment involves activity modifications and an extensive course of PT. If patients remain symptomatic, rigid LSO bracing in a hypolordotic posture is believed to unload the posterior elements and decrease shear stresses across the pars. Healing of the pars defect does NOT correlate with clinical outcome therefore surgical procedures are reserved only for patients who have failed an extensive course of non-operative measures, have neurological deficits or have very high-grade slips.

*Cavalier et al. performed an overview of spondylolysis and different patterns of spondylolisthesis in children and adolescents.* **They note** that the typical presentation is characterized by a history of activity-related low back pain and the presence of painful spinal mobility and hamstring tightness without radiculopathy. **They note** that spondylolysis or low-grade spondylolisthesis often responds to brief periods of activity restriction, immobilization, and physiotherapy. **They conclude** that surgical intervention is reserved for patients in which pain persists despite nonsurgical interventions, progressive vertebral displacement increases, or those with progressive neurologic deficits.

*Nielsen et al. performed a retrospective chart review of 46 patients with symptomatic spondylolysis or spondylolisthesis.* **They noted** that the average time between the onset of symptoms and initial presentation was 24 weeks and the average delay between initial presentation to a health care provider and the diagnosis was 15 weeks. **The authors concluded** that the diagnosis of spondylolysis/spondylolisthesis was significantly longer after seeing a non-orthopedic versus an orthopedic provider and state that the education of primary care providers on this topic is warranted.

Figure A is a lateral radiograph of the lumbar spine which reveals an L5 isthmic spondylolysis. Illustration A is a marked version of the radiograph in Figure A showing the location of the L5 spondylolysis.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: A L4-S1 PSIF would be the treatment of high-grade L5-S1 spondylolisthesis (Myerding III, IV or V). This patient does not have a spondylolisthesis.
Answer 3: A L5-S1 PSIF would be the treatment of L5 spondylosis or low-grade spondylolisthesis that has failed conservative management (including bracing), or is associated with neurological deficits. This patient has not yet tried bracing and has no neurological deficits.
Answer 4: An epidural injection would not be beneficial to this patient since he does not have any radiculopathy.
Answer 5: A pars defect repair would be indicated for a patient with isolated L5 isthmic pars defect who has failed a complete course of non-operative management (rest, PT and bracing)
