## Question

A 12-year-old non-ambulatory girl with a diagnosis of L1 myelomenigocele falls and presents with the clinical image in Figure A. She currently has a temperature of 100.2 F and her thigh is warm to touch. Which of the following is the most likely cause for her presentation?

![[q2236-a-screen-shot-2020-01-29-at-6-50-42-pm.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. She has developed thigh cellulitis as a result of poor hygiene rendered by her parents
2. She has developed a deep abscess as a result of her immunocompromised state
3. She has likely sustained a pathologic femur fracture secondary to disuse osteopenia
4. Her condition will most likely resolve with a short-course of oral antibiotics
5. She has developed osteomyelitis as a result of her immunocompromised state

## Correct option

**3. She has likely sustained a pathologic femur fracture secondary to disuse osteopenia**

## Answer explanation

In patients with myelomeningocele, including those non-ambulatory, fractures of long bones are not uncommon. The physiological response to the fracture is often manifested as fever, warmth, erythema, and swelling, and may be confused with infection.

Myelomeningocele is a congenital malformation of the spinal column that results from failure of neural tube closure. In a myelomeningocele, the neural elements are contained in the posteriorly extruded pouch, resulting in neural elements being only covered by dura and skin. As such, these patients often have significant motor and sensory deficits and non-ambulators often additionally have osteopenia resulting in low-energy lower extremity fractures. Radiographs must be obtained as this may often be confused for local infection. The higher the level of the myelomeningocele defect, the higher the frequency of fractures.

*Lock et al. evaluated a series of patients with myelomeningocele with fractures.* **They reported** that 97% of fractures occurred in the lower extremities, distal to their myelomeningocele. Additionally, all of the fractures in the metaphysis and diaphysis healed uneventfully with immobilization, whereas fractures about the physis were more likely to result in nonunion or growth arrest.

*Kumar et al. reviewed a series of patients with myelomeningocele who sustained fractures.* **They reported** that metaphyseal and diaphyseal fractures typically presented with warmth, swelling, and erythema. Furthermore, most diaphyseal and metaphyseal fractures healed with immobilization in rigid plaster casts within 4 weeks, while physeal injuries had to be immobilized for a minimum of 8 weeks.

Figure A is a clinical image of an erythematous thigh in the setting of a fracture, which may be confused for infection.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1 and 4: While poor hygiene can be a risk factor for cellulitis, this patient's mechanism and history of myelomeningocele makes fracture more likely than cellulitis/infection.
Answer 2 & 5: Patients with myelomeningocele are not at an increased risk of being immunocompromised.
