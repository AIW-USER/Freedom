## Question

A 72-year-old female presents with vague hip pain that radiates into her groin. She states the pain has been present and slowly worsening over the past 3 years despite conservative management with physical therapy, NSAIDs, and lifestyle modifications. Her surgeon indicated her for total hip arthroplasty and is planning on utilizing a multimodal pain control regimen. Which of the following statements is true regarding liposomal bupivacaine periarticular injections in the setting of total hip arthroplasty?

## Options

1. Comparable to peripheral nerve blocks regarding post-operative opioid consumption
2. Equivalent cost to standard bupivacaine periarticular injections
3. Leads to earlier ambulation compared to standard bupivacaine periarticular injections
4. Proven superiority in pain control over peripheral nerve blocks, however only after 48 hours post-operative
5. Results in shorter length of stay compared to fascia iliaca compartment block

## Correct option

**1. Comparable to peripheral nerve blocks regarding post-operative opioid consumption**

## Answer explanation

Liposomal bupivacaine for intraoperative peri-articular injection during total hip arthroplasty consistently imparts equivalent reduction in postoperative pain scores compared to standard bupivacaine (bupivaine hydrochloride) periarticular injections and peripheral nerve blocks.

Liposomal bupivacaine was approved by the US Food and Drug Administration in 2011 as a local anesthetic. On the molecular level, bupivacaine is encapsulated in a liposomal platform which allows for slow elucidation of the active agent (bupivacaine) over time. This allows for a longer duration of action of the active agent while avoiding systematic toxicity from high plasma drug levels. While there was initial enthusiasm for its use in the total hip and knee arthroplasty setting, its peri-articular injection use has not been proven to be clinically superior to peripheral nerve blocks among various randomized control trials.

*Johnson et al. performed a randomized control trial comparing posterior lumbar plexus block to two separate periarticular injection cocktails in total hip arthroplasty patients.* **They found** that opioid consumption was low and did not differ across groups at any time interval. **They concluded** there are various multimodal analgesic regimens that provide opioid-limiting analgesia.

*Perets et al. performed a randomized control trial on total hip patients comparing liposomal bupivacaine to standard bupivacaine hydrochloride for peri-articular injection use.* **They reported** no significant difference in morphine equivalent consumption at any time point up to 72 hours as the final recording. **They conclude** liposomal bupivacaine peri-articular injection did not differ from standard bupivacaine hydrochloride in pain control, opioid-related side effects, time to first ambulation, or length of stay. They also discuss the total cost for pain control using liposomal bupivacaine to be $320.50 compared to $34.50 for standard bupivacaine.

*Tatum et al. performed a randomized control trial comparing liposomal bupivacaine peri-articular injection to fascia iliaca compartment block (FICB) in total hip patients.* **They found** that both modalities significantly improved postoperative pain control, however, only the FICB group showed a significant decrease in total opioid consumption compared to a retrospective control group. **They concluded** both modalities decrease overall pain intensity and are considered appropriate for use.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Liposomal bupivacaine is well known to have a high financial cost.
Answer 3: Liposomal bupivacaine peri-articular injections has not been shown to lead to earlier ambulation compared to peripheral nerve blocks.
Answer 4: Liposomal-based pain medications are presumed to be superior at later time points in the first postoperative week given their slow elucidating properties. However, this has not been shown to be clinically significant even at 72 hours after total hip arthroplasty compared to peripheral nerve blocks.
Answer 5: Liposomal bupivacaine peri-articular injections have not been shown to have a shorter length of stay in total hip arthroplasty patients.
