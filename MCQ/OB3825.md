## Question

A 57-year-old male undergoes the procedure exhibited in Figure A. He reports a long history of painful, flat feet which failed conservative measures. Which stage of posterior tibial tendon insufficiency is likely for this patient?

![[q3825-a-figure-a.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Stage I
2. Stage IIA
3. Stage IIB
4. Stage III
5. Stage IV

## Correct option

**4. Stage III**

## Answer explanation

Patients indicated for a triple arthrodesis (calcaneocuboid, talonavicular, subtalar fusion) have stage III posterior tibial tendon insufficiency (PTTI).

Stage III PTTI is characterized by flatfoot deformity, rigid hindfoot valgus and rigid forefoot abduction. Patients are unable to perform single limb raise and often report severe sinus tarsi pain. Operative treatment consists of triple arthrodesis.

Pinney and Lin provide an update on the management of PTTI, where they review the current evidence for the treatment algorithm. While no level 1 evidence exists to provide grade 1 recommendations for each stage of treatment, the authors note that key points of disease include function of the posterior tibial tendon and intervening prior to excessive deformity that may cause ankle deformity.

*Pomeroy et al. review the work-up, examination and management of all the stages of PTTI.* *The authors denote the importance of physical exam, where differentiating between a flexible and a rigid hindfoot drastically changes the operative management plan from bony correction/tendon transfers to arthrodesis.*

Figure A exhibits a lateral foot radiograph of a triple arthrodesis. Illustration A characterizes the different stages and corresponding clinical characteristics of PTTI.

Incorrect answers:
Answers 1, 2, 3: Stages I, IIA-B include pathologies that have supple, flexible correctable deformities, therefore a triple arthrodesis is not appropriate.
Answer 5: Stage IV is characterized by tibiotalar tilt, which should also be fused in addition to the calcaneocuboid, talonavicular and subtalar joints.
