## Options

1. Two-level disease
2. Advanced facet arthrosis at the affected level
3. Loss of disc height
4. Neutral cervical lordosis/kyphosis
5. Positive bilateral Hoffman's sign

## Correct option

**2. Advanced facet arthrosis at the affected level**

## Answer explanation

Significant facet degeneration is a contraindication for cervical disc arthroplasty (CDA) due to the likely persistent axial neck pain following the procedure from the arthritic facet joints.

Treatment of cervical disc disease with CDA was intended to preserve the range of motion and prevent the progression of adjacent segment disease. Several studies have demonstrated that CDA to have equivalent outcomes to anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) in one- and two-level disease. The presence of significant facet degeneration is a contraindication to a motion-preserving procedure due to the risk of persistent or progressive axial neck pain, which would be better addressed with a fusion.

*Laratta et al. performed a systematic review of several randomized controlled trials, systematic reviews, and prospective studies comparing CDA to ACDF.* **The authors reported** that the body of literature suggests that CDA has equivalent outcomes to ACDF in single-level procedures. **They concluded** that CDA in two-level procedures or hybrid procedures appears to have similar outcomes as a single-level CDA procedure.

*Hu et al. performed a meta-analysis of eight randomized controlled trials comparing CDA to ACDF, which included approximately 2500 patients.* **They reported** that CDA achieved significantly greater improvement in Neck Disability Index (NDI) scores, greater neurological improvement, and lower rates of complications compared to ACDF. **They concluded** that CDA was superior to ACDF for the treatment of cervical disc disease.

*Kelly et al. performed a retrospective study of 52,400 patients in the California Office of Statewide Health Planning database comparing outcomes of CDA and ACDF.* **They reported** lower readmission rates with CDA and higher reoperation rates for ACDF in the immediate perioperative period. **They concluded** that long-term follow-up data revealed similar complication rates between ACDF and CDA.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: CDA can be highly successful in two-level cervical disc disease.
Answer 3: Loss of cervical disc height is not a contraindication for CDA.
Answer 4: Neutral cervical lordosis is not a contraindication for CDA.
Answer 5: CDA can be effective for both cervical radiculopathy and myelopathy. However, in myelopathy cases, a thorough decompression is recommended to prevent recurrent stenosis.
