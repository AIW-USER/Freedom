## Question

A 7-year-old boy falls from the monkey bars and presents to the ED with pain and deformity of the left elbow. Initial radiographs are seen in Figures A and B. He undergoes closed reduction with percutaneous pinning, and is placed in posterior mold splint. The pins and splint are removed at 3 weeks post-operatively. When in the post-operative period is physical therapy indicated to ensure full range of motion at 1-year follow-up?

## Options

1. Once immobilization is discontinued at 3 weeks
2. There is no indication for routine physical therapy
3. 8 weeks post-operatively regardless of the range of motion
4. 8 weeks post-operatively if the elbow lacks 10 degrees of full flexion
5. 12 weeks post-operatively if the elbow lacks 10 degrees of full extension

## Correct option

**2. There is no indication for routine physical therapy**

## Answer explanation

This patient had a supracondylar humerus fracture. There is no indication for routine physical therapy post-operatively.

Supracondylar humerus fractures are common injuries seen in grade school-aged children. This usually occurs from a fall onto an outstretched hand creating a lever arm at the distal humerus. Nearly 95% will fracture in an extension-type pattern meaning the fracture will begin anteriorly and the distal fragment will displace posteriorly with an intact periosteal sleeve posteriorly. The most common surgical technique is closed reduction with percutaneous pinning using either two or three pins laterally, or two lateral and one medial pin.

*Spencer et al. reviewed elbow range of motion following supracondylar fractures.* **They found** that age and severity were correlated with less initial motion immediately following cast removal, but with a rapid increase in motion in the period following this and with improvement of motion up to 1 year. This study demonstrates relative benchmarks for post-operative range of motion.

*Keppler et al. reviewed post-surgical elbow motion following pinning of supracondylar fractures in one cohort receiving weekly physical therapy versus no therapy.* Better early range of motion was seen with physical therapy but at one year, range of motion was equivalent. **They recommend** no post-operative physical therapy for patients without neurovascular complications (they did not study patients with neurovascular problems, and thus could not comment on this cohort of patients).

Figures A and B show an extension type supracondylar humerus fracture.

Illustration A shows a well-healed fracture with acceptable alignment.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1- Immediate therapy is not needed
Answer 3- There is no indication for routine physical therapy
Answers 4 and 5- There are no guidelines pertaining to flexion or extension
