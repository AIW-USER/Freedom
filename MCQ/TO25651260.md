## Question

Which of the following statements accurately characterizes the biochemical degradation profile of the available ceramics, and what specific clinical complication is uniquely associated with the rapid-resorption extreme of the ceramic spectrum (Calcium Sulfate)?

## Options

1. Hydroxyapatite (HA) provides rapid osteoinduction via osteoclast-mediated acidic degradation, while Calcium Sulfate provides a slow-resorbing scaffold; Calcium Sulfate use is primarily complicated by an exothermic curing reaction that can cause thermal necrosis of adjacent cartilage.
2. beta-Tricalcium Phosphate (beta-TCP) relies primarily on cell-mediated resorption (osteoclasts) over 6 to 18 months, whereas Calcium Sulfate undergoes rapid passive dissolution; Calcium Sulfate use is uniquely associated with a sterile, non-infectious serous wound exudate.
3. Calcium Phosphate cements undergo rapid passive dissolution within 4 weeks, whereas Hydroxyapatite (HA) requires decades to resorb; Calcium Phosphate is uniquely associated with paradoxical accelerated osteolysis due to high local acidity.
4. beta-Tricalcium Phosphate (beta-TCP) undergoes rapid, passive hydrolysis in 4-6 weeks, while Calcium Sulfate requires active osteoclastic remodeling; Calcium Sulfate is uniquely associated with stimulating robust heterotopic ossification in adjacent muscle beds.

## Correct option

**2. beta-Tricalcium Phosphate (beta-TCP) relies primarily on cell-mediated resorption (osteoclasts) over 6 to 18 months, whereas Calcium Sulfate undergoes rapid passive dissolution; Calcium Sulfate use is uniquely associated with a sterile, non-infectious serous wound exudate.**

## Answer explanation

Resorption Kinetics: Synthetic bone graft substitutes resorb at markedly different rates and via different mechanisms.

 - Calcium Sulfate (Plaster of Paris) dissolves very rapidly (within 4 to 12 weeks) via passive dissolution
 in tissue fluids. It does not require cells to break it down.

 - beta-TCP resorbs at an intermediate rate (6 to 18 months) and relies heavily on cell-mediated resorption by osteoclasts, which closely matches the rate of new bone ingrowth.

 - Hydroxyapatite (HA) is highly crystalline and resorbs incredibly slowly, often remaining visible on radiographs for decades.

Because Calcium Sulfate dissolves so rapidly via passive hydrolysis, it creates a massive localized osmotic load in the soft tissues. This draws fluid into the surgical site, frequently resulting in a profound sterile serous drainage from the surgical wound. This can be highly distressing as it mimics a postoperative infection, but cultures will be negative.

Why others are incorrect: HA is osteoconductive, not osteoinductive (A). Calcium phosphate cement undergoes an isothermal or mildly exothermic reaction, not severe thermal necrosis like PMMA (A). Heterotopic ossification (D) is a known complication of utilizing BMPs, not Calcium Sulfate.

Answer: 2
