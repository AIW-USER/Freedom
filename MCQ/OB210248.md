## Question

In the most common pattern of adolescent triplane fractures, which of the following is the expected combination of fracture lines and the projection that each can be seen?

## Options

1. Sagittal epiphyseal fracture on AP radiographs and a coronal metaphyseal fracture on lateral radiographs
2. Sagittal metaphyseal fracture on AP radiographs and a coronal epiphyseal fracture on lateral radiographs
3. Sagittal epiphyseal fracture on lateral radiographs and a coronal metaphyseal fracture on AP radiographs
4. Sagittal metaphyseal fracture on lateral radiographs and a coronal epiphyseal fracture on AP radiographs
5. Coronal metaphyseal fracture on AP radiographs and a coronal epiphyseal fracture on AP radiographs

## Correct option

**1. Sagittal epiphyseal fracture on AP radiographs and a coronal metaphyseal fracture on lateral radiographs**

## Answer explanation

The expected pattern of injury in an adolescent triplane fracture is a sagittal fracture line through the epiphysis on AP radiographs and a coronal fracture line through the metaphysis on lateral radiographs.

Triplane ankle fractures occur as an external rotation injury in adolescents nearing skeletal maturity. The pattern is seen at a mean age of 13 when the growth plate is in a transition period and closing in a pattern of central to medial to lateral. This sequence of growth plate closure leads to a fairly consistent fracture pattern in this age group. Most often a sagitally oriented fracture is seen through the epiphysis and a coronally oriented fracture is seen through the metaphysis. The final plane of fracture in a triplane fracture is oriented in a transverse fashion and goes through the physis.

*Spiegel et al. reviewed triplane fractures regarding diagnosis, treatment, and prognosis.* **They state** for displaced fractures closed reduction by internal rotation and anterior translation should be performed, followed by casting. If displaced >2mm on plain radiographs or CT scan, the patient should undergo operative treatment. Patients can expect a good prognosis if adequate reduction is achieved in either a closed or open fashion.

von Laer studied 32 distal tibia and described three types of transitional fractures including a (1)biplane fracture, (2)Type I triplane fracture, and (3)Type II triplane fracture. Type I triplane fractures are described as having a metaphyseal fracture that ends in the physis while Type II triplane fractures demonstrate a metaphyseal fracture that extends through the physis into the epiphysis and joint. They concluded that transitional fractures without fracture gaps >2mm can be treated with plaster casting, whereas fractures of the weight bearing epiphysis with >2mm fracture displacement should be treated with open reduction.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2, 3, 4, 5: These combinations of fracture patterns as well as the plane that they are visualized in do not represent the most common pattern of triplane fractures.
