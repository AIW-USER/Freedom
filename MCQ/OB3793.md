## Question

A 42-year-old carpenter sustains a 10 foot fall from a ladder and sustains a fracture at L1. On imaging there is fracture extension into the posterior vertebral body and a widened interpedicular distance. Magnetic resonance imaging shows the posterior ligamentous complex is intact. Which of the following statements is true regarding this injury pattern?

## Options

1. Canal compromise greater than 50% warrants decompression and fusion
2. A nerve root deficit is an absolute indication to proceed with surgery
3. Patients with adequate pain control can begin early ambulation without bracing
4. Fusion must involve 3 levels above and below the injured level
5. Stabilization with percutaneous screws without fusion is contraindicated.

## Correct option

**3. Patients with adequate pain control can begin early ambulation without bracing**

## Answer explanation

The patient's injury imaging is suggestive of a burst fracture with an intact posterior ligamentous complex (PLC). Patients with adequate pain control can begin early mobilization without bracing.

Thoracolumbar (TL) burst fractures are typically secondary to an axial load applied to the mobile TL junction with a fulcrum centered in the vertebral body. The spectrum of injury can range from mild vertebral body height loss to near complete collapse and disruption of the PLC. In patients with an intact PLC and no neurological symptoms, non-operative treatment is preferred. The use of bracing with a thoracolumbrosacral orthosis (TLSO) can provide symptomatic relief but does not affect outcome. For patients with signficant difficulty with mobilization, internal stabilization with a short segment non-fusion construct can be considered.

*Wood et al. completed a randomized trial investigating operative versus nonoperative (using body cast or orthosis) management of stable TL burst fractures without neurological deficit.* At an average of 44 months follow up, **they found** no difference in multiple endpoints, with a trend towards better SF-36 and Oswestry Disability Index scores in the nonoperative group.

*Bailey et al. reported on a multicenter randomized nonblinded study to evaluate the need for an orthosis in AO type A1-A3 TL burst fractures.* In a study of 96 total patients, **they found** no difference in the Roland Morris Disability Questionnaire (RMDQ) score at 3 months post injury. Longer term data is not yet available

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Canal compromise in itself is not an indication for surgery.
Answer 2: Nerve root injury in the presence of a burst fracture can be treated with close observation or surgery. TLICS score would be 4, thus leaving the choice for surgery up to the provider's discretion.
Answer 4: In patients without ankylosis, shorter segment (<6 levels) fusion can provide adequate stability for burst fractures.
Answer 5: In patients with stable injuries, no neurological deficit, and significant difficulty mobilizing, minimally invasive internal stabilization with a short segment construct can be considered.
