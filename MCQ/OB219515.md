## Question

An 81-year-old male presents to the emergency complaining of left hip pain after a ground-level fall. Radiographs and CT are obtained (Figures A & B), and the patient is diagnosed with a type 1 lateral compression (LC1) pelvic ring fracture. When discussing treatment options for the patient, which of the following is true?

## Options

1. Surgical fixation may decrease pain in the short-term period
2. Surgical fixation is essentially complication-free
3. For patients hospitalized for LC1 fractures, surgical fixation is associated with decreased need for narcotic medications while hospitalized
4. Nonoperative treatment includes nonweightbearing until fracture heals
5. Nonoperative treatment is the gold-standard for all LC1 fracture patterns

## Correct option

**1. Surgical fixation may decrease pain in the short-term period**

## Answer explanation

LC-1 fracture management can be a controversial topic. However, surgical fixation of LC-1 fractures has proven to decrease pain in the short-term period (answer 1).

Lateral compression (LC) pelvic ring injuries are common and frequently result from side impact during a fall onto the side or a motor vehicle collision. According to the Young-Burgess classification (Illustration A), LC injuries can be further divided into 3 patterns based on the increased force and instability. The optimal management of LC1 fractures is a highly debated topic due to the variation in fracture stability, with the majority being safely treated nonoperatively. Surgical fixation for LC1 patterns most commonly includes percutaneous posterior ring fixation by placing iliosacral screws or transiliac, transsacral screws in less stable patterns. In patients undergoing surgical fixation for LC1 injuries, the majority of studies have found a similar decrease in pain in the short-term period (3-12 months).

*Slobogean et al. performed a prospective trial comparing the outcomes of lateral compression pelvis fractures treated surgically versus nonoperatively.* *95 patients with LC pelvic ring injuries of various displacements were evaluated for patient-reported pain and outcome scores.* Surgical fixation resulted in decreased postoperative pain at 3 months and 1 year and increased patient-reported outcomes. Patients with fractures of more than 5 mm of displacement experienced even greater improvements in pain. **The authors concluded** that surgical fixation provides at least a short-term improvement in pain and outcomes in patients with minimally displaced LC pelvic ring injuries.

*Hagen et al. performed a retrospective analysis of 158 LC1 and 123 LC2 fractures treated either surgically or nonoperatively.* *The authors sought to evaluate the immediate postoperative pain (first 48 hours), time to mobilization, and use of narcotic medications while hospitalized.* There was no difference in the treatment groups' early pain scores and minimal difference in narcotic use while hospitalized. **The authors found** that surgical treatment of LC1 fractures allowed earlier mobilization.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Despite percutaneous posterior ring fixation (sacroiliac screws) being minimally invasive, complications including nerve injuries still exist.
Answer 3: While surgical fixation has been shown to decrease short-term pain, there has been an associated increase in the use of narcotic medications in the postoperative period.
Answer 4: For a stable fracture pattern that is being treated nonoperatively, patients are permitted to undergo early, safe mobilization.
Answer 5: The LC1 fracture pattern consists of various fractures that all impact the stability of the pelvis. Although heavily debated, certain LC1 fractures have proven to be better managed with surgical fixation.
