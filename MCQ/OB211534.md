## Question

A 35-year-old longshoreman complains of 12 years of increasingly worse right wrist pain, decreased grip strength, and a feeling of wrist instability. On exam he has deceased supination and extension. During attempted supination his distal radius subluxates dorsally. He reports having an injury as a boy in rural Ireland 15 years ago but cannot recall the specifics and doesn't believe he received treatment. What injury may he have had as a boy which has precipitated his current DRUJ instability?

## Options

1. Distal radius fracture
2. Supracondylar humerus fracture
3. Medial epicondyle fracture
4. Both-bone forearm fracture
5. Elbow dislocation

## Correct option

**1. Distal radius fracture**

## Answer explanation

Distal radius malunions may result in gross deformity, chronic pain, decreased grip strength, distal radio-ulnar joint (DRUJ) instability, and decreased extension and pronosupination arc.

The patient has a loss of supination and experiences dorsal subluxation of the distal radius in supination. Both of these findings may occur in the setting of a volarly angulated distal radius fracture malunion. Dorsally angulated distal radius fracture malunions also affect the alignment of the DRUJ. A concomitant TFCC tear would further potentiate DRUJ instability. Symptomatic distal radial malunions with DRUJ instability may be treated with a distal radial osteotomy through either a volar or dorsal approach.

*Nishiwaki et al performed a cadaveric analysis on the DRUJ joint kinematics following dorsally angulated distal radius fractures.* **The authors found** that a dorsally angulated deformity of 20 degrees decreased the supination range (but not the pronation). Furthermore, the ulna was found to translate volarly in supination even with the TFCC intact. **The authors concluded** that correcting dorsal angulation deformities of the distal radius is critical to maintaining normal DRUJ kinematics.

*Schurko et al reviewed 53 cases of distal radius osteotomies (volar and dorsal approach) for symptomatic malunions.* **They found** that the volar group experienced a better arc of flexion/extension and pronation/supination, and additionally had superior DASH scores. The dorsal cohort had more frequent complications. **The authors concluded** that distal radius malunions are best treated with a volar approach.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: A supracondylar humerus fracture may lead to cubitus valgus and subsequent tardy ulnar nerve palsy or cubitus varus (gunstock deformity) in the setting of medial comminution.
Answer 3: Medial epicondyle fractures may occur in conjunction with elbow dislocation but should not affect the DRUJ.
Answer 4: Both-bone forearm fracture malunions can result in decreased pronation/supination but should not affect the DRUJ given that the relationship between the distal radial and ulnar fragments is most often preserved.
Answer 5: Most pediatric elbow dislocations can be closed reduced without further sequela.
