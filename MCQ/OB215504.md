## Question

A 35-year-old man reports a two-year history of knee pain and swelling. He does not recall any specific inciting event. His pain is currently a 2 out of 10, but it does occasionally increase to a 5 out of 10. His MRI is shown below (Figure A). Which gene over-expression is commonly implicated in the pathogenesis of his underlying diagnosis?

![[q215504-a-pvns.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. CSF-1
2. Ki-67
3. NF-1
4. NTRK
5. Rb

## Correct option

**1. CSF-1**

## Answer explanation

This patient is presenting to your clinic with diffuse pigmented villonodular sclerosis (PVNS). This condition has been found to be caused by an over-expression of the CSF-1 gene. Over-expression of the other genes listed is not associated with the development of PVNS.

PVNS is a locally aggressive neoplastic synovial disease which is characterized by joint effusions, synovial expansion, and bony erosions. The most common site of disease is the knee, and patients typically present with joint pain and swelling. Diagnosis is confirmed with MRI. Classically, the treatment for the symptomatic disease is synovectomy. In 2019, however, Pexidartinib (A CSF-1 receptor antagonist) received FDA approval for use in patients with extensive disease who were unlikely to benefit from synovectomy. There is ongoing research on the role of this drug in patients with the more localized disease to determine if adjuvant or neoadjuvant use may increase regression and/or decrease recurrence.

*Coleman et al. investigated the recurrence rate of PVNS following surgical synovectomy in 103 patients.* *They compared three surgical approaches: combined anterior arthroscopic and posterior open synovectomy, all arthroscopic synovectomy, and combined anterior and posterior open synovectomy.* **They found** recurrence rates to be lowest in the combined anterior arthroscopic and posterior open groups. **They postulate** that higher recurrence rates were seen in the all-arthroscopic group due to the technical difficulties with performing the posterior synovectomy and in the combined anterior and posterior open group due to those patients potentially having a more widespread extent of disease at the time of surgery.

Tap and co-authors investigated the role of PLX3397, a CSFR1 antagonist, in the treatment of tenosynovial giant cell tumors (PVNS). This was a phase I and II trial that was broken into two parts, and 41 patients were enrolled in the first part (the dose-escalation component of the trial) and 23 were rolled in the second part (the escalation component of the trial). The authors found that blockade of CSFR1 can induce a significant regression in tumor volume, and represents a novel pathway in the treatment of PVNS and other tenosynovial giant cell tumors.

Figure A is a sagittal, T2-weighted MRI of the knee demonstrating thickened, hypointense synovium with multiple T2 hypointense loose bodies consistent with PVNS.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 2: Ki-67, a cellular proliferation antigen, is over-expressed in Ewings Sarcoma.
Answer 3: A mutation in NF-1, which codes for the neurofibromin protein, is implicated in the pathogenesis of Neurofibromatosis.
Answer 4: The NTRK fusion protein has been found in a wide variety of solid tumors to include sarcomas and breast cancer. It has not been associated with PVNS.
Answer 5: Rb, the retinoblastoma gene, is a negative regulator of gene expression. Mutations in Rb are present in up to 35% of osteosarcomas.
