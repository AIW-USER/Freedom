## Question

A 41-year-old male sustained the open injury shown in Figures A and B after a motor vehicle accident. He was treated with a tibial intramedullary nail (IMN). The patient presents to your clinic 12 months from his surgery with persistent pain at the fracture site. Figures C-E are the most recent imaging findings. His ESR and CRP are elevated. The decision is made to proceed with irrigation and debridement and nail exchange for a polymethylmethacrylate (PMMA) with vancomycin and tobramycin impregnated IMN. ## Options

1. 24 hours
2. 5 days
3. 2 weeks
4. 1 month
5. 3 months

## Correct option

**1. 24 hours**

## Answer explanation

Peak concentrations for antibiotic elution with the use of an antibiotic-impregnated IMN occurs 1-day following placement. While elution duration is influenced by cement type, generally an antibiotic-impregnated IMN has been found to remain bactericidal for up to 4 months.

Chronic osteomyelitis is often treated with intravenous antibiotics. However, in the case of chronic osteomyelitis that has resulted in an infected nonunion, management should include debridement, local antibiotic delivery, IMN nail removal, and surgical fixation. An antibiotic-impregnated IMN allows for antibiotic elution while also offering the benefits of exchange nailing. Vancomycin and tobramycin are bactericidal antibiotics that are commonly added in cement delivery. Unlike tobramycin, which kills bacteria in a concentration-dependent manner, vancomycin disrupts cell-wall synthesis in a time-dependent manner. The elution duration is dependent on the antibiotic added to the cement. It has been shown that beads implanted in patients for less than 10 weeks eluted significantly higher antibiotic levels than beads were implanted for more than 14 weeks.

*Paley et al. reviewed the utilization of antibiotic-impregnated nails for intramedullary infections.* **They reported** no recurrence of infection. **They concluded** that the use of an antibiotic-impregnated intramedullary nail provides elution of high concentrations of antibiotics up to 36 weeks and is a simple and inexpensive alternative for the treatment of intramedullary infections.

*Riel et al. reviewed their method to create an antibiotic-impregnated nail.* *They reviewed the stability of the nail and found that in contrast to axial and bending stability, little rotational stability is achieved with its use.* **They concluded** that this technique capitalized on local delivery of antibiotics while improving fracture stability to achieve bony union.

*Qiang et al. reviewed 19 patients who were treated with an antibiotic-impregnated intramedullary nail.* **They reported** no recurrence of infection with only one reported nonunion. **They concluded** that antibiotic-impregnated nails are an effective, simple and inexpensive method of treating intramedullary infections.
