## Question

A 42-year-old male presents to clinic with 1 year of atraumatic left knee pain. He also reports subjective fever and chills. His physical examination is remarkable for diffuse knee pain but there is no overlying erythema or induration. His current laboratory values reveal a white blood cell count of 9200/µL (reference range [rr], 5000-12000/µL), absolute neutrophil count of 1800/µL (rr, 2000-7000/µL) with 53.7% lymphocytes (rr, 20-40%), an erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR) of 21 mm/h (rr, 0-20 mm/h), and a C-reactive protein (CRP) level of 2.2 mg/dL (rr, < 2 mg/dL). He has attempted a trial of physical therapy as well as a trial of oral antibiotics with no improvement of symptoms. Imaging is shown in Figures A-C. A biopsy is performed and is shown in Figure D. Which of the following is the best next step in management?

## Options

1. Observation, NSAIDs, continued physical therapy
2. Chemotherapy + limb salvage resection ± adjuvant radiation
3. Joint aspiration
4. IV antibiotics
5. Multi-agent chemotherapy ± local irradiation

## Correct option

**5. Multi-agent chemotherapy ± local irradiation**

## Answer explanation

This patient's laboratory, imaging, and biopsy findings are suggestive of lymphoma. Multi-agent chemotherapy with or without local irradiation would therefore be the next best step in management.

Lymphoma is a malignant hematopoietic tumor that can occur in all age groups (but is most common in those aged 35-55 years old) and that affects men more often than women. On radiographs, lymphoma characteristically appears as large ill-defined diffuse lytic lesions with a subtle mottled appearance. On MR, it typically demonstrates a large soft tissue component and is hypointense on T1-weighted images and hyperintense on T2-weighted images. Histologic features of lymphoma include a mixed small round blue cell infiltrate, diffuse infiltration of trabeculae, and positive staining for CD20, CD45, and lymphocyte common antigen. Multi-agent chemotherapy (with cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, prednisone and vincristine) is the mainstay of treatment, with radiation used as an adjunct to obtain local control in persistent disease.

*Chan et al. published a review on MR imaging of pediatric bone marrow.* **They report** that heterogeneous red marrow and red marrow hyperplasia can mimic marrow disease but should be distinguished from neoplastic replacement and expected post-treatment changes. **They state** that marrow replacement in lymphoma is often focal or patchy and less often diffuse, leading to high rates of false-negative bone marrow biopsies.

*Marina et al. published a case report demonstrating the complementary roles of bone scan, MR imaging, and PET CT imaging in the diagnosis and treatment monitoring of a child with primary non-Hodgkin's lymphoma of the bone (PLB).* *They strongly recommend that bone scan and MR imaging be included in the diagnostic algorithm of children with undefined bone systems.* **They found** that PET CT imaging, however, could not reliably differentiate between the presence or absence of disease in a patient with PLB in clinical remission.

Figure A is an AP radiograph of the left knee demonstrating a mixed lytic and sclerotic lesion in the distal femur. Figures B and C are coronal T1-weighted and axial T2-weighted MR images demonstrating a diffuse and infiltrative intramedullary lesion with minimal cortical erosion. Figure D is a slide demonstrating the small round blue cell infiltrate characteristic of lymphoma.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Observation, NSAIDs, and continued physical would not be appropriate for a patient with lymphoma.
Answer 2: Chemotherapy + limb salvage resection ± adjuvant radiation would not be indicated in a patient with lymphoma.
Answers 3 and 4: Although the patient has a slightly elevated ESR and CRP, his biopsy results are consistent with lymphoma and not infection. Thus, joint aspiration or IV antibiotics would not be warranted.
