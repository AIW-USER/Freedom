## Question

A 4-year-old male is brought to your clinic by his parents for evaluation of bow leg deformity. On exam, he is slightly overweight with varus angulation of his right knee. The knee is not painful and has full range of motion. His radiograph is shown in Figure A and you measure his metaphyseal-diaphyseal angle at 25 degrees. Which of the following is the most appropriate course of action and likely final outcome?

![[q218242-a-blounts.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Bracing – correction of deformity if worn for more than 12 hours per day
2. Observation – resolution of the physiologic deformity by the age of 4
3. Observation – delayed corrective surgery until after skeletal maturity
4. Lateral hemiepiphysiodesis – prevention of deformity worsening
5. Lateral hemiepiphysiodesis – complete correction of deformity

## Correct option

**4. Lateral hemiepiphysiodesis – prevention of deformity worsening**

## Answer explanation

In a 4-year-old patient with a metaphyseal diaphyseal angle of 25 degrees, a lateral hemiepiphysiodesis or a valgus osteotomy are the best options for management. A lateral hemiepiphysiodesis prevents worsening of the deformity but does not fully correct the deformity.

Infantile Blount disease is progressive pathologic genu varum that is centered on the tibia. It is more common in overweight children, early ambulators, and Hispanic or African-American descent. It is confirmed radiographically with an increased metaphyseal-diaphyseal angle. Bracing is indicated in patients with Langenskiold stages I and II in children <3. Surgical management is indicated in stages I and II in children over 3, stages III-VI, failure of brace management, and metaphyseal-diaphyseal angles >20.

*Casteneda et al. retrospectively reviewed 48 patients who underwent guided growth for correction of knee deformities.* **They noted** this method was effective at correcting deformity in skeletally immature patient. The same results differed when specifically looking at patients with Blount’s disease. In these patients, hemiepiphysiodesis prevented the deformity from worsening, but did not correct the deformity.

*Birch reviewed the management of Blount’s disease.* **They discuss** that two distinct forms exist, infantile and adolescent, with juvenile being an intermediate form between the two. **They note** the strong association with childhood obesity and discuss the expected increase in prevalence. They stress the importance of recognition and prompt management in an attempt to reduce the articular distortion and premature arthritis that can arise as a result.

Figure A is an AP view of the bilateral knees showing the classic metaphyseal beaking of Blount’s disease medially.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Bracing is indicated in stages I and II in children under 3, but not in more severe disease in children over 3.
Answers 2 and 3: Physiologic genu varum is present in children less than 2. By the age of 3, children typically have physiologic genu valgum.
Answer 5: Lateral hemiepiphysiodesis has been shown to prevent worsening of deformity, but not fully correct the deformity. It can be used as a less morbid procedure than a valgus osteotomy.
