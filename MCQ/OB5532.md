## Question

An 8-week-old infant comes back to your office following 4 weeks of treatment for a developmental hip dislocation in a Pavlik harness. Figure A is the current ultrasound of her left hip. What is next best step?

![[q5532-a-figure-a.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Discontinue Pavlik harness
2. Readjust Pavlik harness, weekly ultrasounds for 4 more weeks
3. Apply rigid hip abduction orthosis, weekly ultrasounds for 4 more weeks
4. Closed, possible open reduction and hip spica casting
5. Pelvic osteotomy to obtain coverage

## Correct option

**3. Apply rigid hip abduction orthosis, weekly ultrasounds for 4 more weeks**

## Answer explanation

After 4 weeks of treatment with a Pavlik harness, the patient's left hip remains dislocated with a shallow acetabulum (as shown in Figure A). At this age, the next best step is to attempt treatment with a hip abduction orthosis.

Early intervention for developmental hip dislocation is paramount. Treatment begins with a Pavlik harness, which has a success rate over a 90%. When initial treatment with a Pavlik harness fails in patients younger than 6 months old, transition to an abduction orthosis can be attempted for an additional 3-4 weeks with weekly ultrasounds. However, if this treatment fails and/or the child is older than 6-9 months of age (and less than 18 months of age), closed reduction and spica casting is the next best step.

*Tibrewal et al. performed a systematic review of the Pavlik method.* **The authors confirmed** an extremely high success rate (>90%); however, they urged for the judicious use of early intervention and frequent monitoring with ultrasound due to the correlative high rate of avascular necrosis for failed interventions or missed opportunities for treatment.

*Schwend et al. published a 2007 POSNA position statement on screening for DDH with routine hip ultrasounds.* *They urged primary care physicians and specialists to have a low threshold for utilizing ultrasound and radiographs for patients 4-6 months of age or older that are at risk or have any questionable findings on examination.*

Figure A is an ultrasound of the left hip with an alpha angle of approximately 45°. Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: The patient's left hip remains dislocated and further treatment is therefore required.
Answer 2: Pavlik harness treatment should be abandoned if it is not successful after 3-4 weeks of treatment in order to avoid erosion of the acetabulum.
Answer 4: Closed reduction is a viable option once abduction bracing has failed or if the patient is older.
Answer 5: Osteotomy alone is indicated if the hip is reduced but there is a failure to maintain adequate coverage.
