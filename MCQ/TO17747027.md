## Question

Blood supply of femur head at 18 months of age is ( pediatric orthopedics- perthes disease)

## Options

1. MCFA alone
2. MCFA and LCFA
3. MCFA and ligamentum teres artery
4. MCFA, LCFA and ligamentum teres artery

## Correct option

**1. MCFA alone**

## Answer explanation

The vascular supply to the pediatric proximal femur is discussed in terms of three stages (1) birth till formation of the subcapital physis,(birth to 4-7 months) (2) during ossification of the femoral epiphysis,(4-7 months to 14-18 years) and (3) following ossification of the physis (adult stage). This division is based on the appearance of the proximal femoral ossification centre between the fourth and seventh months of life and its fusion by 14-18 years.

In the first stage, typically during the first 4 to 6 months of life, the blood supply to the femoral head comes from three vessels, the medial femoral circumflex artery (MFCA), the lateral femoral circumflex artery (LFCA), and the artery of the ligamentum teres (ALT).

Prior to the development of the subcapital physis the retinacular vessels from the MFCA and LFCA traverse along the femoral neck within capsular extensions termed the retinaculum of Weitbrecht. These vessels freely pass from the femoral neck to the femoral head without interference by the growth plate.

In the second stage, from 4 to 6 months of age until skeletal maturity ( 14-18 years), the blood supply to the femoral head is derived solely from the MFCA. The presence of the subcapital physis blocks the retinacular vessels of the LFCA from crossing the growth plate. The two terminal branches of the MFCA that supply the femoral head are the superior retinacular artery (lateral epiphyseal artery) and the inferior retinacular artery. The superior retinacular artery is the main supply to the femoral head.

In the third stage (adult stage), the blood supply to the femoral head is primarily derived from the MFCA; however limited contributions also come from the LFCA and arteries of ligamentum teres. Following ossification of the subcapital physis , blood vessels pass from the femoral neck into the femoral head along the entire diameter of the femoral neck. The MFCA and the LFCA form an extracapsular ring at the intertrochanteric line. Retinacular arteries extend from this extracapsular ring to supply the metaphysis and epiphysis. The majority of the femoral head is still supplied by the superior retinacular artery. The anterior retinacular artery branching from the LFCA is responsible for supplying the anterolateral aspect of the femoral head
