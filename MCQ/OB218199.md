## Question

Several patients are being considered for total elbow arthroplasty (TEA) after failing extensive courses of nonoperative management. The indications for TEA in these patients vary. Which of the following accurately reports revision rates from lowest to highest?

## Options

1. Traumatic < Inflammatory arthritis < Osteoarthritis
2. Traumatic < Osteoarthritis < Inflammatory arthritis
3. Inflammatory arthritis < Osteoarthritis < Traumatic
4. Inflammatory arthritis < Traumatic < Osteoarthritis
5. Osteoarthritis < Inflammatory arthritis < Traumatic

## Correct option

**1. Traumatic < Inflammatory arthritis < Osteoarthritis**

## Answer explanation

The reported revision rates of TEA are lowest in traumatic patients and highest in patients with osteoarthritis.

Indications for TEA have evolved over the years. Advances in medication have reduced the need for TEA in patients with inflammatory arthritis. Despite the indication for TEA, revision remains much higher than it does for total knee or hip arthroplasties. Revision total elbow arthroplasty is most commonly performed due to aseptic loosening, periprosthetic infection, or periprosthetic fracture with loose implants. The type of revision depends on the etiology of failure, patient's age and patient's comorbidities.

*Fevang et al. reviewed the results of TEA for a relatively large population and compared different prosthesis brands and different patient subgroups.* **They reported** that patients who developed traumatic arthritis after fracture had the worst prognosis compared with inflammatory arthritis. **They also noted** that the risk of revision was also increased when the ulnar component was inserted without cement. **They concluded** that the best results were achieved in patients with inflammatory arthritis.

*Gay et al. evaluated the changes with time in the indications for TEA and the complications of this procedure.* **They reported** that the revision rates for the traumatic, inflammatory arthritis, and osteoarthritis groups were 4.8%, 8.3%, and 14.7%, respectively. **They concluded** that the most common indication for TEA has changed from inflammatory arthritis to trauma. Although the number of total elbow arthroplasties being performed each year has increased, there continues to be a high complication and revision rate.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 2-5: Revision rates of TEA are lowest in traumatic patients and highest in patients with osteoarthritis.
