## Question

True statement regarding Center Edge angle of Wiberg

## Options

1. CE Angle is only considered reliable in patients < 12 years of age
2. CE Angle of > 15% is significant
3. It is the angle formed between the Hilgenreiner and Perkin line
4. CE Angle of < 20° is considered abnormal.

## Correct option

**4. CE Angle of < 20° is considered abnormal.**

## Answer explanation

Answer – d.

CE Angle of < 20° is considered abnormal.

EXPLANATION

Center Edge Angle of Wiberg

The Center Edge angle of Wiberg was a radiographic index introduced in 1939 primarily used to quantify the lateral coverage of the femoral head by osseous acetabular structures in the context of dysplastic hip assessment and preoperative planning of corrective osteotomies.

Center Edge angle of Wiberg is formed by a line drawn from the center of the femoral head to the outer edge of the acetabular roof, and a vertical line drawn through the center of the femoral head. Center edge angle greater than 25 degrees are considered normal. This index is only considered reliable in patients > 5 years of age. Wiberg considered angles between 20 and 25° as uncertain and below 20° as pathological.

Other important angles to remember: -

Acetabular Index / Tonnis angle

In 1963, Lequesne described the “Horizontal Toit Externe” (THE) angle, better known in the English literature as the acetabular index (AI). This angle is a diagnostic tool to determine the orientation of the acetabular roof in the coronal plane and therefore describes the superolateral coverage of the femoral head in an anteroposterior radiograph of the adult pelvis. The angle is formed by the junction of Hilgenreiner's and a line drawn along the acetabular surface. It should be < 25° in patients older than 6 months.
