## Question

A 34-year-old engineer is referred to your tumor clinic from a community orthopaedic surgeon for the work-up of a slow-growing left gluteal mass. The patient brought with him recently obtained outside imaging, shown in Figures A-D. He has no significant past medical history other than having a 10-pack-year smoking history. He endorses no other symptoms other than a palpable, non-tender left gluteal mass that he noticed 6 months prior. On physical exam, he has a golf ball-sized mass posterior to the left greater trochanter that is non-tender to palpation without overlying skin lesions. He has no neuromuscular deficits in the affected extremity and no lymphadenopathy. Of the following options, what is the next step in management?

## Options

1. Wide resection
2. Neo-adjuvant chemotherapy
3. Radiation therapy
4. CT-guided biopsy
5. Observation and 3 month follow-up with repeat imaging

## Correct option

**4. CT-guided biopsy**

## Answer explanation

Of the choices provided, CT-guided biopsy of this aggressive, large, heterogeneous lesion should be performed next.

A slow growing, painless, heterogeneous, sub-fascial, and >5 cm mass is a sarcoma until proven otherwise. After initial imaging, a biopsy should be performed at a sarcoma center. A CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis is also indicated; the lungs are the most common site of metastasis. After appropriate staging, radiation and wide surgical resection is the treatment of choice in most instances, with resection of lung metastases when warranted. In contrast, an MRI may be diagnostic (not requiring biopsy) when evaluating a lipoma, schwannoma, and intramuscular myxoma.

*Rougraff et al. performed a literature review with the intent of answering several questions in regard to the biopsy of soft tissue masses, including when they should be performed, how, and where.* **They identified** 32 studies utilizing a PubMed and Google Scholar search. **They concluded** that open biopsy yields the greatest diagnostic accuracy, no evidence is available in terms of who is best suited to perform the biopsy, and that frozen section at the time of biopsy may improve diagnostic accuracy.

Figures A and B is are coronal T1-weighted and STIR MRI sequences of the left hip, respectively, demonstrating a large mass that enhances on STIR. Figures C and D are axial T1-weighted and T2-weighted MRI sequences showing similar findings. Illustration A shows a CT-guided biopsy of this lesion. Illustration B shows the gross specimen of a myxoid liposarcoma. Illustration C demonstrates the histopathology of a myxoid liposarcoma.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Amputation would be inappropriate prior to biopsy.
Answers 2 and 3: Chemotherapy and radiation would be inappropriate prior to biopsy.
Answer 5: Observation of a lesion with such an aggressive appearance is inappropriate.
