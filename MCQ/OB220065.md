## Question

For many years you have performed total knee arthroplasty (TKA) with a specific technique. You are starting to wonder if there may be a way to make your outcomes slightly better. Which of the following polyethylene insert designs seeks to restore normal kinematics by facilitating femoral rollback of the lateral femoral condyle during deep knee flexion?

## Options

1. Cruciate retaining (CR)
2. Posterior Stabilized (PS)
3. Constrained condylar knee (CCK)
4. Medial congruent (MC)
5. Rotating platform (RP)

## Correct option

**4. Medial congruent (MC)**

## Answer explanation

Medial congruent (MC) polyethylene inserts in total knee arthroplasty (TKA) recreate the medial pivot of the native knee while having less lateral conformity of the insert, allowing femoral rollback of the lateral femoral condyle during deep knee flexion.

TKA design largely coalesced around Cruciate retaining (CR) and Posterior Stabilized (PS) polyethylene insert design in the 1990s and 2000s. CR inserts are relatively flat, providing minimal constraint and relying on the posterior cruciate ligament (PCL) to provide posterior stability. These inserts demonstrate paradoxical anterior femoral translation during knee flexion. On the other hand, PS inserts sacrifice the PCL, relying on cam and post articulation to provide stability and prevent paradoxical rollback. MC polyethylene inserts have become more popular, providing kinematics more similar to the native knee. Some studies have found these inserts to feel more natural to patients, providing higher patient-reported outcome scores.

*Heckmann et al. reviewed conforming polyethylene inserts in TKA.* *They go over the terminology and describe the kinematics and clinical outcomes.* **They found** that conforming polyethylene inserts had a higher Forgotten Joint Score. **The authors concluded** that conforming polyethylene inserts may restore native kinematics in TKA patients more readily than cruciate-retaining implants without the need for the cam and post mechanism present in posterior-stabilized designs.

*Indelli et al. performed a prospective randomized study in which 50 TKAs were randomized to receive MC bearings, while 50 TKAs were randomized to receive PS bearings.* **They found** no difference in two-year outcome scores between the two groups, while the MC group had a statistically greater amount of range of motion (120° in the PS group and 123° in the MC group). **They concluded** that once satisfactory intraoperative stability is obtained, the minimal level of constraint should be used.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: Cruciate retaining (CR) demonstrates paradoxical rollback of the femur in flexion.
Answer 2: Posterior Stabilized (PS) utilizes a cam and post design to prevent paradoxical rollback.
Answer 3: Constrained condylar knee (CCK) inserts provide varus-valgus constraint, as well as rotational constraint.
Answer 5: The rotating platform (RP) design involves a mobile articulation between the polyethylene insert and the tibial baseplate. This provides the potential benefit of less polyethylene wear and less tibial baseplate loosening.
