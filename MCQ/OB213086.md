## Question

Figure A depicts the radiograph of a 76-year-old woman who sustained a mechanical fall. On examination, she has no fevers, chills, or neurological deficits. She has no history of malignancy and CT does not demonstrate any metastatic lesions. Which of the following statements is true regarding her underlying metabolic bone condition and associated fracture?

![[q213086-a-vertebral-compression-fx.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. The underlying metabolic bone condition is characterized by abnormal bone quality
2. The underlying metabolic bone condition is characterized decreased bone quantity
3. A thoracolumbosacral orthosis is strongly recommended if nonoperative management is elected
4. Bisphosphonates are indicated to treat the underlying metabolic bone condition if a DEXA scan shows a T-Score of -1.0 to -1.5
5. Kyphoplasty is indicated as a first line of treatment for the associated fracture if the pain is severe enough to warrant narcotics

## Correct option

**2. The underlying metabolic bone condition is characterized decreased bone quantity**

## Answer explanation

This patient's history is consistent with an acute osteoporotic L4 vertebral body compression fracture. In osteoporosis, the bone quality is normal but decreased in quantity. The majority of patients can be treated with analgesia and a gradual return to activity.

Osteoporotic vertebral compression fractures are common fragility fractures. Patients with vertebral fractures are more likely to fall and sustain subsequent fractures than individuals without such a fracture. Due to the high prevalence of this injury, there is a large socioeconomic burden associated with the disease. The first-line treatment of osteoporotic compression fractures without neurologic compromise includes pain control, progressive return to activity, with or without a thoracolumbosacral orthosis (TLSO). The long-term benefit to TLSO bracing has not been clearly demonstrated.

*Kim et al. performed a prospective randomized controlled trial comparing treatment outcomes after osteoporotic compression fractures without neurologic injury treated with a rigid brace, a soft brace, or no brace.* **They reported** that the Oswestry Disability Index scores for the treatment of compression fractures without a brace were not inferior to those with soft or rigid braces. **They concluded** that back pain improvement and anterior body compression progression are similar among the three groups.

*Bunta et al. performed a study to evaluate the effectiveness of the American Orthopaedic Association's Own the Bone secondary fracture prevention program in the United States.* **They reported** that a growing number of centers have implemented the Own the Bone initiative in the current decade compared to the previous, with half of the patients undergoing BMD testing and/or pharmacologic therapy for osteoporosis. **They concluded** that the Own the Bone intervention has been instrumental in positively changing the behaviors of medical professionals in the area of osteoporosis.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1: Osteoporosis is a disease of decreased bone quantity, not abnormal bone quality.
Answer 3: There is no strong recommendation for or against the use of a thoracolumbosacral orthosis in the management of vertebral insufficiency fractures.
Answer 4: Bisphosphonates are indicated to treat the underlying metabolic bone condition if a DEXA scan shows a T-Score less than -2.5.
Answer 5: Kyphoplasty is not indicated in the acute treatment of vertebral compression fractures.
