## Question

Structure lying in transverse foramina of cervical vertebrae C1 (Atlas) [FNB 2022]

![[TO17138081-1.png]]

## Options

1. Extra dural segment of Vertebral artery
2. Intra dural segment of Vertebral artery
3. Foraminal segment of vertebral artery
4. Pre foraminal segment of vertebral artery

## Correct option

**1. Extra dural segment of Vertebral artery**

## Answer explanation

Ans: A

Transverse foramina of C1 (Atlas) vertebra contains the vertebral artery (extra dural segment), venous system and the cervical sympathetic plexus.

Vertebral Artery course

After arising from the first part of the Subclavian artery, the vertebral artery gets divided into four segments as follows: -

- Pre-foraminal segment: From origin to front of transverse process of C7 vertebra where it lies between longus colli and scaleni anterior muscles.
- Foraminal segment: Here it runs superiorly through the transverse foramina of the C6 to C2 vertebrae. It pursues an almost vertical course as far as the transverse process of C2
- Extradural (atlantic) segment This segment begins after the artery passes through the transverse foramen of the axis (C2) where it is subdivided into two parts; vertical and horizontal. Vertical part courses superiorly, entering the transverse foramen of the C1 vertebra. After passing through the transverse foramen of atlas, the horizontal part (contained in the occipital triangle) begins that enters the vertebral canal.
- Intradural (intracranial) segment: After entering the vertebral canal, the vertebral artery pierces the dura mater and courses superiorly over the anterior surface of the medulla oblongata. At the lower border of the pons, it merges with the opposite vertebral artery and forms the basilar artery. The basilar artery ascends along the basilar groove and finally terminates as it bifurcates into two posterior cerebral arteries.
