## Question

A 65-year-old female presents with persistent pain in the left great toe. She is interested in surgery as she has been seeing a podiatrist for many years who had prescribed wider shoewear and an unknown kind of orthotic. Her exam shows pes planus which reconstitutes with heel raise, calluses under the first MTP. The 1st TMT joint shows excessive passive plantar and dorsiflexion. While examining the rest of the lower extremity, both knees are able to hyperextend to about 15 degrees but show no other malalignment. Additionally, she shows she is able to reproduce the finding shown in Figure A. Her current radiographs are shown in Figure B. What is the best surgical option to address her deformity?

## Options

1. Double metatarsal osteotomy with sesamoidectomy
2. Double metatarsal osteotomy with modified McBride
3. Metatarsophalangeal joint arthrodesis with modified McBride
4. Modified McBride with Akin osteotomy
5. Tarsometatarsal joint arthrodesis with modified McBride

## Correct option

**5. Tarsometatarsal joint arthrodesis with modified McBride**

## Answer explanation

This patient has hallux valgus in the setting of a hypermobile tarsometatarsal (TMT) joint and ligamentous laxity. This deformity would be most amenable to a TMT joint arthrodesis with modified McBride (Lapidus procedure).

The TMT joint was originally thought to be the main culprit causing first ray hypermobility in the setting of hallux valgus with widened inter metatarsal angle (IMA). Early works cited this and concluded that TMT arthrodesis was necessary. Though these studies failed to quantify hypermobility parameters, it is has been shown that arthrodesis significantly limits first ray hypermobility and is a powerful procedure in valgus (and pes planus) correction. Clear contraindications include skeletal immaturity with an open physis and an already shortened first metatarsal.

*Perera et al. discuss the pathogenesis of hallux valgus.* **They note** that the first ray is intrinsically unstable and relies on a balance of static and dynamic structures to maintain alignment. Though narrow footwear and obesity are associated with hallux valgus development, no causative link has been established.

*Sangeorzan and Hansen reviewed the results of 40 feet undergoing a modified Lapidus procedure for hallux valgus.* **They showed** a 90% union rate and an average reduction of the IMA from 14 to 6 degrees. Of note, 7 of these were revisions and all showed satisfactory results.

Figure A shows the thumb being able to abduct flat to the forearm, a sign of generalized ligamentous laxity. Figure B is an AP radiograph showing bilateral hallux valgus with a significant widening of the IMA angle to 17 degrees. Illustrations A and B are pre- and post-op depictions of the Lapidus procedure. Note the plantarflexion gained by the correction which would additionally help correct pes planus.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1 and 2: While these are powerful corrections, they wouldn't address the TMT hypermobility.
Answer 3: This would be reasonable if the MTP demonstrated hypermobility or arthritic change.
Answer 4: This would not address the cause of her deformity and likely lead to recurrence.
