## Question

Figure A is the radiograph of a 31-year-old previous collegiate soccer player who presents for evaluation of left hip pain. The patient reports insidious onset of left groin pain that now prevents him from running. Upon questioning, he denies any specific injuries or traumatic events. On exam, he walks with a normal gait and has no tenderness to palpation along the pelvic rim, pubis or trochanters. On dynamic testing, he has pain with the combination of hip flexion, adduction, and internal rotation. Which of the following is the most likely diagnosis?

![[q211337-a-d701fc0760b5bc170ce09092e9b33e-big-gallery.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Femoral neck stress fracture
2. Cam-type femoroacetabular impingement
3. Pincer-type femoroacetabular impingement
4. Developmental dysplasia of the hip
5. Os acetabuli

## Correct option

**2. Cam-type femoroacetabular impingement**

## Answer explanation

This patient’s radiographs are consistent with a left hip cam-type femoroacetabular deformity. Additionally, he exhibits signs of anterior impingement on physical exam which are classically seen with cam-lesions.

Femoroacetabular impingement (FAI) is divided into cam-type and pincer-type lesions which occur due to abnormal contact between the femur and acetabulum which leads to labral damage and varying degrees of chondral injuries. Cam-type lesions are femoral sided and are characterized by a decreased head-to-neck ratio, aspherical femoral head, decreased femoral offset or femoral neck retroversion. Radiographs classically demonstrate a pistol grip deformity due to the asphericity and contour of the femoral head/neck. Patients typically report activity-related hip pain that is exacerbated by the combination of hip flexion, internal rotation, and adduction (anterior impingement test). Treatment begins with nonoperative modalities including activity modification and PT followed by arthroscopic or open osteoplasty for those who do not improve.

*Philippon et al. review the associated pathologies and return to sport following arthroscopic decompression in 45 professional athletes with FAI.* **They report** that 93% of patients were able to return to sport, with 78% returning to professional competition following arthroscopic treatment. **They conclude** that arthroscopic treatment of FAI is a prudent option that allows even the most competitive athletes to return to sport.

*Nepple et al. review the clinical and radiographic predictors of intra-articular hip disease identified during arthroscopy.* **They report** that labral tears and acetabular cartilage lesions were present in 90.1% and 67.3% of patients, respectively. Furthermore, following logistic regression analysis, male sex, older age, Tonnis grade, and alpha angle were independent risk factors for more severe intraarticular disease. **They conclude** that the recognition of these associations is an important part of patient selection and surgical planning.

*Bedi et al. review the radiographic outcomes following surgical hip dislocations and arthroscopic treatment for FAI.* *They report on the change in the extended-neck lateral alpha angle, AP alpha angle, head-neck offset, and beta angle for both groups.* **They conclude** that arthroscopic osteoplasty is able to restore head-neck offset and achieve similar depth, arc, and proximal resection comparable to that of surgical hip dislocation.

Figure A is the AP radiograph of the left hip demonstrating a cam lesion.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Femoral neck stress fractures are more commonly associated with the “female athlete triad” and do not demonstrate anterior impingement symptoms on physical exam.
Answer 3: Pincer-type lesions are acetabular-based and are demonstrated by over-hang in the anterosuperior quadrant, which is not seen in these radiographs.
Answer 4: While DDH may present with similar complaints, Figure A shows adequate coverage of the femoral head by the acetabulum.
Answer 5: Os acetabuli is an unfused secondary ossification center of the anterosuperior acetabulum. This findings is not seen in Figure A.
