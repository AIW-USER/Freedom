## Question

A 13-year-old female with adolescent idiopathic scoliosis presents to the clinic after failing bracing. Her deformity has progressed and she now has a right thoracic curve measuring 52 degrees. She is appropriately indicated for posterior spinal fusion. Which of the following surgeon-controlled factors has proven to decrease the use of opioids during postoperative admission?

## Options

1. Adherence to postoperative immobilization with full-time bracing
2. Avoidance of placing a subfascial wound drain during closure
3. A short-course of adjunctive postoperative steroids
4. Delaying physical therapy until 48 hours after surgery
5. Recommending preoperative opioid use

## Correct option

**3. A short-course of adjunctive postoperative steroids**

## Answer explanation

The addition of steroids to the postoperative pain regimen has been shown to decrease the use of opioids after posterior spinal fusion for the treatment of adolescent idiopathic scoliosis (answer 3).

Posterior spinal fusion (PSF) is the mainstay treatment for severe adolescent idiopathic scoliosis (AIS) and select patients with neuromuscular scoliosis (NMS) to prevent and correct further deformity. Postoperative pain protocols are instrumental in providing patients with a comfortable and rapid recovery following surgery. A multimodal approach including opioid medications is often employed to better control postoperative pain. Opioids come with several potential adverse effects, including the risk of long-term use. To decrease opioid use in this population, adjunctive steroids have been studied with promising results. Multiple studies show the use of short-term steroids after PSF for AIS or NMS significantly decreases the need for opioid medications for pain control.

*Fletcher et al. performed a retrospective analysis on patients undergoing PSF for AIS at a single institution.* *Patients received the same postoperative pain protocol except for 65 being treated without postoperative steroids and 48 being treated with 3 doses of postoperative steroids.* The two cohorts were matched for curve magnitude, number of vertebrae fused, and estimated blood loss. **The authors found** a 40% decrease in morphine milligram equivalents (MME) during the inpatient stay in the group receiving postoperative steroids and a higher rate of walking with therapy during the first visit. There was a higher rate of wound dehiscence in the steroid treatment group, although this was not statistically significant. **The authors concluded** that a short course of postoperative steroids after PSF was associated with decreased use of opioids.

*Ruska et al. published a retrospective review of 89 patients undergoing PSF for neuromuscular scoliosis.* *32 of those patients received 3 doses of postoperative steroids as part of their postoperative protocol.* *The authors controlled for curve magnitude, number of vertebrae fused, and estimated blood loss between the two groups.* A 70% decrease in postoperative MMEs was noted in patients receiving steroid treatments. **The authors concluded** that postoperative steroid use resulted in a significant decrease in morphine equivalents after PSF for neuromuscular scoliosis without an increase in wound complications.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Bracing is not a standard postoperative practice and has therefore not been shown to decrease opioid use during admission.
Answer 2: Although the utility of subfascial wound drains is controversial in the literature, studies have shown a decrease in postoperative opioid consumption with the use of drains.
Answer 4: Delays in physical therapy have not been shown to decrease the use of opioids in patients undergoing PSF. Most accelerated or rapid recovery protocols recommend physical therapy to begin the day of surgery.
Answer 5: Preoperative opioid use is predictive of increased and prolonged postoperative opioid use.
