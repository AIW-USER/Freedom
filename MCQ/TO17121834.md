Chapter: Arthroscopy MCQs — Wrist Arthroscopy

## Question

Most commonly injured tendon in wrist arthroscopy is

## Options

1. EDC
2. EPL
3. ECU
4. ECR

## Correct option

**1. EDC**

## Answer explanation

Answer: 1

Wrist arthroscopy:

Indications : Wrist arthroscopy has been found to be useful in diagnosing and treating wrist cartilage lesions, synovitis, triangular fibrocartilage complex disorders, and scapholunate and lunotriquetral ligament injuries. Debridement of osteochondritic lesions; reduction and fixation of carpal fractures, distal radial intraarticular fractures, and perilunate injuries; distal ulnar resection; and dorsal ganglion excision can be added to the growing list of indications for wrist arthroscopy.

![[TO17121834-1.png]]

Portals: The usual arthroscopic portals are located between the extensor compartments of the wrist. The portals are numbered according to the compartments on either side of the portal. There are 11 dorsal portals, 9 for radiocarpal and intercarpal access and 2 for the DRUJ. An additional volar portal can be made lateral to the flexor carpi radialis tendon at the proximal wrist flexion crease.

![[TO17121834-2.png]]

Standard radiocarpal and intercarpal portals: STT, Scaphotrapeziotrapezoid; TH, triquetrohamate

Radiocarpal portals:

Portal 1-2: Located between APL and ECRB, along dorsal aspect of snuffbox; Not often utilized, provides access to radial styloid and radial aspect of joint, sometimes used for inflow

Portals most often used for evaluation of the radiocarpal and ulnocarpal joints are portal 3-4 (between the third and fourth extensor dorsal compartments) and portal 4-5 (between the fourth and fifth compartments).

Portal 3-4: Located just distal (1cm) to Lister tubercle, between EPL and EDC; Established first, primary viewing portal.

Portal 4-5: Located in line with ring finger metacarpal, between EDC and EDM; Portal for instrumentation, visualization of TFCC

Portal 6R- It is located on the dorsoradial aspect of the extensor carpi ulnaris tendon. The triangular fibrocartilage and the ulnolunate, ulnotriquetral, and lunotriquetral ligaments can be seen from the 6R portal. The scapholunate interosseous ligament, a potential origin for a dorsal ganglion, also can be seen from this portal

Portal 6U- It is located to the ulnar side of the extensor carpi ulnaris tendon; Primary adjunct for visualization and instrumentation, ulnar-sided TFCC repairs

Mid carpal portals:

The midcarpal radial portal lies to the radial side of the third metacarpal axis proximal to the capitate in a soft depression between the capitate and scaphoid. It is in line with Lister tubercle at the scaphocapitate and scapholunate joint.

The midcarpal ulnar portal is about 1 cm distal to the 4-5 portal, aligned with the fourth metacarpal, at the lunotriquetral-capitate-hamate joint.

STT (Scaphotrapeziotrapezoid) portal - Located along axis of index finger metacarpal just ulnar to EPL at level of STT joint. Allows visualization of scaphotrapezial and scaphotrapezoid joints.

TH (triquetrohamate) portal: The triquetrohumate portal is established on the ulnar side of the wrist, distal to the triquetrum and ulnar to the midcarpal ulnar portal. It provides excellent access for an inflow or outflow canula and can be used for instrumentation in the triquetrohamate joint.

Complications of wrist arthroscopy:

Failure to achieve the procedure is the most common complication.

Finger traction causing edema and transient collateral nerve neurapraxia is the most common set up related complication.

Nerve injury: Most commonly injured nerves are dorsal sensory branch of ulnar or radial nerve. Dorsal sensory branch of ulnar nerve consistently travels intimately around the extensor carpi ulnaris (ECU) and can be found on either side of the tendon, in close proximity to the 6 radial (6R) and 6 ulnar (6U) portals. It is particularly at risk when the 6 ulnar (6U) portal is used for irrigation or placement of sutures in the TFCC. Superficial sensory branch of radial nerve is at risk during arthroscopy of basal joint, as 1U and 1R portals are on either side of the first branch of this nerve. It is also at risk while arthroscopy through 1-2 portal. The distal PIN travels along the floor of the fourth compartment and supplies sensation to the joint capsule. It is less mobile than the surrounding sensory nerves. It is at risk while doing arthroscopy through 3-4 and 4-5 portals.

Tendon injuries: Extensor digitorum communis (EDC) to the index finger, and to the fifth finger are most commonly injured tendons. Late ruptures of the extensor pollicis longus tendon and tenosynovitis of the ECRL have also been reported after wrist arthroscopy.
