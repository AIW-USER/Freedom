## Question

A 62-year-old patient presents with progressive ankle pain, stiffness, and swelling that has worsened over several years. The pain is worse with weight-bearing and limits daily activities despite bracing, NSAIDs, and activity modification. Radiographs demonstrate end-stage tibiotalar arthritis with joint space narrowing and osteophyte formation. The patient asks whether they are a candidate for total ankle arthroplasty (TAA). Which of the following is a contraindication to total ankle arthroplasty?

## Options

1. Correctable coronal plane deformity of 12°
2. End-stage post-traumatic ankle arthritis
3. Obesity with a body mass index of 35
4. Peripheral neuropathy
5. Preserved subtalar joint motion

## Correct option

**4. Peripheral neuropathy**

## Answer explanation

Peripheral neuropathy is a contraindication to total ankle arthroplasty (Answer 4), due to a lack of protective sensation.

Peripheral neuropathy is a contraindication to total ankle arthroplasty because protective sensation is critical for implant survival. Patients with neuropathy, such as those with diabetic neuropathy or Charcot neuroarthropathy, are at increased risk of unrecognized repetitive microtrauma, instability, wound complications, infection, and early implant loosening or failure. Without intact sensation, abnormal loading and delayed recognition of complications can rapidly compromise the prosthesis and surrounding bone.

*Jantzen and colleagues conducted a cohort study to evaluate the short-term clinical effects of a single intra-articular hyaluronic acid injection in patients with symptomatic ankle arthritis.* **The authors reported** modest but statistically significant improvements in pain and patient-reported function at follow-up, with no major adverse events. **They concluded** that a single hyaluronic acid injection may provide temporary symptomatic relief in ankle arthritis, although the magnitude of benefit is limited and should be interpreted cautiously.

*Phisitkul et al published the preferences among AOFAS surgeons for end-stage ankle arthritis, focusing on indications for total ankle arthroplasty versus ankle arthrodesis.* Results showed increasing adoption of TAA, but 93% of members considered poorly controlled diabetes with peripheral neuropathy as an absolute contraindication. **The authors concluded** that despite growing acceptance of TAA, ankle arthrodesis remains preferred in patients with neuropathy and other high-risk factors.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: Correctable coronal plane deformities (up to 20-30 degrees of varus or valgus) can often be addressed with adjunctive osteotomies or soft-tissue balancing.
Answer 2: Post-traumatic arthritis is one of the most common indications for TAA.
Answer 3: Obesity alone is not an absolute contraindication to TAA.
Answer 5: Subtalar joint motion is actually favorable, as it allows for more normal hindfoot mechanics following ankle replacement.
