## Question

Which of the following is true of polyethylene that is sterilized utilizing gamma radiation in an inert environment compared to air?

## Options

1. Decreased cross-linking
2. Increased risk of delamination
3. Decreased pre-implantation oxidation
4. Decreased wear properties
5. More sterile

## Correct option

**3. Decreased pre-implantation oxidation**

## Answer explanation

Gamma radiation is the most common form of polyethylene sterilization and is now performed in an inert environment, rather than air, to decrease pre-implantation oxidation.

Polyethylene undergoes a sterilization process prior to packaging. Prior to the mid-1990's, this process was performed in air, which resulted in unacceptably high rates of "on the shelf" or pre-implantation oxidation which ultimately resulted in poor outcomes following implantation of the components. The switch to sterilization in an inert environment free of oxygen, results in lower oxidative potential. Subsequently, lower rates of early delamination were also seen following implantation, likely given to the fact that components were not oxidized prior to implantation. The sterilization process of polyethylene should also be differentiated from processing to increase cross links, which is done to improve wear characteristics. Radiation with the purpose of improving wear characteristics is done so prior to sterile processing and at a much higher radiation dose.

*Medel et al. reviewed polyethylene components sterilization in a low-oxygen environment or in air.* **They reported** that lower oxidation and oxidation potential in the conventional acetabular liners and tibial inserts that had been gamma sterilized in inert gas as compared with the historical components that had been gamma sterilized in air. **They concluded** that gamma sterilization in inert gas had a lower incidence of delamination in the first decade of implantation for tibial inserts.

*Currier et al. reviewed gamma-Barrier packaging's effectiveness in preventing oxidation of polyethylene during shelf storage and in addressing the problem of early fatigue failure seen in gamma-air-sterilized bearings with long shelf-storage before implantation.* **They reported** that the series of gamma-barrier retrievals studied suggests that oxidation occurs in the body via the same mechanism as seen in gamma-air-sterilized bearings, and that a critical oxidation level is identified above which polyethylene bearings are susceptible to fatigue damage after sufficient cycles of use. **They concluded** that although critical oxidation was not reached in the majority of the retrieved gamma-barrier bearings studied, in vivo oxidation appears to follow an exponential increase with time, resulting in loss of mechanical properties, and susceptibility of polyethylene bearings to eventual fatigue failure.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: Radiation induced crosslinking occurs earlier on in the production of polyethylene, uses a much higher dose, and is a separate process than radiation used for sterilization. Radiation in air was abandoned after it was thought to promote scission and the long term degradation of polyethylene, not due to a change in the number of crosslinks.
Answer 2: Switching from sterilization in air to that of an inert environment was associated with decreased rates of short term delamination.
Answer 4: Wear properties are said to be either equal or increased with the use of sterilization in an inert environment, not decreased.
Answer 5: Both processes result in sterile components.
