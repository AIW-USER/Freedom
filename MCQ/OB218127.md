## Question

A 66-year-old male presents to your clinic for evaluation of shoulder pain. The patient states that his pain has slowly worsened over the past five years and that the pain has been associated with a progressive loss of motion. Despite 6 months of physical therapy, activity modification, and an intraarticular corticosteroid injection, he continues to have symptoms. Radiographs and CT scans demonstrate that the patient has primary glenohumeral osteoarthritis. Based on the Walch classification, the patient has a B2 glenoid. You plan to perform a total shoulder arthroplasty (TSA) for this patient. Given the results of his imaging findings and the patient's pathology, intraoperatively, where on Figure A would you expect to find the highest degree of glenoid erosion?

*Figure A*

## Options

1. Area A
2. Area B
3. Area C
4. Area D
5. Area E

## Correct option

**4. Area D**

## Answer explanation

Patients with glenohumeral osteoarthritis and B2 glenoids typically exhibit the greatest amount of glenoid erosion in the posteroinferior glenoid (Answer 4).

Glenohumeral osteoarthritis is a degenerative disease of the shoulder characterized by damage to the articular surface of the humeral head and/or glenoid. Its incidence increases with age, and it typically affects patients over the age of 60. A variety of pathophysiologic etiologies have been associated with the development of glenohumeral osteoarthritis including, but not limited to, primary osteoarthritis, post-traumatic osteoarthritis, rotator cuff tear arthropathy, dislocation arthropathy, post-capsulorraphy arthropathy, and inflammatory arthritis.

Glenoid wear is classified according to the Walch classification (Illustration A). Type A glenoids exhibit concentric wear, with no subluxation of the humeral head. Type B glenoids exhibit biconcave, asymmetric wear, with posterior humeral head subluxation. Studies have demonstrated that the greatest degree of glenoid wear in B2 glenoids occurs in the posteroinferior glenoid. Type C glenoids exhibit excessive glenoid retroversion greater than 25°. Type D glenoids exhibit glenoid anteversion or anterior humeral head subluxation.

*Knowles et al. investigated the pattern of glenoid erosion in B2 glenoids.* *Their study included 55 patients with B2 glenoids.* Overall, **the authors found** that a majority of the erosion occurred in the posteroinferior glenoid near the 7- to 8-o'clock position. **The authors conclude** that knowledge of the typical orientation of glenoid wear in this subset of glenoid morphology is helpful in formulating a surgical plan.

*Churchill et al. also set out to investigate the pattern of glenoid wear in the setting of B2 glenoids.* *Their study screened 106 patients for inclusion and identified 43 patients with a B2 glenoid.* **The authors conclude**, similar to the aforementioned study by Knowles et al., that the greatest degree of erosion is typically seen posteroinferiorly, and that knowledge of this wear pattern is helpful for intraoperative planning, as patients may require wedged components or glenoid bone grafting in order to allow for adequate component support.

Figure A is a sagittal CT cut of the glenoid. The coracoid lies anterosuperior to the glenoid, and allows for orientation of the remainder of the glenoid. Illustration B is a chart outlining the Walch classification of glenoid wear.

Incorrect Answers:
Answers 1,2,3,5: in B2 glenoids, the highest degree of glenoid erosion is typically located in the posteroinferior glenoid (Area D), between the 7- and 8-o'clock position relative to the long axis of the glenoid.
