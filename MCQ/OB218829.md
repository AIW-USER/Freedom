## Question

A 23-year-old male presents with an open tibial shaft fracture as shown in Figure A. He is scheduled for urgent irrigation and debridement with operative fixation later that evening once cleared by the trauma team. The orthopedic attending is considering placing negative pressure wound therapy (NPWT) over the soft tissue defect with a second look procedure in the next few days. When comparing NPWT to conventional dressings in the setting of severe open fractures, which of the following is true?

![[q218829-a-clinical-photo-open-fracture.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Conventional dressings result in lower rates of deep infection
2. Conventional dressings decrease the rates of amputation
3. NPWT incurs a higher cost of treatment
4. NPWT shortens the total time of hospital stay
5. NPWT significantly decreases the disability index scores by 1 year after injury

## Correct option

**3. NPWT incurs a higher cost of treatment**

## Answer explanation

Negative pressure wound therapy (NPWT) has been controversial when used in the setting of severe open fractures. Despite multiple prospective trials having heterogeneous results, NPWT has consistently been shown to have an increased cost compared to conventional dressings.

Open fractures impart significant risk for the development of deep infections that can be limb and life-threatening. Therefore, open fractures are considered an urgent indication for systemic antibiotics, operative irrigation and debridement, and early fixation with soft tissue coverage, if needed. Polytraumatic patients are often unstable, necessitating staged fixation and soft tissue coverage plans. NPWT has been investigated as a temporizing measure in the setting of open fractures, as the vacuum effect helps approximate wound edges, remove underlying bacteria, provide a sterile environment, and lead to early granulation tissue formation. Recent, large randomized control trials (WOLLF trial) did not find a difference in rates of deep infections when comparing these two treatment modalities. While more research is needed regarding the indications, technique, and use of NPWT for open fractures, NPWT has consistently been shown to incur a higher cost compared to conventional dressings.

*Costa and colleagues performed the WOLLF multicenter randomized control trial through the UK Major Trauma Network comparing NPWT to standard dressings for the initial treatment of severe open fractures.* **They found** no differences in deep surgical infections nor disability rating index scores at 12 months. **The authors concluded** that NPWT was not superior to standard dressings for the treatment of severe open fractures.

*Grant-Freemantle and colleagues performed a systematic review and meta-analysis to compare the use of NPWT versus conventional dressings in the management of open fractures.* **They found** in multiple subgroup analyses that NPWT reduced the rate of deep infections when compared to conventional dressings. However, NPWT did not have a significant effect on the length of ICU stay, rates of flap failures, or rates of amputation. **The authors concluded** that while further research is needed, it appears that NPWT can impart significant benefits for open fractures.

Figure A is a clinical image of a Gustillo-Anderson IIIA open tibial shaft fracture with significant soft tissue injury. Illustration A shows clinical images of the same patient’s injury after initial irrigation and debridement with the use of NPWT followed by xenograft soft tissue coverage.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1. Conventional dressings have NOT been shown to result in lower rates of deep infection. Rather, they are more often shown to result in higher rates of deep infection compared to NPWT.
Answer 2. Conventional dressings have not been shown to decrease the rates of amputation compared to NPWT.
Answer 4. NPWT has not been shown to shorten the total time of in-hospital stay
Answer 5. In the WOLLF trial, NPWT did not significantly decrease the disability index scores by 1 year after injury.
