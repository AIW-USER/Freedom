## Question

A 78-year-old female presents with left hip pain after she tripped over a rug. Injury radiographs are depicted in figure A. What advantage does total hip arthroplasty over internal fixation for this injury pattern?

![[q211705-a-l-fnf.jpg]]
*Figure A*

## Options

1. Shorter OR time
2. Lower intraoperative blood loss
3. Decreased infection rate
4. Lower reoperation rate
5. Shorter hospital stay

## Correct option

**4. Lower reoperation rate**

## Answer explanation

Geriatric femoral neck fractures treated with total hip arthroplasty (THA) have lower reoperation rates and improved function compared to internal fixation.

Femoral neck fractures are highly morbid injuries in the geriatric population with 1-year mortality rates as high as 30% reported. Early surgical treatment with immediate mobilization is paramount to reducing inpatient mortality and postoperative complications. Treatment of these fractures with hemiarthroplasty or THA has lower reoperation rates and increased functional status compared to internal fixation. Hemiarthroplasty has a lower reported dislocation rate compared to THA, but with higher revision rates due to acetabular sided wear.

*Bernstein et al. retrospectively studied 258 geriatric hip fractures for the influence of preoperative testing on hospital stay, time to surgery, influence on treatment decisions.* **They reported** that 27% of the patients underwent preoperative testing and resulted in a >48-hour time-to-surgery in 63% of the patients compared to 20% of the patients without testing and a 3 day longer hospital stay. **The authors concluded** that preoperative testing is associated with greater time to surgery, increased length of hospital stay, and did not affect treatment decisions.

*Chammout et al. performed a randomized controlled trial in 100 patients with displaced intracapsular femoral neck fractures treated with total hip arthroplasty or internal fixation.* *At 17 years follow-up,* **they reported** Harris Hip Scores to be 14.7 points higher in the total hip arthroplasty cohort and a significantly lower risk for reoperation, 23% versus 53%, compared to the internal fixation group. **They concluded** that THA provided better function and decreased reoperation risk in elderly patients with femoral neck fractures.

*Kubiak et al. reviewed early weight-bearing after lower extremity fractures.* **They stated** the literature supports early weight-bearing with a low risk of complications in patients treated for ankle fractures, femoral shaft fractures, femoral neck fractures, and intertrochanteric fractures. However, periarticular fractures are better treated with protected weight-bearing or delayed weight-bearing.

Figure A is an AP radiograph of the left hip with a displaced femoral neck fracture and varus angulation.

Incorrect answers:
Answer 1: Studies have not reported a difference in operative time between THA and internal fixation.
Answer 2: Intraoperative blood loss is likely to be greater with THA than IF due to the large exposure and increased tissue manipulation required.
Answer 3: No difference in infection rate between THA and internal fixation has been reported.
Answer 5: Shorter hospital stay has not been reported for THA compared to IF in patients over 65 years of age. In patients under 65 years, THA has been associated with prolonged hospitalizations.
