## Options

1. Often affects both hips
2. Will require surgical intervention after patient has given birth
3. Focal subchondral bone defects are generally found on MRI studies
4. Hip range of motion is often normal
5. Degenerative changes of the joint space is a late sequela

## Correct option

**4. Hip range of motion is often normal**

## Answer explanation

The constellation of symptoms and imaging findings are consistent with idiopathic transient osteoporosis of the hip (ITOH). Patients with ITOH will often have a normal hip range of motion during physical examination.

ITOH, generally affects women in their 3rd trimester of pregnancy as well as middle-aged men. It is thought to be due to local marrow hyperemia and impaired venous return with marrow edema and increased intramedullary pressure. It presents with an insidious onset of groin pain without limitation in range of motion. Characteristic imaging findings include diffuse osteopenia of the femoral head and neck without joint space involvement on radiographs, as well as decrease marrow uptake on T1 accompanied by an increased marrow signal on T2 MRI studies. Treatment is generally conservative with supportive treatment and restriction in weight-bearing with the majority of symptoms resolving in 6-8 months.

*Korompilias et al. reviewed the etiology and treatment of ITOH.* **They discuss** that the disease primarily affects the bone marrow and the primary hallmark that separates ITOH from other conditions presenting with bone marrow edema is its self-limited nature. **The authors conclude** that early differentiation from more aggressive conditions with long-term sequelae (such as osteonecrosis, inflammatory arthritis, and neoplasm) is essential to avoid unnecessary treatment.

*Rocchietti et al. review the pathophysiology of ITOH.* **They state** that while the exact mechanism is uncertain, several hypotheses have been suggested including impaired venous return of the edematous marrow. **The authors conclude** that ITOH is a self-limited condition, which resolves spontaneously with complete recovery within several months in most cases.

Figure A shows diffuse osteopenia of the left femoral head and neck without degenerative changes in the joint space. Figures B and C are coronal T2 and T1 images, showing a characteristic increase in marrow uptake on T2 accompanied by a decreased signal on T1, respectively.

Incorrect Answers:
Answer 1: ITOH usually only affects 1 hip whereas most cases of AVN generally affect both hips.
Answer 2: ITOH is a self-limiting condition that spontaneously resolves within 6-8 months. Treatment is supportive only.
Answer 3: Focal subchondral bone defects are characteristic of AVN, not ITOH. The femoral head of those with ITOH generally shows edema without any bony defects.
Answer 5: The joint space is relatively spared in ITOH.
