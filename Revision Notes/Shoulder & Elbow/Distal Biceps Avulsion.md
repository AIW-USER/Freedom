# Distal Biceps Avulsion

*Shoulder & Elbow · 10 / 60*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Frequency** — rare; **~10% of all biceps ruptures**

**Demographics** — **dominant elbow (86%)** of **men (93%)** in their **40s** (30–50 y)

**Risk factors** — **anabolic steroids** · **smoking (7.5× risk)** · hypovascularity · intrinsic degeneration · mechanical impingement in space available for tendon

**Patterns** — complete avulsion · **partial** (on **radial side** of tuberosity footprint) · **intersubstance muscle transection** (rope wrapped round arm, **tug-of-war**)

**Rare association** — **median nerve compression**

### Footprint anatomy

**Insertion** — **radial (bicipital) tuberosity**; innervation **musculocutaneous only**\
✗ Wrong options: dual musculocutaneous + radial

| | **Short head** | **Long head** |
|---|---|---|
| Origin | **Coracoid** | Supraglenoid tubercle / superior labrum |
| Footprint | **Distal + anterior** (thin sliver) | **Proximal + posterior** (oval; **apex** of tuberosity) |
| Better at | **Elbow flexion** | **Supination** (furthest from axis of rotation) |

![Long head inserts proximal, short head distal on radial tuberosity](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob3081-biceps-footprint.jpg)

**Biceps function** — **primary supinator**; lesser elbow flexor · **brachialis = primary elbow flexor** (inserts **coronoid + ulnar tuberosity**, not radial tuberosity)

**Annular ligament** — anterior radial head, PRUJ stability; does not attach to radial tuberosity

**Lacertus fibrosus (bicipital aponeurosis)** — from **medial side of distal short head**, distal to elbow crease → obliquely distal + medial across cubital fossa → protects **brachial artery + median nerve** → continuous with flexor-origin fascia; **may be mistaken for intact tendon**; intact lacertus → ↓ deficit in low-demand patient

### Antecubital fossa

**Medial → lateral** — **median nerve** (most medial) · **brachial artery** · **biceps tendon** · **radial nerve** (most lateral)

**Borders** — **pronator teres** ulnarly · **brachioradialis** radially

**Radial recurrent artery** — **superficial** to biceps tendon, crosses near tuberosity

**Superficial radial nerve** — **lateral (radial) + superficial** to biceps tendon; radial nerve bifurcates (PIN + SRN) **at level of antecubital fossa / radiocapitellar joint**

**LABCN** — terminal sensory branch of **musculocutaneous (lateral cord)** → exits **between biceps + brachialis** lateral to musculotendinous junction → pierces fascia, **lateral to biceps tendon, deep to cephalic vein** → superficial along **brachioradialis** → volar-lateral forearm sensation

![Antecubital fossa: radial recurrent artery, SRN, LABCN](../../MCQ/attachments/q219839-illa-ac-fossa-1.jpg)

| Nerve course (✗ Wrong options: distractors for LABCN) | Nerve |
|---|---|
| Branches 5–8 cm distal to lateral epicondyle, **between 2 heads of PT**, along volar FDP → pronator quadratus | **AIN** |
| From radial n. at radiocapitellar joint, **through supinator (arcade of Frohse)**, around **radial neck** → dorsal wrist capsule | **PIN** |
| **Deep to brachioradialis**, lateral to radial artery, pierces fascia **7–9 cm proximal to wrist** → snuffbox, dorsoradial hand | **SRN** |
| With brachial artery between **PT + biceps tendon**, between **FDS + FDP**, emerges between FDS + FPL → carpal tunnel | **Median** |

**Radial nerve motor order (Abrams)** — BR → ECRL → supinator → ECRB → EDC → ECU → EDQ → APL → EPL → EPB → **EIP last**

## 2 Mechanism

### Mechanism – eccentric contraction

**Eccentric contraction** — **muscle lengthens during contraction**; flexed elbow forced into extension ("**flexed elbow unacceptably challenged**")

**Contributors** — **vascular watershed** · mechanical attrition (abrasion during pronosupination)

**Partial tear mechanism** — pronation → **sharp radial margin of tuberosity impinges tendon** → released in supination; rupture through pre-existing degeneration on **radial aspect**

| ✗ Wrong option: contraction type | Definition |
|---|---|
| Isometric | Constant **length** |
| Isokinetic | Constant **speed** |
| Isotonic | Constant **force** |
| Concentric | Muscle **shortens** (biceps curl) |

## 3 Presentation

**History** — painful **"pop"** as elbow eccentrically loaded (catching falling object, lifting furniture/engine, deadlift, curls)

**Hallmark** — **weakness + pain in supination** > flexion

**Inspection** — **"reverse Popeye"** (proximal retraction of muscle belly), contour change, **antecubital / medial ecchymosis**, **palpable defect**

**Complete vs partial** — tendon **absent** in complete · **palpable but painful** in partial (otherwise similar picture)

### Diagnosis – hook test, MRI, partial tears

**Hook test (O'Driscoll) (TO)** — patient looks at palm, **shoulder elevated, elbow 90°, forearm supinated** → intact tendon = cord-like structure finger hooks around; torn → **brachialis is flat** → nothing to hook

**Hook technique** — index finger hooks **lateral edge** of tendon, **lateral → medial**; intact/partial → finger 1 cm beneath tendon

**Accuracy** — **sensitivity + specificity 100%** → **most sensitive exam** for complete rupture

**False "normal" hook** — **partial tear** · **intact lacertus fibrosus** (hooking **medial → lateral** catches lacertus) · underlying **brachialis**

**Partial tears** — hook test **normal but painful**; hook test not useful for partial tears

![Hook test: finger hooks lateral edge of tendon](../../MCQ/attachments/q212062-illb-hook-test.jpg)

**Ruland biceps squeeze test** — elbow **60–80° flexion**, forearm slightly **pronated**, squeeze biceps belly → **no supination = positive** (like Thompson); **sensitivity 96%**

**Other exam (OB)** — **biceps crease interval test**, **passive forearm pronation** test

**✗ Wrong option: milking manoeuvre** — tests **UCL**, not biceps

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

**X-ray** — usually **normal**; occasionally **fleck/avulsion** from radial tuberosity

**MRI** — distinguishes **complete vs partial**, muscle vs tendon, **degree of retraction**; **FABS position** (elbow **flexion**, shoulder **abduction**, forearm **supination**) ↑ sensitivity

**MRI when?** — **needed for partial tear** (palpable but painful tendon) · not always needed for complete tear with conclusive exam

**Partial tear MRI** — ↑ signal + ↑ **diameter** of tendon · **avulsive marrow oedema** in tuberosity · **bicipitoradial bursa distension**

**Ultrasound** — can confirm diagnosis in office

| Clinical finding | Next step |
|---|---|
| Abnormal hook, retraction, ecchymosis | Clinical diagnosis → repair |
| **Palpable tendon, painful hook** | **MRI** (✗ Wrong options: CT · immobilise 3 wk · exploration · early PT) |
| Partial tear, failed ≥6 mo nonop | **Detachment + repair** of biceps tendon |

## 5 Management

```
Distal biceps rupture
├─ Older, low-demand, sedentary → nonop: support + PT
│      (accepts supination/flexion loss)
├─ Young, active, complete tear → anatomic repair within a few weeks
│      └─ chronic, retracted → primary repair in hyperflexion
└─ Partial tear → trial nonop
       └─ persistent pain/weakness → detach + repair
```

### Nonoperative outcome – loss of supination strength

**Greatest loss** — **forearm supination**\
✗ Wrong options: elbow flexion (brachialis compensates; flexion returns near normal)

| Nonop deficit | Orthobullets | OB explanations |
|---|---|---|
| **Sustained supination (endurance)** | **50%** | 50% |
| **Supination strength** | **40%** | 40% (up to 40%) |
| **Flexion strength** | **30%** | **30%** · **10%** (one OB) · **~40% sustained flexion** (one OB) |
| Grip | 15% | — |

**Flexion loss value** — **30%** (Orthobullets, most OB) · **10%** (one OB) · **40% sustained** (one OB) — read the options

**Also with nonop** — **delayed return to activity**\
✗ Wrong options: chronic elbow pain · instability (terrible triad / anteromedial coronoid) · lateral pain on resisted wrist extension (lateral epicondylitis)

**Activity hardest** — **using a screwdriver** (supination)

| ✗ Wrong option: activity affected | Injury |
|---|---|
| Pushing up from chair | **LUCL** tear |
| Bench press | **Pectoralis major** tear |
| Wrist extension | ECRB/ECRL (common extensor) |
| Elbow extension | **Triceps** tear |

**Nonop EXCEPT** — **LABCN neuritis** (exclusively surgical)

**Nonop indications** — **older, low-demand, sedentary**, willing to sacrifice function · not healthy middle-aged with dominant-arm tear

### Operative criteria

| Indication | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Young, healthy, active** | Don't want to sacrifice function (**main aim = restore supination strength**) |
| **Partial tear failing nonop** | Persistent pain + weakness |
| **Subacute / chronic** | **Direct repair** (no allograft) usually possible; may need **hyperflexion** |
| Timing | **Within a few weeks**; delay → harder primary repair, **more extensile approach** |

**Not surgery indications** — cosmesis · preventing elbow degeneration

**Repair outcome** — anatomic repairs **high success**; **loss of terminal extension** common after repair\
✗ Wrong options: high re-rupture

### Surgical anatomy – BR/PT interval & recurrent radial artery

| | **Anterior single-incision** | **Dual-incision (Boyd-Anderson)** |
|---|---|---|
| Rationale | Developed to ↓ **HO + synostosis** of 2-incision | Developed to avoid **radial nerve / PIN** injury |
| Interval | **Brachioradialis (radial n.) / pronator teres (median n.)** — not PT/FCR | Same anterior + **posterolateral: ECU / EDC** |
| ✗ Wrong interval | — | **Kocher (ECU/anconeus)**, **anconeus/ulna** → expose ulna → HO/synostosis |
| Forearm | **Maximal supination** protects PIN (✗ Wrong options: pronation) | Hemostat through IO space along **medial border of tuberosity** → pierces anconeus → tents skin |
| **LABCN injury** | **Higher** | Lower |
| **HO / synostosis** | Lower (↓ periosteal stripping) — **not eliminated** | **Higher** |
| Repair | — | **More anatomic** (better footprint exposure; ulnar placement) |
| Supination strength | Similar | Similar or slightly better |

![Avoid intervals exposing the ulna (ECU/anconeus, Kocher)](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob3081-wrong-intervals.jpg)

**Single-incision steps** — limited antecubital incision → BR lateral, PT medial → **identify LABCN** (exits between biceps + brachialis) and retract laterally → **recurrent radial vessels** coagulated/ligated or retracted → protect PIN\
✗ Wrong options: forceful lateral retraction, keep supinated

**Recurrent radial artery** — **crosses field immediately deep to BR/PT interval, near tuberosity**; large, may bleed profusely → **ligate** if cannot retract → "flash of bright red blood" with deep retractor

| ✗ Artery distractor | Location |
|---|---|
| Anterior interosseous a. | From ulnar a., along **IOM** |
| Anterior recurrent ulnar a. | From ulnar a., proximal + superficial to brachialis insertion |
| Recurrent interosseous a. | From posterior interosseous a., **posterior forearm** |
| Inferior ulnar collateral a. | From brachial a., **medial elbow** |
| Ulnar a. | Far medial, between **FDS + FDP** |

**Approach vs effectiveness** — single vs two-incision: **no proven difference** in effectiveness

**Post-op** — immobilise **110° flexion + moderate supination**

### Fixation techniques

| Technique | Approach | Key step |
|---|---|---|
| **Bone tunnel** | 2-incision | Acorn reamer slot; **2–3 × 2-mm holes 1 cm apart** through far (lateral) radius; sutures tied over bone bridge |
| **Suture anchors** | Single | 2 anchors; **distal anchor tied first** (brings tendon out to length) → maximises contact |
| **Intraosseous (interference) screw** | Single | Whip-stitch sutures through bioabsorbable tenodesis screw |
| **Suspensory cortical button** | Single | Ream anterior cortex, smaller hole far cortex, **flip button** on far cortex, tension-slide tendon in |

| Load | Force |
|---|---|
| Elbow 90°, no load | **50 N** (repair must withstand) |
| Elbow 90°, 1 kg load | 112 N |
| Tendon rupture | 200 N |

**Strength order** — **suture button (400 N) > suture anchor (380) > bone tunnel (310) > interference screw (230)**

**Strongest** — **button + interference screw combination** · **weakest** — **interference screw alone** \
✗ Wrong options: bone tunnels weakest · button alone strongest

### Repair technique – anatomic (ulnar) footprint & chronic retracted tears

**Insertion point** — repair to **ulnar aspect of tuberosity** → ↑ **supination lever arm (windlass effect)**; not more distal (off tuberosity); no flexion advantage

**Chronic retracted (e.g. 5 wk, 6 cm)** — **primary fixation in hyperflexion is well tolerated**: **no flexion contracture / ROM loss**

**Allograft augmentation** — only if tendon **cannot reach tuberosity even in hyperflexion**; less consistent results; not "necessary" for retraction

**Chronic traps** — ✗ Wrong options: two-incision required (may need extensile approach) · add interference screw to button if tendon reaches · nonop equivalent

## 6 Complications

### LABCN injury – most common complication (single-incision)

**LABCN neuropraxia** — **most common complication overall** and **most common nerve** injured, either approach; **more common with single-incision**

**Cause** — **aggressive / prolonged retraction**; not related to fixation type (anchor/screw/button) or timing\
✗ Wrong options: direct laceration

**Deficit** — numbness **volar-lateral (radial) forearm**

**Origin** — **lateral cord → musculocutaneous → LABCN** (labelled musculocutaneous on plexus diagrams)

| LABCN incidence | Source |
|---|---|
| **9%** | Orthobullets, OB |
| **5–7%** | OB (Cohen) |
| **Nearly one-quarter** | OB |

**Resolution** — **3–6 mo** (Orthobullets, most OB) · **by 3 mo** (one OB) · by 6 mo, **up to 2 y** (one OB) — read the options; **observe → gradual resolution**; permanent deficit/neuroma rare (neuroma excision + tension-free repair, traction resection, transposition)

### PIN injury – two-incision technique & forearm positioning

**PIN** — **most commonly injured motor nerve** (**1–2%**; 5% Cohen); resolves 3–6 mo

**Mechanism** — **radially based retractors** around radial neck · prolonged traction · **Beath pin out dorsal forearm** · **entrapment by cortical button** on posterior radius

**Protect PIN** — **maximal supination** + limit forceful lateral retraction

**PIN palsy** — **weak wrist extension with radial deviation** (ECRL intact — radial n. proximal; ECU out — PIN) · weak thumb / **index finger extension**

**PIN risk by approach** — **single-incision higher** (Orthobullets, OB) · **two-incision higher** (other OB explanations) · **similar** (recent meta-analysis, OB) — read the options

### Complication rates (OB)

| Minor (~20%) | Rate | Major (~4%) | Rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| **LABCN neuropraxia** | **9%** | **PIN neuropraxia** | **1–2%** |
| HO | 3–4% | Re-rupture | 1–2% |
| **SRN palsy** | **2–3%** | Deep infection | 1% |
| Superficial infection | 1% | **Radioulnar synostosis** | 1% |
| Stiffness | 1% | Ulnar / AIN injury | **<0.1%** |

**Nerve order** — LABCN > SRN > PIN > median > AIN (OB)

**2nd most common nerve** — **SRN** = 2nd most common **cutaneous/sensory** (Orthobullets, OB) · **PIN** = 2nd most common nerve (one OB) — read the options

**SRN injury** — numbness **dorsoradial hand / dorsal thumb**; lateral + superficial to tendon

**Re-rupture** — **<4–5%** (OB) · **1–2%** (OB) → weak supination > flexion

**HO** — when **IOM + ulnar periosteum disrupted**; risk of **2-incision**; often asymptomatic; some advise prophylaxis

**Synostosis** — loss of pronation/supination; avoid exposing **ulnar periosteum** and dissection between radius + ulna; **more with 2-incision**; ↓ with intermuscular (ECU/EDC) interval

**Proximal radius fracture** — large tunnels or tunnels **proximal to tuberosity**

**Suture rupture** — bone tunnel method

**Expanding hematoma** — brachial artery laceration / radial recurrent bleeding — very uncommon

| Post-op deficit | Nerve |
|---|---|
| **Lateral volar forearm numbness** | **LABCN** |
| Dorsal thumb / dorsoradial hand numbness | **SRN** |
| Weak wrist / thumb / index extension | **PIN** |
| Medial volar forearm numbness | MABCN |
| Can't flex middle finger | Median |
| Can't flex thumb IP | AIN |
| Weak finger abduction/adduction | Ulnar |
