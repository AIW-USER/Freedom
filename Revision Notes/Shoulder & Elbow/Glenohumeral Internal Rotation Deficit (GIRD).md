# Glenohumeral Internal Rotation Deficit (GIRD)

*Shoulder & Elbow · 18 / 60*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Who** — **overhead athletes**, esp. **baseball pitchers**; also javelin

**Definition** — loss of glenohumeral **IR of throwing shoulder vs contralateral** · ↑ ER · ↓ total arc (symptomatic)

**Course** — deficit worsens with ↑ repetitions over a season + a career; may be **painless**

### Definition & thrower adaptations (IR loss, total arc, posterior capsule)

**Characteristic motion change** — **↓ IR** in throwing arm · pitchers: **↓ IR + ↑ ER**\
✗ Wrong options: ER, abduction, adduction, forward elevation

| Adaptation (dominant throwing shoulder) | Direction |
|---|---|
| **Humeral head retroversion** | **↑** (✗ Wrong options: anteversion) |
| **Glenoid retroversion** | **↑** (✗ Wrong options: anteversion) |
| **Posterior capsule / PIGHL** | **Tight + thickened** |
| Anterior capsule / AIGHL | **Lengthened, attenuated** (✗ Wrong options: stretch it) |
| ER at 90° + scapular plane | ↑ |
| IR | ↓ |

**Retroversion mechanism** — humerus retroverted in utero → **de-rotates until maturity** → throwing stress on physis **halts de-rotation** → ↑ retroversion → more ER, less IR · bony changes in those who **began pitching before skeletal maturity**

**Crockett CT** — athletes: ↑ humeral head retroversion, ↑ glenoid retroversion, ↑ ER at 90° + scapular plane, ↓ IR · not in non-athletes

**Why ER gain** — **arc of acceleration ↑ → velocity ↑** · short IR arc + tight posterior capsule → **deceleration harder** → overuse injury

**Posterior capsule thickening** — from **repetitive eccentric stress in follow-through**

### Total arc of motion concept

| Situation | Total arc (IR + ER) |
|---|---|
| **Adaptive, asymptomatic** thrower (ERG ≥ GIRD) | **Normal** vs other side; normal kinematics |
| Thrower vs **average population** | **↑ overall arc** (ER gain > IR loss) |
| **Pathological GIRD** (GIRD > ERG) | **↓** vs contralateral → deranged kinematics |

**Total arc** — should be **equal between shoulders** · ~**180°** (total motion concept)

**Source nuance** — "tight posterior capsule with **normal** total arc" (OB) · "**↑** total arc vs average population" (OB) · "**↓** total arc" in symptomatic GIRD (Orthobullets, OB) — read the stem: adaptation vs pathology

**Injury risk ✓** — **↓ ER vs non-dominant arm** independently linked to injury (esp. with pre-existing IR deficit)\
✗ Wrong options: ↑ ER (compensatory) · ↑ IR (uncommon) · ↑ horizontal adduction · ↓ flexion

**Newer view** — loss of total arc + **failure to gain ER** may matter more than IR loss alone

**GIRD thresholds (TO thrower lecture)** — GIRD = IR loss **≥20°** vs contralateral · **physiologic** = total rotational motion (TRM) equal · **pathologic** = **TRM loss >5°**

## 2 Mechanism

**Cause** — **repetitive throwing**; during **late cocking + early acceleration**

**Capsular constraint mechanism** — tight capsule → humeral head translates **OPPOSITE** to tight region

| Tight region | Position | Head translation |
|---|---|---|
| **Posterior capsule** | Flexion | **Anterosuperior** |
| **Posteroinferior capsule (PIGHL)** | **ABER / cocking** | **Posterosuperior** ✓ |

**Posterosuperior shift** — ↑ clearance for greater tuberosity but → internal impingement + **peel-back** of biceps anchor (biceps vector shifts posterior)

![Throwing phases](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob3055-throwing-phases.jpg)

**Throwing phases** — wind-up → early cocking → **late cocking (max ER)** → acceleration → deceleration → follow-through · "5 distinct phases" (OB)

**Pathologic cascade** — ↓ velocity + command → posterior stiffness, **slow to loosen up** → posterior pain in late cocking / early acceleration (no mechanical symptoms) → **"SLAP event"** (posterosuperior labrum + biceps anchor fail in tension) → **surgery after SLAP event**; stretching works before it

### Internal impingement & associated lesions (SLAP, articular-sided cuff tears)

**Most closely linked pathology** — **internal impingement**\
✗ Wrong options: subacromial · HAGL · Hill-Sachs · biceps tendinitis

**Internal impingement** — in **ABER (late cocking)** greater tuberosity + **posterosuperior glenoid** pinch **articular side of posterior supraspinatus + infraspinatus** and posterosuperior labrum

![Internal impingement](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob3055-illustration-moved.jpg)

**Cuff tear in throwers** — **articular-sided partial**, **SS–IS junction** (posterior SS + anterior IS) · (✗ Wrong options: bursal-sided · full-thickness (not described)) · general population = anterior supraspinatus

**Tear mechanism** — **compressive** (GT vs glenoid) + **tensile** (repetitive eccentric load, hyper-ER twisting)

**Lesion most associated** — **SLAP tear** (type II, posterior extension) \
✗ Wrong options: Bankart · HAGL · anterior capsular contracture

**SLAP risk** — throwers with GIRD **25% more likely** to have SLAP · **peel-back** in late cocking

![Peel-back mechanism](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob3055-peel-back.jpg)

**Cause of internal impingement** — **posterior capsular contracture (GIRD)** or **anterior capsular laxity**

**Chondral injury** — **posterosuperior**\
✗ Wrong options: anteroinferior = anterior instability

**Associated conditions** — GH instability · internal impingement · articular-sided partial cuff tears · SLAP · **ipsilateral elbow pain** (altered throwing)

### SICK scapula

| Letter | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **S** | Scapular malposition (hangs lower) |
| **I** | **Inferior medial border prominence** |
| **C** | **Coracoid pain** + malposition (pec minor contracture → tendinitis) |
| **K** | Dys**K**inesis of scapular movement |

## 3 Presentation

**Symptoms** — vague / **posterior** shoulder pain · may be painless · **↓ velocity + command** · "dead arm" · arm heavy in later innings · slow to loosen up

**Exam** — **stabilise scapula** for true GH rotation · ↓ IR + ↑ ER at 90° abduction · **↑ sulcus sign** (stretched CHL + rotator interval)

**Internal impingement exam** — deep **posterior** pain at **90° abduction + max ER**; negative apprehension

**Subacromial impingement (wrong option)** — **anterolateral** pain with IR in abduction

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Diagnostic criteria

| Criterion | Value |
|---|---|
| **Clinically relevant GIRD** | **IR loss >25°** vs contralateral (Orthobullets, OB) · **20–25°** (OB, Little Leaguer's) — read the options |
| Range reported | up to **50°** |
| Pathological | **GIRD > ERG** (↓ total arc) |
| No | **<15°** loss |

### Key radiology findings

**X-ray** — AP + lateral; **usually normal**

**CT** — **↑ glenoid retroversion**

**MRI** — **ABER view** shows associated lesions (articular-sided cuff tear, labrum) · often normal · not needed initially for isolated posterior tightness

### Little Leaguer's shoulder (proximal humeral epiphysiolysis)

![Little Leaguer's shoulder — widened physis](../../MCQ/attachments/q218674-a-19a-moved.jpg)

**X-ray** — **widened proximal humeral physis** + metaphyseal changes · **17% normal**

**Risk** — pitchers throwing **breaking pitches**, ignoring pitch counts/rest, year-round pitching

**GIRD link** — GIRD exam findings at diagnosis → **increased likelihood of recurrence (3×)**, mean **6.2–7.6 months** · **pain after 2 months PT** = independent predictor of recurrence

**Treatment** — stop throwing (**~3 months** no pitching) → PT (restore motion, kinetic chain) → return-to-throw · **no breaking balls until skeletal maturity**

## 5 Management

```
GIRD in thrower
├─ First line → rest from throwing + posterior capsular stretching
│      (sleeper + cross-body + periscapular/pec minor + cuff)
├─ Fails extensive PT → arthroscopic posteroinferior capsular release
│      (some advocate anterior stabilisation)
└─ After "SLAP event" → surgery (SLAP repair)
```

### Treatment – posterior capsular stretching (sleeper/cross-body) & scapular work

**Initial ✓** — **PT directed at posteroinferior capsule stretching** + periscapular strengthening, **regardless of pathology** · then cuff + scapular stabilisation

**Regimen** — rest from throwing + PT **6 months** (Orthobullets)

**Emphasise** — **sleeper + cross-body stretches + periscapular strengthening**

**Sleeper stretch** — lying on affected side, shoulder **90° abduction** (Orthobullets, OB) · **90° forward flexion** (OB), elbow 90°, push forearm to table, **scapula stabilised** → stretches **posterior capsule**\
✗ Wrong options: anterior · anteroinferior · rotator interval · MGHL

![Sleeper stretch](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob3055-sleeper-stretch-moved.jpg)

| Stretch / exercise | Target | Restores IR? |
|---|---|---|
| **Sleeper stretch** | Posteroinferior capsule | **Yes** |
| Roll-over sleeper | Arm flexed **60°**, body rolled forward **30°** | Yes |
| **Cross-body adduction** | Posterior capsule | Yes |
| Doorway stretch | — | — |
| **Pec minor stretch** (rolled towel, push shoulders back) | SICK scapula, coracoid pain | No (adjunct) |
| Rows (retraction) · punches (protraction) | Scapula | No |
| Humeral head depression, ball on wall | Closed-chain cuff | No |

**Coracoid pain + GIRD** — **pec minor stretching + sleeper stretches**\
✗ Wrong options: weighted ball program · subacromial steroid · arthroscopy

**Stretching protocol (TO)** — posteroinferior capsule only (not global): **sleeper** (90°, scapula stabilised) · **cross-body adduction** · **doorway** (90° abduction, lean forward) · **roll-over** (50–60° flexion) · **5 reps × 30 s daily × 6 wk**

**Outcomes** — **~90%** respond (young throwers) · ~10% older throwers fail → arthroscopic release

**Internal impingement + partial posterior SS tear** — **NSAIDs + posterior capsular stretching + cuff strengthening** · refractory → SLAP repair / cuff debridement \
✗ Wrong options: cuff repair

| ✗ Wrong initial option | Why |
|---|---|
| Arthroscopic posteroinferior release | Only after failed PT |
| **Anterior(-inferior) capsular stretch / release** | Anterior capsule already **lax/lengthened** |
| **Selective ER stretching** | ER already normal / ↑ |
| Posterior labral repair + capsulorrhaphy | **Posterior instability** (linemen, weightlifters) |
| Capsulolabral imbrication / plication | **MDI** |
| Subacromial steroid | Pathology is **intra-articular** |
| Sling | Comfort only |
| Cessation 3–6 months alone | Does not address contracture; deconditioning |
| ↑ deltoid / latissimus weights | ↑ stiffness |
| Change pitch mix | Pitch selection not a risk factor for GIRD |
| MRI arthrogram first | Not needed for isolated tightness |

### Operative criteria

| Procedure | Indication |
|---|---|
| **Arthroscopic posteroinferior capsule release** (± PIGHL) | **Failure of extensive PT** only |
| Anterior stabilisation | Alternative (controversial) |
| Repair thinned cuff | Significant thinning (transcuff or takedown) |

### Posterior capsule release vs anterior stabilization

**Technique** — electrocautery via **posterior portal**, camera **anterior** · release posteroinferior capsule + **posterior band IGHL** from **9 to 6 o'clock** at glenoid rim until cuff fibres seen · **shaver widens gap** (prevents recurrence) · gentle manipulation

**Result** — immediate gain **~65° IR**

| | **GIRD / internal impingement** | **Subacromial impingement** | **Adhesive capsulitis** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pain | **Deep posterior**, ABER | **Anterolateral**, IR in abduction | Global |
| Motion | ↓ IR, ↑ ER | Normal | **Global active + passive ↓** |
| Cuff tear side | **Articular** | Bursal | — |

| | **GIRD thrower** | **Anterior instability** |
|---|---|---|
| Anterior capsule | Lax (microtrauma) | Torn / Bankart |
| Lesion | **SLAP**, articular cuff | **Bankart, HAGL** |
| Chondral injury | **Posterosuperior** | Anteroinferior |
