# Total Shoulder Arthroplasty

*Shoulder & Elbow · 57 / 60*

## 1 Basics

### Principles

**Definition** — replace humeral head + **resurface glenoid**; **cemented all-polyethylene glenoid = standard of care**

**vs THA/TKA** — greater **ROM** · success depends on **soft tissues** (intact cuff, esp. subscapularis) · glenoid **less constrained** → **greater shear stress** → **mechanical loosening**

**Requirements** — **intact + functional rotator cuff** · adequate **glenoid bone stock + version**

**Outcomes** — **pain relief** = most predictable benefit (more than hemi) · preserved IR + ER · survival **93% at 10 y** (Orthobullets, OB) · ">90% at 10 y" (OB) · TSA/hemi for OA: good/excellent **95%** (Norris) · **worse for post-capsulorrhaphy arthropathy**

**Humeral fixation** — cemented or press-fit (porous-coated); both good longevity; humeral loosening **uncommon without infection**

### Humeral anatomy & implant design (version, stemless, platform stems)

| Parameter | Native value |
|---|---|
| **Humeral head retroversion** | Mean **~20°** (transepicondylar axis) (OB) · Boileau & Walch mean **17.9°** (−6.7 to 47.5°) · range **0–55°** depending on method (TO) |
| **Neck-shaft angle** (inclination) | **45° ± 5°** (range **30–50°**); arthritic ≈ 50° (flatter) (TO) |
| **Humeral head height** | Top of head **8–10 mm above top of GT** (TO, native) |
| **Lateral humeral offset (LHO)** | **Lateral base of coracoid → lateral margin of greater tuberosity** (TO) |
| Humeral offset | Centre of rotation → humeral canal axis; medial **4–14 mm**, AP **−2 to 10 mm** (TO) |
| Head thickness | **70% of radius of curvature** (OB) |
| Radius of curvature | Glenoid **22–28 mm** · head **23–28 mm** (TO) |
| **Glenoid version** | **0–3° retroversion** (OB) · **1.5° retroversion** (10.5° retro to 9.5° ante) (TO) · ~0°, <10° either way (TO) |
| Glenoid inclination | Superior **~4°** (4.2°; −7 to 20°) (TO) |

**LHO (TO)** — **↓ LHO** → ↓ lever arm of **deltoid + supraspinatus** → weak abduction · **↑ LHO** → **overstuffing** → ↓ motion, ↑ poly wear · reconstruct within **4 mm** of normal

**Retroversion axes (TO)** — proximal: articular surface plane / COR–articular centre / GT–articular centre · distal: **trochlear axis**, transepicondylar line, forearm

**Restoring COR** — may prolong glenoid fixation + ↓ poly wear (TO)

| Humeral implant | Key fact |
|---|---|
| **Stemless** | **Completely metaphyseal (biological) fixation** · **more bone preserving** · **no osteotomy needed for proximal humerus malunion** (not constrained by stem) · not for 3/4-part fractures (→ stemmed RSA in elderly) |
| **Stemmed** | Cemented or uncemented porous-coated |
| **Short stems** | ↑ **varus malalignment** (metaphyseal-diaphyseal mismatch) |
| Large-diameter, high-fill stems | ↑ **proximal stress shielding** |
| **Platform (convertible) stem** | Modular conversion TSA → rTSA without stem removal → **↓ operative time**, ↓ blood loss, preserves humeral bone, ↓ cost |

**Subscapularis failure** — **independent of implant design**

## 2 Mechanism

### Rocking-horse phenomenon & glenohumeral mismatch

**Rocking horse** — **eccentric glenoid loading** → lifting of opposite unloaded rim → **glenoid loosening** · causes: **superior migration of head (cuff deficiency)** · abnormal glenoid version (ant/post translation)

**Minimised by (TO)** — **glenoid radius of curvature larger than humeral head radius** (**2–4 mm**) → normal translation without rim loading\
✗ Wrong options: perfect congruence · larger head radius

**Mismatch (TO)** — recommended **<4 mm** (2–4 mm larger glenoid) · "mismatch <4 mm ↑ loosening" (another TO explanation) — read the options · matched within 2 mm: no ↑ loosening reported · **too much mismatch** → ↑ translation, ↑ poly wear, fracture

**Modern glenoids** — cemented all-poly, radius **2–6 mm larger** than head (TO)

| Glenoid conformity | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Conforming** | **More stable** · **rim stress** + radiolucencies |
| **Nonconforming** | ↑ **polyethylene wear** |
| Overall | Neither superior |

**Osteolysis** — **rare** in TSA: larger particle size → harder for macrophages to mount RANK–RANKL reaction (OB)

## 3 Presentation

### Indications & contraindications (cuff status, brachial plexus injury, RA, capsulorrhaphy arthropathy)

**Indications** — pain (esp. **night**) + ↓ ADL · **glenoid chondral wear to bone** (primary OA, inflammatory arthritis esp. **RA**) · posterior head subluxation · OA after primary/recurrent dislocation · complicated proximal humerus fracture not fixable (TO)

**TSA vs hemi** — TSA **preferred for OA + inflammatory arthritis** (better pain relief + forward elevation in RA) · **age <55: TSA > hemi** (outcomes, survival, satisfaction)

**AAOS CPG (end-stage GH OA)** — **TSA with all-polyethylene cemented glenoid** · (✗ Wrong options: humeral head resurfacing · CTA head · **metal-backed glenoid (↑ revision/loosening)**) · hemi + ream-and-run = option but TSA preferred

| Contraindication | Note |
|---|---|
| **Upper / preganglionic brachial plexus palsy** | **Absolute** (TO FNB) · root avulsion = flail arm, poor recovery |
| **Active infection** | "**Only absolute** contraindication" (other TO explanation) — read the options |
| **Irreparable rotator cuff tear** | → **hemi or rTSA**; **rocking horse** → glenoid loosening |
| **Rotator cuff arthropathy** (proximal migration on X-ray) | Glenoid resurfacing contraindicated |
| **Insufficient glenoid bone stock** | **Glenoid erosion to coracoid** → **favours hemiarthroplasty** |
| **Deltoid dysfunction** | e.g. can't abduct past **30°** |
| Relative (TO) | **Charcot**, severe plexopathy, **intractable instability**, prohibitive comorbidity, cuff + deltoid dysfunction |
| **Isolated supraspinatus deficiency** | **Relative** only (TO) |

| NOT a contraindication | Why |
|---|---|
| **Non-retracted isolated supraspinatus tear** / **2-cm full-thickness SS tear** | Reparable at TSA |
| Passive ER <10° | Release at surgery |
| **Eccentric posterior glenoid erosion / retroverted glenoid** | Eccentric reaming |
| **Poor glenoid bone stock** | Build up with graft (TO) |
| **Inflammatory arthritis** | **Indication** if bone stock adequate |
| Subchondral cysts | Normal end-stage OA finding |
| Age (young) | TSA favoured over hemi |

**Cuff tears at TSA** — full-thickness in **5–10%** of TSA patients (Orthobullets) · **<5%** in primary OA (OB) — read the options · **+ impingement signs → pre-op MRI**

**RA + good elevation (120°), no superior migration** — **TSA cemented all-poly**\
✗ Wrong options: rTSA · hemi · arthroscopic debridement = less advanced disease

| Hemiarthroplasty indications (TO) | Hemiarthroplasty contraindications (TO) |
|---|---|
| Rough humeral surface, **intact glenoid cartilage**, enough glenoid arc | Recent sepsis |
| **Insufficient bone for glenoid** | **Neuropathic joint** |
| **Fixed upward displacement** (CTA, severe RA) | Paralytic disorder |
| History of **remote infection** | Cuff + deltoid deficiency |
| **Heavy demands** (occupation, sport, LL paresis) | Non-cooperative patient |

### Post-capsulorrhaphy arthropathy

**Putti-Platt** — subscapularis + anterior capsule divided; lateral stump → anterior glenoid neck; medial stump → tuberosity (**pants-over-vest**) → **limits ER** → incongruent contact loading → **post-capsulorrhaphy arthropathy**\
✗ Wrong options: no coracoid transfer

**Magnuson-Stack** — middle subscapularis + piece of LT transferred **lateral to bicipital groove** (restrict ER) → **subscapularis lacerated** during biceps release at TSA → **↑ ER, anterior instability, + belly press**

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| View | Look for |
|---|---|
| **True AP (Grashey)** | Extent of arthritis · **superior migration** (= cuff deficiency) · Iannotti best-fit circle |
| **Axillary** | **Posterior glenoid wear** · head subluxation |
| **CT** | **Glenoid version + glenoid bone stock** |
| **MRI** | Rotator cuff (**Goutallier** fatty change) |

**Cuff-deficiency signs** — **acetabularization of acromion** → incompetent cuff → **rTSA** · disrupted **Shenton's line** of shoulder → cuff not intact

**Infraspinatus atrophy on MRI** — **independent risk factor for post-op superior cuff tear**

### Walch classification of glenoid wear

| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| **A1** | Concentric, centred head; minor central erosion |
| **A2** | Deeper central erosion; line joining rims transects head |
| B0 | Pre-OA **posterior subluxation** of head |
| **B1** | Posterior narrowing, osteophytes, sclerosis, **no bone loss** |
| **B2** | **Biconcave**, **posterior rim erosion**, retroverted · max erosion **posteroinferior (7–8 o'clock)** |
| B3 | **Monoconcave** posterior wear; head subluxation **>70%** or retroversion **>15°** |
| **C1** | **Retroversion >25°** regardless of erosion (dysplastic) |
| C2 | Biconcave, posterior bone loss, posterior translation |
| **D** | **Anteversion** or **anterior** head subluxation (<40%) |

![Walch classification of glenoid wear](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob3075-walch-classification-of-glenoid-wear.jpg)

**Commonest OA pattern (TO)** — **posterior subluxation → posterior glenoid wear → ↑ retroversion**

**B2 vs A1** — **B2 ↑ radiographic loosening** (TO)

## 5 Management

### Implant choice

```
Glenohumeral arthritis
├─ Intact cuff + adequate glenoid → aTSA (cemented all-poly glenoid)
│      └─ retroversion >15° → augment or posterior bone graft
├─ Glenoid eroded to coracoid → hemiarthroplasty
├─ Irreparable cuff / CTA / proximal migration → rTSA (or hemi)
└─ Plexus palsy / active infection → no TSA
```

### Approach

**Deltopectoral** — **cephalic vein** = interval landmark · detach **subscapularis + capsule** from anterior humerus → **dislocate anteriorly** · tight shoulder: release **upper half** (Orthobullets) · **upper 1/3** (OB) of **pectoralis major** tendon

**Superior border of subscapularis (TO)** — **biceps tendon** (just medial to pec major insertion → follow up to **rotator interval**) · biceps ruptured/dislocated → **base of coracoid** marks medial interval

**Inferior border of subscapularis** — landmark for **axillary nerve** (anterior → posterior under GH joint → quadrangular space)

**Palpate cuff insertion** before head cut — ✓ protects cuff

### Subscapularis management & failure (peel, tenotomy, LTO; postoperative restrictions)

**Takedown options** — **peel** (off bone) · **tenotomy** (trans-tendon) · partial tenotomy · **lesser tuberosity osteotomy (LTO)**

**Which is best?** — **no technique superior**: no difference in strength, ROM/function, biomechanical strength or failure rates\
✗ Wrong options: LTO lower failure · peel more ROM · tenotomy higher failure

**LTO** — interest due to **bone-to-bone healing** (biomechanical strength); high union rate · **nonunion** → very poor outcomes, multiple revisions

**Most commonly ruptured muscle after TSA (TO)** — **subscapularis** · factors: **multiple previous operations**, **overstuffing**, **aggressive physio**, tendon length compromise (poor technique)

### Glenoid morphology & retroversion correction (eccentric reaming, bone graft, augments)

![Eccentric reaming of the high anterior side](../../MCQ/attachments/q212183-illa-eccentric-reaming.jpg)

**Why correct** — failure → glenoid malposition → ↓ contact area → ↑ contact pressure → **accelerated loosening**

### Operative criteria – glenoid retroversion

| Glenoid retroversion | Orthobullets / OB | TO |
|---|---|---|
| **<10–15°** (e.g. **9°, 11°, 13°**) | **Eccentric reaming of anterior (high) side** | **Up to 15°** → eccentric reaming |
| **>15°** (e.g. **19°, 21°**) | **Posterior bone graft** (Orthobullets) · **augmented glenoid or bone graft** (OB; augments ↓ complications) | **15–25° → glenoid augments** (reaming perforates anterior vault) |
| **>25°** | Allograft reconstruction of posterior defect | **Bone graft** |
| Insufficient bone (to coracoid) | No eccentric reaming → posterior bone graft or hemi | — |
| Cuff-deficient / acetabularised acromion | **rTSA** (reaming irrelevant) | — |

**15° threshold** — more reaming compromises **subchondral bone** + risks **glenoid vault penetration** · excessive **medialisation** weakens fixation + alters mechanics

**Eccentric reaming not for** — **humeral anteversion/retroversion** (address with head cut + stem) · humeral head collapse · **superior inclination 18°** (exceeds correctable)

**Glenoid deficiency** — build up with **iliac crest autograft** or **resected humeral head** · **never cement** to fill

**Axial CT: retroversion + bone loss, glenoid to be resurfaced** — **bone grafting of the glenoid** (posterior)\
✗ Wrong options: infection work-up · cuff repair · acromioplasty

### Component positioning

| Component | Target |
|---|---|
| **Glenoid version** | **Neutral to 10° retroversion** / "neutral or slight retroversion" (TO FNB) · **neutral** (5° ante to 10° retro acceptable) (TO) · **neutral to slight anteversion** (OB, another TO) · "recreate neutral" (Orthobullets) — read the options |
| Glenoid inclination | **Neutral**; superior/inferior inclination → highest failure; retroverted glenoid loosens more than anteverted (TO) |
| **Humeral stem** | **25–45° retroversion** (Orthobullets, TO) · **20–40°** (one TO explanation) |
| Glenoid retroverted | **↓ stem retroversion** → avoid **posterior dislocation** |
| Stem alignment | **avoid valgus** |
| **Head height (implant)** | Top of head **5–8 mm above GT** (Orthobullets, TO) |
| Head size | **avoid overstuffing** → ↑ joint reaction force + cuff tension |

**Accurate glenoid positioning (TO)** — **most critical** + most challenging step: limited bone stock, variable anatomy, few landmarks

### Glenoid component design

| Better | Worse | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Convex (curved) back** | Flat back | Flat back ↑ loosening |
| **Pegged** | Keeled | Biomechanically superior; studies: no difference or ↑ loosening with keel |
| **All-polyethylene** | **Metal-backed** | Glenoid too small for metal + PE; metal-backed ↑ loosening/revision |
| **Cemented** (Orthobullets: ↓ loosening) | Uncemented | One TO states **uncemented better** — read the options |

**Central peg** — non-keeled designs: central peg usually **not cemented** → not a loosening risk factor

### Capsule & intra-operative fractures

**Anterior capsular contracture** (passive ER **<40°**) — **anterior release + Z-lengthening**

**Stretched posterior capsule** — **plication** (volume reduction)

**Intra-op GT fracture** (minimal displacement) — standard stem + **suture fixation** + cancellous autograft

**Intra-op shaft fracture** — remove → **long cemented stem + cerclage** · most common intra-op complication = **fracture**, humeral shaft **mid–distal diaphysis** (TO, Norris)

### Rehabilitation & postoperative restrictions

**Limiting factor early** — **protection of subscapularis repair** · restriction set by **strength of subscapularis repair**\
✗ Wrong options: supraspinatus · dislocation · loosening · pec release · capsule · bone quality

**Restrictions (≈6 wk)** — **no passive ER >30°** · **no active IR past plane of body** · **no pushing out of a chair** · passive/active-assisted only

**Allowed early** — pendulums · passive FE to 90° · **passive IR to chest** · active elbow flexion

**Progression** — ER isometrics → IR eccentric + isometric

### Postoperative stiffness & opioid use

**Stiff at 6 wk** (FE 90°, ER 0°), X-ray normal, sling still on — **start PT for ROM now**\
✗ Wrong options: more sling · aspiration · MRI · DVT duplex (DVT = swelling + pain)

| Postoperative narcotic use | Factor |
|---|---|
| **↓ use (protective)** | **Increasing age** |
| **↑ use** | **Pre-op narcotic use** · unemployment |
| No correlation | Gender, smoking, alcohol |

**Orthopaedics** — highest narcotic-prescribing surgical specialty

### Professionalism – impaired colleague

**Attending smells of alcohol before TSA** — **notify the department chairman** before case (chain of command) \
✗ Wrong options: medical board first · resident does case · discuss afterwards · refuse to participate

**Substance abuse** — **leading cause of medical board sanctions**; physicians recover better than general population

## 6 Complications

### Overview

| Complication (TO) | Rate |
|---|---|
| **Stiffness** | 66% |
| **Glenoid loosening** | **Commonest cause of failure (39%)** |
| Periprosthetic fracture (GT, shaft) | 11% |
| **Iatrogenic cuff tear** (neck cut, overstuffing) | 7.7% |
| **Humeral loosening** (infection, **RA, osteonecrosis**) | 6.5% |
| **Posterior instability** | **6%** |
| **Anterior instability** (subscapularis failure) | **5%** |
| Neurovascular (arcuate artery) | 4.8% |
| Infection | 4.6% |

**Overall complication rate** — **~15%** · order: glenoid loosening > instability > cuff tear > periprosthetic fracture > infection > modular dissociation > deltoid dysfunction (TO)

**Most common complication / failure** — **glenoid loosening** (TO, OB) · Orthobullets: newer evidence → **cuff failure may be more common** than glenoid loosening — read the options

| Most common (TO) | Hemiarthroplasty | **TSA** | Reverse TSA |
|---|---|---|---|
| Complication | **Progressive glenoid erosion** (fracture cases: **tuberosity displacement**) | **Glenoid loosening** | **Scapular notching** > instability/dislocation |
| Cause of revision | Glenoid erosion | **Glenoid loosening** | Glenoid component loosening |
| Nerve | — | **Axillary (neurapraxia)** | Axillary |

### Glenoid loosening – most common complication & risk factors

**Frequency** — most common cause of TSA failure: **~30% of primary OA revisions** (OB) · **39%** (TO) · reoperation **2.9% primary vs 28% revision** TSA

**Commonest complication at 5 y** — **loosening of glenoid component**\
✗ Wrong options: humeral loosening · acromial stress fracture · infection

| ✓ Risk factor | Not a risk factor |
|---|---|
| **Glenoid morphology**: **biconcave (Walch B2)**, **posterior glenoid wear**, excessive retroversion | **Age** (incl. >65) |
| **Dysfunctional/deficient rotator cuff** (rocking horse) | Inferior humeral head osteophytes |
| Insufficient glenoid bone stock | Subtle **anterior** subluxation (biconcave goes with **posterior**) |
| **Excessive reaming** removing subchondral bone | **Goutallier/cuff atrophy** (not correlated) |
| **Flat-back** glenoid | **Curved-back** (↓ loosening) · **pegged** component |
| Mismatch (see Mechanism) | Stemless humerus · diabetes (infection) · uncemented central peg |
| Soft-tissue instability · infection · aseptic osteolysis | ✗ Wrong options: osteonecrosis · RA · OA (as cause of glenoid loosening) |

**Radiolucent lines** — presence **does not correlate with symptoms** or clinical failure (3 + 7 y) · **progression does correlate** (50% by 3–4 y)

**Treatment** — glenoid removal (if bone stock) ± **bone graft** for later re-implantation · **convert to rTSA** (salvage) · glenoid morphology not fixable with aTSA → rTSA

### Subscapularis failure

**Commonest instability after TSA** — **anterior** (subscapularis + anterior capsule pull-off) (Orthobullets, TO) · TO complication list gives posterior 6% vs anterior 5% — read the options \
✗ Wrong options: "pull-off of rotator cuff" (= supraspinatus)

**Anterior dislocation after TSA** — **subscapularis rupture in all cases** (Wirth & Rockwood) · classic: **pushing up from chair** (fires subscapularis)

**Mechanism** — fall/catching self · **forced IR** or **excessive ER** (beyond normal **40–60°**) · forced ER withdrawal (hot pan) → LTO failure · late rupture with sport (golf, tennis)

| Sign of subscapularis insufficiency | Note |
|---|---|
| **↑ Passive ER** | e.g. 90° at side (abnormal at 72 y), 110° vs 80° |
| **Lift-off / IR lag** | Can't hold hand off back |
| **Belly-press** (abdominal compression) | Weak |
| **Bear hug** | **≥20% weaker** than other side |
| IR weakness · can't tuck shirt / reach back pocket | — |
| **Anterior instability with ER** | (✗ Wrong options: instability with IR = posterior) |
| **Axillary X-ray: anterior subluxation** | Head not centred |

![Anterior subluxation of humeral head after subscapularis failure](../../MCQ/attachments/q217830-a-subscap-tear-atsa.jpg)

**Confirm** — **ultrasound** · X-ray: displaced lesser tuberosity (failed LTO) · glenoid loosening/locked posterior dislocation **visible on X-ray** (normal X-ray → tendon rupture)

**Consequence** — loss of **anterior–posterior force couple** / concavity-compression → instability, early loosening

| Timing / patient | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Acute (<6 wk)**, young/active | **Early exploration + revision repair** (± augmentation) |
| **>6 wk** | Anterior capsular reconstruction · tendon transfer · **rTSA** |
| **Chronic / older** | **Revision to rTSA** (most reliable) |

**Subscapularis innervation** — **upper + lower subscapular nerves** ← **posterior cord**

| ✗ Plexus distractor | Branches |
|---|---|
| Lateral cord | **Lateral pectoral**, **musculocutaneous** |
| Medial cord | Medial pectoral, **medial brachial cutaneous (T1)**, MABC |
| Superior trunk | **Suprascapular**, nerve to subclavius |
| C5 root | **Dorsal scapular** (rhomboids, levator scapulae) |

### Rotator cuff failure after TSA (overstuffing, falls, sizing)

**Frequency** — **one of the most common complications**; common long-term secondary dysfunction · **common reason for conversion to rTSA**

| Cause | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| **Excessive bone removal at humeral neck osteotomy** (cut too distal / excessive retroversion) | **Iatrogenic** cut/detachment of cuff insertion (✗ Wrong options: 30° retroversion = normal · 45° inclination · deltoid retraction = axillary nerve) |
| **Overstuffing** (oversized head, ↑ LHO) | **Attritional supraspinatus + subscapularis** tears; ↓ motion |
| **Fall / trauma** | **Traumatic cuff tear** (SS 2/5, normal X-ray → MRI) |
| Undiagnosed pre-op cuff tear | Improper soft-tissue balancing |
| **Pre-op infraspinatus atrophy** | ↑ **superior cuff tear** |
| Degeneration, malposition | Late tear |

**Iannotti best-fit circle** — Grashey AP: 3 points = **lateral cortex below GT flare + lateral + medial ends of anatomic neck** · resurfaced head **outside circle = overstuffing** → **component sizing** error

![Iannotti best-fit circle for humeral head sizing](../../MCQ/attachments/q220135-illa-screenshot-2025-02-25-at-6-35-51-pm.jpg)

**Anterior + superior escape** — **SS + subscapularis** tears (Jobe + belly-press +; Hornblower −) ← **overstuffing**

**High-riding humerus after fall** — **cuff failure** → **convert to rTSA**

**Late pain at night, can't do overhead** — **positive empty can test** (SS tear) · repair if possible, else **rTSA**

**Catastrophic glenoid fracture** — humeral head **directly on glenoid bone** (normally gap = poly thickness; poly is radiolucent)

**Cuff tear → glenoid loosening** (cuff deficiency predisposes)

### Infection – C. acnes & work-up

| Risk factor | Not |
|---|---|
| **Male** (more sebaceous glands, more C. acnes; more TSA for post-traumatic/CTA) | Female |
| **Age <65** | Age >65 |
| **Reverse** TSA (vs anatomic) | Anatomic TSA |
| **Inflammatory** or post-traumatic arthritis | Primary OA |
| **Corticosteroid injection within 3 months** (e.g. 2 months) | Injection 4 months before |

**Rate** — C. acnes infection **1–2%** after primary TSA (Orthobullets) · **4.6%** (TO)

**C. acnes (P. acnes)** — **gram-positive, facultative, aerotolerant, anaerobic rod**; ferments lactose → propionic acid · **commonest cause of late/indolent infection + implant failure** · **>50% of chronic infections**; **S. epidermidis 2nd (15%)**

**C. acnes facts** — colonises **shoulder > hip/knee** · **men > women** bacterial burden · biofilm 18–90 h · presentation: early **pain + stiffness**, later swelling + redness · **usually no fever** (subtle)

**Cultures** — hold **14 days** (OB) · **10–14 days** in anaerobic bottles, mean detection **6 days** (Orthobullets TSA note) · **14–28 days** (Orthobullets PJI note) — read the options · 16S rRNA PCR · **sonicate** explanted implant

**Sampling** — **arthroscopic tissue culture 100% sensitive + specific** vs fluoroscopic aspiration **17% sensitive**, 100% specific · aspiration may be **normal** · imaging + in only 24%

**New pain after pneumonia / ↑ WBC + ESR** — **haematogenous** → **radiographs + aspiration with Gram stain + culture** · well-fixed implants → I&D may avoid full revision\
✗ Wrong options: wait 2 wk for repeat labs · PT/NSAIDs/stretching · MRI · emergent revision

| Timing | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Early (<6 wk)** | **Open I&D** |
| **Late (>6 wk)** / grossly infected (frozen >10 WBC/HPF, GPC in clusters) | **Remove components + antibiotic spacer** → **2-stage** reimplantation · IV antibiotics **6 wk** (penicillin G, ceftriaxone, clindamycin, vancomycin) then PO **2–6 mo** |

**Avoid in 2-stage stage 1** — bone grafting glenoid (won't heal with infection; do at stage 2) · resection arthroplasty (poor function) · head exchange/arthroscopic debridement (won't eradicate)

### Neurovascular injury during approach (musculocutaneous, axillary, radial, arcuate artery)

| Structure | At risk when | Deficit / fact |
|---|---|---|
| **Axillary nerve** (most common) | Inferior capsular release, inferior subscapularis, labrectomy; **excessive deltoid retraction** | Lateral shoulder numbness, weak abduction; **10–25 mm** below glenoid rim (↑ with abduction + ER); ≤3 mm from inferior capsule |
| **Musculocutaneous** (lateral cord) | **Retraction on conjoint tendon / strap muscles** (short head biceps + coracobrachialis) | **Weak forearm supination + elbow flexion** (biceps) |
| **Radial nerve** | **Latissimus dorsi tenotomy** for transfer (with rTSA) | — |
| **Arcuate artery** (terminal branch of **anterior humeral circumflex**, main GT supply) | **Elevating biceps tendon from groove** (runs lateral side of groove) | Bleeding |
| Anterior humeral circumflex vessels | Distal dissection at **inferior subscapularis** (peel) | — |
| Suprascapular nerve | ≤1 cm from glenoid rim | — |

**MCN distance from coracoid** — **~3 cm** (OB) · **2.0 ± 0.5 cm** (Apaydin; closest with arm **abducted + IR**) · **3.1 cm** (Boardman) — read the options · enters conjoint **5.8 cm** below coracoid · 0.5 cm from labrum at 90° abduction

**Nerve injury rate** — **1–4%** of shoulder arthroplasty · 58% of iatrogenic injuries fail to recover (Carofino)

**Axillary nerve + posterior humeral circumflex artery** — pass beneath GH joint through **quadrilateral space**

**Latissimus transfer** — routine with **Grammont (medialised)** rTSA for ER deficit (+ Hornblower) · ↓ use with **lateralised** designs

### Periprosthetic fracture – Wright & Cofield

| Type | Location | Intra-op | Post-op | |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | Near **stem tip, extends proximally** (tuberosities) | Span with standard (2–3 cortical diameters) or long stem; transosseous sutures for tuberosity | **Nonop** if minimally displaced · significant overlap = loose stem → **long-stem revision** | ![Wright and Cofield](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob3075-wright-amp-cofield-classification.jpg) |
| **B** | **Centred at stem tip**, extends distally | Span with long stem; cement distal canal (✗ Wrong options: cement escape); strut allograft + cerclage | **Revise to long-stem** | |
| **C** | **Distal to stem tip** | Long stem, or near olecranon fossa → plate ± cerclage, strut | **ORIF**; plate overlaps stem by **2 cortical diameters** (avoid stress riser) | |

**Acceptable alignment** — **≤20° flexion/extension · ≤30° varus/valgus · ≤20° rotation**

**Humeral stem loosening** — more common in **RA + osteonecrosis** → **rule out infection** · uncommon with stemmed primary aTSA

## X vs Y

| | **Anatomic TSA** | **Reverse TSA** |
|---|---|---|
| Cuff | **Must be intact** | Deficient (CTA, pseudoparalysis, anterosuperior escape) |
| Design | Less constrained (shear → loosening) | Convex glenosphere + concave humerus; COR **inferior + medial** → longer deltoid lever |
| **ER at side + IR behind back** | **Better** (✓ reach behind back) | Worse |
| Return to activity | **Quicker** | Slower |
| Forward flexion / PROMs / revision / complications | **Similar** | Similar |
| Typical complication | Glenoid loosening, subscap failure | **Scapular notching**, instability, **acromial stress fracture (~4%)** (over-tensioning) |
| Dislocation | **Anterior** (subscapularis) | **Anterior**; mechanism **extension + IR + adduction** (rising from chair) (TO) · **hyperextension + ER** (another TO) — read the options |

| Post-op exam test | Muscle |
|---|---|
| **Belly-press, lift-off, bear hug, IR lag, ↑ passive ER** | **Subscapularis** |
| Jobe / empty can, drop arm / drop sign | Supraspinatus |
| ER lag sign | Infraspinatus |
| **Hornblower** | Teres minor |
| Yergason | Biceps |
| Loss of passive ER (complete) | **Locked posterior dislocation** |

| Late failure clue | Diagnosis |
|---|---|
| ↑ Passive ER, + belly-press, **anterior** subluxation, early post-op | **Subscapularis failure** |
| **High-riding** humerus, + empty can | **Supraspinatus / cuff failure** |
| Head outside Iannotti circle, attritional SS tear | **Overstuffing (sizing)** |
| Progressive glenoid lucency, biconcave pre-op, normal labs/cultures | **Aseptic glenoid loosening** |
| Pain + stiffness, humeral lucency, normal or ↑ labs | **C. acnes infection** |

## How it's asked

**"TSA absolute contraindication?"** → **upper brachial plexus palsy** (TO)\
✗ Wrong options: SS deficiency · poor glenoid bone stock · retroverted glenoid

**"All-poly glenoid contraindicated in?"** → **irreparable rotator cuff tear**\
✗ Wrong options: RA · OA · osteoporosis · prior resurfacing

**"Putti-Platt 20 y ago, painful stiff shoulder — diagnosis?"** → **post-capsulorrhaphy arthropathy**

**"Contraindication to TSA?"** → **preganglionic brachial plexus injury**\
✗ Wrong options: ER <10° · posterior erosion · 2-cm SS tear · inflammatory arthritis

**"End-stage OA, AAOS CPG — next step?"** → **TSA with all-polyethylene cemented glenoid**\
✗ Wrong options: metal-backed · CTA head · resurfacing

**"Most appropriate aTSA candidate?"** → **72-y male, 9 months post TKA, debilitating pain, MRI shows intact rotator cuff**\
✗ Wrong options: plexus palsy · can't abduct >30° · proximal migration · erosion to coracoid

**"RA, FE 120°, no superior migration — most predictable?"** → **TSA with cemented all-polyethylene glenoid**

**"Favours hemi over TSA?"** → **glenoid wear to level of coracoid**\
✗ Wrong options: age · non-retracted SS tear · cysts

**"aTSA vs rTSA counselling?"** → **aTSA: increased ability to reach behind back**\
✗ Wrong options: equal ER · better FF with rTSA · worse PROMs

**"Primary OA, 7° retroversion — aTSA (Figure E) vs rTSA (Figure F)?"** → **procedure shown in Figure E (aTSA) provides better post-operative range of motion** (similar complications + outcomes)

**"Ideal glenoid component position?"** → **neutral to 10° retroversion** (TO)

**"Eccentrically ream the glenoid when?"** → **retroversion 15°** (TO) · **13°** (OB) · **9°** (OB)

**"Axial CT: retroversion + posterior bone loss, resurfacing planned?"** → **bone grafting of glenoid**

**"Walch B2 — maximum erosion?"** → **posteroinferior (7–8 o'clock), area D**

**"Glenoid augmentation needed for?"** → **retroversion 21°**\
✗ Wrong options: 11° · humeral retroversion · head collapse · acetabularization = rTSA

**"Cause of glenoid loosening?"** → **dysfunctional rotator cuff** (TO)\

**"Rocking-horse effect minimised by?"** → **glenoid radius larger than humeral head radius** (TO)

**"Recommended mismatch?"** → **<4 mm** (TO)

**"Commonest complication 5 y after TSA?"** → **glenoid component loosening** (TO)

**"Pre-op feature most linked to early glenoid loosening?"** → **biconcave glenoid / glenoid morphology**\
✗ Wrong options: osteophytes · age · Shenton's line · cuff atrophy · pegged/curved back · stemless · diabetes

**"Aseptic glenoid loosening 5 y post — true?"** → **pre-op posterior glenoid wear is a risk factor**\
✗ Wrong options: 2nd to infection · lucent lines correlate · central peg uncemented

**"Most likely mode of failure to warn about?"** → **glenoid loosening**\
✗ Wrong options: cuff tear · humeral loosening · fracture · infection

**"Most common muscle ruptured after TSA?"** → **subscapularis** (TO)

**"Landmark for superior subscapularis border?"** → **biceps tendon** (TO)

**"Early rehab restricted to protect?"** → **subscapularis tendon**\
✗ Wrong options: supraspinatus · dislocation · loosening

✗ Wrong options: subscapular nerve palsy · biceps · brachial neuritis

✗ Wrong options: with IR = posterior · lateral numbness = axillary

✗ Wrong options: IR to chest · FE 90° · pendulums · elbow flexion
