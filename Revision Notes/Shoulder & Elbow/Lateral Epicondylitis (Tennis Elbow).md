# Lateral Epicondylitis (Tennis Elbow)

*Shoulder & Elbow · 21 / 60*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Frequency** — **most common cause of elbow pain** · **1–3% of adults/y** · **dominant arm**

**Demographics** — **45–64 y** · **♂ = ♀** · up to **50% of tennis players** · labourers with **heavy tools**, repetitive gripping/lifting

**Risk factors (TO)** — **smoking**, **obesity**, repetitive wrist extension

**Tennis risk factors** — **poor swing technique (backhand)**, **heavy racket**, **incorrect grip size**, **high string tension**

**Association** — **radial tunnel syndrome** coexists: **5%** (Orthobullets, associated conditions) · **up to 15%** (Orthobullets, missed-diagnosis complication) — read the options

### ECRB anatomy – origin, insertion & innervation

**Most common tendon (TO)** — **ECRB**; **ECRB > ECRL** (TO); also ED, EDM, ECU, supinator can be involved\
✗ Wrong options: ECRL · supinator · ED · ECU

| Muscle | Origin | Insertion | Key fact |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ECRB** (extensor carpi radialis brevis) | **Lateral epicondyle** (anterior aspect); **13 × 7 mm diamond** superior to centre of epicondyle | **Base of 3rd MC** | **Deep branch radial n. (fibres of sixth + seventh cervical nerves, C6–C7)**; tendon just superficial to capsule; **deep + posterior to ECRL** |
| **ECRL** | **Lateral supracondylar ridge** (just below BR) | **Dorsal base 2nd MC** | **First fleshy fibres proximal to common extensor tendon** |
| **Anconeus** | **Same attachment site as ECRB** | — | — |
| ED, EDM, ECU | Lateral epicondyle (common extensor origin) | — | — |

| ✗ Distractor | Belongs to |
|---|---|
| Largest cross-sectional area of elbow flexors | **Brachialis** |

## 2 Mechanism

**Overload** — **eccentric overload** at common extensor origin → **microtear of ECRB origin** (± ECRL, ECU)

**Triggers** — **repetitive wrist extension + forearm pronation** · tenodesis effect to optimise grip → ECRB overuse · **backhand** in tennis · hammering

**Underuse (TO)** — **stress shielding** / pain-inhibited **underuse** of ECRB → weakening → **TRUE cause**\
✗ Wrong options: "underuse cannot cause it"

**Shear (TO)** — shearing forces → **fibrocartilaginous ECRB enthesis** → weaker bone junction\
✗ Wrong options: tensile

### Histology – angiofibroblastic tendinosis

**Lesion** — **angiofibroblastic hyperplasia / dysplasia / tendinosis** = **fibroblast hypertrophy** (dense fibroblasts) + **disorganised collagen** + **vascular hyperplasia**

**Inflammation** — **NO inflammatory cells** → "**tendinosis**", tendonitis/epicondylitis = **misnomers**; **failed reparative process** (TO)\
✗ Wrong options: macrophages · neutrophils

**Also present** — **normal tendon histology**, **hypercellularity** (TO), nerve sensitisation

**✗ Histology distractors** — abundant inflammatory cells · neutrophils around microvessels · cellular atypia · chondroblastic proliferation · primarily calcium deposition · osteoblastic rimming · reactive hyperaemia · cystic degeneration

![Angiofibroblastic hyperplasia](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob3082-angiofibro-histo.jpg)

**Nirschl (OB)** — 1,213 patients, 88 operated; immature fibroblastic + vascular infiltration of ECRB origin; **97% improved** after excision + repair

### Stages of lateral epicondylitis (TO)

| Stage | Features |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Acute inflammatory** response; may resolve completely |
| **2** | **Angiofibroblastic hyperplasia** (fibroblasts, vascular hyperplasia, disorganised collagen), hypercellularity → **tendinosis**; **most common stage at presentation** |
| 3 | **Structural failure** → partial or complete **rupture** |
| 4 | Stage 2/3 + **fibrosis**, soft matrix calcification, **hard osseous calcification** |

## 3 Presentation

**Symptoms** — lateral elbow pain with **resisted wrist extension**, **gripping**; **↓ grip strength**

**Tenderness** — **point tenderness at ECRB origin**, few mm **distal to tip** of lateral epicondyle

**Neuro exam** — differentiates from **entrapment syndromes**

| Provocative test | Manoeuvre |
|---|---|
| **Cozen (TO)** | **Resisted wrist extension, elbow extended** (± forearm pronated) → lateral pain |
| **Maudsley** | **Resisted extension of 3rd (long) finger** → lateral epicondyle pain |
| Passive stretch | **Maximal wrist flexion** / passive wrist flexion in pronation |
| ✗ Wrong option: radial tunnel | **Resisted supination** (arm + wrist extended); tenderness **3–4 cm distal + anterior** to epicondyle |

**Rule out** — **LUCL insufficiency** (**chair test, push-up test**) · OCD / arthritis (↓ ROM) · **posterolateral plica** (pain/clicking on passive flexion in pronation + supination)

### Differential of lateral elbow pain in athletes

**Differential** — posterolateral plica · **PLRI** · **radial tunnel** · occult fracture · **cervical radiculopathy** · **capitellar OCD** · triceps tendinitis · radiocapitellar OA · **shingles**

**Tennis player, continuous elbow pain (TO)** — source gives no key; Orthobullets causes = **incorrect grip size**, poor (backhand) technique, heavy racket, high string tension

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

**Diagnosis** — **clinical** (symptoms + exam)

### Key radiology findings

**X-ray (AP + lateral)** — usually **normal**; **calcification near epicondyle (up to ½)**; rarely changes management

**MRI** — **not needed**; **↑ signal at ECRB origin (up to 90%)**, thickening/thinning, oedema · **does not correlate with severity** → does not dictate management · use when diagnosis unclear / refractory

**US** — **thickened, hypoechoic ECRB**; **most useful tool in experienced hands** (operator-dependent)

## 5 Management

```
Lateral epicondylitis
├─ First line → activity modification + eccentric PT / OT ± counterforce strap
│      (NSAIDs, ice; injections second line)
├─ Persistent → injection (PRP better at 12 mo than steroid)
└─ Failed ≥12 mo nonop + clear diagnosis → ECRB release + debridement (open = arthroscopic)
```

### Nonoperative treatment – eccentric therapy, steroid & PRP injections

**Natural history** — resolves with time; recovery **6–12 mo**; **no consistent difference between nonop treatment modalities and placebo** / observation

**Success** — **80–90%** at 1 y, only **2–4%** need surgery (Orthobullets, OB) · **up to 95%** (Orthobullets prognosis, TO) · **>95%** (OB) — read the options

**Eccentric exercise** — **first line**; **eccentric > concentric** (TO); ↓ pain (grip gain unclear); beneficial effect on **tendon biology**

**Occupational therapy (OB)** — activity modification + **eccentric strengthening** + pain management = best initial

**PT at 1 y** — **PT > steroid injection**; **steroid (± PT) = worst outcomes**

**NSAIDs** — short-term benefit only; not an inflammatory condition; ↑ **GI side effects**

| Injection | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **Corticosteroid** | **Short-term (2–6 wk)** ↓ pain + ↑ grip vs placebo (TO); **not beyond 6 wk** · RCT vs placebo: **equivalent** pain, grip, PROMs · **worst at 1 y** · **skin/muscle atrophy**, **tendon attenuation + rupture** · second line / not recommended |
| **PRP** | Orthobullets: **better than steroid** for long-term pain, healing, function · OB: **↓ pain + ↑ PROMs at 12 mo vs steroid**, **no ↑ strength/function**, no faster return to work, not better at 6 wk (steroid better short term) · TO (Cochrane): **no patient-important benefit vs placebo** — read the options |
| PRP type | **Leukocyte-rich** promising (✗ Wrong options: leukocyte-poor best); heterogeneous preparations |
| Others | Botulinum toxin, autologous blood, hyaluronate, acupuncture, ionto/phonophoresis, **nitroglycerin patch** |

**Steroid mechanism** — analgesic on **neuropeptides (CGRP, substance P)** ↑ in tendinopathy; **suppresses fibroblast collagen + ECM** production

**ESWT** — **no difference vs placebo at 6 mo**; + stretching = stretching alone (TO)

**Orthoses (TO)** — **inelastic, nonarticular, proximal forearm strap** → may ↓ pain + ↑ grip at **3 wk**; bracing ≤6 wk may help ADLs · conflicting: not better than sham; may be inferior short term to steroid + topical NSAIDs

**Tennis modifications** — slower surface, more **flexible racquet**, **lower string tension**, **larger grip**

### Operative criteria

| Criterion | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Indication** | Failed **prolonged nonop (12 mo)** · **clear diagnosis** (isolated LE) · intra-articular pathology |
| **Contraindication** | **Inadequate nonop trial** · **noncompliance** with nonop |
| **Poor-outcome / surgery predictors** | **Depression, anxiety, poor coping (greatest)** · ipsilateral **radial tunnel** · **prior injection** · **workers' compensation** |

### ECRB release & debridement

**Open** — incision over common extensor origin → **lift ECRL off ECRB** → **Nirschl scratch test** → excise degenerate tissue → **decorticate** epicondyle → **watertight capsule repair** → side-to-side closure → reattach (anchor / tunnel)\
✗ Wrong options: synovial fistula

**Arthroscopic** — visualises + treats intra-articular pathology · resect lateral capsule **anteriorly** · release ECRB where muscle begins · decorticate · **do not pass mid-radial head**

**Outcome** — **open = arthroscopic** (satisfaction, return to work, complications) · **90–100% improve**, **up to 40% persistent pain**

## 6 Complications

### Iatrogenic LUCL injury after debridement

**Cause** — debridement **posterior to equator of radial head** → **LUCL** injury → **PLRI**

**Safe vs unsafe zones** — anterior + superficial resection safe · **too distal → PIN** at risk

**Presentation** — lateral pain, **clicking/catching**, can't push up from chair / do push-ups → **chair push-up test +**

**PLRI mechanism** — instability with **elbow extension + supination + valgus**

**Chronic iatrogenic PLRI (3 mo)** — **LUCL reconstruction with palmaris longus or gracilis allograft**\
✗ Wrong options: primary repair (chronic) · revision ECRB debridement · MCL procedures (milking manoeuvre/thrower)
