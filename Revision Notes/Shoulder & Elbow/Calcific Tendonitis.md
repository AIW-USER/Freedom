# Calcific Tendonitis

*Shoulder & Elbow · 9 / 60*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Definition** — calcification + tendon degeneration **near rotator cuff insertion** → pain + ↓ ROM; **unknown etiology**

**Age** — **30–60 y**

**Sex** — **♀ > ♂**

**Location** — **supraspinatus** most common · supraspinatus and/or **infraspinatus** (OB)

**Risk factors** — endocrine: **diabetes**, **hypothyroidism**

### Genes & lab markers

**Crystal** — **calcium hydroxyapatite** (Orthobullets, OB) · "calcium carbonate apatite" (one OB explanation) — read the options

**Histology** — hydroxyapatite deposits surrounded by **fibroblasts, macrophages, giant cells, lymphocytes**

**Molecular pathways** — **osteopontin** · **cathepsin K** · **transglutaminase 2**

**✗ Wrong option: calcium pyrophosphate** — **positively birefringent rhomboid/rod** crystals → CPPD / **pseudogout**

**✗ Wrong option: calcium oxalate** — **kidney stones**, urine; excess in **ethylene glycol (antifreeze)** poisoning → renal failure

**Process** — **active, cell-mediated**\
✗ Wrong options: purely degenerative; Uhthoff & Loehr

### Phases of disease (resorptive phase pain)

| Stage (Uhthoff) | Phase | Pathology | Pain |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Precalcific** | — | **Fibrocartilaginous metaplasia**; **tenocytes → chondrocytes** | **Pain-free** |
| **Calcific** | **Formative** | **Cell-mediated** calcific deposits | ± |
| | **Resting** | **No inflammation / vascular infiltration** | ± |
| | **Resorptive** | **Phagocytic resorption + vascular infiltration** | **Most painful** |
| **Postcalcific** | — | Calcium resorbed + **tissue remodelling** | — |

**✗ Resorptive distractors** — "vascular infiltration, painless" (it is painful) · lowest inflammation (= resting) · cell-mediated deposits (= formative) · fibrocartilaginous metaplasia (= precalcific)

**Fibrocartilaginous metaplasia** — only in **calcific tendonitis** among shoulder pathologies\
✗ Wrong options: cuff arthropathy · biceps tenosynovitis · chronic cuff tear · neuropathic arthropathy

**Surgery timing** — avoid surgery in **resorptive** phase (Uhthoff & Loehr) · surgery for refractory cases in **formative** phase

## 3 Presentation

**History** — similar to **subacromial impingement**

**Symptoms** — **atraumatic pain** (worst in resorptive phase) · **catching**, crepitus · mechanical block · pain with overhead activity / active elevation

**Exam** — supraspinatus fossa **atrophy** · **↓ active ROM** · scapular dyskinesia · ± ↓ cuff strength · positive **impingement signs**

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Modality | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Radiographs** | **Gold standard / mainstay**; monitor progression; location, density, extent, delineation |
| **AP, supraspinatus outlet, axillary** | **Supraspinatus** calcification |
| **Internal rotation view** | **Infraspinatus + teres minor** |
| **External rotation view** | **Subscapularis** |
| Deposit site | **1–1.5 cm** from supraspinatus insertion (Orthobullets) · **1–2 cm**, zone of ↓ vascularity, can be multifocal (OB) — read the options |
| CT | Rarely required; 3D anatomy |
| **MRI** | **Low signal on all sequences** (globular, T1 + STIR/T2 hypointense); limited diagnostic use; refractory pain → concomitant cuff tear |
| **Ultrasound** | Deposits **hyperechoic**; quantify extent; **guides needle lavage** |
| MR arthrography alone | Poor sensitivity + inter-rater agreement (Zubler) |

![Calcific deposit over supraspinatus insertion](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob3042-calcific-tendinitis.jpg)

**✗ Wrong options (next tests after failed barbotage)** — bone scan · MR arthrogram · EMG (no added value)

**Imaging ✗ distractors** — no acromial spur (✗ Wrong options: impingement) · no tear (✗ Wrong options: partial/full-thickness tear) · **adhesive capsulitis = clinical diagnosis**, no consistent X-ray findings

### Gartner & Heyer classification

| Type | Appearance | Phase | Image |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I** | **Well circumscribed, dense** | **Formative** | ![Gartner and Heyer types](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob3042-gartner-and-heyer-classification.jpg) |
| **II** | Soft contour/dense **or** sharp/transparent | — | |
| **III** | **Translucent, cloudy**, no clear circumscription | **Resorptive** | |

**Radiographic types (one OB explanation)** — Type I = **fluffy, fleecy, poorly defined** → acute pain, formative phase · Type II = **discrete homogeneous, well defined** → subacute/chronic — conflicts with Gartner (Orthobullets: type I well circumscribed) — read the options

| Type | Appearance |
|---|---|
| **A** | Dense, **homogeneous**, sharp contours |
| **B** | Dense, **segmented**, sharp contours |
| **C** | **Heterogeneous, soft** contours |
| **D** | **Dystrophic** calcification at cuff **insertion** |

## 5 Management

```
Calcific tendonitis
├─ All phases, first line → NSAIDs + PT (stretch/strengthen, moist heat) ± steroid injection
├─ Refractory, formative/resting phase → adjunct ESWT
├─ Persistent, resorptive phase → US-guided needle lavage / barbotage
└─ Progressive, refractory, ↓ ADLs → arthroscopic / mini-open debridement of deposit
       └─ remaining cuff defect → ± rotator cuff repair (controversial)
```

### Treatment – conservative, barbotage & arthroscopic debridement

**Nonop outcome** — symptoms resolve **60–70% at 6 months**; most improve spontaneously

**Failure more likely** — **bilateral or large** deposits · deposit under **anterior third of acromion** · deposit **medial to acromion**

**Steroid injection** — common but **controversial**; variable relief

**Formative phase, 4-month mild pain** — **moist heat + stretching** first \
✗ Wrong options: aspiration (deposit **granular** → hard to aspirate) · ESWT (role poorly defined, OB) · **low-dose radiation** (malignant transformation risk) · arthroscopic debridement (only after failed nonop)

**ESWT** — adjunct; refractory **formative + resting** phases; **dose-dependent**: high-energy > low-energy for outcome scores + resorption, but also > procedural pain + local reaction (ecchymosis)

**Needle lavage** — **2 needles** (inflow saline ± anaesthetic + aspiration outflow); indicated for **resorptive phase**; better outcomes **Gartner II/III vs I**

**Barbotage** — needle breaks up deposit → **corticosteroid injection**

![US-guided two-needle lavage](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob3042-usevan.jpg)

### Operative criteria

| Indication for surgical decompression of deposit |
|---|
| **Progression of symptoms** |
| **Refractory to nonoperative treatment** (incl. failed barbotage) |
| **Interference with ADLs** / work |

**Surgery** — arthroscopic or mini-open removal (shaver or spinal needle) ± SAD ± RCR; **paste-like ("toothpaste") calcium** expressed from cuff

![Arthroscopic paste-like calcium expressed from cuff](../../MCQ/attachments/q8975-d-calcium.jpg)

**After failed barbotage** — **arthroscopic debridement**\
✗ Wrong options: isolated RCR without debridement (won't resolve symptoms)

**RCR after debridement** — **controversial**; depends on remaining tissue integrity + defect size · debridement without RCR safe: **3.7%** cuff tears at 7 y (El Shewy)

**Deposit with osseous involvement** — same results as tendon-only (Seyahi)

### Debridement ± subacromial decompression

| SAD + debridement vs debridement alone | Result |
|---|---|
| **Time to pain-free activity** | **↑ (longer)** with SAD (Marder) |
| Pain at midterm | No difference |
| ROM | No difference |
| Recurrence | No difference |
| Cuff tears | **↑ in operated shoulder vs contralateral** regardless (Balke, 6 y) |

**Debridement + SAD** — 91% good/satisfactory at 26 mo (Ark); SAD gives **no apparent benefit**

**Return to work** — longer with SAD and/or RCR

## 6 Complications

**List** — **recurrence** · **persistent pain** · **stiffness** (operative risk) · **iatrogenic cuff injury** · ↑ cuff tears after surgery

## X vs Y

| | **Formative phase** | **Resting phase** | **Resorptive phase** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Process | Cell-mediated deposition | No inflammation / vascular infiltration | Phagocytosis + vascular infiltration |
| Pain | ± | ± | **Most severe** |
| Deposit (Gartner) | **Type I** dense, well circumscribed | — | **Type III** cloudy, translucent |
| Treatment niche | **ESWT**; conservative; aspiration difficult (granular) | **ESWT** | **Needle lavage**; avoid surgery |

| | **Hydroxyapatite** | **CPP** | **Calcium oxalate** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Disease | **Calcific tendonitis** | **Pseudogout / CPPD** | Kidney stones, ethylene glycol |
| Crystal | Deposits ringed by fibroblasts, macrophages, giant cells | **Positively birefringent rhomboid/rod** | Urine crystals |
| Colchicine | **no role** | — | — |

## How it's asked

**"Calcific deposit on X-ray — structure abnormal at arthroscopy?"** → **supraspinatus**\
✗ Wrong options: infraspinatus · labrum · biceps · GH cartilage

**"35-y ♀, insidious pain, low-signal globule in supraspinatus on MRI?"** → **calcific tendinitis**\
✗ Wrong options: impingement · adhesive capsulitis · cuff tear

**"Fibrocartilaginous metaplasia — which image?"** → **AP X-ray with supraspinatus calcification**\
✗ Wrong options: cuff arthropathy · biceps tenosynovitis · neuropathic arthropathy

**"Best description of resorptive phase?"** → **highest pain levels**\
✗ Wrong options: painless vascular infiltration

**"Formative-phase deposit, mild 4-month pain — initial treatment?"** → **moist heat + stretching**\
✗ Wrong options: aspiration · ESWT · radiation · arthroscopy

**"Debrided material pathology + next step?"** → **calcium hydroxyapatite; debridement ± cuff repair**\
✗ Wrong options: CPP · oxalate · IV colchicine

**"Failed PT, NSAIDs and US barbotage — next?"** → **arthroscopic debridement**\
✗ Wrong options: isolated RCR · bone scan · MR arthrogram · EMG

**"Adding SAD to debridement — effect?"** → **longer time to pain-free activity**\
✗ Wrong options: better pain · ROM · fewer tears · ↓ recurrence

**"Most painful phase?"** → **resorptive**

**"View for subscapularis calcification?"** → **external rotation AP**
