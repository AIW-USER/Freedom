# Pectoralis Major Rupture

*Shoulder & Elbow · 33 / 60*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Incidence** — rare (**<1/100,000/y**) but rising · **75%** of reported cases since 1990

**Demographics** — **almost exclusively ♂, 20–40 y** · **weightlifters**, **bench press** · almost no reports in ♀ (TO)\
✗ Wrong options: equal ♂/♀ · old gym-goers

**Risk** — **anabolic steroids** / supplements

**Other sports (TO)** — rugby, football, wrestling, water skiing, boxing

**Most common site** — **tendinous avulsion at humeral insertion (65%)** > **musculotendinous junction (27%)** (TO) · intramuscular rare (direct trauma, stab)\
✗ Wrong options: "MTJ most common" · muscle belly

**Most common portion** — **sternocostal (sternal) head** (✓ TO "all except" answer) · clavicular head often intact

### Anatomy – sternal head & pectoral nerve innervation

| | **Clavicular head** | **Sternocostal head** |
|---|---|---|
| Origin | **Medial half of clavicle** (+ proximal sternum) | Sternum, **costal cartilages 1–6** (TO), external oblique aponeurosis |
| Size | Smaller; may look confluent with deltoid | **Larger, >80% of volume** |
| Tendon layer at insertion | **Anterior** | **Posterior** (lower sternal fibres) |
| Nerve | **Lateral pectoral (C5–C7)** | **Lateral + medial pectoral** (dual, C5–T1) |
| Rupture | Fails last | **Fails first**, often isolated |

**Innervation** — **lateral pectoral (C5–C7)** = clavicular + upper sternocostal · **medial pectoral (C8–T1)** = lower sternocostal + pectoralis minor (TO, Orthobullets)

**Insertion** — **lateral lip of bicipital groove** (humeral shaft lateral to groove), anterior + posterior layers (TO)

**Action** — **adduction + internal rotation** · forward elevation/flexion lesser (chiefly clavicular head) (TO) · not predominant forward flexion = **anterior deltoid + coracobrachialis**

**Thoracoappendicular muscles** — pec major, pec minor, subclavius, serratus anterior

|---|---|
| Suprascapular (C5–C6) | Supraspinatus, infraspinatus |

## 2 Mechanism

### Mechanism – eccentric bench press

**Mechanism** — excessive tension on **maximally eccentrically contracted** muscle · **downward (deceleration) phase of bench press**\
✗ Wrong options: upward · max elevation · bar on chest

**Position** — **final 30° of humeral extension** (inferior sternal fibres at maximal stretch) · extension + abduction + ER · TO: **sudden forceful abduction + ER of contracted muscle**

**Failure sequence** — **inferior sternocostal fibres** (insert more posteriorly) → superior sternocostal → **clavicular head last**

## 3 Presentation

**History** — **sudden pop / tearing** with resisted adduction + IR · pain, weakness

**Inspection** — **swelling + ecchymosis anterolateral chest wall and/or proximal medial arm** · **loss of anterior axillary fold**, muscle retracted medially · palpable defect accentuated by **resisted adduction** · **"dropped nipple" sign**

**Ecchymosis location** — **anterior brachium/upper arm** → **humeral avulsion** · **chest wall** → mid-substance / MTJ tear (less benefit from surgery)

**Weakness** — **adduction + internal rotation** (± forward flexion); ER, abduction normal; apprehension negative

![Loss of axillary fold, chest asymmetry, upper arm ecchymosis](../../MCQ/attachments/q8788-c-pmchest.jpg)

| ✗ Exam-sign distractor | Lesion |
|---|---|
| Popeye | Proximal LHB tear |
| Medial winging | Long thoracic nerve / serratus |
| Belly-press | Subscapularis |
| ER lag | Infraspinatus |

**✗ Wrong option: axillary fold** — subscapularis and biceps injuries do **not** change the axillary fold

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Diagnosis – MRI

**X-ray** — trauma series (true AP, scapular Y, axillary) · usually **normal** · lost pec shadow or bony avulsion

**MRI** — **investigation of choice / gold standard** · complete vs partial · tear site + retraction (muscle belly vs bone–tendon junction) · needs **dedicated chest-wall sequence** (standard shoulder MRI misses it) · **T2 acute**, **T1 chronic** · intact clavicular head may mask partial sternal tear

**EMG** — no role without nerve injury

### Modified Tietjen classification

| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| I | Contusion / sprain |
| II | Partial tear |
| III | **Complete** tear, by location ↓ |
| III-A | Muscle origin |
| III-B | Muscle belly |
| III-C | **Musculotendinous junction** |
| III-D | Intratendinous |
| III-E | **Tendon avulsion off humerus (no bone)** |
| III-F | Bony avulsion off humerus |

**Tietjen (TO)** — sprain · partial · complete (origin, belly, MTJ, tendon) + added **bony flake avulsion** + **intratendinous**

## 5 Management

```
Pectoralis major rupture
├─ Low-demand, elderly, sedentary → sling, ice, NSAIDs, PT
│      └─ muscle belly / low-grade partial / chest-wall tear → nonop
├─ Acute complete avulsion or MTJ, young/active → open primary repair
└─ Chronic, can't mobilise → allograft reconstruction
       └─ primary repair still possible years later
```

### Operative criteria

| Situation | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Avulsion from humeral insertion** (complete pecto-humeral tear) | **Primary repair** (✓ surgery indication) |
| Acute tear, young athlete / labourer | **Primary acute repair** = gold standard |
| MTJ tear | Repair (Orthobullets, OB) · therapy may be considered (one OB) · may need augmentation/reconstruction (one OB) — read the options |
| Muscle belly / partial / chest-wall ecchymosis | **Nonop** |
| Chest asymmetry alone, no deficit | not an indication |
| Chronic, inadequate excursion | **Reconstruction (allograft)** |

### Treatment – acute repair, chronic allograft reconstruction & nonoperative deficits

**Primary repair** — **open, deltopectoral** · reattach to humerus lateral to bicipital groove · **suture anchors or buttons** (TO) · transosseous with cortical trough · cortical button · anchors (PEAK/all-suture) · all comparably excellent; some evidence **button + trough > anchors**

**Acute vs delayed** — acute easier, less scar, avoids later reconstruction/allograft\
✗ Wrong options: delayed repair after failed nonop

**Best long-term result (sternocostal tear)** — **open repair of sternocostal portion**\
✗ Wrong options: PT/NSAIDs · steroid injection · LHB repair · clavicular repair

**Repair outcome** — reliable strength, return to sport, satisfaction; improves regardless of tear site · most can be repaired primarily, **even chronic**\
✗ Wrong options: majority need allograft

**Chronic reconstruction** — release adhesions superficial + deep (**protect medial + lateral pectoral nerves**) ± fascial release · graft: **Achilles allograft (most common)** (Orthobullets) · **semitendinosus weave** (OB) · gracilis weave · inferior to primary repair, better than nonop

**Chronic weakness 2 y after PT, young labourer** — **allograft reconstruction (semitendinosus weave)**\
✗ Wrong options: latissimus transfer · electrical stimulation · arthrodesis · arthroscopic repair

**Nonop deficits** — **weakness of adduction + IR** (esp. isokinetic adduction, peak torque) · cosmetic defect · delayed recovery · poor satisfaction · ↓ return to sport

**Nonop protocol** — sling in adduction + IR · immediate PROM · AAROM/AROM over 6 wk · strengthening/unrestricted at **2–3 mo**

**Tendon transfer** — not for acute tear (pec major is a **donor** for subscapularis insufficiency)

## 6 Complications

| Complication | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Persistent pain** | **Most common** |
| **Re-rupture** | **5–7%**; fails at **suture–tendon interface** |
| Residual weakness | — |
| Cosmetic deformity | — |
| Nerve injury in reconstruction | Medial + lateral pectoral during deep release |

## X vs Y

| | **Pec major tear** | **LHB rupture** | **Subscapularis tear** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sign | **Lost anterior axillary fold**, dropped nipple | **Popeye** | **Belly-press**, ↑ passive ER |
| Weakness | **Adduction + IR** | — | — |
| Axillary fold | **Lost** | Normal | Normal |

| | **Sternocostal head** | **Clavicular head** |
|---|---|---|
| Torn | **Most often, first** | Last, often intact |
| Nerve | Lateral + medial pectoral | Lateral pectoral |

✗ Wrong options: axillary · musculocutaneous · subscapular · suprascapular
