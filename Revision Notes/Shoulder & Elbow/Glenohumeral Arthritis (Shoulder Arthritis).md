# Glenohumeral Arthritis (Shoulder Arthritis)

*Shoulder & Elbow · 17 / 60*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Incidence** — ↑ with age; mostly **>60 y** · **♀ > ♂**

**Dislocation as risk** — **56%** of primary anterior dislocators have arthrosis at **25 y**

### Aetiology

| Type | Key point |
|---|---|
| **Primary OA** | No known cause · irreversible cartilage loss + **hypertrophic subchondral bone** · flattened head, osteophytes, cysts, **posterior subluxation + posterior glenoid wear** |
| Post-traumatic | Healed articular incongruity (humeral fractures, chronic dislocations) |
| **Dislocation arthropathy** | ↑ with **age >25 y** at dislocation + **posterior** dislocation · **not related to number of dislocations** |
| **Post-capsulorrhaphy** | Over-tightening (e.g. **Putti-Platt**) pushes head one way → eccentric wear; presents 5th decade |
| **Cuff tear arthropathy** | Torn cuff → head migrates up → abrasion against acromion |
| **RA** | Affects **90%** of RA patients · **central glenoid wear + medialisation** · cuff tear 25–50% |
| CPPD / gout | Pyrophosphate (pseudogout) / urate crystals |
| **AVN** | Proximal humerus fracture: **35% 3-part, 90% 4-part** (this topic's Orthobullets note) · chronic dislocation · cuff repair · steroid, EtOH, haemoglobinopathy, Gaucher |
| **Chondrolysis** | After arthroscopy: **radiofrequency**, **continuous intra-articular anaesthetic infusion**, bioabsorbable anchors, contrast · **fewer osteophytes** than OA |
| Neuropathic | Charcot |

**Cuff tears with OA** — **5–10%** (Orthobullets) · full-thickness supraspinatus **<10%** in primary OA (OB) · CTA excluded by definition\
✗ Wrong options: 30–55% = asymptomatic >60 y population

### Anatomy

**Version** — glenoid **~3° retroverted** · humerus **20–30° retroverted**

**Stabilisers** — static: GH ligaments, labrum · dynamic: cuff, rotator interval, biceps

## 2 Mechanism

**Posterior wear + subluxation** — posterior capsule stretched · **anterior capsule + subscapularis contract** → **loss of ER**

**Walch B vs C** — **B**: posterior subluxation **causes** posterior wear · **C**: glenoid retroversion causes wear → subluxation later

## 3 Presentation

**Symptoms** — pain with motion, often none at rest · difficulty sleeping · **loss of ER** (anterior capsule contracture)

**Exam** — ↓ ER, FF, IR · crepitus · catching/squeaking · examine cuff carefully

**Subscapularis tear** — **weak belly-press** + **↑ passive ER** (90° at side)

**Outcome scores** — ASES · Constant · Oxford

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

**Views** — **true AP (Grashey)** + AP + **axillary lateral** (axillary/CT to judge posterior glenoid wear)

| Cause | X-ray hallmark |
|---|---|
| **Primary OA** | JSN, sclerosis, cysts, **inferior "goat's beard" osteophyte**, **posterior glenoid wear**, fixed posterior subluxation |
| Dislocation arthropathy | Large osteophytes, hardware |
| **RA** | **Osteopenia**, marginal erosions, **few osteophytes**, **central wear + medialisation**, ↓ acromiohumeral distance |
| CPPD | **Chondrocalcinosis** |
| Gout | Usually unremarkable; erosions after repeat attacks |
| AVN | Normal early → central head resorption → **crescent sign** → collapse |
| **CTA** | **Superior migration**, ↓ acromiohumeral interval, acromial erosion, superior glenoid loss, **"acetabularisation"** of CA arch, osteopenia |

![Primary OA with inferior goat's beard osteophyte](../../MCQ/attachments/q213974-a-osteophyte-shoulder.jpg)

**CT** — **glenoid morphology** + templating · underestimates full-thickness RCT and fatty infiltration vs MRI

**MRI** — cuff assessment · **less accurate than CT** for glenoid type · **Goutallier IV** fatty infiltration = irreparable cuff

### Glenoid wear patterns (Walch, posterior wear, medialisation in RA)

**Most common OA wear** — **posterior** (or central)\
✗ Wrong options: anterior · superior · inferior

**Inflammatory (RA/JRA) vs OA** — **concentric erosion + medialisation of joint line** · severe erosion → glenoid resurfacing not always feasible → some favour HA\
✗ Wrong options: inferior osteophyte · posterior subluxation · sclerotic glenoid · posterior wear = OA

| Walch | Description |
|---|---|
| **A** (59%) | **Concentric**, head centred · **A1** minor central erosion · **A2** deep central erosion (rim line transects head) |
| **B** | **Biconcave**, asymmetric wear, **posterior subluxation** · **B0** pre-arthritic subluxation · **B1** (17%) posterior JSN, osteophytes, sclerosis, no bone loss · **B2** (15%) posterior rim erosion, **retroversion <25°** · **B3** **monoconcave**, posterior wear, subluxation **≥70%** or retroversion **>15°** |
| **C** (9%) | **Dysplastic**, **retroversion >25°** · **C1** >25° regardless of erosion · **C2** biconcave + posterior bone loss + posterior translation · subtypes **"Lazy J"** + **"Delta"** |
| **D** | **Anteversion** or **anterior** subluxation (<40%) |

![Walch classification](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob3058-walch-classification.jpg)

**B2 trap** — biconcave + posterior subluxation + **10°** retroversion = B2 · same with 25° = **C2** · deep concentric = A2 · monoconcave + 70% subluxation = B3 · anteversion = D

**B2 outcomes** — poorer after arthroplasty · anatomic TSA → instability/early glenoid loosening if retroversion **>27°** or **80%** posterior subluxation → **reverse** often better in older/severe deformity

## 5 Management

```
Glenohumeral arthritis
├─ First line → NSAIDs, PT (capsular stretching), activity modification
├─ Second line → intra-articular corticosteroid (HA/PRP/stem cell: limited evidence)
├─ Young, high demand → arthroscopic debridement / CAM (temporising)
└─ Failed nonop, end stage → arthroplasty by cuff status
       ├─ cuff intact (incl. partial tear) → anatomic TSA
       └─ cuff deficient / >80% subluxation / Walch D → RSA
```

### Operative criteria

| Procedure | Indications | Contraindications / cautions |
|---|---|---|
| **Anatomic TSA** | **Intact cuff** (partial tear OK), glenoid wear, posterior subluxation, failed nonop | **Deficient deltoid or cuff** · active infection · **Charcot** · young high-demand (glenoid loosening) |
| **Hemiarthroplasty** | Young · **RA with irreparable cuff / poor glenoid bone stock** · **AVN without glenoid involvement** | Early failure, poor pain/function in OA (not routine) |
| **Ream-and-run** | Young, **intact cuff, no inflammatory arthropathy** | — |
| **RSA** | **Irreparable/large cuff tear** · CTA · OA/RA with major glenoid pathology · age · **failed arthroplasty** · complex fracture | Higher complication rate than TSA |
| **Arthroscopic debridement** | Mild–moderate OA without structural change · loose bodies · small AVN lesions · synovial chondromatosis | Advanced degeneration |
| **Arthrodesis** | Paralysis · recurrent infection · poor deltoid/soft tissue · brachial plexus palsy · failed repair of instability | — |

### Anatomic TSA vs hemiarthroplasty – indications & cuff status

**TSA vs HA** — TSA: **better pain relief + function** · HA: **↑ revision rate**\
✗ Wrong options: better pain · ↑ blood loss · ↑ instability · ↑ infection

**End-stage OA, cuff strong** — **TSA** = most predictable pain relief\
✗ Wrong options: RSA unnecessary · arthroscopic osteophyte removal · interposition · HA

**Partial articular cuff tear (35%)** — **good candidate for anatomic TSA** (± repair)

**Not anatomic TSA candidates** — 52 y powerlifter (glenoid loosening → HA) · prior aTSA with high-riding humerus (cuff tear → **convert to RSA**) · Walch B **85%** subluxation (RSA if **>80%**) · elderly **Walch D** (RSA)

**Tight anterior structures** — ER **<40–45°** under EUA → **subscapularis lengthening + anterior capsule release**

**TSA outcomes** — 10-y survival **92–95%** · complications: **glenoid/humeral loosening**, infection, fracture, nerve injury, cuff tear · **↑ PJI if within 3 months of arthroscopy** · vs RSA: **↑ IR** + **↑ glenoid loosening**

### Reverse shoulder arthroplasty – cuff deficiency, post-instability arthropathy, RA

**Irreparable cuff + OA (Goutallier IV)** — NOT appropriate = **arthroscopic cuff repair** · appropriate = NSAIDs/injection, PT, activity modification, then HA or RSA after failed nonop

**Elderly, can't elevate arm (CTA), failed nonop** — **RSA** most reliable (Delta III: Constant +42, +65° elevation, **96%** minimal/no pain) · HA = historical standard, non-uniform results

**OA + complete subscapularis tear** — **RSA**\
✗ Wrong options: anatomic TSA: subscap repair fails

**Post-Bankart arthropathy + supraspinatus fatty atrophy + posterior subluxation/erosion** — **RSA**\
✗ Wrong options: HA: glenoid pain persists · TSA: incompetent cuff · arthroscopic release

**RA + cuff tear** — **RSA good outcomes**, even with **reparable** tear · options = TSA + cuff repair or RSA · **TSA > HA** in RA (pain, elevation, ROM) · **etanercept/DMARDs/prednisone: no effect** on RSA outcome · RSA complications in RA **≈ CTA** (not more) · no interposition arthroplasty in elderly

**RSA outcomes** — 10-y survival **~90–95%** · complications: **scapular notching**, infection, instability, nerve injury

### Young patients – nonoperative care & arthroscopic debridement (CAM procedure)

**Young OA first step** — **NSAIDs, cortisone injection, PT / ROM stretching** before any surgery\
✗ Wrong options: TSA · fusion · resurfacing · arthroscopy as first step

**Arthroscopic debridement** — **temporising** · improves pain + ROM · better with **age <50**, little JSN, concentric joint, added subacromial procedure · **~20% convert to arthroplasty within 1 y**\
✗ Wrong options: 45%

| Arthroscopic debridement predictor | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Nonconcentric joint space** / moderate–severe degeneration | **Poor** |
| **Posterior glenoid wear**, ↑ retroversion | **Poor** (↑ failure) |
| **Bipolar** (head + glenoid) OA | Poor |
| Preoperative stiffness, loose bodies | **Good** |
| Prior infection / instability without degeneration | No effect |

**Technique** — treat **both glenoid and humeral head aggressively**\
✗ Wrong options: ream glenoid + spare humerus

**CAM procedure** — young patient · GH debridement, chondroplasty, synovectomy, loose body removal, **humeral osteoplasty (goat's beard excision)**, capsular release, subacromial + subcoracoid decompression, **axillary nerve decompression**, biceps tenodesis

**Inferior humeral osteophyte** — **axillary nerve** entrapment → **teres minor** fatty infiltration\
✗ Wrong options: infraspinatus/supraspinatus = suprascapular · subscapularis = upper + lower subscapular · teres major = lower subscapular ± thoracodorsal

## 6 Complications

| Procedure | Typical complications |
|---|---|
| TSA | **Glenoid / humeral loosening**, infection, fracture, nerve injury, cuff tear (most common group) |
| RSA | **Scapular notching**, infection, instability, nerve; overall > TSA |
| HA | Glenoid wear/pain, **↑ revision** |
| Arthroscopy (pain pump, RF) | **Chondrolysis** |

## X vs Y

| | **Primary OA** | **RA** | **CTA** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Glenoid wear | **Posterior** | **Central, medialisation** | **Superior** |
| Osteophytes | **Large, inferior** | **Few** | — |
| Head position | **Posterior subluxation** | Medialised | **Superior migration** |
| Bone | Sclerosis | **Osteopenia**, erosions | Osteopenia, acetabularisation |
| Cuff | Tear 5–10% | Tear 25–50% | Torn by definition |
| Arthroplasty | **TSA** | TSA (+ repair) or RSA; HA if no glenoid | **RSA** |

![RA: osteopenia, central wear, few osteophytes](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob3058-ra.jpg)

| | **TSA** | **RSA** |
|---|---|---|
| Design | Concave glenoid + convex humerus | **Convex glenoid (ball) + concave humerus** |
| Cuff | Must be intact | Deficient OK |
| 10-y survival | 92–95% | ~90–95% |
| Distinct | ↑ IR, ↑ glenoid loosening | Scapular notching, ↑ complications |

## How it's asked

**"72 y, OA X-ray, neutral ER — where is glenoid wear?"** → **posterior**

**"Inflammatory vs osteoarthritis at arthroplasty — more likely?"** → **medialisation of joint line**

**"Walch B2 description?"** → **biconcave, posterior subluxation + erosion, 10° retroversion**\
✗ Wrong options: 25° = C2 · anteversion = D · deep central = A2 · monoconcave 70% = B3

**"Chance of full-thickness supraspinatus tear at TSA for primary OA?"** → **<10%**

**"TSA vs HA for OA with intact cuff — HA gives?"** → **↑ revision surgery**

**"74 y, end-stage OA, posterior erosion, good cuff strength?"** → **TSA**

**"Best candidate for anatomic TSA?"** → **60 y with 35% partial articular cuff tear**

**"80 y, drop arm, Goutallier IV, GH arthritis — NOT appropriate?"** → **arthroscopic cuff repair**

**"75F, can't elevate arm, cuff arthropathy, most reliable surgery?"** → **RSA**\
✗ Wrong options: HA · resurfacing · debridement · fusion

**"Old cuff repair, weak belly-press, ER 90°, end-stage OA?"** → **RSA**

**"Post-Bankart arthropathy, supraspinatus fatty atrophy, posterior erosion?"** → **RSA**

**"RA on etanercept, 2 cm cuff tear — true statement?"** → **RSA has good outcomes in this cohort**

**"Predictor of poor outcome after arthroscopic debridement?"** → **nonconcentric joint space**

**"40 y labourer, OA, no prior treatment?"** → **NSAIDs, cortisone injection, PT**

**"35 y weightlifter, new pain + stiffness, OA X-ray — next step?"** → **anti-inflammatory drugs + range-of-motion stretching program**\
✗ Wrong options: TSA · HA + interposition · resurfacing · debridement first

**"Inferior humeral osteophyte neuropraxia — fatty infiltration of?"** → **teres minor** (axillary nerve)

**"True about arthroscopy for OA?"** → **↑ failure with posterior glenoid wear**\
✗ Wrong options: 45% conversion at 1 y · >50 y better · bipolar no effect · spare humerus
