# Medial Ulnar Collateral Ligament Injury

*Shoulder & Elbow · 27 / 60*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Definition** — attenuation / rupture of elbow UCL → **valgus instability** in **overhead throwers**

**Who** — originally **javelin** throwers → now mostly **baseball pitchers**; ↑ in high school + amateur pitchers; also water polo, wrestlers

**Skeletal maturity** — **uncommon in skeletally immature** → they get **little leaguer's elbow** (medial epicondyle) · isolated MUCL tear = **young skeletally mature** pitcher

**Risk factors** — **exceeding pitch count / innings limits** · short rest, no off-season · **higher pitch velocity** · poor mechanics · **kinetic chain deficits** (shoulder + core weakness, ↓ shoulder motion)

### Anatomy & biomechanics (anterior bundle, isometry, posterior bundle)

**Carrying angle** — **6–11°** valgus (UCL note) · ♂ 5–10° / ♀ 10–15° (anatomy note) · ~11° ♂ / ~13° ♀ (exam note) — read the options

**Stability** — split evenly **osseous vs soft tissue**; UCL = primary valgus restraint **30–120° flexion** (+ flexor-pronator, capsule)

| UCL component | Facts |
|---|---|
| **Anterior oblique ligament (AOL) = anterior bundle** | **Strongest, most important valgus stabiliser**; origin **anteroinferior medial epicondyle** → **sublime tubercle**, **2.8 mm distal** to ulnar articular margin; mean length **54 mm**; primary **30–120°**; greatest resistance at **~70°** |
| **Posterior oblique ligament (POL) = posterior bundle** | **Greatest change in tension** flexion → extension; **tight in flexion**; fan-like **capsular thickening**; inserts **sigmoid notch**; not subdivided into bands; secondary stabiliser at 30° |
| **Transverse ligament** | **No stability** — originates + inserts on **ulna** (same bone) |

![UCL complex – anterior oblique, posterior oblique, transverse ligament](../../MCQ/attachments/q628-illa-safran-elbow.jpg)

**Terminology trap** — AOL = "anterior bundle" = older "anterior band"

**AOL bands** — **2 bands**: anterior + posterior (Orthobullets, most OB) · **3 bands**: anterior, central, posterior (2 OB explanations) · anterior, posterior, **oblique** (1 OB) — read the options

| AOL band | Strain / tautness | Primary role |
|---|---|---|
| **Anterior band** | **Isometric** through ROM (✗ Wrong options: isokinetic); **taut 0–85°**; tightens at end-range **extension**; most vulnerable **in extension** | **Most important valgus restraint**; primary at **30°, 60°, 90°** |
| **Posterior band** | **↑ strain in flexion** (✗ Wrong options: isometric); **taut beyond 55°**; vulnerable **in flexion** | Primary at **120°** |

**Posterior bundle range** — resists from **60° to full flexion** (one OB) · beyond **90°** (others)

**Axis of rotation / isometric point** — at UCL **origin = anteroinferior medial epicondyle**\
✗ Wrong options: anterosuperior · posteroinferior · sublime tubercle = insertion

| Flexion | Main valgus restraint |
|---|---|
| **0–20°** (extension) | **Bony: olecranon in fossa + radiocapitellar articulation** |
| **~25°** | Olecranon **unlocked** → UCL takes over |
| **30–120°** | **AOL (anterior band)** |
| >90° / max flexion | **POL** |

**Radial head** — important **secondary** stabiliser in extension + flexion

**Dynamic stabilisers** — **flexor-pronator mass** (**FCU, FDS**) — dynamic, not static

## 2 Mechanism

**Acute trauma** — often with **elbow dislocation**

**Overuse** — **repetitive valgus microtrauma** → rupture of **anterior band** of AOL

**Iatrogenic** — **excessive olecranon osteophyte resection** puts MCL at risk

**Associated** — **traction ulnar neuritis** · **posteromedial olecranon impingement (VEO)** · elbow arthritis

### Throwing phase of maximal valgus stress (late cocking/early acceleration)

**Max valgus** — **late cocking → early acceleration**: forearm lags behind arm → valgus while elbow depends on **anterior band**; forces near **tensile limit** of UCL

**Single best phase** — **late cocking** (Orthobullets, several OB) · **early acceleration** (other OB: "highest in early acceleration") — choose **late cocking/early acceleration** if offered; else whichever of the two is listed

**Forces** — valgus up to **64 N·m** · **500 N** compression at radiocapitellar joint · elbow **110° → 20°** · **3000°/s**

| Phase | Key activity / pathology |
|---|---|
| **Wind-up** | Benign for elbow |
| **Early cocking** | Peak **deltoid**; cuff active |
| **Late cocking** | **Max shoulder ER ("layback")**, high torque → **max elbow valgus**; FDS + FCU protect |
| **Early acceleration** | Peak **triceps**; valgus + rapid extension |
| **Late acceleration** | Peak **pec major, latissimus, serratus anterior** |
| Ball release | Not a phase; end of acceleration |
| **Deceleration** | **Eccentric** contraction of all muscles, **highest torque**, "most harmful" → **SLAP, biceps, brachialis, teres minor** (✗ Wrong options: UCL) |
| **Follow-through** | Rebalance; elbow flexors prevent hyperextension; minimal UCL stress |

![Throwing phases – A wind-up, B early cocking, C late cocking, D late acceleration, E deceleration](../../MCQ/attachments/q217958-b-throwing-phases-copy.jpg)

**Figure labels vary** — one figure: **C = late cocking** · another: **B = late cocking** (A early cocking, D deceleration) — read the figure

**Valgus ↓ by** — **flexor-pronator contraction** · **forearm pronation** · **scapular protraction/retraction (trunk-scapular kinesis)** · **reduced velocity** · correct mechanics

**Valgus ↑ by** — **↑ glenohumeral IR torque** · poor mechanics

## 3 Presentation

**History** — acute **"pop"** + pain + can't throw · chronic: **loss of velocity ("zip")**, **loss of control/accuracy**

**Pain** — medial/posteromedial in **late cocking + acceleration** · posteromedial in **deceleration** = **valgus extension overload**

**Ulnar nerve** — paraesthesia ring + small (nerve **stretch** in late cocking) → check subluxation

**Exam** — tenderness at/near **MCL origin** (posteromedial = VEO) · flexor-pronator integrity · **palmaris longus present?** (graft) · throwers may **lack full extension** · examine shoulder + kinetic chain

**Throwing tolerance** — pain-free light throwing / several innings, no trauma → partial injury; complete rupture unlikely

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Diagnosis – moving valgus stress, milking manoeuvre & MRI T-sign

| Test | Technique | Positive / accuracy |
|---|---|---|
| **Valgus stress test** | Elbow **20–30°** flexion (unlocks olecranon) + **humerus ER** + valgus | **50% sensitive** |
| **Milking manoeuvre** | **Pull thumb**, forearm **supinated**, elbow **90°**; supine/seated/standing | **Apprehension, instability or pain at MCL origin** |
| **Moving valgus stress test** | Milking position; valgus while ranging full arc (or valgus at full flexion → **quickly extend**) | Pain/apprehension **70–120°**; **100% sensitive, 75% specific**; **most strongly confirms** |

![Milking manoeuvre – valgus via thumb with forearm supinated](../../MCQ/attachments/q216806-a-milking.jpg)

**Milking (TO)** — **shoulder ER, elbow flexed >90°**, pull thumb (valgus) → medial pain · **moving valgus**: constant valgus while flexion → extension, pain **70–120°** (TO)

**Chronic medial instability (TO)** — repeated valgus (javelin, gymnasts) attenuates MCL · difficulty throwing · **ulnar neuritis** (weak grip, ulnar numbness) from traction/medial overload · **valgus stress view under IITV opens ulnohumeral joint** · small medial epicondyle avulsion/ossification on X-ray

**Pain in mid-arc (70–120°)** — **anterior bundle MCL** · **pain at terminal extension** = **posterior compartment / olecranon osteophytes**

| ✗ Exam distractor | Indicates |
|---|---|
| Pain with **resisted wrist flexion** (± pronation) | **Medial epicondylitis / flexor-pronator strain** |
| **Froment's sign** | Ulnar neuropathy |
| **Hawkins** | Shoulder impingement |
| **Lateral pivot shift**, **chair test**, **push-up sign**, PLRI test | **PLRI (LUCL)** |
| Painful **snapping** medially | **Ulnar nerve or triceps subluxation** |

### Key radiology findings

| Modality | Fact |
|---|---|
| **X-ray AP + lateral** | Often **normal**; loose bodies, **UCL calcification**, **posteromedial osteophyte** (VEO) |
| Oblique views | Olecranon |
| **Stress X-rays (gravity/manual), both elbows** | **Medial opening >3 mm = diagnostic** |
| **MRI** | **Gold standard / mainstay**; thickened ligament (chronic), calcification, midsubstance tear, proximal/distal avulsion, partial **undersurface** tear |
| Plain MRI | **57% sensitive, 100% specific** |
| **MR arthrogram** | **Diagnostic**, more accurate: **92% sensitive, 100% specific** (✗ Wrong options: "lacks specificity") |
| **"Capsular T-sign"** | **Contrast extravasates** around distal (undersurface) tear at sublime tubercle |
| Dynamic US | Laxity under valgus; operator dependent |

### Differential diagnosis

| Diagnosis | Discriminator |
|---|---|
| **MUCL injury** | **Moving valgus +**, milking +, loss of velocity |
| **Medial epicondylitis / flexor-pronator strain** | Pain on **resisted wrist flexion/pronation** |
| **Ulnar neuropathy (isolated)** | Doesn't explain valgus instability |
| **Valgus extension overload** | **Pain at terminal extension**; posteromedial osteophyte |
| **Little leaguer's elbow** (medial epicondyle stress fracture) | **Skeletally immature** |

## 5 Management

```
MUCL injury
├─ Most patients / partial tear / non-thrower → rest 6 wk + PT + throwing program
│      └─ fails & high-level thrower → reconstruction
├─ High-level thrower, wants to continue (pro: often immediate) → reconstruction (docking, autograft)
└─ Young athlete, avulsion tear, no intraligament calcification → repair ± suture augmentation
```

### Non-thrower partial injury – nonoperative care

**First line** — most patients + **partial tears**: **rest / activity avoidance 6 wk** + NSAIDs → **flexor-pronator strengthening** (secondary dynamic stabilisers) + mechanics, peri-scapular + core → **progressive throwing program**

**Outcome** — **42%** return to pre-injury sport at avg **24 wk** · most heal without surgery in non-high-level athletes

**Adolescent acute partial (14-y basketball)** — **activity avoidance 6 wk** \
✗ Wrong options: stretching (full ROM) · arthroscopy (unless loose body) · static ex-fix (brace OK) · rheumatology work-up

**Persistent at 6–12 wk** — advanced imaging (MRI, MR arthrogram, US, stress X-rays)

### Operative criteria

| Procedure | Indication |
|---|---|
| **UCL reconstruction (Tommy John)** | **High-level throwers wanting to continue** · **failed nonop partial tears** willing to rehab · **midsubstance** tears · attritional tears with **calcification** (excision leaves void) |
| **UCL repair** | **Young athletes, avulsion-type tears** (proximal/distal), no intraligament calcification; ± high-strength suture augmentation |
| ✗ Wrong option: repair | **Midsubstance tear** (reconstruction superior) |

**Repair history** — early repairs poor → replaced by reconstruction; newer augmented repair promising

### Reconstruction technique (docking vs Jobe) & return to play

**Goal** — anatomic reconstruction of **anterior bundle** (medial epicondyle → sublime tubercle); **none stronger than native**

**Approach** — **flexor-pronator muscle-splitting** (✓ ↓ morbidity)\
✗ Wrong options: **detachment** (original Jobe; flexor-pronator avulsion risk) · **medial epicondylectomy** in throwers

**Graft** — **ipsilateral palmaris longus autograft** most common/standard · gracilis autograft or allograft · PL vs gracilis both successful (PL possibly ↑ high-level return; gracilis does not ↓ laxity) · revision **autograft 1.6% vs allograft 2.6%**

| Technique | Humerus | Ulna | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Original Jobe (1986)** | Tunnels | Tunnel | **Flexor-pronator tenotomy**, **figure-of-8**, **routine submuscular ulnar nerve transposition** → **abandoned** |
| **Modified Jobe** | **2 connected tunnels, "Y"** | Single tunnel (2 angled holes, sublime tubercle) | **Muscle-splitting**, no routine transposition; graft sutured **figure-of-8** |
| **Docking** | **Single socket**; sutures tied over bony bridge | **Two converging tunnels** | **Best**: **↑ return to sport, ↓ complications**, less nerve handling, **better graft tensioning** |
| **Hybrid interference screw (DANE TJ)** | Docking socket | **Single longitudinal socket + interference screw** | ↓ iatrogenic fracture; docking + ulnar screw = **95% native strength** |
| **Cortical button (Endobutton)** | — | Suspensory | Mostly **revision** |

![Docking technique](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob3079-docking-technique.jpg)

**Docking vs Jobe** — **~15% more excellent results** · **3× fewer complications** · **3–5% less ulnar neuropathy** · RTP **>90%** vs **80–85%** (figure-of-8)

**Biomechanics** — docking + cortical button **stronger** than figure-of-8 + interference screw

**TO technique table** — all use **sublime tubercle** ulnar tunnels · **Jobe**: complete flexor-pronator take-down (historical gold standard), **figure-of-8 3-ply** · **modified Jobe**: flexor-pronator split, figure-of-8 3-ply · **docking**: graft ends in **blind humeral tunnel**, tied over bone bridge, **2-ply → easier but weaker** (TO) — conflicts with "docking stronger" above · TO lists ulnar nerve transposition as **obligatory** in all three — read the options

**Calcified attritional UCL in high-level thrower** — **reconstruction (modified Jobe)**\
✗ Wrong options: original Jobe · repair ± augmentation · arthroscopic debridement + repair

| Return to play | Rate |
|---|---|
| Modern reconstruction | **~90%** (Orthobullets) · **85–95%** (OB) — answer **80–95%** |
| All athletes (systematic review) | **86%** |
| **Collegiate** | **95%** |
| Surgery overall good results | 84–94% |
| **Revision** reconstruction | **Worse** |

**Rehab timeline** — early wrist/elbow/shoulder ROM + core/hip · strengthening **4–6 wk** · **no valgus stress until 4 mo** · throwing program **4 mo** · **competition 9–12 mo**

### Ulnar nerve management & postoperative neuritis

| Pre-op ulnar status | At UCL reconstruction |
|---|---|
| **No symptoms** | **No routine decompression/transposition** (in situ release at most) |
| **Symptoms** (cubital tunnel) | Decompression ± transposition |
| **Subluxation** | **Transposition** |

**Routine transposition** — avoid → **↑ post-op ulnar neuropathy**, worse outcomes

**Midsubstance tear + cubital tunnel symptoms + subluxation** — **autograft reconstruction + ulnar nerve transposition**\
✗ Wrong options: repair · reconstruction without nerve

**Ulnar nerve in forearm** — **between FCU and FDP**\
✗ Wrong options: medial to brachial artery / most volar in carpal tunnel = median

### Postoperative rehabilitation – forearm position

| Repair | Elbow extension exercises with forearm in |
|---|---|
| **MUCL reconstruction** | **Supination** |
| **LUCL repair** (± radial head replacement) | **Pronation** |
| Combined medial + lateral | **Neutral** |

## 6 Complications

**Ulnar neurapraxia** — **most common**; **3–26%** (Orthobullets, OB) · "<10%" (one OB) — read the options; **treat by observation** (most resolve weeks–months)

**↓ Neurapraxia by** — muscle-splitting · **more superior exiting proximal humeral tunnels** · selective transposition · docking

**MABC injury** — crosses **distal** incision

**Fracture** — ulna or medial epicondyle; **small bone bridge** → ORIF / larger fixation

**Stiffness** — **HO** → early therapy; HO excision ~**6 mo**

**Others** — failure to regain pre-injury level (esp. **revision**) · infection · flexor-pronator avulsion (detaching approach)

**Not MUCL complications** — PLRI / radial head + coronoid tip fractures (terrible triad, LCL)

|---|---|---|---|

| Taut | **0–85°** | **>55°** | **>90°** / max flexion |
| Role | **Primary valgus restraint** | Primary at 120° | Secondary; max flexion; cubital tunnel floor |

| | **Original Jobe** | **Modified Jobe** | **Docking** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flexor-pronator | **Detached (tenotomy)** | **Split** | **Split** |
| Ulnar nerve | **Routine submuscular transposition** | Not routine | Minimal handling |
