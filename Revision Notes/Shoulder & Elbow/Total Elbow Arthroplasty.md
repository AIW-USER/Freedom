# Total Elbow Arthroplasty

*Shoulder & Elbow · 56 / 60*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology & forms of elbow arthroplasty

**Trend** — **TEA for trauma** (elderly distal humerus fractures) = one of the **fastest-growing** indications; historically for **RA**

**Forms** — **TEA** · **hemi** (radiocapitellar, distal humeral) · **ulnohumeral distraction / interposition arthroplasty** · **olecranon fossa debridement** · radial head arthroplasty

### Implant design (linked, semiconstrained, unlinked) & survivorship

| | **Unconstrained (unlinked, resurfacing)** | **Semiconstrained (linked)** | **Constrained** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Example | **Ewald capitello-condylar** | **Coonrad-Morrey** | **Dee** |
| Hinge | None | **"Sloppy hinge": 7° varus-valgus + 7° axial rotation** | **Rigid hinge** |
| Needs | **Competent collaterals + soft-tissue envelope**, good bone, **precise alignment** | **collaterals not needed**; radial head not needed | — |
| Use | — | **Most commonly used** | **No longer used** |
| Results | No proven superiority | **Best results** of all designs | **Highest loosening** |
| Commonest failure | **Instability** (5–10% dislocation) | Instability (Orthobullets, TO) · bushing wear (OB) | **Aseptic loosening**, then **bushing wear** |

**Semiconstrained laxity** — ↓ stress at **bone–cement interface** → ↓ **aseptic loosening**, at the cost of **bushing wear**

**Constrained** — no coronal/rotational flexibility → **high shear stress → early loosening**; theoretically most stable

**Unconstrained suits** — humeroulnar disease with **intact collaterals + radiocapitellar joint**: OA, post-traumatic arthritis, acute intra-articular distal humerus fracture, distal humerus malunion, bony ankylosis

**Unconstrained relative contraindication** — **late-stage RA** (capsuloligamentous instability + bony erosion); RA → **linked**\
✗ Wrong options: unlinked: tendon injury → instability

![Semiconstrained (linked) TEA: humeral + ulnar implants, bushings, locking pin](../../MCQ/attachments/TO17110333-1.jpg)

### Design pearls

**Stems** — humeral + ulnar stems → better fixation, ↓ loosening

**Anterior humeral flange** — extracortical; resists **posteriorly directed + rotational** forces; designs without flange → **early humeral loosening**; improper flange placement → humeral loosening (TO)

**Radial head** — **not needed for stability** in linked designs; often debrided/resected in RA

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Imaging | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **AP + lateral elbow** | Bone stock, **medial + lateral columns intact**, **canal diameter** |
| **C-spine flexion–extension** | **RA** patients (pre-op) |
| CT | Bone stock / fracture |
| **True AP in full extension** (post-op) | **Bushing wear**: humeral yoke–ulnar component angle **> 7° = wear**, **> 10° = extensive/complete** (TO) · **> 10°** concerning (Orthobullets) |
| **Gruen zones** (4) | Zone 1 periarticular, zones 2–4 = stem thirds; **diffuse lucency ≥ 3 zones**, **focal ≤ 2 zones** |

**Semiconstrained laxity (TO)** — **7° total = 3.5° each direction**; **> 7° = partial wear**; **> 10° = complete wear**

![Bushing wear — varus-valgus tilt of ulnar component on AP](../../MCQ/attachments/q219883-illa-ap-elbow-bushing-wear.jpg)

### Cement mantle quality (Morrey, TO)

| Quality | Radiolucent zone | Cement beyond stem tip |
|---|---|---|
| Adequate | **< 1 mm** | Yes |
| Marginal | ≥ 2 mm | Yes |
| Marginal | < 2 mm | No |
| Inadequate | **≥ 2 mm** | **No** |

### Humeral bone loss grading

| Grade | Humeral bone stock |
|---|---|
| I | Articular surface damage only; **intact subchondral bone** |
| II | **Absent trochlea**; intact medial + lateral epicondyles |
| III | **Absent medial or lateral epicondyle** |
| IV | **Loss of both epicondyles** or resection **proximal to olecranon fossa** |

### Mayo (O'Driscoll & Morrey) periprosthetic fracture classification

| Type | Characteristics | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **I** (**commonest**, TO) | **Periarticular — humeral condyle or olecranon**; osteolysis around hinge + muscle distraction | Undisplaced → **immobilise / soft-tissue repair** (fibrous union OK) · displaced → **ORIF heavy nonabsorbable suture or TBW** |
| **II1** | Along stem | **ORIF, retain components ± strut allograft** |
| **II2** | Along stem, **loose implant, good bone stock** | **Revision long-stem ± strut allograft + impaction grafting** (± locking plate / cerclage) |
| **II3** | Along stem, **loose implant, severe bone loss** | **Revision + extensive allograft**; often **resection arthroplasty** |
| **III** | **Distal to prosthesis tip** | Well-fixed → **immobilise humerus, ORIF ulna** · loose → treat as **II2** |

**Basis** — modelled on **periprosthetic femoral** fracture classification; site + implant fixation + bone stock

## 5 Management

### Operative criteria – RA, elderly distal humerus fracture, failed interposition

| Indication | Criteria |
|---|---|
| **Rheumatoid arthritis** | **10–20%** of RA have elbow disease; **Larsen 3–5** (Orthobullets) · grade III–IV (OB) + pain, functional loss, instability; ideally **> 65 y**; synovectomy for less advanced disease |
| **Primary OA (advanced)** | **> 65 y**; **mid-arc pain** from ulnotrochlear cartilage loss |
| **Fracture** | **Physiologically elderly (> 70 y)**: acute complex **unreconstructable intra-articular distal humerus** fracture, missed fracture-dislocation, **poor bone** |
| Post-traumatic OA (advanced) · chronic instability | — |
| **Failed interposition arthroplasty** | End-stage arthritis + instability → **semiconstrained TEA** |

**Contraindications** — **absolute**: **active infection** (→ arthrodesis), **Charcot joint** · **relative**: poor neurological control (UE paralysis), **active patient < 65 y**, **olecranon osteotomy** · RA list (OB): active/**historical** infection, paralysis, won't follow instructions

**Olecranon osteotomy** — contraindication: preserve **extensor mechanism**; trans-olecranon approach **jeopardises ulnar component stability** → never for TEA

**Not contraindications** — central comminution · **ulnar nerve transposition** · radial head impaction · retrocapitellar comminution (radiocapitellar joint secondary)

```
Elderly unreconstructable distal humerus fracture
├─ Physiologically elderly, low demand, poor bone → TEA (semiconstrained)
│      └─ approach → direct posterior triceps-sparing
└─ not ORIF (unstable fixation), acute fusion or anterolateral approach
```

**Fracture TEA vs ORIF** — **faster recovery + more predictable function**; elderly accommodate limits, fewer complications\
✗ Wrong options: slower recovery

**Anterolateral approach** — brachialis/brachioradialis (radial nerve between) → **no access for TEA**

**Prior radial head resection in RA** — **semiconstrained > unconstrained** (both better in native elbows)

**Failed interposition, 60-yo sedentary** — **semiconstrained TEA**\
✗ Wrong options: unconstrained fails with ligament instability · revision interposition · Outerbridge-Kashiwagi · arthroscopic osteocapsular arthroplasty

### Survivorship & outcomes

| Indication | Outcome |
|---|---|
| **RA** | **Longest survivorship** of all indications (> fracture, flail elbow, OA, post-traumatic); **92.4% revision-free at 10 y**; **> 90% up to 15 y** |
| RA complications | **14%** (Orthobullets) · **~15%** (OB) — triceps avulsion, deep infection, periprosthetic fracture, aseptic loosening (✗ Wrong options: "< 5%") |
| **Primary OA** | **10-y survival 80–85%**; more active → **↑ aseptic loosening + stem fracture vs RA** |
| Overall | **> 80% at 10 y** |
| **Post-traumatic** | Functional ROM, satisfaction at 5 y; **complications 27–43%**, **re-operation 25%** |

**Longest survivorship combination** — **RA + semiconstrained**

**Linked TEA, OA vs RA** — OA **↑ aseptic loosening**; **instability equal** (linked)

### Surgical approach & triceps insufficiency

| Approach | Technique | Key fact |
|---|---|---|
| **Triceps-reflecting (Bryan-Morrey)** | Triceps + **anconeus reflected medial → lateral**; reattached to ulna with **nonabsorbable suture through bone tunnels** | **Triceps failure**; **↓ extension power up to 30%**; **delayed ROM** |
| Triceps tongue | Fascial tongue raised from olecranon proximally; release collaterals | Fractures or TEA |
| Triceps-splitting | Longitudinal split in continuity with forearm fascia; or **V-shaped turndown** (insertion intact) | Allows **extensor lengthening** |
| **Triceps-sparing (e.g. Alonso-Llames, para-olecranon)** | Mobilise medial + lateral triceps borders; insertion retained | **Best for acute fracture TEA**; **earlier ROM**, ↑ final ROM; **↓ ulnar visualisation** |
| **Triceps "on"** | Midline incision; release/protect **ulnar nerve**; release flexor-pronator + MCL, common extensors + LCL; elevate triceps off posterior humerus; dislocate ulnohumeral joint | **No triceps release → no insufficiency** |
| Trans-olecranon | Osteotomy, triceps stays on olecranon | **ORIF only**, never TEA |

**Triceps-off vs triceps-sparing** — triceps-off: **delayed ROM**, ↑ triceps failure · **no difference**: cement mantle, ulnar nerve palsy · sparing: ↑ final ROM

**Bryan-Morrey (1982)** — initial 49 patients no triceps failure; later series ↑ failure

**TO approaches** — splitting/reflecting keep triceps attached to periosteum + forearm fascia (± olecranon wafer); **triceps-preserving (bilateral tricipital)** = exposure by ulnohumeral dislocation, **useful in severe RA**; no consensus on best approach

**Post-op protection (triceps-reflecting)** — **restrict active extension** early; **6–8 wk** before **resisted extension**\
✗ Wrong options: active flexion · supination · passive motion

### Technique

**Positioning** — **supine** (arm over chest, assistant; avoid ETT) or **lateral decubitus** over bolster (no assistant, ↓ manipulation)

**Humerus** — resect **olecranon fossa**, **keep medial + lateral columns**; **bone wedge behind anterior flange**; orient to **posterior flat surface of distal humerus**

**Ulna** — resect **olecranon tip** + **coronoid tip** (prevents impingement on flange → **axial pistoning + loosening**); orient **perpendicular to dorsal flat olecranon**; centre of **greater sigmoid notch**, rotation from **posterior olecranon cortex** (TO)

**Alignment (TO)** — **implant–medullary canal parallelism** (Figgie); COR displacement limit **5 mm**; axis: **anterior humeral cortex** (AP), **roof of coronoid fossa** (depth), **condylar plane** (coronal rotation)

**Pistoning (TO)** — check coronoid, protruding cement, HO on trial motion

**Stability check (TO)** — joint line should separate **≤ 1–2 mm** on distraction at **90° flexion + pronation**; **UCL + extensor mass** contribute most

**Cement** — modern cementing; semiconstrained commonest

### Post-op care & lifelong lifting restrictions

**Immobilisation** — **all protocols acceptable** (immediate motion, or splint 60–90° for 2–5 d, 5–10 d, ≥ 10–14 d → active flexion + gravity-assisted extension); historically 4 wk; immediate motion safe

**Evidence** — **earlier ROM → fewer complications** · **prolonged immobilisation → higher implant survival** (Orthobullets); complications **lowest without immobilisation** in RA (OB)

**Lifting limit** — **5–10 lb** lifelong (Orthobullets, OB) · **≤ 5 lb** (OB) — read the options

**Avoid after TEA** — impact sport (golf, tennis) · immediate aggressive triceps strengthening · **immobilising wrist/hand 4 wk** (mobilise immediately) · avoiding pronation/supination (doesn't stress ulnohumeral implant)

**Patient selection** — > 65 y, low demand, able to obey restrictions ("none do"), **medical optimisation**

## 6 Complications

| Complication | Rate / fact |
|---|---|
| **Aseptic loosening** | **Radiographic 17%, clinical 6%** (Orthobullets) · clinically significant **7–15%** (TO) · **< 10%, usually > 10 y** (OB); **commonest failure of constrained** |
| **Infection** | **8%** (Orthobullets) · **3–8%** (TO) · **5–8%**, within first 5 y (OB); **commonest early failure** |
| **Instability** | **7–19%**; **commonest failure of semiconstrained** (Orthobullets, TO) and unconstrained |
| **Bushing wear** | Constrained + semiconstrained; isolated exchange **1–6%**, mean **7.9 y**; **commonest cause of revision / mechanical failure of semiconstrained** (OB) |
| **Periprosthetic fracture** | **5–30%** of primary TEA; **trauma** commonest cause |
| Component fracture | Humeral **0.65%** · **ulnar 1.2%** (ulna > humerus); usually **ulnar**, late |
| **Triceps insufficiency** | **2–8%**; complete disruption **< 2%** |
| **Ulnar neuropathy** | Paraesthesia **< 40%**, resolves **2–6 wk**; radial nerve less often |
| Wound healing | ↑ with **long-term steroids** |

### Complications – instability & component loosening (TO)

**Unconstrained, 3 months post-op (TO)** — **instability**\
✗ Wrong options: ulnar loosening · infection · fracture

**"True about TEA" (TO)** — **loosening most commonly humeral component** ✓\
✗ Wrong options: instability commonest in semiconstrained (it is commonest in **unconstrained**) · loosening commonest in unconstrained · periprosthetic fracture humerus > ulna

**Loosened component** — **humeral** (TO answer; historically commonest revision cause, now **< 5%** in semiconstrained) · **ulnar** with current flanged designs (another TO) — read the options

**Semiconstrained commonest failure** — **instability** (Orthobullets, TO) · **bushing wear** (OB) · humeral loosening historically (TO) — read the options

**Instability treatment (TO)** — collateral reconstruction usually fails → **revision to semiconstrained hinge**

**Constrained, commonest failure** — **aseptic loosening** > bushing wear > infection (8%); instability = unconstrained/semiconstrained; ulnar neuropathy < instability

### Infection

```
Infected TEA
├─ Acute (< 30 d) → serial I&D + antibiotic beads ± poly exchange
│      └─ S. epidermidis → implant removal + IV antibiotics
└─ Chronic → two-stage reimplantation (~3 months)
       └─ medically ill / poor bone stock → resection arthroplasty
```

**Acute window** — **< 30 days** (Orthobullets) · I&D + **polyethylene exchange within 6 wk** if components stable (OB) — read the options

**Organisms (TO)** — **S. aureus + S. epidermidis**; **S. epidermidis** = encapsulating, **biofilm** → persistent → **implant removal + IV antibiotics**

**Frail 78-yo RA, WBC 25,000, S. epidermidis** — **resection arthroplasty**\
✗ Wrong options: lifelong suppression (only indolent e.g. **C. acnes**) · I&D + poly exchange (not acute) · one-stage (**50%** success only) · two-stage at 3 wk (wait **~3 months**)

**Early failure, semiconstrained post-traumatic** — **infection**\
✗ Wrong options: loosening > 10 y · bushing wear = intermediate · component fracture = late · ulnar neuropathy = not implant failure

### Triceps insufficiency

**Bryan-Morrey, 4 wk, "pop" on resisted extension** — **triceps repair failure**\
✗ Wrong options: ulnar component fracture · axle disengagement · periprosthetic fracture (rare without major trauma, late)

**Can't extend at 6 wk after soft-tissue repair** — **Bryan-Morrey**\
✗ Wrong options: triceps-sparing · triceps-on · trans-olecranon · Alonso-Llames

**Triceps roles (TO)** — **sole overhead extension** + thick **soft-tissue cover** (↓ wound problems, infection); prevention = meticulous approach + repair

### Bone loss & salvage

**Causes** — multiple revisions, fractures, osteolysis

**≤ 8 cm distal humeral loss** — **longer prosthesis with extended anterior flange**; severe → **endoprosthesis (total humerus)**

**Salvage** — flail elbow, amputation, arthrodesis
