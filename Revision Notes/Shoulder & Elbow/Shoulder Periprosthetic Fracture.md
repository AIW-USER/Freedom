# Shoulder Periprosthetic Fracture

*Shoulder & Elbow · 49 / 60*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Incidence** — **0.6–3%** of shoulder arthroplasty (Orthobullets, OB) · **1.5–3%** in stemmed anatomic TSA (OB) · age **50–85**

**Share of complications** — **20% of all complications** (intra-op fractures, Orthobullets) · overall arthroplasty complication rate **11%**; post-op periprosthetic fractures = **6.6%** (reverse) vs **1.2%** (anatomic) of complications

**Timing** — **most are intra-operative**, at the index implant (anatomic + reverse); stemless: few post-op reports

**Location** — **adjacent to stem tip** · **greater tuberosity** (esp. **revision** TSA)

**Risk factors** — **osteoporosis** · **aggressive reaming** · endosteal notching · incorrect reaming entry (ideal **9 mm lateral** to anatomic centre of rotation) · **press-fit > cemented** stems · revision · soft-tissue contracture · instability history · ♀ · post-traumatic arthritis · ↑ comorbidity · osteonecrosis (**steroid** > alcohol, post-traumatic)

## 2 Mechanism

### Intra-operative

**Revision TSA** — highest risk: greater exposure, subacromial/cuff scarring → ↑ retraction + torque

| Intra-op step (revision) | Share of intra-op fractures |
|---|---|
| **Removing previous implant** | **Up to 81%** |
| Reaming / broaching | Up to 31% |
| Trialing / insertion | Up to 19% |
| Exposure (forceful retractor) | Up to 15% |

**Implant removal** — metaphyseal-filling → GT fracture (cement removal, interface disengagement) · diaphyseal-engaging → GT fracture (**metaphyseal stress shielding**) · prevent: clear lateral soft tissue, implant-specific tools, **controlled corticotomy**

**Primary TSA** — lower risk · **endosteal notching from reaming** · **excessive humeral ER during exposure** · cortical breach, esp. osteopenic bone

### Post-operative

**Fall (FOOSH)** — **stem tip = stress riser** → most post-op fractures **at/adjacent to stem tip** · fatigue fracture through stress riser

**Atraumatic** — **loosening** → implant "rattling" weakens cortex

**Nonunion risk ↑** — prosthesis **disrupts endosteal blood supply** · **stem distracts fragments** (no end-to-end healing)

**Nonunion factors** — osteoporosis · **RA** · ♀ · **displacement >2 mm** · **cement at fracture site**

## 3 Presentation

**History** — fall on outstretched hand · **prodromal / worsening pain** = mechanical loosening

**Exam** — **varus/valgus deformity**, ecchymosis, shortening, diffuse tenderness · can't move elbow/shoulder (**pain**) · document full NV exam

**Radial nerve** — check status; palsy common with distal humerus fracture, **mostly neurapraxia**\
✗ Wrong options: surgery for it

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

**X-ray** — orthogonal humerus: **AP, axillary lateral, Grashey, scapular Y** · fracture at **stem tip**, usually **oblique** · decide on **stem stability** + **zones of lucency** (no ingrowth / osteolysis)

**CT (metal suppression)** — concern for **loose stem** (pattern, prodromal pain) or comminution · morphology, bone stock, cuff muscle quality, glenoid version (if revising)

## 5 Management

```
Post-operative periprosthetic humerus fracture
├─ Loose stem (any type) → revision arthroplasty + supplementary fixation
│      (poor stock → long stem bypassing 2–3 cortical diameters; good stock → shorter stem + fixation)
├─ Stable stem, type A/B → ORIF: hybrid locking plate + cerclage cables
│      (long spiral/oblique A/B → bracing possible, union <50%)
└─ Type C → fracture brace (nonop)
       └─ fails / nonunion → ORIF ± allograft strut
```

```
Intra-operative fracture
├─ Type A → stem spanning fracture by 2 cortical diameters
├─ Type B / C → proximally porous-coated long stem, 2–3 cortical diameters
└─ Type C unsuitable for long stem → ORIF
```

### Operative criteria

| Situation | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Long oblique / spiral A or B, stable stem** · **type C** | Immobilisation (coaptation → **functional brace**); **union <50%** |
| **Intra-op type A** | Longer stem spanning **2 cortical diameters** ± supplementary fixation |
| **Intra-op type B or C** | **Proximally porous-coated long stem**, **2–3 cortical diameters**; distal cement |
| **Post-op transverse A/B, stable stem** · C failed nonop · can't tolerate nonop | **ORIF** |
| **Loose prosthesis, any type** | **Revision arthroplasty + supplementary fixation** |
| Poor bone stock (loose) | **Long stem** bypass ≥2–3 cortical diameters ± allograft |
| Good bone stock (loose) | **Short stem** (ends proximal to original) + fixation; tip doesn't distract fracture |

**Source nuance** — long spiral **type B**, stable stem: bracing possible (Orthobullets) · **ORIF hybrid plate + cables** best for active patient (OB) — read the stem

### Stable-stem fractures – ORIF with plate/cerclage & radial nerve risk

**Best for stable stem A/B** — **ORIF hybrid locking plate + cerclage cables**\
✗ Wrong options: revision (stem well fixed) · **lag screw + neutralisation** · nonop near stem tip → nonunion

![Hybrid locking plate and cerclage cables](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob422742-screen-shot-2020-04-12-at-5-28-43-pm.jpg)

**Construct** — plate placed **eccentrically** → locking screws skive past stem (bicortical) · **cables proximal** to stem tip · **screws distal** · usually **4.5 mm LC-DCP** · alternatives: dual plates, **3.5 mm attachment plates** · deficient cortex → **cortical strut allograft** + cables (2 hemicylinders = "sarcophagus")

**Approach** — **anterolateral** (extend deltopectoral: coracoid → DP groove → lateral biceps) · biceps–brachialis interval, **split brachialis** (medial = musculocutaneous, lateral = radial) · **posterolateral** for distal fracture with stable stem

**GT / calcar fracture** — suture or cable fixation

**No nonop with sling / hanging arm cast** — bracing is the nonop method; active patient wants fast return → ORIF

**No APC (allograft-prosthetic composite)** — not with good bone quality + no bone loss

**Evidence** — Kurowicki: 7 type B ORIF without revision → **all united** · Andersen: ORIF 17/17, revision 18/19 united · stable IM stem + supplementary fixation > loose stem

### Radial nerve and cerclage

| Landmark | Distance to radial nerve at spiral groove |
|---|---|
| Coracoid | ~13 cm |
| Distal lesser tuberosity | ~10 cm |
| Superior latissimus insertion | ~8 cm |
| **Inferior latissimus insertion** | **~4 cm** |

**Cable 4 cm below inferior LD insertion** → **iatrogenic radial nerve injury**\
✗ Wrong options: median (medial septum) · axillary (proximal) · subscapularis · brachial artery (superficial, anteromedial)

**Safe zone** — cables **just inferior / proximal to inferior edge of LD insertion**

![Radial nerve in spiral groove](../../MCQ/attachments/q213101-illa-radial-nerve.jpg)

### Long stem technique

**Porous-coated long stem** — proximal coating = metaphyseal stability · **pack cement distally**, **avoid cement extrusion into fracture** · strut + cables if deficient · **olecranon fossa** may prevent it

## 6 Complications

**Complications** — **nonunion** · delayed union · **GH stiffness** · **radial nerve injury** (fracture or **iatrogenic cerclage**)

**Prognosis** — high union when treated appropriately; union **2–8 months** · **significant ↓ shoulder ROM**

## X vs Y

| | **Stable stem** | **Loose stem** |
|---|---|---|
| Clues | No lucency, no prodromal pain | Lucent zones, **prodromal pain**, "rattling" |
| Type A/B | **ORIF hybrid plate + cables** | **Revision long stem** + fixation |
| Type C | Brace → ORIF if fails | Revision |

## How it's asked

**"Fall, stable rTSA stem, type B fracture — best treatment?"** → **ORIF hybrid locking plate + cerclage cables**\
✗ Wrong options: revision cemented/cementless long stem · lag screw · nonop

**"Cable placed 4 cm inferior to latissimus insertion — complication?"** → **iatrogenic radial nerve injury**\
✗ Wrong options: median · axillary · brachial artery

**"Active 69-year-old, T-score −0.45, long spiral B, stable stem — treatment?"** → **ORIF plate + screws/cerclage cables**\
✗ Wrong options: sling · hanging cast · revision · APC

**"Loose stem with any fracture type?"** → **revision arthroplasty + supplementary fixation**

**"Intra-op fracture — stem should bypass by?"** → **2 cortical diameters** (2–3 for type B/C)

**"Why high nonunion near stem tip?"** → **disrupted endosteal blood supply** + stem distracts fragments

**"Most common step causing intra-op fracture in revision?"** → **implant removal** (up to 81%)

**"Type C fracture, stable stem?"** → **fracture brace**\
✗ Wrong options: sling
