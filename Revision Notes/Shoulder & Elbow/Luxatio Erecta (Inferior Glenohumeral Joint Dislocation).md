# Luxatio Erecta (Inferior Glenohumeral Joint Dislocation)

*Shoulder & Elbow · 25 / 60*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Incidence** — **very rare: ~0.5%** of all shoulder dislocations (anterior + posterior far more common)

**Definition** — **inferior** GH dislocation; head trapped **under coracoid + glenoid**

### Anatomy – static glenohumeral ligaments

| Ligament | Restraint |
|---|---|
| **SGHL** | **Inferior** translation at **0° abduction** (neutral rotation) |
| **MGHL** | Anterior + posterior translation in **mid-abduction (~45°)** + ER |
| **Anterior band IGHL** | **Anterior/inferior** translation at **90° abduction + max ER** (late cocking) |
| **Posterior band IGHL** | **Posterior** subluxation at **90° flexion + IR** |
| Superior band IGHL | "Most important static stabiliser" (Orthobullets) |

## 2 Mechanism

**Energy** — typically **high-energy**

**Mechanism** — **hyperabduction** → proximal humerus levered on **acromion** → tears inferior capsule/labrum → head disengages **inferiorly**

**Soft tissue** — variable tearing of static GH ligaments

## 3 Presentation

### Clinical presentation – arm locked in abduction

**Hallmark** — arm **fixed in abduction, overhead** (e.g. **140°**); **can't lower arm** → **inferior** dislocation\
✗ Wrong options: anterior · posterior · superior · lateral

**Clinical diagnosis** — possible on exam alone (unique posture); X-rays still for concomitant injury

![Luxatio erecta — arm locked overhead](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob3132-luxatioclinical.jpg)

| Associated injury | Rate / note |
|---|---|
| **Neurological** | **Up to 60%** — highest NV injury of all shoulder dislocations |
| **Vascular** | **Up to 39%** (axillary artery) |
| Proximal humerus fracture | Esp. **greater tuberosity** |
| **Rotator cuff tear** | **Extremely common** (older + young) |
| Anterior capsule / labral tear | Common |

**NV exam** — **pre- AND post-reduction**: radial + brachial pulse, **axillary nerve** + distal neuro exam

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

**X-ray** — complete shoulder series → **inferior dislocation, arm fully abducted**

![Inferior glenohumeral dislocation](../../MCQ/attachments/q3065-illa-luxatioerecta-xr.jpg)

**MRI** — **after relocation** (soft-tissue injury common) → capsulolabral pathology, **cuff tears extremely common** · **prompt in young** to avoid missed cuff tear

## 5 Management

```
Luxatio erecta
├─ All → closed reduction + NV check before and after
│      (traction–countertraction or two-step technique)
├─ Inactive elderly, no acute cuff tear → sling briefly → ROM + cuff/periscapular PT
└─ Active young → arthroscopic or open repair
       └─ acute cuff tear → prompt repair
```

### Closed reduction techniques

**Traction–countertraction** — as for anterior dislocation

**Two-step technique** — stand at head · **push humerus laterally + pull medial epicondyle superiorly** → head rotates **inferior → anterior** around glenoid (arm moves from abduction to **adduction against chest**) → then any anterior-dislocation reduction

**Post-reduction** — brief immobiliser → ROM (if cuff intact) → periscapular + cuff strengthening

### Operative criteria

| Option | Indication |
|---|---|
| Closed reduction + immobilisation | **Inactive elderly**; no acute traumatic cuff tear |
| **Arthroscopic / open repair** (repair vs reconstruction) | **Active younger** patients |
| **Prompt cuff repair** | **Acute traumatic cuff tear** (delay → retraction → irreparable) |

**Arthroscopy advantage** — addresses **capsulolabral damage + traumatic cuff tear** together

## 6 Complications

| Complication | Key fact |
|---|---|
| **Axillary nerve palsy** | **Most common nerve palsy**; may resolve with reduction · persists → **EMG at 6–12 wk** (prognosis) |
| **Brachial plexopathy** | High energy + head displacement; usually resolves after reduction + observation |
| **Axillary artery thrombosis** | **May occur late** |
| **Rotator cuff tear** | Common in old + young → prompt MRI in young |

## X vs Y

| | **Luxatio erecta (inferior)** | **Anterior / posterior dislocation** |
|---|---|---|
| Frequency | **0.5%** | **Much more common** |
| Arm fixed in abduction/flexion | **Yes** (overhead) | **No** |
| NV injury | **Highest** (neuro ≤60%, vascular ≤39%) | Lower |
| Reduction | Two-step → convert to anterior | Standard techniques |

## How it's asked

**"Shoulder locked at 140° abduction, can't lower arm — direction?"** → **inferior** (luxatio erecta)

**"Dislocation with highest neurovascular injury rate?"** → **luxatio erecta**

**"Most common nerve palsy?"** → **axillary nerve**

**"Persistent axillary palsy — when EMG?"** → **6–12 weeks**

**"Young patient after reduction — next?"** → **MRI** (rotator cuff tear common)

**"Late vascular complication?"** → **axillary artery thrombosis**

**"Two-step reduction converts inferior to?"** → **anterior** dislocation
