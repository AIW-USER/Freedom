# Revision Total Elbow Arthroplasty

*Shoulder & Elbow · 39 / 60*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Lifetime revision rate** — **~13%** · much **higher than TKA/THA**

**TEA survival** — **92% (5 y)**, **81% (10 y)**, **71% (15 y)**, **61% (20 y)**

**Shift in indication** — originally **RA**; with anti-rheumatics (DMARDs) → now mostly **post-traumatic** (distal humerus fractures)

**Risk factors for TEA failure** — **smoking** · **medical comorbidities** (RA, hypothyroidism, uncontrolled DM, iron-deficiency anaemia, obesity) · **non-compliance with activity restrictions** · **non-constrained** (dislocation) or **highly constrained** (loosening) systems

### Failure modes & revision rates by indication

| Mode of failure | Rate | Key facts |
|---|---|---|
| **Aseptic loosening** | **15%** | ↑ with **cementation + highly constrained** designs; **anterior impingement** of coronoid into anterior humeral flange in max flexion · **most common in OA** |
| **Component failure** | **15%** | Mostly **bushing wear** (linked/semiconstrained); **younger** patients or **pre-existing deformity** |
| **Infection** | **~8% → ~3%** since DMARDs (Orthobullets) · **3–5%** (OB) — read the options | **Higher than other joint arthroplasties** |
| **Periprosthetic fracture** | **5%** | Intra-op (preparation, reaming, implantation) · post-op (non-compliance, ground-level falls) |
| **Instability** | — | **Most common failure of unconstrained (unlinked) TEA** — late ligament insufficiency or malposition |

**Revision rate by indication** — **traumatic < inflammatory arthritis < OA** (lowest → highest)

**OA patients** — **aseptic loosening = most common complication** (higher demand) \
✗ Wrong options: infection · fracture

**RA patients** — ↑ **tendon + ligament insufficiency**

**Design** — unconstrained/unlinked · **semiconstrained/linked** (e.g. **Coonrad-Morrey**) = **best longevity + function** · constrained

**Polyethylene / bushing wear** — linked + semiconstrained · not the main failure of unconstrained · **component fracture uncommon**

**Gill & Morrey (OB)** — RA, Coonrad-Morrey, 10–15 y: **97%** no/mild pain · arc **28–131°** · **92.4% survival** · 14% reoperation · 86% good/excellent

### Infection – organisms, risk factors & staged revision

| Organism | Fact |
|---|---|
| **S. aureus** (GPC clusters, catalase +, **coagulase +**) | **Most common (~40%)** |
| **S. epidermidis** (GPC clusters, catalase +, **coagulase −**) | **2nd (30–35%)**; **worst outcomes / lowest reimplantation** — **biofilm / encapsulation** → **most difficult to eradicate** |
| **Cutibacterium (P.) acnes** (anaerobic aerotolerant GP bacillus) | **Most common in TSA**, not common in TEA |
| GPC chains, α-haemolytic, optochin-sensitive | S. pneumoniae (pneumonia) |
| GPC chains, β-haemolytic, bacitracin-sensitive | **Group A strep** → necrotising soft-tissue infection |

**Infection risk factors** — **RA on steroids** · **hypothyroidism** (independent) · **diabetes** · **thin soft-tissue envelope** · **prior elbow infection** · **prior elbow surgery** · **psychiatric illness**

**Not risk factors** — female gender · Nordic heritage · **isolated +RF with −anti-CCP** · HbA1c <6.0%\
✗ Wrong options: RA

### Genes & lab markers

| Test | Value / meaning |
|---|---|
| **TSH ↑ + free T4 ↓** | **Hypothyroidism** = independent PJI risk (TSH normal 0.5–5.0; free T4 normal 0.7–1.9) |
| **ESR, CRP, WBC** | Usually **↑** in PJI (WBC may be normal) |
| IL-6, alpha-defensin | **Not studied** in elbow PJI |
| **Synovial WBC** | **No accepted cut-off** for elbow |
| **Aspiration culture** | **+ = chronic infection**; **very high PPV, very low NPV** → **negative aspirate does not rule out** |
| **Intra-op histology** | **Most effective** diagnostic test; **specificity 93%**, **NPV 90%**, **sensitivity 51%** |
| **Intra-op cultures** | **Negative in 10%** of infected TEA; chronic **~15%** vs acute **3%** |

**No definitive test** — diagnosis needs **high clinical suspicion**

## 3 Presentation

**Symptoms** — **squeaking / crepitus** (bushing wear) · pain · swelling · stiffness

**Red flags for infection** — **pain at night or rest** · **progressive loss of motion without cause** · **early unexplained loosening** · erythema, **sinus tract**, persistent wound drainage

**Exam** — diffuse tenderness, swelling, **deformity** (significant bushing wear), painful limited ROM

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

**X-ray** — **AP + lateral of humerus, elbow, forearm** → loosening, periprosthetic fracture, **bushing wear**

**Bushing wear angle** — angle between ulnar and humeral components · **>10° = mild–moderate wear** (Orthobullets text) · figure labels **7° measurable, 10° extensive** — read the options

![Bushing wear angle](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob422838-bushing-wear.jpg)

**CT** — periprosthetic **osteolysis / loosening**; **cement mantle intact or broken** in fracture

**MRI** — **metal subtraction + contrast** → abscess / soft-tissue infection

### Mayo (O'Driscoll & Morrey) classification of periprosthetic fracture

| Type | Location / features | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **I** | **Periarticular** — humeral condyle or olecranon; osteolysis + muscle distraction | **Undisplaced** → immobilisation / soft-tissue repair (**fibrous union OK**) · **Displaced** → ORIF with heavy non-absorbable sutures or **tension band** · intact extensor + well-fixed stem → **fragment excision** |
| **II1** | Along stem, **well-fixed implant** | **ORIF + component retention ± strut allograft** (unicortical screws + cerclage cables) |
| **II2** | Along stem, **loose implant, good bone stock** | **Revision with long-stem** ± strut allograft + impaction grafting (± locking plate / cerclage) |
| **II3** | Along stem, **loose implant, severe bone loss** | Revision with **extensive allograft**; often **resection arthroplasty** |
| **III** | **Distal to prosthesis** | Well-fixed → **humerus: immobilise**, **ulna: ORIF (plate)** · loose → treat as **II2** · CT/X-ray to confirm implant + cement mantle |

![Mayo classification of periprosthetic TEA fracture](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob422838-mayo-classification-of-periprosthetic-fracture.jpg)

## 5 Management

```
Failed TEA
├─ Bushing wear, stable implants, no infection → I&D + bushing exchange (retain)
├─ Acute PJI (<90 d) → I&D + component retention (63%)
├─ Chronic PJI → explant + 2-stage revision (90%)
│      └─ unfit / severe bone loss / poor soft tissue → resection arthroplasty
└─ Aseptic loosening / Mayo II2 → single-stage long-stem revision
```

### Operative criteria

| Procedure | Indication | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **Nonop** (immobilise 2–4 wk → brace) | **Type I** with stable prosthesis · **Type III humerus** with stable implant (→ Sarmiento) · unfit for surgery | — |
| **I&D + bushing exchange, retain** | **Squeaking/crepitus**, stable implants, no infection · **acute PJI <90 d** | Bushing exchange **75% good at 5 y** · acute PJI **63%** eradicated · chronic only **31%** |
| **ORIF, retain ± excision ± strut** | **Type I olecranon** · **II1** · **III ulna** | **80–90%** no complications |
| **Single-stage revision ± ORIF/allograft** | **Type II2** · **aseptic loosening** | Chronic infection only **66%** eradicated |
| **Explant + 2-stage revision** | **Infected TEA** · Mayo II2 | Chronic infection **90%** eradicated |
| **Resection arthroplasty** | **Salvage**: resistant PJI, can't tolerate multiple surgeries, **severe bone loss**, poor soft tissue · **II3** not reconstructable | **71%** eradication; **lowest MEPS** function |

**Chronic P. acnes (6 mo), well-fixed, good bone stock, healthy** — **2-stage revision**\
✗ Wrong options: IV antibiotics alone · arthroscopic I&D · I&D + poly exchange · single stage

### Technique pearls

**Bushing exchange** — prior approach · ulnar nerve **explore/decompress if symptomatic**, else identify + protect · **uncouple** components → inspect integrity, loosening, rotation → exchange bushing

**ORIF** — humerus: **posterior paratricipital** muscle-sparing · ulna: posterior + subcutaneous border extension · **radial nerve crosses lateral IM septum 14 cm proximal** to lateral epicondyle · Type I with extensor disruption → **tension band, K-wires dorsal to implant** engaging cement + figure-of-8

**Single-stage revision** — remove all loose implant + cement · **long stem in all**, **2 cortical widths beyond fracture** · **cement stem BEFORE struts** (extravasation under strut → no incorporation, nerve irritation) · humerus: **longer posterior + shorter anterior strut** · ulna: dorsal strut under **anconeus** or **FCU**\
✗ Wrong options: flexion impingement

**2-stage (infection)** — **posterior triceps-sparing** · humeral removal: burr + flexible osteotomes, slap hammer; stuck → **posterior humeral cortical split** · well-fixed ulna → **extended olecranon osteotomy** (repair with cables) · **antibiotic spacers/beads** in both canals; no cement in subcutaneous tissue · ± static ex-fix · **≥6 wk IV culture-specific antibiotics** → reimplant when markers normalise, **long stem + antibiotic cement**

**2-stage (fracture)** — stage I: union with **ICBG + plate** · stage II: **longer stem + impaction graft**

**Resection arthroplasty** — thick flaps; nerves in unpredictable places; remove all cement; stabilise with heavy sutures/wires if needed

## 6 Complications

| Complication | Rate / risk factors | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **Persistent deep infection** | **~10% after 2-stage**, **~30% after single stage** · **polymicrobial**, **S. epidermidis**, **RA on steroids**, **extensive bone loss** | **Resection arthroplasty** |
| **Ulnar nerve palsy** | **Cement extravasation**, **not visualising nerve** | — |
| **Radial nerve palsy** | **Cement extravasation**, **long plates** impinging | — |
| **Symptomatic hardware** | **ORIF of periprosthetic ulna** fractures | Remove after union |

**Prognosis** — depends on **cause of failure**, **comorbidities**, remaining **bone stock**

| | **Unconstrained (unlinked)** | **Semiconstrained (linked)** | **Highly constrained** |

| Main failure | **Instability** | **Bushing / poly wear**, loosening | **Aseptic loosening** |

## How it's asked

**"Chronic P. acnes TEA, 6 mo, well-fixed, good bone, healthy — treatment?"** → **two-stage revision arthroplasty**

**"RA, cemented semiconstrained TEA 8 y, ↑ ESR/CRP, lysis — hardest organism to eradicate?"** → **Staphylococcus epidermidis**\
✗ Wrong options: S. viridans, E. coli, Vibrio, C. difficile

**"History item most linked to TEA PJI (TSH 10.6, free T4 0.23)?"** → **TSH and free T4 levels** = hypothyroidism\
✗ Wrong options: female · Nordic · HbA1c 5.9% · +RF with −anti-CCP

**"Draining TEA at 12 wk — organism with worst reimplantation prognosis?"** → **gram-positive cocci in clusters, catalase-positive, coagulase-negative** = S. epidermidis\
✗ Wrong options: coagulase + = S. aureus, most common

**"Most common failure of unconstrained TEA?"** → **instability**\
✗ Wrong options: poly/bushing wear · humeral loosening · component fracture

**"TEA revision rates lowest → highest?"** → **traumatic < inflammatory arthritis < osteoarthritis**

**"TEA for OA — most common complication (figure)?"** → **aseptic loosening (Figure A)**\
✗ Wrong options: fracture · infection · normal X-ray

**"Most effective test for TEA infection?"** → **intra-op histology**\
✗ Wrong options: negative aspirate excludes
