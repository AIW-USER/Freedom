# Quadrilateral Space Syndrome

*Shoulder & Elbow · 37 / 60*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Definition** — compression of **axillary nerve + posterior humeral circumflex artery (PHCA)** in the quadrilateral (quadrangular) space → **posterolateral shoulder pain**

**Incidence** — **rare**; often **misdiagnosed as subacromial impingement**

**Age** — **20–40 y** (Orthobullets) · **20–35 y** (TO) — young adults

**Side** — **dominant shoulder** most commonly (TO)

**Risk factors** — **overhead athletes** (basketball, baseball pitchers), contact/throwing sports, **weightlifting** (muscle hypertrophy)

### Anatomy – quadrilateral space

| Boundary | Structure |
|---|---|
| **Superior** | **Teres minor** (inferior border) |
| **Inferior** | **Teres major** (superior border) |
| **Medial** | **Long head of triceps** (lateral border) |
| **Lateral** | **Surgical neck of humerus** (medial cortex) |
| **Contents** | **Axillary nerve** (C5 root, **posterior cord**) + **PHCA** (+ vein, TO) |

**Location** — posterior compartment of arm; **lateral to triangular space**, **medial to triangular interval** (with lateral + medial triangular spaces, TO)

![Quadrangular space, triangular space and triangular interval](../../MCQ/attachments/q519-illb-shoulder-spaces.jpg)

**Axillary nerve supplies** — **deltoid + teres minor** + sensation **lateral arm** (upper lateral brachial cutaneous)

**Long head of triceps** — **can have axillary innervation** (case report, OB)

**Teres minor motor nerve** — 2 variants of fascial envelopment; nerve takes **highly angulated course at the fascial sling** → **stout fascial sling** may compress it (OB)

## 2 Mechanism

### Etiology – causes of compression

| Cause | Note |
|---|---|
| **Fibrous bands** | **Oblique fibrous bands = most common cause** (TO); compress nerve + artery in abduction/ER |
| **Muscular hypertrophy** | Weightlifting after a break (OB) |
| **Paralabral cyst** | **Most commonly inferior labral tears** (Orthobullets, TO) · **posterior labrum** ("occasionally", TO) — read the options |
| Trauma | Scapular fracture, shoulder dislocation |
| Masses | Benign or malignant |

**Position of max compression** — **late cocking phase** of throwing = **abduction + external rotation** (TO)

**Throwers** — repetitive motion irritates nerve → dysfunction; usually **self-limited** (OB)

## 3 Presentation

### Symptoms

**Pain** — **poorly localised posterior/lateral shoulder pain**; **worse at night**, overhead activity, late cocking/acceleration

**Paraesthesia** — **non-dermatomal**, lateral shoulder + **lateral arm**

**Weakness** — **external rotation** weakness, esp. **arm abducted 90° (throwing position)**; **5/5 ER with arm at side** (OB)

**Aggravated by** — active + resisted **abduction + ER**; forward flexion (OB)

### Examination

**Palpation** — **point tenderness over quadrilateral space** (near teres minor insertion, TO)

**Atrophy** — **teres minor + deltoid** (✓ **deltoid atrophy**, TO)\
✗ Wrong options: supraspinatus atrophy · positive apprehension · positive dynamic labral shear

**Provocation (TO)** — **arm abducted + externally rotated for 1 min** reproduces symptoms\
✗ Wrong options: abducted + internally rotated

**Detecting weakness (TO)** — weakness/atrophy **difficult to detect unless significant deltoid involvement**

**Neuro exam** — usually **normal**; mild sensory change in axillary distribution

**Typical negatives (OB)** — full ROM, negative Hawkins/Neer, belly press/lift-off, O'Brien, normal radiographs, no cuff/labral tear on MRA

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Diagnosis – axillary nerve compression & teres minor atrophy

**Diagnosis** — **clinical** (point tenderness ± teres minor atrophy); MRI may show compression

### Key radiology findings

| Test | Finding |
|---|---|
| **X-ray** (AP, lateral, axillary) | **Usually normal**; rules out other pathology |
| **MRI** | **Fatty atrophy of teres minor** (✓ OB; T1 streaked white + atrophied) · deltoid fatty infiltration · compression of space · **inferior paralabral cyst** with labral tear |
| **Arteriogram** | **PHCA occlusion in abduction + ER, normal with arm at side** (TO) |
| **EMG** | **Confirms diagnosis**; axillary nerve involvement |
| **Lidocaine block** | Confirms diagnosis (see Management) |

**Selective teres minor atrophy on MRI** — **highly suggestive** of QSS (TO)

**Sanders** — teres minor fatty atrophy in **3% of shoulder MRIs** (OB)

**Next step to confirm (OB)** — **subclavian arteriogram** with arm **adducted + abducted/ER**

**MRI ✗ distractors** — ↓ intra-capsular volume (= **adhesive capsulitis** on arthrogram) · ↑ capsular volume · infraspinatus atrophy (suprascapular) · latissimus atrophy

**Isolated teres minor atrophy (OB)** — **common**, unclear aetiology (axillary nerve variants); **clinically distinct** from QSS

![Fatty atrophy of teres minor on sagittal MRI](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob3066-fatty-atrophy.jpg)

### Brown classification (TO)

| Type | Cause | Who |
|---|---|---|
| **Vascular QSS** | **Repetitive trauma to PHCA** in abduction + ER | **Professional/collegiate overhead athletes** |
| **Neurogenic QSS** | **Fixed anatomic anomalies** — **fibrous bands most common** | |

## 5 Management

```
Quadrilateral space syndrome
├─ First line → NSAIDs + activity modification + PT
│      (most improve in 3–6 months)
├─ Diagnosis unclear → lidocaine block into space
└─ Fails 3–6 mo / significant weakness / space-occupying lesion → open decompression
       └─ labral tear + paralabral cyst → ± arthroscopic labral repair
```

### Nonoperative

**Regimen** — NSAIDs, activity restriction, physiotherapy (GH mobilisation + strengthening, **posterior capsule stretching**, massage)

**TO regimen** — activity modification, NSAIDs, **cortisone injections**, PT for **rotator cuff strengthening** — indicated initially for **all** patients

**Outcome** — **most improve with 3–6 months** nonoperative

### Diagnostic lidocaine block

**Technique** — plain lidocaine into quadrilateral space; start **2–3 cm inferior to standard posterior arthroscopy portal**

**Positive** — **no point tenderness + pain-free full ROM** after injection

### Operative criteria

| Indication | Procedure |
|---|---|
| **Failed nonop (3–6 mo)** | Open release of quadrilateral space |
| **Significant weakness + functional disability** | Open release |
| **Space-occupying lesion** (paralabral cyst) | Decompression **± arthroscopic labral repair** |
| **Isolated teres minor atrophy** | **Release of fascial sling** to teres minor → ↓ pain, **Hornblower's** weakness resolves (OB) |

### Open quadrilateral space decompression

**Approach** — **lateral decubitus**; **posterior approach**, 3–4 cm incision over space; reflect posterior border of deltoid superolaterally; fat between teres minor + major

**Technique** — find axillary nerve using **humeral neck** as reference · **avoid cutting PHCA** · free fibrous bands (**lysis of fibrous bands**) · confirm release in **abduction + ER**

**Post-op** — sling for comfort · **early pendulums** (avoid new adhesions) · supervised full active ROM

**Results** — good results in overhead athletes after failed nonop (McAdams, 4 athletes, OB); usually symptom relief (TO)

## 6 Complications

**Prognosis** — long-standing cases → **atrophy/weakness of teres minor + deltoid**

## X vs Y

| Lesion | Atrophy | Weakness |
|---|---|---|
| **QSS (axillary n.)** | **Teres minor** ± deltoid | **ER with arm abducted 90°** |
| **Suprascapular notch** | **Supraspinatus + infraspinatus** | Abduction + ER **at side** |
| **Spinoglenoid notch** (cyst) | **Infraspinatus only** | **Isolated ER at side** |
| Chronic cuff tear | Infraspinatus (retracted tear on MRI) | ER |
| Subscapularis | Rare (**upper + lower subscapular** nerves) | IR |

**QSS vs subacromial impingement** — QSS often **misdiagnosed as impingement**; in QSS **Hawkins + Neer negative**, MRI shows **teres minor atrophy**

**✗ Wrong options (other diagnoses)** — Parsonage-Turner · long thoracic neuritis · internal impingement · external impingement · scapular dyskinesia

## How it's asked

**"Posterior shoulder pain, worse in abduction + ER, paralabral cyst in quadrilateral space — exam finding?"** → **deltoid atrophy** (TO)\
✗ Wrong options: supraspinatus atrophy · apprehension · dynamic labral shear

**"True about quadrilateral space syndrome?"** → **most common cause is oblique fibrous band** (TO)\
✗ Wrong options: anterior pain · abducted + internally rotated · middle-aged diabetic female

**"MRI in chronic QSS?"** → **fatty atrophy of teres minor**\
✗ Wrong options: infraspinatus · latissimus · loss of capsular volume

**"Pitcher, pain + lateral arm paraesthesia, T1 fatty teres minor + deltoid — diagnosis?"** → **quadrilateral space syndrome**\
✗ Wrong options: Parsonage-Turner · long thoracic neuritis · internal/external impingement

**"EMG shows QSS — muscle affected along with deltoid?"** → **teres minor**\
✗ Wrong options: teres major · supraspinatus · subscapularis

**"Weightlifter, weakness of resisted ER at 90° abduction only, isolated teres minor atrophy?"** → **QSS**\
✗ Wrong options: suprascapular notch · spinoglenoid notch · scapular dyskinesia

**"Next step to confirm QSS?"** → **subclavian arteriogram, arm adducted + abducted/ER**

**"Pitcher, weak ER at 90° abduction — muscle and aetiology?"** → **teres minor; axillary nerve at quadrilateral space**

**"Boundaries of quadrilateral space?"** → **teres minor above, teres major below, long head triceps medial, surgical neck humerus lateral**
