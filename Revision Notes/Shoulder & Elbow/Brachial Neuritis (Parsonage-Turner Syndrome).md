# Brachial Neuritis (Parsonage-Turner Syndrome)

*Shoulder & Elbow · 8 / 60*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Incidence** — **1.6–3/100,000/y** (Orthobullets text) · **1–30/100,000/y** (Orthobullets INA vs HNA table); underdiagnosed

**Demographics** — **♂ > ♀** (1.5:1 to 11.5:1) · **middle age (4th decade)**, 20–60 y, mean 41 (range 3–81)

**Side** — unilateral · **bilateral 10–30%** (16% simultaneous)

**Synonyms** — **neuralgic amyotrophy** (NA) · idiopathic neuralgic amyotrophy (**INA**) · Parsonage-Turner syndrome (**PTS**) · acute brachial neuritis/plexitis · idiopathic brachial plexus neuropathy

### Nerves involved

**Distribution** — any brachial plexus nerve/branch · **outside plexus in 17%** · usually **upper plexus + >1 nerve**

**Most common nerves** — **long thoracic** (→ **medial scapular winging**) · **suprascapular** · **axillary** · **musculocutaneous** · radial (Orthobullets, OB)

**Others** — cervical roots, **AIN**, PIN, LABC

**Muscles** — deltoid, supraspinatus, infraspinatus, **serratus anterior**, biceps

![Medial scapular winging (long thoracic nerve) in brachial neuritis](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob3065-36-moved.jpg)

### Pathophysiology

**Aetiology** — unknown; multifactorial → **inflammatory response in brachial plexus**

**Autoimmune** — ↑ blastogenic lymphocyte activity vs plexus tissue · ↑ **complement-fixing antibodies to peripheral nerve myelin**

**Biomechanical** — **mobile upper trunk** → wear of **blood–nerve barrier** → immune factors reach nerve (strenuous exercise, manual labour)

**Operative finding** — **hourglass-like constrictions** of nerves, **no external compression**

### Risk factors / antecedent events

| Trigger | Frequency | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Viral infection** (most common) | **25–55%** | EBV, VZV, Coxsackie B, **parvovirus B19**, CMV, mumps, smallpox, HIV; "flu-like illness", URTI |
| Bacterial infection | — | Leptospira, TB, Yersinia, Salmonella, Borrelia |
| **Immunisation** | **15%** | Tetanus, hepatitis B; **influenza vaccine** (OB stem) |
| **Perioperative / peripartum** | **14%** | Elective surgery |
| **Strenuous exercise** | **8%** | Cross-training, heavy upper-body work |
| Drugs | — | Abacavir, streptokinase, **heroin**, infliximab, **interferon** |
| Iatrogenic | — | **Interscalene block**, surgery, LP, irradiation; burns |

**Viral illness figure** — **25–55%** (Orthobullets, OB) · **25–50%** (one OB explanation) — read the options

### Genes & lab markers

**HNA gene** — **autosomal dominant** · **SEPT9 (septin 9)** on **chromosome 17q24** (cytoskeletal protein in glial cells) · ~200 families worldwide

**Routine labs** — **CBC + ESR usually normal**

**Inconsistent labs** — ↑ liver enzymes · **+ antiganglioside antibodies** · + ANA

**CSF** — usually normal; ± mildly ↑ protein, slight pleocytosis, oligoclonal bands

## 3 Presentation

### Diagnosis – acute pain followed by weakness

| Phase | Features | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| **I – Pain** | **Sudden, severe, unrelenting shoulder pain** (90%); radiates to proximal arm/neck; **wakes from sleep**; burning; often atraumatic | **Days–weeks** (♂ 45 d vs ♀ 23 d) |
| **II – Painless flaccid paralysis** | Weakness as pain subsides: within **24 h (33%) to 4 wk (80%)**; **patchy paresis** (muscles of same nerve unequally affected) | — |
| **III – Recovery** | Slow, steady motor return; length ∝ length of pain phase | **6–18 months** |

**Key exam pattern** — **inability to actively abduct** with **full passive motion** · **normal cervical spine exam** (–ve Spurling)

**Pain behaviour** — **not worsened by motion or palpation** · **not relieved by subacromial lidocaine** (neuropathic)

**Motor** — **severe weakness of ER + abduction** (SS, IS, deltoid) · hypotonia, **areflexia** (LMN) · **atrophy**, fasciculations · **medial winging** (serratus anterior)

**Sensory** — changes in **78%**: paraesthesia/hypoaesthesia over deltoid, lateral arm, radial forearm; often unnoticed · sensation may be **normal** (OB stems)

**Autonomic (15%)** — trophic skin, temperature dysregulation, ↑ sweating, altered nail/hair

**Upper trunk pattern** — pain → weakness + atrophy + scapular winging after viral illness = **upper trunk neuralgic amyotrophy**\
✗ Wrong options: isolated LTN palsy (doesn't explain whole picture)

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

**Diagnosis** — **clinical**; often a **diagnosis of exclusion**; tests rule out mimics; **EMG confirms**

### Key radiology findings

**X-ray shoulder / C-spine** — **normal**; rule out **calcific tendinitis** (also severe incapacitating pain)

**Chest X-ray** — baseline to exclude compressive **Pancoast tumour** (OB)

| MRI timing | Sequence | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| **Early** | **T2 / STIR** | **Diffuse ↑ signal (oedema)** in muscles of plexus (SS, IS, deltoid, teres minor most common) |
| **Late** | **T1** | **Fatty infiltration + atrophy** |

**MRI also excludes** — cuff tear (intact cuff) · SSN notch/spinoglenoid **cyst** · brain MS plaques · cervical disc

![T2 MRI – diffuse muscle oedema in brachial neuritis](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob3065-mri.jpg)

### EMG findings

**EMG** — compatible findings in **96%**; confirms diagnosis + **monitors recovery** (serial)

| Timing | EMG |
|---|---|
| **Early** — **3–4 wk** (Orthobullets) · **first 3 wk** (OB) | **Acute denervation**: **positive sharp waves + fibrillation potentials** in **both peripheral nerve AND nerve root distributions** |
| **Late** — **3–4 months** (Orthobullets) · **as early as 6 wk** (OB) | Chronic denervation + **early reinnervation**: **polyphasic MUPs** (= **resolving** PTS) |

**Sensory NCS** — less useful than EMG · **↓ amplitude (axonal loss)**, **preserved conduction velocity + distal latency** · may be normal (doesn't exclude)

| ✗ EMG distractor | Diagnosis |
|---|---|
| Normal EMG | PTS shows denervation |
| Fibrillations from **spinoglenoid notch** compression | SSN compression (cyst on MRI) |
| Sharp waves + fibs **deltoid + biceps only** | **C5 root** compression |
| Polyphasic MUPs, early reinnervation | **Resolving** PTS (not early) |

### Classification – INA vs HNA

| Factor | **INA** (idiopathic) | **HNA** (hereditary) |
|---|---|---|
| Incidence | 1–30/100,000/y | Very rare (~200 families) |
| Gene | — | **Septin 9 (chr 17q24)**, **AD** |
| Age at onset | Middle age (20–60) | **Young** (childhood/adolescence; mean 28) |
| Recurrence | **Rare** (~1.5 episodes) | **Frequent** (3.5 episodes) |
| Appearance | Normal | **Dysmorphic**: short stature, **hypotelorism**, cleft palate, facial asymmetry, skin folds |
| Nerves outside plexus | Uncommon (**17%**) | Common (**56%**): **lower cranial nerves** (VII, IX, X, XI, XII), lumbosacral plexus, phrenic, recurrent laryngeal |
| Severity / outcome | Better | More severe paresis, worse outcome |

### Differential diagnosis

| Mimic | Distinguishing feature |
|---|---|
| **Cervical radiculopathy** (C5/C6–7, disc) | Single root dermatome/myotome; pain from neck; **↑ with movement**; abnormal neck exam/Spurling (normal neck exam here makes it unlikely) |
| **Rotator cuff tear / impingement** | Pain persists with weakness; impingement signs; **relieved by subacromial lidocaine**; MRI shows tear; does not explain winging |
| **Calcific tendinitis** | Calcification on X-ray; no acute severe weakness |
| **Adhesive capsulitis (frozen shoulder)** | **Loss of passive + active** motion |
| **Entrapment neuropathy** (SSN) | **Single nerve** distribution on EMG (SS + IS only) |
| **Poliomyelitis** | No (excluded by acute severe weakness pattern, OB) |
| **ALS** | **UMN + LMN** signs, **painless, no sensory loss**, cranial nerve signs |
| **Idiopathic hypertrophic brachial neuritis** | **Painless**; plexus hypertrophy on MRI; **demyelinating** NCS (slow velocity, prolonged latency) |
| Multiple sclerosis | Brain MRI demyelination |

**Exclusion logic (OB)** — loss of **active** motion excludes radiculopathy + impingement · **normal MRI** excludes cuff tear · **full passive motion** excludes adhesive capsulitis · normal C-spine exam excludes disc disease

## 5 Management

```
Brachial neuritis
├─ Pain phase → NSAIDs, judicious opioids, immobilisation
│      └─ severe pain → oral prednisone 1 mg/kg/d × 2 wk + 2-wk taper
├─ Weakness phase → physical therapy (ROM + shoulder girdle strengthening)
│      └─ follow monthly: exam + serial EMG
└─ No clinical/EMG recovery by 6–9 mo (9–12 mo in summary) → exploration
       └─ hourglass constriction → neurolysis
```

### Nonoperative

**Mainstay** — **observation + pain control**; self-limiting

**Corticosteroids** — early, severe pain; **2 wk prednisone 1 mg/kg/d + 2 wk taper** · faster pain resolution, **no change in progression/prognosis** · OB: may shorten symptoms/accelerate recovery

**Physical therapy** — once pain abated; ROM + strengthening; reduce traction on nerves · reverses atrophy · **observation alone = observation + PT at 1 y**

**Steroid window (TO)** — **high-dose corticosteroids if diagnosed early (within 72 h)**, rarely possible · some spontaneous recovery usual, **full recovery not assured**

### Operative criteria

| Indication | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Exploration / neurolysis / graft / transfer | **No regeneration on exam + EMG by 6–9 months** (Orthobullets treatment) · **9–12 months** (Orthobullets summary) — read the options |

| Procedure | Note |
|---|---|
| **Neurolysis** | **Superior** to neurorrhaphy + grafting; LTN microneurolysis, ulnar transposition, Guyon/radial tunnel/carpal tunnel release |
| Neurorrhaphy / grafting | Excise diseased segment |
| Nerve transfer | — |
| **Split pectoralis major transfer** | **Serratus anterior paralysis** (winging) |

**✗ Wrong options: decompression / arthroscopic repair** — not indicated (no compressive lesion, cuff intact)

### Prognosis

**Overall** — **good**; **gradual return of strength without long-term functional deficit**; slow · residual weakness may last months–years

| Time | Recovery |
|---|---|
| 1 month | **66%** some motor recovery |
| 1 year | **35%** recovered (excellent 36%) |
| 2 years | Excellent **75%** |
| 3 years | **~90%** full strength, no pain (89%) |
| Up to 8 years | Full strength in some |

**Poor prognosis** — **female** · **lower trunk** involvement (upper trunk = best) · **persistent pain + no motor recovery by 3 months** · **hereditary** cases

**No effect** — **age**

**Recurrence** — **rare** in idiopathic (common in HNA)

## X vs Y

| Prognosis statement (OB distractor) | Disease |
|---|---|
| **Gradual return of strength, no long-term deficit** | ✓ **PTS** |
| Decompression improves strength | **SSN notch cyst** |
| Immunomodulators ↓ relapses | **Multiple sclerosis** |
| Arthroscopic repair best for active patient | **Rotator cuff tear** |
| Progressive weakness → **respiratory failure** | **ALS** (pure motor; no ↓ sensory amplitudes) |

| | **Brachial neuritis** | **Cervical radiculopathy** | **Rotator cuff tear** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pain onset | Acute severe → resolves before weakness | Neck → arm | Persists with weakness |
| Pain with motion | **No** | **Yes** | Yes |
| Subacromial lidocaine | No relief | — | Relief |
| Distribution | **Multiple roots + peripheral nerves**, patchy | One root | Cuff only, no winging |
| EMG | **Root + peripheral nerve** denervation | Root | Normal |

## How it's asked

**"Acute atraumatic burning shoulder pain, can't abduct, full passive ROM, normal C-spine + MRI — diagnosis?"** → **brachial neuritis**\
✗ Wrong options: C6–7 radiculopathy · impingement · cuff tear · adhesive capsulitis

**"Flu-like illness 1 wk earlier, then burning pain → weakness, normal X-rays?"** → **brachial neuritis**\
✗ Wrong options: calcific tendinitis · poliomyelitis · cervical disc disease · impingement

**"URTI, 3 mo pain + weakness, atrophy, medial winging, flaccid — diagnosis?"** → **upper trunk neuralgic amyotrophy**\
✗ Wrong options: isolated LTN palsy · ALS · frozen shoulder · cuff tear

**"Post-influenza-vaccine pain → weakness, T2 oedema SS/IS — expected EMG?"** → **acute denervation in both peripheral nerve + nerve root distributions with sharp waves + fibrillations**

**"Sharp waves + fibs in deltoid + biceps only?"** → **C5 root compression**

**"Cross-training athlete, viral illness, pain → weakness, ↓ sensory amplitudes, normal brain/C-spine MRI — prognosis?"** → **gradual return of strength without long-term deficits**\
✗ Wrong options: decompression · immunomodulators · arthroscopic repair · respiratory failure
