# Rotator Cuff Tears

*Shoulder & Elbow · 41 / 60*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**FT tear prevalence** — **28% >60 y · 65% >70 y** (Orthobullets, OB) · ~1/3 >60 y, >60% at 70 y (OB) · ~30% >60 y (OB) — read the options

**Risk factors** — **age** · **smoking** · **hypercholesterolaemia** · **family history**

**Burden** — leading cause of shoulder disability; ~**250,000 RCR/y** (USA); **2nd most common reason manual labourers miss work**

**Age pattern** — mostly **>40 y**; degenerative/attrition in elderly · acute trauma in young

### Genes & lab markers

**Inherited / metabolic** — **family history** + **hypercholesterolaemia** = risk factors (no gene named in sources)

**Indolent post-RCR infection labs (OB)** — WBC borderline (11.0) · **ESR ↑ (40)** · **CRP mildly ↑ (1.5)** · Gram stain negative · culture positive at **day 7–21** → **Cutibacterium acnes**

### Natural history – asymptomatic prevalence & tear progression

| Fact | Value |
|---|---|
| Asymptomatic tears (FT + PT) **>60 y** on MRI/US | **30–55%** (Tempelhof US **30%** · Sher MRI **54%** = 28% FT + 26% PT) |
| Asymptomatic ≥60 y (Orthobullets) | MRI **55%** · US **23%** of asymptomatic |
| Asymptomatic → symptomatic | **50% in 2–3 y** (Orthobullets) · **30–50% in 2–3 y** (OB) — read the options |
| Symptomatic FT tear enlarges | **50% at 2 y** (Orthobullets) · ~**50% within 2–3 y** (OB) · **20–50% at ~2 y, >50% at 5 y** (OB) |
| Asymptomatic tear enlarges | ~**1/3** within 2–3 y (OB) |

**Progression rules** — **larger initial tears progress faster** (**linear correlation size ↔ rate**) · **FT > PT** (faster, more atrophy) · ↑ pain = marker of progression · ↑ fatty atrophy / ↓ muscle quality ↔ ↑ pain

**✗ Progression traps** — FT tears don't progress · symptoms unrelated to size · pain independent of muscle quality · **all** asymptomatic tears become symptomatic (subset never painful) · "10–15% FT enlarge by 2 y" · "20% symptomatic in 3 y" · PT tears progress faster

**Incidental FT tear, minimal symptoms (72 y, 7 mm)** — **observation**\
✗ Wrong options: repair · debridement · tenotomy

**Atraumatic FT tear + PT** — **subjective functional improvement** (↑ PROMs) · ~**75% avoid surgery at 2 y** · ↑ ROM (forward elevation) · minimal pain long-term · PT aim = strengthen surrounding muscles, **not tendon healing**

### Anatomy – footprint, rotator cable, biceps pulley, innervation & CA ligament

**Function** — **dynamic stability** by **balancing force couples** → stable fulcrum; **coronal**: inferior cuff (IS, TM, SSc) vs deltoid's superior moment · **transverse**: SSc (anterior) vs IS + TM (posterior); goal of treatment = restore equilibrium

| Footprint | Value |
|---|---|
| **Supraspinatus ML width** | **12.7 mm** (Orthobullets) · **12.1–12.7 mm** (Dugas, Ruotolo; OB) · **14–16 mm** (OB) · ~12–13 mm (OB) — read the options |
| Infraspinatus · teres minor · subscapularis | 13.4 · 13.9 · **17.9 mm** (SSc widest) |
| AP dimension | **20 mm** (SS + anterior IS) |
| Max insertional length × width | 23 × 16 mm (OB) |
| Articular cartilage → medial footprint | **1.6–1.9 mm** |
| Tendon thickness | 10–12 mm |
| **Usual tear site** | **15 mm posterior to biceps**, near **SS/IS junction** |

**Footprint ↔ % tear** — **6–7 mm** exposed ≈ **50%** PT tear

**5 histologic layers** — **articular side = half the strength of bursal side** → **most tears articular-sided** · I CHL fibres (1 mm) · II dense parallel fibres (3–5 mm) · III loose 45° bundles (3 mm) · IV loose CT + CHL · V capsule (2 mm)

**Bursal vs articular tissue** — bursal = tendon bundles → greater deformation + tensile strength · articular = tendon + ligament + capsule complex

**Blood supply** — subscapular, suprascapular, humeral circumflex aa (layers II–III); **bursal > articular** (hypovascular); critical zone next to lateral SS insertion

**Rotator interval** — capsule + **LHB** + **SGHL** + **CHL**; gap between SS leading edge and SSc superior border

**Rotator crescent** — thin sheet = distal SS + IS insertions (asterisk on arthroscopy)

**Rotator cable** — **thickened CHL** near its **avascular zone**, **perpendicular to SS fibres**, spans SS + IS insertions · **>2.5× thicker** than crescent · "**suspension bridge**" · cable disruption → destabilises tendon, tear propagates

**Biceps pulley (medial sling)** — **SGHL + CHL** (rotator interval tissue) · SSc = **anterior wall of bicipital groove** → SSc tear tears pulley → **medial LHB subluxation**

**CA ligament** — extra-articular · suspension function · restrains anterior translation (with CHL) · contains head in cuff arthropathy · resection → **↑ GH translation** (anterior/inferior)

**✗ CA resection distractors** — ↑ passive IR · ↑ axillary recess volume · ↓ AC joint forces · ↓ LHB resting tension

| Nerve | Muscles |
|---|---|
| **Upper + lower subscapular** | **Subscapularis** (lower also **teres major**) |
| Suprascapular | Supraspinatus + infraspinatus |
| Axillary | **Teres minor** + deltoid |
| Thoracodorsal (middle subscapular) | Latissimus dorsi |
| Musculocutaneous | Biceps, brachialis, coracobrachialis |
| Lateral pectoral · medial pectoral | Pec major (± minor) · pec major + minor |
| Long thoracic · dorsal scapular | Serratus anterior · rhomboids + levator scapulae |

**Biceps subluxation on MRI → innervation of injured muscle?** — **subscapular nerves** (upper + lower) — SSc tear

**Sagittal MRI marked muscle** — **infraspinatus** (TO, FNB 2022/2023) · **teres minor** (OB, sagittal T1 = muscle quality)

**Lateral subacromial portal view through FT tear** — glenoid, labrum, **biceps tendon** visible

**MGHL** — anterior restraint at **45° abduction**; **deep to SSc** (can be mistaken for it); **absent MGHL** → suspect **SSc tear** (MGHL translates medially with retracted SSc)

**Posterior lever push** — **IR arm + posterior stress on head** → tensions SSc → shows **SSc insertion on lesser tuberosity**

## 2 Mechanism

### Etiology

| Mechanism | Features |
|---|---|
| **Chronic degenerative** (intrinsic = primary aetiology) | Older; **SIT**; large tears extend to superior SSc |
| Chronic impingement (extrinsic) | Starts **bursal surface** or intratendinous |
| **Acute avulsion** | **SSc in young** (hyperabduction/ER, fall) · **SIT >40 y with dislocation** |
| Iatrogenic | Failed repair, esp **SSc after open anterior surgery** (open Bankart) |

**Disease continuum** — subacromial impingement → subcoracoid impingement → calcific tendonitis → **RCT** → cuff arthropathy

**Associated** — AC pathology · proximal biceps subluxation/tendonitis · **internal impingement** (throwers; PT tears)

**PT tear causes (OB)** — age-related metabolic/vascular degeneration · subacromial impingement · **anterior instability** · internal impingement · repetitive microtrauma · acute trauma less often · **articular side = poorer blood supply**

**Articular vs bursal origin** — articular = **intrinsic** degeneration · bursal = **extrinsic** (acromial spurs, AC arthritis); degenerative tears "start bursal side" (one OB explanation) vs articular commoner (Orthobullets, TO) — read the options

### Throwing athletes – mechanism & management

**Phase** — **deceleration** (label E): cuff = principal decelerator → **eccentric tensile failure**

**Site** — **articular side of posterior SS or IS**; **posterior pain after ball release**

**Overhead-athlete cuff dysfunction** — primary impingement · primary tensile overload · secondary impingement/tensile overload from GH instability; microtrauma (overhead) vs macrotrauma (contact)

**FT tear in thrower** — **must be repaired**\
✗ Wrong options: debrided

**Adolescent pitcher, undersurface PT tear, never treated** — **cessation of all throwing 6 wk** → cuff + periscapular strengthening → slow supervised return, pitching mechanics\
✗ Wrong options: ↓ pitch count · continued play · arthroscopic/mini-open repair

### Cuff tear after shoulder dislocation in older patients

**Rule** — **>40 y** anterior dislocation → **rotator cuff tear** (pre-existing degeneration, ↓ elasticity) ≫ recurrent instability; often **missed**

**Which tendon** — **supraspinatus** commonest (TO, OB) · **SIT** (SS, **IS**, TM) > SSc · SSc tears more with **posterior** dislocation or young hyperabduction/ER

**Incidence** — **35–86%** >40 y (OB) · **54%**, rising to **100% >70 y** (Simank) · **15%** >40 y, **up to 40% >60 y** (OB) — read the options

**Bimodal instability** — peak <40 y (Bankart, HAGL) · 2nd peak >60 y (cuff tears, fractures); Hill-Sachs common, usually not addressed

**Clue (TO)** — pain lifting in **scapular plane (scaption)** after 6 wk immobiliser → **US/MRI** for cuff\
✗ Wrong options: LHB entrapment · loose body

**Axillary nerve** — **7–10%**; far less common than cuff tear; usually too mild for gross weakness; **lateral arm sensation intact** → nerve palsy/radiculopathy unlikely

(✗ Wrong options: **Distractors** — posterior Bankart (posterior dislocation)) · HAGL (only ~11% of first dislocations >35 y) · SLAP (throwers, no link to anterior dislocation) · SSN injury (uncommon)

```
>40 y, first anterior dislocation, can't elevate / weak ER
├─ Next step → MRI shoulder (arthrogram if pacemaker)
│      ├─ FT cuff tear → early rotator cuff repair
│      └─ MRI negative → EMG (axillary nerve)
└─ not CT · sling 6 wk · PT first · diagnostic scope · observation
```

**Arthrogram** — dye leaks **GH → subacromial space** (geyser) = cuff tear; normal = dye contained in capsule

**Treatment** — **rotator cuff repair**; improved pain + function vs nonop; delay → atrophy + retraction\
✗ Wrong options: hemiarthroplasty · ORIF · TSA · RTSA · coracoid transfer · labral repair · PT alone

**Most likely to need surgery (52 y)** — **supraspinatus**\
✗ Wrong options: labrum · GH ligaments · humeral head · biceps

**Massive but completely repairable** — **acute tear after dislocation, 45 y** (no retraction/atrophy yet) · (✗ Wrong options: RA (attrition, steroids) · prior repair (revision) · SS fossa atrophy · superior humeral migration (→ arthropathy)) · chronic massive repairs retear **~50%**

## 3 Presentation

**Pain** — insidious, deltoid region, **overhead** activity · **night pain** = poor predictor for nonop

**Weakness** — **active ROM lost, passive ROM preserved** · acute pain + weakness after trauma

### Physical examination – external rotation lag sign

| Muscle | Weakness | Test | Technique / positive |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Supraspinatus** | Elevation in Jobe position | **Jobe / empty can**, **drop arm** | Abducted, IR, **30° forward in scapular plane, thumb down** → resist pressure · drop test: lowers slowly → sudden drop |
| **Infraspinatus** | ER at **0° abduction** | **ER lag sign** | **Elbow 90°, passive ER → can't actively maintain** |
| **Teres minor** | ER at **90° abd + 90° ER** | **Hornblower** | 90° abd, 90° elbow flexion, 90° ER → **arm falls into IR** |
| **Subscapularis** | IR at 0° abduction | **Belly press**, **lift-off**, **bear hug** (upper border), **IR lag**, **↑ passive ER** | Belly press: **elbow drops back** · lift-off: can't lift hand off back |

**✗ Wrong options (non-cuff tests)** — Yergason (resisted supination, elbow 90°) = biceps · Jerk = posterior labrum · Wright = **TOS** · relocation = anterior instability · Hawkins/Neer = impingement · O'Brien = SLAP

### Subscapularis tears – belly-press/lift-off tests, biceps instability

**Who** — **young**, acute avulsion, **hyperabduction/ER** (fall, wave, door handle) · iatrogenic after **open Bankart** · degenerative = superior portion; largest cuff muscle, prevalence under-recognised

**Symptoms** — **anterior shoulder pain** (mimics **subcoracoid impingement**)

**Signs** — **↑ passive ER** (e.g. 90° vs 45°, 100° vs 70°) · **↓ active IR** · belly press, lift-off, bear hug, IR lag

**✗ ROM traps** — ↑ passive flexion/extension/abduction · ↑ active IR · ↑ passive IR

**MRI** — SSc discontinuity off **lesser tuberosity** + **LHB out of groove (medial subluxation/dislocation)** = near-pathognomonic

**LHB dislocated medially → also injured?** — **subscapularis**; rare lesser tuberosity injury\
✗ Wrong options: MGHL · Bankart (not biceps stabilisers)

**Biceps dislocation routes** — beneath SSc · within SSc · extra-articular

**Arthroscopic findings (all of the above)** — uncovered lesser tuberosity · retraction to glenoid · avulsed SGHL · medial biceps subluxation

**Godenèche (218 scopes)** — pathologic LHB with intact SGHL/CHL in only **11%**; even then **medial sheath wall torn in 92%** → SGHL/CHL lesion = LHB nearly always unstable

**Comma sign** — **SGHL + CHL** complex attached to **superolateral corner of retracted SSc** (Lo & Burkhart) · trace it **inferiorly** → SSc; chronic SSc retracts medially, scarred to deltoid fascia

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Modality | Finding / fact |
|---|---|
| **X-ray** | True AP, AP IR/ER, axillary, **outlet view** (acromion); GT cysts, calcific tendonitis, CHL calcification, **type III acromion**, **AHI <7 mm** = proximal migration (chronic RCT) |
| **Arthrogram** | When **MRI contraindicated** (pacemaker); dye **GH → subacromial** = tear; MR arthrogram ↑ sens/spec |
| **MRI** | **Diagnostic standard**; size, shape, retraction; **fatty atrophy best on sagittal**; **medial biceps subluxation = SSc tear**; humeral head cysts in almost all chronic RCT |
| **ABER MRI** | ↓ effacement of articular surface → contrast enters defect → **↑ sensitivity for PT (PASTA) tears** |
| **Ultrasound** | Dynamic, cheap, confirms injections; **user dependent**; = MRI accuracy for cuff |

**Tangent sign** — sagittal oblique: line **scapular spine top → coracoid top**; SS belly **below line = positive** → **SS fatty atrophy → irreparable** / poorer outcome after repair (TO)\
✗ Wrong options: SSc atrophy · retraction medial to glenoid · biceps dislocation

### Diagnostic criteria – irreparable cuff

| Marker | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Size | **>5 cm** (Cofield) or **≥2 tendons** |
| Fatty atrophy | **Goutallier 3–4** |
| AHI | **<6 mm** (TO) · **<5–7 mm** (OB) · <7 mm = proximal migration (Orthobullets) |
| Tangent sign | **Positive** |
| Retraction | Medial to glenoid |
| Clinical | **Chronic pseudoparalysis** · **profound ER weakness** · long tear duration |
| Pattern | **Posterosuperior (SS + IS) > anterosuperior (SS + SSc)** |

**Pseudoparalysis** — can't actively elevate **>90°** (some authors **>45°**, TO), **full passive**; **no improvement after lidocaine** · **SSc + SS complete tear → 80% pseudoparalysis**

### Anatomic classification

| Type | Features |
|---|---|
| **SIT** (SS, IS, TM) | **Majority**; subacromial impingement; degenerative older or **dislocation >40 y** |
| **Subscapularis** | More prevalent than thought; **subcoracoid impingement**; acute avulsion young (hyperabduction/ER) or **iatrogenic failed repair** |

| Size | cm |
|---|---|
| Small | 0–1 |
| Medium | 1–3 |
| Large | 3–5 |

| Shape | Repair |

## 5 Management

```
Rotator cuff tear
├─ Asymptomatic / incidental → observation
├─ Atraumatic FT or PT → PT + NSAIDs + subacromial CSI, 3–6 mo
│      └─ fails → arthroscopic debridement or repair
├─ Acute traumatic FT (young; >40 after dislocation) → early repair
│      (do not delay for PT; better within 3 wk – 3/4 mo)
└─ Massive irreparable → partial repair / SCR / transfer / RTSA
```

### Operative criteria

| Indication | Procedure |
|---|---|
| **Low-grade PASTA** (<50%, <5–7 mm footprint) + impingement, failed nonop | **SAD + cuff debridement** |
| **Bursal >3 mm (>25%)** | Release, debride degenerative tissue + **repair** |
| **Articular >50%** (>6 mm (OB) · >7 mm (Orthobullets, OB) exposed footprint) | **Completion + repair** |
| PASTA, young, acute, **≥25% healthy bursal tissue** | **In situ repair** (bursal tissue left intact) |
| Intrasubstance tear, intact footprint | **Trans-tendon repair** |
| **Acute (<3 mo) traumatic tear, <60 y** | Repair (relative indication) |
| FT or PT failing nonop | Repair (relative) |
| Massive irreparable, intact SSc, no arthritis | SCR / LD / lower trapezius transfer |
| Massive + GH arthritis, **intact deltoid** | **RTSA** |
| **Relative CI to repair** | Advanced fatty infiltration/atrophy · significant GH arthritis |

### Operative vs nonoperative indications (acute traumatic vs atraumatic tears)

**Nonop regimen** — PT + NSAIDs + subacromial CSI = **first line**; avoid overhead; aggressive **cuff + scapular stabiliser** strengthening **3–6 mo**

**Partial tear, improving, never had formal PT (70 y)** — **formal PT**\
✗ Wrong options: repair · debridement · RTSA · repeat MRI unless unexplained progression

**PASTA, full strength, 6 wk (carpenter)** — **physical therapy** first\
✗ Wrong options: PRP · repair · SLAP repair · SAD

**Failed 12 wk PT + injection, PASTA** — **repair or debridement**\
✗ Wrong options: repeat injection · continued PT · capsular release (full passive ROM) · rotator interval release (for adhesive capsulitis or retracted SSc)

**Low-grade PASTA + impingement, 5/5 strength, failed nonop** — **SAD + debridement**\
✗ Wrong options: SR/DR repair · labral repair · tenodesis

**Subacute FT SS tear, weakness, type 1 acromion, no fatty change (47 y)** — **rotator cuff repair alone**\
✗ Wrong options: repeat CSI · porcine SIS xenograft · + acromioplasty · + remplissage/tenodesis/DCE

**AAOS points** — exercise + NSAIDs for PT tears · **routine acromioplasty not required** · **non-cross-linked porcine SIS xenograft not to be used** · workers' comp → less favourable outcome

**Purpose of repair** — restore muscle function; then ↓ pain, prevent irreversible atrophy

**Massive tear SS + SSc, 40 y, 5 mo, no atrophy** — **rotator cuff repair**; attempt repair before salvage; surgery **within 6 mo** → better outcome\
✗ Wrong options: debridement/SAD/DCE · pec major transfer · 6 wk more PT · SCR

**Traumatic FT tear, young — outcome driver** — **time to surgical repair** (repair within **3–4 mo**; acute repair **within 3 wk** best) · (✗ Wrong options: BMI · age · male gender) · higher preop ASES → ↑ VAS improvement only

**Atraumatic FT tear — strongest predictor of surgery** — **patient expectation of PT** (also higher activity, non-smoker) · pain not related to anatomic severity · pain ↔ comorbidities, lower education, race

### Partial-thickness tears – PASTA, bursal vs articular, debridement vs completion & repair

**PASTA (TO)** — **articular side**; far more common than bursal or intratendinous

**Articular vs bursal** — articular **more common** · bursal **more symptomatic** (subacromial bursitis) · **bursal = higher risk of progression** · articular fares better → higher % tolerated before repair

**Repair thresholds** — **bursal >25% (>3 mm)** · **articular >50%**\
✗ Wrong options: bursal <25% · articular >25%

**Articular >50% tear but poor lateral tissue** — **complete the tear → repair**\
✗ Wrong options: fascial graft · allograft · trans-tendon · biopsy

**Bursal tear 4 mm deep, 1.5 cm AP, softening** — **convert to FT + repair with suture anchors**\
✗ Wrong options: debride + acromioplasty · abort · incidental

**Footprint exposed 3 mm, bursal intact** — **debridement + SAD** (<5 mm exposure ≈ <50% thickness; ✗ Wrong options: completion · transtendinous repair · tenotomy · synovectomy)

**6 mm ML width tear** — **50%** of SS tendon

**Rotator cable involved** — **bursal 4 mm tear involving anterior cable → repair** · intact cable 3–4 mm → less likely to need repair · articular <7 mm → debridement · SSc upper-border fraying without retraction → no repair (but often-missed tear)

**High-grade PASTA (>50%), failed 9 mo PT + PRP (tennis, 51 y)** — **tear completion + repair to footprint**\
✗ Wrong options: isolated DCE (negative cross-body) · more nonop · SAD ± tenodesis · trans-tendon + DCE

```
Partial-thickness tear, failed nonop
├─ Articular <50% → debridement ± SAD
├─ Articular >50% → completion + repair
│      └─ young, acute, healthy bursal tissue → in situ repair
├─ Bursal >25% (>3 mm) → completion + repair
└─ Rotator cable disrupted → repair
```

### Subscapularis repair – biceps, 3-sided release (TO)

```
Subscapularis tear
├─ Repairable → SSc repair + biceps tenodesis/tenotomy
│      (not biceps recentering, groove deepening, tenosynovectomy)
├─ Chronic retracted, atrophied → subcoracoid pec major transfer
└─ Elderly, irreparable → RTSA
```

**Biceps with SSc tear** — unstable → **tenodesis or tenotomy** (recentering unpredictable; prevents recurrent biceps pain/failure) · tenodesis **low in groove**; address biceps **first** (rubs/fails repair)

**SSc tear after open Bankart (6 mo)** — **subscapularis repair** + biceps tenotomy/tenodesis\
✗ Wrong options: PT · tenolysis → cosmetic deformity · pec major (tendon repairable)

**Which lesion → RCR + biceps tenodesis?** — **SSc tear with dislocated biceps**; no significant GH arthritis\
✗ Wrong options: Hill-Sachs · GT fracture · bony Bankart · Bankart

**Chronic retracted atrophied SSc (55 y carpenter)** — **pectoralis major transfer** under conjoined tendon · Achilles allograft alternative for degenerated SSc\
✗ Wrong options: fusion = salvage · CSI · arthroscopic/open repair

**Lafosse IV — release from coracoid base (TO)** — **coracohumeral ligament**\
✗ Wrong options: CA ligament · transverse humeral ligament · conjoined origin

| 3-sided release (TO) | Structure |
|---|---|

**Arthroscopic vs open SSc repair** — arthroscopic superior motion + pain (Orthobullets)

### Repair technique – single vs double row, transosseous-equivalent & failure mode

**Mini-open** — deltoid-splitting; ↓ deltoid avulsion vs open; immediate PROM; active ~6 wk · arthroscopic = equivalent results

**Arthroscopic vs open (medium tear)** — **no difference in retear rate** or function · arthroscopic → earlier rehab (no deltoid to protect) · mini-open → worse early pain/function only

**Interval slides** — **anterior** = release SS from rotator interval (incise CHL) · **posterior** = release SS from IS, then margin convergence

**Double-row (TOE)** — medial **mattress** + lateral **simple** → **more anatomic footprint**, larger contact area

| DR vs SR | Finding |
|---|---|
| **Retear** | **DR lower** (esp tears/retraction **>3 cm**); SR higher (Millett meta-analysis, 7 level I RCTs) |
| Healing rate | DR higher |
| Biomechanics | DR **higher ultimate tensile load**; = peak-to-peak elongation, stiffness, conditioning elongation |
| Function, pain, time to heal (short/mid term) | **No difference** (esp <3 cm) |
| Long term | DR may improve function (one RCT) |
| Cost | DR more (more anchors) |
| Blood loss | No difference |

**Lowest retear risk** — **Goutallier 1 + DR TOE**\
✗ Wrong options: Goutallier 3–4 · SR

**Two DR constructs (B, C)** — equivalent retear; SR (A) possibly higher; **A and B = equivalent outcome scores at 1 y**

**Vascularity** — **anchor drilling** (holes in GT) + **peribursal tissue** → main supply to repair; ↑ with exercise

**✗ Wrong options (false repair "truths")** — motion restriction ↑ blood flow · DR better clinical results · SAD ↑ healing · non-healing always → poor outcome (**~half don't heal**, outcomes preserved)

**GT trough (cancellous)** — **not stronger** repair (animal models)

**Failure mode** — **suture pull-out through tendon = tissue failure** (most common, early) → gap formation → poor tendon–bone healing

| Less common failure | Note |
|---|---|
| Anchor breakage | Biocomposite fatigues less than metal |
| Anchor pull-out | Poor BMD, improper placement |
| Eyelet abrasion suture rupture | Metal anchors (sharper) |
| Knot failure | |
| Infection | ~1.9% |

**Causes of failed RCR** — failure to heal (**19–94%**: poor tissue, vascularity, bone) · surgical (deltoid, infection, foreign body, stiffness, nerve) · diagnostic (missed interval, LHB, SSc) · technical (excess tension, anchor placement); arthroscopic RCR complication rate ~10%

**Traumatic retear 3 wk post-op, 1 cm, <65 y** — **revision RCR** (healing >95%)\
✗ Wrong options: PT · CSI · sling · debridement (for chronic irreparable)

**Revision RCR vs RTSA** — RTSA favoured by older age, **static proximal humeral migration**; also aetiology of retear, tissue/MRI quality

### Massive/irreparable tears – partial repair, SCR & tendon transfers

```
Massive irreparable tear
├─ Elderly / CTA / pseudoparalysis → RTSA
├─ Young, posterosuperior, intact SSc + deltoid, no OA → LD or lower trapezius transfer / SCR
│      └─ SSc irreparable → no SCR or LD transfer
├─ Chronic irreparable SSc → pec major transfer
└─ Part of cuff reaches GT → partial repair
```

**Partial repair** — SS can't reach GT, posterior cuff can → **partial repair** (balances coronal + axial force couples) · pain options: acromioplasty + CA release, **reverse acromioplasty (tuberoplasty)**, biceps tenotomy · ✗ RTSA in 48 y · other young options: SCR, transfers, **subacromial balloon spacer** (none superior)

**Pseudoparesis vs pseudoparalysis** — **active FE >90° (e.g. 100°) after lidocaine injection → pseudoparesis** (pain) · no change → pseudoparalysis · arthritis/retraction occur in both

**Young (35 y), pseudoparalysis, AHD <6 mm, Goutallier 3, minimal arthritis — least preferred (TO)** — **RSA** (✓ LD, ✓ lower trapezius, ✓ SCR)

| SCR (TO, Mihata) | Detail |
|---|---|
| Mechanism | Passive constraint to **superior head translation**; space-occupier + tether; restores force couples |
| Graft | **Folded fascia lata 6–8 mm** · acellular dermal **3–4 mm**; anchors **superior glenoid rim → medial GT** |
| **Indications** | Failed nonop massive irreparable **SS + IS**, **Hamada 1–2**, **intact/reparable SSc**, **functional deltoid**; intolerable pain; pseudoparalysis |
| **Contraindications** | **Irreparable SSc** (subscapular nerves) · **Hamada ≥3** · GH OA |
| Best candidate | **59 y, SS + IS, normal X-ray** |

**SCR caveat** — irreparability confirmed only after full diagnostic arthroscopy (Hartzler & Burkhart); short-term data only, graft failure risk

| Transfer | Indication | Key facts |
|---|---|---|
| **Pectoralis major** (subcoracoid, **under conjoined**) | **Chronic irreparable SSc** | Replicates SSc vector; restores IR; **4–6 wk rigid immobilisation**; not posterosuperior |
| **Latissimus dorsi** (± teres major) | **Irreparable posterosuperior (SS + IS)**, **young labourer**, Goutallier ≥3 | To cuff/SSc/GT; brace **6 wk, 45° abd, 30° ER**; restores ER + elevation |
| **Lower trapezius** (± **Achilles allograft**) | Irreparable posterosuperior, middle-aged labourer | More anatomic; **superior outcomes vs LD** (Orthobullets); restores ER |

**Transfer counselling** — pain relief more reliable than function (Omid & Lee)

**LD transfer — best candidate** — **young labourer, massive posterosuperior tear + SS atrophy/fatty infiltration** · **some active elevation (90–100°)** · intact SSc + deltoid, no OA (e.g. 50 y, FE 100°, ER −10°)

**LD transfer — worse outcomes** — **positive lift-off / SSc deficiency** (strongest; Gerber, Irlenbusch) · **deltoid weakness/detachment** · **nonsynergistic** (out-of-phase) action · stage 3–4 fatty degeneration, poor tendon · **female** · preop elevation **<90°** · **complete loss of ER** · superior escape · salvage after failed repair (vs primary)

**Not a worse-outcome factor** — **absence of CA ligament** · other prior surgery

**LD relative CI** — SSc deficiency · deltoid deficiency · pseudoparalysis · advanced GH arthritis · CTA (stays painful) · >80 y pseudoparalytic · acute tear in young (repair first) · thoracodorsal palsy · disability claimant

**Belly press elbow drops back** — SSc dysfunction → **↓ LD transfer benefit**\
✗ Wrong options: ↑ passive IR · drop sign = SS · Hornblower = TM · axillary fold ecchymosis = pec major rupture

**Posterosuperior deficiency + intact belly press → transfer?** — **latissimus dorsi**\
✗ Wrong options: pec major = SSc · pec minor · conjoined = instability · LHB

**LD nerves at risk** — **radial nerve** deep (anterior) to LD tendon, **~3 cm medial to insertion** → **IR arm** at tenotomy; injury → **EPL (extensor pollicis longus)** + wrist/digit extensors · posterior branch **axillary** (deep fascia of posterior deltoid, tendon passage)

**Lower trapezius best for** — **45 y manual labourer, irreparable posterosuperior, loss of ER**\
✗ Wrong options: pitcher SLAP · irreparable SSc → pec major · GH OA → TSA · CTA → RTSA

**Retear 2 mo after massive repair (rough PT), ER lag** — **revision repair SS/IS**; failure: intrinsic (age, size, tissue, systemic disease, smoking) + extrinsic (construct, aggressive rehab)\
✗ Wrong options: SSc revision · LD first · interval release · anatomic TSA

**RTSA mechanism** — **lengthens deltoid lever arm**; COR **medial + inferior**; convex glenoid + concave humerus; ↓ shear · survival **90–95% at 10 y** · most reliable for 65 y painter with SS + IS atrophy, no OA\
✗ Wrong options: dermal augmentation · SCR · partial repair · trapezius transfer

**Other grafts (Orthobullets)** — xenograft (bovine dermis/intestine; mixed) · allograft (human skin/fascia; good short term) · synthetic (foreign body reaction) · **lateral acromionectomy = contraindicated** (historic)

### Concomitant procedures (acromioplasty, distal clavicle excision, suprascapular nerve release)

| Add-on to RCR | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Acromioplasty / SAD** | No difference in PROs, symptomatic retear, ROM, strength, pain · **lower long-term reoperation** (Level 1, esp **type 2–3** acromion; OB) · "routine acromioplasty not recommended" (AAOS, OB) — read the options |
| **Distal clavicle excision** | **No effect on 2-y/long-term outcomes**, ROM, revision |
| **SSN release** | **No difference** in pain or function; SSN dysfunction in **8–27%** of massive tears (traction from retraction → fatty infiltration) |
| LHB tenotomy vs tenodesis | Negligible difference |
| CA ligament release | ↑ anterior/inferior GH translation |

**No long-term effect either** — mini-open vs arthroscopic · starting PT at 6 wk · SR vs DR

**Negative predictors of RCR outcome** — smoking · severe obesity · tear size · fatty infiltration · **age 70+** · pre-existing arthropathy · **workers' comp**

### Postoperative rehabilitation – early vs delayed motion

**Healing** — tendon-to-GT healing **8–12 wk** = rate-limiting step · protect ≥**6 wk**, ~**8 wk** for large 2-tendon tear

**Protocol** — **early PROM** (prevents adhesive capsulitis) → **AROM at 6 wk** → strengthening **≥2–3 mo** · no active strengthening at 1 mo\
✗ Wrong options: isometric ER · eccentric SS · resistance · deltoid (can't isolate from cuff)

**EMG** — **passive forward flexion = minimal cuff activation** · active, active-assisted, isometric, **pulley-assisted** all activate cuff (avoid early)

| Early (<6 wk) vs delayed (6 wk sling) | Result |
|---|---|
| Functional outcomes | **Equivalent** |
| Terminal ROM / stiffness at 1 y | **No difference** |
| Retear / healing | No difference (Cuff & Pupello, 68 pts) · **delayed may ↑ healing** |
| Short-term PROs | Not better with early; early may ↑ pain |

**PT not available first 6 wk** — **won't affect ROM at 1 y**\
✗ Wrong options: peer-to-peer · home band strengthening · strength testing · prophylactic CSI

**Scapular plane forward elevation, prone retraction, 2 mo post-RCR (TO)** — **lower trapezius** (LT > MT + SA) · SA activity ↑ with elevation angle; **LT ↑ steeply at end range** · deficit = endurance/control/timing, not weakness → low–moderate repetitive resistance

| Trapezius part (TO) | Exercise |
|---|---|
| Upper | Shrug ± retraction; overhead shrug |
| Middle | Overhead retraction against wall |
| **Lower** | **Prone scapular setting** (retract + depress, lift arm off table) |
| Lower + middle | Prone scapular retraction, arm to ~120° |

**Return to work** — **full duty 6–10 mo** · light duty after progressive strengthening (earliest **8–10 wk**) · labourers later + lower outcomes than desk jobs; most return to full · PROM PT starts 2–6 wk

**Analgesia** — NSAIDs improve pain at 1 wk (Orthobullets)

## 6 Complications

### Healing & retear risk factors (age, fatty infiltration, workers' comp, injections)

**Retear rate** — **10–90%** · massive chronic ~**50%**\
✗ Wrong options: <5%

| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Age >65** | **Highest risk of non-healing** (✗ Wrong options: protective); >70 → inferior outcomes |
| **Goutallier grade** (3–4) | **Greatest retear risk** (vs age, rehab, technique, gender) |
| Tear **>5 cm**, multiple tendons, retraction **medial to glenoid** | ↑ retear |
| Diabetes · smoking · severe obesity | ↑ retear |
| Poor post-op compliance | ↑ retear |
| Concomitant AC / biceps procedure | ↑ retear (Orthobullets) |
| **Workers' comp / work-related** | **↓ functional improvement + ↓ satisfaction**, later RTW |
| **Frequent CSI** | **↑ revision surgery** (frequency-dependent) |
| Not risk factors | Female gender · DR technique · delayed rehab · hand dominance · BMI/age/sex (young traumatic tear) |

**Timing of surgery** — repair **<3 mo** from acute tear → better · operative time >45 min → not better (longer = overnight stay, SSI)

### Complications – infection, positioning & regional anaesthesia

**Suprascapular nerve (TO)** — on **undersurface of SS + IS**; **~2.9 cm from superior glenoid rim**, **1.8 cm from scapular spine** → **no sharp dissection >1 cm medial to glenoid rim** · tethered at supraspinous fossa + notch → **tension on lateral SS advancement** (and medial retraction) · injured during **posterosuperior cuff mobilisation** in massive RCR, posteriorly directed glenoid-neck sutures (anterior labral repair), glenoid osteotomy\
✗ Wrong options: thoracoacromial a. · axillary n. · PCHA

**Infection** — **<1%** (Orthobullets) · ~**1.9%** (OB) · **0.006–3.4%** (OB) — read the options; S. aureus, strep, **C. acnes** (formerly *P. acnes*)

**C. acnes** — **delayed/indolent** (10 wk), healed portals, mild ↑ ESR/CRP, Gram negative → anaerobe, cultures positive **7–21 days**\
✗ Wrong options: Pseudomonas (water-borne, not shoulder)

**Pre-op steroid injection** — **within 1 month → highest infection risk** (**65–75% ↑**) · 1–3 mo ↑ · **>3 mo = no ↑ risk** → delay surgery ≥3 mo · not dose, location, number, steroid type

**6 injections, surgery 3 mo later** — **↑ revision, no ↑ infection**

**Pneumothorax** — after **interscalene/supraclavicular block** or arthroscopy · risk = **smoking/COPD** (hyperinflated lungs, high pleural dome) · dyspnoea also from block **diaphragm paralysis** → CXR\
✗ Wrong options: DM · revision · obesity · SSc repair

**Beach chair** — **LFCN palsy** up to **2%**; resolves <6 mo; padding, seatbelt, slight reverse Trendelenburg; **BMI >35** risk\
✗ Wrong options: femoral · obturator · pudendal (perineal post/hip scope) · sciatic

**Others** — **deltoid detachment** (open) · AC pain · axillary nerve injury · stiffness (early PT/ROM doesn't reduce at 1 y)
