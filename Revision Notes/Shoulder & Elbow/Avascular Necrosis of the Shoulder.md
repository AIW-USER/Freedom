# Avascular Necrosis of the Shoulder

*Shoulder & Elbow · 5 / 60*

## 1 Basics

### Pathogenesis & diagnosis (corticosteroid risk)

**Pathogenesis** — interrupted blood supply → **cellular death of subchondral bone** → resorption + remodelling → **subchondral collapse** → incongruity → arthritis

**✗ Pathogenesis distractors** — cell-mediated synovial hypertrophy = **RA** · humoral response after infection, HLA-B27 = **Reiter's** · hyperuricaemic crystals = **gout** · bacterial PMN cartilage destruction = **septic arthritis**

**Most common cause** — **corticosteroids** (asthma, Crohn's flares, SLE, leukaemia chemotherapy, graft-versus-host disease); also **sickle cell**, chronic **alcohol**

**Young patients** — high-dose steroids in chemotherapy regimens → ↑ demand for arthroplasty in the young

**vs hip** — humeral head AVN **less common than hip**; same pathophysiology (genetic predisposition + risk factors such as steroids); treatment similar to hip, based on **subchondral collapse**

**Workup** — proximal humerus AVN on X-ray → also get **hip radiographs**

| ASEPTIC mnemonic | Cause |
|---|---|
| **A** | Alcohol, AIDS |
| **S** | **Steroids (most common)**, Sickle cell, SLE |
| **E** | Erlenmeyer flask (**Gaucher**) |
| **P** | Pancreatitis |
| **T** | Trauma |
| **I** | Idiopathic / Infection |
| **C** | Caisson's (the bends) |

**Gaucher (wrong option)** — **large bony infarcts** + severe pain; less common cause than chronic steroids

### Post-traumatic AVN

| Proximal humerus injury | AVN rate |
|---|---|
| **4-part fracture-dislocation** | **~100%** |
| Displaced 4-part | ~45% |
| 3-part | ~14% |
| **Valgus-impacted 4-part** | **~11%** |

### Anatomy – humeral head blood supply

**Anterior humeral circumflex** — **ascending branch** runs **lateral to LHB tendon in bicipital groove** → **arcuate artery** = intraosseous continuation, penetrates head · **35%**\
✗ Wrong options: injure when plating

**Posterior humeral circumflex** — **main supply** per most current literature · **65%**

## 3 Presentation

**Symptoms** — **insidious** shoulder pain, often no inciting event · ↓ motion · crepitus · weakness

**Exam** — limited ROM (pain throughout arc) · crepitus · cuff + deltoid weakness (cuff strength may be normal)

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

**X-ray** — 5 views; best seen on **neutral rotation AP** · **normal at onset** · osteolytic lesion first at **superior-middle humeral head** (most common initial site)

**Stage signs (X-ray)** — **"snowcap" sign** = sclerosis, contour preserved (II) · **crescent sign** = subchondral fracture (III) · flattening/collapse, loss of sphericity (IV) · glenoid changes (V)

**MRI** — **preferred** modality · **~100% sensitive** · early = **↓ subchondral signal on T1 and T2** (oedema) · variegated pattern · metaphyseal oedema common/normal

![Cruess stage II: snowcap sclerosis with preserved contour (X-ray, T1, T2 fat-sat)](../../MCQ/attachments/q4809-a-fig-a-2nd-revision.jpg)

### Cruess classification

| Stage | Radiographic findings | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **I** | **Normal X-ray, MRI changes only** | Core decompression |
| **II** | **Sclerosis** (wedged, mottled), osteopenia; snowcap | Core decompression |
| **III** | **Crescent sign** = subchondral fracture | Resurfacing or hemiarthroplasty |
| **IV** | **Flattening and collapse** (glenoid spared) | Hemiarthroplasty (or resurfacing) |
| **V** | Degenerative changes **extend to glenoid** | **TSA** |

![Cruess stages I–V](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob3060-cruess-classification.jpg)

**Ficat-Arlet** — hip staging adapted to the shoulder (used in core decompression series)

## 5 Management

```
Humeral head AVN
├─ First line → analgesia, activity modification (no overhead/manual labour), PT
├─ Precollapse (Cruess I–II) → core decompression ± arthroscopy
├─ Collapse, glenoid + cuff intact (III–IV) → hemiarthroplasty
│      └─ focal III defect + enough epiphyseal bone → resurfacing
├─ Glenoid involved (V) → TSA
└─ Older, cuff pathology → reverse TSA
```

### Operative criteria

| Procedure | Indication | Not when |
|---|---|---|
| **Core decompression** (+ arthroscopy to confirm cartilage) | **Precollapse Cruess I–II** | Subchondral fracture/flattening (post-collapse = **contraindicated**) |
| **Resurfacing hemiarthroplasty** | **Stage III focal chondral defect + sufficient epiphyseal bone**; least bone removal | Large defect · global head involvement |
| **Hemiarthroplasty** | **III–IV**: collapse, **glenoid intact, cuff intact**; young → preserves glenoid bone stock for later TSA | **Irreparable cuff tear** · glenoid changes |
| **TSA** | **Stage V** (glenoid involved) | Young + intact glenoid (glenoid loosening) |
| **Reverse TSA** | Older + cuff pathology | **Intact cuff**, young |

### Treatment by stage – core decompression vs resurfacing/hemiarthroplasty

**Core decompression** — ↓ subchondral marrow pressure → **neovascularisation** → slows progression · ± bone graft into void · percutaneous small-diameter technique (Harreld: no arthroplasty at 32 mo, 95% improved)

| Core decompression (LaPorte, ~10 y; Ficat stage) | Success |
|---|---|
| I | **94%** |
| II | **88%** |
| III | 70% |
| IV | 14% |

**Stage I–II outcome** — good/excellent **88–100%** at 5–10 y → **~90% successful long term** · better in **non-steroid** than steroid cases\
✗ Wrong options: 50% satisfied · most need arthroplasty by 10 y · unpredictable · not indicated

**Conflict on core decompression** — effective for I–II (Mont, LaPorte; LaPorte recommends I–III; Harreld tries it with arthroscopy in III) · role in precollapse "**highly debated**" and drilling did **not** alter progression (L'insalata) · Sarris: I–II **conservative**, surgery when persistently symptomatic + collapse — read the options

**Arthroscopic debridement / release** — no sustained benefit; AVN keeps progressing; role unknown

**Most reliable, durable treatment after collapse** — **prosthetic arthroplasty** → **hemiarthroplasty**\
✗ Wrong options: arthroscopic debridement · arthrodesis · resection arthroplasty · cortisone injection

**Arthrodesis indications** — brachial plexus injury · infantile paralysis · deltoid + cuff insufficiency · chronic infection · failed revision arthroplasty · refractory instability · tumour resection bone loss

**Stemless resurfacing** — preserves metaphyseal bone · Levy & Copeland 285 cementless · Hawi (Eclipse) 9 y, no loosening

**"Ream and run"** — HA + glenoid reaming → unnecessary with intact glenoid cartilage

**Arthroplasty outcomes** — HA + TSA both safe/effective · 10-y survivorship **HA 88.9%, TSA 80%**; **TSA better internal rotation** (Wang) · RSA ≈ TSA when cuff compromised · AVN arthroplasty has **↑ complications** vs other diagnoses (bone quality, comorbidity)

**Prognosis** — related to **stage**; stage + lesion size predict progression

## 6 Complications

**Hemiarthroplasty** — **glenoid cartilage wear** over time → 45% unsatisfactory (Smith, steroid AVN); still used in young active III–IV

**Glenoid component in young** — **loosening** risk → avoid TSA/RTSA when glenoid spared

## X vs Y

| | **AVN** | **Calcific tendinitis** | **OA** | **Gaucher** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Onset | Insidious, steroid user | **Severe acute pain** | Long-standing symptoms | Severe pain |
| Imaging | **Subchondral** ↓ T1/T2 signal, collapse, fragmentation | **Hypointense deposit in cuff soft tissue** | No subchondral collapse/fragmentation | **Large bony infarcts** |

| Stage | **Core decompression** | **Hemiarthroplasty** | **TSA** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cruess | I–II (precollapse) | III–IV (collapse, glenoid spared) | V (glenoid) |
| Cuff | — | Must be intact | Intact |
| Key trap | Not after collapse | Not with irreparable cuff or glenoid cysts | Not in young with intact glenoid |

## How it's asked

**"Asthmatic, 20 y steroids, humeral head collapse — pathogenesis?"** → **cellular death of subchondral bone after vascular interruption**\
✗ Wrong options: RA · Reiter's · gout · septic

**"Crohn's on prednisone, atraumatic pain, MRI subchondral lesion — diagnosis?"** → **osteonecrosis**\
✗ Wrong options: Gaucher · OA · cuff tendinopathy · calcific tendinitis

**"Steroid user, snowcap sclerosis, contour preserved — surgery?"** → **core decompression**\
✗ Wrong options: HA · RTSA with intact cuff · resurfacing · TSA

**"Leukaemia + high-dose cortisone, collapse — most reliable pain relief?"** → **hemiarthroplasty**\
✗ Wrong options: debridement · arthrodesis · resection · injection

**"39F, collapse + loss of sphericity, glenoid spared, failed PT — procedure?"** → **resurfacing hemiarthroplasty**\
✗ Wrong options: arthroscopic release · core decompression post-collapse · TSA/RTSA glenoid loosening

**"GVHD steroid AVN — best case for hemiarthroplasty?"** → **subchondral fracture + intact cuff**\
✗ Wrong options: sclerosis = decompression · irreparable cuff · glenoid cysts = TSA

**"SLE patient had HA — preop imaging?"** → **isolated humeral head collapse** (Cruess IV)\
✗ Wrong options: glenoid changes = V · osteopenia / MRI-only / mottled sclerosis = I–II

**"Leukaemia, flattening + collapse, glenoid intact, cuff normal?"** → **hemiarthroplasty**\
✗ Wrong options: TSA · core decompression · ream and run · RTSA

**"Alcoholic, Cruess II — core decompression outcome?"** → **~90% successful long term**

**"Humeral head AVN on X-ray — what else to image?"** → **hips**
