# Posterior Shoulder Instability & Dislocation

*Shoulder & Elbow · 36 / 60*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Incidence** — **2–5%** of unstable shoulders (Orthobullets) · **2–12%** of instability (OB) — read the options

**Incidence (TO)** — **2–4%** of all dislocations · causes: **epilepsy, electrocution, seizures, dashboard injuries** · **subacromial type commonest** (head posterior to glenoid, inferior to acromion)

**Missed** — **50%** of traumatic posterior dislocations in ED undiagnosed · Hawkins & Neer: 41 locked dislocations, **50% with fracture**, 20 missed, mean **1 y** to diagnosis

**Prior dislocation** — up to **50%** of patients evaluated for posterior instability report a prior posterior dislocation · frank dislocation in only **2–5%** of posterior labral tears

**Risk factors** — **ligamentous laxity** · **glenoid retroversion** / hypoplasia (less common) · not excessive glenoid **anteversion** = anterior instability

**Seizures + electric shock** — tetanic contraction pulls head out; posterior dislocation unlikely without such force · anterior **still more common** with seizures (Orthobullets, OB) · posterior "more common after seizure" / **as common as anterior in epilepsy** (OB) — read the options

**Atraumatic (esp. posterior) dislocation** — warrants **neurological workup**

### Anatomy – posterior restraints

| Restraint | Role |
|---|---|
| **Posterior band IGHL** | **Primary restraint in IR**; most important at **90° flexion + IR** |
| **SGHL + CHL** | Primary **static** stabiliser to posterior subluxation in **flexion, adduction, IR** · also inferior translation of adducted arm + ER |
| **Subscapularis** | **Primary dynamic restraint against posterior subluxation** (and in ER) |
| Labrum | Deepens glenoid **50%** |

**Restraint conflict** — posterior band IGHL (OB, Orthobullets) vs SGHL (OB, Orthobullets) for flexion + IR — read the position in the stem

| ✗ Restraint distractor | Role |
|---|---|
| Anterior band IGHL | Anterior/inferior at **90° abduction + max ER** (late cocking) |
| MGHL | Anterior + posterior at **~45° abduction** in ER |
| Anterior labrum | Anterior; IGHL anchors here → Bankart |
| Superior labrum | **Biceps anchor** |

**Dynamic stabilisers by direction** — posterior labral tear → **subscapularis** · anterior labral tear / HAGL → **infraspinatus + teres minor**

### Tubercle of Assaki (TO)

**Tubercle of Assaki (TO)** — ridge / **focal elevation of subchondral bone at centre of glenoid** · **normal variant** · **focal thinning of overlying cartilage** · not a cartilage defect \
✗ Wrong options: "depression of humeral head cartilage"

## 2 Mechanism

**High-risk position** — arm **flexed, adducted, internally rotated** + **axial load**\
✗ Wrong options: abduction + ER = anterior · hyperabduction = **luxatio erecta**

| Type | Mechanism | Presentation |
|---|---|---|
| **Acute (dislocation)** | Trauma (~50%), **seizure, electric shock** | Dramatic |
| **Chronic (instability)** | **Repetitive microtrauma** → labral tear, incomplete avulsion, erosion, **patulous posterior capsule** | Insidious, vague **pain > instability** |

**At-risk athletes** — **football linemen** (blocking), linebackers, **weightlifters/bench press**, push-ups, weighted dips, rugby, swimmers, overhead athletes · stretches posteroinferior GHL

**Classification** — acute vs chronic · voluntary vs involuntary

| Associated lesion | Association |
|---|---|
| Posterior band IGHL avulsion | Acute subluxation |
| **Reverse Bankart** | Detached posteroinferior capsulolabral complex |
| **Reverse Hill-Sachs** | **Locked**, hard-to-reduce dislocation |
| Posterior labral cyst / posterior glenoid rim fracture | Chronic reverse Bankart |
| **Lesser tuberosity fracture** | Acute posterior dislocation |
| Large capsular pouch | Chronic instability (MR arthrogram) |

| Posterior lesion (TO) | Description |
|---|---|
| **Reverse bony Bankart** | Avulsion fracture of posteroinferior glenoid |
| **POLPSA** | **Posterior labrocapsular periosteal sleeve avulsion**: capsule stripped with intact periosteum |
| **Reverse HAGL** | Posteroinferior capsule avulsed from **humerus** |
| **Kim lesion** | **Incomplete** posteroinferior tear between labrum + cartilage · **always probe** at arthroscopy |

**Reverse Hill-Sachs (TO)** — **anteromedial** humeral head · Hill-Sachs = **posterosuperolateral**\
✗ Wrong options: posterolateral

**Cuff tears** — **not** commonly associated with posterior dislocation

## 3 Presentation

**Acute dislocation** — **prominent posterior shoulder + coracoid** · **locked in IR + adduction** · comfortable in "sling position"

**Most consistent sign** — **lack of ER** (mechanical block: anterior head defect on posterior glenoid) · ER loss differential: adhesive capsulitis, GH OA

**Chronic instability** — may look normal · pain with **flexion, adduction, IR** · slipping when pass-blocking, pain bench pressing/push-ups

### Recurrent posterior instability – Kim lesion/labral tear, jerk & Kim tests, restraints

**Kim lesion (TO)** — **incomplete + concealed avulsion of posteroinferior labrum** (superficial labrum intact, deep labrum detached) in posterior or multidirectional posteroinferior instability · usually **diagnosed at arthroscopy** · needs **arthroscopic repair**\
✗ Wrong options: ALPSA = anterior labral periosteal sleeve avulsion · meniscal root avulsion

**Posterior labral tear site** — **posteroinferior quadrant**: **6–9 o'clock right**, **3–6 o'clock left**

| Test | Technique | Positive / use |
|---|---|---|
| **Jerk** | 90° abduction/flexion + IR, elbow bent · **axial load** along humerus → adduct into forward flexion (bring back to extension → reduces) | **Clunk** ± pain · posterior labral tear in **mid-range** glenoid |
| **Kim** | Seated, 90° abduction → **flex to 45°** + axial load at elbow + posteroinferior force (Orthobullets) · arm elevated **125°** + IR, downward + posterior force (OB) | **Pain** (± clunk) · more sensitive for **posteroinferior** tears |
| **Jerk + Kim** | Combined | **97% sensitive** for posterior labral tear |
| **Load & shift (TO)** | Push head anterior/posterior with thumb | **90% sensitivity, 85% specificity** |
| **Circumduction (TO)** | Circumduction under load | Reproduces **clunk** → confirms instability |
| Posterior stress | Stabilise scapula, push posteriorly | Pain common, **not specific** |
| **Load & shift** | Supine, neutral rotation, 40–60° abduction + flexion | Grade translation |

![Kim test](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob3051-kim-test-moved.jpg)

| Load & shift grade | Translation |
|---|---|
| 1+ | Apparent, not to rim |
| 2+ | **To glenolabral rim** |
| 3+ | **Over** rim |
| 4+ | Complete dislocation |

| ✗ Test distractor | Assesses |
|---|---|
| Apprehension / relocation (abd + ER) | **Anterior** instability, Bankart |
| Hornblower; ER weakness at 90° abduction | **Teres minor** (axillary n.) |
| Jobe | Supraspinatus |
| Speed / Yergason | Biceps |
| O'Brien | SLAP |
| Belly-press, lift-off, IR lag | Subscapularis |
| Sulcus | Rotator interval |
| Pinch grip | AIN |

| ✗ Lesion distractor | Feature |
|---|---|
| Bankart / Hill-Sachs | Anterior instability, apprehension |
| **GIRD** | Throwers, **tight posteroinferior capsule**, ↓ IR → posterior impingement, **anterior subluxation** |
| AC separation | Piano key sign |
| SLAP | Overhead pain, dead arm |
| PASTA | Partial articular supraspinatus avulsion |
| Pec major tear | ↓ adduction + IR, lost axillary fold |

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Locked posterior dislocation – diagnosis (axillary/Velpeau view, loss of ER, seizures, electrocution)

**AP** — **unreliable** · **"lightbulb" sign** (head internally rotated) · humeral head **overlaps glenoid**

![Lightbulb sign on AP](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob3051-light-bulb-sign.jpg)

| Radiographic sign (TO) | Description |
|---|---|
| **Vacant glenoid** | Empty anterior glenoid fossa |
| **Rim sign** | Anterior rim–head distance **>6 mm** |
| **Light bulb** | Internally rotated head looks circular |
| **Trough sign** | Reverse Hill-Sachs (anterior head impaction line) |
| **Moloney's arch** | **Acute angle** of scapulohumeral arch |

**Apparently normal AP (TO)** — posterior dislocation **often missed**

**Axillary lateral** — **best view**; mandatory (needs **20–30° abduction**) · **more sensitive than trans-scapular** · any suspected dislocation → full trauma series (AP, scapular Y, axillary)

**Can't abduct (pain/spasm)** — **Velpeau view**\
✗ Wrong options: Zanca = AC/clavicle · Stryker notch = Hill-Sachs · MRI not first-line in trauma · EUA is for locked dislocation

**Scapular Y** — humeral articular surface **posterior** to glenoid

**CT** — chronic dislocation (**>2–3 wk**): size of **reverse Hill-Sachs** + bone loss · posterior glenoid bone loss (% diameter)

**MRI / MR arthrogram** — chronic instability without dislocation · posterior labral tear, reverse Hill-Sachs, cuff · **Kim lesion**: incomplete avulsion/cyst with intact chondrolabral junction · **posteroinferior capsulolabral separation**

![MR arthrogram: posterior labral tear](../../MCQ/attachments/q216832-b-kim-lesion.jpg)

**Next step pattern** — any "light bulb" / overlapping head / ER to neutral after seizure or fall → **axillary view**\
✗ Wrong options: PT for frozen shoulder · sling · cuff repair · EMG · injection

**Glenoid hypoplasia** — **posteroinferior glenoid deficiency**, enlarged distal clavicle, glenoid indentation · **usually bilateral** → X-ray other side · rarely syndromic · **PT first** · glenoid osteotomy variable · early OA\
✗ Wrong options: echo / abdominal US / skeletal survey unless Holt-Oram, Apert stigmata

## 5 Management

```
Posterior shoulder instability
├─ Acute traumatic dislocation → reduce, immobilise ER 4–6 wk, then PT
├─ Chronic instability → PT (cuff + periscapular)
│      └─ fails, pain with loading → posterior labral repair
├─ Chronic locked dislocation → by duration + reverse Hill-Sachs size
└─ Uncontrolled seizures → neurology control first
```

### Chronic locked dislocation – reverse Hill-Sachs management (McLaughlin, arthroplasty)

| Source | Closed reduction | McLaughlin / lesser tuberosity transfer | Hemiarthroplasty |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Orthobullets** | All acute (most reduce spontaneously) · disimpaction + graft if **<3 wk** | Chronic **<6 mo** + RHS **<40%** | **>6 mo**, RHS **>40%**, severe head arthritis, **head collapse on reduction** |
| **Hawkins & Neer (OB)** | **<6 wk + <20%** | **6 wk–6 mo + 20–45%** | **>6 mo or >45%** (glenoid normal) |
| **OB (other)** | **<3 wk + <25%** under GA; stable if IR to abdomen → 6 wk orthosis in slight extension + ER | Defect **<~40%** | Larger; **>50%** or several months |
| **TO** | **<3 wk + <20%** · **>3 wk / >20% → open reduction (posterior approach)** | **25–45%** McLaughlin / modified | **>45%** bone graft / arthroplasty |

**TSA** — significant **glenoid arthritis** + an HA indication · glenoid intact → no glenoid component

**Closed reduction after ~3 wk** — exceedingly difficult · may **displace unrecognised surgical neck fracture**

**Neglected >6 wk (TO)** — **open reduction**; **closed reduction contraindicated**

**McLaughlin** — **subscapularis** into anteromedial defect · **modified (Neer/Hawkins)** = **lesser tuberosity + subscapularis** (used by most) · deltopectoral

**Modified McLaughlin candidate** — young man, **locked posterior dislocation after electric shock / seizure**\
✗ Wrong options: AVN · RA = arthroplasty · subscap insufficiency = repair or pec transfer · anterior Hill-Sachs = graft/remplissage

**6-wk locked dislocation, 38 y** — **open reduction ± lesser tuberosity transfer** (CT first)\
✗ Wrong options: PT · closed manipulation · cuff repair · HA

**12-wk locked post-seizure, large RHS + lesser tuberosity fracture, 60 y** — **hemiarthroplasty + subscapularis repair** · younger → **osteochondral allograft** + subscap repair

**6 mo after motorcycle fall, 52 y** — **hemiarthroplasty** most reliable\
✗ Wrong options: PT · MUA · capsular release/plication

**HA version** — **anatomic version (20–30° retroversion)** · not excessive anteversion (re-dislocation, per source)

**7 mo locked, 63F, light bulb** — first **further radiographs (axillary)** → then HA

**Open complications** — stiffness · **AVN** · OA

![CT: locked posterior dislocation with anteromedial reverse Hill-Sachs](../../MCQ/attachments/q3587-illa-locked-post-dislocation.jpg)

**Uncontrolled epilepsy, 9th dislocation, humeral + glenoid bone loss** — **neurology + social work to control seizures first** · later: **posterior bone block** (iliac crest autograft or **distal tibial allograft**) ± modified McLaughlin / head grafting · HA ± glenoid reconstruction for chronic locked\
✗ Wrong options: urgent HA · reconstruction before control · anterior procedure alone

### Operative criteria

| Procedure | Indication |
|---|---|
| **Posterior labral repair** (open/arthroscopic) | **Recurrent instability despite PT** · pain loading arm in forward flexion (bench, blocking) · **negative Beighton** |
| **Capsular shift + rotator interval closure** | **Positive Beighton** (laxity) · shift less desirable in throwers · interval closure controversial |
| **Posterior bone block** | Posterior glenoid bone loss **≥11%** in contact athlete (**subcritical** by anterior standards) |
| **Opening-wedge glenoid osteotomy** | Excessive **congenital retroversion** (limited data) |
| Modified McLaughlin | Chronic locked, RHS <40%, <6 mo |
| HA / TSA | RHS >40%, >6 mo, collapse; TSA if glenoid arthritis |

### Treatment – therapy vs arthroscopic capsulolabral repair & postoperative protocol

**Acute reduction** — immobilise **10–20° ER, elbow at side, 4–6 wk** → PT (cuff + periscapular) + avoid high-risk position

**After reduction, avoid** — **cross-body adduction at 90° flexion + IR** (tensions SGHL + posterior IGHL)

**Chronic instability first line** — **PT** (cuff + periscapular; in-season athlete) · esp. **generalised laxity** · ± brief immobilisation, steroid injection

**Failed rehab, positive jerk, grade 2 load-shift, lineman** — **arthroscopic posterior capsulolabral repair** (arthroscopic ≈ open)\
✗ Wrong options: thermal · open anterior capsulorrhaphy · injection · ER immobilisation for recurrent

**Lineman, clunk, bench-press pain** — **posterior labral tear → arthroscopic labral repair**\
✗ Wrong options: thermal capsulorrhaphy · SLAP debridement/tenodesis

**Thermal capsulorrhaphy** — **contraindicated** · breaks collagen cross-links at **65–75 °C** · capsular necrosis, **chondral damage**, axillary nerve injury, recurrence

**Technique** — **lateral decubitus** · **high lateral / posterosuperior portal** for anchor trajectory

**Posterior repair (TO)** — open: **deltoid split → infraspinatus–teres minor interval** · anchors at **7, 8, 9 o'clock** (right shoulder)

| Portal (TO) | Use |
|---|---|
| **Kim's posterior portal** — **2 cm lateral to posterolateral acromial corner** | Working (anchors) |
| Mid-glenoid anterior | Viewing |
| Trans-cuff anterosuperior | Shuttle |

**Precautions (TO)** — **posterior circumflex humeral artery ~4 cm** · **suprascapular nerve ~3 cm** away · probe to rule out Kim lesion

| Pathology (TO) | Procedure |
|---|---|
| **Posterior Bankart** | **Posterior labral repair** |
| No posterior Bankart | **Posterior capsulolabroplasty** |
| Posteroinferior instability | **Posterior capsular plication** |
| Anterior + posterior + inferior | **Combined labral repair + global capsular plication** |

**Results** — open **80–85% success at 5–7 y** · arthroscopic similar (shorter follow-up)

**Post-op** — sling in **neutral rotation** (ER sling) or standard, up to 6 wk · **avoid flexion, adduction, IR** · full contact/heavy labour **6 mo**\
✗ Wrong options: no elbow flexion = biceps tenodesis · avoid abd + ER = anterior/HAGL repair · PROM ≥6 wk = cuff repair · unrestricted ROM

**Posterior glenoid bone loss** — **lower critical value than anterior**; **11%** of glenoid diameter ↑ recurrence after isolated labral repair → **bone block in contact athletes**\
✗ Wrong options: modified McLaughlin = chronic dislocation · only with large RHS · isolated repair better · augmentation doesn't change recurrence

**First-time traumatic dislocation, young athlete with recurrent subluxations** — **arthroscopic capsulolabral repair reasonable** · recurrence up to **90%** in young active · immobilisation (IR or ER) may not reduce recurrence · arthroscopic ≈ open with modern anchors if no glenoid loss/engaging lesion

## 6 Complications

| Rank | Complication after labral repair |
|---|---|
| **1** | **Stiffness** (↓ **IR**) |
| **2** | **Recurrence** (7–50%) · ↑ with glenoid bone loss ≥11%, uncontrolled seizures |
| **3** | **DJD** |
| Other | Adhesive capsulitis · **overtightening → anterior subluxation / coracoid impingement** · **axillary / suprascapular** nerve |

**Not after posterior repair** — anterior instability not expected · acromial stress fracture not typical

## X vs Y

| | **Posterior instability** | **Anterior instability** |
|---|---|---|
| Position | **Flexion, adduction, IR** | Abduction + ER |
| Symptom | **Pain** > instability | Instability, apprehension |
| Labral lesion | **Reverse Bankart / Kim** (posteroinferior) | Bankart, ALPSA |
| Head lesion | **Reverse Hill-Sachs (anteromedial)** | Hill-Sachs (posterosuperolateral) |
| Tests | **Jerk, Kim**, load & shift | Apprehension, relocation |
| Dynamic stabiliser | **Subscapularis** | Infraspinatus + teres minor |
| Post-op avoid | **Flexion, adduction, IR** | Abduction + ER |
| Critical glenoid bone loss | **Lower (~11%)** | Higher |

| | **Jerk test** | **Kim test** |
|---|---|---|
| Positive | **Clunk** | **Pain** |
| Best for | Posterior (mid-range) | **Posteroinferior** |
