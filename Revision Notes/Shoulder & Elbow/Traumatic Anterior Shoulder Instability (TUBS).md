# Traumatic Anterior Shoulder Instability (TUBS)

*Shoulder & Elbow · 58 / 60*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**TUBS** — **T**raumatic **U**nilateral dislocation, **B**ankart lesion, requires **S**urgery

**Frequency** — one of the most common shoulder injuries; **1.7%/year** general population; shoulder = most commonly dislocated major joint

**High-risk groups** — **military**, **contact/collision athletes** (rugby, American football commonest)

**Hyperlaxity** — global hyperlaxity (Ehlers-Danlos, collagen disorders) → OR **2.68** for anterior instability · **3× recurrence** · fewer capsulolabral lesions · more often atraumatic instability

### Anatomy – static & dynamic restraints

**Static** — bony anatomy, capsule, GH ligaments, **labrum (+50% glenoid depth)**

**Dynamic** — rotator cuff, long head of biceps

**Concavity-compression** — cuff compresses head into glenoid concavity; **lost with anterior glenoid rim loss** → ↓ effective depth\
✗ Wrong options: adhesion-cohesion · negative intra-articular pressure: both preserved in closed capsule

| Ligament | Taut / restrains in | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| **SGHL** | **Arm at side (0° abduction)** | **Inferior** |
| **MGHL** | **45° abduction + ER** | Anterior (and posterior, OB) |
| **Anterior band IGHL** | **90° abduction + ER** | **Anterior – main anterior restraint** |
| Posterior band IGHL | 90° abduction | Posterior |

**Main anterior stabiliser (TO)** — **IGHL**\
✗ Wrong options: MGHL · SGHL · anterior capsule · subscapularis

**Main stabiliser above 45° abduction (TO)** — **IGHL**

**AIGHL glenoid attachment** — usually to **labrum**; less commonly direct to glenoid neck

### Pathoanatomy – Bankart lesion, IGHL & mechanism (abduction–external rotation)

**Bankart lesion** — avulsion of **anteroinferior labrum + anterior band IGHL** from anteroinferior glenoid; labrum separated from the **bony glenoid**; "essential lesion" of traumatic anterior dislocation (TO)\
✗ Wrong options: cartilage–labral junction only

| Bankart frequency | Source |
|---|---|
| **80–90%** of TUBS | Orthobullets |
| **85%** (Rowe) · **87%** (Hintermann) · **97%** (Taylor 63/67) · 73.5% (Wolf) | OB studies |

**Most common surgical finding** — **anteroinferior labral tear**\
✗ Wrong options: anterosuperior · posterior · Hill-Sachs

**Hintermann (212 dislocators)** — Bankart 87% · ventral capsule insufficiency 79% · **Hill-Sachs 68%** · GHL insufficiency 55% · complete cuff tear 14% · posterior labral tear 12% · SLAP 7%

| Lesion | Definition | Key point |
|---|---|---|
| **Bankart** | Labrum + IGHL off anteroinferior rim; **medial periosteal hinge undisrupted** (TO) | Most common |
| **Perthes (TO)** | Labrum **avulsed (not torn)**, attached by **intact but medially stripped periosteum** | May sit in **normal position** (neutral-arm MRI) → **hard to see arthroscopically**; stabiliser function lost → **recurrent dislocation** |
| **ALPSA** | "**Medialised Bankart**": labroligamentous complex **displaced medially (+ inferiorly)** on scapular neck, intact medial hinge, **fluid underneath**; anterior labrum absent on rim (TO) | **Heals medially** on glenoid neck · ↑ arthroscopic failure · common in **recurrent instability treated nonop** |
| **GLAD** | **Partial** anteroinferior labral tear + **adjacent glenoid articular cartilage defect** | ↑ arthroscopic failure · debridement relieves pain (Neviaser) |
| **HAGL** | IGHL avulsed from **humeral** insertion | See HAGL block |
| **Bony Bankart** | Fracture of anteroinferior glenoid | Up to **49%** of recurrent dislocators |
| **Kim lesion (TO)** | Concealed tear at **posterior** glenoid chondrolabral junction + concealed deep labral detachment | **Posterior** instability; visible chondrolabral junction intact; probing → **labral fluctuance**; ± glenoid hypoplasia |

![Labral lesion spectrum – Bankart, GLAD, Perthes, ALPSA, bony Bankart](../../MCQ/attachments/TO17121441-1.jpg)

**Association (TO)** — Bankart, Perthes, HAGL, ALPSA, GLAD → all **recurrent anterior** instability · Kim → **posterior**

**97% rule** — **97%** of recurrent instability have **Bankart or ALPSA**

**Mechanism** — anteriorly directed force with arm **abducted + externally rotated** · posterior instability = **adduction + internal rotation**

**Bankart vs position** — AIGHL/anteroinferior labrum taut in **ER at 90° abduction**\
✗ Wrong options: 45° = MGHL

**Associated lesion in young dislocator** — **Hill-Sachs**\
✗ Wrong options: full-thickness cuff tear = elderly · reverse Hill-Sachs = posterior · coracoid fracture = rare · GT fracture = less common than Hill-Sachs

**"Clunk" in 22-y basketballer** — Bankart + **Hill-Sachs**

### Combined anterior & posterior labral tears

**Pan-labral tear** — **270–360°** labral tear (anterior + inferior + posterior); anterior and/or posterior instability

**Exam** — positive **Jerk + Kim** (posterior) **+ apprehension + relocation** (anterior) → **270° tear** → anterior + posterior labral repair (arthroscopic)

**✗ Wrong options (images)** — bony Bankart / anterior Bankart (anterior repair only) · subscapularis tear (cuff repair) · Kim lesion (posterior only)

**270° repair** — **15% failure** (Mazzocca)

**Military** — high incidence of posterior and combined front-to-back tears → **unrecognised pan-labral tear** = cause of recurrence

### HAGL lesion

**HAGL** — **humeral avulsion of the (inferior) glenohumeral ligament**, anterior band IGHL, **without subscapularis tear**; term coined by **Wolf (1995)**

**RHAGL** — reverse HAGL = posterior band IGHL → **posterior** instability

**Frequency** — **7–9%** of unstable shoulders (Wolf 9.3%: Bankart 73.5%, capsular laxity 17.2%) · Taylor **1/67** · Rhee 2% — **rare**

**Patient** — **slightly older** than Bankart (Orthobullets) · **older + more violent trauma** (OB) · female collegiate athletes · hyper-abduction + ER

**MRI** — best on **coronal** sequences: **J-shaped IGHL**, contrast **extravasating down humeral neck**; bony HAGL variant; "floating" AIGHL = HAGL + capsulolabral detachment

**Associations** — always with other lesions (Bankart, LHB tear, cuff tear); more severe anterior pain

**Significance** — "other end of the IGHL" → **missed HAGL = cause of failed Bankart repair** → higher recurrence if unrepaired

**Treatment** — **open repair = classic / gold standard** · arthroscopic possible but technically hard (**70° scope**, accessory portals)\
✗ Wrong options: arthroscopic contraindicated classically

## 2 Mechanism

### Hill-Sachs lesion

**Hill-Sachs** — **compression/impaction fracture of posterosuperior (posterolateral) humeral head** against **anteroinferior glenoid rim**; chondral impaction injury

**Frequency** — **80–100%** of traumatic dislocations · **25%** of traumatic subluxations · 68% (Hintermann) · 73% on X-ray (Edwards)

**Most common associated intra-articular finding** with Bankart\
✗ Wrong options: cuff tear · SLAP · posterior labral tear · Buford

**Arthroscopy** — indentation on posterior head **beside the bare area**\
✗ Wrong options: bare area = normal cartilage-free zone

**Significance** — clinically irrelevant **unless it engages**; **end-range (ABER) instability**, not mid-range (TO)

**Glenoid bone loss (TO)** — from anteroinferior rim fracture or **erosion** by humeral head; **6.8% loss per dislocation** (average)

**Reverse Hill-Sachs** — anteromedial head; **posterior** dislocation; lesser tuberosity fracture = posterior dislocation

## 3 Presentation

**History** — traumatic event; ask **formal reduction vs spontaneous**; instability feeling; pain from subluxation/excess translation

**Apprehension** — supine, **90° abduction + 90° ER** → apprehension; positive in **mid-range abduction = glenoid bone loss**

**Relocation** — posterior force on humeral head → ↓ apprehension

**Load and shift** — grades 0–III (table below); **sulcus sign** with arm at side

### Diagnostic criteria – hyperlaxity

| Criterion | Threshold |
|---|---|
| **Beighton score** | **>4** |
| **Hyper-ER at side** | **>85°** |
| **Hyperabduction (Gagey)** | **>105°** |
| Load–shift | **>2+ in ≥2 planes** |

**ISIS hyperlaxity** — anterior (ER >85°) or inferior (Gagey) laxity = 1 point

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Modality / view | Use |
|---|---|
| **Trauma series: true AP (Grashey) + scapular Y + axillary** | Required; Grashey = beam ~45° off AP, end-on GH joint; **Y = humeral head over Y intersection** (scapular spine + coracoid) |
| **Axillary** | **Best for detecting dislocation**; bone loss visible here = already significant; Velpeau if can't abduct |
| **West Point** | **Glenoid bone loss / bony Bankart** |
| **Stryker notch** | **Hill-Sachs** (supine, hand on head, **10° cephalic**); poor for Bankart |
| AP in ER | Hill-Sachs (ISIS item) |
| Bernageau profile | Glenoid profile, "cliff sign"; **less accurate than 3D imaging** |
| **CT ± 3D** | Bony injury, **% glenoid bone loss**; **3D CT = gold standard** |
| CT arthrogram | **MRI contraindicated** (pacemaker, cochlear implant); poorer soft tissue |
| **MRI** | **Best for labral tear**; validated for bone loss |
| **MR arthrogram** | **Study of choice for soft tissue**: sens **86–91%**, spec **86–96%** (MRI 44–100% / 66–95%) |

### MRI positioning – ABER view

**ABER** — **abduction + ER** → **humerus in coronal plane, glenoid in axial plane**

**Shows** — **anteroinferior labrum**, **anterior band IGHL**, articular surface of supraspinatus, posterosuperior labral peel-back (sens 73%, spec 100%)

### Glenoid bone loss – assessment & critical thresholds

| Method | Fact |
|---|---|
| **3D CT en face** | **Gold standard**; **highest interrater reliability**; predictive error **similar to MRI** (Rerko) |
| **Best-fit circle (TO)** | Circle fitted to **posterior + inferior** glenoid ≈ original glenoid; or **contralateral glenoid** reference |
| Bare-spot method | Bare spot = glenoid centre (Burkhart); GBL = (B − A) / 2B (A = bare spot→anterior edge, B = →posterior edge) |
| MRI | Tends to **overestimate** GBL (✗ Wrong options: underestimates); less bone–soft tissue contrast; cuts must be en face |
| MR arthrogram | Not a substitute for CT (poorer bony definition) |
| X-ray (Bernageau, West Point) | Significantly less accurate |

**Worked example (OB)** — bare spot 9 mm anterior, 12 mm posterior → (12 − 9)/24 = **12.5%** + non-engaging HS → **Bankart alone**

**Worked example (OB)** — 6.9 mm lost of 28.3 mm → **24.4%** = critical → **coracoid transfer**

**Inverted pear** — glenoid morphology with ↑ bone loss (**>25%**, TO/OB); **89% failure** after arthroscopic repair

**Glenoid shape** — normally pear-shaped (narrow superior); anteroinferior loss → **inverted pear**

| Threshold | Meaning | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **>20–25%** | **Critical**; soft tissue alone fails (>2/3 failure) → bony procedure | Orthobullets, most OB |
| **>25–30%** | Osseous augmentation needed | OB (bone loss explanations) |
| **20–30%** | "Glenoid bone deficiency" → Latarjet; **>60% recurrence** with Bankart alone | OB |
| **15–25%** | Biomechanically unstable | OB |
| **21%** of glenoid length | Instability + ↓ ER after Bankart (Itoi) | OB |
| **17.3%** | Critical value (Shin: 43.9% vs 3.7% recurrence) | OB |
| **>13.5%** | **Subcritical** – critical may be this low; open Bankart ↑ recurrence | Orthobullets, OB |
| **<15%** | Soft-tissue stabilisation alone works | OB, TO |

**Bone-loss conflict** — critical **20–25%** (Orthobullets) · **25–30%** (some OB) · **13.5–17.3%** subcritical (recent studies) — read the options

**Strongest predictor of failure after arthroscopic Bankart** — **glenoid bone loss**\
✗ Wrong options: age at first dislocation · age at surgery · >3 anchors · on-track HS

**Most predictive intra-op finding** — **anterior glenoid deficiency 35%**\
✗ Wrong options: non-engaging HS · loose body · bursitis · 10% PASTA

**Mechanism lost with rim loss** — **concavity-compression**

### Hill-Sachs lesion & glenoid track (on/off-track)

**Bipolar concept** — glenoid bone loss + Hill-Sachs together; bipolar > unipolar engagement risk

**Glenoid track (TO)** — area of posterior humeral articular surface in contact with glenoid along **posterior end-range (ABD + ER)**; width = medial margin of track → medial margin of cuff footprint

| Formula | Value |
|---|---|
| **GT = 0.83D − d** | D = inferior glenoid diameter, d = anterior bone loss width (Orthobullets, TO, most OB) |
| GT = **0.84(D) − ABL** | OB (glenoid-track formula question) |
| 84% vs 83% (TO) | **84% cadaveric** · **83% live at 90° abduction** |
| **HSI = HS + BB** | Hill-Sachs width + **bony bridge** to cuff insertion |
| **HSI > GT** | **Off-track = engaging** → recurrence |
| **HSI < GT** | **On-track = non-engaging** |

**Coefficient conflict** — **0.83** (Orthobullets, TO, most OB) · **0.84** (one OB; "HS >84% of glenoid width = off-track") — read the options

![Glenoid track vs Hill-Sachs interval – on-track (A), off-track from glenoid loss (B) or large Hill-Sachs (C)](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob3050-tracklesions.jpg)

**Medial extension** — **the more medial the HS extends, the more likely off-track** ✓

**✗ Glenoid-track traps** — on-track engages · unipolar > bipolar risk · **GT only by CT** (MRI also usable) · HSI < GT engages

**Dynamic arthroscopic exam (TO)** — assess engagement **after the Bankart repair**

**Tyre & pothole** — glenoid track = tyre, HSI = pothole; off-track = tyre falls in (big HS or big glenoid loss)

**Off-track predictive value** — 75% vs 8% recurrence (Shaha); **superior to >20% bone-loss cut-off**

| Worked example | Calculation | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Kabaddi (TO)**: D 24, width 20, HSI 18 | d = 4 → **16.6%**; GT = 19.92 − 4 = **15.92** | **Off-track + subcritical, elite contact → Latarjet** |
| Water polo (OB): D 29, d 2, HSI 19 | GBL **6.9%**; GT = 24.07 − 2 = **22.07** | **On-track → isolated arthroscopic Bankart** |
| TO: 25% GBL, GT 18, HSI 21.3 | Off-track + critical | **Latarjet** |

## 5 Management

```
First-time traumatic anterior dislocation
├─ Reduce under sedation / IA lidocaine → traction-countertraction (commonest)
│      └─ fails 5–10% → blocks: biceps tendon, capsule, fracture fragments
├─ Low-risk → sling ≤1 wk + PT (cuff + periscapular)
├─ Age >40 + persistent weakness → MRI shoulder (cuff tear)
├─ In-season athlete, no bone loss → rehab, RTP in-season → surgery end of season
└─ Young (<20–25) contact / military + Bankart on MRI → early arthroscopic Bankart
```

```
Recurrent instability – glenoid side (3D CT)
├─ GBL <13.5–15% + on-track → arthroscopic Bankart (≥3–4 anchors)
├─ GBL <15% + off-track → Bankart + remplissage
├─ Subcritical 13.5–25% → contact athlete / high ISIS → Latarjet
│      └─ non-contact, off-track → Bankart + remplissage
├─ GBL >20–25% / inverted pear → Latarjet (coracoid transfer)
└─ Failed Latarjet / massive loss → ICBG (Eden-Hybinette) or distal tibia allograft
```

```
Recurrent instability – humeral side (Hill-Sachs)
├─ On-track / non-engaging (<20%) → nothing extra
├─ Off-track / engaging 20–40% (>25%) → remplissage
└─ >40% → humeral head allograft / ICBG; hemiarthroplasty if >40–45% + arthritis
```

### Operative criteria

| Procedure | Indications |
|---|---|
| **Arthroscopic Bankart ± capsular plication** | **First-time** dislocation + MRI Bankart in athlete **<25 y** · high-demand athletes · **recurrent (>1) dislocation** after nonop · **GBL <20–25%** · on-track (± remplissage if off-track) |
| **Open Bankart ± capsular shift** | GBL <20–25% · **revision after failed arthroscopic Bankart** without GBL >20% · **HAGL** · acute glenoid fracture · **hyperlax needing formal capsular shift** · contact athlete returning to sport |
| **Latarjet / Bristow** | **GBL >20–25%** (inverted pear) · **subcritical >13.5%** in high-risk · **ISIS >6** · contact athletes / athletes of consequence (climbers, big-wave surfers) · Europe uses aggressively |
| **ICBG / distal tibia / distal clavicle graft** | GBL >20–25% · **revision of failed Latarjet** · **less graft resorption** than Latarjet |
| **Remplissage + Bankart** | **Off-track / engaging** HS (**>25–40%**) with **GBL <20–25%** · subcritical GBL (arthroscopic-alone recurrence ~20%) |
| **Hill-Sachs bone graft** | Engaging HS **>40%** |
| **Tendon transfer (conjoint / LHB)** | Capsulolabral deficiency; with labral repair if **GBL <15%** |

**TO Hill-Sachs size rule** — non-engaging (**usually <20%**) → nothing · engaging **20–40%** → remplissage · engaging **>40%** → bone graft (Eden-Hybinette)

**TO soft-tissue zone** — isolated Bankart if GBL **<15–20%** (stricter **13.5%** best-fit circle in high-demand athletes) + on-track

**TO subcritical (15–20%)** — **contact athlete (rugby, kabaddi, wrestling) → Latarjet even at 15%** (Bankart failure ~30%) · non-contact → Bankart + remplissage

**TO bone grafting triggers** — GBL >20–25% · **failed previous Bankart** · bipolar loss making an on-track HS off-track

**Wrestler, 30 dislocations (incl. in sleep), Bankart + 40% HS (TO)** → **Latarjet**\
✗ Wrong options: Bankart · remplissage · McLaughlin

### First-time dislocation – nonoperative vs early arthroscopic Bankart

**First-time dislocators** — management controversial; **ASES: surgery for athletes 14–30 y at end of season** if **positive apprehension + bone loss**

**Reduction techniques** — **traction-countertraction (commonest)** · **Kocher**: arm at side in ER → forward flex → IR · **Hippocratic**: heel in axilla · **Stimson**: prone, weight hung from arm · scapular manipulation

**Immobilisation** — **no benefit >1 wk**; short (≤1 wk) = prolonged (≥3 wk) recurrence (41% vs 37%, Paterson) → **may stop sling at 1 wk**

**ER immobilisation** — early studies ↓ recurrence <40 y (labrum reduced to glenoid) → **subsequent studies refuted**; IR 24.7% vs ER 30.8% (Liavaag, NS)

**Immobilisation conflict** — no benefit / refuted (Orthobullets, OB) · **immobilisation decreases recurrence** (TO) — read the options

**Bracing** — **no evidence it ↓ recurrence**; limits ABD + ER in season

**Return to play (nonop)** — **faster than surgery**; 7–21 days (Orthobullets) · 2–3 wk (OB) · **3–4 wk** with full painless ROM + strength (OB) → can finish season

**Nonop vs surgery recurrence** — nonop **higher**: 88% vs 14% (Arciero) · 77% vs 11% (Bottoni); RTP without recurrence next season 40% vs 90% (Dickens)

**Delaying surgery** — further events → more labral damage, **more bone loss**, ↑ failure after Bankart; trend = **surgery after first dislocation**

**Bony vs soft-tissue (first-time)** — Latarjet **equal or lower** recurrence than Bankart

**Water polo, 3rd dislocation, GBL <7%, HSI 19 < GT 22.07** → **isolated arthroscopic Bankart**\
✗ Wrong options: remplissage → ↓ ER · Latarjet / DTA · humeral OATS

### Arthroscopic vs open Bankart repair

| | **Arthroscopic** | **Open** |
|---|---|---|
| Re-dislocation | **Similar** (RCTs) · slightly higher in some · 11% vs 8% (Harris) · 15.1% vs 7.7% (Rollick) | Similar |
| ROM | **Slightly better** (less pain, ER preserved) | More pain, ↓ ROM |
| Return to work/sport | Equal (RCT) | **Equal or slightly better** (some studies) |
| Arthritis | **No difference** | No difference |
| Approach | Arthroscopic, ≥3 (preferably 4) anchors | **Deltopectoral**; subscapularis **transverse split or tenotomy**; **inferior capsule shifted superiorly** |

**Arthroscopic advantages** — cosmesis, ↓ morbidity, ↓ ER loss, diagnostic confirmation

**Contraindications to arthroscopic (Stein)** — significant GBL · attenuated capsulolabral tissue · engaging HS · **HAGL** (open = gold standard)

**Isolated open Bankart + capsular shift** — **recurrent after arthroscopic repair, minimal GBL (6%), on-track, contact athlete** ✓

**✗ Open Bankart distractors** — 20% GBL rugby (→ Latarjet) · 5% GBL off-track (→ Bankart ± remplissage) · 45% HS (→ humeral graft) · 18% GBL wrestler (→ bony)

**Technique** — **fully mobilise labrum** before repair · **≥3 anchors** (<3 → failure) · "drive-through sign" pre-repair · knotless anchors

**Too many anchors** — **postage-stamp fracture** through anchor holes

**Recurrence after arthroscopic Bankart** — 13% (Ahmed) · 15.3% (Boileau) · 4% without bone defect vs **67%** with (Burkhart) · **89%** contact athletes with inverted pear

### Bony Bankart – arthroscopic fixation

**Small bony Bankart (<20%)** — **arthroscopic repair incorporating the fragment**; 95% RTS (Sugaya)\
✗ Wrong options: ORIF · iliac crest · coracoid transfer

**26-y accountant, 3rd dislocation, 10% bony defect, no prior surgery** → **arthroscopic bony Bankart repair**\
✗ Wrong options: ER immobilisation · remplissage · coracoid/iliac crest

**ORIF** — large **acute** glenoid rim fractures only\
✗ Wrong options: recurrent instability

**Bony Bankart + deltoid weakness after reduction** → **bony Bankart stabilisation** + observe axillary nerve (EMG changes in ~48%; surgery only if no recovery at **3–6 months**)

**Fixation** — open: screws or suture (linear / bridge)

**Imaging** — 3D CT > CT > MRI > X-ray for bone loss (MOON)

**Unaddressed bony Bankart** — commonest cause of failed soft-tissue Bankart\
✗ Wrong options: labral nonunion · HAGL · SLAP

### Remplissage

**Remplissage ("to fill")** — **infraspinatus + posterior capsule** sutured into Hill-Sachs (posterior capsulodesis + infraspinatus tenodesis) → **intra-articular HS becomes extra-articular** (TO) → can't engage rim in ER

**Goal (TO)** — ✓ extra-articular HS\
✗ Wrong options: more secure labrum · ↑ head diameter · ↑ glenoid track width

**Effect** — shortens HSI → **off-track → on-track**; ↑ rotation before engagement (**does not increase glenoid track**)

**Indications** — **off-track/engaging HS** + GBL **<20–25%** (<15% TO) · HS **>25%** (OB) / **25–40%** (Orthobullets) · chronic Bankart + engaging HS catching in **90/90** → **arthroscopic Bankart + remplissage**\
✗ Wrong options: remplissage alone · humeral graft alone

**Technique** — arthroscopic, percutaneous / posterolateral portal; **pass sutures before anterior repair, tie after**; knotted or knotless; **double-pulley**

**Post-op** — **avoid forward flexion + adduction** (tensions posterior capsule; abduction need not be avoided); brief ER immobiliser

**Complications** — **ER loss 5–15°**; recurrence **5% at 2 y**; IR loss <2°

**Outcomes** — vs **Latarjet (2 y): 1.4% vs 3.2% recurrence** despite greater bipolar loss · vs **Bankart alone: ↓ recurrence + revision** (MacDonald RCT) · Latarjet better ER

**Moderate HS + labral tear after 2nd dislocation** → **arthroscopic labral repair + remplissage**\
✗ Wrong options: PT · labral repair alone · hemiarthroplasty – HS not >45% · thermal capsulorrhaphy

**Connolly procedure** — **infraspinatus + GT bone block** into HS · analogous **Neer-McLaughlin** = subscapularis + LT into **reverse HS** (posterior)

**Humeral head allograft (OATS) (TO)** — massive deep HS **>40%** where remplissage would severely limit ER

**HS bone graft outcomes** — small cohorts; early arthritis **16% at 4 y**; Constant 78.5–86.8; options: allograft, arthroplasty, rotational osteotomy

### Latarjet/bone block – indications, mechanism & outcomes

**Latarjet** — transfer of **coracoid + conjoined tendon + CA ligament** to anteroinferior glenoid through **subscapularis split**; deltopectoral; open = arthroscopic outcomes (steep arthroscopic learning curve)\
✗ Wrong options: detached

| Triple effect | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| **Bony** | ↑ glenoid surface/width, **lengthens glenoid track / articular arc** |
| **Sling (TO)** | **Conjoint tendon** (short head biceps + coracobrachialis) over lower subscapularis → **dynamic stability to anteroinferior capsule in ABD + ER**; brace across lower third of joint |
| **Capsular** | **CA ligament stump** repairs capsule |

**Triple effect question** — **all three (bony + sling + CA ligament)** ✓

**Sling effect (TO)** — ✓ conjoint tendon dynamic stability\
✗ Wrong options: CA ligament transfer = capsular · bone surface = bony · LHB tenodesis = DAS

| | **Traditional Latarjet** | **Congruent arc** | **Bristow** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Coracoid orientation | **Undersurface on glenoid**; lateral surface = new track | **Rotated 90°**; **inferior surface** = track, articulates with head | **Distal ~1 cm tip**, orientation unchanged |
| Track gain | Less (~10 mm) | **Most: ~15 mm** | Less |
| Note | **Less fracture-prone** at screw insertion | Better for **severe bone loss** | Less common; **MC nerve tethering** (TO) |

![Traditional (a–b, ~10 mm) vs congruent arc (c–d, ~15 mm) Latarjet](../../MCQ/attachments/q216803-illb-latarjet-types.jpg)

**Graft position** — flush, **no lateral overhang** (→ OA) · not **≥1 cm medial** or >1.5 cm migration (→ redislocation) · intra- vs extra-articular placement (intra-articular OA concern unproven)

**Outcomes** — recurrence **0–8%** (<10%) · good–excellent **>90%** · RTS same level **75–90%** · RTS any level 80–100%

**Return-to-sport conflict** — same level **75–90%** (OB) · 72.6% (Hurley) · 56–95% (Glogovac) · 49% contact athletes (Privitera) — read the options

**Latarjet vs Bankart (bone loss)** — 4.9% vs 67% recurrence (Burkhart); Latarjet 2.7% (Rollick) with ↑ complications

**Chronic dislocation (Sahajpal)** — glenoid defect **>20–30% → bony augmentation**; humeral defect engaging or **20–40% → address**; **>40% → hemiarthroplasty**

**Postage-stamp fractures** — anterior rim fractures through anchor holes; male **≤25 y**, sport, **≥3 anchors**, **knot-tying anchors**, anchor-site osteolysis → **bone block**

**Recurrence predictors after Bankart (OB)** — age <20, Hill-Sachs, glenoid lesion, hyperlaxity, off-track\
✗ Wrong options: dominant side · SLAP · >5 preop dislocations · on-track

**Distal tibia allograft (DTA)** — **true osteochondral graft**; **matches humeral head radius of curvature** + large articular surface (TO); functions mainly by **↑ glenoid track**; revision of failed Latarjet\
✗ Wrong options: sling – differs from Latarjet

**Iliac crest (Eden-Hybinette) (TO)** — autograft for large defects where **coracoid volume inadequate**; 89% healing at 1.4 y; screws or buttons; risks: hardware failure, subscapularis repair failure

**Revising a failed Latarjet** — hardware removal + ICBG or DTA; electrocautery/nerve check to protect MC nerve (scarred, overlaps axillary nerve)

**Dynamic anterior stabilisation (DAS, TO)** — **LHB transferred to anterior glenoid through subscapularis split** → **sling effect**; for **chronic** anteroinferior instability with **poor labrum/GHL quality** and **limited bone loss**; also with SLAP, overhead athletes/throwers

**DAS technique (TO)** — LHB tenotomy, whipstitch · split subscap in middle · drill **3 o'clock**, 1.5–2.0 cm deep · tenodesis screw · add labral repair (bumper effect)

**DAS contraindications (TO)** — **significant bone defects** · LHB lesions · subscapularis lesion · prior LHB tenotomy/tenodesis; fills gap between soft-tissue and bone-block procedures\
✗ Wrong options: alternative to Latarjet with bone loss

**Conjoint tendon / LHB transfer (Orthobullets)** — conjoint detached from coracoid tip (CA ligament preserved) or LHB; socket in anterior glenoid + suspensory fixation through subscap split; low recurrence with labral repair if **GBL <15%**; biceps possibly better; no ROM loss

**Latarjet in epileptics** — contraindicated with **uncontrolled seizures**

### Seizure-related dislocation

**Epileptic, >10 dislocations, uncontrolled seizures, GBL >20–25%** → **closed reduction + neurology referral**\
✗ Wrong options: no reduction attempt · reduction alone · stabilisation now · fusion

**Rule** — **exhaust medical (seizure) management before surgery**; high recurrence even with bony augmentation

**Pathology** — **large bony defects** (HS + bony Bankart) + degenerative change; muscle contraction + trauma; anterior = posterior frequency

**Surgery once controlled** — **bony procedure** (Latarjet / bone graft); soft tissue alone fails

### Failed stabilisation with subscapularis deficiency – tendon transfer

**Signs** — **belly-press** weakness; post-open-repair subscap failure → **positive lift-off + excessive ER**

**Irreparable** — retraction + **atrophy** on MRI

**29-y, failed arthroscopic + open Bankart, irreparable subscap** → **tendon transfer (latissimus dorsi or pectoralis major)**\
✗ Wrong options: PT · Latarjet · Magnuson-Stack · RTSA – age

**Latissimus vs pec major** — latissimus **better replicates subscap line of pull / IR moment arm**, better scores; pec major historically to lesser tuberosity; pec major not after failed subscap repair post-arthroplasty

### Historical & thermal procedures (Putti-Platt, capsulorrhaphy)

| Procedure | Technique | Problem |
|---|---|---|
| **Putti-Platt** | **Lateral advancement of subscapularis + medial advancement of capsule** (imbrication) | **Loss of ER**, **arthrosis** with ↓ ROM (TO) |
| **Magnuson-Stack** | Subscapularis advanced lateral to bicipital groove (± to GT) | ↓ ER, posterior glenoid loading |
| **Boyd-Sisk** | Biceps transferred laterally + posteriorly | **High recurrence** |
| Bristow | Coracoid tip to anterior rim | **MC nerve injury** commoner (TO) |

**Putti-Platt (TO)** — ✓ arthrosis with compromised ROM · (✗ Wrong options: redislocation never reported · rotates HS by ↑ retroversion · MC nerve injury common (infrequent)) · Putti-Platt + Bristow → **IR contractures**

**Post-capsulorrhaphy arthropathy** — 1970s–80s open repair → pain, stiffness, **lack of ER**, **posterior glenoid wear + retroversion**, IR contracture

**Thermal capsulorrhaphy (TO)** — used in **shoulder instability**; monopolar RF, introduced **1996**\
✗ Wrong options: adhesive capsulitis · dural tear · stiff elbow

**Thermal mechanism (TO)** — **65–80°C** shortens capsule; collagen denaturation, ↓ mechanical properties, cell necrosis → fibroblast repair; stretches if loaded early; **grid/stripe** technique aids healing; ↓ capsular proprioceptors

**Thermal outcomes (TO)** — volume ↓ **26–36%** (vs plication **19%**); best in mild, unidirectional anterior instability **with** suture repair; **isolated → unacceptably high failure**

**Thermal complications (TO)** — **failure (commonest)**, **chondrolysis**, axillary nerve injury, capsular attenuation; now contraindicated

## 6 Complications

### Recurrence

**Commonest cause** — **unrecognised glenoid bone loss** with soft-tissue-only repair (80% of failures) · missed off-track HS · **<3–4 anchors** · missed HAGL · missed pan-labral tear (military) · seizures

**Anchor conflict** — **<4 anchors** = risk (Orthobullets) · **<3 anchors** = risk (OB) — read the options

**Age at surgery** — not related to failure (one OB) · independent predictor (Ahmed, OB) — read the options

### Bankart repair complications

**Stiffness** — **loss of ER** (overtightening; worse with remplissage) → treat **Z-lengthening of subscapularis**; overtightening → **post-capsulorrhaphy arthropathy** (older patients)

**Subscapularis failure (open)** — positive lift-off, excessive ER

**Hardware** — anchor pull-out; postage-stamp fracture

**Chondrolysis** — **thermal capsulorrhaphy** or **intra-articular pain pumps** (both now contraindicated)

### Neurovascular injury – axillary nerve & artery (dislocation, capsular shift)

**Most common nerve after dislocation** — **axillary** (course around surgical neck, close to joint); typically **neurapraxia** (stretch)\
✗ Wrong options: neurotmesis = penetrating/laceration · brachial plexus · MC

| Axillary nerve injury rate | Source |
|---|---|
| **Up to 5%** (transient neurapraxia) | Orthobullets, OB (older cohort) |
| **42%** clinical + EMG (Visser) | OB |
| **~48%** EMG changes | OB |
| Any nerve injury **21–65%**; neuro deficit 13.5% (Robinson); 11.6% (Atef) | OB |

**Rate conflict** — **≤5%** clinical (Orthobullets) vs **42–48%** EMG (OB) — read the options

**Axillary nerve** — **C5–6** (upper trunk, posterior cord) → deltoid + teres minor; weakness esp. **30–100° abduction**; ↓ sensation **lateral proximal arm**

**Timeline** — neurodiagnostics after first **2–3 wk** (14–21 d if no recovery) · persistent weakness + sensory loss at **12 wk → EMG/NCS** · surgery (neurolysis, graft, transfer) if **no recovery 3–6 mo**\
✗ Wrong options: observe more · repeat MRI · explore · triceps branch transfer before EDS

**High-grade injury risk (Avis)** — proximal humerus fracture, multiple nerves, neuropathic pain, **delayed reduction**

**Surgical anatomy** — axillary nerve anterior to subscapularis → drapes **inferolateral border** → **quadrilateral space**; **12 mm** (Orthobullets) · **12–16 mm** (OB) · 1–1.5 cm (Kang) from inferior glenoid / infraglenoid tubercle

**Arthroscopic risk zone** — **6 o'clock, inferior to infraglenoid tubercle** (also 4:30) during capsular shift/plication; plication **≤1 cm from rim safe**, >1 cm risk; anteroinferior portals riskiest\
✗ Wrong options: spinoglenoid/suprascapular notch = suprascapular n. · 9 o'clock posterior · 12 o'clock biceps

**Protective position** — **abduction + external rotation** ↑ nerve–capsule distance\
✗ Wrong options: adduction/IR – beach chair adduction + IR = risk

**Open capsular shift** — injury during **dissection of subscapularis off inferior capsule** / retractor on inferomedial subscap belly → **can't abduct, elbow flexion + supination intact = axillary (C5–6)**\
✗ Wrong options: MC C5–7 · lower subscapular nerve

**Axillary artery** — **1–2 cm** from inferior glenoid · closest at **≥90° ER: 4–5 mm** (4.1 mm Stone) · ≥2 cm from coracoid · **greatest risk at inferior glenoid with arm in ER**\
✗ Wrong options: thoracoacromial – at coracoid · MC – medial · radial – triangular interval · ulnar

### Latarjet complications – musculocutaneous nerve, graft resorption & overhang arthritis

**Complication rate** — up to **25%** (Orthobullets) · 15–30% (Gupta) · 30% (Griesser); higher than Bankart

| Complication | Fact |
|---|---|
| **MC nerve injury** | **Most common nerve** (Orthobullets, most OB); **instrumentation / aggressive medial retraction around conjoint tendon**, coracoid harvest; pierces coracobrachialis ~**5.6 cm** distal to coracoid tip |
| **Axillary nerve injury** | **During graft fixation**; 12 mm from infraglenoid tubercle; 2nd commonest |
| **Graft lysis/resorption** | Up to **90%** some resorption **within 6 months**; **superior** graft; late pain + apprehension at ~9 mo |
| **Lateral overhang** | **GH OA** (up to **38%**); 20% new arthritis (Mizuno) |
| **Stiffness** | **ER loss** (✗ Wrong options: IR · elbow flexion) |
| Nonunion, hardware (screw pull-out) | |
| Axillary artery | 4–5 mm at 90° ER |
| Subscapularis rupture | **Low** – split, not detached |

**MC nerve** — **terminal branch of lateral cord**, **C5–7**; → coracobrachialis, biceps, brachialis; injury → **lateral forearm numbness (LACN)** + **↓ elbow flexion/supination**

**Latarjet nerve conflict** — **MC = most common** (Orthobullets, most OB) · **axillary = most common** (one OB; neuromonitoring: all clinical deficits axillary) — read the options

**Resorption conflict** — common, **up to 90%** (Orthobullets, OB) · "uncommon overall", mostly first 6 mo (another OB) — read the options

**Nerve injury management** — mostly **traction/contusion neurapraxia** → **observe 3–6 wk**; delayed EMG if persistent; all resolved by 6 mo (neuromonitoring)

**✗ Nerve distractors** — lateral winging (spinal accessory) · medial winging (long thoracic) · IR weakness (subscapular) · thumb extension weakness (PIN/radial) · median, ulnar, radial (paths far)

**Sling post-Latarjet** — protects **graft healing**\
✗ Wrong options: subscap detached

### Older patients – rotator cuff tear after dislocation

**Rule** — young → recurrence · **old → rotator cuff tear**

| Age | Cuff tear after dislocation |
|---|---|
| **>40 y** | **30%** (Orthobullets, OB) · "30% at 30 y" (one OB) |
| **>60 y** | **80%** |

**Tendons** — "**SIT**": **supraspinatus** (commonest), infraspinatus, teres minor; recurrent instability in elderly → **subscapularis** tears\
✗ Wrong options: subscap – bear hug

**Cuff tear vs axillary palsy in elderly** — cuff tear **far more common**; **deltoid firing + full passive ROM + intact sensation** → cuff tear → **suprascapular nerve** muscles (SS/IS)\
✗ Wrong options: dorsal scapular · lateral pectoral · MC · axillary

**Persistent pain/weakness ≥2 wk after dislocation (>40 y)** → **MRI shoulder**\
✗ Wrong options: EMG · C-spine MRI · steroid injection – harms later repair · PT first · CT glenoid

**Test for commonest pathology** — **drop arm test** (supraspinatus)\
✗ Wrong options: O'Brien – SLAP · apprehension – young/bone loss · bear hug – subscap · Spurling – C-spine

**MR arthrogram** — **most sensitive (and specific) for full- + partial-thickness cuff tears**; MRI ≈ US

**Treatment** — surgical repair → better pain + satisfaction; full-thickness tears don't heal nonop; **GT fracture** with dislocation >50 y (↑ recurrence)

### Terrible triad of the shoulder

**Shoulder terrible triad (TO)** — **anterior dislocation + rotator cuff tear + brachial plexus (mostly axillary) nerve injury**; cuff usually **complete** tear

**EXCEPT (TO)** — **proximal humeral fracture**

### Late complications – chondrolysis, osteonecrosis & dislocation arthropathy

**Osteonecrosis (skeletally immature)** — rare; after fracture-dislocation or stabilisation → **observation** (revascularises)\
✗ Wrong options: arthroplasty · grafting · capsular release

**Chondrolysis** — progressive pain + stiffness ~1 y after Bankart with **heat (RF) probe** → joint space loss\
✗ Wrong options: frozen shoulder · subscap failure

**Dislocation arthropathy** — **age at first dislocation** (>25 y, Hovelius) + high-energy primary dislocation → severe OA; "**goat's beard**" inferior osteophyte; ↓ ER\
✗ Wrong options: current age · dominance · diabetes · sex

**After Bristow-Latarjet** — mild arthropathy 35%, severe 14%; first dislocation >23 y = risk
