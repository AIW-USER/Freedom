# Shoulder Arthroscopy: Indications & Approach

*Shoulder & Elbow · 45 / 60*

## 1 Basics

### Indications

**Indications** — diagnostic · loose bodies · **cuff repair/debridement** · labral/SLAP/instability repair · **subacromial decompression** · AC joint / **distal clavicle resection** · **suprascapular nerve release** · capsular release (contracture) · synovectomy · **biceps tenotomy/tenodesis**

### Diagnostic scope – normal anatomy & variants

**Diagnostic scope** — **30° scope via posterior portal**: biceps, SS/IS/TM, **rotator interval**, MGHL/IGHL, **subscapularis recess (loose bodies)**, anterior labrum, glenoid, humeral head

**Rotator interval** — **biceps tendon + superior edge of subscapularis + glenoid**

**Anterosuperior labrum + MGHL** — **widest anatomic variability**

| Variant | Labrum | MGHL |
|---|---|---|
| **Attached labrum** | Attached | **Broad** — **most common** |
| **Sublabral hole (foramen)** | Sublabral hole | **Cord-like** |
| **Buford complex** | **Absent** anterosuperior labrum | **Cord-like** |

![Sublabral foramen, sublabral recess, Buford complex](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob12064-labralvariant.jpg)

**Normal bare areas** — **central glenoid** + **posterior humeral head**\
✗ Wrong options: pathology

## 5 Management

### Positioning

| | **Beach chair** | **Lateral decubitus** |
|---|---|---|
| Orientation | Upright, **anatomic** | Glenoid parallel to floor (non-anatomic) |
| Traction | **None** → **↓ neurapraxia** | **10–13 lb** (avoid over-distraction) → **traction neurapraxia** (NV + soft tissue) |
| Arm | Mobile; arm holder possible | **30–60° abduction + 20–30° forward flexion** |
| Conversion to open | **Easy** (deltopectoral; no re-prep) | Must reposition/redrape |
| Bleeding | **↓ venous pressure + bleeding** | — |
| **Cerebral perfusion** | **↓ (hypoperfusion, CDEs, stroke, death)** | **Better** |
| Hypotension / bradycardia | **↑ risk** | No ↑ |
| Posterior portals | **Difficult** (head in the way) | Easy |
| Labrum | Less capsular distortion | **Traction accentuates labral tears** |
| Anaesthesia | **Regional + sedation possible** | Not ideal if regional not tolerated |
| 5 o'clock portal | **Safer** (Gelber) | **Axillary + MCN closer** |

**Lateral position (TO)** — **vacuum beanbag + kidney rest**; **10–13 lb** traction; **30–60° abduction, 20–30° forward flexion**

**Lateral modification (TO)** — tilt patient **20–30° posteriorly** → glenoid parallel to floor → **less traction** (↓ plexus neurapraxia), **labral tears accentuated** (pulled away from bed), **better access to inferior third** of labrum/capsule

**Beach chair advantages (TO)** — faster positioning · **↓ neurapraxia (no traction)** · less capsular distortion · arm mobility · easy open conversion

**Beach chair disadvantages (TO)** — difficult posterior portals · **↓ cerebral perfusion with hypotensive anaesthesia → stroke, death**

✗ Wrong options: **EXCEPT (TO)** — "beach chair → **more** brachial neurapraxia" is **false**

### Primary portals

| Portal | Location | Function | Passes / risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Posterior** | **2 cm inferior, 1 cm medial** to posterolateral acromion; aim at **coracoid tip** | **Primary viewing**; **first portal** | Between **infraspinatus (SSN)** + **teres minor (axillary)** or through IS |
| **Anterior** | **Lateral to coracoid**, anterior to AC joint; spinal needle under vision | Viewing + SAD | Between **pec major (pectoral nn.)** + **deltoid (axillary)** |
| **Lateral** | **1–2 cm distal** to lateral acromion | **SAD** | Through **deltoid (axillary)** |

![Portal sites: posterior (P), lateral (L), anterolateral (AL), anteromedial (AM)](../../MCQ/attachments/q4837-illa-3.jpg)

### Secondary portals

| Portal | Location | Function | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Anteroinferior (5 o'clock)** | Slightly inferior to coracoid | **Anterior labral repair anchors** | **Axillary (> 7 o'clock risk, Meyer)** + MCN |
| **Posteroinferior (7 o'clock)** | Under vision, spinal needle | **Posterior labral repair anchors** | **Axillary nerve** (low posterior) |
| **Neviaser (supraspinatus)** | **Just medial to lateral acromion** | Anterior glenoid view, **SLAP repair** | Through **supraspinatus (SSN)**; SSN if into **suprascapular notch** |
| **Port of Wilmington (posterolateral)** | **Just anterior to posterolateral acromion** | **Posterior SLAP + cuff lesions** | — |

**Trans-subscapularis portal** — **avoid** in either position (Gelber)

**Placement** — secondary portals under direct vision from posterior portal with **spinal needle**

## 6 Complications

### Portal-related nerve injury

| Structure | Anatomy | At risk when |
|---|---|---|
| **Axillary nerve** | Quadrangular space → deep surface deltoid, **~7 cm below acromion** | Posterior portal **too inferior** (**7 o'clock**) → pain, weakness, ↓ **lateral arm/shoulder** sensation |
| **Suprascapular nerve** | Supraspinatus → infraspinatus fossa | Posterior portal **too medial**; Neviaser into notch |
| **Cephalic vein** | Deltopectoral groove | Anterior portal **too lateral** — **structure most at risk** (Lo, Meyer) |
| **Musculocutaneous nerve** | Enters muscle **2–8 cm distal to coracoid tip** | Anterior portal **too inferior** |
| Phrenic nerve | — | **Interscalene block** |

**Lo (outside-in)** — all NV structures **> 20 mm** from all portals

**No neural structure at risk** — lateral superior portal · posterior superior portal

### Positioning complications (beach chair, lateral decubitus, obesity)

| Pressure point (beach chair) | Nerve | Deficit |
|---|---|---|
| **A – forehead** (tight face mask) | **Supraorbital** (terminal **CN V**) | Paraesthesia **forehead + anterior scalp** |
| **B – mastoid process** (mask straps) | **Great auricular (C2–C3)** | Paraesthesia **ear, posterior auricular area, angle of mandible** |
| C – chin strap | — | **Skin breakdown lip/chin** |
| D – contralateral elbow | **Ulnar** | Ulnar neurapraxia |
| **E – lateral abdominal belt / ASIS** | **LFCN** | Burning/numbness **anterolateral thigh**, above knee, **no weakness** |

![Beach chair pressure points A–E](../../MCQ/attachments/q8940-a-beach.jpg)

| Neck position | Complication |
|---|---|
| **Hyperextension** (± rotation) | **Stroke** + **CN XII (hypoglossal)** palsy — traction + compression by angle of mandible (✗ Wrong options: face-mask compression) |
| **Hyperflexion** | **Spinal cord ischaemia → paraplegia** |

**Not reported with beach chair** — CN III (ptosis, mydriasis, diplopia) · **AIN** (FPL + index/long FDP) · **femoral** (quadriceps, anterior thigh, medial leg)

### Cerebral hypoperfusion

**CN VI palsy post-op** — **lateral rectus** paralysis (can't abduct eye) → cerebral hypoperfusion in beach chair

**↓ Risk** — **lateral decubitus** · **regional anaesthesia + sedation** (fewer CDEs) · **SBP > 90 mmHg**

**Increases or no effect (not protective)** — **general anaesthesia (↑ CDE)** · **SBP < 70 mmHg** (↑) · open vs arthroscopic · metal vs bioabsorbable anchors

**Stroke** — also reported after lateral decubitus (risk small in both)

### LFCN palsy (obesity)

**Path** — LFCN crosses near **ASIS** → compressed by belt / waist flexion / pannus

**Risk factors** — **obesity (BMI > 30; highest-risk option BMI > 35)** · **prolonged operative time** · **inadequate ASIS padding**

**Not risk factors** — age > 50 · type of surgery (open/arthroscopic/arthroplasty) · **diabetes** · foam between pannus + thighs (= **protective**)

**Prevention** — hips **centred at bed break**, **hip flexion ~45°** (less waist flexion), **padding under belt** + between pannus and thighs

**Holtzman (397 pts)** — LFCN palsy **5.5%**, mostly **transient**; weight + BMI significant

**Levy protocol (BMI > 30)** — LFCN **5 → 1** case (OB) · **none, even BMI > 40** (another OB)

**Obese patients** — ↑ nerve palsies; post-op ↑ **DVT, infection, wound breakdown, hardware failure, mortality**

## How it's asked

**"Lateral arm numbness + weakness after arthroscopy — portal?"** → **posterior inferior (7 o'clock)**\
✗ Wrong options: Neviaser = SSN · anterior = MCN · lateral/posterior superior = none

**"All true about positioning EXCEPT?"** → **beach chair has more brachial neurapraxia** (TO)

**"Lateral decubitus traction?"** → **10–13 lb**, 30–60° abduction, 20–30° flexion (TO)

**"Beach chair pad at mastoid — deficit?"** → **great auricular: ear, posterior auricular, angle of mandible**\
✗ Wrong options: CN III · CN XII · AIN · femoral

**"PACU, can't gaze left after beach chair — prevention?"** → **lateral decubitus position**\
✗ Wrong options: GA · SBP < 70 · open · metal anchors

**"Obese, burning outer thigh above knee, no weakness — pad where?"** → **lateral abdominal belt / ASIS (LFCN)**

**"Anterolateral thigh numbness after beach chair — biggest risk factor?"** → **BMI > 35**\
✗ Wrong options: age · diabetes · surgery type

**"Hypoglossal palsy after beach chair — cause?"** → **neck hyperextension**

**"Absent anterosuperior labrum + cord-like MGHL?"** → **Buford complex**

**"Cephalic vein at risk — portal error?"** → **anterior portal too lateral**
