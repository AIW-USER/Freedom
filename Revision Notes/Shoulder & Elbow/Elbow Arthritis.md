# Elbow Arthritis

*Shoulder & Elbow · 13 / 60*

## 1 Basics

### Types & epidemiology

**3 types** — **osteoarthritis (primary)** · **post-traumatic** · **inflammatory**

| | **Primary OA** | **Post-traumatic** | **Inflammatory (RA)** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency | **Rare** (symptomatic ~**2%**) | **2nd most common** | **Most common / most prevalent** elbow arthritis (historically) |
| Patient | **Middle-aged ♂ laborer**, **♂:♀ 4:1**, 20–70 y (**avg 50**), **dominant arm** | **Younger** than other types | Older ♀; elbow involved in **20–50%** of RA |
| Cause | Strenuous manual labour | Non-op radial head fracture, (fracture-)dislocation, traumatic instability | RA, psoriatic, SLE, PVNS |
| Joint | **Radiocapitellar** preferentially; **ulnohumeral relatively spared** | Whole joint or focal ulnohumeral/radiocapitellar | Global; symmetric narrowing |
| Hallmark | **Osteophytes, capsular contracture, loose bodies** | Incongruity → altered load; stiffness, instability, malunion/nonunion | Synovitis, **osteopenia**, ligament attenuation, instability |

**Secondary OA causes** — post-traumatic · **OCD** · **synovial osteochondromatosis** · **MUCL insufficiency / valgus extension overload**

### Genes & lab markers

**RA pathology** — **pannus** → **hyperplastic synovium** → **collagen + proteoglycan degradation** → chronic synovitis, ligament attenuation, **periarticular osteopenia**, contracture

**RA trap** — synovial proliferation from pannus, **not rheumatoid factor mutation**

**RA pathoanatomy** — fixed flexion contracture · instability · **ulnar** (or less often radial) neuropathy · erosions · cysts · deformity · progressive bone resorption

### Stabilisers (relevance to surgery)

**Primary** — **anterior band MCL** (**anterior oblique fibres** most important; valgus + distraction) · **LCL** · olecranon–coronoid–trochlea congruity

**Secondary** — **radial head** (most important secondary; **30%** valgus stability; **0–30° flexion + pronation**) · capsule (**distraction in full extension**) · anconeus + lateral capsule (varus)

## 3 Presentation

**OA symptoms** — pain at **end range** · **loss of terminal extension** · painful **locking/catching** (loose bodies) · night pain unusual\
✗ Wrong options: mid-range

**OA exam** — forearm rotation preserved early · **ulnar neuropathy in up to 50%**

**RA** — **hand + wrist precede elbow** · fixed flexion contracture · ligamentous incompetence · ± ulnar neuropathy · rheumatoid nodule · **evaluate C-spine in all RA**

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Modality | Fact |
|---|---|
| **X-ray** | **AP + lateral**; **C-spine X-rays for RA before surgery** |
| OA osteophytes | **Coronoid process + fossa** · **radial head + fossa** · **olecranon tip + posteromedial olecranon fossa** |
| Joint space | Narrowing; **ulnohumeral relatively preserved** in OA |
| **Loose bodies** | **Underestimated on plain X-ray** |
| RA | **Periarticular erosions + cysts**, osteopenia; **osteophytes not prominent** (✗ Wrong options: characteristic) |
| **CT** | Surgical planning; better defines **osteophytes + loose bodies** |

![Olecranon osteophyte impinging in olecranon fossa](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob3084-olecranon-osteophyte.jpg)

### Larsen grading (RA)

| Stage | Radiographs |
|---|---|
| 1 | Soft tissues involved; **near-normal** radiographs |
| 2 | **Periarticular erosions + mild cartilage loss**; soft-tissue swelling, osteopenia |
| 3 | **Marked joint space narrowing** |
| 4 | Advanced erosions **penetrating subchondral plate** |
| 5 | Advanced damage, **loss of articular contour** |

**TEA threshold** — **Larsen 3–5** in patients >65 y

## 5 Management

```
Elbow arthritis – failed NSAIDs, injection, splint, activity modification
├─ Mild–moderate OA, arc >90°, painful end-range, mechanical symptoms → arthroscopic OCA
├─ Severe contracture (e.g. 40–90°), prior ulnar transposition → open: O-K or column procedure
├─ Young (<65), high demand, END-STAGE (OA/RA/PTA), stable → interposition arthroplasty
│      └─ isolated radiocapitellar → anconeus interposition / radial head replacement
└─ >65, low demand, severe (Larsen 3–5) → semiconstrained TEA
```

### Primary OA – osteocapsular arthroplasty (arthroscopic vs open) & patient selection

**Nonop** — NSAIDs · cortisone injection · resting splints · activity modification → **mild–moderate** symptoms

**OCA** — removal of osteophytes (coronoid, olecranon, fossae) + loose bodies + **capsular release**

**Best OCA candidate** — **<65 y**, active, **arc >90°**, **mild–moderate** OA, **painless mid-arc + painful end-range**

| Arthroscopic debridement / release | |
|---|---|
| **Indications** | **Mechanical symptoms (loose bodies)** · capsular contracture · **bony block** to motion · **>90° motion** preferred |
| **Contraindications (relative)** | **Prior ulnar nerve transposition** (nerve location unknown) · **severe contracture / arthrofibrosis** · prior extensive open procedures |
| Not a CI | Prior **olecranon osteotomy** · ulnar nerve **subluxation** (✗ Wrong options: absolute CI; identify pre-op, esp. prior submuscular/intramuscular transposition) |
| Complications | **Neurologic** (**ulnar** palsy commonest, **radial** 2nd — anterolateral portal) · **synovial fistula** · recurrent stiffness |

| Arthroscopic vs open OCA | Arthroscopic |
|---|---|
| **Post-op pain** | **↓** |
| HO / ankylosis | **↓** (✗ Wrong options: equal) |
| Access | **Easy anterior + posterior**, better cartilage view |
| Reliability | **Pain relief** more reliable (open → more predictable **ROM** gain) |

**O'Driscoll & Blonna 8 steps** — posterior view → remove debris + fat pad → osteophytes → posterior capsule release → anterior view → strip anterior capsule → coronoid osteophytes/reshape → excise anterior capsule; **4.8% transient nerve injury** (502 pts)

**Elbow arthroscopy indications** — septic I&D · synovectomy (inflammatory) · debridement + loose body removal (OA) · contracture release · OCD capitellum · lateral epicondylitis release

**Advanced radiocapitellar OA** — consider **radial head replacement**

**Avoid radial head resection in laborer** — ↑ load on arthritic ulnohumeral joint; doesn't fix ROM or end-range impingement

**No PT / dynamic splints** — no role initially in advanced painful arthritis with **fixed** contracture · no static splinting when arc functional + complaint is pain

**Fusion** — severe functional loss; rare (unmanageable **sepsis**, **Charcot**)

![Outerbridge–Kashiwagi olecranon fossa fenestration](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob3084-ob-k1.jpg)

**Outerbridge–Kashiwagi (ulnohumeral arthroplasty)** — open **posterior** approach; **burr fenestration of olecranon fossa** → release posterior impingement + anterior access; younger pts with ↓ ROM; **decompress ulnar nerve if pre-op flexion contracture**; failure = missed anterior / peripheral (medial + lateral olecranon) osteophytes

**Column procedure** — medial or lateral open capsular release + bony resection for extrinsic contracture; **lateral most common** (access to all 3 joints) via lateral supracondylar ridge · anterior arthrotomy **ECRL/common extensor interval, stay anterior to LUCL** · posterior: elevate triceps · **go medial to gain flexion** (excise **posterior band MCL**)

**Open release** — elderly regain motion **more reliably than <20 y** (Strubel & Cohen)

### Ulnar nerve – neuritis & decompression when gaining flexion

**Risk** — severely limited pre-op motion: **flexion <100°** · **extensor lag / max extension >60°** → **post-op ulnar nerve dysfunction**

**Rule** — any motion-restoring procedure (OCA, O-K, column, interposition) in such patients → **concomitant ulnar nerve decompression ± transposition**

**Not radial neuritis** — radial tension **↓** with gained extension

**Not column procedure add-ons** — release anterior band MUCL (primary valgus) · release LUCL (primary varus) · radial head resection (rotation full + painless) · **TFL interposition** (reserved **<50 y**)

### Young post-traumatic arthritis – interposition arthroplasty

**Indication** — **young, high-demand, END-STAGE** OA / RA / **post-traumatic** who would otherwise get TEA if older; **no lifting restrictions**; needs **preserved bone stock + inherent stability**

**Technique** — burr distal humerus + proximal ulna congruent → graft over distal humerus (**Achilles allograft**, **autogenous TFL**, dermal/fascial) → ligament repair → **hinged distracting ex-fix** (offloads graft, early ROM, protects repairs)

**Advantage** — can **convert to TEA** later (successful; Blaine)

| Interposition contraindication | Type |
|---|---|
| **Elbow instability** / chronic instability without bone stock for ligament reconstruction | **Absolute** |
| **Active infection** | **Absolute** |
| Elderly **>60 y** (TEA candidates) | Relative |
| Bone loss: **destructive JRA**, **distal humerus osteonecrosis** | → prosthetic replacement |
| Not CI | Young age · post-traumatic · inflammatory · previous debridement |

**Anconeus interposition** — arthritis **isolated to radiocapitellar** joint

**Failed interposition (68 y, pain + instability)** — **convert to TEA**\
✗ Wrong options: revision interposition · hemiarthroplasty (ulnar side arthritic) · arthrodesis

### Rheumatoid elbow – TEA & synovectomy

**Synovectomy** — open or arthroscopic; **young RA, joint space preserved**; most improve

**Radial head resection + synovectomy** — only if synovitis **encircles radial head/neck** + painful crepitus on rotation + **PIN signs** (can't extend thumb)

**Complication of radial head resection** — **rapid progression of ulnohumeral arthritis**\
✗ Wrong options: loss of pronation/supination · longitudinal instability with intact IOM · ulnar palsy (less likely)

**End-stage RA** — **semiconstrained TEA** (concomitant ligamentous instability) \
✗ Wrong options: constrained (**high early loosening**) · arthroscopy (incomplete, unpredictable)

**TEA outcome RA vs OA** — **equal or better** for RA\
✗ Wrong options: poorer

### Operative criteria – TEA

| TEA | |
|---|---|
| **Indications** | **>65 y**, severe arthritis (**Larsen 3–5**) · complex distal humerus fracture, elderly, poor bone · distal humerus non/malunion, elderly low demand · post-traumatic arthritis (lower demand) · **failed interposition** |
| **Contraindications** | **Active <65 y** · **infection** · **Charcot joint** |
| **Restriction** | **Lifelong 5–10 lb lifting** limit |

| TEA design | Feature |
|---|---|
| **Unconstrained** | Needs **competent collaterals**, soft-tissue envelope, good bone |
| **Semiconstrained** | **"Sloppy hinge"** → some varus-valgus + rotational laxity → ↓ bone-cement stress → ↓ loosening; **best longevity + function** |
| **Constrained** | **High early loosening** |

### Elbow arthrodesis position (TO)

| Arthrodesis | Position |
|---|---|
| **Unilateral (TO)** | **90° flexion + neutral to slight valgus** (✗ Wrong options: varus · 110°) |
| **Bilateral (TO)** | One **110°** (feeding) · other **65°** (perineal hygiene) |

## 6 Complications

### Total elbow arthroplasty complications

**Rate** — up to **43%** (Orthobullets) · up to **25%** (OB) — read the options

**Bushing wear** — **most common mode of failure of semiconstrained TEA** at midterm, esp. **post-traumatic indication** (younger, non-adherence to restrictions, unstable injury, deformity, contracture) \
✗ Wrong options: gender · smoking · cement · age

![Bushing wear in semiconstrained TEA](../../MCQ/attachments/q216807-a-bushing-wear.jpg)

**Infection / wound risk factors** — prior elbow surgery · prior infection (**S. epidermidis**) · psychiatric comorbidity · **RA** · wound drainage · reoperation · poor skin (**long-term steroids**); two-stage revision → poor survival

**Aseptic loosening** — **linked** implants · **post-traumatic** OA

**Instability** — **unlinked** implants

**Others** — **ulnar neuritis** (iatrogenic or after motion restored without decompression) · **triceps avulsion/insufficiency** · fracture · mechanical failure

## X vs Y

| | **Arthroscopic OCA** | **Interposition arthroplasty** | **TEA** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stage | **Mild–moderate** | **End-stage** | **End-stage** |
| Age / demand | Active, <65 | **Young, high demand** | **>65, low demand** |
| Motion | Arc **>90°** | Any; needs stability | Any |
| Key CI | **Prior ulnar transposition**, severe contracture | **Instability**, infection | **Active <65**, infection, **Charcot** |
| Restriction | — | **None** | **5–10 lb lifetime** |

| | **Elbow OA** | **Elbow RA** |
|---|---|---|
| X-ray | **Osteophytes**, loose bodies, ulnohumeral spared | **Erosions, cysts, osteopenia**, symmetric narrowing |
| Symptom | **End-range** pain | Diffuse pain, effusion |
| Instability | No | **Ligament attenuation** |
| End-stage op | OCA / interposition / TEA | **Semiconstrained TEA**; synovectomy early |

## How it's asked

**"Plumber, advanced OA, lacks 45° extension, flexes to 110° — initial treatment?"** → **osteophyte resection + capsular release**\
✗ Wrong options: PT/dynamic splints · TEA · fusion · radial head resection

**"Carpenter, mid-range locking, arc 35–130°, failed injection?"** → **arthroscopic capsular release + loose body removal + osteophyte decompression**\
✗ Wrong options: interposition · radial head arthroplasty · TEA

**"Firefighter 58, arc 30–130°, pain — most predictable pain relief?"** → **arthroscopic debridement**\
✗ Wrong options: open debridement = ROM · static splint · interposition

**"True of arthroscopic vs open OCA?"** → **less post-op pain**\
✗ Wrong options: equal HO · limited access · radial palsy commonest · subluxation absolute CI

**"Relative CI to arthroscopic debridement in post-traumatic OA?"** → **prior ulnar nerve transposition**\
✗ Wrong options: loose bodies · contracture · bony block · olecranon osteotomy

**"Who benefits most from arthroscopic OCA?"** → **50-y ♂, painful limited end-range**\
