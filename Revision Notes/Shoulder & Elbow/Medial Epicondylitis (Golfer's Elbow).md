# Medial Epicondylitis (Golfer's Elbow)

*Shoulder & Elbow · 26 / 60*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Frequency** — **5–10× less common than lateral epicondylitis**

**Demographics** — **♂ = ♀** · **dominant arm 75%**

**Age** — 30s–60s, peak **30s–40s** (Orthobullets) · **4th–6th decades** / 40–60 y, peak working years (OB) — read the options

**Sports** — repetitive **wrist flexion + forearm pronation** at ball release: **golf**, pitching, javelin, **bowling**, weightlifting, racquet sports

**Tennis risks** — late ball strike (racquet head behind elbow) · poor forehand mechanics · no vibration dampeners

**Occupation** — lifting **>20 kg**, forceful grip, vibration (plumbers, carpenters, construction)

### Anatomy – common flexor tendon (CFT)

**CFT** — **3 cm** long · anterior medial epicondyle + anterior bundle MCL · fibres parallel to MCL · ulnar head PT confluent with anteromedial capsule

| Flexor-pronator mass | Nerve |
|---|---|
| **Pronator teres** | Median |
| **FCR** | Median |
| FDS | Median |
| Palmaris longus | Median |
| **FCU** | **Ulnar** |

![Flexor-pronator origin at the medial epicondyle](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob3083-common-flexors.jpg)

**Muscles affected** — classically **PT > FCR** · newer studies: **all CFT muscles except palmaris longus** · 5 tendons form one common tendon\
✗ Wrong options: number involved matters

**Valgus role** — flexor-pronators offload the **anterior band of MUCL** (primary static valgus restraint; deep to PT + FCR) · dynamic valgus support in **early acceleration** + wrist flexion at release

## 2 Mechanism

**Pathology** — repetitive **eccentric overload** → microtrauma at flexor-pronator origin

**Stages** — peritendinous inflammation → **angiofibroblastic hyperplasia** (as in lateral epicondylitis) → breakdown / fibrosis / calcification · **inflammation uncommon**

### Associated conditions

**Ulnar neuropathy** — local inflammation involves ulnar nerve

**UCL insufficiency** — rule out, esp. throwers

**Occupational cases (84%)** — CTS · lateral epicondylitis · cuff tendinitis

## 3 Presentation

**History** — insidious medial pain · worse with wrist/forearm motion + **gripping** · **late cocking / early acceleration** · ± ulnar digit paraesthesia · ↓ grip strength · rarely acute blow → CFT avulsion

**Tenderness** — **5–10 mm distal + anterior to medial epicondyle** (PT/FCR origin)

**Chronic** — flexion contracture

### Diagnosis – provocative testing

| Test | Condition |
|---|---|
| **Resisted forearm pronation + wrist flexion (fist clenched)** | **Medial epicondylitis** |
| Resisted supination + wrist extension | Lateral epicondylitis |
| **Moving (dynamic) valgus stress** | MCL injury |
| **Milking manoeuvre** | MCL origin apprehension/instability/pain |
| Pinch grip | AIN palsy |
| Elbow flexion test (max flexion, pronation, wrist extension 30–60 s), Tinel, 2PD | Ulnar neuritis |

**UCL vs flexor-pronator pain** — UCL pain **posterior + distal** to medial epicondyle

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

**X-ray** — usually normal · **25% calcification** of CFT or UCL · posteromedial osteophytes · stress views for valgus instability

**US** — **>90%** sensitivity/specificity/PPV/NPV · dynamic · hypoechoic/anechoic degeneration

**MRI** — standard of care · **↑ T2 signal** in CFT (tendinosis/disruption) + peritendinous oedema · use for UCL/osteochondral injury, loose bodies, flexor-pronator rupture, unclear source

![MRI: T2 hyperintensity at the CFT origin](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob3083-medial-epicondylitis.jpg)

**EMG/NCS** — only if ulnar nerve symptoms (not routine)

**Differential** — MCL injury · cubital tunnel · fracture · cervical radiculopathy · triceps tendinitis · **herpes zoster**

## 5 Management

```
Medial epicondylitis
├─ First line → rest, ice, NSAIDs, activity modification, PT, counterforce brace
│      └─ stop throwing 6–12 wk
├─ Refractory to above → peritendinous CFT corticosteroid injection
└─ Fails prolonged nonop, compliant, clear diagnosis → open debridement ± reattachment
       └─ ulnar symptoms → + cubital tunnel release / transposition
```

### Nonoperative treatment – injection

**Prolonged nonop** — appropriate: surgery **less predictable than lateral** epicondylitis

**Modalities** — counterforce brace / kinesiology tape · therapeutic US beneficial · **ESWT**: no definitive recommendation, equivocal (Orthobullets, OB) · ESWT "~90% pain relief" (one OB explanation) — read the options

**Next step after 2 mo brace + NSAIDs + PT + rest** — **corticosteroid into CFT peritendinous area** (short-term benefit)\
✗ Wrong options: arthroscopic debridement · cubital tunnel injection · EMG without ulnar symptoms · acupuncture, not shown to help

**Steroid complications** — skin **depigmentation** (dark skin) · subcutaneous **atrophy** · tendon weakening · **ulnar nerve injury** · avoid multiple injections

### Operative criteria

| Criterion | Value |
|---|---|
| Failed nonop in **compliant** patient | **Up to 6 mo** (Orthobullets) · **4–6 mo** · **at least 6 mo** (OB) |
| Symptoms | Severe, affecting quality of life |
| Diagnosis | Clear |
| Exception | **Elite athlete with definite tendon tear** → earlier |

### Surgical outcomes & ulnar nerve involvement

**Procedure** — **open** **medial approach** · **PT–FCR interval** · excise pathologic tissue + side-to-side repair · ± epicondyle microfracture · **reattach flexor-pronator** if proximal origin involved · ± cubital tunnel release/transposition\
✗ Wrong options: arthroscopic

**Reattachment** — debridement **with or without** reattachment → equivalent outcomes (Arevalo; complications 4.3%, RTS/RTW 81–100%)

**Outcome** — **pain and function both improve** · good/excellent **80%** (Orthobullets, < lateral) · **80–90%** (OB) · **87%** at 7 y (Gabel & Morrey)

**Worst prognostic factor** — **degree of ulnar neuropathy** (only parameter correlating with outcome; mild/none 24/25 good · moderate–severe 3/5 fair/poor)\
✗ Wrong options: prolonged nonop · no reattachment · number of tendons · ESWT

**Key exam finding** — **pronation weakness at 90°**

**Rehab** — sling **1–2 wk** (avoid wrist volar flexion) · ROM after 2 wk · strengthening **6–8 wk** · **RTS 3–6 mo**

## 6 Complications

**MABC neuropathy** — avulsion/transection → if seen intra-op, **transpose into brachialis**

**Others** — ulnar nerve injury · infection

**Prognosis** — harder to treat + less studied than lateral epicondylitis

## X vs Y

| | **Medial epicondylitis** | **Lateral epicondylitis** |
|---|---|---|
| Frequency | 5–10× less common | More common |
| Origin | Flexor-pronator (**PT, FCR**) | — |
| Provocation | **Resisted pronation + wrist flexion** | Resisted supination + wrist extension |
| Surgical success | **~80%**, less predictable than lateral | — |
| Associated nerve | **Ulnar** | — |

| | **Medial epicondylitis** | **UCL injury** |
|---|---|---|
| Tenderness | 5–10 mm distal + anterior to epicondyle | **Posterior + distal** to epicondyle |
| Test | Resisted pronation + flexion | **Milking**, moving valgus stress |

## How it's asked

**"Provocative test positive in medial epicondylitis?"** → **resisted forearm pronation + wrist flexion with clenched fist**\
✗ Wrong options: supination/extension = lateral · dynamic valgus/milking = MCL · pinch grip = AIN

**"Masters tennis player, 2 mo PT, brace, NSAIDs, rest fail — next?"** → **corticosteroid injection into CFT peritendinous area**\
✗ Wrong options: arthroscopy · cubital tunnel injection · acupuncture · EMG

**"Bowler-carpenter, 7 mo failed nonop, open debridement + reattachment — 1-y outcome?"** → **pain and functional outcome scores both expected to improve**

**"Surgical golfer's elbow — factor linked to inferior outcome?"** → **degree of ulnar nerve involvement**\
✗ Wrong options: prolonged nonop · no reattachment · number of tendons · ESWT

**"Nerve injured at medial elbow surgery — intra-op fix?"** → **MABC, bury in brachialis**
