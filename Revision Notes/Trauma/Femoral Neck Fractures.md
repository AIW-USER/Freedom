# Femoral Neck Fractures

*Trauma · 21 / 67*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Rising** — ageing population · **women > men**, Caucasians > African Americans · **USA highest incidence**

**Cost** — **most expensive fracture per person**

**Associated** — **ipsilateral femoral shaft fracture: 6–9% of shaft fractures** (\~10%, OB) → fix **neck first**

### Healing biology

**Intracapsular** — **bathed in synovial fluid, no periosteum, limited callus** → poor healing; anatomic reduction essential

### Anatomy

**Neck-shaft angle 130 ± 7°** · **anteversion 10 ± 7°**

**Head supply** — **MFCA (lateral epiphyseal artery) = main** · LFCA anterior / inferior head · inferior gluteal · ligamentum teres insignificant

**Displacement** — disrupts retinacular vessels + **intracapsular haematoma (tamponade, effect debated)**

**Lowest intracapsular pressure** — **flexion + abduction + ER** (patients lie this way) · highest = **extension + IR**\
✗ Wrong options: flexion + IR · extension + ER

![Blood supply to the femoral head](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob1037-blood-supply.jpg)

### Genes & lab markers

**FRAX (WHO)** — uses **femoral neck BMD (DEXA)**, BMI, current smoking, parental hip fracture, prior fracture < 50, etc. → **spine T-score NOT used** · \~50% of fragility fractures occur without DEXA-defined osteoporosis

## 2 Mechanism

**Young** — high energy (vertical, Pauwels III) · **Elderly** — low-energy falls

**Cyclic load** — single-leg stance compression \~4× body weight → superior-neck tension fractures

## 3 Presentation

**Impacted / stress** — mild groin or medial thigh / knee pain, no deformity, pain at ROM extremes, **pain on percussion of greater trochanter**

**Displaced** — leg **shortened, externally rotated, abducted**; pain with log-roll

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Study | Use |
|---|---|
| AP pelvis, cross-table lateral, full femur | Standard |
| **Traction-internal rotation AP (15° IR)** | **Best to define fracture type** (accounts for anteversion) |
| **AP in abduction + IR (TO)** / 15–20° IR | **Best position for impacted neck fracture (TO)** |
| Contralateral hip | Templating |
| CT | Displacement, comminution |
| **MRI** | **Occult fracture — imaging of choice** (< 24 h); **not** reliable for head viability |
| **Thin-slice MDCT (TO)** | **Second-line after X-ray for occult fracture (TO)** — sensitivity + specificity 100% with experienced reader |
| Bone scan | Occult fracture **after 72 h** if MRI contraindicated |
| Duplex | **DVT if delayed presentation (> 2 days)** |

**Occult fracture next step** — **MRI hip + pelvis** (Orthobullets, OB) · **thin-slice MDCT** (TO) — read the options\
✗ Wrong options: stress views · bone scan at 7 d · repeat CT at 48 h · WBAT

**Delayed presentation 3 days** — **bilateral lower-limb duplex** before surgery\
✗ Wrong options: inlet / outlet · CT head without head signs · CT / MRI for displaced fracture

### Garden classification

| Type | Description | Group |
|---|---|---|
| **I** | **Incomplete, valgus impacted** | Non-displaced |
| **II** | **Complete, non-displaced** | Non-displaced |
| **III** | **Complete, partially displaced** | Displaced |
| **IV** | **Complete, fully displaced** | Displaced |

### Pauwels classification (angle from horizontal)

| Type | Angle | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **I** | **< 30°** | Compression |
| **II** | **30–50°** | — |
| **III** | **> 50°** | **Shear; most unstable; highest nonunion + AVN** |

## 5 Management

```
Femoral neck fracture
├─ Young (< 50), displaced → urgent anatomic reduction (open if needed) + fixation
│      └─ vertical (Pauwels III) / basicervical → sliding hip screw ± anti-rotation screw
├─ Elderly non-displaced (Garden I–II) → cannulated screws (inverted triangle)
├─ Elderly displaced, active, independent / OA → THA
└─ Elderly displaced, low demand, dementia, Parkinson, > 80 → cemented hemiarthroplasty
```

### Treatment options

| Option | Indication |
|---|---|
| Observation | Non-ambulatory, minimal pain, prohibitive risk |
| **Closed reduction + cannulated screws** | **Non-displaced**; Garden I–II in elderly; displaced in young if reduction anatomic |
| **ORIF** | **Displaced in young / physiologically young (< 50)** (female → ↑ reoperation) |
| **Sliding hip screw** | **Basicervical**, **vertical (Pauwels III) in young** ± anti-rotation screw |
| **Hemiarthroplasty** | Debilitated elderly, metabolic bone disease, low demand |
| **THA** | **Older active**, **pre-existing OA / inflammatory arthritis**, Garden III–IV < 85 yr |

### Young patients – urgent ORIF, reduction & osteonecrosis

**Displaced in young** — **ORIF**\
✗ Wrong options: THA (salvage only) · closed parallel screws ± Pauwels screw · cephalomedullary nail

**Most important for healing + outcome** — **quality of reduction**\
✗ Wrong options: surgery < 12 h · open reduction itself · capsular decompression · approach · comorbidities · implant

**Unacceptable closed reduction** — **> 5 mm displacement in any view** or **> 10° varus / valgus / version** → **open reduction + SHS**\
✗ Wrong options: intra-op CT · repeated closed attempts (↑ AVN) · accept 7 mm · acute THA

**Most prevalent complication after ORIF in young** — **osteonecrosis** (> nonunion) · AVN **15–30% in < 60 with displaced fracture**

**Highest AVN risk** — **45-yo woman, displaced** (young + displaced)

**Approaches** — **Smith-Petersen** (sartorius–TFL, protect LFCN) · **Watson-Jones** (TFL–gluteus medius; capsulotomy **anterior to lesser trochanter** to spare MFCA)

**Reduction** — joysticks (A→P K-wires), evacuate haematoma; **length-stable / compression** to avoid neck shortening (shortening → ↓ SF-36, QoL)

**Ipsilateral neck + shaft** — **lag screws for neck first, then retrograde femoral nail** (✗ Wrong options: shaft first · plate shaft · proximal plate) · in unresuscitated patient (lactate 6, BE −8) → **delay until resuscitated** (EAC), but still early (✗ Wrong options: ex-fix + 1 wk · immediately after IR · single implant proven better · THA)

### Fixation constructs

**Cannulated screws** — **3 screws, inverted triangle**, **inferior screw posterior + along calcar**, max spread; **start at / above lesser trochanter** (below → subtrochanteric stress riser); avoid multiple lateral perforations; 4th screw for posterior comminution (unproven)\
✗ Wrong options: central

**Union by screw–cortex contact (Lindequist)** — posterior + inferior contact **89%** · one site **59%** · none **0%**

**Improve success** — **screws abutting posterior + inferior neck cortex**\
✗ Wrong options: titanium · start below LT · DHS for transcervical · alignment guide for parallelism

**Vertical (Pauwels III)** — **SHS + side plate** mechanically optimal (± anti-rotation screw)\
✗ Wrong options: 2–4 parallel screws (cantilever)

**FAITH (SHS vs cannulated screws)** — overall **no difference in reoperation / union**; **SHS ↑ AVN (9% vs 5%, NS)**; **SHS ↓ reoperation in displaced, basicervical, current smokers**

**55-yo FNF fixation — true?** — **SHS ↑ AVN vs screws**\
✗ Wrong options: cut-out with TAD < 25 mm (> 25 mm) · fixation ↓ reoperation vs arthroplasty · timing > reduction for AVN · screws ↑ reoperation overall

### Active elderly – THA vs hemiarthroplasty

| Outcome | THA vs hemiarthroplasty |
|---|---|
| **Function / outcome scores** | **Better with THA** (more so long-term ≥ 4 yr) |
| Pain relief | Better, more predictable |
| **Reoperation** | **Lower** (hemi → acetabular erosion) |
| **Dislocation** | **Higher (\~10%; 5× [Orthobullets] · 7× [Orthobullets complications, OB])**; \~5% vs 2% (OB) |
| Peri-operative complications, blood loss, time | **Higher** |
| Mortality, infection, LOS, readmission, periprosthetic fracture | **Similar** |

**THA best for** — **active, independent community ambulator** (golf instructor, pickle-ball player), **pre-existing OA**, < 75–80 yr

**Hemi best for** — **> 80**, low demand / household ambulator, **dementia, Parkinson's**, hepatic encephalopathy, can't follow precautions, **dialysis / multiple comorbidities** (THA no better short-term; hemi more cost-effective)

**85-yo Parkinson's, household (TO)** — **hemiarthroplasty** (bipolar; if THA → dual mobility)\
✗ Wrong options: THR · DHS · bed rest

**65-yo active Garden IV (TO)** — options AMP, bipolar, cemented, uncemented hemi — **answer key not provided by the source**

**THA vs internal fixation (elderly displaced)** — **lower reoperation**, less pain, better function; mortality similar; ADL better short-term only\
✗ Wrong options: shorter OR · less blood · less infection · shorter stay

**ORIF in dialysis patient** — **higher complication rate than hemi** (2 / 13 united); mortality 45% at \~2 yr, same between groups

### Hemiarthroplasty – cement, dislocation & conversion

**Cemented > uncemented** — **lower aseptic revision + fewer intra- / post-op periprosthetic fractures**, **better QoL at 4 mo**, better short / medium-term mobility · collared stems ↓ periprosthetic fracture\
✗ Wrong options: in-hospital mortality difference · shorter OR (cement longer)

**Unipolar ≈ bipolar** outcomes · bipolar for instability risk (Parkinson, dementia)

**Dislocation risk** — **posterior approach** (✗ Wrong options: anterior, anterolateral, bipolar, unipolar) · **posterior wall insufficiency**, labral injury, poor capsular repair, oversized head (✗ Wrong options: 20° anteversion · ↑ offset · +1.5 cm length (tighten) · cementless stem (fracture not dislocation))

**Anterolateral approach** — ↑ abductor weakness

**Failed bipolar with acetabular wear + superior migration** — **convert to THA — add acetabular component only** (femoral stem well fixed)\
✗ Wrong options: remove wires · walking aid · PT · revise stem

**Elderly displaced insufficiency fracture (walker user)** — **hemiarthroplasty**\
✗ Wrong options: CMN · DHS (for extracapsular) · ORIF · resurfacing (FNF absolute contraindication)

**Failed screw fixation in 82-yo, comminuted at revision** — **remove hardware + arthroplasty**

**Internal fixation vs hemi at 3 mo–1 yr** — **higher failure with fixation** (most within 2 yr) · **46% vs 8% failure at 2 yr** (> 70 yr) · mortality same

### Perioperative care

**Timing** — **as soon as medically optimised, ideally < 24–48 h**; **< 24 h of admission ↓ 30-d, 90-d, 1-yr mortality**; **delay > 4 days ↑ 1-yr mortality**; delay > 48 h acceptable **only to optimise multiple comorbidities**

**> 3 comorbidities** — **pre-op medical optimisation** matters more than speed

**Delays surgery without changing treatment** — **pre-op echocardiogram**\
✗ Wrong options: fascia iliaca block · geriatric admission · Hb < 10 · aspirin

**DOAC (rivaroxaban 12 h ago)** — **proceed to surgery**\
✗ Wrong options: andexanet (life-threatening bleeding only) · FFP (ineffective) · delay 24 h · PCC

**Anaesthesia** — spinal vs general: **no difference** in delirium, mortality, ambulation at 60 d (Orthobullets) · **general ↑ delirium + mortality** (OB) — read the options

**↓ Mortality + complications** — **surgery < 24 h**, **multidisciplinary / ortho-geriatric co-management**, neuraxial anaesthesia, **TXA**, **early WBAT + mobilisation**, opioid-sparing analgesia\
✗ Wrong options: delayed WB · echo · general anaesthesia · PCA · traction · SNF discharge

**Post-op delirium (\~25%)** — **↑ length of stay**, nursing home placement · risk: age, dementia, pre-injury nursing home\
✗ Wrong options: ICU · in-hospital death

### Mortality & prognosis

| Fact | Value |
|---|---|
| **1-yr mortality** | **\~25–30%** (14–36%; 20% in 85-yo) |
| **In-hospital mortality** | **\~6%** |
| **CRF / dialysis** | **\~45% at 2 yr** — **highest-risk pre-op factor** |
| Loss of independence | **\~50%** at 1 yr |

**Strongest determinant of survival** — **pre-injury mobility**

**Poor pre-injury cognition** — ↑ mortality after **hip fracture**

**Poor outcome predictors (TO)** — older age, not at home, poverty, unmarried, ↓ pre-fracture function, **cognitive impairment**, comorbidities, **high ASA**, ↓ grip, depression, ↓ albumin / folate, visual impairment, **extracapsular** type, delay > 48 h, **NWB post-op**, long LOS · BMI unrelated\
✗ Wrong options: **intracapsular fracture** (NOT a poor predictor)

**Loss of independence after fixation** — **age > 80, ASA > 1, prior walking aid, smoking**, implant placement quality · **pre-op ambulation without aid → independent at 1 yr**

### Timed Up and Go (TUG)

**Normal** — **< 12 s** → predicts **walking without aid at 2 yr** · **> 26 s** → persistent walking aid\
✗ Wrong options: predicts fracture, life expectancy, independent living, falls (**UPST predicts falls**, ASA predicts mortality)

### Neglected fracture (TO)

**AIIMS box technique (TO)** — **neglected femoral neck fracture** with large neck defect: box from GT base, head anterior wall + **quadratus femoris pedicle graft** posteriorly, cancellous autograft + screws

## 6 Complications

| Complication | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Osteonecrosis** | **10–45%**; ↑ displacement, **non-anatomic reduction**, **SHS (FAITH)**; can occur in undisplaced; time to reduction not proven → young > 50% head: FVFG vs THA · old: arthroplasty |
| **Nonunion** | **5–30%**; displaced; **varus malreduction** most linked to failure → **valgus IT osteotomy** (young), FVFG, arthroplasty |
| **Dislocation** | THA \~10% (5–7× hemi); hemi: posterior approach, labral / wall injury, oversized head |
| Failure | Fixation ≫ arthroplasty, mostly first 2 yr |
| Loss of independence | See above |

### Nonunion / malunion – valgus intertrochanteric osteotomy

**Concept** — turns **vertical shear** line into **horizontal compression**; corrects varus; works even with **non-collapsed AVN**

**Valgus osteotomy indication** — **young nonunion with viable head**\
✗ Wrong options: collapsed AVN → arthroplasty · retroversion / cross-over → PAO · high dysplasia → subtroch osteotomy + THA · metastases → cemented arthroplasty

**48-yo runner, varus collapse at 6 mo** — **valgus IT osteotomy + blade plate**\
✗ Wrong options: hemi · in-situ DHS · core decompression · revision screws

**22-yo vertical nonunion at 9 mo** — **valgus IT osteotomy + 130° blade plate**\
✗ Wrong options: convert to SHS · THA · perpendicular 7.0 screw · varus osteotomy

**Wedge + plate maths** — **side-plate angle = guide-pin angle + wedge angle** → pin 130° + **20° wedge → 150° plate**\
✗ Wrong options: 110° / 95° = varus · 140° = lateral gap · 35° + 165° (too short, non-standard)
