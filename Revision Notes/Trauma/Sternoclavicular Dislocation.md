# Sternoclavicular Dislocation

*Trauma · 57 / 67*

## 1 Basics

### Types

**Traumatic** — **anterior (commoner)** · **posterior → mediastinal structures at risk** · high energy (MVA, contact sport)

**Atraumatic subluxation** — younger, **generalised ligamentous laxity**; with **overhead elevation**, reduces on lowering arm

**Medial clavicle physis** — **first bone to ossify, last physis to close (20–25 yr)** · under 20–25 yr "dislocation" is usually a **physeal fracture** · closure \~25 yr (OB)

### Stabilisers — posterior capsular ligament

**SC joint** — **diarthrodial saddle joint**, **incongruous (\~50% contact)**, **fibrocartilage**, intra-articular disc · stability is **ligamentous**

| Structure | Function |
|---|---|
| **Posterior capsular (SC) ligament** | **Most important — primary restraint to anterior-posterior stability** |
| Anterior SC ligament | **Primary restraint to superior displacement** of medial clavicle |
| Costoclavicular (rhomboid) — anterior fasciculus | Resists **superior rotation + lateral displacement** |
| Costoclavicular — posterior fasciculus | Resists **inferior rotation + medial displacement** |
| Intra-articular disc ligament | Prevents **medial displacement**; secondary restraint to superior displacement |

**Prevents posterior dislocation** — **posterior SC joint capsule**\
✗ Wrong options: first rib · disc ligament · costoclavicular · interclavicular

## 3 Presentation

**Anterior** — palpable bump; prominence ↑ with abduction + elevation

**Posterior** — **dyspnoea, dysphagia**, tachypnoea, **stridor worse supine** · **paraesthesia** in arm · **venous congestion (unilateral jugular engorgement)** / ↓ pulse · ↓ arm ROM · turning head to affected side relieves pain

**Posterior complications** — **early**: pneumothorax, great vessel laceration / erosion / occlusion, **oesophageal rupture**, **brachial plexus compression**, respiratory distress, myocardial conduction abnormality · **late**: **tracheo-oesophageal fistula**, stridor, dysphagia

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Study | Finding |
|---|---|
| AP chest | Hard to see; often read as normal |
| **Serendipity view (40° cephalic tilt)** | **Anterior → clavicle above** contralateral · **posterior → clavicle below** |
| **CT (axial)** | **Study of choice** — direction, **mediastinal injury**, **distinguishes physeal fracture** |
| MRI | Adds nothing; delays treatment (avoid) |

## 5 Management

```
Sternoclavicular dislocation
├─ Atraumatic subluxation → observation / reassurance
├─ Chronic anterior (> 3 wk), mildly symptomatic → sling, symptomatic
├─ Acute (< 3 wk) anterior or posterior → closed reduction under GA in OR ± thoracic back-up
│      ├─ stable → immobilise
│      └─ unstable → accept deformity (anterior) / open reduction + reconstruction
└─ Posterior, failed closed reduction or chronic → open reduction + soft-tissue reconstruction
```

### Operative criteria

| Situation | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Atraumatic subluxation** | **Reassurance**, symptomatic |
| **Chronic anterior, minimally symptomatic (> 3 wk)** | **Sling**, unrestricted activity by **3 months** |
| **Acute anterior / posterior (< 3 wk)** | **Closed reduction under GA ± thoracic back-up** |
| **Acute posterior, failed closed reduction** (dysphagia, dyspnoea, ↓ pulses) | **Open reduction + soft-tissue reconstruction** |
| Chronic anterior / posterior, **persistently symptomatic** | **Open reduction + reconstruction** |
| **Chronic posterior (> 3 wk) with dysphagia** | **Open reduction in OR + thoracic back-up** (✗ Wrong options: sling (avoid late mediastinal problems)) |

### Posterior dislocation — reduction in OR with thoracic backup

**Posterior + ↓ pulses (16-yr)** — **reduction in OR with thoracic surgery back-up**\
✗ Wrong options: sling · ED reduction · ORIF + pinning of physis

**Closed reduction fails** — in **up to 75%** → open reduction

**Initial treatment** — **closed reduction in OR with towel clip** (anterior translation) · usually stable\
✗ Wrong options: observation · open first · ORIF · allograft reconstruction first

**After closed reduction** — **immobilisation 4 wk** (OB) · **Velpeau bandage 6 wk** (Orthobullets) — read the options · elbow exercises at 3 wk · sport at 3 months

**Cardiothoracic stand-by** — **reduction of posterior SC dislocation** · routine thoracic surgeon now **controversial** but still recommended\
✗ Wrong options: midshaft clavicle ORIF · distal clavicle excision · SC joint I&D · Judet scapula

**Never** — **ED reduction** (mediastinal injury), **compression plating across SC joint**, **K-wires** (migration)

**Closed reduction technique** — supine, arm at table edge, **abduct + extend** with **axial traction**, direct posterior pressure over medial clavicle · **towel clip / fingers** pull medial clavicle

### Open reduction & reconstruction

**Figure-of-8 tendon graft** — **2 drill holes in manubrium + 2 in medial clavicle**, **1 cm apart** · **gracilis or semitendinosus** · don't disrupt SCM sheath; clean capsulotomy for repair

**Medial clavicle excision** — outdated · resect **< 15 mm** to **preserve costoclavicular ligaments** (repair if injured) · Wirth + Rockwood: costoclavicular reconstruction ± medial head resection for unstable injuries

![SC joint reconstruction](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob1009-sc-joint-dislocation.jpg)

### Fixation hazards — pin migration

**Highest life-threatening complications** — **percutaneous pins (K-wires / Steinmann)** → migrate to **heart, pulmonary artery, aorta**\
✗ Wrong options: cannulated screws · cerclage · Balser plate · locking plate

### Anterior dislocation — natural history

**Anterior dislocation — true** — **rarely symptomatic when left unreduced** · surgery mainly **cosmetic**\
✗ Wrong options: stable after reduction (persistent instability common) · tracheal injury on reduction · fusion · acute medial clavicle excision

### Atraumatic spontaneous subluxation

**Diagnosis** — no trauma, **lump at base of neck**, worse **overhead** · settles in **4–6 wk** nonoperatively\
✗ Wrong options: unreduced posterior · hypoplasia · arthritis · hyperostosis

**Beighton-Horan ≥ 5 / 9 = hypermobility** · **treatment → observation**\
✗ Wrong options: figure-of-8 brace · resection · reconstruction · arthrodesis

**Type III SC instability (TO)** — **non-structural, abnormal muscle patterning** (inappropriate **pectoralis major**, EMG) → **biofeedback physiotherapy** · surgery only if structural abnormality\
✗ Wrong options: closed reduction + sling · open reduction + reconstruction · open reduction + sling

## 6 Complications

| Complication | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Cosmetic deformity** | Anterior — usually well tolerated |
| Mediastinal injury | Posterior — great vessels, trachea, oesophagus |
| **Pin migration** | Heart, pulmonary artery, aorta → **avoid K-wires** |

## X vs Y

| | **Anterior SC dislocation** | **Posterior SC dislocation** |
|---|---|---|
| Frequency | **Commoner** | Rarer |
| Risk | Cosmetic | **Mediastinal** |
| Serendipity | Clavicle **above** | Clavicle **below** |
| Acute | Closed reduction; accept if unstable | **Closed reduction in OR + thoracic back-up** |
| Chronic | **Observe** (if mild) | **Reduce** (open) |

**"Posterior SC + ↓ pulses?"** → **reduction in OR with thoracic back-up**

**"Footballer posterior SC on CT — next?"** → **closed reduction in OR**

**"Best initial treatment, posterior SC?"** → **closed reduction with towel clip**

**"Which surgery needs cardiothoracic stand-by?"** → **posterior SC dislocation reduction**

**"Structure preventing posterior dislocation?"** → **posterior SC joint capsule**

**"Primary A-P restraint?"** → **posterior SC capsular ligament**

**"Anterior SC dislocation — true?"** → **rarely symptomatic when unreduced**

**"Atraumatic subluxation, Beighton 6?"** → **observation**
