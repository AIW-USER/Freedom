# Hand & Forearm Compartment Syndrome

*Trauma · 29 / 67*

## 1 Basics

### Causes

**Pathophysiology** — trauma → bleeding + oedema → ↑ interstitial pressure → vascular occlusion → **myoneural ischaemia**; muscle damage from **3–4 h warm ischaemia** ("time is tissue")

**Fractures (commonest)** — **distal radius (adults)** · **supracondylar humerus (children)**; **floating elbow = highest paediatric risk**

**Other** — **crush**, contusion, GSW, tight casts / dressings, **IV / intra-arterial extravasation**, **burns (circumferential eschar)**, post-ischaemic swelling, bleeding disorders, arterial injury, **drug use (found down)**

**Obtunded patients** — **79% obtunded**, **68% from IV / intra-arterial extravasation** (Ouellette)

### Anatomy

**Upper extremity commonest site** — **forearm, volar compartment**

| Region | Compartments |
|---|---|
| Arm | **2** — anterior, posterior |
| **Forearm** | **3** — **volar (most often)**, dorsal, **mobile wad (BR, ECRL, ECRB; rarely)** (Orthobullets, OB) · **4** — dorsal, **superficial volar, deep volar**, mobile wad (TO) |
| **Hand** | **10** — **thenar, hypothenar, adductor pollicis, 4 dorsal interossei, 3 volar interossei** |

**VIC — commonest muscle + nerve (TO)** — **FDP** + **median nerve**

## 3 Presentation

### Diagnosis & fasciotomy

| Finding | Significance |
|---|---|
| **Pain out of proportion** | **First symptom**; absent with nerve damage; hard in polytrauma, sedated, children |
| **Pain on passive finger stretch (MCP / phalangeal extension)** | **Most sensitive** / early |
| **Tense hand in intrinsic MINUS position** | **Most consistent** clinical finding (✗ Wrong options: intrinsic plus) |
| Paraesthesia / hypoaesthesia | Nerve ischaemia |
| **Paralysis** | **Late** — full recovery rare |
| **Absent pulses** | **Late** — amputation usually inevitable |
| Cool, pale hand | Late; suggests **arterial injury** |
| Loss of vibration sense | Not specific |

**Earliest feature of VIC (TO)** — **pain on passive stretch of middle + ring fingers** (FDP of these fingers first)\
✗ Wrong options: skin changes · thumb stretch · intrinsic minus

**Forearm CS + numb thumb / index** — **acute carpal tunnel** → release CTR with fasciotomy

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

**Radiographs** — rule out fracture only · **no advanced imaging, CTA or Doppler** — delays surgery

### Diagnostic criteria

| Fasciotomy | Observe |
|---|---|
| **Clinical diagnosis** (no pressures needed) | Exam not consistent |
| **Δp < 30 mmHg** (diastolic − compartment) | **Δp > 30** |
| Absolute **> 30 mmHg** | — |

**Pressures indicated** — obtunded, intubated, **after a block**, polytrauma, equivocal

**Intra-op Δp < 30** — check **pre-op diastolic**; follow post-op (anaesthesia lowers diastolic)

### Normal hand compartment pressure

**Normal hand** — **0–15 mmHg** (e.g. **10 mmHg**) · **> 15 mmHg pathological** (some) · finger pressures **not measured** · volar–dorsal interosseous fascia incompetent

## 5 Management

```
Suspected hand / forearm compartment syndrome
├─ Clinical diagnosis → emergent fasciotomy of all involved compartments
│      └─ median nerve symptoms → add carpal tunnel release
├─ Obtunded / equivocal → pressures → Δp < 30 → fasciotomy
└─ Never: analgesia + wait, nerve block, overnight observation, CTA
```

**Crush hand, pain on passive MCP / wrist motion, normal NV** — **surgery**\
✗ Wrong options: imaging · Doppler · admit + observe · analgesia

**Peripheral nerve block** — **contraindicated** when CS suspected (masks symptoms)

### Forearm fasciotomy

| Incision | Releases | Interval |
|---|---|---|
| **Volar** — radial to FCU at wrist → **medial epicondyle**, extend distally | **Volar compartment + carpal tunnel** + lacertus fibrosus | **FCU ulnar, FDS radial** → open deep fascia |
| **Dorsal** — 2 cm distal to lateral epicondyle → mid-wrist | **Mobile wad + dorsal** | **EDC / ECRB** |

**Post-op** — **leave open** (VAC / wet-to-dry) → **re-debride 48–72 h** → delayed closure or **STSG**

![Volar forearm fasciotomy incision](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob1064-volar-incision.jpg)

### Hand fasciotomy

| Incision | Releases |
|---|---|
| **2 dorsal — over 2nd + 4th MCs** | **Dorsal + volar interossei**, **adductor pollicis** (1st volar interosseous + adductor via ulnar side of 2nd MC) |
| **Radial side of 1st MC** | **Thenar** |
| **Ulnar side of 5th MC** | **Hypothenar** |

**All 10 compartments** — released; wounds open → primary closure or STSG

## 6 Complications

### Volkmann ischaemic contracture

**Cause** — missed CS; **brachial artery obstruction (tight plaster, tourniquet)** → muscle necrosis → fibrosis

**Posture** — **elbow flexion, forearm pronation, wrist flexion, thumb adduction (+ flexion), MCP extension, IP flexion** = **intrinsic-minus (claw) hand**

**Progression (TO)** — FDP (middle + ring) → **FPL** → FDS, FCR, FCU → extensors (severe); median + ulnar neuropathy add to claw

| Tsuge stage | Muscles | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **Mild** | **Finger flexors** | **Dynamic splinting, tendon lengthening** |
| **Moderate** | **Wrist + finger flexors** | Excise necrotic muscle, **median + ulnar neurolysis**, **BR → FPL, ECRL → FDP** transfers, **flexor slide** |
| **Severe** | **Flexors + extensors** | As moderate ± **free functioning muscle transfer** |

**Tsuge – TO wording (TO)** — mild = **partial FDP (1–2 slips)** · moderate = **all flexors + nerve involvement** · severe = **flexors + extensors + skin / joint**

**Seddon's ellipsoid (TO)** — **central** part of flexor mass (**FDP + FPL**) most ischaemic → necrosis → fibrosis

**Volkmann sign / fixed-length phenomenon (TO)** — fingers **cannot be passively extended with wrist extended**; extend when wrist flexed · **ring + middle** fingers commonest

**VIC treatment (TO)** — **turnbuckle (dynamic) splint** + physio (mild) · muscle–tendon lengthening / Z-plasty (mild) · **Max Page muscle slide** = flexor–pronator origin released from **medial epicondyle** + slid distally · **arthrodesis / tenodesis / capsulodesis** for severe rigid deformity

**Prognosis** — loss of function, VIC, neurological deficit, infection, amputation

### Extravasation injury mimic

**Purple glove syndrome** — **IV phenytoin extravasation**: oedema, **violaceous** discoloration, pain; resolves with **stopping IV + elevation + monitoring**

**Mechanism** — **vesicant leaking into extravascular space** (phenytoin pH ≈ 12, precipitates)\
✗ Wrong options: axillary DVT · necrotising fasciitis · dermatomyositis · flexor tenosynovitis

**Irritant vs vesicant** — irritant = inflammation only · **vesicant = direct toxicity** (slough, blisters, necrosis, ± CS)

**Agents** — phenytoin, **TPN**, vasopressors, arginine, chemotherapy, contrast

**Management** — stop infusion, mark border, elevate, immobilise, heat / ice, antidotes, irrigation, debridement ± fasciotomy

## X vs Y

| | **Early** | **Late** |
|---|---|---|
| Findings | **Pain out of proportion, passive stretch pain**, swelling | **Paralysis, absent pulses, pallor** |

| | **Compartment syndrome** | **Purple glove (phenytoin)** |
|---|---|---|
| Treatment | **Fasciotomy** | **Stop IV, elevate, observe** |

## How it's asked

**"Earliest feature of VIC (TO)?"** → **pain on passive stretch of middle + ring fingers**

**"Crush hand, passive pain, no fracture — next?"** → **surgical intervention**

**"Burn eschar — early CS finding?"** → **pain on phalangeal extension**\
✗ Wrong options: absent pulses · Δp 38 · intrinsic plus · paralysis

**"Most supports hand CS?"** → **swelling + pain out of proportion**\
✗ Wrong options: vibration loss · finger pressure 15 · intrinsic plus · cool pale hand

**"Forearm crush, thumb / index numbness, passive pain?"** → **emergent forearm fasciotomy + carpal tunnel release**

**"Normal hand compartment pressure?"** → **10 mmHg**

**"Muscle-sliding operation for VIC?"** → **Max Page (flexor–pronator slide)** (TO)

**"Fingers extend only with wrist flexed?"** → **Volkmann sign (VIC)** (TO)

**"Swollen violaceous hand on IV phenytoin?"** → **extravasation of a vesicant**
