## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Glasgow Coma Scale

| Score | **Motor (6)** | **Verbal (5)** | **Eyes (4)** |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6 | Obeys | — | — |
| 5 | Localises pain | Oriented | — |
| 4 | Normal flexion (withdraws) | Confused | Spontaneous |
| 3 | **Abnormal flexion (decorticate)** | Inappropriate words | To speech |
| 2 | **Extension (decerebrate)** | Incomprehensible sounds | To pain |
| 1 | None | None | None |

**Brain injury** — **severe < 9 (≤ 8)** · moderate 9–12 · minor ≥ 13 · range **3–15**

**Limits** — ignores focal / lateralising signs, metabolic processes, intoxication

### Revised trauma score

| Code | GCS | Systolic BP | Resp. rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| **4** | **13–15** | **> 90** | **10–29** |
| 3 | 9–12 | 76–89 | **> 30** |
| 2 | 6–8 | 50–75 | 6–9 |
| 1 | 4–5 | 1–49 | 1–5 |
| 0 | 3 | 0 | 0 |

**RTS** — **GCS + SBP + RR** codes · **most widely used pre-hospital triage tool** · **lower score = more severe** · **RTS < 4 → transfer to trauma centre**

**RTS — true (TO)** — **improves appropriateness of treatment**\
✗ Wrong options: improves diagnostic / investigation error rate (no difference) · higher score = more severe · includes respiratory effort + capillary refill (those were in the old Trauma Score, removed in RTS)

**RTS limits** — **underestimates single-system injury** (may delay referral) · best for predicting survival in **head injury** (TO)

### Injury Severity Score calculation (ISS, MISS)

| AIS grade | Severity |
|---|---|
| 1 | Minor (laceration, contusion) |
| 2 | Moderate (**large joint dislocation**) |
| 3 | Severe, not life-threatening (**femoral shaft, open pelvic fracture**) |
| 4 | Severe, life-threatening, survival probable |
| 5 | Critical, survival uncertain (**crushed larynx**) |
| 6 | **Maximal / unsurvivable** (crushed head) |

**ISS** — **sum of squares of the highest AIS in the 3 most severely injured body regions** (A² + B² + C²) · range **1–75** · **any AIS 6 → 75** · first anatomical scoring system

**Body regions** — **6** (TO; OB: head, face, chest, abdomen, extremities + pelvis, external) · **9 anatomic regions** (Orthobullets) — read the options

**Only the highest AIS per region** — **bilateral femur fractures count once** → NISS fixes this

**Polytrauma threshold** — **ISS > 15** (major trauma; **10% mortality**) (Orthobullets, TO, OB) · **ISS ≥ 18** polytrauma / transfer (OB) — read the options

| Worked example | ISS |
|---|---|
| **Chest 4, liver 4, open tibia 3**, radius 2, scalp 1 (TO) | 16 + 16 + 9 = **41** |
| Open chest 4, colon 4, femur 3, shoulder 2, thyroid 1 | **41** |
| Subdural 4, spleen 4, pelvis / femur 3, nose 2, ribs 2 | **41** |
| **Bilateral femur 3 + abdomen 3 + face 2** + lung 2 | 9 + 9 + 4 = **22** |

**NISS** — **3 highest AIS regardless of region** → better for **severe single-region injury**; **more predictive of complications + mortality**

**ISS limits** — hard to calculate in resus · misses multiple injuries in one region

**MISS (paediatric)** — **5 regions** (neurological, face / neck, chest, abdomen / pelvic contents, extremities / pelvic girdle) · graded 1–5 · same sum-of-squares · **MISS < 40 → no deaths**; > 40 → 50%; > 50 → 75%

**MISS example** — chest 3 + abdomen 2 + face / neck 4 (GCS 14 → neuro grade 1, excluded) → 9 + 4 + 16 = **29**

### Mangled Extremity Severity Score

| MESS component | Grade |
|---|---|
| **Skeletal / soft-tissue injury** | **1–4** (low → very high energy) |
| **Limb ischaemia** | **1–3** (reduced pulse / pulseless / cool, paralysed, insensate) — **doubled if > 6 h** |
| **Shock** | **0–2** (normotensive / transient / persistent hypotension) |
| **Age** | **0–2** (< 30 / 30–50 / > 50) |

**NOT in MESS** — **neurological status** (TO) · **time from admission to surgery** (OB)

**Cut-off** — **≥ 7** (TO; original designers) · **> 7** (Orthobullets; OB) predicts amputation — read the options

**Accuracy** — **high specificity, low sensitivity** (lower scores may still need amputation) · not the sole criterion · MESS more accurate than LSI · doesn't predict function after salvage

### SIRS

| Criterion (1 point each; **≥ 2 = SIRS**) | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Heart rate | **> 90** |
| WBC | **< 4,000 or > 12,000** |
| Resp. rate | **> 20** or PaCO₂ < 32 mmHg |
| Temperature | **< 36 or > 38 °C** |

**SIRS** — ↑ cytokines, complement, hormones · likely genetic predisposition · → **DIC, ARDS, renal failure, MOF, shock**

## 6 Complications

### Reconstruction vs amputation outcomes

**Reconstruction vs amputation at 2 yr (LEAP)** — **reconstruction → higher rehospitalisation**\
✗ Wrong options: better return to work · better SIP · psychosocial SIP improves · better if no high school

**Sickness Impact Profile** — **12 categories** (sleep, eating, work, home, recreation; physical: ambulation, body care, movement; psychosocial: social, alertness, emotion, communication) · **psychosocial subscale doesn't improve with time**

### Sex differences in polytrauma outcomes

**Men vs premenopausal women, ≥ 10 yr after polytrauma** — **men: higher QoL scores** · **women: ↑ PTSD, ↑ sick-leave days** · screen for **depression**\
✗ Wrong options: men more absentee days · CRPS · psychiatric care · amputation

## How it's asked

**"ISS: chest 4, liver 4, tibia 3, radius 2, scalp 1 (TO)?"** → **41**

**"Child MISS: chest 3, abdomen 2, face / neck 4?"** → **29**

**"ISS: chest 4, colon 4, femur 3, shoulder 2, thyroid 1?"** → **41**

**"ISS: subdural 4, spleen 4, femur 3, pelvis 3…?"** → **41**

**"ISS: bilateral femurs 3, abdomen 3, face 2, lung 2?"** → **22**

**"Not in MESS (TO)?"** → **neurological status**

**"MESS EXCEPT?"** → **time from admission to surgery**

**"MESS 9 — specificity / sensitivity?"** → **high specificity, low sensitivity**

**"Reconstruction vs amputation at 2 yr?"** → **higher rehospitalisation**

**"Men vs women after polytrauma?"** → **men higher QoL**

**"RTS — true (TO)?"** → **improves appropriateness of treatment**
