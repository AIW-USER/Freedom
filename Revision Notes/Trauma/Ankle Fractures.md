# Ankle Fractures

*Trauma · 4 / 67*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Incidence** — **187 / 100,000 adults / yr**

**Bimodal** — **young men 15–24 yr** · **elderly women 75–84 yr**

**Pattern split** — isolated malleolus **70%** · bimalleolar **20%** · trimalleolar **7%**

**Risk factors** — male, younger age, **obesity**, **smoking**, **alcohol**

**Associated** — open 2% · **syndesmotic injury 10%** · chondral injury · peroneal tendon tears 4%

### Anatomy – ligaments

| Complex | Components | Key fact |
|---|---|---|
| **Deltoid** | **Superficial** (MM → navicular, sustentaculum, talus) — resists **hindfoot eversion** · **Deep** (MM → talus) — resists **talar ER** | Deep deltoid = **primary restraint to anterolateral talar displacement**; inserts on **posterior colliculus** |
| **Lateral** | **ATFL** — restrains anterior displacement, IR, inversion; **most often injured** · **CFL** — deep to peronei; 2nd · **PTFL** — **strongest**, least injured | — |
| **Syndesmosis** | AITFL · **PITFL** · IOL · interosseous membrane · inferior transverse ligament | See below |

### Syndesmotic ligament anatomy

| Ligament | Tibial origin | Fibular insertion |
|---|---|---|
| **AITFL** | **Chaput** tubercle (anterolateral tibia) | **Wagstaffe** tubercle (anterior fibula) |
| **PITFL** — **strongest** | **Volkmann** (posterior malleolus / posterolateral tibia) | Posterior lateral malleolus |
| IOL | Distal continuation of interosseous membrane | — |
| Inferior transverse | Runs with PITFL to posterolateral plafond | — |

**Pilon fragments** — **Chaput (anterolateral) = AITFL** · **Volkmann (posterolateral) = PITFL**\
✗ Wrong options: ATFL · IOL · ITL · tibionavicular (deltoid)

**Avulsion eponyms** — **Tillaux–Chaput** = AITFL off **tibia** · **LeFort–Wagstaffe** = AITFL off **fibula** (with SER)

### Anatomy – tendons & nerves at risk

| Structure | Location | At risk with |
|---|---|---|
| **Peroneal tendons** (PL, **PB**) | Posterior fibular groove | **Posterolateral (antiglide) fibular plate** |
| **Tibialis posterior** | Posteroinferior to MM | **Posteriorly placed MM screws** |
| ATA + **deep peroneal n.** | Between EDL + EHL | Anterior approach |
| PTA + **tibial n.** | Behind MM, between FDL + FHL | Posteromedial approach |
| **Superficial peroneal n.** | Crosses fibula **posterior → anterior \~5–7 cm** above tip (3–12 cm) | Proximal **direct lateral**, posterolateral, anterior / anterolateral |
| **Sural n.** | Posterolateral | Posterolateral + lateral approaches |

**SPN facts** — **L4–S1** · branches off CPN at fibular neck · motor to **PL + PB** (eversion) · subcutaneous in **lower third of leg** · **two terminal branches** (medial + intermediate dorsal cutaneous)\
✗ Wrong options: 1st web space = deep peroneal · dorsiflexors = deep peroneal

### Biomechanics

**Talus** — trapezoidal, **wider anteriorly** → dorsiflexion = fibula ER + lateral translation; plantarflexion → narrow posterior talus, talar IR

**Fibula** — **lateral buttress** against talar shift

**1 mm lateral talar shift** — **↓ tibiotalar contact area 42%** (40–42%)

## 2 Mechanism

**Usual** — **twisting injury**

### Lauge-Hansen classification

**Name** — first word = **foot position**, second = **force on foot relative to tibia**; predicts MRI ligament injury in **< 50%**

| Type | Stages | Fibula pattern |
|---|---|---|
| **Supination–adduction (SAD)** | 1 talofibular sprain / **distal fibular avulsion (tip)** → 2 **vertical MM** + **anteromedial plafond impaction** | **Low transverse**, infrasyndesmotic |
| **Supination–external rotation (SER)** — **commonest** | 1 AITFL sprain → 2 **short oblique fibula (anteroinferior → posterosuperior)** → 3 PITFL rupture / **posterior malleolus** → 4 **transverse MM or deltoid** | At syndesmosis (Weber B) |
| **Pronation–abduction (PAB)** | 1 **transverse MM / deltoid** → 2 AITFL → 3 **transverse comminuted fibula above syndesmosis** (butterfly) | Comminuted, above / at syndesmosis |
| **Pronation–external rotation (PER)** | 1 **transverse MM / deltoid** → 2 AITFL → 3 **short oblique / spiral fibula (anterosuperior → posteroinferior) above joint** → 4 PITFL / posterior malleolus | High spiral (Weber C); **Maisonneuve** |

**PAB failure sequence** — medial → AITFL → lateral (medial-to-lateral disruption)

**✗ Wrong options: "pronation-adduction" / "supination-abduction"** — do not exist

![Supination–adduction: vertical medial malleolus, low transverse fibula](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob1047-supination-adduction.jpg)

### Supination–adduction specifics

**SAD = 5–20% of ankle fractures** — **highest talar / hindfoot injury** (talar OCD + medial plafond impaction) → PTOA up to 80%

**Vertical MM fracture on axial CT** — SAD

**Hyperdorsiflexion association** — SAD with plafond impaction

## 3 Presentation

**Symptoms** — severe pain, unable to bear weight

**Exam** — swelling, ecchymosis; **medial tenderness / swelling / ecchymosis NOT reliable** for deltoid competence (Tornetta) · **palpate proximal fibula** (Maisonneuve) · fracture blisters, tenting, open wounds · PVD, diabetic neuropathy

**On-field obvious fracture-dislocation in athlete (TO)** — after ABC → **splint limb in situ as it lies**; assessment < 60 s; reduce after leaving field of play\
✗ Wrong options: reduce on field · immediate shift · oral analgesic

**Pulseless foot with fracture-dislocation** — **reduce + splint → re-evaluate pulses**\
✗ Wrong options: vascular consult · CTA · angiogram first

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| View / study | Use |
|---|---|
| **AP, lateral, mortise** | Ankle series |
| **Mortise (TO)** | AP in **15° internal rotation** (clears fibular shadow) — best for articular surfaces |
| **ER stress (manual)** | **Mortise view**, foot **dorsiflexed + ER**, tibia stabilised → **deltoid competence**; more sensitive than tenderness / ecchymosis |
| **Gravity stress** | Lateral decubitus; **= manual stress** |
| **Full-length tib-fib** | Rule out **Maisonneuve** (any medial injury / isolated posterior malleolus / widened MCS without fibula fracture) |
| **CT** | **Trimalleolar** (posterior malleolus size + morphology — **axial + sagittal**; 25% of surgeons change plan) · **SAD** (anteromedial impaction, talar cartilage) · loose bodies, comminution · **suspected syndesmotic malreduction post-op** · **occult nonunion** · **not routine** — comminuted, posterior malleolar, suspected syndesmotic, pre-op planning (TO) |
| MRI | Deltoid, syndesmosis (**lambda sign**), lateral ligaments, peroneals, talar OCL — utility controversial |

### Radiographic parameters

| Parameter | Normal | Abnormal / meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Medial clear space** | **≤ 4 mm** (mortise or stress) | **> 5 mm with ER stress on dorsiflexed ankle → deep deltoid tear** · > 4 mm or **≥ 1 mm more than superior clear space** = deltoid incompetent (SER-4) |
| **Tibiofibular clear space** | **< 6 mm** on AP + mortise, measured **1 cm above joint** | Widened → syndesmotic injury (fixation if **> 5 mm on AP**, treatment section) |
| **Tibiofibular overlap** | **> 6 mm on AP**, **> 1 mm on mortise** | ↓ → syndesmotic injury / **malreduction → CT** (e.g. 2 mm overlap on AP) |
| **Talocrural angle** | **83 ± 4°** (OB) · **75–86°** (one OB explanation) | Fibular **shortening → ↑ angle** → lateral talar tilt |
| Talar tilt | **< 5°** | — |
| Talar tilt / asymmetry | — | **> 2 mm** = instability marker |
| **Ball / dime sign (TO)** | **Unbroken curve** from recess at fibular tip to **lateral talar process** | **Absent → fibula shortened / malreduced** (✗ Wrong options: deltoid tear · ATFL · syndesmosis) |

**Talocrural angle** — line ⟂ plafond (or tibial axis) vs **line between malleolar tips**; not 100% reliable for fibular length

**Weber B stable fracture on AP** — **tibiofibular clear space < 4 mm**\
✗ Wrong options: overlap < 3 mm · Weber C · PER · MCS > 5 mm

**Radiological signs of instability (TO)** — ↑ medial clear space · ↓ tibiofibular overlap · **break in mortise arc** · **proximal fibular migration** · talar tilt / subluxation (TO)

**TF overlap / clear space** — overlap **> 6 mm AP** (Orthobullets) · **> 10 mm AP** (TO) · clear space **< 6 mm** (Orthobullets) · **< 5 mm** (TO) — read the options

![Medial clear space](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob1047-medial-clear-space.jpg)

**Posterior malleolus signs** — double contour, misty mountains, **spur sign**

**Spur sign (hyperplantarflexion variant)** — **double cortical density at inferomedial tibial metaphysis**; **79% sensitive, 100% specific**; pathognomonic

**Fleck sign (TO)** — cortical avulsion off **lateral malleolus** → **superior peroneal retinaculum injury** → peroneal subluxation\
✗ Wrong options: ATFL avulsion · Tillaux · Wagstaffe

### Stability assessment – stress radiographs & deltoid competence

**Best test of deltoid competence** — **ER stress radiograph** (manual or gravity)\
✗ Wrong options: anterior drawer = ATFL · IR / inversion stress · palpation · squeeze · Canale view

**ORIF indication in isolated fibula fracture** — **MCS widening on gravity stress** (= SER-4 / bimalleolar equivalent)\
✗ Wrong options: medial tenderness / swelling · squeeze test · Cotton (intra-op)

**Deep deltoid intact even with 8–10 mm stress widening** (recent MRI studies) — MRI role controversial

### Other classifications

| Danis-Weber / AO-OTA 44 | Level | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **A / 44A** | **Infrasyndesmotic** | Generally stable |
| **B / 44B** | **Trans-syndesmotic** | SER |
| **C / 44C** | **Suprasyndesmotic** | PER; syndesmotic injury common |

**Weber stability (TO)** — **A** = most stable → conservative · **B** = variable → ORIF often required · **C** = most unstable → ORIF + syndesmotic fixation (TO)

**Ring concept (TO)** — ankle = closed ring · **single break = stable** · **≥ 2 breaks = unstable → surgery** (TO)

**Anatomic** — isolated medial / lateral / posterior · bimalleolar-equivalent · bimalleolar · trimalleolar

**Variants** — Bosworth · hyperplantarflexion (6%) · **curbstone** (posterior tibial avulsion from tripping) · LeFort–Wagstaffe · Tillaux–Chaput

### Posterior malleolus classification (Mason / modified Haraguchi)

| Type | Fragment |
|---|---|
| **1** | Small **extra-articular** posterior malleolar fragment |
| **2** | **Posterolateral triangle (Volkmann)** |
| **3** | **Coronal line involving whole posterior plafond** |

### Differential

| Mimic | Clue |
|---|---|
| Ankle sprain | May weight-bear; + anterior drawer / talar tilt; no fracture |
| Syndesmotic injury | **+ squeeze (Hopkin)**, widening on stress, **lambda sign** on MRI |
| Achilles rupture | Gap, + Thompson, ↑ resting dorsiflexion |
| Pilon | High energy, axial load; Chaput, Volkmann, MM, central impaction |
| Subtalar dislocation | High energy; 25% open |

## 5 Management

```
Rotational ankle fracture
├─ Stable (no talar shift on stress) → splint / boot, early WBAT
└─ Talar shift / bimalleolar / Maisonneuve / Bosworth / open → ORIF
       └─ after fixation → stress syndesmosis → fix if unstable
```

```
Syndesmosis after malleolar fixation
├─ Posterior malleolus fractured → fix PM first (restores PITFL)
└─ Cotton / ER stress still widens → open reduction + screw or suture button
```

### Operative criteria

| Nonoperative | ORIF |
|---|---|
| **Stable isolated MM** (no talar shift), avulsion tips | **Any talar displacement** |
| **Stable isolated LM** (stress negative) | **Bimalleolar / bimalleolar-equivalent** |
| **PM < 25% and < 2 mm step-off**, stable syndesmosis | **PM > 25% or > 2 mm** step-off, syndesmotic instability, posterior talar subluxation |
| Unfit for surgery | **Maisonneuve** · **Bosworth** · **open** · symptomatic malleolar nonunion |
| — | **LM > 3 mm displacement** |

### Stable fractures – nonoperative management

**Stable Weber B (stress negative, < 3 mm, TCA 83°)** — **below-knee splint 2–4 wk → WBAT in boot**\
✗ Wrong options: TCC · MRI · CT · ORIF

**Stable LM** — **removable walking boot + progressive WB**\
✗ Wrong options: strict NWB cast · fibula ORIF ± medial repair

**Isolated MM, no talar shift** — NWB 4–6 wk; **> 95% union**

**Unstable fractures in elderly** — operate on same indications as young; cast for unstable (esp. PAB) → **48–73% malunion / nonunion**

### Medial malleolus fixation

| Technique | Indication |
|---|---|
| **Lag screws ⟂ fracture** | Transverse MM |
| **Antiglide / buttress plate + lag screws, screws parallel to joint** | **Vertical shear MM (SAD)** — biomechanically superior to screws alone |
| **Tension band** | Fragment too small, poor bone |

**Bicortical 3.5 mm fully threaded (lag by technique)** > unicortical 4.0 mm partially threaded

### Supination–adduction – marginal impaction & fixation

**Unique, must-recognise feature** — **anteromedial plafond marginal impaction**\
✗ Wrong options: syndesmotic diastasis (syndesmosis not disrupted in SAD) · deltoid tear · fibular overlengthening

**Treatment** — **anteromedial approach / arthrotomy** → **elevate impaction + bone graft** → **medial antiglide (buttress) plate** + fibular plate\
✗ Wrong options: oblique MM screws · tension band · bridge plating

**Unaddressed impaction** — **malunion with anteromedial pain** → **surgical correction**\
✗ Wrong options: syndesmotic fixation · steroid · PT · hardware removal

### Lateral malleolus fixation

| Construct | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Lateral plate** (1/3 tubular / anatomic) | **Distal bicortical screws may be intra-articular** |
| **Posterolateral antiglide plate** | **Biomechanically stronger** than lateral; screws exit anteriorly; **peroneal (brevis) irritation** — hardware removal up to 43% |
| **Locking plate** | **Stiffest / most rigid** (✗ Wrong options: fibular nail · antiglide · non-locked bridge · lag screws alone); risks prominence, excess stiffness |
| **Retrograde IM fibular nail** | Poor soft tissue / wound-healing risk |

**Peroneal tendon lesions** — **low plate + prominent screw in most distal hole**\
✗ Wrong options: trimmed · uncontoured · locked plates

**Plate modes** — **neutralisation** protects a lag screw · **buttress** = under-contoured plate + axilla screw (compression vs shear) · **antiglide** = uncontoured plate fixed at fracture apex (posterolateral in SER) · bridge = relative stability · tension band = tension side of eccentric load

**Weber B reduction (TO)** — fibula displaced **posteriorly + externally rotated** → **traction + internal rotation**; contour plate with an **internal-rotation bend** (TO)

**Plate choice (TO)** — anatomical distal fibular or 1/3 tubular · **hook plate if distal fragment small** · unstable Weber A → plate or **tension band** (TO)

**Fixation sequence (TO)** — **fibula first** (length + rotation) → posterior malleolus → medial malleolus (antiglide / tension band) → **Cotton test** for syndesmosis (TO)

**Absolute stability** — articular fractures: **PM > 25%** / large PM 45% → **ORIF with absolute stability**\
✗ Wrong options: relative stability · cast · nail · ex-fix

### Bimalleolar-equivalent & bimalleolar

**Bimalleolar-equivalent** — fibula ORIF ± syndesmosis; **deltoid repair not needed**; explore medially only if **mortise irreducible** (interposed deltoid)

**Bimalleolar** — ORIF any displacement; ORIF > closed; **early WB → better ROM, earlier return**

### Syndesmosis – diagnosis & intra-operative testing

**Incidence by fibula level** — Weber A **< 10%** · Weber B **40–50%** · **Weber C > 80%**; fixation usually not needed if fibula fracture **within 4.5 cm of plafond**

**Most unstable direction** — **anterior–posterior** → **lateral stress view more reliable**\
✗ Wrong options: medial–lateral

**Cotton (hook) test** — bone hook pulls fibula laterally under fluoro after fixation → **most reliable intra-op test**; best done in **sagittal plane, 100 N, lateral view** (van den Bekerom)

**ER stress after fibular fixation widens** — syndesmosis injured → **open reduction + fixation of syndesmosis**\
✗ Wrong options: arthrotomy · close · revise fibula · ex-fix

**Cotton test without widening** — **no further fixation**

**Best method to assess syndesmosis (Weber B, intra-op)** — **dynamic ER stress (ankle at 90°)**\
✗ Wrong options: MCS alone · overlap · anterior drawer · pre-op CT

### Syndesmosis – reduction technique, fixation & outcomes

**Most important for outcome** — **anatomic syndesmotic reduction**\
✗ Wrong options: immediate WB · OCD debridement · medial repair · neutral casting

**Malreduction rate** — **16–51%** (> 50% reported; up to 30% with screws / flexible fixation on CT)

**Most accurate reduction check** — **direct visualisation at anterolateral joint line** (plafond, talar dome, lateral malleolus)\
✗ Wrong options: radiographic parameters · incisura view

**Open reduction** — shown to guide anatomic reduction\
✗ Wrong options: clamp 2 cm too proximal · bump + IR · plantarflexion = ER → malreduction · dorsiflexion (no effect)

| Clamp rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Lateral tine** | **Directly on lateral fibular (malleolar) ridge** (✗ Wrong options: posterior to ridge → sagittal malreduction) |
| **Medial tine** | **Mid-medial tibia** = **anterior third of tibia on true lateral** |
| Axis | **Parallel to trans-syndesmotic angle** / joint line |
| Level | **1–2 cm above plafond** (incisura) |
| Force | Judicious — avoid over-compression |
| No | Clamp on malleolar tips |

**Fibular shortening** — first causes **syndesmotic malreduction** (lengthening → impingement; gap → nonunion) → restore **length, alignment, rotation** (dime sign / Shenton line)

**Ankle position** — reduce at **neutral**; **full dorsiflexion not required**\
✗ Wrong options: "complete dorsiflexion" → stays widened

| Fixation fact | Value |
|---|---|
| Implant | **1–2 screws** or **suture button**, **2–4 cm above joint** |
| Angle | **20–30° posterior → anterior** (fibula posterior to tibia) |
| Cortices / size | 3 or 4 cortices, 3.5 or 4.5 mm — **no proven difference** |
| **Suture button** | **Lowest malreduction + reoperation** |
| Screw retention | **≥ 8–12 wk** (Orthobullets) · **≥ 12 wk** (OB) — **NWB**, screws can't take gait loads |

**Syndesmotic fixation indications (TO)** — PER injuries · suprasyndesmotic fractures · **MCS widening > 2 mm after fibular fixation** · screw **3.5 mm, 3–4 cm above talar dome, 3 cortices, routine removal at 3 months** · tightrope needs no removal (TO)

**Retained intact screw** — **may be worse than loosened / broken screw**; broken ≈ loose; removing broken screw doesn't help · remove intact screw if **painful prominence + dorsiflexion < 10°** (\~9 mo)

**Posterior malleolus fixation** — restores **PITFL** (strongest syndesmotic ligament) → **70% stiffness vs 40%** with syndesmotic screw alone → **obviates syndesmotic fixation in most cases**; outcomes ≥ screw fixation; still stress-test after

**PM fixation EXCEPT trap** — (✗ Wrong options: "biomechanically **inferior** to trans-syndesmotic fixation") · PITFL usually **intact / partially injured** attached to fragment

**PER-4 with PM fragment — INCORRECT statement** — "**PITFL is disrupted**" (syndesmosis fails through avulsed PM)

**Posterolateral incision in trimalleolar** — to **reduce + stabilise syndesmotic attachments** via PM fixation\
✗ Wrong options: protect SPN · ↓ wound problems · direct syndesmosis repair · see articular surface

### Maisonneuve / proximal fibula injury

**Definition (TO)** — **proximal-third fibula fracture + distal syndesmotic disruption + MM fracture / deltoid** → **pronation–external rotation**\
✗ Wrong options: ER-abduction · SAD

**Missed rate** — 14–44% → **full-length tib-fib films**\
✗ Wrong options: MRI · CT · bone scan · repeat ankle films

**Isolated posterior malleolus fracture / medial injury without fibula fracture** — **image whole tibia-fibula**

**High ankle sprain with diastasis, "no fracture" at urgent care** — missed **proximal fibula fracture**\
✗ Wrong options: proximal TFJ dislocation · ATFL · CFL

**Treatment** — almost always operative: **reduce + stabilise syndesmosis** (open reduction best)

### Posterior malleolus – fixation & approach

**Posterolateral approach** — interval **FHL (tibial n.) ↔ peroneals (SPN)** → true internervous plane; prone / lateral; reaches **PM + posterior fibula** through one incision · workhorse for PM + pilon\
✗ Wrong options: deep peroneal / sural · deep peroneal / tibial · SPN / DPN = anterolateral

**Posteromedial approach** — PM with medial exit; needs separate fibular incision

**Fixation** — **posterior antiglide / buttress plate** · **A→P lag screws** (fibula first, must be reduced)

**Hyperplantarflexion variant (spur sign)** — **vertical shear of posteromedial tibial rim** → **buttress plate PM via posterolateral approach** + fibula ORIF\
✗ Wrong options: A→P screws · syndesmotic stress only

**40–90% of distal-third spiral tibia fractures** — have a posterior malleolus fracture

### Irreducible fracture-dislocation (Bosworth)

**Bosworth** — fibula **entrapped behind posterolateral ridge of tibia** → **irreducible** (foot held ER)\
✗ Wrong options: AITFL · PITFL · PB · FHL · deltoid · EDB

**Treatment** — **open reduction via posterolateral approach** + fixation of fibula + posterolateral fragment

**Risks** — **compartment syndrome** (irreducible) · posteromedial rim extension = "posterior pilon variant" · **post-op loss of dorsiflexion** from **posterior fixation**

**Interposition elsewhere** — FHL blocks **lateral** subtalar dislocation · EDB blocks **medial** subtalar dislocation

### Soft tissue – fracture-dislocation, external fixation, open fracture

**Fracture blisters** — appear **6–72 h**; delay definitive fixation until re-epithelialised; leave intact blisters, unroofed → **silver sulfadiazine**

**Timing (TO)** — urgent: dislocations, open fractures · operate before swelling or after **wrinkle sign (\~10–14 days)** · delay if blisters on incision line · **deep dirty wounds > 12–24 h = contraindication** (TO)

**Failed closed reduction + haemorrhagic blisters over incisions** — **temporary spanning ex-fix → relative stability**\
✗ Wrong options: repeat closed reduction · 1.6 mm K-wires (too thin) · definitive ORIF

**Fracture-dislocation + blisters (well-controlled DM)** — **closed reduction + ex-fix** least loss of reduction / skin necrosis\
✗ Wrong options: TCC · splint · fibular pinning alone

**Ex-fix vs splint in fracture-dislocation** — **lower redislocation + skin complications**

**Pilon (closed, high energy)** — **spanning ex-fix → staged reconstruction** (1–2 wk) ↓ soft-tissue complications

**Open ankle fracture** — emergent debridement + ORIF if soft tissue allows; **primary closure** OK in clean **I, II, IIIA** in healthy patients

**Type I open, clean — NOT true** — "**wound VAC + delayed closure ↓ infection**" (delayed closure ↑ infection) · early antibiotics ↓ infection · **ASA 2 ↑ infection risk**

**Allgöwer-Donati suture** — proven **↑ incisional perfusion**\
✗ Wrong options: faster · fewer complications · cosmesis

**Smoking** — ↑ wound complications; **4 wk cessation → infection risk = non-smokers**\
✗ Wrong options: MTX · gold · HCQ · TNF-α inhibitors

### Diabetes & neuropathy – complications & fixation strategy

**Neuropathy** — **independent risk factor for complications after ankle fracture** (✗ Wrong options: DR · patella · metatarsal · distal femur) · **most important predictor** (✗ Wrong options: BMI · CKD III · age · sex)

**Complication rates** — absent pulses **83%** · neuropathy **92%** → **neuropathy > absent pulses** · surgical complications up to 40% · **deep infection up to 20%** · cast skin breakdown up to 50%

**Commonest post-op complication in diabetics** — **wound problems** · Charcot after neuropathy **> 5%** · neuropathy ↑ delayed / nonunion\
✗ Wrong options: malunion

**Nonop in diabetic** — worst risk = **loss of reduction / fracture displacement** (> Charcot, ulcer, DVT); also Charcot, malunion, nonunion → **ORIF preferred** (may forestall Charcot)

**No neuropathy / vasculopathy** — **standard ORIF**\
✗ Wrong options: TCC for fracture stability (TCC heals soft tissue only)

**Enhanced fixation** — **multiple quadricortical syndesmotic screws even if syndesmosis intact** · **tibiotalar Steinmann pins / retrograde hindfoot nail** · spanning ex-fix · IM fibular K-wires · **stiffer plates** (locking / compression vs 1/3 tubular) · bicortical MM screws · limited dissection, percutaneous where possible

**WB** — **NWB 8–12 wk** (vs 4–8 wk normal) → then protected WB \~6 wk → e.g. **CAM boot at 10 wk**\
✗ Wrong options: 2 wk · 6 wk

**Primary TTC nail candidate** — **complicated diabetes** (neuropathy, nephropathy, PVD) + poor control (e.g. 71 yo, neuropathy, A1c 9.4%)\
✗ Wrong options: smoker · controlled DM without neuropathy · PVD alone · osteopenic refracture

**TTC fusion position** — **neutral dorsiflexion, 5–10° ER, 5° hindfoot valgus, 0–5 mm posterior talar translation**

**TTC salvage** — locking screws, **long nail** (avoid proximal stress fracture)\
✗ Wrong options: short nails

**Failed fixation → PTOA in DM + neuropathy + PVD** — **ankle arthrodesis**\
✗ Wrong options: TAA (PVD contraindicates) · triple · re-ORIF

**Not a supplemental adjunct** — deltoid repair, extraperiosteal bridge plating, denosumab, above-knee immobilisation

### Return to driving – braking time

**After right ankle ORIF** — braking normal at **9 wk** post-op (Egol) · **6–9 wk** (OB) · **6–8 wk** (one OB answer) — read the options

**Lower-limb long bone / periarticular** — **6 wk after starting WB**

**Not proxies** — full WB, radiographic union, plantarflexion strength, walking speed, full ROM

| I | 0–4 | Immobilisation, NWB |
| II | 4–8 | Partial WB, gentle ROM |
| III | 8–12 | Strengthening, proprioception |
| IV | > 12 | Sport, return to activity |

### Early malreduction – revision ORIF of syndesmosis

**Malreduced syndesmosis (CT: fibula posterior in incisura) at 2 wk** — **remove screws, re-reduce, revise**\
✗ Wrong options: PT · observe · add anterior screw · loosen screws

**Malrotated fibula + syndesmotic malreduction at 3 wk** — **revision ORIF fibula + syndesmosis**\
✗ Wrong options: WB · deltoid repair

**Widened syndesmosis + shortened fibula at 4 mo** — **revision ORIF with open syndesmosis reduction**\
✗ Wrong options: just add screw · PT · bracing

**Persistent MCS widening + ossicle after hardware removal** — **revision ORIF + explore syndesmosis + medial ankle** (interposed scar / deltoid / tib post)

**Athlete, persistent syndesmotic widening at 4 mo, fibula anatomic** — **revision ORIF syndesmosis**\
✗ Wrong options: screw removal · fibular osteotomy · deep deltoid repair (not feasible)

### Malunion – fibular length / rotation & corrective osteotomy

**Typical malunion** — distal fibula **shortened + externally rotated** → talar tilt, instability, PTOA

**Prevention** — **restore fibular length + rotation**\
✗ Wrong options: deltoid repair · quadricortical screw · two screws

**Correction** — **fibular lengthening (± rotational, oblique) osteotomy + syndesmosis reduction** · even with **early arthritis** · secondary fusion needed in only 10–15%\
✗ Wrong options: shortening osteotomy · syndesmotic fixation only · fusion

**Joint-preserving vs sacrificing** — decided by **degree of tibiotalar arthritis**\
✗ Wrong options: deformity · date · gender · WC

**Chronic Maisonneuve (12 wk) with shortened fibula, no OA** — **fibular osteotomy + syndesmosis ORIF**

**Old malunion + MM nonunion** — **corrective osteotomy of fibula + MM (graft) ± syndesmosis reconstruction**\
✗ Wrong options: steroid / HA injection · fusion

**Shortened fibula + deltoid instability, no OA** — **medial arthrotomy + debridement + fibular correction**\
✗ Wrong options: arthrodesis

**Instability markers** — asymmetric clear spaces · **talar tilt > 2 mm** · talar subluxation · abnormal TCA

**Persistent pain 1 yr after shortened fixation** — **fibular malunion**\
✗ Wrong options: nonunion · "screw instead of dynamic" · deltoid

### Delayed union / nonunion

**Pain at fibula site 9 mo after nonop** — **CT** (detects nonunion missed on X-ray)\
✗ Wrong options: immobilise · sympathetic blocks · PT

**Symptomatic MM nonunion, large fragment** — **ORIF + autograft** · malleolar nonunion 5–30%\
✗ Wrong options: excision (only small fragments) · percutaneous · PT

### Outcomes & post-traumatic arthritis

**Most important factor for outcome** — **anatomic reduction**\
✗ Wrong options: PT · early WB · early ROM · Ca/vit D

**PM step-off ≥ 2 mm post-op** — **independent risk for arthritis** (fragment size not clearly linked)

**Overall** — \~90% success; final function at **\~2 yr**; 90% mild / no pain at 1 yr

**Good outcome predictors** — **age < 40**, male, **ASA 1–2**, no diabetes

**Adverse** — older age, osteoporosis, **diabetes**, PVD, female, ↑ ASA, **smoking**, **alcohol**, ↓ education, **medial malleolar fracture**

**Age** — **no effect on outcomes / complications of unstable fractures** (EXCEPT / LEAST answer)\
✗ Wrong options: osteoporosis · function · smoking · diabetes · skin

### Fibular stress fracture

**Who** — athletes, **military recruits**; female, training change, older age

**X-ray** — negative or **periosteal reaction along outer distal fibular shaft**\
✗ Wrong options: avulsion = sprain · syndesmotic HO = old high sprain · calcified lesion = enchondroma · anterior spur = impingement

**Treatment** — immobilise + rest → heals 6–12 wk, return \~9 wk

### Paediatric transitional fractures (TO)

| Fracture (TO) | Salter-Harris | Key fact |
|---|---|---|
| **Tillaux** | **SH III** of distal tibia | **AITFL avulses anterolateral epiphysis (Chaput)**; age **12–14**; forced **lateral rotation of foot / medial rotation of leg on fixed foot** (SER); ORIF if **> 2 mm**; \~5% of paediatric ankle fractures |
| **Triplane** | **SH IV** | Age **10–17**; **sagittal** epiphysis (AP view) + **coronal** posterior metaphysis (lateral view) + **axial** physis; lateral commonest (AITFL, weakest lateral physis); medial rare (adduction) |

**Both** — occur during **partial physeal closure** (medial closes first)
