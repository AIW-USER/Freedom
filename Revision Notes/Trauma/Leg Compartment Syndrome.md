# Leg Compartment Syndrome

*Trauma · 36 / 67*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology & causes

**Commonest site** — **leg** · **anterior compartment** most often (TO)

**Fractures** — **69%** (Orthobullets) · **75%** (TO) · **tibial shaft = commonest cause** (TO) · **proximal tibia** commonest overall (other TO) · **plateau 12% vs shaft 3%**, **Schatzker VI** high risk (TO)

**Other causes** — crush, contusion, GSW, **tight casts / dressings**, IV extravasation, burns, post-ischaemic swelling, bleeding disorders / anticoagulants, arterial injury, osteotomy (**Blount's**), **lithotomy position**, snake bite, exercise

| Risk factor | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Young age** | **Highest 12–19 yr**, then 20–29 (more muscle, less room) |
| **Diaphyseal fracture** | More muscle than tendon |
| Open, **segmental / comminuted** | Higher energy (Orthobullets, OB) · open **not** associated (one OB item) — read the options |
| **Higher BMI (children)** | **Most significant paediatric factor** in OB |
| Paediatric | **Older (> 14 yr)**, male, MVC / motorbike, **ipsilateral fibula fracture**, IMN · **< 12 yr protective** |
| Polytrauma (TO) | ↑ lactate / base deficit, transfusion, coagulopathy (warfarin) |

**Not associated (OB)** — male sex, BMI ≥ 30 (adult), open fracture, nicotine

**Anterior compartment vulnerability (TO)** — **type I (slow-twitch, oxidative) fibres** → most sensitive to hypoxia

### Pathophysiology — venous outflow obstruction

**Cascade** — trauma → bleeding + oedema → ↑ interstitial pressure → **↓ venous outflow relative to arterial inflow** → myoneural ischaemia

**Fasciotomy effect** — **↑ venous outflow**\
✗ Wrong options: ↓ arterial inflow · ↑ interstitial pressure · ↓ perfusion · ↓ bleeding (may ↑ by losing tamponade)

**Irreversible damage** — within **6 h**

**Causes by mechanism (TO)** — **↓ compartment size** (tight cast / bandage, circumferential burn eschar) · **↑ compartment content** (haematoma, reperfusion / crush oedema, snake bite, bleeding disorder)

**Eaton & Green ischaemia–oedema cycle (TO)** — arterial spasm → ischaemia → ↑ capillary permeability → oedema → ↑ intramuscular pressure → more ischaemia

**Critical closing pressure theory (TO)** — **Laplace**: transmural pressure = wall tension ÷ radius → interstitial pressure > vessel wall tension → **capillary collapse**

**AV gradient theory (TO)** — flow ∝ **(Pa − Pv) ÷ R** → ↑ interstitial → ↑ venous pressure → ↓ AV gradient → ischaemia

### Compartment anatomy & nerve deficits

| Compartment | Muscles | Nerve / vessel | Deficit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Anterior** | TA, EHL, EDL, peroneus tertius | **Deep peroneal**, anterior tibial artery | **1st web space** numb, ↓ dorsiflexion |
| **Lateral** | Peroneus longus + brevis | **Superficial peroneal** (only nerve) | **Dorsum of foot — hallux, 3rd + 4th toes** |
| **Deep posterior** | **TP, FDL, FHL, popliteus** | **Tibial nerve**, posterior tibial artery | **Plantar foot numb + pain on passive toe EXTENSION** |
| Superficial posterior | Gastrocnemius, soleus, plantaris | Sural | Plantarflexion |

## 3 Presentation

### Clinical signs & risk factors (analgesia requirement, children, young age)

**Earliest / most important (TO)** — **pain out of proportion + pain on passive stretch**\
✗ Wrong options: tight compartment · ↑ pressure · pulse loss + paraesthesia

**Most sensitive before ischaemia** — **pain on passive stretch**

**Late** — **paralysis** (rare full recovery), **absent pulses** (amputation usually inevitable), pallor · **pulses and capillary refill intact in ACS** (TO)

**Early sensory (TO)** — ↓ 2-point discrimination / vibration

**Children** — **"3 A's": anxiety, agitation, increasing analgesic requirement** · **increasing analgesia = most sensitive** · **duration before fasciotomy = most important outcome variable** · **mechanism is NOT the best predictor**\
✗ Wrong options: 5 P's

**Pain unreliable** — polytrauma, **sedated (impossible)**, children, nerve injury

**After 5-h tibial nailing: pain on passive toe flexion, pulse present (TO)** — **operative decompression**\
✗ Wrong options: measure pressure · Doppler · dextran

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

**Radiographs** — rule out fracture only · **CTA / Doppler / ABI / duplex not diagnostic** (duplex = DVT)

### Diagnosis — pressure measurement & delta-P thresholds

### Diagnostic criteria

| Fasciotomy | Observe |
|---|---|
| **Δp (diastolic − compartment) ≤ 30 mmHg** (TO: **< 30 for > 2 h** if minimal signs) | **Δp > 30** + exam not consistent |
| Clinical diagnosis (no pressures needed) | — |
| Absolute **> 30–45 mmHg** (historic; **43% unnecessary fasciotomies** — McQueen) | — |

**Normal compartment pressure** — **< 10 mmHg** (TO)

**Most accurate** — **Δp < 30**\
✗ Wrong options: palpation · paraesthesia · absolute 25

**Intra-op** — anaesthesia ↓ diastolic → **use PRE-op diastolic** · e.g. **intra-op 29 with pre-op DBP 58 → concerning**\
✗ Wrong options: MAP

**Intra-op 28 mmHg, BP 84/57 (pre-op 132/84)** — **re-evaluate in recovery**\
✗ Wrong options: fasciotomy · pressors

**31–35 mmHg, BP 115/78, pain unchanged with stretch** — **support BP + serial exams**\
✗ Wrong options: fasciotomy · MRA · EMG

**Hypotensive polytrauma (80/50), pressures 21–25** — **Δp < 30 → immediate fasciotomy**

**Alert, stable pain, DBP 98, pressure 35 (Δp 63)** — **reduce + splint, serial exams**\
✗ Wrong options: fasciotomy · analgesia alone

| Technique point | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Site** | **Within 5 cm of fracture** (highest pressure; also centre of muscle belly) |
| **Interobserver reliability** | **Poor** |
| Anterior | 1 cm lateral to tibial crest |
| Deep posterior | Just posterior to medial tibial border → toward fibula |
| Lateral | Just anterior to posterior fibular border |
| Superficial posterior | Mid-calf |

| Device (TO) | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Whitesides needle** | **Mercury manometer + syringe + 3-way stopcock** · simplest ward method |
| **Wick / slit catheter** | Fluid-displacement pressure; continuous |
| **Stryker monitor** | Hand-held electronic, real-time |

**Pressure measurement indicated (TO)** — unconscious / polytrauma · **tourniquet palsy / peripheral nerve injury** (pain unreliable)

**Obtunded / intubated** — **measure pressures** · AAOS CPG: **repeated / continuous** monitoring\
✗ Wrong options: palpation · myoglobinuria (lactate / troponin better but inferior)

**Continuous monitoring (slit catheter, anterior)** — **most sensitive + specific in sedated**: sens 94%, spec 98%, PPV 93%, NPV 99%

**Child post-op: tense calf, cool foot, ↓ pulses, little pain** — **measure compartment pressures**\
✗ Wrong options: vascular consult · ABI · duplex

**Child after Blount osteotomies, sedated, passive toe pain** — **remove cast + measure pressures**\
✗ Wrong options: elevate · more analgesia · muscle relaxant

**Children** — measure **under sedation** if suspected · **haemophilia → Factor VIII before** measuring

## 5 Management

```
Suspected leg compartment syndrome
├─ Cast / dressing, no clear CS → bivalve + spread, split padding, ankle neutral–30° PF
├─ Clinically certain → emergent 4-compartment fasciotomy (+ stabilise fracture)
├─ Equivocal / obtunded → pressures → Δp ≤ 30 → fasciotomy
└─ Missed (late, established) → no fasciotomy
```

### Cast-related — ankle position & bivalving

**Safest ankle position in cast (TO)** — **neutral to 30° plantarflexion** · anterior lowest at neutral, deep posterior lowest in PF · resting PF \~37°\
✗ Wrong options: dorsiflexion

**Cast + increasing pain + toe numbness** — **bivalve + spread cast, remove padding**\
✗ Wrong options: elevate · morphine · ice · observe

**Already bivalved, ankle dorsiflexed, recurrent numbness** — **reposition ankle into slight plantarflexion**\
✗ Wrong options: fasciotomy · re-reduction

**Impending CS (TO)** — **limb at heart level** (elevation ↓ arterial inflow)

### Emergent fasciotomy with fracture stabilisation

**Tibial shaft fracture + CS at 6 h (TO)** — **fasciotomy then reamed IMN in same sitting** · reamed = unreamed ICP\
✗ Wrong options: unreamed · delay nail · ex-fix

**Sequence (TO)** — **fasciotomy first**, then stabilise; **cast contraindicated** with ACS; avoid excess traction, thigh-bar pressure, high elevation

**Severe pain despite narcotics, passive stretch pain** — **immediate 4-compartment fasciotomy + ex-fix**\
✗ Wrong options: 2-compartment · fix first

**Teen, knee injury, firm compartments, pressures 33–38 (BP 100/50)** — **emergent 4-compartment fasciotomy + ORIF**

### Fasciotomy technique (incisions, compartments, SPN risk)

| Technique | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Dual incision** — 2 × 15–18 cm, **8 cm skin bridge** | **Anterolateral**: protect **SPN**; anterior fasciotomy **1 cm in front of** septum, lateral **1 cm behind** · **Posteromedial**: protect **saphenous vein + nerve**; superficial posterior; **detach soleal bridge from tibia → deep posterior** |
| **Single lateral (perifibular)** | Fibular head → ankle; anterior + lateral, then superficial posterior; **deep posterior via interosseous membrane behind fibula** |

**Soleus elevated from tibia** — to reach **deep posterior** compartment

**Medial incision does NOT release** — **EHL (anterior)**\
✗ Wrong options: TP, FDL, popliteus, soleus

**Single lateral — nerve at risk proximally** — **superficial peroneal** (OB) · **common peroneal** (Orthobullets) — read the options

**Closure** — **delayed primary / STSG at 3–7 days** (Orthobullets) · 7–10 days (TO)

**Principles (TO)** — extensile incisions, release all involved compartments, protect vital structures, debride

**Contraindication** — **missed (established) compartment syndrome**

**Timing (TO)** — ideal **within 12 h** · **> 24 h usually contraindicated** (infection of necrotic muscle)

**Limited / subcutaneous fasciotomy (TO)** — **no role in acute** compartment syndrome

| Region (TO) | Fasciotomy |
|---|---|
| **Leg** | **Two-incision (Mubarak)** — anterolateral + posteromedial → all 4 |
| **Thigh** | **Lateral** incision (anterior + posterior) ± **medial** for adductors |
| **Arm** | Anterolateral; anterior compartment first |
| **Forearm** | Volar **lazy-S** (curved laterally → mobile wad) ± dorsal |
| **Foot** | **2 dorsal (over 2nd + 4th MT)** ± medial |

![Dual-incision fasciotomy](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob1001-illustration-double-incision-technique.jpg)

### Hyperbaric oxygen

**Mechanism** — **↑ oxygen diffusion gradient** (100% O₂ at ↑ pressure)

**Indications** — CO poisoning, **gas gangrene**, crush, compartment syndrome, **necrotising fasciitis**, chronic osteomyelitis, grafts / flaps, burns

## 6 Complications

### Late sequelae — contracture & tendon transfers

| Missed compartment | Deformity | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **Anterior + deep posterior** | Weak TA + TP → **PL overpowers → cavovarus** (flexible) | **Peroneus longus → dorsolateral midfoot** + Achilles lengthening |
| **Anterior + lateral** | **Foot drop**, flexible equinovarus (TP 5/5) | **PTT through IOM to TA + peroneus tertius (above ankle)** + anterior debridement + **TAL** (✗ Wrong options: PL transfer (weak) · fusion) |
| **Deep posterior** | **Clawing all toes**, equinocavovarus, no heel rise; equinus **not relieved by knee flexion** | **Posterior capsule release + TAL + excise scarred muscle / tendon** |

**Transfer rule** — transferred muscle **loses one grade** → needs **5/5**

**Equinus relieved by knee flexion** — gastrocnemius slide · not relieved → **Achilles lengthening**

## X vs Y

| | **Lateral compartment** | **Anterior compartment** | **Deep posterior** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nerve | **SPN** | **DPN** | **Tibial** |
| Sensory loss | **Dorsum (hallux, 3rd, 4th)** | **1st web** | **Plantar foot** |
| Stretch pain | Inversion | Plantarflexion of toes / ankle | **Toe extension** |
