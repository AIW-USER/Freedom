# Subtrochanteric Fractures

*Trauma · 59 / 67*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Definition** — proximal femur fracture **from the lesser trochanter to 5 cm distal** to it

**Incidence** — **7–34% of femur fractures** · bimodal: **young high energy (MVC)** · **elderly low energy (fall)**

**Rule out** — pathological / **atypical femur fracture** (bisphosphonate — esp. **alendronate**, **denosumab**)

### Deforming forces & reduction manoeuvres

| Fragment | Deformity | Muscle |
|---|---|---|
| **Proximal** | **Abduction** | **Gluteus medius + minimus** (greater trochanter) |
| **Proximal** | **Flexion** | **Iliopsoas** (lesser trochanter) |
| **Proximal** | **External rotation** | **Short external rotators** (piriformis, gemelli, obturator internus) ± iliopsoas, gluteus maximus |
| **Distal** | **Adduction + shortening** | **Adductors** |

**Typical proximal deformity** — **flexion, abduction, external rotation** · lesser trochanter intact → **abduction + flexion**\
✗ Wrong options: adduction · extension · internal rotation

**Responsible muscles (proximal displacement)** — **gluteus medius + iliopsoas**\
✗ Wrong options: gluteus maximus + adductors · rectus · hamstrings

**Reduction of proximal fragment** — **adduction + extension + internal rotation**\
✗ Wrong options: abduction · flexion

**Bring distal to proximal** — after traction, **flex + externally rotate the distal fragment**\
✗ Wrong options: traction alone

**Biomechanics** — weight bearing → **compression medial cortex, tension lateral cortex**

## 3 Presentation

**History** — long-term **bisphosphonate / denosumab** · **prodromal thigh / groin pain** before a low-energy fall

**Exam** — can't bear weight · **shortening + varus** deformity · flexed proximal fragment may threaten skin

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

**Views** — AP + lateral hip, AP pelvis, **full-length femur incl. knee** · traction views optional (comminution)

**Findings** — proximal fragment **flexed + abducted** · distal **adducted + ER**

**Bisphosphonate fracture** — **lateral cortical thickening ("beaking", "dreaded black line")** · ↑ diaphyseal cortical thickness · **transverse / short oblique** · **medial spike** · **no comminution** · varus remodelling

![Atypical femur fracture — lateral cortical thickening](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob1039-ap-hip.jpg)

| Type | Description | Image |

| Code | Pattern |
|---|---|
| 32-A3.1 | Simple, **transverse** |
| 32-B3.1 | Wedge, fragmented |
| 32-C1.1 | Complex, spiral |

### Diagnostic criteria — atypical femur fracture (ASBMR revised)

| Major (**4 of 5 needed**) | Minor (optional) |
|---|---|
| **No / minimal trauma** (standing-height fall or less) | Generalised ↑ diaphyseal cortical thickness |
| Starts at **lateral cortex**, **substantially transverse** (may go oblique medially) | **Prodromal groin / thigh pain** |
| **Noncomminuted** | **Bilateral** fractures |
| Complete: both cortices ± **medial spike** · incomplete: lateral cortex only | **Delayed healing** |
| **Localised periosteal / endosteal thickening of lateral cortex** | — |

**Excluded** — femoral neck, intertrochanteric with spiral subtrochanteric extension, pathological (tumour), periprosthetic

## 5 Management

```
Subtrochanteric fracture
├─ Non-ambulatory, unfit for surgery → observation + analgesia (limited role)
├─ Most → cephalomedullary IM nail (reamed, statically locked)
├─ Fixed-angle plate → femoral neck fracture, narrow canal, shaft deformity, preference
│      └─ 95° blade plate / condylar screw (not sliding hip screw)
└─ Symptomatic impending AFF (lateral cortical stress line) → prophylactic IM nail
```

### Implant choice — cephalomedullary nail vs plate

| Implant | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Cephalomedullary nail** | **Load-sharing**, preserves vascularity, **stronger in unstable patterns** · **reamed + statically locked → highest union** (✗ Wrong options: unreamed (reaming ↑ vascularity + union without ↑ pulmonary / transfusion risk)) |
| **95° blade plate / condylar screw** | **Load-bearing**; lateral approach (split / elevate vastus lateralis, **profunda perforators**) · **tension band** · devitalises fragments, weaker in comminution |
| **135° sliding hip screw** | **Contraindicated** → loss of fixation, **malunion**, distal fragment displaces **medially + proximally**, proximal rotates on single screw |

**CMN vs SHS** — CMN: **lower revision, less pain, better mobility, higher QoL + satisfaction** (EXCEPT; ✗ Wrong options: "higher pain")

**Failed 135° plate (reverse obliquity nonunion) — would have prevented** — **cephalomedullary nail**\
✗ Wrong options: longer plate · autograft · strut allograft · derotation screw

**Tension band principles** — **eccentric loading + implant on tensile side + opposite cortex resists compression** → **subtrochanteric femur**\
✗ Wrong options: comminuted tibia / humerus / olecranon distal to coronoid · valgus impacted proximal humerus

**Anterior distal femoral cortex perforation** — nail with **greater radius of curvature (straighter)** than femoral bow

### Nailing technique

| Position | Advantages |
|---|---|
| **Lateral** | **Easier reduction of distal to flexed proximal fragment**, easier entry (piriformis), vastus retraction |
| **Supine** | **Protects injured spine**, **access in polytrauma**, **easier fluoroscopy**, **easier to judge rotation**, shorter set-up |

**Lateral disadvantages** — harder imaging, rotation hard to judge, impractical in polytrauma · supine: harder start point

**Reduction** — **reduce before + during nail passage** (nail can't reduce) · percutaneous aids / **open clamp** · evaluate proximal fragment **before guidewire**

**Entry** — piriformis nail may avoid malreduction from the **valgus proximal bend** of trochanteric nails

### Atypical femoral fractures (bisphosphonate-associated)

**Cause** — **prolonged bisphosphonate (> 5 yr)** · low-energy fall · prodromal pain\
✗ Wrong options: acute trauma · metastasis · fibrous cortical defect · osteomyelitis

**Symptomatic lateral cortical thickening / "dreaded black line"** — **IM nail**\
✗ Wrong options: observe · physio · MRI · THA

**Bilateral changes, one side painful** — **nail the symptomatic side only** · **image the contralateral femur** in all\
✗ Wrong options: both · bone scan · oncology

**Progression of contralateral AFF** — **continued post-op bisphosphonate** · longer use (> 5 yr), **Asian race**, **varus proximal femur** → **stop bisphosphonates**\
✗ Wrong options: 2 yr · Caucasian · valgus · female sex

| Fixation of AFF | Risk |
|---|---|
| **IM nail** | **↑ iatrogenic fracture** (brittle, thick cortices; canal–nail mismatch) · **↑ nonunion → revision** |
| Plate | **↑ hardware failure** (varus, intramembranous healing inhibited) · no ↑ iatrogenic fracture |

**Main complication** — **delayed healing + nonunion** · **anatomic reduction = best predictor of union**\
✗ Wrong options: LLD · metastasis · femoral head AVN · occult neck fracture

**Teriparatide** — anabolic; **↑ serum calcium via ↑ bone resorption / turnover** (intermittent) · for non-union despite vit D, calcium, stopping bisphosphonate\
✗ Wrong options: vitamin D (gut uptake) · ATP analogues (non-nitrogen bisphosphonates) · mevalonate pathway (nitrogen bisphosphonates) · RANKL antibody (denosumab)

## 6 Complications

### Nonunion, malunion & infection

| Complication | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Varus / procurvatum (flexion) malreduction** | **Most frequent intra-op complication** of antegrade nailing — abductors + iliopsoas vs adductors; portal made with distal femur adducted |
| **Nonunion** | Lowest with **reamed statically locked IMN** · treat with plating (corrects varus) |
| AFF | Iatrogenic fracture, nonunion, hardware failure |

**Most common deformity after nailing — muscles** — **hip abductors + iliopsoas**\
✗ Wrong options: internal rotators · quadriceps · hamstrings

**Atrophic nonunion + varus collapse after CMN** — **remove hardware, correct alignment, plate (or nail) + autograft**\
✗ Wrong options: dynamisation (fails with deformity) · Taylor frame (poorly tolerated) · unreamed exchange nail (same path) · plate around nail

**Reamed exchange nailing** — choice for femoral nonunion **without deformity**

**Draining sinus, healed fracture, heterotopic bone (3 yr)** — **I&D + hardware removal + HO excision + culture-directed antibiotics**\
✗ Wrong options: disarticulation · suppression alone · radiation / NSAIDs

## X vs Y

| | **Typical subtrochanteric** | **Atypical (bisphosphonate)** |
|---|---|---|
| Patient | Young, high energy | Elderly, low energy, prodromal pain |
| Pattern | Comminuted, spiral | **Transverse, medial spike, no comminution** |
| Cortex | Normal | **Lateral thickening / beaking** |
| Nail risk | Varus malreduction | **Iatrogenic fracture, nonunion** |

## How it's asked

**"Proximal fragment deformity?"** → **flexion, abduction, external rotation**

**"Muscles displacing proximal fragment?"** → **gluteus medius + iliopsoas**

**"Muscles causing common post-nail deformity?"** → **hip abductors + iliopsoas**

**"Reduction force on proximal fragment?"** → **adduction + extension**

**"Intra-op manoeuvre (proximal, metastatic hx)?"** → **extension + internal rotation**

**"Distal fragment manipulation after traction?"** → **flexion + external rotation**

**"Supine advantages EXCEPT?"** → **easy reduction of distal to flexed proximal fragment**

**"Best treatment?"** → **cephalomedullary nail**

**"Young, comminuted — maximise union?"** → **reamed statically locked IMN**

**"CMN vs SHS EXCEPT?"** → **higher pain**

**"Failed 135° plate — prevention?"** → **cephalomedullary nail**

**"Tension band construct suitable?"** → **subtrochanteric femur**

**"Anterior cortex perforation cause?"** → **nail with greater radius of curvature**

**"Painful lateral cortical thickening?"** → **IM nail**

**"Prodromal pain then curb-step fracture?"** → **bisphosphonate**

**"AFF nailing vs conventional — true?"** → **↑ iatrogenic fracture with IMN**

**"One painful side, bilateral changes?"** → **IM nail symptomatic side**

**"Contralateral progression?"** → **post-op bisphosphonate use**

**"Primary AFF complication?"** → **delayed healing + nonunion**

**"Teriparatide mechanism?"** → **↑ serum calcium through ↑ bone resorption**

**"Atrophic nonunion with varus collapse?"** → **remove nail, correct, plate + autograft**

**"Draining sinus 3 yr after fixation?"** → **I&D, remove hardware, excise HO, culture antibiotics**
