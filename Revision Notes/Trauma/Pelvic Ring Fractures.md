# Pelvic Ring Fractures

*Trauma · 46 / 67*

## 1 Basics

### Associated injuries

**Orthopaedic** — chest up to **63%** · long bones **50%** · spine **25%**

**Non-orthopaedic** — head + abdomen **40%** · urogenital (**sexual dysfunction up to 50%**) · **> 50%** have other significant injuries

**Children (open triradiate)** — iliac wing weaker than ligaments → **rami + iliac wing** fractures, rarely surgical

### Anatomy

**Ring** — sacrum + 2 innominates; **displacement needs disruption in 2 places**

| Ligament | Resists |
|---|---|
| Symphyseal | External rotation |
| **Sacrospinous** | **External rotation** |
| **Sacrotuberous** | **Shear + flexion** (and ER) |
| **Posterior SI complex** | **Strongest in body**; **more important than anterior** |
| Anterior SI | ER after floor + anterior fail |
| Interosseous SI | AP translation |
| **Posterior SI** | **Cephalad-caudad displacement** |
| Iliolumbar | Rotation; augments posterior SI |

**Anterior structures** — \~**40%** of pelvic stability (OB)

**Vessels** — internal iliac dives posteriorly at SI joint → **posterior division → superior gluteal** · anterior → obturator · **posterior venous plexus = 80–90% of bleeding**

**Nerves** — **lumbosacral trunk crosses ala + SI joint**; **L5 root over sacral ala \~2 cm medial to SI joint**

### Corona mortis

**Anastomosis (TO)** — **external iliac (inferior epigastric / accessory obturator) ↔ internal iliac (obturator)** · **NOT internal pudendal**

**Location** — behind superior ramus, **3–7 cm** (TO) · mean **6.2 cm** (Orthobullets) from symphysis → risk in **ilioinguinal / modified Stoppa**

![Posterior sacroiliac ligaments](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob1030-shows-the-posterior-si-ligaments.jpg)

## 3 Presentation

**Exam** — gentle iliac crest rotation **once** (low sensitivity) · ER limb, LLD · **perineal / scrotal / labial / flank haematoma**, **Morel-Lavallée**

**Neuro** — **L5 + S1 commonest**; rectal tone; **10–15%** neuro injury

**Mandatory** — **vaginal + rectal exam** (occult open fracture) · **gross haematuria** commonest GU sign (M 21% vs F 8%)

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| View | Beam | Shows |
|---|---|---|
| **AP** | — | ATLS; asymmetry, rotation |
| **Inlet** | **\~40° caudad** (25–45°); **S1 overlaps S2** | **AP translation**, **internal / external rotation**, **SI widening**, **ala impaction** |
| **Outlet** | **\~40° cephalad** (up to 60°); **symphysis over S2** | **Vertical translation**, flexion / extension, **sacral foramina + fracture location** |
| **Lateral sacrum** | — | **Transverse sacral / U-type → spinopelvic dissociation** (kyphosis) |
| **Single-leg stance (flamingo)** | Alternating | **Chronic instability** — **vertical translation of pubis** |
| **CT** | Routine | Posterior ring, comminution, foramina, **sacral dysmorphism** |

**Instability signs** — **> 5 mm posterior SI displacement**, **posterior sacral gap**, **avulsions (ischial spine / tuberosity, sacrum, L5 TP)** · **lateral sacral avulsion = instability (APC)**

**Isolated rami fractures — next view (TO)** — answer key not provided by the source; inlet / outlet views complete the pelvic series

**Labs** — **Hb, lactate, base excess**

![Inlet view technique](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob1030-shows-how-to-perform-inlet-xray.jpg)

### Occult instability — stress exam, single-leg stance & lateral views

**Binder + early CT** — **underestimate severity** → **fluoroscopic stress exam under anaesthesia (EUA)**\
✗ Wrong options: remove binder in unstable patient · thinner CT cuts · Judet

**Binder on, CT "normal" but retroperitoneal haematoma** — **keep binder; EUA in OR**

**APC-I (< 2.5 cm), EUA + CT normal** — **protected WB + pain control**

### Classifications

| Tile | Description |
|---|---|
| **A — stable** | A1 avulsion / iliac wing · A2 stable ring · **A3 transverse sacral (Denis III)** |
| **B — rotationally unstable, vertically stable** | **B1 open book (ER)** · **B2 LC (IR)** (B2-1 ipsilateral rami, **B2-2 contralateral = bucket handle**) · B3 bilateral |
| **C — rotationally + vertically unstable** | C1 unilateral (iliac / SI / sacral) · C2 one B + one C · C3 bilateral C |

| Young-Burgess | Injury |
|---|---|
| **APC I** | Symphysis **< 2.5 cm** |
| **APC II** | Symphysis **> 2.5 cm**, **anterior SI open**, **sacrospinous + sacrotuberous torn**, **posterior SI INTACT** |
| **APC III** | **Anterior + posterior SI torn (SI dislocation)** |
| **LC I** | **Oblique / transverse ramus + ipsilateral anterior sacral ala compression** |
| **LC II** | Rami + **ipsilateral posterior ilium crescent fracture-dislocation** |
| **LC III** | Ipsilateral LC + **contralateral APC (windswept)** — rollover, pedestrian |
| **Vertical shear** | Posterior + superior force; **highest hypovolaemic shock (63%)**, mortality up to 25% |

**Tilt fracture** (displaced superior ramus, women) — **lateral compression**; vaginal exam for open fracture

**Sacrospinous + sacrotuberous torn** — **APC II**\
✗ Wrong options: APC I · LC II (protected by IR)

## 5 Management

```
Unstable pelvis + haemodynamic instability
├─ Binder / sheet over greater trochanters + 1:1:1 transfusion
├─ Positive FAST / laparotomy → anterior ex-fix (± pelvic packing)
├─ Still unstable → angiography + embolisation (or packing — institutional)
│      └─ still bleeding → repeat angiogram (new source 68%)
└─ Resuscitated (lactate ≤ 4, pH ≥ 7.25, BE ±5.5) → definitive fixation
```

### Haemorrhage control — pelvic binder / sheet & placement

**First step in unstable patient** — **pelvic binder / sheet**\
✗ Wrong options: C-spine film · ex-fix in OR · angiography · packing · IS screws · inlet / outlet first · CT first

**Centre over** — **greater trochanters** · + IR of legs, tape ankles\
✗ Wrong options: iliac crest / ASIS / umbilicus (ineffective, blocks abdomen)

**Force** — **180 ± 50 N** to stabilise symphysis diastasis (Bottlang)

**Controls** — **retroperitoneal venous bleeding** (holds **up to 4 L**)

**Cautions** — skin necrosis (brief "binder holiday" 1–2 h after 24 h) · hypothetical LC over-rotation / bladder injury (no clinical evidence) · **bladder injury is NOT a contraindication**

**Iliac wing fracture + superior migration** — **skeletal traction**\
✗ Wrong options: binder (worsens) · ex-fix

**Vertical shear** — **skeletal traction + binder**

![Pelvic sheet application over the greater trochanters](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob1030-shows-technique-for-applying-pelvic-antishock-sheet.jpg)

### Haemorrhage — angiography, embolisation, packing & arterial sources

**Sources** — **venous 80% (Orthobullets) · 90% (OB)**, cancellous bone · **arterial 10–20%** · intra-abdominal up to 40%

| Pattern | Artery |
|---|---|
| **APC (posterior ring)** | **Superior gluteal** |
| **LC (anterior ring)** | **Internal pudendal**, **obturator** |

**Commonest embolised** — **internal iliac** + branches > superior gluteal > obturator > internal pudendal

**Laparotomy needed** — **ex-fix** (stabilises clot, aids laparotomy)\
✗ Wrong options: internal fixation (contamination)

**Ex-fix benefit** — **maintains stable clot**\
✗ Wrong options: rigid fixation (long lever arm)

**Packing** — **ex-fix first**, then **6–8 cm subumbilical midline**, 3 lap pads each side **below brim** (SI joint → anterior → retropubic)

**Class III shock despite binder + 1:1:1, FAST negative** — **emergent angiography**

**Persistent bleeding after SGA embolisation** — repeat angiogram → **new source (68%)** > re-bleed (18%) > both (14%)

**Pudendal bleed after splenectomy, lactate > 5** — **IR embolisation** before definitive fixation

**CTA** — **NPV 98–100%** for arterial bleeding

**Embolisation complications** — **gluteal necrosis, impotence**, ↑ infection (**bilateral nonselective internal iliac**)

### Massive transfusion ratio

**PRBC : FFP : platelets = 1 : 1 : 1** (↓ mortality); coagulopathy hardest to correct

### External fixator pins — iliac crest vs supra-acetabular

| | **Supra-acetabular (AIIS)** | **Iliac crest** |
|---|---|---|
| Purchase | **Superior bone (biomechanically better)** | Thin pillar (gluteal pillar) |
| Fluoroscopy | **Needed**: **obturator outlet** (entry, teardrop), **iliac oblique** (above sciatic notch), **obturator inlet** (between tables) | **Less reliance** — fast |
| Future incisions | **Doesn't interfere** with ilioinguinal / lateral window | Interferes |
| Risk | **LFCN**, hip capsule (**start ≥ 2 cm above joint**) | — |
| Pin infection | Equal | Equal |

**Neither** gives definitive posterior stability

**Ex-fix indications** — ER patterns (**APC, VS**), ongoing bleeding, **before laparotomy** · not for ilium / acetabular fracture

### Anterior fixation nerve complications (LFCN, femoral nerve)

**Supra-acetabular pin / INFIX** — **LFCN (lateral thigh numbness) — commonest**

**INFIX too deep** — **femoral nerve palsy** (weak knee extension, **medial thigh + leg numbness**) · also HO, infection

### Iliosacral & transsacral screws — fluoroscopy, L5 root injury & sacral dysmorphism

| View | Guides |
|---|---|
| **Outlet** | **Superior-inferior** |
| **Inlet** | **Anterior-posterior** |
| **Lateral sacral** | **Iliac cortical density** (anterior safe limit) → avoids **L5** |

**Errant (anterior) screw** — **L5 root → weak great toe extension**\
✗ Wrong options: knee extension L4 · plantarflexion S1

**AP breach (TO)** — answer key not provided by the source; **inlet** shows anterior-posterior position (Orthobullets)

**Transiliac screw start** — posterior iliac crest at S1 level, behind sacrum (lateral view)

**Sacral dysmorphism (20–40%)** — **up-sloping ala**, **irregular / elliptical foramina**, **residual S1 disc**, **mamillary processes**, **tongue-and-groove SI** · **S1 corridor smaller + oblique**, **S2 corridor LARGER** → transiliac-transsacral at S2 · NOT unique: "S1 narrower than S2" · not a contraindication

**Anterior sacral retractors (Stoppa / lateral window)** — **L4 + L5** roots

### Fixation strategy & failure (vertical sacral fracture, symphyseal plates, sequence)

| Injury | Definitive treatment |
|---|---|
| **APC I** | Nonop, protected WB |
| **APC II** | Symphyseal plate / ex-fix **± posterior** |
| **APC III** | **Multi-hole symphyseal plate + SI screws** / posterior plate |
| **LC I** | Mostly nonop (**WBAT incomplete**; protected if complete / comminuted) |
| **LC II** | **ORIF ilium (crescent)** |
| **LC III / VS** | Posterior plate / SI screws |

**APC III stable after embolisation** — **multi-hole non-locking symphyseal plate + IS screw**\
✗ Wrong options: 2-hole · ex-fix definitive · locked plate + sacral plate

**Multi-hole vs 2-hole** — **lower failure + malunion**; reoperation equal · **locked = non-locked** stability

**APC II anterior + posterior** — **↓ anterior plate failure + malunion** (no better outcome)

**Most stable for VS** — **IS screw + anterior internal fixation**

**Highest loss of reduction** — **vertical sacral fracture**\
✗ Wrong options: anterior fixation type · screw length · number · age

**Anterior approach to SI — relative contraindications** — **comminuted sacral fracture** (L5 at risk), morbid obesity, iliac crest ex-fix, colostomy

**Ipsilateral pelvic ring + acetabulum** — **fix ring (SI joint) first**

**Subacute malunion (6 wk), flexed / shortened / ER** — **anterior ORIF + posterior fixation**

**S2 alar-iliac vs iliac screws (TO)** — alar: deeper start (15 mm, less prominence), no connector, **MORE SI joint violation** (incorrect: "less violation")

### Operative criteria

| Operative | Nonoperative |
|---|---|
| **Symphysis > 2.5 cm** | **APC I < 2.5 cm**, intact posterior ring |
| **SI displacement > 1 cm** | **LC I** (< 1 cm posterior displacement) |
| **Sacral displacement > 1 cm** | Isolated rami |
| Rotated / displaced hemipelvis | **Peripartum diastasis < 4 cm** (bed rest + binder) |
| Open fracture | Children with open triradiate (most) |
| Peripartum **> 4–6 cm** / chronic pain | — |

**Open pelvic fracture** — aggressive debridement; **diverting colostomy** (perineal / rectal)

### Stable injuries — nonoperative management (LC1, children, peripartum, elderly)

**Peripartum diastasis (2 wk post-delivery)** — **protected WB + binder + observation**\
✗ Wrong options: ex-fix · plate · symphysiotomy

**LC I + contralateral pilon** — **WBAT pelvis side, NWB pilon side**

**Child 7 yr, parasymphyseal, posterior stable** — **WBAT** · surgery if acetabular / triradiate displacement **> 2 mm**; ex-fix if ring **> 2 cm**

**Elderly LC I (incomplete sacral buckle)** — **bilateral WBAT**\
✗ Wrong options: TDWB · screws

**AP I in cowgirl** — **nonop + gradual WBAT**\
✗ Wrong options: binder long-term · ORIF

**LC incomplete sacral, 56-yr** — **immediate WBAT** (screws if pain prevents mobilising or complete / comminuted)

**LC1 surgery** — **↓ short-term pain** (3–12 months) but **↑ narcotics post-op**, not complication-free

**Rehab after fixation** — weight-of-limb on posterior injury side, full WB other side; bilateral posterior → bed-to-chair

## 6 Complications

### Urological injury, sexual dysfunction & childbirth

**GU injury** — **12–20%** (males 21%) · \~15% (OB)

**Commonest urological** — **posterior urethral tear** > bladder rupture (mortality 22–34%)

**Urethral injury signs** — **blood at meatus**, **high-riding mobile prostate**, haematuria, can't void → **retrograde urethrogram (RUG)**\
✗ Wrong options: cystogram · urinalysis · CT · DPL

**Site (TO)** — **membranous urethra** (classic); commonest adult site distal to membranous; children more proximal

**Management** — **suprapubic catheter** (relative contraindication to symphyseal plating) · repair rupture before / with fixation

**Long-term GU** — **urethral stricture (commonest)**, impotence, incontinence, infection

**APC / saddle-horn (horse) injury** — **sexual dysfunction = commonest long-term sequela** (18 / 20); **males up to 90%**, **dyspareunia 60%** females · **35–56%** overall

**Women** — **dyspareunia + caesarean section** ↑ · dyspareunia risk: diastasis, bladder disruption, symphyseal plating, residual > 5 mm

### Mortality predictors & causes of death

**Mortality** — **1–15% closed** · **up to 50% open** · isolated pelvic ring **< 15%** · shock: **25–52%**

| Pattern | Mortality / cause |
|---|---|
| **APC III** | **Highest mortality (\~37%)**, blood loss, **transfusion** · APC overall 26% |
| **LC** | \~13%; **closed head injury = commonest cause of death** |
| VS | 25% |
| CMI | 17.1% |
| Acetabulum | \~1.5% |

**Best predictor** — **shock on presentation** · **transfusion in first 24 h** · also **age > 60**, SBP < 90, ISS / RTS, **> 4 units**, APC III

**APC deaths** — **pelvic + visceral injury / haemorrhage**

### Postoperative infection risk (obesity, embolisation)

**Strongest predictor** — **obesity (↑ BMI)** · also DM, long op, long ICU, **transfusion volume**, GU / abdominal injury, **Morel-Lavallée**, open fracture, **bilateral internal iliac embolisation**\
✗ Wrong options: angiography alone · venous injury · ex-fix · early fixation · head injury (HO)

| Other | Fact |
|---|---|
| **DVT / PE** | DVT **\~60%**, PE \~27%, fatal 2% → LMWH; **IVC filter if head injury** |
| Chronic instability | Rare → flamingo views |
| L5 injury | Anterior SI screw |
| **Poor outcome** | **SI incongruity > 1 cm**, **LLD > 2 cm**, malunion, nonunion, neuro / urethral injury |
