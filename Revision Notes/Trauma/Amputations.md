# Amputations

*Trauma · 3 / 67*

## 1 Basics

### Goals & epidemiology

**Goals** — preserve **functional length** + **useful sensibility** · prevent **symptomatic neuroma** + **adjacent joint contracture** · **early prosthetic fitting** · early return to work

**Prevalence** — **1.7 million** amputees in USA · **80% for vascular insufficiency**

**Commonest cause** — **upper limb = trauma** · **lower limb = vascular disease**

### Wound healing predictors & level selection

| Predictor | Threshold for healing |
|---|---|
| **Serum albumin** | **> 3.0 g/dL** — **best lab predictor** (✗ Wrong options: total protein · Ca · CRP · ESR) |
| **Total lymphocyte count** | **> 1500 /mm³** |
| **ABI** | **> 0.45** |
| **Ischaemic index** | **> 0.5** (Doppler pressure at level ÷ brachial systolic) |
| **TcPO₂** | **> 30 mm Hg** (ideally **45**) |
| **Toe pressure** | **> 40 mm Hg**; **will not heal < 20** |

**TcPO₂ cut-offs** — **> 30** healing (Orthobullets, OB) · **> 40** normal healing, **< 20 → amputation is end result** (TO) — read the options

**Wound healing depends on** — **vascular supply + nutrition + immune status**

### Hyperbaric oxygen

**Indication** — **crush injury / acute open fracture** (✓ NOT a contraindication)

| HBO contraindication | Why |
|---|---|
| **Chemo (bleomycin) / radiation** | Bleomycin + O₂ → **life-threatening pneumonitis** |
| **Pressure-sensitive implanted device** | **AICD, pacemaker, dorsal column stimulator, insulin pump** → malfunction |
| **Undrained pneumothorax** | Gas embolism, tension PTX, pneumomediastinum |
| **COPD** | Air trapping / blebs → PTX |

### Energy expenditure by amputation level

| Level | ↑ Energy over baseline |
|---|---|
| **Syme** | **15%** |
| **Transtibial — traumatic** | **25%** (long BKA 10%, short BKA 40%) |
| **Transtibial — vascular** | **40%** |
| **Bilateral transtibial** | **40%** (41%, OB) |
| **Through-knee** | Between BKA and AKA (habitus dependent) |
| **Transfemoral — traumatic** | **68%** (Orthobullets) · **65%** (Miller, OB) |
| **Transfemoral — vascular** | **100%** |
| AKA + BKA | 118% |
| AKA + AKA | > 200% |

**Rule** — energy ↑ with **more proximal** level; inversely proportional to residual length → amputate at **lowest possible level**

**Exception** — **Syme more efficient than midfoot** despite being more proximal

**Vascular > traumatic** at the same level

**Unilateral AKA > bilateral BKA** in energy cost

**Walking speed, fastest → slowest** — trauma BKA → vascular Syme → trauma AKA → vascular BKA → **vascular AKA (slowest, highest cost)**

**Children** — **through-knee = most proximal level keeping normal walking speed** without ↑ energy\
✗ Wrong options: AKA · BKA · Chopart · Girdlestone

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Stump closure techniques (TO)

| Technique (TO) | Description | Key fact |
|---|---|---|
| **Myofascial closure** | Fascial envelope over muscles | Minimal stabilisation; used in **severe ischaemia** |
| **Myoplasty** | Muscle sewn to **opposing muscle** over bone end | **Bursa, crepitus, pain** → not recommended alone |
| **Myodesis** | Muscle attached **directly to bone / periosteum** | Recommended; **contraindicated in severe ischaemia** (wound breakdown) |
| **Tenodesis** | Tendon fixed to bone | **Most secure + most physiologic**; **used in disarticulations** (knee: patellar tendon → cruciate origin) |

### Landmine / blast zones (TO)

| Zone (TO) | Injury |
|---|---|
| **Zone I** | Nearest blast: traumatic amputation, widespread destruction + contamination → **nonviable** |
| **Zone II** | Focal muscle micro-lacerations + haemorrhage along NV bundles / fascial planes |
| **Zone III** | Arteriole avulsion, impaired venous return, axonal reactive change |

**Amputation level after landmine (TO)** — **border of zone II and III** (muscle contracts, minimal oedema)

## 5 Management

### Upper-extremity amputation

**Indications** — irreparable blood supply loss, severe soft-tissue loss, malignant tumour, smouldering infection, congenital

**Upper vs lower** — mangled **UE has far greater functional impact** than LE amputation; nerve repair **better in UE**; **replantation > UE amputation** for function (worse more proximally, esp. around elbow)

**UE salvage vs amputation (civilian + METALS)** — **no significant difference** in function or psychological outcome; **no broadly accepted indications**; **MESS is a poor predictor in UE** (MESI more reliable)\
✗ Wrong options: blast = contraindication to salvage

| Level | Key facts |
|---|---|
| **Transcarpal** | Divide finger flexors / extensors; **anchor wrist motors to carpus** |
| **Wrist disarticulation** | **Longer lever arm + better pronation / supination**; **preserve radial styloid flare** (suspension); needs intact **DRUJ**; **children — preserves distal radial + ulnar physes**; harder to fit high-function prosthesis |
| **Transradial** | **Middle third ideal**; better cosmesis; **easier myoelectric fit**; **≥ 5 cm ulna** needed for elbow motion (little pronosupination) |
| **Transhumeral** | Max length; ideal **4–5 cm above elbow**; ≥ **5–7 cm** residual for GH mechanics |
| **Elbow disarticulation** | **Children** — avoids **bony overgrowth** of transhumeral |
| **Shoulder disarticulation** | **Retain humeral head** for contour → **shoulder hiking** (unopposed girdle muscles) · child → **scoliosis** |

**Technique** — identify + preserve **named motor + sensory branches** (myoelectric signal, TMR) · **myodesis** recommended

**Return to work after UE amputation** — **offer psychological counselling**\
✗ Wrong options: FCE · wait for final prosthesis · full elbow ROM

### Targeted muscle reinnervation (TMR)

**Concept** — transfer amputated major nerves into **expendable remaining muscles** → new discrete **myoelectric signal**

**Proven for** — **post-amputation neuroma pain** + better **myoelectric prosthesis control**\
✗ Wrong options: brachial plexus neurapraxia · Parsonage-Turner · SCI · CTS

### Transfemoral – adductor myodesis

**Level** — ideal cut **\~12 cm (10–15 cm) above knee** (room for prosthetic knee; resect condylar flare)

**Alignment** — **5–10° adduction** ideal

**Adductor myodesis** — **adductor magnus → drill holes at distal femur end** (Gottschalk) → **most important step**

**Benefits** — **dynamic muscle balance** (vs unopposed abductors + flexors) · **soft-tissue envelope / cushion** · **better prosthetic (suction) fit** · efficient femur position

**NOT a benefit** — **preserving femoral length** (extra femur is actually resected) (EXCEPT answer)

**Missed adductor myodesis** — **femur drifts into abduction / subcutaneous lateral thigh** → side lurch, ↑ energy, persists despite good prosthesis\
✗ Wrong options: flap length · failure to bevel · abductor myodesis

**Transfemoral flaps** — usually **equal anterior + posterior**

**Gritti-Stokes** — through femur at **adductor tubercle**; **excise synovium**; **patella arthrodesed to femur end** (end-bearing); better outcomes than transfemoral

### Osseointegration

**Concept** — implant in residual bone → **transcutaneous stoma** → prosthesis (transfemoral, transtibial)

**Benefits** — ↑ prosthesis wear, mobility, QoL, **proprioception** vs socket

**Commonest complication** — **low-grade soft-tissue infection**, managed without surgery (oral antibiotics)\
✗ Wrong options: failed integration · deep infection · peri-implant fracture

### Knee disarticulation vs other levels

**Indications** — ambulatory patients who **cannot have BKA** · **non-ambulatory** patients

**Technique** — **patellar tendon sutured to cruciates** in notch (**tenodesis**) · gastrocnemius padding

| vs **Transfemoral** | vs **Transtibial (LEAP)** |
|---|---|
| **Less energy** | **Slower** walking speed |
| **End-bearing** limb, proprioception, sitting, lever arm | **Worse SIP** (worst function at 2 yr of all levels) |
| ↓ hip flexion contracture, thigh muscles preserved | Physicians **less satisfied** (cosmesis, function — lost gastrocnemius padding) |
| Children — **no overgrowth** | **More dependence in transfers** |
| **Asymmetry of prosthetic knee** (sitting + gait), limited knee components, cosmesis | **Decreased prosthesis use** |
| No difference in prosthesis fitting | Pain similar to AKA + BKA |

**Through-knee in zone of injury** — **poorest outcome** of LE amputations; AKA ≈ BKA outcome (BKA faster); gender, prosthesis sophistication, muscle anchoring **don't affect outcome**

### Transtibial – flaps

**Level** — **12–15 cm below joint** (10–16 cm residual tibia); more distal → Achilles region (poor blood supply, padding); need **8–12 cm from ground** for modern prostheses

**Flaps (TO)** — **non-ischaemic → equal anterior + posterior flaps** · **ischaemic → long posterior flap** (better vascularity)\
✗ Wrong options: long anterior flap

**Dog ears** — leave in place in long posterior flap → **preserve saphenous + sural arteries** (posterior flap supply)\
✗ Wrong options: ATA · PTA · peroneal

**Fish-mouth (equal coronal) flaps in diabetics / PVD** — ↑ **anterior flap necrosis** (Manoli)

**Burgess (modified)** — standard **long posterior flap**; fibula cut **1–2 cm shorter** than tibia; gastrocnemius fascial closure

### Ertl bone bridge & osteomyoplasty

**Ertl** — **tibia–fibula bone bridge** (original = corticoperiosteal flap; **modified = fibular strut**) → **stable platform for load transfer / end-bearing**; prevents **"chopsticking"**

| Modified Ertl vs Burgess | Fact |
|---|---|
| Op + tourniquet time | **Longer** |
| Reoperation | **Higher** (hardware, **nonunion**, **bone-bridge dislocation**) |
| Wound / deep infection | Equivalent |
| Flaps | Any tension-free flap (posterior, skew, fish-mouth, medial, sagittal) |
| Function | **No definitive improvement** (small retrospective series; TAOS pending) |
| Early fitting / immediate WB | **Contraindicated** (needs union) |

**Osteomyoplasty vs myodesis** — **enhanced end-bearing** · fibular abduction = complication of **myodesis** (syndesmotic instability)\
✗ Wrong options: early fitting · immediate WB · less morbidity

**✗ Wrong option: fibula-pro-tibia** — not an amputation; fibular centralisation for tibial defects

### Syme amputation – heel pad & contraindications

**Syme = ankle disarticulation**\
✗ Wrong options: "hindfoot amputation"

**Requirements** — **patent posterior tibial artery** + **stable, viable heel pad (most important)**

**Contraindication** — **ABI < 0.5 in PT artery** (diabetic)\
✗ Wrong options: albumin 3.4 · osteomyelitis unless hindfoot / tibia · TcPO₂ 45 · neuropathy

**Technique** — malleoli removed **flush with tibial plafond** + flares bevelled · heel pad secured to anterior tibia · **tibialis anterior + EDL tenodesed to anterior heel pad** (stop posterior migration)

**Problems** — **skin slough** (vascular) + **heel-pad migration / hypermobility**

**Uses** — forefoot gangrene in diabetics; tumour, trauma · equinus is **not** its complication

### Hindfoot & partial-foot amputation

| Amputation | Level | Deformity / key fact |
|---|---|---|
| **Pirogoff** | Remove forefoot + talus → **calcaneotibial arthrodesis**; calcaneus rotated **50–90°** | Walk without prosthesis |
| **Chopart / Boyd** | **Talonavicular + calcaneocuboid** | **Equinus (equinovarus)** → **TA lengthening / tenotomy + tibialis anterior → talar neck** |
| **Lisfranc** | **Tarsometatarsal** (2nd MT osteotomised to keep medial cuneiform stable) | **Equinovarus** — unopposed **gastroc-soleus + tibialis posterior** → keep **peroneus brevis** insertion (5th MT base) + TA lengthening; walking cast 4 wk |
| **Transmetatarsal** | MT shafts | **Almost all need TA lengthening**; appeals to patients refusing BKA; **rocker-sole shoe + toe filler + carbon plate** |
| **Great toe** | Keep **1 cm of proximal phalanx base** | Preserves **plantar fascia, sesamoids, FHB** → ↓ transfer ulcers |

**Chopart (TO)** — **forefoot gangrene to navicular** in smoker → **Chopart**: preserves heel support, shoe + AFO, little shortening, heel-skin end-bearing, muscle balancing possible (tibialis anterior transfer + TA lengthening) — easier than Syme / Pirogoff

**Tibialis anterior** — innervated by **deep peroneal nerve**

**Not Chopart prevention** — posterior capsule release · TA → cuboid (cuboid removed) · FHL / peroneus brevis → calcaneus · Achilles shortening

**Lisfranc supination prevention** — **preserve PB, peroneus tertius + plantar fascia around 5th MT base**\
✗ Wrong options: release tib post · TA → cuneiforms · Achilles lengthening alone

**Lisfranc varus** — TA + tib post overpower peroneus brevis · equinus from loss of **EDL + EHL** · fixes: TAL / gastroc recession, **STATT**, 4th–5th flexor-to-extensor tenodesis, PB → PL

**Lisfranc energy** — ≈ BKA

**Soft-tissue balancing required** — **Lisfranc**\
✗ Wrong options: Gritti-Stokes · transtibial · TMA · knee disarticulation

**Commonest deformity after midfoot amputation** — **equinovarus**

**Only partial-foot amputation functional in a shoe** — **transmetatarsal** (others need ankle-controlling prosthesis)

### Hindquarter amputation (TO)

**Standard hemipelvectomy (TO)** — **posterior (gluteal) flap**; **disarticulates symphysis pubis + SI joint** + limb

**Anterior flap hemipelvectomy (TO)** — buttock / posterior thigh lesions; **quadriceps myocutaneous flap** on **superficial femoral vessels**

**Extended hemipelvectomy (TO)** — + sacrum / lumbar spine

**Mortality (TO)** — once > 50% → now **< 10%**

### Limb salvage vs amputation – decision factors

**Highest impact on decision (LEAP)** — **severity of soft-tissue injury**\
✗ Wrong options: reduction quality · smoking · insurance · time to debridement

**2nd highest** — **absent plantar sensation** — **not an absolute contraindication**; often recovers

**High late-amputation risk** — high-energy foot trauma with **1st MT fracture** or **all 5 metatarsals** (up to 20% at 1 yr)\
✗ Wrong options: CRPS commonest

**Late amputation after salvage associated with** — **fracture nonunion**, LE periarticular fracture, limb dysfunction, chronic pain\
✗ Wrong options: UE injury (lower rate) · nerve injury · flap failure · diaphyseal

### Limb salvage vs amputation – outcomes

| Study | Finding |
|---|---|
| **LEAP (civilian)** | **SIP + return to work not different at 2 yr**; \~**50% return to work**; infection 25%; salvage → longer to full WB + more rehospitalisation |
| **LEAP — mangled foot / ankle with free flap or ankle fusion** | **Worse (higher) SIP than BKA** (✗ Wrong options: faster walk · more RTW) |
| **METALS (military)** | Amputation → slightly better PROs, **lower PTSD**, **more return to vigorous sport / activity**; depression, pain, RTW same |

**Strongest predictor of patient satisfaction / PROs after LE amputation** — **ability to return to work** (+ no depression, faster walking, less pain)\
✗ Wrong options: age · gender · graft · plantar sensation · amputation vs salvage · cosmesis · counselling

**Higher SIP (Sickness Impact Profile)** — = **more disability**

**Psychological counselling** — improves outcomes (rehab team incl. psychiatrist, social worker)

## 6 Complications

| Complication | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Wound healing** | See predictors |
| **Contracture** | Adjacent joints → early mobilisation, positioning |
| **HO** | More common in **trauma** amputations |
| **Infection** | **\~34%** in trauma amputations |
| **Neuroma** | **20–30%**; prevent with nerve handling; treat with **TMR** |
| **Phantom limb pain** | **53–100%** of traumatic amputations → **mirror therapy** |
| **Terminal bone overgrowth** | **Commonest paediatric complication** (up to 50% of transosseous) |

### Paediatric amputation – terminal bone overgrowth

**Mechanism** — disorganised **appositional growth** at cut bone end (periosteal, endosteal, heterotopic) → **tapering spike** → skin ulceration, pain

**Prevention** — **disarticulation** (articular-cartilage-covered end never overgrows) · **epiphyseal / osteochondral cap**

**Treatment** — **revision with bone-end capping or Ertl**\
✗ Wrong options: HO + NSAIDs · neuroma + TMR · remodelling · osteomyelitis

**Knee disarticulation in child avoids** — **bone overgrowth**; costs proximal tibial physis → shorter limb\
✗ Wrong options: neurogenic pain · contractures

![Terminal bone overgrowth after paediatric transosseous amputation](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob1052-overgrowth.jpg)

### Phantom & neuroma pain

**Mirror therapy** — for **phantom pain**: visual feedback corrects proprioceptive mismatch\
✗ Wrong options: proprioception training · flexor tendon · plumbism · ACL
