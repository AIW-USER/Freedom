# Proximal Humerus Fractures

*Trauma · 48 / 67*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Incidence** — **4–6% of all fractures** · **3rd commonest non-vertebral fracture > 65 yr** · **F:M 2:1**

**Commonest pattern** — **2-part surgical neck** · \~80–85% minimally displaced

**Risk factors** — osteoporosis, diabetes, **epilepsy**, female · complexity ↑ with age

**Mechanism** — elderly low-energy fall (90% in > 60) · young high-energy

**Associated** — **axillary nerve (commonest)**; arterial injury **5–6%** (older, surgical neck, subcoracoid dislocation; collaterals mask pulses)

### Anatomy

**Anatomic neck** = old physis · **surgical neck** fractures more often · neck-shaft **135°**

| Deforming force | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Pectoralis major** | Shaft **anterior + medial** |
| Deltoid | Proximal fragment lateral (abducts — axillary nerve, **posterior cord**) |
| **SS, IS, TM** | **GT superior + posterior (ER)** |
| **Subscapularis** | **IR articular segment / LT** |

**Ligaments** — SGHL: inferior translation at 0° abduction · **MGHL: AP at \~45°** · **IGHL: AP at 90°**

**Head geometry (TO)** — articular arc **160°** · radius of curvature **~25 mm** (23–29) · **head–tuberosity distance (HTD) 8–10 mm** · head height **18 mm** (¾ of radius) · NSA **135°** (valgus tolerated, **varus poorly tolerated**) · retroversion **20° (10–40°)** · bicipital groove **~7 mm anterior to medullary axis** = retroversion landmark

**Mechanisms (TO)** — glenoid compression of head · bending at surgical neck · cuff tension → tuberosity avulsion · bimodal: young males high-energy, older females osteoporotic · **3rd commonest non-vertebral osteoporotic fracture**

### Humeral head vascularity & ischaemia predictors (Hertel criteria)

**Main blood supply (TO)** — **posterior humeral circumflex artery (PCHA) — 64%** (Hettrich)\
✗ Wrong options: ACHA (old view) · axillary · profunda

**ACHA** — **anterolateral branch → arcuate artery** (main GT supply); 1990 study: supplies whole head except posteroinferior GT + head · **disrupted in \~80%** of fractures yet AVN infrequent → PCHA dominant

**PCHA** — through **quadrilateral space** with axillary nerve

**First site of osteonecrosis (TO)** — **superomedial** head (poorly vascularised)

| Hertel predictor | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Calcar (posteromedial metaphyseal extension) < 8 mm** | **Most predictive** |
| **Disrupted medial hinge (> 2 mm)** | 2nd |
| Anatomic neck fracture | Independent predictor |
| Angulation > 45° (> 30° in one OB item) | 3rd |
| Head split | Least of these |
| Displacement > 10 mm, complexity, medial metaphyseal comminution | — |

**Order of prediction** — **calcar < 8 mm > medial hinge > angulation > 45° > head split**

**NOT predictors (TO)** — **valgus displacement**, **surgical neck fracture**

**Long calcar (> 8 mm)** — **protective** · Hertel PPV **97%** (TO)

**Ischaemia ≠ AVN** — acute ischaemia **doesn't predict** later necrosis (Bastian & Hertel)

**Highest AVN risk** — **4-part fracture-dislocation** · 4-part head split **21–75%** · valgus-impacted 4-part low (posteromedial periosteum intact) · 3-part valgus impacted 5–10%

![Long vs short calcar](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob1015-shows-long-vs-short-calcar.jpg)

**Campochiaro 2015 (TO)** — Hertel criteria **less predictive than quality of reduction** for AVN · anatomical neck fracture **90–100%** AVN

## 3 Presentation

**Exam** — ecchymosis chest / arm / forearm · **deltoid + lateral shoulder sensation (axillary)** · chest wall

**Lesser tuberosity fracture** — **posterior dislocation until proven otherwise** (seizure, **electrocution** — subscapularis avulsion)

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Study | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Trauma series: true AP (Grashey), scapular Y, axillary** | **Axillary view mandatory** (→ Velpeau if painful → CT) |
| Apical oblique, Velpeau, West Point | Additional |
| **Combined cortical thickness** (medial + lateral) | **> 4 mm → good screw purchase**; **< 4 mm precludes fixation (TO)** → sling / transosseous suture / hemi |
| **Pseudosubluxation** | Inferior head (haemarthrosis + atony) |
| AP in **IR** | LT in profile; **lightbulb sign** (posterior dislocation) |
| CT | Head / GT position, comminution, **head split** |
| MRI | Rarely; cuff tear |

### Classification

**Neer** — 4 segments: **GT, LT, articular surface, shaft**; part = **> 1 cm displacement or > 45° angulation**

**AO / OTA** — location, surgical neck status, dislocation

**Codman (TO)** — 4 parts: head · GT · LT · shaft (proximal to pec major insertion) · AO/OTA **11-A extra-articular unifocal · 11-B extra-articular bifocal · 11-C intra-articular** (TO)

## 5 Management

```
Proximal humerus fracture
├─ Minimally displaced (most) → sling 2–3 wk + early ROM
├─ GT > 5 mm (> 3 mm overhead) → ORIF (suture / screw)
├─ Young, displaced 2–4 part, good bone (> 4 mm cortex) → ORIF (calcar screw, medial support)
├─ Head split, 40–65 yr → hemiarthroplasty (or ORIF if young)
└─ Elderly, comminuted / dislocated / poor tuberosities → reverse TSA (cemented, tuberosity repair)
```

**Abbreviations** — ORIF open reduction and internal fixation · CRPP closed reduction percutaneous pinning · RSA reverse shoulder arthroplasty · GT / LT greater / lesser tuberosity · MRI magnetic resonance imaging

### Nonoperative management & natural history

**Indications** — minimally displaced, **GT < 5 mm**, poor surgical candidates; consider age, bone, dominance, health

**Technique** — **sling 2–3 wk + immediate PT / pendulum + elbow ROM within 1–2 wk** · early ROM **doesn't displace**\
✗ Wrong options: immobiliser 6 wk · hanging cast 6–8 wk

**Minimally displaced surgical neck** — **sling + recheck X-ray at 1 wk**

**One-part fracture** — **early structured PT**; union **> 90%**; at 1 yr **forward flexion restored, IR + ER slightly ↓, no functional deficit**; most return to work

**Impacted varus fractures (Court-Brown)** — **age = main outcome factor** · union 93% at 1 yr, **smoking ↑ nonunion 5.5×**\
✗ Wrong options: angulation · PT · dominance · bone

**Varus posteromedial vs valgus impacted nonop (TO)** — **varus posteromedial better** (TO)

**Surgical indications** — **head split**, irreducible dislocation, GT > 5 mm, young, **+ humeral shaft fracture**\
✗ Wrong options: age > 70 · comorbidity

**10 months, can't elevate, motion at fracture** — **nonunion** (surgical neck commonest)

**PROFHER (JAMA 2015) (TO)** — no long-term difference surgery vs sling in selected displaced fractures

| Neer rehab (TO) | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0–3 wk | Pendulums, avoid ER |
| 3–9 wk | Active-assisted elevation after radiographic healing |
| >9 wk | Active + strengthening |

### Greater tuberosity fractures — displacement thresholds & fixation

**Threshold** — **> 5 mm (general)**, **> 3 mm overhead / active** (OB, Orthobullets) · **> 1 cm** or > 20% head overlap; elderly tolerate **2 cm / 40%** (TO) — read the options

**Incorrect statement (TO)** — "**GT fixation required for ≥ 5 mm in active young**" (TO uses > 1 cm)

**> 5 mm residual** — **impingement → loss of abduction + ER**, **altered cuff mechanics** (✗ Wrong options: nonunion · AVN (rich supply)) (✗ Wrong options: IR loss · instability)

**Normal GT** — **5–8 mm below** top of head (≈ 6 mm → nonop sling)

**Fixation** — **heavy non-absorbable suture figure-of-8 / tension band (osteoporotic)** · **isolated screws young + good bone** · avoid prominent hardware

**Associations** — **anterior dislocation**, **longitudinal cuff tear**; **subscapularis NOT** a GT deforming force

**Healed GT, pain + weak overhead, full passive ROM at 5 months** — **MRI (cuff tear)**\
✗ Wrong options: more PT · MUA (passive full) · arthroscopy first · TSA

**Valgus-impacted 4-part, young laborer** — **ORIF**

**3-part, cortex 4.2 mm, active 54-yr** — **ORIF**\
✗ Wrong options: sling · hemi (< 4 mm) · RSA

**Seizure fracture-dislocation (young)** — **ORIF** after reduction (watch for occult neck extension before closed reduction)

**25-yr 4-part** — **ORIF** (retain native head; arthroplasty for elderly / failed fixation)

**Spot AP after trauma** — **axillary view** next

**GT / LT fixation by size (TO)** — GT >5 mm: fragment **>2.5 cm → screw**, **<2.5 cm → suture anchor** · LT: <2.5 cm → suture/anchor, >2.5 cm → ORIF

### CRPP

**Indication** — **2-part surgical neck**; 3-part / valgus-impacted 4-part with good bone, intact calcar · **highest complication rate**

**Technique** — threaded pins, don't cross cartilage, **ER the arm**, enter 2 cm below head

| Pin | At risk |
|---|---|
| **Lateral** | **Anterior branch of axillary nerve** |
| **Anterior** | **LHB tendon**, **musculocutaneous nerve** (\~17 mm), **cephalic vein** (\~11 mm) |
| GT pin through medial cortex | **PCHA** |

**Modified Resch (TO)** — **2 lateral-to-medial + 1 anterior-to-posterior** K-wires · contraindicated: calcar deficiency, LT fracture, metaphyseal comminution

### Locked plating — screw penetration, varus collapse & calcar support

**Commonest complication** — **screw penetration / cut-out**: **14%** (Orthobullets, Südkamp) · **16–30%**, 23% (OB)\
✗ Wrong options: infection · AVN · axillary nerve · HO

**Mechanism** — inadequate head fixation → **varus collapse → penetration** · 40% of complications technical

**Prevent varus collapse** — **inferomedial calcar screw(s)** · **anatomic medial cortical apposition** · **fibular strut** (↓ head height loss + varus)\
✗ Wrong options: cortical + locking mix · tension band · CRPP · IMN

**Loss-of-reduction risks (TO)** — age, osteoporosis, smoking, **varus**, **medial comminution**, no cuff sutures

**Plate placement** — **lateral to bicipital groove + pectoralis tendon** (spare **ascending branch of ACHA**); locking plate more elastic than blade plate

**Rehab** — guided 3-phase program; **stiffness at 4 months with healed fracture → scar tissue**

**ORIF in > 60 yr** — **failure > 30%**, complications > 40%; **fracture type predicts failure**; fibular strut doesn't beat RSA

**PHILOS position (TO)** — **5–8 mm below GT tip**, **2–4 mm posterior to bicipital groove** · **4–6 head screws incl. 2 inferomedial calcar** · ≥3 shaft screws · **Gavaskar 2016: ≥7 locking head screws** · 10 suture holes for cuff · fill defects with calcium phosphate cement · **torus concept** = head on a "three-legged stool"

**Bone quality (TO)** — **deltoid tuberosity index** correlates with BMD (CACT < 4 mm = poor bone, as above)

**Beach chair (TO)** — BP cuff on dependent leg misreads cerebral perfusion

### Surgical approaches — axillary nerve at risk

**Axillary nerve** — **\~5–7 cm below acromion** (7 cm); **deltoid-split / extended anterolateral acromial approach → risk** (vs deltopectoral)

### Intramedullary nailing

**Indications** — **2-part surgical neck** (± shaft extension), **pathological**, 3-part GT (young), proximal humerus + shaft · not varus posteromedial with both tuberosities, head split, 4-part

**Facts** — superior deltoid split; straight nail through articular cartilage; **cuff pain**, migration · **distal lateral → medial screw: radial nerve**; **A→P: musculocutaneous**

**Nail (TO)** — 2-part surgical neck / 3-part GT · **8–12 mm** · 3 cm anterolateral incision, split cuff along fibres · straight nails enter via cuff split · **MultiLoc** (2nd generation) · complication: **cuff dysfunction + shoulder pain**

### Hemiarthroplasty — height, tuberosity position & indications

**Indication** — **40–65 yr** with **head split** / complex fracture-dislocation likely to fail ORIF (**convertible stem**) · head depression > 40% (TO)

**Best predictor of success** — **anatomic tuberosity healing**\
✗ Wrong options: 25° retroversion · < 24 h surgery · labral repair; age > 75 worse

**Poor outcome (TO)** — **GT migration**\
✗ Wrong options: anteversion (**retroversion > 40°** worse) · no glenoid resurfacing

**Height** — **top of head 5.6 cm above superior pectoralis major tendon** (height + **retroversion** reference)

**GT position** — **\~8 mm below top of prosthetic head** (head-to-tuberosity distance 7–8 mm); malposition → **8× ER torque**

**Head too low / below GT** — **impingement, ↓ elevation + abduction**

**Can't elevate 6 months after hemi** — **GT malunion** · LT nonunion → weak lift-off

**Post-op caution** — **passive ER** (+ active IR) stresses **LT / subscapularis**

**Best hemi indication** — **46-yr 4-part with comminuted head split**\
✗ Wrong options: GT dislocation (ORIF) · 3-part intact calcar (ORIF) · OA (TSA) · cuff arthropathy (RSA)

**59-yr carpenter — hemi over ORIF because** — **head-split pattern**

### Reverse shoulder arthroplasty vs hemiarthroplasty / ORIF in the elderly

**Indications** — **low-demand elderly**, **non-reconstructible tuberosities**, poor bone, **fracture-dislocation**, > 70 yr comminuted · **60° initial varus** (> 20°) → arthroplasty

**70-yr comminuted metaphysis** — **cemented RSA + tuberosity repair**\
✗ Wrong options: uncemented (metaphyseal loss) · no tuberosity repair

**Biomechanics** — **COR medial (+ inferior) to native** (lateralised designs still medial) → **deltoid longer lever**; less tuberosity-dependent

**↑ ER after RSA** — **GT healing** (teres minor) · larger glenosphere, lateralised, **135° NSA**, more retroversion help\
✗ Wrong options: inferior tilt · small glenosphere · medialisation · 155° NSA

**GT not repaired / resorbed** — **clinically significant loss of ER**

**Contraindication** — **non-functioning deltoid (upper trunk / C5 / axillary palsy)**\
✗ Wrong options: irreparable cuff (that contraindicates hemi / TSA)

**RSA vs HA** — RSA: **higher complication rate** (OB) but **lower revision**; HA: **more IR**, more resorption issues, **greater motion loss**, unpredictable pain · equal complications (OB) — read the options

**Octogenarian fracture-dislocation** — **RSA > plate + hemi** outcomes

**Primary vs salvage RSA** — primary: **more ER + elevation**, better PROs · salvage after failed ORIF: worse function, ↑ instability, infection, complications · **RSA after failed nonop > after failed ORIF**

**Chronic fracture-dislocation + shaft (81-yr)** — **CT → long-stem RSA + cable / plate**

**Elderly 4-part, independent** — **arthroplasty (hemi or RSA)** · 73-yr 4-part → **RSA** · anatomic TSA not used for fractures

**RSA complications** — scapular notching (↓ by 135° NSA, inferior tilt, inferior offset), dislocation, **glenoid loosening (commonest revision cause)**, **acromial stress fracture**, infection

## 6 Complications

| Complication | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Screw cut-out / penetration** | **Commonest after locking plate** |
| **AVN** | Better tolerated than LL; not related to fixation type |
| **Axillary nerve** | **Up to 58% (EMG)**; suprascapular 48% |
| **Malunion** | **Varus apex-anterior** or GT → **RSA** (not TSA) |
| **Nonunion** | **2-part surgical neck**; **age + smoking** |
| Missed posterior dislocation | **LT fracture** |
| LHB injury | Anterior CRPP pin |
| Adhesive capsulitis, PTOA, infection | — |

### Related anatomy (long head of biceps, deltoid innervation)

**LHB** — **supraglenoid tubercle**; **musculocutaneous**; **supination**; landmark in comminuted fractures

**Deltoid** — **axillary nerve (C5–6) ← posterior cord**
