# Tibial Shaft Fractures

*Trauma · 65 / 67*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Commonest long-bone fracture** — **17% of lower-limb fractures** · 4% of Medicare fractures · M > F, bimodal · proximal ⅓ = 5–10% of shaft fractures · open \~20%

| Mechanism | Pattern | Fibula | Soft tissue |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Low energy, torsion** | **Spiral** | **Different level** | Less · **posterior malleolus** with spiral distal ⅓ |
| **High energy, direct** | **Wedge / short oblique**, comminuted | **Same level** | Severe (Tscherne II / III, open) |

**Amputation predictor** — **severity of muscle / soft-tissue injury**

### Anatomy

**Shaft** — triangular · **proximal canal centred laterally** (start point) · subcutaneous anteromedial crest · tubercle \~3 cm below joint · **tibia carries 80–85% of load**

| Compartment | Contents |
|---|---|
| Anterior | Tibialis anterior, EDL, EHL · deep peroneal n. |
| Lateral | Peroneus longus + brevis · superficial peroneal n. |
| Superficial posterior | Gastrocnemius, soleus, plantaris |
| Deep posterior | Popliteus, tibialis posterior, FDL, FHL · tibial n. |

![Compartments of the leg](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob1045-compartments-of-leg.jpg)

### Associated injuries (posterior malleolus in spiral distal-third fractures)

**Spiral distal ⅓ (twisting)** — **posterior malleolus (Volkmann) fracture** — 25–60% · **CT ankle** (or ankle X-rays) before nailing · nail can displace it → **AP lag screw first** · may affect syndesmosis\
✗ Wrong options: MRI knee / hip · CT knee · calcaneal view · CT chest · arterial duplex

**Not associated** — vertical medial malleolus shear, Chaput, talus, Tillaux, knee dislocation · **PITFL intact** with posterior malleolus fracture

**Compartment syndrome** — **diaphyseal 8.1%** (proximal 1.6%, distal 1.4%) · **occurs in open fractures too** · check all 4

**Floating knee** — antegrade tibial nail + retrograde femoral nail

![Posterior malleolus on axial CT](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob1045-ct-axial-posterior-malleolus.jpg)

## 3 Presentation

**Exam** — deformity, blisters, wounds · **compartments** · DPN, SPN, sural, tibial, saphenous · DP + PT pulses (Doppler → CTA if absent)

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

**X-ray** — full-length AP + lateral tibia · **knee + ankle** AP / lateral / oblique · repeat after splinting

**CT** — plateau / plafond extension · **distal ⅓ or spiral → exclude posterior malleolus** · nonunion

### Classifications

| OTA 42 | Pattern |
|---|---|
| A | Simple |
| B | Wedge |
| C | Complex / comminuted |

| Tscherne (closed soft tissue) | Injury |
|---|---|
| 0 | Indirect, negligible |
| I | Superficial abrasion, simple fracture |
| II | Deep abrasion, contusion, **impending compartment syndrome** |
| III | Crush, degloving, muscle destruction, **compartment syndrome, vessel / nerve rupture** |

| Gustilo-Anderson | Wound / soft tissue |
|---|---|
| **I** | **< 1 cm, clean**, simple |
| **II** | **> 1 cm**, no extensive damage |
| **IIIA** | High energy / comminuted / segmental; usually > 5 cm; **coverage adequate** · **1 cm wound with high-energy comminution = IIIA** |
| **IIIB** | **Flap needed** (STSG doesn't count) · proximal ⅓ **gastrocnemius**, middle ⅓ **soleus**, **distal ⅓ free flap** |
| **IIIC** | **Vascular injury needing repair** |
| Always III | Barnyard, shotgun / high-velocity GSW, > 24 h to treatment |

**Exposed bone, dopplerable pulses, peroneal numbness** — **IIIB → spanning ex-fix, delayed fixation + coverage**\
✗ Wrong options: immediate plating (infection up to 44%)

## 5 Management

```
Tibial shaft fracture
├─ Closed, low energy, acceptable alignment → long leg cast → PTB brace ~4 wk
├─ Most → reamed, statically locked IM nail
│      ├─ proximal ⅓ → lateral start, semi-extended / suprapatellar, blocking screws
│      └─ distal ⅓ → fibular plating, center-center guidewire
├─ Open → antibiotics < 3 h, I&D < 12–24 h, nail or ex-fix
└─ Polytrauma / contaminated → ex-fix → nail when resuscitated (ideally < 7 d)
```

### Nonoperative management — deformity & contraindications

| Acceptable alignment (cast / functional brace) | Limit |
|---|---|
| Varus-valgus | **< 5°** |
| Anterior-posterior | **< 10°** |
| Cortical apposition | **> 50%** |
| **Shortening** | **< 1 cm** |
| Rotation | **< 10°** |

**Contraindication to brace** — **shortening > 10 mm** (24 mm comminuted) · open, intra-articular, non-compliant\
✗ Wrong options: same-level fibula · transverse · 9° sagittal · 7° rotation

**Cast risk** — **shortening hardest to control** (oblique / comminuted; mean 4 mm) · angulation + rotation held well · nonunion \~1% (Sarmiento 1.1%) · union \~18 wk

**Intact fibula** — **varus malunion (\~25%)** — fibula = strut · relative contraindication to bracing

**Nonop most common deformity** — **varus**\
✗ Wrong options: valgus + procurvatum (proximal ⅓ nailing)

**IM nail vs cast EXCEPT** — "**↓ compartment syndrome**" — nailing may ↑ it (11%, Finkemeier) · nail: faster union, ↓ malunion, ↓ shortening, quicker return to work

### Operative criteria

| Option | Indications |
|---|---|
| **IM nail** | Unacceptable closed alignment, soft tissue won't tolerate cast, **segmental, comminuted, floating knee, polytrauma, bilateral, morbid obesity** · union > 80% closed |
| **Ex-fix** | **Damage control**, contaminated / soft-tissue defect, metaphyseal |
| **Plate** | Proximal / distal with poor nail fixation, **adjacent implant (TKA)** |
| **Amputation** (relative) | **Severe soft tissue, warm ischaemia > 6 h, severe ipsilateral foot trauma** |

### Open fractures — classification, antibiotics & infection

**Antibiotics** — **within 3 h** · **cefazolin for 24 h after definitive surgery (I, II, IIIA)** · **+ aminoglycoside IIIB** (Orthobullets) / **type III** (OB) — read the options · + **penicillin** farm (Clostridium) · **tetanus**

**Type III regimen** — **1st-gen cephalosporin (cell wall) + aminoglycoside (30S, bactericidal)**\
✗ Wrong options: 50S macrolide / clindamycin · tetracycline (bacteriostatic 30S) · fluoroquinolone alone (water contamination) · vancomycin alone

**Most important to ↓ infection** — **early antibiotic administration**\
✗ Wrong options: debridement < 6 h · immediate internal fixation · antibiotic irrigation · high-pressure lavage · reamed nail · VAC · tetanus · 9 L irrigation

**Second (after antibiotics, LEAP)** — **time to transfer to definitive trauma centre**\
✗ Wrong options: ex-fix · debridement < 6 h · coverage < 48 h

**I&D** — within **12–24 h** · remove all dead tissue + cortical bone · primary closure if clean, no tension · NPWT doesn't ↓ infection

**Persistent infection with retained implants** — strongest predictor **open fracture**\
✗ Wrong options: smoking · Pseudomonas

### Limb salvage vs amputation (LEAP)

**Most important in salvage vs amputation** — **severity of soft-tissue injury**\
✗ Wrong options: plantar sensation · bone loss · education · contralateral injury

**Insensate foot** — **not** an indication · **55% normal plantar sensation at 2 yr** in both groups

**LEAP** — **amputation = salvage** at 2 + 7 yr (function, psychosocial, return to work) · **depression at 3 months → worse outcome**\
✗ Wrong options: plantar sensation · nail · free vs rotational flap · number of debridements

**METALS (military)** — **amputation better function** than salvage · civilians: equal or slight benefit to amputation (OUTLET) · disability common either way

### Soft-tissue coverage — flaps

| Zone | Flap | Supply |
|---|---|---|
| **Proximal ⅓** | **Medial gastrocnemius** rotation (larger, no CPN risk) | **Medial sural artery** (popliteal branch) |
| **Middle ⅓** | **Soleus** rotation | — |
| **Distal ⅓** | **Free flap** (latissimus, rectus, gracilis) or reverse sural | — |

**Staged ORIF + free flap** — **IIIB intra-articular distal tibia**\
✗ Wrong options: midshaft · proximal Schatzker I · IIIC

### Reamed vs unreamed nailing & reaming hazards

| | **Closed** | **Open** |
|---|---|---|
| **Reamed** | **↓ time to union, ↓ reoperation** (SPRINT) · larger nail | **Equivalent** to unreamed |
| Unreamed | ↑ grafting, dynamisation, exchange · ↑ **locking screw breakage** | Same union, infection, reoperation |

**Unreamed in open** — **no difference** · not an adverse factor · surgery maybe shorter · pulmonary benefit unproven\
✗ Wrong options: ↑ nonunion · ↑ / ↓ infection

**Adverse outcome (open tibia nail)** — **high energy, stainless steel nail, gap > 1 cm, smoking**\
✗ Wrong options: unreamed nail

**Biology** — **mineral apposition similar** reamed vs unreamed · **reamed → most new bone** · **limited reaming → lowest porosity** · **tight nails ↓ cortical reperfusion** · over-ream **1–1.5 mm**

**Massive necrosis after reaming** — **over-reaming**\
✗ Wrong options: tourniquet · SLE · steroids · hyperflexion

**Tourniquet during reaming** — **no thermal necrosis** · with reamed femoral nailing in polytrauma → **↑ pulmonary morbidity** (reperfusion + emboli) · ↑ post-op pain

### Nail start point & malalignment (proximal & distal fractures)

**Start point** — **medial border of lateral tibial eminence** (AP) · just anterior to articular margin (lateral) · proximal ⅓ → **more lateral + proximal** (on lateral eminence) · **lateral knee X-ray** checks nail not proud

**Proximal ⅓ deformity** — **valgus + procurvatum (apex anterior)** — **patellar tendon extends proximal fragment**, **pes anserinus → valgus**, hamstrings / gastrocnemius flex distal · **medial start point → valgus** · malunion up to 50%

**Prevent** — **semi-extended / suprapatellar** (↓ procurvatum), **lateral start**, **blocking (Poller) screws in concavity: posterior in proximal fragment + lateral in metaphysis** (+25% stability), unicortical plate, lateral parapatellar

**Distal ⅓** — **valgus** malalignment with infrapatellar nail vs plate · guidewire **center-center** at physeal scar · nail tip slightly lateral to talar centre · aids: **fibular plating**, clamps, Poller screw, small plate\
✗ Wrong options: **syndesmotic fixation**

**Distal ⅓ extra-articular + fibula fracture** — **reamed IMN + fibular plating**\
✗ Wrong options: tibial + fibular plates (wounds) · nail without fibula (valgus, late loss)

**Malrotation** — **highest after nailing distal ⅓** · check: perfect lateral knee, rotate C-arm **105–110°** → ankle mortise

### Anterior knee pain — infrapatellar vs suprapatellar nailing

**Commonest complication of nailing (TO)** — **anterior knee pain** — **57%** (TO) · **50–75%** (> 70% at 14 yr) (OB) · > 30–50% (Orthobullets) · \~20% (OB) — read the options · activity-related, kneeling · only 10% can't work

**Tendon-splitting vs paratendinous** — Keating 77% vs 50% · **Toivanen: no clinically relevant difference** (67% vs 71%)

| | **Suprapatellar (semi-extended)** | **Infrapatellar** |
|---|---|---|
| **Alignment** | **Better**, **↓ procurvatum + valgus** | More flexion → procurvatum |
| Anterior knee pain | **Less** (OB) · no difference (OB) | **More** |
| Septic knee, nail size, ROM, quads, PF disease, op time | **No difference** | No difference |
| Fluoroscopy | Easier | — |
| Use | **Proximal / distal ⅓** | Midshaft |

### Percutaneous plating — SPN risk & radiation

**LISS / long lateral plate** — **superficial peroneal nerve** at **holes 11–13** (13-hole) → **dorsal foot / midfoot numbness**, motor normal · SPN exits fascia **\~8 cm above lateral malleolus**\
✗ Wrong options: CPN · sural · tibial · compartment syndrome

**Plate vs IMN (distal ⅓)** — plate: **↑ radiation**, **↓ angular deformity**, larger incision, harder removal · nail: **↑ pain**, shorter surgery · same union, infection, wound breakdown, prominent hardware (OB)

### Biological adjuncts — BMP-2 / BMP-7

| BMP | Approval / fact |
|---|---|
| **rhBMP-2** | **FDA: acute open tibial shaft fracture + IM nail** · **↓ secondary bone grafting** (IIIA / IIIB), faster healing, ↓ hardware failure, ↓ infection (controversial) · not for nonunion |
| **rhBMP-7 (OP-1)** | **= autograft for tibial nonunion** (RCT) · **HDE** for recalcitrant long-bone nonunion |
| BMP-3 | **No osteoinductive activity** |

### Timing of definitive fixation in polytrauma

**Convert ex-fix → nail** — **as soon as resuscitated: lactate < 4.0 mmol/L + base excess > −5.5** (day 3, lactate 2.0, BE −2.5 ✓) · ex-fix **> 14 d** (> 15 d, Orthobullets) → **↑ infection** · ideally **< 7 d** (> 8 d ↑ infection, OB)

### External fixator pins — capsular reflection & septic arthritis

**Knee capsule** — up to **14 mm below subchondral line (posterolateral)** → pins **> 14 mm** · too proximal → **septic arthritis**\
✗ Wrong options: foot drop · anterior tibial artery · contracture

**Frame stiffness** — **larger pins, more pins each side, rods closer to bone, multiplanar**

**Definitive ex-fix (type III)** — **slower union, worse function, more malalignment** than nail · pin infection

### Ballistic tibia fractures

**GSW with exposed bone** — **treat as open** · infection + soft-tissue reconstruction **similar to blunt open** · **↑ compartment syndrome + reoperation vs blunt closed**

## 6 Complications

| Complication | Rate / fact |
|---|---|
| **Anterior knee pain** | **Commonest after nailing** · proud nail (lateral X-ray) · nail removal unpredictable |
| Malunion | 8–10% · **proximal ⅓ up to 50%** · nonop → varus |
| **Nonunion** | **2–10%** · **no healing at 9 months** · open, < 50% cortical contact, transverse, gap |
| Malrotation | Distal ⅓ nailing → ankle arthrosis |
| Compartment syndrome | 1–9% · closed + open |
| Infection | \~5% · open, contamination, delayed cover |
| Nerve | SPN (LISS), saphenous (locking screws), **transient peroneal palsy** after nailing, DPN (posterolateral ex-fix pins) |

### Peroneal nerve palsy after nailing

**Transient peroneal neurapraxia (up to 5%)** — **↓ EHL strength** + 1st web numbness (DPN) · improves by 3 months\
✗ Wrong options: lateral hindfoot (sural) · Achilles reflex (S1) · peroneus longus (SPN) · plantar (tibial)

### Nonunion — risk factors, exchange nailing & fibular ostectomy

**Greatest nonunion risk after nailing** — **postoperative fracture gap**\
✗ Wrong options: NSAIDs · fibula fracture · smoking · mechanism

**Delayed union** — 20–26 wk · **nonunion** — > 9 months or no progress for 3 consecutive months · **4 months with healing signs → observe ≥ 6 months**\
✗ Wrong options: exchange now

**First step** — **rule out infection (ESR, CRP)** — then CT / vit D\
✗ Wrong options: CT · vit D · stimulator · exchange

**Reamed exchange nailing** — **aseptic diaphyseal tibial nonunion** · **hypertrophic** (85–90%) · **< 30% cortical bone loss** (76–96%) · **oblique** best · bent nail, no bone loss → exchange\
✗ Wrong options: circular frame first · ICBG alone · cast / brace

**Exchange nailing poor for** — **infection**, **segmental bone loss / 4 cm defect**, **atrophic** (needs graft / BMP), **metadiaphyseal** (plate), comminuted (45–80%), humerus (30–40%), distal femur (50–60%)

**Mid-diaphyseal nonunion after reamed 10 mm nail (10 months)** — **exchange nailing**\
✗ Wrong options: adjunct plate (metaphyseal / hypertrophic) · nail removal + plate (metadiaphyseal) · fibulectomy + cast · rhBMP-2

**Intact / healed fibula holding tibia apart** — **fibular osteotomy / partial ostectomy**\
✗ Wrong options: cast · solid nail same size · BMP-3 · Masquelet

**After ex-fix (IIIB, near stiff TKA), oligotrophic** — **compression plating** (92–96%)\
✗ Wrong options: MUA · cast + stimulator · ex-fix · IMN

**Bone loss** — posterolateral graft · defects **up to 5–6 cm** graftable

### Malunion & deformity correction (CORA, ankle effects)

**Distal ⅓ varus 25°** — **ipsilateral ankle pain + stiffness**\
✗ Wrong options: spine · hip · knee

**CORA** — **intersection of proximal + distal mechanical (≈ anatomical) axes** in tibia\
✗ Wrong options: concave / convex cortex point

**Osteotomy away from apex** — **secondary translation** deformity

**Early malalignment after nailing** — return to OR: remove nail, reduce, renail · late → osteotomy

### Late equinus & claw toes

**Checkrein deformity (TO)** — after **distal ⅓ tibia fracture** · **FHL adheres to callus** → **hallux flexes with ankle dorsiflexion**, extends with plantarflexion\
✗ Wrong options: FHL tear · IP fracture · tethering at MTP

**Claw toes flexible in PF, rigid in DF + equinus not relieved by knee flexion** — **TA lengthening + release / retromalleolar lengthening of FDL + FHL**\
✗ Wrong options: PT · wait · tibialis posterior transfer (normal heel rise) · IP fusion
