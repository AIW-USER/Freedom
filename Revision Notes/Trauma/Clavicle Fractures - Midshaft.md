# Clavicle Fractures - Midshaft

*Trauma · 9 / 67*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Incidence** — **1 / 1000 / yr** · **2.6–4%** of adult fractures

**Location** — **middle third = 75–80%** (80–85%, OB) of clavicle fractures

**Who** — young active **males < 30**

### Anatomy

**Shape** — S-shaped; flat laterally, tubular centrally, prismatic medially

**Weakest point** — **junction of outer + middle thirds**: thinnest, transition in curvature + cross-section, **only part without muscle / ligament reinforcement**

**SC joint stabilisers** — posterior capsular ligament (key), anterior SC ligament, costoclavicular ligament, intra-articular disc · **AC joint** — CC + AC ligaments

**CC insertions** — trapezoid **3 cm**, conoid **4.5 cm** from distal end

**Subclavian vessels** — behind + below clavicle at **medial–middle junction**; **vein closest**, anterior to artery + plexus

**Supraclavicular nerves** — vertical cutaneous nerves over clavicle → injured by plating

**Unique clavicle facts (TO)** — **only long bone lying horizontally** · **first bone to ossify, last to complete** · **only long bone ossifying by intramembranous ossification**

**Why middle third (TO)** — junction of medial convex + lateral concave curves · thinnest, subcutaneous · **nutrient artery entry** weakens cortex · mechanism: **direct fall on shoulder (commonest)** > FOOSH

### Deforming forces

| Fragment | Pulled | By |
|---|---|---|
| **Medial** | **Posterosuperior** | **Sternocleidomastoid** |
| **Lateral** | **Inferomedial** | **Pectoralis major + weight of arm** (± deltoid) |

**Trapezius (TO)** — elevates lateral end / acromion · distal fragment also **medial + anteriorly rotated** (TO)

**Shortening** — pec major pulls medially; shortening ↓ deltoid lever arm

**Open fracture** — medial fragment **buttonholes through platysma**

![Weight of the arm pulls the lateral fragment down](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob1011-weight-down.jpg)

### Associated injuries

**Medical** — **pneumothorax**, **closed head injury**

**Orthopaedic** — **rib fracture = commonest associated fracture (TO)** · floating shoulder · **scapulothoracic dissociation** (wide distraction, widened scapula–spine interval, plexus / vascular injury) · NV injury

**Brachial plexus injury (TO)** — commonest with **medial-third** fractures

**Open clavicle fracture** — **closed head injury 65%**, pulmonary 75%, spine 35%\
✗ Wrong options: STD · calcaneus · pelvis · open tibia

**Ipsilateral 1st–4th rib fractures** — ↑ odds of **late displacement > 100%** of nonop fracture (SSSC instability)\
✗ Wrong options: plexus palsy · contralateral scapula · deltoid atony · head injury

**Weak pulses after fall (arteriogram)** — **axillary artery** (3rd part, distal to subscapular) — collaterals keep hand perfused

## 2 Mechanism

**Fall onto lateral shoulder** — **85%** (Orthobullets) · **87%** (TO) · direct blow 7% · **FOOSH only 6% (TO)**

## 3 Presentation

**History** — pop / crack, anterior shoulder pain

**Exam** — tenderness, crepitus, deformity, **skin tenting** (impending open) · check subclavian vessels + plexus

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| View / study | Use |
|---|---|
| **Upright AP clavicle** | Supine **under-estimates** displacement |
| **15° cephalic tilt (Zanca)** | Removes scapular overlap |
| Shoulder series | Proximal humerus, scapula injuries |
| **Upright CXR** | **Shortening vs contralateral**; PTX |
| CT (± contrast) | Comminution, shortening, nonunion; **vascular injury**, medial fracture, **SC dislocation** |

**Findings** — medial fragment up, lateral fragment down, shortening

**Measures** — **shortening > 2 cm** → ↓ strength + endurance · **> 100% displacement** → nonunion risk

### Classification

| Displacement | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **< 100%** (non-displaced) | Nonop |
| **> 100%** (displaced, no cortical contact) | Op considered |

| AO type | Subtypes | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **A — simple** | A1 spiral, A2 oblique, A3 transverse | Nonop vs op |
| **B — wedge** | B1 spiral, B2 bending, B3 fragmented | Nonop vs op |
| **C — complex** | C1 complex spiral, **C2 segmental**, C3 irregular | Op |

**Allman** — medial / diaphyseal / lateral

| Allman (TO) | Site | Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| **I** | Middle third | **80–85%** |
| **II** | Lateral third | **15%** |
| **III** | Medial third | **<5%** |

**AO location (TO)** — clavicle = **15**: **15A medial** · **15B middle** · **15C lateral**

### Differential

| Mimic | Clue |
|---|---|
| Distal-third fracture | Older, osteoporotic; ↑ CC distance |
| **SC dislocation** | High energy; dysphagia, stridor, asymmetric pulses → **serendipity view / CT** |
| AC injury | Pain + prominence over AC; Zanca / axillary |

## 5 Management

```
Midshaft clavicle fracture
├─ < 100% displaced, < 2 cm short, closed, NV intact → sling (≡ figure-of-8)
├─ Open / tenting / subclavian injury / floating shoulder → ORIF (absolute)
└─ > 100% displaced, > 2 cm short, comminuted / Z, segmental, polytrauma → ORIF (relative)
```

### Operative vs nonoperative – indications

| Absolute | Relative / fracture-specific |
|---|---|
| **Open** | **Shortening > 2 cm** (> 15 mm, OB) |
| **Displaced with skin tenting** | **Displacement > 100% / > 2 cm (TO)** |
| **Subclavian artery / vein injury** | **Increasing comminution (> 3 fragments) (TO)** |
| **Floating shoulder** | **Segmental** |
| — | Scapular malposition / winging, obvious deformity (TO) |
| — | **Bilateral**, **polytrauma**, head injury, seizures, brachial plexus injury (66% recover) |

**Nonop (TO)** — sling, **no reduction attempt**, early pendulums

**Comminuted displaced clavicle + bilateral plateau fractures** — **ORIF plate**\
✗ Wrong options: figure-of-8 · pins · sling

**TO true statement (TO)** — **increasing comminution is an indication for surgery**\
✗ Wrong options: fixation rarely required · figure-of-8 treatment of choice · > 2 cm shortening remodels

### Operative vs nonoperative – outcomes

| Outcome | ORIF vs nonop |
|---|---|
| **Nonunion** | **↓** (op 1–5% / \~2–2.5%; union > 94%) vs nonop **\~15%** |
| **Symptomatic malunion** | **↓** |
| Time to union | **16.4 vs 28.4 wk** (faster) |
| **Constant + DASH** | **Better at all time points to 1 yr** (COTS) |
| **Strength / endurance** | Nonop ↓ to \~**80%** of other side |
| **Shoulder ROM** | **Same** |
| Cosmesis / satisfaction | Better with ORIF |
| Cost | **↑ with ORIF** |
| Secondary surgery | ↑ with ORIF (hardware removal commonest) |

**Pooled recent literature** — ORIF **↓ nonunion + symptomatic malunion**; **no clinically significant differences** in strength, ROM, return to activity, or unplanned surgery (contrasts with COTS 2007) — read the options

**Primary goal of ORIF** — **↓ nonunion**\
✗ Wrong options: faster pain-free union · no cosmetic deformity · elite strength · fewer complications

**Nonop, 1 yr outcome** — **↓ strength + endurance**\
✗ Wrong options: ↓ motion · instability

**3 cm shortening — similar between op + nonop** — **shoulder ROM**\
✗ Wrong options: nonunion · strength · endurance

**Lowest nonunion + malunion (2.6 cm short)** — **ORIF with plate**\
✗ Wrong options: pins · ex-fix · sling

### Sling immobilisation

**Indications** — < 2 cm shortening / displacement, < 1 cm SSSC displacement, closed, NV intact, low demand

**Sling ≡ figure-of-8** — **no difference** in healing time, rate, alignment, function, cosmesis; **figure-of-8 → more pain, shortening, ↓ compliance**\
✗ Wrong options: figure-of-8 advantages

**No reduction attempt** · passive ROM 2 wk · strengthening 6 wk · sport **4–6 mo**

### Plating technique

| Plate | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| **Superior** | **Higher load to failure**, stronger with inferior comminution, less deltoid detachment | **Prominent**, **↑ subclavian / neurovascular risk** |
| **Anteroinferior** | **Less prominent**, **longer screws** (wider front-to-back laterally), ↓ subclavian risk | **Detach part of deltoid origin** |
| **Dual mini-fragment (2.7 sup + 2.4 ant)** | **↓ hardware removal (0–3.7%)**; equivalent (not superior) biomechanics | Cost; union + reduction same |

**Single 3.5 mm superior vs dual** — **single has more hardware removal / reoperation**\
✗ Wrong options: dual 10× stronger · dual more reoperation · union time differs

**Hardware removal risk** — **low BMI**, **female**\
✗ Wrong options: age · male · dual plates

**Plates** — LC-DCP 3.5 mm, reconstruction 3.5 mm, 2.0–2.7 mm for dual

**Approach extras (TO)** — superior = workhorse · anteroinferior = safer but demanding · protect the **three supraclavicular nerve branches** · **TEN = cosmetic but rotationally less stable** · lateral fragment: lateral locking plate or **hook plate under posterior acromion** · CC screw augments construct

**Rehab** — sling 7–10 d → active; strengthen \~6 wk; sport \~3 mo

### Intramedullary fixation

**Pros** — small incision, less stripping, **spares supraclavicular nerves**, **best for simple patterns**

**Cons** — **can't lock → no length / rotation control**, biomechanically inferior, migration, irritation, removal at \~6 mo

**Contraindicated** — **substantial comminution, segmental**

**TO EXCEPT trap (TO)** — "**IM nailing gives better union than plates in comminuted fractures**" = false

**Implant** — nail ≈ **30–40% of midshaft diameter**; cannulated screws, TENS, Hagie pin

### Nonunion management

**Symptomatic atrophic nonunion** — **ORIF compression plate + bone graft**\
✗ Wrong options: stimulator · figure-of-8 · CRPP · IM nail

**Graft** — **local autograft ≈ iliac crest union rates** (✓ true)

**After failed plating** — biology (soft tissue, approaches, infection) vs mechanics (malreduction, weak / short plate, wrong stiffness) → dual mini-fragment revision

**Commonest reoperation after ORIF** — **implant removal**\
✗ Wrong options: infection

## 6 Complications

### Nonunion risk factors

| Factor | Note |
|---|---|
| **Displacement > 100%** | **Strongest predictor** (Jørgensen) |
| **Comminution / Z-deformity** | ✓ |
| **Shortening > 2 cm** (> 15–20 mm) | ✓ |
| **Female** | ✓ (highest in options) |
| **Advanced age** | ✓ |
| Smoking | ✓ |
| Not risk factors | Sling vs figure-of-8, early ROM, male, dominant arm, young age, simple pattern, > 50% displacement, 1 cm shortening, diaphyseal location |

**Nonunion rates** — nonop displaced **\~15%** (OB, Orthobullets) · **10–15%** · historic **1%** · 7–15% vs op 2% · overall 5–6% · **0.1–13% with 85% of nonunions in middle third (TO)** — read the options

**Highest nonunion by location** — **middle third** (TO, most nonunions) · **lateral third** has highest *rate* vs diaphysis (OB)

**6-wk predictors** — **fracture-site motion, no callus, DASH > 40** → 0 factors: 3% · 2–3 factors: **60% nonunion**

**Elderly comminuted displaced** — highest nonop nonunion risk = **comminution + displacement + age**

**Nonop displaced, shortened — commonest complication** — **nonunion**\
✗ Wrong options: skin necrosis · CRPS 2% · SC / AC arthrosis 4–6%

| Nonoperative | Operative |
|---|---|
| **Nonunion \~15%** → symptomatic: **plate + graft** | **Supraclavicular nerve numbness — most common** (83% at 2 wk, \~50% at 1 yr) |
| **Malunion \~20%**: shortening > 3 cm, angulation > 30°, translation > 1 cm → overhead fatigue, **thoracic outlet syndrome**, strap problems → **osteotomy + graft** | **Hardware prominence** — **8–30%** request removal (superior plates) |
| ↓ strength + endurance (> 2 cm short; Lazarides > 18 mm men, > 14 mm women) | Subclavian injury / thrombosis (superior plates) |
| Poor cosmesis | Nonunion 1–5%, infection \~4.8% (drug use, diabetes, prior surgery), failure \~1.4%, PTX, adhesive capsulitis 4% |
