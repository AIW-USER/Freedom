# Neonatal Forearm Compartment Syndrome

*Trauma · 40 / 67*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Rare** — case reports; largest series **24 cases / 20 yr**

**When** — **first 24 h of life**, present **at birth**

**Where** — forearm, wrist, hand; **unilateral**; R = L; **dorsum > volar**

| Risk factor | Type |
|---|---|
| **Hypercoagulable — polycythaemia** | Intrinsic |
| **Prematurity**, multiple gestation | — |
| **Oligohydramnios** | Extrinsic |
| Maternal diabetes, abnormal lie, neonatal respiratory distress | — |

### Pathophysiology

**Commonest** — **idiopathic**

**Extrinsic (compression)** — fetal posture, **oligohydramnios**, **umbilical cord loops**, **amniotic bands**, birth trauma

**Intrinsic (clotting)** — **hypercoagulable state** → arterial / venous thrombosis

## 3 Presentation

**Pathognomonic** — **skin lesion in ALL**: **bullae**, erythema, ulcers, **eschar**, distal hand / digit **oedema**, fingertip gangrene

**Nerves** — **radial + PIN > ulnar = median**; ↓ spontaneous movement

**Usually missed** → found only after complications

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

**Radiographs** — skeletal changes **late**: **physeal distortion** (widening, flaring, premature closure, angulation), shortening, LLD

**MRI** — **late cases** with full-thickness necrosis + contracture (fasciotomy futile) → map necrosis, guide debridement

**Labs** — **CBC, ESR, CRP normal** (vs infection)

**Compartment pressure — NOT measured** — **no neonatal Δp standards**; **DBP at birth < 40 mmHg** → small rise impairs perfusion

### Differential

| Condition | Distinguishing feature |
|---|---|
| Cellulitis | Mother well, cultures negative |
| **Necrotising fasciitis** | Most easily confused; **skin + subcutaneous only** → **excision**, not fasciotomy; confirmed at surgery |
| Vascular injury | **Absent pulses / Doppler**; with brachial plexus lesions |

## 5 Management

```
Neonatal forearm compartment syndrome
├─ Diagnosed < 24 h → emergency fasciotomy (volar, dorsal, mobile wad + carpal tunnel) ± STSG
├─ Hypercoagulable → anticoagulants / thrombolytics
└─ Late → salvage (debride, neurolysis, release, lengthening, angular correction, tendon reconstruction)
```

**Best outcome** — **fasciotomy within first 24 h** · **salvage inferior**

**6-h-old, 34 wk, oligohydramnios, polycythaemia, bullae** — **neonatal compartment syndrome → fasciotomy**\
✗ Wrong options: cellulitis + antibiotics · NF debridement / HBO · needle manometry

## 6 Complications

| Complication | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Ischaemic muscle contracture** | Debridement + release |
| **Fingertip gangrene** | — |
| **Physeal distortion** | Lengthening, angular correction |
| **Nerve dysfunction** | Neurolysis |

**Prognosis** — **missed diagnosis = worst**; **early fasciotomy = best**

## How it's asked

**"Neonate with bullous forearm, oligohydramnios, polycythaemia?"** → **neonatal compartment syndrome; fasciotomy**

**"Why no needle manometry in neonates?"** → **no Δp standards; DBP < 40 mmHg**
