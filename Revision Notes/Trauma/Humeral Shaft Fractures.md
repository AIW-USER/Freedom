# Humeral Shaft Fractures

*Trauma · 31 / 67*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Incidence** — **3–5% of all fractures** · **20% of humeral fractures** · 7–11.3 / 100,000

**Age** — bimodal; **60% > 50 yr** · < 50: 70% men · > 50: 70% women

**Location** — proximal ⅓ 30% · **middle ⅓ 60% (commonest)** · distal ⅓ 10%

**Mechanism** — **ground-level fall 60%** · MVA \~30% · pathological 4.3% · open 3%

**Risk factors** — previous fracture, **smoking (men)**, age, osteoporosis

| Level | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| Proximal ⅓ | FOOSH, elderly (impaction at surgical neck) |
| Middle ⅓ **transverse** | **Direct blow** |
| Middle ⅓ **spiral** | FOOSH / **torsion (throwing, arm wrestling)** |
| Distal ⅓ | Fall on flexed elbow |

**Associated** — **floating elbow** (+ radius / ulna; commoner in children) · ipsilateral shoulder dislocation (**posterior** > anterior)

### Surgical anatomy & approaches

**Canal** — ends **2–3 cm proximal to olecranon fossa**

**Deforming forces** — fracture **proximal to pectoralis major** → **deltoid abducts proximal fragment**

**Radial nerve course** — exits axilla posterior to brachial artery → **triangular interval** → between **medial + long heads** of triceps → **spiral groove** (**medial to long + lateral heads, 2 cm proximal to deep head**) → pierces **lateral intermuscular septum \~10 cm proximal to radiocapitellar joint** (**20 cm** above medial, **14 cm** above lateral epicondyle) → anterior **\~7.5 cm** from articular surface (**safe zone — never posterior within 7.5 cm**)

**Radial nerve in contact with bone** — **\~6.5 cm** of diaphysis in spiral groove

**Radial nerve — surgical (TO)** — identify on posterior approach **10–15 cm above lateral epicondyle** · MIPO anterior: **forearm supinated** to protect radial nerve · palsy rate **3–30%**, usually neuropraxia

**Ulnar nerve** — enters posterior compartment at **arcade of Struthers**, \~8 cm above medial epicondyle

**Axillary nerve** — **4–7 cm below acromion** (limits proximal posterior exposure)

**Posterior antebrachial / inferior lateral brachial cutaneous nerve** — branch of **radial**; trace proximally to find radial nerve (**Gerwin modification**, paratricipital)

**Compartments** — anterior: biceps, brachialis, coracobrachialis, brachial vessels, **musculocutaneous, median, ulnar** · posterior: triceps, **radial nerve**

## 3 Presentation

**Exam** — shortening + **varus**; **document radial nerve pre- and post-reduction** (wrist + thumb IP extension, dorsal hand sensation)

**Radial nerve palsy** — "**A-OK**" + finger crossing intact, ↓ wrist / finger extension, dorsal hand numbness → **Holstein-Lewis**

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Study | Use |
|---|---|
| **AP + lateral humerus**, joint above + below | Standard |
| **Transthoracic lateral** | **Sagittal (apex anterior) deformity**; rotate patient, not distal fragment |
| Traction views | Shortened / extension (not routine) |
| CT | Intra-articular extension |
| CTA | Vascular injury |
| **EMG / NCS** | Nerve palsy follow-up — **not acutely** |

### Classification

| System | Categories |
|---|---|
| Descriptive | Proximal / middle / distal ⅓ · spiral, transverse, comminuted |
| **OTA 12** | **A simple · B wedge · C complex** |
| Garnavos | Location **P / M / D** (+ j = joint) · morphology **S** simple (T transverse / oblique, S spiral) · **I** intermediate (1–2 butterflies) · **C** complex (≥ 3 butterflies / comminution) |
| **Holstein-Lewis** | **Spiral distal ⅓** → radial nerve palsy **7–22%** (Orthobullets) · **22%** (OB); entrapment risk; **7.5% of shaft fractures** |

## 5 Management

```
Humeral shaft fracture
├─ Isolated, closed, acceptable alignment → coaptation splint 7–10 d → functional brace
│      └─ radial nerve palsy alone → still brace (observe nerve)
├─ Absolute indication (open, vascular, plexus, floating elbow, compartment syndrome) → ORIF
└─ Polytrauma / lower-limb fractures → plate (WB through arm) · DCO → splint first
```

### Functional bracing — indications, acceptable alignment & contraindications

| Acceptable alignment (adult) | Limit |
|---|---|
| **Anterior (sagittal) angulation** | **< 20°** |
| **Varus / valgus** | **< 30°** |
| **Rotation** | **< 30°** (Orthobullets, TO) · **< 15°** (OB) |
| **Shortening** | **< 3 cm** |

**TO trap (TO)** — "**angulation < 30°**" is **NOT** acceptable (must be < 20°) · **shortening < 3 cm, rotation < 30°, angulation < 20°** (TO)

**Child (TO)** — **20–30° varus**, **20° apex-anterior**, **15° IR**, **≤ 2 cm shortening**

**Infant / toddler** — **coaptation splint + bandage arm to thorax**\
✗ Wrong options: hanging cast · flexible nails · ex-fix · plate

**Technique** — coaptation splint / hanging cast **7–10 days** → **Sarmiento brace** (2.5 cm below axilla → 2.5 cm above condyles); **no sling** (gravity reduction); **valgus mould** vs varus; weekly X-ray × 3 wk, then every 3–4 wk

**Hanging cast** — **avoid in transverse fractures** (distraction)

**At 9 days, acceptable** — **functional brace 6–8 wk**\
✗ Wrong options: continue splint (stiffness) · ORIF

**Contraindications (absolute)** — severe soft-tissue injury / bone loss · **vascular injury needing repair** · **brachial plexus injury** (no muscle tone → nonunion)

**NOT contraindications** — **radial nerve palsy (closed)** · comminution · 1 cm shortening · 20° varus

**Outcomes** — union **93.5%** (Sarmiento < 2% nonunion); time **10.7 wk**; malunion 12%; **proximal ⅓ oblique / spiral nonunion 29% (Rutgers-Ring)** · 54% proximal ⅓ (Orthobullets)

**Varus malunion** — **commonest deformity** (esp. distal / transverse) → **no effect on function**\
✗ Wrong options: nonunion · pain · satisfaction · nerve recovery

**Nonop nonunion** — higher than all surgical techniques (contemporary literature); 33% nonunion in one series

### Operative indications (floating elbow, polytrauma, plexus injury) & weight bearing

| Absolute | Relative |
|---|---|
| **Open fracture** | **Bilateral humerus** |
| **Vascular injury requiring repair** | **Polytrauma / lower-limb fracture** (crutch WB) |
| **Brachial plexus injury** | **Pathological** (life expectancy > 6 mo) |
| **Floating elbow (ipsilateral forearm)** | Burns / soft tissue precluding brace |
| **Compartment syndrome** | **Distraction**, **segmental**, **short oblique / transverse (OTA A)**, intra-articular, long oblique proximal |
| **Periprosthetic at stem tip** | **Obesity**, large breasts |
| **Inability to maintain reduction** | Radial nerve palsy (relative only) |
| **Progressive nerve deficit after manipulation** | **Young athlete wanting quick return** |

**NOT an operative indication (TO)** — **closed fracture with radial nerve palsy**\
✗ Wrong options: nonunion · floating elbow · pathological with secondaries

**Strongest indication** — **ipsilateral both-bone forearm fracture**\
✗ Wrong options: radial nerve palsy · long oblique · comminution · Holstein-Lewis

**Flaccid limb, C5–T1 avulsions** — **ORIF with plate**\
✗ Wrong options: splint · brace · ex-fix · sling

**Floating elbow (olecranon + humerus)** — **ORIF both + early ROM**\
✗ Wrong options: brace humerus · splint 4 wk

**Polytrauma (humerus + femur)** — **plate humerus + IMN femur** → crutch WB

**WB after plate / IMN** — **full WB**; **no effect on union**\
✗ Wrong options: partial (can't use crutches)

**Plateau + humerus ORIF + contralateral femur IMN** — **NWB plateau side leg**, **WBAT arm + nailed femur**

**DCO, multiple GSWs + abdominal injury** — **closed reduction + splint in ED**\
✗ Wrong options: I&D · ex-fix in ED · ORIF · IMN

**15-yr quarterback, torsional spiral, wants early return** — **surgical fixation**\
✗ Wrong options: splint / brace · EMG acutely

**Ex-fix indications** — high-energy comminution, open, defect, floating elbow, **unstable polytrauma**, **vascular injury**; 80% good, **pin-track infection 12%**

| Special situation (TO) | Preferred |
|---|---|
| Polytrauma / DCO · open 3B/3C | **External fixator** |
| **Paraplegic** (weight-bearing on arms) | **Plating** |
| Pathological / segmental | **IM nail** |

**TEA** — comminuted distal fractures, **low-demand > 65**, osteoporosis / **RA**

### Plate vs intramedullary nail

| | **Plate (ORIF)** | **IMN** |
|---|---|---|
| Union | **Equal** (90–92%) | **Equal** |
| Time to union | 11.9 wk | **10 wk** |
| Op time | 88 min | **61 min (faster)** |
| **Shoulder pain / impingement / stiffness** | **Lower** | **Higher (16–37%)** — rotator cuff (supraspinatus) |
| **Functional shoulder score (ASES) at 1 yr** | **Equal** | **Equal** |
| Radial nerve palsy | Equal | Equal |
| Infection | 5.4% | **1.2%** — **lower** (Orthobullets, OB) · no difference (OB) — read the options |
| Reoperation | 7.6% | 11.6% (NS) · **↑ revision** (McCormack, OB) |
| Total complications | Lower | **Higher** |
| **Hardware complications** | Lower | **Higher** |
| Malrotation | Lower | **Higher → ↓ ROM** |

**IMN — EXCEPT** — "**increased shoulder ROM**" is false

**Antegrade IMN — true** — **higher shoulder impingement**\
✗ Wrong options: ↑ ROM · less radial nerve palsy · less nonunion

**IMN relative indications** — **pathological**, segmental, osteoporotic, poor skin, polytrauma

**MIPO** — anterolateral incisions + extraperiosteal tunnel; **lacks nerve visualisation → ↑ radial nerve risk** (Orthobullets) · **lower transient palsy than ORIF** (OB) — read the options

### Plate technique

**Implants** — **3.5 / 4.5 mm (narrow or broad) DCP / LC-DCP** (LC-DCP: easier contour, ↓ stress shielding, periosteum spared)

**Simple pattern** — **lag + compression** (absolute stability) · **comminuted** — **bridge** mode

**Bone defect** — **≤ 3 cm → shorten** · **> 3 cm → graft**

**Nerve transposition during ORIF** — **no benefit**

### Approaches

| Approach | Use | Key facts |
|---|---|---|
| **Anterior (brachialis split)** | Middle ⅓ | **Intramuscular internervous plane** — **lateral brachialis (radial) / medial brachialis (musculocutaneous)** in \~80% |
| **Anterolateral** (extends deltopectoral) | Proximal–middle ⅓ | Radial nerve **between brachialis + brachioradialis** distally; cephalic vein, anterior circumflex |
| **Posterior triceps-split** | Distal–middle ⅓ | **55%** exposed (no nerve mobilisation), **76%** with; no internervous plane; avoids ulnar nerve dissection |
| **Paratricipital (triceps-sparing)** / **medial reflection of lateral + medial heads** | Distal | **\~94% exposure (greatest)**; lateral window → radial nerve; medial window → mobilise ulnar nerve |
| **Lateral** | Distal ⅔ | **Highest iatrogenic radial palsy (20%)** |
| Medial | Brachial artery, median + ulnar nerves | Rarely for fractures |

**Iatrogenic radial palsy** — **lateral 20%** > **posterior 11%**; only **approach** predicts risk

**All posterior exposures** — limited proximally by **axillary nerve**

![Anterolateral plating of the humeral shaft](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob1016-al-fixation.jpg)

### IMN technique & interlock nerve risk

**Antegrade** — supine; split deltoid (axillary nerve); entry **through supraspinatus as medial as possible** at apex, near **musculotendinous junction** (avascular insertion), **≤ 1 cm**

**Retrograde** — prone / lateral; posterior supracondylar cortex

**Don't ream across the fracture** — radial nerve

| Interlock | Nerve at risk |
|---|---|
| **Distal anterior → posterior** | **Musculocutaneous → lateral antebrachial cutaneous** (numb radial volar forearm) |
| **Distal lateral → medial** | **Radial** (over-penetration → **ulnar**) |
| Anteromedial → posterolateral oblique | Median |
| **Proximal (antegrade)** | **Axillary** + circumflex vessels |

### External fixation pin safe zones

**Distal pins** — **no safe zone** → **mini-open** to bone (radial nerve)\
✗ Wrong options: percutaneous arc · direct posterior (triceps, supine patient)

**Proximal pins** — anterolateral, mini-open (**axillary nerve**, biceps tendon); **most distal pin just above olecranon fossa**

## 6 Complications

### Radial nerve palsy — natural history, recovery order, EMG timing, exploration & tendon transfer

**Incidence** — **12.3% (8–15%)** (Orthobullets) · **11–11.8%** (OB); **distal ⅓ 22%**; **10–20% secondary** (develop during treatment, TO)

**Fracture type** — **transverse 21.2% > spiral 19.8%** > oblique 8.4% > comminuted 6.8% · level: distal > middle > proximal

**Injury type** — **closed → neurapraxia** · **low-velocity GSW → axonotmesis** · **open (high energy) → neurotmesis** (laceration) · complete palsy 50–68% (TO)

**Spontaneous recovery** — **\~70% by 7 weeks** (OB, TO > 70%) · **77.2%** (Orthobullets) · **85–90% within 3 months** · full at **\~6 months** · 100% of low-energy by 4 months (TO) · **88.6% overall** · iatrogenic 93.9%

**At exploration** — **in continuity 62.7%** · lacerated 26.8% · incarcerated 10.5% · transected only **12%** (Pollock, TO)

**Recovery order (proximal → distal)** — **brachioradialis (radial styloid) → ECRL (2nd MC base)** → supinator → ECRB (3rd MC) → EDC → ECU → EDQ → APL → **EPL** → EPB → **EIP (last)**

**First to recover crossing the wrist** — **ECRL** (inserts **base of 2nd MC**) → **wrist extension in radial deviation** first

**Last** — **EIP → index MCP hyperextension**

| Situation | Management |
|---|---|
| **Closed, primary palsy** | **Brace + observe** (✗ Wrong options: explore · IMN + explore) |
| **Low-velocity GSW + palsy** | **Splint + observe**, short antibiotics |
| **Open fracture + palsy** | **I&D, ORIF + explore nerve** |
| **High-energy open + brachial artery** | **Neurotmesis** likely |
| **Secondary palsy after closed reduction** | **Early exploration** (controversial) |
| Holstein-Lewis | **Controversial** ORIF + explore |

**Early exploration (TO)** — **open**, vascular injury, **secondary palsy after reduction**, **high-velocity GSW**, sharp / penetrating, severe soft tissue

**Complete RNP after shaft fracture (TO)** — **hanging cast + explore if no recovery at 3 months**\
✗ Wrong options: explore + plate · brace + explore at 3 wk · nail + observe

| EMG / NCS timing | Source |
|---|---|
| **\~2 months** (baseline) | Orthobullets |
| **1–2 months** → repeat \~4 months; **no exploration between 1–4 months** | OB |
| 3–4 / 3–5 months | OB |
| No recovery on EMG at **6 wk → observe**, repeat at 12 wk | OB |

**EMG interpretation** — **fasciculations / reinnervation = recovery → observe** · **fibrillations = denervation → explore**

**Exploration timing** — **4–6 months** (Orthobullets, OB) · **3–4 months** if fracture healed (TO) · **3–6 months**, before 6 (TO) · **6 months** (OB) · **no advantage waiting > 7 months** (TO) — read the options

**Healed at 4 months, no recovery** — **EMG / NCS**\
✗ Wrong options: observe · explore immediately · MRI · CT

**Pre-op palsy, nerve protected at ORIF, 2 months** — **NCS / EMG**\
✗ Wrong options: tendon transfer · re-explore

**Exploration recovery** — **early (< 3 wk) \~90%** · **late (≥ 8 wk) \~68%**

**Tendon transfers (≥ 1 year)** — **wrist: PT → ECRB** · **fingers: FCR (or FCU) → EDC** · **thumb: PL → EPL** (FCU loses ulnar deviation power)

**Abbreviations** — PT pronator teres · ECRL / ECRB extensor carpi radialis longus / brevis · EIP extensor indicis proprius · EMG electromyography · NCS nerve conduction studies

### Predicting nonunion during bracing

**Motion + no callus at 6 weeks** — **PPV 90–100%**, **specificity 99–100%**, sensitivity 82%, NPV 97%

**Too early** — **4 weeks without callus → continue brace, review in 4 wk**\
✗ Wrong options: LIPUS · ORIF · IMN

**6 wk persistent motion (proximal ⅓)** — **surgery now**

**Gap predicts mobility** — **each mm of gap ↑ mobility risk 40%**; **gap, smoking, female sex** → **8 mm gap at 8 wk → ORIF plate**\
✗ Wrong options: stimulator · pinning

**RUSHU** — score per cortex at 6 wk (1 absent · 2 non-bridging · 3 bridging); **≥ 8 → 86% NPV** · < 8 → 65% PPV

**Nonunion risk by pattern (nonop)** — **OTA A (15–29%) > B (4%) > C (0%)**

**Nonunion treatment** — **plate + autograft > exchange nailing** · atrophic: debride + compression plate + graft (ICBG, **RIA**) or exchange nail (**ream ≥ 1 mm**) · hypertrophic: exchange nail / locked plate

**Malunion** — varus common, rarely matters; **transverse** patterns
