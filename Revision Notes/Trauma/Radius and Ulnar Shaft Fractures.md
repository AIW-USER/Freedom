# Radius and Ulnar Shaft Fractures

*Trauma · 51 / 67*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Who** — men **10–20 yr**, women **> 60 yr**

**Mechanism** — **high energy** (direct blow, MVA, fall from height, sport) · low-energy both-bone → suspect wrist / elbow injury

**Associated** — **Galeazzi, Monteggia, Essex-Lopresti**, compartment syndrome

### Anatomy

**Rotation axis** — **radial head → ulnar fovea**; radius rotates around ulna

**Radial bow** — complex; **max \~15 mm at 60%** of radial length · ulna slight bow in distal 75%

**Nerves** — median between PT heads then FDS / FDP · ulnar between FCU heads → FDP ring + small · **PIN around radial neck through supinator** → posterior IOM → 4th compartment

**Vessels** — brachial bifurcates **1 cm below elbow** · radial artery adherent to FCR

### Interosseous membrane anatomy

**IOM** — oblique radius → ulna; load passes **distal radius → proximal ulna** · distal fibres tight in supination, central in neutral

**IOM central band (TO)** — runs **proximal radius → distal ulna** · torn in Essex-Lopresti

**Displacement / cast position by level (TO)** — proximal ⅓ → **supination** · middle ⅓ → neutral · distal ⅓ → pronation

**Compartment syndrome (TO)** — forearm = **2nd commonest site** after leg

| IOM ligaments (5) | Note |
|---|---|
| **Central band** | **Key load-bearer; reconstruct this** |
| Accessory band, distal oblique bundle, proximal oblique cord, dorsal oblique accessory cord | — |
| **NOT part of IOM** | **Annular ligament** |

**Essex-Lopresti (TO)** — **injury to interosseous membrane** (+ radial head fracture + DRUJ)

## 3 Presentation

**Exam** — deformity, open wounds, **tense compartments**, **passive finger stretch pain**; radial + ulnar pulses; median, radial, ulnar nerves; hand dominance + occupation

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

**Views** — AP + lateral forearm **+ wrist + elbow**; **radial head aligned with capitellum on all views**; contralateral for planning · CT rarely

| OTA (radius / ulna shaft) | Subtypes |
|---|---|
| **A simple** | A1 spiral · A2 oblique · A3 transverse |
| **B wedge** | B2 intact · B3 fragmentary |
| **C multifragmentary** | C2 intact segmental · C3 fragmentary |

### Monteggia & Galeazzi variants (Bado, transolecranon, chronic)

**Bado IV (TO)** — radius + ulna fractures with radial head dislocation (TO image: anterior angulation + anterior head)

**Transolecranon fracture-dislocation — EXCEPT (TO)** — "**a Monteggia variant**" — it is an **anterior elbow dislocation with intact PRUJ**, mostly **bony**, often **coronoid** fracture

**Monteggia ORIF sequence (TO)** — ulna → coronoid → radial head (fix / replace) → LCL repair

**Old malunited ulna + posterior radial head dislocation, 60-yr, painful (TO)** — answer key not provided by the source

## 5 Management

```
Adult both-bone forearm fracture (> 10 yr)
├─ Closed / Gustilo I–IIIA → ORIF, two incisions, 3.5 mm plates (radius first if simpler)
│      ├─ simple → compression (absolute stability)
│      └─ comminuted → bridge plate (relative stability)
├─ Gustilo IIIB / severe soft tissue → ex-fix
└─ Bone loss → graft (< 5 cm) · vascularised fibula / Masquelet (large)
```

### Treatment options

| Option | Indication | Fact |
|---|---|---|
| Cast / brace (Muenster, above elbow) | **Rare** — non-displaced, non-surgical candidates | **> 50° rotation loss in 30%**, high nonunion |
| **ORIF** | **Nearly all ≥ 10 yr**; **Gustilo I–IIIA primary ORIF** | **> 95% union** compression · **88%** bridge plating |
| Ex-fix | **Gustilo IIIB** | Distal pins **2nd + 3rd MC**, **≤ 4 mm** |
| IMN | **Very poor soft tissue** only | No rotational / axial control, can't keep bow, **high nonunion** · **flexible nails contraindicated in length-unstable adult fractures** |

### Fixation principles & constructs

**Comminuted closed both-bone (22-yr)** — **ORIF without acute graft**\
✗ Wrong options: ex-fix · flexible nails · graft · brace

**Transverse + compression plates** — **absolute stability → direct (primary) healing by internal remodelling** (intramembranous)

**Comminuted (GSW)** — **ORIF with relative stability (bridge plate)** → endochondral (secondary) healing

**Greatest bending rigidity** — **3.5 mm stainless steel DCP on both bones**\
✗ Wrong options: titanium · ⅓ tubular

**Technique** — **3.5 mm DCP / LC-DCP** · **≥ 6 cortices** each side · **long plates** (torsion) · **contoured radius plate**, straight ulnar plate · locking in osteoporosis · 2.0 / 2.7 lag screws · dorsal = tension side (volar seats better)\
✗ Wrong options: 4.5 mm (refracture)

**Post-op** — early ROM; **NWB \~6 wk** (Orthobullets) · **WBAT if ≥ 8 cortices each side** (OB) — read the options · clinical healing \~3 months

**Polytrauma (femur IMN, simple fibula ORIF, forearm ORIF, humerus ORIF)** — **WBAT all extremities** (OB)

### Restoration of radial bow & forearm rotation

**Radial bow restoration** — **most important for function → forearm rotation**\
✗ Wrong options: wrist strength · elbow ROM · synostosis

**Limited pronosupination after long straight radial plate** — **loss of radial bow**\
✗ Wrong options: nonunion · PIN · ECU at DRUJ · HO

**Cause of poor pronation** — **straight plate on radius**\
✗ Wrong options: bridge plating · straight ulnar plate · dual incision

**Malunion > 20°** — limits rotation

### Surgical approaches (Henry, PIN, two-incision synostosis risk)

**Two incisions** — **↓ synostosis** (1 / 65 vs 5 / 12 single) · no wound, neuritis, malunion benefit

| Approach | Use |
|---|---|
| **Volar Henry** | Distal + middle ⅓ radius |
| **Dorsal Thompson** | Proximal ⅓ radius |
| **Subcutaneous ulnar** | Ulna (dorsal ulnar cutaneous nerve at risk) |

**Proximal Henry** — **radial artery medially**, **supinator laterally**, forearm **SUPINATED** (moves PIN dorsally)

**Pronated during proximal Henry** — **PIN injury → weak thumb (+ finger) extension**\
✗ Wrong options: 1st web numbness (superficial radial) · opposition (median) · abduction (ulnar)

**Ulnar approach (TO)** — subcutaneous, **FCU–ECU** plane, protect dorsal cutaneous branch of ulnar n.

**Intra-op rotation check (TO)** — radius: **bicipital tuberosity medial vs radial styloid lateral (160–180°)** · ulna: coronoid vs styloid 180°

**Nailing (TO)** — length = olecranon → ulnar styloid (X): **ulnar nail X − 1 cm, radial nail X − 3 cm** · entry radius just lateral to Lister's, ulna slightly lateral to olecranon tip · indications: open, segmental, osteoporotic, polytrauma

**Nonop (TO)** — isolated stable ulnar fracture (**< 50% displacement, < 10° angulation**, nightstick)

### Open fractures & bone loss (bone graft, vascularised fibula, GSW)

**Clean 2 cm open wound** — **I&D + ORIF + immediate closure**

**1 cm segmental gap after debridement** — **autograft**\
✗ Wrong options: TCP, CaPO₄, CaSO₄, **BMP-3 — not osteoinductive**

**Graft by defect size** — **< 5 cm → cancellous autograft** · larger → **vascularised fibula** (↓ infection), allograft, transport, Masquelet

**10 cm defect, strut allograft vs fibula** — **allograft → more infection** (+ nonunion, fracture, delayed union)

**GSW, large soft-tissue + bone loss** — **ORIF + free fibular osteoseptocutaneous flap**\
✗ Wrong options: strut / cancellous allograft (infection) · ex-fix definitive

**GSW comminuted, 2 cm wound** — **I&D + 3.5 mm LC-DCP bridge plate**\
✗ Wrong options: 4.5 mm · compression · delay · ex-fix

## 6 Complications

| Complication | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Synostosis** | **3–9%**; **single incision**, proximal ⅓, head injury, polytrauma, fragments / hardware in interosseous space |
| Infection | 3% |
| **Compartment syndrome** | \~1% (up to 15%); **superficial volar release may suffice** (connected) |
| **Nonunion** | **< 5%** compression · 12% bridge; IMN |
| Malunion | Radial bow |
| Nerve | **PIN (Henry proximal, Monteggia)**; median (modified Henry); dorsal ulnar cutaneous → observe 3 months |
| **Refracture** | **Up to 10%** |

**Synostosis — further risks (TO)** — **same-level fractures** · comminution · **bone graft at the interosseous border**

**Expected losses** — grip **25%**, pronosupination strength **30%**, wrist flexion 16%, extension 37% strength; motion loss < 10°

### Nonunion

**Atrophic** — **plate exchange + autogenous cancellous graft (ICBG) + compression plate** · smokers high risk\
✗ Wrong options: Ilizarov · stimulators · ulnar shortening · IMN · bone scan if labs normal

**Hypertrophic** — ↑ fixation (compression plate) · infected / atrophic → **Masquelet**

### Refracture after plate removal

**Greatest risk** — **removal < 1 yr** · **diaphyseal** location (correlates most) · **4.5 mm plates** · comminution, displacement, lucency, immediate activity

**NOT a risk** — **3.5 mm DCP**\
✗ Wrong options: locking screw removal · metaphyseal implants (lower)

**Prevention** — **functional brace 6 wk + protect 3 months**

### Heterotopic ossification — timing of excision

**Excision** — **as early as 6 months** (4–6 months) with **radiation ± indomethacin**\
✗ Wrong options: wait for ankylosis / maturity

### Paediatric forearm — deformity limiting rotation

**Most limiting (TO)** — **angulation** (10° → 20° rotation loss) > malrotation (10° → 10°) · **bayonet apposition doesn't limit**

| Age (TO) | Sagittal | Coronal |
|---|---|---|
| < 9 yr | 20° | 15° |
| 9–11 yr | 15° | 5° |
| 11–13 yr | 10° | 0° |

## X vs Y

| | **Absolute stability** | **Relative stability** |
|---|---|---|
| Construct | **Compression plate, lag screws** | **Bridge plate**, IMN, cast, ex-fix |
| Healing | **Primary (internal remodelling)** | **Secondary (callus, endochondral)** |
| Fracture | Simple transverse | Comminuted |
