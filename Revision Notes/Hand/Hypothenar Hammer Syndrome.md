# Hypothenar Hammer Syndrome

*Hand · 42 / 103*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology & anatomy

**Definition** — post-traumatic digital ischaemia from **ulnar artery thrombosis / aneurysm at Guyon's canal**

**Demographics** — rare (<1/100,000); **♂:♀ 9:1**; 40s–50s

**Location** — unilateral, dominant **ring ± small** finger; less often index/middle; **thumb spared**

**Risk** — **vibrating tools** (carpenters, mechanics, jackhammer, construction); baseball catchers, mountain biking, golf, volleyball, karate, MMA

**Anatomy** — distal 2 cm: ulnar artery **directly anterior to hook of hamate**, covered only by palmaris brevis, fat, skin; divides at Guyon's exit → deep branch + superficial palmar arch

**Ulnar artery** — **most common site of true and false aneurysms** in the hand; aneurysm = permanent dilatation **≥50%** of normal

### Pathology – true aneurysm vs thrombosis & recurrence

**Mechanism** — single or **repetitive blunt impact** on hypothenar eminence → **hook of hamate acts as an anvil** → thrombosis or aneurysm → **distal embolisation** → ulcers, gangrene

**Hook of hamate fracture** — may precipitate ulnar artery aneurysm (catcher)

| | **True aneurysm** (HHS) | False / pseudoaneurysm |
|---|---|---|
| Cause | **Blunt** (repetitive) trauma | **Penetrating** trauma |
| Wall | **All arterial layers**, true endothelial lining | No endothelial lining |
| Shape | **Gradual uniform dilatation** | Haematoma organises → recanalises → false lumen |

## 3 Presentation

**Symptoms** — pain hypothenar eminence + **ring finger**, **cold sensitivity**, paraesthesia ulnar 2 digits (± ulnar nerve compression)

**Signs** — **hypothenar callus**, tenderness, blanching, mottling, cyanosis, pallor, **ulnar fingertip ulcers**, **splinter haemorrhages**, **pulsatile mass** if aneurysm

**Allen's test** — **positive with occlusion**; **negative with aneurysm** (patent); may show incomplete palmar arch

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

**First-line** — **Doppler US** + **digital brachial index (DBI)**\
✗ Wrong options: MRA first

**Angiography (CTA / MRA / conventional)** — **mandatory for diagnosis** (Orthobullets); contrast arteriography = **gold standard** (Hui-Chou)

**Angiogram signs** — **"corkscrew" tortuous ulnar artery**; **occlusion or aneurysm at hook of hamate**

**Carpal tunnel view** — hook of hamate fracture

### Digital brachial index criteria

| DBI | Meaning / action |
|---|---|
| **0.85–1.25** | Normal |
| **>0.7** | Minimal symptoms, viable digit → **no surgery** / ligation acceptable |
| **<0.7** | **Reconstruction** |
| **<0.5** | Severe symptoms, **digital gangrene** |

**Differential** — **Raynaud's** involves the **thumb** (HHS spares it)

## 5 Management

```
Hypothenar hammer syndrome
├─ Thrombosis, no aneurysm
│      ├─ <2 wk → endovascular fibrinolysis
│      └─ >2 wk / asymptomatic / no threat → nonop (80% success)
└─ Aneurysm / DBI <0.7 / multi-digit ischaemia / failed nonop → excision + reconstruction
       └─ DBI >0.7 → ligation (Leriche) acceptable
```

**Nonop** — smoking cessation, avoid trauma, vascular consult; **80% success**

### Treatment – resection & arterial reconstruction

**Operative criteria** — **DBI <0.7** · **thrombosis with aneurysm** · ischaemia multiple digits · recurrent symptoms after nonop

**Aneurysm** — **always surgical**: **excision of segment + reconstruction ± vein graft** → prevents emboli, removes painful mass, relieves ulnar nerve, removes vasospasm trigger

**Graft** — **reversed interpositional vein graft** (saphenous, forearm, foot dorsum) or arterial (thoracodorsal, SIEA, descending LFCA); gap usually too large for direct repair

**Not for aneurysm** — fibrinolysis (acute thrombosis without aneurysm only) · **systemic thrombolysis** (never) · **stenting** (stenosis, not thrombosis) · warfarin · CCB / abstinence alone

**Ligation** — **contraindicated with incomplete palmar arch**

**Ligation vs repair vs vein graft** — **no outcome difference** (Demetri)\
✗ Wrong options: "ligation superior"

**Outcomes** — vein graft patency **77% at 2 y** (Chloros); **78% occluded at ~10 y** yet patients satisfied (Endress)

## 6 Complications

**Most common post-op complaint** — **persistent cold intolerance**\
✗ Wrong options: digital ischaemia

| Vein-graft failure | Time | Cause |
|---|---|---|
| **Acute** | **<1 mo** | **Thrombosis** (patency 98% → <88% in 1st month) |
| **Subacute** | **1–18 mo** | **Intimal hyperplasia** = vascular smooth muscle migration + proliferation at anastomosis (30–50% fail) |

**Not failure causes at 6 mo** — graft resorption, recurrent aneurysm, hamate nonunion compression, thrombosis (acute only)

## X vs Y

| | **HHS** | **Raynaud's** |
|---|---|---|
| Side | Unilateral, dominant | Bilateral |
| Digits | Ring ± small, **thumb spared** | Includes **thumb** |
| Cause | Blunt trauma, hook of hamate anvil | Vasospasm |
| Angio | Corkscrew, occlusion/aneurysm at hamate | — |

## How it's asked

**"Jackhammer worker, hypothenar callus, pulsatile mass — true statement?"** → **gradual dilatation of all arterial layers (true aneurysm)**

**"DBI that necessitates reconstruction?"** → **<0.7**\
✗ Wrong options: 0.85

**"First-line test?"** → **Doppler US + DBI**\
✗ Wrong options: MRA

**"Most common complication after surgery?"** → **persistent cold intolerance**

**"Pathophysiology of blunt-trauma aneurysm?"** → **true endothelial lining, uniform dilatation**\
✗ Wrong options: recanalised false lumen

**"Vein graft failed at 6 mo — why?"** → **intimal hyperplasia (smooth muscle migration/proliferation)**\
✗ Wrong options: thrombosis = acute

**"HHS with aneurysm and ischaemia — next step?"** → **excision + reconstruction ± vein graft**\
✗ Wrong options: fibrinolysis · warfarin · CCB

**"Aneurysm thrombosed, ulcer, DBI 0.4, incomplete arch?"** → **ulnar artery reconstruction**\
✗ Wrong options: ligation · stenting · thrombolysis

**"Acute thrombosis <2 wk without aneurysm?"** → **endovascular fibrinolysis**
