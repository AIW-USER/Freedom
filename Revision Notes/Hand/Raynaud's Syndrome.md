# Raynaud's Syndrome

*Hand · 75 / 103*

## 1 Basics

### Definitions & epidemiology

**Raynaud's** — exaggerated **vasoconstriction of digital arteries** to stimuli

**Raynaud's phenomenon / syndrome (secondary)** — **known underlying cause**

**Raynaud's disease (primary)** — **idiopathic**; young **premenopausal ♀ <40 y**

**Prevalence** — vasospastic disease **3–5%** of US population; **40–50%** develop painful digital ulcers

### Associations of Raynaud's phenomenon

| Condition | Raynaud's incidence |
|---|---|
| **Scleroderma** | **80–90%** |
| Dermatomyositis | 30% |
| SLE | 18–26% |
| RA | 11% |
| **CREST** | Calcinosis, Raynaud's, oesophageal dysmotility, sclerodactyly, telangiectasia |
| Neurovascular compression | Thoracic outlet syndrome |

## 2 Mechanism

**Triggers** — **cold** or **sympathetic stimuli** (pain, **emotional stress**)

**Triphasic colour change** — **white** (vasospasm) → **blue** (cyanosis, venous stasis) → **red** (rebound hyperaemia) → dysaesthesia

## 3 Presentation

| | **Raynaud's disease** (primary) | **Raynaud's phenomenon** (secondary) |
|---|---|---|
| Age / sex | **<40, young ♀** | **>40**, occasional ♀ predominance |
| Distribution | **Bilateral** | **Asymmetric**, distal digits |
| Progression | **Slow** | **Rapid** |
| Pulses | Present | **Often absent** |
| Trophic changes / ulcer / gangrene | Rare | **Frequent** |
| Allen test | Normal | **Abnormal** |
| Labs | Normal | Often abnormal |
| Angiography / microangiology | Normal | Often abnormal |

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Diagnostic criteria – Allen & Brown (Raynaud's disease)

| Criterion |
|---|
| Intermittent attacks of acral discoloration |
| **Bilateral** involvement |
| No clinical arterial occlusion |
| Gangrene / trophic changes **rare** |
| Symptoms **>2 years** |
| ♀ predominance |
| No other disease explaining findings |

**Differentiate by** — age · gender · time course of progression · severity of exam

### Key radiology findings

**X-ray** — **normal, not indicated**

**Angiography** — secondary: often abnormal (e.g. incomplete ulnar artery obstruction)

**Doppler** — documents ↑ digital flow after BTX

## 5 Management

```
Raynaud's
├─ First line → stop smoking + avoid cold + biofeedback + medication
│      (CCB, topical nitrate, ASA, sildenafil …)
├─ Failed medical Rx, ischaemia/pain (± ulcer) → botulinum toxin A (off-label)
└─ Severe / impending gangrene / ischaemic ulcer → periarterial (digital) sympathectomy ± arterial reconstruction
       └─ segmental occlusion of one major artery → microvascular reconstruction
```

### Medical management

**Critical** — **smoking cessation + avoid cold**; thermal biofeedback; stress, protective garments

**Commonest drug** — **calcium channel blocker** (nifedipine; oral only)

**Other drugs** — ASA, **sildenafil** / PDE inhibitors, intra-arterial reserpine, dipyridamole, pentoxifylline, prostaglandins, ACE-I/ARB, TCAs, SSRIs, α-antagonists

### Topical vasodilator mechanism

**Topical nitrate** — **only topical drug** used; **↑ cGMP in vascular smooth muscle** → vasodilation

**Not topical** — NSAID (COX), steroid (PLA2), lidocaine (Na⁺), CCB (L-type Ca²⁺ — oral only)

### Botulinum toxin – mechanism, outcomes & pre-treatment testing

**Indication** — **failed medical management** + ischaemia/pain; **ulcers not a contraindication**; **off-label**

**Mechanism** — **cleaves pre-synaptic SNAREs → ↓ ACh release** → ↑ digital perfusion

**Other proposed** — blocks **α2 receptor recruitment** on vascular smooth muscle · ↓ **substance P & glutamate** (pain) · inhibits **RhoA kinase** → ↓ smooth-muscle constriction · may ↓ sympathetic tone

**Outcomes** — **all of**: ulcer healing, ↓ pain, ↓ cold intolerance, ↑ digital blood flow

**Absolute contraindications** — **pregnancy / breastfeeding** → **urine pregnancy test before injection** · anaphylactic allergy · active infection · aminoglycosides / anticholinergics

**Caution** — respiratory disease, myasthenia gravis

✗ Wrong options: **Traps** — proprioception (non-depolarising blockers), Ca²⁺ channel excitability (gabapentin/pregabalin), muscle strengthening

![Botox injection method](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob6098-method-of-botox-injection.jpg)

### Periarterial sympathectomy indications

**Indication** — **Raynaud's failed medical Rx + ischaemic digital ulcer** / impending gangrene; esp. collagen vascular / occlusive disease

**Technique** — **periarterial (digital)** = commonest; strip **adventitia + sympathetic fibres**; may extend to forearm

**Not indicated** — **calcific arterial disease** · Buerger's in **active smoker** · CRPS (stellate block instead) · diabetic Charcot · TOS

**Thoracoscopic sympathectomy** — **out of favour** → **rebound hypersympathetic response**

**Microvascular reconstruction** — rare: **small-segment occlusion of one major artery**, other spared

## 6 Complications

**Digital ischaemia** — ulceration, **gangrene → amputation**

## How it's asked

**"BTX mechanism in Raynaud's"** → **cleaves pre-synaptic SNAREs → ↓ ACh → ↑ perfusion**

**"BTX outcomes in vasospastic disorders"** → **all of the above**

**"Before repeat Botox in scleroderma patient"** → **urine pregnancy test**\
✗ Wrong options: HbA1c, sympathectomy, CXR
