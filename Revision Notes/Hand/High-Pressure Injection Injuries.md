# High-Pressure Injection Injuries

*Hand · 39 / 103*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Incidence** — rare; **1 in 600** hand traumas

**Typical patient** — **young male laborer**; paint, grease, solvents, diesel

**Commonest digit** — **non-dominant index finger** (>50%; 84% non-dominant hand in Bean series)

### Pathophysiology

**Force** — **3,000–10,000 PSI**, up to 400 MPH

**Skin breach** — **100 PSI** breaks skin\
✗ Wrong options: 80 PSI

**4 components** — initial injury → **chemical irritation** → inflammation → **secondary infection**

**Spread** — along planes of least resistance = **neurovascular bundles**\
✗ Wrong options: flexor sheath

**Vascular occlusion** — local soft-tissue necrosis, thrombosis, ischaemia → compartment-syndrome-like picture

## 2 Mechanism

### Injected substance & amputation risk

| Substance | Destructiveness |
|---|---|
| **Water, air** | Least; non-toxic → only ones allowing nonop trial |
| **Latex / water-based paint** | Low |
| Grease, chlorofluorocarbon | Intermediate; aggressive debridement restores reasonable function |
| **Oil-based paint** | Highly inflammatory → chronic inflammation → amputation despite early surgery |
| **Organic solvents** (paint thinner, diesel, jet fuel, gasoline, oil) | **Highest amputation risk, up to 50%** |

**Most soft-tissue damage long term** — **oil-based paint**

**Highest amputation risk** — **paint thinner / organic solvents** (some authors: primary amputation)

**Temperature** — **high temperature → worse**\
✗ Wrong options: room temperature

**Organic solvents & infection** — may ↓ infection (do not support bacterial growth)

## 3 Presentation

**History** — document **time since injury** (average ~9 h to physician) + **material injected**

**Entry wound** — **benign-looking**, minimal/no pain; only **1 in 4** present to ED initially

**Entry wound size** — does not correlate with severity (>3 cm wound not prognostic)

**Later** — pain + paresthesias; finger **bloated, oedematous, tense, pale, cold**

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

**Diagnosis** — **clinical**: history + inspection

### Key radiology findings

**Radiographs** — show spread of **radio-opaque paint/dyes**; often unremarkable

![Radiograph: radio-opaque paint tracking](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob12104-radiograph.jpg)

## 5 Management

```
High-pressure injection
├─ Paint / grease / solvent / oil → emergent OR debridement + IV antibiotics + tetanus
│      └─ repeat I&D 24–72 h; admit
└─ Water or air → admit, IV antibiotics, tetanus, elevation, observe
       └─ compartment syndrome / worsening pain → emergent debridement of entire zone + fasciotomy
```

### Management – urgent debridement, antibiotics & decompression

**First line (even 3 days late)** — **immediate debridement in the OR**\
✗ Wrong options: bedside I&D with digital block · IV then oral abx · amputation · clinic referral

**Paint thinner, swollen finger** — **surgical debridement & lavage**\
✗ Wrong options: observation · corticosteroids · neutralising agent · antibiotics alone

**Neutralising agents** — only for **hydrofluoric acid** or chemo extravasation

**Steroids & infection** — do not affect amputation rate

**Water injection + ↓ 2PD, swelling, erythema into palm, can't move** — **emergent operative decompression**\
✗ Wrong options: outpatient because water is non-toxic · because perfused · admit + IV abx only

**Water injection observed → escalating pain** — compartment syndrome → **immediate debridement of entire zone of injury**\
✗ Wrong options: entry site only · cultures · cold compress · amputation

**Antibiotics** — **give before surgery**, don't delay; broad-spectrum **gram + and gram −**

**Analgesia** — **no digital block** and **no ice** (↓ perfusion)

**Nonop care** — tetanus, parenteral abx, elevation, early mobilisation, compartment monitoring; indicated only for **air, water, chicken vaccine**

**Nonop outcome** — **50%** ultimately need surgery; delay → ↑ reoperation & complications

**Approach** — **Bruner** palmar or **midaxial** incisions → full exposure of zone

**Technique** — wide debridement, **decompress compartments**, open tendon sheaths, remove all material, saline irrigation, **repeat I&D 24–72 h**

**Post-op** — **admit** for observation, possible second debridement/fasciotomy

### Prognostic factors

**Worst prognostic variable (MCQ)** — **delay in surgical treatment**

| Factor | Key fact |
|---|---|
| **Time to treatment** | Debride **<6 h** → ↓ amputation & complications; **>10 h** → ↑ amputation |
| Force of injection | ↑ pressure → worse |
| Volume injected | ↑ volume → worse |
| **Composition** | Solvents / oil-based paints worst |
| Temperature | Hot → worse |
| Location | Tendon sheath involvement, proximal spread → worse |

## 6 Complications

**Amputation** — **48%** of finger injuries; ~50% with oil-based paint / organic solvents; Hogan series 30%

**Infection** — **42% positive cultures, mostly polymicrobial**; delay ↑ risk; necrotic tissue = culture medium

**Long term** — residual pain, stiffness, ↓ sensation, difficulty returning to work

## How it's asked

**"Most soft-tissue damage long term?"** → **oil-based paint**\
✗ Wrong options: water, latex, grease, CFC

**"Highest amputation risk?"** → **paint thinner** (organic solvent)

**"Worst prognostic variable?"** → **delay in surgery**\
✗ Wrong options: wound >3 cm · grease · room temp

**"Presents 3 days later, first-line?"** → **immediate OR debridement**

**"Paint thinner, swollen finger — next step?"** → **surgical debridement & lavage**

**"Water gun, ↓ 2PD, can't move finger?"** → **emergent operative decompression**

**"Observed water injection, now severe pain?"** → **debride entire zone of injury**

**"True statement?"** → **antibiotics before surgery**\
✗ Wrong options: 80 PSI · digital block · tracks flexor sheath · no timing difference

**"Pressure that breaks skin?"** → **100 PSI**

**"Which may be managed nonop?"** → **water, air**
