# Lumbrical Plus Finger

*Hand · 49 / 103*

## 1 Basics

### Anatomy – lumbricals

| Lumbrical | Pennation | Nerve | Origin |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1st | **Unipennate** | **Median** | Radial side FDP2 |
| 2nd | **Unipennate** | **Median** | Radial side FDP3 |
| 3rd | **Bipennate** | **Ulnar** | FDP3 & FDP4 |
| 4th | **Bipennate** | **Ulnar** | FDP4 & FDP5 |

**Insertion** — all on **radial** side of extensor expansion (lateral bands)

**Commonest digit** — **middle finger** (2nd lumbrical)

**Why middle** — FDP 3/4/5 share a **common muscle belly**; distal FDP3 cut → FDP shifts **ulnarly** (pull of bipennate 3rd lumbrical) → tightens 2nd lumbrical → amplifies effect

**Why not index** — **independent FDP belly**; can fist with FDS2 alone → avoids paradoxical extension

## 2 Mechanism

### Cause – amputation at DIP level

**Definition** — **paradoxical IP (PIP + DIP) extension** on attempted finger flexion

**Pathoanatomy** — lumbrical arises from FDP → FDP divided distal to lumbrical origin → FDP contraction pulls lumbrical → pulls **lateral bands** → IP extension

| Cause | Note |
|---|---|
| **FDP disruption distal to lumbrical origin** | **Most common**: transection or **avulsion** (jersey finger) |
| **DIP amputation** | Distal to central slip insertion → PIP extends on flexion |
| Amputation through **middle phalanx shaft** | |
| **"Too long" tendon graft** | Excess flexor graft length |

**✗ DIP amputation distractors** — central slip rupture, swan neck, boutonniere, **quadrigia** (FDP advanced >1 cm → adjacent digit flexion weakness, common muscle belly)

![Pathoanatomy of lumbrical plus](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob6017-lp.jpg)

## 3 Presentation

**History** — volar digital laceration (FDP transection) or axial traction on flexed digit (FDP avulsion)

**Symptom** — one digit **sticks out / catches on clothes** when gripping or making a fist

**Exam** — paradoxical IP extension with grip ("**fingers extend while holding a beer can**")

**Diagnosis** — clinical: one digit's IPs extend when fisting all fingers

![Lumbrical plus finger on grip](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob6017-lumbrical-plus.jpg)

## 5 Management

```
Lumbrical plus finger
├─ Minimal symptoms → observe
├─ FDP laceration, repairable → tenodesis FDP to terminal tendon / reinsert to distal phalanx
└─ FDP retracted / segmental loss → lumbrical release at base of flexor sheath (palm)
```

**Tenodesis / reinsertion** — for FDP lacerations; **do not suture flexor–extensor mechanisms over bone**

**Lumbrical release** — **transect in palm at base of flexor sheath** → abolishes paradoxical PIP extension

**No acute routine release** — phenomenon not consistent enough

**Contraindication** — **do not divide lumbricals 1 & 2 with ulnar nerve palsy** (interossei also lost → no IP extensors left)

## X vs Y

| | **Lumbrical plus** | **Quadrigia** |
|---|---|---|
| Cause | FDP too loose / cut distal to lumbrical, long graft, DIP amputation | FDP **too tight** (advanced >1 cm) |
| Sign | Paradoxical IP extension on fisting | Adjacent fingers can't fully flex, weak grip |
| Fix | Lumbrical release in palm | Release tethered FDP |

## How it's asked

**"Complication of DIP-level amputation?"** → **lumbrical plus finger**\
✗ Wrong options: quadrigia, swan neck, boutonniere, central slip rupture

**"Paradoxical PIP extension on making a fist?"** → **lumbrical plus**

**"Where to release the lumbrical?"** → **palm, base of flexor sheath**

**"Most common finger?"** → **middle (2nd lumbrical)**

**"Don't release lumbricals 1 & 2 when?"** → **ulnar nerve palsy**
