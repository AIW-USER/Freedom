# Congenital Trigger Thumb

*Hand · 16 / 103*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology & pathoanatomy

**Prevalence** — **3 per 1,000** by age 1; **♂ = ♀**; **25% bilateral**

**Separate entity** — distinct from adult acquired trigger thumb (and from pediatric trigger finger)

**"Congenital" = misnomer** — usually **acquired / developmental**; not noted at birth; rare AD reports (variable penetrance)

**Pathoanatomy** — **thickened FPL** (collagen degeneration + synovial proliferation) → ↑ diameter vs **A1 pulley** → abnormal gliding

### Thumb pulleys

|---|---|---|

| **B** | **A1 pulley** | **✓ site of triggering** |

## 3 Presentation

**Typical** — **fixed flexion deformity of thumb IP joint** (not triggering), painless; rare trauma or family Hx

**Notta's node (TO)** — prominent **FPL tendon nodule** at **base of thumb / over MCP** = **pediatric trigger thumb**

**Exam** — loss of passive IP extension, **unchanged by wrist/MCP flexion**; no intrinsic IP contracture; NV intact; ± MCP hyperextension\
✗ Wrong options: FPL shortening

**Natural history** — triggering → **fixed contracture**; spontaneous resolution unlikely **after age 2**

![Notta's node outlined](../../MCQ/attachments/q4757-c-fig-c-nodule-new.jpg)

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

**Diagnosis** — **clinical**; imaging not required

**X-ray** — only if **trauma history** (AP/lateral hand ± thumb views) → **usually normal** (IP flexion, no osseous lesion)

## 5 Management

```
Pediatric trigger thumb
├─ Flexible, <2 y → passive extension exercises + intermittent extension SPLINTING
├─ Triggering (not locked), <1 y → OBSERVE ~6 mo
└─ Fixed >12 mo / locked 6 mo at age 2 / failed nonop → OPEN A1 PULLEY RELEASE
```

### Diagnosis (Notta's node) & A1 pulley release timing

**First line, flexible, 1-y-old (TO)** — **passive extension exercises + intermittent extension splinting**\
✗ Wrong options: release · exercises alone

**Splinting (TO)** — **more successful than observation / exercises alone**; IP hyperextension, block MCP hyperextension; **6–12 wk** (several months; not 30 days); high drop-out

| Treatment | Resolution |
|---|---|
| Passive exercises / observation <2 y (TO) | **30–60%** |
| Spontaneous after 2 y | **<10%** |
| Splinting + exercises (TO) | **50–60%** |
| Nonop after 3 y (OB) | **50%** |
| Baek: no treatment, 48 mo | 60% |
| **A1 release** | **65–95%** (Orthobullets) · **95%** (OB) |

**Delay to age 2** — **no sequelae**\
✗ Wrong options: permanent contracture · motor delay · hand dominance change (handedness ~4 y)

### Operative criteria

| Source | Indication for A1 release |
|---|---|
| Orthobullets / TO | **Fixed deformity >12 mo** or failed nonop |
| Shah | Fixed >12 mo; consider surgery **after 2 y** |
| Marek | **Locked for 6 mo at age 2 → surgery**; triggering <1 y → observe 6 mo |
| OB explanation | **>3 y** or failed splinting / stretching |
| Dittmer | **Stage IV → immediate surgery** (4.6× surgery risk); stage II–III observe ≥1 y |

**Technique** — **open** release (gold standard); transverse incision MCP flexion crease; **protect radial digital nerve**; watch nodule glide on passive IP extension

**Avoid percutaneous** — incomplete release, radial digital n. injury, FPL laceration; no advantage (GA needed anyway)

**Not indicated** — nodule resection · volar plate release · FPL lengthening · steroid injection (difficult, no benefit)

**6-y fixed IP flexion + nodule** — **complete A1 release**\
✗ Wrong options: no treatment (won't resolve)

## 6 Complications

**Digital nerve injury** — high risk (close to tendon/A1)

**Bowstringing** — from **oblique pulley release**

**Others** — wound (scar contracture, abscess, infection), IP flexion deficit; Marek: no major complications/recurrence
