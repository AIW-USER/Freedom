# Scaphoid Lunate Advanced Collapse (SLAC)

*Hand · 80 / 103*

## 1 Basics

### SL dissociation – pathomechanics

**Definition** — predictable degenerative arthritis from **chronic SL dissociation**; **most common form of wrist arthritis** (Watson & Ballet 1984)

**Causes** — SL ligament tear (most common cause of carpal instability); also **crystalline arthropathy**

**SLIL** — C-shaped: dorsal, proximal (fibrocartilage), volar; **dorsal = thickest (3 mm) & strongest** → **most critical for SL stability** (volar ~1 mm)

**Not key SL stabilisers** — RSC (sectioning with intact SLIL → no kinematic change), DRC, DIC, volar SLIL

**SL dissociation** — **scaphoid flexes + lunate extends** = **DISI**; SL angle **>70°**, lunate >10° extended

**Why radiolunate spared** — lunate stays **concentric** in spheroidal lunate fossa in all positions of rotation

## 2 Mechanism

### Stages & sequence of arthritis (radial styloid, radioscaphoid, capitolunate; radiolunate spared)

**Sequence** — **radial styloid–scaphoid → entire radioscaphoid → capitolunate** (capitate migrates proximally into SL gap, subluxes dorsally on lunate)

**First joint affected** — **radioscaphoid** (radial styloid tip)

**Spared** — **radiolunate** (and lunotriquetral)

## 3 Presentation

**Symptoms** — pain at SL interval, difficulty weight-bearing through wrist, stiffness, progressive weakness

**Signs** — dorsal SL tenderness, ↓ ROM, ↓ grip

**Watson scaphoid shift** — pressure on **scaphoid tuberosity**, wrist **ulnar → radial deviation** → scaphoid subluxes dorsally (pain) → clunk on release; **positive early only** (arthritis stabilises scaphoid later); compare sides

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

**Views** — PA + lateral; stress views and MRI unnecessary for staging

**SL diastasis (PA)** — **>3 mm** (Orthobullets) · **>2 mm** + asymmetric to other side (OB) — read the options

**PA by stage** — I: **radial styloid beaking**, sclerosis · II: narrowing whole scaphoid fossa · III: capitolunate narrowing, capitate migrates proximally

**Lateral** — **DISI**, capitate subluxed dorsally on lunate

**MRI** — thinning of proximal scaphoid / scaphoid facet / capitolunate cartilage, synovitis

## 5 Management

```
SLAC wrist
├─ Mild → NSAIDs, splint, steroid injection
├─ Stage I → radial styloidectomy / AIN + PIN denervation
├─ Stage II → PRC or scaphoid excision + 4CF
│      (smoker / fewer complications → PRC)
├─ Stage III (capitolunate) → scaphoid excision + 4CF or wrist fusion
└─ Radiolunate / pancarpal → total wrist fusion
```

### Operative criteria by joint status

| Procedure | Needs intact | Contraindicated by |
|---|---|---|
| **PRC** | **Radiolunate facet + capitolunate (capitate head)** + **RSC** | **Capitolunate arthritis (stage III)**, incompetent RSC |
| **Scaphoid excision + 4CF** (capitate–lunate–triquetrum–hamate) | **Radiolunate facet** | **Radiolunate arthritis** |
| Total wrist fusion | — | — (best pain relief + grip, loses motion) |

**Not contraindications** — radioscaphoid arthritis, capitolunate arthritis (for 4CF), SL disruption, scaphoid osteonecrosis

### Stage I – styloidectomy & denervation

**Radial styloidectomy** — open or arthroscopic (1-2 portal); removes scaphoid–styloid impingement

**Denervation** — **PIN + AIN** (distal branches = proprioception/capsule only) ± dorsal ulnar sensory, radial sensory, LABC, palmar cutaneous; **↓ pain, no ↑ function/strength/ROM**; can add to bony procedures for II–III

### Salvage – four-corner fusion vs PRC (capitolunate arthritis contraindication, RSC ligament)

**PRC** — excise scaphoid + lunate + triquetrum; capitate sits in lunate fossa; **preserve RSC** = **primary stabiliser after PRC** (prevents ulnar translocation)\
✗ Wrong options: DRC, DIC, ulnocapitate, ulnotriquetral

**4CF + scaphoidectomy** — motion through radiolunate joint; **only option addressing all stage III sites**

**PRC vs 4CF (stage II)** — similar long-term results; **PRC = fewer complications**, technically easier, no prolonged immobilisation → **preferred in smokers** (Tomaino)\
✗ Wrong options: nonunion, hardware, dorsal impingement

**Not for SLAC** — STT fusion (SL instability, STT OA, Kienböck) · scaphocapitate fusion (Kienböck) · **distal pole excision (SNAC)** · RSL fusion · **TWA** (young/active, e.g. farmer; OK for older low-demand / RA)

**Total wrist fusion** — pancarpal / radiolunate arthritis, failed PRC; less suitable in a 65-year-old needing motion

### Radioscapholunate fusion – distal scaphoid excision

**RSL fusion** — radioscaphoid + radiolunate OA with **preserved midcarpal joint**

**Add distal scaphoid pole excision (~20%)** — ↑ ROM (dart-thrower's motion), ↓ strain → **↑ fusion rate**; ± triquetrum excision

**Without it** — nonunion, poor motion (esp. radial deviation), weak grip

## How it's asked

**"First joint affected in SLAC?"** → **radioscaphoid**\
✗ Wrong options: capitolunate · radiolunate

**"Stage III SLAC joint?"** → **capitolunate**

**"Order of degeneration?"** → **radial styloid → radioscaphoid → capitolunate**

**"Stage III — arthritis absent between?"** → **lunate and radius**

**"Stage III, preserve motion?"** → **scaphoid excision + 4CF**\
✗ Wrong options: PRC · STT · neurectomy · wrist fusion

**"Primary stabiliser after PRC?"** → **radioscaphocapitate**

**"Stage III SLAC treatment?"** → **scaphoid excision + capitate-lunate-triquetrum-hamate arthrodesis**

**"Stage II, smoker, lowest complication risk?"** → **PRC**\
✗ Wrong options: 4CF · styloidectomy · TWA

**"Stage III, farmer, failed injections?"** → **scaphoid excision + 4CF**\
✗ Wrong options: scaphocapitate · distal pole excision · TWA

**"Degeneration that contraindicates PRC?"** → **capitate–lunate**

**"Contraindication to 4CF?"** → **radiolunate arthritis**

**"RSL fusion nonunion + stiff — what step was missed?"** → **distal scaphoid pole excision**

**"Most critical structure for SL stability?"** → **dorsal SLIL**

**"Result of SL dissociation?"** → **scaphoid flexion + lunate extension (DISI)**
