# Carpal Instability Nondissociative (CIND)

*Hand · 10 / 103*

## 1 Basics

### Definition & epidemiology

**CIND (TO)** — instability **between carpal rows** (midcarpal) or **between radius and carpus** (radiocarpal) **without intrinsic (SLIL/LTIL) tear**; each row intact, **extrinsic ligament** insufficiency

**Not CIND** — ligament disruption within a single row (= CID) · trans-scaphoid perilunate (= CIC) · DR malunion tilt alone

**Types** — **midcarpal** (proximal ↔ distal row; **most common CIND**, TO) · **radiocarpal** (radius ↔ proximal row; "inferior arc injury")

**Incidence** — rare, **<1 per 100,000/y**

**Associations** — SL/LT injury, **acute CTS**, compartment syndrome

### Extrinsic ligament anatomy

| Ligament | Origin → insertion | Role (TO) | Failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Radioscaphocapitate (RSC)** | Radial styloid → scaphoid waist + capitate | Fulcrum / sling for scaphoid | Scaphoid instability |
| **Long radiolunate (LRL)** | Radial styloid → volar lunate + triquetrum | **Primary stabiliser vs ulnar translocation** | **Ulnar drift of carpus** (RA) |
| Short radiolunate (SRL) | Ulnar notch rim → volar-ulnar lunate | Lunate anchor | Lunate dislocation |
| Ulnolunate / ulnotriquetral | TFCC → lunate / triquetrum | Ulnar column | **VISI** |
| Arcuate (triquetrohamatocapitate) | Palmar midcarpal | Midcarpal kinematics | **Midcarpal instability** |

**Space of Poirier (TO)** — weak area between RC and LRL over capitolunate joint → carpus dissociates here in perilunate (Mayfield II); lunate stays on SRL

## 2 Mechanism

**Midcarpal** — lax **palmar arcuate ligament** + dorsal radiocarpal ligaments (TO); usually **no trauma**, **generalised laxity**

**Radiocarpal** — high energy; ulnar translation = **global extrinsic rupture**; ± radial/ulnar styloid fractures

**Commonest cause of radiocarpal instability** — **distal radius malunion** (Orthobullets) · ulnar translocation commonly **RA / RSC disruption** (TO) — read the options

**Dorsal radiocarpal instability (TO)** — DR malunion with **loss of volar tilt**

## 3 Presentation

**Symptoms** — wrist **giving way**, painful/painless subluxation, **clunk**

**Midcarpal shift test (TO)** — stabilise forearm, **axial + palmar load**, wrist **neutral → ulnar deviation** (pronation) → painful **"catch-up clunk"** as proximal row snaps **VISI (flexed) → neutral/extended**

(✗ Wrong options: **Distractor exams** — radial pain with ulnar deviation (**Finkelstein**)) · tenderness distal to ulnar styloid (**fovea sign**) · clunk radial deviation + scaphoid pressure (**Watson**) · lunate pain with dorsal pressure (**Kienböck**)

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

**Static X-ray** — AP + lateral; often **normal or mild VISI**

**Dynamic fluoroscopy / cineradiography** — **diagnostic** (TO); sudden jump of proximal row **flexion → extension** with ulnar deviation

**Ulnar translation** — **>50% of lunate width** translated ulnarly off the lunate fossa

### Carpal instability classification

| Type | Lesion | Example |
|---|---|---|
| CID (dissociative) | **Intrinsic** ligament, within a row | SL dissociation |
| **CIND** | **Extrinsic**, between rows / radius–carpus | **Midcarpal instability**, ulnar translocation |
| CIC (complex) | CID + CIND | Perilunate injury |
| CIA (adaptive) | Extracarpal cause | Madelung, DR malunion |

## 5 Management

```
CIND
├─ Midcarpal → splint + FCU/ECU proprioceptive rehab (first line)
│      └─ refractory → capsular plication / reefing → midcarpal fusion
├─ Radiocarpal acute + styloid fractures → immediate open repair + pinning
├─ DR malunion → corrective osteotomy
└─ Failed above → wrist arthrodesis
```

**Nonop** — first line; **midcarpal most amenable to splinting**; rehab FCU + ECU (dynamic stabilisers, TO), activity modification, NSAIDs

**Midcarpal surgery (TO)** — thermal capsulorrhaphy (controversial, capsular necrosis) · open plication of arcuate ligament / dorsal capsule · reefing (Apergis: 13/14 good–excellent) · **limited midcarpal arthrodesis (4-corner / triquetrohamate)** · total wrist fusion if intractable

**Midcarpal fusion** — **preferred over ligament reconstruction** and for late diagnosis failing nonop; **20–35% loss of motion**

**Radiocarpal** — immediate open repair/reduction/pinning if **ulnar translation + styloid fractures**; **late repair poor**; **ligament reconstruction poor long term**

**Ulnar translocation in RA (TO)** — radiocarpal or total wrist arthrodesis

**Radiocarpal arthritis + functional midcarpal joint** — **radioscapholunate (RSL) fusion + distal scaphoid excision** (↑ ROM)

**Wrist arthrodesis** — salvage; radiocarpal fusion → **55–60% loss of motion**

**Not for isolated radiocarpal arthritis** — 4-corner fusion (radiocarpal motion still painful) · capitolunate fusion (midcarpal OK) · TWA (young manual labourer, fails) · total fusion (salvage only)

## 6 Complications

**Prognosis** — **volar dislocation worse than dorsal**; late repair and ligament reconstruction poor

## X vs Y

| | **Midcarpal CIND** | **Radiocarpal CIND** |
|---|---|---|
| Frequency | **Most common** | Less common |
| History | No trauma, lax | High energy / DR malunion / RA |
| Sign | **Catch-up clunk** on ulnar deviation | Ulnar translation >50% lunate |
| Key ligament | Palmar arcuate | **LRL** / RSC |
| Surgery | Plication → **midcarpal fusion** | Open repair, osteotomy, RSL / wrist fusion |

## How it's asked

**"Defining feature of CIND?"** → **instability between rows / radius–carpus without intrinsic tear** (TO)

**"Clunk on ulnar deviation, proximal row jumps flexion → extension, no SL/LT dissociation?"** → **CIND – midcarpal instability** (TO)

**"Most diagnostic exam for midcarpal instability?"** → **clunk with axial + palmar load, neutral → ulnar deviation**\
✗ Wrong options: Watson · Finkelstein · fovea

**"Primary volar restraint to ulnar translocation?"** → **long radiolunate ligament** (TO)\
✗ Wrong options: RSC · SRL · ulnolunate

**"Radioscaphoid + radiolunate arthritis, preserve motion?"** → **RSL fusion + distal scaphoid excision**

**"Diagnostic investigation?"** → **dynamic fluoroscopy**
