# AIN Compressive Neuropathy

*Hand · 2 / 103*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology & aetiology

**Eponym** — **Kiloh-Nevin syndrome** = AIN compression (TO) (✗ Wrong options: PIN compression (TO))

**Incidence** — rare; **<1%** of median neuropathies

**Commonest cause** — **transient neuritis (Parsonage-Turner)**; non-traumatic AIN palsy usually = **neuritis** (viral, autoimmune)

**Parsonage-Turner** — viral **brachial neuritis** → **bilateral AIN signs** (TO); preceded by **intense shoulder pain + viral prodrome**

**Compression sites** — **deep head of pronator teres** (most common anatomical, TO) · **FDS arch** (TO) · **edge of lacertus fibrosus** (TO) · **Gantzer's muscle** = accessory head FPL (TO) · thrombosed radial/ulnar/AI artery · accessory FDS→FDP muscle · FCRB, palmaris profundus

**Surgical finding (Park)** — commonest compressive structure at exploration = **fibrous band of FDS**; 4/11 had **no compression**

![Gantzer's muscle](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob6019-gantzer-39-s-muscle.jpg)

### AIN motor distribution (FPL, FDP index)

**Origin** — **terminal motor branch of median n.**; ~**4 cm distal to medial epicondyle** (Orthobullets) · **5–8 cm distal to lateral epicondyle** (OB/Orthobullets); dorsoradial aspect of median n.

**Course** — between FDS & FDP → between **FPL & FDP** → on **interosseous membrane** with AI artery → **pronator quadratus** → wrist capsule (radiocarpal, intercarpal, DRUJ)

**Innervates (3)** — **FPL** · **FDP index + middle** · **pronator quadratus**

**Sensory** — **none** cutaneous (articular branches only)

**Not AIN** — **pronator teres** (median, before AIN leaves) · FCR (median) · FCU (ulnar) · FDB (foot, medial plantar)

![AIN lineage](../../MCQ/attachments/q9008-illa-ain-lineage.jpg)

### Martin-Gruber anastomosis

**MGA** — **motor** connection **median/AIN → ulnar** in **proximal 1/3 forearm**; sensory spared; **most common forearm anomalous innervation**

**Prevalence** — **15%** (Orthobullets) · **15–40%** (OB) · pooled **19.5%**, 57.6% from AIN (meta-analysis); more often **right**

**Supplies** — **adductor pollicis + dorsal interossei** (intrinsics) → complete AIN palsy may show **intrinsic weakness**

**High ulnar laceration + intrinsics work** — **Martin-Gruber**

**MGA type II** — **most common**; median fibres → **1st dorsal interosseous** → median laceration = median deficit **+ cannot abduct index**; ulnar sensation preserved

**MGA II on NCS** — usually **normal** (1st DI not routinely tested) → careful exam

## 3 Presentation

**Hallmark** — **motor deficit without sensory loss** (TO); **normal median sensory exam**

**OK sign** — **cannot make OK sign** = Kiloh-Nevin sign (TO) → weak **thumb IP flexion (FPL)** + **index DIP flexion (FDP)**

**Pain** — vague deep forearm pain, often **absent**; if present lasts **2–3 wk**

**PQ test** — weak resisted pronation with **elbow maximally flexed** (neutralises pronator teres)

**Tenodesis** — **intact tenodesis** = tendons intact → distinguishes from **FPL attritional rupture** (RA)

**Severe** — forearm atrophy

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

**Diagnosis** — **clinical + EMG/NCS**

### Key radiology findings

**MRI** — if **mass** suspected; T2/STIR ↑ signal FPL, FDP, PQ → **PQ oedema = most reliable**

**EMG** — **fibrillations, positive sharp waves** in **FPL + FDP index/middle**; rules out proximal lesion, grades severity, tracks recovery

**Differentials** — flexor tendon rupture · proximal lesion (C-spine, plexus) · **pronator syndrome** · CTS · conversion disorder (not if objective EMG findings)

## 5 Management

```
AIN palsy
├─ No mass / no trauma → OBSERVATION, rest, PT (12 mo)
│      └─ no recovery at 12 mo → SURGICAL DECOMPRESSION
├─ Space-occupying mass → DECOMPRESSION + excision
└─ Penetrating injury → EXPLORE + primary repair
```

### AIN syndrome – diagnosis & observation (neuritis)

**First line** — **observation, rest, PT** in all without a mass; treatment mostly **non-surgical** (TO); splinting at 90° (Rodner) · massage, forearm stretches

**Recovery** — begins **3–12 mo**; full by **18 mo** (mean ~9 mo) → "**gradual improvement over 1 y**" · "**persistent sx <2 y then spontaneous resolution**"

**Better prognosis** — **age <40** (faster recovery)

**Not indicated** — steroid injection (no data) · CTR · early tendon/nerve transfer

### Operative criteria

| Indication | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Clear **space-occupying mass** | Early decompression |
| Failed nonop | **12 mo** (Orthobullets, Chi & Harness) · **6 mo** in complete palsy (Sood & Burke) |
| **Penetrating injury** | Explore + **end-to-end repair** |
| Age <40 | Surgery controversial (uniform nonop response) |

**Surgical success** — ~**75%**

**Decompression** — lazy-S proximal volar forearm; release superficial head PT, **lacertus fibrosus**, FDS arch, **Gantzer's**, ligate crossing vessels; elbow ROM day 2

## 6 Complications

**Persistent AIN deficit** — rare (~5–10 cases) → **tendon transfer: brachioradialis → FPL**

## X vs Y

| | **AIN syndrome** | Pronator syndrome | PIN syndrome | FPL rupture |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sensory | **Normal** | Radial 3½ + thenar | Normal | Normal |
| Motor | FPL, FDP-I/M, PQ | ± | **Thumb extension/retropulsion**, finger ext | FPL only |
| Pain | Absent / 2–3 wk | **Proximal forearm** | Lateral elbow | — |
| Tenodesis | **Intact** | Intact | Intact | **Lost** |
| Release | Gantzer's, FDS arch | **Lacertus fibrosus** | **Arcade of Frohse** | — |

## How it's asked

**"Kiloh-Nevin — all true EXCEPT"** → **compression of PIN** (TO)

**"Parsonage-Turner + weak pronation, no sensory loss"** → **AIN syndrome → observation**\
✗ Wrong options: Gantzer release

**"Fibs in FPL + FDP-I after flu, no numbness — next step?"** → **observation, improves over 1 y**

**"Weak thumb IP flexion, normal 2PD, normal MRI — course?"** → **resolves spontaneously <2 y**

**"Stab proximal forearm, no index DIP flexion — what else fails?"** → **FPL**\
✗ Wrong options: pronator teres, FCR

**"High ulnar laceration, intrinsics still work"** → **Martin-Gruber**

**"Antecubital laceration, median deficit + no index abduction"** → **Martin-Gruber type II**

**"Most reliable MRI sign"** → **PQ oedema**

**"Persistent AIN palsy — transfer?"** → **brachioradialis → FPL**
