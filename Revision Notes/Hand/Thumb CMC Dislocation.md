# Thumb CMC Dislocation

*Hand · 90 / 103*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Incidence** — **<1% of hand injuries**; **dorsal (dorsoradial) ≫ volar**

### Stabilisers – dorsoradial ligament & anterior oblique ligament

**Ligaments** — 16 total; **7 TMCJ stabilisers** (sAOL, dAOL, DRL, POL, UCL, IML, DIML) + 9 trapezial

**4 key restraints** — **AOL**, POL, IML, **DRL**

| Ligament | Fact asked |
|---|---|
| **Dorsoradial (DRL)** | **Primary stabiliser vs dorsal/dorsoradial subluxation** in all positions; strongest & thickest; "check rein"; taut first; joint stays reduced if all others cut; **torn in dorsal dislocation**; lax in stage IV OA |
| Superficial AOL | does not stabilise in flexion or prevent dorsal subluxation; laxity allows pronation |
| **Deep AOL (beak)** | Volar trapezial tubercle → volar beak of MC; **pivot for pronation**; taut only in **hitchhiker** position; **stripped/peeled off MC base but continuous** in dislocation; stays on volar fragment in Bennett/Rolando |
| Posterior oblique (POL) | Purely dorsal, ulnar to DRL; not primary |
| Intermetacarpal / dorsal IML | **Restrains radial translation** of 1st MC |
| Trapeziotrapezoid | Stabilises TT joint, not CMC |

**Historical vs current** — AOL once thought primary; **DRL now accepted primary** (biomechanical studies)

## 2 Mechanism

**Commonest** — **axial force on flexed thumb**

**Less common** — dorsal force into 1st web (motorcycle handlebar)

**Pathoanatomy** — **DRL torn**; **AOL stripped from MC base, remains continuous**

## 3 Presentation

**Findings** — thenar pain, swelling, bruising; cannot make a fist

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Modality | Fact |
|---|---|
| X-ray PA + lateral | Joint-space widening, **dorsoradial shift of MC** |
| **Roberts view** | Base-of-thumb fractures |
| MRI | Persistent/recurrent instability; plan reconstruction |

## 5 Management

```
Thumb CMC dislocation
├─ Stable after reduction (AOL intact) → CLOSED REDUCTION + SPICA IN EXTENSION + PRONATION
├─ Incongruent / unstable → CRPP ± open capsulorrhaphy
└─ Grossly unstable (AOL possibly torn) → LIGAMENT RECONSTRUCTION (TENDON AUTOGRAFT) + TEMPORARY PINS
```

### Ligament reconstruction with FCR

**Recommended for gross instability** — reconstruction + pinning → **better abduction & pinch** than CRPP

**Structure reconstructed** — **Orthobullets**: dorsal capsuloligamentous complex · **OB (Eaton-Littler, FCR graft)**: **volar oblique ligament** — read the options

**Eaton-Littler** — FCR graft; better results in **non-arthritic thumbs**; APL is used but is not the structure reconstructed

## 6 Complications

**List** — anterior osteophyte often visible · **low recurrence**

## X vs Y

| | CMC dislocation | Bennett / Rolando fracture |
|---|---|---|
| AOL | **Peeled off MC base, continuous** | **Attached to volar fragment** |
| DRL | **Torn** | — |
| MC translation | Ligamentous | Through fracture |

## How it's asked

**"Dorsal or volar? Which ligament torn?"** → **dorsal; DRL torn (AOL intact, peeled off)**

**"Stabiliser in opposition besides AOL?"** → **dorsoradial ligament**

**"Incongruent after reduction — primary restraint to dorsal subluxation?"** → **dorsoradial ligament**

**"Primary stabiliser vs dorsoradial subluxation?"** → **DRL**\
✗ Wrong options: volar oblique (old teaching) · dorsal IML (radial translation)

**"Eaton-Littler FCR reconstruction rebuilds?"** → **volar oblique ligament**\
✗ Wrong options: APL
