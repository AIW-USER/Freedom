# Peripheral Nerve Injury & Repair

*Hand · 60 / 103*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Incidence** — 16.9 / 100,000 / yr; 2.6% of UE trauma, 1.2% of LE trauma; ~3% of UE/hand injuries

**Demographics** — **♂ 80%**, age 32–39

**Risk factors** — penetrating / high-energy trauma, crush, displaced fractures

**Commonest UE nerve injury (OB)** — **ulnar nerve**, working-age males

### Nerve microanatomy (epineurium, perineurium, endoneurium)

| Layer | Covers | Function / MCQ fact |
|---|---|---|
| **Epineurium** | Group of fascicles → whole nerve | **Cushions against external pressure**; collagen + elastin; focus of surgical repair |
| **Perineurium** | **Individual fascicle** | **Tensile strength + elasticity**; blood–nerve barrier; limits diffusion (protects from oedema) |
| **Endoneurium** | **Individual axon** (+ Schwann cell, myelin) | Forms **Schwann cell tube**; nourishes axon; **must be intact for full recovery** |
| Myelin | Axon | Schwann cells; ↑ conduction velocity; saltatory at **nodes of Ranvier** |

**Cross-section labels (OB)** — A axon · B myelin · C epineurium · **D perineurium** · E endoneurium · F node of Ranvier

**✗ distractors** — fibronectin/laminin (ECM, directional branching), N-cadherin (adhesion, growing axons)

**Neuron** — cell body <10% of mass (metabolic centre); axon conducts; dendrites receive

**Blood supply** — extrinsic vessels in loose tissue + intrinsic plexus in epi-, peri-, endoneurium

## 2 Mechanism

**Stretch** — **8% elongation → ↓ microcirculation 50%**; **15% → ↓ 80%**; e.g. stingers, suprascapular n. in volleyball, CPN after TKA valgus correction

**Compression** — **30 mmHg → paresthesia**, ↑ latency; **60 mmHg → complete conduction block**; endoneurial oedema → fibrosis → ↓ gliding

**Chronic compression** — conduction change from **Schwann cell proliferation + apoptosis**; Schwann cells ↑ VEGF\
✗ Wrong options: Wallerian degeneration · axon transection · cell body apoptosis

**Compressive neuropathy (cubital tunnel, NCV 31 m/s)** — **neurapraxia of ischaemic origin**\
✗ Wrong options: axonotmesis · endoneurial disruption

**Laceration** — sharp better than crush; ends retract

| Classic injury | Nerve |
|---|---|
| Anterior shoulder dislocation | Axillary |
| **Holstein-Lewis** (distal 1/3 humerus), Saturday night palsy, extension SCH | Radial |
| Distal humerus ORIF, flexion SCH, OR positioning | Ulnar |
| **Extension SCH**, prominent olecranon TBW pins (anterior ulnar cortex) | AIN |
| Posterior hip dislocation | Sciatic |
| TKA valgus correction | CPN |
| Percutaneous tibial plating, holes 11–13 | Superficial peroneal |

### Regeneration physiology

**Wallerian degeneration** — **distal** to injury (TO); axoplasm + myelin degraded by **phagocytes**; proximal reaction = primary/retrograde degeneration (TO)\
✗ Wrong options: Schwann cells

**Sequence** — distal Wallerian → Schwann cells proliferate, line basement membrane → **proximal budding after ~1 month** → **antegrade** growth **1 mm/day**\
✗ Wrong options: distal → proximal · neurotrophic factors direct phagocytosis

**Advancing Tinel** — ~**1 inch/month** (TO); **most reliable sign of recovery**

**Guidance** — contact guidance (Schwann basal lamina), neurotropism, neurotrophic factors

**Order of recovery** — **sympathetic → pain → temperature → touch → proprioception → motor**; loss in reverse; **motor first lost, last to return**

**Myelin** — disrupted before axon

## 3 Presentation

**Diagnosis** — clinical (injury + deficit); EMG/NCS mainstay; exploration confirms traumatic injury

**Radial palsy (Holstein-Lewis) last to recover** — **wrist extension**\
✗ Wrong options: elbow extension: spared at this level

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Injury classification (Seddon / Sunderland)

| Seddon | Sunderland | Damaged | Wallerian | Tinel advances | Recovery |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Neurapraxia** | **1** | Focal demyelination (ischaemia); axon intact | No | No (no motor march; simultaneous proximal + distal recovery) | **Complete**, days–6 wk |
| Axonotmesis | **2** | **Axon**; endo-, peri-, epineurium intact | Yes | Yes | **Full** (intact endoneurial tube) |
| Axonotmesis | **3** | Axon + **endoneurium**; peri-/epineurium intact | Yes | Yes | **Most variable**, incomplete |
| Axonotmesis | **4** | Axon + endo- + **perineurium**; **only epineurium** intact | Yes | No | **Neuroma-in-continuity**; poor without surgery |
| Neurotmesis | **5** | All layers (transection) | Yes | No | None without repair |
| — | **6 (Mackinnon)** | Mixed degrees in one nerve | | | Mixed; neuroma in continuity (TO) |

**Sunderland 2 = axon disrupted, endoneurial tube intact** (TO); regeneration because **endoneurium** intact\
✗ Wrong options: epineurium, perineurium, myelin, Schwann cell

**Tinel progression** — **Sunderland II & III** (TO)

**Neurotmesis range** — Sunderland **5** (Orthobullets classification) · **3rd–5th** (Orthobullets treatment section, OB214026) — read the options

**Full recovery prevented by** — **endoneurial injury = neurotmesis** (structure A in figure)

### Key radiology findings

**High-resolution US** — dynamic, superficial nerves; localises entrapment, cut ends, neuromas

**MRI / MR neurography** — brachial plexus; **better for muscle denervation**, deep structures, static resolution

### Sensory receptors & electrodiagnostics

| Study | Finding | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **NCS** | ↑ latency, ↓ velocity, ↓ SNAP/CMAP | Large myelinated fibres; compression/demyelination |
| **EMG** | **Fibrillations, positive sharp waves** | **Acute denervation** (axonotmesis / neurotmesis) |
| EMG | Fasciculations, myokymia | Neurogenic |
| EMG | CRDs, myotonic discharges | Myopathy |
| **Polyphasic MUPs** | | **Early reinnervation** (TO) |
| Chronic MUPs (large amplitude, long duration) | | Established reinnervation (TO) |
| ↓ SNAP & CMAP | | Axonal loss (quantitative) (TO) |
| **↓ / absent insertional activity** | | **Poor potential for surgical reinnervation** (TO) |
| ↓ recruitment | | Loss of innervated MEPs (TO) |

**Thresholds** — DSL **>3.2 ms**, DML **>4.3 ms** abnormal; velocity **<52 m/s** (Orthobullets) · **<50 m/s across elbow** (OB); amplitude ↓ 50% vs contralateral

**SNAPs affected before CMAPs**; EMG normal until late

**Cubital tunnel NCS** — **prolonged distal sensory latency** + ↓ velocity\
✗ Wrong options: fibrillations, PSW, fasciculations = EMG · ↑ velocity

**Neurapraxia** — NCV slowing/conduction block, **no fibrillations**

**EMG value** — often only objective evidence (workers' comp)

## 5 Management

```
Nerve deficit
├─ Closed injury / neurapraxia / Sunderland 1–2 → observe + serial EMG (baseline 3–4 wk)
│      └─ no recovery by 3 mo → explore
├─ Sharp / iatrogenic / vascular injury / progressive → early exploration + repair (7–14 d)
└─ Gunshot with transection → repair at 1–3 wk (zone of injury declares)
```

```
Gap after resecting to healthy fascicles
├─ Tension-free → primary epineurial (or fascicular) repair
├─ Small sensory gap (≤20 mm) → collagen conduit / processed allograft
└─ >5 cm or motor/mixed → autograft (sural; cable if size mismatch)
```

### Surgical repair

**Repair window** — best **7–14 d**; unpredictable after **6 mo**; reinnervation by **18 mo** goal; time limit **18 mo**

**Epineurial repair** — resect proximal neuroma + distal glioma to **"mushrooming" fascicles**; align with epineurial vessels; **tension-free**; gain length by transposition/neurolysis

**Fascicular repair** — perineurial sutures; outcome **= epineurial**; ↑ scarring; matching by topography, **stimulation (proximal: sensory in awake pt; distal: motor before Wallerian)**, histology

**Direct muscular neurotization** — stump into muscle; ↓ neuroma; **TMR, RPNI** for amputees

### Timing of repair after ballistic injury

**GSW with transected radial nerve** — definitive repair **1–3 wk** after injury\
✗ Wrong options: at initial debridement · at fracture fixation · after 3 mo · wait for neuroma

**GSW without transection / closed humerus fracture** — observe **3 mo** (mostly neurapraxia); GSW brachial plexus → observe

### Repair options – conduit, allograft & autograft by gap length

| Option | Gap | Key facts |
|---|---|---|
| **Collagen conduit** | **≤20 mm** (Orthobullets, OB) · <3 cm (OB4644) · tubular wrap <1 cm (OB219234) | **Type I collagen** (= **ligaments**; not cartilage/NP II, basement membrane IV, vessels III); relies on **fibrin clot**; nutrient + neurotrophic access; **8 mm digital gap → as good as autograft** (✗ Wrong options: PGA, GTMC, silicone, end-to-end under tension) |
| **Decellular allograft** | Sensory **<3 cm**; effective up to **7 cm** | **Scaffold (microtubules, laminin) for host Schwann cell migration**; no living cells, no immunosuppression; not yet for motor/mixed |
| Non-processed allograft | | Needs immunosuppression — rarely used |
| Vein conduit | Digital | not >5 cm, not superior |
| **Autograft** | **>5 cm (50 mm)** | **Gold standard**; graft **≥10% longer** than gap; sural (palm level), MABC, LABC, terminal PIN; **cable** for mismatch; donor morbidity |

**Quality of recovery** — drops with gaps **>5 mm**

**Segmental defect, motor recovery** — **nerve autograft** best\
✗ Wrong options: allograft, conduits, vein

### Prognosis – nerve, level & order of recovery

**Favourable** — **younger age (most important)**, **distal level (2nd)**, sharp / stretch injuries

**Unfavourable** — older age, proximal level, crush/blast, delay, **ulnar nerve**, concomitant bone/soft-tissue injury, graft length

**Best motor recovery after graft (<6 mo)** — **radial**\
✗ Wrong options: median, ulnar, tibial, peroneal

**Ulnar** — poor outcomes (worse than tibial, radial, femoral, MC)

✗ Wrong options: **Prognostic factor** — **type of autograft** (sural vs saphenous); ✓ time to repair, level, length, rehab

**High median laceration + 6 cm sural cable graft — poorest recovery** — **thumb opposition** (intrinsics; endplates survive ~**12 mo**)\
✗ Wrong options: wrist flexion, finger flexion, FPL, sensation

**Critical period of delay (TO)**

| Nerve | Critical period |
|---|---|
| **Radial** | **15 mo** |
| PIN | 9 mo |
| Ulnar high / low | 9 / 15 mo |
| Median high / low | 9 / 12 mo |
| Sciatic | 12–15 mo |
| CPN | 12 mo |

### Nerve transfers – AIN to ulnar motor (SETS)

**Indication** — proximal injury: deliver axons before **motor endplate** loss; priority shoulder abduction/ER, elbow flexion, hand

| Goal | Transfer |
|---|---|
| Shoulder abduction + ER | **Spinal accessory → suprascapular** |
| Shoulder abduction | **Medial triceps branch (radial) → axillary** (Leechavengvong) |
| Elbow flexion | **Oberlin**: FCU fascicle (ulnar) → biceps branch (MC); double Oberlin adds FCR/FDS fascicle (median) → brachialis |
| Ulnar intrinsics | **Terminal AIN (from PQ) → deep motor ulnar, supercharged end-to-side (SETS)** |

**SETS purpose** — **maintain finger abduction/adduction** (interossei) while repaired ulnar nerve regrows; **motor only**\
✗ Wrong options: sensation

**Proximal forearm ulnar laceration** — **ulnar repair + end-to-side AIN → deep motor branch**\
✗ Wrong options: end-to-end: requires dividing deep branch · repair alone

**SETS contraindication** — **absent preop CMAPs** (endplates gone)\
✗ Wrong options: scarce fibs, intrinsic atrophy = indication, ↓ SNAP, present CMAP

**Martin-Gruber** — 15%; some intrinsic function spared in high ulnar injury

**Success** — ≥ M3 (many M4); easier rehab than tendon transfer; nerve transfer within **6 mo**, tendon transfer any time\
✗ Wrong options: cortical re-education

### Tendon transfers for irreparable palsy (radial, ulnar claw, peroneal)

**Principles** — supple joints first; synergistic; similar power (cross-sectional area); straight line of pull; one tendon one function; **lose one MRC grade**; better <30 y, distal

| Smith excursion rule | cm |
|---|---|
| Wrist flexors / extensors | **3** |
| EDC, FPL, EPL | **5** |
| FDS, FDP | **7** |

| Palsy | Function | Transfer |
|---|---|---|
| **Radial** | Wrist extension | **PT → ECRB** |
| Radial | Thumb extension | **PL (or FDS IV) → EPL** |
| Radial | Finger MCP extension | **FCU → EDC (Jones)** · FCR → EDC (Brand) · FDS → EDC (Boyes) |
| **Median** | Opposition | **EIP → APB (Burkhalter)** · **FDS ring (Riordan)** · **PL → APB (Camitz)** (TO) |
| Median | Thumb IP flexion | **BR → FPL** |
| Median | Index flexion | Side-to-side FDP |
| Ulnar | Simple claw (**+ Bouvier**) | **Zancolli lasso** (FDS around A1), MCP volar plate capsulodesis, A1/A2 release |
| **Ulnar** | **Complex claw (− Bouvier)** | **FDS → lateral bands** (or FCR + grafts) |
| Ulnar | Ring/small flexion | Side-to-side FDP to median FDP |
| Shoulder ER | | Lower trapezius → infraspinatus |
| Elbow flexion | | Steindler flexor-pronator advancement; pec major, lat, triceps |
| **CPN foot drop** | Dorsiflexion | **Tibialis posterior → dorsum of foot** |

**Camitz in long-standing CTS (TO)** — **PL → APB** via same incision\
✗ Wrong options: Burkhalter, Riordan, Jones

**Bouvier test** — block MCP hyperextension → active IP extension? **Yes = simple claw** (static procedure) · **No = complex claw** (dynamic FDS → lateral bands)

**Radial palsy >12 mo, no conduction** — **PT → ECRB, FCU → EDC, PL → EPL**\
✗ Wrong options: neurolysis, grafting, AIN → ECRB / FCR → PIN nerve transfers

**Radial palsy natural history** — 85–90% recover in 2–3 mo; EMG at 3 mo

**CPN palsy after THA, 6 mo, brace intolerant, supple** — **tibialis posterior transfer**\
✗ Wrong options: repeat decompression · arthrodesis: fixed deformity/arthritis · BKA

## 6 Complications

### Neuroma after digital amputation

**Neuroma** — disorganised proximal regrowth; amputees up to **30%** (Orthobullets)

**Digital amputation** — **<10%** symptomatic (6.6–7.8%); **>½ need revision within 1 yr**; rate higher than after neurorrhaphy\
✗ Wrong options: improves spontaneously · revision after 1 yr

**Symptoms** — sharp/burning pain, cold intolerance, pressure hypersensitivity

**Treatment** — gabapentin/antidepressants, phenol/botox, rehab → resection, **TMR**, **RPNI**

**Grafting** — donor neuroma; allograft rejection

### Metabolic neuropathy – vitamin B12

**B12 deficiency** — **peripheral sensory neuropathy** (✓ check B12); **↓ DTR**, Babinski, fatigue\
✗ Wrong options: ↑ DTR · inability to whistle = FSHD · hydrophobia = rabies
