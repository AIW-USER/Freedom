# Trigger Finger

*Hand · 94 / 103*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Incidence** — **2–3%** general population · **10% of diabetics**

**Who** — **♀ >50 y**, **diabetics** (bilateral, multiple digits)

**Adult digits** — **ring & long** most common

**Associations** — RA, calcific tendinitis, septic tenosynovitis, **CTS (>60%** of trigger digits have clinical/EDS CTS), congenital trigger thumb · DM, amyloidosis, hypothyroidism, sarcoidosis, gout, pseudogout

### Pathology – A1 pulley

**Site** — **stenosing tenosynovitis at A1 pulley** (overlies MCP) → tendon–pulley size mismatch\
✗ Wrong options: Grayson's, ORL, TCL, extensor

**Histology** — **fibrocartilaginous metaplasia** of tendon/pulley; chondrocyte proliferation; **↑ type III collagen**; hyperglycaemia → collagen cross-links

**Nodule** — on **FDP** (FDS often unaffected)

**Trigger thumb** — **Av (variable annular) pulley** causes stenosis in up to **75%**

## 3 Presentation

**Symptoms** — progressive pain at A1 (distal palm), clicking, catching, **locking in flexion at PIP**; referred dorsal MCP/PIP pain

**Exam** — tender A1, **palpable nodule**, triggering on flexion/extension

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

**Diagnosis** — **clinical**; X-ray not needed

### Green classification

| Grade | Feature |
|---|---|
| I | Palm pain, tenderness at A1 |
| II | **Catching** |
| III | **Locking, passively correctable** |
| IV | **Fixed locked digit** |

**Differentials** — **lumbrical plus** (paradoxical extension on flexion) · joint contracture (can't passively extend) · **pyogenic flexor tenosynovitis** (Kanavel, ↑ markers) · **MCP locking** (collateral/volar plate caught on MC head osteophyte)

## 5 Management

```
Adult trigger finger
├─ First line → STEROID INJECTION (1–3; series of 2 before surgery) ± splint, NSAIDs
└─ Failed nonop → A1 PULLEY RELEASE (open standard / percutaneous)
       └─ persistent triggering → RELEASE ULNAR FDS SLIP ± A0 / A3
```

### Nonoperative

**Splint** — immobilise **MCP or DIP only**; PIP free; relief 40–97%

**Injection** — **best initial treatment**; relief **60–90%** (up to 90% non-diabetics); in/superficial to sheath

**Diabetics** — less relief (Orthobullets classic) · recent literature: success not influenced by DM — read options; surgery cost-effective in DM

**Poorer response** — longer symptom duration

**Injection complications** — fat atrophy, **tendon rupture**, **transient hyperglycaemia** (diabetics)

### Infection risk – timing after steroid injection

**Rule** — injection **within 90 days of surgery → ↑ postoperative infection**

**Last injection 2 mo ago** → schedule surgery in **1.5 mo** (>90 days)

### Operative

| | Open release | Percutaneous release |
|---|---|---|
| Status | **Standard**; easier to confirm release | 18–19 G needle, bevel parallel to tendon |
| Success | >90% | >90% (↑ with **US**) |
| RTW | — | Earlier |
| Avoid in | — | **Thumb & little finger** (nerve proximity) |

**Recurrence** — ↑ in **diabetics**

**WALANT / local** — allows intraoperative check of triggering; post-op early active ROM 4×/day

**Extra releases** — **A0** (aponeurosis proximal to A1) · one/both FDS limbs · A3

**RA** — **FDS slip excision without A1 release** → spares A1 → does not worsen **ulnar drift**

**Ulnar FDS slip** — resected to help prevent MCP ulnar drift

### Percutaneous thumb release – radial digital nerve risk

**Most at risk** — **radial digital nerve of thumb** (superficial, **oblique** course across A1)

**Distance** — mean **2.9 mm** from release point; within 2 mm in 5/10 hands (Bain) · 2.7 mm (Orthobullets pulley data)

**Little finger** — variable nerve crossing → also avoid percutaneous

### Paediatric trigger finger/thumb – additional releases & digital nerve risk

**Trigger thumb** — **10× more common** than trigger finger in children; A1 constriction; **IP flexion contracture**; **>60% resolve spontaneously** (Baek); surgery → **radial digital nerve** crosses field

**Paediatric trigger finger** — **Notta's node proximal to A1**, flexion contracture, triggering; causes: proximal **FDS decussation**, FDS/FDP nodules, thick A2, **tight A3**

**A1 release alone** — often fails (6/18, Cardon) → **release one/both FDS limbs** ± A3 (Bae: all → A1 + single FDS slip resection)

**Timing** — operate **2–4 y** (prevent IP contracture)

**Revision (persistent triggering)** — **extensile incision**; order per OB answer: **A3 release → then 1 FDS slip** · per OB explanation: ulnar FDS slip → then A3 / remaining slip

**Avoid in children** — complete **A2 release** (bowstringing) · **FDP debulking** (adhesions) · horizontal incision (no prox/distal exposure)

## 6 Complications

**Radial digital nerve injury** — **thumb release** (superficial, oblique)

**Bowstringing** — **A2/A4 damage** → pulley reconstruction

**Others** — stiffness, scar tenderness, dehiscence, haematoma, **tendon scoring** (percutaneous), infection

## How it's asked

**"Diabetic, ring finger locking, distal palm tender — structure?"** → **A1 pulley**

**"Child — release in addition to A1"** → **one or both limbs of sublimis (FDS)**\
✗ Wrong options: A4

**"Paediatric trigger thumb surgery — at risk"** → **radial digital nerve**

**"Persistent paediatric triggering after A1 release"** → **extensile incision, A3 release, then 1 FDS slip**\
✗ Wrong options: A2 release · FDP debulking

**"Percutaneous thumb release — consent for"** → **radial digital nerve of thumb**

**"Injected 2 mo ago — when to operate?"** → **in 1.5 mo (>90 days)**

**"RA trigger finger"** → **FDS slip excision, preserve A1**
