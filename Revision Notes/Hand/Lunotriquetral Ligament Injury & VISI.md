# Lunotriquetral Ligament Injury & VISI

*Hand · 51 / 103*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Incidence** — LT injury **less common than SL** injury

### LT ligament – volar fibres strongest

**Shape** — **C-shaped intrinsic** ligament: dorsal + palmar transverse parts + weak proximal membranous (fibrocartilaginous) part

| Ligament | Strongest part | Yield strength (Berger) |
|---|---|---|
| **LT** | **Volar** (thickest; transmits triquetral extension moment) | Volar **301 N** vs dorsal 121 N |
| **SL** | **Dorsal** | Dorsal **260 N** vs volar 118 N |

**Dorsal LT** — most important **rotational constraint** (but weaker)

**Dorsal radiocarpal (radiotriquetral) ligament** — extrinsic; **secondary restraint to VISI**; loss → lunate flexes easily

**Volar long & short radiolunate** — extrinsic; torn in advanced injury

**Short radiolunate** — stays attached to lunate in perilunate dislocation; not involved in LT tear

## 2 Mechanism

**Mechanism** — wrist **hyperextension** or extension + **radial deviation**

**Kinematics** — LT torn → scaphoid flexes lunate unopposed; triquetrum extends → **lunate flexes with scaphoid**

**VISI** — **LT ligament + dorsal radiotriquetral + volar radiolunate** injury; a **CID** pattern

**VISI in normal wrists** — may occur with **ligamentous laxity**\
✗ Wrong options: DISI (always pathological)

### Rocking lunate

**Rocking lunate (TO)** — **tear of both SL + LT ligaments**\
✗ Wrong options: SL alone · LT alone · + capitolunate

**Lunate balance (TO)** — lunate held between scaphoid (flexion) and triquetrum (extension) via SL & LT; lose one → dominated by the other

| (TO) | SL disruption | LT disruption |
|---|---|---|
| Scaphoid | Flexes | — |
| Lunate | Follows triquetrum → **extends** | Loses triquetral pull → **flexes** |
| SL angle (normal <60°) | **↑** | **↓** |

**Chronic ulnar impaction** — **ulnar positive variance** → long ulna impacts triquetrum → degenerative LT tear ± **TFCC** tear

## 3 Presentation

**Symptoms** — **ulnar-sided wrist pain**, worse with **pronation + ulnar deviation** (power grip); painful snap with ulnar deviation, pronation, axial load

| Test | Technique | Positive |
|---|---|---|
| **LT shuck / ballottement** | Hold lunate, shuck triquetrum dorsal–palmar | Pain, crepitus, laxity |
| **Kleinman shear** | Stabilise radiolunate, load triquetrum AP (forearm neutral) | Pain or **clunk** |
| LT compression | Triquetrum displaced ulnarly during radioulnar deviation | Pain |

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| View | Finding |
|---|---|
| **Lateral** | **Volar-flexed lunate → SL angle <30°** (normal **47°**) = **VISI** |
| Lateral | **Capitolunate angle >15°** (zigzag; normally co-linear) |
| **AP** | **Break in Gilula's arc**; proximal translation of triquetrum ± LT overlap; often **no LT widening** (unlike SL) |
| AP clenched fist | Incongruent LT articulation |
| **Arthroscopy** | Diagnostic when X-rays normal |

## 5 Management

```
LT ligament injury
├─ Initial / acute stable → observe / cast (better if acute)
├─ Acute instability → CRPP (multiple K-wires) + ligament repair ± dorsal capsulodesis
└─ Chronic instability → LT fusion
       └─ due to ulnar positive variance → arthroscopic LT debridement + ulnar shortening
```

**Cast** — more successful when acute; only **37%** operated (Atkinson & Watson)

**Acute** — BLB autograft reconstruction & LT fusion **out of favour** acutely

**LT fusion** — chronic; complication **nonunion**

## X vs Y

| | **SL injury** | **LT injury** |
|---|---|---|
| Frequency | Commoner | Rarer |
| Pain | Dorsoradial | **Ulnar** |
| Instability | **DISI** (always pathological) | **VISI** (may be laxity) |
| SL angle | **>60°** (↑) | **<30°** (↓) |
| AP X-ray | SL widening | Gilula break, no widening |
| Stronger part | **Dorsal** | **Volar** |
| Test | Watson scaphoid shift | Shuck / Kleinman shear |

## How it's asked

**"Strongest portion of the injured ligament (VISI, positive ballottement)?"** → **volar fibres of LT**\
✗ Wrong options: dorsal LT, dorsal SL

**"Rocking lunate results from?"** → **SL + LT tear** (TO)

**"SL angle in VISI?"** → **<30°** (normal 47°)

**"LT disruption effect on SL angle?"** → **decreases** (TO)

**"Chronic LT tear with ulnar positive variance?"** → **arthroscopic debridement + ulnar shortening**

**"Chronic LT instability?"** → **LT fusion** (risk nonunion)
