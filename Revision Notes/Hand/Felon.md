# Felon

*Hand · 30 / 103*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology – paronychia

**Most common hand infection (TO)** — **acute paronychia**\
✗ Wrong options: felon, whitlow, subungual

**Felon share** — **15–20%** of hand infections

**Felon site** — **thumb > index** (TO)

**Felon (TO)** — infection of **subcutaneous tissue of distal pulp**

**Paronychia (TO)** — infection of nail fold (eponychium); ***S. aureus***; poor nail hygiene; risk: DM, steroids, chemo, immunocompromise, manual labourers, farmers

| Perionychium term (TO) | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Perionychium** | All tissue surrounding the nail |
| **Nail bed** | **Germinal matrix** (most nail growth; **lunula** = visible part) + **sterile matrix** (smooth gliding surface) |
| **Hyponychium** | Keratinised tissue distal to nail bed → **barrier to infection** |
| **Paronychium** | Skin fold each side of nail |
| **Eponychium** | Thin fold sealing proximal nail fold over plate |

**Eponychia (TO)** — infection of one nail fold + eponychium · **runaround infection** = pus from one fold to the other via eponychium

**Paronychia work-up (TO)** — **screen for diabetes** · X-ray for osteomyelitis

**Early paronychia, no pus point (TO)** — **1st-generation cephalosporin** + warm **povidone-iodine soaks (1:10 in warm saline, 10–15 min twice daily)** · surgery: "follow the infection" (1–2 incisions, nail plate removal, volar exposure if spread)

| Paronychia stage (TO) | Treatment |
|---|---|
| No pus point | **Antibiotics** |
| Abscess one side of nail | **I&D (single incision)** |
| Pus to opposite side + under nail | **Second incision + remove proximal third of nail** |

### Anatomy & pathophysiology

**Pulp** — closed sac; **fibrous vertical septae** periosteum → epidermis (stabilise pinch/grasp); eccrine sweat glands

**Apical infection (TO)** — infection limited to **skin of pulp tip** (vs felon = pulp abscess)

**Pulp = "second eye" (TO)** — rich sensory supply; septa give firm non-slip grip

**Blood supply** — digital arteries give **nutrient branch to epiphysis before entering pulp** → epiphysis spared → **children rarely get epiphyseal necrosis** (TO)

**Pathophysiology** — ↑ pressure in micro-compartments → **pulp "compartment syndrome"** → diaphysis ischaemia > skin → **diaphyseal necrosis/sequestration** before spontaneous skin decompression

## 2 Mechanism

**Penetrating** — glucose needlestick, splinters

**Local spread** — from paronychia

**No injury** — **50%** (contamination via eccrine glands)

| Organism | Setting |
|---|---|
| ***S. aureus*** | **Most common** |
| Gram −ve | Immunosuppressed |
| ***Eikenella corrodens*** | **Diabetic nail-biters** |

## 3 Presentation

**Symptoms** — severe **throbbing pain**; tense, tender pulp

**Swelling limit** — **not proximal to DIP crease** unless sheath/joint involved

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

**Diagnosis** — **clinical**; X-ray only if trauma (fracture/FB); no MRI, no labs

**Differentials** — **herpetic whitlow**, paronychia, glomus tumour, mucous cyst, psoriasis, **tophaceous gout**

**Herpetic whitlow (TO)** — **HSV-1**; distinction from felon mainly by **history**; **24–72 h burning prodrome** → erythema → **clear vesicles** coalescing around nail folds; turbid not purulent; **pulp NOT tense**; persists ~2 wk, resolves over next 1 wk

## 5 Management

**Early felon (TO)** — oral antibiotics + warm povidone-iodine soaks + immobilisation · **pus point → surgery**

```
Felon
├─ Early, no drainable abscess → ORAL ABX + observation
└─ Abscess (most cases) → BEDSIDE I&D + IV ABX, heal by SECONDARY INTENTION
       ├─ superficial / FB / visible drainage → VOLAR LONGITUDINAL (midvolar)
       └─ deep, no FB, no drainage → MID-LATERAL (non-pressure side)
```

### Incision & drainage technique

**Placement** — over **point of maximal tenderness**; **distal to DIP crease** (avoid contracture & sheath extension)

**Mid-lateral side** — **ulnar side digits 2–4**; **radial side thumb & small**

**Avoid** — **fish-mouth** (unstable pulp, **vascular compromise**) · double longitudinal / transverse (digital n./a. injury) · **proximal probing** (seeds flexor sheath)

**Debridement** — **break up septa**; avoid sheath & DIP joint; Gram stain + culture **before Abx**; gauze wick

**Closure** — **secondary intention**; soaks from 24–48 h; anti-*S. aureus* cover\
✗ Wrong options: primary or delayed primary

### Diabetic hand infection – renal failure & amputation

**Diabetic triad** — poor wound healing + neuropathy + vascular disease → ↑ infection, multiple procedures

**↑ Amputation rate** — **renal failure** · deep **polymicrobial / Gram −ve** infection\
✗ Wrong options: insulin dependence, retinopathy, neuropathy, Gram +ve

## 6 Complications

**Complications** — **fingertip compartment syndrome** · **flexor tenosynovitis** · **osteomyelitis / diaphyseal sequestration** · **DIP septic arthritis** · digital tip necrosis

## X vs Y

| | **Felon** | **Herpetic whitlow** (TO) | Paronychia |
|---|---|---|---|
| Site | Distal pulp | Pulp / nail folds | Nail fold |
| Cause | *S. aureus* | **HSV-1** | *S. aureus* |
| Pulp | **Tense** | **Not tense** | — |
| Lesion | Abscess | Clear vesicles, turbid fluid | Pus at fold |
| Treat | **I&D** | No I&D (self-limiting ~3 wk) | Abx ± I&D |

## How it's asked

**"Most common hand infection"** → **acute paronychia** (TO)

**"Midvolar pulp abscess, failed oral cephalosporin — next step?"** → **I&D, midline volar incision, secondary closure**\
✗ Wrong options: fish-mouth · IV Abx alone · colchicine

**"Diabetic hand infection — ↑ amputation with"** → **renal failure**

**"Felon in diabetic nail-biter"** → ***Eikenella***

**"Mid-lateral incision side, index finger"** → **ulnar**

**"Pus tracking from one nail fold to the other via eponychium?"** → **runaround infection** (TO)

**"Children with felon rarely get epiphyseal necrosis — why?"** → **epiphyseal artery arises proximal to the closed pulp space** (TO)
