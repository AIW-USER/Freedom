# Frostbite

*Hand · 35 / 103*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology & risk factors

**Demographics** — **♂:♀ 10:1**; **30–50 y**; homeless, outdoor workers

**Distribution** — upper limb 20% · **lower limb 47%** · both 30% · head/face 3%

**Most important host risk** — **alcohol abuse** (63% of admissions); homelessness

**Other host** — mental illness, PVD, neuropathy, DM, malnutrition, chronic illness, prior cold injury, **African descent** (Caucasians better cold-induced vasodilatation)

**Smoking** — ↓ NO + ↑ fibrinogen/platelets → **thrombosis**

| Environmental factor | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Temperature | Risk **low > −10°C**; **high < −25°C** |
| Wind at −18°C | Freeze in **1 h at 10 mph** · **10 min at 40 mph** |
| Altitude | >17,000 ft |
| Contact | Water, ice, **metal** (↑ severity) |

### Spectrum of cold injury

| Injury | Key features |
|---|---|
| **Frostnip** | Mildest; superficial skin only, **no dermal damage**; reversible |
| **Chilblain (pernio)** | **Nonfreezing, dry**; burning, pruritus; resolves 2 wk; chronic vasculitis in young/middle-aged ♀ |
| **Trench (immersion) foot** | **Nonfreezing, wet**, <10°C; military |
| **Frostbite** | Freezing → tissue necrosis → amputation |
| Hypothermia | Core temp ↓; can be fatal |

## 2 Mechanism

| Limb phase | Events |
|---|---|
| **I Cooling/freezing** | Vasoconstriction → **"hunting response"** (cyclic AV shunting q10 min); **intracellular ice** → membrane rupture; **extracellular ice** → sludging + **intracellular dehydration** |
| **II Rewarming** | Crystals melt; **endothelium permeable** → blisters/oedema; **avoid refreezing** |
| **III Progressive injury** | Inflammation, thrombosis, necrosis; **↓ PGE2**; **↑ PGF2α + thromboxane B2** |
| IV Resolution | Healing ± sequelae or gangrene |

**Numbers** — sensory nerve dysfunction **−10°C**; extracellular ice **−2 to −15°C**

**Hypothermia** — CBT <35°C; shivering stops at **30–32°C** → rigidity, mimics death → **"no one is dead until warm and dead"**

## 3 Presentation

**Hypothermia grades** — mild **32–35°C** · moderate **28–32°C** · severe **<28°C**; brady, AF/VF, resp acidosis, coma

**Blisters** — form **6–24 h after rewarming**; degree only defined **after rewarming**

**Severe hand injury signs** — no Doppler signal, **absent cap refill**, dark purple digits, **haemorrhagic blisters** → bone scan / angiography

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Classification

| Degree | Findings | Newer class / prognosis |
|---|---|---|
| 1st | **Central white area + surrounding erythema** | Superficial – good |
| 2nd | **Clear/cloudy blisters** within 24 h | Superficial – good |
| 3rd | **Haemorrhagic blisters** (painless) / black eschar | **Deep – poor** |
| 4th | Tissue necrosis | **Deep – poor** |

### Key radiology findings

**Tc-99m bone scan** — assess severity: **1st at day 2** (absent uptake = poor prognosis, not necessarily necrosis); **2nd at day 5**

| Day-5 blood/bone pool | Action |
|---|---|
| Normal | Expectant |
| Diminished | Observe ± early debridement |
| **Absent** | **Early debridement / amputation**; tPA candidate |

**MRI T2** — ↑ signal in necrotic muscle; not the initial test

## 5 Management

```
Frostbite
├─ Field → pad/splint, prevent refreezing; rewarm only if warmth can be maintained
├─ ED → warm IV fluids, tetanus, NSAIDs, RAPID REWARM 40-42°C water bath
│      └─ <24 h, no flow on bone scan, 2nd/3rd degree → IV tPA
└─ Necrosis → wait for demarcation (1-3 mo) → LATE DEBRIDEMENT / AMPUTATION
```

### Prevention – insulated footwear

**Most reliable prevention** — **thermal insulation footwear** / reduce shoe heat loss (toes: little muscle → little heat)

✗ Wrong options: **Distractors** — hard-soled shoes (comfortable shoes ✓), industrial toe-caps, compression stockings, high-protein diet, warm drinks, triple socks, carbo-loading

### Rewarming

**Water bath** — **40–42°C**; **30 min** (Orthobullets) · **15–30 min** (Golant); mild antibacterial

**End point** — skin **pliable and red-purple**

**Avoid 50°C** → thermal burn · lower temp → ↓ survival · **avoid freeze-thaw cycles**

**Hypothermia rewarming** — mild: external (passive/active; active → **"afterdrop"**) · moderate/severe: **internal core** (warm O₂/IV, lavage, bypass, CAVR ~1°C/15 min); avoid alcohol/sedatives

### Acute treatment – blister care & thrombolysis

| Blister | Management |
|---|---|
| **Clear** (superficial) | **Debride + topical aloe vera** (↓ PGF2α, TXB2) |
| **Haemorrhagic** (deep) | **Drain/aspirate, leave skin intact** → prevents dermal desiccation |
| Intact | Leave, dry gauze |

**Liquid nitrogen finger, haemorrhagic blister** — **drain, leave roof intact**\
✗ Wrong options: HBO · full debridement + flap · MRI · wet-to-dry

**IV tPA (tissue plasminogen activator)** — **within 24 h** → **↓ digital amputation**; indication: **no flow on bone scan**, **2nd/3rd degree**\
✗ Wrong options: superficial

**Unconscious in snow, cold blue digits, no cap refill, 2PD >15 mm** — **tPA**\
✗ Wrong options: diltiazem · amlodipine [Raynaud's] · collagenase [Dupuytren] · diclofenac

| tPA contraindications | |
|---|---|
| General | **Alcoholic** (head injury bleed), active bleeding, ICH/surgery <3 mo, **concurrent trauma**, major surgery <14 d, aneurysm/AVM, bleeding diathesis, pregnancy, labile HTN |
| **Cold-related** | **>24 h cold exposure**, **warm ischaemia >6 h**, **multiple freeze-thaw** |

**Other adjuncts** — LMW dextran, anticoagulants; **HBO anecdotal**; IV Abx only if infected

**Rehab** — whirlpool hydrotherapy, PT/OT

### Delayed debridement/amputation after demarcation

**Timing** — **after demarcation, 1–3 months** → **"frostbite in January, amputate in July"**\
✗ Wrong options: days · weeks

**Before demarcation, no sepsis** — **moist dressings + observation** (like burns; much may be superficial)

**Immediate surgery only** — **escharotomy** (circumferential constriction) · **fasciotomy** (compartment syndrome)

**Sympathectomy** — ↓ pain duration + time to demarcation; **no ↓ extent of necrosis**

## 6 Complications

| Adult | Detail / treatment |
|---|---|
| **Persistent pain** | **50%** (intolerable 15%) |
| **Cold intolerance** | — |
| **Vasospasm** | **Raynaud's**, colour change, **hyperhidrosis** → CCB, vasodilators, β-blockers, late sympathectomy |
| Neuropathy | ↓ NCV → decompression (CTR) |
| **Frostbite arthropathy** | Subchondral bone loss, osteopenia, contractures **DIP > PIP** → arthroplasty |

| Child | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Premature physeal closure** | **1–2 y after exposure** (chondrocyte injury) |
| Late (>10 y age) | Joint laxity, angular deformity, **short digits**, excess skin, DJD → physeal arrest, osteotomy, arthrodesis |

**Severity ↑** — **alcohol intoxication**, skin contact with metal/ice, high wind chill

## X vs Y

| | Chilblain | Trench foot | Frostnip | Frostbite |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Freezing | No | No | Yes | Yes |
| Wet/dry | **Dry** | **Wet** | — | — |
| Tissue loss | No | ± | No (reversible) | Yes |

## How it's asked

**"Most reliable way to reduce toe frostbite?"** → **thermal insulation footwear**

**"Most important measure hiking in extreme cold?"** → **reduce thermal heat loss from shoes**

**"Liquid nitrogen, haemorrhagic blister — next?"** → **drain, leave overlying skin intact**

**"Found in snow bank, absent cap refill — medication?"** → **tPA** (within 24 h)

**"When to debride/amputate?"** → **after demarcation, 1–3 months**

**"Rewarming bath temperature?"** → **40–42°C**\
✗ Wrong options: 50°C

**"Stranded days, gangrenous toes, no sepsis — next morning?"** → **moist dressings + observation**\
✗ Wrong options: amputation

**"Growth problem after childhood frostbite?"** → **premature physeal closure 1–2 y later**
