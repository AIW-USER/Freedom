# Epidermal Inclusion Cyst

*Hand · 26 / 103*

## 1 Basics

**Nature** — painless, **benign, slow-growing** soft-tissue tumour; months–years after trauma

**Rank** — **3rd most common hand tumour**

**Demographics** — **♂ > ♀**; **3rd–4th decade**

**Site** — **distal phalanx / fingertip**

## 2 Mechanism

**Pathogenesis** — **penetrating injury** drives **keratinising epithelium** into subcutis or bone → **epithelium-lined cyst filled with keratin**

## 3 Presentation

**Symptoms** — **painless fingertip mass**; erythematous painful lesions rare

**Exam** — flesh/yellow/white; **well-circumscribed, firm, slightly mobile**; superficial, tethered to skin

**vs ganglion** — **firmer**, **does not transilluminate**

**Large lesion near IP joint** — ↓ ROM

**Digital nerve compression** — ↓ 2-point discrimination

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

**Views** — AP, lateral, oblique of digit/hand

**X-ray** — soft-tissue mass; **lytic distal phalanx lesion** if erodes bone → mimics malignancy/infection

**Biopsy** — before excision **if lytic distal phalanx lesion** (exclude neoplasm/infection)

![Intraosseous epidermal inclusion cyst, distal phalanx](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob6087-interosseous-epidermal-inclusion-cyst.jpg)

### Histology

**Gross** — thick **white keratinous** material

**Micro** — **keratin-filled cyst lined by epithelial cells**

**Differentials** — tophaceous gout, foreign-body granuloma, sebaceous cyst, **pyogenic granuloma**, GCT tendon sheath, ganglion, enchondroma, glomus

## 5 Management

**Observation** — **not recommended**

**Marginal excision** — **remove entire capsule** → **curative**, low recurrence

**Bone erosion** — curettage + bone graft; amputation rarely (advanced destruction)

**Indications** — diagnosis, pain, loss of function, cosmesis

**Malignant transformation** — **never reported**

## 6 Complications

**List** — wound problems, infection, digital neurapraxia, recurrence (**low even with bone involvement**)

## X vs Y

| | **Epidermal inclusion cyst** | **Pyogenic granuloma** | Glomus tumour | GCT tendon sheath | Enchondroma |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Growth | **Slow**, after penetrating injury | **Rapid**, after repeated trauma | Rare, benign | Benign nodular | Intramedullary |
| Look | Flesh/white, firm, tethered | **"Beefy red", bleeds on bumping**, ≤2 cm | Subungual | Nodule on tendon sheath | Bone lesion |
| Histology | Keratin, epithelial lining | **Collarette around lobules of endothelial cells** | Glomus body | — | Chondrogenic |
| Treatment | Marginal excision | **Excision + silver nitrate cautery** (office); 85% resolve, mean 1.6 Rx | — | — | — |

**Pyogenic granuloma** — benign **vascular** lesion; commonest **head & neck**; slight ♂; recurrence least with surgical excision

## How it's asked

**"Rapid, bleeding vascular finger mass after repeated trauma, excise + silver nitrate?"** → **pyogenic granuloma**\
✗ Wrong options: epidermal inclusion cyst · GCT · glomus · enchondroma

**"Fingertip mass after penetrating injury, lytic distal phalanx?"** → **epidermal inclusion cyst → biopsy, then marginal excision**

**"Histology?"** → **keratin-filled cyst with epithelial lining**
