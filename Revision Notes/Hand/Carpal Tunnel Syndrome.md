# Carpal Tunnel Syndrome

*Hand · 11 / 103*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology & associations

**Commonest** — **most common compressive neuropathy**; 0.1–10% population; **♀:♂ 3:1**; 40–60 y; bilateral up to 70%

**Commonest causes (TO)** — **idiopathic > hypothyroidism > pregnancy**

**Risk factors** — ♀, **obesity**, **pregnancy**, **hypothyroidism**, **RA**, DM, **smoking**, vibration, acromegaly, amyloidosis, **hyperparathyroidism**, CRF, OCP, DRF malunion; **children → mucopolysaccharidosis**

**NOT associated (TO)** — **Addison's disease**

**Pregnancy CTS (TO)** — usually **resolves after delivery**

**Diabetics (TO)** — **worse surgical outcome** than idiopathic

### Anatomy – tunnel contents, TCL attachments, recurrent motor branch & anastomoses

**Contents** — median nerve + **FPL** + 4 FDS + 4 FDP (9 tendons); **not FCR** (own sheath, trapezial groove) · not ulnar n.

**FDS layering** — FDS 3rd & 4th **volar** to FDS 2nd & 5th

**Most radial structure** — FPL

**TCL (roof) attachments** — radial: **scaphoid tubercle + trapezium**; ulnar: **hook of hamate + pisiform**

**Narrowest** — level of **hook of hamate**

**Floor** — proximal carpal row

**Pressures** — normal 2.5 mmHg (neutral) → 30 (flexion); **20 mmHg → venous congestion**; **60–80 → arteriolar arrest**; CTS 30–110

**Recurrent motor branch** — **APB, OP, superficial FPB** (+ lumbricals 1–2 via digital nn.); arises radial/central

| Recurrent branch variant | % |
|---|---|
| Extraligamentous | 50 |
| Subligamentous | 30 |
| **Transligamentous** (at risk) | **20** |

**Surgical rule** — **cut TCL far ulnar** (radial cut → transects transligamentous branch → **loss of opposition**)

**Palmar cutaneous branch** — arises **~5 cm (4–8)** proximal to crease, **ulnar side of FCR / between PL & FCR**, **superficial to TCL** → sensation **thenar eminence** → **spared in CTS**

**Median nerve laceration vs CTS** — laceration proximal to wrist adds **thenar eminence numbness** (palmar cutaneous br.)

**Spared muscles in CTS** — **FPL, PQ, FDP index** (AIN, proximal); **adductor pollicis** (ulnar)

**Weak muscle in CTS** — **APB** (only purely median-distal-to-TCL); inserts **radial base of thumb proximal phalanx**

![Recurrent motor branch variants](../../MCQ/attachments/q210259-illa-recurrent-motor-branch-anatomy.jpg)

| Median–ulnar connection | Site | Direction | Clinical clue |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Martin-Gruber** | Forearm | Median/AIN → ulnar | Intrinsics spared with high ulnar lesion |
| **Marinacci** | Forearm | Ulnar → median | Reverse MG |
| **Riche-Cannieu** | **Hand** | **Deep ulnar → recurrent median** | **"All-ulnar hand"**: thenar strength **preserved** despite median laceration / severe CTS |
| **Berrettini** | Hand | Sensory, common digital | no motor sparing |

## 2 Mechanism

**Idiopathic** — **inflamed synovium** = commonest cause; ↑ pressure → ↓ intraneural blood flow

### Lab & histology markers

**Histology** — **oedema, fibrosis, vascular sclerosis**; scattered lymphocytes; ± amyloid; no acute inflammation

**Secondary (space-occupying)** — ganglion, tumour, **gout tophus** (nodule worse after alcohol) → **tissue diagnosis + rheumatology**\
✗ Wrong options: splint · excision first

**Acute CTS** — after DRF/carpal dislocation/ORIF → **urgent release**

## 3 Presentation

**Symptoms** — numbness/tingling **radial 3½ digits**, **night waking**, clumsiness, ↓ grip

**Late** — **thenar atrophy**, weak APB

**Sensation spared** — thenar eminence (palmar cutaneous br.)

![Thenar atrophy](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob6018-thenar-atrophy.jpg)

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Diagnosis – provocative tests, CTS-6 & electrodiagnostics

**Diagnosis** — **clinical**; EDS not required (AAOS: **limited** rec)

| Test | Fact asked |
|---|---|
| **Durkan** (carpal compression, 30 s) | **Most sensitive provocative test** |
| **Semmes-Weinstein monofilament** | **Most sensitive sensory test / early CTS** (single fibre threshold) |
| **Hand diagram** (self-administered) | **Most SPECIFIC (76%)** (TO ×3) |
| **Tinel** | High specificity (TO) |
| Phalen (wrist flexion 60 s) | Less sensitive than Durkan |
| **2-point discrimination** | Positive if fails at **5 mm**; innervation density test; best for **nerve regeneration after repair** |
| Scratch collapse | Supportive |

**High sensitivity (TO)** — Durkan, hand diagram, night pain, Semmes-Weinstein

**High specificity (TO)** — **hand diagram, Tinel**

**NOT CTS tests** — **Murphy** (TO) · **Wartenberg** (cubital tunnel) · **Froment** (ulnar) · **OK sign** (AIN) · hypothenar atrophy (ulnar)

### Diagnostic criteria

**Clinical criteria** — median-territory numbness · **nocturnal** numbness · thenar weakness/atrophy · Tinel + · Phalen + · **2PD loss >5 mm** (= CTS-6 items)

**CTS-6** — max 26; **≥12 → 80% probability**; **>5 → 25%**

| CTS-6 item | Points |
|---|---|
| Numbness mainly median territory | 3.5 |
| **Nocturnal numbness** | 4 |
| Thenar atrophy/weakness | 5 |
| Phalen + | 5 |
| **Loss of 2PD (>5 mm)** | 4.5 |
| Tinel + | 4 |

**High/low pretest probability** — EDS **won't change probability** → treat clinically

**EDS role** — confirm (work comp), **prognosis**; AAOS: obtain if surgery planned

| NCS (latency/velocity) | EMG (muscle) |
|---|---|
| **Distal sensory latency >3.5 ms** | ↑ insertional activity |
| **Distal motor latency >4.5 ms** | **Positive sharp waves** |
| **Velocity <52 m/s** (less specific) | **Fibrillations**, fasciculations |
| Side-to-side asymmetry >1 m/s (TO) | ↓ motor recruitment, CRDs |
| Only **large myelinated fibres** tested | = **axonal loss → severe** |

**Cut-off conflict** — **DSL >3.5 / DML >4.5 ms** (CTS note, TO, OB) · **DSL >3.2 / DML >4.3 ms** (Orthobullets NCS note) — read the options

**EMG EXCEPT trap** — **distal sensory latency** is NCS, not EMG

| AAEM severity | Findings |
|---|---|
| Mild | Sensory only |
| Moderate | Sensory + motor latency ↑ |
| **Severe** | **Denervation (fibs, ↓ CMAP)** → early surgery |

### Key radiology findings

| Modality | Fact asked |
|---|---|
| **Ultrasound** | Median nerve **CSA >10 mm²** at **pisiform / proximal TCL** |
| X-ray | **Not routine** (not needed for diagnosis) |
| MRI / CT | Only if **space-occupying lesion** suspected (ganglion, tumour, **gout tophus**) |
| NCS/EMG | See table above: **DSL >3.5 ms · DML >4.5 ms · CV <52 m/s**; fibs/PSW = severe |

**Differentials** — AIN syndrome (motor only), **pronator syndrome** (thenar numbness + forearm pain), ulnar tunnel, **C6 radiculopathy** (steroid injection helps discriminate)

## 5 Management

```
CTS
├─ Mild–moderate → night splint NEUTRAL ± steroid injection  (reassess at 2–7 wk)
│      └─ fails (no response by ~2 mo) → carpal tunnel release
├─ Severe (thenar atrophy / denervation on EMG) → early CTR
└─ Acute CTS (trauma / post-ORIF) → urgent CTR
```

### Nonoperative care – splint position & steroid injection

**Splint** — **neutral** (night); **30° extension (cock-up) ↑ pressure** (avoid)

**AAOS strong** — splints ✓, steroid injection ✓, **surgery > nonop at 6 & 12 mo**, no post-op immobilisation, no magnets

**AAOS moderate** — NSAIDs no benefit; oral steroids ✓ · **limited** — endoscopic short-term benefit

**not appropriate** — B6, phonophoresis, bumetanide, PT alone

**Steroid injection** — **80% improve, 20% of these symptom-free at 1 y** (Green: 81%, mean 3.3 mo)

**Injection response** — good relief → **good surgical prognosis**; no relief → poorer outcome (but still may benefit)

**Dose/agent** — no difference (methylpred = triamcinolone; dose ≠ duration)

**With neck pain** — injection still indicated (diagnostic + partial relief)

**Time rule** — effective nonop works in **2–7 wk**; failed 8 wk splint → **CTR**\
✗ Wrong options: more splinting

### Surgical release – indications, guidelines, open vs endoscopic & prognosis

### Operative criteria

| Indication for CTR | Note |
|---|---|
| **Failed nonop** (splint ± injection) | No response in **2–7 wk** → operate |
| **Severe CTS**: thenar atrophy / **denervation on EMG** | **Early CTR** (limit further denervation) |
| **Acute CTS** (DRF, carpal dislocation, post-ORIF) | **Urgent** release |
| Classic Hx + exam despite splints | Elective CTR (EDS won't change probability) |
| **Revision** | Failure to improve → **incomplete release** commonest → **open** revision |

**Avoid endoscopic (TO)** — revision · anatomic variant · prior tendon surgery/scar · limited wrist extension

**Open (Kaplan's cardinal line)** — 1st web → hook of hamate = **superficial palmar arch**; incision in line with **ulnar border of long finger**; stay ulnar

**No benefit** — internal neurolysis, **tenosynovectomy** (idiopathic), antebrachial fascia release, Guyon release (decompressed anyway)

**Lengthen TCL** — only if flexor tendon repair done (allows wrist flexion splint)

**Endoscopic vs open** — endoscopic: **↓ early pain/analgesia** (main advantage), earlier RTW, better early key pinch, ↓ scar tenderness; **long term = open**

**Endoscopic cons** — learning curve, **incomplete release** (commonest complication), ↑ revision, ↑ nerve injury risk

**Endoscopic contraindications (TO)** — **revision/recurrent CTS** · anatomic variant (APB wasting without sensory change) · previous tendon surgery/scar · **limited wrist extension**

**Recovery** — **pinch 6 wk**; **grip 100% of pre-op by 12 wk (3 mo)**

**Persistent symptoms at 1 y** — **2% moderate · 20% severe**

**Poor prognosis** — **severe EDS (EMG denervation)** = strongest predictor; DM; poor injection response; male

**Good prognosis** — **night symptoms**, relief with injection

**Worst EDS option** — **DML >4.5 AND DSL >3.5** (e.g. 5.0 / 4.0)

### Perioperative care & complications

**Antibiotics** — **not indicated** for clean elective hand surgery → **standard prep only**

**Analgesia** — **ibuprofen + paracetamol only** for soft-tissue procedures (CTR, trigger finger, cubital tunnel)

**Post-op** — neutral night splint 2–3 wk for comfort; ROM/nerve glides immediately; strengthen after 4 wk

## 6 Complications

| Complication | Key numbers / fact |
|---|---|
| **Scar tenderness** | **Most common** (19–61%); massage |
| **Pillar pain** | Thenar/hypothenar ache; 41% → 25% → 6% (1, 3, 12 mo) |
| **Incomplete TCL release** | **Commonest cause of failure/revision**; ↑ with endoscopic |
| Recurrence | 1% 5 y, 1.6% 10 y |
| **Recurrent motor branch** | Open 3% / endo 0.7%; transligamentous; → repair or **opponensplasty (FDS ring → APB, Bunnell)**; EIP/PL alternatives |
| **Palmar cutaneous branch** | **Open 19%** / endo 2.4%; painful neuroma |
| **Proper digital n. index** | → **lumbrical weakness** (index MCP flexion + PIP extension weak) |
| Arterial arch | Endo 0.02% |

**Revision CTR** — 25% complete relief, 25% none

## How it's asked

**"Most specific test?"** → **Hand diagram** (TO)

**"Most sensitive provocative?"** → **Durkan**

**"Most sensitive early sensory?"** → **Semmes-Weinstein**

**"Not a test for CTS"** → **Murphy** / Wartenberg

**"CTS not common in"** → **Addison's** (TO)

**"Not true"** → **endoscopic better in recurrent cases**\
✗ Wrong options: — open is used

**"Contents EXCEPT"** → **FCR**

**"TCL attaches to"** → **trapezium + hook of hamate**

**"Recurrent branch cut — deficit & how?"** → **opposition lost; cut TCL radially**

**"Median laceration but can oppose thumb"** → **Riche-Cannieu**

**"Laceration vs CTS — extra finding"** → **thenar eminence numbness**

**"Which muscle weak in CTS?"** → **APB**\
✗ Wrong options: FPL, PQ, FDP-I, adductor pollicis

**"Cock-up splint at 30°"** → **↑ pressure** (use neutral)

**"Counsel on injection"** → **80% improve / 20% last 1 y**

**"Splinted 8 wk, still symptomatic"** → **CTR**

**"Main advantage of endoscopic"** → **↓ post-op pain**

**"Grip at 3 mo"** → **100%**

**"Persistent sx at 1 y, severe CTS"** → **20%**

**"Predicts poor outcome"** → **EMG/EDS severity**

**"Reduce SSI"** → **standard prep; no antibiotics**

**"Weak index MCP flexion + PIP extension after CTR"** → **proper digital nerve to index (lumbrical)**
