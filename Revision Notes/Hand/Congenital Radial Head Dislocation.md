# Congenital Radial Head Dislocation

*Hand · 14 / 103*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology & pathoanatomy

**Incidence** — rare, **<1 per 100,000**

**Direction** — **posterior ~70%** > anterior 15% = lateral 15%

**Laterality** — **bilateral in majority**; unilateral congenital form can occur (Agnew)

**Pathoanatomy** — **failure of capitellum development** → loss of contact pressure → abnormal radial head → altered PRUJ → abnormal ulna

**Radius** — **bowed**, appears **too long vs ulna**

**Associations** — other congenital anomalies; **radial longitudinal deficiency**, **proximal radioulnar synostosis**; syndromes; familial

## 3 Presentation

**Symptoms** — often **asymptomatic**, **painless**; radial head prominence (posterolateral)

**ROM** — limited, **especially extension & supination**; usually functional

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Diagnostic criteria – congenital vs traumatic

| Clinical | Radiographic |
|---|---|
| **Bilateral** | **Dome-shaped (convex) radial head**, long narrow neck |
| Present at birth, familial | **Hypoplastic / absent capitellum** |
| Other congenital anomalies | **Short ulna / long radius**, bowed radius |
| **No trauma history** | Deficient trochlea, prominent medial epicondyle |
| **Irreducible by closed methods** | Grooving distal radius; anterior curvature of posterior ulnar outline |

![CRHD diagnostic criteria](../../MCQ/attachments/q5359-illc-criteria.jpg)

### Key radiology findings

**Radiocapitellar line** — line along radius long axis should intersect capitellum on all views → misses = dislocation

**X-ray** — radial head **posterior to capitellum**; **convex "dome" radial head**; long, bowed radius

**Next step (suspected CRHD)** — **X-ray contralateral elbow** → confirms bilateral

![Posterior CRHD, convex radial head](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob6069-congenital-dx-of-radial-head.jpg)

## 5 Management

```
CRHD
├─ Asymptomatic / functional ROM → OBSERVATION
└─ Pain / restricted motion / cosmesis (usually adulthood) → RADIAL HEAD RESECTION
       (treat each elbow on its own symptoms)
```

### Diagnosis (bilateral, vs traumatic) & management – observation vs late excision

**First line** — **observation**\
✗ Wrong options: closed reduction · open reduction ± osteotomy · child-abuse workup without other findings

**Radial head resection** — adult, symptomatic → **↓ pain**, improves **forearm rotation**; no change in flex-ext or carrying angle (Bengard)

**Resection downside** — **>25% wrist pain** post-op

**Avoid open reduction** — high **redislocation** (even with ulnar/radial osteotomy or annular ligament reconstruction) — hypoplastic capitellum, dome head, long radius

**Avoid radial head arthroplasty** — redislocation (undeveloped capitellum, non-collinear head)

**Not interposition arthroplasty** — for ulnohumeral arthritis, not radial head disease

**Not physiotherapy** — no gain in pain/ROM

## X vs Y

| | **Congenital RHD** | **Traumatic RHD** |
|---|---|---|
| Laterality | **Bilateral** | Unilateral |
| Radial head | **Convex / dome** | Normal concave |
| Capitellum | **Hypoplastic** | Normal |
| Trauma Hx | None | Present |
| Closed reduction | **Difficult / irreducible** | — |

## How it's asked

**"Atraumatic nontender posterolateral elbow bump, dome radial head — next step?"** → **X-ray contralateral elbow**

**"7-y with convex radial head, vague fall, lacks 20° extension — treatment?"** → **Observation**\
✗ Wrong options: abuse workup · reduction

**"Bilateral CRHD, right painful + stiff, left minimal loss — plan?"** → **resect right radial head + nonop left**

**"Why not open reduction?"** → **redislocation**

**"Commonest direction?"** → **posterior**

**"Complication after radial head excision?"** → **wrist pain (>25%)**
