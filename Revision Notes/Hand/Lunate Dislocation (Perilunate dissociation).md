# Lunate Dislocation (Perilunate dissociation)

*Hand · 50 / 103*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology & categories

**Incidence** — rare, high energy; **missed in ~25%** at first presentation (Herzberg 25%, Dunn 27.5%)

**Carpal instability class (TO)** — **CIC** (complex) = **perilunate dislocations & fracture-dislocations** → intrinsic (SL, LT = dissociative) + extrinsic (midcarpal capsule = non-dissociative) ± fractures\
✗ Wrong options: pure LT dissociation · Colles dorsal tilt · RA ulnar translocation

**Perilunate dislocation** — **lunate stays in fossa**, carpus dislocates (usually dorsal)

**Lunate dislocation** — lunate forced volar (or dorsal), carpus aligned → lunate usually **into carpal tunnel**

**Perilunate types** — transscaphoid-perilunate · perilunate · transradial-styloid · transscaphoid-transcapitate-perilunate

**Most common variant** — **dorsal trans-scaphoid perilunate fracture-dislocation** (OB, TO)

| Arc | Injury |
|---|---|
| **Lesser arc** | **Purely ligamentous** (rapid force) |
| **Greater arc** | Ligament + fractures of radius, ulna or carpus (slower force: transradial styloid, transscaphoid, transcapitate) |

![Greater vs lesser arc](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob6045-greater-and-lesser.jpg)

### Ligament anatomy

**SLIL / LTIL** — interosseous; major proximal-row stabilisers; SL prevents **DISI**, LT tear → **VISI**

**Space of Poirier** — weak volar area **between RSC and long radiolunate** (OB) · between radiocapitate and LRL (TO); over capitolunate joint\
✗ Wrong options: between dorsal intercarpal and radiotriquetral

**Short radiolunate (SRL)** — **remains intact**, tethers lunate to radius\
✗ Wrong options: first ligament injured

**Dorsal intercarpal** — typically remains intact

**Ligament of Testut (RSL)** — neurovascular conduit, little structural role

## 2 Mechanism

### Mechanism – Mayfield progression

**Position** — **wrist extension + ulnar deviation + intercarpal supination**; axial load, FOOSH, fall from height

**Ligament order** — **SL → capitolunate → LT → dorsal radiocarpal** → lunate rotates + dislocates\
✗ Wrong options: SL → LT → CL · DRC first

**First ligament injured** — **scapholunate**\
✗ Wrong options: SRL

**Bony sequence (Mayfield)** — **scaphoid extension → scaphoid failure (space of Poirier opens) → distal row dissociation → triquetral hyperextension → LT failure → dorsal dislocation of carpus**

**Reduction (Mayfield)** — intercarpal **pronation, radial deviation, palmar flexion**

![Progressive perilunar instability: clockwise ligament disruption](../../MCQ/attachments/q219974-illa-progression-of-lig-disruptions.jpg)

## 3 Presentation

**Symptoms** — acute swelling, pain, deformity

**Acute CTS** — **~25%** (Orthobullets) · **25–50%** (OB) · **25–45%** (TO) · 24–45% (Stanbury) · 50% (Dunn) — read the options; highest in **Mayfield IV**

**Vascular exam** — rarely abnormal; DRUJ injury uncommon

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

**Views** — **PA + lateral**; lateral = **most diagnostic** (TO)

| View | Sign |
|---|---|
| PA | **Break in Gilula's arcs** (3 arcs), lunate–capitate **overlap**, **↓ carpal height**, crowded carpus, SL diastasis |
| PA | **"Piece-of-pie" sign** = triangular lunate (palmar rotation) |
| Lateral | **Loss of collinearity radius–lunate–capitate**; SL angle >70° |
| Lateral | Perilunate: lunate in fossa, capitate dorsal · Lunate: **"spilled teacup"** lunate volar, capitate drops into fossa |
| CT | Fracture lines (trans-scaphoid/capitate), planning (TO) |
| MRI | **Not required** / doesn't aid diagnosis (OB); ligament extent (TO) |

**Radial styloid fracture** — avulsion of radiocarpal ligaments

![Lateral: spilled teacup](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob6045-spilled-teecup-sign.jpg)

### Mayfield classification

| Stage | Orthobullets / OB | TO wording | Image |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I** | **SL dissociation** | SL dissociation / **scaphoid fracture** / rotation | ![Mayfield 1](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob6045-mayfield-1.jpg) |
| **II** | + **lunocapitate** disruption | Lunocapitate dislocation through **space of Poirier** / perilunate dislocation | ![Mayfield 2](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob6045-mayfield-2.jpg) |
| **III** | + **LT** disruption = "perilunate" | LT disruption / triquetrum dislocation | ![Mayfield 3](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob6045-mayfield-3.jpg) |
| **IV** | **Lunate dislocated** from fossa (usually volar) → **median n. compression**; DRC fails | Lunate extruded volarly into carpal tunnel | ![Mayfield 4](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob6045-mayfield-4.jpg) |

**Stage I (TO)** — **"not always a scaphoid fracture"** ✓\
✗ Wrong options: LT injury · lunate extruded · RSC detachment

**Stage II (TO)** — capitate–lunate joint through **space of Poirier**

**Stage IV ligament** — **dorsal radiocarpal** fails (Orthobullets/OB) · long radiolunate shown disrupted (OB219974 illustration) — read the options

## 5 Management

```
Perilunate / lunate dislocation
├─ Acute → emergent closed reduction + splint (neuro exam before & after)
│      └─ then ORIF + ligament repair ± CTR (all <8 wk)
│             (worsening / persistent ACTS → emergent CTR + ORIF)
├─ Chronic >8 wk → proximal row carpectomy
└─ Chronic + degenerative change / failed PRC → total wrist arthrodesis
```

### Acute management – urgent reduction, neurological exam & missed diagnosis

**Next step** — **urgent closed reduction + splint**\
✗ Wrong options: cast · MR arthrogram · bone scan

**Before reduction** — **neurological exam** (repeat after)\
✗ Wrong options: vascular · Allen · MRI · DRUJ

**Technique** — finger traps, elbow 90°, **5–10 lb for 15 min** → **wrist extension + traction → flexion with dorsal pressure on carpus** (volar pressure on lunate for lunate dislocation); sugar-tong splint

**Benefit of emergent reduction** — ↓ **median nerve** damage, ↓ **cartilage** damage, ↓ AVN (TO)

**Median nerve (Budoff)** — usually static, non-progressive → no emergent release; **delayed onset / progressive CTS → emergent CTR + ORIF**

**Closed treatment definitive** — **never**; universally poor, redislocation

### Definitive ORIF ± carpal tunnel release

**Timing** — all acute <8 wk; surgery can wait **~4 d** for swelling (Budoff) · standard **within 7–10 d** (TO); **delay >3 wk** worsens outcome (TO)

**Lunate volar + median symptoms** — **ORIF via dorsal + volar approaches + extended CTR**\
✗ Wrong options: CR + cast · CRPP · ORIF without CTR · PRC acute

| Approach | Gives | Limits |
|---|---|---|
| Dorsal (Lister's) | Proximal row + midcarpal exposure, SL/LT repair | not for CTR |
| Volar (extended CT) | **CTR**, close space of Poirier rent, volar ligaments | — |
| Combined | Easier reduction, distal scaphoid, CTR | Swelling, devascularisation, ↓ flexion/grip |

**Steps** — fix fractures (scaphoid headless screw) · **SL repair with suture anchors** · protect with K-wires (SL, LT, scaphocapitate) or cerclage · LT repair optional

**Post-op** — thumb spica → cast **≥6 wk** (Orthobullets); **K-wires out 8–12 wk** (TO)

### Chronic/late injuries – proximal row carpectomy & RSC ligament

**Chronic (>8 wk)** — **PRC**; arthrodesis only if PRC fails / degenerative\
✗ Wrong options: ORIF: cartilage loss + contracture · more splint/cast

**PRC stability** — capitate head in lunate fossa + **preserve RSC** → prevents **ulnar translocation**\
✗ Wrong options: SRL · SL · RSL

**Radial styloidectomy** — **≤4 mm** to preserve RSC; **6 mm destabilises** (Nakamura)

**PRC approach** — dorsal (scaphoid, triquetrum) + volar (lunate + median decompression)

## 6 Complications

### Post-operative lunate ischaemia – observation

**Transient lunate ischaemia** — radiodense lunate in **12.5%**, **1–4 mo** post-injury → **observation** (benign, self-limiting; no Kienböck progression)

**Not Kienböck treatments** — STT pinning (adolescent), capitate shortening (Lichtman II–III), PRC (advanced), cast (stage I)

| Outcome | Figure |
|---|---|
| Post-traumatic arthritis | **40–60%** (TO); **56% at 6 y** (Herzberg); well tolerated, does not correlate with symptoms |
| Wrist motion loss | **30–50%** (TO) |
| Grip | **70–80%** of contralateral (TO) |
| Persistent paraesthesia | 17.5% (Dunn) |
| Other | Scaphoid nonunion/AVN (trans-scaphoid), lunate AVN, full function unlikely |

## X vs Y

| | **Perilunate dislocation** | **Lunate dislocation** |
|---|---|---|
| Mayfield | III | **IV** |
| Lunate | **In fossa** | **Volar, in carpal tunnel** |
| Capitate | Dorsal to lunate | Back in line with radius |
| Lateral | Capitate off lunate | **Spilled teacup** |
| ACTS | Less | **Highest** |

## How it's asked

**"True of Mayfield stage I?"** → **not always a scaphoid fracture** (TO)

**"CIC is best represented by?"** → **perilunate dislocations and fracture-dislocations** (TO)

**"Mayfield II disrupts?"** → **capitolunate joint through space of Poirier** (TO)

**"Sequence of perilunate injury (bony)?"** → **scaphoid extension → scaphoid failure → distal row dissociation → LT failure → dorsal carpal dislocation**

**"First ligament injured?"** → **scapholunate**\
✗ Wrong options: SRL · DRC

**"Order of ligament failure?"** → **SL → CL → LT → DRC**

**"Perilunate + thumb/index paraesthesia — next step?"** → **urgent closed reduction + splint**

**"Before reduction obtain?"** → **neurological exam**

**"True statement?"** → **missed in ~25%**\
✗ Wrong options: ACTS rare · SRL first · MRI aids · Poirier dorsal

**"Volar lunate + hand numbness — definitive?"** → **ORIF dorsal + volar + extended CTR**

**"Stage IV + median numbness — definitive?"** → **ORIF + carpal tunnel decompression**

**"PRC — preserve which ligament?"** → **radioscaphocapitate**

**"Perilunate missed 8 wk ('sprain'), limited ROM?"** → **PRC**\
✗ Wrong options: ORIF · arthrodesis

**"Radiodense lunate 2 mo after ORIF?"** → **observation**
