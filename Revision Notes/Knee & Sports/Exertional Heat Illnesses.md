# Exertional Heat Illnesses

*Knee & Sports · 14 / 61*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology & risk factors

**Cause** — **heat gain > heat loss** (production > dissipation)

**When / who** — ↑ in warmer months · highest in **American football**, running, cycling, adventure races

**Risk factors** — **poor heat acclimatisation**, **dehydration**, sleep deprivation, **prior heat illness** · conditioning, body composition, **protective equipment**, environment

**Heat stroke** — **3rd leading cause of death in athletes** (after cardiac disorders + cervical trauma)

**Football preseason** — **\~74% of EHI = heat cramps** · peak in **first 14 days** (esp. first 7) · risk ↑ when **WBGT ≥ 82°F (27.8°C)**

**Spectrum** — mild (heat oedema, rash, **cramps**, **syncope**) → moderate (**exhaustion**) → severe (**heat stroke**) · heat cramps **don't** predispose to heat stroke

## 3 Presentation

### Spectrum of exertional heat illness

| Condition | Key features | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **Heat syncope** | **Transient LOC**, peripheral vasodilation, ↓ cardiac output, **normal temperature** · rapid recovery supine | **Supine + legs elevated**, fluids |
| **Heat cramps / EAMC** | Painful spasm of large muscles · ↓ hydration, **↓ Na + Cl** · random muscles, spasms **1–3 min** | **Stretching** + oral fluids with **NaCl** |
| **Heat exhaustion** | Can't continue · **core < 39°C (102.2°F)** · **no CNS dysfunction** · **profuse sweat**, pale moist skin, nausea, vomiting, headache, weakness | Cooling, **oral** rehydration, monitor · loosen clothes, ice axilla + groin, cool sprays |
| Hypernatraemic heat exhaustion | **Inadequate water replacement** · thirst, oliguria | Water |
| **Heat stroke** | **Hyperthermia + CNS dysfunction + loss of thermoregulation** ± multi-organ failure | **Immediate cold-water immersion** |
| **Exertional rhabdomyolysis** | Muscle pain, weakness, **↑ CK**, **myoglobinuria** | Cooling, **aggressive IV fluids**, ± dialysis |

| Condition | Key features | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **Exercise-associated collapse (EAC)** | **Conscious** collapse **after finishing** · **loss of venous return** on stopping · **normal mental status, temp, vitals** · **most common in marathon tent (59–85%)** | Rule out serious causes → **oral fluids + Trendelenburg** → discharge |
| Exercise-associated postural hypotension | **SBP falls ≥ 20 mmHg** standing vs supine | |
| **Exercise-associated hyponatraemia** | **Na < 135 + mental status change** · dilutional | Severe → **EMS + IV hypertonic saline** |

**Heat exhaustion core temp** — **< 39°C (102.2°F)** (Orthobullets, OB) · **98.6–104°F** (OB211458) — read the options

### Heat stroke — definition, CNS dysfunction & cold-water immersion

**Key discriminator from heat exhaustion** — **CNS dysfunction** ✓\
✗ Wrong options: temp > 38°C · hypotension / dehydration · cramps + dizziness (dizziness isn't CNS dysfunction) · profuse sweating

**CNS signs** — confusion, disorientation, **bizarre behaviour (often first sign)**, staggering / **ataxia**, seizures, delirium, coma · **CNS depression = most important marker**

| Heat stroke core temperature | Source |
|---|---|
| **> 40°C (104°F)** | Orthobullets (printed "> 102.2°F or 40°C"), ACSM / AFP (OB) |
| **> 41°C (105.8°F)** | OB1428, OB8705 |
| **> 105°F** | OB211458 |
| May reach **106–110°F (41.1–43.3°C)** | OB8735 |

**Sweating** — classic teaching: **anhidrosis, hot dry skin** (OB "hallmark") · **exertional** heat stroke **often sweat-soaked, pale** (OB219812) · sweating is an **inconsequential criterion** (OB211458) · dry, flushed skin → **non-exertional / classic** heat stroke

**Temperature** — **rectal = only reliable** · not oral, temporal, axillary, auricular (ACSM — don't use for diagnosis)

**Trigger for immediate cooling** — **collapse + mental status change** · rectal > 40°C + any CNS change → cool\
✗ Wrong options: temporal temp 39.4°C · anhidrosis · tachypnoea · hypotension (both conditions can show collapse, hypotension, tachypnoea)

## 5 Management

```
Athlete collapses in heat
├─ Mental status change (± rectal > 40°C) → heat stroke → cold-water immersion ON SITE
│      └─ then IV fluids + EMS → cool to 39°C first, transport second
├─ Alert, sweating, temp < 39°C → heat exhaustion → shade, cool, oral fluids
├─ Conscious, normal temp + vitals after finishing → EAC → oral fluids, Trendelenburg
└─ Endurance event, heavy water intake, confused → hyponatraemia → hypertonic saline
```

### Heat stroke treatment

**First step** — **rapid cooling: ice / cold-water immersion** = most effective whole-body method\
✗ Wrong options: IV fluids first · acetaminophen (antipyretic COX block, no effect) · supine + leg elevation (heat syncope) · IV antibiotics · benzodiazepines / Valium (may worsen) · electrolyte or low-osmolarity drinks (cramps / syncope) · warm bath · reverse Trendelenburg

**Cooling target** — **0.2°C / min** to **39°C** · other: cooling blankets, evaporative (fans, sprays), ice to large muscle groups, internal cooling, cool IV fluids

**Cool first, transport second** — whole-body cooling **on site before evacuation** unless other medical emergency (Orthobullets, ACSM, OB219812) · OB8735 key = **transport to ED** with IV fluids + cooling **during transfer** — choose immersion if offered

**Outcome** — **cooling within 1 h of onset → usually full recovery** · on-site immersion safe (half-marathon: 68% treated on site, 59% discharged home)

**Then** — IV resuscitation + treat complications · EMS activation

**RTP after heat stroke** — **no evidence-based guideline** · **rest 7 days** → gradual increase over **next month** → cleared when **heat tolerance returns** (medically supervised)

### Exercise-associated hyponatraemia & collapse

**Ironman, 60°F, heavy bottled-water intake, collapses** — **hyponatraemia**\
✗ Wrong options: hypernatraemia · hypothermia · subendocardial MI · berry aneurysm

**Cause** — **excess free (hypotonic) water** not replacing **sweat Na losses** · usually **> 3 h** ingestion · endurance athletes (triathlon, ultra-marathon, marathon)

**Na thresholds** — **< 135** (Orthobullets prints mg/dl; OB mEq/L) · up to **10% of ultradistance athletes ≤ 135 mEq/L** (mostly asymptomatic) · symptomatic usually **\~125 mEq/L**

**Presentation** — **mental status change**, peripheral paresthesias, **extremity swelling**

**Correction** — **severe acute → rapid correction** (avert **brainstem herniation**) · mild → **oral salty solutions** · **too-rapid correction → central pontine myelinolysis** (OB) — read the options

**Prevention** — fluid balance · **sports drinks (4–8% carbohydrate) for exercise > 1 h** · water best early; electrolyte drinks later prevent dilutional hyponatraemia

**Soldier collapses after 2-mile run, conscious, rectal 37.9°C, BP drop 10 mmHg, recovers** — **exercise-associated collapse**\
✗ Wrong options: heat stroke (no CNS change) · cramps · postural hypotension (needs ≥ 20 mmHg) · hyponatraemia

### Prevention — wet bulb globe temperature

**Wet bulb temperature** — thermometer **wrapped in a wet cloth** → reflects **evaporative (sweat) cooling** capacity in heat + humidity

| Wet bulb temperature | Action |
|---|---|
| **82–86°F** | **1:1 practice : rest** |
| **> 86°F** | **Postpone / cancel** participation (✗ Wrong options: > 75 · > 83 · > 90 · > 93°F) |

**Other prevention** — **heat acclimatisation** (gradual ↑ activity in heat), hydration, activity modification, **immersion tub on site at high-risk events**

## X vs Y

| | **Heat exhaustion** | **Heat stroke** |
|---|---|---|
| CNS | **Intact** | **Dysfunction** (confusion, ataxia, seizure) |
| Core temp | **< 39°C** | **> 40°C** (OB also > 41°C) |
| Skin | Profuse sweat, pale, moist | Classic dry hot · exertional often sweaty |
| Treatment | Cool, oral fluids | **Cold-water immersion on site** |

| | **Heat syncope** | **EAC** |
|---|---|---|
| Timing | During heat exposure | **After finishing** |
| Mechanism | Vasodilation, ↓ cardiac output | **Loss of venous return** on stopping |
| Treatment | Supine, legs up, fluids | Oral fluids, Trendelenburg |

## How it's asked

**"Hyperthermia + CNS dysfunction + loss of thermoregulation?"** → **heat stroke**

**"41°C, dry skin, disoriented, convulsing — most important step?"** → **rapid cooling with ice immersion**

**"Best description of heat stroke?"** → **hyperthermia, CNS dysfunction, loss of thermoregulatory function**

**"Collapsed, confused, not sweating, 80°F + 80% humidity?"** → **transportation to the emergency department**

**"Ataxic, disoriented, 105°F, no sweat?"** → **heat stroke**

**"Lineman confused, 104.5°, no sweat — initial step?"** → **rapid ice water immersion**

**"Finding that mandates immediate cooling in collapsed soldier?"** → **mental status changes**

**"Feature differentiating heat stroke from exhaustion?"** → **presence of CNS dysfunction**

**"Ironman collapse after drinking lots of water?"** → **hyponatraemia**

**"Conscious collapse after 2-mile run, normal temp?"** → **exercise-associated collapse**

**"Wet bulb threshold to postpone sport?"** → **greater than 86 degrees Fahrenheit**
