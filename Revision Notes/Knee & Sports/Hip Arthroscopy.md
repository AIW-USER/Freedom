# Hip Arthroscopy

*Knee & Sports · 18 / 61*

## 1 Basics

### Overview & indications

**Why hard** — deep joint, **high congruity** (vs knee / shoulder) → steep learning curve

**Growth** — **250% ↑** in US 2007–2011 (Orthobullets) · **88-fold ↑** since late 1990s (OB)

**Advantage** — lower morbidity than open arthrotomy, faster early recovery

| Intra-articular indications | Extra-articular indications |
|---|---|
| FAI lesions, labral tears | **AIIS subspine impingement** |
| AVN — diagnosis + staging | Capsular tears / instability |
| **Loose bodies**, **chondral defects** | **Iliopsoas tendon** pathology |
| **Ligamentum teres** injury | **Gluteal tears**, trochanteric bursal debridement |
| **Synovial disease**, **septic arthritis** | **Ischiofemoral impingement**, **deep gluteal syndrome** |
| Impinging osteophytes, unexplained mechanical symptoms | **Piriformis release**, **proximal hamstring tears** |

**Contraindications (Orthobullets)** — **advanced DJD**, **hip ankylosis**, **acetabular dysplasia**, **joint contracture**, **severe osteoporotic bone**, significant protrusio acetabuli

### Compartments

| Compartment | Traction? | Contents |
|---|---|---|
| **Central** | **Yes** | Acetabular fossa, head articular surfaces, ligamentum teres, rim + labrum, AIIS + capsule |
| **Peripheral** (lateral to labrum) | No | **Head-neck junction**, **iliopsoas tendon**, **zona orbicularis**, medial synovial fold |
| Lateral (peritrochanteric) | No | IT band + bursa, gluteus medius / minimus tendons |
| Deep gluteal space | No | Piriformis + **sciatic nerve**, hamstring origin (ischial tuberosity) |

### Arthroscopic landmarks – zona orbicularis

**Zona orbicularis** — circular ring of capsule · **landmark for iliopsoas** tendon lying **immediately deep** to it\
✗ Wrong options: pectineus · sartorius · adductor brevis · rectus femoris

**Use** — pericapsular **iliopsoas release** for **internal snapping hip** (tendon over iliopectineal eminence or femoral head) · alternative: release at lesser trochanter

**Stability** — proximal–middle capsule incl. zona orbicularis → greatest influence on stability during distraction (Ito)

## 3 Presentation

### Clinical sign of intra-articular pathology (C sign)

**Byrd C sign (TO)** — **index finger over anterior hip + thumb over posterior trochanteric region** → **intra-articular hip pathology**\
✗ Wrong options: knee · ankle · shoulder

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings (pre-arthroscopy screen)

| Measurement | Normal | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| **Tönnis grade** (OA) | 0–1 | **Grade 2–3 → contraindication** (failure, conversion to THA) |
| **LCEA** | **> 25°** (28° normal) | **< 20° → dysplasia** (e.g. 15°) |
| **ACEA** | — | **< 20° → dysplasia** |
| **Tönnis (roof) angle** | **0–10°** (5°, 8° normal) | **> 10° → dysplasia** (e.g. 15°) |
| **Alpha angle** | **≤ 42°** (40° normal) | **> 42° → cam** → treatable, not a contraindication |
| **Head-neck offset ratio** | **≥ 0.17** (0.2 normal) | **< 0.17 → cam** → treatable |

## 5 Management

### Setup, positioning & joint access

**Kit** — **70° arthroscope**, long cannulas + guides, flexible instruments · fluoroscopy + arthroscopy towers both visible

**Position** — **supine or lateral decubitus**, fracture / dedicated table · **well-padded perineal post**

**Traction** — **~50 lb** to distract · Trendelenburg ↓ force needed

**Access** — spinal needle at **AL portal** into central compartment under **fluoroscopy** (avoid head + labrum) → saline distension or venting (air arthrogram) → guidewire, cannula, scope → later portals under direct vision

### Portals & nerve injury (LFCN, sciatic)

| Portal | Location | Use | At risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Anterolateral (AL)** | **2 cm anterior + 2 cm superior** to anterosuperior GT (Orthobullets) · **1 cm proximal + 1 cm anterior** to GT tip (TO) | **First** (fluoroscopy, only blind portal) · **primary viewing** | **Superior gluteal nerve** (> 4 cm proximal, TO) · **chondrolabral injury = biggest risk (TO)** |
| **Anterior (AP)** | Line down from **ASIS** × line across top of GT, moved lateral · ~**6 cm distal to ASIS** through sartorius + rectus femoris (OB) | Central compartment (**4:30–8:30 o'clock**) | **LFCN** + **lateral femoral circumflex artery** · femoral NV bundle if medial |
| **Distal anterolateral (DALA)** | **3–5 cm distal to AL** · traction off, neutral or **45° flexion** | Peripheral compartment (neck) | **Ascending branch LFCA** |
| Mid-anterior (MAP) | Between anterior and AL | As anterior | — |
| **Posterolateral (PL)** | **1 cm posterior + 1 cm proximal** to posterosuperior GT tip (Orthobullets, TO) · **2–3 cm posterior** to GT tip (OB) · **neutral rotation** | Posterior joint | **Sciatic nerve** (2.9 cm) · deep branch **MFCA** (> 1 cm) (TO) |

![Hip arthroscopy portals — AP, ALP, PLP relative to ASIS and greater trochanter](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob3100-portals.jpg)

**LFCN distance from anterior portal** — **within 3 mm** (TO) · **15.4 mm** average (OB, Robertson) — read the options

**LFCA distance (TO)** — femoral nerve + ascending LFCA ~**4 cm** · terminal LFCA branches **as close as 2 mm** · transverse LFCA branch **10.1 mm** (OB)

**LFCN at portal level** — divided into **≥ 3 branches** · runs from inguinal ligament along sartorius, variable branching

**Protect LFCN** — **"nick and spread"**: skin-only incision + blunt dissection · stay **lateral to vertical line from ASIS** (femoral artery / vein safe)

**Medialised anterior portal** — no better view · ↑ risk to **femoral vessels + LFCN** (Thorey) · too medial → **femoral nerve**

**Anterolateral thigh numbness** — **LFCN from anterior portal** · AL / distal lateral portals can also hurt LFCN but anterior = greatest risk\
✗ Wrong options: AL portal → SGN · traction → pudendal · fluid extravasation · orthosis

**Sciatic nerve + rotation** — **ER brings sciatic closer** to PL portal → never ER during entry; IR moves it away (OB, Orthobullets) · ↑ risk with **IR or ER** (TO) — read the options

**Standard central portals** — AL, anterior, PL (Byrd) · reference for all others

### Traction-related complications (pudendal nerve)

**Complications mostly due to** — **traction**\
✗ Wrong options: lateral positioning · supine positioning · DVT · HO

**Pudendal nerve** — **commonest neurovascular complication** · compression by **perineal post** → **numb inner thigh + hemiscrotum / groin**\
✗ Wrong options: obturator (no scrotal supply) · LFCN (anterolateral thigh) · pudendal from DALA portal

**Pudendal rates** — **1.8%** (62/3405), **4.3% with post** + longer traction · all resolve 6 wk–3 mo (Habib) · **2%** sensory neuralgia, resolve 3 wk–6 mo (Pailhe)

**Prevent pudendal** — limit traction force + time, padded post **> 8–10 cm** diameter, **post-less** surgery

**Peroneal nerve** — traction or ill-fitting boot → **dorsum of foot** · prevent: **intermittent traction release**, **total traction < 2 h**, **adequate muscle paralysis**

**Overall rates** — complications **3.3%**, nerve injury **0.9%** → **pudendal + LFCN** commonest (Nakano) · Byrd 10-yr series: only 2 complications

**Obturator nerve** — medial thigh sensation + adductors · deep + medial to AL portal → not at risk

### Operative criteria — contraindications (arthritis, dysplasia, age)

| Factor | Contraindication / poor prognosis | Not a contraindication |
|---|---|---|
| Arthritis | **Tönnis grade 2–3** | Acetabular chondrosis (treat at scope) |
| Dysplasia | **LCEA < 20°**, **ACEA < 20°**, **Tönnis angle > 10°** | LCEA 28°, Tönnis angle 5–8° |
| Cam | — | **Alpha 60°**, offset ratio 0.2 (treat it) |
| Age | > 60 → conversion to THA | **< 40 → positive** prognostic (27, 25 yr) |
| Smoking | ↑ poor outcome | **Not** a contraindication |
| Tear site | — | **Anterosuperior** (commonest) |

**Dysplasia + isolated labral repair** — fails (primary cause unaddressed) → **PAO ± arthroscopic labral repair**

**Dysplasia outcomes** — failure **29% vs 2.5%** FAI-only (Vahedi) · **32.2%** failure (Larson) → avoid even mild–moderate dysplasia

**Failed arthroscopy (Bogunovic)** — **37% OA, 43% under-treated FAI, 15% dysplasia** (one OB) · **residual FAI 68%, dysplasia 24%** (another OB) — all visible on preop imaging

**ANCHOR / Clohisy** — standardised radiographs, always read with history + exam

### Conversion to THA after arthroscopy

| Risk factor (Kester) | Odds ratio |
|---|---|
| **Obesity (BMI > 30)** | **5.6** |
| **Age > 60** | **3.4** — most predictive in a 64-yr-old (✗ Wrong options: BMI 27) |
| Hip OA | 2.4 |
| Surgeon **< 40 cases / yr** | 1.9 |
| Tobacco | 1.9 |
| Female | 1.8 |

**Kester** — conversion **5.9%** at 14.7 mo · ipsilateral knee OA not a risk factor

**Degen** — revision **13.2%** at 1.7 yr · THA **9.6%** · OA HR 2.72, **age > 50** HR 2.09, loose body removal HR 1.61 · protective: non-white HR 0.74, surgeon **> 164 cases / yr** HR 0.42

**Age threshold** — **> 50–60** depending on study · protective: male, non-white, high-volume surgeon

### Rehabilitation

**Protocol** — preop PT · foot-flat partial WB → full WB · **early ROM** · strengthening after full ROM · **iliopsoas tendinitis common**

## 6 Complications

| Complication | Cause / risk | Prevention / Rx |
|---|---|---|
| **Iatrogenic chondrolabral injury** | **Commonest reported complication** · scope / instruments (AL portal blind) | Instruments under direct vision |
| **Instability** | Capsular insufficiency · imprecise capsulotomy, **no capsular closure** | Careful capsulotomy, **closure / plication** |
| **Traction neurapraxia** | **Pudendal** (post), **peroneal** (boot / traction) | Post-less, < 2 h, intermittent release |
| **Portal nerve injury** | SGN (AL), **LFCN (anterior)**, **sciatic (PL, ER)**, ascending LFCA (DALA) | Lateral portals, nick and spread |
| **Fluid extravasation** | Long surgery, high pump pressure · rare **abdominal compartment syndrome** | Low pressure · **iliopsoas tenotomy last** |
| **Heterotopic ossification** | — | **Routine postop NSAIDs** |
| Conversion to THA | Obesity, age > 60, female, tobacco, low-volume surgeon, OA | Patient selection |

| "Most common" (read the stem) | Answer |
|---|---|
| Reported complication overall | **Iatrogenic chondral injury** (Orthobullets, OB) |
| Complications related to | **Traction** (OB) |
| Neurovascular complication | **Pudendal nerve** (traction / post) |
| Nerve injuries (Nakano) | **Pudendal + LFCN** |
| Portal-related nerve | **LFCN** (anterior portal) |
| Postop dislocation | **Anterior** |

### Postoperative dislocation

**Pattern** — **anterior** dislocation commonest after arthroscopy · hip in **extension + ER** (Duplantier)

**Management** — **immediate closed reduction → restrict extension + ER**\
✗ Wrong options: emergent ORIF (only irreducible / fracture) · restrict flexion + IR (posterior precautions) · 24 h traction first · vascular surgery consult

**Sequel** — progressive arthritis possible (Wylie)

|---|---|---|

| **Groin / perineum / hemiscrotum / inner thigh** | **Pudendal** | **Perineal post** compression |
