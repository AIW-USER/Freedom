# Athletic Pubalgia & Adductor strain

*Knee & Sports · 6 / 61*

## 1 Basics

**Definition (TO)** — **chronic lower abdominal + groin pain without a true hernia** · aka **sports hernia, Gilmore's groin, core muscle injury (CMI)**

**Who** — **males > females** (TO: **narrower male pelvis** → greater force shifts, less stability) · **hockey + soccer** · high-level athletes (forces ↑ as strength ↑) (TO)\
✗ Wrong options: more common in females

**Sports hip triad** — **adductor strain + osteitis pubis + athletic pubalgia** (adductor strain itself: see Adductor Strain chapter)

### Anatomy — the pubic fulcrum (TO)

**Fulcrum (TO)** — **pubic symphysis** = common attachment of **rectus abdominis + adductor longus**\
✗ Wrong options: main muscle transversus abdominis

**Pubic aponeurosis (TO)** — **rectus abdominis + conjoint tendon (internal oblique + transversus abdominis) + external oblique** → confluent with **adductor longus + gracilis** at the symphysis

**Opposing pull (TO)** — rectus abdominis vs adductors act in **opposite directions** on the symphysis → **imbalance = origin of pubalgia**

**Pain source** — injury to the **common aponeurosis between rectus abdominis and adductor longus** (Orthobullets)

## 2 Mechanism

### Mechanism & surgical referral

**Movement** — **repetitive hip extension + abduction** (± external rotation) with **eccentric adductor contraction** · abdominal hyperextension + thigh abduction (Orthobullets)\
✗ Wrong options: extension + adduction · flexion + abduction / adduction · extension + IR · flexion + IR · lumbar hyperextension

**Imbalance** — **strong adductors vs weak rectus** (lower-limb-focused training) (OB) · **strong adductors vs weak hip abductors** (OB)

**Type** — overuse microtrauma or acute trauma

**Activities (TO)** — running, kicking, cutting, twisting, explosive turns, change of direction

**What tears** — shear on rectus → **transversalis fascia, rectus, adductor magnus origin** (OB) · **adductor longus** (Orthobullets, coronal MRI) · inguinal hernia + **internal oblique** micro-tears at surgery (OB) — read the options

## 3 Presentation

**Pain** — lower abdominal + **inguinal pain at extremes of exertion** · **unilateral**, exercise-related, radiates to **perineum, inner thigh, scrotum** (TO) · settles with rest, **returns with play** (TO)\
✗ Wrong options: bilateral

**Exam** — **tender adductor longus** · tender **pubic tubercle / inguinal region**, pain over pubic rami · **pain with Valsalva + sit-ups** (rectus aponeurosis) · pain on **resisted hip adduction** · **not** worse with hip rotation

**No palpable hernia** — on Valsalva · differentiate from **subtle true inguinal hernia**

**Check hip** — **concomitant FAI** may coexist

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Study | Finding / use |
|---|---|
| X-ray, MRI, bone scan | **Rule out other causes** (Orthobullets) |
| **MRI** | **Oedema adjacent to the aponeurosis** · coronal T2: adductor longus tear **retracted from common aponeurosis** · "helpful for definitive diagnosis" (OB) vs "**not very helpful**" / diagnosis **largely clinical** (other OB) — read the options |
| CT pelvis | Rules out **bony injury (osteitis pubis)** |
| Scrotal US | **No added value** |

## 5 Management

```
Athletic pubalgia
├─ First line → REST + PT (core) ± NSAIDs / injections
└─ Failed nonoperative → SPORTS HERNIA SURGEON
       ├─ Pelvic floor / posterior wall repair, rectus reattachment
       └─ Adductor pain + dysfunction → ADDUCTOR TENOTOMY / RELEASE
```

### Nonoperative

**Duration** — **6–8 weeks** (Orthobullets, OB) · core stability **≥ 2 months** before surgery (OB) · surgery if no improvement in **3 months** (TO) — read the options

**Physiotherapy (TO)** — **core strengthening + stabilisation**, restore **pelvic tilt**, postural training

**Drugs (TO)** — NSAIDs, **oral steroid taper**

**Injections (TO)** — selective **corticosteroid or PRP** into rectus abdominis and / or adductor longus origin

### Operative criteria

| Indication | Procedure |
|---|---|
| **Failed extensive nonoperative care** | **Pelvic floor repair (hernia operation)** · mesh hernia repair · **adductor / rectus recession** |
| Failed nonop (TO) | **Reinforce posterior wall + fix rectus abdominis or conjoint tendon** |
| **Adductor pain + dysfunction** (TO) | **Adductor tenotomy** / release |
| Failed nonop (Orthobullets) | **Decompress genital branch of genitofemoral nerve** |

**Pro soccer player, failed nonop, no hernia, malignancy excluded** — **refer to a sports hernia surgeon** (strengthen anterior pelvic floor)\
✗ Wrong options: more nonop · scrotal US · CT pelvis · **cortisone — doesn't strengthen pelvic floor**

**Groin pain, normal imaging** — tends **not to improve without hernia-type surgery** (OB)

**Outcome** — rectus / adductor **aponeurotic plate repair → 96% return to sport**

## X vs Y

| Sports hip triad | **Athletic pubalgia** | **Adductor strain** | **Osteitis pubis** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lesion | Rectus–adductor **aponeurosis** | **Adductor longus** (pubic ramus) | **Pubic symphysis** inflammation |
| Onset | Chronic, exertional | **Acute**, immediate | Gradual, vague |
| Tender | **Inguinal region / lower abdomen**, adductor longus | Subcutaneous border of pubic ramus | **Directly over symphysis** |
| Provoked by | **Sit-ups, Valsalva** | **Resisted adduction** | Resisted rectus testing, adduction / abduction |
| Imaging | Oedema at aponeurosis | Avulsion / oedema at pubis | **Erosions, symphysis widening**, marrow oedema |
| Failed nonop | **Surgery** | Rehab (repair unproven) | Rest (self-limiting) |

## How it's asked

**"True about athletic pubalgia (TO)?"** → **fulcrum is the pubic symphysis**\
✗ Wrong options: more common in females · bilateral radiating pain · transversus abdominis main muscle

**"Movements contributing to athletic pubalgia?"** → **hip extension and abduction**

**"Failed nonop, no hernia, malignancy excluded — next?"** → **referral to a sports hernia surgeon**
