# Femoroacetabular Impingement (FAI)

*Knee & Sports · 16 / 61*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology & natural history

**Definition** — abnormal femur–acetabulum contact → **labral damage**, chondral injury, progressive hip pain

**Who** — deformity common + often asymptomatic · symptomatic with **extreme ROM** (ballet, gymnastics, martial arts) · **hockey goalies, gymnasts, dancers** → labral tears (repetitive FADIR)

**Onset** — active young adult, slow-onset groin pain, often after minor trauma

**Elite athletes** — prone to **cam** → linked to ↑ training intensity during skeletal development

**Natural history** — early hip dysfunction + arthritis · **FAI = most common cause of OA in nondysplastic hips**\
✗ Wrong options: Perthes · traumatic labral tear · repetitive high-impact sport · dashboard injury

### Anatomy (capsule, labrum, ligamentum teres)

**Acetabulum** — ilium + ischium + pubis · femoral head anteverted **15°** relative to condyles

**Capsular ligaments** — **iliofemoral (Y ligament of Bigelow)**, ischiofemoral, pubofemoral

**Iliofemoral** — **strongest** capsular ligament → restrains **extension + ER** → key restraint vs **microinstability** · **cut during capsulotomy**

**Labrum** — horseshoe fibrocartilage, joined inferiorly by **transverse acetabular ligament** · ↑ acetabular volume + **suction seal**

**Labral seal** — negative intra-articular pressure, retains synovial fluid · no labrum → ↑ cartilage loading + ↓ resistance to distraction

**Ligamentum teres** — cotyloid fossa → femoral head · **negligible** blood supply in adult

**Ligament arrangement** — iliofemoral, pubofemoral + ischiofemoral form a **helical spiral** around the proximal femur

### Cam vs pincer pathomorphology & chondrolabral damage patterns

| | **Cam** | **Pincer** |
|---|---|---|
| Side | **Femoral** | **Acetabular** |
| Typical patient | **Young athletic males** | **Active middle-aged women** |
| Site | Anterolateral neck / anterosuperior head-neck junction | Anterosuperior acetabular rim |
| Morphology | **↓ head-neck ratio**, **aspherical head**, **↓ femoral offset**, **femoral neck retroversion** | **AS rim overcoverage**, **acetabular retroversion**, **protrusio**, **coxa profunda** |
| Mechanism | Aspherical head (radius > acetabulum) jams in → **shear at chondrolabral junction** | Normal neck abuts abnormal rim → **crushes labrum** |
| Labrum | **Chondrolabral separation** — outside-in detachment, anterosuperior | **Intrasubstance tear** / contusion (coup) |
| Cartilage | **Delamination** anterosuperior — **precedes** labral tear | **Contrecoup posteroinferior** acetabulum (head levered back) |
| X-ray | **Pistol grip**, ↑ alpha angle, ↓ offset ratio | **Crossover sign**, posterior wall sign, ↑ LCEA |

**FAI (TO)** — **dynamic conflict**: early abutment of proximal femur on acetabulum · intra-articular impingement = cam or pincer

**Cam (TO)** — aspherical **antero-superior head-neck junction** rotates in → compression + shear at chondrolabral junction → **chondrolabral separation**, labral degeneration, **cartilage detached from subchondral bone**

**Pincer (TO)** — **excessive acetabular wall** → labrum compressed between femoral neck and rim

**Mixed cam + pincer** — **most common** type (up to **80%**)

**Only one lesion needed** — cam or pincer alone can cause symptoms

**SCFE** — epiphysis displaced posteriorly → prominent anterior neck, ↓ IR → **cam / pistol grip** (variable patterns) · externally rotated limb

**After PAO** — acetabulum may become **retroverted** → pincer

**FAI labral tears** — **anterosuperior, 10–2 o'clock**

**Pathologic anatomy of cam** — **morphologic abnormality of femoral head** · impingement in **flexion**\
✗ Wrong options: acetabular retroversion (pincer) · posteroinferior labral tears · femoral anteversion · osteonecrosis

**Cam pathology in a gymnast** — **↓ femoral head-neck offset**\
✗ Wrong options: AS rim overcoverage (pincer) · neck anteversion (cam = retroversion) · ↑ head-neck ratio · spherical head

**Horizontal growth plate sign** — pistol grip + **epiphyseal scar extending lateral to the best-fit circle** of the head → cam

![Bilateral pistol grip deformity (cam)](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob3130-bilateralpistolgrip.jpg)

| Lesion pattern seen | Means |
|---|---|
| **AS cartilage delamination → then labral tear at chondrolabral junction** | **Cam** ✓ |
| AS labral tear at labral-capsular junction → then delamination | Wrong order |
| **Posteroinferior acetabular cartilage lesion** | **Pincer contrecoup** |
| Posterosuperior acetabular eburnation · posterosuperior head fraying | OA |
| **Non-spherical head + AS chondral-labral separation** (scope) | **Cam** ✓ |
| **Anterior labral contusion + intrasubstance tear + posteroinferior chondral softening** | **Pincer** ✓ |
| Lateral acetabular cartilage softening + partial labral detachment | Cam |
| Posteriorly oriented acetabulum + AS labral tear | Retroversion (pincer) |
| Non-hemispherical acetabulum + **hypertrophic labrum** | **Dysplasia** |
| Tense iliopsoas + anterior labral tear · tear at **4 o'clock** + bursal inflammation | **Internal (iliopsoas) impingement** |
| AS femoral head cartilage flap + haemarthrosis | AVN |
| Gluteal enthesopathy + bursal scarring | GTPS (lateral pain, abductor weakness) |
| Ligamentum teres tear + patulous anterior capsule | Hip instability |

## 3 Presentation

### Symptoms

**Pain** — activity-related **groin pain ↑ with hip flexion** · worse with **prolonged sitting / driving** · better standing / lying · deep groin pain on squatting

**Mechanical** — clicking / popping (may be absent) · gluteal or trochanteric pain from aberrant gait

**Byrd C sign (TO)** — patient cups hip: **index finger over anterior hip + thumb over posterior trochanteric region** → **intra-articular hip pathology**\
✗ Wrong options: knee · ankle · shoulder

**Intra-articular injection relief** — temporary relief confirms intra-articular source

### Clinical examination – FADIR & loss of internal rotation

**Hallmark** — **↓ flexion + ↓ internal rotation** (flexion < 90°, IR < 5°)\
✗ Wrong options: ↑ ER · ↑ abduction · ↓ ER · ↓ adduction

**Anterior impingement test (FADIR)** — **supine, hip passively flexed 90°, adducted, internally rotated** → deep groin pain\
✗ Wrong options: abduction / ER combinations

**Posterior impingement test** — **extension + ER** → occasionally positive

**FADIR in FAI** — most sensitive exam · positive in cam, pincer and AS chondrolabral tears

![Anterior impingement test (flexion, adduction, internal rotation)](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob3130-anterior-impingement.jpg)

| Exam finding | Points to (not FAI) |
|---|---|
| **Stinchfield** — groin pain on resisted hip flexion | Femoral neck fracture, **iliopsoas tendonitis**, abscess |
| **FABER** pain | Lumbar spine, iliopsoas, **sacroiliitis** (not routine for FAI) |
| Trendelenburg gait | Abductor weakness |
| **Half sit-up pain + pubic ramus tenderness** | **Athletic pubalgia** |
| Greater trochanter tenderness | Trochanteric bursitis |
| Pain with axial loading, female athlete triad | **Femoral neck stress fracture** |
| Symphysis tenderness, resisted adduction / abduction | Osteitis pubis |
| Pain on resisted hip flexion / sit-up | Adductor / hip flexor strain |

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| View / study | Technique | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **AP pelvis + true lateral** | Lateral with hip in **15° IR** | Recommended routine views (Orthobullets) |
| **Frog-leg lateral** | — | Alpha angle (Orthobullets method) |
| **45° modified Dunn (TO)** | Supine, **45° flexion, 20° abduction, neutral rotation** | **Best view for cam** (✗ Wrong options: frog-leg · cross-table lateral · 90° sitting view) |
| **Cross-table lateral (TO)** | Hip in **10° IR** | Anterior head-neck offset ratio |
| **90° Dunn / seated (TO)** | Seated, **90° flexion, 20° abduction, neutral rotation** | Beta angle |
| **False profile (faux profil)** | **Standing**, affected hip on cassette, ipsilateral foot parallel, **pelvis rotated 65°** | **Anterior coverage** (DDH + FAI) → ACEA (✗ Wrong options: AP · inlet · outlet · frog lateral) |
| CT ± 3D | — | Adjunct for structure · **femoral version** (anteversion / retroversion) |
| **MRI / MR arthrogram** | In-line with femoral neck | **Best for labrum + cartilage** · fraying, tears, chondral damage, subchondral cysts |
| Bone scan | — | **Not** for FAI (used for FNSF when MRI impossible) |

**MRA triad (Kassarjian)** — **AS labral tear + AS cartilage lesion + ↑ alpha angle**

**Cam on MRI** — axial T2: **loss of sphericity of anterior head-neck** (alpha angle measurable, e.g. 80°) · fluid-sensitive images: **labrum separated from chondral surface** · coronal T1: no neck oedema\
✗ Wrong options: stress fracture

**Alpha angle method (other OB views)** — best-fit circle on head + line along neck axis → angle where head exits the circle · AP, frog-leg lateral or Dunn

**False profile extra** — shows cam deformity + **os acetabuli** (small ossicles beside acetabular roof)

**Cam lesion views (TO)** — frog-leg, cross-table lateral, 45° modified Dunn · anterolateral cam best on lateral + Dunn · quantified by **alpha angle + head-neck offset ratio**

### Radiographic measurements

| Measurement | How | Normal | Abnormal |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Alpha angle** | Line head centre → neck centre + line head centre → point where head **loses sphericity** (anterolateral head-neck junction) | **< 42°** (Orthobullets) · **≈ 42°** (TO) · **< 55°** (one OB) | **> 42°** head-neck offset deformity · **> 50–55° cam** · **> 50° diagnostic** (one OB) |
| **Head-neck offset ratio** | (anterior head line − anterior neck line) ÷ head diameter | **> 0.17** | **< 0.17 cam** · **< 0.15 → 95% PPV for FAI** (TO, Beaulé) |
| **Beta angle (TO)** | Acetabular rim–head centre line vs head centre–loss of sphericity line (Wyss, open MRI) | — | **< 30° → impingement** (cam, pincer, mixed) (Brunner) |
| **LCEA (Wiberg)** | AP pelvis / hip | **Under 40°** (Orthobullets) · **25–45°** (one OB) · **25–39°** (another OB) | **> 35°** overcoverage → poor arthroscopy outcome · **< 25°** inadequate coverage · **< 20°** dysplasia |
| **ACEA (Lequesne)** | False profile | **Over 20°** (Orthobullets) · **25–50°** (OB) | **< 20°** structural instability / dysplasia |
| **Tönnis (roof) angle** | Acetabular index (inclination) | **Above 0°** (Orthobullets) · **0–10°** (OB) | **< 0° ↓ inclination → overcoverage (pincer)** · **> 10° ↑ inclination → dysplasia** |
| Head to ilioischial line | Medial head | **< 10 mm** lateral | **> 10 mm lateral → dysplasia** · medial to line → protrusio |

**Alpha angle (Nötzli, MRI)** — **74°** symptomatic vs **42°** asymptomatic → ↓ head-neck concavity in impingement

**Tönnis grading (TO)** — **OA grade → cannot quantify FAI**\
✗ Wrong options: alpha angle · beta angle · anterior head-neck offset ratio

![Alpha angle on frog-leg lateral](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob3130-angle.jpg)

### Pincer/acetabular retroversion – crossover sign

**Normal AP pelvis** — anterior rim runs medially + distally, **diverging** from the more vertical posterior rim · walls meet superolaterally

**Crossover sign** — **anterior rim line crosses posterior rim line** ("figure 8") → crossing occurs more distally → **acetabular retroversion** → **pincer** · the two wall lines **should never cross** normally (two OB explanations word it as the **posterior wall crossing over the anterior**)

**Seen on** — **AP pelvis / AP hip** · hip AP normal head-neck + popping in gait extension → **AP pelvis shows crossover**\
✗ Wrong options: normal film · ↑ femoral offset · SI sclerosis · coxa valga

**Crossover = ?** — **excessive acetabular retroversion**\
✗ Wrong options: low acetabular index · deficient anterior column · labral detachment · pelvic discontinuity

**Crossover in FAI** — **retroversion in pincer**\
✗ Wrong options: retroversion in cam · protrusio (head medial to ilioischial line) · femoral anteversion / retroversion (CT)

**Other pincer signs** — posterior wall sign, protrusio, coxa profunda, ↑ LCEA, negative Tönnis angle

**Pincer AP pelvis** — bilateral **acetabular over-coverage + cephalad retroversion** · bilateral cam + crossover = mixed

![Crossover sign — anterior (red) and posterior (blue) rims](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob3130-crossover.jpg)

### FAI types

| Type | Defect | Classic sign |
|---|---|---|
| **Cam** | Femoral head-neck (aspherical) | Pistol grip, alpha > 50–55° |
| **Pincer** | Acetabular overcoverage / retroversion | Crossover sign |
| **Mixed** | Both — **most common (up to 80%)** | Both |

### Differential diagnosis

| Condition | Discriminator |
|---|---|
| **Femoral neck stress fracture** | Female athlete triad / energy deficiency · **axial-load pain**, no impingement · **linear lucency / sclerosis of inferior or posteromedial neck** · T2 oedema |
| **DDH** | **Shallow acetabulum, ↓ anterolateral coverage**, low CEA, hypertrophic labrum |
| **Os acetabuli** | Unfused secondary ossification centre of **anterosuperior acetabulum** |
| Iliopsoas tendonitis / internal impingement | Resisted hip flexion pain · Rx tenotomy |
| Sacroiliitis | Pain with prolonged standing, low back, **FABER** · **SI joint sclerosis** + T2 signal |
| Osteitis pubis | Central pain, symphysis tenderness · **peri-symphyseal sclerosis + osteolysis** · NSAIDs, rest |
| AVN | Insidious, pain with impact / inclines, risk factors, not end-range |
| Displaced femoral neck fracture | ORIF (young) · arthroplasty (old) |
| Other | Ischiofemoral impingement, adductor strain, athletic pubalgia, lumbar radiculopathy, hip instability |

**Compression-sided FNSF (distractor)** — NWB if **< 50%** neck · percutaneous screws if **> 50%**

## 5 Management

```
Symptomatic FAI
├─ Minimal symptoms, no mechanical symptoms → activity modification, PT, NSAIDs
├─ Failed nonop, mechanical, non-arthritic → arthroscopic osteoplasty + labral repair
│      (no viable labrum → labral reconstruction)
├─ Large femoral deformity (residual SCFE / Perthes) → open surgical hip dislocation (Ganz)
├─ Significant acetabular retroversion / dysplasia → periacetabular osteotomy
└─ Arthritic, end-stage → THA (resurfacing vs THA controversial)
```

### Operative criteria

| Procedure | Indication |
|---|---|
| **Nonoperative** | Minimal symptoms, **no mechanical symptoms** — always first |
| **Arthroscopic osteoplasty** | Symptomatic + **mechanical**, **failed nonop**, **non-arthritic** |
| **Labral repair** | Viable tissue, tear at **chondrolabral junction** (vascular acetabular margin) |
| Labral debridement | Intrasubstance tears |
| **Labral reconstruction** | No viable labral tissue |
| **Open surgical dislocation** | Previous gold standard · **preserved cartilage, correctable deformity, reasonable expectations** · **residual SCFE / Perthes** |
| **PAO** | **Acetabular deformity with significant retroversion** · dysplasia |
| THA | Arthritic, end-stage |
| **Hip preservation contraindicated** | **Tönnis grade 2–3** or **< 2 mm joint space** (Harris) |

### Treatment – nonoperative first

**Initial Rx** — **activity modification + NSAIDs** + PT (core / hip strengthening, kinetic chain) — even pro athlete with labral tear on MRI

**PT to ↑ passive IR** — avoid: worsens impingement + chondrolabral damage

**Untreated cam FAI, pro footballer** — **activity modification + NSAIDs**\
✗ Wrong options: open dislocation (more morbidity) · isolated labral repair (leaves cam) · PAO (no acetabular deformity)

**Hockey player, 3 wk pain, no mechanical symptoms** — **activity modification, PT, NSAIDs**\
✗ Wrong options: bone scan · any surgery yet

### Arthroscopic osteoplasty with labral repair

**Failed 12 wk PT + NSAIDs, cam + labral tear** — **arthroscopic labral repair + femoral osteoplasty**\
✗ Wrong options: more PT · surgical dislocation (slower return) · debridement · PAO (no dysplasia)

**Arthroscopic over open** — less soft-tissue trauma, **↓ AVN risk**, **quicker return** · **equivalent results** to open

**Repair vs debridement** — repair / reconstruction → **better PROMs (HHS, SF-12, VAS) + less pain**\
✗ Wrong options: ↑ OA risk · no difference · ↑ pain · ↑ nerve injury

**Always treat bone** — labral debridement or repair **alone** → suboptimal; address cam / pincer as well · femoral osteotomy no role · minimal arthritis → arthroplasty not indicated

**Pincer + AS chondrolabral tear, failed 6 mo PT** — **pincer osteoplasty + labral repair**\
✗ Wrong options: cam osteoplasty (no cam) · labral reconstruction · more PT + isolated repair

**Cam + labral detachment, minimal arthritis** — **femoroacetabular osteoplasty + labral repair**\
✗ Wrong options: debridement · femoral osteotomy · hemi / THA

**Mixed FAI, wrestler with Trendelenburg + IR 10°** — **hip arthroscopy + osteoplasty**\
✗ Wrong options: abduction orthosis · DEXA · bone scan · osteotomy

**Hockey goalie, failed PT + iliopsoas bursa injection** — **FADIR reproduces pain → femoroacetabular osteoplasty**\
✗ Wrong options: PAO (dysplasia) · iliopsoas tenotomy (internal impingement / snapping) · FABER · loose body removal

**Technique** — **capsulotomy** to reach peripheral compartment (cam) · trim head-neck · rim trim → **labral refixation if destabilised** · debridement vs repair vs reconstruction

**Evidence caveat** — long-term halt of arthritis unproven (Gerdeman)

### Open surgical hip dislocation (Ganz)

**Approach** — lateral decubitus, **Kocher-Langenbeck** incision, gluteus maximus split → **digastric trochanteric flip** (fragment mobilised anteriorly) → capsulotomy → **anterior dislocation**, ligamentum teres transected

**Advantage** — wide exposure, **preserves external rotators + MFCA** → **no ↑ AVN**

**Cam + anterior labral tear (classic)** — **anterior transtrochanteric dislocation + labral repair + femoral osteoplasty**\
✗ Wrong options: THA · arthroscopic debridement alone · arthroscopic repair alone · posterior dislocation

**Alternative** — direct anterior approach → limited posterior acetabular view · combined arthroscopic + limited open → early results promising

**Complication** — **trochanteric hip pain**

### Return to sport

**Adolescent, open treatment** — **median 7 months**\
✗ Wrong options: 4–6 wk · 3 mo · 12 mo · 16 mo

**Pro athlete, no OA** — return at same level **> 85–90%** (Orthobullets) · **> 85%** (OB) · ≈ **90%** after arthroscopy · **> 90%** (OB, athletes)

**With concomitant microfracture** — **92%** return (Byrd and Jones) · 5 transient neurapraxias, all resolved

### Poor prognostic factors – arthritis & subchondral cysts

| Poor outcome after FAI surgery | NOT a poor prognostic factor |
|---|---|
| **Preop OA** / Outerbridge III–IV cartilage | Age < 20 / adolescent (mean 17.6 yr — good results) |
| **Tönnis ≥ 1** (Orthobullets) · **subchondral cyst = Tönnis ≥ 2** (OB) | **Professional athlete** · football player |
| **Subchondral cysts / oedema on MRI** | **Alpha angle > 60°** (treated by osteoplasty) · cam prominence |
| **Age > 45** | Posterior labral tear |
| **BMI > 25–30** (one OB) · **BMI > 30** (another OB) — weaker than OA | Labral tear **repaired** |
| **LCEA > 35°** (overhang hard to modify arthroscopically) | LCEA 25° (normal) |
| **Relative femoral retroversion (anteversion < 5°)** | — |
| Labral **debridement** without repair | — |

**OA on AP** — **lateral joint-line sclerosis + acetabular subchondral cyst** · MRI chondromalacia + **subchondral oedema**

**OA + FAI** — inferior results + **earlier conversion to THA** → counsel on limited benefit · symptomatic care → eventual THA

## 6 Complications

| Complication | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Femoral neck fracture** | Femoroplasty · limit resection depth **< 30% of neck diameter** + multiple fluoroscopy views |
| **Heterotopic ossification** | — |
| **Residual deformity** | Commonest cause of revision · prevent with multiple fluoroscopy views |
| **Capsular deficiency** | No capsular closure → instability |
| Neurapraxia | Traction (see hip arthroscopy) |

### Failed arthroscopy – residual FAI & subspine impingement

**Revision rate** — **5–10%** · causes: **under-resection**, over-resection, capsular deficiency, cartilage injury, unaddressed dysplasia

**Commonest cause of revision** — **residual FAI** (persistent structural deformity)\
✗ Wrong options: progressive OA · HO · iatrogenic soft-tissue injury · AVN

**Persistent FADIR pain, cartilage intact** — **alpha angle 60°** = residual cam\
✗ Wrong options: Tönnis angle 8° · LCEA 25° · ACEA 27° · head 7 mm from ilioischial line (all normal)

**Commonest arthroscopy complication** — **iatrogenic chondral injury** · commonest neurological → **traction pudendal** palsy

**Revision options** — repeat arthroscopy, arthroscopy + limited open capsulorrhaphy, PAO, surgical dislocation, arthroplasty

**Revision success** — complete cam resection on **Dunn 45°** (alpha angle, anterior offset, head-neck ratio) → better outcome

**Subspine (AIIS) impingement** — large AIIS spur → **pain on passive hip flexion > 90° in neutral rotation** · overhang below AIIS must be addressed

**Subspine imaging** — **AIIS spur** on AP + frog-leg lateral (abuts femur in frog-leg position) · MRI: spur **dark, tendon-like on T1** + **head-neck oedema** on T2

**Postop alpha 47° + periportal capsulotomy, still painful** — **residual subspinous impingement**\
✗ Wrong options: inadequate cam (47° < 50–55°) · OA beyond Tönnis 2 (needs large cysts + joint-space loss) · plication (none done) · microinstability (no interportal capsulotomy)

**Periportal capsulotomy** — small separate portal openings, not joined → no repair or plication needed

### Postoperative capsular deficiency

**Cause** — iliofemoral ligament incised in capsulotomy → **close after interportal + T-capsulotomy**

**Risk factors** — no capsular closure, unaddressed laxity, trauma, overaggressive rehab, ignoring ROM limits

**Imaging** — Dunn view: no residual cam / pincer · **T2 FS coronal MRI: superior capsular defect + extracapsular fluid**

**Presentation** — pain **different from preop**, **pain / apprehension with hip extension + ER**, positive dial test, **loss of recoil on log roll**, gross instability

**Treatment** — **capsular repair** · **capsular reconstruction** (autograft / allograft) for large irreparable defects

| Postop exam finding | Diagnosis |
|---|---|
| **Pain with extension + ER** | **Capsular deficiency** ✓ |
| Pain with FADIR | Residual impingement |
| Pain on resisted hip flexion at 90° | Iliopsoas tendonitis |
| Pain on passive flexion > 90° in neutral rotation | Subspine impingement |
| Loss of motion | Capsular adhesions |

### Microinstability – iliofemoral ligament & hyperlaxity

**Strongest restraint** — **iliofemoral ligament**\
✗ Wrong options: ischiofemoral · ligamentum teres · pubofemoral · inguinal

**Causes** — bony / DDH, connective tissue disorder (Beighton), post-traumatic, microtrauma, iatrogenic, idiopathic

**Pathology** — abnormal head translation → microtrauma, capsular stretch, ↑ shear

| Test | Sensitivity | Specificity |
|---|---|---|
| **Abduction-hyperextension-ER** | **80.6%** | 89.4% |
| Prone instability | 33.9% | **97.9%** |
| Hyperextension-ER | 71% | 85.1% |

**Imaging** — **cliff sign** (loss of head sphericity; in **> 89%** of microinstability) · anterior recess widening, thin anterior capsule, capsular defect, labral tear (MRA)

**Gymnast, Beighton 5, cliff sign after labral repair** — **PT first** (core + hip stabilisers) → **> 70% resolve**\
✗ Wrong options: steroid injection · PAO · capsular plication / repair (only after failed PT)

![Cliff sign of hip microinstability](../../MCQ/attachments/q219852-illa-cliff-sign.jpg)

### Ischiofemoral impingement

**Mechanism** — **lesser trochanter abuts lateral ischium** · **↓ native femoral offset predisposes** ✓

**Presentation** — **deep posterior hip pain**, worse after long runs · no relief from hip-joint injection

**IFI test** — **passive hip extension in contralateral decubitus** · pain on extension + ER\
✗ Wrong options: FADIR (that is FAI)

**MRI** — **↓ ischiofemoral space** · **quadratus femoris** signal (nerve to quadratus femoris, not femoral nerve)\
✗ Wrong options: iliofemoral

**Treatment** — stretching, steroid injection into ischiofemoral space → recalcitrant: **partial lesser trochanter resection** · MRI: ↑ signal around quadratus femoris tendon
