# Adductor Strain

*Knee & Sports · 2 / 61*

## 1 Basics

**Incidence** — **10–30% of soccer and hockey players**

**Adductor complex** — **adductor longus (most commonly injured)**, adductor magnus, adductor brevis, gracilis, obturator externus, pectineus · all **obturator nerve** (Orthobullets)

## 2 Mechanism

**"Pulled groin"** — **forceful hip extension + external rotation of an abducted leg** → **strong eccentric contraction** of adductors

**Avulsion** — muscle **fails in tension as the leg is abducted**

**Repetitive hip extension + abduction** (hockey, soccer) → **athletic pubalgia** — adductor longus torn and retracted from the **common aponeurosis** with rectus abdominis (detail in Athletic Pubalgia & Adductor strain chapter)

## 3 Presentation

**Pain** — **immediate, severe groin pain**

**Tenderness** — **subcutaneous border of the (inferior) pubic ramus** · medial pubic ramus

**Strength** — pain ± **weakness on resisted adduction** vs other leg

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Study | Finding |
|---|---|
| X-ray: **AP pelvis + lateral hip** | **Fleck of bone** if avulsion |
| **MRI** | **Avulsion from pubic ramus**, muscle oedema + haemorrhage · T2 ↑ fluid signal at tendon origin |

![T2 MRI: adductor origin on the pubic ramus (1) and avulsed adductor tendon (2)](../../MCQ/attachments/q1413-illa-adductor-tear.jpg)

### Adductor strain classification

| Degree | Findings |
|---|---|
| **1st** | Pain, **minimal loss** of strength + motion |
| **2nd** | **Compromised strength** |
| **3rd** | **Complete disruption**, loss of muscle function |

## 5 Management

### Groin injuries — adductor strain / avulsion management

**Mainstay (incl. avulsion)** — **rest, ice, protected WB as needed** → gentle stretching → resistance → **core strengthening** → gradual return to sport

**Avoid immobilisation** — promotes **muscle tightness + scarring** / contracture

**Rugby player, MRI adductor avulsion** — **immediate rehabilitation with increasing passive + active motion**\
✗ Wrong options: strict immobilisation in flexion + adduction · tendon repair · tenodesis · repair + allograft

**Open repair** — **no data / no high-level evidence** it beats nonoperative care

**Hockey groin syndrome** — **external oblique aponeurosis tear + ilioinguinal nerve entrapment** → ilioinguinal nerve ablation + external oblique reinforcement

### Adductor insertion avulsion syndrome ("thigh splints")

**What** — painful **proximal-to-mid femur at adductor insertions** · insidious thigh pain + limp, no trauma

**Exam / labs** — pain on **resisted adduction** only · afebrile, **normal WBC, ESR, CRP**

**Imaging** — **X-rays normal** · MRI (TSE inversion recovery) → **linear high signal along medial (posteromedial) periosteum** of proximal–mid femoral shaft, **4–9 cm** long

![Inversion-recovery MRI: linear high signal along the medial proximal femur](../../MCQ/attachments/q8881-c-adductor-mri.jpg)

**Treatment** — **activity modification + NSAIDs**, observe · resolves in **1–2 months**\
✗ Wrong options: antibiotics · I&D · prophylactic fixation (not a stress fracture) · biopsy

**Differential** — osteomyelitis, neoplasm · biopsy only if pain **persists or increases**

## X vs Y

| | **Adductor strain** | **Thigh splints** |
|---|---|---|
| Onset | **Acute**, severe | **Insidious** |
| Site | **Pubic ramus** (origin) | **Femoral shaft** (insertion) |
| MRI | Avulsion / oedema at pubis | **Periosteal high signal**, posteromedial femur |
| Treatment | Rest, ice, protected WB, **early motion** | Activity modification, NSAIDs |

## How it's asked

**"Adductor avulsion on MRI — treatment?"** → **immediate rehabilitation with increasing passive and active motion**

**"Insidious thigh pain, normal labs, periosteal signal medial femur — next step?"** → **activity modification and NSAIDs**

**"Repetitive motion causing adductor longus tear from common aponeurosis (sports hernia)?"** → **hip extension and abduction**
