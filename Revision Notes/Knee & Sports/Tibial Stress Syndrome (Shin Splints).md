# Tibial Stress Syndrome (Shin Splints)

*Knee & Sports · 60 / 61*

## 1 Basics

**Definition** — overuse / repetitive-load injury of the shin → **persistent dull anterior leg pain**

**Incidence** — **10–15% of running injuries** · **60% of leg pain syndromes**

**Location** — **medial (posteromedial) tibial stress syndrome = most common** · anterior (anterolateral) tibial stress syndrome · distal + posteromedial tibia

**Risk factors** — **poor shock absorption** (cement / uneven surfaces, improper shoes) · training errors (sudden ↑ intensity + duration) · **> 20 miles / week** · early-season hill training · previous lower-limb injury · **over-pronation / ↑ internal tibial rotation**

**Pathophysiology** — **traction periostitis** · anterolateral → **tibialis anterior** on tibia + interosseous membrane · posteromedial → **tibialis posterior + soleus**

**Associated** — **female athlete triad** (critical to diagnose + treat) · **tibial stress fracture** — **females 1.5–3.5× risk of progression**

**Main culprit (TO)** — **soleus** traction (+ tibialis posterior) · tenderness diffuse at **upper ⅔–lower ⅓ junction**, posteromedial · **pain improves after warm-up** (MTSS / tendinopathy) · extra risk factors (TO): **weak hip abductors**, calf tightness, low vit D / Ca, **RED-S**, prior stress fracture, female

## 3 Presentation

**Symptoms** — **vague, diffuse pain along middle–distal tibia** that **↓ with running** (early) · later: pain earlier with more frequent training

**Exam** — **tenderness along posteromedial tibial border**, from **4 cm above medial malleolus** extending **up to 12 cm** proximally · **pes planus**, **tight Achilles**, **weak core**

**Provocative test** — **pain on resisted plantarflexion**

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Study | Shin splints | vs stress fracture |
|---|---|---|
| Radiographs | **Normal first 2–3 wk** · late **periosteal exostoses** | **"Dreaded black line"** |
| **3-phase bone scan** | **Diffuse, longitudinal** uptake along **posteromedial (posterior) tibial cortex**, **delayed phase (3) only** · phases 1 (flow) + 2 (blood pool) normal | **Focal, intense** hyperperfusion + hyperaemia in phases 1 + 2 · **focal, fusiform** uptake in phase 3 |
| MRI | **Periosteal oedema** → progressive marrow involvement · shows other soft-tissue injury | Marrow oedema ± line |

**Distinguishing finding (OB)** — **bone scan: diffuse, longitudinal uptake of posterior tibial cortex = shin splints**\
✗ Wrong options: shin splints scan more intense · more diffuse tenderness in stress fracture · all 3 phases positive in shin splints · pain persists longer after activity in stress fracture

### Differential diagnosis – exertional leg pain

| Condition | Characteristics | Tissue |
|---|---|---|
| Anterior tibial stress syndrome | Diffuse anterolateral pain, worse at start, **↓ during training** | Periosteum |
| **Medial tibial stress syndrome** | Diffuse middle–distal pain, worse at start, **↓ during training** | Periosteum |
| Tibial / fibular stress fracture | Pain with running, **point tenderness**, **dreaded black line** | Bone |
| Exertional compartment syndrome | Starts **10 min** into exercise, **resolves 30 min** after · sensory / motor loss · ↑ anterior compartment pressure | Muscle + fascia |
| Leg tendinopathy | Achilles, peroneal, tibialis posterior | Tendon |
| Sural / SPN entrapment | Dermatomal symptoms | Nerve |
| Lumbar radiculopathy | Worse sitting (lumbar tension position) | Nerve |
| Popliteal artery entrapment | Vascular studies | Vessel |

## 5 Management

**Nonop — first line, works in vast majority** — **NSAIDs**, rest, activity modification

**Activity modification** — **↓ running distance, frequency + intensity by 50%** · low-impact cross-training · stretching + strengthening · synthetic track · avoid hills, uneven / hard surfaces

**Shoes** — change every **250–500 miles** (lose shock absorption) · **orthotics** for pes planus

**Therapy** — strengthen **invertors + evertors of the calf** · **local phonophoresis with corticosteroids** may help

**TO treatment** — **soleus-focused stretching** + foam rolling · Ca + vit D3 + Mg · medial arch insoles / taping · **avoid anti-pronation (motion-control) shoes** (TO) · severe: Aircast or fascial release

**Operative (failed nonop)** — **deep posterior compartment fasciotomy + release of painful periosteum** · variable results, complete relief unlikely

## 6 Complications

**Recurrence** — common after resuming heavy activity

## X vs Y

| | **Shin splints (MTSS)** | **Tibial stress fracture** | **CECS** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pain with running | **↓ during training** (early) | ↑ → walking | **↑**, starts \~10 min in |
| Tenderness | **Diffuse posteromedial** | **Point** | Normal at rest |
| Bone scan | **Diffuse longitudinal, phase 3 only** | **Focal, all phases** | — |
| Cause | Traction periostitis | Bone fatigue | ↑ compartment pressure |

## How it's asked

**"Bone scan finding separating shin splints from stress fracture?"** → **diffuse, longitudinal uptake of posterior tibial cortex in shin splints**

**"MTSS vs tibial stress fracture on palpation (TO)?"** → **diffuse vs focal tenderness**

**"Key muscle culprit in MTSS (TO)?"** → **soleus**
