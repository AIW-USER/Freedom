# PCL Injury

*Knee & Sports · 41 / 61*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Incidence** — **5–20%** of all knee ligament injuries · **rare in isolation** → usually multiligament (PLC, ACL), knee dislocation

**Sports (TO)** — football, soccer, rugby, skiing → highest incidence of PCL tears

### Anatomy

**Origin (femur)** — **anterolateral medial femoral condyle** · broad crescent-shaped footprint · arthroscopic view from **anterolateral portal** shows AL + PM bundle origins on MFC

**Insertion (tibia)** — **posterior tibial sulcus below (distal to) the articular surface**\
✗ Wrong options: superior to articular margin

**Size** — 38 mm long × 13 mm diameter · **30% larger than ACL** · strength 2500–3000 N · resists 95% of posterior tibial displacement

**Synovial status (TO)** — **cruciates = intra-articular, extrasynovial** (own blood supply, not fed by synovial fluid) · collaterals = extra-articular, extrasynovial · menisci = intra-articular, intrasynovial (fed by synovial fluid)

**Blood supply** — **middle geniculate artery** + fat pad

**Meniscofemoral ligaments** — PCL lies between them · **Humphrey anterior, Wrisberg posterior** · arise posterior horn lateral meniscus → insert into PCL substance

| Bundle | Tight in | Fact |
|---|---|---|
| **Anterolateral (AL)** | **Flexion** | Strongest · main posterior stabiliser at **90°** · **"PAL"** = PCL Antero-Lateral · reconstructed in single-bundle |
| **Posteromedial (PM)** | **Extension** | Reciprocal to AL |
| ✗ Wrong option: anteromedial / posterolateral | — | **ACL** bundles (distractor) |

### Biomechanics

**Function** — **primary restraint to posterior tibial translation** · prevents hyperflexion / sliding · **isolated injury → greatest instability at 90° flexion**

**Popliteus** — synergistic with PCL → controls ER, varus, posterior translation (see PLC)

## 2 Mechanism

**Most common overall** — **direct blow to proximal (anterior) tibia, knee flexed (dashboard)** (TO, OB)

**Most common in athletes** — **fall on flexed knee with foot plantarflexed** → posterior force on tibia\
✗ Wrong options: foot dorsiflexed · blow to back of tibia · ER-valgus or IR-varus twist

**Other (TO)** — non-contact **hyperflexion > hyperextension** (both less common) · hyperextension (stepping into hole) · rotational with varus / valgus · hyperextension often **ACL + PCL**

**Clue in stem** — scars over **anterior proximal tibia + dorsum of ankle / forefoot** → foot plantarflexed + blow to tibia → **PCL** (can extend knee → not patella fracture, patellar tendon or tubercle avulsion; Lachman negative → not ACL)

## 3 Presentation

**History** — high vs low energy · **posterior knee pain** · instability subtle / asymptomatic if isolated · ask about dislocation / nerve injury

**Diagnosis mainly clinical (TO)** — **posterior drawer**; MRI best test for cartilage, ACL, meniscus **but not PCL** (TO) · MRI = confirmatory study (Orthobullets) — read the options

### Clinical tests (posterior drawer, sag, quadriceps active, dial)

| Test | Technique / positive | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Posterior drawer (90°)** | Posterior force on proximal tibia · **most accurate** for PCL | Isolated: **> 10–12 mm neutral, 6–8 mm IR** · combined: **> 15 mm neutral, > 10 mm IR** |
| **Posterior sag sign** | Hips + knees 90°, ankles supported → tibia sags | Normal **medial plateau 10 mm anterior to MFC**; absent / posterior step-off = + |
| **Quadriceps active test** | Quad contraction at 90° | **Anterior reduction of tibia** = + |
| **Reverse Lachman** | Posterior translation near extension | PCL |
| **False Lachman** | Posterior subluxation mimics ↑ anterior travel | PCL (✗ Wrong options: ACL) |
| Varus / valgus at **0°** | Laxity | **Collateral + PCL** (cruciate) |
| Varus / valgus at **30° only** | Laxity | **Isolated MCL / LCL** |
| KT-1000 / KT-2000 | Arthrometer | Less accurate than for ACL |

### Dial test (TO)

| Dial result (ER asymmetry vs other side) | Lesion |
|---|---|
| **> 10° at 30° only** | **Isolated PLC** |
| **> 10° at 30° AND 90°** | **PLC + PCL** |
| **ER 15° more at 30°, further ↑ at 90° (TO)** | **PCL + PLC** |
| 2° asymmetry / symmetric at 30° + 90° | No PLC → isolated PCL |

**Threshold** — **> 10°** (Orthobullets, OB) · **10–15°** (OB) · **15°** (TO supine description) — read the options

**Prone dial (TO)** — **thigh–foot angle in prone position** (angle between sagittal plane of sole and body)\
✗ Wrong options: foot–leg angle · tibial tuberosity angle supine · foot divergence

**Supine dial (TO)** — leg over edge of bed, stabilise distal femur, hold heel → max ER at 30° → note **tibial tuberosity position** → repeat at 90°

**Combined exam (OB)** — 2B reverse Lachman + sag + **varus at 0° / 30°** + negative dial + negative reverse pivot → **PCL + LCL**\
✗ Wrong options: complete PLC (needs + dial / reverse pivot)

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Study | Finding / use |
|---|---|
| AP + supine lateral X-ray | Avulsion fractures (acute) · **posterior tibiofemoral subluxation** · chronic → medial + PF arthrosis |
| **Lateral stress view (knee 70°)** | Asymmetric posterior displacement · **becoming gold standard** for diagnosing + quantifying · side-to-side **> 12 mm → PCL + PLC** |
| Kneeling stress X-ray | Quantifies posterior translation |
| Sagittal CT | Tibial PCL bony avulsion fragment |
| **MRI** | **Confirmatory** · essential to plan concomitant injuries in avulsions (TO) |

![Lateral stress radiographs in PCL injury](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob3009-pcl-injury-lateral-stress-views.jpg)

### PCL grading

| Grade (posterior tibial subluxation at 90°) | Injury | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| **I** | **Partial** tear | **1–5 mm** |
| **II** | **Complete isolated** tear | **6–10 mm** · anterior tibia **flush with femoral condyles** |
| **III** | Row blank in source table | — |

**Veltri (OB)** — isolated PCL **< 10 mm** posterior laxity → nonop · **> 10 mm or combined** → reconstruct

## 5 Management

```
PCL injury
├─ Isolated grade I / II → protected WB + quadriceps rehab (RTS 2–4 wk)
├─ Isolated grade III → immobilise in extension 4 wk → quadriceps strengthening
│      (surgery if bony avulsion or young athlete)
├─ Bony tibial avulsion (displaced > 5 mm) → ORIF (Burks–Schaffer posteromedial)
├─ Combined (ACL / PLC / grade III MCL or LCL) → PCL reconstruction + all lesions
└─ Chronic, functionally unstable / varus → reconstruction ± medial opening-wedge HTO
```

### Nonoperative management – quadriceps rehabilitation

**Principle** — **quadriceps strengthening (knee extensors)** pulls tibia anteriorly → counteracts PCL deficit · ACL-deficient knee → hamstring rehab\
✗ Wrong options: hamstrings · hip · gastroc-soleus

**Grade II protocol (OB)** — **relative protection 10–14 days → ROM + gentle closed-chain quadriceps** strengthening · global strengthening at **4–6 wk** · RTS at full ROM + strength

**Quadriceps-first, possibly definitive (OB)** — **isolated PCL rupture** → **closed-chain** quad strengthening (foot fixed) · no clear prognostic factors\
✗ Wrong options: ACL rupture (hamstring rehab) · patellar tendon rupture · bucket-handle meniscal tear · meniscal root tear (surgery)

**Prone ROM** — flexion done **prone** → limits posterior sag

**Avoid** — **resisted hamstring** until healed (**4–6 wk**) · **plyometrics** early · prolonged immobilisation\
✗ Wrong options: long leg cast 4 wk → stiffness · 12 wk in extension

**Isolated grade II exam (OB)** — 2+ posterior drawer, 1+ stable Lachman, negative dial, no valgus laxity → **PT with quadriceps focus** · persistent anterior / medial pain or "loose" → reconstruct later\
✗ Wrong options: delayed or acute reconstruction

**Grade III isolated** — relative immobilisation **in extension 4 wk** + limited daily ROM → quad strengthening

### Tibial avulsion – ORIF

**Tibial-sided avulsion (TO)** — **most common form of isolated bony PCL disruption** · mechanism: dashboard > sports hyperextension

**Displaced > 5 mm (TO)** — **surgical fixation (arthroscopic or open)** · MRI to plan concomitant injuries \
✗ Wrong options: **long leg cast in full extension 4 wk**

**Young active, displaced (OB)** — **ORIF of fragment** · nonop historically → loss of motion · good results with repair of avulsions\
✗ Wrong options: reconstruction · hinged brace + therapy · HTO

**Exam** — isolated PCL: posterior drawer **11 mm neutral / 7 mm IR**, stable varus / valgus, negative dial

![Tibial PCL bony avulsion, lateral radiograph](../../MCQ/attachments/TO17119378-1.png)

**Burks–Schaffer approach (TO)** — **posteromedial approach for PCL avulsion** · **prone** · horizontal limb over popliteal crease + vertical limb over medial gastrocnemius\
✗ Wrong options: ACL avulsion · TA repair

**Interval (TO)** — **medial gastrocnemius – semimembranosus** → blunt finger dissection to posterior capsule · **ligate middle geniculate artery** if needed · **preserve tibial nerve motor branch to medial head gastrocnemius** · retract medial gastrocnemius **laterally** (protects NV bundle) · vertical capsulotomy

### Operative criteria

| PCL repair / reconstruction indicated | Nonop |
|---|---|
| **Combined: PCL + ACL or PLC** | Isolated grade I, II |
| PCL + **grade III MCL or LCL** | Isolated grade III (unless avulsion / young athlete) |
| **Isolated grade II / III with bony avulsion** | |
| **Isolated chronic, functionally unstable** | |
| **Chronic deficiency + varus → HTO** | |

### Reconstruction technique – graft tensioning, tibial inlay, killer turn

**Graft** — usually **allograft** (Achilles, BPTB, hamstring, tibialis anterior) · no technique clearly superior · results < ACL reconstruction, residual posterior laxity common · **midsubstance primary repair fails** · success needs concomitant ligaments addressed

**Single-bundle** — reconstruct **anterolateral bundle** · **tension at 90° flexion** (ACL tensioned nearer extension; closest to isometric)\
✗ Wrong options: 0° · 30°

**Double-bundle** — **AL at 90°, PM in extension** · biomechanical advantage; clinical advantage unproven · useful with PCL + PLC (rotational control; PLC grafts loosen)

**Transtibial (arthroscopic)** — drill anterior → posterior · **accessory posteromedial portal** 1 cm above joint line, posterior to MCL (**saphenous nerve** branches at risk) · fix graft at **90° with anterior drawer** · risk to popliteal vessels

**Posteromedial corner view** — **70° arthroscope** through notch (modified Gillquist) or posteromedial portal

**Tibial inlay (open)** — posteromedial incision between **medial gastrocnemius + semimembranosus** · graft bone block fixed directly to posterior tibia · screw **within 20 mm of popliteal artery** · ORIF of avulsions

**Killer turn (TO)** — sharp (\~**90°**) bend of graft around **proximal posterior tibia** at tibial tunnel exit → abrasion, thinning, elongation → **main cause of residual laxity + graft failure** · Markolf: **thinning 40.6%, elongation 65.3% (9.8 / 15 mm)**

![Killer turn at the posterior tibial tunnel exit](../../MCQ/attachments/TO21733233-1.jpg)

| Reduce killer turn (TO) | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| **Tibial inlay** (Jakob, Berg) | No tibial tunnel · posterior arthrotomy · anatomic · bone-to-bone healing |
| **Anterolateral tibial tunnel** | ↓ angulation, no ↑ translation vs anteromedial (theoretical: ↓ compressive force → ↑ translation) |
| **Tunnel 15–20 mm deep from posterior tibial margin** (jig) | ↑ tunnel angle → smaller turn |
| **Tunnel exit lower in footprint** | One acute turn → two smoother turns |
| **Aperture fixation** (interference screw) | no suspensory (endobutton) "bungee cord" micromotion · stiffer construct |
| Smooth aperture | Punch / rasp |
| **All-inside** | Half tunnels (sockets) · bone stock · ↓ tunnel widening / convergence · ↓ "windshield effect" |
| ✗ Wrong option: **tunnel exit superiorly (upper half of fossa)** | Does NOT avoid it |
| ✗ Wrong option: **transtibial in place of inlay** | Does NOT help (transtibial causes it) |

**Inlay vs transtibial (OB)** — only advantage = **eliminates the 90° killer turn**\
✗ Wrong options: less invasive · cosmesis · shorter time · faster rehab · better clinical results · normal kinematics

### Rehabilitation after PCL reconstruction (OKC vs CKC)

**Early** — immobilise in extension, **protect against gravity** · early motion **prone** · focus on quadriceps

**Avoid** — **resisted hamstring curls (prone / OKC)** → posterior pull on tibia stresses graft

**4 wk post-op, highest posterior shear (TO)** — **OKC resisted hamstring curls 0–90°** (esp. deep flexion **> 60°**)\
✗ Wrong options: CKC leg press 0–60° · OKC quad extension 90→0° · CKC wall squat 0–45°

**Allowed (OB, TO)** — quadriceps activation · closed-chain terminal extension · **non-WB resisted extension 60°→0°** · prone passive flexion + active terminal extension · ankle pumps · **CKC up to 60°**

**PCL + PLC post-op (OB)** — **no active flexion, active extension 90→0°** · hinged brace + crutches, partial WB first 4 wk

| Kinetic chain (TO, Steindler 1955) | OKC | CKC |
|---|---|---|
| Distal segment | **Free** (leg extension, hamstring curl, SLR) | **Fixed** (squat, leg press, lunge, step-up) |
| Joints | Single (isolation) | Multi-joint, functional |
| Knee force | **Shear** | **Compressive** + quad–hamstring **co-contraction** |
| OKC extension | **Anterior tibial shear**, peaks **0–30°** → stresses ACL | — |
| **ACL rehab** | Restrict 0–45° first **12 wk** (or 90–45° only); full range at **12–16 wk** | **Early (2–4 wk)** — protects ACL |
| **PCL rehab** | **Extension safe** (anterior shear unloads PCL) | **Modify**: co-contraction → posterior shear, esp. **> 60°** |
| Patellofemoral | **90–45°** → lower PFJ force than deep CKC | **0–45°** → lower PFJ force than OKC |
| TKA | SLR, terminal extension (supplement) | Early sit-to-stand, partial squat |
| Fracture | Strength when WB restricted | Axial load → callus (**Wolff's law**) |

### Combined PCL / PLC / LCL injuries & slope-increasing osteotomy

**Dial + at 30° and 90°, sag sign** — **PCL + PLC**\
✗ Wrong options: ACL only · PCL only · PLC + ACL (dial + at 30° only + Lachman)

**Chronic PCL + PLC, varus thrust, 10° varus** — **HTO to INCREASE slope + correct varus, then PCL + PLC reconstruction**\
✗ Wrong options: bracing · PCL alone · ACL + PCL (high failure if coronal alignment untreated)

**Medial opening-wedge HTO** — **↑ tibial slope + tibia shifts anterior** · addresses varus + PCL deficiency\
✗ Wrong options: decreased · posterior

### Posterior compartment access (Gillquist portal / manoeuvre)

**Gillquist portal (TO)** — **central transpatellar portal** · midline **1 cm below inferior pole of patella** · used in ACL reconstruction after graft harvest\
✗ Wrong options: anterolateral · anteromedial · supraclavicular

**Gillquist manoeuvre (TO, 1979)** — **visualise posterior compartment** · 5-mm 30° scope through patellar tendon → **between MFC and PCL** → swap to **70°** · sees posterior horn, meniscocapsular junction, **ramp lesion**, PCL, loose bodies\
✗ Wrong options: PF tracking (superolateral portal)

**Modified Gillquist (TO)** — via **anterolateral or anteromedial** portal · McGinnis: inferior notchplasty of lateral MFC to pass scope (tight in intact cruciates / OA)

| Knee portal (TO) | Site | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Anterolateral** | 1 cm above lateral joint line, 1 cm lateral to patellar tendon | **Main viewing, first portal** · misses anterior horn LM, periphery of posterior horn MM, PCL |
| **Anteromedial** | 1 cm above medial joint line, 1 cm medial to tendon | **Main working** · views lateral compartment |
| **Posteromedial** | — | Posterior horn meniscal repair, **PCL**, posterior loose bodies |
| **Superolateral** | Lateral to quadriceps tendon, 2.5 cm above superolateral patella | **Patellofemoral articulation** |
| **Central transpatellar (Gillquist)** | Midline, 1 cm below inferior pole | ACL reconstruction · posterior compartment |

| Shoulder portal (TO) | Site | Use / at risk |
|---|---|---|
| **Posterior (soft spot)** | 1–2 cm medial, 2 cm inferior to posterolateral acromion · aim coracoid | First, viewing · **axillary n.** inferior, **suprascapular a. + n.** medial |
| **Anteroinferior (Wolf)** | Lateral + inferior to coracoid | Anchors for capsulolabral repair · axillary + musculocutaneous n., axillary a., cephalic v. |
| Anterosuperior | Between coracoid + acromion, anterior to LHB | Outflow / retrieval in Bankart |
| **Wilmington** | 1 cm lateral + 1 cm inferior to posterolateral acromion, through cuff | **Posterior SLAP** |
| **5 o'clock** | — | Inferior glenoid anchors at right angle (Bankart) |
| **Neviaser (superior / supraclavicular)** | Supraspinatus fossa: clavicle, scapular spine, medial acromion | SLAP anchors |
| Lateral subacromial | 2–3 cm below lateral acromion, in line with posterior AC joint | Subacromial |
| Posterolateral subacromial | \~2 cm below lateral acromion, in line with posterior edge | Scope for **cuff repair** |
| Anterolateral subacromial | 2–3 cm below lateral acromion, in line with anterior edge | Subacromial |

## 6 Complications

**Loss of motion (TO)** — **most common complication of PCL reconstruction** · **flexion loss > extension loss** (10–20° flexion loss) · graft malposition / inadequate rehab\
✗ Wrong options: popliteal artery · residual instability · cartilage

**Femoral tunnel (TO)** — **more critical than tibial** · anterior + distal to isometric point → ↑ graft tension → **flexion loss** · extension loss / flexion contracture → prolonged immobilisation in flexion

**Popliteal artery injury** — lies **just posterior to tibial PCL insertion, separated only by posterior capsule** · risk ↑ with **transtibial drilling in knee extension** → drill in **flexion** · accessory posteromedial incision (finger retraction), oscillating drill, tapered bits\
✗ Wrong options: saphenous nerve (that is posteromedial portal) · medial inferior geniculate artery

**Residual posterior laxity** — common; failure to reconstruct both bundles → rotational instability

### PCL deficiency – medial & patellofemoral arthritis

**Chronic PCL deficiency** — ↑ contact pressure **patellofemoral + medial compartment** (varus) · late patellar + MFC chondrosis (controversial) · reconstruction may delay OA vs nonop\
✗ Wrong options: isolated lateral · lateral without PF · isolated PF · medial only · normal (short term only)

**Medial + PF degeneration, old dashboard injury → MRI** — **PCL rupture**\
✗ Wrong options: PHMM tear (medial only) · ACL · trochlear dysplasia (PF only) · MFC OCD

**Young labourer, medial OA after old PCL injury** — **medial opening-wedge HTO** (↑ slope helps PCL deficiency)\
✗ Wrong options: UKA (needs cruciates) · TKA · isolated PCL reconstruction in arthritic malaligned knee · lateral closing wedge
