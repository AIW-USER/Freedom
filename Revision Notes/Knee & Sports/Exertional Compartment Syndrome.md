# Exertional Compartment Syndrome

*Knee & Sports · 13 / 61*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Definition** — exercise-induced, **reversible ischaemia** of muscle within a compartment

**Rank** — **2nd commonest exercise-induced leg syndrome** after **medial tibial stress syndrome**

**Who** — **males > females**, **3rd decade**, **runners** · **long-distance runners + military recruits** (TO) · **paediatric CECS → more common in females** in running sports (OB)

**Sites** — **leg** commonest, then thigh + forearm (OB) · forearm 2nd (another OB) · **volar forearm** with repetitive gripping

| Leg compartment involved | Share |
|---|---|
| **Anterior** | **\~70% — most common** (Orthobullets) · **anterior (TO)** |
| Anterior + lateral | 10% (Orthobullets) · **95% of CECS in anterior + lateral** (TO) |
| **Posterior** | **Less predictable surgical outcome**; more often linked to **PAES** (accessory medial gastrocnemius head) |

### Pathophysiology

**Mechanism** — local metabolism can't clear metabolic waste · **↑ muscle volume in a non-compliant closed compartment** (TO)

**Muscle swelling (OB)** — fibres swell up to **20× resting size** → **20% ↑ muscle volume and weight**

**Aetiology (OB)** — **limited osseofascial expansion**, **↑ fascial hernias**, **arteriole dysregulation** · **↓ capillary density** vs asymptomatic

**Fascial hernias** — **40% of ECS vs 5% asymptomatic** (Orthobullets) · **palpable in 40–60%** (TO) · near the **intermuscular septum of anterior + lateral compartments, where the SPN exits**

### Risk factors – anabolic steroids (TO)

| Risk factor (TO) | Why |
|---|---|
| **Anabolic steroids** | ↑ muscle volume |
| **Creatine** | ↑ muscle volume |
| **Eccentric exercise** | **↓ fascial compliance** |
| **Rear-foot landing / over-pronation** | Differential loading of individual muscle groups |

**Traps (TO)** — ✗ Wrong options: inversion + supination of foot on landing · "most recover with activity modification, eccentric exercise + stretching" · posterior + medial calf pain as commonest symptom

## 3 Presentation

**Symptoms** — **aching / burning** or cramping leg pain · **paraesthesias over dorsum of foot** · **reproduced by exercise, relieved by rest** · often **bilateral** (vs acute CS)

**Timing** — starts at a **predictable time / distance / intensity** · **\~10 min into exercise**, resolves **\~30–40 min** after stopping · patient can predict how long pain lasts

**Exam at rest** — **most likely normal**\
✗ Wrong options: Tinel at fibular neck · firm compartments · 1st web numbness · weak dorsiflexion

**Exam findings when present** — **↓ sensation 1st web space**, **↓ active ankle dorsiflexion** (drop foot)

**Exam after provoking exercise (TO)** — tender muscles, **pain on passive stretch**, **firm compartments**, palpable fascial hernia

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Study | Use |
|---|---|
| Radiographs | **Normal** — exclude other pathology |
| MRI | **Not diagnostic** — excludes others: **marrow oedema = tibial stress fracture**, **ring-enhancing collection = abscess** |
| Near-infrared spectroscopy | Muscle deoxygenation; **normal within 25 min** of stopping |
| **Compartment pressures** | **Required / gold standard** |

### Diagnosis – pressure measurement protocol & thresholds

**Protocol** — limb **relaxed + consistent position** · **rest, 1 min post-exercise, 5 min post-exercise** (± **15 min**, some authors)\
✗ Wrong options: 30 min continuous · rest + 1 min only · 60 min continuous

**Stryker catheter (TO)** — commonest device · TO protocol = **pre-exercise + 5 min** after exercising to severe symptoms

**Forearm (OB)** — **simulated grip activity → forearm compartment pressures**\
✗ Wrong options: CTA · MRA · EMG / NCS · angiography

### Diagnostic criteria

| Time point | Orthobullets / OB | Pedowitz (OB, TO) | TO (FNB) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Rest (pre-exercise)** | **> 15 mmHg** | **≥ 15** | **> 15** (✗ Wrong options: > 10) |
| **1 min post-exercise** | **> 30 mmHg** | **≥ 30** | **> 30** (✗ Wrong options: ≥ 20) |
| **5 min post-exercise** | **> 20 mmHg** | **≥ 20** | **> 20** |
| 15 min post-exercise | **> 15 mmHg** (some authors) | — | — |

**How many criteria** — listed with "and" (Orthobullets) · **"one or more"** (Pedowitz, TO) · "or" / "and/or" (OB) — **any one value above threshold = consistent** in the MCQs

| Values in the options | Verdict |
|---|---|
| **Rest 10 · 1 min 35 · 5 min 25** | **Normal rest, ↑ 1 + 5 min** (OB) |
| **17 · 32 · 21 · 16** (rest, 1, 5, 15 min) | **Confirms ECS** (OB) |
| **5 min = 25** | **Most consistent** (✗ Wrong options: rest 10 · 1 min 15 / 25 · 5 min 15) (OB) |
| **Anterior 25 at 5 min** | **Positive** (✗ Wrong options: superficial posterior 10 at 15 min) (OB) |
| **Pre 18 · 1 min 58 · 5 min 33** | **All point to CECS** (TO) |

## 5 Management

```
Exertional compartment syndrome
├─ First line, ≥ 3 months → activity modification, NSAIDs, rest / ice / stretching
│      (leg: rarely effective; works only if activity stopped completely)
├─ Forearm, no treatment yet → NSAIDs + PT (stretching, endurance)
└─ Refractory → fasciotomy of involved compartments
```

**Nonop success** — **forearm PT \~80%** vs **leg PT < 50%** (OB) · all CECS get a **nonop trial first**

**Definitive (TO)** — **fasciotomy** in non-responders · mainstay = **subcutaneous fasciotomy** (OB)

### Fasciotomy – recurrence & SPN risk

| Incision | Releases | Landmark |
|---|---|---|
| **Lateral** | **Anterior + lateral** | **12–15 cm above lateral malleolus** · protect **SPN** · may see hernia |
| Medial | Posterior compartments (if pressures ↑) | Mid-tibia, **posterior border** |
| Endoscopic | Same | Smaller incisions, **similar complications** |

**Structure at risk (single anterolateral incision)** — **superficial peroneal nerve** crossing near the anterior / lateral fascial border\
✗ Wrong options: sural (posterolateral) · saphenous (medial) · peroneal artery (deep) · posterior tibial artery (posteromedial)

**Success** — **anterior > 80%** · **deep posterior \~60%** · not a "home run" (multifactorial) · no op vs nonop trials

**Recurrence / failure** — **up to 20%** at mean **2 yr** · **fibrosis / scar** · risk = **isolated (anterior + lateral) release** · **four-compartment fasciotomy ↓ reoperation**

**Not risk factors (OB)** — age < 23 (better success + satisfaction), higher pre-op pressures, bilateral involvement

### Forearm exertional compartment syndrome

**Sports** — **motocross, rock climbing, gymnastics, rowing** (repetitive gripping) · **volar compartment** commonest

**Features** — pain, tightness, weakness · **numbness / paraesthesia rare** (vs leg)

**Gymnast, 5-min pressures 25 + 32, untreated** — **NSAIDs, activity modification, PT**\
✗ Wrong options: endoscopic / open fasciotomy · fasciectomy · observation

### Differential – popliteal artery entrapment

**Posterior leg pain, diminished pulses on resisted plantarflexion, sural paraesthesia** — **PAES** (accessory slip of medial gastrocnemius on MRI)

**Provocation** — **passive dorsiflexion + active resisted plantarflexion**

**Traps** — ✗ Wrong options: intraseptal SPN entrapment (gives CECS-type symptoms) · flexor digitorum accessorius longus → **tarsal tunnel** · popliteal thromboembolism

## 6 Complications

| Complication | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Nerve injury** | **SPN — most common** |
| DVT | — |
| **Recurrence** | **Up to 20%**, mean 2 yr — **fibrosis**; isolated compartment release |

## X vs Y

| | **CECS** | **Acute CS** |
|---|---|---|
| Side | Often **bilateral** | Unilateral |
| Course | **Resolves with rest** | Progressive |
| First line | Activity modification | **Emergent fasciotomy** |

| | **CECS** | **Tibial stress fracture** | **PAES** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Exam | **Normal at rest** | **Focal bony tenderness** | ↓ pulses on PF / DF |
| Pain | Resolves with rest | Progresses to **pain at rest** | Claudication |
| Test | **Pressures** | **MRI marrow oedema** | Arteriogram |
| Compartment | **Anterior / lateral** | — | **Posterior** |

## How it's asked

**"Resting pressure NOT a criterion (TO)?"** → **> 10 mmHg** (criterion is > 15)

**"Most common compartment (TO)?"** → **anterior**

**"Pre 18, 1 min 58, 5 min 33 (TO)?"** → **all the above point to CECS**

**"NOT a criterion (TO)?"** → **1 minute postexercise pressure greater than or equal to 20 mm Hg** (criterion is ≥ 30)

**"Bilateral leg pain, dorsal foot paraesthesia, normal X-ray — next?"** → **post-exercise compartment pressure measurement**

**"Pressure most consistent with CECS?"** → **5 min post-exercise 25 mmHg**

**"Optimal diagnostic protocol?"** → **rest, immediately post-exercise, 5 min post-exercise**

**"Normal rest, elevated 1 + 5 min?"** → **rest 10, 1 min 35, 5 min 25**

**"Set that confirms ECS?"** → **17 / 32 / 21 / 16 mmHg**

**"Exam in clinic?"** → **normal examination**

**"Positive confirmatory test, normal X-rays?"** → **anterior compartment 25 mmHg at 5 min**

**"Recurrence risk after fasciotomy?"** → **isolated anterior + lateral release**

**"Structure at risk, anterolateral incision?"** → **superficial peroneal nerve**

**"Forearm CECS, untreated, pressures 25 / 32?"** → **NSAIDs, activity modification, PT**

**"Rock climber, volar forearm pain after 20 m?"** → **simulated grip then forearm compartment pressures**

**"Posterior leg pain, ↓ pulses on resisted PF, MRI accessory head?"** → **popliteal artery entrapment syndrome**

**"Post-exercise CS — true (TO)?"** → **anabolic steroids are a risk factor**
