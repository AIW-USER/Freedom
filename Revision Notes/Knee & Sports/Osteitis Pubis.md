# Osteitis Pubis

*Knee & Sports · 34 / 61*

## 1 Basics

**Definition** — **inflammation of the pubic symphysis** from **repetitive microtrauma** · excessive strain on pubic bone

**Sports** — **repetitive kicking** or repetitive **hip adduction / abduction** · soccer, hockey, football, running · most common in **young football players** (OB)

### Anatomy — pubic symphysis

**Joint** — anterior articulation of the hemipelves · cartilage-covered rami + **fibrocartilage disc** · **very stable**

**Attachments** — adductors (magnus, brevis, **longus**, gracilis), **rectus abdominis**, pectineus

**Ligaments** — superior, inferior, anterior, posterior pubic ligaments

## 3 Presentation

**Pain** — **vague, ill-defined anterior pelvic / groin pain**, dull ache · sharp stabbing pain running, kicking, changing direction · worse with hip adduction / abduction · ± adductor spasm · loss of groin flexibility

**Exam** — **localised tenderness directly over the symphysis** · pain on **resisted rectus abdominis testing**

**Sports hernia contrast** — tender in the **abdomen / inguinal region, not the pubis** (comparison table in Athletic Pubalgia chapter)

**No fever** — excludes osteomyelitis\
✗ Wrong options: aspiration + antibiotics

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Study | Finding |
|---|---|
| **AP pelvis** | **Bony erosions + irregularity**, osteolytic pubis, **symphysis widening (diastasis)**, degenerative change · may be **negative early** |
| **MRI** | **Bone marrow oedema** (early) |
| Bone scan | ↑ uptake at symphysis |

### Differential diagnosis

| Image in the options | Diagnosis |
|---|---|
| **Erosions + irregularity + early widening** | **Osteitis pubis** |
| Widening, **no underlying bony abnormality** | Pubic symphysis diastasis |
| Femoral neck, compression side | Compression-type femoral neck stress fracture |
| Inferior pubic ramus | Pubic ramus stress fracture |
| Pelvic bony lesion | Pelvic osteochondroma |

**Differential** — athletic pubalgia, pubic ramus stress fracture, femoral neck stress fracture, inguinal hernia, oncologic disease (rare)

## 5 Management

**Nonoperative (vast majority)** — **rest, NSAIDs, rehabilitation, activity modification, gradual return to play**

**Soccer player, symphysis tenderness, negative X-rays** — **rest, NSAIDs, rehab, gradual RTP**\
✗ Wrong options: aspiration + antibiotics · hernia referral · symphyseal plating (no instability) · MRI

**Steroid injection** — into symphysis **controversial**

**Course** — **self-limiting**, may take **several months**
