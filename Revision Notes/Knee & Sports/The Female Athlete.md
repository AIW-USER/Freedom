# The Female Athlete

*Knee & Sports · 58 / 61*

## 1 Basics

### Physiologic sex differences

**Women have** — **↑ body fat %** · **↓ maximal O₂ consumption + haemoglobin** · **↓ cardiac output** · **↓ muscle mass + strength**

**↑ incidence in women** — **patellofemoral disorders**, **stress fractures**, **ACL injuries**

### Triad definition & components (low energy availability, amenorrhoea, BMD; thyroid not included)

| Component | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Low energy availability** | **± disordered eating** (with or without an eating disorder) · insufficient caloric intake for demand |
| **Menstrual dysfunction** | **Amenorrhoea** / oligomenorrhoea · energy imbalance, low body fat, hypothalamic–pituitary axis changes |
| **↓ Bone mineral density** | **Osteoporosis** (often presents as a **stress fracture**) |

**NOT components** — **stress fracture (TO)** — a consequence, not a component · **thyroid dysfunction** (OB) · ligamentous laxity, ↑ ACL tears, hirsutism (OB)

**Dysmenorrhoea in the options (TO)** — counted under menstrual dysfunction → not the answer; **stress fracture** is the odd one out

**Eating disorder** — both "LEA **with** an eating disorder" and "LEA **without** an eating disorder" = components (OB)

**Names** — **female athlete triad**, coined **1992 by ACSM** (adolescent + young female athletes) · **anorexia athletica** = synonym (Orthobullets, OB) · **RED-S** (relative energy deficiency in sport) — **IOC 2014**, broader, **includes males**

**Anorexia athletica criteria (OB)** — **GI complaints**, **restriction of caloric intake**, **weight loss**, **menstrual dysfunction** · **requires ABSENCE of an affective disorder** (e.g. depression) · true osteoporosis relatively **uncommon** in female athletes → traditional triad criteria rarely met\
✗ Wrong options: presence of affective disorder

**Thyroid (OB)** — **not a core component**; **may occur secondarily** to the metabolic + hormonal disruption\
✗ Wrong options: core component · unrelated · primary cause · always present / needed for diagnosis

**Associated (OB)** — amenorrhoea, osteoporosis, **↓ immune function**, insufficient caloric intake · not **low LDL** → actually **↑ LDL** (hypoestrogenism)

**Energy availability (TO)** — **EA = (energy intake − exercise energy expenditure) ÷ fat-free mass (kg)** · **≥ 45 kcal/kg FFM/day optimal** · 30–44 subclinical · **< 30 = low EA → triad / RED-S** · RMR **< 90% predicted** = LEA marker

**Continuum (TO)** — ACSM 1992 → **revised 2007 as a continuum** · **even one component = diagnostic** · menses: eumenorrhoea → luteal suppression → anovulation → **oligomenorrhoea (cycle > 35 days)** → **amenorrhoea (> 3 months)** · bone: normal → osteopenia → osteoporosis → stress fracture

**RED-S (TO)** — IOC **2014** (update 2018) · **both sexes** · multisystem: reproductive, bone, endocrine, metabolic, **haematological (anaemia)**, CVS (bradycardia, hypotension), immune, GI, psychological, performance · males: **↓ testosterone** (restore EA first, **avoid early testosterone**)

| Axis in LEA (TO) | Change |
|---|---|
| HPO | ↓ GnRH → ↓ LH / FSH → ↓ oestrogen |
| **HPT** | **↓ T3, normal TSH** (euthyroid sick) |
| HPA | **↑ cortisol** |
| GH–IGF-1 | **↓ IGF-1** |
| Leptin–ghrelin | **↓ leptin, ↑ ghrelin** |

**Screening tools (TO)** — **LEAF-Q** (females) **score ≥ 8 = high risk** · **IOC RED-S CAT**: **green** full participation · **yellow** modified training · **red** no participation until cleared

**Differential of amenorrhoea (TO)** — PCOS (↑ LH:FSH, hirsutism) · pregnancy (β-hCG) · thyroid · **hyperprolactinaemia** → exclude before functional hypothalamic amenorrhoea · **peak bone mass by 25 yr**; adolescent BMD loss irreversible

**Cardiovascular (OB)** — **endothelial dysfunction** (nitric oxide imbalance between vasodilators + vasoconstrictors) → atherosclerosis, ↑ future CVD · RED-S: **dyslipidaemia + ↑ visceral adiposity → precocious coronary atherosclerosis**

### Pathophysiology – energy deficit & hormones

**Root cause** — **low energy availability (LEA)** = cornerstone of RED-S + triad · drives the hormonal + bone deficits

**Axis** — LEA disrupts **hypothalamic–pituitary–ovarian axis** (OB) · "**hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal** axis disruption" (OB, RED-S) → reproductive hormonal dysfunction

**Bone** — **↓ BMD from ↑ bone turnover** · loss of **oestrogen / progesterone** protective effect → osteoporosis → stress fractures

**Undernutrition (TO)** — **↓ IGF-1 + ↓ leptin** → ↓ appetite stimulation, menstrual problems, impaired bone remodelling → **osteopenia + stress fractures**

### Genes & lab markers

| Marker | Direction / value | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Cortisol** | **↑** | Maintains blood glucose (OB) |
| **Free T3** | **↓** | Lowers basal metabolic rate (OB) |
| **LH** | **↓** | Diverts energy from reproduction (OB) |
| IGF-1, leptin | ↓ | Undernutrition (TO) |
| Oestrogen | ↓ | Hypoestrogenism from menstrual dysfunction |
| **LDL** | **↑** | Hypoestrogenism (OB) (✗ Wrong options: low LDL) |
| Serum calcium | **Normal** | Not a useful next test (OB) |
| 25(OH)D | Normal **≥ 30 ng/mL** · **< 75.8 nmol/L = stress fracture risk** | TO |
| **Iron status** | **Ferritin / transferrin ratio** | TO — see Calcium & iron |
| **COL5A1** | **CC genotype under-represented** in females with ACL rupture | Orthobullets |

**Hormone trap (OB)** — **↑ cortisol, ↓ free T3, ↓ LH**\
✗ Wrong options: ↑ T3 · ↑ LH · ↓ cortisol

### Epidemiology – amenorrhoea & sports

| Amenorrhoea prevalence | Source |
|---|---|
| **Elite runners — nearly 50%** | Orthobullets, OB |
| Vigorous exercisers **10–20%** · elite runners + professional ballet dancers **40–66%** | OB |
| Exercise-induced **5–25%** (by level + sport) | OB |

**Sports** — **running + gymnastics** · aesthetic / endurance (**gymnastics, cross-country**) · higher stress fracture risk: **gymnastics, cross-country, lacrosse** · multiple sports (not a risk factor)

### Pregnancy & Title IX

**Pregnancy** — **↑ estradiol → ligamentous laxity** · ↑ body weight → **16–32% ↑ O₂ consumption**

**Title IX** — no person in the US excluded, denied benefits or discriminated against **on the basis of sex** in any educational programme receiving **federal financial assistance**

## 2 Mechanism

### Sex differences in ACL injury

**Risk** — **2–10× males** (Orthobullets, this topic), esp. **pivoting sports** · ACL Tear chapter: **4.5 : 1** (Orthobullets), **2–9×** (OB), **\~8×** (TO) — read the options

| Factor (Orthobullets) | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Landing biomechanics + neuromuscular control** | **Conditioning + strength = biggest role** · land with knee in **more extension + valgus** (from **hip IR**) |
| Notch | **Smaller notch** |
| Ligament | **Smaller ACL** |
| **Cyclic hormones** | ↑ risk in **first half (preovulatory phase)** of cycle |
| Alignment | Leg alignment |
| **Genetics** | **CC genotype of COL5A1** under-represented in females with ACL rupture |

**Menstrual phase conflict** — **preovulatory** (Orthobullets, this topic) · **no consensus** (ACL Tear chapter) — read the options

**Most significant contributor (OB)** — **neuromuscular coordination + training** — **modifiable**\
✗ Wrong options: ACL size · oestrogen levels · notch size · valgus alignment

**Prevention** — **neuromuscular training (jump training)**, plyometric conditioning ↓ ACL rate · NM training **↑ medial hamstring EMG → ↓ dynamic valgus** on sidecutting (Zebis)

## 3 Presentation

### Stress fracture as presentation – menstrual & nutritional work-up

**First contact** — often presents to the **orthopaedic surgeon / team physician with a stress (osteoporotic) fracture**

**Stress fracture risk** — **amenorrhoeic athletes 2–4× eumenorrhoeic** (TO) · **most frequent site = tibia** (TO) · menstrual irregularity → **more and multiple** stress fractures (Warren)

**Sites (OB)** — **weight-bearing cortical bone: tibia, metatarsals, femur** · not usually trabecular bone

**NOT ↑ in amenorrhoeic athletes (OB)** — femoral head AVN, ACL rupture, labral tear, FAI → answer = **stress fracture**

**Amenorrhoea + Beighton 4 (volleyball)** — at risk of **tension-side femoral neck stress fracture** (Figure A) · Beighton **> 5/9** = hypermobility (OB)

![Tension-side femoral neck stress fracture (arrow)](../../MCQ/attachments/q1468-illa-femoral-neck-stress-fx-arrow.jpg)

**Runner, tibial stress fracture, BMI 16, no menses 7 mo** — **poor caloric intake** = **most common cause of amenorrhoea** in female athletes\
✗ Wrong options: NF1 (tibial bowing, MPNST) · osteofibrous dysplasia (anterior eccentric tibial lesion in child) · EWS-FLI1 (Ewing) · focal fibrocartilaginous dysplasia (abrupt varus at metaphyseal–diaphyseal junction)

**4th MT stress fracture + contralateral 1 yr ago** — **amenorrhoea 12 months**\
✗ Wrong options: daily OCP (helps regulate menses) · multiple sports · T-score −0.5 (normal) · BMI 26 (normal)

**Thresholds (OB)** — **BMI < 21** predictive of stress fracture (some) · **T-score < −1.0 osteopenia**, **< −2.5 osteoporosis**

**Marine recruits (OB)** — **\~5%** of female recruits fracture in **13 wk** basic training · **\~40% femoral / pelvic** (more severe) · biggest risk = **no menses in the previous year** (vs 10–12 menses) · lesser irregularity → trend only\
✗ Wrong options: prior mileage · running frequency · self-rated fitness · race

**Peak bone mass** — hypoestrogenic athletes **may never reach peak bone mass** → possibly **irreversible** deficit · **neither calcium nor ↑ activity compensates** · lost BMD may never be regained

### Runner's nipples (TO)

**Cause (TO)** — **irritation from rubbing against clothing** · **cold-weather sports** (cold → hard, prominent nipples) · common in **cyclists** (cold + perspiration)\
✗ Wrong options: warm weather precipitates

**Treatment (TO)** — **tape nipples** to restrict movement · petroleum jelly, moisturisers, vitamin E soaks · **sports bra**

**Sports bra (TO)** — firm but elastic (room for padding), **non-abrasive**, absorptive · **crossed Y-shaped back, no hooks / fasteners** · **no seams over nipple** · personally fitted

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Diagnostic criteria

| Term | Definition | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Secondary amenorrhoea** | **Cessation of menses for 6 months after ≥ 1 normal cycle** | Orthobullets, OB |
| Secondary amenorrhoea | Absence of bleeding **6 months** or of **3–6 consecutive cycles** after normal menses (no pregnancy) | OB |
| Amenorrhoea (RED-S) | No menses for **≥ 3 months** | OB |
| Osteopenia / osteoporosis | T-score **< −1.0** / **< −2.5** | OB |
| Hypermobility | Beighton **> 5/9** | OB |

**Amenorrhoea cut-off conflict** — **6 months** (Orthobullets, OB) · **3 months** (OB, RED-S) — read the options\
✗ Wrong options: ↑ volume 3 mo · pain 2 mo · missed month after peak training · ↓ volume 2 mo

**Pre-participation screening (TO)** — triad domains = **eating patterns, menstrual cycles, stress fracture history** · source gives **no key**; the item **outside the triad** = **supplement / drug-abuse history** (inferred)

### Work-up of a female athlete with a stress fracture

**Any female athlete with a stress fracture** — work up for triad: **menstrual history + nutritional consultation + bone density** (OB)

**DEXA** — female athlete with **amenorrhoea + stress fractures** (Orthobullets)

**Runner, femoral neck stress fracture on MRI** — evaluate **osteoporosis + anorexia + secondary amenorrhoea** (all of the above)

**Prior 5th MT stress fracture, now tension-side femoral neck** — fix + **obtain menstrual history**\
✗ Wrong options: never compete again (can return when counselled) · serum calcium (normal) · psychiatry (only if eating disorder diagnosed) · ↓ training (not next step)

**Femoral neck rule (OB)** — **tension side → internal fixation** · compression side → rest, nonop (line > 50% or effusion → screws: Femoral Neck Stress Fractures chapter)

### Key radiology findings

| Study | Finding / use |
|---|---|
| Radiographs | **May be normal or equivocal** · screening films may miss stress reaction |
| X-ray femur | **Smooth cortical thickening, distal medial femoral diaphysis** = femoral stress injury |
| X-ray tibia (lateral) | **Anterior cortical thickening** = stress reaction · **"dreaded black line"** = poor prognosis → surgery |
| **MRI** | **Preferred** · rule out fracture in **high-risk areas (femur, femoral neck)** · **before return to sport** · stress reaction vs impending / complete fracture · STIR oedema (periosteum, cortex, marrow) |
| Bone scan | **Fallen out of favour** — radioisotope + relatively high ionising radiation |
| **DEXA** | BMD — amenorrhoea + stress fractures |

### Hip flexor tightness – modified Thomas test (TO)

**Technique (TO)** — supine, **knees bent over table edge** · hold one knee to chest · test knee should stay at **90°**, hip + posterior thigh flat on table · **pass** = knee stays at 90° · **fail** = knee extends to < 90°

| Finding (TO) | Interpretation | Muscles short |
|---|---|---|
| Thigh **touches** table, knee **stays flexed** | **Negative** | — |
| Thigh **off** table, knee **extends** | **Both one- + two-joint** flexors short | All below |
| **Thigh touches table, knee extends** | **Two-joint hip flexors short** | **Rectus femoris, TFL, sartorius** |
| Thigh **off** table, knee **flexes > 80°** | **One-joint** hip flexors short | **Iliopsoas, pectineus, adductor longus / brevis / magnus** |
| Thigh abducts as hip extends, **lateral patellar deviation**, thigh IR + leg ER | **TFL** short | TFL |
| Hip abduction + flexion + ER and knee flexion (**≥ 3** = tight) | **Sartorius** short | Sartorius |

| Muscle (TO) | Joints crossed | Actions |
|---|---|---|
| Iliopsoas | **1** (hip) | Hip flexion, ER |
| Pectineus | **1** | Hip flexion · abduction (as listed) |
| Adductor longus / brevis | **1** | Adduction, flexion, ER |
| Adductor magnus | **1** | Adductor part: flexion, adduction, ER · hamstring part: extension, IR |
| **Rectus femoris** | **2** (hip + knee) | Hip flexion, **knee extension** |
| **TFL** | **2** | Hip abduction, flexion, IR · knee extension |
| **Sartorius** | **2** | Hip flexion, abduction, ER · **knee flexion** |

## 5 Management

```
Female athlete triad with stress fracture
├─ First / most critical step → multidisciplinary nutrition + psychological counselling
├─ Thigh pain at rest, equivocal X-ray → stop training + MRI
├─ Tension-side femoral neck → fixation + menstrual history
└─ Tibial stress reaction, no fracture line → nonop + review diet and training
```

### Treatment – nutrition rather than bisphosphonates

| Arm | Content |
|---|---|
| **Psychological counselling** | **Core** of treatment (Sabatini) · eating behaviours, self-esteem |
| **Dietary management** | **Establish energy balance** · ↑ weight / food intake, ↓ exercise · ↑ **calcium + vitamin D** |
| **Training** | **↓ intensity + cross-training** (↓ stress fracture risk) · **limit training until menses resume** |
| Supplements | **Calcium + vitamin D** for osteoporosis · consider **cyclic oestrogens / progesterones** for osteoporosis |
| **OCPs** | **May help amenorrhoea** (Orthobullets, OB) · **use OCPs to normalise menses** (TO) |
| Team | Physician, athletic trainer, **sports dietician**, mental-health provider, family, coaches |

**Least appropriate (OB)** — **bisphosphonates**\
✗ Wrong options: calcium + vitamin D · ↑ food intake · ↓ training · counselling

**Advice NOT suitable (TO)** — **"avoid OCPs"** · TO Rx = OCPs to normalise menstruation + **↑ body fat** via diet + prompt stress fracture care\
✗ Wrong options: improve nutrition · increase body fat

**Hormone conflict** — OCPs beneficial (Orthobullets, OB, TO) · **hormone replacement does not fix the energy deficit; no longer primary Rx for functional hypothalamic amenorrhoea** (OB) — read the options

**Most critical initial step (OB)** — **multidisciplinary nutritional + psychological counselling to correct energy deficit**\
✗ Wrong options: NSAIDs + crutches (NSAIDs impair bone healing) · oral oestrogen / progesterone · 6 wk complete rest (deficit persists → recurrence) · DEXA first

**Tibial stress reaction, 3 mo amenorrhoea (15 yr)** — **discuss eating habits + training regimen** · stop the precipitating activity, protected WB rehab\
✗ Wrong options: keep running · CAM walker / casting (stiffness) · IM nail (no clear fracture line → nonop first)

**Hurdler, vegan, BMI 18.1, femoral cortical thickening, pain walking** — **immediate cessation of training + MRI**\
✗ Wrong options: ↓ intensity + PT · dietician only · bone scan + oncology · bone stimulator / US (evidence limited for femoral)

**Natural course** — appropriate diet + moderated exercise → **natural return of menses**

| TO management detail | Content |
|---|---|
| Energy | **+300–600 kcal/day** initially · target EA ≥ 45 · **↑ intake before ↓ training** |
| Macronutrients | CHO 50–60%, protein 15–20%, fat 25–30% · **20–25 g protein post-exercise** |
| **Ca + vit D** | **1500 mg Ca + 1500–2000 IU vit D / day** (TO, triad lecture) · vs 2000 mg + 800 IU (TO, below) — read the options |
| Training | **↓ load 10–20%**, no double sessions until menses resume |
| **Hormones (amenorrhoea > 6 months)** | **Transdermal oestrogen + cyclic oral progesterone preferred** (bone, no hepatic effects) · **combined OCP symptomatic only — masks LEA** (TO) · read the options vs OCP rows above |
| Bisphosphonates | Severe osteoporosis only, after skeletal maturity, cautiously |
| Psychology | **CBT** preferred |
| Monitoring | DEXA every 6–12 months · vit D 6-monthly · RMR 3-monthly · menses monthly |

**Return to play (TO)** — normal menses (or testosterone) · **EA ≥ 45** · stable weight **≥ 90% ideal body weight** · **BMD (T / Z) ≥ −1.0** · MDT clearance

**Recovery rates** — **energy status: days–weeks** (↑ IGF-1, reverses energy conservation) → **menses: months** (oestrogen anti-resorptive) → **BMD: years**

![Triad components recover at different rates](../../MCQ/attachments/q5522-illa-triadrecov.jpg)

**Return to sport** — when properly counselled, may **return to high-endurance sport** (OB)

### Calcium & iron requirements (TO)

**Calcium for stress-fracture prophylaxis (TO)** — **2000 mg/day** + **vitamin D 800 IU/day** (= **200% + 150%** of recommended intake)\
✗ Wrong options: 500 · 1000 · 1500 mg

**Vitamin D (TO)** — intake up to **2000 IU/day** · normal **≥ 30 ng/mL** · **25(OH)D < 75.8 nmol/L** = stress fracture risk · supplementation ↓ stress fracture prevalence · high 25(OH)D → ↓ body fat, ↑ muscle mass

**Vitamin D biology (TO)** — fat-soluble, first found in **cod liver oil** · from diet (fortified dairy, fish oils) or **UV irradiation of 7-dehydrocholesterol** in skin · active **1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D** regulates **> 900 gene variants**

**Stress fractures in athletes (TO)** — prevalence **6.5–9.7%** · **metatarsals + tibia** commonest · risk factors: **female sex, age > 40 or < 18**, high arches, LLD, flat feet

**Best iron test in female athlete (TO)** — **ferritin / transferrin ratio**\
✗ Wrong options: ferritin · transferrin saturation · hepcidin

**Why not ferritin (TO)** — best marker in non-athletes, but an **acute-phase reactant** → ↑ with training inflammation even when stores are low

**Female iron depletion (TO)** — **menstrual loss**, **exercise-induced haemolysis**, **sweat loss**
