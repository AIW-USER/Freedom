# ACL Tear

*Knee & Sports · 1 / 61*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Incidence** — common · **\~400,000 ACL reconstructions / yr** · 100,000–200,000 ruptures / yr in US (OB) · **half of all knee injuries**

**Most injured knee ligament (TO)** — **MCL > ACL** · **ACL = most commonly operated** knee ligament (MCL mostly nonop)\
✗ Wrong options: LCL · ALL

**Female : male** — **4.5 : 1** (Orthobullets, OB) · **2–9×** (OB) · **\~8×** (TO) · "twice as likely" (OB) — read the options · gap **not seen until puberty**

**Females** — injured **younger**, on the **supporting leg** (males → kicking leg) · ACL = **24.5%** of ligamentous knee injuries in high-school athletes

**Risk factors** — female soccer, male basketball · **valgus moment at knee + adduction moment at hip on landing** · **previous concussion** (neuromuscular alteration)\
✗ Wrong options: varus alignment · LLD · prior meniscal tear · ↑ hamstring strength (protective)

**Associated injuries** — **lateral meniscus tear 54% in acute tears** (most common intra-articular injury) · **medial meniscus in chronic** · PCL, LCL / PLC (high energy, knee dislocation) · chronic ACL-deficient knee → **chondral injury**, **complex unrepairable + bucket-handle MM tears**

### Anatomy – AM/PL bundles, footprints (resident's & bifurcate ridges), blood supply & ALL

| | **Anteromedial (AM)** | **Posterolateral (PL)** |
|---|---|---|
| Named for | **Tibial** insertion | **Tibial** insertion |
| Tight in | **Flexion** (TO: taut in flexion + extension) | **Extension**; loose in flexion + mid-flexion |
| Isometry | **More isometric** | **Greatest length change** |
| Main job | **Anterior tibial translation** (esp. in flexion) | **Rotation** near extension; resists tibial IR + **pivot shift** |
| Exam | **Lachman / anterior drawer** | **Pivot shift** |
| Size (TO) | **Longer + bulkier** (\~38 mm) | Shorter (\~18 mm) (✗ Wrong options: "PL bulkier + longer") |
| Femoral footprint | **Proximal** to bifurcate ridge; high + deep; **10:30** | **Distal** to bifurcate ridge; low + shallow; **9:30** |
| Tibial footprint | Anterior | Posterior |

**Both bundles** — **entire femoral insertion lies posterior to the lateral intercondylar ridge** (medial wall of LFC) · femoral insertion **nearly vertical in extension** (AM proximal to PL), **nearly horizontal at 90°** · parallel + taut in extension (TO) · **no fibre truly isometric** · **no histological separation** of bundles (TO)

**Footprint areas (TO)** — AM femoral 47 ± 13 mm², tibial 56 ± 21 mm² · PL femoral 49 ± 13 mm², tibial 53 ± 21 mm²

**Femoral ridges** — **lateral intercondylar ridge = resident's ridge** → **anterior edge** of ACL, runs proximo-distal · **bifurcate ridge** → separates **AM / PL**, runs antero-posterior (⟂ LIR) (Orthobullets, OB) · TO: "resident's ridge separates AM + PL" — read the options

**Medial intercondylar ridge + bifurcate prominence** — on **lateral wall of MFC** → **PCL** footprint\
✗ Wrong options: ACL

**Ideal femoral tunnel (OB)** — **6.1 mm posterior to resident's ridge + 1.7 mm proximal to bifurcate ridge**\
✗ Wrong options: distal to BR + anterior to resident's ridge

**Tibial insertion (OB)** — **11 mm anterior to anterior border of PCL** · \~6 mm anterior to median / medial tibial eminence · \~9 mm posterior to intermeniscal ligament · medial + anterior to posterior border of **anterior horn LM** · meniscal roots variable (unreliable alone)\
✗ Wrong options: 15 mm anterior to PCL · 16 mm / 11 mm anterior to eminence · 17 mm behind IML

**Dimensions** — **32 mm long** (22–41 mm) × **7–12 mm wide** · 31–38 mm (TO) · **narrowest at mid-substance**; insertions **> 3.5×** larger (TO)

**Tibial insertion (TO)** — **broader than femoral (\~120%)**, fibres fan out · fibres pass under transverse intermeniscal ligament, blend with anterior horn LM · **stronger than femoral** → **avulsion on tibial side**, substance tear femoral side

**Course (TO)** — **intra-articular but extrasynovial** (intracapsular) · origin **posteromedial LFC (medial wall of LFC)** → runs inferomedially → tibia between spines

**Composition** — **90% type I** + 10% type III collagen\
✗ Wrong options: type II (articular cartilage, nucleus pulposus) · type III (Dupuytren) · type IV (basement membrane)

**Blood supply** — **middle genicular artery** + **synovium** · MGA arises **directly from popliteal**, **pierces oblique popliteal ligament / posterior capsule**, runs along dorsal ACL · **inferior medial + lateral genicular** → distal ACL (minor) · **no significant supply from bony attachments** (Scapinelli) · proximal ACL better supplied\
✗ Wrong options: superior genicular · inferior medial genicular as main supply

**Avascular zone (TO)** — **5–10 mm proximal to tibial attachment** (anterior fibrocartilage) → poor healing

**MGA also supplies** — **PCL** (+ fat pad) · ACL tear haemarthrosis = MGA branches · menisci → inferior / superior medial + lateral genicular (**LM = inferior lateral**, **MM = inferior medial**)

**Innervation** — **posterior articular nerve** (branch of tibial nerve)

**Function** — **85%** of restraint to **anterior tibial translation** · secondary restraint to **tibial IR** + varus / valgus (TO: resists varus at full extension) · strength **2200 N**

**ACL reflex (TO)** — mechanoreceptors (**Ruffini, Pacini, Golgi-like**) → proprioception via fusimotor system · **not a protective mechanism** · loss / inhibition → **quadriceps weakness**\
✗ Wrong options: explains female tears · explains poor healing

**Notch (TO)** — narrower in women · **notch width index** (epicondylar : notch width) small → ↑ ACL tear · notch roof = **Blumensaat's line**; notch roof angle **37°** (23–60°)

**PCL contrast (TO)** — bundles named for **femoral** footprint · **AL 55%**, bulkier, tight in **flexion** · **PM 45%**, tight in **extension** · 38 mm long, 10–11 mm diameter · tibial insertion wider than femoral\
✗ Wrong options: "ACL named for femoral, PCL for tibial"

**ALL** — origin **proximal + posterior to lateral epicondyle / LCL** · insertion **anterolateral tibia halfway between Gerdy's tubercle + fibular head** · anterolateral rotatory stabiliser · **Segond = ALL avulsion** · LET recreates it

**Lateral knee distractors** — tibial tubercle = patellar tendon · **anterior** fibular head = LCL (femur just posterior to lateral epicondyle) · **posterior** fibular head = biceps long head · lateral gastrocnemius → femur posterior + proximal to LCL · popliteus → femur anterior + distal to LCL · lateral epicondyle = ALL **origin**

### Genes & lab markers

**COL5A1 genotype** — **↓ risk of ACL rupture in women**\
✗ Wrong options: GH dislocation · cuff tear · patellar dislocation · discoid meniscus

**Collagen** — ACL **90% type I** / 10% type III

**Post-ACLR septic arthritis** — **ESR 105 + CRP 44** at 4 wk (TO) · **CRP 100 mg/L, ESR 40, aspirate 52,000 WBC/mm³** at 3 wk (OB) → infection

### ACL-deficient kinematics & gait

**Gait** — **absent normal femoral IR in terminal swing** · ↓ anterior tibial translation in late swing (adaptive: ↓ quads / ↑ hamstrings)\
✗ Wrong options: absent femoral ER · ↑ posterior femoral translation · ↑ midstance flexion · ↑ hip adduction

**Normal gait** — heel strike near full extension, femur IR on tibia → midstance flexion + femoral ER · late swing: anterior tibial translation (quads)

**Kinematics** — **↑ paradoxical anterior femoral translation with flexion** · **larger AP translation of MFC** · LFC **more posterior** in full extension · axial rotation same in first 30°, differs beyond\
✗ Wrong options: no change

**Medial tibial translation** — at higher flexion in ACL-deficient knee → ↑ contact of tibial spine with inner MFC → degeneration

**Dynamic imaging** — **anterior shift + IR of tibia at low flexion** = **ACL** · posterior shift = PCL · ↑ IR + posterior translation = PCL + MCL · ↑ valgus + slight IR = MCL · ↑ ER = PLC (LCL, popliteus, arcuate)

**Consequence** — abnormal loading → **post-traumatic OA**, meniscal + osteochondral injury

## 2 Mechanism

### Pathoanatomy

**Non-contact pivot** — tibia translates anteriorly with knee in **slight flexion + valgus** · or **blow to lateral knee** · soccer, basketball, skiing, football

**Bone bruise mechanism** — **valgus–ER of femur on fixed tibia** → LFC impacts **posterior lip of lateral tibial plateau** (= pivot-shift event)

**Segond mechanism (TO)** — **IR + varus in flexion** · capsular avulsion (mid-lateral capsule / ALL; meniscotibial ligament — OB)

**Hyperextension** — **kissing contusions of anterior tibia + femur** (PCL-type)\
✗ Wrong options: ACL pattern

**Female predisposition** — landing biomechanics + **quadriceps-dominant** neuromuscular activation (biggest role)

### Mechanism – PCL injury on flexed knee (TO)

**Lands on flexed knee, foot plantarflexed (TO)** — **dashboard-type** posterior force on tibia → **PCL**\
✗ Wrong options: ACL · PLC · LCL

### Female athletes – risk factors & neuromuscular prevention

| Female risk factor (TO) | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Quadriceps dominance** | More quad than hamstring activation; slower to peak hamstring torque · males decelerate with hamstrings |
| Erect landing | **Insufficient knee + hip flexion**; ↑ hip IR + adduction on landing |
| Pelvis / alignment | Wider pelvis, **↑ Q angle**, knee valgus |
| Notch / ligament | **Small intercondylar notch**, smaller ACL |
| Laxity | Greater ligament laxity |
| Hormones | Oestrogen, relaxin — indirect, via neuromuscular system |
| **NOT a risk factor (TO)** | **↑ external rotation at landing** · **hamstring dominance** |

**Other risk factors (TO)** — hard field surface, inappropriate footwear, **↑ BMI**, **high tibial slope**, **foot pronation**, female sex

**Greatest contributor** — **neuromuscular factors** · notch far less important\
✗ Wrong options: notch width · BMI · laxity · limb alignment

**Highest-risk cohort** — **women with knee valgus moment on landing**\
✗ Wrong options: hip adduction moment · men

**Landing pattern (OB)** — **↓ hip + knee flexion, ↑ knee ER, dynamic valgus** · TO says ↑ ER at landing is NOT a risk factor — read the options

**Hamstrings** — relative **hamstring weakness** / ↑ quad : hamstring ratio, electromechanical delay · after **menarche** quad strength ↑ more than hamstrings → hamstring-focused programmes

**Menstrual cycle** — no consensus\
✗ Wrong options: ovulatory · post-ovulatory phase

**Prevention** — **neuromuscular training / plyometrics (jump training)** — core, abductor + hamstring synchrony, avoid dynamic valgus; land with more knee flexion, less valgus (Griffin; **FIFA 11+**) · skiers → teach how to fall

**Bracing** — **no proven prevention** except **ACL-deficient skiers**\
✗ Wrong options: brace to cut re-injury risk

## 3 Presentation

**History** — **"pop"**, pain deep in knee, **immediate swelling (70%) / haemarthrosis**, instability, can't return to sport, hard to weight-bear

**Non-contact + acute haemarthrosis** — **ACL tear > 60%** (literature > 70%; Maffulli 71 / 106) · acute effusion → ACL; slow effusion → meniscus\
✗ Wrong options: MCL · MM tear · patellar dislocation · contusion

**Inspection** — effusion · **quadriceps avoidance gait** · **varus → ↑ re-rupture risk**

**Motion** — lacks extension → **meniscal block** (locked bucket handle) or arthrofibrosis · check McMurray, dial, varus / valgus

**Lateral joint line tenderness** — likeliest extra finding with + pivot shift (LM tear)\
✗ Wrong options: MJL tenderness · dial · varus at 30° · posterior drawer

**Neurovascular** — peroneal function after high-energy / multiligament pattern

### Diagnosis – Lachman grading, pivot shift mechanism, hemarthrosis

| Lachman grade | Orthobullets | OB |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | 3–5 mm | 0–5 mm |
| **2** | 5–10 mm | 6–10 mm |
| **3** | > 10 mm | > 10 mm |
| **A / B** | A = firm endpoint · B = no endpoint | B = soft / no endpoint |

**Lachman** — **20–30° flexion**, stabilise femur, translate tibia · compare sides (physiological laxity) · **2B = 9 mm, soft endpoint at 30°** · 4 mm firm = 1A · 12 mm firm = 3A · at 90° = anterior drawer

**PCL tear** — **"false" Lachman** (posterior subluxation)

| Test | Sensitivity | Specificity | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Lachman** | **87%** | 93% | **Most sensitive**; mainly AM bundle |
| **Pivot shift** | 61% | **97%** | **Most specific**; PL bundle / rotation; easier under anaesthesia |
| Anterior drawer (90°) | 48% | — | Lower sensitivity |
| KT-1000 | — | — | Quantifies anterior laxity; slight flexion, ER 10–30° |

**Pivot shift — mechanism** — extension + **tibial IR + valgus** → **lateral tibial plateau subluxed anteriorly** (impinges LFC) → flex → **ITB becomes a reducing force** → **LTP reduces** with a clunk\
✗ Wrong options: medial plateau · ER · reduced in extension

**Reduction angle** — **20–30°** (Orthobullets) · **30–40°** (OB) · 15–20° (Delhi active test, TO) — read the options

**Pivot shift** — mimics the giving-way event · patient fully relaxed · **better gauge of functional outcome** · stays abnormal with **vertical** graft\
✗ Wrong options: anterior femoral tunnel

| Pivot shift grade | Finding |
|---|---|
| 0 | Normal |
| I | Glide (may be physiological) |
| II | Clunk |
| III | Clunk with locking (gross / explosive) |

**Delhi active test (TO)** — **standing**, active test for **complete ACL tear** · limb abducted, foot on table (tibial IR) · flex → at **15–20°** subluxed LTP relocates (shift / clunk) · extend → anterolateral subluxation (jerk)\
✗ Wrong options: supine · PCL

| Positive test | Points to |
|---|---|
| **Pivot shift / Lachman** | **ACL** |
| Quadriceps active test, reverse pivot shift, posterior drawer (12 mm at 90°) | **PCL** |
| Dial ↑ ER > 10° (\~15°) at 30° only | **PLC** |
| Dial at 30° + 90° | **PLC + PCL** |
| Varus laxity at 30° | LCL / PLC (at 0° + 30° if ACL torn too) |
| McMurray, extending with **IR + varus** | **Lateral meniscus** |
| McMurray, extending with **ER + valgus** | **Medial meniscus** |
| Patellar apprehension | Patellar instability / MPFL |

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings — MRI & bone bruises, Segond fracture

| Study | Finding / use |
|---|---|
| **X-ray** — AP, lateral, sunrise / Merchant | Often **normal**; **Segond**; **deep sulcus sign**; tibial spine avulsion |
| **Segond fracture** | Avulsion of **proximal lateral tibia** by **ALL** (AP or **notch view**) · **pathognomonic** · ACL tear in **75–100%** · present in 9% of ACL tears |
| **Deep sulcus (terminalis) / lateral femoral notch sign** | **Lateral X-ray**: LFC depression at terminal sulcus (junction of tibial + patellar articular surfaces) · **> 2 mm** deep = diagnostic (uncommon, specific) · PD-FSE MRI: sulcus terminalis impaction + oedema |
| Post-ACLR X-ray | Tunnel position (vertical = both tunnels steep) · **tunnel osteolysis / widening** around screws · fluoroscopy: tibial centre at **40% along Amis–Jakob line** |
| **MRI** — confirm + concomitant injury | Sens **97%** / spec **100%** (Orthobullets) · accuracy **90–95%** (TO) |
| MRI direct signs | **Discontinuity on T2** (often proximal), ACL **too flat** vs Blumensaat, **non-visualisation** (subacute: only small distal stump), tibial spine avulsion |
| Femoral bony avulsion (adolescent) | AP X-ray fragment at femoral condyle · MRI: lax fibres + attached bony fragment → **repair** |
| MRI coronal | Fibres fail to reach femur · **empty notch / lateral wall sign** (fluid at femoral attachment → proximal avulsion) |
| **Bone bruise** | **Middle ⅓ LFC (sulcus terminalis) + posterior ⅓ lateral tibial plateau** · > ½ of acute tears (\~50%) · **↑ T2 (fat-sat), ↓ T1** = trabecular haemorrhage |
| Other indirect (TO) | Anterior tibial translation, **PCL buckling** (non-specific), haemarthrosis (non-specific) |
| **CT** | **Revision** — **most sensitive + specific for bone loss / tunnel widening** |

**Bruise "not ACL" (TO)** — **medial tibial plateau** bruise · **anterior proximal tibia oedema** (hyperextension) (✓ posterolateral tibia + LFC)

**Chronic ACL on MRI** — fibres scar to PCL → acute (flat) angle

**Bone bruise significance** — **delayed recovery of ROM** after ACLR · subchondral change persists years → long-term chondral damage · when present, pathognomonic\
✗ Wrong options: re-rupture · laxity · infection

**Double PCL sign** — displaced **bucket-handle MM** tear (vs LM)

|---|---|
| **Segond** (anterolateral proximal tibia) | **ACL** (ALL avulsion) |
| **Arcuate sign** (fibular head tip — LCL / biceps) | **PLC** |

### Diagnostic criteria (MRI signs, TO)

| Sign | Threshold |
|---|---|
| **ACL–Blumensaat angle** | Normal **≤ 15°** · **> 15°** (apex anterior) → torn, collapsed ACL |
| **Deep lateral femoral notch** | **> 2 mm** |
| **Anterior tibial translation** | **≥ 5 mm** indicative · **> 7 mm** diagnostic |
| **PCL index** (B ÷ A: PCL chord ÷ max perpendicular to PCL) | **< 3** (buckled PCL) → ACL tear |
| PCL buckling after ACLR | **Graft laxity** |

### Tibial eminence avulsion – meniscal block (TO)

| Meyers–McKeever | OB (3 types) | Modified (TO, 4 types) |
|---|---|---|
| **I** | Non- / minimally displaced anterior margin | Little or no displacement |
| **II** | Anterior aspect elevated, **posterior hinge intact** | Elevated anteriorly + proximally, **posterior cartilaginous hinge** |
| **III** | **Completely displaced** | Completely displaced, intact fragment |
| **IV** | — | **Comminuted** |

**Epidemiology (TO)** — **2–5%** of knee injuries with effusion, age **8–14** · unossified eminence (< 14 yr) fails through cancellous bone · ACL attaches to **medial spine** · same mechanism as ACL tear

**Imaging** — X-ray → CT + MRI · subtle in adults → MRI (OB)

**Block to closed reduction (TO)** — **anterior horn MM > intermeniscal ligament > anterior horn LM** · entrapment **26% type II (6/23)**, **65% type III (37/57)** (Kocher) (TO stem says "tibial tubercle" — means eminence)

```
Tibial eminence fracture
├─ Type I or type II reduced in extension → aspirate, cast in slight flexion 6 wk
│      └─ then → controlled-motion brace, full ROM by 10–12 wk
└─ Type II–III not reducible, type IV → arthroscopic / open reduction + suture or screw
```

| Fixation (TO) | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| **Screw** | Less demanding, earlier mobilisation | Needs **large fragment**, irritation, not for comminuted, impingement, iatrogenic comminution, **physeal damage**, removal if malpositioned |
| **Suture** | **Minimal physeal damage**, comminuted OK (through ACL base) | Technically demanding, cinch loops constrict vascularity |

**Concomitant injury** — **meniscal** most common

**Complications (TO)** — **persistent ACL laxity \~40%** (types III–IV) · **malunion** (commoner; delayed diagnosis → extension block → debride fragment / notchplasty) · nonunion (more with nonop) · loss of extension · **arthrofibrosis 36% if ROM after 4 wk vs 0% early ROM** (Patel)

### Posterior knee (ACL mucoid degeneration / ganglion) (TO)

**Mucoid degeneration (TO)** — **glycosaminoglycan** infiltration of ACL · **posterior knee pain** (impinges PCL + posterior capsule, bone erosion), pain in **flexion** (AM tightening) or terminal extension, heaviness on stairs

**Treatment (TO)** — **arthroscopic debulking / debridement**\
✗ Wrong options: ACL reconstruction · CKC → OKC rehab · PCL reconstruction

## 5 Management

```
Acute ACL tear
├─ Low demand, low laxity, no cutting / pivoting → 3 months supervised PT, lifestyle change
├─ Stiff, effusion, can't SLR → pre-hab: PT for ROM + quads first
│      (not acute ACLR, immobilisation, rest 4 wk)
└─ Young / active / pivoting sport / instability → ACL reconstruction
       └─ Locked bucket-handle meniscus → ACLR + meniscal repair, urgently
```

### Operative criteria

| Decision | Indication / threshold |
|---|---|
| **ACLR — patient** | Young + active (↓ meniscal / chondral damage), **children** (activity limitation unrealistic), older active (**> 40 not a CI**), partial tear with instability, prior failure |
| **ACLR — knee** | **Full ROM restored first** (unless meniscal block) |
| **Nonop** | Low demand, ↓ laxity, recreational without cutting / pivoting · non-op ↑ meniscal / cartilage damage (buckling episodes, level I–II sport) |
| **ACL repair** | Femoral / tibial **avulsion** without mid-substance rupture, paediatric · (✗ Wrong options: mid-substance tear) |
| **LET (OB)** | Age **14–25** with **≥ 2**: return to contact pivoting sport, **pivot ≥ grade 2**, **Beighton > 4** · also Segond, chronic ACL, lateral femoral notch sign, lateral laxity, **recurvatum > 10°** |
| **ALL reconstruction (TO)** | **Grade 3 / explosive pivot**, pivoting athlete, **Segond**, **revision ACLR**, subjective rotational laxity, **chronic** ACL, high-level sport, **LFC depression**, **Telos > 10 mm** (✗ Wrong options: high-grade tear in young man alone) |
| **Two-stage revision** | Tunnel **> 15 mm**, malpositioned tunnels / hardware blocking, **infection** |
| **Staged revision (TO)** | Motion problem: **lacks 5° extension or 20° flexion** (stability fine) |
| **Osteotomy with ACLR** | **Varus** (> 3° between mechanical axes or WBA medial to medial tibial spine tip) · **slope > 12°** |
| **HTO contraindications** | Inflammatory arthritis, **BMI > 35**, **flexion contracture > 15°**, flexion < 90°, **> 20° correction**, **varus thrust**, PF arthritis, (lateral meniscectomy) |
| **HTO indications** | < 50 yr, active, non-obese, one compartment, good vascular status, compliant |
| **Acute MCL repair** | **Grade III distal avulsion** (pes entrapment) · grade III with valgus instability |
| **Graft size** | **≥ 8 mm** (< 8 mm → failure) |

### Timing – restoring motion before surgery; arthrofibrosis & cyclops lesion

**Pre-hab** — **regain full ROM + quad strength before ACLR**; wait for swelling / inflammatory phase to settle · acute ACLR in stiff, weak knee → **arthrofibrosis + weakness**

**Stiff acute knee** — e.g. ROM 5–70°, can't SLR → **PT for ROM** · Segond + ROM 10–75° + dial 5° → **ACL; PT to optimise ROM** (dial < 10–15° asymmetry = no PLC)

**Adolescent with deep sulcus sign** — **PT, then early reconstruction** (delay → instability, secondary meniscal damage, degeneration)\
✗ Wrong options: immediate ACLR · ORIF · PT + RTS

**Predictors of post-op motion loss** — **female**, **acute surgery**, **limited preop ROM**, **classic bone bruise**

**Prevention** — pre-hab, wait out inflammation, **correct tunnel position**, aggressive cryotherapy

| Shelbourne arthrofibrosis (TO) | Extension loss | Flexion loss | Patella |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | < 10° | Normal | Normal |
| **2** | > 10° | Normal | Normal |
| **3** | > 10° | **> 25°** | ↓ mobility (tight) |
| **4** | > 10° | **> 30°** | ↓ mobility + **patella infera** |

**TO key** — > 10° extension + > 25° flexion loss, **patella normal → Type 2** (patellar mobility is the discriminator) — read the options · prevalence **4–38%** · Rx **arthroscopic debridement**

```
Loss of motion after ACLR (most common complication)
├─ < 12 weeks → aggressive PT + serial splinting
└─ > 12 weeks → lysis of adhesions / MUA
```

**Cyclops lesion** — fibroproliferative nodule **blocks extension**, **"click" at terminal extension** · anterior tibial tunnel → cyclops (TO) · not graft-related (OB)

**Infrapatellar contracture syndrome** — uncommon · **↓ patellar mobility**, **patella infera (baja)** — poor prognostic sign · loss of extension + **active AND passive** flexion\
✗ Wrong options: "active but not passive"

### Reconstruction technique — tunnel targets, tensioning & fixation

**Goal** — anatomic restoration of AP + rotational stability · arthroscopic · clear remnant to see bony landmarks (remnant kept vs removed — no difference)

**Single vs double bundle** — no patient-reported difference · single most common · double may restore kinematics, ↓ laxity · no definitive clinical benefit

**Horizontal graft (TO)** — **prevents pivoting** · femoral tunnel **obliquity** sets rotational stability · isometric (vertical) graft → translation only · 10 o'clock also avoids PCL impingement in flexion\
✗ Wrong options: vertical · roof-top · 11 o'clock

**Femoral tunnel — coronal** — lateral wall **10 o'clock right / 2 o'clock left** (Orthobullets, OB) · **10:30 right / 1:30 left** (OB) · **9:30–10 o'clock right** (TO) — read the options

**Femoral tunnel — sagittal** — **1–2 mm rim** to posterior cortex (Orthobullets) · **\~2 mm**, knee at 90° (TO) · centre on **bifurcate ridge**, posterior to resident's ridge · OB: posterior to LIR, just proximal to BR, **\~1 cm anterior to posterior articular margin of LFC** · drill in **> 70° (70–90°) flexion** → no posterior wall blowout

**Pinczewski (radiographic)** — femoral tunnel **86% along Blumensaat's line** (0% anterior → 100% posterior) · tibial tunnel **48%** AP and **46%** medial → lateral across plateau · graft coronal inclination **19°**

**Tibial tunnel — sagittal** — centre **10–11 mm anterior to PCL** (Orthobullets, OB) · **7 mm anterior to PCL = best guide** (TO) — read the options · 6 mm anterior to median eminence · 9 mm behind intermeniscal ligament · **15 mm behind anterior tibial edge** (TO) · **in line with posterior edge of anterior horn LM** (most used landmark) · **posterior + parallel to Blumensaat's line** in full extension · native insertion starts 10–14 mm behind anterior tibia (TO)

**Tibial tunnel — coronal** — **< 75° from horizontal** (Orthobullets) · **60–75°**, well-placed \~75° (OB) · start halfway between tubercle and posteromedial tibial edge · lateral fluoroscopy: tibial centre at **40% of Amis–Jakob line**

**Tensioning** — pre-conditioning ↓ stress relaxation **up to 50%** · 20 N vs 40 N → no difference · **fix in 20–30° flexion** · initial tension falls soon after fixation

**Fixation** — **aperture** (interference screw) vs **suspensory** (cortical button, screw + washer post, staple) · alone or combined

**No effect on outcome** — graft type, one vs two incisions (OB) · tunnel position **does** (anterior to LIR = definitive adverse effect)

### Femoral tunnel drilling – anteromedial portal vs transtibial

| | **Transtibial** | **Transportal (AM portal)** |
|---|---|---|
| Femoral tunnel drilled | **Through the tibial tunnel** | **Through the AM portal** (AL portal = scope) |
| Clock position | ≥ 11 o'clock / roof-top 12 o'clock | Anatomic **10 o'clock** |
| Graft | **Vertical** | **Oblique / horizontal — more anatomic** |

### All-inside, BEAR & fixation devices (TO)

**Common fixation combos (TO)** — femoral button + tibial interference screw · femoral + tibial interference screws · femoral cross-pin + tibial bio-screw + staple · **femoral button + tibial button = all-inside**

**All-inside (TO)** — **single semitendinosus** (tripled / quadrupled), **gracilis spared** · **BPTB cannot be used** · **sockets** (not full tunnels) via **flip cutter** (retro-reamer) · graft ≤ **7.5 cm** (ideally < 6.5 cm) · **suspensory buttons both ends** (reverse tensioning loops) · **no interference screw**

**All-inside advantages (TO)** — bone-preserving · **↓ pain** (little periosteal disruption) → faster rehab · smaller incision · spared gracilis shields graft · femoral socket drilled outside-in at **90°** (no hyperflexion, no muscle splitting)

**All-inside "limitation" (TO)** — **false: "double bundle impossible"** — it is possible (4 buttons) · real limits: learning curve, larger AM portal / passport cannula · **ST allograft all-inside = most minimally invasive ACLR**

**BEAR (TO)** — **bridge-enhanced ACL repair**: suture repair + **extracellular matrix scaffold** soaked in patient's blood · absorbed \~**8 weeks** → native tissue · BEAR trial: 2-yr results comparable\
✗ Wrong options: syndesmosis · AVN · chondral defect

**ACL repair history** — abandoned (high failure, poor cellularity) · renewed for **avulsions**, paediatrics

**Tibial retro-screw (TO)** — inserted **retrograde, proximal → distal** · **less tunnel widening** (head blocks tunnel mouth, stops synovial fluid)\
✗ Wrong options: "more widening than antegrade"

### Graft fixation – screw divergence

**Graft–screw divergence** — **> 30°** → loss of fixation, **early** failure (explanation range **15–30°**) · risk factor (e.g. 35°) · commoner on **femoral** side\
✗ Wrong options: tibial · late failure · "eliminated by accessory AM portal"

**Avoid** — drill + screw through the **same portal** · hyperflex for screw if femur drilled transtibially · guide wire · suspect if screw hard to insert; check X-ray

### Associated injuries – meniscal secondary restraint, MCL, LET for rotatory instability

**Meniscus with ACL** — **acute → lateral meniscus**, **chronic → medial meniscus**, **posterior horn MM** most common in chronic (TO) · posterior > anterior horn

**PHMM** — **secondary restraint to anterior tibial translation** in ACL-deficient knee (buttress wedging against posterior MFC)\
✗ Wrong options: PCL · popliteus · anterior horn LM · quads

**Partial PHMM meniscectomy** — **↑ medial peak contact loads** · ↑ in situ ACL graft forces\
✗ Wrong options: ↑ contact area · ↓ graft force

**Meniscal tear timing** — **repair / meniscectomy at time of ACLR** · ↑ healing when repaired with ACLR · LM repair doesn't raise ACL failure

**Locked knee + ACL tear** — lacks 40° extension, lateral McMurray → **ACLR + meniscal repair**\
✗ Wrong options: ACL repair for mid-substance · MCL / PLC surgery when stable

```
ACL + MCL injury
├─ Low-grade / proximal (e.g. grade II) → brace 4–6 wk, MCL heals → then ACLR
└─ Grade III distal avulsion or high grade → acute MCL repair with ACLR (within 7–10 days)
```

**Distal MCL** — entrapped around pes ("**Stener lesion of the knee**") → won't heal · acute ACLR with untreated MCL → arthrofibrosis · unaddressed valgus instability → ↑ graft re-rupture

**Not addressed acutely** — LFC impaction fracture, minimally displaced fibular head fracture, grade II PCL, grade II proximal MCL

**Chondral defects** — treat at ACLR (or staged) · consistently ↓ long-term PROs

**PCL / PLC** — reconstruct with ACLR or staged · missed → **varus instability, graft overload**

**Malalignment** — correct coronal + sagittal before or with ACLR · **lateral closing wedge** corrects slope better than medial opening wedge

**LET** — **↓ graft rupture + persistent rotatory laxity at 2 yr** (single-bundle hamstring + LET, young high-risk) · e.g. 16F, **Beighton 6, recurvatum 11°, pivot grade 2** → add LET\
✗ Wrong options: "avoid vertical tunnel" (not an extra procedure) · suspensory / dual fixation · meniscal repair

**ALL (TO)** — key stabiliser of **anterolateral tibial rotation** · affects pivot shift when ACL fails · aim: rotational control + ↓ retear · LET = ITB strip (attached at Gerdy's) under LCL → femur posterior + proximal to lateral epicondyle · Lumiere technique · no long-term data

### Paediatric ACL – skeletal age & physeal-sparing techniques

**Skeletal age** — **onset of menarche = best predictor** in girls · maturity \~**2 yr** after menarche (OB) · most growth in **18 months** after menarche (OB) · \~9 mm distal femur + 6 mm proximal tibia growth / yr · Tanner good; menarche delayed in elite athletes\
✗ Wrong options: shoe size · doubled height at 2 · patellar ossification

**Open physis** — < 14 yr · **60% of adolescent tears partial**

**Nonop (peds)** — compliant, low demand, no other pathology, **partial tear with near-normal Lachman + pivot** · otherwise poor: instability, secondary meniscal + chondral damage, ↓ return to sport

| Maturity | Technique |
|---|---|
| **Pre-adolescent (M ≤ 12, F ≤ 11)** | Combined intra- + extra-articular **ITB** (physeal-sparing) |
| Approaching maturity, open physes (12F premenarchal, Risser I) | **All-epiphyseal** or **partial transphyseal**, **soft-tissue (hamstring) autograft**, tunnel **< 8 mm** |
| **Near / at maturity (M ≥ 16, F ≥ 14)**, Tanner IV, post-menarche | **Adult-type transphyseal** — BPTB, quad or hamstring autograft |
| Femoral-sided avulsion, near maturity | **Arthroscopic repair + transphyseal fixation** (femoral tunnels + cortical button) |
| Stiff knee (ROM 20–70°) | **PT for ROM first**, then decide |

**ITB technique** — ITB harvested free proximally, **left on Gerdy's tubercle** → **over-the-top of LFC** → through notch → **under anterior intermeniscal ligament** → sutured to LFC + proximal tibia (anteromedial tibial periosteum)\
✗ Wrong options: under MM / LM / LCL / biceps

![Physeal-sparing ITB reconstruction: over-the-top and under the intermeniscal ligament](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob3008-itb.jpg)

**Physeal injury — risk factors** — **tunnel diameter** (most important) · **oblique / horizontal tunnel** · **interference screw** · **high-speed reaming** · LET · dissection near **perichondral ring of LaCroix** · sutures near tibial tubercle · BPTB > hamstring (bone block across physis)

**Tunnel size** — **> 12 mm most important**; 8 mm < 3% vs 12 mm > 7–9% of physis (Orthobullets) · **> 8 mm** ↑ injury; **7–9%** of physis enough for disturbance (OB) · **< 8 mm keeps < 5%** (OB) — read the options

**Protective** — vertical tunnel, slow reaming, small diameter, **soft-tissue grafts** (rarely disturb growth) · no difference in growth disturbance between techniques · physeal disruption without disturbance **10%**

**BPTB in children** — **physeal bridging → distal femoral valgus + proximal tibial recurvatum**; tibial tubercle apophysis injury · allograft → high failure (avoid in young)

**Over-the-top femoral graft** — transtibial over-the-top **partial transphyseal**: tibial tunnel transphyseal (anatomic), femoral graft passed over back of LFC → avoids femoral physis but **lax in flexion, tight in extension**

### Rehabilitation – open-chain exercise, accelerated & eccentric protocols, return to sport

**Immediate** — aggressive **cryotherapy** · **immediate WBAT** (↓ PF pain) · **early full passive extension** (esp. with MCL injury / patellar dislocation) · rehab starts immediately\
✗ Wrong options: "start exercises ≥ 10 days after surgery" (TO false-statement stem, no key given)

| Exercise | Early post-op | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Squat** (low degree, wall, dumbbell) | ✓ Closed chain | Foot fixed → compression + hamstring co-contraction ↓ anterior shear |
| **Leg press** | ✓ Closed chain | As above |
| **Stationary bike, stair climber** | ✓ Closed chain | No unopposed anterior translation |
| **Quad sets, straight leg raise** | Yes | Isometric about knee |
| **Isometric hamstrings (any angle)** | ✓ **Lowest graft strain (60°)** | Pulls tibia posteriorly |
| Hamstring curls | Yes (avoid after **PCL** recon) | Open chain but no ACL load |
| Active motion **35–90°** | Yes | Orthobullets |
| **Seated leg extension / resisted knee extension** | **Avoid first 6 wk** | Open chain → quads pull tibia forward (mimics drawer / Lachman) |
| Open-chain extension **0–40°** | No | **↑ graft load 3–4×**, no hamstring co-contraction (OB) |
| Isokinetic quads **15–30°** | No | Orthobullets |
| Short-arc extension **0–30°**, unweighted | ✓ acceptable (OB) | Conflicts with above — read the options |

**↑ strain** — isolated quads at 15° or 30°, co-contraction at 15°, active resisted extension 30° → 0°

**Eccentric strengthening from week 3** — **↑ quadriceps volume + strength at 1 yr**, no extra laxity\
✗ Wrong options: concentric at wk 3 · eccentric from wk 12 · hinged brace 6 wk · CPM 2 wk

**Accelerated vs nonaccelerated rehabilitation programs** — **equal outcomes** (laxity, ROM, strength, RTS) · accelerated = immediate WBAT with crutches · programme **19 wk** vs non-accelerated **32 wk** · ≠ earlier RTS · no ↑ re-injury

**Blood flow restriction (BFR)** — tourniquet ↓ venous return → anaerobic milieu → **↑ quad strength + cross-sectional area** at low load · outcome benefit unproven\
✗ Wrong options: Graston · TENS · ultrasound · acupuncture

**Braces** — no added functional stability · no brace / duration better than none

**Return to sport** — no accepted criteria · historic **≥ 9 months** · pass hop / jump tests · **dynamic valgus → ↑ ipsilateral + contralateral rupture** · RTS before clearance → ↑ re-rupture · RTS at 11 months not a risk factor

**RTS decision** — **psychological + lifestyle factors** dominate; **fear of re-injury** = commonest reason not to return · prior ACLR, poor hop symmetry → not returning\
✗ Wrong options: surgeon advice · pain · sport duration · graft type

### Revision & re-rupture risk factors (tibial slope, varus, PLC injury, tunnel widening, allograft)

**Revision volume** — \~**13,000 / yr** in US · re-rupture \~**5%**

**Re-rupture risk factors** — **young age**, **hyperextension (≥ 5°)**, high activity / contact sport, **slope > 12°**, **varus**, **missed PLC**, **allograft**, **graft < 8 mm**, female sex, **tunnel widening** (> 15 mm worst), open-chain / aggressive rehab

**Hyperextension (MARS)** — 1145 revisions · passive HE **≥ 5° in 33%** · predictors: younger age, allograft, HE ≥ 5° (**> 2-fold** rupture) · e.g. recurvatum 5–10° → "preoperative exam findings"

**Not risk factors** — concomitant MCL injury, Segond on X-ray, accelerated rehab, tibial tunnel 10 mm in front of PCL, interference screws, hamstring graft per se, 9 mm graft, slope 7–8°, new anatomic femoral tunnel, LET, two-stage revision, prior PLC reconstruction, LM repair, mild PF arthrosis

**Female re-rupture** — female sex = biggest listed risk (OB) · re-rupture risk > never-injured

**Missed PLC** — **varus laxity at 30°** (0° + 30° with ACL tear), **dial ↑ ER > 10° at 30° not 90°**, varus stress X-ray +5 mm → **reconstruct PLC with ACL** (or as stage 1) · PLC = **LCL, popliteus, PFL** · LCL resists varus 55% at 5°, **69% at 25°**

**Intra-articular femoral bone plug** — 3 wk post-op, + Lachman → **revision ACLR**\
✗ Wrong options: excise plug · modified rehab

**Revision technique** — find cause of failure · **high-strength autograft** (quad, hamstring) · allograft **> 2×** re-rupture · **re-harvest BPTB contraindicated** · **dual fixation** (suspension + screw) · bone graft dilated tunnels (**15 mm**) · LET (STABILITY trial: ↓ rupture + rotatory laxity) · conservative rehab

**Revision technique (TO)** — **most common cause = tunnel malposition** (✗ Wrong options: graft selection) · tunnel **> 15 mm → stage 1 bone graft** (✗ Wrong options: 10 mm) · **remove hardware only if necessary** (✗ Wrong options: "femoral screw must come out") · bioabsorbable PLA screw still present ≥ 2 yr → leave or ream through · prosthetic ligament out en bloc · reuse incisions, **skin bridge ≥ 7 cm** · generous revision notchplasty

```
Failed ACLR, revision planned
├─ First → standing alignment films, slope, CT tunnels, exam for PLC
├─ Tunnel > 15 mm, blocking hardware or infection → two-stage: graft bone, revise at 3–6 months
├─ Varus malalignment → HTO before or with ACLR (never ACLR first)
│      └─ plus slope > 12° → lateral closing-wedge HTO
├─ Isolated slope > 12° → anterior closing-wedge (deflexion) HTO
└─ Varus / ER laxity at 30° → PLC reconstruction with ACL
```

**Case — 18 mm tibial tunnel, infantry** — **standing alignment films first**, then two-stage revision

**Case — varus 10°, slope 15°, mLDFA 87°** — **single-stage lateral closing-wedge HTO + revision ACLR** (hamstring)\
✗ Wrong options: DFO (femur normal) · isolated revision · revision before HTO · bracing

**Case — varus 11°, slope 7°, mild medial OA, 35 yr** — **medial opening-wedge HTO + ACLR**\
✗ Wrong options: ACLR alone · biplanar (slope normal) · lateral DFO (for valgus) · UKA

**Case — ROM 20–130°, varus** — **flexion contracture > 15° = CI to HTO**\
✗ Wrong options: age 32 · BMI 30 · partial medial meniscectomy · varus itself

### Tunnel malposition – effects & most common cause of failure

| Malposition | Graft behaviour | Clinical picture |
|---|---|---|
| **Femoral too anterior** (anterior to resident's ridge) | **Tight in flexion, lax in extension** (cam effect → stretch) | **Loss of flexion** (**"captured knee"**), graft elongation · e.g. **ROM 0–75°, negative pivot** |

| **Femoral vertical (12 or 11 o'clock)** | Controls AP translation only | **+ Pivot, − Lachman / drawer**, cutting instability, **PL bundle not restored**, ↓ Lysholm · TO: PCL impingement, extension block |

| Tibial too medial / lateral | Impinges MFC / LFC | — |

### Graft harvest complications – saphenous nerve & patellar tendon rupture

### Postoperative septic arthritis

**Incidence** — **< 1%** · most superficial · **CoNS (S. epidermidis) commonest**, S. aureus 2nd · presents **2–14 days** post-op (Orthobullets) · first 10–14 days (OB) · TO case at 4 wk

**Risk** — graft contamination, **dropped graft** · vancomycin soak may ↓ risk · sequential antibiotic washes → no ↑ infection in dropped grafts

**Diagnosis** — ↑ ESR / CRP → **aspirate (Gram stain + culture)**

```
Septic arthritis after ACLR
├─ Gächter 1–2 → arthroscopic I&D, retain graft, ≥ 6 wk IV antibiotics (culture-based)
├─ Gächter 3–4 → medial / lateral arthrotomy
└─ Persistent infection → graft removal (no tunnel bone graft while infected)
```

| Gächter (TO) | Findings |
|---|---|
| 1 | Blurred effusion, hyperaemic synovium |
| 2 | **Purulent** effusion, fibrin, hypertrophic synovium |
| 3 | Synovial adhesions, necrotic synovium + cartilage |
| 4 | Diffuse cartilage necrosis, **bone erosion, osteolysis** |

**Graft retention** — first-line; often repeat I&Ds · works better with **S. epidermidis** than S. aureus\
✗ Wrong options: single-stage revision · graft removal + staged revision first · open arthrotomy first
