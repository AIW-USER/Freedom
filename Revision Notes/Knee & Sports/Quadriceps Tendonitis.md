# Quadriceps Tendonitis

*Knee & Sports · 52 / 61*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Who** — **adult athletes** · **M:F 8:1** · often **recurrent**

**Risk factor** — **jumping sports**: basketball, volleyball, athletics (long jump, high jump)

**Age contrast** — quadriceps tendinopathy = **older adults** · patellar tendinopathy = adolescents / young adults (patellar tendinitis chapter)

### Pathophysiology

**Mechanism** — **repetitive eccentric contractions** of extensor mechanism

**Pathoanatomy** — **microtears at bone-tendon interface** (superior pole of patella)

**Quadriceps tendinosis** — **chronic degeneration, no inflammation**

**Jumper's knee** — **patellar tendonitis** · usually **patellar tendon insertion at patella**, less often at tibial tubercle

### Anatomy

| Quadriceps tendon layer (trilaminar) | Muscle |
|---|---|
| **Anterior (superficial)** | **Rectus femoris** |
| **Middle** | **Vastus medialis + vastus lateralis** |
| **Deep** | **Vastus intermedius** |

**Blood supply** — **medial, lateral + peripatellar arcades**

**Nerve** — **muscular branches of femoral nerve (L2, L3, L4)**

## 3 Presentation

**History** — overuse in a **jumping athlete** · **recent ↑ demand** / activity

**Symptoms** — pain at **superior border of patella** · worse with activity · swelling

**Exam** — **tender on deep palpation at quadriceps insertion** · **pain on resisted open-chain extension** · **can actively extend against gravity** ✓

**Palpable gap** — suggests **quadriceps tendon tear** (not tendonitis)

**Also check** — knee alignment, swelling, **patellar subluxation**

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Study | Finding / use |
|---|---|
| X-ray — AP + lateral (± **sunrise / Merchant** for instability) | **Usually normal** · **tendon calcinosis** in chronic degeneration · varus / valgus alignment |
| X-ray — patellar height | Exclude rupture: **Blumensaat's line reaches inferior pole at 30° flexion** · **Insall-Salvati 0.8–1.2** |
| **Ultrasound** | Detects + localises disruption · **operator- and user-dependent** · tendinosis: **thicker, hypoechoic, power Doppler flow** |
| **MRI** | **Most sensitive** · **intrasubstance signal + tendon thickening** |

**Normal tendon width on US (OB)** — **2–3 cm** (figure shows 3.9 cm in tendinosis)

**Imaging morphology (King, OB)** — localised thickening, **hypoechoic areas**, **↑ vascularity**

## 5 Management

```
Quadriceps tendinopathy
├─ First line → rest, ice, NSAIDs, activity modification, PT (ROM → eccentric)
├─ Failed nonop → PRP (leukocyte-rich) or polidocanol injection
└─ Failed ≥ 3 months, severe → arthroscopic / open debridement (very rare)
```

### Eccentric strengthening

**Best rehab for overuse tendinopathy (TO)** — **eccentric strengthening** (TO answer not given; eccentric = first line in OB)\
✗ Wrong options: strict bed rest 1 month · plaster 1 month · steroid injection first line then strength training

**Nonop** — **mainstay** · rest until pain improves → PT **ROM first → eccentric exercises** · hamstring, quadriceps + core strengthening

**Cortisone injection** — **contraindicated** → **quadriceps tendon rupture**

**CrossFit, 1 month pain, US tendinosis** — **PT for eccentric strengthening**\
✗ Wrong options: arthroscopic debridement · open debridement + repair (needs ≥ 3 months failed nonop) · leukocyte-poor PRP · jumper's knee strap

**PRP** — **leukocyte-rich** PRP effective for **patellar** tendinopathy · **not first line**\
✗ Wrong options: leukocyte-poor

**Dragoo RCT (OB)** — 23 patients, patellar tendinopathy · eccentric exercise + US-guided dry needling + **LR-PRP** → **faster recovery**, benefit **dissipates over time** · no failures at 26 wk

**Injections after failed nonop (King, OB)** — **PRP** + **sclerosing agents (polidocanol)** → good outcomes

**Jumper's knee strap** — **patellar tendon strap** → for **patellar** tendinopathy, not quadriceps tendinosis

**Surgery** — **quadriceps tendon debridement**, **very rarely** · only after **≥ 3 months** failed nonop (OB) · arthroscopic or open → good outcomes

### Kinesiology taping

**K-tape (TO)** — "**kinesis**" = movement · **stretchy cotton / synthetic** tape mimicking **skin elasticity** + muscle extension capacity · invented by Japanese chiropractor **Kenzo Kase, 1970s**

**vs classic rigid taping (TO)** — classic = immobilisation / stability · K-tape → **joints stay mobile**

**Stretch (TO)** — **40–60% of original length** on average · up to **120–150%** by manufacturer

**Mechanism (TO)** — tape contracts → **lifts skin + subcutis off fascia** → space → ↑ blood + **lymph flow** (removes inflammatory factors) → ↓ pain + swelling · ↑ proprioception + muscle function · applied over **trigger points**

**Wear (TO)** — **3–5 days** · waterproof **acrylic adhesive** (avoid rubbing while bathing) · trim hair · **caution with anticoagulants** (skin haemorrhages)

**Pre-stretch (TO)** — manufactured on backing paper with **10% stretch** · still called "**unstretched**" · keep this pre-stretch on application

| Method (TO) | Stretch applied to |
|---|---|
| **Neutral technique** ✓ | **Tissue, not tape** |
| **Stretch technique** | **Tape, not tissue** |

| Application (TO) | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Muscle** | ↑ or ↓ tone · \~**10%** stretch · **tonus decreasing = insertion → origin** · **tonus increasing = origin → insertion** |
| **Ligament** | Chronic tendinopathy, ligament overload, trigger points · **maximum stretch**, fixed to **both osseous insertions** → receptor stimulation |
| **Corrective** | Functional correction, e.g. **patellar maltracking** · **Y tapes**, maximum pre-stretch → shifts bone position |
| **Lymphatic** | **I tapes**, \~**25%** pre-stretch → skin lift → lymphatic drainage |

**Shapes (TO)** — **I, Y, X, web, fan** strips

![Kinesiology tape shapes: I, Y, X, web, fan](../../MCQ/attachments/TO19356182-2.png)

**Myths (TO)** — **"↑ performance" — false** (treatment only; **not banned** under anti-doping unless coated with banned substance; no active drug) · **colours differ — false** (cyan, magenta, beige, black — **same properties**; colour theory only: red stimulating, blue calming) · "can't heal without tape" — false

**K-tape — true (TO)** — **neutral application stretches tissue, not tape** = best pick\
✗ Wrong options: tonus decreasing origin → insertion · colours differ in stretch / hold · "40–60%" (only an average, varies up to 120–150%)

## X vs Y

| | **Quadriceps tendinopathy** | **Patellar tendinopathy (jumper's knee)** |
|---|---|---|
| Site | **Superior pole** of patella | **Inferior pole** of patella |
| Age | **Older adults** | Adolescents / young adults |
| Strap | No | **Patellar tendon (jumper's knee) strap** |
| Steroid injection | **Contraindicated** (rupture) | **Contraindicated** (rupture) |

| | **Tendonitis / tendinosis** | **Quadriceps tendon rupture** |
|---|---|---|

| Palpable gap | No | **Yes, within 2 cm of superior pole** |
