# Quadriceps Contusion

*Knee & Sports · 50 / 61*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Who** — **M:F 2:1** · **football, soccer, rugby** · competition > practice

### Pathophysiology

**Mechanism** — **direct blow** compresses anterior thigh muscles **against the femur**

**Sequence** — small fibre tears → haemorrhage + swelling in **anterior compartment** → myonecrosis + **haematoma** → scar → muscle regeneration

| Thigh compartment | Muscles |
|---|---|
| **Anterior** | Sartorius, rectus femoris, vastus lateralis / medialis / intermedius |
| Medial | Gracilis, adductor magnus / longus / brevis |
| Posterior | Biceps femoris, semitendinosus, semimembranosus |

### Muscle healing biology (immobilisation, losartan)

**Prolonged immobilisation** — **↑ granulation tissue** (→ myositis ossificans) · scar contraction · poor organisation · **↓ tensile strength + ↓ tensile stiffness**\
✗ Wrong options: ↑ stiffness · better tensile strength · ↓ MO

**Brief immobilisation → mobilisation** — **parallel penetration of muscle fibres through (limited) scar**, in line with native muscle · ↑ myotubules, early nerve regeneration, ↓ inflammation\
✗ Wrong options: lymphocyte / macrophage infiltrate · ↓ nerve fibrils · narrowed myotubes

**Immobilisation alone** — **disorganised** fibre penetration through **dense** scar

**Losartan (angiotensin II receptor blocker)** — **↑ muscle regeneration, ↓ fibrosis** (animal model) · ↓ apoptotic cascade · thought to act via **insulin-like growth factor** blockade (OB)\
✗ Wrong options: aspirin · allopurinol · acetaminophen · bupropion

**PRP + losartan** — ↑ angiogenesis + **follistatin**, **↓ phosphorylated Smad2/3**, ↓ fibrosis

## 3 Presentation

**History** — collision / direct blow in competition

**Pain** — **worsens over first 24–48 h** · worse with dynamic movement + **knee flexion**

**Exam** — swelling, **ecchymosis, point tenderness** · compare firmness + circumference · palpable defect = partial / complete tear · ± knee effusion · **↓ knee flexion** · antalgic gait

**Straight leg raise** — tests **extensor mechanism** · able (even with pain) → extensor mechanism intact

**Distal NV exam** — screen for **thigh compartment syndrome**

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Study | Use / finding |
|---|---|
| X-ray | Only for severe injury with **suspected fracture** · chronic → **myositis ossificans ("egg-shell" calcification)** |
| **MRI** | Concern for **extensor mechanism disruption** · best for soft tissue · **oedema / haematoma in anterior compartment** |
| CT for arterial extravasation | not part of initial work-up (no signs of active bleeding) |

### Jackson and Feagin classification

| Grade (knee ROM at 24 h) | Knee flexion |
|---|---|
| **Mild** | **> 90°** |
| **Moderate** | **45–90°** |
| **Severe** | **< 45°** |

## 5 Management

```
Quadriceps contusion
├─ All acute → 120° FLEXION 24–48 h + ICE + NSAIDs → PT
├─ Compartment syndrome → THIGH FASCIOTOMY
└─ Myositis ossificans → OBSERVE
       └─ painful, ↓ ROM / strength, MATURE (6–12 mo) → RESECT
```

### Treatment — immobilisation in 120° flexion

**Immobilise** — **knee in 120° flexion** (ace wrap / hinged brace) **immediately**, **24–48 h** · cryotherapy, NSAIDs · start at once to **limit haematoma**

![Immobilisation of the knee in 120° flexion](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob3103-quad-contusion-moved.jpg)

**Then** — stretching + active ROM, protected WB with crutches → sport-specific rehab once full pain-free ROM

**17-yr, firm thigh, SLR with pain, NV intact** — **cryotherapy, NSAIDs, hyperflexion splinting** (overnight → ↓ MO)\
✗ Wrong options: CT for extravasation · emergent fasciotomy · **splint in extension** · MRI for extensor rupture

**Knee immobilised in 120° flexion → injury?** — **quadriceps contusion**\
✗ Wrong options: iliac crest contusion (hip pointer leg in **extension**) · lesser trochanter avulsion · hamstring rupture · sports hernia

**MRI next day** — **haematoma in anterior compartment of thigh**\
✗ Wrong options: meniscal, MCL, ACL, hamstring tears

| Study | Cohort | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Jackson | Midshipmen, **24 h hyperflexion** | RTP **3.5 days** · MO 1 / 23 |
| Aronen | 47 military, **120° for 24 h** | RTP **3.5 days** |
| Ryan | 117 West Point cadets | Disability **mild 13 d, severe 21 d** · **MO 9%** |

**Disability time** — **3–13 days** across studies · **13 days for mild** (Orthobullets) · time to return ∝ initial severity

### Thigh fasciotomy

**Indication** — **compartment syndrome** (from rupture of **deep perforating branches of deep femoral vessels**)

**Approach** — **single anterolateral incision** → incise **fascia lata** (anterior) → retract **vastus lateralis medially** → incise **lateral intermuscular septum** (posterior) · ± **medial incision** for adductor compartment

## 6 Complications

### Myositis ossificans

**Incidence** — **9–18%** (Orthobullets) · **5% and 9%** (OB study series) — read the options · more with **severe** contusions

**Risk factors (Ryan)** — **knee motion < 120°**, injury in football, **previous quadriceps injury**, **delay in treatment > 3 days**, **ipsilateral knee effusion**

**Timing** — develops **2–4 wk** after injury · serial X-rays → **"egg-shell" calcification**

**Treatment** — **observation** usually · **resect only mature lesions** (no growth, **6–12 months**) with pain + ↓ strength / ROM

**Compartment syndrome** — **thigh fasciotomy**

**Prognosis** — self-limited, but prolonged disability without treatment · early treatment speeds RTP

## X vs Y

| | **Quadriceps contusion** | **Hip pointer** |
|---|---|---|
| Site | Anterior thigh | Iliac crest |
| Position | **Knee flexed 120°** | **Leg in extension** (maximum stretch) |
| Chronic risk | **Myositis ossificans** | — |

| | **Brief immobilisation → mobilisation** | **Prolonged immobilisation** |
|---|---|---|
| Fibres | **Parallel** penetration, limited scar | **Disorganised**, dense scar |
| Tissue | ↓ inflammation, early nerve regrowth | **↑ granulation tissue** |
| Mechanics | Better strength | **↓ tensile strength, ↓ stiffness** |

## How it's asked

**"Immobilised in 120° knee flexion — diagnosis?"** → **quadriceps contusion**

**"Thigh contusion, SLR with pain, NV intact — next step?"** → **cryotherapy, NSAIDs, hyperflexion splinting**

**"MRI after flexion immobilisation shows?"** → **haematoma in the anterior compartment of the thigh**

**"Drug that improves muscle regeneration + ↓ fibrosis (animal)?"** → **losartan**

**"Prolonged immobilisation after contusion leads to?"** → **increased granulation tissue production**

**"Histology at 4 wk with early mobilisation?"** → **parallel penetration of muscle fibre through scar tissue**
