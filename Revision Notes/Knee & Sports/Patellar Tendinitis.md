# Patellar Tendinitis

*Knee & Sports · 38 / 61*

## 1 Basics

### Risk factors & diagnosis

**Incidence** — up to **20% of jumping athletes** (Orthobullets) · prevalence **8.5%** in non-elite athletes (OB) — **volleyball highest (14.4%)**, soccer lowest (2.5%)

**Who** — **adolescents / young adults** · **M > F (2:1)** · **quadriceps** tendinopathy = older adults

**Risk factors** — **poor quadriceps + hamstring flexibility** · **hamstring tightness** · male, **younger, taller, heavier**, type of sport (jumping) · patellar hypermobility · quadriceps atrophy · **hard surfaces** · **↑ training frequency**

**Predisposing factor (OB)** — **hamstring tightness**\
✗ Wrong options: female · ↑ quadriceps strength · soft surfaces · low-frequency training

**Risk factor EXCEPT** — **older age**\
✗ Wrong options: male · higher weight · quads / hamstring inflexibility · type of sport

**Witvrouw vs Zwerver (OB)** — **inflexibility = only significant factor**; height, weight, age, Q-angle, LLD, strength not (Witvrouw) · **younger, taller, heavier** = risk (Zwerver) — read the options

**Malalignment** — **not strongly associated** · malalignment → generalised PF syndrome, maltracking

**Mechanism** — **repetitive forceful eccentric contraction** of extensor mechanism · jumping, acceleration / deceleration, deep flexion

**Histology** — **degenerative, not inflammatory** · **micro-tears** at bone-tendon junction of **inferior pole** · angiofibroblastic + mucoid degeneration\
✗ Wrong options: intra-substance inflammatory reaction

**Theory (Rath, OB)** — **impingement of deep tendon fibres on inferior pole** · flexion + quads contraction ↓ pain

### Sinding-Larsen-Johansson & jumper's knee – site (TO)

**Jumper's knee (TO)** — tendinitis at **tendo-osseous junction of lower (inferior) pole of patella** · common in jumpers\
✗ Wrong options: **superior pole**

**Tooth sign (TO)** — **periosteal reaction on anterior surface of patella** · seen in jumper's knee (TO)

**Jumper's knee treatment (TO)** — eccentric quads strengthening, avoid provoking activity, taping, **deep transverse friction massage**, cryotherapy

**Sinding-Larsen-Johansson (TO)** — **osteochondrosis of inferior pole of patella** · often **bilateral** · **overuse** traction by patellar tendon on partly cartilaginous lower pole · **active adolescents 10–14 yr**

**SLJ clinical (TO)** — pain + tenderness **at lower pole of patella** (↑ loading in flexion), subpatellar oedema · later tendon thickening, **fragmentation of lower pole**, bursitis

**SLJ EXCEPT (TO)** — **pain at lower end of patellar tendon** (false) — pain is at **proximal tendon / inferior pole**

## 3 Presentation

**Symptoms** — **insidious anterior knee pain at inferior border of patella** · early: **after** activity · late: **during** activity · **"movie theater sign"** (prolonged flexion)

**Exam** — swelling over tendon, tenderness inferior pole · local soft-tissue oedema but **no effusion**, no apprehension, no crepitus

**Basset's sign** — **tender at distal pole in full extension**, **not in full flexion** → **tenderness greater in extension than flexion** ✓

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Study | Finding |
|---|---|
| X-ray (AP, lateral, skyline) | **Usually normal** · chronic: **inferior traction spur (enthesophyte)** |
| **Ultrasound** | Tendon thickening, **hypoechoic areas** |
| **MRI** (chronic, surgical planning) | **Tendon thickening** (more diagnostic than oedema) · **↑ signal on T1 + T2** at proximal tendon (✗ Wrong options: decreased) · loss of posterior fat-pad border (chronic) · partial tear |

### Blazina classification

| Stage | Orthobullets (3 phases) | OB illustration (4 stages) |
|---|---|---|
| **I** | Pain **after** activity only | Pain only after sports |
| **II** | Pain **during and after** activity | Pain at start, goes after warm-up, returns with fatigue |
| **III** | **Persistent pain with or without activity** (↓ performance) | **Constant pain at rest and with activity** |
| **IV** | — | **Complete rupture of patellar tendon** |

**Stage III** — pain **at rest** + with activity · **surgical indication** if refractory

## 5 Management

```
Patellar tendinopathy
├─ Early / Blazina I–II → rest, activity modification, quads strengthening, eccentric PT
├─ Blazina III, in season → eccentric PT + rest from sport and jumping
└─ Refractory / Blazina III / partial tear → surgical excision + repair
```

### Nonoperative care – eccentric training & rest

**First line** — ice, **rest, activity modification**, PT (**quads + hamstring stretching**, **eccentric** programme, **quadriceps strengthening**, isometric + short-arc work) · NSAIDs, moist heat, ultrasound, phonophoresis

**Offloading** — taping or **Chopat's strap** ↓ tension across patellar tendon

**Eccentric = isotonic with muscle lengthening** ✓\
✗ Wrong options: plyometric · isotonic shortening = concentric · isometric

**As effective as surgery** — **eccentric training** · also works for Achilles insertional tendinopathy\
✗ Wrong options: electrotherapy · concentric · massage · taping (none proven)

**Most recommended exercise (TO)** — **decline squat** · **3 × 15 reps, twice daily**, single-leg eccentric squat, upright torso, on a decline board\
✗ Wrong options: plyometric · treadmill protocol · concentric loading

**Why eccentric (TO)** — **promotes tendon remodelling** (collagen) · ↓ recurrence

**Plyometric (TO)** — rapid eccentric stretch → concentric of same muscle (jumping, hopping) · builds **power** · **late** rehab · early → can exacerbate (OB)

**Corticosteroid injection** — **contraindicated** → **patellar tendon rupture** · no benefit at 6 months (eccentric + heavy slow resistance keep effect)

**PRP / sclerosing agents** — **experimental**, inconsistent · PRP not first line

**18-yr-old, 6 wk, plays through pain** — **rest, activity modification, quadriceps strengthening**\
✗ Wrong options: low-dose steroid · PRP · arthroscopic debridement · **no sport until pain-free** (complete rest → weakness + inflexibility)

**Blazina III, in season, pain at rest** — eccentric PT + **rest from sport and jumping** · playing through season → no improvement\
✗ Wrong options: steroid · debridement without nonop trial · PRP · sclerosant

### Refractory cases – surgical debridement

**Indications** — **Blazina stage III**, chronic pain / dysfunction refractory to nonop, **partial tears** · severe tendinopathy, extensor disruption

**Technique** — open or arthroscopic · **resect angiofibroblastic + mucoid degenerative tissue** · bone abrasion at insertion · suture repair / anchors

**Rehab** — **immobilisation in extension** → progressive ROM · WBAT

**Outcome** — **80–90% return** to sport · aching 4–6 months · 89% normal / near normal at 12 months (OB)

**18 months pain, can't jump, MRI partial tear** — **debridement and repair**\
✗ Wrong options: cast · aggressive eccentric overload · ice + play on · steroid

**Persistent pain with and without activity, failed PT (pole vaulter)** — **surgical excision of the affected tissue**\
✗ Wrong options: concentric stretching · intratendinous / intra-articular steroid · patellar chondroplasty

## X vs Y

| | **Jumper's knee** | **Sinding-Larsen-Johansson** |
|---|---|---|
| Lesion | Tendinopathy (degenerative) | **Osteochondrosis** of lower pole |
| Age | Adolescent / young adult athlete | **10–14 yr** |
| Site | Tendo-osseous junction, **inferior pole** | **Inferior pole** (proximal tendon) |
| Imaging | Thickening, ↑ T1 / T2 signal · tooth sign (TO) | Fragmentation of lower pole |

| | **Patellar tendinopathy** | **Quadriceps tendinopathy** |
|---|---|---|
| Age | **Adolescents / young adults** | **Older adults** |
