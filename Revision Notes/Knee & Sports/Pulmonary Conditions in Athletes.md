# Pulmonary Conditions in Athletes

*Knee & Sports · 49 / 61*

## 1 Basics

### Airway disorders

**Asthma** — **transient lower airway obstruction** from hypersensitivity · **expiratory** · exercise trigger in an asthmatic = **exercise-induced asthma (EIA)**

**EIB** — **no underlying asthma** (usually; can coexist, OB) · transient **lower**-airway, **expiratory** obstruction after exertion

**EIB risk factors** — **winter / cold-weather sports** (drying + cooling of mucosa → oedema + constriction) · **endurance athletes** · high minute ventilation\
✗ Wrong options: warm weather

**EILO** — transient **upper**-airway obstruction: paradoxical vocal-cord closure or supraglottic narrowing · **inspiratory** · prominent **psychological** component · types: **supraglottic, glottic (vocal cord dysfunction), mixed**

**Exercise-induced anaphylaxis** — rare, life-threatening **type I (IgE)** reaction to exertion · after a food allergen = **food-dependent EIA (FDEIA)**

### Chest wall & vascular conditions

**Pneumothorax causes** — **spontaneous** (smoking, male, FHx, Marfan, Ehlers-Danlos) · **traumatic** (**contact athletes — ice hockey highest risk**) · **iatrogenic** (interscalene block)

**Haemothorax** — **most common result of major chest-wall trauma** · **30%** of rib fractures → pneumohaemothorax

**PE in athletes** — ↑ risk: musculoskeletal injury ± immobilisation, repetitive motion (pitchers, tennis), long-distance travel

## 3 Presentation

**Asthma** — wheeze, SOB, **chest tightness**; **expiratory wheeze**, accessory muscles

**EIB** — **triad: wheeze + SOB + cough** with exercise · "poor performance", fatigue, headache · exam often normal

**EIB timing** — onset **10–15 min after exertion**, resolves with **20 min rest** (Orthobullets) · 10–15 min after the **start** of exercise; resolves **15–20 min** after stopping (OB) — read the options

**Pseudo-asthma syndrome** — exercise-induced hyperventilation; no bronchoconstriction

**EILO** — **rapid onset**, **throat tightness / choking**, "breathing through a straw" · **inspiratory stridor** · **fails bronchodilators** (often misdiagnosed as EIA / EIB)

**Exercise-induced anaphylaxis** — onset **within 30 min** of exercise · pruritus, urticaria, GI cramps, SOB, throat tightness

**Pneumothorax** — sudden unilateral **pleuritic pain**, SOB · ↓ / absent breath sounds, **hyperresonant** · tension adds **tracheal deviation + JVD**, cardiopulmonary compromise

**Tension pneumothorax on the field** — dyspnoea, anxiety, **deviated trachea**, **hypertympanic** chest, ↓ breath sounds\
✗ Wrong options: spontaneous (closed, no deviation) · haemothorax (dull) · EIB · HCM

**Haemothorax** — less pleuritic pain · **hypotension** if large · **dull to percussion**

**PE** — sudden pleuritic pain, SOB, syncope · tachycardia, **hypoxia**, hypotension if large

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Condition | Test | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| Asthma | PFT ± **methacholine** challenge | **↓ FEV1, ↓ FEV1/FVC**, reverses with bronchodilator |
| **EIB** | **Eucapnic voluntary hyperventilation (EVH)** = gold standard | **> 10% ↓ FEV1**; baseline PFT may be normal |
| **EILO** | **Continuous exercise laryngoscopy (CEL)** = gold standard | PFT: **blunted inspiratory loop** |
| Pneumothorax | **Ultrasound** | **Absent lung sliding** |
| Pneumothorax | **CXR** | **Pleural line**, absent lung markings · tension: **mediastinal shift + depressed hemidiaphragm** |
| Haemothorax | CXR | **Opacified hemithorax** |
| PE | **Helical CT** first line · **pulmonary angiography** gold standard | V/Q scan alternative |
| HAPE (TO) | CXR | Patchy opacities, often **right middle lobe** |

![Tension pneumothorax: mediastinal shift, depressed hemidiaphragm](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob3122-cxrtensionptx.jpg)

### Pneumothorax types

| Type | Communication | Pleural pressure |
|---|---|---|
| **Closed** (e.g. bleb rupture) | None | **< atmospheric** → no tracheal deviation |
| **Open** | Air in on inspiration, out on expiration | **= atmospheric**, no tension |
| **Tension** | **One-way: in, cannot exit** | **↑ > atmospheric** → lung + mediastinal compression — **emergency** |

## 5 Management

### Return-to-play criteria

| Condition | Return to play |
|---|---|
| **Asthma / EIB** | As tolerated · **active wheezing = contraindication** |
| **Exercise-induced anaphylaxis** | After **allergist review + comprehensive action plan** · always carry **EpiPen**, exercise with a partner |
| **Pneumothorax** | **Radiographic resolution, \~4 weeks** · pain limits RTP · **no flying 2–4 weeks** |
| **PE** | **\~6 months** · on anticoagulation controversial · **contact athletes withheld** during anticoagulation |

### Exercise-induced bronchospasm

**Acute** — rest, controlled breathing, **inhaled β2-agonist (albuterol q15 min PRN)** · severe / refractory → **epinephrine + ER** · moderate–severe asthma maintenance → **inhaled glucocorticoids**

**Prevention** — **warm-up**, environmental control · **"refractory period"** (deliberate induction) works in **\~50%** · **albuterol 2 puffs 15–20 min before** activity

### EILO & anaphylaxis

**EILO** — nonoperative mainstay: **laryngeal control therapy** (lower-abdominal breathing, controlled exhalation through mouth), speech therapy, SSRIs, botulinum toxin · surgery (debulking) rare

**Anaphylaxis prevention** — no exercise **4–6 h after eating**, avoid temperature extremes, **avoid aspirin / NSAIDs** before exercise · acute: **epinephrine**, antihistamines, steroids

### Tension pneumothorax – diagnosis & needle decompression

**ATLS 10th, adults** — **standard 14-gauge 4.5 cm angiocatheter (5 cm needle), 4th / 5th ICS mid-axillary line** ✓

**Children** — **2nd ICS mid-clavicular line** still recommended

**Why changed** — 5 cm needle at 2nd ICS **fails in 22–50%** (chest-wall thickness) · risk to internal thoracic, subclavian, heart · no original evidence

**Site evidence** — 5th ICS accurate **100%** vs just over **50%** at 2nd ICS (Inaba cadaver study) · either site reasonable, **2nd ICS slightly better in obese** (Azizi) — read the options

**Site wording** — "2nd or 5th ICS" acceptable (Orthobullets, OB)

**Failed decompression** — check catheter patency → **longer 8.25 cm needle** → **finger thoracostomy**\
✗ Wrong options: any of these first

**Traps** — ✗ Wrong options: chest tube first (hospital; supplies rarely on hand) · digital thoracostomy first · long needle first (visceral injury)

```
Pneumothorax
├─ Tension → needle decompression 5th ICS MAL → chest tube in ED
├─ > 20% (lung edge > 2 cm) → chest tube + admit
└─ < 20% (< 2 cm), stable → O2, observe 4–6 h, repeat CXR
```

**Observation arm** — O2 ↑ pleural air absorption · repeat CXR before discharge + at **12–48 h**

### Haemothorax & pulmonary embolism

**Haemothorax** — **chest tube** first · repeat CXR at **4–6 h** and **24 h**

| VATS / thoracotomy criterion | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Initial chest-tube output | **> 1500 cc** |
| Ongoing output | **> 200 cc/h for 3+ h** |
| Haemothorax size | Increasing |
| Despite **2 chest tubes** | Incomplete resolution |

**PE** — anticoagulation **3–6 months (minimum 3)** · IV heparin / Lovenox → oral

### High-altitude pulmonary oedema (TO)

**High-altitude illness (TO)** — **AMS, HACE, HAPE** · usual threshold **8000 ft (2500 m)** with rapid ascent

**HAPE (TO)** — **noncardiogenic** oedema; lowlanders ascending rapidly to **> 2500–3000 m** · **exaggerated hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction**, ↑ PA pressure → capillary **"stress failure"** leak

**Onset (TO)** — **2–5 days** after arrival · rare below 2500–3000 m or after **1 week** of acclimatisation

**Symptoms (TO)** — dry cough, exertional dyspnoea, ↓ performance → rest dyspnoea, orthopnoea · **gurgling + pink frothy sputum = advanced** · cyanosis, tachycardia, temp ≤ **38.5 °C** · rales over middle lung fields

**Prevention (TO)** — **nifedipine, tadalafil, dexamethasone, acetazolamide** (500–1000 mg/day 8–12-hourly or 125 mg BD, start **24 h before ascent**)

**Treatment (TO)** — **supplemental oxygen = first line / first step** ✓ · **immediate descent = definitive** · portable hyperbaric chamber · nifedipine 30 mg SR 12-hourly\
✗ Wrong options: descent by 1000 ft · hypoxia chamber · oral nifedipine first

**Remote, no medical care (TO)** — **descent has first priority** — read the stem's setting

## X vs Y

| | **EIA / asthma** | **EIB** | **EILO** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Underlying asthma | **Yes** | **No** | No |
| Airway | Lower | **Lower** | **Upper (larynx)** |
| Phase | Expiratory | Expiratory | **Inspiratory** |
| Key symptom | Chest tightness, wheeze | Wheeze + SOB + cough | **Throat tightness, choking, stridor** |
| Spirometry | ↓ FEV1, ↓ FEV1/FVC | **> 10% ↓ FEV1** | Blunted inspiratory loop |
| Bronchodilator | Improves | Improves | **No response** |
| Gold-standard test | PFT ± methacholine | **EVH** | **CEL** |

| | **Simple pneumothorax** | **Tension pneumothorax** | **Haemothorax** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Percussion | Hyperresonant | **Hypertympanic** | **Dull** |
| Trachea | Midline | **Deviated + JVD** | Midline |
| CXR | Pleural line | **Mediastinal shift, low hemidiaphragm** | Opacification |
| First step | O2 / observe or chest tube | **Needle decompression** | **Chest tube** |

## How it's asked

**"Tension pneumothorax — ATLS next step?"** → **standard 14-G 5 cm needle, 5th ICS mid-axillary line**

**"Helmet to chest, deviated trachea, hypertympanic?"** → **tension pneumothorax**

**"Occasional β2-agonist for exertional wheeze — true?"** → **FEV1 reduction on spirometry**

**"Pink frothy sputum at altitude — first step (TO)?"** → **oxygen**

**"Throat tightness, stridor, inhaler fails?"** → **EILO → continuous exercise laryngoscopy**

**"Pneumothorax — return to play?"** → **radiographic resolution, \~4 weeks**
