# Osteochondritis Dissecans

*Knee & Sports · 35 / 61*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology & location (lateral aspect of MFC)

**Definition** — acquired, potentially reversible idiopathic lesion of **subchondral bone** → delamination + sequestration ± articular cartilage involvement + instability

**Juvenile form** — **open physes**, age **10–15** (median **13.1** Orthobullets · 13 OB) · **adult form** = skeletally mature

**Typical patient** — **13-year-old male**, lesion on **lateral aspect of medial femoral condyle** · **M:F 5–6:1**

**Sites** — **knee (most common)**, capitellum, talus · knee osteochondrosis on **0.002–0.003%** of knee radiographs

| Knee location | Share |
|---|---|
| **Posterolateral MFC (lateral aspect of MFC) — "classic"** | **70%** (Orthobullets, OB) · **60–70%** (OB) |
| **Inferior-central lateral femoral condyle** | **15–20%** (\~15%) |
| Femoral trochlea | **< 1%** |
| Patella, trochlea | Infrequent |
| **Baseball catchers** | **Posterior femoral condyle** (hyperflexion loading — OB, cartilage chapter) |

### Etiology & pathoanatomy

**Causes** — **hereditary, traumatic, vascular** (multifactorial) · **adult form → vascular** · microtrauma · mechanical (**discoid meniscus**, **tibial spine impingement**) (OB)

**Cascade** — softening of cartilage with **intact surface** → early cartilage separation → **partial detachment** → **osteochondral separation + loose bodies**

## 3 Presentation

**Pain** — **vague, poorly localised, activity-related**

**Mechanical symptoms** — catching, locking → **advanced disease** / unstable flap or loose body

**Other** — **recurrent effusions**, localised tenderness, stiffness, swelling, antalgic gait

**Wilson test** — **pain on internal rotation of tibia during extension between 90° and 30°**, **relieved by tibial external rotation**

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Study | Finding / use |
|---|---|
| Weight-bearing AP + lateral | Lesion (**incongruence of lateral aspect of MFC**), **physeal status**; may be **read as normal** → MRI if pain persists |
| **Tunnel (notch) view** | **Knee flexed 30–50°** — shows posterior condyle lesion |
| X-ray sign | Well-circumscribed **sclerotic subchondral bone + radiolucent halo**; **sclerosis = poor prognosis** |
| **MRI** | **Size**, subchondral bone + cartilage status, **signal around lesion**, **loose bodies** → **stability** |
| MRI unstable | **Fluid (high T2) behind / circumferential to fragment** (**rim sign**), fluid **breaching cartilage** / breaks in cartilage + oedema; cysts deep to lesion = subchondral involvement |
| Bone scan | **Open physes + activity behind lesion → 100% healed**; **closing physes → none** (Paletta) |
| **Arthroscopy** | **Gold standard for instability** — break in cartilage or **mobile flap on probing** |

![Tunnel (notch) view showing an OCD lesion](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob3028-tunnel-notch-view.jpg)

### Diagnostic criteria — stable vs unstable lesion

| | **Stable** | **Unstable** |
|---|---|---|
| MRI T2 | **No ↑ T2 signal deep to fragment** | **High-signal fluid line behind / circumferential** (DeSmet → less likely to heal) |
| Subchondral plate | Intact | **Articular fracture through subchondral plate** |
| Cyst | None | **≥ 5 mm fluid-filled cyst deep to lesion** |
| Cartilage | **Intact cap**, no fragmentation | **Focal osteochondral defect**, fluid through cartilage |
| Arthroscopy | Firm on probing | **Hinged, ballotable**, mobile flap, detached |

**De Smet instability signs (TO, cartilage chapter)** — T2 fluid line **≥ 5 mm** · cysts **≥ 5 mm** · focal cartilage defect **≥ 5 mm** · fluid through cartilage into bone

![MRI — fluid signal behind an OCD fragment](../../MCQ/attachments/q2189-illa-unstable.jpg)

### Clanton classification (Clanton & DeLee)

| Type | Finding |
|---|---|
| I | **Depressed** osteochondral fracture |
| II | Fragment attached by **osseous bridge** |
| III | **Detached, non-displaced** fragment |
| IV | **Displaced** fragment |

**ICRS OCD** — **types II + III = unstable** → often surgery (OB)

### Prognosis — open physes & lesion stability

| Good prognosis (nonop) | Poor prognosis |
|---|---|
| **Open distal femoral physes — best predictor** | **Closing / closed physes**, **adult form** (symptomatic → **DJD** if untreated) |
| **Younger age** | Older adolescent |
| **Classic site: lateral aspect of MFC** | **Lateral femoral condyle, patella** |
| **Size < 2 cm** (e.g. 1.5 cm) | > 2 cm |
| **Intact surface, no fragmentation on MRI** | **Synovial fluid behind lesion** on MRI |
| **No sclerosis** on X-ray | **Sclerosis** on plain radiographs |

**No proven effect** — weight / BMI, gender, trauma history, ethnicity, family history, degree of sport, **concomitant tibial apophysitis**, cartilage thickness

**Location conflict** — classic MFC site = **good prognosis** (Orthobullets, OB) vs **typical location not predictive of healing** (OB, DeSmet / Paletta question) — read the options

**Adult OCD** — rarely heals spontaneously; **very low nonop healing rate**

## 5 Management

```
OCD — first decision
├─ Open physes + stable MRI → RESTRICTED WB / ACTIVITY MODIFICATION ± brace
│      └─ fails 3–6 mo / approaching physeal closure → SUBCHONDRAL DRILLING
├─ Unstable / hinged, salvageable → ARTHROSCOPIC OR OPEN FIXATION
├─ Unsalvageable, large / bone loss → OCA or MACI (± bone graft)
└─ Asymptomatic adult → NONOP
```

```
Arthroscopic finding (OB algorithm)
├─ Stable → TRANSCHONDRAL DRILLING
├─ Unstable, reducible → FIXATION ± GRAFT
├─ Unstable + fragmentation / osteolysis → FIXATION + GRAFT
└─ Unstable + chondral damage → FIXATION + GRAFTS, CHONDROCYTE TRANSPLANT or OSTEOCHONDRAL GRAFT
```

### Operative criteria

| Indication | Source |
|---|---|
| **Impending physeal closure** | Orthobullets, Cahill |
| **Clinical signs of instability** / detachment during treatment | Orthobullets, Cahill |
| **Expanding lesion** on plain films | Orthobullets |
| **Failed nonop** (persistent symptoms in compliant patient) | Orthobullets, Cahill |
| Persistently ↑ or worsening **bone-scan activity** | Cahill |
| Mechanical locking (loose body), displaced fragment | OB |
| **Closed physes**, unstable lesion | Orthobullets, OB |

**Cahill series** — **204 JOCD**: **50% needed surgery** · fragment detachment **34%**, persistent symptoms / combined **26%** of failures

| Procedure | Indication | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **Subchondral drilling** (K-wire / drill) | **Stable** lesion on arthroscopy; **transchondral (antegrade) or retrograde** | **Fibrocartilage**; better if immature; radiographic healing **\~4.4 months** |
| **Fixation** | **Acute unstable** lesion on arthroscopy or MRI, **> 2 cm** | **85% healing** in JOCD |
| **Chondral resurfacing** | **> 2 × 2 cm** | — |
| Osteochondral grafting | **Arthroscopic < 3 cm** · **arthrotomy > 3 cm** | Allograft plugs, autograft OATS |
| Knee arthroplasty | **> 60 yr** | — |

### Stable juvenile lesions — activity modification & restricted weight bearing

**Stable, open physes** — **restricted WB + bracing** / **activity modification** · **50–75% heal without fragmentation** · also **asymptomatic adults**

**Duration** — **NWB 6–8 wk** (OB) · activity restriction **3–9 months** (OB) · cease sport 4–6 months (OB, osteonecrosis chapter)

**11 yr, intact cartilage, Wilson +** — **non-weight bearing for 6–8 weeks**\
✗ Wrong options: removal · ORIF · microfracture · full WB

**11 yr catcher, 14 × 14 mm, no sclerosis / cysts** — **stable, period of modified weightbearing**\
✗ Wrong options: fixation · OCA (larger lesions)

**10 yr, 1 × 1 cm LFC lesion, open physes** — **activity modification**\
✗ Wrong options: microfracture · antegrade drilling · fixation · excision

**13 yr, stable LFC lesion, no prior treatment** — **restricted weight bearing**\
✗ Wrong options: **aggressive physical therapy** (worsens) · excision · drilling yet · OCA

**Immature distal femur OCD** — outcomes **good**, usually heal without surgery (Schenck & Goodnight)

### Stable lesion failing nonoperative care — drilling

**16 yr, stable 1.5 × 1 cm LFC, 6 months failed restriction** — **arthroscopic drilling of the subchondral bone** · near maturity → unlikely to heal nonop; drilling **high success**\
✗ Wrong options: 6 more months · aggressive PT · screw fixation · OATS

**Drilling only works** — **stable** lesions **without circumferential fluid** on MRI

### Unstable lesions — fragment fixation or salvage

**Principle** — **salvage the fragment + restore native surface whenever possible** · stabilise **arthroscopically or open**

**Fixation options** — **cannulated screws, Herbert / headless compression screws**, bone pegs, K-wires, bioabsorbable screws / darts, metal or biocomposite · may need **hardware removal**

**Unstable OCD, 15 yr** — **arthroscopic or open reduction and internal fixation**\
✗ Wrong options: loose-body removal · 9 months restriction · drilling

**Most indicated for fixation** — **16-year-old boy, closed physis, 2.5-cm hinging OCD lesion from the medial femoral condyle**\
✗ Wrong options: intact cap lesions → drill if failed · 5.2 cm → OCA

**Catcher, failed 8 wk NWB + 4-month RTP, MRI unchanged** — **attempted salvage of the osteochondral fragment** (drilling vs screw fixation) · **immature → better salvage**\
✗ Wrong options: play on NSAIDs · more PT / restriction · MACI first

**20 yr, acute unstable 1.5 × 3 cm, no chronic bone change** — **arthroscopic assisted internal fixation of lesion**\
✗ Wrong options: PT + steroid · abrasion · MACI (needs supportive bone bed) · OATS (< 2 × 2 cm unsalvageable)

**Chronic / poor tissue / no bone on fragment** — **loose-body removal + debridement**, later cartilage restoration

**16 yr lineman, \~2.5 × 2 cm unstable lateral-MFC lesion, circumferential fluid** — **arthroscopic fixation of the lesion with headless compression screws** · microfracture **out of favour for large lesions** · same OB item calls him **skeletally mature** (explanation) and **skeletally immature** (figure legend) — fixation either way\
✗ Wrong options: bracing · MACI stage 1 · OATS · retrograde drilling

**Cartilage flap** — lets **subchondral fluid accumulate** behind the fragment

**Microfracture technique** — awl to **1–1.5 cm** below surface · **NWB 4–6 wk + CPM**

### Unsalvageable / failed lesions — osteochondral allograft

**OCA after failed fixation + microfracture** — **reliably high rate of patient satisfaction** · **survivorship > 90% at 10 yr** (✗ Wrong options: failure > 30% at 5 yr) · incorporation by **creeping substitution** (✗ Wrong options: cutting cone) · not replaced by fibrocartilage · **higher BMI → failure to return to sport** (✗ Wrong options: low BMI)

**OCA over MACI** — **subchondral bone loss > 1cm** · MACI needs minimal bone loss; add **bone graft if > 1 cm**\
✗ Wrong options: age · bipolar (worse with any procedure) · location · > 2 cm² (fits both)

| Defect size (OB) | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **< 2 cm²** | Chondroplasty, microfracture, **OATS** |
| **> 2 cm²** | **OCA or MACI**; **OCA** if bone loss / revision |
| **Chronic > 2 up to 4–6 cm²** | **OATS** (limited by harvest sites) |
| **> 6 cm²** | **OCA** (Treatment E) or MACI |

**31 yr, 2.25 × 2.75 cm untreated OCD, MPTA 87°, Mikulicz line 4 mm medial to lateral tibial spine** — normal alignment → **OCA (Treatment E)**\
✗ Wrong options: HTO · OATS · microfracture · screw

**Figure D key** — A **OATS** · B **microfracture** · C **HTO** · D **headless compression screw** · E **OCA**

## X vs Y

| | **Juvenile OCD** | **Adult OCD** |
|---|---|---|
| Physes | **Open** (10–15 yr) | **Closed** |
| Cause | Multifactorial | **Vascular** |
| Nonop healing | **Good** (50–75%) | **Very low** |
| First line | **Restricted WB** if stable | Surgery if symptomatic / unstable; nonop if asymptomatic |
| Prognosis | Better | **Worse → DJD** |

| | **Drilling** | **Fixation** | **OCA** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lesion | **Stable**, intact cartilage | **Unstable / hinged**, salvageable | **Unsalvageable**, large, bone loss |
| Example | Failed nonop, near maturity | Acute, bone on fragment | Failed fixation, > 6 cm² |
