# Patellar Tendon Rupture

*Knee & Sports · 39 / 61*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology & risk factors

**Incidence** — rare, **< 1 / 100,000 / yr**

**Who** — **3rd–4th decade** (Orthobullets) · **< 40 yr** (OB) · males in 30s–40s · **M > F**

**Quadriceps vs patellar** — **quadriceps tendon rupture more common (2:1)** · quadriceps **> 40 yr**, patellar **< 40 yr**

**Systemic (→ bilateral)** — **diabetes**, **SLE**, **RA**, **chronic renal disease** · also infectious + metabolic disorders

**Local** — **patellar degeneration (most common)** · previous injury · **patellar tendinopathy**

**Drugs** — **corticosteroid injection** · chronic steroids · **anabolic steroids**

**Pathobiology** — end-stage **chronic tendon degeneration** → eccentric load on a diseased tendon

### Anatomy & biomechanics

**Extensor mechanism** — quadriceps tendon → patella → **patellar tendon** → tibial tubercle

**Blood supply** — **infrapatellar fat pad** + retinacular (**medial + lateral inferior geniculate arteries**)

**Forces** — **3× BW** ascending stairs · **17× BW** to rupture a normal tendon

**After TKA** — approach disrupts extensor blood supply → poorer healing · acute → primary repair · chronic → **allograft reconstruction**

## 2 Mechanism

**Mechanism** — **tensile overload**: **sudden quadriceps contraction with knee flexed** (jumping, missing a step) · eccentric load (basketball, football, soccer)

**Flexion angle** — **greatest tendon force at > 60° flexion** · PT : QT force ratio **> 1 at < 45°**, **< 1 at > 45°** (distal-pole contact gives quads advantage at low flexion)

| Pattern | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Avulsion ± bone from inferior pole of patella** | **Most common** · strain at tendon-bone interface **3–4× midsubstance** |
| Midsubstance | Less common · reconstruction / augmentation if poor tissue |
| Distal avulsion from tibial tubercle | Least common |

**Associated** — tibial tubercle avulsion, patella fracture, **TKA extensor mechanism rupture**

## 3 Presentation

**History** — **jump / sudden quads contraction**, **"pop"** · infrapatellar pain, **immediate swelling**, can't weight-bear · may mimic ACL (buckling, giving way)

**Inspection** — **high-riding patella**, **large hemarthrosis + ecchymosis**, **palpable gap below inferior pole**

**Straight leg raise** — **cannot actively SLR** or hold passively extended knee · retinaculum intact → active extension with **a few degrees of lag**

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Study | Finding / use |
|---|---|
| **X-ray — AP + lateral at 30° flexion** (± merchant) | **Patella alta** in complete rupture |
| **MRI** | **Most sensitive** · partial vs complete · site, degeneration, associated injury · **not needed** for complete tear |
| Ultrasound | Detects + localises · **operator-dependent** |

![Patella alta after patellar tendon rupture](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob3024-patella-alta-lateral-xray.jpg)

### Diagnostic criteria — patella alta indices

| Index | Calculation | Normal | **Rupture (alta)** | Baja |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Insall-Salvati** | **Tendon length (Y) ÷ patellar length (X)** | **0.8–1.2** (\~1) | **> 1.2** (e.g. 1.3) | **< 0.8** → quadriceps rupture |
| **Blackburne-Peel** | Plateau line → lower articular margin (Y) ÷ articular length (X) | **0.5–1.0** | **> 1.0** (e.g. **Y/X 1.1**) | < 0.5 |
| **Caton-Deschamps** | Anterior tibial plateau → lower articular margin (Y) ÷ articular length (X) | **0.6–1.3** | **> 1.3** (Orthobullets) · **> 1.2** (OB) | **< 0.6** |

![Blackburne-Peel ratio (Y / X)](../../MCQ/attachments/q217470-b-blackbourne-peel.jpg)

**Ratio traps** — **X/Y** inverted ratios are meaningless · IS 0.95 and CD 1.1 = **normal**

**Most reliable index** — **Insall-Salvati** (highest intra- + inter-observer reliability on X-ray, CT, MRI; Verhulst) · **Blackburne-Peel** most useful — articular-surface based (Seil) — read the options

**Diagnosis** — complete tear = **exam + X-ray** · partial tear may need MRI

### Classification

| Type | Features | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **Incomplete** | **Extensor mechanism intact** | Nonop possible |
| **Complete** | **Patella alta + palpable defect** | **Surgical repair** |

## 5 Management

```
Patellar tendon injury
├─ Partial, extensor mechanism intact → brace locked in extension 6 wk, weight bearing
├─ Complete, acute → primary repair within 2 wk
│      (suture anchors: less gap formation)
└─ Chronic > 6–8 wk / degenerate / poor tissue → tendon reconstruction
```

### Operative criteria

| Indication | Procedure |
|---|---|
| **Complete rupture**, tendon can be approximated | **Primary repair** (end-to-end, transosseous, **suture anchors**) |
| Subacute **> 2 wk** | Repair + **quadplasty / scar release** |
| **Chronic > 6–8 wk**, severely disrupted / degenerate tendon | **Reconstruction** |
| Midsubstance tear, poor tissue | Reconstruction / **graft augmentation** |
| Revision or chronic | **Achilles allograft** |

### Treatment – primary repair (suture anchors vs transosseous)

**Timing** — **repair within 2 weeks** → prevents **extensor mechanism contracture** · **timing = most important prognostic factor**

**End-to-end** — needs a cuff of tissue on both sides · often **not feasible** (rupture at inferior pole, no proximal stump) · nonabsorbable locking sutures

**Transosseous** — **#5 nonabsorbable** sutures through **2 trans-patellar tunnels**, tied over top · ± **cerclage wire / tape** patella → tubercle

**Suture anchors** — **≥ 2 anchors** · **less gap formation** (0.9–4.1 mm vs 2.9–10.3 mm transpatellar) · **↑ pull-out strength / ultimate load to failure** (Orthobullets, OB) · load to failure **not different**, gap still less (Danaher, OB) — read the options

**Anchor vs transosseous — no difference** — **re-operation rate**, infection, PROMs, **return to sport**, release from care

**Re-rupture** — **\~8% transosseous vs 1–2% anchors** (Orthobullets) · **7.5% vs 0%** (O'Dowd, OB)

**Best for gap formation (footballer)** — **suture anchor repair**\
✗ Wrong options: end-to-end · rehab then autograft · Achilles allograft · transosseous

**Anchor construct vs bone tunnels** — **increased pull-out strength**\
✗ Wrong options: ↓ re-operation · ↓ infection · better PROMs · quicker RTS

**High load to failure + less gap** — **primary repair with suture anchors**\
✗ Wrong options: 2 or 3 transosseous tunnels · end-to-end · gracilis reconstruction

**24-yr-old, patella alta after rebound** — **primary surgical repair**\
✗ Wrong options: PT then BPTB reconstruction · brace at 30° · medialising TTO + lateral release · arthroscopy

**40-yr-old, alta on X-ray** — **open surgical repair**\
✗ Wrong options: MRI first · ice + observation · PT · arthroscopic repair

**CKD + T2DM, 1 wk after fall** — **primary repair of injured structure**\
✗ Wrong options: + AMZ tubercle transfer (no instability) · immobilisation (partial tears only) · hamstring reconstruction first · CTA (pulses present)

**Partial tear, extensor intact** — **hinged brace locked in extension 6 wk**, weight bearing · progressive **active flexion / passive extension**

### Tendon reconstruction

**Grafts** — ipsilateral **semitendinosus / gracilis**, **central quadriceps tendon-patellar bone** autograft, contralateral **BPTB** autograft or allograft, Achilles allograft

**Technique** — tendons through transosseous holes in patella + tibial tubercle → **circle graft** · retracted patella → quadplasty, scar release

### Postoperative rehabilitation

**Brace** — **locked in extension, immediate weight bearing**, **6 wk** · brace-free by 6 wk

**Early motion** — from **7–10 days** · **passive extension + active flexion**\
✗ Wrong options: any active extension

**ROM exercises** — **heel slides** (active closed-chain flexion) · **prone open-chain flexion** · both with passive extension

**Active extension** — **none for at least 4–6 wk** (OB) · permitted at **3 wk** (Matava, OB) — read the options

**Later** — open-chain strengthening (**leg extensions**) + weight-bearing resistance (**squats, lunges, leg press**)

**Goals** — **full extension + 120° flexion by 6 wk** · **full RTS at 6 months** (100% return at 6 months with cerclage repair, OB)

**Chains** — **closed** = distal segment fixed (squat, leg press, pull-up) · **open** = distal free (leg extension, hamstring curl) · open → more shear · open terminal knee extension = most anterior shear

## 6 Complications

| Complication | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Knee stiffness** | **Most common** · risk: delayed surgery, delayed ROM · **MUA** if flexion < 120° at 6–12 wk · **lysis of adhesions** if < 120° after > 12 wk |
| **Quadriceps atrophy** | **2nd most common** · doesn't compromise strength return · PT |
| **Re-tear** | **\~8% transosseous / 1–2% anchors** · acute + good tissue → repair · chronic / poor tissue → reconstruction |
| Infection | **\~2%** |
| Delayed repair | Functioning extensor but **persistent quads atrophy + ↓ flexion** |

## X vs Y

| | **Patellar tendon rupture** | **Quadriceps tendon rupture** |
|---|---|---|
| Frequency | Less common | **More common (2:1)** |
| Age | **< 40** | **> 40** |
| Patellar height | **Alta (IS > 1.2)** | **Baja (IS < 0.8)** |
| Common site | **Inferior pole of patella** | — |

## How it's asked

**"Buckling, patella alta on X-ray + MRI?"** → **patellar tendon rupture**

**"Preoperative finding before tendon repair?"** → **Insall-Salvati ratio of 1.3**

**"Value suggesting rupture (Figures A–C)?"** → **Y/X 1.1 in Blackburne-Peel**

**"24-yr-old, alta after landing — management?"** → **primary surgical repair**

**"40-yr-old, pop, alta?"** → **open surgical repair**

**"CKD + diabetes, can't extend, 1 wk?"** → **primary repair of injured structure**

**"Best repair for gap formation?"** → **suture anchor repair**

**"Suture anchors vs bone tunnels?"** → **increased pull-out strength**

**"High load to failure, less gap?"** → **primary repair with suture anchors**

**"Rehab that limits stress on repair?"** → **active flexion, passive extension**

**"Immediate post-op active ROM exercise?"** → **heel slides**
