## 1 Basics

### Contraction types – isokinetic, isotonic, isometric & eccentric

| Type | Definition | Example | Fact asked |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Isotonic** | **Constant muscle tension (force)** as length changes · improves motor performance | Free-weight biceps curls · leg extensions + curls | **Concentric + eccentric are isotonic** · force–velocity (force constant) |
| **Isometric** | **Constant length**, tension ∝ external load · causes hypertrophy (Orthobullets) | Push against an immovable object · plantarflex against a wall 15 s | **Length–tension relationship** |
| **Concentric** | Muscle **shortens**, tension ∝ load | Curl with elbow flexing · slow single-leg heel rise | — |
| **Eccentric** | Muscle **lengthens** under constant force | Curl with elbow extending · "negative" of a lift | **Most efficient strengthening** · **highest muscle forces** |
| **Isokinetic** | **Constant velocity, variable resistance** (✗ Wrong options: constant effort at variable speed) | **Cybex / dynamometer** · bike at constant pedal velocity, varied resistance | Objective strength testing in rehab · force–velocity (velocity constant) |
| **Plyometric** | **Rapid eccentric → concentric** (stretch-shortening) | **Box jumps**, bounding, hopping | Power sports · late rehab |
| Open chain | Distal end **moves freely** | Seated leg extensions + curls | — |
| Closed chain | Distal end **fixed** | Squat with planted foot, leg press | — |

**Eccentric = injury contraction** — **muscle strain at myotendinous junction** · **DOMS** · **distal biceps rupture** (forced eccentric load, + hook test)\
✗ Wrong options: isometric · concentric · isokinetic · plyometric

**Achilles tendinitis rehab after rest + CAM boot** — **eccentric strengthening**: rise on both legs on a 1-inch board, **lower slowly on the injured leg only** + gastrocnemius-soleus stretching · avoid steroid injection\
✗ Wrong options: box jumps (plyometric) · wall push (isometric) · slow single-leg rise (concentric) · delay rehab

**Overstretched muscle** — sarcomeres generate little active tension → **< 30% maximal force**

**Rule of 10 (TO, isometrics)** — **10 s contraction × 10 reps × 10 times/day** · isometrics **contraindicated in hypertensive / cardiac patients** (BP spikes) · **kinetic-chain classification = Steindler 1955** · early rehab closed chain (safe) → late open chain (targeted)

**Mobility vs flexibility (TO)** — **mobility** = available ROM, set by **capsuloligamentous** structures → ROM / mobilisation · **flexibility** = beyond ROM, **musculotendinous** stretchability → static / PNF stretching · **ballistic stretching obsolete** (tears) · **PNF most effective** for flexibility

**Physical activity vs exercise (TO)** — activity = any movement spending energy · **exercise = planned, structured, repetitive** · proprioception = joint position sense; **kinesia** = sense of movement · hydrotherapy: buoyancy + hydrostatic pressure → early PWB ROM · biofeedback with isokinetic machines

**Isokinetic in tennis (TO)** — **slow + fast speed isokinetic** → maximal resistance at every point of ROM, uniform strength, concentric + eccentric · add **core strengthening (lower back + hip)** → kinetic-chain power transfer\
✗ Wrong options: ballistic · plyometric (power, added once baseline strength exists) · fartlek (trains energy systems, not muscle)

### Open vs closed kinetic chain exercise

| Characteristic | **Closed chain** | **Open chain** |
|---|---|---|
| Distal segment | **Fixed** against considerable resistance | **Free** in space |
| Stress pattern (TO) | **Linear** (axial loading) | **Rotary** |
| Joint axes (TO) | Multiple | **One primary** |
| Joint segments (TO) | **Both move simultaneously** | One stationary, other mobile |
| Moving joints (TO) | **Multiple** | Isolated joint |
| Planes (TO) | Multiple (triplanar) | One |
| Muscles (TO) | **Significant co-contraction** → predictable movement | **Isolated** group, minimal co-contraction |
| Pattern (TO) | Functional | Often non-functional |
| Loads (OB) | **Compressive** → ↓ shear, translation, distraction | ↑ shear |
| Use (OB) | **Early rehab**; more often **lower limb** | Later; maximal single-muscle strengthening |
| Examples | Squat, leg press, bicycle, push-up | **Seated leg extension**, hamstring curl, waving a hand, swing-phase foot |

**Closed-chain limits (OB)** — individual muscles **not maximally strengthened**, not all joint motion normalised · joint compression **↑**\
✗ Wrong options: distal end free · mainly upper limb · ↓ compression · single joint

**After ACL reconstruction** — **closed chain** (quads + hamstrings co-contract, protects graft) · **open-chain leg extension → strong anterior tibial pull → graft stress** · one EMG study: **more hamstring co-activation with isokinetic** than closed chain

**Push-up on a weight plate (TO)** — **closed chain dynamic** (distal fixed + up-down movement; instability adds core work) · static push-up = plank (isometric)

| | **Type I** | **Type IIA** | **Type IIB** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Name | **Slow oxidative** ("slow red ox") | Fast oxidative-glycolytic | **Fast glycolytic** |

### Energy systems & nutrition (ATP-CP, glycogen, fatty acids)

| | **ATP-CP (alactic)** | **Glycolytic (lactic acid)** | **Aerobic** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fuel | **ATP + creatine phosphate** | Muscle glycogen → lactate | **Glycogen + fatty acids** (Krebs, oxidative phosphorylation) |
| Duration | **< 10 s** high intensity | **2–3 min** (Orthobullets) · **1–4 min** glycogen + lactic acid (OB) | Endurance · **> 4 min** (OB) · predominates from **\~60 s** (TO) |
| Fibre | Type II | Type II | **Type I** |
| O2 | Not needed | Not needed | Required |
| ATP yield | Immediate, tiny stores | **Low**; lactic acidosis after several min | **High** |

**Energy continuum at maximal effort (TO)** — **20 s: PCr 40% · glycolysis 50% · aerobic 10%** · **40 s: 5% · 80% · 15%**

**Marathon** — **muscle glycogen early (< 90 min)** → **free fatty acids after \~90 min** (final hour = FFA) · liver glycogen early, secondary · lactate = intense exercise in low glucose\
✗ Wrong options: creatine phosphate · muscle / liver glycogen late

### Training adaptations – capillary density, fibre types, protein synthesis, periodisation

| | **Endurance (aerobic)** | **Strength (resistance)** |
|---|---|---|
| **Key adaptation** | **↑ capillary density (most important)** | **↑ cross-sectional area (hypertrophy)**; hyperplasia unlikely |
| Mitochondria | ↑ size, number, density · ↑ respiratory capacity → ↑ VO2max | ↑ (Orthobullets weight training) · not aerobic only (OB) |
| Enzymes | ↑ Krebs, fatty acid, respiratory chain · **no change in glycolytic enzymes** | — |
| Fuel | Fatty acids > glycogen over time | Immediate sources |
| Fibres | ↑ oxidative capacity I, IIA, IIB | ↑ IIA, ↓ IIB (OB) |
| Early gains | — | **↑ motor unit recruitment** ± synchronisation; **hypertrophy after 4–6 wk** |
| Force | — | Max force **∝ physiologic CSA** |
| Load | Dynamic exercise ↑ cardiac output via **↑ stroke volume** | High load, low reps |

**Weight training (Orthobullets)** — ↑ CSA, strength, mitochondria, capillary density, thickened connective tissue · **adult gains = hypertrophy**

**Marathoner vs powerlifter (OB)** — **marathoner: ↑ capillary density**\
✗ Wrong options: ↑ CSA in marathoner · ↑ mitochondria / fat use / type I in powerlifter

**Myofibrillar protein synthesis (OB)** — **resistance exercise above 60% 1-repetition maximum (1-RM)** (anabolic ceiling; sigmoidal dose response)\
✗ Wrong options: aerobic (↑ oxidative capacity only) · 30% 1-RM high reps · GH / testosterone (no acute MPS effect; driven by autocrine / paracrine + mechanotransduction)

**Periodisation** — **planned variation in intensity + duration over a predefined time** (Orthobullets, OB) · **planned manipulation of training variables (load, sets, reps) to maximise adaptation** + prevent overtraining (TO)\
✗ Wrong options: rest interval · gradual increase / decline

**Cycles (OB)** — **macro = training year** · **meso = 3–6 months** · **micro = 3–6 weeks** · goal = peak performance via progressive overload

**Models (TO)** — **linear** (one attribute, e.g. 4 wk strength) vs **non-linear** (strength + power + endurance together)

| Training term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Progressive overload** (OB) | Stress above what the body is used to → adaptation |
| **Stacking** (OB) | Multiple performance-enhancing drugs / supplements for synergy |
| Static progressive (OB) | Splinting for contracted joints, not exercise |
| **Interval** (TO) | Near-maximal work (\~**90% max HR**) alternating with recovery; **work : rest \~1 : 1** |
| **Fartlek** (TO) | Continuous steady pace + sprints → aerobic + anaerobic; distance athletes; up to 45 min |
| **Circuit** (TO) | ≥ **6 stations** in sequence |

| State (TO) | Performance | Recovery |
|---|---|---|
| **Supercompensation** | **Balanced training** → dip → rebound **above baseline** | — |
| Functional overreaching | Temporary decline → supercompensation | Short |
| Non-functional overreaching | Short-term decrement ± maladaptation | **Days–weeks** |
| **Overtraining syndrome** | **Long-term** decrement (unbalanced training / non-training stress / rest) | **Weeks–months** |

**Continuum recovery (TO, Meeusen 2006)** — **FOR: days to \~2 weeks** (supercompensation; e.g. pre-season camp) · **NFOR: weeks–months**, full recovery with rest · **OTS: months or longer**, may end a career · no improvement after **2 weeks of rest** → think OTS

**OTS hallmark (TO)** — **inability to sustain intense exercise + ↓ sport-specific performance despite maintained / ↑ load** ("unexplained underperformance syndrome") · commonest in **high-volume endurance sports** (swimming, triathlon, cycling, rowing)

| OTS type (TO) | Sport | Features |
|---|---|---|
| **Parasympathetic** | **Aerobic / endurance** | Fatigue, depression, **bradycardia**, ↑ HRV, low catecholamines |
| **Sympathetic** | **Anaerobic** (sprint, jumps, throws) | Restlessness, irritability, insomnia, **tachycardia, hypertension** |

**OTS diagnosis (TO)** — **clinical diagnosis of exclusion** · **no diagnostic biomarker** (HRV, CK, glutamine, cortisol only supportive) · needs: ↓ performance for weeks–months **despite rest** + **mood disturbance** + other causes excluded (anaemia, thyroid, infection, asthma, diabetes) · cannot diagnose without a rest period · biggest risk = **load–recovery imbalance**, monotonous training

| OTS hypothesis (TO) | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| Glycogen | Depletion → fatigue, catabolic state, ↓ IGF-1 |
| **Central fatigue** | Low glycogen → ↑ FFA displace **tryptophan** from albumin → competes with **BCAA** → ↑ brain **5-HT** |
| **Glutamine** | Falls after > 2 h / repeated exercise → **immunosuppression, infections** |
| Oxidative stress | ROS → ↓ citrate synthase, mitochondrial dysfunction |
| HPA axis | **↓ testosterone : cortisol ratio** · blunted ACTH, GH, LH, FSH |
| Cytokine | IL-1β, IL-6, TNF systemic inflammation |

**OTS management (TO)** — no proven drug · **rest ≥ 2 weeks** → reassess → very low volume (5–10 min/day), **↑ volume before intensity** · training diary (RPE Borg 6–20, resting HR, sleep, mood) · prevention: periodisation, tapering, **> 6 h between bouts**, **≥ 1 rest day / week** · **soreness > 72 h = DOMS, not OTS**

### Strength training in children

**Safety** — age-appropriate, supervised **weight training + plyometrics are safe** · no ↑ injury, **no growth-plate damage**, no ↑ OCD

**Mechanism** — gains from **↑ neuromuscular activation + coordination** / efficiency of muscle action ("muscle memory") · **not hypertrophy** (insufficient testosterone) · gains **reversible** if stopped

### Biomechanics of racing & ball sports (drag, force-time, force platform, cycling posture)

| Drag (TO) | Cause | Reduce by |
|---|---|---|
| **Surface** (friction / skin) | Fluid friction at the boundary layer | Smooth suits, shaving, waxing skis / surfboards |
| **Pressure** (form) | High pressure in front, low-pressure wake behind | **↓ frontal area + streamlining**, **drafting** |
| **Wave** | Surface waves (open-water swimming) | Lane markers + gutters in pools |

**Flow (TO)** — laminar at low velocity · **turbulent boundary layer → later separation → ↓ pressure drag** · "drag crisis" · **dimples / scuffing** → low drag at lower velocity

**Not useful to ↓ drag (TO)** — **increasing frontal area**\
✗ Wrong options: streamlining · drafting · smooth suits

**Drafting (TO)** — **cycling**, car racing (also swimming) · leader uses more energy · running **1 m behind → \~7% ↓ metabolic cost**\
✗ Wrong options: shooting · baseball · ice hockey

**Force–time principle (TO)** — Newton's 2nd law → **impulse–momentum** · longer force application → safer deceleration · **catch with arm / glove extended (A)** · land with legs extended to flex through range\
✗ Wrong options: catching close to body (B)

**Cycling posture (TO)** — **anterior pelvic tilt** flattens lumbar spine → **↓ frontal area** (wind = main retarding force) · **low stack-height cleat** (pedal spindle → cleat top) · 1st MT base over pedal axis · narrow hips → cleats outside, wide hips → inside · toe-out → rotate cleat in

**Force platform (TO)** — measures **ground reaction force** (gait biomechanics)\
✗ Wrong options: ball spin · jump force · shoe friction

## 2 Mechanism

### Muscle injury healing – TGF-β & early mobilisation

| Injury | Key facts |
|---|---|
| **Soreness (DOMS)** | **Oedema + inflammation in connective tissue** → ↑ intramuscular pressure · **type IIB** · **eccentric** · starts at MTJ, spreads · cytoskeletal disruption · **peaks 24–72 h** (Orthobullets) |
| **Strain** | **Myotendinous junction**, **eccentric** contraction · inflammation → fibrosis |
| **Contusion** | Blunt → haematoma · ECM within 2 days, peaks 5–21 days · **myositis ossificans most apparent at 4 wk** |
| **Laceration** | Near MTJ, abnormal contour · **dense scar via myofibroblasts ← TGF-β** · regeneration + reinnervation unpredictable, incomplete |

**Scar cytokine** — **TGF-β** (myofibroblast differentiation + proliferation) · **regeneration from 7–10 days ← IGF-1, FGF, NGF**\
✗ Wrong options: VEGF

**Immobilisation after muscle injury** — **≤ 3–5 days**, then progressive strengthening + stretching · **immediate motion → large dense scar**, poor fibre penetration · **2 wk → atrophy limiting outcome**, ↑ fatty infiltration, less scar, less regeneration, disorganised fibres

**Immobilisation (Orthobullets)** — atrophy, ↓ tension, **↑ fatigability**, fatty infiltration · faster in **single-joint** muscles · **non-linear** (most in first days; myofibril loss) · worse **without tension** → **quadriceps > hamstrings** when knee held in extension

**EILO** — mistaken for exercise-induced bronchoconstriction · **females** · dyspnoea + wheeze · includes exercise-induced laryngomalacia + vocal cord dysfunction · differentials: EIB, hyperventilation, cardiac

| Test | **EIB (exercise asthma)** | **EILO** |
|---|---|---|
| Bronchodilator reversibility (β2-agonist) | **Positive** | **Negative** |
| Bronchoprovocation (methacholine, mannitol, EVH) | **Positive** | **Negative** |
| **Continuous laryngoscopy during exercise (CLE)** | — | **Positive — gold standard** |

**EILO level** — **supraglottic** (arytenoids, closure above glottis) vs **glottic** (**vocal cord closure**) · optimal treatment under study

**Persistent haematuria differential** — **sickle cell trait → papillary necrosis**, UTI / STI (pyuria, dysuria), kidney contusion (flank blow), stone (flank pain)\
✗ Wrong options: pre-workout (only ↑ susceptibility) · mononucleosis · rhabdomyolysis (myoglobin, no RBCs)

### Cardiorespiratory fitness testing (VO2 max, ventilatory threshold, Bruce protocol) (TO)

**VO2 max** — max O2 taken in + used at maximal exercise · **best indicator of cardiovascular fitness** · units **ml/kg/min** · rises with fat loss\
✗ Wrong options: l/kg/min · ml/g/min · ml/kg/hr

**Measurement** — direct (treadmill, ergometer, step test; accurate, costly) vs indirect (Astrand charts, HR, RPE)

| Threshold | Definition |
|---|---|
| Aerobic threshold (Orthobullets) | Effort where anaerobic pathways become significant |
| **Anaerobic (lactate) threshold** (Orthobullets) | **Lactate production > removal** |
| Lactate threshold (TO) | Substantial ↑ blood lactate · **predicts endurance better than VO2max** |
| **Ventilatory threshold** (TO) | **Ventilation rises disproportionately to O2 uptake** · **55–75%** of max aerobic capacity · H+ buffered by HCO3− → extra CO2 · synonyms: lactate threshold, OBLA, HR deflection point, maximal lactate steady state |
| Low intensity 30–50% (TO) | Ventilation coupled to O2 (depth, diaphragm) · ageing blunts maximal ventilation |

**Lactate balance (TO)** — blood lactate = **appearance vs disappearance**, not production alone · produced **even at rest** (\~20 mmol/kg/day) · **LDH: pyruvate + NADH → lactate + NAD⁺** (regenerates NAD⁺ so glycolysis continues) · \~99% dissociated → lactate + H⁺ · lactate = fuel, gluconeogenic precursor, signal · **fatigue multifactorial** (H⁺, Pi, ↓ PCr), not lactate alone

**Lactate shuttle (TO)** — **MCT4 exports** lactate from glycolytic muscle · **MCT1 imports** into oxidative muscle, **heart, brain** (→ pyruvate → TCA) and liver / kidney (**Cori cycle**) · proton co-transport 1:1 · active recovery speeds clearance

| Lactate zone (TO) | Fibres | Blood lactate |
|---|---|---|
| Below LT1 | **Type I**; fat; production = clearance | **0.5–2 mmol/L** |
| **LT1 = aerobic threshold** | **Type IIa** join | First systematic rise (\~2–2.5) · = **VT1** |
| LT1–LT2 | More IIa | Rises, stabilises; sustainable \~30–45 min |
| **LT2 = anaerobic threshold / OBLA** | **Type IIx** | Exponential rise, **\~4 mmol/L** · = **VT2 (respiratory compensation point)** |

| | **OBLA** | **MLSS** |
|---|---|---|
| Definition | Workload at **fixed 4 mmol/L** | **Highest constant workload** with lactate steady |
| Found by | Incremental test | Several constant-load tests |
| Nature | Same for everyone ("4 mmol trap") | Individual, true steady state |
| Training | Definition unchanged | **Workload ↑** (↑ mitochondria + MCT1 uptake, not ↓ production) |

![Ventilation and blood lactate vs VO2: LT, VT, OBLA, respiratory compensation](../../TO notes/attachments/TOn-notes-energy-systems-to-exercise-intensity-lactate-threshold-p04-f010.jpg)

**Training effect (TO)** — lactate curve **shifts right** (lower lactate at same workload) · trained reach OBLA at higher % VO2max · graph stem "lactate stable → rises → steep" = **progressive fibre recruitment + glycolytic flux, set by production / transport / removal**\
✗ Wrong options: abrupt aerobic → anaerobic switch · lactate only when O2 delivery stops · lactate = O2 debt

**RER (TO)** — \~0.7 rest / fat · 1.0 CHO · **> 1.0 near maximal** (HCO₃⁻ buffering adds CO₂)

| CPET normal (TO) | Value |
|---|---|
| Peak VO2 | **> 84% predicted** |
| Ventilatory AT | **> 40%** of peak VO2 (40–80%) |
| Max HR | > 90% age-predicted · HR reserve < 15 |
| BP | < 220/90 |

| VE/VCO2 at AT | **< 34** |

**Field tests (TO)** — VO2max: CPET, **1.5-mile Cooper test**, **Yo-Yo intermittent recovery** · lactate from earlobe / fingertip · training zones from LT2: Z1 < 55% (< 2 mmol) → Z2 55–75 → Z3 75–90 → **Z4 threshold 90–105%** → Z5 VO2max 105–120% (> 8 mmol)

| Test (TO) | Fact |
|---|---|

### Body composition – bulking/cutting & bioimpedance (TO)

**Body mass analyser** — **electrical impedance measurement**\
✗ Wrong options: body fat analysis · dry mass · electron spectroscopy

**BIA** — weak alternating current; Ohm's law R = V/I · current passes faster through **hydrated fat-free tissue** · **more muscle → more water → ↓ impedance** · phase angle read at 50 kHz

**BIA current** — **800 µA at 50 kHz** (answer key; explanation text writes "800 mA") · source electrodes dorsal foot + wrist, detectors at wrist styloids + between malleoli · Siri equation · 5 mm girth or 1.5 cm height error → up to 2% change

**Bulking vs cutting** — never simultaneous · bulking = high CHO + protein · cutting = calorie restriction preserving muscle · **ratio 3 : 1**, bulking 8–12 wk → **gain 12 wk before, cut 4 wk after competition**

### Therapeutic modalities – iontophoresis & aquatic exercise

**Goals** — ↓ inflammation, ↑ local blood flow, ↑ tissue compliance · cryo / heat, massage, ultrasound, electrical stimulation, iontophoresis

**Iontophoresis** — **direct (galvanic) current drives charged medication through skin** · steroids, LA, salicylates, NSAIDs · tendinitis · deep delivery evidence conflicting · shoulder tendinitis **< 45 yr** responds to steroid iontophoresis\
✗ Wrong options: phonophoresis = ultrasound · TENS · EMG · laser · magnetic fields

**Contraindications** — **cardiac pacemaker** (current-sensitive), drug allergy

| Iontophoresis indication | Drug |

### Exertional rhabdomyolysis (TO)

**Warning signs** — swelling + soreness beyond expected · screen with **urinalysis**: scanty, **dark brown "Coca-Cola"** urine, protein + blood · **definitive = CPK**

**Treatment** — **fluids + maintain urine output** (renal protection) · fasciotomy only for severe compartment syndrome

**Biceps swollen after workout, normal urine, CPK 8500, transaminases 1.5–2×** — **admit to ward for IV hydration**\
✗ Wrong options: muscle biopsy · oral hydration + recheck 12–24 h · ICU fasciotomy

### High-altitude illness (TO)

**HAI** — acute mountain sickness, **HACE**, **HAPE**

**HAPE** — non-cardiogenic oedema; **exaggerated hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction**, capillary "stress failure" · **2–5 days** after arrival · dry cough, exertional dyspnoea → rest dyspnoea, **pink frothy sputum** (advanced) · cyanosis, tachycardia, tachypnoea, fever ≤ 38.5 °C, rales mid-zones · patchy opacities, often **right middle lobe** · rare after **1 wk acclimatisation**

**First step** — **oxygen** (TO answer) · **immediate descent = definitive** · remote, no care → **descent first priority** · portable hyperbaric chamber · **nifedipine 30 mg SR 12-hourly**\
✗ Wrong options: hypoxia chamber · oral nifedipine first

**Prevention** — nifedipine, tadalafil, dexamethasone, **acetazolamide** 500–1000 mg/day 8–12-hourly or 125 mg BD (start **24 h before ascent**)
