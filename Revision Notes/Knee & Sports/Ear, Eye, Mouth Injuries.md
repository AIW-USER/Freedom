# Ear, Eye, Mouth Injuries

*Knee & Sports · 11 / 61*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Nose** — **nasal bones = most commonly injured facial bone** · nasal fractures **up to 50%** of sports facial fractures · basketball 26.4%, baseball 22.0% · elbow (basketball), ball (baseball), head (soccer)

**Eye** — baseball, basketball, racquetball, boxing, martial arts · **hyphema = most common intraocular sports eye injury (24%)**

**Ear** — **auricular haematoma**: wrestling, boxing, martial arts

**RTP after facial trauma** — \~**3 weeks** (limited evidence) · full face shields

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| **CT** | **Facial fractures** (study of choice) · **emergent CT** for ↓ acuity / field cut → traumatic optic neuropathy, orbital + optic canal fracture, nerve sheath bleed, foreign body, brain injury |
| **Fluorescein + UV light** | **Corneal abrasion** / corneal foreign body only |
| **Otoscopy** | **First step** in auricular haematoma — **TM perforation** |
| Penlight | Septum — **septal haematoma**, CSF leak, cribriform injury |

## 5 Management

### Auricular haematoma

**Mechanism** — blunt **shear separates perichondrium from cartilage** → blood collects

**First step in ED** — **otoscopic evaluation** (TM rupture; rule out skull fracture)\
✗ Wrong options: compressive dressing + PCP referral · formal surgical decompression · needle decompression **before** otoscopy · head MRI

**Treatment** — **aspiration (urgent needle decompression)** + **wrapping (compressive dressing)** to prevent re-accumulation · **formal surgical evacuation → recurrent or delayed** cases · prevent with **headgear**

**Untreated** — **cauliflower ear** (fibrous / abnormal cartilage), perichondritis, infection, necrosis

### Nasal septal haematoma

**What** — sub-perichondrial / subperiosteal septal haematoma (submucosal vessel rupture) · uni- or bilateral (bilateral → severe trauma) · **any age**\
✗ Wrong options: mostly children

**Commonest symptom** — **nasal congestion / obstruction (\~95%)** · exam: **purple-red bulging septum**\
✗ Wrong options: epistaxis

**Treatment** — **urgent decompression (I&D) under local anaesthesia** + anterior packing\
✗ Wrong options: phenylephrine packing (that's for epistaxis)

**I&D prevents** — **septal cartilage (chondral) avascular necrosis → saddle nose** · **septal abscess within 72 h** (common, not unlikely)\
✗ Wrong options: deviated septum · recurrent epistaxis · olfactory nerve damage · turbinate swelling

### Ocular injury — emergent CT

| Injury | Key finding | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **Traumatic mydriasis** | **Dilated pupil**, **contusion of iris sphincter**, transient | **Bed rest** |
| **Corneal abrasion** | **Fluorescein stain + UV light** | Topical antibiotics + NSAIDs, patch, **non-urgent** ophthalmology |
| **Hyphema** | **Blood in anterior chamber** (haze) — may mean vitreous / retinal injury | Patch + **ER / emergent ophthalmology** |
| **Ruptured globe** | **Sunken** eye, vitreous exudate | Emergent |
| **Orbital wall fracture** | **Extraocular muscle entrapment** | Emergent |
| **Retinal detachment** | **Bright flashes**, stabbing pain, **field cut** | Emergent |
| **Traumatic optic neuropathy** | ↓ acuity, ↓ colour vision, field cut | **Emergent CT** |

**Red-flag symptoms** — vision loss, ↓ acuity, floaters / flashers, diplopia

**Boxer: hyphema + ↓ acuity + ↓ colour vision + field cut** — **emergent CT**\
✗ Wrong options: patch + ophthalmology in 2 days · fluorescein · high-dose steroids before CT · observation

**Traumatic optic neuropathy** — **blunt 78%** / penetrating 22% · sports + MVA · **optic canal fracture on CT = poor prognosis** · treatment controversial (steroids, optic canal decompression, sheath fenestration) · children: only 29–44% improve

### Mandible fracture — airway

**Most serious acute complication** — **airway obstruction**\
✗ Wrong options: blood loss · CVA (carotid injury, rare) · periodontal disease (late) · hearing loss

**Why** — **mandible suspends the tongue anteriorly** → fracture + supine → **tongue falls back** · soft-tissue swelling · worst with **multiple mandible** or combined maxillary-mandibular-nasal fractures

**Airway** — intubation often impossible → **surgical airway**

### Dental avulsion — milk storage & replantation

**Avulsed tooth** — **medical emergency** · survival depends on **time out of socket** + **periodontal ligament** damage

**Handling** — hold by **crown, never root** · **rinse** debris with water / saline · **never brush or scrub**\
✗ Wrong options: toothbrush · hydrogen peroxide

**Replant immediately** — **immediate repositioning / replantation** → bite on towel → dentist / ER\
✗ Wrong options: wait for dentist · emergency root canal

**If not replanted** — keep **moist**: **milk (whole)**, saline, **saliva** / buccal fold, buffered solution kits · **rinse with saline → milk** = usual answer\
✗ Wrong options: ice · water · carbonated drink

**Children** — replanting or carrying in mouth → **aspiration risk**

**Dentist** — as soon as possible, **preferably within 1 hour**

## X vs Y

| | **Auricular haematoma** | **Septal haematoma** |
|---|---|---|
| First step | **Otoscopy** | Penlight inspection |
| Treatment | **Aspiration + compressive wrap** | **Urgent I&D under LA + packing** |
| Complication | **Cauliflower ear** | **Saddle nose**, septal abscess |

## How it's asked

**"Several front teeth knocked out — initial steps?"** → **rinse in saline, place in milk**

**"Emergent management of tooth luxation?"** → **immediate repositioning or replantation**

**"Three teeth out (figure) — next step besides calling dentist?"** → **pour saline on teeth, then place in milk**

**"Unstable jaw, malocclusion — most serious acute complication?"** → **airway obstruction**

**"Hyphema, ↓ acuity + colour vision, field cut?"** → **emergent CT**

**"Purple bulging septum — true?"** → **septal avascular necrosis is a potential complication**

**"I&D of septal haematoma prevents?"** → **chondral necrosis**

**"Auricular haematoma — best next step in ED?"** → **otoscopic evaluation**
