# Plicae

*Knee & Sports · 43 / 61*

## 1 Basics

### Embryology & pathology

**Origin (TO)** — embryonic knee divided by **synovial folds** → absorbed by **3–4 months** → single cavity · **partial resorption** → persistent septations = **synovial plicae** (visible at arthroscopy)

**Mediopatellar plica (TO)** — not a septum remnant (Ogata & Uhthoff) · linked to **lateralised patella** = remnant of mesenchyme that was larger medially

**Prevalence** — medial patellar plica in **5–55%** of people; **few symptomatic** (OB) · mere presence ≠ symptoms (TO)

**Plica syndrome** — painful impairment from **thickened, inflamed synovial fold (usually medial)** · **50%** give history of **blunt anterior knee trauma** · repetitive sport, direct trauma, other knee pathology

**Inflamed plica (TO)** — **fibrotic, thickened, inelastic** → snaps over **femoral condyle** or under **patella** → pain, **synovitis of condylar margins**, **chondromalacia patellae** / cartilage wear

### Plica anatomy, types & symptomatic plica

| Plica | Course | Key fact |
|---|---|---|
| **Infrapatellar = ligamentum mucosum** | **Intercondylar notch → infrapatellar (Hoffa) fat pad**; narrow femoral origin (anterior notch), widens, **anterior to ACL** | **Most common / most frequently seen at arthroscopy**; **rarely symptomatic** |
| **Suprapatellar** | **Anterior femoral metaphysis → medial joint wall** (TO) · → posterior quadriceps tendon above patella (TO) · medial → lateral wall of suprapatellar space (Orthobullets); between joint + suprapatellar bursa | **Most persistent**; impinges **superomedial trochlea in flexion**; usually asymptomatic |
| **Medial (mediopatellar, Lino band, shelf)** | **Medial joint wall → synovium over infrapatellar fat pad** (from suprapatellar origin) | **Most common cause of plica syndrome**; **most significant / most symptomatic**; abrades **MFC** |
| **Lateral** | Lateral wall, **lateral patellar facet → infrapatellar region**; thin, 1–2 cm lateral to patella | **Least common**; existence controversial; asymptomatic |

**Frequency order (TO)** — **infrapatellar > suprapatellar > mediopatellar** · Orthobullets: ligamentum mucosum most common

**Conflict** — **infrapatellar = most common plica** (TO, Orthobullets) vs **medial = most common type + most symptomatic** (TO FNB 2023) — read the options; **most symptomatic = medial** in all sources

**Multiple plicae** — not uncommon in one patient

## 3 Presentation

**Symptoms** — **anterior / anteromedial knee pain**, **snapping, popping, clicking** with repetitive flexion–extension, **buckling**, **pain on sitting**, crepitus, swelling · worse with **poor quadriceps tone / muscle imbalance**

**Cyclist (TO)** — tenderness on **undersurface of medial patellar facet**; **deep flexion + full extension painful**

**Exam** — **medial parapatellar tenderness** · **painful palpable cord** rolled + popped under the finger · band painful with flexion–extension **with tibia internally rotated** · **McMurray negative**

**Provocative test** — knee held in **full extension**, examiner flexes against resistance, then **pushes patella medially** palpating medial border → **pain ± click = positive**

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Modality | Finding |
|---|---|
| **Clinical** | **Diagnosis is primarily clinical** |
| **MRI** | Detects plica but **low sensitivity**, non-specific · order only **after failed conservative** treatment |
| MRI symptomatic plica (TO) | **Thick ± synovitis** (normal plica thin) |
| MRI Sakakibara C (TO) | Axial FS PD **hypointense band covering anterior MFC** |
| MRI suprapatellar (TO) | **Sagittal**: band-like low-signal structure **posterior to patella** |
| **Arthroscopy** | **Gold standard for diagnosis + treatment**; arrow on PF view from inferolateral portal = **plica** (✗ Wrong options: loose body · meniscus · retinaculum · OCD) |
| Arthroscopy, suprapatellar (TO) | **Almost never diagnosed** — complete plica suggested only by a **short suprapatellar pouch** (or needle enters bursa) |

![MRI — thickened medial plica (Sakakibara type C) covering the anterior MFC](../../MCQ/attachments/TO17119465-1.png)

### Sakakibara classification of medial plica

| Type (TO) | Arthroscopic appearance | Symptoms |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Cord-like elevation** in synovial wall | Unlikely |
| **B** | **Shelf-like**, does **not** cover anterior MFC | Unlikely |
| **C** | **Large shelf covering anterior surface of MFC** | **Trapped between MFC + patella** → thick, hard → **cartilage damage** |
| **D** | **Fenestrated** (central defect) | Trapped → cartilage damage |

**Types C + D (TO)** — impinge **medial patellar facet cartilage in flexion** + **MFC cartilage in extension** → **arthroscopic resection = treatment of choice**

**Untreated medial plica syndrome (TO)** — can cause **Outerbridge grade IV** chondral lesions (subchondral bone exposed)

### Misfiled TO items (Brodie abscess, carpal tunnel tests)

| Brodie abscess (TO) | Fact |
|---|---|
| Site / age | **Proximal tibia**, children + young adults |
| Course | **Indolent**; mild–moderate local tenderness; intermittent pain **worse at night**, > 2 wk; low-grade fever |
| Labs | WBC, ESR, CRP **normal or mildly ↑** |
| X-ray | **Cavity at metaphyseal–epiphyseal junction + rim of reactive new bone** |
| Diagnosis / organism | **Bone biopsy** confirms · **S. aureus** commonest |
| **Treatment** | **Curettage + bone grafting under an anti-staphylococcal antibiotic cover** (✗ Wrong options: curettage alone · observation · needle aspiration) |

| Carpal tunnel syndrome (TO) | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Not a CTS test** | **Murphy** (✗ Wrong options: Phalen · Durkan · Semmes-Weinstein) |
| Pathology | Carpal tunnel pressure **> 30 mmHg** |
| Commonest causes | **Idiopathic > hypothyroidism > pregnancy** |
| Risk factors | Female, obesity, pregnancy, hypothyroid, RA, trauma (distal radius fracture / malunion, carpal dislocation), repetitive motion, acromegaly, age, menopause, CRF, space-occupying lesion, OCP, CHF, diabetes, smoking, alcohol, mucopolysaccharidosis (children) |
| Symptoms | Pain, numbness, paraesthesia **first 3 digits + lateral half of 4th**; thenar atrophy |
| **Riche-Cannieu anastomosis** | Deep ulnar → recurrent motor median branch → **thenar strength preserved** |
| Sensory tests | 2PD (fails at **5 mm**), **Semmes-Weinstein = most sensitive (early)**, vibrometry, texture, **hand diagram = most specific** |
| **Phalen** | Dorsal hands together **30–60 s** |
| **Durkan** | Thumb pressure over carpal tunnel just distal to wrist crease → symptoms **within 30 s** |
| Tinel | Tapping over nerve → tingling |
| **CTS-6** | Score **> 12 → 80%** probability |
| Imaging | X-ray limited · MRI if mass · **US CSA > 10 mm²** at pisiform / proximal TCL |
| **NCV** | **Distal sensory latency > 3.5 ms**, **motor latency > 4.5 ms**, CV **< 52 m/s** (less specific than latencies) |
| EMG | Traditional gold standard (AAOS: limited recommendation) · ↑ insertional activity, sharp waves, fibrillations, fasciculations, complex repetitive discharges |

## 5 Management

```
Plica syndrome
├─ First line → ACTIVITY MODIFICATION + PHYSICAL THERAPY (hamstring stretching), NSAIDs
│      └─ persistent → INTRAPLICAL CORTICOSTEROID INJECTION (TO)
├─ Failed conservative → MRI (limited sensitivity)
└─ Refractory / Sakakibara C–D → ARTHROSCOPIC PLICA EXCISION
```

### Diagnosis & initial management

**Most cases** — **nonop**: activity restriction, NSAIDs, PT · avoid **repetitive jumping, squatting, lunging** · usually successful

**PT content** — **moist heat**, **hamstring stretching** · **resistive strengthening avoided early** (Orthobullets) · **quadriceps strengthening** part of rehab (OB) — read the options

**Weightlifter, palpable painful anteromedial fold, McMurray −** — **activity modification & physical therapy**\
✗ Wrong options: diagnostic arthroscopy · MRI · PRP · patellar stabilising brace

**Arthroscopic resection** — **rare**: plica band syndrome **not responding to nonop** · **thickened Sakakibara C plica (TO)** → **arthroscopic excision**

## X vs Y

| | **Infrapatellar (lig. mucosum)** | **Medial (shelf)** | **Suprapatellar** | **Lateral** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency | **Most common** | Common (most common type — one TO) | Most persistent | **Least common** |
| Symptoms | **Rare** | **Most symptomatic** | Usually none | None |
| Course | **Notch → fat pad** | **Medial wall → fat pad** | **Femoral metaphysis → medial wall** | Lateral facet → infrapatellar |

| | **Sakakibara A / B** | **Sakakibara C / D** |
|---|---|---|
| Covers anterior MFC | No | **C: yes**; D fenestrated |
| Cartilage damage | Unlikely | **Yes** |
| Treatment | Nonop | **Arthroscopic resection** |

## How it's asked

**"Arrow on arthroscopic PF view (inferolateral portal)?"** → **plica**

**"Anteromedial fold, popping on squats — next step?"** → **activity modification & physical therapy**

**"Infrapatellar plica (TO)?"** → **runs from intercondylar notch to fat pad**\
✗ Wrong options: medial wall → fat pad = medial · metaphysis → medial wall = suprapatellar

**"Most common symptomatic plica (TO)?"** → **medial**

**"Most significant plica causing knee pain (TO)?"** → **medial**

**"NOT true about synovial plica (TO)?"** → **"Type C Sakakibara medial plica is not associated with articular cartilage damage"** (it is)

**"Cyclist, hypointense band covering anterior MFC (TO)?"** → **Sakakibara type C synovial plica — arthroscopic excision**\
✗ Wrong options: type B ablation · MPFL cyst

**"Brodie abscess — treatment (TO)?"** → **curettage + bone grafting under an anti-staphylococcal antibiotic cover**

**"Not a test for carpal tunnel syndrome (TO)?"** → **Murphy**
