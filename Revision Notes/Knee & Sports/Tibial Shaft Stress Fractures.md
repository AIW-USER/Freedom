# Tibial Shaft Stress Fractures

*Knee & Sports · 59 / 61*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology – tibia most common site

**Most common stress fracture bone** — **tibia** in athletes + military training (OB)\
✗ Wrong options: femur · fibula · navicular · 5th MT

**Most common site (TO)** — **posteromedial tibia, junction of middle + distal third** → answer "**distal third of tibia**"\
✗ Wrong options: 2nd MT · fibula · femoral neck

**Series (Ohta-Fukushima, OB)** — 370 athletes: **tibia 49.1%** > tarsals 25.3% > metatarsals 8.8% · evaluation **> 3 wk** after pain onset → prolonged recovery

**2nd commonest** — **2nd / 3rd MT shaft (March fracture)** (TO) · **tarsals** (OB series) — read the options

**Who** — **runners + military recruits** · after a **change in training routine** · incidence in runners **up to 20%** (OB)

**Risk factors (OB)** — **sudden ↑ training intensity**, **low BMD**, **menstrual irregularities**, **low energy availability** · always evaluate **female athlete triad**

### Pathophysiology

**Mechanism** — **repetitive submaximal loads** → resorption > formation · abrupt ↑ duration / intensity / frequency without rest → **↑ osteoclast activity** (OB)

**Bone type conflict** — **linear microfractures in trabecular bone** (Orthobullets) · **weight-bearing cortical bone, not usually trabecular** (OB, Female Athlete chapter) — read the options

**Healing** — **callus** → **woven bone** → **endochondral bone formation**

**Continuum (TO)** — repetitive submaximal force → microdamage → remodelling imbalance → **stress reaction** (diffuse tenderness) → **stress fracture** (focal tenderness) → complete fracture · pain: diffuse after activity → focal, later at rest / night, **no relief with warm-up**

| Bone-loading test (TO) | Positive |
|---|---|
| **Hop test** | Pain reproduced |
| **Fulcrum test** | Sharp pain → **femoral shaft** stress fracture |
| One-leg standing hyperextension | Pain → **pars** fracture |

**Why the anterior cortex fails** — **tension side** from **posterior muscle force** (Orthobullets) · **tensile forces + relative paucity of blood supply** in anterior midshaft (OB)

### High- vs low-risk sites – tension vs compression

| | **Anterior cortex (tension)** | **Posteromedial cortex (compression)** |
|---|---|---|
| Frequency | **Uncommon** | **Typical / most common** |
| Risk | **High** — delayed union, **nonunion**, **propagation to complete fracture** | **Low** — usually heals |
| X-ray | **"Dreaded black line"** (transverse anterior lucency) | Periosteal reaction, cortical thickening |
| Rx | Often **IM nail** | **Protected WB** |

**Greatest risk of delayed healing / nonunion (OB)** — **anterior cortex of tibia** · 2nd + 5th MT can delay / nonunite (OB)\
✗ Wrong options: posteromedial tibia · distal fibula · inferior femoral neck (compression side) · **3rd MT — heals well**

**High-risk sites (TO, Femoral Neck Stress Fractures chapter)** — tension femoral neck, tension patella, **anterior tibial cortex**, medial malleolus, talar neck, dorsal navicular cortex, 5th MT proximal metaphysis, great-toe sesamoids

**Low-risk sites (TO, same chapter)** — femoral shaft, **medial tibia**, ribs, ulnar shaft, 1st–4th MT · **2nd MT** low risk (TO) vs can delay / nonunite (OB) — read the options

## 3 Presentation

**History** — **change in exercise routine** · **insidious onset** · pain with running first → then **daily activities / walking**

**Exam** — **pain directly over the fracture** · **point tenderness** (TO)

**Army recruit, 6 wk training, posteromedial pain, point tenderness at distal–middle third junction (TO)** — **stress fracture**\
✗ Wrong options: posteromedial tibial stress syndrome · gastrocnemius strain · bone contusion

**MTSS clue (TO)** — **dull pain** ↑ with activity, relieved by rest · **diffuse tenderness along posteromedial tibial border** (vs **point** tenderness in fracture)

**Differential (OB)** — medial tibial stress syndrome (shin splints), periostitis, infection, bursitis, neoplasm, **exertional compartment syndrome**, nerve entrapment

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Study | Findings | Facts |
|---|---|---|
| **Radiographs (AP + lateral)** | **Lateral: "dreaded black line" anteriorly** (tension) · endosteal thickening · periosteal reaction + cortical thickening | Often diagnostic (Orthobullets) · **usually normal early** → later periosteal reaction, new bone, fracture line (OB) |
| **MRI** | **Earliest: T2 periosteal high signal** · marrow oedema · **T1 linear low-signal zone** | **Most sensitive** · **replacing bone scan** · normal X-ray + high suspicion → MRI |
| Tc-99m bone scan | **Focal uptake** in cortical and / or trabecular region | **Highly sensitive, low specificity** (OB) |

**Investigation of choice (TO)** — **MRI** — **best combined sensitivity + specificity even early**; recommended for the whole bone-stress spectrum\
✗ Wrong options: CT · clinical exam · bone scan

| Modality (TO, stress-injury lecture) | Key facts |
|---|---|
| X-ray | Positive after **2–8 wk** (shin-pain lecture: 2–3 wk) · sensitivity **12–56%**, specificity **88–96%** |
| Scintigraphy | Positive **24–36 h** · sensitive, non-specific |
| **CT** | **Best for cortical fracture line** |
| **MRI** | **Gold standard**, early marrow oedema · sensitivity **68–99%** |
| Ultrasound | Periosteal thickening, callus · Doppler hypervascularity |

**MRI grading systems (TO)** — **Fredrickson–Kijowski** (1 periosteal oedema → **4b** cortical line) · **Arendt** (1 STIR oedema → 4 line) · **Nattiv** (1 mild T2 oedema → 4 severe + line)

**X-ray-negative suspected tibial stress fracture (TO)** — **MRI** — **most sensitive + specific** · beats bone scan + CT for soft-tissue / oedema, **earlier** than bone scan · sensitivity ≈ bone scan but **far more precise for location + extent**

**MRI vs bone scan (OB)** — MRI better (Ishibashi)

**Normal films, high suspicion (OB)** — trial of rest + **serial radiographs** also appropriate

## 5 Management

```
Tibial shaft stress fracture
├─ Posteromedial / X-ray normal, MRI oedema → protected WB 4–6 wk, stop running
├─ Anterior cortex, dreaded black line → reamed IM nail
│      (esp. if the line violates the anterior cortex)
└─ Failed nonop, complete, or fixed deadline → IM nail
```

### Operative criteria

| Indication for IM nail (OB, Orthobullets) |
|---|
| **High-risk fracture — anterior tibial cortex** ("dreaded black line", esp. violating the cortex) |
| **Failure of nonoperative management** (no improvement with rest — Boden) |
| **Complete fracture** |
| **Need to return on a specific timeline** (athletes, military) |

### Posteromedial compression-side fractures – protected weight bearing

**Standard (most cases)** — **activity restriction + protected weight-bearing** · **avoid NSAIDs** (slow bone healing) · consider **bone stimulator** · correct nutritional deficiencies

**Duration (OB)** — **4–6 wk limited WB** → progress to full WB

**Runner, normal X-ray, MRI oedema, positive bone scan** — **protected WB 4–6 wk**\
✗ Wrong options: immediate return · IM nail without nonop trial · long-leg cast · biopsy

**Distance runner, proximal tibial pain now with walking, normal X-ray, MRI +** — **protected WB with crutches**\
✗ Wrong options: switch to elliptical · prescription NSAIDs · arthroscopy · ORIF

**Pneumatic bracing + modified rest (OB, Batt)** — effective, but return to full activity **\~12 months**

**Pulsed ultrasound (OB, Rue)** — **no** effect on healing or return to duty (26 servicemen, 43 fractures)

**Medial (posteromedial) tibial SF (TO)** — cavus or over-pronated foot · rest, crutches, **Aircast 2–6 wk**, cross-training (swim, cycle) · return when **pain-free + no tenderness**

| Site-specific treatment (TO) | Management |
|---|---|
| Femoral neck | Bed rest 1 wk → gradual WB · displaced → surgery |
| **Anterior tibial cortex** | **NWB 6–8 wk or IM fixation** |
| Medial malleolus | Cast 6 wk or fixation |
| Talus | NWB cast 6 wk or fragment excision |
| Navicular | NWB cast 6–8 wk or surgery |
| 2nd MT base | NWB 2 wk → PWB 2 wk |
| 5th MT base | Cast 6 wk or percutaneous screw |
| Sesamoid | NWB 4 wk |

**Adjuncts (TO)** — **LIPUS** · **ESWT (avoid if pain > 5/10)** · TECAR (deep heat) · **teriparatide (PTH)** · short-term NSAIDs only · footwear: **rearfoot strikers → cushioned, mid / forefoot → stiff insoles**

**Return-to-running (TO)** — pain-free walking **≥ 5 days** → walk + jog 50% pace alternate days → 60–100% speed → consecutive days (2 run : 1 rest) → full · **anti-gravity treadmill**: after 1 wk pain-free walking, 3 × 5 min at 50–70% BW, +5–10% every 2 wk → ground at 90% BW

**Most important factor in an athlete with a stress fracture (TO)** — source gives **no key**; best fit = **correction of biomechanical + training errors** (inferred) (vs immobilisation 8 wk, gradual return, analgesics)

### Anterior cortex tension-side fractures – nonunion risk & intramedullary nailing

**Footballer, 6 mo pain, failed nonop, anterior linear lucency** — **tibial IM nailing** — fastest WB + return\
✗ Wrong options: posterior plate + graft · graft alone · cast · protected WB

**Special-forces soldier, dreaded black line, deploys in 4 mo** — **tibial IM nail**\
✗ Wrong options: activity modification + vitamin D (high-risk site, no deficiency stated) · pulsed US · pneumatic brace NWB (up to 12 mo) · observe + repeat imaging (risk of completion)

**Outcome (Varner, OB)** — 11 athletes, **reamed IM nail** for chronic anterior stress fracture → **return to sport \~4 months**

**Plating** — **anterior tension-band plating** described · **not posterior**

**Anterior cortex nonop (TO)** — offload (pneumatic Aircast / KT tape) · **stop NSAIDs + smoking** · **LIPUS** · IM nail ± graft if nonunion

**IM nail caveat (OB)** — allows return to sport but **does not guarantee healing**

## 6 Complications

**Anterior cortex** — **delayed union, nonunion, propagation to complete fracture** · **highest risk of all tibial sites**

**Delayed diagnosis** — evaluation **> 3 wk** after pain onset → longer recovery

## X vs Y

| | **Stress fracture** | **MTSS (shin splints)** | **CECS** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tenderness | **Point / focal** | **Diffuse posteromedial border** | Normal exam at rest |
| Pain | With running → **walking / rest** | Dull, worse at start, **↓ during training** | Starts \~10 min in, resolves \~30 min after |
| X-ray | **Dreaded black line** (anterior) | Normal · periosteal exostoses (late) | Normal |
| Bone scan | **Focal, intense, all 3 phases** | **Diffuse, longitudinal, delayed phase only** | — |
| Test | **MRI** (oedema / line) | Clinical · bone scan / MRI | **Compartment pressures** |
| NSAIDs | **Avoid** (slow healing) | Used | Used |
