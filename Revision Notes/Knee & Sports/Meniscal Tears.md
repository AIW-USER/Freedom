# Meniscal Tears

*Knee & Sports · 30 / 61*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology & pathophysiology

**Incidence** — very common · **most common indication for knee surgery** · ↑ risk in **ACL-deficient** knees

**Medial > lateral** — overall · **exception: acute ACL tear → lateral tears commoner** (alpine skiers: LM > 4× MM) · **chronic ACL → medial** (PH MM = secondary restraint)

**MM tears (TO)** — **5–6×** commoner than LM (relative fixation to capsule / MCL) · LM circular, more mobile

**ACL + meniscal injury (TO)** — meniscal tear in **> 50% of acute** and **> 80% of chronic** ACL tears

**Acute tears** — young athletes · **twisting on a loaded, flexed knee**

**Degenerative tears** — older (**> 40 y**, loss of elasticity) → **posterior horn medial meniscus**

**Commonest pattern (TO)** — **radial** in a normal meniscus · **horizontal** in a discoid meniscus

### Meniscal structure & collagen (TO)

| Feature (TO) | Fact |
|---|---|
| Arteries | **Medial, lateral + middle geniculate** (from popliteal) → **perimeniscal capillary network** in synovium / capsule |
| Vascular rim | **Peripheral 10–30% of MM**, **10–25% of LM** (Orthobullets: MM 20–30%) |
| Avascular part | Remaining **65–75%** fed by **synovial fluid diffusion / mechanical pumping** (joint motion) |
| Age | **Fully vascular prenatal → just after birth** · 10–30% at age 10 · **peripheral 10–30% at maturity = red-red** |
| Nerves | **Posterior articular branch of posterior tibial nerve** + terminal **obturator + femoral** · lateral capsule = **recurrent peroneal branch of CPN** |
| Nerve fibres | Concentrated in the **periphery + anterior / posterior horns** (✓ horns rich in mechanoreceptors) |
| Mechanoreceptors | **Ruffini endings, Pacinian corpuscles, Golgi tendon organs** |
| ECM | **Water 72%, collagen 22%** + cells |
| Collagen | **Type I predominates** (+ II, III, V, VI) · collagen = up to **75% of dry weight** · **collagen (type I) gives tensile strength** (✗ Wrong options: type II) |
| Fibres | Type I **circumferential in deep layers** (parallel to periphery) · **radial superficially** |
| Hoop stress | **Axial load → tensile stress along circumferential fibres** (✓ generated on axial loading) |

**Why peripheral tears heal** — **↑ blood supply** (geniculate vessels at capsular attachment)\
✗ Wrong options: elasticity · type I / II collagen · GAG (distribution similar, no effect on healing)

## 2 Mechanism

### O'Donoghue unhappy triad (TO)

**Triad (TO)** — **ACL + MCL + medial meniscus** · **lateral force on knee with foot fixed** · O'Donoghue **1950**\
✗ Wrong options: LCL · PCL · lateral meniscus

**Later arthroscopic studies (TO)** — **lateral meniscus tear** commoner with **acute ACL** tear → triad questioned

**Bucket handle (TO)** — twisting injury, **fixed leg with flexed knee** struck by another player

**Root tear** — MMPRT: **low-energy attritional** · **descending stairs / downhill**, rising from a seat, deep squat → **pop** · LPRT: **with ACL** (high energy)

## 3 Presentation

**Symptoms** — pain localised medial / lateral · **mechanical (locking, clicking), esp. squatting** · **delayed / intermittent swelling** · effusion

**Joint line tenderness** — **most sensitive physical examination finding**

**Locked knee** — **cannot actively or passively extend** (past 40°, past 30°, ROM 20–90°) → **displaced bucket handle in notch** · **MRI indicated** with mechanical symptoms or locked knee

**Root tear** — **pop** in loaded flexion (rising from seat, stairs) · **posteromedial** JLT · **less catching / locking** than flap tears (meniscus extrudes) · exam **lacks specificity**\
✗ Wrong options: exam = gold standard

### Provocative tests

| Test | Technique | Positive |
|---|---|---|
| **McMurray** (Orthobullets / OB) | Flex knee, hand on joint line, rotate tibia, bring **flexion → extension** | Palpable **pop / click + pain** · **ER → medial**, **IR → lateral** |
| **McMurray (TO)** | Knee **> 90° flexion**, full **IR (LM)** or full **ER (MM)**; more flexion → **more posterior segments** | **Thud / click felt** (sometimes heard) |
| **Apley** | **Prone, 90° flexion, axial compression** | Pain with ER (MM) / IR (LM) |
| **Thessaly** | Standing on affected limb, twist IR / ER at **5° then 20°** flexion | Joint line pain, locking / catching |
| **Ege** | **Squat** with knees max ER or IR | Pain / click |

**McMurray stress — conflict** — **valgus force** for medial meniscus (OB) · **no varus / valgus stress** (TO) — read the options

**Deep flexion + ER pain (TO)** — **posterior horn medial meniscus** tear\
✗ Wrong options: body of MM · discoid LM · anterior horn LM

**Acute ACL + joint line tenderness** — **lateral meniscus tear**\
✗ Wrong options: MM · popliteus · proximal tib-fib · pes bursitis

**MM tenderness, intact ACL + PCL, dial 5°** — **medial meniscal tear only** (dial negative → no PLC)

**Exam traps** — no valgus end-point = MCL · no varus end-point = LCL · apprehension = patellar instability · **posterior drawer + quadriceps active test** = PCL · **pivot shift** (clunk with flexion, IR, valgus from extension) = ACL · 3-quadrant lateral translation + **J-sign** = patellar dislocation · posterior sag = PCL

**Most specific test (TO)** — **Thessaly** (WB rotation at 20°) · **joint line tenderness = most reliable** (TO) · swelling **delayed > 24 h** (synovial irritation, not haemarthrosis)

### Differential diagnosis

**MCL sprain** — pain with **valgus stress at 30°** (isolates superficial MCL), medial gapping

**Plica syndrome** — **medial parapatellar** pain, palpable cord

**Osteochondral lesion** — presents similarly → **MRI** differentiates

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Genes & lab markers

**RF + anti-CCP positive, synovitis at arthroscopy** — **active rheumatoid arthritis** → **absolute contraindication to meniscal allograft**

### Key radiology findings

| Study | Finding |
|---|---|
| Radiographs | **Normal** in young acute tear · **meniscal calcification** in crystal arthropathy (**CPPD**) |
| **MRI** | **Most sensitive** test · **high false-positive** rate |
| MRI tear criterion | **Grade III signal** = linear high signal **reaching the superior or inferior surface** |
| Parameniscal cyst | **Indicates a meniscal tear** |
| Extrusion | **Root tear** · < 3 mm extrusion not significant |
| Which compartment (sagittal) | **Lateral**: convex "anvil" tibia + **fibula** visible · **medial**: concave "champagne glass" tibia |

| MRI sign | Means |
|---|---|
| **Double PCL** | **Bucket-handle tear of MM** — low-signal fragment in notch **parallel, anteroinferior to PCL** · **specificity 100%** for flipped bucket handle |
| **Absent bow tie** (TO) | **Bucket handle** — < 2 bow ties on consecutive sagittal images (normal 9–12 mm → 2–3 images at 3 mm slice, 1 mm gap) · **more accurate than double PCL** · sensitivity 88.4% |
| Fragment in notch | Bucket handle · ≥ 1 displaced-fragment sign sensitivity 90.7% · commonest sign with absent bow tie + double PCL |
| **Double anterior horn / double meniscus** | **Flipped meniscal tear** (TO) · **flipped lateral meniscal tear** (OB210282) · **bucket handle**, fragment anterior to native anterior horn, often LM (Orthobullets, OB7436) |
| **Empty / ghost meniscus** (TO) | **Complete radial tear** |
| **Ghost sign** (OB / Orthobullets) | **Root tear** — no identifiable meniscus on sagittal images, or ↑ signal replacing dark meniscus · ± extrusion on coronal |
| **Cleft sign** (TO) | **Radial tear** |
| **Floating meniscus** (TO) | **Meniscotibial (coronary) ligament avulsion** — fluid under posterior horn |
| **Vacuum phenomenon** (TO) | Nitrogen gas (negative pressure, position) → **mimics tear in discoid meniscus** |
| **Meniscus within meniscus** (TO) | Wedge of high signal **not reaching surface → NOT a tear** |
| Ramp (TO) | Thin fluid line **PH MM ↔ posteromedial capsule** (most specific) ± posteromedial tibial oedema |

**Double PCL prerequisite (TO)** — **intact ACL + PCL** · **double delta sign** = flipped meniscus (TO) · **tram-track** = PCL mucoid degeneration (not meniscal)

**Double anterior horn — conflict** — **flipped meniscus tear** (TO, with "bucket handle" as a wrong option; OB210282: flipped LM tear) · **bucket handle** (Orthobullets, OB7436) — read the options

**Ghost — conflict** — "ghost / empty meniscus" = **complete radial tear** (TO) · "ghost sign" = **root tear** (OB / Orthobullets) — a radial tear within 1 cm of the root is a root tear

**Bucket-handle MRI distractors** — bone bruise **mid LFC + posterior lateral tibial plateau** = ACL · **medial patellar facet** bruise = patellar instability · fluid around lesion at **posterolateral MFC** = unstable OCD · T2 signal at **posterior tibial sulcus** = PCL

### Tear classification

| Zone | Distance from MCJ (TO) | Blood supply | Healing (TO) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Red-red** (outer third) | **0–3 mm** | Rich | **> 90%** — repair all |
| **Red-white** (middle third) | **3–5 mm** (5 mm = classic) | Few vessels | **60–80%** — repair, often with augmentation |
| **White-white** (inner third) | **> 5 mm** | **Avascular** | **< 50%** — "almost never heals" |

**Rim width** — distance from tear to **meniscocapsular junction** · **smaller rim = better blood supply = heals** · repairable **< 5 mm** (OB) · best **< 4 mm** (Orthobullets Meniscus) · Henning: healing up to **5 mm** with synovial rasping — read the options

**Other descriptors** — position (anterior, middle, posterior third, root) · size

| Pattern | Key fact |
|---|---|
| **Vertical / longitudinal** | Common, **esp. with ACL**; in line with fibres (separated, not disrupted) → **repair when peripheral** |
| **Bucket handle** | Displaced vertical tear → **notch**; complete tears **> 1 cm** unstable → **block to extension** |
| **Oblique / flap / parrot beak** | **PH–body junction**; **mechanical locking**; repair rarely possible → **excise to stable rim** |
| **Radial** | Cuts **circumferential fibres**; **complete to MCJ = posterior root tear** biomechanically |
| **Horizontal (cleavage)** | **Older**, degenerative · **meniscal cysts** |
| Complex / degenerative | Not reparable |
| **Root** | Avulsion or **radial tear within 1 cm** of root → **= total meniscectomy** |
| **Ramp** | PH MM meniscocapsular / meniscotibial separation **with ACL** |

### Root tears & meniscal avulsion

**Root function (TO)** — converts **axial load → hoop stress** · loss → **extrusion**, altered kinematics, ↓ contact area, **↑ peak contact pressure** → early OA

**Peak contact pressure** — **↑ 25%** (= total meniscectomy) · ↑ **most in flexion** · ↑ ER + lateral tibial translation · repair restores normal\
✗ Wrong options: extension

**Partial-thickness tears** — oblique / parrot beak **< 70% thickness** → no significant pressure change

**Posterior > anterior roots** — esp. **medial (least mobile)** · anterior horn injury rare → **iatrogenic**: ACL tibial tunnel reaming (**posterolateral** tunnel) (TO), tibial nailing

| | **Medial posterior root (MMPRT)** | **Lateral posterior root (LPRT)** |
|---|---|---|
| Patient | **Middle-aged / elderly women**, ↑ age, **obesity**, **varus**, sedentary | **Young, active** |
| Nature | **Degenerative**, attritional | **With ACL tear** (TO: not degenerative) |
| Linked with | **Chondral injury**, extrusion → **rapid medial OA** | Suspect with **grade 3 Lachman + 3+ pivot shift** (TO) |
| Treatment | Root repair if joint preserved | **Always repair with the ACL** (TO) |

![Ghost sign — absent meniscus at the root](../../MCQ/attachments/q211975-illa-ghost-sign-labeleed.jpg)

**Meniscal avulsion (TO)** — **meniscotibial (coronary) ligament** torn → **floating meniscus** · **repair whenever possible** (suture to anatomic position) \
✗ Wrong options: "both meniscotibial + meniscofemoral torn"

**Ligaments (TO)** — meniscofemoral → posterior femur · **meniscotibial = coronary**, short peripheral bands, part of **layer 3 (deepest)** of lateral capsule

**Floating-meniscus mimic (TO)** — **Wrisberg-type discoid** (no posterior tibial attachment)

### Ramp lesions (TO)

**Definition (TO)** — longitudinal / oblique **peripheral tear of PH MM** → meniscocapsular or meniscotibial disruption · Staubli + Rauschning **1994** · lies in **red-red zone** → heals well

**Anatomy (TO)** — posterior junction = **meniscocapsular ligament (superior) + meniscotibial ligament (inferior)** · with PH MM resist **anterior tibial translation + posteromedial rotation**

**ACL link (TO)** — **9–40%** of ACL-injured knees · pivot shift subluxes medial plateau → avulses PH · ↑ with **chronic ACL / delayed reconstruction**

**Risk factors (TO)** — posteromedial tibial **bone contusion**, chronic ACL, **steep medial tibial + meniscal slope**, gradual lateral tibial slope, **varus > 3°**, **male < 30**, concomitant **lateral meniscal tear**

**Diagnosis (TO)** — exam non-specific · **MRI sensitivity 48–65%** · **gold standard = arthroscopy through a posteromedial portal** or **trans-notch (Gillquist) view**\
✗ Wrong options: MRI · McMurray · standard anterior portals (blind spot)

**Differential (TO)** — **zip lesion ("Wrisberg rip")**: tear at Wrisberg MFL–PH LM junction with ACL, **> 1.4 cm** mediolateral from lateral PCL edge (vs Wrisberg pseudotear) · flipped tear · root tear

**Other classifications (TO)** — Seil (extent, capsular injury, **adherent → self-heal vs dehiscent → repair**) · Thaunat · Greif (extended Thaunat)

| Thaunat (TO21733124) | Greif | Stability |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Meniscocapsular tear (isolated, posterosuperior) | 1 Meniscocapsular ligament tear | Stable |
| 2 Partial superior tear | 2 Partial superior peripheral PH tear | Stable |
| **3 Partial inferior tear — "hidden"** | 3A partial inferior PH tear · **3B meniscotibial ligament tear** | Unstable |
| 4 Complete tear | 4A complete peripheral PH tear · 4B complete MCJ tear | Unstable |
| 5 Double tear | 5 Peripheral PH double tear | Unstable |

|---|---|
| 1 | Partial meniscocapsular separation — mildest, stable on probing |
| **2** | **Complete meniscocapsular separation — classic, most common** |

**Ramp classification — conflict** — the two TO explanations describe Thaunat types differently · **"hidden" = type 3** · types 1–2 stable, 3–5 unstable — read the options

### Meniscal calcification (TO)

**Causes (TO)** — **trauma, ochronosis, pseudogout (CPPD), degenerative arthritis / OA, hyperparathyroidism** · **NOT rheumatoid arthritis**, **NOT osteopetrosis**

### Meniscal cysts (TO)

**Association (TO)** — **almost always an underlying meniscal tear** (most common associated lesion) · synovial fluid **extrudes through the tear**

**Side (TO)** — **medial > lateral** on recent radiology reviews (old teaching: lateral)\
✗ Wrong options: lateral commoner · equal · "common with ACL tear"

### Discoid meniscus (TO)

**Discoid (TO)** — **most frequent congenital meniscal malformation** · **lateral > medial** · highest prevalence **Japanese + Korean** · classified by **Watanabe 1969** (arthroscopic)\
✗ Wrong options: "classified by Wrisberg"

| Watanabe (TO) | Shape / coverage | Posterior attachment | Fact |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Complete (I)** | Block / disc, **entire** lateral plateau | Normal | Commoner (with incomplete); usually asymptomatic |
| **Incomplete (II)** | Semilunar, **< 80%** / up to 80% | Normal | Usually asymptomatic; torn → lateral JLT, clicking, effusion |
| **Wrisberg (III)** | **Near-normal size + shape** | **No coronary / popliteomeniscal attachment — only Wrisberg MFL** | **Least common**, most unstable, **younger**, no trauma → **snapping knee** |

**Structure (TO)** — **↓ collagen density, disorganised network** · mucinous change (like degenerative meniscus) · ↑ thickness, enlarged surface, **↓ peripheral vascularity**

**Commonest tear in discoid (TO)** — **horizontal** (repetitive microtrauma after mucoid degeneration)

**Snapping knee syndrome (TO)** — **Wrisberg type** · also a torn stable discoid whose tear spreads to the posterior tibial attachment · audible / palpable **"meniscal thud" at terminal extension (10–20°)**, clunk at end of flexion · **3–4 y**: often painless snapping · **8–10 y**: pain with activity, more acute

**Symptoms (TO)** — insidious popping / snapping without trauma, pain, giving way, effusion, quadriceps atrophy, ↓ ROM, locking

**Neuschwander (TO)** — Wrisberg type = "**lateral meniscal variant with absence of the posterior coronary ligament**" (not truly discoid)

**Discoid medial (TO)** — much rarer · **disc shaped** · linked with **trauma / tears**

## 5 Management

### Algorithms

```
Meniscal tear
├─ Degenerative, older → nonop first (rest, NSAIDs, PT)
│      ("noninferior" to arthroscopic partial meniscectomy)
├─ Young, locked knee / displaced bucket handle → arthroscopic reduction + repair
├─ Peripheral vertical / longitudinal, young → repair
├─ Root tear → root repair (see below)
└─ Complex, degenerative, flap, white-white → partial meniscectomy
```

```
Posterior root tear
├─ Minimal arthritis (KL ≤ 2), BMI < 35 → transtibial pull-out root repair
├─ With ACL tear → ACL reconstruction + root repair
└─ Established arthritis → arthroplasty (UKA / TKA)
```

### Operative criteria — repair indications (TO)

| Patient factors (TO) | Tear factors (TO) |
|---|---|
| **Younger (< 40)**, active | **Red-red / red-white** — ideal, not mandatory |
| No significant comorbidities | **Simple pattern**: vertical, longitudinal, **bucket handle**, **root**, **ramp** |
| **BMI < 30** | **< 3 months old** |
| Willing to follow rehab | **Associated ACL reconstruction** |
| | **Reducible without excess tension** |
| | **Lower threshold for complete radial** tears |

**NOT a repair indication (TO)** — **horizontal tear of 2 cm**\
✗ Wrong options: associated ACL tear · 1-month-old bucket handle · 2-month-old root tear

**Best indication (TO)** — **capsulomeniscal junction tear** = most peripheral red-red, maximum healing · **best zone = red-red**\
✗ Wrong options: red-white · white-white · fresh radial in young

**Best candidate (Orthobullets)** — **peripheral red-red**, **low rim width**, **vertical + longitudinal**, **1–4 cm**, **root tear**, **acute + ACL reconstruction**

**Favouring repair (TO)** — length **> 10 mm** · **unstable (> 3 mm displacement on probing)** · **acute (< 6 wk)** · young active · **concurrent ACL reconstruction**

**Partial meniscectomy instead** — **not reparable (complex, degenerative, radial)** · **repair failed > 2 times**

| Repair prognosis | Good | Poor |
|---|---|---|
| Pattern | **Vertical + longitudinal** | Radial, horizontal, complex, degenerative |
| Location | **Red-red, small rim width** | Red-white junction, **large rim width**, white-white |
| Knee | **ACL reconstructed** | **Unaddressed ACL insufficiency** (meniscus strained as secondary stabiliser) |
| Most important factor | **Width of the meniscal rim** | ✗ Wrong options: days from injury · medial vs lateral · growth hormone · dominant leg |

**Stärke (OB)** — biology matters more than technique · ↓ healing in complex / degenerative, central, unstable-knee tears

**Timing / length — conflict** — time from injury, side and length **not consistently shown to affect healing** (OB) · **acute < 6 wk** and **> 10 mm** favour repair (TO) · **1–4 cm** ideal (Orthobullets) — read the options

**Age** — young → **repair** (better OA prevention + return to sport) · older → nonop or partial meniscectomy

### Bucket-handle tears — locked knee & repair

**Young athlete, locked knee, double PCL (TO)** — **arthroscopic repair**\
✗ Wrong options: partial meniscectomy · RICE 2 wk then exercises or return to sport

**Locked knee, LM bucket on MRI** — **arthroscopic lateral meniscectomy or repair** · large bucket handles usually **red-red → reparable**; badly frayed → partial meniscectomy\
✗ Wrong options: medial side · transplant (not acute) · passive stretching (more damage) · steroid

**Displaced LM bucket handle, 3-mm rim, 16 y** — **inside-out vertical mattress sutures** · standard for bucket handles = **inside-out**\
✗ Wrong options: partial / subtotal meniscectomy · all-inside arrows / darts · horizontal mattress

**12 days, lacks extension, LM bucket handle** — **arthroscopic repair + immediate mobilisation** (or short immobilisation) · **no prolonged immobilisation**\
✗ Wrong options: rest + PT · loose-body removal · PCL reconstruction · immediate ACL reconstruction

**Fixation** — inside-out (**gold standard**), outside-in, all-inside → **equivalent outcomes** in recent studies

**Bucket handle vs radial repair** — **equivalent clinical outcomes + reoperation rates** (VAS, IKDC, Tegner)\
✗ Wrong options: higher reoperation for bucket handle

**Radial repair — conflict** — radial tears "generally not amenable to repair" (OB, Orthobullets meniscectomy indications) · **equivalent to bucket-handle repair** (newer OB, Orthobullets recent evidence) — read the options

### Meniscal repair — healing & outcomes

| Repair success | Orthobullets | OB | TO |
|---|---|---|---|
| Overall | **70–95%** | — | Inside-out 70–90% |
| **With ACL reconstruction** | **90% (highest)** | **> 90%** · Barber **92%** | **85–95%** |
| Intact ACL / stable knee | **60% (modest)** | 60% · Barber **67%** | 50–60% (no ACL reconstruction) |
| Untreated ACL deficiency | **30% (poor)** | — | — |

**Why ACL reconstruction helps** — **haemarthrosis** from tunnel drilling → **growth factors** (+ marrow-derived stem cells, TO), restored stability · **2nd-generation all-inside: no difference** with / without ACL reconstruction (current literature, Orthobullets) · Barber: no change to accelerated ACL rehab needed

**Treated at ACL reconstruction — heals best** — **meniscal tear (> 90%)**\
✗ Wrong options: chondral defect 60–70% · grade III MCL (19% reoperation for arthrofibrosis) · PLC (90% objective stability) · PCL (85–90%)

**Longitudinal tear 5 mm from MCJ, 28 y (TO)** — **red-white zone; concurrent ACL reconstruction improves healing (marrow-derived stem cells)** (keyed) · (✗ Wrong options: white-white → meniscectomy only · red-red, inside-out "to spare saphenous") · isolated red-white repair → **fibrin clot / PRP**

**Biological augmentation (TO)** — **fibrin clot** (venous blood; PDGF, TGF-β, VEGF, MSCs; **Arnoczky**) · **PRP** (mixed evidence; red-white + white-white) · **marrow stimulation** (trephination / microfracture) · **rasping** (Orthobullets)

**Suture configuration** — **vertical mattress = strongest** (captures circumferential fibres)

### Repair techniques

| (TO + Orthobullets) | **Inside-out** | **Outside-in** | **All-inside** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Status | **Gold standard** (Henning 1983) | Warren 1985, Morgan 1991 | **Most common**; modern standard for posterior horn |
| Best for | **Mid-body + posterior horn**, bucket handle, large complex, **revision** | **Anterior horn** (technique of choice) + body | **Posterior horn** of both menisci; with ACL reconstruction |
| Method | Long needles in → out via **zone-specific cannulae**; 2-0 braided non-absorbable, **vertical mattress 3–5 mm apart** | **18-G spinal needle** out → in; Morgan **mulberry knot** | Suture-anchor devices (FasT-Fix: 2 polymer anchors + pre-tied sliding knot) |
| Incision | **Posterior safety incision** 2–4 cm (TO) / 3–4 cm (TO) for posterior half | Small "poke" incisions | **None** — portals only |
| Strength | **Highest** — pull-out **80–120 N** | Suture-dependent; may be < inside-out | **60–80 N**; some give horizontal mattress |
| Advantage | Versatile, surgeon control, longest results | Simplest, **lower NV risk** (anterior entry) | **Fast (2–3 vs 15–20 min per suture)**, no incision, no assistant |
| Main risk | **Saphenous (medial) / peroneal (lateral) nerve** | **Chondral damage** (repeated passes); poor posterior reach | **Implant failure**, migration, **cyst**, chondral injury, breakage, cost $300–600 per device |

**1st-generation all-inside (TO)** — rigid absorbable **arrows / darts / staples** → migration, cartilage damage, foreign-body reaction, cysts → abandoned

**Hybrid repair (TO)** — combined techniques → **bucket-handle tears** · suture **UHMWPE** (FiberWire / FiberTape) · vertical or horizontal mattress · all-inside usable anywhere but **costly**

**Augmentation (TO, Mohindra)** — synovial abrasion / **rasp** · **vascular access channels** (refenestration) · PRP / fibrin clot · **concurrent ACLR = natural biological stimulus**

**Inside-out indications (TO)** — posterior horn, middle third, peripheral capsular, bucket handle · **contraindicated**: **root tears**, severe degeneration, avascular-zone tears

**Open repair** — uncommon; trauma, knee dislocation

**Turman & Diduch** — gold standard = **inside-out vertical mattress** · all-inside for special cases (concurrent ACL reconstruction)

**Failure** — inside-out ≈ all-inside · **synovitis** with absorbable sutures / implants

### Inside-out approach intervals & nerves at risk

| | **Medial meniscus** | **Lateral meniscus** |
|---|---|---|
| Incision | **Posterior to MCL**; incise **sartorius fascia** | Between **ITB and biceps** |
| Retract | **Pes + semimembranosus posteriorly** | Biceps posteriorly → **lateral gastrocnemius posteriorly** (+ medially — shields NV) |
| Deep interval | **Medial head gastrocnemius ↔ capsule** | **Lateral head gastrocnemius ↔ capsule** |
| Nerve at risk | **Saphenous nerve** (infrapatellar branch) + vein — needle or **suture entrapment** | **Common peroneal nerve** — foot drop, the more feared |
| Avoid | Medial incision **too posterior** | Splitting **below biceps** · keep incision **anterior to biceps + lateral gastrocnemius** |
| Also at risk | — | **Popliteal vessels**, **popliteus**, **lateral inferior genicular artery** (posterolateral capsule at joint line) |

**Interval traps** — ✗ Wrong options: medial: semimembranosus–medial gastrocnemius (too posterior) · sartorius–gracilis (splits pes) · lateral: lateral gastrocnemius–biceps (too posterior, peroneal in field) · gastrocnemius retracted anteriorly · split gastrocnemius · LCL retracted

✗ Wrong options: two-incision ACL · tibial inlay PCL (direct posterior / posteromedial) · peroneal nerve exploration (below biceps)

**Saphenous injury** — commonest with **inside-out medial** repair (> all-inside) · **peroneal** with lateral repairs

**Maximum risk, inside-out LM repair (TO)** — **peroneal nerve** · lateral geniculate artery also at risk (TO keyed 1 and 2)\
✗ Wrong options: popliteus · lateral inferior genicular artery · saphenous

**Complex bucket handle, mid-body → PH MM (TO)** — **inside-out for the PH needs a safety incision to protect saphenous nerve + vessels**\
✗ Wrong options: outside-in for PH · all-inside "only option" for mid-body · all-inside less chondral damage

### Root repair

**Indication** — **no / minimal arthritis (KL ≤ 2)**, adequate tissue, **BMI < 35** (TO) · young patients first-line · now also older patients

**Technique (TO)** — **transtibial (transosseous) pull-out** · 2 × No. 2 FiberWire → tunnel from **anteromedial tibial cortex to root footprint** → tied over a **cortical button** in extension · **anatomic anchoring**\
✗ Wrong options: vertical mattress

**Healing** — **> 90%** (OB) · **70–90% if repaired within 3–6 months** (TO) — read the options

**Outcome** — **better function than partial meniscectomy** · contact pressures restored · TKA at 5 y much lower (0% in some studies)

**Natural history, nonop / partial meniscectomy** — **~30% TKA at 5 y** · **up to 28% TKA at mean 3.2 y** (OB) · rapid medial OA

**Which arthroscopic lesion to repair?** — **root tear**\
✗ Wrong options: degenerative tear · complex white-white tear · parrot beak → partial meniscectomy

**Surgery first-line?** — **18-year-old female, medial root tear on imaging, pain with deep flexion after landing awkwardly off a jump**\
✗ Wrong options: 40 y stable MCL, 4 mm gapping · isolated PCL with negative dial · Osgood–Schlatter · Meyers–McKeever type 1 tibial eminence → NWB long-leg cast

**ACL rupture + medial root tear, 22 y** — **ACL reconstruction + root repair**\
✗ Wrong options: meniscectomy · all-inside horizontal mattress · staged inside-out · delayed transplant (only if repair fails)

**43 y medial root tear, wants to avoid TKA** — **arthroscopic root repair**\
✗ Wrong options: PT · steroid · partial meniscectomy (no hoop stress) · transplant (preserve native first)

**60 y MMPRT with extrusion** — **accelerated medial compartment OA**\
✗ Wrong options: ACL-associated · excision better · "30% TKA regardless of treatment"

### Ramp repair (TO)

**Principle (TO)** — peripheral vascular → **repair, not resection**

**Stable (types 1–2) (TO)** — small (< 1 cm) partial tear, stable on probing → may leave (ACL haemarthrosis aids healing) · most surgeons still repair

**Unstable (types 3–5) (TO)** — **posteromedial portal**, **all-inside** (FasT-Fix) or inside-out / suture anchor · rasp edges · 1–3 sutures · **with ACL reconstruction (essential)**

**Missed ramp (TO)** — ↑ **anterior tibial translation + internal rotation** → **ACL graft failure**, persistent laxity, OA

**Outcome (TO)** — healing **85–95%** · better AP stability, fewer re-ruptures

### Partial & total meniscectomy

**Partial meniscectomy** — **> 80% satisfactory** · **50% Fairbank changes** (osteophytes, flattening, joint-space narrowing) · **minimise resection** (OA ∝ amount removed) · **no thermal probes** · early AROM (10 wk immobilisation harmful — dog model)

**Predictors of success** — **age < 40**, normal alignment, minimal arthritis, **single tear**

**Meniscectomy principles (TO)** — excise **only the unstable fragment** · preserve **2–4 mm peripheral rim** · **cut first, then shave** (balance edges) · **bucket handle: reduce first, then resect** · indications: complex, degenerative, white-white tears

**Total meniscectomy** — historical · **20% significant arthritis + 70% radiographic change at 3 y** · **100% arthrosis at 20 y** (Orthobullets) vs **50–70% radiographic OA by 20 y**, contact pressure **↑ 235%** (TO) — read the options

### Rehabilitation after repair (TO)

**Weight bearing (TO)** — **allowed in extension** (axial load compresses the tear) · nonop: PWB, ROM as tolerated (Orthobullets)

**Flexion (TO)** — limit to **90° for 4–6 wk** (deep flexion loads the posterior horn) · Orthobullets: avoid > 90°

**Return (TO)** — no deep squat / pivot for **4–6 months** · contact sport at **6–9 months**

**Repair rehab — TO (Mohindra) table** — **0–6 wk brace, NWB, isometrics** → 6–12 wk PWB, ROM **0–90°**, avoid hamstring activation → 3–6 months full WB, closed chain → **> 6 months** sport · vs WB allowed in extension (above) — read the options

### Meniscal allograft transplantation

**Indication** — controversial salvage · **young (< 50)** after **total / near-total meniscectomy**, esp. **lateral**, with pain in that compartment

| Criteria to proceed (OB) | Contraindications |
|---|---|
| **Prior total meniscectomy** | **Inflammatory arthritis (RA: + RF, + anti-CCP, synovitis) — absolute** |
| **Age ≤ 50** | **Diffuse / advanced arthritis**, **flattened femoral condyle** or tibial plateau |
| **BMI < 30** | **Outerbridge IV** (absolute, OB) · grade III + IV (Orthobullets) |
| Compartment pain, **recurrent effusions** | Untreated **tibiofemoral subluxation**, synovial disease |
| **≥ 2 mm joint space on 45° flexion WB AP** | **Previous joint infection**, **skeletal immaturity**, **marked obesity** |
| **Stable ligaments** (ACL reconstruction before / with) | **Uncorrected malalignment** (HTO before / with) |
| **Normal alignment**, no cartilage loss | **Ligament insufficiency / instability** |

**NOT contraindications** — **age > 30**, prior ACL reconstruction with allograft, **Outerbridge I–II**, BMI 28, healed HTO, intact ACL, 4 mm joint space · focal Outerbridge III if treated · malalignment not absolute (correct it)

**Grade IV — conflict** — **absolute contraindication** (OB853, OB4892) · **relative** if not addressed by cartilage restoration (OB1429) — read the options · Rijk: grade IV → **up to 50% graft failure within 2 y**

**Graft** — **fresh-frozen (−80 °C)** = standard, **no effect on outcome** (cell viability irrelevant — host repopulates) · **gamma irradiation degrades collagen** · cryopreservation / freeze-drying abandoned

**Sizing** — on radiographs · mismatch **> 5–10% → poor** (15% = negative) · **oversized → extrusion** · **undersized → poor congruity, ↑ load**

**Technique** — **bone plugs at each horn or a bone bridge** + peripheral vertical mattress sutures

**MAT technique (TO)** — **bone plug** (cylindrical blocks in tibial tunnels) vs **bone bridge** (single **keyhole** bone bridge → slot) — **bridge preferred** (TO) · indication: **symptomatic young post-meniscectomy knee**, no advanced OA / malalignment, intact ligaments

| MAT graft preservation (TO) | Feature |
|---|---|
| **Fresh** | Best viability · **1-week shelf life** |
| **Fresh-frozen (−80 °C)** | **Commonest** · good balance |
| Lyophilised (freeze-dried) | Long storage · **weak biomechanics** |
| **Cryopreserved** (glycerol) | **Best cell viability** of stored grafts · costly |

**Outcomes** — Cole: **77% satisfied** at ≥ 2 y · return to sport **6–9 months** · 10 y: pain + function still better · re-tears / extrusion common

**OA after MAT — conflict** — most show radiographic progression at 10 y (Orthobullets) · **protective against OA** (OB) — read the options

**Healing — conflict** — graft **incorporates at 8–12 weeks** (OB) · **8–12 months to heal fully** (Orthobullets) — read the options

**Varus + cartilage loss after medial meniscectomy, 34 y** — **isolated MAT would fail (alignment + cartilage loss)**\
✗ Wrong options: literature doesn't support MAT · too old · viscosupplementation · medial closing-wedge HTO (varus-producing)

### Discoid meniscus — treatment (TO)

**Stable complete / incomplete (TO)** — **defer** unless grooving, chondromalacia or other compartment damage

**Torn complete / incomplete, hypermobile medial segment, intact periphery (TO)** — **subtotal meniscectomy / saucerization** of mobile fragment

**Young child (TO)** — saucerized rim may remodel (vascular immature meniscus) · but **< 16 y: total meniscectomy may give the longest symptom-free period** (TO)

**Wrisberg type (TO)** — **total meniscectomy** (subtotal leaves an unstable rim) · alternative: **saucerization + suture rim to capsule**

**Total meniscectomy — conflict** — TO lines above vs OB / Orthobullets (Discoid Meniscus chapter): **avoid total meniscectomy** (~90% degenerative change); **saucerize to 6–8 mm rim**, **repair / stabilize** a detached Wrisberg rim — read the options

```
Discoid lateral meniscus (TO)
├─ Asymptomatic → nonop, close follow-up
├─ Child (usually complete), mechanical symptoms → partial meniscectomy ± repair
├─ Adult, complete, mechanical symptoms → partial meniscectomy ± repair
└─ Adult, incomplete
       ├─ Pain ± mechanical symptoms → partial meniscectomy ± repair
       └─ Clicking only → nonop, close follow-up
```

| After meniscal surgery (Orthobullets) | Rate |
|---|---|
| **Saphenous neuropathy** | **7%** — commonest |
| **Arthrofibrosis** | **6%** |
| Sterile effusion | 2% |
| Peroneal neuropathy | 1% |
| Superficial infection | 1% |
| Deep infection | 1% |
