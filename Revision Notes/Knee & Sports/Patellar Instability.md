# Patellar Instability

*Knee & Sports · 37 / 61*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Incidence** — **2–3% of all knee injuries** (Orthobullets; 2–3% of knee presentations, TO) · first-time dislocation **5.8 / 100,000 / yr** · recurrence risk 3.8 / 100,000

**Who** — **2nd–3rd decade** · usually 10–25 yr (OB) · **10–17 yr** high risk (TO) · young active females

**Hemarthrosis** — acute dislocation = **2nd most common cause of traumatic knee hemarthrosis**

**Recurrence after 1st dislocation** — **15–60%** (Orthobullets) · **17–49%** → **up to 70% after a 2nd dislocation** (TO) · **\~1/3** (OB) · 15–50% at 2–5 yr (OB) — read the options

### Risk factors for first & recurrent dislocation (age, prior episode, trochlear dysplasia, laxity)

| Risk factor | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Previous instability event** | **Strongest predictor** of persistent instability (✗ Wrong options: younger age · ↑ Q-angle · male · lateral tilt) |
| **Age < 20 at 1st dislocation** | ↑ recurrence · **age > 20 = protective** |
| Recurrence predictors (OB) | **Age, open physes, patella alta, trochlear dysplasia, ↑ TT-TG** |
| NOT recurrence predictors (OB) | **MPFL rupture**, **female sex**, CD 1.1 (normal), **TT-TG < 12 mm** |
| Contralateral knee | High + lateral patella, lateral hypermobility, dysplastic VMO → ↑ risk (congenital) |
| **Miserable malalignment** | **Femoral anteversion + genu valgum + external tibial torsion / pronated feet → ↑ Q-angle** |
| Osseous | Patella alta, trochlear dysplasia, lateral tilt (in extension), **LFC hypoplasia** |
| Muscle | **VMO weakness** · overpull of **ITB + vastus lateralis** |
| Systemic | Laxity — **Ehlers-Danlos**, **Marfan**, family history |
| Anatomic cut-offs (TO) | **CDI ≥ 1.2**, **patellar tilt ≥ 20°**, **TT-TG ≥ 20 mm**, trochlear dysplasia |

**Predisposing EXCEPT** — **insufficiency of the vastus lateralis** · VMO insufficiency + tight lateral retinaculum = predisposing\
✗ Wrong options: excess femoral IR · external tibial rotation · LFC hypoplasia · ↑ Q-angle

**Beighton 6/9, hip IR 70°, genu valgum, patella alta** — **patellar instability**\
✗ Wrong options: ACL rupture · patellar tendon rupture · MFC osteochondral lesion (dislocation gives **lateral** condyle lesions) · lateral bucket-handle

**Sex** — females ↑ **incidence** (↑ Q-angle) but female sex **not** a predictor of recurrence (OB)

### MPFL anatomy & function (Schöttle point, restraint 0–30°)

**Most important static stabiliser (TO)** — **MPFL** in a structurally normal knee\
✗ Wrong options: VMO · Q-angle · TT:TG ratio

| Stabiliser (TO) | Structures |
|---|---|
| **Static** | Trochlear shape (lateral edge elevated, contains patella after 30°), patellar shape / position, medial retinaculum, **MPFL** |
| **Dynamic** | **Vastus medialis (VMO)** — attaches to MPFL; stabilises in early flexion |
| **Deeper flexion (> 30°)** | **Bony** — trochlear morphology, patellar height, tracking |

**MPFL size** — 4.5–6.4 cm × 1.9 cm · **208 N** tensile strength

**MPFL (TO, Mohindra)** — **60–70%** of restraint to lateral translation · lies in **layer II** of medial knee (Warren & Marshall; between medial retinaculum / layer I and capsule / layer III) · also patellomeniscal + patellotibial ligaments

**Femoral origin** — **between medial epicondyle and adductor tubercle** ("saddle") · **proximal to sMCL** · proximal + posterior to medial epicondyle · **anterior + distal to adductor tubercle**\
✗ Wrong options: on adductor tubercle = adductor magnus · distal to sMCL · lateral wall of MFC = PCL

**sMCL femoral attachment** — \~3.2 mm proximal, 4.8 mm posterior to medial epicondyle (OB)

**Patellar insertion** — **junction of proximal + middle thirds of medial border** (Orthobullets) · **proximal half** of medial border (OB) · undersurface of VMO

**Function** — **primary passive restraint to lateral patellar translation 0–30°** (Orthobullets, TO, OB) · **0–20°** (other OB, PF-joint note) — read the options · lax in deeper flexion

**Function traps** — valgus at 30° = **MCL** · ER + posterior tibial translation = **POL** (AMRI) · knee flexion = **gastrocnemius** · hip adduction = **adductor magnus**

**Schöttle point (lateral X-ray)** — **1 mm anterior to line extended from posterior cortex of shaft** · **2.5 mm distal** to **adductor tubercle** (Orthobullets) · to **posterior origin of MFC** (OB) · **proximal to posterior point of Blumensaat's line** · true lateral with **both posterior condyles superimposed** · = **isometric point** of the graft (OB)

![Schöttle point — MPFL femoral attachment](../../MCQ/attachments/q3611-illa-schottle-lines.jpg)

**Schöttle traps** — ACL femoral = posterior + distal to Blumensaat (in notch) · PCL = lateral wall MFC, posterior to Blumensaat · LCL = lateral femoral condyle

**Paediatric** — femoral fixation **more anterior / distal** to Schöttle point (physis)

### Patellofemoral mechanics – contact, sulcus angle, Q-angle (TO)

**Maximum PF contact (TO)** — **90°** · contact area ↑ 30° → 90° · **> 90°** quadriceps tendon contacts trochlea · **90–135° odd facet** contacts MFC · **45°** in the Orthobullets PF-joint note — read the options\
✗ Wrong options: 30° · 60° · 120°

**PFJRF (TO, this topic)** — **level walking < stair ascent < stair descent < running < squat < deep squat < jumping** · **descent > ascent** · maximal in deep squat + jumping · the PF-joint topic's TO places **jumping just after walking** — read the options

**Q-angle** — **ASIS → centre of patella → tibial tubercle** · **14° M / 17° F** (TO) · 14° M / 18° F (OB) · 13° M / 18° F (Orthobullets PF joint) · **measure in slight flexion** (full extension = falsely normal, patella unengaged)

**↑ Q-angle (TO)** — **↑ femoral anteversion**, **external tibial torsion**, **lateral tibial tubercle**, **genu valgum**\
✗ Wrong options: medialised tuberosity · internal tibial torsion · genu varum

**Standing alignment (TO)** — < 11° normal · **≥ 20° = risk factor needing correction**

### Patellofemoral pain risk factors (TO)

**Risk factors (TO)** — flat / pronated feet, **gastrocnemius tightness**, **patellar instability without laxity**, excessive femoral anteversion + tibia vara

**EXCEPT (TO)** — **ligamentous laxity without patellar instability** (not necessarily painful)

## 2 Mechanism

**Commonest** — **noncontact twisting, knee extended, foot externally rotated** (external tibial rotation, foot fixed)

**Reduction** — reflex **quadriceps contraction** reduces patella → **osteochondral fracture as patella relocates**

**Less common** — **direct blow to medial knee** (knee-to-knee, helmet)

**Impact** — **medial patellar facet** strikes **lateral trochlear ridge / LFC** → bone bruises at both

## 3 Presentation

**Symptoms** — instability, anterior knee pain, painful **"pop" / "clunk"**, often **spontaneous reduction**

**Acute signs** — **large hemarthrosis** · **medial tenderness over MPFL** / medial facet / medial epicondyle · no swelling → laxity / habitual

**Patellar translation** — quadrants (midline = 0) vs other side · **normal < 2** · medial border to lateral trochlear edge = **2 quadrants = abnormal** · acute dislocation: **> 2 quadrants at 30°**

**Apprehension** — guarding with passive lateral translation · **moving patellar apprehension test 0–45°** most sensitive in chronic / lax knees (TO)

**Medial apprehension (TO)** — after **over-aggressive lateral release**, **over-tensioned MPFL**, **overcorrected tibial tubercle**

**J sign** — lateral in extension → "pops" into groove in early flexion (inverted J) · **associated with patella alta**

**Glide / tilt (TO)** — glide **> 3 quadrants** lateral = excessive · **tilt test** = cannot lift lateral border off femur (tight lateral retinaculum) · apprehension at **20–30°** · risk cut-offs: **TT-TG normal < 15 mm, pathological > 20 mm** · **Q angle > 20° (females)**

**Tight lateral retinaculum (TO)** — **cannot evert patella to neutral** (parallel to table) · repeat at **EUA** → decides **lateral lengthening**

**Beighton (TO)** — thumb to forearm, MCP hyperextension, elbow hyperextension, knee recurvatum (bilateral) + palms to floor → **/9**

**Rotational exam (TO)** — prone femoral anteversion (greater trochanter parallel to floor) + **thigh-foot axis**

**Aspiration** — tense effusion · **fat globules → fracture**

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Study | Finding / use |
|---|---|
| X-ray | Exclude fracture / loose body — **medial patellar facet (most common)**, LFC |
| AP (± scannogram, TO) | **Malalignment**, OA, angular deformity |
| **Lateral** | **Trochlear dysplasia** + **patellar height** · **Blumensaat line → inferior pole at 30°** |
| **Sunrise / Merchant** | **Lateral tilt**, lateral PF angle, congruence + sulcus angles |
| **CT** | **TT-TG** |
| **MRI** | Loose bodies, **bone bruise medial patella + LFC**, **MPFL tear**, TT-PCL, trochlear depth |

**Bone bruise — dislocation** — **medial patellar facet + anterior / lateral LFC**\
✗ Wrong options: patellar tendon / quadriceps rupture · ACL · PCL · LCL

**Bone bruise — ACL** — LFC at **sulcus terminalis (central)** + **posterior lateral tibial plateau**; patella spared

**Trochlear dysplasia (TO)** — present in **96%** with objective dislocation · trochlear groove normally **0.8 mm posterior** to anterior femoral cortex line

| Lateral X-ray sign | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Crossing sign** | Trochlear floor reaches anterior border of condyles → **flat groove** at that point; **96%** of true dislocations |
| **Double contour** | Anterior LFC border **anterior** to MFC border → convex groove / **hypoplastic medial facet** |
| **Supratrochlear spur** | Proximal (superolateral) trochlea → **more severe** dysplasia |

**Trochlear depth (TO)** — **< 3 mm** on axial MRI = dysplasia (sensitive + specific)

### Patellar instability classification

| Type | Features |
|---|---|
| **Acute traumatic** | M = F; may follow direct blow |
| **Chronic patholaxity** | Recurrent subluxation; **more in women**; malalignment |
| **Habitual** | **Painless**, every flexion; **proximal** cause (tight ITB, vastus lateralis); no effusion |

**Types (TO)** — **recurrent = commonest form** in practice · habitual = dislocates every flexion, relocates in extension · **congenital** = from birth, severe bony dysplasia · acute = first-time with haemarthrosis

### Patellar height

| Index | Measure | Normal | Alta | Baja |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Blumensaat** | Lateral at **30°** | Inferior pole at line | Above | Below |
| **Insall-Salvati** | **Patellar tendon length ÷ patellar length** | **0.8–1.2** | **> 1.2** | **< 0.8** |
| **Blackburne-Peel** | Plateau line → lower articular margin ÷ articular length | **0.5–1.0** | **> 1.0** | **< 0.5** |
| **Caton-Deschamps** | Lower articular margin → anterior tibial plateau ÷ articular length | **0.6–1.3** (\~1) | **> 1.3** (TO) · **≥ 1.2** risk / **> 1.2** surgery (TO) | **< 0.6** |
| Plateau-patella angle | — | **20–30°** | — | — |

**Insall-Salvati (TO)** — **ratio of patellar tendon to patella** (✗ Wrong options: patella to tendon) · **> 1.2 = alta** (✗ Wrong options: baja) · tendon measured along its **posterior surface**, lower pole → tibial insertion

**Best index (TO)** — **Caton-Deschamps "best"** · Blackburne-Peel **more accurate than IS** (lower articular margin easier to define)

**Best index — conflict** — **Insall–Salvati = best indicator of patellar height** (TO, Mohindra notes) · Caton-Deschamps (TO, above) — read the options

### Axial & rotational measurements

| Measure | Normal | Abnormal / meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Lateral PF angle** | **> 11°** (Orthobullets) · **> 0°, opens laterally in 97%** (TO) | **< 11°** = excess tilt (OB) · subluxation: **parallel 60%**, opens medially 40% |
| **Congruence angle** | **−6°** (medial = negative) | **> +16°** (lateral) → subluxation |
| **Sulcus angle** | **138–142°** (TO) · Merchant **138° ± 6** · **130–145°** (TO answer) · **137° ± 8°** (OB) | **> 140°** (Orthobullets, OB) · **> 145°** (OB, Dejour A) · **> 150°** (TO) — read the options |
| **Patellar tilt** | **< 5°** (OB) | **> 20°** → tight lateral retinaculum, lateral release / lengthening |
| **TT-TG (CT)** | **9–13 mm** (Orthobullets) · **8–10 mm** (OB) | **> 20 mm** highly associated with instability · **< 12 mm** not a risk factor |
| **TT-PCL (MRI, TO)** | **11.9 ± 4.67 mm** | **> 20 mm** pathological · **> 24** in TO procedure table |

**TT-TG** — perpendiculars from posterior condylar line to **tibial tubercle** and **trochlear groove** · highly specific for recurrence (TO)

**TT-PCL (TO)** — tubercle midpoint → **medial border of PCL** · tibial references only → **negates femoral rotation**

### Dejour classification of trochlear dysplasia

| Type | Lateral X-ray | Axial | Trochleoplasty |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | Crossing sign | Shallow, symmetric, concave · sulcus **> 145°** | **None** — not high-grade |
| **B** | Crossing + **supratrochlear spur** | **Flat or convex** | **Sulcus-deepening** |
| **C** | Crossing + **double contour** | **Convex lateral facet + hypoplastic medial** | no deepening (no spur) · lateral facet elevating (controversial) |
| **D** | Crossing + **spur + double contour** | **Cliff** (vertical asymmetric facets) | **Sulcus-deepening** |

**Severity** — **A + C mild** · **B + D severe** (spur = what is resected)

**Dejour — TO lecture wording** — A shallow trochlea · B flat + spur · C asymmetric facets, lateral convexity · D **cliff** + spur · **higher grade → higher recurrence** · Dejour C / D → trochleoplasty (TO) vs B / D (above) — read the options

## 5 Management

```
Lateral patellar dislocation
├─ First-time, MRI: no fragment / loose body → nonoperative: brace + PT
├─ First-time + osteochondral fracture / loose body → arthroscopic removal vs fixation ± MPFL repair
│      (fragment on X-ray → MRI before surgery)
├─ Recurrent, normal bony anatomy → isolated MPFL reconstruction
└─ Recurrent + bony risk factor → MPFLR + TTO / distalisation / trochleoplasty
```

### Nonoperative care

**Indications** — **first-time dislocator without loose body / intra-articular damage** · **habitual dislocator**

**Protocol** — short immobilisation → **6 wk controlled motion** (Orthobullets) · **2 wk brace locked at 0°** → ROM brace (TO)

**First-time protocol (TO, Mohindra)** — immobilise **in extension 3 weeks** → quads / VMO rehab · MRI for osteochondral fragment / MPFL · read the options vs 2 wk / 6 wk above

**Rehab** — **closed chain short arc quads**, core + hip (abductors, gluteals) · patellar sleeve / **"J" brace**

**First dislocation (TO)** — **operative only for osteochondral fracture** · recurrence = secondary indication\
✗ Wrong options: MPFL reconstruction · always conservative · MPFL + retinaculum repair

**Surgical indication after 1st dislocation (TO)** — **X-ray shows osteochondral fracture**\
✗ Wrong options: complete MPFL rupture on MRI · MPFL laxity · grade B dysplasia

**13-yr-old female, no prior history of knee injury, MRI findings Figure A (MPFL rupture, no fracture / loose body)** — **bracing + PT**\
✗ Wrong options: recurrent + TT-TG 26 → distal realignment · Figure B: MPFL rupture + medial facet fracture + loose bodies → arthroscopy · recurrent despite PT → MPFLR

### Acute dislocation – MRI findings, osteochondral fragments & initial management

**Most consistent injury (TO)** — **MPFL tear**\
✗ Wrong options: patellotibial / patellomeniscal ligaments · LFC trochlea fracture · VMO disruption

**MPFL tear site** — **patellar insertion** commonest (Orthobullets) · **femoral attachment** common avulsion site (OB) · **soft-tissue avulsion** most common pattern; midsubstance + soft-tissue avulsion > **bony avulsion** (OB) — read the options

**MRI after dislocation (TO)** — contusion pair **LFC + inferomedial patella** (axials) · fragments may lie in **lateral suprapatellar or posterior recesses** · repeated episodes → MPFL / medial retinaculum **thick, separated from bone by fluid cleft** (insufficient) · **TT-TG on axials**: bicondylar line, perpendicular through deepest trochlear groove → most prominent tibial tuberosity (PT-TG uses patellar tendon midpoint)

**Fragment origin** — **medial patellar facet (most common)** > **LFC** / lateral trochlear ridge · **transverse patella fracture not associated**\
✗ Wrong options: lateral facet · odd facet · medial / central trochlea

![Medial patellar facet osteochondral fragment after dislocation](../../MCQ/attachments/q211667-c-img-5547-2.jpg)

**Radiograph after self-reduced dislocation, 3 quadrants** — **Figure C = medial patellar facet fragment**\
✗ Wrong options: A lateral facet fracture · B knee dislocation · D LFC fracture (less common) · E inferior pole / transverse fracture

**Fragment on X-ray + large effusion** — **next = magnetic resonance imaging (MRI)**\
✗ Wrong options: NSAIDs + PT · aspiration + cortisone · ORIF · arthroscopy before MRI

**Fragments missed on X-ray** — **up to 40%** of first dislocations have large fragments → MRI

**12-yr-old, 1st dislocation, large fragment in lateral recess + partial MPFL tear on MRI** — **arthroscopic loose body removal versus osteochondral fragment fixation**\
✗ Wrong options: aspirate + brace · PT · immediate isolated MPFLR · TTO + MPFLR ± trochleoplasty

**Fixation** — screws / pins if enough bone; else removal (arthroscopic or open)

**Vertical intra-articular patella fracture after dislocation (TO)** — **internal fixation (ORIF)**\
✗ Wrong options: arthroscopy · cylinder cast · patellectomy

| Patella fracture (TO) | Rule |
|---|---|
| Commonest mechanism | **Direct impaction on flexed knee**; indirect = eccentric quads contraction |
| **Nonop** | Displacement **< 4 mm**, step-off **< 2 mm**, **active SLR intact** → brace \~10° flexion, neutral rotation (avoid baja) |
| **Surgery** | Displacement **> 4 mm** and / or step-off **> 2 mm**, extensor disruption |
| Other ORIF | Open fracture, articular gap **> 3 mm**, loose bodies, **osteochondral fractures**, paediatric **sleeve fracture** |

### MPFL repair vs reconstruction

**MPFL repair** — **acute first-time dislocation with bony fragment** / avulsion with intact midsubstance · within first few days · no studies prove it beats nonop · not after many dislocations (attenuated tissue)

**MPFL reconstruction** — **recurrent instability without significant malalignment** · autograft or allograft

**Graft** — **gracilis or semitendinosus** (stronger than native), two-tailed · **open physes → medial quadriceps turndown on suture anchors** (no femoral tunnel, TO)

**Graft choice** — **all equal rates of postoperative instability** (allograft = autograft = quadriceps = synthetic) · infection + PROs also equal

**MPFLR technique (TO)** — **ipsilateral gracilis most preferred** · superficial quadriceps technique (TO: "Konkani") · femoral point between **adductor tubercle + medial epicondyle** · **fix at 30–45° flexion** (TO) vs 30° (OB) · graft tight only on lateral translation · **always diagnostic arthroscopy first** · **overtightening → medial facet overload**

**Complications (TO)** — knee stiffness, **patella fracture**, continued instability · femoral graft position is critical

### MPFL reconstruction – femoral tunnel malposition

| Femoral tunnel | Result |
|---|---|
| **Too proximal ("high and tight")** | **Graft tight in flexion** → loss of flexion / failure, overconstrains medially |
| **Too distal ("low and loose")** | Graft **loose in flexion** |
| Too posterior | Not tighter in flexion than a proximal tunnel |
| **Schöttle point** | Correct — anteroproximal quadrant |

**Proximal tunnel traps** — ✗ Wrong options: tight in extension (minimal MPFL tension in extension) · lateral tilt · patella baja · cause = **cam-shaped distal femur**

**Tension** — at **30° flexion** (OB)

### Operative criteria

| Finding | Procedure |
|---|---|
| **Recurrent, no significant malalignment** | **Isolated MPFLR** |
| **TT-TG > 20 mm (CT)** · TT-PCL > 24 | **TTO** (AMZ / Elmslie-Trillat) ± MPFLR |
| **Patella alta, CDI > 1.2** | **Distalisation** ± MPFLR |
| **Dejour B or D** | **Trochleoplasty + MPFLR** ± TTO |
| Dejour A or C | MPFLR ± TTO |
| **Femoral anteversion > 25°** | **Femoral derotation osteotomy + MPFLR** |
| **Lateral tilt > 20°** / tight lateral retinaculum | Lateral release (TO) / **lengthening** (OB) + MPFLR |
| **Genu valgum > 10°**, ≥ 6 mo growth | **Guided growth** (8-plate, staples) |

### Surgical selection – isolated MPFL vs tibial tubercle osteotomy (TT-TG) vs trochleoplasty

**Isolated MPFLR (TO)** — **TT-TG ≤ 20 mm**, **normal or Dejour A trochlea**, **CDI < 1.2**, **tilt < 20°**

**Isolated MPFLR (OB)** — **IS < 1.2**, **TT-TG < 20 mm**, **sulcus < 140°**, **negative crossing sign** · e.g. **Insall-Salvati ratio 1.15, TT-TG distance 15 mm, sulcus angle 138°, negative crossing sign**\
✗ Wrong options: sulcus 150° + crossing · TT-TG 21 · IS 1.3 · IS 1.25 + TT-TG 20 + sulcus 150°

**Best isolated-MPFLR candidate** — **recurrent patellar dislocation, 3 quadrants lateral translation at 30 degrees knee flexion, presence of J sign, TT-TG of 15mm**\
✗ Wrong options: first-time (surgery controversial) · TT-TG 24 → consider TTO

**Residual instability after isolated MPFLR** — **severe trochlear dysplasia = most important predictor** · failure risks: dysplasia, ↑ TT-TG, **age < 20** · IS ratio + tibial slope **not** factors

**14-yr-old recurrent, TT-TG 20, sulcus 150°** — **presence of trochlear dysplasia places him at risk for recurrent instability**\
✗ Wrong options: MPFLR alone poor (it does well) · IS 1.2 protective (normal) · age protective · slope 15°

**Recurrent, TT-TG 12, CDI 1.2, no dysplasia, 7 dislocations** — **MPFL reconstruction alone**\
✗ Wrong options: MPFL repair (attenuated) · TTO medialisation / distalisation · trochleoplasty

**3rd dislocation, mild valgus, CDI 1.2, TT-TG 14.5 mm, mild dysplasia, wants fast RTS** — **MPFL reconstruction**\
✗ Wrong options: MPFL repair · TTO alone · MPFLR + TTO (longer recovery, no proven benefit) · lateral release

**TT-TG 20 exactly** — isolated MPFLR still good · some add TTO only if **> 20** · TTO importance controversial

**TTO threshold** — usually **not** done if TT-TG < 15–20 mm (e.g. 13 mm → no TTO)

### Tibial tubercle osteotomy (Fulkerson / AMZ)

**Fulkerson** — oblique osteotomy → tubercle **anterior + medial** (anteromedialisation, AMZ) · medialisation restores TT-TG, anteriorisation **unloads PF** · cut **long (> 5 cm) + thick (> 0.75 cm)** for **2 screws** (OB)

**Indication (OB)** — **TT-TG > 20 mm** (e.g. **25mm**) + recurrent instability ± cartilage restoration\
✗ Wrong options: open apophysis · medial defect · sulcus 165° · tilt 25°

**Indication (TO)** — **grade 3 and 4 chondromalacia patella and recurrent patellar dislocation** (**lateral facet or distal pole** arthritis) + TT-TG > 20 mm\
✗ Wrong options: habitual dislocation · grade 1–2 · Osgood-Schlatter

**Contact pressures after AMZ** — **↑ proximal & medial** → **proximal / medial facet arthritis = contraindication** · medial patellar defect = relative contraindication\
✗ Wrong options: distal · lateral

**Correction target** — **TT-TG 10–15 mm, never < 10** (Orthobullets) · **\~10 mm** (TO) · TO: medialisation = TT-TG − 20 mm

**Other TTOs (TO)** — **Elmslie-Trillat = direct medial** · **Maquet = direct anterior** · TTO when **TT-TG > 20**, ↑ TT-PCL, **CDI > 1.2**

**Distal realignment (TO)** — **medial shift → TT-TG > 20 mm** · **distal shift → patella alta** · **AMZ (Fulkerson)** → tracking + ↓ contact pressure · **Roux–Goldthwait** adjunct · proximal realignment = MPFLR (cornerstone), VMO advancement, lateral release

**Distalisation (TO)** — for **CDI > 1.2** → target **CDI 1** on C-arm · usually **\~10 mm** bone removed

**Open tibial apophysis** — **absolute contraindication** → growth arrest → **recurvatum** · paediatric: **preserve physis**

**TTO complications (TO)** — **tuberosity nonunion**, painful hardware, **tibial shaft fracture** · longer recovery

**Skin approach** — midline incision → **infrapatellar branch of saphenous nerve** → inferolateral numbness

### Trochleoplasty

**Best suited (TO)** — **Dejour type B and D** (spur)\
✗ Wrong options: A + B · B + C · C + D

**Indications (TO)** — **high-grade dysplasia** + instability / maltracking, **no established OA** · revision with residual instability · rarely done in USA

**Not for (TO)** — **flatness alone** (spur must be present) · type A (no trochlear procedure) · type C (lateral facet elevating instead)

**Contraindications (TO)** — **open physes**, **PF arthrosis**

**Complications (TO)** — **arthrofibrosis / stiffness (most frequent)** · **trochlear shingle fracture** propagation (brittle subchondral bone in PF arthritis)

**Techniques** — arthroscopic or open sulcus deepening · open recession wedge

**Recurrent + Dejour D (cliff + spur)** — **MPFL reconstruction with trochleoplasty**\
✗ Wrong options: MPFL repair ± VMO · TTO alone · MPFLR alone (residual instability)

### Lateral release – lateral patellofemoral angle

**Isolated lateral release** — **not indicated for instability** → **iatrogenic medial instability**

**Lateral lengthening vs release** — lengthening: **better outcomes**, less quadriceps atrophy, **less medial instability** · only for **excess tilt / tightness after medialisation**

**2nd dislocation, 3 quadrants, J sign, tight lateral retinaculum, TT-TG 13, CDI 1.15** — **MPFL reconstruction + lateral retinacular lengthening**\
✗ Wrong options: isolated release / lengthening · MPFLR + release · AMZ TTO

**Main indication for isolated release** — **lateral patellar (facet) compression syndrome**, no instability

**Measurement for lateral release** — **lateral patello-femoral angle** (**negative tilt**) on CT / sunrise\
✗ Wrong options: congruence angle · Q-angle · sulcus angle · patellar height

**Congruence + sulcus angles** — assess **malalignment + instability**, not release

### Other bony procedures

**Guided growth** — **genu valgum > 10°** + instability + **≥ 6 months growth** · tension band **8-plate** or **staples** (more rigid, faster)

**Femoral osteotomy (TO)** — severe valgus → distal femoral osteotomy · **anteversion > 25°** → supracondylar derotation + MPFLR · hemiepiphysiodesis in immature valgus

## 6 Complications

| Complication | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Recurrent dislocation** | Nonop **15–60% at 2–5 yr** · highest if 1st dislocation **< 20 yr** |
| **Medial dislocation + medial PF arthritis** | **Almost exclusively iatrogenic** (prior stabilisation) |
| Inferolateral numbness | **Infrapatellar branch of saphenous nerve** (TTO midline incision) |
| MPFLR | Stiffness, patella fracture, recurrence, high-and-tight tunnel |
