# Posterolateral Corner Injury

*Knee & Sports · 45 / 61*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Incidence** — **7–16%** of knee ligament injuries (Orthobullets) · **10–15%** (OB) — read the options

**Isolated** — **only 28%** isolated (Orthobullets) · **< 5%** isolated (OB) — read the options · usually combined with cruciate (**PCL > ACL**)

**Missed PLC** — **common cause of ACL (and PCL) reconstruction failure** · often missed (subtle exam + imaging)

### Anatomy – LCL, popliteus, biceps femoris

**3 major static stabilisers** — **LCL (FCL)**, **popliteus tendon**, **popliteofibular ligament (PFL)**

**Other static** — lateral capsule thickening (meniscofemoral + meniscotibial ligaments) · arcuate ligament (variable) · fabellofibular ligament (variable)

**Dynamic** — **biceps femoris**, popliteus muscle, **ITB**, **lateral head of gastrocnemius**

**Lateral quadruple complex (TO)** — **ITB + LCL + popliteus tendon + biceps femoris**\
✗ Wrong options: fabellofibular ligament · popliteofibular ligament

**Arcuate complex** — LCL + arcuate ligament + popliteus tendon

**Full PLC (OB, Covey)** — ITB, LCL, popliteus complex (dynamic MTU; static PFL, popliteotibial + popliteomeniscal fascicles), middle third lateral capsular ligament, fabellofibular, arcuate, posterior horn LM, lateral coronary ligament, posterolateral capsule

| Structure | Femur | Fibula / tibia | Key fact |
|---|---|---|---|
| **LCL** | LFC, **proximal + posterior to popliteus** | **Most anterior** on fibular head · **anterolateral to PFL** | Runs **superficial to popliteus** · primary varus restraint |
| **Popliteus tendon** | **Distal + anterior to LCL** (✗ Wrong options: proximal) · through LM hiatus | Muscle → posteromedial tibia | **Dynamic internal rotator of tibia** |
| **PFL** | Popliteus musculotendinous junction | **Medial to LCL** on fibular head (✗ Wrong options: lateral) | Anterior + posterior divisions |
| **Biceps femoris** | — | **Posterior to LCL** on fibular head | Long head: direct arm posterolateral, anterior arm lateral to LCL · short head: superior fibular head, medial + posterior to LCL |

**Fibular insertion order (anterior → posterior)** — **LCL → PFL → biceps femoris**

**Biceps–capsulo-osseous ITB confluence** — injury correlates with **positive Lachman** (Terry & LaPrade)

**PMC distractors** — **posterior oblique ligament** (from adductor tubercle region, posterior + proximal to MCL) + deep MCL (meniscofemoral + meniscotibial) → **medial**; no positive dial

| Lateral layer | Structures |
|---|---|
| **I** | Iliotibial tract, biceps |
| — | **Common peroneal nerve between layers I and II** |
| **II** | Patellar retinaculum, patellofemoral ligament |
| **III superficial** | **LCL**, fabellofibular ligament |
| — | Lateral geniculate artery between superficial + deep III |
| **III deep** | Arcuate, coronary ligament, **popliteus tendon**, **PFL**, capsule |

### Function

**Popliteus + PCL** — synergistic → control **external tibial rotation, varus, posterior tibial translation**

**Popliteus primary action** — **internal tibial rotation** · popliteus complex reinforces posterior third of lateral capsule; restrains ER, posterior translation, varus\
✗ Wrong options: ER · posterior / anterior translation

**Popliteus + PFL** — maximal **in flexion** → resist ER

**LCL** — primary varus restraint at **5° (55%)** and **25° (69%)** (PLC note) · **5° and 30°** (LCL note) — read the options

## 2 Mechanism

**Mechanisms** — **blow to anteromedial knee** · **varus blow to flexed knee** · contact / non-contact **hyperextension** · **ER twisting** · knee dislocation · varus force + ER component (OB)

**Associated** — **common peroneal nerve 15–29%** (Orthobullets etiology) · **\~25%** (Orthobullets exam) · vascular injury

## 3 Presentation

**Symptoms** — instability **in full extension** · difficulty reciprocating stairs, pivoting, cutting · lateral + posterior pain

**Gait** — standing **varus**, **varus thrust** or hyperextension thrust

**Peroneal nerve** — ↓ sensation dorsum of foot + weak ankle dorsiflexion

**PLRI** — **lateral tibial plateau moves posterior to femoral condyle** on rotational stress → **posterolateral rotatory instability** · instability named by tibial position relative to femur\
✗ Wrong options: AMRI · ALRI · PMRI

### Diagnosis – dial test, reverse pivot shift & PLRI

| Test | Technique | Positive means |
|---|---|---|
| **Varus stress 30°** | — | **LCL** |
| **Varus stress 0°** | — | **LCL + cruciate (ACL or PCL)** |
| **Dial test** | Prone or supine; 30° + 90° | **> 10° ER at 30° only → isolated PLC** · **30° + 90° → PLC + PCL** |
| **ER recurvatum** | Supine, suspend legs by great toes | ↑ recurvatum + varus + **anterior** tibial translation → **ACL + PLC** |
| **Posterolateral drawer** | Hip 45°, knee 80°, foot ER 15° · posterior drawer + ER | ↑ posterolateral translation (lateral tibia ER on LFC) |
| **Reverse pivot shift** | Tibia ER + valgus | Clunk of reduction → **PLC** (PLRI) |
| Posterior drawer | 90° | PCL (negative in isolated PLC) |

**Dial threshold** — **> 10°** (Orthobullets) · **10–15°** (OB) · **15°** (TO supine description) — read the options

**Supine dial (TO)** — leg over bed edge, stabilise distal femur, hold heel, max ER at 30° → **tibial tuberosity position** vs other side → repeat at 90°

**Prone dial (TO)** — both feet rotated at 30° then 90° · angle between **sagittal plane of sole and body** (thigh–foot angle)

**Prone preferred (Jung)** — dial more clearly positive when **posterior sag is reduced**

**Other PLC tests (TO)** — ER recurvatum · posterolateral drawer · reverse pivot shift

**Dial ↑ at 30° + 90° — least likely torn?** — **posterior oblique ligament** (medial)\
✗ Wrong options: FCL · popliteus · PCL · arcuate

**+ Reverse pivot, negative posterior drawer** — PLC without PCL → expect **10° ↑ ER at 30°**\
✗ Wrong options: at 90° · valgus opening · anterior drawer

**LaPrade (OB)** — reverse pivot shift ↔ injury to **LCL, popliteal components, mid-third lateral capsular ligament** · LCL injury ↔ ↑ ER at 30° · **Gollehon: sectioning LCL → 20° ↑ ER at 30°**

| Pivot tests (TO) | Movement | Instability |
|---|---|---|
| **Pivot shift (Macintosh)** | **Extension → flexion**, tibia **IR** + valgus · tibia relocates posteriorly | **Anterolateral** (ACL + lateral capsule / ALL) |
| **Jerk test (Hughston & Losse)** | **Flexion → extension** (reverse of pivot shift) | **Anterolateral** |
| **Reverse pivot shift** | Tibia **ER** + valgus; clunk | **Posterolateral** (PLC, ± PCL) |

**Reverse pivot direction** — start at **90°**, ER + valgus, **extend** → tibia reduces from posterior subluxation at **\~20°** (ITB flexor → extensor) (Orthobullets) · knee moved **extension → flexion** to feel clunk (TO) — read the options

![Reverse pivot shift: valgus and external rotation](../../MCQ/attachments/TO17119513-2.jpg)

**ER recurvatum (TO)** — historically relative hyperextension + ER of tibial tubercle + varus · **LaPrade: sensitivity 7.5%** in 134 PLC patients · X-ray showed **anterior** tibial translation · **all positives = ACL + PLC** · doesn't identify which PL structures (multiple inferred)\
✗ Wrong options: posterior

**ER recurvatum yield** — identifies **\~10%** of PLC injuries (Orthobullets) · **7.5% sensitivity** (TO) — read the options

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Study | Finding / use |
|---|---|
| **AP X-ray** | **Arcuate sign** = fibular head avulsion (LCL / biceps) · or femoral condyle avulsion |
| **Varus stress X-ray** (bilateral, **20° flexion**) | Side-to-side **2.7–4 mm → isolated LCL** · **> 4 mm → PLC** |
| **Long-leg standing** | **Chronic** PLC → **triple varus**, mechanical axis, need for **proximal tibial osteotomy** |
| **MRI** | LCL, popliteus, biceps · acute **bone bruise MFC + medial tibial plateau** · **coronal oblique thin slices through fibular head** best |
| MRI (sagittal T2) | Posterolateral tibia oedema + **oedema posterior to popliteus tendon** |
| MRI (coronal) | ITB, LCL, popliteus torn · coronary ligament torn from tibia → displaced LM |

**Arcuate sign (OB)** — **highest association with PLC**\
✗ Wrong options: Segond (ALL + ACL) · Schatzker II lateral plateau (varus PLC injury → **medial** plateau injury commoner) · PCL avulsion · tibial spine avulsion (ACL)

**Fragment size (Lee)** — arcuate ligament / PFL → **small styloid** avulsion · LCL / biceps → **larger** fragment · **Huang**: fibular head avulsion predicts **PLC + PCL** injury (13 pts; LCL > popliteus)

![Arcuate fracture (fibular head avulsion)](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob3012-arcuate-fracture.jpg)

| Varus alignment (chronic) | Cause |
|---|---|
| **Primary** | Tibiofemoral malalignment |
| **Secondary** | LCL deficiency → ↑ lateral opening |
| **Triple** | Remaining PLC deficient → varus recurvatum |

### Modified Hughston classification

| Grade | Examination | Findings |
|---|---|---|
| **I** | **0–5 mm** lateral opening on varus stress | Sprain, no tensile failure |
| **II** | **6–10 mm** opening · **6–10°** rotational instability on dial | Partial, moderate disruption |
| **III** | **> 10 mm**, **no endpoint** | Complete disruption |

## 5 Management

```
PLC injury
├─ Grade I or isolated midsubstance grade II → brace locked in extension 4 wk → rehab
├─ Isolated acute grade II avulsion, reducible → repair (augment with graft if tenuous)
├─ Grade III midsubstance / irreparable avulsion → reconstruction (± repair hybrid)
└─ Combined cruciate injury → PLC reconstruction with or before ACL / PCL
       └─ Varus mechanical axis → valgus HTO first (long-leg films)
```

### Operative criteria

| Option | Indication |
|---|---|
| Nonop (brace in extension 4 wk, quad rehab, RTS 8 wk) | **Grade I** · **isolated midsubstance grade II** |
| **Repair** | **Isolated acute grade II avulsion** · structures **anatomically reducible to attachment** |
| Fibular head avulsion | Screws or suture anchors |
| **Reconstruction (hybrid)** | **Grade III midsubstance** · avulsion not repairable / poor tissue |
| **PLC + ACL / PCL reconstruction** | Acute + chronic **combined** injuries |
| **Valgus HTO** | **Varus mechanical alignment** (else ACL + PLC reconstruction fail) |

### Treatment – reconstruction over repair

**Repair → higher failure (OB)** — **midsubstance repair 40% failure** (Orthobullets) · **Levy: 40% repair vs 6% (1 pt) reconstruction** · **Stannard: 37% vs 9%** (57 PLC injuries; both "near-normal" IKDC)\
✗ Wrong options: quicker ROM · better stability · higher short-term scores · worse kinematics

**After revision of failed repair** — outcome scores ≈ primary reconstruction

**ACL reconstruction + PLC repair vs reconstruction** — **33% vs 88%** IKDC A / B

**Outcomes** — operative > nonop · **early treatment better** · anatomic reconstruction restores **rotatory but not all varus** stability

| Reconstruction | Graft / technique | Rebuilds |
|---|---|---|
| **Fibular-based (Larson)** | Soft-tissue graft (semitendinosus / Achilles) through fibular head tunnel → **figure-of-eight** → single femoral tunnel | **LCL + PFL** |
| **Transtibial double-bundle** | Split Achilles to femoral epicondyle isometric point · fibular limb + tibial limb | Fibular limb **LCL** · tibial limb (posterior tibia) **PFL** |
| **LaPrade anatomic** | **Two grafts** · graft 1 femoral LCL site → fibular head (lateral → medial) · graft 2 popliteus site · both dock in tibial tunnel posterior → anterior | **LCL + PFL + popliteus** |

![LaPrade anatomic PLC reconstruction](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob3012-laprade-anatomic-plc-reconstruction.jpg)

### Alignment correction & combined ACL revision

**Sequence** — PLC reconstruction **same time or before** ACL / PCL → prevents early cruciate graft failure

**Missed PLC + ACL graft (OB)** — 3+ Lachman, varus laxity at 30°, dial + at 30° dissipating at 90°, varus thrust → **revision ACL + PLC reconstruction** · unrecognised PCL → medial + PF OA without lateral laxity / thrust\
✗ Wrong options: PCL · PMC · hamstring autograft alone

**ACL + PLC + varus + MM tear (OB)** — HTO + ACL + meniscus + PLC reconstruction; **NOT PCL reconstruction** (quad active negative, normal ER at 90°) · untreated varus thrust → ACL graft fails

**Noyes (staged HTO + ACL, 41 pts, \~50% PLC)** — 4 yr: pain ↓ **71%**, giving way gone **85%**, light recreation **66%**

**Grade 3 PLC laxity + varus after ACL + PCL reconstruction (OB)** — **long-leg alignment films first** → primary varus → **medial opening-wedge proximal tibial osteotomy before PLC reconstruction**\
✗ Wrong options: straight to fibular / tibial-fibular reconstruction · PT · bracing

**Untreated PLC ↑ PCL graft force** — Harner (↑ posterior translation, ER, in-situ graft force) · LaPrade (grade 3 PLC)

### Rehabilitation

**After PLC reconstruction (hybrid)** — hinged brace, **NWB 6 wk** · ROM varies (passive 0–90° at once vs 2 wk immobilisation) · at 6 wk WB + closed-chain · **RTS 6–9 months**

**After combined reconstruction** — immobilise + **protected WB 4 wk** (long leg cast controls ER better than brace) · **passive ROM at 4 wk** (avoid arthrofibrosis) · **avoid active hamstring** · full active extension allowed

**PCL reconstruction + PLC repair (OB)** — **no active flexion, active extension 90→0°** · hinged brace, crutches, partial WB first 4 wk · quad sets + SLR\
✗ Wrong options: patellar tendon repair · ACL ± MCL · ACL + PLC

## 6 Complications

**Missed PLC** — failure of ACL or PCL reconstruction

**Peroneal nerve injury** — **15–29%**

**Arthrofibrosis**

## How it's asked

**"Lateral quadruple complex EXCEPT (TO)?"** → **fabellofibular ligament**

**"NOT part of lateral quadruple complex (TO)?"** → **popliteofibular ligament**

**"LCL relationship in PLC?"** → **inserts anterolateral to PFL on fibula, superficial to popliteus**

**"Normal anatomy of region injured (dial + at 30°)?"** → **biceps femoris inserts posterior to LCL on fibular head**

**"Function of structure at probe tip (popliteus)?"** → **internal tibial rotation**

**"Use of test in image (TO)?"** → **detect PLC injury (dial test)**

**"Positive reverse pivot shift (TO)?"** → **PLC injury**

**"Positive ER recurvatum test (TO)?"** → **combined PLC + ACL injury**

**"Lateral plateau moves posterior to femoral condyle on rotation?"** → **posterolateral rotatory instability**

**"↑ ER at 30° + 90° — least likely torn?"** → **posterior oblique ligament**

**"Finding indicating PLC + PCL?"** → **dial + at 30° and 90°**

**"+ Reverse pivot, negative posterior drawer — other finding?"** → **10° ↑ ER at 30°**

**"Radiograph most associated with PLC injury?"** → **arcuate sign (fibular head avulsion)**

**"Post-op: no active flexion, active extension 90→0°?"** → **PCL reconstruction + PLC repair**

**"Grade 3 PLC laxity + varus after ACL + PCL reconstruction?"** → **long-leg alignment films**

**"Acute PLC repair vs reconstruction?"** → **higher rate of failure**
