# Osgood Schlatter's Disease (Tibial Tubercle Apophysitis)

*Knee & Sports · 33 / 61*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology & pathophysiology

**Definition** — **osteochondrosis / traction apophysitis of the tibial tubercle** → paediatric anterior knee pain

**Who** — **boys > girls** · **bilateral 20–30%** · **jumpers (basketball, volleyball)**, sprinters, soccer, gymnastics, track · non-athletes too

**Age** — boys **12–15 yr** (Orthobullets, OB) · boys **5–12 yr** (OB) · girls **8–12 yr** — read the options

**Cause** — repetitive pull of **extensor mechanism / patellar tendon** · **growth spurt**: bone lengthening outpaces muscle–tendon flexibility → ↑ tension on apophysis

**Patellar tendon strain ratio** — **↓ in OSD** (elastography, Enomoto: 18 vs 42 legs) → **less compliant tendon** → force reaches tibial tubercle **faster**

### Anatomy — tibial tubercle ossification

| Age | Tubercle |
|---|---|
| **< 11 yr** | **Cartilaginous** |
| **11–14 yr** | **Apophysis forms** (secondary ossification centre) |
| 14–18 yr | Apophysis **fuses with tibial epiphysis** |
| **> 18 yr** | Epiphysis + apophysis **fused** to tibia |

## 3 Presentation

**Symptoms** — dull **anterior knee pain**, worse with **kneeling**, jumping, squatting, stairs · often **bilateral**

**Exam** — **enlarged, tender tibial tubercle** · **pain on resisted knee extension** (most painful) · no effusion, no limp, stable knee

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Study | Finding |
|---|---|
| **Lateral knee X-ray** | **Irregularity + fragmentation of tibial tubercle** · early: apophysis **separation** · late: fragmentation + **intratendinous ossicles** · may be normal |
| X-ray role | Diagnosis is **clinical**; films rule out other causes |
| MRI | **Not essential** · soft-tissue swelling, **thickened oedematous inferior patellar tendon**, fragmented ossification centre |
| Mature patient | **Persistent ossicle** after tibial epiphysis fuses |

### Diagnosis & differential (Sinding-Larsen-Johansson, sleeve fracture)

| Condition | Distinguishing fact |
|---|---|
| **Osgood-Schlatter** | **Tibial tubercle** tenderness, chronic, walks normally |
| **Sinding-Larsen-Johansson** | Chronic **apophysitis / minor avulsion of inferior patellar pole**; **10–14 yr**, esp. **cerebral palsy** |
| **Patellar sleeve avulsion** | **Inferior pole** tenderness, cortical elevation of inferior patella, **can't walk** |
| **Tibial tubercle avulsion fracture** | **Acute**, ambulation difficulty |
| Tibial spine avulsion | Acute, can't bear weight, big effusion, **Lachman +** |
| **Bipartite patella** | Normal variant (failed fusion of 2 ossification centres); **painless, incidental** |
| Others | Jumper's knee, **proximal tibial osteochondroma**, Lyme (isolated effusion), plica, fat-pad impingement, infection, malignancy |

## 5 Management

```
Osgood-Schlatter
├─ First line → NSAIDs, rest, ice, activity modification, strapping, quads stretching
│      └─ severe, not responding → CAST 6 WEEKS
└─ Skeletally mature + persistent symptoms (~10%) → OSSICLE EXCISION ± TUBERCLEPLASTY
```

**Nonop outcome** — **90% complete resolution** · **self-limiting** but **resolves only when growth stops** (physeal closure)

**Persistent pain** — **60.5%** still had knee pain at median **3.75 yr** (Guldhammer) → "self-limited" dogma questioned

**Cast complication** — **quadriceps wasting**

### Persistent symptomatic ossicle — excision

**Surgery indications** — pain localised to tubercle, limits activity, **failed nonop**, **persists into skeletal maturity**

**Procedure** — **ossicle excision + tubercleplasty** (resect prominence) → **relieves symptoms, return to sport expected**\
✗ Wrong options: never indicated · prednisolone injection · hamstring lengthening · RTS unlikely

| Surgical outcome (Pihlajamäki) | Result |
|---|---|
| 178 military recruits, 10 yr | **85% no pain** at operative site · **75% back to full activity** |
| 107 patients (117 knees), median 10 yr | Good Kujala / VAS, no long-term harm · but **39% still pain on kneeling** |
| Mital (16 knees) | Only **1** did not return to sport → combine excision + tubercleplasty |

## X vs Y

| | **Osgood-Schlatter** | **Sinding-Larsen-Johansson** | **Tibial tubercle fracture** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Site | **Tibial tubercle** | **Inferior patellar pole** | Tibial tubercle |
| Onset | Chronic, activity-related | Chronic | **Acute** |
| Walking | Normal | Normal | **Difficult** |
| X-ray | Fragmentation, ossicles | Inferior pole fragmentation | Displaced fracture |

## How it's asked

**"13 yr, bilateral tibial tubercle pain on jumping / stairs?"** → **Osgood-Schlatter disease**\
✗ Wrong options: Lyme · plateau stress fracture · patellar tendinopathy · SLJ

**"11 yr, growth spurt, pain on resisted extension — radiograph?"** → **Figure C: tubercle irregularity / intratendinous ossification**\
✗ Wrong options: A tubercle avulsion · B sleeve fracture · D bipartite patella · E tibial spine

**"Decreased patellar tendon strain ratio — radiograph?"** → **Figure B: fragmented tibial tubercle apophysis**\
✗ Wrong options: normal · tubercle fracture · sleeve · loose body

**"18 yr, OSD since 13, failed nonop — surgery?"** → **ossicle excision and tubercleplasty will relieve symptoms and return to sport is expected**
