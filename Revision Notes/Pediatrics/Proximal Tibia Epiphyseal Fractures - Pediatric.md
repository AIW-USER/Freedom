# Proximal Tibia Epiphyseal Fractures - Pediatric

*Pediatrics · 90 / 112*

## 1 Basics

**Epidemiology** — <1% of pediatric fractures · **12–14 yr** · high energy · varus/valgus or **hyperextension**

**Associated** — part of **type III tibial tubercle fracture** · **popliteal artery injury (5%)** — **hyperextension** · peroneal nerve (5%) · **ligament injury up to 40% in SH III/IV** · **compartment syndrome 3–4%**

### Anatomy

**Physis** — proximal tibia **6 mm/yr** · knee = 15 of 23 mm/yr leg growth · closes **posterior → anterior**, **medial → lateral**, **posteromedial → anterolateral**

**Buttresses** — **superficial MCL inserts below physis** (medial buttress) · **LCL to fibula** (lateral buttress) · **patellar tendon** (resists posterior displacement) → physeal fractures are rare

**Popliteal artery** — **tethered** to posterior proximal tibia by septa · **trifurcation at physeal level** → injury with displacement · geniculate arteries around popliteus

## 3 Presentation

**Exam** — can't bear weight · physeal tenderness · step-off · varus/valgus instability · **thorough neurovascular exam**

**Normal X-ray + exquisite physeal tenderness after high-energy hit** → may have displaced + reduced → **splint, admit, frequent neurovascular / compartment checks** (treat like knee dislocation) · MRI/CT to define\
✗ Wrong options: cylinder cast + discharge · arthroscopy · prophylactic fasciotomy

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

**X-ray** — AP, lateral ± obliques · stress views risk physis

**CT** — displacement · **best for SH III / IV**

### Salter-Harris classification

| Type | Features |
|---|---|
| **I** | Mean age 12 · usually displaced (>50%) |
| **II** | Mean age 14 · usually displaced (>67%) · **medial gapping + lateral Thurston-Holland + proximal fibula fracture** |
| III | Exits epiphysis · usually **tibial tubercle** fractures |
| IV | Physis + metaphysis + epiphysis |

## 5 Management

| Fracture | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Non-displaced (<2 mm), stable SH I/II | **Long leg cast, slight flexion, 6 wk** · redisplacement common |
| **Unstable SH I/II** · redisplaced | **CRPP** |
| **Irreducible** (periosteal flap) · **displaced >2 mm SH III/IV** · **vascular injury** | **ORIF** |

**Fixation** — **crossed smooth pins**: **transphyseal** for SH I/II with small Thurston-Holland · **extraphyseal** for SH III/IV · **cannulated screws parallel to physis** for SH II with large metaphyseal fragment or III/IV · cast in slight flexion 4–6 wk

**Approach** — anterior midline (patella → tubercle) · **medial approach if vascular injury**

![ORIF proximal tibial physeal fracture](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob4024-orif.jpg)

### Popliteal artery injury & vascular monitoring

**Hyperextension physeal fracture** → **popliteal artery** most at risk\
✗ Wrong options: tibial n. · peroneal n. · PCL

## 6 Complications

**Growth disturbance 25%** (LLD, angulation; ↑ open fractures) · loss of reduction · compartment syndrome · ligament instability

## How it's asked

**"9-yr hyperextension proximal tibial physeal fracture — at risk?"** → **popliteal artery**

**"11-yr sled hit, normal X-rays, tender physis, NV intact?"** → **splint, admit, frequent neurovascular checks**
