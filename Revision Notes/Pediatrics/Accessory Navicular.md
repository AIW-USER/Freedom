# Accessory Navicular

*Pediatrics · 1 / 112*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Incidence** — normal variant in **up to 12%** of population (Orthobullets) · **2–21%** (OB explanation) · **majority asymptomatic**

**Sex** — more commonly **symptomatic in females**

### Genes & lab markers

**Inheritance** — **autosomal dominant**

### Pathoanatomy

**Lesion** — **plantar medial enlargement** of the navicular\
✗ Wrong options: dorsal medial · dorsal lateral · plantar lateral · central

**Forms** — separate accessory bone **or** completely ossified extension of navicular

**Origin** — tuberosity develops from a **secondary ossification centre that fails to unite** in childhood · accessory navicular does **not ossify before age 8**

**Normal navicular** — single ossification centre · ossifies **age 3 (girls) / 5 (boys)** · fuses **~13 yr**

**Associated** — **flat feet (pes planus)** · **posterior tibial tendon insufficiency** (rare)

### Anatomy & tibialis posterior insertion

**Tibialis posterior** — inserts on **medial tuberosity of navicular** · innervated by **tibial nerve**\
✗ Wrong options: deep/superficial peroneal · medial/lateral plantar

**PTT function** — **inverts hindfoot + assists ankle plantarflexion**\
✗ Wrong options: dorsiflexion = tibialis anterior · plantarflexion = gastrocsoleus · eversion = peroneus brevis

![Tibialis posterior inserting on navicular tuberosity](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob4070-navitp.jpg)

**Other insertions (distractors)** — FDL → bases of 2nd–5th distal phalanges · FHL → base of great-toe distal phalanx · PB → base 5th MT · PL → plantar medial cuneiform + lateral base 1st MT

## 3 Presentation

**Symptoms** — usually **asymptomatic** · **medial arch pain**, worse with overuse/after practice · may follow a perceived **inversion injury / ankle sprain**

**Pain source** — repeated **microfracture at the synchondrosis** or **PTT insertional inflammation**

**Exam** — firm, **tender medial-plantar navicular prominence** · ± swelling · pain with **resisted inversion**

**Rigid arch supports** — rub on the prominence → **worsen pain** → use soft, cushioned inserts

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

**Radiographs** — **AP, lateral, external oblique** · **best seen on external oblique view**

**Finding** — bony enlargement or accessory ossicle at medial navicular · no periosteal reaction / destruction / matrix\
✗ Wrong options: biopsy · MRI + chest CT

**MRI** — only to evaluate **other pathology** · not needed for work-up\
✗ Wrong options: "obtain MRI" as next step

## 5 Management

```
Symptomatic accessory navicular
├─ First line → activity + shoe modification, orthotics, NSAIDs, PT
├─ Failed above → short cast immobilization
└─ Failed extended nonop → excision through synchondrosis
       └─ Flatfoot is primary problem → add MCO + PTT reattachment
```

### Nonoperative care – orthotics & casting

**First line** — **activity restriction, shoe modification, non-narcotic analgesics/NSAIDs**, PT (✓ even after acute sprain)

**UCBL orthosis** — **inverts heel** during walking → offloads prominence → ↓ symptoms

**Orthotic rule** — must **offload** the accessory navicular or it **exacerbates** symptoms

**Cast immobilization** — **next step** after failed activity + shoe modification\
✗ Wrong options: continued activity modification · MRI · ORIF

**Outcome** — most become **asymptomatic at skeletal maturity**

### Surgery – excision ± PTT advancement; with flatfoot correction

**Indication** — recalcitrant pain after **extended nonoperative management**

**Procedure** — medial approach (distal talus → medial cuneiform) · reflect PTT / **tendon-splitting** approach · excise ossicle **through the synchondrosis** · **trim navicular body** flush with medial border of medial cuneiform

**PTT advancement (Kidner)** — Orthobullets: **do NOT advance PTT** (no added benefit, ↑ morbidity) · OB: **simple excision = Kidner** in outcomes · ✓ "split PTT and excise prominence"\
✗ Wrong options: PTT advancement · hindfoot osteotomy · osteosynthesis of ossicle (does not shrink prominence)

**Flatfoot is the primary problem** (flexible, hindfoot valgus, symptomatic Type II) — **MCO + excision + PTT reattachment** · otherwise flatfoot **not** corrected at the same time\
✗ Wrong options: isolated excision (fails) · FDL transfer · lateral closing wedge · subtalar fusion

## 6 Complications

**Most common** — **persistent medial prominence and pain** → navicular body **not trimmed enough**

## X vs Y

| | **Simple excision** | **Kidner (+ PTT advancement)** | **MCO + excision + PTT reattachment** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Use / evidence | Isolated ossicle · relieves pain · **= Kidner** | Historical · **no added benefit**, ↑ morbidity | **Flexible flatfoot + hindfoot valgus** primary |

## How it's asked

**"Accessory navicular excision — tendon elevated is innervated by?"** → **tibial nerve**

**"Function of muscle attached to the ossicle?"** → **inverts hindfoot, assists plantarflexion**

**"Rigid arch insoles make medial foot pain worse — which tendon inserts there?"** → **posterior tibialis**

**"Underlying anatomy?"** → **plantar medial enlargement of navicular**

**"Failed 6 months activity + shoe modification — next?"** → **cast immobilization**\
✗ Wrong options: MRI · continued modification

**"Symptomatic after sprain / incidental ossicle — initial treatment?"** → **activity modification, orthotics (UCBL), PT**\
✗ Wrong options: cast 6 weeks · excision + PTT advancement

**"Failed orthotics + casting — next?"** → **excise medial prominence including synchondrosis**\
✗ Wrong options: ORIF / bone stimulator for "nonunion"

**"Best surgical procedure?"** → **split PTT, excise prominence**\
✗ Wrong options: Kidner advancement (no outcome difference)

**"Symptomatic accessory navicular + flexible flatfoot with hindfoot valgus?"** → **MCO + excision + PTT reattachment**

**"Most common complication of excision?"** → **persistent prominence/pain — inadequate trimming**
