# Achondroplasia

*Pediatrics · 2 / 112*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Most common skeletal dysplasia** — **80–90% of all dwarfism** (TO) \
✗ Wrong options: diastrophic · MED · nail-patella

**Incidence** — **1 in 15,000–30,000** (Orthobullets) · **1/30,000** live births (OB)

**Sex / timing** — no sex predominance · diagnosed in utero or early infancy · short stature apparent at birth

**Risk factor** — **advanced paternal age** (>50 yr: 1 in 1,875; >35 yr still ↑)

### FGFR3 genetics, inheritance & affected physeal zone (proliferative)

**Inheritance** — **sporadic 80%** (de novo) · **autosomal dominant 20%** · **100% penetrance** · once present → **AD transmission**, 50% risk per child (TO) · "most common inheritance pattern" → ✓ **AD**\
✗ Wrong options: AR · X-linked · mitochondrial

**Gene** — **FGFR3** (fibroblast growth factor **receptor** 3, not FGF-3 the ligand) on **chromosome 4p**

**Mutation** — point mutation **Gly → Arg** in **transmembrane domain** · **G380R** (OB) = nucleotide **Gly1138Arg** (Orthobullets) · always the same site\
✗ Wrong options: COL1A1 (>200 sites)

**Effect** — **gain of function** ("turned on") → ↑ **tyrosine kinase** activity → ↑ **inhibition of chondrocyte proliferation + differentiation** (TO)

**Zone** — **proliferative zone** of physis (TO) · **quantitative cartilage defect** · defect of **endochondral** ossification

**Intramembranous ossification normal** — normal skull vault, clavicles, diaphyseal **width** (TO)

**Mechanism detail** — mutation stabilises FGFR3 dimers → escapes **ubiquitin** degradation → persistent signalling

**Pathways** — **STAT1** (inhibits chondrocyte proliferation) · **MAPK** · PLCγ · PI3K-AKT

**Modulator** — **C-type natriuretic peptide (CNP)** → NPR-B → ↑ cGMP → ↓ FGFR3 activity → basis of **vosoritide**

## 3 Presentation

### Clinical features – rhizomelia, frontal bossing, trident hand

**Rhizomelic dwarfism** — **proximal** segment shortest (humerus < forearm, femur < tibia) · **disproportionate** short stature · **normal trunk** (TO)

**Height** — adult ~**50 in** (male 4′4″, female 4′) · 6–7 SD below normal

**Face** — **macrocephaly**, **frontal bossing**, **midface hypoplasia**, depressed nasal bridge

**Hands** — brachydactyly · **trident hand** = all fingers same length, divergent **middle and ring** fingers (TO)

**Upper limb** — limited elbow extension · **radial head subluxation/dislocation** · posterior humeral bowing

**Lower limb** — **genu varum** · **hypermobile hips and knees** · muscular hypotonia · long fibulae\
✗ Wrong options: genu valgum

**Spine** — **thoracolumbar kyphosis** · **lumbar hyperlordosis** (up to 80%) · **lumbar stenosis** · **foramen magnum stenosis**

**Neuro / development** — **normal intelligence** · **delayed motor milestones**

**ENT** — **otitis media 90% before age 2** · hearing loss · tonsillar hypertrophy · obesity

**NOT a feature** — **cervical spine instability** (→ pseudoachondroplasia) · **atlantoaxial instability** (→ Morquio MPS IVA, Down) · dural ectasia (→ NF, Marfan) · cauliflower ear (→ diastrophic)

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

**Diagnosis** — clinical + **skeletal survey** in most · **FGFR3 molecular testing** only if atypical / to distinguish similar disorders

**Prenatal** — US 2nd/3rd trimester: short long bones, wide metaphyses, **↑ biparietal diameter (>95th centile)** · cell-free fetal DNA

**Sleep study** — **all infants** (screen for apnea)

### Key radiology findings

**Views** — **skeletal survey** · **AP pelvis + femur** (most diagnostic features)

**Spine** — **↓ interpedicular distance L1 → S1/L5 (pathognomonic)** · **short pedicles** · posterior vertebral scalloping · T12/L1 apical wedging · bullet-shaped vertebrae in infants · hyperlordosis with flat sacrum

**Pelvis** — **champagne-glass pelvis** (wider than deep) · **squared ("ping-pong paddle") iliac wings** · **narrow sciatic notch** · flat horizontal acetabula

**Limbs** — short thick bones · metaphyseal flaring · **inverted-V distal femoral physis** · proximal femoral radiolucency · **epiphyses normal**

**MRI** — FMS: obliterated posterior subarachnoid space, cord "nicking/waisting", T2 signal change, syrinx · also lumbar stenosis

## 5 Management

### Thoracolumbar kyphosis – natural history

**Most common dysplasia with TLK** — **achondroplasia**\
✗ Wrong options: Trevor · MED (normal spine) · Larsen (cervical kyphosis) · diastrophic (cervical)

**Cause** — flexible, forward slouching with unsupported sitting · 50–100% born with TLK

**Natural history** — **resolves by 12–18 months** (OB) · **1.5–2 years** (TO) as child **walks** and gains trunk strength · resolution 90% by 18 months · ~70% by age 3 (OB) · prevalence ~87% at 1–2 yr → ~10% at 5–10 yr

```
Thoracolumbar kyphosis in achondroplasia
├─ Flexible (most) → observe; no unsupported sitting
├─ Fixed >30° / anterior wedging → TLSO until walking
└─ Neuro deficit / failed brace / >45–60° (>50° TO) → posterior fusion ± anterior decompression
       (delay to age ≥4 for instrumentation)
```

**Observation** — firm-backed seating · avoid C-position · **no sitting >60°**

**Bracing (TLSO)** — **fixed component >30°** (prone lateral) · substantial anterior wedging · posterior apical displacement · worn until independent walking · mean residual 8°

**Fusion** — neuro compromise · kyphosis **>45–60°** (Orthobullets) · fixed **>50°** (TO) · failed brace · **wait until age 4** · corpectomy if severe anterior cord impingement

### Foramen magnum stenosis – sleep apnea

**FMS → central sleep apnea** → **SIDS** (2–5% mortality in 1st year) (TO)

**Central vs obstructive** — **adenotonsillar hypertrophy → obstructive** apnea, no hypotonia · **FMS → central** apnea + **hypotonia**

**Signs** — clonus, **hypotonia**, hyperreflexia, clumsiness, **dysphagia** (lower CN), weakness

**Work-up** — **sleep study all infants** · **MRI/CT** if abnormal exam or sleep study

```
Foramen magnum stenosis
├─ No central apnea, no compression, neuro intact → observe, repeat MRI 6–12 mo
└─ Central apnea / cord compression / neuro deficit → foramen magnum decompression + upper cervical laminectomy ± duraplasty
       (intra-op US; 10% recurrence)
```

### Lumbar stenosis

**Prevalence** — 37–89% · **25% need surgery** · disability in **3rd–4th decade**

**Causes** — short thick pedicles · ↓ interpedicular distance L1→L5 · hyperplastic disc + ligamentum flavum

**First line** — weight loss, PT, corticosteroid injections

**Surgery** — severe neurogenic claudication · failed nonop · neuro symptoms at rest → **multilevel laminectomy + fusion**

**Technique** — **3 levels above block → at least S2** · **no sublaminar wires / laminar hooks** (narrow canal) · pedicle screws preferred but pedicles **cranially directed, ~10 mm shorter**, smaller sagittal diameter

**Post-laminectomy kyphosis** — **common** despite >50% facet preservation → **fuse if ≥5 levels or skeletally immature**

### Operative criteria

| Problem | Surgery when |
|---|---|
| **TLK — brace** | Fixed **>30°**, anterior wedging |
| **TLK — fusion** | Neuro deficit · **>45–60°** (Orthobullets) / **>50°** (TO) · failed brace · age ≥4 |
| **FMS** | **Central sleep apnea** · cord compression · weakness, hyperreflexia, clonus |
| **Lumbar stenosis** | Severe claudication · failed nonop · neuro symptoms at rest |
| **Genu varum** | Progressive severe deformity · **fibular thrust** |
| **Upper-limb lengthening** | **Bilateral** humeral shortening preventing **perineal / head hygiene** (TO) |

### Extremities & stature

**Genu varum** — bracing generally unsuccessful · **tibial ± femoral osteotomy** at CORA for progressive deformity / fibular thrust · TO: osteotomy > growth modulation

**Growth hormone** — controversial · +6–8 cm over 5 yr

**Vosoritide** — **CNP analogue** · daily injection · age **≥5 yr with open physes**

**Lower-limb lengthening** — very controversial · early adolescence or two stages (age 7 + 12) · gain ~20.5 cm

### Upper-limb lengthening

**Indication** — **bilateral affection** with humeral shortening limiting **perineal hygiene** (TO)\
✗ Wrong options: unilateral LLD (upper limb non-weight-bearing, cosmetic)

**Gain** — humerus ~10 cm

**Complications** — **25–43%**: fracture, early consolidation, nonunion, malalignment, stiffness, infection (TO)

## 6 Complications

**Hydrocephalus** — shunt in **5%**

**ENT** — otitis media 90% < age 2 · **50% need tympanostomy tubes**

**Spinal surgery** — recurrent FMS · CSF leak · malpositioned screw (abnormal pedicles) · neuro injury (overcorrection, wires/hooks) · **post-laminectomy kyphosis**

**Life expectancy** — ~**61 yr** (10 yr less) · highest mortality **<4 yr** (FMS)
