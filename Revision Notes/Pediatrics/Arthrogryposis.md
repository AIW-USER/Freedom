# Arthrogryposis

*Pediatrics · 6 / 112*

## 1 Basics

### Definition & epidemiology

**Definition** — **non-progressive** congenital disorder · **multiple rigid joints** in all 4 limbs · a physical finding in ~300 disorders (TO)

**Incidence** — **1:3000** live births (TO)

**Course** — contractures **do not worsen with growth**\
✗ Wrong options: "progressive, peak at maturity"

### Etiology

**Mechanism** — **fetal akinesia** (↓ movement in utero) → **symmetric** contractures · movement needed from **8 weeks' gestation** (TO)

**Neurogenic vs myopathic** — **neurogenic 90%**, myopathic 10% (Orthobullets) · neurologic **70–80% / 80%** (TO)

**Pathophysiology** — maternal antibodies vs fetal **acetylcholine receptors** → ↓ anterior horn cells · anterior horn cell disease (Werdnig-Hoffmann, SMA)

**Other causes (TO)** — connective tissue (diastrophic dysplasia, Larsen, Beals) · myopathies · uterine space limits (oligohydramnios, fibroids, multiple pregnancy) · vascular compromise · maternal disease (diabetes, MS, myasthenia, myotonic dystrophy, rubella, CMV, toxoplasma)

**No genetic predisposition** for classic form (OB)

### Classification

| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| **I** | Single localised deformity (e.g. forearm pronation) |
| **II** | **Full expression** — absent shoulder muscles, thin limbs, elbows extended, wrists flexed + ulnar deviated, intrinsic-plus hands, adducted thumbs, **no flexion creases** |
| **III** | Type II + **polydactyly** + non-neuromuscular systems |

**By neurological function (TO)** — normal: **amyoplasia**, distal arthrogryposis, fetal crowding, systemic tissue disorder, multiple pterygium · abnormal neurological function

**Amyoplasia** — **most common recognisable form** · **sporadic**, symmetric · normal/above-average IQ · **midline facial hemangioma** · hip involvement ~**80%**

### Distal arthrogryposis & related syndromes (TO)

**Distal arthrogryposis** — hands + feet, large joints spared · ulnar-deviated fingers at MCP · "cup-like" palm, single crease · flexed adducted thumb · equinovarus or vertical talus · **better prognosis**

**Freeman-Sheldon** — **common variant of type 2 distal arthrogryposis** · **whistling face** (pursed lips, deep eyes)\
✗ Wrong options: Larsen · Escobar

**Pterygium syndromes** — skin webs across joints · **Escobar** = multiple pterygium (neck webs, vertebral anomalies) · **popliteal pterygium** variant

**Larsen** — **filamin B** · hypermobility · **congenital dislocation of multiple joints** (anterior knee dislocation) · **cervical kyphosis/injury** · normal IQ

**Bruck syndrome** — **OI + congenital contractures**

## 3 Presentation

### Clinical characteristics

| Region | Classic finding |
|---|---|
| **Shoulder** | **Adducted, internally rotated** (absent deltoid) |
| **Elbow** | **Extended**, no flexion creases (deficient biceps, brachialis) · rarely flexed |
| **Wrist** | **Flexed + ulnar deviated** (TO) |
| **Hand** | **Intrinsic plus** · IP flexion + relative MCP extension · **adducted thumb / thumb-in-palm** |
| **Hip** | **Flexed, abducted, externally rotated** · subluxation / **teratologic dislocation** |
| **Knee** | Extended (classic) but **most often flexed** · weak quads · dimple over patella |
| **Foot** | **Clubfoot (most common deformity)** · vertical talus rarer |
| **Spine** | **C-shaped neuromuscular** scoliosis — **33%** (Orthobullets) · 28–67% / 10–30% (TO) · long thoracolumbar curves |

**Normal** — **intelligence**, facies, **sensation**, viscera

**Other** — **fractures 25%** · limbs cylindrical/fusiform with ↓ skin creases · **nonambulatory 25%**

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Diagnosis & upper-limb pattern

**Diagnosis** — **clinical**: multiple symmetric contractures (e.g. stiff fingers with few creases, IR arms, flexed-ER hips, stiff clubfeet) → **arthrogryposis**\
✗ Wrong options: Larsen · CP · Down

**Studies at 3–4 months** — neurologic studies · enzyme tests · **muscle biopsy** · genetic testing

**Prenatal US (TO)** — lack of movement, fixed flexion, abnormal position, ↑ nuchal translucency, polyhydramnios-type signs, thin ribs, diaphyseal fractures

## 5 Management

**Principle** — multidisciplinary · **stretching + casting first** → soft-tissue release / osteotomy · correct alignment, preserve ROM, tendon transfers for active motion

### Operative criteria

| Region | Indication / timing |
|---|---|
| **Upper limb surgery** | After age **4** (independent eating) (Orthobullets) · after ambulation, **3–4 yr**; not >8 yr (TO) |
| **Elbow extension contracture** | **Triceps V-Y lengthening + posterior capsulectomy at 1.5–3 yr** |
| **Unilateral hip dislocation** | **Open reduction** ≥6 mo (Orthobullets) · **6–9 mo** (OB) · **before 1 yr** (OB) |
| **Knee flexion contracture** | **>30°** → hamstring release at **6–9 mo** (Orthobullets) · not corrected by 6–12 mo (TO) |
| **Hip flexion contracture** | **>45°** → surgical release (TO) |
| **Knee extension contracture** | Failed casting by **6–12 mo** → **quadricepsplasty** (TO) |
| **Foot** | Around time of standing (↓ recurrence) (TO) |

### Upper limb – passive manipulation & casting

**Goals** — drive electric chair, use computer devices · **one elbow extended** (positioning, **perianal care**) + **one flexed** (**feeding**)

**First line** — **passive manipulation + serial casting** (13-month-old, good triceps)\
✗ Wrong options: observation · EMG/NCS · osteotomy · soft-tissue release + transfer

| Deformity | Procedure |
|---|---|
| **Elbow extension** | **Triceps V-Y lengthening + posterior capsulectomy** (passive flexion) · active flexion: **bipolar pectoralis major / latissimus transfer**, long-head triceps transfer, **Steindler flexorplasty** |
| Stiff flexed elbow | Not severe → **no surgery** (TO) |
| **Wrist palmar flexion + ulnar deviation** | **FCU release/lengthening/transfer to wrist extensors** · **dorsal carpal closing-wedge osteotomy** · fusion = salvage · ideal 5–20° dorsiflexion |
| Thumb-in-palm, syndactyly | **Z-plasty**, adductor release, syndactyly release |
| Finger deformity | **PIP arthrodesis** |
| Shoulder IR | External rotation humeral osteotomy (TO) |

### Hip dislocation – open reduction (not Pavlik)

**Teratologic hip** — **68–80%** · dislocates **early in utero** → stiff, rigid

**Pavlik / abduction brace** — **unlikely to succeed** → US still shows **dislocation (α ~40°, β unmeasurable)** · Pavlik in dislocated hip prevents posterior wall development

**Closed reduction** — **rarely successful** (skin/skeletal traction not helpful)

```
Arthrogrypotic hip dislocation
├─ Unilateral → open reduction (prevent pelvic obliquity, scoliosis)
│      ├─ <1 yr (6–9 mo) → medial approach ± femoral shortening
│      └─ Older child → anterior approach + femoral shortening ± pelvic osteotomy
└─ Bilateral → observe (controversial; many ambulate)
       └─ Good arc of motion (e.g. 80°) → bilateral medial open reduction (OB)
```

![Teratologic hip dislocation in arthrogryposis](../../MCQ/attachments/q5255-illa-1305479242-680x400.jpg)

**Approach** — **medial** (good results 80%; Szoke) · **anteromedial or anterolateral** both good (OB) · anterior reserved for **older children**

**Prerequisite** — reasonable flexion/extension arc + active lower-limb movement

**Risk** — resulting **hip flexion contracture worsens gait**

**Sequence conflict** — knee releases **before** hip reduction to help maintain reduction (Orthobullets; TO: correct knees before hip) · **"hip first, knee second"** (other TO explanation) — read the options

### Knee contractures

**Flexion contracture** — serial splinting/casting → **hamstring release** (>30°, early) · ambulation possible with residual **15–20°** · **supracondylar femoral extension osteotomy** at maturity · guided growth with osteotomies (may not fix poor quads) · Ilizarov for severe

**Extension contracture / dislocation** — early stretching + casting · **open quadriceps tenotomy at 4–6 mo** · late **V-Y quadricepsplasty** · reduce knee dislocation first

### Clubfoot & lower-limb deformity management

**Most common deformity in AMC** — **talipes equinovarus** (TO)\
✗ Wrong options: CVT · knee flexion · IP flexion

**Newborn** — start **serial stretching + splinting** immediately (TO)\
✗ Wrong options: wait for weight gain · wait 1 month

**Ponseti** — **effective but more casts** than idiopathic · **high relapse** (27% at 2 yr) (TO)\
✗ Wrong options: as effective · not effective · not used

```
Arthrogrypotic clubfoot
├─ Early → Ponseti casting (more casts)
├─ Rigid / failed Ponseti → posteromedial + posterolateral release
└─ Recurrence after release → talectomy (child) / triple arthrodesis (adolescent)
       (alternatives: Verebelyi-Ogston talar decancellation, lateral column shortening, circular frame)
```

### Congenital vertical talus

**CVT in arthrogryposis** — **soft-tissue release first** · **talectomy** if recurrence

**CVT associations** — **hip dislocation 43–50%** (✓ "congenital hip dislocation") · myelomeningocele, arthrogryposis, **trisomy 18**, Cri-du-Chat · familial **HOXD10** · 60–85% have associated anomalies\
✗ Wrong options: amniotic band · fibular hemimelia · PFFD · polydactyly

**Spine** — multilevel fusion for arthrogrypotic curves

## X vs Y

| | **Arthrogryposis (amyoplasia)** | **Larsen** | **Distal arthrogryposis** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Joints | **Stiff, rigid**, all 4 limbs | **Hypermobile**, multiple **dislocations** | Hands + feet; large joints spared |
| Gene | Sporadic | **Filamin B** | Variants incl. Freeman-Sheldon |
| Hallmark | IR shoulders, extended elbows, flexed-ulnar wrists | Anterior knee dislocation, **cervical kyphosis** | Ulnar-deviated fingers, cup palm |
| IQ | Normal | Normal | Normal |

## How it's asked

**"All characteristic of arthrogryposis EXCEPT?"** → **progressive contractures peaking at maturity**

**"Arthrogrypotic newborn, Pavlik 4 weeks — US at 1 month?"** → **α 40°, β unmeasurable** (still dislocated)

**"Amyoplasia, unilateral irreducible hip?"** → **delayed open reduction ± pelvic/femoral osteotomy** (6–9 mo)\
✗ Wrong options: Pavlik · semi-rigid brace · traction · closed reduction + spica

**"10-month-old arthrogrypotic, bilateral dislocations, 80° arc?"** → **bilateral open reduction, medial approach**\
✗ Wrong options: traction + CR · anterior approach

**"6-month-old, rigid unilateral dislocation — initial management?"** → **open reduction (anteromedial/anterolateral)**\
✗ Wrong options: Pavlik · observe · wait >1 yr · spica

**"13-month-old, little elbow motion, good triceps?"** → **passive manipulation + serial casting**

**"Rigid rocker-bottom foot — associated condition?"** → **congenital hip dislocation**

**"Stiff fingers, IR arms, flexed-ER hips, stiff clubfeet — diagnosis?"** → **arthrogryposis** (TO)\
✗ Wrong options: Larsen · CP · Down

**"Common variant of distal arthrogryposis?"** → **Freeman-Sheldon** (TO)

**"Classical arthrogryposis commonly involves?"** → **wrist flexed with ulnar deviation** (TO)\
✗ Wrong options: thumb abduction · flexed elbows · finger extension

**"Most common deformity in AMC?"** → **talipes equinovarus** (TO)

**"10-day-old with AMC — advice for lower limbs?"** → **serial stretching and splinting** (TO)

**"Ponseti in arthrogrypotic clubfoot?"** → **effective but more casts needed** (TO)
