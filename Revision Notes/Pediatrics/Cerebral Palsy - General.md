# Cerebral Palsy - General

*Pediatrics · 16 / 112*

## 1 Basics

### Definition & epidemiology

**Definition** — **non-progressive UMN disease** (static encephalopathy) from **injury to immature brain** · onset **before age 2** · musculoskeletal picture **worsens with growth** (bone grows faster than spastic muscle)

**Incidence** — **2–3 per 1000** · **most common cause of chronic childhood disability**

**Risk factors** — **prematurity (most common)** (Orthobullets) · **prenatal causes most common (70%)** (TO) — TORCH, toxins, Rh (kernicterus), malformations · perinatal: anoxia, traumatic delivery, chorioamnionitis · postnatal: meningitis, encephalitis, HIE, **NAT**

**Brain MRI** — **periventricular leukomalacia (PVL) most frequent (56%)** (preterm, spastic diplegia) · grey matter 18% · malformations 9%

**Manifestations** — primary: abnormal tone, **spasticity**, loss of motor control, impaired balance · secondary: **contractures** (dynamic → static), hip subluxation, spine deformity, foot deformity, gait disorders, **fractures**

### Classification & ambulation prognosis

| Physiologic | Feature |
|---|---|
| **Spastic** | **Most common (80%)** · velocity-dependent ↑ tone, hyperreflexia, clonus, Babinski · agonist-antagonist co-contraction · **most amenable to surgery** |
| Athetoid (dyskinetic) | Slow writhing involuntary movements · normal reflexes · contractures uncommon |
| Choreiform | Continuous purposeless distal movements |
| Rigid | Lead-pipe/cogwheel, no hyperreflexia · most hypertonic |
| Ataxic | Cerebellar · wide-based, drunken gait, tremor |
| Hypotonic | Precedes spastic/ataxic for 2–3 yr |
| Mixed | Spastic + athetoid, total body |

| Anatomic (geographic) | Feature |
|---|---|
| **Diplegia** | **Legs > arms** · usually **ambulatory** · near-normal IQ · **most common** (TO) |
| **Hemiplegia** | One side, **arm > leg** · ~50% mentally retarded (TO) |
| **Quadriplegia** | All four, **nonambulatory**, low IQ, ↑ mortality |
| Paraplegia | Legs only, arms spared |
| Double hemiplegia | All four, **arms > legs** |
| Monoplegia | One limb (rare) |
| Total body | + absent head control, drooling, dysphagia |

**Lower > upper limb** → **diplegia**\
✗ Wrong options: paraplegia (arms spared) · double hemiplegia (arms > legs)

### GMFCS (best motor-function score)

| Level | Ability (age 6–12) |
|---|---|
| **I** | Walks without limits · runs, jumps · stairs without rail |
| **II** | Walks with limits — difficulty uneven ground, inclines, long distances · stairs with rail · minimal run/jump |
| **III** | **Walks with handheld mobility device** (walker, crutches) · wheeled mobility for community/long distance |
| **IV** | Self-mobility limited · **powered mobility** / pushed wheelchair |
| **V** | **Transported in manual wheelchair** · dependent in all care |

**Best score for motor function** — **GMFCS** · EDACS = eating/drinking\
✗ Wrong options: MACS (upper limb/hands) · CFCS (communication) · PEDI

**Motor plateau** — levels I–II by ~5 yr · III by ~8 · IV–V by ~7, may decline in adolescence

### Ambulation prognosis

**Most reliable predictor** — **independent sitting by age 2** (OR 26:1)\
✗ Wrong options: creeping · rolling · CP pattern · motor type

**Poor prognosis** — no sitting by **4 yr** · not walking by **8 yr** (without severe contracture) → unlikely ever

**Bleck poor signs** — asymmetric tonic neck reflex · **persistent Moro** · **strong extensor thrust** on vertical suspension · **persistent neck-righting reflex** · **absent parachute reaction after 11 months** → "all EXCEPT" = **presence of parachute reaction** (TO)

## 3 Presentation

**History** — perinatal, development, function (sitting, standing, hands, communication, hearing, vision)

**Spine** — scoliosis flexibility, pelvic obliquity · **hamstring contracture → ↓ lordosis** · **hip flexion contracture → ↑ lordosis**

**Hip** — flexion + **adduction** contractures · dislocation may be missed as "contracture" → thigh length sitting, Galeazzi

**Foot** — equinovarus, planovalgus · toe walking, knee hyperextension for heel contact · Silfverskiöld

![Equinus feet in CP](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob4084-equinus-feet.jpg)

### Foot & knee deformity patterns (recurvatum, foot deformities)

**Common CP foot** — **equinocavovarus (hemiplegia)** · **equinoplanovalgus (diplegia in crouch)** · **hallux valgus** · **isolated cavus is NOT typical** → think **spinal dysraphism / CMT** (TO)

**Knee recurvatum causes (TO)** — quad–hamstring **co-spasticity** (quads stronger) · **weakened hamstrings** (over-lengthening/transfer) · gastrocsoleus weakness · **ankle equinus** · **quadriceps weakness** is NOT a cause

**Recurvatum work-up** — **prone rectus test** (quad spasticity) · SLC/AFO at neutral: still recurvatum → not from equinus · treat with long-leg braces locked in 20° flexion

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

**Baseline** — **AP + lateral hips** · **standing spine** films

### Hip surveillance (TO)

**Reimers migration percentage (MP)** — % of femoral head lateral to **Perkin line** (Hilgenreiner line through triradiates) · supine, hips together, patellae forward

| MP | Meaning |
|---|---|
| <30% | Stable — monitor |
| **>30%** (surveillance) / **>33%** (Reimers) | **Displacement / subluxation** |
| **>60%** | High risk of dislocation · **soft tissue alone fails** |
| **>100%** | **Dislocation** |
| Rise **>10%/yr** | Red flag — rapid progression |

| GMFCS | Hip displacement risk | X-ray frequency |
|---|---|---|
| I | ~0–5% (≈1%) | Age 2, then if gait changes |
| II | ~15% | Age 2, then 2-yearly to 10 |
| III | ~35% | Age 2, then yearly to 10 |
| IV | ~70% | Age 2, then 6–12-monthly |
| **V** | **~90%** | Age 2, then **6-monthly** |

**Clinical red flags** — passive **abduction <30°** (knee extended) · new pain with dressing/hygiene/sitting · new LLD

**Hip at risk** — flexion contracture **>20°** + abduction **<30°** · ↑ neck-shaft angle (**coxa valga**) · ↑ **femoral anteversion** · acetabular dysplasia (**posterosuperior** in CP vs anterolateral in DDH)

**Dislocation by type** — **70–90% occur in quadriplegia**

## 5 Management

### Spasticity management – botulinum toxin, baclofen (withdrawal) & SDR contraindications

| Agent | Mechanism | Use / note |
|---|---|---|
| **Botulinum toxin A** | Binds **motor nerve terminal** → enters → cleaves **SNARE** → **blocks presynaptic ACh release** (flaccid chemical denervation) | **Dynamic** contracture, too young for surgery · little benefit in **static** contracture · lasts **2–3 mo** (Orthobullets) / **3–4 mo** (OB) / 3–6 mo (OB) → reverses by axonal sprouting · inject belly near nerve entry |
| **Baclofen** | **GABA-B agonist** (spinal cord, central) | **Intrathecal** preferred (oral → sedation, poor bioavailability) · **nonambulatory GMFCS IV–V** |
| Lidocaine | Na⁺ channel block | — |
| Acetylcholinesterase | Degrades ACh | — |
| Pyridostigmine / neostigmine | AChE inhibitors → ↑ ACh | — |
| Trihexyphenidyl | Muscarinic antagonist | — |
| Gabapentin | Ca²⁺ channel | — |
| Methacholine | Direct cholinergic agonist | — |

**Botox distractors** — ✗ Wrong options: destroys ACh receptor (myasthenia gravis) · binds muscle receptor (curare, succinylcholine)

**Baclofen withdrawal (pump failure)** — rebound rigidity, myoclonus, **hyperthermia**, **rhabdomyolysis** → **↑ creatinine**, **↑ K⁺**, **↑ CPK**, multi-organ failure\
✗ Wrong options: ↓ K⁺ · ↓ CPK · hypothermia · atony

**Spasticity grading (TO)** — **R1** = angle of muscle catch · **R2** = passive ROM · **R2 − R1 >20°** = significant **dynamic** component → **botox**

**Phelps test** — hip abduction ↓ only with knee extended → **gracilis** spasticity

**5-yr GMFCS III diplegic, jump gait, DF R1 −10/R2 +20, popliteal R1 80/R2 40, abduction 45° flexed / 20° extended** → **botox to gastrocnemius + gracilis + semitendinosus + semimembranosus** (TO)\
✗ Wrong options: surgery · adductor release (abduction already 45°)

### Selective dorsal rhizotomy

**Procedure** — section **L2–S1 dorsal rootlets** with abnormal response to stimulation

**Ideal candidate** — **spastic diplegia**, ambulatory, **age 4–8** (Orthobullets, OB) / **3–8** (TO), GMFCS II–III, voluntary + trunk control, pure spasticity, **no fixed contractures**, preterm, motivated family · improves **one GMFCS level** (TO)

**Contraindications** — **athetosis** (unpredictable) · **nonambulatory spastic quadriplegia** (spinal deformity) · hemiplegia not recommended (TO)\
✗ Wrong options: "hemiplegics benefit most before 5"

**Complications** — **hip subluxation/dislocation** · lumbar **hyperlordosis** (esp. >60° preop) · scoliosis · spondylolysis/listhesis · weakness

**Athetoid 5-year-old — contraindicated?** → **SDR**\
✗ Wrong options: botox · UE tendon transfer · SEMLS · intrathecal baclofen

### Operative principles

| Muscle behaviour | Procedure |
|---|---|
| **Continuously active** | **Tenotomy** (hip adductor) or **lengthening** (Achilles, hamstring) |
| **Out of phase** | **Tendon transfer** (rectus femoris, tibialis posterior) |

**Soft-tissue surgery** — age **3–5** with spasticity + voluntary control

**Bony surgery** — later childhood/adolescence

**SEMLS** — one event, gait-lab guided · simple lengthenings "uncover" other contractures and worsen gait

### Upper extremity surgery – voluntary control predicts outcome

**Voluntary motor control** — best predicts improvement after **wrist/hand tendon transfers**\
✗ Wrong options: botox · SDR · femoral/tibial derotation osteotomy

### Hip subluxation in GMFCS V – adductor release

```
CP hip by migration percentage
├─ MP 30–40%, dynamic, abduction <30° → adductor ± psoas release (preventive)
├─ MP >40–60%, coxa valga, anteversion → VDRO (bilateral) ± femoral shortening
│      └─ Acetabular index >25–30° → add Dega acetabuloplasty (posterosuperior)
└─ Painful, deformed head, GMFCS IV–V → salvage: McHale / proximal femoral resection
```

**Adductor release** — goal **>60° abduction hips flexed, 45° extended** · bilateral (avoid **windswept** pelvis) · then abduction splinting 6 mo · avoid excessive release in IV/V → abduction contracture

**Psoas** — flexion contracture **15–30° → intramuscular recession over pelvic brim** · **>30°** → add rectus, sartorius, TFL, anterior glutei release · ambulators: fractional at brim (complete lesser-trochanter release → flexor void, crouch)

**VDRO** — neck-shaft angle **110–115°** · correct anteversion · **shorten 1–3 cm** if tight (↓ AVN, sciatic tension)\
✗ Wrong options: 105° → abductor weakness, Trendelenburg

**Acetabuloplasty** — **Dega / San Diego** (incomplete trans-iliac, hinge on triradiate) → **deeper posterior graft** for **posterosuperior** coverage · Pemberton = volume-reducing, open triradiate\
✗ Wrong options: Salter (anterolateral redirection, leaves posterior uncovered) · extra varus · isolated psoas tenotomy

**GMFCS V, painful sitting, hygiene problems, coxa valga + unilateral dislocation, failed therapy** → **bilateral adductor release + bilateral VDRO ± pelvic osteotomy**\
✗ Wrong options: observe · soft tissue alone (MP >60% fails) · shortening alone · unilateral VDRO (asymmetry)

**5-yr GMFCS IV, MP 18% → 31% in 1 yr, abduction 25°** → **6-monthly X-rays + refer for preventive soft-tissue surgery** (TO)\
✗ Wrong options: annual surveillance · VDRO now · discharge at 5

### Operative criteria

| Situation | Choice |
|---|---|
| **Open reduction of dislocated hip** — all 4 | Moderately mature intellect · at least **sitting potential** · minimal/corrected **pelvic obliquity** · ideally **unilateral** |
| **GMFCS IV, MP 100%, deformed head** | **Femoral head resection** (McHale: resection + subtrochanteric valgus osteotomy + interposition) over reconstruction (TO) |
| Schanz osteotomy | Pelvic-support for sitting platform |
| THA | Selected higher-function painful arthritic hips |
| Hamstring lengthening | Popliteal angle **>45–50°** · **medial** preferred (lateral → ↑ ext. tibial torsion, lordosis) |
| **DFEO — "rule of 15"** | Fixed knee flexion **>15°** (TO) · guided growth/soft tissue below |
| Guided growth (anterior 8-plate) | FKFD with growth left (<11–12 yr) |

### Crouch gait & knee surgery (DFEO, patellar advancement)

**PFKE couple** — **plantarflexion–knee extension couple**: competent gastrocsoleus stops tibia falling forward · failure = **iatrogenic over-lengthened Achilles / triceps surae weakness** → crouch (TO)\
✗ Wrong options: rectus spasticity · psoas contracture · hamstring shortening

**Apparent equinus** (toe-walking, ankle neutral, knee 35° + hip 20° flexion, **patella alta** Insall-Salvati 1.4) → **DFEO + patellar tendon advancement** (TO)\
✗ Wrong options: TAL (causes severe crouch) · isolated hamstring lengthening · gastroc recession + AFO

**Patellar tendon advancement/plication** — **mandatory with DFEO** in chronic crouch to restore quad lever arm (TO)

**Post-DFEO anterior knee pain / extensor shift** — **unaddressed patella alta** with shortened femur (TO)

**GRAFO (floor-reaction AFO)** — shifts GRF **anterior to knee** → **knee extension moment** (TO)

**Hamstring activity throughout stance on EMG** → **hamstring lengthening likely to fail** (TO)

**Post-op** — early weight bearing · night knee immobilisers for years

### Stiff-knee gait – rectus femoris transfer

**Rectus femoris transfer** — distal rectus → gracilis/semitendinosus · **removes swing-phase restraint → ↑ peak knee flexion** (TO)\
✗ Wrong options: ↑ extension moment · pure hip extensor

**Stiff knee + internal rotation in swing + anteversion (hemiplegia Winters IV, double-bump rectus EMG)** → **femoral derotation osteotomy + rectus femoris transfer** (TO)

### Equinus – gastrocnemius recession & lengthening zones

| Zone | Procedure | Structures |
|---|---|---|
| **Zone 1** | **Strayer** (transverse, above junction) | **Isolated gastrocnemius** — safest vs crouch |
| Zone 1 | Baker (tongue-in-groove) | Gastroc + partial soleus |
| Zone 2 | **Vulpius** (inverted V at MTJ) | Gastroc + often soleus fascia |
| **Zone 3** | **Hoke triple hemisection / TAL** | **Tendo-Achilles** (TO) |
| Intramuscular | Baumann | Multiple deep gastroc recessions |

![Gastrosoleus lengthening zones](../../MCQ/attachments/TO17138290-1.jpg)

**Diplegia** → usually **isolated gastroc** contracture · **hemiplegia** → **gastrosoleus** tight (TO)

**8-yr diplegic, −10° knee extended, +15° knee flexed** → **Strayer** (TO)\
✗ Wrong options: percutaneous TAL (crouch) · Vulpius (weakens soleus) · SPLATT (varus)

### Neuromuscular scoliosis surgery – evaluation, outcomes & complications

![Neuromuscular scoliosis in CP](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob4084-cp-spine.jpg)

**Pre-op work-up** — nutrition (weight) · pulmonary (PFT/sleep study/clinical) · swallow + **GERD** (aspiration) · **valproate** may impair coagulation (↓ vWF) · **MRI NOT routine** (only if neuro change or rapid progression)

**Most predictable outcome** — **↑ caregiver satisfaction** (perceived comfort)\
✗ Wrong options: cognition · nutrition · pain · mobility

**Respiratory complication risk factor** — **gastrostomy tube** · resp. complications **15–28%** · NMS has highest complication rate (congenital 10.6%, idiopathic 6.3%)\
✗ Wrong options: lymphocytes <1000 · albumin <3.5 · reactive airway · Hb <8

**Wound drainage 3 weeks after T2–pelvis fusion** → **culture + I&D + IV antibiotics + retain instrumentation**\
✗ Wrong options: oral abx · aspiration · hardware removal → rapid progression

### Bone health – bisphosphonates for fragility fractures

**Fragility fractures** — **non-ambulators**, low BMD (immobility, nutrition, **anticonvulsants**, contractures) · prevalence 6–50% · annual 4% → **7% after a fracture**

**IV pamidronate** — **>3 fractures + DEXA Z-score <−2 SD** (Orthobullets) · prevents **refracture**\
✗ Wrong options: LLD · nonunion · malunion · contracture

**Mechanism** — **nitrogen-containing** (pamidronate, alendronate) inhibit **farnesyl pyrophosphate synthase** (mevalonate pathway) → osteoclast apoptosis · non-N (clodronate, etidronate) → toxic ATP analogues

**CROW walker** — **Charcot foot** (TO)\
✗ Wrong options: CP equinus · polio · spina bifida

## X vs Y

| | **Botulinum toxin A** | **Baclofen** | **SDR** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Site | Peripheral NMJ, presynaptic | Central spinal GABA-B | Dorsal rootlets L2–S1 |
| Duration | Temporary (months) | Continuous via pump | **Permanent** |
| Best for | Dynamic, focal, young | **Nonambulatory GMFCS IV–V** | **Ambulatory spastic diplegia 4–8** |
| Avoid | Static contracture | Abrupt withdrawal | **Athetosis**, quadriplegia |

| | **CP hip** | **DDH** |
|---|---|---|
| Deficiency | **Posterosuperior** | Anterolateral |
| Pelvic osteotomy | **Dega / San Diego** | Salter, Pemberton |
| Driver | Spastic adductors + psoas | Capsular laxity |
