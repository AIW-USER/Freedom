# Calcaneovalgus Foot

*Pediatrics · 12 / 112*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Incidence** — mild form up to **40% of newborns** · true deformity **1 in 1,000**

**Demographics** — **females**, **first-born**, young mothers

### Etiology

**Cause** — positional, **intrauterine "packaging"** → benign **soft-tissue contracture** · hindfoot **eversion + dorsiflexion**

## 3 Presentation

**Exam** — excessively dorsiflexed hindfoot, **passively correctable** · **dorsum of foot rests on anterior tibia**

**Associated** — **posteromedial tibial bowing** (almost always some calcaneovalgus)

![Calcaneovalgus foot — dorsum against tibia](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob4067-clinical-photo-showing-a-calcaneovalgus-foot.jpg)

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

**Diagnosis** — clinical: calcaneovalgus foot **without congenital deformity or dislocation**

### Key radiology findings

**Not required** if foot flexible enough to exclude CVT

**AP + lateral tibia** — look for **posteromedial bowing**

**Plantarflexion lateral** — distinguishes from **CVT** · before navicular ossifies (**age 3**) use **1st metatarsal** as proxy · calcaneovalgus: **1st MT lines up with talus** · CVT: talar axis passes **plantar** to 1st MT on standard + plantarflexion laterals

**Apex of deformity** — calcaneovalgus: **ankle joint** · posteromedial bowing: **distal tibia**

### Diagnosis & posteromedial tibial bowing

**Posteromedial bowing** — intrauterine positioning · named for apex (posterior + medial) · **"PosteroMedial = Probably Mild"** · bowing improves; follow for **LLD**

![Posteromedial bowing — apex at distal tibia](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob4067-calcaneovalgus-foot.jpg)

## 5 Management

```
Calcaneovalgus foot
├─ Plantarflexes beyond neutral → observation + parental passive stretching
└─ Cannot plantarflex beyond neutral → serial casting
       (resolves spontaneously by 3–6 months)
```

**Outcome** — **resolves by 3–6 months** · severity of initial deformity **does not predict** outcome

## 6 Complications

**LLD** — when associated with **posteromedial bowing** · LLD correction = **most common surgery** for posteromedial bowing

**Flexible flatfoot** — years after resolution

## X vs Y

| | **Calcaneovalgus** | **Congenital vertical talus** |
|---|---|---|
| Flexibility | **Flexible**, passively correctable | **Rigid** |
| Hindfoot | **Calcaneus (dorsiflexion)** | **Equinus** + valgus |
| Midfoot | Normal | **Dorsal TN dislocation**, rocker-bottom, medial talar head |
| Plantarflexion lateral | **1st MT lines up with talus** | Talus plantar to 1st MT |
| Treatment | Stretching ± casting | Reverse Ponseti / surgery |

| Paralytic calcaneovalgus (L5 spina bifida) | |
|---|---|
| Overactive / spared | **Dorsiflexors (L4, L5)**, **evertors (S1)** |
| Weak | **Plantarflexors (S1, S2)**, **invertors (L5)** |

## How it's asked

**"Newborn, dorsum of foot resting on tibia — associated X-ray?"** → **posteromedial bowing of tibia**\
✗ Wrong options: fibular hemimelia · anterolateral bowing · clubfoot · tibial deficiency

**"Apex posteromedial tibia, dorsiflexed foot correctable to neutral?"** → **calcaneovalgus**\
✗ Wrong options: cavovarus · clubfoot · skewfoot · CVT (not correctable)

**"Foot cannot plantarflex past neutral?"** → **serial casting**

**"Natural history?"** → **resolves by 3–6 months**
