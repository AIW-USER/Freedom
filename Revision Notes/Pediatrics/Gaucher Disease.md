# Gaucher Disease

*Pediatrics · 50 / 112*

## 1 Basics

**Most common lysosomal storage disease** · ~**1 in 40,000** · **Ashkenazi Jewish**

### Enzyme deficiency

### Genes & lab markers

**Inheritance** — **autosomal recessive** · chromosome **1q21** (TO)

**Enzyme** — **β-glucocerebrosidase** deficiency → **glucocerebroside (sphingolipid)** accumulates in macrophages of **liver, spleen, marrow**

| Disease | Deficient enzyme |
|---|---|
| **Gaucher** | **β-glucocerebrosidase** |
| Niemann-Pick | Sphingomyelinase |
| Fabry | α-galactosidase |
| Tay-Sachs | Hexosaminidase A |
| Krabbe | Galactocerebrosidase |

**Labs** — **anaemia + thrombocytopenia** · **↑ plasma glucocerebrosides** (diagnostic)

**Histology** — marrow: **giant (binucleate) Gaucher storage cells** filled with glucocerebroside

**Physis** — **reserve (resting) zone** (OB, achondroplasia chapter)

### Classification

| Type | Features | Prognosis |
|---|---|---|
| **1 (adult)** — **most common** | Bruising, anaemia, fractures, no CNS | **Enzyme replacement** works |
| 2 (infantile) | Brain + organs | **Lethal by ~3 yr**, untreatable |
| 3 (juvenile) | Teen onset, thrombocytopenia, hepatomegaly, fractures, gradual CNS | Non-CNS responds to ERT |

## 3 Presentation

**Systemic** — fatigue (anaemia), bleeding (thrombocytopenia), infection, seizures / delay (CNS) · **hepatosplenomegaly** · murmur

**Orthopaedic** — bone pain · **"bone crisis"** (acute infarct, like sickle crisis) · joint pain/contracture · **pathologic fractures** · osteomyelitis

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

**Erlenmeyer flask** — **distal femur / proximal tibia** in **~80%** · **most common radiological finding**, **asymptomatic abnormal remodelling**, needs no treatment

![Bilateral distal femoral Erlenmeyer flask flaring](../../MCQ/attachments/q966-illa-untitled.jpg)

**Osteopenia** — **almost all** (worse after splenectomy)

**Osteonecrosis** — medullary (often asymptomatic) + corticocancellous: **proximal/distal femur, proximal tibia, proximal humerus** · **10× more likely after splenectomy**

**Osteomyelitis** — ↑ prevalence

**FALSE statement (TO)** — "**medullary osteonecrosis is the most common skeletal manifestation**" (✓ Erlenmeyer flask most common radiological; ✓ abnormal remodelling most common asymptomatic; ✓ post-splenectomy ↑ AVN)

![Erlenmeyer flask deformity](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob10132-erlynmeyer-flas.jpg)

## 5 Management

| Treatment | Indication |
|---|---|
| Observation / supportive | Unaffected (normal bloods, minimal organomegaly, no MRI bone lesions) |
| **Enzyme replacement** (imiglucerase, velaglucerase, taliglucerase) | **All children + symptomatic** · not type 2 |
| **Substrate reduction** (miglustat) | Milder, can't tolerate IV ERT |
| **Bone marrow transplant** | Early → may be curative |

### Fracture surgery

**Pre-op** — **optimise with ERT** · blood, clotting factors, platelets available (bleeding) · maintain **oxygenation** (avoid bone crisis) · ↑ infection risk

## How it's asked

**"Absent glucocerebrosidase causes?"** → **Gaucher disease**\
✗ Wrong options: Niemann-Pick · Fabry · Tay-Sachs · Krabbe

**"Gaucher — all correct EXCEPT?"** → **medullary osteonecrosis is the most common skeletal manifestation** (TO)

**"Erlenmeyer flask distal femur?"** → **Gaucher**

**"Most common type?"** → **type 1 (adult), treatable with ERT**
