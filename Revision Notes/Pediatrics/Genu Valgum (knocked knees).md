# Genu Valgum (knocked knees)

*Pediatrics · 51 / 112*

## 1 Basics

### Physiologic tibiofemoral angle by age

| Age | Alignment |
|---|---|
| Birth – <2 yr | **Varus** (10–15° at birth, TO) |
| **~2 yr** | **Neutral** |
| **3–4 yr** | **Peak valgus** — **15–20°** (Orthobullets) · **10° at age 3** (TO answer) |
| **≥7 yr** | Adult — valgus **≤12°**, intermalleolar distance **<8 cm** · adult **~7°** (TO) / 4–7° / 5–10° (OB) |

**Presentation age** — **3–5 yr** (2–8) · rarely worsens after **7**

**Pathologic site** — **distal femur** most common

**Risk factors** — prior infection/trauma · **rickets** · obesity · skeletal dysplasia · lysosomal storage

### Causes

| Bilateral | Unilateral |
|---|---|
| **Physiologic** | **Physeal injury** (trauma, infection, vascular) |
| **Renal osteodystrophy**, hypophosphatemic rickets | **Proximal tibial metaphyseal fracture (Cozen)** |
| **Morquio (MPS IV)** · **SED (tarda)** · **Ellis–van Creveld** · pseudoachondroplasia | **Fibrous dysplasia**, **osteochondroma/MHE**, **enchondroma** |
| — | Fibular hemimelia |

**Apparent valgus** — obese thighs · femoral anteversion · external tibial torsion

### Anatomy & mechanics

**Normal LDFA / MPTA** — **85–90°** each

**Mechanical axis** — hip centre → ankle centre should pass knee centre · valgus → **lateral** deviation, ↑ lateral compartment load

![Mechanical axis, LDFA and MPTA](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob4052-mechanical-axis-mechanical-lateral-distal-femur-angle-medial.jpg)

**Hueter-Volkmann** — compression ↓, distraction ↑ growth · growth change mostly from **hypertrophic zone (75%)** vs proliferative (25%)

**Patellar instability** — ↑ Q angle, hypoplastic lateral femoral condyle / shallow sulcus

## 3 Presentation

**Complaints** — cosmesis (most common) · medial knee pain · circumduction gait · patellar instability

**Exam** — knees touch, **intermalleolar distance >8 cm** · rotational profile · stigmata of rickets/dysplasia (Maffucci)

![Intermalleolar distance](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob4052-intermalleolar-distance.jpg)

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

**Indications** — asymmetry · beyond physiologic range for age · short stature · trauma/infection · LLD

**View** — **standing long-leg AP, patellae forward** · lateral MAD · physeal narrowing/closure · Park-Harris lines

**CT/MRI** — rarely (tumour, physeal bar)

### Genes & lab markers

**Rickets** — Ca, PO₄, **25-OH vitamin D**, PTH · **Morquio** — urinary **keratan sulfate** · syndromes — genetic tests

## 5 Management

### Operative criteria

| Situation | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **<7 yr**, angle **<15°** | **Observe** (bracing ineffective long term) |
| **>15–20° valgus at 7–10 yr** · TF angle >15° · **IMD >10 cm after age 10** · rapid progression after 7 | **Medial hemiepiphysiodesis** |
| Age **>10**, mechanical axis in **lateral quadrant** (outer 25%) of plateau | Medial hemiepiphysiodesis |
| **Skeletally mature** / insufficient growth / physeal bar | **Osteotomy** at site of deformity |

**Hemiepiphysiodesis** — temporary (**8-plate**, staples, Metaizeau screw — most common) or permanent (Phemister) · extraperiosteal, mid-sagittal · one 8-plate or 2 staples per physis · correction ~**7°/yr femur, 5°/yr tibia** · >95% idiopathic / 80% pathologic correct · remove when axis at/just medial of centre

**8-yr, IMD 12 cm** → **bilateral medial proximal tibial tension-band plates**\
✗ Wrong options: 11-yr Drennan 18° (that's Blount → lateral plate) · 4-yr 20° (physiologic) · 7-yr 9° (normal) · 16-yr (near mature → osteotomy)

**12-yr bilateral valgus, open physes** → **temporary hemiepiphysiodesis of bilateral medial distal femurs**\
✗ Wrong options: HKAFO · osteotomy · unilateral · lateral (worsens)

### Treatment - hemiepiphysiodesis vs osteotomy

**Mature 18-yr, mLDFA 73° (femoral valgus), normal MPTA** → **medial closing-wedge distal femoral (varus) osteotomy**\
✗ Wrong options: tibial osteotomy → oblique joint line

| | **Lateral opening-wedge DFO** | **Medial closing-wedge DFO** |
|---|---|---|
| Pros | Adjustable correction | **Stable**, shorter immobilisation, **no peroneal stretch** |
| Cons | Graft, less stable, **peroneal nerve stretch**, nonunion, lengthens | Technically demanding, shortens |

## 6 Complications

**Rebound** — **56%** (≥5° loss after plate removal) · risks: **femoral deformity, younger age, faster correction**, intentional overcorrection → slight overcorrection / operate nearer maturity

**Overcorrection** — lost to follow-up (12%) · **undercorrection** — little growth left

**Peroneal nerve** — opening-wedge / acute correction → **decompress** (lateral compartment fascia + anterior-lateral septum) or gradual frame

**Nonunion** — opening wedge, >20° · **physeal closure** <1% (extraperiosteal, remove within 2–3 yr)

**Cozen** — post-fracture valgus peaks **12–18 mo**, remodels in **2–4 yr** → **observe**

## How it's asked

**"18-yr, mLDFA 73°, MPTA 87°, TF angle 25°?"** → **medial closing-wedge femoral osteotomy**

**"12-yr skeletally immature bilateral severe valgus?"** → **temporary hemiepiphysiodesis of bilateral medial distal femoral physes**

**"Medial proximal tibial tension-band plates — best candidate?"** → **8-yr with intermalleolar distance 12 cm**

**"Normal tibiofemoral axis at age 3?"** → **valgus of 10°** (TO)
