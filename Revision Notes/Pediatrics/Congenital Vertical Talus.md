# Congenital Vertical Talus

*Pediatrics · 30 / 112*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Incidence** — rare, **1:150,000** · **M:F 2:1** · **50% bilateral**

**Also called** — congenital convex pes valgus · "teratologic dorsolateral dislocation of the talocalcaneonavicular joint"

### Pathoanatomy

**Rigid** — **irreducible dorsolateral navicular dislocation** on talar neck · **vertical talus** · calcaneal **eversion** + equinus · **attenuated spring ligament**

**Contractures** — **Achilles** + **peroneals** (hindfoot equinovalgus) · **EDL, EHL, tibialis anterior** (forefoot DF + abduction)

**Tendon displacement** — **peroneus longus + tibialis posterior** shift to act as **dorsiflexors**

### Associations – neuromuscular/genetic conditions & neural axis work-up

**~50%** associated with **neuromuscular disease or chromosomal/syndromic** conditions (>50% / 60–85% in other OB explanations)

| Associated | Not associated |
|---|---|
| **Myelomeningocele** / spinal dysraphism, **diastematomyelia** | **Amniotic band syndrome** (TO, OB) |
| **Arthrogryposis** | Posteromedial tibial bowing (→ calcaneovalgus) |
| **Congenital hip dislocation** (43–50%) | Chromosome **22q11** deletion |
| Cerebral palsy · **spinal muscular atrophy** · NF | — |
| **Chromosomal deletions**, trisomy 18, Cri-du-Chat | — |
| Cleft lip/palate, CHD, chr 12 duplication (less often) | — |

**Highest association** → **neuromuscular disease**

### Genes & lab markers

**Familial** — up to **20%** · **HOXD10** (transcription factor) in familial/"idiopathic" cases · shared pathway with clubfoot

## 3 Presentation

**Rigid rocker-bottom foot** — **convex plantar surface**\
✗ Wrong options: "concave" (the false statement)

**Hindfoot** — **fixed equinus + valgus** (heel in **equinus**; ✗ Wrong options: calcaneus · equinovarus · supple)

**Midfoot** — rigid dorsiflexion (dislocated navicular) · **prominent talar head** in medial plantar arch

**Forefoot** — **abducted + dorsiflexed**

**Gait** — **"peg-leg" / calcaneal gait** (weak push-off, excess heel contact)

**Neuro exam** — always (frequent neuromuscular cause)

**CVT features (TO)** — detected at birth · convex sole with **deep dorsolateral crease** · rounded medial + plantar prominence (talar head) · forefoot dorsiflexed at midtarsal joint · with growth: **"hourglass" talus**, callosities under anterior calcaneus + medial border, severe forefoot abduction, **heel off ground in stance** · ↑↑ Meary angle (TO)

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Diagnosis – forced plantar-flexion lateral radiograph; oblique talus

**Diagnostic test** — **lateral X-ray in maximum (forced) plantar flexion** → **persistent dorsal TN dislocation**\
✗ Wrong options: MRI · US in DF · max-DF lateral · CT

### Key radiology findings

| View | Finding |
|---|---|
| **Neutral lateral** | **Vertical talus**, dorsal navicular · talar axis passes **plantar to 1st MT–cuneiform axis** |
| **Forced plantarflexion lateral** | TN **stays dislocated** · **Meary's angle >20°** · **TAMBA >35°** (talar axis–metatarsal base angle, Hamanishi) |
| Forced dorsiflexion lateral | **Fixed equinus** |
| **AP** | **Talocalcaneal angle >40°** (normal 20–40°) |

**Before navicular ossifies (~age 3)** — use **1st metatarsal** as navicular proxy (✓ the true statement)

**Radiograph details (TO)** — DF lateral: **calcaneal pitch stays negative** (fixed equinus) · AP: talus–1st MT and calcaneus–4th MT **> \~30°** abduction, Kite > 40° · incidence \~1 in 10,000 (TO) · 1st MT surrogate **< 2 yr** (TO)

| (TO) | **CVT** | **Oblique talus** | **Flexible flatfoot** |
|---|---|---|---|
| PF stress view | **No reduction** | Reduces | Reduces |
| TAMBA | **> 35°** | < 35° | < 35° |
| Calcaneum | Locked | Locked | **Unlocked** |

**MRI** — **neuraxial imaging** to exclude neurologic disorder

**Rigid flatfoot in infant (no plantarflexion, no subtalar motion, vertical talus not reducing)** → **neurologic work-up incl. neural-axis imaging** first\
✗ Wrong options: urgent surgery · foot MRI · reassurance

**4-month-old arthrogrypotic with suggestive X-rays** → **obtain forced plantarflexion radiograph** first\
✗ Wrong options: cast or operate before confirming

## 5 Management

```
Congenital vertical talus (confirmed on forced PF lateral)
├─ Reverse-Ponseti serial casting (plantarflexion + inversion) — stretches dorsolateral tissues
└─ Then TN reduction + pinning (closed / mini-open) + percutaneous Achilles tenotomy, 6–12 mo
       ├─ Resistant / older → pantalar (extensive) release + tendon lengthening + TA to talar neck
       └─ Resistant / salvage → talectomy · triple arthrodesis (late)
```

### Treatment – reverse Ponseti casting then talonavicular pinning/Achilles tenotomy or open release

**Casting purpose** — **stretch dorsolateral soft tissues before surgery** · plantarflexion + supination/inversion until 1st MT aligns with talus\
✗ Wrong options: casting alone corrects (that's oblique talus)

**Initial management at 10 months** — **manipulation + casting**\
✗ Wrong options: shoes/scaphoid pad · immediate open reduction · LCL · observe

**After full casting course** → **TN reduction + pinning** (± Achilles tenotomy)\
✗ Wrong options: gastroc recession alone · observe · tenotomy alone · isolated tendon reconstruction

**Standard of care (Dobbs)** — **serial casting → mini-open/percutaneous TN reduction + K-wire + percutaneous Achilles tenotomy** (TO)\
✗ Wrong options: casting alone · open extensive release first · subtalar fusion

**Toddler, irreducible CVT** → **casting then open reduction + Achilles lengthening**\
✗ Wrong options: Ponseti alone · talectomy · subtalar fusion

### Operative criteria

| Item | Criterion |
|---|---|
| Timing | **6–12 months** (Orthobullets) · **before 2 years** (TO) |
| <2 yr | Release + tendon lengthening + TN reduction + fixation (neural tube defect: + tendon transfers) |
| >2 yr | Extensive release + tendon transfers · **bony procedures >4 yr** |
| Missed / >3 yr | Less predictable → **triple arthrodesis** salvage |
| Options (TO) | **Open reduction 1–4 yr** · **Grice-Green extra-articular subtalar fusion** · triple arthrodesis in older children |
| Approach with least AVN | **Dorsal** (vs posteromedial) (TO) |
| Extensive release components | Lengthen **peroneals, Achilles, toe extensors** · reduce + pin TN · **spring ligament reconstruction** · **TA transfer to talar neck** |

## 6 Complications

**Missed CVT** — reconstruction unpredictable after **age 3** → triple arthrodesis

**Untreated** — painful rigid foot, midfoot calluses, significant disability

**Talar AVN** — less with dorsal approach

## X vs Y

| | **Congenital vertical talus** | **Congenital oblique talus** | **Calcaneovalgus** |
|---|---|---|---|
| TN joint | **Fixed dorsal dislocation** | Subluxation | Normal |
| Forced PF lateral | **Stays dislocated**, 1st MT dorsal to talar axis | **Reduces** — talus parallels 1st MT | Normal |
| Hindfoot | **Equinus**-valgus | — | **Calcaneus** (DF) |
| Rigidity | **Rigid** | Flexible | Flexible |
| Treatment | **Cast → TN pin + tenotomy** | **Stretching** / casting; rarely pin + TAL | Stretch |
| Associations | Neuromuscular / syndromic ~50% | — | Posteromedial bowing |

**Short-foot exercises** — strengthen intrinsics, raise medial arch → **flexible flatfoot** (TO)\
✗ Wrong options: plantar fasciitis · tennis elbow · post-triple arthrodesis

✗ Wrong options: reduces with PF · equinovarus · talectomy usually
