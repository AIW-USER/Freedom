# Bipartite Patella

*Pediatrics · 8 / 112*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Incidence** — **2–3%** (Orthobullets) · **~2%** (TO) · "1 in 20" (OB)

**Sex** — **no sex predilection** (Orthobullets) · **M:F 9:1** (TO) — read the options

**Bilateral** — **50%** (Orthobullets, OB) · "most patients" (TO)

**Location** — **superolateral (Saupe type III)** most common

### Etiology & anatomy

**Nature** — **normal variant**, **failure of fusion** of accessory ossification centre · often confused with **patella fracture**

**Ossification** — patella = **largest sesamoid**, single nucleus · ossifies 4–5 yr ♂ / 3 yr ♀ · **accessory centre at 8–12 yr** · fragment joined by **fibrocartilage**

**Painful bipartite patella** — trauma disrupts **fibrocartilaginous zone** → cannot unite (no arterial penetration to fragment) · **vastus lateralis traction** → separation/nonunion

**Blood supply** — predominantly **distal → proximal** · 6 arteries: superior + inferior medial/lateral geniculates (popliteal), supreme geniculate (SFA), anterior tibial recurrent

**Associated** — **nail-patella syndrome**

### Histology

**Interposed tissue** — **fibrocartilage** > fibrous > hyaline · **avascular**

**Adjacent bone** — scalloped, osteoclasts, **well vascularised**

## 3 Presentation

**Symptoms** — mostly **asymptomatic, incidental** · only **2% symptomatic**: anterior knee pain after direct trauma (fall, kick) or overuse (cycling, hill climbing) · worse squatting, jumping, stairs · giving way

**Exam** — tenderness over fragment (superolateral) · hematoma · quad inhibition · palpable defect / large patella

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

**AP knee** — **best view** to see bipartite patella

**Skyline** — prone (non-weight-bearing) vs **squatting (weight-bearing)** → ↑ **separation** of fragments on squatting view

**Contralateral knee** — similar findings in 50%

**Bipartite vs fracture** — **superolateral** · **smooth, rounded borders** · often bilateral

**MRI** — painful bipartite patella → **edema** around fragment = pain source

**Bone scan** — equivocal X-ray: ↑ **uptake superolateral** · controversial

**CT** — shows fragment, **not edema**

## 5 Management

```
Bipartite patella
├─ Asymptomatic / benign exam → reassure, symptomatic Rx, RTP as tolerated
├─ Symptomatic → rest, NSAIDs, isometric quads, brace 30° flexion, ± steroid ≥6 months
└─ Failed >6 mo / displaced / significant impairment → excision of fragment
       └─ Superolateral fragment → ± lateral release or subperiosteal VL release
```

### Location & nonoperative care

**Incidental with benign exam** — **reassurance + observation** · **symptomatic treatment, return to play as tolerated**\
✗ Wrong options: MRI · ORIF · cast 6 wk · immobilise 120° (that's quad contusion)

**Nonop ≥6 months** — rest/sport restriction · NSAIDs · **isometric quadriceps** strengthening · **brace in 30° flexion** · local corticosteroid · less successful in young athletes (compliance)

### Operative criteria

| Indication for surgery |
|---|
| Failed nonop **>6 months** |
| **Displaced fragment** needing reduction |
| Direct trauma causing onset of pain |
| Significant impairment of daily activities |

| Procedure | Note |
|---|---|
| **Open excision** | **Most common**, good results · poor if **large articular** fragment (PF incongruity) |
| Arthroscopic excision | Faster recovery, spares quad tendon |
| **Lateral retinacular release** | Superolateral fragment — removes **VL traction** |
| **Subperiosteal VL release** | Superolateral; avoids long lateral release; arthroscopic = quicker, less effusion |
| ORIF | **Large fragments** · controversial |

## 6 Complications

**PF maltracking** — excision of large fragment / lateral release → PF degeneration

**Others** — **effusion** (arthrocentesis) · persistent pain (after lateral release) · quad weakness · osteonecrosis

### Differential – dorsal defect of patella; double-layered patella (MED)

**Double-layered patella** — **pathognomonic of MED** · two segments split by **coronal septum** · anterior in quad/patellar tendons · posterior articulates · painful snapping, locking, dislocation → **fusion** of segments (best) or resection (TO)\
✗ Wrong options: CDP · SED · achondroplasia

**Dorsal defect of patella** — round, lytic, well-defined **subchondral** lesion, **superolateral** quadrant, sclerotic rim, no matrix, no periosteal reaction · MRI: T2 bright, no marrow edema · usually incidental, **self-limited** → activity modification · rarely curettage + graft (TO)\
✗ Wrong options: OCD · chondroblastoma · Brodie abscess

## X vs Y

| | **Bipartite patella** | **Patella fracture** | **Dorsal defect of patella** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Site | **Superolateral** | Any | Superolateral, **subchondral dorsal** |
| Edges | **Smooth, rounded** | Sharp | Sclerotic rim, lytic |
| Bilateral | 50% | No | — |
| Treatment | Symptomatic | Per fracture | Self-limited |

## How it's asked

**"Knee pain after fall, benign exam — which X-ray warrants reassurance?"** → **bipartite patella**\
✗ Wrong options: patellar dislocation · patella alta/tendon rupture · Segond · GCT

**"Helmet to knee, benign exam, bipartite patella on X-ray — next?"** → **symptomatic treatment, RTP as tolerated**\
✗ Wrong options: MRI · ORIF

**"Most common location?"** → **superolateral** (Saupe III)

**"Double-layered patella is pathognomonic of?"** → **MED** (TO)

**"12-year-old, superolateral dorsal subchondral lucency with sclerotic rim?"** → **dorsal defect of patella** (TO)\
✗ Wrong options: OCD · chondroblastoma · Brodie abscess
