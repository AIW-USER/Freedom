# Sprengel's Deformity

*Pediatrics · 101 / 112*

## 1 Basics

### Diagnosis & associations (Klippel-Feil)

**Definition** — **congenital elevation of a small, hypoplastic scapula** from **failure of caudal migration** · **most common congenital shoulder anomaly**

**Demographics** — **M:F 1:3** (Orthobullets) · equal (TO) · **left** more common (TO) · **bilateral 10–30%** (10% TO)

**Cause** — interrupted **embryonic subclavian supply** (subclavian, internal thoracic or suprascapular) · Poland = subclavian interruption proximal to internal thoracic, distal to vertebral

**Embryology (TO)** — arrest of descent between **9th–12th weeks** · hypoplasia of **trapezius, rhomboids, serratus anterior**

**Omovertebral connection** — cartilage/fibrous/bony bar from superomedial scapula to **lower cervical spine** · **30–50%** (Orthobullets) · ~30% (OB) · ~½ (TO) · limits abduction to ~90°

**Associations** — **Klippel-Feil** (most common associated finding: **cervical spine anomalies**) · congenital scoliosis · fused/cervical ribs · torticollis · diastematomyelia · myelomeningocele · kidney disease · UE anomalies · iniencephaly (TO)

| Overlap | Rate |
|---|---|
| Klippel-Feil patients with Sprengel | ~⅓ (Orthobullets) · 35% (OB) · 20% (TO) |

### Klippel-Feil (TO)

**Triad (~50%)** — short neck · low posterior hairline · ↓ neck motion · fusion **C2–C3 most common (71%)** · gene chromosome 8 · fetal alcohol → 50% fusions

**Associated** — **scoliosis 60%** (most common orthopaedic) · renal anomaly · VSD · deafness · **synkinesis** · respiratory · occipitalization, hemivertebrae, basilar impression · symptomatic → posterior C3–C7 fusion ± decompression

## 3 Presentation

**Exam** — scapula **high-riding, medially rotated** (inferior pole medial) · small equilateral-triangle shape, loss of long medial border · **abduction most limited** + forward flexion · short, broad, webbed-looking neck (Turner-like) · often referred for "scoliosis"\
✗ Wrong options: adduction · rotation

**Differential** — FSHD (no omovertebral bone) · Turner · torticollis

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Classification & surgery (Cavendish, Woodward)

**Cavendish** — 4-grade **cosmetic** classification of **congenital elevation of scapula**\
✗ Wrong options: torticollis · radial head dislocation · clavicle pseudarthrosis

| Cavendish (TO) | Severity |
|---|---|
| I | Very mild |
| II | Mild |
| III | Moderate |
| IV | Severe — scapula **near the occiput** |

| Rigault (TO) | Superomedial angle |
|---|---|
| 1 | Below T2, above T4 transverse process |
| 2 | Between C5 and T2 |
| 3 | Above C5 |

**Pre-op imaging** — **MRI or CT for omovertebral bar**

## 5 Management

### Operative criteria

**Observe** — no significant cosmetic or functional problem

**Operate** — severe cosmesis or **abduction <110–120°** · ideal **age 3–8** (soon after 3) · **>8 yr → brachial plexus risk**\
✗ Wrong options: defer until after 8 (TO false statement) · wait for maturity

**Surgical age** — **3–8 yr** (Orthobullets) · **3–6 yr** (TO lecture) — read the options · **Woodward preferred** (TO) · omovertebral bar excision · **partial scapulectomy**

| Procedure | Essence |
|---|---|
| **Woodward** | Detach + reattach medial parascapular muscles (**trapezius + rhomboid origins shifted distally at spinous processes**) → scapula descends, rotates to abduction · modified: + superomedial border resection |
| **Schrock / Green** | Extraperiosteal detachment of muscles at scapular insertion, scapula pulled down with traction, reinserted |
| **Clavicle osteotomy / morcellation** | **Severe deformity**, before scapular descent → protect **brachial plexus** (2 cm midclavicular if >8 yr, 1 cm younger) (✗ Wrong options: mild deformity) |
| Bony resection | Extraperiosteal superomedial prominence (cosmesis) |
| Mears | Most abduction gain (TO) |

**Outcome** — **abduction +40–50°** (Orthobullets) · ~40° (TO) · +38° (Farsetti)

**Complication** — **brachial plexus palsy = most severe and most common** (TO) · SSEP monitoring

**Other complications (TO)** — **keloid** · shoulder stiffness · one TO lecture names **radial nerve** as the most commonly affected nerve (TO — verify: brachial plexus palsy is the standard answer)

## How it's asked

**"4-yr Klippel-Feil, elevated left scapula — most limited motion?"** → **abduction**

**"Failure of scapular caudal migration?"** → **Sprengel deformity**\
✗ Wrong options: Poland

**"5-yr elevated, medially rotated scapula — commonest imaging association?"** → **cervical spine abnormalities**\
✗ Wrong options: absent clavicle · horseshoe kidney

**"6-yr bilateral, omovertebral bone on CT, can't raise arms?"** → **Sprengel deformity**\
✗ Wrong options: FSHD · Turner

**"Woodward procedure used for?"** → **Sprengel shoulder** (TO)

**"6-yr, abduction 100°, surgery planned — NOT true?"** → **defer 2 yr because plexus palsy is rare after 8** (TO)

**"Cavendish classification for?"** → **congenital elevation of scapula** (TO)

**"True about Sprengel?"** → **brachial plexus palsy is the most severe and common surgical complication** (TO)\
✗ Wrong options: delay to maturity · morcellation for mild · right more common
