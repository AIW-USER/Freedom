# Supracondylar Fracture - Pediatric

*Pediatrics · 102 / 112*

## 1 Basics

**Epidemiology** — most common pediatric elbow fracture (50–80%) · age **5–7** · **M = F** · FOOSH

**Type** — **extension 95–98%** (98% TO) · **flexion <5%** (2–4%) — fall on olecranon

**Associated** — **ipsilateral distal radius / forearm 5–10%** (TO) · vascular compromise **5–17%** · nerve injury 10–20%

**Epidemiology (TO)** — \~**60%** of paediatric elbow injuries · age **4–10 yr**

**Site (TO)** — just proximal to **olecranon fossa** (thinnest part of distal humerus)

### Elbow ossification order

| Centre | Appears (yr) | Fuses (yr) |
|---|---|---|
| Capitellum | 1 | 12–14 |
| Radial head | 3 | 14–16 |
| **Medial (internal) epicondyle** | 5 (6 in Orthobullets text) | **16–18 — LAST to fuse** (16–19 OB) |
| Trochlea | 7 | 12–14 |
| Olecranon | 9 | 15–17 |
| **Lateral (external) epicondyle** | **11 — last to appear** | 12–14 |

## 3 Presentation

### Nerve injuries (AIN, radial, ulnar)

| Pattern | Nerve | Deficit |
|---|---|---|
| **Extension (overall most common)** | **AIN** (median branch) | **Can't flex thumb IP (FPL) + index DIP (FDP)** · no "OK" sign · PQ |
| Extension, **posteromedial** displacement | **Radial** (2nd most common) | Wrist drop, no thumb/MCP extension (PIN: EPL, ECU) · dorsal hand numbness |
| Extension, **posterolateral** displacement | **Median** nerve, **brachial artery** | Volar index sensory loss |
| **Flexion** | **Ulnar** (C8–T1) | **Intrinsics**: can't cross fingers / abduct index (FDI) · small-finger numbness |
| **Medial pin** | **Ulnar** (iatrogenic) | Can't cross fingers |

**Rates** — extension neurapraxia 11% · flexion 17% · pre-op deficit ~20% in displaced extension (OB)

**Management** — examine **before** any reduction · **observe** · recovery 6–12 wk, complete 3–6 months (2–3 months OB)\
✗ Wrong options: EMG · exploration · neurolysis · nerve repair

**Recovery (TO)** — radial 100% · median 88% · **ulnar only 25% fully recover**\
✗ Wrong options: "majority recover in 3 months" (TO false statement)

**Obese children** — more pre- and post-op nerve palsy (TO)

### Vascular exam

**Assess** — pulse (palpation, Doppler) + **perfusion** (warm, pink vs **cold, pale, CRT >2 s**) · **decision to explore is based on perfusion, not pulse** · radial pulse absent at presentation 7–12%

**Brachialis sign** — antecubital ecchymosis, **dimpling/puckering**, palpable bone → proximal fragment buttonholed through brachialis → more arterial injury, swelling, harder reduction

**Bedside nerve tests (TO)** — median / AIN: **OK sign** · radial: **thumbs up**, finger extension · ulnar: **finger abduction**, fist

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Classification & radiographic assessment

| Gartland | Description | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **I** | **Nondisplaced** (beware medial comminution → varus) | Long arm cast **3–4 wk**, X-ray at 1 wk |
| **II** | Displaced in 1 plane, **posterior cortex/hinge intact** (IIA no rotation, IIB rotation) | **CRPP** (US standard) · cast only if AHL hits capitellum, little swelling, no medial comminution |
| **III** | Displaced in 2–3 planes, no cortical contact | **CRPP** ± open |
| **IV** | **Periosteum completely disrupted** — unstable in flexion AND extension (found under EUA) | CRPP ± open |
| **Medial comminution** | Medial column collapse, ↓ Baumann | **CRPP** (valgus force) — else **cubitus varus** |
| **Flexion type** | Distal fragment **anterior** | **CRPP**, **more often open** |

**Fat pads** — **posterior fat pad** = occult fracture · **anterior "sail" sign** (TO) = effusion · most common cause: supracondylar (child), radial head (adult)

**Anterior humeral line** — through middle ⅓ of capitellum **>5 yr** · **<5 yr: anterior and middle ⅓ about equally**, posterior ⅓ in a few normal kids · capitellum visible from ~1 yr · extension type → capitellum **posterior** to line · compare other side\
✗ Wrong options: always fracture

**Baumann angle** — humeral shaft vs lateral condylar physis on AP · **70–75°** · deviation **>5–10°** unacceptable

**Baumann angle (TO)** — normal **64–82°** · every **5° change = 2° change in carrying angle** · **↑ Baumann** in cubitus varus · key **intra-operative guide**

**Anterior humeral line** — middle ⅓ of capitellum **> 5 yr** (Orthobullets) · **> 4 yr** (TO) — read the options

**Other lines (TO)** — **shaft-condylar angle ≈ 40°** (lateral) · **teardrop sign** (lateral) · **crescent sign** = cubitus varus (overlap of capitellum and radial head) · AP: metadiaphyseal angle, humeroulnar (carrying) angle

**Views (TO)** — AP + lateral elbow + **entire humerus and forearm** (floating elbow)

### Other classifications (TO)

| Classification | Feature | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| **Gartland IIA** (TO) | Posterior hinge intact, **no rotation** | Cast / CRPP |
| **Gartland IIB** (TO) | Posterior hinge intact **with rotation** | **CRPP** |
| **Leitch type IV** (TO) | **Unstable in flexion and extension** — global instability | CRPP |
| **De Boeck** (TO) | **Medial column comminution** | High risk of **cubitus varus** |

**Angiography** — not indicated (lesion is at fracture)

## 5 Management

### Treatment indications - CRPP

```
Supracondylar fracture — timing by neurovascular status
├─ Pulseless + POORLY perfused (cold, pale) → EMERGENT CRPP (hours, override NPO)
├─ Pulseless + well perfused, sensory deficit, swelling, brachialis sign, open, floating elbow → URGENT (same day, 6–8 h)
└─ Warm, perfused, neuro intact (± isolated AIN) → next day (splint 30–40°, elevate)
```

**Operative** — **Gartland II, III, IV** · **flexion type** · **medial comminution** · displaced type II with medial impaction (3-yr) → **CRPP**\
✗ Wrong options: cast (varus) · hyperflexion cast (compartment syndrome)

**Floating elbow** (supracondylar + forearm / distal radius) → **pin BOTH** (✗ Wrong options: casting either (compartment risk up to **33%**)) · watch for compartment syndrome · ↑ nerve injury (✗ Wrong options: ↑ vascular · ↑ open)

**Isolated AIN palsy** → **not** a reason for overnight surgery

### Pin configuration & iatrogenic ulnar nerve injury

**Reduction (extension type)** — traction, correct coronal/rotation, **hyperflexion** · posteromedial → **pronate** · posterolateral → **supinate** (try opposite if fails)

**Reduction order (TO)** — longitudinal traction → correct varus-valgus → flexion + **olecranon pressure** → pronation / supination by periosteal hinge

**Brachialis sign fracture (TO)** — **brachialis milking technique** · **Tansen joystick technique** (Nepal)

**Stable lateral pinning rules (TO)** — **≥ 2 cortices** purchase · pin separation **≥ ⅓ humeral width** at fracture · **two 2 mm pins better than three thin pins**

**Indications for a medial pin (TO)** — **medial oblique** fracture · **intercondylar extension** · **high** supracondylar fracture · **internal rotation instability**

| Construct | Use |
|---|---|
| **2 lateral pins** | Type II · test stability |
| **3 lateral pins** | **Type III / IV, comminution (medial column)** · = crossed-pin stability · **construct of choice** (AAOS) |
| **Crossed (medial + lateral)** | **Strongest in torsion** (experimental) · **ulnar nerve injury 3–8%** |

**AAOS** — **2–3 lateral pins**, avoid medial pin (weak recommendation) · **no Level 1 evidence that lateral is superior** (RCT: equal outcomes) — the INCORRECT statement · lateral = fewer ulnar injuries, equal radiographic + ROM outcomes

**Pin tips** — **maximise separation at fracture**, engage both columns, cross **proximal** to fracture · 3rd lateral pin ↑ stiffness with medial comminution or residual IR

**Elbow position for pinning** — **flexion** for extension type · **extension for flexion type**

**Medial pin safety** — place with **elbow extended** (ulnar nerve subluxes anteriorly in flexion) · small incision · anterior-to-posterior sagittal angle ↑ injury

**Post-op ulnar palsy after crossed pins** → **observe + avoid hyperflexion >90°** (TO) · resolves 1–7 months\
✗ Wrong options: remove medial pin now · exploration/transposition

**Open reduction** — failed closed · **open fracture** · irreducible with gap (entrapped vessel/nerve) · **flexion type more often** · **coronal displacement >7 mm** predicts it · approach **anterior** (pulseless / median injury) or medial/lateral where periosteum torn · **never posterior** (AVN)

### Pulseless hand & vascular management

| Situation | Action |
|---|---|
| **Pulseless + cold/pale** at presentation | **Emergent CRPP** → reassess (✗ Wrong options: angiogram · splint and wait · explore first) |
| **Pink pulseless** (warm, brisk CRT ± Doppler) | **Urgent CRPP** · after: if still pink (± triphasic Doppler) → **long arm splint + admit 48 h observation** · pulse returns **24–48 h** in most |
| **Pulseless + still cold/pale after CRPP** | **Explore antecubital fossa** (✗ Wrong options: observe · re-pin · fasciotomy) |
| **Perfused hand becomes pale/pulseless after CRPP** | **Remove pins, re-displace, reassess** → still bad → **open exploration** (✗ Wrong options: medial pin · CT angiogram · warm and wait) |
| **Irreducible + anterior gap + poor perfusion** | **Vascular exploration** (entrapped artery) |

**Pulseless + poorly perfused after CRPP** → brachial artery injury up to 82%

**After CRPP (TO)** — pale, non-perfused → **immediate exploration** · pink, perfused, no deficit → **close observation** · **pink + poor Doppler or nerve palsy → exploration** · **prophylactic fasciotomy** if prolonged ischaemia

**Emergency surgery (TO)** — open fracture · **skin tenting** · neurovascular compromise · floating elbow · compartment syndrome · otherwise splint, operate next morning

### Postoperative care (pin removal, PT, pin infection)

**Pins** — remove at **3 (–4) wk** in clinic, stop immobilisation\
✗ Wrong options: 3 more wk cast

**ROM** — ~**94% by 6 months** · **no routine PT** (no functional or motion benefit)

**Pin infection** — 1–2.4% · **only proven risk: age <4.5 yr** · oral antibiotics\
✗ Wrong options: delay >24 h · no antibiotics · late follow-up; daily pin care ↑ infection

**Pin tract infection** — **1–2.4%** (Orthobullets) · **≈ 4%** (TO) — read the options

## 6 Complications

### Compartment syndrome

**Rate** — 0.1–0.3% (TO) · <1% · ↑ with **floating elbow**, splint >90°, type III, vascular injury, delay

**Signs in children** — **3 A's: anxiety, agitation, ↑ analgesic need** · rising narcotic need overnight → **emergent surgery ± fasciotomy**\
✗ Wrong options: rely on 6 P's

**Volkmann ischemic contracture** — deep volar compartment · **casting in >90° flexion**

### Cubitus varus

**Cubitus varus (gunstock)** — **most common complication** (OB) · Orthobullets lists **pin migration (~2%)** as most common · **malreduction / malunion** (medial collapse, IR, extension) — **NOT growth arrest**\
✗ Wrong options: medial physeal arrest · lateral overgrowth

**Effects** — mainly **cosmetic**, function normal · posterolateral rotatory instability · forearm IR deficit · **tardy ulnar palsy** (anterior nerve subluxation most common, then FCU bands)

**Osteotomy** — for **cosmesis** · **lateral closing wedge → lateral prominence** · reverse V, step-cut, dome, medial opening with medialisation avoid it

**Cubitus valgus** — malunion → tardy ulnar palsy · **recurvatum** — non-op type II/III

**Cubitus varus = 3-plane deformity (TO)** — coronal **varus** · sagittal **extension** · axial **internal rotation**

**Rotational tests (TO)** — **Yamamoto test** · **Bhende test** · radiology: ↑ Baumann · crescent sign · fishtail (progressive)

**Remodelling (TO)** — **sagittal** deformity may remodel · **varus does NOT remodel**

**Surgery indications (TO)** — cosmesis · function · **PLRI** · **ulnar nerve instability** · **recurrent lateral condyle fractures**

**Surgery criteria (TO lecture 2)** — varus **> 5–10°** · age **> 5 yr** · **≥ 1 yr after trauma** · flexion/extension component · other sequelae: **triceps snapping**, tardy ulnar palsy · options: medial open wedge, lateral closing wedge (most common), step-cut, dome, **French osteotomy** (corrects rotation), guided growth in young · **PLRI → LUCL reconstruction + osteotomy**

**Oblique lateral closing wedge osteotomy (TO)** — **current gold standard** · apex near original fracture · equal-limb · **no lateral prominence** · K-wires or plate · options: simple closing wedge · step-cut · dome

### TRASH lesions (TO)

**TRASH** — "The Radiographic Appearance Seemed Harmless" · unossified **medial condyle** fracture · **transphyseal** distal humerus fracture · entrapped **medial epicondyle** · osteochondral fracture-dislocation · **radial head compression** fracture · **Monteggia variants**

**Fishtail deformity (TO)** — **lateral trochlear ossification centre fails/resorbs** (AVN of posterior vessels to **lateral** trochlea, "type A") → inverted-V distal humerus · hard to see before **~9 yr** · after supracondylar (most), lateral/medial condyle, SH I · (✗ Wrong options: "medial trochlea blood supply") · **Hegemann** = trochlear osteochondrosis\
✗ Wrong options: epicondylar

## X vs Y

| | **Extension type** | **Flexion type** |
|---|---|---|
| Frequency | 95–98% | <5% |
| Distal fragment | Posterior | **Anterior** |
| Nerve | **AIN** (then radial) | **Ulnar** |
| Pin elbow position | Flexion | **Extension** |
| Open reduction | Less | **More** |
