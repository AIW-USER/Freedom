# Clubfoot (congenital talipes equinovarus)

*Pediatrics · 23 / 112*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Most common musculoskeletal birth defect** — **1:1000** (1:250 in some; highest in **Hawaiians, Maoris**) · TO: 1.2/1000

**Sex** — **M:F 2:1**\
✗ Wrong options: equal

**Side** — **half bilateral**\
✗ Wrong options: majority unilateral

**Isolated** — **80%** idiopathic · 20% with syndromes / neuromuscular conditions

### Associated findings, genetics & prognosis (limb hypoplasia, neurological cause, prenatal diagnosis)

**Familial** — **25%** · unaffected parents with one child → **2.5–6.5%** recurrence · monozygotic > dizygotic concordance

**Family risk (TO)** — **sibling 2–5%** · parent + sibling affected **up to 25%** · M:F **2.5:1** (TO) · arrest at **5th week** (one TO lecture) vs 6–8 wk (another) — read the options

**Gene** — **PITX1–TBX4** transcription pathway (hindfoot/limb development) · shared pathway with CVT\
✗ Wrong options: PI3K-AKT achondroplasia · COL2A1 Kniest · FGFR2 Apert · RUNX2 CCD

**Inheritance (TO)** — **polygenic multifactorial** · **CASP10** gene implicated in idiopathic clubfoot

**Aetiology theories (TO)** — mechanical (intrauterine) · neuromuscular · **vascular (hypoplastic dorsalis pedis, medial compartment ischaemia)** · germ plasm defect · genetic

**Syndromic associations (TO)** — arthrogryposis · Larsen · **Freeman-Sheldon** · diastrophic dysplasia · myelomeningocele · amniotic band

**Theories (TO lecture 2)** — **arrest of embryonic development at 6–8 wk** (foot normally in equinus, cavus, adduction) · **fibroblastic proliferation** (↑ collagen in ligaments, tendons, capsules) · **germ plasm theory = most accepted** — defect in **cartilage anlage of talus** → dysmorphic talar neck, navicular subluxes medially · AD with incomplete penetrance described

**Deforming structures (TO)** — medial: **deltoid, spring ligament, TN capsule, tibialis posterior, FHL, FDL** · posterior: **TA, posterior ankle + subtalar capsules** · joints: talocalcaneal, TN, CC

**Associations** — **arthrogryposis** · **diastrophic dysplasia** · **myelodysplasia** · **tibial hemimelia** · **amniotic band** (Streeter; hand anomalies) · Pierre Robin · Opitz · **Larsen** · prune-belly

**Vascular** — **anterior tibial artery hypoplasia/absence** common · **posterior tibial artery** predominant supply

**Limb** — **small foot + calf, short tibia** = **intrinsic hypoplasia** of the limb · calf girth difference in unilateral cases\
✗ Wrong options: tethered cord · scoliosis · contralateral hemihypertrophy

**Walking** — clubfoot does **not** delay walking · not walking by **18–24 months** → **neurologic disorder**\
✗ Wrong options: hip dysplasia · in-toeing · LLD · foot

**Prognosis** — with treatment → **walks and runs normally**\
✗ Wrong options: prosthesis

### Pathoanatomy — CAVE

| Component | Tight structures |
|---|---|
| **C**avus (midfoot) | Intrinsics, **FHL, FDL** · plantarflexed 1st ray |
| **A**dductus (forefoot) | **Tibialis posterior** |
| **V**arus (hindfoot) | **Tendoachilles, tibialis posterior, tibialis anterior** |
| **E**quinus | **Tendoachilles** |

**Bony** — medial spin of mid/forefoot around hindfoot · **talar neck medially + plantarly deviated** · calcaneus varus, rotated medially under talus · **navicular + cuboid medially displaced** · forefoot **pronated relative to hindfoot** (whole foot supinated)

**Bony (TO)** — talus: **short neck**, medially deviated, broad superior surface · calcaneus small, medial, varus · navicular medial, **hypoplastic**

**Contracted ligaments (TO)** — spring · deltoid · calcaneofibular · subtalar ligaments · capsules of ankle, subtalar, TN joints

**Acetabulum pedis (TO)** — **TN joint**; talus has **no muscle attachments**, mostly cartilage → stays still while calcaneo-pedal unit rotates around talar head

**Wrung-towel biomechanics (TO)** — **forefoot pronation maintains hindfoot varus** · **calcaneal eversion only after forefoot supination** → correct in supination, then abduction

## 3 Presentation

**Exam** — small foot/calf · **medial + posterior skin creases** · hindfoot **equinus + varus**, midfoot **cavus**, forefoot **adduction** · **rigid** equinus resisting passive correction (vs positional deformity)

**General exam (TO)** — spine + neuro · **sacral dimple, sinus, hairy patch** · hips, knees, upper limbs (syndromic) · **toe-to-shin (dorsiflexion) test** rules out positional foot

### Atypical (complex) clubfoot (TO)

**Features (TO)** — **short, stubby foot** · severe cavus with **transverse plantar crease** · all metatarsals flexed · rigid equinus · **hyperextended short 1st toe** · fibrotic TA

**Modified Ponseti (TO)** — **two-thumb technique** · **heel pull-down** manoeuvre · max abduction **only 20°** · cast in **100–110° knee flexion**

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

**Diagnosis** — **clinical**

### Pirani score (TO)

| Pirani (0–6; each sign 0 / 0.5 / 1) | Signs |
|---|---|
| **Midfoot score** (0–3) | **Medial crease** · **curved lateral border** · **talar head coverage** |
| **Hindfoot score** (0–3) | **Posterior crease** · **rigid equinus** · **empty heel** |

**Midfoot contracture score ≈ 0** before tenotomy (TO) · **Dimeglio score** — older and neglected cases (TO)

### Types of clubfoot (TO)

| Type | Definition (TO) |
|---|---|
| **Untreated** (preferred to "neglected") | Never treated, presents after walking age (**> \~18 mo–2 yr**) |
| **Recurrent** | Corrected, then returns · brace failure · short TA (**equinus recurs FIRST / most often**) · overactive tibialis anterior |
| **Resistant** | A component cannot be corrected during treatment (TN won't reduce) · atypical clubfoot · tarsal coalition · syndromic |
| **Atypical / complex** | Severe cavus, all MTs flexed → **modified Ponseti** |
| **Syndromic** | Arthrogryposis, myelomeningocele, diastrophic → rigid, **recurrence is the disease** |

**Untreated vs neglected (TO lecture 2)** — untreated: never treated, any age · **neglected: no treatment after 18 months** (walking age; not 6 or 9 mo) · **relapse** = deformity returns **before** treatment completed · **recurrence** = returns **after** completion

**Pirani (TO lecture 2)** — scored in the **maximally corrected position** · given as **0–10** with a forefoot component (adduction rigidity, long-flexor contracture) (TO — verify: standard Pirani is 0–6, midfoot + hindfoot)

### Dimeglio classification (TO)

**Dimeglio (0–20)** — grades **equinus, varus, adduction, rotation** (calcaneopedal block) · preferred in **older, neglected, rigid** feet

| Dimeglio score (TO) | Type |
|---|---|
| **1–4** | Soft-soft (benign) |
| **5–9** | Soft-stiff |
| **10–14** | Stiff-soft |
| **≥ 15** | Stiff-stiff (very severe) |

### Key radiology findings

**Usually not needed** · simulate weight-bearing

| View | Clubfoot finding |
|---|---|
| **Dorsiflexion lateral (Turco)** | **Hindfoot parallelism** · **talocalcaneal angle <25°** · flat talar head (older) |
| **AP** | **Kite (talocalcaneal) angle <20°** (normal 20–40°) · **negative talus–1st MT angle** (normal 0–20°) · parallelism |
| **Lateral talocalcaneal angle** | **< 25°** (Orthobullets) · **< 35°** (TO) — read the options |
| Talus–1st MT angle (TO) | **> 15°** (TO — verify: Orthobullets gives a negative angle, normal 0–20°) |
| Indications (TO) | Not routine — **hemimelias**, **tarsal coalition**, **flat-top talus** (outliers) |
| Older-child signs (TO) | **Talocalcaneal index (AP + lateral TC) < 40** · **tibiotalar angle negative** · talus–1st MT negative |

**Corrected TN joint after PMR (AP)** — **talar axis passes through centre of navicular and 1st MT shaft** (TO)\
✗ Wrong options: points at 1st MT base only (residual adduction) · Kite <15° (that's deformity) · lateral parallelism

**Prenatal US** — from **12 weeks** · **1st trimester** dx → associated anomalies very common · 2nd trimester → true clubfoot · **3rd trimester → ↑ false positives** (crowding)

## 5 Management

**Gold standard** — **Ponseti** (**>90%** avoid comprehensive release) · start **as early as possible** (first 1–2 weeks); older infants (e.g. 9 months) still treated with Ponseti

**French method** — daily PT manipulation + taping/splints · 1–3 mo · family-dependent · rarely used in US

### Ponseti technique – correction sequence, supination/abduction around talus & casting errors

**Principle** — **abduct the foot, rotating it laterally around a fixed talus** · **counterpressure on lateral talar head** (fulcrum) \
✗ Wrong options: push against calcaneocuboid joint (Kite → rocker bottom) · manipulate heel directly

**Order** — **C → A → V → E**

| Step | Manoeuvre |
|---|---|
| 1 **Cavus** (first cast) | **Forefoot SUPINATION** — elevate 1st MT, align forefoot with hindfoot (✗ Wrong options: pronation → ↑ cavus, midfoot break) |
| 2 Adductus + varus (together) | **Abduct** foot around talar head; supination gradually ↓ to neutral |
| 3 Equinus (last) | Only when heel neutral/valgus + foot **≥60° abducted** → **percutaneous Achilles tenotomy** → cast **3 weeks** in max DF (15°) |

**Casts** — **long-leg (toe-to-groin), knee 90°**, changed **weekly** · 5–6 casts usually · plaster\
✗ Wrong options: short-leg (slips, doesn't control tibial torsion)

**Final cast** — max abduction **~70°** (hypercorrection) + DF 15° · end-point before tenotomy: TN reduced + **60° passive abduction** (TO)\
✗ Wrong options: **30° abduction** (the false statement)

**Manipulated in** — **supination + abduction** (TO)\
✗ Wrong options: pronation · adduction · dorsiflexion first

**Casting details (TO)** — manipulate **1–3 min** before each cast · change every **5–7 days** · tenotomy if **< 15–20° DF** · final cast **3 wk** in max DF + abduction

**End-point of abduction (TO)** — **TN joint reduction** (navicular covers talar head; head no longer felt), not an angle · usually **50–70°**, atypical foot as little as **20°** · no tenotomy before foot is **past the midline**

**Cavus (TO)** — no separate cast needed: keep forefoot **supinated** (1st ray raised) and abduct in the same cast → fewer casts · **never press on the calcaneus**

```
Idiopathic clubfoot (newborn or infant)
├─ Weekly long-leg Ponseti casts: cavus → adductus/varus (~4–6 casts)
├─ Residual equinus (>80–90%) → percutaneous TA tenotomy + 3-wk cast
└─ FAO 23 h/day × 3 mo → nights/naps to age 3–4
       ├─ Relapse <2 yr → recast (± repeat tenotomy)
       └─ Dynamic supination ≥2–4 yr → whole TA transfer to lateral cuneiform
```

### Percutaneous Achilles tenotomy for residual equinus

**Rate** — **80–90%** (Orthobullets) · **70–75%** / 70% (OB) · **>90%** (OB) — "most/almost all"

**When** — residual rigid equinus after cavus/adductus/varus corrected (~6–8 wk) · **under local anaesthetic** in clinic\
✗ Wrong options: more casting · fiberglass · FAO first · TA transfer

**Why** — bracing a foot in equinus fails (slips, blisters) · prevents rocker bottom

**Technique** — **medial → lateral** (protect **posterior tibial artery**) · bleeding from **peroneal artery + lesser saphenous vein** (TO)

**Open Z-lengthening of TA in varus foot** — **distal horizontal limb medial** (corrects residual varus) (TO)

**Equinus is NOT corrected by casting (TO)** — tenotomy to reach **15–20° DF** · completion: sudden DF, palpable gap, **"pop"** · GA in older children

**Spurious dorsiflexion (TO)** — through lax midfoot · **lock subtalar by inverting** and judge true ankle DF · < \~**20°** real DF → tenotomy

**Tenotomy level (TO)** — **\~1 cm above the palpable tuberosity** · too low → apophyseal damage, bony bump

**Any age (TO)** — first-time percutaneous tenotomy heals even at **8–10 yr** (intact paratenon; US-confirmed) · **repeat tenotomy rarely works** (unless \~10° needed) → **open Z-lengthening**

### Ponseti casting & bracing protocol

**Foot abduction orthosis (FAO / Denis Browne / Mitchell / Dobbs / Iowa / Steenbeek)** — **critical**; noncompliance = biggest recurrence risk

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Wear | **23 h/day × 3 months** → **nights + naps until age 3–4** (✗ Wrong options: 23 h for 12 months (TO false statement)) |
| Affected foot ER/abduction | **60–70°** (Orthobullets 60° · OB 70° · TO 70°) |
| Normal foot (unilateral) | **30–40°** |
| Dorsiflexion | **10–15°** (TO answer: **70° abduction/ER + 15–20° DF**) |
| Bar width | Heels at **shoulder width** (~1 inch wider) |
| Shoes | Straight medial border, low posterior cut, heel cup |

**After walking age** — "daytime AFO + night bar" stated as **FALSE** (TO)

**Bracing duration** — nights + naps to **3–4 yr** (Orthobullets) · **4 yr (4–5 yr)** (TO) — read the options · braces: Steenbeek · Denis Browne · **John Mitchell** (TO)

**"CTEV shoes" (TO)** — straight inner border shoes are **not part of Ponseti** · walking children need no special shoes

### Recurrence – bracing compliance & risk factors

**Most dependent on** — **brace (FAO) compliance**\
✗ Wrong options: maternal age · family history · walking age · BMI

**Risk factors** — **noncompliance** + **parental education ≤ high school**\
✗ Wrong options: race · sex

**Relapse <2 yr** → repeat manipulation + casting · **>2 yr** → casting, then **TA transfer** ± repeat TAL / gastroc recession

**Highest recurrence (TO)** — during the **bracing phase** · **equinus recurs first** · **recurrent cavus → think neurological** (tethered cord, dysraphism, CP, CMT) → image spine

**Relapse / late presentation (TO)** — **Ponseti first at any age** (even untreated at 5 yr) · **6 mo–2 yr hardest** to cast · limit recurrent casting to \~**3 casts**, then correct the residue surgically · **JESS** if child cannot comply with casting

### Dynamic supination – tibialis anterior transfer

**Cause** — incomplete navicular reduction → **TA becomes a supinator** (medial overpull vs weak peroneals) · ~**one-third** of patients · begins **3–5 yr** (older than 12–18 mo)

**Gait** — **supination in swing**, lands on **lateral border** · forefoot inverts on active DF · foot passively plantigrade

**Dynamic supination vs relapse (TO)** — dynamic: foot **normal when standing**, problem only in gait · static deformity = **relapse** · **30–50%** at 4–5 yr (TO) · physiotherapy first (peronei) → TATT **> 4 yr**

**In-toeing after clubfoot (TO)** — internal tibial torsion, mostly resolves · surgery only after **8–10 yr**

**Treatment** — **whole TA transfer to lateral (3rd) cuneiform** (Garceau principle) · or split TA to cuboid / PB · Point = lateral cuneiform (more common) or cuboid · TA harvested from medial cuneiform + 1st MT base

**Distractors** — ✗ Wrong options: more casting (to 60° abduction) · PTT through IOM (poor in clubfoot; for paralysed TA) · TA lengthening · TA to calcaneus (myelodysplasia calcaneus) · midtarsal osteotomy (fixed) · floor-reaction AFO · cuboid wedge

| TA transfer criteria | |
|---|---|
| Rate | **10–50%** (Orthobullets) · **10–20% / 15–20%** (OB) |
| Age | **2–5 yr** (Orthobullets) · **~4 yr** (OB) · lateral cuneiform **ossified** — ≥2, usually ≥3 (TO) (✗ Wrong options: <1 yr · 12–15 yr) |
| TA strength | **≥ grade 4** |
| Subtalar joint | **Supple** — **rigidity = contraindication** (✗ Wrong options: "rigidity supplements") |
| Fixed hindfoot varus | Not a TA transfer case → osteotomy |

### Surgical release – outcomes & late deformities (dorsal bunion, residual cavus, rocker bottom)

| Procedure | Indication |
|---|---|
| **Posteromedial release + tendon lengthening** | Resistant/recurrent after Ponseti · **rocker bottom from failed casting** · syndromic failures · at **9–10 mo** (non-syndromic) · <6 mo surgery → poorer results |
| Medial column lengthening / lateral column shortening / **cuboid decancellation** | >2–3 yr with release · **3–10 yr** recurrent "bean-shaped" foot |
| **Talectomy** | Severe rigid recurrent clubfoot in **arthrogryposis**, 6–10 yr |
| Multiplanar supramalleolar osteotomy | Salvage, older (8–10 yr), complex rigid or **insensate** foot |
| Ring fixator (TSF / Ilizarov / JESS) | Complex resistant/neglected · **high recurrence after removal** |
| Triple arthrodesis | **Almost never** · not in insensate feet (ulcers) |

**Outcome** — **extent of soft-tissue release correlates inversely** with long-term function · stiffness + pain common\
✗ Wrong options: subtalar/ankle function · cast duration

**Master knot of Henry release** — frees **FDL/FHL tether** to spring ligament + sustentaculum → allows **TN reduction** (TO)

**Neglected clubfoot >3 yr** — Ilizarov distraction histogenesis → **better joint mobility, ↓ recurrence** vs open PMR (TO)

**JESS** — distract **CC + TN joints (correct midfoot) before equinus** → avoids rocker bottom (TO)

**PMR post-op** — TN K-wire 6 wk · cast change at 2 wk · then FAO

### À la carte surgery by residual component (TO)

| Component left | Procedure (TO) |
|---|---|
| **Equinus** | 1st time: percutaneous tenotomy · recurrent / scarred: **open TA Z-lengthening ± posterior capsulotomy** (ankle + subtalar) |
| **Adductus** (short medial, long lateral column) | **Superficial → deep plantar-medial release** · **lateral column shortening**: CC resection/fusion = **Dillwyn Evans**, cuboid decancellation, cuboid closing wedge · **double tarsal osteotomy** (medial cuneiform opening + cuboid closing wedge) for **"kidney-bean" foot** |
| **Varus (hindfoot)** | **Dwyer lateral closing-wedge calcaneal osteotomy** — min age **3–4 yr** |
| **Cavus** | **Plantar fascia release** → medial cuneiform plantar opening wedge or MT dorsal closing wedge (CORA near medial cuneiform) |
| **Dynamic supination** (> 4 yr) | **TA transfer to lateral cuneiform** |
| **All components, rigid, older child** | **Shingade (modified Japas) osteotomy** at midtarsal apex + TAL · PMSTR (older option) |
| Severe neglected / syndromic / rigid recurrence | **JESS / Ilizarov** gradual correction · **talectomy** salvage |

**Evans trap (TO)** — clubfoot **Dillwyn Evans SHORTENS** lateral column (CC resection/fusion) · adult flatfoot **Evans LENGTHENS** it (calcaneal lengthening)

**Dwyer vs midfoot osteotomy (TO)** — CORA of adductus is in the **midfoot** · correcting at calcaneus → **Z-shaped (skew) foot**

**Lichtblau (TO)** — for long lateral column · no long-term stiffness · complication **Z / skew foot**

**Superficial plantar-medial release (TO)** — open **laciniate ligament** · free 3 origins of **abductor hallucis** from plantar vessels/nerves · divide plantar fascia · deep: TN capsule, **long plantar + spring** ligaments, Z-lengthen tibialis posterior

**PMSTR (TO)** — **Turco · Cincinnati** (circumferential) · releases **knot of Henry** (FHL × FDL) · **> 50% midfoot arthritis at 10–15 yr** → now avoided except posterior release

**PMSTR (TO lecture 2)** — after failed Ponseti (**up to 10–11 casts**) · incisions: **Turco**, **Cincinnati**, **Carroll dual incision** · releases **3 tendons** (TP, FHL, FDL) + **3 ligaments** (deltoid, spring, TN) + **3 capsules** (ankle, subtalar, TN) ± TA Z-lengthening · lateral column shortening: **Lichtblau**, **Evans**, cuboid decancellation, CC fusion

| Age (TO lecture 2) | Approach |
|---|---|
| **< 4 yr** | Casting + soft-tissue release |
| **4–8 yr** | Selective osteotomies |
| **≥ 10 yr** | **Triple arthrodesis** (rigid feet) |

**Triple arthrodesis** — **almost never** (Orthobullets) · **after 10 yr in rigid feet** (TO) — read the options

**JESS (TO)** — **BB Joshi** external stabilisation system · gradual soft-tissue distraction without osteotomy, Ponseti order (equinus last)

**Talectomy (TO)** — rigid uncorrectable recurrence, esp. **arthrogryposis** → tibia on calcaneus · **triple arthrodesis** (TO) — painful arthritic foot after PMSTR

**Syndromic (TO)** — higher threshold for surgery · aim **braceable plantigrade** foot · lifelong (often 24 h) bracing

## 6 Complications

**Rocker bottom** — **correcting equinus before heel varus** → DF through **midfoot** · failed casting with rocker bottom → **PMR + TAL**\
✗ Wrong options: under-corrected pronation → residual cavus · over-corrected adductus → abduction · below-knee casts

**Residual cavus (after surgery)** — **navicular pinned dorsally subluxated** (22%) · insufficient plantar release\
✗ Wrong options: inadequate TAL → equinus · LCL → worsens adductus

**Pes planus** — overcorrection (extensive subtalar release)

**Intoeing** — **internal tibial torsion** / talar IR in mortise

**Talar osteonecrosis** — vascular insult → collapse

**Dorsal bunion** — **strong TA overpowering weak peroneus longus** (PL = **superficial peroneal nerve**) → elevated 1st ray · weak gastrocsoleus after release → **FHB/FHL** compensate → plantarflexed hallux · inadvertent PL lengthening · Rx: **TA lengthening/transfer + FHL transfer to 1st MT head (reverse Jones)** ± plantarflexion osteotomy

**NOT a complication** — **tarsal tunnel syndrome** (TO)

**Complications (TO)** — **flat-top talus** · dorsal bunion · rocker bottom · residual deformity · early arthritis
