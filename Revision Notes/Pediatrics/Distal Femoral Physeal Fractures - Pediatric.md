# Distal Femoral Physeal Fractures - Pediatric

*Pediatrics · 35 / 112*

## 1 Basics

**Who** — open physes · often **misdiagnosed as collateral ligament sprain** · 2–5% of physeal injuries (<2% OB)

**Most common type** — **Salter-Harris II** (physis fails on **tension** side, metaphysis on **compression** side → **Thurston-Holland**)

### Anatomy

**Ossification** — single nucleus present **at birth** — **first epiphysis to ossify**

**Physis** — **undulating** (mammillary processes) → fracture crosses **multiple layers** → high arrest rate

**Muscles** — gastrocnemius + plantaris arise just proximal → **flex** distal fragment

**Ligaments** — collaterals + cruciates attach to **epiphysis** → stress transmitted to physis (ligament injury uncommon)

**Vessels** — **popliteal artery directly posterior**, trifurcates here, poor collaterals → limb-threatening

### Growth

| Physis | Growth / yr |
|---|---|
| Proximal femur | 3 mm |
| **Distal femur** | **9 mm** (Orthobullets) · **10 mm** (TO) · 10–12 mm at peak |
| Proximal tibia | 6 mm |
| Distal tibia | 5 mm |

**Distal femur** — **70% of femoral growth, 37% of limb** (TO) · most lower-limb growth at the **knee**

**Epidemiology (TO)** — \~2% · peak **10–12 yr** · **males 6×** · neonates: birth injury or **NAI** (metaphyseal corner / bucket-handle fractures = abuse)

**Growth stops** — **14 yr girls, 16 yr boys** (Orthobullets) · fuses 14–16 ♀ / 16–18 ♂ (TO)

### Physeal histology – hypertrophic zone

**Fracture plane** — **hypertrophic zone** (chondrocytes **enlarge**, then **apoptosis** + matrix calcification in zone of provisional calcification)\
✗ Wrong options: proliferative (cells ↑ in number, max matrix) · reserve (precursors) · primary spongiosa (woven bone) · secondary spongiosa (lamellar)

**Distal femur SH I** — **propagates through multiple layers** (undulating physis) (OB)

## 2 Mechanism

**Mechanism** — direct trauma + rotation · **valgus** or **hyperextension** force · sports, vehicle

**Displacement direction** — anterior = hyperextension · posterior = hyperflexion · medial = valgus · lateral = varus

## 3 Presentation

**Exam** — pain, can't bear weight · knee held **flexed** (hamstring spasm) · **tenderness over physis** (medial epicondyle region) · effusion · varus/valgus "instability" · popliteal swelling → vascular injury

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Diagnosis – occult Salter-Harris I, MRI & imaging

**Initial** — **AP, lateral, oblique** knee\
✗ Wrong options: MRI first · stress views · CT · notch view

**Physeal widening** — normal 3–5 mm · compare contralateral

**Stress radiographs** — **fallen out of favour** (pain, sedation, physeal damage) → **MRI** (or US) replaced them

**MRI** — **modality of choice to confirm** occult physeal fracture\
✗ Wrong options: CT · weight-bearing views · aspiration/NSAID trial

**CT** — intra-articular extension, fixation planning

**Angiography** — **ABI <0.9** · wide displacement, posterior spike

**Alternative** — suspected SH I → long-leg cast + repeat X-ray 2–3 wk

### Key radiology findings

**Peterson classification (TO)** — I metaphyseal transverse into physis · II SH II equivalent · III through physis · IV epiphyseal along physis · V traverses all · **VI part of physis missing**

## 5 Management

### Operative criteria

| Fracture | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Nondisplaced** (incl. occult SH I) | **Long-leg cast 4–6 wk**, crutches, close follow-up (✗ Wrong options: hinged brace WBAT · immobiliser) |
| **Displaced SH I / II** (majority) | **Closed reduction + percutaneous fixation + cast** (unstable even if reduced) (✗ Wrong options: cast alone · traction · plate) |
| SH III / IV with anatomic closed reduction | CRPP |
| **SH III / IV** with articular displacement | **ORIF** (avoid physis) |
| **Irreducible SH I / II** | **Open reduction** — remove **periosteum on tension side** |

```
Distal femoral physeal fracture
├─ Nondisplaced → long-leg cast 4–6 wk, close follow-up
└─ Displaced → gentle reduction under GA (90% traction, 10% manipulation)
       ├─ SH I / SH II with small T-H fragment → smooth K-wires across physis (remove 3–6 wk)
       ├─ SH II with large T-H fragment → metaphyseal lag screws (avoid physis)
       └─ SH III / IV → epiphyseal lag screws (open if needed)
```

### Fixation – smooth pins vs screws (Thurston-Holland fragment)

**By SH type (TO)** — I: closed reduction, open if periosteum entrapped, smooth K-wires · II: **2 screws in Thurston-Holland fragment** if large, K-wires if small · III/IV: anatomic ORIF, screws · reduction = gentle traction, **knee flexion relaxes gastrocnemius**, recheck NV status

**Smooth K-wires across physis are safe (TO)** — few passes, low torque, central, avoid perichondrial ring · **growth arrest is from the injury, not the wires**

**Pins vs screws** — decided by **size of Thurston-Holland fragment** · large → **metaphyseal lag screws** · small / SH I → **smooth K-wires across physis** + supplemental cast

**SH I** — very unstable → **anatomic reduction + smooth pins + cast**\
✗ Wrong options: screws (↑ physeal injury) · blade plate · flexible nails

**Avoid** — threaded wires, screws, plates across physis (rigid tether)

**Pin direction** — retrograde (through joint) theoretical ↑ septic arthritis (no proven difference)

**Screws** — fully vs partially threaded: no difference with lag technique

### Vascular injury

**Pulses only on Doppler / ABI 0.8** → **urgent reduction + (percutaneous) fixation, then reassess** vascular status\
✗ Wrong options: arteriogram/CT/MRI first · cast · fasciotomy first · vascular exploration before reduction · plate across physis

**Highest risk** — **anterior epiphyseal displacement (hyperextension)** / **posterior metaphyseal spike** → treat like **knee dislocation** · arterial injury up to 20% in some series · compartment syndrome <1–3%

## 6 Complications

### Growth arrest risk – LLD & angular deformity

**Most common complication** — **physeal arrest → LLD / angular deformity**\
✗ Wrong options: nonunion (rare) · ligament injury · stiffness (transient) · AVN

**Physeal arrest rate** — **30–50%** (Orthobullets, OB "~40%") · **~60%** (OB) · 50–55% / 50–80% (OB) — read the options

**LLD >2 cm** — **one-third** of cases

| SH type | Growth disturbance |
|---|---|
| I | 36% |
| II | 58% |
| III | 49% |
| **IV** | **64% — highest** (✗ Wrong options: "type III maximal" (TO false statement)) |

**Pattern** — **SH I + V → LLD (non-angular)** · **SH II → angular deformity**

**Predictors of complications** — SH type · **displacement** · **hardware crossing physis** · articular incongruity · open fracture · surgical treatment · **younger age / less skeletal maturity** (tibial tuberosity ossification stage) · **NOT direction of displacement**

**Rate of arrest ↑** with displacement; older age (OB)

### LLD calculation
**Surveillance (TO)** — remove implants **3–4 months** · **MRI at 4–6 months (T1 spoiled gradient echo)** · follow to maturity · growth arrest **30–40%** (TO)


**Complete arrest LLD** = **remaining years × ~9–10 mm** · e.g. 11-yr girl (stops 14) → **3 cm** · 13-yr boy with 2 cm LLD (stops 16) → 20 + 27 = **~4.7–5 cm**, affected side short

### Physeal bar – resection

```
Post-traumatic distal femoral bar / LLD
├─ Predicted LLD <2 cm, no angulation → observe
├─ Bar <50%, ≥2 yr (2–2.5 cm) growth left → bar resection + PMMA / fat
│      (angulation >20° → add osteotomy)
└─ LLD 2–6 cm → contralateral distal femur ± proximal tibia epiphysiodesis
       (>5 cm → ipsilateral lengthening)
```

**11-yr, 5° varus, 25% bar, rest open** → **bar resection + PMMA** · results 62–90% good\
✗ Wrong options: observe · osteotomy · guided growth · epiphysiodesis

**12-yr girl after fixation (~2 yr growth left)** → **physeal disturbance — close observation** (predicted LLD <2 cm)\
✗ Wrong options: contralateral epiphysiodesis · lengthening

**Septic arthritis** — **intra-articular pins**

## X vs Y

| | **Smooth K-wires** | **Lag screws** |
|---|---|---|
| Use | **SH I**, SH II with **small T-H** fragment | SH II **large T-H** (metaphyseal), SH III/IV (epiphyseal) |
| Physis | Cross it (smooth) | **Parallel, avoid physis** |
| Removal | 3–6 wk | — |

## How it's asked

**"NOT true about distal femoral physeal fracture?"** → **type 3 has maximum LLD risk** (it's type 4) (TO)

**"Risk factors for complications — EXCEPT?"** → **direction of displacement**

**"SH II treated with lag screws — least predictive of outcome?"** → **direction of displacement**

**"11-yr girl with complete arrest — LLD?"** → **3 cm**

**"Closed reduction + cast at rural hospital — main complication?"** → **limb angular deformity**

**"Prognosis?"** → **high risk of premature arrest that frequently causes deformity**

**"13-yr fell from truck, SH II — likely complication?"** → **growth arrest**
