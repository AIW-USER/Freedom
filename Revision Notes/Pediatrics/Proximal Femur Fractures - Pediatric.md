# Proximal Femur Fractures - Pediatric

*Pediatrics · 88 / 112*

## 1 Basics

**Epidemiology** — **<1%** of pediatric fractures · **boys 2.5:1** · bimodal: **<2–3 yr (NAT)** and adolescents (MVA, ATV) · **high energy 75–80%** · low energy → pathologic bone

**Associated injuries** — **30–85%** (head/face, spleen, retroperitoneal bleed, perineum, pelvis/acetabulum, hip dislocation, femur)

### Anatomy

**Proximal femoral physis** — ossifies 4–6 months (girls), 5–7 (boys) · **3 mm/yr** · **13–15% of leg length**, 30% of femur · arrest → LLD >2 cm rare (only very young)

**Trochanteric apophysis** — ossifies ~4 yr · appositional GT growth · **GT injury/arrest → coxa VALGA** · **GT overgrowth → coxa VARA**

**Superior gluteal nerve (L4–S1)** — gluteus medius + minimus

### Vascular anatomy (MFCA)

| Age | Femoral head supply |
|---|---|
| **<4 yr** (OB: <18 months) | **MFCA + LFCA + ligamentum teres** (+ metaphyseal <3 yr) |
| ~18–36 months | Ligamentum teres stops first (Trueta) |
| **≥4 yr** (dominant by 6, Trueta) | **MFCA** via **posterosuperior + posteroinferior retinacular branches** |
| 3 to 14–17 yr | Physis blocks metaphyseal vessels |
| >14–17 yr | Metaphyseal-epiphyseal anastomoses |

**AVN after fracture** → retinacular branches of **MFCA**\
✗ Wrong options: LFCA · ligamentum teres

**Piriformis-entry nail contraindicated** → damages **posterosuperior MFCA** branches → AVN · use **lateral/trochanteric** entry for IM nails

### Deforming forces

**Subtrochanteric proximal fragment** — **flexion** (iliopsoas: L1–3 / femoral n.) · **abduction** (gluteus medius/minimus — **superior gluteal nerve**) · **external rotation** (short rotators — sciatic)

## 3 Presentation

**Exam** — severe hip pain, can't bear weight · **shortened + externally rotated** leg · <2–3 yr type I → think **NAT** (skeletal survey)

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

**X-ray** — AP + cross-table lateral · broken trabeculae at Ward triangle = undisplaced/impacted

**Occult / stress fracture** — **MRI best** (TO) · low-signal line + T2 edema · CT alternative · **US in infants** (hemarthrosis, epiphyseal mobility)

**Differential** — Perthes · transient synovitis · hemarthrosis · infection

### AVN risk by fracture type (Delbet)

| Type | Fracture | Incidence | AVN | Nonunion |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **I** | **Transphyseal** (IA no dislocation / **IB with epiphyseal dislocation**) | <10% | **38–100%** (IB ~100%) | — |
| **II** | **Transcervical** (most common) | 40–50% | **28%** (0–61% OB) | 15% |
| **III** | **Cervicotrochanteric / basicervical** | 30–35% (25–35% TO) | **18%** (27% OB · 20–30% OB) | 15–20% |
| **IV** | **Intertrochanteric** (best outcome) | 10–20% (6–15% TO) | **5%** (14% OB) | 5% |

**AVN ranking** — **IB > IA > II > III > IV** · I/II/III = 15×/6×/4× type IV risk · overall ~30%

## 5 Management

### Treatment & fixation

| Situation | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Nondisplaced I (no dislocation), II, III, IV **and <4 yr** | **Closed reduction + abduction spica** (rare) · OB: spica only <6 yr types III/IV |
| Displaced / >4 yr, types I–III | **CRPP** (smooth pins <4 yr · cannulated screws >4 yr) |
| Can't get anatomic closed reduction | **Open reduction + screws** (Watson-Jones) |
| **Type IV** displaced / >4 yr | **Pediatric (or adult) sliding hip screw** (lateral approach) |
| Open fracture · vessel repair · **epiphyseal / hip dislocation (IB)** · marked displacement | **Emergent ORIF + capsulotomy / aspiration** |

**Implant by age (OB)** — 0–3 yr smooth wires · 4–10 yr 4.5–6.5 mm cannulated screws · >10 yr 6.5–7.3 mm screws \
✗ Wrong options: **cephalomedullary nail** · immediate WB

**Physis** — **short of physis** <4–6 yr, most type III (less stable) · **transphyseal** >12 yr, little metaphysis, or needed for stability (**type II: transphyseal** — poor head purchase otherwise) · "easier to treat LLD than nonunion" · within 5 mm of subchondral bone, avoid anterolateral epiphysis + posterior neck

**Implant age cut-off** — smooth pins **< 4 yr** (Orthobullets) · **< 8 yr** smooth pins, **≥ 8 yr** cannulated screws (TO) — read the options · avoid multiple passes across physis (TO)

**TO principles** — **vascular emergency** · gentle reduction under GA · **capsular decompression** (tamponade by intracapsular haematoma) · internal fixation + spica · after **age 3** physis blocks metaphyseal supply → **lateral epiphyseal artery (MFCA)** main; **3–12 yr most vulnerable** · Delbet **II most common** (TO) · **nonunion up to 10%** (TO)

**Reduction** — gentle traction + **abduction + internal rotation** · weekly X-rays ×3 wk if casted · accept type II **<2 mm translation, <5°, no malrotation** · III/IV **<10°**

**Post-op** — spica (abduction + IR) **6–12 wk** if <4 yr or screws short of physis

### Minimising AVN - anatomic reduction

**Most important factor** — **anatomic reduction** · open reduction OK if it gets anatomy\
✗ Wrong options: timing · closed vs open · capsulotomy · transphyseal screw

**Timing** — reduction **<24 h** may ↓ AVN (Orthobullets) · surgery **<48 h** ↓ AVN (OB4584) · delay to 24 h doesn't ↑ complications; literature conflicting (OB) — fix with rested experienced team

**Capsulotomy** — favoured by some, unproven · **skeletal traction ↑ intracapsular pressure** (avoid)

## 6 Complications

**AVN** — **most common** · kinked/torn vessels, intracapsular tamponade · risk: **type (I highest)**, displacement, **age (×1.14 per year; >10 yr)**, delay >24 h, poor reduction · presents **2–12 months** (groin/knee pain) · Ratliff I whole head, II partial, III neck · **core decompression**, vascularised fibula · annual X-rays to maturity (≥2 yr in older teens)

**Coxa vara** (NSA <120°) — **2nd most common** · non-op treatment, types I–III (**25% type III**; ~15% OB) · 0–3 yr remodels if NSA >110° · **GT apophyseal arrest** (mild, <6–8 yr) · **valgus osteotomy** (nonunion, Trendelenburg, FAI, older)

**Coxa valga** — type IV in young child → premature GT apophysis closure

**Nonunion** — non-op II/III, occult infection, malreduction → **ORIF** (spica if young) ± graft

**Physeal arrest** — implants across physis, AVN (Ratliff II/III) · LLD: lift <2 cm, **contralateral distal femur ± proximal tibia epiphysiodesis 2–5 cm**

**Chondrolysis** — rare, with AVN or hardware penetration · TO: "**not a sequel**" in its MCQ (✓ answer) despite listing it as rare

**Order (TO)** — **osteonecrosis > coxa vara > physeal arrest/LLD > nonunion > infection > chondrolysis**

**Poor function** — head trauma · amputation · nerve damage · AVN
