# Legg-Calve-Perthes Disease

*Pediatrics · 60 / 112*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology – age

**Definition** — **idiopathic AVN of the proximal femoral epiphysis** in children · self-limiting, heals + remodels\
✗ Wrong options: adult osteonecrosis

**Age** — **4–8 yr** (Orthobullets) · TO answer **5–10 yr** — read the options\
✗ Wrong options: 4–8 yr in the TO MCQ; text says 4–10

**Other hip ages (TO)** — SCFE boys **12–15**, girls **11–13** · transient synovitis **4–10** (mean 6)

**Demographics** — **1 in 10,000** · **M:F 5:1** · urban · low socioeconomic class · high latitude · Caucasian > East Asian, African American

**Bilateral** — **12%** (Orthobullets) · 11% (OB) · **asymmetric + asynchronous** · symmetric, same stage → think **MED**

**Risk factors** — family history · **low birth weight** · abnormal birth presentation · **second-hand smoke** · Asian, Inuit, Central European

### Pathophysiology

**Sequence** — infarction → revascularisation → **resorption → collapse** → creeping substitution → remodeling

**Theories** — **coagulopathy** (protein C/S deficiency; thrombophilia 50%, **some coagulopathy up to 75%**) · repeated subclinical trauma (multiple-infarction theory) · smoking aggravates

**Associated** — **ADHD 33%** · **delayed bone age 89%** · hypercoagulability (TO)

**Histology** — disorganised cartilage, hypercellularity, fibrillation in epiphysis + physis

### Femoral head blood supply in childhood

| Age (TO) | Supply |
|---|---|
| **Birth → 4–7 months** | **MFCA + LFCA + artery of ligamentum teres** (retinacular vessels cross freely) |
| **4–7 months → 14–18 yr** | **MFCA ALONE** (physis blocks LFCA) · **superior retinacular (lateral epiphyseal) artery = main** |
| **Adult** | Mainly MFCA · small LFCA (anterior retinacular) + ligamentum teres |

**18 months** → **MFCA alone**\
✗ Wrong options: MFCA + LFCA · + ligamentum teres

## 3 Presentation

**Symptoms** — insidious **painless limp** · intermittent **hip, groin, thigh or knee** pain

**Exam** — loss of **internal rotation + abduction** · **Trendelenburg** (collapse → GT rises → slack gluteus medius) · **Catterall sign** = obligatory ER on hip flexion (TO) · LLD late, worsened by adduction contracture

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

**X-ray** — **AP pelvis + frog lateral**

**Earliest X-ray sign** — **medial joint space widening** (teardrop → ossific nucleus)

**Other** — small, sclerotic ossific nucleus · **crescent sign** = subchondral fracture · X-ray may stay normal **3–6 months**

**Bone scan** — **cold** (↓ uptake) before X-ray changes · extent of involvement

**MRI** — early / occult disease · **more sensitive** than X-ray

**Perfusion studies** — predict max lateral pillar involvement · **dynamic arthrogram** → coverage, containment, hinge abduction

### Waldenström stages

| Stage | X-ray | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Initial** | Small sclerotic epiphysis, **medial joint space widening** | Occult 3–6 months |
| **Fragmentation** | **Crescent sign**, head fragments/dissolves | **Most symptomatic** · **lateral pillar graded here** · 6 months–2 yr |
| **Reossification** | New bone replaces necrotic bone | Up to 18 months |
| **Healing / remodeling** | Head remodels to maturity | Trabeculae return |

### Lateral pillar classification timing

**Lateral pillar (Herring)** — **best agreement, most predictive** · graded in **early fragmentation**, **~6 months after symptom onset**\
✗ Wrong options: at first visit

| Group | Lateral pillar | Prognosis |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Full height**, no density change | Consistently good |
| **B** | **>50%** height | Poor if **bone age >6** (Orthobullets) · >6 yr age (TO) |
| **B/C** | Narrow (2–3 mm) / poorly ossified, ~50% | Added for consistency |
| **C** | **<50%** height | **Poor in all**, regardless of treatment |

### Catterall classification & head-at-risk signs

| Group | Head involvement |
|---|---|
| I | Anterior epiphysis only |
| II | Anterior + **central sequestrum** |
| III | Only a small part spared |
| IV | **Total head** |

**Head-at-risk signs (poor)** — **Gage sign** (lateral V-shaped lucency) · **calcification lateral to epiphysis** · **metaphyseal cyst** · **lateral subluxation** · **horizontal physis**

![Gage sign](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob4119-gage-sign.jpg)

**Salter-Thompson** — crescent sign **<½** head (A) vs **>½** (B)

**Stulberg** — **gold standard for residual head shape + congruence** at maturity · poor inter/intra-observer reliability

### Differential - bilateral disease workup

**Differential** — infection (septic hip, osteomyelitis, pyomyositis) · transient synovitis · **MED** · **SED** · sickle cell · Gaucher · hypothyroidism · **Meyers dysplasia**

**Symmetric bilateral changes** → **X-rays of knees (MED) and spine (SED)**\
✗ Wrong options: PT · brace · Salter

## 5 Management

**Goals** — symptom relief (NSAIDs, traction, crutches) · **restore ROM** (PT, muscle lengthening, Petrie casts) · **containment** (head seated in acetabulum)

```
Perthes disease
├─ <8 yr (bone age <6) or pillar A → observation, activity limits, ROM PT
├─ >8 yr (bone age >6), pillar B or B/C → femoral varus and/or pelvic osteotomy
├─ Pillar C → poor regardless of treatment
└─ Hinge abduction → valgus (abduction-extension) ± shelf osteotomy
```

### Treatment (osteotomy, containment, observation)

| Scenario | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **<8 yr** (bone age <6) · **pillar A** | **Observation**, activity restriction / protected weight bearing, **ROM PT** (60% need no surgery) |
| **>8 yr** (bone age >6) · **pillar B, B/C** | **Proximal femoral varus osteotomy** and/or **pelvic osteotomy** (Salter, triple) |
| Lateral subluxation | **Shelf** → prevents lateral epiphyseal overgrowth |
| **Hinge abduction** | **Valgus (abduction-extension) osteotomy** ± shelf — moves hinge away from acetabular edge, fixes adduction shortening, improves abductor length |
| Head no longer containable | Shelf or **Chiari** |
| Healed LCPD with FAI | Hip arthroscopy (emerging) |
| Arthrodiastasis (ex-fix distraction) | Controversial |

**Bracing / casting for containment** — **no benefit** in large prospective study

**Surgery evidence** — pillar A + B <8 yr do well regardless · surgery improves **B and B/C >8 yr** · earlier surgery before deformity may be best

**Varus derotation osteotomy** — early extrusion → re-seats head

**Never** — core decompression · hip aspiration (no infection)

**TO false statement** — "**early containment surgery before age 6 prevents arthritis**" is false · ✓ true: loss of abduction + IR · ✓ age at presentation = most important prognostic factor · ✓ 7-yr with maintained pillar → activity modification

**Valgus femoral osteotomy is for** — **Perthes with painful hinge abduction** \
✗ Wrong options: CP MI 40% (VDRO) · DDH AI 35° at 4 yr (pelvic osteotomy; AI should be <25° by 6 months) · coxa vara HE 35° (observe) · post-SCFE FAI (osteochondroplasty)

## 6 Complications

**Head deformity** — **coxa magna** (wide) · **coxa plana** (flat) · Stulberg grades it

**Lateral subluxation (extrusion)** — poor prognosis → **hinge abduction**

**Premature physeal arrest** — **trochanteric overgrowth** · **coxa breva** · mild LLD

**Other** — acetabular dysplasia · labral tear / FAI · OCD (loose bodies)

### Prognostic factors

**Best good prognostic factor** — **bone age <6 yr at presentation** · <6 yr with pillar A/B → **80% good outcome** (spherical, congruent)

**Clinical predictors** — **age at presentation + hip ROM**\
✗ Wrong options: LLD · pain duration

**Poor** — **female** · adduction contracture / ↓ abduction · heavy · long course · stiffness · head-at-risk signs · **pillar C** · bilateral · extensive subchondral fracture · age >9 worst (TO)

**Arthritis predictor** — **asphericity** of head at maturity → OA after 40\
✗ Wrong options: lateral calcification

**Natural history** — most fine until **5th–6th decade** · **~½ get premature OA** (aspherical head) · Stulberg I + most II do well lifelong · 2–5 yr course
