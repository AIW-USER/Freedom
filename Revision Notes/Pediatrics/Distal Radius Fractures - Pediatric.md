# Distal Radius Fractures - Pediatric

*Pediatrics · 37 / 112*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology – most common paediatric fracture

**Most common fracture <16 yr** — **distal radius (22.7%)** > hand phalanges (18.9%) > carpal/MC (8.3%) > clavicle (8.1%) > supracondylar (3.3%) > distal femur (1.6%)

**Forearm** — ~40% of pediatric long-bone fractures (OB: distal radius = 40% of all pediatric fractures) · **M 2–3×** · peak **10–12 yr ♀, 12–14 yr ♂** (metaphyseal growth spurt)

**Lifetime fracture risk <16 yr** — boys 42%, girls 27%

### Anatomy & remodelling

**Distal radial physis** — **75% of radial growth**, **40% of upper limb** · ~**5.25 mm/yr**

**Location** — **metaphysis 62%** > diaphysis 20% > physis

**Remodelling** — greatest **near physis** + in **plane of joint motion (sagittal — flexion/extension)** · ~**1°/month** · **least for rotation** (rotation doesn't remodel)

## 2 Mechanism

**FOOSH**, wrist extended · rule out **abuse** (inconsistent history, multiple-age injuries, grip marks)

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

| Type | Feature |
|---|---|
| **Torus / buckle** | **Unicortical**, nondisplaced, compression side |
| **Greenstick** | Fails on **tension (convex)** side; plastic deformation on compression side |
| Plastic deformation | Many microfractures, lower force over time |
| Complete metaphyseal | Apex volar (Colles-type) / apex dorsal (Smith-type) |
| Physeal | SH I–V (SH II most common) |
| **Galeazzi** | Distal-third radius + **DRUJ** injury |
| **Monteggia** | Ulna + radiocapitellar dislocation |

### Key radiology findings

**Views** — AP + lateral **wrist** and **forearm** · elbow if tender or diaphyseal · check SL interval, **DRUJ**, ulnar styloid

**CT** — intra-articular only (radiation)

## 5 Management

### Remodelling potential & acceptable angulation (buckle fractures need no follow-up)

| Age | Bayonet | Shaft angulation | Malrotation | **Distal radius dorsal angulation** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **<10 yr** | **<1 cm** | 15–20° | 45° | **30°** |
| **>10 yr** | **None** | 10° | 30° | **20°** |

**Other cut-offs** — young child remodels sagittal deformity up to **~20°**, older **~10°** (OB) · operate <9 yr if post-reduction **dorsal angulation >30°** or malrotation · >10 yr: angulation >20°, rotation >30°

**Diaphyseal 10° angulation** → blocks 20–30° rotation

**4-yr, 12° apex volar at 2 weeks** → **observe — remodels within ~1 year**\
✗ Wrong options: osteoclasis · re-reduction · 2 years

**9-yr, 18° unreduced metaphyseal deformity** → **no long-term deformity or deficit**

**Buckle (torus) fracture** → **removable splint, no cast, no follow-up** · same pain + refracture as cast\
✗ Wrong options: physeal-arrest monitoring · close follow-up

### Operative criteria

| Situation | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Unicortical/bicortical, **<10°**, buckle | **SAC 2–3 wk** / removable splint |
| **>10–20° angulation**, SH I/II unacceptable | **Closed reduction + cast** |
| Unstable / **lost reduction** in cast / reduced only in OR / SH I–II with **NV compromise** | **CRPP** |
| **Displaced SH III/IV** · **irreducible** (periosteum, **pronator quadratus**) | **ORIF** |
| Near-mature, redisplaced shaft / bayonet (15-yr) | **ORIF** (✗ Wrong options: accept · remanipulate) |

```
Pediatric distal radius fracture
├─ Buckle → removable splint, no follow-up
├─ Angulated beyond limits → closed reduction + well-moulded cast (index ≤0.7–0.8)
│      └─ Redisplaced (unacceptable) → re-reduce + percutaneous pins
└─ Irreducible / SH III–IV displaced → open reduction
```

### Cast index & loss of reduction – management of redisplacement

**Cast index** — **sagittal (lateral) ÷ coronal (AP) inner width** · **best predictor** of failure · good ≈ **0.7** · loss of reduction **>0.8** (mean 0.79 in failures) · OB answer **>0.85**\
✗ Wrong options: padding, gap, three-point, 2nd MC–radius indices

![Cast index = lateral ÷ AP inner width](../../MCQ/attachments/q3289-illa-cast-index.jpg)

**Short vs long arm cast** — well-moulded SAC **= LAC** (no ↑ displacement, fewer missed school days)\
✗ Wrong options: LAC/SAC/plaster/sedation as risks

**Redisplacement** — **20–25%** early · risks: **poor cast index**, ↑ **initial displacement**, incomplete reduction

**11-yr, redisplaced to 22° at 1 week despite good mould** → **closed reduction + percutaneous fixation**\
✗ Wrong options: accept · recast · flexible nail (too distal) · plate

**Physeal reduction** — gentle, **one attempt**, **not >1 week** after injury (late reduction → arrest)

**Technique** — metaphyseal: recreate deformity, use periosteal hinge (traction counterproductive) · intact ulna: ulnar-deviate wrist

### Reduction manoeuvre – greenstick fractures

**Rotate the palm toward the apex** — **apex-volar (supination injury) → pronate** · apex-dorsal (pronation injury) → supinate

### CRPP technique

**Radial styloid pins** — entry just proximal to physis (smooth if transphyseal) · avoid **dorsal sensory radial nerve** · dorsal pin for volar tilt · DRUJ pin → long-arm cast

## 6 Complications

### Cast thermal injury

| Risk factor | |
|---|---|
| **Dip water >24 °C (75 °F)** | Yes |
| **>8 plaster layers** / thick cast | Yes |
| **Resting on pillow while curing** (most significant) | Yes |
| **Fibreglass over plaster** | Yes |
| Excess moulding pressure | Yes |
| **Fracture pattern** | **not a risk** |
| 22 °C water, thin fibreglass, single padding layer | No |

### Physeal growth arrest (radius/ulna) – CT & epiphysiodesis

**Distal ulnar physis** — **most likely to arrest** (**30–40%**) vs distal radius **<5%**

**Radial arrest** → ulnar-positive variance → **ulnocarpal impaction**, TFCC + DRUJ injury, radial deviation · variance changes visible **3–4 months**

**Risk factors** — repeated injury, **late presentation/late reduction**, multiple attempts, SH III/IV

```
Distal radial physeal arrest
├─ First → CT (physeal map) to size the bar
├─ Bar <50% + growth left → bar resection
├─ Bar >50% / little growth → complete radial + ulnar epiphysiodesis
│      (± radial lengthening for established discrepancy)
└─ Skeletally mature, ulnar-positive → ulnar shortening osteotomy
```

**10-yr, painful radial deviation after SH II** → **CT to assess bar** first\
✗ Wrong options: osteotomy · bilateral epiphysiodesis

**14-yr asymptomatic, radial arrest, small discrepancy** → **ulnar epiphysiodesis**\
✗ Wrong options: bar resection · lengthening · styloid repair

**>50% radial bar, ulnar-positive, immature** → **distal ulna epiphysiodesis**\
✗ Wrong options: mature (ulnar shortening) · Kienböck · Madelung (Vickers ligament release)

**Other** — **malunion (most common complication)** · median neuropathy / acute carpal tunnel · cast-saw burns (cool blade)

## How it's asked

**"4-yr, 12° apex volar at 2 weeks?"** → **observe — remodels within 1 year**

**"9-yr, 18° unreduced?"** → **no long-term deformity or deficit**

**"2-yr buckle fracture — true?"** → **no cast or follow-up needed**

**"Risk of displacement after reduction?"** → **cast index >0.85**\
✗ Wrong options: long/short arm cast · plaster · sedation

**"11-yr redisplaced 22° at 1 week?"** → **closed reduction + percutaneous fixation**

**"15-yr redisplaced distal radial shaft, physis closing?"** → **ORIF**

**"Best predictor of failure of closed treatment?"** → **cast index**

**"Isolated pronation reduces?"** → **apex-volar greenstick (supination injury)**

**"Thermal injury risk — EXCEPT?"** → **fracture pattern**

**"Most significant cast-burn risk?"** → **resting wet cast on pillow**

**"9-yr radius + ulna physeal fracture — likely complication?"** → **distal ulna growth arrest**

**"14-yr, radial arrest, asymptomatic?"** → **ulnar epiphysiodesis**

**"10-yr painful deformity after SH II?"** → **CT to evaluate arrest**

**"Best treated by distal ulna epiphysiodesis?"** → **immature >50% radial bar with ulnar-positive variance**

**"Most common fracture <16 yr?"** → **distal radius**
