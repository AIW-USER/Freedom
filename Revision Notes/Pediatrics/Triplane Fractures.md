# Triplane Fractures

*Pediatrics · 112 / 112*

## 1 Basics

**Definition** — complex **Salter-Harris IV** pattern in **3 planes** · **transitional** fracture (physis closing) · 2, 3 or 4 parts

**Epidemiology** — **5–15% of pediatric ankle fractures** · **males** · ages 10–17 · **mean 13 yr** · **younger than Tillaux**

**Physeal closure** — central → anteromedial → posteromedial → lateral (Orthobullets) · central → medial → lateral (OB) · 18-month transitional period

**Associated** — **fibula fracture 50%** (spiral, above physis · unstable after tibial fixation → **fix fibula**) · ipsilateral tibial shaft fracture

## 2 Mechanism

### Mechanism

| Pattern | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| **Lateral triplane** | **Supination–external rotation** (like Tillaux) |
| Medial triplane | **Adduction** |

**Asked** — **external rotation**\
✗ Wrong options: internal rotation · supination–adduction · pronation–abduction · axial load

## 3 Presentation

**Findings** — pain · can't bear weight · swelling · focal tenderness · deformity

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Radiographic fracture pattern

| Plane | Structure | Seen on |
|---|---|---|
| **Sagittal** | **Epiphysis** (lateral) | **AP X-ray → SH III** |
| Axial (transverse) | Physis | — |
| **Coronal** | **Posterior metaphysis** | **Lateral X-ray → SH II** |

**Overall** — SH IV pattern, but **no true SH IV component** on either view\
✗ Wrong options: SH II on AP · SH III on lateral

**von Laer** — SH III epiphyseal component on AP depends only on **physeal maturity**, not mechanism · biplane vs triplane type I (metaphyseal line ends in physis) vs type II (into epiphysis + joint)

### Key radiology findings

**X-ray** — AP · lateral · **mortise = best for displacement**

**CT** — usually **required** (pattern + articular congruity) · after closed reduction · all 3 planes · **"Mercedes-Benz sign"** · 4-part only seen on CT

### Classification by parts

| Parts | Fragments |
|---|---|
| **2-part** | (1) anterolateral + posterior epiphysis with posterior metaphyseal fragment · (2) anteromedial epiphysis with rest of tibia |
| **3-part** | (1) anterolateral epiphysis · (2) posterior epiphysis + posterior metaphyseal fragment · (3) anteromedial epiphysis + rest of tibia |
| **4-part** | Comminuted · **CT only** separates from 3-part |

### Classification by pattern

| Pattern | Epiphysis | Metaphysis | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Lateral** | **Sagittal** | **Coronal** | **Most common** · looks like Tillaux on AP → SH II on lateral distinguishes |
| Medial | Coronal | Sagittal | — |
| Intramalleolar | I — intra-articular, weight-bearing · II — intra-articular, non-weight-bearing · III — extra-articular | — | — |

## 5 Management

### Surgical indications & articular gap outcomes

### Operative criteria

| Displacement | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **<2 mm** | **Closed reduction + long leg cast 3–4 wk** → short leg cast / boot 2–4 wk · ideal for **2-part** |
| **>2 mm** (Orthobullets) · >2.5 mm (OB220849) | **CRPP or ORIF** |

**Closed reduction** — **reduce fibula first** · lateral → **internal rotation** · medial → **eversion** (closed) / external rotation (operative) · post-reduction CT

**ORIF** — anterolateral approach (lateral) · anteromedial (medial) · arthroscopic assist described · K-wires / cannulated screws · **epiphyseal screws parallel to physis** · metaphyseal screw if large · long leg cast 3–4 wk → short leg walking cast 2 wk · hardware irritation, transient neuropathy

**Why operate** — **restore articular congruity → ↓ posttraumatic arthritis** (near maturity)\
✗ Wrong options: avoid growth arrest · osteonecrosis · rotation deformity · tarsal tunnel

**Residual gap** — **>2.5 mm → worse functional outcome**\
✗ Wrong options: observation fine to 5 mm · bracing / WB protocol matters more · arthritis risk negligible · physeal closure dominates

## 6 Complications

**Growth arrest** — **7–21%** · ↑ **pronation–abduction** vs SER · ↑ residual displacement · usually insignificant · watch if **>2 yr growth left**

**Ankle pain / degeneration** — ↑ with **step-off >2 mm**

## X vs Y

| | **Triplane** | **Tillaux** |
|---|---|---|
| Age | **Younger** (mean 13) | Older |
| Sex | Males | Girls (Orthobullets) |
| Incidence (peds ankle) | 5–15% | 3–5% |
| Lateral X-ray | **Coronal posterior metaphyseal fracture (SH II)** | None — entirely epiphyseal |
| Surgery | >2 mm | >2 mm |

## How it's asked

**"15-yr skateboarder, triplane — mechanism?"** → **external rotation**\
✗ Wrong options: supination–adduction · axial load · pronation–abduction

**"Most common triplane — fracture lines and views?"** → **sagittal epiphyseal on AP + coronal metaphyseal on lateral**

**"Most common triplane — TRUE?"** → **SH III component seen on AP**\
✗ Wrong options: SH II on AP · SH IV on either view

**"14-yr failed closed reduction — main reason for surgery?"** → **↓ posttraumatic arthritis**\
✗ Wrong options: avoid growth arrest · osteonecrosis

**"13-yr post-op CT 3 mm gap — counsel?"** → **residual gap >2.5 mm → worse function**\
✗ Wrong options: observe to 5 mm · arthritis negligible
