# Traumatic Hip Dislocation - Pediatric

*Pediatrics · 111 / 112*

## 1 Basics

**Epidemiology** — **80% posterior** · **more common than hip fracture** in children

**Mechanism** — **<10 yr: low-energy** sport / trip and fall (lax ligaments, cartilaginous acetabulum; <6 yr minimal force, OB) · **>10 yr: high-energy** (MVA)

**Associated** — acetabular fractures **less common than adults** (posterior wall most common) · femoral head / neck / proximal femoral physis fracture · missed ipsilateral femoral shaft fracture (OB) · **AVN ↑ if not reduced within 6 h**

## 3 Presentation

| Direction | Limb position |
|---|---|
| **Posterior** (most common) | **Slight flexion, adduction, internal rotation** · LLD (large posterior wall fragment → short without malalignment) |
| Anterior | Slight flexion, **abduction, external rotation** |
| Inferior | External rotation + extension |

**Nerves** — **sciatic or gluteal** palsy (rare) · usually resolves after reduction

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

**X-ray** — **AP pelvis** (most diagnosed) · **lateral** (anterior vs posterior; check femoral neck before reduction) · **post-reduction films → joint incongruity / nonconcentric reduction**

**MRI** — **study of choice** for abnormal post-reduction films (joint widening) · less radiation · **best for interposed soft tissue, entrapped labrum / capsule**

**CT** — **2nd choice** · **osteochondral fragments** (older children) · soft-tissue interposition hard to see

## 5 Management

```
Pediatric hip dislocation
├─ First → urgent closed reduction under GA / sedation, within 6 h (85% succeed)
├─ Post-reduction films normal → spica / abduction splint (<10 yr) or protected WB (older)
└─ Nonconcentric / fragment / irreducible → MRI or CT, then open reduction
       └─ Approach → in direction of dislocation (usually posterior)
```

**Closed reduction** — **adequate anesthesia mandatory** (avoid displacing unrecognized **proximal femoral epiphyseal fracture**) · **under fluoroscopy** (epiphyseolysis risk) · traction in flexion + gentle rotation · test stability before waking · post-reduction imaging

**Aftercare** — spica / bed rest with abduction splint **4 wk** if <10 yr · brace + **6–12 wk** protected WB in older

### Operative criteria

| Open reduction indications |
|---|
| **Nonconcentric reduction** |
| **Intra-articular fragment** |
| Unstable acetabular rim fracture · femoral head / neck fracture |
| **Irreducible closed** |

### Non-concentric reduction - arthrotomy

**Persistent incongruity** — soft-tissue interposition (**torn labrum + lateral acetabular apophysis**) · osteochondral fragment → **hip arthrotomy via POSTERIOR approach** (posterior structures already torn)\
✗ Wrong options: anterior approach · repeat closed reduction · traction 1 month · WBAT

## 6 Complications

### AVN with delayed reduction

**AVN** — **3–15%** (Orthobullets) · 0–43% (OB) · ↓ under age 5 · less than adults (without neck fracture) · **only complication linked to delay in reduction** · >24 h → 66% (OB)\
✗ Wrong options: coxa magna · redislocation · chondrolysis · HO (not time-dependent)

| Complication | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Coxa magna** | **20%**, radiographic, no functional limit |
| Redislocation | Rare · prolonged immobilization → capsulorrhaphy if recurrent |
| Nerve | Sciatic / gluteal, usually resolves |
| Other (OB) | Femoral head deformity · premature physeal fusion · degenerative arthritis · chondrolysis |

**Prognosis** — good when treated promptly · most return to high-demand activity

## How it's asked

**"10-yr post-reduction CT — joint widened, fragment?"** → **hip arthrotomy via posterior approach**\
✗ Wrong options: anterior approach · repeat closed reduction · traction

**"3-yr MVC hip dislocation — delayed concentric reduction ↑ risk of?"** → **avascular necrosis**\
✗ Wrong options: coxa magna · recurrent dislocation · chondrolysis · HO
