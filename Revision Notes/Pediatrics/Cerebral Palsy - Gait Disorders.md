# Cerebral Palsy - Gait Disorders

*Pediatrics · 15 / 112*

## 1 Basics

**Gait disorder** — **primary reason for orthopaedic consultation** in CP

### Ambulatory prognosis & milestones

**Independent walking** — expected **12–18 months**\
✗ Wrong options: towers of 4 blocks = 2–3 yr · transfers toys = 6–9 mo · jumping = 2–3 yr · overhand throw = 18–24 mo

**Good prognostic sign for walking** — **positive parachute reaction** (reaches toward floor when lowered prone)

**Poor signs** — **Moro beyond 6 months** · **asymmetric tonic neck reflex**, **extensor thrust**, **absent foot placement** (abnormal at any age)

### Pathophysiology – deviations

| Level | Definition | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary** | Direct CNS insult | **Spasticity**, weakness, ↓ proprioception |
| **Secondary** | Growth-related from abnormal loading | **Myostatic contractures**, **femoral anteversion**, hip subluxation, equinoplanovalgus |
| **Tertiary** | **Compensations** for secondary deviations | — |

**Modern approach** — define deviation, **plane** (sagittal, coronal, transverse) and **level** (hip, knee, ankle)

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Gait patterns – crouch, jump & stiff-knee (rectus femoris)

| Pattern | Hip | Knee | Ankle | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **True equinus** | Normal | Normal / hyperextended | **Equinus** (foot < plantigrade) | Isolated, **hemiplegics** · GRF anterior to knee |
| **Jump gait** | **Flexed** | **Flexed** | **Equinus** | + anterior pelvic tilt · diplegia |
| **Apparent equinus** | Flexed | Flexed | **Plantigrade-capable** but toe-walks | Proximal deviation (knee flexion) |
| **Crouch gait** | **Flexed** | **Flexed** | **Excess dorsiflexion (calcaneus)** | GRF **posterior to knee** · diplegia, quadriplegia at puberty |
| **Stiff-knee gait** | — | **↓ swing flexion** | — | **Rectus femoris** out of phase |
| Asymmetric | Each limb in a different group | | | — |

**Crouch causes** — **iatrogenic isolated TAL in jump gait** · pubertal growth spurt → ↓ strength-to-weight · breaks plantarflexion–knee-extension couple → very inefficient, tiring

**Stiff-knee gait** — **rectus femoris overactive in pre-swing + early swing** · EMG: quadriceps active from **terminal stance through swing** · compensation from hip deviation → surgery won't help\
✗ Wrong options: adductor magnus · gluteus medius · sartorius · TFL

**Descriptive classifications** — fail to classify **up to 40%** of patterns

### Key radiology findings & gait analysis

**Quantitative gait analysis** — **more accurate than observation** · physical exam (spasticity, contractures, torsion) · **kinetics** (forces, moments) · **kinematics** (3-plane motion) · **pedobarography** · **dynamic EMG** · video

**Planes** — sagittal: pelvic tilt, hip/knee/ankle flexion · coronal: pelvic obliquity, hip ab/adduction · **transverse = least reliable** (rotation, foot progression)

## 5 Management

### Equinus management – AFO, serial casting & gastrocnemius recession

| Orthosis | Indication |
|---|---|
| **Solid AFO** | Flexible equinus — ankle passively correctable to neutral (subtalar neutral) · stance + swing control · excessive midstance DF |
| **Posterior leaf-spring (PLSO) / hinged** | **Absent heel strike + excessive swing plantarflexion + some passive DF (~5°)** |
| **Floor-reaction AFO** | **Crouch** gait |
| **KAFO** | Knee instability in minimally ambulatory/standers |
| **SMO** | Flexible **coronal** hindfoot supination/pronation |

**3-year-old diplegic (PVL, walked at 2), mild contractures, gastroc tightness** → **ankle bracing + therapy**\
✗ Wrong options: percutaneous/open TAL (surgery not indicated at this age) · steroids (dystrophy)

**Botulinum toxin A** — temporise / primary · **hamstring spasticity without fixed deformity** in ambulators

### Silfverskiöld + popliteal angle — choosing surgery

| DF knee extended | DF knee flexed | Popliteal angle | Procedure |
|---|---|---|---|
| **−5°** | **+20°** | **25° (normal)** | **Gastrocnemius recession only** ✓ |
| −5° | +20° | 55° | Gastroc recession **+ hamstring lengthening** |
| −10° | −10° | 25° | **TAL** (Achilles) |
| −20° | −20° | 55° | TAL + hamstring lengthening |
| +20° | +20° | 55° | Hamstring lengthening only |

![Silfverskiöld test](../../MCQ/attachments/q9027-illa-silfverskiold.jpg)

**Normal popliteal angle ~25°** · **>50°** = significant hamstring tightness

**Gastrocnemius recession** — **Silfverskiöld positive** · protect **sural nerve** (superficial to fascia) · divide tendon only · goal **10° DF**

**TAL** — rigid (not correctable to neutral), true equinus, Silfverskiöld negative · **contraindicated in spastic diplegia** → weakness, **calcaneus/crouch**

### Operative criteria — SEMLS

**SEMLS** — **gold standard** · correct all primary + secondary deviations at multiple levels in **one** operation (avoids iatrogenic worsening) · AFOs + aggressive PT · **~1 year recovery**

| Deviation | SEMLS procedure / criterion |
|---|---|
| ↑ Femoral anteversion (lever-arm) | **External rotation proximal femoral osteotomy** |
| Hip flexion contracture | **Intramuscular psoas lengthening** |
| Mild knee flexion (<5° fixed), young | **Medial hamstring fractional lengthening** (✗ Wrong options: lateral (excess weakness)) |
| Knee flexion **10–25°**, **≥2 yr growth** | **Guided growth** (anterior distal femur) |
| Knee flexion **10–30°** + quad lag, at/near maturity | **Distal femoral extension osteotomy** ± patellar tendon advancement |
| **Stiff-knee gait** | **Distal rectus femoris transfer** posterior to knee axis (better than release) |
| Equinus | TAL or gastrocnemius recession |
| Flatfoot | Reconstruction |

### Selective dorsal rhizotomy

**Permanent tone reduction** — **selective posterior (dorsal) rhizotomy**\
✗ Wrong options: tone-reducing AFOs · botox (wears off 3–6 mo via axonal sprouting) · single intrathecal baclofen injection (pump gives prolonged reduction) · fractional lengthening

**Rhizotomy drawbacks** — excess weakness · **hip dislocation** · **spinal deformity**

## 6 Complications

**Recurrent hamstring contracture** — esp. jump gait

**Worsening crouch** — isolated / over-lengthened **Achilles**

**Patella alta** — multiple releases **without gait analysis** · hard to treat

**Knee pain** — TAL without gait analysis

## X vs Y

| | **Jump gait** | **Crouch gait** |
|---|---|---|
| Ankle | **Equinus** | **Excess dorsiflexion / calcaneus** |
| Hip + knee | Flexed | Flexed |
| GRF at knee | Anterior (early) | **Posterior** |
| Cause | Spastic gastroc + hamstrings + psoas | **Isolated TAL**, growth spurt weakness |
| Orthosis | — | **Floor-reaction AFO** |

## How it's asked

**"Crouch gait combination?"** → **hip flexion, knee flexion, ankle dorsiflexion**

**"Stiff-knee gait in spastic diplegia — overactive muscle?"** → **rectus femoris**

**"Equinus, knee + hip flexion contractures, anterior pelvic tilt?"** → **jump gait**\
✗ Wrong options: crouch (calcaneus) · apparent equinus

**"Posterior leaf-spring AFO appropriate for?"** → **absent heel strike, swing plantarflexion, 5° passive DF**

**"3-year-old hemiplegic, AFO not fitting, DF −10° after botox?"** → **serial casting**\
✗ Wrong options: repeat botox <3 mo · baclofen pump · heel-cord lengthening

**"3-year-old diplegic toe-walker, mild contractures?"** → **ankle bracing and therapy**

**"Hemiplegic, ONLY gastrocnemius recession needed?"** → **DF −5° extended, +20° flexed, popliteal angle 25°**

**"Milestone at 12–18 months?"** → **walks without assistance**

**"Good prognostic sign for walking at 22 months?"** → **positive parachute reaction**

**"Permanent decrease in tone?"** → **selective posterior rhizotomy**
