# Obstetric Brachial Plexopathy (Erb's, Klumpke's Palsy)

*Pediatrics · 73 / 112*

## 1 Basics

**Definition** — birth stretch injury of brachial plexus, usually difficult vaginal delivery · rare after C-section

**Incidence** — **1–4 per 1,000** (Orthobullets) · 0.9/1,000 · 0.38–1.56/1,000 (OB) · 0.1–0.4% (TO) · falling with better obstetric care · often right-sided or bilateral

### Risk factors

**Greatest risk** — **shoulder dystocia**\
✗ Wrong options: macrosomia · gestational diabetes · race

**Others** — **macrosomia** (LGA) · multiparous · difficult presentation · **forceps** · **breech** (→ lower plexus) · **prolonged labor** · maternal diabetes · induced labor · prior affected child · **50% have no risk factor**

**Other risk factors (TO)** — **bicornuate uterus** (in utero) · **macrosomia > 4.5 kg** · birth asphyxia · vacuum · humeral / clavicular fracture · **C-section reduces but does not eliminate** risk

### Anatomy & injury patterns (Erb upper trunk)

| Narakas group | Roots | Deficit | Frequency (TO) | Spontaneous recovery (TO) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **I — Erb-Duchenne** | **C5–C6** | **Deltoid + biceps** paralysed · wrist/digit flex/ext intact | **46%** (most common) | **>80%** |
| II — Intermediate / extended Erb | **C5–C7** | + **wrist and digit extension** (wrist drop) | 30% | ~60% |
| III — Total | C5–T1 | **Flail**, no Horner | 20% | Shoulder/elbow 30–50% |
| IV — Total + Horner | C5–T1 | Flail + **Horner** | — | **Worst** |
| Klumpke | C8–T1 | Claw hand | **<1%** (TO) · 2–3% (TO) | Poor |

**Erb frequency elsewhere** — 50–60% (TO) · >80% (OB) — read the options

**OB212897 variant** — most common injury = deltoid + biceps paralysis **+ weak wrist/digit extension** (C6) · isolated deltoid + biceps = C5 only

## 2 Mechanism

**Erb** — **lateral flexion of head away + depression of ipsilateral shoulder** (shoulder dystocia)

**Klumpke** — **traction on abducted arm** / arm presentation (breech)

**Total** — stretch, rupture + avulsion

## 3 Presentation

**Erb (waiter's tip)** — shoulder **adducted + internally rotated**, elbow **extended**, forearm **pronated**, wrist flexed · C5: axillary (deltoid, teres minor), suprascapular (SS, IS), musculocutaneous (biceps, brachialis) · C6: radial (brachioradialis, supinator)

**Klumpke** — **claw hand**: wrist extended (unopposed extensors), MCP hyperextended, IP flexed · intrinsics (ulnar + median) lost · often **preganglionic + Horner**

**Total** — flaccid arm, motor + sensory loss · **CXR for hemidiaphragm (phrenic)**

**Exam tools** — Moro, asymmetric tonic neck, Vojta reflexes · pain on gentle shaking of flail arm → **pseudoparalysis** (infection / fracture)

**Associated** — clavicle + humerus fractures (no prognostic value) · torticollis

![Klumpke claw hand](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob4117-klumpke.jpg)

### Assessment tools

| Tool | Use |
|---|---|
| **Toronto Test Score** | Surgical indications, post-reconstruction assessment |
| **Hospital for Sick Children Active Movement Scale** | Whole plexus, **15 movements** · must reach **4 (full ROM gravity eliminated)** before higher scores |
| **(Modified) Mallet** | **Most common outcome measure** · weighted to ER · Abzug added hand-to-navel (IR) |
| Wechsler (WISC-IV) | Not used — neuropsychological IQ test (TO) |

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

**X-ray** — clavicle/humerus fractures · little use in infant shoulder (unossified) · **axillary view** in older child for subluxation

**Ultrasound** — glenohumeral subluxation/dislocation in infants

**Myelography / CT myelography / MRI** — **root avulsion (preganglionic) vs extraforaminal rupture** · MRI may show C6 avulsion

**EMG/NCV** — poor reliability, **underestimates** severity · baseline at 4 wk, repeat at 3 months if no recovery (TO)

**Preganglionic (root avulsion) signs**

| Sign | Nerve |
|---|---|
| **Horner** (ptosis, miosis, anhidrosis) | Sympathetic chain / T1 |
| **Elevated hemidiaphragm** | Phrenic |
| **Winged scapula** | Long thoracic |
| **Absent rhomboids** | Dorsal scapular |
| Absent rotator cuff | Suprascapular |
| Absent latissimus | Thoracodorsal |

## 5 Management

```
Obstetric brachial plexus palsy
├─ All → observation + daily passive ROM by parents (first 3 months)
├─ Antigravity biceps by 3 months → continue therapy, expect full recovery
├─ No biceps by 3 months, postganglionic → neuroma excision + sural graft (3–9 months)
└─ Preganglionic (Horner, phrenic, winging, rhomboids) → nerve transfer at ~3 months
```

### Prognosis (biceps recovery by 3 months, Horner)

**Overall** — **90% recover** without surgery · recovery up to **2 yr** · most with elbow flexion by 3 months normal by 18 months

**Good** — **Erb palsy** (absent shoulder abduction + ER, intact wrist/hand = best) · **antigravity biceps + deltoid by 3 months** · **early biceps twitch** · elbow flexion by 3 months → recovery up to 100% · partial antigravity strength in first 2 months

**Key indicator** — **active elbow flexion (biceps, musculocutaneous) at 3 months**\
✗ Wrong options: shoulder abduction · ER · elbow extension · axillary at 1 yr

**Poor** — **no biceps by 3 months** · **preganglionic injury (worst)** · **Horner → <10% recover** motor function · **C7 involvement** · **Klumpke** · total plexus\
✗ Wrong options: 30–40%

**No prognostic value** — clavicle fracture · age at diagnosis · head circumference

### Indications for microsurgery / nerve transfer

| Lesion | Surgery | Timing |
|---|---|---|
| **Postganglionic** (rupture, neuroma-in-continuity) | **Neuroma excision + sural nerve grafting** (shorter grafts <10 cm better · no direct repair) | **3–9 months** (Gilbert: biceps < MRC 3 at 3 months → surgery at 4–6 months, TO) |
| **Preganglionic** (avulsion) | **Nerve transfer / neurotization** (grafting impossible) | **~3 months** (or less) |
| Global palsy + Horner | Microsurgery | ~3 months (TO) · flail + Horner at 1 month (OB) |

**Nerve transfer** — fascicles from one nerve into another nerve's motor branch · **neurotization** = fascicles directly into neuromuscular junction

**Transfers** — **ulnar fascicle → biceps branch (Oberlin)** + **median fascicle → brachialis branch** · accessory → suprascapular (abduction) · intercostals → musculocutaneous (elbow flexion) · triceps (long head) / thoracodorsal → axillary · contralateral C7 in total avulsion

**Most important activity for timing surgery** — **return of elbow flexion** (TO)

**13-yr traumatic plexus injury, 5 months, no Horner, Tinel at clavicle** → **exploration + nerve grafting** (TO)\
✗ Wrong options: neurotization (preganglionic only) · tendon transfer (later)

**Urgent sub-specialist referral at 7 weeks** → **Horner (miosis, anhidrosis)**\
✗ Wrong options: absent biceps (normal at this age) · Moro present

**Cookie test (TO)** — normal: brings food to mouth with elbow flexion + **shoulder adduction** · **failed at 9 months → surgery**

**Intervene (TO)** — **no biceps recovery at 3 months** or **failed cookie test at 9 months**

### Glenohumeral dysplasia & IR contracture

**Cause** — strong internal rotators vs weak external rotators → **IR contracture** (most common problem needing treatment) → **glenohumeral dysplasia in 70%** · by age 2

**Deformity** — **glenoid hypoplasia + RETROVERSION** · **posterior humeral head subluxation** · **humeral head flattening** · pseudoglenoid ·\
✗ Wrong options: anteversion · anterior-superior escape · anterior subluxation

**Zancolli (TO)** — most common: **IR-adduction contracture with joint deformity + posterior subluxation/dislocation** (>60%)

**Putti sign** — prominent scapula on adduction

**Before shoulder reconstruction** — **MRI of shoulder**\
✗ Wrong options: EMG · brain MRI

| Waters type | Feature | Treatment (OB5599) |
|---|---|---|
| I | <5° retroversion difference | — |
| II | >5° retroversion difference | **Latissimus + teres major transfer** |
| III | Posterior subluxation (<35% anterior to scapular spine axis) | Transfer ± open/arthroscopic reduction + anterior release |
| IV | **False glenoid** | Reduction + release, or **humeral derotation osteotomy** |
| V | Flattened head, progressive / complete dislocation | **Humeral derotation osteotomy** |
| VI | Infantile posterior dislocation | — |
| VII | Proximal humeral growth arrest | — |

![Waters classification](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob4117-waters-classification.jpg)

### Secondary reconstruction (tendon transfer, osteotomy, elbow contracture)

**Hoffer (latissimus + teres major → posterior rotator cuff)** — IR contracture / ER weakness **without dysplasia** (congruent joint) · ± pectoralis major / subscapularis lengthening · arthroscopic release

**Hoffer contraindications** — **convex or absent glenoid** · **complete posterior dislocation** · **humeral head flattening** · latissimus/teres major <4/5 · deltoid <2/5 ·\
✗ Wrong options: IR contracture (that's the indication) · no subscapularis function · positive hornblower

**Wickstrom humeral derotation osteotomy** — IR contracture / ER weakness **with dysplasia** (Waters IV–V; grade V TO) \
✗ Wrong options: glenoid osteotomy

**Shoulder arthrodesis** — non-functional deltoid, good hand

| Elbow flexion contracture | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **<40°** (Orthobullets) · <35° (OB) | **Serial nighttime extension splinting** (prevents progression, doesn't correct) |
| **>40°** | **Serial extension casting** |
| Severe persistent | Anterior capsular release + biceps/brachialis lengthening (recurs) |

**Cause of elbow contracture** — persistent **triceps (C7) weakness** vs biceps (C5–6)

**Forearm supination contracture** — unopposed biceps (Klumpke / mixed) · **biceps rerouting** (supinator → pronator) if passive pronation intact · + **forearm osteotomy** if passive pronation limited · ± interosseous membrane release (TO)

**Wrist / hand transfers** — wrist drop: **PT → ECRB** · finger extension: **FCR or FCU → EDC** · thumb abduction: **EIP → APB** · force ∝ cross-section, amplitude ∝ length

**Co-contraction (TO)** — partially recovered NBPP; antagonists fire together · **trumpet sign** = shoulder abducts + forearm pronates when bringing hand to mouth · **Botox** for co-contractions

**Secondary OBPI procedures (TO)** — **external rotation osteotomy** · **triangular tilt surgery** · **Mod Quad** (LD, teres major, subscapularis, pectoralis release)

## 6 Complications

**Phrenic palsy** — persistent → **diaphragm plication**

**Shoulder tendon transfers** — radial + axillary nerve palsy

## X vs Y

| | **Preganglionic** | **Postganglionic** |
|---|---|---|
| Site | Root avulsed from cord (proximal to DRG) | Distal to DRG (rupture, neuroma) |
| Signs | Horner, phrenic, winging, rhomboids | — |
| Recovery | **None spontaneous** | Possible |
| Surgery | **Nerve transfer ~3 months** | **Graft 3–9 months** |

| | **Erb** | **Klumpke** |
|---|---|---|
| Roots | C5–C6 | C8–T1 |
| Posture | Waiter's tip | Claw hand |
| Mechanism | Head-shoulder distraction | Abducted arm traction |
| Prognosis | **Best** | Poor, Horner |
