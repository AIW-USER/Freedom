# Pelvis Fractures - Pediatric

*Pediatrics · 82 / 112*

## 1 Basics

**Incidence** — **1–2%** of pediatric fractures · acetabulum 1–15% of these · avulsions almost only in **adolescents**

**Mechanism** — ring: **high energy** (MVA, pedestrian struck) · avulsion: low energy, **explosive** activity (sprint, jump, kick)

**Associated** — **CNS + abdominal visceral injury >50%** (energy needed to break elastic pelvis) · chest injury ~63%, long bones ~50%, spine ~25% · femoral head fracture/dislocation with acetabular fracture · **GU injury with Torode IV**

### Child vs adult pelvis

| Child | Reason |
|---|---|
| More **lateral compression** (adult: APC) | — |
| More **single-bone ring fractures** | Elastic bone, cartilage absorbs energy, elastic SI + symphysis, **thick periosteum** |
| "Dislocations" heal like fractures | Periosteal tube |
| **Less hemorrhage** | Small vessels vasoconstrict · pelvic volume rarely ↑ |
| Acetabulum: **transverse** > both column | Triradiate injury with less force → growth arrest |

### Anatomy

**Primary centres** (endochondral) — ilium 9 wk · ischium 16 wk · pubis 20 wk · fuse at triradiate **12 (girls) / 14 (boys)**

**Acetabulum** — enlarges by **interstitial growth of triradiate cartilage** · concavity from femoral head pressure · secondary centres appear 8–9, fuse 17–18: **os acetabuli (anterior wall)**, acetabular epiphysis (superior), ischial secondary centre (posterior wall)

| Apophysis | Appears | Fuses |
|---|---|---|
| **Iliac crest** (Risser) | 13–15 | 15–17 |
| **Ischial** | 15–17 | **19–25** |
| AIIS | 14 | 16 |

### Apophyseal avulsion fractures

| Avulsion site (TO) | Muscle | Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| **Ischial tuberosity** | Hamstrings | **Most common** |
| AIIS | Rectus femoris | Common |
| ASIS | Sartorius | Common |
| Pubic symphysis / iliac crest | Abdominals | Rare |
| **Lesser trochanter** | Iliopsoas | **Least common** |

**Child pelvis (TO)** — elastic, cartilaginous · more single-bone fractures than ring disruptions · **less haemorrhage** (vasoconstriction, mobile vessels) · **visceral injury without obvious fracture** · complications: **premature triradiate closure** → acetabular dysplasia, LLD

| Site | Muscle | % |
|---|---|---|
| **Ischial tuberosity** | **Hamstrings + adductors** | **54%** |
| **AIIS** | **Rectus femoris** | 22% |
| **ASIS** | **Sartorius** | 19% |
| Pubic symphysis | Abdominals | 3% |
| Iliac crest | Abdominals (external oblique) | 1% |
| **Lesser trochanter** | **Iliopsoas** | — |

**Ischial avulsion** — age **13–17** as tuberosity ossifies (apophysis 2–5× weaker than tendon) · hip extension + knee flexion weakness · **X-rays negative → MRI**\
✗ Wrong options: CT (for displacement) · EMG · bone scan · steroid

**AIIS** — groin pain, snap, **pain on resisted knee extension** · **ASIS** — hockey check / eccentric sartorius

**Iliac crest apophyseal avulsion (TO)** — adolescent (e.g. 16-yr-old cricketer), sudden crest pain · unfused apophysis pulled off by **core muscles** = **type I physeal injury** · often missed

## 3 Presentation

**Exam** — ATLS · **rectal + GU exam** (open fracture) · log-roll for ecchymosis · palpate ASIS, crests, SI, symphysis · iliac crest compression / posterior pressure pain · excess mobility = serious injury

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

**Views** — **AP pelvis** · **Judet** (45° obliques — acetabulum) · **inlet/outlet** (35° caudal/cranial — ring)

**CT** — X-rays miss **~50%** of pediatric pelvic fractures · SI tenderness, negative films with suspicion, planning, spine injury

**MRI** — occasionally for apophyseal avulsions

### Torode/Zieg classification (pediatric ring)

| Type | Injury | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **I** | **Avulsion** | Protected WB if <2 cm · ORIF >2–3 cm (historically >3 cm) |
| **II** | **Iliac wing** | Protected WB if <2 cm · ORIF >2–3 cm |
| **III** | Ring, **no segmental instability** | WBAT 6 wk · ORIF if acetabulum displaced >2 mm |
| **IV** | Ring **with segmental instability** | **<2 cm → bed rest** · unstable / >2 cm → ORIF |

**Tile** — A stable · B rotationally unstable, vertically stable · C unstable both

![Torode/Zieg type IV](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob3000-pediatric-pelvic-fractures.jpg)

### Bucholz classification (acetabulum / triradiate)

| Type | Physeal | Features |
|---|---|---|
| **Shearing** | SH I / II | Blow to pubis/ischium/femur · triangular medial Thurston-Holland fragment (SH II) |
| **Crushing / impaction** | **SH V** | Hard to see · narrowed triradiate · **premature closure** · earlier = worse deformity |

## 5 Management

### Pelvic ring injury management

**Non-op** — symphysis/SI "dislocations" heal by periosteum · types I–II <2 cm: protected WB 2–4 wk → stretch/strengthen 4–8 wk → sport after 8 wk · type III WBAT 6 wk

**Stable type IV open book (symphysis 1.2 cm, SI 0.7 cm, stable)** → **bed rest + progressive mobilisation**\
✗ Wrong options: ex-fix · binder · fixation

**Hemodynamically unstable + rotationally unstable (APC) ring** → **pelvic binder** · **ex-fix** for instability / ↑ volume · **angiography** if still bleeding\
✗ Wrong options: iliac wing · rami · avulsions

**Adolescent with displaced symphysis + SI widening** → **anterior symphyseal plate + percutaneous SI screw**\
✗ Wrong options: non-op · anterior only · ex-fix

**Operative indications** — displaced posterior SI · iliac fracture >2 cm · anterior disruption with vertical/rotational instability · older children/adolescents do better with fixation

**Acetabulum ORIF** — **displacement >2 mm** · incongruity · **intra-articular fragments** · instability (medial/posterior subluxation) · central fracture-dislocation · open · comminution not improved by traction

**Technique** — ilioinguinal / Stoppa · **physeal-sparing** · if needed **smooth pins across triradiate 4–6 wk, removed early**

### Apophyseal avulsion management

**First line** — crutches, **protected WB**, surveillance X-rays (e.g. minimally displaced lesser trochanter avulsion in hurdler)

**Differential on hip films** — SCFE → in situ screw · FAI → PT · compression femoral neck stress fracture → NWB (screw if >50% width) · **tension-sided** → cannulated screws

## 6 Complications

**Triradiate premature closure** — **<5%** · risk: **age <10 yr**, **Bucholz crush (SH V)** · thick medial wall → **shallow acetabulum, lateral subluxation**, hypoplastic hemipelvis · bar excision (may still close) · late **pelvic osteotomy (Chiari)** · follow to maturity

**Others** — LLD (vertical displacement >2 cm) · malunion (well tolerated) · nonunion (rare) · NV injury · HO · **femoral head AVN** (acetabular fracture + dislocation) · hip OA (displaced acetabular) · death (head/visceral)

**Prognosis** — complications rare · surgery more often needed after triradiate closure

## How it's asked

**"8-yr hit by car, SBP 48 — which pelvis benefits from binder?"** → **AP compression (open book) ring injury**

**"6-yr, symphysis 1.2 cm + SI 0.7 cm, stable?"** → **bed rest + progressive mobilisation**

**"14-yr, displaced symphysis + SI widening?"** → **anterior plate + percutaneous SI screw**

**"14-yr soccer snap, pain on resisted knee extension — muscle?"** → **rectus femoris (AIIS)**\
✗ Wrong options: sartorius · iliopsoas

**"14-yr gymnast, posterior thigh, negative X-rays?"** → **MRI**\
✗ Wrong options: CT · EMG

**"16-yr hockey check, ASIS avulsion — muscle?"** → **sartorius**

**"14-yr hurdler — which injury gets crutches + repeat films?"** → **lesser trochanter avulsion**\
✗ Wrong options: SCFE · tension stress fracture
