# Ankle Fractures - Pediatric

*Pediatrics · 4 / 112*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Incidence** — **25–40% of all physeal injuries** (**2nd most common**) · 5% of pediatric fractures

**Demographics** — males **2:1** · age **8–15 yr**

**Risk factors** — sports · ↑ BMI

**Patterns** — SH I, II · **SH III → Tillaux**, medial malleolus · **SH IV → triplane**, medial malleolus

### Anatomy – physes

**Distal tibial physis** — **35–40% of tibial growth**, 15–20% of limb growth · **3–4 mm/yr** · growth until **14 yr girls / 16 yr boys** · closes over **18 months**

**Closure order** — **central → anteromedial → posteromedial → lateral** (basis of Tillaux, triplane)

![Distal tibial physeal closure: central → anteromedial → posteromedial → lateral](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob4027-pediatric-ankle-fractures.jpg)

**Distal fibular physis** — closes **12–24 months after** distal tibia

**Ligaments** — origins **distal to physes** → physis fails first

| Ligament | Attachments | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **AITFL** | **Chaput** (anterolateral tibial epiphysis) → **Wagstaffe** (anterior fibula) | Avulses **Tillaux** / transitional fractures |
| PITFL | **Volkmann** tubercle → posterior fibula | — |
| Inferior transverse | Posterior fibula across posterior tibial plafond | Posterior labrum |
| Deep deltoid | — | Primary restraint to lateral talar shift |

## 2 Mechanism

**Mechanism** — direct trauma · **twisting about a planted foot**

**High-energy abduction injuries** (e.g. skateboarding) → ↑ physeal closure risk

## 3 Presentation

**Symptoms** — pain, inability to bear weight

**Exam** — swelling, ecchymosis, deformity if displaced · **distal fibular physeal tenderness = nondisplaced SH I**

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

**Views** — **AP, mortise, lateral** · full-length tibia to rule out **Maisonneuve**

**CT** — displacement (**best post-reduction**), articular step-off, pre-op planning

**Physeal widening** after reduction → **interposed periosteum**

### Salter-Harris classification

| Type | Freq. | Description | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I** | 15% | Through physis | Nondisplaced: fibular tenderness only |
| **II** | **45%** | Physis + metaphysis (**Thurston-Holland**) | Most common |
| **III** | 25% | Physis + epiphysis | **Tillaux**, medial malleolus · ↑ arrest |
| **IV** | 25% | Metaphysis + physis + epiphysis | **Triplane**, medial malleolus shear · ↑ arrest |
| **V** | 1% | Crush | Diagnosed late at growth arrest |
| **VI** | Rare | Perichondral ring | Lawnmower / iatrogenic |

![Salter-Harris classification](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob4027-illustration-of-the-salter-harris-fracture-classification.jpg)

### Dias & Tachdjian (mechanism) classification

| Mechanism | Injury |
|---|---|
| **Supination–inversion** | Gr 1: **fibular SH I/II** avulsion · Gr 2: + **medial malleolus SH III/IV** |
| **Supination–plantarflexion** | Tibial SH I/II, epiphysis displaced **posteriorly**, posterior Thurston-Holland · **no fibular fracture** · hard to see on AP |
| **Supination–external rotation** | Gr 1: tibial SH II, fragment **posteromedial**, line runs proximal-medial on AP · Gr 2: + **low spiral fibula** |
| **Pronation/eversion–ER** | Tibial SH I/II + **transverse fibula**, displaced **laterally** · ± diastasis |
| **Axial compression** | **SH V** — diagnosed late |

## 5 Management

**Reduction (TO)** — **75% traction, 25% manipulation** · avoid repeated manipulation · step-off **< 1–2 mm** · in adolescents **joint congruity before physeal preservation** · **look at the fibula first**: no fibular fracture → think transitional; isolated fibula fracture may hide distal tibial physeal injury · SH II most common, **III \~25%, IV \~25%** (TO) · ankle = **most common lower-limb physeal injury** (15–20% of physeal injuries)

### Operative criteria

| Fracture | Nonop | CRPP | ORIF |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Isolated distal fibula** | **<2 mm** → boot / NWB SLC 4 wk · >2 mm acceptable CR → NWB cast 6 wk | >2 mm, failed CR, **>2 yr growth** | >2 mm, failed CR, **<2 yr growth** |
| **Distal tibia SH I/II** | Acceptable CR → NWB cast 6 wk | Unacceptable CR + **>2 yr growth** | Unacceptable CR + **<2 yr growth** |
| **Distal tibia SH III/IV** | — | **<2 mm** after CR | **>2 mm** after CR |

**Acceptable SH I/II tibia** — **no varus · <10° valgus · <10° recurvatum/procurvatum · <3 mm physeal widening**

**Acceptable tibial reduction** — **<2 mm** · confirm on **mortise**

```
Displaced distal tibial physeal fracture
├─ CR acceptable → long-leg NWB cast 6 wk
└─ Irreducible / residual physeal gap → open reduction, remove interposed periosteum
       ├─ Stable after open reduction → cast
       └─ Unstable → internal fixation (smooth wires across physis)
```

### Irreducible fractures – interposed periosteum & open reduction

**Closed reduction** — adequate sedation + relaxation · **max 2 attempts** (repeated attempts injure physis)

**Block to reduction** — **periosteum on the tension side** · also tendon, NV structures\
✗ Wrong options: compression side · comminution · unrecognised fracture line

![Periosteum entrapped on the tension side](../../MCQ/attachments/q210245-illa-entrapped-periosteum.jpg)

**Failed CR under sedation** → **ORIF**\
✗ Wrong options: repeat CR under GA · cast and follow-up · admit

**Why open** — interposed periosteum → **premature physeal closure** (makes bone, cartilage or fibrous tissue in physis) \
✗ Wrong options: "to prevent nonunion" (nonunion rare)

**SH I lower extremity** — growth problems are **common**\
✗ Wrong options: "rare"

### Salter-Harris III/IV medial malleolus – ORIF parallel to physis

**SH III/IV medial malleolus** — large **cartilaginous** portion (bigger than X-ray fragment) → articular incongruity → **ORIF avoiding physis and joint**\
✗ Wrong options: casting · CAM walker

**Fixation** — **transepiphyseal, parallel to physis** · 2 parallel epiphyseal screws (medial malleolus shear) · cannulated screws parallel to physis (Tillaux, triplane) · transverse metaphyseal screw if large fragment · **smooth K-wires** if crossing physis\
✗ Wrong options: screw crossing physis

**Follow-up** — long-term (high growth-arrest rate)

## 6 Complications

### Physeal arrest risk – residual gap

**Rate** — premature physeal closure **8–50%** after distal tibial physeal fractures

**Highest growth disturbance** — **medial malleolus SH IV**

| Risk factor for premature physeal closure | |
|---|---|
| **Residual physeal gap** — **greatest risk** | **>3 mm** (Orthobullets, OB) · **>2 mm** (OB) — read the options |
| Initial displacement | **+15% per 1 mm** |
| Entrapped periosteum | Yes |
| High-energy (abduction, skateboarding) | Yes (✗ Wrong options: football) |
| **SH III / IV** | Yes (all types at risk) |
| **Age >8 yr** | **NOT a risk factor** |
| Number of reductions, ORIF vs closed | not associated if anatomic |

**Partial arrest** — **distal fibular arrest → valgus** · **medial tibial arrest → varus** (continued fibular growth)

**Complete arrest** — **LLD**

### Physeal bar – resection, osteotomy & fibular epiphysiodesis

```
Distal tibial physeal bar
├─ <50% area + >2 yr growth → bar resection + interposition (fat)
│      (add osteotomy if angulation >10–20°)
├─ >50% + ≥2 yr growth → complete epiphysiodesis + ipsilateral fibular epiphysiodesis
└─ Skeletally mature with deformity → osteotomy
```

**Bar resection** — **<50% physis**, **>2 yr (or 2 cm) growth**, angulation **<20°** (Orthobullets) · interpose fat, cranioplast, bone wax, cartilage, muscle, silicone · via **metaphyseal window**\
✗ Wrong options: through the joint (bar not visible)

**Osteotomy** — angulation **>10–20°** (won't remodel after resection) → **osteotomy + bar resection through osteotomy site**

**Fibular epiphysiodesis** — bar **>50%** + ≥2 yr growth + open fibular physis → prevents **varus** + lateral impingement

**Bar types** — **peripheral** (angular deformity, resectable) · **elongated** (SH III/IV, middle) · **central** (tents articular surface) · small **<25% central** = best prognosis

**LLD** — bar resection if <50% + >2 yr growth · **contralateral epiphysiodesis** near maturity

### Rotational malunion

**Unrecognised deformity** — **external rotation** through physis, noticed months after walking (↑ foot-progression angle)\
✗ Wrong options: internal rotation · procurvatum/recurvatum (seen and corrected initially) · valgus

**Prevention / treatment** — correct rotation at casting · **derotational osteotomy**

**Other malunions** — anterior angulation / plantarflexion after **supination–plantarflexion SH II** · **valgus** after **ER SH II**

### Others

**Arthritis** — articular step-off **>2 mm**

**Extensor retinacular syndrome** — posteriorly displaced fractures

**Reflex sympathetic dystrophy** — more common in **girls** · PT, counselling, drugs, sympathetic block

## X vs Y

| | **Distal fibular arrest** | **Medial distal tibial arrest** |
|---|---|---|
| Deformity | **Valgus** | **Varus** |
| Prevention | — | **Fibular epiphysiodesis** (bar >50%) |

| Scenario | Fibular epiphysiodesis? |
|---|---|
| 13 yr ♂, **>50% bar**, 5 months post-fracture | **✓ Yes** |
| 10 yr ♀, <50% bar, ≥2 yr growth | ✗ Wrong option: bar resection |
| 13 yr ♀, <50% central bar, <2 yr growth | ✗ Wrong option: nothing (little growth) |
| 15 yr ♀, 25° varus 2 yr later | ✗ Wrong option: osteotomy (mature) |
| 10 yr ♂, SH IV with >3 mm residual | ✗ Wrong option: repeat reduction |

## How it's asked

**"Supination-inversion fracture, CR under sedation unchanged — next?"** → **ORIF**\
✗ Wrong options: CR under GA · cast

**"Physeal widening after two CR attempts?"** → **open reduction, remove periosteum, smooth wires — to ↓ premature physeal closure**\
✗ Wrong options: to prevent nonunion

**"Block to reduction?"** → **periosteum on the tension side**

**"NOT a risk factor for premature physeal closure?"** → **age >8 years**

**"Greatest risk of premature physeal closure?"** → **residual gap after CR**\
✗ Wrong options: initial displacement · attempts · ORIF

**"6-year-old SH IV medial malleolus?"** → **ORIF parallel to physis**\
✗ Wrong options: screw crossing physis

**"12-year-old SH III/IV medial malleolus?"** → **ORIF avoiding physis and joint**

**"SH IV 2 yr ago, 30° varus, small central bar?"** → **osteotomy + bar resection + fat through osteotomy**\
✗ Wrong options: resection alone · arthroscopic through joint · epiphysiodesis

**"Fibular epiphysiodesis indicated when?"** → **>50% distal tibial bar with ≥2 yr growth**

**"Deformity unrecognised until months after walking?"** → **external rotation**
