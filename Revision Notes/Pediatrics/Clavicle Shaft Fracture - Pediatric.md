# Clavicle Shaft Fracture - Pediatric

*Pediatrics · 21 / 112*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Incidence** — **15% of pediatric upper-extremity injuries** · shaft = **80%** of pediatric clavicle injuries

**Birth fractures** — **0.5%** normal deliveries · **1.6%** breech · **90% of obstetric fractures**

**No trauma history** → think **congenital pseudarthrosis of clavicle** (usually **right**, except dextrocardia)

### Anatomy

**Deforming forces** — **medial fragment → posterosuperior** (**sternocleidomastoid**) · **lateral fragment → inferomedial** (**pectoralis** + arm weight)

**Open fractures** — buttonhole through **platysma**

**AC stability** — AC ligament (AP stability; **superior** strongest, then posterior) · CC ligaments trapezoid + **conoid (strongest)** (superior/inferior stability) · deltoid + trapezius dynamic

## 2 Mechanism

**Mechanism** — **FOOSH** or **direct blow to lateral shoulder** · birth trauma

## 3 Presentation

**Exam** — pain, deformity · **skin tenting** (impending open fracture) · NV exam (rare NV / brachial plexus injury)

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

**Views** — **upright (sitting/standing) AP bilateral shoulders** · **15° cephalic tilt (Zanca)** for superior/inferior displacement · ± 5–10 lb weight in hand

### Allman classification

| Type | Location |
|---|---|
| **I** | **Middle third (most common)** |
| II | Lateral third, distal to CC ligaments |
| III | Medial (proximal) third |

## 5 Management

### Nonoperative care even when displaced

**Newborn birth fracture** — observation, careful lifting · union ~**1 week**

**<12 years** — **sling / shoulder immobiliser / figure-of-eight** (no difference sling vs figure-of-eight) · **no reduction attempted** · ROM at 2–4 wk, strengthening 6–10 wk · callus bump fades 6–12 mo

**Displacement / shortening in <12 yr** — **100% displaced + 12 mm short at 10 yr** → **nonoperative because of age**\
✗ Wrong options: plate for displacement · high nonunion risk

**Adolescent** — **14 yr, completely displaced, 24 mm short** → sling → **union without significant shortening** (remodelling)\
✗ Wrong options: malunion with shortening · nonunion

**Symptomatic malunion** — only **10–20%**\
✗ Wrong options: 50%

### Operative criteria

| Absolute | Controversial |
|---|---|
| **Open fracture** | **Shortening >2 cm** — not an indication <12 yr; debated in adolescents |
| **Skin at risk from tenting** | — |
| **Subclavian artery/vein injury** | — |

```
Pediatric clavicle shaft fracture
├─ Newborn → observe, careful handling
├─ <12 yr (even 100% displaced, shortened) → sling
└─ Open / tenting / vascular injury → ORIF (precontoured plate)
       (adolescent >2 cm shortening — controversial)
```

**ORIF** — precontoured plate (± recon / locking / IM rod) · sling 7–10 d · strengthening ~6 wk · sport ~3 mo

## 6 Complications

**Nonoperative** — **nonunion / malunion rare**

**Operative** — **hardware prominence (up to 59%)** → plate removal · **anterior chest wall numbness** (supraclavicular nerves) · refracture · infection/dehiscence ~5%

**TO points** — 10–15% of paediatric fractures · **90% midshaft** · simple sling · **figure-of-8 no advantage** · posteriorly displaced **proximal (medial)** fracture → tracheo-oesophageal compression

## How it's asked

**"10-yr, 100% displaced, 12 mm short, skin intact — true?"** → **nonoperative because of age**\
✗ Wrong options: plate · nonunion risk · 50% symptomatic malunion

**"14-yr, 24 mm shortened midshaft, sling — long-term?"** → **union without significant shortening**

**"Absolute indications for ORIF?"** → **open fracture, tenting skin, subclavian vessel injury**

**"Medial fragment displaced by?"** → **sternocleidomastoid (posterosuperior)**

**"Commonest complication of plating?"** → **hardware prominence**
