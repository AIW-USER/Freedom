# Proximal Tibia Metaphyseal Fractures - Pediatric

*Pediatrics · 91 / 112*

## 1 Basics

**Epidemiology** — **3–6 yr** (2–10 yr OB) · "Cozen fracture"

**Mechanism** — low-energy **valgus** force → **incomplete (greenstick/torus)** medial fracture · torsion · classic: **down a slide on an adult's lap**, leg extended · fibula fracture = higher energy, less stable

**Anatomy** — posterior tibial artery → nutrient (inner ⅔) + periosteal vessels

## 3 Presentation

**Exam** — pain, refuses to bear weight · little swelling / deformity · check compartments

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

**X-ray** — AP + lateral · complete vs incomplete · displacement · usually **valgus** · fibula fracture

![Proximal tibial metaphyseal fracture](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob4025-proximal-tibia-fracture-cropped.jpg)

## 5 Management

### Initial treatment & counselling

| Fracture | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Nondisplaced | **Long leg cast in EXTENSION with VARUS mold** (slight overcorrection) 4–6 wk · WB at 2–3 wk |
| Displaced | **Reduction under sedation** (complete the greenstick) → same cast, three-point varus mold |
| Irreducible (rare) | **Limited medial open reduction** — remove interposed **periosteum, pes tendons, MCL** ± crossed K-wires |

**Counsel before treatment** — valgus may develop despite perfect care but **often resolves spontaneously**\
✗ Wrong options: knee immobilizer · prophylactic surgery · "always needs surgery"

## 6 Complications

### Post-traumatic valgus (Cozen) - natural history & observation

**Cozen phenomenon** — **late valgus** · etiology unknown (medial physeal overgrowth / stimulation, lateral tether, pes anserinus loss) · **occurs regardless of treatment** · not physeal arrest

| Feature | Value |
|---|---|
| Incidence | **50–90%** (14–100% OB) |
| Appears | **5–15 months** |
| Maximum | **12–18 months**, average **18°** |
| Resolves | By **~3 yr**, residual ~6° (can leave S-shaped tibia) |
| Risk | Incomplete reduction · physeal injury · infolded periosteum · pes injury |

**Treatment** — **observation 12–24 months** · surgery only **>15–20° near skeletal maturity** → **medial proximal tibial hemiepiphysiodesis** or varus proximal tibia/fibula osteotomy\
✗ Wrong options: KAFO · hemiepiphysiodesis early · osteotomy · ring fixator · MRI · bar resection

**LLD** — affected tibia **longer** (~9 mm) · **doesn't resolve** · usually no treatment

## How it's asked

**"3-yr, 6 months after metaphyseal fracture, valgus?"** → **follow-up X-ray in 6 months**\
✗ Wrong options: hemiepiphysiodesis · osteotomy · KAFO

**"Which can be observed alone?"** → **5-yr with 15° valgus 8 months after fracture**\
✗ Wrong options: Blount MDA 18° · SCFE · AIS 80°

**"5-yr trampoline proximal tibia — counsel about?"** → **valgus**

**"6-yr, 15° valgus 1 yr after healed fracture?"** → **continued observation**

**"4-yr knock-knee 9 months after fracture?"** → **observation**\
✗ Wrong options: stapling · bar resection

**"5-yr proximal tibia — deformity + treatment?"** → **valgus; observation**

**"Deformity within a year — initial treatment?"** → **observation**

**"13 months post-injury, leg looks funny?"** → **maximum ~18 months, resolves over ~3 yr**\
✗ Wrong options: shorter leg · short immobilisation

**"Valgus 12 months after perfectly reduced injury?"** → **late valgus from proximal tibial metaphyseal fracture**\
✗ Wrong options: vitamin D · physiologic bowing

**"4-yr proximal tibia fracture — tell parents?"** → **reduce + long leg cast; deformity may develop but often resolves**
