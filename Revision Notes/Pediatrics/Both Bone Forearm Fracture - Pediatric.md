# Both Bone Forearm Fracture - Pediatric

*Pediatrics · 10 / 112*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Incidence** — **~40% of all pediatric fractures** · males > females

**Location** — distal physis 14% · **distal metaphysis 60%** · midshaft 20% · proximal third 4%

**Peak** — during peak bone turnover (remodelling mismatch)

**Associated** — **floating elbow** (ipsilateral supracondylar) **15%** · nerve injury **1%**, most often **median**

### Anatomy

**Bowing** — physiologic **apex-lateral radial bow** · apex-posterior ulnar bow

**Deforming forces** — **biceps + supinator** flex/supinate proximal fragment · **pronator teres + quadratus** pronate distal fragment · **brachioradialis** dorsiflexes + radially deviates distal fragment

**Periosteum** — intact on **concave** side · interosseous membrane taut in **neutral to slight supination**

## 2 Mechanism

**Mechanism** — fall from height, sport, **playground (monkey bars)**

## 3 Presentation

**Symptoms** — forearm pain, refuses to use arm

**Exam** — swelling, deformity, ecchymosis · **subtle poke-hole open wounds** · check elbow + wrist · NV exam · **compartment syndrome**

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

**Types** — **incomplete**: greenstick, torus, **plastic deformation** · **complete** · proximal / middle / distal third · apex volar or dorsal

### Assessing rotational alignment

| View | Landmarks **180° apart** when aligned |
|---|---|
| **AP** | **Radial styloid + bicipital tuberosity** |
| **Lateral** | **Ulnar styloid + coronoid** |

**Other clues** — fragment **diameters** and **cortical thickness** should match

![Rotational alignment — bicipital tuberosity vs radial styloid](../../MCQ/attachments/q3427-illa-bbfa5.jpg)

### Key radiology findings

**Views** — **AP + lateral forearm** + orthogonal **elbow and wrist**

**Findings** — both bones fractured · one fracture + **plastic deformation** of the other · atypical bowing without fracture = plastic deformation

## 5 Management

### Acceptable alignment & remodelling by age

| Age | Angulation | Malrotation | Bayonet apposition |
|---|---|---|---|
| **<10 yr** | **<15°** | **<45°** | **Yes** if <1 cm short (100% translation OK) |
| **≥10 yr** | **<10°** | **<30°** | **No** |
| **<2 yr growth left** | **0°** | **0°** | **No** — adult tolerances |

**By level (girls ≤8, boys ≤10)** — **distal third 20° · middle 15° · proximal 10°** · remodelling ↓ distal → proximal

**Bayonet** — acceptable (esp. distal radius) if angulation ≤20° and ≥2 yr growth remain

**8-year-old, 12° dorsal angulation at 1 week** → acceptable → **no sequelae**\
✗ Wrong options: ↓ rotation · malunion · nonunion

### Operative criteria

| Indication | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Unacceptable alignment** after CR (beyond table) | Fixation |
| Bayonet apposition **>10 yr** | Fixation |
| Both-bone fracture **<13 yr** | **Elastic IM nails** |
| Both-bone fracture **>13 yr** (near maturity) | **ORIF (plate)** |
| **Open fracture**, **refracture** | ORIF |
| Comminuted / segmental, highly displaced | ORIF (relative) |
| **Malunion with callus** + lost rotation (missed follow-up) | **ORIF** (open callus removal) (✗ Wrong options: brace · recast · repeat CR · cast wedging) |

```
Pediatric both-bone forearm fracture
├─ Acceptable after CR → cast, weekly X-rays
└─ Unacceptable / lost reduction
       ├─ <13 yr, simple midshaft → elastic IM nails
       └─ >13 yr, comminuted, open, refracture → ORIF
```

### Casting technique – cast index & loss of reduction

**Closed reduction** — traction (finger traps), thumb pressure, rotation, **3-point bending** · **apex volar → pronate** · **apex dorsal → supinate**

**Plastic deformation** — accept if **≤20°** · reduce with **slow steady 3-point bending** (abrupt force → fracture)

**Short vs long arm cast** (distal BBFF) — **no difference** in loss of reduction · SAC fewer complications\
✗ Wrong options: more swelling, pain, remanipulation

**Cast index** — **lateral inner width ÷ AP inner width** · **>0.8 → loss of reduction**\
✗ Wrong options: pain · physeal arrest · nonunion · swelling

**Ideal cast index (TO)** — **0.7** (sagittal ÷ coronal width)

![Cast index](../../MCQ/attachments/q4865-illa-cast-index.jpg)

**Remanipulation risk** — **initial fracture displacement** (angulation + translation) · same-level radius + ulna fractures · cast index >0.8 · residual deformity\
✗ Wrong options: mechanism · distal location · below-elbow cast · cast index <0.7

**Swelling** — bivalve cast (compartment syndrome)

### Operative fixation – intramedullary nails vs plates

**Ulnar nail** — **olecranon tip or through anconeus** (protect ulnar nerve)

**Ulnar nail entry (TO)** — just distal to olecranon apophysis · radius just proximal to physis

**Radial nail** — just proximal to **radial styloid** or dorsal distal radius proximal to physis · dorsal central start risks **EPL**

**Technique** — reduce easiest bone first · **open the fracture after 2–3 failed passes** · nail removal at 3–4 months

| | **Elastic IM nails** | **ORIF plate** |
|---|---|---|
| Time | **Shorter** | Longer |
| Blood loss | **Less** | More |
| Union, **radial bow**, rotation | **Equal** | Equal |
| Refracture | **Lower** (no screw holes) | Higher after plate removal |
| Best for | Midshaft, transverse/oblique, minimal comminution, **acute** (pre-callus) | Comminution, older, open |

**TO points** — midshaft acceptable angulation: infants **< 25°**, children **< 10°** · displacement up to **90%** in young children · **any rotational deformity → reduce** (rotation never remodels) · above-elbow cast, **3-point moulding** · **plastic deformation does not remodel** → reduce if visible deformity or ↓ pronation-supination

## 6 Complications

### Complication – loss of forearm rotation

**Most common long-term after IM fixation** — **loss of pronation/supination** (soft-tissue injury, stripping)\
✗ Wrong options: infection · malunion · refracture · nonunion

**Refracture** — **5–10%** · risks: **plate removal**, **greenstick** pattern → **ORIF**

**Malunion** — loss of rotation · from initial reduction or delayed diagnosis → **corrective osteotomy**

**Compartment syndrome** — high energy · **multiple reduction / nail-passage attempts** → **fasciotomy**

**Synostosis** — rare · head injury, high energy · resection rarely helps

## How it's asked

**"14-year-old both-bone fracture — acceptable angulation, malrotation, bayonet?"** → **0°, 0°, none**

**"8-year-old, 12° angulation after loss of reduction — outcome?"** → **no additional sequelae**

**"7-year-old acceptable alignment at 1 week?"** → **observation**\
✗ Wrong options: repeat CR · ORIF · IMN

**"Short vs long arm cast after anatomic CR of distal BBFF?"** → **no difference in outcomes**

**"Cast index 0.95 — risk?"** → **loss of reduction**

**"Factor predisposing to remanipulation?"** → **initial fracture displacement**

**"IMN vs plate — true statement?"** → **radial bow restoration similar**\
✗ Wrong options: IMN higher nonunion · more blood loss · longer surgery

**"Missed follow-up, 6 weeks, healed malaligned, lost pronosupination?"** → **ORIF radius + ulna**\
✗ Wrong options: cast wedging · repeat CR

**"Radiographic rotational alignment?"** → **AP: radial styloid + biceps tuberosity 180° apart**

**"Most common long-term complication after IM fixation?"** → **loss of forearm rotation**
