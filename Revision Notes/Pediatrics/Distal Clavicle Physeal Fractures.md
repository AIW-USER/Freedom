# Distal Clavicle Physeal Fractures

*Pediatrics · 34 / 112*

## 1 Basics

**Incidence** — rare · **5–10%** of pediatric clavicle fractures

**Equivalent** — childhood **"pseudodislocation"** = adult **AC separation**

### Pathoanatomy – intact periosteal sleeve

**Injury** — clavicle (metaphysis) displaces **superiorly** out of its **periosteal sleeve**, through the **physis** · **inferior periosteal sleeve + epiphysis stay attached to intact CC + AC ligaments** · **AC joint intact**

**Healing** — new clavicle forms **within the sleeve** → **"Y-shaped" clavicle**; displaced fragment remodels/resorbs

### Anatomy & ossification

**Clavicle** — **first bone to ossify** (5th week in utero, intramembranous) · **physes last to close**

**Medial physis** — **80% of growth** · ossifies **18–20 yr** · **last physis to close (20–25 yr)** → teenage "SC dislocations" are **physeal fracture-dislocations**

**Lateral epiphysis** — ossifies ~18 yr

**CC ligaments** — vertical stability · **trapezoid** 2 cm, **conoid** 4 cm from AC joint · **AC ligaments** — horizontal stability

## 2 Mechanism

**Fall on side of shoulder** / outstretched arm · direct blow · rarely **child abuse**

## 3 Presentation

**Exam** — pain, distal clavicle tenderness, deformity, ecchymosis · rarely tenting

**Newborn** — **pseudoparalysis** with **intact reflexes** (vs brachial plexus palsy)

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

**Views** — **AP ± Zanca** (intra-articular extension) · **axillary lateral** (type IV posterior displacement)

**Later** — new bone from intact sleeve **inferior** to the superiorly displaced fragment

![Distal clavicle physeal fracture](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob4122-distal-clavicle-physeal-xray.jpg)

### Rockwood (Dameron-Rockwood) classification

| Type | Injury |
|---|---|
| I | AC ligament sprain, **periosteal tube intact** |
| II | Partial periosteal tube disruption |
| III | Large split, **superior** displacement |
| IV | **Posterior** displacement through **trapezius** |
| V | Complete disruption, through deltoid + trapezius |
| VI | **Inferior**, below coracoid |

## 5 Management

### Pseudodislocation – intact periosteal sleeve & sling treatment

**Most (even displaced, elite athletes)** → **sling immobilization**\
✗ Wrong options: CRPP · plate · CC reconstruction/suture · stress views · MRI

![Remodelling within intact periosteal sleeve](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob4122-periosteal-sleeve-healing-pediatric-clavicle-fracture.jpg)

### Operative criteria

| Absolute (rare) | Relative |
|---|---|
| **Open fracture** | Severely displaced, **near physeal closure** |
| **Skin compromise** | Fragment entrapped in **trapezius** |
| **Displaced intra-articular extension** | Floating shoulder |
| **NV injury needing surgery** | Some type III near maturity · **types IV–VI** (open reduction + sleeve repair) |

**Avoid pins** around clavicle (**migration**)

## 6 Complications

**Subclavian vessel laceration** — rare · **rapidly expanding hematoma** → vascular repair (thick periosteum protective)

**Nonunion** — rare, after open reduction → fixation + iliac crest graft

## How it's asked

**"6-yr distal clavicle fracture, lateral fragment superior?"** → **sling**

**"16-yr hockey player, likely injury pattern?"** → **physeal separation, intact inferior sleeve, intact CC + AC**

**"12-yr elite gymnast, distal clavicle physeal fracture?"** → **sling**\
✗ Wrong options: stress X-ray · MRI · fixation

**"MRI of 11-yr distal clavicle fracture shows?"** → **intact inferior periosteal sleeve**\
✗ Wrong options: CC disruption · AC dislocation

**"Last physis to close?"** → **medial clavicle (20–25 yr)**
