# External Tibial Torsion

*Pediatrics · 44 / 112*

## 1 Basics

**Definition** — abnormal **external rotation of the tibia** → **out-toeing** · usually **right-sided** if unilateral

**Normal development** — tibia externally rotates ~15° in childhood · femoral anteversion falls ~25°

**Natural history** — **worsens** in late childhood/adolescence (tibia rotates externally with growth) · **more often needs surgery than internal tibial torsion**

**Associated** — **miserable malalignment** (ETT + femoral anteversion) · **Osgood-Schlatter** · **OCD** · early knee DJD · neuromuscular (myelodysplasia, polio)

## 3 Presentation

**Symptoms** — out-toeing, tripping · **anterior knee pain**, **patellofemoral instability** (positive apprehension)

![External tibial torsion](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob4121-external-tibial-torsion-example.jpg)

**Out-toeing by age** — infancy/toddler → **hip external rotation contracture** · late childhood/adolescence → **external tibial torsion**

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

**Thigh–foot axis (TFA)** — **best clinical measure** · prone, knee 90° · infancy ~5° internal · **age 8: ~10° external (−5 to +30°)** · **>20° external** = ETT

**Transmalleolar axis** — supine · infancy 4–5° internal · adult **23° external** (0–40°)

**Foot progression angle** — ~5° external in late childhood

**CT torsional study** — tibial version = distal minus proximal axis; femoral version neck minus condyles

**Imaging** — usually none

![Thigh–foot axis](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob4121-schematic-of-the-thigh-foot-axis.jpg)

## 5 Management

**First line** — rest, rehab, activity modification (bracing ineffective)

### Operative criteria

| Source | Indication |
|---|---|
| Orthobullets | **>8 yr** + ETT **>3 SD (>40° external)** |
| OB | **≥9 yr** + **TFA >30°** (or < −15°) · normal TFA −5° to 20° |

**Procedure** — **supramalleolar (distal tibial) derotation osteotomy** (most common) ± oblique fibular osteotomy · pins or plate · IM nail only if mature

**Distal vs proximal** — distal: easier, safer (↓ NV injury, ↓ compartment syndrome) · proximal: more contact/soft tissue but **peroneal nerve risk** → anterior fasciotomy

**12-yr, right ETT 45° (left 32°), normal femoral version** → **right supramalleolar osteotomy**\
✗ Wrong options: observe · KAFO · distal tibial guided growth (causes coronal deformity) · femoral osteotomy

## X vs Y

| Cause of out-toeing | Age |
|---|---|
| **Hip ER contracture** | Infant / toddler |
| **External tibial torsion** | Late childhood / adolescent |

## How it's asked

**"12-yr, out-toeing, TFA +30°, patellar apprehension, symmetric hip rotation?"** → **excessive external tibial torsion**\
✗ Wrong options: femoral anteversion · ER contracture · internal torsion

**"12-yr right ETT 45° on CT?"** → **supramalleolar osteotomy**

**"Best exam measure?"** → **thigh–foot axis**
