# Transient Synovitis of Hip

*Pediatrics · 110 / 112*

## 1 Basics

**Definition** — nonspecific inflammation + hypertrophy of synovium · **most common cause of pediatric hip pain** · diagnosis of exclusion vs **septic arthritis**

**Epidemiology** — **3% of children 3–10 yr** · peak **4–8 yr** · **M:F 2:1** · hip most common joint · **recurrence up to 20%**

### Diagnosis & preceding viral illness

**Cause** — largely unknown · **viral upper respiratory infection** (most cited) · bacterial (**poststreptococcal toxic synovitis**) · trauma · ↑ interferon · allergy

**Preceded by** — **infectious respiratory illness**\
✗ Wrong options: hematogenous bacterial inoculation (septic arthritis) · hypothyroidism (SCFE) · secondhand smoke (Perthes) · LLD (painless limp)

### Genes & lab markers

| Lab | Transient synovitis |
|---|---|
| WBC | Normal / slightly ↑ |
| **CRP** | **<20 mg/L** — **most important to RULE OUT septic arthritis** · CRP >20 = independent risk factor for sepsis |
| ESR | Usually <20 mm/h (can be ↑ — 35 with normal CRP + rapid improvement still synovitis) |
| Synovial WBC | Near normal · **>50,000 → treat as septic** |

## 3 Presentation

**History** — recent URI or trauma · mild / no fever · acute or **insidious groin / thigh pain** · refuses to bear weight → **better later in day** (limps by evening) · muscle spasm

**Exam** — hip in **flexion, abduction, external rotation** (lowest intracapsular pressure) · not toxic · **↓ internal rotation = most sensitive** ROM sign · painless arc → synovitis > sepsis · log-roll guarding · spine + knee non-tender · toe-walking / cavus / claw toes → neurological limp

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

**X-ray** — AP + frog-leg / lateral · ± spine · **usually normal**

**US** — effusion (accurate) · synovial thickening · **can't separate synovitis from sepsis**, but **no effusion → infection unlikely**

**MRI** — myositis / osteomyelitis suspicion · markedly ↑ labs

**Aspiration** — if septic suspicion (clinical / Kocher criteria)

**Differential** — septic hip · osteomyelitis (point tenderness, swelling, ↑ markers) · **SCFE** (10–16 yr, visible on AP) · **Perthes** (visible on X-ray) · leukemia (normal CBC rules out) · JIA (rarely hip) · fracture (no trauma)

## 5 Management

**Low septic suspicion** — **IV / PO NSAIDs + close observation 24 h** · minimize walking · ± traction · **improves on NSAIDs → synovitis**

**Course** — **marked improvement 24–48 h** · resolves **<1 wk** (Orthobullets treatment) · 1–2 wk (prognosis)

## 6 Complications

**Recurrence** — **~20%**

**Perthes** — 0–10% (controversial; most series no sequelae)

## X vs Y

| | **Transient synovitis** | **Septic arthritis** |
|---|---|---|
| Fever | Mild / none | Higher |
| CRP | **<20 mg/L** | >20 |
| Course | Rapid improvement on NSAIDs | Doesn't improve |
| Synovial WBC | Near normal | >50,000 |

## How it's asked

**"8-yr hip pain, CRP 1.8, ESR 35, walking after 24 h ketorolac?"** → **transient synovitis**\
✗ Wrong options: septic arthritis · leukemia · JIA

**"5-yr recent URI, CRP 9, ESR 20, limited IR?"** → **transient synovitis**\
✗ Wrong options: septic · SCFE · Perthes

**"5-yr limp, normal labs — what preceded?"** → **infectious respiratory illness**\
✗ Wrong options: bacterial inoculation · hypothyroidism · secondhand smoke

**"2-yr improving, afebrile, normal CRP, US effusion?"** → **toxic synovitis**\
✗ Wrong options: septic · osteomyelitis · leukemia
