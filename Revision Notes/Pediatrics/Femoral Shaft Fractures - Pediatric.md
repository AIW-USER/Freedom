# Femoral Shaft Fractures - Pediatric

*Pediatrics · 46 / 112*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Incidence** — **1.6–2%** of pediatric fractures · **most common orthopaedic cause of pediatric hospitalisation** · **bimodal: toddlers 2–4 yr + adolescents** · **M 2.6:1**

**Mechanism by age** — 0–2 yr: **NAT** · <10 yr: **falls** · >10 yr: **MVA** (cortex thickens with age)

**Associated** — OI · neuromuscular osteopenia · tumours

### Deforming forces

**Proximal fragment** — **iliopsoas** (flexion) + **abductors** (gluteus medius/minimus → abduction) + **short external rotators** (piriformis → ER)\
✗ Wrong options: adductors · hamstrings · rectus (insert distally)

**Distal fragment** — **adductors** → shortening + **varus**

**Typical malunion** — **varus + flexion (procurvatum)** of distal fragment

### Stability

**Length-stable** — transverse, short oblique

**Length-unstable** — **long spiral (length >2× diameter)**, comminuted

**TO rule** — **TENS contraindicated / not alone in length-unstable** fractures → 5–11 yr unstable: **submuscular bridge plate or ex-fix** · TENS nail **30–40% of canal each**, same size, don't bend at entry · rigid nail **trochanteric entry only, never piriformis**, > 11 yr / > 50 kg (40–50 kg TO) · before walking age → suspect NAI

## 3 Presentation

**Exam** — thigh pain, can't walk, deformity, shortening, swelling

**Hemodynamic instability** — **isolated pediatric femur rarely causes shock** → **re-evaluate for other injuries** (fluids if Hb >7, transfuse if <7)\
✗ Wrong options: pressors · transfusion first if Hb >7 · fasciotomy · emergent fixation

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

**Views** — **AP + lateral femur** + ipsilateral **hip and knee**

### Non-accidental trauma

**Suspect** — **<3 yr** (AAOS: evaluate <36 months), **especially non-walkers**

**Patterns most predictive** — **transverse** femoral shaft · **metaphyseal corner fracture (pathognomonic)** · **posterior rib fractures**, scapula, sternum, spinous process · injury inconsistent with history (9-month-old "running")\
✗ Wrong options: spiral, long oblique, toddler's

**Infant with metaphyseal corner fracture** → **admit + social services**\
✗ Wrong options: Pavlik and discharge · spondylometaphyseal dysplasia/syphilis work-up first · infection work-up

**Distractors** — lytic lesion = pathologic · **blue sclera = OI** · low vitamin D / metaphyseal fraying = **rickets**

## 5 Management

### Treatment selection by age & weight

| Age | Pattern / factor | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **<6 months** | Any | **Pavlik harness** (or spica) |
| **6 mo – 5 yr** | Stable, **shortening <2 cm** | **Immediate spica** |
| 6 mo – 5 yr | Unstable, **>2–3 cm short**, polytrauma, open | Traction → delayed spica · ex-fix |
| **5–11 yr** | **Length-stable + <49 kg (100 lb)** | **Flexible (titanium elastic) nails** |
| 5–11 yr | **Length-unstable** (comminuted, long spiral), very proximal/distal, any weight | **Submuscular bridge plate** · stainless Ender nails · ex-fix |
| **>11 yr / >49 kg / near maturity** | Diaphyseal | **Antegrade rigid nail — trochanteric or lateral entry** (✗ Wrong options: piriformis) |
| >11 yr | Proximal/distal, severe comminution | Submuscular plate |
| Polytrauma, open, vascular repair, damage control | — | **External fixation** |

**Age ranges vary** — spica 6 mo–5 yr (Orthobullets) · 6 mo–6 yr / 1–6 yr (OB) · Pavlik <6 mo (Orthobullets) / <1 yr (OB) · trochanteric rigid nail from **~9 yr** (trochanteric apophysis no longer key; earlier → **coxa valga**) (OB)

```
Pediatric femoral shaft fracture
├─ <6 mo → Pavlik harness
├─ 6 mo–5 yr, <2 cm short → immediate spica
├─ 5–11 yr
│      ├─ Length-stable, <49 kg → flexible nails
│      └─ Length-unstable / heavy / proximal-distal → submuscular plate
└─ >11 yr or >49 kg → trochanteric/lateral-entry rigid IM nail
```

### Operative criteria

| Device | Key criterion |
|---|---|
| **Flexible nails** | 5–11 yr · **<49 kg / 100–108 lb** · length-stable · open or closed OK · not comminuted |
| **Contraindication to flexible nails** | **Weight** (e.g. 146 lb) — not age, height, multiple fractures, open fracture |
| **Submuscular plate** | Length-unstable · >49 kg · proximal/distal · comminuted (incl. **open comminuted in 4-yr**) |
| **Rigid IM nail** | >11 yr / near maturity · >49 kg · unstable · trochanteric/lateral entry |
| **Spica** | Shortening **<2 cm** (loss of reduction doubles per cm: 12% → 24% → 50%) · not polytrauma, open, >2–3 cm |
| **Ex-fix** | Polytrauma (damage control), open, vascular injury, segmental |

**Ipsilateral femur + tibia (floating knee) in 7-yr** → **flexible nails both**\
✗ Wrong options: spica · reamed nails (physeal/AVN risk)

### Infant fractures - Pavlik harness

**<6 months** → **Pavlik** (no anaesthesia, easy diapering, fewer skin problems than spica) · not in **neuromuscular spasticity** or **polytrauma** · 9-month-old → spica\
✗ Wrong options: flexible nails, ex-fix, growing rods, traction, plate

**Pavlik risk** — **femoral nerve compression** with excessive flexion + swollen thigh (↓ quads)

### Spica technique

**Position** — hips **60–90° flexion**, ~**30° abduction**, **external rotation** to correct rotation · mould into **recurvatum + valgus** · single-leg or 1½ spica (special car seat sometimes)

**Spica tips (TO)** — traction during application · **valgus moulding** (prevents varus collapse) · reinforce the **V-junction** · hip and knee flexion equal · **connecting bar** between limbs

**Pavlik for femur (TO)** — < 6 mo · **more proximal fracture → more hip flexion** · usually ≤ 90° · \~**2 wk** · watch femoral nerve · confirm union on X-ray

![Spica cast](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob4019-screen-shot-2012-07-26-at-11-11-52-am.jpg)

**Acceptable** — **<10° coronal, <20° sagittal, ≤2 cm shortening, ≤10° rotation**

**Union** — 4–8 wk · check at 7–10 d

### Spica cast complications

**Most common needing revision** — **loss of reduction** (11%)\
✗ Wrong options: delayed/nonunion · cosmetic · LLD

**Compartment syndrome risk** — **"90/90" technique: short-leg portion first then traction** · **knee flexion >90°** (e.g. 105°) · **firm popliteal mould** · high energy · **NOT** immediate ED casting under sedation, single-leg spica, casting within 24 h → apply **body + thigh first**, lower leg last

### Traction

**Distal femoral pin** (proximal to physis) · **avoid proximal tibial pin → tibial tubercle damage → genu recurvatum** (also from SH V physeal injury) · 2–3 wk then spica · more complications than immediate spica

### Flexible nailing technique & complications

**Entry** — retrograde, medial + lateral, **~2–2.5 cm proximal to distal physis**

**Nail size** — each = **0.4 × isthmus**, **two equal nails**, **80% canal fill** (e.g. 8-yr, 35 kg → **two 4.0 mm**) · **largest equal nails** → best stability\
✗ Wrong options: asymmetric · impacted ends · open reduction routinely · hinged brace

**Most common complication** — **pain/irritation at insertion site near knee (up to 40%)** → keep protrusion **<25 mm**\
✗ Wrong options: shortening · AVN · malunion · rotation

**↑ Complications** — **≥11 yr or >49–50 kg (>108 lb)** → ↑ nonunion, malunion, complications, reoperation · comminuted/proximal/distal → malunion · ~50% have 15° malalignment

**Union** 10–12 wk · remove ~1 yr

### Rigid nail entry point & femoral head AVN

**Piriformis entry** — **contraindicated with open physes** → injures **deep branch of MFCA** (lateral epiphyseal / superior retinacular vessels) → **femoral head AVN** (groin/hip pain months later) · AVN **1–2%** (Orthobullets) · **≥4%** (OB)

**MFCA** — main head supply **after age 4**

**Safest** — **lateral entry** (lateral GT) > GT tip · GT entry risks **GT apophyseal arrest → coxa valga**, neck narrowing, hip subluxation

**Rigid vs flexible** — rigid: **higher AVN** (with open physes), less angular malunion, better in >11 yr / >49 kg · flexible: more knee pain, more malunion in older/heavier

### Polytrauma, head injury & timing

**Polytrauma + emergent laparotomy (13-yr, GCS 6)** → **external fixation** (damage control)\
✗ Wrong options: traction · nailing · plate

**Early fixation with head injury** — **no ↑ pulmonary or CNS complications**, **↓ length of stay**\
✗ Wrong options: ↑ ARDS/pneumonia · worse mental function

## 6 Complications

**Overgrowth** — **most common in young** · **0.7–2 cm <10 yr** (avg 0.9 cm 2–10 yr) · within **2 yr** → accept shortening ≤2–3 cm

**Shortening >2–3 cm** — symptomatic → traction/fixation

**Malunion** — varus + flexion · remodels best in **sagittal plane** · **rotation doesn't remodel**

**Refracture** — **after ex-fix removal**, esp. **varus** (1.5–21%) · ex-fix: pin-tract infection (~50% of its complications), delayed/nonunion

**Plate** — refracture after hardware removal

**Genu recurvatum** — tibial tubercle arrest (traction pin / SH V)

## X vs Y

| | **Flexible nails** | **Submuscular plate** | **Rigid trochanteric nail** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Age | 5–11 | 5–11+ | >11 (≥9 OB) |
| Weight | <49 kg | Any | >49 kg |
| Pattern | **Length-stable** | **Length-unstable**, comminuted, proximal/distal | Any |
| Main complication | Knee pain | Refracture on removal | AVN (piriformis), coxa valga (GT) |
