# Bladder Exstrophy

*Pediatrics · 9 / 112*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Classic exstrophy** — **1/40,000**

**Cloacal exstrophy** — **1/200,000** (intestinal tract also involved)

### Etiology

**Pathology** — abnormal anterior rupture of the **cloacal membrane** early in embryonic period · inhibited mesenchymal ingrowth into abdominal wall · **altered sclerotome migration** to anterior elements (pubis)

**Result** — failure of abdominal wall closure → **posterior bladder wall protrudes** through lower abdomen

## 3 Presentation

**GU** — **exposed bladder** · urinary tract infections · epispadias (males)

**Musculoskeletal** — acetabula **~12° retroverted** (no pubis to tether anterior ring → posterior elements retrovert) · **waddling gait** with **external foot progression**

![Bladder exstrophy — exposed bladder](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob4125-bladder-clinical.jpg)

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

**AP pelvis** — **pubic rami diastasis** · **shortened pubic rami** · **acetabular retroversion**

### Acetabular retroversion

**Most common acetabular finding** — **retroversion**\
✗ Wrong options: anteversion · protrusio · neutral · hypoplasia

![Pubic diastasis in bladder exstrophy](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob4125-bladders-exs-xr.jpg)

## 5 Management

**Goals** — close abdominal wall · **urinary continence** · normal renal function · functional genitalia · reconstruction depends on a **stable anterior pelvic ring**

**All cases** — **surgery**, staged and multidisciplinary (urology, general surgery)

| Stage | Procedure | Age |
|---|---|---|
| **I** | **Primary bladder closure** | Newborn |
| **II** | **Epispadias repair** (males) | 1–2 yr |
| **III** | **Bladder neck reconstruction** | 4 yr |
| Any stage | **Pelvic osteotomies** — ↓ tension on bladder + abdominal wall → ↓ **dehiscence** | — |

```
Pelvic osteotomy fixation by age
├─ Newborn → none (skin traction, hips flexed 90°)
├─ Young child → external fixation
└─ Age >8 → plate fixation to augment diastasis correction
```

## 6 Complications

**Recurrent pubic diastasis** — common **with or without osteotomy** · does not affect activity level

**Anterior innominate osteotomy** — wound dehiscence · **transient femoral nerve palsy**

## How it's asked

**"Most common acetabular finding in bladder exstrophy?"** → **retroversion**

**"Pelvic radiograph findings?"** → **pubic diastasis, short pubic rami, acetabular retroversion**

**"Why pelvic osteotomy?"** → **↓ tension on bladder/abdominal closure → ↓ dehiscence**

**"Gait in bladder exstrophy?"** → **waddling, external foot progression**
