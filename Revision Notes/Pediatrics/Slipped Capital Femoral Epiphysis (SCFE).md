# Slipped Capital Femoral Epiphysis (SCFE)

*Pediatrics · 98 / 112*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Incidence** — **most common adolescent hip disorder** · 10 per 100,000

**Who** — **obese** (single greatest risk) · **males** (2:1.4 Orthobullets; ~2× TO) · **African American**, Pacific Islander, Latino · rapid growth **10–16 yr** · boys **12–13.4**, girls **11.2–12.2** · **left hip** more common (2× TO)

**Bilateral** — 17–50%, average **25%** (Orthobullets) · 25–40% (TO) · 20–30% at presentation (TO) · second slip usually within **12–18 months**

**Risk factors** — obesity · **acetabular + femoral retroversion** (↓ anteversion, ↓ neck-shaft angle) · prior **radiation** · **↑ leptin (4.9× odds, independent of obesity)** · Down syndrome · open triradiate

### Pathophysiology

**Slip** — **metaphysis moves ANTERIOR + externally rotates** · epiphysis stays in acetabulum, **posterior/inferior** to neck

**Plane** — through the **hypertrophic zone** (✓) · renal SCFE through **secondary spongiosa**

**Why adolescents** — **perichondral ring thins** · mammillary processes unlock · physis still vertical (160° birth → 125° maturity) → shear · epiphyseal tubercle pivot

**vs SH I fracture** — antecedent epiphysiolysis, slower, periosteum intact (chronic) · type I transepiphyseal fracture = high energy, young child, M = F (TO)

### Histopathology

**Chronic SCFE histology** — **granulation tissue between columns in hypertrophic zone** · ↓ perichondral ring thickness · ↓ physeal undulations · abnormal proteoglycan accumulation · ↑ hypertrophic zone height\
✗ Wrong options: vascular invasion of proliferative zone · thick ring

### Endocrine & metabolic associations

**Endocrine** — **hypothyroidism (most common non-idiopathic; ↑ TSH)** · **renal osteodystrophy** (↑ BUN, creatinine) · **GH deficiency** · **panhypopituitarism** (pituitary adenoma) · Loder: hypothyroidism 40%, GH deficiency 25%, all hypothyroid → bilateral

**Endocrine work-up if** — **<10 yr** · **weight <50th percentile** · prepubertal / short · labs: **TSH, free T4, BUN, creatinine**

**Atypical SCFE** — <10 or >16 yr (TO) · renal failure, radiation, hypogonadism, Down

### Blood supply

**Lateral epiphyseal artery (MFCA)** = main supply · LFCA anterior ring · ligamentum teres insignificant · **posterosuperior** hardware endangers vessels

## 3 Presentation

### Presentation & diagnosis (knee pain, hip films)

**Pain** — hip 52%, **thigh 35%**, groin 14% · **knee pain 15–50%** (23% Matava; obturator nerve referral) · hip pain still more common than knee\
✗ Wrong options: "knee more often than hip"

**Delay in diagnosis** — **knee pain** · stable slip · Medicaid · severity · 88% of unstable slips had ~42 days of prior symptoms

**Exam** — antalgic / Trendelenburg / out-toeing gait · **obligatory ER with hip flexion (Drehmann sign)** · ↓ **IR**, abduction, flexion · sits with leg crossed · tender Scarpa triangle (TO)

**Obese teen with knee pain + normal knee X-rays** → **examine hip + AP pelvis/frog-lateral**\
✗ Wrong options: knee MRI · arthroscopy · labs · PT

**Unmanipulated severe SCFE on AP** → leg lies in **external rotation**

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

**Views** — **AP + frog-lateral of both hips** · lateral best for subtle slip · **unstable → cross-table lateral** (not frog)

**Klein line** — along superior neck · normally cuts lateral epiphysis · SCFE: intersects less / not at all · ≥2 mm asymmetry improves sensitivity 40% → ~80% (TO)

**Other signs** — **metaphyseal blanch sign of Steel** (double density, AP) · **S sign** broken on frog · **epiphysiolysis** (early widening)

**MRI** — **pre-slip**: physeal widening + **metaphyseal edema** (T1 ↓, T2 ↑) · CT: physeal closure, osteotomy planning

**Age pattern (TO, radiology)** — hip pain without sport at **11–12 yr → SCFE** · **7–8 yr → Perthes** · frog-leg shows irregular physis · MRI: **physeal widening + fluid**, effusion, head slipped backwards

![Klein line](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob4040-kleins-line-moved.jpg)

**"Metaphysis moves, not the epiphysis" (TO)** — metaphysis displaces **anterosuperiorly**, epiphysis stays in acetabulum · weak **hypertrophic zone** + thinned **perichondrial ring** · shear ↑ by obesity, vertical physis, **femoral retroversion**

**Signs (TO)** — Klein line = **Trethowan sign** · **blanch sign of Steel** (chronic) · widened irregular physis · broken Shenton · risk: **prior radiotherapy** · **< 10 yr or thin → endocrine work-up**

**Loder AVN (TO)** — stable **< 10%** · unstable **up to 40%** · stability predicts AVN better than slip angle

### Loder classification (stability)

| Type | Definition | AVN |
|---|---|---|
| **Stable** (85–95%) | Can walk ± crutches | **<10%** (~0%) |
| **Unstable** (5–15%) | **Can't walk even with crutches** | **24–47%** (Orthobullets) · 47–50% · 25–50% · 15–50% · up to 60% (OB) · 2–5× stable |

**Temporal** — acute <3 wk · chronic >3 wk · acute-on-chronic (no prognostic value)

### Slip severity measurement

**Southwick (epiphyseal-shaft) angle** — **severity** · frog lateral (± AP) · affected minus normal · bilateral reference **145° AP / 10° lateral** (Orthobullets) · 12° (TO)\
✗ Wrong options: presence · AVN prognosis · chronicity

| Severity | Orthobullets | OB / TO |
|---|---|---|
| Mild | **<30°** | <30° |
| Moderate | **30–50°** | 30–60° |
| Severe | **>50°** | >60° |

**% displacement grade** — I 0–33% · II 34–50% · III >50%

![Southwick angle](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob4040-southwick-angle.jpg)

**Differential** — septic hip, transient synovitis, osteomyelitis, Perthes, DDH, trauma (adductor strain, AIIS avulsion) · breaststroke swimmer, open physes, normal head-neck → **external rotator (obturator externus) tear**

## 5 Management

```
SCFE
├─ Stable → single central screw in situ (no reduction), WB after
├─ Unstable → urgent bed rest → in situ fixation ± 2nd screw, ± decompression/capsulotomy, TDWB
├─ High-risk contralateral → prophylactic pinning
└─ Residual deformity → osteochondroplasty (<30°) / Imhauser osteotomy (>30–45°)
```

### In situ fixation technique

**Rule** — **percutaneous in situ fixation for stable AND unstable** · **no forceful reduction** (↑ AVN) · serendipitous positional reduction OK

**Screw** — **single cannulated screw** usually (↓ AVN vs multiple) · **2 screws more stable** (some for unstable) · not inverted triangle (adult neck fracture, ↑ penetration + AVN)

**Placement** — **start on ANTERIOR femoral neck** → **perpendicular to physis** → **centre-centre** of epiphysis on AP + lateral · **not medial to intertrochanteric line** (screw head impinges, labral tear) · severe slip → more oblique from intertrochanteric region

**Depth** — **≥5 threads across physis** (<5 → 41% progression vs 0%) · tip ~5 mm from subchondral bone · partially threaded: 40–60% of threads in epiphysis strongest · **approach-withdraw** fluoroscopy to exclude penetration

**Post-op** — stable WB · unstable TDWB · young child: short-thread screw not crossing physis (TO)

**Stable mild slip in 13-yr** → **single-screw in situ fixation**\
✗ Wrong options: spica (chondrolysis) · ORIF · NWB · closed reduction

**Stable SCFE seen only on MRI** → **in situ fixation**\
✗ Wrong options: observation · labral repair

![In situ screw fixation](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob4040-scfe4-pinning.jpg)

### Unstable SCFE & AVN

**Unstable slip** → **strict bed rest + urgent surgery** (within hours; 8 h OB, 24 h OB) on affected hip\
✗ Wrong options: MRI · elective · NSAIDs trial

**AVN risk factors** — **instability (greatest)** · initial displacement · **forceful / over-reduction**, reduction of chronic slip · **posterosuperior (superolateral) hardware** · multiple pins · femoral neck osteotomy\
✗ Wrong options: age, sex, obesity · single screw · in situ without reduction

**Capsulotomy / decompression** — unstable ICP ~2× normal · may ↓ AVN (low evidence) · TO advocates routine capsulotomy in acute unstable · **ICP probe monophasic waveform** → **decompress hip, then capsulotomy if still monophasic**

**Modified Dunn (surgical dislocation, capital realignment)** — unstable + severe · posterior retinacular flap carries blood supply · remove posterior callus · AVN **~26%** vs 24% in situ · complications 37% · steep learning curve

**Most dreaded complication** — **AVN** (TO); MFCA injury

![Modified Dunn](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob4040-illustration-of-modified-dunn.jpg)

### Contralateral prophylactic pinning

| Indication | NOT an indication |
|---|---|
| **Age <10** (girls <10, boys <12–12.5) | Unstable presenting slip |
| **Open triradiate cartilage** | Chronicity / duration of symptoms |
| **Endocrinopathy** (hypothyroid, renal, GH, pituitary adenoma) | Height >95th percentile |
| **Obesity** (BMI >95th) / obese males | African American ethnicity |
| **Posterior sloping angle >12° (TO) / >14.5° (OB)** | Elite athlete · DI · weight 40th percentile |
| **Modified Oxford score 16–18** (~98% risk; 24 = 0%) | Closed triradiate |
| Weight <10th or >90th percentile · unreliable follow-up | Continued slipping after fixation (TO) |

**Contralateral slip** — **most common complication after unilateral fixation** · 20–80% · up to 36% high-risk · ↓ with weight loss · **more common in YOUNGER children**\
✗ Wrong options: older (TO false statement)

**Prophylactic pinning risk** — 2–6% complications

**Both hips involved / silent grade 1 on other side / pre-slip on MRI** → **pin both**

### Chronic deformity, osteotomy & natural history

**Residual deformity** — ↑ α-angle, **pistol-grip**, cam FAI · sequelae: **shortening, ↓ flexion, ↓ IR**, out-toeing

| Deformity | Procedure |
|---|---|
| Mild–moderate (<30°) cam FAI | **Osteochondroplasty** (arthroscopy, limited anterior arthrotomy, surgical dislocation) |
| Severe (>30–45°), painful, no OA/AVN | **Imhauser intertrochanteric osteotomy** — **flexion + internal rotation + valgus** (most used; AVN 2–7%) |
| Base-of-neck (TO) | **Kramer** closing wedge (lesser → greater trochanter, posterior cortex intact) — away from physis, ↓ AVN · subcapital: **Fish**, Dunn, modified Dunn · intertrochanteric "good indication" for chronic SCFE **25–50°** (TO) |
| | Southwick subtrochanteric osteotomy |
| Greatest correction | **Femoral neck (subcapital) cuneiform osteotomy** — AVN 37%, OA 37% · sole indication **Southwick >60°** (TO) |

**Natural history** — most well tolerated · **~5% THA at 20 yr** · realignment not proven to prevent OA · closed reduction worse\
✗ Wrong options: 25%

![Imhauser osteotomy](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob4040-osteotomy.jpg)

## 6 Complications

### Complications - chondrolysis, impingement, slip progression

**Chondrolysis** — 0–2% · joint space **<3 mm** (normal 4–6) + ↓ ROM (TO) · **pin penetration, ANTEROSUPERIOR quadrant** · **spica cast** · CT best for penetration\
✗ Wrong options: grade of slip · no reduction

**Labral tear / impingement** — **anterior 2nd screw medial to IT line** (in-out-in)

**Slip progression** — 1–2% with single screw · prevented by **two (fully threaded) screws**, ≥5 threads, WB restriction\
✗ Wrong options: spica · bone graft epiphysiodesis

**Other** — AVN (op-related 4–6%) · infection 0–2% · chronic pain 5–10% · knee pain 5–10% · LLD · OA · femoral neck fracture (extra drill holes)

## X vs Y

| | **SCFE** | **Delbet I (transepiphyseal) fracture** |
|---|---|---|
| Energy | Minor / none | High |
| Age | 10–16 | Young child |
| Sex | M > F, obese | M = F |
| Course | Insidious ± acute-on-chronic | Acute |
