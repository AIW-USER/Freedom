# Pseudosubluxation of the Cervical Spine

*Spine · 48 / 69*

## 1 Basics

**Definition** — **physiologic** anterior translation (up to **4 mm / 40%**) in young children

**Incidence** — **\~20%** of children admitted with polytrauma · **no association with gender**, trauma, intubation or injury severity\
✗ Wrong options: commoner in girls

**Age** — **< 8 yrs**

**Level** — **C2 on C3 commonest** · C3 on C4 second

**Cause** — **horizontal facet joints** → become vertical with age

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

**Views** — lateral + **flexion-extension**

**Swischuk's line** — spinolaminar line **C1 → C3** · C2 spinolaminar point within **1.5 mm** = pseudosubluxation

![Swischuk's line](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob2048-lateral-xray-pseudosubluxation.jpg)

### Pseudosubluxation vs true subluxation

| Pseudosubluxation | True traumatic subluxation |
|---|---|
| **Reduces on extension** | Persists |
| Spinolaminar line **within 1.5 mm** of C2 | > 1.5 mm |
| **No significant trauma** history/exam | Trauma (e.g. **Hangman's fracture**) |
| **No anterior soft-tissue swelling** | Prevertebral swelling |

![True subluxation — Hangman's fracture](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob2048-hangman-fracture.jpg)

## 5 Management

**Observation** — no ↑ morbidity or mortality

## How it's asked

**"Pseudosubluxation — EXCEPT?"** → **more common in female children** (TO)

**"5-yr, no trauma, C2-3 step reduces on extension?"** → **pseudosubluxation of the cervical spine**
