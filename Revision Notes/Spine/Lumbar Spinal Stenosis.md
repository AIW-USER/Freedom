# Lumbar Spinal Stenosis

*Spine · 36 / 69*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Incidence** — **commonest reason for lumbar surgery > 65 yrs** · seen in 20–25% · M:F 1.5:1 · average age 65

**Level** — **L4-5 (91%)**

**Risk factors** — Caucasian, ↑ BMI, congenital anomalies (20%: short pedicles + laminae)

**Strongest factor for symptomatic degenerative disc disease** — **genetic predisposition** (TO)\
✗ Wrong options: heavy lifting · vibration · tobacco

### Pathoanatomy

**Cascade** — nucleus loses cells, water, proteoglycan → disc height ↓, annulus bulges → **anterior column absorbs less stress → force transfers to posterior elements** → facet hypertrophy, osteophytes, **ligamentum flavum buckling / hypertrophy**

**Compressing structures** — bony: **facet osteophytes**, uncinate (posterior body) spur, spondylolisthesis · soft: bulging disc, **ligamentum flavum**, synovial facet cyst

**Commonest compression source** — **superior articular process** (facet hypertrophy in the lateral recess) (TO)\
✗ Wrong options: disc · lamina · inferior articular process

**Posture (TO)** — canal space **maximal in flexion** (and axial distraction) · ↓ with **extension + axial loading** → side-lying (flexed) better than flat · uphill easier · **shopping-cart sign**

**Facets** — become **more coronal** moving inferiorly

![Facet orientation down the lumbar spine](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob2037-facet-orientation.jpg)

### Ligamentum flavum

**Origin** — **ventral surface of the cranial lamina, 60–70% of the way up (closer to the superior edge)** · inserts on the **superior edge of the caudal lamina** (OB) · TO: origin **lower half of anterior surface of lamina above**, insertion **posterior surface + upper margin of lamina below** — read the options

**Composition** — **80% elastin, 20% type I collagen** (✗ Wrong options: type 2) · **posterior column** (✗ Wrong options: middle column) · not the primary flexion restraint (= **supraspinous ligament**)

## 3 Presentation

**Neurogenic claudication** — back, **buttock**, thigh pain/heaviness · **worse with extension** (walking, standing, **downhill/down stairs**, lying supine) · **relieved by flexion** (sitting, shopping cart, cycling, fetal position)

**Neuro exam** — often **normal** (examined seated) · SLR usually negative · no UMN signs (clonus, Babinski = cord) · no step-wise progression (= cervical myelopathy) · Valsalva doesn't worsen (unlike HNP)

**Kemp sign** — unilateral radicular pain from **foraminal** stenosis worse with **back extension**

**Bladder** — recurrent UTIs up to 10% (autonomic sphincter dysfunction) · CES rare

**L2-3 / L3-4 stenosis** — pain in **anterior thigh + anteromedial leg**\
✗ Wrong options: plantar/lateral foot = S1 · anterolateral leg/dorsum = L5 · inguinal = L1

### Neurogenic vs vascular claudication

| | Neurogenic | Vascular |
|---|---|---|
| Postural change | **Yes** | No |
| Walking upright | Symptoms | Symptoms |
| **Standing still** | **Symptoms persist** | **Relieves** |
| Sitting | Relieves | Relieves |
| **Stairs** | **Up easier** (flexed); down worse | Down easier; up worse |
| **Stationary bike** (flexed) | **Relieves** | **Causes** |
| **Leaning backwards** | **Worse** (✓ favours neurogenic) | No effect |
| Pulses | Normal | **Abnormal / asymmetric** |
| Other | — | **Night pain relieved by hanging leg**, trophic changes, calcified iliacs |

**Suspected vascular** → **ankle-brachial index** · ABI 0.4–0.8 → claudication\
✗ Wrong options: SNRB · EMG/NCS · hip X-ray · CT myelogram

**Hip-spine syndrome** — hip OA + stenosis → **diagnostic lidocaine + steroid injection** (hip or epidural) to find the primary generator · severe stenosis → decompress anyway (risk of motor weakness)\
✗ Wrong options: THA · decompression first · EMG

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Study | Finding |
|---|---|
| Standing AP + lateral | Degenerative changes, **degenerative spondylolisthesis**, scoliosis · poor clinical correlation |
| **Flexion-extension** | **Segmental instability** → decides fusion |
| **MRI** | **Modality of choice** · thecal sac **< 100 mm²**, lost perineural fat, facet + flavum hypertrophy · positive in asymptomatic people |
| CT myelogram | Can't have MRI; bony detail · superior to MRI for decompression planning (OB) · can't separate vascular from neurogenic |
| Plain myelogram | Dynamic cut-off in extension (invasive) |

### Diagnostic criteria – critical central stenosis (axial CT)

| Measure | Threshold |
|---|---|
| **AP canal diameter** | **< 10 mm** |
| **Cross-sectional area** | **< 100 mm²** |
| No | Transverse < 20 mm, combined < 30 mm, area 130 mm², AP < 20/50 mm |

![AP diameter X–Y on axial CT](../../MCQ/attachments/q3742-a-area-xy.jpg)

### Anatomic classification

| Zone | Cause | Root (at L4-5) |
|---|---|---|
| **Central** | Flavum hypertrophy + bulging disc → thecal sac | **Lower / non-specific (L5)** |
| **Lateral recess (subarticular)** | Facet arthropathy, **superior articular facet overgrowth** | **Descending / traversing (L5)** |
| **Foraminal** (between pedicle borders) | Disc-height loss, foraminal disc/osteophyte, scoliosis · **superior facet overhang** | **Exiting (L4)** |
| **Extraforaminal** (lateral to pedicle) | **Far lateral disc** | **Exiting (L4)** |

### Etiologic classification

| Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Acquired** | **Degenerative (commonest)**, post-surgical, post-traumatic, AS, Paget, acromegaly, fluorosis |
| Congenital | Short pedicles, medial facets · idiopathic or developmental (**achondroplasia**) |

## 5 Management

```
Lumbar spinal stenosis
├─ First line → NSAIDs, PT, WEIGHT LOSS, BRACE, EPIDURAL STEROID
└─ Failed 3–6 months / progressive deficit → WIDE DECOMPRESSION
       ├─ Stable (normal flexion-extension) → DECOMPRESSION ALONE
       └─ Instability / spondylolisthesis / > 50% facets removed → DECOMPRESSION + INSTRUMENTED FUSION
```

### Natural history & SPORT

**Non-op prognosis** — **most get some relief within 3 months** · > 50% non-op satisfactory at 10 yrs · **delay of surgery doesn't worsen result** · complete myelographic block responds poorly · catastrophic neuro progression unlikely

**SPORT (as-treated)** — surgery → **better pain + function (+ disability, satisfaction) at 2 and 4 yrs** · same 4-yr mortality · benefit persists at 8 yrs (some convergence)

**Reoperation** — **\~13% at 4 yrs**, \~half restenosis at same level\
✗ Wrong options: 25%

**Commonest surgical complication (SPORT)** — **dural tear**\
✗ Wrong options: infection

**ESI** — may avoid surgery (Orthobullets) · efficacy vs placebo questioned (OB) · temporary relief only → surgery

### Operative criteria

| Procedure | Indication |
|---|---|
| **Decompression alone** | Persistent pain **3–6 months** failing non-op · **progressive deficit** (weakness, bowel/bladder) · **no instability** |
| **Decompression + instrumented fusion** | **Segmental instability** (isthmic / degenerative spondylolisthesis, degenerative scoliosis) · **surgical instability** (complete laminectomy + **> 50% facets**) |
| ✗ Wrong option: fusion | Stable spine (even stable grade I listhesis elsewhere) — fusion adds nothing (Försth: same 2- and 5-yr outcomes, more time, blood, cost) |
| ✗ Wrong option: microdiscectomy | Flavum / facet stenosis (any approach) |
| ✗ Wrong option: fusion alone | Doesn't decompress |
| ✗ Wrong options: facet injection · manual therapy | Don't treat neurologic symptoms |

### Iatrogenic instability (TO)

| Destabilising (✓ needs fusion) | Not destabilising |
|---|---|
| **Bilateral inferior facet (inferior articular process) resection** | Near-total discectomy (> 50% nucleus) |
| > 50% facet removal | **Laminotomy + spinous process removal** (+ interspinous ligament) |
| — | **Medial facetectomy ≤ 1/4 (20%)** |
| — | Unilateral hemilaminectomy |

**Rule of thumb (TO)** — unilateral decompression: up to **half** of a facet · bilateral: **one third** each side

### Surgical technique

**Wide pedicle-to-pedicle decompression (L4-5)** — inferior half L4 spinous process · L4 lamina to flavum insertion · flavum · **medial facetectomy + lateral recess undercutting** · explore L4-5 + L5-S1 foramina, palpate L4 + L5 pedicles

![Pedicle-to-pedicle decompression](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob2037-decompression.jpg)

**Level matching** — single-level → **single-level laminectomy** · multilevel → **multilevel laminectomy** · stenosis at L3-4 + stable L4-5 listhesis → **L3-4 laminectomy alone**\
✗ Wrong options: L4-5 laminotomy only

**Midline-sparing** — **unilateral laminectomy undercutting the spinous process** preserves spinous process + inter/supraspinous ligaments

**Stenosis + spondylolisthesis / instability** — **bilateral laminectomies + single-level (L4-5) instrumented fusion**\
✗ Wrong options: L4-S1

**Fusion** — instrumentation controversial · PLIF/TLIF accepted, not superior · **adjacent segment degeneration > 30% at 10 yrs**

**Outcomes** — commonest cause of failure **recurrence above/below** · **comorbidities = strongest predictor of outcome**

### Prognostic factors

**Strongest predictor (stenosis surgery)** — **comorbid medical conditions**\
✗ Wrong options: smoking · multilevel · income

**Degenerative disc disease surgery** — **workers' compensation** most predictive\
✗ Wrong options: duration · severity · levels · arthroplasty

**Chronic preop opioids** — **prolonged hospital stay**, ↑ costs, harder analgesia, ↓ PROs\
✗ Wrong options: more SSI

### Fusion assessment & bone health

**Most reliable fusion assessment** — **CT** (X-ray only 60–80% accurate)

**Osteoporosis + fusion** — **teriparatide** improves fusion rates\
✗ Wrong options: denosumab · alendronate · clodronate · calcitonin

### Related concepts

**Adult deformity HRQL** — **sagittal balance** = best predictor\
✗ Wrong options: coronal · curve correction · shoulder

**SLR** — pain below knee at **30–70°**

**Far lateral disc (L3-4 → L3, quad weakness)** — **lateral paraspinal (Wiltse) approach + partial discectomy**\
✗ Wrong options: midline · fusion

## 6 Complications

| Complication | Rate / fact |
|---|---|
| **Wound infection** | **10%** (fusion) · discectomy 0.7% → **1.4% with microscope** · deep → **I&D + cultures + antibiotics** |
| Pneumonia / renal failure | 5% each |
| Persistent neuro deficit | 4% |
| Transient neuro deficit | **36%** |
| UTI / transfusion / confusion | 34% / 27% / 27% |
| Risk ↑ with | Age, blood loss, levels fused |

### Persistent leg pain after decompression

| Timing | Cause |
|---|---|
| **No improvement from day 1** | **Residual foraminal stenosis** (inadequate decompression) |
| Recurrence after pain-free interval | Recurrent herniation, instability, restenosis |
| Slow axial pain | Infection |
| Immediate saddle anaesthesia | CES (rare) |
| Decompression alone in degenerative listhesis | Instability |

### Dural tear

**Repaired watertight** — **outcome not affected** · drains safe · commoner in revision · rate 2–17%

**Persistent CSF leak** — positional headache + nausea weeks later, normal WBC/ESR → **MRI** → **reoperation = gold standard** (fibrin glue, subarachnoid drain, blood patch less invasive) · lumbar drain only after failed repairs

### Surgical site infection

**Risk factors** — **HbA1c > 7.5%** (✓), op time **> 3 h**, posterior approach, **BMI > 35** (> 30 obese), age > 70, active smoking, albumin low

**Morphine paste on dura** — **↑ SSI** (epidemic) · ✓ protective: **intrawound vancomycin**, **clipping**, **alcohol prep** · chlorhexidine wash not proven

### Lateral transpsoas fusion

| Structure | Injury result |
|---|---|
| **Genitofemoral nerve (L1-2)** — on psoas, mid-axis **L3-4**; at risk at **L4-5** | **Groin / medial thigh numbness + burning** ("liquid trickling") — **commonest lateral-approach nerve injury** |
| **Sympathetic chain** (anterolateral spine) | **Ipsilateral leg anhidrosis**, warmth (post-sympathectomy) |
| Femoral / obturator | Prolonged retraction at L4-5 |
| Ilioinguinal | Base of penis |
| Iliohypogastric | Suprapubic skin |
| Superior hypogastric plexus | Retrograde ejaculation |
