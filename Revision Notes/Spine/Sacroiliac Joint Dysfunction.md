# Sacroiliac Joint Dysfunction

*Spine · 50 / 69*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Incidence** — overlooked; **15–30% of outpatient LBP**

**TO figures** — SI joint = source of **\~25%** of LBP · **32–37% after lumbosacral fusion** · women (more translation) · pain **below L5**, non-midline, unilateral · aggravated sitting, stairs, lying on side

**Innervation (TO)** — posterior **L4–S3** dorsal rami, anterior L2–S2 · **L5 dorsal ramus** important · RFA targets **L5–S2 dorsal rami** · strongest ligament: **interosseous** · anterior SI ligament thin + nociceptive

**Risk factors** — **previous lumbar fusion, especially > 3 levels** (≈ adjacent segment disease) · **pregnancy + vaginal delivery** (3rd-trimester oestrogen/relaxin → hypermobility) · pelvic trauma · **iliac crest graft harvest** \
✗ Wrong options: age > 65 · male sex · THA · prior SI injection

### Causes

| Mechanism | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Idiopathic (commonest)** | **Repetitive trauma** → capsule/ligament tension, shear, hypo/hypermobility |
| Arthritis | **\~100% of spondyloarthropathies** (AS, Reiter's) · HLA-B27 |
| Infection | Haematogenous, **unilateral** · S. aureus, Pseudomonas, Cryptococcus, TB · IVDU, endocarditis, immunosuppression |
| Metabolic | CPPD, gout, ochronosis, hyperparathyroidism, renal osteodystrophy, acromegaly |
| Tumour | Metastasis commonest (pelvis 40% of bony mets, 2nd to spine) · primary rare: GCT, PVNS, chondrosarcoma |
| Extra-articular | Enthesopathy (posterior ligaments), **insufficiency fractures**, post-traumatic (**lateral compression** pelvic injuries) |

### Anatomy & biomechanics

**Joint** — **largest axial joint**, \~17.5 cm² · called synovial though **superior 75% non-synovial** · flat until puberty → ridges by 30 → erodes by 50 · ankylosis common in men by 50

**Ligaments** — thin anterior capsule · **posterior interosseous ligament** (posterior capsule rudimentary) · sacrotuberous (→ ischial tuberosity), sacrospinous (→ ischial spine)

**Innervation** — anterior **L2–S2 ventral rami** + sacral plexus · posterior **L4–S4 dorsal rami** (RFA targets **L5–S3 lateral branches**)

**Biomechanics** — **triplanar shock absorber** · "keystone in arch" · takes 6× medial load of lumbar spine but fails at 1/20 of its axial load · motion **< 4° rotation, 1.6 mm translation** · **nutation (nodding) = commonest motion** (backward rotation of ilium on sacrum)

## 3 Presentation

**Pain** — **just inferior to the PSIS** · only 4% above L5 · may radiate past knee · **relieved by a tight belt**

**Tenderness** — **sacral sulcus (most)** > **PSIS (2nd)**

**Neuro** — intact (pain-inhibited weakness only)

### Provocative tests

**Rule** — no single test 100% · **> 3 positive = highly suggestive**\
✗ Wrong options: 2

| Test | Technique |
|---|---|
| **FABER (Patrick)** | Flexion, abduction, ER → SI pain (groin pain = hip/iliopsoas) |
| **Fortin finger** | Patient points **twice** to area **inferomedial to PSIS** |
| **Gaenslen** | **Affected hip extended off the table**, contralateral hip + knee flexed → shear |
| SI compression | Lateral decubitus, medial force on iliac crest |
| Anterior sacral thrust | Prone, anterior force on sacrum |
| SI distraction | Supine, posterior force on ASIS |
| SLR | Usually negative; may hurt **> 60°** (SI motion) |

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Diagnostic criteria – SI injection

| Result | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **> 75% pain relief after a single injection** | **Confirms diagnosis** |
| > 50% relief after **two** injections | Diagnostic |
| Injection response | Does **not** predict pain relief after fusion |

**IASP criteria (TO)** — pain in **3 × 10 cm area inferior to PSIS** · reproduced by provocative tests · relieved by LA injection · **> 75% relief = SI origin**, 50–75% = partial · algorithm: diagnostic block → > 50–70% relief → PT + NSAIDs 4 wks → therapeutic injection → RFA / arthrodesis (after **6 months** failed care)

**Guidance** — fluoroscopy/US (blind injection reaches joint only **22%**) · max **3 in 6 months / 4 per year**

### Key radiology findings

| Study | Fact |
|---|---|
| X-ray: pelvis AP, inlet, outlet, obliques, **SI views**, lumbar flexion-extension | Narrowing, sclerosis, cysts, osteophytes, ankylosis · **25% of asymptomatic > 50 abnormal** |
| **Flamingo views** | Single-leg standing → **pelvic instability** |
| CT | Surgical planning · poorer than injection diagnostically |
| MRI | Exclude tumour, infection |
| **Bone scan** | **Specificity 90%**, sensitivity 12%, PPV 86%, NPV 72% |

**Differential (top 5)** — lumbar stenosis, DDD, hip OA, hip labral tear, disc herniation

## 5 Management

```
SI joint dysfunction
├─ 1st line → NSAIDs, PT (core), PELVIC BELT, ± prolotherapy (≥ 4 wks)
├─ 2nd line → SI CORTICOSTEROID INJECTION (60% relief at 6 months)
├─ 3rd line → RADIOFREQUENCY ABLATION (lateral sacral branches)
└─ Confirmed generator, failed non-op → SI ARTHRODESIS
       ├─ Normal anatomy → MINIMALLY INVASIVE (triangular titanium implants)
       └─ Dysmorphic sacrum / revision / aberrant anatomy → OPEN POSTERIOR
```

**Pelvic belt** — 4–8 inch belt around **greater trochanters** (medial compression) · best **after pregnancy**

**Prolotherapy** — phenol/glucose at ligament bases → scarring · best for **ligamentous laxity**

**Injection** — **lower success after lumbar fusion** · not first line

**RFA** — limited (can't denervate anterior joint)

**MIS vs open arthrodesis** — MIS: shorter stay, smaller incision, less blood loss, **earlier full weight bearing**, fewer SSIs (theory) · "fusion" by **ongrowth onto implants** · open: posterior approach, protected WB **12 wks**

## 6 Complications

| Complication | Risk |
|---|---|
| SSI | Immunocompromise, smoking, diabetes |
| Wound | **Open** (dependent wound) |
| **Nerve injury (L5, S1, S2)** | **MIS**, **sacral dysmorphism** |
| Pseudarthrosis | **Up to 5%** → open revision |

**QoL** — worse than COPD + mild heart failure; ≈ hip/knee OA

## How it's asked

**"Buttock pain after spine fusion, >75% relief with injection — risk factor?"** → **spine fusion**

**"Post-fusion SI pain, positive provocative tests — confirm with?"** → **diagnostic SI joint injection**

**"SI dysfunction after 3-level fusion — true?"** → **injection less successful after lumbar fusion**

**"SI joint pathology — exam finding?"** → **pain extending hip off table with contralateral hip/knee flexed (Gaenslen)**
