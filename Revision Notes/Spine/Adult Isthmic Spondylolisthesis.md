# Adult Isthmic Spondylolisthesis

*Spine · 2 / 69*

## 1 Basics

### Pathogenesis, prevalence & classification

**Definition** — forward slip of a vertebra on the one below due to a **pars interarticularis defect** (spondylolysis)

**Pars** — thin bicortical bridge where **lamina + inferior articular process** meet **pedicle + superior articular process**; defect = fibrocartilaginous cleft

**Prevalence** — spondylolysis **4–6%** of North American adults · 4.4% at age 6 → 6% adult (Beutler)\
✗ Wrong options: < 2% · 8–10%

**Sex** — **male** (2/3 of defects); **slip progression** commoner in **females**

**Level** — **L5-S1** **82%** (Orthobullets) · 86–90% (OB) · L4-5 11%\
✗ Wrong options: thoracolumbar junction

**Why L5-S1** — highest lumbar forces + facet orientation: "**more sagittal**" (Orthobullets) · "**more coronal**" (OB) — read the options

**Sports** — **repetitive hyperextension**: gymnasts, weightlifters, football linemen · high incidence in **Inuits** (genetic)\
✗ Wrong options: hyperflexion

**Natural history** — only **5%** of spondylolysis → spondylolisthesis · progression mostly in **adolescence**, rare after maturity\
✗ Wrong options: adults progress more

### Wiltse-Newman classification

| Type | Name | Lesion |
|---|---|---|
| **I** | **Dysplastic** | Congenital L5 arch / upper sacrum anomaly |
| **IIA** | **Isthmic – lytic** | **Pars fatigue (stress) fracture** — commonest adult |
| **IIB** | Isthmic | **Elongated intact pars** (healed stress fractures) |
| **IIC** | Isthmic | **Acute pars fracture** |
| **III** | **Degenerative** | Facet instability, **pars intact** (L4-5) |
| **IV** | **Traumatic** | Acute fracture of arch **other than pars** (e.g. pedicle) |
| **V** | **Pathologic** | Local / generalised bone disease |

## 2 Mechanism

**Foraminal stenosis (main cause of leg pain)** — **exiting L5 root** in L5-S1 foramen compressed by **hypertrophic fibrocartilage of pars defect** · **uncinate spur** of posteroinferior L5 body · bulging L5-S1 disc · facet hypertrophy

**Lateral recess stenosis** — facet arthrosis + hypertrophic ligamentum flavum

**Central stenosis = rare** — posterior arch stays behind as body slips → canal **enlarges**; only in spondyloptosis\
✗ Wrong options: "central stenosis compresses traversing root"

**Disc** — bilateral pars defect → ↑ shear across disc → early degeneration → pain

**Nutcracker vs shear (TO)** — **low PI + low SS** → L5 posterior elements **clamped between L4 and S1** in extension = **nutcracker** (low progression risk) · **high PI + high SS** → ↑ **shear** on L5 pars

## 3 Presentation

**Axial back pain** — **commonest**; long history, episodic

**Leg pain** — **L5 radiculopathy** (foraminal) → **EHL + ankle dorsiflexion weakness**, dorsum-of-foot dysaesthesia, hip abductor weakness\
✗ Wrong options: FHL · quadriceps · gastrocsoleus · iliopsoas

**Tight hamstrings** — common accompanying sign

**Neurogenic claudication / cauda equina** — **rare** (slips rarely exceed grade II)

**Root logic traps** — central L3-4 / far-lateral L4-5 HNP → **L4** · central L5-S1 HNP → **S1** (plantar flexion, lateral foot) · isthmic L3-4 → not L5

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Study | Finding |
|---|---|
| **Lateral X-ray** | Slip + pars defect (diagnosis) |
| **Oblique X-ray** | Pars defect ("Scottie dog collar") |
| **Flexion-extension** | **Instability = 4 mm translation or 10° angulation** vs adjacent segment |
| **AP X-ray** | Coronal deformity · **inverted Napoleon hat sign** = **marked anterolisthesis L5 on S1** (TO) (✗ Wrong options: retrolisthesis · facet dislocation · sacralisation) |
| **CT** | Best bony pars defect |
| **MRI – T2 parasagittal** | **Best for foraminal stenosis** / neural compression |

### Pelvic incidence & spinopelvic parameters

**Pelvic incidence (PI)** — angle between **perpendicular to S1 endplate at its midpoint** and line from **S1 endplate midpoint → femoral head centre**

**PI = PT + SS** — PT = PI − SS · SS = PI − PT

| Parameter | Measured | Type |
|---|---|---|
| **Pelvic incidence** | Perpendicular to S1 endplate vs line to hip axis | **Morphologic – fixed**, same standing/sitting/lying (TO) |
| **Pelvic tilt** | **Vertical** through hip axis vs line to S1 midpoint | Positional |
| **Sacral slope** | S1 endplate vs **horizontal** | Positional |
| **Slip angle** | L5 **inferior** endplate vs perpendicular to posterior sacral cortex | Local L5-S1 kyphosis; **> 30°** predicts progression |
| **Lumbosacral angle** | L5 **upper** endplate vs perpendicular to upper sacrum | **> 10°** predicts progression |
| Lumbar index | Anterior ÷ posterior L5 height | L5 wedging |

![PI = PT + SS](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob2038-shows-pi-pt-ss.jpg)

**PI values** — normal **55° ± 10°** (TO) · \~47° childhood → ↑ in adolescence → \~57° adult, then constant\
✗ Wrong options: "PI greater sitting than standing"

**PI correlates with** — **severity / Meyerding grade** of slip (direct linear) · determines SS, PT, **lumbar lordosis** · correlates with **sacral slope** (TO)

**Pelvic tilt (TO)** — varies with position · opens at the back · mean **13° ± 6°** standing · ↑ PT → sacral plateau becomes more **horizontal**\
✗ Wrong options: "more vertical"

**Balanced pelvis (TO)** — **high SS + low PT** · unbalanced = **low SS + high PT** (retroverted pelvis, vertical sacrum) · PT is the better indicator (SS changes secondary to PT)

**Slip progression predictor** — **slip angle** (Orthobullets, OB — most predictive of progression + surgical outcome) · **pelvic incidence** (TO answer) — read the options

**Global balance** — SVA (C7 plumb vs posterosuperior S1); **SVA > 50 mm** → worse outcome after decompression-fusion

### SDSG classification (L5-S1)

| Grade | Type | Defining feature |
|---|---|---|
| Low (< 50%) | **1 – Nutcracker** | **PI < 45°** |
| Low | 2 | PI 45–60° |
| Low | **3 – Shear** | **PI > 60°** → prone to progress |
| High (> 50%) | 4 | **Balanced pelvis** (high SS, low PT) |
| High | 5 | Retroverted pelvis, **balanced spine** (C7 plumb on/behind hips) |
| High | 6 | Retroverted pelvis, **unbalanced spine** |

**High-grade slips** — mean **PI > 60°**

## 5 Management

**First line** — activity restriction, **NSAIDs**, PT; bracing in acute phase; injections unclear · acute pars defect after fall in adult → **observe + mobilise**\
✗ Wrong options: cast · TLSO 6–8 wks · ORIF · fusion

### Operative criteria

| Indication for decompression + fusion |
|---|
| **Incapacitating pain failing 6 months** non-op (commonest) |
| **Progressive neurologic deficit** |
| Slip progression |
| Cauda equina syndrome |

```
Isthmic spondylolisthesis failing 6 months non-op
├─ Low grade (I–II) → L5-S1 DECOMPRESSION + INSTRUMENTED FUSION
│      (Gill laminectomy + foraminal decompression must be fused)
└─ High grade (III–V) → L4-S1 DECOMPRESSION + FUSION + ANTERIOR COLUMN SUPPORT
       └─ Reduction? → PARTIAL (correct slip angle)
```

### Surgery – decompression & fusion; ALIF vs TLIF

**Gill laminectomy** — removes loose lamina + scarred pars → decompresses root; **destabilising** → always fuse\
✗ Wrong options: laminectomy alone → further slip · decompression alone in high grade = poor

**Pars repair** — not if > grade I slip

**Contraindicated** — **stand-alone ALIF / stand-alone PLIF** (high failure) · **total disc replacement** (not for isthmic slip)

**Recurrent L5 radiculopathy after L5-S1 discectomy in isthmic slip** — **posterior foraminal decompression + instrumented fusion + graft**\
✗ Wrong options: revision hemilaminotomy · TDA · Gill alone

**Best technique** — **surgeon preference; outcomes similar**\
✗ Wrong options: PSF highest fusion · reduction lowest L5 injury · TLIF lowest dural tear (TLIF ↑ root injury + dural tears)

| | ALIF (+ posterior fixation) | TLIF |
|---|---|---|
| Approach | Retroperitoneal; ALL released | Posterior; facetectomy, lateral to dura |
| Decompression | **Indirect** (disc height → foraminal distraction) | **Direct** visualisation of roots |
| Disc height / segmental lordosis | **Greater** (✓) | Less |
| Fusion rate | Similar (some ↑) | Similar |
| 1-yr pain / overall complications | Similar | Similar |
| Specific risks | **Retrograde ejaculation** (superior hypogastric plexus), retroperitoneal haematoma, persistent radiculopathy, pars-defect pain fibres left behind | Dural tear, root injury |
| Use | **Low-grade** slips, even with radicular pain (87–94% good at 2 yrs) · not high grade | Any |

**PLIF vs TLIF** — PLIF cage **medial to facets** · TLIF = facetectomy, more lateral · interbody ↑ time, blood loss, stay

### Reduction – L5 nerve root injury

**Root at risk with reduction** — **L5** (exits under L5 pedicle; S1 traverses) → post-op **EHL / great-toe extension weakness**\
✗ Wrong options: S1 plantar flexion · L2–3 hip flexion · L4 patellar reflex · genitofemoral (anterior approach) · cauda equina

**Strain biomechanics** — **71% of L5 strain in the second half** of complete reduction (non-linear after > 50%) → **partial reduction** · ↑ disc height and ↑ lordosis protect L5

**Reduction goal** — correct **slip angle / lumbosacral kyphosis** first; slip % second

```
High-grade slip (SDSG)
├─ Balanced pelvis (type 4) → IN-SITU FUSION
├─ Retroverted pelvis, spine clinically balanced (type 5) → PARTIAL REDUCTION + FUSION
└─ Retroverted pelvis + sagittal imbalance / stooping (type 6) → REDUCTION MANDATORY (360°)
```

| Complication | Note |
|---|---|

**"Inverted Napoleon hat sign on AP indicates?"** → **anterolisthesis of L5 on S1** (TO)

**"Cause of this spondylolisthesis (pars fracture on CT)?"** → **fatigue fracture of pars**\
✗ Wrong options: congenital arch · degenerative · traumatic non-pars

**"Commonest level + sex for pars defect?"** → **L5-S1, male**

**"Prevalence of spondylolysis in adults?"** → **4–6%**

**"Lacrosse player, bilateral pars defect — true?"** → **foraminal stenosis from fibrocartilage**\
✗ Wrong options: central stenosis · TL junction · adults progress more · fuse first
