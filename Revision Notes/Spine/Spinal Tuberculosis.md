# Spinal Tuberculosis

*Spine · 58 / 69*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Pott's disease** — M. tuberculosis spondylitis · ↑ with immunocompromise (**HIV, CD4 50–200**)

**Frequency** — 15% of TB extrapulmonary · **spine = commonest extrapulmonary site** (thoracic) · 5% of all TB

**TO figures** — **< 1% of all TB** but **\~50% of skeletal TB** · **TB = \~70% of spinal infections** (endemic) · commonest site **thoracolumbar junction** · neuro deficit **10–43%** · **80% develop kyphosis**, 5% > 60° despite non-op · deformity: **knuckle (1 vertebra), gibbus (2), kyphus (≥ 3)**

**Level (TO)** — **lower thoracic** > lumbar > upper thoracic > cervical > sacral · children: **anterior lower thoracic** (OB)

### Pathoanatomy

**Seeding** — haematogenous to end-arterioles in the **anterior vertebral body next to the endplate (metaphysis)** · **commonest pattern: anterior** (TO key) · spreads **under the ALL** → multilevel, **skip lesions (15%)**, **paraspinal abscess (50%)** — large, anterior (≫ pyogenic)

**Disc** — **spared early** (vs pyogenic disc-first) → may mimic tumour

**Aneurysmal phenomenon (TO)** — **long-standing abscess** → concave anterior scalloping of bodies · thoracic abscess = **bird-nest shadow**

**End result in spine (TO)** — **bony ankylosis** (weight-bearing + cancellous ends) vs fibrous in limb joints

### Kyphosis

**Adults** — static after healing · mean 15° non-op; > 60° in 5%

**Children** — **progresses in 40%** after healing (growth) · **anterior + posterior element involvement = best predictor of progression**\
✗ Wrong options: age · level count · bracing timing; thoracic-only does better than thoracolumbar

| Rajasekaran progression | Course |
|---|---|
| **Type I** | ↑ until growth stops → **surgery** |
| Type II | Decreasing progression with growth |
| Type III | Minimal change |

## 3 Presentation

**Onset** — insidious · malaise, **night sweats**, weight loss · back pain often late

**Earliest (TO)** — symptom **back pain** · sign **paraspinal muscle spasm** · first neuro sign **ankle clonus** (motor before sensory — anterior disease near motor tracts), then extensor plantar

**Neuro deficit** — **10–47%** · abscess, granulation, debris, caseation, instability, **ossified flavum** near severe kyphosis · upper thoracic commonest (narrow canal) · Pott's paraplegia 10–30% (TO)

### Pott's paraplegia (TO)

| | Early onset | Late onset |
|---|---|---|
| Timing | **Active disease, < 2 yrs** | **> 2 yrs**, healed disease |
| Cause | Oedema, granulation, abscess, caseation, ischaemia | **Deformity** (internal gibbus), debris, sequestra, stenosis |
| Prognosis | **Better** | Worse |
| Treatment | ATT ± decompression | Deformity correction + stabilisation |

**Commonest cause of late-onset paraplegia** — **deformity** (TO)

**Prognosis by cause (Orthobullets)** — abscess / bony sequestra better than **meningomyelitis**

| Modified Tuli stage | Findings |
|---|---|
| 1 | Subtle — clonus, brisk reflexes, Babinski |
| 2 | UMN deficit + spasticity, **ambulatory** |
| 3 | **Bedridden**, spastic, lateral column sensory loss |
| 4 | Bedridden, severe sensory loss (posterior + lateral), sores |
| 5 | Stage 4 + **bladder/bowel**, flexor spasms, flaccidity |

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Study | Fact |
|---|---|
| **CXR** | Abnormal in **66%** — always obtain |
| X-ray early | Anterior body involvement, **disc preserved** |
| X-ray late | Collapse, disc destruction, kyphosis |
| **MRI + gadolinium** | **Preferred** · T1 low, T2 high · **septated smooth-walled para/prevertebral abscess with subligamentous spread** · multilevel 16–70% · endplate disruption (sens 100%, spec 81%), paravertebral shadow (97/85) · cord oedema, myelomalacia, syrinx |
| CT | Lesions < 1.5 cm; fragmentary, osteolytic, subperiosteal, sclerotic destruction · poor for epidural extent |
| Tc + gallium scan | Highest sensitivity for infection |

### Labs & tissue

| Test | Fact |
|---|---|
| CBC | Relative lymphocytosis, low Hb |
| ESR | Usually ↑ (normal in 25%) |
| **PPD** | **+ in \~80%** |
| **CT-guided biopsy** | **Most specific test (TO)** · **AFB** stain (smear + 52%), culture + 83% (**up to 10 wks**), **PCR** 95% sensitive |
| Yield (TO) | **Histopathology 60–90%** · **BACTEC culture 84%** · **L-J 61%** · **TB-PCR 60–75% sensitive, 94–100% specific** (detects resistance) · **30–40% remain unconfirmed** |
| ESR (TO) | **> 100 mm/h significant** · falls by **3rd month** of ATT (prognostic) · X-ray improvement by 4th month |
| Serology (TO) | Tuberculin outdated · ELISA IgM/IgG screening only |
| No | **Empirical antibiotics without biopsy** (TO) |

**Differential** — Actinomyces, Nocardia, **Brucella**, fungi (Coccidioides, Blastomyces, Cryptococcus, Aspergillus), syphilis

### Pyogenic vs TB (TO)

| Treat as pyogenic | Treat as TB |
|---|---|
| Acute, septic, severe pain | Subacute / chronic |
| **Lumbar, disc-predominant**, no big abscess | **Skip / multiple lesions** |
| Older, immunocompromised | **Paraspinal abscess** |
| — | **Thoracic / thoracolumbar** |

| MRI (TO) | Pyogenic | Tuberculous |
|---|---|---|
| Speed | Rapid, weeks | Slow |
| Disc | **Destroyed early** | **Relatively preserved early** (avascular) |
| Soft tissue | Minimal | **Large pre/paravertebral collections** |
| Contrast | Diffuse enhancement | **Rim enhancement** of abscess |

| Feature (TO) | Tuberculous | Pyogenic |
|---|---|---|
| Onset | Insidious, evening fever, weight loss | Acute, septic, high fever |
| Location | Thoracic / TL | **Lumbar** |
| Levels | **Multiple, skip lesions** | Single level |
| Abscess wall | **Thin, smooth** | **Thick, irregular** |
| First target | Vertebral body | **Disc space** |

**MRI forms of TB (TO)** — vertebral: **paradiscal (commonest), anterior, central**; atypical solitary, multifocal, posterior element · non-vertebral: epidural, leptomeningeal, intramedullary · **isolated epidural TB** with normal body + disc possible

**Infection vs tumour (TO)** — infection: disc + bodies + collections · tumour (myeloma, lymphoma, metastasis): **disc spared**, solid mass

## 5 Management

### Medical

**Regimen** — **HRZE 2 months → HR 9–18 months** (Orthobullets) · **HRZE 2 months → HRE ≥ 10 months, total 12–16 months, daily** (TO, India) · with surgery: continue H, R, Z

**HIV + cervical Pott's with Ziehl-Neelsen +** — **isoniazid, rifampin, pyrazinamide, streptomycin**

**Orthosis** — pain + deformity prevention · **steroids only for arachnoiditis/meningitis** (TO)

**ATT (TO lecture)** — **2HRZE + 7HRE = 9 months minimum** · DOTS · bed rest 2–3 wks → brace → mobilise · MDR (INH + RIF resistant): culture-guided, 5 drugs (incl. moxifloxacin, cycloserine) \~18 months · XDR → debridement + salvage regimen

### Operative criteria

| Indication | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Neuro deficit** | Worsening deficit, acute severe paraplegia, **not responding to ≥ 3 wks ATT** (TO) |
| Instability | Panvertebral disease ± subluxation · **Ganga score > 3**, **≥ 2 spine-at-risk signs** (TO) |
| **Kyphosis** | **> 60° adults** (Orthobullets) · **thoracic > 40°, lumbar > 30°** (TO) · **progressive in a child** · ≥ 3 vertebrae with loss of ≥ 1.5 bodies (thoracic) · child ≤ 7 with ≥ 3 bodies + ≥ 2 at-risk signs |
| Late-onset paraplegia | Kyphosis correction |
| Others | **Caseation** blocking drugs, failure after 3–6 months, uncertain diagnosis, > 4 levels (TO), large abscess |

**Benefits** — less kyphosis, faster healing, fewer sinuses, better neuro recovery · implant colonisation rarer than pyogenic · autograft or allograft struts fine

### Approach

| Situation | Approach |
|---|---|
| Classic | **Anterior decompression / corpectomy + strut ± posterior instrumentation ± posterior shortening** (single-stage transpedicular, two-stage, extrapleural anterolateral) |
| **TO modern** | **Posterior / transpedicular** preferred (lung already involved; diseased body poor for screws) · **posterior abscess (e.g. D6-7) → posterior laminectomy** · lumbar: anterior abscess drainable via laminectomy (dura retractable) |
| **TO progressive deficit, T7-9, no paravertebral collection** | **Posterior decompression + stabilisation** |
| **TO worsening paraplegia + bladder after 3 wks ATT** | **Decompression + evaluate for MDR-TB** (✗ Wrong options: add streptomycin · steroids · repeat MRI) |
| Cervical kyphosis | Halo traction → anterior decompression, graft, plate |
| Lumbar kyphosis | **Pedicle subtraction osteotomy** |
| Healed thoracic/TL kyphosis | **Internal kyphectomy** (cord moved anteriorly) |
| Transthoracic | **Left side** (aorta easier than IVC) |
| Atypical TB (granuloma) | Laminectomy (extradural/subdural) · myelotomy (intramedullary) |

| Approach (TO) | Use |
|---|---|
| **Transpedicular** | Single level, posterior debridement, little deformity correction |
| **Costotransversectomy** | 1–2 levels, mild deformity → anterolateral decompression + cage |
| **Extracavitary** | Multilevel collapse / severe kyphosis (2–3 ribs removed) |
| TLIF | Lumbar discitis — debridement + cage |
| Anterior cervical | Cervical TB — corpectomy + graft + plate |

**Rajasekaran (TO)** — active disease: posterior stabilisation then anterior debridement + graft · healed rigid deformity: anterior first then posterior

### Drug-resistant TB (TO)

**Rif-resistant on Xpert** — next **second-line LPA / DST for fluoroquinolone resistance** (MDR vs pre-XDR) · XDR work-up: SL-LPA + **gene sequencing** (Bdq/Lzd)

| WHO 2022 group | Drugs |
|---|---|
| **A (all three)** | **Levofloxacin/moxifloxacin, bedaquiline, linezolid** |
| B | Clofazimine, cycloserine/terizidone |
| C | Ethambutol, delamanid, pyrazinamide, amikacin/streptomycin, ethionamide |

**Regimens** — shorter all-oral **9–12 months** (FQ-sensitive) · longer **18–20 months** · **BPaLM** 6 months · injectables last resort · never add a single drug to a failing regimen

**XDR (2021)** — MDR + FQ resistance + bedaquiline or linezolid resistance

**Monitoring** — **bedaquiline → QTc (ECG)** · **linezolid → myelosuppression (CBC)**

## 6 Complications

**Deformity** — highest after **anterior graft alone** (graft slips/breaks, ≥ 2 levels) · lowest with **anterior + posterior fusion**

**Others** — retropharyngeal abscess (dysphagia, hoarseness), TB arteritis/pseudoaneurysm, costopelvic impingement, **sinus formation**, Pott's paraplegia

**Atypical spinal TB** — compressive myelopathy **without visible deformity** · intraspinal granuloma, neural arch disease, concentric collapse, sclerotic bridging

**Spina ventosa (TO)** — **tubercular dactylitis**: phalanges of the hand, destruction + new bone, **spindle-shaped finger swelling**
