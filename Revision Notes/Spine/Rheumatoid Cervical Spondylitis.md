# Rheumatoid Cervical Spondylitis

*Spine · 49 / 69*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Prevalence** — cervical involvement in **90% of RA** (often missed) · cervical spine = **2nd commonest RA site** after peripheral joints (TO) · **C1-C2 (synovial joint) = commonest segment** (TO)

**Risk factors (TO)** — older age at onset, active synovitis, **↑ CRP**, rapidly erosive peripheral disease, early joint subluxation

**DMARDs** — have **↓ cervical surgery** rates

### Three patterns

| Pattern | Frequency (Orthobullets) | Frequency (TO) |
|---|---|---|
| **Atlantoaxial subluxation (commonest)** | **50–80%** | **19–70%** |
| **Basilar invagination / cranial settling** | 40% | 38% |
| **Subaxial subluxation** | 20% | 7–29% |

**Others (TO)** — inflammatory discitis, **atraumatic odontoid erosion / fracture**

### Genes & lab markers

**Anti-CCP** — **most sensitive + specific RA marker** (✓ for C2 pannus)\
✗ Wrong options: CRP/WBC (non-specific) · HLA-B27 (AS) · M-spike (myeloma)

## 3 Presentation

**Symptoms** — **neck pain** (craniocervical, 69%), stiffness · **occipital headache** (**lesser / greater occipital nerve, C2**), mastoid pain · **myelopathy** (hand clumsiness, gait instability, hyperreflexia) · vertebrobasilar: tinnitus, vertigo, visual change, dysphagia (TO)

**C1-2 facet arthrosis / O-C1 ankylosis** — **axial neck pain + ↓ rotation** with occipital headache\
✗ Wrong options: Spurling · Hoffmann · Lhermitte

### Ranawat classification (neurologic)

| Class | Findings |
|---|---|
| I | Pain, no deficit |
| II | **Subjective** weakness, hyperreflexia, dysaesthesia |
| IIIA | **Objective** weakness + UMN signs, **ambulatory** |
| **IIIB** | Objective weakness + UMN, **non-ambulatory** → **do NOT operate** (poor results) |

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

**Flexion-extension X-rays** — **always before elective surgery** (anaesthesia)

**CT** — bony anatomy, **vertebral artery** course before transarticular screws

**MRI** — **cord compression + myelomalacia** (study of choice) · **cervicomedullary angle**

### Atlantoaxial subluxation – diagnostic criteria

| Measure | Threshold |
|---|---|
| **AADI normal** | **< 3 mm** adult (< 5 mm child) |
| **Instability** | **> 3.5 mm** change flexion↔extension (Orthobullets) · AADI > 3 mm not fixed (TO) — **not alone an indication for surgery** |
| > 7 mm motion | Alar ligament disruption |
| **AADI surgery** | **> 10 mm** (Orthobullets, OB) · 6–10 mm or > 8 mm cited (TO) |
| **PADI / SAC surgery** | **< 14 mm** (Orthobullets, OB) · **< 13–14 mm** (TO) · PADI < 14 mm **97% sensitive** for deficit |
| **PADI predicting recovery** | **> 13 mm** (Orthobullets, OB, TO) · **> 14 mm** (OB) — read the options |

**Best predictor of neuro deficit** — **PADI** · cord 10 mm + 1 mm dura + 1 mm CSF each side → PADI ≥ 14 mm (TO)\
✗ Wrong options: AADI

**Mechanism** — **pannus** between dens and C1 ring → destroys **transverse ligament + dens** · usually **anterior** C1 on C2

### Basilar invagination – lines

| Line | Landmarks | Abnormal |
|---|---|---|
| **Ranawat C1-2 index** | **Centre of C2 pedicle → C1 arch line** | Normal **17 mm men / 15 mm women** · **< 13 mm = impaction** (Orthobullets) · **< 15 mm in men** (TO) · **most reproducible** |
| **McGregor** | **Posterior hard palate → most caudal occiput** | Dens tip **> 4.5 mm above** · **routine screening line** (TO) |
| **Chamberlain** | Dorsal hard palate → **posterior foramen magnum (opisthion)** | Dens **> 5 mm above** · hard to see |
| **McRae** | **Foramen magnum** opening (basion → opisthion) | Dens below = no impaction (asymptomatic) |
| Fischgold-Metzger | **AP view** — digastric grooves | Normally 10.7 mm above dens tip (TO) |

**Best combination for cranial settling (TO)** — **Clark station + Ranawat + Redlund-Johnell**

**MRI** — **cervicomedullary angle < 135°** → impending deficit → surgery

### Subaxial subluxation

**Features** — multilevel, with upper instability · ↑ with **steroids, males, seropositive, nodules, severe RA** · pannus + facet/Luschka laxity

**Cord compression** — slip **> 4 mm or > 20%** · **cervical height index (body height ÷ width) < 2.0** → \~100% sensitive + specific for deficit

## 5 Management

**Goal** — prevent progression; **may not reverse existing deficit** · pharmacotherapy first

### Operative criteria

| Pattern | Indication | Operation |
|---|---|---|
| **AAS** | **AADI > 10 mm** or **PADI < 14 mm** (even without deficit), **progressive myelopathy** · not 3.5 mm change alone | **Posterior C1-2 fusion** (reducible; **transarticular screws** avoid halo; CT for VA) |
| AAS + **basilar invagination** / irreducible AAS | — | **Occiput–C2 fusion ± C1 posterior arch resection** |
| **Basilar invagination** | **Progressive migration > 5 mm**, deficit, **CMA < 135°** | **Occiput–C2 fusion** · transoral / retropharyngeal odontoid resection for **brainstem compromise** |
| **Subaxial** | **> 4 mm / > 20% slip + intractable pain + neuro** | Posterior fusion + wiring |

**Pannus** — **resolves after posterior fusion** (stability) → **posterior fusion ± decompression** for periodontoid pannus myelopathy · **odontoidectomy** only for residual anterior compression after fusion\
✗ Wrong options: transoral resection first (CSF leak, wound, more morbidity)

**TO basilar impression** — surgery by **symptoms, not degree** · anterior impingement → **occiput-C1-2 fusion in extension** ± later anterior odontoid excision · posterior impingement → suboccipital craniectomy + C1 (± C2) laminectomy

## 6 Complications

**Failure to improve** — **Ranawat IIIB**

**Pseudarthrosis** — **10–20%** · ↓ by **extending to occiput**

**Adjacent level degeneration**
