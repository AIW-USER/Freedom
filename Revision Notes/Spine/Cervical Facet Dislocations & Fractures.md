# Cervical Facet Dislocations & Fractures

*Spine · 13 / 69*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Young** — high-energy (MVC, motorcycle, contact sport) · **elderly** — low-energy

**C7 / C7-T1** — **17%** of injuries → always visualise cervicothoracic junction

**Associated** — head injury, **non-contiguous spine injuries** (TL, CT, OC junctions), limb injuries

### Pathoanatomy

| Injury | Key facts |
|---|---|
| **Facet fracture** | **Superior facet** more often; ↓ threshold for dislocation (loses interlock); may be a **spontaneously reduced dislocation** |
| **Unilateral facet dislocation** | **Most frequently missed** C-spine injury on X-ray · **\~25%** subluxation · **monoradiculopathy** that improves with traction (inferior facet of cephalad vertebra in foramen) · rotation component |
| **Bilateral facet dislocation** | **\~50%** subluxation · **SCI in \~80%** |

**Mechanism** — **flexion-distraction** ± rotation → posterior structures torn (ISL, capsule, LF, posterior annulus), **ALL usually preserved**\
✗ Wrong options: flexion-compression · vertical compression · extension

**PLL / posterior annulus disruption** — **40%** unilateral · **80%** bilateral

## 2 Mechanism

### Allen & Ferguson classification (research)

| Group | |
|---|---|
| 1 Flexion-compression | — |
| 2 Vertical compression | — |
| **3 Flexion-distraction** | **Stage 1** facet sprain, < 10° kyphosis · **Stage 2 unilateral dislocation** · **Stage 3 bilateral, 50% (perched)** · **Stage 4 complete (100%)** |
| 4 Extension-compression | — |
| 5 Extension-distraction | — |
| 6 Lateral flexion | — |

**Clinical (descriptive)** — compression, burst, flexion-distraction, unilateral/bilateral dislocation, facet fracture

## 3 Presentation

**Unilateral** — single-arm paraesthesia: **C5-6 → C6** (wrist extension weakness, thumb numbness) · **C6-7 → C7** (triceps + wrist flexion weakness, index + middle finger numbness) · angular deformity

**Bilateral** — bilateral UL + LL weakness, sensory change → **ASIA** documentation

**History** — AS / DISH, prior fusion · scalp lacerations → head injury

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Study | Findings / indication |
|---|---|
| **X-ray** (AP, lateral, oblique, open mouth) | Subluxation (25% unilateral, 50% bilateral), disc height loss (retropulsed disc), widened interspinous distance, focal hypolordosis, soft-tissue swelling |
| **Flexion-extension** | **Whenever a facet fracture is seen** (spontaneous reduction / occult instability) |
| **CT** | Most cases; subtle facet subluxation, facet/pedicle/lamina fractures |
| **MRI** | **Before any open reduction / surgery**; **obtunded** patient (emergent); **failed closed reduction**; **neuro worsening during reduction** — looks for **disc herniation**, PLC injury, cord compression; **cord haematoma = poor motor prognosis** |

**MRI timing (TO)** — best sensitivity for haemorrhage/oedema **within 48 h**

**Differential** — **lateral mass fracture-separation** → needs **2-level** fusion (dislocation = 1 level)

## 5 Management

### Operative criteria – facet fractures

| Stable (non-op 6–12 wks, collar / halo) | Unstable (single-level instrumented fusion) |
|---|---|
| Reduced unilateral fracture, **no instability on flex-ex** | **Bilateral** facet fractures |
| **< 40% of lateral mass** or **< 1 cm** height | **> 40% of lateral mass** or **> 1 cm** height |

**Non-op risk** — **> 30% re-subluxation / redislocation**, persistent pain

**Halo** — suboptimal in lower C-spine; **most likely to fail with facet fracture-dislocation** · hard orthosis often satisfactory\
✗ Wrong options: C1 lateral mass · C2 pars · burst

### Closed reduction vs MRI first

```
Facet dislocation
├─ Awake, alert, cooperative → EMERGENT CLOSED REDUCTION (Gardner-Wells)
│      → MRI after reduction → URGENT STABILISATION (< 24 h)
├─ Obtunded / intoxicated / intubated / GCS ↓ → EMERGENT MRI FIRST
│      → OPEN REDUCTION + STABILISATION
└─ Failed closed reduction or neuro worsening → STOP → MRI → OPEN REDUCTION
```

**Awake patient — source conflict** — **immediate closed traction** (Orthobullets, OB — especially with deteriorating neuro, ASIA E → D) · **hard collar + MRI first** (TO answer for awake, intact patient 30 min post-injury) — read the options

**Pre-reduction MRI** — not routine in awake patients (delays reduction, early reduction ↑ recovery)

**MRI finding that changes plan** — **herniated disc at dislocated level** → **anterior discectomy before/with reduction**\
✗ Wrong options: cord oedema · PLC disruption · lamina fracture · ALL signal

| Reduction fact | Value |
|---|---|
| Unilateral | **Harder** to reduce, **more stable** after; closed success \~25% |
| Bilateral | **Easier** (PLL torn), **less stable** after |
| Fail closed reduction | **26%** |
| Never | Closed reduction with **altered mental status** |

### Closed reduction technique

**Gardner-Wells tongs** — **1 cm above pinna**, in line with **EAM**, **below skull equator** · **posterior pin placement** → flexion moment

**Weights** — 5–10 lb increments · up to **140 lb or 70% body weight** · \~**9.4–9.8 lb per level above injury** · carbon-fibre tongs for MRI (\~80 lb limit)

**After reduction** — drop to **10–15 lb**, add **extension** (pulley / thoracic pad)

**Abort** — neuro worsening (→ immediate MRI) · **overdistraction > 1.5× adjacent disc space**

**Requirements** — sedation, respiratory supervision, serial cross-table laterals + neuro exams

### Surgical options

| Procedure | Indication / technique |
|---|---|
| **ACDF (single level, plate)** | Disc herniation compressing cord after reduction; anterior open reduction of **unilateral** dislocation — Caspar pin distraction + **rotate cephalad pin toward dislocated side** |
| Anterior reduction of bilateral | Converging Caspar pins (10–20°) compressed to unlock; curette pushes rostral body back · **lamina spreaders not effective for bilateral** |
| **Posterior reduction + lateral mass screws** | No disc herniation; irreducible from front/closed; Penfield 4 lever, remove tip of caudal superior facet; often **2 levels** (poor purchase) |
| **Anterior → posterior** | Disc needs removal but can't reduce anteriorly: discectomy, plate fixed to **superior body only** (stops graft kick-out, allows rotation) |

**Biomechanics** — posterior fixation > anterior plating for flexion-distraction (Sutterlin, Coe) · anterior overdistraction risks cord + hardware

## 6 Complications

| Complication | Fact |
|---|---|
| SSI | ↑ posterior approach |
| **Redislocation** | Unilateral treated in orthosis → ACDF |
| Respiratory | ARDS, pneumonia, tracheostomy |
| **Vertebral artery** | Up to **11%**; ↑ with lateral mass / transverse process involvement; often unrecognised |
| Oesophageal | Anterior reduction → primary repair |
| Pin-tract | Halo; meningitis if inner table breached |

**Prognosis** — worse with severe deficit, older age, **cord haematoma** · **bilateral dislocation = worst outcome** among subaxial patterns

## X vs Y

| | Unilateral | Bilateral |
|---|---|---|
| Subluxation | **25%** | **50%** |
| Neuro | **Radiculopathy** | **SCI \~80%** |
| PLL torn | 40% | 80% |
| Reduce | Harder, stable after | Easier, unstable after |
| X-ray | Most missed | Obvious |

## How it's asked

**"Characteristic mechanism of facet dislocation?"** → **flexion-distraction**

**"Right C6-7 facet dislocation — which CT image?"** → **unilateral right perched/jumped facet**

**"Awake, alert, 30 min post-injury, C4-5 jumped facets — next (TO)?"** → **hard collar + MRI** (TO) · OB awake-patient stems → **immediate closed reduction** — read the options

**"Awake, cooperative, ASIA E → D after CT?"** → **immediate closed reduction with traction**\
✗ Wrong options: MRI first · steroids

**"GCS 3, intubated, bilateral C5-6 dislocation?"** → **MRI**

**"Intubated, moving limbs, bilateral C6-7?"** → **cervical MRI then reduction**

**"Intoxicated, obtunded, bilateral C4-5?"** → **immediate MRI then surgery**

**"MRI finding mandating surgery instead of closed reduction?"** → **herniated disc at dislocated level**

**"GCS 10, C7-T1 fracture-dislocation?"** → **immediate MRI**

**"Altered mental status — MRI finding that changes approach?"** → **herniated disc (→ anterior)**

**"Nondisplaced C6 facet fracture, stable on flex-ex?"** → **hard collar**

**"Halo most likely to fail?"** → **C6-7 facet fracture-dislocation**

**"Worst outcome with incomplete SCI?"** → **bilateral facet dislocation**
