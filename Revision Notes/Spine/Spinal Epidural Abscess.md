# Spinal Epidural Abscess

*Spine · 57 / 69*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Definition** — pus / granulation tissue between **dura and epidural fat**

**Patients** — usually **> 60 yrs** · 2–10 per 10,000 admissions (TO) · location usually **dorsal thoracolumbar** · cervical SEA commoner in **IVDU injecting arms/neck** (TO)

**Risk factors** — **IVDU**, immunodeficiency, HIV, malignancy, immunosuppressants, **recent spinal procedure**, diabetes, endocarditis

### Pathogenesis

**Routes** — **haematogenous \~50%** (commonest non-iatrogenic) · contiguous discitis \~33% · direct inoculation

**Nidus (TO)** — **skin** commonest\
✗ Wrong options: respiratory

**Organisms** — **Staph aureus 50–65%** · gram-negatives (E. coli 18%) · **Pseudomonas in IVDU**

**Association** — **spondylodiscitis** (SEA in \~18% of spondylodiscitis)

**Neurologic** — deficit in **33%** · permanent paralysis **4–22%** (compression or cord infarction) · risk highest cervical, lowest lumbar (TO)

## 3 Presentation

**Symptoms** — **severe insidious pain (87%)** · **fever only \~50%** · sicker than osteomyelitis · **rapid neuro progression (days)** (TO) · meningism, radicular pain · sequence: back ache → root pain → weakness → paralysis

**Exam** — radiculopathy or myelopathy (clonus, Hoffmann → look above)

### Labs

| Marker | Fact |
|---|---|
| WBC | Mean 22,000; raised in only **\~42%** |
| **ESR** | Raised **> 90%** |
| **CRP** | Raised **90%** |

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Study | Fact |
|---|---|
| X-ray | Usually normal |
| CT | Poor sensitivity |
| CT myelogram | 90% sensitive, invasive |
| **MRI + gadolinium** | **Study of choice** · **ring-enhancing lesion = pathognomonic**, T2 bright · pus vs CSF · extent, osteomyelitis, compression |

### Whole-spine MRI (skip lesions)

| Indication |
|---|
| Concomitant infection outside the spine |
| **Delayed presentation > 7 days** (strongest predictor among listed options ; ✗ Wrong options: IVDU · diabetes · CRP · fever) |
| **ESR > 95 mm/h** |
| **Neuro signs not explained by the lesion found** (e.g. Hoffmann with lumbar SEA; clonus with L2-3 discitis) |

## 5 Management

```
Spinal epidural abscess
├─ Neuro intact, small abscess, minimal compression, can be observed → ADMIT, IV ANTIBIOTICS ± BRACE
│      (also non-surgical candidates)
└─ Deficit / cord compression / failure risk / persistent infection / instability → SURGICAL DECOMPRESSION ± FUSION
       ├─ Posterior abscess, no discitis → LAMINECTOMY (avoid wide facetectomy)
       └─ Anterior abscess / spondylodiscitis → ANTERIOR DEBRIDEMENT + STRUT ± POSTERIOR FIXATION
```

### Failure of medical treatment

| Predictor | Note |
|---|---|
| **Neurologic deficit** | **Strongest predictor** |
| **Diabetes** | — |
| **CRP > 115 mg/L** | — |
| **WBC > 12–12.5 k** | — |
| **Positive blood cultures** | — |
| **Age > 65** | — |
| **MRSA** | — |
| No | Smoking, IVDU |

**> 40%** of medically managed patients fail (pain, deficit)

**Neuro-intact 53-yr, MSSA bacteraemia** — **admit, brace, IV cefazolin 6 wks** · serial exams\
✗ Wrong options: discharge on oral linezolid · laminectomy

**Most likely to need surgery** — **67-yr diabetic, leg weakness, MRSA bacteraemia** (all risk factors)

### Operative

**Indications** — **neuro deficit**, cord compression on imaging, failure risk factors (> 65, diabetes, new deficit, MRSA), persistent infection, deformity/instability

**Antibiotics after surgery** — **2–4 wks if no bone involvement** · **6 wks if bony involvement**

**Cervical anterior abscess + deficit (IVDU)** — **anterior decompression + fusion**\
✗ Wrong options: posterior · steroids · antibiotics alone

**Cervical spondylodiscitis C5-7 + kyphosis + deficit + sepsis** — **anterior debridement, corpectomy, fusion + posterior instrumentation** (instrument despite infection)

**Lumbar 3-level destruction + abscess after endocarditis** — **I&D, corpectomy, fusion**

**Antibiotic timing** — hold until intraoperative cultures if stable (OB 213125)

### Prognosis

**Best predictor** — **pre-operative neuro deficit** · mortality \~5% · early diagnosis key · only 18% with frank abscess / 23% with paralysis fully recover

### Intracranial infections (TO)

**Brain abscess** — **congenital cyanotic heart disease**, endocarditis, lung, sinusitis (**frontal/ethmoid → frontal lobe**), otitis, trauma · overall **Streptococcus**; trauma S. aureus; transplant fungi; post-neurosurgery S. epidermidis/aureus · stages: early cerebritis 1–3 d → late cerebritis 4–9 d → early capsule 10–13 d → late capsule > 14 d · IV antibiotics 6–8 wks then oral · medical alone if **< 3 cm, cerebritis stage, < 2 wks** symptoms

**Neurocysticercosis** — **Taenia solium** larvae (pork / contaminated vegetables) · **commonest CNS parasite**, commonest acquired epilepsy in low-income countries · C. cellulosae: 5–15 mm transparent cyst with **scolex** · stages: vesicular → colloidal → granular nodular → calcific · **seizures** commonest · **EITB serology** \~100% specific · **MRI choice** (scolex pathognomonic) · treatment: **albendazole** + steroids (start before) + antiepileptics ± surgery/shunt

### Pneumorrachis (TO)

**Definition** — **air in the spinal canal** (traumatic, iatrogenic, spontaneous) · mostly cervical · from pneumothorax/pneumomediastinum tracking via retropharynx

**Gas from necrotising fasciitis / emphysematous osteomyelitis** — hypointense epidural lesion · CT best (can't separate air from gas) · usually resolves conservatively

## How it's asked

**"53-yr neuro-intact SEA, MSSA?"** → **admission, bracing, IV cefazolin 6 wks**

**"Most likely to need surgery?"** → **67-yr diabetic, leg weakness, MRSA bacteraemia**

**"Strongest predictor of failure in IVDU with weakness?"** → **lower extremity weakness**

**"Lumbar SEA + positive Hoffmann — next?"** → **MRI cervical + thoracic spine**

**"IVDU, diffuse weakness, anterior cervical abscess?"** → **anterior decompression + fusion**

**"Post-endocarditis, 3-level lumbar destruction, weakness?"** → **I&D, corpectomy, fusion**

**"C5-7 spondylodiscitis, kyphosis, deficit, sepsis?"** → **anterior debridement, corpectomy, fusion + posterior instrumentation**

**"Cervical anterior abscess, 1 month fevers, weakness?"** → **anterior decompression + fusion**

**"SEA — EXCEPT?"** → **commonest nidus is respiratory infection** (TO — it's skin)

**"Post-endocarditis back pain, clonus — imaging?"** → **thoracic ring-enhancing epidural collection (Figure B)**

**"L2-3 discitis, neuro signs above — next?"** → **MRI of the entire spine**

**"Factor predicting skip lesions?"** → **duration of symptoms (≥ 7 days)**

**"Large fronto-parietal lesion + congenital heart disease?"** → **brain abscess** (TO)

**"Multiple white transparent cysts?"** → **neurocysticercosis** (TO)

**"Necrotising fasciitis, hypointense epidural lesion?"** → **pneumorrachis** (TO)
