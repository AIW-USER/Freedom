# Vertebral Artery Injury

*Spine · 69 / 69*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

| Setting | Rate |
|---|---|
| All trauma | **0.5%** · 70% of blunt VAI have a cervical fracture |
| Cervical spine fractures | **33–39%** |
| Anterior cervical surgery | 0.3% |
| **Posterior cervical surgery** | **4.1–8.2% (C1-2 transarticular screws)** |

### Anatomy

| Segment | Course | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| **V1** | **Subclavian** → anterior to C7 TP → enters **C6 transverse foramen** | **Highest blunt injury risk at C6 entry** |
| **V2** | Foramina **C6 → C1** | **Most traumatic injuries**; lateral mass/pedicle screw drilling |
| **V3** | Over C1 arch → foramen magnum | C1 lateral exposure, laminectomy, caudal-lateral **transarticular screws** (2nd highest risk at C1-2) |
| V4 | Intradural → joins opposite VA → **basilar** | — |

**Anomalies** — 2.7% in anterior surgery (foramen medial to / within 1.5 mm of uncovertebral joint) · **2.3–20% at C1-2**: **high-riding C2 foramen**, C2 pedicle erosion, thin lateral mass → CTA before transarticular screws

### Safe zones

**C1 posterior dissection** — stay **< 1.5 cm lateral to midline above the C1 arch** (Orthobullets, OB) · < 1 cm (OC fusion chapter) · retraction **> 2 cm** past midline injures VA (OB) — read the options · dissect **subperiosteally on the inferior arch**

**C1 lateral mass screw** — **10° medial, 22° cephalad** → **inferior + medial to the VA** · C2 root crossed first (some sacrifice it)

## 3 Presentation

**History** — C1/C2 fractures, **subaxial facet fractures/dislocations**, cervical/CVJ surgery

**Vertebrobasilar insufficiency** — dizziness, vertigo, nausea, **diplopia**, blindness, **ataxia**, bilateral weakness, **dysphagia** · onset variable (days–years); many asymptomatic

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Study | Fact |
|---|---|
| X-ray | Fracture patterns raise suspicion |
| **CT angiography** | **Sensitivity \~100%** · screening test |
| MRA | 93.9% · deficits from VA/basilar ischaemia |
| **Intra-op / post-op angiography** | Collaterals before ligation, fistula/pseudoaneurysm embolisation |

### Diagnostic criteria – CTA screening (any one)

| Indication |
|---|
| **C1, C2 or C3 fracture** · **fracture into transverse foramen** (≥ 1 mm displacement) · subluxation (VAI in 20%) |
| **Cervical SCI** · **hanging injury** · occipitocervical dissociation, basilar skull fracture |
| Unexplained central/lateralising deficit · acute infarct on CT · **GCS < 9** · diffuse axonal injury |
| Le Fort II/III or facial fracture · major thoracic injury / **1st rib fracture** |

**C1 floating lateral mass + visual change, nausea, dysphagia, dizziness** — **CT angiogram first**\
✗ Wrong options: traction → may be lethal

### Biffl grading

| Grade | Lesion |
|---|---|
| I | Dissection / intramural haematoma **< 25% stenosis** |
| II | Thrombus, intimal flap, **> 25% stenosis** |
| III | **Pseudoaneurysm** |
| IV | **Occlusion** |
| V | **Transection** |

## 5 Management

```
Vertebral artery injury
├─ Traumatic → ANTICOAGULATION / ANTIPLATELET (aspirin first line, heparin)
│      (CI: major intracranial infarct, intraspinal haematoma/AVF) · stabilise fracture as needed
└─ Intra-operative bleeding → TAMPONADE (haemostatic agents, bone wax, insert the screw)
       ├─ Controlled → consider DIRECT MICROVASCULAR REPAIR
       └─ Uncontrolled + adequate collaterals → ENDOVASCULAR EMBOLISATION / clip / ligation
```

**Tamponade risks** — delayed haemorrhage, fistula

**Ligation** — angiogram first (collaterals; left VA usually dominant) · risks **cerebellar infarct (PICA)**, cranial nerve palsy, hemiplegia

**Complex right VA injury during O-C3 fusion, tamponade failed, segmental defect, good collaterals** — **embolisation**\
✗ Wrong options: antifibrinolytic · direct repair · graft

**Post-op** — observe or immediate angiography (fistula, pseudoaneurysm, collaterals)

## 6 Complications

**Late** — AV fistula, delayed haemorrhage, **pseudoaneurysm**, thromboembolism, **stroke** (persistent vertigo), death

### Cerebrovascular (TO)

| Topic | Fact |
|---|---|
| **SAH work-up** | NCCT → LP if negative · CTA first for source · **DSA = gold standard** (finds source in 80–85%) |
| Aneurysm sites | Carotid system 85–95%: **ACoA (commonest, 30%)**, PCoA 25%, MCA 20% · posterior 5–15% (basilar tip commonest) · multiple in 20–30% |
| **PCoA aneurysm** | **3rd nerve palsy (ptosis, dilated pupil)** in \~9% · new CN III palsy = impending rupture |
| ISUIA | **> 10 mm → \~5%/yr rupture** · 7–12 mm 2.6%, 13–24 mm 14.5%, ≥ 25 mm 40% (5-yr) · anterior commoner, posterior ruptures more |
| **AVM** | No capillary bed, **no intervening parenchyma** in nidus · 2–4%/yr bleed · **small AVMs bleed more** (higher feeder pressure) · death 1%/yr · **rebleed 6–18% in yr 1, evenly spread** (✗ Wrong options: highest in first 2 wks) · treatment: embolisation, stereotactic radiosurgery, surgery — **all** |
| Spetzler-Martin | Size < 3 / 3–6 / > 6 cm = 1/2/3 · eloquent 1 · deep drainage 1 |
| **Cavernoma** | **No intervening brain parenchyma** |
| **Anterior choroidal occlusion** | Contralateral hemiplegia, hemihypaesthesia, homonymous hemianopia · cognition spared |
| Carotid endarterectomy | Symptomatic **50–99%**, asymptomatic 60–99% → **endarterectomy** (less stroke than stenting, more MI) · clamp release **ECA → CCA → ICA** |
| **Subclavian steal** | **Left subclavian occluded proximal to the vertebral origin** |
| ICA : ECA flow | **80 : 20** |
| Malignant MCA infarct + haemorrhagic transformation | **Decompressive craniectomy** |

### Spinal vascular malformations (TO)

| Type | Fact |
|---|---|
| **I — dural AVF** | **Commonest**, **acquired**, slow progressive myelopathy from **venous congestion + steal** (✗ Wrong options: recurrent bleeding) · treat by **ligating/embolising the draining (leptomeningeal) vein** · 15–20% other AVMs |
| **II — glomus** | **Intramedullary** nidus (congenital) |
| III — juvenile | Intramedullary-extramedullary, high flow, SAH risk |
| IV | Perimedullary (intradural extramedullary) fistula |

**Aggressiveness** — **leptomeningeal vein** (fistulous) determines outcome\
✗ Wrong options: location · symptoms

**Unkeyed TO items** — SAH CT appearance (classic: hyperdensity in basal cisterns + sulci) and basilar tip aneurysm treatment (coiling usual) — answers not provided by source

## How it's asked

**"C1 lateral mass screw lies relative to VA?"** → **inferior + medial**

**"VA injured during C1 dissection — where?"** → **cephalad to C1 arch > 1.5 cm lateral to midline**

**"Structure injured > 2 cm lateral on atlas arch?"** → **vertebral artery — from subclavian through transverse foramina**

**"Uncontrolled VA bleeding, segmental defect, good collaterals?"** → **embolisation**

**"C1 floating lateral mass + VBI symptoms — next?"** → **CT angiogram**

**"AVM without intervening parenchyma?"** → **cavernous haemangioma** (TO)

**"Clamp removal after CEA?"** → **ECA, CCA, ICA** (TO)

**"5 mm ACoA aneurysm — ISUIA true?"** → **> 10 mm ≈ 5%/yr rupture** (TO)

**"Anterior choroidal occlusion?"** → **hemiplegia, hemihypaesthesia, hemianopia** (TO)

**"Perimesencephalic SAH — gold standard?"** → **DSA** (TO)

**"AVMs — EXCEPT?"** → **rebleed highest in first 2 weeks** (TO)

**"Subclavian steal?"** → **left subclavian occluded proximal to vertebral origin** (TO)

**"Frontal AVM — options?"** → **all (embolisation, radiosurgery, combined)** (TO)

**"80% symptomatic ICA stenosis?"** → **open endarterectomy** (TO)

**"Malignant MCA infarct with bleed, hemiplegia?"** → **decompressive craniectomy** (TO)

**"Headache, neck pain, right ptosis?"** → **PCoA aneurysm SAH** (TO)

**"ICA : ECA flow?"** → **80 : 20** (TO)

**"Spinal dural AVF — EXCEPT?"** → **recurrent bleeding causes symptoms** (TO)

**"Aggressive spinal AVM factor?"** → **leptomeningeal vein** (TO)

**"Glomus AVM?"** → **type II** (TO)
