# Incomplete Spinal Cord Injuries

*Spine · 28 / 69*

## 1 Basics

### Definition

**Incomplete SCI** — some motor or sensory function preserved below the level: **sacral sparing** (voluntary anal contraction, perianal sensation) or palpable/visible muscle contraction below level · **sacral sparing separates complete from incomplete**

### Epidemiology

**US** — \~11,000 new SCI/yr · **34% incomplete tetraplegia** (central cord commonest) · 17% incomplete paraplegia · 47% complete

### Anatomy – tracts

| Tract | Function | Crosses |
|---|---|---|
| **Lateral corticospinal (LCT)** | **Motor** (voluntary) — **upper limb fibres central/medial, lower limb lateral** | Brainstem (medulla) → ipsilateral in cord |
| Ventral corticospinal | Motor (smaller) | — |
| **Dorsal columns** (gracilis = legs/lower trunk, cuneatus = arms) | **Fine touch, vibration, proprioception** | Brainstem |
| **Lateral spinothalamic (LST)** | **Pain, temperature**, crude sensation | **In cord, 1–2 levels above entry** |
| **Ventral spinothalamic** | **Light (crude) touch** | In cord |

**Blood supply** — **anterior spinal artery → anterior 2/3 of cord** (fed by segmental arteries incl. **artery of Adamkiewicz**: largest, usually **left, T8–L1**)

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### ASIA impairment scale

| Grade | Definition |
|---|---|
| **A** | **Complete** — no motor or sensory in **S4-S5** |
| **B** | Sensory incomplete — sensation preserved below level incl. S4-S5, **no motor** |
| **C** | Motor incomplete — **> half key muscles below level grade < 3** |
| **D** | Motor incomplete — **≥ half key muscles grade ≥ 3** |
| **E** | Normal |

### Incomplete cord syndromes

| Syndrome | Mechanism | Lost | Preserved | Prognosis |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Central cord** | **Elderly, hyperextension**, spondylotic stenosis (anterior osteophytes + infolded LF) | Motor **UE > LE**, **hands > arms**; burning dysaesthesia (hyperpathia) in arms | **Sacral sparing**; LE motor | **Good** (ambulation > 80%); **clumsy hands** |
| **Anterior cord** | **Flexion-compression** (bone) or **anterior spinal artery** injury (aortic surgery, T12 corpectomy) | **Motor (LCT) + pain/temperature (LST)** ± light touch; **LE > UE** | **Dorsal columns** (proprioception, vibration) | **Worst**; 10–20% motor recovery; mimics complete |
| **Brown-Séquard** | **Hemitransection** — **penetrating** (stab) | **Ipsilateral motor + proprioception/vibration**; **contralateral pain + temperature** | — | **Best** — **99% (98%) ambulatory** |
| **Posterior cord** | Very rare; posterior spinal artery / tumour / trauma | **Proprioception** | Motor, pain, temperature, light touch | — |

## 5 Management

### Central cord syndrome

**Pathophysiology** — transient compression → **central cord oedema** → selective **lateral corticospinal white matter** injury\
✗ Wrong options: central grey haemorrhage · anterior/posterior spinal artery · chronic compression = myelopathy · disc herniation · OPLL

**Late picture** — **UE LMN signs** (clumsy) + **LE UMN signs** (spastic)

**Treatment** — **controversial** · no active compression → non-op · active compression / instability → decompression · ASIA C may benefit from **early (< 24 h) decompression** (OB) \
✗ Wrong options: high-dose steroids · routine non-op for all

**Recovery order** — **LE first → bowel/bladder → proximal UE → hands last** · **age < 50** → best recovery · usually walk + regain bladder; **hands rarely normal**\
✗ Wrong options: stepwise decline = myelopathy · rapid decline = abscess/tumour

**Better neurologic outcome** — **ASIA C/D** (mild) · T2 cord signal → less early decline (OB)

### Prognosis & surgical indications

**Most important prognostic variable** — **completeness / severity of neurologic deficit**\
✗ Wrong options: mechanism · steroids < 8 h · gender · early surgery

**Gunshot wounds** — surgery only for **retained canal fragment + incomplete SCI with progressive deficit** (or persistent CSF leak) · trans-colonic GSW → **broad antibiotics, no surgery** · stable burst without deficit → non-op (Wood RCT) · central cord without compression → non-op · type III odontoid neuro intact → non-op

## X vs Y

| Finding | Syndrome |
|---|---|
| Arms + hands weak > legs, elderly fall on forehead | **Central cord** |
| Legs paralysed, pain/temp lost, proprioception kept | **Anterior cord** |
| Ipsilateral motor + proprioception, contralateral pain/temp | **Brown-Séquard** |
| Proprioception lost only | **Posterior cord** |
| After thoracoabdominal aneurysm repair / left-sided T12 corpectomy | **Anterior cord (Adamkiewicz)** |

| | ASIA C | ASIA D |
|---|---|---|
| Key muscles below level | > half grade **< 3** | ≥ half grade **≥ 3** |

## How it's asked

**"78-yr fall, hands weakest, urinary dysfunction — prognosis non-op?"** → **likely to walk independently**\
✗ Wrong options: full hand recovery · stepwise · < 10% bladder recovery

**"79-yr forehead strike, UE weak, legs normal — pathophysiology?"** → **selective injury to central lateral corticospinal tract**

**"Tract causing motor deficit (UE 3/5, LE 4/5)?"** → **lateral corticospinal**

**"Wrist/finger flexion 3/5, legs 4+/5?"** → **lateral corticospinal tract**

**"NOT associated with central cord?"** → **LE dysaesthesias**

**"Who gets central cord syndrome?"** → **67-yr after hyperextension**\
✗ Wrong options: penetrating → BSS · aortic repair → anterior cord

**"73-yr, arms 3/5, legs 4+/5 after forehead strike — pathogenesis?"** → **transient compression + central cord oedema**

**"75-yr falls on head, stenotic MRI — symptoms?"** → **UE weakness primarily**

**"Central cord — true?"** → **age < 50 → better ambulation + motor recovery**\
✗ Wrong options: steroids · routine non-op

**"88-yr, arms > legs weakness — pattern?"** → **central cord diagram**

**"C5-6 dislocation with Brown-Séquard — findings?"** → **ipsilateral motor loss, contralateral pain/temp loss**

**"Stab with hemitransection?"** → **ipsilateral motor + proprioception, contralateral pain/temp**

**"Right neck stab, left pain loss, right proprioception loss — motor + temp?"** → **right motor loss, left temperature loss**

**"Most consistent with anterior cord?"** → **paraplegia after thoracoabdominal aneurysm repair**

**"T12 corpectomy (left approach), new leg weakness, sensation intact, canal patent?"** → **occlusion of arterial supply (Adamkiewicz)**

**"MVC, legs weak, ↓ sensation below T9, position sense kept — spared region?"** → **dorsal columns**

**"Posterior cord syndrome loses?"** → **proprioception**

**"Most important prognostic variable?"** → **severity of neurologic deficit**

**"Surgical indication?"** → **GSW, retained fragment compressing cauda equina, progressive motor deficit**
