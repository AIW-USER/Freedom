# Low Back Pain

*Spine · 33 / 69*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology & natural history

**Lifetime** — **50–80%** (70–85%) · \~$100 billion/yr · 2nd only to respiratory infection for office visits · leading cause of disability worldwide

**Work** — back injury \~2% of workforce/yr · of those off work: **60–70% back by 6 wks**, **80–90% by 12 wks**; slow after 12 wks

**Course** — **90% resolve within 1 yr** · chronic in 5–10% · recurrence 20–72%

**Commonest cause** — **muscle strain (musculogenic)**; commonest degenerative: stenosis, disc herniation, discogenic pain

**Risk factors** — obesity, smoking, sex, lifting, vibration, prolonged sitting, **job dissatisfaction**

### Red vs yellow flags (TO)

| Red flags (serious pathology) | Yellow flags (chronic disability risk) |
|---|---|
| **Age < 20 or > 50** | Belief pain is severely disabling |
| **Significant trauma** | **Fear-avoidance** behaviour |
| Constant, **night pain**, worsening | Low mood |
| **Cancer**, steroids, **IVDU** | Expects passive treatment |
| **Unexplained fever / weight loss** | Poor job satisfaction |
| **Neurologic deficit** · bowel/bladder change (CES) | **Lack of family support** (✗ Wrong options: red flag) |

## 3 Presentation

| Pain type | Features |
|---|---|
| **Musculogenic** | Commonest; activity-related stiffness, difficulty bending |
| Discogenic | Controversial; discogram |
| Mechanical | Facet degeneration / instability; worse lifting, standing |
| Sacroiliac | SI joint |
| Radicular | Unilateral dermatomal leg pain |
| Referred | Buttocks, posterior thighs, groin (L5-S1) |
| **Neurogenic claudication** | Buttock/leg pain with standing — fairly specific for **stenosis** |
| Myelopathy | Cord lesion (\~L1 or above) |
| CES / conus | Bilateral leg pain, weakness, saddle anaesthesia, bowel/bladder |

### Non-organic (Waddell) signs

**Significant if ≥ 3 of 5**

| Waddell sign |
|---|
| **Superficial / non-anatomic tenderness** |
| **Simulation** — pain with **axial compression** or **simulated rotation** |
| **Distraction** — **negative SLR when distracted** (seated) |
| **Regional** non-dermatomal sensory / motor disturbance |
| **Overreaction** |

**Organic sign** — **positive SLR with distraction** · **neuroanatomic** deficit is NOT Waddell (TO)\
✗ Wrong options: axial-load pain · diffuse tenderness · non-dermatomal numbness · simulated rotation

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

**X-ray** — only if **> 1 month** failed treatment or **red flags**\
✗ Wrong options: at first visit for 2-day / 2-week atraumatic pain

**MRI** — sensitive + specific but **many asymptomatic abnormalities**

| Asymptomatic age | HNP | Bulge | Degeneration |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20–39 | 21% | 56% | 34% |
| 40–59 | 22% | 50% | 59% |
| 60–79 | **36%** | **79%** | **93%** |

**High-intensity zone (annular tear)** — 59% symptomatic vs 25% asymptomatic (Carragee); discography provokes pain \~70% regardless

**Compression fracture acuity** — **MRI** (oedema ∝ recency)\
✗ Wrong options: CT · flex-ext · indium scan

**Differential** — strain, HNP, DS, stenosis, radiculopathy, **AAA**, SI infection, AS, coccydynia, sacral insufficiency fracture

## 5 Management

```
Acute atraumatic LBP, no red flags
├─ REASSURANCE + LIMITED ANALGESIA + EARLY ROM / activity, follow-up ~4 wks
│      (no X-ray, MRI or ESI at first visit)
└─ > 1 month failed / red flags → IMAGING
```

**Chronic work-related pain — strongest negative predictor** — **high pre-treatment VAS**\
✗ Wrong options: race · smoking · occupation · gender

**Chronic non-specific pain, non-surgical (smoker, depression, narcotics)** — **cognitive intervention + exercise + smoking cessation**\
✗ Wrong options: discography · EMG · myelogram

### Coccydynia (TO)

**Cause** — commonest **direct trauma**; childbirth, surgery, epidural

**Acute (< 2 months)** — rest + **NSAIDs 8 wks**, stool softener, **U-shaped cushion**

**Chronic (> 2 months)** — steroid + LA injection ± manipulation under GA → after 2 failed monthly injections → **coccygectomy (60–80% success)** (TO answer **80%**)

### Baastrup disease ("kissing spines") (TO)

**Pathology** — **crowding / contact of adjacent spinous processes** with excessive lordosis → interspinous ligament degeneration, **adventitious bursa**, sclerosis, flattening · commonest **L4-L5**\
✗ Wrong options: spina bifida · cervical instability/myelopathy

**Clinical** — midline pain radiating up/down, **worse in extension, better in flexion**, point tenderness · rarely claudication (epidural cyst)

**Imaging** — lateral X-ray contact + sclerosis; MRI interspinous bursitis (< 10%)

**Treatment** — NSAIDs, steroid + LA injection, bursa excision / osteotomy

## X vs Y

| | Organic | Non-organic (Waddell) |
|---|---|---|
| SLR distracted | **Positive** | Negative |
| Deficit | Dermatomal | Regional |
| Tenderness | Localised | Superficial / diffuse |

## How it's asked

**"2-day atraumatic LBP — all appropriate EXCEPT?"** → **lumbar radiographs**

**"Missed work for acute LBP — return within 6 wks?"** → **60–70%**

**"Severe atraumatic LBP, no red flags — management?"** → **reassurance, limited analgesics, early ROM**

**"Return to work within 12 wks?"** → **80–90%**

**"Radiographs NOT indicated initially?"** → **28-yr with 2 wks isolated LBP**

**"Waddell signs EXCEPT?"** → **neuroanatomic motor/sensory compromise** (TO)

**"Finding indicating organic cause?"** → **positive SLR with distraction**

**"Most likely non-organic LBP?"** → **superficial tenderness, non-dermatomal numbness/weakness, negative SLR, unrelated disc degeneration**

**"Strongest negative predictor of non-op success?"** → **high pre-treatment VAS**

**"4-yr LBP, smoker, depression, narcotics, annular tear on MRI?"** → **cognitive intervention, exercise, smoking cessation**

**"Acuity of compression fracture?"** → **MRI**

**"Red flags EXCEPT?"** → **lack of family support** (TO — yellow flag)

**"Coccygectomy success after failed non-op?"** → **80%** (TO)

**"Baastrup disease?"** → **crowding of spinous processes** (TO)
