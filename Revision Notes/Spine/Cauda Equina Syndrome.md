# Cauda Equina Syndrome

*Spine · 9 / 69*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Incidence** — **rare**: 1.5–3.4 per million/yr; prevalence \~1/65,000 · in **\~3%** of lumbar disc herniations (TO: 1–10%)

**Demographics** — **males**, **4th decade (30s)**

**Level** — **L4-5** commonest

### Causes

| Cause | Note |
|---|---|
| **Disc herniation** | **Commonest** (large central HNP) (✗ Wrong options: burst fracture commonest) |
| **Spinal epidural haematoma** | Spontaneous on high-dose DVT prophylaxis, **epidural anaesthesia**, early post-op prophylaxis |
| Tumours | Myxopapillary ependymoma, schwannoma, meningioma |
| Synovial facet cyst · epidural abscess | Ask about **IVDU** |
| Trauma | **Lumbar burst** retropulsion, dislocation |
| High-grade developmental spondylolisthesis | Intact lamina |

### Pathobiology

**Compression** → ↓ nutrient delivery (↓ blood flow, ↓ CSF diffusion) → venous congestion + **intraneural oedema** → "intraneural compartment syndrome" → **root ischaemia**

**Vulnerability** — roots covered only by **endoneurium** (no Schwann cell covering per TO) + hypovascular central zone

### Anatomy

**Conus medullaris** — terminal cord, ends **T12–L1** · **filum terminale** → coccyx

**Cauda equina** — **L1–S5 roots** in lumbar canal → **lower motor neuron** lesion

| Bladder innervation | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Parasympathetic** (pelvic splanchnic, S2–4) | **Voiding** — detrusor contracts, internal sphincter relaxes |
| **Sympathetic** (hypogastric plexus) | **Retention** — detrusor relaxes, internal sphincter contracts |
| **Pudendal nerve** | **External sphincter** (voluntary) |

**CES bladder** — LMN interrupts reflex arc → can't sense filling → **retention → overflow incontinence**

## 3 Presentation

**History** — heavy lifting with flexed spine, coughing spell · ask **anticoagulants**, recent procedures (haematoma), **IVDU** (infection)

| Symptom | Frequency |
|---|---|
| **Back pain** | **Commonest** (Orthobullets, OB) — may be the only initial symptom · 83% |
| **Leg pain (radicular)** | **2nd commonest** (Orthobullets) · 90% — "commonest presenting symptom" in some OB stems — read the options |
| Urinary retention | 60% |
| Urinary incontinence | 55% |
| Leg weakness / sensory deficit / absent ankle jerk | 55% / 62% / 62% |
| Perianal sensory deficit | 76% |
| ↓ rectal tone | 50% (relatively **late**) |
| Sexual / bowel dysfunction | Rare |

**Order of frequency** — **leg pain > urinary retention > urinary incontinence**

**Saddle anaesthesia** — less common but **most specific** → trigger emergency surgery protocol

**Saddle anaesthesia alone, no other LL deficit (TO)** — lesion **below S2** (S3–S5)

**Exam** — palpate bladder · ↓ voluntary rectal tone · **pinprick** loss S2–S4 perianal/perineum/posterior thigh · ↓ / absent LL reflexes · **↓/absent bulbocavernosus + anal wink** \
✗ Wrong options: **Babinski** (UMN sign — EXCEPT answer)

**Classic triad (TO)** — **asymmetric areflexic paralysis** + **bladder/bowel (S2–4)** + **saddle anaesthesia** (S3–4, **perianal S5 spared initially**)

**32-yr, unilateral radiculopathy + saddle anaesthesia (TO)** — **CES + urinary retention + absent ankle jerk**\
✗ Wrong options: perianal anaesthesia + areflexia · conus

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

**Diagnosis is clinical** (history + exam); MRI confirms cause + plans surgery

### Key radiology findings

| Study | Use |
|---|---|
| **MRI** | **Study of choice**, emergent (**within 1–2 h** of ED arrival); large **central disc** obliterating canal, haematoma, abscess |
| **CT myelography** | **Can't have MRI (pacemaker)** (TO: myelo-CT; ✗ Wrong options: plain CT · angiography · no imaging) |
| X-ray | Usually normal; don't delay MRI; burst fracture / high-grade slip |

### Genes & lab markers

**CBC, ESR, CRP** — if abscess suspected (fever, IVDU) — not routine\
✗ Wrong options: labs before MRI without infection signs

**Post-void residual (PVR)** — pre-op if it doesn't delay surgery · normal **< 50–100 mL** · **PVR < 200 mL → 97% NPV** · **> 200 mL highly suggestive**\
✗ Wrong options: "> 200 mL excludes"

### Classification – bladder function

| Grade | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Incomplete (CES-I)** | ↓ urgency / sensation, **no** retention or incontinence |
| **Complete (CES-R)** | Clear **retention** and/or incontinence |

## 5 Management

```
Suspected CES (saddle anaesthesia / bladder change + back-leg pain)
├─ EMERGENT MRI (CT myelogram if MRI contraindicated)
└─ Compression confirmed → DECOMPRESSION ASAP
       (< 24 h preferable · < 48 h standard of care)
```

### Timing of decompression

**< 48 h vs > 48 h** — significant improvement in **sensory, motor, urinary + rectal** function when < 48 h · **no added benefit < 24 h vs 24–48 h** (Ahn meta-analysis)\
✗ Wrong options: < 24 h better than < 48 h · < 60 h · < 1 week

**Not indicated** — epidural steroid injection · methylprednisolone · outpatient MRI · reassurance/bed rest

### Surgical technique

| Procedure | Indication |
|---|---|
| **Microdiscectomy** (unilateral laminotomy + medial facetectomy + discectomy) | Massive soft disc, **young**, minimal degeneration |
| **Wide laminectomy** (bilateral laminectomy, LF + medial facetectomy, discectomy) | **Older**, hypertrophic LF + lateral recess stenosis; ↓ dural retraction, better visualisation |
| **Laminectomy + fusion** | High-grade spondylolisthesis; insidious CES with degenerative spondylolisthesis |

**Microdiscectomy vs wide laminectomy** — **similar outcomes**; goal is adequate thecal sac decompression\
✗ Wrong options: laminectomy → greater recovery

## 6 Complications

### Prognosis – bladder dysfunction recovery

**Worse prognosis** — **saddle anaesthesia + bladder dysfunction** at presentation · **decompression > 48 h** · pre-op chronic back pain (urinary/rectal) · pre-op rectal dysfunction · older age (sexual function, neuro recovery)\
✗ Wrong options: age per se in some OB stems

**Least likely to recover** — **bladder dysfunction** · **long-term urinary dysfunction common (\~50%)** · bladder improves up to **16 months**; motor up to **1 yr**; sexual over years\
✗ Wrong options: EHL weakness · perianal sensation · foot numbness · leg pain

**Expected course** — **pain + sensory improve; residual bladder dysfunction possible**\
✗ Wrong options: complete recovery · no change

| Complication | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Dural tear** | 1–4%; primary repair ± fat graft, bed rest 4–7 d, ± lumbar drain; outcomes unaffected if treated |
| Iatrogenic instability | Aggressive medial facetectomy |
| **Epidural fibrosis** | Back/leg pain at **\~3 months**; **gad MRI** separates from recurrent HNP; poor response to re-exploration |
| Wound infection | \~1%; diabetics |
| **Vascular injury** | Curette through **ALL**; mortality up to **50%** |
| DVT | Prophylaxis held for haematoma fear; antiplatelets safe at **48–72 h** post-op |

## X vs Y

| Feature (TO) | Cauda equina | Conus medullaris |
|---|---|---|
| Level of lesion | Roots, **L1 vertebra and below** | Sacral cord, **T12 (D12)–L1** (T11–L2) |
| Presentation | **Asymmetric** | **Symmetric** |
| Radicular pain | **Severe** | Usually absent |
| Sensory | **Saddle (S2–S4)**, perianal S5 spared initially | **Perianal (S5)** anaesthesia; sensory dissociation possible |
| Motor | Asymmetric **flaccid** (LMN) | Symmetric, usually flaccid, sometimes hyperreflexic |
| Reflexes | **Areflexia** (ankle; knee lost if L3–4 involved) | **Knee reflex preserved** |
| Bladder / bowel | **Late** (TO table) — retention first → overflow | **Early** |
| BCR / anal wink | Absent | **Persistently absent** (irreversible sacral cord injury) |
| Babinski | No | — |

**Diabetic, bilateral saddle anaesthesia + bladder/bowel + absent BCR (TO)** — **conus medullaris**\
✗ Wrong options: central cord · Brown-Séquard · mixed

| | Hemi-cauda equina | Complete CES |
|---|---|---|
| Legs | **Unilateral** | Bilateral |
| Saddle | Unilateral S1–S5 | Bilateral |
| Motor | Often absent | Present |
| Sphincter | **Retention / incontinence present** | Present |
| Cause | **Disc herniation (same — EXCEPT answer, TO)** | Disc herniation |

**Neurology rather than spine referral** — non-compressive cause (e.g. MS / demyelination)\
✗ Wrong options: burst fracture · large disc · sacral metastasis · epidural lymphoma

## How it's asked

**"Young man, unilateral radiculopathy + saddle anaesthesia — diagnosis + signs?"** → **CES, urinary retention, absent ankle jerk** (TO)

**"Order of findings in CES, commonest → least?"** → **leg pain > urinary retention > urinary incontinence**

**"Back pain + perianal numbness, bilateral weakness — next?"** → **immediate MRI lumbar spine**\
✗ Wrong options: CT myelogram · labs · X-ray · discharge

**"Cough → back pain, retention, faecal incontinence, saddle paraesthesia?"** → **immediate MRI**

**"Commonest presenting problem?"** → **back + leg pain**

**"Bladder + bowel loss, saddle numbness — management?"** → **immediate MRI + acute surgery**

**"Decompression window for bladder + motor recovery?"** → **< 48 hours**

**"After decompression, least likely to improve?"** → **bladder dysfunction**

**"Prognosis after urgent surgery with dense perineal anaesthesia?"** → **pain + sensation improve, bladder may remain**

**"12 h of retention + buttock numbness — true?"** → **saddle anaesthesia + bladder dysfunction → poor prognosis**\
✗ Wrong options: CT myelogram · ESI · steroids

**"CES treatment — true?"** → **long-term urinary dysfunction common**\
✗ Wrong options: < 24 h better than < 48 h · laminectomy better · sexual dysfunction commonest · burst fracture commonest

**"L4-5 HNP occluding sac — true?"** → **urinary function can have a prolonged recovery**\
✗ Wrong options: PVR > 200 mL excludes · elderly recover better

**"Not true about CES?"** → **positive Babinski** (TO)

**"Diabetic, saddle anaesthesia, bladder/bowel, absent BCR?"** → **conus medullaris** (TO)

**"Hemi-CES differs from CES EXCEPT?"** → **cause is mostly herniated disc** (TO)

**"Saddle anaesthesia only — lesion level?"** → **below S2** (TO)

**"Pacemaker + conus-cauda picture — pre-op study?"** → **myelo-CT** (TO)
