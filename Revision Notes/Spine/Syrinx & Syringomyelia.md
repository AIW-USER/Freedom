# Syrinx & Syringomyelia

*Spine · 63 / 69*

## 1 Basics

### Definitions

**Syringomyelia** — fluid cavity in the **cord** that expands → deficits · **syringobulbia** — in the brainstem

**Epidemiology** — 8.4 per 100,000 · **M > F** · 3rd–4th decades; insidious from adolescence to 45

### Causes (partial CSF obstruction)

| Cause | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Chiari malformation** | **> 50%** · tonsils act as **pistons** at foramen magnum → pressure wave each heartbeat · syrinx in **69% of adult, 40% of child Chiari** (TO) |
| Spinal cord trauma | **4–10% of SCI**, may appear years later |
| Cord tumour | **30%** develop syrinx (TO: tumour cysts not true syringomyelia) |
| Post-infectious | Arachnoiditis (meningitis, subarachnoid blood) |

**Primary spinal syringomyelia (TO)** — pathology **entirely within the spine** (trauma, arachnoid cysts/webs, meningitis scarring) (✗ Wrong options: "secondary")

**Chiari syrinx mechanism (TO)** — **Oldfield: pulsatile tonsils on a closed spinal subarachnoid space drive CSF into the cord** (+ arterial pulsations, Stoodley) · (✗ Wrong options: "pressure differential from partial block" (TO EXCEPT key)) · old "communicating" 4th-ventricle theory abandoned · cause: **small posterior fossa** relative to cerebellum

### Associations

| Condition | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Scoliosis** | **25–80%** of syringomyelia · neural axis anomaly in 1.8–25% of "idiopathic" curves |
| Klippel-Feil | — |
| **Charcot joint** | **< 5%**, usually **shoulder** |

## 3 Presentation

**Symptoms** — **occipital headache worse with Valsalva** (cough, sneeze — tussive, dural stretch), back + radicular pain · **central cord syndrome** picture

**Dissociated sensory loss** — **pain + temperature lost in a cape** over neck/shoulders · **light touch, vibration, position preserved**

**Reflexes** — **asymmetric abdominal reflexes**, asymmetric/hyperactive reflexes, asymmetric sweating, spasticity

**Motor / deformity** — asymmetric bulk, weakness, LE contractures, scoliosis

**Syringobulbia** — CN XII tongue atrophy, XI SCM/trapezius, IX–X dysphagia/dysarthria, VII facial palsy

**Chiari brainstem signs (TO)** — nystagmus, hoarseness, swallowing/speech problems, sleep apnoea, imbalance

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Study | Fact |
|---|---|
| X-ray | **Lack of thoracic apical lordosis** suggests syrinx · scoliosis series |
| **MRI** | **Diagnostic** · **gadolinium** to exclude tumour |
| CT myelogram | Post-traumatic syrinx / arachnoid webs — shows obstruction (TO) |

### Diagnostic criteria – MRI in scoliosis

| Indication |
|---|
| **Atypical curve — left thoracic apex, double curve** |
| **Neurologic deficit** (e.g. ↓ flank/hand pinprick, absent abdominal reflex) |
| **Infantile or juvenile onset** |
| **Male** with atypical or large curve |
| **Thoracic kyphosis > 30°** |
| Valsalva headache |

**Histology** — grey matter cavitation, near/continuous with central canal, **gliotic lining**

**Differential** — hydromyelia, glioependymal cyst, myelomalacia, cystic tumour, persistent central canal

### Neuropathic shoulder

**Clue** — severe destruction both sides of joint, little pain, minor trauma, chronic dislocation · **MRI brain + cervical spine** → Chiari / syrinx\
✗ Wrong options: RF, HLA-B27, synovial fluid, bone scan

## 5 Management

```
Syrinx ± Chiari
├─ Asymptomatic, not enlarging → OBSERVE (± brace during work-up)
└─ CN deficit, weakness, cerebellar signs, sensory deficit → FORAMEN MAGNUM DECOMPRESSION ± SHUNT
       └─ Scoliosis → FUSION 3–6 MONTHS AFTER decompression
```

**Chiari decompression (TO)** — suboccipital craniectomy to uncover tonsils + **C1 laminectomy** + intradural inspection ± tonsil reduction + **duraplasty**

**Primary spinal syringomyelia (TO)** — shunting (pleura/peritoneum) has **high failure** (cavity collapses on tube) → **resect focal obstruction**

**Scoliosis** — decompression alone may improve curve but large curves / **> 8 yrs** still progress

## 6 Complications

**Undiagnosed syrinx + fusion** — **↑ neurological deterioration / paralysis**

## How it's asked

**"Arnold-Chiari — EXCEPT?"** → **"partial CSF block pressure differential" as syrinx cause** (TO)

**"Syringomyelia — EXCEPT?"** → **"pathology wholly in spine = secondary"** (it's primary) (TO)

**"62-yr painless destroyed shoulder — confirm with?"** → **MRI brain + cervical spine**

**"Chronic dislocation + destruction after minor trauma — positive test?"** → **MRI of upper cervical spine**

**"5-yr, 20° left thoracic, hand pinprick loss?"** → **MRI of the entire spine**

**"12-yr left thoracic curve + Valsalva headaches — MRI?"** → **syrinx + tonsillar herniation (Chiari I)**
