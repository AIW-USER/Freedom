# Atlantoaxial Instability

*Spine · 6 / 69*

## 1 Basics

### Aetiology

**C1-2** — "transitional zone" → instability from degenerative + traumatic causes

| | Degenerative / congenital | Traumatic / infective |
|---|---|---|
| **Adult** | **Down syndrome** · **rheumatoid arthritis** · **os odontoideum** | Type I odontoid fracture (very rare) · atlas fractures · transverse ligament injury |
| **Paediatric** | **JRA** · **Morquio** (lysosomal storage) | **Rotatory atlantoaxial subluxation** · Grisel syndrome |

**Os odontoideum = ADULT cause (TO)** — not paediatric (EXCEPT answer) · aetiology debated, most support **post-traumatic**

**Other causes (TO)** — skeletal dysplasias (**pseudoachondroplasia – COMP**), neurofibromatosis, Grisel (infection)

**Pseudoachondroplasia vs achondroplasia** — pseudoachondroplasia → **atlantoaxial instability** · achondroplasia → **lumbar stenosis**

### Anatomy & ligaments

| Ligament | Connects / role |
|---|---|
| **Transverse atlantal ligament (TAL)** | Horizontal limb of cruciate; medial tubercles of C1; straps dens to anterior arch; **most important stabiliser — strongest ligament of the spine** |
| **Alar ligaments** | **Paired parasagittal**; dens → occipital condyles (+ C1 lateral mass); "check" ligaments → **limit rotation** |
| **Apical ligament** | **Single midline**; dens tip → foramen magnum; little strength |
| Tectorial membrane | Cephalad continuation of **PLL**; dens/C2 → foramen magnum |
| Anterior / posterior atlanto-occipital membranes | ALL continuation / posterior FM → posterior C1 arch |

![Transverse, alar and apical ligament complex](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob2049-combined-4.jpg)

**Biomechanics** — C1-2 provides **\~50%** (Orthobullets) · **60%** (TO) of cervical rotation; peg-in-ring; near-horizontal facets

## 3 Presentation

**Symptoms** — neck pain, neuro symptoms

**Deficits appear late** — capacious canal at C1 (Steel's rule of thirds)

**Myelopathy** — hyperreflexia, weakness, **broad-based gait**, ↓ hand dexterity, loss of motor milestones, bladder dysfunction

**Child, trivial fall → transient quadriparesis, recovered (TO)** — **congenital atlantoaxial instability**\
✗ Wrong options: ossified LF (age) · syrinx (wouldn't recover) · bifid atlas

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Measure | Definition | Threshold |
|---|---|---|
| **Atlantodens interval (ADI)** | Dens → **posterior border of anterior C1 arch**, flexion-extension | Adult **> 3.5 mm** unstable (Orthobullets) · **> 3 mm** (TO) · child **> 5 mm** · **> 10 mm → surgery in RA** |
| **Space available for cord (SAC / PADI)** | Posterior dens → anterior posterior arch | Normal 17–29 mm · **< 14 mm** → ↑ neuro risk, surgery in RA |
| **Sum of lateral mass displacement (open mouth)** | C1 masses over C2 | **> 8.1 mm** → TAL rupture, unstable (Orthobullets) · **> 6.9 mm** Rule of Spence (TO) |

![ADI and SAC on lateral flexion view](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob2049-adi-labels.jpg)

**Lateral masses can displace only if** — C1 ring fractured **or** TAL ruptured

**Flexion-extension views** — mandatory pre-op in **all high-risk patients** (Down, RA, dysplasia) before any surgery

**Pseudoachondroplasia + myelopathy** — **flexion-extension C-spine; ADI difference 6 mm**\
✗ Wrong options: lateral mass 9 mm = atlas fracture · hip OA · scoliosis · lumbar stenosis

**CT** — essential for rotated C1 on C2 · MRI for TAL

### Fielding & Hawkins classification (TO)

| Type | Displacement | TAL | Treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I** | Simple **rotatory**, no anterior shift | Intact | Collar 6–8 wks |
| **II** | Anterior **3–5 mm**, one lateral mass pivots | Deficient | Collar / surgery |
| **III** | Anterior **> 5 mm** | Disrupted | **C1-2 fusion** (unstable) |
| **IV** | **Posterior** displacement (dens erosion) | — | **C1-2 fusion** (unstable) |

## 5 Management

**Determined by specific condition** (RA, Down, os odontoideum, fracture, TAL injury, JRA, Morquio, AARS)

### Operative criteria (TO)

| Adults | Children (≥ 1 of) |
|---|---|
| **ADI > 5 mm** | **Neurologic deficit** |
| Moderate displacement / instability on dynamic films | **Persistent ADI > 4 mm** |
| RA: ADI > 10 mm or SAC < 14 mm | Deformity **> 3 months** |
| — | Recurrence despite **6 weeks** immobilisation |

### C1-2 instability – os odontoideum

```
Os odontoideum
├─ Asymptomatic, stable → STOP CONTACT SPORTS
└─ Neuro signs or widened ADI → POSTERIOR C1-2 FUSION
       (not anterior C1-2 fusion — osseous anatomy)
```

**Os odontoideum types (TO)** — **orthotopic** (ossicle in normal dens position) · **dystopic** (migrated toward foramen magnum) · risk of **sudden cord compression after minor trauma**

**Down syndrome (TO)** — **screen for AAI before anaesthesia / sports** · paediatric **ADI > 5 mm** = instability · MPS → odontoid hypoplasia · achondroplasia → foramen magnum stenosis

**Paediatric landmarks (TO)** — BDI / BAI **< 12 mm** · Pavlov **> 0.8** · centre of motion C2–C3 (child) vs C5–C6 (adult) · odontoid cartilaginous until \~6 yrs

### Grisel syndrome

**Definition (TO)** — inflammatory **atlantoaxial rotatory subluxation** after **upper respiratory / head-neck infection** (or **tonsillectomy, adenoid** inflammation); hyperaemia damages facet capsules + TAL

**Presentation (TO)** — child, **painful torticollis** ("cock-robin": chin rotated one way, neck tilted the other, slight flexion), **facial asymmetry**, **change in voice / nasal resonance**, painful attempted correction\
✗ Wrong options: muscular torticollis (painless, infant) · Sandifer (reflux)

| Grisel type | Treatment (TO) |
|---|---|
| I | **Soft collar** |
| II | **Hard collar** |
| III | **Halo** |
| IV | **Open fixation** (neuro complications) |

**Grisel by duration (TO)** — acute: rest + **antibiotics** + soft collar · chronic **> 3 weeks**: traction → **posterior C1–2 fusion** · CT shows asymmetric C1–C2 facets

## X vs Y

| | Grisel | Muscular torticollis |
|---|---|---|
| Age | Child (\~4–12) | Infant |
| Trigger | URTI / tonsillectomy | Birth (intrauterine positioning) |
| Pain | **Painful** | Painless |
| Mass | No | SCM tumour |
| Head | **Cock-robin**: tilt one side, chin to the other, slight flexion | Tilt toward, chin away from tight SCM |
| Lesion | C1-2 rotatory subluxation | SCM fibrosis |

| | ADI | SAC (PADI) |
|---|---|---|
| Measures | Anterior instability | Cord space |
| Abnormal | > 3.5 mm adult, > 5 mm child | **< 14 mm** |
| RA surgery | > 10 mm | < 14 mm |

## How it's asked

**"NOT a cause of paediatric atlantoaxial instability?"** → **os odontoideum** (TO)

**"10-yr, trivial fall, transient quadriparesis?"** → **congenital atlantoaxial instability** (TO)

**"11-yr os odontoideum with myelopathic signs — treatment?"** → **posterior C1-2 fusion**\
✗ Wrong options: PT · soft collar · stop sports only · anterior fusion

**"Pseudoachondroplasia with myelopathy — study + finding?"** → **flexion-extension C-spine, ADI difference 6 mm**

**"4-yr girl, painful torticollis, facial asymmetry, voice change after URTI?"** → **Grisel syndrome** (TO)

**"Most important C1-2 stabiliser?"** → **transverse ligament**

**"Single midline CVJ ligament?"** → **apical ligament**

**"Sum of lateral mass overhang implying TAL rupture?"** → **> 8.1 mm** (Orthobullets) · **> 6.9 mm** (TO, Spence) — read the options

**"SAC predicting neuro injury in RA?"** → **< 14 mm**

**"ADI needing surgery in RA?"** → **> 10 mm**
