# Lumbar Disc Herniation

*Spine · 35 / 69*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Incidence** — peak **4th–5th decades** · lifetime prevalence 10% · only \~5% symptomatic · **M:F 3:1**

**Commonest level** — **L5-S1** (Orthobullets) · **L4-L5** (TO) — read the options · 95% at L4-5 or L5-S1

**Foraminal herniations** — commonest at **L3-L4** (OB)

### Pathoanatomy

**Mechanism** — recurrent **torsional strain** → outer annulus tears → nucleus herniates · **PLL weakest posterolaterally** → paracentral site

**Pain fibres** — **sinuvertebral nerve** → posterior annulus (vertebrogenic back pain precedes herniation)

**Who herniates** — young, **well-hydrated** discs (child: **ring apophysis Salter-Harris II**) · desiccated old discs less likely

**Sciatica** — compression + inflammation: **TNF-α, MMP, NO, PGE₂, IL-6**

| Disc part | Collagen | Property |
|---|---|---|
| **Annulus fibrosus** | **Type I**, high collagen / low proteoglycan | Tensile strength, 15–25 lamellae |
| **Nucleus pulposus** | **Type II**, low collagen / high proteoglycan, 88% water | Compressibility; avascular (endplate diffusion) |

### Root anatomy

**Lumbar** — **vertical** root: L5 runs under L5 pedicle (match) → **paracentral and foraminal disc at same level hit different roots**

**Cervical** — horizontal root: C6 under C5 pedicle (mismatch) → central + foraminal hit same root

![Vertical lumbar vs horizontal cervical roots](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob2035-diagram-depicting-the-vertical-orientation-of-the-lumbar-ner.jpg)

## 3 Presentation

**History** — sudden pain after lifting · vibration + sitting with bending/rotation · relief **supine with hips + knees flexed**

**Radicular pain** — **worse sitting**, cough, Valsalva, sneeze · better standing · walking doesn't worsen

**Posture** — **leans away** from the radiculopathy side (opens foramen) · **sciatic scoliosis** (list)

**Sciatic list (TO)** — **shoulder (lateral) disc → leans away** from pain · **axillary disc → leans toward** pain · **leg pain > back pain = hallmark** · relieved supine with hips + knees flexed

**Cauda equina** — 1–10% (central disc): bilateral leg pain, weakness, saddle anaesthesia, bowel/bladder → **emergent discectomy within 48 h**

### Root localisation

| Root | Motor | Sensory | Reflex |
|---|---|---|---|
| **L3** | **Hip adduction**, knee extension | **Anteromedial thigh**, near **medial femoral epicondyle**, groin | — |
| **L4** | **Ankle DF (L4 > L5)**, quadriceps (**single-leg sit-to-stand**) · **can't heel walk** (TO) | Lateral thigh → across knee → **medial leg/malleolus** | **↓ Patellar** |
| **L5** | **EHL** (great toe extension), **foot inversion (tib post)**, **hip abduction (glut medius → Trendelenburg)** | Anterolateral leg, **dorsum of foot, first web space** | — |
| **S1** | **Plantar flexion** (10 single-leg calf raises) | Posterior calf, **lateral foot** | **↓ Achilles** |

**Tibialis anterior** — L4 or L5 dominant (\~50:50); L3-4 paracentral → **traversing L4** → weak tib anterior ± quads (OB)

### Paracentral vs far lateral

| Location | Share | Root hit | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Central** | — | Back pain; **CES** | Surgical emergency |
| **Paracentral (posterolateral)** | **90–95%** | **Traversing (lower)** | **L4-5 → L5** · L5-S1 → S1 · L3-4 → L4 |
| **Foraminal / far lateral** | **5–10%** | **Exiting (upper)** + **DRG** → more severe pain | **L4-5 → L4** (TO) · **L5-S1 → L5** · L3-4 → L3 |
| Axillary | — | Exiting + traversing | — |

![Far lateral disc → exiting root](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob2035-fld-illustration.jpg)

**Trendelenburg gait** — L5 → gluteus medius → **far lateral L5-S1** (TO) or **posterolateral L4-5** (TO answer "L4L5 herniated disc with positive Trendelenburg test")

**L4-5 extraforaminal disc** — L4 → **heel walk not possible** (TO) · pain worse standing + extension, eased by flexion

**First dorsal web-space numbness** — L5 → **L4-5 disc prolapse** (TO)

**Weak great-toe dorsiflexion — causes** — L5 root, peroneal palsy, sciatic lesion \
✗ Wrong options: **Morton's neuroma** (TO)

### L5 radiculopathy vs peroneal palsy

| | L5 radiculopathy | Common peroneal palsy |
|---|---|---|
| Ankle inversion (tib post, L5 + tibial n.) | **Weak** | **Intact** |
| DF, toe extension, eversion | Weak | Weak |
| Hip abduction | Weak | Intact |
| Cough / Valsalva / SLR | Worse | No effect |

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Provocative tests

| Test | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Straight leg raise** | Reproduces leg pain at **30–70°** hip flexion · **L4, L5, S1** tension · **most predictive finding for a good surgical candidate** · false positive in hip disease (TO) |
| SLR range (TO) | **35–75°** · tests: bowstring (popliteal pressure), **sitting SLR / slump**, femoral stretch (L2–L4) |
| **Laségue** | Orthobullets: = SLR · **TO: two-step — SLR, then flex the knee → sciatic pain disappears (hip pain doesn't)** · "Laségue = SLR" is the **incorrect statement** (TO) — read the options |
| **Bragard (reinforcement)** | SLR to pain, lower slightly, **dorsiflex ankle** → pain returns · makes SLR **specific for nerve compression** (TO answer over crossed SLR) |
| **Crossed (well-leg) SLR** | **Less sensitive, more specific** · large **central** disc indenting thecal sac (TO) |
| **Bowstring (McNab)** | SLR to pain, flex knee, **press tibial nerve in popliteal fossa** → pain |
| **Femoral stretch (Wassermann)** | **Prone, knee flexed, hip extended** → anterior thigh pain · **L2, L3** |
| Kernig | Neck flexion + hip flexion + leg extension |
| Naffziger | Jugular compression → cough → pain |
| Milgram | Supine straight-leg elevation held 30 s |
| ✗ Wrong options: apprehension · Murphy | Shoulder/patella instability · gallbladder (TO) |

### Key radiology findings

| Study | Use / finding |
|---|---|
| **X-ray AP + lateral** | Usually normal · loss of lordosis (spasm), disc-height loss, sciatic scoliosis · counts transitional vertebrae (localisation) · poor sensitivity |
| Flexion-extension | **Instability → adds fusion** |
| **MRI without gadolinium** | **Modality of choice** · if pain **> 1 month** failing non-op or **red flags** · many asymptomatic findings → correlate · distinguishes **synovial facet cyst** |
| **MRI with gadolinium** | Revision: **scar enhances**, **recurrent disc doesn't** |
| **CT myelogram** | Can't have MRI (pacemaker) · 93% accurate · **misses foraminal/extraforaminal** discs |

**Read every slice (TO)** — foraminal/extraforaminal discs invisible on mid-sagittal cut · check **parasagittal "keyhole" foramen** (root dot in bright fat; **lost fat = compression**) · 3 axial cuts per level: **disc, foraminal, pedicle** · sequestrated fragment may sit **behind the pedicle**

**Whole-spine screening (TO)** — **\~30% have a second lesion** (tandem stenosis, skip TB, metastases) · **1.5 T** standard, avoid low-field 0.2–0.5 T

**Disc location (TO)** — sagittal 3: **at disc, up-migrated, down-migrated** · axial 4: **central, paracentral, foraminal, far lateral** · commonest **L4-5 > L5-S1 > L3-4** · new terms "right/left central"

**Migrated fragment (TO)** — **down-migrated → axilla** of root (between dura and root) · **up-migrated → shoulder** · hook, don't pull · markedly up-migrated disc may hit the root above

### Red flags → MRI (TO)

**Red flags** — **unexplained fever**, IVDU, **age < 20**, **significant trauma**, cancer history, CES \
✗ Wrong options: **lack of family support** (yellow flag) (TO)

![Red and yellow flags of backache](../../MCQ/attachments/TO17134965-1.png)

### Classifications

| Morphology | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Protrusion** | Eccentric bulge, **annulus intact** |
| **Extrusion** | Through annulus, **still continuous** with disc space |
| **Sequestered (free)** | Through annulus, **no continuity**; migrates up/down |

**Protrusion vs extrusion (TO)** — protrusion: **base wider than dome**, < 25% circumference · extrusion: **dome wider than base** or migrates on sagittal · sequestration: no connection (may lie behind pedicle / next level)

**Bulge vs protrusion (TO)** — **bulge > 90°** (> 25%) of circumference · **protrusion < 90°**, base wider · extrusion: neck narrower, **subligamentous (PLL lifted)** · anterior herniation: usually no root

| Containment | Definition |
|---|---|
| Contained | Beneath PLL |
| Uncontained | Dorsal to PLL |

| Timing | Duration |
|---|---|
| **Acute** | **< 3–6 months** (surgical outcome depends on chronicity) |
| Chronic | > 6 months |

## 5 Management

```
Lumbar disc herniation
├─ CES → EMERGENT DISCECTOMY (within 48 h)
├─ No CES, no significant weakness → NSAIDs + ACTIVITY MODIFICATION + PT
│      (mild non-progressive weakness still non-op)
├─ Failed ~6 wks, severe pain → TRANSFORAMINAL SELECTIVE ROOT INJECTION
└─ Persistent disabling pain > 6 wks / progressive weakness → MICRODISCECTOMY

**Natural history (TO)** — **\~60% recover within 6 wks** · rest 2–7 days · NSAIDs, pregabalin · MRI if **> 6 wks**, deficit, red flags · surgery after **6–8 wks** failed care · ideal candidate: leg > back pain, **MRI matches clinical**, single level

**Microlumbar discectomy (TO)** — **gold standard** · prone, flexed hips + knees (**"French" position**) · \~2.5 cm midline incision · flavectomy → root → fragment · not for instability / multilevel disease · endoscopic (transforaminal/interlaminar) for contained discs

**Degeneration sequence (TO)** — **endplate sclerosis → ↓ diffusion → desiccation → annular fissure → prolapse** · peak 30–50 yrs · recurrence \~**5%** (TO) vs **5–15%** (Orthobullets)
       ├─ Paracentral → MIDLINE LAMINOTOMY + LIMITED DISCECTOMY
       └─ Far lateral → PARASPINAL (WILTSE) APPROACH
```

### Natural history & non-operative care

**Natural history** — **90% improve** within 3 months without surgery · less likely if still symptomatic at 6 wks · TO stem: **70% resolve with NSAIDs + PT**

**Resorption** — herniation **shrinks over time** · **sequestered fragments resorb most** · by **macrophage phagocytosis** + enzymatic degradation

**Non-op predictors** — good: higher education, no radiculopathy · poor: **BMI > 30**, symptoms > 6 months before treatment

**Medications** — NSAIDs, muscle relaxants · oral steroid → modest function gain, no pain gain · narcotics 2–3 days only (worse surgical outcomes)

**PT** — from \~3 wks · **extension exercises** (✓ acute posterior-thigh radicular pain) · traction, careful manipulation

**Early (2-day, 2-week) radicular pain, no deficit** — **anti-inflammatories + activity modification**\
✗ Wrong options: MRI · surgery · injection

**Selective nerve root injection** — 2nd line after \~6 wks · **transforaminal best for far lateral** · durable relief **\~50%** (vs \~90% surgery) · **best with extruded discs** · steroid adds nothing over lidocaine

**Picking the injection level** — target the **compressed root's foramen**: lateral calf → **L5 transforaminal** · anterior thigh with L3-4 foraminal disc → **L3-L4 transforaminal (L3 root)** · L5-S1 foramen block → **dorsal foot + great toe**\
✗ Wrong options: lateral foot = S1 foramen · shin = L4

**Facet joint injection** — relief on **two occasions** → indication for **facet ablation**; medial branch block predicts better

### Operative criteria (SPORT)

| Indication | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Persistent disabling radicular pain** | **> 6 wks**, failed non-op ± injection · **refractory radicular pain = clearest indication** · better if done **within 2 months** |
| **Progressive / significant weakness** | — |
| **Cauda equina** | Emergent |
| No | Back pain alone · no instability → no fusion (✗ Wrong options: ALIF · PLIF) |

| Outcome | Finding |
|---|---|
| Surgery vs non-op | **Better pain + function at 4 yrs** · **early relief sustained to 2 yrs** · equal disability at 5 yrs · long term equivalent |
| As-treated analysis | **Significant benefit of surgery** (pain, function, satisfaction) |
| Return to work | Same |
| **Workers' compensation** | **Less symptom relief, less QoL gain** at 5 yrs |

| Better surgical result | Worse surgical result |
|---|---|
| **Leg pain dominant**, **positive SLR**, weakness matching MRI | **Workers' comp** |
| **Married**, **age > 41**, **no joint problems** | Smoking |
| **Progressively worsening symptoms** | Chronic headaches, depression |
| Paracentral / foraminal, **caudal levels** (L5-S1 > L2-3) | Central / extraforaminal |
| Severe leg pain, short duration, younger, active | — |

### Surgical approach

| Pathology | Operation |
|---|---|
| **Paracentral** | **Midline approach** (between spinous process and **multifidus**) → laminotomy + **limited discectomy** (e.g. L4 laminotomy, partial flavum resection, medial facetectomy) |
| Lateral (not far lateral) protrusion, e.g. L1-2 | **Midline microdiscectomy** (OB) |
| **Far lateral** (e.g. L4-5 → L4, medial malleolus) | **Paraspinal muscle-splitting approach of Wiltse** (multifidus–longissimus) · outside-in, **preserves facet** · tubular/crank retractor |
| Recurrent paracentral | Revision via **midline** |

![Far lateral L5-S1 disc](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob2035-l5-s1-far-lateral-disc.jpg)

**Fragment excision vs subtotal discectomy** — fragment excision: **less long-term back pain**, **more reherniation at 2 yrs** · limited beats complete discectomy

**Technique** — microscope = loupes · endoscopic → lower infection · all MIS techniques similar

**Rehab** — **early return to high-intensity exercise (\~4 wks)** · Orthobullets 4–6 wks\
✗ Wrong options: bed rest · avoid exercise 6–8 wks

### Endoscopic anatomy (TO)

**Kambin's triangle** — answer not keyed by source · options: exiting root / superior endplate / traversing root vs alternatives — the commonly taught boundaries are **exiting root (hypotenuse), superior endplate of the lower vertebra (base), traversing root / dural sac (height)**

**Kambin's triangle (TO definition)** — **anterior: exiting root** · **inferior: superior endplate of lower vertebra** · **posterior: SAP of lower vertebra** · **medial: traversing root + dural sac**

| Endoscopic approach (TO) | Use |
|---|---|
| **Transforaminal** (Kambin's triangle) | **Foraminal / far-lateral** discs, recurrent discs, facet cysts, discitis, high-risk patients (**local + sedation**) |
| **Interlaminar** | **Central / paracentral, esp. L5–S1** (iliac crest blocks transforaminal) · over-the-top contralateral decompression |
| **UBE (biportal)** | Two portals, arthroscopy instruments, cheaper, less radiation |

**Transforaminal technique (TO)** — **outside-in preferred** over inside-out (sees root) · entry **8 cm (L1-2), 10 cm (L3-4), 12 cm (L4-5/L5-S1)** from midline · needle stops at **medial pedicular line** · root pulsation = decompressed · history: **Kambin & Sampson 1986**, Hijikata 1989

**Endoscopy limits (TO)** — dural repair not feasible · difficult with dorsal / highly migrated fragments · residual fragments, epidural haematoma, fluid overload

### Recurrent herniation & postoperative course

**Definition** — recurrent sciatica at the operated level after a **6-month pain-free interval** (ipsi- or contralateral)

**Rate** — **5–15%** · **SPORT reoperation 15% at 8 yrs, with or without durotomy** · protective: discrete herniation, **annular defect < 6 mm**

**Treatment** — non-op first → **revision discectomy** · **outcomes = primary** (equal leg pain + function) · fusion only if **instability**\
✗ Wrong options: PLIF · ALIF · disc replacement · interspinous spacer

| Post-op MRI | Diagnosis | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 3 wks, recurrent leg pain + radicular signs, no enhancement of disc | **Recurrent herniation** | **Revision laminotomy + discectomy** |
| 8 wks, back pain only, no neuro, **Modic** endplate change | Spondylosis | **Physical therapy** |
| Disc fluid + enhancement | Discitis | Biopsy + IV antibiotics |

### Chronic pain & central sensitisation

**Pain diagram** — **screening tool** for pain sensitisation, psychosocial load, pain-resource use · correlates **positively** with anxiety/depression · not sensitive or specific for surgical benefit

**Central sensitisation** — **amplified CNS neural signalling → pain hypersensitivity** · multiple surgeries, solid fusion, negative infection work-up, **PI-LL 9° (normal)**\
✗ Wrong options: coronal deformity · PJK · adjacent segment

**Far lateral disc + DRG** — DRG compression → **more severe pain** than paracentral\
✗ Wrong options: CBT most cost-effective · standard laminotomy · stimulator · ALIF

**Chronic discogenic back pain** — **CBT added to non-op care improves function** (= surgery) · disc replacement no clinically significant gain · fusion **\~60% satisfaction** · discography **↑ herniation** at tested level · successful treatment **improves depression**

### Return to play

**Athletes** — **more games played before injury → better return** · **older age → worse** · RTP \~80%, only \~60% active at 2 yrs · **football: less likely to return but greatest benefit from surgery**

## 6 Complications

| Complication | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Dural tear** | **0–4%** (Orthobullets) · **1%** (OB) · watertight repair · **no effect on long-term outcome** |
| **Recurrent HNP** | 5–15% |
| Wound infection | Up to 3% · epidural abscess 0.3% |
| **Discitis** | **2.3%** → IV antibiotics ± I&D |
| **Epidural fibrosis** | Radicular symptoms; **3.2× recurrent radiculopathy** after revision |
| Chronic LBP | Central sensitisation; **Modic changes** |
| **Vascular catastrophe** | Through **anterior annulus** → aorta / IVC |
| **Instability** | Over-resection of lamina / **pars** → fusion |

### Post-op neuro deficit timing (TO)

| Timing | Cause |
|---|---|
| **Immediate** | **Battered root** (retraction), screw / decompression injury |
| **Delayed, day 3 after TLIF** | **Haematoma** (✓) or gel foam → **immediate re-exploration + evacuation** · prevent with haemostasis + drain |

### Fusion approach complications

| Approach / implant | At risk |
|---|---|
| **ALIF transperitoneal** | **Retrograde ejaculation** (4–10×) — **superior hypogastric plexus at L5-S1** → internal sphincter fails |
| ALIF retroperitoneal | **Ureter** |
| Lateral / XLIF / OLIF | **Lumbar plexus** |
| **Long S1 pedicle screw** | **L5 root** on the **anterior sacral ala** |

**After posterior lumbar fusion — motion most preserved** — **axial rotation** (mostly thoracic) (TO)
