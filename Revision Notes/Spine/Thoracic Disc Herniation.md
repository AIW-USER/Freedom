# Thoracic Disc Herniation

*Spine · 64 / 69*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Frequency** — **\~1% of all HNP** (Orthobullets) · **0.25–1%** (TO) · rare: limited motion, short discs, rib cage

**Age** — **4th–6th decades** (Orthobullets, TO) · 3rd–5th (OB)

**Level** — **lower third** · **T11-12 commonest** (maximal mobility, thin PLL) · **75% at T8–12** · T1–5 only 1% · flexion + torsion concentrate T10–L1 (floating ribs 11, 12)

**Risk factor** — **Scheuermann's disease** (also ↑ neuro deficit)

**Asymptomatic prevalence** — abnormalities 73%, **frank herniation 37%**, cord compression 29% (Orthobullets) · **\~40%** (OB) — read the options

**Natural history** — **most symptomatic cases resolve non-operatively**; most imaging lesions never cause symptoms; large herniations shrink

### Classification

| Type | Definition |
|---|---|
| Bulge | Annulus intact |
| Extruded | Through annulus, contained by PLL |
| Sequestered | Free in canal |
| Location | Central · posterolateral · lateral |

## 3 Presentation

**Commonest symptom** — **axial back / chest pain** (dorsal backache, TO)

**Radicular** — **band-like** chest/abdominal pain along the intercostal nerve, **around or straight through to the anterior chest wall** · **T10 dermatome** commonest (TO) · upper thoracic + lateral discs · arm pain with T2–5

**Myelopathy** — **LE hyperreflexia with normal UE reflexes** (T11-12 cord), clonus, Babinski, wide-based spastic gait, paraparesis, rectal tone · **bowel/bladder 15–20%** · sexual dysfunction

**Horner's** — T2–5 herniation

**Work-up of LE UMN signs with normal UE** — **thoracic MRI** · hip OA + wide-based gait → **thoracic + lumbar MRI before THA**\
✗ Wrong options: cervical/lumbar surgery

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Study | Fact |
|---|---|
| Lateral X-ray | Disc narrowing, **calcification** |
| **MRI** | **Most useful** · tumour, intradural pathology, myelomalacia · may **under-show calcified disc** · high false positives |
| CT / CT myelogram | Calcified component |

## 5 Management

```
Thoracic disc herniation
├─ Axial / radicular pain → REST, PT, NSAIDs, gabapentin, injections (6–12 wks)
└─ Myelopathy / progressive deficit / intolerable pain → DISCECTOMY ± FUSION
       ├─ Central (calcified) → TRANSTHORACIC (± VATS)
       ├─ Lateral, extruded, sequestered → COSTOTRANSVERSECTOMY / LATERAL EXTRACAVITARY
       └─ Posterior → TRANSPEDICULAR + instrumented fusion
       (NOT LAMINECTOMY ALONE)
```

### Operative criteria

| Indication |
|---|
| **Myelopathy from the disc** (sensory loss + clumsy gait → **discectomy**), especially progressive |
| Persistent intolerable pain |
| ✗ Wrong option: most cases need surgery (TO EXCEPT) |

### Approaches

| Approach | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Posterior laminectomy** | **Avoid — highest neurological deterioration** — cord can't be retracted; thoracic kyphosis drapes cord over disc |
| **Transthoracic** (transpleural, T4–T12; VATS) | **Best for central discs** · T12-L1 needs diaphragm takedown · complication **intercostal neuralgia** |
| **Costotransversectomy** | Lateral, extruded, sequestered · no pleural violation · hard for calcified discs/osteophytes |
| Lateral extracavitary | More bone resection + blood loss, less neuro risk than laminectomy |
| **Transpedicular + instrumented fusion** | Posterior option for myelopathy with cord signal change |

**Thoracic stenosis (degenerative, flavum/facet)** — **laminectomy** appropriate (posterior compression) — differs from disc

**FVC** — transthoracic not safe with FVC 20%

## 6 Complications

**Intercostal neuralgia** — **commonest after transthoracic / endoscopic** surgery (portal compression)

## X vs Y

| | Thoracic disc | Thoracic stenosis |
|---|---|---|
| Compression | **Anterior** (disc, often calcified) | **Posterior** (flavum, facets) |
| Approach | Anterior / lateral / transpedicular | **Laminectomy** |

## How it's asked

**"Commonest thoracic disc level?"** → **T11-12** (TO)

**"Large T11-12 disc with imbalance — reflexes?"** → **normal UE, hyperreflexic LE**

**"Posterolateral thoracic disc pain pattern?"** → **around/through the chest to the anterior wall**

**"Thoracic disc herniation — true?"** → **found in 40% of asymptomatic people**

**"New LE weakness, UMN signs, normal arms — next?"** → **thoracic MRI**

**"Thoracic discs typically occur?"** → **lower third**

**"Hip pain + wide-based gait + LE weakness — next?"** → **thoracic + lumbar MRI, spine referral**

**"Thoracic disc — EXCEPT?"** → **most cases need surgery** (TO)

**"2 wks dermatomal rib-cage pain, normal neuro?"** → **PT + NSAIDs**

**"Axial pain, thoracic disc — true?"** → **usually resolves with conservative care**

**"T7-8 disc, sensory loss, clumsy gait?"** → **discectomy** (TO)

**"Endoscopic transthoracic surgery — commonest complication?"** → **intercostal neuralgia**

**"29-yr thoracic myelopathy from disc?"** → **thoracic discectomy**

**"Thoracic disc + myelopathy — approach with most neuro morbidity?"** → **posterior laminectomy**

**"Thoracic myelopathy, cord signal change, normal cervical MRI?"** → **laminectomy + transpedicular discectomy + instrumented fusion**

**"Thoracic stenosis with claudication, hyperreflexia, incontinence?"** → **laminectomy**
