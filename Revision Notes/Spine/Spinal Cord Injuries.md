# Spinal Cord Injuries

*Spine · 54 / 69*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Incidence** — 11,000/yr US · **incomplete tetraplegia 34% (central cord commonest)** · complete paraplegia 25% · complete tetraplegia 22% · incomplete paraplegia 17%

**Distribution** — bimodal: young high-energy · elderly minor trauma + spondylotic stenosis · **50% cervical** · **MVA 50%**, falls, GSW · **3–25% occur after the initial trauma** (poor immobilisation/transport)

### Pathophysiology

**Primary injury** — direct, **irreversible**

**Secondary injury** — ↓ perfusion, **lipid peroxidation**, free radicals/cytokines, apoptosis → target of methylprednisolone

**Associated** — head injury, **non-contiguous spine fractures**, **vertebral artery injury** (atlas fractures, facet dislocations; usually asymptomatic unilateral; **MRA** least invasive; **stent only if basilar insufficiency symptoms**)

## 3 Presentation

### Spinal vs neurogenic vs hypovolaemic shock

| | Spinal shock | Neurogenic shock | Hypovolaemic |
|---|---|---|---|
| BP | ↓ | ↓ | ↓ |
| Pulse | **Bradycardia** | **Bradycardia** | **Tachycardia** |
| **Bulbocavernosus reflex** | **Absent** | Variable / **can be intact** | Variable |
| Motor | **Flaccid areflexic** | Variable | Variable |
| Mechanism | Neurons **hyperpolarised**, unresponsive | **Loss of sympathetic tone** (injury above **T6**), ↓ SVR, pooling, unopposed vagus | ↓ preload |
| Skin / urine | — | **Warm extremities**, urine output preserved (✗ Wrong options: **absent urine output**) | Cold |

**Bulbocavernosus reflex** — anal contraction on squeezing glans/clitoris or **tugging the Foley** · **its return = end of spinal shock** · conus/cauda equina injury → **may never return**\
✗ Wrong options: "returns in all cauda equina injuries" (TO)

**Spinal shock ruled out** — BCR present **or** 48 h elapsed

### Spinal shock phases

| Phase | Orthobullets | TO (Ditunno) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **0–48 h** areflexia | **0–24 h** areflexia; **delayed plantar** first, then BCR, abdominal, cremasteric |
| 2 | 1–2 days: **polysynaptic (BCR) return**, monosynaptic (patellar) absent | Day 1–3: cutaneous reflexes, DTRs absent |
| 3 | 1–4 wks hyperreflexia | Day 4–1 month: DTRs return, Babinski |
| 4 | 1–12 months spasticity | 1–12 months: hyperreflexia, **autonomic dysreflexia** |

**First reflex to return** — **delayed plantar** (TO) · classical teaching **bulbocavernosus** — read the options · order: delayed plantar → BCR → cremasteric → ankle jerk → Babinski → knee jerk

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### ASIA impairment scale

| Grade | Motor | Sensory |
|---|---|---|
| **A** Complete | None | None (incl. **S4-5**) |
| **B** Incomplete | **None** (none > 3 levels below NLI) | **Preserved** (incl. sacral) |
| **C** Incomplete | Preserved; **> half key muscles < grade 3** | Preserved |
| **D** Incomplete | Preserved; **≥ half key muscles ≥ grade 3** | Preserved |
| E | Normal | Normal |

![ASIA classification](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob2006-2006-asia-classification.jpg)

### Diagnostic criteria – classifying an injury

**Step 1 – neurologic level** — **lowest segment with normal motor + sensory both sides** (e.g. intact wrist extension, absent triceps → **C6**)

**Step 2 – complete?** — **no voluntary anal contraction + 0/5 distal motor + no perianal sensation + BCR intact** → complete

**Step 3 – grade** — any **perianal sensation = at least B** (sacral sparing → incomplete)

**BCR absent** → **cannot assign ASIA grade** (spinal shock) · prognosis by **repeated examinations over 48–72 h**\
✗ Wrong options: MRI · SSEP · EMG

**Descriptive** — **tetraplegia** (cervical: arms, trunk, legs, pelvis) · **paraplegia** (arms spared)

**ASIA B at C6 (OB)** — deltoid 5/5, long-finger + medial forearm sensation may be present, intrinsics 0/5 · no **triceps power** (2/5 OB; 3/5 in the unkeyed TO variant)

### Incomplete cord syndromes

| Syndrome | Mechanism | Deficit | Prognosis |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Central cord (commonest)** | **Hyperextension in elderly spondylotic neck** (cord pinched between body + buckled flavum) | **UE > LE**, distal > proximal (UE fibres medial), sacral sparing | Good |
| **Brown-Séquard** | Hemisection, **penetrating** | **Ipsilateral motor + proprioception/vibration**, **contralateral pain + temp** (2–3 levels below) | **Best** |
| **Anterior cord** | Hyperflexion / compression, **anterior spinal artery** (e.g. **aortic dissection**) | Motor + pain/temp lost, **proprioception preserved** | **Worst** (\~90% no recovery) |
| Posterior cord | Rare (tabes dorsalis atraumatic mimic) | Proprioception lost | — |
| Conus / cauda equina | — | — | Conus recovers better than proximal lesions |

**Rear-end collision → quadriparesis (TO)** — **extension–distraction** (whiplash mechanism)

## 5 Management

### Field & initial care

**Immobilisation** — rigid collar + spine board (transport only) · **decubiti after 30–60 min on board** · log roll · athletes: **leave helmet + shoulder pads on** until controlled simultaneous removal

**Airway** — injury **above C5** → likely intubation

**Exam** — remove collar, inspect face/scalp, rotational head posture (unilateral facet dislocation), **posterior midline tenderness** (absent in alert patient → low probability of injury), log roll, neuro exam · **seat-belt sign → flexion-distraction**

**Prevention** — DVT prophylaxis (LMWH, rotating bed, pneumatic compression) · avoid hypotension · pulmonary toilet · skin

### Neurogenic shock treatment

**TO** — IV fluids first → **vasopressors/inotropes** if persists · phenylephrine (pure α1 → reflex bradycardia) · bradycardia: **atropine**, glycopyrrolate (before suction), isoproterenol, theophylline/aminophylline

**OB** — hypotension unresponsive to initial fluids → **pressors** · Swan-Ganz-guided fluids\
✗ Wrong options: more rapid fluid → overload, CHF

### Methylprednisolone

**Current** — **not recommended**: no clear benefit, **↑ complications** (infection, GI bleed, ventilator days, LOS) · no effect on cord oedema · OB variant: "**controversial, may be given in appropriate circumstances**" — read the options

**Historical NASCIS III** — non-penetrating SCI **within 8 h** · **30 mg/kg bolus** + **5.4 mg/kg/h** for **23 h (< 3 h)** or **47 h (3–8 h)** · CI: **GSW, pregnancy, < 13 yrs, > 8 h**, peripheral nerve injury

**Presenting at 10 h** — **don't start methylprednisolone**

**GM-1** — controversial; no long-term benefit · **hypothermia** — not recommended (coagulopathy, sepsis, pneumonia, arrhythmia)

### Reduction & surgery

**Closed reduction** — **awake, alert** patient with deficit from fracture-dislocation (commonest: **bilateral facet dislocation**) · add 10 lb every 10 min with exams · **abort**: overdistraction, **neuro worsening (→ remove traction)**, failure

**TO (stable C4-5 fracture-dislocation with weakness)** — **MRI first** (cord, compression, disc)\
✗ Wrong options: immediate traction

**Decompression + stabilisation** — **most incomplete and complete SCI (except GSW)** · **early (< 24 h) improves recovery even in complete injury** · may recover 1–2 root levels · stabilise for rehab

**GSW** — **most → brace / treat extraspinal injury** (stable lateral masses, no CSF leak) · surgery for **progressive deficit with canal bullet**, **cauda equina**, **bullet in thecal sac (lead poisoning)**

**C6 SCI + spondylotic C6 fracture** — **early decompression + stabilisation alone**\
✗ Wrong options: steroids · hypothermia

### Functional level

| Level | Function |
|---|---|
| C1-3 | Ventilator; electric chair head/chin control |
| C3-4 | Ventilator → may wean; head/chin (**C4 puffer**) |
| **C5** | Deltoid, biceps · **electric wheelchair with hand control** · can't feed (no wrist extension/supination) |
| **C6** | Wrist extension + supination → feeds self · **manual wheelchair + sliding-board transfers** · drives with hand controls |
| **C7** | Triceps · **manual wheelchair + independent transfers** |
| C8-T1 | Hand dexterity · fully independent transfers |
| T2-6 | Normal UE, wheelchair |
| T7-12 | Abdominal control; bracing walk possible |
| L1-5 | Variable; aids |
| S1-5 | Walk with minimal assistance |

**Posterior deltoid → triceps transfer** — **complete C5/C6** with **5/5 deltoid + biceps, 0/5 triceps**\
✗ Wrong options: axillary nerve injury · weak deltoid

**FES** — stimulates **skeletal muscle**

**TENS** — **presynaptic inhibition in the dorsal horn** (small myelinated afferents block C-fibre input) + endorphins

## 6 Complications

| Complication | Fact |
|---|---|
| Pressure injury | Prevention: **frequent repositioning**, off board early · NPUAP 2016: "**injury**", **Arabic stages** (TO keys: "Grade II pressure injury" FNB 2022 · "Grade 2 pressure injury" — read the options) |
| VTE | Immediate prophylaxis |
| **Urosepsis** | **Common cause of death** |
| **Sinus bradycardia** | **Commonest arrhythmia** acutely |
| Orthostatic / supine hypotension, arrhythmia | Lost sympathetic control (**T6 or above**) · not **spasticity** (not sympathetic) |
| **Autonomic dysreflexia** | **Above T5-6** · headache, agitation, **hypertension** (SBP ↑ ≥ 20%), bradycardia, sweating · **unchecked visceral stimulus** → check **Foley**, disimpact, X-ray for **occult fracture** (swollen thigh) |
| **Major depression** | **\~11% (5–20%)** · suicidal ideation |
| Shoulder | Long-term paraplegia → **4× rotator cuff tears** |
| Post-traumatic instability | Missed adjacent ligamentous slip below construct → **revise + extend fusion** |

### Prognosis

**Complete** — **80% gain 1 root level**, 20% gain 2 · **1% full recovery** · **ASIA A: least recovery, highest lifetime cost** (ASIA B 46.6% marked recovery)

**Incomplete** — more sparing + faster early recovery = better · plateau rarely resumes

**MRI predicting complete injury** — **intramedullary cord haematoma**\
✗ Wrong options: oedema · ALL disruption · prevertebral oedema

**SCIWORA** — commoner < 10 yrs · **severity of initial deficit = best predictor**

### Dural tears (TO)

**Commonest site** — **lateral**, then root sheath, axilla · **suture whenever possible** · sealants (Tachosil, fibrin glue) if not · small unsuturable → tight fascia + skin\
✗ Wrong options: posterior

### SIADH vs cerebral salt wasting (TO)

| | SIADH | Cerebral salt wasting |
|---|---|---|
| **Volume** | **Euvolaemic / hypervolaemic** | **Hypovolaemic** |
| Serum osm | < 275 | ↓ |
| Urine osm | > 100 | — |
| Urine Na | **> 40 mEq/L** | High |
| Criteria exclude | Diuretics, thyroid/adrenal disease (✗ Wrong options: **renal insufficiency is not a criterion**) | — |
| Supplementary | Uric acid < 4, corrected by fluid restriction, ↑ ADH | — |
| Treatment | Na < 125 acute/severe → **3% saline** + furosemide · correct **≤ 8 mEq/L per 24 h** (osmotic demyelination) · chronic → **fluid restriction**, demeclocycline, conivaptan | Saline |

### Whiplash (TO)

**Mechanism** — rear impact → lower cervical **hyperextension** + upper flexion (S-shape) · commonest MVA injury (up to 83%) · "WAD"

**Poor outcome predictors** — **rapid-onset pain**, **severe early pain**, hospital admission, **radiation to arm**, **headache** · **least predictive: pain isolated to neck** (TO key)

**Management** — MRI not initially (false positives) · **early mobilisation** > soft collar
