# Femoral nerve

*Basic Science · 35 / 108*

## 1 Basics

### Anatomy

**Course** — runs **within the fascial sheath of iliopsoas** → vulnerable to iliopsoas haematoma

## 3 Presentation

### Iliopsoas haematoma

**Setting** — **over-anticoagulation** (warfarin), alcoholism → spontaneous bleed into iliopsoas sheath → compartment-like syndrome

**Features** — rapid **LLQ abdominal/groin pain → anterior thigh** · **"psoas sign"** (pain on resisted hip flexion) · **quadriceps weakness → knee buckling** · progressive femoral motor loss

**Management** — rapid evaluation · **release iliopsoas fascia + evacuate haematoma** to prevent permanent palsy

## X vs Y

| Cause of acute quadriceps weakness | Clue |
|---|---|
| **Iliopsoas haematoma** | Anticoagulated · **abdominal pain** · psoas sign |
| L3–4 posterolateral / L4–5 far-lateral disc | Back/leg pain, **no abdominal pain** |
| Epidural haematoma | No abdominal pain · cord/cauda signs |
| Femoral pseudoaneurysm | Palpable mass · rare cause of palsy |

## How it's asked

**"Anticoagulated alcoholic, LLQ pain to anterior thigh, knee buckling, quads 1/5, pain on resisted hip flexion?"** → **spontaneous bleed into iliopsoas sheath**\
✗ Wrong options: disc herniation · epidural haematoma · pseudoaneurysm
