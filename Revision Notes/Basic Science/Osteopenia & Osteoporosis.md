# Osteopenia & Osteoporosis

*Basic Science · 75 / 108*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Prevalence** — **10 million** Americans · **200 million** worldwide · 34 million US osteopenia

**Fragility fractures / yr** — **1.5 million** → 700,000 vertebral · 300,000 hip · 200,000 wrist

**Commonest fragility fracture** — ✓ **vertebral body > peritrochanteric (hip) > distal radius**\
✗ Wrong options: "distal radius more common than VCF"

**Peak age** — wrist 50–60 · vertebral 60–70 · hip 70–80

**M:F** — **1:4** · men → **secondary osteoporosis in 60%** (hypogonadism, glucocorticoid excess, alcoholism)

**Bone mass** — peak bone mass attained **before 30 yr** · failure to attain it = major cause of osteoporosis

**Ageing** — ✓ **trabecular (cancellous) bone loses strength more than cortical** · cancellous ↓ **8.5%/decade** vs cortical **5%/decade** · volume fraction (apparent density) explains most of the loss

### Pathophysiology

**Osteoporosis** — ✓ **quantitative** defect · **↓ bone quantity, normal bone quality (mineralisation)**\
✗ Wrong options: qualitative (= osteomalacia)

**Causes** — failure to build peak mass · bone loss later in life · ↓ loading (non-weight-bearing)

**Histology** — **thinned trabeculae** · **↓ osteon size** · enlarged Haversian + marrow spaces

![Osteoporotic bone — thinned trabeculae](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob9032-histo-1.jpg)

| Osteoclast ruffled border | Agents |
|---|---|
| **↑ (activated)** | **PTH** · **1,25(OH)₂ vit D₃** · **prostaglandin E** |
| **Flattened** | **Bisphosphonates** · **calcitonin** |

### Disuse osteopenia — Wolff's law

**Wolff's law** — ✓ bone densifies under repetitive load, ↓ density with ↓ load → **disuse osteopenia** after non-weight-bearing (e.g. bilateral calcaneus fractures) · dense calcar = load path

| ✗ Wrong option | What it actually describes |
|---|---|
| Hydraulic stiffening | Tissue fluid stiffens skeleton → ↑ strength |
| Hooke's law | Stress–strain of a solid |
| Hueter-Volkmann law | Compression inhibits physeal growth |
| Delpech's law | Growth across physis |

### Genes & lab markers

**Gene polymorphisms** — **calcitonin receptor** · **estrogen receptor-1** · **vitamin D receptor** · **COL1A1** (+ >45 others) · not **COMP** (= multiple epiphyseal dysplasia) · no single cause

**Labs** — generally **normal** in osteoporosis · **↓ 25-OH vitamin D** = most likely abnormal lab after low-energy fracture · other labs exclude secondary causes\
✗ Wrong options: hydroxyproline, PTHrP, Sox-9, LRP5

**Vitamin D** — D₃ → 25-OH D (liver) → **1,25-(OH)₂ D** (active) or 24,25-(OH)₂ D (inactive) in kidney · deficient **<20 ng/mL** · not diagnostic of osteoporosis

**Vitamin D replacement (TO)** — ✓ **↑ 25-OH D and ↑ 1,25-(OH)₂ D, ↓ sclerostin** **(TO)** · osteocytes have 1,25-D receptors → ↓ sclerostin synthesis → ↑ bone formation **(TO)**

**Bone remodelling (TO)** — peak mass at **30 yr** · resorption ~**10 days**, formation ~**3 months** · up to **20%** of skeleton remodelled/yr **(TO)**

| Bone turnover markers (TO) | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Formation** (osteoblast) | **P1NP / P1CP** (type 1 collagen propeptides) · **ALP** (total, bone-specific) · **osteocalcin** |
| **Resorption** (osteoclast) | **CTX-1, NTX-1** (telopeptides) · hydroxyproline · pyridinoline / deoxypyridinoline · bone sialoprotein · **TRAP** · **cathepsin K** |
| Osteocyte activity | RANKL · OPG · Dickkopf-1 · sclerostin |
| **Reference markers (IOF)** | ✓ **serum CTX-1** (resorption) + **serum P1NP** (formation) **(TO)** |

**BTM uses (TO)** — fracture risk (↑ BTM ∝ risk, independent of BMD) · **monitor compliance/response** — resorption markers **fall >40% within 3 mo** of bisphosphonate, formation markers fall over 6–12 mo · denosumab → very rapid fall in resorption markers · guide restart after drug holiday · **circadian variation** — time assays **(TO)**

**Response thresholds (TO)** — **>30–40% ↓ CTX** (antiresorptive) or **>30% ↑ P1NP** (anabolic) at 3–6 mo = response **(TO)**

## 3 Presentation

**Risk factors for osteoporosis** — increasing age · postmenopausal · long-term ↓ calcium · steroids · alcohol · smoking · low BMI

| Drugs ↑ osteoporosis risk | Not associated |
|---|---|
| **Oral glucocorticoids** · androgen deprivation · aromatase inhibitors · **protease inhibitors** · **SSRIs** · prolactin-raising **antiepileptics (phenytoin)** · cytotoxics | ✓ **NSAIDs** · cephalexin · simvastatin · glipizide · allopurinol |

**Phenytoin mechanism** — **hepatic CYP450 induction → ↑ vit D catabolism** · direct osteoblast inhibition · ↓ Ca absorption · ↑ homocysteine · PTH resistance · ↓ sex hormones · ↓ vit K

**Disease associations** — hyperparathyroidism · thyrotoxicosis · type I DM · hypogonadism · malnutrition / malabsorption · immobilisation · **RA** · alcoholism · vit D deficiency · multiple myeloma

**Pelvic ring insufficiency fracture** — risk: **osteoporosis, corticosteroids, RA, THA with constrained liner** (redistributes force to pelvis) · not **ceramic bearings** · groin pain in elderly · treat bed rest + analgesia

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

**X-ray** — thinned cortices · loss of trabecular bone · kyphosis · **codfish vertebra** · views: lateral spine, AP pelvis/hip

**X-ray sensitivity** — ✓ loss not apparent until **30–40%** (Orthobullets/OB5227) · **40%** (OB8542) — read the options · normal X-ray does not exclude osteopenia

**DEXA** — ✓ **most accurate, least radiation** · **lumbar L2–L4** + hip (neck, trochanter, intertrochanter) · measures **axial AND appendicular** skeleton\
✗ Wrong options: "axial only"

**Other densitometry** — **SPA** reliable in appendicular only (spine unreliable) · **QCT ~10× DEXA radiation** · conventional **MRI** ≈ QCT accuracy without radiation · **QCT** also used to monitor therapy (cost/radiation limit use) **(TO)**

**After low-energy fracture** — ✓ **DEXA** (✗ Wrong options: MRI pelvis, urine electrophoresis, CT, bone scan) · old multilevel VCFs on MRI, no acute pain → ✓ **DEXA** then medical Rx (✗ Wrong options: brace, cement, fusion)

![DEXA report — postmenopausal woman](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob9032-dexa-scan-report-for-post-menopausal-white-female.jpg)

### Diagnostic criteria (WHO 1994)

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **T-score** | BMD vs **young normal controls** (peak bone mass, 25–30-yr-old same sex/ethnicity) |
| **Z-score** | BMD vs **age-, sex-, race-matched** controls |
| **Normal** | **T ≥ −1.0** (e.g. T −0.7 = normal) |
| **Osteopenia** | **T −1.0 to −2.5** |
| **Osteoporosis** | **T ≤ −2.5** (e.g. −2.6, −3.5 = osteoporosis regardless of other factors) |

**Which score** — **T-score for postmenopausal women** (lowest of spine, femoral neck, trochanter, total hip; not peripheral sites) · **Z-score for premenopausal women, young men, children** → metabolic bone disease in young \
✗ Wrong options: osteopenia/osteoporosis "by Z-score"

**WHO limits** — no data to define criteria for men or other ethnic groups · T-score tells nothing about metastases

**Vit D level** — low but not diagnostic · DEXA remains the diagnostic test

**Biopsy** — after **tetracycline labelling** · rules out osteomalacia

### Type I vs Type II

| | **Type I (postmenopausal)** | **Type II (senile)** |
|---|---|---|
| Age | 50–70 | >70 |
| Bone | Almost exclusively **trabecular** | Trabecular > cortical |
| Fractures | **Distal radius, vertebral** | **Hip, pelvis** |
| Calcium | Net negative (↓ gut absorption, ↑ urinary loss) | Poor absorption |
| Vit D | ↓ total (not free) 1,25-D | — |

## 5 Management

### Risk factors & fracture prediction

**Strongest predictor of future fragility fracture** — ✓ **prior fragility fracture**\
✗ Wrong options: T < −2.5, low vit D, family history, prednisone

**Future vertebral fracture** — ✓ **prior vertebral fracture** (~**4×**; risk ↑ with number) > prior hip/wrist · **2 previous VCFs = strongest predictor** in postmenopausal women\
✗ Wrong options: T −2.8, hip fracture in 6 mo, age >75, bisphosphonate

**After 1 VCF** — **5× 2nd vertebral** · **5× hip** · **15% ↑ 5-yr mortality** · morbidity: back pain, height loss, balance, restrictive lung disease, pneumonia

**After hip fracture** — **20% ↑ mortality** · **men > women** · only **1/3** regain prior function · up to **10× 2nd hip fracture**

**Baseline risk** — 65-yr-old white woman, no risk factors → **10% 10-yr fragility fracture risk**

**FRAX** — ✓ **Fracture Risk Assessment tool** · WHO **2008** (Kanis, Sheffield) · **10-yr probability** of **hip** and **major osteoporotic fracture** (proximal humerus, wrist, hip, clinical vertebral) · with/without **femoral neck BMD** **(TO)**

**FRAX inputs** — age · sex · race · height · weight · BMI · prior fragility fracture · **parental hip fracture** · oral glucocorticoids · **RA** · secondary osteoporosis · current smoking · alcohol **≥3 units/day** **(TO)**

**FRAX does not include** — falls risk · **bone turnover markers** · dietary calcium · vitamin D deficiency **(TO)** · developed because **~50%** of fragility fractures occur with BMD above osteoporosis threshold **(TO)**

**Monitoring response to treatment (TO)** — ✓ **DEXA** (guideline tool) · **QCT** (reliable, costly) · **BTMs**\
✗ Wrong options: **FRAX — least useful for assessing adequacy of therapy** **(TO)**

**DXA monitoring (TO)** — every **2 yr** (1 yr on anabolics) · BMD ↓ **>5% spine or >4% hip** (beyond 1–2% precision error) = failure → look for secondary causes · **new fragility fracture on treatment = failure** regardless of BMD **(TO)**

![FRAX tool](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob9032-frax-tool.jpg)

**Screening (USPSTF / OB)** — **women ≥65** + younger women with risk ≥ 65-yr-old white woman · BMD testing also postmenopausal women with ≥1 risk factor · men ≥70 (OB922) · evidence insufficient for men (USPSTF)

### Operative criteria — when to start pharmacological treatment (NOF 2008)

| Postmenopausal women / men ≥50 with ANY of | Treat? |
|---|---|
| **Hip or vertebral fracture** (even without DEXA) | Yes |
| **T ≤ −2.5** femoral neck / spine | Yes |
| **T −1.0 to −2.5** AND **10-yr hip ≥3%** | Yes |
| **T −1.0 to −2.5** AND **10-yr major ≥20%** | Yes |
| **Major ≥10%** | not a criterion **(TO)** |
| FRAX hip ≥3% **without** documented osteopenia | No |
| T −2.0, hip 1.5%, major 8.9% | **no antiresorptive** |
| T −1.5, hip **4.8%**, major 8% (smoker on phenytoin) | ✓ **Ca 1200 + vit D 800 + bisphosphonate + neuro review of phenytoin + repeat DEXA 1 yr** |

```
Fragility fracture / low BMD
├─ Hip or vertebral fragility fracture → TREAT
├─ T ≤ −2.5 → TREAT
├─ T −1.0 to −2.5
│      ├─ FRAX hip ≥3% or major ≥20% → TREAT
│      └─ below both → CALCIUM + VIT D, NO ANTIRESORPTIVE
└─ T ≥ −1.0 → NORMAL
```

### Calcium & vitamin D

| Age | Daily calcium |
|---|---|
| 1–3 yr | 500 mg |
| 4–8 yr | 800 mg |
| **9–18 yr** (e.g. 13-yr-old) | **1000–1500 mg** |
| **Adults >50 / osteoporosis** | **1200–1500 mg** |

**Vitamin D** — **800–1000 IU/day** (NOF, >50 yr) · prevention dose **400–800 IU** (OB418/OB6265) · on 1200 mg Ca alone after DRF → ✓ **add 800–1000 IU vit D**\
✗ Wrong options: 1600–2000 IU · ↑ Ca to 2000 · calcitonin

**Healthy 52–55-yr-old woman** — ✓ **1000–1500 mg Ca + 400–800 IU vit D** · ✓ 1500 mg + 1000 IU\
✗ Wrong options: semiannual BMD · estrogen (HRT not approved for treatment — OB6265)\
✗ Wrong options: 2200 mg · 5000–10,000 IU

**Premenopausal, T −2.2, DRF** — ✓ **1200 mg calcium** (prevention dose, not enough for T < −2.5)

**Effects** — Ca ↓ fracture risk **34%** · vit D ↓ hip 10%, non-vertebral 7% · high-dose (~800 IU) ↓ hip **24%**, non-vertebral **30%** · best for **type II (senile)**

### Osteoporosis drugs

| Drug | Mechanism | Key facts |
|---|---|---|
| **Non-N bisphosphonates** (etidronate, clodronate, tiludronate) | **Toxic ATP analogue** → osteoclast apoptosis | Esophagitis, ulcers, ONJ, atypical fractures |
| **N-bisphosphonates** (pamidronate, alendronate, risedronate, zoledronate, ibandronate) | **Inhibit farnesyl pyrophosphate synthase** (mevalonate) | **1st line** · renal excretion, unmetabolised · bind hydroxyapatite → detectable **years** later |
| **HRT** (estrogen-progestin) | — | Type I within 6 yr of menopause · ↓ hip fracture but ↑ breast ca, CAD/MI, stroke, PE/DVT, Alzheimer's |
| **Estrogen only** | ER on osteoblasts + osteoclasts | **Prior hysterectomy only** · unopposed → endometrial hyperplasia / uterine ca |
| **Testosterone** | — | Hypogonadal men · not FDA-approved |
| **Salmon calcitonin** | **Binds osteoclast receptors** → ↓ resorption (from thyroid **parafollicular cells**) | Women **>5 yr postmenopause** · ✓ **↓ pain of acute VCF** (neurotransmitter) · nasal/injection (gastric acid destroys) · rhinitis; nausea, flushing |
| **Raloxifene** (SERM) | **ER agonist bone, antagonist breast** (competitive partial agonist) | **Vertebral fractures only** · not anabolic · hot flashes, leg cramps · **CI: VTE** |
| **Teriparatide** | **PTH 1–34**, intermittent → osteoblasts | **Anabolic** · daily SC · transient ↑ Ca |
| **Denosumab** | **Anti-RANKL** IgG2 (like OPG) | 68/40/20% · **CI severe hypocalcaemia** |
| **Romosozumab** | **Anti-sclerostin** → Wnt ↑ | ↓ vertebral **73%** at 12 mo, **75%** at 24 mo · **CV events** |
| Strontium ranelate | Dual action (↑ formation, ↓ resorption) | not FDA-approved · ↑ MI |

**FDA-approved for active treatment (OB418)** — alendronate, risedronate, raloxifene, estrogen, calcitonin → ↓ fragility fractures up to **50%**

### Bisphosphonates (indications, atypical fractures, ONJ, drug holiday)

**Mechanism** — incorporate at remodelling sites → released in acid resorption pit → taken up by osteoclasts → **flattened ruffled border**, ↓ function + **survival** of osteoclasts\
✗ Wrong options: formation

| Agent | Fracture reduction |
|---|---|
| **Alendronate** | Hip, spine, wrist **~50%** · vertebral **48%**, non-vertebral **47%** · 70 mg weekly or 10 mg daily |
| **Risedronate** | Vertebral **65%** / non-vertebral **39%** (table) · **40%** each over 3 yr (text) |
| **Ibandronate** | Vertebral **77%**, hip 41%, non-vertebral 15% (Orthobullets) · ✓ **failed to significantly ↓ hip / non-vertebral fracture in BONE trial** **(TO)** — read the options |
| **Zoledronic acid** (IV annual) | Spine **70%**, hip **40%** over 3 yr · ✓ **only bisphosphonate ↓ all-cause mortality** — **HORIZON-RFT**, infusion within **90 days** of hip fracture repair **(TO)** |

**Fragility VCF / hip fracture** — ✓ start antiosteoporotic medication regardless of DEXA → ✓ **inhibit farnesyl pyrophosphate synthase**\
✗ Wrong options: calcitonin receptor inhibition · RANK activation · ER inhibition · continuous PTH

**Atypical femoral fracture (AFF)** — long-term BP → **extremely low turnover** (↓ urinary **NTx**) → microdamage unrepaired in lateral tension cortex

| AFF radiographic features |
|---|
| **Focal lateral cortical thickening** (beaking) |
| **Transverse** orientation |
| **Medial spike** |
| **Simple, non-comminuted** pattern |
| Subtrochanteric / diaphyseal (✗ Wrong options: distal femur) |

![Atypical subtrochanteric fracture — transverse, lateral beak, medial spike](../../MCQ/attachments/q5684-e-subtroch.jpg)

**ONJ** — **IV bisphosphonates** (✗ Wrong options: oral) · also **denosumab** · rare · stop inciting drug · long-term IV zoledronate for Paget → ✓ **osteonecrosis of mandible** (✗ Wrong options: sarcoma (teriparatide risk))

**Long-term alendronate** — ✓ **diaphyseal femoral insufficiency fractures** · ONJ only jaw\
✗ Wrong options: femoral head AVN · retinal detachment · uterine ca · scurvy

**Drug holiday** — not in **high risk / declining BMD / severe osteoporosis / chronic steroids** in first **10 yr** → continue (e.g. 8 yr BP, steroids, T −2.6) · after 10 yr → holiday **1–2 yr** or until BMD ↓ / fracture (OB)

**Drug holiday (TO)** — alendronate skeletal half-life **~10 yr** · reassess after **3–5 yr oral or 3 yr IV ZA** · moderate risk → holiday **2–3 yr** (oral) / **3 yr** (ZA) with DXA + BTM · high risk → continue or switch · common practice holiday after 3–5 yr **(TO)**

**No drug holiday (TO)** — **denosumab** (rebound) · **anabolics** (BMD lost) · **SERMs** (no residual effect) **(TO)**

### Anabolic agents (teriparatide, abaloparatide, romosozumab)

**Teriparatide** — recombinant **N-terminal PTH 1–34** (of 84 aa; chief-cell hormone) · activates **adenylate cyclase** · **20 µg/day SC** (abdomen/thigh) · max **2 yr** · ✓ **stimulates osteoblast function** · **only US anabolic** (OB, older)

**Dosing pattern** — **intermittent (daily) → anabolic** · **continuous infusion → net resorption** + hypercalcaemia (e.g. 2-wk infusion)

**Intermittent PTH effects** — ↑ osteoblast number · activates existing osteoblasts · lining cells → osteoblasts · ↓ osteoblast apoptosis · osteoblasts secrete RANKL + IL-6 (coupling)

**Anabolic mediators (TO)** — ↑ **IGF-1** · ↑ **FGF-2** · ↓ **sclerostin** (Wnt/β-catenin ↑) · ↑ **Runx2** · not **RANKL** (= catabolic pathway, with M-CSF) **(TO)**

**Signalling (TO)** — PTH1R (GPCR) on osteoblasts, osteocytes, renal tubule → Gs-cAMP-**PKA** (main) + Gq-PKC · **both anabolic and catabolic** effects **(TO)**

**FDA indications (TO)** — postmenopausal high-risk · **men with primary or hypogonadal osteoporosis** · **glucocorticoid-induced** **(TO)**

| Teriparatide contraindications (TO) |
|---|
| **Paget disease** / unexplained ↑ ALP |
| Skeletal malignancy / **bone metastases** |
| **Prior skeletal radiation** |
| **Open epiphyses** (children) |
| **Hypercalcaemia** |
| **Hypercalciuria / urolithiasis** |

**Osteosarcoma** — **rats**, dose/duration dependent (3–60× human exposure) · not shown in humans · black-box (since removed per TO; 24-mo course still standard — plateau) **(TO)**

**Adverse effects** — **transient hypercalcaemia** · hypercalciuria · nausea, headache, dizziness, orthostatic hypotension · no hyperphosphataemia · **↑ ALP** (formation marker, not ↓)

**Monitoring (TO)** — serum + urine Ca before · serum Ca at **1 mo** · hypercalcaemia → ↓ calcium supplement first, then alternate-day dosing **(TO)**

**Fracture effect** — ↓ vertebral + non-vertebral (if anything, preferential **vertebral**) · Neer: non-vertebral ↓ 35% (20 µg) / 40% (40 µg)

**With bisphosphonates** — **concomitant use beneficial** (NOT true) — BPs **blunt** anabolic effect **(TO)** · IV ZA concomitant does not blunt; **denosumab + teriparatide → enhanced BMD** **(TO)** · prior antiresorptive blunts response **(TO)** · OB213138: may be given alone or as adjunct; long-term BP users → stop BP\
✗ Wrong options: abolishes

**Refractory osteoporosis** (5 yr BP, prior cement augmentation, new VCFs) — ✓ **recombinant N-terminal PTH**

**Abaloparatide (TO)** — synthetic **PTHrP 1–34** (41% homology) · ✓ **binds more selectively to RG state** (transient cAMP → formation) · **8.5×** lower R0 affinity · → less resorption, less cortical porosity, better radius/femoral neck BMD, **less hypercalcaemia**\
✗ Wrong options: PTHR2 agonist **(TO)**

| PTH1R conformation (TO) | Signal | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **R0** | Prolonged | Osteoclast → resorption |
| **RG** | Transient | Osteoblast → formation |

**Sclerostin** — **SOST** gene, **osteocytes** · binds **LRP5/6**, blocks Wnt → β-catenin degraded → ↓ osteoblast differentiation · loss of SOST → **↑ bone mass** (**van Buchem**, **sclerosteosis**) · (✗ Wrong options: hereditary osteoporosis · antagonises BMP) · ✓ **anti-sclerostin antibodies ↑ BMD**

**Romosozumab** — ✓ **inhibits an antagonist of Wnt/β-catenin** · humanised IgG2 · **dual action** (↑ formation, ↓ resorption via ↓ RANKL, ↑ OPG) · ✓ **most rapid + greatest ↑ lumbar BMD in 12 mo** (**13.3%** spine, 6.9% hip) **(TO)**

**Romosozumab dose** — **210 mg SC monthly** (2 × 105 mg) × **12 mo** **(TO)** · **120 mg monthly** × 12 mo (TO17130219) — read the options

**Romosozumab indication** — FDA **Apr 2019** · **treatment** of postmenopausal women at high risk · not **prevention** **(TO)** · very high risk = T < −2.5 + vertebral/hip fracture in 24 mo or >2 vertebral fractures, or FRAX major >30% / hip >4.5% **(TO)**

**Romosozumab trials (TO)** — **FRAME** ↓ vertebral **73%** at 12 mo · **ARCH** vs alendronate: 48% fewer vertebral, 38% fewer hip · **CV events 2.5% vs 1.9%** → black box: avoid if MI/stroke within 1 yr **(TO)** · must follow with antiresorptive (BMD → baseline 1 yr after stopping) **(TO)**

### Denosumab

**Mechanism** — fully human **IgG2 anti-RANKL** · blocks RANKL–RANK (like **OPG**) · ✓ **inhibits bone resorption** · ↓ osteoclast **formation, function, survival** (BPs do not affect formation) **(TO)**\
✗ Wrong options: RANK · OPG · TNF

**Pharmacology** — does not bind hydroxyapatite · cleared within **6 mo**, undetectable at **1 yr** · **fully reversible** · usable with **bone metastases** · also **GCT of bone**

**Dose** — **60 mg SC every 6 mo** (100% compliance) **(TO)**

**FREEDOM (TO)** — ↓ vertebral **68%**, hip **40%**, non-vertebral **20%** over 3 yr · 10-yr extension: spine BMD **+21.7%**, **no plateau** (BPs plateau 3–5 yr) **(TO)**

**Indications (TO)** — BP alternative (**eGFR <30–35**, oesophageal disease, intolerance) · second line **(TO)**

**Adverse** — arthralgia · nasopharyngitis · back pain · **ONJ** (✓ antibody-based therapy linked to ONJ; ~0.04%) · cellulitis · hypocalcaemia (renal impairment, vit D deficiency) · CI **severe hypocalcaemia**

**Abrupt discontinuation** — ✓ **rebound turnover → multiple vertebral fractures** **(TO)**\
✗ Wrong options: hypercalcaemia · ONJ · AFF

## 6 Complications

**Bisphosphonates** — esophagitis · dysphagia · gastric ulcer · **ONJ (IV)** · **atypical subtrochanteric fracture**

**Teriparatide** — transient hypercalcaemia · rat osteosarcoma · Paget sarcoma risk (contraindicated)

**Denosumab** — ONJ · rebound vertebral fractures on stopping · hypocalcaemia

**Romosozumab** — **CV events** · hyperostosis · OA · cancer · ONJ · AFF

**Raloxifene** — VTE · hot flashes · leg cramps

**HRT** — breast ca · CAD/MI · stroke · PE/DVT · Alzheimer's

| | **Osteoporosis** | **Osteomalacia** |
|---|---|---|
| Defect | **↓ mass, normal mineralisation** (quantitative) | Variable mass, **↓ mineralisation** (qualitative) |
| Age | Postmenopausal / elderly | Any |
| Cause | Endocrine, age, inactivity, alcohol, ↓ Ca | Vit D deficiency/pathway, ↓ PO₄, hypophosphatasia, RTA |
| Symptoms | Pain at fracture site | Generalised bone pain |
| X-ray | **Axial** fractures | **Appendicular**, symmetric, **Looser zones** |
| Serum Ca / PO₄ | Normal | Low or normal |
| ALP | **Normal** | **↑** (except hypophosphatasia) |
| Urinary Ca | High or normal | Normal or low |
| Tetracycline biopsy | Normal | **Abnormal** |

## Antiresorptive vs Anabolic

| | **Bisphosphonate** | **Denosumab** | **Teriparatide** | **Romosozumab** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Target | FPP synthase / ATP analogue | RANKL | PTH1R | Sclerostin |
| Action | Antiresorptive | Antiresorptive | **Anabolic** | **Anabolic + antiresorptive** |
| Dosing | Oral weekly / IV yearly | 60 mg SC 6-monthly | 20 µg SC daily, 2 yr | SC monthly, 12 mo |
| Persistence | Years in bone | Gone by 6–12 mo | BMD lost without relay | BMD lost without relay |
| Drug holiday | Yes (moderate risk) | No (rebound) | No | No |
| Signature risk | ONJ, AFF | Rebound VCFs, ONJ | Osteosarcoma (rats), ↑ Ca | CV events |

## How it's asked

**"T-score −3.5 / −2.6?"** → **osteoporosis**\
✗ Wrong options: needs more information · normal for elderly

**"Osteoporosis T-score?"** → **−2.5 or below**\
✗ Wrong options: −3.0 · +2.5

**"Postmenopausal, T −0.7, Z −0.4?"** → **normal**\
✗ Wrong options: osteopenia by Z-score

**"Osteopenia definition?"** → **T −1 to −2.5**\
✗ Wrong options: Z-score definitions · T vs age-matched
