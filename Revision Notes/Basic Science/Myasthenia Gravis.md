# Myasthenia Gravis

*Basic Science · 66 / 108*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology & pathophysiology

**Bimodal** — **young women <40**, **older men >50**

**Risk factors** — **HLA-B8** · drugs: **penicillamine, aminoglycosides**

**Mechanism** — ✓ **autoantibodies (Ig) at the neuromuscular junction** against **nicotinic ACh receptor** (commoner) or **MuSK** · **type II hypersensitivity** · T cells stimulate B-cell antibodies

**Associations** — **thymoma**, thymic hyperplasia

### Genes & lab markers

**First test** — ✓ **anti-AChR antibodies** · anti-MuSK

**EMG** — **decremental CMAP** on repetitive stimulation

## 3 Presentation

**Hallmark** — **fluctuating weakness worse with use / end of day**, better with rest · true fatigue (↓ contractile force)

**Commonest presenting symptoms** — **ocular: ptosis, diplopia** (ptosis on sustained upgaze)

**Bulbar** — dysphagia, dysarthria, fatigable chewing · **proximal** weakness

**Bedside tests** — **ice-pack test** (cold ↓ acetylcholinesterase → ptosis improves) · **edrophonium (Tensilon)** (short-acting AChE inhibitor; only with ptosis/ophthalmoparesis)

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

**CT chest** — exclude **thymoma**

### Differential

| Condition | Distinguishing feature |
|---|---|
| **Myasthenia gravis** | **Worse with use** · antibodies to AChR (postsynaptic) |
| **Lambert–Eaton (LEMS)** | **Improves with use** · autonomic features |
| **Botulism** | **Poor pupillary light response** · ↓ ACh release |
| Non-depolarising NMB | ↓ ACh binding |
| **Muscular dystrophy** | **Absent dystrophin**, excess sarcolemmal Ca²⁺, young onset |
| **CMT** | **Demyelination**, distal long-axon degeneration |
| Thyroid ophthalmopathy | — |

## 5 Management

**First-line symptomatic** — **AChE inhibitor: pyridostigmine** (side effects: cramps, diarrhoea, bradycardia, sweating, secretions)

**Chronic immunotherapy** — **oral prednisone**

**Myasthenic crisis (respiratory weakness)** — **IVIG or plasmapheresis**

**Thymectomy** — any **thymoma** (with or without MG)

## 6 Complications

**Myasthenic crisis** — respiratory failure · most ocular MG progresses to generalised

## How it's asked

**"45-yr-old woman, best in morning, fatigable arms, ptosis on upgaze — pathology?"** → **Ig antibodies at NM junction**\
✗ Wrong options: ↓ ACh release (botulinum) · demyelination (CMT) · absent dystrophin

**"Weakness that improves with use?"** → **Lambert–Eaton**
