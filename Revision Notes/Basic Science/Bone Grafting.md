# Bone Grafting

*Basic Science · 16 / 108*

## 1 Basics

### Overview

**Volume** — ~**1 million** grafting procedures/yr in the US · growth ~12–13%/yr

**Indications** — fractures, delayed union, **nonunion** · arthrodesis, **spinal fusion** · defects after trauma/tumour

### Graft properties (osteogenic, osteoinductive, osteoconductive)

| Property | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Osteoconductive** | **Scaffold** — permits ingrowth of capillaries, perivascular tissue, osteoprogenitors → **creeping substitution** | Allograft, ceramics, DBM |
| **Osteoinductive** | Factors that **recruit + differentiate MSCs** into osteoblasts (bind transmembrane receptors) | **BMP** (TGF-β superfamily), DBM |
| **Osteogenic** | **Living bone-forming cells** — MSCs, osteoblasts, osteocytes | **Fresh autograft, bone marrow aspirate** only |
| Osseointegration | Direct bone–implant contact (Brånemark) · **not a graft property** (TO) | Ca-phosphate-coated stems |

**Most common / most important property (TO)** — ✓ **osteoconduction**

**Live mesenchymal precursor cells** — ✓ only **autograft / bone marrow aspirate**\
✗ Wrong options: fresh-frozen allograft · rhBMP · DBM · Ca phosphate

**Cancellous > cortical** for new bone — larger surface area

| Material | Conductive | Inductive | Osteogenic |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Autograft** (ICBG, **RIA**, local cancellous/cortical) | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| **Bone marrow aspirate** | No (no structure) | Yes (OB) | ✓ **(TO: osteogenic potential)** |
| **DBM** | Yes | Yes | No |
| **BMP** (rhBMP-2, -7) | No | ✓ **only** | No |
| **PRP** | No | "**solely osteoinductive**" (Orthobullets/OB) · TO: mitogenic + angiogenic only, **not true osteoinduction** | No |
| Allograft (freeze-dried, cortical, cancellous, fibular strut) | ✓ **only** | No (fresh/fresh-frozen retain some BMP) | No |
| Ceramics (Ca phosphate, sulfate, carbonate, TCP, HA), polymers | ✓ **only** | No | No |

**Most osteoinductive (of listed)** — **cancellous autograft**

### Diamond concept & graft biology in nonunion (TO)

**Giannoudis Diamond (4 pillars)** — **osteoconductive scaffold** · **osteoinductive mediators** · **osteogenic cells** · **mechanical stability** **(TO)**

**Diamond vs Pentagon (second TO note)** — Diamond = **3 components** (osteogenic cells · osteoconductive scaffold · osteoinductive signals) · **Pentagon** adds **mechanical stability + vascularity / host** (TO) — read the options: earlier TO note puts stability inside the Diamond

**Polytherapy** — all Diamond pillars applied **simultaneously** (biologics + graft + stable fixation) (TO)

**HA/β-TCP + rhBMP-2 + LR-PRP + locking plate** — deficient in ✓ **osteogenesis** (no viable MSCs) → add **autograft or BMAC** **(TO)**

**rhBMP-2** — serine/threonine kinase receptors → **SMAD** → MSC → osteoblast **(TO)**

### Antigenicity

**Antigens** — class I + II MHC on graft cells → host **T cells** · **marrow cells most immunogenic** · endothelium · type I collagen (humoral + cell-mediated) · non-collagenous matrix

**Hydroxyapatite** — no immune response

**Rejection** — **cell-mediated**, MHC incompatibility · greater mismatch → less new bone

**Ligament allograft antigenicity** — related to **bone** > soft tissue → **BPTB for ACL = highest** (also meniscal bone plugs)

## 2 Mechanism

### Graft healing stages

| Stage | Event |
|---|---|
| 1 Inflammation | Necrotic debris → chemotaxis |
| 2 Osteoblast differentiation | From mesenchymal precursors |
| 3 Osteoinduction | Osteoblast + osteoclast stimulation |
| 4 Osteoconduction | Bone forms on scaffold |
| 5 Remodelling | Years |

### Types of autograft

| Type | Facts |
|---|---|
| **Cancellous** | Little structure · ↑ osteoconduction · **rapid incorporation by creeping substitution** |
| **Cortical** | Slow (remodel existing Haversian canals) · structural · interstitial lamellae preserved |
| **Vascularised** | Faster union, cells preserved · **free fibula (peroneal a.)**, free iliac crest (deep circumflex iliac a.), **distal radius 1,2-ICSRA** for scaphoid |

**Cortical graft weakness** — **weak during resorption phase → fatigue fracture** risk (TO)

**Graft source types (TO)** — autograft (same individual, **all 3 properties**) · allograft (human donor, no donor-site morbidity, infection risk) · **xenograft** (animal, rarely used)

### Structural allograft biology & outcomes

**Incorporation** — particulate > structural · massive cortical graft largely **never remodels, stays devascularised**

**Insufficiency fracture** — **15–30%** (~**25%**) of massive structural allografts

**Retrieved bulk allograft (10 yr)** — junction heals by **bridging external callus** + persistent callus **perpendicular** to junction · little penetration\
✗ Wrong options: osteonal/Haversian remodelling · complete incorporation

**Osteoarticular allograft cartilage at 5 yr** — **completely acellular** (no donor or host chondrocytes)

**Meniscal allograft** — success depends most on **accurate sizing (within 5%)** \
✗ Wrong options: donor cell viability (repopulated by host) · immunosuppression · cryopreservation

## 5 Management

### Types of allograft & processing

| Allograft | Immunogenicity | BMP | Disease transmission | Shelf life |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Fresh** | **Highest** | Preserved | **Highest** | Hours–days |
| **Fresh-frozen** | Less | Preserved (inductive) | Intermediate · **stronger** — better structural | **−20 °C: 1–2 yr** · **−70 °C: up to 5 yr** |
| **Freeze-dried** ("croutons") | **Least** | **Depleted** (purely conductive) | **Lowest** (theoretical HIV 1 : 2.6 billion) | **Indefinite** (5 yr–∞) |

**Shelf life order** — **freeze-dried > fresh-frozen −70 °C > fresh-frozen −20 °C > fresh**

**Processing** — debride soft tissue → **ethanol** wash (removes cells) → **gamma irradiation** (dose-dependent: kills pathogens but ↓ mechanical properties)

| Method | Process |
|---|---|
| **Cryopreservation** | ✓ **Chemicals (DMSO/glycerol) displace cellular water + controlled-rate freezing** → no ice crystals · keeps some chondrocytes (osteoarticular) |
| Freeze-drying | Water → alcohol to 5% moisture → vacuum (sublimation) · alters growth-factor proteins |
| Fresh-frozen | Frozen twice, packaged dry at −80 °C |
| Fresh | Not frozen · max viability, short shelf life |

**DBM vs freeze-dried allograft** — DBM additionally **osteoinductive** (factors bind transmembrane receptors on MSCs) · neither is osteogenic

**DBM** — **acid extraction** of allograft → removes mineral, keeps collagen + non-collagenous proteins (**BMPs, TGF-β, IGF, FGF**, residual Ca) · no structural support · **inter-lot variability**

### Bone graft substitutes (calcium sulfate, calcium phosphate, HA)

**All ceramics** — osteoconductive void fillers · **scaffold for progenitor cells** · **low shear, tensile, torsional strength** · biocompatible with steel + titanium · used in spinal fusion

**Resorption (fastest → slowest)** — **calcium sulfate > tricalcium phosphate > hydroxyapatite**

| Substitute | Resorption | Strength | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Calcium sulfate** (plaster of Paris, OsteoSet) | **4–12 wk** (5–7 wk; gone by 6 wk) · **passive dissolution** · **faster than bone grows** | ≈ cancellous | **Sterile serous wound drainage** (osmotic load) **(TO)** · fails alone in nonunion |
| **β-TCP** | **6–18 months** · **cell (osteoclast)-mediated** **(TO)** · dissolution + fragmentation | ≤ cancellous · brittle | Less bone formed than resorbed → used as adjunct |
| **Calcium phosphate cement** | ~1 yr (1–10 yr) | **Highest compressive — 4–10× cancellous** | Hardens to **dahllite** · **early WB** without subsidence (plateau, calcaneus) · **osseointegration + osteoconduction** (stem coatings) · reduce + fix **before** injecting |
| **Coralline HA** (Interpore) | Very slow (decades) | Slightly > cancellous | Coral CaCO₃ → HA by hydrothermal exchange · **neovascularisation + appositional bone** (✗ Wrong options: creeping substitution · dissolution) |
| Calcium carbonate (Biocoral) | — | — | Unaltered coral · conductive only |
| Collagen matrix + HA coating | Collagen fast, HA slow | < cancellous | — |
| Aluminium oxide | — | — | Bonds to bone under stress/strain |

**Level I evidence** — Ca-phosphate fills defects, allows early rehab, **prevents articular subsidence** (distal radius, tibial plateau)

**Ca-phosphate cure (TO)** — isothermal / mildly exothermic · no PMMA-type thermal necrosis

**HO** — complication of **BMP**, not calcium sulfate **(TO)**

### Bone morphogenetic proteins

**rhBMP-2, rhBMP-7** — FDA-approved, long bones + spine · **solely osteoinductive** · perivascular MSC → osteoblast via **serine-threonine kinase receptors**

**Complications** — under/over-production of bone · inflammation · early bone resorption · near-zero disease transmission (synthetic)

### Autograft harvest (iliac crest, RIA)

**Autograft** — **gold standard** · all three properties · **least immunogenic**

**Iliac crest** — commonest site · cortical + cancellous · complications **2–36%**

**Anterior vs posterior** — **anterior = more complications** (pain, gait) · **posterior = more stem cells** (viability same), less pain

| Structure at risk | Where |
|---|---|
| **Superior cluneal nerves** — **commonest posterior complication** (buttock numbness) | Cross crest **>8 cm lateral to PSIS** → ✓ **stay within 8 cm** **(TO)** |
| **Superior gluteal artery** | Retractor/elevator slipping **caudally into sciatic notch** · brisk bleeding (✗ Wrong options: vein · inferior gluteal) |
| SI joint posterior ligaments | Full-thickness posterior graft → **SI instability/dislocation** |
| **LFCN**, ilioinguinal nerve | **Anterior** crest |
| Deep circumflex iliac artery | Anterior crest |
| Corona mortis | Anterior intrapelvic approaches |
| **External iliac artery** | ✓ **Not at risk** **(TO)** |

**Other complications** — haematoma · hernia · infection · fracture · cosmetic defect · chronic pain

**Vascularised autograft sources** — **fibula, ribs**

**Gold-standard site (TO)** — **anterior iliac crest** · others: posterior crest, femoral / tibial condyle, **olecranon, distal radius**

**No substitute equals autograft** in combined efficacy (TO)

**Polymers (PLA, PGA)** — **osteoconductive**, resorbable synthetic scaffold · ceramics can be **antibiotic-loaded beads** (TO)

### Reamer–irrigator–aspirator (RIA)

| RIA vs ICBG | |
|---|---|
| Volume | **Higher** (~50–90 mL per femur) |
| Union | **Comparable (~90%)** |
| Operative time | **Shorter** · technically easier |
| Immediate complications | **Fewer** |
| Blood loss | **Higher → transfusion** (up to 44%) |
| Structural support | **Less** (fragments ≤2 mm) |
| Equipment | More expensive |
| Biology | All three properties · **>95% cell viability** · ≥ ICBG growth factors/MSCs · similar RNA transcription profile |
| Specific risks | **Femoral shaft fracture** (eccentric reaming) · insertion-site (abductor) pain |

**RIA false statement (TO)** — false: "**heat injury from reaming abolishes osteoinductive properties**" · RIA is a viable alternative to cancellous autograft, product of long-bone nailing, osteogenic **(TO)**

**RIA (TO, gap-nonunion notes)** — graft contains MSCs + **FGF, IGF, TGF** · **↓ intramedullary pressure → less fat embolism** · risks: **fat embolism, femoral fracture, thermal necrosis**

## 6 Complications

### Allograft disease transmission & processing

| Pathogen | Risk (fresh-frozen bone) |
|---|---|
| **HIV** | **1 in 1,000,000 – 1,670,000** (1 in 1.6 million despite screening) · last bone transmission 1985 |
| **Hepatitis C** | 1 in 100,000 · last 2000 |
| **Hepatitis B** | 1 in 63,000 |
| HTLV | One case (improper screening) |
| CJD | **Never** in bone (cornea, dura only) |
| Bacteria | Up to 12% massive allograft · 0.7% morselised · Clostridium (2001) |

**AATB required serology** — ✓ **HIV, HBV, HCV, syphilis** (+ HTLV-I/II)\
✗ Wrong options: CMV · hepatitis A

**Donor exclusions (TO)** — malignancy (except skin BCC) · collagen vascular disease · metabolic bone disease · toxins

**Positive preimplantation culture (CoNS), no clinical infection** — ✓ **observe** · 4.8–13.3% positive, none became infected · routine culture not recommended

**Calcium sulfate** — ↑ **serous wound drainage**
