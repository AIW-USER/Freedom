# Structural Properties

*Basic Science · 101 / 108*

## 1 Basics

**Implant behaviour** — **structural properties** (shape, configuration — cylinder, rectangle) + **material properties** · structural ≠ strength characteristic

### Bending rigidity (stiffness)

**Definition** — slope of **elastic range** on structural stress–strain curve · **THA stem stiffness** → proximal **stress shielding**

| Shape | Bending rigidity ∝ |
|---|---|
| **Solid cylinder** | **Radius⁴** |
| **Hollow cylinder** | **Radius³** |
| **Rectangle (plate)** | **Thickness³** |

### Moments of inertia

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Area moment of inertia (I)** | Shape property predicting deflection, bending, stress · **∝ length⁴** · function of width, thickness, polar moment · = flexural stiffness |
| **Polar moment of inertia (J)** | Resistance to **torsion** |
| Moment of inertia | Resistance to change in rotation |

**Deflection** — displacement under load · ∝ (force / elastic modulus) × area moment of inertia (as printed)

### Bone anisotropy

**Cortical bone** — ✓ **anisotropic** (collagen fibre orientation) → mechanical properties **vary with orientation** · loaded along **long axis → highest stiffness + tensile strength** vs oblique/transverse\
✗ Wrong options: hydroxyapatite/water content differences

## How it's asked

**"Why orient cortical bone tensile specimens relative to femoral shaft?"** → **mechanical properties vary with orientation (anisotropy)**\
✗ Wrong options: hydroxyapatite structure · water content · easier cutting
