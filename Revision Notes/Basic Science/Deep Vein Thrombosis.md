# Deep Vein Thrombosis

*Basic Science · 28 / 108*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Incidence** — 80 / 100,000 / yr · LE 1 / 1,000 / yr · 2.5–5% of population · usually **>40 yr** · rare in children

**High-risk procedures** — spine fracture with paralysis · **TKA (2–3× DVT rate of THA)** · THA · hip fracture · polytrauma

**DVT rate ≠ PE or death** — AAOS review: DVT rate does **not correlate** with PE/death after THA/TKA

**Risk factors** — immobilisation · mechanical compression · trauma · **prior DVT** · surgery · polycythaemia vera · thrombocytosis · dehydration · malignancy · CHF · obstructive airway disease

**No ↑ risk** — **factor V Leiden in THA** (no ↑ DVT or PE vs controls) · **rheumatoid arthritis in TKA** (age/comorbidity-matched)

### Pathophysiology

**Virchow's triad** — hypercoagulability · venous stasis · endothelial injury

**Factor V Leiden** — factor V resistant to **protein C** inactivation → hypercoagulable (factor V = cofactor for Xa → thrombin)

### Anatomy

| | Deep veins |
|---|---|
| **Proximal LE** | **Popliteal and above** (above trifurcation) |
| Distal LE — axial | Peroneal, anterior + posterior tibial |
| Distal LE — muscular | Soleal, gastrocnemius |
| UE | Radial, ulnar, brachial, axillary, subclavian, IJ, brachiocephalic |

## 2 Mechanism

### Timing & intraoperative embolisation

**Peak marrow embolisation in cemented THA** — ✓ **femoral stem insertion** (also acetabular prep, reduction)

**Symptomatic VTE after TJA** — ✓ **1–6 weeks** (2nd–6th week) · events on day of surgery = fat embolism or cardiac · ~10% readmitted within 3 months

## 3 Presentation

**Symptoms** — **swelling (70%)** · **calf pain (50%)** · redness · warmth

**Exam** — unilateral/bilateral swelling · erythema · dilated veins · tenderness · **Homans' sign not specific** (or sensitive)

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Test | Sens / spec | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Complete duplex US** | **96% / 97%** | ✓ **First line** · non-compressible vein, loss of phasic flow with Valsalva, ↑ superficial flow · operator-dependent · **routine screening not recommended**|
| Contrast venography | — | **Historical gold standard** · invasive · if US unavailable/inconclusive |
| CT venography | 90% / 95% | Suspected PE or inconclusive US |
| MR direct thrombus imaging | 90% / 90% | Not widely used |
| Plethysmography (proximal) | 75% / 90% | — |

### D-dimer

**High sensitivity, low specificity** — excludes VTE in **low clinical probability** · >500 ng/mL suggests PE · **useless after injury/surgery** (false positives)

### Differential

| Mimic | Clue |
|---|---|
| Cellulitis | Infective labs |
| Post-thrombotic syndrome | Prior DVT, chronic swelling, stasis ulcers |
| **Ruptured Baker cyst** | US: no venous thrombus |
| Superficial thrombophlebitis | Cord-like superficial vein |
| CHF, nephrotic, cirrhosis | Chronic bilateral oedema |

## 5 Management

### Anticoagulation

| Agent | Role |
|---|---|
| **DOACs** (dabigatran, rivaroxaban, apixaban, edoxaban) | ✓ **First line** for DVT + PE (ACCP 2016, ESC) |
| **LMWH** (enoxaparin) | **Early postoperative period** · high bleeding risk |
| **Warfarin** | **Bridge with LMWH until INR 2–3** · preferred with **mechanical heart valve** |

**Indication** — postoperative **above-knee DVT** · below-knee treatment controversial

| Scenario | Duration |
|---|---|
| **Isolated provoked DVT** | **3 months** |
| With PE | **>3 months** (longer if large clot, persistent risk, recurrence) |
| **>3 months** | Unprovoked · recurrent · active malignancy · paralysis/immobilisation · thrombophilia |

### Procedures

| Intervention | Indication |
|---|---|
| **IVC filter** | **DVT identified preop** in high-risk pelvic/LE trauma · contraindication to anticoagulation |
| Thrombolysis (systemic) | Life/limb-threatening PE or acute DVT · high morbidity |
| **Catheter-directed thrombolysis** | Initial treatment **only for threatened limb loss** |
| Thrombectomy | Anticoagulation ineffective, high-risk thrombus |

### Risk factors & individualised prophylaxis

**Foot & ankle (ICM-VTE)** — ✓ **patient-specific risk assessment** (age, DM, prior DVT, NWB, obesity) · **NWB = independent risk factor** but no prophylaxis rule based on NWB alone\
✗ Wrong options: routine chemoprophylaxis for all NWB · blanket "none for below-knee" · surveillance duplex · postop D-dimer

## 6 Complications

**Post-thrombotic syndrome** — chronic venous insufficiency · venous HTN · swelling, pain, pigmentation, ulceration, induration · **recurrent DVT 4–8×**

**PE** — 1 in 1,000 / yr · anticoagulation ± intervention

**Bleeding** — anticoagulants, thrombolysis

## How it's asked

**"Peak marrow embolisation in cemented THA?"** → **femoral stem insertion**

**"When do most symptomatic VTE occur after TJA?"** → **1–6 weeks**

**"Factor V Leiden and THA?"** → **no increased risk of DVT or PE**

**"RA (RF, anti-CCP, MTX) vs OA patient for TKA — VTE prophylaxis?"** → **equal risk, standard prophylaxis for both**

**"Hindfoot fusion, NWB 6 wk, BMI 31, DM, prior DVT — strategy?"** → **patient-specific risk assessment, consider chemoprophylaxis**\
✗ Wrong options: none · routine for all · screening duplex · D-dimer

**"First-line imaging for DVT?"** → **complete duplex ultrasound** · routine screening not recommended

**"First-line DVT treatment?"** → **DOAC** · warfarin if mechanical valve
