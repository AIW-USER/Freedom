## 1 Basics

### Definitions

| Term | Definition | Units |
|---|---|---|
| Load | Force on a body | N |
| **Stress** | Internal force intensity = **force / area** | Pa (N/m²) |
| **Strain** | Relative deformation = **ΔL / L** | None |
| **Elastic deformation** | Reversible — returns to shape when unloaded | — |
| **Plastic deformation** | ✓ **Irreversible** — doesn't return to original length | — |
| **Toughness** | ✓ **Energy absorbed per volume before fracture = area under stress–strain curve** | J/m³ |
| **Creep** | ✓ **↑ deformation (strain) over time under constant load** | — |
| **Load (stress) relaxation** | ✓ **↓ stress (peak load) over time at constant strain/elongation** | — |
| **Hysteresis** | Loading ≠ unloading curve (viscoelastic) · area = energy dissipated | — |
| Ductility | Ability to deform plastically under tension before rupture | — |
| Strain rate | Strain ÷ time | — |
| Finite element analysis | Complex shape split into triangles/quadrilaterals, forces balanced | — |

**Creep in polyethylene liners** — plastic deformation under load **without wear debris** · TO note: creep "at high temperature, below elastic limit"

**Creep (TO, FNB)** — answer key not provided by source · options: ↑ length under constant load, ↓ length under constant load, stiffness change from viscoelasticity, ↑ stiffness with time · Orthobullets definition = **increased deformation under constant load**

### Stress–strain curve (yield, elastic, plastic, toughness, fatigue)

**Axes (TO)** — ✓ **strain on X-axis**, stress on Y-axis

![Stress–strain curve](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob9062-basic-stress-strain-curve-moved.jpg)

| Feature | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Elastic zone** | Linear — returns to shape (A → B "elastic behaviour") · **Hooke's law**: stress ∝ strain |
| **Young's modulus** | **Slope in elastic zone = stiffness** (resistance to deformation) |
| **Yield point / yield strength** | ✓ **Elastic → plastic transition** · stress producing a set permanent deformation |
| **Plastic zone** | Permanent deformation |
| **Ultimate (tensile) strength** | Highest point — **maximum stress / load to failure** · then necking (ductile) |
| **Breaking (fracture) point** | Failure |
| Toe region | Ligament/tendon — crimped fibrils straighten |

**Implant absorbing most energy before failure** — ✓ **high toughness**\
✗ Wrong options: modulus · yield stress alone · brittle

**Two plates load–displacement** — larger area = **tougher** · higher peak = stronger · earlier break = more brittle

**Hip screw returns to shape after bending** — **elastic** deformation

### Metal characteristics

**Fatigue** — ✓ **failure below ultimate tensile strength after repetitive cycles** · depends on stress magnitude + cycles · ✓ mode of failure of a nailed femur that fails to unite (**cyclic fatigue**)\
✗ Wrong options: static/catastrophic (single supraphysiological load)

**Endurance (fatigue) limit** — max cyclic stress never causing failure · arthroplasty implants designed above physiological loads · fracture fixation only until union (TO)

**Bone vs implants (TO)** — bone ultimate strength ~4× lower than steel → fails first in single load (periprosthetic fracture) · bone **resists fatigue better** (remodelling repairs microcracks)

## 2 Mechanism

### Material descriptions

| Type | Definition | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Brittle** | Linear to failure, little/no plastic deformation | **PMMA, ceramics**, hydroxyapatite |
| **Ductile** | Large plastic deformation before failure | **Metals** |
| **Viscoelastic** | Stress–strain depends on **rate + duration** of load (internal friction) | **Ligaments, bone, cartilage** |
| **Isotropic** | Same properties in all directions | **Metals (steel, titanium)**, golf ball |
| **Anisotropic** | Properties depend on load direction | **Ligaments, bone** |

### Viscoelasticity (creep, stress relaxation, anisotropy, strain rate)

**Ligaments** — viscoelastic → **strength depends on rate of load** · creep, stress relaxation, non-linear elasticity · not **isotropic** · load–elongation region B = linear elastic after fibrils straightened (A = toe, C = ultimate load, D = energy, E = ultimate elongation)

**Cortical bone — ↑ strain rate** — ✓ **↑ elastic modulus AND ↑ ultimate strength** · physiological rates (<0.1/s) elastic + ductile · high-impact rates brittle · modulus up to 2× in falls (TO)

**Bone true statement (TO)** — ✓ **time-dependent viscoelastic properties**\
✗ Wrong options: isotropic · less fatigue-resistant than steel · repetitive tension → oblique fracture (→ transverse)

**Anisotropy of cortical bone (TO)** — compressive strength **50% > tensile** · longitudinal modulus **17 GPa** vs transverse **12 GPa** · longitudinal strength **193 MPa** vs 133 MPa

**Articular cartilage** — biphasic viscoelastic (flow-dependent fluid drag + flow-independent matrix) · modulus changes with **duration + rate** of load

**Allograft ACL** — may lengthen by **creep**

### Bone failure (fracture patterns)

**Bone strength** — ✓ **strongest in compression, weakest in shear (TO)**, intermediate tension

| Load | Fracture |
|---|---|
| **Tension** | **Transverse** (muscle pull) |
| **Compression** | Crush · short oblique (fails at 45° plane of max shear) (TO) |
| **Bending** | **Butterfly fragment** (convex side fails in tension) |
| **Torsion** | **Spiral** · longer bone → ↑ outer-cortex stress |
| Bending + compression / bending + torsion | **Short oblique** (TO) |

**Bone composition** — collagen (low modulus, tensile strength) + hydroxyapatite (stiff, brittle, compressive) · remodels to ↑ **moment of inertia**

**Ligaments/tendons** — tolerate **5–10% strain** (bone 1–4%)

### Specific materials

| Material | Composition | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Titanium** (Ti-6Al-4V) | — | Most biocompatible · **self-passivation** (adherent oxide) → corrosion resistant · **low modulus** (≈ bone) | **Notch sensitivity, poor wear** → not for femoral head · more metal debris |
| **316L stainless steel** | **Iron + carbon (L = low, 0.03%) + chromium (~17–18%) + nickel (~12%) + molybdenum (~2.5–3%)** · **no cobalt (TO)** | Stiff, fracture resistant, cheap, ductile | **Pitting + crevice corrosion** · stress shielding |
| **Cobalt alloy** | **Co + Cr + Mo** | Very strong · better corrosion resistance than steel · TKA femoral components | Very stiff → stress shielding (✓ for cemented stems — protects mantle) |
| **UHMWPE** | — | Tough, ductile, resilient, wear-resistant, **high fracture toughness** | Abrasion (third body), thermoplastic, weak in tension · **gamma cross-linking ↑ wear resistance, ↓ fatigue + fracture resistance** |
| **PMMA** | Powder: polymer, **benzoyl peroxide (initiator)**, **barium sulfate (radio-opacifier)**, colour · Liquid: monomer, **DMPT (accelerator)**, hydroquinone (stabiliser) | **Interlocks with bone** (grout, not glue) · strongest in **compression** · ultimate strength at **24 h** | **Brittle**, notch-sensitive · poor **tension + shear** · **exothermic** polymerisation · hypotension on insertion · fails by microfracture |
| Silicones | — | Non-WB joint replacement | Poor strength/wear → **synovitis** |
| **Ceramics** | Alumina, zirconia | **Best wear vs PE**, high compressive strength, inert | **Brittle, low fracture toughness**, high modulus, low tensile strength, poor crack resistance |

**316L components (TO)** — chromium → oxide layer (anti-rust, passivation) · nickel → manufacturing (austenite) · molybdenum → protects oxide layer from pitting

## 5 Management

### Corrosion (galvanic, fretting, crevice, passivation)

| Type | Mechanism | Key facts |
|---|---|---|
| **Galvanic** | ✓ **Electrochemical potential between two dissimilar metals in conductive medium** (anode corrodes) | **316L + Co-Cr = highest risk** · steel very susceptible · Co-Cr "most susceptible" of listed (✗ Wrong options: Ti, ceramics, PE) · cerclage wire SS on Ti stem · mixed-metal neck/stem |
| **Crevice** | **O₂ tension difference** in cracks/crevices | **316L most prone** · **titanium least** · plate countersinks, cementless cups |
| **Fretting** | ✓ **Micromotion at contact sites under load** → passive layer scraped off → pits, grooves, oxide debris | **Head–neck taper** (trunnionosis), ↑ serum Co/Cr, synovitis, ALTR · **commonest cause of modular mid-stem junction failure** · ↑ with more interfaces · worsens over time · ceramic heads ↓ it |
| **Fretting fatigue** | Cyclic load + small oscillation → cracks | ✓ **Initiates mid-stem modular junction fracture** (completed by bending) · risk: high BMI, poor proximal bone support |
| Pitting | Local depassivation → holes | Stainless steel |
| Intergranular | Grain-boundary attack | — |
| Stress corrosion | High tensile stress + corrosive environment (manufacturing) | — |
| Degradation / oxidative | Polymers / free radical | — |

**Self-passivation** — ✓ **reason titanium is corrosion resistant** (oxide layer forms spontaneously)

### Ceramic properties & fracture

**Ceramic disadvantage** — ✓ **low fracture toughness** (poor crack resistance)

**Most serious CoC complication** — ✓ **fracture** (fragments embed → third-body wear) · squeaking (annoying, may herald failure)

**CoC THA: new squeak + stiffness + LLD, fractured ceramic, normal CRP/ESR** — ✓ **thorough debridement + one-stage head + liner revision**\
✗ Wrong options: two-stage (no infection)

### Structural mechanics (moment of inertia, bending rigidity, tension band)

**Area moment of inertia** — ✓ ∝ **distance² of material from the centre of mass** (tube > solid rod of same area)\
✗ Wrong options: density · modulus

| Implant | Bending rigidity ∝ |
|---|---|
| **Solid pin / rod** | **radius⁴** (diameter⁴) |
| Hollow (cannulated) nail | ~radius³ |
| **Plate** | **thickness³** (2× thickness → 8× stiffness) |
| Ex-fix pin | diameter⁴ · pin stiffness ∝ 1/(bone–rod distance)³ · fragment contact most important |

**Plate on tension side** — ✓ **minimises bending stresses in the plate** (fracture closes under load, bone shares load)

**Strain sharing** — 5 mm displacement across one 5 mm gap = 100% strain · shared across multiple gaps (each 5 → 6 mm) = 20% → **80% ↓ strain** (Perren)

**Stress risers around plates/prostheses (TO)** — ✓ **overlap plate with stem**, gap <8 cm risky · interprosthetic distance <110 mm risky in weak bone · end-screw fracture 1–3% (non-locking), 2.6% (locking) · ✓ **oblique end screw** · ✓ **non-locking end screw in all-locked construct** · **unicortical end screw — NOT recommended** (weaker) · insert non-locking before locking screws

### Screw mechanics (TO)

| Term | Fact (TO) |
|---|---|
| **Core (root/minor) diameter** | = **drill size** · determines **strength, fatigue resistance, bending strength, tensile strength** |
| **Major (thread) diameter** | Thread depth (major − minor) → **pull-out strength**, stripping torque |
| **Runout** | Shaft–thread junction · **weakest part of partially threaded screw** |
| Pitch | Distance between threads · lead = advance per turn |
| **Mechanical advantage** | = **circumference / pitch** (✗ Wrong options: pitch/circumference) |
| **Pull-out strength** | ↑ outer diameter, ↑ cortical thickness · (✗ Wrong options: thread density (if 5–6 threads in cortex)) · ✓ **↓ cortex thickness ↓ pull-out** |
| **AO 3.5 mm cortical screw drill** | ✓ **2.7 mm** (TO key) |

**Screw types (TO)** — non-tapping (tap needed) · self-tapping (cutting flutes) · self-drilling · cancellous (deep threads; partially threaded = lag) · locking (threaded head) · **Herbert headless** (variable pitch → compression) · interference (no compression, presses graft)

### Plating principles (TO)

**MIPO ideal (TO)** — ✓ **comminuted metaphyseal fractures of long bones**\
✗ Wrong options: abraded skin · compound · diabetic

**DHS barrel (TO)** — standard (long) **38 mm** · short **25 mm** · lag-screw thread **22 mm**

**Bridge plating (TO)** — plate spans comminution, screws away from fracture

**Working length (TO)** — ✓ **distance between the screws/pins closest to the fracture on either side** · ↑ working length → more flexible, ↓ stress risers · unicortical screw has shorter working length than bicortical · ex-fix: pin/cluster spacing · >3 diaphyseal screws add little\
