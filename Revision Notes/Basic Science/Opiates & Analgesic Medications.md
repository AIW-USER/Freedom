# Opiates & Analgesic Medications

*Basic Science · 73 / 108*

## 1 Basics

### Pain physiology

**Definitions** — acute (tissue damage) · chronic (3–6 months, no damage needed) · pathological (neuropathic)

**Pathway** — nociceptors (**substance P** — depletion ↑ pain threshold; **capsaicin** depletes it) → **A and C fibres** → dorsal column + spinothalamic tract → thalamus · brainstem = endogenous opioid modulation

### Analgesic mechanisms (bupivacaine, gabapentin, acetaminophen)

| Drug | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| **Acetaminophen** | Central · ✓ **inhibits PGE2 production via IL-1β** · minimal anti-inflammatory · = aspirin for pain · **hepatotoxic** (avoid in liver disease) |
| NSAIDs | ↓ pain transduction (COX) |
| **Local anaesthetics (lidocaine, bupivacaine)** | ✓ **Block intracellular portion of voltage-gated Na⁺ channel** → no depolarisation |
| **Gabapentin / pregabalin** | ✓ **Bind α2δ subunit of presynaptic voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels** → ↓ excitatory neurotransmitter release, ↑ GABA · effective: postherpetic neuralgia, diabetic neuropathy, fibromyalgia · gabapentin = pregabalin, cheaper · **taper** to stop |
| Opioids (fentanyl) | **μ** (μ1 supraspinal analgesia, μ2 respiratory depression/dependence) + **κ** (sedation, spinal analgesia) |
| **Tramadol** | μ-opioid agonist |
| Midazolam | ↑ GABA-A Cl⁻ influx → hyperpolarisation |
| Rocuronium | Competitive ACh-receptor block |
| Phentolamine | α-adrenergic blocker |
| Cyclobenzaprine | Central · ↑ noradrenaline, serotonin receptors · helps first 2 weeks · not for neuromuscular spasticity · interacts with MAOIs, alcohol |

| Local anaesthetic | Duration (± adrenaline) | Max dose (± adrenaline) |
|---|---|---|
| **Lidocaine** (amide) | 2 h (4 h) | **4.5 mg/kg (7 mg/kg)** |
| **Bupivacaine** (amide) | 4 h (8 h) | **2.5 mg/kg (3 mg/kg)** |

**Amides** — lidocaine, bupivacaine (liver metabolism, renal excretion) · **esters** — procaine, cocaine, benzocaine

### Opioid metabolism

**CYP2D6 activates** — oxycodone, hydrocodone, tramadol, **codeine** · **SSRIs/SNRIs block CYP2D6 → analgesic failure**

**Morphine milligram equivalents** — **hydrocodone 1 · oxycodone 1.5 · hydromorphone 4** (3–7× morphine) → ✓ **hydromorphone > 2× oxycodone**

**Methadone** — synthetic, 3× morphine bioavailability, cheap · CYP450 · distribution 2–3 h, **elimination 15–60 h** → accumulates · **respiratory depression, torsades** → pain specialist

## 5 Management

### Special populations

**Morbidly obese** — ✓ **IV PCA dosed on ideal body weight**\
✗ Wrong options: actual weight · IM/SC (poor fat vascularity)

**Red hair (MC1R mutation)** — ✓ **need more anaesthetic (MAC) + higher postoperative opioid doses** (esp. women)

**Elderly** — ↑ opioid sensitivity (↑ fat %, ↓ hepatic/renal function)

**Children** — AAP dosing guidelines

### Opioid prescribing & consumption

**Bony > soft-tissue procedures** — ORIF distal radius uses **more** than cubital tunnel release

**Upper limb consumption** — 75% need ≤15 pills · 45% <5 · reasonable: **15 pills + 1 refill** (3–4% use it) · ✓ **>50% of prescribed pills unused**

**Carpal tunnel release** — ✓ **ibuprofen 600 mg ≈ oxycodone 5 mg** pain scores · most use <10 tablets for ≤2 days · opioids → more adverse events · open = endoscopic · standardised protocol ↓ prescribing

| Hand prescribing guide | Tablets |
|---|---|
| Trigger finger, lumps/bumps | 0 |
| Mucous cyst, CTR, de Quervain, Dupuytren | 10 |
| Wrist ganglion, hand ORIF, LRTI, tendon transfer | 20 |
| Large trauma, wrist fusion, DRUJ reconstruction | 40 |

**Chronic opioids** — pain specialist, written contract, in-person refills · for OA → ↑ surgical complications

**Weaning opioids 50% before THA** — ✓ **better outcome than not weaning, similar to opioid-naïve**

## 6 Complications

### Pain-pump chondrolysis

**Continuous intra-articular local anaesthetic infusion** — **FDA warning** · **glenohumeral chondrolysis** (onset ≥2 months, mean diagnosis 8.5 months) · pain, stiffness, ↓ ROM after shoulder scope → ✓ **shoulder radiographs to assess chondrolysis**
