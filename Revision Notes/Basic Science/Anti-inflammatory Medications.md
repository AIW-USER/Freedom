# Anti-inflammatory Medications

*Basic Science · 5 / 108*

## 1 Basics

### NSAID mechanism (COX-1, COX-2)

**NSAID effects** — anti-inflammatory · antipyretic · analgesic · **antiplatelet**

**Arachidonic acid** — directly metabolised by ✓ **cyclooxygenase** → prostaglandins, prostacyclin, **thromboxane**

**5-lipoxygenase** — arachidonic acid → **leukotrienes**\
✗ Wrong options: "1-lipoxygenase"

**HMG-CoA reductase** — mevalonate / cholesterol pathway (statins) · not arachidonic acid

**Steroids** — inhibit ✓ **phospholipase A2** → no arachidonic acid from membrane phospholipids · earliest step of cascade

![Arachidonic acid metabolism — steroids block PLA2, NSAIDs block COX](../../MCQ/attachments/q867-illa-aa-metabolism.jpg)

| Enzyme | Role |
|---|---|
| **COX-1** | Constitutive · **gastric mucosal protection (PGE2)** · renal blood flow · **platelet TXA2** |
| **COX-2** | Inducible · **inflammation, pain, fever** · no platelet effect · **needed for endochondral ossification** |

### Drug classes

| Drug | Binding | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Aspirin** | **Irreversible** · acetylates **serine 530** of COX | Blocks **TXA2** → antiplatelet · effect >1 wk (platelet life) |
| Ibuprofen | **Reversible**, competitive · non-selective COX-1 + COX-2 | GI ulcer, ↓ TX + ↓ PG |
| Indomethacin, naproxen, diclofenac, meloxicam | **Reversible** COX-1 | Indomethacin = **HO prophylaxis** · **highest GI toxicity** |
| Ketorolac (Toradol) | COX-1 selective (OB) | — |
| **Celecoxib**, rofecoxib | **COX-2 selective** | Spare platelets + gastric mucosa · **cardiac toxicity** |

### Aspirin — dose-dependent action

| Dose | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Low 75–81 mg/day** | Irreversibly acetylates COX-1 → ↓ platelet TXA2 → **antithrombotic** |
| **Intermediate 650 mg–4 g/day** | Inhibits COX-1 + COX-2 → ↓ PG → **analgesic, antipyretic** |

## 5 Management

**Indications** — pain · **heterotopic ossification prophylaxis**

**Contraindications** — severe renal disease · gastric ulcers

**COX-2 selective** — anti-inflammatory without COX-1 loss (gastric mucosa, renal blood flow, platelets) · usable **perioperatively** (no platelet effect) · **no more effective for OA** than non-selective

**Perioperative NSAID (OB)** — dose- and time-dependent effect on bone healing → **lowest effective dose, ≤7 days postop**

### Corticosteroids

**Systemic GC bone loss** — adverse skeletal effects **within 3 months** · at doses **as low as 2.5–5.0 mg/day** prednisone-equivalent · dose-dependent

**High fracture risk on long-term oral GC** — ✓ give **bisphosphonate** (antiresorptive) + calcium + vitamin D

**After stopping GC** — fracture risk normalises unless previously high-dose

**Epidural steroid injection** — ✓ **↓ lumbar BMD** (esp. postmenopausal women) · knee/other large-joint injections do not ↓ lumbar BMD

**Other GC effects** — transient **hyperglycaemia** (caution in diabetics) · interfere with RANKL pathway

## 6 Complications

### NSAID side effects & fracture healing

**Most strongly associated** — ✓ **renal impairment** (all NSAIDs, incl. COX-2)\
✗ Wrong options: hepatic · QTc · seizures · haematuria

**GI** — dyspepsia · ulcer perforation/bleeding/obstruction **2–4%** of chronic users · elderly **4–5×** risk of death from GI haemorrhage

**GI mechanism** — **non-specific COX-1 + COX-2 inhibition → ↓ thromboxane + ↓ prostaglandin** → uncoupled mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation, ↑ permeability, **disruption of intestinal phospholipid bilayer**

**GI risk factors** — **concurrent anticoagulant (most important)** · age >60 · prior GI disorder

**Other** — **↑ bleeding time** · **↑ creatinine** · **tinnitus**/sensorineural hearing loss (reversible) · cardiac toxicity · NOT ↑ IL-6 (NSAIDs ↓ IL-6)

**Indomethacin for HO** — most likely side effect = ✓ **GI ulceration**

![Peptic ulcer from NSAID use](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob9060-ulcer.jpg)

### Fracture healing

**Mechanism** — ↓ COX-2 → **↓ PGE2** → ↓ mesenchymal stem cell → osteoblast/chondrocyte differentiation (PGE2 acts via RANKL/OPG balance)

**Effect** — ✓ **↓ endochondral ossification** → ↓ callus → delayed union/nonunion

**Which healing** — **secondary (callus) > primary**

**Risk ↑** — prolonged use · large doses · **adults** · no effect shown in **children**

**COX-2** — thought **necessary for normal endochondral ossification** (animal data) · not "definitively shown in human studies"

### Intra-articular corticosteroid injection complications

**Described** — local flare · fat atrophy · skin pigmentation (depigmentation) · facial flushing · injection-site pain, bleeding

**Not described** — **apoptosis of myocytes**

## X vs Y

| | Steroids | NSAIDs |
|---|---|---|
| Enzyme inhibited | **Phospholipase A2** | **COX-1/COX-2** |
| Level in cascade | Earliest (no arachidonic acid) | Arachidonic acid → prostanoids |
| Leukotrienes | ↓ | Unaffected (5-LOX intact) |

| | COX-1 | COX-2 |
|---|---|---|
| Expression | Constitutive | Inducible (inflammation) |
| Function | Gastric protection, platelets, renal flow | Pain, fever, inflammation, **endochondral bone** |
| Selective drug | Ketorolac (OB) | **Celecoxib** |
| Inhibiting it causes | GI ulcer, bleeding | ↓ fracture healing, cardiac events |

## How it's asked

**"Arachidonic acid is directly metabolised by?"** → **cyclooxygenase**\
✗ Wrong options: 1-lipoxygenase · HMG-CoA reductase · carbonic anhydrase · thromboxane synthetase

**"All bind COX-1 reversibly EXCEPT?"** → **aspirin** (irreversible)\
✗ Wrong options: meloxicam, diclofenac, indomethacin, naproxen

**"Mechanism of aspirin?"** → **binding of COX-1 and COX-2**\
✗ Wrong options: vitamin K carboxylation · Xa via antithrombin · direct thrombin inhibition

**"Chronic ibuprofen → GI ulcer mechanism?"** → **non-specific COX-1 + COX-2 inhibition → ↓ thromboxane + ↓ PG → phospholipid bilayer disruption**

**"Indomethacin for HO prophylaxis — likely side effect?"** → **GI ulceration**

**"Side effect most strongly associated with NSAIDs?"** → **renal impairment**

**"NOT a side effect of prolonged NSAIDs?"** → **↑ circulating IL-6**\
✗ Wrong options: ulcer · ↑ creatinine · ↑ bleeding time · tinnitus

**"True about COX-2?"** → **necessary for normal endochondral ossification**\
✗ Wrong options: constitutive form · more gastric irritation · proven in humans

**"True about NSAIDs in femoral nailing?"** → **prolonged use ↓ endochondral ossification**\
✗ Wrong options: inhibits PLA2 · COX-1 selectivity ↓ GI effects · adults lower risk than children · primary > secondary healing

**"How do NSAIDs impair bone healing?"** → **↓ PGE2 and COX-mediated osteoblastic MSC differentiation**\
✗ Wrong options: ↑ osteoclastogenesis · ↑ COX-2

**"Corticosteroids inhibit?"** → **phospholipase A2**\
✗ Wrong options: lipoxygenase · COX-1 · TXA2 · prostacyclin

**"NOT a complication of corticosteroid injection?"** → **apoptosis of myocytes**

**"Most accurate about corticosteroid complications?"** → **epidural steroid injection ↓ lumbar BMD**\
✗ Wrong options: no effect <1 yr · bisphosphonate contraindicated · 2.5–5 mg safe · knee injection ↓ lumbar BMD
