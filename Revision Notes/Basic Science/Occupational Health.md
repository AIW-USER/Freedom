## 1 Basics

### Radiation safety (fluoroscopy technique, lead, pregnancy, cataract dose)

| ↑ Radiation exposure | ↓ Radiation exposure |
|---|---|
| Standing **close to emitter** · on the **emitter (tube) side** | **Distance** (inverse square law) · stay >2 m (6 ft) |
| **Larger body part** / large patient (↑ kVp, mA, scatter) | **Smaller part** · **mini C-arm** |
| Limb **close to X-ray source** | Limb/patient **close to image intensifier** (detector) |
| Large C-arm, **continuous fluoro**, cine | Short **beam-on time**, pulsed shots, **surgeon controls pedal** |
| No collimation | ✓ **Collimation** (↓ scatter, better image) |
| **Geometric magnification** (moving intensifier away / tube toward patient) | Electronic magnification only when needed |
| Intraop CT (O-arm) — patient cancer risk ~2× (2.3/10,000) | **Inverted beam** (tube beneath patient, intensifier above) |
| — | Lead apron (≥0.5 mm), thyroid shield (2.5×), **lead glasses ≥0.15 mm**, **sleeves + axillary supplements** |

**Highest scatter with a lateral shot** — ✓ **3 ft from the emitter side** (patient attenuates beam toward collector)

**False statement (TO)** — false: "**image magnified by moving X-ray tube away from patient**" · (geometric magnification = intensifier away → ↑ dose) · large patients need more exposure, mini C-arm less, collimation ↓ scatter **(TO)**

**Lead aprons (TO)** — ↓ exposure 4× (lateral), 16× (PA)

**Distance (TO)** — at 18 inches from beam only **0.1%** of direct dose · scatter <1 mSv outside 2 m · in-beam **1,200–4,000 mrem/min**

### Dose limits

| Site | Annual limit |
|---|---|
| **Whole body (TO)** | **20 mSv** |
| **Thyroid / eyes (TO, ICRP)** | **150 mSv** |
| **Hands (TO)** | **500 mSv** |
| **Lens — cataract threshold (NCRP)** | ✓ **50 mGy** cumulative/yr (1 mSv = 1 mGy in diagnostic imaging) |

**Surgeon cancer risk** — ↑ **breast cancer** (**upper outer quadrant** — commonest site, poorly covered by vests → ✓ **sleeves + axillary supplements**) · lateral images expose breast more · breast as radiosensitive as marrow, colon, lung · **cataracts** (lead glasses)

### Bloodborne pathogen transmission

| Exposure | Risk |
|---|---|
| **HIV needlestick** | ✓ **~0.3%** (↑ with blood volume, viral titre) · PEP with NRTIs protective |
| HIV mucous membrane | ~0.09% |
| HIV frozen bone allograft | **<1 per million** · donor screening key · none since **2001** (this note) |
| HIV blood transfusion | **1 / 500,000 units** (window period) |
| **Hepatitis B needlestick** | **37–62% seroconvert** · 22–31% clinical hepatitis |
| **Hepatitis C needlestick** | **0.5–1.8%** |

### Resident duty hours (ACGME)

**Limits** — **≤80 h/week** (4-week average) · +10% if educationally justified · **1 day off in 7** · in-house call **≤ every 3rd night** · **10 h off** between shifts · includes clinical, academic, admin, call

**Concern** — **continuity of care** (handoffs) · patient safety

**Handoff failures** — omitted current condition, no anticipatory guidance, no rationale, no tasks assigned · **signing out to physician extenders** is **not** cited

### Occupational lung disease (TO)

**Miller's asthma (TO)** — **mill-workers' / grain workers' asthma** · hypersensitivity to **granary weevil** (*Sitophilus granarius*)

**Millar's asthma (TO)** — **spasmodic croup** of **infants** (<2 yr, nocturnal glottic spasm) — TO keys "seen in infants" for this spelling — read the options

**"↑ patient exposure on C-arm?"** → **imaging a larger body part**\
