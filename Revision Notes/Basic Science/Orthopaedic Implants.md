## 1 Basics

### Determinants

**Implant behaviour** — **material properties** (modulus, strength) + **structural properties** (shape, size, moment of inertia)

### Screws

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Pitch | Distance between threads |
| Lead | Distance advanced per revolution |
| **Working length (distance) of a screw** | ✓ **Length of bone traversed by the screw** |
| Root / core diameter | Narrowest diameter |
| **Bending strength** | ∝ **inner (minor) diameter³** |
| **Pull-out strength** | ∝ **outer (major) diameter²** · ↑ with large major–minor difference, **fine pitch** |

**Pull-out in osteoporotic bone ↑ by** — cortical purchase · placement **parallel to trabeculae** · **locking (fixed-angle)** construct · **PMMA augmentation** \
✗ Wrong options: **tapping** (↓ pull-out)

**Pedicle screw pull-out** — most affected by **bone quality** · pedicle screws need the **largest force** to disrupt bone–implant interface (vs sublaminar cables, laminar/pedicle hooks)

### Plate biomechanics (compression, bridging, working length, stress shielding)

**Load-bearing device** — best on **tension side** · bending rigidity ∝ **thickness³** · titanium modulus closest to cortical bone

**Plate working distance** — ✓ **distance between the two screws closest to the fracture on either side** · ↓ working distance → ↑ stiffness

| Plate function | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| **Compression (DCP)** | ✓ **Cortical screw placed eccentrically (away from fracture) in oval hole** → head slides to centre → compression · transverse fractures |
| **Concave pre-bend** | Compresses **near and far cortices** (transverse fracture) |
| **Neutralisation / protection** | With lag screws — resists shear, rotation (absolute stability) |
| **Buttress / antiglide** | Resists shear |
| Tension band | Converts tension to compression |
| **Bridging** | Spans comminution, screws far from fracture · **relative stability**, length + alignment · preserves blood supply · **flexible but axially stable** (long working length) · indirect reduction (distraction) · locking not required |

| Stability | Healing | Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| **Absolute** | Primary (Haversian/intramembranous) | **Lag screw ± neutralisation / compression plate** · **low strain (<2%), high stiffness** · no micromotion |
| **Relative** | Secondary (endochondral) | Strain **<10%** (else fibrous union) · nail, bridge plate, ex-fix, cast |

**Strain** — residual 2 mm gap after lag screws (simple oblique) → **greater interfragmentary strain** than bridged comminuted fracture (strain shared)

**Open vs submuscular plating** — open plating ✓ **compromises periosteal and medullary blood flow more**

**LC-DCP vs DCP** — ✓ **less contact-induced osteopenia** (less plate–bone contact)

**Stress shielding** — ✓ **↓ physiological stress in bone because a stiffer structure shares load** → resorption (Wolff)

**Locked bridge plate** — biomechanically ≈ **external fixator without compression** ("internal fixator")

### Locking plates (indications, hybrid, unicortical, stiffness)

**Indications** — indirect reduction · **osteoporotic** diaphyseal/metaphyseal fractures · bridging severe comminution · **short periarticular segments** · can't plate tension side · periprosthetic · proximal humerus, distal radius, distal femur, proximal tibia

**Not indicated** — **compression plating of transverse fractures** · simple transverse **both-bone forearm** · oligotrophic diaphyseal nonunion · lateral malleolus (least evidence)

**Advantages** — less angulation in comminuted metaphyseal fractures · **axial load borne by the plate** (no plate–bone friction needed) · preserves periosteum · ↓ screw toggle

| ↑ Locked construct stiffness | ↓ Stiffness |
|---|---|
| **Bicortical locking screws** (vs unicortical — biggest difference in **torsion**) | Unicortical screws |
| **More screws** | **Far cortical locking screws** (flexible shaft, unthreaded near cortex) |
| **Longer plate** | Overdrilling near cortex |
| Screw divergence from hole **<5°** | Angular cross-threading |
| Screws **closer to fracture** (short working length) | Screws far from fracture |
| Stainless steel > titanium · supplemental medial plate | — |

**Unicortical locked vs bicortical non-locked** — ✓ **less torsional, greater axial** strength

**Locked vs non-locked rigid construct** — ✓ **↓ interfragmentary strain** (stiffer)

**Hybrid plating** — **non-locking screws first** (lag plate to bone = reduction aid) → then **locking screws** (fixed angle, resist **varus collapse**; osteoporotic diaphysis)

**Closed MIPO locked articular fracture** — risk ✓ **non-anatomic articular reduction** (articular surface must be reduced under vision) · percutaneous → less stripping, ↑ malunion

## 2 Mechanism

### Intramedullary nails (rigidity, curvature, dynamisation)

**Load-sharing** device

| Property | Proportional to |
|---|---|
| **Torsional rigidity** | **radius⁴** (shear modulus, polar moment) · ↑ by reaming (larger nail) · ↓ by **slotting** |
| **Bending rigidity — solid nail** | **radius⁴** |
| Bending rigidity — hollow nail | ~**radius³** |

**6 mm vs 12 mm solid nail** — ✓ **1/16 torsional rigidity** (½⁴)

**Radius of curvature** — ✓ **nail ROC > femur ROC** (straighter) → **anterior distal cortical perforation** risk · closed-section stiffer than slotted · start point affects varus/valgus · healing mostly endochondral

**Locking** — dynamic (axially + rotationally stable) · static (unstable) · **secondary dynamisation**: remove proximal screw or move it into the **dynamic (long) slot** — hole A

## 5 Management

### Total hip implants

**Stem rigidity** — length + radius

**Femoral component** — neutral or slight **valgus** (↓ moment arm, ↓ cement stress)

**↑ Femoral offset** — ✓ ↑ abductor moment arm, ↓ joint reaction force, ↓ abductor force needed\
✗ Wrong options: ↑ implant strain + medial cement-mantle strain

### Additive manufacturing (TO)

**Additive vs subtractive (TO)** — additive builds **layer by layer** (3D printing, minimal waste) · subtractive **mills from a solid block** (CNC, wasteful)

**History (TO)** — **Charles Hull 1983** → first practical printer = **stereolithography (SLA)** · 1945 Murray Leinster first fictional reference

**Workflow (TO)** — CT / MRI (**DICOM**) → **segmentation** → **STL** (Standard Triangular Language) → **G-code** (printer-readable, one cross-section per line) → print

**Methods by use (TO)** — **FDM → anatomical models** (PLA, PETG, ABS thermoplastics) · **powder bed fusion** (laser / electron beam on metal powder) **→ implants** · SLA / DLP → detailed polymer models

| Technology **(TO)** | Material | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **FDM** | PLA, ABS, PEEK filament | Anatomical models, splints · **PLA Tg ~60 °C → warps in autoclave** |
| **SLA** | Photopolymer resin (UV) | Highest resolution · models, autoclavable PSI (HDT >135 °C resins) |
| **SLS** | Polyamide (nylon), metal powders | ✓ **Autoclavable PSI cutting guides** (HDT >135 °C) |
| **SLM / DMLS** | **Ti-6Al-4V**, Co-Cr (full melt) | Custom implants, cages, tumour prostheses · ✓ **gradient porosity → ↓ effective modulus → ↓ stress shielding** |
| **EBM** | Titanium · **vacuum, >700 °C** | ✓ **Porous acetabular cups / cones (pores 400–600 μm)** · in-situ annealing ↓ residual stress |
| Bioprinting | Cells in bioink | Experimental |

**AM limits (TO)** — doesn't eliminate fatigue (micro-voids → need HIP/polishing) · introduces build-axis anisotropy · no intrinsic anti-biofilm property

**Solid titanium modulus (TO)** — ~110 GPa vs cortical 10–20, cancellous <3 GPa

| Application | Key facts (TO) |
|---|---|
| **Surgical planning models** | Complex fractures, deformity, tumour, arthroplasty · plate **pre-bending**, hardware choice, counselling |
| **Mirror imaging** | Print **normal contralateral** side as template · clavicle, calcaneus, pilon, ankle, hip deformity |
| **Orthoses** | 3D-printed **AFOs** replace plaster moulds · plantar fasciitis, post-stroke |
| **Non-custom implants** | Porous acetabular cups (thinner) · **titanium trabecular cages > 98% lumbar fusion** · porous metal ↓ stress shielding |
| **Custom implants / screws** | Bone loss after trauma / tumour · **3D-printed Ti screws ↑ pull-out** · ↓ inventory |
| **PSI** | Printed jigs + cutting guides · osteotomies (genu varum: NCCT → STL → virtual osteotomy → **printed wedge as custom chisel**), arthroplasty |

| Year | Implant material (TO) |
|---|---|
| 1960 | Stainless steel |
| 1970 | Cobalt-chrome |
| 1980 | Milled titanium |
| 2010 | **3D-printed titanium** |
| 2020 | **"Smart titanium"** — porous surface, optimised volume |

**Bioprinting (TO)** — living cells + biomaterial **bioinks** layer by layer · first cell printing **Thomas Boland 2003** · cartilage (MSC constructs, Shim et al.) · bone (calcium phosphate scaffolds) · limits: **cell viability under shear**, **poor vascularisation**, no standardisation

**4D printing (TO)** — 4th dimension = **time** · structures change shape with temperature, pH, magnetic field · e.g. **shape-memory scaffold softening at 37 °C**
