# Juvenile Idiopathic Arthritis

*Basic Science · 53 / 108*

## 1 Basics

### Definition & epidemiology

**JIA** — chronic autoimmune arthritis **>6 weeks** in a child **<16 yr** · **diagnosis of exclusion** (rule out infection, malignancy, rheumatic fever, IBD, SLE, Lyme)

**Sex** — **female > male**

**Joints** — **knee** > hand/wrist > ankle > hip > C-spine

### Genes & lab markers

| HLA | Subtype |
|---|---|
| **DR4** | Polyarticular |
| DR8, DR5, DR2.1 | Pauciarticular |

**RF** — ✓ **seronegative common** (RF+ <15% · 97% normal RF) · RF+ → chronic, destructive, progresses to adult RA

**ANA** — "positive is diagnostic" (Orthobullets) · "so often false-positive that alone it has no diagnostic value" (OB) · **ANA+ → ↑ uveitis risk**

**HLA-B27** — positive in only **~8%**

**ESR, CRP** — frequently elevated (OB) · Orthobullets: basic serology often normal, not diagnostic · anaemia, ↓ albumin, leukocytosis

**Serology does NOT confirm JIA** — "definitive diagnosis by serology" (false)

## 3 Presentation

**Symptoms** — **morning stiffness**, limp that **improves during the day**, joint swelling, fever, rash, visual change

**Diagnostic features (one required)** — rash · RF · **iridocyclitis** · C-spine involvement · pericarditis · tenosynovitis · intermittent fever · morning stiffness

### Classification

| Type | % | Joints | Features |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pauciarticular (oligoarticular)** | **50% — most common** | **<5**, large (knee, ankle), asymmetric | **Girls 1–4 yr (peak 2–4)** · M:F 1:4 · **affected limb LONGER** · **highest uveitis/blindness risk (TO)** · best prognosis |
| Polyarticular | 30% | **>5**, hand/wrist | Wrist ulnar deviation + flexion, MCP stiff in extension, radially deviated fingers · 60% remission |
| **Systemic (Still's)** | 20% | Multiple | **Fever, rash, splenomegaly**, anaemia, ↑ WBC, ESR, CRP, platelets · M = F · 5–10 yr · **lowest uveitis risk** · worst prognosis |

**Onset** — early (pre-teen) vs late (teens)

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Uveitis & ophthalmology screening

**Iridocyclitis (anterior uveitis)** — **often asymptomatic/indolent** · rapid irreversible vision loss if missed · up to 30% lose vision

**Risk by type (TO)** — ✓ **pauciarticular > polyarticular > systemic** · pauciarticular + **ANA+** highest (~75%, OB2240 · ~20% in OB1331 — read the options)

**Screening** — ✓ **urgent slit-lamp exam** · **every 4 months if ANA+**, twice yearly if ANA− · pupil asymmetry → immediate

| Eye finding | Condition |
|---|---|
| **Anterior uveitis** | **JIA (pauci, ANA+)** — urgent, preventable |
| Superior lens dislocation | Marfan |
| Inferior lens dislocation | Homocystinuria |
| Corneal clouding | Hurler (not preventable), Hunter (mild) |
| Ptosis | Pompe |

### Key radiology findings

**X-ray** — often **normal** early · late juxta-articular osteopenia, destruction

**C-spine** — kyphosis, facet ankylosis, **atlantoaxial subluxation** → ✓ **flexion–extension C-spine films before intubation/surgery**

## 5 Management

**First line** — **DMARDs** (etanercept — TNF inhibitor · rituximab — anti-CD20 · azathioprine — purine synthesis inhibitor · IA methotrexate/etanercept) · **intra-articular steroids** · NSAIDs/high-dose aspirin (less now) · gold occasionally · **frequent ophthalmology exams**

**Painless elbow loss of motion + multiple joint pains (13-yr-old pitcher)** — ✓ **DMARDs** (JIA, not OCD — ✗ Wrong options: microfracture, drilling, immobilisation)

**Operative** — synovectomy (failed medical) · **epiphysiodesis** for LLD (affected leg longer) · osteotomy deferred to maturity · arthrodesis/arthroplasty for severe disease

## 6 Complications

**Prognosis** — 50% resolve · 25% mild disability · 25% crippling arthritis or blindness

### Lead toxicity (related)

**Lead** — inhibits ✓ **PTHrP** → growth inhibition · ↓ BMD (animal studies)

## X vs Y

| | Pauciarticular JIA | Lyme arthritis |
|---|---|---|
| Joints | Knee, oligo | Knee, oligo |
| Rash | No | Erythema migrans |
| Antibiotic response | No | Yes |
| Association | **Uveitis** | Bilateral facial palsy, carditis |

## How it's asked

**"JRA type with highest iritis/blindness risk (TO)?"** → **pauciarticular**\
✗ Wrong options: systemic (lowest)

**"4-yr-old girl, knee effusion, limp better later in day, ANA+ low titre — next workup?"** → **ophthalmology evaluation**\
✗ Wrong options: skeletal survey, MRI, bone scan, factor levels

**"Highest risk of anterior uveitis needing ophthalmology?"** → **pauciarticular JRA with +ANA**\
✗ Wrong options: Marfan (lens) · Hunter (cornea)

**"Urgent ophthalmology to prevent irreversible complication?"** → **6-yr-old girl, 2 mo knee swelling, fever, ↑ ESR (JIA)**

**"5-yr-old, oligoarthritis better at end of day, antibiotics failed, RF− — association?"** → **uveitis**\
✗ Wrong options: facial palsy (Lyme)

**"FALSE about JIA?"** → **"definitive diagnosis by serology"**

**"4-yr-old with presumed JIA — likely serum result?"** → **normal RF**\
✗ Wrong options: normal ESR/CRP · ASLO · HLA-B27

**"Peak onset of pauciarticular JRA?"** → **2–4 years**

**"14-yr-old with JIA before rectal surgery — required work-up?"** → **cervical (flexion–extension) radiographs**

**"Painless elbow stiffness + polyarthralgia in young pitcher — first line?"** → **DMARDs**

**"Lead toxicity inhibits?"** → **PTHrP**
