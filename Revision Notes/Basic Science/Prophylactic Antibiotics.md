# Prophylactic Antibiotics

*Basic Science · 83 / 108*

## 1 Basics

### Overview

**Share of antibiotic use** — **25–50%** of all antibiotics used are prophylactic

**Indications** — **implanted foreign body** (e.g. IM nail — not "no antibiotics needed") · bone grafting · large dissection / dead space / haematoma · significant blood loss

**Not needed** — **carpal tunnel release** · diagnostic arthroscopy controversial

**Commonest pathogens** — **S. aureus** · **S. epidermidis** · aerobic streptococci · anaerobic cocci · gram-positive skin flora

**Most effective SSI measure** — ✓ **IV antibiotics within 1 h before incision** · lumbar spine: no iodine vs chlorhexidine difference, no benefit from >10 min scrub, laminar flow helps TJA but not shown in spine\
✗ Wrong options: MRSA screening/decolonisation · horizontal laminar flow · body exhaust suits

## 5 Management

### Timing before incision

| Agent | Timing |
|---|---|
| **Cefazolin** | ✓ **Within 1 h before incision** · (✗ Wrong options: after incision) · **before tourniquet inflation** |
| **Vancomycin** | **Within 2 h** before incision |
| Redosing | **3–4 h** after first dose for long cases (OB8335) · 2017 CDC: insufficient evidence on intra-op redosing |

**WHO Surgical Safety Checklist** — 3 checkpoints: **before induction · before skin incision · before leaving OR** · ✓ **antibiotics given within 60 min before incision**\
✗ Wrong options: blood products on standby · VTE prophylaxis ordered · implant rep · credentialing

### Agent selection & allergy (TO)

| Situation | Agent |
|---|---|
| **No β-lactam allergy** | ✓ **Cefazolin** (or cefuroxime) — **1st-generation cephalosporin** (S. aureus cover, low toxicity, penetration, half-life, cost) **(TO)** |
| **Dose** | **Double cefazolin if >80 kg** |
| **Penicillin / β-lactam allergy** | ✓ **Vancomycin** (TO key) or clindamycin **(TO)** |
| **MRSA history / high-prevalence area** | **Vancomycin + cefazolin** · judicious — ↑ **VRE** risk |
| β-lactam allergy + MRSA risk | Vancomycin ± **gentamicin or aztreonam** |
| Trauma IM nail, no allergy, no MRSA | ✓ **Cefazolin** (MRSA colonisation in trauma only **1–4%**) · (✗ Wrong options: vancomycin · cipro · metronidazole)|

| Antibiotic class | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| **Vancomycin** (glycopeptide) | ✓ **Bactericidal, blocks peptidoglycan cross-linkage · red man syndrome** |
| Cephalosporin | Bactericidal, peptidoglycan cross-linkage, no red man |
| Aminoglycoside | Bactericidal, irreversible **30S** |
| Macrolide / clindamycin | Bacteriostatic, reversible **50S** |
| Fluoroquinolone | Bactericidal, **DNA gyrase** |

**Other prophylaxis** — ciprofloxacin: GU surgery, brackish/salt/fresh water wounds · metronidazole: GI surgery, soil/faecal contamination

### CDC guidelines — duration

**AAOS (TJA)** — prophylaxis **should NOT exceed 24 h** after surgery

**CDC 2017 (Category IA)** — ✓ **clean + clean-contaminated: no additional doses after closure in OR, even with a drain** → single perioperative dose sufficient\
✗ Wrong options: continue until drains out · 24 h for drains · duration by length of surgery

**CDC 2002** — start within 1 h · appropriate agent · stop within **24 h**

### Dental prophylaxis in TJA patients (AAOS & ADA)

| At-risk TJA patients |
|---|
| **All patients for first 2 yr after TJA** |
| **Immunocompromised** — drug- or radiation-induced |
| **Inflammatory arthropathy** (RA, SLE) |
| Previous PJI · **type I DM** · malnourishment · haemophilia · HIV · malignancy |

**Evidence** — **limited / inconclusive** → clinical judgment

| Dental regimen (1 h before) | Drug |
|---|---|
| No penicillin allergy | **Amoxicillin or cephalexin 2 g PO** · IV/IM: cefazolin 1 g or ampicillin 2 g |
| Penicillin allergy | **Clindamycin 600 mg** PO (or IV) |

### Splenectomy prophylaxis

**Vaccines** — **pneumococcal** (all, incl. functional hyposplenism) · **Hib** + **meningococcal C conjugate** (if not immunised) · **influenza annually**

**Antibiotics** — **lifelong** oral phenoxymethylpenicillin or erythromycin

### Sterilisation & disinfection (TO)

**Sterilisation vs disinfection** — ✓ **endospores**: disinfection leaves spores · sterilisation destroys spores + pathogens **(TO)**

**Arthroscopic equipment** — ✓ **plasma sterilisation** **(TO)**\
✗ Wrong options: autoclave (damages delicate scopes) · ETO (**12-h cycle**, can't turn around same day) · formaldehyde dip

**Plasma systems (TO)** — **Sterrad-100**: vaporised **H₂O₂** (45 min) + **radiofrequency** (13.56 MHz, 300 W, 0.5 Torr) · **Plazlyte**: **peracetic acid** vapour + microwave plasma (O₂/H₂/Ar) **(TO)**

**Spaulding classification (1957)** — ✓ **principles of disinfection and sterilisation** of reusable devices by risk **(TO)**\
✗ Wrong options: skull, pelvic fractures, DDH

| Spaulding (TO) | Definition | Examples | Reprocessing |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Non-critical** | Intact skin only | BP cuffs, stethoscopes, toys, walking aids | Clean ± **low-level disinfection** |
| **Semi-critical** | Non-intact skin / intact mucosa, no penetration | Trans-rectal probes, specula | **High-level disinfection** minimum, sterilisation preferred |
| **Critical** | Penetrate tissue / bloodstream / sterile area | **Surgical + dental instruments** | **Sterilisation** after every use |

**Semi-critical touching open lesions** — treat as **critical** **(TO)**

| Methods (TO) | Agents |
|---|---|
| Disinfection — oxidising | Sodium hypochlorite · povidone iodine · H₂O₂ · peracetic acid |
| Disinfection — non-oxidising | Chlorhexidine · **70% alcohol** · quaternary ammonium · **glutaraldehyde**, OPA |
| **Sterilisation** | **Autoclave** · **ETO** · H₂O₂ / peracetic acid vapour or **plasma** |
