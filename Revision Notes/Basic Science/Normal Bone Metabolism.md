# Normal Bone Metabolism

*Basic Science · 68 / 108*

## 1 Basics

### Regulators

**Hormones** — PTH · calcitonin · sex hormones (oestrogen, androgens) · growth hormone · thyroid hormone

**Steroids** — vitamin D · glucocorticoids

### Peak bone mass & oestrogen

**Peak bone mass** — hip **16–25 yr** · greater in **men, African-Americans** · ✓ **timing varies by anatomic site** (appendicular before axial · women earlier at hip, later at spine; spine PBM may be 3rd–4th decade) · strong genetic + environmental influence · **correlates poorly** with postmenopausal BMD

**Bone loss** — **0.3–0.5%/yr** after maturity · **2–3%/yr** in untreated women years 6–10 after menopause

**Most important sex steroid for peak bone mass (men and women)** — ✓ **oestrogen**

**Osteoporosis risk factors** — age · female · early menopause · fair skin · family history of hip fracture · low weight · smoking · glucocorticoids · alcohol · low protein · anticonvulsants, antidepressants

### Hormones

| Hormone | Source | Net effect | Key mechanisms |
|---|---|---|---|
| **PTH** (84 aa) | **Chief cells** of 4 parathyroids | **↑ Ca, ↓ PO₄** | Bone: osteoblasts → IL-1, IL-6, **RANKL, M-CSF** → osteoclasts (**osteoclasts lack PTH receptor**) · kidney: ↑ **1α-hydroxylase** (25-OH → 1,25-(OH)₂D), ↑ Ca reabsorption, ↑ PO₄ excretion · gut: indirect via 1,25-D · **intermittent = anabolic** |
| **PTHrP** | Tumours | ↑ Ca | Same receptor (osteoblast, kidney) · metaphyseal dysplasia link |
| **Calcitonin** (32 aa) | Thyroid **parafollicular C cells** | Limited role · ↓ osteoclast number + activity | Osteoclasts **have** calcitonin receptor · ↑ in **medullary thyroid Ca / MEN II** · used in Paget's, osteoporosis, malignant hypercalcaemia |
| **Vitamin D** (secosteroid) | Skin UVB + diet | ↑ Ca (gut absorption, osteoclast activation) · ✓ **mineralisation of osteoid** | Liver → **25-OH-D** · kidney → **1,25-(OH)₂D** (↑ by ↑ PTH, ↓ Ca, ↓ PO₄) · or inactive **24,25-(OH)₂D** (↓ PTH, ↑ Ca, ↑ PO₄) · terminal osteoclast differentiation |
| **Oestrogen** | Ovaries | **Prevents bone loss** (↓ resorption + remodelling frequency) | ↑ femoral-neck BMD, ↓ hip fracture |
| **Growth hormone** | Pituitary | ↑ Ca (gut absorption, ↓ urinary loss) | Excess → **gigantism (proliferative zone)** |
| **Thyroid hormone** | Thyroid | Physeal growth (chondrocytes, **type X collagen**, ALP) · ↑ resorption | Excess thyroxine → **osteoporosis** |
| **Glucocorticoids** | — | ↑ bone loss | ↓ gut Ca absorption · ↓ formation (**cancellous > cortical**) — ↓ collagen, ↓ osteoblasts |

**Phenytoin** — impairs vitamin D metabolism

**Vitamin D deficiency** — osteomalacia, rickets

### Therapeutic oestrogen

**Benefit** — ↓ bone loss if started within **5–10 yr of menopause** · BMD gain **2–4%/yr** for first 2 yr

**Risks/effects** — ↑ **heart disease, breast cancer** · ↓ **hip fracture**, ↓ **endometrial cancer** (with cyclic progestin) · ↓ **urinary pyridinoline**, ↓ **serum ALP**

### Anabolic vs antiresorptive

**Anabolic** — ✓ **intermittent PTH / teriparatide** (receptor on osteoblasts, preosteoblasts) · sustained PTH → resorption

**Antiresorptive** — bisphosphonates · oestrogen · SERMs · **denosumab** (RANKL inhibitor) · **calcitonin**

## How it's asked

**"Peak bone mass most dependent on which sex steroid?"** → **oestrogen**

**"True of peak bone mass?"** → **timing varies by anatomic site**\
✗ Wrong options: always end of 2nd decade · independent of environment · correlates with postmenopausal BMD

**"Hormone over-secretion affecting proliferative zone (picture)?"** → **gigantism**

**"True of anti-osteoporosis drugs?"** → **intermittent PTH is anabolic**\
✗ Wrong options: calcitonin anabolic · denosumab ↑ osteoclasts · SERMs anabolic

**"NOT a bone formation marker (TO)?"** → **urine deoxypyridinoline**
