# Hypophosphatasia

*Basic Science · 47 / 108*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Severe (perinatal/infantile)** — 1 : 100,000–300,000 · **mild (adult)** — 1 : 500–3,000 · diagnostic delay common

### Genes & lab markers

**Gene** — ✓ **loss-of-function TNSALP** (tissue-nonspecific alkaline phosphatase) in osteoblasts

**Inheritance** — **severe forms autosomal recessive** · mild/adult often autosomal dominant

**Pathophysiology** — ↓ ALP → extracellular **↑ inorganic pyrophosphate (PPi)**, pyridoxal-5'-phosphate, **phosphoethanolamine** · **PPi inhibits hydroxyapatite** formation → hypertrophic-zone osteoid fails to mineralise → no zone of provisional calcification

| Lab | Finding |
|---|---|
| **Serum ALP** | ✓ **↓ (low)** |
| **Urine phosphoethanolamine** | ✓ **↑ — diagnostic** |
| Urine PPi | ↑ |
| Ca, PO₄ | Normal or ↑ (hypercalcaemia) |
| PTH | Normal |

**Low ALP in metabolic disease** — ✓ **hypophosphatasia**\
✗ Wrong options: FHH, X-linked hypophosphataemia, secondary/tertiary HPT (all ↑ ALP)

## 3 Presentation

**Rickets-like** — genu varum, short stature, ↑ fractures

| Form | Features |
|---|---|
| **Perinatal** | Hypomineralisation, long-bone (pretibial) bowing, osteochondral spurs, caput membranaceum, **respiratory failure / lung hypoplasia** |
| **Infantile** | **Craniosynostosis**, rachitic ribs, flared metaphyses |
| **Childhood** | **Premature loss of deciduous teeth**, short stature, bone pain, fractures |
| **Adult** | **Femoral pseudofractures**, **metatarsal stress fractures**, chondrocalcinosis, poor healing, osteopenia |

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

**X-ray** — "deossification" adjacent to physis (tongues of lucency) · **physeal widening** · flared metaphyses

## 5 Management

**Therapy** — summary lists **enzyme replacement**, anti-inflammatories, multidisciplinary care · treatment section: "no approved therapies, phosphate under investigation" (Orthobullets) — read the options

### Hypophosphataemic rickets — contrast

**Child with marked genu varum + wide physes** — ✓ **first get serum phosphate, calcium, ALP**\
✗ Wrong options: osteotomy, orthosis, scanogram, reassurance

**X-linked hypophosphataemic (vit D-resistant) rickets** — **X-linked dominant** · presents 18–24 mo · renal **phosphate wasting** · **↓ PO₄, ↑ ALP**, normal Ca, normal vit D · short (<10th centile)

**Treatment** — **phosphate + calcitriol** together · calcitriol alone → ✓ **no effect on alignment or height** · phosphate alone → ↓ Ca → 2° HPT → more phosphaturia

## X vs Y

| | Hypophosphatasia | Hypophosphataemic rickets |
|---|---|---|
| ALP | **↓** | **↑** |
| PO₄ | N / ↑ | **↓** |
| Urine | **Phosphoethanolamine** | Phosphate wasting |
| Inheritance | AR (severe) | X-linked dominant |

## How it's asked

**"Hypophosphatasia lab finding?"** → **↓ serum ALP**\
✗ Wrong options: ↓ urinary phosphoethanolamine · ↓ urinary PPi

**"Metabolic disorder with ↓ ALP?"** → **hypophosphatasia**

**"2-yr-old, marked genu varum, wide physes — first step?"** → **serum phosphorus, calcium, ALP**

**"Hypophosphataemic rickets treated with calcitriol only?"** → **no effect on alignment or height**
