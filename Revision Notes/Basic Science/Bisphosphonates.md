# Bisphosphonates

*Basic Science · 13 / 108*

## 1 Basics

### Overview

**Class** — stable **pyrophosphate analogues** · strong affinity for **hydroxyapatite** (calcium) → concentrate in bone → taken up by **osteoclasts** during resorption

**Net effect** — ✓ **inhibit osteoclast bone resorption** · ↓ recruitment + activity, ↑ **apoptosis** · loss of **ruffled border** (cytoskeleton/microtubules) · ↓ bone turnover · bone formed on therapy is **histologically normal**

**Excretion** — ✓ **renal** → avoid in severe renal disease

![Bisphosphonate disrupts the osteoclast ruffled border](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob9058-ruffledborder.jpg)

### Mechanism (nitrogen vs non-nitrogen containing)

| | **Nitrogen-containing** | **Non-nitrogen (simple)** |
|---|---|---|
| Drugs | **Alendronate, risedronate, pamidronate, zoledronate** (IV yearly) | **Etidronate, clodronate, tiludronate** |
| Target | **Farnesyl pyrophosphate (diphosphate) synthase** — **mevalonate (cholesterol) pathway** | Metabolised to **non-functional / toxic ATP analogue** (replaces terminal pyrophosphate of ATP) |
| Result | ↓ **protein prenylation** (geranylgeranylation) of **small GTPases** → osteoclast inactivation/apoptosis | Competes with ATP → targets **mitochondria** → **osteoclast apoptosis** |
| Potency | **Much more potent** | Earlier, weaker |

![Nitrogen vs non-nitrogen bisphosphonates](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob9058-types-of-bisphosphonates.jpg)

**Traps** — ✗ Wrong options: HMG-CoA reductase (statins) · caspase · MMP · reverse transcriptase · ↑ osteoblast activity · ↑ gut Ca · ↓ PTH

## 5 Management

### Clinical uses (OI, fibrous dysplasia, osteolysis, fracture prevention)

**Indications** — osteoporosis · metastatic bone disease · **multiple myeloma** · **Paget's** · **polyostotic fibrous dysplasia** · osteolysis after TJA · early AVN · **osteogenesis imperfecta** · **metastatic hypercalcaemia**

| Use | Outcome |
|---|---|
| **Osteoporotic VCF** (T-score ≤ −2.5) | 1 yr therapy → vertebral fracture risk ↓ **50–60%** |
| **Hip fracture risk** (N-BP, e.g. alendronate) | ↓ **~50%** (45–55%) |
| Non-vertebral fragility fracture | ↓ risk of multiple fractures |
| **After hip fracture fixation** | Resume at **1 week** → **no effect on outcome** (✗ Wrong options: nonunion, cutout, AVN, faster union) |
| **OI** — cyclical **IV pamidronate** | ↓ bone pain, ↓ fractures, ✓ **↑ bone density**, ↑ ambulation |
| **McCune-Albright / polyostotic FD** pain | ✓ **Bisphosphonates** (✗ Wrong options: calcitonin, PTH, vit D, MTX) |
| **Myeloma / metastases** (pamidronate, zoledronate) | ↓ skeletal events, pain, radiotherapy need · **no survival benefit** · monthly IV most effective |
| **TJA osteolysis** | ✓ ↓ periprosthetic bone loss (alendronate, 6 yr DXA) · may ↑ BMP-2 |
| **Precollapse AVN** | Investigational · RCT collapse at 2 yr **6.9% vs 76%** placebo |

### Contraindications

**Severe renal disease** — renal excretion

**After lumbar fusion** — animal models: ↑ fusion-mass size but **↓ fusion rate**

**Invasive dental work** — complete **before** starting bisphosphonates

## 6 Complications

### Osteonecrosis of the jaw & dental work

**ONJ** — ✓ chronic **alendronate** / bisphosphonates\
✗ Wrong options: warfarin, LMWH, diclofenac, ketorolac

**Risk factors** — **IV** therapy · duration **>36 months** (length of exposure most important) · elderly **myeloma** · **recent dental extraction** · incidence **4–17%**

**Teriparatide** — used to treat bisphosphonate ONJ

![Osteonecrosis of the jaw](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob9058-on-of-jaw.jpg)

### Atypical femur fractures

**Site** — ✓ **subtrochanteric / proximal diaphyseal femur** · **lateral cortical thickening ("beaking")** → transverse fracture

**ASBMR features** — no/minimal trauma · **transverse lateral, oblique medial** · periosteal reaction · behaves as stress fracture

**Numbers** — **17–29%** of subtroch fractures atypical · **28% bilateral** → ✓ **contralateral films** · risk 3.2–50 / 100,000 person-yr → up to **100** with prolonged use

**Prodrome** — thigh pain (often misdiagnosed as quadriceps strain)

![Lateral cortical beaking, subtrochanteric femur](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob9058-stress-fx-therapilates-files-wordpress-com.jpg)

**Incomplete AFF with "dreaded black line"** — stop bisphosphonate + **prophylactic IM fixation**

### Atypical femur fracture — drug management after nailing (TO)

**Denosumab AFF** — ✓ **stop denosumab → start teriparatide (anabolic window) → mandatory short-acting bisphosphonate after the anabolic course** **(TO)**

**Why** — denosumab **doesn't embed** in matrix → withdrawal = **rebound osteoclastogenesis**, rapid bone loss, **multiple vertebral fractures** → bisphosphonate "locks in" bone **(TO)**

**Traps (TO)** — ✗ Wrong options: drug holiday alone (rebound) · continue denosumab ± teriparatide (suppressed turnover) · switch to IV zoledronate (keeps turnover paralysed)

### AFF nailing technique (TO)

| Step | Rule (TO) |
|---|---|
| Implant | **Full-length cephalomedullary nail** (protects neck) · nail with **smaller radius of curvature (1.0–1.5 m)** vs standard 2.0–3.0 m |
| Plates | **Contraindicated** — fatigue failure before union |
| Entry | **Lateralised** (± slightly posterior) trochanteric start |
| Reaming | ✓ **Over-ream ≥1.5–2.0 mm** above nail diameter · (✗ Wrong options: line-to-line (incarceration)) · sharp reamers, slow, flexible shafts (avoid anterior blowout, thermal necrosis) |
| Nail size | Largest feasible (fatigue life) · never deliberately under-ream/downsize as routine |
| Extreme bow | Downsize slightly **or** closing-wedge osteotomy at apex |
| Biology | Pack **reamer dust** into fracture · **no distraction** (→ nonunion) — back-slap or compress |

### Paediatric effects

**Osteopetrosis-like radiographic changes in children** — ✓ **bisphosphonates** (osteoclast is the common pathway)\
✗ Wrong options: RANKL antibody, TNF inhibitor, BMP, fluoroquinolone

## X vs Y

| Drug | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| **Bisphosphonate** | Inhibits osteoclasts |
| **Denosumab** | Anti-RANKL antibody · no matrix binding → rebound on withdrawal |
| **Teriparatide** | PTH analogue → **activates osteoblasts** (anabolic) |
| SERM (raloxifene) | Oestrogen action on bone → ↓ vertebral fractures |
| Corticosteroid | Cause of osteonecrosis |
