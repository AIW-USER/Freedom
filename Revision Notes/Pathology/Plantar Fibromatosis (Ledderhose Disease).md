# Plantar Fibromatosis (Ledderhose Disease)

*Pathology · 55 / 66*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Nature** — benign **myofibroblast + collagen** proliferation of the plantar fascia · **commonest soft-tissue neoplasm of the foot**

**Demographics** — **males** · **middle-aged / elderly** (any age) · Caucasian

**Bilateral** — **25–50%**

**Risk factors** — male · Caucasian · **epilepsy** · repeated trauma · **diabetes** · **long-term alcohol** · chronic liver disease · other fibrosing conditions

**Associations** — **Dupuytren's** (plantar fibromatosis in **25%** of Dupuytren's) · **Peyronie's**

**Genetics** — basis **unclear** (unlike Dupuytren's)

### Anatomy

**Plantar fascia** — **3 bands** (central, medial, lateral) · **medial band most affected** · origin medial + anterior calcaneus → 5 digital slips at MTPJs → base of proximal phalanges

### Phases

| Phase | Feature |
|---|---|
| **Proliferative** | ↑ Fibroblast activity, cell proliferation |
| **Involutional / active** | **Nodule** formation |
| **Residual / resting** | ↓ Fibroblast activity, collagen maturation, scar/contracture |

## 3 Presentation

**Subcutaneous thickening / nodules**, **medial plantar** foot · usually asymptomatic, may become painful · **digital contractures rare**

**Exam** — look for Dupuytren's, Peyronie's · ankle/hindfoot motion · Achilles/gastrocnemius contracture

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

**X-ray (AP + lateral)** — usually normal

**MRI (study of choice)** — nodular thickening on **medial** plantar aponeurosis · **low T1** (acellular, high collagen) · **low/medium T2** (high-signal areas if aggressive)

![Low T1 nodule on plantar aponeurosis](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob8061-t1-mri.jpg)

**US** — less reflective fibroma within bright fascia · **comb sign** — alternating hypo- and iso-echoic linear bands

![Comb sign](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob8061-ultrasound-comb-sign.jpg)

### Histology

**Dense fibrocellular tissue** — mature collagen, fibrocytes at varying maturity · **no atypia, no abnormal mitoses** · gross: lobulated firm irregular mass

**Differential** — post-traumatic neuroma · fibrosarcoma · clear-cell sarcoma

## 5 Management

```
Plantar fibromatosis
├─ Small / minimally painful → OBSERVATION + SUPPORTIVE
│      (NSAIDs, orthotics, PT, steroid injection, radiotherapy)
└─ Large, painful, activity-limiting → TOTAL FASCIECTOMY ± RADIATION
```

### Treatment & recurrence

| Excision | Recurrence |
|---|---|
| **Local excision** | **57–100%** |
| Wide excision (2–3 cm margins) | 8–80% |
| **Total fasciectomy (preferred)** | **0–50%** |

**Technique** — avoid incisions directly over nodules · partial fasciectomy → **excise nodule + overlying skin** (± skin graft)

**Surgical complications** — skin necrosis · nerve entrapment (medial plantar nerve) · **loss of arch height / windlass** · wound breakdown

**Radiotherapy** — best evidence: **randomized double-blind sham-controlled trial (2023)**, durable efficacy at ≥1 yr · **post-op RT** for high-grade lesions to cut recurrence

**Evidence grades** — collagenase: insufficient (case reports) · orthotics: expert opinion (Level V) · surgery: no Level I superiority data

## 6 Complications

**Recurrence** — **60% of excised lesions** · recurs **more aggressive** · risk: **bilateral**, **multiple nodules**, **family history** · prevent with adjuvant RT

**Prognosis** — malignant transformation **rare**

## How it's asked

**"Plantar fascial lesion — excisional biopsy's likely complication?"** → **recurrence**\
✗ Wrong options: metastasis · wound breakdown, nerve injury, windlass loss = wide excision problems

**"Bilateral plantar nodules, failed orthotics/NSAIDs — highest-grade evidence?"** → **radiotherapy (RCT, double-blind sham-controlled)**\
✗ Wrong options: collagenase RCTs · surgery RCTs
