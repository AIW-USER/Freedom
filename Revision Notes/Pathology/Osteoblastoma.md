# Osteoblastoma

*Pathology · 46 / 66*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Rare** — **1% of primary bone tumours**, 1–5% (3%) of benign · **10% of osseous spinal neoplasms** · less common than osteoid osteoma

**Demographics** — **M:F 2:1** · **10–30 yr**

**Site** — **posterior elements of spine + sacrum (30–40%; >35–40%)** · long bones (lower > upper), diaphysis/metadiaphysis (femur commonest), never epiphyseal · **mandible ("cementoblastoma")**

**Spinal level (TO)** — **cervical > lumbar** > thoracic > sacrum (17%) — no firm consensus; keyed answer **cervical**

**Posterior element tumours (TO)** — **osteoid osteoma > osteoblastoma > ABC** · GCT → vertebral body

**Nature** — **benign, locally aggressive, progressive**\
✗ Wrong options: self-limiting

### Genes & lab markers

**FOS (chr 14) / FOSB (chr 19) rearrangements**

**Nuclear β-catenin** staining → osteoblastoma · **cytoplasmic/membranous** → osteosarcoma

**Associations** — **secondary ABC 10–40%** · **oncogenic osteomalacia**

## 3 Presentation

**Pain** — slowly progressive **dull ache** · night pain **not typical** · **partial NSAID response** (TO FNB stem: night pain improving with NSAIDs, posterior element, 28 yr → keyed osteoblastoma)

**Spine** — **neurologic symptoms** (root compression, weakness, rarely paraplegia), scoliosis

**Signs** — swelling, muscle atrophy, limp · may be incidental

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

**X-ray** — **lytic or mixed lytic-blastic**, **radiolucent nidus 2–10 cm** (>2 cm), intralesional densities · reactive sclerosis · expansile · 66% cortical, 33% medullary · **25% look aggressive** (mistaken for malignancy)

![Osteoblastoma on CT](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob8013-osteoblastoma-ct.jpg)

**CT** — full characterization: size, location, cortical breach, soft-tissue component

**MRI** — reactive soft-tissue/marrow **oedema** · T1 low–intermediate, T2 intermediate–high

**Bone scan** — **hot**, intense focal uptake

### Histology

**Like osteoid osteoma, more giant cells** · **sharp demarcation nidus–reactive bone** · nidus of **immature osteoid + plump osteoblasts** (normal nuclei) · irregular osteoid/woven spicules rimmed by osteoblasts in **loose fibrovascular stroma** · mitoses but **no atypical** ones · rich vascularity, extravasated RBCs · **no permeation of host lamellar bone**

![Osteoblastoma histology](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob8013-histology-c-parsons-moved.png)

### Osteoid osteoma vs osteoblastoma

| | **Osteoid osteoma** | **Osteoblastoma** |
|---|---|---|
| Incidence | 10% of benign | 3% of benign |
| Size | **<2 cm** (<1.5) | **>2 cm** (3–3.5) |
| Site | >50% long-bone diaphysis (proximal femur > tibia > phalanges > spine) | **>35% posterior spine** |
| Natural history | **Self-limited** | **Progressive** |
| Symptoms | **Night pain, relieved by NSAIDs** · painful scoliosis, lesion on **concave** side | Dull ache, **partial NSAID response**, neuro symptoms |
| Spine management | Nonoperative first → RFA/resection | **Always surgery** |

### Differential

| Lesion | Distinguishing |
|---|---|
| **Osteosarcoma** | Periostitis, soft-tissue mass, **permeates lamellar bone**, malignant cells, cytoplasmic β-catenin |
| **ABC** | Fluid levels, septations, blood-filled sinusoids |
| Osteoid osteoma | <2 cm, thick sclerosis around small nidus |
| GCT | Epiphysis–metaphysis, giant cells with stromal nuclei |
| **Chondroblastoma** | Epiphyseal, chondroid |
| NOF | Eccentric metaphyseal, storiform |
| FD | Ground glass, Chinese letters |
| Osteomyelitis · stress fracture | Sinus/skin changes · no large lucent lesion |

## 5 Management

```
Osteoblastoma (doesn't respond to nonoperative care)
├─ Most → CURETTAGE / MARGINAL EXCISION + BONE GRAFT (± phenol, cryo)
├─ Large, locally aggressive, recurrent, some spinal → EN BLOC RESECTION (lower recurrence)
└─ Selected small lesions → ABLATION (RFA / MRgFUS)
```

**Observation** — rarely (keeps growing)

### Treatment — ablation (MRgFUS vs RFA) (TO)

**MRgFUS** — **less invasive** (needleless, no ionizing radiation), real-time thermal map · does not penetrate deeper (deep lesions → RFA needle reaches better)

**RFA** — CT-guided, ~17G needle, single-day stay

**Both** — equally safe/effective · equal resolution of **bone oedema** (oedema loss = inactive lesion) · post-ablation **"halo/ring"** sign (necrotic centre + sublethal rim) · not "RFA less chondral damage" (claim not supported)

## 6 Complications

**Local recurrence** — **15–25%**, mostly within **2 years**

**Prognosis** — excellent, most disease-free after first surgery

## How it's asked

**"Most common site of osteoblastoma (spinal levels)?"** → **cervical spine** (keyed) **(TO)**

**"28-yr-old, posterior element tumour, night pain improves with NSAIDs, no deficit?"** → **osteoblastoma** (keyed; ✗ Wrong options: ABC · GCT · lymphoma) **(TO)**

**"17-yr-old, distal humeral bone-forming lesion extending beyond cortex, large osteoblasts, no atypia?"** → **osteoblastoma**\
✗ Wrong options: osteoid osteoma (smaller) · OS · chondroblastoma · GCT

**"18-yr-old knee, well-marginated lytic/blastic, benign osteoblasts?"** → **osteoblastoma**\
✗ Wrong options: FD · OS · NOF · osteomyelitis

**"23-yr-old, back pain radiating down leg relieved by NSAIDs, mixed lesion >2 cm?"** → **osteoblastoma**\
✗ Wrong options: OS · GCT · ABC · stress fracture

**"True about osteoblastoma ablation?"** → **MRgFUS less invasive than RFA** **(TO)**

**"Touch-me-not lesions EXCEPT?"** → **osteoblastoma** (painful, needs intervention) **(TO, FD chapter)**
