# Giant Cell Tumor

*Pathology · 22 / 66*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Nature** — **benign but locally aggressive** · 3–5% of primary bone tumours · 1.7/million

**Demographics** — **females** (unlike most bone tumours) · **30–50** (Orthobullets) · 20–40 / 22–40 (OB, TO) · **skeletally mature, closed physes** (immature → metaphyseal)

**Multicentric** — **1–2%** **(TO)**  · synchronous or metachronous (TO)

**Population** — more common in **Indian and Chinese** populations (TO)

### Anatomic distribution

**Order** — **distal femur > proximal tibia > distal radius > sacral ala** · **50% around the knee**

**Axial** — **sacrum** commonest axial site (10% sacrum + spine) · spine → **vertebral body**\
✗ Wrong options: **posterior elements**

**Others** — proximal humerus · **phalanges of hand** · apophysis (like chondroblastoma) · proximal fibula

**Not typical** — **diaphysis** (e.g. tibial shaft)

**"Extra-articular GCT" (TO)** — **GCT of tendon sheath** (giant cell synovioma) → **extremities** (hand/wrist flexor tendons) · firm painless nodule · excision

### Cells

| Cell | Identity |
|---|---|
| **Type I — mononuclear stromal cell** | **Neoplastic cell** · fibroblast-like, mesenchymal stem-cell features · **overexpresses RANKL** · carries **H3F3A (H3.3 G34W)** mutation |
| Type II — monocyte/macrophage | Recruited from blood · **giant-cell precursor** |
| **Type III — multinucleated giant cell** | **Hallmark** · osteoclast-like (TRAP, carbonic anhydrase II, cathepsin K, vacuolar ATPase) · **non-neoplastic bystander** · express **RANK** |

### Genes & lab markers

**H3F3A (H3.3 G34W)** — driver mutation in stromal cells **(TO)**

**RANKL overexpression** by stromal cells — **key pathogenic pathway** → denosumab target

**c-myc / p53** alterations — in **metastatic** lesions (lung mets overexpress c-myc)

**Telomeric associations** — 80% · IGF-I/II activity in type II/III cells

**Brown tumour** — must check **serum calcium / PTH** (hyperparathyroidism, multiple lesions)

## 3 Presentation

**Pain** — insidious, activity/night/rest · joint-referred · difficulty walking

**Signs** — mass, tenderness, swelling, **effusion** (periarticular), ↓ ROM, antalgic gait

**Pathologic fracture** — common (subchondral collapse)

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

**X-ray** — **eccentric, purely lytic, epiphyseal–metaphyseal**, extends to **subchondral bone** · **no sclerotic rim** (or partial) · poorly defined transition · expands/breaks cortex · "neocortex" · no matrix · intra-articular extension rare · **soap-bubble** appearance **(TO — FNB 2021)**

![Eccentric lytic subchondral lesion](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob8046-case-j-ankle-xray-parsons-moved.jpg)

**MRI** — extent · **dark T1 (clear demarcation from fatty marrow)**, bright T2, avid enhancement · intermediate T2 signal (like lymphoma, Ewing) · fluid levels = **secondary ABC**

**Bone scan** — variable / hot

**Chest X-ray or CT chest** — **mandatory** staging (lung mets)\
✗ Wrong options: bone scan · skeletal survey · SPEP/UPEP · MRI brain/spine · mammogram

**Biopsy first** — even when imaging looks classic → **incisional biopsy** before reconstruction (malignancy can mimic)

### Histology

**Diagnostic** — numerous **multinucleated giant cells (40–60 nuclei)** in a sea of mononuclear stromal cells · **giant-cell nuclei identical to stromal nuclei** · storiform areas, reactive bone, foam cells, haemosiderin

![Giant cells with nuclei identical to stromal cells](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob8046-histology-b-parsons-moved.png)

**Secondary ABC** — 10–14% (Orthobullets/OB) · 20% (TO) · GCT has **enhancing solid component** (primary ABC doesn't)

### Campanacci grading (TO)

| Grade | Behaviour | Radiographic features |
|---|---|---|
| **I** | Latent | Well-marginated, **thin rim of mature bone**, cortex intact or slightly thinned |
| **II** | Active | Well-defined, **no radiopaque rim**, cortex thinned + **moderately expanded** but present · (II + fracture graded separately) |
| **III** | Aggressive | **Fuzzy borders**, permeative · **soft-tissue mass** not contained by bone shell |

**Enneking (benign)** — most GCT **stage 2**; aggressive = **3**

### Differential

| | Epiphyseal | Usually curettage + graft |
|---|---|---|
| **GCT** | ✓ | ✓ |
| Chondroblastoma (epiphyseal) | ✓ | ✓ |
| ABC · osteoblastoma · CMF | — | ✓ |

**Chondroblastoma** — **open physes**, chicken-wire calcification, marked oedema, S-100

**Brown tumour** — multiple lesions, abnormal calcium

**Giant-cell-rich / telangiectatic / fibroblastic OS** — **atypia + osteoid**

**Chordoma** — sacral **midline**, physaliferous cells

**ABC** — fluid levels, USP6 · **UBC** — no cortical destruction

**ABC vs GCT (TO)** — ABC: **younger**, fluid levels **without solid component** · GCT: **solid component** ± secondary ABC (fluid levels)

**Clear cell chondrosarcoma** — epiphyseal GCT mimic · sclerotic rim possible (TO)

**MRI (TO)** — epi-metaphyseal, eccentric · **T1 hypointense**, **T2 hyper / mixed** · solid component · **whole-bone MRI** for skip lesions

**"Giant-cell-rich lesion" report (TO)** — **not = GCT** · insist on definite diagnosis (exclude **giant-cell-rich OS**, ABC, brown tumour) · **biopsy before curettage**, target solid part

## 5 Management

```
Giant cell tumour of bone
├─ Joint salvageable (most; Campanacci I–II, many III) → EXTENDED CURETTAGE + BURR + ADJUVANT + PMMA/GRAFT ± FIXATION
│      (pathologic fracture is NOT a contraindication)
├─ Structural loss / collapsed subchondral bone → EN BLOC RESECTION + RECONSTRUCTION
│      ├─ Expendable bone (proximal fibula, distal ulna) → RESECTION ± LCL/biceps repair
│      └─ Vertebral body → TOTAL EN BLOC SPONDYLECTOMY
├─ Unresectable (sacrum, spine, pelvis) / multiply recurrent → DENOSUMAB ± EMBOLIZATION (RT last resort)
└─ Hand with cortical breakthrough → INTERCALARY RESECTION or RAY AMPUTATION
```

### Treatment — extended curettage with adjuvants

**Preferred treatment** — **extended curettage + adjuvants + reconstruction** (TO FNB 2024 — key not given; sources' standard)\
✗ Wrong options: curettage alone · curettage + graft · primary RT

**Technique** — **large cortical window (≥ lesion size)** — no curetting under overhanging ridges · head lamp, dental mirror, angled curettes · **high-speed burr 1–2 cm** in all directions · pulsatile lavage · don't perforate subchondral plate

**Most important step** — **aggressive curettage with high-speed burr** (no adjuvant proven superior)

**Burr depth** — **1–2 cm** (Orthobullets) · **1–2 mm** of peripheral bone (TO) — read the options

**Burr (TO)** — **prerequisite, not an adjuvant** · "never curette without a burr" · adjuvants do not significantly improve control when curettage + burr done well

**Exposure (TO)** — muscle-preserving (lift **vastus lateralis off septum**) · isolate with **H₂O₂-soaked mops** · remove soft-tissue component **en bloc** · **start small window, enlarge** after debulking (avoid spillage) · headlight + loupes

**C-arm (TO)** — before curettage, after gross curettage, after burring · **pulsed lavage before + after adjuvants**

| Adjuvant (TO) | Penetration | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Liquid nitrogen** | **14 mm** (maximal) | **Fracture, nerve injury** |
| **Phenol** | **< 1 mm** | Wash with **ethanol** · soft-tissue irritation |
| **Argon beam** | **4 mm** | Thermal coagulation · expensive |
| **Hydrogen peroxide** | Contact only | Always used, cheap |

**Workflow before reconstruction (TO)** — **change gloves, drapes, instruments** · separate trolley · **harvest graft before curettage** (iatrogenic donor-site implantation reported)

| Filling (TO) | For | Against |
|---|---|---|
| **Bone graft** | Biological · subchondral protection | Donor morbidity · delayed WB · **hard to detect recurrence** |
| **PMMA cement** | Immediate · **early WB** · **easier recurrence detection** · heat kills residual cells | — |
| **Sandwich** (subchondral graft + cement) | Minimal subchondral bone | **Subchondral collapse** |

**Cement and OA (TO)** — cement **not proven to cause arthritis** · **loss of subchondral bone** is the risk factor

**Recurrence on X-ray (TO)** — **lysis around cement / graft** · progressive lucency · new soft-tissue shadow

**Adjuvants** — **phenol**, **liquid nitrogen (cryo)**, **hydrogen peroxide**, **argon beam**, electrocautery, ethanol (= phenol) · burr alone recurrence **12%**

**Filling** — **PMMA** (thermal necrosis; recurrence seen as **lucency at bone–cement interface**) · graft · ± internal fixation · not DBM + marrow aspirate (higher recurrence)

| Treatment | Local recurrence |
|---|---|
| Curettage + graft alone | **20–40%** (OB: 15–25%) |
| + adjuvant | **3–10%** |
| High-speed burr only | 12% |
| Overall | ~20% |

**Distal radius, cortices + joint intact** — **curettage + liquid nitrogen + PMMA**\
✗ Wrong options: en bloc + allograft · fusion with fibula · RT

**Hand** — no breakthrough → **curettage + cementing** · breakthrough → **intercalary resection** (free fibula) **or amputation** (lowest recurrence)

### En bloc resection

**Lateral condyle with collapsed subchondral bone + fracture** — **condylar resection + osteoarticular allograft**\
✗ Wrong options: cement injection · chemo · RT · amputation

**Proximal fibula** — **en bloc resection + repair LCL + biceps femoris to lateral tibia** → lowest recurrence, low instability\
✗ Wrong options: curettage · structural allograft · denosumab (high recurrence) · observation

**Knee** — hemicondylar osteoarticular allograft or rotating-hinge endoprosthesis · **distal radius** aggressive → resection + proximal fibular autograft (arthroplasty or arthrodesis) · distal ulna/clavicle → resection alone

**Resection indications (TO)** — **path fracture with deformity** · **recurrence with joint destruction** · **insufficient residual / subchondral bone** · **expendable bones** (distal ulna, proximal fibula, proximal radius) · massive soft-tissue extension · en bloc in **one piece**

**Path fracture / recurrence (TO)** — fracture **not an absolute indication** for resection (curettage + cement in many) · recurrent GCT → curettage often still possible; resect if joint destroyed

| Distal radius reconstruction (TO) | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|
| **Fibular arthroplasty** | **Wrist motion**, cosmesis | **Unstable**, incongruent joint, donor morbidity |
| **Ulnar translocation** | **Stable**, vascularised, no donor morbidity → preferred when stability needed | Cosmetic deformity (**hour-glass wrist**) |

**Spine** — total en bloc spondylectomy (± staged anterior/posterior, cage/cement) → 10-yr disease-free up to 100%

### Denosumab

**Drug** — **fully human IgG2 monoclonal antibody against RANKL (receptor activator of nuclear factor kappa-B ligand)** (mimics OPG) · FDA approved for unresectable GCT / when resection → severe morbidity, adults + skeletally mature

| Option | Identity |
|---|---|
| **Denosumab** | ✓ **Human mAb → RANKL** |
| Osteoprotegerin | Decoy receptor → RANKL |
| Infliximab | Chimeric mAb → TNF-α |
| Rituximab | → CD20 |
| Trastuzumab | → HER2 |
| (none) | mAb or decoy → RANK itself |

**Mechanism (TO)** — kills/prevents **osteoclast-like giant cells** · **stromal (neoplastic) H3F3A clone survives**, quiescent\
✗ Wrong options: directly kills stromal cells · halts stromal proliferation · OPG antagonist

**Effect** — **85–90% giant-cell destruction** · sclerosis, cortical reconstitution, pain relief

**Histology after denosumab (TO)** — **loss of giant cells + dense fibro-osseous tissue, new woven bone, stromal spindling** (mimics low-grade fibro-osseous lesion)

**Dosing (TO)** — **120 mg SC days 1, 8, 15, 29** → **every 4 weeks** · neoadjuvant 3–6 mo · **calcium + vitamin D** mandatory

**Neoadjuvant downside (TO)** — **sclerotic, ossified margin obscures tumour boundary → ↑ recurrence after curettage** (20–30% vs 10–15%)

**Stopping (TO)** — **rebound RANKL surge** → rapid recurrence + **severe rebound hypercalcaemia** (3–9 mo) · recurrence 20–60% · mitigate with **zoledronic acid**, tapering, calcium monitoring

**Long-term (TO)** — **1–6% malignant transformation**, ↑ with prolonged therapy · ONJ 1–5% · atypical femoral fracture (>2 yr)

**Side effects** — hypocalcaemia (contraindication: severe hypocalcaemia), nasopharyngitis, arthralgia

**Denosumab extras (TO)** — **hard shell** eases dissection · **inform pathologist** (mimics low-grade sarcoma) · **not an adjuvant** after curettage · **single preop dose** → ↓ vascularity where no tourniquet (pelvis, proximal humerus) · "masks, does not kill stromal cells"

**Zoledronic acid (TO)** — **4 mg monthly** · osteoclast apoptosis · may ↓ recurrence · inoperable tumours

**Bisphosphonates** — osteoclast inhibitors · postop → **↓ recurrence 25–30%** **(TO: used to prevent recurrence)** · zoledronic acid stabilises inoperable disease · denosumab > bisphosphonate\
✗ Wrong options: calcitonin · teriparatide · methotrexate

### Radiation & embolization

**Radiation** — only inoperable / multiply recurrent (sacrum, large vertebral) · **~15% malignant transformation** (megavoltage)

**Embolization** — unresectable sacral/pelvic GCT, monthly until palliation · preop to ↓ blood loss · **definitive for inoperable** pelvis/sacrum/spine, combined with **zoledronic acid** (TO) · **pelvic GCT → embolization + limited resection + curettage** (TO)

**Not used** — chemotherapy · RFA

## 6 Complications

### Lung metastasis & chest CT

**"Benign" metastasizing tumour** — lung mets **histologically identical** to primary (also chondroblastoma)

| Source | Lung metastasis rate |
|---|---|
| Orthobullets | **2–4%** (1–6% on chest CT) |
| OB | **~2% overall, 6% if recurrent → counsel ~4%** · 1–9% · ~5% |
| TO | **~3%** |
| Spine GCT series | 13.7% |

**Risk factors** — **local recurrence** · **aggressive / stage 3 (Campanacci III)** **(TO)** · **wrist/hand, distal radius**, proximal femur, sacrum/spine · younger · immunocompromised

**Timing** — 18–24 mo, most within 3 yr

**Treatment** — **resect (metastasectomy)** → ~76–80% disease-free · denosumab · RT

**Mortality with lung mets** — **~15% (TO)** · 17% (Orthobullets) · 5-yr disease-free 76%

**Recurrence** — usually **benign GCT again** (identical histology) · diagnose with CT-guided biopsy · lucency at cement interface

### Malignant transformation

**Rates** — <1% (Orthobullets) · **1.4–6.6%** (OB) · <5% overall malignancy · radiation → ~15%

**Primary vs secondary malignant GCT** — secondary after **radiation** or multiple resections · latency **9 yr after RT**, **19 yr spontaneous**

**Late lytic change years after curettage + biopsy shows high-grade sarcoma** — **wide resection + reconstruction** (+ medical oncology)\
✗ Wrong options: repeat curettage · denosumab

**Pathologic fracture** — periarticular lesions · **cryotherapy (liquid nitrogen)** → fracture + **vascular injury**, wound/nerve problems (phenol too)

### Chemotherapy response grading (Huvos)

*(osteosarcoma necrosis grading, filed under this topic's TO set)*

| Huvos grade | Tumour necrosis | Response |
|---|---|---|
| **I** | 0–50% | Poor (little/none) |
| **II** | 51–90% | **Poor** (partial) |
| **III** | 91–99% | **Good** |
| **IV** | 100% | Complete |

**88% necrosis** → **Huvos II, poor responder** → continue standard MAP (EURAMOS-1: changing regimen doesn't help) **(TO)**
