# Nodular Fasciitis

*Pathology · 44 / 66*

## 1 Basics

**Nature** — **commonest fibrous soft-tissue lesion** · **pseudosarcomatous, self-limiting reactive** proliferation of **fibroblasts + myofibroblasts**

**Demographics** — M = F · adults **20–40** · young people

**Site** — **half in upper limb** · **volar forearm**, back, chest wall, head and neck

**Trigger** — often a recalled trauma

## 3 Presentation

**Rapidly enlarging mass over 1–2 weeks** (present only weeks) · **1–2 cm** · painful in half

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

**X-ray** — normal

**MRI** — deep-seated, **inhomogeneous** mass · **nodularity extending along fascial planes** · **avid gadolinium enhancement** · usually superficial, also intramuscular · T2 hyperintense, T1 iso/hypo

![Nodular fasciitis on MRI](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob8063-46a-moved.jpg)

### Histology

**Plump, immature-looking fibroblasts** (like granulation tissue) in **short, irregular bundles and fascicles** · **dense reticulin network** · small amounts of mature collagen

**Evolution** — short duration → **myxoid** · longer → **hyaline fibrosis**

## 5 Management

**Marginal resection**

| Treated with marginal excision |
|---|
| **Nodular fasciitis** |
| Periosteal chondroma |
| **Neurilemmoma** |
| Epidermal inclusion cyst |
| Glomus tumour |

## How it's asked

**"40-yr-old, rapidly enlarging shoulder mass, short irregular fibroblast fascicles in reticulin?"** → **nodular fasciitis**\
✗ Wrong options: fibrosarcoma · intramuscular haemangioma · schwannoma · rhabdomyoma

**"Deep fascia lesion, homogeneous T2-bright (vs schwannoma)?"** → **nodular fasciitis** arises in deep fascia
