# Heterotopic Ossification

*Pathology · 26 / 66*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Definition** — bone in atypical **extraskeletal** soft tissue · after **local trauma**, **neurologic injury** (within **2 months** of brain/spinal cord injury), **THA/TKA**, or spontaneous

**Demographics** — **M:F 2:1** · men with **hypertrophic OA**, women >65

**Commonest location** — **between muscle and joint capsule**

| Injury | Order of joints |
|---|---|
| **TBI / stroke** | **Hip > elbow > shoulder > knee** · **spastic side** · elbow HO commoner after brain injury · knee rare |
| **Spinal cord injury** | **Hip > knee > elbow > shoulder** · hip flexors + abductors · medial knee |

**Subtypes** — neurogenic HO · traumatic myositis ossificans · **fibrodysplasia ossificans progressiva** (Münchmeyer)

### Pathophysiology

**Mechanism** — activation/proliferation of **mesenchymal stem cells** → osteoprogenitors · genetic predisposition · experimental HO linked to **BMP** expression

### Risk factors

| Risk factor | Detail | Incidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Injury Severity Score** | **Highest direct correlation** with severe HO after knee dislocation (✗ Wrong options: GCS · timing · number of ligaments · open reconstruction) | 11% |
| **TBI** | Spastic limbs · prolonged coma (20–30 yr), **prolonged ventilation** | 11% |
| **Spinal cord injury** | **Complete > incomplete** | 20% |
| **Decubitus ulcers** + SCI/TBI | — | **70%** |
| **Burns** | Local and remote | — |
| **Amputation through zone of injury** | **Blast** mechanism worst | 63% |
| **Antegrade femoral nail** | **Piriformis** entry | 25% |
| **Distal femoral traction pin** | Large Steinmann pin → **quadriceps HO** (rare) (✗ Wrong options: proximal tibia, calcaneus, radius, metacarpal) | Rare |
| **Acetabular approach** | **Extended iliofemoral > Kocher-Langenbeck > ilioinguinal** · combined approaches → HO is the commonest complication | 25% |
| **THA** | **Psoas tenotomy**, **cementless** (debris, marrow spillage) · **Smith-Petersen and Hardinge > transtrochanteric > posterior (lowest)** · DA "**bikini**" incision ↓ high-grade HO (4% vs 12%) | 53% (significant 5%) · symptomatic ~7% |
| **TKA** | **Anterior femoral notching**, quadriceps trauma, periosteal stripping, **MUA**, high lumbar BMD · **ankylosing spondylitis** (~20%) | — |
| **Other** | **DISH**, hypertrophic OA, previous HO | — |
| **Not proven** | **Open fractures** · **fluorosis** **(TO)** | — |

**THA risk groups (TO)** — **high**: previous HO, hypertrophic post-traumatic arthritis, **men with hypertrophic OA** · **moderate**: AS, DISH, Paget, Parkinson, RA, unilateral hypertrophic OA

### Genes & lab markers

| Marker | Use |
|---|---|
| **ALP >250 IU/L** | ↑ early, nonspecific · **↑ at 12 wk post-op predicts** HO · **cannot determine maturity** (OB) · normalization used in timing (Orthobullets) |
| **CRP** | Tracks inflammatory activity better than ESR · **normalization ≈ maturity** |
| **ESR >35 mm/h** | At 12 wk after THA predicts HO |
| **CK** | Extent of muscle involvement |

## 3 Presentation

**Painless loss of ROM** → ADL interference · ankylosis

**Early** — **warm, painful, swollen** joint, effusion, fever (mimics infection/DVT) · CRPS association

**Complications** — **nerve impingement** (ulnar neuropathy at elbow) · contractures → **decubitus ulcers** · skin maceration · pathologic fracture of osteoporotic bone · TKA: quadriceps snapping, patellar instability

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Modality | Role |
|---|---|
| **Triphasic bone scan** | **Best for early diagnosis**, most used · decreasing activity = maturing |
| **Ultrasound** | Early hip HO — echogenic surface + acoustic shadow |
| **X-ray** | Not early (calcium appears **7–10 days**; may be normal ~3 wk) · **maturity = cortex + sharp demarcation + trabeculae** · **Judet views** for hip |
| **CT (3D)** | **Preoperative planning** · characterize synostosis |

**Histology** — mature trabecular (lamellar) bone + fatty marrow

### Brooker classification (HO after THA)

| Class | Description |
|---|---|
| **1** | **Islands of bone** in soft tissue |
| **2** | Spurs from pelvis/femur, **≥1 cm** between opposing surfaces |
| **3** | Spurs, gap **<1 cm** |
| **4** | Apparent **bony ankylosis** |

**Brooker 1–2** — don't affect THA outcome · **3–4** → pain, ↓ ROM

## 5 Management

### Prophylaxis (radiation, indomethacin)

**Indomethacin** — **75 mg/day for 10 days to 6 weeks** · proven for **distal biceps repair** (radioulnar synostosis)\
✗ Wrong options: 7.5 mg

**Radiation** — single **700 cGy** · **4 h preop or within 72 h postop** · **<550 cGy ineffective** · blocks mesenchymal → osteoprogenitor differentiation\
✗ Wrong options: 500 cGy · 2 wk preop · fractionated after

**Bisphosphonates** — not effective: only delay mineralization of osteoid · HO forms when stopped

**Distal biceps** — **single-incision** technique → lower synostosis rate than two-incision

**Not prophylaxis** — PTH 1-34, VAC chemo

### Timing of excision & maturity

```
Established, symptomatic HO
├─ Immature (<6 mo, bone scan active, ALP/CRP raised) → OBSERVE, PT, re-evaluate
├─ Mature + severe ROM loss / function → WIDE EXPOSURE + EXCISION (CT 3D first)
│      └─ Post-op → INDOMETHACIN (5 d) and/or RT + EARLY GENTLE ROM
└─ HO after THA/TKA, Brooker 3–4 → EXCISION (± revision)
```

| Setting | Wait before excision |
|---|---|
| **General trauma** | **6 months** |
| **Spinal cord injury** | **1 year** |
| **TBI** | **1.5 years** |
| THA | 6–18 months to mature |

**Maturity** — **radiographs: sharp cortical margins** ✓ · marked ↓ bone scan activity + normal ALP (Orthobullets) · (✗ Wrong options: time alone · bone scan always negative · ALP (OB)) · some data: early = late results

**Elbow HO 2 months after TBI with mild ulnar symptoms** — **continue, re-evaluate in 3 months**\
✗ Wrong options: excision ± transposition · RT · isolated release

**Terrible triad, synostosis at 3 months** — **await maturation → CT 3D in 3 months**\
✗ Wrong options: indomethacin/RT now · radial head excision

**Revision THA, 18 months, painful restricted hip** — **excision**\
✗ Wrong options: PT · alendronate · indomethacin · RT (none treat established HO)

**Observation case** — **HO 4 months after THA** (still maturing)\
✗ Wrong options: mature lesions with functional loss

## 6 Complications

**Surgical** — haematoma/bleeding · **infection** (↑ in arthroplasty with HO) · fracture of osteoporotic bone · **AVN** with extensive stripping

**Recurrence** — ∝ severity of neurological injury

## How it's asked

**"Highest correlation with severe HO after knee dislocation?"** → **Injury Severity Score**

**"Amputee at highest HO risk?"** → **through the zone of injury after blast** (propane explosion, transfemoral)

**"Traction pin causing HO?"** → **distal femur** (quadriceps)

**"NOT shown to increase HO?"** → **open fractures** (✓ ventilation, SCI, blast amputation, burns)

**"NOT a risk factor after THR?"** → **fluorosis** (✓ previous HO, hypertrophic OA, DISH) **(TO)**

**"Commonest complication of combined-approach acetabular fixation?"** → **HO**

**"Ankylosing spondylitis TKA — likely complication?"** → **HO**

**"DA THA 'bikini' vs longitudinal incision?"** → **lower HO**\
✗ Wrong options: less LFCN injury · more wound problems

**"What could have prevented hip HO after head injury + fracture fixation?"** → **700 cGy within 72 h of surgery**

**"Prevent radioulnar synostosis after distal biceps repair?"** → **indomethacin 75 mg daily × 6 wk**

**"True about elbow HO 4 months post-injury?"** → **radiographs assess maturity**\
✗ Wrong options: early excision ↓ recurrence · ALP for maturity

**"Painful Brooker 3–4 HO 18 months after revision THA?"** → **excision**

**"Elbow HO 2 months after TBI with mild ulnar paraesthesia?"** → **re-evaluate in 3 months**

**"Terrible triad, forearm stuck at 3 months?"** → **allow maturation, CT 3D in 3 months**

**"Which HO patient can simply be observed?"** → **4 months after THA**

**"Brooker classification is for?"** → **HO around hip after THR** **(TO)**
