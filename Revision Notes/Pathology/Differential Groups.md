## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Workup of incidental lesion

**Principle** — a radiograph suggesting a **potentially malignant process** needs **complete staging before any treatment**, even if it looks like GCT

**Next step** — **cross-sectional imaging (MRI) of the limb** · then chest imaging, labs, ± bone scan → sarcoma centre referral if needed

**Severe pain, insists on treatment today** — **admit for parenteral analgesia + tumour work-up**\
✗ Wrong options: outpatient opioids

**Not appropriate first** — intra-articular steroid injection · physiotherapy + NSAIDs · hip radiograph

**Starting treatment before oncologic work-up** — can be devastating (contamination, lost limb salvage)

```
Aggressive-looking bone lesion on X-ray
├─ First → MRI OF THE LIMB (local staging)
├─ Then → CHEST IMAGING + LABS ± BONE SCAN
└─ Then → BIOPSY AT TREATING CENTRE → DEFINITIVE TREATMENT
```

## How it's asked

**"50-yr-old, obese, knee pain, aggressive proximal tibial lesion, wants treatment today — next?"** → **MRI of the lower extremity**\
✗ Wrong options: steroid injection · opioids · PT + NSAIDs · hip X-ray
