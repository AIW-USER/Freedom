# Differential Groups

*Pathology · 14 / 66*

## 1 Basics

### Clinical clues (TO)

**Triad** — **clinical + radiology + pathology must correlate** · if one does not fit, rethink the diagnosis (TO)

| Age (TO) | Likely lesions |
|---|---|
| **Children / adolescents** | SBC, ABC, FD, NOF, osteochondroma, chondroblastoma, osteoid osteoma, osteoblastoma, LCH · malignant: **Ewing, OS, leukaemia** |
| **Young adult / middle age** | **GCT**, FD, enchondroma, chondroblastoma (to 25), **adamantinoma**, chondrosarcoma (from middle age) |
| **Elderly** | **Metastasis (commonest)**, **myeloma**, lymphoma, chondrosarcoma, **secondary OS** |

**Age** — strongest single diagnostic clue · most bone tumours **10–25 yr** · older: chondrosarcoma, haemangioma, metastasis (TO)

**Pain pattern (TO)** — **night pain → osteoid osteoma** · intermittent → FD · **painless → enchondroma, osteochondroma**

**Site clues (TO)** — knee: GCT, OS, Ewing · scapula/pelvis: Ewing (youth), chondrosarcoma (adult) · wrist (distal radius epiphysis): **GCT** · hand: **enchondroma until proven otherwise** · proximal humerus cyst: **SBC**

**Commonest bone tumour overall** — **metastasis** (TO) · breast → **mixed lytic-sclerotic** mets (TO)

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### X-ray: 7-point systematic read (TO)

**Checkpoints** — **age (skeletal maturity) · location · type of lesion · matrix · zone of transition · periosteal reaction · soft-tissue component** (TO)

| Longitudinal location (TO) | Lesions |
|---|---|
| **Epiphyseal** | **GCT, chondroblastoma, clear cell chondrosarcoma** |
| **Metaphyseal** (majority) | Osteochondroma, enchondroma, UBC, ABC, NOF, FD, OS, chondrosarcoma, Ewing (50%) |
| **Diaphyseal** | NOF, FD, OFD, **adamantinoma**, **Ewing**, myeloma, metastasis, osteoid osteoma, osteoblastoma, occasional OS |

| Transverse location (TO) | Lesions |
|---|---|
| **Cortical** | NOF, **adamantinoma**, OFD, **osteoid osteoma**, infection, rare metastasis |
| **Medullary** | Enchondroma, chondrosarcoma, myeloma, lymphoma, metastasis |

| Type of destruction (TO) | Lesions |
|---|---|
| **Geographic** (well defined) | UBC, ABC, **GCT**, NOF |
| **Moth-eaten** | **Myeloma** (Approach note) · **osteosarcoma** (Overview note) — read the options |
| **Permeative** | OS, **Ewing**, leukaemia, metastasis · Overview note adds chondrosarcoma |

| Matrix (TO) | Appearance | Lesion |
|---|---|---|
| **Fibrous** | **Ground glass** | Fibrous dysplasia |
| **Chondroid** | **Popcorn / rings & arcs** calcification | Enchondroma, chondrosarcoma |
| **Osteoid** | Ivory / cloud-like ossification | **Osteosarcoma** |
| **None** | — | GCT, ABC, Ewing |

**Zone of transition (TO)** — **narrow = benign** · **wide = malignant**

| Periosteal reaction (TO) | Lesion |
|---|---|
| **Solid** | NOF, osteoid osteoma, infection |
| **Lamellated (onion peel)** | **Ewing** |
| **Spiculated (sunburst)** | **Osteosarcoma** |
| **Codman triangle** | Any aggressive lesion |

**Soft-tissue component** — **GCT** and malignant bone tumours (TO)

### MRI (TO)

**Uses** — extent of disease · relation to NV bundle · **skip lesions → always full-length MRI** · ideal biopsy site (solid enhancing part)

**Fluid-fluid levels** — **ABC vs telangiectatic OS** — always differentiate (TO)

**T2-hyperintense lobulated lesion** — **chondroid** tumour (TO)

### Benign lesion pearls (TO)

**Fibrous cortical defect** — **commonest lesion of bone** · 10–25 yr · **distal femoral metaphysis** · eccentric lytic, incidental, no treatment · **NOF = larger version** (TO)

**Chondromyxoid fibroma** — NOF-like X-ray but **proximal tibial metaphysis**, **no calcification** (TO)

**Simple bone cyst** — central, symmetrical expansion · aspirate **straw-coloured** · steroid injection first, then curettage + graft (TO)

**ABC** — eccentric, expansile, septated · aspirate **blood** · extended curettage, **liquid nitrogen = most potent adjuvant** (TO)

**Pathological fracture through a cyst** — treat the fracture; fracture **often heals the cyst** (TO) · Orthobullets: fracture healing usually does **not** resolve a UBC — read the options

**Osteochondroma** — symptomatic only → **extraperiosteal excision + wedge of normal bone** (TO)

**Enchondroma of hand** — DD **spina ventosa (TB dactylitis)** (TO)

**Enchondroma vs bone infarct** — enchondroma has **no central lucency** (TO)

**GCT sandwich technique** — lesion **<1 cm from subchondral bone** (TO) · **pulsatile** soap-bubble epiphyseal lesion, 20–40 yr (TO)

### Workup of incidental lesion

**Principle** — a radiograph suggesting a **potentially malignant process** needs **complete staging before any treatment**, even if it looks like GCT

**Next step** — **cross-sectional imaging (MRI) of the limb** · then chest imaging, labs, ± bone scan → sarcoma centre referral if needed

**Severe pain, insists on treatment today** — **admit for parenteral analgesia + tumour work-up**\
✗ Wrong options: outpatient opioids

**Not appropriate first** — intra-articular steroid injection · physiotherapy + NSAIDs · hip radiograph

**Starting treatment before oncologic work-up** — can be devastating (contamination, lost limb salvage)

```
Aggressive-looking bone lesion on X-ray
├─ First → MRI OF THE LIMB (local staging)
├─ Then → CHEST IMAGING + LABS ± BONE SCAN
└─ Then → BIOPSY AT TREATING CENTRE → DEFINITIVE TREATMENT
```

## How it's asked

**"50-yr-old, obese, knee pain, aggressive proximal tibial lesion, wants treatment today — next?"** → **MRI of the lower extremity**\
✗ Wrong options: steroid injection · opioids · PT + NSAIDs · hip X-ray

**"Fluid-fluid levels on MRI — must differentiate?"** → **ABC vs telangiectatic osteosarcoma** (TO)

**"Epiphyseal lesions?"** → **GCT, chondroblastoma, clear cell chondrosarcoma** (TO)

**"Onion peel vs sunburst?"** → **Ewing vs osteosarcoma** (TO)
