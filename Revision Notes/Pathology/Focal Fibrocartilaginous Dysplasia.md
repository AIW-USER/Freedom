# Focal Fibrocartilaginous Dysplasia

*Pathology · 21 / 66*

## 1 Basics

**Nature** — rare, benign tumour-like bone dysplasia · aetiology unknown

**Age** — **infants and toddlers**

**Site** — **tibia** commonest · also humerus, ulna/forearm, phalanx, femur

**Association** — a variant of **infantile tibia vara** that can **resolve without surgery** (vs Blount)

## 3 Presentation

**Unilateral tibia vara** — painless bowing in a walking toddler

**Gait** — knee hyperextension with **lateral thrust**

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

**X-ray (pathognomonic)** — **abrupt varus at the metaphyseal–diaphyseal junction** · **medial cortical sclerosis** · **lucency in the medial cortex** just proximal to it (fibrocartilaginous tissue)

![Focal fibrocartilaginous dysplasia of the tibia](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob8079-focal-fibrocartilaginous-dysplasia-xray.jpg)

**Bone scan** — shows deformity, **not "hot"** (not aggressive)

**MRI** — only in ambiguous cases

### Histology

**Dense bland fibrous tissue** — layers of collagenous fibrous tissue + thick dense fibrocartilage · no malignant features

## 5 Management

```
Focal fibrocartilaginous dysplasia
├─ Most (tibia) → OBSERVATION (± orthosis) — spontaneous correction with physeal growth
└─ Persistent / progressive deformity, joint function compromised → CORRECTIVE OSTEOTOMY (or curettage)
```

**Resolution** — **~half of tibial cases** resolve spontaneously · much less often in femur and humerus

**Osteotomy risks** — secondary tibia valgus, neurologic injury

## X vs Y

| | **FFCD** | **Infantile Blount** |
|---|---|---|
| Side | **Unilateral** | Often bilateral |
| Apex | **Metaphyseal–diaphyseal junction**, medial cortical lucency + sclerosis | Proximal medial physis / metaphyseal beak |
| Natural history | **Often resolves** | Often progresses |

## How it's asked

**"18-month-old, unilateral bowing, medial metaphyseal cortical lucency, bland fibrous histology?"** → **focal fibrocartilaginous dysplasia**\
✗ Wrong options: NF · toddler's fracture · NOF · ABC

**"Treatment?"** → **observation** · osteotomy only if persistent/progressive
