# Paget's Disease

*Pathology · 50 / 66*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Nature** — idiopathic **abnormal bone remodelling** — excessive resorption + abnormal new bone formation (osteitis deformans) · **2nd commonest chronic bone-remodelling disorder** in the US after osteoporosis · **3–4% of US population >40**

**Demographics** — **>40 yr**, peak **5th decade** · **M = F** · **Caucasians** (northern European / **Anglo-Saxon**)

**Distribution** — **monostotic or polyostotic**

**Sites** — **femur > pelvis > tibia > skull > spine** (Orthobullets) · **pelvis > tibia** (TO) · also clavicle · **sacrum** typically involved

### Pathophysiology — osteoclast abnormality

**Primary abnormality** — ↑ **osteoclastic bone resorption** (✗ Wrong options: osteoblast · osteocyte · chondroblast · synoviocyte) → **focally increased remodelling** (✗ Wrong options: ↓ vascularity · sclerotic bone replacing marrow)

**Cause** — **slow virus** (intranuclear nucleocapsid-like inclusions) — **paramyxovirus**, **RSV**

**Osteoclast diseases** — Paget · **osteopetrosis** (defective osteoclastic resorption) · pycnodysostosis — share **nuclear inclusion bodies** \
✗ Wrong options: OI (osteoblast) · osteochondroma (chondrocyte) · myeloma · osteoporosis (uncoupling, not inherited)

**Osteopetrosis genes** — carbonic anhydrase II · vacuolar proton pump · chloride channel 7

### Genes & lab markers

**Inheritance** — mostly spontaneous · familial clusters, **~40% autosomal dominant** · family history in **15%** (OB)

**SQSTM1 (p62/sequestosome 1), 5q35-QTER** — most important · severe disease

**TNFRSF11A** — insertion mutation in the gene encoding **RANK**

**Labs** — ↑ **serum ALP** (most useful test; tracks extent + activity) · ↑ **urinary collagen cross-links** · ↑ **urinary hydroxyproline** · ↑ **urinary N-telopeptide, α-C-telopeptide, deoxypyridinoline** · **calcium + phosphate normal** · PTH normal (↑ in small subset with very active disease)

**α-CTX** — rises more than β-CTX → ↑ α/β CTX ratio **(TO)** · **urinary NTX** — sensitive, monitors treatment response **(TO)**

**Hypercalcaemia** — only with **immobilization** (↑ osteocyte **sclerostin**), fracture, predominant lytic phase, or sarcoma **(TO)** · ambulatory → normal calcium

**vs myeloma** — **ALP ↑ in Paget**, normal in myeloma **(TO)**

### Phases

| Phase | Process | X-ray |
|---|---|---|
| **Lytic** | Intense osteoclastic resorption, hypervascular | Lucent, expanded, thin intact cortex · **blade of grass / flame** advancing edge · skull **osteoporosis circumscripta** |
| **Mixed** | Resorption + compensatory formation (weak brittle bone) | Lysis + sclerosis, coarse trabeculae |
| **Sclerotic (burnt-out)** | Osteoblastic formation dominates · dense avascular **mosaic** bone | **Bone enlargement, cortical thickening** — commonest presentation |

**All three phases can coexist in one bone**

## 3 Presentation

**Asymptomatic** — commonest · found incidentally (↑ ALP, X-ray for another complaint)

**Pain** — commonest presenting symptom · dull ache · stress fractures, hypervascularity, warmth · **cyclic** recurring leg pain

**Deformity** — **long-bone bowing** (femur, **tibia varus**) · kyphosis · short neck (flattened skull base, basilar invagination) · skull enlargement

**Fractures** — brittle bone · **transverse** — **banana / incremental fracture** **(TO)** · subtrochanteric

**Arthritis** — hip + knee OA (83% have a rheumatic syndrome — OB)

**Neuro** — **deafness** (CN VIII compression, otosclerosis, cochlear BMD loss) · impaired vision, facial palsy, trigeminal neuralgia · **spinal stenosis** · **vascular steal** → cerebral ischaemia, spinal claudication **(TO)**

**Cardiac** — **high-output heart failure** — large/multiple lesions + poor cardiac reserve

**Red flag** — **new intense pain + swelling** → **Paget sarcoma**

**Bone scan** — marks disease sites accurately · **intensely hot in lytic + mixed**, less in sclerotic · pagetoid bone hot even without sarcoma

**MRI** — thick trabeculae with **normal marrow signal** · lumbar stenosis

**CT** — cortical thickening, coarse trabeculae

**Sarcoma** — cortical destruction + soft-tissue mass

### Histology

**Mosaic pattern** — woven + lamellar bone, **broad irregular trabeculae**, **disorganized cement lines** · **large osteoclasts, many nuclei (up to 100)**, **virus-like inclusion bodies** · **fibrovascular tissue** between trabeculae · biopsy rarely needed **(TO)**

## 5 Management

```
Paget's disease
├─ Asymptomatic → OBSERVATION + SUPPORTIVE (physio, orthoses, NSAIDs, analgesics)
│      (treat if active disease at sites where complications likely)
├─ Symptomatic → BISPHOSPHONATES (1st line) → CALCITONIN (2nd line)
├─ Hip/knee OA → THA / TKA after medical pre-treatment
└─ Bowing with fracture / impending fracture / failed brace → METAPHYSEAL OSTEOTOMY + PLATE
```

### Medical treatment — bisphosphonates

**Indications** — **bone pain** · **pre-op** (lower ALP to **50% of pretreatment** → less bleeding) · hearing loss, spinal stenosis, HOHF · prevention of fracture/deformity

**Mechanism** — **osteoclast inhibition**\
✗ Wrong options: TNF-α · IL-1R · ER blockade · COX

| Drug | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Alendronate, risedronate** | Oral |
| **Etidronate** | Old, inhibits osteoclasts **and** osteoblasts · **≤6 months** at a time |
| **Pamidronate, zoledronic acid** | IV, osteoclasts only · **single 5 mg IV zoledronic acid** — most effective, normalizes ALP up to **6.5 yr** |
| **Calcitonin** | **2nd line** (bisphosphonate intolerance) · osteoclasts shrink within minutes · SC/IM |
| **Calcium + vitamin D** | Prevent hypocalcaemia on bisphosphonate/calcitonin · not as treatment |
| **Teriparatide** | **Contraindicated** — secondary OS risk |

**Teriparatide contraindications** — Paget · hypercalcaemic disorders · OS · bone mets · pregnancy · skeletal radiation

**NSAIDs** — pain relief, no change in course \
✗ Wrong options: methotrexate · monoclonal antibody · chemo · radiation · wide resection

**Benign biopsy, lytic sacral lesion in old melanoma patient** — Paget → **bisphosphonate**

### Arthroplasty & osteotomy in Paget disease

| Procedure | Commonest complication |
|---|---|
| **THA** | **Bleeding** (rich blood supply) · loosening ~15% |
| **TKA** | **Malalignment** |

**Before arthroplasty** — **pharmacological pre-treatment** to cut bleeding · activity by **ALP + urine hydroxyproline**

**Bowing + persistent pain after 6 mo brace** — **corrective osteotomy + plate fixation**\
✗ Wrong options: IMN without osteotomy · amputation · knee arthrodesis

## 6 Complications

### Paget sarcoma

**Incidence** — **<1%** · **5–10%** in advanced polyostotic disease **(TO)**

**Type** — **osteosarcoma > fibrosarcoma, chondrosarcoma** (spindle-cell sarcomas) · pleomorphic spindle cells, giant cells, atypia

**Patient** — older (**55–80**, 7th decade) · polyostotic · **pelvis, femur, humerus**, proximal skeleton

**Treatment** — **chemotherapy + wide resection + reconstruction** · palliative RT if too infirm\
✗ Wrong options: surgery alone

**Prognosis** — **worst of bone sarcomas** · metastatic 5-yr survival **<10%** (Orthobullets/OB) · **<5%** (OB2004) · non-metastatic **>50%** · secondary OS in >60 yr 7.5% vs primary 37% (Huvos)

**Premalignant for secondary OS (TO)** — FD · Paget · irradiation · multiple osteochondromatosis · bone infarct

**Others** — fractures · OA · HOHF · deafness · stenosis
