# Metastatic Disease of Extremity

*Pathology · 37 / 66*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Commonest cause of a destructive bone lesion in adults** · commonest tumour of bone · aggressive lytic lesion >40 yr: **metastasis > myeloma > lymphoma > Paget sarcoma > post-radiation sarcoma**

**Solitary bone lesion >40 yr** — **500×** more likely metastatic carcinoma than primary bone tumour

**Bone = 3rd commonest metastatic site** (after lung, liver)

**Age** — >50

**Primaries (BLT + Kosher Pickle)** — **Breast** (commonest in women; overall), **Lung**, **Thyroid**, **Kidney**, **Prostate** (commonest in men) · overall **breast > prostate > lung** **(TO)** · men: prostate > lung · women: breast > lung · children: **neuroblastoma**

**Breast** — bone is the first metastatic site in 40–75% **(TO)**

### Sites

**Commonest site of bone metastasis** — **spine (thoracic)** **(TO)** > pelvis > proximal femur > humerus · then ribs, skull · red marrow

**Pathologic fracture** — **proximal femur** commonest (then proximal humerus) · femoral neck 50%, subtrochanteric 30%, peritrochanteric 20% (OB: 50% neck, 30% subtroch, 20% intertroch) · TO: femoral neck commonest, transverse lines

**Acral (distal to elbow/knee)** — rare · **lung** commonest (then kidney) → **arterial tree** spread · **tibia** commonest bone **(TO)** · poor prognosis

**Occult primary** — biopsy shows carcinoma but CT CAP + bone scan negative → **lung**\
✗ Wrong options: colon, kidney, prostate, breast

**Batson vertebral plexus** — **valveless** venous plexus → lung, kidney, breast, prostate, thyroid to **vertebral bodies, pelvis, skull, proximal girdles** · breast → azygos → Batson in **thoracic** spine

**Spine** — vertebral body involved before pedicle, but **pedicle destruction ("winking owl")** is commonest first plain-film sign **(TO)**

### Biology of metastasis (integrins, MMPs, RANKL, endothelin)

| Step | Molecule |
|---|---|
| Release from primary | **↓ E-cadherin** (cell adhesion lost) · **PDGF** → migration |
| Intravasation, avoid immune surveillance | — |
| **Homing to bone** | **CXCL12** (marrow stromal chemokine) |
| **Attachment to endothelium / bone** | **Integrin** (tumour cell CAM) |
| **Extravasation, ECM degradation** | **MMPs** (collagenases, gelatinases, stromelysins) |
| Angiogenesis | **VEGF** |
| Survival | Genomic instability, **↓ apoptosis** · **thrombospondin inhibits** tumour growth |

**Necessary steps** — intravasation, immune evasion, target localization, angiogenesis \
✗ Wrong options: **direct osteoclast stimulation** (indirect, via RANKL) · **increased apoptosis**

**Lytic "vicious cycle"** — tumour **PTHrP** (+ IL-6) → **osteoblasts release RANKL** → RANK on osteoclast precursors → osteolysis → **TGF-β, IGF-1, Ca²⁺** released → more PTHrP · blocked by bisphosphonates, denosumab, OPG

**Blastic** — **endothelin-1** → ET-A receptor on osteoblasts, ↓ **DKK-1** → WNT ↑ · also TGF-β, BMPs **(TO)**

### Lytic vs blastic

| Pattern | Primaries |
|---|---|
| **Lytic** | **Kidney, thyroid, lung** (also GI, melanoma) |
| **Blastic** | **Prostate (90%)**, seminoma, carcinoid, medulloblastoma · breast 60% (OB) |
| **Mixed** | **Breast** — lytic, blastic or mixed ("all of the above") **(TO)** · commoner lytic (TO) |

**Pulsatile metastases** — **follicular thyroid + RCC** (most pulsatile tumours are mets > ABC > GCT) **(TO)**

**Bone-to-bone metastasis** — Ewing > osteosarcoma **(TO)**

**Breast receptor status** — **ER, PR, HER2/neu** guide therapy (tamoxifen/letrozole, trastuzumab)\
✗ Wrong options: RANKL · BRCA · CA-125 · t(11;22)

## 3 Presentation

**Pain** — dull, gradual; **night pain** (tumoral) or **weight-bearing** (mechanical) · weight loss, mass

**Pathologic fracture** — **8–30% at presentation** · **90% need surgery** (rarely heal)

**Atraumatic lesser trochanter avulsion in an adult** — **malignancy until proven otherwise**

**Metastatic hypercalcaemia (emergency)** — confusion, weakness, **polyuria/polydipsia**, N/V, dehydration → **IV fluids**, loop diuretic, **bisphosphonate**

**Complications (Coleman)** — hypercalcaemia 19%, pathologic fracture 19%, cord compression 10%, marrow failure 9%

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Workup of solitary lytic lesion — staging & biopsy before fixation

```
New painful/destructive bone lesion, >40 yr
├─ 1. History + exam (breast, thyroid, prostate) + LABS (SPEP/UPEP, PSA…)
├─ 2. X-ray whole bone + CXR → CT CHEST/ABDOMEN/PELVIS + WHOLE-BODY BONE SCAN (± MRI lesion)
├─ 3. Primary found + multiple lesions → biopsy most accessible lesion if needed
└─ 4. Solitary lesion (even with remote/known cancer) → BIOPSY (treating team, intramuscular tract)
       └─ Carcinoma confirmed → FIX (whole bone) + RT · Sarcoma → wide resection protocol
```

**Never treat a bone lesion without a tissue diagnosis** \
✗ Wrong options: IM nail with **reamings sent as biopsy** (contaminates femur, abductors, skin) · empirical chemo/RT · ORIF

**Remote cancer + solitary lesion** — **biopsy** (could be primary sarcoma) — breast cancer 10–26 yr earlier, lung cancer resected, colon cancer, Paget patient

**Nailed "metastasis" proves high-grade chondrosarcoma** — whole femur contaminated → **wide resection incl. hip disarticulation** (or total femoral resection)

**Open biopsy** — **intramuscular** interval\
✗ Wrong options: intermuscular

**Lab + CT + MRI before biopsy** for atraumatic lesser trochanter fracture\
✗ Wrong options: suture anchor repair · IM nail first

**Pathologic humeral shaft fracture, no cancer history** — **coaptation splint, then bone scan + CT CAP** (then biopsy)\
✗ Wrong options: immediate nail with reamings · ORIF + biopsy first

**Painful TKA with new tibial osteolysis, normal infection labs** — **CT CAP / PET-CT staging**\
✗ Wrong options: revision

**Primary identified** — **~85%** by history + exam + X-ray + CT CAP (OB: CT CAP **90%**, >90% sensitive) · bone scan + CT ~50%

### Key radiology findings

| Modality | Fact |
|---|---|
| **X-ray** | Two planes, joint above + below · lytic / mixed / blastic · **sensitivity ~50%** — needs **30–50% mineral loss**, 1–2 cm · lung → **cortical** mets |
| **CT CAP** | **Find primary in all >40 yr with unknown lytic lesion** · CT of lesion for cortical containment · sens 71–100% |
| **MRI** | **Most sensitive (95%) + specific (90%)** · marrow change before blastic change · T1 low, T2 high · local staging |
| **Bone scan** | Other skeletal lesions · **cold in myeloma + thyroid** → **skeletal survey** · **investigation of choice for occult blastic mets** **(TO)** |
| **PET-CT** | Investigation of choice for **occult lytic** mets **(TO)** · PET alone 30% specificity |
| Indium scan | Infection (✗ Wrong options: tumour) |

### Mirels criteria

| Score | Site | Size (of shaft diameter) | Appearance | Pain |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | Upper limb | <1/3 | Blastic | Mild |
| **2** | Lower limb | 1/3–2/3 | Mixed | Moderate |
| **3** | **Peritrochanteric** | **>2/3** | **Lytic** | **Functional (weight-bearing)** |

**Recommendation (Orthobullets)** — **<8 RT + observe** · **8 judgement** · **>8 prophylactic fixation** · OB explanations vary: "≥8", "≥9", ">9" — read the options

| Mirels | Fracture risk (6 mo) |
|---|---|
| 4–6 | 0% |
| 7 | 4% |
| 8 | 15% |
| **9** | **33%** |
| 10 | 72% |
| 11 | 96% |
| 12 | 100% |

**Sensitivity 80–90%, specificity 30–35%**

**Harrington (prophylactic fixation)** — **>50% diaphyseal cortex** destruction · **>50–75% metaphysis** · **permeative subtrochanteric** destruction · **persistent pain after RT** · pain with weight bearing · (OB: proximal femur lesion >2.5 cm, avulsion of lesser trochanter)

### Spinal metastasis prognostic scores (Tokuhashi, Tomita)

**Tokuhashi** — **prognosis after spinal metastasis** to guide treatment **(TO)** · ✓ includes **general condition** · **≤8 → <6 months** (conservative) · **9–11** individual · **≥12 → >1 yr** (excisional)

| Modified Tokuhashi parameter | Scores |
|---|---|
| General condition (KPS) | Poor 10–40% = 0 · moderate 50–70% = 1 · good 80–100% = 2 |
| Extraspinal bone mets | >2 = 0 · 1–2 = 1 · 0 = 2 |
| Vertebral body mets (original score) | 0–2 |
| Visceral (major organ) mets | Non-removable 0 · removable 1 · none 2 |
| Primary | **Lung, OS, stomach, bladder, oesophagus, pancreas 0** · liver, gallbladder, unidentified 1 · other 2 · **kidney, uterus 3** · rectum 4 · **thyroid, breast, prostate, carcinoid 5** |
| Palsy | Frankel A–B 0 · C–D 1 · E 2 |

**Tomita (2001)** — **3 parameters**: primary growth, visceral mets, bone mets \
✗ Wrong options: **general condition** (that's Tokuhashi) **(TO)**

| Tomita parameter | Score |
|---|---|
| Primary: slow (breast, thyroid) / moderate (kidney, uterus) / rapid (lung, stomach) | 1 / 2 / 4 |
| Visceral mets: none / treatable / untreatable | 0 / 2 / 4 |
| Bone mets: solitary / multiple | 1 / 2 |

**Tomita action** — **2–4 aggressive surgery** · **5–7 palliative surgery** · **8–10 non-operative / supportive**

**Others** — Bauer, Linden, Rades, Douglas, Katagiri scores **(TO)**

## 5 Management

### General principles

**Goals** — pain control, independence, prevent fracture · **implant should outlive the patient**, allow **immediate weight bearing**, **protect the whole bone**, not rely on healing

**Nonoperative** — asymptomatic · non-displaced fractures of humeral shaft, pelvis, scapula · non-weight-bearing bones · **short life expectancy** · systemically sensitive tumours

**Life expectancy for surgery** — lower-limb fixation if ≥6–12 weeks (OB) · arthroplasty if >3 months (OB) · **>6 months = best predictor of fracture healing** (Orthobullets) · RT alone for pain if <6 months (OB)

**Bisphosphonates** — lytic, blastic, mixed · ↓ skeletal-related events + hypercalcaemia · oral clodronate, IV pamidronate/**zoledronic acid** · not for acute impending fracture · complication **ONJ**

**Denosumab** — solid-tumour mets + myeloma · **superior to zoledronic acid** for SRE prevention · SC · ONJ

**Radiation** — palliation + local control · **pain ↓ in 70% at 2 wk**, complete relief ~30% · **RCC + thyroid radioresistant** (✓ breast, prostate, lymphoma, myeloma radiosensitive) · post-RT bone weakens first → fracture risk ~1 yr · radiating a high-risk lesion first ↑ fracture risk

**Other** — RFA (periosteal nerves), **cementoplasty** (pain, not structure), cryoablation, HIFU · radionuclide therapy (TO)

### Postoperative radiation

**All patients** after fixation (unless death imminent or previously irradiated) · start **2–3 weeks post-op** · **field covers entire implant** (e.g. whole femur after nailing)\
✗ Wrong options: tumour site only

**Preoperative RT** — ↑ wound complications

### Femoral lesions — cephalomedullary nailing & radiation

| Location | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Femoral head / neck** | **Arthroplasty (cemented)** · hemiarthroplasty if acetabulum free · **THA if acetabular lesions** / OA (✗ Wrong options: screws · DHS · pinning (high failure)) |
| **Intertrochanteric** | Calcar-replacing hemi/THA **or** cephalomedullary nail |
| **Peri-/subtrochanteric** | **Long, statically locked cephalomedullary nail** ± PMMA · or proximal femoral replacement (✗ Wrong options: **DHS / sliding hip screw** · plate · dynamic locking (shortens) · standard antegrade nail · Enders + cement) |
| **Femoral diaphysis** | **Statically locked cephalomedullary nail** |

**Arthroplasty vs nail/ORIF** — arthroplasty **lower failure** · THA more dislocation than hemi · arthroplasty more infection than nails

**Cemented stems** — immediate stability, **RT inhibits ingrowth** · intra-op metastatic surprise during THA → **convert to cemented stem + cup**

**PMMA** — fills defects, immediate structural support\
✗ Wrong options: autograft (unreliable)

**Post-op rehab** — **full weight bearing** with cane (lung mets, Mirels 12) · no axillary crutches with axillary nodes

### Periarticular lesions — arthroplasty & endoprosthesis

| Site | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Proximal humerus** | Arthroplasty / **endoprosthesis** (ORIF option) · RCC → **proximal humerus replacement** (✗ Wrong options: locking plate · nail · standard reverse) |
| **Humeral diaphysis** | **IM nail** |
| Distal humerus | Flexible nails / elbow replacement |
| **Tibia** | **IM nail** (pathologic fractures progress, don't heal in cast/brace) |

### Solitary renal cell metastasis — embolisation & wide resection

**Preoperative embolization** — **RCC + thyroid** (hypervascular, VEGF) → ↓ blood loss, no effect on healing · palliative pain control

**Solitary RCC metastasis** — **wide (en bloc) resection + endoprosthetic reconstruction** (cemented long stem) → ↓ revision, ↑ survival (median **1.3 → 4.8 yr** with complete resection), ↓ complications, ↓ local recurrence \
✗ Wrong options: IM nail / plate (progression, hardware failure)

**RCC = strongest predictor of local progression after nailing**

**Polymetastatic RCC** — en bloc resection only gives **lower local recurrence**\
✗ Wrong options: survival benefit · cheaper · less blood loss, reoperation equivalent

**RCC features** — lytic, **acral** possible, **radio- and chemo-resistant**, hypervascular, high complication rate with intralesional treatment, survival variable (6 mo – 4–5 yr) · fracture healing **44%** · IL-2 immunotherapy response 18–35%\
✗ Wrong options: sensitive

**Thyroid met (subtrochanteric)** — **curettage + IM nailing** · long survival → stabilise even tibial fracture

## 6 Complications

**Pathologic fracture healing** — **65% nonunion** overall · prostate **42%**, RCC **44%** (comparable across carcinomas)

**Prostate mets** — **blastic (90%)**, **radiosensitive**, **fewer SREs**, **better survival**, comparable fracture healing

**1-yr survival after surgery for metastatic disease** — ~30% (prostate series)

### Prognosis

| Primary | Median survival with bone mets |
|---|---|
| **Thyroid** | **48 months** |
| **Prostate** | **40 months** (Coleman 53) |
| **Breast** | **24 months** (Coleman 20) |
| Kidney | Variable: **6 months – 4–5 years** |
| Myeloma | 2–3 years |
| **Lung** | **6–7 months** (worst) · <5% 5-yr |
