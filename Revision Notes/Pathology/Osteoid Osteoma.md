# Osteoid Osteoma

*Pathology · 49 / 66*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Incidence** — **10–14% of benign** bone tumours · **2–3% of primary** bone tumours · **most common true benign bone tumour (TO)**

**Demographics** — **M:F 2.5:1** (Orthobullets) · **2–4:1** (TO, spine) · **5–25 yr**, **70% before 20** · peak 2nd decade

**Site (Orthobullets)** — lower limb >50%: **proximal femur > tibial diaphysis**, **cortical** · spine 10–15% (thoracolumbar > cervical/sacral, **posterior elements**, **concave side** of scoliosis) · hand 5–10% (**scaphoid, proximal phalanx**) · foot <5% (**talar neck**)

**Site (TO)** — **femur > tibia, cortical, diaphysis** (keyed "femur, cortical diaphysis") · vertebrae **7–20%** · usually cortical, can be intramedullary, subperiosteal, intra-articular

**Spinal OO (TO)** — **lumbar** · **posterior elements 75%** · **pars interarticularis** commonest · CT: lesion at **pedicle–lamina junction**

![CT — nidus at pedicle–lamina junction (TO)](../../MCQ/attachments/TO17747111-1.png)

### Pathophysiology (COX, PGE2)

**Nidus** — central nodule of **woven bone + osteoid with osteoblastic rimming** · **reactive zone** — thickened bone + fibrovascular tissue

**Pain** — ↑ **prostaglandin E2** (+ PGI2) and **COX-1 / COX-2** expression · ↑ number and size of **unmyelinated nerve fibres** in nidus

**NSAIDs** — **cyclooxygenase inhibition** → ↓ PGE2 · ibuprofen/naproxen competitive · aspirin irreversible acetylation\
✗ Wrong options: mu-opioid agonist · 30S · transpeptidase · ACh agonist

**Most important pain mediator** — **PGE2** · first shown by **Makley and Dunn**\
✗ Wrong options: PGI2 (endothelial COX-2, anti-platelet) · IGF-1 · glutamate · ATP

**Associations** — **scoliosis, lesion on concave side** (paravertebral spasm) · **LLD** (epiphyseal → overgrowth) · flexion contractures

## 3 Presentation

**Pain** — constant, progressive, **worse at night** and with **alcohol**, **not activity-related**, **relieved by NSAIDs/aspirin** · near joint → mimics arthritis

**Spine** — **night pain 80–100%** · **painful scoliosis 63–70% (TO)** · 75% (Orthobullets) · backache + paravertebral spasm + scoliosis in adolescent → suspect OO

**Hand** — painless swelling possible

**Exam** — swelling, tenderness, effusion, contracture, limp, muscle atrophy

**Delay** — mean ~**14 months** to diagnosis · often misdiagnosed as **stress fracture** in runners

**Labs** — **ESR, CRP, WBC normal**\
✗ Wrong options: osteomyelitis

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

**X-ray (orthogonal views)** — intense **reactive sclerosis around a radiolucent nidus** · may be normal early · spine: subtle pedicle sclerosis, typical only in half · **obliques** help (pedicle, facet, pars) **(TO)**

**CT (modality of choice)** — **nidus <1.5 cm** (>1.5 cm → osteoblastoma) · **vascular groove sign** in **80%** (1 mm serpentine cortical lucencies) · bull's-eye calcified centre (50%) · sclerosis may exceed nidus · **fine-cut CT** separates OO from **stress fracture**

![CT — radiolucent nidus with sclerosis](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob8012-osteoid-osteoma-1.jpg)

**Bone scan** — **most sensitive for localization (TO)** · **double-density sign** — intense nidus uptake + lower surrounding uptake

![Double-density sign](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob8012-double-density-sign.jpg)

**MRI** — **not recommended** — marrow **oedema masks features** · useful for intra-articular nidus · not needed for diagnosis

**Diagnosis** — clinical history + NSAID response + X-ray/CT → **no biopsy, no staging, no labs needed**

**Femoral neck OO in young athlete (TO)** — marrow oedema + effusion but **no fracture line**, thickened neck cortex → **CT for the nidus** · **intracapsular** location explains the large effusion · can occur in the 20s

### Histology

**Distinct demarcation nidus vs reactive bone** · uniform **immature osteoid trabeculae (woven bone)** with **osteoblastic rimming** · plump, regular osteoblasts · vascular stroma · **no infiltration** of host bone · no neutrophils/dead bone\
✗ Wrong options: Brodie

![Nidus — woven bone with osteoblastic rimming](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob8012-osteoid-osteoma-histology-moved.jpg)

| Stage | Grade | Examples |

### Differential

| Lesion | Distinguishing |
|---|---|
| **Brodie's abscess** | Neutrophils, dead bone · similar cortical X-ray |
| **Stress fracture** | Activity-related · **fine-cut CT** separates |
| **Osteoblastoma** | **>2 cm**, intramedullary, aggressive, dull ache |
| **ABC** | Posterior elements, fluid levels |
| Ewing | Permeative, onion skin |
| Low-grade intramedullary OS | Intramedullary sclerosis, ~1% of OS |
| Enchondroma | Intramedullary, painless, cartilage |
| Osteoma | **Dense juxtacortical** bony mass |
| **Chondroblastoma** | **Epiphyseal** — OO is diaphyseal **(TO)** |

## 5 Management

```
Osteoid osteoma
├─ First line (most extremity; painful spine without scoliosis) → NSAIDs + OBSERVATION
│      (50% burn out over several years)
├─ Failed NSAIDs / GI side effects → RFA (standard of care) or MR-HIFU
└─ Near skin / nerve / cord, digit, recurrence after RFA, fixed spinal deformity → SURGICAL EXCISION
```

### NSAIDs & observation

**First line** — NSAIDs + observation \
✗ Wrong options: RFA/HIFU/resection before trial · MRI

**Incidental OO** — unrelated acute symptoms (e.g. contralateral radiculopathy after injury) → **continued observation**

### Radiofrequency ablation & MR-HIFU

**RFA** — **CT-guided** electrode into nidus · **90°C for 4–6 min** → **1 cm** necrosis zone · **90% success in 1–2 sessions** (95% one procedure — OB) · **10–15% recurrence** · least invasive, fastest resolution, least morbidity

**CT-guided percutaneous drilling** — alternative to RFA (TO) · **"vessel sign"** around nidus on CT (TO)

**RFA distances** — **>1 cm from skin** · **>1 cm from articular surface** · **1–1.5 cm from neurovascular** structures · spine safe with motor response testing

**RFA after failed NSAIDs** — hip, tibia, femur → **RFA**\
✗ Wrong options: biopsy · CT chest · en bloc · curettage + cement

**MR-HIFU** — non-invasive, **non-ionizing** · thermal ablation of periosteal nerves + nidus · comparable to RFA · for failed medical management (e.g. NSAID GI side effects)

### Surgical excision (spine, hand)

**Indications** — not amenable to RFA (**close to skin, nerve, cord/dura**) · **digital lesions** (RFA → thermal skin necrosis, digital NV injury) · **recurrence after RFA** · **spine with fixed deformity**

**Spine near neural elements** — **surgical resection / en bloc excision of nidus**\
✗ Wrong options: RFA · vertebrectomy/corpectomy · fusion

**Digit** — **curettage with power burr** (<10% local recurrence)\
✗ Wrong options: RFA · wide resection

**Principle** — complete marginal removal of **nidus** (sclerotic bone can stay) · **94% success**

**Spinal deformity** — **resolves if excised within 15 months** of onset · no TLSO for mild curve

## 6 Complications

**RFA** — recurrence 10–15% · cellulitis, thrombophlebitis, **skin necrosis** (<1 cm from skin) · NV damage (<1–1.5 cm)

**LLD** — epiphyseal lesion → limb overgrowth

**Open curettage/en bloc near hip** — late joint morbidity

**Prognosis** — pain resolves ~**3 yr** (2–4 yr, hand) · lesion resolves **5–7 yr** · malignant transformation extremely rare

## X vs Y

| | **Osteoid osteoma** | **Osteoblastoma** |
|---|---|---|
| Incidence | 10% of benign | 3% of benign |
| Size | **<2 cm (typically <1.5)** | **>2 cm** (3–3.5) |
| Site | >50% long-bone diaphysis · proximal femur > tibia > phalanges > spine | >35% posterior spine |
| Natural history | **Self-limited** | **Progressive** |
| Symptoms | **Night pain, relieved by NSAIDs** · painful scoliosis (concave side) | Dull ache, partial NSAID response, neuro symptoms |
| Spine treatment | Nonoperative first → RFA/resection | **Surgery always** |
