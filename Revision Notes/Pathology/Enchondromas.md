# Enchondromas

*Pathology · 15 / 66*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Rank** — **2nd commonest benign cartilage lesion** (osteochondroma 1st)

**Demographics** — **M = F** · **20–50 yr** (3rd–4th decade)

**Site** — **medullary, metaphysis or diaphysis** · **hand 60%** > feet · distal femur 20% > proximal humerus 10% > tibia · metaphyseal when first seen → appears diaphyseal as bone grows

**Hand** — **commonest primary bone tumour of the hand** (42% in small tubular bones) \
✗ Wrong options: periosteal chondroma · subungual exostosis · osteoid osteoma

### "Most common" list (TO)

| Category | Answer |
|---|---|
| **Tumour of the hand (overall)** | **Squamous cell carcinoma** **(TO keyed answer)** |
| Bone tumour of hand bones | **Enchondroma** |
| Malignant bone tumour of hand bones | **Chondrosarcoma** |
| Tumour of bone | **Metastasis** |

### Pathophysiology

**Origin** — **incomplete endochondral ossification** · physeal chondroblasts **escape into the metaphysis** and proliferate\
✗ Wrong options: intramembranous

### Genes & lab markers

**IDH1 / IDH2** — enchondroma, Ollier (and chondrosarcoma)

**Ollier / Maffucci** — **sporadic, no inheritance pattern** **(TO)** · (✗ Wrong options: GNAS (McCune-Albright) · p53 · chromosome 6q13 (CMF) · PGE2/COX (osteoid osteoma))\
✗ Wrong options: AD (that is MHE/EXT)

## 3 Presentation

**Asymptomatic** — **incidental** on films (long bones, foot)

**Pathologic fracture** — typical in the **hand** after minor trauma

**Pain** — uncommon · usually **another cause** (cuff tendinopathy, cervical radiculopathy, ankle sprain, vascular claudication) · **rest/night pain** → think chondrosarcoma

**Syndromes** — shortening, bowing, angular deformity · bluish angiomas (Maffucci)

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

**X-ray (usually diagnostic)** — well-defined, **lucent, central medullary** lesion, **1–10 cm**, calcifies over time · **"popcorn" stippling, rings, arcs, whorls** · endosteal erosion **<50% (<2/3) of cortex** · hands/feet: expansion + thinning, may be purely lytic

![Enchondroma of the distal phalanx](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob8018-case-e-distal-phalanx-xray-parsons-moved.png)

**MRI** — not needed for diagnosis · **lobular, T2 bright** (water), **no marrow oedema, no periosteal reaction** · "sled-runner tracks" · **medullary fill >90% → chondrosarcoma**

**Bone scan** — ↑ uptake (remodelling) but **< ASIS** (only 20% > ASIS) · identifies **polyostotic** disease

**CT** — optional, for endosteal scalloping

**Skeletal survey** — if polyostotic suspected (Ollier dysplasia, Maffucci phleboliths)

### Histology

**Gross** — blue-grey lobulated hyaline cartilage, scattered calcification

**Long bones** — **hypocellular, bland mature hyaline cartilage ("blue balls") separated by normal marrow** · encased by lamellar bone · abundant matrix, **no myxoid** component · **small bland chondrocytes in lacunae**, no pleomorphism/mitoses/binucleation

**Hand / small tubular bones / fibula** — **hypercellular, mild atypia** → not malignant

**Biopsy** — rarely helpful · sampling error, can't separate from low-grade CS (SLICE study: poor reliability)

### Enchondroma vs chondrosarcoma

| | **Enchondroma** | **Chondrosarcoma** |
|---|---|---|
| Age | 20–50 | >50 |
| Size | <5 cm | **>5 cm** |
| Pain | None / other cause | **Rest + night pain** |
| Endosteal scalloping | **<50% (<2/3) cortex** | **>2/3 cortex** |
| Cortex | Intact | Thickened, destroyed, periosteal reaction |
| Soft-tissue mass | No | Yes |
| MRI | No oedema | Oedema, >90% medullary fill |
| Bone scan | < ASIS | > ASIS |
| Histology | Hypocellular, lacunar cells, normal marrow between | Hypercellular, plump binucleate nuclei, permeation |

**Bone infarct** — **smoke up the chimney**, **low T2** (vs bright T2 enchondroma)

### Enneking staging

**Enchondroma = Enneking stage 1 (latent)** (with NOF) \
✗ Wrong options: ABC, CMF, chondroblastoma = 2 · GCT = 3

### Cartilage-origin tumours (TO)

**WHO** — osteochondroma, enchondroma, **chondroblastoma, CMF** all cartilage tumours → "all are cartilage tumours EXCEPT" → **none** **(TO)** · WHO 5th ed. moved chondroblastoma + CMF to **benign** (no intermediate cartilage tumour now)

## 5 Management

### Latent lesion — observation vs low-grade chondrosarcoma

```
Enchondroma
├─ Asymptomatic / pain from another cause → OBSERVE, X-rays at 6 + 12 mo (3–6 mo)
├─ Growth / change on serial X-ray, true lesional pain → EXTENDED INTRALESIONAL CURETTAGE + GRAFT
├─ Hand pathologic fracture → CURETTAGE + GRAFT (immediate = delayed)
└─ Aggressive features (scalloping >2/3, mass) → REFER ORTHO ONCOLOGY, treat as chondrosarcoma
```

**Observation** — vast majority · serial radiographs · long-term follow-up for syndromes · no biopsy, MRI, CT, bone scan or CT chest/abdomen (even with prostate cancer history if benign-looking)

**Curettage + graft (or cement) indications** — **change on serial X-rays** · **symptomatic** · **pathologic fracture** · suspicious for low-grade CS · large lesion at risk of re-fracture · local recurrence unusual

**Not indicated** — chemo, RT (radiation-induced sarcoma), RFA, wide resection + arthroplasty (over-treatment), amputation

### Pain from another source

**Treat the real cause** — shoulder pain with overhead activity → **cuff** (PT + observe lesion) · deltoid paraesthesia + elbow flexion weakness → **cervical spine X-rays (C5)** · ankle sprain instability → **functional rehab, proprioception** · walking pain relieved by standing still, ABI 0.8 → **vascular claudication**

### Hand enchondroma & pathologic fracture

**Painful/growing thumb lesion** — **curettage + bone grafting**\
✗ Wrong options: amputation · chemo · RT

**Pathologic fracture** — **curettage + grafting** · **immediate = delayed** (immobilise then curette) for consolidation, DASH, recurrence, complications — only difference is **longer recovery** with delayed · void fillers: autograft, calcium phosphate/sulfate, PMMA, coralline HA

**Hand chondrosarcoma** — wide resection / **ray resection**

## 6 Complications

### Ollier disease & Maffucci syndrome

| | **Ollier** | **Maffucci** |
|---|---|---|
| Lesions | **Multiple enchondromas** (metaphyses + diaphyses, hands) | Multiple enchondromas + **soft-tissue haemangiomas/angiomas** |
| Inheritance | **Sporadic, none** | Sporadic, none |
| Bones | **Dysplastic, short, bowed**, LLD | Marked expansion · **calcified phleboliths** |
| Chondrosarcoma risk | **25–30%** (Orthobullets) · 10–30% · "<30%" · ~25% (OB) | **25–30%** (Orthobullets) · **>50%** (OB) |
| Other malignancy | — | **Visceral + CNS** (astrocytoma, GI adenocarcinoma) → any malignancy **up to 100%** |

**Solitary enchondroma** — malignant transformation **<1%** (~1%)\
✗ Wrong options: 5–8%

**Secondary chondrosarcoma** from enchondroma — usually **low grade** **(TO)**

## X vs Y

| | **Enchondroma** | **Osteochondroma** | **Periosteal chondroma** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Location | **Medullary** | **Surface**, exophytic, cortical/medullary continuity | Surface, shallow cortical defect |
| Origin | Escaped physeal cartilage | Zone of Ranvier (EXT1/2 in MHE) | Periosteum |
| Treatment | Observe | Observe / marginal excision | Marginal excision |

| | **Enchondroma** | **Bone infarct** |
|---|---|---|
| X-ray | Rings and arcs throughout | Serpiginous sclerotic rim |
| T2 MRI | **Bright** | Low / central fat |

✗ Wrong options: biopsy · MRI · CT CAP + referral

✗ Wrong options: biopsy · curettage
