# THA Prosthesis Design

*Recon · 29 / 65*

## 1 Basics

### Determinants

**THA biomechanics depend on** — prosthesis design · **bearing surface + lubrication** · fixation

**Components** — femoral (cemented, press-fit: tapered, extensively coated, modular) · acetabular (cemented PE or metal; press-fit metal; **modular dual mobility** — ↓ revision dislocation, risk of **liner malseating**) · bearings (PE, metal, ceramic)

**Arthroplasty types (TO)** — **excision** · **interposition** (fascia lata) · **replacement** · hemiarthroplasty = femoral head only · THR = acetabular + femoral

**Charnley principles (TO)** — **small 22 mm head** (↓ friction torque) · **thick PE socket** · **medialize cup + lateralize GT** (↑ abductor arm → ↓ JRF) · **PMMA** both components · failures: **dislocation** (small head), **acetabular perforation** (over-medialization), **trochanteric osteotomy nonunion** · modern principle: **recreate native anatomy** (offset, version, inclination, length), no trochanteric transfer

**Charnley head** — ✓ **22.225 mm (7/8 inch)** (TO)

**Charnley all-PE cemented cup** — ✓ **20–30% failure at 35 yr** (88% intact at 20 yr, 78% at 35 yr; 77.3% at 21 yr)

## 5 Management

### Press-fit femoral stems

**Principle** — **biologic fixation** · **hoop stresses** give initial stability

| Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Tapered** (proximally coated) | Tri-Lock, M/L Taper |
| **Extensively coated** (diaphyseal) | **AML**, VerSys Full Coat |
| **Modular** (mix-and-match body + stem) | **S-ROM**, ZMR |

**Cementless stem classification (TO, Khanduja)** — **1** single wedge (proximal, flat AP/tapered ML, broach only) · **2** double wedge (proximal, ML + AP) · **3** double-wedged, fixation at **metaphyseal–diaphyseal junction** (3a/b/c) · **4** **extensively coated**, full-length fixation · **5** **modular** · **6** **anatomic** (posterior metaphyseal bow) · ✓ **not distally fixed = type 2** (types 3b, 3c, 4 are)

**Khanduja — TO lecture version** — 1 single wedge (Dorr B/C) · 2 dual wedge (**Dorr A**) · 3 round / rectangular (revision, diaphyseal) · 4 fully coated (revision, dysplasia) · 5 highly modular (**S-ROM**, DDH) · 6 anatomic · type 3 described as metaphyseal–diaphyseal double wedge above — read the options

**Complications** — **intraop fracture** (lateral approach + under-reaming) · **loosening** in **irradiated bone** · **junctional (taper) corrosion** in modular necks → metallosis, pseudotumour

### Cemented femoral stems

**Indications** — **irradiated bone** · **Dorr C** (poor proximal stock) · elderly · grout; limited remodelling

**Material** — **Co-Cr or stainless steel** (stiff → ↓ cement stress) · not **cemented titanium** (less stiff → micromotion, debonding, **crevice corrosion** — no longer recommended)

**Stem breakage** — smaller stems, poor tolerance of **cantilever bending**

**Desired features (TO)** — **high-modulus superalloy (Co-Cr)** · broad rounded edges · collar (depth control, ↓ calcar resorption) · **macrotexturing** (grooves) · **polished surface** (less debris if debonded; Exeter matte > polished loosening) · **no PMMA precoat** · **centralizer** · ✓ **NOT microtexturing / matte finish** (that is for uncemented ingrowth)

**Gruen failure modes** — **I pistoning** (linear lucency around stem + cement, commonest) · II medial midstem pivot · III calcar pivot · IV bending cantilever

**Force-closed vs shape-closed** — taper-slip polished collarless (**Exeter, C-stem**; subsidence intended, >5 mm or progressive after 2 yr = loose) vs composite beam rough ± collar (**Charnley**; subsidence >0.15 mm after 2 yr → ↑ revision) · mantle **2–4 mm** · line-to-line = "French paradox"

### Elderly Dorr C femur

**82-yr-old walker, Dorr C** → ✓ **cemented femoral stem**\
✗ Wrong options: cemented cup (shear loosening) · diaphyseal stem (save for revision) · 4.5 g antibiotic for prophylaxis (≤1 g; 4.5 g for spacers) · smallest stem for >1 cm mantle

### Dorr classification (TO)

**Classifies** — ✓ **proximal femur**\
✗ Wrong options: femoral stem · acetabulum

| Type | Ratio | Shape | Stem |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **<0.5** | **"Champagne flute"**, thick cortices, young men | Uncemented (either works) |
| **B** | **0.5–0.75** | Medial + posterior cortical loss, wider canal | Uncemented |
| **C** | **>0.75** | **"Stovepipe"**, thin cortices, elderly postmenopausal women | ✓ **Cemented** (most suited) |

### Bearing surfaces

| Bearing | Benefits | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|
| **Metal-on-PE** | **Longest record**, **cheapest**, most modular | **↑ wear + osteolysis** · small head → impingement |
| **Metal-on-metal** | ↓ linear wear · particles **smaller + more numerous** (smaller total volume) · large head → ↑ ROM | Cost · **ions 5–10× normal** (serum + urine; peak **12–24 months** run-in) · **pseudotumour** · **type IV hypersensitivity** · no proven cancer link |
| **Ceramic-on-ceramic** | ✓ **Best wear** (alumina 4000× < CoCr-on-PE) · **lowest friction** · **inert** | Cost · **brittle — fracture** · **squeaking** · **stripe wear** · fewer neck lengths · 28 mm heads only zirconia |
| **Ceramic-on-PE** | **Standard of care** · ↓ PE wear vs MoP | **Zirconia phase transformation** (tetragonal → monoclinic: >8 yr, pressure, heat — 99 °C vs alumina 50 °C) |
| **Titanium-on-PE** | — | **High wear** — not recommended |

**Gold-standard bearing** — **ceramic-on-PE = standard of care** (current chapter) · **metal-on-cross-linked PE = gold standard** (TO lecture) — read the options · TO: CoC least wear, brittle, squeaking, **malposition-intolerant** · MoM obsolete (ALVAL, ions)

**Modular options (TO)** — neck length **25–50 mm** · neck-shaft **127–132°** · head **28–32 mm ideal** (jump distance, low dislocation) · **> 36 mm → thinner PE → early wear** (TO) · PE thickness **≥ 5 mm** (TO, this lecture) · **≥ 6 mm** (TO osteolysis lecture) — read the options

**Morse taper (TO)** — **12/14 taper = lowest failure** · trunnionosis = **3–5% of THR revisions**

**Cups (TO)** — **cementless press-fit cup = gold standard** · avoid screws if possible (↑ osteolysis via effective joint space) · cemented cup rarely used · **hybrid hip (cemented stem + uncemented cup) = commonest combination** (TO)

**Stiffness order (TO)** — **PE (lowest) < bone < titanium < Co-Cr < ceramic (highest)** · **Ti for cementless**, **Co-Cr for cemented** · distal tapering ↓ stiffness · proximal wedging → **hoop stresses** → stability

**Fixation choice (TO)** — PMMA = **grout, not adhesive** · cemented → elderly, short life expectancy, poor bone · cementless → young, good bone · ingrowth (porous, tantalum) vs **ongrowth** (grit-blast, plasma spray)

**Liners (TO)** — **dual mobility** = two articulations, ideal for **elderly / weak abductors** · **constrained liner outdated** (high mechanical failure)

**MoM contraindications** — **pregnancy / child-bearing women** (ions cross placenta) · **renal disease** (renal excretion) · metal hypersensitivity

**Wear order (TO)** — **CoC (0.5–2.5 µm/yr) < MoM (2.5–5 µm/yr) < MoP** · ✓ **CoC lowest wear of any bearing**

### Ceramic properties

**Ceramic** — ✓ **non-ductile, high wettability, highest modulus, low fracture toughness** · inert, biocompatible · alumina + zirconia composites

**Modulus order** — ceramic > Co-Cr > stainless steel > titanium (OB) · TO: polymers < cement < cortical bone < **Ti** < SS < Co-Cr < ceramics

**MoM advantage over CoC** — ✓ **↑ fracture toughness**\
✗ Wrong options: wettability, hardness, roughness, friction

**CoC vs metal on HXLPE** — ✓ **lower volumetric wear**\
✗ Wrong options: jump distance/ROM (head size) · cost · stripe wear (unique to CoC)

**CoC complications (TO)** — **stripe wear** (head–rim line contact during subluxation, crescent line), **squeaking** (0.3–24.6%; onset 14–40 months; fundamental 400–7,500 Hz; friction from lost lubrication), **fracture**, impingement · ✓ **NOT high wear rate**

**Ceramic fracture risk** — **28 mm** > 32/36 mm heads · **short neck** · canted liner · malposition

**Ceramic generations** — 1st pure zirconia (roughening, fracture) · 2nd CaO/MgO (↓ grain) · 3rd **hot isostatic pressing** (ultrapure alumina, zirconia, yttrium) · 4th **strontium oxide platelets** deflect cracks

**After ceramic head fracture** — revise with **ceramic** head, not metal · metal head → retained ceramic fragments cause ✓ **massive third-body (abrasive) wear**\
✗ Wrong options: infection · pseudotumour · metallosis · psoas

### Head size & wear

**Large head** — ✓ **↑ ROM** before impingement · ↑ head-neck ratio · **↑ jump distance (~½ head diameter)** · more stable · **↓ ceramic fracture** · **↓ neck-to-rim impingement** · ✓ **↑ volumetric wear**\
✗ Wrong options: "decreases ROM" · "↑ neck-rim impingement" (TO EXCEPT answers)

**Volumetric wear** — **V = πr²w** · ✓ **head size (radius²) most important** · ✓ relates to **total contact area** (TO) · main determinant of particle number

**22 mm vs 32 mm** — 22 mm: ✓ **higher linear wear (creep)**, ↓ volumetric wear, ↑ dislocation, ↓ ROM · 32 mm: ↑ volumetric wear, ↓ linear · **28 mm compromise**

**Linear wear** — penetration of head into cup · **>0.1 mm/yr → osteolysis** · conventional UHMWPE **0.1–0.2 mm/yr** · **RSA most accurate** wear measure

**Wear modes (TO)** — **adhesive** · **abrasive** ("cheese grater"; two- and three-body) · **third-body** (cement, bone, metal, PE, HA) · **surface fatigue/delamination** (TKA) · **fretting** (THA backside shell–liner) · corrosive · hip PE wear mainly **adhesive/abrasive**; TKA **delamination**

**Wear stages** — run-in → steady state → rapid failure

### MoM tribology

**Lubrication (TO)** — ✓ **elastohydrodynamic** in MoM · **boundary** in MoP · mixed lubrication (OB)

**Diametral clearance** — most important wear variable · ideal **100–200 µm** · too small → equatorial contact, seizing · too large → lost fluid film, rapid wear · ✓ TO false statement: "large clearance = better fluid film" · OB: ideal **radial mismatch ~50 µm**

**High carbon (>0.20%)** Co-Cr → ↓ wear · **double heat treatment** + **low carbon** → ↑ wear, pseudotumour

**Larger MoM bearings wear similarly** — ✓ **greater sliding speed draws in more fluid**\
✗ Wrong options: boundary lubrication · flexibility · carbides · smaller clearance

**Not affecting MoM wear** — ✓ **head-to-neck ratio**\
✗ Wrong options: radial mismatch, sphericity, surface finish, carbon content

**ALVAL** — delayed-type hypersensitivity, ~**0.3%** of MoM

### MoM systemic effects

**True** — ionically charged particles · ↑ **Co + Cr in serum + urine** · **higher frictional torque than CoC** · **no activity–ion correlation** · ions **renally excreted** (not liver) · cross **placenta** · ✓ **pseudotumours** · ↑ registry revision rates

**Not true / not shown** — ✓ **higher cancer risk** (EXCEPT answer) · sarcoma, carcinomas · ↑ dislocation · nephrotoxicity

**Observed more often** — ✓ **leukocyte chromosomal aberrations** (translocations, aneuploidy) — significance unknown · haematopoietic malignancy data mixed

**Hypersensitivity (type IV)** — T-cell mediated · **nickel > cobalt, chromium** · most cells are macrophages (lymphocytes **5%**) · cytokines → macrophage activation → MHC II, IL-2 → more T-cells

### Taper corrosion & modularity

**Taper (trunnion / neck–stem) corrosion in MoP** — ✓ **pseudotumour** (ALTR) · fretting + crevice corrosion (mechanically assisted) · affects **>⅓ of modular devices**\
✗ Wrong options: PE wear · dislocation · squeaking · sepsis

**Modular-neck ALTR findings** — X-ray usually normal; **medial calcar erosion** commonest · MRI **thickened capsule + fluid** · **↑ cobalt with near-normal chromium** (Cr precipitates as orthophosphate) · black bore of female taper

**Treatment** — ✓ **single-stage revision of liner, head, neck + stem to a MONOBLOCK stem**\
✗ Wrong options: revise stable cup · two-stage · retain stem if female taper corroded · modular revision stem

**Impingement ↓ ROM** — ✓ **skirted modular heads** · avoid skirts, maximize head-neck ratio, chamfered liners, **trapezoidal** (not circular) neck · cup 45–55° with 10–20° combined anteversion (wear/instability if >50°)

**Malseated modular dual-mobility liner** (Co-Cr inelastic, hard bone deforms shell; "4-quadrant test") → ✓ **↑ fretting corrosion**\
✗ Wrong options: dislocation · PJI · fracture · ↓ ROM

### Implant choice in nickel allergy

**Nickel** — in **Co-Cr and stainless steel**, not titanium or ceramic

**Answer** — ✓ **press-fit titanium stem + alumina ceramic head + cementless titanium cup** · TKA: **oxidized zirconium femur + titanium tibia**\
✗ Wrong options: cemented Ti (less stiff) · Co-Cr · titanium head (abrasive wear)

### Stem design, stress shielding & fracture risk

**Stress shielding** — proximal bone loss around a well-fixed stiff stem (**Hooke's law** — stiffer "spring" carries load) · **Wolff's law** bone loss

| ↑ Stress shielding | ↓ Stress shielding |
|---|---|
| **Large diameter** (stiffness ∝ **r⁴**; ≥16 mm; OB >18 mm) ← most important | Small diameter |
| **Co-Cr** (10× femur; alumina 19×) | ✓ **Titanium** (lower modulus) |
| **Solid, round** | Hollow, slotted, fluted, tapered, short |
| ✓ **Cylindrical distal-fitting / extensively coated / distally fixed** | **Tapered, proximally fixed** |
| **Cementless** | ✓ **Cemented** stems (TO true statement) |
| Osteoporosis | — |

**Fully coated Co-Cr stem → X-ray** — ✓ **proximal femur / trochanteric osteopenia** · late GT insufficiency fracture\
✗ Wrong options: distal · whole femur · acetabulum

**Grade III stress shielding with 16.5 mm stem — EXCEPT** → ✓ **titanium stem**\
✗ Wrong options: round · large diameter · solid

**Material to ↓ shielding** → ✓ **titanium** (highest fatigue strength among alloys) · flexible, short, hollow stems

**Osteolysis location** — extensively coated → **proximal** · patch-coated → **distal** (joint fluid reaches diaphysis) · HA fully coated → ↑ cortical shielding vs proximally porous

**FNB 2024** — stem diameter threshold for proximal resorption (11 / 12.5 / 13.5 / 14 mm) and "proximal stress shielding occurs in…" — answers not given by source (diaphyseal-fitting > tapered fits the rule above)

**Titanium vs Co-Cr (TO)** — Ti: ✓ **lower tensile strength + fatigue resistance (than Co-Cr)** · **lower modulus**, less stiff · **more biocompatible**, better osseointegration · Ti porous coat restricted to bulky proximal stem (notch sensitivity)\
✗ Wrong options: higher modulus · stiffer · less biocompatible

**Intraop femur fracture — highest risk** — ✓ **final insertion of uncemented stem via lateral approach in female >65** (medius directs broach medial/anterior → calcar stress)

### Impaction bone grafting & conversion after failed fixation (TO)

**Conversion after failed neck fixation (screw holes)** → ✓ **long uncemented stem + prophylactic cerclage**, bypass last hole by **2 cortical diameters**\
✗ Wrong options: cemented (leaks through holes)

**Impaction grafting (femur)** — Hastings & Parker 1975, Nijmegen/Exeter · **1–2 mm cancellous allograft chips** impacted → **cemented** stem in neo-canal · loads transmitted as **compression on graft** · **some subsidence expected** · indication: **Paprosky 3B in young**, type 4 · complications: dislocation **4.1%**, fracture **5.4%** · ✓ **incorrect: "stem must be tapered fluted, collarless, flexible titanium"** (no single required stem; polished collarless or collared used)
