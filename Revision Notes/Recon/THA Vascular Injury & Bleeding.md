# THA Vascular Injury & Bleeding

*Recon · 37 / 65*

## 1 Basics

**Incidence** — vascular injury **0.1–0.2%** · rare but devastating

**Risk factors** — **acetabular screws** · prior vascular bypass / dysvascular limb · **revision** · **intrapelvic cup** and femoral cerclage wiring

**Mechanisms** — screw puncture/laceration · **errant retractor** · compression by **anti-protrusio cages** · direct laceration during approach

### Vessels at risk

| Vessel | Location / threat |
|---|---|
| **Obturator a. + v.** | Distal to **transverse acetabular ligament (TAL)** · along quadrilateral surface, exits at superolateral obturator foramen · **inferior retractor under TAL** · **anterior-inferior screw** |
| **External iliac a. + v.** | **Anterior-superior quadrant** ("zone of death") · ~**7 mm** from bone at ASIS (may lie directly on bone) |
| **Common femoral a. + v. (+ nerve)** | Superficial to iliopsoas · only **anterior capsule + iliopsoas** separate from hip · **anterior rim retractor** |
| **Medial femoral circumflex a.** | Under **quadratus femoris** / gluteus maximus tendon · posterior approach |
| **Ascending branch of LFCA** | Between **TFL and sartorius** · **direct anterior approach** |
| **Superior gluteal a.** | Posterior-superior quadrant (safe zone, still possible) |
| **Inferior gluteal + internal pudendal** | Posterior-inferior quadrant |

### Acetabular quadrants

| Quadrant | At risk | Zone |
|---|---|---|
| **Posterior-superior** | Superior gluteal vessels, sciatic n. | **Safe** (best bone) |
| **Posterior-inferior** | Inferior gluteal + internal pudendal vessels, sciatic n. | Safe-ish |
| **Anterior-inferior** | **Obturator** vessels + nerve | Danger |
| **Anterior-superior** | **External iliac** vessels | **Danger ("death")** |

### Obturator artery injury

**Retractor deep to TAL → sudden massive bleed** → ✓ **obturator artery** (TO)\
✗ Wrong options: external iliac a./v. · MFCA

**Greatest risk to obturator a.** → ✓ **inferior retractor under TAL** (into obturator foramen)\
✗ Wrong options: posterior wall retractor (sciatic n.) · posterior-superior screw (sciatic n. + superior gluteal a., safe zone) · anterior-superior screw (external iliac) · anterior wall retractor (external iliac/femoral)

**Uncontrollable obturator bleed** — **ligate internal iliac** artery reported

### Sources of hematoma (TO)

**Most common source** — ✓ **branches of obturator vessels** (near ligamentum teres, TAL, inferior acetabular osteophytes) (TO)

**Others** — **first perforating branch of profunda femoris** (deep to gluteus maximus insertion) · femoral vessel branches near anterior capsule · superior + inferior gluteal branches (TO)

### Anterior retractor – femoral neurovascular bundle (TO)

**Hohmann/cobra on anterior acetabular rim** — between rim and **psoas tendon** · over psoas → ✓ **femoral artery + nerve** at risk (TO) · risk ↑ the **more inferior** the retractor · safest near **ASIS level** (TO)\
✗ Wrong options: psoas tendon · external iliac · obturator

### Nerve injury link (TO)

**Sciatic** — **80%** of THA nerve injuries (femoral **20%**) · **compression** commonest mechanism · closest to acetabulum at **ischium** · **peroneal division** most affected · errant retractor commonest intra-op cause (TO)

**Nerve injury risk factors (TO)** — **female** · post-traumatic arthritis · **revision** · **DDH** · **lengthening >3 cm**

## 3 Presentation

**Signs** — hemodynamic instability · **pulsatile intra-op bleeding** · **abdominal distension** · loss of limb pulses (palpation/Doppler)

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

**Radiograph** — **extra-osseous acetabular screw**

**Angiography** — stable patient with high suspicion of uncontrolled bleed · delineates vessel · allows **immediate embolization**

## 5 Management

```
Vascular injury during THA
├─ Hemodynamically stable → angiography + endovascular embolization
└─ Unstable + pulsatile bleeding → emergent retroperitoneal exploration
       └─ Prompt hemostasis + vascular surgery consult
```

**Outcome with treatment** — mortality **9%** · amputation **12%** · permanent ischaemic disability **17%**

### Transfusion predictors

**Best predictor of transfusion** — ✓ **low pre-op hemoglobin**\
✗ Wrong options: shorter height · lower weight · previous contralateral THA · female gender

**Other risks** — **RA** · advanced age · longer operative time · no clear link with BMI, gender, prophylactic anticoagulation

**Anemia symptoms** — syncope, dyspnea, chest pain · ↓ SpO₂, tachycardia, tachypnea, delayed capillary refill, pallor · check CBC, coagulation, iron

**Prevention** — pre-op **erythropoietin** · topical/IV **tranexamic acid** · hypotensive regional anaesthesia

### Transfusion criteria

| Patient | Transfuse if |
|---|---|
| **No ischaemic cardiovascular disease** | **Hb <7** + symptomatic anemia |
| **Ischaemic CVD (MI, CHF)** | **Hb <8** + symptomatic anemia |

**Transfusion complications** — ✓ **↑ PJI** · transfusion reactions

## X vs Y

| Retractor / screw | Structure at risk |
|---|---|
| **Inferior retractor under TAL** | **Obturator a.** |
| **Anterior rim retractor over psoas** | **Femoral a. + n.** |
| **Posterior wall retractor** | **Sciatic n.** |
| **Anterior-superior screw** | **External iliac a./v.** |
| **Anterior-inferior screw** | **Obturator** a./v./n. |
| **Posterior-superior screw** | Sciatic n., superior gluteal a. (safe zone) |

## How it's asked

**"Best predictor of post-op transfusion?"** → **lower preoperative hemoglobin**

**"Massive bleed after retractor deep to TAL?"** → **obturator artery** (TO)

**"Most common source of hematoma in THR?"** → **branches of obturator vessels** (TO)

**"Manoeuvre placing obturator artery at greatest risk?"** → **inferior retractor under the TAL**

**"Hohmann on anterior acetabular rim — structure at risk?"** → **femoral artery and nerve** (TO)
