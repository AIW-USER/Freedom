# TKA Prosthesis Design

*Recon · 57 / 65*

## 1 Basics

### History

| Era | Milestone |
|---|---|
| **19th c** | Soft-tissue interposition |
| **1950s** | **Walldius** — first **hinged** knee |
| **1958** | **MacIntosh & McKeever** — acrylic tibial plateau |
| **1960s** | **Gunston** — first cemented surface arthroplasty |
| **1970** | **GUEPAR** hinge (↑ motion, ↓ bone loss) · first-gen hinges → **high loosening from over-constraint** |
| **~1973** | **Total condylar** — first to resurface all 3 compartments · PCL sacrificing, no cam-post → flexion ~**90°** |
| Later | **Duo-patellar (kinematic) = CR** · **Insall–Burstein PS** · LCS rotating platform · medial pivot · ultracongruent (TO) |

### Concepts in prosthetic design

**Femoral rollback** — ✓ **posterior translation of the femur with flexion** → ↑ quad lever arm + flexion by **preventing posterior impingement** in deep flexion (TO) · native: controlled by **ACL + PCL** · CR: native PCL · PS: **tibial post contacts femoral cam**

**Constraint** — V/V + F/E stability despite **ligament laxity or bone loss** · more constraint → more force to fixation → **aseptic loosening**

| Least → most constrained |
|---|
| **Bicruciate-retaining** (keeps ACL; ↑ op time + complications) → **CR** → **PS** → **VVC / CCK (non-hinged)** → **rotating hinge** |

### Constraint classification (TO)

| Class (TO) | Ligament status | Designs |
|---|---|---|
| **Unconstrained** | Collaterals **+ PCL intact** | **CR** |
| **Semi-constrained** | Collaterals intact, **PCL removed** | CS, **PS**, **UC** |
| **Constrained** | **Collaterals deficient** | CCK / hinge |

**Ultra-congruent (deep dish)** — substitutes PCL **without cam–post** (TO) · rollback produced by PCL / cam–post / deep dish (TO)

**Design limitations (TO)** — total condylar (Insall + Ranawat): cupped femur, flat tibia, **flexion < 90°, no rollback** · duo-patellar: **dependent on intact PCL** · Insall–Burstein PS: needs **femoral notch (box) cut** · LCS rotating platform: complex technique · medial pivot: closest to native kinematics

**Modularity** — metal tray + modular insert, augments, stems · customise intra-op · but **↑ osteolysis** + **backside wear** (micromotion insert–tray) · more expensive

## 2 Mechanism

### Kinematics (medial pivot, paradoxical motion)

**Native knee** — ✓ **femur pivots about a medial axis** in flexion (lateral condyle rolls back more: larger radius / convex lateral plateau) · flexion → **femoral ER (tibial IR)** + rollback · terminal extension → **femur IR, lateral condyle translates anteriorly** (screw-home = tibial ER last 15°) · lateral condyle posterior **12.7 mm** vs medial **2.9 mm** (TO)

**Paradoxical translation** — ✓ **anterior translation of tibiofemoral contact point from extension → flexion** · commonest in **CR** (no ACL, posterior resting point; PCL not tight until ~**45°**) · also PS before cam engages · CR shows it in first **~40°**

**Medial-pivot / medial-stabilised / medial congruent (MC)** — ✓ **ball-and-socket medial** (**spherical single-radius MFC**), less congruent lateral → lateral rollback · asymmetric liner · raised anterior lip · higher PROMs in some studies

**NOT true of medial pivot (TO)** — ✓ **"spherical variable radius MFC"** (it is **single** radius)

**Flexion → extension in medial-congruent design** → ✓ **medial pivot, femur internally rotates + translates anteriorly**

**Insert truths** — ✓ **standard CR can show paradoxical anterior femoral translation** · AS lip prevents femur rolling **forward** when PCL weak (✗ Wrong options: replaces ACL) · **dual-pivot** = lateral pivot early, **medial pivot deep** · mobile bearing (✗ Wrong options: medial pivot) has insert–tray motion · PS **promotes** rollback (✗ Wrong options: minimises)

## 5 Management

### Cruciate-retaining vs posterior-stabilised

| | **CR** | **PS (cruciate-substituting)** |
|---|---|---|
| Needs | **Intact functional PCL** | PCL **sacrificed** → flexion gap ↑ |
| Indications | Minimal bone loss/laxity · **varus <10°**, **valgus <15°** | **Patellectomy** · **inflammatory arthritis** (late PCL rupture) · deficient PCL · severe deformity (allows **lateralisation** of components → better tracking) (TO) |
| X-ray | **No box** | **Box / cam** |
| Advantages | No post impingement/dislocation · less distal femur cut · **proprioception** · closer kinematics (debated) | Easier balance without PCL · arguably more ROM · easier exposure · ✓ **tolerates joint-line change better** |
| Disadvantages | Tight PCL → **accelerated PE wear** · loose PCL → **flexion instability** · ↑ PF contact pressure · **does not tolerate joint-line alteration** (TO) | **Cam jump** · **tibial post wear** · ✓ **patellar clunk** · **more bone resected** · ↑ **intra-op fracture** (box) |
| Survivorship / flexion | **Equal** | **Equal** |
| Cementless | Available | Available |

**Low-volume surgeons (<20 TKA/yr)** — use PS (CR balancing demanding) (TO)

**PCL resection simulates** → ✓ **excessive posterior femoral resection** (↑ flexion gap)\
✗ Wrong options: distal resection (loose extension) · augments/oversize (tight flexion)

**CR — recreate PCL tension EXCEPT** → ✓ **increasing posterior tibial slope** (loosens flexion, under-tensions PCL) · do: match AP femoral size, conservative distal cut + soft-tissue release, anatomic rotation (femoral IR / tibial ER alter tension most), resect only what is replaced

**PCL injured intra-op, gaps balanced → CR with conforming (UC/AS) insert** → ✓ **no difference in functional outcomes vs PS**\
✗ Wrong options: flexion instability · paradoxical translation (only with standard insert) · clunk · PJI

**Patellectomy** → ✓ **PS** (AP instability with weak extensor) · gold standard, though newer CR data similar\
✗ Wrong options: UKA · CR · constrained

**Best CR candidate** → ✓ **OA with 10° valgus**\
✗ Wrong options: RA · patellectomy · bicruciate rupture · steroid-treated SLE

**PS vs CR — true** → ✓ **higher patellar clunk**\
✗ Wrong options: less bone · better survivorship · fewer fractures · cementless only

**Flexed femoral component + excess tibial slope (PS)** → ✓ **excessive tibial post wear** (anterior post impingement → osteolysis)\
✗ Wrong options: recurvatum · coronal instability · clunk · patellar instability

### PS complications

| Complication | Mechanism | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **Cam jump** | **Loose flexion gap** or hyperextension | Closed reduction (**anterior drawer at 90°**) → revise flexion gap |
| **Post wear** | Femoral flexion, slope, hyperextension | Avoid excess distal resection, flexion, slope |
| **Patellar clunk** | Scar nodule (posterior distal quad tendon / superior pole) catches in box ~**30–45°** extension · small femur, flexed femur, undersized button · within 12 mo | Observe or **arthroscopic/open excision** · longer trochlear groove designs ↓ |

### Constraint selection (VVC, hinge)

| Design | Mechanism | Indications |
|---|---|---|
| **CCK / VVC (constrained non-hinged)** | **Large tall tibial post in deep femoral box**, no axle · V/V + rotational stability (partial) · functions as PS otherwise | **MCL or LCL attenuation/deficiency** · **V/V laxity >5 mm** · **flexion gap laxity** · **moderate bone loss in neuropathic** knee · **severe deformity (>20°) with collateral incompetence** · irreparable intra-op MCL · post-polio (some) · stems advised |
| **Rotating hinge** | **Linked** components (axle) · tibial bearing rotates on yoke (↓ constraint) · **extension stop** | ✓ **Global ligament deficiency** · **hyperextension instability / recurvatum (>5°)** (polio, tumour) · tumour resection · **massive bone loss in neuropathic joint** · **gross flexion–extension mismatch (>15 mm)** despite releases/upsizing · chronic MCL loss in **elderly low-demand** · periprosthetic fracture / DFR |

**CCK cannot control hyperextension** → ✓ **only hinge for hyperextension deformity** (TO) · hinge **can** be used in primary TKR (TO) · CCK is not "deep dish + small post"

**CCK identification** — picture of large post = **CCK** (TO) · CCK = "semi-hinge", low-budget back-up for on-table collateral transection (TO) · TO also states on-table **MCL transection → hinge ideal** vs OB: repair + CR/PS + brace or CCK — read the options

**Both constrained** — ↑ femoral bone resection · ✓ **aseptic loosening** from constraint (first-gen **GUEPAR hinge** → high loosening)

| Scenario | Answer |
|---|---|
| **Charcot valgus**, still medial lax after ITB/popliteus/LCL release + thicker poly | ✓ **convert to constrained TKA** (✗ Wrong options: lateral retinacular release ± MPFL · MCL imbrication · valgus recut) |
| Diabetic **Charcot knee** — initial implant | ✓ **hinge / constrained** (✗ Wrong options: CR · PS · fusion · UKA) |
| Charcot, PS used → instability, maltracking, AP laxity | ✓ **should have used hinged TKA** |
| Charcot valgus with bone loss — counsel | ✓ **↑ constraint needed for global instability** (↑ loosening risk; cones + long stems) |
| **84 yo, grossly attenuated MCL** (valgus) | ✓ **VVC** (96% at 10 yr) (✗ Wrong options: MCL repair/scaffold + PS · LCL release · lateral UKA) |
| Obese, **MCL-deficient** revision | ✓ **constraint resisting varus–valgus loads** |
| **Absolute indication for non-hinged constrained** | ✓ **varus >20° with LCL incompetence** (✗ Wrong options: arthrodesis conversion · hyperextension (hinge) · repairable MCL · UKA revision) |
| **Post-polio, hyperextension 15°, global instability** | ✓ **hinged TKA** (✗ Wrong options: CR + reconstruction · PS · DFO · AKA) |
| **Polio, quad weakness, valgus + recurvatum** | ✓ **rotating hinge** (extension stop) (✗ Wrong options: PS with under-resection · VVC) |
| **CCK kept available because** | ✓ **27° valgus** (MCL likely incompetent) (✗ Wrong options: RA · 22° recurvatum (hinge) · LLD · patellar dislocation) |
| **Strongest hinge indication** | ✓ **aseptic revision with >15 mm F/E gap mismatch despite femoral upsizing** (✗ Wrong options: 15° deformity + erosion (wedges) · after dynamic spacer (collaterals intact → CCK) · baja) |
| **Elderly RA, 20° FFD, valgus laxity, + posterior drawer, low demand** | ✓ **cemented rotating hinge + metaphyseal cones** (✗ Wrong options: press-fit · MCL reconstruction) |
| **Implant for polio genu recurvatum** | ✓ **rotating hinge** (✗ Wrong options: intra-op LCL/MCL disruption · diabetic neuropathy · aseptic loosening) |
| **CCK on X-ray — indication** | ✓ **MCL insufficiency + 13° valgus** (✗ Wrong options: hyperextension · 6° valgus with 2 mm laxity (CR) · unresurfaced patella · PCL over-resection (UC or PS)) |
| **Rotating hinge figure — constrained motions** | ✓ **partial V/V + partial IR/ER** · use least constraint that gives stability |
| **Overconstrained, high loosening** | ✓ **GUEPAR (first-gen hinge)** |

### Mobile bearing & polyethylene conformity

**Mobile bearing** — PE **rotates on tibial baseplate** · PCL removed · ↑ contact area → theoretically **↓ PE wear + ↓ bone–implant stress** · relative indication: young active · ✓ **no improvement in survivorship** vs fixed (flexion 112° vs 108°; recovery, strength, wear not proven)\
✗ Wrong options: 15° more flexion · faster recovery

**Bearing spin-out** — ✓ mobile-bearing-specific · **loose flexion gap** → tibia rotates behind femur · lateral X-ray without cam outline (TO) · closed reduction at 90° + anterior drawer → revise flexion gap

**↑ Conformity (fixed bearing)** — ✓ **↓ contact stress** in PE · ✓ **↓ femoral rollback** · ↑ shear to fixation\
✗ Wrong options: ↑ cracking · ↑ delamination · ↓ loosening · PF stability change

### Modular vs all-polyethylene tibia

| | **Modular (metal tray + insert)** | **All-polyethylene (monoblock)** |
|---|---|---|
| Wear | ✓ **More backside wear** (articular side unchanged) | No backside wear → ✓ **less osteolysis** |
| Cost | Higher | ✓ **Less expensive** |
| Flexibility | Poly exchange possible · sizing after cementing | Size chosen before cementing · full tibial revision if problem |
| Outcomes | — | **Equivalent function** (2–10 yr) · equivalent loosening (Orthobullets) · recent: better survival, ↓ infection, instability, loosening, fracture (OB) · OK in BMI <37.5 |
| Load distribution | More even (biomechanics) | — |
| Prep / bone removal / stability | Same | Same |

### Materials & component geometry (TO)

| Item | TO fact |
|---|---|
| **Femoral component** | **CoCr** (strength + polish) · zirconium / ceramic = hypoallergenic |
| **Tibial tray** | **Titanium** — modulus close to bone |
| **PE** | **6th-generation cross-linked UHMWPE + vitamin E** → best wear / longevity |
| **Single-radius femur** | Constant curvature → consistent rollback, **greater flexion** |
| **Multi-radius (J-curve) femur** | Mimics native knee · smooth ligament tension |
| **Deep trochlear groove** | Better **patellar tracking** |
| **Symmetric tibia** | Easier rotational alignment, less anatomic |
| **Asymmetric tibia** | Matches anatomy, **better coverage** |
| **Insert anterior bevel** | Prevents **patellar tendon impingement** |
| **Size matching** | Femoral + tibial sizes may differ by **± 1 size only** |

**Posterior condylar offset (TO)** — posterior femoral cortex → posterior condyle · **↑ offset = ↑ flexion**

**High-flex design (TO)** — **high posterior condylar offset** · slope **~7°** · deep trochlea · bevelled anterior insert · balanced gaps → **≥ 130° flexion**

**Bone cement (TO)** — **Simplex = white** (standard PMMA) · **Palacos = green** (faster setting, high visibility) · antibiotic cement only if infection risk

### Fixation – cemented vs cementless, cement viscosity

**Most consistent tibial fixation** — ✓ **cement metaphysis + keel**\
✗ Wrong options: cement plateau only + press-fit keel → ↑ loosening

**Cementless** — trabecular/porous ingrowth · ✓ **equivalent functional outcomes** (RCTs to 2 yr) · **shorter** op time, ↑ cost · no difference in femoral loosening, DVT, Hb drop · ideal: ✓ **young, active, obese** (e.g. 52 yo, BMI 44)\
✗ Wrong options: **elderly** (esp. elderly white females), **osteoporosis**, **irradiated bone**, **inflammatory arthritis**, metabolic bone disease

**High-viscosity cement** — ✓ **longer working time** · shorter mixing/waiting · bone penetration equal or less · loosening data conflicting (possibly ↑) · pulmonary complications same

### Patellar resurfacing

**Routine resurfacing vs retention** — ✓ **↓ revision risk** (retention: **20–30% ↑** revision in registries; 5.7% revised for anterior pain)\
✗ Wrong options: no complications · ↑ anterior pain · ↓ fracture

**TO lecture notes** — **do not resurface routinely in OA** · resurface for **RA / crystal arthropathy (gout)** or severely damaged cartilage · routine resurfacing ↓ revision (current chapter) — read the options

### Gender-specific implants

**Women with standard components** — ✓ **greater implant survivorship** than men · no evidence of inferior outcomes · female designs: narrower ML, thinner anterior flange, ↑ trochlear angle

### Metal allergy

**Nickel allergy** → ✓ **oxidised zirconium femur + titanium (or all-poly) tibia** · CoCr + stainless contain nickel · **titanium** unsuitable as articulating femur (metallosis)

### Patient-specific instrumentation

**PSI** — custom guides from CT/MRI · ✓ **no IM femoral hole needed** (also navigation) → theoretical ↓ fat embolism (TO) · claimed ↓ time, blood loss, trays (TO)

**Only proven benefit (OB)** — ✓ **fewer instrument trays** · (✗ Wrong options: cost · alignment · function · pain/satisfaction) · custom guides → **no difference in ROM, alignment, outcome scores**

### Highly congruent liners & minimum thickness

**Highly congruent (medial-pivot) liners** — medial concavity + lateral translation · equivalent short/mid-term outcomes + survival

**Minimum PE insert thickness** — ✓ **8 mm** (TO)
