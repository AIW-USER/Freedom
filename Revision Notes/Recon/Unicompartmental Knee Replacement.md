# Unicompartmental Knee Replacement

*Recon · 64 / 65*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Incidence** — **5%** of knee arthroplasties · **medial** compartment most common

**Classic candidate** — older (**>60**), lower-demand, thin (**<82 kg**) · only **6%** of patients meet these criteria with no contraindication · indications expanding to younger, heavier, more active patients

### Implant types – fixed vs mobile bearing

| | **Fixed-bearing** (Miller-Galante, Zuk) | **Mobile-bearing** (Oxford) |
|---|---|---|
| Design | PE **locked** in tray · **flat** tibial surface | Fully **congruent, unconstrained** PE glides between components |
| Contact | **Flat-on-round** → point loading, high contact stress | Large contact area → low stress |
| PE wear | Theoretically **↑** (fails in **later** years) | **↓** · excellent survivorship to 2nd decade |
| Dislocation | **Impossible** | ✓ **Bearing dislocation (spin-out)** + impingement (fails in **early** years) |
| Technique | Easier · forgiving of laxity / small gap mismatch | **Technically demanding** · flexion gap must **equal** extension gap · stability from **MCL** tension |
| ACL deficiency | Controversial | **Contraindicated** (spin-out) |
| Loosening | Component loosening more common | Poor bone preparation / thermal necrosis |

**Mobile-bearing specific disadvantage** → ✓ **higher risk of bearing dislocation** (TO)\
✗ Wrong options: PCL sacrifice · limited femoral sizing · ↑ PE wear

**After UKA + twisting injury — true?** → ✓ **mobile-bearing UKA has more dislocation** (TO)\
✗ Wrong options: lateral UKA more linear wear · medial more dislocation · fixed less linear wear

### Anteromedial OA (AMOA) concept (TO)

**AMOA** — wear confined to **anterior + middle thirds** of the medial tibial plateau · needs a **functional ACL** + competent **MCL** · varus is **intra-articular + correctable** once cartilage height restored ✓\
✗ Wrong options: justifies fixed bearing · eliminates PF symptoms · allows PCL sacrifice

**Why** — intact ACL keeps femur anterior on tibia in flexion · ACL deficient → tibia subluxes anteriorly → femur articulates further back → **posterior tibial wear**

**True AMOA** — **bone-on-bone in extension** · joint space partly preserved in **deep flexion** (posterior cartilage spared) · **full-thickness lateral cartilage**

### Advantages

| vs **TKA** | vs **Osteotomy (HTO)** |
|---|---|
| ✓ **Faster rehab + recovery** · ↓ blood loss · ↓ morbidity · ↓ cost · ✓ **↓ PJI + wound complications** · ↓ MI, PE, VTE, mortality · ✓ **near-native kinematics** (ACL, PCL, other compartments kept) · ✓ **better ROM** · smaller incision → less pain, shorter stay · bone stock preserved | Faster rehab · better cosmesis · ↑ initial success · fewer short-term complications · **lasts longer** · **easier conversion to TKA** |

**Disadvantages vs TKA** — **higher revision rate** · lower survivorship · ↑ aseptic loosening · **technically more demanding** · **OA progression** (unique) · **2×** early (90-day) periprosthetic fracture

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

**Lateral compartment** — confirm **full-thickness cartilage** on **valgus stress view** (TO)

**Lateral view of medial OA** — anterior / central tibial wear = AMOA ✓ · ✓ **posterior-third tibial wear = ACL incompetent** (TO) → contraindication to standard (mobile-bearing) UKA\
✗ Wrong options: PCL incompetent · global OA · MCL contracted

**Mobile bearing** — contains a **radiographic marker** → shows bearing dislocation

**Stress fracture** — X-ray may be normal · ✓ **bone scan uptake under the tibial component**

**PFA work-up** — standing AP + lateral · **Rosenberg** (45° flexion PA) · **skyline** (Merchant) · standing full-leg

### Diagnostic criteria – Kozinn & Scott vs Oxford (TO)

| Criterion | **Kozinn & Scott 1989** (fixed bearing) | **Oxford** (mobile bearing) |
|---|---|---|
| Age & weight | **>60 yr, <82 kg (180 lb)** | **No limits** |
| Activity | Low demand / sedentary | No restriction |
| Deformity | **Varus <10°, valgus <5°** | Varus intra-articular + **manually correctable to neutral** |
| Flexion | **>90°, contracture <5°** | **>90°, contracture <15°** |
| Ligaments | Intact ACL | Intact **ACL + competent MCL** |
| PF joint | No PFJ arthritis | Ignored unless **severe lateral facet grooving + bone loss + anterior pain** |
| Lateral compartment | Pristine | **Full-thickness cartilage** (valgus stress view) |

**No longer a barrier (modern / Oxford)** → ✓ **high BMI** (TO)\
✗ Wrong options: lateral cartilage loss · fixed flexion deformity · inflammatory arthritis (still barriers)

## 5 Management

```
Isolated compartment knee OA, failed nonop
├─ Inflammatory / multicompartment / unstable → TKA
├─ Young, active, mild OA, malaligned → OSTEOTOMY
│      └─ Lateral OA in correctable valgus knee → VARUS-PRODUCING DFO
└─ Older, bone-on-bone, intact ACL + MCL → UKA
       ├─ Medial tibiofemoral → MEDIAL UKA
       └─ Isolated patellofemoral → PFA
```

### Indications & contraindications

**Indications (TO)** — isolated medial or lateral OA · **osteonecrosis** · age **>60** · weight **<82 kg** · deformity **<15°**, passively correctable to neutral · flexion contracture **<5°** · ROM **>90°**

| Contraindication | Threshold / note |
|---|---|
| ✓ **Inflammatory arthritis** (RA, psoriatic) | **Absolute** — involves all compartments → progression (TO, OB) |
| **Fixed varus** | **>10°** |
| **Fixed valgus** | **>5°** → ✓ fixed valgus **10°** = absolute CI (TO) |
| **Flexion contracture** | **>5–10°** (Orthobullets) · **>10°** (TO list) · **≥15°** (TO) · **15°** = CI (OB) · >5° relative (OB) — read the options |
| **Arc of motion** | **<90°** |
| **ACL deficiency** | **Absolute for lateral UKA** · controversial for medial fixed-bearing · **contraindicated for mobile-bearing** (spin-out) · TO: **relative** — reconstruct ACL at same sitting |
| **Previous meniscectomy, other compartment** | e.g. complete **lateral** meniscectomy for a medial UKA |
| **Tricompartmental OA** | Diffuse / global pain |
| Opposite-compartment cartilage erosion | Weight-bearing area |
| **Grade IV PF chondrosis** | Anterior knee pain · exposed subchondral bone under patella |
| Young, high-activity, heavy labourer | Relative |
| **Joint subluxation ≥5 mm** | Relative (OB) |
| **Obesity** | Relative historically (BMI >30, OB) · no longer a barrier (TO, OB) |

**NOT contraindications** — **SONK / osteonecrosis of MFC** (UKA works: 90% good at 5.5 yr, **96%** survivorship at 12 yr) · prior **ACL reconstruction** with functioning graft (**grade 1A Lachman**) · prior **medial meniscal transplant** · **contralateral knee OA** · asymptomatic / medial PF chondromalacia · **age >70** · age <60 with modern implants · **prior HTO** · previous trauma / plateau ORIF (scar only) · **fixed varus 5°** · **9° varus** · BMI 36, weight 80 kg · **Q-angle 13°** in a male (normal)\
✗ Wrong options: traps

**ACL found torn at arthrotomy for planned UKA (55 yr)** → ✓ **reconstruct ACL + proceed with UKA** (TO)\
✗ Wrong options: continue + intervene later · convert to TKR and tell after · simply fixed-bearing UKA

**Most important relative CI to mobile-bearing UKA** → ✓ **prior failed ACL reconstruction** (ACL deficiency → spin-out)\
✗ Wrong options: medial PF OA · age >70 · flexion contracture 10° (OK if <15°) · previous HTO

**Relative CI** → ✓ **Outerbridge IV MFC + grade IIb Lachman** (non-functioning ACL)\
✗ Wrong options: meniscal transplant · ACLR with grade Ia Lachman · Outerbridge IV medial alone (that is the indication)

### UKA vs HTO – patient selection

| | **UKA** | **HTO or UKA** | **HTO** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Age | >55 yr | 55–65 yr | <65 yr |
| Activity | Low demand | Moderately active | Active |
| BMI | <30 | Any | — |
| Alignment | 0–5° | 5–10° | 5–15° |
| AP instability | None–grade I | None–grade I | Any |
| ML instability | None–grade I | None–grade I | None–grade II |
| ROM | Arc 90°, FC <5° | Arc 100°, FC <5° | Arc 120°, FC <5° |
| Arthrosis | Any | Ahlbäck II | Ahlbäck I–II |

**HTO prerequisites (TO)** — <60 yr, active · varus **5–15°** · < grade 3 medial involvement · intact lateral + PF · flexion **>120°** · no laxity · expanded: posterolateral laxity, varus thrust, ACL deficiency (slope change)

**Kissing bone-on-bone lesion, good ligaments, one compartment** → **UKA** over HTO · **KL 4 + other compartments / instability / gross malalignment** → **TKA**

**UKA** — older, less active, **minimal varus, more severe OA**, little instability · **HTO** — younger, active, **milder OA, more malalignment**, AP instability

**40 yr, lateral OA after lateral meniscectomy, correctable 5° valgus, stable** → ✓ **varus-producing DFO** · osteotomy CIs: inflammatory, cruciate deficiency, FFD **>15°**, flexion **<90°**\
✗ Wrong options: valgus HTO · TKA · arthroscopic debridement · AMZ

**68 yr, isolated medial Outerbridge IV, worse after arthroscopy** → ✓ **medial UKA**\
✗ Wrong options: opening-wedge HTO (younger, milder) · bicompartmental (medial + PF) · PFA · lateral UKA

**71 yr, BMI 28, mild varus, stable, wants to kneel** → ✓ **UKA** — best ROM\
✗ Wrong options: HTO · CR / PS / rotating-platform TKA (equivalent ROM to each other)

### Technique

**Alignment** — ✓ **undercorrect mechanical axis by 2–3°** · overcorrection overloads unresurfaced compartment → OA progression · Orthobullets: correct varus to **1–5° valgus**\
✗ Wrong options: overcorrect into valgus 2–3°

**Steps** — remove peripheral + **notch osteophytes** · minimal bone resection · **avoid extensive releases** (never release the **MCL**) · avoid edge loading · correct ML position → no tibial spine impingement · **avoid a varus tibial cut** (↑ loosening) · care with proximal tibial **guide pins** (stress fracture)

**Gaps (mobile bearing)** — flexion gap set by **posterior femoral cut** · flexion = extension gap · tibial slope parallels native **3–7°**

**Bearing unstable in mid-flexion, tight in full extension** → ✓ **inadequate posterior femoral resection** (TO)\
✗ Wrong options: tibial over-resection · excess slope · MCL over-release (lax in both flexion + extension)

**Small incision without patellar dislocation** → ✓ **faster recovery** (2× standard UKA, 3× TKA)\
✗ Wrong options: conversion option · better positioning · better ultimate ROM · ↑ 10-yr survival

### Patellofemoral arthroplasty – indications & failure (TO)

**Ideal PFA candidate** — **<65 yr**, debilitating **isolated PF OA**, no patellar malalignment · pain localised to PFJ, failed NSAIDs / injection · flexion **120°**, FFD **<10°** · not if **valgus >8°** or **varus >5°** unless osteotomy first

**Not an indication** → ✓ **BMI >30** (implant overload) (TO) · inflammatory arthropathy = CI · **tibiofemoral OA** = principal CI

| | **Inlay** (1st gen, resurfacing) | **Onlay** (2nd gen, anterior cut) |
|---|---|---|
| Trochlea | Replaces cartilage only; sits in native trochlea | Uses TKA anterior femoral cut; replaces **entire anterior trochlea** |
| Main problem | **Patellar instability** (short-term failure) | Instability minimised |

**Commonest failure (overall + long-term)** → ✓ **progression of tibiofemoral OA** (TO, OB) · up to **22%** at 5–15 yr\
✗ Wrong options: PF instability (inlay short-term) · patellar tendon rupture · aseptic loosening (<0.5% at 7 yr)

**Early PFA complications** — higher than TKA · persistent anterior pain (extensor malalignment, **oversized femoral component**) · catching / snapping · extensor rupture

**TF progression after PFA** → **convert to TKA** · PFA **not** superior to medial/lateral UKA\
✗ Wrong options: add medial UKA

## 6 Complications

### Failure modes (OA progression, loosening, bearing dislocation)

| Timing | Commonest cause |
|---|---|
| **Early (<5 yr)** | ✓ **Aseptic loosening** — **25–45.3%** of early failures (✗ Wrong options: OA progression · PE wear · stress fracture ~2%) |
| **Mid (5–10 yr)** | **OA progression (38%)** |
| **Late (>10 yr)** | ✓ **OA progression (40%)** · then aseptic loosening **29%**, PE wear **10%** |
| **Overall (TO)** | Contralateral arthritis **39.5%** · aseptic loosening **25.4%** · instability **15.3%** · PE wear **14%** · arthrofibrosis **2.5%** · pain **1.8%** · infection **1.5%** |

**Late (>5 yr) causes (Orthobullets)** — OA progression (idiopathic, **over-correction**, more with mobile-bearing) · component failure (**under-correction** overload) · component loosening (fixed-bearing) · **patellar impingement** on femoral component · PE wear

**Unique to UKA vs TKA** → ✓ **OA progression**\
✗ Wrong options: periprosthetic fracture · PJI · liner dissociation · PE wear (all shared)

**Price & Svard (682 Oxford UKA)** — 29 revisions: **10 OA progression** · 9 loosening · 5 infection · 2 bearing dislocation · 3 unexplained pain

**Survivorship** — fixed-bearing 10 yr **87.4–96%** (failure ~**1%/yr** first decade) → 2nd decade **79–90%** · mobile-bearing **93% at 15 yr** · lateral = medial long-term · UKA revision results worse than TKA revision

![Overcorrection → lateral compartment OA after medial UKA](../../MCQ/attachments/q5665-illa-overcorrection.jpg)

### Aseptic loosening

**Risk** — **all-PE tibia** · **fixed-bearing** · component malposition (**varus tibia**) · poor initial fixation · weight **>82 kg** · tibial side more common

**43 yr, BMI 43, loose varus tibial component at 1 yr, normal ESR + CRP** → ✓ **revise UKA to TKA** (no aspiration needed when markers normal)\
✗ Wrong options: aspiration · explant + spacer · tibial-only or both-component UKA revision

### Bearing dislocation (mobile bearing)

**Cause** — almost always **technical error**: **gap mismatch** · **MCL damage / release** · impingement by retained **cement or osteophytes** · component malalignment

| Technical error | Effect |
|---|---|
| ✓ **Flexion gap 1–2 mm looser than extension** (bearing sized to tight extension gap) | **Posterior spin-out in deep flexion** (squatting) — most classic |
| Flexion gap tighter than extension | Stiffness + deep-flexion pain · no dislocation |
| Retained **anterior** femoral / notch osteophytes | Push bearing **posteriorly** in **terminal extension** |
| Retained **posterior** condylar bone | Pushes bearing **anteriorly** in deep flexion |
| **MCL** injury / pie-crusting | Medial opening → bearing escapes |
| **Excess posterior slope (>10°)** | Bearing slides posteriorly in flexion |
| Too little / reverse slope | Pushes bearing anteriorly |
| Internally rotated tibial tray | Edge-loading, twists out |

**Presentation** — pain, giving way on stairs, **clicking**, effusion, ↓ ROM, stable ligaments, normal markers → ✓ **bearing dislocation**\
✗ Wrong options: PJI · ACL rupture · lateral OA · lateral meniscal tear

**Treatment** — closed reduction · open exploration + **bearing exchange** · convert to fixed UKA · convert to TKA

**Anteromedial knee pain after mobile-bearing medial UKA** → ✓ **anterior impingement** (TO) — technique / malposition\
✗ Wrong options: bearing dislocation · loosening · instability

![Dislocated mobile bearing after medial UKA](../../MCQ/attachments/q217342-a-uka.jpg)

### Medial vs lateral UKA (TO)

**Lateral UKA** — traditionally ↑ dislocation + failure · LCL allows **6.7 mm** lateral distraction in flexion (vs **2 mm** medial) · lateral tibial condyle **convex** (medial **flat**) · lateral femoral condyle subluxes posteriorly in high flexion · newer designs → **equivalent** survival, pain relief, function

### Tibial plateau stress fracture

**Features** — **always tibia** · high activity + heavy patient (**>200 lb**) · **pain-free interval → spontaneous activity pain** · **blood on aspiration** (RBC 45,000, WBC 6,500) · normal X-ray + **bone scan uptake under component**\
✗ Wrong options: infection (WBC >20,000, ↑ ESR/CRP) · loosening · crystals · synovial entrapment · pes bursitis · CRPS

**Technical risk factors** — guide pin **penetrating posterior tibial cortex** · **peripheral medial cortical pin** · **re-drilling** guide holes (≥3 holes) · **undersized tibial component** · **vertical sagittal saw cut too deep** past the horizontal cut (TO)

**NOT a cause of the post-op stress fracture** → ✓ **excessive impaction force** → **intra-operative** fracture, seen at once

**Rate** — ~**2%** of UKA failures · nondisplaced → protected weight bearing · displaced → ORIF or revision

## X vs Y

| | **UKA** | **TKA** |
|---|---|---|
| Recovery / rehab | ✓ **Faster** | Slower |
| Kinematics | ✓ **Near-native** | Altered |
| ROM | **Better** | Less |
| Blood loss, pain meds, stay | Less | More |
| PJI, wound, stiffness | **Lower** | Higher |
| VTE, MI, mortality | Lower | Higher |
| Early periprosthetic fracture (90 d) | **2× higher** | ✓ Lower |
| Revision / survivorship | **Higher revision**, lower survival | ✓ Lower revision, better survival |
| Aseptic loosening | Higher | Lower |
| OA progression | **Yes** — commonest revision cause | No |
| Technical demand | **More** | Less |
| Mobile bearings | Successful | Successful |

| | **Early failure (<5 yr)** | **Late failure (>10 yr)** |
|---|---|---|
| Commonest | **Aseptic loosening** | **OA progression** |
| Typical design | Mobile bearing (dislocation, impingement) | Fixed bearing (PE wear) |
