# Idiopathic Transient Osteoporosis of the Hip (ITOH)

*Recon · 13 / 65*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Incidence** — rare · **men > women (3 : 1)** · rare in Asians

**Two groups** — **middle-aged men (40–55)** (OB: sixth decade) · **women in 3rd trimester of pregnancy** · also non-pregnant women (TO)

**Laterality** — **unilateral** · may **recur**

### Pathogenesis

**Mechanism** — local **hyperaemia** + impaired **venous return** → **marrow oedema** → ↑ intramedullary pressure

**Synonyms** — bone marrow oedema syndrome · regional migratory osteoporosis · migratory osteolysis

## 3 Presentation

**Symptoms** — progressive **atraumatic hip/groin pain** over weeks · sudden onset in some (TO) · may not bear weight · pain with WB + rest

**Exam** — local tenderness · ✓ **hip ROM often normal** (preserved except at extremes) · antalgic gait

### Genes & lab markers

**ESR** — **elevated** (Orthobullets) · OB stem: WBC, ESR, aspiration **normal** → still ITOH

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

**Radiographs (AP + lateral)** — lag clinical signs **4–8 wk** · **diffuse osteopenia of head + neck** · subchondral cortical loss · effusion · **joint space always preserved** · may be normal early

**MRI (modality of choice)** — **diffuse marrow oedema of head + neck** · **T1 ↓ signal** (loss of fatty marrow) · **T2 / STIR ↑ signal** · effusion · **no focal subchondral defect**, no double line

**Bone scan** — **homogeneous ↑ uptake** of head + neck · **precedes X-ray change**

**Mimic** — often mistaken for **metastasis** (no focal mass on MRI) · early subchondral bone formation favours ITOH (not seen in ON)

### Diagnosis of exclusion

**Exclude** — femoral neck **stress fracture** · **infection** · **malignancy** · **AVN**

### ITOH vs avascular necrosis

| | **ITOH** | **AVN** |
|---|---|---|
| Sex | **Male 3:1** | **Equal** (ITOH table) · male > female (AVN note) |
| Age | **3rd-trimester women, middle-aged men** | **20–40** (ITOH table) · 35–50 (AVN note) |
| Laterality | **Unilateral** | **Bilateral >50%** (80% per AVN note) |
| Risk factors | **Pregnancy** | **Alcohol, steroids** |
| Symptoms | Acute, improves with PWB | Insidious, progressive |
| Exam | Preserved except extremes | **Marked ↓ ROM, esp. IR** |
| Radiographs | **Diffuse osteopenia** head + neck at 4–6 wk | **Localized sclerosis, crescent, collapse** |
| Bone scan | **Homogeneous** ↑ head + neck | **Localized** |
| MRI | **Diffuse oedema, no focal/subchondral defect** · ↓ T1, ↑ T2 | **Focal anterosuperior subchondral defect**, double line · ↓ T1 + T2 band |
| Treatment | **Protected WB, symptomatic** | Early surgery |
| Prognosis | **Self-limiting** | Progressive |

## 5 Management

### Diagnosis & protected weight bearing

**First line** — ✓ **protected / partial / toe-touch weight bearing** + supportive care + **NSAIDs**, mild analgesics → prevents **stress fracture**

**Natural history** — **resolves spontaneously in 6–8 months**

**Avoid** — core decompression · vascularized fibula · intraosseous steroid (that is for simple bone cysts) · oral steroids · hemiarthroplasty · THA · PAO · biopsy · pinning · resection/megaprosthesis (malignancy) · radiation · antibiotics (osteomyelitis)

## X vs Y

| | **ITOH** | **Femoral neck stress fracture** |
|---|---|---|
| MRI | **Diffuse** head + neck oedema | **Fracture line** with oedema |
| Treatment | Protected WB | NWB / fixation by site |

## How it's asked

**"37-yr-old woman, sudden groin pain, MRI marrow oedema, no collapse?"** → **toe-touch WB + supportive care** (TO)

**"58-yr-old man, diffuse STIR signal through head and neck?"** → **protected WB + NSAIDs**\
✗ Wrong options: core decompression · fibula · hemi/THA

**"Normal labs + aspirate, diffuse ↓ T1 / ↑ T2?"** → **protected WB + observation**\
✗ Wrong options: biopsy · pinning

**"63-yr-old woman, normal X-ray/CT, marrow replaced on T1?"** → **partial WB + observation**\
✗ Wrong options: megaprosthesis · radiation · antibiotics · core decompression

**"Pregnant (30 weeks), groin pain, ITOH — true?"** → **hip ROM often normal**\
✗ Wrong options: bilateral · needs surgery after birth · focal subchondral defects · late joint degeneration

**"Time to resolution?"** → **6–8 months**

**"Imaging of choice?"** → **MRI**

**"Joint space in ITOH?"** → **always preserved**
