# Hip Arthrodesis

*Recon · 6 / 65*

## 1 Basics

### Biomechanics

**Gait efficiency** — **↓ ~50%**

**Energy** — **↑ O₂ consumption** · **30% more energy** for ambulation

**Adjacent joints** — ↑ contralateral pelvic rotation · ↑ stress on **lumbar spine, ipsilateral knee, contralateral hip**

## 5 Management

```
Hip arthrodesis decision
├─ Young labourer, unilateral painful ankylosis (infection/trauma) → primary fusion
├─ Failed THA (most common) → salvage fusion
└─ Painful fused hip (back > knee > contralateral hip) → convert to THA
       └─ Abductors nonfunctional on EMG → constrained cup
```

### Operative criteria – primary arthrodesis

| Indications | Contraindications |
|---|---|
| **Salvage for failed THA** (most common) | **Active infection** |
| **Young active labourer, painful unilateral ankylosis** after infection or trauma | **LLD >2.0 cm** |
| Neuropathic arthropathy | **Bilateral** hip arthritis |
| Tumour resection | **Adjacent joint degeneration** — lumbar spine, contralateral hip, ipsilateral knee |
| Missed SCFE with severe arthritis, 18-yr-old (OB) | Severe osteoporosis |
| | **Contralateral THA** (THA failure **40%** with contralateral fusion) |

### Optimal fusion position

| Plane | Orthobullets / OB | TO |
|---|---|---|
| **Flexion** | **20–35°** | **20–30°** |
| **Adduction** | **0–5°** | **0–5°** |
| **External rotation** | **5–10°** | **0–15°** |

**Avoid abduction** — pelvic obliquity → ↑ back pain

**OB answer** — ✓ **5° ER, 5° adduction, 20° flexion**\
✗ Wrong options: any abduction · 0° flexion · 15° ER with 0° adduction

**TO answer** — ✓ **20–30° flexion, 0–5° adduction, 0–15° ER**\
✗ Wrong options: 10° flexion · abduction

### Technique

**Goals** — apposition of surfaces · **rigid internal fixation** · early mobilization

**Approach** — **lateral with trochanteric osteotomy** preferred · preserve **abductors** + **superior gluteal nerve** · anterior approach also popular

**Fixation** — **cobra plate** · anterior plating

### Conversion of fusion to THA

**Indications** — **severe low back pain (most common)** · ipsilateral knee pain + instability · contralateral hip pain

**Preop** — **EMG of gluteus medius** · **nonfunctional abductors → constrained acetabular component**

**TO lecture notes** — also **painful pseudarthrosis** · **fusion in improper position → corrective osteotomy, not THR** · abductors assessed **clinically (palpation in lateral decubitus, knee flexed) — no role of EMG / MRI** (TO) vs EMG gluteus medius (Orthobullets) — read the options

**Technique (TO, ankylosis / fusion)** — **double femoral neck osteotomy** (divergent cuts) · ream under C-arm after drilling for depth · centre from Shenton + Köhler lines

**Prognosis (TO)** — **poorest of all difficult THR scenarios** · ↑ **HO** + infection · abductor recovery up to **2 years**

**Outcome** — depends on **abductor function** · weakness → prolonged rehab, **severe lurching gait**

## 6 Complications

**Low back pain** — improved by takedown, but depends on **abductor function**

**Ipsilateral knee** — degeneration + **laxity**

**Contralateral hip** — degeneration

**Adjacent joint degeneration** — **60%** · LBP + ipsilateral knee pain most common · within **25 yr**

## How it's asked

**"Ideal hip arthrodesis position?"** → **20–30° flexion, 0–5° adduction, 0–15° ER** (TO)

**"18-yr-old, missed SCFE, hip fusion — optimum position?"** → **5° ER, 5° adduction, 20° flexion**

**"Most common indication for hip arthrodesis?"** → **salvage of failed THA**

**"Most common reason to convert fusion to THA?"** → **low back pain**

**"Before converting fusion to THA?"** → **EMG of gluteus medius** (nonfunctional → constrained cup)

**"Gait efficiency after hip fusion?"** → **↓ ~50%**, energy ↑ 30%

**"Contraindication to hip fusion?"** → **ipsilateral knee / lumbar / contralateral hip arthritis, LLD >2 cm, active infection**

**"Position to avoid in hip fusion?"** → **abduction**

**"Hip fused in malposition, painful back — next?"** → **corrective osteotomy, not THR** (TO)
