# THA Sciatic Nerve Palsy

*Recon · 33 / 65*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Incidence** — **0–3%** (OB ~**1%**; TO 0.6–3.7%, revision **7.6%**) · by diagnosis (TO): **primary OA 0.5%**, **DDH 2.3%** (5.8% sciatic), **revision 3.5%** (3–8%)

**Litigation** — ✓ **nerve injury = most common cause of THA malpractice litigation (38%)** · LLD second (26%) · dislocation not reported · TO: suits arise mainly from **failure to diagnose/manage** the palsy

### Nerves involved

**Sciatic** — **~80%** (TO >90%) of THA nerve injuries · ✓ **peroneal division almost always** · peroneal: lateral, superficial, tightly packed fascicles, less connective tissue, tethered\
✗ Wrong options: tibial (isolated only ~1%)

**Anatomy** — sciatic nerve **closest to acetabulum at the ischium** · care with **posterior acetabular retractor when hip flexed**

| Approach (TO) | Nerve at risk |
|---|---|
| **Anterior** | **LFCN** (15–81%) > femoral > sciatic |
| **Anterolateral / direct lateral** | **Superior gluteal** |
| **Posterior** | **Sciatic (peroneal)** |

**Other nerves (TO)** — **femoral** (2nd commonest, 2.3% of injuries, ~0.8% of THAs) — anterior retractors, hyperextension, **iliacus haematoma** · **obturator** (rare) — **cement extrusion** through medial wall, anteroinferior screws → **groin pain, medial thigh paraesthesia, adductor weakness** · **superior gluteal** — medius split **>5 cm** above GT → Trendelenburg, often recovers

### Causes

**Mechanisms** — **direct trauma** (retractor, laceration) · **stretch** (lengthening, lateralization, dislocation manoeuvres) · **compression — haematoma**, tight dressings · **cement heat** · **unrecognized lumbar lateral recess stenosis** ("double crush") · positioning (CPN in lateral position) · **unknown ~40%** (TO ≥50%)

### Risk factors

| ✓ Risk factors | Not risk factors |
|---|---|
| **DDH** ✓ (congenitally short neurovascular structures → over-lengthening) | **Obesity / BMI** |
| **Revision** ✓ (highest) | **Rheumatoid arthritis** |
| **Female** | **Osteonecrosis / AVN** |
| **Limb lengthening** | Male sex |
| **Post-traumatic arthritis** | Anterolateral approach |
| **Posterior approach** | Posterosuperior quadrant screws (safe zone) |
| **Cementless femoral fixation** | DMARDs, chronic analgesics, low pre-albumin |
| **Surgeon rating case as difficult** ✓ (Eggli — only correlating factor) | Coxa vara (no exploration needed) |
| **Pre-existing lumbar stenosis / spine surgery** | — |
| **Operative time** ✓ · younger age · smoking · uncontrolled DM · VTE history · experienced surgeon, later case | — |

**Lengthening thresholds** — Orthobullets **>4 cm** · OB keep lengthening **<3 cm** · TO **>3.8 cm** peroneal at risk, **>4 cm** complete sciatic palsy

**TO lecture notes** — **sciatic = commonest nerve** (esp. posterior approach) · highest risk: **revision, DDH, lengthening > 2.5–4 cm** · ↑ risk with **cementless** fixation (snug fit, over-lengthening) · exploration **only in revision** (scarred bed)

**Sciatic exploration** — not routine · advisable with distorted anatomy: ER deformity, **protrusio**, short head/neck, **revision**, **DDH** · ✓ **not needed in coxa vara** (TO)

## 3 Presentation

**Symptoms** — numbness, paraesthesia in sciatic distribution · **foot drop** (no dorsiflexion, no EHL, weak eversion) · "slapping gait"

**Prognosis by deficit** — **worst: complete motor + sensory**\
✗ Wrong options: motor with intact sensation

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

**CT / ultrasound** — suspected **haematoma**

**EMG** — later, to confirm level + guide prognosis · MRI of hip limited by artefact\
✗ Wrong options: as immediate step

**Foot drop with back pain, stable Hb, no swelling (POD 2)** → ✓ **lumbar lateral recess stenosis (double crush)** → lumbar MRI\
✗ Wrong options: positioning · haematoma · infection · VTE

**Foot drop after therapeutic enoxaparin for PE + transfusions** → ✓ **haematoma compressing sciatic nerve**\
✗ Wrong options: mechanical · stenosis · embolic infarct · anaemia

## 5 Management

```
New sciatic palsy after THA
├─ Intraop (wake-up test / neuromonitoring) → extend hip, ↓ lengthening (subtrochanteric shortening)
├─ Immediate post-op → hip extension + knee flexion; loosen dressings
│      └─ Haematoma (drop in Hb, drainage, anticoagulated) → reverse + urgent evacuation
└─ Persistent foot drop → AFO; tendon transfer (PTT) if AFO fails
```

### Prevention – femoral shortening & nerve monitoring

**DDH** — pre-op planning · lengthening **<3 cm** · ✓ **subtrochanteric (femoral) shortening osteotomy** · **downsize components** · **intraop wake-up test** · **neurophysiologic monitoring**\
✗ Wrong options: anterior approach · modular components · larger head · acetabular osteotomy

**Wake-up test shows no dorsiflexion with equal lengths in dysplastic hip** → ✓ **shortening subtrochanteric osteotomy**\
✗ Wrong options: EMG · continue + observe · spacer · neurology

**Intraop MEP/EMG alert** → ✓ **extend the hip**\
✗ Wrong options: traction · flex hip (impinges on retractor) · extend knee · irrigation

### Postoperative haematoma & other causes

**Immediate palsy** — ✓ **hip extension + knee flexion** (slackens nerve) · then search for reversible cause (axial imaging for haematoma)\
✗ Wrong options: hip flexion + knee extension · flat bed NWB · EMG first · knee X-ray

**Haematoma** (warfarin INR 6, Hct drop, saturated dressing, complete palsy) → ✓ **reverse INR + urgent surgical evacuation (I&D)**\
✗ Wrong options: VAC + monitor · transfuse + observe · switch to enoxaparin · IVC filter + exploration

### Prognosis & management of foot drop

**Recovery** — ✓ only **35–40% regain full strength after complete palsy** → **60–65% never fully recover** (OB: 30–40%; TO: best series ~50%) · maximal recovery takes **~2 years**

**Persistent foot drop** — ✓ **AFO first line** (restricts plantarflexion in swing) · continue AFO at 4 weeks–3 months\
✗ Wrong options: spine MRI · ankle fusion · revision THA · sural grafting · early tendon transfer · neurology first

**AFO not tolerated, chronic** → ✓ **posterior tibial tendon transfer through interosseous membrane to lateral cuneiform** (donor needs **≥4/5**, loses 1 grade)\
✗ Wrong options: ankle fusion · split TA transfer (TA 0/5) · peroneus longus (weak) · FHL + TAL

### Retractor injury

**DAA, foot drop + dorsal foot numbness after retractor slip** → ✓ **posterior acetabular retractor (4)** · deep trochanteric retractor also risks sciatic · position 1 → superior gluteal · 2 → **femoral** (≈2 cm from anterior lip) · 3 → **obturator**

### Obturator nerve injury (TO)

**Persistent groin pain, no foot drop, no plantarflexion weakness, no Trendelenburg** → ✓ **obturator nerve**\
✗ Wrong options: femoral (anterolateral thigh) · sciatic (foot drop) · superior gluteal (Trendelenburg)

## X vs Y

| Nerve | Deficit after THA | Typical cause |
|---|---|---|
| **Sciatic (peroneal)** | **Foot drop**, dorsal foot numbness | Lengthening, posterior retractor, haematoma |
| **Femoral** | Quadriceps weakness, anterior thigh numbness | Anterior retractor, iliacus haematoma, cement |
| **Obturator** | **Groin pain**, adductor weakness | **Cement extrusion**, anteroinferior screw |
| **Superior gluteal** | **Trendelenburg** | Medius split >5 cm |
| **LFCN** | Lateral thigh numbness | Direct anterior approach |
