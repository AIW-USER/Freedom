# Adult Hip Dysplasia

*Recon · 1 / 65*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Definition** — abnormal hip development → **shallow acetabulum**, deficient **anterior + lateral** coverage

**Incidence** — US **3–5%** · **~10% of all THA** done for dysplasia · **48% of THA in patients <50 yr** are for dysplasia

**Most common cause of THA <50 yr** (excluding osteonecrosis) — **DDH**\
✗ Wrong options: FAI · SCFE · Perthes/MED · femoral neck fracture

**Demographics** — **females > males** (2–4× relative risk)

**Risk factors** — **breech**, **female**, **primiparity**, **family history**

### Pathophysiology

**Mechanism** — undercoverage → load concentrated on **superolateral acetabular rim** → ↑ contact stress → **degenerative labral tear**, cartilage delamination → **secondary OA**

**Labrum** — **hypertrophied**, intrasubstance tearing (labrum stabilises the hip → overloaded)

**Arthritis timing** — untreated → THA typically in **30s–40s**

### Anatomic abnormalities

| | Dysplastic hip |
|---|---|
| **Acetabulum** | **Shallow, lateralized, anteverted** · deficient **anteriorly + superiorly** (superolateral) · may be **retroverted** with superolateral bone loss (OB) |
| **Femoral head** | **Small**, aspherical |
| **Femoral neck** | **Short**, **↑ anteversion** (+5° to +16°, rises with subluxation) · **coxa valga** (↑ neck-shaft angle) |
| **Greater trochanter** | **Posteriorly placed** |
| **Femoral canal** | **Small, narrow, straight** · thick cortices |
| **Contact area** | **↓ head–acetabulum contact area** |

**"Most common abnormalities"** → ✓ **excessive femoral anteversion + posterior greater trochanter**\
✗ Wrong options: medialized acetabulum · large contact area · large head/long neck · ↓ neck-shaft angle

**Femoral concern before THA** → ✓ **excessive anteversion**\
✗ Wrong options: osteopenia · varus · bowing · stove-pipe femur

**Rotational deformity site** — **diaphysis between lesser trochanter and isthmus**\
✗ Wrong options: metaphyseal torsion

**Normal values** — acetabulum **anteversion 15°, abduction 45°** · femoral neck anteversion **15°** · neck-shaft angle **125°** · head centre at level of **greater trochanter**

## 3 Presentation

**Symptoms** — insidious **groin/hip pain** · worse with hip flexion or ER in weight-bearing · **lateral hip pain + limp / Trendelenburg** (abductor fatigue)

**Exam** — ↑ **internal rotation in flexion** (↑ femoral anteversion) · generalized laxity (**Beighton**) · ± FADIR pain (labral)

**Anterior apprehension sign** — lateral decubitus, hip extended, progressive **ER + adduction** → apprehension/pain

**Prone external rotation test** — anterior force on posterior greater trochanter

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

**Views** — **AP pelvis**, lateral, **false-profile view**

**Findings** — femoral head **asphericity** · **coxa valga** · **narrow femoral canal**

**CT** — preoperative planning only (ordered by treating surgeon) · include **distal femur** if clinical anteversion · canal diameter **over-estimated on AP, under-estimated on lateral**

### Diagnostic criteria – radiographic parameters

| Measure | View | Dysplastic | Borderline | Normal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **LCEA (Wiberg)** — vertical through head centre vs line to lateral acetabular edge | **AP** | **<20°** | **20–25°** | **25–39°** |
| **Tönnis angle** (acetabular roof / sourcil inclination) | **AP** | **>10°** | — | **<10°** |
| **ACEA (Lequesne)** — anterior coverage | **False-profile** | **<20°** | — | **25–40°** · **>40° = FAI** |
| **FEAR index** | AP | — | — | **<5° = stable hip, no treatment** |
| **Extrusion index** (uncovered head / head width) | AP | **>27%** suggests dysplasia (TO) | — | — |

**LCEA cut-off variants** — Orthobullets **<20° dysplastic**, OB **<25° dysplastic** (LCEA or ACEA) · borderline/mild dysplasia **18–25°** (OB) · Wiberg (TO): **>25° normal, 20–25° uncertain, <20° pathological** · CE angle reliable only **>5 yr** (TO)\
✗ Wrong options: <12 yr

**Tönnis angle = acetabular index / acetabular roof angle** — used in **hip dysplasia** (TO) · line teardrop-to-teardrop + line from medial to lateral edge of **sourcil** (weight-bearing subchondral condensation)\
✗ Wrong options: genu varum · Blount · recurvatum

**Paediatric acetabular index** — Hilgenreiner's line + acetabular surface · **<25° after 6 months** (TO)

**Other TO angles** — **MCE** (head centre → medial sourcil) · **acetabular arc** = LCE + MCE

### Acetabular version signs (TO)

**Normal** — slight anteversion · **anterior wall projects medial** to posterior wall · posterior wall descends **through femoral head centre**

**Retroversion** — **anterior wall lateral to posterior wall** · signs: **crossover sign**, **posterior wall sign**, **ischial spine sign** (spine protrudes into true pelvis) · also narrow ischium, laterally protruding iliac wing

**Crossover sign** — anterior rim crosses posterior wall below superolateral margin → **anterior overcoverage OR whole-acetabulum retroversion OR posterior deficiency — any alone or combined** (TO)

**Posterior wall sign** — posterior wall runs **medial to head centre** → posterior deficiency

**Posterior wall lateral to head centre** — prominent posterior wall → **excessive anteversion**

![Posterior wall sign](../../MCQ/attachments/q219941-illc-posterior-wall-sign.jpg)

### Coxa profunda vs protrusio (TO)

| | Coxa profunda | Protrusio acetabuli |
|---|---|---|
| Relation to **ilioischial line** | **Acetabular fossa** medial to line, head is **not** | **Femoral head** crosses medial to line |
| Meaning | Overcoverage **or normal variant (women)** | Excess depth, overcoverage, **pincer** |

### Crowe classification

| Grade | Proximal displacement (% pelvic height) | Femoral head subluxation |
|---|---|---|
| **I** | **<10%** | **<50%** of head vertical diameter (head–neck junction from inter-teardrop line) |
| **II** | **10–15%** | **50–75%** |
| **III** | **15–20%** | **75–100%** |
| **IV** | **>20%** | **>100%** (high dislocation) |

![Crowe classification](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob5008-crowe-classification.jpg)

### Hartofilakidis classification

| Type | Head position | Acetabulum |
|---|---|---|
| **A — Dysplasia** | Within acetabulum despite subluxation | **Segmental superior wall deficiency** · inadequate depth |
| **B — Low dislocation** | **False acetabulum** superior to true | **Superior wall absent** · inadequate depth |
| **C — High dislocation** | Completely uncovered, migrated **superior + posterior** | **Complete deficiency** · excessive anteversion of true acetabulum |

## 5 Management

```
Symptomatic adult hip dysplasia
├─ Concentric, congruent, joint space preserved → PAO (Bernese/Ganz)
│      └─ Retroverted acetabulum → reverse (anteverting) PAO
├─ Unreduced / incongruous joint → salvage (Chiari, shelf)
├─ Borderline/mild only → ± arthroscopy + labral repair + capsular plication
└─ End-stage OA → THA (cup in true acetabulum)
       └─ Lengthening >4 cm (Crowe IV) → + subtrochanteric shortening osteotomy
```

### Nonoperative

**Supportive** — weight loss, NSAIDs, activity modification, intra-articular injection · **limited role** (premature OA) · PT + steroid injection not for lasting relief in 20s

**No remodelling** — triradiate closes ~10 yr · at 18 yr **no pelvic growth left**\
✗ Wrong options: "continued remodelling (observe")

### Periacetabular osteotomy (PAO) indications

**Indication** — **symptomatic dysplasia**, adolescent/adult, **concentrically reduced hip + congruous joint space** · preserved ROM · **minimum 2 mm joint space** (OB) · best: **very low VCE 0–15°**, concentric through ROM

**Typical stem** — young woman, LCEA ~15°, ACEA ~14–18°, Tönnis 15°, labral tear, **no chondral lesion** → ✓ **Bernese PAO**\
✗ Wrong options: surgical dislocation + osteoplasty (for FAI) · proximal femoral osteotomy (paediatric/abnormal NSA) · Salter · THA (needs advanced OA)

**Intraop test** — need **≥90° flexion + 15° IR** after correction, otherwise add femoral osteotomy (prevent FAI)

**Advantages** — **hyaline cartilage coverage** · **posterior column intact → WBAT** · large multidirectional corrections · preserves external rotators · delays THA

**Outcome** — **92% survivorship at 15 yr** avoiding THA · **>90% at 10–15 yr** · **better long-term survivorship than arthroscopy** (✓) · retroversion series: 10-yr survivorship **PAO 79% vs rim trimming 23%**

**Secondary instability** — after arthroscopic labral debridement, **not after PAO**

**Technique** — **modified Smith-Petersen** approach · osteotomies of **pubis (superior pubic root), ilium, ischium** · **avoids posterior column** (fracture → pelvic discontinuity → plate)

**Later THA after PAO** — ↑ risk of **retroverted cup**

**Reverse (anteverting) PAO** — **acetabular retroversion**: ✓ **LCEA <20° + positive posterior wall sign**\
✗ Wrong options: LCEA 21° + negative PWS · LCEA 23° + Tönnis 8 · LCEA 30°

![Bernese periacetabular osteotomy](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob5008-ganz-osteotomy.jpg)

### Pelvic osteotomy descriptions

| Osteotomy | Description | Patient |
|---|---|---|
| **Bernese / Ganz PAO** | **Multiple cuts: pubis, ilium, ischium** near acetabulum ✓ | Skeletally mature |
| **Salter** | **Above acetabulum through ilium to sciatic notch** · opening wedge · hinges on **mobile symphysis / triradiate** | Immature |
| **Pemberton** | From **AIIS posteriorly** → ends at **ilioischial limb of triradiate** | Immature |
| **Dega** | Acetabular roof → triradiate · **incomplete** pericapsular cuts · **does not violate sciatic notch** · volume-reducing | **Neuromuscular** DDH |
| **Chiari** (salvage) | Cut **above acetabulum to sciatic notch**, **shift ilium laterally** · relies on **fibrocartilage metaplasia** · **shortens limb** | Incongruous joint |
| **Staheli shelf** (salvage) | **Extra-articular buttress** of bone over subluxed head · fibrocartilage cover | Incongruous joint |

**Salvage outcome** — **84% survivorship at 17 yr** (advanced OA endpoint) · poor with advanced DDH, head asphericity

### Hip arthroscopy failure in dysplasia

**Role** — controversial · **adjunct to PAO** (chondral, labral, cam lesions; abandon PAO if major chondral damage) · **contraindicated in moderate–severe dysplasia** · alone only for **borderline/mild** (with **labral repair + capsular plication**)

**Prevalence** — dysplasia in **~13%** of hip arthroscopy patients (mostly borderline/mild)

**Outcome vs no dysplasia** — ✓ **greater failure rate** · **accelerated arthritis** (~⅓) · ↑ **femoral head subluxation/migration** · **reoperation ~½** · inferior function · **iatrogenic instability**\
✗ Wrong options: decelerated arthritis · ↓ subluxation · ↓ reoperation · greater improvement

**Missed DDH in FAI patients** → poor outcomes after arthroscopic osteoplasty

### Hip resurfacing

**Indication** — **Crowe I or II** · **cannot address LLD** · **10% revision at 6 yr** · higher revision/complications than general population · posterior approach · complication: **femoral neck fracture**

### THA in dysplasia – cup coverage, shortening osteotomy, nerve palsy

**Indication** — **end-stage OA** secondary to dysplasia ✓\
✗ Wrong options: hemiarthroplasty (acetabular arthritis) · redirectional osteotomies (best before arthritis)

**Cup goal** — **true acetabulum** (low + medial) · **small reamers 38–44 mm**, **small cup <50 mm** · no jumbo cup (reams away columns, medial wall blow-out)

**Coverage rule** — **uncemented cup OK if <30% uncovered** → ✓ accept **25% superolateral uncoverage** and proceed · insufficient coverage = **<60–70%** covered\
✗ Wrong options: ↑ abduction to 60° (↓ coverage, ↑ wear) · breach medial wall · elevate hip centre · cement augments

**Options for deficient coverage** — downsize cup · **medialize** · elevate hip centre · **trabecular metal / cement augments** · superolateral bone graft · tantalum revision shells

**Pseudoacetabulum** — in **ilium**, poor support · cup there → **loosening, malposition, ilium fracture** (avoid)

**High hip centre** — gains bony support but **↓ abductor tension, ↑ JRF, ↑ loosening, ↑ dislocation** · bone poorer higher in ilium

**Cemented cup** — PMMA poor in **shear** → high loosening (avoid)

**Bearing** — avoid **MoM** in young · **ceramic-on-PE** longest survivorship, fewest complications (OB)

**Femur** — **modular stems** correct rotational deformity · trochanteric osteotomy aids exposure in **Crowe III/IV**

### Femoral shortening & sciatic nerve palsy

**Risk** — restoring true hip centre in high dislocation → **over-lengthening** → **sciatic palsy (peroneal division)** → **foot drop** (TO)\
✗ Wrong options: hip centre too inferior (concern is too superior) · over-medialization (over-lateralization is the concern) · acetabular fracture (less likely)

**Threshold** — lengthening **>4 cm** → palsy (Orthobullets, OB) · **2–4 cm** associated with palsy (OB)

**Muscle at risk** — ✓ **EHL** (peroneal)\
✗ Wrong options: quadriceps (femoral) · lateral gastrocnemius/tibialis posterior/FHL (tibial) · adductor magnus · semitendinosus

**Prevention** — ✓ **subtrochanteric femoral shortening osteotomy** · also corrects **angular/rotational deformity** · obviates GT osteotomy · proximal + **diaphyseal-engaging** fit, no extra hardware · alternatively **downsize components**\
✗ Wrong options: upsizing

**Not preventive** — hip extension + knee flexion during exposure (helps exposure only) · **anterior approach** (no ↓ palsy; ↑ femoral nerve risk) · ↑ offset (does not change length) · navigation · ↑ medialization · anterior retractor placement · EMG · hamstring/quadriceps lengthening · GT osteotomy with progressive shortening (↑ nonunion, needs hardware)

## 6 Complications

| Complication | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Sciatic palsy** | **10× ↑** incidence (**5–15%**) · lengthening **>4 cm** · peroneal division **80%** · only **35–40%** recover fully |
| **GT osteotomy nonunion** | **29%** · ↓ with **subtrochanteric osteotomy** + trochanteric advancement |
| **Dislocation** | **5–10%**, esp. **high hip centre** |
| **Loosening** | ↑ with **high hip centre** cup · Crowe III/IV revision rates > non-dysplastic |
| Others | Periprosthetic femur fracture · infection · neurovascular injury |

**Long-term** — higher THA revision rate in dysplastic hips · Crowe I–II short-term = primary OA

**Sciatic palsy risk factors** — **dysplasia**, revision, **female**, post-traumatic OA, **lengthening** · causes: posterior-approach trauma, stretch, haematoma, cement heat

## How it's asked

**"Young woman, LCEA 16°, Tönnis 15°, labral tear, cartilage intact — best surgery?"** → **Bernese PAO**\
✗ Wrong options: surgical dislocation + osteoplasty · Salter · THA

**"Which statement about osteotomies is true?"** → **Bernese = multiple cuts in pubis, ilium, ischium**\
✗ Wrong options: Salter from AIIS (that is Pemberton)

**"Dysplasia, 18 yr, labral tear — true statement?"** → **PAO long-term survivorship > arthroscopy**\
✗ Wrong options: secondary instability after PAO · remodelling continues · Dega for retroversion

**"Reverse PAO — imaging that led to decision?"** → **LCEA 18° + positive posterior wall sign**

**"Hip arthroscopy with dysplasia vs without?"** → **greater failure rate**

**"Most common femoral abnormality in DDH?"** → **excessive anteversion + posterior greater trochanter**

**"Femoral concern on THA templating in DDH?"** → **excessive anteversion**

**"Crowe IV THA — clinical scenario most likely?"** → **over-lengthening the extremity** (TO)

**"Crowe IV THA — nerve supply of which muscle at risk?"** → **EHL** (TO)

**"Foot drop after THA with large lengthening — what would have prevented it?"** → **subtrochanteric femoral shortening osteotomy**\
✗ Wrong options: anterior approach · ↑ offset · upsizing stem

**"Template shows 25% superolateral uncoverage — next step?"** → **accept and proceed as templated** (<30% OK)

**"Crowe IV with pseudoacetabulum — preop planning?"** → **subtrochanteric osteotomy to avoid foot drop**\
✗ Wrong options: cup in pseudoacetabulum · jumbo cup · MoM · cemented cup

**"Most common cause of THA <50 excluding osteonecrosis?"** → **DDH**

**"52-yr-old with dysplasia and secondary OA?"** → **THA**\
✗ Wrong options: PAO · hemiarthroplasty

**"Positive crossover sign indicates?"** → **anterior overcoverage, retroversion or posterior deficiency — any alone or combined** (TO)

**"Medial acetabular fossa medial to ilioischial line, head not?"** → **coxa profunda** (TO)\
✗ Wrong options: protrusio

**"CE angle of Wiberg — true?"** → **<20° abnormal** (TO)\
✗ Wrong options: only reliable <12 yr · Hilgenreiner–Perkin angle

**"Tönnis angle used in?"** → **hip dysplasia** (TO)
