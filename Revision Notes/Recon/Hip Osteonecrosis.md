# Hip Osteonecrosis

*Recon · 10 / 65*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Incidence** — **20,000 / yr** (US) · **10% of all THAs**

**Demographics** — **male > female** · age **35–50**

**Bilateral** — **80%** · **multifocal** (≥3 joints) **3%** → image contralateral hip

### Etiology & risk factors

| Direct causes | Indirect causes |
|---|---|
| **Irradiation** | **Alcoholism** |
| **Trauma** (MFCA injury) | **Hypercoagulable states** |
| **Haematologic** — leukaemia, lymphoma | **Steroids** (endogenous / exogenous) |
| **Dysbaric** — Caisson disease, "the bends" | **SLE** |
| **Marrow replacement** — **Gaucher** | Transplant |
| **Sickle cell** | Viruses — CMV, hepatitis, **HIV**, rubella, rubeola, varicella |
| | **Protease inhibitors** · **idiopathic** |

**Also (OB)** — **antiphospholipid syndrome** ✓ (20% asymptomatic ON without steroids) · antiseizure drugs · **collagen II mutation** (idiopathic ON)\
✗ Wrong options: collagen I disease · haemochromatosis · achondroplasia · Paget

### Pathophysiology

**Idiopathic final common pathway** — **intravascular coagulation**: microcirculation coagulation → **venous thrombosis** → retrograde arterial occlusion → **intraosseous hypertension** → ↓ flow → AVN → chondral fracture + collapse

**Trauma** — injury to **medial femoral circumflex artery**

| Injury | AVN rate |
|---|---|
| **Femoral head fracture** | **75–100%** |
| **Basicervical fracture** | **50%** |
| **Hip dislocation** | **2–40%** (**2–10% if reduced <6 h**) |
| Femoral neck fracture (OB) | **10–45%** (even non-displaced) |
| Intertrochanteric | Rare |

**↑ risk** — greater displacement, poor reduction · **↓ risk** — quick reduction, capsular decompression of haematoma

### Paediatric hip fracture AVN (TO)

**Best predictors** — ✓ **fracture type + patient age** (meta-analysis, 360 cases)\
✗ Wrong options: open reduction + rigid fixation · surgery <36 h + capsular decompression · displacement + associated injuries

| Delbet type | Level | AVN |
|---|---|---|
| **IA** | Transphyseal **with dislocation** | **~100% (highest)** |
| IB | Transphyseal, no dislocation | High |
| **II** | Transcervical | Intracapsular |
| **III** | Cervicotrochanteric | Intracapsular |
| **IV** | Intertrochanteric | **Extracapsular — lowest** |

## 3 Presentation

**Symptoms** — insidious **anterior hip/groin** pain · worse with **stairs, inclines, impact**

**Exam** — normal early · late like OA (↓ **IR**)

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

**Radiographs** — **AP + frog-lateral** hip · **AP + lateral of contralateral hip**

**MRI** — **sensitivity 99%, specificity 99%** (best test) · order when X-rays negative but suspicion persists · **double-line sign**: **T1 dark band**, **T2 bright inner line** (marrow oedema / granulation) · serpentine low-signal border with central fat-like signal · wedge-shaped subchondral T1 low signal

**Double-line sign meaning (TO)** — **active transition zone** dead bone ↔ granulation tissue = ongoing, often failing repair\
✗ Wrong options: "repair complete, lesion stable"

**Bone marrow oedema on MRI** — ✓ **best predictor of worsening pain + collapse**\
✗ Wrong options: steroid aetiology · effusion · age <30 · Kerboul 180°

**Histology** — **wedge-shaped infarct** with head collapse\
✗ Wrong options: OA · Perthes (no wedge) · TB (more cartilage destruction)

**Bone scan** — cold spot early (Ficat 0–1)

### Steinberg classification (modified Ficat)

| Stage | Radiograph | MRI / bone scan |
|---|---|---|
| **0** | Normal | Normal |
| **I** | Normal | **Abnormal** |
| **II** | **Cystic / sclerotic** change | Abnormal |
| **III** | **Crescent sign** (subchondral collapse) | Abnormal |
| **IV** | **Flattening** of femoral head | Abnormal |
| **V** | **Joint narrowing** | Abnormal |
| **VI** | **Advanced degenerative** change | Abnormal |

![Steinberg III — crescent sign](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob5006-ficat-iii.jpg)

### Ficat–Arlet staging (TO)

| Stage | Radiograph |
|---|---|
| **0** | None (bone scan ↓ uptake) |
| **1** | Normal (bone scan **cold spot**) |
| **2A** | Diffuse/localized osteoporosis, cyst, sclerosis |
| **2B** | **Crescent sign — subchondral collapse with maintained sphericity** ✓ |
| **3** | **Loss of sphericity** |
| **4** | Joint-space narrowing, arthritis |

**Note** — crescent sign = **Steinberg III** (Orthobullets) = **Ficat 2B** (TO) · OB uses Ficat III for crescent, IV flattening, V acetabular change — read which system the stem uses

### Prognosis of collapse – lesion size, Kerboul angle

**Most important factor** — ✓ **lesion size** (>⅓ of head)\
✗ Wrong options: age · sex · BMI/activity · alcohol

**Modified Kerboul combined necrotic angle** — **arc on mid-coronal + mid-sagittal MRI** added (e.g. 126° + 94° = **220°**)

| Combined angle | Orthobullets / OB risk | TO grade |
|---|---|---|
| **<160°** | Low | **Grade 1** — low; CD works |
| **160–190°** | Low | **Grade 2** — moderate |
| **190–240°** | **Moderate** (✓ 220° = moderate) | **Grade 3 — high** |
| **>240°** | **High** | **Grade 4 — very high**, preservation often contraindicated |

**TO high-risk thresholds** — Kerboul **>200°** · **combined necrotic index >33 → >80% collapse** untreated · lesions **>30% volume** — bisphosphonates don't prevent collapse

**Sickle cell** — asymptomatic MRI ON → **>75% progress to collapse** (vs **33%** non-sickle) · screen contralateral hip · some treat both

## 5 Management

```
Hip osteonecrosis
├─ Pre-collapse (Steinberg 0–II), small → bisphosphonate ± core decompression
│      └─ High-risk large lesion → CD + BMAC or vascularized fibula
├─ Small lesion <15%, rotatable → rotational osteotomy
├─ Young, pre- or early collapse, reversible cause → vascularized fibula
└─ Crescent sign / collapse / >40 yr large lesion / chronic steroids → THA
```

### Medical treatment – bisphosphonates

**Indication** — **pre-collapse** (Ficat/Steinberg 0–II) · **alendronate** prevented collapse with subchondral lucency · later RCTs (zoledronate) — **no benefit** in medium/large lesions

**Answer** — ✓ **bisphosphonate** ↓ collapse\
✗ Wrong options: cyclic PTH · RANKL/RANK therapy · SERM

**Mechanism** — ✓ **↓ osteoclast resorption by flattening ruffled border** + ↑ osteoclast apoptosis\
✗ Wrong options: vitamin D · denosumab (RANKL antibody, no benefit) · teriparatide · COX inhibition

**Risks** — atypical femur fracture · ONJ · gastric ulcer · oesophagitis

### Core decompression ± grafting

**Indication** — **early, pre-collapse** · **reversible** aetiology · not after collapse

**Technique** — traditional **8–10 mm** core through subchondral necrosis · alternative **3.2 mm pin passed 2–3×** · relieves **intraosseous hypertension** → ↓ pain · stimulates angiogenesis · augment with BMP / **BMAC**

**High-risk pre-collapse (TO)** — 28-yr-old, double-line sign, Kerboul 210°, CNI 45 → ✓ **core decompression alone insufficient → vascularized fibula or BMAC**\
✗ Wrong options: isolated multiple small-diameter CD · bisphosphonates + protected WB · THA (still young + pre-collapse)

### Rotational osteotomy

**Indication** — **small lesions (<15%)** rotatable off weight-bearing zone · intertrochanteric

**Direction** — **medial** disease → **varus** rotational · **anterolateral** disease → **valgus flexion** osteotomy

**Outcome** — **60–90%** success (Japan) · distorts head → harder THA · not after collapse

### Head-preserving grafting

**Techniques** — ✓ **core track**, **trapdoor**, **light bulb** · **"tramtrack"** — not a technique (TO)

| Technique | Route | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Core track** | Standard core | Wide debridement, simple, no dislocation, low complications |
| **Trapdoor (Mont)** | **Articular surface** flap via **surgical dislocation** | Direct view · cartilage damage, iatrogenic ON risk |
| **Light bulb (Merle d'Aubigné)** | **2 × 2 cm window at head–neck junction** | Allograft packed · **↑ neck fracture risk** |

### Vascularized free fibula

**Indication** — **pre-collapse and collapsed** AVN in **young** patient · reversible aetiology preferred

**Technique** — large core removes necrosis · fibular strut under subchondral bone (prevents/tamps collapse)

**Outcome** — **80% success at 5–10 yr** (some centres) · **less predictable >40 yr**

**Donor complications** — sensory deficit · motor weakness · **FHL contracture** · **tibial stress fracture**

**Tibial stress fracture after graft** — X-ray normal → ✓ **MRI** (or bone scan) → protected WB\
✗ Wrong options: compartment pressures · CT (inferior) · US · biopsy

### Surgical hip dislocation (TO)

**Artery to preserve** — ✓ **deep branch of MFCA**\
✗ Wrong options: deep/ascending LFCA · descending MFCA

**Deep MFCA course** — inferior border of **obturator externus**, between **pectineus (medial) and iliopsoas (lateral)** → intertrochanteric crest → terminal branches under reflected capsule, **posterosuperior neck** → enter bone **2–4 mm distal to head–cartilage junction** · **intact obturator externus protects it**

**Ganz** — ✓ **posterior (Kocher–Langenbeck/Gibson) approach → trochanteric flip osteotomy (≤1.5 cm, 5 mm medius cuff + piriformis left on bed) → anterior arthrotomy (Z-capsulotomy) → anterior dislocation** · capsulotomy stays **anterior to lesser trochanter**\
✗ Wrong options: posterior arthrotomy · posterior dislocation

### THA – outcomes & complications

**Indications** — **crescent sign or worse** (any age) ± acetabular DJD · **irreversible** cause (chronic steroids) · **>40 yr with large lesion** · most reliable pain relief + return of function

**Typical answers** — ✓ **27-yr-old with subchondral collapse** (✗ Wrong options: IT fracture → nail · non-displaced neck → fixation · compression stress fracture → NWB/screws · young displaced neck → ORIF) · 70-yr-old, chronic steroids, bilateral collapse → ✓ **THA** (✗ Wrong options: fibular graft >40 · osteotomy (collapse) · CD · resurfacing (poor bone)) · 68-yr-old, large MRI necrosis → ✓ **THA** (✗ Wrong options: ORIF · biopsy · resection · arthrodesis)

**Technique** — **cementless cup + stem** · **cemented THA → higher failure** in AVN · **hemiarthroplasty abandoned** (poor in young)

**Sickle cell THA** — ✓ **uncemented MoP THA** best · most common intraop complication ✓ **femoral canal perforation (4.9–18.2%)** — marrow hyperplasia + patchy sclerosis → **ream over guidewire**\
✗ Wrong options: fracture distal to implant · pelvic discontinuity · fat embolism arrest · sciatic injury

**Risk vs OA patients** — ✓ **higher post-op dislocation** · **canal perforation** · medial wall breach · **higher medical + surgical complications** · poor bone + soft-tissue laxity (steroids)

**Young AVN vs elderly OA** — ✓ **↑ PE wear + osteolysis**\
✗ Wrong options: sciatic palsy · ↑ longevity · ↓ ROM · ↓ revision

### Resurfacing contraindication

**Indication** — advanced DJD with **small isolated** focus · needs good bone

**Contraindicated** — **chronic steroids** / underlying disease (poor bone) · **renal disease** (metal ions) · **large necrotic segment**

**Higher failure** — ✓ **osteonecrosis of femoral head** · best results: **young men <55 with OA**\
✗ Wrong options: BMI 32 · acetabular changes (acetabulum resurfaced too) · age <55 · male

**Outcome** — acetabular erosion + pain at medium term

### Other options

**Hip arthrodesis** — only very young **labour-intensive** worker

### Sequelae after femoral neck fracture fixation

**AVN collapse after ORIF (72-yr-old)** → ✓ **THA**\
✗ Wrong options: Girdlestone · hardware removal ± revision ORIF

**Hemiarthroplasty for post-traumatic AVN, groin pain, normal labs, aspirate <500 cells** → ✓ **acetabular protrusio** (erosion) → convert to THA · THA gives better function than hemi for AVN\
✗ Wrong options: infection · radiculopathy · impingement · psoas tendinitis
