# Hip Biomechanics

*Recon · 7 / 65*

## 1 Basics

### Definitions

**Joint reaction force (JRF)** — force within a joint in response to forces acting on it · hip: balances **body-weight moment vs abductor moment** → **level pelvis**

**Joint congruence** — fit of surfaces · ↑ congruence → ↑ contact area

**Instant centre of rotation** — point of rotation, shifts with translation · body **centre of gravity just anterior to S2**

**Friction** — **not a function of contact area** · lubrication ↓ friction · **human joint μ = 0.002–0.04** · **metal-on-PE TJA μ = 0.05–0.15**

### Degrees of freedom

**Hip** — ball-and-socket → **3 rotational DOF**: flexion–extension (sagittal), abduction–adduction (frontal), axial rotation (transverse) · **constrains all translations**\
✗ Wrong options: 2 DOF · translations · 6 DOF

### Free-body physics

| Quantity | Equation |
|---|---|
| Newton 1st | No net force → constant velocity |

## 2 Mechanism

### Hip free-body analysis

**W** — body weight minus stance limb = **5/6 body weight**

**M** — abductor force · **R** — JRF = **3–6× body weight**

**Step 1 (moments)** — **A × My = B × W** · A = 5 cm (abductor arm), B = 12.5 cm (body-weight arm) → **My = 2.5 W**

**Step 2** — **Ry = My + W = 3.5 W**

**Step 3** — **R = Ry / cos 30° ≈ 4 W**

![Hip free-body diagram](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob9064-hip-mechanics.jpg)

### Lever-arm ratio (TO)

**Normal ratio** — body-weight lever arm : abductor lever arm ≈ **2.5 : 1** → abductors ≈ **2.5 × BW** to keep pelvis level\
✗ Wrong options: 1:2 · 2:1 · 1:2.5

**FNB 2024** — "abductor lever arm ratio in single-leg stance" with options 0.5/1.5/2.5/3.5 — answer not given by source; matches **2.5** above

**Arthritic hip** — **abductor lever arm shortened** (head lost, neck short) · body-weight arm **unchanged** · ✓ **ratio ↑ (up to 4 : 1)**\
✗ Wrong options: abductor arm lengthened · body-weight arm lengthened · unchanged

**Surgical aim** — ratio toward **1 : 1** → total hip load ↓ **~30%** (theoretical)

## 3 Presentation

### Trendelenburg gait

**Cause** — ✓ **hip abductor weakness**\
✗ Wrong options: adductors · flexors · extensors · knee extensors

**Pattern** — ✓ **pelvis on swing side drops → ↑ adduction of affected hip in stance**\
✗ Wrong options: swing side elevates · ↓ adduction · circumduction

**Compensation (lurch)** — trunk leans **over affected hip** → COG closer → ↓ moment arm → ↓ abductor demand + ↓ JRF

## How it's asked

**"Arthritic hip — true?"** → **ratio of body-weight lever arm to abductor arm ↑** (TO)

**"Ratio of body-weight lever arm to abductor arm?"** → **2.5 : 1** (TO)

**"JRF after medializing cup (1800 N, arm 100 → 50 mm)?"** → **1200 N**

**"Contralateral cane reduces JRF by?"** → **reducing abductor pull**

**"High-offset stem — effect on JRF?"** → **↓ — lengthens abductor moment arm**

**"Trendelenburg gait compensates for weak?"** → **hip abductors**

**"Trendelenburg gait pattern?"** → **swing-side pelvis drops, ↑ adduction of stance hip**

**"Hip degrees of freedom?"** → **3 — flexion–extension, abduction–adduction, axial rotation**

**"JRF in single-leg stance?"** → **~3–6 × BW** (worked example ≈ 4 W)

**"Coefficient of friction, human joint vs metal-on-PE?"** → **0.002–0.04 vs 0.05–0.15**
