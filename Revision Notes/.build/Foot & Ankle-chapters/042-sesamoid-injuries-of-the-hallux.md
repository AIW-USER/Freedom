# Sesamoid Injuries of the Hallux

*Foot & Ankle · 42 / 48*

## 1 Basics

**Spectrum** — fracture (hyperextension + axial load) · dislocation · sprain (**turf toe**) · sesamoiditis (trauma / infection) · chondromalacia · OCD · FHB tendonitis

### Epidemiology

**Tibial (medial) sesamoid** — **more commonly injured**: **greater weight-bearing** + **larger** than lateral

**Stress fracture** — **commonest sesamoid problem** (OB) · **medial (tibial) sesamoid** · **mainly females** (OB)

**Bipartite sesamoid** — **10–25%** · **97% tibial** · **25% bilateral**

### Anatomy – intersesamoid ligament

**Held together by** — **intersesamoid ligament + plantar plate** (✗ intermetatarsal ligament — bases of 2nd–5th MTs, ✗ spring ligament — supports talar head / arch, ✗ plantar fascia, ✗ FHL — inserts distal phalanx)

**Plantar plate** — inserts **base of proximal phalanx** of hallux

**Embedded in** — tendons of **FHB** (short flexor); FHB attaches to **both** sesamoids · both lie **within 1st MTP capsule**, plantar to MT head (TO)

| Sesamoid | Muscle attached | Flexor-tendon complex (TO) |
|---|---|---|
| **Tibial (medial)** | **Abductor hallucis** | **Medial FHB head + abductor hallucis** → medial proximal phalanx |
| **Fibular (lateral)** | **Adductor hallucis** | **Lateral FHB head + adductor hallucis** → lateral proximal phalanx |

**Functions** — **absorb weight-bearing** pressure · ↓ **friction** at MT head · **protect FHL** (glides **between** sesamoids) · **fulcrum for FHB → ↑ MTP flexion power**

**Analogy** — **patella**: ↑ mechanical advantage of FHB

### Metatarsal load distribution (TO)

**Most weight during walking (TO)** — **2nd MT** · **standing → 1st MT** (TO) · foot anatomy note (Orthobullets): **1st MT 50% in gait** — read the options

**1st MTP joint (TO)** — carries **\~119% body weight** each step

**Sesamoid complex (TO)** — transmits **50% body weight** · **> 300% during push-off**

## 2 Mechanism

**Commonest** — **forced dorsiflexion (hyperextension) of 1st MTP** → plantar plate avulsion off **base of proximal phalanx** → **proximal migration of sesamoids**

**Fracture** — **hyperextension + axial loading**

**Chronic overuse** — dancers, players: **grade 3 1st MTP sprain → chronic sesamoiditis** (TO)

**Complete plantar plate tear (TO)** — may cause **sesamoid fracture** · **separation of bipartite sesamoid** · rarely **proximal migration**

**Bilateral sesamoiditis** — red flag → **Reiter's disease** (urethritis, conjunctivitis / iritis, IBD) · **psoriatic arthritis** · **seronegative RA**

## 3 Presentation

**Symptoms** — generalised big-toe pain · **worse in terminal stance** / hyperextension

**Exam** — possible **plantar-flexed MTP with cavus foot**

### Sesamoiditis in dancers (TO)

**Dancer, walks on lateral margin, painful great-toe MTP plantar flexion (TO)** — **sesamoiditis of first MTP joint** (✗ 1st MTP joint arthritis, ✗ stress fracture of 1st MT head, ✗ AVN of 1st MT head) (TO)

**Features (TO)** — **transfer of weight-bearing to lateral surface** · pain + restricted MTPJ movement · **painful push-off** of great toe

**DDx (TO)** — sesamoid stress fracture · AVN · osteochondral fracture · **hallux rigidus** (1st MTP arthritis)

**Treatment (TO)** — orthotics + immobilisation → sesamoidectomy if operative

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Study | Use / finding |
|---|---|
| **X-ray** | **AP + lateral foot** · **medial oblique (sesamoid view)** · **axial sesamoid view** · (TO) start with radiographs incl. **axial + lateromedial** sesamoid views |
| X-ray finding | **Proximal migration of sesamoids** → suspect **intrinsic-minus hallux** (plantar plate avulsion) |
| Early X-ray | **Often negative** in stress fracture (OB) |
| **Bone scan** | **Bipartite vs fracture** (↑ uptake = fracture) · **25–30% of asymptomatic** show ↑ uptake → **compare with uninjured side** |
| **Bone scan + CT** | **More reliable** for confirming **stress fracture** (OB) |
| **MRI** | **Confirms** diagnosis · indicated if radiographs **inconclusive** (TO) |

## 5 Management

```
Sesamoid injury
├─ First line → NSAIDs, ↓ WB, activity change, orthoses
│      (acute fracture: SLC with toe extension — controversial)
├─ Keratotic lesion, normal alignment → shave plantar half of sesamoid
├─ Plantar-flexed 1st ray → dorsiflexion osteotomy
└─ Failed nonop 3–12 mo → partial / complete sesamoidectomy
       └─ Nonunion, 2 large fragments, no osteonecrosis → ORIF / autologous bone graft
```

### Operative criteria

| Procedure | Indication |
|---|---|
| **Partial / complete sesamoidectomy** | **Failed nonop 3–12 months** (Orthobullets) · **≥ 3 months** (OB) |
| **Autologous bone grafting** | **Nonunion** or fracture |
| **ORIF of sesamoid** | **Two large fragments without osteonecrosis** (OB) |
| **Dorsiflexion osteotomy** | **Plantar-flexed first ray** with sesamoid injury |
| **Sesamoid shaving** | **Keratotic lesion** ↑ pressure on sesamoid · **contraindicated with plantar-flexed 1st MT** |
| ✗ **Both sesamoids** | **Never excise both** |

### Stress fracture/osteonecrosis – excision indications & outcomes

**Initial treatment** — almost always **accommodative orthosis** (OB)

**Medial sesamoid excision indicated** — **recalcitrant medial sesamoid stress fracture with fragmentation** (✗ with Lapidus, ✗ with 1st MTP arthrodesis — neither needs sesamoidectomy, ✗ acute fracture — nonop first, ✗ Morton's neuroma — neurectomy, bury in intrinsic muscle)

**Excision technique** — **proximal fragment** in some · **entire sesamoid** in others

**3-yr medial sesamoid pain, fragmentation + ↑ density, failed orthotics** — **medial sesamoid excision** (✗ 1st MTP arthrodesis — doesn't unload sesamoid, ✗ ORIF — only 2 large fragments without osteonecrosis, ✗ shoe / heel-height change — already tried, ✗ steroid injection — no long-term relief)

**Triathlete, failed nonop tibial sesamoid fracture — fastest reliable RTS** — **tibial sesamoidectomy** (✗ ORIF + autogenous calcaneal bone graft — valid but **slower** RTS, ✗ plantar plate reconstruction — turf toe, ✗ MTP arthroscopy + debridement — mild arthritis, ✗ chevron + Akin — bunion)

### Intractable plantar keratosis – sesamoid shaving

**IPK under 1st MT head, normal standing alignment, adequate passive DF** — **planing / excising superficial (plantar) half of medial sesamoid** → preserves function, ↓ pressure

**✗ IPK traps** — **complete medial sesamoid excision** (→ hallux valgus) · **FHL transfer** (no cock-up toe) · **dorsiflexion osteotomy** (no deformity) · **gastrocnemius recession** (passive DF adequate → more instability) · Keller bunionectomy

### Turf toe (TO)

**Definition (TO)** — **hyperextension injury to plantar plate + sesamoid complex** of hallux MTP · mechanism **hyperextension + axial loading**

**Plantar plate (TO)** — fibrocartilaginous: **thickened plantar capsule + medial and lateral flexor-tendon complexes** · 1st MT head → proximal phalanx · stabilisers allow **\~50° DF + 30° PF**

**Plantar plate insufficiency (TO)** — **primary pathology of MTP instability** → main focus of surgery

**Clinical (TO)** — pain, swelling, tenderness at MTP · **push-off painfully weak** · **negative plantar (palmar) grip** · **positive vertical Lachman / drawer test**

**Treatment (TO)** — **mostly nonop: ice, rest, taping, stiff-sole shoe / rocker-bottom sole** · **plantar plate repair only in a few** → "treatment is plantar plate repair" is the **false** statement (TO)

**Surgery indications (TO)** — failed nonop · **retraction or fracture of sesamoids with diastasis** · **loose fragments** in joint · **hallux deformity**

### Approaches – sesamoidectomy

| Sesamoid | Approach | Nerve at risk |
|---|---|---|
| **Tibial (medial)** | **Medial-plantar**, at junction of **glabrous skin** | **Proper branch of medial plantar nerve** = **plantar-medial cutaneous nerve of hallux** (directly under incision) |
| **Fibular (lateral)** | **Plantar** | **Proper branch to lateral hallux** + **1st common digital branch to 1st web space** |

**Medial sesamoidectomy — most vulnerable** — **plantar-medial cutaneous nerve** → painful neuroma, shoe-wear problems (✗ abductor hallucis tendon — **dorsal** to incision, ✗ intermetatarsal ligament, ✗ dorsomedial cutaneous nerve, ✗ crista)

## 6 Complications

### Complications of sesamoidectomy – cock-up toe, hallux malalignment & nerve injury

| Excision | Complication |
|---|---|
| **Both sesamoids** | **Cock-up toe deformity** (commonest) |
| **Tibial (medial)** | **Hallux valgus** |
| **Fibular (lateral)** | **Hallux varus** |
| Either | Pain, **nerve injury (neuroma)**, infection, deformity |

**Cock-up toe** — ↓ **FHB moment arm** / FHB weakening → **MTP extension** + relative flexor overpull → **IP flexion** · prevent by **meticulous FHB repair**; **avoid excising both**

**Hallux valgus after tibial sesamoidectomy** — **commonest reported deformity** · from **inadequate medial capsular repair / reefing** (OB)

**HV 1 yr after "removing a painful bone"** — **medial (tibial) sesamoidectomy** (✗ accessory navicular excision, ✗ dorsal cheilectomy, ✗ lateral sesamoidectomy → varus, ✗ os vesalianum excision)

## X vs Y

| | **Tibial (medial) sesamoid** | **Fibular (lateral) sesamoid** |
|---|---|---|
| Injury frequency | **More common** (larger, ↑ WB) | Less |
| Bipartite | **97%** | Rare |
| Muscle | Abductor hallucis | Adductor hallucis |
| Approach | Medial-plantar | Plantar |
| Excision → | **Hallux valgus** | **Hallux varus** |

| | **Bipartite sesamoid** | **Sesamoid fracture** |
|---|---|---|
| Bone scan | No ↑ uptake vs other side | **↑ uptake** |
| Laterality | 25% bilateral | Usually unilateral |

| | **Sesamoiditis (TO)** | **Turf toe (TO)** |
|---|---|---|
| Cause | **Chronic overuse** (dancers) | **Acute hyperextension + axial load** |
| Sign | Walks on **lateral margin**, painful push-off | **+ drawer / vertical Lachman**, negative plantar grip |
| Treatment | Orthotics, immobilise → sesamoidectomy | **Nonop** (taping, stiff sole) · repair in few |
