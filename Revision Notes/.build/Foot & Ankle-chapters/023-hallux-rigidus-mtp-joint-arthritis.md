# Hallux Rigidus (MTP joint arthritis)

*Foot & Ankle · 23 / 48*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Commonest site of OA in the foot** — 1st MTP (Orthobullets) · **2nd commonest great-toe pathology after hallux valgus** (OB)

**Incidence** — **2.5% of > 50-yr-olds** · **F : M = 2 : 1** · peak **5th–6th decade**

**Risk factors** — **trauma to 1st MTP** (**\~80%** of unilateral cases; e.g. old **turf toe**) · high-impact sport

**Causes of 1st MTP arthritis (OB)** — **trauma**, **hallux valgus**, **elevated 1st metatarsal**

### Etiology & associations

**Primary cause** — **unknown** · acute trauma + repetitive microtrauma predispose

**Pathoanatomy** — osteophytes + cartilage loss **start dorsally** → whole joint later

**Anatomic variants** — metatarsus adductus · MT head morphology · **long 1st MT** · hallux valgus / varus

**Associated** — **sesamoid arthritis** · gout · RA · seronegative arthropathies

### Anatomy & biomechanics

**1st MT** — **shortest and widest**

**Medial branch of medial dorsal cutaneous nerve** — **overlies 1st MTP** → irritated by **dorsal osteophytes** · plantar-medial hallucal nerve also supplies joint

**Load** — 1st MTP carries **\~120% body weight** each step

**Running** — needs **60° dorsiflexion**

## 2 Mechanism

### Checkrein deformity (TO)

**Cause (TO)** — **fracture of distal third of tibia** → **FHL adheres to fracture callus** → bowstring to hallux insertion (✗ FHL tear, ✗ IP joint fracture, ✗ **FHL tethering at 1st MTP**) (TO)

**Sign (TO)** — **ankle dorsiflexion → great toe flexes** · **ankle plantarflexion → hallux IP extends** (TO)

## 3 Presentation

**Pain** — 1st ray / 1st MTP · **worse at push-off (toe-off / heel-rise)** · swelling · gait abnormality · difficulty with shoes

**Inspection** — **dorsal prominence** (osteophyte) · shoe irritation + redness · severe → **hyperextension deformity**

**Motion** — **↓ dorsiflexion** · **pain at terminal dorsiflexion** early → **pain through whole arc** as it progresses

**Neuro** — **dorsomedial paraesthesia** / ↓ sensation distal medial dorsal foot (**medial dorsal cutaneous nerve**, ± Tinel)

**Grind test +** — **severe disease, central chondral wear**

### Associations & sequelae (transfer metatarsalgia)

**Hallux rigidus leads to** — **restricted dorsiflexion** → forefoot **internal rotation** → **↓ push-off strength** → **inverted gait** → **transfer metatarsalgia** (✗ **flexor hallucis longus (FHL) rupture**, ✗ everted gait, ✗ ↑ push-off, ✗ Haglund deformity)

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Study | Finding |
|---|---|
| **Weight-bearing AP, lateral, sesamoid, oblique** | **Dorsal osteophytes** · **joint space narrowing** · **subchondral sclerosis + cysts** |
| **MRI** | Rarely needed · **osteochondral lesion with normal X-ray** · characterises mild OA |

**Radiographic progression** — may **not correlate** with symptoms

### Coughlin & Shurnas classification

| Grade | Exam | Dorsiflexion | Radiograph | X-ray |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **0** | **Stiffness**, no pain | **40–60°** (10–20% lost) | **Normal** | — |
| **1** | **Mild pain at extremes** | **30–40°** (20–50% lost) | **Mild dorsal osteophyte**, normal joint space | ![Grade 1](../Orthobullets/attachments/ob7009-grade-1-ap.jpg) |
| **2** | **Moderate pain at extremes**, more constant | **10–30°** (50–75% lost) | **Moderate osteophyte**, **< 50% JSN** | ![Grade 2](../Orthobullets/attachments/ob7009-grade-2-ap.jpg) |
| **3** | Significant stiffness, near-constant pain at extremes, **no mid-range pain** | **≤ 10°** (75–100% lost) | **Severe osteophyte**, **> 50% JSN** | ![Grade 3](../Orthobullets/attachments/ob7009-grade-3-ap.jpg) |
| **4** | As grade 3 **+ pain at mid-range** | As grade 3 | As grade 3 | ![Grade 4](../Orthobullets/attachments/ob7009-grade-4-ap.jpg) |

**Scenario → grade (OB)** — stiffness + ↓ push-off, **no pain** = **0** · dorsal osteophyte, no stiffness, **pain at terminal DF** = **1** · moderate pain at extremes + mild osteophyte + **< 50% JSN** = **2** · JSN + stiffness + pain at end-range = **3** · large osteophyte + stiffness + **mid-range pain** = **3** (OB) · severe JSN + **pain throughout** = **4**

**Other staging (OB)** — "**Stage III** = end-stage, cartilage lost from **phalanx + metatarsal**" · "**Stage 4** = severe dorsal osteophyte + **> 50% JSN**" — read the stem

## 5 Management

```
Hallux rigidus
├─ Grade 0–1 → NSAIDs, activity mod, injection, Morton's extension
├─ Grade 1–2, pain only at terminal DF → dorsal cheilectomy
│      └─ < 30–40° DF after cheilectomy / runner → + Moberg
├─ Grade 3–4, mid-range pain, + grind → MTP arthrodesis
└─ Elderly, low demand → Keller resection arthroplasty
```

```
Failed 1st MTP arthroplasty
├─ Silastic synovitis, great-toe pain only → implant removal + synovectomy
├─ Infection → antibiotic spacer → staged arthrodesis
└─ Loosening / bone loss / shortening + lesser metatarsalgia → removal + structural graft + arthrodesis
```

### Operative criteria

| Grade / situation | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Grade 0** (motion loss, no X-ray change) | Nonop · **plantar release** (OB) |
| **Grade 0–1** | **Nonop** (NSAIDs, injections, **Morton's extension**) · **up to 55%** succeed |
| **Grade 1–2** · select grade 3 with pain only at terminal DF | **Dorsal cheilectomy** (± synovectomy, debridement) |
| Cheilectomy gives **< 30–40° DF** · **runner** | **+ Moberg** dorsal closing wedge osteotomy |
| **Grade 3–4** · **mid-range pain** · **+ grind** · failed cheilectomy · grade 3 with **< 50% MT head cartilage** left | **MTP arthrodesis** (gold standard) |
| Grade 3–4, wishes to keep motion | Prosthetic / **interpositional arthroplasty** |
| **Elderly, low demand**, fast rehab | **Keller resection arthroplasty** |
| Mild disease + **intra-articular loose bodies** | Dorsal synovectomy + debridement |

### Nonoperative care – Morton's extension & carbon plate

**Morton's extension** — stiff insert with rigid extension **under 1st ray, past the 1st MTP** → **limits 1st MTP dorsiflexion** at push-off · ↓ plantar loading across MTP · **mainstay / first line for hallux rigidus** (✗ hallux valgus, ✗ hallux varus, ✗ Morton's neuroma, ✗ spring ligament, ✗ 2nd MTP synovitis, ✗ Jones, ✗ hammer / mallet toe — high toe box, ✗ midfoot arthritis, ✗ Freiberg)

**Limits** — **hallux MTP dorsiflexion** (✗ midfoot collapse, ✗ hindfoot varus, ✗ MTP plantarflexion, ✗ adduction)

**Poor surgical candidate, + grind** — **Morton's carbon fiber extension** (✗ **UCBL** + ✗ **Arizona brace** — flexible pes planovalgus, ✗ **medial flare** — ankle instability, ✗ **negative heel rocker** — ↑ PF in terminal stance)

**Other nonop** — NSAIDs · avoid excessive great-toe DF · **stiff-sole shoe** / carbon fiber shank · toe-box stretching · rocker-bottom shoes · molded inserts

**Injections** — **corticosteroid + sodium hyaluronate** → ↓ pain at **4 + 8 wk** in low-grade disease

**✗ Not first line (OB)** — CAM boot, AFO, hard-soled shoes · **PT aggravates** · extending Morton's to 2nd MT adds nothing

### Cheilectomy ± Moberg osteotomy – indications

**Ideal candidate** — **pain at terminal DF only** + **shoe-wear irritation** from dorsal prominence · **grade 1–2** · **no mid-range pain**

**Contraindications** — **mid-range pain** on passive motion · **+ grind test** · **sesamoid arthritis** · comorbidities (poor DM control, PVD)

**Technique** — dorsal approach · **complete synovectomy** · resect dorsal osteophyte + **up to 30% dorsal MT head** (Orthobullets) · **25%** (OB) · **> 30% → subluxation / instability**

**Goal** — **70–90% of dorsiflexion** intra-op (Orthobullets) · **70–90° DF** (OB)

**Outcomes** — grade 1–2: **97% excellent**, **92% pain relief at 10 yr** · higher grades: **25–56% → arthrodesis** · progression needing fusion **7–9% within 10 yr**

**Grade 2, 35° arc, failed Morton's** — **cheilectomy + joint debridement** (✗ observation, ✗ sesamoidectomy, ✗ Keller, ✗ fusion)

**Carpenter, terminal-DF pain, mild OA** — **cheilectomy** (✗ Lapidus, ✗ fusion, ✗ resurfacing, ✗ chevron) · cheilectomy ≠ Helal / Weil / chevron osteotomy

**Moberg** — **dorsal closing wedge osteotomy of proximal phalanx** → ↑ DF by sacrificing plantarflexion arc · with cheilectomy: **88% satisfaction at 2 yr**

**Moberg indications** — **runners** (need 60°) · cheilectomy gives **< 30–40° DF** (Orthobullets) · **< 30°** (OB) · failed cheilectomy

**Runner, 30% resected, only 20° DF** — **Moberg procedure** (✗ close + WBAT, ✗ FDL → EDL, ✗ Keller — high demand, ✗ fusion)

**Ballet dancer, stage 2** — **cheilectomy, débridement ± Moberg** (✗ **fusion = career-ending**, ✗ Keller, ✗ uni- / bipolar replacement)

### MTP arthrodesis – indications & optimal position

**Indications** — **standard of care / gold standard** for **grade 3–4** · **commonest procedure** · **pain throughout MTP motion** · failed cheilectomy · failed arthroplasty

**Best long-term function + pain relief** — **arthrodesis** · restores **WB + propulsion of 1st ray** (✗ synovectomy, ✗ cheilectomy, ✗ hemiarthroplasty, ✗ interposition — worse long-term)

**Failed cheilectomy at 1 yr** — **1st MTP arthrodesis** (✗ Moberg — won't relieve pain)

**Grade 4, young active golfer** — **great-toe MTP fusion** (✗ Keller — not for young / active, ✗ bipolar replacement, ✗ cheilectomy, ✗ fascia lata interposition)

**Stage 3, + grind, 10° DF** — **arthrodesis** (✗ cheilectomy, ✗ Moberg, ✗ Keller — ↓ propulsion + transfer metatarsalgia, ✗ resurfacing hemiarthroplasty — not with phalangeal cartilage loss)

**Fixation** — wires, pins, lag screws, crossed screws, plates · **dorsal plate + compression screw = strongest** · medial→lateral compression screw common

| Position | Orthobullets | OB |
|---|---|---|
| **Valgus** (vs MT shaft) | **5°** | **10–15°** · **5–10°** · **15°** — read the options |
| **Dorsiflexion** (vs floor) | **15°** | **10–15°** · **10°** |
| Rotation | — | **Neutral** |
| **Footplate clearance** | **4–8 mm** toe tip off plate | **\~5 mm** |

**Footplate test** — flat sterile tray, ankle neutral, simulates WB · **5 mm clearance → irrigate and close** (✗ Moberg, ✗ superior capsulotomy, ✗ add screw, ✗ revise plate)

![Intra-op footplate test – toe tip clears plate](../MCQ/attachments/q212994-b-screen-shot-2019-08-07-at-7-20-18-am.jpg)

**Outcomes** — fusion **90–100%** (Orthobullets) · union **\~94%** (OB) · **95–96% satisfaction** · return: **hiking 92%, golf 80%, jogging 75%, tennis 75%**

**Tennis player, stage 4, failed orthosis** — **arthrodesis** (✗ cheilectomy + Moberg — stage 1–2, ✗ interpositional — not for athletes, ✗ hemi / total arthroplasty — subsidence, radiolucency)

**Mid-range pain, 10° DF, eroded joint intra-op** — **arthrodesis** (✗ cheilectomy, ✗ bunionectomy + cheilectomy, ✗ resection, ✗ interposition)

### Alternatives – synthetic cartilage implant & Keller resection arthroplasty

**Keller** — **resect base of proximal phalanx** ± capsular interposition · **elderly, low demand → fastest rehab** · interposition: **90% pain improvement, 4% reoperation at 4.5 yr**

**80-yo minimally ambulatory, plantar-flexed toe** — **Keller + osteophyte removal** (✗ silicone implant, ✗ Moberg, ✗ cheilectomy, ✗ arthrodesis)

**Keller contraindications** — **rigid hyperextension deformity** · **sesamoid arthritis** · young active (instability)

**Keller risks** — **cock-up (hyperextension)** · **weak push-off** · **transfer metatarsalgia** (↓ with capsular interposition) · gross MTP instability

**Synthetic cartilage interposition** — keeps motion · **equivalent pain reduction + function vs arthrodesis** · **longer time to max pain relief** · **no prolonged WB restriction** · **not contraindicated in grade 2** · ✗ if **HV / varus > 20°**

**Interpositional arthroplasty (capsule / EHB)** — younger **< 60 yr** late-stage, **strongly opposed to fusion** · limited toe function → not for athletes

### Prosthetic arthroplasty

**Indication** — **grade 3–4** wishing to **preserve motion** · indications unclear

**Contraindications** — **active infection** · **insufficient bone stock** · **sesamoid arthritis**

**Hemiarthroplasty** — unipolar MT head or phalanx base · **keeps 1st ray length** · **easier conversion to fusion** · hemi = total for function, complications, satisfaction

**Generations** — 1st–2nd **silastic** (silicone) · 3rd **press-fit metal** · 4th **threaded-stem metal**

**Silastic** — good short-term, **\~60% failure** · **silicone wear, synovitis, osteolysis** · **abandoned**

**Current implants** — **80–90% survival at \~5 yr** · later **loosening + complex revision with bone loss** · **worse than primary fusion**; fusion after failed arthroplasty worse than primary fusion

## 6 Complications

| Complication | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Arthritis progression after cheilectomy** | **7–9% → fusion within 10 yr** |
| **Instability** | **Excessive cheilectomy resection** (> 30%) |
| **Malunion (fusion)** | **16%** → transfer metatarsalgia, **hallux IP arthritis**, poor push-off |
| **Nonunion (fusion)** | **5–10%** · **< 40% symptomatic** → revision fusion + bone graft |
| **Hallux IP OA** | **15%** after MTP fusion · usually asymptomatic · ↑ with **excess valgus** |
| **Cock-up deformity** | **Keller** → treat with **1st MTP arthrodesis** |
| **Failed arthroplasty** | Silicone / early implants; loosening, osteolysis, technical error |
| Infection | **< 2%** |
| Symptomatic hardware | — |

### Arthrodesis malposition complications

| Fused position | Result |
|---|---|
| **Excess dorsiflexion** | Pain at **toe tip, IP joint, under 1st MT head** · **sesamoid / plantar pain** · **dorsal shoe irritation** |
| **Excess plantarflexion** (too straight) | **↑ pressure at toe tip** · can't roll over → **walks on lateral foot**, **lateral calluses**, lateral sole wear (supinates) |
| **Excess valgus** | **Pressure on 2nd toe** → **soft corn / ulcer** · **IP joint degeneration** |
| **Varus** | **Shoe-wear problems** (narrow toe box) · **medial hallux irritation** (✗ sesamoid pain, ✗ rollover problem) |
| **Shortened** | **Transfer metatarsalgia** (2nd MT) + **varus of 2nd toe** (lost medial support) · IP degeneration |

**2nd MTP plantar pain + new 2nd-toe deformity after fusion** — **fused shortened** (✗ varus, ✗ valgus, ✗ plantarflexion, ✗ dorsiflexion)

**Lateral foot pain, lateral calluses, stands on outside of foot** — **fused in plantarflexion**

**Sesamoid / plantar 1st MT head pain, fixed MTP** — **first MTPJ arthrodesis in 5 degrees of valgus** (relation to metatarsal shaft), **30 degrees of dorsiflexion** (relation to floor) → ↑ plantar pressure under 1st MT head (✗ cheilectomy 10% or 30%, ✗ 10° valgus / 15° DF — ideal, ✗ 5° varus)

### Dorsiflexion malunion after prior surgery

**Dorsal great-toe irritation in closed shoes despite hardware removal** — **dorsiflexion malunion** · functional, but shoe problems (✗ neuropathy, ✗ **nonunion** — activity pain + swelling, relieved by stiff shoes, ✗ **transfer metatarsalgia** — plantar 2nd MT head pain, ✗ **plantarflexion malunion** — gait difficulty, walks on lateral foot)

### Failed implant/hemiarthroplasty – removal & bone-block arthrodesis

**Commonest failure modes** — **aseptic loosening**, **subsidence**, **infection**

**Silastic, 15 yr, synovitis, normal ESR / CRP / WBC, erythema settles on elevation** — **implant removal + synovectomy** (✗ steroid injection, ✗ orthosis, ✗ I&D + IV antibiotics, ✗ revision silastic)

**Silastic long-term complication complicating revision fusion** — **osteolysis** (✗ recurrent deformity, ✗ infection — not ↑, ✗ chronic synovitis — less impact, ✗ **floating toe** — Weil osteotomy) · also loss of fixation, pathologic fracture

**Lucency around phalangeal component, pain since return to labour, normal labs** — **mechanical failure** (✗ septic arthritis, ✗ periprosthetic fracture, ✗ **aseptic loosening from PE debris** — needs prior stable period, ✗ transfer metatarsalgia)

**Failed arthroplasty + lesser-toe metatarsalgia + callosities, negative aspirate** — **implant removal, synovectomy, structural bone graft, arthrodesis** (✗ long-stem revision, ✗ silastic conversion, ✗ spacer + reimplant — no infection, ✗ removal + synovectomy alone — weak push-off)

**Failed silastic, osteolysis, metatarsalgia** — **implant removal + structural bone graft + MTP fusion** (✗ Morton's extension, ✗ in situ fusion, ✗ Keller — worsens metatarsalgia, ✗ titanium total toe)

**Failed hemiarthroplasty with bone loss** — **bone-block arthrodesis** (✗ **in situ arthrodesis** — short 1st ray → transfer metatarsalgia, ✗ Keller, ✗ Moberg, ✗ silicone)

**Failed hemiarthroplasty + shortening + cock-up** — **arthrodesis with structural bone graft** (✗ tenotomy — treats consequence not cause, ✗ osteotomy, ✗ resection, ✗ **particulate graft** — minimal bone loss only)

**Severe 1st MT shortening after arthroplasty** — **distraction bone block arthrodesis** (✗ antibiotic spacer, ✗ shortening arthrodesis, ✗ **ex-fix lengthening** — brachymetatarsia, ✗ revision arthroplasty)

| Structural bone graft indication |
|---|
| **1st MT shortening not rebalanced by lesser MT osteotomy** (usually **> 5 mm**) — commonest after **failed arthroplasty** |
| **Proximal phalanx bone loss** — not enough bone for fixation without violating IP joint |
| **Shortening with loss of medial support of 2nd toe** → **2nd MTP varus** |

**Graft** — **tricortical iliac crest** (allograft commonest, Orthobullets) · autograft or allograft (OB)
