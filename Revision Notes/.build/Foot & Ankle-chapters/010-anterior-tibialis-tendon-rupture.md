# Anterior Tibialis Tendon Rupture

*Foot & Ankle · 10 / 48*

## 1 Basics

### Anatomy

**Tibialis anterior** — **primary ankle dorsiflexor (80%)** · inserts **medial cuneiform + base of 1st MT** · also inverts foot with dorsiflexion

**Secondary dorsiflexors** — **EHL + EDL** → mask the rupture → **delayed diagnosis common**

**Site** — rupture **at level of ankle joint**, proximal stump retracts (\~3 cm)

### Epidemiology & risk factors

**Attritional (atraumatic)** — **older patients**, men **> 45 yr** · eccentric load against **plantarflexed ankle** (missed step) · degenerative

**Traumatic** — **laceration** or closed rupture · strong eccentric contraction in **young**

**Risk factors** — **older age** · **diabetes** · **fluoroquinolones** · **local steroid injection** · **inflammatory arthritis** · gout

## 3 Presentation

### Diagnosis – foot drop & attritional rupture

**Acute** — **"pop"** → painful anterior ankle swelling · weak dorsiflexion

**Chronic** — **painless** · difficulty clearing foot · minimal swelling

**Classic triad** — **pseudotumour (painless mass) at anteromedial ankle** = retracted stump · **loss of TA contour** (not palpable on resisted DF) · **weak DF + hyperextension of all toes** (EHL / EDL over-firing)

**Eversion on dorsiflexion** — unopposed EHL + EDL (normal foot **inverts** with DF via TA)

**Gait** — **steppage gait** (↑ hip flexion in swing) · **foot slap** after heel strike · toe dragging

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Study | Finding / role |
|---|---|
| **X-ray** (3 views foot + ankle) | Exclude osseous injury · usually unremarkable |
| **CT** | **Not indicated** |
| **MRI** | **Complete vs partial tear** · sagittal **"empty sheath"** · ✗ does not decide need for interposition graft |

![Sagittal MRI — empty tibialis anterior sheath](../MCQ/attachments/q3821-a-tib-ant-rupture.jpg)

### Differential diagnosis

| Condition | Distinguishing feature |
|---|---|
| **Attritional TA rupture** | **EHL + EDL normal**, painless anterior mass, steppage gait, full passive ROM |
| **L4 radiculopathy / lumbar stenosis** | **Tendon palpable, no ankle mass**, dermatomal sensory loss (medial leg), weakness of several groups incl. quadriceps, +ve lumbar MRI |
| **Common peroneal nerve palsy** | **EHL + EDL also weak**, sensory loss, compression history |
| **Deep peroneal nerve palsy** | Toe extensors weak (✗ in isolated TA rupture) |
| Acute TA laceration | Clear sharp injury, **abrupt** onset (✗ progressive over months) |
| Anterior ankle impingement | **No** DF weakness, steppage gait or mass |

## 5 Management

```
Tibialis anterior rupture
├─ Low-demand / elderly → AFO
├─ Partial rupture → CAST
├─ Acute < 6 wk, active → DIRECT REPAIR
│      (attempt up to 3 months · gastroc recession if < 5° DF knee extended)
└─ Chronic > 6 wk → RECONSTRUCTION
       └─ gap → INTERPOSITION GRAFT (plantaris, hamstring) / EHL transfer
```

### Operative criteria

| Option | Indication |
|---|---|
| **AFO** | **Low-demand** patient |
| **Casting** | **Partial rupture** |
| **Direct (primary) repair** | **Acute < 6 wk**, active high-demand; may be attempted **up to 3 months** |
| **Gastrocnemius recession** (before tensioning) | **< 5° passive DF with knee extended** |
| **Reconstruction** | **Chronic > 6 wk** |
| **PTT transfer** | **Permanent foot drop** only |

**Surgery even late** — functional DF + gait improved in **18/19** (early 3 d–6 wk and late 7 wk–5 yr repairs) · traumatic ruptures do better with surgery regardless of timing · atraumatic presenting late → bracing acceptable

### Treatment – repair or reconstruction & expected outcome

**Direct repair** — open: through laceration · closed: longitudinal incision over defect · hemostat under **extensor retinaculum** to retrieve proximal stump · **non-absorbable Krackow / Bunnell / Kessler** · oversew frayed ends · avulsion → **suture anchors / bone tunnels**

**Sliding graft** — **half-width of TA turned down** · secured to medial cuneiform / dorsal navicular distal to retinaculum

**Free graft** — **autograft (hamstring, plantaris)** or allograft · also Achilles, EDL, EHB interposition

**EHL transfer / tenodesis** — **distal EHL → EHB** · **proximal EHL stump** used as graft to TA insertion · TA tensioned then sutured to proximal EHL

**Augment with** — **extensor tendons (EHL, EDL)** (✗ **FHL** — out of phase, too short; FHL for chronic Achilles)

**Expected outcome** — **restores active DF with some residual weakness + improved activity level** · ↑ AOFAS, high satisfaction · peak torque **72–84%** of other side

**Commonest long-term residual** — **decreased dorsiflexion strength** (+ ↓ inversion strength) (✗ persistent foot drop / AFO — chronic missed or nonop, ✗ numbness — missed superficial nerve laceration, ✗ persistent pain)

**✗ Wrong treatments** — **VY advancement / turndown flap** (Achilles rupture with gap) · **SPR repair + groove deepening** (peroneal instability) · AFO in active patient (permanent gait disturbance) · chest CT + skeletal survey + oncology biopsy of the "mass" · MRI of proximal tibiofibular joint for ganglion + peroneal EMG · PTT transfer

## 6 Complications

**List** — failure of repair / reconstruction · **weakness of dorsiflexion** · adhesions · neuroma

**Prognosis** — good with treatment

## X vs Y

| | **TA rupture** | **Peroneal nerve palsy** | **L4 radiculopathy** |
|---|---|---|---|
| EHL / EDL | **Normal** | **Weak** | Variable |
| Anterior mass | **Yes (pseudotumour)** | No | No |
| TA tendon palpable | **No** | Yes | **Yes** |
| Sensation | Normal | Sensory abnormality | Dermatomal |

| | **Acute (< 6 wk)** | **Chronic (> 6 wk)** |
|---|---|---|
| Treatment | **Primary repair ± gastroc recession** | **Reconstruction** — interposition graft, sliding graft, EHL transfer |

## How it's asked

**"Elderly soccer injury, video of left anteromedial mass + toe hyperextension — next?"** → **left tendon repair and possible graft augmentation** (check the side)

**"67-yo diabetic, steroid injections, painless anterior mass, EHL / EDL normal?"** → **attritional rupture of the tibialis anterior tendon**

**"Chronic rupture 7 months, 15° DF knee extended — next?"** → **surgical reconstruction with plantaris tendon interposition augmentation**

**"Surgery after acute laceration will most likely?"** → **restore active dorsiflexion with some residual weakness and improve activity level**

**"Most common long-term residual after repair?"** → **decreased dorsiflexion strength**

**"70-yo active, acute rupture — associated procedure?"** → **primary repair with possible gastrocnemius recession**

**"When add gastroc recession?"** → **< 5° passive DF with knee extended**

**"Graft never used for TA?"** → **FHL**
