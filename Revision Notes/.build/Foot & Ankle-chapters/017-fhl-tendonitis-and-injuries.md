# FHL Tendonitis & Injuries

*Foot & Ankle · 17 / 48*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Site** — **posterior ankle** (behind talus, fibro-osseous tunnel) · great toe

**Risk factor** — **excessive / maximal plantarflexion** → **ballet dancers en pointe / demi-pointe**, **gymnasts**

**Dancers** — FHL tendinitis = **common cause of posteromedial (medial-sided) ankle pain** · dancers **endure symptoms longer** before seeking care than non-dancers

**Associated conditions** — **posterior ankle impingement (PAIS)** · **os trigonum** (posterolateral tubercle)

### Diagnosis in dancers & FHL anatomy

**Origin** — **inferior 2/3 of posterior surface of fibula + lower part of interosseous membrane**

**Course** — between **posteromedial + posterolateral tubercles of talus** in a **fibro-osseous tunnel** → **beneath sustentaculum tali** → crosses **dorsal to FDL at knot of Henry** (FHL "higher", FDL "down") → stays dorsal to FDL + NV bundle · multiple FHL–FDL connections

**Insertion** — plantar base of **distal phalanx of great toe**

**Action** — primary: **plantarflexion of hallux IP + MTP** · secondary: ankle plantarflexion

**Os trigonum link** — FHL runs **directly medial to lateral tubercle of posterior talar process** → coexistent tendinopathy (✗ peroneus brevis / longus, ✗ FDL, ✗ tibialis posterior — not as close)

**Os trigonum** — unfused **secondary ossification centre of lateral tubercle of posterior talar process** · or united **stress fracture** of that tubercle

| Origin (as in the options) | Muscle |
|---|---|
| **Inferior 2/3 posterior fibula + lower interosseous membrane** | **FHL** ✓ |
| Anterior fibula + adjacent interosseous membrane | EHL ✗ |
| Cuboid + lateral cuneiform | FHB ✗ (doesn't cross ankle) |
| Fibular head + upper 1/2–2/3 lateral fibula + septa | Peroneus longus ✗ |
| Inferior 2/3 lateral fibula + septa | Peroneus brevis ✗ (subfibular pain with eversion + dorsiflexion) |

## 2 Mechanism

**Mechanism** — activities with **maximal plantarflexion** → **impingement of FHL** along posterior ankle → tendinitis → even **rupture**

**Pathoanatomy** — posterior to talus, **within fibro-osseous tunnel** (posteromedial ankle) · or secondary to **os trigonum**

**Chronic** — **nodule formation → triggering** of great toe

**PAIS** — **scarring + tendinopathy of FHL** · Achilles in vicinity but **remains normal**

**FHL laceration** — direct acute trauma · **acute laceration = most common form of injury**

## 3 Presentation

**Pain** — **posteromedial ankle** (✗ posterolateral = os trigonum) · worse **en pointe / demi-pointe**

**Hallux** — **triggering**, **locking**, **clicking / catching**, **snapping behind medial malleolus** with great-toe motion

**Key sign** — **painful crepitus of tendon with passive motion of great toe** (passive hallux motion moves inflamed FHL at ankle)

**Other signs** — pain with **resisted flexion of hallux IP** (resisted great-toe plantarflexion) · **↓ passive extension of great toe** · posteromedial tenderness, fullness, crepitus · **no tenderness at 1st MT head**

**Forced ankle plantarflexion** — painful in FHL tendinitis (Orthobullets, OB PAIS stem) · **no pain** on forced passive PF in a classic FHL stem; forced PF pain = **posterior impingement** (OB) — read the options

**Tib post working** — **heel inverts on toe rise** → excludes PTT

**Laceration** — **loss of active IP joint flexion**

| Exam finding | Diagnosis |
|---|---|
| **Painful crepitus with passive great-toe motion** | **FHL tendinitis** ✓ |
| Pain on percussion of posterior tibial nerve | Tarsal tunnel ✗ |
| Posterior ankle pain with forced passive plantarflexion | Posterior impingement ✗ |
| Medial pain with resisted inversion | Posterior tibial tendinitis ✗ |
| Foot drop, weak dorsiflexion | Tibialis anterior rupture ✗ |

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Study | Finding |
|---|---|
| **MRI (axial T2)** | **Fluid around FHL tendon at level of ankle joint** = **tenosynovitis of FHL sheath** as it wraps posteromedial ankle · intrasubstance signal |
| **MRI – laceration** | **Retracted tendon ends** · fluid in FHL sheath |
| X-ray / bone scan / MRI in ballet student | **Os trigonum** → look for associated **FHL** pathology |

## 5 Management

```
FHL tendinitis
├─ First line → rest, ice, NSAIDs, activity modification, arch support, PT
│      (no steroid injection – posterior tibial nerve close)
├─ Fails nonop (e.g. 6 mo) → release FHL from fibro-osseous tunnel at ankle
│      (arthroscopic or open posteromedial; tenosynovectomy ± repair)
└─ Acute laceration of FHL + FHB → acute surgical repair
```

### Operative criteria

| Indication | Procedure |
|---|---|
| **Recalcitrant symptoms** · athletes with symptoms despite rest + nonop | **Release of FHL from fibro-osseous tunnel**, tenosynovectomy, ± tendon repair |
| **Lacerations of both FHL and FHB** | **Acute surgical repair** |

### Treatment – FHL decompression

**Next step after failed 6 mo nonop** — **decompression of FHL at the level of the ankle** (✗ at metatarsal — ankle level commoner than distal tarsal tunnel in midfoot · ✗ peroneal decompression — lateral pain · ✗ 1st MT osteotomy — hallux valgus · ✗ gastrocnemius recession — isolated gastroc equinus)

**Steroid injection** — **contraindicated** (posterior tibial nerve close by)

**Compression** — contracture of **medial retinaculum** over FHL → posteromedial pain

**Outcomes** — **> 90% good / excellent in dancers** · good in dancers + non-dancers · professional dancers do better than amateurs (Hamilton)

## X vs Y

| | **FHL tendinitis** | **Os trigonum / posterior impingement** |
|---|---|---|
| Pain | **Posteromedial** | **Posterolateral** |
| Hallux motion | **Painful, clicking, triggering, crepitus** | Not painful |
| Forced passive PF | ± (see conflict above) | **Reproduces posterior pain** |
| History | Insidious, en pointe | After ankle sprain / PF injury (os trigonum fracture) |

| Posterior / medial ankle structure | Why ✗ in a dancer's PAIS stem |
|---|---|
| **FHL** | ✓ impinged in fibro-osseous tunnel |
| Tibialis posterior | Heel inverts on toe rise = functioning |
| Achilles | In vicinity, remains normal |
| EHL, tibialis anterior | Opposite (dorsal) side |
| Tibial nerve | Tarsal tunnel (Tinel) |

## How it's asked

**"Dancer, posteromedial pain in demi-pointe, fluid around FHL on MRI — exam finding?"** → **painful crepitus with passive great-toe motion** (✗ tibial nerve percussion, ✗ forced passive PF, ✗ resisted inversion, ✗ foot drop)

**"Ballet dancer, snapping behind medial malleolus + great-toe triggering — impinged structure?"** → **flexor hallucis longus tendon** (✗ PTT, ✗ tibial nerve)

**"Ballet student, os trigonum on X-ray / bone scan / MRI — tendon most often involved?"** → **flexor hallucis longus**

**"En pointe, posteromedial tenderness, no pain on forced PF, pain with hallux motion?"** → **FHL tendinitis** (✗ painful os trigonum, ✗ knot of Henry stricture, ✗ FDL tendinitis)

**"En pointe ballerina, posteromedial crepitus, heel inverts on toe rise — structure?"** → **flexor hallucis longus tendon** (✗ tib post, ✗ Achilles)

**"Origin of the implicated muscle (fluid in FHL sheath)?"** → **inferior 2/3 posterior surface of fibula + lower interosseous membrane**

**"Dancer, failed 6 months of rest / NSAIDs / arch support — next step?"** → **decompression of FHL at the level of the ankle**

**"Loss of active hallux IP flexion after a cut?"** → **FHL laceration → acute repair**
