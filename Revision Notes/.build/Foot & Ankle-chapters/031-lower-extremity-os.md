# Lower Extremity Os

*Foot & Ankle · 31 / 48*

## 1 Basics

### Definitions & epidemiology

**Accessory ossicle** — **secondary ossification centre that stays separate** from parent bone · may mimic fracture

**Sesamoid** — **bone incorporated into a tendon**, moves with it

**Number** — up to **40 accessory ossicles** + multiple sesamoids in foot and ankle

**Commonest ossicles** — **os trigonum**, **accessory navicular (os tibiale externum)**, os intermetatarseum · os trigonum = **second commonest accessory bone** (OB)

**Commonest sesamoids** — **os peroneum** (in **peroneus longus**) · **hallux sesamoids** (in **FHB**, under 1st MT head)

### Os trigonum – anatomy

**Origin** — separated **posterolateral (lateral) tubercle of talus** · ossifies **8–13 yr**, normally **fuses within 1 yr** · unfused → **synchondrosis** with talus

**Stieda process** — elongated lateral tubercle (unfused) that may fracture with repetitive trauma

**Relations** — os lies **lateral to FHL, tibial nerve, PTT, posterior tibial artery** · FHL runs in fibro-osseous tunnel between **posterolateral (os) + posteromedial talar tubercles**

![FHL runs just medial to the os trigonum (OT)](../MCQ/attachments/q210173-illa-illustration-of-fhl-near-ot.jpg)

## 2 Mechanism

**Os trigonum syndrome** — **repetitive forced plantarflexion** (microtrauma → stress fracture) or **acute forced plantarflexion** (acute fracture) · traumatic disruption of synchondrosis → pain

**Impingement** — os squeezed between **posterior tibial malleolus + calcaneal tuberosity / superior calcaneus**

**At risk** — **ballet dancers (en pointe)**, basketball, soccer, football, kicking, downhill running, push-off

**Os peroneum injury** — direct trauma · **sudden inversion + supination** → associated **PL rupture**

## 3 Presentation

### Os trigonum & posterior impingement – diagnosis

**Symptom** — chronic / recurrent **posterolateral ankle pain** with **forced plantarflexion**, **en pointe**, push-off, jumping

**Exam** — pain with **passive maximal plantarflexion** · no Achilles insertion tenderness · ± FHL swelling / tenderness if FHL tenosynovitis

| Feature | **Os trigonum syndrome** | **FHL tendinitis / stenosing tenosynovitis** |
|---|---|---|
| Pain site | **Posterolateral** | **Posteromedial** (behind medial malleolus) |
| Provocation | **Forced plantarflexion** | Hallux motion, push-off, deep squat |
| Sign | Plantarflexion pain | **Triggering**, crepitus, **great toe locking** |
| X-ray | Tibio-calcaneal impingement on plantarflexion lateral | May show incidental os |

**Inflamed structure in os trigonum + posteromedial tenderness** — **FHL** (labelled C on axial MRI)

**✗ Posterolateral look-alikes** — peroneal pathology (instability, pain with passive DF + eversion) · subtalar arthritis / loose body (pain with inversion / eversion) · tarsal tunnel (medial heel, plantar paraesthesia) · PTT tendinitis (posteromedial; **does not abut os trigonum**)

### FHL stenosing tenosynovitis – examination

**Functional hallux rigidus** — **low-lying distal FHL muscle belly** jams in retrotalar fibro-osseous tunnel ("**cork in a bottle**")

**Test** — **maximal knee extension + ankle dorsiflexion → passive hallux dorsiflexion limited + painful** (✗ forced plantarflexion — os trigonum, ✗ posterolateral tenderness behind peroneals — os trigonum, ✗ DF + eversion — anterior impingement, ✗ calf squeeze — Achilles)

**FHL origin** — inferior fibula + interosseous membrane

**Hamilton** — good results after **FHL release** · **presence of os trigonum on X-ray does not mandate excision**

### Os subfibulare & os peroneum – presentation

**Os subfibulare** — often asymptomatic · focal tenderness + swelling · **chronic ankle instability, recurrent sprains** · laxity on anterior drawer + inversion / eversion stress

**Os peroneum** — painful os peroneum syndrome · fracture · diastasis · PL rupture

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Accessory ossicles and sesamoids

| Accessory bone | Prevalence | Clinical significance | Differential |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Os trigonum** ![Os trigonum on lateral X-ray](../Orthobullets/attachments/ob7049-lateral-xr-os-trigonum.jpg) | **10–25%** | **Posterior ankle impingement, FHL entrapment** | **Shepherd's fracture** (posterolateral process) |
| **Type II accessory navicular** | **2–12%** | **PTT dysfunction** | Navicular tuberosity avulsion, type I accessory navicular |
| **Os subfibulare** | **2%** (table) · **1–2%** (text) | Painful os subfibulare, instability | **Lateral malleolus (ATFL) avulsion fracture** |
| **Os peroneum** | **9–20%** · **bilateral 60%** · **bipartite 30%** | Painful os peroneum, fracture, diastasis, **PL rupture** | Painful os vesalianum, bipartite os peroneum |
| **Os vesalianum** | **2%** | Painful os vesalianum | **Avulsion fracture of 5th MT base** |
| **Hallux sesamoids** | **\~100%** | Fracture, stress fracture | **Bipartite tibial sesamoid** |

### Key radiology findings

| Ossicle | Study | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| **Os trigonum** | **Lateral X-ray in plantarflexion** | Round / oval / triangular ossicle **impinged between posterior tibia + calcaneal tuberosity** |
| Os trigonum | **Axial CT** | Ossicle **off posterior talus** (✗ posterior malleolus — tibia, ✗ accessory navicular — medial + distal, ✗ coalition — distal, ✗ anterior loose body) |
| Os trigonum | **MRI** | **Fluid around os + marrow oedema in os**, **↑ T2 signal**, FHL oedema / inflammation · **no talar marrow oedema** (vs acute posterior process fracture) |
| **Os subfibulare** | **Weight-bearing AP, lateral, mortise + varus stress view** | Ossicle · **talar tilt** on varus stress → instability · **↑ separation of os from fibular tip** |
| **Os peroneum** | X-ray + MRI | Normal os · acute fracture · **proximal migration = PL rupture** |

### Os subfibulare vs type VII transepiphyseal fracture (child)

| | **Os subfibulare** | **Type VII transepiphyseal fracture** |
|---|---|---|
| X-ray | **Smooth-edged, short ossicle** at **inferior pole** of epiphysis | **Long, irregular line in middle third** of distal fibular epiphysis |
| Nature | Anatomic variant **or ossified ATFL avulsion** | **Sleeve avulsion** through secondary ossification centre into joint (ATFL, CFL, PTFL stronger than physis) |
| Initial treatment | **Protected weight-bearing (CAM boot)** | **Protected weight-bearing (CAM boot)** |

**Differentiates them** — **radiographs**, not clinical picture

**Paediatric ankle injuries** — **\~17% of all physeal injuries**

## 5 Management

```
Symptomatic os trigonum
├─ First → NSAIDs, rest, immobilisation (CAM), restricted WB, PT
└─ Failed ≥ 3 mo → excision (open lateral or posterior arthroscopy)
       └─ FHL involved → ± FHL tenolysis, debridement or repair
```

### Os trigonum excision (arthroscopic vs open, sural nerve)

**Nonop** — NSAIDs, rest, immobilisation, restricted weight-bearing, activity modification, PT · PT = short-term relief only · **steroid injection not recommended** here

**Surgery** — after **≥ 3 months failed nonop** → **excision**, open **or** arthroscopic · **similar return-to-play** both (Orthobullets) · **open excision = high RTP, low complications** (OB) · **arthroscopic = 80% good / excellent at 2–5 yr** (Van Dijk) · **RTP 2–3 months**

**✗ Os trigonum traps** — **fixation / ORIF** (not indicated) · FHL debridement alone (doesn't remove culprit) · PTT debridement · peroneal debridement + SPR repair (no snapping / instability) · retrial of failed rest · Haglund excision · os naviculare excision · biopsy

**Posterolateral approach** — **sural nerve at greatest risk** (✗ SPN — more anterior)

**Ballet dancer, failed 1 mo rest + NSAIDs + PT** — **arthroscopic os trigonum excision**

**Basketball player, chronic posterior pain** — **open excision** = highest success (✗ PT, ✗ steroid, ✗ arthroscopic fixation, ✗ ORIF)

**Soccer player, posterolateral pain with plantarflexion, ↑ T2 around os** — **arthroscopic excision** (✗ PB tubularisation, ✗ FHL debridement, ✗ EMG, ✗ subtalar loose body removal)

### Os subfibulare & os peroneum treatment

**Os subfibulare / type VII** — **protected weight-bearing first** (✗ initial MRI — only for chronic pain / instability, ✗ acute surgery, ✗ mandatory ATFL re-attachment) · **failed nonop → excision** ± ligament re-attachment

**Os subfibulare excision for functional instability without laxity (children)** — better AOFAS than rehab alone

| Os peroneum situation | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Painful os peroneum syndrome, minimally displaced fracture | **NSAIDs, rest, immobilisation, restricted WB** |
| Refractory syndrome (minimal tendon involvement), **displaced fracture** | **Excision** |
| **With PL tendon rupture** | **Excision + PL repair or tenodesis to peroneus brevis** |

## How it's asked

**"Ballet dancer, posterior pain en pointe, failed 1 mo nonop?"** → **arthroscopic os trigonum excision**

**"Basketball player, painful max plantarflexion — highest success?"** → **open excision**

**"CT arrow posterior to talus?"** → **os trigonum fragment**

**"Posterolateral approach for os trigonum — diagnosis + nerve at risk?"** → **symptomatic os trigonum, sural nerve**

**"Dancer with os trigonum, posteromedial tenderness — inflamed structure?"** → **C — FHL**

**"Posterolateral pain with plantarflexion, no pain DF + eversion, ↑ T2?"** → **arthroscopic excision of os trigonum**

**"Elite dancer — high RTP, low complications?"** → **open surgical excision of the os trigonum**

**"Child, os subfibulare vs type VII fracture — true?"** → **both treated initially with protected weightbearing**

**"Functional hallux rigidus from low FHL belly — exam?"** → **maximal knee extension + ankle dorsiflexion, assess passive hallux dorsiflexion**
