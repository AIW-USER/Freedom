# Deep Peroneal Nerve Entrapment

*Foot & Ankle · 14 / 48*

## 1 Basics

**Synonym** — **anterior tarsal tunnel syndrome** · rare compression neuropathy of the **deep peroneal nerve (DPN)**

**Commonest site** — fibro-osseous tunnel under the **inferior extensor retinaculum**

**Zone of compression** — **1 cm proximal to ankle joint → talonavicular joint**

**Risk factors** — **high heels**, compressive shoe wear (tight laces, ski boots), previous fracture

### Anterior tarsal tunnel anatomy

| Border | Structure |
|---|---|
| **Roof (superficial)** | **Inferior extensor retinaculum** |
| **Floor (deep)** | **Talonavicular joint capsule** |
| Lateral | Lateral malleolus |
| Medial | Medial malleolus |

**Contents** — **EDL, EHL, tibialis anterior, peroneus tertius, DPN, dorsalis pedis artery + vein** (✗ superficial peroneal nerve, ✗ posterior tibial artery, ✗ intermediate dorsal cutaneous nerve, ✗ medial calcaneal nerve)

**At risk at release** — **dorsalis pedis artery** (vascular bundle runs with the DPN)

**DPN in tunnel** — divides into **lateral (mixed motor + sensory → EDB)** and **medial (sensory only → 1st web space)** branches · nerve lies **lateral to EHL**

**EHB** — crosses superficially over the inferior extensor retinaculum → contributor to DPN compression (OB)

## 2 Mechanism

**Intrinsic** — **dorsal osteophytes over tibiotalar or talonavicular joints** (commonest) · pes cavus, post-fracture deformity (**navicular nonunion**) · ganglion · tumour · hypertrophic EHL / EDL / TA belly · peripheral oedema

**Extrinsic** — **tight laces / ski boots** · **high heels** (plantar flexion) · trauma incl. recurrent ankle instability

**Stretch position** — **ankle plantar flexion + inversion**

## 3 Presentation

**Symptoms** — **vague dorsal foot pain** · paraesthesia **lateral hallux, medial 2nd toe, 1st web space**

**Motor** — **EDB weakness / atrophy**

**Sensory** — ↓ two-point discrimination

**Provocative** — **Tinel over DPN radiating to 1st web space** · worse with **plantar flexion + inversion** · relief with **lidocaine DPN block**

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Study | Use |
|---|---|
| **Lateral foot + ankle X-ray** | **Dorsal osteophytes**, fracture sequelae |
| CT | Bony anatomy of canal |
| **MRI** | **Mass lesions** (ganglion, tumour) |

## 5 Management

```
DPN entrapment
├─ First line → shoe modification + NSAIDs (≥3 mo)
└─ Failed nonop → release inferior extensor retinaculum + osteophyte / ganglion resection
       └─ RSD symptoms → contraindication to release
```

**Nonoperative** — **padded shoe tongue**, **alternative lacing** (skip the tender area), full-length rocker-sole steel shank, **night splint** (ankle drifts into plantar flexion), PT if instability, injection, diuretic for chronic oedema · **≥ 3 months** (OB)

**Surgery** — **S-shaped dorsal incision** ankle → base of 1st/2nd MT · start distal, find nerve, release **both branches** proximally · resect osteophytes, debulk muscle bellies · no compressive shoes after

**Outcome** — **80% good–excellent** · recovery prolonged; **persistent symptoms** = main complication

### Foot drop – tendon transfer (TO)

**Complete CPN palsy (anterior + lateral groups) (TO)** — motor of choice = **tibialis posterior** to dorsum of foot → removes unopposed inversion + restores dorsiflexion · ± **FDL with tib post** (double transfer to EDL + EHL) · or **anterior transposition of lateral gastrocnemius + peroneal neurotization** (TO)

**Isolated DPN palsy (peronei spared) (TO)** — **rerouting the peroneus longus** (✗ tib post transfer — removes inversion balance against working evertors, ✗ FDL + tib post, ✗ gastrocnemius transposition, ✗ foot drop splint) (TO)

## X vs Y

| | **Deep peroneal nerve** | **Superficial peroneal nerve** |
|---|---|---|
| Tunnel | **Anterior tarsal tunnel** (inferior extensor retinaculum) | Not in tunnel |
| Sensation | **1st web space** | Dorsum of foot |
| Motor in foot | **EDB** (+ **EHB**, Orthobullets nerves note) | — |

## How it's asked

**"Dorsal foot pain, medial 2nd-toe tingling, worse PF + inversion — structure in same tunnel to protect?"** → **dorsalis pedis artery** (✗ SPN, ✗ PTA)

**"Commonest intrinsic cause?"** → **dorsal talonavicular / tibiotalar osteophytes**

**"Muscle weak or atrophied?"** → **EDB**

**"Isolated deep peroneal palsy, foot drop — ideal procedure?"** → **reroute peroneus longus** (TO)

**"Complete CPN palsy — tendon transfer motor?"** → **tibialis posterior** (TO)
