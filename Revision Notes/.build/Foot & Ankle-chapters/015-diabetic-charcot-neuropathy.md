# Diabetic Charcot Neuropathy

*Foot & Ankle · 15 / 48*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Definition** — chronic, progressive, **non-infectious destructive arthropathy** from **loss of protective sensation** (pain + proprioception) · first described by **Charcot in tabes dorsalis, 1868** (TO)

**Incidence** — **0.1–1.4%** of diabetics (Orthobullets) · acute Charcot **0.15–2.5%** (OB) · **1% of diabetics after 12 yr of disease** (TO) · **7.5%** of diabetics **with neuropathy** — read the options

**Age** — **type 1 DM → 5th decade** (20–25 yr after diagnosis) · **type 2 DM → 6th decade** (5–10 yr after diagnosis)

**Bilateral** — **9–35%**

**Site** — **foot and ankle commonest** · also shoulder, elbow · knee → ligamentous instability + bone loss

**Commonest cause** — **diabetic neuropathy** (now) · **tabes dorsalis** = former commonest lower-limb cause, now **2nd** (TO)

### Pathophysiology, classification & upper-limb Charcot

**Neurotraumatic theory** — insensate joint + **repetitive microtrauma** → no protective response · overuse injury (TO)

**Neurovascular theory** — **autonomic dysfunction → ↑ blood flow (AV shunting, hyperaemia)** → bone resorption, periarticular osteopenia, demineralisation (TO)

**Combined (TO)** — both neurotraumatic + neurovascular · often initiated by trauma, frequently unrecalled · **osteoclast overactivity**, vascular shunting · twisting / minor injury may trigger (OB)

| Cause | Typical joints (TO) |
|---|---|
| **Diabetes (commonest)** | **Tarsus + tarsometatarsal (commonest)** · MTP · **ankle > knee + spine** |
| **Tabes dorsalis (2nd commonest)** | **Knee (commonest)** · hip · ankle · lumbar spine |
| **Leprosy (Hansen's)** | IP joints (hand) · MTP (feet) · lower limbs |
| **Syringomyelia** | **Shoulder (commonest)** · elbow · wrist · cervical spine |
| **Myelomeningocele** | Ankle · intertarsal |

**Upper-limb Charcot (TO)** — **syringomyelia = commonest cause** (rare overall) (✗ Hansen's, ✗ diabetes, ✗ tabes) (TO)

**Other causes** — alcoholism / alcoholic neuropathy · leprosy · myelomeningocele / spina bifida · tertiary syphilis · syringomyelia · post-traumatic sensory deficit · **stroke with residual sensory deficit** · HMSN / **Charcot-Marie-Tooth** · congenital insensitivity to pain · amyloidosis · MS · **intra-articular steroids in weight-bearing joints** · RA · methotrexate toxicity · idiopathic

**✗ NOT a cause** — **Reiter's syndrome**

### Genes & lab markers

| Marker | Role |
|---|---|
| **CGRP** (TO) | Normally **antagonises RANKL synthesis** at nerve terminal · **non-functioning in neuropathic foot** → RANKL unchecked · maintains **joint capsule integrity** → loss = destruction + dislocation |
| **RANKL** (TO) | Drives **osteoclastogenesis** → excess bone turnover |
| **OPG** (TO) | **Decoy receptor** for RANKL · RANKL–OPG balance **disrupted** |
| **IL-1, TNF-α** | ↑ **NF-κB** + **RANK / RANKL / OPG** pathway |
| **✗ Transforming growth factor-beta (TGF-β)** | **Not** a key marker (TO) |
| **ESR, CRP, WBC** | **↑ in both** Charcot + infection → don't discriminate |
| **Wound healing** | **Absolute lymphocyte count > 1500/mm³** · **albumin > 3.0 g/dL** |
| **HbA1c > 8.0%** | Poor control → **poor surgical outcomes** |
| **Histology** | **Synovial hypertrophy** · **detritic synovitis** (cartilage + bone fragments in synovium) |

**Bisphosphonates (pamidronate)** — theoretical role, ↑ osteoclast activity (TO)

## 3 Presentation

### Clinical features

**Charcot's triad (TO)** — **gross joint swelling** · **exaggerated movements (instability)** · **painless** presentation (TO)

**Pain** — **painless classically**; absence of pain diagnostic (TO) · usually **some pain**, less than expected (TO) · **50% painful / 50% painless** (Orthobullets) — read the options

**Single joint first** · swollen, deformed, subluxed, unstable ("floppy") · **crepitus** from loose bodies (TO)

**Acute** — **swollen, warm, erythematous**; **3.3 °C warmer** (Orthobullets) · **3–7° warmer** (OB) · mimics infection · **intact skin + loss of protective sensation** ≈ pathognomonic

**Chronic** — structurally deformed · bony prominences · **rocker-bottom deformity** · **medial arch collapse** · ligamentous instability

**Skin rule (TO)** — **skin intact → think Charcot** · **skin broken → most likely infection** (TO)

**Undiagnosed patient with Charcot foot** — **evaluate for diabetes mellitus**

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Modality | Finding / fact |
|---|---|
| **X-ray** (AP + lateral foot, ankle series) | **Early** — mimics OA · **Late** — joint space obliteration, **fragmentation of both articular surfaces → subluxation / dislocation**, bone "chunks" in fibrous tissue, soft-tissue oedema, distension, HO |
| **X-ray (TO)** | Marked destruction, **periarticular erosions**, JSN, **osteophytes**, **subchondral sclerosis**, **loose bodies** — simulates OA, **absence of pain diagnostic** |
| **Technetium bone scan** | **Positive in both** neuropathic joint + osteomyelitis (high turnover, non-specific) |
| **Indium WBC scan** | **Cold (negative) in neuropathic joint** · **hot in osteomyelitis** |
| **Sulfur colloid marrow / indium-111** | Added to Tc for specificity |
| **MRI (with + without contrast)** | **Most sensitive for soft tissue infection + osteomyelitis** · **best for abscess vs soft-tissue swelling** · ✗ **cannot reliably separate Charcot from infection** (T2 / STIR oedema in both) |
| Ultrasound | Fluid collections; less specific |
| **Biopsy** | **Guides antibiotics** in osteomyelitis / abscess |

![Charcot ankle — destruction and fragmentation of the hindfoot (TO)](../MCQ/attachments/TO17142017-1.png)

### Diagnosis – elevation test, neuropathy & Charcot vs infection imaging

**Elevation test** — **erythema ↓ / resolves with elevation** (15–30 min, pillows, overnight) = **Charcot** · **unchanged in infection** (cellulitis, osteomyelitis, abscess) = **key differentiator**

**Dependent rubor** — redness only when leg dependent → Charcot

**Semmes-Weinstein monofilament (5.07)** — **confirms neuropathy** · **most sensitive test** · sensitivity **40–95%** · neuropathy has greatest effect on diabetic foot pathology (✗ Thompson, ✗ Cotton, ✗ syndesmosis squeeze, ✗ Babinski)

**Favours Charcot over infection** — no ulcer / intact skin · **normal WBC + normal serum glucose** · no fever / sepsis · fracture without ulceration

**Low-grade fever + normal CRP + erythema settles** — still **neuropathic arthropathy** (✗ navicular stress fracture, ✗ osteomyelitis, ✗ embolic ischaemia, ✗ RA)

**Not useful as next step** — inflammatory markers (↑ in both) · MRI (low specificity) · Tc scan alone

### Diagnostic criteria – Charcot vs infection

| Feature | **Charcot** | **Infection / osteomyelitis** |
|---|---|---|
| **Elevation** | **Erythema decreases** | **Unchanged** |
| Skin | **Intact** | **Broken / ulcer** |
| WBC, glucose | Normal | **Leukocytosis, ↑ glucose**, fever |
| ESR / CRP | ↑ | ↑ |
| Tc bone scan | **+** | **+** |
| **Indium WBC** | **Cold** | **Hot** |
| MRI | Oedema | Oedema; **abscess** |

| ✗ Mimic | Distinguishing point |
|---|---|
| **Cellulitis** | Erythema persists on elevation · no radiographic destruction |
| **CRPS** | Aberrant inflammation, **no** Charcot X-ray changes |
| **DVT** | Swelling + **posterior calf tenderness**; erythema (if any) persists |
| **Occult fracture** | Typically **spinal cord injury** patients |
| Gout | **Normal uric acid** · no rapid bony destruction |
| Charcot-Marie-Tooth, Lisfranc injury, Freiberg | Different history / X-ray |

### Brodsky classification (anatomical)

| Type | Joints | Feature | Frequency |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** (midfoot) | **TMT + naviculocuneiform** | **Fixed rocker-bottom with valgus** · **commonest** | **60%** |
| **2** (hindfoot) | **Subtalar, TN, CC** | **Unstable**, immobilise **up to 2 yr** · 2nd commonest (TO) | **10%** (Orthobullets) · **30–35%** (TO) — read the options |
| **3A** | **Tibiotalar (ankle)** | Late varus / valgus → **malleolar ulcer + osteomyelitis**; significant deformity + disability | **20%** |
| **3B** | **Calcaneal tuberosity fracture** | Distal foot changes / **proximal migration of tuberosity** | < 10% |
| 4 | Combination | — | < 10% |
| 5 | **Forefoot only** | — | < 10% |

**Brodsky trap (TO)** — **"Type 3A = forefoot" is FALSE** → 3A = **ankle** · forefoot = type 5 (TO)

![Brodsky classification — types 1, 2, 3A, 3B](../Orthobullets/attachments/ob7047-brodsky.jpg)

### Eichenholtz classification (staging)

| Stage | Name | Clinical | X-ray | Treatment (TO) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **0** | — | Oedema, erythema, **loss of protective sensation**, instability | **Negative** (bone scan + in all stages) | **Frequent follow-up + serial X-rays** |
| **1** | **Fragmentation** (dissolution, acute) | **Warm, oedema, ↑ ligamentous laxity** | **Osteopenia, periarticular fragmentation, subluxation / dislocation** | **TCC** |
| **2** | **Coalescence** (subacute, reparative) | **↓ oedema + warmth** | **Coalescence**, absorption of fine debris, early fusion, sclerosis | **TCC → CROW** |
| **3** | **Reconstruction** (Orthobullets) · **consolidation** (TO) — chronic | **No inflammation**, stable, often deformed | **Consolidation + remodelling**, arthrosis, osteophytes | Plantigrade → **custom inlay shoes / CROW** · non-plantigrade / recurrent ulcer → **debridement ± arthrodesis** |

**Time to consolidation (TO)** — **forefoot 6 mo** · **midfoot 12 mo** · **hindfoot 18 mo** (TO)

## 5 Management

```
Charcot foot — goal: stable plantigrade foot in shoewear
├─ Acute (Eichenholtz 0–2), skin intact → TOTAL CONTACT CAST
│      └─ then → CROW → depth shoes + double rocker
├─ Braceable, equinus + focal prominence / ulcer → EXOSTECTOMY + TAL
├─ Unbraceable / unstable deformity → REDUCTION + ARTHRODESIS
│      (ring fixator if ulcer / poor bone · TTC nail for ankle + subtalar)
└─ Failed surgery / recurrent infection → AMPUTATION
```

### Operative criteria

| Procedure | Indication |
|---|---|
| **TCC (nonop)** | **First line** — acute Charcot **without skin breakdown** · **75% success** |
| **Exostectomy + TAL** | **"Braceable" foot** with **equinus** + **focal bony prominence** causing skin breakdown / ulcer failing TCC |
| **Deformity correction, arthrodesis ± osteotomy** | **Severe deformity not "braceable"** · unstable deformity · **persistent plantar ulcer** · **unstable Charcot ankle** · failed orthotics |
| **External (ring) fixation** | **Poor bone quality** or **compromised soft tissues / ulcer** |
| **Amputation** | **Failed previous surgery (unstable arthrodesis)** · **recurrent infection / osteomyelitis** · troublesome ulcers |
| **✗ Surgery in acute stage** | Not enough evidence for stage 1 surgery; avoid during inflammation (some authors propose early fixation) |

**Operative indications (Orthobullets)** — recurrent infection · deformity · severe skin breakdown

**Joint replacement** — **relatively contraindicated** (TO)

### Acute stage – total contact casting

**First line / gold standard** — **total contact cast (TCC)** + offloading for **acute (Eichenholtz 1–2) Charcot without ulcer**

**Duration** — cast changed **every 2–4 wk for 2–4 months** (Orthobullets) · **up to 4 months** (OB) · **weekly cast changes until erythema + swelling decrease** (OB) · **2–4 months** (TO) — read the options

**Weight bearing** — **NWB or limited WB = gold standard** until swelling resolves + consolidation on X-ray (✓ "TCC, no weight bearing, weekly cast changes") · **WB TCC effective** in early stages, no harm (De Souza, OB) · NWB **not conclusively necessary** (OB) — read the options

**Alternative** — pneumatic bracing

**Medications** — bisphosphonates · neuropathic pain drugs · antidepressants · topical anaesthetics

**Stage 1 on cast, still warm at 2 months** — **continue TCC** (✗ gout treatment — uric acid normal, ✗ PT — aggravates stage 1, ✗ ORIF of navicular, ✗ biopsy + ID referral)

**Goal in fragmentation** — **prevent further collapse + deformity**, avoid high-contact-stress ulceration

| ✗ Wrong first step | Why |
|---|---|
| **IV antibiotics / admission** | No infection |
| **I&D / emergent debridement** | No ulcer, **negative indium scan**, erythema settles |
| **MRI, inflammatory markers, Tc scan alone** | Don't change management (low specificity) |
| **Midfoot / TN + TMT / subtalar / triple arthrodesis, ORIF, ring fixator** | Only after failed nonop, gross deformity or ulceration |
| **Plantar exostectomy** | Not in acute phase |
| **BKA / transtibial amputation** | Salvage only |
| **Shoe modification, custom orthotics** (Jones bar, carbon shank, AFO leaf spring, plastizote insole) | Not for acute stage |

### Footwear & bracing after casting or partial amputation

**CROW (TO)** — **Charcot restraint orthotic walker** · used **in Charcot joint** (✗ subtalar arthritis, ✗ ankle arthritis, ✗ gout of great toe) (TO)

**CROW design** — custom **bivalved total-contact AFO** · full foot enclosure · **rigid rocker sole** · custom insole · rigid polypropylene anterior + posterior shell · **DF stop**, heel lift · rocker sole counters **"nutcracker effect"** · Velcro (TO)

**CROW use** — **after TCC** (oedema reduced first) · controls oedema · enables ambulation · **prevents deformity** during coalescence · **removable → hygiene, wound care, comfort**

**CROW drawbacks** — **high cost** · bulky → poor compliance · **knee + lumbar pain** (TO)

![Charcot restraint orthotic walker (CROW)](../Orthobullets/attachments/ob7047-crow-walking-boot.jpg)

**Sequence** — **TCC → CROW → custom shoe / depth-inlay shoe with accommodative orthoses**

**Eichenholtz 3 shoe** — **double rocker sole** → ↓ ulcer risk at **plantar apex / midfoot prominence**

![Double rocker sole](../MCQ/attachments/q4467-d-double-rocker.jpg)

| Rocker sole | Use |
|---|---|
| **Double rocker** | **Plantar midfoot prominence (Charcot rocker-bottom)** |
| Severe-angle rocker | Pressure **distal to ball of foot** (toe-tip ulcers) |
| Mild rocker | Mild **MT head relief**, propulsion |
| Negative heel rocker | **Fixed dorsiflexion**, forefoot pressure → shifts load to midfoot + heel |
| **Heel-to-toe rocker** | **After ankle / subtalar fusion** · fixed lesser toe deformities |

**Partial 1st ray amputation (TA intact, 10° DF)** — **steel shank** (flat 1-inch steel strip) → **extends foot lever, prevents deformity at toe break** ± rocker sole (✗ snug shoe — volume fluctuation, ✗ custom shoe — unnecessary expense, ✗ leather sole — no catching with 10° DF + intact TA, ✗ lateral wedge)

### Chronic deformity & ulceration – exostectomy & reconstructive arthrodesis

**Ulcer cause** — malpositioned tarsal bones after joint collapse

**Recurrent midfoot ulcer, failed TCC, stable deformity, equinus** — **exostectomy + Achilles tendon lengthening + protective brace** · limb salvage **up to 90%** (✗ ex-fix, ✗ BKA — viable tissue, ✗ observation, ✗ exostectomy alone — equinus needs lengthening)

**Silfverskiöld test** — **positive → tight gastrocnemius** (gastroc recession) vs whole Achilles complex (TAL)

**Talar head ulcer (Chopart dislocation), failed 2 TCC cycles** — **reduction + arthrodesis of Chopart joint with ring fixator** (no hardware near ulcer, soft tissue monitored)

| ✗ Option for talar head ulcer | Why wrong |
|---|---|
| CROW / repeat offloading | Failed already → contraindicated |
| Achilles lengthening alone | Doesn't reduce talar head |
| **In-situ TTC with IM nail** | TTC suits **Brodsky 2–3**, but **in-situ** fusion contraindicated — must **reduce** |
| Midfoot osteotomy + Lisfranc plates | For **Brodsky 1**; **plates contraindicated** with poor bone + ulcer |

**Charcot ankle + subtalar, failed bracing, no wounds, active** — **TTC fusion with retrograde nail** (small incisions) (✗ anterior plate tibiotalar fusion — wound complications + misses subtalar, ✗ BKA, ✗ TCC — for ulcers, ✗ CAM orthosis)

**Fixation** — internal: screws, pins, plates, **tibiocalcaneal nail** (internal may need **staging**) · **external fixation** (poor bone / soft tissue; usually **single stage**)

**Post-op** — **minimal WB for 3 months**

### Amputation level (Chopart)

**Forefoot gangrene with necrosis up to navicular (smoker, hypertensive) (TO)** — **Chopart amputation** (✗ Pirogoff, ✗ Syme, ✗ Lisfranc) (TO)

**Chopart advantages (TO)** — **conserves heel support** · technically easier · **shoe + AFO** instead of knee-high prosthesis · little shortening · **heel skin = weight-bearing end** · easier muscle balancing (TO)

**Equinus (TO)** — common cause of foot amputation failure · Chopart → **tibialis anterior transfer proximally + tendo-Achilles lengthening** · Syme → only TA lengthening, slight equinus ruins ambulation (TO)

**Goal** — partial / limited amputation if vascularity allows · BKA for severe deformity with soft-tissue involvement or failed reconstruction

## 6 Complications

| Complication | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Arthrodesis complications** | **Up to 70%** — hardware failure, infection, malposition, **recurrent ulceration**, fracture |
| **Overall complication rate** | **50–56%** (Sämann) |
| **Recurrence (secondary episodes)** | **Type 1 DM 41.6% vs type 2 18.3%** |
| **Amputation rate** | **3–9%** — depends on avoiding ulceration (TO) |
| Late complications | Chronic ulceration → infection → **osteomyelitis** |
| Ankle (Brodsky 3A) | **Malleolar ulcer + osteomyelitis** |
