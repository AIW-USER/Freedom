# Foot Anatomy and Biomechanics

*Foot & Ankle · 18 / 48*

## 1 Basics

### Plantar fascia

**Origin** — **medial calcaneal tuberosity**

**Insertion** — **plantar plate + bases of all 5 proximal phalanges** · lateral band → **5th MT base**

**Function** — **windlass mechanism**: toes dorsiflex at toe-off → fascia tightens → **↑ arch height, foot shortens** · **2nd most important medial arch support**

**Load transfer** — **plantar aponeurosis = primary structure of force transfer hindfoot → forefoot in stance** · tension peaks in **late stance**, up to **96% body weight** (OB) (✗ Achilles, ✗ Lisfranc ligament, ✗ tibialis posterior, ✗ tibialis anterior)

![Windlass mechanism – toe dorsiflexion tightens the plantar fascia and raises the arch](../MCQ/attachments/q17-illa-windlass.jpg)

### Hindfoot – subtalar joint

**Facets** — **posterior = largest** · **middle = medial, on sustentaculum** · **anterior = continuous with talonavicular joint**

**Motion** — **inversion / eversion** only · **no role in plantar / dorsiflexion**

### Transverse tarsal (Chopart) joint

**Components** — **talonavicular + calcaneocuboid**

**Talonavicular support** — **spring ligament** = **superomedial + inferior calcaneonavicular ligaments**

**Calcaneocuboid** — **saddle-shaped** · plantar support = **superficial + deep inferior calcaneocuboid ligaments** · superior support = **lateral limb of bifurcate ligament**

### Midfoot – Lisfranc (TMT) joint

**Articulations** — 1st–3rd metatarsocuneiform + 4th–5th metatarsocuboid · naviculocuneiform + intercuneiform joints = dense ligaments, **little motion**

| Column | Bones | Mobility / role |
|---|---|---|
| **Medial** | **1st MT, medial cuneiform, navicular** | **Carries most load in standing** |
| **Middle** | **2nd + 3rd MT, middle + lateral cuneiforms** | **Least mobile** → rigidity for push-off |
| **Lateral** | **4th + 5th MT, cuboid** | **Most mobile** → uneven ground |

| TMT ligament layer | Strength |
|---|---|
| **Interosseous** (contains **Lisfranc ligament**: **plantar medial cuneiform → base of 2nd MT**) | **Strongest** |
| Plantar | Next strongest |
| **Dorsal** | **Weakest** |

**Transverse Roman arch** — dorsal surface wider than plantar · **2nd MT base = keystone**

![Medial column of the foot](../Orthobullets/attachments/ob7006-medial-column-of-foot-moved.jpg)

### Forefoot

**1st MT** — **shortest + widest** · takes **50% of weight during gait** (Orthobullets)

**2nd MT** — **longest** · **most stress during gait** → **commonest MT stress fracture**

**Which MT bears most weight walking?** — **1st MT 50% in gait** (Orthobullets) · **standing = 1st MT, walking = 2nd MT** (TO) — read the options

**Transverse metatarsal ligament** — **holds hallucal sesamoids in place** as 1st MT head drifts medially → **false image of sesamoid subluxation** (head moves, not sesamoids)

**Adductor hallucis conjoined tendon** — lateral aspect of **lateral sesamoid** + lateral base of proximal phalanx

**2nd MTP plantar plate** — dense phalangeosesamoidal complex · must become **lax before dorsal translation** of proximal phalanx · once attenuated, main deforming force = **EDL** · **weakest at origin (metatarsal neck)**

**Seymour fracture (hallux distal phalanx)** — remove nail + interposed physeal tissue, repair nail bed, splint in extension, **antibiotics**

### Intrinsic muscle function

**Foot intrinsics** — **flex MTP joints + extend IP joints** (✗ extend MTP + flex IP, ✗ flex both, ✗ extend both)

## 2 Mechanism

### Subtalar & transverse tarsal joint axes in gait

| Phase | Subtalar / calcaneus | Chopart axes | Foot |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Heel strike** | **Eversion** | **Parallel → unlocked** | **Supple** → shock absorption, accommodates ground |
| **Push-off / toe-off** | **Inversion** | **Divergent → locked** | **Rigid lever arm** for propulsion |

**✗ Gait traps** — eversion **unlocks** (✗ locks) · calcaneus **everted** at heel strike (✗ inverted throughout stance) · **tibialis anterior contracts eccentrically** in stance (✗ concentrically) · push-off foot is **rigid** via inversion (✗ flexible via eversion)

![Subtalar eversion – parallel Chopart axes; inversion – divergent axes](../Orthobullets/attachments/ob7006-subtalar-joint-chopart-joint-axes-moved.jpg)

### Windlass mechanism & push-off

**Flexible → rigid foot at toe-off** — (1) **tightening of plantar fascia** · (2) **progressive external rotation of lower extremity** (pelvis → ankle → subtalar) · (3) **subtalar inversion locks transverse tarsal joint**

**Does NOT occur at push-off** — **loosening of the spring ligament** (✗ subtalar inversion, ✗ Chopart locking, ✗ external rotation, ✗ plantar fascia tightening — all do occur)

**Flatfoot causes (OB)** — triceps surae insufficiency, obesity, PTTD, laxity of **spring ligament** or other ligaments

## X vs Y

| | **Heel strike** | **Push-off** |
|---|---|---|
| Subtalar | Eversion | **Inversion** |
| Transverse tarsal | **Unlocked** (parallel axes) | **Locked** |
| Limb rotation | — | **External rotation** |
| Plantar fascia | — | **Tight** (windlass) |

| | **1st MT** | **2nd MT** |
|---|---|---|
| Size | **Shortest, widest** | **Longest** |
| Load | **Standing / 50% in gait** (Orthobullets) | **Walking** (TO) · most stress |
| Classic | Sesamoids | **Stress fracture**, keystone, Lisfranc ligament |

## How it's asked

**"Primary site of force transfer hindfoot → forefoot in stance?"** → **plantar aponeurosis**

**"NOT at normal push-off?"** → **loosening of spring ligament**

**"Subtalar–transverse tarsal relationship in gait?"** → **Chopart axes parallel at heel strike**

**"Eversion of subtalar joint does what to Chopart?"** → **unlocks** (flexible)

**"Tibialis anterior in stance?"** → **eccentric contraction**

**"Intrinsic muscles act on toes by?"** → **flexing MTP + extending IP joints**

**"Metatarsal bearing most weight during walking?"** → **2nd** (TO)

**"Lisfranc ligament runs?"** → **plantar medial cuneiform → base of 2nd MT**

**"Strongest TMT ligament layer?"** → **interosseous**

**"Least mobile column?"** → **middle**
