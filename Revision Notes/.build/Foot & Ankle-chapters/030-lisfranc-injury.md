# Lisfranc Injury

*Foot & Ankle · 30 / 48*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Definition** — TMT fracture-dislocation · disruption between **medial cuneiform and base of 2nd metatarsal** · purely ligamentous or fracture-dislocation (treatment implications)

**Incidence** — **0.2% of all fractures** · **1 per 55,000 / yr** · **males > females** · **3rd decade**

**Missed** — **easily missed**, up to **20%** at initial encounter · missed → **progressive planovalgus**, chronic pain

**Lisfranc equivalents** — **contiguous proximal metatarsal fractures** or **tarsal fractures** · may involve multiple TMT joints

### Anatomy – Lisfranc & plantar ligaments

**Lisfranc joint complex** — **tarsometatarsal** + **intermetatarsal** + **intertarsal (intercuneiform)** articulations · 5 MTs with 3 cuneiforms + cuboid

| Column | Joints | Mobility |
|---|---|---|
| **Medial** | **1st TMT** | Little / none |
| **Middle** | **2nd + 3rd TMT** | Little / none |
| **Lateral** | **4th + 5th TMT** | **Most mobile** — accommodates uneven ground |

**✗ Mobility trap (TO)** — 1st–3rd cuneiform–MT joints have **greater** mobility than 4th–5th ✗ (they are **stable, rigid lever for push-off**)

**Bony stability** — **trapezoidal bases of 1st–3rd MTs** → **transverse "Roman arch"** · **2nd TMT = keystone**, 2nd MT recessed in mortise between **medial and lateral (recessed middle) cuneiforms** · **shallow 2nd TMT mortise = risk factor** (TO)

| Ligament | Key facts |
|---|---|
| **Lisfranc ligament** | **Plantar interosseous** · **lateral surface of medial cuneiform → medial base of 2nd MT** (near plantar surface) · **largest + strongest** interosseous TMT ligament · **8–10 mm wide, 5–6 mm thick** · **tightens with pronation + abduction** of forefoot · stabilises 1st/2nd TMT + midfoot arch (✗ "dorsal interosseous" — TO trap) |
| **Plantar TMT ligaments** | **Plantar ligament medial cuneiform → 2nd + 3rd MTs** + Lisfranc ligament both needed for **transverse instability** |
| **Dorsal TMT ligaments** | **Weaker than plantar** (TO ✓) → **displacement usually dorsal** |
| **Intermetatarsal ligaments** | **2nd–5th MT bases only** · **no IM ligament between 1st and 2nd MTs** (✗ "1st–2nd IM ligament is strongest" — TO trap) |
| **Lisfranc ligament complex (TO)** | **TMT ligaments of 1st + 2nd rays + Lisfranc ligament** |
| **1st MT base ↔ medial cuneiform** | **Joint capsule** (not Lisfranc ligament) |

**Point on X-ray** — Lisfranc ligament runs **base of 2nd MT → medial cuneiform** (✗ 1st MT → 2nd MT, ✗ 2nd MT → middle cuneiform, ✗ medial → middle cuneiform)

**✗ Ligament distractors** — **spring** (calcaneus → navicular, supports talar head) · **plantar fascia** (plantar calcaneus → MT heads) · **AITFL** (tibia → fibula) · bifurcate, long plantar, Chopart, CFL

![Lisfranc ligament — plantar, medial cuneiform to 2nd MT base](../Orthobullets/attachments/ob7030-illustration-anatomy-moved.jpg)

## 2 Mechanism

**Indirect (commonest)** — **rotational force + axial load through hyper-plantarflexed forefoot** → hyperflexion / compression / **abduction** moment → MTs displaced **dorsal + lateral** · usually involves at least **2nd TMT**

**Settings** — MVA, fall from height, **athletes** (football player fallen on, stirrup in equestrian, soccer pivot, twist under motorcycle, dancer)

**Direct** — crush (workplace) · **hockey puck to medial foot ≠ Lisfranc** → occult trabecular fractures (below)

## 3 Presentation

**Symptoms** — severe midfoot pain · **inability to bear weight** · pain at push-off

**Plantar ecchymosis** — **medial plantar** bruising = **highly suggestive of Lisfranc** (TO) · also seen in **calcaneal fracture** (TO) (✗ mycetoma, ✗ hydatid cyst of talus, ✗ vertical talus)

**Exam** — midfoot swelling, TMT tenderness · pain with **pronation + abduction** of forefoot · midfoot squeeze · **piano key test** (hypermobility)

**Instability test** — grasp MT heads, **dorsal force** while palpating TMTs → **dorsal subluxation = instability** · 1st + 2nd MTs displace medially + laterally → **global instability → surgery** · **plantar ligaments intact → no dorsal subluxation → nonop possible**

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Sign | View |
|---|---|
| **Medial base 2nd MT ↔ medial middle cuneiform discontinuity** | **AP** — diagnostic |
| **Widening 1st–2nd ray interval** ± **fleck sign** (avulsion of Lisfranc ligament from 2nd MT base, in **1st IM space**) | **AP** — diagnostic |
| **Dorsal displacement of proximal base of 1st or 2nd MT** | **Lateral** |
| **Medial 4th MT base ≠ medial cuboid** | **Oblique** |
| **Disrupted medial column line** (tangent to medial navicular + medial cuneiform) | **Oblique** |

| Study | Use |
|---|---|
| **AP, lateral, oblique** | Initial study of choice |
| **Weight-bearing (bilateral, comparison)** | **Non-WB films normal + suspicion → next step** · reveals subtle diastasis · **contralateral** for low-energy injuries |
| **Physician-assisted stress exam / stress fluoroscopy** | Dynamic alternative · **EUA reserved for equivocal cases** |
| **CT** | Comminution, **pre-op planning**, subtle fractures, fleck sign |
| **MRI** | **Confirms purely ligamentous injury** · plantar ligament integrity · ✗ doesn't assess **dynamic instability** as well as WB films |

**Mulcahy alignment check** — AP: 2nd MT–middle cuneiform, 3rd MT–lateral cuneiform, 1st MT–medial cuneiform · oblique: middle cuneiform–2nd MT, lateral cuneiform–3rd MT · lateral: talus–2nd MT axis, medial cuneiform–5th MT, dorsal TMT surfaces

![Fleck sign in the 1st intermetatarsal space](../Orthobullets/attachments/ob7030-ap-moved.jpg)

### Diagnosis – weight-bearing/stress radiographs, MRI & plantar ecchymosis

**Midfoot pain, plantar ecchymosis, non-WB X-ray normal** — **standing / weight-bearing AP radiographs** (✗ CT, ✗ MRI, ✗ bone scan — expensive, not warranted, ✗ compartment pressures) · **"of both feet"** when CT shows no fracture (✗ MRI first, ✗ RTP stiff-soled shoe, ✗ fix, ✗ cast 6 wk before stability known)

**Football player, plantar flexed foot, nonstanding films normal** — **weight-bearing AP radiograph** (✗ RTP, ✗ PT, ✗ orthosis, ✗ NWB cast)

**WB radiograph most likely shows** — **dorsal displacement of 1st MT proximal base on lateral** (✗ fleck lateral to 5th MT base — 5th MT avulsion, ✗ calcaneal sclerotic line — stress fracture, ✗ proximal medial sesamoid — **turf toe**, ✗ talar head directly over navicular — **medial subtalar dislocation**)

**MRI: dorsal capsule torn, plantar ligament intact, WB films normal** — **stable → protected WB in CAM boot** (✗ bridge plate, ✗ strict NWB cast 6 wk, ✗ stress fluoro EUA, ✗ primary arthrodesis)

**Hockey puck direct blow to medial foot, X-rays normal** — **MRI** → occult fractures **medial malleolus, medial talar neck, medial cuneiform, 1st MT** (trabecular stress fractures + marrow oedema; medial malleolus can displace) (✗ bilateral WB X-rays — mechanism inconsistent with Lisfranc, ✗ WBAT, ✗ cast without diagnosis, ✗ RTP)

### Diagnostic criteria – instability

| Finding | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **> 2 mm** displacement / shift between 1st + 2nd MT on WB film | **Unstable → ORIF / anatomic reduction** |
| Diastasis 2nd MT base ↔ medial cuneiform | **Pathognomonic** of Lisfranc ligament rupture |
| **Lisfranc + plantar ligament (medial cuneiform → 2nd/3rd MT) torn** | **Transverse instability** |
| Plantar ligament intact on MRI | **Stable** → nonop |
| Medial gapping on stress view | Injury of that TMT even without frank displacement |
| **> 5 mm** diastasis, neglected pure ligamentous (TO) | **Medial column arthrodesis** |

**DDx** — MT base fracture · MT stress fracture · tarsal fracture

## 5 Management

```
Suspected Lisfranc
├─ Non-WB X-ray normal → bilateral WB / stress X-ray (MRI if equivocal)
│      └─ stable, plantar ligament intact → CAM boot / cast
└─ Unstable (> 2 mm) or displaced → operative
       ├─ massive swelling → temporary pinning, definitive at 2–3 wk
       └─ soft tissues OK → ORIF (< 24 h) or primary arthrodesis
```

```
Operative choice
├─ Bony fracture-dislocation, acute → ORIF rigid 1–3 TMT
├─ Purely ligamentous → primary arthrodesis medial ± middle column
├─ Delayed / chronic / arch collapse → arthrodesis
└─ Lateral column (4–5) displaced → temporary K-wires
       (never fuse 4th–5th TMT)
```

### Operative criteria

| Nonoperative | ORIF | Primary arthrodesis (1st–3rd TMT) |
|---|---|---|
| **Non-displaced, stable on WB** · partial Lisfranc rupture, no displacement | **Any instability (> 2 mm shift)** | **Purely ligamentous** (controversial) |
| **Nonambulatory**, serious vascular disease, **severe neuropathy** (insensate diabetic) | **Bony fracture-dislocation**, acute | **Delayed treatment** (e.g. 8 wk, 6 mo) |
| Medically unfit | Young active (soldier) | **Chronic deformity**, missed injury |
| **Cast 8 wk** (Orthobullets) · **NWB cast 6 wk** then gradual return (OB) | — | **Complete A / C2** · severe intra-articular comminution · failed fixation |

**Nonop of displaced / unstable** — significantly **worse** functional + radiographic outcomes · persistent midfoot instability

### ORIF indications & fixation technique (medial vs lateral columns)

**Goal** — **anatomic reduction + stable fixation** = the outcome predictor · **open > closed reduction** (see reduction, **loose osteochondral fragments, soft-tissue interposition** — ligament / bony avulsion may be **incarcerated**)

**Standard construct** — **rigid fixation 1st–3rd TMT (screws or spanning plates)** + **K-wires for 4th + 5th TMT** (flexible, removed later) · stiff fixation of all 5 TMTs → poor function

**Plate vs screw vs wire** — **cortical screws > K-wires** (more rigid) · **pin fixation inferior** (ligament needs prolonged fixation) · plates span TMTs when **MT base comminuted** · more screws = more rigid

**Fix only what's injured** — 1st + 2nd TMT ligamentous injury → **ORIF of 1st and 2nd TMT** (✗ CR + K-wires, ✗ 1–3 + K-wire 4–5 — only when all five injured, e.g. **homolateral**) · no lateral instability → **ORIF medial two columns alone** (✗ add lateral column stabilisation)

**Closed manipulation + percutaneous screw** — acceptable **only if anatomic alignment achieved closed**

**Bridge vs transarticular** — acute displaced → open reduction with **spanning-bridge plate or transarticular screws** · acute reducible → CRPP option

**Timing** — **within 24 h** or wait for swelling (up to **2–3 wk**) · massive swelling → **temporary percutaneous pinning** of medial + lateral columns → delayed ORIF / arthrodesis (↓ wound infection, low-level evidence)

**Approach** — single or dual **longitudinal** incisions · **1st–2nd web space** · 1st TMT exposed **between EHL and EHB** · **protect deep NV bundle (DPN + dorsalis pedis)**

**Sequence** — **reduce intercuneiform instability first** → fix 1st–3rd TMT transarticular screws · check intercuneiform + naviculocuneiform joints

**Post-op** — early midfoot ROM, protected WB · **K-wires out 6–8 wk** · **screws out 3–6 mo** · **full WB 8–10 wk** (Orthobullets) · **protected WB 3–5 mo** + passive midfoot ROM (OB) — read the options · asymptomatic screws across only 1st–3rd TMT may stay · **no vigorous sport 9–12 mo**

![Temporary lateral column pinning](../Orthobullets/attachments/ob7030-lateral-column-pinning.jpg)

### ORIF vs primary arthrodesis for ligamentous injuries

**Purely ligamentous — preferred treatment (TO)** — **medial tarsometatarsal joint arthrodesis** (TO) (✗ NWB cast, ✗ anatomical screw fixation, ✗ WBAT cast)

**Why fuse ligamentous injuries** — **ligament healing less reliable than bone** → deformity recurs after ORIF · ligamentous injuries do **worse** than bony · nonop → uniformly poor (redislocation)

**Ly & Coetzee RCT (purely ligamentous)** — primary arthrodesis 1st–3rd rays → **higher AOFAS at 2 yr** · **25% of ORIF** → early arthritis → revised to fusion

**Best result, acute purely ligamentous** — **open reduction + primary arthrodesis with screw fixation** (✗ CRPP, ✗ open + pins, ✗ metallic or bioabsorbable screws alone)

**Alternative to ORIF (ligamentous)** — **open reduction + arthrodesis of medial and middle columns, reduction + stabilisation of lateral column** (✗ fuse all three columns, ✗ CRPP all, ✗ closed reduction + cast, ✗ boot)

| Outcome | **ORIF** | **Primary arthrodesis (PA)** |
|---|---|---|
| **Hardware removal** | **Higher** (\~**75%**, often planned) | **Lower** (\~**20%**) ✓ |
| **Overall reoperation** | **Higher** (driven by HW removal) | **Lower** ✓ |
| Reoperation **excluding HW removal** | Equal (Buda: 163 ORIF vs 54 PA) | Equal |
| **Functional outcome** | **Equivalent** overall (level 1, meta-analyses, delayed cases) | **Better for purely ligamentous** (Ly & Coetzee) · **improved return to activity** |
| **Patient-reported outcomes** | **No difference** (OB) | **Higher with PA** (prospective, OB) — read the options |
| **Cost** | **Higher overall** (OB) · **ORIF less expensive** (another OB) — read the options | — |
| Infection, malreduction, hardware failure, alignment, satisfaction, NWB time | **Equal** | **Equal** |
| PTOA | **Higher** | Lower |
| Adjacent joint stress | — | **Greater concern** |

**✗ PA traps** — "higher implant removal with PA" · "lower PTOA with ORIF" · "higher malreduction with ORIF" · "worse function with PA" · "higher wound infection with PA" · "decreased reduction quality with PA" · "**medial + lateral column arthrodesis better**" (✗ — inferior)

**Lateral column** — **never fuse 4th + 5th TMT** → **inferior functional outcomes** (Mulier: total arthrodesis worse than medial arthrodesis + lateral ORIF) · use **temporary K-wires** · **medial column fusion superior to combined medial + lateral**

**All 5 TMT fused (B2 injury)** — **inferior functional outcomes** vs medial ray arthrodesis + temporary lateral K-wires

**Homolateral fracture-dislocation (medial + lateral columns)** — **arthrodesis of medial column + K-wire fixation of lateral column** (✗ cast, ✗ percutaneous screw / ORIF medial column alone, ✗ fuse medial + lateral)

**MVC, medial pain, plantar ecchymosis** — **open reduction + arthrodesis of medial two TMT joints** (✗ ex-fix + staged screws, ✗ percutaneous wires 1–3, ✗ Morel-Lavallée debridement, ✗ screws + ligament reconstruction)

**Equestrian, stirrup, 1 wk** — **arthrodesis of medial TMT joints** (✗ aircast NWB, ✗ walking cast, ✗ Broström, ✗ delayed osteotomy) · operative reduction mandatory whether fusion or ORIF

**Crush at work** — **ligamentous patterns better with primary arthrodesis than ORIF** (✗ workplace = no difference, ✗ fusion only as salvage — post-ORIF salvage fusion does worse than primary, ✗ rigid 4th–5th TMT fixation, ✗ suture fixation alone for complete dislocations)

**Suture-button** — possible role in **isolated Lisfranc ligament** injury · complete dislocations need rigid fixation

**Fusion technique** — denude TMT cartilage → **cortical screws or square plate** · cast / splint 6 wk → WB in boot 6–12 wk → **full WB in shoes by 12 wk**

## 6 Complications

| Complication | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Post-traumatic midfoot arthritis** | **Commonest complication** · ↑ with delay + ORIF · **up to 80% non-anatomic ORIF** · **54% symptomatic OA at \~10 yr after ORIF** (Orthobullets) · **> 25% even with anatomic reduction** (OB) → **midfoot arthrodesis** |
| **Malunion** | Non-anatomic ORIF → **shoe modification** (nonsurgical) or **corrective arthrodesis** |
| **Nonunion** | **Smoking** → revision arthrodesis + graft (unless elderly low-demand) |
| **Hardware removal** | **\~75% ORIF** · **\~20% arthrodesis** |
| **Deep infection** | **3–4%** · swelling at definitive surgery → I&D ± HW removal |
| **Planovalgus deformity** | **Nonop** or non-anatomic ORIF · missed injury → arch collapse + **forefoot abduction** |
| Dorsal foot numbness | **Dorsal osteophytes impinging SPN** (tight shoes) |

**Prognosis** — military: **\~70%** resume occupational running at \~3 yr after ORIF or PA · **missed diagnosis = poor prognosis**

### Chronic/missed injuries – midfoot arthritis & arthrodesis

**Most common complication (10 yr after boot NWB 6 wk)** — **midfoot arthritis** (✗ nonunion, ✗ planovalgus, ✗ 1st TMT hypermobility — "not associated", ✗ dorsal numbness)

**Untreated trauma → chronic pain + progressive deformity** — **Lisfranc injury → pes planovalgus** (✗ navicular — no planovalgus, ✗ **calcaneus / talar neck → varus**, ✗ ATFL — ankle instability)

**Chronic PTOA nonop** — **shoe inserts / modifications + NSAIDs** · fail → **arthrodesis of affected joints**

**Malunion, pain at push-off / stairs** — **shoe modification with cushioned heel + rocker sole** (unloads TMT in terminal stance) (✗ neutral-posted rigid orthosis, ✗ UCBL, ✗ AFO locked 90°, ✗ Arizona)

**2 yr after cast, push-off pain, arch loss** — **TMT joint arthrodesis** (✗ Evans lateral column lengthening, ✗ TN fusion, ✗ Dwyer, ✗ triple)

**6 mo old (ICU head injury), 1st–3rd TMT** — **arthrodesis of 1st through 3rd TMT** (✗ orthotics, ✗ CR + screws, ✗ ORIF, ✗ add triple)

**> 6 mo, instability + arch collapse** — **open reduction + primary arthrodesis** ± **tricortical ipsilateral iliac crest autograft** for bone loss (✗ RTW, ✗ PT / orthotics — only if unfit, ✗ cast WBAT — insensate diabetic / unfit, ✗ ORIF — late presenters fuse)

**Factors favouring primary arthrodesis** — **primarily ligamentous injury + delay in treatment** (✗ significant bony injury → **ORIF**)

**Midfoot arthrodesis best indicated** — **chronic ligamentous Lisfranc injury** (ligament won't heal functionally) (✗ acute **nutcracker cuboid** — restore lateral column length, ✗ diabetic **equinus** — stretching / TAL / gastroc recession, ✗ **TN nonunion** — revise that joint, ✗ **ulcers under 2nd–3rd MT heads** — relative plantarflexion / 1st ray dorsiflexion)

**Chronic Lisfranc + progressive flatfoot (alcoholic, supple hindfoot)** — **midfoot arthrodesis** (✗ PT / AFO — PTTI first line, ✗ MCO + transfers + LCL — flexible flatfoot, ✗ triple — rigid arthritic hindfoot)

**Midfoot arthrodesis technique** — denude 1st–3rd TMT + bone graft · **reduce with windlass mechanism** · cast 6 wk → boot 6–12 wk → WBAT at 12 wk if healed

## X vs Y

| | **ORIF** | **Primary arthrodesis** |
|---|---|---|
| Best for | **Bony fracture-dislocation**, acute | **Purely ligamentous**, delayed, chronic, A / C2 |
| Hardware removal | **\~75%** | **\~20%** |
| Function | Equivalent overall | Better in ligamentous (Ly & Coetzee) |

| | **Medial + middle columns (1st–3rd TMT)** | **Lateral column (4th–5th TMT)** |
|---|---|---|
| Motion | Rigid lever for push-off | **Mobile** — uneven ground |
| Fixation | **Rigid screws / plate** or fuse | **Temporary K-wires** |
| Fusion | Acceptable | **Avoid — inferior function** |

| | **Lisfranc planovalgus** | **PTTI planovalgus** |
|---|---|---|
| History | Trauma, plantar ecchymosis | Insidious |
| Treatment | **Midfoot (TMT) arthrodesis** | PT / AFO → MCO + transfer + LCL → triple |
