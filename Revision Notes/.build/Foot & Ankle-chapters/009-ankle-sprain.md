# Ankle Sprain

*Foot & Ankle · 9 / 48*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Commonest reason** — **missed athletic participation**

**Dancers** — ankle sprain = **commonest injury** (Orthobullets) · inversion sprain = **commonest acute skeletal injury in dancers** (OB)

**Low vs high** — **low (ATFL ± CFL) > 90%** · **high (syndesmosis) 1–10%**

**Risk factors** — patient: **limited dorsiflexion, ↓ proprioception, poor balance** · environment: **indoor-court sports (basketball, volleyball)** highest

### Anatomy & examination – ATFL vs CFL

| Ligament | Attachments | Tight in / injured by | Test | Frequency · strength |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **ATFL** | **Anterior fibula → lateral talar neck** | **Plantar flexion + inversion** (parallel to foot axis in PF) | **Anterior drawer in plantar flexion** | **Commonest injured · weakest** |
| **CFL** | **Fibular tip → lateral calcaneal tubercle** | **Dorsiflexion + inversion** | **Drawer laxity in dorsiflexion · talar tilt (varus stress)** | **2nd commonest** |
| **PTFL** | **Distal fibula → lateral tubercle of posterior talus** | Rarely | Not tested by anterior drawer | **Least injured · strongest + deepest of lateral complex** (OB, TO) |

**Strength order** — **ATFL (weakest) < CFL < PTFL (strongest)**

**CFL crosses 2 joints** — contributes to **subtalar + ankle** stability → subtalar instability hard to tell from posterior ankle instability (✗ ATFL)

![Lateral ankle ligaments](../Orthobullets/attachments/ob7028-ankle-ligaments.jpg)

### Deltoid ligament (TO)

| Layer | Bands | Crosses | Limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Superficial (4)** | **Tibiospring** + **tibionavicular** (major) · superficial posterior tibiotalar + tibiocalcaneal (additional) | **Ankle + subtalar** | **Talar abduction** |
| **Deep (2)** | **Deep posterior tibiotalar (major)** · anterior deep tibiotalar | **Ankle only** | **External rotation** |

**Strongest tibiotalar / deltoid part (TO)** — **deep posterior tibiotalar ligament** (✗ anterior deep tibiotalar, ✗ "posterior tibiotalar", ✗ talonavicular) (TO)

**Pronation of talus** — superficial + deep **equally** limit it (OB)

**Function** — fan-shaped; resists **valgus + rotatory** forces · isolated deltoid injury **very rare**

### Syndesmosis anatomy (TO)

| Ligament | Course | Restrains |
|---|---|---|
| **AITFL** | **Chaput tubercle (anterolateral tibia) → Wagstaffe tubercle (anterior fibula)** | **Fibular external rotation** |
| **IOL** | Distal continuation of **interosseous membrane** | **Lateral fibular translation** |
| **PITFL** | **Volkmann tubercle (posterior tibia) → posterolateral fibula** | **Posterior fibular translation** · **strongest component** |
| **Inferior transverse ligament** | Distal to PITFL | **Posterior talar displacement** |

**PITFL > AITFL** — **PITFL stronger** (TO) (true statement in "false EXCEPT")

![Syndesmosis – AITFL, PITFL, transverse ligament, IOM](../MCQ/attachments/TO17141970-2.png)

### Peroneal weakness in dancers

**Relative peroneal weakness** → **inversion ankle sprain** (✗ fibular fracture, ✗ cuboid subluxation, ✗ Achilles rupture, ✗ midfoot sprain) · **restore full peroneal strength** to prevent recurrence

## 2 Mechanism

**Low sprain** — **inversion on a plantarflexed foot** · recurrent sprains → functional instability

**Sequence in inversion (TO)** — **anterolateral capsule → ATFL → CFL → PTFL** (✗ ATFL first then capsule) (TO) · ATFL = first or only ligament; total rupture adds CFL + PTFL (TO)

**Eversion (TO)** — **deltoid** injury

**Syndesmosis mechanism** — **hyperdorsiflexion** (TO) · **external rotation**, pain on forced **dorsiflexion + ER** (TO) · **pronation–ER > pronation–abduction > infrequently supination–ER** (Weber C / B) (TO) — read the options (✗ eversion + PF, ✗ inversion, ✗ hyperplantar flexion)

## 3 Presentation

**Symptoms** — pain on WB, swelling, ecchymosis · recurrent instability, **catching / popping** after recurrent sprains · "giving way"

**Anterior drawer** — talus translates anterior to tibia · seated, knee flexed over table edge, stabilise leg, pull heel forward · **10° PF** = greatest translation (OB; 20° PF in another OB stem) · **8–10 mm** = positive · **PF → ATFL**, **DF → CFL** · reflects ATFL ± CFL · limited use acutely

**Talar tilt** — tibia stabilised, ankle neutral, invert · **> 15° vs contralateral = ATFL + CFL** · **isolated ATFL cannot give + talar tilt** → CFL must be torn (✗ PTFL, ✗ AITFL / PITFL)

**Syndesmosis tests (TO)** — **squeeze** (compress tibia + fibula mid-calf → syndesmotic pain) · **fibular translation** (fibula forward/back) · **Cotton** (talus side-to-side, neutral) · **ER stress** (ER + DF, hip + knee 90°) · gross swelling **usually absent**; tenderness at anterior syndesmosis ± deltoid

**Cotton / hook test intra-op (TO)** — translation considered positive asked (0–1 / 1–2 / 2–3 / 3–4 mm) — **answer key not provided by the source** (TO)

### Associated injuries – talar bone bruise & deltoid

| Associated finding (acute lateral sprain MRI) | Rate |
|---|---|
| **Talar bone bruise** | **Commonest** |
| Talar + medial malleolar bruises | 8.1% |
| Peroneus brevis injury | 7.5% |
| Lateral malleolus tip avulsion | 2.7% |

**Other associations (Orthobullets)** — **OCD talus**, **peroneal tendon injuries**, **subtle cavovarus**, **anterior / anterolateral synovitis (commonest intra-articular)**, anterior impingement, deltoid injury, **CRPS**, fractures (**5th MT base, anterior process calcaneus, lateral / posterior process talus**)

**Medial pain, ER hurts medially, squeeze −, ER stress X-ray normal** — **deltoid ligament complex** (✗ inferior transverse ligament, ✗ AITFL — squeeze/X-ray normal, ✗ ATFL — usually recovers nonop, ✗ spring ligament — would give flatfoot)

**Deltoid injury** — nonop first; **reconstruct if instability persists**

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Diagnostic criteria – Ottawa ankle rules

| X-ray series | Pain zone | Any one of |
|---|---|---|
| **Ankle** | Malleolar zone | **Bone tenderness posterior edge / tip of lateral malleolus (distal 6 cm)** · **same for medial malleolus** · **inability to bear weight immediately + in ED** |
| **Foot** | Midfoot zone | **Bone tenderness 5th MT base** · **navicular** · inability to bear weight |

**Sensitivity** — **96–99%** for ruling out fracture

**✗ Not Ottawa criteria** — **swelling**, **ecchymosis** (medial or lateral), **antalgic gait / walking 10 ft** (only inability to bear weight counts), pain with hindfoot motion, **1st MT base tenderness** (peroneus longus), **deltoid / soft-tissue tenderness**

![Ottawa ankle rules](../Orthobullets/attachments/ob7028-ottawa-ankle-rules.jpg)

### Key radiology findings

| Study | Use / finding |
|---|---|
| **WB AP, lateral, mortise** | Lateral: **anterior talar translation = ATFL** |
| **ER stress view** | Syndesmosis: asymmetric mortise widening · **MCS > 4 mm** · **TF clear space 6 mm** |
| **Varus stress (talar tilt) view** | **CFL** · measures talar tilt |
| **WB stress X-ray (TO)** | Suggests instability in obscure syndesmotic cases; **guides management** |
| **MRI** | If pain **> 6–8 wk** · **peroneal tendons, OCD, syndesmosis** · not needed acutely (✗ intra-articular contrast) |
| **MRI syndesmosis (TO)** | **Gold standard** · **lambda sign = > 2 mm diastasis on coronal** (sensitive + specific) (TO) · also **hyperintense signal contiguous from mortise into tibiofibular joint** (OB) |
| Peroneal tendon pathology | **MRI** (✗ CT) |

### Syndesmotic radiographic parameters (TO)

| Parameter | Where | Normal | Injury |
|---|---|---|---|
| **TF clear space** | **1 cm above plafond**; medial fibula → lateral posterior tibial prominence (incisura) | **< 6 mm** (AP + mortise) · **< 5 mm** (Orthobullets ligament note) | **↑ > 6 mm** |
| **TF overlap** | 1 cm above plafond | **> 6 mm on AP** · **> 10 mm AP** (Orthobullets ligament note) | **↓** |
| **Medial clear space** | Mortise | **< 4 mm** | ↑ = lateral talar shift |
| Superior joint space | Mortise | Within 2 mm medially of lateral width | — |
| **Dime (ball) sign** | Fibular tip recess → lateral talar process | **Unbroken curve** | **Broken** |
| Shenton's line of ankle | Mortise | Intact | Broken |

**Best view for syndesmosis (TO)** — **AP** (mortise better for joint congruity) · **"TF overlap < 6 mm on mortise = unstable" is FALSE** (TO)

**Medial clear space** — **> 4 mm** abnormal (Orthobullets; TO normal < 4 mm) · **> 5 mm = deltoid tear → unstable syndesmosis** (TO) — read the options

### Low ankle sprain grading

| Grade | Ligament | Ecchymosis / swelling | Pain on WB | Treatment |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **I** | **No tear** | Minimal | Normal | **Functional rehab** |
| **II** | **Stretch without tear** | Moderate | Mild | **Functional rehab** |

### Syndesmotic injury classification (TO)

| By time | By stability |
|---|---|
| **Acute < 6 wk** | **Stable** — AITFL ± IOL tear, **deltoid intact** → nonop |
| **Subacute > 6 wk** | **Unstable** — **AITFL + IOL + deltoid** (MCS > 5 mm) → surgery |
| **Chronic > 6 months** | — |

## 5 Management

```
Acute low ankle sprain (grade I–III)
├─ Ottawa + → X-ray
├─ All grades → RICE → early functional rehab (brace, proprioception, peroneals)
│      (± ~1 wk WB boot; grade III ± 10 d cast)
└─ Pain / swelling > 6–8 wk → MRI (OCD, peroneals, syndesmosis)
```

```
Chronic lateral instability
├─ Untreated / under-treated → proprioception + peroneal strengthening first
├─ Failed nonop, normal alignment → modified Broström-Gould ± arthroscopy
├─ + cavovarus, Coleman corrects → 1st MT dorsiflexion osteotomy + reconstruction
└─ + fixed hindfoot varus → lateralising calcaneal osteotomy + reconstruction
```

### Operative criteria

| Situation | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Grade I–III, persistent pain + instability despite extensive nonop** | **Anatomic reconstruction (Broström-Gould)** or tendon transfer / tenodesis |
| **Grade I–III with bony avulsion** | Reconstruction |
| **Open ankle dislocation, gross lateral ligament disruption** | **Acute repair** (OB) |
| Recurrent sprains + **impingement lesion** (AITFL, posteromedial) | **Arthroscopic debridement** |
| **Syndesmosis with tibiofibular diastasis** | Fixation |
| **Peroneal tear > 50–60%**, irreparable, severe tendinopathy | **Peroneus brevis tenodesis** |

### Acute management – functional rehabilitation & proprioception

**Protocol** — **RICE + elastic wrap** → short (**≈ 1 wk / 7–10 d**) WB immobilisation (boot, Aircast, walking cast) if needed → **early functional rehab**: ROM → strengthening → **proprioception** (tilt-board) → sport-specific · **functional brace** (controls inversion + eversion) during strengthening + prophylactically in high-risk activity

**Quickest return to activity** — **early functional rehabilitation** · supervised PT helps early, no long-term difference · chronic instability **cannot be predicted** by history, exam or X-ray

**Recurrence reduction** — **functional bracing + early proprioceptive training** (✗ NWB cast, ✗ WB boot, ✗ splint, ✗ **neuromuscular training alone** — higher recurrence) · pathway to retrain = **proprioception** (✗ tactile, ✗ pressure, ✗ pain, ✗ motor)

**Proprioception roles (TO)** — EXCEPT stem: promotes fracture healing / maintains joint stability / prevents reinjury / improves neuromuscular control + balance — **answer key not provided by the source** (TO)

| | **Functional management** | **Cast immobilisation** |
|---|---|---|
| Return to sport | **Faster, more likely long-term** | **Longer time to RTS** |
| ROM | **Greater** | Less |
| Swelling | **Less** (short term) | More |
| Laxity on stress X-ray | **Less** | **More** |
| Satisfaction | **Higher** | Lower |

**FALSE about functional treatment** — "**greater risk of ankle laxity**" (it has **less**)

**Grade III (ATFL + CFL torn on MRI), no syndesmosis / avulsion** — **RICE, elastic wrap, then therapy** (✗ 6 wk cast, ✗ suture button, ✗ Broström-Gould, ✗ tenodesis)

**Acute ATFL tear on MRI, laxity PF + DF** — **early functional rehab, proprioception** (✗ Broström, ✗ Broström-Gould, ✗ Watson-Jones — weaved PB, ✗ Evans — non-weaved PB)

**Basketball rebound, ATFL on MRI** — **early WB, functional bracing, peroneal strengthening** (✗ sinus tarsi steroid + taping, ✗ suture-tape repair, ✗ peroneal autograft reconstruction, ✗ boot 4–6 wk — deconditioning + recurrence)

**After 7–10 d splint, + talar tilt + DF drawer** — **early functional rehabilitation** (✗ CT / MRI acutely, ✗ 2–4 wk more NWB cast, ✗ rigid walking boot; semirigid brace OK for grade III)

**Lateral sprain, 2 mm drawer, squeeze −, X-ray normal** — **functional bracing + PT** (✗ short leg cast 4 wk, ✗ MRI, ✗ Broström-Gould)

**Chronic untreated sprain (5 mo), stable drawer** — **proprioception + peroneal strengthening** (✗ NSAIDs alone, ✗ observation, ✗ boot, ✗ surgery before full nonop)

**PT success** — **> 80%** (OB)

### Chronic instability – Broström-Gould, reconstruction & rehabilitation

**Chronic instability** — **> 6 wk** · repeated episodes "giving way"

**Modified Broström-Gould** — **anatomic shortening + reinsertion (imbrication) of ATFL + CFL** · reinforced with **inferior extensor retinaculum + distal fibular periosteum** · **90% good–excellent** · commonest procedure; preferred in athletes with normal alignment (✗ Evans, ✗ allograft fibula → 5th MT, ✗ added osteotomy if aligned)

**Gould modification facts** — **no biomechanical difference in ankle stability** (✗ superior extensor retinaculum, ✗ adds PTFL, ✗ ↑ nerve injury, ✗ ↓ ROM at 6 wk)

**Primary repair works even when chronic** — ligaments stay attached to capsule · **graft / internal brace = for failed primary repair** · no technique proven superior

**Suture-tape augmentation** — ↑ tensile strength, no better clinical outcome · **over-tightening → ↓ dorsiflexion** (✗ inversion, ✗ eversion, ✗ PF, ✗ pronation)

**Tenodesis (Watson-Jones, Chrisman-Snook, Colville, Evans)** — **nonanatomic** tendon transfer · **subtalar stiffness** · alters biomechanics → discouraged · **correct any malalignment** first

**Arthroscopy with Broström** — **high incidence of intra-articular pathology** (OCD talus, anterior impingement, synovitis) → **ankle arthroscopy** is the add-on (✗ subtalar fusion, ✗ peroneal debridement if no eversion pain, ✗ 1st ray / Dwyer osteotomy if aligned)

**Instability + anterior impingement** — **arthroscopic debridement + ATFL + CFL reinsertion with inferior extensor retinaculum + fibular periosteum** (✗ PTFL, ✗ superior extensor retinaculum)

| Post-op mobilisation | **Early (< 3 wk)** | **Delayed (> 3 wk)** |
|---|---|---|
| **Functional scores** | **Better** | Worse |
| Ankle laxity (drawer, tilt) | **↑** | Less |
| **Wound complications** | **↑** | Less |
| Return to sport | Same | Same |

### Failure factors – cavovarus / hindfoot alignment & its correction

**Commonest reason for failure** — **hindfoot malalignment (native hindfoot varus)** · normal = **5–7° valgus**, axis through medial calcaneus · varus loads lateral ligaments + peroneals

**Predispose to failure** — **hindfoot varus + 1st ray plantarflexion + cavovarus** = **all of the above** (✗ planovalgus — strains medial side, ✗ low demand, ✗ anatomic reconstruction, ✗ pre-op acute traumatic instability — all ↓ failure)

**✗ Traps** — baseline laxity (Beighton) · **excessively pronated** subtalar (varus foot **supinates**) · **fixed dorsiflexion** of 1st ray (it's **plantarflexion**) · planovalgus

**Subtle cavovarus** — **mild heel varus + plantarflexed 1st ray** → lateral column overload, **stress fractures**, **peroneal tendon + lateral ligament injury** · forefoot-driven (plantarflexed 1st MT) or hindfoot-driven (tarsal coalition)

**Orthotic for cavovarus** — **full-length semirigid**, **lateral hindfoot (+ forefoot) wedge / posting**, **1st MT head recess**, **lowered medial arch** (✗ UCBL, ✗ medial hindfoot posting ± rigid arch — planovalgus, ✗ lace-up brace alone)

| Coleman block | Driver | Add to ligament reconstruction |
|---|---|---|
| **Corrects (flexible hindfoot)** | **Forefoot (plantarflexed 1st ray)** | **1st MT dorsiflexion osteotomy** (± **plantar fascia release**) |
| **Negative (fixed hindfoot varus)** | Hindfoot | **Lateralising (valgus) calcaneal osteotomy (Dwyer)** |

**Cavovarus + recurrent sprains, Coleman corrects, failed orthotics** — **1st MT osteotomy + lateral ligament reconstruction (peroneus brevis graft)** (✗ sinus tarsi steroid, ✗ ligament repair alone, ✗ triple arthrodesis)

**Lowest recurrence, flexible cavovarus** — **reconstruction + plantar fascia release + 1st MT dorsiflexion osteotomy** (✗ reconstruction alone, ✗ syndesmosis repair, ✗ FDL transfer + MDCO — planovalgus)

**Cavovarus, Coleman negative, failed nonop** — **lateralising calcaneal osteotomy** with Broström (✗ syndesmotic fixation, ✗ post-op PT / orthosis / IER reinforcement — less important)

**X-ray that predisposes to recurrent sprains** — **hindfoot varus** (✗ pes planus, ✗ syndesmotic widening — result not cause, ✗ talar OCD — result, ✗ tibiotalar osteophytes)

### Associated peroneal tendon pathology

**Persistent lateral pain after Broström, drawer −, normal labs + X-ray** — **peroneus brevis (longitudinal split) tear** (✗ infection, ✗ repair failure, ✗ PL tear, ✗ arthritis — takes > 3 mo) · linked to **SPR laxity**

**Pain with resisted eversion after sprain** — **MRI** (✗ immobilise 4 wk, ✗ more PT, ✗ steroid, ✗ CT)

| PB tear | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Small / ~30% split, repairable** | **Debridement, repair + tubularisation** |
| **> 50–60%, irreparable, severe tendinopathy** (e.g. only **35% intact**) | **Peroneus brevis tenodesis** (✗ nothing, ✗ groove deepening, ✗ SPR imbrication alone) |
| + instability | Reconstruct SPR, flatten fibular ridge / **deepen groove** |

**After SPR release + repair** — **avoid resisted eversion** early (✗ inversion, PF, pronation, supination) → else peroneal subluxation

### High ankle (syndesmotic) sprain – mechanism, tests & fixation (TO)

**Stable (TO)** — **nonop**: RICE + **NWB immobilisation 2 wk** → brace, ROM, strength → neuromuscular + proprioception

**Unstable (TO)** — **surgery**: **syndesmotic screw or suture button ± AITFL repair** · **deltoid + AITFL tear = indication for surgery** (TO)

**Predicting IOM tear / instability in bimalleolar fracture (TO)** — **intraoperative stress testing** (✗ fibular fracture level, ✗ Lauge-Hansen class, ✗ medial clear space — widens with deltoid + fibula without IOM tear) (TO)

| Syndesmotic fixation (TO) | |
|---|---|
| **Indicated** | **Proximal fibular fracture not fixed + unstabilisable medial injury** · **disruption > 5 cm above plafond** |
| **Not needed** | **< 3 cm above plafond** · medial malleolus fixed / deltoid repaired · no medial injury (✗ "fix all") |
| Controversial | **3–5 cm**, deltoid not repaired |
| Mechanics altered | **> 4.5 cm** with medial lesion |

**Screw position (TO)** — **2–3 cm above plafond**, **parallel to joint**, **angled 30° anteriorly** (perpendicular to tibiofibular joint) · **2–5 cm** (Orthobullets syndesmosis note) — read the options (✗ 3 cm + 30° posterior, ✗ 10–15° cephalad / caudad)

| Screw error (TO) | Result |
|---|---|
| Too proximal | **Deforms fibula → mortise widens** |
| Not parallel | **Fibula shifts proximally** |
| Not perpendicular | **Fibula stays laterally displaced** |

**Screw type (TO)** — AO: **fully threaded, neutralisation** · others: lag more secure · **max dorsiflexion during fixation** traditional, but may induce **ER moment → malreduction**

| **Grade I / II** | **1–2 wk** |

## 6 Complications

**Persistent symptoms** — **up to 30%** (Orthobullets) · **up to 40%** residual symptoms (OB) — read the options · pain at 1 yr **5–33%** · pain falls fast in first 2 wk · ↑ risk of sprain both ankles

**Commonest cause of chronic pain** — **missed injury**: fractures (**anterior process calcaneus, lateral / posterior process talus, 5th MT**), **OCD**, **peroneal tendons**, **syndesmosis**, **tarsal coalition**, impingement, **hindfoot varus**

**Impingement** — **anteroinferior tibiofibular ligament** + posteromedial lesions → arthroscopic debridement

### Complications – SPN neurapraxia & CRPS

**SPN stretch neurapraxia** — 4 wk after grade III sprain: **burning, exquisite light-touch tenderness anterolateral ankle / dorsolateral foot**, worse with **PF + inversion**, ATFL torn (✗ DPN, ✗ deltoid, ✗ sural, ✗ saphenous) · SPN injury **1.6–4.4%** · **> 80% of grade III sprains have EMG evidence of tibial or CPN injury** (OB)

**Neuropathic drug** — **gabapentin / pregabalin**: **↓ hyperexcitability of voltage-dependent presynaptic calcium channels** · TCA (amitriptyline) also

| ✗ Drug mechanism | Drug |
|---|---|
| Intracellular **Na channel** block | Lidocaine, bupivacaine |
| **Mu agonist** | Opioids |
| **Direct GABA** | Diazepam, baclofen |
| **COX-2** selective (not COX-1) | Anti-inflammatory |

**Nerve complication of lateral ligament reconstruction** — **SPN (commonest) → paraesthesia proximal dorsum of foot**

| Nerve | Sensory / motor sign |
|---|---|
| **SPN** | **Dorsum of foot, anterolateral ankle / distal leg** ✓ |
| **DPN** | **1st dorsal web space** ± TA / EHL weakness |
| **Sural** | **Posterolateral ankle + lateral foot** |
| **Tibial** | **Can't plantarflex** |
| **Saphenous** | **Anteromedial leg / medial hindfoot** |

**CRPS** — listed association of ankle sprain
