# Ankle Arthritis

*Foot & Ankle · 5 / 48*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology & etiology

**Incidence** — **30 / 100,000 / yr** · prevalence **\~1%** · less common than hip / knee OA

**Post-traumatic** — **commonest, 75–80%** · mostly **ankle fractures**; rest = **chronic (lateral) ligamentous laxity**

**Primary OA** — **< 10%**

**Other causes** — **RA / inflammatory**, osteonecrosis, neuropathic, septic, gout, haemophilia

**Risk factors** — prior ankle fracture · chronic ligamentous laxity · inflammatory arthropathy

**Pathoanatomy** — nonanatomic fracture healing → altered contact forces + load bearing → cartilage loss on **talar body + plafond** → narrowing, subchondral sclerosis, **eburnation**

### Anatomy

**Joint** — **ginglymus** (tibia, talus, fibula) · talar dome **biconcave with central sulcus**

**ROM** — **dorsiflexion 20°** · **plantarflexion 50°**

## 3 Presentation

**Symptoms** — pain with weight-bearing · stiffness · locking / catching

**Exam** — variable effusion · pain + ↓ ROM vs contralateral · angular deformity (trauma) · crepitus

**Localising sign (OB)** — pain through tibiotalar arc · **painless subtalar inversion / eversion** → isolated tibiotalar disease (→ no need to fuse subtalar)

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Modality | Use / finding |
|---|---|
| **Weight-bearing AP, lateral, mortise** | Diagnosis: **joint space loss**, subchondral sclerosis + cysts, eburnation, angular deformity |
| **CT** | **Surgical planning** (arthrodesis + arthroplasty); quantify healing of a fusion nonunion |
| **MRI** | Focal cartilage disease · **↑ sensitivity in early disease** · cartilage injury, subchondral marrow oedema, ligament tears |
| Labelled WBC scan | Infection workup in fusion nonunion |
| Scanograms / contralateral films | Malalignment planning before revision |

### Takakura classification (mortise view, varus-type OA)

|---|---|---|
| **I** | Osteophytes + early sclerosis, **no narrowing** | ![Takakura I](../Orthobullets/attachments/ob7037-stage-i.jpg) |
| **II** | **Narrowing of medial joint space**, no subchondral contact | ![Takakura II](../Orthobullets/attachments/ob7037-stage-ii.jpg) |
| **IIIA** | Obliteration **at medial malleolus**, subchondral bone contact | ![Takakura IIIA](../Orthobullets/attachments/ob7037-stage-iiia.jpg) |
| **IIIB** | Obliteration **over roof of talar dome**, subchondral contact | ![Takakura IIIB](../Orthobullets/attachments/ob7037-stage-iiib.jpg) |
| **IV** | Obliteration with **complete tibiotalar contact** | ![Takakura IV](../Orthobullets/attachments/ob7037-stage-iv.jpg) |

| Stage | Treatment (OB) |
|---|---|
| **I** | Conservative / symptomatic |
| **II, IIIA** | **Supramalleolar osteotomy** (joint preserving) |
| **IIIB** | SMO **poor results / higher failure** → TAA or arthrodesis |
| **IV** | **Arthrodesis** or **TAA** (whole joint) |

### Talar osteonecrosis (differential)

**Late talar head osteonecrosis (rare)** — **talonavicular + subtalar** pain (selective injections) · X-ray / CT = advanced degenerative change of TN + subtalar joints

**MRI** — **↓ T1, ↑ T2 with dark serpiginous line**

**✗ Look-alikes** — **PVNS** (erosions + hemosiderin) · **chronic osteomyelitis** (fluid collections, bony destruction) · talar neck / head fracture (no fracture line)

## 5 Management

```
Ankle arthritis
├─ Mild / first line → NSAIDs, activity change, Arizona brace, rocker sole, steroid injection
├─ Anterior osteophytes, isolated impingement → arthroscopic debridement + exostectomy
├─ Asymmetric (Takakura II–IIIA), malaligned, near-normal ROM → supramalleolar osteotomy
├─ Young, moderate–severe PTOA, good ROM → distraction arthroplasty
└─ End-stage (IIIB–IV)
       ├─ Young high-demand labourer, obese, neuropathic → ankle arthrodesis
       └─ Elderly, low demand, inflammatory, contralateral fusion → total ankle arthroplasty
```

### Nonoperative management – bracing, rocker sole & injection

**First line** — activity modification, **bracing to immobilise ankle**, NSAIDs · **intra-articular corticosteroid = mainstay injection** · PRP / hyaluronic acid **no consensus** (PRP not proven)

**Untried nonop (OB)** — **steroid injection + cushioned rocker-bottom shoe** (± cane) (✗ opiates + limited WB 6 wk, ✗ cast NWB 12 wk, ✗ triple / pan-talar arthrodesis)

**Arizona (gauntlet) brace** — **immobilises ankle + hindfoot** → best brace for tibiotalar OA at any age · PT may worsen pain

| Orthosis / shoe | Indication |
|---|---|
| **Single rocker sole** | **End-stage tibiotalar (or subtalar) arthritis with limited motion**, or after ankle / subtalar fusion · ↑ toe-off propulsion, ↓ heel-strike pressure, ↓ ankle motion needed |
| **Double rocker sole** | **Midfoot prominences (Charcot)**, midfoot OA |
| **Severe angle rocker** | **Toe-tip ulcers** |
| **Negative heel rocker** | **Fixed ankle dorsiflexion** deformity |
| **CROW boot** | Charcot / midfoot |
| **Lateral hindfoot post** | **Cavovarus** foot |
| **Hinged AFO** | **Foot drop** — does **not** immobilise ankle |
| Rocker sole ✗ | Fixed planovalgus |

### Arthroscopic debridement & distraction arthroplasty

**Debridement + anterior tibial / dorsal talar exostectomy** — **isolated anterior impingement** from anterior osteophytes · joint preserving · open option (plan future approaches) · **not for advanced arthritis** (✗ won't correct alignment)

**Distraction arthroplasty** — **young**, moderate–severe **post-traumatic** OA · offloading → biologic repair · **needs good pre-op ROM** · static / hinged ex-fix or spatial frame ± microfracture, chondroplasty, orthobiologics · outcomes controversial

### Supramalleolar osteotomy – indications (varus, asymmetric arthritis)

**Indications** — **near-normal ROM** · **minimal talar tilt** · **varus heel alignment** · **medially focused (asymmetric) arthritis = Takakura II / IIIA** · tibiotalar malalignment, eccentric wear · young active patient wanting high activity

**Biggest change in ankle forces** — **tibial osteotomy** (✗ deltoid / lateral ligament reconstruction, ✗ calcaneal osteotomy, ✗ PL → PB transfer — adjuncts only)

| Deformity | Osteotomy |
|---|---|
| **Varus** | **Medial opening wedge** (or lateral closing wedge) |
| **Valgus** | **Medial closing wedge** (or lateral opening wedge) ± **oblique fibular lengthening osteotomy** |
| Dome | Less translation / length change; technically hard |
| Multiplanar / large | **Gradual correction with spatial frame** |

**Fibular osteotomy** — outcomes **same** whether or not needed

**Medial opening wedge best for** — **symptomatic medial narrowing + varus alignment** (✗ stage IV valgus → fusion, ✗ stage I → conservative, ✗ lateral narrowing + neutral alignment → look at knee / hip, ✗ IIIB → poor results)

| Preoperative talar tilt | Five-year survival rate |
|---|---|
| **4–10°** | **85%** |
| **> 10°** | **65%** (higher failure; also Takakura IIIB) |

**Contraindications** — **< 50% preserved tibiotalar surface** · **age > 70** · hindfoot instability not fixable by ligament reconstruction · significant vascular disease · **inflammatory arthritis** · **neuroarthropathy** · (✗ **age < 50 is NOT** a contraindication)

**Complications** — **reoperation for hardware removal commonest**, then nonunion

### Operative criteria – arthrodesis vs TAA

| | **Ankle arthrodesis** | **Total ankle arthroplasty** |
|---|---|---|
| Indications | Moderate–severe **PTOA or inflammatory**, refractory · **young, high-demand labourers** · malalignment (+ osteotomy) · comorbidities | PTOA or **inflammatory (RA)** · **elderly**, low demand · ? concomitant TN / subtalar fusion · **contralateral ankle fusion** · wants motion (hiking, uneven ground) |
| Relative contraindications | **Vascular compromise (ABI 0.4, claudication)** — poor wound + fusion healing | **Obesity** (✗ not absolute, BMI 35) · **young labourers** · severe deformity |
| Absolute contraindications | — | **Active infection** · inadequate bone stock / **severe osteoporosis** · **talus osteonecrosis** · **Charcot / neuropathic joint** · **peripheral neuropathy** |
| Other contraindications (OB) | — | Uncorrectable deformity · ankle instability · neurologic muscle imbalance · severe malalignment |

### Arthrodesis vs total ankle arthroplasty – gait, adjacent joints

**Not TAA contraindications** — post-traumatic arthritis (commonest indication) · inflammatory arthritis / RA · age > 50 · reconstructible ligament damage · **correctable coronal deformity (up to 20–30°)** via osteotomy / soft-tissue balancing (TO: **> 10–15° = contraindication** — read the options) · **preserved subtalar motion = favourable**

**Peripheral neuropathy** — no protective sensation → microtrauma, instability, wounds, infection, **early loosening**

**Arthrodesis** — **gold standard** for end-stage OA · reliable pain relief + ADLs · **union \~90%** (Orthobullets) · **93–94% with cancellous screws**, 74% overall (OB)

**Arthrodesis position** — **neutral dorsiflexion**, **5–10° external rotation** (10° OB / TO), **\~5° valgus** (0–5° OB), **talus \~5 mm posterior**

**Young labourer (28 yo, BMI 31, construction)** — **ankle fusion** (✗ low-profile TAA, ✗ bipolar allograft, ✗ interposition, ✗ arthroscopic debridement — too advanced)

**Healthy 66-yo hiker, subtalar pain-free** — **TAA** (✗ TTC nail — spare subtalar, ✗ tibiotalar fusion → younger, ✗ distraction → younger, moderate)

**Large OCD + 5° varus, 41 yo** — joint / cartilage preservation first

| Function (TAA vs arthrodesis) | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Gait** | TAA patients: **superior gait mechanics compared to AA post-operatively** — **↑ stride length, cadence, stride / gait velocity** → arthrodesis = **↓ stride length** (✗ hip abduction, ✗ leg length) |
| **Slopes / stairs** | TAA **higher scores walking uphill, upstairs, downstairs** |
| Uneven surfaces at 12 mo | **Both improve** vs pre-op |
| Gait function at 12 mo | Arthrodesis **improves** it (✗ "loss of gait function") |
| **Bilateral arthrodesis** | Bad for gait → TAA for the second ankle |

**TAA outcomes** — up to **90% good–excellent at 5–10 yr** (Orthobullets) · **\~90% survivorship at 10 yr** (OB) · **> 80% survivorship at 5 yr if < 50 yo** (Orthobullets) · survivorship ↓ if **< 54 yo** (OB) · **70–98% at 3–6 yr, 80–95% at 8–12 yr** (OB, Easley) · **5-yr up to 60%, reoperation up to 30%** (another OB) — read the options · long-term data pending

**Arthrodesis long-term** — **ipsilateral midfoot / hindfoot degeneration** (✗ revision surgery, ✗ hardware failure, ✗ walking aids, ✗ knee / hip DJD)

**Subtalar arthrosis after arthrodesis** — **50% at 10 yr** (Orthobullets) · **> 50% within 10 yr** (OB) · **higher after AA than TAA** (✗ "rare after well-done fusion")

### Arthrodesis & arthroplasty technique

**Arthrodesis approaches** — anterior · **transfibular (8–10 cm distal fibula resected)** · mini-open (burr) · arthroscopic

**Arthrodesis fixation** — transarticular compression screws · bridge plate · ex-fix · **hindfoot retrograde nail (TTC)**

**TAA approach** — anterior, **between tibialis anterior + EHL** · protect **SPN, DPN, anterior tibial artery**

**TAA design** — fixed vs mobile bearing · adjuncts: **TAL / gastrocnemius recession**, foot osteotomies, ligament reconstruction

### Failed fusion or talar AVN – revision arthrodesis

**Workup** — exclude **infection + metabolic** causes · plain films · CT (bony healing) · **labelled WBC scan** · scanograms if malaligned

**Nonunion, subtalar pain-free** — **revision ankle arthrodesis + bone graft** with **stable compression fixation** (transfibular approach + compression screws) · **spare subtalar** (young)

**✗ Nonunion traps** — external / internal bone stimulator · **dynamisation → instability** · PRP · bone grafting alone · hardware removal + **IM rod / hindfoot nail (TTC)** when subtalar is normal

**Revision union** — **≥ 85%**

## 6 Complications

| Procedure | Complication |
|---|---|
| **Arthrodesis** | **Subtalar arthrosis 50% at 10 yr** · **nonunion 10%** |
| Nonunion risk factors | **Smoking**, adjacent joint fusion, **failed previous arthrodesis**, **AVN** |
| **TAA** | **Intra-op medial malleolus fracture** (prophylactic pinning) · PJI · aseptic loosening · **periprosthetic cysts** · wound + deep infection · osteolysis · periprosthetic fracture |
| Periprosthetic cysts | Infection or aseptic loosening · small asymptomatic → **observe** · large / progressive / painful → **debridement + bone graft** or **revision TAA** |
| **SMO** | Hardware removal (commonest reoperation) → nonunion |

### TAA complication – syndesmotic nonunion

**Pain 10 mo after TAA, stable ligaments, syndesmotic screws** — **syndesmotic nonunion** → **↑ tibial component loosening** (✗ poly wear loosening, ✗ lateral ligament failure, ✗ "normal at 10 mo", ✗ wrong procedure)

## X vs Y

| | **Arthrodesis** | **TAA** |
|---|---|---|
| Patient | **Young labourer**, obese, neuropathic, vasculopath | **Elderly**, RA, low demand |
| Gait | ↓ stride length | **↑ stride length, cadence, velocity**, uphill |
| Adjacent joints | **Subtalar 50% at 10 yr**, midfoot / hindfoot DJD | Preserved |
| Talar osteonecrosis | Option | **Contraindicated** |

| | **Supramalleolar osteotomy** | **Fusion / TAA** |
|---|---|---|
| Takakura | **II–IIIA** (asymmetric, medial) | **IIIB–IV** (whole joint) |
| Talar tilt | Minimal (> 10° → 65% survival) | Any |
| Ages | Young OK (< 50 not CI); **> 70 CI** | — |
