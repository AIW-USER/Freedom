# Septic Arthritis - Adult

*Trauma · 55 / 67*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Joints (descending)** — **knee (\~50%)** > hip > shoulder > elbow > ankle > **sternoclavicular** (IV drug users)

**Risk factors** — **age > 80**, diabetes, RA, cirrhosis, HIV, crystal arthropathy, endocarditis / recent bacteraemia, **IV drug use**, recent joint surgery, prosthetic implant

### Pathophysiology

**Seeding** — **bacteraemia** · **direct inoculation** (trauma, surgery) · **contiguous spread** (adjacent osteomyelitis)

**Cartilage destruction** — irreversible; **begins by 8 hours** · proteolytic enzymes from **PMNs**

**S. aureus vs gonococcus — joint destruction** — **S. aureus → greater WBC influx (> 50,000)** (✗ filamentous appendages — that is gonococcal adhesion; S. aureus uses MSCRAMMs, ✗ IL-1 inhibitors / endotoxin — both organisms induce IL-1 + have endotoxin activity, ✗ penicillin sensitivity)

### Organisms

| Organism | Setting |
|---|---|
| **Staphylococcus aureus** | **Commonest (> 50%)** in all patients, **including IV drug users** · gram +ve cocci in **grape-like clusters** |
| **Neisseria gonorrhoeae** | **\~20%** · commonest in **healthy sexually active adolescents / young adults** · **intracellular + extracellular gram −ve diplococci** |
| Gram −ve bacilli (E. coli, Proteus, Klebsiella, Enterobacter) | **10–20%** · neonates, IV drug users, elderly, immunocompromised diabetics |
| Streptococcus (Group A pyogenes commonest; Group B agalactiae) | Gram +ve cocci in **chains** · Group B → infants, elderly, diabetics |
| **Pseudomonas aeruginosa** | **IV drug abuse** (commonest SC organism in the **1980s**) |
| Propionibacterium acnes | **Shoulder surgery** |
| Salmonella / Strep. pneumoniae | **Sickle cell disease** |
| Bartonella henselae | **HIV** |
| Pasteurella multocida | **Dog / cat bite** |
| Eikenella corrodens | **Human bite** |
| Fungal / Candida | Immunocompromised |

### Sternoclavicular septic arthritis

**Frequency** — **\~1% of septic joints** · IV drug users, **diabetes**, renal insufficiency / **haemodialysis**, immunocompromise, alcoholism, HIV

**Organism** — **S. aureus** — even in IV drug users (✗ Pseudomonas — ↑ incidence but not commonest, ✗ Strep. pneumoniae, ✗ S. epidermidis, ✗ P. acnes, ✗ gonococcus) · gonococcal, fungal, candida in HIV

**Presenting symptom** — **chest wall tenderness** (✗ shoulder pain — much less often)

**Imaging** — **CT / MRI routinely before surgery** → **retrosternal abscess, chest wall phlegmon**, extent of osteomyelitis (may need general / cardiothoracic surgeon) · MRI better; CT if MRI unavailable

**Treatment** — **I&D + antibiotics**; **aspiration alone ineffective** · with **medial clavicular osteolysis (osteomyelitis) → resection of SC joint** (best outcome, lowest recurrence) (✗ antibiotics alone, ✗ repeat aspiration, ✗ hyperbaric O₂, ✗ simple I&D — high recurrence)

![Sternoclavicular septic arthritis](../Orthobullets/attachments/ob1058-sc-joint-septic-arthritis-moved.jpg)

### Septic olecranon / prepatellar bursitis

**Organism** — **S. aureus \~80%** → **gram +ve cocci in pairs and clusters** (✗ chains — Strep, ✗ branching bacilli — Nocardia / Actinomyces, ✗ gram −ve diplococci — Neisseria, ✗ thin rods — E. coli) · mixed flora common

**Route** — **direct inoculation** (poor bursal blood supply) · middle-aged men

## 3 Presentation

**Symptoms** — joint pain · **fever in only 60%** · may look toxic

**Exam** — erythema, effusion, warmth, tenderness · **can't bear weight**, **can't tolerate PROM** · limb held in position of **maximum joint volume** → **hip in FABER**

![Hip held in FABER](../Orthobullets/attachments/ob1058-faber-aafp-org.jpg)

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Study | Finding |
|---|---|
| **Radiographs** (AP + lateral) | Joint space widening / effusion, periarticular osteopenia |
| **Ultrasound** | Confirms effusion in **large joints (hip)**; guides aspiration |
| **MRI** | Effusion + adjacent **osteomyelitis** |
| CT | Intra-articular air (traumatic arthrotomy adjunct) · SC joint abscess |

![Lateral knee — septic effusion](../Orthobullets/attachments/ob1058-lateral-knee-septic-effusion.jpg)

### Diagnostic criteria

| Test | Septic value |
|---|---|
| Serum WBC | **> 10,000** with left shift |
| **ESR** | **> 30 mm/h** · may be normal early; rises in 2 days, may rise 3–5 days after antibiotics, normal by 3–4 wk |
| **CRP** | **> 1 mg/dL** · **most helpful** · rises within hours, **normalises within 1 wk** → **best to monitor treatment** |
| **Synovial WBC** | **> 50,000** (native joint) = diagnostic · lower counts still infected if gram stain / culture +ve |
| Synovial PMN | High (e.g. 90%) |
| Gram stain | Identifies organism only **⅓ of the time** |
| Glucose | **< 60% of serum** |
| Viscosity | **Negative "string" sign** (low viscosity) |

**Aspiration** — **gold standard**; send **cell count + differential, gram stain, culture, glucose, crystals**

**Antibiotics within 24 h before arthrocentesis** — **↓ synovial WBC → false negative**

**Crystals + septic** — coexist in **< 5%** · **crystals do NOT exclude infection**

**Septic shock** — hypotension needing **vasopressors to keep MAP ≥ 65 mmHg** + **lactate > 2 mmol/L** despite fluids → mortality **> 40%** · aspirate, then **immediate broad-spectrum IV antibiotics**

![Negative string sign](../Orthobullets/attachments/ob1058-string-sign.jpg)

### Diagnosis — aspiration & coexisting crystals

**Red, hot 1st MTP, ↑ ESR / CRP** — **joint aspiration first** (septic vs gout) (✗ I&D before diagnosis, ✗ oral antibiotics, ✗ indomethacin before aspiration, ✗ MRI)

**IV drug user, ankle WBC 95,000 + negatively birefringent crystals** — **urgent I&D** (✗ await culture, ✗ 24 h IV antibiotics then reassess, ✗ analgesia, ✗ ESR / CRP / urate) · gout / pseudogout does not remove need for surgery

**Diabetic bacteraemia → septic knee, septic shock** — **S. aureus** (✗ gonococcus, ✗ gram −ve bacilli, ✗ Strep. pyogenes, ✗ Pseudomonas)

### Traumatic arthrotomy — saline load test

| Saline into knee | Sensitivity (1 cm inferolateral wound) |
|---|---|
| 100 mL | **< 75%** — insufficient |
| **110 mL** | **75%** |
| 145 mL | 90% |
| **155 mL** | **95%** |
| **175 mL** | **99%** |

**Technique** — **18 G** needle, saline with **passive ROM** · **egress through wound = positive** (arthrotomy)

**Traps** — **methylene blue does NOT ↑ sensitivity** · **exam alone performs worse** than SLT · **CT → intra-articular air** (adjunct; ✗ MRI routinely)

**100 mL, no egress, wound near patella** — **inject at least another 55 mL** (✗ close + discharge, ✗ arthroscopy yet, ✗ MRI, ✗ secondary intention)

**Positive** — surgical I&D (open / arthroscopic) to prevent septic arthritis

### Differential

**Crystal arthropathy** (gout, pseudogout) · **cellulitis** · **bursitis** (prepatellar)

## 5 Management

```
Suspected native-joint septic arthritis
├─ Aspirate first (cell count, gram, culture, glucose, crystals)
├─ WBC > 50,000 / organisms → urgent open or arthroscopic I&D + IV antibiotics
│      (crystals present → still I&D)
├─ Gonococcal → aspiration / arthroscopic drainage + ceftriaxone
│      └─ not responding → open I&D
└─ SC joint with osteolysis → CT / MRI, then joint resection
```

**Emergency** — orthopaedic surgical emergency; damage within **8 hours**

**Empirical antibiotics** — **young healthy: S. aureus + N. gonorrhoeae** · **immunocompromised: S. aureus + Pseudomonas** → switch to culture-directed

**Monitoring** — serum WBC, ESR, **CRP**

**Technique** — open or arthroscopic · remove all pus, irrigate · **synovectomy** as needed · fluid + tissue for culture

### Gonococcal arthritis

**Antibiotic** — **ceftriaxone** (3rd-gen cephalosporin; or cefotaxime / ceftizoxime) (✗ penicillin, ✗ tetracycline — high resistance, ✗ erythromycin, ✗ vancomycin) · **fluoroquinolones** if sensitive · **+ doxycycline** where chlamydia common

**Forms** — **arthritis-dermatitis syndrome (\~60%)** · localised purulent arthritis (\~40%) · bacteraemic infection; newborns + adolescents

**Disseminated (polyarthralgia + trunk rash, gram −ve diplococci)** — **serial aspiration + antibiotics** (✗ open I&D first — reserved for non-responders / severe, ✗ I&D without antibiotics, ✗ colchicine, ✗ observation)

**Workup** — screen for **HIV, syphilis, chlamydia** · response → fever, pain, leucocytosis resolve

**Clue** — young, sexually active, new vaginal discharge → take sexual history

### Paediatric septic hip — Kocher criteria & urgent washout (TO)

| Criterion (1 point each) (TO) | In original Kocher? |
|---|---|
| **Fever > 38.5 °C** | **Yes** |
| **ESR > 40** | **Yes** |
| **WBC > 12,000/mm³** | **Yes** |
| **Refusal to bear weight** | **Yes** |
| **CRP** | **No — added later by Caird** |

**Sensitivity order (TO)** — **fever > CRP > ESR > refusal to bear weight > WBC** · **CRP most sensitive lab**

**5-yr, fever 38.5 °C, ↑ CRP / WBC, effusion (TO)** — **emergency aspiration + washout, then IV antibiotics** (✗ blood cultures + antibiotics, ✗ US-guided aspiration + antibiotics, ✗ antibiotics alone) · **US = investigation of choice** for the painful child's hip · arthrotomy gives tissue for culture

**4-yr, fever, ↑ ESR / CRP, hip collection on US (TO)** — answer key not provided by the source

### Metaphyseal osteomyelitis & joint spread (TO)

**Intracapsular metaphyses (TO)** — **proximal radius, proximal humerus, proximal femur, distal tibia + fibula** → osteomyelitis decompresses into joint → septic arthritis

**EXCEPT (TO)** — **head (distal end) of ulna**

### Tuberculosis of joints (TO)

**Rice / millet / melon seed bodies (TO)** — **tuberculous synovitis / tenosynovitis** (✗ PVNS, ✗ RA, ✗ Madura foot)

**First radiographic sign of healing (TO)** — **remineralisation of bone** (trabecular pattern, ↓ osteopenia) (✗ joint space reappears, ✗ sequestrum removal, ✗ bony ankylosis) · spine: + sharpened disc margins, ↓ paravertebral shadow, fusion

**TB hip (TO)** — **second commonest osteoarticular TB** · usually ends in **fibrous ankylosis** · advanced → **FADIR** usually; complete iliofemoral ligament resorption → **FABER**

**FALSE statement (TO)** — "**Shanmugasundaram classification applies only to paediatric hip TB**" — it applies to **children and adults**

| Shanmugasundaram (1983, radiological) (TO) | Feature |
|---|---|
| Normal | Synovial disease |
| **Travelling / wandering acetabulum** | Roof lesion → head migrates upward |
| Dislocating | Posterior dislocation / subluxation (lax capsule) |
| Perthes type | Fragmented, dense, flat epiphysis |
| **Protrusio acetabuli** | Floor lesion → medial migration |
| Atrophic | Marked joint narrowing |
| **Mortar & pestle** | Head / acetabulum destroyed → mismatch |
| Unclassified (Agarwal, 8th) | Triradiate, pseudarthrosis coxae, ankylosed |

**Other TB hip classifications (TO)** — **Babhulkar + Pande 2002** (clinico-radiological) · Tuli modification · **Moon 2012**: **stable** — Perthes, atrophic, protrusio · **unstable** — travelling acetabulum, mortar + pestle, dislocating

| **Fibrous ankylosis** | — |

## X vs Y

| | **S. aureus** | **N. gonorrhoeae** |
|---|---|---|
| Share | **> 50%** | **\~20%** |
| Gram | +ve cocci, clusters | −ve diplococci, intra + extracellular |
| Host | All, incl. IV drug users | Healthy sexually active young |
| Synovial WBC | **Higher → more destruction** | Lower |
| Treatment | **Urgent I&D** + IV antibiotics | **Aspiration / drainage + ceftriaxone** |

| | **Septic arthritis** | **Gout** |
|---|---|---|
| Synovial WBC | **> 50,000** | Usually lower |
| Crystals | Coexist < 5% | **Negatively birefringent** urate |
| Treatment | **I&D** | **Indomethacin** |
