# Intertrochanteric Fractures

*Trauma · 33 / 67*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Share** — **\~50% of hip fractures** · 150,000 / yr (US) · F 500 vs M 200 / 100,000 / yr

**Who** — average **\~80 yr** (older than neck fractures) · **F:M 2:1 to 8:1**

**Risk factors** — osteoporosis, age, comorbidities, ADL dependency, recurrent falls (dementia, Parkinson's, vision, drugs)

### Osteoporosis — sentinel fractures & bisphosphonates

**Sentinel fracture** — **proximal humerus fracture → ↑ hip fracture risk (for 1 yr)** (✗ sacral, ✗ radius, ✗ fibula, ✗ distal humerus)

**Early bisphosphonate after fragility hip fracture** — **greatest BMD gain in first year**; **no ↑ nonunion, delayed union or peri-implant fracture**; oral ≈ IV

### Anatomy

**Neck-shaft** — **130 ± 7°** · anteversion **10 ± 7°**

**Calcar femorale** — dense vertical wall from **posteromedial shaft → posterior neck**; its involvement decides stability

**Femoral radius of curvature** — **114–120 cm**; **smaller (more bowed)** in **elderly, Asian, short stature**

| Fragment | Deformity | Muscles |
|---|---|---|
| **Proximal** | **Flexion** | **Iliopsoas**, sartorius, rectus, pectineus |
| **Proximal** | **External rotation** | Short external rotators |
| **Proximal** | **Abduction** | **Gluteus medius + minimus**, maximus, TFL |
| **Distal** | **Adduction + shortening → varus** | Adductors, gracilis |

**Blood supply** — rich (**trochanteric anastomosis**: MFCA + LFCA ascending branches, superior + inferior gluteal) → **nonunion < 2%**

**Trabeculae** — **Ward's triangle** = lowest BMD, bounded by primary compressive, principal tensile, secondary compressive trabeculae

## 3 Presentation

**Exam** — **shortened, externally rotated**, GT tenderness, pain on log-roll, **no straight-leg raise**

**History** — pre-injury function (predicts outcome), antecedent pain (OA / pathological), **anticoagulants**, ASA

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Study | Use / fact |
|---|---|
| AP pelvis, AP hip, **cross-table lateral**, **full-length femur** | Pattern; **posterior comminution** (lateral); subtroch extension, lesions, nail length, **bow**, canal |
| **Traction internal-rotation view** | **Better classification**, assess reduction |
| **MRI** | **Occult fracture (best; T1 100% sensitive, T2 84%)** · **isolated GT fracture → MRI for IT extension** (✗ CT, ✗ traction view, ✗ fix first) |
| CT (1–2 mm) | **Second line** if no MRI: sens 86%, spec 98% |
| Bone scan | Rare; **false negative up to 72 h**; sens up to 98% |

**MRI findings** — marrow oedema on **STIR / fat-suppressed T2**; low-signal **T1 line**

### Stability classification

| | **Stable** | **Unstable** |
|---|---|---|
| Definition | **Intact posteromedial cortex** → resists medial compression | Collapses into **varus** / shaft **medialises** |
| Examples | 2-part; small LT fragment without medial comminution | **Large / comminuted posteromedial (LT) fragment** · **reverse obliquity (transtrochanteric)** · **subtrochanteric extension** · **lateral wall < 20.5 mm** |

**Lateral wall thickness** — measured **3 cm below innominate tubercle at 135°** to fracture line; **< 20.5 mm → postop lateral wall fracture → CMN, not SHS**; 25 mm = **not** a risk

| AO / OTA 31A | Description |
|---|---|
| **A1** | Simple 2-part pertrochanteric, **intact lateral cortex** |
| **A2** | Separate **posteromedial fragment**, **intact lateral cortex** |
| **A3** | Through **lateral + medial cortex** (reverse oblique / transverse) |

![AO/OTA 31A classification](../Orthobullets/attachments/ob1038-ao-ota-31a-classification.jpg)

| Evans | Description |
|---|---|
| Stable | Posteromedial cortex intact / minimal comminution |
| Unstable | Greater posteromedial comminution — **convertible to stable with medial cortical opposition** |
| **Reverse obliquity** | Unstable — **adductors medialise shaft** |

![Reverse obliquity intertrochanteric fracture](../Orthobullets/attachments/ob1038-ap-x-ray-demonstrating-a-reverse-oblique-fracture-pattern.jpg)

### Reduction quality — cortical support

| Medial cortical support (TO) | Proximal fragment |
|---|---|
| **Positive — most acceptable** | **Medial** to shaft cortex |
| Neutral | Anatomical |
| Negative | **Lateral** — collapses |

## 5 Management

```
Intertrochanteric fracture
├─ Stable (intact posteromedial + lateral wall ≥ 20.5 mm) → SHS or CMN (equal outcomes)
├─ Unstable / reverse obliquity / subtroch extension / thin lateral wall → CMN
│      └─ subtroch extension ≤ 3–4 cm → short CMN · more distal → long CMN
└─ Comminuted + severe OA / bone won't hold → calcar-replacing arthroplasty
```

### Treatment options

| Option | Indication | Fact |
|---|---|---|
| Nonop (NWB, bed → chair) | **Non-ambulatory**, too high risk, skin breakdown, incomplete fracture | **1-yr mortality 84.4%**; pneumonia, UTI, sores, DVT |
| **SHS (most common ORIF)** | **Stable** | **Cheapest**, **spares abductors**, dynamic compression; **2-hole side plate = 4-hole** |
| **CMN** | **Stable or unstable** — **AAOS strong for unstable, reverse oblique, subtroch extension** | Load-sharing, **shorter lever arm**, **intramedullary buttress limits medialisation**, ↓ / = blood loss; **violates abductors**; dearer; **↑ use over last decade** |
| Proximal femoral locking plate | Young + unstable (rare) | Surgeon-dependent; **no early WB** |
| 95° blade plate / DCS | Rare | Acceptable for reverse oblique |
| **Arthroplasty (long-stem, calcar-replacing)** | Salvage, severe comminution, **pre-existing OA**, very osteoporotic | Fix GT; more blood loss, time, cost |

### Stable vs unstable patterns — implant choice (SHS vs cephalomedullary nail, reverse obliquity)

**Stable** — **SHS = CMN outcomes**; **reduction quality > implant choice**

**Reverse obliquity** — **NOT SHS (56% failure)** → shear, **shaft medialisation + cut-out** → **CMN** (trochanteric or piriformis), **blade plate, DCS, locking plate, ex-fix** all acceptable

**SHS advantages over CMN** — **none** for DVT, blood loss, nonunion, biomechanics (✗ all)

**CMN preferred because** — **stability for the fracture pattern** (✗ shorter LOS, ✗ less abductor damage, ✗ easier THA conversion, ✗ cheaper)

**Most cost-effective (stable)** — **SHS** (✗ cannulated screws, ✗ hemi)

**Spare abductors** — **SHS** (✗ lateral position, ✗ lag screw vs blade, ✗ omitting distal lock)

**Short vs long CMN** — equal function, reoperation, peri-implant fracture, cut-out, mortality · **long: ↑ OR time, ↑ blood loss, ↑ postop anaemia + transfusion**, radiation, bow mismatch · **short tolerates 3–4 cm subtroch extension**

**Distal locking screw** — **protective against peri-implant fracture** (omitting ↑ refracture)

**SHS contraindicated** — reverse oblique, **lateral wall comminution / < 20.5 mm**, **subtrochanteric fractures**

### Tip-apex distance & cut-out

**TAD** — **sum of tip → apex distances on AP + lateral**, corrected for magnification

**Goal** — **TAD < 25 mm** · **> 45 mm → 60% failure** · 27 / 32 / 40 mm → ↑ **cut-out**

**Strongest predictor of cut-out** — **TAD** (✗ age, ✗ stability, ✗ reduction, ✗ plate angle)

**Lag screw position** — **centre–centre (TO)**; **low-central AP + central lateral** acceptable; **never peripheral / posterior** (head rotates → superior cut-out) (✗ posterior on lateral)

**Lag screw vs helical blade** — controversial · blade compacts bone, ↑ cut-out resistance (biomechanical), but **much higher "cut-through"**

![Tip-apex distance](../Orthobullets/attachments/ob1038-tad-illustration.jpg)

| Failure | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Cut-out** | **Anterocranial** perforation **with varus collapse** |
| **Cut-through** | **Central** perforation **without collapse** — **helical blade** |

### Lateral wall integrity & sliding hip screw failure

**Intra-op lateral wall fracture** — **↑ collapse / sliding** (✗ nail length, ✗ WB status)

**Predictor of postop lateral wall fracture with SHS** — **lateral wall thickness** (✗ reverse obliquity, ✗ T-score, ✗ calcar comminution)

**Highest SHS failure** — **wall 18.5 mm + TAD 28.5 mm**

**DHS failure — EXCEPT** — **2-hole side plate** (✗ reverse oblique, ✗ TAD 27 / 32, ✗ lateral wall comminution)

**Left-sided unstable fracture + DHS** — **anterior spike** (screw torque) → **derotation wire** first

**SHS problems in unstable fractures** — excessive collapse, **limb shortening**, shaft medialisation

### Anterior cortical perforation & nail technique

**Cause** — **radius-of-curvature mismatch (nail straighter / larger radius than bowed femur)** + **posterior start on GT** (✗ anterior start, ✗ large implant, ✗ lateral position)

**Prevent** — lateral view of distal femur while seating · avoid posterior start

**Too-lateral start (> 3 mm)** — **varus** → collapse + cut-out

**Guidewire stuck in nail (no fragment)** — **remove nail, check guidewire bend** (✗ insert lag wire, ✗ re-ream, ✗ smaller nail, ✗ tighten jig)

**PFN-A2 vs PFN-A (TO)** — **all**: **less lateral cortex pressure (flat lateral surface)**, **smaller proximal diameter (16.5 vs 17.0 mm)**, **smaller blade**; higher bend point, **5° ML bend** — for **Asian femora**

**PFN types (TO)** — conventional PFN (**2 screws**: lag + anti-rotation) → **Z / reverse Z** · **PFN-A** (single **helical blade**, unreamed, 2003)

## 6 Complications

| Complication | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Cut-out / implant failure** | **Commonest after CMN**; **4–20%**, within **4 months** (Orthobullets) · 3 months (OB) |
| Nonunion / malunion | **< 2%**; diagnosis: pain + lucency **4–7 months**; rule out infection |
| Peri-implant fracture | **1–3% at 1 yr**; short = long; short → **at nail tip**, long → around rod |
| Anterior perforation | Long CMN, bow mismatch, posterior start |
| **Anaemia** | **> 30% transfused**; threshold **≤ 8 g/dL** if asymptomatic; **TXA (strong)** |

### Failed fixation — conversion to THA

**Z effect (TO)** — **lag screw migrates laterally**, varus collapse, **derotation screw perforates head**

**Reverse Z effect (TO)** — **derotation screw migrates laterally + lag screw migrates medially** (TO case answer)

**Cause** — unstable calcar, **deficient lateral buttress**, no medial support; head vs neck compressive-strength mismatch

| Failure | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Elderly / joint damaged by cut-out** | **THA** (✗ NWB, ✗ valgus osteotomy, ✗ revision ORIF, ✗ resection) |
| **Malunion + hardware into acetabulum** | **THA with calcar-replacing stem bypassing hardware** (✗ bipolar — acetabular erosion) |
| **Active 72-yr, cut-out + acetabular erosion** | **Conversion THA** (✗ exchange CMN, ✗ osteotomy, ✗ proximal femoral replacement, ✗ hemi) |
| **Young / intact head** | **Valgus osteotomy ± graft** / revision ORIF |

### Mortality, surgical timing & perioperative care

**1-yr mortality** — **15–30%** (Orthobullets) · 20–30% (OB) · \~10% at 1 mo, 20% at 4 mo, **30% at 1 yr**; **nonop 84.4%**

**↑ Mortality** — **male (25–30% vs 20%)**, **IT > neck fracture**, **age > 85**, **≥ 2 comorbidities**, **ASA III–IV**, delay **> 48 h** (> 2 days, Orthobullets · ≥ 3 days, OB)

**Recognised predictor** — **ASA class** (✗ WB status, ✗ comminution, ✗ device, ✗ anaesthetic)

**Not associated with ↑ mortality** — **surgery within 48 h**, **spinal vs general (REGAIN)**, nail length, interdisciplinary care (↓)

**Delay > 48 h** — **↑ 30-day mortality**, **↑ pneumonia** (✗ OR time, ✗ transfusion, ✗ infection)

**Timing** — **24–48 h** (AAOS moderate) · **co-management** (AAOS strong)

**Dementia patient** — **geriatric co-management, avoid centrally-acting drugs (meperidine)**, rapid optimisation, **surgery < 48 h** (✗ neurology consult, ✗ routine echo, ✗ nephrology for normal CrCl — low muscle mass masks renal impairment)

**Routine echo** — **delays surgery** without changing outcome (unless valve disease, arrhythmia, worsening CHF, CAD) (✗ hospitalist, ✗ fascia iliaca block, ✗ Foley, ✗ fluids cause delay)

**DAPT (aspirin + clopidogrel)** — **continue, proceed when optimised** (✗ vitamin K — warfarin, ✗ idarucizumab — dabigatran, ✗ FFP, ✗ stop + wait 48 h)

**Function at 1 yr** — **41%** regain status · 40% more aids · 12% household · 8% non-functional · **rule of thirds**: ⅓ regain, ⅓ lose a level, ⅓ die

**Post-op** — **WBAT** ↓ complications
