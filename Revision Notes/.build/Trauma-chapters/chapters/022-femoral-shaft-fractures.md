# Femoral Shaft Fractures

*Trauma · 22 / 67*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Incidence** — **37.1 / 100,000 / yr**

**Who** — high energy in young (MVC) · low energy in elderly · gunshot

### Associated injuries

| Association | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Ipsilateral femoral neck fracture** | **2–6%** (Orthobullets) · \~5% · 1–9% / 2.5–9% / 0–15% (OB) · 7.5% (Tornetta); **basicervical, vertical, non-displaced**; **missed 19–31% (\~30%)** |
| **Bilateral femur fractures** | ↑ **mortality** (5.6% vs 1.5%), initial hypotension, head + pelvic injuries (✗ **not** ↑ rib fractures); pulmonary complications |
| Ipsilateral tibia (floating knee), acetabulum | — |
| Pulmonary injury | Early fixation → ARDS \~2% if under-resuscitated |
| Head injury | Intra-op hypotension → ↓ cerebral perfusion |

**Blood loss** — **closed femur 1000–1500 mL** · closed tibia 500–1000 mL · **open ≈ double** · **bilateral closed femur 2000–3000 mL**

### Anatomy

**Femur** — largest, strongest; **anterior bow** (radius of curvature **120 ± 36 cm**); **linea aspera** = posterior compressive strut

**Compartments** — anterior (sartorius, quads) · posterior (hamstrings) · adductor (gracilis, adductors)

**Blood supply after fracture** — **periosteal + soft-tissue supply predominates**; reaming disrupts endosteal but stimulates periosteal

### Deforming forces

| Fragment | Deformity | Muscle |
|---|---|---|
| **Proximal** | **Abducted** | Gluteus medius + minimus |
| **Proximal** | **Flexed** (+ ER) | **Iliopsoas** (+ short rotators) |
| **Distal** | **Varus** | Adductors |
| **Distal** | **Extension** | Gastrocnemius |

## 2 Mechanism

| Force | Pattern |
|---|---|
| **Pure bending** | **Transverse** |
| Rotation | Spiral |
| Uneven bending | Oblique |
| **4-point bending** | Segmental |
| High-speed crush / torsion | Comminuted |

## 3 Presentation

**Exam** — tense swollen thigh, shortened, tender; hip exam unreliable (neck fracture easily missed); **document distal NV status**

**Adequate resuscitation** — HR < 100, SBP > 100, DBP > 70, temp > 35 °C, urine 0.5–1 mL/kg/h, **lactate < 2.5**, **BD −2 to +2**, **IL-6 < 500 pg/dL**, gastric pH > 7.3

**Compensated shock clues** — normotensive, afebrile tachycardia, cool limbs, narrow pulse pressure, weak pulses, slow refill

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Study | Use |
|---|---|
| **AP + lateral whole femur**, ipsilateral **hip** + **knee** | Standard |
| **AP hip in \~10° internal rotation** | Neck in profile (✗ ER view) |
| **Fine-cut (2 mm) CT of hip** | **Best pre-op study for occult neck fracture**; **CT capsular sign** = **> 1 mm more capsular distension** than uninjured side |
| **Intra-operative fluoroscopy of neck** | **Still needed even after negative 2 mm CT** |
| **Post-op hip X-ray in OR** + follow-up **AP hip in 15–20° IR** | **New groin pain after nailing → hip X-ray first** |

**Neck protocol** — 10° IR AP hip + 2 mm CT + intra-op fluoro + post-op film (✗ bone scan, ✗ traction views, ✗ MRI routinely, ✗ frog-leg — too painful)

**Septic nonunion labs** — **CRP most sensitive for occult infection**, ESR, WBC

### Winquist classification

| Type | Comminution |
|---|---|
| **0** | None |
| **I** | Insignificant |
| **II** | **> 50% cortical contact** |
| **III** | **< 50% cortical contact** |
| **IV** | **Segmental** — no contact between main fragments |

|---|---|

## 5 Management

```
Femoral shaft fracture
├─ Resuscitated → reamed, statically locked IMN < 24 h (WBAT)
│      ├─ antegrade = gold standard
│      └─ retrograde: neck fracture, floating knee, acetabulum, bilateral, obese, pregnant, polytrauma
├─ Unstable / head injury with ICP problems / vascular / severe open → ex-fix → IMN within 2–3 wk
└─ Ipsilateral neck fracture → fix NECK first (open if displaced), then retrograde nail / plate
```

### Treatment options

| Option | Indication | Fact |
|---|---|---|
| Spica / long leg cast | Children (< 5 yr spica), unfit adults | — |
| **Antegrade reamed IMN** | **Gold standard** | **98–99% union**, infection \~2%; **< 24 h** → ↓ ARDS, VTE, LOS, cost |
| **Retrograde IMN** | **Ipsilateral neck fracture**, **floating knee**, **acetabular fracture**, **bilateral femurs**, **polytrauma**, **morbid obesity**, **pregnancy (1st trimester)**, infra-isthmal | ✗ skeletal immaturity, knee sepsis, knee soft-tissue injury |
| **Ex-fix → IMN within 2–3 wk** | Unstable polytrauma, vascular injury, severe open | Union + infection = acute nailing; **infection ↑ if ex-fix > 28 days**; ↓ ARDS / fat embolism; knee stiffness |
| **Plate** | Neck fracture needing screws, distal meta-diaphyseal fracture, no canal access | **↑ infection, nonunion, hardware failure** |

**Immediate (retrograde / antegrade) nailing** — safe for **GSW** femur fractures

**WB after statically locked appropriately sized nail** — **immediately** (even comminuted)

### Antegrade vs retrograde nailing

| | **Antegrade** | **Retrograde** |
|---|---|---|
| Union | Equal | Equal |
| Pain | **Hip pain**, abductor weakness, **abductor HO** | **Knee pain**, **symptomatic distal locking screws** |
| Setup | Fracture table / lateral | **Supine, all limbs accessible, compare rotation + length** |
| Bilateral femurs | Reposition | **Shorter op — no re-prep** |

**Retrograde preferred because** — **less hip pain** (✗ union, ✗ infection, ✗ time, ✗ less knee pain)

**Long-term weakness after antegrade nail** — **hip abduction + knee extension** (quads + abductors weaker)

**Stable, floating knee + open ankle** — **retrograde femur nail + tibial nail + ankle I&D + ORIF** (✗ ex-fix)

**Femur + ipsilateral open tibia, stable** — **reamed retrograde nail, static locking** (✗ unlocked, ✗ unreamed, ✗ dynamic locking — length unstable)

**Bilateral femurs, resuscitated (lactate 1.2)** — **reamed retrograde nailing of both** (✗ traction, ✗ ex-fix, ✗ plates, ✗ unreamed antegrade)

### Antegrade technique & entry points

**Positioning** — fracture table (perineal post, well-leg lithotomy) or **sloppy lateral**

| Entry | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| **Piriformis fossa** | **Colinear with shaft** | Hard in obese; **most damage to piriformis tendon, abductors, MFCA** (→ AVN in children) |
| **Trochanteric** | Less abductor injury, easier | Not colinear → **trochanteric-design nail** required; **too lateral → varus** |

**Trochanteric start** — **depends on trochanter position relative to shaft axis** + nail geometry (tip or just medial) (✗ "always at the tip")

**Varus causes** — **trochanteric start too lateral** · **straight (piriformis) nail through trochanteric portal** · wrong-side trochanteric nail · (trochanteric nail through piriformis → **valgus**)

**Too ANTERIOR start (≥ 6 mm)** — ↑ **hoop stress → iatrogenic proximal femur fracture ("bursting")** (esp. piriformis nail); **over-ream ≥ 0.5 mm** to reduce · 3 mm anterior improves **cephalic screw position** in CMN

**Too POSTERIOR start + straight nail (larger radius of curvature) + short / bowed femur** — **anterior cortical perforation** of distal femur / anterior thigh pain → prevent: **more anterior start**, **smaller radius of curvature (more curved) nail**, **anterior** blocking screws, **smaller nail**, notched tip (✗ larger nail, ✗ posterior blocking screw, ✗ straighter nail)

**Insertion** — nail inserted **rotated 90° internally** then rotated back (uses bow, avoids medial comminution)

**Piriformis nail** — single sagittal bow (either side) · **trochanteric nail** — double bow, side-specific (twist on insertion); 3-point fit

### Reamed vs unreamed nailing

**Reamed** — **↑ union**, ↓ time to union, **no ↑ pulmonary complications**, **no ↑ infection in open**; unreamed → **4.5× nonunion** (Canadian RCT)

**Why reaming helps** — **reaming debris ≈ iliac crest autograft** (osteogenic cells) · stimulates periosteal flow · **larger nail (stiffness ∝ r⁴)** · more isthmic contact · fluted nails ↓ marrow pressure (greatest at nail insertion)

**Unreamed** — consider only with **bilateral pulmonary injury**; **biggest nonunion risk factor** among options (✗ open reduction, ✗ cerclage, ✗ retrograde, ✗ staged ex-fix)

**Nail diameter** — **10 mm ≈ larger nails** for union (✗ bigger canal needs bigger nail)

### Retrograde technique

**Start** — **centre of notch (AP)** + **extension of Blumensaat line (lateral)** (posterior → cruciate injury); medial condylar start needs curved nail (else valgus) · knee flexed **30–50°**

**Steps** — reduce before reaming (F-tool, bumps, Schanz joysticks); ream 1–1.5 mm over nail; **nail \~1 cm deep to cartilage**; guidewire **proximal to lesser trochanter**; **long nail to LT**

**Proximal interlocks** — safe **above lesser trochanter**; **A→P screws below LT → femoral nerve branches + deep femoral artery** at risk

**Periprosthetic (CR TKA)** — start **more posterior → hyperextension (recurvatum)**; arthrotomy needed; PS box may need burr; backup = locking plates (multiple distal points)

### Ipsilateral femoral neck + shaft

**Priority** — **neck first** (AVN / nonunion) → **open reduction + screws** if displaced → **reamed retrograde nail** (or plate) for shaft

**Two implants** — **lower malreduction** than single cephalomedullary nail (✗ single CMN — **highest malreduction**; no faster neck healing, less pain)

**Missed neck found after antegrade nail** — screws **anterior to nail** (difficult)

### Polytrauma & head injury

**Head injury** — early stabilisation **does not worsen neurological outcome** if **hypotension + hypoxia avoided**; **intra-op hypotension → lower GCS at discharge**

**Severe head injury with ↑ ICP (bifrontal contusion, ICP 30)** — **ex-fix femur** (✗ due to L4 burst, open tibia, LC1, rib fractures)

**SAH + femur — adverse outcome from?** — **intra-operative hypotension** (traction if ICP labile)

**GCS 13, normal CT head, stable** — **early fixation, no neuro risk** (✗ ↑ pulmonary risk, ✗ delay, ✗ DCO, ✗ chest injury always contraindicates)

**Unilateral contusion + chest tube, now stable (BD −2)** — **reamed IMN**

**Shock, bilateral contusions, BD −10** — **ex-fix** (✗ plating, ✗ reamed / unreamed nails)

**Borderline after splenectomy, bilateral open femurs + pelvis** — **bilateral femoral ex-fix + anterior pelvic ex-fix**

**BD −6, lactate 3.5, contusions, hip reduced** — **bilateral temporising ex-fix**

### Distal interlocking

**Perfect circles** — hole widened **proximal–distal → adjust abduction / adduction** · widened **anterior–posterior → adjust internal / external rotation**

**Computer navigation** — **↓ fluoroscopy time** (✗ accuracy, time, image quality, length)

**Single distal screw failure** — **4-point bending → screw shaft fractures inside the nail** (✗ pull-out, ✗ head shear)

## 6 Complications

| Complication | Fact |
|---|---|
| **HO** | **25%**, rarely significant (abductors after antegrade) |
| **Pudendal nerve** | **10%** with traction on fracture table |
| Femoral artery / nerve | Retrograde proximal locking screws |
| **Malrotation** | **Proximal fractures 30%**, distal 10%; fracture table, **comminution**, night surgery |
| Delayed union | Dynamise ± graft |
| **Nonunion** | **< 10%**; NSAIDs, **smoking**, **indomethacin** |
| Infection | < 1% → remove nail, ream, ex-fix if unhealed |
| Weakness | Quads + abductors |
| Iatrogenic fracture | Anterior start ≥ 6 mm, under-reaming |
| MAD | Lengthening → **lateral** MAD · shortening → **medial** MAD |
| **Anterior cortical perforation** | Radius mismatch; posterior start + straight nail |
| **Compartment syndrome** | **Ballistic > blunt** |

### Rotational malalignment

**Most associated factor** — **fracture comminution** (TO, OB) (✗ entry point, ✗ level, ✗ surgeon experience, ✗ closed technique) · also night-time surgery

**Fracture table** — **↑ internal malrotation** (vs manual traction supine)

**Direction by level** — **proximal fracture → INTERNAL malrotation** (proximal fragment ER by iliopsoas, rotators, abductors) · **distal fracture → EXTERNAL malrotation** (gastrocnemius / plantaris ER distal fragment) · antegrade vs retrograde and entry type don't matter

**Lesser trochanter larger on injured side (ER proximal fragment)** — **internal rotation malalignment**

**Iatrogenic internal malrotation** — **distal segment internally rotated relative to proximal** (↑ anteversion)

**CT measurement (Jeanmart)** — angle between **posterior condylar tangent** and **femoral neck axis**; rotation = β − α; compare sides

**Thresholds** — **< 10° = normal variation** · **≤ 15° well tolerated** · **> 15° + symptomatic → derotation** (✗ dynamisation)

**Acceptable differences** — **9° IR + 14° ER** (✗ 18°)

**CT: ER malalignment on exam** — **more ER, less IR** of hip (tibia as pointer)

**Worked example** — right neck IR13 / condyles ER17, left neck ER13 / condyles ER3 → right distal ER30 vs left IR10 → **rotate right distal fragment INTERNALLY 40°**

**Correction** — intra-op: remove distal locks + derotate · after union: **osteotomy**

### Nonunion

**Definition** — **no healing by 9 months** or **no progress over 3 months**; broken distal locks = race lost

| Nonunion | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Aseptic, after reamed nail (smoker)** | **Reamed antegrade exchange nailing** (✗ graft in situ, ✗ stimulator, ✗ biopsy) |
| Hypertrophic, **undersized nail** | **Reamed exchange nailing** |
| **Hypertrophic, sub-isthmal** | **Nail retention + plate augmentation + graft** (highest union + immediate WB) |
| **Failed exchange nailing** (union only \~53%) | **Bone graft + compression plating** |

**Exchange nailing works by** — stiffer larger nail, better isthmic fit, reaming graft · **dynamic locking** + grafting ↑ union · **smoking** = biggest culprit when it fails

**Indomethacin HO prophylaxis (25 mg TDS × 6 wk)** — **↑ long-bone nonunion** (inhibits IGF-1); radiation preferred

### Skeletal traction (TO)

**Upper tibial pin (sliding) traction (TO)** — **counter-traction by raising foot end of bed** (\~1 inch per lb) with BB splint (✗ couch, ✗ none, ✗ muscle spasm) · **Thomas splint = fixed traction** (ring in groin)
