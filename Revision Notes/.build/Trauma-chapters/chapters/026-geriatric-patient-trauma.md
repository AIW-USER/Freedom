# Geriatric Patient Trauma

*Trauma · 26 / 67*

## 1 Basics

### Definition & epidemiology

**Elderly** — commonly **age > 65**; **physiological age / frailty** more clinically relevant

**Growth** — **> 85-year group fastest growing** (30%, 2000–2010)

**Falls** — **30% of people > 65 fall ≥ 1×/yr** · **2.1 million fragility fractures / yr**

## 3 Presentation

**History** — mechanism: mechanical, **syncope**, **elder abuse** · **accurate medication list** (polypharmacy, **anticoagulants**) · baseline ambulation + mental status

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

**Radiographs** — **joint above + below** (other injuries, adjacent arthroplasty / implants)

### Pre-operative work-up

**ED** — **ECG, CXR, urinalysis, labs**

**Optimise** — coagulopathy, **heart failure**, sepsis · prevent: **avoid over-sedation + anticholinergics**, ulcer prophylaxis

**Cardiac risk** — **Revised Cardiac Risk Index (RCRI)** or **NSQIP calculator** → guides work-up + timing

| Situation | Next step |
|---|---|
| **Low risk, normal ECG, no CHF signs** | **Proceed with surgery** (general anaesthesia safe) |
| Signs / symptoms of **CHF** | **BNP, echocardiogram** |
| **Elevated MACE risk + very poor functional capacity** | **Pharmacological stress test** |

## 5 Management

### Treatment principles

**Nonop** — comorbidities, low function, family wishes → pain control, ease of transfer

**Operative goals** — **early mobilisation**, return to pre-injury function, pain control

**Acute arthroplasty** — **comminuted distal humerus (TEA)**, **proximal humerus (reverse TSA)**, **femoral neck**, acetabulum, intra-articular distal femur

**Adjuncts** — **fibular strut allograft (proximal humerus)**, multiple implants, **locking plates**

![Reverse TSA for proximal humerus fracture](../Orthobullets/attachments/ob322053-proximal-humerus-fracture-treated-with-a-reverse-tsa.jpg)

### Perioperative optimisation — regional block, co-management, timing

**↓ Narcotic use while awaiting optimisation** — **pre-op fascia iliaca block** (✗ PCA, ✗ skeletal traction, ✗ pain service consult alone, ✗ prophylactic haloperidol)

**Hip fracture < 48 h** — ↓ complications, pain, LOS, **mortality**

**Ortho-geriatric co-management — proven** — **↓ mortality**, **↓ complications**, **↓ time to surgery**, **↑ mobility at 4 months**, ↓ cost; LOS conflicting

**Co-management — NOT proven** — **↓ need for post-discharge rehab facility**

## 6 Complications

### Mortality predictors

| Factor | 1-yr mortality |
|---|---|
| **Mechanical ventilation at admission** | **78.7% — highest** |
| ISS > 25 | 73.2% |
| Head injury | 51.1% |
| Hip fracture | \~30% (in-hospital **6%**) |
| Open fracture | ≈ hip fracture |

**Spinal cord injury** — **higher mortality** than in young

### Delirium

**Incidence** — **13–50%** post-op

**Risk factors** — dementia, polypharmacy, **narcotics**, dehydration, restraints, **Foley catheters**, frequent awakenings

**Treatment** — **co-management**, reorientation, minimise narcotics (**fascia iliaca block**), early mobilisation, early catheter removal

**Medical complications** — pneumonia, **PE / DVT**, **pressure ulcers**, MI, stroke

## How it's asked

**"Reduce narcotics pre-op in hip fracture?"** → **fascia iliaca block**

**"Ortho-geriatric co-management — EXCEPT?"** → **decreased need for rehab facility**

**"Low-risk patient, normal ECG — next?"** → **proceed with surgical fixation** (✗ BNP, ✗ echo, ✗ stress test, ✗ neuraxial only)

**"Highest 1-yr mortality predictor?"** → **need for mechanical ventilation** (✗ head injury, ✗ ISS > 25, ✗ hip fracture, ✗ open fracture)
