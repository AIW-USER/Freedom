# Distal Femur Fractures

*Trauma · 11 / 67*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Frequency** — **3–6% of femur fractures** · < 1% of all fractures

**Bimodal** — **young males, high energy** (displaced) · **elderly osteopenic females, low-energy fall**

**Open** — **5–10%** of supracondylar fractures

### Anatomy

**Anatomic axis** — **6–11° valgus**; medial condyle extends further distal

**Trapezoidal cross-section** — **lateral cortex slopes \~10°**, **medial cortex \~25°** → straight AP hides long medial screws → check with **AP in 30° internal rotation**

**Condyles** — posterior halves lie **behind** posterior shaft cortex

![Trapezoidal distal femur](../Orthobullets/attachments/ob1041-distal-femur-anatomy.jpg)

### Deforming forces

| Muscle | Deformity |
|---|---|
| **Quadriceps + hamstrings** | **Shortening** |
| **Adductor magnus** | **Varus or valgus** (depends on comminution vs adductor tubercle) |
| **Gastrocnemius** | **Extension / apex-posterior** (recurvatum) + condylar rotation with intercondylar split |

## 2 Mechanism

**Young** — high energy, displaced · **Elderly** — fall from standing, osteoporotic, less displaced

## 3 Presentation

**Exam** — distal thigh tenderness, swelling, varus / valgus deformity, effusion if intra-articular, open wounds

**Vascular** — **popliteal artery** at risk with displacement; femoral vessels tethered at **adductor hiatus**

### Vascular assessment (ABI)

| ABI | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **> 0.9** | **NPV 99%** (Orthobullets, OB) · **100%** (one OB explanation) → serial exams |
| **< 0.9** | **95% sensitive, 97% specific** for major arterial injury → **angiography / CTA** |
| 0.9–1.3 | Normal |

**Pulse returns after traction but "feels different"** — **ABI** next (✗ serial exams — inaccurate, ✗ angiography first — time / dye risk, ✗ duplex — operator / availability, ✗ compartment pressures)

**GSW, ABI 0.81** — **CTA**

**Angiography also for hard signs** — pulselessness, expanding haematoma, massive bleeding

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Study | Use |
|---|---|
| AP + lateral | Standard |
| Traction views | Characterise (painful) |
| Whole femur, contralateral femur | Associated injuries; templating |
| **CT** | **Intra-articular extension**, **Hoffa**, notch osteochondral fragments; after ex-fix |
| Elderly | Look for **pre-existing knee OA** |

**Commonest intra-articular split** — **sagittal**

### Hoffa (coronal-plane) fractures

| Fact | Value |
|---|---|
| Incidence in supra- / intercondylar fractures | **38%** (X-ray, Orthobullets) · **\~40%** (CT, OB) |
| Condyle | **Lateral 80%** |
| Missed on X-ray | **up to 31%** (\~30%) |
| Best seen | **Lateral view / CT** (✗ AP) |
| Association | **Open fractures \~3× more likely**; high energy (✗ ground-level fall) |
| OTA | **33B3** |
| Fixation | **Anterior → posterior lag screws across lateral condyle** (interfragmentary) |
| Implant ✗ | **95° blade plate / DCS contraindicated** |

**CT after motorcycle supracondylar fracture — likely finding** — **coronal fracture of lateral femoral condyle** (✗ tibial eminence, ✗ sagittal MFC, ✗ Schatzker I, ✗ axial MFC)

![Hoffa fracture on lateral radiograph](../Orthobullets/attachments/ob1041-hoffa-fracture-2.jpg)

### AO / OTA 33 classification

| Type | Definition |
|---|---|
| **33A** | **Extra-articular** |
| **33B** | **Partial articular** — part of joint still attached to shaft; **B3 = coronal (Hoffa)** |
| **33C** | **Complete articular** — articular block separated from shaft |

![AO/OTA classification of distal femur fractures](../Orthobullets/attachments/ob1041-ao-ota-classification-of-distal-femur-fractures.jpg)

## 5 Management

```
Distal femur fracture
├─ Unstable polytrauma / bad soft tissue / contamination → spanning ex-fix → staged
├─ Extra-articular or simple articular → retrograde nail or lateral locked plate
│      └─ intra-articular → reduce + lag articular block first
├─ Partial articular (33B) → lag screws + buttress plate (medial approach for B2/B3)
└─ Elderly + OA / unreconstructable → distal femoral replacement (immediate WB)
```

### Treatment options

| Option | Indications | Key facts |
|---|---|---|
| **Hinged knee brace** (rare) | Stable non-displaced, non-ambulatory, prohibitive risk | 6–8 wk; ROM 3–4 wk; stiffness, wounds |
| **Ex-fix** | Temporising (polytrauma, soft tissue, contamination); definitive for severe open / comminuted | Definitive union **92–100% at 4–6 mo**; keep pins out of plate zone; narrow anterior safe zone |
| **ORIF** | Displaced, intra-articular, **osteoporotic periprosthetic**, nonunion | **Fixed-angle for metaphyseal comminution** (non-fixed → **varus collapse**); nonunion up to 18% |
| **Retrograde IMN** | **Extra-articular**, **simple intra-articular**, periprosthetic with **open-box** femoral component | **≥ 4 cm intact distal femur** (traditional); more symmetric callus, ↓ malunion, ↑ satisfaction |
| **TKA / DFR** | **Pre-existing OA**, **unreconstructable**, **loose TKA**, low demand | **Immediate WB**, better ambulation, ↓ nonunion; same complications + reoperations; ↓ longevity |

**Up to 20% wheelchair-bound** at 1 yr after internal fixation (elderly)

### Locked plating principles & implant choice

**Locking plates** — screw–plate interface (angular stability), no bone–plate contact needed → **comminuted metaphyseal / diaphyseal**, **short segments**, **osteoporotic periprosthetic** (✗ simple diaphyseal, ✗ displaced articular unless lagged first)

**Lag first, then lock** — locked screws can't reduce or compress → **reduce + lag articular block, then attach to shaft**

**Osteoporotic 68-yo, metaphyseal comminution + sagittal split** — **lag screws then locking plate** (✗ non-locking plate, ✗ retrograde nail, ✗ ex-fix)

**Lateral locking plate NOT for** — **33B partial articular (isolated medial condyle)** → **medial approach, lag + buttress plate**

| Implant | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Locking compression plate** | **Best control of coronal-plane + small distal segments**; multiplanar screws for Hoffa; may be **too stiff → nonunion / failure** |
| **95° angled blade plate** | Precise insertion needed; **greater torsional strength** than LCP but **more subsidence, lower load to failure**; ✗ C3, ✗ Hoffa; poor in osteoporosis |
| **Dynamic condylar screw** | Sagittal alignment forgiving (rotates on barrel); **removes most bone** |
| **Dual plating (lateral + medial)** | **Greatest axial + torsional stiffness** — best for **medial comminution in osteoporosis** |
| **Nail-plate combination** | **↓ reoperation** vs lateral plate alone (LLP nonunion \~20%); same infection; allows immediate WB |

**Plate failure risk** — **short working length**, plate-screw density **> 0.5**, short plates, non-locked diaphyseal screws, **stainless steel may be worse than titanium**, improper bridging (✗ early ROM, ✗ bridging, ✗ density < 0.5)

**Percutaneous submuscular precontoured locking plate** — ↓ infection + nonunion, preserves perfusion, **but ↑ malreduction (rotational)**

**Intercondylar screw length** — **AP in 30° internal rotation** (✗ ER view, ✗ full extension AP)

### Approaches

| Approach | Use |
|---|---|
| **Lateral** | All types; arthrotomy for joint |
| **Swashbuckler (modified anterior)** | Extra-articular / lateral; submuscular plate |
| **Lateral parapatellar** | **Complex articular extension**, Hoffa; evert patella |
| **Medial parapatellar** | Complex **medial condyle** |
| **Medial** | **B2, B3**; medial plate augment in C3 |
| **Posterior (prone, between gastrocnemius heads)** | Very posterior Hoffa |

### Retrograde nail technique

**Start** — **superior margin of Blumensaat line (lateral)**, **centre of notch (AP)**; knee flexion **≥ 70°**

**Approach** — patellar tendon split or medial parapatellar (extend 2–8 cm if articular)

**Rules** — fix articular fragments first (lag screws out of nail path); **long nail to lesser trochanter** (short nails rarely; vascular risk)

**Blocking (Poller) screws** — narrow the metaphysis; place in **proximal part of distal segment** on **concave side**: **varus → AP screw medial to nail** · **recurvatum → ML screw anterior to nail** · valgus → lateral · procurvatum → posterior

**Gastrocnemius** — recurvatum + posterior translation; wide metaphysis → varus / valgus

### Elderly & periprosthetic

**Retrograde nail contraindicated** — **distal femoral replacement** (long stem) (✗ PS knee — technically harder, ✗ CR, ✗ spiral, ✗ **flexion** contracture helps; **extension** contracture blocks access)

**Periprosthetic nail** — box needs **posterior start** → **recurvatum** risk

**Elderly intra-articular + pre-existing OA (cane user)** — **DFR** (✗ nail-plate, ✗ dual plates, ✗ lateral plate, ✗ stemmed TKA)

**Lateral locked plate, extra-articular elderly** — **WBAT** immediately (no ↑ failure / reoperation) (✗ NWB, ✗ TDWB, ✗ partial)

**Plate vs nail, elderly extra-articular + immediate WB** — **no difference in function**; no shorter op / stay

## 6 Complications

| Complication | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Knee pain / stiffness** | Early ROM, PT; **nail → anterior knee pain** |
| **Symptomatic hardware** | **Lateral plate → IT band** pain with flexion / extension · **long medial screws** |
| **Malunion** | After plating: **rotation, recurvatum, coronal**; **"golf-club" deformity = plate too posterior distally → medial translation of articular block**; percutaneous plate → **rotational**; **malalignment commoner with nails**; acceptable < 5° any plane |
| **Nonunion** | **Up to 19%** (metaphyseal; articular part heals) → **revision ORIF + autograft** ± change fixation |
| Infection | Diabetics with foot ulcers → debride, antibiotics |
| **Implant failure** | Up to 9% |
| **Loss of fixation** | **Varus collapse (commonest)** — toggling non-fixed-angle screws; proximal screw failure with short plates / non-locked diaphysis |

**Oligotrophic supracondylar nonunion** — **open reduction + plating + autograft** (✗ nail + BMP, ✗ ex-fix + BMP, ✗ percutaneous plate + BMP, ✗ calcium phosphate)

### External fixator pin placement

**Anterior femoral half-pin safe zone** — narrow: **femoral nerve branches** up to **58 ± 36 mm (0–136)** below lesser trochanter (174 ± 43 mm from ASIS) · **suprapatellar pouch** reaches **46 ± 13 mm (20–74)** above patella (up to 7.4 cm) → avoid **intra-articular pins (infection)**

## X vs Y

| | **Lateral locked plate** | **Retrograde nail** | **DFR** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Best for | Comminuted metaphyseal, periprosthetic, articular (after lagging) | Extra-articular, simple articular, open-box TKA | Elderly + OA, unreconstructable, loose TKA |
| Problem | Varus collapse, rotation malunion, IT band, nonunion \~20% | Malalignment, knee pain | Longevity, mechanical failure |
| WB | WBAT OK | WBAT OK | **Immediate** |

| | **Blade plate** | **DCS** | **LCP** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Insertion | Precise 3-plane | Forgiving sagittal | Multiplanar |
| Bone removed | — | **Most** | — |
| Coronal / Hoffa control | ✗ | ✗ | **✓** |

**"Pre-op CT likely shows?"** → **coronal (Hoffa) fracture of lateral condyle**

**"Commonest Hoffa — screw orientation?"** → **A→P across lateral condyle**

**"Hoffa — true?"** → **commoner in open fractures**

**"Implant controlling coronal fractures best?"** → **locking plate**

**"Most appropriate use of locking plates?"** → **osteoporotic periprosthetic distal femur**

**"Lateral locking plate NOT appropriate for?"** → **partial articular (33B) medial condyle**

**"Osteoporotic comminuted metaphyseal + sagittal split?"** → **lag screws then locking plate**

**"Check intercondylar screw length?"** → **AP with leg 30° internally rotated**

**"Submuscular locking plate ↑ risk of?"** → **malreduction (rotational)**

**"Lateral plate failure risk factor?"** → **short working length**

**"Varus + recurvatum during nailing — blocking screws?"** → **AP medial to nail + ML anterior to nail in distal segment**

**"Highest axial stability, medial comminution, osteoporosis?"** → **lateral locked + medial plate**

**"Nail-plate vs lateral plate?"** → **↓ reoperation**

**"Contraindication to retrograde nail around TKA?"** → **distal femoral replacement**

**"WB after lateral locked plate, 73-yo?"** → **WBAT**

**"Elderly intra-articular + OA?"** → **DFR**

**"Plate + immediate WB vs nail in elderly?"** → **no difference in function**

**"Oligotrophic nonunion?"** → **plate + autograft**

**"Pulse back after traction but different?"** → **ABI**
