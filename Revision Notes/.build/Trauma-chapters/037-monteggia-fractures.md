# Monteggia Fractures

*Trauma · 37 / 67*

## 1 Basics

### Definition & epidemiology

**Monteggia** — **proximal ⅓ ulna fracture + radial head dislocation** (PRUJ disruption)

**Who** — **rare in adults** (high energy) · **children 4–10 yr** commoner, different protocol

**Associated** — olecranon fracture-dislocation, radial head (\~5%), coronoid, LCL injury, **terrible triad**

**Key ligament** — **annular ligament**

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Bado classification — direction of radial head dislocation

**Rule** — **radial head dislocates toward the apex of the ulnar fracture** (apex anterior → anterior)

| Bado | Share | Pattern |
|---|---|---|
| **I** | **60%** | Proximal / middle ulna, **anterior** dislocation — **commonest in children + young adults** |
| **II** | 15% | **Posterior** dislocation — **70–80% of adult Monteggia**; **worst prognosis** |
| **III** | 20% | Ulnar **metaphysis** (distal to coronoid), **lateral** dislocation — **most often irreducible (annular ligament)** |
| **IV** | 5% | **Ulna + radius** fractures, dislocation any direction |

![Bado type I — anterior dislocation](../Orthobullets/attachments/ob1024-type-i-lateral.jpg)

| Jupiter (type II) | Ulnar fracture level |
|---|---|
| **IIA** | **Coronoid** level |
| **IIB** | Metaphyseal-diaphyseal junction |
| **IIC** | Distal to coronoid (diaphysis) |
| **IID** | Extends into distal half |

![Jupiter classification of Bado II](../Orthobullets/attachments/ob1024-jupiter-classification-of-type-ii.jpg)

**Monteggia "variant"** — + **radial head / neck fracture** → separate approach; ↑ OA, instability, nonunion, ↓ ROM

### Key radiology findings

**Views** — AP + lateral **elbow, wrist, forearm**; **radiocapitellar line** congruent on every view

**CT** — coronoid, olecranon, radial head involvement

## 3 Presentation

**PIN palsy** — **most likely nerve injury**; **wrist extension in radial deviation** (ECRL spared), weak thumb + **MCP extension**, sensation intact

## 5 Management

```
Monteggia fracture
├─ Child, stable ulna, reduced RC joint → closed reduction + cast (supination for Bado I / III)
├─ Adult (almost all) → ORIF ulna (plate) → radial head usually reduces
│      └─ radial head still out → check ulna (revise) → then open reduction (annular ligament)
└─ Transverse / short oblique, easily reduced → closed reduction + IM fixation of ulna
```

### Treatment options

| Option | Indication |
|---|---|
| **Closed reduction + cast** | **Children**, stable transverse ulna; **supination for Bado I + III**; oblique / unstable → pinning |
| **ORIF ulna + closed radial head reduction** | **Adults**, open, **unstable (long oblique)**, comminuted |
| **ORIF ulna + open radial head reduction** | **Radial head won't reduce after correct ulnar ORIF**, complex injury, **variants** |
| Closed reduction + **IM ulnar fixation** | Transverse / short oblique, easily reduced |

**Technique** — lateral decubitus; posterior incision lateral to olecranon tip; **FCU / anconeus** proximally, **FCU / ECU** distally; **dorsal plating**; **dorsal cortex** to judge length; **distractor** if comminuted; fluoroscopy through full motion

**Radial head open reduction** — **posterolateral (Kocher)**; separate lateral incision

### Persistent radial head dislocation — ulnar malreduction / length

**After ulnar fixation, radiocapitellar joint dislocated** — **revise ulnar ORIF** (✗ radial neck osteotomy first, ✗ radial head arthroplasty, ✗ splint in supination, ✗ shortening osteotomy)

**Commonest cause of persistent / recurrent dislocation** — **failure to restore ulnar length / anatomic alignment** (✗ annular ligament not repaired, ✗ missed radial head fracture, ✗ LCL, ✗ fixation rigidity)

**Late (6 mo) subluxation + stiffness** — **ulnar corrective osteotomy + open radial head reduction**

### Irreducible radial head — annular ligament interposition

**Non-concentric after reduction (child, posterolateral)** — **annular ligament interposed** (✗ LUCL, ✗ MCL bands, ✗ anconeus) · **Bado III** most often

**How common** — "often found interposed" (Orthobullets) · "rare complication" in adults (OB) — read the options

### Radial head excision — MCL competence

**Chronic malunion** — ulnar osteotomy + radial head reduction · or **radial head excision** (restores rotation)

**Must be competent before excision** — **anterior bundle of MCL** (primary valgus stabiliser) (✗ posterior bundle — secondary, ✗ brachialis, ✗ RCL / LUCL — varus)

## 6 Complications

| Complication | Fact |
|---|---|
| **PIN palsy** | **Up to 10%** acute; blunt trauma from radial head → **observe 2–3 months** (usually resolves) → NCS if not |
| **Radial head malunion / redislocation** | **Ulnar malalignment** |
| **Stiffness** | **Diagnosis delayed > 2–3 wk** → complications ↑↑ |
| Synostosis, nonunion, plate failure | Type II: **nonunion + plate failure** |

### Outcomes & prognostic factors

**Bado II** — **highest complication rate**, worst prognosis; nearly all lose some ROM (✗ full recovery, ✗ casting = surgery, ✗ nerve injury common, ✗ ulnohumeral instability)

**Worse DASH** — **radial head / neck fracture** (✗ age, ✗ radius injury type, ✗ treatment method)

## X vs Y

| | **Monteggia** | **Galeazzi** |
|---|---|---|
| Fracture | **Proximal ulna** | **Distal ⅓ radius** |
| Joint | **Radial head / PRUJ** | **DRUJ** |
| Key to reduction | **Ulnar length + alignment** | Radial ORIF then DRUJ |
| Nerve | **PIN** | — |

## How it's asked

**"Apex-anterior proximal ulna — radial head direction?"** → **anterior**

**"Radial head out after ulnar fixation?"** → **revision ORIF of ulna**

**"Commonest cause of persistent dislocation?"** → **failure to restore ulnar length**

**"Pain + ↓ motion 6 months after ORIF?"** → **inadequate ulnar reduction**

**"Child, non-concentric after closed reduction?"** → **annular ligament interposition**

**"Bado II in 54-yr-old — counsel?"** → **high complication rate**

**"Worse functional scores?"** → **radial head / neck fracture**

**"Must be competent before radial head excision?"** → **anterior bundle of MCL**
