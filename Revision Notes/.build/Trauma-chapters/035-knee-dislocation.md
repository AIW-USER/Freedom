# Knee Dislocation

*Trauma · 35 / 67*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Incidence** — **0.02%** of orthopaedic injuries; under-reported — **\~50% self-reduce** before ED

**Who** — **M:F 4:1** · **morbid obesity → "ultra-low-energy" dislocation** with ADLs + ↑ neurovascular injury

**Mechanism** — high energy (MVC, **dashboard → axial load on flexed knee**, fall) · low energy (sport, walking) · **hyperextension → anterior** · **posterior force on proximal tibia → posterior**

**Associated** — **vascular**, **common peroneal nerve (\~25%)**, **fractures 60%**, patellar tendon rupture, avulsions, displaced menisci

### Anatomy

**Ligaments at risk** — ACL, PCL, MCL, LCL, PLC (bony stability minimal)

**Popliteal artery tethered** — **proximally at adductor hiatus** (fibrous tunnel) + **distally at soleus arch**

**Geniculate collaterals** — can give **palpable pulses despite complete popliteal occlusion**

### KD classification & cruciate injury pattern

| Kennedy (tibial direction) | Share | Key fact |
|---|---|---|
| **Anterior** | **30–50% — most common** | **Hyperextension**; vascular injury = **intimal tear (traction)** |
| **Posterior** | 30–40% — 2nd | **Highest vascular injury → complete popliteal artery tear** |
| Lateral | 13% | Varus / valgus |
| Medial | 3% | Varus / valgus |
| **Rotational (posterolateral)** | 4% | **Usually irreducible** (buttonholed MFC) |

| Schenck | Ligaments torn |
|---|---|
| **KD I** | Multiligament with **ACL or PCL** intact |
| **KD II** | **ACL + PCL** only |
| **KD III** | ACL + PCL + **PMC (IIIM)** or **PLC / LCL (IIIL)** — **IIIL → highest peroneal nerve risk** |
| **KD IV** | ACL + PCL + PMC + PLC — **highest vascular injury (5–15%)** |
| **KD V** | Multiligament + **periarticular fracture** |

**KD IV cruciate pattern (TO)** — **ACL midsubstance + PCL femoral avulsion** · ACL: midsubstance 45% > femoral 34% > tibial 21% · PCL: **femoral avulsion 76%** > midsubstance 17% > tibial 7%

## 3 Presentation

**Diagnosis** — **instability on exam** (X-ray may be normal); **recurvatum** in extension (✗ procurvatum)

**Dimple / pucker sign** — **MFC buttonholed through medial capsule** → **irreducible posterolateral** dislocation → **no repeated closed reduction (skin necrosis)**

**Exam before + after reduction** — DP + PT pulses vs other side; peroneal + tibial nerves; **serial exams mandatory**

### Clinical tests for combined injuries (dial, ERR, collaterals)

| Test (TO) | Positive means |
|---|---|
| **Dial test ↑ ER (> 10–15°) at 30° only** | **PLC** |
| **Dial test at 30° AND 90°** | **PLC + PCL** |
| **Anterior drawer with foot in EXTERNAL rotation** | **ACL + MCL** (anteromedial rotatory instability) |
| **External rotation recurvatum test** | **ACL + PLC** (recurvatum, varus, anterior tibial translation; sensitivity 7.5%) |
| **Varus / valgus stress at 30°** | **Isolated collateral (LCL / MCL)** — **best position**; > 10 mm + soft end-point = complete |
| Varus stress opens **in full extension** | **LCL + PLC + PCL** |
| Valgus stress opens **in full extension** | **MCL + POL / posteromedial capsule + PCL** |

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Study | Finding / use |
|---|---|
| Pre- + post-reduction AP / lateral | **Asymmetric joint space**, **Segond (lateral tibial avulsion)**, osteochondral defects |
| CT | Fractures on post-reduction films (eminence, tubercle, plateau) |
| **MRI** | **After reduction, before hardware** — ligaments, menisci, planning |

### Vascular injury — incidence, ABI, CT angiography & exploration

**Incidence** — **5–15% all** (Orthobullets) · 10–40% / 18–45% / \~16% / \~25% (OB) · **40–50% anterior or posterior** · **low-velocity anterior \~40%** (OB) — read the options

**ABI = ankle systolic ÷ brachial systolic** (normal 0.9–1.1) — **< 0.9 → concern** (e.g. **0.8**) · **< 0.85** (TO); ABI < 0.9: **sens 87%, spec 97%**; **> 0.9 → NPV 100%**

```
After reduction
├─ Pulses absent / cool limb → emergent vascular exploration (no imaging delay)
├─ Pulses present, ABI < 0.9 → CT angiography → vascular surgery if injury
├─ Pulses present, ABI > 0.9 → serial exams (2–4 hourly, 48 h)
└─ Pulses absent pre-reduction, return after (TO) → CT angiography (intimal tear)
```

**Present pulses** — **do not exclude** popliteal injury (collaterals) · **pulse oximetry** doesn't exclude either

**Ischaemia > 8 h** — **amputation up to 86%** (Orthobullets) · > 6–8 h **\~85%** (OB)

**Bilateral, ABI 0.78 left / 0.96 right** — **CTA left only, observe right**

**ABI 0.0 after posterior dislocation** — **complete popliteal artery tear**

**Ischaemic after reduction, BMI 52** — **emergent vascular exploration** (✗ ex-fix first, ✗ CTA, ✗ IR stenting) · on-table angiogram

**Surgery for** — flow-limiting intimal flap, absent pulses, ischaemia, haemorrhage, expanding haematoma · **non-flow-limiting flap → observe** (anticoagulation doesn't help)

## 5 Management

### Reduction & initial stabilisation (irreducible posterolateral dislocation, spanning ex-fix)

**Orthopaedic emergency** — **reduce first, then reassess pulses**

| Direction | Reduction |
|---|---|
| Anterior | Traction + anterior translation of **femur** |
| Posterior | Traction, extension, anterior translation of **tibia** |
| Medial / lateral | Traction + translation |
| Rotatory | Traction + rotate opposite to deformity |

**Splint** — **20–30° flexion**

**Absent pulses, peroneal palsy (wrestler)** — **closed reduction in ED + reassess** (✗ acute reconstruction, ✗ MRI, ✗ arteriogram first)

**Symmetric pulses, ACL + PCL + PLC on MRI** — **emergent reduction + EUA, repeat NV exam, admit overnight, delayed reconstruction** (✗ discharge, ✗ angiogram routinely, ✗ immediate reconstruction)

**Irreducible posterolateral + pucker sign** — **reduction in OR** → **open reduction via anteromedial approach** (medial parapatellar) + **spanning ex-fix**; pulses still absent → **popliteal exploration** (✗ femoral distractor closed, ✗ traction pin, ✗ posterior approach)

**After vascular repair + fasciotomy, unstable knee** — **spanning ex-fix** (✗ cylinder cast, ✗ acute ligament surgery, ✗ arthroscopy)

### Operative criteria

| Open reduction | Spanning ex-fix |
|---|---|
| **Irreducible / posterolateral** | **Vascular repair** (protect it) |
| Open fracture-dislocation | Open fracture-dislocation |
| **Obesity** (hard closed) | **Compartment syndrome** |
| Vascular injury | **Obesity (BMI > 50)**, polytrauma |

**Ex-fix position** — **20–30° flexion**, reduced in AP + sagittal planes

**Nonop definitive** — rare; **worse outcomes** (stiffness + instability)

### Timing of ligament reconstruction

**Acute (< 3 wk)** — **↑ residual instability** + **↑ stiffness recalcitrant to treatment** (✗ less instability, ✗ best outcomes)

**Staged** — more good / excellent outcomes (older data; now ≈ equal); **no algorithm proven superior**

**Early treatment improves outcomes** (Orthobullets treatment section) vs staged better (technique section) — **read the options**

**Arthroscopic** — risky if large capsular tear (**fluid extravasation → compartment syndrome**); **PLC + PMC need open** reconstruction

**Hinged ex-fix** — adjunct, ↓ re-rupture

### Combined ligament reconstruction — timing, sequence & graft fixation order

**ACL + PCL (± collaterals) (TO)** — **simultaneous reconstruction at 2–4 weeks**

**Fixation order (TO)** — **PCL femoral → ACL femoral → PCL tibial → ACL tibial**

**Tension angles (TO)** — **PCL at 70–90°** (anterolateral bundle 90°, restore step-off) **first** → **LCL at 20–30°** + slight valgus → rest of PLC at 60°, neutral rotation → **ACL at 30°** → posteromedial corner last

**PLC before ACL (TO)** — ACL tension in PLC-deficient knee **↑ external tibial rotation**

**ACL + grade 1 MCL (TO)** — **hinged brace (locked 30°) \~2 wk, then ACL reconstruction** (✗ MCL repair, ✗ brace 1 month, ✗ conservative)

| MCL grade with ACL (TO) | Plan |
|---|---|
| **1** | ACL surgery at **2 wk**, no motion restriction |
| **2** | ACL within 2 wk; **7 days** brace locked at 30° |
| **3** | ACL at **3 wk**; 10–14 days locked at 30° |

**Acute MCL repair (TO)** — intra-articular entrapment, large bony avulsion, plateau fracture, complete tibial-side avulsion (athlete), AMRI, valgus laxity at 0° in valgus knee · **MCL heals nonop > 90%**

**ACL + PLC (LCL, popliteus, biceps), PCL intact (TO)** — **ACL + PLC reconstruction** within 3 wk (✗ PLC **repair** — higher failure; ✗ delayed ACL at 6 wk)

**PLC repair only for (TO)** — **avulsions off fibular head** (FCL, PFL, biceps) or popliteus, capsular / meniscocapsular

**PLC static stabilisers (TO)** — **FCL (LCL), popliteus tendon, popliteofibular ligament**; up to **16%** of knee ligament injuries

| Fanelli PLC (TO) | Findings |
|---|---|
| A | ↑ ER 10° — PFL, popliteus |
| B | + slight varus (5–10 mm) — + FCL |
| C | + severe varus (> 10 mm) — + capsule, cruciate |

**Dial + at 30° and 90° (TO)** — **arthroscopic PCL + LaPrade PLC reconstruction** · **LaPrade = anatomical (LCL, popliteus, PFL)** · Larson = LCL + PFL only

### O'Donoghue unhappy triad

**Triad (TO)** — **ACL + MCL + medial meniscus** (lateral force, fixed foot) (✗ PCL, ✗ LCL) · arthroscopically **lateral meniscus** tears commoner with acute ACL

## 6 Complications

| Complication | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Stiffness (arthrofibrosis)** | **Most common (38%)**; delayed mobilisation → early motion, LOA, MUA |
| Laxity / instability | **37%**; redislocation rare |
| **Vascular** | Emergent repair + **prophylactic fasciotomy** |
| **Peroneal nerve** | **25%** (16–40%) |

### Peroneal nerve injury — examination & EMG

**Commonest nerve** — **common peroneal (25%)** > tibial (subclinical tibial in \~half of peroneal cases); **most associated structure** after posterior dislocation (✗ popliteal artery 16%)

**Pattern** — **KD IIIL**, anterior + posterolateral dislocations; ↑ with **vascular injury**, male, **↑ BMI**, **fibular head fracture**

**Exam** — **weak ankle dorsiflexion**, EHL (great toe extension), **eversion (peroneus brevis)**, numb **dorsum + 1st web** (✗ plantar foot, ✗ plantarflexion, ✗ sural, ✗ saphenous)

**Level** — **common peroneal at fibular neck** (✗ deep / superficial branches alone)

**Recovery** — **\~50% partial** (Orthobullets) · complete 30–50% · **partial palsy \~85% vs complete \~35%** · < ⅓ regain dorsiflexion (OB)

**EMG / NCS** — **unreliable before 4–6 wk**; at **3 months with weak TA firing → NCS / EMG** (✗ PT alone, ✗ neurolysis, ✗ PTT transfer yet)

**Partial palsy** — wait **6–12 months**; **AFO** vs equinus · **complete neurotmesis on early EMG → early repair / transfer**

### Foot drop — tendon transfer (tibialis posterior)

**Transfer** — **tibialis posterior: medial navicular → dorsal lateral cuneiform through interosseous membrane** (gold standard) (✗ FDL, ✗ PL, ✗ PB — peroneal-innervated, ✗ FHL→EHL)

**Bridle procedure** — PTT + **tibialis anterior + peroneus longus** anastomosis · tenodesis to TA → weaker

**1 yr foot drop, nerve in continuity** — **PTT transfer** (✗ re-neurolysis, ✗ cable graft, ✗ EHL transfer, ✗ ankle fusion)

**Contraindication** — **rigid deformity / can't passively dorsiflex past neutral** (✗ TA 0/5, ✗ prior nerve surgery, ✗ L4 radiculopathy, ✗ corrected gastrocnemius contracture) · **weak PTT / S1 radiculopathy** also limits

### Septic arthritis after reconstruction

**4 wk after ACL, fever, ESR 105, CRP 44 (TO)** — **arthroscopic debridement + graft retention + IV antibiotics** (✗ graft removal first)

| Gächter (TO) | Findings | Approach |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Turbid effusion, hyperaemic synovium | Arthroscopic |
| 2 | **Purulent**, fibrin, hypertrophic synovium | Arthroscopic |
| 3 | Adhesions, necrotic synovium / cartilage | Arthrotomy |
| 4 | Cartilage necrosis, **bone erosion** | Arthrotomy |
