# Scapulothoracic Dissociation

*Trauma · 54 / 67*

## 1 Basics

### Mechanism & associations

**Mechanism** — **lateral traction** on the shoulder girdle (high energy, motorcycle) · heart, chest wall, lung trauma

**Orthopaedic** — scapula, **clavicle** (widely displaced), **AC separation**, SC dislocation · **flail extremity 52%**

**Vascular** — **subclavian artery (commonest)** > axillary

**Neurological** — **up to 90% (> 90%)**; ipsilateral **brachial plexus**, often **complete** · **neuro > vascular** injuries

### Anatomy

**Scapulothoracic joint** — sliding articulation over **ribs 2–7** · abduction **2:1 (GH 120° : ST 60°)**

## 3 Presentation

**Exam** — massive shoulder swelling + bruising · **↓ / absent pulses** (hallmark) · neurological deficits (**critical**)

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Study | Finding |
|---|---|
| **AP chest** | **Medial scapular border > 1 cm further from spinous process** than other side |
| Shoulder views | Displaced clavicle fracture, **AC widening**, SC dislocation |
| **Chest CT (axial)** | **Asymmetric spinous process–scapula distance** |
| **Angiogram** | Subclavian / axillary injury |

## 5 Management

```
Scapulothoracic dissociation
├─ No significant vascular injury, stable → supportive care (collaterals)
├─ Unstable + axillary injury → high lateral thoracotomy + repair
├─ Unstable + subclavian injury → median sternotomy + repair
├─ Clavicle / AC injury → ORIF (avoid neurogenic nonunion)
└─ Complete brachial plexus injury → early forequarter amputation
```

**Flail extremity treatment** — sling, tendon transfers, **forequarter amputation** · **\~21% early above-elbow amputation**

## 6 Complications

### Prognosis — neurovascular injury and flail extremity

**Mortality** — **10%** (✗ 30%)

**Best predictor of function** — **neurological status / deficit** (✗ open wound size, contamination, debridement timing, ✗ pulses, ✗ age)

**Worst outcome** — **complete brachial plexus injury** · vascular injuries usually repairable → don't worsen outcome

**Most likely outcome with neuro injury** — **flail extremity** (death also common)

**Uniquely associated** — **flail upper extremity** (✗ malunion, ✗ intubation, ✗ abdominal / C-spine injury)

**EMG** — **3–6 wk** after injury

## How it's asked

**"Best predictor of functional outcome?"** → **neurological deficit**

**"Likely outcome with neuro injury?"** → **flail extremity**

**"Exam finding predicting outcome?"** → **neurological compromise**

**"Most important outcome predictor (open humerus too)?"** → **neurovascular status**

**"Uniquely associated?"** → **flail upper extremity**

**"Prognosis — true?"** → **complete brachial plexus injury has the worst outcome**
