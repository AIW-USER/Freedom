# Proximal Humerus Fracture Nonunion and Malunion

*Trauma · 47 / 67*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology & risk factors

**Proximal humerus** — **4–5% of all fractures**

| Nonunion risk | Factor |
|---|---|
| **Fracture** | **2-part surgical neck**, **head split**, **displaced tuberosity** |
| **Patient** | **Osteoporosis**, CKD, alcohol / steroids, smoking |

**Malunion origin** — initial displacement, **secondary displacement**, fixation failure

| Malunion | Typical position |
|---|---|
| **Head** | Varus / valgus, **impacted > 1 cm**, articular incongruity |
| **Greater tuberosity** | **Posterior, superior, externally rotated** |
| **Lesser tuberosity** | **Medial** |

**Associated** — cuff tear, **osteonecrosis**, stiffness, PTOA, instability, **subacromial impingement**

### Anatomy

**Head** — spheroidal 90%, \~**43 mm**; **30° retroversion** (to transepicondylar axis); **neck-shaft 130–140°**

**Greater tuberosity** — medial edge **\~10 mm lateral** to canal axis · superior edge **\~6 mm below** head top

## 3 Presentation

**Symptoms** — pain, weakness, atrophy

**Cuff** — **GT malunion → weak abduction + ER** · **LT malunion → weak IR** · head malunion → apprehension

**Check** — **axillary nerve**

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

**Views** — true AP, scapular Y, **axillary** ± apical oblique, Velpeau, West Point

### Diagnostic criteria

| Malunion | Threshold |
|---|---|
| **Head** | **> 45° in any plane**, symptomatic incongruity, **neck-shaft < 120° or > 150°** |
| **Tuberosity** | **> 1 cm** from native position |

**CT** — bone stock, **glenoid version + stock**, articular injury · **MRI** — cuff / labrum, **deltoid atrophy (axillary nerve)**, LHB, **osteonecrosis**

**Labs** — CBC, ESR, CRP, cultures (infection) · EMG if nerve concern

### Classifications

| Beredjiklian | Description |
|---|---|
| **I** | **Tuberosity malposition (> 1 cm)** |
| **II** | **Articular incongruity** (extension, OA) |
| **III** | **Articular surface malalignment (> 45°)** |

| Boileau (fracture sequelae) | Description | Arthroplasty |
|---|---|---|
| **I** | **Head necrosis / impaction** | Anatomic possible (tuberosities preserved) |
| **II** | **Chronic dislocation / fracture-dislocation** | Anatomic possible |
| **III** | **Surgical neck nonunion** | **Reverse** |
| **IV** | **Severe tuberosity malunion** | **Reverse** |

## 5 Management

```
Proximal humerus nonunion / malunion
├─ Low demand / painless / can't rehab → NSAIDs, physio, injection
├─ Good bone stock, articular surface + head blood supply intact → ORIF ± osteotomy ± graft
└─ Poor bone stock, incongruity, AVN, chronic dislocation, resorbed tuberosities, cuff tear → arthroplasty (reverse preferred)
```

### Malunion — osteotomy vs reverse shoulder arthroplasty

**Head deformity** — minor: **tuberoplasty ± acromioplasty, capsular release, bursectomy** · severe: **varus / valgus ± rotational osteotomy + lateral plate** (young / active; ± strut allograft)

**Greater tuberosity** — **< 1.5 cm → arthroscopic subacromial decompression ± cuff repair** · **> 1.5 cm → tuberosity osteotomy**

**24-yr, 45° varus after ORIF, stiff at 9 months** — **revision ORIF + osteotomy** (✗ MUA, ✗ resurfacing, ✗ hemi, ✗ RSA)

**Reverse vs hemiarthroplasty for sequelae** — **greater functional improvement**, **lower complication + reoperation** (less cuff dependence) (✗ more complications, ✗ weaker)

**68-yr active, surgical neck nonunion, osteoporotic** — **reverse TSA** (Boileau III) (✗ anatomic TSA, ✗ hemi — inferior, ✗ locking plate)

**Nonop outcome** — impacted varus / valgus → good–excellent, **\~90%** of normal function

**Surgery for malunion** — **higher complications** than acute fracture surgery

### Nonunion — ORIF with bone graft vs arthroplasty

**69-yr, painful nonunion after ORIF** — **shoulder arthroplasty** (✗ revision ORIF, ✗ valgus osteotomy, ✗ fusion, ✗ head resection)

**72-yr, 2-part nonunion, GOOD bone stock, no OA / AVN, tuberosities fine** — **ORIF (locked plate) ± bone graft** (✗ CRPP, ✗ hemi, ✗ graft alone, ✗ RSA + latissimus — latissimus gives **ER**, not IR)

**Arthroplasty instead when** — **marked osteopenia, AVN, resorbed tuberosities, pre-existing cuff tear**

**45-yr nonunion after IM nail** — **compression plating + bone graft** (✗ exchange nail, ✗ hemi, ✗ RSA — too young, ✗ add screws) · union **> 90%**

## 6 Complications

**After surgery** — persistent pain + weakness, stiffness, loss of fixation, infection, bleeding

## X vs Y

| | **ORIF ± osteotomy** | **Reverse TSA** |
|---|---|---|
| Bone stock | **Adequate** | Poor |
| Head | Intact blood supply, congruent | AVN, incongruity, chronic dislocation |
| Patient | Young / active | Older; Boileau III–IV |

## How it's asked

**"24-yr, 45° varus malunion?"** → **revision ORIF + osteotomy**

**"Reverse vs hemi for malunion?"** → **greater functional improvement**

**"68-yr osteoporotic surgical neck nonunion?"** → **reverse TSA**

**"69-yr painful nonunion after ORIF?"** → **shoulder arthroplasty**

**"72-yr nonunion, good bone stock?"** → **ORIF ± bone graft**

**"45-yr nonunion after IM nail?"** → **compression plating + bone graft**
