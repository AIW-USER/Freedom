# Domestic and Elder Abuse

*Trauma · 16 / 67*

## 1 Basics

### IPV epidemiology

| Fact | Value |
|---|---|
| Women experiencing domestic violence | **\~25%** (Orthobullets) · lifetime **\~30% / 33% (PRAISE)** (OB) |
| Women in ER with injuries due to DV | **up to 35%** |
| Past-year IPV in women at fracture clinics | **1 in 6** · **32%** within 1 yr before orthopaedic visit |
| Fracture-clinic visits directly from IPV | **1 in 50** |
| Ever asked about IPV in health care | Only **14%** |
| Men who are victims | 15% |
| Children abused in DV homes | **\~50%** |
| Surgeon-perceived vs actual prevalence | **\~10% vs up to 40% → 4× under-estimated** |

**DV = commonest cause of non-fatal injury to women**

### Elder abuse epidemiology

**Scale** — \~2 million Americans / yr; **1 in 25** over 65; widely **under-reported**; forms: physical, mental, neglect, financial, abandonment

## 2 Mechanism

### IPV risk factors

| Risk factor | Note |
|---|---|
| **Female** | — |
| **Age 19–29** (Orthobullets) · **16–49** (OB) · 20–29 (OB) | Young |
| **Pregnancy** | **Highest risk**; 40–60% of battered women abused in pregnancy |
| **Short-term relationship** | — |
| **Cohabitation** | — |
| **Low income / SES** (< $10,000 / yr) | ✗ "equal in all incomes" |
| Alcohol / drug dependence | — |
| ≥ 1 prior child | Higher prevalence with children |
| ✗ Not risk factors | **Prior divorce**, no children, older age, long relationship, living apart |

### Elder abuse risk factors

**Victim** — **↑ age**, **functional disability**, **cognitive impairment / dementia**, poor physical + emotional health, disruptive / aggressive behaviour, social isolation, poverty, race, prior violence toward abuser; regional child-abuse rates

**Caretaker** — **substance abuse**, mental illness, **financial dependence on elder**, burden perception, shared living, stress

**✗ Gender** — **not** a risk factor for elder abuse

## 3 Presentation

### Red flags (injury pattern)

**Injuries** — **inconsistent with history**, **long delay to treatment**, **repeat injuries**, **multiple injuries of varying ages**, multiple providers / EDs, unusual patterns

**Most common IPV manifestation** — **head, neck, face injuries** (unwitnessed)

**Elder abuse** — **long-bone + rib fractures**, bruises **> 5 cm on face, neck, back**, burns, lacerations, poor hygiene, behaviour change

### Behaviour

**Victim** — changed affect, withdrawn, seeks partner's approval, **repeated ED visits**, wants to stay in hospital, **missed work / ↓ productivity**, chronic headaches, sleep disturbance, high-risk behaviour, GI / psychosomatic / pelvic symptoms

**Abuser** — won't leave, overly attentive, hostile, **answers for the patient**

**Repeated ED visits, vague pain, frightened, ecchymoses, negative imaging** — **IPV** (✗ malingering, ✗ CRPS, ✗ anxiety, ✗ fibromyalgia)

### Barriers to disclosure

**Barriers** — **fear of retaliation**, shame, embarrassment, **reluctance with male physicians**, custody fears, downplaying

**Emotional abuse** — **harder to detect** than physical

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

**No skeletal survey** in competent adults (or pregnancy)

**Documentation** — exact quotes, abuser's name, body maps / drawings, **photographs with consent**, demeanour, opinion if injuries don't fit story

## 5 Management

```
Suspected abuse
├─ Child → report (all states)
├─ Elder → report (almost all states); in danger → admit / transfer to ED
└─ Adult IPV → interview alone, screen, document, social work, resources
       └─ report only per state law / with consent
```

### Screening & management

**First step in suspected IPV** — **speak with patient alone, privately, non-judgementally** → resources (✗ confront partner, ✗ treat + discharge, ✗ mandatory psych referral, ✗ call police first)

**Pregnant, inconsistent history, bruises** — after treating fracture → **social work consult** (✗ arthroscopy, ✗ MRI, ✗ skeletal survey, ✗ urgent ORIF)

**Shoulder dislocation + positive pregnancy test** — **ask privately about injury + home safety** (✗ skeletal survey, ✗ nutritionist, ✗ anorexia admission, ✗ MRI)

**Treat injuries promptly** regardless of social situation (✗ "resume care once social situation assessed")

**Interdisciplinary collaboration** → better outcomes

### Reporting obligations

| Victim | Duty |
|---|---|
| **Child** | **Mandatory in all states** |
| **Elder** | **Mandatory in almost all states**; **hospitalise if in immediate danger** |
| **Adult IPV** | **Not standardised**; many states need consent; physician **has no authority to provide protection** → encourage victim to report |

**Federal law does NOT mandate photographs** (EXCEPT answer) — only with consent

**AAOS roles** — know state laws, be aware you may be first physician, keep accurate records, transfer endangered elder to ED + notify, advocate legislation
