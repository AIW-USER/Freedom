# Open Fractures Management

*Trauma · 42 / 67*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Incidence** — **30.7 / 100,000 / yr** · average age **45**

**Commonest sites** — **tibia + finger phalanx** · tibia = commonest long-bone fracture, **up to \~24% open**

**Associated injuries** — **30%** · **compartment syndrome still possible** with an open wound

**Wound near a fracture** — treat as **open until proven otherwise**

**Psychiatric history** — **pre-existing depression = independent predictor of complications**; depression + substance abuse commonest in polytrauma

## 3 Presentation

**Exam** — wound size **may not reflect deep damage** · **ABI if vascular concern** (> 0.9 normal; **< 0.9 → vascular consult + angiogram**)

**Traumatic arthrotomy** — **saline load test** or **CT (more sensitive for knee)**

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

**Radiographs** — joint above + below · **CT** — periarticular injury, **knee arthrotomy (intra-articular air)**

### Classification & scoring (Gustilo, Ganga, AO soft tissue)

| Gustilo (TO) | Wound | Soft tissue |
|---|---|---|
| I | **< 1 cm**, clean | Minimal |
| II | **1–10 cm**, moderate | **No periosteal stripping** |
| **IIIA** | Any size (**high energy**) | **Local coverage possible** |
| **IIIB** | — | **Periosteal stripping + exposed bone → flap needed** |
| **IIIC** | — | **Arterial injury needing repair** |

**6 × 4 cm dirty crush wound, distal tibia, exposed bone + periosteal stripping (TO)** — **IIIB**

**Automatically type III (TO)** — **gunshot**, **farm injury**

**Gustilo drawbacks (TO)** — many modifications, wide IIIB spectrum, wound-size based, **doesn't address salvage**, **poor interobserver reliability**

| Ganga Hospital Open Injury Score (TO) | Points |
|---|---|
| **Covering (skin + fascia)** | 1–5 (circumferential skin loss = 5) |
| **Skeletal (bone + joint)** | 1–5 (**bone loss ≥ 4 cm = 5**) |
| **Functional (musculotendinous + nerve)** | 1–5 (loss of ≥ 2 compartments / subtotal amputation = 5) |
| **Comorbidities — 2 each** | **Debridement > 12 h**, **age > 65**, sewage / farm contamination, **SBP < 90**, compartment syndrome / other major injury same limb, DM / cardiorespiratory, **polytrauma ISS > 25** / fat embolism |

**Ganga facts (TO)** — designed for **IIIB**; **scored AFTER debridement** (✗ in ED before); **max 29**; **better reliability than Gustilo**; **doesn't predict survival** of polytrauma

**Ganga thresholds (TO)** — **≤ 14 → salvage** · **15–16 = grey zone** · **≥ 17 → amputation** · ("≥ 14 amputation threshold" in another TO item — read the options)

**AO soft-tissue (TO)** — **extensive closed degloving = IC4** (IC closed vs IO open)

**Tscherne** — closed / open soft-tissue grading

## 5 Management

```
Open fracture
├─ ED: IV antibiotics (ASAP, < 1–3 h), tetanus, splint, saline dressing
├─ OR: debridement (< 24 h; IIIB tibia < 12 h; gross contamination 6–12 h) + low-pressure saline / soap
│      └─ temporary ex-fix (high-energy LL) ± antibiotic beads / NPWT
└─ Definitive fixation + coverage < 7 days → bone defect: Masquelet / fibula / transport
```

### Antibiotic prophylaxis — choice, timing & infection risk

**First thing to do (grade III tibia)** — **IV antibiotics** (✗ bedside I&D, ✗ splint, ✗ CTA, ✗ OR first)

**Timing** — **as soon as possible**; **> 3 h → ↑ infection (4.7% → 7.4%)**; **within 1 h of arrival**; **> 66 min independent risk in type III tibia** (OB)

**Most modifiable infection risk** — **delay in antibiotics** (✗ free vs rotational flap, ✗ flap at 4 days, ✗ nail vs plate, ✗ debridement at 15 h, ✗ personal transport, ✗ reamed nailing)

**Septic nonunion after 15-h transport delay** — **prolonged time to antibiotics** (✗ coverage < 7 days, ✗ unreamed nail, ✗ vitamin D, ✗ low-pressure irrigation)

**Multidisciplinary training** — **↓ time to antibiotics**

| Wound | Regimen |
|---|---|
| **Gustilo I / II** (e.g. 3 cm wound) | **Cefazolin** (clindamycin / vancomycin if allergic; fluoroquinolone 2nd line) |
| **Gustilo III** | **Cefazolin + aminoglycoside** · or 3rd-gen cephalosporin alone · or vancomycin + cefepime |
| **Farm / soil / bowel** (e.g. hay loft, lawnmower) | **+ high-dose penicillin → Clostridium** |
| Fresh water | Fluoroquinolone or 3rd / 4th-gen cephalosporin |
| Salt water | Doxycycline + ceftazidime or fluoroquinolone |

**Clostridium** — **gram-positive, obligate anaerobic, spore-forming rod**, **catalase negative**; C. perfringens (gas gangrene), C. tetani, C. difficile, **C. botulinum (botulism)**

**Duration** — **24 h after injury if closed primarily** · **24 h after final closure** (**48 h type III**) (Orthobullets) · **72 h or until closure** for grade III tibia (TO) · 24–72 h after I&D (Gustilo note) — read the options

### Tetanus prophylaxis

**Booster within 5 yr** — **no prophylaxis** (✗ vaccine, ✗ TIG)

**Grade II, no booster since high school** — **Td** + cefazolin within 3 h + I&D

**Toxoid** — **0.5 mL** any age · **TIG** — < 5 yr 75 U · 5–10 yr 125 U · > 10 yr 250 U · **separate syringes + sites, IM**

**Depends on** — vaccination history (3 doses), last dose, wound severity

### Debridement as the key infection deterrent

**Most important infection deterrent** — **surgical debridement of devitalised tissue** (✗ bacitracin, ✗ NPWT, ✗ 6-h window, ✗ pulse lavage)

**6-hour rule** — **not supported** · **delayed debridement doesn't ↑ infection if antibiotics given** (TO) · **< 24 h** (GOLIATH: type III); **IIIB tibia < 12 h**; gross contamination **6–12 h**

**Technique** — extend wound longitudinally; **remove bone fragments without soft-tissue attachment**; debride **beyond** zone of injury; repeat every **24–48 h**

**Exception to I&D** — **low-energy puncture open fracture in children**

**ED** — remove gross debris, **don't remove bone**, saline-soaked dressing; **no aggressive / antiseptic irrigation in ED**

**Most common source of infection (TO)** — **hospital-acquired** (✗ site of injury, ✗ soil, ✗ haematogenous)

**Infection risk factors (TO)** — **inadequate skeletal stabilisation**, organic contamination, poor debridement, dead space, debridement > 24 h, shock, ARDS, age > 65, DM, smoking, compartment syndrome, malnutrition

### Irrigation — pressure & solution (FLOW)

**Pressure** — **low-pressure (bulb / gravity) = high-pressure** for **infection + union**; **cheaper**; pulse lavage damages bone, pushes bacteria deep, ↑ rebound contamination at 48 h (✗ nerve injury, ✗ wound problems)

**Solution** — **saline (± castile soap)**; **soap < antibiotic solution for wound-healing problems** · **saline = betadine** · peroxide / iodine / alcohol harm osteoblasts + cartilage

**Volume** — **3 L per Gustilo grade** (**9 L for type III**)

**After debridement of IIIB** — **saline, low-pressure gravity flow**

**Low-pressure parameters (TO)** — 14 psi / 550 pulses·min⁻¹ vs high 70 psi / 1,050

### Negative pressure wound therapy

**Benefits** — **↑ granulation tissue**, **↑ angiogenesis**, ↓ oedema, ↑ wound-bed flow, ↓ complex reconstruction, **outpatient** care (✗ **↓ angiogenesis**, ✗ ↓ bacterial count, ✗ ↓ hospital stay, ✗ fewer debridements)

**NPWT vs conventional (WOLLF)** — **higher cost**; no difference in deep infection, amputation, LOS, disability at 1 yr

**Not an indication (TO)** — **ulcer with necrotic wound bed** (✗ necrotising fasciitis post-debridement, ✗ large diabetic ulcer, ✗ dehiscence)

**Open > 7 days** — ↑ infection

### Local antibiotics

**Beads / spacer** — PMMA + **heat-stable vancomycin / tobramycin**; heavy contamination, large soft-tissue or bony defect

### Temporising spanning external fixation

**Polytrauma, open distal femur + proximal tibia** — **I&D + knee-spanning ex-fix** (✗ traction, ✗ I&D alone, ✗ ORIF, ✗ nails)

**Child (10 yr), open tibia, gravel** — **I&D + ex-fix** (✗ reamed nail — physes, ✗ K-wires, ✗ cast, ✗ submuscular plate) · flexible nails also an option

**Definitive internal fixation vs ex-fix** — **IF: faster union, better function, shorter stay**; **definitive ex-fix → poor function (LEAP)**

### Soft-tissue coverage timing — flap within 7 days

**Target** — **< 7 days**; **infection odds +16% per day beyond day 7** · flap **> 7 days → ↑ amputation, osteomyelitis, pneumonia** (DVT same)

**Older thresholds** — ≤ 72 h (early studies, OB218814) · **LEAP: no difference at 3 days** · closure > 5 days risk (one OB item)

**Flap by tibial level** — **proximal ⅓ → gastrocnemius** · **middle ⅓ → soleus** · **distal ⅓ → free flap (within 7 days)**

**ORIF before / at / after fasciotomy closure** — no difference

### Bone defects — induced membrane (Masquelet) & vascularised fibula

**Masquelet stage 1** — **debridement + cement spacer + temporary fixation**

**Stage 2 at 4–6 wk** — **remove spacer, PRESERVE membrane, graft into cavity (around retained nail)** (✗ debride membrane, ✗ exchange nail, ✗ remove nail, ✗ spacer only)

**Membrane biology** — BMP-2, **VEGF, IL-6, type I collagen** — **peak at 1 month (\~4 wk)**, baseline \~6 months · defects **up to 25 cm**

**7 cm tibial defect, refuses transport** — **ORIF + vascularised fibular autograft** (osteogenic + inductive + conductive) (✗ DBM, ✗ calcium phosphate, ✗ cortical allograft — not osteogenic, ✗ amputation)

**Other** — distraction osteogenesis (transport)

### Aseptic nonunion after nailing — exchange nailing

**"Elephant foot", gaps on 4 cortices after unreamed nail** — **hypertrophic nonunion = lack of stability** (✗ infection, ✗ blood supply — atrophic, ✗ vitamin D, ✗ OI)

**Treatment** — **reamed exchange nailing** (stiffness ∝ diameter⁴, isthmic fit, reaming graft) (✗ observe, ✗ antibiotic nail, ✗ new locking screws, ✗ unreamed exchange)

**Nonunion rates** — **up to 17% closed, 40% open** tibia

**SPRINT** — reamed benefit **only in closed** tibia; open: reamed = unreamed

## 6 Complications

| Complication | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Fracture-related infection** | **< 1% type I → 30% type III** |
| **Osteomyelitis** | **1.8–27%**; **tibia commonest** |
| Risk factors | **Blast mechanism**, acute amputation, **coverage > 7 days**, higher Gustilo, contamination, **tibial location** |
| **Depression** | **Commonest post-op issue** after open tibia (✗ arthritis, ✗ malunion) |

### Outcomes & infection risk factors (blast, definitive ex-fix, location)

**↑ Infection** — **blast mechanism** (✗ coverage 3–7 days, ✗ NPWT, ✗ debridement 6–24 h, ✗ grafting > 3 months)

**Poor function in type III tibia** — **definitive external fixation** (✗ coverage < 3 days, ✗ grafting at 3 months, ✗ debridement 6–24 h, ✗ free flap)

**Ballistic fracture — highest infection** — **tibial shaft** (subcutaneous) (✗ radius, femur, metacarpal, humerus) · **blunt > penetrating** infection

### Psychological sequelae — depression

**Pre-existing depression** — **independent predictor of complications**, ↑ readmission; psychiatric comorbidity **up to 40%**; ortho patients **less** likely to get psych follow-up

**Predictor of depressive symptoms** — **previous psychiatric diagnosis** (also unemployment) (✗ employment, ✗ implant, ✗ male, ✗ private insurance)

### Jehovah's Witness — bloodless resuscitation

**Unstable intra-op, GCS 8, open pelvis** — **plasma expanders** (✗ blood, ✗ cell saver, ✗ ask family, ✗ ethics delay)

**Prohibited** — allogeneic blood, pre-donated autologous · **personal choice** — cell salvage, haemodilution, EPO, dialysis
