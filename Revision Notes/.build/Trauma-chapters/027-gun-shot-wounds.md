# Gun Shot Wounds

*Trauma · 27 / 67*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**GSW** — **2nd leading cause of death in US youth** (after MVA) · buttock GSW ≈ **8%** of extremity GSWs

### Ballistics — kinetic energy transfer

**KE = ½ m v²** — **velocity matters more than mass** (m at 2v > 2m at v); injury rises **exponentially** with velocity

**Damage** — missile passage + **secondary shock wave** + **cavitation**; fractures possible without direct impact

**Max energy transfer** — **penetrating (no exit)** > perforating (exits) · **yaw 90° (sideways)** > nose-on; yaw ↓ with longer travel

**Thresholds (TO)** — **50 m/s penetrates skin** · **65 m/s fractures bone** · larger calibre → slower (TO)

**Contamination (TO)** — **bullets are not sterile** (heat + barrel friction insufficient); skin flora + clothing; **civilian GSW infection risk is LOW**

### Associated conditions

**Lead intoxication (plumbism)** — **intra-articular** missile; **lead soluble in synovial fluid**; blood lead **peaks 4–6 months (TO)** → neurotoxicity, **anaemia**, emesis, **abdominal colic**; local **lead synovitis → chondrolysis, hypertrophic arthritis**

**GSW to hip / acetabulum** — **bowel perforation > vascular > urogenital**

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Velocity classification

| Velocity | Muzzle speed | Weapon | Gustilo equivalent |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Low** | **< 350 m/s** (**< 1,200 ft/s**) | **Handguns (except magnums)** | **I or II** (by wound size) |
| **Intermediate** | **350–650 m/s** (Orthobullets) · 350–600 m/s (TO); 1,200–2,000 ft/s | **Shotguns**, magnum handguns | Variable — mostly III |
| **High** | **> 600 m/s** (**> 2,000 ft/s**) | **Military + hunting rifles** | **III regardless of wound size** |

**Low-velocity cut-off** — < 350 m/s or **< 1,200 ft/s** (Orthobullets, TO) · **< 2,000 ft/s** (OB) — read the options

**Shotgun wounding** — **shot pattern, load (pellet size), distance**; **< 21 ft (close range) ≈ high velocity**; **wadding** → contamination

**Low-velocity GSW + fracture (OB)** — **< 8 h = Gustilo I** · **> 8 h = Gustilo II**

### Key radiology findings

| Study | Use |
|---|---|
| Radiographs | Bone involvement, pattern |
| **CT** | **Intra-articular missile**; **hollow viscus injury** communicating with fracture (pelvis / spine) |
| **CT abdomen** | **Hip / pelvic GSW → exclude bowel perforation (commonest associated injury)** (✗ bladder US, ✗ MRI, ✗ CTA, ✗ CT acetabulum first) |
| **Sigmoidoscopy** | **Blood on DRE** or path **crossing midline at / below pelvic brim** |
| **CT with intra-articular air** | **\~100% sensitive + specific for traumatic arthrotomy** (more than saline load test; less cost-effective) |

### Vascular injury assessment & infection risk

| Finding | Action |
|---|---|
| **Hard signs** — pulsatile bleeding, **expanding haematoma**, thrill / bruit, pulse deficit, ischaemia | **Operative exploration** |
| Soft signs — haematoma, bleeding at scene, unexplained hypotension | Further assessment |
| **ABI < 0.9** (e.g. 0.85) | **Vascular surgery consult** ± CTA |
| Symmetric pulses | **Do not exclude** injury → **ABI first** (✗ CTA as screen) |

**ABI** — **highly sensitive + specific**, quick, non-invasive · CTA expensive, finds irrelevant occult findings · duplex user-dependent

**Wound location alone** — **not** an indication for exploration

**Vascular injury + fracture** — **↑ infection** → antibiotics (✗ ↑ malunion, ✗ hardware failure, ✗ hip disarticulation) · **retained fragments do not ↑ infection**

## 5 Management

```
Gunshot wound
├─ Low velocity, no fracture / stable fracture → local wound care, tetanus ± oral antibiotic
├─ Low velocity, unstable fracture → treat as CLOSED fracture (nail / ORIF)
├─ High velocity / close-range shotgun → open-fracture protocol: debride + ex-fix
└─ Transabdominal → laparotomy first, then hip / spine decisions
```

### Low-velocity wounds without fracture — local wound care

**Treatment** — **irrigation + local wound care in ED**, tetanus, ± **short oral antibiotic** (cephalosporin) (TO, OB) (✗ OR debridement, ✗ IV × 7 d, ✗ primary closure, ✗ remove all fragments)

**True statement (TO)** — **low-velocity GSW → local debridement, oral antibiotics, tetanus** (✗ infection risk high, ✗ all grade III, ✗ formal debridement always)

**Low-velocity arm wound, entry + exit, no fracture (TO)** — **local wound care + oral antibiotics** (✗ surgical debridement + IV)

**Primary closure** — **contraindicated**

**Antibiotics** — controversial; give if contaminated · **single long-acting IM cephalosporin = 48 h IV (TO, Geissler)** · **oral = IV** for prophylaxis (TO)

**Subcutaneous bones (ulna, tibia) (TO)** — exposed fragments → **treat as open fracture**

**Retained fragments (TO)** — benign; remove if **palm, sole, periarticular, intra-articular**

### Fracture management after GSW (nail, external fixation, debridement)

**Low-velocity femoral shaft** — **reamed antegrade or retrograde IMN** (direction + timing don't matter) (✗ ex-fix, ✗ formal I&D, ✗ IV antibiotics 6 wk); union + infection = closed fractures

**IMN (TO)** — **statically locked** for low / mid-velocity femur + tibia (incl. subtroch, supracondylar)

**High-velocity / shotgun / vascular injury (TO)** — **ex-fix** → **IMN \~2 weeks** when wound healed

**Shotgun humerus with radial nerve weakness** — **I&D + ex-fix** (✗ acute fibular osteoseptocutaneous flap, ✗ nerve repair + retrograde nail, ✗ CT + ORIF, ✗ coaptation splint)

**Shotgun wadding (TO)** — **must be removed** (organic) · **lead shot — leave** (removal damages tissue)

**Humerus + dense radial nerve palsy** — **closed IMN relatively contraindicated** → plate + explore, or open the fracture to protect nerve (✗ age < 25, ✗ GSW, ✗ athlete, ✗ comminution)

### GSW to hand / foot

**Antibiotics** — gross contamination, **joint penetration**, contamination unclear

**Surgery** — **articular involvement**, **unstable fracture**, **≥ 8 h presentation**, **tendon involvement**, **superficial fragments in palm / sole**

**Foot: unstable 1st MT base + cuneiform, plantar fragments** — **surgical debridement + ex-fix / ORIF + IV antibiotics** (✗ irrigation + cast ± antibiotics)

**Type I stable, < 8 h** — tetanus, surface irrigation, cast / hard-sole shoe · **type II unstable → ex-fix + repeat debridement**

### Transabdominal / bowel-contaminated GSW — antibiotics & debridement

**Peritonism + positive FAST** — **laparotomy first** (✗ DPL, ✗ CT, ✗ saline load, ✗ arthroscopic bullet removal)

**Laparotomy precedes arthrotomy** for transabdominal trajectories

**Spine bullet + bowel injury, neuro intact** — **broad-spectrum IV antibiotics × 7 days** (✗ gram-negative only, ✗ oral, ✗ observation, ✗ decompression)

**Spine bullet + bowel (cecum), weakness, missile not in canal** — **broad-spectrum IV antibiotics 7–14 days** (✗ fragment removal, ✗ oral 3–5 d, ✗ **methylprednisolone — contraindicated in GSW**)

**Bullets through alimentary canal → SCI** — **removal not required**

**Spine surgery indications** — **instability** · **deficit matching compression by missile** · **lead in contact with CSF** · retained canal fragment + **incomplete** deficit (relative)

**Transabdominal hip GSW (blood on DRE)** — **hip debridement + ≥ 24 h antibiotics (gram +, gram −, enteric)** (✗ bullet removal unless intra-articular, ✗ single dose, ✗ omit gram +)

**Extra-articular, stable, no rectal blood (low velocity, bullet in ilium)** — **empiric antibiotics + observation** (✗ laparotomy, ✗ bullet removal, ✗ tract debridement, ✗ sigmoidoscopy)

**Acetabular GSW — septic arthritis risk** — **associated Letournel pattern** + **bowel injury** = independent risks → **least risk: anterior wall, no bowel injury**

**Pelvic / acetabular GSW complications** — septic arthritis, **enterocutaneous / enteroacetabular / vesicoacetabular fistula**, infected nonunion, iliac vessel injury

### Intra-articular bullets & traumatic arthrotomy

**Retained intra-articular bullet** — **arthrotomy / removal** → lead synovitis, plumbism, **third-body wear**, sepsis

**Transabdominal intra-articular GSW** — contaminated → **arthrotomy** (after laparotomy)

**Transarticular, no retained bullet / IA pathology** — **IV antibiotics + local care, no arthrotomy** (OB)

**Low-velocity knee GSW, no fracture (TO)** — **arthroscopic debridement + IV antibiotics** (TO) (✗ open debridement, ✗ local care + oral) · contrast OB: no IA pathology → antibiotics alone — **read the options**

**Arthroscopy (TO)** — ↓ morbidity, faster recovery; locate bullet → mini-arthrotomy

**Antibiotics for IA fractures (TO)** — **1–2 days** if bullet through clean skin / clothing · **1–2 weeks broad-spectrum** if through **lung, bowel, contaminated skin**

**Small traumatic arthrotomy (< 5 cm), minimal contamination** — **no surgery**: bedside irrigation, tetanus, antibiotics (✗ saline load always needed, ✗ CT air not sensitive, ✗ retained lead needs no surgery, ✗ cost equal — surgery \~$10,000 more)

## 6 Complications

| Complication | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Compartment syndrome** | Muscle oedema with higher-velocity wounds; ballistic femur > blunt |
| **Plumbism / lead arthropathy** | Intra-articular retained bullet |
| **Infection** | ↑ with **vascular injury**, **bowel transit**, high velocity, associated acetabular pattern |
| Septic arthritis | Transabdominal → hip / acetabulum |

## X vs Y

| | **Low velocity** | **High velocity / shotgun close range** |
|---|---|---|
| Treat as | **Closed fracture** | **Open fracture (Gustilo III)** |
| Debridement | Superficial / local | **Aggressive, repeated** |
| Antibiotics | Gram + / oral, short | **Broad spectrum** |
| Femur | **Immediate reamed IMN** | **Ex-fix → IMN \~2 wk** |

| | **Bullet passed through bowel → spine** | **Bullet passed through bowel → hip joint** |
|---|---|---|
| Treatment | **IV broad-spectrum 7–14 d; leave bullet** | **Debride hip** + ≥ 24 h antibiotics |

**"Shotgun humerus?"** → **I&D + external fixation**
