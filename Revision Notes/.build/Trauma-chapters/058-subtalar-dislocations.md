# Subtalar Dislocations

*Trauma · 58 / 67*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Incidence** — **1% of all dislocations** · < 1 / 100,000 / yr · young / middle-aged men

**Definition** — dislocation of **both subtalar (talocalcaneal) + talonavicular** joints · + tibiotalar = **total talar dislocation (extrusion)**

**High energy** — **25% open** (**lateral more often open**) · irreducible **\~33%** (OB)

### Anatomy

**Talus** — **no muscle or tendon attachments** · inferior surface → posterior facet of calcaneus · head → navicular + sustentaculum tali · lateral process → posterior facet + fibula · posterior process: medial + lateral tubercles with **FHL groove**

| Talar blood supply | Supplies |
|---|---|
| **Artery of tarsal canal** (posterior tibial) | **Most of body — main supply** |
| Calcaneal branches (posterior tibial) | Posterior talus |
| Anterior tibial artery | Head + neck |
| Artery of tarsal sinus (perforating peroneal) | Head + neck |
| **Deltoid artery** (deep deltoid) | Body · may be the only supply left after talar neck fracture |

![Blood supply of the talus](../Orthobullets/attachments/ob1050-blood-supply-to-the-talus.jpg)

## 2 Mechanism

**Medial** — **inversion of plantarflexed foot**; talar neck pivots on **sustentaculum tali**

**Lateral** — **eversion of plantarflexed foot**; **anterior process of calcaneus = fulcrum** for anterolateral talus

## 3 Presentation

**Medial** — foot **locked in supination** = **"acquired clubfoot"**

**Lateral** — foot **locked in pronation** = **"acquired flatfoot"**

![Medial subtalar dislocation — foot locked in supination](../Orthobullets/attachments/ob1050-clinical-photo-subtalar-dislocation.jpg)

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Study | Finding |
|---|---|
| AP + lateral foot | **Medial → talar head superior to navicular** (lateral view) · **lateral → talar head collinear / inferior to navicular** |
| **CT after reduction** | Associated fractures, **subtalar osteochondral debris** (residual subluxation) |

**Radiograph** — talonavicular dislocation is easily missed; it is part of every subtalar dislocation

### Anatomic classification (direction of midfoot / forefoot)

| Type | Features | Image |
|---|---|---|
| **Medial** | **Commonest** — **65–80%** (Orthobullets) · 65% / \~75% (OB) · **lateral malleolus buttress** · fractures: **dorsomedial talar head, posterior process of talus, navicular** | ![Medial](../Orthobullets/attachments/ob1050-medial-dislocation.jpg) |
| **Lateral** | **More often open**, more associated fractures, **higher energy** · locked in pronation · fractures: **cuboid, anterior calcaneus, lateral process of talus, fibula** | ![Lateral](../Orthobullets/attachments/ob1050-lateral-dislocation.jpg) |
| Anterior | Rare | — |
| Posterior | Rare | — |
| **Total (talar extrusion)** | Out of ankle + subtalar + talonavicular · **usually open** | — |

### Patterns & associated fractures (lateral more often open)

**Associated fractures** — **up to 44%** · **commonest: talus** (✗ fibula, ✗ cuboid, ✗ calcaneus, ✗ navicular) · occult **lateral process of talus**

**Medial vs lateral — true** — **lateral more likely open** (✗ medial more associated fractures, ✗ lateral blocked by EDB, ✗ medial blocked by tibialis posterior, ✗ lateral more stable — medial more stable)

**Isolated injury, no fractures — other joint dislocated** — **talonavicular** (✗ tibiotalar, calcaneocuboid, MTP, TMT)

## 5 Management

```
Subtalar dislocation
├─ Closed → closed reduction under sedation (60–70% succeed)
│      ├─ stable → short leg NWB cast 4–6 wk
│      └─ irreducible → open reduction (up to 32%)
├─ Open → I&D + open reduction
└─ Unstable after reduction → transarticular pins / spanning ex-fix
```

**Closed reduction** — knee flexion + ankle plantarflexion → **distraction** → hindfoot inversion / eversion (opposite to dislocation) · lateral: traction + hyperpronation, then supination (OB) · **post-reduction CT**

### Irreducible dislocation — blocks (EDB medially, tibialis posterior laterally)

| Dislocation | Blocks (lateral / medial structures) | Approach |
|---|---|---|
| **Medial** | **EDB** (talar head **buttonholes** through EDB), **peroneal tendons**, **talonavicular capsule**, deep peroneal NV bundle | **Sinus tarsi** |
| **Lateral** | **Tibialis posterior (commonest)**, **FHL**, **FDL**, posterior tibial NV bundle | **Medial** (between tibialis anterior + posterior) ± sinus tarsi for debris |

**Not blocks** — tibialis anterior, EHL (too dorsal), plantar fascia, extensor retinaculum, CFL

**Irreducible medial** — **\~10%** even under anaesthesia (OB)

![Tibialis posterior interposed — lateral dislocation](../Orthobullets/attachments/ob1050-shows-posterior-tibial-tendon-interposed.jpg)

### Total talar dislocation

**Failed closed reduction (closed pantalar, no fracture)** — **percutaneous / open reduction + soft-tissue repair** + temporary pins / spanning ex-fix (✗ primary talectomy + tibiocalcaneal fusion, ✗ talectomy + total talus replacement, ✗ primary tibiotalar / subtalar arthrodesis) · open → urgent I&D + reduction (reimplant)

**Salvage later** — talectomy, fusion, talus replacement for **osteonecrosis / arthritis**

## 6 Complications

### Outcomes — arthritis & osteonecrosis

| Complication | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Post-traumatic arthritis** | **Subtalar** commonest — radiographic **up to 89%**, **symptomatic 63%** |
| **Stiffness** | **Most common complication** (Orthobullets) |
| Talar osteonecrosis | **Total talar dislocation** — common; **without collapse at 12 months** |
| Infection | Open — poor muscle cover, poor vascularity |

**Most common complication** — **stiffness** (Orthobullets) · **post-traumatic arthritis** (OB) — read the options; ✗ osteonecrosis of talus

**Most symptomatic degenerated joint** — **subtalar** (✗ tibiotalar, ✗ talonavicular, ✗ calcaneocuboid, ✗ Lisfranc)

**12 months after total talar extrusion** — **talar osteonecrosis without collapse** (✗ pantalar arthritis, ✗ collapse yet, ✗ navicular / calcaneus ON)

**Poor prognosis** — **high energy**, **lateral**, **open**, **concomitant subtalar fractures**

## X vs Y

| | **Medial** | **Lateral** |
|---|---|---|
| Frequency | **Commoner (65–80%)** | Less common |
| Mechanism | Inversion | Eversion |
| Foot | Supinated (acquired clubfoot) | Pronated (acquired flatfoot) |
| Talar head (lateral X-ray) | **Superior** to navicular | Collinear / inferior |
| Block | **EDB**, peroneals, TN capsule | **Tibialis posterior**, FHL, FDL |
| Open / fractures | Less | **More** |
| Outcome | Better, more stable | **Worse** |

## How it's asked

**"Irreducible lateral subtalar — block?"** → **tibialis posterior tendon**

**"Irreducible medial subtalar — block?"** → **extensor digitorum brevis**

**"Most common associated fracture?"** → **talus**

**"Joint dislocated with subtalar?"** → **talonavicular**

**"Lateral vs medial — true?"** → **lateral more likely open**

**"Most symptomatic arthritis?"** → **subtalar joint**

**"Most common complication (OB)?"** → **post-traumatic arthritis**

**"12 months after extruded talus?"** → **osteonecrosis without collapse**

**"Closed pantalar dislocation, failed closed reduction?"** → **open reduction + soft-tissue repair**
