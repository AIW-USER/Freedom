# Evaluation, Resuscitation & DCO

*Trauma · 18 / 67*

## 1 Basics

### Trimodal death after trauma

| Peak | Share | Cause |
|---|---|---|
| **Minutes** | **50%** | Massive bleeding, neurological injury |
| **Hours** | **30%** | Shock, hypoxia, neurological injury |
| **Days–weeks** | **20%** | **MOF, infection** |

**Golden hour** — \~60% of preventable deaths

**Airbags (head-on)** — ↓ head injury, facial fractures, thoraco-abdominal injury (splenic rupture, flail chest), extraction · **✗ pelvic ring injuries NOT reduced**

**Psychological** — **PTSD \~50%** after orthopaedic trauma (**females 4×**; LE / pelvic injuries 2× UE) · depression 33%

**PTSD risk factor** — **high ISS** (> 15 = polytrauma) (✗ social history, ✗ timing of surgery, ✗ tobacco, ✗ rehab duration)

**Female sex is linked to** — **PTSD** (✗ nonunion, ✗ malunion, ✗ infection, ✗ fat embolism)

### Genes & lab markers

**IL-6** — **most reliable marker of systemic inflammatory response**; correlates with **injury severity, complications, mortality** (✗ IL-8, IL-10, CRP, TNF-α — too variable) · **> 500 pg/dL → DCO**

**Elevated IL-6** — ↑ **mortality** (✗ osteomyelitis, rhabdo, compartment syndrome) · also sensitive for **PJI**

**Lactate** — normal **< 2–2.5 mmol/L** (0.6–1.7 in one OB explanation); **most sensitive** marker of occult hypoperfusion; **last to normalise**; clearance within **12–24 h** predicts survival

**Base deficit** — normal **−2 to +2**; global hypoperfusion; **most predictive of mortality** (incl. normotensive elderly); confounded by saline (hyperchloraemia), renal failure, DKA, CO₂ retention

**Gastric mucosal pH** — normal **> 7.3 (7.30–7.35)**; regional endpoint

### SIRS criteria (≥ 2)

| Parameter | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Temperature | **> 38 °C or < 36 °C** |
| Heart rate | **> 90 / min** |
| Respiration | **RR > 20 or PaCO₂ < 32 mm Hg** |
| WBC | **> 12,000, < 4000, or > 10% bands** |

**Lactate is NOT a SIRS criterion**

**Adults vs children** — **adults: robust systemic response, lungs fail first (day 2–3)** · children: dampened systemic, robust local, all organs simultaneously

## 2 Mechanism

**Two-hit theory** — **1st hit** (hypoxia, hypotension, soft tissue, fractures) + **2nd hit** (surgery, ischaemia-reperfusion, compartment syndrome, infection) → SIRS → ARDS / MOF

**Compensated shock** — normal vitals with ongoing hypoperfusion (**up to 85%**) → **"primed" immune system → exaggerated SIRS** to a 2nd hit (✗ "no risk", ✗ ↑ nonunion / infection after staged fixation)

**Lethal triad (TO)** — **hypothermia + acidosis + coagulopathy** (✗ haemorrhage, ✗ hypoxia, ✗ hypotension)

## 3 Presentation

### Primary / secondary / tertiary survey

**Primary (ABCDE)** — treat greatest threat first; **A** incl. C-spine · **B** · **C** incl. haemorrhage control (pelvis!) · **D** · **E**

**Pregnancy** — **left lateral decubitus** (or right hip wedge) → relieves aortocaval compression (supine ↓ CO 25%)

**Tertiary survey** — when mental status recovers → ↓ missed injuries

**Missed fractures** — commonest cause = **extremity never imaged** (✗ X-ray read wrongly, ✗ MRI / CT not done)

### Haemorrhagic shock classes (ATLS)

| Class | Blood loss | HR | BP | Pulse pressure | Urine | Mental state | Treatment |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **I** | **< 15% (< 750 mL)** | Normal | Normal | Normal / widened | > 30 mL/h | Anxious | Fluid |
| **II** | **15–30% (750–1500)** | **> 100** | **Normal** (↑ diastolic) | **Narrowed** | 20–30 mL/h | Confused, irritable | Fluid |
| **III** | **30–40% (1500–2000)** | **> 120** | **↓ (first hypotension)** | Narrow | 5–15 mL/h | Lethargic | **Fluid + blood** |
| **IV** | **> 40% (> 2000)** | **> 140** | ↓↓ | **Very narrow** | Negligible | Coma | Fluid + blood |

**Blood volume** — adult **\~5 L (4.7–5 L, \~7% body weight)** · child 2–10 yr **75–80 mL/kg**

**First tachycardia + narrow pulse pressure** — **\~25% loss (class II)**

**2 L loss in 70 kg** — **class IV**: ↓ SBP + narrow pulse pressure (✗ widened = class I, ✗ normal SBP with ↑ diastolic = class II)

**HR 135, BP 85/58, low urine** — **30–40% (class III)**

**Each thigh** — holds **\~20%** of blood volume; isolated femur fracture can cause **class II** shock

### Non-haemorrhagic shock

| | **Hypovolaemic** | **Septic** | **Neurogenic** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **SVR** | **↑** | **↓ (key discriminator)** | ↓ (vasodilation) |
| Cardiac output | ↓ | **↑** (early; ↓ end-stage) | ↓ |
| PCWP | ↓ | ↓ | — |
| CVP | ↓ | ↓ | — |
| Mixed venous O₂ | ↓ | **↑** | — |
| HR | **Tachycardia** | Tachycardia | **Bradycardia** |
| Skin | Cool, clammy | — | **Warm, dry** |

**Haemorrhagic vs neurogenic** — **tachycardia** favours haemorrhagic (✗ bradycardia, ✗ ↓ CO — both, ✗ vasodilation, ✗ warm skin)

**Septic shock** — **↓ SVR** (✗ ↑ SVR — EXCEPT answer) · mortality 25–50%

**Swan-Ganz (TO)** — differentiates shock types: **PAWP ↑ in cardiogenic**, ↓ in hypovolaemic / distributive (TO stem: cardiogenic shock after spinal injury)

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Study | Use |
|---|---|
| **AP chest** | Mediastinal widening, PTX |
| Lateral C-spine | Must see C7–T1; largely replaced by **CT** |
| **AP pelvis** | Ring, acetabulum, proximal femur; **CT after preliminary pelvic stabilisation** |
| **CT C-spine, chest, abdo, pelvis** | Rule out life-threatening injuries |
| **Image any limb** with pain, crepitus, ecchymosis, deformity | Avoid missed fractures |

### Resuscitation endpoints

| Endpoint | Adequate |
|---|---|
| **Lactate** | **< 2 mmol/L** (best, TO) · **< 2.5** (Orthobullets) |
| **Base deficit** | **−2 to +2** |
| Gastric mucosal pH | **> 7.3** |
| Urine output | **≥ 0.5–1 mL/kg/h** (\~30 mL/h) |
| Pulse pressure | > 15 mm Hg |

**Resuscitation complete** — **oxygen debt repaid, aerobic metabolism restored in ALL tissue beds** (✗ MAP > 90, ✗ pulse pressure, ✗ urine output, ✗ maximised O₂ delivery)

**Normal BP, HR, urine after fluids** — resuscitation **cannot be determined** without lactate / BD / pHi

**Occult hypoperfusion** — **lactate 4.5–5 mmol/L** (✗ HR 80, ✗ SBP 120, ✗ BD −1.8, ✗ urine 40 mL/h)

**Successfully resuscitated** — **lactate 1.9** (✗ urine 0.25 mL/kg/h, ✗ BD 5.5, ✗ gastric pH 6.5, ✗ pulse pressure 15)

**Alternative to lactate / BD** — **gastric intramucosal pH 7.4**

**Normal lactate + BE despite abnormal vitals** — **adequate tissue perfusion** (✗ arterial line best, ✗ normal Hct = no shock — Hct lags)

**Variable best indicating readiness for definitive fixation** — **base deficit / lactate** (✗ pulse pressure, ✗ HR, ✗ Hct, ✗ urine, ✗ SBP, ✗ platelets)

**Most predictive of mortality** — **base deficit (e.g. BE −12)** (✗ ETCO₂, ✗ Hct, ✗ HR, ✗ BP)

### Patient condition (Pape) — 3 of 4 criteria

| Criterion | Stable | Borderline | Unstable | In extremis |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **SBP (mm Hg)** | ≥ 100 | 80–100 | **< 90** | **≤ 70** |
| Blood units / 2 h | 0–2 | 2–8 | 5–15 | > 15 |
| **Lactate** | Normal | ≈ 2.5 | **> 2.5** | Severe acidosis |
| Base deficit | Normal | — | — | > 6–8 |
| ATLS class | I | II–III | III–IV | IV |
| **Platelets** | > 110,000 | 90–110,000 | **70–90,000** | **< 70,000** |
| Fibrinogen (g/L) | > 1 | ≈ 1 | < 1 | DIC |
| **Temperature (°C)** | — | **33–35** | **30–32** | ≤ 30 |
| PaO₂/FiO₂ | 350–400 | 300–350 | 200–300 | < 200 |
| Chest AIS | 1–2 | ≥ 2 (2–3 rib fractures) | ≥ 3 | ≥ 3 (unstable chest) |
| Pelvic (AO) | A | B / C | C | C (crush) |

**OB unstable thresholds** — **SBP < 90**, **lactate > 2.5**, **platelets < 90,000**, fibrinogen < 1, **temp < 35 °C**, pulmonary contusion, severe liver / spleen, pelvic ring disruption · lactate **> 6 alone** → DCO

**TO DCO criteria (TO)** — **pH < 7.24**, **lactate > 2.5**, **temp < 35 °C**, **platelets < 90,000**, op time > 90 min, > 10 U PRBC · **SBP < 90 is NOT among these (TO)**

## 5 Management

```
Polytrauma with long-bone / pelvic fractures
├─ Unstable / extremis / ongoing acidosis / head or chest injury → DCO
│      └─ ex-fix long bones + pelvis, splint UE, I&D open → definitive later
├─ Stable, resuscitated → early definitive fixation (EAC, < 36 h)
└─ Borderline → DCO if any parameter fails
```

### Fluids & transfusion

**Resuscitate** — isotonic crystalloid → **O-negative / type-specific / cross-matched blood**

**Massive transfusion** — **1 : 1 : 1 (PRBC : platelets : plasma)** + **IV calcium** (≥ 4 U)

| Transfusion risk | Rate |
|---|---|
| **HBV — highest viral risk** | 1 in 205,000 (Orthobullets) |
| HCV | 1 in 1.8 million (Orthobullets) · 1 in 103,000 (OB) |
| HIV | 1 in 1.9 million (Orthobullets) · 1 in 500,000 (OB) |
| **Clerical error → haemolytic reaction — most prevalent adverse event** | **1 in 12,000–50,000** |
| Anaphylaxis | 1 in 150,000 |
| Bacterial sepsis | 1 in 1 million |

### Damage control vs early total care

**ETC (1980s)** — fix everything at once ("too sick not to operate") → **2nd hit** in unstable, head, chest injuries

**DCO (2000)** — temporary stabilisation (ex-fix, traction, splints, I&D) → definitive once physiology improves

**EAC (2013)** — definitive fixation of **spine, pelvis, acetabulum, femur within 36 h** once **any ONE** of: **lactate < 4.0 mmol/L**, **pH ≥ 7.25**, **base excess ≥ −5.5 (BD ≤ 5.5)** → ↓ delay, ↓ complications (ARDS, MOF, death), ↓ LOS, ↑ revenue; main delay = surgeon decision

### Operative criteria – DCO indications

| DCO indication | Threshold |
|---|---|
| **ISS** | **> 40** without chest trauma · **> 20** with chest trauma |
| **GCS** | **≤ 8** · **head injury AIS ≥ 3** |
| **Bilateral femur fractures** | ✓ |
| **Pulmonary contusion on radiograph** | ✓ (✗ on CT only, ✗ rib fractures alone) |
| **Hypothermia** | **< 35 °C** |
| **Severe pelvic / abdominal injury + haemorrhagic shock** | ✓ (e.g. grade IV liver + SBP 85) |
| **IL-6** | **> 500 pg/dL** |
| Persistent acidosis | Lactate, BD, pH out of range |

**✗ Not DCO triggers** — HR alone, SBP 90 alone, unilateral femur fracture, lactate 2.3–2.5, Hb < 9, rib fractures + PTX (borderline), pubic rami fracture (stable), temp 35.5 (borderline)

**Head injury** — **intra-operative hypotension ↑ mortality** → avoid long surgery; SDH needing CPP monitoring → **ex-fix femur** (✗ reamed / unreamed nail, ✗ traction, ✗ beads); maintain **CPP > 70 mm Hg** (TO)

### Inflammatory window & timing

**Acute inflammatory window** — **days 2–5** → avoid elective definitive surgery; operate only for life / limb threats: **unstable pelvis**, compartment syndrome, vascular injury, unreduced dislocations, amputations, unstable spine, cauda equina, open fractures

**Definitive conversion** — **pelvis 7–10 d** · **femur ex-fix → nail within 3 wk** · **tibia 7–10 d**

**Ex-fix → nail at 7 d** — **equal union + infection rates** (✗ higher infection / nonunion / ARDS / mortality)

### DCO scenarios

| Scenario | Answer |
|---|---|
| Unstable (MAP 51, BD 10, SDH) femur + open plateau | **I&D + ex-fix femur + ex-fix plateau** |
| BD 10, bilateral femurs, open tibia, forearm | **Ex-fix both femurs + tibia, I&D, splint forearm** (✗ ORIF forearm, ✗ nails) |
| Bilateral chest tubes, lactate 7, open femur + tibia | **I&D + bilateral ex-fix** (✗ nails, ✗ splints alone) |
| Lactate 10 unchanged after laparotomy, contusions | **Ex-fix tibia + femur** (✗ nails, ✗ slab + traction) |
| Lactate 8 → 7, femur + pelvis | **Ex-fix femur + pelvis** (✗ ORIF pelvis, ✗ splint femur) |
| High lactate, BD, lung injury — femur | **Ex-fix** (✗ nail, ✗ plate, ✗ traction — supine harmful) |
| BD 10, oliguria, open foot, femur, forearm, head injury | **I&D, ex-fix ankle + foot + femur, splint forearm** |
| Rising lactate 3 → 5, core temp 34 °C | **Ex-fix femur** (✗ reamed nail, ✗ traction, ✗ plate, ✗ MAST) |
| pH 7.21, BD −11, pelvis + distal femur + humerus | **Ex-fix pelvis + femur, splint humerus** |
| pH 7.21, BE −6.5 | **DCO** (not EAC) |
| Vertical shear pelvis, lactate 9, laparotomy | **Skeletal traction** (✗ binder — worsens, ✗ anterior ex-fix, ✗ ORIF, ✗ SI screws now) |
| SFA transection, unresuscitated, guarded prognosis | **Ex-fix femur + temporary arterial shunt** (+ fasciotomy) → definitive repair 24–48 h |
| **Stable, lactate 1.7**, femur + ankle + humerus | **IMN femur, splint ankle + humerus** |
| Stable, open ankle, femur + tibia | **IMN femur + tibia, I&D + ex-fix ankle** |
| Isolated floating knee, stable (ISS 27) | **IMN tibia + femur** (retrograde femur + antegrade tibia, one setup) |

**Upper-limb fractures in DCO** — **splint**

**Inadequately resuscitated for definitive care** — **BE −6.5** (✗ lactate 2.0, ✗ IL-6 300, ✗ pH 7.28)

**Supports early definitive care** — **BD 5.5** (✗ IL-6 550, ✗ pH 7.21, ✗ lactate 4.4, ✗ BE −5.8)

**Femur fixation decision in polytrauma (TO)** — **lactate < 4.0** (✗ BP, ✗ > 3 long bones, ✗ BE 5.5 as written) · **best resuscitation marker (TO)** — **lactate < 2 mmol/L**

### Pelvic haemorrhage

**Order** — **reduce pelvic volume (sheet, binder, ex-fix, traction)** → stable → **CT chest / abdo / pelvis** · unstable → laparotomy and / or **angiography + embolisation** (venous bleeding commoner than arterial)

**Stabilised after sheet** — **CT** next (✗ swap to binder, ✗ ER ex-fix, ✗ embolisation, ✗ ORIF)

**APC II — avoid (TO)** — **log-rolling** (disturbs retroperitoneal clot) (✓ binder, ✓ perineal exam, ✓ early contrast CT)

**Pelvic packing** — **subumbilical midline incision** (6–8 cm up from symphysis) → split rectus fascia → **3 lap pads each side** below brim, SI joint → retropubic (✗ ASIS–symphysis, ✗ ASIS–ASIS, ✗ pararectus)

### Triage, transfer & battlefield

**Mass casualty triage** — immediate, delayed, minor, expectant

**ATLS transfer to trauma centre** — open pelvic fracture, major crush, multiple proximal long-bone fractures, solid-organ injury managed expectantly · **isolated clean open forearm fracture → may stay**

**Battlefield mangled leg bleeding (TO)** — **tourniquet first** (✗ pressure dressing, ✗ haemostatic dressing, ✗ evacuate first)

**Vascular reconstruction limit (TO)** — **6 h warm ischaemia** (TO) · ideally **< 8 h** from injury (OB)

**Localises, eyes open spontaneously, words make no sense (TO)** — **E4 V3 M5**

| | **Extradural (TO)** | **Acute subdural (TO)** |
|---|---|---|
| Space | **Inner table ↔ dura** | **Under dura** |
| CT | **Biconvex** | **Crescentic** |
| Source | **Middle meningeal artery (85% arterial)**, pterion, temporoparietal fracture | Bridging veins / cortical laceration |
| Classic | **Lucid interval** (10–25%), contralateral hemiparesis, ipsilateral dilated pupil | More brain injury; worse prognosis |
| Surgery | **> 30 cm³** regardless of GCS; nonop if < 30 cm³ + < 15 mm + MLS < 5 mm + GCS > 8 + no deficit; **GCS < 9 + anisocoria → immediate** | **Thickness > 10 mm or MLS > 5 mm → evacuate regardless of GCS**; else if GCS ↓ ≥ 2, pupils abnormal, ICP > 20 |

**SDH age on CT (TO)** — acute 1–3 d **hyperdense** · subacute 4 d–3 wk **isodense** · chronic > 3 wk **hypodense** · anticoagulation ↑ ASDH 7× men, 26× women

**Uncal herniation (TO)** — **CN III palsy** (ipsilateral fixed dilated pupil, down + out)

**Kernohan notch (TO)** — midline shift pushes **contralateral cerebral peduncle** onto tentorium → **ipsilateral hemiplegia** (false localising)

**CSF rhinorrhoea after facial trauma (TO)** — **observe \~2 wk** (70% stop in 1 wk) (✗ immediate dural repair, ✗ acetazolamide first, ✗ cautery) · **β2-transferrin** confirms · **CT cisternography** localises · surgery if > 2 wk, delayed / spontaneous, recurrent meningitis; anosmia 78%

**Tension pneumocephalus (TO)** — **Mount Fuji sign**; trauma commonest; burr holes if symptomatic

**Carotico-cavernous fistula (TO)** — **pulsatile proptosis, chemosis, bruit, ↓ vision**, **CN VI palsy** commonest diplopia; direct type A (high flow, traumatic 0.2%) → embolisation

**Stems without answer key (TO)** — elderly on antiplatelets with confusion + gait disturbance (options EDH, chronic SDH, SAH, infarct) · 14-month-old enlarging parietal mass after injury at 8 mo (options growing skull fracture, hygroma, NCC, cephalhaematoma) · CT image identification — **answer key not provided by the source**

### Chest injuries (TO)

**Flail chest (TO)** — **≥ 2–3 adjacent ribs broken in ≥ 2 places** → **paradoxical movement**

**Stove-in chest (TO)** — flail segment **collapses inward**; **blunt** trauma (✗ penetrating — NOT true)

## 6 Complications

**ARDS + MOF** — 2nd-hit during inflammatory window; ETC in unstable patients

**Fat embolism** — immediate nailing in unresuscitated chest-injured patients

**Hypothermia < 35 °C** — ↑ mortality
