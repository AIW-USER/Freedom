# Tibial Plateau Fractures

*Trauma · 64 / 67*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Incidence** — **1–2% of all fractures** · 10.3 / 100,000 / yr · mean age 52 · **bimodal**: men 40s (high energy), women 70s (low energy)

**Location** — **lateral 70–80%** · bicondylar 10–30% · medial 10–20%

**Mechanism** — **valgus → lateral** · **varus → medial** · **axial → bicondylar** · high energy → medial, fracture-dislocation, soft-tissue injury · low energy → lateral

### Plateau anatomy (medial vs lateral)

| | **Medial plateau** | **Lateral plateau** |
|---|---|---|
| Shape | **Concave, larger** (TO) | **Convex**, smaller |
| Height | **More distal** | **More proximal** |
| Load | **60% — primary weight-bearing** | 40% |
| Bone | Stronger | Less dense |
| Meniscus | Less mobile (coronary ligaments) | **More mobile** → **submeniscal arthrotomy** |

**Alignment** — **posterior slope 6–10°** · **3° varus** to tibial mechanical axis

**Kinematics** — 0–140° (walking 0–70°) · concave medial pivot + convex lateral → **femoral rollback**, screw-home (tibia ER in extension)

**Insertions** — ACL between spines · PCL posterior sulcus · superficial MCL deep to pes · **LCL anterolateral fibular head** · ITB **Gerdy's tubercle** · pes anteromedial

**Nerves** — **common peroneal around fibular neck (commonest nerve injury)** · tibial with popliteal artery

### Associated meniscal & ligament injuries

| Injury | Association |
|---|---|
| **Lateral meniscus** | **Commoner than medial** · **Schatzker II** · **> 10 mm depression + > 6 mm widening** (Orthobullets; OB) · **≥ 6 mm depression + 5–6 mm widening** (OB) — read the options |
| **Medial meniscus** | **Schatzker IV** |
| **ACL** | **Types IV + VI (25%)** (Orthobullets; OB) · **types V + VI** (TO; OB) — read the options |
| Neurovascular | **Schatzker IV fracture-dislocation**; hyperextension bicondylar |
| Compartment syndrome | High energy |

**Meniscal tear type** — **peripheral / meniscocapsular detachment** (vascular zone, heals) (✗ horizontal cleavage, ✗ radial, ✗ root, ✗ bucket-handle medial) · meniscus stays with femur as plateau widens

**Rates** — meniscal tear \~30% at surgery, **up to 91% on MRI** · ligament injury > 75% on MRI · LM injury > 75% of displaced Schatzker II

**Lateral meniscal tear risk** — **> 10 mm depression**, Schatzker II > I, **age < 48, male** (✗ > 50, ✗ female, ✗ calcaneus fracture) · **6 mm widening** suggests LM tear (✗ 3 mm depression, ✗ spine fracture — ACL)

**Management** — **submeniscal arthrotomy dividing meniscotibial ligaments**, repair at fixation (longitudinal → repair · radial → partial resection) (✗ meniscofemoral, ✗ leave untreated)

**Repaired LM tear — 1-yr outcome** — **similar function + QoL** (✗ ↑ TKA, ✗ ↓ ROM, ✗ ↑ OA grade, ✗ ↑ re-arthroscopy)

**ACL + PLC with plateau fracture** — grade 3 Lachman + varus laxity 0° + 30° + ER asymmetry → **ACL + PLC reconstruction (LCL + popliteofibular)**, LM repair · missed PLC → ACL graft fails

## 3 Presentation

**Exam** — rule out **open** wound · **compartment syndrome** · varus / valgus stress in **full extension: laxity > 10° = instability** · pulses → **ABI if asymmetric; < 0.9 → arteriogram** · tibial + common peroneal nerves

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Study | Finding |
|---|---|
| AP, lateral, **oblique** (depression) | Depressed surface, **sclerotic band**, alignment · lateral: **posteromedial fracture lines**, slope |
| **Plateau view** | **10° caudal tilt** (matches posterior slope) |
| **CT** | Depression, comminution, **posterior coronal split** (axial + sagittal) · **after ex-fix** if staged |
| MRI | Meniscus + ligaments, occult fracture |

### Lipohaemarthrosis — occult fracture

**Lipohaemarthrosis** — **intra-articular fracture**: **tibial plateau commonest** (TO) · **occult fracture** (OB) (✗ meniscus tear, ✗ MPFL, ✗ tendon rupture, ✗ ligament injury, ✗ arthritis)

**Layers** — **fat (top), serum, RBCs (bottom)** · separation up to **3 h** · CT / MRI as sensitive as arthrocentesis

|---|---|

**3-column concept** — **medial, lateral, posterior** (CT) · captures **posterior** fractures Schatzker misses · guides approach

**Hyperextension varus bicondylar (HEVBTP)** — **loss / reversal of posterior slope** · posterior cortex tension failure, anterior compression, coronal varus · **popliteal artery 12%** (vs 1%), **peroneal nerve 16%**, compartment syndrome 12%, **PLC injury** (✗ posteromedial corner, ✗ ↓ peroneal risk, ✗ posterior tibial artery) · fix: **anteromedial buttress + graft anterior defect**

**Posterior column + varus** — **poor function + sports limitation** (✗ low energy, ✗ highest meniscal injury, ✗ high satisfaction, ✗ Schatzker-classifiable)

## 5 Management

```
Tibial plateau fracture
├─ Minimally displaced, stable to varus / valgus → hinged brace, NWB / PWB 8–12 wk, early ROM
├─ Unicondylar lateral (I–III) → lateral approach, elevate + graft, lateral buttress plate / rafts
├─ Medial (IV) → ABI → posteromedial / medial buttress plate
├─ Bicondylar / VI, swollen or polytrauma → spanning ex-fix → CT → dual-incision dual plating
└─ > 65, osteoporotic, non-reconstructable → acute TKA
```

### Operative criteria

| Indication | Threshold |
|---|---|
| **Articular depression / step-off** | **> 5–10 mm** (Orthobullets) · **> 3 mm** step-off (OB) — read the options |
| **Condylar widening** | **> 5 mm** |
| **Varus / valgus instability** | **> 10°** (in extension) |
| **Medial plateau fracture** | All |
| **Bicondylar** | All |

**Decision for minimally displaced fracture** — **physical exam (varus / valgus stability)** (✗ MRI, ✗ age, ✗ compensation) · may decide up to 2 wk

**Nonoperative** — minimally displaced split / depression, stable, non-ambulatory, unfit · PTB cast / immobiliser / **hinged brace** · **early passive ROM**

### Spanning external fixation — indications & effects

**Indication** — **axially / length-unstable** (**Schatzker VI with shortening**, bicondylar) or **knee subluxation** · significant swelling / **blisters**, polytrauma, open (✗ I–III, ✗ minimally displaced IV)

**Bicondylar VI + blisters next day** — **knee-spanning ex-fix** (✗ splint / cast, ✗ ORIF)

**Polytrauma, open femur + plateau, head + abdominal injury** — **ex-fix femur + plateau**, splint ankle + forearm (✗ acute nailing / ORIF)

**Technique** — **two 5 mm half-pins in femur + two in tibia**, outside future incisions · **ligamentotaxis** · lock in **slight flexion** · **wires > 14 mm from joint** (capsule / septic arthritis)

**Expected** — **transient ↑ compartment pressure** during placement (✗ ↑ compartment syndrome) · ✗ ↓ pulses, ✗ pulmonary injury, ✗ blocks wound access, ✗ ↑ deep infection / malunion

**Downside** — **arthrofibrosis \~15% → return to OR** (MUA, quadricepsplasty, lysis) · **each day in frame ↑ odds 10%** · **CPM ↓ risk up to 70%**

**Posterior shear fracture, swollen, no skin wrinkles** — **ex-fix + realign now**, delayed posteromedial ORIF (✗ immobiliser, ✗ fasciotomy without signs, ✗ prompt ORIF)

### Lateral plateau fixation — buttress principle & constructs

**Partial articular (Schatzker II)** — **lateral plate + metaphyseal graft** (✗ medial plate) · rafting subchondral screws

**Good bone** — **undercontoured lateral nonlocking plate (buttress)** ± graft substitute (✗ locking — no buttress in pure locking mode, + cost, ✗ ex-fix, ✗ screws alone, ✗ dual plates) · **osteopenic (63-yr, 15 mm depression) → still lateral nonlocking**

**Osteoporotic screw order** — **3.5 mm nonlocking (lag) first → then 3.5 mm locking** (compress / narrow rim, then hold elevation) (✗ locking first, ✗ 6.5 mm screws)

**Buttress technique** — **undercontoured plate + nonlocked screw near apex** of vertical fracture · resists axial load at **90° to deformity** · best for **simple partial articular / Schatzker I** (not depression, not metadiaphyseal)

**Split-depression (skiing)** — **ORIF with absolute stability** (✗ relative, ✗ IMN, ✗ ex-fix, ✗ cast)

| Plate function | Stability |
|---|---|
| **Buttress** | Resists axial / shear |
| Compression | Absolute |
| Neutralisation (with lag screw) | Absolute |
| Bridge | Relative |

![Buttress plate](../Orthobullets/attachments/ob1044-buttress-moved.jpg)

### Bone void fillers — calcium phosphate cement

**Least subsidence + highest compressive strength** — **calcium phosphate cement** (✗ ICBG, ✗ cancellous allograft, ✗ reamings, ✗ calcium sulfate — no structural strength, ✗ tricalcium phosphate, ✗ hydroxyapatite, ✗ rhBMP-7) · osteoconductive, porous, biodegradable

**Depression technique** — "open the book" or **cortical window + tamps** → fill void → rafts

### Bicondylar fractures — dual plating & posteromedial fragment

**Posteromedial fragment** — common; **lateral locking plate can't capture it** → **lateral + posteromedial plates via two incisions** (✗ single lateral / polyaxial plate, ✗ single anterior approach — infection, ✗ hybrid frame, ✗ IMN)

**Bicondylar + blisters** — **ex-fix → delayed dual-incision dual plating** (✗ lateral locked plate, ✗ single-incision dual plating, ✗ splint)

**Posterolateral fragment** — must be **fixed separately** or fails (✗ single plate, ✗ rafting screws, ✗ cement)

**Hybrid ex-fix definitive (V / VI)** — **unacceptably high malunion / nonunion** · shorter surgery, ↑ superficial infection, faster early return, shorter stay

### Posteromedial approach — interval, positioning & buttress plating

**Interval (Lobenhoffer)** — **pes anserinus (sartorius, gracilis, semitendinosus) ↔ medial head of gastrocnemius** · semimembranosus ↔ medial gastrocnemius (OB) — read the options (✗ superficial MCL, ✗ POL, ✗ soleus, ✗ popliteus)

**Position** — supine figure-4 or **prone** (extensile posterior / posterolateral shear) · elevate popliteus subperiosteally · knee **extension + valgus** aids reduction

**At risk** — **saphenous nerve + vein**, **posterior tibial recurrent artery** (distal / lateral)

**Schatzker IV / medial condyle** — **medial (posteromedial) buttress plate, absolute stability** (✗ lateral locking — shear failure, ✗ medial bridge, ✗ lag screws alone — leg lever arm, ✗ IMN, ✗ ex-fix)

**Posteromedial shear (fall on flexed knee)** — **posteromedial approach + buttress plate** (✗ direct medial, ✗ lateral, ✗ anteromedial, ✗ hybrid)

**Posterior fragment only** — **posterior buttress plate**

![Posteromedial approach](../Orthobullets/attachments/ob1044-posteromedial-approach.jpg)

### Other approaches

| Approach | Interval / fact |
|---|---|
| **Anterolateral** (commonest) | Hockey-stick over **Gerdy's**, elevate anterior compartment + ITB, **submeniscal arthrotomy** |
| **Posterolateral** | **Gastrocnemius-soleus ↔ peroneals** (TO; OB) · lateral gastrocnemius ↔ biceps, **CPN in plane** (OB) |
| Direct posterior | Medial ↔ lateral gastrocnemius — popliteal artery, small saphenous vein, CPN |
| Fibular neck osteotomy | Rare — NV risk |
| **Midline** | **Avoid** (stripping) |

### Elderly — acute arthroplasty

**Primary TKA > 65–70 yr, osteoporotic** — **earlier full weight bearing** (✗ ↑ implant failure — that's ORIF, ✗ fewer complications than TKA for OA, ✗ worse than TKA after failed ORIF)

### Infection risk & fixation timing

**Infection after ORIF** — **male, smoking, pulmonary disease, bicondylar, operative time > 3 h** (✗ age, ✗ unicondylar, ✗ prior injury, ✗ mechanism) · vancomycin powder ↓ deep infection

**Fixation vs fasciotomy closure timing** — **does not alter infection risk**

### Articular cartilage healing — early motion

**Best for surviving chondrocytes** — **gentle compressive loading via early ROM** (✗ immobilisation, ✗ shear, ✗ distraction, ✗ glucosamine)

## 6 Complications

| Complication | Rate / fact |
|---|---|
| **Post-traumatic arthritis** | **25–35%** · meniscectomy, malalignment > 5°, instability |
| **Conversion to TKA** | **3–7% at 10+ yr** (Orthobullets) · **5–10% at 10 yr** (OB) · **\~5× general population** (3–7×) — read the options |
| **Compartment syndrome** | **7–20%** · **young age**, high energy, **Schatzker IV** (Orthobullets) / **VI**, **V + VI** (OB), fibula fracture, plateau-shaft |
| Infection | **2–11%** (Orthobullets) · 6–12% (OB) |
| Nonunion / malunion | 2–4% · **Schatzker VI**, comminution |
| **Stiffness / arthrofibrosis** | **10–25%** · **\~15% after temporary ex-fix = commonest early complication** · MUA + lysis if < 90° flexion at 4 wk |
| Loss of reduction | 5–30% · e.g. posteromedial coronal fragment missed by lateral plate |
| DVT | Nonop 9%, op 6% |

### Vascular injury — medial / Schatzker IV patterns & ABI

**Schatzker IV** — **fracture-dislocation variant**: femur follows the **medial** fragment · **popliteal artery + peroneal nerve stretch** → **ABI** (✗ compartment pressures, ✗ ex-fix before vascular check) · ABI < 0.9 → CTA / MRA

**Highest vascular risk** — **Schatzker IV** (✗ II, ✗ V, ✗ tibial shaft, ✗ pilon, ✗ subtrochanteric)

**Schatzker IV — true** — **medial meniscus + ACL injuries common** (✗ femur follows lateral plateau, ✗ lateral plate, ✗ bicondylar plating, ✗ vascular injury rare)

### Outcomes & arthritis — alignment, stability, age, conversion to TKA

**Strongest predictor of long-term outcome** — **restoration of joint stability** (✗ meniscal repair, ✗ surgery < 48 h, ✗ depression correction, ✗ diastasis)

**Most ↑ arthritis** — **alteration of mechanical axis (> 5°)** (✗ step-off, ✗ type, ✗ male, ✗ age) · mild incongruity tolerated

**Age > 50** — **worse outcomes even in low-energy fractures**; only 35% satisfied · complication rates **don't differ** with age (✗ older → more infection, ✗ younger → more nonunion)

**Prognosis** — 1-yr mortality 5% · return to work 70–90% · ROM 10–145°
