# History and Physical Exam of the Knee

*Knee & Sports · 20 / 61*

## 1 Basics

### Fabella anatomy

**Fabella (TO)** — accessory sesamoid ossicle **almost always in lateral head of gastrocnemius**, rarely medial head (can be in both) · **\~20% (10–30%)** of population · **bony or cartilaginous**

**Articulation (TO)** — **often articulates directly with lateral femoral condyle** → "no bony attachment" is the FALSE statement

**Function (TO)** — stabilisation of the medial femoral condyle (as stated in source) + **fabella complex**: plantaris, gastrocnemius, arcuate, **fabellofibular**, fabellopopliteal, oblique popliteal ligaments

### Normal values

**ROM** — **10° recurvatum → 130° flexion** · rotation minimal in extension · **at 90°: 45° ER, 30° IR** · ab / adduction 0° in extension, few degrees at 30°

**Quadriceps girth** — measured at **10 cm (VMO)** + **15 cm (quadriceps)**

**Tibial step-off** — medial plateau **\~1 cm anterior** to MFC at 90°

## 3 Presentation

### History & exam by lesion

| Lesion | History | Exam |
|---|---|---|
| **ACL** | Non-contact, awkward landing, **"pop"**, **immediate swelling** | **Lachman**, pivot shift, large haemarthrosis |
| **PCL** | **Dashboard**, fall on **plantarflexed** foot, posterior pain | **Sag**, **posterior drawer (90°)**, quad active |
| MCL | Blow to outside of knee, medial pain | Valgus instability |
| LCL | Varus injury, lateral pain | Varus instability |
| **PLC** | Lateral + posterior pain, usually combined | **Dial + at 30°** |
| **Meniscus** | **Mechanical** (catching, locking), joint line pain, **delayed swelling** | **Joint line tenderness**, McMurray |
| **Patella** | Fall on **dorsiflexed** foot, **2 "pops"**, anterior pain, stairs | Apprehension, **MPFL tenderness**, effusion, crepitus, ↑ Q angle |

**Inspection / palpation** — scars, swelling, quadriceps atrophy, gait (antalgia, stride), standing alignment · joint line, pes bursa, patellar tendon, ITB · effusion: ballot, milking

### Neurovascular exam

| Nerve | Sensation | Motor |
|---|---|---|
| Obturator | Medial thigh | Thigh adduction |
| Femoral | Anterior thigh | Knee extension |
| Sciatic | Posterolateral calf | Knee flexion |
| **Peroneal** | **Dorsal foot** | **Toe extension** |
| Tibial | Plantar foot | Toe flexion |

**Vascular** — popliteal, DP, PT pulses · **ABI < 0.9 abnormal**

### ACL tests

**Lachman** — **most sensitive** test · **PCL tear → "false" Lachman** (posterior subluxation)

| Lachman grade | Translation |
|---|---|
| **1** | **< 5 mm** |
| **2 A / B** | **5–10 mm** |
| **3 A / B** | **> 10 mm** |
| A / B | **A = firm endpoint**, **B = no endpoint** |

**Pivot shift** — **extension → flexion**, reduces at **20–30°** · needs full relaxation (easier under anaesthesia) · mimics giving way

**KT-1000** — quantifies anterior laxity · slight flexion, **10–30° ER**

**Gait** — **quadriceps avoidance** (doesn't actively extend)

### PCL tests

**Posterior drawer (90°)** — **most accurate** for PCL

**Posterior sag** — hips + knees 90°, ankles supported → tibia sags vs other side

**Quadriceps active test** — quad contraction at 90° → **anterior reduction of tibia** = +

### Collateral tests

| Stress | 30° only | 0° + 30° |
|---|---|---|
| **Valgus** | **Isolated MCL** | **MCL + ACL and / or PCL** |
| **Varus** | **Isolated LCL** | **LCL + ACL and / or PCL** |

**Varus opening + ↑ ER at 30°** — **LCL + PLC**

**MCL grade (opening)** — **I 0–5 mm · II 6–10 mm · III 11–15 mm** (this topic) · 1–4 / 5–9 / ≥ 10 mm (MCL topic exam) — read the options

**Anterior drawer, tibia in ER** — positive → associated injury · **grade III MCL often with ACL + posteromedial corner** tears

### Posterolateral corner – dial test

| Test | Technique | Positive means |
|---|---|---|
| **Dial test** | ER asymmetry at 30° + 90° | **> 10° at 30° only → isolated PLC** · **30° + 90° → PLC + PCL** |
| Varus stress | 0° vs 30° | 0° → LCL + cruciate · 30° → LCL |
| **Posterolateral drawer** | Hip 45°, knee 80°, foot **ER 15°**, posterior drawer + ER | ↑ posterolateral translation |
| **Reverse pivot shift** | Knee **90°**, ER + valgus, **extend** | Tibia reduces with clunk at **\~20°** (ITB flexor → extensor) |
| **ER recurvatum** | Suspend leg by toes, supine | Leg falls into ER + recurvatum |
| Gait | — | **Varus thrust** or hyperextension thrust |
| Peroneal nerve | — | ↓ dorsal foot sensation, weak dorsiflexion |

**Dial threshold** — **> 10°** (Orthobullets) · 10–15° (OB) · 15° (TO) — see PCL / PLC chapters

**Reverse pivot direction** — 90° → extension (Orthobullets) · extension → flexion (TO, PLC chapter) — read the options

![External rotation recurvatum test](../Orthobullets/attachments/ob3003-hughston-test.jpg)

**Isolated PLC (OB)** — blow from medial side, lateral tenderness, Lachman **1A**, normal posterior drawer, **20° ↑ ER at 30°**, symmetric at 90° → **PLC reconstruction** (repair or reconstruction in high-level athlete) (✗ BTB ACL, ✗ ACL + PLC, ✗ PCL, ✗ PCL + PLC)

**Veltri (OB)** — isolated PCL **< 10 mm** posterior laxity → **nonop** · **> 10 mm or combined** → reconstruct

### Meniscus tests

**Findings** — **joint line tenderness** · effusion

**McMurray** — flex knee, hand on medial side, **externally rotate**, extend → palpable pop / click → **medial meniscus** tear

### Patella tests

**Haemarthrosis** — large · **absent swelling → ligamentous laxity / habitual** dislocation

**Tenderness** — **medial (MPFL)**

**Passive translation** — quadrants (midline = 0) vs other side · normal **< 2 quadrants** · medial border to lateral trochlear edge = **2 = abnormal**

**J sign** — excessive lateral translation in extension, "pops" into groove in early flexion → **patella alta**

**Also** — apprehension · ↑ **Q angle**

## How it's asked

**"ER 20° ↑ at 30°, symmetric at 90°, normal drawer and Lachman — surgery?"** → **posterolateral corner reconstruction**

**"NOT true about fabella (TO)?"** → **no bony attachment (it articulates with lateral femoral condyle)**

**"Fabella usual site (TO)?"** → **lateral head of gastrocnemius (rarely medial, can be both)**

**"Fabella composition (TO)?"** → **bony or cartilaginous**
