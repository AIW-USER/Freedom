# Prepatellar Bursitis (Housemaid's Knee)

*Knee & Sports · 46 / 61*

## 1 Basics

**Frequency** — **most common bursitis of the knee**

**Anatomy** — bursa **anterior to patella** · potential space → gliding of tissue over patella

**Risk factors** — **excessive kneeling** · **wrestlers** → **concern for septic bursitis**

**Septic vs aseptic** — **20% septic**

**Organism** — **S. aureus** = most common in septic prepatellar (and olecranon) bursitis

## 3 Presentation

**Symptoms** — pain, swelling over patella, history of kneeling · **warm**, especially if septic

**Septic vs aseptic** — hard to tell clinically · **extra-articular** → no septic-arthritis pain on knee motion

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

**Aspiration + Gram stain + culture** — **rarely indicated** · use only to separate septic from aseptic

## 5 Management

```
Prepatellar bursitis
├─ Aseptic → compressive wrap, NSAIDs, ± aspiration, immobilisation 1 wk
└─ Septic (fever, spreading erythema) → open bursectomy + IV antibiotics
```

**Aseptic** — compression, NSAIDs, padding, ice, ± aspiration, **1 wk immobilisation** · corticosteroid **controversial**

**Septic** — **complete bursectomy + systemic (IV) antibiotics** = standard · antibiotics + aspiration / percutaneous drain succeed in some

**Recurrent after aspiration, fever 101.7°F, spreading erythema** — **perform open bursectomy + start intravenous (IV) antibiotics** (✗ oral antibiotics 7 days, ✗ re-aspirate + steroid, ✗ padding + ice)

**Bursal resection** — rarely needed · open or **arthroscopic**

## How it's asked

**"Febrile, erythematous prepatellar bursa after aspiration?"** → **open bursectomy + IV antibiotics**
