# Anterior Superior Iliac Spine (ASIS) Avulsion

*Knee & Sports · 4 / 61*

## 1 Basics

**Who** — adolescents **14–17 yr** approaching skeletal maturity, **males > females** · sprinting, running, baseball, soccer, hockey

**Avulsion physiology** — sudden violent contraction or excess stretch across an **open apophysis** · often **no external trauma** · low-energy → **no associated life-threatening injuries**

### Anatomy

| Muscle from ASIS | Nerve | Insertion |
|---|---|---|
| **Sartorius** | **Femoral** | **Pes anserinus** → source of **pes anserine bursitis** |
| **Tensor fascia lata** | **Superior gluteal** | Gerdy's tubercle via ITB |

**Insertion distractors** — fibular head = **biceps femoris** · femoral insertion anterior + distal to LCL = **popliteus** · ACL autograft = **gracilis (inferior pubic ramus) + semitendinosus (ischial tuberosity)**, both at pes · extends hip + flexes knee = **hamstrings**

## 2 Mechanism

### Sartorius origin avulsion — mechanism, innervation & protected weight bearing

**Mechanism** — indirect · **sudden forceful (eccentric) contraction of sartorius ± TFL** · **hip extended** (+ knee flexed) → **sprinting** (out of blocks, 400 m) or **swinging a baseball bat** (✗ external oblique, ✗ rectus femoris, ✗ iliopsoas, ✗ gluteus medius)

**Avulsed muscle nerve** — sartorius → **femoral** (✗ sciatic, ✗ obturator, ✗ pudendal, ✗ inferior gluteal)

## 3 Presentation

**History** — **"pop" or snap** · may be **misdiagnosed as a muscle strain**

**Exam** — tender over ASIS (superior to hip joint), groin ecchymosis · **pain with straight-leg raise** · weak **hip flexion + knee extension** · limp if severe

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Study | Use |
|---|---|
| **AP pelvis** | Displaced fractures visible · small fragment **easily missed** |
| CT / MRI | Confirm **nondisplaced** fractures |
| CT (relative) | **Rarely needed** — measure displacement, or tell **ASIS vs AIIS** origin (✗ urgent CT, ✗ extensive work-up) |

### Diagnostic criteria

| Criterion | ASIS avulsion |
|---|---|
| **History** | Sudden contraction of sartorius + TFL |
| **Exam** | ASIS tenderness, pain on SLR |
| **Age** | Adolescent / young adult |
| **Radiographs** | Fracture on standard pelvis views |

### White classification

| White | Muscle | Activity | Fragment |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Type 1** | **Sartorius** | **Sprinting** | **Small**, displaced **anteriorly + inferiorly** |
| **Type 2** | **TFL** | **Twisting trunk / start of bat swing** | **Larger**, displaced **laterally** |

## 5 Management

```
ASIS avulsion
├─ Displacement < 3 cm (most) → PROTECTED WB, CRUTCHES, EARLY ROM
└─ Displacement > 3 cm or painful nonunion → ORIF
```

**Nonoperative** — rest, ice, NSAIDs, **protected / progressive WB as tolerated with crutches**, early ROM + stretching, PT · position limb to ↓ stretch on muscle · **return when pain-free motion** · outcomes **equivalent to surgery**

**14-yr sprinter, pop, ASIS fragment** — **progressive weightbearing, return when pain-free** (✗ fix femoral neck stress fracture, ✗ iliopsoas recession — internal snapping hip has normal X-rays + can bear weight, ✗ fix sartorius origin, ✗ fix rectus femoris direct head — that is AIIS)

**1 wk after injury, improving, still on crutches** — **WBAT + crutches for 3 weeks total** (✗ NWB 6 wk, ✗ closed reduction + pinning — operative choice is ORIF, ✗ aspiration, ✗ urgent CT)

**12-yr bat swing, 2 cm displaced** — **rest + protected WB with crutches** (✗ ORIF with rectus femoris / sartorius / iliopsoas, ✗ excision)

### Operative criteria

**ORIF** — **displacement > 3 cm** (Orthobullets, most OB) · **> 2 cm in elite athletes** or failed nonop (OB) — read the options · **painful nonunion**

**High-energy paediatric acetabular fracture** — ORIF if **> 2 mm** intra-articular or triradiate displacement (contrast with low-energy avulsions)

### Iliac crest apophysis avulsion — abdominal wall innervation

**Mechanism** — **rapid lateral trunk flexion / twisting** → contraction of **external + internal oblique, transversus** · sprinter out of blocks · **rare**

**Nerve** — abdominal wall = **lower intercostal nerves** (✗ sciatic — hamstrings, ✗ femoral — rectus femoris, ✗ obturator — adductor longus, ✗ superior gluteal — TFL)

![Left iliac crest apophyseal avulsion](../MCQ/attachments/q218866-a-iliac-crest-avulsion-fx.jpg)

**Treatment** — **protected WB + activity as tolerated**, rest, analgesics, PT (✗ oncology work-up + biopsy, ✗ ORIF, ✗ abduction brace in 45° flexion, ✗ **120° knee flexion 24 h — quadriceps contusion**)

**Surgery** — displaced **> 3 cm** or failed nonop

### Apophyseal avulsion series

| Series | Finding |
|---|---|
| Schuett (228, mean 14.4 yr, **76% male**) | **AIIS > ASIS > ischial tuberosity > iliac crest** · surgery **3%** |
| Schuett — nonunion | 5 nonunions, **4 ischial tuberosity** · displacement **> 2 cm → 26× nonunion risk** |
| Rossi (203) | **Ischial tuberosity \~50%** > AIIS 25% > ASIS 20% > pubic symphysis 4% > iliac crest 1% · soccer + gymnastics |
| Schiller (lower-limb avulsions) | Surgery: displaced tibial tubercle, failed nonop, nerve impingement, **> 2 cm** |

**Most common site** — ischial tuberosity (Rossi) · AIIS (Schuett) · ASIS listed first (other OB) — read the options

## 6 Complications

**Missed ASIS avulsion** — **exostosis** needing excision

**Painful nonunion** — ORIF

## How it's asked

**"ASIS avulsion — eccentric contraction of?"** → **sartorius**

**"Sprinter, ASIS avulsion — treatment?"** → **progressive weightbearing, return when pain-free motion achieved**

**"True of the avulsed (ASIS) muscle's insertion?"** → **can be source of pes anserinus bursitis**

**"Sprinter, iliac crest avulsion — next step?"** → **protected weight bearing and activity as tolerated**

**"1 wk after ASIS avulsion, improving — management?"** → **weight-bearing as tolerated and crutches for 3 weeks total**

**"Bat swing, ASIS displaced 2 cm?"** → **rest and protected weight bearing with crutches**

**"ASIS avulsion — nerve to avulsed muscle?"** → **femoral**

**"Iliac crest avulsion — nerve to attached muscles?"** → **lower intercostal nerves**
