# Femoral Neck Stress Fractures

*Knee & Sports · 15 / 61*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Incidence** — **0.04%** of military personnel (**20 / 10,000 recruits / yr**) · **9%** of lower-limb stress fractures

**Share of sport stress fractures** — **3–5%** (Orthobullets) · **3%** (TO)

**Who** — age 16–56 · **female > male** (TO: higher in female athletes) · most often caucasian

**Sports (TO)** — **marathon / long-distance running**, basketball, gymnastics, ballet · Orthobullets: track + cross-country, military recruits

### Risk factors

| Group | Risk factors |
|---|---|
| Demographic | **Female**, caucasian, older age · **poor baseline fitness** (TO) |
| Activity | **Military**, track / cross-country, high training volume · **sudden ↑ training intensity** (TO) |
| Medical | **Low BMI**, **↓ BMD**, **energy deficiency** (expenditure > intake), tobacco · **acute weight loss** (independent, Armstrong) |
| Anatomical | **FAI**, **coxa vara**, abnormal running gait · **coxa profunda + acetabular retroversion** (pincer) (TO) |
| Triad | **Female athlete triad** (TO + Orthobullets) |

**FAI association** — present in **50%** of FNSF · **cam 42%**, **pincer 78%**

**Female athlete triad** — **amenorrhoea + eating disorder + osteoporosis** · **2–4× risk** · consider in every female athlete with a stress fracture

**Triad mechanism** — HPG-axis dysregulation → **↓ oestrogen** (needed for osteoblast maturation) → **↑ osteoclast relative to osteoblast activity** · OCP ↑ BMD

**Workup** — endocrine / metabolic bone workup · training, diet + menstrual history

### Genes & lab markers

**3× risk if absent** — **calcitonin receptor C allele** · **vitamin D receptor C-A haplotype**

### Anatomy & biomechanics

**Proximal femur** — neck-shaft angle **130 ± 7°** · anteversion **10 ± 7°** · **calcar** = strongest part (dense posteromedial bone)

**Head blood supply** — **MFCA predominant** · LFCA · artery of ligamentum teres (~**10%**) · ↑ displacement → ↑ vascular disruption

**Capsular ligaments** — iliofemoral (Y of Bigelow, AIIS → IT line) resists **ER + extension** · ischiofemoral resists IR · pubofemoral resists abduction + ER in extension

**Load** — **3–5× BW jogging** · **8.4× BW running**

**Mechanism** — repetitive loading > elastic limit → microfractures outpace osteoblastic repair · **fatigued hip muscles lose shock absorption** (Boden)

**Muscle strain** — abductors → highest compressive strain **superolateral** neck · extensors / flexors → tensile vs compressive strain posterior / anterior cortex · rectus femoris (knee extensor) loads neck least

| | **Compression side** | **Tension side** |
|---|---|---|
| Site | **Inferomedial** neck (calcar) | **Superolateral** neck |
| Propagation | Starts inferomedial → superiorly · **45°** → **stable oblique** | Starts superolateral → inferiorly · **90°** to cortex → **unstable transverse** |
| Cause | Compressive load | Bending; **abductors fatigue** |
| Risk | Low (if < 50%) | **High — displaces** |
| Rx | Nonop if < 50% | **Screw fixation** |

## 3 Presentation

**History** — **overuse** running, **recent ↑ training**, high-impact activity

**Symptoms** — **insidious thigh / groin pain** ± radiates to knee · ↑ with weight-bearing, ↓ with rest · pain while running → then walking · completion → crack / pop + can't bear weight

**Exam** — groin tenderness (**62%**), pain at extremes of motion (**79%**), antalgic gait · pain with **SLR, log roll, axial load**, passive IR

**No mechanical symptoms** — no impingement signs (vs FAI / labral tear)

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Study | Findings | Facts |
|---|---|---|
| **Radiographs** | AP pelvis, AP hip, cross-table lateral | **90% initial normal** · 50% normal at 4–6 wk repeat · changes take **6–8 wk** |
| X-ray late signs | **Grey cortex sign**, **linear lucency**, endosteal callus, **sclerotic line** (inferior neck = compression) | Visible line → MRI not needed, ↑ chance of displacement |
| **MRI** | Oedema on **STIR / fat-sat T2** · **low-signal line on T1** · hip effusion | **Study of choice** if X-ray negative · **sens 100%, spec 100%** · defines side + % width |
| MRI effusion | Hip effusion | **8× risk of propagation** |
| **Bone scan** | ↑ uptake (remodelling) | Only if **MRI contraindicated** · sens 93–100%, spec 76–95%, **false + 32%** |

**Gradual groin pain in runner, normal X-rays** — **MRI hip** (✗ IA steroid — labral tear, ✗ oral steroids, ✗ EMG / NCS — radiculopathy, ✗ CT for sports hernia)

**Slender runner, inferior neck sclerosis on X-ray** — **urgent MRI** to define extent (✗ aspiration, ✗ immediate fixation before extent known, ✗ protected WB with 2-wk films, ✗ return to running)

**Normal X-ray, superolateral marrow oedema on MRI** — **tension-side fracture** (alpha 45°, LCEA 30° = normal → ✗ FAI surgery)

### Fullerton–Snowdy & Shin classifications

| Fullerton–Snowdy (X-ray + bone scan) | Shin (X-ray + MRI) |
|---|---|
| **Type I — tension** (superolateral) (TO numbering) | Compression: **oedema, no fracture** |
| **Type II — compression** (inferomedial) | Compression: **line < 50%** |
| **Type III — displaced** | Compression: **line > 50%** |
| — | Tension-sided |
| — | Displaced |

**Fullerton–Snowdy (TO)** — **most commonly used** classification · Orthobullets lists the three groups unnumbered

**MRI compression subgroups (TO)** — no line · line **< 50%** · line **≥ 50%** of neck width

### Rohen-Quinquilla & Steele classifications

| Rohen-Quinquilla (MRI) | Steele (X-ray + MRI) |
|---|---|
| **Low grade I** — endosteal oedema **≤ 6 mm** | Compression: oedema, no fracture |
| **Low grade II** — oedema **> 6 mm**, no fracture | Fracture **< 50%, no effusion** |
| **High grade III** — fracture **< 50%** neck width | Fracture **< 50% with effusion** |
| **High grade IV** — fracture **> 50%** neck width | Fracture **> 50% with effusion** |
| — | Tension-sided |
| — | Displaced |

### High- vs low-risk stress fracture sites

| **High risk (TO)** | **Low risk (TO)** |
|---|---|
| **Femoral neck — tension side** | **Femoral shaft** |
| **Patella — tension side** | Medial tibia |
| **Anterior tibial cortex** | Ribs |
| **Medial malleolus** | Ulnar shaft |
| **Talar neck** | **1st–4th metatarsals** |
| **Dorsal navicular cortex** | — |
| **5th MT proximal metaphysis** | — |
| **Great toe sesamoids** | — |

**High risk (TO)** — tension sites → progression to complete fracture, delayed / nonunion, refracture, worse if diagnosis delayed

**Low risk (TO)** — compressive side · favourable natural history · respond to activity modification · rarely recur or go to nonunion

### Differential diagnosis

| Condition | Clue |
|---|---|
| Early OA | Older, ↓ IR, joint-space narrowing + sclerosis |
| Labral tear | Snapping, young, FAI · MRA |
| Chondral defect | Overlaps labral / FAI / dysplasia · MRI |
| Rectus strain | Sudden onset, AIIS tenderness, pain on resisted flexion / extension |
| Osteonecrosis | Steroids, alcohol, sickle cell · crescent sign, head flattening |
| Osteoid osteoma | Night pain, relieved by NSAIDs, nidus |
| Lumbar disc | Positional pain, back pain, paraesthesia, positive SLR |

## 5 Management

```
Femoral neck stress fracture on MRI
├─ Compression, line < 50%, no effusion → NWB / protected WB + activity restriction
├─ Compression, line > 50% or effusion or progression → percutaneous cannulated screws
├─ Tension side → percutaneous screw fixation (urgent)
│      (MRI stress change without line → nonop per Orthobullets)
└─ Displaced → urgent ORIF, anatomic reduction
```

### Operative criteria

| Finding | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Compression, line < 50%** | **Nonoperative** — NWB / protected WB, activity restriction |
| Tension-side stress change, **no line** (MRI) | Nonoperative (Orthobullets) |
| **Tension-side fracture** | **Cannulated screw fixation** |
| **Compression, line > 50%** | **Cannulated screws** (or strict NWB + weekly X-rays — Shin) |
| **Compression + hip effusion** | **Cannulated screws** (8× progression) |
| **Progression** of compression fracture | Cannulated screws |
| **Displaced** | **ORIF** — cannulated screws or sliding hip screw |

| TO treatment table | Incomplete (< 50% width) | Complete (> 50% width) |
|---|---|---|
| **Compression** | **Conservative** — unless significant pain or **can't straight-leg raise** | Surgical — **cannulated screws or DHS** |
| **Tension** | Surgical — **DHS** | Surgical — **DHS** |
| **Displaced** | — | **Immediate reduction + fixation** — DHS ± derotation screw |
| Atypical tension | Conservative | Surgical — DHS |

**Implant choice conflict** — **3 cannulated screws, inverted triangle** (Orthobullets, OB) · **DHS** for tension-side (TO table) — read the options

### Initial management – protected weight bearing

**Compression < 50% — NWB vs protected WB** — **NWB + activity modification** (TO; ✗ protected WB with crutches, ✗ screws, ✗ bed rest + calcium / vitamin D) · **crutches with protected WB** (OB; ✗ skeletal traction, ✗ calcium, ✗ ORIF, ✗ pulsed US) — read the options

**Nonop protocol (Orthobullets)** — **NWB 6 wk** → ↑ **25% BW per week** to painless FWB → **unrestricted at 3–4 months** · **75–100%** heal and return

**OB protocol** — protected WB + close observation **6–8 wk** · cessation of running until healed

**Nonop cons** — refracture (case reports), progression

**No role** — corticosteroids, calcium, pulsed ultrasound, skeletal traction in acute FNSF

**Low-risk stress fracture (TO)** — relative rest + activity modification · not healed or symptoms > **4–6 wk** → immobilisation + NWB or surgery

**High-risk incomplete fracture (TO)** — **immobilisation + strict NWB** · worsening / progression → fixation · complete high-risk fracture → strongly consider surgery

### Tension vs compression side – surgical fixation

**Tension side** — high risk of displacement → **percutaneous screws as soon as diagnosed** (Shin & Gillingham) → avoids displacement + harms of bedrest, allows early crutch WB (✗ cam / pincer arthroscopy, ✗ NSAIDs + ↓ mileage, ✗ PWB)

**Nonop tension side** — rare reports: strict NWB with **3+ wk bed rest** then prolonged crutches

**Compression > 50%** — **percutaneous screw fixation** (✗ oncology referral, ✗ valgus intertrochanteric osteotomy, ✗ arthroscopic labral repair, ✗ I&D)

**Recruit, 10% weight loss, compression line 60%** — **compression-sided; surgical fixation** (✗ complete fracture, ✗ crutches only, ✗ tension-sided)

**Deciding factor** — **compression vs tension side + % width** · bone density **not** important

**Displaced fatigue fracture** — **urgent surgery**

**Screw technique** — **three 7.0 / 7.3 mm cannulated screws, inverted triangle** (> two): **inferior calcar** (highest load to failure), posterosuperior, anterosuperior · start **at or above lesser trochanter** (avoid stress riser) · parallel, maximal spread · threads in head, not across fracture · washer vs GT penetration · percutaneous or mini-open lateral

**Screw outcomes** — prevent displacement · outcomes ≈ nonop lower-risk FNSF · **more military separation**

**Screw complications** — cutout, **varus collapse**, implant failure, neck shortening, nonunion

### ORIF of displaced FNSF

**Principle** — **anatomic reduction** + **early surgery** ↓ AVN

| Approach | Interval | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Smith-Petersen (anterior)** | Superficial **TFL (SGN) / sartorius (femoral)** · deep **gluteus medius (SGN) / rectus femoris (femoral)** — internervous | Best view of whole neck · implants via separate lateral incision |
| **Watson-Jones** | **TFL / gluteus medius** (both SGN) — intermuscular | Same incision for reduction + implant · poor subcapital view |

**Reduction aids** — K-wire / Schanz joysticks, pointed clamps, **medial buttress plate** · fix with cannulated screws or DHS · autograft vs nonunion

**ORIF complications** — infection, AVN, nonunion, cutout, malreduction, implant failure · lower return to activity than nondisplaced

## 6 Complications

| Complication | Incidence | Risk factors | Treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Progression / completion** | **14%** | **Hip effusion (8×)**, delayed diagnosis | Cannulated screws |
| **AVN** | Rare if incomplete · **5–42% displaced** | Delay to surgery, initial displacement, **varus malreduction** | Pre-collapse: core decompression / vascularised fibula · collapse: arthroplasty |
| **Nonunion** | Rare if incomplete · **9–44% displaced** | Delay, noncompliance | **Valgus intertrochanteric osteotomy** |
| **Varus malunion** | **5–33%** | — | Revision ORIF + graft, arthroplasty |
| Refracture | Case reports | After nonop | — |

**Fracture size and progression** — size **not** associated with progression (Orthobullets complications) · **> 50%** width → **high risk of progression** (OB, Robertson & Wood) — read the options

### Prognosis

| Outcome | Nonoperative | Operative |
|---|---|---|
| Return to military | **~75%** | **~40–45%** |
| Return to sport | **30–100%** | **48–56%** |

**Negative predictors** — displacement, high athletic demand (vs recreational), delayed treatment · natural history → progression + displacement

## X vs Y

| | **FNSF** | **FAI / labral tear** |
|---|---|---|
| Pain | Insidious, **weight-bearing / axial load** | Flexion, sitting, **FADIR** |
| Mechanical symptoms | **None** | Click, catch, lock |
| Imaging | **MRI oedema / line** | Alpha angle, crossover, MRA tear |
| Rx | Rest / screws | Arthroscopy |

## How it's asked

**"Marathoner, groin pain, normal X-rays, pain on SLR + IR?"** → **MRI of the hip**

**"Slender XC runner, inferior neck sclerosis — next?"** → **urgent MRI**

**"Marathoner, compression fracture > 50% on MRI?"** → **percutaneous screw fixation**

**"Superolateral neck oedema, normal X-ray?"** → **percutaneous screw placement**

**"Marine recruit, compression line 60%?"** → **compression-sided fracture; surgical fixation**

**"Athlete, limp, compression line < 50% (TO)?"** → **non-weight-bearing + activity modification**

**"Same TO stem, repeated paper?"** → **NWB + activity modification** (✗ protected WB with crutches)

**"Recruit, compression fracture ⅓ neck width on MRI?"** → **crutches with protected weight-bearing**

**"Low-risk stress fracture (TO)?"** → **femoral shaft** (✗ tension patella, ✗ anterior tibial cortex, ✗ medial malleolus)
