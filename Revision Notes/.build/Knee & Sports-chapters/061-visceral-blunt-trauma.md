# Visceral Blunt Trauma

*Knee & Sports · 61 / 61*

## 1 Basics

### Overview

**Injuries** — **abdominal wall**, **spleen**, **liver**, **kidney**, **testicular + penile**

**Risk factors** — **contact sports**, **direct blows** · **deceleration** → spleen, liver, kidney

**Commonest sport injuries (Rifat, OB)** — **abdominal wall muscle, liver, spleen** · **spleen = most frequent intra-abdominal organ** in sport

| Organ | Rank / fact | Clue |
|---|---|---|
| **Spleen** | **Most common** abdominal organ in blunt trauma · **most common cause of death** from abdominal / visceral injury | **LUQ pain**, **Kehr's sign** (left shoulder), N/V · **mononucleosis** |
| **Liver** | **2nd most common** | **RUQ pain → right shoulder** |
| **Kidney** | Contact athletes, **blow to flank** | **Haematuria** (may be absent), flank ecchymosis |
| Abdominal wall | Commonest sport injury | **Localised pain**, pain on **trunk flexion / rotation** |

## 3 Presentation

**Warning pattern** — **localised pain → diffuse pain** = visceral injury

**Spleen** — **nausea + vomiting** · **sharp LUQ pain → dull + diffuse** · shoulder pain (**Kehr's sign**) **before hypotension + collapse**

**Signs (Juyia, OB)** — **referred shoulder pain**, **umbilical or flank ecchymosis**, **guarding / rebound** · high index of suspicion with any abdominal pain

### Diaphragmatic spasm

**"Wind knocked out"** — **direct blow to epigastrium** → diaphragmatic spasm → **observe**, resolves, **may continue play**

### Abdominal wall injury / rectus sheath haematoma

**Cause** — **epigastric or intramuscular vessel** injury

**Clinical** — **acute localised pain** · **mimics surgical abdomen** (N/V) · **pain on active trunk flexion or rotation**

**Treatment** — **ice + rest** (most) · **surgical decompression** if progressive + persistent pain

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Study | Use |
|---|---|
| **CT abdomen (± pelvis)** | **Diagnosis for all organs** — wall haematoma, spleen, liver, kidney, bowel · grades severity, guides treatment |
| **Scrotal ultrasound (urology)** | **Testicular rupture / haematocoele** |
| Pelvic X-ray | Fracture → posterior urethral injury |

## 5 Management

### Hypotension after abdominal trauma – work-up

```
Athlete with abdominal blow
├─ Haemodynamically unstable → fluid resuscitation + transfer to ER → abdominal CT
├─ Stable, abdominal pain / haematuria → ER + CT abdomen / pelvis
└─ Epigastric blow, transient breathlessness → observe, may play
```

**Footballer, BP 70/48, vomiting ×2, resolving arm numbness** — **fluid resuscitation + transfer to ER for abdominal CT** (✗ antiemetics + sideline rest, ✗ non-emergent C-spine MRI — **stinger** resolved, ✗ RTP when arm settles, ✗ head CT — no concussion signs)

**RTP (Juyia, OB)** — varies with organ + grade · **up to 6 months** after severe spleen / liver injury

### Splenic rupture – mononucleosis risk

**Mono** — **acute splenomegaly** → ↑ rupture risk with trauma

**Spread** — **oral mucosa**: **sharing water bottles**, kissing ✓ (✗ creatine, ✗ spider bite — skin necrosis, ✗ alcohol, ✗ NSAIDs on empty stomach — gastric irritation)

**Timing** — **4–21 days after infection** (OB, this topic) · **first 3 weeks** / within a month (infectious diseases chapter)

**Farley (OB)** — **> 8,000** IM patients → **9 splenic ruptures** (\~0.1%; 0.1–0.5% in infectious diseases chapter)

**17-yr-old, mono 1 month ago, tackled, LUQ pain, tachycardia** — **splenic rupture** (✗ fractured kidney, ✗ ruptured appendix, ✗ ruptured gallbladder, ✗ pneumothorax)

**RTP after mono** — **no contact sport until splenomegaly resolved** · radiographic surveillance **debated**

**Spleen treatment** — **observation** in most · **splenectomy only for severe** injury · range: observation → **laparotomy + packing ± splenectomy**

### Liver injury

**Treatment** — **conservative: monitoring + rest**

### Renal contusion – haematuria

**Pattern** — **males**, **single blow to the flank** · flank / abdominal pain, **ecchymosis**, **haematuria**

**Lacrosse, right flank blow, pink urine > 5 RBC/hpf, stable** — **renal laceration** (✗ splenic laceration — left side, intra-abdominal bleed not haematuria, ✗ haemothorax — chest pain + dyspnoea, ✗ liver laceration — no haematuria, ✗ posterior urethral injury — **displaced pelvic fracture**)

**Work-up** — **CT abdomen + pelvis ASAP** (risk of instability with continued bleeding)

**Treatment** — **stable → nonop** · small subset → exploration, partial / complete nephrectomy · **urgent surgery: extensive bleeding with renal fracture or vascular pedicle injury**

**Patel (OB)** — grade III / IV: **contact sports + skiing / snowboarding** commonest · sports injuries more **isolated**, ↓ instability + mortality

**Dangle (OB)** — paediatric: **low-grade injuries in males** commonest · few need surgery

**McAleer (OB)** — **< 5%** of testicular / renal injuries from team sports, no organ loss → **solitary kidney / testis may not need restriction**

![Right renal laceration with subcapsular haematoma (CT)](../MCQ/attachments/q215510-illa-right-renal-laceration-ct.jpg)

### Penile & testicular injuries

**Pudendal nerve neuropraxia** — **cyclists** · **penile shaft numbness** · **seat modification**

**Testicular rupture** — usually a **kick** → **tunica albuginea rupture** → **haematocoele** · **painful firm scrotal mass, doesn't transilluminate** · **urology ultrasound** · observation

## X vs Y

| | **Splenic injury** | **Liver injury** | **Renal injury** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Side / blow | **Left**, LUQ | **Right**, RUQ | **Flank** |
| Referred pain | **Left shoulder (Kehr)** | **Right shoulder** | — |
| Urine | Normal | Normal | **Haematuria** |
| Bleeding | Intra-abdominal | Intra-abdominal | **Haematuria** |
| Treatment | Observe · **splenectomy if severe** | **Conservative** (monitor + rest) | Nonop if stable · surgery for renal fracture / **pedicle injury** |

**"Peak window for splenic rupture after mono?"** → **4–21 days**

**"BP 70/48 + vomiting after hit — next step?"** → **fluid resuscitation + ER transfer for abdominal CT**

**"Flank blow, pink urine, > 5 RBC/hpf — CT shows?"** → **renal laceration**

**"Most common cause of death from abdominal injury?"** → **splenic injury**

**"Cyclist with penile numbness?"** → **pudendal neuropraxia — seat modification**
