# Popliteal Artery Entrapment Syndrome

*Knee & Sports · 44 / 61*

## 1 Basics

**Definition** — **constriction of the popliteal artery** by adjacent muscles, tendons or fibrous tissue · usually an **underlying anatomic abnormality**

**Demographics** — **male 4:1** · age **25–40 years**

**Pathophysiology** — ↓ flow distal to the popliteal fossa → symptoms **mimic compartment syndrome**

**Classic pathoanatomy (OB)** — compression by the **medial head of gastrocnemius** · **accessory slip of medial gastrocnemius head** on MRI

### Modified Whelan classification

| Type | Anatomy | Image |
|---|---|---|
| **I** | **Normal medial gastrocnemius head**, popliteal artery takes an **aberrant course** | ![Type I](../Orthobullets/attachments/ob3107-popliteal-artery.jpg) |
| **II** | **Medial gastrocnemius head located laterally**, artery not deviated | |
| **III** | **Abnormal muscle bundle from medial gastrocnemius head** surrounds + constricts artery | |
| **IV** | Artery entrapped by **popliteus** | |
| **V** | **Popliteal vein entrapped with the artery** in any of the above | |

## 3 Presentation

**Symptoms** — **intermittent claudication** (OB) · **limb swelling often the primary complaint** · foot numbness, **toe tingling after vigorous exercise** · **calf cramping even after light exercise** as it worsens · may be asymptomatic

**Exam** — **diminished pulses with active plantarflexion or passive dorsiflexion** · cool posterior calf, paraesthesias · **easily confused with posterior compartment syndrome**

**Sensory pattern (OB)** — dysaesthesia in the **sural** distribution (posterolateral leg / foot)

**Runner with pain, coolness, tingling (OB)** — **pain with passive dorsiflexion + plantarflexion**, **compartment pressures normal** → **PAES** (✗ exertional compartment syndrome, ✗ tibial stress fracture, ✗ lumbar radiculopathy, ✗ piriformis)

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Study | Finding / use |
|---|---|
| Radiographs | **Usually normal** |
| **Doppler US** | Less invasive; detects **pulse change during active plantarflexion / passive dorsiflexion** |
| **Arteriogram** | **Confirms diagnosis**, **\~100% sensitivity** → **stenosis, obliteration, post-stenotic dilation** |
| MRA / CTA | Usefulness still under study · MRA can show **segmental popliteal occlusion** (OB) |

![Arteriogram — popliteal artery entrapment](../Orthobullets/attachments/ob3107-popliteal-artery-entrapment-syndrome-image.jpg)

### Differential of exertional leg pain (OB)

**Exertional compartment syndrome** — needs **↑ compartment pressures**: rest > 15 · 1 min post > 30 · 5 min post > 20 · 15 min post > 15 mmHg (Orthobullets) → normal pressures exclude it

**Lumbar radiculopathy / piriformis** — neuropathy does not explain a **cool, crampy leg**

**Tibial stress fracture** — **not relieved by running**, no claudication signs

**Other differentials** — CECS, medial tibial stress syndrome, tibial / fibular stress fracture, fascial defects, nerve entrapment, **vascular claudication (atherosclerotic or PAES)**, lumbar disc herniation

## 5 Management

```
Popliteal artery entrapment
├─ Mild symptoms, rigorous exercise only → activity modification + observation
└─ Refractory / artery or vein damage → vascular surgery
       ├─ release the entrapping structure (depends on type)
       └─ damaged artery → saphenous vein bypass vs endarterectomy
```

**Natural history** — **most patients eventually need surgery**

**Approach** — **posterior → better exposure** · **medial → used when bypass needed**

## 6 Complications

| Complication | Rate |
|---|---|
| **Surgical failure (entrapment returns)** | **30%** → can need **amputation** |
| Wound infection | **< 5%** |
| **DVT** | **\~10%** |
| **Asymptomatic after surgery** | **70–100%** |

## X vs Y

| | **PAES** | **Exertional compartment syndrome** |
|---|---|---|
| Compartment | **Posterior** leg | **Anterior** (± lateral) commonest |
| Mechanism | Artery compressed (medial gastrocnemius) | ↑ pressure in a closed compartment |
| Key sign | **↓ pulses with active plantarflexion / passive dorsiflexion**, cool leg | Normal exam at rest |
| Nerve symptoms | Sural distribution | **Dorsum of foot / 1st web** |
| Confirm | **Arteriogram** (Doppler US screen) | **Compartment pressures** |

## How it's asked

**"Claudication, coolness, tingling; pain on passive DF + PF; normal pressures?"** → **popliteal artery entrapment syndrome**

**"Confirmatory test?"** → **arteriogram (stenosis, obliteration, post-stenotic dilation)**
