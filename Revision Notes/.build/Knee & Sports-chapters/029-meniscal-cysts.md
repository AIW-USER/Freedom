# Meniscal Cysts

*Knee & Sports · 29 / 61*

## 1 Basics

**Definition** — local collection of **synovial fluid** within or next to the meniscus, **most often from a meniscal tear**

**Incidence** — no general-population data · **1–4% of knee MRIs** · no age trend

**Association** — **meniscal tear** — almost always present, the most common associated lesion (TO) · also articular cartilage injury, ACL tear (Orthobullets) · TO: ✗ "common with ACL tear"

| Type | Site | Usual tear |
|---|---|---|
| **Perimeniscal** | Small fluid collection **within** the meniscus · medial → **posterior horn** · lateral → **anterior horn / mid-portion** | **Radial or vertical** |
| **Parameniscal** (e.g. Baker) | **Extruded outside the meniscus — most common** · usually between **semimembranosus + medial head of gastrocnemius** | **Horizontal or complex** |

**Side** — **medial slightly commoner, 2:1** (Orthobullets — literature conflicting) · **medial > lateral** on recent radiology reviews; lateral was the old teaching (TO)

**Lateral meniscal cysts (OB)** — **myxoid degeneration** spreading from the meniscal centre outward · **horizontal cleavage tears** · most often from a **middle-third LM tear** (✗ discoid meniscus, ✗ PLC injury, ✗ vertical tears, ✗ popliteus tear)

## 2 Mechanism

**One-way valve** — the tear lets synovial fluid out, not back → fluid concentrates into gel-like material

## 3 Presentation

**History** — ± recent trauma · may be **asymptomatic**

**Symptoms** — pain at the **medial / lateral joint line or back of knee** · **locking + clicking** · delayed / intermittent swelling · weakness or claudication (**neurovascular impingement**)

**Exam** — **popliteal mass, best seen in extension** · joint line tenderness · palpable mass · crepitus

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Study | Finding |
|---|---|
| Radiographs | **Normal** in young patients with acute meniscal injury or cyst |
| **MRI** | **Most sensitive test** for both the **cyst and the tear** · cyst = **bright T2** |
| MRI mimics | **Necrotic tissue, nerve sheath tissue, pus** look like cysts on T2 → **IV contrast** may be needed |

![Parameniscal (Baker) cyst between semimembranosus and medial gastrocnemius — axial T2 MRI](../Orthobullets/attachments/ob3006-ganglion-cyst-baker-mri.jpg)

**Parameniscal cyst on MRI** — **implies a meniscal tear** (Meniscal Tears chapter)

## 5 Management

```
Meniscal cyst
├─ Small perimeniscal or parameniscal → rest, NSAIDs, physiotherapy (first line)
├─ Isolated Baker cyst, young patient → aspiration + US-guided steroid injection
├─ Tear communicates with joint, not repairable → arthroscopic partial meniscectomy + decompression
└─ Symptomatic parameniscal / tear not open to joint → open posterior cyst excision
```

**Nonop** — first line · trial to gauge pain response · may work with degenerative tears

**Aspiration + steroid** — for **isolated Baker cyst in the young** · **poor results in older patients with degenerative tears + cysts**

**Arthroscopic debridement, decompression + partial meniscectomy** — perimeniscal cyst with a **tear not amenable to repair** (complex, degenerative, radial) · decompress fully · **incomplete resection → recurrence** · repair the meniscus if the tear allows (Orthobullets summary)

**Tear doesn't open into joint (OB)** — arthroscopy **followed by open cystectomy**

**Open posterior excision** — **prone**, curved incision over popliteal fossa · interval **medial gastrocnemius ↔ semimembranosus** · sharp dissection of cyst down to capsule · incomplete resection → recurrence

## How it's asked

**"Lateral meniscal cyst — associated pathology?"** → **middle-third lateral meniscal tear**

**"True about meniscal cysts (TO)?"** → **most common associated lesion is a meniscal tear**
