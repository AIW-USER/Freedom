# Neck Injuries in Athletes

*Knee & Sports · 32 / 61*

## 1 Basics

### Spectrum & epidemiology

**Spectrum** — **ligament sprain** → **burner / stinger** → **spear tackler's spine** → **cervical fracture** → **transient quadriplegia** → **quadriplegia**

**Who** — contact sports, **football + rugby** · better protective gear → ↓ incidence · **9–15%** of \~12,000 new spinal cord injuries / yr from contact / collision sports

**Catastrophic** — \~**6 quadriplegic events / yr** (US high school + college football; more at college level)

**Highest rate of cord injury** — **bilateral facet dislocation**

### Predisposing conditions (↑ severity, may bar play)

**Underlying conditions** — **previous C-spine trauma** (fracture, ligament injury), **cervical stenosis**, **congenital odontoid hypoplasia**, **os odontoideum**, **Klippel-Feil** anomalies

## 2 Mechanism

### Spear tackling & axial loading

**Mechanism** — **axial load (compression) on a flexed neck** · most injuries during **tackling**

**Spear tackling** (leading with crown of helmet) — **most common mechanism of neck injury in football** → gradual **cervical stenosis + loss of lordosis**

**Catastrophic injury** — **axial loading of the subaxial spine during spear tackling** → fracture, dislocation, ligament disruption (✗ arm-tackle traction root avulsion — neurotmesis, most severe "stinger"; ✗ lateral bending; ✗ flexion-distraction / whiplash cut block; ✗ face-mask rotation)

**Boden series (196 catastrophic injuries, 13 yr)** — **subaxial 95**, C1-C2 9, combined 7, unknown 41 · **43 cord neurapraxia**, 1 root avulsion · **defensive backs + spear tackling** = greatest risk

| # | Spear tackler's spine — all 4 criteria |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Developmental cervical canal stenosis** |
| 2 | **Persistent straightening / reversal of lordosis** |
| 3 | Post-traumatic radiographic abnormalities |
| 4 | **Documented spear-tackling technique** |

**Spear tackler's spine** — **contraindication to contact sports**

## 3 Presentation

### On-field management of suspected cervical spine injury

**Assume spinal injury** — any athlete with **lost or altered consciousness**

**Most important prehospital step** — **maintain neutral cervical alignment** (✗ assess off field first, ✗ high-dose steroids — weak evidence, surgeon's discretion)

```
Prone player, can't move limbs
├─ 1 → log-roll SUPINE with C-spine stabilised
├─ 2 → remove FACEMASK (airway)
├─ 3 → remove helmet + shoulder pads together (if safe)
└─ 4 → hard collar, controlled backboard (log-roll)
       (equipment kept: towel rolls taped beside helmet)
```

**✗ Traps** — **hard collar with helmet still on** · keeping player **prone** · **helmet off before facemask**

**Helmet + pads** — **NATA now allows on-field removal of both** by trained personnel if neutral alignment kept (Orthobullets, Mills consensus) · older advice: **remove facemask, retain helmet** (Schroeder & Vaccaro) · cadaver C5-6: **helmet alone → kyphosis, pads alone → lordosis, both retained → neutral** — remove or retain **together**

**Exam** — inspect deformity · palpate spinous processes (**step-off**, pain) · motor, sensation + reflexes in **all 4 limbs**

### Transient quadriplegia (cervical cord neurapraxia)

**Features** — neurapraxia of the cord → **bilateral upper + lower limb** pain, paresthesia, weakness → resolves **minutes–hours** (Orthobullets) · **10 min–48 h** (OB, stinger chapter)

**Mechanism** — hyperextension, hyperflexion or axial load · 3 of 16 NFL disc herniations presented with transient paresis

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Study | Fact |
|---|---|
| Radiographs — indications | **Recurrent stingers** · neurologic symptoms / **transient quadriplegia** · C-spine trauma series |
| **Canal diameter (SAC)** | Normal **\~17 mm** · **< 13 mm = stenosis** · **< 10 mm = absolute stenosis** (OB) |
| **Torg-Pavlov ratio** | **Canal ÷ vertebral body** (sagittal) · normal **1.0** · stenosis **< 0.8** (Orthobullets) · **< 0.82** (Pavlov + Torg original) |
| Torg ratio limits | **Technique dependent**, **low PPV / not predictive**, **inaccurate in large athletes** (big bodies skew ratio) |
| Torg ratio < 0.8 prevalence | **49% of asymptomatic pro football players** |
| **MRI** | **Bilateral neurologic symptoms** → stenosis, **loss of CSF around cord**, cord signal change |
| "Functional" stenosis | **Loss of CSF / cord deformation on MRI or CT myelogram** — bone ratios alone can't define it |

**Torg original data** — canal **14 mm** in symptomatic athletes vs **18.9 mm** controls

**SAC at injury level vs deficit** — **10.5 mm complete** · 13.1 mm incomplete · 15.9 mm root · 16.7 mm none

## 5 Management

### Return-to-play contraindications (cord signal change, fusion, transient quadriparesis, Torg ratio)

| ✓ May return to play | Condition |
|---|---|
| Stinger | **Complete resolution**, normal strength + ROM |
| Transient quadriplegia | **Normal MRI** |
| **Congenital stenosis (Torg < 0.8), no instability** | ✓ — counsel risks (✗ absolute contraindication) |
| **Solid 1- or 2-level ACDF** | Asymptomatic, **no T2 signal change** |
| Transient paralysis, **resolved T2 change, SAC 14 mm** | ✓ RTP |

| Case-by-case | Condition |
|---|---|
| Solid **1-level ACDF, continued T2 change** | Individual decision |
| Transient paralysis, **never had T2 change** (e.g. SAC 9 mm) | Individual decision |
| Transient paralysis, **resolved T2 change, SAC ≥ 10 mm** (e.g. 11 mm) | Individual decision |
| **Torg type 2 Klippel-Feil below C3**, asymptomatic | **Relative** contraindication |

| ✗ No return to play | Condition |
|---|---|
| **Cord signal change on MRI** | **Absolute** · multiple transient quadriparesis episodes + MRI cord injury → avoid contact |
| Transient quadriplegia + **severe / functional stenosis** | No RTP |
| **Spear tackler's spine** | No RTP |
| Cord neurapraxia + **ligamentous instability** | No RTP |
| **C0–C2 anomalies** | **Odontoid hypoplasia, os odontoideum, atlanto-occipital fusion** — absolute |
| **Torg type 1 Klippel-Feil** (long fusion mass) | Absolute |
| Torg type 2 Klippel-Feil (1–2 segments) | With limited motion, degeneration or instability |
| **3-level ACDF** | Even if solid, asymptomatic, **no T2 change** |
| Continued T2 change after **2- or 3-level ACDF** | No RTP |
| **Resolved T2 change + SAC < 10 mm** after transient paralysis | No RTP |
| Cervical myelopathy history | No RTP |

**Bilateral UE numbness > 15 min, Torg 0.80** — **no RTP until advanced imaging (MRI)** (✗ Torg has high PPV, ✗ "low PPV → return now", ✗ only useful in pros, ✗ congenital stenosis = absolute bar)

**Transient quadriplegia + residual paresthesia, Torg 0.7, C5-6 disc compressing cord, canal 9 mm, lost lordosis** — **disqualify from further contact play** (✗ waiver, ✗ graduated RTP, ✗ PT + epidural, ✗ decompression + fusion then RTP)

**Disc herniation in athletes** — commonest = **radiculopathy after one event** · disc abutting cord → nonop allowed but **no RTS until symptoms improve + repeat imaging shows no cord compression** · usual **6-week conservative trial**

**Operative** — as for any traumatic spinal injury

## X vs Y

| | **Stinger** | **Transient quadriparesis** |
|---|---|---|
| Lesion | Root / brachial plexus | **Spinal cord** |
| Limbs | **Unilateral** arm | **Bilateral**, arms ± legs |
| Imaging | Radiographs if recurrent | **MRI** |
| RTP | Same day if fully resolved | Only if **MRI normal**, no instability / severe stenosis |

| | **Torg-Pavlov ratio** | **MRI "functional" stenosis** |
|---|---|---|
| Measures | Bony canal ÷ body width | **CSF reserve around the cord** |
| Predicts injury | **Poorly** (low PPV) | Better — bars contact sport |
