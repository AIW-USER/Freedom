# Cardiac Conditions

*Knee & Sports · 8 / 61*

## 1 Basics

### Overview

**Leading cause** — cardiac conditions = **most common cause of sudden death in young athletes** → **HCM (most common)**, coronary artery disease, commotio cordis

**Conditions covered** — syncope, HCM, CAD, long QT syndrome, commotio cordis, sudden cardiac death

### Sudden cardiac death – epidemiology & causes

**Definition** — death from a cardiovascular cause **within 1 hour of symptom onset**

**Incidence** — **0.76–13 per 100,000** (Orthobullets) · up to **1 in 40,000** young athletes (OB) · **2.8×** commoner in adolescent / teenage athletes than non-athletes

**Mechanism** — arrhythmia → ventricular ectopy → sustained **VT / VF**; usually a **pre-existing condition**

| Age group | Usual cause |
|---|---|
| Birth – 13 yr | **Congenital abnormality** |
| 14 – 24 yr | **HCM**, ARVC, congenital coronary anomalies, WPW, Marfan |
| Young athletes (TO) | **HCM + ARVC > 50%**; congenital coronary anomalies + premature coronary atherosclerosis **20%** |
| Structurally normal heart (TO) | **Ion channelopathies** — congenital long QT, Brugada, CPVT |
| **> 35 yr** | **Atherosclerotic CAD** — **> 80%** of SCD (TO) |

**Most common cause, < 35 yr** — **hypertrophic cardiomyopathy** ("cardiomyopathy" in TO) (✗ CAD, ✗ Brugada, ✗ channelopathy, ✗ commotio cordis, ✗ aortic rupture, ✗ myocarditis, ✗ long QT, ✗ WPW)

| Cause at autopsy (OB, Garrick) | Share |
|---|---|
| **HCM** | **36%** |
| Coronary anomalies | 19% |
| Increased cardiac mass | 10% |
| Ruptured aorta (Marfan) | 5% |
| Dilated cardiomyopathy | 3% |
| Mitral valve prolapse | 2% |

**HCM share** — **36%** (OB, Garrick) · **20.6%**, then idiopathic LVH 13.4%, coronary anomalies 12.0% (OB illustration) — read the options

![Causes of sudden death in athletes](../MCQ/attachments/q215478-illa-causes-for-sudden-death-in-athletes-diagram.jpg)

**2nd commonest cause** — **coronary artery disease** (Orthobullets; mostly older) · coronary anomalies 19% (OB, Garrick) · **anomalous origin of left main coronary** → compression → ischaemia

**Risk factors** — **male**, **African-American (black)** race, **cardiomyopathy**, **contact sports**, **basketball** (OB) · ✗ "commoner in white American males" = the incorrect statement (TO)

**High-risk sports (TO)** — **basketball (most deaths)**, American football, soccer

**Age trend** — SCD **commoner in adolescent than adult athletes** (true statement, TO) · risk **increases with age** (Orthobullets) — read the options

**High-risk markers (TO)** — **unheralded syncope**, **LVH > 30 mm**, **non-sustained VT**, **family history of SCD**, **↓ BP in response to exercise**

**Prognosis** — **> 90% mortality**; early recognition + **AED** ↑ survival

**Survival (TO)** — **time from arrest to defibrillation** = single most important factor · **one shock → immediate CPR**

**Sequence (TO)** — **C-A-B** = chest compressions, airway, breathing · AHA **30:2** compression : ventilation (OB)

**AED** — primarily for **ventricular fibrillation** (✗ AF, ✗ bradycardia, ✗ tachycardia, ✗ asystole); may ↑ survival by **90%** (OB)

### Genes & lab markers

| Condition | Inheritance / gene / lab |
|---|---|
| **HCM** | **Autosomal dominant**, variable penetrance; mostly **missense** (less often frameshift) |
| HCM genes | **TNNT2** (cardiac troponin T), TNNI3 (troponin I), **MYL2** (regulatory light chain), **MYL3** (essential light chain) |
| Long QT | Mostly **AD** (Orthobullets) · AD or AR (TO) · genetic testing for all, **yield 70%** (TO) |
| Long QT labs | **Basic metabolic panel + magnesium** (electrolytes worsen QT) |
| SCD work-up | **Cardiac enzymes**; genetic testing for **Brugada, LQTS, ARVC** |

### Athlete's heart – physiology

**Well-conditioned heart (OB)** — **↑ wall thickness** → **↑ stroke volume** · **↑ vagal tone** → **↓ resting HR** ✓ · CO = SV × HR, ↑ in exercise (✗ ↓ SV, ✗ ↓ CO, ✗ ↓ wall thickness, ✗ ↓ vagal tone)

**Normal ECG in endurance athletes** — **ventricular hypertrophy**, **1st-degree (primary) AV block**, **nonspecific ST-T changes in lateral leads**, **resting sinus bradycardia 40 bpm**

**Athletic heart syndrome** — physiological **LVH may mimic HCM** ✓ (mild phenotypes overlap) · asymptomatic, normal exam · **no extra SCD risk**

## 2 Mechanism

### Commotio cordis – mechanism

**Mechanism** — **blunt blow to anterior chest (precordium) during ventricular repolarization** → **upstroke of T wave** → aberrant depolarization → **ventricular fibrillation** (✗ during depolarization)

**Vulnerable window** — T-wave upstroke = **1%** of cardiac cycle (Orthobullets) · **< 1%** (OB)

**Physics** — energy **> 50 J** → myocardial membrane stretch → ion channels via **mechanical-electrical coupling** · **smaller balls ↑ risk** (smaller impact area) · higher energy → structural damage rather than isolated VF

**Heart structure** — **no structural damage at autopsy** ever found (✗ "often structural damage") · = sudden death **without structural cardiac damage** (OB)

![Commotio cordis: T-wave upstroke risk window](../MCQ/attachments/q211821-illa-screen-shot-2020-03-23-at-11-57-48-am.jpg)

**Epidemiology** — **< 30 cases / yr**; **average age 15**; **M:F 20:1**; contact sports, hard-ball sports (baseball, lacrosse), karate punch, Little League batter hit by pitch

**Screening** — **not detectable on pre-season physical** (✗; unlike HCM)

**Prevention** — **chest protectors / pads not shown to prevent** (baseball, hockey) · soft-core baseballs ≈ standard balls for fatal risk (OB, Janda) · educate coaches + **AED access**

## 3 Presentation

### Red flags & syncope

**Best screening tool** — **history + physical exam** (high-school athletes)

**Symptoms** — **chest pain**, **palpitations**, **syncope** with exercise

**Murmurs** — **diastolic murmur → work-up**; **II/VI systolic murmur**

**Syncope** — **red flag for serious cardiac disease** in a young athlete · annual incidence **7%**, F > M · risk: cardiac disease, dehydration, female

**Vasovagal (neurocardiogenic)** — **50%** of syncope; vasodilation + **bradycardia** (↓ sympathetic, ↑ parasympathetic); prolonged standing, heat

**Syncope RTP** — **medical evaluation before return** to sport

### Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy – clinical features

**Pathology** — abnormal thick / thin myofilament proteins → **asymmetric LV wall + septal hypertrophy** → **LVOT obstruction**, diastolic dysfunction, ↑ SCD · **interventricular septum in 66%**

**Epidemiology** — **1 in 500**; present from birth, diagnosed **20–30 yr**; **M:F 1:1** (females more often symptomatic); risk: hypertension, genetics

**Symptoms** — dyspnoea on exertion, chest pain, dizziness, presyncope / syncope · **> 50% minimal or no symptoms** (OB)

**Murmur** — **II/VI systolic crescendo-decrescendo** · obstruction at rest **25%**, with provocation **70%**

**Murmur ↑** — **↓ preload: Valsalva, standing, diuretics** → ↓ venous return → asymmetric septum obstructs LVOT

**Squatting / handgrip** — ↑ preload / afterload; Orthobullets lists them with the provocative manoeuvres (wording inconsistent) — the tested fact is **murmur ↑ with standing / Valsalva**

**Other signs** — **double carotid pulse**, **double apical pulse**, arrhythmia, family history

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Test | Finding / role |
|---|---|
| **Echocardiogram** | **Nondilated, thickened LV wall** vs age-matched · **80%** accuracy · outflow obstruction, valves · **confirms HCM**; best test with **FHx of SCD** (OB) |
| **Cardiac MRI** | **Gold standard for HCM** — when echo inconclusive |
| **ECG** | **Most sensitive diagnostic test** (Orthobullets) · **LVH + right atrial enlargement** highly suggestive · Q waves inferior + lateral, **left axis deviation**, inverted T |
| Ambulatory ECG (Holter) | **All HCM**, 24–48 h → ventricular arrhythmias, SCD risk |
| Exercise stress test | Preferred over pharmacological · **LVOT gradient → risk stratification** · ✗ does not diagnose HCM (OB) |
| CAD | **Coronary angiography + MR angiography** = gold standard |
| Commotio cordis | ECG → **ventricular fibrillation** |
| Long QT | **ECG gold standard** (Orthobullets) · long QT on resting ECG in **\~60%** (TO) |

**SCD work-up** — cardiac MRI, coronary angiography, echo, exercise stress test, **electrophysiology testing**, ECG

**Best HCM test** — **ECG most sensitive** (Orthobullets) · **echo most effective screen / confirms** (OB) · **cardiac MRI gold standard** (Orthobullets) — match the stem's wording

### Pre-participation screening (history, ECG, echocardiogram)

**AHA** — careful **personal + family history + physical exam** = best, most practical screening at any age

**ECG not routine in US** — **cost**; **98.8%** of mass-ECG cost spent on **false positives** (O'Connor) · H&P more cost-effective · ECG **can** detect SCD causes (✗ "cannot detect" = the EXCEPT answer)

**Most cost-effective HCM screen** — **history of chest pain, syncope, FHx of early cardiac death** (✗ echo, ✗ ECG, ✗ auscultation — may be normal at rest, ✗ stress test)

**Screening echo cost** — **> $500,000** to diagnose one HCM case

**Black male basketball player, no history** — **routine history + physical** (✗ ECG, ✗ stress test, ✗ Hb electrophoresis, ✗ lung diffusion) · ECG only if personal / family cardiac history

**2 relatives died playing sport (AD trait)** — **echocardiogram** (✗ ECG, ✗ renal US, ✗ PFT, ✗ cervical MRI)

```
Pre-participation evaluation
├─ All athletes → history + physical exam
├─ FHx SCD, exertional syncope / chest pain, murmur → ECG ± echocardiogram
└─ Echo inconclusive → cardiac MRI
```

### Diagnostic criteria

| Condition | Criteria |
|---|---|
| **SCD** | Cardiovascular death **< 1 h** from symptom onset |
| **HCM** | **Echo + ECG + genetic testing + family history** |
| **Commotio cordis** | **Witnessed chest blow → collapse + ECG VF** |
| Long QT, symptomatic (TO) | **QTc > 440 ms (male)**, **> 460 ms (female)** |
| Long QT, no symptoms / FHx (TO) | **QTc > 500 ms** · or shorter QT + exercise paradoxical QT prolongation, polymorphic VT, or long QT in first-degree relative |
| Long QT extras (TO) | FHx positive in **40%** · **Holter** for polymorphic VT |

### Long QT syndrome

**Definition** — prolonged ventricular action potential (QT) → arrhythmia · congenital (ion-channel genes) or acquired (electrolytes, K-channel blockers → ↑ intracellular positive charge)

**Prevalence** — **1 in 2,500–10,000** (Orthobullets) · **1 in 2,500**; long QT in **0.42% of elite athletes** (TO)

**Who** — diagnosed **< 20 yr** · rarer in females (Orthobullets) · **SCD mostly in females < 40 yr** (TO)

| Type | Gene / chromosome | Channel | Share | Key fact |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **LQT1** | **KCNQ1, chr 11** | K⁺ efflux (loss of function) | **45% — commonest** | **Adrenergic surge** → swimming, diving into cold water (TO) |
| **LQT2** | **KCNH2, chr 7** | K⁺ | 25–40% | **Most dangerous — most SCDs (females)** (TO) |
| LQT3 | SCN5A, chr 3 | **Na⁺** | 5–10% | — |

**LQT1–3 (TO)** — **\~95%** of cases; **12 types** (LQTS-1 to 12)

**Acquired drugs** — antiarrhythmics (**sotalol, amiodarone**), **macrolides, fluoroquinolones**, antipsychotics (haloperidol, olanzapine), cisapride (TO)

**Presentation (TO)** — palpitations, dizziness, **unheralded syncope** (polymorphic VT) · brief **myoclonic jerks → misdiagnosed as epilepsy** · most asymptomatic (Orthobullets)

**Torsades de pointes** — polymorphic VT → VF → SCD · 50% asymptomatic · **Romano-Ward syndrome** (abnormal repolarization → torsades, VT, SCD)

## 5 Management

### Return-to-play criteria

| Condition | Return to play |
|---|---|
| **HCM** | **Absolute contraindication to vigorous exercise + competitive sport** · mild–moderate activity allowed |
| **Commotio cordis** | **No restrictions** · precautions against future chest blows |
| **Long QT** | By genotype, presentation, pacemaker need; intermittent ECG; **no exercise restriction** (Orthobullets) · **avoid all sports with adrenergic surges** (TO) — read the options |
| **Syncope** | **Medical evaluation first** |
| Cardiac condition found | **Avoid vigorous exercise + sport** (OB) |

### Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy – treatment

**Lifestyle** — asymptomatic, mild LVH, no obstruction → **observation**, repeat echo if symptoms change, **hydration**, **avoid vigorous exercise**

**Medical** — symptomatic → **beta-blocker (metoprolol) first line** (non-vasodilating) · calcium-channel blockers · **symptom relief only**

**Prognosis** — **1–2% annual mortality**

### Coronary artery disease

**Features** — older patients; risk: hypertension, family history; mostly **asymptomatic** (chest pain, palpitations, syncope)

**Treatment** — lifestyle · β-blocker, aspirin, ACE inhibitor, nitroglycerin, CCB · angioplasty + stenting, CABG

### Commotio cordis – AED & defibrillation

**Treatment** — **immediate cardiac defibrillation** (✗ IV epinephrine, ✗ pericardial needle, ✗ rescue breathing, ✗ fluid bolus) · CPR alone inferior

**Collapsed, apnoeic, pulseless pitcher** — **protect airway, start CPR, prepare to cardiovert** (✗ smelling salts, ✗ shade + reverse Trendelenburg, ✗ seizure precautions, ✗ slow move to spine board)

**Prognosis** — mortality **59%** (Orthobullets); **time to defibrillation predicts survival** ✓

```
Athlete collapses after chest blow
├─ Unresponsive, pulseless → CPR (C-A-B) + AED now
│      └─ VF on AED → immediate defibrillation
└─ Survives → return to sport, no restriction
```

### Long QT syndrome – management (TO)

**All patients** — **beta-blocker** unless contraindicated · avoid sports with **adrenergic surges**

**ICD** — **resuscitated cardiac arrest**, **β-blocker-resistant symptoms**, β-blocker contraindicated · ± asymptomatic high-risk

## X vs Y

| | **Athletic heart syndrome** | **HCM** |
|---|---|---|
| LVH | Physiological — **may mimic HCM** | Pathological, asymmetric septal |
| **LV cavity** | **Maintained** | **Diminished / nondilated** |
| Ventricle | **Left** (✗ right) | Left |
| SCD risk | **No extra risk** | **Most common cause < 35 yr** |
| Clinical | Asymptomatic, normal exam | Exertional symptoms, FHx SCD, systolic murmur |
| ECG | Sinus bradycardia, 1st-degree AV block, nonspecific ST-T | **Lateral T-wave inversion** = pathology; LVH + RA enlargement |

| Pathophysiology in options | Condition |
|---|---|
| Dynamic outflow obstruction by enlarged LV | **HCM** |
| Plaque blocking coronary arteries | CAD (angina / MI, unlikely in the young) |
| **Direct impact during ventricular repolarization** | **Commotio cordis** |
| Lengthening of QT interval | Long QT → VT, torsades |
| AV-node dysfunction | Heart block |
