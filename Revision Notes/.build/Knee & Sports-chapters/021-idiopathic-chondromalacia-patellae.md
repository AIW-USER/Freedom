# Idiopathic Chondromalacia Patellae

*Knee & Sports · 21 / 61*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology & pathophysiology

**Who** — **adolescents + young adults** · **F > M** · PF pain = **most common condition affecting adolescents** (OB) · mainly adolescent females

**Pain generator** — unclear, **multifactorial** · roughened / damaged **undersurface cartilage of patella** · biologic, mechanical + emotional causes (OB)

**Proposed factors** — limb malalignment, muscle weakness, chondral lesions, **maltracking** (narrow contact points → **↑ PF contact pressure → pain**) · **dynamic valgus** = excessive medial movement of limb in weight bearing (OB)

**Pain receptors** — **anterior fat pad + joint capsule = highest potential** · subchondral bone weak

**Miserable malalignment** — **femoral anteversion + genu valgum + external tibial torsion / pronated feet** → ↑ Q-angle · assess tibial + femoral rotation

### Associations & aetiology (TO)

**Associated with chondromalacia (TO)** — **rheumatoid arthritis**, **alkaptonuria**, **recurrent haemarthrosis**

**EXCEPT (TO)** — **ankylosing spondylitis**

## 3 Presentation

**Pain** — **diffuse peripatellar / retropatellar**, insidious, vague · always weigh physical, mental + social elements

**Aggravated by** — **climbing or descending stairs** · **theatre sign** (prolonged sitting, knee bent — long car rides, back seat) · **squatting / kneeling** · rising from a deep seat

**Exam** — **pain on patellar compression** with ROM or **resisted (open-chain) knee extension** · crepitus · quads atrophy · full ROM, **no effusion**, stable ligaments

**Maltracking signs** — ↑ femoral anteversion / tibial ER, lateral subluxation or **loss of medial mobility**, positive apprehension

**Differential** — quadriceps or patellar tendinitis, **saphenous neuroma**, post-operative neuroma

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Study | Finding / use |
|---|---|
| X-ray (AP, lateral, notch ± Merchant) | Often **normal** · chondrosis, shallow sulcus, patella alta / baja, **lateral tilt** |
| **Merchant view** | Knee **45° flexion**, beam **30°** · lateral tilt = possible contributor, **not specific** for PF pain |
| CT | PF alignment, fracture, trochlear geometry, **TT-TG**, limb torsion · TT-TG CT is for **recurrent dislocation** |
| **MRI** | **Best for articular cartilage** · **T2** best · abnormal cartilage = **high signal** · only if intra-articular / intra-osseous lesion suspected |

### Outerbridge MRI classification

| Grade | Cartilage |
|---|---|
| 0 | Normal |
| **I** | Surface intact, heterogeneous, **high signal** |
| **II** | Fissures + fragmentation extending down to articular surface |
| **III** | **Partial-thickness defect**, focal ulceration |
| **IV** | **Exposed subchondral bone** |

## 5 Management

```
Chondromalacia / PF pain
├─ All patients → rest, NSAIDs, quads (VMO) + core + hip PT, ≥ 1 yr
├─ Failed rehab + Outerbridge 2–3 → arthroscopic debridement
└─ Tight lateral retinaculum + lateral tilt → lateral retinacular release
       (severe, failed extensive PT → realignment osteotomy / MPFLR)
```

### Physical therapy – quadriceps, core & hip strengthening

**Mainstay** — rest, rehab, NSAIDs for **minimum 1 year** · **NSAIDs > steroids** · activity modification

**Rehab focus** — **VMO**, **core**, **closed-chain short-arc quads**, **hip external rotators** · low-impact aerobic exercise

**15-yr-old runner, 5 wk, normal X-rays** — **PT focused on quadriceps and core strengthening** (✗ FAI hip series, ✗ CT for TT-TG, ✗ Graston / ASTYM / iontophoresis — chronic tendinopathy, ✗ chest CT + oncology)

**Failed 4 wk closed-chain PT** — **continued PT with addition of hip exercises** (✗ steroid injection, ✗ MRI, ✗ diagnostic arthroscopy, ✗ patellar taping — adjuvant only)

**Exercise that ↑ pain (TO)** — **leg extension** = open chain against resistance in functional range → ↑ contact pressure (✗ leg press, ✗ squat, ✗ lateral step)

**Best quads exercises (TO)** — **closed kinetic chain** (mini-squat, lateral step, high-saddle cycling) in **0–30°** (minimal pressure) · **leg press tolerated** · mini-squat + **hip adduction (ball)** favours VMO

**Least PF stress (TO)** — **CKC restricted to 0–45° + OKC restricted to 90–45°** (✗ OKC full 90–0°, ✗ CKC 60–90° + OKC 30–0°, ✗ deep squats 0–90°)

### Operative options

| Procedure | Indication |
|---|---|
| **Arthroscopic debridement** (mechanical, radiofrequency) | **Outerbridge grade 2–3** chondromalacia |
| **Lateral retinacular release** (open or arthroscopic) | **Tight lateral retinaculum, loose medial capsule, lateral tilt** |
| **Maquet** (anterior tubercle elevation) | Elevate **≤ 1 cm** → else **skin necrosis** |
| **Fulkerson** (anteromedialisation) | ↑ Q-angle, instability (controversial) · ✗ **superior medial arthrosis** (scope first), ✗ skeletal immaturity |
| Elmslie-Trillat, MPFL reconstruction | Severe symptoms after failed extensive PT |

## X vs Y

| | **Closed kinetic chain** | **Open kinetic chain** |
|---|---|---|
| Examples | Leg press, mini-squat, lateral step | **Leg extension** |
| Safe PF arc | **0–45°** (best 0–30°) | **90–45°** |
| PF pain | Tolerated | **↑ pain** (full-range resisted extension) |

## How it's asked

**"Exercise likely to increase PF pain (TO)?"** → **leg extension**

**"PFJ-sparing quads prescription (TO)?"** → **CKC 0–45° + OKC 90–45°**

**"Associated with chondromalacia EXCEPT (TO)?"** → **ankylosing spondylitis**

**"Adolescent, theatre sign, normal X-rays — next?"** → **PT: quadriceps + core strengthening**

**"Failed closed-chain PT, normal sunrise views?"** → **continue PT, add hip exercises**
