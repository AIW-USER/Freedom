# Articular Cartilage Defects of Knee

*Knee & Sports · 5 / 61*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology & pathomechanics

**Incidence** — **5–10% of people > 40 yr** have high-grade chondral lesions · isolated defects in athletes est. **36%** (OB)

**Chronic ACL tear** — **anterior lateral femoral condyle + posterolateral tibial plateau**

**OCD** — **70%** on **posterolateral (lateral aspect of) medial femoral condyle**

**Mechanism** — acute trauma or chronic repetitive overload → softening, fissuring, flap tears, delamination

**Threshold** — impaction **> 24 MPa** disrupts normal cartilage

**Healing** — **limited spontaneous healing + propensity to worsen**; defects isolated from blood supply / marrow → **no repair response** (basis of microfracture)

**Overloaded cartilage (runner)** — chondropenia, chondrocyte apoptosis, subchondral bruising, sclerosis, fracture, necrosis; cartilage volume ↑ linearly with loading until a threshold

### Genes & lab markers (collagen type of each repair tissue)

| Tissue / procedure | Collagen | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Hyaline (articular) cartilage** | **Type II** | Columnar organisation + **lamina splendens** (separates it from "hyaline-like") |
| **Microfracture / drilling → fibrocartilage** | **Type I > II** (mostly I + III, some II) | Stiffer, less durable → deteriorates over time |
| Microfracture (TO) | **Types I, II and III** | Fibrocartilage alone → mix of fibrocartilage + hyaline-like |
| **ACI / MACI** | **I + II, type II predominates** | "Hyaline-like"; ACI (TO) may give hyaline-like, hybrid or fibrocartilage (I + II) |
| **OATS / mosaicplasty, OCA** | **Type II** | Direct transfer of articular cartilage; chondrocytes viable |
| Particulated juvenile cartilage allograft | Type II | Live cells in native ECM |
| Fibrous tissue | No type II | Made by fibrocytes |
| Elastic cartilage | — | Ear, epiglottis, larynx |

**Labs** — only to rule out inflammatory disease

## 3 Presentation

**History** — precipitating trauma common · **some found incidentally** on MRI / arthroscopy

**Symptoms** — asymptomatic vs localised pain, effusion, motion loss, **mechanical symptoms** (catching, locking) — esp. unstable / loose lesions

**Exam** — look for background factors: **laxity, malalignment, compartment overload**; ROM, ligaments, gait

### Outerbridge arthroscopic grading

| Grade | Orthobullets | TO version |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Normal | — |
| **I** | **Softening + swelling** (probe) | Softening + swelling |
| **II** | **Partial-thickness**, surface fissures, **not to subchondral bone, ≤ 1.5 cm diameter** | Fragmentation + fissuring, area **< 0.5 inch** |

### Chondromalacia patellae — diagnosis & grading

**Gold standard (TO)** — **arthroscopy** (✗ X-ray, ✗ MRI, ✗ CT)

| Modified Outerbridge — MRI (TO) | FS PD finding |
|---|---|
| I | Focal hyperintensity, **normal contour** |

## 5 Management

### Decision factors & size thresholds

**Nonop first** — rest, NSAIDs, physio, weight loss when **mild**; viscosupplement, steroid, unloader brace — **controversial**, symptom relief, defect won't heal

**Treatment spectrum** (Alford) — **marrow stimulation (MST)** → **ACI** → **osteochondral grafting (OCG**, autograft / allograft)

**Patient factors** — age, skeletal maturity, demand, rehab tolerance · **defect factors** — size, location, **contained vs uncontained**, **subchondral bone involvement**

**First correct** — **malalignment, ligament instability, meniscal deficiency** (before / at time of cartilage surgery); patellofemoral → **maltracking + malalignment**

```
Femoral condyle defect (after correcting axis, ligaments, meniscus)
├─ < 4 cm² → MICROFRACTURE or OATS (palliative if older / low demand)
└─ > 4 cm² → OSTEOCHONDRAL ALLOGRAFT or ACI
```

```
Patellofemoral defect (after correcting maltracking)
├─ < 4 cm² → MICROFRACTURE or OATS
└─ > 4 cm² → ACI (microfracture if older / low demand)
```

| Size / bone status (OB algorithm) | Procedure |
|---|---|
| **< 1 cm², no osseous defect** | **Microfracture** |
| < 1 cm², osseous defect | OATS |
| **1–2 cm², no osseous defect** | Microfracture or ACI |
| 1–2 cm², osseous defect | OATS |
| **> 2 cm², osteochondral defect or AVN** | **OCA** or ACI + bone graft |

| Procedure | Size range quoted (source) |
|---|---|
| **Microfracture** | **< 2 cm²** / < 2 × 2 cm (Orthobullets, OB, TO) · < 1 cm² (OB patella, OB algorithm) · with OATS < 4 cm² (Orthobullets) |
| **OATS** | < 2 cm², some 2–4 cm² (OB) · up to 4–6 cm² (OB, OCD chapter) · grafting **up to 5 cm** (TO) |
| **ACI** | **1–10 cm²** (OB) · **2–10 cm²** large grade IV (TO) · > 4 cm² (Orthobullets) |
| **OCA** | **> 2 cm²** (OB) · > 4 cm² (Orthobullets, OB) · > 6 cm² (OB, OCD chapter) |
| Threshold debate | **4 cm²** usual; some use **2 cm²** (OB) — read the options |

### Operative criteria

| Indication | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Failed nonop** | Symptomatic defect |
| **Acute osteochondral fracture** | **Full-thickness cartilage loss** |
| Size (OB) | Defects **> 2 × 2 cm** failing nonop |
| **Arthroplasty instead** | **> 55 yr** (OB) · PF arthroplasty **40+ yr** with isolated PF arthritis |

### Debridement / chondroplasty

**Debridement / chondroplasty** — removes loose flaps → relieves mechanical symptoms · **short-term benefit 50–70%** · simple, fast rehab · leaves exposed bone; natural history unknown

**Chondroplasty for large PF defect** — only **older patients with diffuse degeneration** (✗ young)

### Microfracture & marrow stimulation — fibrocartilage, outcomes & overgrowth

**Principle** — perforate subchondral bone → **marrow MSCs** into defect → **fibrocartilage** (✗ true hyaline regeneration) · includes **microfracture, abrasion chondroplasty, drilling**

**Technique** — **stable vertical walls**; **remove calcified cartilage layer** (✓ should be done); awl holes **3–4 mm apart**; entry **perpendicular (90°)** to avoid skipping / hole collapse

**Osseous overgrowth** — risk ↑ with **removal / violation of subchondral plate** (over-aggressive debridement) (✗ more holes per area, ✗ small awls, ✗ 90° entry, ✗ calcified layer removal)

**Awl size** — smaller (**1.0 mm**) better than **1.2 mm** in some studies

**Drilling steps** — debride **sclerotic bone** → trim unstable cartilage edges → drill **past subchondral plate (\~4 mm)** → enough coverage between holes for a **mesenchymal clot**

**Drilling** — alters subchondral plate → micro-cysts + intralesional osteophytes (later fill with cancellous bone)

**Benefits** — cheap, **single-stage**, arthroscopic; **best for acute, contained lesions < 2 × 2 cm**

**Limits** — poor if **> 2 × 2 cm**; ✗ bone defects; ✗ **uncontained** lesions (no rim); **restricted WB 6–8 wk** (Orthobullets) · **\~6 wk** (OB) · NWB 4–6 wk + CPM (Orthobullets OCD note)

**Good short-term outcome** — **high MRI fill rate**, **low BMI**, **short preop symptoms** · **patellar microfracture → poor**

**Durability** — gains maintained only **2–3 yr**; not maintained long term

### ACI / MACI — hyaline-like repair & patellofemoral indications

**ACI** — cell therapy → autologous **"hyaline-like"** cartilage · arthroscopic harvest from low-load area → culture → inject under **periosteal patch** at 2nd (open) surgery

**Needs** — **full-thickness cartilage margins**, healthy subchondral bone (✗ significant osseous defect: **< 8 mm deep** OK (OB) · add bone graft if bone loss **> 1 cm** (OB, OCD chapter))

**Limits** — open, **2-stage**, prolonged protection · better histology than marrow stimulation; long-term ≈ microfracture

**Periosteal patch (TO)** — harvested **proximal tibia / distal femur**, slightly oversized · **cambium layer faces the defect** (down to bone) · sewn **watertight** with interrupted **6-0 absorbable** sutures + **fibrin glue** at margins · patch is **chondrogenic** + paracrine (✗ cambium away, ✗ sewn into defect, ✗ mixed with matrix)

**1st-stage harvest (TO)** — **200–300 mg** cartilage · cells expanded **2–3 wk** (TO) · implanted after **3–6 wk** (TO) — read the options

**Collagen membrane (TO)** — replaced periosteum → avoids **patch hypertrophy + harvest morbidity**

| ACI generation (TO) | Features |
|---|---|
| **1st** (Brittberg 1994) | **2-stage**: arthroscopic biopsy → open / mini-open implantation under **periosteal flap (PACI)** or collagen membrane |
| **2nd = MACI** | **Autologous chondrocytes on a scaffold for expansion**; 3D bioabsorbable scaffold press-fit (hyalograft C, bioseed-C) → **all-arthroscopic** implantation possible; NeoCart (bioreactor loading) |
| **3rd** | **Single stage**; allogeneic tissue or **stem cells**; CAIS (minced autologous chondrocytes + marrow MNCs on polycaprolactone + PGA + PDS scaffold); De Novo ET (minced juvenile allograft); CARTISTEM (umbilical cord MSCs, hyaluronate gel) |

**3rd vs 1st generation (TO)** — **all-arthroscopic, single-stage** (✗ 2-stage, ✗ collagen cover, ✗ periosteal sheath)

**Generation conflicts** — **MACI = 2nd generation** (TO) vs **3rd generation, first to use scaffold** (OB) · **CAIS = 2nd-gen single-stage** (one TO) vs **3rd-gen classic** (other TO) — read the options

**MACI** — cells cultured on **porcine collagen membrane** · fixed with **fibrin glue or sutures** · **only FDA-approved cell therapy for cartilage (USA)** · 2-stage, expensive · **type II > I**

**5 cm² MFC defect, 32 yr (TO)** — **ACI** (✗ microfracture, ✗ UKA, ✗ mosaicplasty)

**ACI in PF joint** — **JSN on Merchant view = contraindication** · acceptable: grade 4 MFC / trochlea lesion, varus axis (corrected), concomitant **Fulkerson AMZ** · **ACI not FDA-approved for patella** (off-label)

**PF defect + recurrent dislocation, TT-TG 26 mm, 2.2 × 2.1 cm, exposed bone** — **MPFL reconstruction + autologous chondrocyte implantation + antero-medializing (Fulkerson) TTO** (✗ lateralising TTO — ↑ Q angle; ✗ microfracture — < 1 cm² only; ✗ PF arthroplasty — 40+ yr)

**PF lesions** — inferior outcomes vs condyles (**high shear**) · **TT-TG > 20 mm** = maltracking → AMZ protects the repair · recurrent dislocation may avulse the **MPFL patellar insertion** (osseous fragment)

### Osteochondral allograft — indications, contraindications & failure risk

**Goal** — **live chondrocytes in mature matrix + underlying bone** · **fresh, refrigerated** grafts (✗ freeze-dried) · bulk (screws) or shell / dowel press-fit · donor = **cadaver hemicondyle** · size + **radius of curvature** matched

**Indications** — **large defects (> 2 cm² / > 4 cm²)**, **subchondral bone loss / oedema / AVN**, **uncontained** lesions, **revision** of failed techniques · **single-stage**

**OCA over ACI** — **depth of osseous involvement** (✗ footprint > 6 cm², ✗ location, ✗ age, ✗ time since injury)

**Contraindications** — **ligament instability, meniscal insufficiency, uncorrectable malalignment, multicompartment / tricompartmental arthrosis, inflammatory arthropathy** · ✓ defect > 2 cm² is an **indication**

**Failure risk** — **patellofemoral joint defect** (adverse biomechanics) · **bipolar** lesions (✗ unipolar, ✗ male sex, ✗ Outerbridge IV, ✗ concomitant AMZ)

**Outcomes** — PF worse than condyles (contact pressure) · **> 80% return** to activity in young · limits: **availability, cost**, infection / immune risk (✗ more easily available)

**Immune response** — only a concern with **allograft** (✗ OATS) · allografts **cleansed in antibiotics** before implantation

**Varus axis + large MFC lesion** — correct alignment **before / at time** of OCA (✗ isolated OCA)

**MFC 25 × 25 mm, 32 yr** — **osteochondral allograft** (ACI also correct if listed) (✗ 1–2 autograft plugs, ✗ microfracture, ✗ UKA, ✗ abrasion)

**MFC 1.7 × 1.3 cm (2.21 cm²) + loose bodies, neutral axis** — **removal of loose osteochondral bodies + osteochondral allograft transplantation to the MFC** (✗ osteotomy, ✗ microfracture — osseous defect, ✗ OATS alone — loose bodies stay) · loose bodies sit in **suprapatellar pouch**; hip-to-ankle film neutral, sunrise view normal

### Osteochondral autograft (OATS) — graft fixation & donor site

**Principle** — cylinders of normal cartilage + bone from **low-load areas** (periphery of trochlea, notch) press-fit into recipient socket · chondrocytes **viable**, cartilage **heals**, biopsy **type II** (✗ scaffold, ✗ replaced by fibrocartilage)

**Benefits** — autologous, cheap, **single-stage**, arthroscopic possible · **limits** — size + **donor-site morbidity**, curvature matching

**Fixation strength** — **initially decreases in early healing, then increases with subchondral bone healing** → **delay WB 3 months**; early non-WB motion

**Donor site** — **distal medial trochlea** = **lowest PF contact pressure + no tibiofemoral contact** · also **peripheral / superior lateral trochlea** · grafts **< 10 mm wide** (✗ central trochlea, ✗ condyles — weight-bearing)

**Ahmad map (figure)** — 1 proximal medial trochlea · 2 medial condyle · 3 central trochlea · **4 distal medial trochlea ✓** · 5 lateral condyle

![OATS donor sites — distal medial trochlea has the lowest contact pressure](../MCQ/attachments/q216272-illa-femur.jpg)

### Malalignment & unloading procedures (osteotomy, tibial tubercle transfer)

| Procedure | Indication | Contraindication / risk |
|---|---|---|
| **Maquet** (anteriorisation) | **Distal pole** lesions only | **Superior patellar arthrosis**; elevate **≤ 1 cm** → else **skin necrosis** |
| **Fulkerson AMZ** (anterior + medial) | **Lateral + distal pole** lesions, ↑ Q angle, offload PF restoration, **bipolar PF lesions** | **Superior-medial patellar arthrosis**, **medial facet + MFC arthrosis**, skeletal immaturity |
| **Distal femoral varus osteotomy** | **Valgus** + lateral compartment chondral disease | — |
| HTO (medial opening wedge) | Varus-pattern disease | Not if axis neutral |

**Scope before** tubercle transfer — need **intact proximal + medial cartilage**

**Anteriorised 2.5 cm** — **skin necrosis** / wound breakdown (✗ instability, ✗ nonunion, ✗ progression, ✗ fracture)

**Lateral FC + plateau chondromalacia, 10° valgus, 31 yr** — **distal femoral varus osteotomy** (✗ OAT, ✗ UKA, ✗ HTO, ✗ Fulkerson) · **5° valgus → 5-fold risk of progressing ≥ 1 grade in 18 months** (Sharma)

**PF joint load** — up to **6.5 × body weight** in flexion

### Fixation of unstable fragments & instability-related lesions

**Fixation** — needs fragment with **adequate subchondral bone** · debride base, ± drill / local graft · absorbable or nonabsorbable screws · **best: unstable OCD, open physes** · lower healing when mature · **headless nonabsorbable screws removed at 3–6 months**

**Acute patellar dislocation + osteochondral fragment + MPFL tear** — **fragment fixation (ORIF) + MPFL reconstruction** (best: **14-year-old female athlete, first-time dislocation, imaging shown in Figure D**) · sites: **patella + LFC** · chronic, no fragment → OATS (✗ fixation without OC defect, ✗ partial-thickness chronic defect, ✗ bucket-handle tear, ✗ Outerbridge IV OA)

**Unfixable unstable MFC flap 1 × 1.5 cm, single procedure → type I + II** — **microfracture**

### Osteochondritis dissecans & instability-related fragments

**Commonest site (TO)** — **knee: lateral surface of medial femoral condyle** (classic, posterolateral MFC) (✗ LFC) · others: **talus anteromedial corner**, **femoral head superomedial**, **capitellum**, **1st MT head**

**Who (TO)** — **males**, adolescents · **commonest cause of loose body** in knee in adolescents / young adults

**Catchers (OB)** — **posterior femoral condyle** (hyperflexion loading) · ✗ anterior LFC / posterolateral plateau (ACL injury pattern), ✗ patella, ✗ trochlea

**Wilson test (TO)** — seated, legs hanging · **pain on internal rotation**, relieved by external rotation

**Imaging (TO)** — X-ray: loose body / loosely attached fragment · **MRI = investigation of choice**, earliest diagnosis, guides management · **MRI = best to follow healing** (✗ CT — insensitive, ✗ bone scan — can't separate healed vs unhealed; high Tc uptake → repair potential, ✗ SPECT)

**"Healing" MRI criteria (TO17118770)** — (1) round homogeneous high signal **≥ 5 mm** beneath lesion · (2) articular focal defect **≥ 5 mm** wide · (3) high-signal line through cartilage + subchondral plate into lesion — another TO lists the same ≥ 5 mm signs as **De Smet instability criteria** — read the options

**JOCD (TO)** — **activity modification** first (✗ in situ fixation, ✗ microfracture, ✗ excision) · most heal · small lesions → **multiple drilling** · large → **mosaicplasty / OATS**

| ROCK / De Smet (TO) | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Nonop protocol** | Stop impact sport, protected WB, unloader brace or cast · **≥ 3–6 months**; healing on 6-month MRI → extend to **12–18 months** |
| **Strongest success predictor** | **Younger age / open physes** (boys **< 14**, girls **< 12**) |
| Other success predictors | Small **normalised lesion width**; **no cysts** (< 1.3 mm may still heal at 12 months); extreme medial / lateral (coronal) or far posterior (sagittal) site |
| **ROCK nomogram** | Age + normalised width + cyst size → **> 48% healing probability at 6 months** → continue nonop |
| **Strongest failure predictor** | **Subchondral cysts > 5 mm on MRI** (fluid intrusion = instability) (✗ sclerotic rim — common, chronicity) |
| **De Smet instability** | **T2 fluid line ≥ 5 mm** behind fragment · **cysts ≥ 5 mm** · **focal cartilage defect ≥ 5 mm** · fluid through cartilage into bone |
| Other failure signs | Closing physes, **mechanical symptoms**, **patella / LFC** site, no progress after 3–6 months |
| Surgery options | Trans- / retroarticular **drilling** (intact cartilage), **internal fixation** (unstable), MACI / OCA (unsalvageable) |

**Stable lesion (TO)** — no fluid behind fragment, **firm on probing** → failed conservative → **drilling alone**

**Unstable but salvageable, immature (TO)** — circumferential T2 fluid, **ballotable**, minimally displaced → **in situ fixation of the fragment using bioabsorbable screws, darts or compression screws** ± graft (✗ drilling alone, ✗ excision + microfracture, ✗ OATS)

## 6 Complications

| Complication | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Osseous overgrowth** (microfracture) | **Subchondral plate removal** → ↓ repair cartilage volume |
| Microfracture deterioration | Type I fibrocartilage; gains last **2–3 yr** |
| **OCA dropped on floor** | **40% contamination**, **Staphylococcus** commonest → **5-min povidone-iodine bath + saline rinse** (max cell viability, ↓ bacteria) |
| OCA | Infection / immune reaction risk |
| **Maquet > 1 cm** | **Skin necrosis** |
| OATS | **Donor-site morbidity** |
| ACI periosteal patch | **Hypertrophy** + harvest morbidity |
| Retained headless screws | Remove at **3–6 months** |

| | **Microfracture** | **OATS** | **OCA** | **ACI / MACI** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Stages | **1** | **1** | **1** | **2** (3rd-gen ACI: 1) |
