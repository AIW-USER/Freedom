# Rectus Femoris Strain

*Knee & Sports · 53 / 61*

## 1 Basics

**Who** — **soccer + football** players

**Anatomy** — rectus femoris **crosses hip + knee** → **flexes hip, extends knee** · origin **AIIS (direct head)** → one of four quadriceps → **patellar tendon**

**Site by chronicity** — **acute → distal thigh** · **chronic → near origin**

### Rectus femoris origin avulsion (AIIS)

**Adolescents** — proximal **bony avulsion of AIIS** at the **direct head** of rectus femoris (see AIIS avulsion chapter)

**Track athlete, AIIS fragment above acetabulum** — **rectus femoris** (✗ TFL, ✗ gluteus medius, ✗ gluteus minimus, ✗ biceps femoris)

![AIIS avulsion in an adolescent (arrow)](../Orthobullets/attachments/ob3104-aiis.jpg)

## 2 Mechanism

**Mechanism** — **sudden forceful eccentric contraction** · **sprinting from standing**, **kicking a ball hard**

## 3 Presentation

| Pain site | Lesion |
|---|---|
| **Anterior hip** | Strain or **avulsion at AIIS** |
| **Mid-thigh** | Strain in **muscle fibres** (belly) |
| **Distal (near knee)** | **Most common finding** |

**Exam** — tenderness over the injured zone · **palpable defect = full tear (unusual)**

**Provocative test** — **pain on resisted hip flexion or extension** (Orthobullets) · AIIS avulsion → **most pain on resisted knee extension** (OB) — read the options

**11-yr-old hurdler, AIIS avulsion — most painful test (OB)** — **resisted knee extension** (rectus contraction greatest) (✗ resisted adduction — adductors arise from **symphysis pubis**, ✗ resisted abduction — **outer iliac crest**, ✗ resisted hip extension — **posterior iliac crest**)

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Study | Finding / use |
|---|---|
| **X-ray** | **Adolescents with suspected AIIS avulsion** · otherwise **normal in most** |
| **MRI** | Strain extent · pseudotumour vs neoplasm |
| Ultrasound | Strain |

![Rectus femoris strain on MRI (arrow)](../Orthobullets/attachments/ob3104-1892024.jpg)

### Mid-substance strain / pseudotumour

**14-yr-old, 3 wk, firm tender mid-rectus mass after soccer** — **midsubstance partial rupture = "pseudotumour"**

**MRI = injury, not tumour** — **no mass effect on T1** · **defined fluid signal on T2** · **no significant gadolinium enhancement**

## 5 Management

```
Rectus femoris strain
├─ Vast majority → NSAIDs, rest, ice, stretching / strengthening
│      (resolves in 4–6 weeks)
└─ Midsubstance pseudotumour → NSAIDs + PT + repeat MRI at 6–8 weeks
       └─ Not resolved at 3–6 months → late debridement + repair
```

**Nonop** — **definitive treatment for the vast majority** · **resolves within 4–6 wk**

**Pseudotumour** — **nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, physical therapy + repeat MRI scan in 6–8 weeks** to confirm resolution (✗ I&D of abscess, ✗ open biopsy, ✗ haematoma evacuation + musculotendinous repair, ✗ wide resection + radiotherapy)

## X vs Y

| | **Acute strain** | **Chronic strain** | **Adolescent AIIS avulsion** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Site | **Distal thigh** | **Near origin** | **AIIS (direct head)** |
| Imaging | MRI / US | MRI / US | **X-ray** |

## How it's asked

**"Teen track athlete, AIIS avulsion — muscle?"** → **rectus femoris**

**"Hurdler, AIIS avulsion — most painful test?"** → **resisted knee extension**

**"Firm mid-rectus mass, T2 fluid, no enhancement — management?"** → **NSAIDs, PT, repeat MRI in 6–8 weeks**

**"Most common pain site in rectus strain?"** → **distal**
