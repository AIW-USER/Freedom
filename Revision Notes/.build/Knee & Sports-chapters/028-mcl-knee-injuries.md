# MCL Knee Injuries

*Knee & Sports · 28 / 61*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology – most commonly injured knee ligament

**Most common (TO)** — **MCL = most commonly injured knee ligament** · **ACL = most common needing surgery** (✗ ACL, ✗ MPFL, ✗ ATFL)

**Incidence** — **40%** of knee ligament injuries (under-reported) · **8%** of athletic knee injuries · **males** > females · skiing, rugby, football, soccer, ice hockey

**Associated ACL tear** — **most common** associated injury · **\~95%** of non-isolated MCL injuries · **ACL + MCL = most common multiligament injury** · hemarthrosis suggests it · ↑ with MCL grade (III > II > I)

**Meniscus** — **medial > lateral** · up to **5%** of isolated MCL injuries

### Anatomy

| | **Superficial MCL (sMCL)** | **Deep MCL (dMCL)** |
|---|---|---|
| Layer | **II** (with PMC ligaments, MPFL) | **III** (with capsule) |
| Femur | Medial epicondyle · **3.2 mm proximal + 4.8 mm posterior** to ME · **1 cm anterior + distal to adductor tubercle** | Meniscofemoral ligament |
| Tibia | Proximal **11.2 mm** below joint line (soft tissue) · distal **6 cm below joint line**, on bone, **deep + posterior to pes** | Meniscotibial ligament |
| Function | **Primary valgus stabiliser at all angles** · max at **25° (78%)** · 2° to ER + AP translation | **2° valgus stabiliser**, max in **full extension** |

**Blood supply** — superior + inferior medial geniculate arteries

**Other medial stabilisers** — **POL** (resists IR in full extension; 2° valgus; primary rotational restraint) · oblique popliteal ligament · posterior capsule · dynamic **semimembranosus complex** (5 attachments) · vastus medialis, medial retinaculum, pes (sartorius, semitendinosus, gracilis)

### Anatomic sMCL reconstruction landmarks

**Graft footprints (OB)** — femur **3.2 mm proximal + 4.8 mm posterior to medial epicondyle** (between AT and ME) → tibia **6 cm distal to joint line** · location **B : 3** on figure

**Distractors** — tibial point 1 = **proximal** sMCL attachment (✗ distal) · point 2 = **pes anserinus**

**Wijdicks** — anatomic augmented repair ≈ anatomic reconstruction (18 matched pairs); neither restores native kinematics · **LaPrade** — sMCL + POL anatomic reconstruction (28 pts) → ↓ valgus gapping, ↑ scores

## 2 Mechanism

### Mechanism of valgus injury

**Commonest** — **valgus stress**, knee in slight **flexion + external rotation**

**Contact (more common)** — **direct blow to lateral knee** → **high-grade / complete** · rupture usually at **femoral insertion**

**Non-contact** — commoner in **skiing** · pivot / cut with valgus + ER → **low-grade**

| MCL mechanism (TO) | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Valgus direct blow to outer knee** | **Most common** |
| Non-contact valgus + twist (foot planted) | Basketball, skiing, tennis · often **with ACL** |
| Hyperextension + valgus | Less common · gymnastics, skiing, football |
| Fall with knee forced into valgus | Catching ski edge, falling sideways |

**Level of the blow (TO)** — hit on lateral **distal leg**, knee slightly flexed, foot planted → **forced flexion, valgus → ACL tear** · force higher (thigh / knee), knee nearer extension → **MCL tear** (✗ forced valgus MCL, ✗ varus LCL, ✗ valgus ACL without flexion)

| ACL (TO) | Fact |
|---|---|
| Contact vs non-contact | **\~30% vs \~70%** |
| Classic | **Forced flexion + valgus, foot planted**, femoral adduction, tibial ER, ankle eversion (pivot / cut) |
| Landing | Stiff (extended) or valgus collapse · volleyball, basketball, gymnastics |
| Deceleration | Sudden stop · soccer, skiing, football |
| Hyperextension | Tibia moves forward |
| Risk factors | Poor core stability · **stiff landing (knee flexion < 20°; source prints "<200")** · weak hip abduction · ↑ knee valgus · **heel-strike landing** |

| PCL (TO) | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Dashboard** | Posterior force on proximal tibia, knee flexed |
| Hyperflexion | Foot plantarflexed, fall on bent knee |
| Hyperextension | Often **ACL + PCL** |
| Rotational + varus / valgus | Rugby, football, wrestling |
| Fall on knee, foot plantarflexed | Wrestling, ice hockey, American football |

## 3 Presentation

**History / symptoms** — **"pop"** · medial joint line pain · difficulty walking

**Exam** — medial tenderness, ecchymosis, effusion · **saphenous nerve** exam · check ACL, PCL, patellar dislocation, medial meniscus

| Valgus stress | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **30° flexion** | **Isolates sMCL** · gap vs other knee = grade |
| **0° (full extension)** | **Posteromedial capsule or cruciate** injury |

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Study | Finding / use |
|---|---|
| AP + lateral | **Usually normal** |
| **Stress X-ray (skeletally immature)** | Gapping through **physeal fracture** |
| X-ray (chronic) | **Pellegrini-Stieda** calcification at medial femoral insertion |
| **MRI** | **Modality of choice** · location, extent, other injuries |

### Pellegrini-Stieda lesion

**Definition (TO)** — **ossification of MCL at / near proximal (femoral) insertion on MFC** (✗ fibular LCL, ✗ tibial sMCL, ✗ tibial dMCL) · **chronic** MCL tear / deficiency

**Pellegrini-Stieda disease / syndrome (TO)** — lesion **+ medial knee pain or restricted ROM**

![Pellegrini-Stieda calcification at the medial femoral MCL origin](../Orthobullets/attachments/ob3010-pellegrini-stieda.jpg)

| Look-alike (TO) | Site | Association |
|---|---|---|
| **Pellegrini-Stieda** | **Medial femoral condyle** (MCL origin) | **Chronic MCL** |
| **Segond fracture** | **Lateral proximal tibia** avulsion (= **ALL**) | **ACL** |
| **Reverse Segond** | **Medial tibial plateau** cortical avulsion | **PCL** mainly · MCL, MM, probably ACL |
| **Stieda process** | Elongated **lateral tubercle of posterior process of talus** | Anatomical variant |

### Classification

| AMA (1966, laxity alone) | Opening |
|---|---|
| Grade I | **3–5 mm** |
| Grade II | **6–10 mm** |
| Grade III | **> 10 mm** |

| Hughston modification | Endpoint | Injury |
|---|---|---|
| **I (mild)** | **Firm, no laxity** | Stretch / few fibres |
| **II (moderate)** | **Firm ± mild laxity** | Partial tear |
| **III (severe)** | **No endpoint** | Complete · **1+ 3–5 mm, 2+ 6–10 mm, 3+ > 10 mm** |

**Gap at 30° → grade** — **1–4 mm I · 5–9 mm II · ≥ 10 mm III** (Orthobullets exam) · AMA 3–5 / 6–10 / > 10 mm · **0–5 / 6–10 / 11–15 mm** (Orthobullets knee exam topic) — read the options

## 5 Management

```
MCL injury
├─ Grade I → NSAIDs, rest, therapy (RTP 5–7 days)
├─ Grade II → hinged brace + therapy (RTP 2–4 wk)
├─ Grade III, stable in extension, no cruciate → brace (RTP 4–8 wk)
└─ Grade III + multiligament / Stener / entrapment → acute repair
       └─ Chronic or poor tissue → reconstruction
```

**Therapy** — immediate quad sets, SLR, hip adduction above knee · cycling + progressive resistance

### Natural history & healing by tear location (nonoperative grade III)

**Proximal (femoral) tears** — **heal most reliably** (vascularity) · ↓ residual valgus laxity · more **calcification** (temporary pain + stiffness)

**Distal (tibial) tears** — heal worse, residual valgus laxity · **Stener-type lesion** = torn MCL **flipped over pes anserinus insertion** → can't heal

**Shelbourne** — grade III at **femoral origin / midsubstance → nonop** · **tibial origin → primary repair**

**Combined injuries** — heal less reliably than isolated

**Grade III + ACL tear** — **ACL does NOT mandate MCL repair** (EXCEPT answer) · grade III = > 10 mm valgus opening, can form Stener lesion

![Distal grade III MCL rupture, coronal MRI](../MCQ/attachments/q179-illa-knee-mcl-mri-cor.jpg)

| ACL reconstruction delay by MCL grade | OB (MCL topic) | TO (Knee Dislocation, Trauma) |
|---|---|---|
| Grade I | **3–4 wk** | **2 wk**, no motion restriction |
| Grade II | **4–6 wk** | **within 2 wk**; brace locked 30° × 7 days |
| Grade III | **6–8 wk** | **3 wk**; brace locked 30° × 10–14 days |

**Delay conflict** — OB 3–4 / 4–6 / 6–8 wk vs TO 2 / ≤ 2 / 3 wk — read the options

### Operative criteria

| Option | Indication |
|---|---|
| **Acute repair, grade III** | **Multiligament** injury · **displaced distal avulsion (Stener-type)** · **entrapment of torn end in medial compartment** |
| **Subacute repair, grade III** | Instability despite nonop · **> 10 mm medial opening in full extension** |
| **Reconstruction** | **Chronic** injury · **inadequate tissue** |
| All surgical candidates | **Diagnostic arthroscopy** first |

**Repair technique** — avulsions → **suture anchors at 30° flexion** · interstitial → anterior advancement to origins · **internal brace** · medial approach

**Reconstruction graft** — semitendinosus autograft / hamstring, tibialis anterior or Achilles allograft

### Prophylactic knee bracing

**Hinged prophylactic brace** — **trend to ↓ MCL tears only** (✗ ACL, ✗ ankle — may ↑ ankle stress) · functional bracing may help **interior linemen**

**Evidence** — Albright: non-significant trend · **West Point RCT: 3.4 vs 1.5 / 1000 exposures** (unbraced vs braced), MCL in defensive players · Pietrosimone SR: inconsistent → can't advocate or discourage

## 6 Complications

**Loss of motion** · **saphenous nerve** injury · **laxity** (distal injuries)
