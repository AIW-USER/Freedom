# Hamstring Injuries

*Knee & Sports · 17 / 61*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Incidence** — **30% of new lower-extremity injuries in athletes** · ↑ 4% / yr in soccer over \~15 yr

**Sports** — rapid acceleration: **soccer, track and field, football** · sprinters, gymnasts (TO)

**Most common muscle injury in athletes (TO)** — **biceps femoris** (✗ adductor longus, ✗ gastrocnemius, ✗ rectus femoris)

**Two-joint muscles (TO)** — exposed to acceleration / deceleration forces → **hamstrings, quadriceps, gastrocnemius** most often injured

**Which hamstring (TO)** — **biceps femoris \~83%** > semimembranosus \~12% > semitendinosus \~5%

**Site — adult** — **myotendinous junction (MTJ)** = most common rupture site, sprinting

**Site — skeletally immature** — **ischial tuberosity avulsion** · also **water skiers** · **10% of pelvic avulsion fractures** in the skeletally immature (Orthobullets) · ischial tuberosity **\~50%** of pelvic apophyseal avulsions (OB, Rossi — see AIIS / ASIS chapters) — read the options

### Risk factors

**Previous hamstring injury** — **×6 reinjury risk** (weak scar) → **greatest predictor** of proximal avulsion

**Strength** — **hamstring : quadriceps ratio < 0.6** · side-to-side difference **> 10–15%**

**Other** — inadequate warm-up · **↓ hip extension** (Orthobullets) · ↓ baseline hip flexion (OB) · leg-length difference (shorter leg = tighter hamstrings) · ↑ age, high training demand, ↑ BMI, **tight hip flexors** (OB)

### Anatomy

**4 muscles** — **semimembranosus, semitendinosus, biceps femoris long head + short head** · **gracilis is NOT a hamstring** (pubic origin) (OB) · TO text lists "semitendinosus and gracilis medially, biceps femoris laterally" — read the options

**Origin** — **all from ischial tuberosity except short head** (**linea aspera**)

**Footprint** — **conjoint tendon (ST + BF long head) medial** (inferomedial / posteromedial), **BF more proximal than ST** · **SM = separate, most lateral** (superolateral / anterolateral), slightly proximal · **SM closest to sciatic nerve**

![Ischial tuberosity footprint: BF long head proximal-medial, ST distal-medial, SM lateral](../Orthobullets/attachments/ob3102-tuberosity-hamstring-insertion.jpg)

| Muscle | Insertion | Nerve |
|---|---|---|
| Semimembranosus | Posterior **medial tibial condyle** | **Tibial** division of sciatic |
| Semitendinosus | Superomedial tibia — **pes anserine** | **Tibial** division |
| BF long head | **Fibular head** | **Tibial** division |
| **BF short head** | Fibular head, BF long head, lateral capsule | **Common peroneal** division |

**Blood supply** — **inferior gluteal + profunda femoris** arteries

**Distances** — origin **\~6 cm proximal** to inferior border of gluteus maximus · **sciatic nerve 1.2 cm lateral** to origin · **pudendal nerve 2–3 cm superomedial**

**Biomechanics** — cross **hip + knee** (except short head — knee only) · **BF long head + SM** most active in **hip extension** · **ST + BF short head** most active in **knee flexion**

### Muscle regeneration — satellite cells

**Satellite cells** — dormant in muscle until injury → **regenerate skeletal muscle** + new satellite cells (✗ osteoblast signalling, ✗ periosteum, ✗ chemotherapy binding, ✗ **sonic hedgehog = limb bud**)

## 2 Mechanism

### Mechanism & origin anatomy

**Hip flexion + knee extension** — **eccentric** contraction at **end of swing phase** (maximal fibre elongation) → **biceps femoris** (✗ sartorius, ✗ rectus femoris, ✗ adductor magnus, ✗ TFL)

**Intramuscular / MTJ injury** — **sudden take-off phase of running** · acute injuries usually **BF**

**Chronic repetitive stretch** — hip flexion + knee extension → **proximal semimembranosus** (OB)

**Older patients** — same hyperflexion in **slips and falls**, water-skiing

**Adolescent hurdler** — ischial tuberosity avulsion = **"hurdler's fracture"** · same mechanism in adult → **MTJ rupture** (✗ muscle belly, ✗ tendinous insertion, ✗ ramus fracture, ✗ sports hernia)

| Hamstring strain (TO) | Type I | Type II |
|---|---|---|
| Mechanism | **High-speed running** | **Slow stretch** — hip flexed + knee extended (**high kicking, splits**) |
| Site | **BF long head, proximal MTJ** | **Semimembranosus near ischial tuberosity** |
| Acute loss of function | **More marked** | Less dramatic |
| Rehabilitation | **Shorter** | **Longer** |

**Tennis leg (TO)** — **medial head of gastrocnemius** torn at muscle belly–aponeurosis junction

## 3 Presentation

**History** — sudden posterior thigh pain running / kicking / jumping ± **"pop"** · **pain with sitting** = proximal avulsion

**Ecchymosis** — posterior thigh → proximal avulsion or high-grade MTJ tear · **latent ecchymosis** appears **\~1 week** after injury (haematoma tracking)

**Palpable mass** — middle ⅓ posterior thigh → **MTJ rupture**

**Gait** — **"stiff-legged"** (avoids hip + knee flexion)

**↑ Popliteal angle** — hip 90°, knee 90° → extend knee slowly → angle at pain vs other side

**Strength** — prone knee flexion at 90° weak · **resisted knee flexion prone painful** (TO)

**Neuro** — may have peroneal weakness (foot drop)

| Provocative test (pain = +) | Method | Sens / spec |
|---|---|---|
| **Puranen-Orava** | Heel on raised surface, reach for toes | 0.76 / 0.82 |
| **Bent-knee stretch** | Supine, hip + knee max flexed, **slow** passive knee extension | 0.84 / 0.87 |
| **Modified bent-knee stretch** | Same, **rapid** full extension | **0.89 / 0.91 — best** |

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Study | Finding / use |
|---|---|
| X-ray: **AP pelvis, AP + lateral femur** | **Bony avulsion of ischial tuberosity** |
| **MRI — gold standard** | **Number of tendons + retraction**, sciatic nerve position (chronic) |
| MRI T1 | ↑ signal = **tendinopathy** |
| MRI T2 | ↑ signal = **partial tear** |
| MRI partial avulsion | **"Sickle sign"** at hamstring origin |
| MRI complete rupture | **Ischial tuberosity denuded of tendon**, haematoma, retraction (e.g. **> 5 cm**) · e.g. BF long head + ST (conjoint) torn on axial |
| US + MRI (TO) | Support diagnosis · **MRI better** for oedema, haemorrhage, tear; BAMIC grading |
| CT | **No indication** (✗ urgent CT) |

### Hamstring tear MRI classification

| Grade | MRI |
|---|---|
| 1 | T2 ↑ signal about tendon / muscle, **no fibre disruption** |
| 2 | T2 ↑ signal + fibre disruption **< ½ width** |
| 3 | Fibre disruption **> ½ width** |

### British Athletics Muscle Injury Classification (BAMIC) (TO)

| Location (TO) | Site |
|---|---|
| **a** | **Myofascial** (surface / fascia) |
| **b** | **Muscle–tendon junction** |
| **c** | **Intratendinous** |
| **d** | **Tendon avulsion** |

| Grade (TO) | Injury | Severity |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | No structural damage, soreness | Mild |
| 1 | Small tear, minimal fibre disruption | Mild |
| 2 | Moderate tear, partial fibre disruption | Moderate |
| 3 | Extensive tear | Severe |

### Prognosis for return to play (TO)

| Predictor (TO) | Effect |
|---|---|
| **MRI-negative** strain | **Early RTP** |
| **More proximal** (near ischial tuberosity) | **Prolonged RTP** |
| ↑ **craniocaudal length** on MRI | Longer RTP |
| Pain-free walking > 1 day | **4× more likely RTP > 3 weeks** |
| Days to pain-free jogging | **1–2 d → < 2 wk · 3–5 d → > 2 wk · > 5 d → > 4 wk** |
| **Intramuscular conjoint tendon** injury | RTP delayed **3–4 months** |
| Pain on passive SLR, longer tender length | Longer RTP |
| Stopped within 5 min, ↑ max pain | Longer RTP |

## 5 Management

```
Hamstring injury (MRI: tendons + retraction)
├─ MTJ / single tendon / 2 tendons < 2 cm → NONOPERATIVE
├─ 2 tendons > 2 cm, young active → OPEN REPAIR
├─ 3-tendon / complete proximal avulsion → OPEN REPAIR (acute)
├─ Partial avulsion, symptoms > 6 months → REPAIR TO ISCHIAL TUBEROSITY
└─ Bony avulsion → by displacement
       ├─ < 2 cm → PROTECTED WB 4 wk → PT
       └─ > 2 cm or chronic symptomatic → ORIF
```

### Nonoperative care

**Indications** — **most injuries** · **all single-tendon tears** · **2 tendons < 2 cm retraction** · **MTJ rupture** · less active / comorbid

**Regimen** — **rest, ice, NSAIDs, protected WB 4 wk** → stretching + strengthening · heals in **up to 6 wk** · OB: WBAT, gentle stretching, over 4–6 wk (✗ NWB 6 wk)

**Single tendon < 2 cm** — ruptured tendon **scars to intact tendons**

**Return** — strength **≥ 90% of contralateral**

**Modalities** — massage, ultrasound, electrical stimulation

**PRP** — acute strains, **high-level athletes** · **within 24–48 h**, US-guided · RTP **3–5 days earlier** (NFL, low-level data)

### Operative criteria

| Tendon repair | ORIF |
|---|---|
| **Proximal avulsion ruptures** | **Bony avulsion > 2 cm displaced** (fragment big enough for hardware) |
| **Partial avulsion, failed nonop 6 months** | **Chronic symptomatic bony avulsion** |
| **2 tendons > 2 cm retraction, young (< 50 y) active** | |
| **3-tendon tears** (all avulsed) | |

**Complete tears** — nonop → poorer outcomes → **repair** · **acute complete** > chronic partial for improvement after surgery

**Arthroscopy** — ✗ no intra-articular pathology · endoscopic repair = case reports only

### Proximal avulsion — surgical indications & repair

**22-yr sprinter, complete proximal avulsion retracted > 5 cm** — **surgical repair first-line** (✗ ATFL signal = low ankle sprain → rest, ice, early rehab · ✗ PCL avulsion → nonop, quadriceps strengthening · ✗ partial cuff tear → NSAIDs + PT · ✗ compression-sided femoral neck stress fracture < 50% width → activity restriction + NWB)

**Hurdler, 3 tendons avulsed (BF, SM, ST)** — **open surgical repair of all tendons to the ischial tuberosity** (✗ NSAIDs + NWB 6 wk, ✗ stretching, ✗ CT, ✗ hip arthroscopy)

**42-yr, acute 3-tendon tear, massive retraction, no bony fragment** — **primary suture-anchor repair + 4–6 wk PWB ± brace** (✗ ORIF, ✗ Achilles allograft + sciatic neurolysis, ✗ PRP + NWB, ✗ crutches 6 wk) · bracing optional

**Partial avulsion, ischial pain at 6 months** — **repair to ischial tuberosity** (✗ sciatic decompression, ✗ labral repair, ✗ repair to intertrochanteric crest / femoral shaft — nonanatomic)

**Approach** — **prone**, knee free to flex · **transverse incision in gluteal crease** → **"T" extension** distally for large retracted / chronic tears · fascia usually intact; vertical fascial incision → haematoma

**Footprint** — scrape with **elevator / curette, avoid burr** (sciatic) · **4–6 suture anchors**, knee flexed

**Chronic, unable to re-approximate** — **Achilles allograft** bridge (≈ acute results, small studies)

**Post-op** — **PWB 4–6 wk, knee flexed 40°** ± knee / hip brace

**ORIF technique** — direct reduction, **partially / fully threaded screws + washers** ± suture anchors / interference screws

**Outcomes** — **80% RTS at 6 months** · complications **up to 23%** (23.17%) · **chronic > acute (< 6 wk)** complications · 91% return of strength after operative repair (OB)

### Surgical nerve risk (sciatic, pudendal)

| Nerve | Position vs ischial tuberosity | Fact |
|---|---|---|
| **Sciatic** | **1.2 cm lateral** | **Most commonly injured** in repair · neurolysis just lateral to hamstring sheath · transient palsy (retraction) |
| **Pudendal** | **2–3 cm superomedial** | **Aggressive Hohmann superomedially** → sexual dysfunction, dyspareunia, impotence, pain sitting / defecating → use glenoid retractors |
| Posterior femoral cutaneous | Lateral, beside sciatic | With inferior gluteal artery deep to gluteus max · transient posterior thigh numbness |
| Inferior gluteal | Below piriformis, under gluteus max | Retracted with gluteus max · 5 cm cephalad to its inferior border |
| Superior gluteal | Far superolateral | Exits **above** piriformis (OB) · "inferior to piriformis, then superior" (other OB) — not in field |
| Nerve to obturator internus | Medial, with pudendal | Rarely encountered |

### Rehabilitation & recurrence prevention (eccentric training)

| Phase (TO) | Exercise |
|---|---|
| **Acute** (painful) | **Isometric** (no joint movement), ice, compression, elevation, NSAIDs as needed |
| **Subacute** (pain-free walking) | **Isotonic** = concentric + eccentric → **emphasis on eccentric**: **Askling L-protocol + Nordic hamstring curls** |
| Adjunct | **Lumbopelvic stability (balance board)**, adductor + glute strengthening |

**Recurrent hamstring injury, 15-yr footballer (TO)** — **eccentric training** (✗ balance board, ✗ isotonic, ✗ isometric)

**Nordic hamstring exercise** — kneel, heels held, lean forward to prone + return · **↓ injuries 50–70%**

![Nordic hamstring exercise](../Orthobullets/attachments/ob3102-nordic-hamstring-exercise.jpg)

**Prevention** — add abdominal, hip + quadriceps strengthening · **correct hamstring–quadriceps imbalance**

**Recurrent sprinter returning — advise (TO)** — work on **hamstring strength** · assess **hip position** while running · **lumbopelvic control** · ✗ **"avoid high-speed training"** — sprinter must train at high intensity

**Terminal swing (TO)** — hip max flexion **\~30°** → controlled by **hamstrings + glutes**; hamstrings slow the extending knee before heel strike → **eccentric** load = injury phase · hip adductors switch eccentric → concentric

**Anterior pelvic tilt (TO)** — strains lumbar spine + hamstrings, impairs gluteal function → lumbopelvic control prevents recurrence

## 6 Complications

| Complication | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Recurrence** | **Most common — 12–31%** · weakness, H:Q imbalance, premature return |
| Peroneal nerve injury | **Distal non-insertional** injuries · usually self-resolves |
| **Sciatic nerve injury** | **8% of surgical cases** · chronic scarring → **nerve exploration** |
| **Hamstring syndrome** | Buttock / ischial pain after **nonop avulsion**, worse sitting, stretching, sprinting, hurdling, kicking · hamstrings scarred to sciatic → **surgical release + sciatic decompression** |
| **Ischial tuberosity nonunion** | **Bony avulsion > 2 cm treated nonop** → **ORIF ± bone graft** |

**Prognosis** — unpredictable · **84% regain strength, 89% endurance** · poor: **severely retracted**, **chronic scarred to sciatic nerve**
