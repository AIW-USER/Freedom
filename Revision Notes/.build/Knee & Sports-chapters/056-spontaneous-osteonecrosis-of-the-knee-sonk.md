# Spontaneous Osteonecrosis of the Knee (SONK)

*Knee & Sports · 56 / 61*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Who** — **middle-aged + elderly females** · **women > 55 yr** (Orthobullets, OB) · **women > 60 yr** (OB5977) — read the options

**Joints** — **99% only one joint**

**Site** — **epiphysis of medial femoral condyle** (weight-bearing MFC) · also **lateral femoral condyle**, **patella** (OB) · **tibial plateau** (OB)

**Pareek (OB)** — 223 patients with subchondral insufficiency fracture: **femur 69%**, tibia 55%, **medial compartment 89%** · > 5 yr: **47% surgery, 37% arthroplasty** (\~**⅓ → TKA**)

**Ecker + Lotke (OB)** — 15 patients (16 knees), **medial tibial plateau** tenderness · first X-ray lucent in **9 / 16**, all 16 eventually · **TKA 9, UKA 3**, 2 refused, 2 resolved

### Types of knee osteonecrosis

| Type | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Spontaneous (SONK)** | **Most common** · idiopathic · MFC · elderly women |
| **Secondary** | Risk factors (**lupus, alcohol, sickle cell**) · more **diffuse** · see osteonecrosis chapter |
| **Post-arthroscopic** | **Rarest** · after **meniscectomy**, age **> 55 yr** (Di Caprio) |

### Pathophysiology

**Core lesion** — **subchondral insufficiency fracture** in poor-quality bone → **crescent-shaped** osteonecrosis

**Cascade (OB)** — **SIF of MFC → osteonecrosis + flattening → degenerative posterior horn medial meniscus tear**

**Meniscus** — **posterior horn medial meniscal tears**, may involve the **root** · **root injuries in up to 80%** of SONK (OB)

**Root tear (Bhatia)** — loss of **hoop stresses** → **extrusion** → ↑ tibiofemoral contact pressure ≈ **total meniscectomy**

**Post-arthroscopic** — mostly after **partial meniscectomy** → altered biomechanics + **loss of shock absorption** → insufficiency fracture → crescent osteonecrosis

**Weight bearing** — exacerbates · **treatment based on size + signal** (Zaremski)

## 3 Presentation

**Onset** — **sudden severe knee pain** · **worse at night** + with weight bearing

**Exam** — **effusion** · ROM limited by pain · **tenderness over MFC** / medial joint line · slight synovitis

**Tibial plateau lesion (OB)** — medial pain + tenderness, ROM normal, no deformity · **misdiagnosed as degenerative meniscus or OA**

**Post-arthroscopic pattern** — well at 2 wk after partial medial meniscectomy → **↑ medial pain at 6 wk** → MRI: **osteonecrosis** (✗ osteomyelitis — no infection signs, ✗ advanced OA, ✗ inflammatory arthritis, ✗ osteochondral lesion)

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Study | Finding / use |
|---|---|
| X-ray — **standing AP + lateral of hip, knee + ankle** | **Diagnostic in advanced disease** · **radiolucent subchondral lesion** · flattened MFC · **osteopenic area** in medial tibial plateau on lateral · early films may be normal |
| **MRI** | **Most useful study** · confirms + **sizes** lesion → guides treatment |
| MRI — lesion | **Crescent-shaped** · **subchondral hypointense crescent line**, cortical irregularity |
| MRI — signal | **T1 marrow hypointensity** + **T2 hyperintensity (oedema)** · **serpiginous border** · localised low-signal area + surrounding oedema |

### Diagnosis – MRI (osteonecrosis vs bone bruise / insufficiency fracture)

**74-yr-old woman, acute medial pain, no trauma** — **osteonecrosis** (✗ OA, ✗ RA, ✗ medial meniscal tear, ✗ transient osteoporosis)

**52-yr-old man, sudden atraumatic pain, worse at night, tibial plateau lesion** — **osteonecrosis** (✗ CRPS — **MRI normal**, ✗ OA — both MFC + MTP involved, ✗ osteosarcoma — bone-forming, ✗ inflammatory arthritis — whole knee)

**28-yr-old skier, MRI after injury** — **bone bruise** · **↑ T2 = haemorrhage from trabecular microfractures** · common after **ACL + MCL** injury (✗ osteosarcoma, ✗ bucket-handle tear, ✗ LCL tear, ✗ tibial spine avulsion)

| Concurrent injury with SONK (OB) | Likely? |
|---|---|
| **Horizontal tear of posterior horn medial meniscus** | ✓ **Most likely** |
| Complex lateral meniscus tear | ✗ (medial predilection) |
| PCL tear | ✗ |
| Patellofemoral arthritis | ✗ |
| LCL avulsion (lateral epicondyle) | ✗ |

### Differential diagnosis

| Condition | Who / key fact |
|---|---|
| **SONK** | **Elderly women**, **MFC (weight-bearing)**, 99% one joint |
| **Osteochondritis dissecans** | **Adolescent / young males**, **lateral aspect of MFC** |
| **Transient osteoporosis** | **Young–middle-aged men** |
| **Bone bruise / occult fracture** | **Trauma**, bone fragility, overuse |
| Secondary osteonecrosis | Risk factors, multifocal |

## 5 Management

```
SONK
├─ Most / pre-collapse / small (< 3.5 cm) → NSAIDs, protected WB, quads PT, unloader brace
├─ Failed nonop, unicompartmental → UKA
├─ Failed nonop, large (> 5 cm) / collapse / diffuse → TKA
└─ Younger with angular malalignment → HTO
```

**Nonop** — **mainstay** (most resolve, **self-limiting**) · **NSAIDs, narcotics, protected WB** · **PT for quadriceps strengthening** · **unloader brace**, sometimes **bisphosphonates** (OB) · good results

**Post-arthroscopic** — most nonop · **protected WB 4–8 wk** (Di Caprio)

**Timing (OB)** — most patients seen **post-collapse** → **arthroplasty** treatment of choice · pre-collapse treatment **controversial**

### Operative criteria

| Indication | Procedure |
|---|---|
| Failed nonop, **small lesion**, one compartment | **UKA** |
| Failed nonop, **large lesion / bone collapse**, diffuse / multicompartment | **TKA** |
| **Angular malalignment** (younger) · offload an osteochondral allograft | **HTO** |
| Younger / active (OB) | Microfracture, **osteochondral allograft**, HTO |
| Lesion size (OB) | **< 3.5 cm → nonop** · **> 5 cm + failed nonop → arthroplasty** |

**59-yr-old librarian, MFC lesion, failed nonop** — **UKA** (✗ open biopsy — no matrix / periosteal reaction, ✗ chest CT + bone scan — subchondral condyle not a metastatic site, ✗ OATS — defect size + age, ✗ valgus HTO — 59 yr, no varus)

**58-yr-old, antiphospholipid, crescentic collapse, failed PT / NSAIDs / PRP** — **arthroplasty** (✗ core decompression — can't treat full-thickness collapse, ✗ HTO — younger + angular deformity, ✗ medial root repair — doesn't fix cartilage loss, ✗ stem cells — not FDA approved)

### Treatment – unicompartmental / total arthroplasty & outcomes

**UKA for SONK — true** — **reliable long-term outcomes** · **treatment of choice for severe localised disease** · TKA **rarely needed** as primary

**UKA traps** — ✗ only for spontaneous (works for **secondary** localised too) · ✗ higher failure than OA (**comparable**) · ✗ tibial stress fracture commonest failure · ✗ TKA needed because UKA fails

| UKA series (OB) | Result |
|---|---|
| **Myers** (systematic review) | TKA / UKA for SONK ≈ for OA (correct indications) |
| **Parratte** | Pain relief **96%** (≥ 3 yr) · 12-yr survivorship **96.7%** · spontaneous + secondary |
| **Heyse** (52) | **97.3%** satisfied · survival **93.1% at 10 yr**, **90.6% at 15 yr** |
| **Bruni** (84) | 10-yr survival **89%** · revisions: **tibial subsidence 4**, aseptic loosening 3 · **no lateral progression** |
| **Kaneko** (61) | 10-yr implant survival **90%** |
| **Chalmers** (46) | **Secondary ON less durable** than SONK · failure from **arthritis progression** |

**Commonest reason for revision after UKA** — **progression of arthritis** (OB213949) (✗ aseptic loosening, ✗ bearing dislocation, ✗ implant failure, ✗ periprosthetic fracture) · **aseptic loosening** most often (OB213790) · loosening + progression both (OB213790 answers) · subsidence + loosening, no progression (Bruni) — read the options

**UKA contraindications (OB)** — **inflammatory arthropathy**, **fixed deformity > 5–10°**, **motion < 90°**, **previous meniscectomy**, symptomatic other compartment

### Bone marrow oedema adjuncts

**Persistent BME (Akhavan)** — prognostic for pain, dysfunction, cartilage damage · surgery: **core decompression**, bone-graft injections, arthroplasty

**Zoledronic acid (Flores-Robles)** — primary BMES (ankle > hip) · **75% complete response** at 12 months

**Subchondroplasty (Cohen)** — fluoroscopic **calcium phosphate** injection into bone marrow lesions · ↑ VAS + IKDC to 2 yr

## X vs Y

| | **SONK** | **Secondary osteonecrosis** |
|---|---|---|
| Who | **Women > 55 yr** | Risk factors (steroids, lupus, alcohol, sickle cell) |
| Extent | **One joint (99%)**, MFC epiphysis | Multiple compartments, **80% bilateral** (osteonecrosis chapter) |
| Cause | **Subchondral insufficiency fracture** ± root tear | ↓ blood flow |
| UKA durability | **Better** | Less durable (Chalmers) |

| | **SONK** | **OCD** | **Bone bruise** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Patient | **Elderly woman** | **Adolescent male** | Young, trauma |
| Site | **Weight-bearing MFC** | **Lateral aspect of MFC** | After ACL / MCL injury |
| MRI | **Crescent**, T1 dark + T2 bright, serpiginous border | Osteochondral fragment | **↑ T2**, trabecular microfractures |

## How it's asked

**"74-yr-old woman, acute medial pain — MRI diagnosis?"** → **osteonecrosis**

**"Skier, MRI after injury?"** → **bone bruise**

**"52-yr-old, sudden atraumatic pain, worse at night — MRI?"** → **osteonecrosis**

**"Medial pain 6 wk after partial meniscectomy, crescent ↑ T2?"** → **osteonecrosis (post-arthroscopic)**

**"Lupus, SONK on MRI — concurrent injury?"** → **posterior horn medial meniscus tear (Injury C)**

**"59-yr-old, MFC lesion, failed nonop — best treatment?"** → **unicompartmental knee arthroplasty**

**"UKA for SONK — true?"** → **reliable long-term outcomes**

**"Commonest reason for revision after UKA for SONK?"** → **progression of arthritis**

**"Crescentic collapse, failed PRP / NSAIDs — treatment?"** → **arthroplasty**

**"OCD vs SONK — site?"** → **lateral aspect of MFC (OCD) vs weight-bearing MFC (SONK)**
