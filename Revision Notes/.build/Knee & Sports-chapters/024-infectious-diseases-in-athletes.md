# Infectious Diseases in Athletes

*Knee & Sports · 24 / 61*

## 1 Basics

### Overview

**Skin infections** — **10–15% of time-loss injuries** in collegiate athletes (OB, Johnson) · NCAA + NFHS return-to-play rules · mandatory **team skin checks**

| Infection | Organism | Spread | Typical sport |
|---|---|---|---|
| **CA-MRSA** | S. aureus (PVL +) | **Direct skin contact**; **abrasions ("turf burns")**; shared razors, towels, soap | Contact sports, wrestling |
| **Herpes gladiatorum** | **HSV-1** (80%, OB) | **Skin-to-skin**; blister fluid, saliva, mucosa | **Wrestling (2–7%)**, rugby |
| **Tinea** | Dermatophytes | Direct contact, broken skin; survives off host in warm, moist places | **Wrestling** |
| **Impetigo** | **S. pyogenes, S. aureus** | Highly contagious | Wrestling |
| Acne mechanica | Friction, heat, occlusion | — (not infectious) | **Padded sports** (hockey, football) |
| Pitted keratolysis | **Corynebacterium** | Moist feet | Runners |
| **Mononucleosis** | **EBV** (herpesvirus) | **Saliva** — kissing, shared cups / utensils, sneeze / cough | Any |
| HIV | CD4 (T-helper) cells | ↑ haemophiliacs, IV drug users, MSM | Any |

### Genes & lab markers

| Marker | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Panton-Valentine leukocidin (PVL)** | **Cytotoxin that defines CA-MRSA** (not HA-MRSA) → lyses WBCs, tissue necrosis → rapid abscess, "spider bite" |
| **mecA → PBP2a** | Low β-lactam affinity → **methicillin resistance** (all MRSA) |
| **SCCmec type IV** | **CA-MRSA**; lacks extra elements → **less multidrug resistance** (HA-MRSA SCCmec carries them) |
| β-lactamase | In most S. aureus; methicillin resists it |
| **D-zone test** | Erythromycin-resistant isolate → clindamycin + erythromycin discs → **D-shaped zone = inducible clindamycin resistance** |
| **Heterophile Ab (Monospot)** | **87% sensitive, 91% specific**; may stay positive |
| **VCA IgG + IgM** | **97% sensitive, 94% specific** |
| CBC | Absolute + relative **lymphocytosis, > 10% atypical lymphocytes** |
| LFTs (TO) | Mildly ↑ in **\~90%** of IM |
| **KOH prep** | **Hyphae → tinea** |
| **Wood's lamp red fluorescence** | **Corynebacterium** (pitted keratolysis; erythrasma too) |
| EBV (TO) | **Double-stranded DNA gamma herpesvirus**; isolated **1964 from Burkitt lymphoma** |
| AIDS | HIV + **CD4 < 200** or opportunistic infection |

## 2 Mechanism

**MRSA risk** — **exposed skin from abrasions** ↑ risk significantly · sharing hygiene products · prevention: cover compromised skin + good hygiene

**HSV** — incubation **2–14 days** (Orthobullets) · up to 14 days (OB) · **5–10 days** (OB) · head, neck, shoulders · eye contact → **herpetic conjunctivitis**

**EBV** — incubation **30–50 days** · **transmissibility low → isolation unnecessary** (✗ blood-borne spread more severe) · transmission to teammates possible in acute phase (OB)

**IM epidemiology** — annual incidence **1–3% of college freshmen / students** (OB)

## 3 Presentation

### CA-MRSA – presentation

**CA-MRSA** — **community-acquired methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus**; ↑ in sport

**Lesion** — boil / pimple · **"spider bite"** · **pustules on an erythematous base**

**Wrestler, 6-cm elbow lesion "spider bite"** — **CA-MRSA** (✗ psoriasis, ✗ tinea, ✗ HSV)

**Abscess → amoxicillin → recurs with fever + pain** — **CA-MRSA** (✗ group A strep — amoxicillin covers it, ✗ tinea, ✗ HSV) · up to **70%** of players hospitalised

**7-day ankle swelling after slide tackle + malaise, normal X-ray** — **MRSA abscess**

![CA-MRSA abscess](../Orthobullets/attachments/ob3124-mrsa.jpg)

### Herpes gladiatorum – presentation

**Lesion** — **clusters of fluid-filled vesicles on an erythematous base** · grouped **1–2 mm** clear vesicles → rupture → moist ulcers / crusted plaques

**Primary infection** — constitutional symptoms; burning, tingling, stinging · bacterial infections = larger pustules

**DDx** — impetigo, tinea corporis, eczema

![Herpes gladiatorum vesicles on the neck](../Orthobullets/attachments/ob3124-herpesgladiatorum.jpg)

### Other skin infections – impetigo, tinea, pitted keratolysis

**Tinea types** — pedis, corporis, capitis, cruris (by site) · **corporis** excludes feet, groin, scalp, bearded face, nails

**Tinea corporis ("ringworm")** — **scaly red circular patches** · well-demarcated plaque, **raised border, central hypopigmentation** · hypo- / hyperpigmented dry, dusty oval patches · highly contagious → minor epidemics

![Tinea corporis](../Orthobullets/attachments/ob3124-tineacorporis.jpg)

**Impetigo** — fluid-filled blisters → **crusting after a few days** · **golden-brown crusts around nose + mouth** (OB)

**Acne mechanica / folliculitis** — **red papules**, inflamed follicles under padding

**Pitted keratolysis** — **shallow crater-like pits on weight-bearing sole** (palms less often), thickened skin, **foul odour**, sweating · **red fluorescence on Wood's lamp** (Corynebacterium)

### Infectious mononucleosis – presentation

**Prodrome (3–5 days)** — **malaise, myalgia, nausea, headache**

**Hoagland's triad** — **fever, pharyngitis, lymphadenopathy** (Orthobullets, OB) · TO "triad": fatigue + pharyngitis (chief complaint), **posterior cervical >> axillary / inguinal nodes**, athlete with only fatigue / ↓ performance

**Pharyngitis** — 30% (Orthobullets, GAS) · **exudative** (white / grey pseudomembrane) in 50% · exudative pharyngitis + **tonsillar enlargement** classic (TO)

**Lymphadenopathy** — **posterior cervical chain**, lasts 2–3 wk

**Rash** — **after ampicillin / amoxicillin**; petechial / maculopapular / urticarial · **10–40%** (TO)

**Other (TO)** — **posterior palatine petechiae (⅓)**, **jaundice 10–15%**

**Splenomegaly** — palpable only **50%** (OB) · lymphocytic infiltration pushes spleen beyond rib cage · resolves **4–6 wk** (TO)

| IM symptom (TO) | Frequency |
|---|---|
| **Sore throat** | **95%** |
| Cervical lymphadenopathy | 80% |
| Fatigue | 70% |
| Upper respiratory symptoms | 65% |
| Headache · ↓ appetite | 50% each |
| Fever | 47% |
| Myalgia | 45% |

**Course** — symptoms resolve **4–8 wk** (Orthobullets) · acute phase **7 days to 2–3 wk**, recovery up to **2–3 months** (TO) · most findings ≤ 10 days; **fatigue + cervical nodes ≥ 3 wk** (TO)

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Study | Finding / use |
|---|---|
| IM imaging | **Generally unnecessary** |
| **Ultrasound** | **First choice if imaging** — noninvasive, reliable, no radiation · confirms **normal spleen size**; baseline variability limits RTP use (TO) |
| **CT** | **Exclude splenic rupture** (stable patient) |
| FAST | Free fluid + unstable → **laparotomy** |

![Splenic rupture after mononucleosis (CT)](../MCQ/attachments/q218194-illa-splenic-rupture1.jpg)

### Diagnostic criteria

| Condition | Criteria |
|---|---|
| **AIDS** | **HIV positive + CD4 < 200** or **opportunistic infection** |
| **Tinea** | KOH scraping → **hyphae** |
| **Pitted keratolysis** | Crater-like pits + odour + **red Wood's fluorescence** |
| **IM** | Heterophile Ab (Monospot); VCA IgG / IgM; lymphocytosis > 10% atypical |

## 5 Management

### Return-to-play criteria

| Infection | Return to play |
|---|---|
| **CA-MRSA** | After **72 h on antibiotics** (e.g. TMP-SMX) (OB) · **cover lesions** · NCAA bacterial: **no new lesions 48 h** + **72 h antibiotics** + **no exudative lesions** (OB) |
| **Herpes gladiatorum** | **No new lesions 72 h** + **≥ 5 days (120 h) antivirals** + **all lesions dry / crusted** + no systemic symptoms (NCAA) |
| **Tinea corporis** | **≥ 72 h topical antifungal** + lesions **covered** + **screened before competition** |
| **Tinea capitis (scalp)** | **14 days** antifungal (NCAA, OB) |
| **Molluscum contagiosum** (pox virus, painless papule) | Lesions **removed (curetted)** + **covered with Tegaderm** before play (NCAA, OB) |
| **Impetigo** | **All lesions clear of crusting** |
| **Mononucleosis** | **No contact sport 3–5 wk** (up to 3 months) **until splenomegaly resolves** — see sources below |
| **HIV** | **HIV alone does not bar competition** · universal precautions · out until **bleeding stops** |

### CA-MRSA – treatment & prevention

| Lesion | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Small** | **Topical mupirocin** |
| **Larger** | **I&D + oral TMP-SMX + rifampin** |
| **Severe** | **I&D + IV antibiotics** |

**Abscess** — **incise, drain, culture** first; antibiotics = necessary adjunct (✗ topical antibiotics + occlusive dressing, ✗ training-room debridement + oral antibiotics, ✗ ice only) · empiric anti-MRSA cover until sensitivities

**Superficial, no systemic illness** — **oral** antibiotics (✗ IV)

**Erythromycin-resistant + D-zone positive** — **switch clindamycin → oral doxycycline** (✗ continue clindamycin, ✗ IV vancomycin, ✗ IV cefazolin, ✗ **rifampin alone** — rapid resistance)

**Mupirocin small + I&D / TMP-SMX large** — = **MRSA boil** (✗ impetigo, ✗ molluscum contagiosum, ✗ ringworm, ✗ HSV)

**Most effective team prevention** — **cover lesions with occlusive dressing during sport** (✗ separate locker room, ✗ prophylactic antibiotics for teammates, ✗ ban from team events)

**Hygiene** — showering, hand washing, breathable clothing, shower sandals, **no shared towels / equipment**, daily skin surveillance, disinfect equipment, restrict contact

```
Suspected CA-MRSA skin lesion
├─ Small → topical mupirocin
├─ Abscess / larger → I&D + culture + oral TMP-SMX
│      └─ D-zone positive → oral doxycycline (not clindamycin)
└─ Severe → I&D + IV antibiotics
```

### Herpes gladiatorum – antivirals & return to play

**Treatment** — **acyclovir, valacyclovir, famciclovir** for any active lesion → **inhibit viral DNA polymerase / inactivate viral DNA synthesis** ✓

**Duration** — **5 days regardless of agent** (✗ famciclovir 3 days)

**Traps** — ✗ TMP-SMX (MRSA) · ✗ unroof / debride · ✗ RTP 48 h after starting · ✗ streptococci on culture (impetigo) · ✗ wrestle if covered · ✗ out 2 weeks · ✗ keflex 48 h

**Wrong-drug distractors (OB)** — clotrimazole → **ringworm** · mupirocin → **impetigo** · erythromycin → **erysipelas**

### Tinea – treatment

**Topical antifungal** — **tinea corporis, cruris, pedis** · **topical ketoconazole 14 days, RTP after 72 h** ✓ · imidazoles (clotrimazole, ketoconazole, miconazole) or allylamines (terbinafine, naftifine)

**Systemic antifungal** — **tinea capitis** (oral itraconazole; griseofulvin first line) + severe cases · **oral fluconazole** → widespread, failed topical, immunocompromised (✗ for an isolated lesion)

**✗ Bactrim** — antibiotic, no role in tinea

**No sport** — while infection active

### Impetigo, acne mechanica, pitted keratolysis – treatment

**Impetigo** — **erythromycin, topical Bactroban (mupirocin)** first line · no sport while active

**Acne mechanica** — **observation first** (resolves after season) · **tretinoin** (keratolytic) if severe · wash immediately after play, moisture-wicking clothing

**Pitted keratolysis** — keep feet dry, **topical clindamycin / erythromycin / mupirocin**, antiperspirants; oral antibiotics if severe · clindamycin 1%–benzoyl peroxide 5% gel effective (OB)

### Infectious mononucleosis – splenomegaly, splenic rupture & return to play

**Supportive** — fluids, acetaminophen, rest · **no isolation** · **valacyclovir not needed**

**Strep pharyngitis** — **IM penicillin once** or **oral penicillin 10 days**; erythromycin if allergic · **NO amoxicillin** (rash)

**Corticosteroids** — shrink tonsils if dysphagia / dehydration (Orthobullets) · **no role for antivirals or corticosteroids** (OB) — read the options

**Other** — airway management if respiratory distress · **stool softener** (↓ Valsalva straining)

**Rest (TO)** — rest **3 weeks**, then light activity · no strenuous activity, weights, contact, roughhousing first **21 days** · spleen still palpable at 3 wk → light activity until **28 days**

**RTP prerequisites (TO)** — **afebrile, hydrated, asymptomatic, no palpable liver or spleen** · individualise (contact vs noncontact, in- vs off-season) · explain splenic risk — **full recovery may take months**

**Not RTP markers** — ✗ negative heterophile test (stays positive) · ✗ fatigue / sore throat better (may resolve before spleen) · ✗ lymphocytosis resolved · ✗ 48 h after antibiotics

| Mono RTP rule | Source |
|---|---|
| **3 weeks** — contact sport after 3 wk of illness if no ongoing signs | **TO** |
| **3 wk after symptoms resolve**, no splenomegaly | OB |
| **4 wk after onset** (seen at 1 wk → 3–4 wk more) | OB |
| **4 wk after onset AND normal-sized spleen** | OB |
| **3–5 wk** off until splenomegaly resolves | OB, Orthobullets |
| No exertion 3 wk, then **light non-contact activity** | OB (consensus) |

**Diagnosed 2 wk ago, asymptomatic 1 wk** — **RTP in 2 weeks if no splenomegaly** (✗ now, ✗ valacyclovir course)

## 6 Complications

### Splenic rupture

**Rate** — **0.1–0.5%** (Orthobullets, OB) · 0.1–0.2% (OB) · **< 1%** (TO) — most feared complication

**Timing** — **most in first 3 weeks** (21 days) (Orthobullets, OB, TO) · within a month (OB)

**Mechanism** — **sudden ↑ portal venous pressure** · **50% atraumatic Valsalva** (rowing, weightlifting) + 50% external trauma (Orthobullets) · **up to 86% atraumatic** (OB) — read the options

**Normal-sized spleens** — rupture reported in nonathletes + normal spleens too

**Returned at 2 wk, tackled, severe left abdominal pain** — **splenic rupture** (✗ liver contusion, ✗ sports hernia, ✗ iliac / ASIS avulsion)

**Signs** — LUQ pain, **left shoulder pain (Kehr's sign)**, fatigue

**Unstable + FAST positive** — **exploratory laparotomy** (✗ CT, ✗ IV fluids alone, ✗ angiography, ✗ Monospot)

### Other complications of mononucleosis

**Long-term performance (TO)** — **chronic fatigue** (> 6 months) ✓ (✗ thrombocytopenia, ✗ myocarditis, ✗ hepatitis)

**Rare, \~1% (TO)** — airway obstruction, strep pharyngitis, meningoencephalitis, haemolytic anaemia, thrombocytopenia

**Orthobullets list** — aplastic anaemia, Guillain-Barré, meningitis / encephalitis, neuritis, lymphoma, HUS, DIC

**Malignancy link (TO)** — **Burkitt, Hodgkin lymphoma, nasopharyngeal carcinoma**

**HIV wound care** — **compressive dressing**; participation stops until all bleeding ceases
