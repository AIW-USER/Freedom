# Quadriceps Tendon Rupture

*Knee & Sports · 51 / 61*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology & risk factors

**Frequency** — **more common than patellar tendon rupture** (2:1 in the patellar tendon rupture chapter)

**Age** — usually **> 40 yr** (Orthobullets, OB) · **patellar tendon rupture < 40 yr** (OB)

**Sex + side** — **M > F (up to 8:1)** · **nondominant limb > 2× as often**

**Site** — usually at the **insertion of the tendon on the patella**

**Systemic risk factors** — **renal failure**, **diabetes**, **rheumatoid arthritis**, hyperparathyroidism, connective tissue disorders

**Local / drug risk factors** — **steroid use** · **intra-articular injections (in 20–33%)** · previous knee surgery (OB)

**Pathobiology (OB)** — degeneration from **reduced blood supply** + **repetitive microtrauma** · **little evidence that inflammation is active** · high mechanical load + poor blood supply impede repair

### Anatomy

**Quadriceps tendon** — **2 to 4 distinct layers** → matters for partial vs complete tear and for repair

## 2 Mechanism

**Mechanism** — **eccentric loading** of extensor mechanism · **foot planted, knee slightly bent**

**Younger patients** — usually **direct trauma**

## 3 Presentation

### Diagnosis – patella baja & palpable defect

**History** — preceding pain from **underlying tendinopathy** · "pop" · large haematoma / effusion

**Palpable defect** — usually **within 2 cm of superior pole of patella**

**Extensor function** — **can't extend against resistance** · **no straight leg raise in complete rupture** · e.g. **extension lag 30°**

**29-yr-old basketball, 30° lag, suprapatellar defect** — **surgical repair of ruptured quadriceps tendon** (✗ MRI, ✗ diagnostic arthroscopy, ✗ immobiliser 6 wk then sport brace, ✗ limited WB 3 wk then PT)

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Study | Finding / use |
|---|---|
| **X-ray — AP + lateral knee** | **Patella baja** |
| **MRI** | Only if **diagnosis uncertain** · **partial vs complete** tear |

### Diagnostic criteria – Insall-Salvati index

**ISI** — **patellar tendon length ÷ patellar height** (superior → inferior pole) · tendon and patella ≈ equal, no variation > 20% (Insall + Salvati)

| ISI | Meaning | Cause |
|---|---|---|
| **< 0.8** | **Patella baja** | **Quadriceps tendon rupture** |
| **0.8–1.2** | Normal | — |
| **> 1.2** | **Patella alta** | **Inferior pole patellar sleeve avulsion**, patellar tendon rupture |

**Worked example (OB)** — patella **4 cm**, tendon **3 cm** → **ISI 0.75 = baja** → **quadriceps tendon rupture**

**Distractors (OB)** — ✗ OCD (no ISI change) · ✗ sleeve avulsion (alta, ISI > 1.2) · ✗ MPFL disruption (instability, abnormal **patellar tilt**) · ✗ nail-patella syndrome (**small or absent patellae**)

**MRI ISI (Shabshin)** — normal **0.79–1.52** · **females higher** ratios than males

**Recurrent subluxation (Aglietti)** — normal knees: ISI \~1, congruence angle −8, **Q angle 15°** · subluxating: **ISI 1.23**, congruence +16, **Q angle 20°**

![Insall-Salvati index: tendon length ÷ patellar length](../MCQ/attachments/q5821-illa-insall-salvati.jpg)

### Classification

| Type | Extensor mechanism | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **Partial** | **Intact** | Brace immobilisation |
| **Complete** | **Disrupted** (no SLR, baja) | **Primary repair to patella** |

## 5 Management

```
Quadriceps tendon rupture
├─ Partial, extensor mechanism intact → knee immobilisation in brace
├─ Can't tolerate surgery → knee immobilisation in brace
├─ Complete, acute → primary repair to patella (anchors or transosseous)
└─ Chronic > 2 wk, retracted → repair + V-Y lengthening (Codivilla) ± graft
```

### Operative criteria

| Indication | Procedure |
|---|---|
| **Complete rupture with loss of extensor mechanism** | **Primary repair with reattachment to patella** |
| Chronic, retracted (**> 2 wk → up to 5 cm**) | Repair + **tendon lengthening (Codivilla V-Y)** |
| Tendon can't reach patella | **Auto- or allograft** tissue |

### Repair technique – transosseous vs suture anchors

**Approach** — **midline incision**

**Sutures** — **nonabsorbable**, **running locking** in tendon, free ends through **transpatellar drill holes** or tied to **suture anchors** · retinaculum → **heavy absorbable** sutures

**End-point** — knee should **flex to 90° after repair**

**Strength (OB)** — ↑ strands across repair + ↑ suture calibre → ↑ strength but **wound complications**

**Biomechanics** — **suture anchors → less gap formation + higher ultimate load to failure** (Orthobullets, OB) (✗ transosseous less gap, ✗ transosseous higher load)

**Petri (OB)** — 30 cadaver knees · **5.5-mm double-loaded anchors** vs transpatellar tunnels with No. 2 suture + **Krackow** → anchors less gap on cyclic loading, higher ultimate failure

**Kindaya (OB)** — **knotless suture-tape anchors** → less displacement, ↑ stiffness, ↑ load to failure vs transosseous **and** vs traditional anchors

**Onggo (OB)** — 212 knees (114 quadriceps) → anchors smaller gap; higher load to failure in **2 of 5** studies

**Conflict** — anchors vs simple patellar holes **no apparent biomechanical advantage** (OB7683) vs anchors biomechanically superior (OB210177, OB219988, Orthobullets) — read the options

**Clinical outcomes** — **equivalent functional + clinical outcomes** ✓ (OB, Orthobullets) · possibly **better PROMs with anchors** (Orthobullets) vs **no PROM difference** (OB) — read the options

**Mehta (OB)** — transosseous: **higher ROM**, **lower complication rate** (not lower re-rupture) · **no Lysholm difference**

**Transosseous vs anchors — true (OB)** — **equivalent functional outcomes** (✗ less gap, ✗ higher load to failure, ✗ higher complication rate, ✗ worse PROMs)

### Postoperative care

**Post-op** — immobilisation in **brace, cast or splint** → progressive flexibility + strengthening

## 6 Complications

| Complication | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Strength deficit** | **33–50%** of patients |
| **Stiffness** | Common |
| **Functional impairment** | **50% can't return to prior activity / sport** |
| **Re-rupture** | ↑ with **pre-existing inflammatory arthropathy** |

## X vs Y

| | **Quadriceps tendon rupture** | **Patellar tendon rupture** |
|---|---|---|
| Frequency | **More common** | Less common |
| Age | **> 40 yr** | **< 40 yr** |
| Patellar height | **Baja (ISI < 0.8)** | **Alta (ISI > 1.2)** |
| Defect | **Within 2 cm of superior pole** | Below inferior pole |

| | **Suture anchors** | **Transosseous tunnels** |
|---|---|---|
| Gap formation | **Less** | More |
| Ultimate load to failure | **Higher** | Lower |
| Complications (Mehta) | Higher | **Lower** |
| ROM (Mehta) | Lower | **Higher** |
| Functional outcome | **Equivalent** | **Equivalent** |

## How it's asked

**"Patella 4 cm, tendon 3 cm — cause?"** → **quadriceps tendon rupture** (ISI 0.75)

**"Inferior pole sleeve avulsion — ISI?"** → **> 1.2 (alta)**

**"30° extension lag + suprapatellar defect — management?"** → **surgical repair of ruptured quadriceps tendon**

**"Anchors (B) vs transosseous (A)?"** → **B: higher ultimate load to failure + less gap formation**

**"Transosseous vs anchor repair — tell the patient?"** → **equivalent functional outcomes**

**"Which repair has lower complication rate (Mehta)?"** → **transosseous**

**"Chronic retracted rupture — added procedure?"** → **Codivilla V-Y lengthening**

**"Complication in 33–50%?"** → **strength deficit**
