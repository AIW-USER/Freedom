# Proximal Tibiofibular Joint Ganglion Cysts

*Knee & Sports · 48 / 61*

## 1 Basics

**Incidence** — **< 1%** of knee MRIs for pain · age 20–76 · **median 67** (Orthobullets) · "middle-aged males" (OB) · **male 90%** · right leg \~70%

**Location** — **extraneural 80%** > intraneural 20%

**Cause** — repetitive microtrauma · **degeneration** · associated **OA**

**Pathology** — **mucin-filled, synovial-lined sac without true epithelial lining** · collagen walls · viscous (hyaluronic acid, mucopolysaccharides)

**Anatomy** — fibular head in groove behind lateral tibial ridge · capsule stabilisers: anterior + posterior tibiofibular ligaments, LCL, popliteus, biceps · **anterior tibial artery** just distal to PTFJ · **common peroneal nerve** around fibular neck → superficial + deep branches

**Biceps femoris** — long head (posterior on fibular head) → **tibial nerve** · short head (anterior) → **common peroneal nerve**

**PTFJ function** — anterolateral / posteromedial sliding → ↓ ankle torsion, disperses axial load

| Classification | Site |
|---|---|
| **Intraneural** | Within epineurium |
| **Extraneural** | Outside epineurium |

## 3 Presentation

**Symptoms** — usually asymptomatic · pain, **mobile palpable mass distal to lateral joint line**, fullness, **paraesthesias dorsum of foot**

**Neuro** — **foot drop** from common peroneal compression

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Study | Finding / use |
|---|---|
| X-ray (AP + lateral knee / tibia) | Usually normal · ± sclerosis lateral proximal tibia |
| **MRI** | **Diagnostic tool of choice** · well-circumscribed homogeneous cyst · **T1 low, T2 high** |
| US | If MRI not possible · thin-walled fluid cyst |
| EMG / NCS | Extent of sensory + motor deficit |
| Histology | **Confirms** · no true epithelium, no atypia / mitoses |

![PTFJ ganglion, T2 MRI](../Orthobullets/attachments/ob423108-t2-ptfj-cyst.jpg)

**Differential** — ABC, chondroblastoma, GCT, juxta-articular myxoma, **lumbar disc herniation**, PVNS, solid nerve tumours

## 5 Management

```
PTFJ ganglion
├─ Asymptomatic / first presentation → observation, NSAIDs, aspiration
├─ Neuro symptoms / failed nonop / progressive → decompression + marginal excision
└─ Recurrence after first excision → PTFJ arthrodesis (or fibular head resection)
```

| Treatment | Recurrence |
|---|---|
| **Aspiration alone** | **80%** (Orthobullets) · **25–80%** (OB) |
| **Decompression + excision** (hockey-stick lateral approach, neurolysis, excise stalk) | **8–25%** · 1st excision success 7 / 8; repeat excision 3 / 3 recurred (Miskovsky) |
| **PTFJ arthrodesis** (6.5 mm partially threaded cancellous screw) | **< 5%** — lowest |

**Painful growing mass + dorsal foot paraesthesias (OB)** — **surgical decompression + cyst excision** (✗ NSAIDs / activity change, ✗ aspiration, ✗ arthrodesis / fibular head resection — reserved for recurrence)

**Recurrent cyst** — Gulati: proximal fibular excision, good results in 9 · arthrodesis (Miskovsky: 2 fused, no recurrence)

## 6 Complications

**Peroneal nerve dysfunction** — **up to 50%** · risk: **intraneural** · decompression; tendon transfer if complete palsy · prolonged neuro symptoms = poor prognosis

**Recurrence** — **5–80%** · aspiration only highest

**Other** — neurapraxia, perineural fibrosis · hardware irritation after arthrodesis

## How it's asked

**"PTFJ ganglion with peroneal paraesthesias — reduce recurrence?"** → **surgical decompression and excision of cyst**
