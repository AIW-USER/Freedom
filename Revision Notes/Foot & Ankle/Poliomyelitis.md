# Poliomyelitis

*Foot & Ankle · 40 / 48*

## 1 Basics

### Post-polio syndrome & epidemiology

**Pathology** — viral destruction of **anterior horn cells** (spinal cord) + **brain stem motor nuclei** · **dorsal columns spared** · up to **95% of anterior horn cells** may be infected in acute stage (TO)

**Virus** — **enterovirus (poliovirus)** · **faecal–oral** spread

**Household contacts** — **seroconversion > 90% (90–100%)** · 4–7 secondary infections per case

**Outcome of infection** — **\~75% asymptomatic** / mild flu-like · **paralysis in \~1%** · respiratory failure **5–15%** of paralytic cases

**Age at risk** — **younger children (< 5 yr)**\
✗ Wrong options: older > 5 yr

**Course** — **transient infection** → **lifelong motor dysfunction** in most paralytic cases\
✗ Wrong options: paralysis from active / chronic infection

**Eradicated in the USA** (vaccine) · late sequelae common in **developing countries** (TO)

## 3 Presentation

### Acute polio presentation

**Acute polio (TO)** — **flaccid paralysis, DTR absent, muscle atrophy** (TO)\
✗ Wrong options: spastic paralysis · DTR ++

**Probable case definition (TO)** — **acute flaccid paralysis of ≥ 1 limb** + **↓ / absent tendon reflexes** · no other cause · **no sensory or cognitive loss** (TO)

**Acute stage (TO)** — lasts **7–10 days** · malaise → encephalomyelitis · **asymmetrical paralysis** (TO)

**Younger children (TO)** — listlessness, sore throat, low fever → recurrence with hyperaesthesia / paraesthesia, headache, vomiting, **nuchal rigidity**, back pain, **limited SLR** (TO)

**Older children + adults (TO)** — low fever, **flushing**, apprehension, **tender muscles** · **superficial reflexes lost first**, **DTRs disappear when muscle group paralysed** (TO)

**Paralytic polio** — **proximal, asymmetric weakness**, back pain + stiffness, **no sensory deficit** (after diarrhoeal / flu-like illness)

**Hallmark (Orthobullets)** — **muscle weakness with normal sensation**

### Polio foot deformities

**Causes (TO)** — **muscle imbalance** · abnormal posture · gravity · walking on the deformed foot (TO)

**Tibialis anterior (TO)** — **one of the most frequently completely paralysed muscles** → **foot drop → fixed equinus** (TO)

**Commonest deformity** — **equinocavovarus** (OB) · TA weakness (↓ dorsiflexion), **cavus**, **claw toes**, **equinus**

**Range (TO)** — equinus · calcaneus · hindfoot varus / valgus · pes cavus / planus · combinations (TO)

**Neglected deformity effects (TO)** — abnormal gait · pressure areas + **callosities** · ↑ energy expenditure · arthritis (TO)

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Diagnostic criteria – post-polio syndrome (TO)

| Criterion (TO) | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Prior paralytic polio** | Motor neuron loss — history, residual weakness + atrophy, **EMG denervation** |
| **Recovery + stable interval** | Partial / complete recovery → **stable function usually ≥ 15 yr** |
| **New weakness** | **Gradual** (rarely abrupt), progressive, persistent weakness or **↓ endurance** ± fatigue, atrophy, muscle / joint pain · may follow trauma, surgery, inactivity |
| **Duration** | Symptoms **≥ 1 year** |
| **Exclusion** | Other neuromuscular, medical, orthopaedic causes |

## 5 Management

```
Polio foot deformity — goal: painless, stable, plantigrade foot
├─ First → ORTHOTICS, physio, stretching, bracing
├─ Flexible, failed nonop → SOFT TISSUE: release, lengthening, tendon transfer
└─ Fixed / rigid → BONY: osteotomy, wedge resection, arthrodesis
       └─ Adult, flail (TA + TP grade 2), rigid equinus → PANTALAR ARTHRODESIS
```

### Operative criteria

| Factor | Rule (TO unless stated) |
|---|---|
| **Flexible deformity** | **Soft-tissue procedures** — release, lengthening, **tendon transfer** |
| **Fixed / rigid deformity** | **Osteotomy + arthrodesis**; wedge resection **extra-articular** where possible |
| **Arthrodesis timing** | **Reserved until skeletal maturity** |
| **Tendon transfer** | Needs **supple joints** + donor power **grade IV–V (normal / near normal)** · document **manual muscle testing** first |
| **Younger child** | Release / lengthen contracted tendons · **older child → bony surgery** |
| **Orthoses fail to give satisfactory standard of living** (Orthobullets) | **Contracture release, tendon transfer, arthrodesis** |

**Lightweight orthoses** — **first line** for polio foot deformity · keep patient functionally independent

### Equinocavovarus correction

**Rigid equinus, 18 yr, TA + TP grade 2 (TO)** — **pantalar arthrodesis** (TO)\
✗ Wrong options: tendon transfer (rigid + weak donors) · ankle arthrodesis alone · triple (second best)

**Pantalar vs triple (TO)** — pantalar = **triple + tibiotalar arthrodesis** → better stability · triple without muscle balance → **late secondary ankle deformity** (unbalanced forces on mobile joint proximal to fusion) · **pantalar > triple** (TO)

**Flexible equinocavovarus, failed nonop** — **Achilles tendon lengthening + tibialis posterior tendon transfer (to dorsum) + plantar fascia release** · **FDL → EDL** for claw toes

| Component | Procedure |
|---|---|
| **Equinus** | **Achilles lengthening** |
| **↓ Dorsiflexion (TA weak)** | **Tibialis posterior transfer to dorsum** |
| **Cavus** | **Plantar fascia release** |
| **Claw toes** | **FDL → EDL transfer** |

**✗ Flexible-foot traps** — **triple arthrodesis** / subtalar arthrodesis + medial cuneiform osteotomy (for **fixed** deformity) · **medializing calcaneal osteotomy + FDL transfer** (tibialis posterior insufficiency) · **lateralizing calcaneal osteotomy + tibialis anterior transfer** (TA usually weak)

### Post-polio syndrome – management

**Nonop (first line)** — **limited exercise with rest periods** ± lightweight orthosis · **exercise to sub-exhaustion levels** → tone muscle without breakdown · maintain, don't overuse

**Operative** — tendon transfers, contracture releases, arthrodesis to optimise function

**Drugs (TO)** — **no effective treatment** · lamotrigine, **IVIg** some success · analgesics / antidepressants (amitriptyline, duloxetine, codeine) — may worsen fatigue (TO)

**Team (TO)** — physio, OT, speech, respiratory, podiatry, psychology, dietetics, pain, orthotics (TO)

## 6 Complications

### Post-polio syndrome (PPS)

**Nature** — **aging phenomenon**: more nerve cells inactive with time · **NOT viral reactivation** / chronic infection

**Incidence** — **up to 50%** of polio cases (Orthobullets, OB) · **20–85%** depending on criteria (TO) — read the options

**Onset** — **≥ 15 yr after initial infection** (TO) · **20–40 yr after** (OB) · **after middle age** (Orthobullets) · **not 5–10 yr after paralytic polio** (TO)

**Severity (TO)** — **more severe after acute / severe initial disease**; minimal initial symptoms → mild PPS (TO)

**Risk factors (TO)** — **female sex** + **respiratory symptoms** = most important · permanent disability, severe residual weakness, aging, muscle overuse / disuse, immune reaction (TO)

**Motor features** — **new weakness**, ↓ endurance, atrophy, **myalgia**, fasciculations, **fatigue**, cold intolerance, dysarthria, **dysphagia**, respiratory compromise · ↑ difficulty with ADLs

**Sensory symptoms (TO)** — **frank sensory deficits + paraesthesias occur in PPS** (sensory evoked potential changes ↔ cord atrophy on MRI) · contrast: acute polio = **normal sensation** (Orthobullets) (TO)

**Cognitive (TO)** — word-finding difficulty, poor concentration, memory impairment, mood change → social isolation (TO)

**Counselling a recovered child** — **may experience muscle weakness and fatigue as an adult**\
✗ Wrong options: no risk · safe after skeletal maturity · certain paralysis + avoid sport · wheelchair by 25

## X vs Y

| | **Acute polio** | **Post-polio syndrome** |
|---|---|---|
| Cause | Active poliovirus infection | **Aging of depleted motor units** (✗ Wrong options: reactivation) |
| Timing | 7–10 day acute stage | **≥ 15 yr** (TO) / **20–40 yr** (OB) later |
| Weakness | **Flaccid, asymmetric, proximal** | New, gradual, previously affected muscles |
| Reflexes | **Absent DTRs** | — |
| Sensation | **Normal** | **Sensory symptoms possible** (TO) |

| | **Flexible deformity** | **Fixed deformity** |
|---|---|---|
| Surgery | **Soft tissue** — TAL, PT transfer, plantar fascia release | **Bony** — osteotomy, **triple / pantalar arthrodesis** |
| Age | Younger | Older child / **after maturity** |
