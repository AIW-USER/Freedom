# High Ankle Sprain & Syndesmosis Injury

*Foot & Ankle · 27 / 48*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Incidence** — **0.5% of ankle sprains** without fracture · **13% of all ankle fractures**

**Associated injuries** — OCD **15–25%** · peroneal tendon injury **up to 25%** · **deltoid ligament** · loose bodies · fractures: **Weber C**, Weber B, 5th MT base, anterior process calcaneus, lateral / posterior process talus

### Anatomy – distal tibiofibular syndesmosis

| Ligament | Origin → insertion | Key fact |
|---|---|---|
| **AITFL** | **Chaput tubercle** (anterolateral tibia) → **Wagstaffe tubercle** (anterior fibula) | Injured in isolated high ankle sprain |
| **PITFL** | **Volkmann tubercle** (posterior tibia) → posterior lateral malleolus | **Strongest component** |
| **Interosseous ligament (IOL)** | Distal continuation of IOM | **Main restraint to proximal migration of talus** |
| **Interosseous membrane (IOM)** | — | Disrupted → synostosis risk |
| **Inferior transverse ligament (ITL)** | — | — |

![Syndesmosis anatomy — IOM, IOL, AITFL, PITFL](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob7029-syndesmosis-anatomy-ap.jpg)

**Not syndesmosis** — **ATFL** (anteroinferior fibula → **talar neck**) · CFL, PTFL (lateral complex) · **bifurcate (Chopart) ligament** (subtalar + midtarsal) · IPR (peroneal tendons)

**Fibular incisura morphology** — **crescentic commonest (61.3%)** · trapezoid · chevron · widow's peak · **flat least (3.1%)**

### Biomechanics

**Function** — maintains tibia–fibula integrity · resists **axial, rotational, translational** forces · widens **1 mm** in normal gait

| Ankle position | Talus in mortise | Distal fibula |
|---|---|---|
| **Dorsiflexion** | **Wider anterior talus** engages | **External rotation** + proximal, posterolateral translation |
| **Plantarflexion** | Narrow posterior talus | **Internal rotation** + distal, anteromedial translation |

**Inversion / eversion** — mainly subtalar → little effect on fibular rotation · **supination** (PF + inversion + adduction) → fibular IR

**Deltoid** — **indirectly stabilises** medial mortise · **superficial = restrains eversion** · **deep = prevents excessive ER**

| Sectioned (Massri-Pugin, cadaver) | Result |
|---|---|
| Isolated AITFL or PITFL | **No significant instability** |
| **AITFL + PITFL + IOL** | Diastasis **posterior incisura only** |
| **All syndesmotic + deltoid** | Diastasis **anterior + posterior** incisura |

**Instability** — needs rupture of **≥ 2 ligaments** (Rammelt) · instability ↑ **immensely** once **deltoid** also cut (Zalavras)

## 2 Mechanism

**Commonest** — **external rotation** of foot (or **foot planted, body internally rotates**) → talus rotates laterally, pushes fibula away from tibia

**Consequences** — ↑ tibial compressive stress · lateral fibular subluxation · mortise incongruence

**Eversion injury** — medial + lateral pain, positive calf squeeze

## 3 Presentation

**Symptoms** — **anterolateral ankle pain proximal to AITFL** · ± medial tenderness / swelling · **difficulty bearing weight** (lateral sprains usually can WB)

**Clue** — nagging pain with dynamic moves, **relieved by tight circumferential wrap above ankle** → **AITFL** (wrap holds fibula in incisura)

**Syndesmosis tenderness** — **single best predictor of return to play**

| Test | Technique | Positive |
|---|---|---|
| **Squeeze (Hopkin)** | Compress tibia + fibula at **midcalf** | Pain at syndesmosis |
| **External rotation stress** | **Knee + hip 90°**, ankle neutral; ER / dorsiflex foot | Pain / widening |
| **Cotton (lateral stress)** | **Lateral pull on fibula** | Syndesmosis widening |
| **Fibular translation** | AP drawer on fibula, tibia fixed | ↑ translation + pain · **most false positives** (Beumer) |

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Diagnosis – examination, stress radiographs & MRI; Maisonneuve

**Views** — AP, lateral, mortise ankle **+ AP, lateral entire tibia / fibula** (**proximal fibula fracture = Maisonneuve**)

**Optional** — **external rotation stress view** · **gravity stress view** (deltoid competence) · **contralateral ankle** (widening vs normal variant)

**Persisting WB pain 2 wk after "sprain"** — **ankle stress radiographs + ipsilateral tibia-fibula radiographs** (bilateral comparison)\
✗ Wrong options: boot WBAT · NWB boot without further films · MRI first · tib-fib films alone

**Fibular fracture + syndesmotic injury** — **ORIF fibula, then assess syndesmosis** (repair may stabilise; most still need fixation)

### Key radiology findings

| Measurement | Normal | Injury |
|---|---|---|
| **Tibiofibular overlap** | **> 6 mm AP** · **> 1 mm mortise** · **> 10 mm AP** (Orthobullets ligament note) | ↓ |
| **Tibiofibular clear space** | **< 6 mm AP + mortise** · **< 5 mm** (Orthobullets ligament note) | ↑ |
| **Medial clear space** | **≤ 4 mm** (best on mortise) | ↑ · good specificity, **very poor sensitivity** |
| **Lateral tibiofibular ratio** | APTF ratio avg **0.94**; anterior fibular cortex crosses **middle of tibial shadow** | **Most reliable plain-film measure of reduction** |

![AP measurements — 1 tibiofibular overlap, 2 tibiofibular clear space, 3 medial clear space](../../MCQ/attachments/q217419-a-syndesmosis-ap.jpg)

**Clear space (TO)** — **> 5 mm on mortise, 1 cm above joint** = syndesmosis open · loss of TF overlap on AP abnormal (TO)

**MRI clue (TO)** — **posterior malleolar marrow oedema without fracture** → syndesmotic injury · read syndesmotic ligaments on **axial slices above the talus**; normal striated look ≠ tear (TO)

**Adolescent equivalent (TO)** — **Tillaux (Chaput tubercle) avulsion** (TO)

![Lateral tibiofibular ratio — segment 4 (anterior tibia → anterior fibula) over 5 (anterior fibula → posterior tibia)](../../MCQ/attachments/q217419-b-x-ray-of-normal-ankle-lateral.jpg)

**AP measurements** — limited sensitivity, poor reliability · **cannot reliably predict** injury (Krähenbühl)

| Study | Use |
|---|---|
| **CT** | Suspicion with normal X-rays · **more sensitive than X-ray for minor injury** · **post-op check of reduction** · Wagstaffe tubercle (AITFL insertion) on axial |
| **MRI** | Suspicion with normal X-rays · **> 90% sensitivity / specificity** (Han) · **\~100%** (Krähenbühl) — diagnostic tool of choice |
| **Lambda sign (coronal MRI)** | **Hyperintense signal contiguous between mortise and tibiofibular articulation** (OB) · **> 2 mm diastasis on coronal** (TO) · "**highly sensitive + specific**" (Orthobullets) · not an independent predictor, sensitive + specific **with positive exam** (Ryan) |
| Deltoid discontinuity on MRI | Poor indicator — no deltoid ruptures in Han's 8 syndesmotic injuries |

![Coronal T2 MRI lambda sign — fluid tracking from mortise into syndesmosis](../../MCQ/attachments/q212100-illa-lambda-sign-illustration.jpg)

## 5 Management

```
Syndesmotic injury
├─ No diastasis, stable ankle → NWB CAM boot / cast 2–3 wk
├─ Unstable on stress views / refractory sprain → syndesmotic fixation
└─ Fracture + syndesmosis → fix fracture, then stress test
       ├─ stable → no syndesmotic fixation
       └─ unstable → screw or suture button
```

### Operative criteria

| Nonoperative | Operative (screw or suture button) |
|---|---|
| **Sprain without diastasis or ankle instability** | **Sprain (no fracture) unstable on stress radiographs** |
| — | **Sprain refractory to conservative treatment** |
| — | **Fracture still unstable after fracture fixation** |
| — | **PER injuries · suprasyndesmotic (Weber C) fractures** (TO) |
| — | **Medial clear space widening > 2 mm after fibular fixation** (TO) |

**Nonop** — delayed WB until pain-free · PT in **brace limiting ER** · recovery **notoriously prolonged + variable**, up to **2× standard sprain**

### Intraoperative assessment & reduction

**Best intra-op test** — **external rotation stress test**: **fewest false positives, smallest inter-observer variance**\
✗ Wrong options: squeeze · fibular translation · Cotton · anterior drawer

**Cotton test** — previously reported least false positives · produces **greater tibiofibular clear space** widening than ER test in Weber C (Stoffel) · ER stress gives **35% more medial clear space** widening (Matuszewski) — read the options

**Dorsiflexion** — **externally rotates fibula** (wide anterior talus) · aids rotational reduction · **maximal dorsiflexion before screw not required** (Tornetta: PF fixation did not limit DF)

**Clamp** — **lateral malleolar ridge of fibula → centre of AP width of tibia** = most accurate reduction (Gardner)

**Goal** — restore **fibular length, rotation, position in incisura** · **outcome depends on reduction, not fixation type**

**Sequence (TO)** — **fibula first** (length + rotation) → posterior malleolus → medial malleolus → **test syndesmosis (Cotton)** → fix if unstable

**Cotton test (TO)** — **lateral pull on fibula** with bone hook → look for **medial clear space widening** · best intra-op assessment of syndesmosis (TO)

**Persistent talar tilt after suture button** — **deltoid** incompetent\
✗ Wrong options: interosseous (addressed by button) · bifurcate · CFL · PTFL

**Reconstruction order** — proximal → distal: **IOL + AITFL first** → **repair deltoid** if medial talar tilt persists (Wake)

**AITFL repair** — restores mortise stability · syndesmotic fixation unnecessary once repaired · reconstruct with **4th EDL autograft** or suture (Nelson)

### Fixation – screw vs suture button

**Screw technique** — **two 3.5 or 4.5 mm** screws · **3 or 4 cortices** · **2–5 cm above plafond** (Orthobullets) · **2–3 cm, parallel to joint, angled 30° anterior** (TO) — read the options · NWB **6–12 wk**

**Screw technique (TO, ankle fracture lecture)** — **3.5 mm** · **3–4 cm above talar dome** · **30° posterior → anterior**, parallel to joint · **3 cortices** · **routine removal at 3 months** · 3.5 or 4.5 mm cortical screw both used (TO)

**Screw angle > foot position (TO)** — **angle matters more** than ankle position; **neutral or dorsiflexion acceptable** (TO)

**No difference** — stainless steel vs **titanium** · **3 vs 4 cortices** (pullout) · **bioabsorbable** similar · two screws preferable

| | **Syndesmotic screw** | **Suture button (TightRope)** |
|---|---|---|
| **Malreduction** | **Higher — up to 40%** (**39%** vs 15%, Sanders RCT) | **Lower** |
| **Reoperation / implant removal** | **30%** — often requires removal | **4%** — **no removal needed** |
| Function | Same | Same · early results: **earlier return to activity** |
| Mechanics | Medial + anterior talar translation | Closer to native |
| Zone 2 fibular screws | ↑ malreduction | — |

## 6 Complications

| Complication | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Malreduction** | Commoner than believed · **up to 40% with screws** |
| **Missed injury** | **End-stage ankle arthritis** |
| **Chronic instability** | Inadequate reduction → instability + post-traumatic arthritis |
| **Tibiofibular synostosis** | **\~10% after Weber C** (Orthobullets) · 15 / 230 operative ankle fractures, mostly Weber C (Albers) |

### Post-injury synostosis / heterotopic ossification

**Cause** — high ankle sprain with **IOM disruption** · prior **tibial stress fracture** (Henry) · Weber C fixation

**Timing** — HO develops within **6–12 months**

**Presentation** — **activity-related leg / anterior shin pain** for 1–2 yr · **loss of ankle dorsiflexion** + tib-fib motion · often **asymptomatic**; most return to sport

**Treatment** — symptomatic first · **surgical excision** (anterolateral approach) only for **persistent pain despite nonop + no uptake ("cold") on bone scan**

**Next step, synostosis with ongoing uptake** — **observation + repeat bone scan → resect when no uptake**

**✗ Synostosis traps** — **indomethacin 25 mg TID 6 wk** + **700 cGy** = HO prophylaxis after acetabular surgery · **60 Gy** (sarcoma / myeloma; wounds won't heal) · chemotherapy (only with biopsy-proven neoplasm) · excise when "hot" · instability or ↓ plantarflexion as indication

## X vs Y

| | **High ankle sprain** | **Low (lateral) ankle sprain** |
|---|---|---|
| Ligaments | **AITFL**, PITFL, IOL, IOM, ITL | **ATFL, CFL, PTFL** |
| Mechanism | **External rotation** | **Inversion** |
| Weight bearing | **Difficult** | Usually possible |
| Test | Squeeze, ER stress | Anterior drawer |
| Recovery | **≈ 2× longer** | Shorter |

| | **ER stress test** | **Cotton test** |
|---|---|---|
| Manoeuvre | ER foot, tibia stabilised | Lateral pull on fibula |
| Intra-op | **Fewest false positives, least variance** (recent) | Least false positives (older) · more clear-space widening in Weber C |

## How it's asked

**"MRI clue to syndesmotic injury without fracture?"** → **posterior malleolar marrow oedema** (TO)

**"CT arrow at Wagstaffe tubercle — structure + next step?"** → **anterior inferior tibiofibular ligament (AITFL); ORIF of the fibula with subsequent syndesmotic assessment**

**"Foot planted, body turned, better with a tight wrap above ankle — injured?"** → **AITFL**

**"Sprain, WB pain at 2 wk, ?MCS widening — next?"** → **ankle stress radiographs and ipsilateral tibia-fibula radiographs**

**"Most sensitive + specific imaging finding?"** → **coronal MRI hyperintense signal contiguous between mortise and tibiofibular articulation** (lambda sign)

**"Intra-op test with fewest false positives?"** → **external rotation stress test**

**"Manoeuvre that externally rotates the fibula?"** → **ankle dorsiflexion**

**"Most reliable X-ray measure of syndesmotic reduction?"** → **lateral tibiofibular ratio (measurements 4/5)**

**"Persistent talar tilt after suture button?"** → **deltoid**

**"Syndesmotic screw direction?"** → **30° posterior → anterior, parallel to joint** (TO)

**"Indication for syndesmotic fixation after fibula ORIF?"** → **medial clear space widening > 2 mm** (TO)

**"True of syndesmotic screws?"** → **higher rate of malreduction with screw fixation versus flexible fixation**

**"Syndesmotic synostosis, pain, uptake on bone scan — next?"** → **observation and repeat bone scan, then surgical resection if no increased uptake**

**"Indication + timing for synostosis excision?"** → **persistent pain despite non-operative treatment and no uptake on bone scan**

**"Single best predictor of return to play?"** → **syndesmosis tenderness**
