# Turf Toe

*Foot & Ankle · 48 / 48*

## 1 Basics

**Definition** — **hyperextension (hyperdorsiflexion) injury to the plantar plate + sesamoid complex** of the **hallux MTP joint**

**Epidemiology** — **contact sports on rigid / artificial turf** · **flexible cleats** · **football** (first described **1976**, collegiate players) · **up to 45% of NFL players** · **running backs + quarterbacks** · games > practice

**Surgery needed** — **< 2%** · **1.7%** of turf toe injuries → **< 5% need surgery** ✓

**Lesser toes** — great toe = commonest, but **plantar plate tears also occur in lesser toes** → **crossover toe**

### Anatomy – hallux MTP stabilisers

| Stabiliser | Components |
|---|---|
| Osseous | MT head – proximal phalanx articulation |
| **Tendons** | **FHB** (contains **tibial + fibular sesamoids**) · **abductor hallucis → medial sesamoid** · **adductor hallucis → lateral sesamoid** |
| **Ligaments** | **Medial + lateral collateral** · intermetatarsal · **intersesamoid** · sesamoid-phalangeal · accessory sesamoid |
| **Plantar plate** | Joint capsule · attaches to **transverse head of adductor hallucis, flexor tendon sheath, deep transverse intermetatarsal ligament** |

**Plantar plate** — **main stabiliser of the MTP joint**

![Hallux MTP ligaments — medial / lateral sesamoid-phalangeal, intersesamoid, accessory sesamoid, collateral ligaments](../../MCQ/attachments/q218687-illa-toe-1.jpg)

## 2 Mechanism

**Mechanism** — **forefoot fixed to ground** + **hallux MTP hyperextended** + **axial load on the heel** (ankle / hindfoot plantarflexed) → attenuation or tear of plantar capsuloligamentous complex

**Two patterns** — **axial load on a dorsiflexed great toe (35%)** · **landing on another player's foot → hyperdorsiflexion (33%)** · most after contact with surface or another player

**Tear site** — capsuloligamentous-sesamoid complex tears **off the proximal phalanx (phalangeal end), not the metatarsal**

![Turf toe mechanism — axial load on the heel with the hallux hyperextended](../../MCQ/attachments/q8920-illa-mechanism.jpg)

| Associated injury | Note |
|---|---|
| Varus / valgus hallux MTP injury | **Traumatic hallux valgus** = turf toe variant (**MCL** rupture) |
| **Sesamoid fracture** | ± diastasis |
| **Proximal sesamoid migration** | Grade III |
| Cartilage injury / loose body | Debride |
| **Proximal phalanx stress fracture** | Often overlooked |
| **Hallux rigidus** | **Late sequela** |

## 3 Presentation

**Symptoms** — acute pain, stiffness, swelling · **inability to push off** · reduced agility · **pain at toe-off** · difficulty with **cutting drills**

**Exam** — **plantar swelling + ecchymosis** · **pain with passive toe extension / dorsiflexion** · **inability to hyperextend without pain** · gait: shortened time after heel rise

**Vertical Lachman** — **↑ dorsal / plantar laxity vs contralateral side** = plantar plate injury

**Lateral stress → excessive lateral deviation + lateral sesamoid subluxation** — **medial collateral ligament** rupture (label B)\
✗ Wrong options: A = IP joint · C = accessory sesamoid ligament · D = intersesamoid ligament · E = lateral sesamoid-phalangeal ligament

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Study | Finding / indication |
|---|---|
| **WB AP, lateral, oblique foot** | Usually **concentric** MTP (no dislocation seen) |
| **Sesamoid axial + forced dorsiflexion views** | **Sesamoid-to-joint distance vs contralateral** · **medial sesamoid displaced proximally** · sesamoid fracture |
| **Bone scan** | Negative X-ray + persistent pain, swelling, weak push-off → **↑ uptake at 1st MTP** · **proximal phalanx stress fracture** |
| **MRI** | **Most information** on plantar structures + cartilage · indicated for **abnormal X-ray**, **clinical partial / complete plantar plate tear**, + bone scan, persistent symptoms · **not routine** · rules out phalanx stress fracture |

**MRI in severe tear** — **plantar plate / intersesamoid ligament rupture** · osteochondral injury + contusion **plantar medial MT head** next to distal medial sesamoid · **dorsal capsule avulsion → slight plantar subluxation of proximal phalanx**

![Sagittal MRI — disruption of the hallux plantar plate](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob7011-27a-moved.jpg)

**Grade III X-ray** — **proximal retraction of sesamoids** vs normal side · MRI: **complete plantar plate disruption distal to the sesamoid**

![Grade III turf toe — left sesamoids retracted proximally vs normal right](../../MCQ/attachments/q5778-a-fig-a.jpg)

### Classification

| Grade | **Orthobullets** | **OB (clinical)** | Treatment (Anderson) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I** | **Sprain** of plantar plate | **Attenuation**, localised swelling, **minimal ecchymosis** | **Taping + early rehab**, early RTP |
| **II** | **Partial tear** | Partial tear, **moderate swelling**, **motion restricted by pain** | **Turf-toe plate / carbon-fibre orthosis** limiting extension, taping, RTP **\~2 wk** (often time off) |
| **III** | **Complete tear** | Complete disruption, **marked swelling / ecchymosis**, **hallux flexion weakness**, **frank MTP instability** | Nonop possible (**splint in plantarflexion**) · **direct primary repair** good in elite athletes, RTP **6–12 mo** |

**Evidence** — **fair** for nonop grade I–II · **insufficient** for operative grade III (Kadakia)

## 5 Management

```
Turf toe (most grade I–III)
├─ Acute → rest, ice, NSAIDs, immobilise in PLANTARFLEXION
├─ Grade I–II → TAPING + PT + RIGID / STIFF-SOLE ORTHOSIS
└─ Grade III / failed nonop
       ├─ Sesamoid retraction, capsular avulsion → CAPSULOLIGAMENTOUS REPAIR
       └─ Fragmented sesamoid → repair (screw / suture) or EXCISION
```

**Nonoperative (most injuries, grade I–III)** — rest, ice, **NSAIDs**, **taping**, **stiff-sole / rocker-bottom shoe**, walking boot · severe → **boot or short-leg cast 2–6 wk** · progressive motion once stable

**✗ Nonop traps** — **taping in acute phase** (vascular compromise with swelling) · **splinting in 5–10° dorsiflexion** (immobilise in **plantarflexion**) · **early motion in grade II–III**

### Operative criteria

| Indication for surgery (usually grade III) | Source |
|---|---|
| **Failed conservative treatment** | Orthobullets + OB |
| **Retraction of sesamoids** | Orthobullets + OB |
| **Sesamoid fracture with diastasis** · **diastasis of bipartite sesamoid** | Orthobullets + OB |
| **Traumatic bunion / traumatic hallux valgus** | Orthobullets + OB |
| **Loose fragments / bodies**, chondral injury | Orthobullets + OB |
| **Large capsular avulsion** · **vertical instability** | OB |
| Hallux toe deformity | Orthobullets |

**✗ Surgery traps** — **surgical repair as best initial treatment** of complete tear · **sesamoid excision as best initial treatment** (only failed nonop, depends on fragment size)

### Treatment – nonoperative vs capsuloligamentous repair

**Grade III, sesamoid retraction, failed nonop** — **capsuloligamentous repair**\
✗ Wrong options: **resection** (sesamoid fracture / fragmentation) · **tendon repair / tenotomy** (no tendon disruption or contracture) · **internal fixation** (no fracture)

**Running back, 1 wk, sesamoids aligned, MRI plate intact with signal (low–mid grade)** — **taping the first MTP + physical therapy + rigid orthotic**\
✗ Wrong options: bone scan (MRI already done) · **1st MTP arthrodesis** / **lateral closing wedge osteotomy** (hallux varus options) · **sesamoidectomy** (sesamoid fracture)

**Technique** — **medial plantar incision** · **repair or excision of sesamoid** by fragmentation · **headless screw or suture** for sesamoid fracture · synovitis / OCD → **debridement or cheilectomy** · **abductor hallucis transfer** if plantar plate / flexors irreparable

**Post-op** — immediate **NWB** → progressive ROM + PT · **RTP 3–4 months** (Orthobullets) · **6–12 months** for grade III repair (OB, Anderson) — read the options

**Traumatic hallux valgus** — **74% return to pre-injury level at \~3.4 months** · **¼ never return**

### Lesser-toe plantar plate injury (crossover toe)

**Hyperextension injury 1 yr ago → 2nd toe crossover (varus)** — exam: **↑ laxity of MTP with dorsal / plantar directed force (vertical Lachman)**\
✗ Wrong options: pain improves with hyperextension (it worsens) · valgus instability = MCL deficiency

**Pathology** — **transverse plantar plate tears** in all crossover specimens · severe → **mid-substance + collateral ligament tears**, complete disruption (Coughlin anatomical grading)

**Treatment** — **activity + shoe-wear modification** first → **direct plantar plate repair** (plantar or dorsal approach) or **advancement through bone tunnels**

## 6 Complications

| Complication | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Hallux rigidus** | **Late sequela** → **cheilectomy vs arthrodesis** by severity |
| **Proximal phalanx stress fracture** | **May be overlooked** → bone scan / MRI |
| Career impact | **Devastating** for professional athletes |

## X vs Y

| Lesser MTP pain | Provocation |
|---|---|
| **Plantar plate tear** | **+ vertical Lachman**, pain **worse with hyperextension** |
| **Early Freiberg's** | Pain with **distraction** |
| **Late Freiberg's** | Pain with **axial loading / compaction** |
| **MCL deficiency** | **Valgus instability** |

| | **Turf toe (plantar plate)** | **Sesamoid fracture** |
|---|---|---|
| Mechanism | Hyperextension + axial load | Same, ± diastasis |
| Imaging | Sesamoids **retracted proximally**, plate torn | Fracture line / fragments |
| Surgery | **Capsuloligamentous repair** | **Screw / suture repair or sesamoidectomy** |

## How it's asked

**"Hyperdorsiflexion, plantar ecchymosis, pain at toe-off, MRI plate torn — diagnosis?"** → **rupture of the plantar plate**\
✗ Wrong options: FHL tear · FHB tear · medial sesamoid fracture · MT head fracture

**"Lateral deviation on stress + lateral sesamoid subluxation — structure?"** → **medial collateral ligament (B)**

**"Crossover 2nd toe after old hyperextension — exam?"** → **increased laxity of the MTP joint with dorsal / plantar directed force**

**"Grade III with sesamoid retraction, failed nonop — next?"** → **capsuloligamentous repair**

**"Low–mid grade turf toe, MRI plate intact — next?"** → **taping the first MTP, physical therapy, rigid orthotic**

**"Turf toe treatment — true?"** → **less than 5% of injuries require surgery**
