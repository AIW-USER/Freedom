# Acquired Spastic Equinovarus Deformity

*Foot & Ankle · 4 / 48*

## 1 Basics

### Etiology

**Causes** — **cerebrovascular accident (CVA)** · **traumatic brain injury (TBI)** → **upper motor neuron** injury

**Commonest physical finding after CVA** — **spastic equinovarus** deformity of foot and ankle\
✗ Wrong options: flaccid · calcaneovalgus · equinovalgus

**Associated** — joint contractures · **knee hyperextension in stance**

### Pathophysiology

| Component | Overactive muscle |
|---|---|
| **Equinus** | **Gastrocnemius–soleus complex** |
| **Varus** | **Tibialis anterior** (relative overactivity) · lesser: **FHL, FDL, tibialis posterior** |

## 3 Presentation

**Symptoms** — deformity · difficulty with gait

**Exam** — **spastic equinovarus** · **↑ tone** · **hyperreflexia**

![Spastic equinovarus foot after stroke](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob7000-equinovarus.jpg)

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

**Diagnosis** — **clinical**: spastic equinovarus foot + prior CVA / TBI

### Key radiology findings

**Radiographs** — **AP, lateral, oblique** of foot and ankle

## 5 Management

```
Spastic equinovarus after CVA / TBI
├─ During neurologic recovery → PT, botulinum / phenol, AFO
└─ Fixed contracture after recovery, not braceable → surgery
       ├─ Equinus → Achilles lengthening (TAL)
       ├─ Varus → SPLATT
       └─ Recurrence despite soft-tissue surgery → osteotomies / fusions
```

### Nonoperative (first line)

**Therapy** — stretching + strengthening · maintain joint ROM · splinting

**Injections** — **phenol blocks** · **botulinum toxin**

**AFO** — use while in **bed or wheelchair**

**Timing** — **neurologic recovery 6–18 months after CVA** · **years after TBI** → operate only after recovery period

### Operative criteria

| Indication for surgery |
|---|
| **Fixed contractures persist after neurologic recovery** and **not braceable** |
| Functional deficits |
| **Skin problems** secondary to deformity / braces |
| Recurrence despite proper soft-tissue procedures → **osteotomies + fusions** |

### Post-stroke equinovarus – SPLATT with TAL

**Equinus** — **Achilles tendon lengthening (TAL)** (open / percutaneous) or **gastrocnemius recession**

**Varus** — **split anterior tibialis tendon transfer (SPLATT)**\
✗ Wrong options: medializing calcaneal osteotomy + FHL transfer (that's flatfoot)

**SPLATT technique** — tibialis anterior split → **lateral half to cuboid** through drill hole, sutured · ± lengthening / dorsal transfer of **PTT**

![SPLATT – lateral half of tibialis anterior to cuboid](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob7000-splatt-2.jpg)

**Bridle procedure** — **posterior tibialis tendon** through **interosseous membrane** to dorsum → dual anastomosis to **tibialis anterior** + rerouted **peroneus longus** (in front of lateral malleolus)

## 6 Complications

**Hindfoot valgus** — overcorrection from **inadvertent PTT lengthening**

### Prognosis after CVA

| Outcome | Rate |
|---|---|
| **Normal ambulation** | **25%** |
| **Some level of ambulation** | **75%** |
| Neurologic recovery | **6–18 months** (CVA) · years (TBI) |

## X vs Y

| | **SPLATT** | **Bridle** |
|---|---|---|
| Tendon moved | **Lateral half of tibialis anterior** | **Tibialis posterior** |
| Route / insertion | **Cuboid** (drill hole) | Through **interosseous membrane** → dorsum, anastomosed to TA + rerouted PL |
| Corrects | **Varus** (spastic) | Foot drop / varus |

## How it's asked

**"Foot deformity after stroke?"** → **spastic equinovarus**

**"Post-stroke, correctable contracture, brace skin problems — add to TAL?"** → **SPLATT**\
✗ Wrong options: medializing calcaneal osteotomy + FHL transfer

**"Varus after CVA — prime mover?"** → **tibialis anterior** (relative overactivity)

**"Equinus after CVA — cause?"** → **gastrocnemius–soleus overactivity**

**"Surgery indicated when?"** → **fixed contracture after neurologic recovery, not braceable**

**"Neurologic recovery after CVA takes?"** → **6–18 months**

**"Hindfoot valgus after SPLATT?"** → **inadvertent PTT lengthening**
