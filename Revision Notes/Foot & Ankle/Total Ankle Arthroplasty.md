# Total Ankle Arthroplasty

*Foot & Ankle · 47 / 48*

## 1 Basics

**Role** — **alternative to ankle arthrodesis for end-stage ankle OA** · aims to **preserve functional ROM** sacrificed by fusion

**Ankle OA** — typically **post-traumatic** · less frequent than hip / knee OA · nonop: gauntlet brace, PT, steroid injection, NSAIDs

**Indications (TO)** — primary OA · **post-traumatic (commonest in India)** · **RA with stable, well-aligned hindfoot = best indication** (TO)

**Classification factors (TO)** — cemented vs uncemented · **2 vs 3 components** · constrained / semi- / unconstrained · congruent vs incongruent · anatomic vs non-anatomic · **fixed vs mobile bearing** (TO)

### Implant generations

| Generation | Design | Problem / feature |
|---|---|---|
| **1st** | Highly **constrained / semi-constrained, two-component**, **cemented** both sides | **High loosening**, wide osteolysis, subsidence, mechanical failure |
| **2nd** | **Two-component fixed-bearing**, **polyethylene bearing** built into talar or tibial component | Conservative bone cuts · **cementless press-fit, porous coating** for ingrowth |
| **3rd** | Adds a third component: **independent polyethylene mobile-bearing meniscus** | Ligaments for stability · anatomic balancing · minimal resection |

**Current standard (TO)** — **3-component, uncemented, mobile-bearing** = best kinematics, technically demanding (TO) · no clear design superiority; clinical results similar (TO)

**Three vs two components** — **no evidence three-component designs are better**

| Two-component | Three-component (mobile bearing) |
|---|---|
| **Agility** (cemented era; tibia titanium, central fins) | **STAR** (Scandinavian Total Ankle Replacement) |
| **INBONE** (tibial **IM stem**) | Mobility · Buechel-Pappas |
| **Salto Talaris** (**no modular polyethylene liner**, OB) · Eclipse | HINTEGRA · BOX |

**FDA-approved (Orthobullets)** — **STAR · INBONE · Agility · Salto Talaris**

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Study | Use |
|---|---|
| **WB AP + lateral ankle** | Pre-op: **extent of arthritis** · non-WB views **underestimate joint space narrowing** |
| **MRI** (pre-op) | **Osteonecrosis** (amount), bone loss, **subchondral cyst size** |
| **CT with metal artifact-reducing protocol** | **Best study for extent of osteolysis** / loosening / subsidence in painful TAA |
| Bone scan | Infection **or** aseptic loosening but **cannot differentiate** · high false-positive + false-negative |
| SPECT | **Not indicated** for PJI |
| Flexion–extension X-rays + gutter exam | Impingement, arthrofibrosis |

### Diagnostic criteria – painful TAA & infection

**Painful TAA — first step** — **CBC, ESR, CRP** (rule out chronic infection) → aspiration + synovial fluid to confirm → then **CT (metal artifact-reducing)**

**Causes of failure** — **aseptic loosening**, **subsidence**, **impingement**, **arthrofibrosis**, **infection** · persistent pain after "successful" TAA is common

| Parameter | **Aseptic** (OB) | **PJI** (OB) |
|---|---|---|
| ESR / CRP | **19 mm/h / 1.1 mg/L** (normal) | **Elevated** |
| Serum WBC | **9K** | — |
| Synovial nucleated cells | **900 / mL, 44% PMN**, culture negative | **25,000 / µL, 85% PMN** · or **70K**, **gram-positive cocci in clusters** |
| Intra-op | — | **Purulence · > 5 PMN / hpf** |
| Next | **CT metal artifact-reducing** | **Explant + antibiotic spacer** |

| PJI timing | Definition | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **Acute** | **< 4 wk** from index | **I&D + polyethylene exchange** (option) |
| **Chronic** | **> 4 wk** | **Explant + antibiotic spacer** (biofilm on metal) |

### Heterotopic ossification classification (Brooker-based) (TO)

| Class | Finding (TO) |
|---|---|
| **0** | **No HO** |
| **I** | **Islands of bone in soft tissue** about the ankle |
| **II** | **Bone spurs from tibia or talus, posterior joint space ↓ < 50%** |
| **III** | Spurs **↓ posterior joint space > 50%**, **non-bridging** |
| **IV** | **Bridging bone** continuous **tibia ↔ talus** |

### Periprosthetic fracture classification (Manegold, OB)

| Fracture type | Location | Prosthesis stability |
|---|---|---|
| **1 Intraoperative** | **A Medial malleolus** | **S Stable** |
| **2 Postoperative trauma** | B Lateral malleolus | **U Unstable** |
| **3 Postoperative stress** | C Tibia · D Talus | — |

## 5 Management

### Operative criteria – patient selection

| | **Orthobullets** | **TO** |
|---|---|---|
| **Indication** | **Unilateral or bilateral end-stage ankle OA** | Degenerative, **inflammatory** or post-traumatic arthritis |
| **Ideal patient** | **Older (middle–old), low demand**, reasonably mobile, no major comorbidity · **normal / low BMI** · **well-aligned, stable hindfoot** · good soft tissues | **Older, thin, low-demand**, **minimal deformity**, **retained ankle ROM** |
| **Contraindications** | **Active infection** · **peripheral vascular disease** · inadequate soft-tissue envelope · **Charcot arthropathy** · insufficient bone stock · **severe osteoporosis** · **osteonecrosis of the talus** · **peripheral neuropathy** | **Age < 50** · poor compliance · **heavy industrial labourer** · heavy smoker · **uncontrolled diabetes with neuropathy** · **significant ankle instability** · **angular deformity > 10–15°** · vascular insufficiency · **obesity > 250 lb** · significant bone loss · **osteonecrosis (AVN talus)** · **active or previous infection** |

**RA — NOT a contraindication (TO)** — arthrodesis used to be standard · arthroplasty now chosen to **preserve motion + ↓ stress on midfoot and subtalar joints**\
✗ Wrong options: AVN talus · ankle instability · heavy labourer · young age

**AVN talus (TO)** — **avascular, fragmented, collapsed talar body → not a candidate → arthrodesis**

**Diagnosis vs outcome** — **best for primary OA** · **worse with post-traumatic + inflammatory arthritis** (prior surgery, tissue laxity) (OB) · TO lists **inflammatory** among ideal indications — read the options

**Coronal deformity** — **> 10–15° = contraindication** (TO) · **> 15° varus / valgus** did as well as neutral when corrected, third-generation implants (OB, Queen) — read the options

**Young, active, heavy job** — **high stress on prosthesis + bone–implant interface → ↑ failure** → fuse

```
End-stage ankle OA, failed nonop
├─ Older, low demand, aligned hindfoot, good bone → TOTAL ANKLE ARTHROPLASTY
├─ Young / heavy labourer, isolated tibiotalar → TIBIOTALAR ARTHRODESIS
│      (direct anterior or arthroscopic → keeps later conversion to TAA)
└─ Rigid flatfoot (PTTI IV) + tibiotalar OA → PANTALAR ARTHRODESIS
       └─ Supple hindfoot, PTT strong, no forefoot deformity → TAA + realignment
```

### TAR vs arthrodesis criteria (TO)

| Criterion (TO) | **Arthrodesis** | **TAR** |
|---|---|---|
| Age / demand | **< 50 yr**, high demand | **> 50 yr**, low demand (walking, golf, cycling) |
| Aetiology | Post-traumatic (young), neuromuscular | **Rheumatoid**, degenerative, **haemophilic** |
| **ROM** | **< 10°** | **> 15°** |
| Bilateral disease | No | **Yes** |
| Adjacent joint arthritis | Absent | **Present** |
| Deformity / instability | **> 15°**, severe | **< 10°**, mild, correctable |
| Minor: soft tissue · BMI · DM · compliance | Poor · **> 25** · uncontrolled · low | Good · **< 25** · controlled · high |

**TO contraindications** — absolute: active infection · **skeletal immaturity** · poor bone stock · **Charcot** · deformity **> 15°** · relative: previous infection · **smoking** · DM / vascular disease · neuromuscular imbalance · comorbidities · **AVN or infection → avoid TAR** (TO)

**If in doubt / all borderline → arthrodesis** (TO)

**Pre-op (TO)** — WB AP + lateral · **CT for adjacent joint arthritis** · **MRI for osteonecrosis + ligaments** · record ROM with **Iowa ROM device** / goniometer (TO)

### Patient selection & outcomes vs arthrodesis (deformity, flatfoot)

**49-yo construction worker, isolated tibiotalar OA, no subtalar pain** — **tibiotalar arthrodesis through direct anterior or arthroscopic approach** → allows **later conversion to TAA**\
✗ Wrong options: TAA (young, high demand) · TTC (no subtalar arthritis) · **medial malleolar osteotomy / transfibular approach** (prevents later conversion, ↑ wound complications after conversion) · repeat steroid

**Arthroscopic arthrodesis** — **< 15° coronal deformity** · **100% fusion** without graft (OB, Duan)

**PTTI stage IV, rigid hindfoot, tibiotalar OA, flatter despite Arizona brace** — **pantalar arthrodesis** (addresses arthritic joint + forefoot deformity)

| ✗ Option in stage IV rigid + ankle OA | Why wrong |
|---|---|
| **Medial slide calcaneal osteotomy + FDL transfer + LCL + Cotton + spring / deltoid recon** | Early disease; ignores tibiotalar OA |
| **Supramalleolar osteotomy** | Only when tilt comes from **distal tibia**; stage IV tilt = **deltoid insufficiency** |
| **TAA** | Deformity must be **reconstructed first** (staged or concurrent) |
| **Triple arthrodesis + deltoid reconstruction** | Only if **no ankle arthritis** (< 10° valgus) |

**Stage IV (Bluman)** — **rigid hindfoot → TTC or pantalar fusion** · **supple → realignment + deltoid reconstruction**

**Isolated TAA in valgus ankle with deltoid insufficiency (collapsing flatfoot, minimally correctable)** — **lateral polyethylene edge-loading + progressive lateral talar tilt**\
✗ Wrong options: durable neutral alignment + symmetric wear · medial gutter impingement from deltoid overconstraint · subtalar nonunion (no fusion done) · peroneal rupture from varus overcorrection

**Adjuncts to get plantigrade foot** — **deltoid reconstruction**, osteotomies, arthrodeses · **pain relief after tibiotalar injection = ankle is pain generator**

| Outcome | **TAA vs arthrodesis** |
|---|---|
| **Pain relief** | **Equivalent** |
| **Stairs (up + down)** | **TAA better** ✓ · better on **uneven surfaces** |
| ROM | **TAA greater** (✗ Wrong options: decreased ROM) |
| Gait | **TAA ↑ stride length**, gait velocity, **cadence** (✗ Wrong options: decreased stride length) |
| **Daily step count** | **Neither improves** |
| AOFAS / SF-36 | No difference (meta-analysis) |
| Reoperation / surgical complications | **Higher with TAA** |
| Adjacent joints | Arthrodesis → **premature OA of subtalar, TN, CC, NC, TMT, 1st MTP** (✗ Wrong options: knee) at 22 yr |

**Post-op ROM** — **pre-op ROM = best predictor** · minimal extra ROM gained · little high-quality TAA vs fusion evidence

**Survivorship** — **70–90% at 10 yr** · **> 80% at 5 yr if < 50 yo** (Orthobullets) · **70–98% at 3–6 yr, 80–95% at 8–12 yr** (OB, Easley) · **5-yr survival up to 60%, reoperation up to 30%** — worse than TKA / THA (OB) — read the options

**Outcomes (TO)** — **90% survivorship at 5 yr** (1105 cases; range 68–100%) · revision > THR / TKR (small bone stock, thin soft tissue) · **success = pain-free, mobile, plantigrade, ≥ 15° ROM** (TO)

### Surgical technique

**Approach** — **anterior, between tibialis anterior + EHL**

**Lateral approach (TO)** — **oblique fibular osteotomy**, fibula refixed · specific deformities · position supine, pad under hip, foot vertical, fluoroscopic check of cuts (TO)

**Balance (TO)** — balanced ligament tension (**especially deltoid**) · failure → **early loosening or instability** (TO)

**Goal** — restore **mechanical alignment** (guides for tibia, talus ± fibula cuts) · **ligament balancing** · **stable, neutral, plantigrade** ankle + hindfoot · may need ligament reconstruction, tendon transfer, osteotomy, **heel cord lengthening**, arthrodesis

**Soft tissue** — **long incision** (↓ skin tension) · **thick flaps** · minimise retractors · **avoid tibialis anterior sheath** (prevents **bowstringing** + wound problems)

**Technical errors** — prosthesis **too lateral** · **too small → subsides** · uncorrected varus / valgus · replacing an ankle **too anteriorly subluxated**

### Failure – loosening/subsidence work-up & salvage (TTC arthrodesis)

```
Failed TAA
├─ Infection (erythema, drainage, ↑ ESR/CRP, pus) → STAGED: SPACER + IV ABX → FUSION
├─ Severe talar loss / arthritic STJ / subsidence into STJ → TTC FUSION
│      └─ Large defect → FEMORAL HEAD ALLOGRAFT + nail / plate / nail-plate
└─ Good residual talus + healthy STJ → ISOLATED ANKLE FUSION
       ├─ Small defect → shortening / cancellous graft + anterior plate
       └─ > 2 cm defect → structural iliac crest / distal tibial allograft
```

**4 key decisions (Berkowitz)** — **rule out infection** → **ankle only vs ankle + subtalar** → **which bone graft** → **which fixation**

**Include subtalar joint (TTC) if** — **insufficient talar bone stock** · **talar subsidence crosses STJ** · **pre-existing STJ arthrosis**

| Bone graft | Use |
|---|---|
| **Particulate cancellous** | **< 2 cm** talar bone loss |
| **Bulk / structural allograft** | **> 2 cm** bone loss |
| **Femoral head allograft** | **Graft of choice for large talar loss / TTC** |
| **Campbell technique** | **Tricortical iliac crest wedges** |
| Fresh-frozen distal tibial allograft | Structural option |

**Fixation** — **nail · plate · nail + plate**

**9 yr, talar subsidence into STJ, infection negative** — **TTC arthrodesis with structural (femoral head) allograft**\
✗ Wrong options: revise talar component (subsided not loose, no bone stock) · revise tibial (not loose) · revise both (early failure, poor talar purchase) · TTC without / cancellous graft (only < 2 cm)

![Talar component subsidence into the subtalar joint with talar bone loss](../../MCQ/attachments/q5754-c-fig-c.jpg)

**Fusion outcomes** — isolated tibiotalar fusion: **1 nonunion** vs **5** with ankle–hindfoot fusion · **subtalar nonunion = commonest complication** of ankle–hindfoot fusion → separate STJ approach · **arthrodesis after failed TAA worse than primary arthrodesis**

**Revision TAA preferred when** — **sufficient bone stock**, adequate soft tissue, **preserved ROM**, compliance → **one-stage revision** (long-stem)

| Salvage choice | When |
|---|---|
| **One-stage revision** | Preserved ROM + **sufficient bone stock** |
| **Two-stage revision** | **Established infection** |
| **Tibiotalar arthrodesis** | Extensive bone loss but **preserved talar stock + no subtalar arthritis** |
| **Tibiotalar + subtalar (TTC)** | **Insufficient talar stock** or **subtalar arthritis** |
| Below-knee / transtibial amputation | **Non-salvageable** only |

**Painful TAA, labs + aspirate negative, osteolysis** — **CT with metal artifact-reducing protocol**\
✗ Wrong options: bone scan · repeat aspiration · routine follow-up (subsidence worsens) · two-stage revision

**Persistent worsening pain since TAA, bone loss on CT** — **CBC, ESR, CRP** first\
✗ Wrong options: two-stage · one-stage · tibiotalar arthrodesis · tibiotalar + subtalar arthrodesis

### Periprosthetic infection

**Deep infection** — **0.5–3.5%** · **risk factors: prior ankle surgery, diabetes, wound healing problems > 14 days**, low pre-op scores

**Gold standard** — **two-stage revision of all components: explantation + cement spacer** → reimplantation or arthrodesis

**Reimplantation** — **rarely successful**: **0% early prosthesis salvage**, **16% reimplantation** (Myerson) · persistent infection, bone loss from debridement, recurrence risk

**Myerson series (19 infections)** — 3 revision arthroplasty · 6 arthrodesis · **7 permanent spacer** · 3 transtibial amputation

**Diabetic, 2 yr post-op, 5 wk symptoms, 70K WBC, GPC clusters** — **antibiotic spacer; reimplantation unlikely**\
✗ Wrong options: IV antibiotics + retention (only for suppression) · **I&D + poly exchange — biofilm** · spacer with successful reimplantation · amputation first

**Salto Talaris, 18 mo, ↑ CRP / ESR, 25,000 WBC 85% PMN** — **two-component explantation + antibiotic spacer**\
✗ Wrong options: oral antibiotics 6 wk · SPECT · poly exchange (no modular liner) · below-knee amputation

**Salvage order** — preserve joint → then limb: **permanent spacer, fusion, reimplantation** before **amputation**

## 6 Complications

| Complication | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Delayed wound healing** | **Most common** (Orthobullets) · **4–17%** |
| Superficial wound infection | — |
| **Deep infection** | **0.5–3.5%** → explant, antibiotics, reimplant or **salvage arthrodesis** |
| **Sensory deficit** | **Up to 21%** · anterior incision near **SPN + DPN** |
| **Intra-op fracture** | **Medial > lateral malleolus** |
| **Component loosening** | **Talar fails more than tibial** |
| **Subsidence** | → convert to fusion |
| **Osteolysis** | **Polyethylene wear** → large expansive cysts in tibia / talus · **CT metal artifact reduction** |
| **Heterotopic ossification** | **3.8–82%** (TO) |
| **Chronic anteromedial pain** (TO) | Impingement / **oversized implant** |
| **Gutter (malleolar) pain** (TO) | Malposition / residual osteophytes |
| Infection (TO) | Thin soft tissue — **Staph aureus** |
| Instability (TO) | Inadequate ligament balance |

### Intraoperative malleolar fracture – prevention

**Most frequent intra-op complication (TO)** — **fracture of the medial or lateral malleolus**\
✗ Wrong options: fracture of the talus dome

**Hallmark intra-op complication (TO lecture)** — **medial malleolar stress fracture** from saw misplacement → **avoid notching** (TO)

**Most common complication (TO lecture)** — **wound problems + loosening** (TO lecture) · TO question marks wound dehiscence as not most common — read the options

**Commonest cause of revision (TO)** — **aseptic loosening** (most series) · **arthrofibrosis / impingement** (some series) · **debridement of heterotopic bone** = commonest reoperation (some series)

**Major reported complications (TO)** — **wound dehiscence + heterotopic ossification** · TO marks "**wound dehiscence is the most common postoperative complication**" as false and "**HO is rare**" as false · Orthobullets: **delayed wound healing most common** — read the options

**Rate** — **4% periprosthetic fracture** (2% intra-op, 2% post-op) · **66% medial malleolus** · most occur **intra-operatively**

**Site** — **narrow bone bridge** between ankle joint and outer cortex of tibia / fibula

| Cause of malleolar fracture | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Plafond cut overextended medially / laterally** (more medial cut) | ↑ medial malleolus fracture |
| **Tibial cut too proximal** | Narrows bone bridge |
| **Oversized tibial component** | High compressive load on medial malleolus |
| **Ex-fix over-distraction / early removal** | Sudden bending moments on malleoli |
| Uncontrolled saw-blade excursion · tibial component too medial / lateral | Also risk factors (OB) |

**Prevention** — **prophylactic K-wire (or screw) pinning of the medial malleolus before the osteotomy cut** ✓

### Postoperative heterotopic ossification grading (TO)

**Onset (TO)** — **as early as 3 months** · incidence **3.8–82%** · ↓ ROM, poor function

**Effect (TO)** — osteophytes → **malleolar impingement** → **pain on uneven terrain** → may need repeat surgery

**Risk (TO)** — **males 2× females** · large soft-tissue dissection · **bone debris left in field** · post-op haematoma · prolonged surgery · **wrong-sized components** · mispositioned components

**Spurs, posterior joint space ↓ < 50% (TO)** — **Grade 2**\
✗ Wrong options: 1 (islands) · 3 (> 50%) · 4 (bridging)

**Management (TO)** — **early active + active-assisted ROM** once confirmed → **surgical resection of mature HO** if symptomatic · exclude **component malalignment** (else recurs → **TAA revision**) · **indomethacin** prophylaxis works for THR, **doubtful for TAA**
