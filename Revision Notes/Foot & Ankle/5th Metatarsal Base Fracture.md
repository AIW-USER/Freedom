# 5th Metatarsal Base Fracture

*Foot & Ankle · 1 / 48*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Frequency** — among the **commonest foot fractures** · base fractures = **25% of all metatarsal fractures** · **90% are zone 1**

**Who** — **athletes, military recruits, manual labourers** · runners, dancers

### Anatomy & blood supply

**Regions** — tuberosity → base → metadiaphysis → diaphysis → neck → head

**Base + tuberosity** — **cancellous, highly vascular** · tuberosity = insertion of **peroneus brevis + lateral band of plantar fascia**

**Metadiaphysis** — **no tendon attachments** · **vascular watershed** between **metaphyseal vessels** and **diaphyseal nutrient artery** (terminal capillaries)

**Diaphysis** — dorsal curve in distal ⅓ · **peroneus tertius** inserts dorsally

**Shear force (OB)** — fracture lies in the **hypovascular zone between peroneus brevis and peroneus tertius insertions** → tendons shear across fracture site

**Function** — lateral border of forefoot · **lever during push-off**

**Mimics** — **open apophysis** or **os peroneum** → comparison radiographs

![Watershed zone between metaphyseal arteries and the nutrient artery](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob7031-shows-blood-supply.jpg)

### Genes & lab markers

**CMT (cavovarus → 5th MT stress fracture)** — **duplication on chromosome 17**\
✗ Wrong options: absent dystrophin (muscular dystrophy, equinovarus) · PITX1 (clubfoot) · PTT insufficiency (flatfoot) · tarsal coalition

**Nonunion / refracture labs** — **vitamin D deficiency (hypovitaminosis D)** · **thyroid** (hormonal) deficiency

**Female athlete triad (TO)** — disordered eating + **menstrual irregularities** + ↓ BMD → ↑ metatarsal stress fractures (TO)

## 2 Mechanism

| Zone | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| **1** | **Plantarflexion + hindfoot inversion** → avulsion by **peroneus brevis**, **lateral band of plantar fascia** or **long plantar ligament** |
| **2** | **Forefoot adduction** (Orthobullets, OB) · **strong abduction force to forefoot → bending moment at metaphyseal–diaphyseal junction** (TO) — read the options · acute inversion injury (OB) |
| **3** | **Repetitive microtrauma** → **stress fracture** |

**Associated conditions** — **Lisfranc / midfoot injury** · **lateral ankle ligament laxity** · **cavus foot + varus hindfoot**

**Hindfoot varus / cavus** — **overloads lateral column** → stress fracture distal to 4/5 articulation

## 3 Presentation

**Symptom** — **pain over lateral border of forefoot**, worse with WB · stress fracture: **antecedent pain**, rapid ↑ workload / training change

**Exam** — point tenderness at base · rare **skin tenting (zone 1)** · **varus hindfoot**, **cavus**, **lateral shoe-tread wear**, **5th MT head callosity**

**Check** — lateral ligament instability · is varus hindfoot correctable · **pain with resisted eversion** (peroneal weakness)

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Study | Use / finding |
|---|---|
| **X-ray AP, lateral, oblique foot** | **Diagnosis**; location + displacement decide fixation |
| X-ray chronicity | **Intramedullary sclerosis + no periosteal callus** = chronic · callus forms **medially first → laterally** |
| **Plantar fracture gap** | **Poor prognosis for nonop union** |
| Cavovarus signs | **Plantarflexed 1st MT**, **high Meary angle** |
| **Bone scan** | Suspected stress fracture, equivocal X-ray → **diaphyseal uptake** |
| **MRI** | Equivocal X-ray / scan → **high-signal stress reaction + oedema** |
| **CT** | **Healing in delayed / nonunion** or after fixation · **not at time of injury** (X-ray suffices) |
| **Screw planning (TO)** | **AP = screw diameter** · **lateral = screw length** (TO) |

### Lawrence & Botte anatomic zones (also called Torg anatomic zones)

| Zone | Name | Location | Key facts |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Pseudo-Jones** | **Proximal tubercle avulsion**; may enter **cubometatarsal (5th TMT) joint**; **proximal to 4–5 intermetatarsal joint** | Well vascularised; nonunion rare, **usually asymptomatic (fibrous)** |
| **2** | **Jones** | **Metaphyseal–diaphyseal junction**; **enters 4th–5th intermetatarsal articulation** | **Watershed**; acute; **nonunion 15–30%** |
| **3** | **Proximal diaphyseal** | **Distal to 4–5 articulation**; **proximal 1.5 cm of diaphysis (TO)** | **Stress fracture in athletes**; cavovarus, sensory neuropathy; ↑ nonunion |

![Anatomic zones 1–3 of the proximal 5th metatarsal](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob7031-illustration-of-the-torg-anatomic-classification-zone-1-incl.jpg)

**Jones location** — **1.5–3 cm** from proximal tip (OB) · **\~2.5 cm** distal to base (OB) · intra-articular at 4–5 **intermetatarsal** facet; does **not** extend distal to it or into metatarsocuboid joint

**Joint entered** — **Jones → intermetatarsal (4–5) joint** · **avulsion → tarsometatarsal joint** (OB) · one OB explanation calls the Jones joint "4th–5th tarsometatarsal" — read the options

**Zone 2 vs 3 (TO)** — some studies: **no distinction needed**, treatment + outcome the same (TO)

### Torg radiographic classification (stress fractures, zone 3)

| Type | Orthobullets | TO | Age |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I** | **Narrow line, no IM sclerosis** | **Acute fracture** | **Acute** |
| **II** | **Widened line + IM sclerosis** | **Periosteal reaction, widening, some IM sclerosis** | **Delayed union** |
| **III** | **Widened (obliterated) canal, no callus** | **IM sclerosis + blunted fracture edges** | **Nonunion** |

## 5 Management

```
Proximal 5th MT fracture
├─ Zone 1 → protected WB, stiff-soled shoe / boot
│      └─ rotational displacement / skin tenting → fixation
├─ Zone 2, non-athlete / recreational → NWB short-leg cast 6–8 wk
│      └─ failed 6–8 wk nonop or displaced → IM screw
├─ Zone 2, elite / competitive athlete → early IM screw
└─ Zone 3 → NWB cast ~8 wk
       └─ athlete, cavovarus, sclerosis (Torg II–III), not healing 8–12 wk → IM screw ± graft
```

### Operative criteria

| Nonoperative | Operative (IM screw) |
|---|---|
| **Zone 1** without rotational displacement — **even elite athletes / dancers** | **Zone 1 with rotational displacement or skin tenting** · gross displacement / articular involvement in young active (TO) |
| **Zone 2 in non-athlete / recreational athlete** (e.g. 45-yo recreational soccer) | **Zone 2 in elite / competitive athlete** (collegiate, Olympic, professional) — **even acute + nondisplaced** |
| **High-school / recreational athlete** until **6–8 wk nonop failed** | **Non-elite after failed 6–8 wk** nonop or **unacceptable displacement** |
| **Zone 3 acute**, low demand | **Zone 3 in athletes, cavovarus, sclerosis / nonunion (Torg II–III)** · **not healing at 8–12 wk (TO)** · occupation can't tolerate NWB (TO) |

### Zone 1 avulsion – weight bearing in a hard-soled shoe

**Treatment** — **protected / immediate WB in stiff- (hard-) soled shoe, postoperative shoe, walking boot or short-leg walking cast**, advance as pain allows

**Outcome** — **union by 8 wk** · fibrous unions rarely symptomatic · early RTW, symptoms up to **6 months**

**Soft dressing (TO)** — compressive dressing + WB in cast boot → **shorter recuperation, better foot score than cast** (TO)

**Elite dancer, zone 1** — **protected WB, stiff-soled shoe, gradual return**\
✗ Wrong options: immediate return to competition · NWB short-leg cast (unnecessary) · IM screw (true Jones only) · resection + peroneus brevis advancement

**57-yo, inversion off curb, zone 1** — **immediate WB in postoperative shoe**\
✗ Wrong options: acute fragment excision · IM screw · NWB cast · K-wires

**Which pattern → immediate WB in boot?** — **zone 1**\
✗ Wrong options: **Lisfranc** (unstable) · **displaced 1st MT** fracture (no intermetatarsal ligament → surgery) · zone 2 (NWB if nonop)

**Symptomatic zone 1 nonunion** — **fragment excision + peroneus brevis reattachment**\
✗ Wrong options: acutely

**Zone 1 ORIF (TO)** — rarely; **displaced intra-articular in highly competitive** · plate, screw or **tension-band** (TO)

### Acute Jones (zone 2) – IM screw in athletes vs casting

**Nonop** — **NWB short-leg cast 6–8 wk** (Orthobullets, OB) · then WB cast until union (TO) · advance WB with callus (\~4–6 wk) · **NWB cast > walking cast** for healing

| Zone 2 outcome | Value |
|---|---|
| **Nonunion, nonop** | **15–30%** (Orthobullets) · **10–30%** (OB) · **25%** (OB) · **up to 33%** (OB) · **> 30%** in some studies (OB) — read the options |
| **Union with cast** | **72–93%** (OB) · **healing rate 75%** (TO) |
| **Refracture after nonop** | **33%** (Orthobullets) · **\~30%** (OB) |
| **Union with IM screw** | **Approaching 100%** |
| **Reoperation after ORIF (Yates)** | **8.5%** — refracture, persistent nonunion, symptomatic hardware |

**What IM screw achieves** — **improved union rate** (primary advantage) · **faster / decreased time to union** · **earlier return to play / work** · minimises nonunion + prolonged activity restriction · better patient-reported scores in athletes

**What it does not do** — ↓ pain · ↓ HO · ↑ ROM · ↓ cost · **eliminate refracture** · avoid hardware complications · ↓ sural irritation (**more** with surgery) · prevent midfoot arthritis (not a sequela)

**✗ Casting traps** — **> 95% union** with cast (false) · **asymptomatic fibrous nonunion** with cast (that's zone 1; zone 2 nonunion usually symptomatic) · "casting unacceptable" (false — still valid if avoiding surgery) · "ORIF ≈ casting" (false) · "ORIF high hardware failure" (false)

**Bone graft** — **not required** in primary fixation · use in **revision / nonunion / refracture** (± BMAC)

**✗ Other wrong answers for athlete Jones** — **K-wires** (not supported) · **tubular plate** (long lines into zone 3 / multifragmentary diaphyseal) · **MRI** · **taping + immediate RTS** · **Brostrom** · **Lisfranc repair** · **midfoot fusion**

**TO answer key (TO)** — painful 5th MT base stress fracture in runner: **"All correct"** = **internal fixation always required** + **more in females with menstrual abnormalities** + **RICE is first treatment** (TO) · conflicts with OB/Orthobullets (cast for non-athletes) — read the options

**RICE (TO)** — rest, ice, compression, elevation = **primary treatment in all fractures** (TO)

**Competitive athletes (TO)** — **early ORIF to ↓ disability time** (TO)

### Stress fractures, delayed union & nonunion – fixation

**Zone 3 nonop (TO)** — chronic (canal obliteration, sclerosis) → **NWB casting \~8 wk** may work (TO) · Orthobullets: NWB 6–8 wk (often 6–7 wk) · **ESWT** union ≈ internal fixation (reports)

**Refracture common in zone 3 (TO)** — risk factors: **cavovarus**, **hypovitaminosis D**, **metatarsus adductus**, **curved 5th MT with prominent base** (TO)

**Two proven operations, zones 2–3 (TO)** — **IM screw (5.5 mm)** · **corticocancellous inlay bone graft** after clearing canal of sclerotic bone (TO)

**Delayed union after 6 wk cast, widened line (15-yo)** — **percutaneous IM screw**\
✗ Wrong options: ORIF (not needed) · **electrical stimulation alone** · more casting · hard-sole shoe

**Zone 3, 11 wk, sclerotic edges, minimal callus** — **ORIF ± bone grafting**\
✗ Wrong options: MRI · bone stimulator (controversial) · continued cast / boot

**HS quarterback, refracture after 8 wk NWB boot** — **IM compression screw**\
✗ Wrong options: plate · fragment excision (zone 1) · NWB + stimulator · midfoot fusion

**Zone 3, triathlete wants running at 6 wk** — **IM screw**\
✗ Wrong options: boot / hard-soled shoe / early PT (zone 1) · NWB cast (non-athletes)

**Nonunion risk factor (dancer, 4 months)** — **fracture location** (zone 2–3)\
✗ Wrong options: NSAIDs · high calcium · early WB · female sex

**Nonunion treatment** — **revision IM screw + bone graft** · plate = salvage after failed IM screw

### IM screw technique & failure

**Position** — supine, bump under hip, fluoroscopy

**Approach** — short incision **proximal to tuberosity**, parallel to sole · blunt dissection past **sural branches** · between **peroneus longus + brevis**

**Start point** — **"high and inside"** = **superior + medial** on tuberosity · K-wire not past MT curvature

**Screw** — **> 4 mm diameter** · **4.5, 5.5 or 6.5 mm partially threaded** (solid or cannulated) · **largest diameter the canal accepts** (max fill + compression, ↓ refracture) · **5.5 mm** (TO)

**Check** — **all threads cross fracture** · no distal cortical perforation · tap measures length

| Screw error | Result |
|---|---|
| **< 4.5 mm** (not including 4.5) | **Delayed / nonunion** |
| **Too long** | **Straightens curved shaft** → **fracture gapping + distal / medial cortical perforation** → failure, nonunion |
| **Too short** | **No compression** |
| **Prominent head** | **Sural nerve irritation** |

![Too-long screw straightening the shaft and gapping the fracture](../../MCQ/attachments/q219330-illa-screenshot-2023-12-19-at-5-07-22-pm.jpg)

**Rehab** — **NWB 1–3 wk** → protected WB (from \~2 wk) → **running + impact at 6 wk** if pain-free + **radiographic callus** · RTP average **4–6 wk**

**Failure risk factors** — **elite athletes** · **return to sport before radiographic union** · **early unprotected WB in athletes** · **too-long screw**\
✗ Wrong options: female · age < 20 or > 40 · solid vs cannulated · **4.5 mm** or **> 4.5 mm** screw · no bone graft · no ultrasound stimulator · partially threaded

**Bent 5.0 mm screw at 12 wk** — cause = **return to play before radiographic union** · fix = **remove + larger IM screw**

**NFL QB, failure at 8 wk** — **too long partially threaded screw**\
✗ Wrong options: WB at 2 wk · impact at 6 wk · no BMAC · largest screw

**IM screw vs plate** — IM **more rigid** + **less hardware prominence** (OB) · plate **equivalent strength** in early data (Orthobullets) — read the options

**Plate (3 mm, plantar-lateral)** — same indications; **salvage after failed IM screw**; reduces plantar gap / rotation

### Association with cavovarus foot (CMT)

**Cavus foot** — **↑ refracture** + ↑ nonunion even after successful treatment\
✗ Wrong options: ↓ nonunion · earlier / later RTS

**Varus hindfoot** — overloads lateral column → **zone 3 stress fracture** distal to 4/5 articulation

**Refracture treatment** — internal fixation **+ correct cavovarus** · **leave screw in until end of athletic career** (removal ↑ refracture)

## 6 Complications

| Complication | Key facts |
|---|---|
| **Nonunion** | Zone 2 **15–30%**; zones 2–3 (watershed); **screw < 4.5 mm**; vitamin D / thyroid deficiency → **revision IM screw + graft** |
| **Failure of fixation** | **Elite athlete**, **RTS before radiographic union**, **screw length** (too long → gapping) → revision fixation |
| **Refracture** | **33% zone 2 nonop**; cavovarus, stress fracture, vitamin D, **screw removal** → fix + correct cavovarus |
| **Painful hardware** | Rare; prominent head → **modified shoewear** |
| **Sural nerve injury** | **Dorsolateral branch within 2–3 mm of tuberosity** · prevent: **tissue protector + countersink head** |
| **Chronic pain** | Zone 1 nonunion after nonop → **fragment excision + peroneus brevis reattachment** |

## X vs Y

| | **Zone 1 (pseudo-Jones)** | **Zone 2 (Jones)** | **Zone 3 (diaphyseal stress)** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Site | Tuberosity | **Metadiaphysis** | **Proximal diaphysis** |
| Joint | 5th TMT (cubometatarsal) | **4–5 intermetatarsal** | Distal to 4–5 joint |
| Mechanism | **PF + inversion** | **Forefoot adduction** (abduction — TO) | **Repetitive stress** |
| Blood supply | Good | **Watershed** | Watershed |
| Nonop | **WB hard-soled shoe** | **NWB cast 6–8 wk** | **NWB cast \~8 wk** |
| Athlete | Still nonop | **Early IM screw** | **IM screw** |
