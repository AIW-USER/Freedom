# Osteochondral Lesions of the Talus

*Foot & Ankle · 37 / 48*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Incidence** — **69% of ankle fractures**, **70% of ankle sprains** (Orthobullets, OB) · **2–6% of all ankle injuries** (another OB) — read the options

**Unexplained chronic ankle pain** — OLT in **81%**

**Sprains that fail to settle** — **\~10%** → think **OLT** or persistent **instability**

**Bilateral** — **10%**

**Eponyms (TO)** — **König 1888** = OCD knee · **Kappis 1922** = applied to ankle · **Berndt & Harty 1959** = "**transchondral fracture**" + radiographic classification (TO)

**Sprain association (TO)** — **50% of acute ankle sprains** have an OLT (TO) · 70% (Orthobullets) — read the options

**Clinical clue (TO)** — **pain + effusion + locking = think OLT** · delayed recovery after "sprain" (TO)

**Medial vs lateral frequency** — **medial more common**: \~**68%** (OB) · 50–56% vs lateral 34–44% · paediatric medial **53–73%** · most lesions actually **medial + central** on MRI (classic teaching: posteromedial / anterolateral)

**Chronic lateral ankle instability (CLAI)** — **32–56%** develop OLTs · repetitive talar tilt + translation → **impaction on medial dome**

**Medial vs lateral (TO)** — medial **\~65–70%** · lateral **\~30–35%** (TO)

**Aetiology (TO)** — **trauma commonest (85%)**: **98% of lateral, 70% of medial** lesions traumatic · also chronic microtrauma / instability · **osteonecrosis** · systemic vasculopathy / endocrine · **malalignment → kissing / bipolar lesions** · genetic (laxity, collagen) (TO)

| Zone (Berndt & Harty 9-zone grid, TO) | Frequency |
|---|---|
| **Zone 4 (centromedial)** | **Most common (65–70%)** |
| **Zone 6 (centrolateral)** | **2nd most common** — answer if zone 4 not offered |

**Zones 4 + 6 (TO MRI lecture)** — lesions here **often need repair** (TO)

### Diagnosis & aetiology – medial vs lateral lesions

| | **Medial talar dome** | **Lateral talar dome** |
|---|---|---|
| Trauma history | **Usually none** (overuse / microtrauma / ischaemia) | **Usually traumatic** (inversion ± dorsiflexion) |
| Trauma rate | 80% (Flick) · 60% (McGahan) | 100% (Flick) · \~90% (McGahan) |
| Position | **More posterior** (posteromedial) | **More central or anterior** (anterolateral) |
| Size / depth | **Larger, deeper** | **Smaller, superficial / shallow** |
| Frequency | **More common** | Less common |
| Spontaneous healing | Better | **Lower** → harder with nonop |
| Behaviour | Less symptomatic | **More often displaced + symptomatic** |
| **Shape / force (TO)** | **Deep, cup-shaped** · **impaction** · usually **non-displaced** | **Shallow, wafer-like** · **shearing** · often **displaced** |

### Anatomy

**Talus** — frustum shape · **anterior broader than posterior** · **no muscular attachments**

**Cartilage** — covers **70% of talus** · **among thickest in body** · keeps tensile strength longer than femoral head with age

**Blood supply** — relies on **extra-osseous** supply · **deltoid artery supplies majority of talar body + dome**\
✗ Wrong options: release deltoid in medial malleolar osteotomy

**Mortise** — highly congruent · oriented **15° externally** from midsagittal line

## 2 Mechanism

**Lateral OLT** — **inversion + dorsiflexion + axial load** → shear of lateral dome

**Medial OLT** — **inversion + external rotation + plantarflexion + axial load** → shear of medial dome

**Pathophysiology after inversion injury** — **chondral shearing injury**\
✗ Wrong options: failure of embryonic segmentation = coalition · ATFL / CFL sprain · SPR tear

**Microtrauma** — repetitive microtrauma → ischaemic environment → loss of subchondral integrity → softened, disrupted cartilage

**Talar tilt** — **inverse relationship** between talar tilt on stress X-ray and OLT (bony constraint) · stable ankle + effusion after sprain → OLT

**Associated** — **cavus hindfoot** · **CLAI**

**LIFT lesion** — **lateral inverted osteochondral fracture of talus** after inversion · fragment's joint surface **faces talus, not tibia** · arthroscopy → **open reattachment** (most) or excision · good function at \~9 yr, osteophytes in all

## 3 Presentation

**History** — **inversion ankle sprain** · persistent pain after sprain that does not heal

**Symptoms** — pain over **ankle joint line** · **effusion** · **mechanical catching / locking**

**Exam** — effusion · **palpation rarely reproduces pain** · cavus · ROM limited by pain / effusion · **stable anterior drawer** (no instability) in classic stem

**Check ligaments** — **untreated lateral ligament insufficiency at time of OLT repair ↑ failure**

| Differential (option) | Why wrong |
|---|---|
| Tarsal coalition (failure of segmentation) | Sprains but ↓ subtalar motion |
| ATFL sprain | **Anterior drawer laxity in plantarflexion** |
| CFL sprain | **Anterior drawer laxity in dorsiflexion** · 2nd commonest sprained |
| SPR tear | Peroneal instability, **no effusion** |

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Study | Key facts |
|---|---|
| **Weight-bearing ankle X-ray** | First step · **often normal** · subtle lucency / fragmentation · shows **open physes** |
| **Bone scan** | Equivocal X-ray · **94% sensitive, 96% specific** |
| **CT** | **Subchondral bone + cysts** · pre-op planning · **less reliable for purely cartilaginous / nondisplaced** lesions |
| **MRI** | **Persistent pain after sprain, normal X-ray, stable ankle → next step** · size, oedema (may **overestimate**), stability · **fluid deep to subchondral bone = unstable** · **92% sensitive** for stability |

**Persistent pain 6 mo after sprain, no instability, normal X-ray** — **MRI of ankle**\
✗ Wrong options: continue PT · CAM boot 2 wk · steroid injection · diagnostic arthroscopy

**Imaging (TO)** — X-ray: **oblique + plantarflexion views** improve visualisation · "**plantarflexion view crucial for anterolateral lesions**" (TO — verify: plantarflexed mortise usually shows **posteromedial**, dorsiflexed shows anterolateral) · **Tc bone scan** = subtle early lesions · **CT 2 mm cuts** = size, displacement, cysts · MRI / MR arthrography = cartilage, oedema, cysts (TO)

**Grade 1 lesions (TO)** — marrow oedema / cartilage delamination **invisible on X-ray** (TO)

### Juvenile lesions – nonoperative care & prognostic MRI features

**Only factor correlated with stability** — **open physes / skeletal immaturity**\
✗ Wrong options: absence of effusion · subchondral sclerosis · perilesional marrow oedema · T1 homogeneity · cysts · signal rim · intra-articular body

**MRI prognostic factor** — **incomplete cartilage cap with fluid penetration** beneath lesion = **unstable, poor prognosis** · intact cap = stable\
✗ Wrong options: perilesional stress fracture · ATFL / CFL rupture (not prognostic) · physeal closure (seen on X-ray) · synovial hypertrophy

**Open physes vs MRI sign** — open physes = only correlate of stability (OB, Patel) · four MRI stability criteria: **cartilage defect, oedema-like signal, T2 bright rim, subchondral cysts** (Hembree) — read the options (physes are judged on **X-ray**)

**Juvenile OCD counselling** — **nonop frequently relieves pain but often no radiographic healing even at 6 months**\
✗ Wrong options: spontaneous healing common (rare) · healing < 12 wk · hyperbaric O₂ · fusion frequently needed

**Paediatric** — mean age \~13 yr · stable lesions in skeletally immature → **trial of nonop** · conservative treatment best in children

## 5 Management

```
OLT
├─ Acute, nondisplaced, incomplete → immobilise + NWB 6 wk
├─ Acute displaced, viable cartilage + bone (> 0.5 cm²) → surgical fixation
├─ Chronic, < 1 cm, or small fragment with little bone → arthroscopic debridement + marrow stimulation
├─ > 1 cm, intact cartilage cap → retrograde drilling ± bone graft
└─ > 1 cm displaced, shoulder, cystic, failed BMS / drilling → osteochondral graft (OAT, ACI, allograft)
```

**Treatment depends on** — **age, activity demands, lesion size, stability** · surgery if nonop fails after **6 months**

**Nonop (TO)** — **PTB brace = brace of choice** (avoids axial loading on talus) **4–6 wk** · restricted WB · NSAIDs, physio · **children stage 2–3 nondisplaced → immobilise** (TO)

**Operative indications (TO)** — symptomatic displaced · **acute stage 3–4 → early surgery** · **large / cystic > 1.5 cm² → surgery** (TO)

### Operative criteria

| Lesion | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Acute injury, nondisplaced fragment, incomplete fracture** | **Immobilisation + NWB** (cast / boot 6 wk → PT) · **45% good–excellent** (Orthobullets) · 45–50% (OB) |
| **Acute displaced, viable cartilage, adequate bone, > 0.5 cm²** | **Surgical fixation** (headless compression screws / absorbable pins) |
| **Chronic, < 1 cm**, displaced small fragment with minimal bone | **Arthroscopic removal of loose fragment, debridement + marrow stimulation** |
| Ideal for BMS | **< 10 mm diameter, < 100 mm² area, < 5 mm depth** · excellent < 1.5 cm², best < 1 cm |
| **> 1 cm with intact cartilage cap** | **Retrograde drilling ± bone grafting** |
| **> 1 cm + displaced, shoulder lesions**, salvage of failed BMS / drilling | **Osteochondral grafting** (OAT, ACI, bulk allograft) |
| **Contraindications to grafting** | **Diffuse ankle arthritis · bipolar kissing lesions · advanced talar dome osteonecrosis** |

**Early surgery favoured by** — **displaced fragment no longer congruent with talar dome**\
✗ Wrong options: lateral location alone · < 1 cm · presenting within 2 wk · subchondral sclerosis (chronic / contained)

**Surgery indicated** — chronic lesions · acute displaced fragments · higher grade · failed nonop · **loose bodies**

**Success rates** — excision + curettage + drilling **85%** · excision + curettage 78% · nonop **45%** · excision alone **38%** · arthroscopic excision + BMS **87%** (OB)

### Small lesions – arthroscopic marrow stimulation

**Chronic medial OLT < 1 cm, failed nonop** — **arthroscopic marrow stimulation** (microfracture)\
✗ Wrong options: total contact cast (diabetes) · arthrodesis (no arthritis) · open ACI (large lesions, not first line) · steroid injection (short-lived)

**Antegrade microfracture** — **subchondral bone violated through tibiotalar joint arthroscopically**\
✗ Wrong options: via sinus tarsi (retrograde) · knee autograft (larger) · posterolateral talus autograft (not described)

**Technique** — debride unstable flaps → stable contained defect · **awl perpendicular**, **2–4 mm deep**, holes **2–3 mm apart** · stop inflow → **fat / blood** from holes = adequate · K-wire via anterior portals or **transmalleolar** for central / posterior lesions

**Repair tissue** — **fibrocartilage (type I collagen)** · fibrocartilage in 60% at second look, no correlation with outcome\
✗ Wrong options: hyaline at 6 months

**Outcomes** — **85% pain improvement** · 65–90% ↑ PROs · larger lesions worse · **age not a predictor**

**Complications** — cartilage delamination, graft failure

### Intact cartilage cap – retrograde drilling ± grafting

**Indication** — **> 1 cm (e.g. 15 mm), contained, intact cartilage cap** → **retrograde drilling and/or bone grafting** (initial surgery)

**Best outcomes** — **1.2 cm defect + intact cartilage cap + no fragment displacement**

**Retrograde drilling not for** — displaced fragment (→ fixation or debridement + antegrade drilling) · 0.8 cm, no oedema (→ immobilise) · 0.5 cm displaced (→ debridement + antegrade drilling) · acute loose body (→ arthroscopic removal + debridement + antegrade drilling)

**Technique** — probe cap (softening, dimpling) · **K-wire from sinus tarsi into defect** (fluoro / guide) · cannulated drill → curette cyst → graft

**Complication** — **violation of intact cartilage cap**

**Percutaneous arthroscopic drilling (TO)** — **early lesions**, mild osteosclerosis, intact cartilage, stable fragment · **avoids medial malleolar osteotomy** (TO)

![Retrograde drilling from the sinus tarsi beneath an intact cap](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob7034-retrograde-drilling.jpg)

### Displaced fragments – fixation

**Acute displaced 1 × 1 cm anterolateral, viable cartilage + intact subchondral bone** — **surgical fixation**\
✗ Wrong options: observation · excision · excision + microfracture (chronic, < 1 cm², poor healing potential) · OAT (failed BMS or > 1.5 cm², cystic)

### Large / cystic lesions – osteochondral autograft

**1.6 cm² displaced lesion** — **osteochondral autograft transplantation of type II collagen** (hyaline)\
✗ Wrong options: marrow stimulation (type I fibrocartilage) · NWB 6 wk · retrograde drilling (needs intact cap) · excision

**OAT indications** — **> 1.0–1.5 cm²** · cystic / uncontained · **failed BMS** · displaced · failed drilling · CLAI with > 50–60 mm²

**OAT contraindication** — **bipolar kissing lesions** · diffuse arthritis · advanced talar dome osteonecrosis\
✗ Wrong options: displaced · failed drilling · failed BMS · > 1 cm (all indications)

**OAT technique** — donor **ipsilateral knee** · graft depth **12–15 mm** · perpendicular access, **flush** placement · knee cartilage **thinner** than talus → graft looks **prominent on X-ray** though cartilage flush

**OAT results** — 80–87% good / excellent · failure ↑ **> 2.25 cm²** · **donor-site morbidity up to 15%** · no worse after prior BMS · lesion size ∝ worse MOCART

**Fresh osteochondral allograft** — failed prior arthroscopic cartilage procedures · **large cystic component** · contained > 1.5 cm² · uncontained shoulder · **knee OA / prior knee infection** · patient fears knee donor morbidity · comparable to femoral autograft

**ACI** — **two-stage**: arthroscopic chondrocyte harvest (ankle or knee) → culture → implant via osteotomy under **tibial periosteal patch** (fibrin glue) · > 1 cm², ± cyst, shoulder, primary or revision · **MACI** = equivalent, may avoid osteotomy

| Graft option (TO) | Indication / key fact |
|---|---|
| **OATS** | Defects **> 5 mm** (TO) · single plug, usually **femoral condyle** · hyaline surface · > 1–1.5 cm² (Orthobullets) — read the options |
| **Mosaicplasty** | **Multiple small plugs** for irregular large lesions |
| **Mega-OATS / allograft** | **> 12 mm** defects · **fresh talar allograft within 7 days of harvest** · demanding, costly |
| **ACI** | Culture **2–5 wk** · stage 3–4, **large contained**, failed prior surgery · **age 15–55** · **contraindicated: malalignment, arthritis, instability, kissing lesions** |
| **MACI** | Collagen **type I–III membrane** preloaded with cells, **fibrin glue** |
| **CACI** | **Porcine collagen membrane** over cultured chondrocytes |
| **Hyalograft (Hylofast)** | Synthetic membrane, defects **≤ 10 mm**, **no periosteal flap** |

**Rehab (TO)** — 0–4 wk NWB immobilised → 4–8 wk partial WB, ROM → 8–12 wk loading, proprioception → > 12 wk sport · **full recovery 4–6 months** (TO)

**Approach** — **medial malleolar osteotomy** for **medial + posterior** lesions (2 parallel K-wires aim at lateral extent of OLT; predrill 2 holes) · lateral lesions → **lateral malleolar osteotomy or ATFL / CFL release**

### Concomitant instability – ligament reconstruction

**7 mm medial OLT + CLAI (> 15° talar tilt, + drawer) for BMS** — add **lateral ligamentous complex reconstruction**\
✗ Wrong options: peroneal debridement · deltoid reconstruction · OATS · OCA

**Unaddressed CLAI** — **↑ clinical failure** (AOFAS < 80) · larger lesions (**≥ 150 mm²**, > 1.5 cm) · **bipolar lesions** (medial malleolus tip, tibial plafond)

**1.8 cm posteromedial OLT, 12 mm cyst, intact cartilage, CLAI** — **OAT via medial malleolar osteotomy + concomitant lateral ligament repair** (+ subchondral bone graft)\
✗ Wrong options: microfracture + Broström (> 1.5 cm², cystic) · **staged** ligament surgery 6 mo later · retrograde drilling alone · ACI via anterior arthrotomy (two-stage)

## 6 Complications

| Complication | Cause / procedure |
|---|---|
| **Graft failure** | All grafting procedures |
| **Persistent pain** | Small % regardless of treatment |
| **Osteotomy delayed / nonunion** | Malleolar osteotomy |
| **Talar body ischaemia** | **Deltoid released** (deltoid artery) |
| **Ankle impingement** | **Graft plug left proud** |
| **Donor-site morbidity** | Knee autograft harvest (up to 15%) |
| **Violation of cartilage cap** | Retrograde drilling |
| Progression | Lesion may involve entire ankle |

## X vs Y

| | **Antegrade BMS / microfracture** | **Retrograde drilling** | **OAT** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Size | **< 1 cm** | **> 1 cm** | **> 1–1.5 cm²**, cystic |
| Cartilage cap | **Absent / unstable** | **Intact** | Displaced / shoulder |
| Route | **Through tibiotalar joint** | **Through sinus tarsi** | Malleolar osteotomy |
| Tissue | **Fibrocartilage (type I)** | Own cap preserved | **Hyaline (type II)** |
| Pitfall | Delamination | **Breach of cap** | Proud plug, donor site |
