# Metatarsal Fractures

*Foot & Ankle · 32 / 48*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Adults** — **5th MT** most commonly fractured

**3rd MT** — rarely isolated · **68%** with 2nd or 4th MT fracture

**Peak age** — 2nd–5th decade

### Paediatric metatarsal fractures – most common location

**Young child** — **1st MT (proximal)** most common · **< 4 yr** (Orthobullets) · **< 5 yr** (OB) — read the options

**Older child (> 5 yr)** — **5th MT**

**Course** — usually minimally displaced, rarely operative

**Ossification (age clue)** — lateral cuneiform **1 yr** · middle **3 yr** · medial **4 yr**

### Anatomy

**1st MT** — **widest + shortest** · plantar crista articulates with sesamoids · **bears 30–50% of weight** in gait · **50%** (Orthobullets anatomy) · TO: standing = 1st MT, walking = **2nd MT** — read the options · **no intermetatarsal ligament** support

**2nd MT** — **longest** · **commonest stress fracture site**

**Distal intermetatarsal ligaments (2nd–5th)** — maintain length + alignment in **isolated** fractures · implicated in Morton's neuroma · **multiple fractures lose this stability → ↑ displacement**

**Muscle balance** — extrinsic (EDL, FDL) vs intrinsic (interossei, lumbricals)

**Blood supply** — dorsal + plantar metatarsal arteries

## 2 Mechanism

**Indirect (commonest)** — forefoot fixed, hindfoot / leg rotates

**Direct crush** — significant soft-tissue injury

**Multiple proximal MT fractures** — **Lisfranc-equivalent** injury

**Stress** — repetitive microstress; MTs loaded **perpendicular to long axis** · relatively fixed proximal 2nd + 3rd MTs → diaphyseal stress fractures

## 3 Presentation

**Symptoms** — pain, inability to bear weight · **antecedent pain** suggests stress fracture

**Exam** — foot alignment (neutral / **cavovarus** / planovalgus) · tenderness · soft tissues in crush · **overlap / malrotation** of toes · Semmes–Weinstein monofilament if neuropathy

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Study | Use |
|---|---|
| **AP, lateral, oblique foot** | **Required** · ± contralateral, stress / WB views |
| **Repeat X-ray at 2 wk** | X-rays **lag symptoms by weeks** → callus / periosteal reaction appears |
| **CT** | Not routine · periarticular injury, **rule out Lisfranc** · better shows bone when fracture visible on X-ray |
| **MRI** | **Occult / stress fracture** · fluid sequences show **endosteal marrow + periosteal oedema** (earliest) · useful screening tool |
| **Bone scan (Tc-99)** | Occult stress fracture when X-rays unclear · **most useful** (OB1095 reference) · finds **noncontiguous simultaneous fractures** (e.g. 2nd MT + tibia) |

### Classification

| Descriptor | Record |
|---|---|
| Location | Base / shaft / neck / head |
| Pattern | Transverse, spiral, comminuted |
| Displacement | mm translation |
| Angulation | Sagittal plane degrees |
| Articular involvement | Yes / no |

## 5 Management

```
Acute metatarsal fracture
├─ 1st MT → any displacement → ORIF / pinning
├─ Central (2–4) → isolated, < 10° sagittal, < 4 mm → stiff shoe / boot, WBAT
│      └─ > 10° sagittal, > 4 mm, multiple → fixation
├─ 5th MT diaphyseal spiral (dancer's) → hard-soled shoe, WB
└─ Open fracture → fixation
```

### Operative criteria

| Nonoperative (stiff-soled shoe / walking boot, WBAT) | Operative (percutaneous or open) |
|---|---|
| **1st MT non-displaced** | **1st MT any displacement** (✗ Wrong options: 3 mm displaced 1st MT nonop) |
| **Central MT isolated, non / minimally displaced** | **Central MT sagittal deformity > 10°** (✗ Wrong options: 12°) |
| — | **> 4 mm translation** (✗ Wrong options: 6 mm) |
| — | **Multiple MT fractures** |
| **5th MT spiral diaphyseal (dancer's fracture)** | **Open fractures** |
| **Stress fractures** | Failed nonop stress fracture / nonunion |

### Shaft fractures – nonoperative indications

**5th MT spiral diaphyseal (dancer's) fracture** — **high union, low morbidity** → **hard-soled shoe + WB**

**Fixation goals** — restore alignment for normal force transmission across MT heads · **maintain length** → avoid **transfer metatarsalgia**

**Techniques** — antegrade / retrograde pinning · lag screws or mini-fragment plates for length-unstable patterns

**Prognosis** — majority of isolated MT fractures heal nonop

### Stress fractures – second MT, dancers, management & metabolic work-up

**March fracture** — **2nd, 3rd, 4th MTs**, **2nd commonest** · diaphyseal, callus + good alignment · runners, **military recruits**

**Ballet dancers** — **base of 2nd MT** · **amenorrhoea / delayed menarche** · pain with push-off + en pointe\
✗ Wrong options: Lisfranc injury · cuneiform · 1st MT base · plantar fascia strain

**Commonest stress site** — **distal 2nd MT shaft** (length + immobility) · "2nd + 3rd most frequent", also 5th (OB)

**Risk factors** — **long 2nd MT**, **hypermobile 1st ray**, **female**, recent ↑ training, **cavovarus** (recurrent), metabolic bone disease

**Female athlete triad** — repeated stress + **low BMD** + **dietary restraint** → ↑ fracture risk

**Metabolic work-up** — **serum cortisol, TSH, vitamin D, calcium** · menstrual history · **DEXA** when needed · (hypothyroidism, amenorrhoea) · older patient (55 yr) → **fragility fracture**

**Treatment** — **protected WB / short walking cast / fracture boot WBAT / hard-soled shoe** + activity modification · rest **4–6 wk** (OB1095) · return over **6–8 wk** (OB218223) — read the options · base fractures sometimes limited WB

**✗ Stress-fracture traps** — ORIF, K-wires, IM screw, calcium phosphate / BMA (only after failed nonop) · **biopsy / oncology referral** (no red flags) · continue running with orthotic · observation alone · NWB cast · **bone stimulator** (no evidence) · NSAIDs + immediate RTP · bone density in a teenager (normal, no help) · MRI / bone scan once X-ray diagnostic

**MRI already shows oedema, X-ray normal** — **fracture boot WBAT** (limit miles + repeat X-ray at 2 wk only if no MRI)

**Nonunion (dancer, 2nd MT base)** — fixation

![Second metatarsal base stress fracture in a ballet dancer](../../MCQ/attachments/q1112-a-stress-fracture-base-of-second.jpg)

**Differential** — metatarsalgia, MTP synovitis, **Freiberg's infarction**, Morton's neuroma

## 6 Complications

**Malunion** — **transfer metatarsalgia** or plantar keratosis → **corrective osteotomy**

**Multiple fractures** — loss of intermetatarsal ligament stability → displacement

## X vs Y

| | **1st MT** | **Central MTs (2–4)** | **5th MT diaphysis** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ligament support | **None** | Distal intermetatarsal ligaments | — |
| Load | **30–50%** of weight | — | — |
| Fix if | **Any displacement** | **> 10° sagittal, > 4 mm, multiple** | Rarely (spiral → nonop) |

| | **Bone scan** | **MRI** |
|---|---|---|
| Strength | Occult fracture · **noncontiguous fractures** | Earliest oedema · occult fracture · screening |
| When not needed | X-ray already diagnostic | X-ray already diagnostic |

## How it's asked

**"Runner, 3rd MT callus — next step?"** → **protected weightbearing with crutches, slow return to running**

**"Ballet dancer, delayed menarche, midfoot pain?"** → **second metatarsal base stress fracture**

**"55-yo ballet, 2nd MT stress fracture on X-ray / scan / MRI?"** → **walker boot + evaluation for metabolic bone disease**

**"Ballerina, 2nd MT shaft, callus on 2-wk X-ray — next?"** → **protected weight-bearing**\
✗ Wrong options: MRI · bone scan · biopsy · observation

**"Runner, normal X-ray, MRI oedema of 3rd MT?"** → **fracture boot, WBAT**

**"Recruit, amenorrhoea, distal 2nd MT on MRI — treatment?"** → **immobilisation in a short walking cast**

**"Field-hockey player, 3rd MT stress reaction — next?"** → **serum cortisol, TSH, vitamin D + calcium levels**

**"Young child fell — commonest MT fracture site?"** → **1st MT** (label 1)

**"Best managed nonoperatively?"** → **5th MT spiral fracture**
