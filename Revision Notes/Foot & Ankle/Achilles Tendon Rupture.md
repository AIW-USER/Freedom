# Achilles Tendon Rupture

*Foot & Ankle · 2 / 48*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology & risk factors

**Incidence** — **18 : 100,000 / yr** · **missed in up to 25%**

**Demographics** — **men** · **30–40 yr** (Orthobullets) · **males 30–39** (TO)

**Classic patient** — episodic athlete, **"weekend warrior"** (basketball, tennis, pickleball, volleyball)

| Risk factor | Source |
|---|---|
| **Fluoroquinolones** | Orthobullets, TO |
| **Steroids** — local injections + systemic | Orthobullets, TO |
| **Hypercholesterolaemia**, **gout**, **RA** | TO |
| **Long-term dialysis**, **renal transplantation** | TO |
| **Chronic tendinopathy with Haglund disease** | TO |
| Foot / ankle malalignment, training errors, degenerative tendon | TO |

**Haglund deformity ↑ TA rupture** — true (TO)

**"Antithrombolytics have a role"** — the **incorrect statement** in the TO list (TO)

### Anatomy

**Achilles** — **largest** (Orthobullets) / **strongest** (OB) tendon in body · confluence of **soleus + medial and lateral gastrocnemius**

### Tendon blood supply (TO)

| Fact | TO answer |
|---|---|
| **Three sources** | **Musculotendinous junction**, **osteotendinous junction**, **paratenon** (**paratenon = most important**) |
| **Major artery** | **Posterior tibial artery** (also Orthobullets) |
| Minor | **Peroneal artery** — small contribution to mid-section |
| **Most abundant zone** | **At the tendon insertion** (✗ Wrong options: mid-section between MTJ and bone–tendon junction) |
| **Least vascularised zone** | **2–6 cm above calcaneal insertion**, at all ages → poor repair |
| **Main surface of supply** | **Anterior (deep) surface** (✗ Wrong options: posterior · medial · lateral) — branches loop round / pass through to posterior surface |

### Sural nerve

**Course** — **lateral to the TA** · accompanied by **small saphenous vein**, which lies **just lateral** to the nerve

**Protect** — **skin incision slightly medial to TA** (TO, Orthobullets) · injury → **heel + lateral foot numbness** (TO)\
✗ Wrong options: saphenous · tibial · lateral plantar nerve

## 2 Mechanism

**Mechanism** — **sudden forced plantarflexion** · **violent dorsiflexion of a plantarflexed foot** · **push-off with weight-bearing forefoot while extending knee** (TO) · **sudden unexpected dorsiflexion** (TO) · **eccentric load** (OB)

**Site** — **4–6 cm above calcaneal insertion** (Orthobullets, OB) · **3–6 cm above os calcis**, watershed (TO) · least vascular zone **2–6 cm** (TO) — read the options

## 3 Presentation

**History** — **"pop"** · weakness, difficulty walking, heel pain · ecchymosis

**Inspection** — **↑ resting ankle dorsiflexion prone with knees bent** (loss of resting tension) · calf atrophy (chronic)

**Palpation** — **palpable gap**

**Motion** — **weak plantarflexion** · **↑ passive dorsiflexion** · chronic: **unable to toe off / toe walk**

![Loss of resting tension – affected foot rests in more dorsiflexion](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob7021-picture-1-resting-tension.jpg)

### Diagnosis – Thompson test & ultrasound

| Test (TO) | Method | Positive = rupture |
|---|---|---|
| **Thompson (Simmonds) squeeze** | Prone, feet over couch edge; squeeze calf **just distal to maximal girth** | **Little / no plantarflexion** vs other side · **sensitivity 0.89–0.93** |
| **Matles** | Prone, **actively flex knees to 90°** | Foot falls to **neutral or slight dorsiflexion** |
| **O'Brien needle** | **25-gauge needle** in midline of proximal tendon, passive ankle ROM | **Needle doesn't move** (moves = continuity) |

**"Thomas test is a reliable test"** — TO uses "Thomas / Simmonds" for the calf squeeze (true statement, TO) · **Thomas test = hip flexion contracture**, **Thessaly = meniscus** (OB) — read the options

**Movement tested against resistance (TO)** — options inversion + PF / dorsiflexion / plantarflexion / eversion + DF — **answer key not provided by the source** · Orthobullets: **weakness to ankle plantarflexion**

### Differential – xanthomas (TO)

**Bilateral swellings over Achilles (TO)** — options rheumatoid nodules / tophaceous gout / **tendoachilles xanthomas** / ganglion — **answer key not provided by the source** (module filed as xanthomas) · hypercholesterolaemia = rupture risk factor (TO)

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Study | Key facts |
|---|---|
| **X-ray** | **Rule out other pathology** (✗ Wrong options: IOC) |
| **Ultrasound** | **Investigation of choice** after + Thompson (TO) · **complete vs partial** rupture (Orthobullets) · as good as MRI for **tendinopathy + full-thickness tear** (TO) |
| **MRI** | **Highest accuracy** (TO) · **superior for partial tears + types of tendinopathy** (TO) · **equivocal exam**, **chronic ruptures**, gauging **gap size** · retracted tendon edges |
| Complete tear | **US = MRI** (TO) |
| No | **Venography**, **CT** (TO) |

**Sphygmomanometer test (TO)** — cuff on calf; pressure rise **< 10 mmHg** on contraction → rupture · O'Brien needle test invasive (avoided) · **Thompson = most specific** (TO)

**Dynamic US (TO)** — preferred to assess **actual apposition in plantar flexion** → conservative vs surgical · MRI static, **may overestimate gap**

### Diagnostic criteria – ultrasound criteria for nonoperative treatment (TO)

| Position | Criterion |
|---|---|
| **Maximal plantarflexion** | **Gap < 5 mm** |
| **Neutral** | **Gap < 10 mm** |
| **20° plantarflexion** | **> 75% tendon apposition** |

### Acute vs chronic – definition

**Chronic** — **> 3–4 wk** (OB) · **> 4 wk** (OB, JAAOS algorithm) · **> 6 wk** (other OBs) · Orthobullets: acute = **< 6 wk** · TO: chronic **4–6 wk** — read the options

### Chronic sleeve avulsion – diagnosis

**Chronic Achilles avulsion (sleeve) rupture** — old cast-treated rupture + new fall · X-ray: **calcified / ossified distal tendon moved away from insertion** · MRI: **chronic avulsion from insertion**\
✗ Wrong options: HO of proximal tendon · untreated midsubstance · gout · musculotendinous junction

### Medial gastrocnemius tear (tennis leg)

**Mechanism** — **simultaneous knee extension + ankle dorsiflexion** · athletes

**Presentation** — sudden **medial proximal calf** pain + ecchymosis · US / MRI confirm (often unnecessary)

**Treatment** — **conservative first line** → **excellent outcomes, most return to activity within 3 months**\
✗ Wrong options: surgery · quarter need surgery (nonop failure rare)

**Myotendinous Achilles rupture** — nonop boot + PT from 3 wk → complete healing (Orthobullets: more proximal injuries → nonop)

## 5 Management

```
Acute Achilles rupture
├─ Sedentary, frail, proximal / myotendinous, or preference → functional bracing in equinus + early rehab
├─ Active, wants surgery → open end-to-end or percutaneous repair + early functional rehab
└─ Deep infection after repair → debride necrotic tendon, no reconstruction, IV antibiotics 6 wk
```

```
Chronic rupture – gap after debridement
├─ < 2 cm → end-to-end repair
├─ 2–5 cm → V-Y advancement ± FHL transfer
└─ > 5 cm → FHL transfer ± V-Y / turndown
       └─ Defect too big for local tissue (> 10 cm) → allograft
```

```
Chronic / neglected rupture > 4 wk (JAAOS, OB)
├─ Avoid sacrificing tendons / donor morbidity → synthetics
├─ Gap 3–5 cm, viable ends → V-Y or local turndown ± FHL
└─ Gap > 5 cm or unhealthy tissue → FHL / FDL transfer ± dermal matrix
```

### Operative criteria

| Indication | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Acute, preference for nonop · **sedentary** · **medically frail** · **proximal (musculature) injuries** | **Functional bracing / cast in resting equinus (20° PF)** + early functional rehab |
| **Acute (< \~6 wk)** | **Open end-to-end repair** |
| **Cosmesis concern** | **Percutaneous repair** |
| **Chronic, defect < 3 cm** | **V-Y advancement** (Orthobullets) |
| **Chronic, defect > 3 cm** | **FHL transfer ± V-Y advancement** — **requires functioning tibial nerve** (Orthobullets) |
| Defect < 3 cm after debridement + < 3 months | **Direct end-to-end repair** (TO, OB) |

**Conservative (TO)** — never "no role" even in chronic diabetics · walker boot with **heel lift** · plantar flexion 4–6 wk → gradual dorsiflexion · eccentric calf exercises · post-op rehab: 0–4 wk 20° PF NWB → 4–6 wk neutral, partial WB → 6–10 wk ROM → 10–12 wk strengthening → > 3 mo sport (total 3–6 mo)

### Operative vs nonoperative acute treatment

| Outcome | Surgery vs nonop with **functional rehab** |
|---|---|
| **Re-rupture** | **Lower with surgery vs cast 6 wk** (classic) · **equal with early functional rehab** (Willits, Soroceanu) · mixed (OB) |
| Re-rupture rates | **\~2% op vs 10–40% nonop** (Orthobullets) · 2.3% vs 3.9% (meta-analysis) · op **3–4%** (TO) · **1–2%** (OB) |
| **Complications** | **↑ with surgery** (\~2×; 4.9% vs 1.6%) — wound, infection, sural |
| Strength, ROM, calf girth, SMFA at 12 mo | **No difference** · **equivalent PF strength** (some ↑ PF strength with surgery; no ↑ DF strength) |
| DF ROM at 6 mo | Not improved — some studies ↓ in op group |
| **Functional outcomes** | **No significant difference** |
| **DVT** | **No difference** (7–10%) |
| **Return to work** | **Quicker with surgery** |
| **Return to sport** | **Quicker with surgery** (one OB) · **similar** (another OB) — read the options · TO: \~60% after surgery |
| Nerve injury | **Higher with surgery** |
| Cost | **Higher with surgery** |
| Satisfaction, ultimate strength | Same (even vs cast) |

**Cast 6 wk nonop vs surgery** — **higher risk of re-rupture**\
✗ Wrong options: ↓ satisfaction · less return to sport · ↓ strength · ↑ skin problems

**Re-rupture: greater in conservatively managed cases** — true (TO)

**↓ Re-rupture after repair** — **early functional rehabilitation protocol** (WB in boot \~2 wk + ROM)\
✗ Wrong options: delayed WB > 6 wk (stiffness) · gastrocnemius fascial flap augmentation · biological / PRP augmentation · percutaneous technique

**Nonop rehab** — start **within 48 h** · strict adherence · supervised PT · functional bracing → low re-rupture esp. **> 40 yr, women**

**Post-op immobilisation** — **20° plantarflexion for 4–6 wk** (↓ skin tension, protects repair) · **functional bracing beats casting** (↓ adhesions, ↓ complications)

### Open vs percutaneous / minimally invasive repair – wound complications & sural nerve

| Outcome | **Open** | **Percutaneous / MIS** |
|---|---|---|
| **Wound complications / infection** | **Higher** (superficial infection 6.0%) | **Lower** (0.4%) |
| **Overall complications** | **Higher** | Lower |
| **Local scarring** | More | **Less** |
| **Sural nerve injury** | Nerve seen + protected (0%) | **Higher** (3.4%) — blind needle passage (Orthobullets, OB) · **no difference** (other OBs) — read the options |
| Re-rupture, PF strength, calf circumference | Same | Same |
| Return to sport / running, outcome scores | Same | Same |
| Return to work | — | **More rapid** (Orthobullets) |
| 1-year function | Same | Same |

**Open technique** — incision **just medial to tendon** (sural) · incise **paratenon** · heavy **non-absorbable** suture

### Repair techniques – eponyms (TO)

| Eponym | Technique |
|---|---|
| **Ma and Griffith** | **Percutaneous repair of acute rupture** |
| **Lynn** | Repair + **fanned-out plantaris** over it (injury **< 10 days**) |
| **Lindholm** | **Two flaps** from proximal tendon + gastrocnemius aponeurosis **turned down 180°** to distal tendon |
| **Maffulli** | **Peroneus brevis transfer** (loop across defect) → **NOT an acute repair technique** (TO answer) |
| Teuffer | **PB loop** when end-to-end impossible |
| White and Kraynick | Teuffer modification: **PB through calcaneal tuberosity** + plantaris through ends |
| Turco and Spinella | PB through **midcoronal slit** in distal stump |
| **Bosworth** | **Gastrocnemius-soleus turndown** graft |
| **Abraham and Pankovich** | **V-Y tendinous flap** |
| **Wapner** | **FHL tendon transfer** |

### Wound complications – most common complication & risk factors

**Most common complication of surgical repair** — **skin healing problems / wound complications** (**5–10%**, poor posterior blood supply)\
✗ Wrong options: infection (common, not commonest) · neurological injury (rare after open) · re-rupture (1–2%) · stiffness · loss of motion (nonop)

**Risk factors** — **tobacco (most common)** · **steroid use** · **female sex** · **diabetes** · **open technique**\
✗ Wrong options: ↑ BMI · immediate surgery · male · age > 40

**≥ 1 of diabetes / tobacco / steroid** — **> 40%** complications vs 6% without

### Infected repair – debridement

**Deep infection + re-rupture (5 cm)** — **debridement of necrotic + infected tendon, no attempt at reconstruction** → **culture-specific IV antibiotics 6 wk** → later soft-tissue cover + reconstruction\
✗ Wrong options: primary repair · V-Y · turndown · FHL transfer (not with active infection)

### Chronic rupture – V-Y advancement, turndown & FHL transfer by gap size

| Gap (after scar debridement) | Treatment | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **\~1–2 cm** / **< 2 cm** | **End-to-end repair** | OB (most) |
| < 2–3 cm | Direct repair | OB review |
| **< 3 cm** (+ < 3 months) | **Direct repair or V-Y alone** | Orthobullets, OB, TO |
| **2–5 cm** | **V-Y advancement (gastroc-soleus) ± FHL** · V-Y fills 2–5 cm | OB |
| **> 3 cm** | **FHL transfer ± V-Y** (muscle weak, scarred) | Orthobullets, OB |
| **> 5 cm** | **FHL transfer** ± V-Y / turndown · ± allograft | OB |
| **> 10 cm** | **Allograft** (not suitable for local transfer) | OB · (> 5 cm in another OB) |

| Gap (TO lecture) | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **< 3 cm** | End-to-end (Krackow / Bunnell / SpeedBridge), epitendinous reinforcement |
| **3–6 cm** | **V-Y plasty (ideal 3–5 cm)** or turndown flap |
| **> 6 cm** | **FHL (> FDL) transfer** |

**Turndown (Bosworth / Lindholm, TO)** — bridges chronic gaps **up to 10–12 cm**; long incision, skin problems

**Repair position (TO)** — **5° plantar flexion** · insertional / Haglund: suture anchor (simple) vs **pull-through calcaneal tunnel + button (poor bone stock)**

**FHL preferred over FDL (TO)** — better excursion, similar line of pull, synergistic; best for chronic + diabetic patients

**4 cm chronic defect** — **V-Y ± FHL augmentation** (one OB) · **open FHL transfer** (another OB) — read the options

**Gap is measured after scar debridement** — 1.5 cm + 1.0 cm scar = **2.5 cm → V-Y lengthening + end-to-end**\
✗ Wrong options: insertional tear · 0.5 + 1.0 = 1.5 cm → end-to-end alone · 1.5 cm no scar · 3.0 + 2.5 = 5.5 cm → combined procedures

**V-Y advancement** — **V apex at musculotendinous junction**, limbs diverge to exit tendon · cut **superficial tendinous portion only**, muscle intact · avoids sacrificing a normal muscle–tendon unit

**Gastrocnemius turndown** — **slip of central third of gastrocnemius tendon** bridges gap · augment with **FHL** (proximity + vascularity)

**FHL transfer technique** — excise degenerate ends · **release FHL at knot of Henry** · tenodese distal FHL stump to FDL · reroute **through calcaneus** → FHL + triceps surae both act on calcaneus · open / extended approach\
✗ Wrong options: percutaneous · limited open

**Calcaneal attachment** — **posterior = greater force** (longer lever arm, advocated) · anterior = more plantarflexion range · transtendinous = transosseous outcomes

| Why FHL is preferred |
|---|
| **Axis of contractile force replicates Achilles** |
| **Fires in phase** with gastroc-soleus |
| **Location / harvest avoids NV bundle** |
| **Expendable** · **stronger** than FDL / PB · **biomechanically strong** bridge |
| Close + well-vascularised · preserves foot muscle balance |

**FHL anatomy** — origin **interosseous membrane + distal 2/3 fibula** · **lateral to FDL at ankle** · **dorsal to FDL at knot of Henry** · inserts **base of 1st distal phalanx** · **tibial nerve**

| Option | Tendon | Why wrong |
|---|---|---|
| **1st distal phalanx, tibial nerve** | **FHL** ✓ | — |
| 1st proximal phalanx | FHB | Wrong insertion |
| 1st distal phalanx (dorsum), deep peroneal | EHL | Anterior |
| 5th MT base, superficial peroneal | **Peroneus brevis** | **Eversion weakness**, doesn't replicate pull, historical, worse longevity |
| Medial cuneiform + 1st MT base, deep peroneal | Tibialis anterior | **Out of phase** (anterior compartment) |
| Medial to FDL, inserts navicular | **Tibialis posterior** | **Chopart weakness, ↓ inversion, pes planus** |
| Plantar to FHL at knot of Henry | **FDL** | Not adjacent, less power (described 1991; FHL 1993) |
| Lateral femoral condyle origin | **Plantaris** | Not robust (**absent in 10%**), must be supplemented |
| — | Peroneus longus, EDL | No (PB acceptable, not PL) |
| — | **Synthetic / carbon fibre** | **Foreign body reaction**, longevity, no proven benefit |
| — | Xenograft, hamstring autograft | Poor outcomes / not used |

**After FHL transfer** — **only significant change: ↓ hallucal phalangeal pressure** · AOFAS MTP-IP **96.4** · no transfer metatarsalgia · no ↑ 1st MT head pressure\
✗ Wrong options: ↑ short flexor recruitment

**Hallux after FHL transfer** — **residual hallux PF / IP flexion weakness** (Orthobullets, OB review) · **no functional hallux deficit** (OB study) — read the options

**Strength** — near-normal maximal strength, **↓ endurance** · torque deficit \~35% · FHL hypertrophy · main benefit = pain relief

**FHL for tendinosis / insertional** — when **> 50% of tendon involved**

**✗ Chronic traps** — **equinus casting** (retracted ends won't appose) · **primary repair in max PF + stretching** (equinus contracture, re-rupture) · end-to-end repair (contracted gastroc-soleus, gap too big) · percutaneous / limited open

### Open insertional rupture on Haglund / insertional tendinopathy

**Open insertional rupture, prior steroid injections, Haglund on X-ray** — **Haglund excision + gastrocnemius recession + Achilles repair + FHL transfer**\
✗ Wrong options: open primary repair alone (distal disease) · functional rehab (never studied for insertional / tendinopathy) · bone-block allograft (> 10 cm) · percutaneous

**Chronic tendonitis + Haglund** — debridement of osseous spur + repair

## 6 Complications

| Complication | Fact |
|---|---|
| **Re-rupture** | **Higher nonop (\~10–40% vs 2%)** classically · **no difference** with functional rehab (Level 1) → treat with **surgical repair** |
| **Wound healing** | **5–10%** · **smoking = most common** risk factor · female, steroids, open technique |
| **Deep infection** | Debride necrotic tendon + **6 wk culture-specific antibiotics** |
| **Sural nerve injury** | **Higher with percutaneous** approach |
| **DVT** | **7–10%**, **no op vs nonop difference** |
| FHL harvest | Hallux weakness, harvest morbidity |
