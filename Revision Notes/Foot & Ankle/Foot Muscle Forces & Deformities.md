# Foot Muscle Forces & Deformities

*Foot & Ankle · 19 / 48*

## 1 Basics

### Simple deformities – strong vs weak muscle

| Deformity | Strong (deforming) | Weak |
|---|---|---|
| **Equinus** | **Gastrocnemius–soleus** | Dorsiflexors |
| **Cavus** | **Plantar fascia, intrinsics** | Dorsiflexors |
| **Varus** | **Tibialis posterior, tibialis anterior** | **Peroneus brevis** |
| **Supination** | **Tibialis anterior** | **Peroneus longus** |
| **Flatfoot** | **Peroneus brevis** | **Tibialis posterior** |

### Complex deformities

| Deformity | Strong | Weak |
|---|---|---|
| **Equinovarus + supination** | Gastroc–soleus, tib post, tib anterior | **PB + PL** |
| **Equinovalgus** | Gastroc–soleus, **peroneals** | **Tib post, tib anterior** |
| **Calcaneovalgus** | Dorsiflexors / evertors (**L4, L5**) | Plantarflexors / invertors (**S1, S2**) |

### Muscle antagonists & tendon transfers in cavovarus (PL→PB, EHL transfer)

| Muscle | Antagonist / force couple | Fact |
|---|---|---|
| **Tibialis posterior** | **Peroneus brevis = primary antagonist** | Inverts, secondarily plantarflexes |
| **Tibialis anterior** | **Peroneus longus** | TA secondarily inverts → only **partial** antagonist of TP · **TA eccentrically contracts after heel strike** |
| **Peroneus longus** | Tibialis anterior | **Plantarflexes the first ray** |
| **Peroneus brevis** | Tibialis posterior | **Primary evertor**; does **not** contribute to cavus |

**Cavus driver** — **weak tibialis anterior + strong peroneus longus** → **plantarflexed first metatarsal**

| Nerve | Motor supply |
|---|---|
| **Tibial** | **Tibialis posterior**, long toe flexors (deep + superficial posterior compartments) |
| **Deep peroneal** | Anterior compartment: **TA, EDL, EHL, peroneus tertius** |
| **Superficial peroneal** | Lateral compartment: **PL, PB** |
| **Sural, saphenous** | **No motor** (sensory only) |

**DPN / SPN / L5 root injury** — anterior + lateral compartment weakness → **unopposed tib post + long toe flexors** → hindfoot + forefoot **varus**

**Tendon transfer requirements** — adequate **strength + excursion** · insertion close to substituted tendon, routed in **direct line** · **intact nerve + blood supply** · **functional (supple) joints** · donor must be **innervated**\
✗ Wrong options: TA / peroneals in CPN palsy

## 3 Presentation

### Foot drop

**Definition** — inability to dorsiflex ankle ± toes · **commonest cause = peroneal nerve palsy**

**Causes** — central (brain, cord, roots) · peripheral (sciatic, peroneal) · **traumatic (knee dislocation, laceration, blunt)** · compressive (mass, deformity correction) · systemic (**diabetic polyneuropathy**, mononeuropathy) · iatrogenic (casting, positioning, surgery) · mechanical (muscle debridement, tumour excision) · **anterior compartment syndrome** (devitalised muscle) · **CMT** · polio

| Nerve | Motor loss | Sensory loss |
|---|---|---|
| **DPN** | **Ankle / toe dorsiflexion** | **First dorsal web space** |
| **SPN** | **Ankle eversion** | **Lateral leg + dorsal foot** |

**Gait** — **foot slaps floor at heel strike** = tibialis anterior weakness · **toes extend in swing** = extensors substituting for weak TA

**Knee-level injury** — lateral leg sensory loss + ↓ eversion + ↓ dorsiflexion

### Equinovarus after stroke

**Commonest ankle–foot deformity after stroke** — **equinovarus**

**Equinus** — overactive **gastrocnemius–soleus** · **Varus** — overactive **tibialis anterior** (+ tib post, FHL, FDL)

**Other features** — clonus, intrinsic-minus foot, supple claw toes, compensatory **knee hyperextension**, premature triceps surae firing

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Silfverskiöld test & gastrocnemius recession indications

**Test** — ankle dorsiflexion with **knee extended**, then **knee flexed** (hindfoot locked)

**Why it works** — **gastrocnemius crosses the knee** (origin posterior femur) → relaxes with knee flexion · **soleus does not**

![Silfverskiöld test: dorsiflexion with knee extended then flexed](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob7054-silverskiold-moved.jpg)

### Diagnostic criteria

| Silfverskiöld result | Diagnosis | Surgery |
|---|---|---|
| **↑ DF with knee flexed** (e.g. −10° ext → +10° flex; −5° → 10°; 0° → 15°; 3° → 16°) | **Isolated gastrocnemius contracture** (positive test) | **Gastrocnemius recession (Strayer)** |
| **Same DF knee flexed + extended**, can't pass neutral in either | **Achilles (gastroc–soleus) contracture** | **Achilles lengthening** (Hoke triple-cut, percutaneous, open) or gastroc + soleus recession |
| DF past neutral in both positions | **Normal** | — |
| ✗ Wrong option: DF better with knee extended | Opposite of positive test | — |

**Thresholds** — gastroc contracture if **DF < 10° with knee extended**, improving with knee flexion (OB) · **difference > 10° = significant** (OB) · **shy of neutral → past neutral** with knee flexion (OB) — read the options

**Not a Silfverskiöld finding** — calf squeeze ± plantarflexion = **Thompson test** (Achilles rupture) · resting ankle position irrelevant

**Other exams (wrong options)** — **Ober** = IT band · **Dial** = knee posterolateral corner · **Thessaly** = meniscus · **Thomas** = hip flexion contracture · **Coleman** = hindfoot flexibility

**Gastroc contracture causes** — ↑ **forefoot strike** → ↑ plantar tension → **plantar fasciitis**, **arch collapse**, **Achilles tendinopathy**, **metatarsalgia** (plantar callus under MT heads), **neuromas**

**Lateral head of gastrocnemius** — muscle tested by the Silfverskiöld test

### Coleman block test – forefoot-driven cavovarus

**Method** — block under **lateral border (lateral column / hindfoot)**, first ray allowed to drop off

**Heel corrects to neutral / slight valgus** — **flexible subtalar / hindfoot** → varus is **forefoot-driven** = **plantarflexed first ray** (valgus forefoot)\
✗ Wrong options: dorsiflexed first ray · varus forefoot · rigid flatfoot

**Heel stays in varus** — **hindfoot-driven (rigid hindfoot)** → needs calcaneal osteotomy

![Coleman block test: forefoot-driven vs hindfoot-driven varus](../../MCQ/attachments/q3878-illa-coleman-block-1.jpg)

**Subtle cavus associations** — **lateral column overload**, recurrent ankle sprains / functional ankle instability, **5th MT stress fracture**

**5th MT stress fracture (esp. chronic / recurrent)** — look for **hindfoot varus deformity**\
✗ Wrong options: lateral ligament instability · talocalcaneal coalition (causes flatfoot, protects 5th MT) · PB rupture · anterior process fracture

## 5 Management

```
Equinus
├─ Silfverskiöld positive (gastroc only) → gastrocnemius recession (Strayer)
└─ Fixed in both knee positions → Achilles lengthening (TAL)
```

```
Cavovarus
├─ Supple, Coleman corrects → dorsiflexion 1st MT osteotomy (± PL→PB)
├─ Coleman does not correct → + lateralising calcaneal (Dwyer) osteotomy
└─ Rigid / degenerative → triple arthrodesis
```

```
Chronic flexible foot drop
├─ < 6–12 mo, nerve may recover → AFO + observation + therapy
├─ Acute laceration → nerve repair, graft or transfer
└─ No recovery, supple joints → tib post transfer to dorsum (lateral cuneiform)
       └─ Equinus contracture → + gastroc / Achilles lengthening
```

### Gastrocnemius recession vs Achilles lengthening

**Strayer** — **fascial release of gastrocnemius** (distal GT) for recession + gentle lengthening · after **failed nonop**\
✗ Wrong options: failed PT alone is not an indication (try orthotics / injections)

| Level | Procedure | Eponym |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | Proximal gastrocnemius tenotomy | Silfverskiöld |
| 4 | Deep gastroc–soleus recession | Baumann |
| 3 | **Distal gastrocnemius tenotomy** | **Strayer** |
| 2 | Superficial gastroc–soleus recession | Vulpius, Baker |
| 1 | **TAL** | **Hoke (triple cut)**, White, Paley |

![Levels of gastrocnemius–soleus lengthening](../../MCQ/attachments/q3878-illb-gastroc-recession-levels.jpg)

**Sural nerve** — at risk with gastrocnemius intramuscular aponeurotic recession

**Plantar fasciitis + positive Silfverskiöld** — **gastrocnemius recession**\
✗ Wrong options: unchanged test · 3 mo failed PT · flexible planovalgus · too-many-toes

**Cavovarus + positive Silfverskiöld (0° → 15°)** — **gastrocnemius recession**\
✗ Wrong options: heel cord / percutaneous / open TAL · gastroc + soleus recession (those for combined contracture)

**Runner, callus under 1st MT head, −5° → 10°** — **Strayer**\
✗ Wrong options: FHL → Achilles (chronic Achilles rupture / degeneration) · Hoke TAL · 1st MT extension osteotomy (cavus with flexible hindfoot) · posterior capsule release

**Metatarsalgia 2nd–4th, normal X-ray, 3° → 16°** — **gastrocnemius recession**\
✗ Wrong options: Weil osteotomies · PTT debridement + MDCO · triple-cut TAL · MTP steroid

**Stage IIA AAFD after MDCO + FDL → navicular** — gastroc recession if **can't DF above neutral with knee extended but can past neutral with knee flexed**

**Isolated Achilles lengthening** — **can't DF past neutral with knee extended or flexed**\
✗ Wrong options: CP gastroc-only → gastroc lengthening · runner anterior impingement → arthroscopic debridement · foot drop → tendon transfer · normal Silfverskiöld

### Cavovarus correction

**Flexible forefoot-driven (Coleman corrects)** — **dorsiflexion first metatarsal osteotomy** → hindfoot resumes slight valgus, **avoids calcaneal osteotomy**\
✗ Wrong options: triple / subtalar arthrodesis (degenerative only) · PB lengthening · medialising calcaneal osteotomy (flexible flatfoot)

**Coleman corrects + Silfverskiöld unchanged (−5° both)** — **first ray dorsiflexion osteotomy + Achilles lengthening**\
✗ Wrong options: gastroc recession · calcaneal osteotomy · triple arthrodesis (deformity supple)

**Rearfoot orthotic** — won't correct a forefoot cause · may still need **lateralising calcaneal osteotomy**

**Subtle cavus, lateral column overload, mild instability, no joint-line pain** — **PT + cavus orthotic: lateral forefoot posting + first metatarsal head recessing** (another OB: **lateral hindfoot posting** + 1st MT head recess)\
✗ Wrong options: MRI (no joint-line pain) · boot (stress fracture) · NSAIDs + lace-up (ignores lateral column) · arch support + **medial posting — contraindicated** in cavus

**Decreases plantarflexion force on 1st MT head** — **peroneus longus → peroneus brevis transfer** (also improves eversion when PB weak / torn)

| Transfer | Indication |
|---|---|
| **PL → PB** | **↓ plantarflexed 1st MT** (cavovarus), weak PB / chronic PB tear |
| **Tib post → dorsum (lateral cuneiform)** | **Weak dorsiflexion / foot drop** |
| **SPLATT** | **Equinovarus**, excessive **forefoot inversion in swing**, spastic TA |
| **FDL → tib post / navicular** | **PTT dysfunction** (flexible flatfoot) |
| **FDL → EDL** | **Flexible hammer / claw toes** |
| **EHL → 1st MT neck (Jones)** | **Hallux claw / hyperextended 1st MTP** |
| **FHL → Achilles** | Chronic Achilles rupture / degeneration |
| **TA → talus dorsum** | **Midfoot amputation** with firing TA (prevents equinus) |

**Jones procedure** — **hallux IP fusion + EHL transfer to 1st MT neck** for **hyperextended 1st MTP** in cavus\
✗ Wrong options: FHL, EHB, EDL, TA (wrong length / direction)

**Polio, flexible cavus, claw toes, equinus, foot slap** — soft tissue only: **plantar fascia release + Achilles lengthening + EHL → 1st MT neck + tib post → dorsum + FDL → EDL**\
✗ Wrong options: calcaneal / metatarsal osteotomies (flexible) · triple arthrodesis (young)

### Foot drop – tibialis posterior transfer to dorsum

**Nonop** — **AFO** + observation + therapy (stretch, keep joints supple) · AFO downsides: ↓ ankle ROM, discomfort

**Wait** — if no return in **6–12 mo** → transfer · **no dorsiflexion 1 yr after nerve repair → assume no recovery**

**Operation** — **tibialis posterior (tibial nerve) → through interosseous membrane → lateral cuneiform / dorsum of foot** (or circumtibial) ± **gastroc / Achilles lengthening** · converts deforming invertor into **strongest available dorsiflexor** · ↓ plantarflexion + inversion torque

**CPN laceration, best single-tendon transfer (MRI label B)** — **tibialis posterior**\
✗ Wrong options: A = TA · E = peroneals (non-functional) · C = FDL (adjunct for toe extension only) · D = Achilles (transfer not described; lengthen it instead)

![Axial T1 MRI ankle: B = tibialis posterior, C = FDL, A = tibialis anterior, E = peroneals, D = Achilles](../../MCQ/attachments/q3842-a-t1-ankle-label.jpg)

**Combined PTT + FHL + FDL transfer (Movahedi)** — foot drop correction excellent **60%**, toe extension excellent **47%**

**Equinovarus 13 mo after MVC, flexible** — **tib post → lateral midfoot + Achilles lengthening**\
✗ Wrong options: ankle / subtalar / pantalar arthrodesis (fixed deformity or end-stage arthritis only) · SPLATT (TA not working)

**CMT, no TA / PB, 5° equinus, TP + FDL 5/5** — **gastrocnemius lengthening + tib post → dorsum**\
✗ Wrong options: PB → dorsum · FDL → dorsum · ankle fusion · triple (supple)

**Knee dislocation, CPN transection, no recovery at 1 yr** — **tib post → dorsum**\
✗ Wrong options: sural cable graft (less reliable) · PL → TA · EHL → TA (peroneal-innervated) · ankle fusion in young

**Anterior compartment syndrome, DF 0/5, eversion 4/5, Silfverskiöld unchanged at 20° DF** — **tib post through interosseous membrane to dorsum**\
✗ Wrong options: PL → PB (eversion only) · Achilles lengthening + AFO (no fixed equinus) · **FHL → navicular — inadequate excursion + strength** · TA → talus (TA dead)

### Spastic hemiplegia – bracing & SPLATT

**Timing after stroke** — **AFO + PT for ≥ 6 mo** (Orthobullets, Miller) · **18–24 mo** before reconstruction (Younger & Hansen) — read the options

**Early (3 mo after stroke)** — **AFO + physical therapy** (± phenol / Botox, stretching, ROM)\
✗ Wrong options: Strayer · SPLATT · FHL transfer + toe flexor release · tib post fractional lengthening (all reasonable later)

**Surgical options (after recovery plateau)** — **SPLATT** · **FHL → dorsum of foot + release FDL / FDB at each toe base** · gastrocnemius / Achilles lengthening

**Dynamic varus, TA active in swing + stance, weak evertors, clonus, equinus, 2 yr** — **percutaneous Achilles lengthening + split TA transfer to lateral cuneiform**\
✗ Wrong options: TAL alone · **out-of-phase tib post transfer** or **Bridle** (TA still active, risk **planovalgus**) · toe flexor tenotomy

**TAL in spasticity** — restores DF + **↓ clonus** (stretch response) · if DF still inadequate → **tib post lengthening**

### Flatfoot – FDL transfer & orthotic posting

**Supple pes planoabductovalgus, can't single-leg heel raise** — bony surgery + **FDL tendon transfer** (similar dynamic function to PTT) · alternatives FHL, PB\
✗ Wrong options: EHL · PL repair · **spring ligament release — worsens flatfoot**; repair / reconstruction is OK · lateral ligament reconstruction

**Flexible planovalgus + lateral hindfoot (impingement) pain** — **medial hindfoot posting / wedge** = simplest offload\
✗ Wrong options: lateral hindfoot posting ↑ lateral pressure · rigid / semi-rigid / accommodative orthotic alone (foot still pronates)

## X vs Y

| | **Gastrocnemius contracture** | **Achilles contracture** |
|---|---|---|
| Silfverskiöld | **DF improves with knee flexion** | **No change** |
| Treatment | **Gastroc recession (Strayer)** | **TAL (Hoke, percutaneous, open)** |

| | **Tib post transfer to dorsum** | **SPLATT** |
|---|---|---|
| Problem | **Flaccid foot drop** (CPN palsy, CMT, compartment syndrome) | **Spastic** equinovarus (stroke, TA overactive) |
| TA | **Dead** | **Active / overactive** |
| Insertion | Lateral cuneiform / dorsum via interosseous membrane | Lateral cuneiform |

| | **Forefoot-driven cavovarus** | **Hindfoot-driven cavovarus** |
|---|---|---|
| Coleman | **Heel corrects** | Heel stays varus |
| Surgery | **Dorsiflexion 1st MT osteotomy** | + **calcaneal osteotomy** |

## How it's asked

**"Test in figure (knee extended vs flexed)?"** → **Silfverskiöld — gastrocnemius tightness vs Achilles contracture**

**"Indication for gastroc recession in plantar fasciitis?"** → **10 degrees less than neutral with knee in extension versus 10 degrees beyond neutral with knee flexed**

**"Cavovarus, DF 0° → 15° with knee flexion — Achilles management?"** → **gastrocnemius recession**

**"Runner, 1st MT callus, −5° → 10°?"** → **Strayer procedure**

**"Test for structure at white arrow (lateral gastrocnemius)?"** → **Silfverskiöld test**

**"Gastroc recession after stage IIA flatfoot correction when?"** → **can't DF above neutral with knee extended, can with knee flexed**

**"Metatarsalgia, 3° → 16°, failed orthotics?"** → **gastrocnemius recession**

**"Who benefits from isolated Achilles lengthening?"** → **can't DF past neutral with knee extended or flexed**

**"Coleman corrects, Silfverskiöld unchanged — procedures?"** → **first ray dorsiflexion osteotomy + Achilles lengthening**

**"Coleman block correction proves varus due to?"** → **plantar flexed first ray**

**"Heel corrects to valgus on Coleman — procedure?"** → **dorsiflexion first metatarsal osteotomy**

**"Associated pathology with 5th MT stress fracture?"** → **hindfoot varus deformity**

**"Subtle cavus, lateral midfoot pain, recurrent sprains?"** → **PT + orthotic with lateral forefoot posting + first metatarsal head recessing**

**"CPN laceration, best single tendon (MRI label)?"** → **B — tibialis posterior**

**"Transfer for anterior + lateral compartment deficit — true?"** → **antagonist is peroneus brevis**

**"Flexible post-traumatic equinovarus, no DF / eversion?"** → **tib post transfer to lateral midfoot + Achilles lengthening**

**"CMT foot drop, supple, 5° equinus?"** → **gastrocnemius lengthening + tib post to dorsum**

**"No DF 1 yr after CPN repair?"** → **tibialis posterior transfer to dorsum of foot**

**"Nerve to the transferred muscle in foot drop?"** → **tibial nerve**

**"Foot drop after compartment syndrome — restore DF?"** → **posterior tibialis transfer through interosseous membrane to dorsum**

**"Polio, foot slap, claw toes, flexible cavus?"** → **plantar fascia release, TAL, EHL → 1st MT neck, tib post → dorsum, FDL → EDL**

**"Hyperextended 1st MTP after cavus reconstruction — transfer?"** → **extensor hallucis longus (Jones)**

**"Transfer that ↓ plantarflexion force on 1st MT head?"** → **peroneus longus to peroneus brevis**

**"Peroneus brevis is primary antagonist of?"** → **posterior tibialis**

**"Stroke 3 months ago, spastic TA varus?"** → **AFO with physical therapy**

**"Spastic hemiplegia 2 yr, TA active swing + stance, weak evertors?"** → **percutaneous TAL + split TA transfer to lateral cuneiform**

**"Soft-tissue procedure with bony flatfoot surgery?"** → **flexor digitorum longus transfer**

**"Simplest shoe change for lateral hindfoot pain in planovalgus?"** → **medial hindfoot posting**
