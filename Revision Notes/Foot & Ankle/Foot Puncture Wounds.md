# Foot Puncture Wounds

*Foot & Ankle · 20 / 48*

## 1 Basics

### Epidemiology

**Setting** — common **workplace** injury (construction sites) · stepping on a **nail** or stick through sock / sole

**Commonest object** — **nails** (TO) · also needles, glass, wood, plastic, sharp metal (TO)\
✗ Wrong options: needles commonest

**Infection** — **\~10%** · **osteomyelitis 1–2%** (penetration to cartilage / periosteum) (TO)

**Complications (TO)** — soft-tissue infection · deep abscess · **pyogenic granuloma** · **epidermal inclusion cyst** · osteomyelitis

### Risk factors for infection (TO)

**Wound (TO)** — presentation **> 6 h** · location · depth · contamination of object · **retained foreign body**

**Host (TO)** — elderly · immunocompromised · PVD · **diabetes = most significant risk factor** → often osteomyelitis

### Microbiology – S. aureus vs Pseudomonas (rubber-soled shoes)

| Infection | Commonest organism | Features asked |
|---|---|---|
| **Soft tissue** (cellulitis, abscess) | **Staphylococcus aureus** (Staph + Strep species) | **Coagulase positive, haemolytic gram-positive cocci** · commonest in diabetics + non-diabetics · S. aureus + group A strep in children |
| **Osteomyelitis** (non-diabetic) | **Pseudomonas aeruginosa** | **Gram-negative rod, single polar flagellum** (motile) · **secretes blue-green pigment in vitro** · grape-like odour · biofilm, exotoxin, moist environments |
| Through **rubber sole of shoe** | **Pseudomonas** (TO) | Grows in **moist inner layers of a used athletic shoe** (not new / very old) → inoculated with rubber |
| Diabetic osteomyelitis | S. aureus more common · **polymicrobial** | Diabetics → more osteomyelitis, more organisms |
| Bodies of water (TO) | Pseudomonas, **Aeromonas hydrophila**, **Vibrio**, **Mycobacterium marinum** | — |
| **Toothpick** (TO) | **Eikenella** (oropharyngeal flora) | — |

**Pseudomonas ✗ traps** — no flagellum · spore-forming · ferments lactose · clear colonies (colonies **blue-green** on nutrient agar)

| ✗ Distractor organism | Description | Classic association |
|---|---|---|
| **Staph epidermidis** | Coagulase-negative, non-haemolytic GPC | **Periprosthetic joint infection** |
| **Salmonella** | GN rod, **multiple (peritrichous) flagella** | **Osteomyelitis in sickle cell** |
| **Clostridium tetani** | Spore-forming GP rod, multiple flagella, anaerobe | **Tetanospasmin** blocks glycine + GABA release |
| **Pasteurella multocida** | — | **Animal bites** |
| **Eikenella corrodens** | — | **Human bites** (OB) · toothpick (TO) |
| **Vibrio** | — | Marine environments |

## 3 Presentation

**Timing** — often **weeks to a month** after injury · limp · fever, drainage

**Exam** — swollen, tender foot · obvious wound ± tract · **well-demarcated erythema** · ± lymphadenopathy

![Plantar nail puncture wound with surrounding erythema](../../Orthobullets/attachments/ob7026-screen-shot-2014-10-20-at-1-33-14-pm.jpg)

**Relapse after initial improvement** — suggests **osteomyelitis or retained foreign body**

**Child, nail 1 wk ago, heel pain, tender calcaneal tuberosity** — **calcaneal osteomyelitis** (Pseudomonas)

## 4 Diagnosis & Classification

### Key radiology findings

| Study | Use |
|---|---|
| **X-ray** (AP + lateral) | **Normal early** · bone destruction later · **exclude radiopaque foreign body**, fracture · multiple views for **all puncture wounds except knife wounds** (TO) |
| **Ultrasound** | **Most accurate for wood / toothpick** in plantar arch (abrupt density change) · **non-radiopaque organic FB** + abscess · detects wooden FB **≥ 5 mm** (TO) |
| **MRI** | **Rule out osteomyelitis** (1–2%) · obtain **before operative I&D** · osteomyelitis: **low T1 / high T2 marrow** · abscess + retained FB on post-contrast T1 |
| Bone scan | not indicated when imaging shows no osteomyelitis |

**Missed foreign bodies** — **\~40%** missed on initial examination (TO)

## 5 Management

```
Foot puncture wound
├─ Acute (hours), no infection → tetanus + bedside I&D ± antipseudomonal cover
├─ Soft-tissue infection only → oral amoxicillin-clavulanate
├─ Fails oral abx / through shoe sole → add ciprofloxacin
└─ Delayed deep infection, abscess, FB, osteomyelitis → surgical debridement + IV abx
       └─ improving → convert to PO antibiotics
```

### Prophylaxis & tetanus (TO)

**Prophylactic antibiotics** — **controversial · not advised for all** (TO) · no standard regimen; acute injury should cover **Pseudomonas** (Orthobullets)

**Indications (TO)** — **highly contaminated** · presentation **> 6 h** · **at-risk host (diabetes, immunosuppression)**

**Agents (TO)** — 1st-generation oral cephalosporin · antistaphylococcal penicillin · fluoroquinolone · macrolide

**Through the sole of a shoe** — **antibiotic prophylaxis against Pseudomonas**: oral **fluoroquinolone (ciprofloxacin)** ✓ (TO)

**Tetanus booster** — all puncture wounds with **outdated or unknown** immune status

| Previous tetanus toxoid doses (TO) | Clean, minor: TT vaccine | Clean, minor: HTIG | All other wounds: TT vaccine | All other wounds: HTIG |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **< 3 or unknown** | **Yes** | No | **Yes** | **Yes** |
| **> 3** | Yes if last dose **> 10 yr** | No | Yes if last dose **> 5 yr** | No |

**TO "all FALSE EXCEPT" key** — **antipseudomonal prophylaxis through shoe sole** · keyed false: osteomyelitis = S. aureus, needles commonest, **TT for clean minor wound if status unknown** (the TO's own table says Yes) — read the options

### Microbiology & antibiotic choice

| Situation | Antibiotic |
|---|---|
| Soft-tissue only (S. aureus, β-haemolytic strep) | **Amoxicillin-clavulanate** = first line |
| Pseudomonas, adult | **Ciprofloxacin 750 mg PO q12h** · levofloxacin 750 mg PO q24h — **fluoroquinolones = only oral class active vs Pseudomonas** |
| Pseudomonas, **child** | **Ceftazidime or cefepime** (no fluoroquinolones in children) |
| Fluoroquinolone contraindicated | Parenteral **ceftazidime**, piperacillin, ticarcillin-clavulanate |
| Post-debridement | **IV antipseudomonal** → PO once clinically improved |

**✗ Antibiotic traps** — doubling amoxicillin dose · **TMP-SMX** (MRSA; poor Pseudomonas cover) · **rifampin** (TB) · **vancomycin** (gram-positive, MRSA) · **cephalexin** (superficial gram-positive skin) · **ampicillin-sulbactam** (human bite, polymicrobial)

### Deep infection – surgical debridement & IV antibiotics

**Indications** — **late / delayed presentation with deep infection ± osteomyelitis** · **foreign body removal** · **no improvement with oral antibiotics**

**Technique** — **tract + soft-tissue debridement** · deep culture · **bony curettage** (if osteomyelitis) · **pack with wick** → heal by secondary intention

**Established infection 24–36 h after plantar puncture** — **admit for parenteral antibiotics** (Eidelman)

**Rubber-soled shoe, months of pain + swelling** — retained **rubber foreign body → abscess** → **surgical wound exploration, débridement + IV (broad-spectrum) antibiotics**\
✗ Wrong options: bone scan · IV antibiotics alone · reinstituting oral antibiotics · expectant · percutaneous / CT-guided drainage (too small to retrieve FB) · débridement + hydrotherapy

**Calcaneal osteomyelitis** — **IV antibiotics + debridement**

## X vs Y

| | **Soft-tissue infection** | **Osteomyelitis** |
|---|---|---|
| Rate | \~10% | 1–2% |
| Organism | **S. aureus** (coag +, haemolytic GPC) | **Pseudomonas** (GN rod, polar flagellum, pigment) |
| Oral drug | Amoxicillin-clavulanate | **Ciprofloxacin** (child: ceftazidime) |
| Surgery | If abscess / FB / no response | Debridement + bony curettage + IV abx |

## How it's asked

**"Commonest organism, soft-tissue infection after puncture?"** → **Staphylococcus aureus**

**"Characteristic of that organism?"** → **coagulase positive, haemolytic gram-positive cocci**

**"Commonest organism, osteomyelitis after puncture (non-diabetic)?"** → **Pseudomonas**

**"True of the osteomyelitis organism?"** → **secretes a pigment in vitro**\
✗ Wrong options: no flagellum · spores · lactose fermenter

**"Nail through shoe, failed amoxicillin-clavulanate — add?"** → **ciprofloxacin**

**"Child, calcaneal osteomyelitis after nail — drug?"** → **may be treated with ceftazidime**\
✗ Wrong options: TMP-SMX · cephalexin · amox-clav

**"Puncture wounds — only true statement?"** → **antipseudomonal prophylaxis if through shoe sole** (TO)

**"Commonest object causing puncture wounds?"** → **nails** (TO)

**"Most significant host risk factor?"** → **diabetes mellitus** (TO)

**"Rubber-soled shoe, 2–3 months pain, abscess on MRI — next?"** → **surgical wound exploration, debridement + IV antibiotics**

**"Imaging to locate a toothpick in the plantar arch?"** → **ultrasound**

**"Imaging to rule out osteomyelitis?"** → **MRI**
