### OVERVIEW
- Lecture covers patient positioning, basic spinal instruments, and principles of pedicle screw fixation.
- Forms the foundation for understanding lumbar, thoracic, and cervical spinal surgeries.
- Emphasis on safe exposure, identification of landmarks, and use of key tools for decompression and fixation. “Positioning is half the surgery in spine cases – perfect exposure begins before the incision.” – Dr. C S Vishnu Prasath

PATIENT POSITIONING
### For Lumbar Spine Surgery
- Position: Prone on bolsters or pillows.
- Proximal bolster: at the nipple level.
- Distal bolster: at the ASIS (Anterior Superior Iliac Spine).
### Key Principles
- Abdomen must hang free → prevents IVC compression & reduces epidural venous bleeding.
- Avoid pressure on the rib cage and chest.
- Ensure bladder is empty / catheterize for long surgeries (>2 hrs).
### Bolster Types

| Type | Use | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Small bolster | Instrumentation cases | Maintains lumbar lordosis |
| Large bolster | Discectomy / Decompression | Flexes hips → widens interlaminar space |

Tip: Use small bolsters for lordosis restoration and large bolsters when wider
exposure is needed.

### Why Pedicle Screws?
- Pedicle = strongest part of vertebra (cortical shell + cancellous core).
- Provides 3-column fixation and superior biomechanical control.

### ♂ INDICATIONS FOR INSTRUMENTATION IN DISC SURGERY
- Instability on flexion-extension X-ray.
- Recurrent disc prolapse.
- Severe degeneration in obese / heavy patients → prophylactic stabilization.
- Avoid unnecessary fusion – degeneration is progressive; operate only with valid indication.

### EXAM & VIVA PEARLS
- Small bolster = lordosis; large bolster = flexion.
- Cobb’s elevator → detach paraspinal muscles.
- Kerrison rongeur → remove lamina / ligamentum flavum.
- Love’s retractor → nerve root + suction.
- Ball-tip probe → check all five pedicle walls.
- Monoaxial = stronger; Polyaxial = easier.
- Pedicle screw angulation: L1–10°, L3–15°, L5–20°.
- T11–T12 = straight path; S1 = 25° inferior angulation.
- Banana (TLIF) > Rectangular (PLIF) biomechanically.
- Harm’s cage → anterior reconstruction after corpectomy.
- Instrumentation indicated only in instability or recurrence.
