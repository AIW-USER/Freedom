Prasath (Sep 2025)
### Overview / Key Concepts
- A practical, surgeon's approach to spine MRI: disc prolapse (location and migration), canal stenosis, degenerative disc disease, Schmorl nodes, degenerative vs lytic listhesis, fractures in the ankylosed spine, cervical myelopathy and OPLL, and infection vs metastasis vs osteoporotic fracture.
- Always ask for WHOLE SPINE SCREENING: about 30% have a second pathology (e.g. tandem lumbar/thoracic stenosis with cervical myelopathy, skip lesions in TB, metastases elsewhere). Radiologists often screen for level counting but do not print it: ask for the screening film.
- Write the clinical question on the request (e.g. "MRI lumbar spine with whole spine screening, left leg radiating pain").
- Magnet: 1.5 T is standard and adequate; avoid low-field 0.2–0.5 T; 3 T for specific purposes.
- Sequences: T1, T2 and STIR. Oedema is dark on T1, bright on T2, and stands out most on STIR.

### Lumbar Disc Prolapse: Describe the Location

| Plane | Locations |
|---|---|
| Sagittal (3) | At disc level; SUPERIOR (up-) migrated; INFERIOR (down-) migrated |
| Axial (4) | Central, paracentral, foraminal, far lateral (extraforaminal) |

- Small bulges that do not compress a root are usually not significant; look for the one disc that matches the symptoms when several levels are abnormal.
- Commonest levels: L4–5, then L5–S1, then L3–4 (most motion and stress in the lower lumbar spine).
- Root affected: L4–5 paracentral disc compresses the TRAVERSING L5 root; L4–5 far lateral/foraminal disc compresses the EXITING L4 root; a markedly up-migrated disc may affect the root above.
- Axilla vs shoulder: inferiorly migrated fragments sit in the AXILLA (between dura and root); superiorly migrated ones at the SHOULDER of the root. Hook fragments out rather than pull, to protect the root. Knowing the exact location is essential for minimally invasive surgery.
- Far lateral discs are often missed when only T2 sagittal images are read: check the axial cuts and the parasagittal foramina (a normal foramen shows the dark root surrounded by fat/CSF).

### Fractures in the Ankylosed Spine
- In ankylosing spondylitis/DISH, any fracture is UNSTABLE until proven otherwise (e.g. L1 flexion-distraction, C6–7 Chance-type fractures through the fused spine).
- CT is the best investigation (MRI adds value); fix at least three levels above and three below (vs two and two in normal bone). Cervical injuries carry the highest neurological risk.

### Cervical Spine
- Canal and foramina are small, so finding the disc needs care: review sagittal then axial cuts at each level.
- First rule out myelopathy (it progresses naturally) before calling it radiculopathy.
- Read: (1) compression level(s); (2) the compressing structure (disc-osteophyte complex anteriorly, ligamentum flavum hypertrophy posteriorly, OPLL); (3) cord signal: T2 hyperintensity = cord oedema/myelomalacia.
- OPLL: a dark band behind the vertebral BODIES (not just at disc level) compressing the cord; confirm with CT; may extend from the skull base into the thoracic spine (whole spine screening avoids missing it). Surgery for OPLL/OLF carries a higher risk of neurological worsening.
- Thoracic discs are less common; look for them on the screening images.

- Pathological collapse with paravertebral and epidural collections (abscess) and soft-tissue oedema around the vertebral body.

- Epidural abscess without bone/disc destruction: surgical emergency; drainage alone may be enough.
- TB neurological deficit: paraplegia of active disease (abscess, sequestrum, disc) vs late- onset paraplegia (healed kyphosis stretching the cord).
- TB vs pyogenic: TB shows more vertebral BODY destruction (slow, late presentation, cold abscess); pyogenic shows early DISC SPACE reduction (toxic patient, presents early). Biopsy and culture whenever possible.
- Early infection before destruction: T1 hypointense, T2/STIR hyperintense vertebra and disc.

### Infection vs Metastasis vs Osteoporotic Fracture
Feature Infection Metastasis Osteoporosis
(spondylodiscitis)

| Disc | Early involvement | Spared | Spared |
|---|---|---|---|
| End plates | Irregular, destroyed | Sharp destruction | Intact / mild concavity |
| T1 | Hypointense vertebra + disc | Hypointense focal/multiple | Diffuse hypointense (acute fracture) |
| T2/STIR | Hyperintense vertebra + disc | Hyperintense lesion | Local hyperintensity at fracture |
| Enhancement | Disc + bone + abscess (rim enhancement) | Lesion ± soft-tissue mass (uniform) | Fracture line / marrow only |
| Paravertebral | Abscess common | Tumour mass possible | Minimal |
| Levels | Contiguous | Multiple, skip lesions | Single (unless multiple fractures) |

- Metastasis: posterior cortical BULGING into the canal (soft tumour-filled bone) and skip lesions.
- Osteoporotic fracture: oedema = fresh fracture; no oedema = old, healed fracture.

### Screen the Hip and SI Joints
- Spine MRI should include the hips and SI joints: groin/thigh pain may be hip osteoarthritis (example: left hip OA treated surgically); unilateral buttock pain may be SI joint strain; SI hyperintensity suggests inflammatory sacroiliitis.

### Exam & Viva Pearls
- Whole spine screening: 30% have a second lesion.
- 3 sagittal and 4 axial disc locations; paracentral = traversing root, far lateral = exiting root.
- Down-migrated fragment in the axilla, up-migrated at the shoulder.
- Degenerative listhesis L4–5, traversing root, stenotic canal; lytic L5–S1, exiting root, wide canal.
- Ankylosed spine fracture: unstable; CT; fix 3 above and 3 below.
- Disc involved = infection; disc spared = metastasis/osteoporosis.

### Take-Home Summary
- Order the right MRI (1.5 T, whole spine screening), read it systematically in sagittal and axial planes, name the exact location and compressing structure, and use disc, endplate, signal and soft-tissue patterns to separate degeneration, infection, tumour and fracture.
