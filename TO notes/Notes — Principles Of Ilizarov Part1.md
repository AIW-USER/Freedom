Principles of Ilizarov Part 1 – Dr. Daivik T Shetty
Overview
This session focuses on understanding the Ilizarov apparatus, its components,
instrumentation, and the logic behind its construction. Dr. Daivik explains that Ilizarov
is a circular external fixator capable of handling tensile, compressive, shear, bending,
and torsional forces due to its tubular frame design.
Faculty Remark: “You cannot master Ilizarov unless you first know every instrument
by name, size, and purpose.”

## Introduction to Ilizarov
Concept
- Circular external fixator designed by Dr. Gavriil Ilizarov, known as the Magician of Kurgan.
- Mimics the cylindrical shape of bone, distributing forces efficiently.
- Allows correction of:
  - Tension (distraction osteogenesis)
  - Compression (non union)
  - Bending (angular deformity corrections using hinges)
  - Shear (axis deviation)
  - Torsion (derotation)
  - Combined forces
Accordion Technique
Alternate compression and distraction, used in non unions to stimulate regenerate
formation.

## Components of Ilizarov Apparatus
Primary Components
These connect the bone to the frame. 1. Rings 2. Wires (K wires, olive wires) 3. Wire

Secondary Components
fixation bolts and buckles 4. Schanz pins / Orthofix pins 5. Pin clamps

Used to build and modify the frame. - Spanners, wrenches - Rods, posts - Hinges -
Plates, supports - Washers - Nuts, sockets - Bolts, bushings

## Rings
### Key Functions
- Support trans fixation wires and half pins.
- Build the fixator frame when joined.

### Ring Structure
- Bear tensioned wire forces up to 150 kg.

- Perforated holes are 8 mm wide.
- Distance between holes 4 mm.
- Centre to centre distance 11 mm.
- Ring thickness 5 mm.
- Must remain perpendicular to bone axis.

### Half Rings
- Made of stainless steel or carbon fiber.
- Ends have offsets that help achieve coplanar assembly.
- Must be connected in handshake position.
- Used widely because they can be removed intraoperatively, unlike full rings.
Sizes
- Internal diameter 80–240 mm.
- Pediatric: 80–140 mm.
- Adult: 150–240 mm.
### Rod and Bolt Diameter
- Connecting rod / bolt diameter: 6 mm.
- Hole diameter is 2 mm larger to allow angulation.

### Full Rings
Advantages: - More perforations, 3 extra holes compared to half rings. - Useful for
adding secondary components.
Disadvantages: - Cannot be detached once fixed. - Less flexible. - Commonly used at
foot end frames.

<!-- page 3 -->

### Special Ring Configurations
### 5/8th Ring
- Used around joints where mobility is needed.
- Proximal tibia, distal femur, elbow.
- Sizes: 130, 150, 160 mm.
- Biomechanically weaker; always used with supplemental fixation.
### Omega Rings
- Designed for proximal humerus.
- Contoured to fit over deltoid.
- Weak, require multiple fixation levels.
Arches
### Russian Arch
- Earlier design.
- Semi circular with multiple perforations.
- Risk of sciatic nerve injury.
### Italian Arch
- Modified with slotted holes.
- Accepts half pins instead of wires.
- Safer around proximal femur.

## Choosing Ring Size
- Measure circumference: D = circumference / π.
- Thumb rule: 2 finger breadths above, 3 finger breadths below.
- Alternatively: Bone diameter + 60 mm = ring diameter.

## Connecting Elements
Rods
### Standard Connecting Rods
- Connect rings.
- Diameter 6 mm, pitch 1 mm.
- Length 60–400 mm.
- Four rods typically connect two rings.

<!-- page 4 -->

### Slotted / Cannulated Rods
- Function as connecting rods + allow K wire passage for pulling.
### Telescopic Rods
- Stiffer than threaded rods.
- Useful in bone lengthening.
### Graduated Telescopic Rod
- Invented by ASAMI Italy.
- Square head, hand adjustable.
- Automatic locking lever.
- Allows precise 0.25 mm adjustments.
Exam Pearl: All rods in Ilizarov have 1 mm pitch to standardize
distraction/compression.

## Wires
Properties
- Stainless steel.
- Available as beaded and non beaded.
- Pediatric: 1.5 mm, adult: 1.8 mm.
- Minimum 2–3 wires per ring.
### Wire Tips
- Bayonet tip: cancellous bone (metaphysis, epiphysis).
- Trochar tip: cortical bone.
### Olive Wires
- Have beads.
- Used for:
  - Interfragmentary compression.
  - Stability.
  - Distraction.
  - Fragment translation.
### Half Pins / Schanz Pins
- Alternative to olive wires.
- Sizes: 4.5, 5, 6 mm.
- Better biomechanical performance.

<!-- page 5 -->

## Bolts and Buckles
### Connecting Bolts
- Head: 10 mm hexagonal.
- Shaft diameter 6 mm.

### Wire Fixation Bolts
- Sizes: 10 mm, 16 mm (most used), 30 mm, optional 25 mm.

- Cannulated: central wire passage.
- Slotted: eccentric passage.

### Wire Fixation Buckles
- Enable coupled effect wires to prevent torsion.

- Used when no holes available.
- Attach directly to ring surface.
- Types:
  - Dual sided (two wires)
  - Detachable (preferred)

## Nuts
- Types: Full (6 mm), Three quarter (5 mm), Half (3 mm).
- Threads = thickness (6 threads in full nut).
- Never reuse nuts or bolts.

Special Nuts
### Quadrangular Special Nut
- Has turn markings (1 to 4).
- 15 threads, very stable.
### Nylon / Stopper Nut
- Nylon insert prevents loosening.
- Used only for hinges.

## Washers
- Types: Plain, Grooved, Paired Spheric.
### Preferred Washer for Wire Fixation
- 2 mm washer, 14 mm diameter.

<!-- page 6 -->

### Washer for Italian Arches
- 2 mm wide body washer.
### Paired Spheric Washers
- Allow 7.5–15 degrees angulation.

## Other Components
Wrenches
- Box wrench, combination wrench.

Plates
- Always used in pairs for torque application.

- Short & long connecting plates.
- Twisted plates: horizontal to vertical transition.

### Threaded Sockets and Bushings
- Curved plates: for oblique wires.

- Threaded socket: internal threads.

Posts
- Bushing: unthreaded, 1 mm wider for easy rod passage.

- Male post: protruding threaded leg.

### Rancho Cube
- Female post: threaded hole.

Tensioners
- Connects half pins to half rings.

- Standard and dynamometric.
- Wire tension:
  - 1.5 mm wire: 90–105 kg
  - 1.8 mm wire: 130–140 kg
- Beveled edge up, flat edge down during tensioning.

### Exam Pearls
- Ilizarov = circular external fixator capable of handling all force types.
- Rings must remain perpendicular to bone axis.
- Full rings have 3 more holes than half rings.

<!-- page 7 -->

- Bayonet tip for cancellous bone, trochar tip for cortical bone.
- Italian arch has slotted holes for half pins.
- Rod pitch universally 1 mm for predictable distraction.
- Wide body washer used with Italian arches.
- 5/8th and Omega rings are structurally weaker.
- Stopper nut (nylon nut) used only for hinges.
- Never reuse nuts or bolts.

Final Faculty Insight: “Know your instruments first. Mechanics and frame assembly
only make sense once every bolt, washer, rod, and ring becomes familiar.”
