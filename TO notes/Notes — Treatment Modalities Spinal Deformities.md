Dr. Vishnu’s Quote to Remember:

IDIOPATHIC SCOLIOSIS
Definition: Lateral spinal curvature >10° (Cobb’s method) without identifiable cause.
Diagnosis: Diagnosis of exclusion – no neurological, structural, or syndromic findings.

| Type | Age Range |
|---|---|
| Infantile | 0–3 years |
| Juvenile | 4–9 years |
| Adolescent (AIS) | 10–20 years – Most common |

Typical Pattern: - Right thoracic curve
- Female predominance (6:1)

NATURAL HISTORY & PROGRESSION
Untreated AIS may lead to: - Early degenerative changes (in 40s instead of 50s)
- Cosmetic deformity & psychological distress
- Restrictive lung disease in severe curves (>90°)

ASSESSMENT OF SKELETAL MATURITY
### Risser Grading (Iliac Apophysis):

| Grade | Ossification % | Fusion Status |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | None visible | Immature |
| 1 | 25% | Early growth |
| 2 | 50% | Accelerating growth |
| 3 | 75% | Decelerating growth |
| 4 | 100% coverage | Nearly mature |
| 5 | Fully fused | Mature |

### Other Indicators:
- Triradiate cartilage closure → marks onset of peak growth.

- Fusion of greater trochanteric epiphysis → late growth stage.
Growth Peak: - Occurs at Risser 0–1 (around menarche in girls).
- Little progression after Risser 4–5.

### MANAGEMENT STRATEGY – AIS

| Cobb Angle | Skeletal Maturity | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| <25° | Immature | Observation (6-month X-rays) |
| 25°–45° | Immature & flexible | Bracing |
| >45° | Mature / Progressive | Surgery |

♀ OBSERVATION
Indication: - Curve <25° with no progression.
- Interval X-rays every 6 months.
- Frequency of follow-up based on Risser stage: - Risser 0–2: Every 6 months.
- Risser 4–5: Yearly.

BRACING
Indications: - 25°–45° Cobb angle.
- Flexible curve.
- Skeletally immature (Risser 0–2).
Goal: Prevent progression, not correct deformity.
Duration: 16–23 hours/day (minimum 12 hrs required). Continue until skeletal maturity
(Risser 4–5 or 2 years post-menarchal), then wean over 6 months.

| Curve Apex | Brace Type | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Above T7 | Milwaukee (CTLSO) | Extends to neck; used for high thoracic curves |
| T7 or Below | Boston (TLSO) | Underarm brace (most common) |
| Night Use | Charleston Bending Brace | Used for mild/moderate flexible curves |

Bracing Not Indicated: - Skeletally mature.
- Curve >45° or <25° without progression.
- Obese patients.
- Thoracic lordosis present.
Success Criteria: - No curve progression.
- Avoidance of surgery.
- Cosmetic satisfaction.

SURGICAL MANAGEMENT – AIS
Indications: 1. Curve >45–50° (progressive even post maturity).
2. Pain or cosmetic deformity.
3. Failure of bracing.

### Instrumentation Evolution

| Generation | System | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1st | Harrington Rods | Distraction-bas ed; risk of flat back |
| 2nd | Hartshill Rectangle | Sublaminar wires; limited derotation |
| 3rd | Pedicle Screw Constructs | 3D correction (rotation + translation) – gold standard |

### Level Selection
  - Apex Vertebra: Maximum deviation from midline.

  - End Vertebrae: Most tilted towards apex.

  - Stable Vertebra: Bisected by CSVL (Central Sacral Vertical Line).

  - Upper Instrumented Vertebra (UIV):
  - = Upper End Vertebra + 1.

  - Or determined by shoulder level rule:
    - Right shoulder high → T4

- Equal shoulders → T3

    - Left shoulder high → T2

### Correction Techniques

| Technique | Description |
|---|---|
| Ponte’s Osteotomy | Posterior release – excision of facet joints for flexibility |
| Cantilever Technique | Levering rod to bring curve to neutral alignment |
| Rod Rotation (Derotation) | 3D correction – converts coronal deformity to sagittal alignment |

### Intraoperative Neuromonitoring

| Type | Monitors | Key Point |
|---|---|---|
| SSEP | Sensory tracts | Continuous monitoring (posterior column) |
| MEP | Motor tracts | Intermittent; drop >50% amplitude = warning |

If Neurological Event: - Check technical setup, BP, Hb.
- Reverse correction, transfuse, consider wake-up test.
- Remove implants if instability.

### Common Surgical Complications
- Neurologic injury (cord/root).

- Pseudoarthrosis (1–2%).

- Infection (1–2%).

- Flat back syndrome (esp. with Harrington rods).

- Crankshaft phenomenon – anterior column continues growing after posterior fusion.

- SMA syndrome – duodenal compression post-correction.

- Hardware failure.

CONGENITAL SCOLIOSIS
Non-Operative

| Approach | Indication |
|---|---|
| Observation | Incarcerated or non-segmented hemivertebra (no progression) |
| Bracing | Not effective for primary curve; may help compensatory curves |

### Operative Options

| Procedure | Indication |
|---|---|
| Posterior Fusion ± Osteotomy | Unilateral bar with contralateral hemi-vertebra |
| Anterior + Posterior Fusion / | Young (<10 yrs girls, <12 yrs boys) |
| Vertebrectomy | with progressive deformity |
| Growing Rod Constructs | Delay final fusion, allow thoracic growth |
| Rib Osteotomies | For multiple fused ribs → prevent TIS |
| Hemi-Vertebrectomy | 3–8 yrs, progressive deformity with kyphosis |

Complications: Crankshaft, short stature, neurological injury, soft-tissue compromise.

### EARLY ONSET / GROWTH-FRIENDLY TECHNIQUES

| Stage | Method | Indication |
|---|---|---|
| <5 yrs | Casting (EDF Method) | Primary correction under traction; repeated every 6 months |
| 5–10 yrs | Growing Rods (Dual/Single) | Allow spinal and thoracic growth; distracted every 6 months |
| >10 yrs | Definitive Fusion | After thoracic height ~22 cm |

Avoid Posterior Fusion in <10 yrs due to crankshaft phenomenon.

### EXAM & VIVA PEARLS
- AIS = right thoracic curve, adolescent girls.

<!-- page 7 -->

- Cobb’s >10° = scoliosis; >25° before maturity = progression risk.
- Risser 0–2 → active growth → close follow-up.
- Bracing only 25–45° and flexible curves.
- Surgical cutoff: >45° (mature) or >25° (immature + progression).
- Pedicle screw + rod rotation = 3D correction gold standard.
- Avoid early fusion (<10 yrs) → crankshaft phenomenon.
- Always use intraoperative neuromonitoring.

### TAKE-HOME SUMMARY
- Observation → Bracing → Surgery = progressive ladder.
- Always assess skeletal maturity (Risser + triradiate + trochanter fusion).
- Posterior fusion is mainstay; anterior fusion rare.
- Ponte’s osteotomy, cantilever, and rod rotation are key corrective tools.
- Growth rods & casting preserve thoracic development in early onset scoliosis.
- Neuromonitoring essential for safe correction.
- Goal: Balanced spine, preserved function, and halted progression.
