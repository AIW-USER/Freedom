Target Ortho Quick Revision Notes – Clinical
Assessment of Spinal Deformities
Faculty: Dr. C.S. Vishnu Prasath

OVERVIEW
Definition:
According to the Scoliosis Research Society (SRS), scoliosis is defined as a lateral
curvature of the spine greater than 10°, measured by the Cobb method on a
standing thoracolumbar X-ray.
Clinical Evaluation Framework: 1. History taking
2. Clinical examination
3. Radiological assessment

CLINICAL HISTORY
### 1⃣ Age Classification

| Type | Age Range |
|---|---|
| Infantile Idiopathic Scoliosis | 0–3 years |
| Juvenile Idiopathic Scoliosis | 4–9 years |
| Adolescent Idiopathic Scoliosis | 10–20 years |
| Congenital Scoliosis | From birth |

Pubertal Growth Spurts: - Girls: 10–14 years
- Boys: 12–16 years
Clinical Note:
Scoliotic deformity before 5 years → high risk of restrictive lung disease due to limited
thoracic growth.

### 2⃣ Gender Distribution

| Type | Male : Female Ratio |
|---|---|
| Infantile | 1:1 to 2:1 |
| Juvenile | 1:3 (<6 yrs), 1:6 (>6 yrs) |
| Adolescent | 1:6 |

As curve magnitude increases, female predominance rises.

<!-- page 2 -->

### 3⃣ Birth & Developmental History
- Birth asphyxia & delayed milestones: suggests Cerebral Palsy → Neuromuscular scoliosis.

- Regression of milestones: indicates Neuromuscular disorders.

- Multiple congenital anomalies: suggest syndromic scoliosis (e.g., Arthrogryposis multiplex congenita).

### 4⃣ Special Clinical Complaints
- Pain associated with deformity: May indicate osteoid osteoma, disc pathology, infection.

- Weakness in limbs: Suggests spinal cord pathology (syringomyelia, tethered cord).

- Bowel/bladder disturbance: Red flag → possible cord compression → MRI mandatory.

CLINICAL EXAMINATION
### A. Morphometric Measurements

| Parameter | Description | Importance |
|---|---|---|
| Standing Height | Subischial height + sitting height | Monitors growth & puberty onset (>0.5 cm/month) |
| Sitting Height | More reliable for spinal growth | Indicates spinal column length |
| Arm Span | ≈ 97% of standing height | ↑ in Marfan’s syndrome |
| Weight | Doubles between 10–17 years | Obesity → brace failure |

Overweight → poor brace compliance; Underweight → delayed menarche.

B. General & Systemic Examination
Skin:
Look for neurocutaneous markers indicating spinal dysraphism:
- Hairy patches

<!-- page 3 -->

- Dimples
- Lipomatous lesions
- Postoperative scars of tethered cord release
Neurofibromatosis:
- Café-au-lait spots
- Skin tags
- Axillary freckles
Connective Tissue Disorders:
- Marfan’s syndrome – tall, long limbs, ligamentous laxity.
- Ehlers–Danlos syndrome – hyperelastic skin & joints.
Syndromic Features:
- Arthrogryposis multiplex congenita – multiple joint deformities.
- Mucopolysaccharidoses – coarse facies, dwarfism, intellectual delay.
- Epiphyseal dysplasias – multiple joint stiffness.

### C. Systemic Examination

| System | Key Findings |
|---|---|
| Cardiac | 7% of congenital scoliosis → congenital heart defects (septal/valvular, aortic root dilation) |
| Respiratory | Lung growth completed by 8 yrs; thoracic curves >100° ↓ vital capacity (<70%), >120° → respiratory failure |
| Abdominal | Hepatosplenomegaly (storage disorder), umbilical/inguinal hernia |
| Genitourinary | 6–12% congenital scoliosis with renal anomalies (ectopic kidney, PUJ obstruction, renal agenesis) |

SPINE EXAMINATION
Inspection
- Plane & side of deformity: Scoliosis (coronal), Kyphosis (sagittal), Kyphoscoliosis (combined).

- Shoulder level:
  - Same side high → major structural curve.

  - Opposite side high → proximal structural curve.

<!-- page 4 -->

- Coronal balance: Head centered over pelvis; C7 plumb line passes through gluteal cleft.
- Sagittal balance: C7 plumb line through posterosuperior corner of S1.
  - Positive imbalance: Plumb line anterior → stooping.

  - Negative imbalance: Posterior → excessive lordosis.

Palpation
- Spinal tenderness: Infection/disc pathology if present.

- ASIS level: Unequal → leg length discrepancy or fixed pelvic obliquity.
  - Corrects on equalizing leg length → LLD.

  - Does not correct → fixed pelvic obliquity (may need pelvic fixation during surgery).

NEUROLOGICAL ASSESSMENT
### Higher Functions & Cranial Nerves
- Check consciousness, orientation, and speech.

- Horizontal gaze palsy with progressive scoliosis (HGPPS): absence of conjugate horizontal eye movements (ROBO3 gene mutation).
### Gait Assessment
- Observe balance, stride, and support.

<!-- page 5 -->

- Myelopathic gait: cord compression/stretch in scoliosis.

- Spastic gait: CP / UMN causes.

- Crouched gait: pelvic obliquity and hamstring tightness.
### Motor System

| Parameter | Observation | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Bulk | Atrophy → tethered cord / SMA / polio | Hypertrophy → DMD |
| Tone | Hypotonia (LMN) / Hypertonia (UMN) | Neuromuscular scoliosis |
| Power | Decreased in neuromuscular / cord anomalies | — |

### Sensory & Reflexes
- Sensory: Syringomyelia → loss of pain/temp; crude touch preserved.

- Abdominal Reflex (T7–T12): Asymmetrical = UMN lesion (syrinx/diastematomyelia/tumor).

- Plantar Reflex: Extensor → UMN lesion.

- Deep Tendon Reflexes: Exaggerated → cord involvement.

RADIOLOGICAL ASSESSMENT
### Basic Imaging

| Modality | View | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| X-ray | Whole spine (standing AP & lateral) | Assess curves, balance, and flexibility |
| CT Scan | Axial details | Pedicle size, segmentation, bone anomalies |
| MRI | Cord status | Syrinx, tethered cord, ACM, tumor |

<!-- page 6 -->

### X-ray Assessment Components

| Parameter | Description |
|---|---|
| Apex Vertebra | Farthest deviated vertebra from spinal midline or maximum rotation |
| End Vertebra | Maximal tilt toward apex |
| Neutral Vertebra | No rotation (both pedicles visible) |
| Stable Vertebra | Bisected by Central Sacral Vertical Line (CSVL) |

### Cobb’s Angle Measurement
- Tangents drawn to superior endplate of upper end vertebra and inferior endplate of lower end vertebra.

- Intersection of perpendiculars gives Cobb’s angle.

- Structural curve: Bending Cobb ≥ 25°.

- Non-structural: <25° on bending films.
### Lenke Classification (Overview)

| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| 1 | Main thoracic curve |
| 2 | Double thoracic curve |
| 3 | Double major curve |
| 4 | Triple major curve |
| 5 | Thoracolumbar/Lumbar curve |
| 6 | TL/Lumbar + Main thoracic curve |

### RADIOLOGICAL BALANCE
- Coronal balance: Distance between C7 plumb line & CSVL >2 cm → imbalance.

- Sagittal balance: Distance between C7 plumb line & posterosuperior S1 >2 cm → imbalance.

### EXAM & VIVA PEARLS
- Scoliosis = lateral curvature >10° (Cobb’s method).

<!-- page 7 -->

- Assess history → examination → radiology sequentially.

- Adams test: structural vs non-structural.

- Neurocutaneous markers = MRI mandatory.

- Left thoracic curve = rule out Arnold–Chiari with syrinx.

- Abdominal reflex asymmetry = spinal cord anomaly.

- Coronal vs Sagittal imbalance: sagittal causes more pain.

- Structural curve: bending Cobb ≥25°.

- Standing X-ray: mandatory for curve analysis.

### TAKE-HOME SUMMARY
- Comprehensive assessment = History + Clinical + Radiological.

- Always inspect for syndromic & neurocutaneous signs.

- MRI for all atypical or painful scoliosis.

- Determine balance, flexibility, & neurology before planning surgery.

- Goal: Identify cause early, prevent progression, and plan correction safely.
