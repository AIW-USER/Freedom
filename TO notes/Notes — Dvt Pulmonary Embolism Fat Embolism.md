DVT, Pulmonary Embolism & Fat Embolism – Dr. Anurag Tiwari

### Key Concepts / Overview
- Venous Thromboembolism (VTE) includes Deep Vein Thrombosis (DVT) and Pulmonary Embolism (PE) – major preventable causes of death in orthopedic patients.

- Fat Embolism Syndrome (FES) is a distinct entity following long bone fractures, causing pulmonary and neurological manifestations.

- DVT itself is not life-threatening, but embolization to the lungs causes PE, which can be fatal.
- Seen commonly after THR/TKR, pelvic, and spinal fractures due to prolonged immobilization.
Dr. Anurag’s Key Remark:
“DVT is just the tip of an iceberg – most cases are asymptomatic, but the few that
embolize can be deadly.”

Pathophysiology / Mechanism
### Coagulation Cascade
- Balance between procoagulant and anticoagulant mechanisms maintains hemostasis.
- Two pathways – Intrinsic (factors XII, XI, IX, VIII) and Extrinsic (factor VII). → Merge at factor X activation → converts prothrombin → thrombin → fibrinogen → fibrin (clot formation).
- Fibrinolysis: Plasminogen → Plasmin (via tPA) → dissolves fibrin.
- Anticoagulants act by interrupting various points in this cascade to prevent clot formation.

### Pulmonary Embolism (PE)
- Life-threatening complication of DVT.
- Classic triad: Dyspnea + Tachycardia + Tachypnea → always suspect PE in post-op patients with this triad.

| Submassive | Dyspnea with right heart strain, signs of heart failure |
| Low risk | Intermittent dyspnea, mild chest pain |

Guidelines Summary
ACCP (9th ed.) 1. THR/TKR: Chemoprophylaxis for 10–35 days.
2. Agents: LMWH, Warfarin, NOACs, Aspirin.
3. LMWH preferred. Start ≥12 hr pre- or post-op.
4. Dual prophylaxis (mechanical + chemical) during hospital stay recommended.
NICE Guidelines * THR: LMWH ×10 days + Aspirin ×28 days.
- TKR: Aspirin 75 mg ×14 days (or NOACs if contraindicated).

Complications
- Bleeding – most common.
- Heparin Induced Thrombocytopenia (HIT).
- Osteoporosis (long-term UFH).
- Warfarin Syndrome in fetus → contraindicated in pregnancy.
- Post-thrombotic syndrome (chronic venous insufficiency) – edema, varicosities post-DVT due to valve damage.

Fat Embolism Syndrome (FES)
Definition
- Fat Embolism: Fat particles in circulation (seen in 95–99% of long bone fractures).

<!-- page 5 -->

- Fat Embolism Syndrome: Systemic manifestations (pulmonary, neurologic, skin) due to fat emboli.

Clinical Features (within 24–72 hrs of trauma)
Triad:
1. Pulmonary: Dyspnea, tachypnea, hypoxemia
2. Neurological: Confusion, agitation, seizures
3. Skin: Petechial rash (neck, axilla, conjunctiva; resolves in 5–7 days)
Mnemonic: PNS → Pulmonary, Neurological, Skin.

### Gurd’s Criteria
Major Criteria Minor Criteria
1. Hypoxemia (PaO2 <60 mmHg) 1. Tachycardia
2. CNS symptoms (confusion, 2. Fever >38.5°C agitation, seizures)
3. Petechial rash 3. Icterus
    4. Fat globules in urine/sputum/retina
    5. ↓ Hb, ↓ Platelets, ↑ ESR

Diagnosis: 2 Major or 1 Major + 4 Minor.

Investigations

| Test | Findings |
|---|---|
| ABG | PaO₂ <60 mmHg |
| CXR / CT Chest | “Snowstorm” / ground-glass opacities |
| CBC | ↓ Hb, ↓ Platelets, ↑ ESR |

Management

| Step | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Prophylaxis | Early immobilization, early fixation, gentle handling |
| Supportive | Oxygen therapy, IV fluids, mechanical ventilation (if ARDS) |
| Steroids | Controversial – some studies suggest IV Methylprednisolone reduces inflammation & mortality |
| Surgery | Consider undreamed nailing or damage control orthopedics in high-risk fractures |

Dr. Anurag’s Remark:
“Fat embolism is not always fatal — timely immobilization and early fixation save lives.”

“Prevention is the best treatment. DVT and Fat Embolism are largely preventable with
