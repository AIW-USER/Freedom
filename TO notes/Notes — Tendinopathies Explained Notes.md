OVERVIEW

### TERMINOLOGY

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Tendinopathy | Chronic tendon pain and dysfunction caused by abnormal mechanical loading. |
| Tendinitis | Outdated term implying inflammation — true inflammatory cells are minimal. |
| Tendinosis | Degenerative change in tendon without significant inflammation. |
| Tear / Rupture | Structural discontinuity of the tendon fibers. |

IOC 2019 Consensus: The preferred term for chronic tendon disorders is
“Tendinopathy.”

### HISTOLOGY & COMPOSITION
- 70% water, 30% solid mass.
- Collagen (86%) — predominantly Type I; smaller amounts of Type III, IV, V, VI, IX.
- Cells: 90–95% tenocytes, 5–10% non-tenocyte cells (fibroblasts, chondrocytes, vascular cells).
- Elastin (2%), Proteoglycans (1–2%) — maintain flexibility and shock absorption.

<!-- page 2 -->

Function: - Transfer muscle-generated force to bone.
- Store and release elastic energy.
- Absorb load to protect muscles from damage.
Tendons are designed for tension, not compression.
### METABOLISM & BLOOD SUPPLY

| Aspect | Key Points |
|---|---|
| Energy Source | Aerobic metabolism (Krebs cycle) and anaerobic glycolysis. |
| Oxygen Consumption | 7–8× lower than skeletal muscle → delayed healing. |
| Vascular Supply | Dual: intrinsic (myotendinous & osteotendinous junctions) + extrinsic (paratenon/synovial sheath). |
| Innervation | Golgi tendon organs (tension sensors) + unmyelinated nociceptors (pain). |

PATHOPHYSIOLOGY
Load–Capacity Mismatch
Tendinopathy develops when load > tendon’s adaptive capacity, leading to
microfailure and dysrepair.

### CLINICAL FEATURES
- Localized pain, tenderness, stiffness.
- Morning stiffness or pain after inactivity.
- Start-up pain → improves with warm-up → returns post-activity.
- Pain on resisted eccentric contraction & passive stretch.
- May have crepitus, swelling, or palpable thickening.
Common Sites: - Lateral epicondyle, Achilles, Rotator cuff, Patellar, Gluteal, Peroneal,
Posterior tibial, Adductor longus.
Always correlate with overuse history or load spikes.
### INVESTIGATIONS

| Test | Findings |
|---|---|
| Ultrasound (Dynamic) | Hypoechoic areas, tendon thickening, neovascularization (Doppler) |
| MRI | High T2/STIR signal → edema, partial tears |
| Blood Work | Rule out systemic causes (RA, HLA-B27, Uric acid, Thyroid) |

### HISTOPATHOLOGICAL INSIGHTS
- Type I → Type III collagen shift → ↓ tensile strength.
- Tenocyte hyperplasia → rounded nuclei (metabolic activation).
- Neovascularization + nerve ingrowth → chronic pain source. Pain is often neurogenic — not purely inflammatory.

MANAGEMENT PRINCIPLES
### 1⃣ Stage-Based Approach

| Stage | Goal | Management |
|---|---|---|
| Reactive | Reduce load, control pain | Rest, load management, taping, cryotherapy |
| Dysrepair | Stimulate remodeling | Eccentric loading, manual therapy, shockwave |
| Degenerative | Restore function & prevent rupture | Progressive strengthening, regenerative therapy |

2⃣ Conservative Management
### A. Load Management
- Modify activity; maintain within pain threshold (≤4/10 rule).

- Avoid complete rest → leads to matrix weakening.
### B. Exercise Therapy (Gold Standard)

| Type | Mechanism | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Eccentric | Collagen alignment, tendon | Alfredson protocol |
| Loading | stiffness improvement | (3×15 reps × 2/day × 12 weeks) |
| Heavy Slow | Strengthens tendon + muscle | 3×/week, slower |
| Resistance | unit | tempo, progressive |

(HSR) load

| Isometric | Analgesic effect, early-stage | 5 reps × 45 sec |
|---|---|---|
| Contractions | rehab | holds, pain-free angle |

“Pain-free load is medicine — not rest.”
### C. Manual Therapy & Adjuncts
- Deep friction massage – promotes local hyperemia.

- K-taping / Strapping – for proprioceptive offloading.

- Dry needling / Percutaneous tenotomy – triggers collagen synthesis.

- Shockwave (ESWT) – best evidence for chronic Achilles & Patellar tendinopathy.

4⃣ Surgical Management
Indications: Failure of 6–9 months of structured conservative management.
Procedure: - Debridement of degenerated tissue.
- Longitudinal tenotomy or tendon reconstruction.
- Augmentation with autograft or synthetic grafts (if required).

### PREVENTION STRATEGIES
- Warm-up and dynamic stretching before activity.

- Avoid training errors (sudden mileage or load increases).

- Optimize footwear and ground surface.

- Incorporate eccentric and kinetic chain strengthening.

- Maintain adequate Vitamin D, calcium, and protein intake.

### TAKE-HOME SUMMARY
- Tendinopathy = failed healing response, not inflammation.

- Diagnosis is clinical, imaging confirms severity.

- Eccentric + HSR training are mainstays of rehab.

- Regenerative injections (PRP) helpful for chronic cases.

- Avoid prolonged rest; progressive loading restores tendon health. “You can’t rest a tendon back to health — you must train it to heal.”
