Non-Union & Bone Grafting – Dr. Anurag Tiwari

### Key Concepts / Overview
- Non-union is among the most challenging and commonly encountered complications after fracture treatment.
- It holds major theoretical and practical importance for DNB/FNB exams and clinical management.
- Successful treatment requires an understanding of both mechanical stability and biological stimulus.
Dr. Anurag’s Quote:
“Every non-union teaches us whether we respected biology or mechanics — and often,
it’s because we didn’t respect both.”

| Type | Definition / Criteria |

Mechanism of Non-Union
### Primary Factors
1. Inadequate Mechanical Stability – Poor fixation, implant failure, distraction, or excessive motion.

2. Inadequate Biological Activity – Loss of osteogenic potential or poor vascularity.

| Mechanism | Radiological Appearance |
|---|---|
| Mechanical Failure | Hypertrophic non-union (excess callus) |

<!-- page 2 -->

| Mechanism | Radiological Appearance |
|---|---|
| Biological Failure | Atrophic non-union (no callus) |

Mnemonic:
> “High callus = motion problem; No callus = biology problem.”

### Risk Factors

| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| Fracture Related | Open fracture (hematoma loss), intra-articular, precarious vascular supply (neck of femur, scaphoid, talus), comminution, bone loss |
| Host Related | Age, smoking, diabetes, vascular disease, vitamin D deficiency, malnutrition, immunosuppression |
| Treatment Related | Inadequate reduction, poor fixation, periosteal stripping, implant loosening, infection |

Smoking – impairs angiogenesis and osteoblast function; nicotine itself delays union.
Diabetes – decreases osteoblast activity and vascularity.

### Dror-Paley Classification

| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| A (<1 cm loss) | A1 – Mobile, A2 – Stiff (A21: without deformity, A22: with deformity) |
| B (>1 cm loss) | B1 – Bone defect, B2 – Shortening, B3 – Both defect & shortening |

Relevance:
Stiff = Hypertrophic (needs compression);
Mobile = Atrophic (needs graft).

Principles of Treatment
Identify what’s missing – Mechanics or Biology.

| Deficit | Primary Strategy |
|---|---|
| Mechanical Instability | Improve fixation – nail, plate, or external fixator |
| Biological Deficit | Stimulate with bone graft / substitute |

Dr. Tiwari’s Principle
“When mechanics are right, biology follows — but not the other way around.”

### Non-Operative Management

| Indications | Techniques |
|---|---|
| Well-aligned hypertrophic non-union | PTB cast, gradual weight-bearing |
| Patient unfit for surgery | Electrical stimulation, LIPUS (Low-Intensity Pulsed Ultrasound) |

LIPUS: 30 mW/cm², 1.5 MHz – enhances endochondral ossification, not cell
proliferation.
Adjuncts: Stop smoking, correct Vitamin D deficiency, improve nutrition.

### Operative Management
1. Nailing
- Preferred for diaphyseal non-unions (femur/tibia).
- Options:
  - Primary Nailing – First surgical fixation after conservative failure.
  - Exchange Nailing – Replace undersized/failed nail with larger diameter (+2 mm).

  - Dynamization – Remove interlocking screw to allow compression.

| Type | Indication | Key Tip |
|---|---|---|
| Exchange | Rotational instability, | Over-ream by 1–2 mm; |
| Nailing | undersized nail, bone defect | use different company nail; static locking |
| Dynamizatio Gap due to resorption / n | distraction | Done at 3–6 months; remove screw from longer fragment end |

<!-- page 5 -->

Avoid: Beyond 6 months; may cause penetration into joint.

2. Plating
- Preferred for metaphyseal non-unions.
- Provides absolute stability but is invasive and soft-tissue compromising.
- Limitation: Cannot correct shortening; risk of implant failure.
3. Augmentation Plating (Nail + Plate)
- Combines relative stability (nail) with absolute stability (plate).
- Prevents nail fatigue and offers superior construct for complex non-unions.

4. External Fixation
- Ilizarov Ring Fixator – Gold Standard for:
  - DDD: Defect, Deformity, Decrease in bone length.
  - Immediate weight-bearing & multiplanar correction.
Other Systems: LRS (Limb Reconstruction System) for bone transport.

5. Arthroplasty / Amputation / Excision

| Situation | Preferred Management |
|---|---|
| Elderly + periarticular non-union | Arthroplasty |
| Non-reconstructable fracture + | Amputation |

Bone Grafting
### Types of Grafts

| Type | Source | Key Feature |
|---|---|---|
| Autograft | Same individual | Gold standard; all 3 properties present |
| Allograft | Different human donor / bone bank | No donor site morbidity; infection risk |
| Xenograft | Animal source | Rarely used |

### Biological Properties

| Property | Definition |
|---|---|
| Osteoconduction | Scaffold for bone ingrowth |
| Osteoinduction | Growth factors induce cell differentiation |
| Osteogenesis | Viable osteoblasts form new bone |

Autograft = all three properties; Iliac crest is gold standard.

### Donor Sites
- Anterior Iliac Crest (Gold Standard) – all 3 biological properties.
- Posterior Iliac Crest, Femoral/Tibial Condyle, Olecranon, Distal Radius.
- RIA (Reamer–Irrigator–Aspirator): Harvests graft from femoral canal with minimal morbidity.
Risks: Fat embolism, femoral fracture, thermal necrosis.

### Special Situations
1. Articular Non-Union

| Scenario | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Arthritis / instability + old age | Arthroplasty |
| Arthritis + young | Arthrodesis / Excisional arthroplasty |
| No arthritis | Non-union fixation (plating, debridement) |

2. Defect (Gap) Non-Union

| Cause | Examples |
|---|---|
| Post-trauma | Bone loss, resorption |
| Post-surgery | Tumor resection, sequestrectomy |

Treatment: 1. Masquelet Technique (Induced Membrane) – Two-stage (spacer →
graft insertion).
2. Fibula Graft – Vascularized/non-vascularized fibular segment transfer.
3. Ilizarov Bone Transport – Mono-, bi-, or trifocal corticotomy to fill defect.
Spacer Role: Prevents fibrous ingrowth and delivers antibiotics locally.

### Infected Non-Union Management
Approach Stage Description

| Conventional | 1⃣ Radical |
|---|---|
| (Two-stage) | debridement 2⃣ Wound coverage 3⃣ Bone grafting |
| Active | Simultaneous |
| (Single/Modified) | debridement + stabilization ± bone graft |

Implant Choices: Antibiotic beads, nails, or cement spacers.
Exam Tip: For theory, always write Conventional > Active in management
hierarchy.

### Exam / Viva Pearls
- Non-union = lack of healing potential after expected duration.
- Hypertrophic → Mechanical problem; Atrophic → Biological problem.
- Weber–Cech classification: Based on vascularity (Strontium-85 uptake).
- Dror–Paley: A (<1 cm), B (>1 cm) with subtypes.
- Iliac crest autograft – Gold standard for grafting.
- Masquelet: 2-stage membrane induction technique.
- Ilizarov: Corrects deformity, defect, and length simultaneously.
- BMP-2 / BMP-7: Osteoinductive proteins.
- Ceramic beads: Osteoconductive & biodegradable.
Final Faculty Insight:
“Non-union isn’t failure; it’s biology asking for a second chance — through stability, graft,
and patience.”
