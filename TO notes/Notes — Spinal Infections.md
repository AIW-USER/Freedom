OVERVIEW
Definition:
Spinal infections include tuberculous (granulomatous), pyogenic (bacterial),
postoperative, and immunocompromised-related infections of the spine.
Dr. Vishnu’s Quote:
“TB is a great mimicker — it can look like anything from a disc prolapse to a
tumor. Always keep your suspicion high.”
Why Spinal Infections Matter: - Uncommon but extremely destructive if not treated
early.
- Can lead to spinal instability, cord compression, and permanent deficits.
- Wide spectrum of presentations → difficult to diagnose.

### SPINAL TUBERCULOSIS (POTT’S DISEASE)

| Feature | Details |
|---|---|
| Epidemiology | <1% of all TB cases; 50% of skeletal TB. |
| Common site | Thoracolumbar junction (stress transition zone). |
| Neuro deficit | 10–43% of cases. |
| Spread | Hematogenous (via venous plexus), contiguous from lungs/pleura, lymphatic. |

Pathogenesis:
1. Infection begins in anterior vertebral body near endplate.

2. Spreads to adjacent vertebrae via disc space.

3. Caseation and bone destruction → collapse → deformity (kyphosis).

4. Abscess formation → cord/root compression.

### PYOGENIC SPONDYLODISCITIS

| Feature | Description |
|---|---|
| Common organisms | Staphylococcus aureus, Gram-negative bacilli |
| Common sources | Genitourinary tract (29%), soft tissue infections (13%), upper respiratory tract (11%) |
| Spread | Hematogenous, iatrogenic, or contiguous |

Key Differentiating Features: - Rapid onset, severe pain, septic features.
- Disc involvement early, single level, thick-walled abscess.
- Neurological deficit due to epidural abscess or arterial thrombosis.

Deformities: - Knuckle: Single vertebra collapse.
- Gibbus: Two vertebrae involved.
- Kyphus: ≥3 vertebrae collapse.
80% develop kyphosis; 5% >60° deformity even after conservative care.
SPINE AT RISK SIGNS (Dr. Rajasekaran)
Used in children with TB spine to predict deformity progression: 1. Facetal subluxation
2. Retropulsion of vertebral body fragment
3. Lateral translation
4. Toppling of superior vertebra over inferior
→ Early fixation recommended if ≥2 signs present.

RADIOLOGY
### X-RAY Findings:
- Endplate erosion → vertebral body destruction → collapse.

- Loss of disc space.

- Paraspinal shadow = abscess (loss of psoas margin in lumbar TB).

- Cervical spine: widened prevertebral soft tissue.

<!-- page 4 -->

### MRI Features:

| Feature | Tuberculous | Pyogenic |
|---|---|---|
| Location | Thoracic / Thoracolumbar | Lumbar |
| Segments | Multiple with skip lesions | Single level |
| Abscess | Thin-walled, smooth | Thick, irregular wall |
| Pattern | Vertebral body involvement | Disc space first |
| Signal | T1 low, T2 high (edema) | T1 low, T2 high |

Always order STIR (fat-suppressed) images → better for edema visualization.

### INVESTIGATIONS

| Test | Utility |
|---|---|
| ESR (>100 mm/hr) | Significant; prognostic marker, decreases after 3 months of therapy |
| CRP | Monitors pyogenic infections (short-term response) |
| Blood/Urine Culture | Detects source of bacteremia (16–30% positive) |
| Tuberculin Test | Outdated; many false positives |
| ELISA (IgM/IgG) | Screening only; low specificity |
| Biopsy / Aspiration | Gold standard for diagnosis |
| PCR (TB-PCR) | 60–75% sensitive, 94–100% specific; detects drug resistance |

Diagnostic Yield: - Histopathology: 60–90%
- Culture (BACTEC): 84%
- L-J Media: 61%
Even after all tests, 30–40% remain unconfirmed — clinical correlation is
key.

MANAGEMENT
### Tuberculous Spondylitis (Medical Disease)

| Phase | Regimen |
|---|---|
| Intensive (2 mo) | HRZE (Isoniazid, Rifampicin, Pyrazinamide, Ethambutol) |
| Continuation (7 mo) | HRE (Isoniazid, Rifampicin, Ethambutol) |

Total Duration: 9 months minimum (2HRZE + 7HRE)
Supportive: Bed rest 2–3 weeks → brace → gradual mobilization.
Monitoring: ESR trend ↓ by 3rd month; X-ray improvement by 4th month.
DOTS Strategy:
Directly observed therapy improves compliance and reduces relapse.

### PYOGENIC INFECTION MANAGEMENT
- IV antibiotics: 6 weeks → Oral antibiotics: 6 weeks (based on culture).

- Monitor: CRP and radiology.

- Surgery: Debridement, drainage, decompression, stabilization.
Limited Laminectomy Technique:
Alternate-level laminectomy + irrigation catheter → less time, blood loss, and
postoperative deformity.

### DRUG-RESISTANT TB

| Type | Definition | Management |
|---|---|---|
| MDR-TB | Resistant to INH & Rifampicin | Culture + sensitivity → HRZES + Moxifloxacin + Cycloserine (adjust 5 drugs for 18 mo) |
| XDR-TB | Resistant to 1st-line (INH, Rifampicin) + fluoroquinolones + ≥1 injectable (Amikacin/Kanamycin/Capr eomycin) | Surgical debridement + salvage drug regimen |

### EXAM & VIVA PEARLS
- TB = 70% of spinal infections.

- Commonest site = thoracolumbar junction.

<!-- page 7 -->

- Pain + deformity + deficit = triad.

- ESR >100 mm/hr significant.

- MRI = gold standard.

- Thin-walled abscess = TB; thick irregular = pyogenic.

- Spine-at-risk signs → early fixation in children.

- PCR = most specific for TB (94–100%).

- Surgery: Pain, deficit, instability, deformity.

### TAKE-HOME SUMMARY
- Maintain high suspicion for TB in endemic regions.

- Confirm with biopsy or PCR whenever possible.

- Treat TB spine medically first; operate for pain, deficit, deformity, or instability.

- Use posterior-only approach where feasible.

- Monitor ESR/CRP trends to judge healing.

- MDR/XDR TB needs prolonged, guided multi-drug regimen.

- Goal: Eradicate infection, preserve stability, prevent deformity, and restore function.
