Target Ortho Quick Revision Notes – Pediatric
Cervical Spine Problems (2025)
Faculty: Dr. C.S. Vishnu Prasath

OVERVIEW
Definition:
Pediatric cervical spine problems include congenital, developmental, traumatic, and
infective disorders affecting the upper and lower cervical spine in children.
They are distinct from adult conditions due to anatomical, biomechanical, and
physiological differences.
Dr. Vishnu’s Quote:
“In children, the cervical spine is not just a smaller version of the adult spine —
it’s a moving target of growth and development.”
Key Features of Pediatric Cervical Spine: - Increased ligamentous laxity.
- Shallow and horizontally oriented facet joints.
- Incomplete ossification.
- Large head-to-body ratio → predisposes to instability.
Commonly Affected Regions:
- Craniovertebral junction (C1–C2)
- Subaxial cervical spine (C3–C7)

### ANATOMICAL & DEVELOPMENTAL DIFFERENCES

| Parameter | Pediatric Spine | Adult Spine |
|---|---|---|
| Facet orientation | More horizontal | Oblique |
| Vertebral body | Wedge-shaped | Rectangular |
| Odontoid | Cartilaginous until 6 years | Fully ossified |
| Ligaments | Lax and elastic | Firm |
| Head–body ratio | Large | Smaller |
| Center of gravity | Higher (C2–C3) | Lower (C5–C6) |

Result:
→ Predisposition to atlantoaxial instability, subluxation, and high cervical injuries.

RADIOLOGICAL ASSESSMENT
### 1⃣ Dynamic X-rays
- Lateral flexion–extension views under supervision.

- Detects instability and hypermobility.
### 2⃣ MRI
- Assesses cord compression, soft tissue injury, infection, and ligamentous laxity.
### 3⃣ CT with 3D Reconstruction
- Defines bony anatomy, congenital anomalies, fractures.

- Useful for surgical planning.

CONGENITAL ANOMALIES
1⃣ Atlantoaxial Instability (AAI)
Definition: Excessive movement between atlas (C1) and axis (C2) due to bony or
ligamentous abnormality.
Causes: - Congenital: Os odontoideum, Down syndrome.
- Acquired: Trauma, infection (Grisel’s syndrome).
Clinical Features: - Neck pain, torticollis.
- Myelopathic signs (weakness, clumsiness).
- Sudden paralysis after trivial trauma.
Radiology:
- ADI >5 mm on lateral view.
- Instability increases on flexion.
Treatment: - Mild/asymptomatic: Collar, avoid contact sports.
- Symptomatic/unstable: Posterior C1–C2 fusion (Gallie/Brooks or transarticular
screws).
Down syndrome = always screen for AAI before anesthesia or sports
participation.

2⃣ Os Odontoideum
Definition:
Failure of fusion between odontoid process and body of C2 → round ossicle separate
from dens.
Types: | Type | Description | |——|————–| | Orthotopic | Ossicle in normal odontoid
position | | Dystopic | Ossicle migrated toward foramen magnum |
Clinical Significance:
Potential for sudden cord compression after minor trauma.
Management: - Asymptomatic → Observe.
- Symptomatic / Instability → Posterior C1–C2 fixation.

3⃣ Basilar Invagination (BI)
Definition:
Odontoid projects abnormally above the Chamberlain’s line (>5 mm).
Causes: - Congenital: Klippel–Feil, Chiari malformation.
- Acquired: Rheumatoid arthritis, infection.

<!-- page 4 -->

Clinical: - Short neck, restricted neck movements.
- Neurological deficit (cord compression).
Radiology:
- Chamberlain’s line, McGregor’s line, McRae’s line used for diagnosis.
Treatment: - Posterior fossa decompression ± Occipitocervical fusion.

4⃣ Klippel–Feil Syndrome (KFS)
Definition:
Congenital fusion of two or more cervical vertebrae.
Triad (Classic): 1. Short neck
2. Low posterior hairline
3. Restricted neck movements
Associated Anomalies:
- Renal, cardiac, Sprengel’s deformity (elevated scapula).
- Occipitalization of atlas → basilar invagination.
Treatment: - Observation if asymptomatic.
- Fusion if instability or neurological symptoms.

INFECTIVE & TRAUMATIC LESIONS
1⃣ Grisel’s Syndrome
Definition: Non-traumatic atlantoaxial subluxation secondary to infection (pharyngitis,
tonsillitis, post-ENT surgery).
Pathophysiology:
Inflammatory hyperemia → ligamentous laxity → rotatory subluxation.
Clinical:
- Painful torticollis, restricted rotation.
- Head tilted to one side, chin rotated to opposite side.
Imaging:
- CT: Asymmetry of C1–C2 facet joints.
Treatment: - Acute: Bed rest + antibiotics + soft collar.
- Chronic (>3 weeks): Traction followed by posterior C1–C2 fusion.

<!-- page 5 -->

2⃣ Trauma in Children
Mechanisms: - High head–body ratio and ligamentous laxity → high cervical injury
common.
- Pseudosubluxation (C2–C3) often misdiagnosed as dislocation.
Common Injuries: - Odontoid synchondrosis fracture (<6 yrs).
- C1 ring fractures.
- SCIWORA – Spinal Cord Injury Without Radiographic Abnormality.
Management: - Stable: Collar, observation.
- Unstable: Traction or surgical fixation (wire/screw-based).

### NEUROMUSCULAR & DEVELOPMENTAL DISORDERS
- Cerebral Palsy / Muscular Dystrophy: Chronic muscle imbalance → cervical deformity.

- Achondroplasia: Foramen magnum stenosis → cord compression.

- Mucopolysaccharidoses: Odontoid hypoplasia → atlantoaxial instability.

MANAGEMENT PRINCIPLES
Non-operative:
- Observation, physiotherapy, and avoidance of extreme motion.

- Soft collar for comfort.

- Treat underlying infection (Grisel’s).
Operative:

| Indication | Procedure |
|---|---|
| Atlantoaxial instability | Posterior C1–C2 fusion (Gallie, Brooks, Goel-Harms) |
| Basilar invagination | Foramen magnum decompression ± OCF |
| Klippel–Feil with instability | Posterior fusion |
| Odontoid hypoplasia / Os | C1–C2 fusion |

### EXAM & VIVA PEARLS
- Pediatric spine = ligamentous laxity + incomplete ossification.

- ADI >5 mm → instability.

- Pseudosubluxation C2–C3 = physiological in <8 yrs.

- Grisel’s syndrome = infective AAI.

- Os odontoideum = risk of sudden cord compression.

- KFS triad = short neck + low hairline + restricted motion.

- Always screen Down syndrome before anesthesia.

- Use dynamic X-rays + MRI for safe assessment.

### TAKE-HOME SUMMARY
- Pediatric cervical spine differs anatomically and biomechanically from adults.

- Instability often asymptomatic → needs vigilance.

- Early imaging (X-ray, MRI, CT) crucial for diagnosis.

- Nonoperative for stable, fusion for unstable lesions.

- Avoid manipulation in suspected instability.

- Goal: Preserve stability, prevent neurological compromise, and allow normal growth.
