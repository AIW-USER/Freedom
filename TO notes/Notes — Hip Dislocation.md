Hip Dislocation – Dr. Daivik T Shetty
Overview
Hip dislocation is an orthopedic emergency that requires immediate reduction to prevent
avascular necrosis. It is a high velocity injury with 95 percent cases associated with
other injuries.
Faculty Remark: “Hip dislocation reduction is a race against time, every minute lost
increases the risk of AVN.” – Dr. Daivik

Anatomy Essentials
### Joint Structure
- Ball and socket joint with emphasis on stability.
- Femoral head forms two thirds of a sphere.
- Acetabulum has an inverted U shaped articular surface.
- Acetabular labrum deepens the socket and increases coverage by 10 percent.
### Capsular Attachments

| Region | Ligaments / Features |
|---|---|
| Anterior | Iliofemoral ligament (strongest in body), Pubofemoral ligament, Attached exactly at intertrochanteric line |
| Posterior | Ischiofemoral ligament, Capsule attaches half inch proximal to intertrochanteric ridge |

### Blood Supply
- Medial circumflex femoral artery (MCFA) is the major contributor.
- Ascending cervical branches supply the femoral head and are highly susceptible to injury.
- Artery of ligamentum teres, significant only in children.
- Posterior superior retinacular vessels are the most crucial.
Why Immediate Reduction?
Dislocation kinks MCFA and retinacular vessels causing cessation of blood supply
leading to AVN.

Mechanism of Injury and Types
### Simple vs Complex
- Simple dislocation: No associated fractures.
- Complex dislocation: Associated acetabular or proximal femur fractures.
### Pure Hip Dislocations

| Type | Mechanism | Key Features |
|---|---|---|
| Posterior | Dashboard injury, Axial | Flexion, adduction, internal |
| (most common) | load on flexed and adducted hip | rotation (FADIR) |
| Anterior | Abduction + external rotation | Flexion, abduction, external rotation (FABER) |

### Fracture Dislocations
- Can be anterior, posterior, or central.
- Increased flexion and internal rotation → more chance of pure dislocation.
- Decreased flexion or internal rotation → fracture dislocation.
### Influence of Femoral Anteversion
- Lower anteversion → posterior pure dislocation.
- Higher anteversion → posterior fracture dislocation.

Clinical Features
### Posterior Hip Dislocation
- Flexion, adduction, internal rotation.
- Femoral head palpable in gluteal region.
- Limb shortened.
- Vascular sign of Narath: feeble or absent femoral pulse.
- Sciatic nerve palsy common (10 to 20 percent), presents as foot drop.
### Why Foot Drop in Sciatic Nerve Injury?
1. Common peroneal nerve fibers are peripheral.
2. They are phylogenetically newer and weaker → more prone to injury.
### Anterior Hip Dislocation
- Flexion, abduction, external rotation.
- Femoral head palpable in groin or femoral triangle.
- Limb lengthening possible.
- Femoral vessel and nerve injury possible.

- Pubic type is dangerous due to compression of femoral artery → risk of gangrene.

### Classification Systems
1. Thompson–Epstein (Posterior Dislocation) – Primary System

| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| 1 | Pure dislocation |
| 2 | Single large posterior wall fracture |
| 3 | Comminuted posterior wall fracture |
| 4 | Acetabular floor fracture |
| 5 | Femoral head fracture |

### Pipkin Classification (Type 5 Subdivision)

| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| 1 | Fracture below fovea |
| 2 | Fracture above fovea |
| 3 | Type 1 or 2 + neck femur fracture |
| 4 | Type 1 or 2 + acetabulum fracture |

2. Epstein Classification (Anterior Dislocation)

| Type | Region | Subtype |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Superior (pubic/iliac) | A – no fracture, B – head/neck fracture, C – acetabulum fracture |
| 2 | Inferior (obturator) | Same subtypes A, B, C |

3. AO/OTA
- 30D10: Anterior
- 30D11: Posterior
- 30D30: Obturator (anterior inferior)

Imaging
### Pre Reduction
- Single AP view usually sufficient.
- Look for: congruence, head size, Shenton line break, trochanter prominence.

<!-- page 4 -->

Post Reduction Protocol
Mandatory 5 views: - AP - Inlet - Outlet - Two Judet views

### CT Scan (Essential Post Reduction)
- Confirms congruent reduction.
- Detects intra articular fragments.
- Evaluates femoral head fractures.
- Rules out acetabular involvement.
### MRI
- Not routinely needed in acute setting.
- Useful for predicting early AVN by muscle morphology (obturator externus and piriformis).

Reduction Techniques
### Posterior Dislocation

| Technique | Notes |
|---|---|
| Allis | Supine, traction at 90 degrees hip flexion |
| Bigelow | Traction, exaggerate deformity (higher fracture risk) |
| Stimson | Prone, gravity assisted |
| East Baltimore Lift | Needs two assistants |
| Rochester | Single assistant technique |
| PGI Technique | Single surgeon, most atraumatic. Flexion → abduction → external rotation |

### Anterior Dislocation
- Walker modification of Allis is preferred.
### Open Reduction – Indications
- Failure of two closed reduction attempts.
- Intra-articular fragments blocking reduction.
- Femoral head fractures.
- Iatrogenic sciatic nerve palsy.
- Unstable hip after reduction.

<!-- page 5 -->

Approach: - Anterior dislocation → Smith Peterson. - Posterior dislocation → Safe
surgical dislocation (Ganz) or Kocher-Langenbeck if wall fracture.

### Post Reduction Assessment
- Check limb length.
- Check ROM.
- Stability test at 90 degrees hip flexion.
- Early ROM from Day 1.
- Weight bearing as tolerated.
- No role for traction after reduction.
### Athletic Recovery Protocol
- Stretching and ROM day 1 onward.
- Strengthening within first week.
- Jogging by 6 to 8 weeks.
- Return to sports by 3 to 4 months.

Complications

| Complication | Notes |
|---|---|
| AVN | Increases exponentially if reduction after 6 hours. Seen by MRI at 6 to 8 weeks. |
| Sciatic Nerve Palsy | 10 to 15 percent adults. Presents with foot drop. Usually neuropraxia. |
| Osteoarthritis | 16 percent in isolated dislocations, 88 percent with acetabular fractures. |
| Heterotopic Ossification | More in anterior dislocations. Incidence 2.8 to 9 percent. |
| Instability | Due to inadequate reduction or ligamentous injury. |

### Windswept Hip Dislocation
- One hip anterior, the other posterior.
- Atraumatic cause: Cerebral palsy.
- Adult cause: High velocity trauma.

### Viva and Exam Pearls
- Posterior dislocation → posterior wall acetabulum fracture.
- Anterior dislocation → femoral head impaction fracture.
- Earliest AVN predictor: Obturator externus/piriformis injury on MRI.
- Post reduction CT is mandatory.
- PGI technique is most atraumatic, single surgeon.
- Walker modification of Allis for anterior dislocation.
- Reduction ideally within 6 hours.

Final Faculty Insight:
“Restore blood supply, restore stability, and restore motion. Miss any one, and the hip
will not forgive.”
