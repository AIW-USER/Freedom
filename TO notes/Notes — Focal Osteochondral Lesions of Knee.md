Target Ortho Quick Revision Notes – Focal Osteochondral Lesions of Knee
Faculty: Dr. Mukul Mohindra

### OVERVIEW
- Focal osteochondral lesions (FCLs) refer to localized damage to articular cartilage, typically <10 cm², often <5 cm².
- Goal: manage early before it progresses to diffuse osteoarthritis.
- These lesions result from trauma, overload, malalignment, or genetic predisposition. Quote: “If you treat cartilage while it’s still focal, you can stop arthritis from happening.” – Dr. Mukul Mohindra

### PREDISPOSING FACTORS
- Obesity and mechanical overload.
- Age-related degeneration.
- Malalignment: Genu varum/valgum, recurvatum.
- Sports / overuse injuries.
- Genetic susceptibility. Focal lesions act as precursors to osteoarthritis — due to matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs) degrading collagen and triggering inflammatory cytokines (IL‑1, IL‑6, TNF‑α).

### PATHOGENESIS SUMMARY
1. Cartilage insult → activates MMPs → collagen degradation.
2. Exposed collagen acts as antigen → synovial inflammation.
3. Cytokines released: IL‑1, IL‑6, TNF‑α → NF‑κB & Ras pathway activation.
4. Cycle of inflammation leads to further cartilage breakdown → early OA.

### PROCEDURES IN DETAIL
1. Debridement & Lavage (Chondroplasty)
- Arthroscopic smoothing of fibrillated cartilage & joint lavage.
- Removes inflammatory mediators → temporary pain relief.
- Indication: Grade I–II lesions, <50% depth.
- Goal: Stop inflammatory cycle by eliminating antigenic collagen fragments. Palliative, not restorative.

2. Microfracture (Marrow Stimulation Technique)
- Instrument: AWL – creates 1 mm holes, 3 mm deep, spaced 3 mm apart.
- Mechanism: Penetrates subchondral bone → releases marrow → mesenchymal clot forms fibrocartilage.
- Advantages: Simple, cost-effective, any size lesion.
- Limitations: Forms fibrocartilage (Type I) instead of hyaline cartilage → lower load tolerance. Ideal for elderly or low-demand patients.

3. OATS – Osteochondral Autologous Transfer System
(Also called Mosaicplasty if multiple small plugs are used)
- Principle: Transfer osteochondral plugs (dowels) from non–weight-bearing to weight-bearing zone.
- Donor Site: Medial or lateral trochlear edge (not central trochlea).
- Recipient Site: Weight-bearing condyle (defect area).
### Technical Details:
- Dowel diameter: 6, 8, or 10 mm (Arthrex set).
- Depth: 15–25 mm (donor longer by 1–2 mm to keep graft “proud”).
- Up to 3 dowels (≈2.5 cm defect coverage).
- Rehabilitation: Non–weight-bearing 6 weeks.

### CAIS (Cartilage Autograft Implantation System):
- Scaffold: Polycaprolactone + PGA + PDS.
- Minimal cartilage required; in vivo stimulation aids ECM production. Coverage: Up to 10 cm² defect.

5. BMAC – Bone Marrow Aspirate Concentrate (Stem Cell Therapy)
- Procedure: Iliac crest marrow aspirated → centrifuged → buffy coat (growth factors + stem cells) harvested.
- Injected as paste over defect.
- Goal: Regeneration of hyaline-like cartilage.
- Advantages: Single-stage, cost-effective (~₹70–80k), minimal morbidity.
- Limitation: Long-term outcomes still under study.

### EXAM PEARLS
- Cartilage collagen type: II; Meniscus: I.
- Proteoglycans (aggrecan): main functional molecule.
- Cytokines in OA: IL‑1, IL‑6, TNF‑α.
- Outerbridge classification most practical.
- Microfracture: fibrocartilage formation.
- OATS vs Mosaicplasty: same principle, dowel size differs.
- ACI (3rd gen / CAIS): single-stage, minced cartilage → scaffold.

- BMAC: stem-cell–based regeneration.
- Diacerein: IL‑1 blocker, 50 mg OD/BD.
