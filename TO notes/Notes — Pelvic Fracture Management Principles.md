Pelvic Fracture Management Principles – Dr. Sanjay
Arora
Overview
Pelvic fractures represent high energy injuries with significant mortality risk.
Management focuses on resuscitation, hemorrhage control, assessment of ring
instability, and timely mechanical stabilization. Understanding pelvic biomechanics
and ligamentous support guides operative decision making.
Faculty Remark: “Posterior ring decides stability. If the posterior ring is broken, the
pelvis is unstable until proven otherwise.”

## Understanding Pelvic Instability
### Key Concepts
- Pelvic ring functions as a closed ring, so a break in one point often signals a second injury.
- Posterior ring integrity (SI joint, sacrum) is the main determinant of stability.
- Anterior ring injury indicates high suspicion for associated posterior disruption.
### Physiologic Load Transfer
- Standing: torso weight → sacrum → SI joints → ilium → acetabulum → femur.
- Sitting: load transfers through posterior ring and ischium.
### Indicators of Instability
- Visible deformity on AP pelvis.
- SI joint widening or asymmetry.
- Pubic symphysis diastasis >2.5 cm.
- Clues to soft tissue injury:
  - L5 transverse process fracture → iliolumbar ligament tear.
  - Ischial spine avulsion → sacrospinous ligament injury.
  - Lateral sacral avulsion → sacrotuberous injury.

## Classification Reference
Although covered in the prior lecture, classification guides treatment: -
Young–Burgess: APC, LC, VS, Combined. - Tile: - A: Stable. - B: Rotationally
unstable, vertically stable. - C: Completely unstable.

## Goals of Pelvic Fracture Treatment
1. Define pelvic ring stability.
2. Decide between operative vs non operative care.
3. Control hemorrhage.
4. Restore stability to allow early mobilization.
5. Prevent long term functional impairment.

## Resuscitation and Hemorrhage Control
Life Saving Priorities
Hemorrhage in pelvic fractures is 80 to 90 percent venous, from retroperitoneal
plexus. Arterial bleeds are less common but critical.

### Essential Steps (ABCD)
- Airway, Breathing, Circulation.
- Early fluid and blood replacement.
- Assess for associated abdominal, urethral, bladder injuries.

### Pelvic Binder Application
- First line non invasive stabilization.
- Reduces pelvic volume and decreases bleeding.
- Binder must be applied over greater trochanters, not the waist.
Advantages
- Quick, cost effective.
- Temporary bridge to external or internal fixation.
### Binder Removal
- Remove within 16 to 24 hours once physiology stabilizes.
- Replace with external fixator if still unstable.

### REBOA and Resuscitative Thoracotomy
- Considered only for extremis polytrauma patients with uncontrollable hemorrhage.
- REBOA provides temporary proximal aortic control.
- Used to gain time for packing or fixation.

<!-- page 3 -->

### Pre Peritoneal Pelvic Packing (PPP)
- Indicated when hemorrhage persists despite hemodynamic measures.
- Always combined with pelvic stabilization.
- Packs placed in retropubic space provide tamponade.

Angioembolization
- Effective for arterial bleeding.
- CT angiography showing contrast extravasation predicts need.
- Elderly patients more likely to need angioembolization.

## External Pelvic Fixation
Indications
- Hemodynamically unstable pelvic fractures.
- Adjunct before packing.
- Temporary stabilization to reduce pelvic volume.
Techniques
Anterior Frames: - Iliac crest pins. - Supra acetabular pins (better bone stock).
Posterior C Clamp: - For vertical shear injuries. - Closes SI joint diastasis rapidly.
Rationale: 1. Reduces intrapelvic volume. 2. Provides counter pressure for packing.

## Non Operative Management
Indications
- LC I injuries with <1.5 cm displacement.
- Pubic rami fractures without posterior involvement.
- Minimal symphyseal gapping (<2.5 cm) without SI joint motion.
### Follow Up Requirements
- Serial radiographs.
- Stress radiographs if instability suspected.
- Post mobilization imaging.
### Reasons for Failure
- Displacement after mobilization.

<!-- page 4 -->

- Excessive pain preventing early ambulation.

## Operative Management Principles
### Key Goals
- Restore anatomy of posterior ring.
- Provide sufficient stability to maintain reduction during healing.
- More fixation points generally improve stability.
### Role of Anterior Fixation
- Supports posterior fixation mechanically.
- Helps maintain reduction of SI joint and sacral fractures.

## Preoperative Planning
- Ensure adequate resuscitation.
- Check skin condition, open injuries, degloving.
- Consider timing:
  - Best window: 24 to 48 hours.
  - If not feasible, delay for at least 7 days.

### Imaging Requirements
- Radiolucent table, proper C arm techniques.
- Necessary views: inlet, outlet, obturator oblique, iliac oblique.

## Surgical Approaches
Anterior Pelvic Ring
### Pfannenstiel (Low Transverse)
- Exposure of pubic symphysis.
- Protect rectus abdominis attachments.
### Modified Stoppa
- Access from pubic symphysis → SI joint.
- Provides excellent exposure of quadrilateral plate.
- Useful for anterior column and ramus fixation.

<!-- page 5 -->

Posterior Pelvic Ring
Approaches
- Anterior approach (iliac window) for SI joint.
- Posterior approach for sacral fractures and crescent fractures.
- Midline or paraspinal incisions.

## Reduction Techniques
### Symphysis Pubis
- Weber clamp on pectineal eminence.
- Jungbluth clamp using screws.

### Ramus Fractures
- 6 hole reconstruction plate preferred.

- Plating.

### SI Joint Dislocation
- Antegrade or retrograde medullary screw fixation.

- Anterior exposure facilitates reduction.

### Sacral Fractures
- Combine plate and screws if required.

### Indirect Reduction
- Reduction of anterior ring can realign sacrum.
- Distractors or traction may aid.
### Direct Reduction
- Requires posterior approach.
- Weber clamps on sacral fragments.

## Fixation Strategies
### Iliosacral Screws
- Insert in S1 and S2 segments only.
- Screws must stay in anterior one third of sacrum.
- Fully threaded for stability.
- Cannulated for accurate placement.

<!-- page 6 -->

### Crescent Fracture Fixation
- ORIF with plate and SI screws.

### Lumbopelvic Fixation
- Sometimes combined approaches.

- For unstable U, H, and Y type sacral fractures.
- Provides strong vertical stability.

### Trans Iliac Plate
- Useful for severe sacral comminution.

- Supplements SI screws.
- Useful in comminuted sacral fractures.

## Biomechanics of Pelvic Fixation
### Key Principles
- More fixation points provide better stability.
- Anterior plating superior to external fixation for rotation.
- Neither technique fully controls vertical shear.
- Posterior fixation is critical for stability.
### Fixation Options
- Multiple SI screws.
- Double plating of SI joint.
- Trans iliac plate.
- Plate plus SI screw.
- Lumbopelvic fixation for highest stability.

### Exam Pearls
- Posterior ring determines pelvic stability.
- SI joint widening or symphysis >2.5 cm suggests instability.
- LC I usually stable; APC II and III unstable.
- Pelvic binder must be positioned over trochanters.
- REBOA is temporary and indicated only in extremis.
- Pre peritoneal packing only works with pelvic stabilization.
- Iliosacral screws placed only in S1 and S2.
- Crescent fractures require combined fixation.
- Anterior fixation supports posterior fixation.
