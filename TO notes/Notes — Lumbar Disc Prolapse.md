Target Ortho Quick Revision Notes – Lumbar Disc Prolapse
Faculty: Dr. C S Vishnu Prasath

### OVERVIEW
- Lumbar disc prolapse (LDP) is one of the most common causes of low back pain with sciatica.
- Common levels: L4–L5 and L5–S1 (due to maximum mobility and stress).
- Results from degeneration of the intervertebral disc → nucleus pulposus herniates through annulus fibrosus. “Most lumbar disc prolapses will settle conservatively within 6 weeks — patience and proper rehab are key.” – Dr. C S Vishnu Prasath

### PATHOPHYSIOLOGY
- Disc degeneration → dehydration of nucleus pulposus → annular tears → herniation.
- Pfirrmann’s Grading (MRI-based):
  - Grade I–II: Normal to mild degeneration.
  - Grade III: Moderate degeneration.
  - Grade IV–V: Advanced degeneration with loss of height and signal.
- Pain generation:
  - Direct mechanical compression of nerve root.
  - Inflammation: nucleus pulposus releases TNF-α → nerve root irritation.

### HISTORY & SYMPTOMS
- Low back pain (LBP) with radiating leg pain (sciatica).
- Often history of long-standing mechanical LBP aggravated by trauma or heavy lifting.
- Pain pattern:
  - ↑ with bending, lifting, coughing.
  - ↓ on lying supine with hips and knees flexed.
- Leg pain > Back pain – hallmark of disc prolapse.
- Lumbar canal stenosis: pain mainly during walking.

### Radicular Features
- Numbness, paresthesia, weakness.
- Severe cases → bladder/bowel dysfunction (cauda equina syndrome).

### CLINICAL EXAMINATION
- Inspection:
  - Sciatic scoliosis / list.
  - Paraspinal muscle spasm.
- Palpation: Tenderness over paraspinal muscles.
- SLR (Straight Leg Raise) Test:
  - Positive = pain radiating below knee.
  - Indicates nerve root irritation (usually L4–S1).
- Bowstring test, Sitting SLR, Femoral nerve stretch test (for upper lumbar discs).
- Motor testing:
  - L4 → Knee extension (quadriceps).
  - L5 → Ankle dorsiflexion (EHL) – foot drop.
  - S1 → Ankle plantarflexion (gastrocnemius) – toe walking.
Heel walking → L5, Toe walking → S1.

IMAGING
### X-Ray (Lumbosacral Spine)
- First-line: Standing lateral view.
- Does not show disc herniation, but reveals:
  - Loss of lumbar lordosis.

- Instability on dynamic views.

MRI – Gold Standard
Indications:
- Persistent symptoms >6 weeks.
- Neurological deficit.
- Red flags (fever, malignancy, infection).
- Bladder/bowel involvement.

### CONSERVATIVE MANAGEMENT
- Natural History: ~60% recover within 6 weeks.
- Rest: 2–7 days of bed rest, then gradual activity.
- Analgesics: NSAIDs (Aceclofenac), pregabalin for radicular pain.
- Physiotherapy:
  - Pelvic traction.
  - IFT (Interferential therapy).
  - Ultrasound massage.
  - Gentle back-strengthening exercises.
- Oral steroids: Controversial; limited benefit.
- Nerve root blocks: Diagnostic + therapeutic in selective cases. “If symptoms persist beyond 6–8 weeks despite proper therapy, re-evaluate for surgery.” – Dr. Vishnu

### Surgical Steps (MLD):
1. Position – prone, flexed hips & knees.
2. Midline incision (~2.5 cm).
3. Hemostasis → fascia incision near midline.
4. Paraspinal muscle elevation → expose lamina.
5. Flavectomy and gentle retraction of root.
6. Remove extruded disc fragment → check free root.

CAUDA EQUINA SYNDROME (CES)
Definition:
- Compression of cauda equina → bladder, bowel, and sexual dysfunction. Symptoms:
  - Bilateral sciatica.
  - Saddle anesthesia.
  - Urinary retention or incontinence. Management:
  - Emergency decompression within 24–48 hours.
  - Avoid delay – recovery of bladder function time-sensitive.

<!-- page 5 -->

### COMPLICATIONS
- Persistent back pain.
- Recurrent disc prolapse.
- Surgical site infection.
- Iatrogenic instability.

### EXAM & VIVA PEARLS
- Leg pain > back pain = disc prolapse.
- Pfirrmann grading – MRI-based disc degeneration.
- Heel vs toe walking: L5 vs S1 weakness.
- Root correlation: L4 = L3–L4, L5 = L4–L5, S1 = L5–S1.
- Natural recovery: 60% within 6 weeks.
- Gold standard: Micro Lumbar Discectomy.
- CES: Operate emergently.
- Never perform MLD in instability or multi-level disease.
