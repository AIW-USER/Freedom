Target Ortho Quick Revision Notes – Biceps Tendon Lesions
Faculty: Dr. Mukul Mohindra

### OVERVIEW
- The long head of biceps (LHB) is called the “policeman of the shoulder” in arthroscopy — it defines orientation and pathology.
- But it’s also the “thief” — as its lesions are often subtle, chronic, and diagnostically challenging.
- LHB pathologies are frequent causes of persistent anterior shoulder pain, often associated with rotator cuff tears or SLAP lesions. Exam Pearl: Short head → Functional strength. Long head → Source of pathology.

RELEVANT ANATOMY
### Structure & Zones
- Origin: Supraglenoid tubercle (intra-articular portion ~3.5 cm).
- Course: Passes through bicipital groove → fibrosseous tunnel → musculotendinous junction (total length ~9 cm).
- Zones:
  1. Zone 1: Traditional bicipital groove.
  2. Zone 2: Fibrosseous tunnel (subscapularis fibers form part of tunnel).
  3. Zone 3: Below the groove (musculotendinous junction).

### Medial Pulley System (Stabilizer of LHB)
- Formed by Subscapularis, SGHL (Superior Glenohumeral Ligament), and CHL (Coracohumeral Ligament).
- Prevents medial subluxation of the tendon.
- Failure → Biceps instability / subluxation. Old concept of Transverse Humeral Ligament as stabilizer is obsolete.
### Comma Sign
- Found in subscapularis tears.
- Represents thickened CHL + SGHL + Subscapularis pulled together → comma-shaped structure.
- Indicates associated biceps pulley lesion.
### Functional Relevance
- Elbow: Flexion and Supination (twice as strong a supinator as the supinator muscle when elbow extended).

<!-- page 2 -->

- Shoulder: Assists in abduction in externally rotated arm.
  - Depressor of humeral head (balances superior migration in rotator cuff tear).

### TYPES OF BICEPS LESIONS
  1. Biceps Tendonitis
  2. Biceps Instability (Pulley lesions)
  3. Biceps Rupture (Proximal / Distal)
  4. Type II SLAP Lesions (covered separately) ~95% of pathology involves the long head of biceps.

### Clinical Clues
  - Pain in anterior shoulder.
  - Clicking or snapping sensation.
  - MRI: Medial subluxation or dislocation of LHB.
  - Often missed unless rotator cuff tears or subscapularis tears are evaluated.
Management:
  - Conservative initially → physiotherapy, NSAIDs, local steroid injection.
  - Persistent symptoms → Tenodesis or Tenotomy.

BICEPS TENDONITIS
### Two Main Groups:
  1. Young active individuals (throwers, swimmers, gymnasts)
  - Overuse → inflammation of intra-articular LHB.
  - Usually isolated pathology or part of internal impingement.

<!-- page 3 -->

- Management: Rest, NSAIDs, local steroid injection; Tenodesis if >3 months pain.
2. Elderly / Rotator Cuff Disease patients
  - Caused by subacromial impingement or osteophytes.
  - Often with supraspinatus tear.
  - Treatment: Address impingement + RC repair + Biceps Tenotomy (or Tenodesis if active patient).
Key Insight: Always assess biceps in RC disease — treating only the cuff may
leave pain unresolved.

### Clinical Signs
- Popeye Sign: Bulge in lower arm (proximal rupture).
- Reverse Popeye: Bulge near shoulder (distal rupture).
- Pain and weakness in supination > flexion.

### IMAGING FINDINGS
- Normal: Coffee bean appearance of LHB in groove on axial MRI.
- Tendonitis: Irregular / thickened tendon with peritendinous fluid.
- Rupture: Absent tendon in groove; retracted distally.
- Degenerative: Frayed, intermediate signal; may subluxate medially. MRI confirms tendonitis, instability, or rupture; ultrasound can be dynamic adjunct.

### Clinical Tip:
- Zone 2 (tunnel) harbors hidden inflammatory lesions.
- Subpectoral approach = lowest recurrence, easiest revision.

### KEY CLINICAL CORRELATIONS
- Always evaluate biceps with rotator cuff & subscapularis tears.
- Tenderness in bicipital groove = LHB pathology.
- Comma sign = subscapularis tear + pulley lesion.
- LHB = Depressor of humeral head → avoid tenotomy alone if superior migration.
- Tenotomy for elderly; Tenodesis for active patients.
- Zones 1–3 guide level of tenodesis depending on pathology extent.

### EXAM & VIVA PEARLS
- Short head = Function; Long head = Pathology.
- Stabilizer: SGHL + CHL + Subscapularis (Medial Pulley System).
- Comma sign: CHL + SGHL + Subscapularis.
- Most common rupture site: Top of intertubercular sulcus.
- Speed’s test: Pain over bicipital groove.
- Tenotomy: Elderly, sedentary.
- Tenodesis: Young, active.
- Preferred site: Subpectoral.
- Zone 2: Hidden pathology → decompress sheath.
- Always evaluate in RC tears and SLAP.
