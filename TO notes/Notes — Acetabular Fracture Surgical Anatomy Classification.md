Acetabular Fracture – Surgical Anatomy and
Classification – Dr. Sanjay Arora
Overview
This lecture revisits the column concept, key osseous and ligamentous anatomy,
radiological landmarks, CT interpretation, and the full Letournel–Judet classification.
Understanding three dimensional acetabular structure is essential before planning any
fixation.
Faculty Remark: “If the anatomy is clear, the classification becomes simple, and the
fixation becomes predictable.”

## Surgical Anatomy of the Acetabulum
### Osseous Anatomy
- Formed by ilium, ischium, pubis meeting at the triradiate cartilage.
- Two strong structural pillars:
  - Anterior column: pubis → superior pubic ramus → anterior acetabulum → anterior ilium.
  - Posterior column: ischium → posterior acetabulum → region below greater sciatic notch.
- Columns meet at the ischial buttress, transmitting body weight from acetabulum to sacroiliac region.
### Internal Features
- Quadrilateral plate: thin medial wall, often involved in both column or anterior column with posterior hemitransverse fractures.
- Iliopectineal eminence: important intraoperative landmark, marking anterior column.

Ligamentous Anatomy
### Key Ligaments
- Sacrotuberous ligament: resists vertical shear.
- Sacrospinous ligament: resists external rotation, divides greater and lesser sciatic foramen.
- Posterior SI ligaments: strongest in the body, primary stabilizers.
- Anterior SI ligaments: resist external rotation forces.
- Symphyseal ligaments: stabilize anterior ring.

### Greater Sciatic Foramen Contents
- Piriformis.
- Superior gluteal nerve and vessels (above piriformis).
- Sciatic nerve (below piriformis).
- Internal pudendal vessels and nerve.
### Lesser Sciatic Foramen Contents
- Obturator internus tendon.
- Internal pudendal vessels.
Exam Pearl: Superior gluteal nerve injury risk is high with superior posterior
acetabular approaches.

## Radiological Anatomy
### Important Radiological Landmarks (AP Pelvis)
1. Iliopectineal line – anterior column.
2. Ilioischial line (Köhler’s line) – posterior column.
3. Teardrop – quadrilateral plate and obturator canal.
4. Sourcil (weight bearing dome).
5. Anterior wall – concave shadow.
6. Posterior wall – straight shadow.

### Judet Views

| View | Visualizes |
|---|---|
| Obturator oblique | Anterior column + posterior wall |
| Iliac oblique | Posterior column + anterior wall |

CT Based Understanding
### Axial CT Rules
- Column fractures: horizontal on axial.
- Transverse fractures: vertical on axial.
- Wall fractures: oblique on axial.
Exam Pearl: Transverse fractures appear vertical on axial CT.
### 3D CT Reconstruction
- Defines fracture line orientation and displacement.
- Complements, but does not replace, axial CT interpretation.

## Key Radiological Signs
### Double Beak Sign
- Seen in anterior wall fractures.
- Two parallel shadows created by intact posterior column and elevated anterior wall.
### Spur Sign
- Pathognomonic of both column fractures.
- Free floating acetabular fragment shows bony spur on obturator oblique view.
### Seagull Sign
- Represents superomedial dome impaction.
- Seen on oblique iliac views.
- Highly suggestive of posterior wall involvement.

## Letournel–Judet Classification

Elementary Fractures
Accepted worldwide; divides fractures into 5 elementary and 5 associated patterns.

1. Posterior Wall (most common)
- Simple or comminuted.
- Marginal impaction common.
2. Posterior Column
- Entire posterior column detaches.
3. Anterior Wall
- Rare; involves anterior acetabular rim and adjacent quadrilateral plate.
4. Anterior Column
- Fracture extends from iliac crest → pelvic brim → quadrilateral plate.
- Variants: high, intermediate, low.
5. Transverse Fracture
- Cuts both columns.
- Seen vertically on axial CT.

### Associated Fracture Patterns
1. Posterior Column + Posterior Wall
- Common variant with posterior dislocation.
2. Transverse + Posterior Wall
- Common and unstable.
- Requires sequential correction: transverse first, wall second.
3. T Type
- Transverse with vertical limb into obturator foramen.
- Looks like both column but maintains iliac attachment.
4. Anterior Column + Posterior Hemitransverse
- Most common associated injury.
- Posterior column only partially separated.
5. Both Column Fracture
- Complete dissociation from axial skeleton.
- Secondary congruence may exist.
- Spur sign pathognomonic.

## Additional Practical Insights
### Understanding Mechanism
- Limb position and forces dictate fracture pattern.
- Dashboard type injuries produce posterior wall and posterior column fractures.
- Axial load on abducted hip may produce anterior column fractures.
### CT Clues for Difficult Patterns
- Marginal impaction → needs elevation and graft.
- Quadrilateral plate displacement → suggests medial migration.
- Femoral head alignment with fragments guides classification.

### Exam Pearls
- Anterior wall = concave shadow; posterior wall = straight shadow.
- Spur sign → both column fracture.
- Seagull sign → dome impaction (posterior wall).
- Transverse fractures appear vertical on axial CT.
- Obturator oblique: anterior column + posterior wall.

- Iliac oblique: posterior column + anterior wall.
- Quadrilateral plate involvement common in both column fractures.
