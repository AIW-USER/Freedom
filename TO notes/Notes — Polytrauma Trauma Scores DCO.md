Polytrauma, Trauma Scores & Damage Control Orthopedics –
Dr. Daivik T Shetty

### Key Concepts / Overview
- Polytrauma remains one of the leading causes of death globally, especially in the younger age group.

- Trimodal distribution of death: Immediate (<1 hr), Early (1–4 hrs), and Late (>24 hrs).
  - ATLS has reduced immediate and early deaths, but late deaths remain high due to multi-organ failure and sepsis in polytrauma patients.

- Focus has shifted from Early Total Care (ETC) to Damage Control Orthopedics (DCO) to reduce mortality from the systemic inflammatory response.

Definition and Classification
### Polytrauma – Evolving Definitions
- Old definitions (controversial):
  - Two or more severe injuries involving head, chest, or abdomen with one extremity injury.
  - Two or more injuries, one being potentially life-threatening.
  - Injury Severity Score (ISS) >15.
- Current Definition – Berlin Definition (2014): Two injuries with AIS ≥3, plus one or more of the following:
  1. Hypotension (SBP ≤ 90 mmHg)
  2. Unconsciousness (GCS ≤ 8)
  3. Acidosis (Base deficit ≤ −6.0)
  4. Coagulopathy (PTT ≥ 40 sec or INR ≥ 1.4)
  5. Age ≥ 70 years

Trauma Scoring Systems
### Why Scoring Systems Are Needed
- Predict outcome and guide triage.
- Aid clinical decision-making and resource allocation.
- Enable comparative audit and quality improvement.
- Facilitate research and end-of-life decision-making.

### Abbreviated Injury Score (AIS)
- Developed by AAAM (1971).

- Classifies injury by body region and severity on a 6-point ordinal scale:

| Score | Description |
|---|---|
| 1 | Minor |
| 2 | Moderate |
| 3 | Serious |
| 4 | Severe |
| 5 | Critical |
| 6 | Unsurvivable |

Body Regions (7): External skin, Head/Face, Neck, Thorax, Abdomen/Pelvis, Spine,
Extremities.
Limitations: * Does not reflect cumulative effect of injuries. * Non-linear correlation
between score and mortality. * Summation of AIS inadequate for multi-region trauma.

### Injury Severity Score (ISS)
- Introduced by Baker et al., 1974.

- Based on six body regions (Spine excluded as a separate category).

<!-- page 3 -->

- Formula: ISS = (A² + B² + C²) where A, B, and C are the three highest AIS scores in different body regions.

| ISS Range | Severity |
|---|---|
| 1–8 | Minor |
| 9–15 | Moderate |
| 16–24 | Severe |
| ≥25 | Very severe |

Limitations: * Non-linear relationship with mortality. * Ignores multiple injuries in same
region. * Not designed to predict disability. * Requires training and is time-consuming.

### New Injury Severity Score (NISS)
- Proposed by Osler et al., 1997.
- Sum of squares of three most severe injuries, regardless of body region.
- Always equal or higher than ISS.
- Better correlation with mortality and prognosis.

### Anatomical Profile (AP) & Modified AP
- Uses 4 regions – Brain, Spinal Cord, Thorax, Neck.
- Accounts for serious vs. non-serious injuries.
- Modified AP includes only injuries with AIS ≥3.

Limb Salvage and Open Injury Scoring
### Mangled Extremity Severity Score (MESS)
- Predicts amputation vs. salvage.
- Parameters: Skeletal/soft tissue injury, limb ischemia, shock, age.
- Limitations – Assumes vascular injury; not useful for non-ischemic limbs.
### Other Scores
- Limb Salvage Index (LSI)
- Predictive Salvage Index (PSI)
- Hannover Fracture Scale (HFS-97)
- OTA-OFC score

Ganga Hospital Open Injury Score (GHOIS)
Developed by Dr. S. Rajasekaran to address limitations of ischemia-based scores.
Parameters (1–5 each): 1. Skin & Fascia
2. Bone & Joints
3. Muscles & Nerves
4. Comorbid Factors: Add 2 points for each – * Delay >12 hrs * Contamination
(sewage/farmyard) * Age >65 yrs * Anesthetic risk * ISS >25 or fat embolism *
Hypotension (SBP <90) * Associated major limb injury / compartment syndrome
Interpretation: | Total Score | Management Recommendation |
|————–|—————————–| | ≤14 | Attempt Salvage | | 15–16 | Grey Zone
(depends on expertise & setup) | | ≥17 | Amputation advised |
Clinical Use: * Individual component scores guide closure timing: * Score 1–2 →
Primary closure * Score 3–4 → Early/delayed flap * Score 5 → Staged reconstruction
Advantages: * Useful for non-ischemic open injuries. * Global limb assessment +
prognostic value. * Guides reconstruction strategy and timing.
Dr. Daivik’s Key Remark:
“Remember, not all severe injuries have vascular compromise. The Ganga Score
uniquely helps in decision-making for limbs without ischemia — it tells you when to save
and when to stage.”

Damage Control Orthopedics (DCO)
Evolution
- Early Total Care (ETC): Immediate definitive fixation of fractures (esp. long bones).
- Initially believed to reduce ARDS, fat embolism, and MOF — but led to increased late deaths due to SIRS and sepsis.
Pathophysiology
- First Hit: Initial trauma → surge of pro-inflammatory mediators (SIRS).
- Second Hit: Early extensive surgery, transfusion, hypoxia, or pain → overwhelms immune system → ARDS/MODS.
Goal: Prevent the second hit by staged stabilization until physiology improves.
SIRS vs CARS
Predominant

|---|---|---|
| Early | SIRS (TNF-α, IL-1, IL-6) | Hyperinflammatory state |

### Clinical Pearls
- DCO reduces ARDS and MODS.
- Shorter ICU stay and better rehabilitation.
- Always treat the patient, not the X-ray – “The fracture always has a patient attached to it.”
