OVERVIEW
Definition:
Complications in spine surgery extend beyond neurological injury and include vascular
trauma, pseudoarthrosis, and surgical-site infections (SSI) — all of which can
severely affect outcomes.
Dr. Vishnu’s Quote:
“In spine surgery, saving the patient’s life takes precedence over completing
the procedure — control the bleeding first, then finish the operation.”

VASCULAR INJURY
Incidence: Rare but potentially catastrophic (mortality up to 80%).
Classification:
- Early: Intraoperative or immediate post-op → massive bleeding, requires urgent
control.
- Late: Days to years post-op → pseudoaneurysm or arteriovenous fistula formation.

### Mechanisms of Injury
- Direct trauma: During discectomy or pedicle screw placement.

- Excessive retraction: Especially in anterior cervical approaches.

- Long screw projection: Chronic pulsatile contact → delayed aneurysm.

VERTEBRAL ARTERY INJURY (VAI)
### Anatomy & Risk Zones

| Segment | Location | Risk & Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| V1 | Subclavian → C6 foramen | Slips laterally with retractor in ACDF |
| V2 | C6 → C2 foramina | Injury during corpectomy (anterior) or pedicle screws (posterior) |
| V3 | C2–C1 junction | Most common site; over-dissection >1.5 cm from midline or high-riding artery |
| V4 | Intradural (cranial) | Rarely injured; forms basilar artery |

### Intraoperative Strategy
- If injured within screw hole: Insert screw to tamponade; do not place contralateral screw (avoid bilateral occlusion → cerebellar stroke).

- If open-space injury: Immediate finger tamponade → gel foam / hemostatic agents.

- If vascular surgeon available → direct repair or endovascular clipping/embolization.
Algorithm: 1. Control bleeding (tamponade/packing).
2. Resuscitate & maintain hemodynamics.
3. Assess dominance (angiogram).
- Non-dominant VA: Ligation acceptable.
- Dominant VA: Attempt repair/stenting.
4. Endovascular embolization if pseudoaneurysm/fistula develops. Pearl: At C1–C2, avoid dissection beyond 1–1.5 cm from midline to prevent V3 injury.

PSEUDOARTHROSIS (NON-UNION)
Definition: Failure to achieve solid fusion >1 year after index surgery.
Incidence: 5–35% of fusion surgeries.

Pathophysiology:
Fusion requires: 1. Osteogenesis – viable cells from graft.
2. Osteoinduction – growth factors stimulating bone formation.
3. Osteoconduction – scaffold for new bone growth.

### Key Risk Factors

| Patient-related | Technique-related |
|---|---|
| Smoking | Inadequate graft bed preparation |
| Diabetes / Malnutrition | Poor graft size/quality |
| Chronic steroid / NSAID use | Instability / inadequate fixation |
| Osteoporosis | Non-instrumented PLF fusion |
| Hormonal imbalance | Sagittal imbalance post-fusion |

NSAIDs inhibit inflammatory cascade → delayed fusion. Avoid
post-fusion.

SURGICAL SITE INFECTION (SSI)
Definition (CDC):
- Superficial: Skin & subcutaneous tissue.
- Deep: Beneath fascia → discitis, osteomyelitis, epidural abscess.
Timing:
| Type | Duration | |——-|———–| | Early | ≤1 month post-op | | Late | 2–6 months
post-op | | Very Late | >6 months post-op |

### Risk Factors

| Patient-related | Surgeon/Procedure-related |
|---|---|
| Diabetes, obesity, malnutrition | Long operative time |
| Smoking, alcoholism | Poor asepsis or OT traffic |
| Rheumatoid / HIV | Inadequate sterilization |
| Nasal carriage of S. aureus | Multiple revisions / implants |

### Prevention Strategies
- Preoperative antibiotics: Cefazolin / Cefuroxime within 60 min of incision.

- Skin prep: Betadine scrub + paint, allow drying.

- Hand hygiene: Strict double gloving.

- Topical vancomycin powder: 1 g in wound before closure (proven to reduce SSI).

- Laminar airflow OT: Reduces airborne contamination.

- Minimize OR traffic and talking.

### Clinical Features

| Type | Findings |
|---|---|
| Superficial | Tenderness, warmth, erythema, edema, shiny skin |
| Deep | Unexplained back pain (2 days–6 mo), fever, anorexia, weight loss, wound discharge |

Investigations: - Labs: ESR ↑, CRP ↑, anemia, hypoproteinemia.
- X-ray: Disc space loss, implant loosening, osteolysis.
- MRI: T2 hyperintensity, epidural/paraspinal collection.
- CT-guided biopsy: Culture & sensitivity (gold standard).
- PET-CT: Useful when MRI is distorted by implants.

### Treatment Protocol

| Scenario | Management |
|---|---|
| Stable implant, mild infection | IV antibiotics 6 wks → Oral 6 wks, close monitoring |
| Loosened implant / abscess | Surgical debridement ± implant revision |
| Extensive tissue loss | VAC dressing ± flap coverage |
| No growth culture | Empirical: Amikacin + Linezolid (IV) or Levofloxacin (oral) |

Spine is an exception — implants must stay for stability to allow healing.
### EXAM & VIVA PEARLS
- Most common vascular injury: Left common iliac vein (L4–L5).

- Tamponade + screw insertion = first step for vertebral artery injury.

- Pseudoarthrosis: CT = gold standard.

- NSAIDs delay fusion → avoid post-fusion.

- SSI prevention: Antibiotics, asepsis, vancomycin powder.

- Posterior-only fixation can still be used safely in infected spine.

<!-- page 7 -->

### TAKE-HOME SUMMARY
- Recognize vascular risk zones before screw placement or discectomy.

- Tamponade first, complete surgery later in vascular bleeds.

- Ensure fusion biology + stable fixation to prevent pseudoarthrosis.

- CT scan is most reliable for fusion assessment.

- Follow strict infection prevention: antibiotic prophylaxis, asepsis, and OT discipline.

- Goal: Safe surgery, solid fusion, and infection-free recovery.
