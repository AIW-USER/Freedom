Radiographic Evaluation of the Paediatric Foot – Dr.
Shalin Shah (Sep 2025)
### Overview / Key Concepts
- How to read paediatric foot X-rays (with CT/MRI where needed) and apply standard angles to common conditions: congenital vertical talus, tarsal coalitions, juvenile hallux valgus, clubfoot, focal gigantism, equinus vs cavus, cavovarus and skew foot.
- Clinical examination comes FIRST; X-rays confirm and quantify it, and tell you which extra views to order.

Principles
Rule Details
1. Get the right X-rays Standing (weight-bearing) AP and lateral of feet and ankles; orthogonal views (one view is no view). Extras: oblique (standard/reverse), Saltzman, Harris, Coleman block, max dorsiflexion/plantarflexion laterals
2. Be systematic Segments: forefoot, midfoot, hindfoot. Columns: medial, lateral. Find the CORA(s)/apex
3. Find the apex Location and magnitude of deformity guide the osteotomy
4. X-rays do not replace examination Exam decides the special views (e.g. plantarflexion lateral for vertical talus, max dorsiflexion for equinus)
5. Confirm the exam Coleman block test; clubfoot tenotomy; max DF/PF laterals
6. Special views with intent Harris: talocalcaneal coalition (sustentaculum, middle facet). Saltzman: hindfoot alignment. External (standard) oblique: calcaneonavicular coalition. Reverse (internal) oblique: accessory navicular

- Tripod concept: the foot stands on the 1st MT head, 5th MT head and calcaneus. In cavovarus (commonly Charcot-Marie-Tooth: intrinsic weakness first; peroneus longus overpowers a weak tibialis anterior) the 1st ray drops (plantarflexed), so the hindfoot goes into varus to bring the lateral column down. Coleman block shows whether the hindfoot varus is flexible (forefoot-driven).
- Ossification: most foot ossification centres appear by about 5 years; before that the 1st metatarsal is used as a surrogate for the navicular (tarsals are collinear with it). The 1st MT physis is at its BASE; the other metatarsal physes are at the heads.

### Congenital Vertical Talus (CVT)
- Definition: FIXED (irreducible) dorsal dislocation of the navicular on the talus. Oblique talus = the dislocation REDUCES on plantarflexion.
- Key view: forced PLANTARFLEXION lateral: in CVT the talar axis and 1st MT axis stay non-collinear (1st MT used for the navicular under 2 years). TAMBA (talar axis–1st metatarsal base angle) over 35° = vertical talus.

<!-- page 3 -->

- Dorsiflexion lateral: calcaneal pitch stays negative (hindfoot equinus remains); calcaneus more than perpendicular to the tibia (obtuse), talus nearly vertical/plantar.
- AP: forefoot/midfoot abduction (talus–1st MT and calcaneus–4th MT angles over about 30°), Kite angle over 40° (valgus).
- Rocker-bottom appearance: only the midfoot and forefoot are dorsiflexed; the hindfoot is in equinus. Dobbs method: serial casts to reduce the talonavicular joint, K-wire, then percutaneous tendo-Achilles tenotomy to correct equinus.

### Juvenile vs Adult Hallux Valgus
- Hallux valgus affects 20–25% of people; 20–30% of these begin in childhood (mostly inherited).
- Juvenile: often long/abnormal 1st MT, INCREASED DMAA (articular surface of the MT head tilted laterally), MTP joint CONGRUENT, open physes.
- Adult-onset: DMAA near normal, joint INCONGRUENT (proximal phalanx subluxed laterally).
- Guided growth: in children around 7–8 years, a lateral hemi-epiphysiodesis of the 1st MT (screw/drill) lets the medial side grow and straightens the ray (newer technique).

### Clubfoot (CTEV) Radiographs
- Four elements (X-rays are not routine): hindfoot equinus (lateral talocalcaneal angle under 35°, negative calcaneal pitch), hindfoot varus (AP talocalcaneal/Kite under 20°, converging axes), metatarsus adductus (talus–1st MT over 15°), talonavicular subluxation (navicular medial on the talus).
- X-rays are useful for associated anomalies: hemimelias (fewer rays: tibial hemimelia; more rays: fibular hemimelia (as taught)), tarsal coalition, and complications.
- Tenotomy pitfall: a tendo-Achilles tenotomy done too low (at the calcaneal tuberosity) can damage the apophysis, giving a bony bump/ossification; cut about 1 cm above the palpable tuberosity.

### Deformity Analysis (CORA) in the Foot
- As in long bones: proximal and distal axes meet at the CORA; an osteotomy at the CORA corrects without translation.
- Foot: hindfoot axis = talar axis, forefoot axis = 1st MT axis on both AP (adductus) and lateral (cavus); combining both gives a 3D picture (ideal is 3D CT).
- If they meet at the navicular (too small to cut), osteotomise the MEDIAL CUNEIFORM: medial opening wedge for adductus, plantar opening wedge for cavus.
- If the CORA is at the talus/calcaneus (mostly adductus): e.g. lateral closing-wedge subtalar fusion.

### Exam & Viva Pearls
- Weight-bearing AP and lateral; one view is no view.
- Kite 25–40°; talus–1st MT 0–7° (AP); calcaneal pitch 10–15°.
- CVT: forced plantarflexion lateral; fixed dorsal talonavicular dislocation; TAMBA over 35°.
- Anteater sign (CN coalition, external oblique); C-sign and Harris view (TC coalition).
- Juvenile hallux valgus: high DMAA with congruent joint.
- Silfverskiöld test for gastrocnemius contracture.

### Take-Home Summary
- Examine first, then order weight-bearing films and only the special views you need; analyse each segment and column with standard angles, find the CORA, and use it to choose the right osteotomy.
