Target Ortho Quick Revision Notes – Occlusive
Cerebrovascular Disease (2025)
Faculty: Dr. Himanshu Champaneri

OVERVIEW
Definition:
Occlusive Cerebrovascular Disease (OCVD) refers to stenosis or occlusion of the
arteries supplying the brain, resulting in transient or permanent ischemia due to
reduced cerebral blood flow.
Dr. Himanshu’s Quote:
“A stroke doesn’t kill the brain in seconds — hypoperfusion does. It’s not about
blockage, it’s about balance.”
Categories: 1. Extracranial occlusive disease – affects carotid and vertebral
arteries in the neck.
2. Intracranial occlusive disease – involves Circle of Willis and its branches.
Pathophysiology: - Atherosclerosis is the most common cause.
- Thrombosis and embolism result in focal brain ischemia.
- Secondary causes: dissection, vasculitis, radiation arteritis, fibromuscular
dysplasia.

| Parameter | Data |

- 80–85% are ischemic; 15–20% hemorrhagic.

### ETIOPATHOGENESIS

| Mechanism | Description |
|---|---|
| Atherosclerosis | Plaque deposition → lumen narrowing → thromboembolism |
| Thrombosis | Plaque rupture → local clot formation |
| Embolism | Cardiac (AF, MI, endocarditis) or atheromatous emboli |
| Dissection | Intimal tear → false lumen → occlusion |
| Vasculitis | Inflammation of vessel wall (TA, PAN, SLE, Moyamoya) |
| Hypercoagulable states | Malignancy, protein C/S deficiency, pregnancy |

Cerebral Blood Flow (CBF): - Normal: 50–60 mL/100g/min.
- Ischemia threshold: 20 mL/100g/min.
- Infarction threshold: 10 mL/100g/min.

### ANATOMY OF CEREBRAL CIRCULATION

| Circulation | Arteries | Major Territories Supplied |
|---|---|---|
| Anterior | ICA → ACA + MCA | Frontal, parietal, temporal lobes |
| Posterior | Vertebral → Basilar → PCA | Occipital lobe, brainstem, cerebellum |

Watershed Zones: Between ACA–MCA and MCA–PCA territories → vulnerable to
hypoperfusion.

SPECIAL VARIANTS
### 1⃣ Moyamoya Disease
- Progressive stenosis of terminal ICA with development of abnormal collateral network (“puff-of-smoke” appearance on angiography).

- Common in children & young adults.

- Symptoms: TIA, stroke, seizures.

- Treatment: Indirect or direct revascularization (e.g., EDAS, STA–MCA bypass).
### 2⃣ Takayasu Arteritis
- Granulomatous vasculitis affecting large arteries (aorta, subclavian, carotid).

- Predominantly young females.

- Presents with limb claudication, weak pulses, BP discrepancy, and cerebrovascular insufficiency.

- Treatment: Steroids + immunosuppressants + angioplasty/stenting for fixed lesions.
### 3⃣ Radiation-Induced Arteriopathy
- Late complication of neck irradiation → intimal fibrosis and stenosis.

- Management same as atherosclerotic disease; often requires stenting.

### COMPLICATIONS
- Recurrent stroke or TIA.

<!-- page 6 -->

- Hemorrhagic transformation post-thrombolysis.

- Hyperperfusion syndrome after CEA/CAS (headache, seizure, hemorrhage).

- Contrast-induced nephropathy.

- Restenosis after endarterectomy or stenting.

### EXAM & VIVA PEARLS
- Most common cause of stroke: Atherosclerosis.

- Most common artery: ICA at bifurcation.

- CT vs MRI: MRI DWI = earliest infarct detection.

- NASCET criteria: % stenosis = [1 – (residual lumen / distal normal lumen)] × 100.

- tPA window: 4.5 hours.

- Mechanical thrombectomy window: up to 24 hours (if viable penumbra).

- CEA indication: ≥70% symptomatic stenosis.

- Moyamoya: “Puff-of-smoke” collaterals on angiogram.

- Watershed infarct: Between ACA–MCA or MCA–PCA territories.

### TAKE-HOME SUMMARY
- OCVD = narrowing or occlusion of major cerebral vessels causing ischemia.

- Atherosclerosis = primary cause.

- Diagnosis: CT/MRI + vascular imaging (CTA/MRA/DSA).

- Thrombolysis (tPA) within 4.5 hours is key for salvageable tissue.

- CEA / CAS prevent future strokes in severe carotid stenosis.

- Control of BP, diabetes, lipids, and lifestyle is lifelong prevention.

<!-- page 7 -->

- Goal: Restore perfusion, prevent recurrence, preserve function.
