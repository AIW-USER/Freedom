BLOOD SUPPLY OF THE BRAIN
Total Cerebral Blood Flow: ~750 mL/min (15% of cardiac output).
Brain supplied by two pairs of arteries: 1. Internal Carotid Arteries (ICA) → anterior
circulation.
2. Vertebral Arteries → posterior circulation.

### 1⃣ Internal Carotid Artery (ICA) System

| Branch | Supplies |
|---|---|
| Anterior Cerebral Artery | Medial frontal & parietal lobes, |
| (ACA) | paracentral lobule |
| Middle Cerebral Artery (MCA) | Lateral surface of hemispheres, Broca’s & Wernicke’s areas |
| Anterior Choroidal Artery | Internal capsule, optic tract, hippocampus |

Clinical Correlation:
- ACA stroke: Contralateral leg weakness > arm.
- MCA stroke: Contralateral face & arm weakness; aphasia (dominant side).
- Anterior choroidal stroke: Pure motor hemiplegia.

### BRAIN MENINGES & SPACES

| Layer | Description |
|---|---|
| Dura Mater | Tough outer layer; forms venous sinuses |
| Arachnoid Mater | Middle, avascular layer; subarachnoid space contains CSF |
| Pia Mater | Thin, vascular layer adherent to brain surface |

Spaces: - Epidural (potential): Between skull & dura – hematoma from middle
meningeal artery.
- Subdural (potential): Between dura & arachnoid – bridging vein rupture.
- Subarachnoid: Between arachnoid & pia – contains CSF & vessels.

### CEREBROSPINAL FLUID (CSF)

| Parameter | Description |
|---|---|
| Production | Choroid plexus (mainly lateral ventricles) – ~500 mL/day |
| Circulation | Lateral → 3rd → 4th ventricles → subarachnoid space |
| Absorption | Arachnoid villi → superior sagittal sinus |
| Volume | 150 mL |

Blockage Sites (Hydrocephalus): - Foramen of Monro: Lateral → 3rd ventricle
(Monro block → unilateral dilation).
- Cerebral Aqueduct (Sylvius): 3rd → 4th (aqueductal stenosis → non-communicating
hydrocephalus).
- Arachnoid villi dysfunction: Communicating hydrocephalus.
Lumbar Puncture Site: L3–L4 (below conus medullaris).
CSF Pressure: 10–18 cm H₂O (normal).

VASCULAR SURGERY & ANEURYSMS
### Cerebral Aneurysms

| Type | Description | Common Sites |
|---|---|---|
| Saccular | Congenital wall weakness | AComA, PComA, MCA |

(berry) bifurcation

| Fusiform | Diffuse arterial dilatation | Basilar artery |
|---|---|---|
| Dissectin g | Tear in intima, blood enters wall | Vertebral / basilar system |

Presentation: - Sudden severe headache (“thunderclap”)
- Vomiting, photophobia, neck stiffness
- Cranial nerve palsy (esp. III in PComA aneurysm)
Investigation: CT brain (SAH), CT/MR angiography, DSA (gold standard).

### Management Approaches

| Approach | Principle |
|---|---|
| Microsurgical Clipping | Aneurysm neck clipped via craniotomy – gold standard for accessible lesions |
| Endovascular Coiling (Guglielmi | Catheter-based packing with coils |
| Detachable Coil – GDC) | – preferred for posterior circulation or deep aneurysms |
| Flow Diverter Stents | Redirect flow away from aneurysm sac – for wide-neck aneurysms |

Adjunct Measures:
- Nimodipine 60 mg q4h × 21 days (prevents vasospasm).
- Control BP, avoid rebleed (SBP < 140).
- Maintain euvolemia.

### COMPLICATIONS OF ANEURYSM SURGERY
- Rebleeding (within 24–48 h).

- Vasospasm (4–14 days post-SAH).

- Hydrocephalus (obstructive).

<!-- page 6 -->

- Seizures.

- Cranial nerve palsy (esp. oculomotor).
### Vasospasm Prevention (Triple-H Therapy): 1. Hypertension (MAP 90–100)
2. Hypervolemia
3. Hemodilution

### EXAM & VIVA PEARLS
- MCA = most common artery involved in infarct.

- ACA = leg weakness; MCA = face/arm weakness + aphasia.

- PCA = visual loss.

- Wallenberg syndrome (PICA): Dysphagia, hoarseness, Horner’s, contralateral pain/temp loss.

- Berry aneurysm: AComA > PComA > MCA bifurcation.

- Cavernous sinus thrombosis: Ophthalmoplegia + facial infection.

- Basilar artery occlusion: Locked-in syndrome.

### TAKE-HOME SUMMARY
- Brain has dual circulation: carotid (anterior) + vertebrobasilar (posterior).

- MCA territory infarcts are most common.

- Circle of Willis provides crucial collateral flow.

- PICA/Vertebrobasilar strokes → brainstem syndromes.

- Berry aneurysms → most frequent cause of spontaneous SAH.

- Management = early clipping/coiling + vasospasm prevention.

- Goal: Preserve perfusion, prevent rupture, and protect neurological function.
