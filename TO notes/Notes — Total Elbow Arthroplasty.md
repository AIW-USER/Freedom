Faculty: Dr. Rahul Upadhyay

OVERVIEW
Total Elbow Arthroplasty (TEA) is a less commonly performed arthroplasty compared
to hip or knee, mainly due to its higher complication rate and restricted
post-operative functional demands. It aims to restore a painless, stable, and
mobile elbow while preserving soft-tissue integrity.
Quote: “A successful elbow arthroplasty is not just a mobile joint—it’s a stable,
well-balanced one.” – Dr. Rahul Upadhyay

IDEAL PROSTHESIS CHARACTERISTICS
An ideal elbow implant should be:
- Painless, stable, mobile, durable, inert
- Retrievable & reproducible
- Preserve epicondyles & olecranon
- Maintain carrying angle (valgus in extension → varus in flexion)
- Sacrifice minimal bone
- Provide stable fixation with minimal dead space
- Allow functional ROM (30°–130° flexion arc)

IMPLANT SELECTION CRITERIA
Selection depends on:
- Capsuloligamentous integrity – especially anterior medial ligament complex (70% of stability)
- Muscular status: Triceps, biceps, brachialis, flexor–extensor balance
- Bone stock and quality of distal humerus & proximal ulna

### INDICATIONS FOR TOTAL ELBOW ARTHROPLASTY
- End-stage rheumatoid arthritis or post-traumatic arthritis
- Unreconstructable distal humerus fractures in elderly (>60 yrs)
- Bilateral or fibrous ankylosis in poor position
- Loss of bone stock (tumor, trauma)
- Hemophilic arthropathy

<!-- page 3 -->

Contraindications
- Active/recent infection or sepsis
- Poor soft-tissue envelope
- Non-functional biceps/triceps
- Poor compliance (weight-lifting refusal)
- Flaccid paralysis of upper limb
- Young, vigorous post-traumatic elbow destruction
- Neuropathic joint / Ipsilateral shoulder ankylosis

### PRE-OPERATIVE PLANNING
- Radiographs: AP + Lateral views (for humeral bow & canal diameter)
- Assess ulnar medullary canal size & curvature (both views)
- Pre-op templating with implant template
- Ulnar nerve evaluation
- Rule out sepsis: aspiration if warmth/redness suspected (esp. RA)

### SURGICAL TECHNIQUE (Coonrad–Morrey Type)
- Position: Supine, elbow flexed above shoulder; sandbag under arm.
- Approach: Triceps reflecting osteoperiosteal flap.
- Steps:
  1. Identify & protect ulnar nerve.
  2. Expose humeral & ulnar canals.
  3. Sequential reaming of medullary canals.
  4. Trial implantation – ensure axis aligns with trochlear groove.
  5. Cement both stems; center articulation in scapular fossa.
  6. Repair triceps flap & capsuloligamentous sleeve.
  7. Confirm ulnar nerve free of tension through full ROM.

### PROSTHESIS DESIGN – COONRAD-MORREY
- Semi-constrained design with metal–polyethylene articulation
- 7° varus–valgus & rotatory laxity for force dissipation
- Triangular humeral stem – flattened base for stability & anterior flange to resist torque
- Long medullary stem → rigid fixation in humerus & ulna
- High molecular weight polyethylene bushing between titanium humeral & ulnar components Provides resistance to torsional and shear stresses → excellent long-term stability.

### POST-OPERATIVE CARE
- Elevate limb overnight (elbow above shoulder)
- Remove drains & compression next day
- Passive flexion–extension as tolerated (after drain removal)
- Cuff & collar immobilization; supervised movement only
- No active extension for 3 months (to allow triceps healing)
- Avoid lifting >2.2 kg (5 lbs) for 3 months → lifelong limit ≈ 4.5 kg (10 lbs)

OUTCOME ASSESSMENT
### Morrey Rating System
- Good: No pain, >90° flexion, >60° rotation, no radiolucent lines.
- Fair: Mild pain, 50–90° flexion, 40° rotation, <1 mm radiolucency.
- Poor: Significant pain, <50° motion, >2 mm radiolucency.

### Key Exam Pearls
- Semi-constrained TEA is the implant of choice (best long-term survival).
- Anterior medial ligament (70%) is the key stabilizer → assess in all cases.
- Coonrad–Morrey prosthesis: 7° laxity, triangular stem, anterior flange.
- Post-op restriction: No lifting >4.5 kg lifelong.
- Most common failure: Instability > Aseptic loosening > Infection.
- Mayo Classification: for periprosthetic fractures.
- Infection rate: higher than THR/TKR due to thin soft tissue.
