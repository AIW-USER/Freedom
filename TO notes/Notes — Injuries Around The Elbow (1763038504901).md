Injuries Around the Elbow – Dr. Daivik T Shetty

### Key Concepts / Overview
- The elbow is a complex joint functioning as both a hinge (flexion–extension) and pivot (supination–pronation) mechanism.
- It is the second most common dislocation in adults and often presents with combined ligamentous and bony injuries.
- Understanding anatomy, biomechanics, and instability patterns is crucial for correct diagnosis and management.
Dr. Daivik’s Remark:
“Instability around the elbow is never just about one structure—it’s always a story of
disrupted balance between bone, ligament, and motion.”

Important Anatomical Structures
### Coronoid Process
- Acts as anterior bony buttress preventing posterior dislocation.
- Fracture >50% height → elbow instability.
- Sublime tubercle: attachment of anterior bundle of MCL.
### Radial Head
- Involved in both radiocapitellar & PRUJ articulation.
- Secondary valgus stabilizer; resists posterolateral rotation.
- Safe zone for plate fixation: 280° of radial head articular surface; posterior-lateral 80° non-articular.

### Common Injuries Around the Elbow
1. Radial Head Fracture
- Most common elbow fracture in adults (F>M, 20–50 years).
- Mechanism: FOOSH (fall on outstretched hand) → valgus + axial load.
- May be part of Terrible Triad or Essex–Lopresti lesion.
### Essex–Lopresti Injury
- Radial head fracture + interosseous membrane rupture + DRUJ subluxation.
Imaging

| Modality | Use |
|---|---|
| X-ray (AP, lateral) | Initial evaluation |
| Greenspan view | Radiocapitellar view (45° cephalad beam) |
| CT scan | Defines comminution & displacement |

### Mason Classification (Modified Hotchkiss–Broberg–Morrey)

| Type | Description | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| I | Non-displaced | Sling + early ROM |
| II | Displaced >2 mm, stable | ORIF |
| III | Comminuted, non-reconstructable | Replacement |
| IV | With dislocation | ORIF or prosthesis |

Surgical Approaches: * Kocher (ECRB–anconeus interval) – standard, protects PIN
with forearm pronation. * Kaplan (ECRL–ECRB interval) – for anterior exposure. *
Avoid deep retraction near annular ligament → PIN injury.

Treatment Principle: * Isolated, low-energy → Excision.
- Unstable/complex → Fixation or prosthesis.
- Never excise radial head if MCL, coronoid, or interosseous membrane are injured.

2. Coronoid Process Fracture
- Provides anterior stability; fractured in 10–15% of dislocations.
- Associated with PLRI or PMRI.

4. Elbow Dislocation
- Second most common dislocation in adults (after shoulder).
- Mechanism: FOOSH → valgus, axial, or rotational load.

Most common associated nerve injury: Ulnar nerve.
### Reduction Technique – Parvin’s Method
1. Flex elbow to 30°.
2. Traction–counter-traction.
3. Correct medial–lateral alignment.
4. Supinate forearm, apply anterior pressure on olecranon while flexing.
If irreducible or unstable → Open reduction via posterior approach ± ligament repair.
Immobilization Position: | Repair | Position | |———|———–| | LCL repaired, MCL
intact | Pronation | | MCL repaired, LCL intact | Supination | | Both repaired | Neutral |

Complex Elbow Dislocations – Terrible Triad
Components: 1. Posterior dislocation. 2. Radial head fracture. 3. Coronoid process
fracture.
Why “Terrible”? * Extremely unstable and difficult to repair. * Small fragments, poor
soft tissue cover, and frequent residual stiffness.
Sequential Injury Pattern (Horii Cycle): 1. LUCL rupture → PLRI. 2. Anterior &
posterior capsule rupture. 3. MCL rupture → gross instability.
Reversed Horii Cycle: Varus + pronation → PMRI, coronoid AM facet injury.

### Treatment Protocol (McKee & Pugh)
1. Fix or suture coronoid (buttress or suture anchor).
2. Repair or replace radial head.
3. Repair LCL (primary stabilizer).
4. If unstable → Repair MCL.
5. If still unstable → Hinged external fixator.
Preferred Lateral Approach: Kocher’s.

### Chronic Elbow Instability

| Type | Mechanism | Reconstruction |
|---|---|---|
| PLRI | LUCL deficiency | LUCL reconstruction using Palmaris longus graft |
| PMRI | MCL deficiency | MCL reconstruction (Jobe or Docking technique) |

Tests: * Valgus stress test – for MCL. * Pivot shift test – for LUCL insufficiency
(reduction at 40° flexion during supination–valgus stress).

Complications

| Complication | Notes |
|---|---|
| Instability | From missed ligamentous injury |
| Stiffness | Most common long-term issue |
| Heterotopic Ossification | Common after surgery / trauma |
| Ulnar Neuropathy | Entrapment or scarring |
| Hardware failure / loosening | Especially with radial prostheses |
| Post-traumatic arthritis | Due to incongruent reduction |

### Exam / Viva Pearls
- Valgus stabilizer: MCL anterior bundle.
- Varus stabilizer: Coronoid process.
- PLRI stabilizer: LUCL.
- Safe zone for radial head fixation: Posterolateral 80° (between radial styloid & Lister’s tubercle).
- Terrible triad: Radial head + Coronoid + Posterior dislocation.
- Parvin’s method – used for elbow dislocation reduction.
- MCL repaired → supination; LCL repaired → pronation; both → neutral.
Final Faculty Takeaway:
“Elbow stability depends on harmony between its bones, ligaments, and
muscles—restore all three, and function will follow.”
