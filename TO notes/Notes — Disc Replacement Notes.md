Target Ortho Quick Revision Notes – Total Disc
Replacement (TDR)
Faculty: Dr. C.S. Vishnu Prasath

OVERVIEW
Definition: Total Disc Replacement (TDR) or Spinal Arthroplasty involves replacing the
degenerated intervertebral disc with an artificial prosthesis to preserve motion and
relieve pain.
Goal: Maintain motion, restore disc height, and normalize load transmission — unlike
fusion, which eliminates motion.
Common Levels: L4–L5 and L5–S1 (maximum motion segments).
First Introduced: Fernström (1950s) – steel ball implants; modern devices evolved in
1980s–90s (Charité, ProDisc-L, Maverick).

ANATOMY & BIOMECHANICS
### Normal Lumbar Disc
- Annulus fibrosus: Collagen fibers + fibroblasts → resist tension & expand circumferentially.

- Nucleus pulposus: Proteoglycans (GAGs) + chondrocytes → hydrophilic, convert axial load → radial force.

- Nutrition: Diffusion through endplates; dependent on motion.
### Degenerative Cascade
1. Disc degeneration → loss of hydration.

2. Dysfunctional motion segment → abnormal motion, instability.

3. Restabilization → osteophytes, facet arthrosis, ligamentum flavum hypertrophy.

4. Consequence → loss of motion, canal stenosis, adjacent segment disease.

INDICATIONS & PATIENT SELECTION
Ideal Stage: Early degenerative phase (dysfunction or early instability).
Primary Indication: Symptomatic degenerative disc disease (DDD) with chronic low
back pain refractory to conservative treatment.

### Inclusion Criteria
1. Severe discogenic low back pain with failed prolonged non-surgical management.

2. VAS > 4 and Oswestry > 40%.

3. Age 18–60 years (optimally <50 years).

4. MRI/CT evidence of single-level DDD or lumbar spondylosis without stenosis or instability.
Contraindications
- Spondylolisthesis / Spondylolysis.

- Facet joint arthritis or resection.

- Central / lateral recess stenosis.

- Fixed deformity or infection.

- Osteoporosis (T-score < –2).

- Herniated disc with radiculopathy.

- Psychological instability (depression, anxiety, chronic pain behavior).

### INVESTIGATIONS
- MRI: Identify degenerative disc disease, exclude nerve compression.

- CT: Assess facet joint integrity.

- Dynamic X-rays: Detect instability.

- DEXA scan: Rule out osteoporosis.

- Provocative discography: Occasionally used to confirm discogenic pain (now rarely recommended).

DEVICE CHARACTERISTICS
Ideal Total Disc Prosthesis Should: 1. Restore normal physiological motion.
2. Transmit and absorb load effectively.
3. Allow controlled translation.
4. Restore disc & foraminal height.
5. Permit bony ingrowth for stability.
6. Be biocompatible, modular, and durable.

### Design Types
- Non-constrained: More motion, stress on facets.

- Constrained: Limited motion, requires precise centering.

- Semi-constrained: Balanced; allows controlled motion within normal range.

### COMMON TDR IMPLANTS

| Implant | Articulation Type | Remarks |
|---|---|---|
| Charité | Metal-on-Polyethylene | First FDA-approved; good long-term data |
| ProDisc-L | Metal-on-Polyethylene (fixed core) | Controlled motion; stable keel design |
| Maverick | Metal-on-Metal (Cobalt-Chromium) | Durable; low wear but metal ion risk |

### SURGICAL APPROACH & TECHNIQUE
- Approach: Anterior retroperitoneal (L4–L5, L5–S1 levels).

- Requires access surgeon (vascular/general) due to vessel mobilization.

- Position: French position.
### Surgical Steps
1. Expose disc space.

<!-- page 4 -->

2. Excise entire disc → prepare endplates.

3. Select implant size using template.

4. Insert prosthesis under fluoroscopy → ensure snug fit and alignment.

COMPLICATIONS
Approach-Related
- Vascular injury (commonest serious risk).

- Retrograde ejaculation (due to sympathetic plexus injury).

- Infection, hematoma, or visceral injury.
Implant-Related
- Malposition, subsidence, dislocation.

- Facet joint degeneration (esp. non-constrained designs).

- Implant wear or failure (10–20% over 10–15 years).
### Revision Issues
- Anterior re-exploration extremely difficult → high vascular risk.

- Most failed TDRs are treated with posterior pedicle screw fusion, leaving prosthesis in situ.

NUCLEUS REPLACEMENT
Concept: Replace nucleus pulposus only — preserve annulus and endplates.

Advantages
- Tissue-preserving, simpler, posterior approach.

- Maintains motion; less invasive than TDR.
Limitations
- Risk of extrusion (through annulotomy defect).

- Not suitable if annulus compromised or disc collapsed.

<!-- page 5 -->

Examples
- Aquerelle, Neudisc, EBI Regain (hydrogel-based implants).

POSTERIOR DYNAMIC STABILIZATION
Concept: Motion-preserving posterior systems to prevent iatrogenic instability.

Indications
- Post-decompression stabilization.

- Early DDD or adjacent segment preservation.
Examples
Graf system, Dynesys, Isobar, Bioflex, Wallis system, TOPS device.

Function
- Allows limited flexion-extension.

- Prevents abnormal motion.

- Reduces stress on adjacent segments.

### VIVA & EXAM PEARLS
- Ideal TDR candidate: Early DDD, no instability or facet degeneration.

- Most common level: L4–L5 or L5–S1.

- Fusion vs TDR: TDR preserves motion, reduces adjacent segment disease (~10% after 10 yrs in fusions).

- All TDR implants: Uncemented; rely on bony ingrowth via porous metal or keels.

- Failure rate: 10–20% at 10–15 years.

- Revision: Avoid anterior re-entry; perform posterior fusion if needed.

- Cervical Disc Replacement: Technically easier, safer, and more accepted than lumbar TDR.

<!-- page 6 -->

### TAKE-HOME SUMMARY
- TDR aims to treat painful DDD while preserving spinal motion.

- Proper patient selection is critical — early DDD without facet arthritis or instability.

- Complications and revision difficulties limit widespread use.

- Nucleus replacement and posterior dynamic stabilization are emerging alternatives.

- Cervical disc replacement is well established; lumbar TDR remains under evaluation.
