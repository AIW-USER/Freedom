Target Ortho Quick Revision Notes – Multiligament Knee Injuries
Faculty: Dr. Mukul Mohindra

OVERVIEW
Multiligament Knee Injury (MLKI) = Injury to two or more of the four major
stabilizers of the knee (ACL, PCL, MCL, LCL). It represents a knee dislocation
equivalent, often following high-energy trauma, and carries risk of vascular and
neurological complications.
Quote: “Every multiligament knee is a potential dislocation — think vascular
first, ligament later.” – Dr. Mukul Mohindra

### DEFINITION & INCIDENCE
- At least 2 ligaments torn, typically one cruciate + one collateral.
- True incidence often underreported as 30–50% spontaneously reduce.
- Overall knee dislocation incidence: 0.02% of all trauma cases.

### CLASSIFICATION SYSTEMS
1. Direction-Based (Anatomic)
- Based on tibial displacement relative to femur:
  - Anterior (40%) – hyperextension injury.
  - Posterior (33%) – dashboard injury.
  - Lateral (18%)
  - Medial (4%)
  - Rotatory (5%) – combination of forces.

2. Based on Energy
- High-energy: Road traffic accidents, sports trauma.
- Low/Ultra-low energy: Obese or elderly individuals (simple fall can dislocate knee).
3. Based on Time of Presentation

| Stage | Duration |
|---|---|
| Acute | <3 weeks |
| Subacute | 3 weeks – 3 months |
| Chronic | >3 months |

4. Schenck Classification

| Type | Structures Involved |
|---|---|
| KD-I | ACL + one collateral (MCL/LCL) with intact PCL |
| KD-II | ACL + PCL; collaterals intact |
| KD-IIIM | ACL + PCL + Medial corner (MCL/POL) |
| KD-IIIL | ACL + PCL + Lateral corner (LCL/PLC) |
| KD-IV | All four ligaments torn |

| Posterior | Dashboard injury (posterior force on flexed knee) |
| Medial/Later | Varus or valgus stress |

VASCULAR & NEUROLOGICAL INJURY
### Vascular Compromise
- Incidence: 16–64% overall; highest in posterior dislocations (44%).
- Mechanism:
  - Stretch injury: anterior dislocation.
  - Direct contusion or transection: posterior dislocation.
- Critical time: Ischemia >8 hours → 86% amputation rate.
### Assessment Protocol

| Finding | Action |
|---|---|
| Absent distal pulses / signs of ischemia | Immediate reduction → vascular consult ± on-table angiogram |
| Pulses present, ABI >0.9 | Observe for 24 hours |
| ABI <0.9 or asymmetry | CT/Conventional angiography |

<!-- page 3 -->

ABI = Ankle–Brachial Index. Pulses alone are unreliable.
### Neurological Injury
- Peroneal nerve injury: 10–40% (avg. 15%), most common with posterolateral dislocation.
- Mechanism: traction > transection.
- Recovery: only 50% regain full function; superficial peroneal division recovers first.

INITIAL MANAGEMENT
### Emergency Priorities
1. Immediate reduction under sedation.
  - Longitudinal traction from ankle + manipulation of proximal tibia.
2. Post-reduction: reassess neurovascular status + X-ray confirmation.
3. Immobilization: Long leg splint in extension; towel bump under calf.

### Absolute Indications for Surgery / Ex-fix
- Vascular compromise.
- Irreducibility (Dimple sign – posterolateral dislocation).
- Unstable reduction.
- Open dislocation or associated fractures.
- Warm ischemia >6 hrs → mandatory fasciotomy. Hinged knee–spanning external fixator preferred if instability persists.

IMAGING
X-Ray:
- AP/Lateral for initial assessment.
- Look for associated signs:
  - Segond sign → ACL injury.
  - Reverse Segond sign → PCL/PLC injury.
  - Arcuate sign → PLC avulsion.

### MRI:
- Performed after acute stabilization.
- Defines ligament pattern (KD type) and guides reconstruction plan.

### SURGICAL TIMING & PRINCIPLES

| Condition | Approach |
|---|---|
| Stable reduction, ROM >90°, skin healthy | Early (within 3 weeks) repair/reconstruction |
| Polytrauma, poor skin, vascular | Delayed / staged surgery |

Early repair gives best results in young, active patients.
### Factors Affecting Timing
- Vascular status.
- Skin viability.
- Associated fractures.
- Patient’s systemic condition.

| Stage | Plan |
|---|---|
| Acute | One-stage ACL + PCL reconstruction (if tissues viable) |

### Tensioning Sequence (Combined Reconstruction)
1. PCL tensioned at 90° flexion (anterior drawer position).
2. PLC tensioned in slight varus + internal rotation.
3. ACL tensioned in full extension.

### 4⃣ Tricruciate (ACL + PCL + MCL or PLC)

| Combination | Plan |
|---|---|
| <3 weeks (acute) | One-stage ACL + PCL + collateral repair |
| >3 weeks (chronic) | Stage 1: PCL + PLC/MCL → Stage 2: ACL (after 3 mo) |

Late medial instability → delayed MCL reconstruction.

### REHABILITATION PROTOCOL

| Phase | Duration | Key Guidelines |
|---|---|---|
| 0–6 weeks | PCL brace, NWB | No supine knee bending (prone ROM only) |
| 6–8 weeks | Begin partial WB, ROM >90° | Closed chain only; avoid ER |
| 8–12 weeks | Full WB + Active ROM | Quadriceps squats <70°, continue brace |
| >12 weeks | Open chain strengthening | Proprioceptive & functional drills |

<!-- page 6 -->

| Phase | Duration | Key Guidelines |
|---|---|---|
| Return to sports | 9 months | Only after full recovery & stability |

Early aggressive rehab causes graft failure; protection phase critical.

### COMPLICATIONS
- Missed vascular injury → amputation.
- Arthrofibrosis (stiffness).
- Residual instability (missed PLC/MCL tear).
- Graft failure (premature rehab / malposition).
- Peroneal nerve palsy (persistent foot drop in 50%).

### EXAM PEARLS
- Multiligament injury = dislocation until proven otherwise.
- KD-IV: all four ligaments torn.
- Posterolateral dislocation: Dimple sign = irreducible → open reduction.
- Vascular injury: ABI <0.9 → angiography.
- Commonest nerve injured: Peroneal nerve.
- Surgical sequence: PCL (90°) → PLC → ACL (extension).
- Two-stage: chronic cases / poor soft tissues / large tunnel overlap.
- Rehab: No supine flexion; prone bending only for first 6 weeks.
- Fasciotomy: if ischemia >6 hours.
