### Overview / Key Concepts
- A neuroradiology crash course for orthopaedic surgeons: reading a trauma head CT, intracranial bleeds and their look-alikes, cervical spine trauma, thoracolumbar fractures, epidural haematoma, disc herniation nomenclature and spinal tumours.
- CT is like an X-ray: bone is brightest, air and fluid darkest; fresh blood is hyperdense (bright).
- In every trauma film, look at the scalp, the bone (bone window), and for blood inside the skull.
- Spine: trace the four lines on the lateral view, check the C1–C2 and craniovertebral relations, and count the columns involved.

### Normal Head CT
- Axial: lateral ventricles drain through the third ventricle and the aqueduct of Sylvius into the fourth ventricle; sulcal (extra-axial) spaces, basal cisterns, brainstem, cerebellum.
- Basal ganglia (caudate, putamen), thalami and cerebellar dentate nuclei are the sites of HYPERTENSIVE bleeds, not traumatic ones.
- Coronal: tentorium cerebelli, interhemispheric falx. Sagittal: corpus callosum, midbrain, pons, medulla; craniovertebral junction (clivus, basion, dens, C1 ring, occipital condyles).
- Many windows (brain, soft tissue, bone); use the bone window for fractures.

- Large collections cause mass effect: midline shift, compressed lateral ventricle. "Extra-axial haemorrhage with mass effect and midline shift" is a safe description.
- Ageing blood: fresh blood is bright; after about 5–6 days it becomes isodense to brain, and older SDH shows layering (dense blood behind, less dense in front).
- SAH with a focal epicentre: get a CT angiogram (DSA rarely needed) to exclude an aneurysm, which must be secured before orthopaedic surgery.
- Do not confuse contrast in vessels on a post-contrast scan with SAH; check whether contrast was given.

Cervical Spine Trauma
Open-mouth (odontoid) view
- Overlapping occiput and teeth create false "fracture" lines at the base of the dens: trace each line beyond the bone; if it continues, it is artefact.
- Check the lateral masses of C1 sitting on C2: symmetry of the gaps, and whether the C1 lateral mass overhangs the C2 contour. Asymmetry or overhang: get a CT.

Lateral view: the four lines

| Line | Runs along |
|---|---|
| Anterior vertebral line | Anterior cortex of the bodies |
| Posterior vertebral line | Posterior cortex of the bodies |
| Spinolaminar line | Junction of laminae and spinous processes (posterior canal wall) |
| Posterior spinous line | Tips of the spinous processes |

- All four should be smooth and continuous; a step means disruption. Trauma straightening or reversal of the lordosis (spasm, collar) is common.
- Disc opening and widening between spinous processes = posterior ligamentous injury (MRI shows torn ligamentum flavum and interspinous ligaments).
- Columns: anterior = anterior two-thirds of the body; middle = posterior third of the body and disc; posterior = everything behind. One column stable; two columns starting to be unstable (evaluate further); three columns always unstable.

### Thoracolumbar Fractures
- Acute compression fracture: condensation of trabeculae (absent in old fractures).
- Simple compression (anterior wedge or central end-plate depression, often osteoporotic): posterior cortex intact.
- Burst fracture (axial load): through the anterior AND posterior cortex, linear or comminuted; risk of retropulsion into the canal; unstable. Always get MRI (epidural haematoma, cord). A burst without retropulsion can be stable.
- Intact ALL/PLL with a fracture: subligamentous haemorrhage; torn ligaments: blood spills into the epidural or prevertebral space.
- Look for associated injuries: basiocciput fractures, rib fractures.
- Ankylosing spondylitis: chalk-stick (carrot-stick) fractures through all three columns: unstable.

### Epidural Haematoma and Spinal AVM
- Posterior element fractures can bleed into the posterior epidural space, pushing the cord forward: needs urgent decompression.

<!-- page 5 -->

- Can also follow a minor twist in a patient on antiplatelets, without a fracture (dark on MRI because of deoxy/methaemoglobin; cauda equina pushed forward).
- On T2 the bright CSF outlines the stripped posterior dura.
- Punctate flow voids (dots) in an epidural or intramedullary bleed = spinal AVM (supplied, for example, via the artery of Adamkiewicz).

### Disc Herniation

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Bulge | Circumferential (more than 90° of the disc circumference); asymmetric bulge e.g. in scoliosis |
| Protrusion | Focal (less than 90°), base wider than the herniated part |
| Extrusion | Neck narrower than the herniated part; stays extradural and subligamentous (PLL lifted) |
| Sequestration | Fragment separated from the parent disc |

| Location | Root affected (lumbar) |
|---|---|
| Central | Whole cauda equina (canal stenosis) |
| Paracentral (subarticular) | Traversing root (L4 at L3–L4) |
| Foraminal / extraforaminal | Exiting root (L3 at L3–L4) |
| Anterior | Usually none |

- Cervical and thoracic roots exit at their own level; in the lumbar spine (conus at L1) one root exits and the next traverses at each disc.
- Disc = nucleus pulposus (jelly) inside the onion-like annulus fibrosus; annular tears let the nucleus herniate (e.g. sudden weightlifting); ageing causes desiccation and bulges.
- Correlate the level and side of compression with symptoms to choose a root block or surgery.

### Exam & Viva Pearls
- EDH lentiform with a fracture; SDH crescentic along the convexity; SAH feathery in sulci and cisterns.
- Focal SAH: CT angiogram for aneurysm before orthopaedic surgery.
- Central bleeds are hypertensive; peripheral heterogeneous bleeds with oedema suggest tumour or AVM.
- No MRI with a retained metallic foreign body.
- Four lines on the lateral cervical film; open-mouth artefacts at the dens base.
- Jefferson = C1 ring burst with lateral mass overhang; clay-shoveler = spinous process only.
- Burst fracture breaches the posterior cortex: MRI for retropulsion and haematoma.
- Protrusion vs extrusion: base wider vs neck narrower than the fragment. Paracentral disc hits the traversing root; foraminal hits the exiting root.
- Dural tail = meningioma; ice-cream cone = acoustic schwannoma.

### Take-Home Summary
- Read every trauma CT systematically: scalp, bone window, extra-axial blood, intra-axial lesions, oedema.
- In spine trauma, lines and columns decide stability; CT for bone, MRI for cord, ligaments and haematoma.
- Name discs and tumours precisely by shape and compartment.
