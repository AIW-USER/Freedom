Faculty: Dr. Shikha Dhaundiyal

OVERVIEW
Focus: Biomechanics and pathology of the thrower’s shoulder – understanding
glenohumeral joint mechanics, force couples, and adaptations seen in repetitive
overhead athletes.
Dr. Shikha’s Quote: “Every powerful throw is built on precision — stability,
not strength, protects the shoulder.”
Thrower’s Shoulder: A chronic overuse condition (CIOU – Chronic Injury of Overuse
Shoulder) characterized by instability, microtrauma, and impingement from repetitive
throwing motions.

SHOULDER ANATOMY & BIOMECHANICS
### Structure Overview
- Type: Diarthrodial ball-and-socket joint → 6 degrees of freedom.
- Joints involved:
  1. Glenohumeral (GH)
  2. Acromioclavicular (AC)
  3. Sternoclavicular (SC)
  4. Scapulothoracic (functional articulation)
### Degrees of Motion:
- Translation: Anteroposterior, Mediolateral, Superoinferior.
- Rotation: Axial rotation, Varus–Valgus, Flexion–Extension.
### Static & Dynamic Stabilizers:

| Static | Dynamic |
|---|---|
| Bony congruence | Rotator cuff (SITS) |
| Glenoid labrum | Scapulothoracic muscles |
| GH ligaments | Deltoid–RC force couple |
| Capsule & negative | Serratus–Trapezius synergy |

### BONY STABILITY
- Humeral head retroversion: ~30°
- Glenoid retroversion: ~7°
- Articular offset: ~2 mm
- Stability maintained through concavity-compression.
Concept: During abduction + external rotation → increased GH congruence, contact
area ↑, pressure ↓.
Purpose of Retroversion: Maintains humeral head centralization in the glenoid cavity
and aligns the arm during throwing.

MUSCULAR STABILITY – SCAPULOTHORACIC
### ARTICULATION
- Interface between scapula & posterior thoracic cage.
- Provides additional 60° elevation beyond 120° GH motion.
### Force Couples:

| Function | Muscles Involved |
|---|---|
| Scapular Rotation | Upper, Middle & Lower Trapezius + Serratus Anterior |
| Elevation/Depression | Deltoid + Rotator cuff (counterbalance) |

Force Couple Concept: - Deltoid pulls humeral head upward → RC provides
depressing counterforce. - Maintains centralization & prevents superior migration.
Upward rotation = trapezius + serratus; depression = deltoid countered by
RC.

### RESTING POSITION & SCAPULAR DYNAMICS

| Position | Normal Range |
|---|---|
| Internal rotation | 30–45° |
| Anterior tilt | 10–20° |
| Upward rotation | 20° |

If scapular mobility is compromised (fusion/malposition), internal rotation & daily
activities are restricted.

### THROWER’S SHOULDER – KEY EXAM FINDINGS
- Pain during overhead activity (deep/posterior ache).
- Medial border prominence → Scapular dyskinesis.
- Tests:
  - Scapular Assistance Test (SAT): ↓ pain with posterior tilt → dynamic cause.
  - Scapular Retraction Test (SRT): ↑ strength with scapular repositioning.
  - Pectoralis Minor Tightness: >3 cm asymmetry = abnormal.
Always assess scapular position before labeling as impingement.
### SCAPULOHUMERAL RHYTHM
- Ratio: GH : Scapulothoracic = 2:1.
- First 30° – pure GH movement.
- Beyond 30° → scapular rotation assists deltoid & maintains length–tension balance.
- 60° abduction → ratio shifts towards 1:1, reaching ~5:4 near 120°.
Clinical Insight: - Without scapular rotation, deltoid fatigue and active insufficiency
occur → abduction limited to 60–70°.
Scapular rhythm prevents deltoid overactivity and impingement.
### ROTATOR CUFF & FORCE COUPLES

| Muscle | Function | Contribution |
|---|---|---|
| Supraspinatus | Initiates abduction (0–15°) | Stabilizes superiorly |
| Infraspinatus | External rotation | 60% of ER force |
| Teres Minor | External rotation | 45% of ER force |
| Subscapularis | Internal rotation | Prevents anterior translation |

### Force Couples:
- Transverse (AP): Subscapularis ↔ Infraspinatus/Teres minor.
- Coronal (Vertical): Deltoid (upward) ↔ RC (downward depressors).
Clinical: - Tears in infraspinatus/teres minor → disrupt transverse couple → instability. -
Isolated supraspinatus tear → minimal instability.

ANTEROSUPERIOR ESCAPE
Mechanism: Loss of RC (esp. subscap/infra) → deltoid overpull → superior migration
of humeral head.
Radiographic Sign: Sourcil sign – sclerosis under acromion.
Consequences: - Coracoacromial impingement. - Anterosuperior translation of humeral
head.
“When cuff fails, deltoid dominates — and instability begins.”
### LIGAMENTOUS STABILITY

| Ligament | Function |
|---|---|
| SGHL | Resists inferior translation (adduction) + limits ER (with CHL). |
| MGHL | Provides anterior stability (0–45° abduction). |
| IGHL Complex | Anterior & posterior bands + axillary pouch; resists translation during abduction & ER. |
| CHL | Resists posterior/inferior translation; tightens in ER. |

### IGHL Bands:
- Anterior band: Stabilizes in abduction + ER (cocking phase).

- Posterior band: Engages in flexion + IR → posterior stability.

### GLENOID LABRUM & STABILITY
- Triangular fibrocartilaginous rim deepening the glenoid.
- Functions:
  1. Increases congruency & suction effect.
  2. Attachment for GH ligaments.
  3. Anti-shear bumper in midrange motion.
  4. Keeps humeral head centered.
Key Region: Tubercle of Assaki → thinnest cartilage area providing “concavity
compression.”
Concavity Compression: - Compression of humeral head into glenoid resists
translation & deltoid pull. - Labrum amplifies this via suction cup effect.

### THROWER’S SHOULDER PATHOPHYSIOLOGY

| Key Findings | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| Pain | Posterior shoulder ache in late cocking phase |
| Increased ER | Due to anterior capsule laxity |
| ↓ IR (GIRD) | From posterior capsule tightness |
| Anterior microinstability | Repetitive microtrauma → pseudo-laxity |
| Posterior capsule fibrosis | Shifts humeral head posterosuperiorly |
| Type II SLAP | From peel-back mechanism |

Internal Impingement: - Greater tuberosity abuts posterosuperior glenoid → RC
entrapment. - Repeated cocking → anterior capsule stretched, posterior tight →
microinstability.

GIRD – Glenohumeral Internal Rotation Deficit
Definition: Loss of ≥20° internal rotation compared to contralateral side.
Physiologic GIRD: Total rotational motion (TRM) same bilaterally.
Pathologic GIRD: TRM loss >5°.

Management:
- Posteroinferior capsule stretching (NOT global stretches):
  - Sleeper Stretch (90° flexed, scapula stabilized)
  - Cross-body Adduction Stretch
  - Doorway Stretch (90° abduction, lean forward)
  - Roll-over Stretch (50–60° flexion)
Protocol: 5 reps × 30 sec daily × 6 weeks.
“Don’t stretch everything — focus on what’s tight, not what’s weak.”
### INTERNAL IMPINGEMENT MECHANISM
- GT–Glenoid Contact: GT abuts posterosuperior glenoid → RC entrapment.
- IGHL Role: Posterior band contraction → shifts contact point posterosuperiorly.
- Result: Posterior–superior translation → tuberosity clearance → more ER.

<!-- page 6 -->

Cam Effect: - Posterior capsule tightens → humeral head migrates posterosuperiorly. -
Reduces anterior capsule tension → functional pseudo-laxity. - Increases ER but
predisposes to instability.
Concavity Compression & Cam Effect Combined: - Maintain centralization of
humeral head. - Excessive shift → instability + impingement.

### PEEL-BACK MECHANISM
- In hyper-ER (late cocking) → torsional stress on biceps anchor.
- Posterior vector shift → labrum rotates medially → SLAP lesion (Type II common).
Summary: | Mechanism | Effect | |————|——–| | Repetitive cocking | Anterior
capsule laxity (pseudo-laxity) | | Posterior capsule contracture | Posterosuperior shift of
humeral head | | Hyper-ER | Tuberosity clearance, ↑ arc of motion | | Peel-back | SLAP
lesion from biceps anchor torsion |
“More ER may win the throw — but it costs the shoulder.”
### TAKE-HOME SUMMARY
- Thrower’s Shoulder = Overuse + Microinstability + Adaptive GIRD.
- Key lesions: Anterior laxity + Posterior fibrosis + Type II SLAP.
- Biomechanical Triad: Concavity compression, Cam effect, Peel-back mechanism.
- Treatment: Posterior capsule stretch, scapular rehab, kinetic chain correction. “Stability before speed — protect the shoulder to preserve the throw.”
