Biopsy in Bone and Soft Tissue Tumors – Tips and
Tricks – Dr. Rajat Gupta
Overview
Biopsy is the first definitive step in the management of bone and soft tissue tumors. A
well planned biopsy preserves oncologic planes, avoids contamination, and ensures an
accurate diagnosis. A poorly done biopsy may compromise limb salvage and may even
lead to amputation.
Faculty Remark: “A bad biopsy can destroy a good limb salvage. Do it right the first
time.”

## Why Biopsy?
### Core Purposes
- Establish histopathological diagnosis.
- Plan appropriate treatment strategy.
- Prognosticate the patient.
### Dangers of Skipping Biopsy
- Misdiagnosis leading to wrong surgery.
- Contamination of compartments.
- Delayed chemotherapy which worsens prognosis.
Examples
- Clear cell chondrosarcoma misdiagnosed as ABC, leading to curettage and eventual amputation turn32file0.
- Osteosarcoma mistaken for Brodie abscess after debridement with large contaminated scar requiring wide resection turn32file0.

## When to Biopsy?
- After complete imaging (X ray, MRI, CT/PET). Imaging helps decide:
  - Ideal biopsy site.
  - Solid vs cystic component.
  - Soft tissue extension.
- Campbell: biopsy after non invasive investigations.

Identify “Touch Me Not” Lesions
Biopsy usually not needed when classic radiology is present: - Non ossifying fibroma.
- Fibrous dysplasia (ground glass appearance). - Osteochondroma. - Enchondroma.
- Calcaneal lipoma (cockade sign). - Bone infarcts.
If atypical features exist, biopsy may still be required.

## Principles of Biopsy
1. Should be done by the surgeon performing the definitive surgery
- Prevents wrong incision placement.
- Avoids contamination of compartments that will not be resected.
- Reduces morbidity and recurrence risk.
Mankin study: Complications, diagnostic errors, and changes in outcome were 2–12
times higher when biopsy was done outside a tumor center turn32file1.

2. Plan the Biopsy Tract Carefully
- Must lie in line with the planned surgical incision.
- Should pass through only one compartment.
- Should avoid neurovascular bundles, joint capsules, and uninvolved muscles.
- Avoid arthroscopic biopsy (contaminates both compartments) turn32file1.
Examples (Slides)
- Page 1: MRI image showing dangerous trajectories near neurovascular bundle.
- Page 2–3: Single point of entry emphasized.
- Page 4: Avoid raising flaps, maintain hemostasis.
- Page 5: Drain site should be in line with the incision.

3. Shortest Route Through One Compartment Only
- Proximal humerus: go through anterior deltoid, avoid deltopectoral groove.
- Distal femur: go dead medial or dead lateral, close to septum.
- Tibia: prefer anteromedial unless soft tissue dictates otherwise.
(Visual examples on pages 67–71 of PPT.)

4. Do Not Divide the Specimen
- Tumors are heterogeneous.
- Dividing tissue leads to conflicting reports.
- Always send entire sample to the same musculoskeletal pathologist.
(Pages 8–12 show cartoon and pathological report illustrating errors.)

5. Hemostasis & Contamination Control
- Do not raise flaps.
- Avoid drains unless absolutely needed.
- If using a tourniquet, do not exsanguinate.
- Minimize hematoma formation.

## How to Biopsy – Techniques
### Solid Lesions
- Insert J needle into solid component.
- Rotate gently to scrape tissue.
- Apply suction during extraction.
- Immediate pressure over tract to reduce hematoma.
(Example: 10 year old osteosarcoma case, pages 21–23 turn32file1.)

Cystic Lesions (e.g., ABC)
Challenges: aspiration yields only blood clots.

Steps
1. Heparinize the J needle.
2. Enter cystic cavity under C arm.
3. Aspirate fluid into heparinized syringe.
4. Deposit into heparinized bowl.
5. Scrape cyst walls to collect lining.
6. Sieve contents over mop to isolate solid tissue.
Outcome: representative tissue for diagnosis (page 27–28).

### Lesions With Soft Tissue Component
- Use biopsy gun.
- Insert through thin cortex or directly through soft tissue.
- Fire gun after reaching target zone.
- Apply pressure to avoid hematoma.
(Page 29–31 shows gun technique.)
“Gun biopsy is the most atraumatic method when soft tissue component is present.”

## Avoiding Pitfalls
- Never biopsy the necrotic center; always target solid, enhancing areas.
- Never create multiple entry points.
- Never biopsy through contaminated scars.
- Never pass through joints.
- Never assume diagnosis based solely on MRI report.

## Complications of Poorly Done Biopsy
- Increased surgical morbidity.
- Need for wider excision or amputation.
- Local recurrence due to contamination.
- Need for flaps and grafts.
- Delay in chemotherapy.
(Page 60–64 illustrate consequences with photographs.)

### Exam Pearls
- Biopsy should always follow imaging.
- One compartment, one incision, one path.
- Needle biopsy is as accurate as open biopsy with fewer complications.
- Never divide the specimen.
- Avoid neurovascular structures and joints.
- Always target solid component of heterogeneous tumors.
- Gun biopsy is ideal for soft tissue rich lesions.
- Avoid arthroscopic biopsy.
- Proper biopsy planning determines whether limb salvage is possible.
