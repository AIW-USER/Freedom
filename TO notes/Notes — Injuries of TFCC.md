Faculty: Dr. Amit Mittal

### OVERVIEW
- TFCC (Triangular Fibrocartilage Complex) is the primary stabilizer of the ulnar wrist and distal radioulnar joint (DRUJ).
- Acts as a load distributor between ulna and carpus and maintains ulnocarpal & DRUJ stability.
- Common cause of ulnar-sided wrist pain. “When the radial side talks about movement, the ulnar side whispers about balance.” – Dr. Amit Mittal

FUNCTIONAL ANATOMY
### Functions of TFCC
1. Distributes axial load from carpus to ulna (~20% load in neutral).
2. Stabilizes DRUJ during pronation–supination.
3. Supports ulnocarpal articulation.

### KINEMATICS & BIOMECHANICS
- During pronation and supination, the palmar & dorsal RULs act reciprocally:
  - In supination → palmar RUL tight, dorsal relaxed.
  - In pronation → dorsal RUL tight, palmar relaxed.
- UL & UT ligaments also demonstrate reciprocal tightening during radio-ulnar deviation.
- Load sharing:
  - 80% through radius.
  - 20% through ulna (increases with positive ulnar variance).
Injury predisposition: TFCC tears occur in pronation + ulnar deviation
(rotational load).

### Types of Prestyloid Recess
1. Narrow–long connection to ulnocarpal space.
2. Wide–short connection.
3. No opening to ulnocarpal space but connects to DRUJ. Clinical note: Variations influence arthroscopic portal approach.

### ECU SHEATH & CAPSULAR RELATION
- Origin: Dorsal triquetrum.
- Insertion: Dorsal & ulnar styloid surface.
- Function:
  - Prevents dorsal dislocation of ulna head in pronation (sheath taut).
  - Relaxes in supination, may impinge → ulnar styloid impaction.
ECU sheath functions as dynamic stabilizer and part of functional ulnar
collateral ligament.

### 2⃣ Foveal Sign
- Tenderness at fovea (ulnar depression between styloid & FCU tendon).
  - Positive: TFCC foveal avulsion (DRUJ unstable).
  - Negative: UT ligament tear (DRUJ stable).

### 3⃣ Dimple Sign (DRUJ Instability)
- With hand flat on table, dorsal ulna head shifts palmarly → dorsal dimple seen.
- Suggests multidirectional DRUJ instability.

### DIFFERENTIAL DIAGNOSIS – ULNAR-SIDED WRIST PAIN
1. TFCC tear (traumatic or degenerative).
2. Ulnocarpal impaction syndrome.
3. ECU tendinitis / tenosynovitis.
4. Hook of hamate fracture / nonunion.
5. Lunotriquetral (LT) instability.
6. Ulnar styloid impaction syndrome.
7. TILT syndrome (Triquetral impingement–ligament tear).
8. DRUJ osteoarthritis. Mnemonic: “The 8 Ulnar Pains” – TFCC, Impaction, ECU, Hamate, LT, Styloid, TILT, DRUJ OA.

| Stage | Description |
|---|---|

Central perforations (1A) → poor healing (avascular).
Peripheral tears (1B/1C) → vascular → repairable.

MANAGEMENT PRINCIPLES
### 1⃣ Conservative (First Line)
- Immobilization (above-elbow cast, 4–6 weeks).
- NSAIDs, activity modification.
- Injections: corticosteroid or PRP.
- Physiotherapy – proprioceptive and grip strengthening.

### EXAM & VIVA PEARLS
- TFCC components: TFC, MH, RUL, UL, UT, ECU sheath, capsule.
- Key stabilizer of DRUJ: Radioulnar ligament (RUL).
- Load distribution: 80% radius, 20% ulna.
- Tight in supination: Palmar RUL; Tight in pronation: Dorsal RUL.
- Meniscus homologue: Highly vascular, elastic tissue near ulnar styloid.
- Foveal tenderness: Diagnostic for TFCC avulsion (DRUJ unstable).
- Palmer classification: 1A–1D (traumatic), 2A–2E (degenerative).
- Avascular zone: Central portion → poor healing → debridement.
- Peripheral tear: Repairable.
- Arthroscopy portals: 3–4 (viewing), 6R (working), 6U (ulnar recess).
- Surgery choice: Repair for peripheral, debridement for central.
- ECU sheath: Dynamic stabilizer, prevents dorsal ulna translation.
- Post-op immobilization: 4–6 weeks.
