Spine Surgery
Faculty: Dr. C.S. Vishnu Prasath

OVERVIEW
Definition:
Endoscopic spine surgery is a minimally invasive technique performed under
continuous endoscopic visualization and irrigation for the treatment of spinal canal and
paraspinal pathologies.
Unlike microscopic or tubular-assisted procedures, full-endoscopic surgery is performed
entirely through a uniportal endoscope with dedicated working and irrigation channels.
### Key Approaches:
1. Full-Endoscopic Interlaminar Approach
2. Full-Endoscopic Transforaminal/Extraforaminal Approach
3. Unilateral Biportal Endoscopy (UBE) – an endoscopic-assisted technique using two portals.

### HISTORICAL BACKGROUND
- Early 20th century: Access to the spinal canal via interlaminar laminectomy described.

- 1940s: Posterolateral approach introduced for vertebral biopsies.

- 1986: Kambin & Sampson performed the first posterolateral percutaneous nucleotomy.

- 1989: Hijikata described a similar approach in Japan.

- Modern era: Advancements in optics, irrigation, and specialized instruments have made endoscopic spine surgery effective and reproducible.
Pioneers:
- Dr. Parviz Kambin described the Kambin’s Triangle — a safe anatomical zone for
transforaminal access.

INDICATIONS
General Indications:
- Sequestered or non-sequestered lumbar disc herniation.
- Recurrent disc herniation after previous surgery.
- Lateral recess or foraminal stenosis.
- Central canal stenosis.
- Facet cysts and zygapophyseal joint cysts.
- Infective conditions (spondylodiscitis / epidural abscess) – for debridement and
drainage.
- Minimally invasive fusion (Endo-fusion) with cage placement under endoscopic vision.

### Approach-Specific Indications

| Approach | Indications |
|---|---|
| Interlaminar | Central & paracentral herniations (esp. L5–S1), cysts within canal, central stenosis |
| Transforaminal | Foraminal/extraforaminal herniations, recurrent discs, facet cysts, infective discitis, high-risk patients (done under sedation) |

### ADVANTAGES
- Excellent illumination and visualization (25° endoscope).

<!-- page 3 -->

- Muscle and bone preservation → less postoperative pain.

- Early mobilization and short hospital stay.

- Minimal blood loss and lower risk of infection.

- Reduced epidural scarring → easier revision surgery.

- Can be performed under local anesthesia in selected cases.

- Cost-effective with rapid return to work.

### DISADVANTAGES
- Steep learning curve.

- Limited maneuverability within the spinal canal.

- Difficult in migrated or dorsal fragments.

- Dural repair not feasible endoscopically.

- Limited ability to convert in case of major complications.

- Expensive instrumentation (for beginners).

ANATOMICAL CONCEPT – KAMBIN’S TRIANGLE
Defined by Dr. Parviz Kambin (1991):
A triangular safe zone for transforaminal access to the disc.

| Boundary | Structure |
|---|---|
| Anterior | Exiting nerve root |
| Inferior | Superior endplate of the lower vertebra |
| Posterior | Superior articular process of inferior vertebra |
| Medial Limit | Traversing nerve root and dural sac |

Key Point:
Entry should remain within the triangle to avoid injury to the exiting or traversing roots.

### TRANSFORAMINAL ENDOSCOPIC TECHNIQUES
1. Inside–Out Technique
- Endoscope introduced into the disc space first.

- Disc material removed retrograde through the annular defect.

- Limitation: Nerve root visualization limited; possible residual fragments.
2. Outside–In Technique (Preferred Today)
- Endoscope introduced through the foramen.

- Foramenoplasty done using a burr if narrow.

- Direct visualization of nerve root and annular defect.

- Ensures confirmed decompression of the neural elements.

SURGICAL TECHNIQUE (TRANSFORAMINAL
ENDOSCOPY)
### Preoperative Setup
- Position: Prone with slight hip flexion (widens foramina).

- Anesthesia: General or local (with sedation).

- Imaging: Continuous fluoroscopic guidance (AP and lateral).

- Equipment:
  - 18G spinal needle, guidewire, sequential dilators.

  - Rigid 25° wide-angle endoscope with dual irrigation channels.

### Entry Landmarks

| Level | Lateral Entry from Midline |
|---|---|
| L1–L2 | 8 cm |
| L3–L4 | 10 cm |
| L4–L5 / L5–S1 | 12 cm |

### Key Steps:
1. Mark level under true AP fluoroscopy.
2. Insert spinal needle toward posterior superior corner of lower vertebra.

<!-- page 5 -->

3. Stop at medial pedicular line (avoid dural injury).
4. Introduce guidewire → dilator → working cannula.
5. Introduce endoscope; visualize disc and neural structures.
6. Remove fragment under direct vision.
7. Confirm nerve root pulsation = adequate decompression.

INTERLAMINAR ENDOSCOPIC SURGERY
Indications
- Large central/paracentral disc at L5–S1.

- Central stenosis or ligamentum flavum hypertrophy.

- Cases where iliac crest obstructs transforaminal route.
### Key Steps
1. Entry at spino-laminar junction of the caudal lamina.

2. Dilate soft tissues and insert cannula into interlaminar window.

3. Remove ligamentum flavum (flavectomy).

4. Identify dura and traversing root.

5. Remove extruded fragment.

6. Confirm free root and dural pulsations.
Over-the-Top Decompression:
Possible to decompress contralateral side via single interlaminar portal.

UNILATERAL BIPORTAL ENDOSCOPY (UBE)
Principle
Two independent portals – one for endoscope, one for working instruments.
Essentially a biportal endoscopic-assisted technique.

Advantages
- Greater range of motion.

- Better visualization and irrigation control.

<!-- page 6 -->

- Familiar instrumentation (arthroscopy-based).

- Less radiation exposure.

- Lower cost compared to full endoscopy.
Disadvantages
- Long learning curve.

- Requires bimanual coordination and specialized training.

### COMPLICATIONS
- Dural tear (repair difficult endoscopically).

- Residual disc fragments → persistent pain.

- Recurrent disc herniation.

- Epidural hematoma → postoperative radiculopathy.

- Infection / postoperative discitis (due to inadequate sterilization).

- Fluid overload–related complications in long cases.

### EXAM & VIVA PEARLS
- Pioneers: Kambin & Sampson (1986), Hijikata (1989).

- Safe Zone: Kambin’s triangle.

- Inside-Out vs Outside-In: Outside-In ensures better decompression.

- Transforaminal vs Interlaminar:
  - Transforaminal – for foraminal/far-lateral discs.

  - Interlaminar – for central/paracentral (esp. L5–S1).

- Endo-fusion: Cage delivery through endoscopic portal.

- UBE: Two portals; easier learning curve; arthroscopy instruments used.

<!-- page 7 -->

- Contraindications: Dorsal or high-migrated fragments, severe stenosis, intradural pathology.

### TAKE-HOME SUMMARY
- Endoscopic spine surgery = true minimally invasive option for lumbar disc and stenosis.

- Transforaminal approach uses Kambin’s triangle for safe access.

- Interlaminar approach preferred for L5–S1 and central pathology.

- UBE bridges open and endoscopic techniques – cheaper, easier, versatile.

- Success depends on case selection and surgeon expertise, not the device.
