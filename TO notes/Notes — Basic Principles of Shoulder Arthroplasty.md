OVERVIEW
Shoulder Arthroplasty refers to the surgical replacement of the shoulder joint to
relieve pain and restore mobility in conditions such as arthritis, osteonecrosis, or
complex proximal humerus fractures. It includes Hemiarthroplasty, Total Shoulder
Arthroplasty (TSA), and Reverse Shoulder Arthroplasty (RSA).
Quote: “A functional cuff and a well-preserved glenoid are the foundation of
successful shoulder replacement.” – Dr. Anurag Tiwari

SHOULDER JOINT ANATOMY & BIOMECHANICS
### Glenohumeral Joint
- Ball-and-socket synovial joint (most mobile joint of body).
- Glenoid = shallow, Humeral head = large → mobility > stability.
- Stability maintained by static & dynamic stabilizers.
### Static Stabilizers
- Glenoid Labrum → increases glenoid depth by 50%.
- Glenohumeral ligaments (Superior, Middle, Inferior).
- Negative intra-articular pressure.
### Dynamic Stabilizers
- Rotator cuff muscles (SITS): Supraspinatus, Infraspinatus, Teres minor, Subscapularis.
- Long head of Biceps – anterior dynamic stabilizer. Concept: Contraction–Compression Model – coordinated cuff contraction compresses humeral head into glenoid → stabilizes during motion.

### IMAGING & WORK-UP
- X-Ray:
  - Goat’s Beard Sign → Inferior osteophyte in OA shoulder.
  - Sourcil Sign → Acromial sclerosis in cuff tear arthropathy.
  - Multiple loose bodies → Post-capsulorrhaphy arthropathy.
  - Posterior subluxation → Posterior glenoid wear.
- CT Scan: Evaluates Glenoid bone stock and version (2° anteversion to 7° retroversion normal).
- MRI: Assesses rotator cuff integrity and fatty degeneration. CT & MRI guide decision: Total vs. Hemi vs. Reverse Shoulder Arthroplasty.

SURGICAL PRINCIPLES
### Approach & Exposure
- Approach: Deltopectoral (identify & protect cephalic vein).
- Exposure: Retract Deltoid & Pectoralis Major → identify Subscapularis.

<!-- page 4 -->

- Subscapularis Release: 2 cm from lesser tuberosity; Z-lengthening if contracted.
- Dislocation: Achieved in ABER (Abduction & External Rotation).
- Preserve: Coracohumeral ligament if cuff deficient (adds stability).

### Humeral Head Osteotomy
- Inclination: 135° to humeral shaft.
- Retroversion: 30° external rotation of arm → cut anterior–posterior.
- Level: Just proximal to rotator cuff insertion.
- Entry Point: Posterior to bicipital groove, 1 cm medial to GT tip.
- Fixation: Cemented or uncemented (proximal-coated stems preferred).

### Glenoid Preparation & IMPLANTS

| Type | Description | Key Points |
|---|---|---|
| All-Poly | Cemented | Peg type preferred in poor bone |
| (Peg/Keeled) | polyethylene glenoid | stock; Keeled in good bone |
| Metal-Backe d | Porous coated, uncemented | Higher loosening rates; less used |
| Hybrid | Central metal peg + poly pegs | Combines stability & biological fixation |

Posterior Glenoid Wear: Ream excess anterior glenoid or bone graft
posterior aspect. Medial Wear: Options → Hemiarthroplasty, soft-tissue
interposition (Fascia Lata / Meniscus), or Ream-and-Run arthroplasty.

### Persistent Posterior Subluxation – Management Options
1. Use a larger humeral head.
2. Reduce humeral retroversion.
3. Tighten posterior capsule.
4. Immobilize in external rotation or delay ROM exercises.

### EXAM PEARLS & TAKEAWAYS
- TSA requires intact, functional rotator cuff & adequate glenoid bone stock.
- Hemiarthroplasty preferred for AVN or poor glenoid.
- RSA preferred for cuff tear arthropathy or non-functional cuff.
- Key radiological signs: Goat’s Beard, Sourcil Sign, Posterior Subluxation, Loose Bodies.
- Inclination 135°, Retroversion 30°.
- Rocking Horse Effect → Most common cause of glenoid loosening.
- Posterior glenoid wear → Most typical in OA shoulder.
