Target Ortho Quick Revision Notes – Spinal Injection
Techniques in Back Pain
Faculty: Dr. C.S. Vishnu Prasath

OVERVIEW
Purpose:
Spinal injections are used both for diagnosis and treatment of back and radicular pain.
They help bridge the gap between conservative management and surgery, offering
symptom relief and buying time for natural healing.
Common Indications:
- Lumbar/cervical disc prolapse with acute radicular pain
- Lumbar canal stenosis (mild/moderate)
- Facet joint or sacroiliac (SI) joint–mediated pain
- Post-laminectomy syndrome
- Diagnostic localization of pain generators

### PATHOPHYSIOLOGY OF RADICULAR PAIN
- Mechanical compression is not the main cause.

- Inflammatory mediators (esp. TNF-α) from nucleus pulposus trigger nerve root irritation → pain.

- Steroids act by reducing inflammation, washing out cytokines, and decreasing central sensitization.

SELECTIVE NERVE ROOT BLOCK (SNRB)
Indications
- Acute radicular pain (<6 weeks) without major deficit.

- Failure of conservative therapy.

- To identify symptomatic level before surgery.
Technique
1. Position: Prone on radiolucent table.

2. C-arm positioning:
  - Oblique (30–40°) → “Scottie Dog” view.

  - Aim needle at the neck of the dog = foramen region.

3. Confirm position with Iohexol dye – reproduces patient’s radicular pain.

4. Inject: 1 mL 0.5% bupivacaine + 1 mL (40–80 mg) triamcinolone.

<!-- page 3 -->

5. Observe: Pain relief within minutes; lasts 2–3 weeks.

Notes
- Acts as a “ring block” for the nerve root (like numbing a scorpion sting).

- Buys time for disc resorption and natural recovery.

- Can be repeated after 2–3 weeks if pain recurs.

EPIDURAL STEROID INJECTIONS (ESI)
Types

| Approach | Site | Comments |
|---|---|---|
| Caudal | Through sacral hiatus | Easy, safe; covers L5–S1 roots |
| Interlaminar | Between | Given by anesthetist; wide |
| (Translaminar) | spinous processes | spread |
| Transforaminal | Through neural foramen | Targeted delivery; for unilateral radiculopathy |

Mechanism
- Anti-inflammatory effect of steroids.

- Washout of inflammatory mediators.

- Breaks pain–spasm–pain cycle.

- Provides temporary neural desensitization.
Drugs
- Bupivacaine 0.5% (3 mL) + Triamcinolone 80 mg (2 mL)

- Alternatives: Methylprednisolone, Betamethasone, Dexamethasone.
Indications
- Lumbar/cervical radiculopathy.

- Post-laminectomy syndrome.

- Mild/moderate lumbar canal stenosis.

<!-- page 4 -->

- Avoid in severe stenosis or local infection.
Contraindications
- Coagulopathy, sepsis, pregnancy (if fluoroscopy used), severe stenosis.
Repeat interval: Minimum 12 weeks between injections.
Effectiveness: Good short-term relief; long-term benefit uncertain.

FACET JOINT INJECTIONS & MEDIAL BRANCH BLOCKS
### Anatomy & Pain Mechanism
- Facet joint: True synovial joint formed by IAP of upper vertebra and SAP of lower vertebra.

- Innervated by medial branch of dorsal ramus (each joint supplied by two levels).

- Pain from degeneration → local tenderness, worse on extension/rotation.
Indications
- Localized low back pain with normal imaging.

- Post-laminectomy pain without recurrent disc disease.

- Facet arthropathy on MRI/CT.
Technique
1. Identify joint using fluoroscopic oblique (Scottie Dog) view.

2. Inject: 2 mL 0.5% bupivacaine + 2 mL steroid.

3. If unable to enter joint: Periarticular injection.

4. For chronic relief:
  - Diagnostic: Local anesthetic alone.

  - Therapeutic: Local + Steroid.

  - Long-term: Medial Branch Radiofrequency Ablation (RFA).

### Target for MBB:
- Junction of superior articular process (SAP) and transverse process (TP).

<!-- page 5 -->

- To block one facet → block medial branch at the level and level above.

SACROILIAC JOINT (SIJ) INJECTIONS
Anatomy
- Upper 2/3 – fibrocartilaginous; lower 1/3 – true synovial joint.

- Innervation: Dorsal rami of L5–S3.
Indications
- Mechanical dysfunction (post-fusion, trauma).

- Inflammatory sacroiliitis (e.g., ankylosing spondylitis).

- Degenerative or postural pain.
### Clinical Tests
- FABER’s test and Gaenslen’s test positive.

- Pain radiates to gluteal region, not below knee.
Technique
1. Position: Prone.

2. C-arm: 20° caudal + oblique tilt.

3. Needle: Into lower 1/3 of joint (true joint).

4. Injection: 2 mL bupivacaine + 2 mL triamcinolone.

5. If joint inaccessible: Periarticular injection.

DISCOGRAPHY (Provocative Disc Testing)
Purpose
- Identifies pain-generating discs before fusion or TDR.
Indications
- Chronic discogenic back pain with ambiguous MRI.

- Preoperative planning for fusion or disc replacement.

<!-- page 6 -->

Technique
- Two-needle system (21G outer, 26G inner).

- Contrast: Iohexol (non-ionic).

- Position: Modified lateral decubitus (symptomatic side down).

- Positive test: Concordant pain (reproduction of typical pain).
Complications
- Discitis, nerve root injury, chemical meningitis, bleeding, allergic reaction.
Note: Rarely performed today due to poor outcome correlation.

VERTEBROPLASTY & KYPHOPLASTY
Indication
- Painful osteoporotic compression fractures unresponsive to conservative care.
Principles

| Parameter | Vertebroplasty | Kyphoplasty |
|---|---|---|
| Cement Type | PMMA (viscous, radiopaque) | Same |
| Pressure | High | Low |
| Balloon Use | None | Balloon used to create cavity |

Deformity Correction Minimal Significant
Leak Risk Higher Lower

### Key Points
- Start needle at 3 o’clock (right) or 9 o’clock (left pedicle) under C-arm.

- Confirm with AP + lateral views.

- Inject cement when it becomes semi-viscous (not fluid).

- Stop when cement nears posterior vertebral border to prevent leakage.

### EXAM & VIVA PEARLS
- SNRB: Targeted root block — for radicular pain.

<!-- page 7 -->

- Epidural steroid: Diffuse inflammation — for bilateral or vague pain.

- Facet injections: Localized low back pain.

- SI joint block: Pain radiating to gluteal area, not below knee.

- Discography: Diagnostic only; rarely used now.

- Vertebroplasty vs Kyphoplasty: Balloon-assisted cavity in kyphoplasty.

- SNRB vs ESI: SNRB is targeted; ESI is diffuse.

### TAKE-HOME SUMMARY
- Inflammatory pain drives radiculopathy more than compression.

- SNRB gives quick relief and buys time for disc resorption.

- Epidural steroids: Useful short-term; no long-term cure.

- Facet, SIJ, and disc injections are diagnostic and therapeutic tools.

- Vertebroplasty/Kyphoplasty restore stability in osteoporotic fractures.

- Each technique demands accurate image guidance and aseptic precision for safety and efficacy.
