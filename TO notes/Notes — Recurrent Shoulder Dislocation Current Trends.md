Target Ortho Quick Revision Notes – Recurrent Shoulder Dislocation: Current Trends
Faculty: Dr. Daivik T. Shetty
Category: Shoulder Instability – Contemporary Management

OVERVIEW
“I first understood the true meaning of instability when I watched a camel kneel
under the pyramids of Egypt — flexible yet balanced. The shoulder, however,
often loses that balance.” – Dr. Daivik T. Shetty
- Recurrent Shoulder Dislocation (RSD) occurs when the shoulder repeatedly dislocates after an initial traumatic episode.
- Commonly affects young, active males and contact-sport athletes.
- Shoulder sacrifices stability for mobility — humeral head is nearly four times larger than the glenoid cavity.
- Instability may be:
  - Traumatic (Torn loose) – labrum or capsular failure after injury.
  - Atraumatic (Born loose) – congenital or hyperlax.
  - Repetitive microtrauma (Worn loose) – overhead sports, throwers.

ANATOMY & STABILIZERS
### Static Stabilizers
- Glenoid concavity & labrum.
- Glenohumeral ligaments (SGHL, MGHL, IGHL).
- Capsule & negative intra-articular pressure.

### Dynamic Stabilizers
- Rotator cuff muscles.
- Scapular stabilizers.
- Deltoid & long head of biceps.

“X-Factors”
- Volition (conscious control).
- Proprioception (feedback & reflex stabilization).

### WHY RECURRENCE OCCURS
- After first dislocation, soft-tissue restraints fail to heal anatomically.
- Leads to chronic capsulolabral incompetence and bony attrition.
- Risk factors:
  - Age <30 yrs.
  - Overhead or contact sports.
  - Ligamentous laxity.
  - Inadequate immobilization or early return to sport.
Anterior instability is far more common than posterior or multidirectional forms.

BONE LOSS & THE BIPOLAR CONCEPT
Recurrent dislocation produces bony attrition on both sides:
- Glenoid bone loss → reduces socket depth.
- Hill–Sachs lesion → impaction defect on humeral head. “Bipolar bone loss” = lesions on both glenoid & humeral sides.
Assessment (CT preferred)
- 3D CT with en-face glenoid reconstruction.
- Sugaya and PICO methods used for quantification.
- Critical bone loss: >21% of glenoid width → soft-tissue repair fails.

### Glenoid Track Concept (Itoi & Yamamoto)
- Glenoid track ≈ 83% of glenoid width.
- GT = 0.83D – d, HSI = HS + BB. | Relation | Interpretation | |———–|—————-| | HSI < GT | On-track (stable) | | HSI > GT | Off-track (engaging) | Rule: On-track = safe; Off-track = needs bony procedure.

### MANAGEMENT ALGORITHM

| Glenoid Loss | Hill–Sachs | Procedure |
|---|---|---|
| <10% | Non-engaging | Bankart Repair |
| <10% | Engaging | Bankart + Remplissage |
| 10–25% | Non-engaging | Bankart ± Capsular Shift |
| 10–25% | Engaging | Bankart + Remplissage |
| 25–40% | Any | Latarjet |
| >40% | Any | ICBG / Allograft Reconstruction |

ISIS >6 → Latarjet required.

KEY SURGICAL PROCEDURES
### 1⃣ Bankart Repair (Open / Arthroscopic)
- Indicated for <20% glenoid loss.
- Single anterior portal arthroscopy gaining favor:
  - Less surgical time and morbidity.
  - Comparable stability outcomes.

### 2⃣ Latarjet Procedure
- For >20–25% glenoid bone loss or off-track lesions.
- Combines bony extension, dynamic sling, and capsular reinforcement.
### Triple Effect (Patte):
1. Glenoid reconstruction (bone block)
2. Dynamic sling (conjoint tendon)
3. Capsular reinforcement Key tip: Overmedialization → persistent instability; lateral overhang → arthritis risk.

### 3⃣ Remplissage (Wolf, 2007)
- Posterior capsulotenodesis of infraspinatus into Hill–Sachs defect.
- Converts intra-articular lesion → extra-articular, eliminating engagement.

MODERN TRENDS
Single Anterior Portal
- → simplicity and minimal iatrogenic injury.
- All-suture soft anchors → preserve bone stock, reduce artifact.
- Knotless anchor systems → eliminate suture abrasion, consistent fixation.
- Biocomposite anchors → promote bone integration. Shift: From purely mechanical repair → biologic & precision-based arthroscopy.

### ♂ REHABILITATION
- Immobilization: HIGHLIGHTS 2–3 weeks in sling.
- Early Passive ROM: Begin at 3 weeks (avoid ER stress initially).
- Active Strengthening: 6–8 weeks post-op.
- Return to sport: 4–6 months depending on procedure (Latarjet later). Overaggressive rehab → early recurrence; delayed motion → stiffness.

EXAM & VIVA PEARLS
### Anatomy & Biomechanics
- Mid-range stabilizer → MGHL.
- ABER stabilizer → IGHL.
- Buford complex → normal variant.

Lesions
- ALPSA = displaced; Perthes = nondisplaced.

### Bone Loss & Surgery
- Critical glenoid defect = >21%.
- GT = 0.83D – d; HSI = HS + BB.
- HSI > GT = Off-track → needs bone augmentation.
- ISIS >6 → Latarjet.
- Triple effect: Bone + Sling + Capsule.
- Remplissage = infraspinatus tenodesis.
- ICBG = for >40% loss.

### Trends & Innovations
- Single portal Bankart repair.
- Knotless, all-suture anchors.
- Integration of CT-based pre-op planning.
