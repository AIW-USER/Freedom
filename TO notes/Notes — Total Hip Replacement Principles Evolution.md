OVERVIEW
This lecture outlines the principles and evolution of total hip replacement (THR),
tracing its development from excision and interpositional arthroplasty to the modern-day
modular, low-friction, cemented and cementless systems. It emphasizes
biomechanical principles, fixation methods, material choices, modularity, and
bearing surface evolution—core areas for DNB/FNB Arthroplasty exam prep.

### ARTHROPLASTY – DEFINITION & TYPES
- Arthroplasty = Resurfacing of a joint (not replacement).
- Types:
  - Excision arthroplasty – Removal of articular surfaces (fibrous interposition).
  - Interposition arthroplasty – Placement of graft (fascia lata) between joint ends.
  - Replacement (Resurfacing) arthroplasty – Substitution with metal/plastic components.
Modern day:
- Hemiarthroplasty: Single-piece prosthesis → femoral head replaced only.
- Total Hip Replacement (THR): Modular prosthesis → acetabular + femoral components. Mnemonic: Hemi = one-piece; Total = modular with multiple components.

CHARNLEY’S LOW-FRICTION ARTHROPLASTY –
### PRINCIPLES
- Aim: Reduce friction & torsional stresses.
- Key Principles:
  1. Small femoral head (22 mm): ↓ torque & friction.

<!-- page 3 -->

2. Thick polyethylene socket: ↓ contact stress.
  3. Medialize cup & lateralize GT: ↑ lever arm → ↓ joint reaction force.
  4. Use bone cement (PMMA): Stable fixation of both components.
Outcome: Excellent short-term results, but reproducibility poor due to
technical challenges.
Failures:
- Small head → ↑ dislocations.
- Over-medialization → acetabular perforations.
- Trochanteric osteotomy nonunion common.

PRINCIPLE OF MODERN THR
Recreate the natural anatomy.
- Maintain normal offset, inclination, version, and limb length.
- Avoid medialization or trochanteric transfer.
- Use modular, anatomically contoured implants with precise fixation.

FIXATION METHODS
### 1⃣ Cemented Fixation
- Material: PMMA (Polymethyl methacrylate).
- Acts as grout (space filler), not adhesive.
- Best for elderly with poor bone stock.
Techniques: 3rd/4th generation cementing — vacuum mixing, restrictor, centralizer,
pressurization.

### 2⃣ Cementless Fixation
- Initial: Press-fit fixation (bone elasticity allows interference fit).
- Long-term: Achieved by osteointegration:

<!-- page 4 -->

- Bone Ingrowth: Into porous metal (e.g., tantalum).
  - Bone Ongrowth: Onto roughened surface (grit-blast / plasma spray).
Problem: Stress shielding → proximal bone resorption → thigh pain.
Choice:
- Cemented → elderly, short life expectancy.
- Cementless → young, good bone stock.

### Design Modifications:
- Distal tapering: ↓ stiffness → ↓ stress shielding.
- Proximal wedging: Generates hoop stresses → enhances stability.

### MORSE TAPER (TRUNNION)
- Junction between femoral head and stem neck.
- Standard geometry: 12–14 mm taper → lowest failure rate.
- Trunnionosis: Metal debris & corrosion at taper → cause of 3–5% THR revisions.

### MODULARITY
- Allows independent control of offset, version, limb length.
- Femoral modularity: Neck length (25–50 mm), neck-shaft angle (127–132°), head size (28–32 mm).
- Head choice: 28–32 mm ideal → optimal jump distance & low dislocation. > > 36 mm → thinner poly → early wear.

### SPECIAL TOPICS
- Stress Shielding: Seen in uncemented titanium stems → proximal osteoporosis.
- Dual Mobility Cup: Two articulations (head–liner + liner–shell) → ↓ dislocation risk; ideal for elderly/weak abductors.
- Constrained Liner: Outdated – high mechanical failure rates.

### KEY EXAM PEARLS
- THR = resurfacing, not replacement.
- Charnley (Father of Arthroplasty): Bone cement, small head, low friction.
- Fixation: Cemented → old; Cementless → young.
- Trunnion: 12–14 taper = ideal.
- Material: Ti for cementless; Co–Cr for cemented.
- Polyethylene thickness: ≥5 mm.
- Best bearing: Metal-on-Poly (cross-linked).
- Complication marker: IL-6 best for infection detection.
