Malignant Bone Tumors – Part 1 (Osteosarcoma) –
Dr. Rajat Gupta
Overview
Osteosarcoma is the most common primary malignant bone tumor of children and
adolescents and the second most common primary bone malignancy overall. It
arises from malignant osteoblasts that produce malignant osteoid, the hallmark of
diagnosis.
Faculty Remark: “Never forget, malignant osteoid on histology is what defines
osteosarcoma.”

## Epidemiology & Risk Factors
### Key Epidemiological Points
- Incidence: ~1 per million per year.
- Peak age: 10–20 years (bimodal distribution – elderly peak with secondary OS).

### High Risk Conditions
- Predilection for: distal femur, proximal tibia, proximal humerus.

- Hereditary retinoblastoma.
- Li Fraumeni syndrome.
- Rothmund–Thomson syndrome.
- Paget disease (secondary OS).
- Post radiation sarcoma.

## Clinical Features
- Pain (often progressive).
- Swelling and mass.
- Limp if lower limb involved.
- Pathological fracture in some cases.

## Radiology – Classical Appearance
### X Ray Assessment (Systematic)
- Age: usually skeletally immature.

<!-- page 2 -->

- Location: metaphyseal (most common). Diaphyseal in 7–9 percent.
- Type: permeative lytic, sclerotic or mixed.
- Matrix: bone forming, sclerotic.
- Zone of transition: wide, ill defined.
- Periosteal reaction: Codman triangle, sunburst.
- Soft tissue mass: usually present.
### Typical Features
- Mixed lytic sclerotic lesion.
- Periosteal elevation (Codman triangle).
- Radiodense osteoid in soft tissues.
- Aggressive periosteal reactions.

### Secondary Osteosarcoma
- Due to Paget disease.
- Due to radiation exposure (radiation induced OS).
- Post bone infarct, chronic osteomyelitis, fibrous dysplasia.

### Arlen Criteria for Radiation Induced Sarcoma
- Non malignant or different malignant histology before RT.
- Sarcoma must develop within radiation field.
- Long latency (minimum 5 years, may extend to 30 years).
- Histological confirmation of sarcoma.

### Huvos Grading (Chemotherapy Response)
- Grade 1: <50 percent necrosis.
- Grade 2: 50–90 percent necrosis.
- Grade 3: 90–99 percent necrosis (good response).
- Grade 4: 100 percent necrosis (excellent response).
### Picci Grading
- Grade 1: Macroscopic viable tumor.
- Grade 2: Microscopic viable tumor.
- Grade 3: No viable tumor.

## Investigations & Staging
### X Ray
- Defines bony destruction, periosteal reaction, fracture risk.
### MRI (Full Length)
- Entire bone must be imaged to exclude skip lesions.
- Defines marrow and soft tissue extent.

<!-- page 4 -->

- Helps plan biopsy route and surgical resection.
### PET CT (Whole Body)
- Detects metastasis.
- Evaluates chemotherapy response.
- Good response = >50 percent reduction in SUV.
### Biopsy Principles
- Needle biopsy preferred.
- In line with planned incision.
- Avoid contaminating compartments.
- Do not divide specimen.
- Avoid transverse incisions.

## Treatment Principles

### Chemotherapy
Standard regimen: MAP Protocol (Methotrexate, Adriamycin, Cisplatin).

Drug Dosages (per m²)
- Adriamycin: 75 mg over 3 days.
- Cisplatin: 100 mg over 2 days.
- High dose Methotrexate: 12 g over 4 hours.
- Ifosfamide: 9 g over 5 days.
Sequence
1. Neoadjuvant chemotherapy (2–3 cycles).
2. Limb salvage surgery.
3. Adjuvant chemotherapy (3–4 cycles).
Low grade variants (parosteal, low grade intramedullary) → surgery alone, no
chemotherapy.

## Limb Salvage Surgery
Barriers (Relative)
- Poor biopsy placement.
- Major vessel encasement.

<!-- page 5 -->

- Major motor nerve involvement.
- Pathological fracture.
3 Strike Rule
If three of the following are involved → limb salvage generally not advisable: - Bone -
Nerve - Vessel - Soft tissue envelope

## Reconstruction Options
### Non Biological
- Megaprosthesis (most common).
- Expandable megaprosthesis for children.

### Biological Options
- Nail cement spacers (palliative reconstruction).

### ECRT (Extracorporeal Radiation Therapy)
- Tumor bearing bone removed, irradiated with 50 Gy single fraction, reimplanted.
- Requires canal cementing due to devascularisation.
Cryotherapy
- Tumor bearing bone dipped in liquid nitrogen at −196°C, sterilized, reimplanted.
### Pedicle Cryotherapy
- Tumor segment remains attached proximally.
- Blood supply partly preserved.
- Lower risk of graft fracture.
Rotationplasty
- Functional ankle becomes knee.
- Excellent functional outcome in children and young.
- Addresses limb length issues effectively.
### Canadell’s Procedure
- Used in children with open physis when joint salvage possible.
- Step 1: Physeal distraction.
- Step 2: Resection through physis.
- Sterilization by ECRT.
- Reimplantation preserving growth potential.

### Exam Pearls
- Malignant osteoid defines osteosarcoma.
- Most common site: distal femur.
- Wide zone of transition = hallmark of malignancy.
- Chondroblastic OS shows whorled MRI pattern.
- Telangiectatic OS mimics ABC due to fluid levels.
- MDM2/CDK4 positivity differentiates low grade intramedullary OS from FD.
- Good chemotherapy response = >90 percent necrosis.
- Full length MRI to detect skip lesions.
- MAP protocol is standard regimen.
- Rotationplasty gives superior function in children than amputation.

Final Faculty Insight: “Osteosarcoma is curable if principles are respected. Correct
staging, proper biopsy, optimal chemotherapy and well planned surgery determine
survival.”
