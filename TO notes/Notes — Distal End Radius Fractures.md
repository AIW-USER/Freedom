Distal End Radius Fractures – Dr. Amit Mittal
Overview
Distal radius fractures require understanding of wrist anatomy, radiographic alignment,
fracture morphology, and stability criteria. Proper assessment prevents long term
complications such as malunion, DRUJ instability, and carpal collapse.
Faculty Remark: “Mechanism of injury and stability assessment decide the
treatment, not just the X ray.”

Three Column Concept (Rikli & Regazzoni)
(Ref: PPT pg 1–3)

### Lateral Column
- Radial styloid.
- Scaphoid fossa.
### Intermediate Column
- Lunate facet.
- Sigmoid notch.
- Key to radiocarpal and DRUJ stability.
### Medial Column
- Ulnar head.
- TFCC.
Injury to intermediate column is a major predictor of instability.
Principles of Wrist Fracture Treatment – “A R M S”
(Ref: pg 3) - A – Articular congruity - R – Radial alignment & length - M – Motion -
S – Stability by preserving anatomical length.

Anatomy & Radiographic Parameters
### Radial Inclination (Slope)
- Normal ~ 23 degrees. (Pg 5)

<!-- page 2 -->

### Ulnar Variance
- Measured using perpendicular line method.
- Normal: < 1 mm difference. (Pg 6)
### Volar Tilt
- Normal ~ 11 degrees. (Pg 7)
### Tear Drop Angle
- Normal ~ 70 degrees. (Pg 8–9)
- Increased: Volar marginal fragment.
- Decreased: Dorsally angulated fracture.
### PA View – Volar vs Dorsal Rim Distance
- Normal: Dorsal rim projects 3–5 mm distal to volar rim. (Pg 10,13)
### AP / Lunate Diameter Ratio
- Normal ratio ≈ 1.
- Ratio >1.3 suggests significant AP widening → instability. (Pg 12)

Named Fractures
(Ref: pg 15–17)

### Colles Fracture
- Dorsal displacement + dorsal comminution.
- Radial shortening.
### Smith Fracture (Thomas Classification)
- Type I: Extra articular with volar angulation.
- Type II: Intra articular, volar displacement (Reverse Barton).
- Type III: Extra articular, volar displacement (more oblique line).
### Chauffeur’s Fracture
- Radial styloid fracture due to forced dorsiflexion + radial deviation.
### Die Punch Fracture
- Depressed lunate fossa fracture.
- Indicates intermediate column injury → unstable.

Classification Systems
Frykman Classification (Pg 19–20)
Simple system based on: - Extra vs intra articular. - Presence of ulnar styloid fracture. -
Limited prognostic value.

### AO Classification (Pg 22–24)
- Type A: Extra articular.
- Type B: Partial articular (some part attached to metaphysis).
- Type C: Complete articular with metaphyseal detachment.

Mayo Classification (Pg 25)
Classifies into: - Extra articular – nondisplaced / displaced. - Intra articular –
reducible / irreducible / unstable.
Primary joints involved: - Type I – Radioscaphoid. - Type II – Radiolunate. - Type III –
Radioscapholunate.

Fernandez Classification (Pg 27,30–34)
Based on mechanism of injury:
1. Type I – Bending Injuries
  - Colles, Smith Type I.
2. Type II – Shear Injuries
  - Barton, Chauffeur, volar/dorsal rim fractures.
3. Type III – Compression Injuries
  - Die punch → subchondral collapse.
4. Type IV – Avulsion Injuries
  - Radiocarpal fracture-dislocation.
5. Type V – High Energy Combined Mechanisms
  - Complex, multifragmentary.

Stability Criteria (Critical Slide)
(Ref: pg 46–49) A fracture is unstable if ANY of the following are present: - Dorsal
angulation >20 degrees. - Dorsal comminution >50 percent. - Intra articular
fractures. - Ulnar fracture. - Age >60 years. - AP widening (ratio >1.3). - Carpal
malalignment → capitate axis not intersecting radius axis. (Pg 49–50)

DRUJ Injury (Accompanying Distal Radius Fracture)
(Ref: pg 42–43, 57–59)

### Type I – Stable DRUJ
- Sigmoid notch intact.
- Tip fracture of ulnar styloid or stable ulna neck fracture.
- Treatment: No DRUJ surgery needed, apply cast.
### Type II – Unstable DRUJ
- TFCC foveal injury OR base of styloid fracture.
- Positive shuck test.
- Treatment:
  - TFCC repair or ulnar styloid fixation.
  - Above elbow cast in supination.
  - Radioulnar K wire if needed.
Type III – Intra articular Sigmoid Notch Injury
- Fragmentation of palmar/dorsal lips.
- Treatment: anatomical reduction + fixation; consider long arm cast.

### Treatment Strategy (Step Wise – From Transcript)
1. Identify mechanism of injury → assign Fernandez type.
2. Assess stability using criteria above.
3. Assess displacement pattern and number of fragments.
4. Check for associated injuries (median nerve compression, ulnar styloid, DRUJ).
### If Stable → Cast
- No significant comminution.
- No displacement.
- No instability markers.
If Unstable → Surgical
Options: - Percutaneous pinning. - Volar locking plate (preferred for most patterns). -
Fragment specific fixation. - External fixation / bridge plating in high energy fractures. -
Bone grafting for comminution.

### Special Scenarios
- Type IV (radiocarpal fracture dislocation): Always requires ORIF + ligament repair.

<!-- page 5 -->

- Type V (complex): Combined dorsal + volar approach + bridge plate + bone graft.

### Exam Pearls
- Intermediate column injury = unstable fracture.
- Die punch fracture → treat as unstable.
- Radial inclination 23 deg, volar tilt 11 deg, ulnar variance <1 mm.
- Tear drop angle normal 70 deg; decreased in dorsal angulation.
- Carpal malalignment = capitate axis abnormal.
- DRUJ Type I stable; Type II needs repair; Type III needs anatomical reduction.
- PRC contraindicated in capitolunate arthritis (related pearl from SLAC/SNAC).

Final Faculty Insight: “If you understand the mechanism and stability, distal radius
fracture management becomes logical and predictable.”
