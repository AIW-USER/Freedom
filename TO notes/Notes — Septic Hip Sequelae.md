Septic Hip Sequelae – Dr. Sanjay Arora
Overview / Key Concepts
Post septic hip sequelae represent the long term structural and functional
consequences of infantile septic arthritis of the hip. The extent of deformity depends on
the child’s age at infection, virulence of the organism and adequacy plus timing of initial
management.
Early infection causes more severe damage because the entire femoral head is
cartilaginous and highly vulnerable. Sequelae may arise from: - Direct damage by
infective toxins and host inflammatory response. - Tamponade effect due to
intracapsular pus causing ischemia. - Mechanical failure following destruction of femoral
neck or proximal femur.
These mechanisms lead to necrosis, physeal damage, deformities, instability and
functional disability.

Pathogenesis and Deformity Spectrum
### Mechanisms of Deformity
- Cartilage necrosis and chondrolysis.
- Ischemic necrosis of the femoral head from raised intracapsular pressure.
- Premature closure of triradiate cartilage causing acetabular dysplasia.
- Symmetric or asymmetric proximal femoral physeal closure leading to:
  - Coxa breva.
  - Coxa vara or coxa valga.
  - Limb length discrepancy.
  - Greater trochanteric overgrowth.
- Femoral neck pathological fracture leading to pseudoarthrosis.
- Complete destruction of head and neck in severe cases.
Clinical functional disabilities include severe shortening, limp, difficulty in walking or
sitting cross legged and abductor lurch.

Radiographic Classifications
Choi Classification (most widely used)
Based on damage to capital epiphysis, physis and metaphysis.
Type I - IA: Almost normal hip. - IB: Mild coxa magna.

Type II - IIA: Coxa breva due to symmetrical physeal closure. - IIB: Coxa vara or valga
due to asymmetrical closure. Associated acetabular dysplasia.
Type III - IIIA: Severe angular deformity with excessive anteversion or retroversion. -
IIIB: Femoral neck pseudoarthrosis due to complete epiphyseal slip.
Type IV - IVA: Complete loss of head, small remnant still articulating. - IVB: Complete
loss of head and neck.

Comparative Classifications
Other commonly referenced systems include Hunka, Forlin Milani and Johari
classifications. All correlate with Choi type patterns.

Assessment of Residual Deformity
### Imaging Modalities
- X rays
- MRI
- Ultrasound
- Arthrogram when ossific nucleus poorly visualized
### Important Parameters
- Head size and morphology
- Acetabular coverage
- Presence of pseudoarthrosis
- Neck shaft angle
- Trochanteric overgrowth
- Limb length discrepancy
- Hip stability
Algorithm suggests narrower management choices depending on age (less or greater
than 6 years), degree of residual neck remnant and congruency of articulation.

Principles of Management
Treatment is guided by specific deformities and aims to restore biomechanics and
stability.

### Key Objectives
- Improve femoral head coverage
- Correct limb length discrepancy
- Provide a stable hip articulation

<!-- page 3 -->

- Improve abductor mechanics
- Restore alignment of proximal femur and acetabulum
- Use salvage procedures when reconstruction is not feasible

### Surgical Options
1. Improving Head Coverage
- Abduction cast or brace
- Open reduction to center head in the socket
- Femoral osteotomy (varus or valgus depending on deformity)
- Pelvic osteotomy (reorientation, reshaping or augmentation)
- Osteochondroplasty or head reduction when coxa magna or irregular head shape present
2. Limb Length Equalization
- Shoe raise
- Contralateral epiphysiodesis
- Ipsilateral limb lengthening via external fixator
3. Providing a Stable Hip
- Closed or open reduction
- Femoral and pelvic osteotomies
- Reconstruction procedures in cases with partial head remnant
4. Improving Abductor Insufficiency
- Trochanteric arthroplasty: trochanter placed inside acetabulum and abductors reattached distally
- Neck lengthening osteotomies:
  - Wagner femoral neck lengthening
  - Morscher modification
  - Hasler technique
  - Papavasiliou and Kruminis variants
- Distal and lateral transfer of greater trochanter
5. Restoring Alignment
- Femoral realignment osteotomies
- Pelvic osteotomies including Salter, Dega, Pemberton, Shelf procedures depending on acetabular deficiency pattern
6. Salvage Procedures
Indicated in severe deformity, instability or absent head.
- Arthrodesis (hip fusion)

<!-- page 4 -->

- Ilizarov hip reconstruction
- Pelvic support osteotomy
- Trochanteric arthroplasty

Special Procedures for Choi Type IV
### Albee Arthroplasty
- Removal of head and placing denuded greater trochanter into acetabulum.
Modified Albee (greenstick fracture variation)
- Splitting proximal femur and inserting medial fragment into acetabulum.
### L’Episcopo Procedure
- Slitting proximal femoral remnant and turning medial half into acetabulum to create femoropelvic articulation.
### Modified Harmon Procedure
- Incomplete open wedge osteotomy at proximal remnant with graft insertion.
- Useful when cartilage cap preserved.
These procedures aim to create a stable articulation where head and neck are absent.

Pelvic Support Osteotomy (PSO) / Ilizarov Hip Reconstruction
Concept
A subtrochanteric valgus osteotomy that restores hip stability, reduces Trendelenburg
gait and improves limb length mechanics.

Indications
- Coxa breva
- Coxa vara or valga
- Instability
- Abductor insufficiency
- Limb length discrepancy
Timing
- Most authors recommend PSO after skeletal maturity.
- Some advocate earlier intervention to allow abductor stretching.
### Technical Considerations
- Osteotomy level: subtrochanteric

<!-- page 5 -->

- Valgus angle: maximum adduction possible plus contracture angle plus overcorrection 15 to 30 degrees
- Fixation with bent plate or blade plate
- Implant must often be removed at 1 year due to remodeling
Ilizarov Hip Reconstruction
Two osteotomies: 1. Subtrochanteric valgus osteotomy 2. Distal femoral varus
osteotomy Followed by gradual limb lengthening.
Indications: instability, limb length discrepancy, abductor insufficiency, partial head loss.

Neck Lengthening Procedures
### Morscher Neck Lengthening
- Performed when head congruent with acetabulum
- Involves osteotomy, lateralization of shaft and distal transfer of greater trochanter
- Improves abductor lever arm and restores biomechanics
### Wagner Osteotomy
- Similar but includes change in neck shaft angle
- Applied in cases with coxa vara or valga with diminished neck length

Faculty Quote
“Management of post septic hip sequelae must be individualized. Reconstruction is
rewarding when anatomy is preserved; otherwise salvage procedures offer stability and
improved function.” – Dr. Sanjay Arora

### Exam Pearls
- Choi classification is most commonly used.
- Type II involves physeal damage and trochanteric overgrowth.
- Type IIIA shows severe anteversion or retroversion.
- Type IIIB signifies femoral neck pseudoarthrosis.
- Type IV indicates complete loss of head and neck.
- Pelvic support osteotomy improves stability and gait.
- Neck lengthening requires congruent head acetabulum relationship.
- Trochanteric arthroplasty is salvage option for absent head.
- Ilizarov reconstruction addresses LLD, instability and abductor lurch.
