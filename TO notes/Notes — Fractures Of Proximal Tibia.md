Fractures of Proximal Tibia – Dr. Daivik T Shetty

Overview
- Tibial plateau fractures are intra-articular injuries that affect the articular surface of the proximal tibia and, if improperly managed, lead to deformity or functional deficit.
- They constitute 1% of all fractures and are more common in elderly osteoporotic bones.
- Proper anatomical reduction is vital to restore joint congruity and prevent early osteoarthritis.
Dr. Daivik’s Remark:
“Tibial plateau fractures look benign on X-ray, but a millimeter of step here means years
of pain later.”

### Anatomy of Tibial Plateau

| Feature | Lateral Plateau | Medial Plateau |
|---|---|---|
| Shape | Convex | Concave |
| Position | 2–3 mm higher (proximal) | Slightly distal |
| Cartilage Thickness | ~4 mm | ~2–3 mm |
| Size | Smaller | Larger |

### Normal Slope
- Sagittal Slope: 5° (posteroinferior)
- Coronal Slope: 3°
### Neurovascular Structures
- Common Peroneal Nerve (lateral)
- Popliteal Artery (posterior)
Surgical Tip: When placing subchondral screws parallel to the lateral plateau,
remember the medial side lies lower, to avoid joint penetration.

### Associated Injuries
- Vascular injury (especially medial plateau – popliteal artery)
- Compartment syndrome (high-velocity trauma)
- Collateral ligament injury: MCL, LCL
- Meniscal tears:
  - Lateral meniscus – more common in Schatzker II
  - Medial meniscus – in medial/bicondylar types
- Segond fracture: Lateral tibial avulsion (associated with ACL tear)
- Reverse Segond fracture: Medial avulsion (deep MCL fibers)
- Semimembranosus insertion / Anteromedial margin fractures

### CT Scan
  - Gold standard for fracture mapping.
  - Defines personality, displacement, and comminution.
  - Essential for surgical planning and approach selection.
### MRI
  - Used for meniscus/ligament injuries if suspected.
  - Not routinely required for bony pattern assessment.

Classifications
1. Schatzker Classification (1979) – Gold Standard

| Type | Description | Features |
|---|---|---|
| I | Pure split fracture (lateral plateau) | Low-velocity, young, cleavage fracture |
| II | Split + depression fracture | Older age, lateral meniscus tear (80% if >5 mm widening & >6 mm depression) |
| III | Pure depression fracture | Elderly, osteoporotic bones |
| IV | Medial plateau fracture | High-velocity, MCL/meniscus injury, popliteal artery risk |
| V | Bicondylar fracture, metaphysis Both plateaus, intact intact | metaphyseal–diaphysea l junction |
| VI | Bicondylar with metaphyseal–diaphyseal dissociation | Complete separation, high-energy trauma |

Limitation: Does not consider coronal or posterior components.

2. Hohl and Moore Classification
  - Considers coronal cuts and mechanism.
  - Types – Split, Entire condyle, Rim avulsion (e.g., Segond), Rim compression, Four-part fracture.

### Operative Indications
- Articular step-off >2 mm
- Condylar widening >5 mm
- Varus/valgus instability >10°
- Medial or bicondylar fractures
- Open fractures
- Associated compartment/vascular injury
Dr. Daivik’s Tip:
“In high-energy injuries with blisters and edema, span first, scan next, and plan later.”

### Span–Scan–Plan Protocol
1. Span: Apply spanning external fixator to restore geometry and protect soft tissues.
2. Scan: Perform CT after spanning for 3D assessment.
3. Plan: Definitive fixation after soft tissue recovery.

Surgical Techniques by Schatzker Type
### Type I – Pure Split
- Closed or percutaneous reduction.
- 6.5 mm cancellous lag screws + washer.
- Young: Screws adequate. Elderly: Add buttress plate.
### Type II – Split + Depression
- Anterolateral approach (workhorse incision).
- Elevate depressed fragment, fill defect with bone graft / calcium phosphate filler.
- Fix with lateral buttress plate and raft screws (subchondral support).
Raft Screws: 3.5 mm screws parallel to subchondral bone for articular support.

### Type III – Pure Depression
- Elevate fragment via metaphyseal window.
- Fill defect with bone graft.
- Fix using subchondral plate or raft screws.
### Type IV – Medial Plateau
- High-energy varus angulation injury.
- Fix with medial buttress plate + cancellous screws.
- Always check for vascular compromise (popliteal artery).
### Type V & VI – Bicondylar
- Double column plating (Post & Lintel System).
- Indications: Comminuted medial condyle, displaced, small fragments.
- Approaches: Anterolateral + Posteromedial (180° apart).
- Medial side fixed first; ensure accurate reduction before lateral plate.
- Benefits: Stronger construct, early mobilization.

Complications

| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| Mechanical | Loss of reduction, Varus collapse |
| Soft tissue | Wound dehiscence, infection |
| Functional | Knee stiffness, painful hardware |
| Union issues | Non-union (rare), Malunion |
| Late | Post-traumatic osteoarthritis |

### Key Viva / Exam Pearls
- Commonest fracture: Lateral plateau (55–70%).
- Worst prognosis: Medial plateau.
- Popliteal artery injury: Type IV (medial plateau).
- Meniscal injury association: Type II (lateral meniscus).
- Raft screw: Subchondral support screw in depression fractures.
- Dual plating principle: Post & Lintel system.
- CT is mandatory for surgical planning.
- Span → Scan → Plan: Rule for high-energy trauma.
Final Faculty Insight:
“Reconstruction of tibial plateau is a reconstruction of motion. The better you restore the
articular slope, the longer the knee will thank you.”
