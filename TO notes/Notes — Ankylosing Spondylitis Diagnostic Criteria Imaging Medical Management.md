Ankylosing Spondylitis: Diagnostic Criteria, Imaging
and Medical Management – Dr. Shikha Dhaundiyal
Overview
This section outlines how ankylosing spondylitis (AS) is diagnosed, the imaging
spectrum, and principles of medical management. Diagnosis relies on clinical features,
examination findings, classification criteria, and imaging. Management focuses on

Clinical Presentation
symptom control, functional preservation, and prevention of deformity.

### Key Symptoms
- Low back pain and stiffness of insidious onset that is worse in the morning or after rest and improves with activity.
- Morning stiffness lasting more than 30 minutes.
- Onset typically before age 40.
- Symptoms present for more than 3 months.
- Alternating buttock pain.
- Night pain with improvement upon getting up.
- Pain may radiate to posterior thigh, chest wall, cervical spine.
- Sleep disturbance and daytime fatigue common.
### Extra-Axial Symptoms
- Peripheral arthritis, especially in lower limbs.
- Enthesitis (Achilles, plantar fascia).
- Dactylitis.

### Physical Examination
- Restrictive lung pattern in advanced disease.

### Cervical and Thoracolumbar Spine
- Forward stooping posture.
- Occiput-to-wall distance to assess flexion deformity.
- Chest expansion measurement at the fourth intercostal space; normal more than 2 cm.
- Modified Schober test. Normal increase more than 2 cm.
- Lateral spinal flexion measured against the thigh or floor.
### Sacroiliac and Hip Assessment
- SI joint tenderness by direct pressure.
- FABER test.

<!-- page 2 -->

- Hip internal and external rotation to assess flexion deformity.
### Systemic Features
- Dactylitis (sausage digits).
- Cardiovascular: Aortitis, valvular disease.

### Classification and Diagnostic Criteria
- Respiratory: Apical fibrosis.

Modified New York Criteria (1984)
### Clinical Criteria
1. Low back pain for at least 3 months, improved with exercise, not relieved by rest.
2. Limitation of lumbar spine motion.
3. Decreased chest expansion.
### Radiological Criterion
- Bilateral sacroiliitis grade 2 or higher.
- Unilateral sacroiliitis grade 3 or higher.
Definite AS is diagnosed when radiological criteria are present with any clinical criterion.

Amor Criteria
A point-based clinical scoring system incorporating inflammatory back pain, enthesitis,
uveitis, HLA-B27, family history, response to NSAIDs.

ESSG Criteria
Presence of inflammatory spinal pain or synovitis plus at least one of: - Family history of
SpA - Psoriasis - IBD - Urethritis - Buttock pain - Enthesitis - Sacroiliitis on imaging

ASAS Criteria for Axial Spondyloarthritis
In patients with more than 3 months back pain and age less than 45 years. - Imaging
arm: Sacroiliitis on imaging plus 1 SpA feature. - Clinical arm: HLA-B27 positivity plus
2 SpA features.

### CALIN Criteria for Inflammatory Back Pain
- Age at onset less than 40.
- Insidious onset.
- Improvement with exercise.
- No improvement with rest.

Imaging
- Night pain improving on getting up.

### Conventional Radiography
- Mandatory for diagnosis of AS.

<!-- page 3 -->

- SI joint involvement is essential.
- Progression: Blurring of joint margins → erosions → sclerosis → narrowing → ankylosis.
- Erosions appear earlier on the iliac side.
### Radiographic Features of Spine
- Squaring of vertebral bodies.
- Romanus lesions (shiny corners).
- Syndesmophytes (marginal) leading to bamboo spine.
### Scoring Systems
- BASRI.
- SASSS.
- Modified SASSS (mSASSS), evaluates anterior corners from C2 to T1 and T12 to S1.
### MRI
- STIR sequences detect bone marrow edema and early inflammatory changes.
- Useful in early axial SpA when X-rays are normal.
- MRI essential in suspected cauda equina syndrome.
CT

Histopathology
- Helpful in evaluating syndesmophytes and fractures.

- Enthesitis is the hallmark.

### Management Principles
- Chronic inflammation with CD4+, CD8+ lymphocytes and macrophages.

### Patient Education
- Disease course, posture training, exercise, self-management.
### Physical Therapy
- Spinal extension exercises.
- Deep breathing exercises for chest expansion.
- Swimming is highly beneficial.

### Pharmacologic Management
- Firm mattress and thin pillow recommended.

NSAIDs
First line for axial symptoms. - Indomethacin most effective. - COX-2 inhibitors equally
effective.

<!-- page 4 -->

Sulfasalazine
Useful for peripheral arthritis or coexisting IBD.

Corticosteroids
- Limited role.
- Local injections helpful for SI joint or enthesitis.
DMARDs and Biologic Therapy
### TNF Inhibitors
- Etanercept
- Infliximab
- Adalimumab
- Golimumab
- Certolizumab
Indicated after failure of at least 2 NSAIDs.
### IL-17 Inhibitors
- Secukinumab
- Ixekizumab
### JAK Inhibitors
- Upadacitinib
- Tofacitinib
### Side Effects of Biologics
- Injection reactions.
- Increased risk of infections including TB reactivation.
- Possible malignancy risk.
- Autoimmune reactions.

Uveitis Management
Patients must be screened for TB, hepatitis B, and HIV.

- Topical steroids and mydriatics.
- TNFi reduce recurrence rates.

### Monitoring Disease Activity
- Adalimumab FDA approved for noninfectious uveitis.

Tools include: - BASDAI - BASFI - BASMI - ASAS core set - ESR and CRP - X-rays
every 2 years for structural progression. - MRI STIR for inflammatory activity.

Surgical Management
Indications
- Severe deformity affecting line of sight.
- Spinal fractures in fused spines.
- Advanced hip involvement requiring THA.
Procedures
- Vertebral osteotomy.
- Stabilization of fractures.

### Faculty Quote
- Total hip or shoulder replacement.

“Early diagnosis changes the trajectory of disease. The goal is not just to treat pain but

Exam Pearls
to preserve function and prevent irreversible deformity.”

- Earliest radiographic change in SI joint: blurring of margins.
- Earliest spinal radiographic sign: Romanus lesion (shiny corner).
- Most effective NSAID for AS: Indomethacin.
- Syndesmophytes in AS are marginal, unlike non-marginal in DISH.
- MRI is the most sensitive test for early sacroiliitis.
- HLA-B27 is supportive but not diagnostic.
- Chest expansion decrease is a key clinical criterion.
- mSASSS is preferred scoring system for monitoring progression.
- TNF inhibitors are first line biologics after NSAID failure.
- IL-17 inhibitors beneficial in TNFi inadequate response.
