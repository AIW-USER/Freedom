BLOOD DOPING (M1 CATEGORY)
Definition
Artificial enhancement of oxygen transport or manipulation of blood or its components
for improved performance.

### Prohibited Actions
1. Administration / Reintroduction of Blood: Any autologous, homologous, or heterologous blood transfusion.

2. Artificial Oxygen Enhancement: Use of substances like Perfluorochemicals, Efaproxiral (RSR13), or modified hemoglobin-based products to alter O₂ unloading.

3. Intravascular Manipulation: Physical or chemical alteration of blood or components.
### Physiology Refresher – Bohr Effect
- ↓ pH or ↑ pCO₂ → ↓ Hb affinity for O₂ → ↑ O₂ unloading to muscles.

- Crucial for endurance athletes; exploited in blood manipulation.
MCQ Tip: Bohr Effect favors oxygen delivery by shifting the oxyhemoglobin
dissociation curve to the right.

### Hemoglobin Mass and Performance
- Performance = VO₂ max = Qmax × (a–vO₂ difference).

- Total Hb mass, not [Hb] concentration, predicts aerobic capacity. Note: Volume loading (plasma expanders) alone doesn’t improve performance unless paired with increased Hb mass.

(Mircera) to detect

Case: ♂ Riccardo Riccò (Italy) – Positive for Mircera, banned 24 months. Later
self-transfused blood → 12-year ban.
Dose: 60–350 U/kg SC for 4–6 weeks → ↑ VO₂max, ↑ time to exhaustion.
Detection: - Direct: Flow cytometry for foreign RBCs.
- Indirect: CO rebreathing technique – measures tagged Hb mass.

### CO Rebreathing Method – Dilution Principle
- Inhaled CO tags Hb; dilution between tagged & untagged Hb gives total Hb mass.

- Safe but highly specialized detection method.

### CRYOPRESERVED RBCs

| Component | Temp | Shelf Life |
|---|---|---|

| Cryo-RBCs | -40°C | 1 year |
| Platelets | 20–24°C | 5 days |

Why Cryo-RBCs? Long shelf life, immediate post-transfusion performance gain (↑
RBCs, Hb mass, O₂ delivery).

PLATELET-RICH PLASMA (PRP)
Definition: Autologous blood fraction with platelet count > baseline (≈1,000,000
platelets/µL) in ~5 mL plasma.

### Key Components
- Platelets (α, δ, λ granules)

- Leukocytes

- Fibrin mesh

| Type | Platelet | Leukocyte | Fibrin |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pure PRP | ↑↑ | ↓ | ↓ |
| Leukocyte-rich | ↑↑ | ↑ | ↓ |

### PRP

| Pure PRF | ↑ | ↓ | ↑↑ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Leukocyte-rich | ↑ | ↑ | ↑↑ |

PRF
PRP rich in RBCs → ↑ oxidative stress → tissue damage. RBC
contamination releases toxic hemin & free Hb.
### Granule Biology

| Granule | Key Contents | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Alpha (α) | PDGF, VEGF, TGF-β, IGF | Healing, angiogenesis |
| Dense (δ) | ADP, Serotonin, Polyphosphate | Platelet activation, thrombus formation |
| Lambda (λ) | Enzymes | Tissue remodeling |

Activation: Degranulation causes α-granules to fuse with cell membrane → release GF
→ bind to receptors on MSCs, osteoblasts, fibroblasts → initiate regeneration cascade.
⏱ GF release: 95% secreted within 1 hr; continued synthesis for 5–7 days.

PREPARATION OF PRP
Methods
1. PRP Method – Two spins:
  - Soft spin → separates RBC, buffy coat, plasma.

  - Hard spin → concentrates platelets.

2. Buffy Coat Method – High-speed centrifugation → isolates platelets + WBCs.

### Storage & Anticoagulants
- Maintain 20–24°C (never freeze).

- Use Sodium Citrate or ACD-A; avoid EDTA (damages membranes).
Activation Agents: CaCl₂, autologous thrombin, or mechanical trauma.
Collagen naturally activates PRP in soft tissues.
MCQ Tip: Platelet granules release growth factors within 10 minutes of
activation and peak within 1 hour.

BIOLOGICAL EFFECTS OF PRP
### Muscle Healing
- ↑ Myocyte proliferation & VEGF expression.

- ↑ αB-crystallin phosphorylation → ↓ apoptosis.

- ↑ NF-κB activation → ↑ cell survival during regeneration.
### Cartilage Healing

| Effect | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| ↑ Proteoglycan & Collagen II | Chondrocyte upregulation |
| ↓ IL-1β & TNF-α | Anti-inflammatory via NF-κB inhibition |
| ↓ MMP-13 expression | ↓ cartilage degradation |
| ↑ IGF, VEGF, bFGF | Growth pathway activation (marker: ↑ VEGF for 4 weeks post-injection) |

MCQ: VEGF levels rise for up to 4 weeks post-PRP → used as testing
marker (AJSM, 2013).

### DEPA CLASSIFICATION OF PRP

| Parameter | Description | Range |
|---|---|---|
| Dose | Platelet count × PRP volume | <1B (low) → >5B (very high) |
| Efficiency | % Platelet recovery | <30% (poor) → >90% (high) |
| Purity | Platelets vs WBC/RBC ratio | <30% (whole blood) → >90% (very pure) |
| Activation | Exogenous clotting used | Thrombin/CaCl ₂ if required |

### WADA STATUS OF PRP
- Temporarily banned (2009–2011).

- Currently permitted, but individual growth factors remain prohibited (e.g., IGF-1, PDGF).

- FDA: Approved for bone grafts; off-label for office use.

- Leagues (NCAA, NFL, NBA): No restrictions. Dr. Shikha: “PRP isn’t a shortcut — it’s science assisting healing, not enhancing performance.”

### TAKE-HOME SUMMARY
- Blood doping = M1 class prohibited method.

- PRP: Allowed, but GF components (IGF, PDGF) banned.

- Mannitol (IV), Dialysis, Laser therapy → prohibited.

- Topical ophthalmic drugs mostly allowed, except β-blockers in select sports.

- Sun Yang case = hallmark for metabolic modulator misuse. Dr. Shikha’s Final Remark: “In sports medicine, the safest prescription is knowledge.”
