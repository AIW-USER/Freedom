Interesting Cases Review: Knee Joint MRI – Dr.
Nafisa Shakir Batta (Aug 2026)
### Overview / Key Concepts
- An interactive case session after the main knee MRI lectures: quick anatomy revision, then cases chosen by the participants: patellofemoral dislocation, posterolateral corner injury, discoid meniscus variants, PCL avulsion, ACL tear with root and bucket-handle tears, lateral extra-articular tenodesis, cyclops lesions, mucoid degeneration of the ACL, and two participant cases.
- Make diagnoses on sagittal and coronal images; use axials as an adjunct (except for patellofemoral problems and the popliteus tendon, where axials are key).
- Read the whole set of sequences; printed films with bright window settings can hide bone, cartilage, fat and synovium.

### Normal Anatomy Revision
- ACL: taut anteromedial (AM) bundle and greyer, more fibrillar posterolateral (PL) bundle; normally greyer than the PCL (fibrofatty tissue). High signal between intact taut fibres is NOT a sprain.
- PCL: uniformly dark, buckled in extension.
- Fat pads and plica: infrapatellar plica (ligamentum mucosum) divides Hoffa fat pad; suprapatellar fat pad.
- Patellar osteophytes arise at articular (cartilage) margins; spurs at tendon insertions are enthesophytes.
- Coronal: medial and lateral retinacula, menisci and their posterior roots (the lateral root lies close to the ACL tibial footprint, so it tears with the ACL); popliteus tendon, fibular (lateral) collateral ligament, conjoint tendon with biceps femoris; anterolateral ligament (femur to tibia); superficial and deep MCL (meniscofemoral and meniscotibial parts), POL.
- Axial: patellotrochlear cartilage, medial and lateral patellar fat pads, MPFL; menisci; ACL and PCL attachments (resident's ridge).

### Patellofemoral Dislocation
- Classic contusion pair: lateral femoral condyle and inferomedial patella (impaction during dislocation and relocation); assess on axial images.
- Look for cartilage injury and osteochondral fragments: in place, or displaced into the lateral suprapatellar recess or the posterior recesses (old fragments become loose bodies).
- Check MPFL and medial patellar retinaculum (MPR): intact (dark) or torn; after repeated episodes they become very thick and separated from bone by a fluid cleft (insufficient).
- Chronic maltracking grinds down the lateral patellar cartilage (fissures, microcysts, marrow oedema) before the acute event.
- Predisposing anatomy: trochlear dysplasia (Dejour types, e.g. C with a rounded lateral facet), Wiberg patella types, high tibial slope, discoid lateral meniscus in one case.
- TT-TG distance: on axials, draw the bicondylar line and a perpendicular through the deepest trochlear groove; measure to the most prominent part of the tibial tuberosity (about 15 mm in the case). PT-TG uses the patellar tendon midpoint instead.

### Posterolateral Corner (PLC) Injury
- A popliteus tendon injury never occurs alone: expect FCL, ALL and cruciate injuries (PLC injuries go with PCL tears, but are seen more with the far commoner ACL tears).
- Road accident case: ACL and PCL not reaching the femur; popliteus tendon fails to reach its femoral sulcus (fluid cleft); FCL detached from the femur with haematoma; deep MCL meniscofemoral fibres torn, superficial MCL intact.
- Normal popliteus: muscle belly to musculotendinous junction, then the tendon curves around the lateral condyle into the sulcus. Some fluid in the sulcus and magic-angle signal are normal; trace it to its attachment. Axial images are excellent for it.
- Coronal search from lateral: iliotibial band to Gerdy tubercle, ALL, popliteus, FCL, biceps.

### Meniscus Variants and Tears

| Case | Findings |
|---|---|
| Discoid lateral meniscus | Meniscus continues across the condyle into the notch instead of a triangular inner edge |
| Footballer: discoid lateral meniscus with deficient popliteomeniscal fascicles | Hypoplastic or torn posterior fascicles leave a gap between capsule and meniscus; rounded posterior edge, loss of the bow-tie; hypermobile meniscus that slips in and out and degenerates |
| Wrestler (21): torn fascicles + bucket-handle tear | Probably a discoid meniscus; peripheral bucket- handle tear with the whole meniscus flipped into the notch; empty lateral compartment near the popliteus |
| Discoid MEDIAL meniscus | Rare; horizontal cleavage tear through it (seen with the PCL avulsion case) |
| ACL tear + lateral root tear + medial bucket handle | Small medial meniscus with a flipped fragment; lateral posterior root fragmented near the ACL footprint. Always look for a lateral root tear in ACL injuries; meniscofemoral ligaments (Humphrey, Wrisberg) can hide it, so use sagittal and axial images |

### PCL Avulsion Fracture
- Fracture at the posterior tibial (retrospinal) slope carrying the PCL footprint; PCL grey and oedematous.
- Coronal images show whether there are one or more fragments; measure fragment size and distraction, but fluid in the fracture line can mimic distraction (and the reverse).
- For fragment size, comminution and displacement get a CT: better bone resolution than MRI.
- Always check the popliteal artery (dark flow void) and vein in PCL injuries.
- Fabella in the lateral gastrocnemius head is a normal finding.

### Post-operative Knee
- Revision ACL in a 17-year-old powerlifter: two tibial and two femoral tunnels with two Endobuttons; small medial meniscus (previous bucket-handle excision).
- Lateral extra-articular tenodesis (LET): anchor at the femur; a strip of iliotibial band harvested and looped around the FCL to reinforce the lateral side. The ITB discontinuity at the harvest site is expected, not a tear.
- Metal causes paramagnetic artefact that can mimic oedema; with a lot of hardware request MAVRIC (metal artefact reduction) MRI.
- Cyclops lesion (localised anterior arthrofibrosis): nodular, heterogeneous tissue in front of the ACL graft, often at 3–9 months when the patient (still in physiotherapy) loses extension; the graft is intact; treated by excision.
- Posterior cyclops: after a revision PCL reconstruction: new tissue behind the graft not present on the previous MRI; GRE with no blooming excluded PVNS.
- An ACL graft takes about 1–1.5 years to ligamentise; some signal before that is acceptable.

Diffuse mucoid ("celery stalk") Thick, lax ACL with fluid-like mucoid material
between intact fibres; stuffs the notch and causes
locking or block to flexion
Mixed Both

- Usually over 40 years; younger patients usually have a dysplastic knee (trochlear dysplasia, high tibial slope).
- Ultrasound-guided fenestration: approach through the medial gastrocnemius belly (avoiding the popliteal vessels laterally and the tendons medially); local anaesthetic, then multiple passes with a 16–18 G needle until the feel changes from mushy to gritty; inject steroid and local anaesthetic. Needs a big enough, not too deep bulge and a slim knee.

### Participant Cases
- 43-year-old woman, recurrent synovitis, negative inflammatory markers: high-grade patellofemoral arthritis, patella alta, trochlear dysplasia with lateral condyle and inferior patella oedema: maltracking causing early degenerative synovitis. Aspirate and analyse the fluid if inflammation is still suspected.
- 61-year-old woman, painful knee with fixed flexion: discoid lateral meniscus; medial meniscus posterior root tear with extrusion and a complex posterior horn tear; medial femoral and tibial marrow oedema from bone-on-bone loading (can lead to subchondral insufficiency fracture).

### Exam & Viva Pearls
- Lateral femoral condyle + inferomedial patella contusions = transient patellar dislocation.
- TT-TG measured from the deepest trochlear groove to the tibial tuberosity.

<!-- page 6 -->

- Isolated popliteus tears do not happen: look for FCL, ALL and cruciate injury.
- Look for a lateral meniscus root tear in every ACL tear.
- Deficient popliteomeniscal fascicles = hypermobile lateral meniscus.
- CT for PCL avulsion fragment assessment; check the popliteal artery.
- Loss of extension months after ACL reconstruction with an intact graft = cyclops lesion.
- Celery-stalk ACL = diffuse mucoid degeneration; LET leaves an expected ITB defect.

### Take-Home Summary
- Know the normal grey ACL, buckled PCL, popliteus course and meniscal fascicles so you do not over-call tears.
- Follow every major injury to its partners (patellar dislocation: MPFL and fragments; cruciate tears: roots, corners, vessels).
- Post-operative knees need an understanding of the operation (tunnels, LET) and artefact- reducing MRI when there is a lot of metal.
