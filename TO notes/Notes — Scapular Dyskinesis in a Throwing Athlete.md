OVERVIEW
Scapular Dyskinesis (SD):
Abnormal position or motion of the scapula during shoulder movement, resulting in
altered shoulder mechanics, pain, and dysfunction.
Dr. Shikha’s Quote:
“The scapula is the steering wheel of the shoulder — if it’s off-track,
performance and stability both suffer.”
Common in: Throwing athletes (baseball, cricket, volleyball, javelin, tennis).
Incidence: Seen in 61–100% of athletes with shoulder injury.

NORMAL SCAPULAR KINEMATICS
### At Rest (Neutral Position):

| Parameter | Normal Range |
|---|---|
| Internal rotation | 30–45° |
| Upward rotation | 5–10° |
| Anterior tilt | 10–15° |
| Vertebral border position | 2–3 cm from spine |
| Scapular plane | 30–45° anterior to frontal plane |

### During Arm Elevation:
- Upward rotation: ~60° (with 120° humeral elevation).

- Posterior tilt: 20° for clearance under acromion.

- External rotation: 15–20° to avoid impingement. Scapulohumeral rhythm: 2:1 → 120° humeral + 60° scapular motion.

### BIOMECHANICAL ROLE OF SCAPULA
1. Stable base for humeral movement.

2. Link in the kinetic chain – transfers energy from trunk to arm.

3. Shock absorber during deceleration.

4. Maintains glenoid orientation for optimal muscle function.

5. Facilitates elevation by upward rotation & posterior tilt.

| Muscle | Function |
|---|---|
| Serratus anterior | Upward rotation, posterior tilt |
| Lower trapezius | Depression, upward rotation |
| Upper trapezius | Elevation, upward rotation |
| Rhomboids & Levator scapulae | Downward rotation, retraction |
| Pectoralis minor | Anterior tilt, internal rotation |

SCAPULAR DYSKINESIS – DEFINITION & MECHANISM
Definition:
Abnormal static position and/or dynamic movement of the scapula, often secondary to
muscle imbalance, fatigue, or kinetic chain dysfunction.
Primary Mechanisms: 1. Muscle Imbalance: Weak serratus anterior/lower trap; tight
pectoralis minor or levator scapulae.
2. Glenohumeral Instability: Increased demand on stabilizers → dysrhythmia.
3. Thoracic Kyphosis / Postural Faults: Alter resting scapular alignment.
4. Kinetic Chain Disruption: Lower limb or core dysfunction → compensatory scapular movement. Important: SD is often a symptom, not a diagnosis — treat underlying pathology.

SICK SCAPULA SYNDROME
SICK = Scapular malposition, Inferior medial border prominence, Coracoid
pain/malposition, and dysKinesis of scapular movement.

| Component | Description |
|---|---|
| S | Scapular malposition (inferior medial border prominence) |

<!-- page 3 -->

| Component | Description |
|---|---|
| I | Inferior angle prominence due to downward rotation |
| C | Coracoid pain/malposition from tight pectoralis minor |
| K | Kinesis abnormality during arm elevation |

Typical Features: - Prominent inferomedial border.
- Protracted scapula with anterior tilt.
- Tight pectoralis minor; weak lower trap/serratus anterior.
- Shoulder fatigue, discomfort, or dead-arm feeling.
Note: Seen commonly in throwing athletes due to repetitive
acceleration-deceleration stress.

### ETIOLOGY IN THROWING ATHLETES
1. Repetitive Overhead Motion – eccentric overload during deceleration.

<!-- page 4 -->

2. Posterior Capsule Tightness – limits internal rotation, promotes anterior tilt.

3. Kinetic Chain Fatigue – hip, trunk, or scapular stabilizer weakness.

4. Nerve Injury:
  - Long thoracic nerve → serratus anterior palsy (winging).

  - Spinal accessory nerve → trapezius weakness.
Associated Lesions:
- Internal impingement.
- SLAP tear.
- GIRD (Glenohumeral Internal Rotation Deficit).
- Subacromial impingement.

CLINICAL ASSESSMENT
Observation:
- Static: Medial border prominence, asymmetry, anterior tilt.

- Dynamic: Dysrhythmia during abduction or lowering arm.
Palpation:
- Coracoid tenderness (pectoralis minor tightness).

- Inferomedial border prominence.

REHABILITATION PRINCIPLES
### 1⃣ Stretch Tight Muscles

| Muscle | Technique |
|---|---|
| Pectoralis minor | Doorway or corner stretch |
| Levator scapulae | Contralateral flexion + rotation stretch |
| Posterior capsule | Cross-body adduction stretch |

### 2⃣ Strengthen Weak Muscles

| Muscle | Exercise |
|---|---|
| Serratus anterior | Push-up plus, dynamic hugs |
| Lower trapezius | Prone Y raises, wall slides |
| Rhomboids | Prone retraction, rows |
| Rotator cuff | External rotation with bands |

### 3⃣ Core & Kinetic Chain Integration
- Emphasize closed-chain exercises initially.

- Include hip and trunk control drills.

- Progress to open-chain overhead activities (throwing motion simulation). Principle: “Rehabilitate proximal stability to achieve distal mobility.”
### RETURN-TO-PLAY (RTP) CRITERIA

| Criteria | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Full, pain-free ROM | No scapular winging or asymmetry |
| Strength symmetry | ≥90% of contralateral side |
| No kinetic chain deficit | Proper trunk–hip–shoulder coordination |
| Functional test | Negative SAT/SRT |

Tip: Always compare to pre-season baseline video for overhead athletes.

<!-- page 6 -->

### COMPLICATIONS / CONSEQUENCES
- Chronic impingement or labral pathology.

- Fatigue-related instability.

- Decreased throwing velocity or accuracy.

- Persistent shoulder pain despite cuff rehab. Dr. Shikha’s Note: “Fix the scapula, and the shoulder often heals itself.”
### EXAM & VIVA PEARLS
- Definition: Altered scapular motion or position = Scapular Dyskinesis.

- Kibler Classification: Type I – Inferomedial winging; Type II – Medial; Type III – Superior shrug.

- SICK Scapula: Scapular malposition + Inferior angle + Coracoid pain + Dyskinesis.

- Tests: SAT (assist), SRT (retract), Lateral slide (>1.5 cm abnormal).

- Primary muscles: Serratus anterior, Trapezius (lower), Rhomboids.

- Rehab: Stretch–Strengthen–Stabilize (kinetic chain focus).

- Key concept: Treat the cause, not the scapula.

### TAKE-HOME SUMMARY
- Scapular Dyskinesis is a kinetic chain disorder, not just a shoulder problem.

- Common in throwing athletes due to repetitive eccentric stress.

- SICK scapula syndrome represents functional imbalance and malposition.

- Early identification + targeted rehab prevents secondary impingement or labral injury. Dr. Shikha’s Closing Line: “Train the scapula like an athlete — stability, rhythm, and timing are everything.”
