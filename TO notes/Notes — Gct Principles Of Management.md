Giant Cell Tumor – Principles of Management –
Dr. Rajat Gupta
Overview
Giant Cell Tumor (GCT) is a benign but locally aggressive bone tumor, classically
arising in skeletally mature individuals. It can cause extensive local destruction and
may rarely metastasize to the lungs.
Faculty Remark: “GCT is not synonymous with curettage. Poor case selection and
poor technique, not the tumor, are the real reasons for recurrence.”

## Epidemiology & Basics
### Key Features
- Benign aggressive lesion.
- Age group: 20 to 40 years most common.
- Slight female predominance.
- More common in Indian and Chinese populations.
- Can metastasize to lungs in 2 percent, usually in recurrent cases.
- Can be multicentric in 2 percent: synchronous or metachronous.
### Common Sites
- Distal femur (most common).
- Proximal tibia.
- Distal radius.
- Can occur in any bone.

## Radiological Evaluation
X Ray Assessment
Use the standard seven point bone tumor approach: - Age: skeletally mature. -
Location: epiphyseal most common (epiphyseal metaphyseal in immature skeleton). -
Type: geographic lytic lesion. - Matrix: absent. - Zone of transition: narrow. -
Periosteal reaction: typically none. - Soft tissue extension: may be present.

### Typical Features
- Expansile lytic lesion.
- Soap bubble appearance.
- No sclerotic rim.

### Campanacci Grading

| Grade | Radiographic Appearance |
|---|---|
| 1 | Well marginated border |
| 2 | Expanded cortex, cortex intact |
| 3 | Cortical breach with soft tissue mass |

## Differential Diagnosis
- Aneurysmal Bone Cyst (ABC): younger age, fluid fluid levels, no solid component.
- Chondroblastoma: epiphyseal, younger patients.
- Clear cell chondrosarcoma: epiphyseal, sclerotic rim possible.
- Brown tumor: always check serum calcium.
- Giant cell rich osteosarcoma: must biopsy before curettage.

## MRI Features
- Epi metaphyseal, eccentric.
- T1: hypointense.
- T2: hyper or mixed intensity.
- Extraosseous extension possible.
- Fluid levels may indicate secondary ABC.
- Always get whole bone MRI screening to rule out skip lesions.

## Workup Protocol
### Blood Investigations
- Serum calcium: rule out hyperparathyroidism (Brown tumor).
### Biopsy Principles
- A report of “giant cell rich lesion” is not GCT.
- Must confirm GCT of bone on pathology.
- Target solid component under MRI guidance.

## Treatment Overview
Management includes surgical and non surgical modalities.

## Extended Curettage – Principles and Steps
Indications
- Adequate residual bone present.
- Adequate clearance possible.

### Steps of Extended Curettage
- Aim is joint preservation with low recurrence risk.

1. Adequate Exposure
- Atraumatic soft tissue handling.
- Lift muscle via intramuscular septum when possible.
- Isolate area with hydrogen peroxide mops.
- Remove soft tissue component en bloc.
2. Curetting and Burring
- Make a small window first, then enlarge to prevent tumor spillage.
- Scoop out tumor thoroughly.
- High speed burr to remove additional 1–2 mm of bone.
- Burr is a prerequisite, not an adjuvant.
- Break ridges and crevices.
3. Pulsed Lavage
- Flush out debris and microscopic tumor cells.
4. Adjuvants

| Adjuvant | Penetration | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Liquid nitrogen | 14 mm | Risk of fracture, nerve injury |
| Phenol | <1 mm | Must wash with ethanol |
| Argon beam | 4 mm | Thermal effect |
| Hydrogen peroxide | contact based | Always used |

None of the adjuvants replace good burring.

5. Reconstruction
Before reconstructing: - Change gloves. - Change drapes. - Change instruments. -
Prevent cross contamination. - Prefer harvesting bone graft before curettage.

## Reconstruction Options
### Bone Graft
- Biological.
- Donor site morbidity.
- Longer non weight bearing.

Cement
- Hard to detect recurrence.

- Easily available.
- Helps detect recurrence.
- Heat may kill residual tumor cells.
- No proven increase in osteoarthritis.

### Sandwich Reconstruction
- Subchondral bone, not cement, predicts arthritis risk.

- Subchondral bone graft + cement.
- Useful where subchondral bone is minimal.

## Resection – Indications
- Pathological fracture with deformity.
- Recurrent tumor with joint damage.
- No residual subchondral bone.
- Expendable bones: distal ulna, proximal fibula, proximal radius.
- Resection must be en bloc in one piece to prevent recurrence.
### Reconstruction After Resection
- Distal radius: fibular arthroplasty or ulnar translocation.
- Ulnar translocation preferred when stability is needed.
- Fibula arthroplasty allows wrist motion but is unstable.

## Non Surgical Treatments
Angioembolization
- Reduces vascularity.
- Helpful prior to curettage in areas without tourniquet.
- Can be combined with zoledronic acid in unresectable tumors.

<!-- page 5 -->

### Zoledronic Acid
- Causes osteoclast apoptosis.
- May lower recurrence risk by killing stromal cells.

Denosumab
- Useful as adjuvant or in unresectable cases.

- RANKL inhibitor.
- Dose: 120 mg at day 1, 8, 15 then monthly.
- Reduces vascularity and pain.
- Facilitates resection by forming a hardened rim.
### Cautions with Denosumab
- Does not kill stromal cells.
- May increase recurrence after curettage.
- Not useful as adjuvant to reduce recurrence.
- Must inform pathologist to avoid misdiagnosis of low grade sarcoma.
- Rare reports of sarcomatous transformation.

### Exam Pearls
- GCT is epiphyseal in skeletally mature patients.
- Soap bubble appearance, no sclerotic rim.
- Campanacci grade 3 has cortical breach.
- Primary ABC shows only fluid levels, no solid component.
- Brown tumor must be ruled out with serum calcium.
- Denosumab increases recurrence risk when followed by curettage.
- Burr is mandatory for extended curettage.
- Cement does not increase osteoarthritis independently.

Final Faculty Insight: “Recurrence in GCT is not fate. It is almost always poor case
selection or poor technique. Do the basics right and GCT behaves predictably.”
