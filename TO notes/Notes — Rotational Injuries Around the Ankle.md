Target Ortho Quick Revision Notes – Rotational Injuries Around the Ankle
Faculty: Dr. Rahul Upadhyay

### OVERVIEW
- Ankle fractures are the most common intra-articular fractures of weight-bearing joints.
- Even 1 mm talar shift causes 42% loss of tibiotalar contact area (Ramsey & Hamilton study) → accelerates arthritis.
- Goal: Anatomic reduction, joint congruity, early mobilization, and prevention of arthritis.
- Frequently underestimated due to perceived simplicity → results in malunions and post-traumatic osteoarthritis. “A millimeter of malreduction can cost the joint a lifetime of pain.” – Dr. Rahul Upadhyay

### ANATOMY OF THE ANKLE
- Type: Modified hinge joint allowing sagittal and rotational motion.
- Bones: Tibia, Fibula, Talus.
- Movements:
  - Sagittal – Plantarflexion & Dorsiflexion.
  - Coronal/Axial – Inversion, Eversion, Rotation.

RADIOLOGY & IMAGING
Ottawa Ankle Rules
Radiographs required if:
1. Pain in malleolar region AND
2. Bony tenderness at posterior edge/tip of malleolus OR
3. Inability to bear weight immediately and in ER.
### Standard Views
- AP, Lateral, Mortise, Oblique.
- Full-length tibia-fibula view: To rule out Maisonneuve fracture (Type C).
- CT scan: Complex or comminuted injuries.
### Syndesmotic Imaging Criteria (AP View)

| Parameter | Normal | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Talocrural Angle | 83° ± 4° | Compare with opposite side |
| Medial Clear Space | ≤4 mm | >4 mm → deltoid rupture |
| Tibiofibular | >10 mm (A–B), <5 | < values = syndesmotic |
| Overlap | mm (B–C) | injury |
| Talar Tilt | ≤2 mm | >2 mm → instability |

AP view is best for evaluating syndesmotic injury.

Unstable fractures (B/C) → always require operative fixation.

### SURGICAL SEQUENCE
1. Fix Fibula first → restores length and rotation.
2. Address Posterior Malleolus → same incision feasible.
3. Fix Medial Malleolus → anti-glide or tension band.
4. Test Syndesmosis → Cotton’s stress test; fix if unstable.

### Syndesmotic Fixation Principles
- Fix all obvious diastasis (Type C).
- Screw: 3.5 mm, 3–4 cm above talar dome, 30° post→ant, parallel to joint line.
- Engage 3 cortices.
- Routine screw removal: 3 months post-op.
- Alternative: Tightrope device (no removal needed). Screw angle more important than foot position (neutral/dorsiflexion acceptable).

### TIMING & SOFT-TISSUE CONSIDERATIONS
- Urgent reduction for dislocations.
- Delay surgery if blisters or edema near incision line.
- Operate after wrinkle sign appears (~10–14 days).

### PREOPERATIVE PLANNING
- Template fracture pattern, plate, and screw positions.
- Plan incision placement to avoid blistered/compromised skin.
- Preferred plates: anatomical distal fibular plate or 1/3 tubular plate (hook plate if distal fragment small). Plate Molding: Provide internal rotation bend to counter external rotation deformity.

### RADIOLOGICAL SIGNS OF INSTABILITY
1. Increased medial clear space.
2. Reduced tibiofibular overlap.
3. Break in ankle mortise arc.
4. Proximal fibular migration.
5. Talar tilt/subluxation.

Stable: Single break in ring.
Unstable: Two or more breaks.

### COMMON INJURY PATTERNS

| Mechanism | Key Features |
|---|---|
| SA | Infrasyndesmotic fibular + medial shear fracture |
| SER | AITFL → fibula → PITFL → medial malleolus |
| PA | Medial side first → syndesmosis → fibula |
| PER | Medial → syndesmosis → high fibula → posterior malleolus |

### ♂ POSTOPERATIVE REHABILITATION

| Phase | Weeks | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| I | 0–4 | Immobilization, NWB |
| II | 4–8 | Partial WB, gentle ROM |
| III | 8–12 | Strengthening, proprioception |
| IV | >12 | Sports and return to activity |

Early mobilization prevents stiffness and CRPS.

### EXAM & VIVA PEARLS
  - Commonest mechanism: Supination–external rotation (SER).
  - Most unstable: Type C (PER/Maisonneuve).
  - Most stable: Type A.
  - Talocrural angle: 83° ± 4°.
  - Medial clear space: ≤4 mm.
  - Cotton test: Assesses syndesmotic integrity.
  - Screw direction: 30° post→ant.
  - Wrinkle sign: Readiness for surgery.
  - Templating: Improves fixation accuracy.
  - CT scan: Not routine; used for post-op reduction assessment.
