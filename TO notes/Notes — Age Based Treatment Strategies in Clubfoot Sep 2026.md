Age Based Treatment Strategies in Clubfoot – Dr.
Shalin Shah (Sep 2026)
### Overview / Key Concepts
- Clubfoot affects about 1 in 1000 children; idiopathic clubfoot is fully treatable (not a reason for termination of pregnancy). Goal: a stable, painless, plantigrade (and braceable) foot.
- Ponseti is first-line at EVERY age, including walking-age, late-presenting and recurrent feet. Surgery, when needed, is chosen by which COMPONENT of the deformity remains.
- CAVE components: Cavus (forefoot pronation relative to the hindfoot), Adductus (forefoot medially deviated on the hindfoot), Varus (heel tilt and inversion), Equinus (ankle plantarflexion).

### Ponseti vs Kite
Kite (sequential) Ponseti (simultaneous)

| Fulcrum | Calcaneocuboid joint / cuboid | Head of the TALUS |
|---|---|---|
| Order | Corrects components one by one (adduction before equinus) | Cavus, adductus and varus corrected TOGETHER; equinus last |
| Result | Rigid midtarsal joints, rocker- bottom deformity | Mobile, plantigrade foot |

- Acetabulum pedis: the talus has no muscle attachments and is mostly articular cartilage, so it stays still while the calcaneo-pedal unit rotates around the talar head (like the femoral head in the acetabulum).
- Common misunderstanding: cavus does not have to be corrected first in a separate cast. As long as the forefoot is kept in SUPINATION (first ray raised), the foot can be abducted in the same cast; fewer casts are needed.
- Never press on the calcaneus during casting; the thumb stays over the talar head (fulcrum discipline).
- End point of abduction: reduction of the talonavicular joint (navicular comes lateral to and covers the talar head; head no longer felt). Usually 50 to 70 degrees of abduction, but in an atypical foot it may be as little as 20 degrees. Do not do the tenotomy before the foot is past the midline.

### Tendo Achilles Tenotomy
- Equinus is NOT corrected by casting. About 80 to 90% of early-presenting feet need a percutaneous tenotomy to reach 15 to 20 degrees of dorsiflexion.
- Before tenotomy: midfoot contracture score (curved lateral border, medial crease, talar head coverage; each 0, 0.5 or 1) should be about zero.
- Technique: under local anaesthesia (general anaesthesia in older children), from MEDIAL to lateral (main neurovascular bundle is medial); the PERONEAL artery is the one more commonly injured. Feel the loss of resistance, sudden increase in dorsiflexion, "pop". Cast for 3 weeks.
- Common mistake: skipping the tenotomy because of spurious dorsiflexion through a lax midfoot/forefoot. Lock the subtalar joint by inverting the foot and judge true ANKLE dorsiflexion of the heel; without about 20 degrees of real dorsiflexion, do the tenotomy.
- A first-time percutaneous tenotomy works at any age (healing confirmed on ultrasound even at 8 to 10 years; the intact paratenon allows healing). A REPEAT tenotomy rarely works unless only about 10 degrees is needed: then do an open Z-lengthening.

Bracing
- Foot abduction brace (e.g. Steenbeek, Denis Browne bar): 23 hours a day for the first 3 months after casting, then 12 to 14 hours (night and naps) until 4 years of age (4 to 5 years).
- Recurrence is HIGHEST during the bracing phase; bracing compliance is the single biggest factor in keeping the foot corrected. Show parents what recurrence looks like and explain that casting may not work later.
- Special "CTEV shoes" with a straight inner border while walking are traditional, but NOT part of the Ponseti protocol; walking children need no special shoes.

### Types of Clubfoot (Definitions)

| Type | Definition |
|---|---|
| Untreated (preferred to "neglected") | Never treated and presenting after walking age (over about 18 months to 2 years). "Neglected" is avoided because it blames parents; lack of resources/knowledge is the usual cause. |
| Recurrent | Corrected by treatment, then the deformity returns. Causes: brace failure, short tendo Achilles (equinus recurs FIRST/most often), overactive tibialis anterior. |
| Resistant | Any component cannot be corrected during treatment (e.g. cannot abduct beyond neutral; talonavicular joint will not reduce). Causes: atypical clubfoot (needs modified Ponseti), tarsal coalition, syndromic feet. |
| Atypical / complex | Primary problem is severe cavus with all metatarsals flexed; treated with the modified Ponseti technique. |
| Syndromic | Arthrogryposis, myelomeningocele, diastrophic dysplasia: rigid, bound to recur. |

### Dynamic Supination vs Relapse
- 30 to 50% of corrected children at 4 to 5 years walk on the LATERAL border of the foot (inversion in swing) from an overactive tibialis anterior with weak peronei.
- Differentiate: in dynamic supination the foot is NORMAL when standing (static) and the problem appears only in gait. A static deformity means relapse.
- Treatment: physiotherapy first (train peronei, centred dorsiflexion); if it fails and the child is over 4 years, tibialis anterior tendon transfer (TATT) from its medial insertion to the LATERAL cuneiform.
- In-toeing from internal tibial torsion is common after clubfoot; most resolves with growth; surgery only considered after 8 to 10 years.

### Late (Walking-Age) and Untreated Presentation
- First treatment is still Ponseti casting. Case: untreated clubfoot at 5 years corrected with casts and a tenotomy (under general anaesthesia), giving a plantigrade, painless foot.
- Recurrence in older children: 6 months to 2 years is the hardest age to cast; children over 2 years tolerate casting better. The speaker limits recurrent casting to about 3 casts (prolonged non-walking weakens bone and muscle), then corrects what remains surgically.
- Exam answer for a relapse: start with Ponseti casting; JESS can be used instead in a child who cannot comply with casting (it applies Ponseti principles with a fixator).

### Surgery by Component (A La Carte Approach)

| Component left | Procedure |
|---|---|
| Equinus | First time: percutaneous tenotomy (any age). Recurrent/scarred: open TA Z-lengthening +/- posterior capsulotomy of the ankle (above the talus) and subtalar joint (below the talus). |
| Adductus (medial column short, lateral column long) | Soft tissue: superficial then deep plantar-medial release. Bone: lateral column SHORTENING (calcaneocuboid resection/fusion = Dillwyn Evans; joint-sparing cuboid decancellation or closing-wedge cuboid osteotomy) or double tarsal osteotomy (medial cuneiform opening wedge + cuboid closing wedge) for the "kidney-bean" foot. |
| Varus (hindfoot) | Dwyer lateral closing-wedge calcaneal osteotomy (minimum age 3 to 4 years; when soft tissue surgery is not possible). |
| Cavus | Plantar fascia release; then medial cuneiform plantar opening-wedge or metatarsal dorsal closing-wedge osteotomy (CORA of cavus near the medial cuneiform). |
| Dynamic supination (over 4 years) | Tibialis anterior transfer to the lateral cuneiform. |
| All components, rigid (older child) | Shingade (modified Japas) osteotomy at the apex in the midtarsus + tendo Achilles lengthening; |

Details of key procedures
- Superficial plantar-medial release: open the laciniate ligament (flexor retinaculum), separate the three origins of abductor hallucis from the medial and lateral plantar vessels and nerves, slide its origin distally, divide plantar fascia/intrinsics. Only if not enough: deep release of the talonavicular capsule (dorsal to plantar), long plantar and spring (plantar calcaneonavicular) ligaments, with Z-lengthening of tibialis posterior.
- Posteromedial soft tissue release (PMSTR): Turco, Cincinnati (circumferential) or double incisions (choice is surgeon preference); releases the knot of Henry (where FHL and FDL cross), lengthens tibialis posterior and FHL, opens and pins the talonavicular joint. Gives good early correction, but more than 50% develop midfoot arthritis at 10 to 15 years, so it is now avoided except for the posterior release.
- Evans terminology trap: in clubfoot the (Dillwyn) Evans procedure SHORTENS the lateral column (calcaneocuboid resection/fusion); in adult flatfoot the Evans osteotomy LENGTHENS the lateral column (calcaneal lengthening).
- Dwyer osteotomy vs midfoot osteotomy: the CORA of adductus lies in the midfoot (where the heel bisector and the second metatarsal axis meet). Correcting at the calcaneus (Dwyer) instead can leave a Z-shaped (skew) foot.
- Lichtblau procedure (slide): for a long lateral column; advantage: no long-term stiffness; complication: Z-foot/skew foot.
- Persistent CAVUS recurrence: think NEUROLOGICAL (tethered cord, myelomeningocele, spinal dysraphism, cerebral palsy/hemiplegia, Charcot-Marie-Tooth); rule out spinal pathology.
- JESS (BB Joshi external stabilisation system) / Ilizarov: gradual distraction of the soft tissues without osteotomy, same Ponseti order (equinus last); for severe neglected feet or rigid recurrences; skill dependent, painful, multiple pins.

- Talectomy (astragalectomy): salvage for rigid, uncorrectable recurrence, especially arthrogryposis (muscles fibrotic or absent); gives a stable, plantigrade, shorter foot with the tibia on the calcaneus.
- Triple arthrodesis: for a painful arthritic foot (e.g. after PMSTR); correct the deformity first or at the same time (e.g. with a Shingade-type closing wedge), then fuse the talonavicular, calcaneocuboid and subtalar joints.

### Syndromic Clubfoot
- Arthrogryposis (amyoplasia), myelomeningocele, diastrophic dysplasia: rigid fibrotic tissues, poor response to casting alone, recurrence is the disease, not failure of treatment.
- Higher threshold for intervention; aim for a BRACEABLE plantigrade foot, not a normal- looking one; expect lifelong (often 24-hour, including walking) bracing and staged corrections.

### Exam & Viva Pearls
- Ponseti fulcrum = talar head; Kite = calcaneocuboid joint (causes rocker-bottom).
- Cavus, adductus, varus corrected together with the forefoot supinated; equinus last.
- 80 to 90% need tenotomy; medial to lateral; peroneal artery at risk; cast 3 weeks.
- Brace 23 h/day for 3 months, then nights and naps until 4 years; most relapses occur in this phase.
- Equinus recurs first; cavus recurrence suggests a neurological cause.
- Dynamic supination (foot normal when standing) = TATT to the lateral cuneiform after 4 years.
- Evans for clubfoot shortens the lateral column; Evans for flatfoot lengthens it.
- Dwyer = lateral closing-wedge calcaneal osteotomy for heel varus (age 3 to 4 years or more).
- Ponseti first at any age, even for walking-age and relapsed feet.

### Take-Home Summary
- Start every clubfoot, at any age, with Ponseti casting around the talar head, tenotomy for equinus and strict bracing; when something persists or recurs, identify the exact component (equinus, adductus, varus, cavus, dynamic supination) and choose the matching targeted procedure, keeping big releases, fixators and talectomy for rigid, neglected or syndromic feet.
