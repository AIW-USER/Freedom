Nerve Palsies Tip to Toe – Ulnar Nerve –
Dr. Priyanka Sharma
Overview / Key Concepts
The ulnar nerve, known as the musician’s nerve, is responsible for fine motor
control and intrinsic hand function. Ulnar nerve palsy leads to characteristic clawing,
intrinsic muscle wasting, loss of key pinch, and multiple classical clinical signs.
Understanding anatomy, level of lesion, ulnar paradox, and reconstructive options
is critical for exams and clinical practice.

Anatomy
### Root Value and Origin
- Root value: (C7) C8–T1
- Arises from medial cord of brachial plexus
- Primarily anterior divisions of C8–T1
### Course in Arm
- Lies between axillary artery (lateral) and axillary vein (medial)
- Descends along medial arm
- No muscular branches in the arm
- At mid-arm pierces medial intermuscular septum to enter posterior compartment
### Cubital Tunnel
- Passes posterior to elbow through cubital tunnel
- Tunnel formed between medial epicondyle and olecranon
- Gives an articular branch to elbow joint
- Common site of compression
### Course in Forearm
- Pierces two heads of flexor carpi ulnaris (FCU)
- Runs between FCU and FDP
### Branches in Forearm
- Muscular branch: FCU and FDP to ring and little finger
- Palmar cutaneous branch: ulnar half of palm
- Dorsal cutaneous branch: dorsal ulnar one and half fingers

### Course at Wrist and Hand
- Lies superficial to flexor retinaculum
- Enters hand via Guyon’s canal (ulnar tunnel)
- Divides into:
  - Superficial branch (sensory + palmaris brevis)
  - Deep motor branch (intrinsic muscles)

Motor Innervation
Forearm
- Flexor carpi ulnaris
- FDP to ring and little finger
### Hand (Deep Branch)
- Hypothenar muscles (ADM, FDM, ODM)
- Medial two lumbricals
- All interossei (3 palmar, 4 dorsal)
- Adductor pollicis
- Deep head of FPB
Exception: Palmaris brevis supplied by superficial branch

### Guyon’s Canal
- Bounded by pisiform medially
- Roof formed by palmar carpal ligament
- Common site of compression after cubital tunnel
- Palmar cutaneous branch arises proximal (4–6 cm)

### Etiology of Ulnar Nerve Palsy
- Trauma
- Inflammatory conditions
- Leprosy
- Occupational hazards
- Cubital tunnel syndrome
- Guyon’s canal compression
- Tumors

Clinical Signs of Ulnar Nerve Palsy
### Duchenne Claw Sign (Ulnar Claw)
- Hyperextension at MCP and flexion at IP joints of ring and little fingers
- Due to lumbrical paralysis with overaction of extrinsic extensors
### Andre–Thomas Sign
- Wrist flexion with attempted IP extension
- Demonstrates tenodesis effect
### Bouvier Sign
- Passive MCP hyperextension → IP extension not possible
- Passive MCP flexion → IP extension possible
- Indicates supple joints, guides claw correction strategy
### Froment Sign
- Hyperflexion of thumb IP during key pinch
- Compensation by FPL due to adductor pollicis palsy
### Bunnell O Sign
- Inability to form a round O between thumb and index finger
- Loss of adductor pollicis
### Wartenberg Sign
- Persistent abduction of little finger
- Overaction of extensor digiti minimi
### Pollock Sign
- Inability to flex DIP of ring and little finger
- FDP palsy
### Sunderland Sign
- Inability to oppose little finger to thumb
- Hypothenar muscle paralysis
### Masse Sign
- Flattened transverse metacarpal arch
- Loss of hypothenar bulk
### Jeanne Sign
- Hyperextension of thumb MCP during pinch
- Seen in chronic palsy

<!-- page 4 -->

### Inverted Pyramid Sign
- Hollowing of thenar eminence
- Chronic adductor pollicis wasting
### Confrontation Sign
- Weak hand pushed during resisted abduction

### Ulnar Paradox
- Higher lesion → less clawing
- FDP to ring and little finger also paralyzed
- Less IP flexion, hence less claw deformity

Nerve Transfers
### Motor Transfer
- AIN → deep motor branch of ulnar nerve (forearm level)
- Restores intrinsic muscle function
### Sensory Transfer
- Topography: SMS (Sensory–Motor–Sensory) from medial to lateral
- 3rd webspace median nerve fascicle → ulnar sensory component
- End-to-side dorsal cutaneous branch transfer
- Mandatory release of Guyon’s canal and carpal tunnel

Intrinsic Positions
### Intrinsic Plus Position
- MCP flexion, IP extension
- Ideal splinting position
### Intrinsic Minus (Claw Hand)
- MCP hyperextension, IP flexion
- Seen in ulnar palsy, median palsy, Volkmann ischemia, leprosy

### Tendon Transfers – Principles
- Supple joints
- Dispensable donor
- Correct route and pulley
- Proper tensioning and immobilization

Tendon Transfers for Claw Correction
### Static Procedures
- Zancolli capsulodesis
- Omer modification
- Tenodesis (Parkes, Fowler, Riordan)
Dynamic Procedures
### Modified Stiles–Bunnell (Littler)
- Low ulnar palsy
- Ring finger FDS split into two
- Inserted into radial lateral bands of ring and little finger
### Zancolli Lasso
- Low ulnar palsy
- FDS looped around A1 pulley
### Brand Transfers
- EE4T: ECRB via extensor route
- EF4T: ECRL via flexor route
- PF4T: Palmaris longus (weak donor)
### Palande Transfer
- Direct interossei activation
- Ring finger FDS split into 5 slips
- Restores adduction and abduction

Other Functional Transfers
### Little Finger Deviation
- Fowler modification of EDM
### Thumb Adduction (Key Pinch)
- Smith transfer (ECRB + graft)
- Littler (Ring FDS)
### Index Finger Abduction
- Bunnell (EIP)
- Bruner (EPB)

### High Ulnar Nerve Palsy
- FDP and FCU involved

<!-- page 6 -->

- Side-to-side FDP tagging
- FDS middle finger to FDP ring and little
- FCR to FCU transfer if required

Combined Median and Ulnar Nerve Palsy
### Low Combined Palsy
- Prevent first web contracture
- Index abduction (APL)
- Thumb adduction (ECRB)
- Opponensplasty (EIP)
- Claw correction last
### High Combined Palsy
- Restore finger flexion first
- ECRL → finger flexion
- Brachioradialis → FPL
- Static claw correction
- FDMA flap for thumb sensation

Faculty Quote
“In ulnar nerve palsy, understanding the claw is not enough. You must understand why it
is less in proximal lesions.” – Dr. Priyanka Sharma

### Exam Pearls
- Ulnar nerve is the musician’s nerve
- Cubital tunnel and Guyon’s canal are common compression sites
- FDP involvement explains ulnar paradox
- Froment and Wartenberg signs are high-yield
- Bouvier sign guides claw correction choice
- EE4T and EF4T are commonly asked dynamic transfers
- Palande transfer restores true intrinsic function
