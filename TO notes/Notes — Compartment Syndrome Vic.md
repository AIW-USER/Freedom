Compartment Syndrome & Volkmann Ischemic Contracture –
Dr. Anurag Tiwari

### Key Concepts / Overview
- Compartment Syndrome is an orthopedic emergency — diagnosis is clinical and management must be immediate.
- Delay >12–24 hours can lead to irreversible ischemia, amputation, or Volkmann Ischemic Contracture (VIC).
- VIC is the end-stage sequel of untreated or partially treated compartment syndrome, causing fibrosis and contracture of muscles.
Dr. Anurag’s Remark:
“Compartment syndrome is a clinical diagnosis. When in doubt — perform fasciotomy.
Waiting for investigations can cost the limb.”

Definition
- Compartment Syndrome: Elevation of interstitial pressure in a closed osteofascial compartment to a level that compromises the circulation and function of the tissues within it.

Etiopathogenesis
### Common Causes

| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| Decreased compartment size | Tight cast/bandage, circumferential burn eschar |
| Increased compartment content | Fracture hematoma, edema (reperfusion, crush injury), snake bite, bleeding disorders |

Eaton & Green Ischemia-Edema Cycle
Trauma → Arterial spasm/occlusion → Ischemia → Capillary permeability ↑ → Edema ↑

→ Intramuscular pressure ↑ → Further ischemia.
A vicious ischemia–edema cycle leading to necrosis.

### Pathophysiological Theories
1. Critical Closing Pressure Theory (Laplace’s Law):
  - Transmural Pressure (Tm) = Tension (Tc) / Radius (r)

<!-- page 2 -->

- Once interstitial pressure exceeds the vessel wall tension, capillary collapse occurs → ischemia.
2. AV Gradient Theory:
  - Blood flow ∝ (Pa − Pv) / R.
  - ↑Interstitial pressure → ↑Venous pressure → ↓AV gradient → ↓Perfusion → Ischemia.

Clinical Presentation
### The Six Ps

| Stage | Clinical Feature | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| Early | Pain out of proportion | Most sensitive sign; pain not relieved by analgesics. |
| Early | Pain on passive stretch | Diagnostic of impending compartment syndrome. |
| Intermediate | Paresthesia | Due to nerve ischemia. |
| Late | Pallor | Indicates arterial compromise. |
| Late | Paralysis | Suggests irreversible muscle ischemia. |
| Very Late | Pulselessness | Terminal stage; large vessel compression. |

Key Differentiation: Distal pulses may still be palpable in early stages — presence of
pulse does not rule out compartment syndrome.

Diagnosis
- Clinical – most important.
- Pressure Measurement indicated in:
  - Unconscious / polytrauma patients.
  - Tourniquet palsy / peripheral nerve injury.

| Method | Description |
|---|---|
| Whitesides Needle | Uses mercury manometer, syringe, 3-way |
| Method | stopcock; simplest ward method. |

### Diagnostic Thresholds

| Parameter | Fasciotomy Indicated When |
|---|---|
| Absolute Pressure | >30 mmHg |
| Delta Pressure (DBP − ICP) | <30 mmHg (more sensitive) |

Timing:
- Ideal – within 12 hours.
- After 24 hours, fasciotomy is usually contraindicated due to risk of infection and necrosis.

Management
Principles
- Remove external compression (splint/cast/bandage).

- Keep limb at heart level (not elevated excessively).

- Immediate fasciotomy if clinical signs present.

| Forearm & | Lazy S incision – volar aspect | May add dorsal |

### Chronic Exertional Compartment Syndrome (CECS)
- Reversible muscle ischemia induced by exercise.
- Seen in runners and athletes.
### Clinical Features
- Tightness and pain after 15–20 min of running, relieved within 15–20 min of rest.
- Paresthesia in the involved nerve territory.
### Pedowitz Diagnostic Criteria

| Timing | Pressure (mmHg) |
|---|---|
| Pre-exercise | >15 |
| 1 min post-exercise | >30 |
| 5 min post-exercise | >20 |

Treatment

| Type | Management |
|---|---|
| Conservative | Rest, NSAIDs, physiotherapy (stretching & strengthening). |
| Surgical | Mini-incision fasciotomy if symptoms or pressures persist. |

Complications

| Untreated Compartment Syndrome | Result |
|---|---|
| Acute ischemia | Muscle necrosis → infection, gangrene, amputation |
| Partial treatment | Volkmann Ischemic |

### Volkmann Ischemic Contracture (VIC)
- Definition: End-stage result of ischemic necrosis → fibrosis and contracture of forearm flexor muscles.
- Most affected: Flexor digitorum profundus (FDP), flexor pollicis longus (FPL), median nerve.
### Pathophysiology – Seddon’s Ellipsoid Concept
- Central portion of muscle (FDP & FPL) most ischemic → necrosis → fibrosis.
### Clinical Features
- Volkmann Sign / Fixed Length Phenomenon: Passive extension of digits not possible when wrist is extended.
- Commonly affected: Ring & middle fingers.
### Tsuge Classification

| Type | Involvement |
|---|---|
| Mild | Partial FDP involvement (1–2 slips) |
| Moderate | All flexors + nerve involvement |
| Severe | Flexors + extensors + skin/joint involvement |

Dr. Anurag’s Tip:
“Once fibrosis has set in, prevention is the only cure — treat acute compartment
syndrome early to avoid a lifetime of disability.”

### Exam / Viva Pearls
- Most sensitive sign – pain on passive stretch.
- Fasciotomy indication – ICP >30 mmHg or ΔP <30 mmHg.
- Two-incision Mubarak technique for leg decompression.
- Pedowitz criteria for CECS.
- Volkmann sign: No finger extension with wrist extension.
- Tsuge classification – mild, moderate, severe.
- Max Page operation – muscle sliding procedure for VIC.

Final Faculty Takeaway:
“Compartment syndrome demands clinical vigilance; VIC is a preventable tragedy.
Recognize early, decompress decisively.”
