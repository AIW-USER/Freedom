Rheumatoid Arthritis Part 1 – Dr. Shikha Dhaundiyal
Overview / Key Concepts
Rheumatoid arthritis is a chronic autoimmune inflammatory disease primarily affecting
synovial joints. Persistent synovitis leads to cartilage destruction, bone erosions, joint
deformity and disability. It also causes significant extra-articular involvement.
Key clinical points: - Symmetrical polyarthritis is most common. - Morning stiffness more
than one hour. - Small joints of the hands and feet typically affected. - Rapid
progression with irreversible damage if untreated. - Predominantly affects women in

Anatomy and Pathophysiology
their 30s to 50s.

### Joint Anatomy (Page 3)
- Articular cartilage acts as a shock absorber and provides friction-free movement.
- Synovial membrane secretes synovial fluid that nourishes cartilage and cushions bone.
- In RA, synovial membrane becomes inflamed and hypertrophic.
### Three Domains of RA Pathobiology (Page 5)
1. Genetic predisposition plus environmental triggers.
2. Self-perpetuating intra-synovial immune response.
3. Tissue injury mediated by cytokines and degradative enzymes.
Histopathology (Pages 6 to 7)
### Synovial Lining
- Early proliferative changes with hypertrophy and hyperplasia.
- Involves Type A (macrophage-like) and Type B (fibroblast-like) cells.
Interstitium
- Dense infiltration of CD4 and CD8 lymphocytes, dendritic cells and antigen-presenting cells.
- Formation resembles lymphoid tissue with germinal centers.
Microvasculature
- Endothelial activation followed by exuberant neovascularization.
- Multinucleated giant cells and plasma cells appear in mature lesions.
### Pannus Formation
- Hyperplastic synovium invades hyaline cartilage and subchondral bone.
- Causes joint destruction and deformity.

Role of Enzymes and Cytokines
### Cathepsins and Matrix Metalloproteinases (Pages 8 to 9)
- TNF alpha and IL 1 beta stimulate synovial cells to produce cathepsin B and K.
- Cathepsin B contributes to subchondral bone destruction.
- MMPs (MMP 1, 3, 8, 9) cause cartilage erosion and joint damage.
- MMP 3 is most closely linked to cartilage destruction.
Pro-inflammatory Cytokines
Main mediators include: - TNF alpha - IL 1 beta - IL 6, IL 8, IL 18 These cytokines

Genetics and Environmental Factors
amplify inflammation, enhance angiogenesis and activate osteoclasts.

### Genetic Associations (Pages 10 to 12)
- Monozygotic twin concordance: 15 to 30 percent.
- Major gene: HLA DR4 via shared epitope hypothesis.
- HLA DRB1 susceptibility alleles highly associated.
Other disease associated genes: - PTPN22 - CTLA4 - STAT4 - TNFAIP3 - CCL21 -
PADI4 (particularly significant in Japanese populations)

### Environmental Triggers (Page 13)
- Smoking
- Gingivitis
- Altered gut microbiota
- Epigenetic changes leading to cytokine overexpression
### Gender Predilection (Page 14)
- Women affected three times more often.
- Proposed explanations include X-chromosome inactivation differences and

Autoimmunity and Immune Cascade
hormonal influences.

### Autoantigen and Citrullination (Pages 15 to 16)
- Citrullination of extracellular proteins (filaggrin, fibrinogen) breaks immune tolerance.
- Autoreactive T and B cells accumulate in synovium.
- Immune complexes deposit in tissues and activate complement.
### Intrasynovial Immune Response (Pages 17 to 19)
- Activated T cells, macrophages and dendritic cells generate a persistent inflammatory cycle.

<!-- page 3 -->

- TNF alpha and IL 1 major drivers of synovitis.

Serology
- Chondrocytes shift to catabolic phase, reducing matrix synthesis.

### Rheumatoid Factor (Page 20)
- Autoantibody against Fc fragment of IgG.
- Present in 75 to 85 percent of RA cases.
- Negative RF in early disease may become positive in two years.
- High titers indicate poor prognosis and more extra-articular disease.
Causes of false positives include Sjogren syndrome, SLE, vasculitis, chronic infections,
liver disease and malignancies (Page 21).

### Anti-CCP Antibodies (Page 22)
- More specific than RF.
- Sensitivity: 65 percent, specificity: 95 percent.
- Predicts erosive disease, radiologic progression and poorer function.

### Clinical Features
- Diagnostic and prognostic value.

### Typical Articular Features (Pages 23 to 25)
- Symmetrical polyarthritis and tenosynovitis.
- Morning stiffness more than one hour.
- Fusiform swelling of joints.
- Pain even at rest.
- Most commonly affected joints: MCP, PIP, MTP, wrist.
### Relative Joint Involvement Frequency (Page 35)
- MCP/PIP/MTP: 90 percent
- Knees, ankle, wrists: 80 percent
- Shoulders: 60 percent
- Elbows: 50 percent
- TMJ, AC, SC joints: 30 percent
### Spine Involvement (Page 36)
- Cervical spine frequently affected, may cause atlanto-axial instability.

### Extra-articular Manifestations
- Thoracolumbar, sacroiliac and DIP joints not involved.

### Summary (Pages 26, 41 to 45)
- Present in 30 to 40 percent of patients.

<!-- page 4 -->

- Associated with severe disease markers: high RF, high anti-CCP, HLA DR4, male sex, smoking.
### Major Systems Involved
- Ocular: keratoconjunctivitis sicca, episcleritis, scleritis.
- Pulmonary: interstitial lung disease, pleural effusion, rheumatoid nodules.
- Cardiac: pericarditis, cardiomyopathy.
- Hematological: anemia, thrombocytosis, Felty syndrome.
- Neurological: peripheral neuropathy, mononeuritis multiplex, cervical myelopathy.
- Vascular: rheumatoid vasculitis, digital gangrene.
- Skin: rheumatoid nodules on pressure points.
### Rheumatoid Nodules (Pages 44 to 46)
- Firm, non tender, movable subcutaneous nodules.
- Common sites: olecranon, extensor tendons, occiput, heels, ischial tuberosity.

### Palindromic Rheumatism (Pages 27 to 28)
- High mortality association with extensive extra-articular disease.

- Recurrent episodic arthritis lasting hours to days.
- No symptoms between attacks.
- No erosions on imaging.
- Equal sex distribution.
- Can mimic early RA.
- Frequently associated with Whipple disease.
### Classification (1992 Guerne and Weismann)
1. Six month history of episodic mono or polyarthritis.
2. At least one attack observed by physician.
3. At least three different joints involved in separate episodes.
4. No radiological erosions.

### Diagnostic Criteria
5. Exclusion of RA, SLE and gout.

1987 ACR Diagnostic Criteria (Page 30)
RA diagnosis requires four of seven criteria (first four for at least six weeks): - Morning
stiffness more than one hour. - Swelling in at least three joint areas. - Swelling in wrist,
MCP or PIP. - Bilateral joint involvement. - Subcutaneous nodules. - Positive rheumatoid
factor. - Radiographic erosions or decalcification.

### 1991 ACR Functional Classification (Page 31)
- Class I: normal activities.
- Class II: limited leisure activities.

<!-- page 5 -->

- Class III: limitations in work and leisure.
- Class IV: limitations even in self care.
2010 ACR/EULAR Criteria (Page 32)
Requires score ≥6 out of 10 using: - Joint involvement. - Serology. - Acute phase
