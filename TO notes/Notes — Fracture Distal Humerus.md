Fracture of Distal Humerus – Dr. Anurag Tiwari

Overview
- Distal humerus fractures occur in both adults and children, with different mechanisms and treatment approaches.
- Adult fractures are usually intra-articular (intercondylar) or extra-articular (epicondylar/supracondylar).
- Pediatric fractures include supracondylar, lateral condyle, medial condyle, and T-shaped distal humerus fractures.
Dr. Anurag’s Remark:
“Distal humerus fractures test a surgeon’s understanding of anatomy and fixation
principles. Early mobilization is the real goal, not just fixation.”

Anatomy Review
### Bony Anatomy

| Structure | Description |
|---|---|
| Trochlea | Medial articular surface articulating with ulna |
| Capitulum | Lateral articular surface articulating with radius |
| Fossae | Coronoid (anterior medial), Radial (anterior lateral), Olecranon (posterior) |
| Columns | Medial and Lateral columns diverging distally to form the condyles |

Definition (AO):
A fracture within a square drawn between the two epicondyles and equal in height to
their distance apart is termed a distal humerus fracture.

### Ligamentous Anatomy
- Lateral Collateral Ligament Complex:
  - Radial Collateral Ligament (lateral epicondyle → annular ligament)
  - Annular Ligament (encircles radial head)
  - Lateral Ulnar Collateral Ligament (lateral epicondyle → supinator crest) → most important for lateral stability
- Medial Collateral Ligament Complex:

<!-- page 2 -->

- Anterior, posterior, and transverse parts (arising from medial epicondyle to ulna)
  - Anterior band provides primary stability medially.
### Neurovascular Structures

| Nerve | Location |
|---|---|
| Radial Nerve | Crosses anteriorly, enters spiral groove ~20 cm above medial epicondyle, exits ~14 cm above lateral epicondyle |
| Ulnar Nerve | Behind medial epicondyle (cubital tunnel) |
| Median Nerve | Anterior aspect of elbow |

Blood Supply: * Rich collateral circulation around the elbow → vascular
compromise is rare.

### Clinical Presentation
- Pain, swelling, deformity, ecchymosis.

<!-- page 3 -->

- Check for skin condition (blisters, puncture wounds).
- Neurological exam: Radial, ulnar, and median nerves.
- Vascular exam: Pulses and capillary refill (pink hand = collateral flow adequate).
- Check for compartment syndrome, especially in children.

Imaging

| Investigation | Purpose |
|---|---|
| X-ray (AP & Lateral) | First-line assessment |
| CT with 3D Reconstruction | For intra-articular comminution, surgical planning, approach decision |

CT Indications: 1. Decision for surgical approach. 2. Evaluate articular
comminution. 3. Decide between ORIF vs. replacement.

Management Principles
Non-Operative
- Rarely indicated.
- Consider only for:
  - Undisplaced fractures
  - Medically unfit patients
- Immobilization → High stiffness risk → Mostly historical.
Operative (Preferred)

| Option | Indication |
|---|---|
| ORIF with Plate | Gold standard for most cases |
| Total Elbow Arthroplasty | Elderly with comminuted, |
| (TEA) | non-reconstructible fractures |
| Hemiarthroplasty | Selected cases with isolated articular loss |

Goal: Anatomical reduction + Rigid fixation + Early mobilization (<3 weeks)

### Surgical Approaches

| Approach | Key Features |
|---|---|
| Posterior (Most common) | Provides exposure for column plating |

| Approach | Key Features |
|---|---|

Nerve Precautions: Always identify & protect ulnar nerve. If extending proximally,
protect radial nerve.

### Reduction Tips
- Anatomic reduction of articular surface is mandatory.
- Avoid trochlear shortening → Use fully threaded screws.
- Avoid screws in olecranon fossa → Prevent impingement & limited ROM.

### Fixation Principles

| Parameter | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Number of Plates | Minimum 2 (bicolumnar), occasionally 3 (triple plating) |
| Configuration | Parallel plating biomechanically superior to orthogonal |
| Implants | Anatomical contoured LCP, extra-articular LCP, reconstruction plates |
| Immobilization Period | <3 weeks, followed by early physiotherapy |

Dr. Anurag’s Insight:
“Parallel locking plates provide superior biomechanical stability compared to 90–90
plating, though technically demanding.”

Treatment
- ORIF with headless Herbert screws.
- Technique of Reduction: Extension + Supination + Varus pressure.
- Screw direction: Posterior → Anterior (avoids articular cartilage violation).

### Lateral Condyle Physis Injury
- Forms: Metaphysis + Lateral Epicondyle + Lateral Condylar Physis + Capitellum + Lateral trochlear crista.
- Mechanism: Push-off (Salter-Harris IV) or Pull-off (Salter-Harris II).
- Milch Classification:
  - Type 1 – Fracture through trochlear physis (Salter-Harris IV)
  - Type 2 – Fracture above physis (Salter-Harris II) → Most common & unstable
Treatment

| Type | Management |
|---|---|
| <2 mm displacement | Non-operative (cast/slab) |
| >2 mm displacement | ORIF (Cocker’s approach) |

Key Points: * Don’t dissect posteriorly → prevents osteonecrosis. * Obtain anatomical
articular reduction. * K-wires (2 or more) – parallel or divergent configuration. *
Fracture of necessity – always needs fixation if displaced.

### Common Complications

| Complication | Explanation |
|---|---|
| Non-union | Due to rotation of fragment and synovial fluid interference |
| Cubitus Valgus | Hyperemic overgrowth of lateral column |
| Tardy Ulnar Nerve Palsy | 20–25 years post-injury due to valgus stretch |

Dr. Anurag’s Note:
“Lateral condyle fracture is a fracture of necessity — treat surgically, or it will teach you
humility years later.”

### Distal Humerus Physis Separation (DeLee Classification)
- Seen in <3 years of age.
- Often mistaken for elbow dislocation.
- Diagnosis: Arthrography or MRI.
- Treatment:
  - <2 mm – Non-operative (cast)
  - 2 mm – ORIF (K-wires via Cocker’s approach)

| Displacement | Structure at Risk |
|---|---|

### Management Summary

| Type | Treatment |
|---|---|
| I | Cast/slab immobilization (90° flexion) |
| II, III, IV | Closed reduction + K-wire fixation |

### Reduction Principles
- Gentle traction (10–20° flexion).
- Flex elbow while pushing olecranon posteriorly.

<!-- page 8 -->

- Supination for lateral displacement, Pronation for medial (based on periosteal hinge).
### K-wire Fixation

| Configuration | Notes |
|---|---|
| Two lateral pins | Safe, avoids ulnar nerve |
| Cross pin (lateral + medial) | More stable, but ulnar nerve risk → flex elbow <45° while inserting |
| K-wires inside vs. outside | Short term (<3 wks) – outside; Long term (>6 wks) – buried inside |

### Pulse-less Hand Protocol

| Finding | Action |
|---|---|
| Pulse-less but pink | Observe (collateral circulation adequate) |
| Pulse-less and pale | Explore brachial artery (angiography ± repair) |

Complications

| Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| Early | Neurovascular injury, Compartment syndrome |
| Late | Cubitus varus/valgus, Malunion, Myositis ossificans, Tardy ulnar palsy |

### Key Takeaways
- ORIF with plate is gold standard for adult distal humerus fractures.
- Parallel plating offers best biomechanical stability.
- Early mobilization (<3 weeks) is essential to prevent stiffness.
- Identify & protect ulnar nerve in all posterior approaches.
- Lateral condyle humerus – fracture of necessity.
- Cubitus valgus & tardy ulnar palsy develop years after.
- Pulse-less pink hand – never panic, just observe.
Final Faculty Note:
“Fixation is not success unless motion returns. Stability without stiffness is the hallmark
of mastery.”
