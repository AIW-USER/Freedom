OVERVIEW
Focus: Understanding the Female Athlete Triad — its components, pathophysiology,
systemic effects, and management strategies in athletic populations.
Dr. Shikha’s Quote: “The strongest women in sport are not the leanest —
they are the most balanced.”

DEFINITION
Female Athlete Triad (FAT): A spectrum of interrelated conditions involving: 1. Low
Energy Availability (LEA) with or without disordered eating.
2. Menstrual Dysfunction.
3. Decreased Bone Mineral Density (BMD).
Originally defined (1992) by the American College of Sports Medicine (ACSM).
Revised (2007): Recognized as a continuum, not a fixed triad.

PATHOPHYSIOLOGY
### 1⃣ Low Energy Availability (LEA)
- EA = (Energy Intake – Exercise Energy Expenditure) / Fat-Free Mass (kg)

- LEA occurs when intake < energy used, impairing metabolic & hormonal functions.
Causes: - Disordered eating (intentional or unintentional).
- Excessive training volume.
- Restrictive diets (vegan, low-carb, intermittent fasting).
- Pressure to maintain leanness in sports (e.g., gymnastics, ballet, running).
“Energy availability is not about calories — it’s about fueling physiology.”

Mechanism:
↓ Energy → ↓ GnRH → ↓ LH surge → ↓ Estrogen → impaired ovulation + bone turnover

4⃣ Bone Mineral Density (BMD) Loss
Estrogen Deficiency → ↓ Osteoblastic activity + ↑ Osteoclastic resorption.
Results in: - Decreased BMD → Osteopenia / Osteoporosis.
- Microarchitectural deterioration → Stress fractures.
- Common sites: Tibia, femoral neck, metatarsals, pelvis.

| T-score | Classification |
|---|---|
| ≥ −1 | Normal |
| −1 to −2.5 | Osteopenia |
| < −2.5 | Osteoporosis |

BMD loss in adolescence = irreversible; peak bone mass forms by age 25.
CLINICAL FEATURES
### Early Signs
- Fatigue, irritability, low motivation.

- Poor recovery, sleep disturbances.

- Frequent injuries or infections.
### Musculoskeletal Signs
- Recurrent stress fractures.

- Muscle weakness or chronic pain.

- Low lean body mass.
### Menstrual & Metabolic Signs
- Amenorrhea / oligomenorrhea.

- Cold intolerance, brittle nails, hair loss.

<!-- page 4 -->

- Weight fluctuations or body dysmorphia.

### DIFFERENTIAL DIAGNOSIS

| Disorder | Key Distinguishing Features |
|---|---|
| PCOS | ↑ LH/FSH ratio, acne, hirsutism, cystic ovaries |
| Pregnancy | β-hCG positive |
| Thyroid Disorder | Abnormal TSH, T3/T4 |
| Hyperprolactinemia | ↑ Prolactin, galactorrhea |

Always rule out other causes before diagnosing functional hypothalamic
amenorrhea (FHA).

RED-S (Relative Energy Deficiency in Sport)
An expanded model by IOC (2014) — includes both genders.
Core Principle: LEA affects multiple systems beyond the triad: - Metabolic
- Immune
- Cardiovascular

- Endocrine
- Gastrointestinal
- Psychological
Mnemonic: “LEA leads to systemic fatigue — not just hormonal failure.”

MANAGEMENT APPROACH
1⃣ Multidisciplinary Care
Team: Physician, Sports Dietitian, Psychologist, Physiotherapist, Coach.
Goals: - Restore Energy Availability (EA).
- Normalize Menstrual Cycle.
- Prevent Bone Loss & Fractures.

### 2⃣ Nutritional Management

| Aspect | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Energy Availability | ≥45 kcal/kg FFM/day |
| Macronutrient Ratio | CHO 50–60%, Protein 15–20%, Fat 25–30% |
| Calcium | 1500 mg/day |
| Vitamin D | 1500–2000 IU/day |
| Iron & B12 | Correct deficiencies (common in vegetarians) |

“Fuel before, during, and after — the 3 golden windows of nutrition.”
### 3⃣ Training Modification
- Reduce training load by 10–20% initially.

- Avoid double sessions until menstrual function resumes.

- Include rest & recovery microcycles.

- Emphasize periodized training with adequate nutrition.

### 5⃣ Psychological Support
- Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) for disordered eating/body image issues.

- Stress management to improve hypothalamic function.

- Education for athletes, coaches, and parents. “Healing starts when the athlete stops competing with her own body.”
### BONE HEALTH MONITORING

| Parameter | Tool | Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| BMD (DEXA) | Hip + Lumbar spine | Every 12 months |
| Serum Vit D | Blood test | Every 6 months |
| Menstrual Record | Symptom diary | Monthly |

Fracture Prevention:
- Avoid NSAIDs & steroids.
- Encourage low-impact cross-training (cycling, swimming).
- Use heel raises & proper footwear.

RETURN-TO-PLAY (RTP) CRITERIA
Must meet all: 1. Normalized energy intake & menstrual function.
2. Stable weight (≥90% ideal body weight).
3. BMD ≥ −1.0 on DEXA.
4. Cleared by multidisciplinary team.
Caution: Athletes with ongoing amenorrhea or low BMD should not compete in
high-impact events.

<!-- page 7 -->

### PREVENTION STRATEGIES
- Annual screening for menstrual, dietary, and bone health.

- Education programs for coaches & athletes.

- Promote body positivity & avoid weight shaming.

- Encourage period tracking as a vital sign.

- Early intervention → better prognosis.

### TAKE-HOME SUMMARY
- FAT = Low Energy Availability + Menstrual Dysfunction + Low BMD.

- It’s a continuum, not a triad; even one component = diagnosis.

- Management is multidisciplinary — restore nutrition before medication.

- Estrogen therapy supports bone; training moderation restores physiology. “Strong bones, steady hormones, sufficient fuel — that’s true performance.”
