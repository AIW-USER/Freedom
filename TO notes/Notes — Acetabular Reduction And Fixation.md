Acetabular Fracture – Reduction Techniques and
Fixation Principles – Dr. Sanjay Arora
Overview
Reduction and fixation of acetabular fractures require a detailed understanding of
fracture pattern, stability, congruency, and three dimensional CT based assessment.
Posterior wall and transverse fractures form the bulk of surgical cases. Dr. Sanjay Arora
emphasizes correct interpretation of CT cuts, meticulous planning, and mastering
multiple reduction tools.
Faculty Remark: “Plan on the CT, execute in the OT. Reduction must be perfect, not
approximate.”

## Principles of Decision Making
### Two Primary Determinants
1. Congruency of reduction.
2. Stability of the hip after reduction.
If the joint is incongruent or unstable, operative management is mandatory.

Step 1: Assess Congruency
What to check
- Medial joint space widened compared to opposite hip.
- Non parallel joint surfaces.
- Persistent subluxation after reduction.
### CT Indicators of Incongruency
- Incarcerated fragment inside joint (capsule or wall fragment).
- Posterior wall fragment flipped into joint.
If incongruency is present → Surgery required.

Step 2: Assess Stability
### CT Based Posterior Wall Size

| Wall Size | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| < 20 percent | Stable |
| 20 to 50 percent | Indeterminate |
| > 50 percent | Unstable |

### Stress Testing Under Anaesthesia
- C arm in obturator oblique.
- Flexion.
- Adduction.
- Internal rotation.
- Telescoping force.
Loss of parallelism → unstable fracture → needs fixation.

## CT Based Planning – Four Critical Questions
1. How close is the fracture to the dome?
  - Posterior superior wall involvement → highly unstable.
  - May require trochanteric flip osteotomy for exposure.
2. Is the posterior wall comminuted?
  - Use spring plates or Allis T plates.
3. Are there intra articular fragments?
  - Small foveal → can be left.
  - Large dome fragments → must be removed.
4. Is there marginal impaction?
  - Elevate.
  - Graft (often from GT).
  - Fix with mini fragment screws.

## Operative Approaches
Kocher Langenbeck Approach
Indications
- Posterior wall fractures.
- Posterior column fractures.

<!-- page 3 -->

- Transverse / Juxtatectal / Infratectal.
- Transverse with posterior wall.
- Some T type fractures.
### Advantages of Prone Position
- Improves reduction of ischiopubic segment.
- Allows clamp placement through sciatic notch.
- Improves palpation of quadrilateral surface.
### Structures Visualized
- Entire posterior column.
- Greater and lesser sciatic notch.
- Ischial spine and tuberosity.
- Retroacetabular surface.

Reduction Tools and Techniques
### For Transverse Fractures
- Distal femoral traction.
- Hip joint distraction.
- Ischial tuberosity Schanz pin.
- Weber clamp (angled jaw) through notch.
- Farabeuf clamp.
### Sequence in Transverse + Posterior Wall
1. Reduce and fix transverse component first.
2. Then address posterior wall.
Challenges
- Transtectal fracture pattern.
- Osteochondral dome fragment.
- Ipsilateral SI joint injury.
- Symphysis or ring injury.

## Posterior Wall Fixation – Key Concepts
### Interfragmentary Screws
- Hold large wall fragments.
- Always combined with buttress plate.

<!-- page 4 -->

### Plate Application
- Plate must be under contoured.
- Plate should rest on fragment, close to rim.
Why Under contour?
- The plate loads the fragment.
- Screws contour the reduction.
- Prevents displacement during tightening.
### Lag Screws
- Through plate or independent.
- Never use lag screws alone.

Avoiding Intra Articular Screw Penetration
How to confirm
- Use end on view under fluoroscopy.
- AP, obturator, iliac oblique alone not sufficient.
- Adjust intensifier until joint surface becomes a line.
If threads not crossing that line → safe.

## Trochanteric Flip Osteotomy
Indications
- Posterior superior wall fractures involving roof.
- Need to shift abductors anteriorly.
- Better exposure of dome and superior acetabulum.
### Technique Pearls
- Flip fragment must contain gluteus medius/minimus attachments.
- Preserve blood supply.
### Preventing Heterotopic Ossification
- Gentle dissection.
- Avoid unnecessary muscle trauma.
- Excise damaged gluteus minimus.

## Percutaneous Fixation Techniques
Posterior Column Screws
### Antegrade Trajectories
1. Iliopubic eminence → ischial tuberosity.
2. Iliopubic eminence → ischial spine.
3. Iliac crest → sciatic notch.
### Retrograde Trajectory
- From ischial tuberosity → posterior column.
Tips
- Retrograde entry must be medial half of ischial tuberosity.
- Avoid visceral injury by staying along bone.

Anterior Column Screws
Antegrade
- Start superior to acetabulum.
- Narrow corridor near pubic root.
- Must avoid femoral vessels, corona mortis, bladder.
Retrograde
- Start inferior and lateral to pubic tubercle.
- Flexible nail technique helps find safe corridor.
### Combined Views Required
- Inlet.
- Obturator.
- Iliac oblique.
- Ensure no hip penetration.

## Key Precautions
- Posterior column must not be comminuted for screw fixation.
- Know all safe corridors before attempting percutaneous screws.
- Radiation exposure significant; protect team.

<!-- page 6 -->

### Viva and Exam Pearls
- Wall size <20 percent stable, >50 percent unstable.
- Marginal impaction must be elevated and grafted.
- Trochanteric flip improves exposure for roof fractures.
- Posterior wall incongruency = look for incarcerated fragment.
- End on view is essential to avoid joint penetration.
- Address transverse component before posterior wall.

Final Faculty Insight: “Understand the fracture on CT, respect soft tissues, and use
every reduction tool wisely. Perfect reduction is the only acceptable goal.”
