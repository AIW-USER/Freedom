Basics of Spine MRI – Dr. Daivik T Shetty (Jul 2026)
### Overview / Key Concepts
- A "life skill" lecture: read the MRI images yourself instead of reaching straight for the report.
- "It is all about fat and water": T1 = only fat bright; T2 = fat AND water bright; STIR / T2 fat- sat = only water bright.
- A systematic read: count the vertebrae, alignment, marrow, cord, discs, facets, ligaments, paraspinal muscles, adjacent structures.
- Covers sequences and planes, lumbar and cervical anatomy, degenerative disc terminology, stenosis, trauma, pathological vs osteoporotic fractures, and infection vs tumour.

Sequences
- MRI aligns free protons in the magnetic field; a short radiofrequency pulse excites them and the scanner records their relaxation.
- Fat protons realign fast and give a strong T1 signal; water dephases slowly and gives a strong T2 signal.

| Sequence | Bright | How to recognise / use |
|---|---|---|
| T1 | Fat only | CSF dark; epidural and subcutaneous fat bright. Anatomy, marrow fat, haematoma |
| T2 | Fat and water | CSF bright; healthy nucleus pulposus bright with dark annulus. Cord signal, discs |
| STIR (short tau inversion recovery) / T2 fat-sat | Water only (fat suppressed) | Pus, oedema, occult fractures with no loss of height; first sequence to check in trauma and infection |
| Post-contrast T1 fat-sat | Enhancing tissue | Infection, tumour, rim enhancement |
| Myelogram | CSF column | Occasionally helps plan the approach |

- A lesion dark on T1 and bright on T2 / STIR = water content (e.g. infection, oedema), not fat or simple degeneration.
- Standard protocol: sagittal T1 and T2, axial T1 and T2, STIR, post-contrast T1 fat-sat, and sometimes myelography. Coronal is less used.

Lumbar Anatomy on MRI
Sagittal
- Do not judge on one mid-sagittal slice: scroll from one side to the other; foraminal and extraforaminal discs are invisible on the midline cut.
- Mid-sagittal checklist: vertebral body signal, end plates, discs, ALL (prevertebral collections), PLL, cord / conus / cauda equina in the thecal sac, ligamentum flavum, interspinous ligaments, epidural and subcutaneous fat.
- Foraminal (parasagittal) cuts: "keyhole" foramen with the pedicle above, body and disc in front and the dot of the exiting root and ganglion surrounded by bright fat. Loss of that fat means compression (by a foraminal disc or a thick ligamentum flavum).

Slide 18: Foraminal sagittal cut: the keyhole with the exiting nerve root and its fat

![[TOn-notes-basics-of-spine-mri-jul-2026-p02-f005.jpg]]


Axial
- Three axial cuts per level: disc cut (both facets clearly seen, one over the other), foraminal cut (facets only partly seen), pedicle cut (pedicle visible; vertebral body).

<!-- page 3 -->

- Pedicle cuts matter: a sequestrated fragment may sit behind the pedicle, out of reach of a routine microdiscectomy.
- Structures: spinous process, lamina, pars, transverse process, facet joint, thecal sac, dorsal root ganglion; on T2 the cauda equina roots appear as dots and the traversing root sits at the side of the sac.
- Lateral recess (subarticular zone): at the pedicle level, between the back of the vertebral body and the superior articular process; the traversing root lives here. Less than about 4 mm is narrowed (as taught); judge it on the image, not only the report number.
- Right and left: imagine the patient lying supine with the feet towards you.

### Cervical Spine
- Common lesion: the disc-osteophyte complex rather than a soft disc.
- Sagittal: osteophytes, disc, PLL thickening, CSF in front of the cord (loss of the CSF cushion = cord at risk; myelopathy), cord signal on T2, ligamentum flavum.
- Axial: vertebral end plate, uncovertebral joint (uncinate hypertrophy, like facet arthropathy in the lumbar spine), vertebral artery, facets, lamina, ligamentum flavum, spinous process.
- Sites of compression: central disc or osteophyte, lateral (uncovertebral) disc or osteophyte, medial facet osteophyte, ligamentum flavum hypertrophy, PLL thickening.
- Always use the gradient image for discs and osteophytes; T2 for cord oedema.

### Degenerative Disc Disease

| Finding | MRI appearance |
|---|---|
| Annular fissure (tear) | Bright T2 spot or line within the normally dark annulus; can cause severe back or radicular pain even without a large disc |
| Desiccation (dehydration) | Disc loses its bright T2 nucleus and turns dark ("the disc is the opposite of hair: white is young, black is old"); early degeneration, may cause back pain |
| Diffuse bulge | Broad-based, symmetric or asymmetric |

Disc location and nerve roots

| Zone (old name / new name) | Root affected at L4–L5 |
|---|---|
| Central / paracentral (now right or left central) | Traversing L5 (central discs can compress the whole cauda equina) |
| Lateral recess / subarticular | Traversing L5 |
| Foraminal / neural foraminal | Exiting L4 |
| Extraforaminal / far lateral | Exiting L4 |

- Pure central discs are rare, so the new nomenclature uses "right central" or "left central".
- Correlate the zone and root with the clinical deficit before blaming the disc: radiculopathy can come from anywhere from the brain down.

Modic end-plate changes

| Type | Meaning | T1 | T2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Oedema | Dark | Bright |
| 2 | Fatty change | Bright | Bright |
| 3 | Sclerosis | Dark | Dark |

- Type 3 changes have been mistaken for metastasis; recognise them.

Stenosis
- Causes: disc, ligamentum flavum hypertrophy (look just under the facets on sagittal and axial), facet hypertrophy with osteophytes and joint fluid (compare all levels; one facet much larger = hypertrophy), uncinate hypertrophy in the neck.
- Facet arthropathy is graded 0–3 (normal 2–4 mm joint space to severe narrowing with large osteophytes and erosions).
- Facet cyst: can cause radiculopathy (case: left radiculopathy thought to be a disc); usually resolves without surgery.
- Central canal stenosis: mild (only lateral CSF lost), moderate (little CSF), severe (cauda equina roots no longer separable). Schizas grading A–D.

- Foraminal stenosis: mild (fat around the root kept), moderate (little fat), severe (root not seen).

Trauma
- Start with STIR and T2 fat-sat: bone oedema, soft-tissue oedema, haemorrhage (e.g. an odontoid fracture missed on X-ray; whiplash soft-tissue oedema).
- Then T2 for ligaments, soft tissue and cord; CT for bone detail and spatial relations; T1 for anatomy and haematoma.

### Pathological vs Osteoporotic Fractures
- Cardboard box analogy: normal vertebra = box full of stuffing (some loss of height, stable); osteoporotic = empty box (flattens with sharp edges); malignant = box full of styrofoam (collapses with the contents bulging out: posterior convexity).

| Feature | Osteoporotic | Malignant |
|---|---|---|
| Posterior wall | Retropulsed angular fragment, sharp edges | Convex posterior bulge |
| Levels | Contiguous | Skip lesions |
| Marrow | Some normal fat signal spared | Whole body replaced, no spared marrow |
| Posterior elements | Fracture lines only | Pedicle and posterior body involved (Batson plexus spread) |
| Paraspinal tissue | Smooth thin haematoma | Thick, lobulated, enhancing mass |

Infection
- Spinal TB: vertebral (paradiscal, anterior, central; atypical solitary, multifocal, posterior element) and non-vertebral (epidural, leptomeningeal, intramedullary).
- Early TB: anterior and paradiscal body involvement; the disc is spared early (avascular). Later: discitis, subligamentous spread, prevertebral and epidural collections.
- Isolated epidural TB can occur with a normal body and disc (biopsy proved). Pyogenic Tuberculous

| Speed | Rapid destruction over weeks | Slow |
|---|---|---|
| Disc | Destroyed early and markedly | Relatively preserved early |
| Soft tissue | Minimal | Extensive pre- and paravertebral collections |
| Contrast | Diffuse enhancement | Rim enhancement of abscesses |

Infection vs tumour: infection involves the disc and the bodies with collections; a tumour
(myeloma, lymphoma, metastasis) spares the disc and shows a solid mass. "Bad disc is good
news, good disc is bad news."

### Exam & Viva Pearls
- T1: fat only; T2: fat + water; STIR: water only.
- Three axial cuts per level: disc, foraminal, pedicle.
- Lateral recess is measured at the pedicle level; holds the traversing root.
- Paracentral / subarticular disc: traversing root; foraminal / far lateral: exiting root.
- Protrusion base wider than dome; extrusion dome wider or migrated; sequestration separated.
- Modic 1 oedema, 2 fat, 3 sclerosis.
- Cervical spine: gradient image for discs; T2 for the cord.
- Malignant collapse: posterior convexity, skip levels, no spared marrow, pedicle involvement, lobulated mass.
- TB: paradiscal anterior start, rim enhancement, big collections; disc spared in tumours.

### Take-Home Summary
- Identify the sequence from fat and water first, then read every slice in every plane, not just the mid-sagittal cut.
- Describe discs precisely (type, level, zone, side) and match the compressed root to the clinical deficit.
- Use STIR for trauma and infection; use the fracture and disc rules to separate osteoporosis, tumour and infection.
