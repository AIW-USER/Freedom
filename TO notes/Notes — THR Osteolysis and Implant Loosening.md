Target Ortho Quick Revision Notes – Osteolysis & Implant Loosening in THR
Faculty: Dr. Mukul Mohindra

Overview
This lecture covers aseptic osteolysis and implant loosening in THR—focusing on
tribology, debris generation, wear mechanisms, biological response, and
prevention. The session also explores diagnostic modalities and the evolution of
polyethylene generations, a crucial topic for DNB/FNB and arthroplasty viva.

### TRIBOLOGY & DEFINITIONS
- Tribology: Study of interacting surfaces in relative motion — involving friction, lubrication, and wear mechanics.
- Osteolysis: Bone loss caused by histocytic reaction to wear debris, leading to progressive bone resorption and prosthesis loosening.
- Two types:
  - Septic Osteolysis → from infection.
  - Aseptic Osteolysis → inflammatory response to wear debris (main topic here).
Key point: Aseptic osteolysis is the most common cause of THR failure
(≈70% of revisions worldwide).

PATHOGENESIS OF OSTEOLYSIS
### Stages of Development
1. Debris Generation → from bearing surfaces (polyethylene, metal, ceramic, or cement).
2. Debris Migration → particles move via the effective joint space into periprosthetic bone.
3. Cellular Response → macrophage activation → cytokine release → osteoclastic activity.
4. Micromotion & Pressure → increases dissemination and bone resorption.
5. Implant Loosening → irreversible bone loss, requiring revision.

### Types of Wear:
- Adhesive wear – dominant in THR; soft (poly) peels off hard (metal/ceramic).
- Abrasive wear – scraping of softer surface by a harder one (“cheese grater” effect).
- Delamination wear – fissuring of loaded surface; commoner in TKR.
- Third-body wear – loose particles in joint space cause further abrasion. MCQ Tip: Adhesive volumetric wear is the prime cause of failure in THR.

### EFFECTIVE JOINT SPACE (HARRIS CONCEPT)
- Refers to periprosthetic regions accessible to debris migration.
- Large effective joint space → more osteolysis.
- Contributing factors:
  - Non-circumferential porous coating.
  - Excess screw holes in acetabular shell.
  - Poor cement sealing or gaps.
Prevention:
- Use circumferential porous coating.
- Minimize screw holes.
- Achieve perfect press-fit or cement mantle to seal the joint space.

CELLULAR & BIOCHEMICAL RESPONSE
### Key Cells:
- Macrophages (central) — engulf debris → release cytokines.
- Osteoclasts — resorb bone under cytokine stimulation.
- Osteoblast inhibition — reduced bone formation.

<!-- page 3 -->

### Important Cytokines:
- TNF-α, IL-1, IL-6, PGE2 → activate RANKL pathway → bone resorption.
- RANKL on osteoblast binds RANK on osteoclast → osteoclastogenesis.
- MSC differentiation into osteoblasts suppressed → bone formation decreases. Histology: Numerous osteoclasts, few osteoblasts, macrophages filled with polyethylene debris.

X-ray Interpretation
### For Cemented Components:
- Loosening starts:
  - Femoral stem: Stem–cement interface.
  - Acetabular cup: Bone–cement interface.
- Evaluate via Gruen zones (1–7):
  - Zone 1 → Debonding.
  - Zone 4 → Subsidence.

<!-- page 4 -->

- Zone 7 → Cement fragmentation.
- Significant Lucency: >2 mm or progressive widening.
### For Uncemented Components (Engh’s Classification):
- Bone ingrowth: Stable, no subsidence, no radiolucent lines.
- Fibrous ingrowth: Parallel radiolucent lines ± subsidence.
- Unstable implant: Divergent lines + cortical hypertrophy at stem tip.

PREVENTION STRATEGIES
### 1⃣ Pharmacologic
- Bisphosphonates: Inhibit osteoclasts; reduce bone resorption.
- Denosumab: Anti-RANKL antibody; under investigation for preventing osteolysis.
- BMP-2: Promotes osteoblast activity and bone formation.
### 2⃣ Surgical & Implant Factors
- ## Use correct head size: 22–46 mm. > 46 mm → thinner polyethylene → ↑ wear.
- Cementing technique: Prefer 3rd or 4th generation (vacuum mixing, restrictor, pressurization, centralizer).
- Porous coating: Circumferential coating ↓ effective joint space.
- Head material: Ceramic or metal-on-poly (avoid metal-on-metal).
- Polyethylene thickness: ≥6 mm mandatory.
### 3⃣ Patient Factors
- Avoid early THR in young, active patients (high wear).
- Educate on activity moderation post-THR.

### MATERIALS SCIENCE INSIGHTS
- Ultra-high molecular weight polyethylene (UHMWPE):
  - Polymerized ethylene gas under high temp (200–400°C) & pressure.
  - Crystalline + amorphous phases; improved by cross-linking.
- Free radicals formed during gamma sterilization → cause oxidation → reduced strength.
  - Neutralized by annealing/remelting or Vitamin E addition.
- Alternative materials under study:
  - PEEK (Polyether ether ketone) – high wear resistance, under evaluation.
  - Polyacetal, PTFE – abandoned due to poor wear properties.

### KEY EXAM PEARLS
- Aseptic osteolysis = macrophage-mediated reaction to wear debris.
- Most common debris: Polyethylene (90%).
- Effective joint space: Pathway for debris → bone.
- Gold standard for detecting micromotion: Radiostereometric analysis (RSA).
- Cytokines: TNF-α, IL-1, IL-6, PGE2 → RANKL activation.
- Bisphosphonates & Denosumab: Prevent progression.
- Cemented stem interface: Femur – stem–cement; Acetabulum – bone–cement.
- Head size range: 22–46 mm.
- Best polyethylene: 3rd-gen, ram-extruded, low-oxygen processed.
- Ethylene oxide: Sterilization, not antioxidant.
- New material under study: PEEK (Polyether ether ketone).
