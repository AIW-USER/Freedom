OVERVIEW
Definition:
Cervical myelopathy is a progressive spinal cord dysfunction caused by
compression and ischemia at the cervical level, leading to upper motor neuron
(UMN) signs in the lower limbs and hand clumsiness in the upper limbs.

Radiculopathy vs Myelopathy:

Site of Nerve root Spinal cord
involvement

Type of lesion LMN UMN

Symptoms Radiating pain, Hand clumsiness, gait disturbance,
paresthesia spasticity

Etiology: - Degenerative spondylosis (most common).
- OPLL (Ossified Posterior Longitudinal Ligament).
- Disc herniation.
- Congenital stenosis.
- Post-traumatic or iatrogenic causes.

CLINICAL FEATURES
History & Symptoms
Upper Limb: - Clumsiness of hand, difficulty buttoning shirt, handwriting changes.
- Loss of grip strength.
- Trouble with fine motor tasks (mixing food, combing, holding pen).
Lower Limb: - Unsteady gait, frequent falls, worsens in darkness.
- Stiffness and heaviness in legs.
Other Symptoms: - Neck pain, backache.
- Numbness/paresthesia in upper and lower limbs.
- Vague limb weakness.

- Hand grip weakness.

- Sensory loss in glove and stocking pattern.

### NURICK’S GRADING OF CERVICAL MYELOPATHY

| Grade | Description |
|---|---|
| 0 | Signs of root involvement only |
| 1 | Signs of cord involvement; normal gait |
| 2 | Mild gait difficulty but independent |
| 3 | Gait abnormality; needs support |
| 4 | Ambulation possible only with assistance |
| 5 | Bedridden or wheelchair-bound |

RADIOLOGICAL EVALUATION
### X-RAY (Lateral View):
- Alignment abnormalities (lordosis, kyphosis).

- Instability (flexion–extension views).

- OPLL (ossified posterior longitudinal ligament).
MRI – Investigation of Choice:
Indications: Any clinical suspicion of myelopathy → MRI first.
Assess: - Severity and level of compression.
- Signal intensity changes in the cord (T2 hyperintensity = cord edema/myelomalacia).
- Alignment (lordotic/kyphotic).
- Presence of tandem stenosis (coexistent cervical and lumbar canal stenosis).

### CT Scan:
- Defines bony compression and OPLL classification.

### OSSIFIED POSTERIOR LONGITUDINAL LIGAMENT (OPLL)
- Pathological ectopic bone formation within the PLL.

- Common in Asian males.

<!-- page 4 -->

- May coexist with cervical spondylosis.

- Causes gradual cord compression and stiffness.
### Types (Radiological Classification):

| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| Segmental | Discrete ossification behind each vertebral body |
| Continuous | Long ossified bar behind multiple vertebrae |
| Mixed | Combination of both |
| Circumscribed | Localized around disc level only |

Key Point: OPLL does not always correlate with disc degeneration severity.

MANAGEMENT PRINCIPLES
Non-Operative:
- Reserved for mild, non-progressive symptoms.

- Physiotherapy + cervical collar for stability.

- Analgesics, muscle relaxants.

- Avoid manipulation/traction.
Indications for Surgery: - Nurick grade ≥ 2 with progressive deficit.
- Severe cord compression with T2 signal change.
- Unstable or kyphotic alignment.

SURGICAL OPTIONS
Anterior Approach
Indications: - Predominant anterior compression (disc/OPLL).
- Single or two-level involvement.
Techniques: 1. Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF): For single-level
soft disc or focal OPLL.
2. Anterior Corpectomy and Fusion (ACCF): For multilevel or central OPLL.
Advantages: Direct decompression and restoration of lordosis.
Limitations: Dysphagia, graft complications, risk of dural tear in OPLL.

<!-- page 5 -->

Posterior Approach
Indications: - Multilevel compression.
- Posterior compression (ligamentum flavum hypertrophy).
- Lordotic or neutral alignment.
### Techniques: 1. Laminectomy ± Fusion.
2. Laminoplasty (Open-door / Double-door).
Posterior Decompression Ineffective If: - Anterior canal encroachment > 7 mm.
- Cervical lordosis < 10°.

### Combined or Selective Procedures:
- Selective Laminectomy (C3–C6) preserving C2 & C7 to prevent post-laminectomy kyphosis.

- Segmental Partial Laminectomy (SPL) in focal compression.

### LAMINOPLASTY – TECHNIQUE & BENEFITS
- Open-door laminoplasty: Hinge on one side, opening on the other to expand canal.

- Maintains spinal stability and allows posterior decompression without removing lamina.

- Useful for multilevel OPLL and lordotic spines.

- Fixation: Mini plates or sutures used to stabilize lamina after expansion.
Advantages:
- Preserves posterior elements.
- Maintains motion.
- Reduces risk of post-laminectomy deformity.

### COMPLICATIONS
- Dural tear (especially in OPLL).

- C5 palsy (postoperative root traction).

- Post-laminectomy kyphosis.

- Instability and recurrence.

<!-- page 6 -->

- Residual or recurrent compression.

### EXAM & VIVA PEARLS
- Cervical myelopathy = UMN lesion below the level of lesion.

- Hoffmann’s sign = pathognomonic for early myelopathy.

- Most common cause: Degenerative spondylosis.

- OPLL: Common in Asian men; causes progressive stenosis.

- MRI: Investigation of choice.

- Surgical decision: Based on alignment + compression direction + number of levels.

- Lordosis <10°: Posterior decompression less effective.

- Nurick grade ≥2: Surgical indication.

### TAKE-HOME SUMMARY
- Cervical myelopathy = spinal cord compression causing UMN signs.

- Early signs: Hand clumsiness, gait unsteadiness.

- MRI is mandatory for diagnosis and grading.

- Surgical approach depends on alignment and compression site.

- Anterior decompression: Direct but limited to 1–2 levels.

- Posterior decompression: Preferred for multilevel and lordotic spines.

- Preserve C2 & C7 to prevent post-laminectomy deformity.

- Goal: Decompression with alignment preservation and functional recovery.
